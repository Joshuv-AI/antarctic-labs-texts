Abstract
. We give a classification of rank r r torus equivariant vector bundles ℰ \mathcal{E} on a toric scheme 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} over a discrete valuation ring 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , in terms of graded piecewise linear maps Φ \Phi from the fan of 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} to the (extended) building of GL ⁡ ( r ) \operatorname{GL}(r) . This is an extension of Klyachko’s classification of torus equivariant vector bundles on toric varieties over a field on one hand, and Mumford’s classification of equivariant line bundles on toric schemes over 𝒪 \mathcal{O} on the other hand. We also give a simple criterion for equivariant splitting of ℰ \mathcal{E} into a sum of toric line bundles in terms of its piecewise linear map. Among other things, this work lays the foundations for study of arithmetic geometry of toric vector bundles.



Introduction

Since their inception in the early 20th century, vector bundles have been in the focus of many important developments in modern geometry. Nevertheless, we still know little about the structure of vector bundles, specially over varieties of dimension greater than
1
. Due to their combinatorial nature, equivariant vector bundles on toric varieties provide a rich class of examples that allows one to have a glimpse at the world of vector bundles on higher dimensional varieties.

Let
T
be an
n
-dimensional (split) algebraic torus over a field
K
and let
X=X_{\Sigma}
be a
T
-toric variety associated to a fan
\Sigma
. We recall that a
torus equivariant vector bundle
(or
toric vector bundle
for short) on
X
is a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
together with a linear action of
T
that lifts that of
X
. It is well-known that toric line bundles are classified by integral piecewise linear functions on the fan
\Sigma
of the toric variety. They are central objects that connect geometry of toric varieties with theory of convex polytopes and this connection has proven to be extremely fruitful in algebra, geometry and combinatorics.

Toric vector bundles have been famously classified by Klyachko in the remarkable work
[
19
]
which has been very influential in subsequent development of the theory. His classification is in terms of certain data of compatible filtrations in a finite dimensional vector space
E
(see Section
1.1
). We point out that the first classification of toric vector bundles goes back to Kaneyama and is in terms of certain data of cocycles (
[
14
]
). In
[
16
,
17
]
and
[
22
]
, the Klyachko data of a toric vector bundle has been interpreted as a piecewise linear map from the fan of the toric variety to the space of valuations on the vector space
E
(the
extended Tits building
of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
).

In this paper, we are concerned with toric schemes over
a discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
. Basic examples of a discrete valuation ring are
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{k}[[t]]
, the power series ring (which often is considered in tropical geometry) and
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the ring of
p
-adic integers (which often is considered in arithmetic geometry). Toric schemes over a discrete valuation ring have been classified by Mumford in
[
18
, §IV]
. In
[
12
]
, Gubler extends the classification to non-discrete valuation rings.

The main result of the paper is a combinatorial classification of toric vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
on a toric scheme over a discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
. When the toric scheme is proper, the classification is in terms of
piecewise affine maps
from the polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
of the toric scheme to the
extended Bruhat-Tits building
of
\operatorname{GL}(r)
where
r
is the rank of
\mathcal{E}
. This on one hand extends Klyachko’s classification and on the other hand extends the classification of toric line bundles on toric schemes due to Mumford (see
[
18
, §IV.3(e)]
,
[
5
, Section 3.6]
).

The present paper can be considered as a continuation of ideas in
[
16
]
where classifying torus equivariant principal bundles (or
toric principal bundles
for short) on toric varieties over a field
K
is connected with the theory of Tits buildings of algebraic groups. The main result in
[
16
]
states that for a reductive group
G
, toric principal
G
-bundles on
X_{\Sigma}
are classified by
piecewise linear maps
from the fan
\Sigma
to the
extended Tits building of
G
(also called the
cone over the Tits building of
G
).

Several applications of the results of the present paper are already emerging in arithmetic and combinatorial algebraic geometry.

•

In
[
3
]
the results of the paper are used to give a combinatorial description of equivariant Chern classes of toric vector bundles over a DVR. We hope this to be useful in advancing our understanding of arithmetic Chern classes.

•

In
[
2
]
, the authors define tautological classes of matroids which are certain Chow cohomology classes of the permutahedral toric variety. They use this construction to give geometric interpretations of some important invariants of matroids and prove related conjectures. In a work in progress, we use the classification in the present paper, to define notion of a tropical toric vector bundle over a discrete valuation ring. We then use it to extend the
[
2
]
construction and define tautological classes of
valuated matroids
and their equivariant Chern classes. We hope this approach to lead to a generalization of vector bundles on metric graphs in
[
11
]
to higher dimensional tropical varieties.

•

Extending Klyachko’s classification of toric vector bundles over a field, it is desirable and ambitious to ask for a combinatorial classification of equivariant vector bundles on (normal) varieties with an action of a torus (of possibly smaller dimension than that of the variety). In
[
8
]
, using the results of the paper as a main ingredient, a combinatorial classification of torus equivariant vector bundles on (normal) varieties with a codimension-one torus action (complexity-one
T
-varieties) is given.

•

Similarly, the results of the paper can be used to give a classification of toric vector bundles over
\operatorname{Spec}(D)
where
D
is a Dedekind domain, e.g. the ring of integers of a number field.

•

By considering toric vector bundles on toric schemes over higher rank local fields, the main classification in the paper can be used to suggest an extension of the notion of Bruhat-Tits building to higher dimensional local fields (cf.
[
21
]
).

Now we give a more detailed account of the main results of the paper. Let
K
be a field with discrete valuation
\operatorname{val}:K\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
and let
\mathcal{O}
be its discrete valuation ring. Let
T
be a (split) torus over
\mathcal{O}
. Recall that a
T
-toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
over
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O})
is given by a fan
\Sigma
in the upper half-space
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
where
N
denotes the cocharacter lattice of
T
(Section
1.2
).

To make the results easier to state, first we state them in the case where
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
is proper over
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O})
, or equivalently
\Sigma
is complete, that is, the support of
\Sigma
is the whole
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. We denote by
\Sigma_{1}
(respectively
\Sigma_{0}
) the polyhedral complex (respectively the fan) obtained by intersecting the cones in
\Sigma
with the hyperplane
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
(respectively
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
). The fan
\Sigma_{0}
is the
recession fan
of the polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
. The generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}
is the toric variety (over
K
) associated to the fan
\Sigma_{0}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
E
be an
r
-dimensional vector space over
K
. For any
m\geq 0
, a
level
m
additive norm
on
E
is a function
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

v(\lambda e)=m\operatorname{val}(\lambda)+v(e)
, for all
e\in E
and
\lambda\in K
.

(2)

(Non-Archimedean property)
v(e_{1}+e_{2})\geq\min\{v(e_{1}),v(e_{2})\}
, for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
.

(3)

v(e)=\infty
if and only if
e=0
.

We will be mainly concerned with the cases where
m=0,1
. When
m=1
we have
v(\lambda e)=\operatorname{val}(\lambda)+v(e)
, and when
m=0
, we have
v(\lambda e)=v(e)
, for all
\lambda\neq 0
.

We denote the space of all level
m
additive norms by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{m}(E)
. In particular, we denote the space of all level
1
additive norms on
E
by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)=\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{1}(E)
. We also denote the space of all level
0
additive norms by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)=\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}(E)
. We call
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
(respectively
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
), the
extended Bruhat-Tits building
(respectively the
extended Tits building
) of
E
. See Section
2
, and also Section
3
for the connection with Tits and Bruhat-Tits buildings of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
. Finally we let

\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)=\bigsqcup_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{m}(E),

and call it the
total extended building
of
E
.

A choice of a basis
B
for the vector
K
-space
E
, specifies a subset of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
which is an
r
-dimensional real vector space where
r=\dim(E)
.
We denote this subset by
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
and call it the
extended apartment
associated to the basis
B
. Similarly, we have a subset of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
which is an
r
-dimensional real affine space. We denote it by
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
and call it the
extended apartment
associated to the basis
B
.
Both
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
are unions of their apartments.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
be as above.
In Section
4.2
, we define the following notions:

•

A
piecewise linear map

\Phi_{0}
from a fan
\Sigma_{0}
to the extended Tits building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
. Roughly speaking it means that
\Phi_{0}
maps each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma_{0}
to an extended apartment and restriction to
\sigma
is a linear map.

•

A
piecewise affine map

\Phi_{1}
from a polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
to the extended Bruhat-Tits building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
. Roughly speaking it means that
\Phi_{1}
maps each polyhedron
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
to an extended apartment and restriction to
\Delta
is an affine map.

Recall that given a piecewise affine function
\phi:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\mathbb{R}
, one can define
\phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\mathbb{R}
, the
linear part of
\phi_{0}
. Here
\Sigma_{0}
is the collection of recession cones of the polyhedra in
\Sigma_{1}
. More generally, for a piecewise affine map
\Phi
, we can define
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
, the
linear part of
\Phi
.

There is a natural notion of morphism of piecewise affine maps (Definition
4.9
) and piecewise affine maps to extended Bruhat-Tits buildings of finite dimensional vector spaces
E
form a category.

Let
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
be a toric scheme over
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O})
associated to a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with corresponding polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
. Given a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
, we denote the fiber
\mathcal{E}_{x_{0}}
, over the distinguished point
x_{0}
, by
E
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 2

(Main theorem, first part)
.

With notation as above, let
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
be a proper toric scheme over
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O})
. Then there is a bijection between the isomorphism classes of toric vector bundles on
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
and the piecewise affine maps from
|\Sigma_{1}|
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
, for finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
. Moreover, this bijection extends to an equivalence of the corresponding categories.

The moral of Theorem
2
is that the extended Bruhat-Tits building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
can be considered as an analogue of the classifying space of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
for rank
r
toric vector bundles over proper toric schemes.

Theorem 3

(Main theorem, second part)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle over a proper toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
with the corresponding piecewise affine map
\Phi:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
. Then the generic fiber
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}
, which is a (usual) toric vector bundle over the toric variety
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}=X_{\Sigma_{0}}
over the field
K
, corresponds to
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
, the linear part of

\Phi
, which is a piecewise linear map.

Theorems
2

and
3
extend the well-known results on classification of torus equivariant line bundles on a toric scheme (
[
5
, Section 3.3]
).

When
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
is not proper over
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O})
(equivalently, support of
\Sigma
is not the whole
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
) the main theorems still work but beside the piecewise affine map
\Phi_{1}:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
we also need the data of the piecewise linear map
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
which is compatible with
\Phi_{1}
. It is convenient to encode the data of
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{0}
in the notion of a
graded piecewise linear map

\Phi
from the fan
\Sigma
to the total extended building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)=\bigsqcup_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{m}(E)
. We say that a map
\Phi
is a graded pieceiwse linear map if it is piecewise linear in the sense of Definition
4.3
, and moreover for any
m\geq 0
, it sends every point
(x,m)\in|\Sigma|
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{m}(E)
(see Definition
4.4
).

Theorem 4

(Main theorem, general case)
.

Suppose
\Sigma
is a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
that is not necessarily complete. Then there is an equivalence of categories between the category of toric vector bundles on the toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
and the category of graded pieceiwse linear maps
\Phi:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
for finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
. Moreover, the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}
over the generic point corresponds to the piecewise linear map
\Phi_{0}:=\Phi_{|\Sigma_{0}}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
.

In Section
5
we give a criterion for equivariant splitting of a toric vector bundle on a toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
. It is an extension of Klyachko’s criterion for equivariant splitting of toric vector bundles over a field to the setting of toric schemes over a DVR. We use this criterion to give an example of a rank
2
toric vector bundle on the canonical model of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over the
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
that does not split (Example
5.3
).

As Klyachko shows, the splitting of low rank toric vector bundles over projective space (over a field) follows from a Helly-type theorem for the Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
(
[
19
, Theorem 6.1.2]
). In Example
5.4
, we observe that an analogue of Klyachko’s Helly’s theorem holds for the Bruhat-Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(which is a
(p+1)
-regular infinite tree). In a future work we extend Klyachko’s Helly’s theorem to the Bruhat-Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
, for any finite dimensional
K
-vector space
E
. We end the introduction by proposing some problems for further study:

•

Give a combinatorial characterization of ampleness of a toric vector bundle over a DVR in terms of its piecewise affine map, analogous to
[
13
,
17
]
.

•

Construct analogue of parliament of polytopes (
[
9
]
) for a toric vector bundle over a DVR capturing the space of global sections and give a criterion for global generation. The paper
[
15
]
which concerns the line bundle case is relevant to this.

•

Give criterion for (semi)stability of a toric vector bundle over a DVR in terms of its piecewise affine map.

1.
Preliminaries on toric varieties and toric schemes

1.1.
Klyachko’s classification of toric vector bundles over a field

In this section we review Klyachko’s classification of toric vector bundles in
[
19
]
.
Let
T\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
denote an
n
-dimensional algebraic torus over a field
\mathbb{k}
. We let
M
and
N
denote its character and cocharacter lattices respectively. We also denote by
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}
the
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces spanned by
M
and
N
. For a cone
\sigma\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
let
M_{\sigma}
be the quotient lattice:

M_{\sigma}=M/(\sigma^{\perp}\cap M).

Let
\Sigma
be a (finite rational polyhedral) fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
X_{\Sigma}
be the corresponding toric variety. Also
X_{\sigma}
denotes the invariant affine open subset in
X_{\Sigma}
corresponding to a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
. We denote the support of
\Sigma
, that is the union of all the cones in
\Sigma
, by
|\Sigma|
. For each
i
,
\Sigma(i)
denotes the subset of
i
-dimensional cones in
\Sigma
. In particular,
\Sigma(1)
is the set of rays in
\Sigma
. For each ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
we let
\nu_{\rho}
be the primitive vector along
\rho
, i.e.
\nu_{\rho}
is the unique vector on
\rho
whose integral length is equal to
1
.

We say that
\mathcal{E}
is a
toric vector bundle
on
X_{\Sigma}
if
\mathcal{E}
is a vector bundle on
X_{\Sigma}
equipped with a
T
-linearization. This means that there is an action of
T
on
\mathcal{E}
which lifts the
T
-action on
X_{\Sigma}
such that the action map
\mathcal{E}_{x}\to\mathcal{E}_{t\cdot x}
for any
t\in T
,
x\in X_{\Sigma}
is linear.

We fix a point
x_{0}\in X_{0}\subset X_{\Sigma}
in the dense orbit
X_{0}
. We often identify
X_{0}
with
T
and think of
x_{0}
as the identity element in
T
. We let
E=\mathcal{E}_{x_{0}}
denote the fiber of
\mathcal{E}
over
x_{0}
. It is an
r
-dimensional vector space where
r=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})
.

For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
we have an invariant open subset
X_{\sigma}\subset X_{\Sigma}
. The space of sections
H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
is a
T
-module. We let
H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}\subseteq H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
be the weight space corresponding to a weight
u\in M
; these spaces define the weight decomposition:

H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})=\bigoplus_{u\in M}H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}.

Every section in
H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}
is determined by its value at
x_{0}
. Thus, by restricting sections to
E=\mathcal{E}_{x_{0}}
, we get an embedding
H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}\hookrightarrow E
. Let us denote the image of
H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}
in
E
by
E_{u}^{\sigma}
. Note that if
u^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
then
multiplication by the character

\chi^{u^{\prime}}

gives an injection
H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u}\hookrightarrow H^{0}(X_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})_{u-u^{\prime}}
. Moreover, the multiplication map by
\chi^{u^{\prime}}
commutes with the evaluation at
x_{0}
and hence induces an inclusion
E_{u}^{\sigma}\subset E_{u-u^{\prime}}^{\sigma}
. If
u^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\perp}
then these maps are isomorphisms and thus
E_{u}^{\sigma}
depends only on the class
[u]\in M_{\sigma}=M/(\sigma^{\perp}\cap M)
. For a ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
we write

E^{\rho}_{j}=E_{u}^{\rho},

for any
u\in M
with
\langle u,\nu_{\rho}\rangle=j
(all such
u
define the same class in
M_{\rho}
). Equivalently, one can define
E^{\rho}_{u}
as follows (see
[
19
, §0.1]
). Pick a point
x_{\rho}
in the orbit
O_{\rho}
and let:

E^{\rho}_{u}=\{e\in E\mid\lim_{t\cdot x_{0}\to x_{\rho}}\chi^{u}(t)^{-1}(t\cdot e)\textup{ exists in }\mathcal{E}\},

where
t
varies in
T
in such a way that
t\cdot x_{0}
approaches
x_{\rho}
. One checks that
E^{\rho}_{u}
does not depend on the choice of
x_{\rho}
and only depends on
j=\langle u,\nu_{\rho}\rangle
.

We thus have a decreasing filtration on
E
:

(1)

\cdots\supset E^{\rho}_{j-1}\supset E^{\rho}_{j}\supset E^{\rho}_{j+1}\supset\cdots

An important step in the classification of toric vector bundles is that a toric vector bundle over an affine toric variety is

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle of rank

. Then

splits equivariantly into a sum of line bundles

We denote the multiset

by
u(\sigma)
. The above shows that the filtrations
(E^{\rho}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

\rho\in\Sigma(1)

There is a decomposition

of

and a multiset

(2)

E^{\rho}_{i}=\sum_{\langle u_{j},\nu_{\rho}\rangle\geq i}L_{j}.

We call a collection of decreasing

if for any
\sigma\in\Sigma

The following is Klyachko’s result on classification of toric vector bundles (see

Theorem 1.2

The category of toric vector bundles

on

is naturally equivalent to the category of compatible collections of filtrations on finite dimensional vector spaces
E
.

2.
Preliminaries on additive norms and valuations

In this section we review some basic facts about valuations and additive norms on a finite dimensional
K
-vector space
E
. These are related to the geometric realizations of the Tits and Bruhat-Tits buildings of the reductive algebraic group
\operatorname{GL}(E)
. These are the main gadgets in our main theorems on classifying toric vector bundles over toric schemes (Section
4
).

As usual let
E
be an
r
-dimensional vector space over a field
K
equipped with a discrete valuation
\operatorname{val}:K\to\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
. We will be interested in two kinds of “valuations” on
E
, first the ones that extend the valuation
\operatorname{val}
on
K
to
E
, and secondly the ones that extend the trivial valuation on
K
to
E
. Following terminology from Bruhat-Tits theory for
\operatorname{GL}(E)
, we refer to the first kind of valuations as
additive norms
. We will refer to the second kind simply as
valautions
on
E
. More precisely we make the following definitions:

Definition 2.1

(Additive norm)
.

We call a function
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
an
additive norm
if the following hold:

(1)

For all
e\in E
and
\lambda\in K
we have
v(\lambda e)=\operatorname{val}(\lambda)+v(e)
.

(2)

For all
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
, the non-Archimedean inequality
v(e_{1}+e_{2})\geq\min\{v(e_{1}),v(e_{2})\}
holds.

(3)

v(e)=\infty
if and only if
e=0
.

An additive norm is
integral
if it attains values in
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Note that if
v
is an additive norm then for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
v_{a}
defined by
v_{a}(e)=v(e)+a
is also an additive norm. Two additive norms
v
,
v^{\prime}
are said to be
equivalent
if their difference is a constant.

Definition 2.2

(Valuation)
.

We call a function
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
a
valuation
if the following hold:

(1)

For all
e\in E
and
0\neq\lambda\in K
we have
v(\lambda e)=v(e)
.

(2)

For all
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
, the non-Archimedean inequality
v(e_{1}+e_{2})\geq\min\{v(e_{1}),v(e_{2})\}
holds.

(3)

v(e)=\infty
if and only if
e=0
.

A valuation is
integral
if it attains values in
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
.

More generally, we define the notion of a level
m
additive norm which extends both additive norms and valuations.

Definition 2.3

(Level
m
additive norm)
.

For
m\geq 0
, we call a function
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
a
level
m
additive norm
if the following hold:

(1)

For all
e\in E
and
0\neq\lambda\in K
we have
v(\lambda e)=m\operatorname{val}(\lambda)+v(e)
.

(2)

For all
e_{1},e_{2}\in E
, the non-Archimedean inequality
v(e_{1}+e_{2})\geq\min\{v(e_{1}),v(e_{2})\}
holds.

(3)

v(e)=\infty
if and only if
e=0
.

Clearly, an additive norm is a level
1
additive norm and a valuation is a level
0
additive norm.

Definition 2.4

(Extended buildings)
.

Throughout we use the following notation and terminology:

(a)

We denote the set of all additive norms on
E
by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
and call it the
extended Bruhat-Tits building of
E
. In the literature, also it is sometimes called the
Goldman-Iwahori space of
E
.

(b)

We denote the set of all valuations on
E
by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
and call it the
extended Tits building of
E
(or the
cone over the Tits building of
E
).

(c)

For
m\geq 0
, we denote the set of all level
m
additive norms by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{m}(E)
. Clearly
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{1}(E)=\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}(E)=\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
. We let

\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)=\bigsqcup_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{m}(E),

and call it the
total Bruhat-Tits building of
E
(or the
total building of
E
for short
).

Remark 2.5
.

We note that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
comes with a natural action of the multiplicative semigroup
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
: if
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a level
m
additive norm and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
then one verifies that
\lambda v
is a level
m\lambda
additive norm.

A
frame
is a direct sum decomposition of
E=\sum_{i=1}^{r}L_{i}
into one-dimensional subspaces
L_{i}
. In other words, frames correspond to vector space bases up to multiplication of basis elements by nonzero scalars.

We say that a level
m
additive norm
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is
adapted
to a frame
L=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}\}
if the following holds. For any
e=\sum_{i}e_{i}
,
e_{i}\in L_{i}
, we have:

v(e)=\min\{v(e_{i})\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

In other words, if
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
is a basis with
b_{i}\in L_{i}
, then for any
e=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{i}
we have:

v(e)=\min\{m\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+v(b_{i})\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

That is, the additive norm
v
is determined by its values on the basis
B
.

Definition 2.6

(Extended apartment)
.

Let
L=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}\}
be a frame for

. We denote the collection of all level
m

by
\widetilde{A}_{m}(L)
and call it the extended level
m

L
. We put
\widetilde{A}(L)=\bigsqcup_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{A}_{m}(L)
. We also set

\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(L)=\widetilde{A}_{0}(L)
and
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(L)=\widetilde{A}_{1}(L)
.
If
B
is a basis for
E
spanning a frame
L
, we also denote the extended apartments corresponding to
L
by
\widetilde{A}(B)
,
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(B)
and
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(B)
, respectively.

v\mapsto\Lambda_{v}
and
\Lambda\mapsto v_{\Lambda}
are inverses of each other and hence provide a one-to-one correspondence between integral additive norms and lattices.

Fix a basis for
E
to identify it with
K^{r}
. The group
\operatorname{GL}(r,K)
acts transitively on the set of all
\mathcal{O}
-lattices and the stabilizer of the standard lattice
\mathcal{O}^{r}
is
\operatorname{GL}(r,\mathcal{O})
. Thus, the collection of all
\mathcal{O}
-lattices in
K^{r}
can be identified with
\operatorname{GL}(r,K)/\operatorname{GL}(r,\mathcal{O})
. It is usually referred to as the
affine Grassmannian
of the group
\operatorname{GL}(r,K)
.

2.0.2.
Valuations and labeled flags

A labeled flag
(F_{\bullet},c_{\bullet})
is a flag of subspaces in
E
:

F_{\bullet}=(\{0\}=F_{0}\subsetneqq F_{1}\subsetneqq\cdots\subsetneqq F_{k}=E),

and a sequence of decreasing numbers
c_{\bullet}=(c_{1}>\cdots>c_{k})
.
Each valuation
v:E\to\overline{R}
naturally gives rise to a labeled flag as follows: let
v(E)=\{\infty=c_{0}>c_{1}>\cdots>c_{k}\}
be the value set of
v
. For each
i=1,\ldots,k
, put

F_{i}=E_{\geq c_{i}}=\{e\in E\mid v(e)\geq c_{i}\}.

Conversely, given a labeled flag
(F_{\bullet},c_{\bullet})
we define a valuation
v
by
v(e)=\max\{c_{i}\mid e\in F_{i}\}
,
\forall e\in E
. One verifies that the above gives a one-to-one correspondence between valuations and labeled flags.

2.0.3.
Floor, ceiling and pull-back of an additive norm

Next we discuss some basic operations on additive norms, namely the floor and ceiling functions and pull-back, and prove some facts about them for future use in Section
4.3
.

One has a natural partial order
\preceq
on the set
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
of all additive norms.
Let
v
,
v^{\prime}
be additive norms on
E
. We say that
v\preceq v^{\prime}
if
v(e)\leq v^{\prime}(e)
, for all
e\in E
.

Recall that for
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the floor
\lfloor r\rfloor
(respectively the ceiling
\lceil r\rceil
) is the largest integer less than or equal to
r
(respectively the smallest integer greater than or equal to
r
).

Definition 2.7

(Floor and ceiling of an additive norm)
.

For an additive norm
v:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
we define its
floor

\lfloor v\rfloor
and
ceiling

\lceil v\rceil:E\to\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
to be functions from
E
to
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
given by:

\lfloor v\rfloor(e)=\lfloor v(e)\rfloor,

\lceil v\rceil(e)=\lceil v(e)\rceil,\quad\forall e\in E.

Proposition 2.8
.

\lfloor v\rfloor
and
\lceil v\rceil
are additive norms.

Proof.

We write the proof for the ceiling, the proof for the floor is similar. We know that there exists a basis
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
such that
v
is adapted to
B
. Thus:

v\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\right)=\min\{\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+v(b_{i})\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

Now

\displaystyle\lceil v\rceil\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\lceil v(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{i})\rceil

\displaystyle=\lceil\min\{\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+v(b_{i})\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}\rceil

\displaystyle=\min\{\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+\lceil v(b_{i})\rceil\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

But any expression
\min\{\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+a_{i}\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}
,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, defines an additive norm. This proves that
\lceil v\rceil
is indeed an additive norm.
∎

The floor (respectively ceiling) of an additive norm
v
is the greatest integral additive norm that is smaller than or equal to
v
(respectively smallest integral additive norm that is greater than or equal to
v
).

Definition 2.9

(Pull-back of an additive norm)
.

Let
F:E\to E^{\prime}
be a linear map between
K
-vector spaces
E
and
E^{\prime}
. Given an additive norm
v^{\prime}:E\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
one can consider its pull-back
F^{*}(v^{\prime})
given by

F^{*}(v^{\prime})(e)=v^{\prime}(F(e)),\quad\forall e\in E.

We note that
F^{*}(v^{\prime})
may not be an additive norm on
E
for the following reason:
F
may have nonzero kernel in which case
F^{*}(v^{\prime})
assigns value
\infty
to nonzero elements in the kernel. We will need the following in Section
4.3
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
F:E\to E^{\prime}
be a linear map between
K
-vector spaces
E
and
E^{\prime}
. Let
v
and
v^{\prime}
be integral additive norms on
E
and
E^{\prime}
respectively. Then, for all
e\in E
,

v(e)\leq F^{*}(v^{\prime})(e)

if and only if

F(\Lambda_{v})\subset\Lambda_{v^{\prime}}.

Recall that
\Lambda_{v}
,
\Lambda_{v^{\prime}}
denote the lattices corresponding to the integral additive norms
v
,
v^{\prime}
respectively (see (
3
)).

Proof.

Suppose
v(e)\leq F^{*}(v^{\prime})(e)
, for all
e\in E
, but
F(\Lambda_{v})\not\subset\Lambda_{v^{\prime}}
. Then there exists
e\in\Lambda_{v}
with
F(e)\notin\Lambda_{v^{\prime}}
. Since
e\in\Lambda_{v}
we have
v(e)\geq 0
and since
F(e)\notin\Lambda_{v^{\prime}}
we have
v^{\prime}(F(e))<0
, the contradiction proves the claim. Conversely, suppose
F(\Lambda_{v})\subset\Lambda_{v^{\prime}}
then for any
e\in E
we have:

\displaystyle v(e)

\displaystyle=\max\{k\mid\varpi^{-k}e\in\Lambda_{v}\},

\displaystyle\leq\max\{k\mid\varpi^{-k}F(e)\in F(\Lambda_{v})\},

\displaystyle\leq\max\{k\mid\varpi^{-k}F(e)\in\Lambda_{v^{\prime}}\},

\displaystyle=v^{\prime}(F(e)).

∎

3.
Tits and Bruhat-Tits buildings of
\operatorname{GL}(E)

A
building
is an (abstract) simplicial complex together with a collection of distinguished subcomplexes called
apartments
that satisfy certain axioms (see
[
1
, Definition 4.1]
). One can think of the notion of building as a combinatorial version of the notion of symmetric space from Lie theory and differential geometry.

There are two important kinds of buildings:
spherical buildings
and
affine buildings
.

•

In a spherical building, every apartment is a triangulation of a sphere. To an algebraic group
G
over a field there corresponds a spherical building which is usually referred to as
the
Tits building
of
G
. As a simplicial complex, it encodes the relative position of
parabolic subgroups
of
G
.

•

In an affine building every apartment is a triangulation of an affine (Euclidean) space. To an algebraic group
G
over a discretely valued field there corresponds an affine building which is usually referred to as the
Bruhat-Tits building
of
G
. As a simplicial complex, it encodes the relative position of
parahoric subgroups
of
G
.

As mentioned before, in this paper, we are only concerned with the Bruhat-Tits building of a general linear group
\operatorname{GL}(r,K)
and we do not discuss the technical construction of the Bruhat-Tits building of an arbitrary linear algebraic group. For the general construction we refer the reader to
[
4
]
or or
[
23
, Section 3]
. For the general theory of buildings we refer the interested reader to the monographs
[
1
]
and
[
10
]
.

3.1.
The Bruhat-Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(r,K)

In this section we review the descriptions of the Bruhat-Tits building of the general group in terms of lattices and additive norms. The reader can also consult
[
1
, Section 6.9]
and
[
23
, Section 1.2.1]
.

Let
E\cong K^{r}
be an
r
-dimensional vector space over a discretely valued field
K
. We first describe the Bruhat-Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
as an abstract simplicial complex which we denote by
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
. This description is in terms of of
lattices
in
E
. Then we describe the geometric realization of the simplicial complex
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
as a topological space which we denote by
\mathfrak{B}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
. This geometric realization is in terms of
additive norms
on
E
. Every simplex in
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
corresponds to a subset of
\mathfrak{B}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
homeomorphic to a standard simplex. By abuse of terminology we refer to both
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
and
\mathfrak{B}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
as the Bruhat-Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
.

Recall that a lattice
\Lambda
in
E
is a rank
r
free
\mathcal{O}
-submodule in
E
, and two lattices
\Lambda_{1}
,
\Lambda_{2}
are said to be equivalent if for some nonzero
c\in K^{\times}
we have
c\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}
. We denote the equivalence class of a lattice
\Lambda
by
[\Lambda]
.

The vertices in the Bruhat-Tits building
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
correspond to the equivalence classes of lattices in
E
. Two vertices
[\Lambda_{1}]
and
[\Lambda_{2}]
are connected if for some representatives
\Lambda_{1}
,
\Lambda_{2}
we have
\varpi\Lambda_{1}\subsetneqq\Lambda_{2}\subsetneqq\Lambda_{1}
. A
(k-1)
-simplex in
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
corresponds to
k
vertices
[\Lambda_{1}],\ldots,[\Lambda_{k}]
such that:

(4)

\varpi\Lambda_{k}\subsetneqq\Lambda_{1}\subsetneqq\cdots\subsetneqq\Lambda_{k}.

We call a chain of lattices as in (
4
) a
periodic lattice chain
.

A
full periodic lattice
chain is a chain of lattices

(5)

\varpi\Lambda_{r}\subsetneqq\Lambda_{1}\subsetneqq\ldots\subsetneqq\Lambda_{r}.

Then each subsequent quotient
\Lambda_{i+1}/\Lambda_{i}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}
-module of rank
1
. The full periodic lattice chains correspond to simplices of maximum dimension (also called
chambers
).

Let
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
be a
K
-vector space basis for
E
and let
\Lambda_{i}\subset E
be the free

\mathcal{O}
-module (lattice) generated by

\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{i},\varpi b_{i+1},\ldots,\varpi b_{r}\}
. The chain of lattices

(6)

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\varpi\,\mathcal{O}\,b_{i}=\varpi\Lambda_{r}\subsetneqq\Lambda_{1}\subsetneqq\ldots\subsetneqq\Lambda_{r}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}\,b_{i}

is a full periodic lattice chain. Moreover, every full periodic lattice chain has the above form (
6
) for some choice of a basis
B
.

Also recall that a frame
L=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}\}
is a direct sum decomposition
E=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}L_{i}
of
E
into
1
-dimensional subspaces
L_{i}
.
To a frame
L
there corresponds a subcomplex of
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
called the
apartment
associated to
L
as follows. Let
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
be a basis of
E
with
b_{i}\in L_{i}
.
The vertices in the apartment are equivalence classes
[\Lambda]
where
\Lambda
is of the form

(7)

\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}\varpi^{a_{i}}b_{i},

for any choice of
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that a different choice of a basis
B
in the same frame
L
gives rise to the same collection of vertices but possibly with different coordinates
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
. More precisely, let
\{b^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{r}\}
be another basis with
b^{\prime}_{i}=\lambda_{i}b_{i}
,
0\neq\lambda_{i}\in K
. Then
\Lambda
from (
7
) can be written as

\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}\varpi^{a_{i}-\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})}b^{\prime}_{i}.

Clearly, if
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
runs over all the points in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
then
(a_{1}-\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{1}),\ldots,a_{r}-\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{r}))
also runs over all the points in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. The set of vertices in the apartment is a copy of
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
but without a specified point as the origin. A choice of a basis
B
, spanning the frame
L
, distinguishes a point in the apartment as the origin.

Remark 3.1

(Iwahori and parahoric subgroups)
.

The
\operatorname{GL}(E)
-stabilizer of a full periodic lattice chain is called an
Iwahori subgroup
. Let us fix a basis
B
and identify
E
with
K^{r}
. Then the Iwahori subgroup that is the stabilizer of a full lattice chain as in (
6
) is equal to the preimage of a Borel subgroup
B\subset\operatorname{GL}(r,\mathbb{k})
under the evaluation map
p:\operatorname{GL}(r,\mathcal{O})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(r,\mathbb{k})
sending the unifromizer
\varpi
to
0
. The stabilizer of a periodic lattice chain is called a
parahoric subgroup
. It is the preimage, under
p
, of a parabolic subgroup in
\operatorname{GL}(r,\Bbbk)
.
Each frame
L
in
E
determines a split maximal torus
H_{L}\subset\operatorname{GL}(E)
. The simplices in the apartment of
L
correspond to the parahoric subgroups in
\operatorname{GL}(E)
that contain the maximal torus
H_{L}
.

Next we talk about the geometric realization of the abstract simplicial complex
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
. By the
geometric realization of
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
we mean a topological space
\mathfrak{B}(E)
together with a triangulation (that is, a decomposition into subsets homeomorphic to standard simplices) such that the simplices in the triangulation give the same abstract simplicial complex as
\Delta_{\textup{aff}}(E)
.

We say that two additive norms
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
on
E
are
equivalent
if their difference is a constant.

Definition 3.2

(Geometric realization of Bruhat-Tits building)
.

We define
\mathfrak{B}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
, the
(geometric realization of) the Bruhat-Tits building
of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
to be the set of equivalence classes of additive norms on
E
. For a frame
L
, the
(geometric realization of) the apartment
associated to
L
, denoted by
A_{\textup{aff}}(L)
, is the set of equivalence classes of all additive norms adapted to
L
.
Let
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
be a basis spanning the frame
L
. Each additive norm
v
adapted to
L
is uniquely determined by the values
\{v(b_{1}),\ldots,v(b_{r})\}
. It follows that
A_{\textup{aff}}(L)
can be identified with the
(r-1)
-dimensional affine space
\mathbb{R}^{r}/\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{r-1}
(since we are taking equivalence classes of additive norms).

The space
\mathfrak{B}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
has a natural topology and a triangulation. For a frame
E
, a choice of a basis
B
spanning the frame
L
gives an identification of the apartment
A_{\textup{aff}}(L)
with the affine space
\mathbb{R}^{r-1}
.

[\Lambda^{\prime}]

\Lambda^{\prime}

\varpi\Lambda\subsetneqq\Lambda^{\prime}\subsetneqq\Lambda.

Let
\overline{\Lambda^{\prime}}

3.2.
The Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(r,K)

We now turn to the discussion of Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
, which is a spherical building associated to the group
\operatorname{GL}(E)
. As in the case of Burhat-Tits building, we describe the Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
in two ways: as a simplicial complex
\Delta_{\textup{sph}}(E)
and, its geometric realization
\mathfrak{B}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
as a topological space.

The abstract simplicial complex,
\Delta_{\textup{sph}}(E)
, can be described as follows. Its vertices are the proper nonzero vector subspaces of
E
. Two subspaces
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are connected if one of them is a subset of the other. The
(k-1)
-simplices of
\Delta_{\textup{sph}}(E)
are formed by sets of
k
mutually connected vertices, hence
(k-1)
-simplices correspond to partial flags

F_{\bullet}=(\{0\}\subsetneqq F_{1}\subsetneqq\cdots\subsetneqq F_{k}\subsetneqq E).

Maximal simplices, i.e. chambers, correspond to complete flags. The apartments in
\Delta_{\textup{sph}}(E)
, correspond to frames in
E
. A flag
F_{\bullet}
is said to be
adapted
to a frame
L=\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}\}
if all the subspaces in the flag are spanned by a subset of the
L_{i}
. The apartment corresponding to a frame
L
is the collection of all flags adapted to
L
.

The geometric realization of the spherical building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
can be described using valuations on
E
. This is an analogue of the Goldman-Iwahori realization of the Bruhat-Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
as the space of equivalence classes of additive norms on
E
(Section
3.1
). This description of Tits building of the general linear group appears in
[
16
, Section 1.4]
, the authors are not aware of another reference for this.

Two valuations
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
are said to be
equivalent
if there exist
\lambda,c\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda>0
such that
\lambda v_{1}+c=v_{2}
.

Definition 3.3

(Geometric realization of Tits building)
.

The geometric realization
\mathfrak{B}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
can be constructed as the set of all equivalence classes of valuations on
E
. The collection of all equivalence classes of valuations that are adapted to a given frame
L
is the (geometric realization of) the apartment associated to
L
, which we denote by
A_{\textup{sph}}(L)
. Each valuation
v
adapted to
L
is uniquely determined by the values
\{v(L_{1}\setminus\{0\}),\ldots,v(L_{r}\setminus\{0\})\}
. it follows that
A_{\textup{sph}}(L)
can be identified with the
(r-2)
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{R}^{r-1}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cong S^{r-2}
(since we are taking equivalence classes of valuations).

\Sigma
is a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with the corresponding polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
. We recall that
\mathfrak{X}
comes with a distinguished point
x_{0}
in the open chart
\mathfrak{U}_{0}
giving an identification of this open set with the torus
T_{\eta}=T_{K}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
toric vector bundle over
\mathfrak{X}
is a
T
-linearized vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathfrak{X}
.
A toric vector bundle is the same as a
T
-linearized locally free sheaf of constant rank
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-modules on
\mathfrak{X}
. By a
morphism
of toric vector bundles over
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
we mean a torus equivariant morphism of vector bundles. Toric vector bundles over
\mathfrak{X}
and their morphisms form a category.

We refer the reader to
[
20
, Definition 1.6]

for the definition of a
T
-linearization of a vector bundle on a
T
-scheme (the definition in the above mentioned reference is for line bundles but the same works for vector bundles as well). In short, a
T
-linearization of
\mathcal{E}
is a lifting of action of
T
on
\mathfrak{X}
to an action of
T
on
\mathcal{E}
by morphisms of vector bundles.

In this section we give a classification of toric vector bundles over
\mathfrak{X}
in terms of piecewise linear maps from the fan
\Sigma
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
, the total Bruhat-Tits building of
\operatorname{GL}(E)
, for finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
. When
\Sigma
is complete, this is the same as a piecewise affine map from the polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
to the extended Bruhat-Tits building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
.

4.1.
Local equivariant triviality

As in the case of toric vector bundles over a field, the following is a key step in the classification. The proof is similar to the one for toric vector bundles over a field (see
[
19
, Proposition 2.1.1]
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle on an affine toric scheme
\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}
corresponding to a cone
\sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is equivariantly trivial. That is, there exist characters
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\in M
such that
H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}R_{\sigma,i}
where
R_{\sigma,i}\cong R_{\sigma}
and
T
acts on
R_{\sigma,i}
via character
u_{i}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show there are
T
-weight sections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
that freely generate
H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
as an
R_{\sigma}
-module. If the cone
\sigma
lies in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
, there is no special fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma,o}
and we are in the case of a toric vector bundle over an affine toric variety
\mathfrak{X}_{\sigma,\eta}
. Thus, we can assume that
\sigma
does not lie in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
. In this case,
there is a unique closed
T_{s}
-orbit
O_{\sigma}
in the special fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{s}
. Recall that
T_{s}=T_{\mathbb{k}}
is the torus over the residue field
\mathbb{k}=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}
. Since
O_{\sigma}
is an orbit, the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{|O_{\sigma}}
is equivariantly trivial. That is, we can find a basis of
T
-weight sections
s^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,s^{\prime}_{r}\in H^{0}(O_{\sigma},\mathcal{E}_{|O_{\sigma}})
. By general properties of coherent sheaves on affine schemes, one knows that the
R_{\sigma}
-module homomorphism:

j^{*}:H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})\to H^{0}(O_{\sigma},\mathcal{E}_{|O_{\sigma}}),

induced by the inclusion morphism
j:O_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}
, is surjective. Note that the
R_{\sigma}
-module structure on
H^{0}(O_{\sigma},\mathcal{E}_{|O_{\sigma}})
comes from the homomorphism
R_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{k}[O_{\sigma}]
determined by the embedding
O_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}
. Moreover,
j^{*}
is
T
-equivariant. In other words,
j^{*}
is
M
-grading preserving. Thus we can find
T
-weight sections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})

such that
j^{*}(s_{i})=s^{\prime}_{i}
, for all
i
. We claim that these
s_{i}
freely generate
H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
as an
R_{\sigma}
-module. First we claim that for any
x\in\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}
, the images of the
s_{i}
in the fiber
\mathcal{E}_{x}
are linearly independent. This is because the subscheme of
\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}
where these images are linearly dependent is a closed
T
-invariant subscheme and hence, if non-empty, should contain
O_{\sigma}
. However, since the
s^{\prime}_{i}
form a basis for
H^{0}(O_{\sigma},\mathcal{E}_{|O_{\sigma}})
, the degeneracy locus cannot contain
O_{\sigma}
as a subscheme and must therefore be empty, which proves the claim. Now from the assumption that
\mathcal{E}
is locally free we conclude that for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
the images of
s_{i}
in
\mathcal{E}_{x}
form a basis. It follows that the
s_{i}
are an
R_{\sigma}
-basis for
H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E})
as required. ∎

4.2.
Graded piecewise linear maps

As usual, let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
(respectively
\Sigma_{0}
) the corresponding polyhedral complex in the affine hyperplane
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
(respectively, fan in the hyperplane
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
).

In
[
16
]
, the notion of an (integral) piecewise linear map from a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
to the extended Tits building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
is introduced. These integral piecewise linear maps are exactly the gadgets that classify toric vector bundles over a field. For the sake of completeness we recall this definition here.

Definition 4.3

(Piecewise linear map to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
)
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
.
We say that a map
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
is a
piecewise linear
map if the following holds:

(a)

For any cone
\sigma\in\Sigma_{0}
, there is an extended apartment
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(\sigma)
(not necessarily unique) such that the restriction
\Phi_{0,\sigma}:={\Phi_{0}}_{|\sigma}
maps
\sigma
into
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(\sigma)
.

(b)

For any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{0}
,
\Phi_{0,\sigma}
is the restriction of a linear map from
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
to
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(\sigma)\cong\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

We say that
\Phi_{0}
is an
integral
piecewise linear map if for each
\sigma\in\Sigma_{0}
,
\Phi_{0,\sigma}
is the restriction of an integral linear map from
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
to
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(\sigma)
. In other words,
\Phi_{0,\sigma}
sends lattice points to lattice points.

In this section, we define the similar notion of a graded piecewise linear map
\Phi
from a fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
to the total Bruhat-Tits building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
. The main result of the paper (Section
4.3
) is that these integral graded piecewise linear maps are exactly the gadgets to classify toric vector bundles on toric schemes over a DVR.

We also define the notion of a piecewise affine map
\Phi_{1}
from a polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
to the extended Bruhat-Tits building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
. The restriction of a graded piecewise linear map
\Phi
to
|\Sigma_{1}|
(respectively
|\Sigma_{0}|
)
gives a piecewise affine map
\Phi_{1}
(respectively a piecewise linear map
\Phi_{0}
). In fact, the information of
\Phi
is equivalent to the information of
(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})
(see Proposition
4.6
below).

Recall that when
\Sigma
in a complete fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the fan
\Sigma_{0}
can be recovered from the polyhedral complex
\Sigma_{1}
as its recession fan. Thus, in this case, the information of
\Phi
is the same as the information of
\Phi_{1}
alone, and
\Phi_{0}
can be recovered from
\Phi_{1}
as its linear part.

Definition 4.4

(Graded piecewise linear map to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in the upper half-space
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
We say that a map
\Phi:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
is a
graded piecewise linear
map if the following holds:

(a)

For any cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there is an extended apartment
\widetilde{A}(\sigma)
(not necessarily unique) such that the restriction
\Phi_{\sigma}:=\Phi_{|\sigma}
maps
\sigma
into
\widetilde{A}(\sigma)
.

(b)

For any
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
\Phi_{\sigma}
is the restriction of a linear map from
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
to
\widetilde{A}(\sigma)\cong\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

(c)

For any
m\geq 0
, the map
\Phi
sends
|\Sigma_{m}|:=|\Sigma|\cap(N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{m\})
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{m}(E)
. That is, it sends a point of level
m
in the fan to a point of level
m
in the extended building.

We say that
\Phi
is
integral
if for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
\Phi_{\sigma}
is the restriction of an integral linear map from
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
to
\widetilde{A}(\sigma)
.

We recall that a function
\Phi:N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
is an
affine map
if
\Phi
is a linear map followed by a translation. In other words,
there is a linear map
\Phi_{0}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
such that for all
x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
we have:

\Phi(x,1)=\Phi_{0}(x,0)+b.

The linear map
\Phi_{0}
is called the
linear part
of
\Phi
. We say that
\Phi
is an
integral
affine function if
\Phi(N\times\{1\})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. This means that the entries of the matrix of
\Phi_{0}
, with respect to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
N
and the standard basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, are integers, and moreover,
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

Definition 4.5

(Piecewise affine map to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
)
.

Let
\Sigma_{1}
be a polyhedral complex in the affine space
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
.
A map
\Phi_{1}:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
is a
piecewise affine
map if the following holds:

(a)

For any polyhedron
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
, there is a (not necessarily unique) extended apartment
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(\Delta)
such that the restriction
\Phi_{1,\Delta}:={\Phi_{1}}_{|\Delta}
maps
\Delta
into
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(\Delta)
.

(b)

For any
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
,
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
is the restriction of an affine map from
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
to the affine space
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(\Delta)
.

We say that
\Phi
is an
integral
piecewise affine map if
for each
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
,
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
is the restriction of an integral affine map from
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
to
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(\Delta)
.

Let
\Phi:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
be a graded piecewise linear map. Then the restriction
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}(E)=\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
of
\Phi
to
|\Sigma_{0}|
is a piecewise linear map in the sense of Definition
4.3
. Moreover, the restriction
\Phi_{1}:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{1}(E)=\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
of
\Phi
to
|\Sigma_{1}|:=|\Sigma|\cap(N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\})
is a piecewise affine map in the sense of Definition
4.5
.

One can recover the graded piecewise linear map
\Phi
from
\Phi_{0}
and
\Phi_{1}
in an obvious manner. For
(x,m)\in|\Sigma|\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
we have:

(9)

\Phi(x,m)=\begin{cases}m\Phi_{1}(x/m,1)&\quad m\neq 0,\\
\Phi_{0}(x,0)&\quad m=0.\\
\end{cases}

One verifies the following.

Proposition 4.6
.

The equation (
9
) gives a one-to-one correspondence between the graded piecewise linear maps
\Phi:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
and pairs
(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})
of piecewise linear maps
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
and piecewise affine maps
\Phi_{1}:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
that are compatible in the following sense: For any polyhedron
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
, the linear part of
\Phi_{1}
on the recession cone of
\Delta
agrees with
\Phi_{0}
. In particular, when the fan
\Sigma
is complete, the piecewise linear map
\Phi_{0}
is determined by the piecewise affine map
\Phi_{1}
as its linear part.

Remark 4.7
.

The above definitions extend to arbitrary affine buildings.

Remark 4.8
.

Let
\Phi_{1}:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
be an integral piecewise affine map. For any polyhedron
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
, the requirement that
\Phi_{1,\Delta}:={\Phi_{1}}_{|\Delta}:\Delta\to\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(\Delta)
is an integral affine map means that there exists a
K
-basis
B_{\Delta}=\{b_{\Delta,1},\ldots,b_{\Delta,r}\}
for
E
and
\{u_{\Delta,1},\ldots,u_{\Delta,r}\}\subset M
such that for any
x\in\sigma
,
\Phi_{1,\Delta}(x)
, as an additive norm on
E
, is given by

(10)

\Phi_{1,\Delta}(x)\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{\Delta,i}\right)=\min\{\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+\langle u_{\Delta,i},x\rangle\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

Definition 4.9

(Morphism of graded piecewise linear maps)
.

Let
F:E\to E^{\prime}
be a linear transformation between finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
,
E^{\prime}
and let
\Phi:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
and
\Phi^{\prime}:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E^{\prime})
be two graded piecewise linear maps. We say that
F
gives a
morphism

\Phi\to\Phi^{\prime}
if for any
(x,m)\in|\Sigma|
we have:

\Phi(x,m)\preceq F^{*}(\Phi^{\prime}(x,m)),

(see Definition
2.9
and cf. Proposition
2.10
).

For a fixed fan
\Sigma
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the collection of graded piecewise linear maps
\Phi:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
, for finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
, together with the above notion of morphism, form a category.

4.3.
Main theorems

We break our classification of toric vector bundles over a DVR into a few theorems (Theorems
4.10
,
4.11
,
4.12
). We present the proofs later in this section. The proofs involve using the notion of
ceiling
of an additive norm (Definition
2.7
).

As usual, let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
the corresponding toric scheme. Recall that
x_{0}
is the distinguished point in the open orbit in
\mathfrak{U}_{0}
in the generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}\cong T_{\eta}
. For a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
, we let
E=\mathcal{E}_{x_{0}}
. It is an
r
-dimensional
K
-vector space where
r=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})
.

Theorem 4.10
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
between the isomorphism classes of toric vector bundles over a toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
and the integral graded piecewise linear maps from
|\Sigma|
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
, the total Bruhat-Tits buildings of general linear groups of finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a toric vector bundle over a toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
then the generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}
is a usual toric variety over the field
K
and
\mathcal{E}_{|\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}}
is a usual toric vector bundle on it. The next theorem describes the piecewise linear map (equivalently, the Klyachko filtrations) associated to the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{|\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}}
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Given a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
with associated integral graded piecewise linear map
\Phi=\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
, the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{|\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}}
over the generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}
, which is a toric vector bundle over the field
K
, corresponds to the piecewise linear map
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
, obtained by restricting
\Phi
to
|\Sigma_{0}|
(see
[
16
, Example 2.8]
).

Theorem 4.12
.

The correspondence
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
gives
an equivalence of categories between the category of toric vector bundles over the toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
and the category of integral graded piecewise linear maps from
|\Sigma|
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
, for finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
.

When
\Sigma
is a complete fan, Theorem
4.12
can be restated in terms of piecewise affine maps. As before, for a graded piecewise linear map
\Phi:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
let
\Phi_{1}:|\Sigma_{1}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
denote its restriction to
|\Sigma_{1}|
which is a piecewise affine map. Recall that in this case,
\Phi_{0}
is the linear part of
\Phi_{1}
.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Then the correspondence
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\Phi_{\mathcal{E},1}
gives
an equivalence of categories between the category of toric vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
over the toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
and the category of integral piecewise affine maps
\Phi_{1}
from
|\Sigma_{1}|
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
, for finite dimensional
K
-vector spaces
E
.

Proofs of Theorems
4.10
and
4.11
.

Recall from Section
1.2
that, for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we have the affine toric scheme
\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}=\operatorname{Spec}(R_{\sigma})
where:

(11)

R_{\sigma}=\mathcal{O}[\chi^{u}\varpi^{k}\mid(u,k)\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap\widetilde{M}]\subset K[T_{\eta}].

In particular,
R_{0}=K[T_{\eta}]
is the coordinate ring of the torus
T_{\eta}\cong(K^{\times})^{r}
, isomorphic to the Laurent polynomial ring over
K
in
r
indeterminates.
The affine toric schemes
\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in\Sigma
, glue together to form the toric scheme
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
.
The distinguished point on the scheme
\mathfrak{X}_{0}=\operatorname{Spec}(K[T_{\eta}])
is
x_{0}=1\in T_{\eta}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a toric vector bundle on
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
of rank
r
. First we see how to associate to
\mathcal{E}
an integral graded piecewise linear map
\Phi=\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}:|\Sigma|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
. The toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is equivalent to the data of a
T
-linearized sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-modules of rank
r
on
\mathfrak{X}
. Let
E=\mathcal{E}_{x_{0}}
which is an
r
-dimensional
K
-vector space. For each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, consider the
M
-graded
R_{\sigma}
-module
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}:=H^{0}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma},\mathcal{E}_{|\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma}})
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
is trivial over the open orbit
\mathfrak{X}_{0}=T_{\eta}
, we can identify
\mathcal{E}_{|\mathfrak{X}_{0}}
with
T_{\eta}\times E
and hence
\mathcal{F}_{0}
with
K[T_{\eta}]\otimes_{K}E
.
Then, for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the restriction of
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
to
\mathfrak{X}_{0}
gives us an embedding

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{0}=K[T_{\eta}]\otimes_{K}E.

(From now on, for the ease of notation, we may drop the tensor product sign and write
ab
in place of
a\otimes b
.) Consider the decomposition of
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
into
M
-homogeneous components:

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}=\bigoplus_{u\in M}\mathcal{F}_{\sigma,u},

where
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma,u}\subset\chi^{u}E\subset K[T_{\eta}]\otimes_{K}E
is the
u
-homogeneous component of
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
(note that the
u
-homogeneous component is the
(-u)
-weight space in
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
). Since the
u
-homogeneous component in
K[T_{\eta}]\otimes_{K}E
is
\chi^{u}E
, we can write:

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma,u}=\chi^{u}\Lambda_{\sigma,u},

for some
\mathcal{O}
-submodule
\Lambda_{\sigma,u}\subset E
.

On the other hand, by the local equivariant triviality (Proposition
4.2
) the
M
-graded
R_{\sigma}
-module
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}
is
free of rank
r
and we can write:

(12)

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}R_{\sigma}\cdot s_{\sigma,i},

where
s_{\sigma,i}
is an
M
-homogeneous section whose
M
-degree we denote by
u_{\sigma,i}
, that is, the image of
s_{\sigma,i}
in
K[T_{\eta}]\otimes_{K}E
lands in
\chi^{u_{\sigma,i}}E
.
Let us write:

s_{\sigma,i}=\chi^{u_{\sigma,i}}b_{\sigma,i}

for some
b_{\sigma,i}\in E
. Since (
12
) is a direct sum, the set of vectors
B_{\sigma}=\{b_{\sigma,1},\ldots,b_{\sigma,r}\}
forms a
K
-vector space basis for
E
. We thus have:

(13)

\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\bigoplus_{(u^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap\widetilde{M}}\mathcal{O}\cdot\chi^{u^{\prime}}\varpi^{k^{\prime}}\,\chi^{u_{\sigma,i}}b_{\sigma,i}.

To compute the
u
-homogeneous component
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma,u}
in (
13
) we should sum over all
(u^{\prime},k^{\prime})
such that
u^{\prime}=u-u_{\sigma,i}
and
(u^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap\widetilde{M}
. This means that

\langle(u-u_{\sigma,i},k^{\prime}),y\rangle\geq 0,~~\forall y\in\sigma.

That is,

(14)

\Lambda_{\sigma,u}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k^{\prime}:(u-u_{\sigma,i},k^{\prime})\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap\widetilde{M}}\mathcal{O}\,\varpi^{k^{\prime}}b_{\sigma,i}.

We note that the condition
(u-u_{\sigma,i},k^{\prime})\in\sigma^{\vee}
is equivalent to
(u-u_{\sigma,i},k^{\prime})\in\rho^{\vee}
, for all
\rho\in\sigma(1)
. where
\sigma(1)
denotes the set of rays in
\sigma
. Thus, from (
14
), we have:

\Lambda_{\sigma,u}=\bigcap_{\rho\in\sigma(1)}\Lambda_{\rho,u}.

Hence we can conclude that the sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is completely determined by the
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\Lambda_{\rho,u}
, for
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
and
u\in M
.
Let
\rho
be a ray in
\Sigma(1)
. We consider two cases:

Case 1:
The ray
\rho
does not lie in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
. Let
(\nu,1)
be the unique point of intersection of
\rho
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{1\}
. Note that
\nu
is a rational point but in general may not be a lattice point. Then:

(15)

\displaystyle(u-u_{\sigma,i},k^{\prime})\in\rho^{\vee}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle~\langle u-u_{\sigma,i},\nu\rangle+k^{\prime}\geq 0

(16)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle~k^{\prime}\geq\langle u_{\sigma,i}-u,\nu\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle~k^{\prime}\geq\lceil\langle u_{\sigma,i}-u,v\rangle\rceil.

We recall that
\lceil r\rceil
denotes the ceiling of a real number
r
, that is, the smallest integer greater than or equal to
r
.
From (
17
) we then get:

(18)

\Lambda_{\rho,u}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}\,\varpi^{\lceil\langle u_{\sigma,i}-u,\nu\rangle\rceil}b_{\sigma,i},

Since
B_{\sigma}=\{b_{\sigma,1},\ldots,b_{\sigma,r}\}
is a
K
-basis for

we see that, for any
u\in M
, the
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Lambda_{\rho,u}\subset E
is of full rank, i.e. it is an
\mathcal{O}
-lattice.

Case 2:
The ray
\rho
lies in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
. Let
(\nu,0)
be a primitive vector in
\rho
. Then:

(19)

(u-u_{\sigma,i},k^{\prime})\in\rho^{\vee}\Leftrightarrow~\langle u-u_{\sigma,i},\nu\rangle\geq 0.

Noting that
K=\bigcup_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}\varpi^{k^{\prime}}
we get:

(20)

\Lambda_{\rho,u}=\bigoplus_{i:\langle u,\nu\rangle\geq\langle u_{\sigma,i},\nu\rangle}K\cdot b_{\sigma,i}.

In other words,
\Lambda_{\rho,u}
depends only on the integer
\langle u,\nu\rangle
, that is, on the class
[u]
of
u
in
M_{\rho}:=M/(\rho^{\perp}\cap M)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Let us write:

(21)

E^{\rho}_{j}=\Lambda_{\rho,u}

where
j=\langle-u,\nu\rangle
(the reason for
-u
instead of

is to make the

a decreasing filtration and hence match with the convention in
[
19
]
). We observe that for rays
\rho
that lie in
\sigma\cap(N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\})
, the equation (
20
) for the filtrations
\{E^{\rho}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the Klyachko compatibility condition. It follows, exactly as in the classification of toric vector bundles over a field (Theorem
1.2
) that
\mathcal{F}
restricted to the generic fiber
\mathfrak{X}_{\eta}
is determined, up to an equivariant isomorphim, by the decreasing
\mathbb{Z}
-filtrations
\{E^{\rho}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
\rho\in\Sigma_{0}(1)
.

Now we are ready to construct the graded piecewise linear map
\Phi
associated to the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
. Let
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
be a polyhedron with
\sigma\in\Sigma
the corresponding cone over it. In this case, we denote the basis
B_{\sigma}=\{b_{\sigma,i}\}
by

. Also, if
\nu
is a vertex in
\Delta
with corresponding ray
\rho\in\sigma(1)
, we denote the
\mathcal{O}
-module
\Lambda_{\rho,u}
by
\Lambda_{\nu,u}
.

Let
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(\Delta)
be the extended apartment in the extended building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
corresponding to the basis
B_{\Delta}
. Recall that it consists of additive norms that are adapted to the basis
B_{\Delta}
(see Section

Note that choice of a basis

gives an identification of the extended apartment

with
\mathbb{R}^{r}

\Phi_{1,\Delta}:\Delta\to\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(\Delta)

\Phi_{1,\Delta}(x)=(\langle u_{\sigma,1},x\rangle,\ldots,\langle u_{\sigma,r},x\rangle).

In terms of additive norms,

is the additive norm given by (Remark

):

(22)

\Phi_{1,\Delta}(x)\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{\Delta,i}\right)=\min\{\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+\langle u_{\sigma,i},x\rangle\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

For a vertex
\nu
of the polyhedron
\Delta
and

\Lambda_{\nu,u}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}\varpi^{\lceil\Phi_{1,\Delta}(\nu)(b_{\Delta,i})-\langle u,\nu\rangle\rceil}b_{\Delta,i}.

We also need to check that the affine map
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
contains the data of filtrations
\{E^{\rho}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where the

are horizontal rays in the cone
\sigma
(see (

) and (
21
)). We see that the filtrations

\Phi_{0,\Delta}
of

.
For any
(x,0)
in the linear span of the recession cone of
\Delta
, we can compute the value of the valuation
\Phi_{0,\Delta}(x,0)
on a vector

\displaystyle\Phi_{0,\Delta}(x,0)\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{\Delta,i}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\min\{\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+\langle u_{\Delta,i},tx\rangle\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}}{t},

\displaystyle=\min\{\langle u_{\Delta,i},x\rangle\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}.

Let
(\nu,0)
be the primitive vector for a ray
\rho
in
\sigma
, the cone over
\Delta
.
If we let

is given by:

\Phi_{0,\Delta}(-\nu,0)\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{\Delta,i}\right)=\min\{\langle u_{\Delta,i},-\nu\rangle\mid i=1,\ldots,r\},

and its corresponding

But this is the same as (

), the filtration associated to the ray

It remains to show that the
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
,
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
, glue together to give a piecewise affine map

and

in (

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\nu
be a vertex of the convex polyhedron
\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}
. Then the additive norms
\Phi_{1,\Delta}(\nu)
and
\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}(\nu)
coincide.

Proof.

Let
B_{\Delta}=\{b_{\Delta,i}\}
and
B_{\Delta^{\prime}}=\{b_{\Delta^{\prime},i}\}
be the bases corresponding to
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
respectively. We note that, by (
23
), the additive norm corresponding to
\Lambda_{\nu,u}
is:

(24)

\lceil\Phi_{1,\Delta}(\nu)(\cdot)-\langle u,\nu\rangle\rceil=\lceil\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}(\nu)(\cdot)-\langle u,\nu\rangle\rceil.

By contradiction, suppose
\Phi_{1,\Delta}(\nu)\neq\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}(\nu)
. Then for some
e\in E
,
\Phi_{1,\Delta}(\nu)(e)\neq\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}(\nu)(e)
. Let
e=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{\Delta,i}=\sum_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}b_{\Delta^{\prime},i}
. From (
23
) we see that, after a reordering of basis elements if necessary, there is an index
j
such that:

\Phi_{1,\Delta}(e)=\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{j})+\langle u_{\sigma,j},\nu\rangle\neq\operatorname{val}(\lambda^{\prime}_{j})+\langle u_{\sigma^{\prime},j},\nu\rangle=\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}(e).

On the other hand, in (
24
), if we let
u=u_{\Delta,j}
we get:

\lceil\langle u_{\Delta^{\prime},j},\nu\rangle-\langle u_{\Delta,j},\nu\rangle\rceil=\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{j})-\operatorname{val}(\lambda^{\prime}_{j}).

Also, in (
24
), if we let
u=u_{\Delta^{\prime},j}
, in the same way we obtain:

\lceil\langle u_{\Delta,j},\nu\rangle-\langle u_{\Delta^{\prime},j},\nu\rangle\rceil=\operatorname{val}(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})-\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{j}).

It follows that
\langle u_{\Delta,j},\nu\rangle-\langle u_{\Delta^{\prime},j},\nu\rangle
is an integer. Thus,
\Phi_{1,\Delta}(\nu)(e)-\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}(\nu)(e)
is an integer. This together with (
24
) implies that
\Phi_{1,\Delta}(\nu)(e)=\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}(\nu)(e)
. The contradiction proves the claim.
∎

We have shown that
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
and
\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}
coincide on the vertices of the polyhedron

. We need to show that the linear parts of
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
and
\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}

be a ray in the recession cone of the polyhedron

and
\Phi_{0,\Delta^{\prime}}(\nu,0)
on

correspond to the filtration

on

and hence coincide.

Now
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
and
\Phi_{1,\Delta^{\prime}}
are two affine maps on
\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}
that coincide on the vertices of
\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}
and also have the same linear parts on
\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}
. This implies that they coincide everywhere on
\Delta\cap\Delta^{\prime}
as required. Hence the
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
,
\Delta\in\Sigma_{1}
, glue together to give a piecewise affine map from
|\Sigma_{1}|
to the extended building
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
. Also, exactly as in
[
16
,
19
]
, we construct a piecewise linear map
\Phi_{0}:|\Sigma_{0}|\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{sph}}(E)
.

Note that the above arguments show that
\Phi_{0}
and
\Phi_{1}
are uniquely determined by the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
. This is because, as shown above, the
\Phi_{1,\Delta}
and
\Phi_{0,\rho}
(hence
\Phi_{1}
and

,

∎

Next we prove the claim about the equivalence of categories. Again, if all the vertices in the the polyhedral complex

are lattice points, then arguments are easier. In general, we need extra details involving the ceiling function.

Proof of Theorem
4.12
.

A
T
-equivariant morphism of vector bundles is the same as a
T
-equivariant morphism of the corresponding locally free sheaves of modules.
Let
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be toric vector bundles on
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
with the corresponding sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
-modules
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
respectively. Let
F:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be a
T
-equivariant morphism. Since
\mathfrak{U}_{0}=T_{\eta}
is open in
\mathfrak{X}
,
F
is determined by its restriction to
\mathfrak{U}_{0}
. On the other hand,
F
is
T
-equivariant and hence

is determined by its restriction to

. We note that

of

and any character

Lemma 4.15
.

The following are equivalent:

(a)

For all
\rho\in\Sigma
and
u\in M
,

F(\Lambda_{\rho,u})\subset\Lambda^{\prime}_{\rho,u},

where
\Lambda_{\rho,u}
is given by (
18

(b)

For all

(c)

and

\Phi_{1}(x)(e)\leq F^{*}(\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(x))(e):=\Phi_{1}^{\prime}(x)(F(e)).

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b): Define

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c):
Suppose by contradiction that there is

By the piecewise affineness of
\Phi_{1}^{\prime}

(c)

5.
Equivariant splitting of toric vector bundles on toric schemes

In this section we give a simple criterion for splitting of a toric vector bundle into a sum of toric vector bundles in terms of its associated piecewise affine map. It extends the similar criterion for splitting of toric vector bundles over a field (see
[
19
]
as well as
[

, p. 5, A little application]
).

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}

splits equivariantly
if it is equivariantly isomorphic to a direct sum of toric line bundles.

Theorem 5.2

(Criterion for equivariant splitting)
.

A toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
splits equivariantly if and only if the image of the corresponding piecewise linear map
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
lands in one extended apartment in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}(E)
. Moreover, if
\Sigma
is complete (and hence
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
is proper) this is equivalent to the image of the piecewise affine map
\Phi_{\mathcal{E},1}
to land in one extended apartment in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\textup{aff}}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}=\underset{i=1}{\overset{r}{\bigoplus}}\mathcal{L}_{i}
where the
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are toric line bundles on
\mathfrak{X}_{\Sigma}
. As before, the local equivariant triviality (Propositions
1.1
and
4.2
) applied to each toric line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{i}
implies that there exists a basis
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\}
of
E
with
b_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{i|_{x_{0}}}\subset E
, and for any
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there exists a multi-set of weights
\{u_{\sigma,i}\mid i=1,\ldots,r\}
, such that the corresponding piecewise linear map
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
is then given by (cf. (

)):

\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}(x,m)\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\right)=\min\{m\operatorname{val}(\lambda_{i})+\langle u_{\sigma,i},x\rangle\mid i=1,\ldots,r\},

for any
(x,m)\in\sigma\cap(N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{m\})
. This shows that all the level
m

\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}(x,m)
, are adapted to the basis
B
and hence the image of
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
lands in the extended apartment
\widetilde{A}(B)
.

Conversely, if there is a basis
B=\{b_{i}\}
such that all the additive norms
\Phi_{\mathfrak{B}}(x)
are adapted to
B
then, as above, one sees that
\mathcal{E}=\underset{i=1}{\overset{r}{\bigoplus}}\mathcal{L}_{i}
where
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is the toric line bundle whose sheaf of sections
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is given as follows: for any
\sigma\in\Sigma
,

\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathfrak{U}_{\sigma})=\bigoplus_{(u^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap\widetilde{M}}\mathcal{O}\cdot\chi^{u^{\prime}}\varpi^{k^{\prime}}\,\chi^{u_{\sigma,i}}b_{i}.

To prove the last part, we only need to show that if the image of
\Phi_{\mathcal{E},1}
lands in an (extended) apartment
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{aff}}(B)=\widetilde{A}_{1}(B)
then the image of
\Phi_{\mathcal{E},0}
lands in the (extended) apartment
\widetilde{A}_{\textup{sph}}(B)=\widetilde{A}_{0}(B)
. But this follows from the continuity of
\Phi_{\mathcal{E}}
and the fact that the intersection of the closure of
\sqcup_{m>0}\widetilde{A}_{m}(B)
with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}(E)
is
\widetilde{A}_{0}(B)
.
∎

4.16
the image of
\Phi_{1}
lands in an apartment, that is, an infinite two-sided path, while the image of
\Phi_{2}
does not.
It follows from Theorem
5.2
that the toric vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi_{1}}
, associated to the piecewise affine map
\Phi_{1}

\mathcal{E}_{\Phi_{2}}
, associated to
\Phi_{2}

splits equivariantly (see
[
19

as well as
[
16

\mathcal{E}_{\Phi_{2}}
shows that this is not the case over a discrete valuation ring.

Example 5.4

(A Helly’s theorem for the tree of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Q}_{p})
)
.

We observe that a version of Kyachko’s Helly’s theorem (
[
19
, Theorem 6.1.2]
) holds for the infinite
(p+1)
-regular tree
T_{p}
: Let
A
be a finite collection of vertices in the tree
T_{p}
such that any
3
vertices in
A
lie on the same path. Then all of the vertices in
A
lie on the same path. One can give a direct combinatorial proof of the above fact as follows: By induction on
|A|
, it suffices to show if any
k-1
vertices in
A
lie on a path then any
k
vertices also lie on a path. Take vertices
v_{i}\in A
,
i=1,\ldots,k
. Let
P
be a path containing the vertices
v_{i}\in A
,
i=1,\ldots k-1
. We can assume that
v_{1}
and
v_{k-1}
are the first and last vertices in
A
that appear in
P
. We recall that, in a tree, there is a unique path joining any two given vertices. Now by assumption,
v_{1},v_{k-1},v_{k}
lie on the same path
Q
. The uniqueness of path joining
v_{1}
and
v_{k-1}
then implies that all the
v_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
, lie on
Q
as well. This proves the claim.

Abstract
. The shot-down process is a strong Markov process which is annihilated, or shot down, when jumping over or to the complement of a given open subset of a vector space. Due to specific features of the shot-down time, such processes suggest new type of boundary conditions for nonlocal differential equations. In this work we construct the shot-down process for the fractional Laplacian in Euclidean space. For smooth bounded sets D D , we study its transition density and characterize Dirichlet form. We show that the corresponding Green function is comparable to that of the fractional Laplacian with Dirichlet conditions on D D . However, for nonconvex D D , the transition density of the shot-down stable process is incomparable with the Dirichlet heat kernel of the fractional Laplacian for D D .

Introduction

Let
D
be a nonempty open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The study of diffusions in
D

naturally leads to the question what happens when the process reaches the boundary,
\partial D
. There are various options and nomenclatures, including
killing
,
absorption
,
censoring
,
resetting
,
resurrection
,
reflection
,
diffusion along the boundary
, etc., which lead to diversified boundary value problems. The situation is similar for general Markov (jump) processes
X=(X_{t},t\geq 0)
. Analysis of these problems has generated a lot of research in partial differential equations, potential theory and stochastic analysis, see, e.g.,
[
31
]
.
The simplest option is to
kill
the Markov process at the first exit time of
D
,

\tau_{D}:=\inf{}\{t>0:X_{t}\in D^{c}\},

where
D^{c}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D
. The
killed process
on
D
is then defined by

X^{D}(t):=\begin{cases}X_{t}&\mbox{for }t\in[0,\tau_{D}),\\
\partial&\mbox{for }t\in[\tau_{D},\infty).\end{cases}

Here
\partial
is an arbitrary isolated point attached to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, called
cemetery
and we extend functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
D
by letting
f(\partial)=0
.

The aim of this work is to introduce and to study a new variant of killing.
To this end we define the
shot-down time
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{D}=\inf{}\{t>0:[X_{t^{-}},X_{t}]\cap D^{c}\neq\emptyset\}\,.

Here, as usual,
X_{t^{-}}=\lim_{s\to t^{-}}X_{s}
,
X_{0-}=X_{0}
, and
[v,w]
is the line segment between
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Trivially,
\sigma_{B}\leq\sigma_{D}
if
B\subset D
,
\sigma_{D}\leq\tau_{D}
,
and
\sigma_{D}=\tau_{D}
if
D
is convex. Further, if
D^{\prime}
is a connected component of
D
containing
X_{0}
, then
\sigma_{D}=\sigma_{D^{\prime}}
, therefore below we sometimes assume that
D
is a
domain
, i.e., a nonempty connected open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given
x\in D
, we define
D_{x}=\{y\in D:[x,y]\subset D\}
.
Only jumps from
x\in D
to
D_{x}
are possible without shooting-down.

Figure 1.
D

is an annulus in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The process
X
is shot down when
[X_{t^{-}},X_{t}]
intersects the complement of
D
.

Analogous to the killed process
X^{D}
, the
shot-down process

\hat{X}^{D}
is defined by

\hat{X}^{D}(t):=\begin{cases}X_{t}&\mbox{for }t\in[0,\sigma_{D}),\\
\partial&\mbox{for }t\in[\sigma_{D},\infty).\end{cases}

In particular,
\hat{X}^{D}(t)
can neither visit
D^{c}
nor
D\setminus D_{X_{t^{-}}}
, for
t\geq 0
.

So far we have not specified to which jump processes
X
we apply the shooting-down procedure. In principle the construction is very general, but in this work we focus on the isotropic
\alpha
-stable Lévy process

[
29
]
.
The process is associated with the fractional Laplacian
\Delta^{\alpha/2}:=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
as generator, see, e.g.,
[
28
]
. The resulting shot-down process will sometimes be called
shot-down stable
. For many results, we also restrict ourselves to bounded smooth (
C^{1,1}
) sets
D
,
see Definition
1.1
.
We leave largely open
the case of less regular open sets, even Lipschitz, and more general
Markov processes killed at the shot-down time.

The paper is organized as follows. In the Section
1
we present necessary definitions and elementary facts concerning the shot-down process.
In Section
2
we define the heat kernel of the process
and discuss the relationship between the heat kernels of the killed process and the shot-down process.
In Section
3
we compare the killing measures of the two processes.
In Section
4
we compute the corresponding Dirichlet form. Sharp estimates of the Green function of the shot-down process are provided in Section
5
, which ends the paper.

Let us describe the results in more detail. One important aim of our work is to study the heat kernel
\hat{p}_{D}
of the shot-down process. To this end, in Theorem
1.2
we first assert that
\sigma_{D}
is a stopping time for general open sets
D
. In Theorem
2.11
we show that
\hat{p}_{D}
is symmetric in the space variables.
In Theorem
2.21
we prove for bounded smooth
D
that
\hat{p}_{D}
is comparable to the heat kernel
p_{D}
of the killed process if and only if
D
is convex.

Then we study the quadratic form of the shot-down process for bounded
C^{1,1}
domains
D
. For this, we first estimate the intensity of shooting down. Recall that, given
x\in D
,

D_{x}
consists of all the points where the process can possibly jump from
x
without being shot down. Denote

(1)

\displaystyle\nu(z)=\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha}|z|^{-d-\alpha}\,,\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

where
\mathcal{A}_{d,\alpha}=2^{\alpha}\Gamma((d+\alpha)/2)\pi^{-d/2}/|\Gamma(-\alpha/2)|
.
Since
\nu(y-x)
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is the jump intensity of the process
X
from
x
to
y
, the shooting-down intensity for
D
is defined by

\iota_{D}(x):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D_{x}}\nu(y-x)\,dy,\kern 5.0ptx\in D.

In Theorem
3.3
we prove a bound for the difference between the shooting-down intensity and the standard killing intensity for
D
,

\kappa_{D}(x):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D}\nu(y-x)\,dy,\kern 5.0ptx\in D.

The result is used to establish a representation of the corresponding Dirichlet form in Theorem
4.2
and to give a precise description of the domain of the form.

We then move on to the Green function. Here the shot-down stable process behaves like the killed stable process and the two respective Green functions are comparable for every bounded smooth domain
D
, which is proved in Theorem
5.10
. As mentioned before, such comparability in general fails for the corresponding heat kernels (Theorem
2.21
); yet both results build on the study of
\hat{p}_{D}
in Section
2
.
Our proofs use potential-theoretic tools typical of jump-type Markov processes, such as the Ikeda-Watanabe formula, but also employ a chaining method more common in the analysis of diffusions. In fact, certain aspects of the shot-down process—its dependence on connectedness and geometry, for instance—resemble diffusions more than jump processes. The analysis of the corresponding harmonic functions is also subtle; we make preliminary steps in this direction.

As a motivation for our study, we mention that there is considerable interest in applied sciences on modeling physical motion by
Lévy flights
. For some microscopic and macroscopic objects, e.g., bacteria or sharks, it is observed that they migrate by trajectories that have straight stretches. The notion of shot-down processes should be relevant for such studies. To our best knowledge, the mathematical model of shot-down processes is new, but a related concept of
visibility constrained jump process
was introduced in
[
26
]
. As in our work, the visibility constrained jump process can jump from
x\in D
only to
D_{x}\subset D
. However, the emphasis of
[
26
]
is on functional inequalities rather than stochastic processes and the process is
censored
rather then killed, should the line segment
[X_{t^{-}},X_{t}]
intersect the complement of
D
. Namely, the process in
[
26
]
is
restarted
at
X_{t^{-}}
, so the
censoring
is meant in the sense of
[
9
]
.

Concluding the Introduction, we like to mention various approaches in literature to define
reflection
from
D^{c}
for jump processes and nonlocal operators, because reflection also uses the process
(X_{t^{-}},X_{t})
; see

[
3
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
32
]
and
[
13
, Introduction]
.

1.
Preliminaries

In what follows
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the Euclidean space of dimension
d\geq 1
,
x\cdot y
is the Euclidean scalar product of
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
|y|
is the length of
y
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
\text{dist}(x,A)=\inf\{|x-y|:y\in A\}
and
\text{dist}(A,B)=\inf\{|y-z|:y\in A,z\in B\}
. We define
A^{c}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus A
and,
for
r>0
,
B_{r}(x)=B(x,r)=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|z-x|<r\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

An open set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
C^{1,1}
at scale
r>0
if for every
Q\in\partial D
there are balls
I=B(x^{\prime},r)\subset D
and
O=B(x^{\prime\prime},r)\subset D^{c}
tangent at
Q
. We call
I
and
O

the
inner
and
outer
ball, respectively and we call
r
localization radius.

A bounded open set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
C^{1,1}
if and only if its boundary can be represented locally as the graph of a function whose gradient is Lipschitz continuous
[
1
, Def. 1.1 and Lemma 2.2]
. Similarly, open set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is Lipschitz if its boundary is locally equal to the graph of a Lipschitz function
.

All our functions, measures and sets are Borel either by construction or assumptions.
As usual,
dy
,
dx
etc. stand for the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the considered integrals are assumed to be well defined, to wit, nonnegative or absolutely convergent.

Let
0<\alpha<2
and let
X=(X_{t},t\geq 0)
be the standard isotropic
\alpha
-stable Lévy process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
29
,
28
]
. In particular,
X
is right continuous with left limits.
The process is determined by the jump (Lévy) measure with the density function (
1
).
The constant in (
1
) is so chosen that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[1-\cos(\xi\cdot z)]\nu(z)\,dz=|\xi|^{\alpha},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For every
t>0
, we consider the continuous probability density
p_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,\infty)
with

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x)e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,dx=e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Namely, we let

p_{t}(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}e^{-t|\xi|^{\alpha}}\,d\xi,\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The function has the
scaling
property:

p_{t}(x)=t^{-d/\alpha}p_{1}(t^{-1/\alpha}x),\quad\text{ or }\quad p_{r^{\alpha}t}(rx)=r^{-d}p_{t}(x),\quad t>0,\ r>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Furthermore,
p_{t}
form a convolution semigroup of probability densities.
It is well known that for
\alpha=1
,

p_{t}(x)=\frac{C_{d}t}{(t^{2}+|x|^{2})^{(d+1)/2}},\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
C_{d}=\Gamma((d+1)/2)/\pi^{(d+1)/2}
, and, for any
\alpha\in(0,2)
,

(2)

p_{t}(x)\approx t^{-d/\alpha}\land\frac{t}{|x|^{d+\alpha}},\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here
\land
stands for the minimum and (
2
) means that there is a number
c\in(0,\infty)
(i.e., a
constant
, depending on
d
and
\alpha
) such that
c^{-1}p_{t}(x)\leq t^{-d/\alpha}\land\frac{t}{|x|^{d+\alpha}}\leq cp_{t}(x)
. This notation for
comparability
is used throughout the paper.
We denote

p(t,x,y)=p_{t}(x,y)=p_{t}(y-x).

The process
X_{t}
is Markovian
with the time-homogeneous transition probability

(t,x,A)\mapsto\int\limits_{A}p(t,x,y)\,dy,\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We let
\mathbb{P}_{x}
and
\mathbb{E}_{x}
be the law and expectation for the process starting at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
We consider the operator semigroup
\{P_{t},t\geq 0\}
on bounded or nonnegative function
f
.
Thus, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}f(X_{t})
and

P_{t}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy,\quad t>0,

see
[
30
, Section 2]
for a direct construction of the semigroup.
We will be interested in semigroups obtained by
killing

X
at suitable stopping times, chiefly at the shot-down time for open sets
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Recall that
X
is defined on the measurable space
\Omega
of càdlàg paths:
[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
29
]
, so that
X_{t}=X_{t}(\omega)
for
t>0,\omega\in\Omega
. As usual, we suppress
\omega
from the notation.

In the Introduction we defined
\tau_{D}
, the first exit time from
D
,
X^{D}
, the process killed upon exiting
D
,
\sigma_{D}
, the shot-down time, and
\hat{X}^{D}
, the shot-down process.
We now introduce the corresponding operator semigroups,

and

\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[t<\sigma_{D};f(X_{t})],\quad t\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here, as usual,

is nonnegative or absolutely integrable and
\mathbb{E}[A;F]=\int_{A}Fd\mathbb{P}
.

1.1.
Progressive measurability

The first time the process
X
flies over the complement of
D
, that is the shot-down time, is indeed a stopping time. The proof depends on the debut theorem for progressively measurable processes.

Theorem 1.2
.

\sigma_{D}=\inf{}\{t{>}0:[X_{t^{-}},X_{t}]\cap D^{c}\neq\emptyset\}
is a stopping time.

Proof.

Of course,
X
is
c\grave{a}dl\grave{a}g
and
X_{t^{-}}
is
c\grave{a}gl\grave{a}d
. By
[
18
, Theorem 1, p. 38]
, each process is progressively measurable, and so is
the pair
(X_{t},X_{t^{-}})
.
We easily see that
F(D):=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:[x,y]\cap D^{c}\neq\emptyset\}

is closed, so Borel. Of course, the first hitting of
F(D)
by
(X_{t},X_{t^{-}})
is
\sigma_{D}
, hence
\sigma_{D}
is a stopping time by
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
; see also
[
5
]
and
[
12
, Subsection 1.1]
.
∎

1.2.
Ikeda-Watanabe formula

For the rest of the paper, we return to the setting of the fractional Laplacian in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open (we will make additional assumptions on

In this section we prove the Ikeda-Watanabe-type formula (
4
) for the shot-down stable processes. To this end we use the so-called Lévy system for

and follow the presentation for the killed process in
[
14

Note that
{\hat{P}^{D}}_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)\leq P^{D}_{t}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)
for all
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By the Radon-Nikodym theorem, for all

and functions

{\hat{P}^{D}}_{t}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy,

where
\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)
is defined for almost all

\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)\leq p_{D}(t,x,y)\leq p_{t}(x,y)
. Here

is the Dirichlet heat kernel of
D
for
\Delta^{\alpha/2}
, discussed in Section
2
.
We may call
\hat{p}_{D}
the heat kernel of the shot-down process, because

(3)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\sigma_{D}}f(t,X_{t})\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)f(t,y)\,dy\,dt.

Theorem 1.3
.

The joint distribution of
(\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}^{-}},X_{\sigma_{D}})
restricted to
\{X_{\sigma_{D}^{-}}\in D\}
and calculated under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
for
x\in D
, satisfies for
A\subset D,B\subset(\overline{D})^{c},I\subset[0,\infty)

(4)

\mathbb{P}_{x}[\sigma_{D}\in I,X_{\sigma_{D}^{-}}\in A,X_{\sigma_{D}}\in B]=\int\limits_{I}\int\limits_{A}\int\limits_{B}\,\nu(w-y)\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)\,dw\,dy\,dt.

Proof.

In view of the monotone convergence theorem, we may assume that
I
and
B
are compact.
Let
F(u,y,w)=\mathbbm{1}_{I}(u)\mathbbm{1}_{A}(y)\mathbbm{1}_{B}(w)
and

\displaystyle M(t)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u\leq t\\
|\Delta X_{u}|\neq 0\end{subarray}}F(u,X_{u^{-}},X_{u})-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u,X_{u},X_{u}+z)\nu(z)\,dz\,du,\textrm{ }0\leq t<\infty.

By the Lévy system, see, e.g.,
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u\leq\infty\\
|\Delta X_{u}|\neq 0\end{subarray}}F(u,X_{u^{-}},X_{u})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u,X_{u},X_{u}+z)\nu(z)\,dz\,du.

Thus,
\mathbb{E}_{x}M(t)=0
. In fact,

is a martingale. Indeed, let

. By considering the Lévy process
u\mapsto X_{s+u}-X_{s}
, independent of
\{X_{r}
,

, we calculate the conditional expectation:

\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<u\leq t\\
|\Delta X_{u}|\neq 0\end{subarray}}F(u,X_{u^{-}},X_{u})-\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u,X_{u},X_{u}+z)\nu(z)\,dz\,du\ \bigg|\ X_{r},0\leq r\leq s\bigg]=0.

Since

\displaystyle|M(t)|\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u<\infty\\
|\Delta X_{u}|\neq 0\end{subarray}}F(u,X_{u^{-}},X_{u})+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u,X_{u},X_{u}+z)\nu(z)\,dz\,du,

and the right hand side has expectation not bigger than

is a uniformly integrable

,
we obtain

\mathbb{E}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u<\sigma_{D}\\
|\Delta X_{u}|\neq 0\end{subarray}}F(u,X_{u^{-}},X_{u})=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\sigma_{D}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(u,X_{u},X_{u}+z)\nu(z)\,dz\,du.

By (
3

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{x}[\sigma_{D}\in I,\;X_{\sigma_{D}^{-}}\in A,\;X_{\sigma_{D}}\in B]=\int_{I}\int_{B-y}\int_{A}\hat{p}_{D}(u,x,y)\nu(z)\,dy\,dz\,du.\qquad\qed

If open set

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}=t)=0
. Indeed,
\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in\partial D)=0
and by
[
29
, (1.10)]

\mathbb{P}_{x}-a.s.

X
is continuous at
t
, which implies that
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}\neq t)=1
.
Therefore,

(8)

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\sigma_{D};X_{t}\in A)&=\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in A)-\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}\leq t;X_{t}\in A)\\
&=\int\limits_{A}p(t,x,y)\,dy-\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}<t;X_{t}\in A).\end{split}

Let
0<u<t
,
n\geq 1
and
1\leq k\leq 2^{n}
. We define stopping times

S_{n}=\begin{cases}ku2^{-n},&\mbox{if }(k-1)u2^{-n}\leq\sigma_{D}<ku2^{-n},\\
\infty,&\mbox{if }\sigma_{D}\geq u.\end{cases}

By the Markov property
[
18
]
,

(9)

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}<u;X_{t}\in A)&=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\mathbb{P}_{x}\bigg(\frac{(k-1)u}{2^{n}}\leq\sigma_{D}<\frac{ku}{2^{n}};X_{t}\in A\bigg)\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\frac{(k-1)u}{2^{n}}\leq\sigma_{D}<\frac{ku}{2^{n}},\mathbb{P}_{X_{ku2^{-n}}}(X_{t-ku2^{-n}}\in A)\bigg]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\sigma_{D}<u;\int\limits_{A}p(t-S_{n},X_{S_{n}},y)\,dy\bigg].\end{split}

If
\sigma_{D}<u
, then
t-S_{n}\geq t-u>0
and we get
S_{n}\downarrow\sigma_{D}
. We let
n\to\infty
. Then we let
u\uparrow t
in (
9
). By the right continuity of the process, joint continuity of
p(t,x,y)
, dominated convergence, Fubini-Tonelli and monotone convergence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}<t;X_{t}\in A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{u\uparrow t}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}<u;X_{t}\in A)

\displaystyle=\lim_{u\uparrow t}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<u;\int_{A}p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\,dy\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t;\int_{A}p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\,dy\right]

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{A}\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\sigma_{D}<t;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\bigg]\,dy.

By (
8
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(t<\sigma_{D};X_{t}\in A)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{A}p(t,x,y)\,dy-\int\limits_{A}\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\sigma_{D}<t;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\bigg]\,dy

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{A}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)\,dy.\qed

The heat kernel of the killed process enjoys the scaling:
r^{\alpha}p_{rD}(r^{\alpha}t,rx,ry)=p_{D}(t,x,y)
. Here
r>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
rD=\{rx:x\in D\}
. We will prove a similar equality for
\hat{p}_{D}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The law of
\sigma_{rD}
under
\mathbb{P}_{rx}
equals that of
r^{\alpha}\sigma_{D}
under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle\sigma_{rD}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{t>0:\left[X_{t^{-}},X_{t}\right]\cap(rD)^{c}\neq\emptyset\right\},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{t>0:\left[X_{t^{-}}/r,X_{t}/r\right]\cap D^{c}\neq\emptyset\right\}.

The distribution of
X_{t}/r
when
X
starts at
rx
is the same as the distribution of
X\left(t/r^{\alpha}\right)
when
X
starts at
x
. In the same sense

X_{t^{-}}/r
and
X\left(t/r^{\alpha}-\right)
equal in distribution,
and the last infimum above equals in distribution to

\inf\left\{t>0:\left[X\left(t/r^{\alpha}-\right),X\left(t/r^{\alpha}\right)\right]\cap D^{c}\neq\emptyset\right\}=r^{\alpha}\sigma_{D}.\qed

Lemma 2.4
.

r^{d}\hat{p}_{rD}(r^{\alpha}t,rx,ry)=\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)
for
r>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

By the Hunt formula in Definition
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle r^{d}\hat{p}_{rD}(r^{\alpha}t,rx,ry)

\displaystyle=r^{d}{p}(r^{\alpha}t,rx,ry)-r^{d}\mathbb{E}_{rx}[\sigma_{rD}<r^{\alpha}t,p(r^{\alpha}t-\sigma_{rD},X_{\sigma_{rD}},ry)]

\displaystyle={p}(t,x,y)-r^{d}\mathbb{E}_{x}[r^{\alpha}\sigma_{D}<r^{\alpha}t;p(r^{\alpha}(t-\sigma_{D}),X_{r^{\alpha}\sigma_{D}},ry)]

\displaystyle={p}(t,x,y)-\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{D}<t;r^{d}p(r^{\alpha}(t-\sigma_{D}),rX_{\sigma_{D}},ry)]

\displaystyle={p}(t,x,y)-\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{D}<t;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)]=\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y).\qquad\qed

2.2.
Perturbation formula

We note the following extension of Hunt’s formula.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
U
be open,
U\subset D
. Then, for
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\hat{p}_{U}(t,x,y)=\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)-\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{U}<t;[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\hat{p}_{D}(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)].

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle p(t,x,y)-\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{D}<t;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)]

and

\displaystyle p(t,x,y)-\hat{p}_{U}(t,x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{U}<t;p(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)].

Subtracting we get

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)-\hat{p}_{U}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t;p(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t,\sigma_{U}<\sigma_{D};p(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t,\sigma_{U}<\sigma_{D};p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;p(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\right],

where the next to last equality follows since
\sigma_{U}\leq\sigma_{D}
and both integrands are equal on the set
\sigma_{U}=\sigma_{D}
. Denote the first expectation by
I_{1}
and the second by
I_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{U}}
be the usual
\sigma
-field of pre-
\sigma_{U}
events and
\theta_{\sigma_{U}}
be the usual shift, e.g.,
\theta_{\sigma_{U}}X_{t}=X_{t+\sigma_{U}}
. By the strong Markov property, since
\sigma_{D}=\sigma_{U}+\sigma_{D}\circ\theta_{\sigma_{U}}
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t,\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\sigma_{D}<t}\ p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)|\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{U}}\right]\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\sigma_{U}+\sigma_{D}\circ\theta_{\sigma_{U}}<t}\ p(t-\sigma_{U}-\sigma_{D}\circ\theta_{\sigma_{U}},X_{\sigma_{D}}\circ\theta_{\sigma_{U}},y)|\mathcal{F}_{\sigma_{U}}\right]\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\mathbb{E}_{X_{s}}\left[\sigma_{D}<t-s;p(t-s-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\right]\mid_{s=\sigma_{U}}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;p(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)-\hat{p}_{D}(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\right],\end{split}

so

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}-I_{2}=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\hat{p}_{D}(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)].\qquad\qed\end{split}

The Hunt formula defines the kernel of the killed process and the kernel of the shot-down process in terms of
p
. Here is a direct relationship between
p_{D}
and
\hat{p}_{D}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following equality holds

\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=p_{D}(t,x,y)-\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t,X_{\sigma_{D}}\in D\setminus D_{X_{\sigma_{D}-}};p_{D}(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\right].

Proof.

Combining definitions of
p_{D}
and
\hat{p}_{D}
for
t>0,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we get

p_{D}(t,x,y)-\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{D}<t;p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)]-\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau_{D}<t;p(t-\tau_{D},X_{\tau_{D}},y)].

We have
\sigma_{D}\leq\tau_{D}
so the last expression equals

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{D}<t,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};p(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)]-\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau_{D}<t,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};p(t-\tau_{D},X_{\tau_{D}},y)].

We write
r^{D}(t,x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau_{D}<t;p(t-\tau_{D},X_{\tau_{D}},y)]
to simplify the notation. Making use of the main part of the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 2.3]
, we will show below that

(10)

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\tau_{D}<t,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};p(t-\tau_{D},X_{\tau_{D}},y)]=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\sigma_{D}<t,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};r^{D}(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)].

Indeed, the strong Markov property yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{x}&[\tau_{D}<t,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};r^{D}(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\sigma_{D}<t,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};\mathbb{E}_{X_{\sigma_{D}}}[\tau_{D}<t-s;p(t-s-\tau_{D},X_{\tau_{D}},y)]\big|_{s=\sigma_{D}}\bigg]\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\sigma_{D}<t,\,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D},\tau_{D}\circ\Theta_{\sigma_{D}}+\sigma_{D}<t;p(t-\tau_{D}\circ\Theta_{\sigma_{D}}-\sigma_{D},X_{\tau_{D}}\circ\Theta_{\sigma_{D}},y)\bigg].\end{split}

On the set
\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D}
we have
\tau_{D}\circ\Theta_{\sigma_{D}}+\sigma_{D}=\tau_{D}
and
X_{\tau_{D}}\circ\Theta_{\sigma_{D}}=X_{\tau_{D}}
, so simplifying the last expression we get

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t,\,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};p(t-\tau_{D},X_{\tau_{D}},y)\right],

which ends the proof of (
10
). From (
10
) we obtain

\displaystyle p_{D}(t,x,y)-\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{D}<t,\,\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D};p_{D}(t-\sigma_{D},X_{\sigma_{D}},y)\right].\qquad\qed

Note that the condition
\sigma_{D}<\tau_{D}
can be written as
X_{\sigma_{D}}\in D\setminus D_{X_{\sigma_{D}-}}
.
The next (Duhamel) formula shows
\hat{p}_{D}
as a
nonlocal perturbation
of
p_{D}
, in the sense of
[
15
]
.

Corollary 2.7
.

For Lipschitz
D
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
, the following equality holds

\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=p_{D}(t,x,y)-\int\limits_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{D}\int\limits_{D\setminus D_{w}}\!\!\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\nu(z-w)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,dz\,dw\,ds.

Proof.

Remark

gives the joint probability distribution of
\sigma_{D}
,
X_{\sigma_{D}-}
and
X_{\sigma_{D}}
. Substituting this in Lemma
2.6
, we obtain the statement.
∎

2.3.
The Chapman-Kolmogorov equation

In this section we prove the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation for
\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)
.
Let
\hat{\nu}(x,y):=\nu(y-x)\mathbbm{1}_{D_{x}^{c}}(y)
, where

D_{x}=\{y\in D:[x,y]\subset D\}
. Of course,
\hat{\nu}(x,y)=\hat{\nu}(y,x)
.
We start with a regularity result.

Lemma 2.8
.

The function
y\mapsto\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)
is continuous on
D
for all
t>0
,
x\in D
.

Proof.

Fix
t>0,\,x\in D
. Corollary
2.7
states:

\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=p_{D}(t,x,y)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D}\ \hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,dz\,dw\,ds.

Since
p_{D}(t,x,\cdot)
is continuous, it is enough to prove the continuity of the function

y\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D}\ \hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,dz\,dw\,ds.

To this end, take
D\ni y_{n}\to y\in D
. Let
\delta=\inf_{n}\text{dist}(y_{n},D^{c})>0,\text{ and }A=\{z\in D:\text{dist}(z,D^{c})<\delta/2\}
.

If
z\in A
, then
|z-y_{n}|\geq\delta/2
for every
n
, so

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{A}(z)\leq C2^{d+\alpha}t\delta^{-d-\alpha}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)\mathbbm{1}_{A}(z).

This majorant is integrable by the Ikeda-Watanabe formula:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{A}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)\,dz\,dw\,ds=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\sigma_{D}<t,X_{\sigma_{D}}\in A)\leq 1<\infty,

so our integrand converges by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem for
n\to\infty.

Now, assume
z\in D\setminus A
. We have
B(z,\delta/2)\subset D_{z}
, thus

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{D\setminus A}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}_{{d,\alpha}}2^{d+\alpha}\delta^{-d-\alpha}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)p_{D}(t-s,z,y_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{D\setminus A}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{A}_{{d,\alpha}}2^{d+\alpha}\delta^{-d-\alpha}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\left((t-s)^{-d/\alpha}\land\frac{t-s}{|z-y_{n}|^{d+\alpha}}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{D\setminus A}(z).

Let
\lambda=\sup_{n}|y-y_{n}|
. Then
\inf_{n}|z-y_{n}|\geq|z-y|-\lambda,
so

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y_{n})\mathbbm{1}_{D\setminus A}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\mathcal{A}_{{d,\alpha}}2^{d+\alpha}}{\delta^{d+\alpha}}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\left((t-s)^{-d/\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{B(y,2\lambda)}(z)+\frac{2^{d+\alpha}(t-s)}{|z-y|^{d+\alpha}}\mathbbm{1}_{B(y,2\lambda)^{c}}(z)\right)\mathbbm{1}_{D\setminus A}(z).

This function is integrable with respect to
dw\,dz
. By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus A}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y_{n})\,dz\,dw

\displaystyle\rightarrow\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus A}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,dz\,dw.

Further, by the symmetry of
p_{D}
, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus A}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y_{n})\,dz\,dw

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}_{{d,\alpha}}2^{d+\alpha}\delta^{-d-\alpha}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus A}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)p_{D}(t-s,y_{n},z)\,dz\,dw\leq\mathcal{A}_{{d,\alpha}}2^{d+\alpha}\delta^{-d-\alpha},

and, applying the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus A}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y_{n})\,dz\,dw\,ds

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus A}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\hat{\nu}(w,z)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,dz\,dw\,ds,

as
n\to\infty
. Thus,
D\ni y\mapsto p_{D}(t,x,\cdot)
is continuous.
∎

The proof of the following theorem is based on the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Theorem 2.9

(Chapman-Kolmogorov equation)
.

For all
t>s>0,x,y\in D
,

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=\int_{D}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,z)\hat{p}_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,dz.

Proof.

Fix
t>s>0,x\in D
. Let
A\subset D
. By the Markov property,

\displaystyle\int_{A}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)\,dy=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(t<\sigma_{D},X_{t}\in A\right)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[s<\sigma_{D};\mathbb{P}_{X_{s}}\left(t-s<\sigma_{D},X_{t-s}\in A\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[s<\sigma_{D};\int_{A}\hat{p}_{D}(t-s,X_{t-s},y)\,dy\right]=\int_{A}\int_{D}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,z)\hat{p}_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,dz\,dy.

Thus
\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=\int_{D}dz\ \hat{p}_{D}(s,x,z)\hat{p}_{D}(t-s,z,y)
for almost every
y\in D
. The identity actually holds for all
y\in D
. Indeed, the function on the left-hand side is continuous in
y
. Since
\hat{p}_{D}(t-s,z,y)\leq C(t-s)^{-d/\alpha}
, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem the right-hand side is also continuous. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)>0
for
x,y\in D
,
t>0
.

Proof.

There is
n\geq 2
and a sequence of balls
\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
in
D
such that
x_{1}=x
,
x_{n}=y
and
B(x_{i},r_{i})\cap B(x_{i+1},r_{i+1})\neq\emptyset
for
i=1\dots n
.
By the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation and domain monotonicity of the heat kernel

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\cdots\int_{D}\hat{p}_{D}(t/n,x,z_{1})\cdots\hat{p}_{D}(t/n,z_{n-1},y)\,dz_{1}\cdots\,dz_{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{1}}\cdots\int_{B_{n-1}}\hat{p}_{B_{1}}(t/n,x,z_{1})\cdots\hat{p}_{B_{n}}(t/n,z_{n-1},y)\,dz_{1}\cdots\,dz_{n-1}.

Since the balls are convex sets, we have
\hat{p}_{B_{i}}=p_{B_{i}}
for
i=1\dots n
, because
\sigma_{B_{i}}=\tau_{B_{i}}
; see the Introduction, Definition
2.1
, and (
6
). Therefore, the right-hand side equals

\int_{B_{1}}\cdots\int_{B_{n-1}}p_{B_{1}}(t/n,x,z_{1})\cdots p_{B_{n}}(t/n,z_{n-1},y)\,dz_{1}\cdots\,dz_{n-1},

which is bigger than
0
by the strict positivity of the heat kernel of killed process; see
[
16
]
or
[
11
]
.
∎

2.4.
Symmetry

The goal of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 2.11
.

For all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t>0
, we have
\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)=\hat{p}_{D}(t,y,x)
.

The proof is given at the end of the section, after several auxiliary results. Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t>0
. We will construct, in Lemma
2.14
, the so-called bridge between
x
and
y
in time
t
. We begin by defining the finite-dimensional distributions of the bridge.

Definition 2.12
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N},\ s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in(0,t),\ s_{1}<\dots<s_{n}
, we define measure
\pi_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
by

\pi_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}(A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n})=\int_{A_{n}}\dots\int_{A_{1}}\frac{p(s_{1},x,z_{1})p(s_{2}-s_{1},z_{1},z_{2})\dots p(t-s_{n},z_{n},y)}{p(t,x,y)}dz_{1}\dots dz_{n}

for
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We also define

\pi_{0,s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}(A_{0}\times A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n})=\delta_{x}(A_{0})\pi_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}(A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n}),

\pi_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n},t}(A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n}\times A_{n+1})=\pi_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}(A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n})\delta_{y}(A_{n+1}),

\pi_{0,s_{1},\dots,s_{n},t}(A_{0}\times A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n}\times A_{n+1})=\delta_{x}(A_{0})\pi_{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}}(A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n})\delta_{y}(A_{n+1}).

As usual, we extend the definition to
0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{n}\leq t
by skipping the repeated
s_{i}
’s, e.g.,

\pi_{s,s}(A_{1}\times A_{2})=\pi_{s}(A_{1}\cap A_{2}).

Lemma 2.13
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
s\in(0,t)
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\pi_{s}(z:|z-y|\geq\varepsilon)\leq\frac{C(t-s)}{p(t,x,y)\varepsilon^{d+\alpha}},

and for
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<t,\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\pi_{s_{1},s_{2}}((z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:|z_{1}-z_{2}|\geq\varepsilon)\leq\frac{C(s_{2}-s_{1})}{p(t,x,y)\varepsilon^{d+\alpha}}.

Proof.

By (
2
),

p(s,z,w)\approx s^{-d/\alpha}\wedge\frac{s}{|z-w|^{d+\alpha}}.

To prove the first inequality we
write

\displaystyle\pi_{s}(z:|z-y|\geq\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(y)^{c}}\frac{p(s,x,z)p(t-s,z,y)}{p(t,x,y)}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(t-s)}{p(t,x,y)\varepsilon^{d+\alpha}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(y)^{c}}p(s,x,z)\,dz

For the remainder of the paper, we assume that

Recall that

.
If

Here is an intuitive explanation: The

and (small) time

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\tilde{y}|<r\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{|\tilde{y}|^{2}}\left(\frac{|\tilde{y}|^{2}}{2}+y_{d}+x_{d}\right)^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}dy_{d}\,d\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=c\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}}\left(\rho^{2}+y_{d}^{2}+x_{d}^{2}\right)^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}dy_{d}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{d-2}\rho^{2}\left(\rho^{2}+x_{d}^{2}\right)^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}d\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x_{d}}\rho^{d}\left(\rho^{2}+x_{d}^{2}\right)^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}d\rho+\int_{x_{d}}^{r}\rho^{d}\left(\rho^{2}+x_{d}^{2}\right)^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}d\rho.

For
\alpha\neq 1
, we estimate the first integral with
\int_{0}^{x_{d}}\rho^{d}(x_{d}^{2})^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}d\rho=cx_{d}^{-\alpha-1}
and the second integral with

\int_{x_{d}}^{r}\rho^{d}(\rho^{2})^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}d\rho\leq cx_{d}^{-\alpha-1}
. Similarly, for
\alpha=1
, the first integral may be bounded by
\int_{0}^{x_{d}}\rho^{d}(x_{d}^{2})^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}d\rho\leq c,
and the second integral by
\int_{x_{d}}^{r}\rho^{d}(\rho^{2})^{\frac{-d-\alpha}{2}}d\rho=c(\log r-\log x_{d})
. Therefore, again,

(16)

\displaystyle\iota_{D}(x)-\kappa_{D}(x)\leq c\begin{cases}\delta_{D}(x)^{-\alpha+1},&\text{ $\alpha>1$,}\\
\log\left(e+\frac{1}{\delta_{D}(x)}\right),&\text{ $\alpha=1$,}\\
1,&\text{ $\alpha<1$.}\end{cases}

∎

4.
Quadratic forms

From now on we assume that
D
is a bounded
C^{1,1}
set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
As usual, we let

P^{D}_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{D}(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy
and
\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy
,
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It is well known that
\{P^{D}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on
L^{2}(D)
.
Let
f\in L^{2}(D)
, that is
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f=0
on
D^{c}
. For
t>0
, we define as usual,

(17)

\mathcal{E}^{D}_{t}[f]=\frac{1}{t}\langle f-P^{D}_{t}f,f\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\langle f,f\rangle-\frac{1}{t}\langle P^{D}_{t/2}f,P^{D}_{t/2}f\rangle.

Clearly,
\mathcal{E}^{D}_{t}[f]\geq 0
.
Let
E_{\lambda}
be the spectral family of projections corresponding to (the generator of)
P^{D}_{t}

[
22
]
. Then

\mathcal{E}^{D}_{t}[f]=\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{1}{t}(1-e^{-\lambda t})\,d[E_{\lambda}f,f]=\int_{[0,\infty)}\int_{0}^{\lambda}e^{-\mu t}d\mu\,d[E_{\lambda}f,f]

is finite and nonincreasing in
t
, as is well known, see
[
22
, Lemma 1.3.4]
. We define

\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]=\sup_{t>0}\mathcal{E}^{D}_{t}[f]=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathcal{E}^{D}_{t}[f],

the Dirichlet form of the killed process.
It is well known that

\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]=\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f]
, for
f\in L^{2}(D)
.
Since
p(t,x,y)=p(t,y,x)
,
\int p(t,x,y)\,dy=1
,
p(t,x,y)/t\leq c\nu(y-x)
by (
2
), and
p(t,x,y)/t\to\nu(x,y)
by
[
28
, (2.10)]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f]=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)f(x)[f(x)-f(y)]\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f(y)-f(x)]^{2}p(t,x,y)/t\,dy\,dx

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f(y)-f(x)]^{2}\nu(y-x)\,dy\,dx.

Since
f=0
on
D^{c}
, we get the following Hardy-type inequality

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]

\displaystyle\geq 2\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\int_{D^{c}}f(x)^{2}\nu(y-x)\,dy\,dx=\int_{D}f(x)^{2}\kappa_{D}(x)\,dx,\quad f\in L^{2}(D).

Lemma 4.1
.

\{\hat{P}^{D}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on
L^{2}(D)
.

Proof.

The contractivity is trivial: by Corollary
2.20
and Jensen’s inequality, for
f\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,x,y)f(y)^{2}\,dy\,dx=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}.

For general
f\in L^{2}(D)
, we write
f=f_{+}-f_{-}
, where
f_{+}=f\vee 0
and
f_{-}=(-f)\vee 0
, and we note that
\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}=\|f_{+}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}+\|f_{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}=\|\,|f|\,\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}
, while
\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)f(y)\,dy\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)|f(y)|\,dy
. This extends the contractivity to arbitrary (signed)
f\in L^{2}(D)
. Such extensions will be used tacitly in what follows. The semigroup property follows from Chapman–Kolmogorov equations for
\hat{p}_{D}
.

For
t>0
and nonnegative
f,g\in L^{2}(D)
, by Corollary
2.7
, Theorem
2.11
, the inequality
ab\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}+b^{2}\right)
, Theorem
3.3
, Hardy inequality (
19
), and strong continuity of the semigroup
P^{D}_{t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle P_{t}^{D}f-\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\nu(z-w)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)f(y)g(x)\,dz\,dw\,ds\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hat{P}^{D}_{s}g(w)\nu(z-w)P^{D}_{t-s}f(z)\,dz\,dw\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\left(\hat{P}^{D}_{s}g(w)^{2}+P^{D}_{t-s}f(z)^{2}\right)\nu(z-w)\,dz\,dw\,ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}P^{D}_{s}g(w)^{2}\kappa_{D}(w)\,dw\,ds+c\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}P^{D}_{t-s}f(z)^{2}\kappa_{D}(z)\,dz\,ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}^{D}[P^{D}_{s}g]\,ds+c\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}^{D}[P^{D}_{s}f]\,ds

\displaystyle=c\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\|P^{D}_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}-\|P^{D}_{t}g\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\right)\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\to 0.

It follows that
\hat{P}^{D}_{t}=P^{D}_{t}-(P_{t}^{D}-\hat{P}^{D}_{t})
is weakly, hence strongly continuous on
L^{2}(D)
, see
[
21
, Theorem 1.6]
.
∎

By Section
2.5
, the heat kernels of
X
and
\hat{X}
are not comparable in general.
In this section, however, we will show that their quadratic forms are comparable.

The Dirichlet form of the shot-down process is defined in the usual fashion: we let

(20)

\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[f]=\frac{1}{t}\langle f-\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f,f\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\langle f,f\rangle-\frac{1}{t}\langle\hat{P}^{D}_{t/2}f,\hat{P}^{D}_{t/2}f\rangle

and consider the (monotone) limit

\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}[f]=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[f]=\sup_{t>0}\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[f].

The domains of the considered forms
\mathcal{E}^{D}
and
\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}
are, respectively,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})=\{f\in L^{2}(D):\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]<\infty\},\qquad\mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D})=\{f\in L^{2}(D):\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}[f]<\infty\}.

They are linear subspaces of
L^{2}(D)
because of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, e.g.,

(21)

\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}(f,g):=\frac{1}{t}\langle f-\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f,g\rangle\leq\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[f]^{1/2}\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[g]^{1/2},\quad t>0,\quad f,g\in L^{2}(D).

Then,
\mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
.
Indeed, if
f\in\mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D})
, then the following is bounded for
t>0
,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[f]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\left(\langle f,f\rangle-\langle\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f,f\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\big(\langle f_{+},f_{+}\rangle-\langle\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f_{+},f_{+}\rangle+\langle f_{-},f_{-}\rangle-\langle\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f_{-},f_{-}\rangle+\langle\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f_{+},f_{-}\rangle+\langle\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f_{-},f_{+}\rangle\big),

and so
\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[f_{+}]
is bounded for
t>0
. Thus
f_{+}\in\mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D})
. Since
f_{+}\geq 0
, by (
17
) and (
20
) we get

\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}^{D}[f_{+}]\geq\mathcal{E}^{D}_{t}[f_{+}]
, so

f_{+}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
. Similarly,
f_{-}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
, hence
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
, as needed.
Here is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded
C^{1,1}
set,
\alpha\in(0,2)
, and
\nu
as in (
1
). For
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
,

(22)

\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}[f]=\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]+\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}f(w)\nu(z-w)f(z)\,dz\,dw.

Furthermore,
\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}|f(w)\nu(z-w)f(z)|\,dz\,dw\leq\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]<\infty
and
\mathcal{D}(\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
and
t>0
. We have
\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}_{t}[f]=\mathcal{E}^{D}_{t}[f]+\frac{1}{t}\langle P_{t}^{{D}}f-\hat{P}_{t}^{{D}}f,f\rangle
.
By Corollary
2.7
,

\langle P_{t}^{D}f-\hat{P}_{t}^{D}f,f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(p_{D}(t,x,y)-\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)\right)f(y)f(x)\,dy\,dx:=I_{1}-I_{2},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}p_{D}(s,x,w)\nu(z-w)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)f(y)f(x)\,dz\,dw\,ds\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\nu(z-w)P_{s}^{D}f(w)P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)\,dz\,dw\,ds

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w^{\prime}}}\hat{p}_{D}(s^{\prime},x,w^{\prime})\nu(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})p_{D}(s-s^{\prime},z^{\prime},w)\nu(z-w)

\displaystyle\quad\times p_{D}(t-s,z,y)f(y)f(x)\,dz^{\prime}\,dw^{\prime}\,ds^{\prime}\,dz\,dw\,ds\,dx\,dy.

Since
\hat{p}_{D}(t,u,v)
and
p_{D}(t,u,v)
are symmetric in the space variables,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w^{\prime}}}\nu(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})p_{D}(s-s^{\prime},z^{\prime},w)\nu(z-w)

\displaystyle\quad\times\hat{P}^{D}_{s^{\prime}}f(w^{\prime})P^{D}_{t-s}f(z)\,dz^{\prime}\,dw^{\prime}\,ds^{\prime}\,dz\,dw\,ds.

Then,

\displaystyle 2|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w^{\prime}}}\nu(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})p_{D}(s-s^{\prime},z^{\prime},w)\nu(z-w)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(P^{D}_{s^{\prime}}f(w^{\prime})^{2}+P^{D}_{t-s}f(z)^{2}\right)\,dz^{\prime}\,dw^{\prime}\,ds^{\prime}\,dz\,dw\,ds=K_{1}+K_{2}=2K_{1}.

To justify the latter equality, namely
K_{1}=K_{2}
, we change variables
t-s=u^{\prime}
,
t-s^{\prime}=u
, use the symmetry of
\nu
, the symmetry of
p_{D}
in the space variables and the equivalence:

z\in D\setminus D_{w}
if and only if
w\in D\setminus D_{z}
.

Since
|P^{D}_{t}f|\leq P^{D}_{t}|f|
,
|\hat{P}^{D}_{t}f|\leq\hat{P}^{D}_{t}|f|
and
|f|\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
by (
18
), to show that
K_{1}
is small, we may and do assume that
f\geq 0
.
Denote
F_{D}(w)=\kappa_{D}(w)-\iota_{D}(w)
and consider the following factor of the above integral

L=L(s-s^{\prime},z^{\prime}):=\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}p_{D}(s-s^{\prime},z^{\prime},w)\nu(z-w)\,dz\,dw=\int_{D}p_{D}(s-s^{\prime},z^{\prime},w)F_{D}(w)\,dw.

It is well known
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
that for any
T>0
and all
t\leq T,x,y\in D
,

p_{D}(t,x,y)\approx\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{D}(x)^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{D}(y)^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\left(t^{-d/\alpha}\wedge\frac{t}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}|}\right)

with comparability constant depending only on
\alpha,\,D
and
T
. Thus, for small
t
,
L/\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{D}(z^{\prime})^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{s-s^{\prime}}}\right)
is comparable to

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle:=\int_{D}\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{D}(w)^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{s-s^{\prime}}}\right)\left((s-s^{\prime})^{-d/\alpha}\wedge\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{|z^{\prime}-w|^{d+\alpha}}\right)F_{D}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{D}(w)>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}\left((s-s^{\prime})^{-d/\alpha}\wedge\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{|z^{\prime}-w|^{d+\alpha}}\right)F_{D}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\delta_{D}(w)\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}\frac{\delta_{D}(w)^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{s-s^{\prime}}}\left((s-s^{\prime})^{-d/\alpha}\wedge\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{|z^{\prime}-w|^{d+\alpha}}\right)F_{D}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta_{D}(w)>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{|z^{\prime}-w|^{d+\alpha}}F_{D}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\delta_{D}(w)>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}(s-s^{\prime})^{-d/\alpha}F_{D}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\delta_{D}(w)\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}\frac{\delta_{D}(w)^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{s-s^{\prime}}}\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{|z^{\prime}-w|^{d+\alpha}}F_{D}(w)\,dw

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\delta_{D}(w)\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}\frac{\delta_{D}(w)^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{s-s^{\prime}}}(s-s^{\prime})^{-d/\alpha}F_{D}(w)\,dw.

Let
\alpha>1
. Then, by (
16
),
F_{D}(w)\lesssim\delta_{D}(w)^{1-\alpha}
, hence,
I_{3}
does not exceed a multiple of

\displaystyle\int_{\delta_{D}(w)>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}\frac{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}{|z^{\prime}-w|^{d+\alpha}}\,dw

\displaystyle+\int_{\delta_{D}(w)>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha-1-d/\alpha}\,dw

\displaystyle+\int_{\delta_{D}(w)\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|>(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}\frac{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}{|z^{\prime}-w|^{d+\alpha}}\,dw

\displaystyle+\int_{\delta_{D}(w)\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha},\,|z^{\prime}-w|\leq(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha}}(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha-1-d/\alpha}\,dw\lesssim(s-s^{\prime})^{1/\alpha-1}.

If
\alpha=1
, then by (
16
), we similarly obtain that
K_{1}\lesssim\log\left(e+1/(s-s^{\prime})\right)
and if
\alpha<1
, then
I_{3}
is bounded, because
F_{D}
is bounded and
p_{D}
is a subprobability density.

Summarizing,

(23)

I_{3}\leq cg(s-s^{\prime}),

where, for
s>0
,

g(s):=\begin{cases}s^{1/\alpha-1},\,\alpha>1,\\
\log\left(e+\frac{1}{s}\right),\,\alpha=1,\\
1,\,\alpha<1.\\
\end{cases}

By (
16
), Hardy inequality (
19
) and (
23
) we have

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w^{\prime}}}g(s-s^{\prime})\left(1\wedge\frac{\delta_{D}(z^{\prime})^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{s-s^{\prime}}}\right)\nu(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})P_{s^{\prime}}^{D}f(w^{\prime})^{2}\,dz^{\prime}\,dw^{\prime}\,ds^{\prime}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w^{\prime}}}g(s-s^{\prime})\nu(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime})P_{s^{\prime}}^{D}f(w^{\prime})^{2}\,dz^{\prime}\,dw^{\prime}\,ds^{\prime}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{D}g(s-s^{\prime})\kappa_{D}(w^{\prime})P_{s^{\prime}}^{D}f(w^{\prime})^{2}\,dw^{\prime}\,ds^{\prime}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}g(s-s^{\prime})\mathcal{E}^{D}[P^{D}_{s^{\prime}}f]\,ds^{\prime}\,ds.

We easily see that the function
G(s):=\int_{0}^{s}g(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}
is finite, nonnegative and bounded for small
s
, with
G(0)=0
, hence, by the monotonicity of
s\mapsto\mathcal{E}^{D}[P^{D}_{s}f]
, we get

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}G(s)\sup_{s^{\prime}\in[0,s]}\mathcal{E}^{D}[P^{D}_{s^{\prime}}f]\,ds=\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]\int_{0}^{t}G(s)\,ds=o(t),\text{\,as\, }t\rightarrow 0^{+}.

We conclude that
\frac{1}{t}I_{2}\rightarrow 0
, as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

We now let
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
be arbitrary, that is, not necessarily nonnegative, and focus on the integral

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}P_{s}^{D}f(w)\nu(z-w)P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)\,dz\,dw\,ds.

It is finite for nonnegative, hence arbitrary
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
. Indeed,
by Theorem
3.3
, Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, Hardy inequality (
19
) and spectral theorem,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}P_{s}^{D}f(w)^{2}\nu(z-w)\,dz\,dw\,ds}\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)^{2}\nu(z-w)\,dz\,dw\,ds}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}P_{s}^{D}f(w)^{2}\kappa_{D}(w)\,dw\,ds}\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)^{2}\kappa_{D}(z)\,dz\,ds}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}^{D}[P^{D}_{s}f]\,ds=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}-\|P_{t}^{D}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}<\infty.

for nonnegative, hence arbitrary
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{D})
.

Moreover,
\{P^{D}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a semigroup of contractions, so by (
19
),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{t}I_{1}-\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}f(w)\nu(z-w)f(z)\,dz\,dw\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\nu(z-w)\left|P_{s}^{D}f(w)P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)-f(w)f(z)\right|\,dz\,dw\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\nu(z-w)\left|P_{s}^{D}f(w)-f(w)\right||P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)|\,dz\,dw\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\nu(z-w)\left|P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)-f(z)\right||f(w)|\,dz\,dw\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hskip-12.91663pt\nu(z-w)\left|P_{s}^{D}f(w)-f(w)\right|^{2}\,dz\,dw}\sqrt{\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hskip-12.91663pt\nu(z-w)|P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)|^{2}\,dz\,dw}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hskip-12.91663pt\nu(z-w)\left|P_{t-s}^{D}f(z)-f(z)\right|^{2}\,dz\,dw}\sqrt{\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hskip-12.91663pt\nu(z-w)|f(w)|^{2}\,dz\,dw}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{D}[P^{D}_{s}f-f]\mathcal{E}^{D}[P_{t-s}f]}\,ds+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{D}[P_{t-s}^{D}f-f]\mathcal{E}^{D}[f]}\,ds\rightarrow 0,

as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}

Here and below, for

. We next propose and verify the following alternative to (

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{E}}^{D}[f]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\int_{D}(f(w)-f(z))^{2}\nu(z-w)\,dw\,dz+\int_{D}f^{2}(w)\nu(w,D^{c})\,dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iint\limits_{[z,w]\subset D}(f(w)-f(z))^{2}\nu(z-w)\,dw\,dz+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\left(f^{2}(w)+f^{2}(z)\right)\nu(z-w)\,dw\,dz

5.
The Green function

We recall the definition of the Green function for the killed process
[
19
, Section 2.3]
:

G_{D}(x,y)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}p_{D}(t,x,y)\,dt,\text{ }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Analogously we define the Green function for the shot-down process.

Definition 5.1
.

\hat{G}_{D}(x,y)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\hat{p}_{D}(t,x,y)\,dt
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We next derive a perturbation formula, Hunt formula, scaling, and positivity.

Lemma 5.2
.

\hat{G}_{D}(x,y)=G_{D}(x,y)-\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hat{G}_{D}(x,w)\nu(z-w)G_{D}(z,y)\,dz\,dw,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.7
, Fubini-Tonelli, and the above definitions,

\displaystyle G_{D}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\hat{G}_{D}(x,y)-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\nu(z-w)p_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,ds\,dz\,dw\,dt

\displaystyle=\hat{G}_{D}(x,y)-\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,w)\nu(z-w)\,ds\int_{{s}}^{\infty}\,p_{D}(t-s,z,y)\,{dt}\,dz\,dw

\displaystyle=G_{D}(x,y)-\int_{D}\int_{D\setminus D_{w}}\hat{G}_{D}(x,w)\nu(z-w)G_{D}(z,y)\,dz\,dw.\qquad\qed

Lemma 5.3
.

For open
U\subset D
,

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{D}(x,y)=\hat{G}_{U}(x,y)+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\hat{G}_{D}(X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\right].

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{D}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\hat{G}_{U}(x,y)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sigma_{U}<t,[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\hat{p}_{D}(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\right]\,dt

\displaystyle=\hat{G}_{U}(x,y)+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\int_{\sigma_{U}}^{\infty}\hat{p}_{D}(t-\sigma_{U},X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\,dt\right]

\displaystyle=\hat{G}_{U}(x,y)+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[[X_{\sigma_{U}-},X_{\sigma_{U}}]\subset D;\hat{G}_{D}(X_{\sigma_{U}},y)\right].\qquad\qed

Lemma 5.4
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ r>0

\hat{G}_{rD}(rx,ry)=r^{\alpha-d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{p}_{D}(s,x,y)\,ds=r^{\alpha-d}\hat{G}_{D}(x,y).

.

\hat{G}_{D}(x,y)>0

The result follows from Lemma

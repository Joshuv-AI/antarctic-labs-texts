A remark on Gersten complex for Milnor K K -theory
Rakesh Pawar

Abstract
. In this note, we consider the Gersten complex for Milnor K K -theory over a regular local Henselian domain S S and prove that in degrees ≥ dim S ≥ 1 \geq\dim S\geq 1 , the Gersten complex of an essentially smooth Henselian local S S -scheme is exact.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be an excellent scheme as in
[
5
, §7, 7.8]
. In
[
8
]
Kato considered the sequence of abelian groups

(1.1)

\bigoplus_{\eta\in X^{(0)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n}(\kappa(\eta))\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(1)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-1}(\kappa(x))\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-d}(\kappa(x))\to\cdots\to 0

for Milnor K-theory (similar to the Gersten complex for Quillen’s algebraic K-theory), and showed that for
X
, this sequence of abelian groups is a complex
[
8
, Proposition 1]
. Here
X^{(p)}
denotes the set of codimension
p
points of
X
.

According to the Gersten conjecture for Milnor K-theory, for any Noetherian regular local ring
A
of dimension
d
, the complex (which we refer to as
Gersten complex
)

0\to{K}^{\rm M}_{n}(A)\to{K}^{\rm M}_{n}(Frac(A))\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in{\rm Spec\,}A^{(1)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-1}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\in{\rm Spec\,}A^{(d)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-d}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\to 0

is exact, for each
n\geq 0
. Here
Frac(A)
denotes the field of fractions of
A
and
{\rm Spec\,}A^{(p)}
denotes the set of codimension
p
points of
{\rm Spec\,}A
.
For
A
containing a field, Gersten conjecture has been verified by M. Kerz
[
9
,
10
]
. More recently, M. Lüders proved the following relative version.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
)
Let
X={\rm Spec\,}A
be the spectrum of a smooth local ring
A
over an excellent discrete valuation ring, then the complex

{K}^{\rm M}_{n}(A)\to{K}^{\rm M}_{n}(Frac(X))\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(1)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-1}(\kappa(x))\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-d}(\kappa(x))\to 0.

is exact in degrees
>0
for each
n\geq 0
.
Furthermore, it is exact and the first map is injective if the exactness on the left holds for all discrete valuation rings.

In this note, we consider the complex (
1.1
), for an excellent scheme
X
over a base
S={\rm Spec\,}R
, the spectrum of a regular local Henselian domain
R
. We show that a certain tail (depending on the Krull dimension of
S
) of the Gersten complex is exact. More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

(see Theorem
3.3
)
Let
S={\rm Spec\,}R
for
R
a regular local Henselian domain of Krull dimension
\geq 1
.
Let
X
be an essentially smooth Henselian local
S
-scheme of dimension
d
, then

A^{p}({K}^{\rm M}_{n})(X)=0

for

p\geq\dim S\geq 1
and
n\geq 0.
More explicitly,

{K}^{\rm M}_{n}(Frac(X))\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(1)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-1}(\kappa(x))\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-d}(\kappa(x))\to 0

is exact in degrees
p\geq\dim S
.

We note that when
S
is of dimension one, we recover M. Lüders’ result
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
. The result in
loc. cit.
makes crucial use of the fact that the base is of dimension one. The main contribution of this paper is to realise that Gabber’s presentation lemma can be used to by-pass certain results special to dimension one.

Remark 1.3
.

We recall some related results in the literature to put our results in the historical context.

(1)

In case the base
S
is the spectrum of a field, then for a regular local ring over a field, the Gersten conjecture holds for Motivic cohomology
[
18
]
, Higher Chow groups
[
1
]
, Algebraic K-theory
[
17
]
, and étale cohomology
[
2
]
. All these results can be extended to equi-characteristic regular local rings by using the techniques of
[
16
]
.

(2)

The Gersten conjecture holds for Motivic cohomology with finite coefficients for smooth schemes over a discrete valuation ring
[
6
, Corollary 4.3]
. The Gersten conjecture holds for Algebraic K-theory with finite coefficients
[
7
, Theorem 8.2]
. Using comparison with Algebraic K-theory, there is a weaker result: for
m
coprime with
(n-1)!
(for example
m=p^{r}
and
p>n-1
), the Gersten conjecture holds for
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{n}/m

[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
, where
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{n}
denotes the improved Milnor K-theory. This result has been improved in
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
to show that the Gersten conjecture holds for
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{n}/m
, for
m=p^{r}
, for
p
-Henselian, ind-smooth algebras over a complete discrete valuation ring.

2.
Preliminaries on Rost’s cycle complex over a regular base

In this section we recall the basic notations from
[
8
,
14
]
relevant to this paper.
Let
S
be a Noetherian regular scheme, hence an excellent scheme. All
S
-schemes throughout this paper are essentially finite type over
S
, hence are excellent schemes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
denote a finite type
S
-scheme. Let
n\geq 0
. Rost’s cycle complex for the Milnor K-theory
{K}^{\rm M}_{n}
on
X
is defined as

C^{\bullet}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n}):C^{0}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\xrightarrow{d}C^{1}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\xrightarrow{d}\cdots\xrightarrow{d}C^{n-1}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\xrightarrow{d}C^{n}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to 0

where

C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n}):=\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(q)}}{K}^{\rm M}_{n-q}(\kappa(x))

and
X^{(q)}
is the set of codimension
q
points of
X
. The differential
d
is defined as follows:

Let
x\in X^{(q)}
, and
y\in X^{(q+1)}
. For
y\notin\overline{\{x\}},
define
d^{x}_{y}=0
. Let
y\in\overline{\{x\}}
. Let
Z
be the normalization of
\overline{\{x\}}
. For every
z\in Z^{(1)}
lying over
y
, we define

d^{x}_{y}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in Z^{(1)}\\
z\mapsto y\end{subarray}}c^{z}_{y}\circ d^{x}_{z}

where

d^{x}_{z}:{K}^{\rm M}_{*}(\kappa(x))\to{K}^{\rm M}_{*-1}(\kappa(z))

is the residue map for Milnor K-theory associated to the discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{Z,z}
with fraction field
\kappa(x)
and the residue field
\kappa(z)
, and

c^{z}_{y}:{K}^{\rm M}_{*}(\kappa(z))\to{K}^{\rm M}_{*}(\kappa(y))

is the Norm morphism associated to the finite field extension
\kappa(y)\subset\kappa(z)
.
Define the differential

d:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\\
y\in\overline{\{x\}}\end{subarray}}d^{x}_{y}:C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to C^{q+1}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n}).

We denote the
p^{th}
cohomology of the complex
C^{\bullet}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})
by
A^{p}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n}).

Remark 2.2
.

We recall the notion of cycle complex with support on a closed subscheme.
Let
Z\subset X
be a closed subscheme of
X
of codimension
c
and
U:=X-Z\xrightarrow{j}X
be the open complement of
Z
in
X
. Define

C^{\bullet}_{Z}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n}):=\ker\Big(C^{\bullet}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\xrightarrow{j^{*}}C^{\bullet}(U,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\Big).

We denote by
A^{p}_{Z}(X,M)
the
p^{th}
cohomology of the chain complex
C^{\bullet}_{Z}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n}).

We also recall some basic properties of the cycle complexes.

Definition 2.3
.

(1)

Let
f:X\to Y
be a finite type morphism of equi-dimensional
S
-schemes with
\dim X=d
and
\dim Y=e
. Then there is a map

f_{*}:C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to C^{e-d+q}(Y,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

defined for

\alpha\in{K}^{\rm M}_{n-q}(\kappa(x))
as

\displaystyle({f_{*}})_{xy}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}c^{\kappa(x)}_{\kappa(y)}(\alpha)&\text{if $f(x)=y$ and $\kappa(y)\subset\kappa(x)$ is a finite field extension}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
c^{\kappa(x)}_{\kappa(y)}:{K}^{\rm M}_{*}(\kappa(x))\to{K}^{\rm M}_{*}(\kappa(y))
is the Norm morphism for Milnor K-theory of fields.

(2)

Let
g:Y\to X
be a flat morphism of equi-dimensional
S
-schemes with constant relative dimension
s
. Then there is a map

g^{*}:C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to C^{q}(Y,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

defined for

\alpha\in{K}^{\rm M}_{n-q}(\kappa(x))
as

\displaystyle({g^{*}})_{xy}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}l(\mathcal{O}_{Y_{x},y})i_{*}(\alpha)&\text{if $g(y)=x$ and $y$ is a generic point of $Y_{x}$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where

is the length of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{Y_{x},y}
of

at

and

is the restriction map.

(3)

For a unit
u\in\mathcal{O}(X)
, there is a map

\{a\}:C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n+1})

defined by

\displaystyle\{a\}_{xy}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\{a\}\cdot\alpha&\text{if $x=y$ }\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(4)

Let
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
be a closed subscheme of
X
and
U:=X-Z\xrightarrow{j}X
be the open complement of
Z
in
X
. There are term-wise maps
j_{*}:C^{q}(U,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})
and
i^{*}:C^{q}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to C^{q}_{Z}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})
, then there is a map

\partial^{U}_{Z}:C^{q}(U,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to C^{q+1}_{Z}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

defined as

\partial^{U}_{Z}:=i^{*}\circ d_{X}\circ j_{*}.

We recall some properties satisfied by the above maps which will be used in the next section. The properties below are proved in
[
14
, Proposition 4.6]
when the base is the spectrum of a field. When the base in the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring, the properties are proved in
[
12
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(1)

For a proper morphism

of

(2)

(3)

Exactness of the Gersten complex for Milnor K-theory

In this section we will further assume that the base

Lemma 3.1
.

(

Let

We now prove the main result of this paper, Theorem
1.2
in the introduction.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be an essentially smooth Henselian local
S
-scheme, then

A^{p}({K}^{\rm M}_{n})(X)=0

for

p\geq\dim S\geq 1
and

Proof.

The proof follows the ideas in
[
14
, Proposition 6.1]
and
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
) with input from Proposition
3.2
.

Let
X=U_{u}^{h}:={\rm Spec\,}\mathcal{O}_{U,u}^{h}
where
U
is a smooth, irreducible, equi-dimensional
S
-scheme of dimension
d
and a point
u
in
U
lying over the closed point
s
in
S
. We have

C^{p}(X,{K}^{\rm M}_{n})=\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{(U^{\prime},u)}C^{p}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

and

C^{p}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})=\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{Y\subset U^{\prime}}C^{p}_{Y}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

where
Y
runs over closed subschemes of
U^{\prime}
of dimension

.

We have

A^{p}({K}^{\rm M}_{n})(X)=\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{(U^{\prime},u)}A^{p}({K}^{\rm M}_{n})(U^{\prime})

where

runs over Nisnevich neighbourhoods of
u
in
U
.
Thus, every element of
A^{p}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})
is represented by an element in
C^{p}_{Y}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

for some closed subscheme
Y
of
U^{\prime}
of dimension
d-p
.

We claim that, every element of
A^{p}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})
is represented by an element of
C^{p}_{Y}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})
where
Y
runs over all closed subschemes of
U^{\prime}
of dimension

, such that
\dim Y_{s}<\dim U^{\prime}_{s}
(note that

).

Let

such that

. If
y\notin U^{\prime}_{s}
, then

d-\dim S=\dim U^{\prime}_{s}=\dim\overline{\{y\}}_{s}\leq\dim\overline{\{y\}}-1=d-p-1

which is impossible, by the assumption on

. So we can assume that

By

,

of codimension

(2)

(3)

We observe that
\overline{\{y\}}\in Z_{s}^{(1)}
. We claim that

is a finite set and

.

To see the claim, first we have by assumption that
\dim(\overline{\{y\}})_{s}=\dim U^{\prime}_{s}
. (This implies that a maximal component of

.

\dim W_{s}<\dim U^{\prime}_{s}
.
Thus, we have that any element of
A^{p}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})
is represented by
an element in
A^{p}_{Y}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

for some closed subscheme
Y\subset U^{\prime}
of codimension
p
such that
\dim Y_{s}<\dim U^{\prime}_{s}
.
Now by Proposition
3.2
, for some
(U^{\prime\prime},z)
a Nisnevich neighbourhood of
z
in
U^{\prime}
,

A^{p}_{Y\times_{U^{\prime}}U^{\prime\prime}}(U^{\prime\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to A^{p}(U^{\prime\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

is zero map.

Thus, the map

A^{p}_{Y}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to A^{p}_{Y\times_{U^{\prime}}U^{\prime\prime}}(U^{\prime\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to A^{p}(U^{\prime\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

is zero implies that

A^{p}_{Y}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to A^{p}(U^{\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})\to A^{p}(U^{\prime\prime},{K}^{\rm M}_{n})

is the zero morphism.

This shows that

A^{p}({K}^{\rm M}_{n})(X)=\mathop{\varinjlim}\limits_{(U^{\prime},u)}A^{p}({K}^{\rm M}_{n})(U^{\prime})=0.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

It is worthwhile, to point out that the above proof fails for degrees

Z_{s}^{(1)}

a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}
,
Z
has codimension 1 point
z_{a}
associated to the ideal
(l+ax),
so that
\overline{\{z_{a}\}}

(l,x)

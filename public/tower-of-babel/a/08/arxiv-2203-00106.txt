Touching multifunctions on a Hilbert space
Stephen Simons

Abstract
We introduce the concept of the touching of two multifunctions on a real Hilbert space, and deduce that certain multifunctions on the space have a unique fixed point. These result are applied to the theory of genaralized cycles and generalized gap vectors for the composition of the projections onto a finite number of closed convex space in a real Hilbert space.

If

Definition 1
.

Let
\mu>0

(y_{1},q_{1}),(y_{2},q_{2})\in G(Q)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\langle y_{1}-y_{2},q_{1}-q_{2}\rangle+\mu\|(y_{1}-y_{2},q_{1}-q_{2})\|^{2}\leq 0.

We write
{\cal B}(Y)
for the set of all

from

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mu>0
and
Q\colon\ Y\rightrightarrows Y
be
\mu
–unmonotone. Then
-Q-\mu\mathbb{I}_{Y}
is monotone.

Proof.

For all
(y_{1},q_{1}),(y_{2},q_{2})\in G(Q)
,

\displaystyle\langle y_{1}-y_{2},(-q_{1}-\mu y_{1})

\displaystyle-(-q_{2}-\mu y_{2})\rangle=\langle y_{1}-y_{2},-(q_{1}-q_{2})-\mu(y_{1}-y_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle y_{1}-y_{2},q_{1}-q_{2}\rangle-\mu\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\langle y_{1}-y_{2},q_{1}-q_{2}\rangle-\mu\|(y_{1}-y_{2},q_{1}-q_{2})\|^{2}\geq 0.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2
.

On the other hand, since

Corollary 8
.

Let
Q\in{{\cal B}}(Y)

\lambda>0

\hbox{for all}\ y\in Y,\quad\langle y,Qy\rangle+\lambda\|y\|^{2}\leq 0.

(2)

Proof.

Let
\mu:=\lambda/(1+\|Q\|^{2})
. Let

and
q_{2}=Qy_{1}

\displaystyle\langle y_{1}-y_{2},q_{1}-q_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle+\mu\|(y_{1}-y_{2},q_{1}-q_{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\langle y_{1}-y_{2},Q(y_{1}-y_{2})\big\rangle+\mu\|(y_{1}-y_{2},Q(y_{1}-y_{2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big\langle y_{1}-y_{2},Q(y_{1}-y_{2})\big\rangle+\mu(1+\|Q\|^{2})\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\langle y_{1}-y_{2},Q(y_{1}-y_{2})\big\rangle+\lambda\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}\leq 0.

Lemma 9

Let

\hbox{for all}\ x\in Y,\ \langle x,Tx\rangle+\lambda\|Tx\|^{2}\leq 0.

Theorem 13

Let

\hbox{for all}\ x\in X,\ \langle x,Sx\rangle+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|Sx\|^{2}=0.

(6)

Let

[\partial(f^{*}|_{Y})]\circ S|_{Y}\colon\ Y\rightrightarrows Y

(7)

Furthermore:

(a)
If

and

Sx\in\partial f(x)\iff f^{*}(Sx)+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|Sx\|^{2}+f(x)=0\iff Sx=Se.

(8)

(b)
If
x\in X

then (
8
) holds.

Proof.

From Fact
10

\partial(f^{*}|_{Y})
is maximally monotone, and so Lemma

(
with

,

and

)
implies that

\langle e,Se\rangle=f^{*}(Se)+(f^{*}|_{Y})^{*}(e).

(9)

We now establish (

Sx\in\partial f(x)
. Then

,
f^{*}(Sx)<\infty

\langle x,Sx\rangle=f(x)+f^{*}(Sx).

From the Fenchel–Young inequality:

-\langle x,Se\rangle\geq-f(x)-f^{*}(Se)\quad\hbox{and}\quad-\langle e,Sx\rangle\geq-(f^{*}|_{Y})^{*}(e)-f^{*}(Sx).

(11)

Now
\langle x-e,S(x-e)\rangle=\langle e,Se\rangle+\langle x,Sx\rangle-\langle x,Se\rangle-\langle e,Sx\rangle
. Consequently, by adding (

-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|Sx-Se\|^{2}=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\|S(x-e)\|^{2}=\langle x-e,S(x-e)\rangle\geq 0.

(a) Suppose that

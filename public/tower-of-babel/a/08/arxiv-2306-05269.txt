Scrollar invariants, syzygies and representations of the symmetric group II
Floris Vermeulen

Abstract
. Let φ : C → 𝐏 1 \varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1} be a degree d d cover of curves. In work by Castryck, Zhao and the author, we showed how one can attach to each partition λ \lambda of d d a multi-set of scrollar invariants of λ \lambda with respect to φ \varphi . We studied these invariants when φ \varphi is simply branched, and related these new scrollar invariants to known geometric data. In this article we show how one can remove this simple branching condition, using the notion of S d S_{d} -closure as developed by Bhargava and Satriano. With this new framework, we are able to generalize all results from this work to arbitrary covers C → 𝐏 1 C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1} . In particular, we obtain a syzygy-free interpretation for the splitting types of the syzygy bundles in the Casnati–Ekedahl resolution of an arbitrary cover φ : C → 𝐏 1 \varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1} . By using the Maroni bound, we are able to give new general bounds on these splitting types.

1.
Introduction

This article is an extension of recent work by Castryck, Zhao and the author
[
13
]
, in which we studied scrollar invariants of curves using Galois theoretic tools. Throughout this work, all curves will be projective and reduced, but they may be singular or reducible. We use the words smooth and non-singular interchangeably. By a
nice curve
, we will mean a smooth, projective and geometrically integral curve.

Let
d
be a positive integer. We will work over a field
k
of characteristic zero or larger than
d
. Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a separable degree
d
cover of curves, where
C
is a nice curve of genus
g
. Then the
scrollar invariants
, see e.g.
[
14
]
, of
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
are defined as the unique integers
e_{1}\leq\ldots\leq e_{d-1}
for which

(1)

\varphi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-e_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-e_{d-1}).

Equivalently,
e_{i}
is the smallest integer
n
such that
h^{0}(C,nD)-h^{0}(C,(n-1)D)>i
, where
D
is a fibre of
\varphi
. Using Riemann–Roch, one can prove that

(1)

e_{1}+\ldots+e_{d-1}=g+d-1
, and

(2)

e_{d-1}\leq\frac{2g+2d-2}{d}
.

This last inequality is known as the
Maroni bound
.

In
[
13
]
, we developed a representation theoretic viewpoint for these scrollar invariants using Galois theory. We showed how one can attach to every irreducible representation of the symmetric group
S_{d}
a multi-set of
generalized scrollar invariants
of
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Recall that the irreducible representations of
S_{d}
correspond naturally to partitions
\lambda
of
d
. The corresponding irreducible representation is called the
Specht module
V_{\lambda}
. Then in more detail, to each partition
\lambda\vdash d
one may associate a multiset
e_{\lambda,1}\leq\ldots\leq e_{\lambda,\dim V_{\lambda}}
of
scrollar invariants corresponding to
\lambda
. For the partition
\lambda=(d-1,1)
one recovers the usual scrollar invariants
e_{i}
, while for
\lambda=(d)
one obtains
0
.

In
[
13
]
we studied these generalized scrollar invariants with the added restriction that the map
\varphi
is simply branched, i.e. all ramification patterns of
\varphi
are of the form
(2,1^{d-2})
. The main goal of this article is to extend the results from
[
13
]
to arbitrary degree
d
covers
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
(in characteristic zero or larger than
d
). Several new ideas are needed for this, which we discuss in more detail in section
(1.2)
.

(1.1).
Main results

Let us discuss the main results in some more detail. As above, let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree
d\geq 4
cover of curves, where
C
is a nice curve of genus
g
. Let
K/k(t)
be the corresponding function field extension, where
k(t)=k(\mathbf{P}^{1})
is the function field of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Let
L/k(t)
be the
S_{d}
-closure
of
K/k(t)
, as defined in
[
5
]
. This is a formal construction of an étale algebra over
k(t)
which behaves like the Galois closure of
K/k(t)
, but always with Galois group
S_{d}
. In case that the Galois closure
M/k(t)
of
K/k(t)
already has Galois group
S_{d}
, we simply have that
M=L
, but in general
L
may be reducible. As an
S_{d}
-representation,
L
is isomorphic to the regular representation of
S_{d}
, hence it decomposes as

L\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash d}W_{\lambda},

where
W_{\lambda}\cong V_{\lambda}^{\dim V_{\lambda}}
is the
isotypic component of
\lambda
in
L
, i.e. it consists of all elements of
L
which generate
V_{\lambda}
as a representation (together with zero). We show how one may attach to each such
W_{\lambda}
(equivalently each
\lambda
) a multi-set of
\dim V_{\lambda}
integers called the
scrollar invariants of
\lambda
(with respect to
K
)
:

e_{\lambda_{1}},e_{\lambda,1},\ldots,e_{\lambda,1},\quad e_{\lambda,2},\ldots,e_{\lambda,2},\quad\ldots\quad e_{\lambda,\dim V_{\lambda}},\ldots,e_{\lambda,\dim V_{\lambda}}

where each
e_{\lambda,i}
occurs with multiplicity
\dim V_{\lambda}
. For example, the unique scrollar invariant of the trivial partition
(d)
is
0
, while those of the partition
(d-1,1)
are the usual scrollar invariants
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d-1}
of
\varphi
.

Our main results include extensions of all of the results in
[
13
]
to arbitrary covers
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
, thereby removing the condition of simple branching. These results include the following:

(1)

Proposition
32
: a description of the scrollar invariants of hooks
(d-i,1^{i})
, generalizing
[
13
, Prop. 1]
,

(2)

Proposition
31
: a duality statement relating the scrollar invariants of a partition
\lambda
with those of its dual
\lambda^{*}
, as in
[
13
, Prop. 31]
, and

(3)

Corollary
28
: a
volume formula
for the sum of the scrollar invariants corresponding to
\lambda
, generalizing
[
13
, Prop. 30]
.

Moreover, our interpretation of the scrollar invariants of the partitions
\lambda_{i}=(d-i,2,1^{d-2-i})
(
2\leq i\leq d-2
) as the splitting types of the syzygy modules of the Casnati–Ekedahl resolution of
\varphi
extends as well. In particular, this gives a syzygy-free way of defining these splitting types, and gives a natural candidate definition in the number field setting, following the analogy from
[
21
, Sec. 7]
.

In more detail, define

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e_{2})\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e_{d-1}).

Then the morphism
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
decomposes as
C{\mathrel{\rightarrow}^{\iota}}\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbf{P}^{1}
, where
\pi
is the natural bundle map and
\iota
is the relative canonical embedding. Let
j:\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{e_{1}+\ldots+e_{d-1}+d-2}=\mathbf{P}^{g+2d-3}
be the
tautological morphism
. Let
R=[\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1)]
be the
ruling class
in the Picard group of
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})
and let
H=[j^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{g+2d-3}}(1)]
be the
hyperplane class
. The Picard group of
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})
is generated by these two elements. Then the
Casnati–Ekedahl resolution of
\varphi

[
12
]
is the minimal free resolution of
C
inside
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})
, which takes the form

(2)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-dH+(g-d-1)R)\rightarrow\\
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{d-3}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-(d-2)H+b_{j}^{(d-3)}R)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{d-4}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-(d-3)H+b_{j}^{(d-4)}R)\rightarrow\\
\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-2H+b_{j}^{(1)}R)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow 0.

The
b_{j}^{(i)}
are certain integers which we call the
splitting types
(see also Schreyer
[
33
]
). Here

\beta_{i}=\frac{d}{i+1}(d-2-i)\binom{d-2}{i-1}.

We prove that these splitting types
b_{j}^{(i)}
are also scrollar invariants.

Theorem 1
.

Let
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a cover of degree
d\geq 4
over a field of characteristic zero or larger than
d
, where
C
is a nice curve, and take
i\in\{1,\ldots,d-3\}
. Let
e_{1}^{\prime}\leq\ldots e_{\beta_{i}}^{\prime}
be the multi-set of scrollar invariants of the partition
(d-i-1,2,1^{i-1})
with respect to
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
and let
b_{1}^{(i)}\leq\ldots\leq b_{\beta_{i}}^{(i)}
be the splitting types of the
i
-th syzygy bundle of the Casnati–Ekedahl resolution of
C
. Then for every
j

e_{j}=b_{j}^{(i)}.

For a subgroup
H
of
S_{d}
, consider the étale subalgebra
L^{H}
of
L
. This corresponds to another curve equipped with a morphism of degree
[S_{d}:H]
to
\mathbf{P}^{1}
, which we call the
resolvent curve (with respect to
H
)
, and denote by

\res_{H}\varphi:\res_{H}C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}.

In
[
13
]
we gave a description of the scrollar invariants of such resolvent curves in terms of the scrollar invariants of various partitions
\lambda

, these resolvent curves can become singular or reducible, as will be explained in more detail in section

. However, there is still a notion of scrollar invariants in this context, and the description from
[
13
]
carries over as well.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree
d\geq 4
morphism over a field
k
of characteristic zero or larger than
d
, where
C
is a nice curve. Let

be a subgroup of

\res_{H}\varphi:\res_{H}C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}

\mult(V_{\lambda},\Ind^{S_{d}}_{H}\mathbf{1}).

(Here
\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}
denotes the permutation representation of

Since these resolvent curves can be singular or reducible (compared to
[
13
]
), it is natural to wonder when precisely this happens. As for being non-singular, this is determined representation theoretically.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree
d\geq 4
morphism over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero or larger than
d
, where
C
is a nice curve. Let
H
be a subgroup of
S_{d}
. Then the resolvent curve
\res_{H}C
is non-singular if and only if for every ramification pattern
e
of
\varphi
and every partition
\lambda
of

for which

appears in
\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}
we have one of the following:

(1)

e=(1^{d})
or
e=(2,1^{d-2})
, or

(2)

\lambda=(d)
or
\lambda=(d-1,1)
, or

(3)

\lambda=(a,b)
for some
a,b
and
e=(3,1^{d-3})
.

In particular, the maximal resolvent
\res_{1}C
is non-singular if and only if
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
simply branched.

The case (1) in the above proposition corresponds to no ramification or simple branching, while (2) simply expresses the fact that our starting curve

is non-singular. One can think of (3) as classifying the exceptional cases when a resolvent curve is non-singular.

Whether a resolvent curve is reducible or not depends only on the Galois theory of
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree
d\geq 4
morphism over a field

of characteristic zero or larger than
d
, where
C
is a nice curve. Let
H
be a subgroup of
S_{d}
. Let

be the Galois group of the Galois closure of

. Then the resolvent curve

At first sight, it looks like this condition depends on the choice of injection

, i.e. different choices will lead to conjugate subgroups. And indeed, replacing

. However, if

Main differences with
[
13
]

Let us discuss the main differences with
[
13
]
, and the new methods required. Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree
d\geq 4
cover of curves over a field
k
of characteristic
0
or larger than
d
, where
C
is a nice curve of genus
g
. Write
K/k(t)
for the corresponding function field extension and let
L/k(t)
be the Galois closure of
K/k(t)
. For simplicity let us assume that
L/k(t)
has
S_{d}
as its Galois group. In
[
13
]
we used the maximal orders in
L/k(t)
, i.e. the integral closures of
k[t]
and
k[t^{-1}]
inside
L
, to define and study the scrollar invariants corresponding to partitions
\lambda
of
d
. However, when the original cover
\varphi
is not simply branched, then almost all of the results in
[
13
]
are no longer true with this definition. For example, there is no simple description of the scrollar invariants of hooks
(d-i,1^{i})
, there is no duality statement connecting the scrollar invariants of a partition
\lambda
with those of its dual partition
\lambda^{*}
, and there is no simple formula for the genus of
L
. Most importantly however, is that the volume formula, which computes the sum of the scrollar invariants corresponding to a partition, is no longer true. To get around this problem, it is natural to use non-maximal orders in
L/k(t)
, which hence correspond to singular curves. Indeed, this is already clear from the Recilass trigonal construction on a tetragonal curve, which produces singular or reducible curves in the presence of ramification of type
(4)
or
(2,2)

[
17
,
11
]
. The orders we use come from Bhargava and Satriano’s theory of
S_{d}
-closure

[
5
]
, which gives a formal construction of a Galois closure with Galois group
S_{d}
. With this formalism, practically all of the results from
[
13
]
become valid again. As an added bonus, this framework works perfectly well even if the Galois group of
L/k(t)
is not all of
S_{d}
. In that case, our orders (and hence the resolvent curves) may become reducible.

The main difficulty with this new perspective is to properly study this
S_{d}
-closure in this context, and in particular prove the volume formula. Moreover, a better understanding of the representation theory of the symmetric group is required for this, compared to
[
13
]
. In particular we need results about higher Specht polynomials
[
38
,
1
]
.

Let us also mention that there is likely an alternative approach possible to generalizing
[
13
]
beyond the simply branched case, based on attaching to each partition
\lambda\vdash d
the parabolic vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
associated to the representation
V_{\lambda}
of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\mathbf{P}^{1}\setminus\text{ branch locus of }\varphi)
via the Mehta–Seshadri correspondence
[
26
]
. The generalized scrollar invariants of
\lambda
are precisely the splitting types of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. We refer to
[
24
,
23
]
for more details. This article sticks closer to the treatment from
[
13
]
.

Lemma 24
.

Let
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{e})
be a sequence of positive integers with

\Tr(\alpha_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{e}^{n_{e}})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }e\nmid\sum n_{i},\\
ct^{\sum n_{i}/e}&\text{ if }e\mid\sum n_{i},\end{cases}

where
c\in\mathbf{Z}
.

Proof.

We have that
\Tr(\alpha_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{e}^{n_{e}})=cm_{n}
for some

. It is well known that the elementary symmetric functions form a basis for the space of symmetric functions, so we may write

m_{n}=\sum_{n^{\prime}}c_{n^{\prime}}s_{n^{\prime}}

where the sum is over decreasing sequences

is not divisible by

is equal to
c_{n^{\prime}}t^{\sum n_{i}/e}
for some

. This proves the lemma.
∎

(3.5).

To prove Theorem
1
, we will rely on a formula for the sum of the scrollar invariants of an irreducible representation. So for

(with respect to

)
to be

\vol_{K}(\lambda)=e_{\lambda,1}+\ldots+e_{\lambda,\dim V_{\lambda}},

i.e. it is the sum of the scrollar invariants of
\lambda
with respect to

. The terminology comes from the analogy with the successive minima of a number field and Minkowski’s second theorem
[
21
, Sec. 7]
.

Lemma 27
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
d
. Then

\disc\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\left(\disc\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)^{p(\lambda)\dim V_{\lambda}},

and similarly for
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda,\infty}
.

Proof.

The statement is local, so it is enough to prove that

\disc\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}=\left(\disc\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{K}\right)^{p(\lambda)\dim V_{\lambda}}.

Assume that
0
has ramification pattern
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
in
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. As mentioned above, we have that

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{L}=\Ind_{S_{e_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{e_{r}}}^{S_{d}}\left(\bigotimes_{i}G(k[t^{1/e_{i}}]/k[t])\otimes_{k[t]}k[[t]]\right).

As in Section
(3.4)
, denote
G(k[t^{1/e_{i}}]/k[t])
by
R_{e_{i}}
. Since the discriminant passes through the completion, it is enough to prove that

\disc\left(\Ind_{S_{e_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{e_{r}}}^{S_{d}}\big(\bigotimes_{i}R_{e_{i}}\big)_{\lambda}\right)=\left(t^{p(\lambda)\dim V_{\lambda}\sum_{i}(e_{i}-1)}\right).

To do this, we construct a
k[t]
-basis for the module on the left hand side. We can decompose

\Res_{S_{e_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{e_{r}}}^{S_{d}}V_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})}a_{\mu}^{\lambda}V_{\mu_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes V_{\mu_{r}},

where the sum is over all partitions
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}
of
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
, and
a_{\mu}^{\lambda}\in\mathbf{Z}
. Note that then for every

\chi_{\lambda}=\sum_{\mu_{i}\vdash e_{i}}\left(\sum_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\hat{\mu}_{i},\ldots,\mu_{r}}a_{\mu}^{\lambda}\prod_{j\neq i}\dim V_{\mu_{j}}\right)\cdot\chi_{\mu_{i}}.

For any
\mu_{i}\vdash e_{i}
we fix a standard Young tableau
T_{\mu_{i}}
and define the set

\{\omega_{\mu_{i},1},\ldots,\omega_{\mu_{i},\dim V_{\mu_{i}}}\}=\{\psi_{0}(F_{T}^{S})\mid S\in\ST(\mu_{i})\}.

By Lemma
26
, acting with
S_{e_{i}}
on
\{\omega_{\mu_{i},j}\}_{j}
gives a
k[t]
-basis for
R_{e_{i},\mu_{i}}
. Let
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}
be partitions of
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
, and let
\ell_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,\dim V_{\mu_{i}}\}
. For each element

\omega(\mu,\ell)=\omega_{\mu_{1},\ell_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\omega_{\mu_{r},\ell_{r}}\in\bigotimes_{i}R_{e_{i},\mu_{i}}

we obtain
a_{\mu}^{\lambda}
elements in

\Ind_{S_{e_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{e_{r}}}^{S_{d}}\big(\bigotimes_{i}R_{e_{i}}\big)_{\lambda}

each of which generates a different copy of
V_{\lambda}
upon tensoring with
k(t)
. Together, these elements will form a
k[t]
-basis for
\Ind_{S_{e_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{e_{r}}}^{S_{d}}\big(\bigotimes_{i}R_{e_{i}}\big)_{\lambda}
. When we compute the discriminant we obtain

\displaystyle\disc\left(\Ind_{S_{e_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{e_{r}}}^{S_{d}}\big(\bigotimes_{i}R_{e_{i}}\big)_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=t^{\dim V_{\lambda}\sum_{\mu}\sum_{S_{i}\in\ST(\mu_{i})}a_{\mu}^{\lambda}\sum_{i}m(S_{i})}

\displaystyle=t^{\dim V_{\lambda}\sum_{i}\sum_{\mu_{1},\ldots,\hat{\mu}_{i},\ldots,\mu_{r}}\sum_{\mu_{i}}a_{\mu}^{\lambda}p(\mu_{i})(e_{i}-1)\prod_{j\neq i}\dim V_{\mu_{j}}}

\displaystyle=t^{\dim V_{\lambda}p(\lambda)\sum_{i}(e_{i}-1)},

where we have used Corollary
13
. Since the characteristic of
k
does not divide
d
, the extension
\mathcal{O}_{K}/k[t]
is tamely ramified and we have that
\disc(\mathcal{O}_{K}/k[t])=t^{\sum(e_{i}-1)}
. Hence we conclude that indeed

\disc(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/k[t])=\disc(\mathcal{O}_{K}/k[t])^{\dim(V_{\lambda})p(\lambda)}.\qed

We can deduce our volume formula.

Corollary 28

(Volume formula)
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
d
. Then

\vol_{K}(\lambda)=p(\lambda)\cdot(g+d-1).

Proof.

The representation
V_{(1^{d})}
is one-dimensional. Let
\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
be a reduced basis of
\mathcal{O}_{(1^{d})}
and note that
\delta^{2}\in k[t]
. Then by Lemma
27
, we have that
\disc(\mathcal{O}_{K}/k[t])=\delta^{2}
. Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}
be a reduced basis for
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
, where
r=\dim(V_{\lambda})^{2}
, with scrollar invariants
e_{\lambda,1},\ldots,e_{\lambda,r}
. Consider the discriminant matrix
T=(\Tr(\omega_{i}\omega_{j}))_{i,j}
, which has determinant

\disc(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/k[t])=\disc(\mathcal{O}_{K}/k[t])^{p(\lambda)\dim(V_{\lambda})}=\delta^{2p(\lambda)\dim(V_{\lambda})},

by Lemma
27
. We now play the same game over infinity, so we define
T_{\infty}=(\Tr(t^{-e_{\lambda,i}-e_{\lambda,j}}\omega_{i}\omega_{j})))_{i,j}
. On the one hand, by simply taking out all powers of
t
from
T_{\infty}
, we have that

\frac{\det T}{\det T_{\infty}}=t^{\dim(V_{\lambda})(2\vol_{K}\lambda)}.

On the other hand, the discussion above shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\det T}{\det T_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\disc(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/k[t])^{p(\lambda)\dim(V_{\lambda})}}{\disc(\mathcal{O}_{\infty,\lambda}/k[t^{-1}])^{p(\lambda)\dim(V_{\lambda})}}=\frac{\delta^{2p(\lambda)\dim(V_{\lambda})}}{(t^{-g-d+1}\delta)^{2p(\lambda)\dim(V_{\lambda})}}

\displaystyle=t^{(2g+2d-2)p(\lambda)\dim(V_{\lambda})},

from which the result follows.
∎

As for resolvent curves, we obtain the following.

Corollary 29
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
S_{d}
. Then

\disc\mathcal{O}_{L}^{H}=\left(\disc\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)^{p(H)},

and similarly for
\mathcal{O}_{L,\infty}^{H}
, where

p(H)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}-\chi_{\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}}((12))\right).

Equivalently, one may define
p(H)
as

p(H)=(d-2)!\frac{\#\{\text{transpositions }\sigma\notin H\}}{\#H}.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
27
and Theorem
2
.
∎

One may also state this result as a computation of the (arithmetic) genus of the resolvent curve
\res_{H}C
.

Corollary 30
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
C
and suppose that

is irreducible. Then the (arithmetic) genus of

is equal to

Proof.

This follows from the previous result via the Riemann–Hurwitz formula
[

(3.6).
Duality

There is a duality statement connecting the scrollar invariants of a partition
\lambda
and those of the dual partition
\lambda^{*}
, obtained by transposing the corresponding Young diagram. Recall that we have that
V_{\lambda^{*}}=V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{(1^{d})}
.

Proposition 31

(Duality)
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
d
with scrollar invariants
\{e_{\lambda,i}\}_{i}
in
L
. Then the scrollar invariants of
\lambda^{*}
are given by
\{g+d-1-e_{\lambda,i}\}_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}
be a reduced basis for
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
, where
r=\dim(V_{\lambda})^{2}
, with scrollar invariants
e_{\lambda,1},\ldots,e_{\lambda,r}
. Let
\delta
be a reduced basis of the one-dimensional
\mathcal{O}_{(1^{d})}
. By Lemma
27
the scrollar invariant of
\delta
is
g+d-1
. Since the extension
K/k(t)
is separable, the trace pairing
\Tr:L\rightarrow k(t)
is non-degenerate. Hence there exists a dual basis
\omega_{1}^{*},\ldots,\omega_{r}^{*}
to
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}
with the property that
\Tr(\omega_{i}\omega_{j}^{*})
is
1
if
i=j
and
0
if
i\neq j
. We claim that the elements

\delta\omega_{i}^{*},i=1,\ldots,\dim(V_{\lambda})^{2}

form a reduced basis for
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{*}}
. These elements are indeed contained in
W_{\lambda^{*}}
. The strategy for this will be to use Lemma
22
. First, note that the candidate scrollar invariants indeed sum up to the correct value, since

\sum_{i}(g+d-1-e_{i})=\frac{1}{2}(\dim(V_{\lambda})+\chi_{\lambda}((12)))(g+d-1).

So we want to prove that
\delta\omega_{i}^{*}
is in
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{*}}
and that
t^{-(g+d-1-e_{\lambda,i})}\delta\omega_{i}^{*}
is in
\mathcal{O}_{\infty,\lambda^{*}}
.

To prove the claim, we work locally again. Indeed,
\delta\omega_{i}^{*}
is in
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda^{*}}
if and only if it is contained in
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda^{*}}
for every completion of
L
. So let us localize at
0
. Assume that
0
ramifies as
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{s})
in
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Then

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{L}=\bigotimes_{i}G(k[[t^{1/e_{i}}]]/k[[t]]).

We first focus on one of the components
G(k[[t^{1/e_{i}}]]/k[[t]])
. For these we have an explicit basis via higher Specht polynomials by Lemma
26
. Let
\mu
be a partition of
e_{i}
. Let
T,S\in\ST(\mu)
and
T^{\prime},S^{\prime}\in\ST(\mu^{*})
. Then
\psi_{0}(F_{T}^{S})\psi_{0}(F_{T^{\prime}}^{S^{\prime}})
is in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, and so the coefficient of this product at
\delta
is in
k[t]
. This coefficient is (up to an element of
k^{\times}
)
\Tr(\psi_{0}(F_{T}^{S})\psi_{0}(F^{T^{\prime}}_{S^{\prime}})/\delta)
. Since
\ch(S)+\ch(S^{\prime})<2\binom{e}{2}
if
\mu\neq(1^{e})
we have in fact that
\Tr(\psi_{0}(F_{T}^{S})\psi_{0}(F^{T^{\prime}}_{S^{\prime}})/\delta)
is in
k
. Since the matrix with these elements as its entries is invertible (as the
\psi_{0}(F_{T}^{S})/\delta
generate
W_{\lambda^{*}}
as a representation), we conclude that the
\delta\psi_{0}(F_{T}^{S})^{*}
are in
G(k[[t^{1/e_{i}}]]/k[[t]])
. A similar strategy to the proof of Lemma
27
allow us to conclude that
\delta\psi_{0}(F_{T}^{S})^{*}
is in
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
. The same reasoning above the other points of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
give the desired conclusion.
∎

(3.8).
Maximal resolvents

Compared to
[
13
]
, our resolvent curves can become singular or reducible. So it is a natural question to classify precisely when this happens. In this section we prove Proposition
3
. This shows that
\res_{H}C
being singular is determined representation theoretically, and depends only on the ramification of
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

For the proof, we first rewrite what it means for
\res_{H}C
to be maximal in terms of characters of
S_{d}
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
be the maximal order of
L
, i.e. it is the integral closure of
k[t]
in
L
. Similarly define
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L,\infty}
as the integral closure of
k[t^{-1}]
in
L
. For
\lambda\vdash d
we define
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
as
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\cap W_{\lambda}
, where
W_{\lambda}
is the isotypic component of
\lambda
in
L
. Let us say that
\lambda
is
maximal in
L
if
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
. For
p
a point in
\mathbf{P}^{1}
, let us say that
\lambda
is
maximal in
L
at
p
if
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is equal to
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
after localizing at
p
. By Theorem
2
, we see that
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{H}
is maximal if and only if for every partition
\lambda
of
d
such that
\mult(V_{\lambda},\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\lambda)>0
we have that
\lambda
is maximal in
L
.

For a partition
e=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
of
d
let
\rho\in S_{d}
be a permutation with cycle type
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
. For an integer
i
we let
e^{i}
be the partition of
d
corresponding to the cycle type of the permutation
\rho^{i}
. We denote by
\lcm(e)=\lcm\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
. We begin by computing the discriminant of the maximal order of the resolvent.

Lemma 33
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
S_{d}
. Assume that
0
ramifies as
e=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}/k[t]
. Then the discriminant of the completion
\hat{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}_{L}^{H}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{H}
above
0
is equal to

\prod_{i=1}^{\lcm(e)-1}t^{\frac{n(H,e^{i})}{\lcm(e)}},

where for
\sigma\in S_{d}
,
n(H,\sigma)=\chi_{\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}}(\id)-\chi_{\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}}(\sigma)
.

Proof.

We rely on a discriminant formula due to Lenstra, Pila and Pomerance, to compute the discriminant of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{H}

[
25
, Thm. 4.4]
. This result is only stated for number fields, but the formula carries over directly to our setting, and states that

(\disc\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}^{H})^{d!}=\prod_{\sigma\in S_{d}\setminus\{\id\}}\Norm_{L/k(t)}(\mathcal{I}_{\sigma})^{\#\{\tau:L^{H}\hookrightarrow L\mid\sigma\circ\tau\neq\tau\}},

where
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
is the ideal of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
generated by all elements of the form
\sigma(x)-x
, for
x\in\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
. Note that a non-zero prime
\mathcal{B}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
above
0
divides
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
if and only if
\sigma
is in the inertia group of
\mathcal{B}
. By
[
39
, SZ. 1]
or
[
27
, Cor. 7.59]
, this inertia group is cyclic and generated by a permutation
\rho
of cycle type
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
. So in the product we can disregard those
\sigma
which are not conjugate to a power of
\rho
. Now note that

\#\{\tau:L^{H}\hookrightarrow L\mid\sigma\circ\tau\neq\tau\}=n(H,\sigma).

Since
\charac k
does not divide
d
, the extension
K/k(t)
is tamely ramified, ensuring that
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
is a square-free ideal, i.e.
\mathcal{B}^{2}
never divides
\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}
. This description of the inertia group moreover yields that the ramification index of
0
in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
is equal to
\lcm(e)
, the least common multiple of the ramification indices of
0
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Hence the number of primes above
0
in
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
is equal to
d!/\lcm(e)
. Since the action of
S_{d}
is transitive on the primes above
0
in
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
, a computation shows that

\disc(\hat{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}_{L}^{H}/k[[t]])=\prod_{i=1}^{\lcm(e)-1}t^{\frac{n(H,e^{i})}{\lcm(e)}},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 34
.

Let
\lambda
be a non-trivial partition of
d
. Then
\lambda
is maximal in
L
if and only if for every ramification pattern
e=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
of

and every

, we have that

\frac{\dim V_{\lambda}-\chi_{\lambda}(e^{i})}{\dim V_{\lambda}-\chi_{\lambda}((12))}=\frac{d-\#\mathrm{Fix}(e^{i})}{2},

where
\mathrm{Fix}(e^{i})\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}
is the set of fixed points of a permutation of cycle type

.

Proof.

The statement is local and geometric, so it is enough to prove this above
0\in\mathbf{P}^{1}
with the assumption that

is algebraically closed. Let
e=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
be the ramification pattern of
0
in

. We first investigate when

is maximal. By Lemma
29
the resolvent
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{H}
has discriminant

(\disc\mathcal{O}_{K})^{p(H)}.

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{H}
is maximal if and only if its discriminant is equal to the discriminant of

. So by the previous lemma for

and

,

Rewriting, this equation is equivalent to the statement that

\frac{[S_{d}:H]-\chi_{\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}}(e^{i})}{[S_{d}:H]-\chi_{\Ind_{H}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}}((12))}=\frac{d-\#\mathrm{Fix}(e^{i})}{2}.

The result now follows by considering all Young subgroups

in

of

.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

The proof follows directly from Lemma

, Lemma

(3.9).
Irreducible resolvents

If the Galois closure of
K/k(t)
does not have the full symmetric group

as its Galois group, then the curve corresponding to
L
will be reducible. However, it can still happen that a resolvent

is irreducible. This will depend only on the Galois group of the Galois closure of

and the subgroup

, as described by Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

Denote by
M
the Galois closure of the extension
K/k(t)
. Let
r=[S_{d}:G]=d!/\#G
. Then by
[
5
]
,
L
is isomorphic to
M^{r}
as a
k(t)
-algebra. In fact,
L
is isomorphic as a
k(t)[S_{d}]
-algebra to

\Ind_{G}^{S_{d}}M.

Let us be a bit more explicit about the
S_{d}
-action on
L
. Let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
be coset representatives for
G
in
S_{d}
. Then we can write
L=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}s_{i}M
as
M[S_{d}]
-module. Every
\sigma\in S_{d}
determines a permutation
j_{\sigma}
of the
s_{i}
via
\sigma s_{i}=s_{j_{\sigma}(i)}g_{\sigma,i}
for some
g_{\sigma,i}\in G
. For
x=\sum_{i}s_{i}x_{i}
in
L
the action of
\sigma
on
x
is defined by

\sigma(x)=\sigma\left(\sum_{i}s_{i}x_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}s_{j_{\sigma}(i)}g_{\sigma,i}(x_{i}).

Now assume that
GH=S_{d}
. Equivalently, this means that the action of
H
on
S_{d}/G
via left translation is transitive. Take any element
x=\sum_{i}s_{i}x_{i}
in
L^{H}
. For every

there is by transitivity a
\sigma\in H
such that

. Acting with this

shows that
v_{i}=g_{\sigma,1}(v_{1})
and hence all non-zero elements of

are invertible. In other words,

is a field.

Conversely, assume that
GH\neq S_{d}
. Equivalently, the action of
H
on

is not transitive. Then we find

such that for every

we have that

. Now consider the element

\sum_{h\in H}h\cdot(s_{i}\cdot 1)\in L,

(here

is interpreted in

.) The result is clearly a non-zero non-invertible element of

, since the coordinate at

will be zero while the coordinate at

is

is not a field.

4.
Scrollar syzygies and splitting types

Let

Let

(4.2).
The construction

We know explain how the maps in the resolution are constructed, beginning with the first step in the resolution. Let

, where

(4.3).
The splitting types are scrollar

In this section we give a scrollar interpretation for Schreyer’s invariants. Let us first recall the setting. Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree
d\geq 4
cover, where
C
is a nice curve of genus
g
. Define the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbf{P}^{1}
as

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e_{2})\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(e_{d-1}),

where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d-1}
are the scrollar invariants of
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. The map
\varphi
decomposes as the composition of the
relative canonical embedding

C\hookrightarrow\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})
with the natural bundle map
\pi:\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. The Picard group of
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})
is generated by two classes
R,H
where
R
is the
ruling class

R=\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1)
, and
H
is the
hyperplane class

H=j^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{g+2d-3}}(1)
with
j:\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{g+2d-3}
the tautological map. Casnati–Ekedahl
[
12
]
, generalizing work by Schreyer
[
33
]
, give a minimal graded free resolution of
C
inside
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})
of the form

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-dH+(g-d-1)R)\rightarrow

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{d-3}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-(d-2)H+b_{j}^{(d-3)}R)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{d-4}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-(d-3)H+b_{j}^{(d-4)}R)\rightarrow

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-2H+b_{j}^{(1)}R)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow 0,

for certain integers
b_{j}^{(i)}
, called the
splitting types
. These splitting types satisfy a duality statement
[
33
, Cor. 4.4]
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,d-3\}
we have that

\{b_{j}^{(d-2-i)}\}_{j}=\{g+d-1-b^{(i)}_{j}\}_{j}.

This can also be seen as a corollary of our duality result, Proposition
31
, in combination with Theorem
1
.

The fibres of the map
\pi:\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
are isomorphic to
\mathbf{P}^{d-2}
, and we equip these with homogeneous coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1}
. We write
s,t
for coordinates on
\mathbf{P}^{1}
. This allows us to speak about defining equations for
C
, and more generally for the syzygies appearing in the Casnati–Ekedahl resolution. In more detail, the first map

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-2H+b_{j}^{(1)}R)\rightarrow\mathcal{O_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}}

is described by
\beta_{1}
quadratic forms (
j=1,\ldots,\beta_{1}
)

Q^{j}=\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}+\ldots+j_{d_{1}}=2}Q^{j}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d-1}}(s,t)x_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots x_{d-1}^{j_{d-1}},

where
Q^{j}_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d-1}}(s,t)\in F[s,t]
is homogeneous of degree

j_{1}e_{1}+j_{2}e_{2}+\ldots+j_{d-1}e_{d-1}-b_{j}^{(1)}.

For
i\in\{1,\ldots,d-4\}
, the map

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{i+1}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-(i+2)H+b_{j}^{(i+1)}R)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\beta_{i}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{E})}(-(i+1)H+b_{j}^{(i)}R)

can be represented by a
\beta_{i}\times\beta_{i+1}
matrix whose
(j_{1},j_{2})
entry is a linear form
L_{1}(s,t)x_{1}+\ldots L_{d-1}(s,t)x_{d-1}
where
L_{j}(s,t)\in F[s,t]
is homogeneous of degree

e_{j}+b_{j_{1}}^{(i)}-b_{j_{2}}^{(i+1)}.

The last step in the resolution may be described similarly using quadratic forms.

To prove Theorem
1
we apply the construction of the resolution from section
(4.1)
to the generic fibre of
C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. By applying this construction with reduced bases, we find exactly the Casnati–Ekedahl resolution as above.

(5.1).
The Maroni bound

There are many results in the literature about understanding what the scrollar invariants of a cover
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
can be, see e.g.
[

. Using ideas from

Theorem 38

(Maroni bound)
.

Let
d\geq 4
be an integer and
k
a field of characteristic zero or larger than
d
. Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree
d
morphism over
k
, where
C
is a nice curve of genus
g
. Let
H
be a subgroup of
S_{d}
for which
\res_{H}C
is irreducible. Denote by
r_{1}\leq\ldots\leq r_{[S_{d}:H]-1}
the scrollar invariants of
\res_{H}\varphi:\res_{H}C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Then

r_{[S_{d}:H]-1}\leq\frac{2g(\res_{H}C)+2[S_{d}:H]-2}{[S_{d}:H]},

where
g(\res_{H}C)
is the arithmetic genus of
\res_{H}C
.

The proof of this result crucially depends on the fact that
\res_{H}C
is irreducible.

Proof.

Let
n=[S_{d}:H]
, which is the degree of the map
\res_{H}C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Denote by
K
the function field of
C
and by
L
its
S_{d}
-closure, so that
L^{H}
is the function field of
\res_{H}C
. Let
1,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}
be a reduced basis for
L^{H}
, with scrollar invariants
r_{1}\leq\ldots\leq r_{n-1}
. Consider the
(n-2)\times(n-2)
matrix
M=(m_{ij})
where
m_{ij}
is the coefficient of
\alpha_{n-1}
in
\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}
. We claim that
\det M\neq 0
. If not, then there is an element
\beta
in the span of
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-2}
such that multiplication by
\beta
fixes the
k(t)
-subspace
A
of
L^{H}
spanned by
1,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-2}
. Since
L^{H}
is a field, so is
k(t)(\beta)
. Hence
A
is a
k(t)(\beta)
-vector subspace of
L^{H}
. But
k(t)(\beta)
has
k(t)
-dimension at least
2
, while
A
is of codimension
1
in
L^{H}
. This is a contradiction.

It follows that for some permutation
\pi\in S_{n-2}
,
m_{i\pi(i)}\neq 0
for all
i
. For each
i
, both
v_{n-1}
and
v_{i}v_{\pi(i)}
complete
\{1,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-2}\}
to a
k(t)
-basis of
L^{H}
. Since we are working with a reduced basis we have that
r_{n-1}\leq r_{i}+r_{\pi(i)}
. Summing over all
i
and adding

gives that

nr_{n-1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}(r_{i}+r_{\pi(i)})+2r_{n-1}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}r_{i}=2g(\res_{H}C)+2n-2.\qed

Applying this to
H=S_{\lambda}
we obtain the following.

Corollary 39
.

Let
\varphi:C\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a degree

morphism over a field

of characteristic
0
or larger than
d
. Let
\lambda=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
be a partition of

and assume that

is irreducible. Then the scrollar invariants

of
\lambda

\varphi

e_{\lambda,j}\leq\frac{d^{2}-\sum_{i}d_{i}^{2}}{d(d-1)}(g+d-1).

Proof.

Apply the previous theorem to

and use the fact that
V_{\lambda}
appears in
\Ind_{S_{\lambda}}^{S_{d}}\mathbf{1}
.
∎

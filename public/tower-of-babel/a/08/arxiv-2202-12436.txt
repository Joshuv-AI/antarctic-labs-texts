A hybrid high-order scheme for the stationary, incompressible magnetohydrodynamics equations
Jérôme Droniou, Liam Yemm

Abstract
We propose and analyse a hybrid high-order (HHO) scheme for the stationary incompressible magnetohydrodynamics equations. The scheme has an arbitrary order of accuracy and is applicable on generic polyhedral meshes. For sources that are small enough, we prove error estimates in energy norm for the velocity and magnetic field, and L 2 L^{2} -norm for the pressure; these estimates are fully robust with respect to small faces, and of optimal order with respect to the mesh size. Using compactness techniques, we also prove that the scheme converges to a solution of the continuous problem, irrespective of the source being small or large. Finally, we illustrate our theoretical results through 3D numerical tests on tetrahedral and Voronoi mesh families. Key words: hybrid high-order methods, magnetohydrodynamics MSC2010: 65N12, 65N15, 65N30.

Developed in

Additionally, HHO methods are of arbitrary order, dimension independent, and are built on polynomial reconstructions that account for the local physics and enable robustness with respect to the model’s parameters. Hybrid high-order methods are also amenable to static condensation of the system matrix which drastically reduces the number of globally coupled degrees of freedom. A thorough review of the analysis and application of HHO methods is given in the monograph

[

devises a HHO method for a magnetostatics problem, albeit without fully exploiting the principle of high-order reconstruction of HHO methods for the curl operator. However, to the best of our knowledge, no hybrid high-order schemes for MHD models exist.

Closely related to the HHO method are the virtual element methods (VEM)

This paper designs and analyses an HHO discretisation of the stationary, incompressible MHD equations. By considering the problem posed in a polyhedral domain we are able to consider a weak formulation where both the fluid and magnetic unknowns are
H^{1}
and the main differential operator is the Laplacian, and exploit certain symmetries of the model. Compared to the existing literature, our main contributions are:

•

arbitrary-order scheme for the full MHD equations, that is applicable on generic polyhedral meshes,

•

optimal-order error estimates, in the case of small source terms, that are fully robust with respect to small faces,

•

convergence analysis without any assumption on the magnitude of the source terms,

•

3D tests with various polynomial degrees.

The analysis of the scheme is carried out in Section
3

A conclusion is presented in Section

1.1
Governing equations

The mathematics of MHD combines the Navier–Stokes equations governing the motion of a viscous fluid, and Maxwell’s equations and the Lorentz force at the core of electromagnetism. A number of key assumptions are made in deriving the central model (
1.1
) used throughout this paper. We assume that the fluid satisfies a continuum assumption so that any discrete behaviour of particles may be ignored, and that typical length scales are much greater than the Debye length so that the net charge of any fluid element can be assumed to be zero. We consider here the case of an incompressible, Newtonian fluid. This assumes a sufficiently small Mach number, and a linear relation between the stress and strain tensors. We also assume that the fluid velocity is sufficiently smaller than the speed of light to ignore any relativistic effects, and that the relationship between the fluid velocity and magnetic field is governed by Ohm’s law. All the assumptions mentioned above fall in one of the following four categories: the typical length scale is sufficiently large, the fluid velocity is sufficiently small, the viscosity is sufficiently small, and the electrical conductivity is sufficiently large.

2
Hybrid high-order scheme for the MHD model

We consider polyhedral meshes as in
[
18
, Definition 1.4]
.
Fixing a countable set of mesh sizes
\mathcal{H}\subset(0,\infty)
with a unique cluster point at
0
, for each
h\in\mathcal{H}
we consider a mesh
\mathcal{M}_{h}=(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathcal{F}_{h})
of the domain
\Omega
as follows:
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a collection of disjoint open polyhedra (the elements) such that
\overline{\Omega}=\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\overline{T}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{h}
is a collection of disjoint planar sets (the faces) such that
\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\partial{T}}=\cup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\overline{F}
.

We shall also collect the boundary faces
F\subset\partial\Omega
in the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm{b}}}
, and the interior faces in the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm{i}}}=\mathcal{F}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm{b}}}
. For
X=T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
or
X=F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
,
h_{X}
denotes the diameter of
X
.
The parameter
h
is related to the elements diameters by
h\vcentcolon=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}
. For each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the set
{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}:=\{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}:F\subset{\partial{T}}\}
denotes the collection of faces contained in the boundary of
T
. Similarly, the one or two elements attached to a face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{F}\vcentcolon=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:F\subset{\partial{T}}\}
. The (constant) unit normal to
F\in{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}
pointing outside
T
is denoted by
{\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}
, and
{\bm{n}}_{{T}}:{\partial{T}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the piecewise constant outer unit normal defined by
({\bm{n}}_{{T}})|_{F}={\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}
.

We make the following assumption on the mesh which ensures that boundary trace inequalities, and Lebesgue and Sobolev embeddings hold independent of the parameter

.

Assumption 1

(Regular mesh sequence)
.

There exists a constant
\varrho>0
such that, for each
h\in\mathcal{H}
, each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is star-shaped with respect to a ball of radius

.

Remark 3

(About the mesh regularity property)
.

It has recently been proved in
[

]
that HHO methods for elliptic equations are robust and convergent under weaker mesh regularity assumptions than Assumption
1
. The analysis carried out here would also hold under the weaker assumption introduced in this reference. We adopt here the slightly stronger Assumption

(which still allows for arbitrarily shaped and sized faces, on the contrary to standard mesh regularity assumptions for HHO methods – see e.g.
[

, Definition 1.9]
) to simplify the presentation, mostly the proof of the discrete Poincaré–Sobolev–Wirtinger inequality in Lemma

From hereon, we shall denote

to mean

where

is a constant independent of the quantities
X
and

, of the mesh size

, and of the model data
(\nu_{k},\nu_{m},{\bm{f}},{\bm{g}})
.

2.1
Local construction

In this section we construct local approximations of each of the bilinear forms

is the dimension of

\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathcal{F}_{T})\vcentcolon=\{v\in L^{1}({\partial{T}}):v|_{F}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(F)\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}\}.

On each element

, the local space of unknowns is defined as follows:

\underline{\bm{U}}_{T}^{k}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{P}^{k}({T})^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}({\mathcal{F}_{{T}}})^{3}.

We also endow the space

with the

-like seminorm

defined for all

\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{1,T}^{2}\vcentcolon=\|\nabla{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{{T}}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{{\partial{T}}}^{2}.

The local interpolator

is given by

\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{v}}=({\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}},{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}^{0,k}{\bm{v}})\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in H^{1}(T)^{3}

2.1.1
Discretisation of the bilinear forms

The local reconstruction
{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{T}^{k+1}:\underline{\bm{U}}_{T}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k+1}(T)^{3}

Remark 4

(Hybrid pressure spaces)
.

Hybrid pressure spaces –that is, discrete pressure spaces with unknowns in the elements and on the faces– have been considered for the hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin discretisation of the Navier–Stokes equations in
[
49
]
, and for the HHO discretisation of Stokes and Navier–Stokes equations in
[
5
,
7
]
(which, for a given number of globally coupled unknowns, achieves a higher degree of accuracy than
[
49
]
thanks to the HHO potential reconstruction). Using hybrid pressure spaces ensures that the element velocities are pointwise divergence-free and have continuous normal traces (that is,
\DIV{\bm{u}}_{T}=0
for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and
{\bm{u}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{TF}+{\bm{u}}_{T^{\prime}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{T^{\prime}F}=0
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{T^{\prime}}
).

A variation of the HHO scheme (
2.12
) with hybrid pressure spaces could be designed following the approach of
[
5
,
7
]
, and would lead to a scheme for which element velocities and magnetic fields are pointwise divergence-free and with continuous normal traces. This would, however, substantially increase the total number of globally coupled unknowns (after static condensation): for (
2.12
), the globally coupled unknowns are the face velocities and magnetic fields, and one pressure unknown per element; for a hybrid pressure version, the globally coupled unknowns would be the face velocities and magnetic fields, but also all the face unknowns of
q,r
. For the MHD model, which essentially consists of two coupled Navier–Stokes problems, this can lead to quite an expensive system to solve. We however note that some form of (reconstructed) divergence-free property is obtained by (
2.12
), namely:
{\rm{D}}_{T}^{k}\underline{\bm{u}}_{T}={\rm{D}}_{T}^{k}\underline{\bm{b}}_{T}=0
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

(recalling that the space dimension is

here), see also
[
17
, Lemma 5.8 and Remark 5.9]
. Inspecting the proof of
[
18
, Lemma 6.35]
, the hidden constant depends on a Poincaré inequality, an inverse Lebesgue inequality for polynomials, and the equivalence
|T|_{d}\approx h_{T}^{d}
, all of which hold under Assumption
1

[
26
]
.

We state here discrete Sobolev–Poincaré estimates on
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
. Since
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\subset\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
, these estimates also hold in
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}
.

Lemma 7

(Discrete Sobolev–Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality)
.

For all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}
it holds that

\|{\bm{v}}_{h}-\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)^{3}}\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}

(3.3)

where

\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}{\bm{v}}_{h}.

(3.4)

Proof.

A proof of (
3.3
) is provided by
[
18
, Theorem 6.5]
for meshes possessing a regular matching simplicial submesh, by considering projections of the high-order HHO space on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
into the lowest-order space on the matching simplicial submesh. The arguments, however, extend easily to the case of star-shaped elements by instead projecting onto the lowest-order space on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
itself, by relying on the boundary trace inequality (
3.2
) rather than a trace inequality on each face, and by invoking
[
25
, Lemma B.24]
(the latter lemma assumes the number of faces attached to each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is bounded above; however, a closer inspection of the proof reveals this assumption is not necessary).
∎

Lemma 8
.

It holds for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
that

\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h},

(3.5)

where
\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}
is defined in (
3.4
).

Proof.

Define
\psi(\boldsymbol{x})=\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\Omega})\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\Omega)
, where
\boldsymbol{x}_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{x}\,d\boldsymbol{x}
is the centre of mass of
\Omega
. Then,
\int_{\Omega}\psi=0
and
\nabla\psi=\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}
, and thus

\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}{\bm{v}}_{h}\cdot\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}{\bm{v}}_{T}\cdot\nabla\psi=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big(-\int_{T}(\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}}_{T})\psi+\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})\psi\Big).

(3.6)

Consider, by the homogeneous condition on
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
and the single-valuedness of
\psi
on the interior mesh faces,

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})\psi=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}(({\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})\psi.

By a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the continuous trace inequality (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}(({\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})\psi\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}\|_{{\partial{T}}}\|\psi\|_{{\partial{T}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}\|_{{\partial{T}}}\Big(\|\psi\|_{T}+h_{T}\|\nabla\psi\|_{T}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}\|_{{\partial{T}}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(3.7)

where the final inequality results from a discrete Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and
h_{T}\lesssim 1
. We also have

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\int_{T}(\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}}_{T})\psi\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}\|\psi\|_{T}\leq\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(3.8)

Substituting (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) into (
3.6
) yields

\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(3.9)

As
\int_{\Omega}\psi=0
, the following Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality holds:
\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim|\psi|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}
.
Thus, we infer from (
3.9
) that
\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}
and the proof of (
3.5
) follows by simplifying by
\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

For all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
it holds that

\|{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)^{3}}\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

(3.10)

Proof.

We use a triangle inequality to write
\|{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq\|{\bm{v}}_{h}-\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)^{3}}
and invoke then Lemma
7
to bound the first addend, while for the second we use the fact that
\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}
is constant together with (
3.5
) to write
\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)^{3}}=|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{2}}\|\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}
.
∎

We note that as a result of Lemma
9
,
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}
and
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
are Banach spaces equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,h}
. While this is a standard result on the space
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}
, to the best of our knowledge the result on
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
is novel. In particular, we note the existence of a Poincaré constant
C_{p}>0
independent of
h
such that, for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
,

\|{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{\Omega}\leq C_{p}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

(3.11)

We conclude our series of discrete functional analysis results on
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
by establishing a discrete Rellich compactness result in that space, equivalent of
[
18
, Theorems 6.8 and 6.41]
in the case of Neumann or Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Theorem 10

(Discrete Rellich theorem in
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{h})_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
be a regular mesh sequence as per Assumption
1
and, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, let
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
. Assume
that
(\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h})_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
is bounded. Then, there exists
{\bm{v}}\in\bm{B}
such that, up to a subsequence as
h\to 0
,

(i)

{\bm{v}}_{h}\to{\bm{v}}
and
{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{h}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\to{\bm{v}}
in
L^{s}(\Omega)^{3}
for all
s\in[1,6)
,

(ii)

\nabla_{h}{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{h}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\to\nabla{\bm{v}}
and, for all integers
l\geq 0
,
\bm{G}_{h}^{l}\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\to\nabla{\bm{v}}
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{3\times 3}
,

where
\bm{G}_{h}^{l}:\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{l}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3\times 3}
is defined by
\bm{G}_{h}^{l}\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}|_{T}=\bm{G}_{T}^{l}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}
and all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Proof.

The proof uses the corresponding compactness result
[
18
, Theorem 6.8]
for Neumann boundary conditions (as well as the straightforward adaptation of
[
18
, Theorem 9.29]
, stated for Dirichlet boundary conditions, to Neumann boundary conditions). These theorems are established under more restrictive mesh assumptions than Assumption
1
but, following the approach described in the proof of Lemma
7
and using
[
25
, Lemma B.27]
, they can easily be established under our notion of regular mesh sequence.

Define
\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
by (
3.4
) and set
\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}=\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}-\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}
. We have
\|\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{1,h}=\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}
and
\int_{\Omega}{\bm{w}}_{h}=\int_{\Omega}({\bm{v}}_{h}-\overline{\bm{v}}_{h})=\bm{0}
. Hence, applying
[
18
, Theorems 6.8 and 9.29]
with
p=2
to each component of
\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}
, we find
{\bm{w}}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{3}
such that the convergences (i)–(ii) hold with all letters
{\bm{v}}
replaced by
{\bm{w}}
, and such that
\bm{\gamma}_{h}\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\to{\bm{w}}_{|\partial\Omega}
, where the discrete trace operator
\bm{\gamma}_{h}:\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}\to L^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{3}
is defined by: for all
\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}
,
(\bm{\gamma}_{h}\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})_{|F}={\bm{z}}_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm{b}}}
.

Since
(\overline{\bm{v}}_{h})_{h}
is also bounded by (
3.5
), upon extracting a subsequence from the previous one we can assume that
\overline{\bm{v}}_{h}\to\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
as
h\to 0
and thus, setting
{\bm{v}}={\bm{w}}+\bm{\xi}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{3}
, we obtain the convergences (i)–(ii), together with
\bm{\gamma}_{h}\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\to{\bm{v}}_{|\partial\Omega}
. For all
h\in\mathcal{H}
we have
\bm{\gamma}_{h}\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0
on
\partial\Omega
(since
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
), which proves by passing to the limit that
{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0
on the boundary and thus that
{\bm{v}}\in\bm{B}
.
∎

Lemma 11

(Boundedness of the interpolator)
.

It holds

\|\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{v}}\|_{1,T}\lesssim\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{T}\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in H^{1}(T)^{3}.

Proof.

The proof follows
[
18
, Proposition 2.2]
; we just provide some details to show that the relaxed mesh regularity assumptions and change of discrete norm, compared to this reference, do not impact the result. Using the boundedness
\|\nabla{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\|_{T}\lesssim\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{T}
(see
[
18
, Eq. (1.77)]
) we have

\|\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{v}}\|_{1,T}^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\|{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\|_{\partial T}^{2}.

[
26
, Lemma 7]
shows that
\|{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\|_{\partial T}\lesssim h_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla{\bm{v}}\|_{T}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

3.2
Properties of the discrete forms

We state here the design properties of each of the bilinear forms
{\rm{a}}_{h}
and
{\rm{d}}_{h}
, and the trilinear form
{\rm{t}}_{h}
. Most of these properties have already been obtained under the assumption of zero Dirichlet boundary conditions in
[
18
, Chapters 8, 9]
. However, we require several of these results with boundary conditions that either only impose vanishing normal components, and/or vanishing tangential curl of vector fields (see (
1.1g
), (
1.1f
)). Moreover, another difference with respect to
[
18
, Chapters 8, 9]
is that we consider here weaker assumptions on the meshes, and that the discrete norm defined by (
2.1
) differs from that of
[
18
, Eq. (8.15)]
due to the change of scaling
h_{F}^{-1}\to h_{T}^{-1}
. We therefore provide proofs of these results, focusing on the modifications coming from the different boundary conditions and mesh assumptions.

Proposition 12

(Properties of
{\rm{a}}_{h}
)
.

The discrete bilinear form
{\rm{a}}_{h}
satisfies the following properties:

(A1)

Stability and Boundedness. There exists a
C_{{\rm{a}}}\geq 0
independent of
h
such that, for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}
,

C_{{\rm{a}}}^{-1}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}\leq{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})\leq C_{{\rm{a}}}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}.

(3.12)

(A2)

Consistency.
For all
{\bm{w}}\in\bm{B}\cap H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}
such that
\Delta{\bm{w}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and
{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}\times(\nabla\times{\bm{w}})=\bm{0}
on
\partial\Omega
, and for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
,

\left|-\int_{\Omega}\Delta{\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{v}}_{h}-{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})\right|\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}h^{k+1}|{\bm{w}}|_{H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}.

(3.13)

Proof.

The stability and boundedness
(A1)
follows trivially from the properties of
{\rm{a}}_{T}
stated in Assumption (
2.5
). Let us turn to
(A2)
.
An integration by parts in each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
yields

-\int_{\Omega}\Delta{\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{v}}_{h}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[\int_{T}\nabla{\bm{v}}_{T}:\nabla{\bm{w}}-\int_{{\partial{T}}}{\bm{v}}_{T}\cdot({\bm{n}}_{{T}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\Big].

Let us also consider

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}\cdot({\bm{n}}_{{T}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{T}}\int_{F}{\bm{v}}_{F}\cdot({\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{F}}\int_{F}{\bm{v}}_{F}\cdot({\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{{\rm{b}}}}\int_{F}{\bm{v}}_{F}\cdot({\bm{n}}_{{F}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}=0,

where the integrals on each of the internal faces have cancelled in the third line due to continuity of
(\nabla{\bm{w}}){\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}
(since
\Delta{\bm{w}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and
{\bm{w}}\in H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}
), and the conclusion follows from the boundary conditions on
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}
and
{\bm{w}}
, and equation (
1.3
). Therefore, we may write

-\int_{\Omega}\Delta{\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{v}}_{h}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[\int_{T}\nabla{\bm{v}}_{T}:\nabla{\bm{w}}+\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{v}}_{T})\cdot({\bm{n}}_{T}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\Big].

(3.14)

By the commutation property (
2.4
), and invoking the definition (
2.2
) of
{\bm{{\rm r}}}_{T}^{k+1}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})={}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[\int_{T}\nabla{\bm{v}}_{T}:\nabla{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{1,k+1}{\bm{w}}+\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{v}}_{T})\cdot({\bm{n}}_{T}\cdot\nabla){\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{1,k+1}{\bm{w}}\Big]

(3.15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}).

Thus, subtracting (
3.15
) from (
3.14
), noting the
H^{1}
-orthogonality of
{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{1,k+1}
,

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\Delta{\bm{w}}{}

\displaystyle\cdot{\bm{v}}_{h}-{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{v}}_{T})\cdot({\bm{n}}_{T}\cdot\nabla)({\bm{w}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{1,k+1}{\bm{w}})-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{{\partial{T}}}\Big(|{\bm{w}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{1,k+1}{\bm{w}}|_{H^{1}(T)}+h_{T}|{\bm{w}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{1,k+1}{\bm{w}}|_{H^{2}(T)}\Big)

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{w}})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\rm{s}}_{T}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{T},\underline{\bm{v}}_{T})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we have invoked a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the continuous trace inequality (
3.1
). The proof then follows from a discrete Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the volumetric approximation properties
[
18
, Theorem 1.48]
of the elliptic projector, the consistency property (
2.6
) of the stabilisation form, and the norm equivalence (
2.5
).
∎

Proposition 13

(Properties of
{\rm{d}}_{h}
)
.

The discrete bilinear form
{\rm{d}}_{h}
satisfies the following properties:

(D1)

Inf-sup stability. There exists a
C_{{\rm{d}}}>0
independent of
h
such that, for all
q_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

C_{{\rm{d}}}^{-1}\|q_{h}\|_{\Omega}\leq\sup_{\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{\underline{\bm{0}}\}}\frac{{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}}\leq\sup_{\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}\backslash\{\underline{\bm{0}}\}}\frac{{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}}.

(3.16)

(D2)

Consistency. For all
s\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
,

\left|\int_{\Omega}{\bm{v}}_{h}\cdot\nabla s-{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\pi_{h}^{0,k}s)\right|\\
\lesssim h^{k+1}|s|_{H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

(3.17)

Proof.

The first inequality in
(D1)
is established in
[
18
, Lemma 8.12]
, for a slightly different discrete
H^{1}
-norm with the scaling
h_{T}^{-1}
in (
2.1
) replaced by local face-based scaling
h_{F}^{-1}
. This change actually only impacts, in this proof, the boundedness of the interpolator
\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}
, which we established in Lemma
11
for the norm with the scaling
h_{T}^{-1}
.
The second inequality in
(D1)
follows from the inclusion
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\subset\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
.

We now turn to
(D2)
. It follows from the definition (
2.9
) of
{\rm{d}}_{h}
and integrating by parts the defining equation (
2.3
) of
{\rm{D}}_{T}^{k}
that

{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\pi_{h}^{0,k}s)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\int_{T}{\rm{D}}_{T}^{k}\underline{\bm{v}}_{T}\pi_{T}^{0,k}s=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[-\int_{T}(\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}}_{T})\pi_{T}^{0,k}s+\int_{\partial T}({\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}}\pi_{T}^{0,k}s\Big].

(3.18)

Since
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
and
s\in H^{1}(\Omega)
we have

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})s=0.

Hence, integrating by parts on each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and introducing the term above,

\int_{\Omega}{\bm{v}}_{h}\cdot\nabla s=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[-\int_{T}(\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}}_{T})s+\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}}s\Big].

(3.19)

Therefore, combining equations (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), and noting that
s-\pi_{T}^{0,k}s
is
L^{2}(T)
-orthogonal to
\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}}_{T}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(T)
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}{\bm{v}}_{h}\cdot\nabla s-{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\pi_{h}^{0,k}s)\Big|={}

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}({\bm{v}}_{T}-{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}}(s-\pi_{T}^{0,k}s)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\bm{v}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{v}}_{T}\|_{{\partial{T}}}\Big(\|s-\pi_{T}^{0,k}s\|_{{T}}+h_{T}\|\nabla(s-\pi_{T}^{0,k}s)\|_{{T}}\Big)

where we have applied a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the integral and invoked the continuous trace inequality (
3.1
). The proof then follows by invoking the volumetric approximation properties
[
18
, Theorem 1.45]
of
\pi_{T}^{0,k}
and applying a discrete Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to the sum.
∎

Proposition 14

(Properties of
{\rm{t}}_{h}
)
.

The discrete trilinear form
{\rm{t}}_{h}
satisfies the following properties:

(T1)

Skew-symmetry. For all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}

{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})=-{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}).

(3.20)

(T2)

Boundedness. There exists
C_{{\rm{t}}}\geq 0
independent of
h
such that, for all
\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
,

|{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})|\leq C_{{\rm{t}}}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

(3.21)

(T3)

Consistency. For all
{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}}\in W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}\cap W^{k+1,4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}
such that
\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}}=0
and
{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}=0
and for all
\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}
,

\left|\int_{\Omega}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{h}-{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{v}},\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})\right|\\
\lesssim\|\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\|_{1,h}h^{k+1}\Big(\|{\bm{v}}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}}|{\bm{w}}|_{W^{k+1,4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}+\|{\bm{w}}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}}|{\bm{v}}|_{W^{k+1,4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}\Big).

(3.22)

Proof.

The skew-symmetry
(T1)
is trivial from the definitions (
2.10
) of
{\rm{t}}_{h}
and (
2.8
) of
{\rm{t}}_{T}
.
The proof of
(T2)
is done as in
[
18
, Proposition 9.17]
(see also Lemma 9.15 therein), using generalised Hölder inequalities with exponents
(4,2,4)
, the boundedness
[
18
, Eq. (9.37)]
of
\bm{G}_{T}^{2k}
(which is easily checked to hold with the modified discrete norm (
2.1
)), and the Poincaré–Sobolev inequality (
3.10
).

We now turn to
(T3)
. It follows the integration by parts formula (
3.48
) with
\Omega
replaced by a generic
T
that

\int_{\Omega}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{h}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[\int_{T}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{T}-\int_{T}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{z}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{w}}+\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})({\bm{z}}_{T}-{\bm{z}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}})\cdot{\bm{w}}\Big],

where we justify including the term

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}}){\bm{z}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}\cdot{\bm{w}}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{F}}\int_{F}({\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}){\bm{z}}_{F}\cdot{\bm{w}}=0

by the single valuedness of
{\bm{v}}
and
{\bm{w}}
on each interior face, and
{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}{F}}=0
for all
F\subset\partial\Omega
. The discrete trilinear form
{\rm{t}}_{h}
may be expanded using equation (
2.7
) as

\displaystyle{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{v}},\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big[\int_{T}\Big(({\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{G}_{T}^{2k})\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{T}-({\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{z}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{w}}\Big)

\displaystyle{}-\int_{{\partial{T}}}({\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})({\bm{z}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{z}}_{T})\cdot{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{w}}\Big].

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{h}-{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{v}},\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\Big[({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{T}-({\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\cdot\bm{G}_{T}^{2k})\underline{\bm{I}}_{T}^{k}{\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{T}\Big]

\displaystyle{}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\Big[(({\bm{v}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}})\cdot\nabla){\bm{z}}_{T}\cdot{\bm{w}}+({\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{z}}_{T}\cdot({\bm{w}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{w}})\Big]

\displaystyle{}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{{\partial{T}}}\Big[(({\bm{v}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}})\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})({\bm{z}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{z}}_{T})\cdot{\bm{w}}+({\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{{T}})({\bm{z}}_{{\mathcal{F}_{{T}}}}-{\bm{z}}_{T})\cdot({\bm{w}}-{\bm{\pi}}_{T}^{0,k}{\bm{w}})\Big].

The conclusion follows as in
[
18
, Proposition 9.17]
, using the consistency of
\bm{G}_{T}^{2k}
(Lemma 9.15 in this reference), the Poincaré–Sobolev inequality (
3.10
), the continuous trace inequality (
3.1
), and the approximation properties of the

-projectors
[
18
, Theorem 1.45]
.

3.3
Consistency errors

For all
({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s)\in\bm{U}\times\bm{B}\times P\times P
, the kinetic consistency error
\mathcal{E}_{k,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\cdot):\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
and magnetic consistency error
\mathcal{E}_{m,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\cdot):\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined via

\mathcal{E}_{k,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})=\nu_{k}\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{a}},h}({\bm{v}};\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{t}},h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}});\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})-\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{t}},h}(({\bm{w}},{\bm{w}});\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{d}},h}(p;\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})

(3.23)

and

\mathcal{E}_{m,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})=\nu_{m}\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{a}},h}({\bm{w}};\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{t}},h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}});\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})-\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{t}},h}(({\bm{w}},{\bm{v}});\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})+\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{d}},h}(s;\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})

(3.24)

where the linear forms
\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{a}},h}({\bm{v}};\cdot):\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{d}},h}(s;\cdot):\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{t}},h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}});\cdot):\underline{\bm{U}}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined as

\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{a}},h}({\bm{v}};\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})\vcentcolon=-\int_{\Omega}\Delta{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{h}-{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{v}},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}),

\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{d}},h}(s;\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})\vcentcolon=\int_{\Omega}{\bm{z}}_{h}\cdot\nabla s-{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{z}}_{h},\pi_{h}^{0,k}s),

and

\mathcal{E}_{{\rm{t}},h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}});\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})\vcentcolon=\int_{\Omega}({\bm{v}}\cdot\nabla){\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{h}-{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{v}},\underline{\bm{I}}_{h}^{k}{\bm{w}},\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}).

Theorem 15

(Consistency error)
.

Suppose that
({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s)\in\bm{U}\times\bm{B}\times P\times P
satisfy the additional regularity:
{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}}\in H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}\cap W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}
,
\Delta{\bm{v}},\Delta{\bm{w}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
,
\nabla\cdot{\bm{v}}=\nabla\cdot{\bm{w}}=0
,
{\bm{n}}_{\Omega}\times(\nabla\times{\bm{w}})=\bm{0}
on
\partial\Omega
, and
p,s\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. Then the kinetic consistency error
\mathcal{E}_{k,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\cdot)
and magnetic consistency error
\mathcal{E}_{m,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\cdot)
satisfy the following estimates:

\sup_{\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\backslash\{\underline{\bm{0}}\}}\frac{|\mathcal{E}_{k,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})|}{\|\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\|_{1,h}}\lesssim h^{k+1}\Big[\nu_{k}|{\bm{v}}|_{H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}}|{\bm{v}}|_{W^{k+1,4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}\\
+\|{\bm{w}}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}}|{\bm{w}}|_{W^{k+1,4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}+|p|_{H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\Big],

(3.25)

and

\sup_{\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}\backslash\{\underline{\bm{0}}\}}\frac{|\mathcal{E}_{m,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})|}{\|\underline{\bm{z}}_{h}\|_{1,h}}\lesssim h^{k+1}\Big[\nu_{m}|{\bm{w}}|_{H^{k+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}+\|{\bm{v}}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}}|{\bm{w}}|_{W^{k+1,4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}\\
+\|{\bm{w}}\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)^{3}}|{\bm{v}}|_{W^{k+1,4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}}+|s|_{H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\Big].

(3.26)

Proof.

The proof follows trivially by a triangle inequality on the terms
|\mathcal{E}_{k,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})|
and
|\mathcal{E}_{m,h}(({\bm{v}},{\bm{w}},p,s);\underline{\bm{z}}_{h})|
, and applying the estimates (
3.13
) (and
[
18
, Eq. (8.32)]
for the Dirichlet boundary conditions on
{\bm{v}}
), (
3.17
) and (
3.22
).

3.4
Stability of the scheme

The existence of a solution to the scheme and its uniqueness and error estimates for small data all follow from a general stability result that we now establish. To simplify the presentation and proof of this result, we start by re-casting the variational formulation (
2.12
) in a more compact way: find
\underline{x}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}^{k}
such that

{\rm{A}}_{h}(\underline{x}_{h},\underline{y}_{h})+{\rm{T}}_{h}(\underline{x}_{h},\underline{x}_{h},\underline{y}_{h})={\rm{F}}_{h}(\underline{y}_{h})\quad\forall\underline{y}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}^{k},

(3.27)

where we have defined the linear form
{\rm{F}}_{h}:\bm{X}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, bilinear form
{\rm{A}}_{h}:\bm{X}_{h}^{k}\times\bm{X}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
and trilinear form
{\rm{T}}_{h}:\bm{X}_{h}^{k}\times\bm{X}_{h}^{k}\times\bm{X}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
by,
for
\underline{x}^{i}_{h}=(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{i},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{i},q^{i}_{h},r^{i}_{h})\in\bm{X}_{h}^{k}
(with
i=\sharp,\flat
) and
\underline{y}_{h}=(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h},s_{h},z_{h})\in\bm{X}_{h}^{k}
,

{\rm{F}}_{h}(\underline{y}_{h})=({\bm{f}},{\bm{v}}_{h})_{\Omega}+({\bm{g}},{\bm{w}}_{h})_{\Omega},

{\rm{A}}_{h}(\underline{x}^{\sharp}_{h},\underline{y}_{h})=\nu_{k}{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})+\nu_{m}{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})+{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},q_{h}^{\sharp})+{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{w}}_{h},r_{h}^{\sharp})-{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},s_{h})-{\rm{d}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp},z_{h})

and

{\rm{T}}_{h}(\underline{x}^{\sharp}_{h},\underline{x}^{\flat}_{h},\underline{y}_{h})={\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})-{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{v}}_{h})+{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})-{\rm{t}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}).

Simple algebra shows that

{\rm{A}}_{h}(\underline{x}^{\sharp}_{h},\underline{x}^{\sharp}_{h})=\nu_{k}{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp})+\nu_{m}{\rm{a}}_{h}(\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp})=\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp})\|_{{\rm{a}},{\rm{a}},h}^{2},

(3.28)

where
\|(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{{\rm{a}},{\rm{a}},h}
is defined by (
2.17
) and, using the skew-symmetry (
3.20
) of
{\rm{t}}_{h}
, that

{\rm{T}}_{h}(\underline{x}_{h},\underline{y}_{h},\underline{y}_{h})=0.

(3.29)

We define the norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{1,1,h}
on
\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\times\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}
by

\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})\|_{1,1,h}=(\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2}+\|\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{1,h}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\forall(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\times\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}

and, for a linear mapping
{\rm{G}}:\bm{X}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
depending only on the first two components (such as
{\rm{F}}_{h}
above), the dual norm by

\|{\rm{G}}\|_{\bm{X},\star}\vcentcolon=\sup\left\{\frac{{\rm{G}}((\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h},0,0))}{\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})\|_{1,1,h}}\,:\,(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})\in\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,0}^{k}\times\underline{\bm{U}}_{h,{\bm{n}}}^{k}\backslash\{(\underline{0},\underline{0})\}\right\}.

Lemma 16

(Boundedness of
{\rm{T}}_{h}
)
.

It holds, for all
\underline{x}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{x}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{y}_{h}\in\bm{X}_{h}^{k}
,

|{\rm{T}}_{h}(\underline{x}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{x}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{y}_{h})|\leq\sqrt{2}C_{{\rm{t}}}\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp})\|_{1,1,h}\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat})\|_{1,1,h}\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})\|_{1,1,h}.

(3.30)

Proof.

Using (
3.21
) we have

\displaystyle C_{{\rm{t}}}^{-1}|{\rm{T}}_{h}(\underline{x}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{x}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{y}_{h})|\leq{}

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}+\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle+\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{1,h}+\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{1,h}.

(3.31)

We then use Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities to write

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}+\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}\leq{}

\displaystyle\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp})\|_{1,1,h}\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat})\|_{1,1,h},

\displaystyle\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}+\|\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp}\|_{1,h}\|\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat}\|_{1,h}\leq{}

\displaystyle\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp})\|_{1,1,h}\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat})\|_{1,1,h}.

Plugged into (
3.31
), this gives

C_{{\rm{t}}}^{-1}|{\rm{T}}_{h}(\underline{x}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{x}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{y}_{h})|\leq\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\sharp},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\sharp})\|_{1,1,h}\|(\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\flat},\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}^{\flat})\|_{1,1,h}\left(\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}+\|\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\right)

and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
\|\underline{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{1,h}+\|\underline{\bm{w}}_{h}\|_{1,h}\leq\sqrt{2}\|(\underline{\bm{v}}_{h},\underline{\bm{w}}_{h})\|_{1,1,h}
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 17

(Stability of the scheme)
.

3.30

We provide here a variety of numerical tests for the scheme (
2.12
) on two families of polyhedral meshes. The method is implemented using the

open source C++ library available at
https://github.com/jdroniou/HArDCore
. The nonlinear algebraic system is resolved via a Newton iterative scheme:

\bm{U}^{(n+1)}=\bm{U}^{(n)}+{\bm{\delta}}^{(n)};

iterate,

denotes the Newton step,

is a nonlinear function such that the system (

) corresponds to

, with Jacobian matrix denoted by

.
The system (
4.1
) is initialised with a vector of zeroes. At each step, we measure the discrete

norm of
{\rm{G}}(\bm{U}^{(n)})
and once its value relative to the initial value is less than

we exit the Newton scheme. At each iteration, the degrees–of–freedom (DOFs) of
\underline{\bm{u}}_{h}
and
\underline{\bm{b}}_{h}
in each element of the system (
4.1
), and all but one pressure DOF in each element are eliminated via static condensation
[
21
, Section 6.2]
. The resulting linear system is solved using the Pardiso solver found in the
Eigen
library (which internally invokes the Intel Math Kernel Library (Intel MKL)), with documentation available at
https://eigen.tuxfamily.org/dox/index.html
.

The domain is taken to be the unit cube
\Omega=(0,1)^{3}
. We consider source terms
{\bm{f}},{\bm{g}}
corresponding to the exact solution
({\bm{u}},{\bm{b}},q,r)
,
{\bm{u}}=(u_{i})_{i=1}^{3}

\displaystyle u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})={}

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have introduced and analysed a novel HHO method for the incompressible MHD equations. We consider a formulation of the problem with the Laplacian as the key differential operator for both the kinetic and magnetic equations. Such an approach allows the design of a scheme where the key spaces are
H^{1}
, and we show the method to be consistent in any polyhedral domain. The method locally preserves a discrete-divergence free constraint of the kinetic and magnetic unknowns. We prove uniqueness of the discrete solution and optimal approximation rates under a small data assumption. More generally, the existence of a discrete solution and convergence under general data is proven following a compactness approach. The paper is concluded with some numerical tests on polyhedral meshes.

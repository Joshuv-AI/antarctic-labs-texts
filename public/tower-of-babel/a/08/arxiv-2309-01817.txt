Invariants and reversibility in polynomial systems of ODEs
Mateja Grašič, Abdul Salam Jarrah, Valery G. Romanovski

Abstract
This paper explores a relationship between invariants of certain group actions and the time-reversibility of two-dimensional polynomial differential systems exhibiting a 1 : − 1 1:-1 resonant singularity at the origin. We focus on the connection of time-reversibility with the Sibirsky subvariety of the center (integrability) variety, which encompasses systems possessing a local analytic first integral near the origin. An algorithm for generating the Sibirsky ideal for these systems is proposed and the algebraic properties of the ideal are examined. Furthermore, using a generalization of the concept of time-reversibility we study n n -dimensional systems with a 1 : ζ : ζ 2 : … : ζ n − 1 1:\zeta:\zeta^{2}:\dots:\zeta^{n-1} resonant singularity at the origin, where n n is prime and ζ \zeta is a primitive n n -th root of unity. We study the invariants of a Lie group action on the parameter space of the system, leveraging the theory of binomial ideals as a fundamental tool for the analysis. Our study reveals intriguing connections between generalized reversibility, invariants, and binomial ideals, shedding light on their complex interrelations.



Keywords:
Polynomial systems of ODEs, invariants, time-reversibility, symmetries, normal forms, binomial ideals

Introduction

The theory of polynomial invariants provides a powerful framework for analyzing and understanding the behavior of solutions to differential equations. It offers insights into the global dynamics, stability, and structural properties of the solutions
and aids in classifying different types of differential equations based on their qualitative behavior. For instance, such invariants are
crucial for the recent classification of quadratic systems presented in
[
2
]
.
Furthermore, polynomial invariants can help to identify conservation laws
(which are first integrals from the mathematical point of view)
in physical and biochemical models
described by differential equations.

One of objectives of this work is to conduct a study of polynomial
invariants following the direction proposed in
[
16
,
25
,
26
]
. In these works, the authors focus on two-dimensional polynomial systems of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) that depend on parameters and can be represented in the form

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{(p,q)\in S}a_{pq}x^{p+1}y^{q},

(1)

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=-y+\sum_{(p,q)\in S}b_{qp}x^{q}y^{p+1}\,,

where
x
and
y
are complex variables (unknown functions).
The dot means the differentiation with respect to the variable
\tau
,
the coefficients
a_{pq},b_{qp}
are complex parameters, and

S=\{(p_{j},q_{j})\mid p_{j}+q_{j}\geq 1,j=1,\ldots,\ell\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{-1}\times\mathbb{N}_{0},

(2)

where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{-1}=\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{-1\}
.
The parameter space of (
1
) is
\mathbb{C}^{2\ell}
, and the ring of polynomials in the variables
a_{p_{1}q_{1}},\dots,a_{p_{\ell}q_{\ell}},b_{q_{\ell}p_{\ell}},\dots,b_{q_{1}p_{1}}
with coefficients in the field
\mathbb{C}
will be denoted by
\mathbb{C}[a,b]
.

Let
i=\sqrt{-1}
and
{\varphi}\in\mathbb{R}
or
{\varphi}\in\mathbb{C}
. The action of the one-parameter group of transformations of the phase space

x^{\prime}=e^{-i{\varphi}}x,\quad y^{\prime}=e^{i{\varphi}}y,

(3)

defines the corresponding action on the space
of parameters. Invariants of this group action were studied in
[
16
,
25
,
26
]
with a special focus on their connection to the problem of
analytic integrability of vector field (
1
). It was shown that the Lyapunov quantities
(also called saddle quantities, focus quantities
[
23
]
, and values of singular points
[
15
]
)
are elements of the polynomial subalgebra generated by these invariants. It was proved in
[
16
,
23
]

that the coefficients of normal forms of (
1
) have similar properties.
These properties are valuable for the study of integrability, limit cycle bifurcations, and critical period bifurcations, as demonstrated in works such as
[
9
,
13
,
14
]
, along with the references provided therein.

Polynomial invariants are closely related to symmetries of differential equations.
In this study, we specifically focus on what is known as the time-reversible symmetry, a fundamental symmetry with significant relevance in various applications (see, for example,
[
1

3
,

35

and the references given there). Additionally, we explore a generalization of this symmetry that was introduced in

For the

For some families of systems (

[

,
17
,
34

). In this paper we will consider an
interconnection of invariants of group (

) and the time-reversibility in system (

[

,
22
,
23
,
25
,
26
]

For system (
1
),
our study begins by examining the invariants of a group associated with (
3
), which results in an algorithm for computing a basis of these invariants. Furthermore, we discuss some algebraic properties of the invariants in view of the theory of binomial ideals. In particular, we show that a binomial ideal generated
by the invariants, the so-called Sibirsky ideal of system (

is the lattice ideal and it defines the set of time-reversible systems in the space of parameters of (

). Then we investigate similar problems for
the
n
-dimensional polynomial systems
of the form

\dot{\bf x}=\mathcal{Z}{\bf x}+X({\bf x})=F({\bf x}),

where

It was shown in

Under some conditions on the nonlinear system

of (

One of such conditions is the generalized

Clearly, the algebra of polynomial first integrals

Thus the study of local integrability of system

For system (
6
),
we first investigate polynomial invariants of the
action of the Lie group of transformations

\displaystyle{\bf y}=e^{\mathcal{Z}\psi}{\bf x}

(12)

on system (
6
), where
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
and

is a real or complex parameter, and give an algorithm to compute
a basis of the polynomial subalgebra of these invariants. Then we show that the coefficients of the Poincaré-Dulac normal form are polynomials of the polynomial subalgebra of these invariants.

Finally, we study properties
of system (
6
) related to the reversibility
and their connection to the invariants. In particular, we define
a generalization of the Sibirsky ideal for a family of systems of the form (

). Unlike the two-dimensional case, it is not a lattice ideal in general.
The main result of the paper is

4.20
which establishes a simple relation between the ideal defining the set of invariants and the ideal defining
the set of generalized reversible systems (which is a subvariety of the integrability variety). To prove the theorem we introduce a generalization of toric ideals.

and is called the toric
ring of
\mathfrak{A}.
The kernel of
\pi
is denoted
I_{\mathfrak{A}}
and called the

of
\mathfrak{A}
.

The Lawrence lifting of an integer matrix

is the matrix

\Lambda(\mathfrak{A})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{A}&0\\
E_{n}&E_{n}\end{pmatrix},

where

denotes the

identity matrix. It is clear that the

\mathcal{G}
returned by Algorithm

defines the toric ideal

, which is the kernel of homomorphism (

).

Denote by
\mathfrak{M}
the
1\times 2\ell
matrix on the left hand side of (

), that is,

\mathfrak{M}=[(p_{1}-q_{1})\ \dots\ (p_{\ell}-q_{\ell})\ (q_{\ell}-p_{\ell})\ \dots\ (q_{1}-p_{1})].

(35)

Then the kernal of the homomorphism given by

a_{p_{s}q_{s}}\mapsto y_{s}t^{q_{s}-p_{s}},\quad b_{q_{s}p_{s}}\mapsto y_{2\ell-s+1}t^{p_{s}-q_{s}},\qquad s=1,\dots,\ell,\quad y_{i}\mapsto y_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,2\ell,

is the toric ideal

.

For
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{2\ell})
, we denote by

the monomial
y_{1}^{\nu_{1}}y_{2}^{\nu_{2}}\ldots y_{2\ell}^{\nu_{2\ell}}
.
We are interested in the
Hilbert basis
H_{\mathfrak{M}}
of the monoid
\mathcal{M}
of solutions to equation (

).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{M}}
be the Gröbner basis of
I_{\Lambda(\mathfrak{M})}

computed according to Algorithm
3.1
, in particular
Y=\left\{[\nu]-{\bf y}^{\nu}\ :\ \nu\in H_{\mathfrak{M}}\right\}
is a subset of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{M}}
.
Then
{G}=\{[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]\ :\ \nu\in H_{\mathfrak{M}},\nu\neq\hat{\nu}\}
is a Gröbner basis of
I_{S}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
{G}
is a
basis of the ideal
I_{S}
.
Let
[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]\neq 0
(
[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]\not\in G
) be an element of the generating set

\{[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]\ :\ \nu\in\mathcal{M}\}

of the ideal
{I}_{S}
.
We show that
[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]\in\langle G\rangle.

Since
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
,
H_{\mathfrak{M}}
is the Hilbert basis of
\mathcal{M}

and
\mu\neq{\hat{\mu}}
,
there exists a
\nu\in H_{\mathfrak{M}}
such that
[\nu]\mid[\mu]
. Let
\sigma_{1}=\mu-\nu.
Then
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{M}
and

[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]=\frac{1}{2}([\sigma_{1}]+[\hat{\sigma}_{1}])([\nu]-[\hat{\nu}])+\frac{1}{2}([\nu]+[\hat{\nu}])([\sigma_{1}]-[\hat{\sigma}_{1}]),

(37)

where
\sigma_{1}<\mu
with respect to the degree lexicographic monomial order (the deglex).

If
[\sigma_{1}]-[\hat{\sigma}_{1}]\in G
, then

[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]\in\langle G\rangle.

If not, there is a
\nu_{1}\in H_{\mathfrak{M}}
such that

[\nu_{1}]|[\sigma_{1}]
. Letting
\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}-\nu_{1}
and continuing the process we obtain a sequence

\mu>\sigma_{1}>\sigma_{2}>\sigma_{3}>\dots.

(38)

If for some
\sigma_{k}
it holds
[\sigma_{k}]-[\hat{\sigma}_{k}]\in G
, we are done.
Otherwise the sequence (
38
) is infinite, which is impossible since the deglex is a term order
and, therefore, it is a well-ordering.
Thus,
G
is a basis for
I_{S}
.

Since all monomials appearing in
G
are relatively prime,
G
is a
Gröbner basis of
I_{S}
(see e.g. Corollary 1.30 in
[
10
]
).
∎

The set
G
contains both binomials
[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]
and
[\hat{\nu}]-[\nu]
. For every such pair of binomials, we delete from
G
the binomial for which the leading coefficient is
-1
and denote the obtained set by
{G}_{R}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The set
{G}_{R}
is a reduced Gröbner basis of
{I}_{S}
.

Proof.

Assume that for
[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]\in{G}_{R}
there exists
[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]\in{G}_{R}
such that

[\nu]\mid[\mu]\quad{\rm or}\quad[\nu]\mid{[\hat{\mu}]}.

(39)

By the definition of the set
G_{R}
, these
\nu,\mu,{\hat{\mu}}
are in the Hilbert basis of
\mathcal{M}
, thus
(
39
) contradicts the minimality of the Hilbert basis.
∎

When we pass from
Y
to
G
, the
self-conjugate
binomials, that is, binomials for which
\nu=\hat{\nu}
, disappear.
Hence, the Hilbert basis
H_{\mathfrak{M}}
is the union of the exponents that appear in
G_{R}
and the exponents of the self-conjugate binomials,

{H_{\mathfrak{M}}}=\{\nu,\hat{\nu}\,:\ [\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]\in G_{R}\}\cup\{\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{2\ell-j+1}\,:\ j=1,\ldots,\ell\ {\rm and}\ \pm([\mathbf{e}_{j}]-[\mathbf{e}_{2\ell-j+1}])\notin{G_{R}}\}.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]\in{G}_{R}
. Then there is no
\theta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2\ell}
such that

{[\theta]}\,\mid\left([\nu]-[{\hat{\nu}}]\right).

(40)

Proof.

We note that for any monomial
[\kappa]
the exponent of
GCD([\kappa],[\hat{\kappa}])
is a self-conjugate element of
\mathcal{M}
. This holds since
GCD([\kappa],[\hat{\kappa}])=[\mu]
, where
\mu_{i}=\min\{\kappa_{i},\kappa_{2\ell-i+1}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,2\ell
. Thus
\mu_{i}=\mu_{2\ell-i+1}
for all
i=1,\ldots,\ell
yielding
\mu=\hat{\mu}\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

Assume there exist
\nu\in H_{\mathfrak{M}}
and
\kappa
such that

[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]={[\kappa]}\left([\mu]-[{\hat{\mu}}]\right)

for some
[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]
. Since
[\kappa]|[\nu]
and
[\kappa]|[\hat{\nu}]
,
[\hat{\kappa}]|[\nu]
and
[\hat{\kappa}]|[\hat{\nu}]
,
we conclude that

GCD([\kappa],[\hat{\kappa}])|[\nu].

Since the exponent of
GCD([\kappa],[\hat{\kappa}])
is in
\mathcal{M}

this contradicts to the minimality of
H_{\mathfrak{M}}
.
∎

We now discuss some algebraic properties of the ideal
I_{S}
.

For a field
k
, we denote by
k[{\bf x}]:=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

the polynomial ring in the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.
A subgroup
\mathsf{L}
of the free abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is called a
lattice
. By definition, a
basis
of a lattice
\mathsf{L}
is a basis of the free abelian group
\mathsf{L}
. For
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we write

m=m^{+}-m^{-},

where
m^{+},m^{-}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
, and denote by
f_{m}
the binomial

{\bf x}^{m^{+}}-{\bf x}^{m^{-}}
. The ideal of
k[{\bf x}]
generated by all binomials
f_{m}
, where

m\in\mathsf{L}
,
is the so-called
lattice ideal
of
\mathsf{L}

[
10
]
.

In our case
n=2\ell
and the polynomial ring is
\mathbb{C}[a,b]
. Let
\mathsf{L}
be the set consisting of all elements
\nu-\hat{\nu}
, where
\nu\in\mathcal{M}
. Since
\mathcal{M}
is a monoid closed under the action of involution on its elements,
\mathsf{L}
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2\ell}
.
Let us denote by
I_{\mathsf{L}}
the lattice ideal of
\mathsf{L}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

I_{S}=I_{\mathsf{L}}
.

Proof.

We first show that
I_{S}\subset I_{\mathsf{L}}
. If
f=[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]
is a binomial from the Gröbner basis
G_{R}
of
I_{S}
, then,
by Proposition
3.4
, the monomials
[\nu]
and
[\hat{\nu}]
have a disjoint support. Therefore
f\in I_{\mathsf{L}}
yielding

I_{S}\subset I_{\mathsf{L}}
.

To prove the opposite inclusion, let
\nu\in\mathcal{M}
. If

\nu
and
\hat{\nu}
have a disjoint support, then
f_{\nu-{\hat{\nu}}}=[\nu]-[\hat{\nu}]
, and hence
f_{\nu-{\hat{\nu}}}\in I_{S}
.
Otherwise, there is
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
and
\kappa\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\nu=\mu+\kappa
,
\hat{\nu}=\mu+\hat{\kappa}

and
\kappa
and
\hat{\kappa}
have a disjoint support.
But then
f_{\kappa-{\hat{\kappa}}}=[\kappa]-[\hat{\kappa}]\in I_{S}
. Therefore
f_{\nu-{\hat{\nu}}}=[\mu]([\kappa]-[\hat{\kappa}])\in I_{S}
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The Sibirsky ideal
I_{S}
is a lattice ideal.

The fact that

]
and

Proof of Theorem
4.20
.
According to Theorem
4.25
,
I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}:{\mathsf{a}}^{\infty}=I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}
. Furthermore,
I^{(n)}_{R}\subseteq I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}

\zeta^{|\mu|}[\mu]-[\hat{\mu}]\in I^{(n)}_{R}
, where

By Lemma
4.23

w(\hat{\mu})[\mu]-w(\mu)[\hat{\mu}]\in I^{(n)}_{R}
.
Moreover,

w(\hat{\mu})[\mu]-w(\mu)[\hat{\mu}]=w(\theta)[\theta](w(\gamma)[\tau]-w(\tau)[\gamma])

where
\theta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n\ell}
and
supp(\tau)\cap supp(\gamma)=\emptyset
. Let
\omega=\mu-\hat{\mu}
. Then
\omega^{+}=\tau
and
\omega^{-}=\gamma
.
Since
\mathfrak{M}^{(n)}\omega=0
and

\sum_{s=1}^{n}\omega_{n(i-1)+s}=\sum_{s=1}^{n}\mu_{n(i-1)+s}-\sum_{s=1}^{n}\hat{\mu}_{n(i-1)+s}=0\quad{\rm for}\quad i=1,\dots,\ell,

we have

\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}\omega=0
. Hence by Proposition
4.24
,
w(\hat{\mu})[\mu]-w(\mu)[\hat{\mu}]\in I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}
.
Thus,
I^{(n)}_{R}\subseteq I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}
, and, therefore,

I^{(n)}_{R}:{\mathsf{a}}^{\infty}\subseteq I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}:{\mathsf{a}}^{\infty}=I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}.

Next we show that
I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}\subseteq I^{(n)}_{R}:{\mathsf{a}}^{\infty}
. Let
w(\gamma)[\tau]-w(\tau)[\gamma]\in I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}
. Since
I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}:{\mathsf{a}}^{\infty}=I^{(\zeta)}_{\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}}
, without loss of generality, we assume that
supp(\tau)\cap supp(\gamma)=\emptyset
.

Now let
\omega\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n\ell}
be such that
\hat{\tau}+\hat{\omega}=\gamma+\omega
. To find such
\omega
, notice that the latter equation is equivalent to
\omega-\hat{\omega}=\hat{\tau}-\gamma,
so

n\ell\times n\ell

\mu=\tau+\omega

\hat{\mu}=\gamma+\omega
and
\mu-\hat{\mu}=\tau-\gamma
yielding
\mathfrak{A}^{{}^{(n)}}\mu=\mathfrak{A}^{{}^{(n)}}\hat{\mu}
. Next we prove that
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{(n)}
. To this end, notice that from the definition of matrix
\mathfrak{A}^{(n)}
it follows that,

1\leq i\leq n-1
,

(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\mu)=(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\hat{\mu}),

where
(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\mu)
is
L^{i}(\mu)-L^{i+1}(\mu)
. At the same time, it is easy to see that

(L^{i-1}-L^{i})(\mu)=(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\hat{\mu}),

for all
2\leq i\leq n-1
. Consequently,
for all
2\leq i\leq n-1

(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\mu)=(L^{i-1}-L^{i})(\mu).

(106)

\sum^{n-1}_{i=1}(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\mu)=(L^{1}-L^{n})(\mu)=-(L^{1}-L^{2})(\mu)

n(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\mu)=0

(L^{i}-L^{i+1})(\mu)=0

.

4.6

.

w(\hat{\mu})[\mu]-w(\mu)[\hat{\mu}]=w(\omega)[\omega]\left(w(\gamma)[\tau]-w(\tau)[\gamma]\right)\in I^{(n)}_{R}.

.

In conclusion, our study delves into the complex interplay between group actions, time-reversibility, integrability of polynomial systems of ODEs, and binomial ideal theory. Our finding not only shed lights on these issues but also lay the groundwork for future exploration in this important and promising direction of research.

It would be particularly interesting to extend our analysis to systems described by (

Abstract




Abstract.
We build up a quantitative
second-order
Sobolev estimate of
\ln w

for positive
p
-harmonic functions
w
in Riemannian manifolds under Ricci curvature bounded from
below
and also for positive weighted
p
-harmonic functions
w

in weighted manifolds under the Bakry-Émery curvature-dimension condition.

1
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold with dimension
n\geq 2
.
Suppose that the Ricci curvature is bounded from below, that is,

Ric_{g}\geq-\kappa
for some
\kappa\geq 0
. For any positive
harmonic function
w
in a domain
\Omega\subset M^{n}
, Cheng-Yau
[
3
]
established the
following famous gradient estimate:

|\nabla\ln w|=\frac{|\nabla w|}{w}\leq C(n)\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\quad\text{in $B(z,r)\subset B(z,2r)\subset\Omega$}.

(1.1)

Recall that a harmonic function
w
in
\Omega
is a weak solution to the Laplace equation

\Delta w:={\rm div}(\nabla w)=0\mbox{ in $\Omega$.}

We also refer to
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
for a quantitative
W^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
-regularity
of harmonic functions.

Motivated by the application in
the inverse mean curvature flow (see
[
12
,
16
]
), Cheng-Yau type gradient estimate was extended by
[
17
,
12
,
22
,
16
]
to
p
-harmonic functions in
\Omega
for
1<p<\infty
, that is,
weak solutions to the
p
-Laplace equation

\Delta_{p}w={\rm div}(|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w)=0\ \mbox{
in $\Omega$. }

Precisely, if
(M^{n},g)
is flat (that is,
the
Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) or its sectional curvature is bounded from below by
-\kappa
,
via Cheng-Yau’s approach Moser
[
17
]
and Kotschwar-Ni
[
12
]
showed that any positive
p
-harmonic function
w
in
\Omega
satisfies

|\nabla\ln w|\leq C(n)\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\quad\mbox{in $B(z,r)\subset B(z,2r)\subset\Omega$},

(1.2)

where the constant
C(n)>0
is independent of
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Under the Ricci curvature lower bound
Ric_{g}\geq-\kappa
, it was asked in
[
12
]
whether
(
1.2
) holds or not. Some progress was made as below.
Based on Cheng-Yau’s argument, Wang-Zhang
[
22
]
proved that

|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\gamma}<0}

(1.3)

and
the following weaker revision of (
1.2
):

|\nabla\ln w|\leq C(n,p)\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\quad\mbox{in $B(z,r)\subset B(z,2r)\subset\Omega$},

(1.4)

where the constant
C(n,p)>0
blows up as
p\to 1
.
Recently, with the aid of the fake distance coming from capacity,

C(n,p)
was proved by Mari-Rigoli-Setti
[
16
]
to be bounded by
\frac{n-1}{p-1}
as
p\to 1
.
Moreover, (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) were generalized to weighted manifolds
(M^{n},g,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g})
.
A weighted
p
-harmonic function
w
in a domain
\Omega\subset M^{n}
is a weak solution to the weighted
p
-harmonic equation

\Delta_{p,h}w:=e^{h}{\rm div}(e^{-h}|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w)=0\ \mbox{in $\Omega$}.

Under the Bakry-Émery curvature-dimension condition
Ric_{h}^{N}\geq-\kappa
for some
N\in[n,\infty)
and
\kappa\geq 0
(see Section 2 for details),
Dung-Dat
[
6
]
showed that if
w>0
, then
|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
with
{\gamma}<0
and also

\displaystyle|\nabla\ln w|\leq C(n,N,p)\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\quad\text{in $B(z,r)\subset B(z,2r)\subset\Omega$.}

(1.5)

The main aim of this paper is to
build up a quantitative second-order Sobolev estimate of
\ln w

for positive
p
-harmonic functions
w
in Riemannian manifolds under Ricci curvature
bounded from
below
and also for positive weighted
p
-harmonic functions
w

in weighted manifolds under the Bakry-Émery curvature-dimension condition. See Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 separately.
These improve the corresponding second-order Sobolev regularity in
[
22
,
6
]
mentioned above.

To be precise, under the Ricci curvature lower bound, we have the following result.
For convenience, below we write
{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 4.02504pt--\hss}\hskip 2.21939pt\int}\ignorespaces_{E}f\,dm
as the average of
f
in the set
E
with respect to the measure
m
, that is,
{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 4.02504pt--\hss}\hskip 2.21939pt\int}\ignorespaces_{E}f\,dm=\frac{1}{m(E)}\int_{E}f\,dm
. We use
C(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
to denote a positive constant depending on absolute constants
a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(M^{n},g)
satisfies
Ric_{g}\geq-\kappa
for some
\kappa\geq 0
.
Let
1<p<\infty
and
{\gamma}<3+\frac{p-1}{n-1}
.
For any positive
p
-harmonic function
w
in a domain
\Omega\subset M
,
we have
|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\nabla\ln w\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 4.02504pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{B(z,r)}\left|\nabla[|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\nabla\ln w]\right|^{2}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,{\gamma})\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\right]^{p-{\gamma}+4}e^{\sqrt{\kappa}r}

(1.6)

whenever
B(z,4r)\Subset\Omega
.

In particular, if
1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, then

\nabla^{2}\ln w\in L^{2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 4.02504pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{B(z,r)}|\nabla^{2}\ln w|^{2}\,d{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\right]^{4}e^{\sqrt{\kappa}r}

(1.7)

whenever
B(z,4r)\Subset\Omega
.

Here and throughout the paper for domains
A
and
B
, the notation
A\Subset B
stands for that
A

is a bounded subdomain of
B
and its closure
A\subset B
.

Recall that if
(M^{n},g)
is flat, that is, the
Euclidean
space
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
p
-harmonic functions
w
in a domain
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
are proved to satisfy

|\nabla w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\nabla w\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
with some quantative bound
whenever
{\gamma}<3+\frac{p-1}{n-1}

see
[
14
,
10
,
5
,
15
]
and also the
references
therein for some earlier partial results.
In particular, if
1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, noting
p<3+\frac{p-1}{n-1}
and taking
{\gamma}=p
, one has
w\in W^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
.
When
n\geq 3
and
p\geq 3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, it is not clear whether

w\in W^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
or not. When
n=2
, the range
{\gamma}<3+\frac{p-1}{n-1}=p+2
is optimal as
witnessed
by some construction in
[
10
]
.

Moreover, we extend Theorem
1.1
to weighted
manifolds
satisfying Bakry-Émery curvature-dimension condition,

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-h}{\rm vol}_{g})
be a
weighted manifold with
Ric_{h}^{N}\geq-\kappa
for some
n\leq N<\infty
and
\kappa\geq 0
.
Let

1<p<\infty
and
{\gamma}<3+\frac{p-1}{N-1}
.
For any positive weighted
p
-harmonic function
w
in a domain
\Omega\subset M
, we have

|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\nabla\ln w\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 4.02504pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{B(z,r)}\left|\nabla[|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\nabla\ln w]\right|^{2}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,N,p,{\gamma})\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\right]^{p-{\gamma}+4}e^{\sqrt{\kappa}r}\

(1.8)

whenever
B(z,4r)\Subset\Omega
.

In particular, if
p\in(1,3+\frac{2}{N-2})
, then

\nabla^{2}\ln w\in L^{2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle{\hbox to0.0pt{\bf\kern 4.02504pt--\hss}\int}\ignorespaces_{B(z,r)}|\nabla^{2}\ln w|^{2}\,d{\rm vol}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,N,p)\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{\kappa}r}{r}\right]^{4}e^{\sqrt{\kappa}r}

(1.9)

whenever
B(z,4r)\Subset\Omega
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, one gets that
|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}+2}{2}}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
for
{\gamma}<3+\frac{p-1}{n-1}
or
{\gamma}<3+\frac{p-1}{N-1}
,
while in
[
22
,
6
]
, one has
|\nabla\ln w|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}+2}{2}}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
for all
{\gamma}<2
(see (
1.3
) and the line above (
1.5
)). Thus our range for
{\gamma}
obviously improves the one obtained in
[
22
,
6
]
respectively.

Now we sketch the ideas to prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
.
Note that when
N=n
and
h\equiv 1
, we have
Ric_{h}^{N}=Ric_{g}
, and hence
Theorem
1.1
corresponds to the special case
N=n
and
h\equiv 1
in Theorem
1.2
.
We only need to prove Theorem
1.2
.
As usual, we approximate
u=-(p-1)\ln w
by smooth solution

to
the standard approximation/regularized equation (
3.3
), that is,

e^{h}{\rm div}(e^{-h}[|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\nabla u^{\epsilon})=[|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}.

(i)

Using Bochner formula and the approximation equation (
3.3
), for

we bound the
integral
of

(1-\eta)|\nabla^{2}u^{\epsilon}|^{2}+(p-{\gamma})\frac{|\nabla^{2}u^{\epsilon}\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}}{|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon}+(p-2)(2-{\gamma})\frac{(\Delta_{\infty}u^{\epsilon})^{2}}{[|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon]^{2}}

(1.10)

from above by
the
integral
of

Ric_{g}(\nabla u^{\epsilon},\nabla u^{\epsilon})+\langle\nabla^{2}h\nabla u^{\epsilon},\nabla u^{\epsilon}\rangle

and other first order
terms
, where all
integrals
are taken against
[|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\phi^{2}\,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g}

where

is a test function and
U\Subset\Omega
; see Lemma
3.2
.

Here in (
1.10
) and in what follows, for any
C^{2}
function
f
,
\Delta_{\infty}f:=\langle\nabla^{2}f\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle
.

(ii)

If

, via a fundamental inequality given in Lemma

and the approximation equation (
3.3
), for sufficiently small
\eta>0
we bound (

)

\eqref{xxx}\geq\eta|\nabla^{2}u^{\epsilon}|^{2}-\frac{\langle\nabla h,\nabla u^{\epsilon}\rangle^{2}}{N-n}-C\frac{1}{\eta}|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{4}\quad\mbox{ everywhere};

see Lemma
3.4
. This is crucial to get Theorem

. Note that the approach in
[

,
6
]
could not give Lemma

; see Remark

for details.

(iii)

Combining (i)&(ii) together, the

of

from above by
the
integral
of
-Ric_{h}^{N}(\nabla u^{\epsilon},\nabla u^{\epsilon})
and other first order
terms
,
where all
integrals
are taken against
[|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\phi^{2}\,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g}
; see Corollary

.

Under the assumption
Ric_{h}^{N}\geq-\kappa
, in Lemma
3.7
we obtain an upper

\nabla[|\nabla u^{\epsilon}|^{\frac{p-{\gamma}}{2}}\nabla u^{\epsilon}]\phi

all

are against
\,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g}
. A standard argument then leads to the proof of Theorem

.

Finally, we also notice that the Cheng-Yau gradient estimate (
1.1
)
was generalized to positive harmonic functions
w
in Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded from below by Zhang-Zhu in
[
23
]
,
where the authors showed
|\nabla\ln w|^{2}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
as a key step.
Furthermore, one could study the regularity of
p
-harmonic functions in more general metric measure spaces. In these spaces, a natural generalization of the (weighted) Ricci curvature bound is the curvature-dimension condition
RCD(\kappa,N)
in the sense of Bakry-Émery or Ambrosio-Gigli-Savaré. The two senses turned out to be equivalent by the work of Erbar-Kuwada-Sturm
[
7
]
(in the finite dimensional case) and Ambrosio-Gigli-Savaré
[
2
]
and the spaces satisfying one of the two equivalent conditions are known as
RCD(\kappa,N)
spaces.

Some progress was made in

RCD(\kappa,N)
spaces. The
Cheng-Yau gradient estimate was established by Jiang in
[
11
]
for positive harmonic functions
w
in
RCD(\kappa,N)
spaces;
recently, Gigli-Violo in
[
8
]
established
|\nabla\ln w|^{\beta/2}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
under
RCD(0,N)
spaces if
\beta>\frac{N-2}{N-1}
.
However, when
p\neq 2
, it remains open to prove the Cheng-Yau type gradient estimates for positive
p
-harmonic functions in Alexandrov spaces and
also
RCD(\kappa,N)
spaces.

2
Preliminaries

Let
n\geq 2
and
M^{n}
be a Riemannian manifold, and
g
be the Riemannian metric.
By abuse of notation we also write
|\xi|^{2}=g(\xi,\xi)
and
\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=g(\xi,\eta)
for all
\xi,\eta\in T_{x}M^{n}
.
The corresponding Riemannian volume measure is written as
d{\rm vol}_{g}
,
and the volume of a set
E
is written as
{\rm vol}_{g}(E)
.
Denote by
Ric_{g}
the Ricci curvature 2-tensor and write
Ric_{g}\geq-\kappa

if
Ric_{g}(\xi,\xi)\geq-\kappa|\xi|^{2}
for all
\xi\in T_{x}M^{n}
.

For
1<p<\infty
, the
p
-Laplace operator
\Delta_{p}
in
M^{n}
is given by

\Delta_{p}f={\rm div}(|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla f)\ \mbox{ $\forall f\in C^{2}(M^{n})$}.

Obviously,
\Delta_{2}
is exactly the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
in
(M^{n},g)
.
A function
w
defined in a domain
\Omega\subset M^{n}
is called
p
-harmonic if

w\in W^{1,p}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to the
p
-Laplace equation
\Delta_{p}w=0
in
\Omega
, that is,

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p-2}\langle\nabla w,\nabla\phi\rangle d{\rm vol}_{g}=0\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

Note that

2
-harmonic functions are the well-known harmonic functions.

Next we recall some basic facts of weighted Riemannian manifolds
(M^{n},g,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g})
, where the weight
h
is a positive smooth function in
M^{n}
.
The weighted measure
d{\rm vol}_{h}=e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g}
can be viewed as the volume form of a suitable conformal change of the metric
g
.
Denote by
{\rm vol}_{h}(E)
the weighted volume of a set
E
.
For
n\leq N<\infty
, the corresponding
N
-Bakry-Émery curvature tensor is

Ric^{N}_{h}=Ric_{g}+\nabla^{2}h-\frac{\nabla h\otimes\nabla h}{N-n},

where when
N=n
, by convention,
h
is
a
constant function and hence
Ric^{N}_{h}=Ric_{g}
. We say that
(M^{n},g,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g})
satisfies the Bakry-Émery curvature-dimension condition

Ric_{h}^{N}\geq-\kappa
if

Ric_{h}^{N}(\xi,\xi)=Ric_{g}(\xi,\xi)+\langle\nabla^{2}h\xi,\xi\rangle-\frac{\langle\nabla h,\xi\rangle^{2}}{N-n}\geq-\kappa\langle\xi,\xi\rangle\ \forall\xi\in T_{x}M^{n}

By
[
19
]
, under
Ric^{N}_{h}\geq-\kappa
, one has the following volume comparison result

{\rm vol}_{h}(B_{2r}(x))\leq C(N)e^{\sqrt{\kappa}r}{\rm vol}_{h}(B_{r}(x))\quad\forall x\in M,\ r>0.

(2.1)

For
1<p<\infty
, the weighted
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{h,p}
is defined as

\Delta_{p,h}f=e^{h}{\rm div}(e^{-h}|\nabla f|^{p-2}\nabla f)=\Delta_{p}f-|\nabla f|^{p-2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla h\rangle\quad\forall f\in C^{2}(M^{n}).

In the case
p=2
, one writes
\Delta_{2,h}
as
\Delta_{h}
,
and hence

\Delta_{h}f=\Delta f-\langle\nabla h,\nabla f\rangle.

A function
w
in a domain
\Omega\subset M^{n}
is called as a weighted
p
-harmonic function if

w\in W^{1,p}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to the weighted
p
-harmonic equation
\Delta_{p,h}w=0
in
\Omega
, that is,

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p-2}\langle\nabla w,\nabla\phi\rangle\,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g}=0\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega).

(2.2)

By a density argument, we can relax
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
to
\phi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
in (
2.2
).

We also recall the following Bochner formula in
(M^{n},g,e^{-h}d{\rm vol}_{g})
:

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}|\nabla f|^{2}=|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}+\langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_{h}f\rangle+Ric_{g}(\nabla f,\nabla f)+\langle\nabla^{2}h\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle\quad\forall f\in C^{3}(M),

(2.3)

which will be used in Section 3.

Finally, we recall the following fundamental inequality; see for example
[
22
,
6
,
15
]
. For
the reader’s convenience we include it
here.
Recall that
\Delta_{\infty}f=\langle\nabla^{2}f\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Omega
be a domain of
M^{n}
. For any
f\in C^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla f|^{4}|\nabla^{2}f|^{2}\geq 2|\nabla f|^{2}|\nabla^{2}f\nabla f|^{2}+\frac{[|\nabla f|^{2}\Delta f-\Delta_{\infty}f]^{2}}{n-1}-(\Delta_{\infty}f)^{2}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,

(2.4)

where when
n=2
, “
\geq
” becomes “
=
”.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for any
symmetric

n\times n
matrix
A
one has

\displaystyle|A|^{2}|\xi|^{4}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}({\rm tr}A|\xi|^{2}-\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle)^{2}+2|A\xi|^{2}|\xi|^{2}-\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle^{2}\quad\forall\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

(2.5)

Note that if
\xi=0
, (
2.5
) holds obviously.
Below assume that
\xi\neq 0
.
Up to a scaling we may assume
|\xi|=1
. By a change of coordinates,
we may further assume
\xi=e_{n}=(0,\cdots,0,1)
; in this case, (
2.5
) reads as

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}({\rm tr}A-\langle Ae_{n},e_{n}\rangle)^{2}+2|Ae_{n}|^{2}-\langle Ae_{n},e_{n}\rangle^{2}.

Denoting by
A_{n-1}
the
(n-1)
order principal submatrix of
A
, one has

|A|^{2}=|A_{n-1}|^{2}+2|Ae_{n}|^{2}-\langle Ae_{n},e_{n}\rangle^{2}.

Noting that

|A_{n-1}|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}({\rm tr}A_{n-1})^{2}=\frac{1}{n-1}({\rm tr}A-\langle Ae_{n},e_{n}\rangle)^{2},

where when
n=2
, one has
|A_{n-1}|^{2}=({\rm tr}A_{n-1})^{2}
, one concludes (
2.4
).
∎

3
Proof of Theorem

Let
w
be a positive weighted
p
-harmonic function in a domain
\Omega
.
Set

Given any smooth domain
U\Subset\Omega

(3.2)

It is well known that if
u
is the solution to (
3.1
), then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)

for some

; see
[
4
,
13
,
20
,
21
]
.
Moreover, in the following lemma,
we summarize some properties of the solution
u
to (
3.1
) and
u^{\epsilon}
to (
3.3
), which result from
[
4
]
as a special case. See also
[
20
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, there exists a unique solution
u^{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(U)\cap C^{0}(\overline{U})
to (
3.3
), and moreover,
u^{\epsilon}\to u
in
C^{0}(\overline{U})
and
u^{\epsilon}\to u
in

uniformly in

as
\epsilon\to 0
for all

where

is the solution to (

).

To show Lemma

, we just need to check that equations (
3.1
) and (

) are special cases of those considered in

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

It suffices to check equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are special ones of (
A.1
) and (
A.3
) respectively. To this end,
let
\vec{a}(x,\eta)=e^{-h(x)}|\eta|^{p-2}\eta
,
b(x,\eta)=-e^{-h(x)}|\eta|^{p}
,
\vec{a^{\epsilon}}(x,\eta)=e^{-h(x)}(|\eta|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\eta
, and
b^{\epsilon}(x,\eta)=-e^{-h(x)}(|\eta|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\eta|^{2}
for all
x\in U
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Then in the weak sense, the equations

\int_{\Omega}[\langle\vec{a}(x,\nabla u),\nabla\phi\rangle+b(x,\nabla u)\phi]\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=0,\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)

and

\int_{\Omega}[\langle\vec{a^{\epsilon}}(x,\nabla u),\nabla\phi\rangle+b^{\epsilon}(x,\nabla u)\phi]\,d{\rm vol}_{g}=0,\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)

are exactly (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) respectively.

We show
\vec{a}
satisfies (
A1
). Noting that
a_{j}(x,\eta)=e^{-h(x)}|\eta|^{p-2}\eta_{j}
, we compute

\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial\eta_{i}}(x,\eta)=e^{-h(x)}[(p-2)|\eta|^{p-4}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}+\delta_{ij}|\eta|^{p-2}],\quad\forall 1\leq i,j\leq n

where
\delta_{ij}=1
if
i=j
and
\delta_{ij}=0
if
i\neq j
.
Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial\eta_{i}}(x,\eta)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=e^{-h(x)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}[((p-2)|\eta|^{p-4}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}+\delta_{ij}|\eta|^{p-2})\xi_{i}\xi_{j}]

\displaystyle=e^{-h(x)}|\eta|^{p-4}[(p-2)(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}\xi_{i})^{2}+|\eta|^{2}|\xi|^{2}],\quad\forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

If
1<p<2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial\eta_{i}}(x,\eta)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-h(x)}(p-1)|\eta|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

And if
p\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial\eta_{i}}(x,\eta)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-h(x)}|\eta|^{p-2}|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

By taking
{\gamma}_{0}:=\min_{x\in\overline{U}}\{e^{-h(x)}\}
,
we conclude that
a
satisfies (
A1
).
By direct computations, one can also check
\vec{a},\vec{a^{\epsilon}}\in C^{\infty}(U\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

satisfy (
A2
)-(
A4
), (

) and (
A1,ϵ

A4,ϵ
) respectively. We omit the details.
Thus by Theorem
A.1
, we get the desired result.

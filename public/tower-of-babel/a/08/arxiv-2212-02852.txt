Impact of combinations of time-delay interferometry channels on stochastic gravitational wave background detection
Zheng-Cheng Liang, Zhi-Yuan Li, Jun Cheng, En-Kun Li, Jian-dong Zhang, Yi-Ming Hu

Abstract
The method of time delay interferometry (TDI) is proposed to cancel the laser noise in space-borne gravitational-wave detectors. Among all different TDI combinations, the most commonly used ones are the orthogonal channels A, E and T, where A and E are signal-sensitive and T is signal-insensitive. Meanwhile, for the detection of stochastic gravitational-wave background, one needs to introduce the overlap reduction function to characterize the correlation between channels. For the calculation of overlap reduction function, it is often convenient to work in the low-frequency approximation, and assuming the equal-arm Michelson channels. However, if one wishes to work on the overlap reduction function of A / E \rm A/E channels, then the low-frequency approximation fails. We derive the exact form of overlap reduction function for A / E \rm A/E channels. Based on the overlap reduction function, we calculate the sensitivity curves of TianQin, TianQin I+II and TianQin + LISA. We conclude that the detection sensitivity calculated with A / E \rm A/E channels is mostly consistent with that obtained from the equal-arm Michelson channels.

For an anisotropic

\displaystyle\times 4\pi^{2}\sum_{PP^{\prime}}\langle\widetilde{h}_{P}(f,\hat{k})\widetilde{h}^{*}_{P^{\prime}}(f^{\prime},\hat{k}^{\prime})\rangle e^{P}_{ab}(\hat{k})e_{P^{\prime}}^{ab}(\hat{k}^{\prime})

V
Detection method

The output
s(t)
of detector channel mainly contains
SGWB
signal
h(t)
and channel noise
n(t)
.
Unless the
SGWB
signal is much larger in magnitude than the channel noise, it is almost impossible to separate the signal from the noise through a single-channel measurement
Allen and Romano 1999
.
The general strategies are cross-correlating the outputs from different noise-independent channels
Hellings and Downs 1983
;
Christensen 1992
;
Flanagan 1993
and auto-correlating the output from one channel under the noise monitoring of the null channel
Robinson et al. 2008
;
Romano and Cornish 2017
.

In cross-correlation method, one can define the product of two outputs as the correlator, then the measurement can be obtained by integrating the correlator over time:

\displaystyle S_{IJ}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-T/2}^{t_{0}+T/2}{\rm d}t\int_{t_{0}-T/2}^{t_{0}+T/2}{\rm d}t^{\prime}s_{I}(t)s_{J}(t^{\prime})Q_{IJ}(t-t^{\prime},t_{0})

(28)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-T/2}^{t_{0}+T/2}{\rm d}t\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}f\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}f^{\prime}\,\widetilde{s}_{I}(f,t_{0})\widetilde{s}_{J}^{*}(f^{\prime},t_{0})

\displaystyle\times\frac{P^{*}_{{\rm h}_{IJ}}(f,t_{0})}{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)W_{IJ}(f,t_{0})}e^{-{\rm i}2\pi(f-f^{\prime})t},

where
P_{{\rm n}}
is the noise
PSD
, and the correction function

\displaystyle W_{IJ}(f,t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{P_{{\rm h}_{I}}(f,t_{0})P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)+P_{{\rm h}_{J}}(f,t_{0})P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)}{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)}

(29)

\displaystyle+\frac{P_{{\rm h}_{I}}(f,t_{0})P_{{\rm h}_{J}}(f,t_{0})+|P_{{\rm h}_{IJ}}(f,t_{0})|^{2}}{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)}.

Under the assumption that
SGWB
signal and channel noise are stationary in the time interval
[t_{0}-T/2,t_{0}+T/2]
, the filter function
Q_{IJ}(t,t^{\prime})=Q_{IJ}(t-t^{\prime})
.

In terms of the expectation value and variance of the measurement

\displaystyle\mu(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle S_{IJ}(t_{0})\rangle,

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle S_{IJ}(t_{0})S_{IJ}(\eta_{0})\rangle-\langle S_{IJ}(t_{0})\rangle\langle S_{IJ}(\eta_{0})\rangle,

(30)

the
SNR
(
SNR
) can be obtained by

\rho(t_{0})=\frac{\mu(t_{0})}{\sigma(t_{0})}=\sqrt{2\,T\int_{f_{\rm min}}^{f_{\rm max}}{\rm d}f\,\frac{|P_{{\rm h}_{IJ}}(f,t_{0})|^{2}}{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)W_{IJ}(f,t_{0})}}.

(31)

For a single TianQin-like detector, the correlation between the
SGWB
signal of noise-independent channels cancels
Cutler 1998
;
Adams and Cornish 2010
.
Thus the cross-correlation method will fall, and the null-channel method need to be introduced.

For the channel group
\rm AET
, one can construct the correlator for null-channel method by

s_{0}(t,t^{\prime})=\sum_{I=\rm A,E}\bigg[s_{I}(t)s_{I}(t^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}f\,e^{{\rm i}2\pi f(t-t^{\prime})}P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)\bigg],

(32)

where the noise
PSD
of
\rm A/E
channels can be monitored by the null channel
\rm T
.
Similar to Eq. (
28
), we can obtain the measurement by

\displaystyle K(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-T/2}^{t_{0}+T/2}{\rm d}t\int_{t_{0}-T/2}^{t_{0}+T/2}{\rm d}t^{\prime}s_{0}(t,t^{\prime})Q_{II}(t-t^{\prime},t_{0})

(33)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{I=\rm A,E}\int_{t_{0}-T/2}^{t_{0}+T/2}{\rm d}t\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}f\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}f^{\prime}\,\bigg[\widetilde{s}_{I}(f,t_{0})\widetilde{s}_{I}^{*}(f^{\prime},t_{0})-\frac{1}{2}P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)\bigg]\frac{P_{{\rm h}_{I}}(f,t_{0})e^{-{\rm i}2\pi(f-f^{\prime})t_{0}}}{P^{2}_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)W_{I}(f,t_{0})},

where the correction function

\displaystyle W_{I}(f,t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(1+\frac{P_{{\rm h}_{I}}(f,t_{0})}{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)}\bigg)^{2}.

(34)

For a symmetric scenario, one can further assume

\displaystyle P_{{\rm h}_{\rm A}}(f,t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{{\rm h}_{\rm E}}(f,t_{0}),

\displaystyle P_{{\rm n}_{\rm A}}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{{\rm n}_{\rm E}}(f).

(35)

Then the
SNR
of null-channel method is given by

\displaystyle\rho(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle K(t_{0})\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle K(t_{0})K(\eta_{0})\rangle-\langle K(t_{0})\rangle\langle K(\eta_{0})\rangle}}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\,T\int_{f_{\rm min}}^{f_{\rm max}}{\rm d}f\,\frac{P^{2}_{{\rm h}_{I}}(f,t_{0})}{P^{2}_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)W_{I}(f,t_{0})}}.

By setting
I=J
, the
SNR
of cross-correlation method (i.e., Eq. (
31

36
)).
More details are shown in Appendix
D
.

As the
SNR
is proportional to
\sqrt{T}
, we can accumulate a sufficiently high
SNR
by correlating plenty of data sets.
For the total correlation time
T_{\rm tot}=nT
, the
SNR

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{t_{0}=0}^{(n-1)T}\rho^{2}(t_{0})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\,T\int_{f_{\rm min}}^{f_{\rm max}}{\rm d}f\,\sum_{t_{0}=0}^{(n-1)T}\frac{|P_{{\rm h}_{IJ}}(f,t_{0})|^{2}}{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)W_{IJ}(f,t_{0})}}.

When
SGWB
signal is much stronger than channel noise, the total
SNR

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\,T\int_{f_{\rm min}}^{f_{\rm max}}{\rm d}f\,\sum_{t_{0}=0}^{(n-1)T}\frac{1}{\frac{P_{{\rm h}_{I}}(f,t_{0})P_{{\rm h}_{J}}(f,t_{0})}{|P_{{\rm h}_{IJ}}(f,t_{0})|^{2}}+(1-\delta_{IJ})}}

(38)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{(1+\delta_{IJ})T_{\rm tot}(f_{\rm max}-f_{\rm min})},

where
|P_{{\rm h}_{IJ}}(f,t_{0})|^{2}\leq P_{{\rm h}_{I}}(f,t_{0})P_{{\rm h}_{J}}(f,t_{0})
.
We can find that the
SNR
is limited to a certain value, which is determined by three factors: (i) the type of detection method; (ii) the correlation time
T_{\rm tot}
; (iii) detection frequency band
[f_{\rm min},f_{\rm max}]
.
On the contrary, when
SGWB
is much weaker than channel noise, the correction function
W_{IJ}(f,t_{0})\to 1
.
In this case, the
SNR
for an isotropic
SGWB
can be simplified to the following form:

\rho=\sqrt{2\,T_{\rm tot}\int_{f_{\rm min}}^{f_{\rm max}}{\rm d}f\,\frac{\big[\bar{\Gamma}_{IJ}(f)S_{\rm h}(f)\big]^{2}}{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)}}.

(39)

When
SNR
exceeds the preset threshold, the
SGWB
detection will be announced.

As for the
SNR

Allen and Romano 1999
:

{\rm{\rho}_{\rm thr}}=\sqrt{2}[{\rm erfc}^{-1}(2\alpha)-{\rm erfc}^{-1}(2\gamma)],

(40)

where the false alarm rate
\alpha
and the detection rate
\gamma
are set in advance.
For example, when choosing
\alpha=0.1
and
\gamma=0.9
, the
SNR
threshold
{\rho}_{\rm thr}=2.56
; or
{\rho}_{\rm thr}=3.30
with
\alpha=0.05
and
\gamma=0.95
.
Because the lower false alarm rate and the higher detection rate imply that the detector is more sensitive to
GW
, the higher
SNR
threshold is required.

VI
Sensitivity curve

We can further demonstrate the detection capability of the detector through the sensitivity curve.
The
sky-averaged
sensitivity curve can be directly obtained based on the response to
SGWB
and noise lever of the channel
Cornish 2002
:

S_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)=\frac{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)}{\mathcal{R}_{I}(f)},

(41)

which is applied to an isotropic
SGWB
.

As for two channels, the effective sensitivity curve is defined as
Thrane and Romano 2013

S_{{\rm n}_{IJ}}(f)=\frac{\sqrt{P_{{\rm n}_{I}}(f)P_{{\rm n}_{J}}(f)}}{\bar{\Gamma}_{IJ}(f)},

(42)

which reduces to Eq. (
41
) when
I=J
. Then, Eq. (
39
) can be further simplified:

\rho=\sqrt{2\,T_{\rm tot}\int_{f_{\rm min}}^{f_{\rm max}}{\rm d}f\,\bigg[\frac{S_{\rm h}(f)}{S_{{\rm n}_{IJ}}(f)}\bigg]^{2}}.

(43)

Meanwhile, the corresponding energy spectral density
\Omega_{{\rm n}_{IJ}}
can be convert to
S_{{\rm n}_{IJ}}
through

\Omega_{{\rm n}_{IJ}}(f)=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{3H_{0}^{2}}f^{3}S_{{\rm n}_{IJ}}(f).

(44)

As mentioned above, the correlation time and frequency band of the detector have a significant impact on
SGWB
detection. Therefore, Thrane et.al
Thrane and Romano 2013
proposed the
PLIS
curve, which is applied to the power-law
SGWB
with the following form:

\Omega_{\rm gw}(f)=\Omega_{0}(\epsilon)(f/f_{\rm ref})^{\epsilon}|_{\epsilon=\epsilon_{0}},

(45)

where
\Omega_{0}
is related to the index
\epsilon
, and the reference frequency
f_{\rm ref}
is arbitrary.

By combining Eq. (
43
)-Eq. (
45
),

\Omega_{0}(\epsilon)=\rho_{\rm thr}\left[2\,T_{\rm tot}\int_{f_{\rm min}}^{f_{\rm max}}{\rm d}f\frac{(f/f_{\rm ref})^{2\epsilon}}{\Omega_{{\rm n}_{IJ}}^{2}(f)}\right]^{-1/2},

(46)

where the
SNR
is set to threshold
\rho_{\rm thr}
.
For each frequency, we can obtain the maximum
\Omega_{\rm gw}
with a specific index
\epsilon
to generate the
PLIS
curve:

\Omega_{\rm PLIS}(f)={\rm max}_{\epsilon}[\Omega_{0}(\epsilon)(f/f_{\rm ref})^{\epsilon}].

(47)

The
PLIS
curve (on the log-log plot) specifies the envelope of power-law
SGWB
, of which the
SNR
is equal to the preset
SNR
threshold.
Once a power-law
SGWB
spectrum is somewhere above the
PLIS
curve, the
SGWB
is expected to be detected, and vice versa.
It is explicit to determine whether the
SGWB
can be detected by the detector configuration.

We show the
PLIS
curves of TianQin, TianQin I+II, TianQin + LISA in Fig.
5
.
The solid and dashed lines are the results for the equal-arm Michelson and
\rm A/E
channels, and both of them basically coincide.
The result implies that when
\rm A/E
channels are employed instead of the equal-arm Michelson, the detection capability of the detector configuration is nearly unchanged.

The spectrum of astrophysical
SGWB
is usually in power-law form, which is not true for the cosmological
SGWB

PT
(
PT

Therefore, Schmitz et.al

The energy spectral density of the first-order

(48)

where

the

Once selecting a specific parameter group
\{p_{i}\}
, the peak frequency

and peak amplitude

If the peak amplitude is above
\rho_{\rm thr}
times of the

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we have analyzed the impacts of
\rm A/E
channels on
SGWB
detection for space-borne detectors.
We first selected the reference interference site for
\rm A/E
channels consisting multiple interference sites.
In this way, it is clear to define the detector separation through the distance between reference interference sites, which works well for any channel.
By means of the reference interference site, we derived the
ORF
that is valid across all frequency bands.
For TianQin, TianQin I+II and TianQin + LISA, we calculated the
ORF
for the equal-arm Michelson and
\rm A/E
channels, respectively.
In addition to
ORF
, the noise level, correlation time and frequency band of the detector also need to be folded into detection sensitivity, according to which we plotted the corresponding sensitivity curves to assess the detection capabilities.

In a certain detector configuration, the
ORF
and noise
PSD
of A/E channels differ from those of the equal-arm Michelson, but their differences share the same coefficient
6\sin^{2}(f/f_{\ast})
under the low-frequency approximation.
Since the detection sensitivity is determined by the ratio of
ORF
and noise
PSD
, the detection sensitivity of the
\rm A/E
channels and the equal-arm Michelson are basically the same.
We have shown that the difference between A/E channels and the equal-arm Michelson stems from the extra phase related to the position difference.
For the low-frequency approximation to be valid, the wavelength of the
GW
should be longer than the armlength, which makes the extra phase negligible.

However, the configuration design can affect the correlation between detectors.
In order to obtain optimal
ORF
, the configuration design is well worth a discussion.
Meanwhile, the constructions of equal-arm Michelson and
\rm A/E
channels are different.
Therefore, the configuration design corresponding to the optimal
ORF
for
\rm A/E
channels may differ from that for the equal-arm Michelson.
TianQin I+II is one such configuration, where the launch time difference between TianQin and TianQin II is the key factor of configuration design.
If one expects the optimal
ORF
of TianQin I+II in
SGWB
detection, then the launch time difference should be set to an integer and a semi-integer multiple of the orbital period for the equal-arm Michelson and
\rm A/E
channels, respectively.

Recently, Bartolo et al.
Bartolo et al. 2022
reviewed the sensitivity curve for the detection of the anisotropic
SGWB
by a single LISA.
Unlike for the isotropic
SGWB
, where only the
\rm A/E
channels and the equal-arm Michelson contribute to the detection sensitivity, for the anisotropic
SGWB
the null channel
\rm T
also has a non-negligible impact on the sensitivity.
Therefore, if only considering the A/E channels and the equal-arm Michelson, the detection sensitivities for the anisotropic
SGWB
are different.
However, by incorporating the null channel, the detection sensitivities for
\rm A/E
and
\rm T
, as well as the equal-arm Michelson and
\rm T
for the anisotropic
SGWB
will be equivalent.

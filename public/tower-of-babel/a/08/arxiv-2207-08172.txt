Pluripolar hulls and fine holomorphy Thanks: Part of this work was done while the author visited Jagiellonian University in Krakow. He is grateful to Armen Edigarian, Lukasz Kosinski, and Włodzimierz Zwonek for the invitation and for very stimulating discussions.
Jan Wiegerinck

Abstract
. Examples by Poletsky and the author and by Zwonek show the existence of nowhere extendable holomorphic functions with the property that the pluripolar hull of their graphs is much larger than the graph of the respective functions, and contains multiple sheets. We will explain this phenomenon by fine analytic continuation of the function over part of a Cantor-type set involved. This gives more information on the hull, and allows for weakening and effectiveness of the conditions in the original examples.

1.
Introduction

We study the relation between pluripolar hulls of graphs of certain holomorphic functions and fine holomorphy. These notions are recalled in Section 2. Pluripolar hulls are of importance because of an old result of Zeriahi
[
21
]
that roughly states that a pluripolar set
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is
complete
if it is an
F_{\sigma}
as well as
G_{\delta}
and equal to its pluripolar hull. The last two conditions are necessary too. Recall that
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is pluripolar if it is contained in the
-\infty
set of a plurisubharmonic function, and it is complete if it equals the
-\infty
set of such a function.

Levenberg, Martin, and Poletsky in
[
15
]
conjectured that the pluripolar hull of a graph of an analytic function
f
is equal to the graph of the maximal analytic extension of
f
in the sense of Weierstrass. This conjecture was refuted in
[
5
]
. Other counterexamples to this conjecture showed remarkable behavior: the hull could extend far beyond the domain of the maximal extension and could consist of many sheets,
[
4
,
17
,
22
]
. The connection with fine holomorphy was made in
[
8
]
. This eventually led to Theorem
2.2
, which together with the observation in
[
3
]
that the examples from
[
5
,
7
]
are all instances of finely analytic continuation, suggested the following refined conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
20
]
The pluripolar hull of a graph of an analytic function
f
consists of the graph of the (possibly multi-valued) maximal finely analytic extension of
f
.

In
[
20
]
the author boldly stated without much proof that in all the known examples it can be seen that the pluripolar hull occurs because of finely analytic continuation. The purpose of this paper is to corroborate this statement and the conjecture.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
,
a=(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\cdots)
,
b=(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\cdots)
,
a_{j}<b_{j}
, be a Cantor set in the real line formed by omitting from the interval
[a_{0},b_{0}]
open intervals
(a_{i},b_{i})
,
i\geqslant 1
, in such a way that
[a_{i},b_{i}]\cap[a_{j},b_{j}]=\varnothing
(
i>j\geqslant 0
) and the maximal length of an interval in
[a_{0},b_{0}]\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}(a_{j},b_{j})
tends to 0 for
N\to\infty
. Without loss of generality, we can and will assume that for all
i
we have
b_{i+1}-a_{i+1}\leqslant b_{i}-a_{i}
.

In
[
17
]
Poletsky and the author studied the holomorphic function
f_{a,b}
defined by

f_{a,b}(z)=\frac{z-b_{0}}{z-a_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{z-a_{i}}{z-b_{i}}

and its square roots, which are both well-defined single valued functions on the complement of
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
. The main result here was that it is possible to choose the Cantor set in such a way that the
pluripolar hull
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
of the graph
\Gamma
of each of these roots consists of precisely the graphs of the two square roots above the complement of the Cantor set. What happens above the Cantor set remained unclear. Then Zwonek
[
22
]
constructed holomorphic functions on the unit disc that can not be extended over the disc, but with the property that the pluripolar hull of their graph consists of infinitely many points above
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{|z|=1\}
, see also Siciak,
[
19
]
. In
[
4
]
similar results were obtained for functions on the complement of certain Cantor-like sets and, moreover, the hull above these Cantor sets was partially described.

We show that a computable condition on Cantor sets
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
in terms of properties of the sequences
a
and
b
guarantees that the pluripolar hull of a square root of
f_{a,b}
indeed comes about because of fine analytic extension of this root over part of the Cantor set. This is done in Section 4. In particular, it follows, as in
[
4
]
, that the pluripolar hull also contains points above this part of the Cantor set. Under some extra conditions we show in Section 5 that above this part the hull consists of precisely the graphs of the fine extension of the roots. In Section 3 we deal with some elementary properties of
f_{a,b}
and its square roots, and correct an error in
[
17
]
.

In the final Section 6 we follow up on the examples in
[
22
]
and
[
19
]
. In these articles Blaschke products
B
with extra conditions on their zeroset are constructed. The domain of existence of
B
is the unit disc and for every holomorphic function
f
on a larger domain than the unit disc, the hull of the graph of
fB
is strictly larger than the graph of
fB
over the unit disc and may contain infinitely many sheets. We show that the Blaschke product
B
and therefore the functions
fB
admit finely analytic continuation over part of the unit circle. As in
[
4
]
the condition is local, and altogether less restrictive than the ones in
[
22
,
19
]
. Moreover, we obtain information on the hull over this part of the unit circle.

2.
Finely holomorphic functions and pluripolar hulls

Recall that the fine topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, in particular on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}={\mathbb{C}}
is the coarsest topology that makes all subharmonic functions continuous. See
[
10
,
11
,
2
,
1
]
for background on the fine topology. On finely open sets one can define
finely holomorphic functions
. For our purposes the following definition is the most convenient. See
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
for more information on finely holomorphic functions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be finely open subset of
{\mathbb{C}}
. A function
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{C}}
is called
finely holomorphic
(or
finely analytic
) on
\Omega
if for every
z\in\Omega
there exists a fine neighborhood
K_{z}\subset\Omega
, which can be taken to be compact in the Euclidean topology, with the property that
f|_{K_{z}}
is a uniform limit of rational functions with poles off
K_{z}
.

Note that if
\Omega
is Euclidean open, then finely holomorphic functions on
\Omega
are just ordinary holomorphic functions. Finely open sets of the form
\{z\in D:h(z)>c\}
, where
h
is a subharmonic function on an open set
D
in
{\mathbb{C}}
, form a base of the fine topology.

Next let us recall that the (
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
-)pluripolar hull of a pluripolar set
X\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is defined as

X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\isdef\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:\ \forall u\in\psh({\mathbb{C}}^{n})\ u|_{X}=-\infty\Rightarrow u(z)=-\infty\}.

Here
\psh({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
denotes the plurisubharmonic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. See
[
16
]
for background and properties of pluripolar hulls.

Note that if
X
is a sufficiently large part of the graph of a holomorphic function
f
, (the projection on the domain of
f
should have positive capacity), then
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
contains the graph of any analytic extension of
f
.

Edlund and Jöricke,
[
8
]
observed a connection between pluripolar hulls of graphs and fine analytic extension.
This eventually led to the following result
[
9
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
E
be a set of positive capacity in
{\mathbb{C}}
and let
f
be a finely holomorphic function on a fine neighborhood of
E
. Let
X
denote the graph of
f
over
E
. Then
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
contains the graph of the (possibly multi-valued) maximal finely analytic continuation of
f
.

3.
The product
f_{a,b}
and its square roots

We denote by
d(z,A)
the Euclidean distance from
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
to
A\subset{\mathbb{C}}
. Writing

f_{a,b}(z)=\left(1+\frac{a_{0}-b_{0}}{z-a_{0}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{b_{i}-a_{i}}{z-b_{i}}\right)

we see that the product converges uniformly on sets

\Omega_{\delta}=\{z:d(z,{\mathcal{C}}(a,b))\geqslant\delta\},\quad\delta>0,

because
0\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-a_{i})\leqslant b_{0}-a_{0}
.
Hence
f_{a,b}
represents a holomorphic function on the complement of
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
. It is bounded at infinity and therefore extends to be holomorphic at infinity. The Laurent expansion on
\{|z|>|a_{0}|+|b_{0}|\}
is

(3.1)

f_{a,b}(z)=1+\frac{c_{1}}{z}+\frac{c_{2}}{z^{2}}+\cdots,

with
c_{1}=-(b_{0}-a_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(b_{i}-a_{i})
, so that
c_{1}
equals the length (1-dimensional Lebesgue measure) of the Cantor set.

If
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
has length zero it was claimed in
[
17
]
that
f_{a,b}
is constant. This is in general not true.
Consider
\log f_{a,b}
a branch of the logarithm, which is well-defined in a neighborhood of
\infty
. Its derivative equals

\left(\frac{1}{z-b_{0}}-\frac{1}{z-a_{0}}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{z-b_{i}}-\frac{1}{z-a_{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{0}^{k}-a_{0}^{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}^{k}-a_{j}^{k}}{z^{k}}.

If
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
has length zero, then the first term (
k=1
) in this sum vanishes, but the others generally need not.

Let

f^{N}=f^{N}_{a,b}=\frac{z-b_{0}}{z-a_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{z-a_{i}}{z-b_{i}}

be a partial product. There exist two holomorphic branches
g^{N}_{+}
and
g^{N}_{-}
of
\sqrt{f^{N}}
on

({\mathbb{C}}\setminus[a_{0},b_{0}])\cup\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}(a_{j},b_{j})\right)

with
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}g^{N}_{+}(z)=1
and
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}g^{N}_{-}(z)=-1
.

On

({\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}})\cup(a_{0},b_{0})\setminus\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}[a_{j},b_{j}]\right)

there are two branches of
\sqrt{f^{N}}
:
\tilde{g}^{N}_{+}
with
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty\atop\im z>0}\tilde{g}^{N}_{+}(z)=1
, and
\tilde{g}^{N}_{-}
with
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty\atop\im z>0}\tilde{g}^{N}_{-}(z)=-1
.

In particular, it follows that
\lim\limits_{z\to\infty\atop\im z<0}\tilde{g}^{N}_{\pm}(z)=\mp 1
, as
\tilde{g}^{N}_{\pm}=g^{N}_{\mp}
on the lower half-plane.
The four functions
g^{N}_{\pm}
,
\tilde{g}^{N}_{\pm}
are analytic extensions of each other and together form a 2-valued holomorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{a_{j},b_{j},j=0\ldots N\}
.

Clearly
g^{N}_{\pm}
both converge uniformly on compact sets to holomorphic square roots
g_{\pm}
of
f_{a,b}
on

(3.2)

D=({\mathbb{C}}\setminus[a_{0},b_{0}])\cup\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(a_{j},b_{j})\right),

while
\tilde{g}^{N}_{\pm}
both converge uniformly on compact sets to square roots
\tilde{g}_{\pm}
of
f_{a,b}
on

(3.3)

\tilde{D}={\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}.

Obviously
g_{\pm}=\tilde{g}_{\pm}
on the upper half-plane and
g_{\pm}=-\tilde{g}_{\pm}
on the lower half-plane. The maximal analytic extension of each of these functions is a single valued function on
D
.

4.
Fine holomorphy on the Cantor set

We start with a condition on
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
that ensures that
f_{a,b}
converges uniformly on compact sets in a set
E
that contains a subset of
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
and is sufficiently big, namely that it is a fine neighborhood of certain points in
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
. To make the connection with
[
17
]
we will write
b_{j}-a_{j}=e^{-jc_{j}}
, where
\{c_{j}\}_{j}
is an increasing sequence of positive numbers tending to
\infty
.

The following lemma is obvious.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(p_{n})_{n}

be a sequence of positive numbers with converging sum.
Then

(4.1)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}-a_{n}}{|z-b_{n}|}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left(1+\frac{a_{0}-b_{0}}{z-a_{0}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{b_{i}-a_{i}}{z-b_{i}}\right)

converge uniformly on compact sets in

(4.2)

E_{N}=\bigcap_{n=N}^{\infty}\{z:b_{n}-a_{n}\leqslant p_{n}|z-b_{n}|\}\setminus\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{N-1}\},\quad N>0.

Note that only if
\sum\frac{e^{-jc_{j}}}{p_{j}}
is finite,
E_{N}\cap{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
will eventually be nonempty.

The following Lemma is a variation of Lemma 2 and Example 14 in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
be a Cantor set formed by sequences
a
,
b
as before and let
\{c_{j}\}_{j}
be an increasing sequence tending to
\infty
with
b_{j}-a_{j}=e^{-jc_{j}}
, and such that

(4.3)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{jc_{j}}<\frac{1}{2}.

Then for
N
sufficiently large, the set
E_{N}
in (
4.2
) contains a fine neighborhood
E
of a nonempty subset of
C(a,b)
.The functions in (
4.1
) converge uniformly on compact subsets of
E
.

Proof.

We can assume
b_{0}-a_{0}=1
. Let

p_{j}=e^{-jc_{j}/2}
and set

(4.4)

F_{N}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}[a_{i},b_{i}]\cup\bigcup_{n=N}^{\infty}\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\ |z-b_{n}|\leqslant(b_{n}-a_{n})/p_{n}\}.

Note that
F_{N}
is compact.

We have
\Capa[a_{j},b_{j}]=e^{-jc_{j}}/4
and
\Capa(\{|z-b_{j}|\leqslant(b_{j}-a_{j})/p_{j}\})=e^{-jc_{j}/2}
.
We now use Theorem 5.1.4 in
[
18
]
, which estimates the capacity of a union of Borel sets. It reads in our setting

(4.5)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\log(\frac{1}{\Capa(F_{N})})}&\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\log(4e^{jc_{j}})}+\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\log(e^{jc_{j}/2})}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{jc_{j}+\log 4}+\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}\frac{2}{jc_{j}}.\end{split}

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By taking
N
sufficiently large, the final sum
\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}
in this equation can be made smaller than
\varepsilon
. Then

(4.6)

\frac{1}{\log(\frac{1}{\Capa(F_{n})})}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{jc_{j}}+\varepsilon,

or

(4.7)

\Capa(F_{N})\leqslant\exp\left(\frac{-1}{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{jc_{j}}+\varepsilon}\right)<\frac{1}{4},

if
\sum\frac{1}{jc_{j}}\leqslant 1/2
and
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small.

Define the compact set
J_{N}:=[0,1]\cup F_{N}
. Let

u(z):=g_{F_{N}}-g_{J_{N}},

where
g_{X}
denotes the Green function of
X
with pole at infinity. Being the difference of two subharmonic functions,
u
is finely continuous, therefore
\tilde{E}=\{u>0\}
is a finely open set.
Since the complement of
F_{N}
is contained in
E_{N}\cup\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{N-1}\}
, and
u\equiv 0
on
F_{N}
, the functions in (
4.1
) converge uniformly on compact sets in the finely open set
E=\tilde{E}\setminus\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{N-1}\}
.

It remains to show that
E
contains points of
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
. We have

u(\infty)=\log(\Capa J_{N})-\log(\Capa F_{N}),

cf.
[
18
, Thm 5.2.1]
. Since
J_{N}
contains
[0,1]
,
\Capa J_{N}\geqslant 1/4
, hence
u(\infty)>0
if
N
is sufficiently large.
Let
\varphi
be the Riemann map of the unit disc to the complement
\Omega
of
J_{N}
that maps
0
to
\infty
. Observe that the boundary of
\Omega
is locally connected and regular for the Dirichlet problem, therefore
\varphi
extends continuously to the unit circle and
G_{J_{N}}
is a continuous function with
G_{J_{N}}\equiv 0
on
J_{N}
. Note that for every
\theta
the curve
r\mapsto\varphi(re^{i\theta})
, (
0\leqslant r\leqslant 1
) is non-thin at
\varphi(e^{i\theta})\in J_{N}
.

Let
v=u\circ\varphi
. Then
v
is harmonic on the unit disc and
v(0)=u(\infty)>0
. Since

v(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}v^{*}(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta,

where
v^{*}(e^{i\theta})=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}v(re^{i\theta})
a.e.,
it follow that
\lim_{r\uparrow 1}v(re^{i\theta})>0
on a subset
\Gamma
of positive measure on the unit circle.
Using the above observations, we find for
\theta\in\Gamma

u\circ\varphi(e^{i\theta})=g_{F_{N}}(\varphi(e^{i\theta}))-g_{J_{N}}(\varphi(e^{i\theta}))=\liminf_{r\uparrow 1}g_{F_{N}}(\varphi(re^{i\theta}))-g_{J_{N}}(\varphi(re^{i\theta}))=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}v(re^{i\theta})>0.

It follows that
\varphi(e^{i\theta})\in J_{N}\setminus F_{N}\subset{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
and we are done.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

It is a well-known consequence of Wiener’s criterion that relatively finely open subsets of
[0,1]
have positive Lebesgue measure. We don’t need this fact, but it strengthens the connection with
[
4
, Lemma 2]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
4.2
. Let
E\subset{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
be the set constructed in the lemma. The functions
f_{a,b}
and
\tilde{g}_{\pm}
extend over
E
as finely holomorphic functions from the upper half plane to the lower half plane.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
the functions
f^{N}
, and therefore the functions
g^{N}_{\pm}
converge uniformly on compact sets in a finely open neighborhood
E
of points of
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
to
f_{a,b}
, respectively
\tilde{g}_{\pm}
. By the definition it follows that their limits are finely holomorphic on
E
. These limits clearly coincide with
f_{a,b}
, respectively
\tilde{g}_{\pm}
on
E\cap({\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}})
and are therefore the sought for finely holomorphic extensions.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

One may wonder if, perhaps,
f_{a,b}
extends as a finely holomorphic function to the complement of the set of poles
\{b_{j}\}
. However, this is not the case. The polar set
\{b_{j}\}
is of the first Baire category in its closure
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
and then Theorem 3.4. in
[
14
]
states that
f_{a,b}
would extend finely holomorphically over part of
\{b_{j}\}
, which is clearly not the case.

Corollary 4.6
.

With
D
and
\tilde{D}
as in (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and assuming that
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
4.2
, the four finely analytic function elements
\tilde{g}_{\pm}
on
\tilde{D}\cup E
and
g_{\pm}
on
D
are (finely) holomorphic continuations of each other.
Let
X
denote the graph of
g_{+}
, then
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
contains the graphs of
\tilde{g}_{\pm}
over
\tilde{D}\cup E
and the graph of
g_{-}
over
D
. In particular
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
consists of precisely two points over
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
and at least two points over
E
.

Proof.

Note that
\tilde{D}\cup E
is finely connected, and
D
is (finely) connected. Hence the first statement follows directly from Theorem
4.4
and the fact that
g_{\pm}=\tilde{g}_{\pm}
on the upper half plane and
g_{\pm}=-\tilde{g}_{\pm}
on the lower half plane.
That
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
consists of at most two points
(z,w)
for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
is part of Theorem 3.6. in
[
17
]
. The proof of this part only uses that
\{c_{n}\}_{n}
tends to
\infty
.
Theorem 4.5 in
[
9
]
shows that points of the form
(z,\tilde{g}_{\pm}(z))
belong to
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
if
z\in E
.
∎

5.
The pluripolar hull above
E

As a proof of principle we will show in this section that under a strong condition on the size of the deleted intervals,
for every
z\in E
the fiber
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\cap(\{z\}\times{\mathbb{C}})
equals
\{(z,w):\ w^{2}=f_{a,b}(z)\}
. Here we have written
f_{a,b}
also for its finely analytic extension over
E
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
be a Cantor set as before, with
b_{j}-a_{j}=e^{-jc_{j}}
and
c_{j}=(j+2)!
, in particular the condition of Lemma
4.2
is satisfied. Let
E
and
F_{N}

be the sets constructed in Lemma
4.2
and
f=f_{a,b}
the finely holomorphic function on
V=({\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{C}}(a,b))\cup E
. Then for
N
sufficiently large
there is a plurisubharmonic function
v
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
such that

\{v=-\infty\}\cap([a_{0},b_{0}]\setminus F_{N}\times{\mathbb{C}})=\Gamma_{f}\cap([a_{0},b_{0}]\setminus F_{N}\times{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
[a_{0},b_{0}]=[0,1]
. Set
p_{j}=e^{-jc_{j}/2}
. Let

f_{n}(z)=\frac{z-b_{0}}{z-a_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z-a_{i}}{z-b_{i}}=\frac{P_{n}(z)}{Q_{n}(z)},

where
P_{n}
and
Q_{n}
are monic polynomials of degree
n+1
.
We have for
C>1

(5.1)

\displaystyle|Q_{n}(z)|\leqslant(C+1)^{n+1}\quad\text{if $|z|<C$},

for
z\in[a_{0},b_{0}]\setminus F_{N}
we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle|P_{n}(z)|<1,\quad|Q_{n}(z)|<1,

and

(5.3)

\displaystyle|Q_{n}(z)|\geqslant\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{e^{-jc_{j}}}{p_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-jc_{j}/2}.

Thus on
[a_{0},b_{0}]\setminus F_{N}
we have the following estimates

(5.4)

\begin{split}|f(z)-f_{n}(z)||Q_{n}(z)|&\leqslant\left|\left(\prod_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{b_{i}-a_{i}}{z-b_{i}}\right)-1\right)P_{n}(z)\right|\\
&\leqslant\prod_{j=n+1}^{\infty}(1+p_{j})-1\leqslant\exp(\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}p_{j})-1\leqslant 2p_{n+1},\end{split}

if
n\geqslant 2
.

Next for every
\delta>0
and
|w-f(z)|\geqslant\delta

(5.5)

|w-f_{n}(z)||Q_{n}(z)|\geqslant\delta\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-jc_{j}/2}.

Now consider the plurisubharmonic functions

v_{n}(z,w)=\log(|w-f_{n}(z)||Q_{n}(z)|)\quad\text{ on ${\mathbb{C}}^{2}$.}

Note that

(5.6)

\frac{(n+1)c_{n+1}}{nc_{n}}=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{n}\to\infty,\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Let
\{e_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of positive numbers such that
\sum e_{n}
is finite but
\sum e_{n}\frac{-(n+1)c_{n+1}}{nc_{n}}=-\infty
, and form

(5.7)

v(z,w)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\max\{v_{n}(z,w),\log p_{n+1}\}}{nc_{n}}e_{n}.

Because
v_{n}(z,w)\leqslant(n+2)\log(C+1)
on
\{|z|,|w|<C\}
, for
C
sufficiently large, the function
v(z,w)
is plurisubharmonic on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
For
z\in{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)\setminus F_{N}
the function
v(z,w)
has the following properties.
If
w=f(z)
, then
v_{n}(z,w)\leqslant\log 2+\log p_{n+1}
and therefore
v(z,f(z))=-\infty
. On the other hand, if
|w-f(z)|\geqslant\delta
, then there exists
n_{0}>0
with

(5.8)

v_{n}(z,w)\geqslant\log(\delta)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-jc_{j}/2)\geqslant\log\delta-nc_{n},

therefore
v(z,w)
is finite.

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
be a Cantor set that satisfies the conditions of Lemma
5.1
.
Let
X
denote the graph of
g_{+}
over
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)
and let
g
denote the maximal finely analytic extension of
g_{+}
. Then
X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\cap\{(D\cup E)\times{\mathbb{C}}\}=\Gamma_{g}\cap\{(D\cup E)\times{\mathbb{C}}\}
.

Proof.

Because of Corollary
4.6
, we only have to prove that for
z\in E

X^{*}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\cap(\{z\}\times{\mathbb{C}}=\{(z,g_{+}(z)),(z,g_{-}(z))\}
.
The set
E
has positive length and hence positive capacity. The function
v
constructed in the previous lemma is plurisubharmonic function and equals
-\infty
on
\{(z,f_{a,b}(z)),z\in E\}
. Then by Theorem
2.2
it equals
-\infty
on the graph of
f_{a,b}
.
Consider the function
u(z,w)=v(z,w^{2})
. The function
u
is plurisubharmonic and equals
-\infty
on the graph of
g_{+}
and for
z\in E
we have
u(z,w)=-\infty
if and only if
w
is one of the two values of
g(z)
.
∎

6.
Blaschke products

In this section we will consider Blaschke products of the form

(6.1)

B(z)=z^{l}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|a_{j}|}{a_{j}}\frac{a_{j}-z}{1-\overline{a_{j}}z}\right)=z^{l}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|a_{j}|}\frac{a_{j}-z}{1/\overline{a_{j}}-z}\right)

on the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
.
In the next lemma we formulate a condition similar to (
4.3
) that guarantees that such a Blaschke product extends as a finely holomorphic function over a part
E
of the boundary of
{\mathbb{D}}
, while
E
is in the closure of the zeros of
B
. The lemma leads then to an easy proof of an extension of Theorem 1 in
[
22
]
.

It is well known that under the Blaschke condition
\sum_{k}(1-|a_{k}|)<\infty
the product (
6.1
) represents a function that is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}\cup({\mathbb{D}}^{e}\setminus X)
, where
{\mathbb{D}}^{e}=\{z:|z|>1\}
and
X=\{1/\overline{a_{j}}\}
, the set of poles of
B
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
S=\{e^{i\varphi}:\ \alpha\leqslant\varphi\leqslant\beta\}
be a proper segment of
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
,
(a_{j})_{j}
a sequence in
{\mathbb{D}}
that satisfies the Blaschke condition and such that
S\subset\overline{\{a_{j},j=1,2,\ldots\}}
. Let
B
be the Blaschke product with zeros
\{a_{j}\}
. Suppose that there exists an increasing sequence of positive numbers
\{c_{j}\}
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{jc_{j}}
is finite and that

(6.2)

\left|a_{j}-\frac{1}{\overline{a_{j}}}\right|=e^{-jc_{j}}.

Then there exists a finely open set
E\subset{\mathbb{C}}
that meets
S
with the property that
B
extends as a finely holomorphic function over
E
.

Proof.

The proof goes much the same as the proof of Lemma
4.2
. First observe that

\left|\frac{1}{|a_{j}|}\frac{a_{j}-z}{1/\overline{a_{j}}-z}-1\right|=\left|\frac{1}{|a_{j}|}\frac{a_{j}-1/\overline{a_{j}}}{1/\overline{a_{j}}-z}-(1-1/|a_{j}|)\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{|a_{j}|}\frac{|a_{j}-1/\overline{a_{j}}|}{|1/\overline{a_{j}}-z|}+\frac{(1-|a_{j}|)}{|a_{j}|}.

It follows that the product (
6.1
) converges uniformly on compact sets in

E_{N}=\left(\bigcap_{j=N}^{\infty}\{z:|1/\overline{a_{j}}-z|\geqslant|a_{j}-1/\overline{a_{j}}|/e^{-jc_{j}/2}\}\right)\setminus\{1/\overline{a_{1}},1/\overline{a_{2}},\ldots,1/\overline{a_{N-1}}\}.

Let

F_{N}=\bigcup_{j=N}^{\infty}\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z-1/\overline{a_{j}}|\leqslant e^{-jc_{j}/2}\}.

Put
J_{N}=S\cup F_{N}
Note that
J_{N}
is compact, its complement in
{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
is simply connected and
\Capa J_{N}\geqslant\Capa S=\sin((\beta-\alpha)/4)
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.2
we can take
N
so large that
\Capa F_{N}<\Capa J_{N}
. Now form again
u(z):=g_{F_{N}}-g_{J_{N}}
and the proof continues just like the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
S=\{e^{i\varphi}:\ \alpha\leqslant\varphi\leqslant\beta\}
be a proper segment in
\partial{\mathbb{D}}
and let
B
be a Blaschke product with zero set
\{a_{k},b_{l}\}
, where
\alpha\leqslant\arg a_{k}\leqslant\beta
and
\arg b_{k}\notin[\alpha,\beta]
. Suppose that the
a_{k}
satisfy (
6.2
).

Let
U
be a domain with
S\subset U
and
E\subset S
the set constructed in Lemma
6.1
. For
f
holomorphic on
U
put
g=fB
on
(U\setminus(\partial{\mathbb{D}}\cup X))\cup E
.
Then

•

\Gamma^{*}_{g|_{{\mathbb{D}}\cap U}}\supset\Gamma_{g|_{(U\setminus(S\cup X))\cup E}}

•

\Gamma^{*}_{g|_{{\mathbb{D}}^{e}\cap U}}\supset\Gamma_{g|_{(U\setminus(S\cup X))\cup E}}

In fact both
\Gamma^{*}_{g|_{{\mathbb{D}}\cap U}}
and
\Gamma^{*}_{g|_{{\mathbb{D}}^{e}\cap U}}
contain the graph of the maximal finely analytic extension of
g
.

Proof.

The function
f
is holomorphic on
U
and
B
is finely holomorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\partial{\mathbb{D}}\cup X)\cup E
. therefore
g
is finely holomorphic on the fine domain
(U\setminus(S\cup X))\cup E
. Theorem
2.2
now gives the result.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
B
be a Blaschke product as in Theorem
6.2
with the additional property that it admits no holomorphic extension outside
{\mathbb{D}}
. Take
f(z)=p.v.\log(z+2)
on
|z|<2
. Then of
g=fB
cannot be extended outside
{\mathbb{D}}
but
\Gamma^{*}_{g|_{{\mathbb{D}}\cap U}}
contains the graph of the maximal finely holomorphic extension of
g
. In particular, all fibers
\Gamma^{*}_{g|_{{\mathbb{D}}\cap U}}\cap(\{z\}\times{\mathbb{C}})
will be infinite, and most of them will be polar sets in the complex line
\{z\}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 6.4
.

(1)

In
[
22
]
Zwonek proves his version of Corollary
6.3
under the following condition on
B
:

(6.3)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\Capa(\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}:|B(z)|\leqslant r\})=0.

Siciak
[
19
]
replaced this condition by the seemingly weaker condition: There exists
r_{0}
with
0<r_{0}<1
such that

\Capa(\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}:|B(z)|\leqslant r_{0}\})<1.

In fact it is not known whether Siciak’s condition is really strictly weaker. In Proposition 7 of
[
22
]
two conditions are given that together guarantee (
6.3
). Our condition is equivalent to Zwonek’s second condition; we don’t need his first.

(2)

In both
[
22
]
and
[
19
]
the conditions on the capacity and the zeros of the Blaschke product are on all of
{\mathbb{D}}
, whereas in Theorem
6.2
and its corollary the condition on the zeros is only required in a sector.

(3)

In
[
4
]
similar results as in Theorem
6.2
and its corollary are proved by potential theoretic methods. The explanation by fine holomorphy is new.

Remark 6.5
.

While it would be possible to obtain more precise information of the pluripolar hull of the graph of the Blaschke product along the lines of Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
, it is not clear how to do this in general for the products
fB
. We will not pursue this further.

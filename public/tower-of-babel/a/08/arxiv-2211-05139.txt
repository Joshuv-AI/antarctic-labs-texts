Millicharged Relics Reveal Massless Dark Photons Preprint: FERMILAB-PUB-21-724-SQMS-T
Asher Berlin, Jeff A. Dror, Xucheng Gan, Joshua T. Ruderman

Abstract
The detection of massless kinetically-mixed dark photons is notoriously difficult, as the effect of this mixing can be removed by a field redefinition in vacuum. In this work, we study the prospect of detecting massless dark photons in the presence of a cosmic relic directly charged under this dark electromagnetism. Such millicharged particles, in the form of dark matter or dark radiation, generate an effective dark photon mass that drives photon-to-dark photon oscillations in the early universe. We also study the prospect for such models to alleviate existing cosmological constraints on massive dark photons, enlarging the motivation for direct tests of this parameter space using precision terrestrial probes.



I
Introduction

What is the underlying gauge symmetry of our Universe? Existing observations are consistent with an
{\rm SU}(3)\times{\rm U}(1)
structure at low energies. However, there may be additional unbroken gauge symmetries accompanied by yet undetected massless vector bosons.
To have escaped detection, the visible sector’s contribution to the unbroken symmetry’s conserved current must be absent or at least extremely suppressed.
1

With the inclusion of right-handed neutrinos
{\rm U}(1)_{\text{B}-\text{L}}

Heeck 2014
;
Hardy and Lasenby 2017
. The other known candidates are the non-anomalous currents associated with differences in lepton number, but these currents are broken by the observed neutrino masses even if neutrinos are Dirac fermions
Dror 2020
.

In this work, we focus on the former case such that the only direct interaction of the new massless boson with the visible sector is through its kinetic mixing with electromagnetism. Unfortunately, barring any additional interactions, such a massless “dark photon” or
A^{\prime}
is completely decoupled from the visible sector since its effects are indistinguishable from a redefinition of the usual electromagnetic coupling.

It is a natural possibility that the dark sector is non-minimal, such that the
A^{\prime}
is not the only low-energy degree of freedom. In fact, a massless dark photon can become physically observable if there exists additional matter content, denoted as
\chi
, that is directly charged under the dark
\text{U}(1)
. In this case, the kinetic mixing between the dark and visible sectors has the well-studied effect of endowing
\chi
with a parametrically small but potentially detectable electromagnetic charge, commonly referred to as a “millicharge.” A cosmological population of such millicharged particles (mCPs) interacts with both sectors, indirectly coupling the dark and visible photons. Such interactions can leave striking signatures in the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and are the focus of this work.

Measurements of the CMB by the COBE
Fixsen et al. 1996
and WMAP satellites
Bennett et al. 2013
ushered in the era of precision cosmology, confirming that photons have free-streamed after decoupling from the thermal plasma in the hot early universe. In the process, their energy spectrum has preserved a nearly-perfect thermal blackbody, while the small anisotropies have continued to grow, giving rise to the large-scale structure that we see today. While recent efforts by, e.g., Planck
Aghanim et al. 2020
and ACT
Choi et al. 2020
have continued to measure these anisotropies with increasing levels of precision over a handful of frequency regions, the most precise observations of the monopole spectrum were made over three decades ago by the FIRAS instrument aboard the COBE satellite
Fixsen et al. 1996
, finding agreement with an ideal blackbody to within
\sim 1
% throughout the entire frequency range consisting of 43 frequency bins.

Measurements of the CMB spectrum have been used as a probe of a variety of new physics scenarios, including density perturbations during inflation
Hu et al. 1994
;
Pajer and Zaldarriaga 2012
and energy injection from decaying or annihilating relics
Hu and Silk 1993
;
Chluba and Sunyaev 2012
(see, e.g., Ref.
Chluba et al. 2021
and references within). In this work, we focus on new physics that is weakly-coupled to electromagnetism that may slightly distort the CMB spectrum away from that of a blackbody. At redshifts
z\lesssim\order{10^6}
, the decoupling of photon number-changing processes means that non-standard processes that add or remove photons may lead to observable deviations that persist to the present time. The absence of such spectral distortions in the COBE/FIRAS dataset place some of the strongest bounds on models of light weakly-coupled physics beyond the Standard Model (SM)
Chluba et al. 2021
. For instance, the ionized plasma in the early universe modifies the photon’s dispersion relation, which gives rise to a redshift-dependent effective photon mass spanning
M_{\text{eff}}(z)\sim 10^{-14}\ \text{eV}-10^{-5}\ \text{eV}
during redshifts of
z\sim 0-10^{6}
, respectively. If, during its redshift evolution, the value of
M_{\text{eff}}(z)
approaches the mass
m_{A^{\prime}}
of a dark photon, the probability for
\gamma\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}
interconversion is resonantly enhanced. This has been used to place stringent upper bounds on the photon’s coupling to massive dark photons
Mirizzi et al. 2009a
;
Mirizzi et al. 2009b
;
Kunze and Vázquez-Mozo 2015
;
McDermott and Witte 2020
;
Caputo et al. 2020a
;
Caputo et al. 2020b
;
Garcia et al. 2020
.

An analogous effect occurs for massless dark photons in a background of mCP relics if the
A^{\prime}
acquires an in-medium contribution to its effective mass,
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}(z)
, which is comparable to the SM plasma’s contribution to
M_{\text{eff}}(z)
. As we show in this work, even cosmological populations of mCPs that make up a tiny subcomponent of dark matter (DM) or dark radiation (DR) can induce
\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}
conversion in the early universe. In this way, the COBE/FIRAS measurement places a powerful bound on the existence of new unbroken gauge symmetries. In addition, in theories of massive dark photons, the effects of mCPs can even
open up
distinct regions of the dark photon parameter space by preventing epochs of resonant conversion. This possibility implies an increased importance of terrestrial experiments, whose sensitivity to dark photons is unaffected by the presence of such mCPs. We note that mCP-induced
\gamma\to A^{\prime}
signals were previously investigated in Ref.
Bogorad and Toro 2022
, which focused on ultralight mCP DM. We expand upon and go beyond this work by investigating a larger set of models (including heavier mCP DM, massless DR, and massive dark photons) and we improve upon such calculations by estimating the region where mCP self-interactions can significantly effect
\gamma\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}
conversion.

\gamma\to A^{\prime}

(1)

Here,

Although massless dark photons are completely decoupled from the SM in vacuum, resonant

This process is due to the SM electromagnetic field driving collective oscillations in the mCP plasma, which in turn generate dark electromagnetic fields (and vice-versa). This transfer of electromagnetic energy is maximally likely when the dispersion relations of the initial and final states are matched
Dubovsky and Hernández-Chifflet 2015

\gamma\to A^{\prime}

A^{\prime}\to\gamma

\gamma\to A^{\prime}

IV

III
Imprints on the CMB

Armed with the expression for the photon to dark photon conversion rate in Eq. (
8
), we now discuss the imprint of
\gamma\to A^{\prime}
transitions on the CMB spectrum and how non-observation of such signals bounds the strength of these processes. To begin, it is helpful to rewrite Eq. (
8

t
to cosmological redshift
z

z

\omega_{0}=\omega/(1+z)
today

\frac{\Delta f_{\gamma}(\omega_{0})}{f_{\gamma}(\omega_{0})}\simeq-\frac{\pi\epsilon^{2}}{\omega_{0}}\sum_{z_{\text{res}}}\,\frac{M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}}{(1+z)^{2}H}~\,\bigg|\frac{d\log{\w_p^2}}{dz}-\frac{d\log{\meff^{\p\, 2}}}{dz}\bigg|^{-1}~\Bigg|_{z=z_{\text{res}}}~,

(10)

where
H
is the Hubble parameter and the sum is over all such redshifts
z_{\text{res}}
at which the resonance
\omega_{p}^{2}\simeq M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}
occurs (we remind the reader that compared to the discussion above, we have shortened our notation such that only the real part is included in
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}

Note that replacing
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}\to m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}

10

\gamma\to A^{\prime}
transitions for massive dark photons in the absence of mCPs
Mirizzi et al. 2009a
;
Mirizzi et al. 2009b
;
Kunze and Vázquez-Mozo 2015
;
McDermott and Witte 2020
;
Caputo et al. 2020a
;
Caputo et al. 2020b
;
Garcia et al. 2020

m_{A^{\prime}}\gtrsim M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}

M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}\to M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}+m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}

M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}
plays the role of an effective redshift-dependent dark photon mass. In our analysis below, we use Eq. (
10

In general, multiple resonances can contribute to the sum in Eq. (
10

In the early universe, the thermal blackbody distribution of photons is maintained by their tight coupling to the SM plasma such that any deviation away from equilibrium at high redshift is quickly erased by rapid collisions. However, the decoupling of photon number-changing reactions (in the form of double Compton scattering) at redshifts of
z\lesssim 2\times 10^{6}
implies that late-time deviations away from equilibrium can persist to the present time (see, e.g., Ref.
Chluba and Sunyaev 2012

I_{{\rm obs}}

T_{0}\simeq 2.7~{\rm K}

I(\omega_{0},T_{0})\simeq\frac{\omega_{0}^{3}/2\pi^{2}}{e^{\omega_{0}/T_{0}}-1}\,\bigg(1+\frac{\Delta f_{\gamma}(\omega_{0})}{f_{\gamma}(\omega_{0})}\bigg)~.

(11)

\chi^{2}=\sum_{i}\left(\frac{I_{\text{obs}}(\omega_{i})-I(\omega_{i},T_{0})}{\sigma(\omega_{i})}\right)^{2}~,

(12)

IV
Constraining Millicharged Relics

In the previous section, we calculated limits as a function of
\epsilon
and
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}(0)
for models of mCDM and mCDR (see the right panel of Fig.
1
). These bounds can be translated straightforwardly into the mCP parameter space spanned by
\epsilon
, the mCP mass
m_{\chi}
, and the dark gauge coupling
e^{\prime}
, assuming a fixed cosmological history. In this section, we investigate how these bounds apply to the simplest cosmologies of mCP dark sectors.

IV.1
Subcomponent of Millicharged Dark Matter (mCDM)

Let us begin by investigating the case of a millicharged dark matter (mCDM) subcomponent. The effective dark photon mass arising from such a population of mCPs, assuming that their self-interactions are negligible (
\Gamma^{\prime}\ll\omega
), is approximately

M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}\simeq\bigg(\frac{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}\,n_{\chi}}{m_{\chi}}\bigg)^{1/2}\sim 10^{-17}\ \text{eV}\times(1+z)^{3/2}~\bigg(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{10^{-4}}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\frac{m_{\chi}}{1\ \text{GeV}}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{f_{\text{DM}}}{10^{-2}}\bigg)^{1/2}~,

(16)

where
\alpha^{\prime}=e^{\prime\,2}/4\pi
is the dark fine structure constant and in the second equality we have normalized the mCP number density
n_{\chi}
by the fractional contribution
f_{\text{DM}}=m_{\chi}\,n_{\chi}/\rho_{{}_{\text{DM}}}
to the total DM energy density
\rho_{{}_{\text{DM}}}
. In the following, we only consider
f_{\text{DM}}\lesssim 0.4\times 10^{-2}
, since for such fractions the stringent CMB bounds on DM-baryon scattering do not apply
Dubovsky et al. 2004
;
de Putter et al. 2019
;
Xu et al. 2018
;
Kovetz et al. 2018
;
Buen-Abad et al. 2022
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
.

One of the simplest scenarios of mCDM corresponds to when mCPs arise as a thermal relic of the early universe. This naturally occurs for
q_{\chi}\gtrsim 10^{-7}\times(m_{\chi}/\text{GeV})^{1/2}
, such that annihilations of electrons into mCPs equilibrate the SM and dark sectors at early times. In this case, the depletion of the thermal mCP density is typically governed by the freeze-out of annihilations into massless dark photons,
\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}A^{\prime}
, which easily dominates over annihilations into pairs of SM particles for
e^{\prime}/e\gg\epsilon
(this is the case for the entire parameter space of interest). These processes deplete the total mCDM density to fractional DM abundances
f_{\text{DM}}
for couplings of roughly

\alpha^{\prime}\sim 10^{-4}\times\bigg(\frac{f_{\text{DM}}}{10^{-2}}\bigg)^{-1/2}\,\bigg(\frac{m_{\chi}}{1\ \text{GeV}}\bigg)~.

(17)

To derive Eq. (
17
), we employ the standard freeze-out estimate
\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}\sim\alpha^{\prime 2}/m_{\chi}^{2}\sim 1/(T_{\text{eq}}\,m_{\text{pl}})
, where
T_{\text{eq}}\sim 1\ \text{eV}
is the temperature at matter radiation equality and
m_{\text{pl}}
is the Planck mass. In our analysis, we go beyond this order of magnitude estimate and employ a semi-analytic solution to the Boltzmann equation, as in Ref.
Chu et al. 2012
;
Hambye et al. 2019
;
Agrawal et al. 2017
. As discussed above, this mCP density contributes an in-medium correction to the dark photon’s dispersion relation. For values of the dark coupling fixed to the freeze-out estimate in Eq. (
17
), the
A^{\prime}
effective mass at redshift
z
is approximately

M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}\sim 10^{-17}\ \text{eV}\times(1+z)^{3/2}\,\bigg(\frac{m_{\chi}}{1\ \text{GeV}}\bigg)^{-1/2}\,\bigg(\frac{f_{\text{DM}}}{10^{-2}}\bigg)^{1/4}~.

(18)

Using this expression, we can use FIRAS data to place limits on thermal freeze-out cosmologies of mCDM. More specifically, for a given mass
m_{\chi}
and fractional abundance
f_{\text{DM}}
, we fix
\alpha^{\prime}
according to the cosmologically-motivated value in Eq. (
17
), which in turn is used to determine the dark photon’s effective mass, as in Eq. (
18
). The rest of the procedure is the same as that described in Sec.
III
to place an upper limit on the kinetic mixing
\epsilon
, which can be reexpressed as a limit on the effective charge
q_{\chi}=\epsilon\,e^{\prime}/e
. The resulting FIRAS limits are shown as solid blue lines in Fig.
2
for the parameter space spanned by
q_{\chi}
and
m_{\chi}
and particular choices of
f_{\text{DM}}=0.4\times 10^{-2}
(top-left panel),
10^{-4}
(top-right panel), and
10^{-5}
(bottom panel).

Note that Eq. (
18
) implies that there are no resonant
\gamma\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}
transitions for fixed
f_{\text{DM}}
and very light mCPs, since in this case
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}\gtrsim\omega_{p}
at all redshifts for sufficiently small masses. This corresponds to the sharp edge at the left-most part of the sensitivity regions in Fig.
2

\propto f_{\text{DM}}^{1/2}
(this is visually apparent within the mass range shown only for the top-left panel of Fig.
2

V
Opening Up the Parameter Space for Massive Dark Photons

Above, we investigated the prospects of detecting unbroken symmetries using the presence of mCPs. In doing so, we placed new limits within the mCP parameter space, assuming the simplest cosmologies for mCDM and mCDR. Alternatively, one may ask how the usual bounds on massive dark photons as derived from
\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}
oscillations are altered in the presence of mCP relics. More specifically, we seek to evaluate the robustness of such bounds in regards to minor modifications to the cosmic history. In the presence of mCPs, massive dark photons have two contributions to their effective mass, which can be incorporated by replacing
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}\to m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}+M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}
in the results presented above, where
m_{A^{\prime}}
is the usual in-vacuum dark photon mass and
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}
is the in-medium mCP contribution. The redshift dependence of
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}
depends on whether or not the mCP relics are relativistic (see, e.g., Eq. (
13

M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}\propto(1+z)^{3/2}
for non-relativistic mCPs implies that mCDM can significantly weaken existing cosmological constraints on massive dark photons. On the other hand, the weaker scaling
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}\propto(1+z)
for relativistic mCPs implies that mCDR typically only strengthens such bounds. Since this latter point was already investigated in Sec.
IV.2
, we focus solely on mCDM in this section.

The mCDM contribution to the dark photon effective mass
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}
has the same redshift scaling as the free electron contribution to the SM photon plasma mass
\omega_{p}

\omega_{p}

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we studied the prospects of detecting massless dark photons through a population of cosmic relics charged under the corresponding U(1) gauge symmetry. Such relics can make up a small fraction of the universe’s energy budget but still substantially alter the massless dark photon dispersion relation by inducing a redshift-dependent dark plasma mass. If during the cosmic history, this effective mass is comparable to the Standard Model photon plasma mass, then a kinetic mixing between the sectors will induce
\gamma\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}
interconversion. Most importantly, photons from the cosmic microwave background can convert to dark photons, introducing detectable spectral distortions in their spectrum.

We began by presenting a pedagogical introduction to the millicharged particle-induced
\gamma\leftrightarrow A^{\prime}
resonant interconversion and the resulting distortion of the CMB away from a blackbody spectrum. We then applied the results to both dark matter and dark radiation. For a dark matter subcomponent generated from thermal freeze-out, we showed that the bounds on millicharged particles from CMB spectral distortions are complimentary to stellar and cosmological limits. Furthermore, for heavier dark matter, spectral distortions can close parameter space corresponding to the “overburden region” where dark matter is strongly interacting in the
(1-100)\ \text{GeV}
range and direct detection bounds are substantially weakened. For millicharged dark radiation, we find that FIRAS is sensitive to tiny energy densities, well below current cosmological limits on additional neutrino degrees of freedom. Furthermore, a future satellite experiment similar to the proposed PIXIE mission would be capable of probing the abundance of millicharged particles generated from freeze-in. In the final part of our paper, we discussed how bounds on massive dark photons are altered in the presence of cosmic relics, finding modifications to previously computed bounds that assume the absence of a millicharged particle background. Our results stress the importance of terrestrial experiments as probes of dark photons, since they are less sensitive to the cosmic history of our Universe.

We conclude here by commenting on several possible future developments. First, we note that in our analysis, we treated the universe as homogeneous. Inhomogeneities in the Standard Model plasma (as previously considered in Refs.
Caputo et al. 2020a
;
Caputo et al. 2020b
for massive dark photons) or in the dark plasma can also induce resonant oscillations during low redshifts. This motivates future work to model both the electron and millicharged particle density fluctuations and their impact on CMB spectral distortions. Second, in our study of dark radiation, we considered two possible extremes for the phase space distribution: a thermal phase space peaked at
p_{\chi}\sim T_{\chi}
and a freeze-in phase space peaked at
p_{\chi}\sim T_{\gamma}
. The
\gamma\to A^{\prime}
transition probability depends sensitively on the millicharged particle phase space density and hence plays an important role in deriving the corresponding constraints. Incorporating the phase space evolution due to self-interactions requires solving the full set of Boltzmann equations, which we leave to future work. Lastly, dark sector self-interactions can also suppress the dark plasma mass (as evident from Eq. (
3
)), which in turn suppresses the likelihood of
\gamma\to A^{\prime}
oscillations. This effect is potentially important for ultralight millicharged particles
Bogorad and Toro 2022
, where the large phase space density may lead to Bose-enhanced processes. We leave a detailed study of this regime to future work.

. In this section, we provided two alternative derivations of this result, using either a quantum mechanical formulation (that works explicitly in terms of time instead of frequency), or the Boltzmann equation. For simplicity, we focus on the transition between transverse modes.

B.1
Schrödinger Equation

We begin with the traditional time domain method which was first developed by Landau and Zener
Landau 1932
;
Zener 1932
, and later used within the context of neutrino physics
Parke 1986
;
Kuo and Pantaleone 1989
(see also the discussion in Ref.
Caputo et al. 2020b
). Working in the ultrarelativistic and collisionless limit, the dispersion relation of Eq. (
2
) corresponds to (up to an arbitrary phase) the following Schrödinger-type time-dependent equation

i\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{T}\\
\bm{A}^{\prime}_{T}\end{pmatrix}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}\xi&\eta\\
\eta&-\xi\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{T}\\
\bm{A}^{\prime}_{T}\end{pmatrix}~,

(37)

where
\xi=(M_{\text{eff}}-M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime})/4\omega
and
\eta=\epsilon\,M_{\text{eff}}/2\omega
(we remind the reader that we have shortened our notation such that only the real part is included in
M_{\text{eff}}^{2}
and
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}
). Now, expanding
\bm{A}_{T}
and
\bm{A}_{T}^{\prime}
in terms of the instantaneous basis of the
\order{\eps^0}
Hamiltonian, we have

\bm{A}_{T}=c_{\gamma}(t)\,e^{-i\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\xi(t^{\prime})}~,~\bm{A}_{T}^{\prime}=c_{A^{\prime}}(t)\,e^{i\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\xi(t^{\prime})}~.

(38)

To determine the time-dependent coefficients
c_{\gamma}
and
c_{A^{\prime}}
, we substitute Eq. (
38
) into Eq. (
37
), which yields the following differential equations,

i\partial_{t}c_{\gamma}=\eta\,c_{A^{\prime}}\,e^{2i\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}\xi(t^{\prime})}~,~i\partial_{t}c_{A^{\prime}}=\eta\,c_{\gamma}\,e^{-2i\int^{t}_{-\infty}dt^{\prime}\xi(t^{\prime})}~.

(39)

Since Eq. (
39
) is symmetric under
c_{\gamma}\leftrightarrow c_{A^{\prime}}
, it follows that conversion probability satisfies
P_{\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}}=P_{A^{\prime}\rightarrow\gamma}
. Assuming an initially negligible dark photon density,
\gamma\to A^{\prime}
dominates over the inverse process, corresponding to the initial condition
c_{\gamma}(-\infty)=1
,
c_{A^{\prime}}(-\infty)=0
. For
\epsilon\ll 1
,
c_{\gamma}(t)\simeq 1
and the
\gamma\to A^{\prime}
transition probability at
t=\infty
(to leading order in
\epsilon
) is approximately

P_{\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\simeq|c_{A^{\prime}}(\infty)|^{2}\simeq\absolutevalue{ \eta\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty} dt' e^{-2i \int^{t'}_{-\infty} dt'' \xi(t'')} }^{2}~.

(40)

This integral can be evaluated using the saddle point approximation, which gives

P_{\gamma\to A^{\prime}}\simeq\sum_{t_{\text{res}}}\pi\,\eta^{2}\,\absolutevalue{\frac{d \xi}{dt}}^{-1}\bigg|_{t_{\text{res}}}~,

(41)

where the sum includes all times
t_{\text{res}}
at which
\xi(t_{\text{res}})=0
. Using the definitions of
\eta
and
\xi
, we find that Eq. (
41
) gives
P_{\gamma\to A^{\prime}}
agrees with
-\Delta f_{\gamma}/f_{\gamma}
, where
\Delta f_{\gamma}/f_{\gamma}
is given by integrating Eq. (
8
) over time.

In order to calculate
P_{\gamma\to A^{\prime}}
to the all orders in
\epsilon
, we utilize the Dykhne-Davis-Pechukas (DDP) method
Dykhne 1962
;
Davis and Pechukas 1976
;
Berry 1990
;
Vitanov and Suominen 1999
;
Schilling et al. 2006
. This gives

P_{\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}}=1-\prod_{t_{\text{res}}}e^{-\frac{1}{\omega}\imaginary\int^{t_{c}}_{0}dt~\left(\Pi_{+}-\Pi_{-}\right)}~,

(42)

where

\Pi_{\pm}=\frac{M_{\text{eff}}^{2}+M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{\bigg(\frac{d\left(M_{\text{eff}}^{2}-M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}\right)}{dt}\bigg|_{t_{\text{res}}}\,\bigg)^{2}\,t^{2}+4\,\epsilon^{2}\,M_{\text{eff}}^{4}}

(43)

and

t_{c}=2i\,\epsilon\,M_{\text{eff}}^{2}\,\absolutevalue{\frac{d \left( \meff^2 - \meff^{\p\, 2} \right)}{dt}}^{-1}\,\Bigg|_{t_{\text{res}}}~.

(44)

Performing the time integral in Eq. (
42
) from
t=0
to
t=t_{c}
, we find that the transition probability to all orders in
\epsilon
is

P_{\gamma\to A^{\prime}}\simeq 1-\exp\left(\sum_{t_{\text{res}}}\pi\,\eta^{2}\,\absolutevalue{\frac{d \xi}{dt}}^{-1}\bigg|_{t_{\text{res}}}\right)~.

(45)

The probability for
\gamma\to\gamma
is given by
P_{\gamma\to\gamma}=1-P_{\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}}
.

, we compare the numerical solution of Eq. (
37
) (
|c_{\gamma}|^{2}
in red and
|c_{A^{\prime}}|^{2}
in blue) to the semi-analytic approximation of Eq. (
45
) (dashed gray lines). In the figure, we have defined the dimensionless quantities
\tilde{t}\equiv t\,\absolutevalue{ d \xi(t_\text{res}) / d t}^{-1/2}
and
\tilde{\eta}\equiv\eta\,\absolutevalue{d \xi(t_\text{res}) / d t}^{-1/2}
. The resonantly enhanced transitions occurs when
M_{\text{eff}}^{2}\simeq M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}
, denoted as
\tilde{t}\simeq 0
near the vertical gray line. Before this time,
\absolutevalue{c_\g}\simeq 1
and
\absolutevalue{c_{\Ap}}\simeq 0
. Near the resonance, photons convert to the dark photons. After the resonance,
c_{\gamma}
and
c_{A^{\prime}}

).

B.2
Boltzmann Equation

In this section we provide an alternative derivation of the
\gamma\to A^{\prime}
transition rate, utilizing an explicit calculation involving the Boltzmann equation. The modification of the SM photon phase space density
f_{\gamma}
due to
\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}
is described by

\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{\partial\left(a^{3}f_{\gamma}\right)}{\partial t}\simeq-f_{\gamma}\,\Gamma_{\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}},

(46)

where
a
is the scale factor,

\Gamma_{\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}}=\frac{g_{A^{\prime}}}{2\omega_{\gamma}}\int\frac{d^{3}k_{A^{\prime}}}{2\omega_{A^{\prime}}\,(2\pi)^{3}}\left(2\pi\right)^{4}\delta^{(4)}\left(k_{\gamma}-k_{A^{\prime}}\right)\absolutevalue{\mathcal{M}_{\gamma\rightarrow\Ap}}^{2}~,

(47)

\omega_{i}
,
k_{i}
, and
f_{i}
are the energy, momentum, and phase space density of species
i
,
|\mathcal{M}_{A^{\prime}\to\gamma}|^{2}
is the squared matrix element for
A^{\prime}\to\gamma
averaged over initial and final spin,
g_{A^{\prime}}=2
is the number of internal
A^{\prime}
spin degrees of freedom, and we have taken
f_{A^{\prime}}\simeq 0

Kolb and Turner 1990
.

From Ref.
An et al. 2021
, we have that
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}}=\epsilon\,M_{\text{eff}}^{2}\,\epsilon_{\mu}^{i}(k_{\gamma})\,\epsilon^{j}_{\mu}(k_{A^{\prime}})
, where
\epsilon(k)^{i}
are polarization vectors. Averaging over the SM and dark photon polarizations, this gives
\absolutevalue{\mathcal{M}_{\gamma\rightarrow\Ap}}^{2}\rightarrow\epsilon^{2}M_{\text{eff}}^{4}/2
, such that Eq. (
46
) can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{\partial\left(a^{3}f_{\gamma}\right)}{\partial t}\simeq-f_{\gamma}\frac{\pi\,\epsilon^{2}\,M_{\text{eff}}^{4}}{2\omega_{\gamma}^{2}}\,\delta\left(\omega_{\gamma}-\omega_{A^{\prime}}\right)~,

(48)

where
\omega_{\gamma}\simeq k_{\gamma}+M_{\text{eff}}^{2}/2k_{\gamma}
,
\omega_{A^{\prime}}\simeq k_{A^{\prime}}+M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}/2k_{A^{\prime}}
, and
k_{\gamma}=k_{A^{\prime}}
by energy-momentum conservation. Hence, we finally arrive at

\frac{1}{a^{3}}\frac{\partial\left(a^{3}f_{\gamma}\right)}{\partial t}\simeq-\frac{\pi\,\epsilon^{2}\,f_{\gamma}}{\omega_{\gamma}}\,M_{\text{eff}}^{4}\,\delta\left(M_{\text{eff}}^{2}-M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}\right)~,

(49)

which agrees with Eq. (
8
) in the limit that we can ignore variations to the scale factor during the moment of resonance.

Appendix C
Freeze-in of mCDR

In Sec.
IV.2
, we discussed signals arising from massless dark photons in the presence of mCDR. In particular, we discussed the minimal cosmology in which the mCDR energy density is frozen in from plasmon decay,
\gamma^{*}\to\chi^{+}\chi^{-}
. In this section, we provide a detailed derivation of the number density,
n_{\chi}
, energy density,
\rho_{\chi}
, and resulting effective dark photon mass,
M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}
, produced from mCDR freeze-in. In doing so, we follow the calculations detailed previously in Ref.
Dvorkin et al. 2019
;
Chu et al. 2012
;
Hambye et al. 2019
.

Our starting point is the Boltzmann equation for massless mCPs produced from plasmon decay
\gamma^{*}\to\chi^{+}\chi^{-}

Kolb and Turner 1990
,

\frac{\partial f_{\chi^{+}}}{\partial t}-H\,p_{\chi^{+}}\,\frac{\partial f_{\chi^{+}}}{\partial p_{\chi^{+}}}\simeq\frac{g_{\gamma}\,g_{\chi^{-}}}{2p_{\chi^{+}}}\,\int\frac{{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}^{3}k_{\gamma}}{2\omega_{\gamma}}~\frac{{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}^{3}p_{\chi^{-}}}{2p_{\chi^{-}}}~f_{\gamma}~(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}\left(k_{\gamma}-p_{\chi^{+}}-p_{\chi^{-}}\right)~\overline{\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^{2}}~,

(50)

where
\overline{\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^{2}}
is the matrix element for plasmon decay (averaged over initial and final states),
{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}^{3}p\equiv d^{3}p/(2\pi)^{3}
, and we have neglected the inverse process
\chi^{+}\chi^{-}\rightarrow\gamma^{*}
. This can conveniently rewritten in terms of the plasmon decay rate
\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\to\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}
as

\frac{\partial f_{\chi^{+}}}{\partial t}-H\,p_{\chi^{+}}\,\frac{\partial f_{\chi^{+}}}{\partial p_{\chi^{+}}}\simeq\frac{n_{\gamma}}{g_{\chi^{+}}}\,\bigg\langle\frac{d\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\to\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}}{{\mathchar 22\mkern-12.0mud}^{3}p_{\chi^{+}}}\bigg\rangle~,

(51)

where
n_{\gamma}\simeq(g_{\gamma}\,\zeta(3)/\pi^{2})\,T^{3}
is the plasmon number density and the brackets corresponds to a thermal average over the plasmon phase space. For the remainder of this section, we focus exclusively on the decay of transverse plasmons (
g_{\gamma}=2
), since the longitudinal contribution is subleading
Dvorkin et al. 2019
. Furthermore, for fermionic mCDR we set
g_{\chi^{+}}=g_{\chi^{-}}=2
.

C.1
mCP Number Density

Integrating Eq. (
51
) over the mCP phase space and defining the total mCP number density as
n_{\chi}=n_{\chi^{+}}+n_{\chi^{-}}
, we have

\dot{n}_{\chi}+3Hn_{\chi}=2n_{\gamma}\left\langle\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\to\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}\right\rangle~,

(52)

where from Ref.
Dvorkin et al. 2019
the thermally-averaged decay rate is approximately

\left\langle\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\rightarrow\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}\right\rangle\simeq\frac{\pi\,(eq_{\chi})^{2}}{144\,\zeta(3)}\,\frac{M_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{T_{\gamma}}~.

(53)

Assuming an initially negligible abundance of mCDR, Eq. (
52
) can then be solved in terms of the comoving mCP number density (yield)
Y_{\chi}=n_{\chi}/s_{\gamma}
at late times,

Y_{\chi}\simeq\int^{\infty}_{0}dT_{\gamma}~\frac{2n_{\gamma}}{s_{\gamma}H\,T_{\gamma}}~\left\langle\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\to\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}\right\rangle\simeq 3.3\times 10^{-12}\,\left(\frac{q_{\chi}}{10^{-14}}\right)^{2}~.

(54)

C.2
mCP Energy Density

Multiplying Eq. (
51
) by
p_{\chi}
and then integrating over the mCP phase space yields the following equation for the total mCP energy density

\dot{\rho}_{\chi}+4H\rho_{\chi}=n_{\gamma}\left\langle\omega_{\gamma}\,\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\rightarrow\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}\right\rangle,

(55)

where

\left\langle\omega_{\gamma}\,\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\to\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}\right\rangle\simeq\frac{(eq_{\chi})^{2}}{12\pi}\,M_{\text{eff}}^{2}~.

(56)

Analogous to
Y_{\chi}
, we define the quantity
A_{\chi}\equiv\rho_{\chi}/s_{\gamma}^{4/3}
. Similar to the previous subsection, Eq. (
55
) can then be solved to determine
A_{\chi}
at late times

A_{\chi}=\int^{\infty}_{0}dT~\frac{n_{\gamma}}{s_{\gamma}^{4/3}H\,T_{\gamma}}\,\left\langle\omega_{\gamma}\,\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\rightarrow\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}\right\rangle~.

(57)

We evaluate Eq. (
57
) numerically in order to determine the additional number of effective neutrino degrees of freedom in mCDR. This gives

\Delta N_{\text{eff}}\simeq 2\times 10^{-11}\left(\frac{q_{\chi}}{10^{-14}}\right)^{2}~.

(58)

C.3
Dark Plasmon Mass

From Eq. (
19
), the effective dark photon mass arising from mCDR is

M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}\simeq\frac{8\alpha^{\prime}}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}dp_{\chi}~p_{\chi}\,f_{\chi}~.

(59)

Multiplying Eq. (
51
) by
1/p_{\chi}
and then integrating over the mCP phase space gives

\frac{\partial M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}}{\partial t}+2H\,M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}=16\pi\alpha^{\prime}\,n_{\gamma}\,\left\langle\frac{\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\rightarrow\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}}{\omega_{\gamma}}\right\rangle~,

(60)

where

\left\langle\frac{\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\rightarrow\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}}{\omega_{\gamma}}\right\rangle\simeq\frac{(eq_{\chi})^{2}}{24\pi\,\zeta(3)}\frac{M_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{T_{\gamma}^{2}}\,\log{\bigg(\frac{T_\g}{\meff}\bigg)}~.

(61)

Solving Eq. (
60
) for
B_{\chi}\equiv M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime\,2}/s_{\gamma}^{2/3}
yields

B_{\chi}\simeq 16\pi\alpha^{\prime}\int^{\infty}_{0}dT_{\gamma}~\frac{n_{\gamma}}{s^{2/3}_{\gamma}H\,T_{\gamma}}\left\langle\frac{\Gamma_{\gamma^{*}\rightarrow\chi^{+}\chi^{-}}}{\omega_{\gamma}}\right\rangle~.

(62)

The above expression can be evaluated numerically, which gives

M_{\text{eff}}^{\prime}\simeq 3\times 10^{-17}\ \text{eV}\times\bigg(\frac{q_{\chi}}{10^{-14}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{10^{-7}}{\epsilon}\bigg)\,\left(1+z\right)~.

(63)

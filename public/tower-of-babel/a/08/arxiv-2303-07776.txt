\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq k\leq y_{N}-Ma_{m}}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}=y_{N}-k\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}\geq Ma_{m}\right).

Since
\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}\geq Ma_{m}\right)=0
, it follows that

R_{3}(M,m,N)\leq\varepsilon(M)b_{N}\text{$V$}^{-}(x_{N})\text{$V$}^{+}(y_{N}),

(43)

where
\varepsilon(M)\downarrow 0
as
M\rightarrow\infty.

Further, in view of (
15
) and (
7
)

\displaystyle R_{4}(M,m,N)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle(1-\zeta)g(0)b_{N-m}\text{$V$}^{-}(x_{N})\sum_{y_{N}-Ma_{m}<k\leq y_{N}+za_{m}}\text{$V$}^{+}(k)\mathbb{P}_{k}\left(S_{m}=y_{N},L_{m}\geq 0\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle(1-\zeta)g(0)b_{N}\text{$V$}^{-}(x_{N})\text{$V$}^{+}(y_{N})\sum_{y_{N}-Ma_{m}<k\leq y_{N}+za_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{k}\left(S_{m}=y_{N},L_{m}\geq 0\right)

uniformly in
x_{N}=o(a_{N})
and
y_{N}=o(a_{N})
. Thus, it remains to
evaluate the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{y_{N}-Ma_{m}<k\leq y_{N}+za_{m}}\mathbb{P}_{k}\left(S_{m}=y_{N},L_{m}\geq 0\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{y_{N}-Ma_{m}<k\leq y_{N}+za_{m}}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}=y_{N}-k,L_{m}\geq-k\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{-Ma_{m}<j\leq za_{m}}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}=j,L_{m}\geq j-y_{N}\right).

Clearly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}\in[-Ma_{m},za_{m}],L_{m}\geq za_{m}-y_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{-Ma_{m}<j\leq za_{m}}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}=j,L_{m}\geq j-y_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}\in[-Ma_{m},za_{m}]\right).

Further,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}\in[-Ma_{m},za_{m}],L_{m}\geq za_{m}-y_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S_{m}\in[-Ma_{m},za_{m}]\right)

\displaystyle-\mathbb{P}\left(L_{m}<za_{m}-y_{N}\right).

Since
y_{N}/a_{m}\rightarrow\infty
, it follows by the invariance
principle for random walks whose increments have distributions belonging
without centering to the domain of attraction of a stable law that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{L_{m}}{a_{m}}<z-\frac{y_{N}}{a_{m}}\right)=0.

Observing that

if
m=o(N)
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, we
get

R_{4}(M,m,N)\sim(1-\zeta)g(0)b_{N}\text{$V$}^{-}(x_{N})\text{$V$}^{+}(y_{N})\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{1}\in\left[-M,z\right]\right)

as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Since

may be selected arbitrary large and
\varepsilon(M)>0
in (
43

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x_{N}}\left(S_{N-m}-y_{N}\leq za_{m};L_{N}\geq 0,S_{n}=y_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\sim(1-\zeta)g(0)b_{N}\text{$V$}^{-}(x_{N})\text{$V$}^{+}(y_{N})\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{1}\leq z\right)

uniformly in
x_{N}=o(a_{N})
and
y_{N}=o(a_{N})

A1
and A2. Let

\mathcal{\ }\left\{S_{j},j=0,1,...,k\right\}

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}\left[H_{n};\Psi\left(n,m,za_{m},\varphi\right)\right]-A_{1}(z\lambda^{-1/\alpha})\mathbb{E}_{0}^{\uparrow}\left[H_{\infty}\right]\mathbb{P}\left(\Psi\left(\lambda n,\lambda m,za_{m},\varphi\right)\right)|

by (

6
Branching processes in random environment

In this section we apply the results obtained for random walks to study the
population size of the critical branching processes evolving in unfavorable
random environments. To describe the model and the problems we plan to
consider we intruduce the space
\mathcal{M}

=\left\{\mathfrak{f}\right\}
of all probability measures on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For notational reason, we
identify a measure
\mathfrak{f}=\left\{\mathfrak{f}(\left\{0\right\}),\mathfrak{f}(\left\{1\right\}),...\right\}\in

\mathcal{M}
with the
respective probability generating function

f(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{f}(\left\{k\right\})s^{k},\quad s\in[0,1],

and make no difference between
\mathfrak{f}
and
f
. Equipped with a
metric,
\mathcal{M}

=\left\{\mathfrak{f}\right\}=\left\{f\right\}

becomes a Polish space. Let

F(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}F\left(\left\{j\right\}\right)s^{j},\quad s\in[0,1],

be a random variable taking values in
\mathcal{M}
, and let

F_{n}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}F_{n}\left(\left\{j\right\}\right)s^{j},\quad s\in[0,1],\quad n\in\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\left\{0\right\},

be a sequence of independent copies of
F
. The infinite sequence
\mathcal{E}=\left\{F_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is called a random environment.

A sequence of nonnegative random variables
\mathcal{Z}=\left\{Z_{n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
specified on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is called a branching process in random environment
(BPRE), if
Z_{0}
is independent of
\mathcal{E}
and, given
\mathcal{E}

the process
\mathcal{Z}
is a Markov chain with

\mathcal{L}\left(Z_{n}|Z_{n-1}=z_{n-1},\mathcal{E}=(f_{1},f_{2},...)\right)=\mathcal{L}(\xi_{n1}+\ldots+\xi_{ny_{n-1}})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
z_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f_{1},f_{2},...\in\mathcal{M}
, where
\xi_{n1},\xi_{n2},\ldots
is a
sequence of i.i.d. random variables with distribution
f_{n}.
Thus,
Z_{n-1}
is the
(n-1)
th generation size of the population of the
branching process and
f_{n}
is the offspring distribution of an individual
at generation
n-1
.

The sequence

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{n}=X_{1}+...+X_{n},\ n\geq 1,

where
X_{i}=\log F_{i}^{\prime}(1),i=1,2,...
is called the associated
random walk for the process
\mathcal{Z}
.

We impose the following restrictions on the properties of the BPRE.

Condition B1.

The elements of the associated random walk satisfy Hypothesis A1 and
A2.

According to the classification of BPREs (see, for instance,
[
1
]

and
[
18
]
), Condition B1 means that we consider the critical BPRE’s.

Our second assumption on the environment concerns reproduction laws of
particles. Set

\gamma(b)=\frac{\sum_{k=b}^{\infty}k^{2}F\left(\left\{k\right\}\right)}{\left(\sum_{i=b}^{\infty}iF\left(\left\{i\right\}\right)\right)^{2}}.

Condition B2.

There exist
\varepsilon>0
and
b\in
\mathbb{N}

such that

\mathbb{E}[(\log^{+}\gamma(b))^{\alpha+\varepsilon}]\ <\ \infty\ ,

where
\log^{+}x=\log(\max(x,1))
.

It is known (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2]
) that if
Conditions B1, B2 are valid then there exist a number
\theta\in(0,\infty)
and a sequence
l(1),l(2)...,
slowly varying at infinity such that, as
n\rightarrow\infty

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}>0\right)\sim\theta n^{-(1-\rho)}l(n)

and for any
t\in\left[0,1\right]
and any
x\geq 0

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log Z_{\left[nt\right]}}{a_{n}}\leq x|Z_{n}>0\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{S_{\left[nt\right]}}{a_{n}}\leq x|Z_{n}>0\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{t}^{+}\leq x\right),

(52)

where
\mathcal{Y}^{+}=\left\{Y_{t}^{+},0\leq t\leq 1\right\}
denotes the
meander of the strictly stable process
\mathcal{Y}
with index
\alpha
.

Thus, if a BPRE is critical, then, given
Z_{n}>0
the random variables
\log Z_{\left[nt\right]},t\in(0,1],
and
S_{n},
the value of the
associated random walk that provides survival of the population to a distant
moment
n
, grow like
a_{n}
times random positive multipliers.

These results were complemented in
[
27
]
by considering the properly
scaled distributions of the random variables
\log Z_{\left[nt\right]},t\in(0,1]
given that
Z_{n}>0
and
S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),
where
\varphi(n)\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in such a way that

\varphi(n)=o(a_{n})
. In view of (
52
) the event
\left\{S_{n}\leq\varphi(n)\right\}
may be treated in this case as an
unfavorable one for the development of a critical branching process given
its survival.

Introduce the notation
A_{u.s}:=\left\{Z_{n}>0\text{ for all }n>0\right\}

for the event of ultimate survival. Observe that according to Theorem 1 in

[
26
]
, if Conditions B1, B2 are valid, the distribution of the
increments of the associated random walk is absolutely continuous and
\varphi(n)=o(a_{n})

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Theta\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),L_{n}>0\right)

(53)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\Theta}{na_{n}}\int_{0}^{\varphi(n)}V^{+}(w)dw,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where

\Theta=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(Z_{j}=k,\tau_{j}=j)\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}\left(A_{u.s}|Z_{0}=k\right)\in(0,\infty).

(54)

The same asymptotics is valid for
(1;0)
-lattice case, where
\int_{0}^{\varphi(n)}V^{+}(w)dw
should be replaced by
\sum_{j=0}^{\varphi(n)}V^{+}(j).

Along with the asymptotic behavior of the survival probability of a critical
BPRE, the growth of the population size of such a process at the logarithmic
scale was described in the following theorem.

Theorem 15

(see
[
27
]
) Let Conditions B1, B2 be valid. If
\varphi(n)\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in such a way that

\varphi(n)=o(a_{n})
then for any

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\log Z_{n}\leq y|S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)=y^{\alpha\rho+1}

(55)

and for any

where

\Theta_{j}(K):=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{j}=k,\tau_{j}=j\right)\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}\left(A_{u.s}|Z_{0}=k\right).

Note that
\Theta_{j}(\infty)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{j}=j\right)
.

Applying the dominated convergence theorem we have for fixed
K<\infty

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n-m}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n)),\tau_{n}=j,S_{j}>-\sqrt{\varphi(n)},Z_{j}\leq K\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\sqrt{\varphi(n)}}^{0}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{j}\in dq,Z_{j}=k,\tau_{j}=j\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[H_{n-j}(k);S_{n-m-j}\leq za_{m},Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n)-q)\right]}{\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=A_{1}(z)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{j}\in dq,Z_{j}=k,\tau_{j}=j\right)\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}\left(A_{u.s}|Z_{0}=k\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=A_{1}(z)\Theta_{j}(K).

(69)

Combining the obtained estimates and letting
K\rightarrow\infty
we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n-m}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n)),\tau_{n}=j\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n))\right)}=A_{1}(z)\Theta_{j}(\infty),

or, in view of (
53
)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n-m}\leq za_{m},S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0,\tau_{n}=j\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)}=A_{1}(z)\frac{\Theta_{j}(\infty)}{\Theta}.

Summing over
j
and taking into account the definition of
\Theta
in (
54
), we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{n-m}\leq za_{m},S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)}=A_{1}(z),

as desired.

2) Assume now that
\varphi(n)\sim Ta_{m},T\in(0,\infty)
. Then,
recalling (
48
) and applying the dominated convergence theorem we
obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n-m}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n)),\tau_{n}=j,S_{j}>-\sqrt{\varphi(n)},Z_{j}\leq K\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{-\sqrt{\varphi(n)}}^{0}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{j}\in dq,Z_{j}=k,\tau_{j}=j\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[H_{n-j}(k);S_{n-m-j}\leq za_{m},Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n)-q)\right]}{\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=B(z,T)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\left(S_{j}\in dq,Z_{j}=k,\tau_{j}=j\right)\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}\left(A_{u.s}|Z_{0}=k\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=B(z,T)\Theta_{j}(K).

Summing over
j
and taking into account (
53
) and the
definition of
\Theta
, we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{n-m}\leq za_{m},S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)}=B(z,T),

as desired.

3) Finally, consider the case
m=m(n)\rightarrow\infty
and
a_{m}=o\left(\varphi(n)\right)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We introduce the notation
S_{n-m,n}:=S_{n-m}-S_{n}
and for
z\in(-\infty,\infty)
write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{n-m}-S_{n}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n))\right)

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\log\hat{Z}_{n-m}+S_{n-m,n}\leq za_{m},\hat{Z}_{n-m}\in(M^{-1},M),R_{n}(\varphi(n))\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{n-m}-S_{n}\leq za_{m},\hat{Z}_{n}\notin(M^{-1},M),R_{n}(\varphi(n))\right).

Now it is not difficult to check that if we replace in all the relations
between (
61
) and (
63
)
S_{n-m}
by
S_{n-m,n}

and
S_{n-m-j}
by
S_{n-m-j,n-j}
then all the estimates between (
61
) and (
63
) remain valid. As a result,

\lim_{J\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{n-m}-S_{n}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n)),\tau_{n}>J\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(R_{n}(\varphi(n))\right)}=0

and, for each fixed
j\in[0,J]

\lim_{K\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{n-m}-S_{n}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n)),\tau_{n}=j,Z_{j}>K,S_{j}>-\sqrt{\varphi(n)}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(R_{n}(\varphi(n))\right)}=0.

Recalling (
49
) and applying the dominated convergence theorem, we
obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n-m,n}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n)),\tau_{n}=j,S_{j}>-\sqrt{\varphi(n)},Z_{j}\leq K\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{1}\leq z\right)\Theta_{j}(K).

Combining the estimates above and letting
K
to infinity, we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n-m,n}\leq za_{m},R_{n}(\varphi(n)),\tau_{n}=j,S_{j}>-\sqrt{\varphi(n)}\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{n-j}(\varphi(n))\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{1}\leq z\right)\Theta_{j}(\infty).

Summing over
j
, taking into account (
53
) and the definition
of
\Theta
, we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\log Z_{n-m}-S_{n}\leq za_{m},S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}\leq\varphi(n),Z_{n}>0\right)}=\mathbf{P}\left(Y_{1}\leq z\right).

Theorem
16
is proved.

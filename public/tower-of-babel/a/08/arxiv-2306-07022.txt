1.
Introduction and preliminaries

The aim of this paper is to obtain a bidisc counter-part of the theory of Dirichlet-type spaces of the open unit disc as presented in
[
26
]
(see
[
8
]
for a ball counter-part of this theory). Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{D}
denotes the open unit disc
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}.

Recall that Dirichlet-type spaces of
\mathbb{D}
are model spaces for the class of cyclic analytic
2
-isometries (see
[
26
]
). Thus to arrive at an appropriate notion of the Dirichlet-type spaces of the unit bidisc
\mathbb{D}^{2}
, it is helpful to look for function spaces which support the class of
2
-isometries naturally associated with
\mathbb{D}^{2}.
Let us first recall the definition of such
2
-isometries.

For a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the
C^{*}
-algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}.

For a positive integer
d,
a
commuting
d
-tuple
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is the
d
-tuple

(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
of operators
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
satisfying
T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i},

1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant d.
Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a commuting
d
-tuple on
\mathcal{H}
. We say that
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is a
toral isometry
if
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
are isometries.
Following
[
1
,
5
,
26
]
,
T
is said to be a
toral
2
-isometry
if

\displaystyle I-T^{*}_{i}T_{i}-T^{*}_{j}T_{j}+T^{*}_{j}T^{*}_{i}T_{i}T_{j}=0,\quad i,j=1,\ldots,d.

(1.1)

A toral isometry is necessarily a toral
2
-isometry, but the converse is not true (see
[
5
, Example 1]
).

To propose a successful analog of Dirichlet-type spaces on
\mathbb{D}^{2},
it is helpful to examine examples of toral
2
-isometries arising from function spaces. Since the operator of multiplication by the coordinate function on the classical Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})
is a
2
-isometry, it is natural to seek the classical Dirichlet space of the unit bidisc.
Recall that the
Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})
of
\mathbb{D}^{2}

is given by

\displaystyle\Big\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}^{2}):\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})}:=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}}|\hat{f}(m,n)|^{2}(m+1)(n+1)<\infty\Big\},

where
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
denotes the space of holomorphic functions on a domain
\Omega,

\mathbb{Z}_{+}
denotes the set of nonnegative integers and
\hat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f.

It turns out that if
\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}}
are the operators of multiplication by the coordinate functions
z_{1}
and
z_{2},
respectively, on
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}),
then
the commuting pair
(\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}},\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}})
satisfies (
1.1
) for
1\leqslant i=j\leqslant 2,
but it fails to satisfy (
1.1
) for
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant 2.

This failure may be attributed to the fact that the mapping
(m,n)\mapsto\|z^{m}_{1}z^{n}_{2}\|^{2}
is a polynomial of bi-degree
(1,1).
Interestingly, there is a “natural” choice
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
of the Dirichlet space containing
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D})
for which the associated pair
(\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}},\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}})
is a toral
2
-isometry:

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}^{2})=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}^{2}):\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}:=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}}|\hat{f}(m,n)|^{2}(m+n+1)<\infty\Big\}.

The norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}
can also be written as follows:

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}+\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}f(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}\,dA(z_{1})d\theta

(1.2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}f(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}\,dA(z_{2})d\theta,

where
d\theta
(resp.

dA
) denotes the
normalized Lebesgue arc-length
(
resp. area
)
measure
on
\mathbb{T}
(resp.
\mathbb{D}
).
Recall that the
Hardy space

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
of the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{d}
is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (see
[
23
]
for the definition of the reproducing kernel Hilbert space) associated with the
Cauchy kernel

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1-z_{j}\overline{w}_{j})^{-1},\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}),~w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{d})\in\mathbb{D}^{d},

where
d
is a positive integer.
It is worth noting that for any
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d}),

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}}|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}

(1.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{d}}|f(re^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,re^{i\theta_{d}})|^{2}d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{d}

(1.4)

(see
[
27
, Section 3.4]
).

For a nonempty subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{C}^{d},
let

M_{+}(\Omega)
denote the set of finite positive Borel measures on
\Omega.
Let

P_{\mu}(w)
denote the
Poisson integral

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{|w-\zeta|^{2}}d\mu(\zeta)
of the measure
\mu\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}).

For future reference, we record the following consequence of the Fubini theorem (see
[
28
, Theorem 8.8]
) and the fact that the mapping
r\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(z,re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta
is increasing.

Lemma 1.1
.

For
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}^{d+1})
and
\mu\in M_{+}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
, the extended real-valued mapping

\phi(r)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{d}}|f(z,re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\mu(z)d\theta,

r\in(0,1),
is increasing.

The formula (
1.2
) together with Richter’s notion of Dirichlet-type spaces (see
[
26
, Sect. 3]
) motivates us to the following:

Definition 1.2
.

For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T})
and
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}^{2}),

the Dirichlet integral

D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f)
of
f
is given by

\displaystyle D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}f(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}f(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta.

If either
\mu_{1}
or
\mu_{2}
is
0,
then the
Dirichlet-type space

\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the space of functions
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
satisfying

Otherwise, we set
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D}^{2}):D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f)<\infty\}.

Before we define a norm on the Dirichlet-type space
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),
we present a
2
-variable analog of
[
26
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 1.3
.

For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}),

\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\subseteq H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2}).

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),
we may assume that both measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are non-zero.
Note that

\displaystyle P_{\mu}(w)\geqslant\frac{\mu(\mathbb{T})}{4}(1-|w|^{2}),\quad\mu\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}),~w\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.5)

Thus, for any
f(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}a_{m,n}\,z^{m}_{1}z^{n}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}f(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{Poisson-lb}}{\geqslant}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{1}(\mathbb{T})}{4}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{m,n}|^{2}m^{2}r^{2n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z^{m-1}_{1}|^{2}(1-|z_{1}|^{2})dA(z_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{1}(\mathbb{T})}{4}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{m,n}|^{2}\frac{mr^{2n}}{m+1}.

A similar estimate using (
1.5
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}f(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta\geqslant\frac{\mu_{2}(\mathbb{T})}{4}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{m,n}|^{2}\frac{nr^{2m}}{n+1}.

Since
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{m,n}|^{2}\frac{mr^{2n}}{m+1}<\infty,\quad\sup_{0<r<1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{m,n}|^{2}\frac{nr^{2m}}{n+1}<\infty.

It is now easy to see using the monotone convergence theorem (see
[
28
, Theorem 1.26]
) that

f
belongs to
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2}).

∎

In view of Lemmas
1.1
and
1.3
, the Dirichlet-type space

can be endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}+\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}f(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}f(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta.

We see that

is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (see Lemma

).

The present paper is devoted to the study of Dirichlet-type spaces with efforts to understand the bidisc counter-part of the work carried out in
[
26
]
.
Before we state the main results of this paper, we need some definitions.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space such that
\mathscr{H}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Omega).

A function
\varphi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be a
multiplier
of
\mathscr{H}
if
\varphi f\in\mathscr{H}
for every
f\in\mathscr{H}.

For a nonempty subset
U
of
\Omega,
we say that
Gleason’s problem can be solved for
\mathscr{H}
over
U
if for every
f\in\mathscr{H}
and
\lambda\in U,
there exist functions
g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}
in
\mathscr{H}
such that

\displaystyle f(z)=f(\lambda)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}(z_{j}-\lambda_{j})g_{j}(z),\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in\Omega.

We say that
Gleason’s problem can be solved for
\mathscr{H}
if Gleason’s problem can be solved for
\mathscr{H}
over
\Omega
(the reader is referred to
[
31
]
for a solution of Gleason’s problem for Bergman and Bloch spaces of the unit ball). It turns out that Gleason’s problem can be solved for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{d})
(see Remark
5.2
).

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space such that
\mathscr{H}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Omega).

We say that

\mathscr{H}
has
the
j
-division property

j=1,\ldots,d,
if
\frac{f(z)}{z_{j}-\lambda_{j}}
defines a function in
\mathscr{H}

whenever
\lambda\in\Omega,

f\in\mathscr{H}
and
\{z\in\Omega:z_{j}=\lambda_{j}\}
is contained in
Z(f),
the zero set of
f.
If
\mathscr{H}
has
j
-division property for every
j=1,\ldots,d,
then we say that
\mathscr{H}
has
the
division property
.

In case of
d=1,
this property appeared in
[
3
, Definition 1.1]
.
One of the main results of this paper shows that
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
has the division property.
In what follows, we require a generalization of the notion of the wandering subspace introduced by Halmos (see
[
20
, P. 103]
).

Definition 1.5
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a commuting
d
-tuple on
\mathcal{H}.
A closed subspace
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
wandering
for

if for every

\displaystyle\prod_{{j=1}}^{d}T^{\alpha_{j}}_{j}\mathcal{W}\perp\prod_{j=1}^{d}T^{\beta_{j}}_{j}\mathcal{W},\quad\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},~j=1,\ldots,d,~\alpha_{i}=0,~\beta_{i}\neq 0.

Remark 1.6
.

If
d=1,
then
\mathcal{W}
is a wandering subspace for
T
if and only if
\mathcal{W}\perp T^{k}(\mathcal{W})
for every integer
k\geqslant 1.
In particular,
\ker T^{*}
is a wandering subspace for any
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).
Moreover, if
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is a commuting
d
-tuple such that
T^{*}_{j}T_{i}=T_{i}T^{*}_{j},

1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant d,
then
\ker T^{*}
is a wandering subspace for
T.

It follows from Remark
1.6
that the space spanned by the constant function
1
is a wandering subspace for the multiplication
2
-tuple
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2}).
Interestingly, this fact extends to the multiplication
2
-tuple
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
(see Corollary

).

Recall that a commuting
d
-tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
on
\mathcal{H}
is
cyclic
with
cyclic vector

f_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
if
\bigvee\big\{T^{\alpha}f_{0}:\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}\big\}=\mathcal{H},
where

denotes the closed linear span and

For later purpose, we state the following property of cyclic tuples (see

If
T
is cyclic, then for any
\omega\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

\dim\ker(T^{*}-\omega)
is at most
1,

(1.6)

where
\ker S=\cap_{j=1}^{d}\ker S_{j}
for the
d
-tuple
S=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})

and

A commuting
d
-tuple
T
on
\mathcal{H}
has the

\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}T^{\alpha}(\ker T^{*}).

Following

, we say that a commuting

\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{k}}T^{\alpha}\mathcal{H}=\{0\},

\Gamma_{k}:=\{\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}:\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}=k\}.

Note that if

is analytic, then
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
are analytic.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in

For a positive integer

-tuples

\bullet

\bullet

.

We call the set

[

for the basic theory of Cowen-Douglas class).

2.
Statements of main theorems

The following three theorems collect several basic properties of Dirichlet-type spaces
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Theorem 2.1
.

For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}),
we have the following statements
:

\mathrm{(i)}

the coordinate functions
z_{1},z_{2}
are multipliers of
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),

\mathrm{(ii)}

the polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),

\mathrm{(iii)}

for non-negative integers
k,l
and a polynomial
p
in
z_{1}
and
z_{2},

\displaystyle\|z^{k}_{1}z^{l}_{2}p\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|p\|^{2}_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}+k\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|p(e^{i\eta},e^{i\theta})|^{2}d\mu_{1}(\eta)d\theta

(2.7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle l\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|p(e^{i\theta},e^{i\eta})|^{2}d\mu_{2}(\eta)d\theta.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}),

\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
has the division property.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}),
Gleason’s problem can be solved for
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
over
\mathbb{D}^{2}_{r}

Here

denotes the bidisc

where

is a positive real number. Unlike the one variable situation, we do not know whether Gleason’s problem can be solved for
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
over the unit bidisc.
It is worth noting that not all facts about Dirichlet-type spaces of the unit disc have successful counterparts in the bidisc case. For example, the commuting pair

The following result asserts that

on

is a canonical model for

2
-isometries for which
\ker T^{*}
is a cyclic wandering subspace.

Theorem 2.4

(A representation theorem)
.

Let
T=(T_{1},T_{2})
be a commuting pair on
\mathcal{H}.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

-isometry with cyclic vector

\ker T^{*}
is a wandering subspace for
T,

(ii)

is a cyclic toral

-isometry with cyclic vector

belongs to
{\bf B}_{1}(\mathbb{D}^{2}_{r})

\ker T^{*}
is a wandering subspace for

(iii)

\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T})
such that

is unitarily equivalent to
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Theorems

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
and its consequences

We need several lemmas to prove Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Dirichlet-type space
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. If
\kappa:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),
then for any
r\in(0,1),

\bigvee\{\kappa(\cdot,w):|w|<r\}=\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
and

\kappa(\cdot,0)=1.

Proof.

We borrow an argument from the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 1.6.3]
.
Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).
Since
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
is complete (see
[
27
, p 53]
), there exists a
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
such that
\|f_{n}-f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Moreover, since
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, for every
j=1,2,

\partial_{j}f_{n}
converges compactly to
\partial_{j}f
on
\mathbb{D}^{2}.

Also, since
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
, by Lemma
1.1
,
there exists an
M>0
such that for every integer
n\geqslant 0
and
r\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}f_{n}(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta<M,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}f_{n}(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta<M.

By Fatou’s lemma (see
[
28
, Lemma 1.28]
), for any
r\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}f(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\liminf_{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}f_{n}(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta\leqslant M.

Similarly, one can see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}f(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta\leqslant M,\quad r\in(0,1).

This shows that
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

We may now argue as in (
3.8
) (with
f
replaced by
f_{n}-f
and
f_{n}
replaced by
f_{n}-f_{m}
) and use Fatou’s lemma to conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}(f_{n}-f)(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\liminf_{m}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{1}(f_{n}-f_{m})(z_{1},re^{i\theta})|^{2}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}(f_{n}-f)(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\liminf_{m}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\partial_{2}(f_{n}-f_{m})(re^{i\theta},z_{2})|^{2}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta.

These two estimates combined with Lemma
1.1
yield

\displaystyle D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f_{n}-f)\leqslant\liminf_{m}D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f_{n}-f_{m}),\quad n\geqslant 0.

This shows that
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
converges to
f
in
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).
Finally, since
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, so is
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
(see Lemma
1.3
).

To see the ‘moreover’ part, note that for any
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),
by the reproducing property of
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{1}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}=\langle{f},\,{1}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}=f(0)=\langle{f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,0)}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})},

and hence
\kappa(\cdot,0)=1.
The rest follows from the reproducing property of
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
together with an application of the identity theorem.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function.
For

and

consider the holomorphic function

then

for every

and

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|f_{r,\theta}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}d\theta=\|f\|^{2}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})},\quad f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2}).

(3.9)

Proof.

The proof relies on the formula (
1.3
).
First, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}_{r,\theta}(m)|^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Big|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(m,n)r^{n}e^{in\theta}\Big|^{2}.

(3.10)

If
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2}),
then applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to (
3.10
) gives that
f_{r,\theta}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
for every
r\in(0,1)
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi].
Moreover, integrating both sides of (

) with respect to
\theta

[0,2\pi]
yields (
3.9
).

Remark 3.3
.

We note that

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{M}_{w}f(\cdot,re^{i\theta})\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1})}\leqslant\|\mathscr{M}_{w}\|\|f(\cdot,re^{i\theta})\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1})}~\mbox{for~a.e.}\,\theta\in[0,2\pi].

\displaystyle D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(z_{1}f)

\displaystyle=

For
R=(R_{1},R_{2})\in(0,1)^{2}

Lemma 3.6
.

For any

Here is a key step in deducing the density of polynomials in
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),

\lim_{R_{1},R_{2}\rightarrow 1^{-}}D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f-f_{R})=0.

Proof.

The proof is an adaptation of that of
[
14
, Theorem 7.3.1]
to the present situation.
For
R=(R_{1},R_{2})\in(0,1)^{2},
by the Parallelogram law (which holds for any seminorm) and Lemma
3.6
,

\displaystyle D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f-f_{R})+D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f+f_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f)+D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f_{R}))

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f).

We claim that

\displaystyle\liminf_{R_{1},R_{2}\rightarrow 1^{-}}D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f+f_{R})\geqslant 4D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f).

(3.15)

To see this, fix
r\in(0,1).
By Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\liminf_{R_{1},R_{2}\rightarrow 1^{-}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{1}}((f+f_{R})(\cdot,re^{i\theta}))d\theta+\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{2}}((f+f_{R})(re^{i\theta},\cdot))d\theta\Big)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle 4\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{1}}(f(\cdot,re^{i\theta}))d\theta+\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{2}}(f(re^{i\theta},\cdot))d\theta\Big).

On the other hand, by Lemma
1.1
,

\displaystyle D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f+f_{R})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{1}}((f+f_{R})(\cdot,re^{i\theta}))d\theta+\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{2}}((f+f_{R})(re^{i\theta},\cdot))d\theta.

After taking
\liminf
on both sides (one by one) and applying (
3.16
), we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{R_{1},R_{2}\rightarrow 1^{-}}D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f+f_{R})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle 4\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{1}}(f(\cdot,re^{i\theta}))d\theta+\int_{\mathbb{T}}D_{\mu_{2}}(f(re^{i\theta},\cdot))d\theta\Big).

Letting
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
on the right-hand side now yields (
3.15
) (see (
3.12
)). Finally, note that by (
3.14
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{R_{1},R_{2}\rightarrow 1^{-}}D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f-f_{R})\leqslant 4D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f)-\liminf_{R_{1},R_{2}\rightarrow 1^{-}}D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f+f_{R}),

and hence by (
3.15
), we get

\limsup_{R_{1},R_{2}\rightarrow 1^{-}}D_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}(f-f_{R})=0,

which completes the proof.

.

and

, respectively. To see (ii),

Before we state the next application of Theorem
2.1
, we need a formula for the inner-product of monomials in
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

Proposition 3.10
.

For
\mu\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T})
and
j\geqslant 0,
let
\hat{\mu}(j)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\zeta^{-j}d\mu(\zeta).
Then

\displaystyle\langle{z^{m}_{1}z^{n}_{2}},\,{z^{p}_{1}z^{q}_{2}}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if}~m\neq p,~n\neq q,\\
\min\{n,q\}\hat{\mu}_{2}(q-n)&\mbox{if}~m=p,~n\neq q,\\
\min\{m,p\}\hat{\mu}_{1}(p-m)&\mbox{if}~m\neq p,~n=q,\\
1+m\hat{\mu}_{1}(0)+n\hat{\mu}_{2}(0)&\mbox{if}~m=p,~n=q.\end{cases}

(3.18)

In particular, the monomials are orthogonal in
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
if and only if
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are nonnegative multiples of the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

Fix non-negative integers
m,n,p,q.

By the polarization identity,

\displaystyle\langle{z^{m}_{1}z^{n}_{2}},\,{z^{p}_{1}z^{q}_{2}}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}=\langle{z^{m}_{1}z^{n}_{2}},\,{z^{p}_{1}z^{q}_{2}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}r^{n+q}e^{i(n-q)\theta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\partial_{1}(z^{m}_{1})\,\overline{\partial_{1}(z^{p}_{1})}P_{\mu_{1}}(z_{1})\,dA(z_{1})d\theta

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}r^{m+p}e^{i(m-p)\theta}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\partial_{2}(z^{n}_{2})\,\overline{\partial_{2}(z^{q}_{2})}P_{\mu_{2}}(z_{2})\,dA(z_{2})d\theta.

Since
\langle{z^{m}_{1}z^{n}_{2}},\,{z^{p}_{1}z^{q}_{2}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})}=\delta(m,p)\delta(n,q)
with
\delta(\cdot,\cdot)
denoting the Kronecker delta of two variables, (
3.18
) may be deduced from the following formula for the inner-product of the Dirichlet-type space
\mathcal{D}(\mu)
(see
[
22
, Equation (3.2)]
):

\displaystyle\langle z^{r},z^{s}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}(\mu)}=\delta(r,s)+\min\{r,s\}\hat{\mu}(s-r),\quad r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

(3.19)

(this formula may also be derived directly using
[
28
, Theorem 11.9]
).
The “In particular” part follows from the Weierstrass approximation theorem and Riesz representation theorem.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Assume that
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are non-zero.
It is easy to see using (

) and (
3.19
) that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1})\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mu_{2})}

f
if
and only if at least one of

and

is the zero measure.
In particular,

in general.

The following is a consequence of (

) (see Definition

Corollary 3.12
.

For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}),

the subspace of
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
spanned by the constant function

is a wandering subspace for
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

A bounded linear operator
T
on a Hilbert space is
essentially normal
if
T^{*}T-TT^{*}
is a compact operator.
An essentially normal operator is said to be
essentially unitary
if

is compact.
Unlike the case of one variable Dirichlet-type spaces (see
[

, Proposition 2.21]

\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
does not support essentially normal multiplication

-tuple
\mathscr{M}_{z}.

Corollary 3.13
.

The multiplication operators
\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
are never essentially normal.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, the multiplication
2
-tuple
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is a toral
2
-isometry. In particular,
\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}}
are
2
-isometries. If these are essentially normal, then
the image of
\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}}
in the Calkin algebra is a normal
2

\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}}
are essentially unitary (since a normal

and

Lemma 5.1

For a domain
\Omega

Let

\displaystyle\mbox{ran}(D^{*}_{\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}-\overline{w}})=\{(z_{1}-w_{1})g_{1}+(z_{2}-w_{2})g_{2}:g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathscr{H}\},

For
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant 2,

\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{j}}=\bigvee\big\{z^{k}_{i}:k\geqslant 0\big\}.

Proof.

As observed in the proof of Corollary
3.13
,

\displaystyle\{p(z_{i}):p\in\mathbb{C}[w]\}\subseteq\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{j}},\quad 1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant 2.

(5.35)

To see the reverse inclusion, let

f\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{1}}.

By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a sequence
\{p_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
of complex polynomials in
z_{1},z_{2}
converging to
f.

By Lemma
5.4
,
f(0,\cdot)\in\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),
and
\{p_{n}(0,\cdot)\}_{n\geqslant 1}
converges to
f(0,\cdot).
Hence, by (
5.35
),

f(0,\cdot)\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{1}}.
Thus
f-f(0,\cdot)\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{1}}.
However, there exists a holomorphic function
h:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})-f(0,z_{2})=z_{1}h(z_{1},z_{2}),\quad z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{D}.

(5.36)

By Theorem
2.2
,
we have
h\in\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).
It now follows from (
5.36
) that
\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}h\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{1}}.
Since
\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{1}}\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}
is invertible, we must have
h=0,
and hence, by (
5.36
),
f=f(0,\cdot),
or equivalently,
f
belongs to the closure of
\{p(z_{2}):p\in\mathbb{C}[w]\}.
Similarly, one can check that
\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z_{2}}
is equal to the closure of
\{p(z_{1}):p\in\mathbb{C}[w]\}.

∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
2.4
and its consequences

The proof of Theorem
2.4
relies on revealing the structure of toral
2
-isometries
T
with
\ker T^{*}
as a wandering subspace (see Definition
1.5
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
T=(T_{1},T_{2})
be a toral
2
-isometry. Then the following statements are true:

(i)

for any integers
k,l\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle T^{*k}_{1}T^{*l}_{2}T^{l}_{2}T^{k}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{*k}_{1}T^{k}_{1}+T^{*l}_{2}T^{l}_{2}-I

(6.37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kT^{*}_{1}T_{1}+lT^{*}_{2}T_{2}-(k+l-1)I,

(ii)

for
f_{0}\in\ker T^{*},
assume that

\displaystyle\langle{T^{m}_{1}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle=0,\quad q\geqslant 1,m,p\geqslant 0,

(6.38)

\displaystyle\langle{T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle=0,\quad p\geqslant 1,n,q\geqslant 0.

(6.39)

Then we have the following
:

\displaystyle\langle{T^{m}_{1}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if}~m\neq p,~n\neq q,\\
\langle{T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle&\mbox{if}~m=p,~n\neq q,\\
\langle{T^{m}_{1}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}f_{0}}\rangle&\mbox{if}~m\neq p,~n=q,\\
\|T^{m}_{1}f_{0}\|^{2}+\|T^{n}_{2}f_{0}\|^{2}-\|f_{0}\|^{2}&\mbox{if}~m=p,~n=q.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i) To see (
6.37
), we proceed by strong induction on
k+l,

k,l\geqslant 0.
Clearly, (
6.37
) holds for
0\leqslant k+l\leqslant 1.
Assume that (
6.37
) holds for integers
k,l\geqslant 0
such that
0\leqslant k+l\leqslant n.
Note that for
k\geqslant 1
and
l\leqslant n-1,
by the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle T^{*}_{1}(T^{*k}_{1}T^{*l}_{2}T^{l}_{2}T^{k}_{1})T_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{*k+1}_{1}T^{k+1}_{1}+T^{*}_{1}T^{*l}_{2}T^{l}_{2}T_{1}-T^{*}_{1}T_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{*k+1}_{1}T^{k+1}_{1}+T^{*l}_{2}T^{l}_{2}-I.

Similarly, for
k\leqslant n-1
and
l\geqslant 1,
(
6.37
) holds. This completes the induction argument. The remaining identity in (i) now follows from the known fact that for any
2
-isometry
S,
we have

\displaystyle S^{*k}S^{k}=k(S^{*}S-I)+I,\quad k\geqslant 0

(6.40)

(this known fact can be seen by induction on
k\geqslant 1
).

(ii)
Let
m,n,p,q
be integers such that
m\neq p
and
n\neq q.

Consider the case when
m<p
and
n<q.
Since
f_{0}\in\ker T^{*},
we have

\displaystyle\langle{T^{m}_{1}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{T^{*n}_{2}T^{*m}_{1}T^{m}_{1}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p-m}_{1}T^{q-n}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{formula-moment}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle{T^{*m}_{1}T^{m}_{1}f_{0}},\,{T^{p-m}_{1}T^{q-n}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle+\langle{T^{*n}_{2}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p-m}_{1}T^{q-n}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{T^{m}_{1}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q-n}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle+\langle{T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{q}_{2}T^{p-m}_{1}f_{0}}\rangle,

which, by (
6.38
) and (
6.39
), is equal to
0.
Since the inner-product is conjugate linear,

\langle{T^{m}_{1}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle=0
when
p<m
and
q<n.

Consider the case when
m<p
and
q<n.
Arguing as above, we have

\displaystyle\langle{T^{m}_{1}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{T^{*q}_{2}T^{*m}_{1}T^{m}_{1}T^{q}_{2}T^{n-q}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p-m}_{1}f_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{formula-moment}\&\eqref{assumption-k-condition-2}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle{T^{*m}_{1}T^{m}_{1}T^{n-q}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p-m}_{1}f_{0}}\rangle+\langle{T^{*q}_{2}T^{q}_{2}T^{n-q}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p-m}_{1}f_{0}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{T^{m}_{1}T^{n-q}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}f_{0}}\rangle+\langle{T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{q}_{2}T^{p-m}_{1}f_{0}}\rangle,

which, by (
6.38
) and (
6.39
), is equal to
0.

Once again, by the conjugate-symmetry,

\langle{T^{m}_{1}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle=0
when

p<m
and
n<q.

If
m=p
and
n\neq q,
then one may argue as above using (
6.40
) (by making cases
n<q
and
q<n
) to show that
\langle{T^{m}_{1}T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{p}_{1}T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle=\langle{T^{n}_{2}f_{0}},\,{T^{q}_{2}f_{0}}\rangle.
Similarly, one may derived the formula in case of
m\neq p
and
n=q.
Finally, if
m=p
and
n=q,
then the formula follows from (
6.37
).
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

For
j=1,2,
let
\mu^{(j)}_{1},\mu^{(j)}_{2}\in M_{+}(\mathbb{T}).
Then the multiplication
2

\mathscr{M}^{(1)}_{z}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mu^{(1)}_{1},\mu^{(1)}_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to the multiplication
2
-tuple
\mathscr{M}^{(2)}_{z}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mu^{(2)}_{1},\mu^{(2)}_{2})
if and only if
\mu^{(1)}_{j}=\mu^{(2)}_{j},

j=1,2.

Proof.

Suppose there is a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{D}(\mu^{(1)}_{1},\mu^{(1)}_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mu^{(2)}_{1},\mu^{(2)}_{2})
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{(2)}_{z_{j}}U=U\mathscr{M}^{(1)}_{z_{j}},\quad j=1,2.

(6.43)

Since the joint kernel of the adjoint of multiplication tuples is spanned by
1,
by (
6.43
),
U
must map
1
to some constant of modulus
1.
After multiplying
U
by a unimodular constant, if required, we may assume that
U1=1.
It now follows from (
6.43
) that
U
is identity on polynomials. By Lemma
3.5
(applied twice), we obtain for any polynomial
p
in two variables,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|p(e^{i\eta},e^{i\theta})|^{2}d\mu^{(1)}_{1}(\eta)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|p(e^{i\eta},e^{i\theta})|^{2}d\mu^{(2)}_{1}(\eta)d\theta,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|p(e^{i\theta},e^{i\eta})|^{2}d\mu^{(1)}_{2}(\eta)d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|p(e^{i\theta},e^{i\eta})|^{2}d\mu^{(2)}_{2}(\eta)d\theta.

It is easy to see that for any polynomial
p
in one variable,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}|p(e^{i\eta})|^{2}d\mu^{(1)}_{j}(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|p(e^{i\eta})|^{2}d\mu^{(2)}_{j}(\eta),\quad j=1,2.

Combining polarization identity with the uniqueness of the trigonometric moment problem yields the desired uniqueness.

7.
Concluding remarks

We conclude the paper with a brief discussion on the spectral picture of the multiplication
2
-tuple
\mathscr{M}_{z}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

We claim that

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathscr{M}_{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{D}}^{2},

(7.44)

\displaystyle\sigma_{e}(\mathscr{M}_{z})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{D}}^{2}\setminus\Omega

(7.45)

for some open set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
containing
\big(\mathbb{D}\times\{0\}\big)\cup\big(\{0\}\times\mathbb{D}\big).

To see (
7.44
), note that by
[
12
, Theorem 4.9]
,
for any commuting pair
T=(T_{1},T_{2}),

\sigma(T)\subseteq\sigma(T_{1})\times\sigma(T_{2}).
Since the spectrum of any
2
-isometry is contained in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
(see
[
2
, Lemma 1.21]
) and both
\mathscr{M}_{z_{1}}
and
\mathscr{M}_{z_{2}}
are
2
-isometries (see Corollary
3.8
), we obtain

\sigma(\mathscr{M}_{z})\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}^{2}.

Also, by Corollary
3.9
,
\mathbb{D}^{2}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z})\subseteq\sigma(\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}).
Since
\sigma(\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z})=\{\overline{z}:z\in\sigma(\mathscr{M}_{z})\},
we have the inclusion
\mathbb{D}^{2}\subseteq\sigma(\mathscr{M}_{z}).
Finally, since the Taylor spectrum is closed (see
[
12
, Corollary 4.2]
), we obtain (
7.44
).
On the other hand,
an examination of the proof of Theorem
2.3
(using the full power of Lemma
5.3
together with Lemma
5.4
) shows that

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{D}\times\{0\}\big)\cup\big(\{0\}\times\mathbb{D}\big)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\sigma_{e}(\mathscr{M}_{z}).

Since the essential spectrum is a closed subset of the Taylor spectrum, (
7.45
) now follows from (
7.44
).
The natural question arises
whether the unit bidisc lies in the complement of the essential spectrum of
\mathscr{M}_{z}
(there are interesting examples of toral
2
-isometries supporting this possibility; see
[
5
, Proposition 5(iii)]
). If this question has an affirmative answer, then
\sigma_{e}(\mathscr{M}_{z})=\partial(\mathbb{D}^{2}).

Indeed, if
\lambda\in\partial(\mathbb{D}^{2})\backslash\sigma_{e}(\mathscr{M}_{z}),
then there exist two sequences in
\mathbb{D}^{2}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\overline{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
converging to
\lambda,
which together with the continuity of the Fredholm index (see (
4.26
))
leads to a contradiction. This in turn leads to an improvement of Corollary
5.5
providing a bidisc analog of
[
26
, Corollary 3.8]
and also solves Gleason’s problem for
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
(see Lemma
5.1
).

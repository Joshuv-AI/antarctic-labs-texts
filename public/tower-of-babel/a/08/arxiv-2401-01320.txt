Separable homology of graphs and the Whitehead complex
Becky Eastham

Abstract
. We introduce the Whitehead complex , a one-complex associated to a finite regular cover of the rose and show that it is connected if and only if the fundamental group of the associated cover is generated by its intersection with the set of elements in proper free factors of 𝐅 n \mathbf{F}_{n} . The Whitehead complex admits an action of Out ⁡ ( 𝐅 n ) \mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n}) by isometries if the associated cover corresponds to a characteristic subgroup of 𝐅 n \mathbf{F}_{n} . We prove that the Whitehead complex of the rose has infinite diameter and is nonhyperbolic, implying it is not quasi-isometric to the free splitting complex or the free factor complex.

2.1.
Graphs and Free Groups

Definition 2.1
.

A
directed graph
is a 1-complex given by a tuple
(V,E,\iota,\tau)
where
V
is a set of 0-cells called
vertices
,
E
is a set of 1-cells called
edges
, and
\iota:E\rightarrow V
and
\tau:E\rightarrow V
are maps that send
e\in E
to the endpoints of
e
in
V
. For all
e\in E
,
\iota(e)
will be called the
initial vertex
of
e
and
\tau(e)
will be called the
terminal vertex
of
e
. By assigning initial and terminal vertices of
e
, we assign an orientation to
e
. We represent the orientation of
e
by an arrow on
e
pointing toward
\tau(e)
. The
valence
of a vertex
v
is the number of components of
N(v)\setminus\{v\}
, where
N(v)
is a small neighborhood of
v
.

Note that, under our definition of graph, we allow multiple edges with the same initial and terminal vertices. Often we will want to label the edges or vertices with elements of some set. We will denote the label of an edge
e
or vertex
v
by
\mathrm{lab}(e)
and
\mathrm{lab}(v)
, respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

A
rose
is a graph with one vertex and one or more edges. We denote the rose with
n
edges by
\mathbf{R}_{n}
.

Throughout the paper, we let
\mathbf{F}_{n}
be the free group on
n
generators

X_{n}:=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}.

We denote the inverse of an element

by
\overline{v}
. Let
X_{n}^{*}:=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},\overline{x_{1}},\overline{x_{2}},\ldots,\overline{x_{n}}\}
. A

on
X_{n}^{*}
is an element of the set

\mathcal{W}_{n}=\{w\in\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{n}^{*}\mid m<\infty\}.

Elements of
\mathbf{F}_{n}
are equivalence classes of words under
free reduction
. A word is
freely reduced
if it contains no subword of the form
y\overline{y}
where
y\in X_{n}^{*}
. The freely reduced word
w=y_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{m}
where
y_{i}\in X^{*}
is
cyclically reduced
if
y_{1}\neq\overline{y_{m}}
. A
cyclic subword
of a word

is a subword of one of the cyclic permutations of
w
. The length of
w=y_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{m}
where
y_{i}\in X_{n}^{*}
is denoted by
|w|
: in this case,
|w|=m
. When we refer to the length of
v\in\mathbf{F}_{n}
, we mean the length of the freely reduced word representing
v
. For an automorphism
\varphi:\mathbf{F}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{F}_{n}
, there is a unique map
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathcal{W}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{n}
which extends
\varphi
: for every
y\in X_{n}^{*}
, let
\tilde{\varphi}(y)=\varphi(y).

Since
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{R}_{n})\cong\mathbf{F}_{n}
, we will assume throughout that the edges of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
are labeled by elements of
X_{n}
. A cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
will inherit this labeling, so that a graph
\Gamma
is a cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
if and only if for every vertex

in
\Gamma

, oriented in opposite directions. We identify
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
with
\mathbf{F}_{n}
and, for

\mathbf{F}_{n}
.

Every finite-index subgroup of
\mathbf{F}_{n}
is represented by a finite cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
. Suppose

2.2.
Van Kampen diagrams

Throughout the paper, we make use of Van Kampen diagrams, first introduced in 1933 by E. R. Van Kampen. For a more complete discussion, see Chapter V of
[
LSLS77
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}=\langle X\mid R\rangle
be group presentation. We will assume throughout the paper that elements of
R
are cyclically reduced. Let
R^{*}
be the
symmetric closure
of
R
: that is, the set of cyclic permutations of elements of
R
and their inverses.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\langle X\mid R\rangle
be a presentation for a group, and assume elements of
R
are cyclically reduced. A
Van Kampen diagram
for
w\in\langle\langle R\rangle\rangle
over
\mathcal{P}
is a finite, simply connected planar 2-complex
D
with oriented edges labeled by elements of
X
. The boundary of
D
is labeled by
w
beginning at some basepoint for
D
. The 1-skeleton of
D
is a folding (not necessarily a complete folding) of a wedge of cycles labeled by conjugates of elements of
R^{*}
. The 2-disks of
D
are called
regions
. Regions are attached along cycles labeled by elements of
R^{*}
. Regions of
D
have completely folded boundaries which are embedded in
D
. The
area
of
D
is the number of regions of
D
. A Van Kampen diagram
D
for
w
over
\mathcal{P}
is
minimal
if there is no Van Kampen diagram
D^{\prime}
for
w
over
\mathcal{P}
of smaller area.

Let
G
be a quotient of
\mathbf{F}_{n}
, and let
\mathcal{P}=\langle X_{n}\mid R\rangle
be a presentation for
G
. A word
w\in\langle\langle R\rangle\rangle
has a Van Kampen diagram over
\mathcal{P}
. The converse is also true: if
w\not\in\langle\langle R\rangle\rangle
, then
w
has no Van Kampen diagram over
\mathcal{P}
.

Remark 2.8
.

When constructing Van Kampen diagrams over a presentation
\langle X\mid R\rangle
, we adopt the convention that the boundaries of disks are glued along cycles labeled by elements of
R^{*}
(as opposed to conjugates of elements of
R^{*}
). For example, if
w=aba\overline{c}\overline{b^{2}}ab\overline{a}\overline{b}\overline{a}
, the boundary of a region corresponding to
w
in a Van Kampen diagram will be glued along a cycle labeled by
\overline{c}\overline{b^{2}}ab

2.3.

A

is a nontrivial proper subgroup

such that
\mathbf{F}_{n}
splits as a free product

for some nontrivial, proper

\mathbf{F}_{n}
and words that are not.

Definition 2.11

(
[
Sta99
]
)
.

A word in

is

if it belongs to a proper free factor of
\mathbf{F}_{n}
. Otherwise,

is

Some authors refer to these words as
simple
; we use Stallings’ terminology in order to avoid confusion with the definition of

on a surface. The proof of the following theorem of Stallings relies on ideas of Whitehead in
[
Whi36
]
, hence the name “Whitehead’s algorithm for separable words.”

Theorem 2.12

(
[
Sta99
]
)
.

Given a word

there is an efficient algorithm to determine whether or not
w
is separable.

In fact, Algorithm 2.5 of
[
Sta99
]
determines whether finite sets of words

are conjugate into the factors of any splitting

). We will only need the result for words in

constructs a graph called the

of

is a subword of one of the cyclic permutations of

Definition 2.13

(Whitehead graph of a word)

Let

•

Definition 2.14
.

A vertex

Whitehead’s algorithm to decide separability of

Definition 2.15
.

A

Note that Whitehead automorphisms of the

Given a choice of

and a partition

of

into two nonempty subsets such that

and

as follows:

\varphi_{x,Y,Z}(y)=\begin{cases}y&\quad\text{if }x=y\text{ or }\{y,\overline{y}\}\subset Y;\\
xy&\quad\text{if }y\in Y\text{ and }\overline{y}\in Z;\\
y\overline{x}&\quad\text{if }y\in Z\text{ and }\overline{y}\in Y;\\
xy\overline{x}&\quad\text{if }y,\overline{y}\in Z.\\
\end{cases}

(
[

]
)

The following process will determine whether

(2)

(3)

The Whitehead complex

The purpose of this section is to introduce the Whitehead complex and describe some of its properties. The Whitehead complex is a tool to study finite regular covers
\Gamma
of the rose (equivalently, finite-index normal subgroups of
\mathbf{F}_{n}
). A key property of
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
is that it is connected if and only if
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
is generated by
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{sep}}\cap\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
(see
1.1
). Throughout the paper,
\Gamma
is a finite-index regular cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
for
n\geq 2
, and
\Gamma^{\mathrm{sep}}
is the cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
representing the subgroup of
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
generated by elements of
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{sep}}\cap\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
. See
Figure

8
for a small subgraph of the Whitehead complex of a specific cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
.

Definition 3.1

(Whitehead complex)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite regular cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
for some
n\geq 2
. The Whitehead complex of
\Gamma
, denoted
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
, is a locally infinite directed graph constructed as follows:

•

The vertex set

of
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
is the set of equivalence classes of elements of
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
under conjugation by
\mathbf{F}_{n}
;

•

Each edge is labeled by the (free) conjugacy class of a nontrivial element of
\pi_{1}(\Gamma^{\mathrm{sep}})
. There is an edge
[\alpha]
from
[v]
to
[w]
whenever there are
\hat{\alpha}\in[\alpha],

\hat{w}\in[w],

\hat{v}\in[v]
such that
\hat{w}=\hat{v}\hat{\alpha}
in
\mathbf{F}_{n}
.

We will generally use Latin letters in square brackets to refer to vertices in
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
and Greek letters in square brackets to denote edge labels in
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
. Note that we can take
\hat{w}
to be any representative of
[w]
by conjugating the equation
\hat{w}=\hat{v}\hat{\alpha}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Equivalently, we can think of vertices of
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
as equivalence classes of labeled Whitehead graphs (see

and

are adjacent whenever there is an

An edge in
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
under this definition of

corresponds to a labeled Whitehead graph of a separable word. The name “Whitehead complex” comes from this alternate definition of

over

Theorem 3.4
.

\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
admits an
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
-action by isometries.

Proof.

First we show
\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
admits an
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
-action. Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
. The automorphism
\phi
sends
[v]
to
[\phi(v)]
. Suppose there is an edge
[\alpha]\in\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
from
[v]
to
[w]
. Then there are elements
c,d\in\mathbf{F}_{n}
satisfying
vc\alpha\overline{c}=dw\overline{d}
. If we apply
\phi
to this equation, we get
\phi(v)\phi(c)\phi(\alpha)\overline{\phi(c)}=\phi(d)w\overline{\phi(d)}.
So in
\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
, the image of the edge
[\alpha]
with endpoints
[v],[w]
is an edge
[\phi(\alpha)]
with endpoints
[\phi(v)],[\phi(w)]
. Since
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
is an automorphism, it permutes conjugacy classes of separable words, hence
\phi(\alpha)
is separable. Note that if
\phi
is an inner automorphism,
\phi
fixes
\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
pointwise, so we can upgrade the
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
-action on
\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
to an
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
-action.

The action of
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
preserves the metric on
\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
: let
\phi\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
and
{[v],[w]\in\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbf{R}_{n})}
. Let
p([v],[w])
be any path between
[v]
and
[w]
. Since automorphisms of the free group preserve separability,
||v||_{\mathrm{sep}}=||\phi(v)||_{\mathrm{sep}}
, and
\mathrm{length}(p)=\mathrm{length}(\phi(p))
. The action of
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
preserves

, so the action of
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n})
on the Whitehead complex of the rose is an action on a rooted graph.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that the only vertex of

could be altered to apply to

We get a similar result to

for all
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)

There is a connected component of
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
containing all nontrivial separable conjugacy classes of

represents a characteristic subgroup, this “separable component” is preserved under the action of
\mathrm{Out}(\mathbf{F}_{n})

Let

For the first statement, note that since

3.1.

Definition 3.8

Lemma 3.9

Let

Let

is inseparable.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be freely reduced and let

be the map

Proof.

Suppose that

For all

in
\phi(x_{j})

\phi(x_{j})
are, possibly, one copy of

and one copy of

is a letter of

y
in

such that

. Suppose there is some letter

and

in

\overline{y}

and
\overline{y}

and
\overline{y}
until the only letters between some copy of

and
\overline{z^{\prime}}

are copies of

and
\overline{y}
in
\tilde{\phi}(w)
are copies of

.

, and possibly one copy of

and
\overline{y}
in

and

in

5.
Homology version of the Whitehead complex

We define a nested sequence of spaces

\mathrm{W}_{1}(\Gamma)\supset\mathrm{W}_{2}(\Gamma)\supset\mathrm{W}_{3}(\Gamma)\supset\ldots

with intersection
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
. If
\Gamma
is a regular cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
and the deck group
G_{\Gamma}
is nilpotent, this sequence will terminate after finitely many steps.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite cover of
\mathbf{R}_{n}
. We define a sequence of spaces of curves whose intersection is
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
. For all
i\geq 1
, we let
W_{i}(\Gamma)
be the directed graph defined below.

•

The set of vertices of
W_{i}(\Gamma)
is
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)^{(0)};

•

Edges are labeled by elements of the set
E_{i}:=\Gamma^{\mathrm{sep}}\cup\big(\pi_{1}(\Gamma)\big)_{i+1}
, where
\big(\pi_{1}(\Gamma)\big)_{j}\text{ is the }j
th term in the lower central series of
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
;

•

There is an edge
[\alpha]\in E_{i}
from
[v]
to
[w]
if and only if there are some
g\in[v],{h\in[w]},{c\in\mathbf{F}_{n}}
such that
gc\alpha\overline{c}=h.

Note that if any of the
W_{i}(\Gamma)
are connected, so is
W_{1}(\Gamma)
.
W_{1}(\Gamma)
is the “integral homology version” of the Whitehead complex. It is connected if and only if
\mathrm{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{sep}}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})=\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}).

See
1.6

Proof.

Suppose
\mathrm{H}^{\text{sep}}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})=\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
, and take an element
v
of
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
. Since
\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
is the abelianization of
\pi_{1}(\Gamma)
,
v
is expressible as a product
\prod_{i\leq m}\alpha_{i}
such that
{\alpha_{i}\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{sep}}\cup[\pi_{1}(\Gamma),\pi_{1}(\Gamma)]}.
Thus there is a directed path from each vertex
[v]\in W_{1}(\Gamma)
to the trivial vertex corresponding to multiplying
v
by the inverses of the
\alpha_{i}
from
i=m
to
i=1
.

Now suppose
W_{1}(\Gamma)
is connected. Then there is an undirected path from every vertex
v\in\Gamma
to the trivial loop. Observe that for every edge
[\beta]\in W_{1}(\Gamma)
connecting
[v]
to
[w]
, there is an edge
[\overline{\beta}]
from
[w]
to
[v]
. Therefore, there is a directed path in
W_{1}(\Gamma)
from the trivial vertex to every vertex in the cover, and a directed path from the trivial loop to a vertex
[w]
is an expression for
w
as a product of commutators of elements of
\Gamma^{\mathrm{sep}}\cup[\pi_{1}(\Gamma),\pi_{1}(\Gamma)]
. Connectedness of
W_{1}(\Gamma)
implies there exists such a path for every vertex in
W_{1}(\Gamma)
, so
\mathrm{H}_{1}^{\text{sep}}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})=\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}).

∎

While we do not know whether
\mathrm{W}_{1}(\Gamma)
is always connected, we do know that
\mathrm{W}_{1}(\Gamma)
always has finitely many components. This follows from work of Boggi, Putman, and Salter in
[
BPS23
]
.

Theorem 5.2

(Boggi–Putman–Salter)
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{\Sigma}\rightarrow\Sigma
be a finite branched covering between closed oriented surfaces. Then
\mathrm{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{pant}}(\widetilde{\Sigma};\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{H}_{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma};\mathbb{Q})
.

Here
\mathrm{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{pant}}(\widetilde{\Sigma};\mathbb{Q})
is the subspace of
\mathrm{H}_{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma};\mathbb{Q})
spanned by

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{pant}}:=\{[w]\in\mathrm{H}_{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma};\mathbb{Q})\mid\pi(w)\text{ is contained in a pair of pants in }\Sigma\}.

As the authors state in Remark 1.5 of
[
BPS23
]
, this result also applies to punctured
\Sigma
of finite type, as if
\widetilde{\Sigma}\rightarrow\Sigma
is a finite branched cover of punctured surfaces, forgetting the punctures of
\Sigma
and
\widetilde{\Sigma}
yields a branched cover closed surfaces. Forgetting the punctures kills exactly the homology classes surrounding punctures, and curves surrounding punctures are simple. Since
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{pant}}
contains all simple closed curves, it follows that
\mathrm{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{pant}}(\widetilde{\Sigma};\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{H}_{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma};\mathbb{Q})
. We now show that
\mathrm{W}_{1}(\Gamma)
has finitely many components.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbf{R}_{n}
be a finite regular cover. Then
\mathrm{W}_{1}(\Gamma)
has finitely many components.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
be generated by the set

\{[\alpha_{1}],[\alpha_{2}],\ldots,[\alpha_{l}],[\beta_{1}],[\beta_{2}],\ldots,[\beta_{m}]\}

where the
[\alpha_{i}]
are separable homology classes, the
[\beta_{i}]
are inseparable homology classes, and
m
is minimal amongst generating sets for
\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}).
Since
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{pant}}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{sep}}
,
\mathrm{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{sep}}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
is finite-index inside
\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
, and since
\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
is abelian, every element
[\gamma]\in\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
can be written as a product

[\gamma]=[\alpha_{1}^{j_{1}}][\alpha_{2}^{j_{2}}]\ldots[\alpha_{l}^{j_{l}}][\beta_{1}^{k_{1}}][\beta_{2}^{k_{2}}]\ldots[\beta_{m}^{k_{m}}]

where the
j_{i}
are integers and the
k_{i}
are nonnegative integers bounded above by
K:=[\mathrm{H}_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}):\mathrm{H}_{1}^{\mathrm{sep}}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})]<\infty
. Thus there are at most
K2^{m}
components of
\mathrm{Wh}(\Gamma)
, each corresponding to a subset of the set

\{[\beta_{1}],[\beta_{1}^{2}],\ldots[\beta_{1}^{K-1}],[\beta_{2}],[\beta_{2}^{2}],\ldots,[\beta_{2}^{K-1}],\ldots,[\beta_{m}],[\beta_{m}^{2}],\ldots,[\beta_{m}^{K-1}]\}

containing at most one element of
\{[\beta_{i}],[\beta_{i}^{2}],\ldots,[\beta_{i}^{K-1}]\}
for each
i
.
∎

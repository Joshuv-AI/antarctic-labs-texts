General one-loop contributions to the decay H → ν l ​ ν ¯ l ​ γ H\rightarrow\nu_{l}\bar{\nu}_{l}\gamma
Khiem Hong Phan 1,2, Dzung Tri Tran 3, and Le Tho Hue 4

Abstract
General one-loop contributions to the decay amplitudes H → ν l ​ ν ¯ l ​ γ H\rightarrow\nu_{l}\bar{\nu}_{l}\gamma are presented, considering all possible contributions of additional heavy vector gauge bosons, fermions, and charged (and also neutral) scalar particles appearing in the loop diagrams. Moreover, the results can be applied directly when extra neutrinos (apart from three ones in standard model) are taken into account in final states. Analytic results are presented in terms of Passarino-Veltman scalar functions which can be evaluated numerically using LoopTools . In the standard model framework, these analytical results are generated and cross-checked with previous computations. We find that our results are well consistent with these computations. Within standard model limit, phenomenological results for the decay channels are also studied using the updated input parameters at the Large Hadron Collider.

2.1
Method

In this calculation, we follow tensor reduction method developed in
Ref.
[
18
]
. Following the technique, tensor one-loop
integrals with
N
-external lines can be decomposed into scalar
functions with
N\leq 4
. The approach will be explained briefly in
the following paragraphs.

First, one-loop one-, two-, three- and four-point tensor integrals
with rank
P
are defined:

\displaystyle\{A;B;C;D\}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\cdots\mu_{P}}=(\mu^{2})^{2-d/2}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\dfrac{k^{\mu_{1}}k^{\mu_{2}}\cdots k^{\mu_{P}}}{\{D_{1};D_{1}D_{2};D_{1}D_{2}D_{3};D_{1}D_{2}D_{3}D_{4}\}}.

(1)

In this formula,

D_{j}
(
j=1,\cdots,4
) are the inverse Feynman propagators

\displaystyle D_{j}=(k+q_{j})^{2}-m_{j}^{2}+i\rho,

(2)

q_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}p_{i}
,

p_{i}
are the external momenta, and

m_{j}
are internal masses in the loops.
We are working on space-time dimension

d=4-2\varepsilon
. The parameter
\mu^{2}

plays role of a renormalization
scale. We then present explicit reduction
formulas for one-loop one-, two-, three- and four-point tensor
integrals up to rank
P=3
as follows
[
18
]
:

\displaystyle A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(3)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}A_{00},

(4)

\displaystyle A^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(5)

\displaystyle B^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\mu}B_{1},

(6)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}B_{00}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}B_{11},

(7)

\displaystyle B^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{g,q\}^{\mu\nu\rho}B_{001}+q^{\mu}q^{\nu}q^{\rho}B_{111},

(8)

\displaystyle C^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\mu}C_{1}+q_{2}^{\mu}C_{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}q_{i}^{\mu}C_{i},

(9)

\displaystyle C^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}C_{00}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}q_{i}^{\mu}q_{j}^{\nu}C_{ij},

(10)

\displaystyle C^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}C_{00i}+\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}q^{\mu}_{i}q^{\nu}_{j}q^{\rho}_{k}C_{ijk},

(11)

\displaystyle D^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\mu}D_{1}+q_{2}^{\mu}D_{2}+q_{3}^{\mu}D_{3}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}q_{i}^{\mu}D_{i},

(12)

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}D_{00}+\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{3}q_{i}^{\mu}q_{j}^{\nu}D_{ij},

(13)

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{00i}+\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}q^{\mu}_{i}q^{\nu}_{j}q^{\rho}_{k}D_{ijk}.

(14)

The short notation
[
18
]

\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}
is used

\{g,q_{i}\}^{\mu\nu\rho}=g^{\mu\nu}q^{\rho}_{i}+g^{\nu\rho}q^{\mu}_{i}+g^{\mu\rho}q^{\nu}_{i}
in the

]

Analytic formulas of the PV functions are well-known

[
20
]
for

2.2
General one-loop contributions
to
H\rightarrow\nu_{l}\bar{\nu}_{l}\gamma

As a result, we apply the following relation:

q_{3}^{\nu}\epsilon_{\nu}^{*}=0

Kinematic invariant variables

\displaystyle p^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{H}^{2},\quad q_{1}^{2}=q_{2}^{2}=q_{3}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle q_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{2}=(q_{1}+q_{2})^{2}=2q_{1}\cdot q_{2},\quad q_{13}=2q_{1}\cdot q_{3},\quad q_{23}=2q_{2}\cdot q_{3}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\text{loop}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}\Big\{[q_{3}^{\mu}q_{i}^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}q_{3}\cdot q_{k}]\bar{u}(q_{1})(F_{k,R}\gamma_{\mu}P_{R}+F_{k,L}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L})v(q_{2})\Big\}\varepsilon^{*}_{\nu}.

\displaystyle F_{k,L/R}

\displaystyle F_{k,L/R}^{\text{Trig}}+F_{k,L/R}^{\text{Box}}

(17)

for

After using them to write down all one-loop

3
Conclusions

We have presented analytic formulas for all possible
one-loop contributions to the SM-like Higgs decay

H\rightarrow\nu_{l}\bar{\nu}_{l}\gamma
that are valid
in many BSMs. Additional vector bosons, charged fermions and
charged (and also neutral) scalar
particles exchanging in the loop diagrams
have considered in this computation.
General statement, we conclude that the
evaluations can be extended directly for
general numbers of the extra neutrinos in final
states. Analytic results are expressed in a general form
which written in terms of Passarino-Veltman scalar
functions that can be evaluated numerically
using

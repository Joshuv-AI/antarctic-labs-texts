A study of nonlinear multiview varieties
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Oliver Clarke, Fatemeh Mohammadi

Abstract
. We study the nonlinear generalization of the classical multiview variety, which is a fundamental concept in computer vision. In this paper, we take the first comprehensive step to develop the nonlinear analogue of multiview varieties. To this end, we introduce a multigraded version of the saturated special fiber ring. By applying this tool, we are able to compute the multidegrees of several families of nonlinear multiview varieties.

Primary: 14E05, Secondary: 13D02, 13D45, 13P99.

1.
Introduction

Motivation.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field.
The classical
multiview variety
is an important tool in computer vision, see
[
2
]
.
It is defined as the closure of the image
Y
of a linear rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
.
Conceptually, the image of
\mathcal{G}
is a sequence of cameras that take
2
-dimensional images of
3
-dimensional space.
Naturally, properties about
Y
are crucial to solving the problem of reconstructing a
3
-dimensional object from a collection of images.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is linear, the defining ideal
J
of
Y
has a structure that is amenable to study via a number of different methods.
Firstly,
J
is an example of a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal, see
[
2
,
22
,
21
]
.
And so,
J
satisfies a number of pleasant properties, see
[
21
]
.
Secondly, it is possible to give an explicit description of the generators of
J
, see
[
1
]
. In particular, if the focal points of the cameras are distinct then
J
is generated by certain determinants of degree
2
and
3
, see
[
1
, Theorem 3.7]
.
Thirdly, one can study the structure of the coordinate ring of
Y
in terms of products of linear ideals, see
[
23
]
.
In particular, it is possible to give an explicit formula for the Betti numbers in terms of the polymatroidal data defined by the positions of the cameras. These results demonstrate connections between properties of
Y
and properties of the base locus of
\mathcal{G}
.
The goal and purpose of this paper is to extend such connections for when
\mathcal{G}
is nonlinear.
Therefore, our approach embodies the general and fruitful philosophy of studying nonlinear algebra
[
48
,
6
]
.

Previous Work.
Let us consider a rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
, as above, which is not necessarily linear. Note that, none of the methods above can be extended to study this case.
In particular, it is difficult to determine the generators of
J
and, in general, this ideal is not necessarily a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal.
So, it is natural to ask how to compute its Hilbert function.
More specifically, it is important to study the
multidegrees
of
Y
as a subvariety of the target
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
of
\mathcal{G}
.
Our approach to determine the multidegrees of
Y
is based on the study of blow-up algebras and syzygies of the ideals generated by the defining polynomials of the rational map
\mathcal{G}
.

A syzygy-based approach to study rational maps seems to have been initiated in
[
37
]
, and it has now become an active and fruitful research area, see e.g.
[
29
,
54
,
44
,
30
,
34
,
50
,
3
,
49
,
35
,
8
,
19
,
17
,
20
,
5
,
4
,
45
,
46
,
43
,
26
,
24
,
25
]
.
In the area of Computer Aided Geometric Design (CAGD), a somewhat similar story has emerged where the concept of syzygies is substituted by an equivalent one in
\mu
-bases, see
[
28
,
27
]
.
In all these previous works, the main goal is to study a (singly projective) rational map of the form
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
.
To illustrate the general idea, we recall an instance where the degree of the syzygies of the base ideal completely determines the degree of the image.
In
[
28
]
, Cox considers a parametric surface
Y
given by the image of
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
.
In this case, under some technical assumptions, the degree of
Y
can be computed by an elementary symmetric polynomial evaluated at the degrees of the syzygies of the base ideal, see
[
28
, Proposition 5.3]
.
In
[
19
, Theorem A]
, this result is widely generalized using the saturated special fiber ring to the case of rational maps
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
where the base locus is given by a perfect ideal of height two. The
saturated special fiber ring
has been recently used to successfully study rational maps in many different contexts, see
[
19
,
17
,
20
,
18
]
.
It was introduced by Busé – Cid-Ruiz – D’Andrea
[
8
]
for studying the degree and birationality of rational maps. In this paper we define the multigraded version of the saturated special fiber ring, see
§3.2
.

Main Results.
We consider rational maps
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
whose image can be thought of as the nonlinear generalization of a multiview variety.
Note that, the closure of the image
Y
of
\mathcal{G}
naturally admits a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-grading, and so we will be concerned with the multidegrees of
Y
instead of the degree. Our first main result is
Theorem 3.6
which relates the mixed multiplicities, see
§2.1
, of the saturated special fiber ring with the multidegrees of
Y
.
In particular, if the special fiber ring is integrally closed, we obtain a criterion for birationality of
\mathcal{G}
in terms of the saturated special fiber ring.

Our second main result is
Theorem 3.8
which gives an upper bound for the multidegrees of
Y
, and, if the base locus of
\mathcal{G}
is zero-dimensional, then it yields an exact formula for the multidegrees of
Y
in terms of the mixed multiplicities of the base points.
This generalizes the well-known degree formula for rational maps with finite base locus to the multiprojective setting, see
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
,
[
53
, Theorem 6.6]
and
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
.
Note that, if
\mathcal{G}
is a linear rational map, then
Y
is multiplicity-free, see
Theorem 4.2
which recovers
[
47
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
23
, Theorem 3.9]
.

Our third main result is
Theorem 5.5
which gives an explicit formula for the multidegrees of
Y
in the case that the base ideals
I_{1},\dots,I_{p-1}
are zero-dimensional and
I_{p}
is either a height two perfect ideal or a height three Gorenstein ideal.
In particular, this result generalizes
[
19
, Theorem A]
and
[
17
, Theorem A]
to the multigraded setting.

Lastly, for the case of monomial rational maps,
Theorem 6.4
provides a closed formula for the multidegrees of
Y
in terms of mixed volumes of some naturally constructed lattice polytopes.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

We begin by fixing some common notation and recalling some preliminary results. Let
p\geq 1
be a positive integer and, for each
1\leq i\leq p
, let
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
be the
i
-th elementary vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}=\left(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0\right)
.
For any
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
, we define its weight as
\lvert{\mathbf{n}}\rvert:=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}
.
For two vectors
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we denote the dot product by
{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta:=n_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}\delta_{p}
.
If
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}),\,{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
are two vectors, we write
{\mathbf{n}}\geq{\mathbf{m}}
whenever
n_{i}\geq m_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
, and
{\mathbf{n}}>{\mathbf{m}}
whenever
n_{j}>m_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq p
.
We write
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for the zero vector
\mathbf{0}=(0,\ldots,0)
.

2.1.
Multigraded schemes and multidegrees

We fix the following setup during this subsection.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
S
a finitely generated standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded algebra, i.e.
\left[S\right]_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{k}
and
S
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
by elements of degree
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq p
.

Given a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
S
, we consider the corresponding multigraded scheme.

Definition 2.2
.

The multiprojective scheme
\text{MultiProj}(S)
is given by
\text{MultiProj}(S):=\big\{\mathfrak{P}\in{\text{Spec}}(S)\mid\mathfrak{P}\text{ is $\mathbb{N}^{p}$-graded and }\mathfrak{P}\not\supseteq{\mathfrak{N}}\big\},

and its scheme structure is obtained by using multi-homogeneous localizations, see e.g.
[
39
, §1]
.
The multigraded irrelevant ideal
{\mathfrak{N}}\subset S
is given by
{\mathfrak{N}}:=\left([S]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}\right)\cap\cdots\cap\left([S]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}\right)
.

Let
P_{S}(\mathbf{t})=P_{S}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{t}]=\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
be the
(multigraded) Hilbert polynomial
of
S
, see e.g.
[
36
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
20
, Theorem 3.4]
.
Then, the degree of
P_{S}
is equal to
r:=\dim(\text{MultiProj}(S))=\dim(S/(0:_{S}{\mathfrak{N}}^{\infty}))-p
and

P_{S}(\nu)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([S]_{\nu}\right)

for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that
\nu\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Furthermore, if we write

(1)

P_{S}(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{p}\geq 0}e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\binom{t_{1}+d_{1}}{d_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+d_{p}}{d_{p}},

then
0\leq e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{p}=r
.
Interchangeably,
P_{S}(\mathbf{t})
is the Hilbert polynomial
P_{X}(\mathbf{t})
of the multiprojective scheme
X=\text{MultiProj}(S)
.

We embed
X=\text{MultiProj}(S)
, as a closed subscheme, into the multiprojective space
{\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
Under the notation of
(1)
, we define the following invariants.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
. Then:

(i)

e({\mathbf{d}},S):=e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})
is the
mixed multiplicity of
S
of type
{\mathbf{d}}
.

(ii)

\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(X):=e(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})
is the
multidegree of
X=\text{MultiProj}(S)
of type
{\mathbf{d}}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
.

Whenever the chosen embedding of
X
into a product of projective spaces
{\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is clear from the context, we simply write
\deg^{\mathbf{d}}(X)
instead of
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(X)
.
In classical geometrical terms, when
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed,
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(X)
is also equal to the number of points (counting multiplicity) in the intersection of
X
with the product
L_{1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}L_{p}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
, where
L_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}
is a general linear subspace of dimension
m_{i}-d_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq p
, see
[
57
]
and
[
20
, Theorem 4.7]
.

2.2.
Mixed multiplicities of ideals

Let us recall the definition of mixed multiplicities of ideals in the setting that we are considering. For more details, we refer the reader to
[
55
]
. Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring,
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})
the graded irrelevant ideal, and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}\subset R
a sequence of homogeneous ideals.
We consider the
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra

T({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\,:=\,\bigoplus_{(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}+1}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}}.

The mixed multiplicities of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
are defined as the mixed multiplicities of the algebra
T({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})=(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
with
d_{0}+\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
.
The
mixed multiplicity of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
of type
(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})
is
e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}\left({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}\right):=e\left((d_{0},{\mathbf{d}}),\,T({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\right)
.

Our main tool: the saturated special fiber ring

In this section, we develop and study the multigraded saturated special fiber ring. Originally, the saturated special fiber ring was introduced in
[
8
]
, and has been successfully used to study several classes of rational maps
[

,
10
,
20
,

,

,

]
.

We begin by fixing the following setup, which is used throughout this section.

Setup 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be an arbitrary field,
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
a standard graded polynomial ring and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
the graded irrelevant ideal of
R
.
For each
1\leq i\leq p
, let
\{f_{i,0},f_{i,1},\ldots,f_{i,m_{i}}\}\subset R
be a set of forms of the same degree
\delta_{i}\geq 1
,
I_{i}=(f_{i,0},f_{i,1},\ldots,f_{i,m_{i}})\subset R
the ideal generated by these forms and
\mathcal{G}_{i}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}
the rational map determined by
(\mathbf{x})=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\mapsto(f_{i,0}(\mathbf{x}),f_{i,1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,f_{i,m_{i}}(\mathbf{x}))
.
Let

\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{p})\;:\;{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}

be the rational map given by the product of
\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{p}
.
Let
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be the closure of the image of
\mathcal{G}
and
\Gamma=\overline{\{(\mathbf{x},\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{x}))\}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
the closure of the graph of
\mathcal{G}
.
Let
{\mathbb{P}}
be the multiprojective space
{\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.

With respect to the above setting, we call
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
the
nonlinear multiview variety
associated to
\mathcal{G}
.
Note that, if
\delta_{i}=1
for all
i
, then we are in the classical case of
multiview varieties
.

3.1.
Rational maps and some related algebras

In this subsection, we recall some basic facts regarding rational maps.
The
(multigraded) Rees algebra
of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
is given by

\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}):=R[I_{1}t_{1},\ldots,I_{p}t_{p}]=\bigoplus_{n_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,n_{p}\geq 0}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots I_{p}^{n_{p}}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots t_{p}^{n_{p}}\;\subset\;R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}].

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded polynomial ring

\mathfrak{A}\,:=\,R\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{y}_{1}]\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{y}_{p}]\,=\,R\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[y_{1,0},y_{1,1},\ldots,y_{1,m_{1}}]\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[y_{p,0},y_{p,1},\ldots,y_{p,m_{1}}],

where
\deg(x_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
for all
0\leq i\leq r
, and
\deg(y_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq p,0\leq j\leq m_{i}
.
By setting
\deg(t_{i})=-\delta_{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{1}+{\mathbf{e}}_{i+1}\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
, the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
inherits a natural
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded structure.
And so, we can present
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
as quotient of
\mathfrak{A}
via the following
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-homogeneous surjective
R
-homomorphism

\mathfrak{A}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}),\qquad y_{i,j}\mapsto f_{i,j}t_{i}.

Thus, the
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded structure of
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
is given by

\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\;=\;\bigoplus_{c\in\mathbb{N},\;{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}{\big[\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big]}_{c,{\mathbf{n}}}\ ,

where each
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded part is

{\big[\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big]}_{c,{\mathbf{n}}}\;=\;{\big[\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\big]}_{c+{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}\;=\;{\big[I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\big]}_{c+n_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}\delta_{p}}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}}.

Since we are primarily interested in the
R
-grading of the Rees algebra, we fix the following notation.

Notation 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be an
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded module over
\mathfrak{A}
.
We denote by
{\left[\mathbb{M}\right]}_{c}
the “one-sided”
R
-graded part

{\left[\mathbb{M}\right]}_{c}=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}}{\left[\mathbb{M}\right]}_{c,{\mathbf{n}}}.

Then,
{\left[\mathbb{M}\right]}_{c}
has a natural
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded structure, and its
{\mathbf{n}}
-th graded part is given by

{\big[{\left[\mathbb{M}\right]}_{c}\big]}_{\mathbf{n}}={\left[\mathbb{M}\right]}_{c,{\mathbf{n}}}

where
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

The
(multigraded) special fiber ring
of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
is given by

\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\,:=\,\big[\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big]_{0}\,\cong\,\mathbb{k}\big[[I_{1}]_{\delta_{1}}t_{1},\ldots,[I_{p}]_{\delta_{p}}t_{p}\big]\,\cong\,\bigoplus_{n_{i}\geq 0}\left[I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\right]_{n_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}\delta_{p}}.

Note that
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
is naturally a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
As the ideals
I_{i}
’s are equigenerated in degrees
\delta_{i}
’s, Nakayama’s lemma yields the natural isomorphism
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\cong\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{m}
.

In the following proposition, we recall the known relationships between the rational map
\mathcal{G}
and the algebras
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
and
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

(i)

The closure of the image of
\mathcal{G}
coincides with
Y=\text{MultiProj}(\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))
.

(ii)

The closure of the graph of
\mathcal{G}
coincides with
\Gamma=\text{MultiProj}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))
.

Proof.

For details, see e.g.
[
18
, §3]
.
∎

We will now recall some equivalent conditions for a rational map to be generically finite.
For any homogeneous ideal
J\subset R
, we denote by
\ell(J):=\dim\left(\mathcal{R}(J)/{\mathfrak{m}}\mathcal{R}(J)\right)
the
analytic spread
of
J
.
We have the following equality

\dim(Y)=\dim\left(\mathbb{k}\Big[[I_{1}]_{\delta_{1}}t_{1},\ldots,[I_{p}]_{\delta_{p}}t_{p}\Big]\right)-p.

The Segre embedding yields the isomorphism

Y=\text{MultiProj}\left(\mathbb{k}\Big[[I_{1}]_{\delta_{1}}t_{1},\ldots,[I_{p}]_{\delta_{p}}t_{p}\Big]\right)\cong\text{Proj}\left(\mathbb{k}\Big[[I_{1}\cdots I_{p}]_{\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{p}}\Big]\right).

Thus, we obtain that
\dim(Y)=\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{p})-1
.
Finally, we say that the rational map
\mathcal{G}
is
generically finite
when any of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\left[K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}):K(Y)\right]<\infty
where
K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r})
and
K(Y)
denote the fields of rational functions of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
and
Y
, respectively.

(ii)

\dim(Y)=r
.

(iii)

\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{p})-1=r
.

Whenever
\mathcal{G}
is generically finite, we define the
degree
of
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
to be

\deg(\mathcal{G}):=\left[K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}):K(Y)\right].

The map
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
birational
if
\deg(\mathcal{G})=1
.
If the degree of the field extension
K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r})|K(Y)
is infinite, then we say that
\mathcal{G}
has no well-defined degree and, by convention, we write
\deg(\mathcal{G})=0
.

3.2.
A multigraded version of the saturated special fiber ring

Our main tool to study the rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is the following algebra.

Definition 3.4
.

The
(multigraded) saturated special fiber ring
of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
is the
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra given by

\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\,:=\,\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\Big[\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}:_{R}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\Big]_{{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}=\bigoplus_{n_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,n_{p}\geq 0}\Big[\left(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}:_{R}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\Big]_{n_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}\delta_{p}}.

An important result regarding the saturated special fiber ring is the following.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded module over the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.
In particular,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
has a Hilbert polynomial
P_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}(\mathbf{t})
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 2.7]
.
∎

Our main result is the following theorem, which says that the mixed multiplicities of the saturated special fiber ring are equal to the product of the multidegrees of
Y
and the degree of
\mathcal{G}
.
This provides an extension
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
for nonlinear multiview varieties.

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is generically finite.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

For all
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
, we have the equality

e\big({\mathbf{d}},\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)\;=\;\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y).

(ii)

Under the additional condition of
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
being integrally closed,
\mathcal{G}
is birational if and only if
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})=\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.

Proof.

(i) For ease of notation, let
S:=\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
and
T:=\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.
We consider the following functions

F_{S}({\mathbf{n}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[S\right]_{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}}\right)}{n^{r}}\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[T\right]_{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}}\right)}{n^{r}}.

These functions coincide with the top-degree parts of the Hilbert polynomials of
S
and
T
.
In other words, we can write

F_{S}({\mathbf{n}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r}\frac{\deg^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\,{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r}\frac{e\big({\mathbf{d}},\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\,{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathbf{d}}.

Since we have the equalities

S^{({\mathbf{n}})}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[I_{1}^{nn_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{nn_{p}}\right]_{nn_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+nn_{p}\delta_{p}}=\mathfrak{F}(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})

and

T^{({\mathbf{n}})}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\left(I_{1}^{nn_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{nn_{p}}:_{R}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\right]_{nn_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+nn_{p}\delta_{p}}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}),

it follows that the functions
F_{S}
and
F_{T}
can be obtained by computing the multiplicities of the special fiber ring and the saturated special fiber ring of products of powers of the ideals
I_{i}
’s.
More precisely, we have that

F_{S}({\mathbf{n}})=e\left(\mathfrak{F}(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})=e\left(\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})\right).

For each
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r}
, we compose Veronese and Segre embeddings to obtain the following natural embedding

\Delta:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}\;\hookrightarrow\;{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\binom{n_{1}+m_{1}}{m_{1}}-1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\binom{n_{p}+m_{p}}{m_{p}}-1}\;\hookrightarrow\;{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m}

where
m=\binom{n_{1}+m_{1}}{m_{1}}\cdots\binom{n_{p}+m_{p}}{m_{p}}-1
.
Hence, we obtain the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime},}
\cong
\mathcal{G}
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}

where
Y_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m}
is the image of
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
under the embedding
\Delta
and
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow Y_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m}
is the rational map induced by the set of generators naturally obtained for the ideal
I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}
.
It is clear that
\deg(\mathcal{G})=\deg(\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime})
.
So, for each
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{p}
,
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
may be applied to the rational map
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}
and gives the following equalities

F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})=e\left(\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})\right)=\deg(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\cdot\deg(Y_{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime})=\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot e\left(\mathfrak{F}(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})\right)=\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot F_{S}({\mathbf{n}}).

Finally, by comparing the coefficients of the polynomials
F_{S}({\mathbf{n}})
and
F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})
, the claimed equality

e\big({\mathbf{d}},\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)\;=\;\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot\deg^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)

follows for all
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
.

(ii) The proof follows similarly to the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 2.4(iv)]
.
∎

3.3.
A cohomological formula relating the degree of the rational map and the multidegrees of the image

Throughout this subsection, we continue using
Section 3
. Here, we give a formula that relates the degree of
\mathcal{G}
with the mixed multiplicities of
{\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]}_{0}
and the multidegrees of the image
Y
.
Notice that
{\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]}_{0}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded module over the special fiber ring
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
, and so we can consider its mixed multiplicities.
The following result extends
[
8
, Corollary 2.12]
into the current setting.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is generically finite.
Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
, we have the equality

\deg^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\cdot\big(\deg(\mathcal{G})-1\big)\,=\,e\left({\mathbf{d}},{\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]}_{0}\right).

In particular,
\mathcal{G}
is birational if and only if
\dim\left(\text{Supp}\left({\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]}_{0}\right)\cap Y\right)<r
.

Proof.

We have the short exact sequence

0\,\rightarrow\,\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\big[\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\big]_{{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}\,\rightarrow\,\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\Big[\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}:_{R}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\Big]_{{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}\,\rightarrow\,\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\Big[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\Big]_{{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}\,\rightarrow\,0,

which can then be written as

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\rightarrow{\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]}_{0}\rightarrow 0.

The additivity of mixed multiplicities gives the equality

e({\mathbf{d}},\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))=e({\mathbf{d}},\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))+e({\mathbf{d}},{\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]}_{0}),

and so the result follows from
Theorem 3.6
.
∎

3.4.
A degree formula for rational maps

Here, we study the case when
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is not defined only in a finite number of points.
In particular, we provide a formula that relates the degree of
\mathcal{G}
, the multidegrees of the image, and the mixed multiplicities of the base points.
This can be seen as an extension of a known degree formula, see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
,
[
53
, Theorem 6.6]
and
[
8
, Theorem 3.3]
).
This formula could also be derived by utilizing
[
33
, Proposition 4.4]
.
We also give a general upper bound for the degree of the image for arbitrary rational maps that are generically finite.

Hereafter, we use the same notations and conventions of
Section 3
.
We have that the
base locus of
\mathcal{G}
(i.e. the points where
\mathcal{G}
is not well-defined) is given by

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}):=V(I_{1}\cdots I_{p})\,\subset\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}.

We then have that
\dim(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))=0
(i.e. the base locus is
finite
) if and only if
\dim(R/I_{i})\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq p
.
Assuming that
\dim(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))=0
,
we then have the equalities

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\Big(\text{H}^{0}\Big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}/(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})^{\sim}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\Big({\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}/(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})^{\sim}\big)}_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}\left[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):\mathbb{k}\right]\cdot\text{length}_{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathfrak{p}}}\Big({\big(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}/(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})^{\sim}\big)}_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}\left[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):\mathbb{k}\right]\cdot\text{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\Big(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})R_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big)

for all
n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
denotes the residue field of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since we have
\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/I_{i}R_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
by assumption, it follows that the function

P_{R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;:=\;\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\text{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\Big(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/(I_{1}^{nn_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{nn_{p}})R_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big)}{n^{r}}

is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}]
. For further details, we refer the reader to the survey
[
55
]
and Chapter 17 of the book
[
38
]
.
The polynomial
P_{R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
is of degree
r
if there is some
i
with
I_{i}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and the zero polynomial otherwise.
We write the polynomial
P_{R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}}
as

P_{R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\,=\,\sum_{{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,|{\mathbf{d}}|=r}\frac{e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{p}!}n_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots n_{p}^{d_{p}},

where the numbers
e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
are nonnegative integers called the
mixed multiplicities
of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
with respect to
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
.
By summarizing the above results and discussion, it follows that the following expression

P_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;:=\;\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\Big(\text{H}^{0}\Big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}/(I_{1}^{nn_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{nn_{p}})^{\sim}\Big)\Big)}{n^{r}}

is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}]
.
We have that
P_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}
is a polynomial of degree
r
if
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})\neq\emptyset
, and the zero polynomial otherwise.
Furthermore, we can write
P_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}
as

P_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\,=\,\sum_{{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\,|{\mathbf{d}}|=r}\frac{e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))}{d_{1}!\cdots d_{p}!}n_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots n_{p}^{d_{p}},

where the numbers
e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))
are nonnegative integers and given by

e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))\;:=\;\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}\left[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):\mathbb{k}\right]\cdot e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}).

So, the nonnegative integers
e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))
gather the mixed multiplicities of the base points, and we call them the
mixed multiplicities of the base locus of
\mathcal{G}
.

We are now ready to state the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is generically finite.
Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
, the following statements hold:

(i)

\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\deg(\mathcal{G})\,\leq\,\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p}^{d_{p}}
.

(ii)

If
\dim(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))=0
, then we have the equality

\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p}^{d_{p}}\,=\,\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\deg(\mathcal{G})+e_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})).

Proof.

For any
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{p}
, we have the exact sequence of sheaves

0\rightarrow{(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})}^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\rightarrow\frac{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}}{{(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})}^{\sim}}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\rightarrow 0,

that yields the following exact sequence in cohomology

0\rightarrow\text{H}^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}})^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)\rightarrow\text{H}^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)\rightarrow\text{H}^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\frac{\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}}{(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}})^{\sim}}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)\rightarrow\text{H}^{1}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}})^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)\rightarrow 0.

Notice that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}(\text{H}^{0}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)))=\binom{{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta+r}{r}
is a polynomial of degree
r
in
\mathbb{Q}[{\mathbf{n}}]
. By
[
18
, Theorem 4.4]
, we have that

\dim\left(\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0}\right)\leq(\dim(R)+p)-i=r+1+p-i.

This implies that for any
i\geq 1
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{p}
with
n_{i}\gg 0
, the expression

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big(\text{H}^{i}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},{(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}})}^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)\big)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\big({\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i+1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]}_{0,{\mathbf{n}}}\big)

is a polynomial in
{\mathbf{n}}
of degree strictly less than
r
. So, for any
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{p}
we have the equalities

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},(\mathbf{I}^{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}})^{\sim}(n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)-h^{1}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},(\mathbf{I}^{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}})^{\sim}(n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)}{n^{r}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},(\mathbf{I}^{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}})^{\sim}(n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)}{n^{r}}=F_{T}({\mathbf{n}}),

where

F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[T\right]_{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}}\right)}{n^{r}}
is the polynomial function that encodes the mixed multiplicities of the saturated special fiber ring
T=\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.
Since
F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
r
in
\mathbb{Q}[{\mathbf{n}}]
, it follows that the following function

Q({\mathbf{n}})\,:=\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\,\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}/(\mathbf{I}^{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}})^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)}{n^{r}}\,=\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\binom{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta+r}{r}}{n^{r}}-F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})

is also a homogeneous polynomial of degree
r
in
\mathbb{Q}[{\mathbf{n}}]
.
The fact that
Q({\mathbf{n}})\geq 0
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
implies that the coefficients of
Q({\mathbf{n}})
are all nonnegative.
Therefore, the equality
F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})+Q({\mathbf{n}})\,=\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\binom{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta+r}{r}}{n^{r}}
and
Theorem 3.6
yield the general upper bounds
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\deg(\mathcal{G})\,\leq\,\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p}^{d_{p}}
.
This completes the proof of part (i).

Next, we assume that the base locus of
\mathcal{G}
is zero-dimensional. Since
\dim(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))=0
, we obtain the equalities

Q({\mathbf{n}})\,:=\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\,\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}/(\mathbf{I}^{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}})^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\big)}{n^{r}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h^{0}\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\,\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}/(\mathbf{I}^{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}})^{\sim}\big)}{n^{r}}=P_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}({\mathbf{n}}).

So, combining the above expressions for
Q({\mathbf{n}})
, we obtain the following equality of polynomials

F_{T}({\mathbf{n}})+P_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})}({\mathbf{n}})\,=\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\binom{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta+r}{r}}{n^{r}}.

And so, the result of part (ii) follows from
Theorem 3.6
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

As a direct consequence of the above theorem, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that all the ideals
I_{i}
are
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary
(
i.e. the rational map
\mathcal{G}
is actually a morphism
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
)
.
Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
, we have the equality

\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\deg(\mathcal{G})\,=\,\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p}^{d_{p}}.

4.
Linear rational maps

In this section, we revisit the case of linear multiview varieties that has been extensively studied. Here, we use the following setup.

Setup 4.1
.

Assume

Section 3
and let
\delta_{i}=1
for each
i
. That is, the forms
f_{i,j}
defining the rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
are linear. In addition, we will assume that the field is algebraically closed.

We start by recalling that, in this setting, the mixed multiplicities of the special fiber ring are either zero or one.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
47
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
23
, Theorem 3.9]
)
.

Assume
Section 4
and suppose that the map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is generically finite.
Then, the closure
Y
of the image of
\mathcal{G}
is multiplicity-free.
That is,
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\in\{0,1\}
for all
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r
.

Proof.

This result follows directly from
Theorem 3.8
(i).
∎

Our next observation is the following interesting corollary of
Theorem 3.6
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume
Section 4
and suppose that
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
is generically finite.
Then, the saturated special fiber ring and the special fiber ring coincide.
Explicitly, for each
(n_{1},\dots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we have

\left[\left(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}:_{R}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)\right]_{n_{1}+n_{2}+\dots+n_{p}}=\left[\left(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\right)\right]_{n_{1}+n_{2}+\dots+n_{p}}.

Proof.

By
Theorem 4.2
we have that
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\dots,I_{p})
is multiplicity-free.
Hence, by a theorem of Brion, see
[
22
, Theorem 1.11]
and
[
7
, Theorem 2]
, the special fiber ring
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\dots,I_{p})
is integrally closed. We may therefore apply
Theorem 3.6
(ii) to deduce that
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\dots,I_{p})=\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\dots,I_{p})
if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is birational. By assumption, all forms
f_{i,j}
defining
\mathcal{G}
are linear and so the
\mathcal{G}
is birational.
∎

We now restrict our attention to the case where the base locus of
\mathcal{G}
is zero-dimensional. For each
1\leq i\leq p
, the rational map
\mathcal{G}_{i}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}
is then given by

(x_{0},\dots,x_{r})\mapsto A_{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{r})^{T}\quad\text{for some matrix }A_{i}\in\mathbb{k}^{(m_{i}+1)\times(r+1)}.

Under the assumption that
\dim(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))=0
, we set-theoretically obtain
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})=\{\text{Ker}(A_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq p\}
and we have the equivalent conditions
\dim(R/I_{i})\leq 1
and
\text{rank}(A_{i})\geq r
.
We note that each ideal
I_{i}
is linear, hence prime.

Remark 4.4
.

Suppose that
r=3
and
m_{i}=2
for all
i
. The assumption that the base locus is zero-dimensional is equivalent to the defining matrices
A_{i}
of the rational map
\mathcal{G}_{i}

be of rank
3
.
In particular, this is the case studied in
[
1
]
which we generalize in
Section 4
.

By applying
Theorem 3.8
, we can give an explicit description of the degrees
{\mathbf{d}}
, where
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, such that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)=1
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume
Section 4
and suppose that
\dim(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}))=0
.
Let
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
.
Then
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{d}}}(Y)=1
if and only if for each point
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})
there exists
1\leq i\leq p
such that
I_{i}\neq\mathfrak{p}
and
d_{i}\geq 1
.

Proof.

Fix a point
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})
in the base locus. We write
I_{i_{1}}=I_{i_{2}}=\dots=I_{i_{t}}=\mathfrak{p}
for all of the ideals which correspond to the same point in the base locus. We proceed by calculating the value of
e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\dots,I_{p})
. Note that for each
1\leq j\leq p
we have

I_{j}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}&\text{if }j\in\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{t}\},\\
R_{p}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

So, it follows that

e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\dots,I_{p})=\begin{cases}e(\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})=1\quad\text{if }d_{i_{1}}+d_{i_{2}}+\cdots+d_{i_{t}}=r\\
0\quad\qquad\qquad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for more details see
[
38
, §17.4]
.

By
Theorem 3.8
, we have

1=\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{d}}}(Y)\deg(\mathcal{G})+e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})).

The mixed multiplicities of the base locus are given by
\sum[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):\mathbb{k}]e_{{\mathbf{d}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}};I_{1},\dots,I_{p})
where the sum is taken over the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})
.
Since,
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed, for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})
, we have
[\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):\mathbb{k}]=1
.
Finally, it follows that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)=1
if and only if for each point
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G})
there exists some
1\leq i\leq p
such that
I_{i}\neq\mathfrak{p}
and
d_{i}\geq 1
.
∎

5.
Certain special families of rational maps

In this section, we consider some special families of rational maps for which we give exact formulas for the product of the degree of the rational map with the multidegrees of its image.
We shall extend the computations obtained in
[
19
,
17
]
for perfect ideals of height two and Gorenstein ideals of height three.
Throughout this section we continue using
Section 3
.

We begin by studying the multidegrees of the graph
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
of the rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
To simplify notation, let

\mathbb{D}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}.

Then, for any
(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
with
d_{0}+|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, we shall consider the multidegrees
\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)=e\big((d_{0},{\mathbf{d}}),\,\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)
.
We proceed by relating the multidegrees of the graph
\Gamma
with the mixed multiplicities of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
. The following result provides an extension of
[
20
, Theorem 5.4]
to our setting.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
Section 3
.
Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, we have

\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(0,{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)\,=\,e\big({\mathbf{d}},\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big).

Proof.

This proof follows similarly to
[
20
, Theorem 5.4]
.
Let
\mathfrak{b}=(\mathbf{y}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(\mathbf{y}_{p})
,
{\mathfrak{N}}={\mathfrak{m}}\cap\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{M}={\mathfrak{m}}+{\mathfrak{N}}
. Note that
{\mathfrak{N}}
is the irrelevant ideal in
\mathbb{D}=\text{MultiProj}(\mathfrak{A})
.
From the Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence

\text{H}_{\mathfrak{M}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\oplus\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}_{\mathfrak{N}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\,\rightarrow\,\text{H}_{\mathfrak{M}}^{i+1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))

and the fact that
\left[\text{H}_{\mathfrak{M}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0,{\mathbf{n}}}=\left[\text{H}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0,{\mathbf{n}}}=0
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}
, we obtain the isomorphism

\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0,{\mathbf{n}}}\cong\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{N}}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0,{\mathbf{n}}}\quad\text{for all }{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}.

Let
X=\text{Proj}_{R\text{-gr}}\left(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\right)
be the projective scheme obtained by considering
\mathcal{R}(I)
as a single-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra with the grading of
R
.
By using the relations between sheaf and local cohomologies, see e.g.
[
39
, Corollary 1.5]
and
[
31
, Appendix A4.1]
, we have that

\left[\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\right]_{\mathbf{n}}\;\cong\;\left[\text{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\right]_{\mathbf{n}}\;\cong\;\text{H}^{0}(\Gamma,\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(0,{\mathbf{n}}))\quad\text{for all }{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}.

The multigraded Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
is given by

P_{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}(c,{\mathbf{n}})\,=\,\sum_{i\geq 0}{(-1)}^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\Big(\text{H}^{i}\left(\Gamma,\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(c,{\mathbf{n}})\right)\Big),

see e.g.
[
42
, Lemma 4.3]
.
By
[
18
, Theorem 4.4]
, we have that
\left[\text{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded module over
\mathfrak{F}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
such that

\dim\left(\left[\text{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0}\right)\,\leq\,(\dim(R)+p)-i=r+1+p-i.

Hence, the fact that
\text{H}^{i}\left(\Gamma,\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}(0,{\mathbf{n}})\right)\cong\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{N}}}^{i+1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0,{\mathbf{n}}}\cong\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i+1}(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}))\right]_{0,{\mathbf{n}}}
for all
i\geq 1
and
{\mathbf{n}}\gg\mathbf{0}
gives the equality

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{P_{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}(0,n\cdot{\mathbf{n}})}{n^{r}}\;=\;\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\right]_{n\cdot{\mathbf{n}}}\right)}{n^{r}}

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{p}
.
So, the equality
\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(0,{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)\,=\,e\big({\mathbf{d}},\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)

holds for all
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=r
.
∎

We also relate the multidegrees of the graph
\Gamma
with the mixed multiplicities of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume
Section 3
. Then, for all
(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
with
d_{0}+|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, we have

\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)\,=\,e_{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}\left({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}\right).

Proof.

Consider the
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
T({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.
Since the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
are equally generated in degrees
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{p}
, by Nakayama’s lemma we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[T({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\right]_{n_{0},{\mathbf{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}+1}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{0}}I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\right]_{n_{0}+{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\right]_{n_{0}+{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\right]_{n_{0},{\mathbf{n}}}\right)

for all
(n_{0},{\mathbf{n}})=(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
.
This shows that
T({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
and
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
have the same Hilbert function, and so the result of the lemma follows.
∎

The following proposition deals with the process of cutting the graph
\Gamma
with general hyperplanes in the target space
{\mathbb{P}}
. Note that this is similar to
[
20
, Proposition 5.6]
, where the case of cutting
\Gamma
with general hyperplanes in the source
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
was treated.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume
Section 3
with
\mathbb{k}
being an infinite field.
Fix
1\leq i\leq p
.
Let
h=\alpha_{0}y_{i,0}+\alpha_{1}y_{i,1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m_{i}}y_{i,m_{i}}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{k}
such that
V(h)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}
is a general hyperplane.
Let
f=\alpha_{0}f_{i,0}+\alpha_{1}f_{i,1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m_{i}}f_{i,m_{i}}\in I_{i}
,
S=R/fR
and
J_{j}=I_{j}S\subset S
for all
1\leq j\leq p
.
Then, for all
(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})=(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
with
d_{0}+|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
and
d_{i}\geq 1
, we have

\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)\,=\,e\big((d_{0},\ldots,d_{i}-1,\ldots,d_{p}),\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})\big).

Proof.

By using
[
20
, Lemma 3.7]
, we choose
(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{m_{i}})
in such a way that

h=\alpha_{0}y_{i,0}+\alpha_{1}y_{i,1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m_{i}}y_{i,m_{i}}\in\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{y}_{i}]\subset\mathfrak{A}=R\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{y}_{1}]\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{y}_{p}]

becomes a filter-regular element on
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
and on

\text{gr}_{I_{i}}\big(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,\widehat{I_{i}},\ldots,I_{p})\big)=\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\otimes_{R}R/I_{i}=\bigoplus_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}\geq 0}\frac{I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{i}^{n_{i}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}}{I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{i}^{n_{i}+1}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}},

where
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,\widehat{I_{i}},\ldots,I_{p})=\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{i-1},I_{i+1},\ldots,I_{p})
.
So, by
[
20
, Lemma 3.9]
, we have that

\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)\,=\,e\big((d_{0},{\mathbf{d}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}),\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})/h\,\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)

for all
(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
with
d_{0}+|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
and
d_{i}\geq 1
.
Notice that we have the following natural surjection

\mathfrak{s}\,:\;\frac{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}{h\,\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}\cong\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\frac{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}{f\,\mathbf{I}^{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\;\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})\cong\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\frac{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}+fR}{fR}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}.

For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(R)\setminus V(I_{i})
, the localization
\mathfrak{s}\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
of the surjection
\mathfrak{s}
becomes an isomorphism.
Hence, there is some
l>0
such that
I_{i}^{l}\cdot\text{Ker}(\mathfrak{s})=0
.
Known dimension computations give us that
\dim\left(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\right)=r+1+p
and
\dim\left(\text{gr}_{I_{i}}\big(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,\widehat{I_{i}},\ldots,I_{p})\big)\right)=r+p
, see e.g.
[
38
, §17.5]
.
For any finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{p+1}
-graded
\mathfrak{A}
-module
M
, we set
\text{Supp}_{++}(M):=\text{Supp}(M)\cap\text{MultiProj}(\mathfrak{A})
.
Since
h
is a filter-regular element on
\text{gr}_{I_{i}}\big(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,\widehat{I_{i}},\ldots,I_{p})\big)
and on
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
,
[
20
, Lemma 3.9]
and
[
39
, Lemma 1.2]
imply that

\displaystyle\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}\left(\text{Ker}(\mathfrak{s})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}{h\,\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}\otimes_{R}R/I_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}\left(\text{gr}_{I_{i}}\big(\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,\widehat{I_{i}},\ldots,I_{p})\big)\otimes_{\mathfrak{A}}\mathfrak{A}/h\mathfrak{A}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq(r+p)-(p+1)-1=r-2

and

\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}{h\,\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}\right)\right)=(r+1+p)-(p+1)-1=r-1.

Finally, the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\text{Ker}(\mathfrak{s})\rightarrow\frac{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}{h\,\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{s}}\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})\rightarrow 0
and the additivity of mixed multiplicities yield

\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(d_{0},{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)\,=\,e\left((d_{0},{\mathbf{d}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}),\frac{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}{h\,\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}\right)\,=\,e\left((d_{0},{\mathbf{d}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}),\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})\right),

and so the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

The next lemma will allow us to simplify the families of rational maps we consider in this section.
It shows that the mixed multiplicities of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
depend only on a certain part of the data given and is similar to
[
17
, Proposition 2.11]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p-1}
are
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

We have the equality

\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\,=\,\bigoplus_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}\geq 0}\left[\big(I_{p}^{n_{p}}\big)^{{\text{sat}}}\right]_{n_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}\delta_{p}}.

In particular, for all
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, the value of

e\big({\mathbf{d}},\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)\,=\,\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)
depends only on the degrees
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{p-1}
and on the ideal
I_{p}
.

(ii)

e\big((0,\ldots,0,r),\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)\;=\;e\big((0,r),\,\mathcal{R}(I_{p})\big)\;=\,e\big(\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{p})\big).

Proof.

(i) By the assumption of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p-1}
being
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideals, we have
I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}=I_{p}^{n_{p}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(R)\setminus\{{\mathfrak{m}}\}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
.
We then obtain the equality
\big(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\big)^{\sim}=\big(I_{p}^{n_{p}}\big)^{\sim}
of sheaves.
It follows that

\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\text{H}^{0}\left({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\big(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{p}^{n_{p}}\big)^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\right)=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\text{H}^{0}\left({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\big(I_{p}^{n_{p}}\big)^{\sim}({\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta)\right)=\bigoplus_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\left[\big(I_{p}^{n_{p}}\big)^{{\text{sat}}}\right]_{{\mathbf{n}}\cdot\delta}.

So, the first statement of the lemma follows.

(ii) Consider the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I_{p})
with the usual bigrading
\mathcal{R}(I_{p})=\bigoplus_{c,n\in\mathbb{N}}[\mathcal{R}(I_{p})]_{c,n}
where
[\mathcal{R}(I_{p})]_{c,n}=\big[I_{p}\big]_{c+n\delta_{p}}
.
Let
Q(c,n):=P_{\mathcal{R}(I)}(c,n)
be the bigraded Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{R}(I_{p})
.
Choose
e>0
such that
\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}\left(\mathcal{R}(I_{p})\right)\right]_{k}=0
for all
k\geq e
.
From the grading chosen for the Rees algebra, it follows that

\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(R/I_{p}^{n})\right]_{n\delta_{p}+k}\cong\left[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(I_{p}^{n})\right]_{n\delta_{p}+k}=0

for all
n\geq 0
and
k\geq e
.
This implies that
\left[I_{p}^{n}\right]_{n\delta_{p}+k}=\left[(I_{p}^{n})^{\text{sat}}\right]_{n\delta_{p}+k}
for all
n\geq 0
and
k\geq e
.

Let
P({\mathbf{n}}):=P_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
be the multigraded Hilbert polynomial of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
.
Choose positive integers
n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{p}^{\prime}
with
n_{1}^{\prime}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}^{\prime}\delta_{p-1}\geq e
such that
P(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
equals the Hilbert function of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})
when
n_{i}\geq n_{i}^{\prime}
. We can also assume that
Q(c,n)
equals the Hilbert function of
\mathcal{R}(I_{p})
when
c\geq c^{\prime}:=n_{1}^{\prime}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}^{\prime}\delta_{p-1}
and
n\geq n_{p}^{\prime}
.
Therefore, with the above choices in place, we obtain the following equalities

\displaystyle P(n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{p-1}^{\prime},n)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[\left(I_{p}^{n}\right)^{\text{sat}}\right]_{n_{1}^{\prime}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}^{\prime}\delta_{p-1}+n\delta_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[I_{p}^{n}\right]_{n_{1}^{\prime}\delta_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}^{\prime}\delta_{p-1}+n\delta_{p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([\mathcal{R}(I_{p})]_{c^{\prime},\,n}\right)=Q(c^{\prime},n)

for all
n\geq n_{p}^{\prime}
.
As a consequence, we obtain

e\big((0,\ldots,0,r),\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)\,=\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\frac{P(n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{p-1}^{\prime},n)}{n^{r}/r!}\,=\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\frac{Q(c^{\prime},n)}{n^{r}/r!}\,=\,e\big((0,r),\,\mathcal{R}(I_{p})\big).

Finally,
[
20
, Theorem 5.4]
(or
Theorem 5.1
above) implies that
e\big((0,r),\,\mathcal{R}(I_{p})\big)=e\big(\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{p})\big)
, and so we are done.
∎

We are now ready to state our main result in this section.
We say that an ideal
I\subset R
satisfies the condition
G_{r+1}
when
\mu(I_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in V(I)\subset{\text{Spec}}(R)
with
\text{ht}(\mathfrak{p})<r+1
, where
\mu(I_{\mathfrak{p}})
is the minimal number of generators of
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The following theorem gives exact formulas for the multidegrees of the saturated special fiber ring
e\big({\mathbf{d}},\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)
for several families of rational maps.

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p-1}
are
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary and that
m_{p}\geq r
.

(I)

Suppose that the following conditions hold:

(a)

I_{p}
is perfect of height two with Hilbert-Burch resolution of the form

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m_{p}}R(-\delta_{p}-\mu_{i})\rightarrow{R(-\delta_{p})}^{m_{p}+1}\rightarrow I_{p}\rightarrow 0.

(b)

I
satisfies the condition
G_{r+1}
.

Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, we have

\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\,=\,\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p-1}^{d_{p-1}}\cdot e_{d_{p}}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m_{p}}),

where
e_{d_{p}}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m_{p}})
denotes the elementary symmetric polynomial

e_{d_{p}}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m_{p}})=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{d_{p}}\leq m_{p}}\mu_{j_{1}}\cdots\mu_{j_{d_{p}}}.

(II)

Suppose that the following conditions hold:

(a)

I_{p}
is a Gorenstein ideal of height three.

(b)

Every nonzero entry of an alternating minimal presentation matrix of
I_{p}
has degree
D\geq 1
.

(c)

I_{p}
satisfies the condition
G_{r+1}
.

Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, we have

\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\,=\,\begin{cases}\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p-1}^{d_{p-1}}\cdot D^{d_{p}}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{m_{p}-d_{p}}{2}\rfloor}\binom{m_{p}-1-2k}{d_{p}-1}\quad\text{ if }d_{p}\geq 3\\
\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p-1}^{d_{p-1}}\delta_{p}^{d_{p}}\qquad\qquad\quad\;\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Due to
Theorem 3.6
, it suffices to compute the mixed multiplicities
e\big({\mathbf{d}},\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p-1},I_{p})\big)
.
The assumption of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
being
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary allows us to reduce to the case where
I_{1}=\cdots=I_{p-1}={\mathfrak{m}}
.
By
Section 5
(i), we have the following relation of multigraded Hilbert polynomials

P_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p-1},I_{p})}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p-1},n_{p})\;=\;P_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}({\mathfrak{m}},\ldots,{\mathfrak{m}},I_{p})}(n_{1}\delta_{1},\ldots,n_{p-1}\delta_{p-1},n_{p})

for all
n_{i}\gg 0
.
This implies that

e\big({\mathbf{d}},\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p-1},I_{p})\big)\;=\;\delta_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\delta_{p-1}^{d_{p-1}}\cdot e\big({\mathbf{d}},\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}({\mathfrak{m}},\ldots,{\mathfrak{m}},I_{p})\big)

for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
.
Therefore, for the rest of the proof, we assume that
I_{1}=\cdots=I_{p-1}={\mathfrak{m}}
.

(I)
We can assume that
\mathbb{k}
is an infinite field.
By
Theorem 5.1
, we have that
e\big({\mathbf{d}},\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p-1},I_{p})\big)=\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(0,{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)
.
Since
I_{1}=\cdots=I_{p}={\mathfrak{m}}
, by applying
Section 5
successively we have that

\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(0,d_{1},\ldots,d_{p-1},d_{p})}(\Gamma)\;=\;e\big((0,0,\ldots,0,d_{p}),\,\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p-1},J_{p})\big).

Here
S=R/(l_{1},\ldots,l_{s})R
,
s=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{p-1}
, each
l_{i}=\alpha_{i,0}x_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{i,r}x_{r}
gives a general hyperplane
V(l_{i})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
, and
J_{1}=\cdots=J_{p-1}={\mathfrak{m}}S
,
J_{p}=I_{p}S
.
Then
Section 5
(ii) gives

\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(0,d_{1},\ldots,d_{p-1},d_{p})}(\Gamma)\;=\;e\big((0,0,\ldots,0,d_{p}),\,\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p-1},J_{p})\big)\;=\;e\big((0,d_{p}),\,\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{p})\big).

By
[

, Proposition 5.6(i)]
, we have
e\big((0,d_{p}),\,\mathcal{R}_{S}(J_{p})\big)=e\big((s,d_{p}),\,\mathcal{R}(I_{p})\big)=d_{s}(\mathcal{G}_{i})
, where
d_{s}(\mathcal{G}_{i})
denotes the
s

\mathcal{G}_{i}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
Finally, by applying
[
20
, Theorem 5.7]
, we obtain the formula

d_{s}(\mathcal{G}_{i})=e_{d_{p}}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m_{p}}),

and so the result follows.

(II) The proof follows similarly to (I), however we use
[

instead of
[

. ∎

6.
Monomial rational maps

In this section, we study the case of a monomial rational map.
We shall express the mixed multiplicities of the saturated special fiber ring in terms of mixed volumes of some naturally constructed polytopes.
The following setup is used throughout this section.

Setup 6.1
.

Assume
Section 3
and suppose that the polynomials
f_{i,k}\in I_{i}
are monomials.
So,
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}\subset R
are monomial ideals.

First, we need to recall some important results and fix our notation. Let
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{p})
be a sequence of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
.
For any sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
of nonnegative integers, we denote by
\lambda\mathbf{K}
the Minkowski sum
\lambda\mathbf{K}:=\lambda_{1}K_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{p}K_{p}
and by
\mathbf{K}_{\lambda}
the multiset
\mathbf{K}_{\lambda}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}\bigcup_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}\{K_{i}\}
of
\lambda_{i}
copies of
K_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq p
.
Let us recall Minkowski’s classic theorem, see e.g.
[
32
, Theorem 3.2, page 116]
.

Theorem 6.2

(Minkowski)
.

{\text{Vol}}_{r}(\lambda\mathbf{K})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
r
.

We write the polynomial
{\text{Vol}}_{r}(\lambda\mathbf{K})
as

{\text{Vol}}_{r}(\lambda\mathbf{K})=\sum_{{{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\;\lvert{\mathbf{d}}\rvert=r}}\;\frac{1}{{\mathbf{d}}!}\,{\text{MV}}_{r}(\mathbf{K}_{\mathbf{d}})\,\lambda^{\mathbf{d}},

where
{\text{MV}}_{r}(-)
denotes the
mixed volume
.

For a monomial ideal
J\in R
with monomial generating set
G(J)
, we define the following lattice convex polytope

\Gamma(J)\,:=\,\text{ConvexHull}\left(\big\{(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\,\mid\,x_{0}^{n_{0}}x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}\in G(J)\big\}\right)\,\subset\,\mathbb{R}^{r+1}.

We denote by
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{r+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}
the natural projection given by
(x_{0},x_{1}\ldots,x_{r})\mapsto(x_{1}\ldots,x_{r})
.
We have the following important result that expresses the mixed multiplicities of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
in terms of mixed volumes.

Theorem 6.3

(
[
56
, Theorem 2.4, Corollary 2.5]
)
.

Assume
Section 6
.
Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, we have

e_{(0,{\mathbf{d}})}\left({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}\right)\,=\,{\text{MV}}_{r}\left({\Big(\pi(\Gamma(I_{1})),\,\ldots,\,\pi(\Gamma(I_{p}))\Big)}_{\mathbf{d}}\right).

It should be mentioned that this result was generalized in
[
16
,
15
]
for not-necessarily-Noetherian graded families of ideals.

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume
Section 6
.
Then, for all
{\mathbf{d}}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
, we have

\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\,=\,{\text{MV}}_{r}\left({\Big(\pi(\Gamma(I_{1})),\,\ldots,\,\pi(\Gamma(I_{p}))\Big)}_{\mathbf{d}}\right).

Proof.

The result follows directly by combining
Theorem 3.6
,
Theorem 5.1
,
Section 5
and
Theorem 6.3
.
More precisely, we obtain the following sequence of equalities

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{G})\cdot\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{d}}(Y)

\displaystyle=e\big({\mathbf{d}},\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{p})\big)

\displaystyle=\deg_{\mathbb{D}}^{(0,{\mathbf{d}})}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=e_{(0,{\mathbf{d}})}\left({\mathfrak{m}}\mid I_{1},\ldots,I_{p}\right)

\displaystyle={\text{MV}}_{r}\left({\big(\pi(\Gamma(I_{1})),\,\ldots,\,\pi(\Gamma(I_{p}))\big)}_{\mathbf{d}}\right)

for all
{\mathbf{d}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{d}}|=r
.
∎

We now illustrate the above result with three simple but instructive examples.

Example 6.5
.

Consider the following classical rational maps

\mathcal{G}_{1}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2},\quad(x,y,z)\mapsto(x^{2},y^{2},z^{2})\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\mathcal{G}_{2}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2},\quad(x,y,z)\mapsto(xy,xz,yz)

and denote their corresponding base ideals
I_{1}=(x^{2},y^{2},z^{2})
and

. Let
\pi
be the map that projects onto the
x
and
y

The polytopes
\Gamma(I_{1})
and
\Gamma(I_{2})
are depicted in

along with their projections.

\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2},\qquad(x,y,z)\mapsto((x,y,z),(x^{3},yz^{2},xy^{2}))

7.
Application / Interpretation

The problem of
implicitization
is fundamental in the area of Computer Aided Geometric Design (CAGD).
The main problem, in the case of curves, can be stated as follows. Let
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{k}[t]
be polynomials, then
(x,y):=(a_{1}/a_{0},a_{2}/a_{0})
is a rational parametric curve in
\mathbb{k}^{2}
.
The implicitization of the curve is a polynomial in
x
and
y
which carves out this curve.
This problem is studied in
[
28
,
27
]
using a collection of generators of the syzygy module of
I=(a_{0}x-a_{1},a_{0}y-a_{2})\subseteq\mathbb{k}[x,y,t]
called a
\mu
-basis.
This setup can be translated into the language used throughout this paper by homogenizing. Let
d=\max\{\deg(a_{0}),\deg(a_{1}),\deg(a_{2})\}
and, for each
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, if
a_{i}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}t^{i}
then define
\overline{a_{i}}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}t^{i}s^{d-i}\in\mathbb{k}[s,t]
.
The parametric curve is the image of the rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
given by
\mathcal{G}(s,t)=(\overline{a_{0}},\overline{a_{1}},\overline{a_{2}})
, restricted to a particular affine patch.

The concept of a
\mu
-basis naturally extends to higher dimensions.
For example,
\mu
-bases for surfaces
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}:(s,t)\mapsto(a,b,c,d)
are studied in
[
28
, Section 5]
.
The
\mu
-basis allows us to determine properties about the parametric surface, i.e. the image of
\mathcal{G}
.
Under particular assumptions, Cox computes in
[
28
, Proposition 5.3]
that the degree of the image of
\mathcal{G}
can be determined completely in terms of the degrees of a
\mu
-basis. Explicitly, Cox requires that
\deg(\mathcal{G})=1
and that the base locus
V(a,b,c,d)\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
of the rational map is a local complete intersection. As a result, the formula for the degree is
\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}}(Y)=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}+\mu_{1}\mu_{3}+\mu_{2}\mu_{3}=e_{2}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
, where the
\mu
-basis of
(a,b,c,d)
has degrees
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}
.
In more recent work,
[
19
, Theorem A]
gives a generalization of this result.
It is shown that, for a rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
determined by a perfect base ideal of height two, the degree of the image times the degree of the map equals an elementary symmetric polynomial evaluated at the degrees of the
\mu
-basis. The main tool used in the proof of this formula is the saturated special fiber ring, see
Section 3
.

The study of
\mu
-bases has played an important role in a number of applications. As mentioned above, they appear in the implicitization problem of parametric surfaces and curves.
But, they are also an important tool for detecting singularities and in studying the degree and birationality of rational maps.
They are nowadays an essential tool in many developments in CAGD, see e.g.
[
41
,
11
,
12
,
40
,
13
,
52
,
51
]
.

More generally, in a somehow parallel story,
\mu
-bases form part of the general syzygy-based approach for studying rational maps. This method appears to have been originally initiated in
[
37
]
. In this paper, Hulek, Katz and Schreyer give a characterization of when a map is a Cremona transformation in terms of properties of the syzygies. The use of syzygies in studying rational maps has become an active and particularly fruitful research area, see e.g.
[
29
,
54
,
44
,
30
,
34
,
50
,
3
,
49
,
35
,
8
,
19
,
17
,
20
,
5
,
4
,
45
,
46
,
43
,
26
,
24
,
25
]
.

In this paper, we further extend the syzygy-based approach (a.k.a
\mu
-bases) by considering a family of rational maps which has, until this point, been mostly unobserved.
We are interested in rational maps from a projective variety to a multiprojective variety, typically, a rational map of the form
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
It should be mentioned that rational maps from a multiprojective variety to a projective variety (i.e. a rational map typically of the form
\mathcal{H}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
) are an important gadget in the field of Geometric Modeling, see e.g.
[
3
,
52
,
51
]
.
We define and use a
multigraded generalization of the saturated special fiber ring
that allows us to obtain important results regarding the
(nonlinear) multiview variety
given as the image of
\mathcal{G}
.

Our work is motivated by the study of multiview varieties that were considered in
[
2
]
and in
[
1
]
.
The classical multiview variety is the image of a linear rational map
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
.
Each component of this map
\mathcal{G}_{i}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
is called a
(pinhole) camera
and is given by a full-rank
3\times 4
matrix
A_{i}
. This setup naturally arises in the study of computer vision, and the multiview variety is the closure of the image
Y
of
\mathcal{G}
.
In general, the defining ideal
J
of
Y
is a Cartwright-Sturmfels ideal, and so it has many desirable properties, see
[
22
,
21
]
.
The kernel of
A_{i}
is a single point in
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
and the base locus of
\mathcal{G}
is the collection of these points.
In the case that these points are distinct, by
[
2
, Theorem 3.7]
, we have an explicit description for the multigraded Hilbert function of
J
.
This result follows from explicitly computing the generic initial ideal of the defining ideal
J
of
Y
.
Our primary goal is to show that similar relationships hold even in the nonlinear case.
In particular, we focus on the highest degree coefficients of the multigraded Hilbert polynomial of
J
, which are the multidegrees of the multiprojective variety
Y
.

To study the ideal of the nonlinear multiview variety, see
Section 3
, it is not possible to take the same approach as
[
2
]
since the generic initial ideals are not radical.
Instead, we are able to compute the multidegrees of the image of
\mathcal{G}
using the saturated special fiber ring.
We show in
Theorem 3.6
that our multigraded version of the saturated special fiber ring maintains similar important properties (cf.
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
).
Similarly to
[
2
, Theorem 3.7]
, we show in
Theorem 3.8
that the multidegrees of
Y
are related to the mixed multiplicities of the base points.
In
Section 4
, we recover several known results in the setting of the classical linear multiview varieties (see
Theorem 4.2
,
Section 4
,
Section 4
).
In
Section 5
, we provide explicit formulas for several interesting families of rational maps.
The techniques used in

Future directions.
In this paper, we have computed for large families of rational maps, the multidegrees of their image.
Following this, it is natural to ask how we may compute the other coefficients of the multigraded Hilbert polynomial.
In particular, we ask whether these coefficients can be determined from the points in the base locus analogously to
Theorem 3.8
.
Of course, it is also quite desirable to study other families of nonlinear multiview varieties. And so, we ask for which families of multiview varieties is possible to find explicit formulas for their multidegrees.

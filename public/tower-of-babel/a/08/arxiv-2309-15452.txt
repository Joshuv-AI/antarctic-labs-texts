On invariance of John domains under quasisymmetric mappings
Vasudevarao Allu, Alan P Jose

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that if a homeomorphism is quasisymmetric relative to the boundary of the domain then it maps a length John domain to a diameter John domain. Moreover, we prove a necessary and sufficient condition for a diameter John domain to be length John and thereby prove that if f : G → G ′ f:G\rightarrow G^{\prime} is ( M , C ) − (M,C)- CQH map, where G G is a John domain, and the map extends to the boundary such that the extension is η − \eta- QS relative to δ ​ G \delta G then G ′ G^{\prime} is a John domain. In addition, we characterize distance John domains using weak minimizing property.

1.
Introduction

The concept of quasiconformality in the plane was introduced by Grotözsch in 1928. The idea was later extended by a number of authors to the case where
\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ for }n\geq 3
.
Later, Gehring and his students Palka and Osgood
[
3
,
2
]
have introduced the quasihyperbolic metric which is the generalization of the classical hyperbolic metric in the unit disk.
Since then the quasihyperbolic metric has become an important tool in the studying the quasiconformal maps in Euclidean spaces.
In the 1990s, Väisälä, through a series of papers, see
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, began his systematic investigation of quasiconformality in Banach spaces of infinite dimension, which he termed the free quasiworld.
Since in infinite dimensional Banach spaces many of the methods and tools that are helpful in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are unavailable, he constructed the theory using just elementary arguments. Recently, there have been many enquiries towards this subject (see
[
23
,
22
,
24
,

]
).

John domain were introduced by F. John
[
8
]
in 1961 in connection with elasticity theory. Later, in 1978 O. Martio and J. Sarvas
[
10
]
coined the term ”John domain” and used it to define uniform domains.
These two classes were used to study injectivity properties of locally quasiconformal and locally bilipschitz maps.
The properties and characterizations of John domains in Euclidean spaces and metric spaces were explored by several authors (see
[
9
,
1
,
4
,
5
,
6
]

[

2.1.
Notations

The norm of a vector

For a set

For an element

A curve is a continuous function

The length of

2.2.
John domains

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a domain and let

. Then

is called

-John, if each pair of points

in

can be joined by a curve

in

(2.1)

\min_{j=1,2}l(\gamma[x_{j},x])\leq c\delta_{G}(x)\text{ for all }x\in\gamma,

\gamma[x_{j},x]
denotes the part of the curve

from

to

If the length of the curve in (

is replaced by diam

then the domain is said to be diameter

- John domain. We say that

is distance

-John if (

\min_{j=1,2}|x_{j}-x|\leq c\delta_{G}(x),

for all

.

Definition 2.2
.

For

, a non complete metric space

is called

-John domain, if there is

, called the John center of

in

can be joined to

by a curve

for each

, and we say that

is a
c
-carrot arc.

We define diameter
c
-John with center and distance

-John with center similarly as we have defined for general John domains.

Remark 2.3
.

It is easy to see that if

is a John domain with center

then

. In particular every John domain with center

is bounded. If the domain

is bounded then both definitions

and

are quantitatively equivalent, (see

]
). In this article, we focus primarily on domains that are John in the cigar sense.

It is easy to see that length

-John
\implies

c
-John
\implies

-John.
In

, Martio and Sarvas

have shown that for a John domain

with center

-John. In 1991,Näkki and Väisälä

have showed that this result still holds in the case of unbounded domains in

.
In
[

Näkki and Väisälä

Väisälä
[

constructed an example of a diameter John domain in infinite dimensional Hilbert space which is not a length John domain.

2.3.
Quasihyperbolic metric

Quasihyperbolic metric was first introduced by Gehring and Palka
[
3
]
for proper domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is a very crucial tool in studying quasiconformality in Banach spaces.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G\subsetneq E
be a domain in a Banach space and let
\gamma
be a rectifiable arc then the quasihyperbolic length of
\gamma
is defined as the number(see
[
16
]
)

l_{k}(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{|dz|}{d_{G}(z)}.

For each pair of points
x,y\in G
the quasihyperbolic distance denoted by

k_{G}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma}l_{k}(\gamma),

where the infimum runs over all arcs
\gamma
joining
x
and
y
.

We shall usually abbreviate
d(z)=d_{G}(z)
,
k(x,y)=k_{G}(x,y)
and
k^{\prime}(x,y)=k_{G^{\prime}}(x,y)
where
G^{\prime}\subsetneq E
is a domain.
We also recall some of the elementary inequalities regarding quasihyperbolic metric:

(2.2)

\left|\log\left(\frac{d(x)}{d(y)}\right)\right|\leq k(x,y)\text{ for every }x,y\text{ in }G.

More generally, we have

(2.3)

\log\left(1+\frac{l(\gamma)}{\min\{d(x),d(y)\}}\right)\leq l_{k}(\gamma).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\psi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an increasing function. We say that a curve
\gamma
satisfies the
\psi-
natural condition if the following holds:

(2.4)

\text{ diam}_{k}(\gamma[x,y])\leq\psi\left(\frac{\text{ diam}(\gamma[x,y])}{\text{ dist}(\gamma[x,y],\delta G)}\right)\text{ for every }x,y\in\gamma

where
\text{ diam}_{k}
means the diameter under the quasihyperbolic metric.

A domain
G\subsetneq E
is said to be
\psi-
natural if (
2.4
) holds for every connected subset
A\subset G
.

We remark that the class of natural domains is fairly large, in fact each domain in a finite dimensional Banach space is
\psi-
natural with
\psi
depending only on the dimension (see
[
18
]
).
Väisälä has constructed an example of a broken tube domain in infinite dimensional Hilbert space which is not natural (see
[
20
]
).

2.4.
Maps in freequasiworld

Let
X
be a metric space. By a triple in
X
we mean an ordered sequence
T=(x,y,z)
of three distinct points in
X
. The ratio of
T
is the number

\rho(T)=\frac{|y-x|}{|z-x|}.

If
f:X\rightarrow Y
is an injective map, the image of a triple
T=(x,y,z)
is the triple
f(T)=(f(x),f(y),f(z)).
Suppose that
A\subset X
. A triple
T=(x,y,z)
in
X
is said to be a triple in the pair
(X,A)
if
x\in A
or if
\{y,z\}\subset A
.
Equivalently, both
|y-x|
and
|z-x|
are distances from a point in
A
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces an embedding
f:X\rightarrow Y
is said to be
\eta
-quasisymmetric if there is a homeomorphism
\eta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

(2.5)

\rho(fT)\leq\eta(\rho(T))

holds for each triple
T
in
X
.

Let
A\subset X
, then
f
is said to be
\eta
-quasisymmetric relative to
A
, abbreviated
\eta
-QS rel
A
, if the condition (
2.5
) holds for every triple
T
in
(X,A)
.
Thus quasisymmetry rel
X
is equivalent to ordinary quasisymmetry.

Note that an embedding
f:X\rightarrow Y
is
\eta
-QS rel
A
if, and only if,
\rho(T)\leq t
implies that
\rho(f(T))\leq\eta(t)
for each triple
T
in
(X,A)
and
t\geq 0
.

In 1999, Väisälä
[
19
]
proved that (
2.5
) implies

is an embedding unless
f
is a constant.

Definition 2.7
.

A homeomorphism
f:G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
between domains
G\subsetneq E
and
G^{\prime}\subsetneq E^{\prime}
, where
E
and
E^{\prime}
are Banach spaces, is

coarsely
M-
quasihyperbolic if

\frac{k(x,y)-C}{M}\leq k^{\prime}(f(x),f(y))\leq Mk(x,y)+C\text{ for all }x,y\in G.

∎

\gamma
, then

John domain.
Let

\Gamma\coloneqq\left\{\gamma:x\curvearrowright y:\gamma\text{ is a $c$-cone arc joining $x$ and $y$ }\right\}.

Since
G
is
c
-John,
\Gamma
is not empty and for each
u,v\in G
there exists a
c
-cone arc
\gamma\in\Gamma
connecting
u
and
v
. Then from Lemma
3.1
it follows that
\Gamma
is the required family of curves and therefore
G
has the minimizing property.

Now, suppose
G
has the minimizing property with the family of curves
\Gamma
and constant
c
. Then it follows from Lemma
3.2
that each
\gamma\in\Gamma
is a diameter
c^{\prime}-
John arc. Since for any
u,v\in G
there exists a curve
\gamma\in\Gamma
joining them we obtain that
G
is diameter
c^{\prime}
-John domain.

The reverse implication follows similarly from the arguments in the first implication.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be proper domains in Banach spaces
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively. Suppose
G
has the minimizing property,
f:\bar{G}\rightarrow\bar{G^{\prime}}
is
\eta
QS rel
\delta G
, and maps
G
onto
G^{\prime}
.
We show that
G^{\prime}
has the minimizing property.
Suppose
\Gamma
is the family of curves in
G
such that for any two given points there exists a curve
\gamma\in\Gamma
connecting them and satisfies (
1.1
) with constant
c
for any ordered triple of points
x_{1},x,x_{2}\in\gamma
and
y\in\delta G
. We claim that
\Gamma^{\prime}=f(\Gamma)
is the required family of curves in
G^{\prime}
.

Let
\gamma^{\prime}
be a curve in
\Gamma^{\prime}
and fix ordered triple of points
x_{1}^{\prime}=f(x_{1}),x^{\prime}=f(x),x_{2}^{\prime}=f(x_{2})
in
\gamma^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}=f(y)
be in
\delta G^{\prime}
. Then
x_{1},x,x_{2}
is an ordered triple of points in
\gamma
and
y
is in
\delta G
. Without loss of generality assume that
\min_{j=1,2}|x_{j}-y|=|x_{1}-y|
.
Then by the minimizing property of
G
we obtain
|x_{1}-y|\leq c|x-y|,
which implies
|f(x_{1})-f(y)|\leq\eta(c)|f(x)-f(y)|.

Hence,

1.11

1.10
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that in the proof of Theorem
1.11
we have actually proved that a map which is quasisymmetric relative to the boundary of a domain maps a curve with the minimizing property to a curve with the minimizing property with the constants depending only on the hypothesis. This together with Lemma
3.2
shows that the image of a diameter John arc under a quasisymmetric map relative to the boundary is again a diameter John arc with the constants depending only on the hypothesis. In particular, it implies that diameter John domains are invariant under quasisymmetric maps relative to the boundary. For Euclidean spaces, this leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
c-
John domain and
f:\bar{D}\rightarrow\bar{D^{\prime}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\eta
-QS rel
\delta D
, then
D^{\prime}
is
c^{\prime}-
John domain with
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(c,\eta)
.

Now we provide the proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Suppose that
G\subset E
is a distance
c
- John domain. Set

\Gamma=\left\{\gamma:x_{1}\curvearrowright x_{2}:\gamma\text{ is the distance $c$-John arc },x_{1},x_{2}\in G\right\}.

Fix
x_{1},x_{2}\in G
and
\gamma:x_{1}\curvearrowright x_{2}
in
\Gamma
. Then for
x\in\gamma
and
y\in\delta G
, we have

(3.3)

\min_{j=1,2}|x_{j}-y|\leq\min_{j=1,2}|x_{j}-x|+|x-y|\leq c\delta_{G}(x)+|x-y|\leq(1+c)|x-y|.

Therefore,
G
satisfies the weak minimizing property with the family of curves
\Gamma
and constant
1+c
.

Conversely, suppose
G
has the minimizing property with
\Gamma^{\prime}
as the family of curves and
c^{\prime}
as the constant satisfying (
1.2
). Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in G
and
\alpha:x_{1}\curvearrowright x_{2}
be the curve in
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
For
x\in\alpha
, choose
z\in\delta G
with
|x-z|\leq 2\delta_{G}(x).

Then by (
1.2
), we have

\min_{j=1,2}|x_{j}-x|\leq\min_{j=1,2}|x_{j}-z|+|x-z|\leq(c+1)|z-x|\leq 2(c+1)\delta_{G}(x).

Therefore,
G
is distance
c^{\prime}
- John, where
c^{\prime}=2(c+1)
.
∎

1.12

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose

and

be domains. Let

be
\eta
-QS rel

and maps

onto

. Further, suppose that

Proof.

Fix
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
and

. Then there exist

,

such that
f(x_{i})=x_{i}^{\prime}
, for

and
f(y)=y^{\prime}
. Further, there exists a curve
\gamma:x_{1}\curvearrowright x_{2}
in

Thus by the quasisymmetry of

Hence, it follows that

4.
Proofs of Theorems
1.14
,
1.16
and Corollary
1.15

We break down the proof of Theorem
1.14
into two lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G\subsetneq E
be a
c-
John domain then every pair of points can be joined by an arc
\gamma
which satisfies the
\psi-
natural condition.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in G
and let
\gamma
be the cone arc joining
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. We show that
\gamma
satisfies the natural condition.
Let
x_{0}\in\gamma
be such that
l(\gamma[x_{1},x_{0}])=l(\gamma[x_{2},x_{0}])
.
Set
\alpha_{1}=\gamma[x_{1},x_{0}]
and
\alpha_{2}=\gamma[x_{2},x_{0}]
.

If
\alpha_{1}\subset\bar{B(x_{1},d(x_{1})/2)}
then we have

\frac{d(x_{1}}{2}\leq d(x_{1})-|x_{1}-x_{0}|\leq d(y)\leq d(x_{1})+|x_{0}-x_{1}|\leq\frac{3}{2}d(x_{1})

which yields

l_{k}(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}])\leq\frac{2}{d(x_{1})}l(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}])\leq 2c\frac{d(x_{0})}{d(x_{1})}\leq 3c.

If
\alpha\nsubseteq\bar{B(x_{1},d(x_{1})/2)}
, then there is a point
w\in\alpha_{1}
such that
l(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},w])=\frac{1}{2}d(x_{1}).

Hence
l_{k}(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},w])\leq 3c
.

Next, if
\alpha_{1}
is parametrized by arclength
s
with
\alpha_{1}(0)=x_{1}
, then

s\leq l(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},z])\leq cd(z)

and thus we obtain

l_{k}\alpha_{1}[w,x_{0}])=\int_{\alpha_{1}([w,y])}\frac{ds}{d(z)}\leq c\int_{\frac{d(x_{1})}{2}}^{l(\alpha_{1}([x_{1},x_{0}])}\frac{ds}{s}\leq c\log\left(\frac{2cd(x_{0})}{d(x_{1})}\right).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle l_{k}\left(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}]\right)

\displaystyle=l_{k}\left(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},w]\right)+l_{k}\left(\alpha_{1}[w,x_{0}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\log\left(2c\frac{d(x_{0})}{d(x_{1})}\right)+3c

(4.1)

\displaystyle=a\log\left(\frac{d(x_{0})}{d(x_{1})}\right)+b

where
a=c
and
b=c\log(2c)+3c
.

Similarly,

l_{k}(\alpha_{2}[x_{2},x_{0}])\leq a\log\left(\frac{d(x_{0})}{d(x_{2})}\right)+b.

Now from (
4.1
) we obtain,

\displaystyle\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}])

\displaystyle\leq l_{k}(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}])\leq a\log\left(\frac{d(x_{0})}{d(x_{1})}\right)+b

\displaystyle\leq a\log\left(1+\frac{|x_{1}-x_{0}|}{d(x_{1})}\right)+b

\displaystyle\leq a\log\left(1+\frac{\text{ diam}(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}]}{\text{ dist}\left(\alpha_{1},\delta G\right)}\right)+b

which yields

(4.2)

\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}])\leq a\log\left(1+\frac{\text{ diam}(\gamma[x_{1},x_{2}]}{\text{ dist}(\gamma,\delta G)}\right)+b

and similarly,

(4.3)

\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha_{2}[x_{1},x_{0}])\leq a\log\left(1+\frac{\text{ diam}(\gamma[x_{1},x_{2}]}{\text{ dist}(\gamma,\delta G)}\right)+b.

Finally,

\displaystyle\text{ diam}_{k}(\gamma[x_{1},x_{2}])

\displaystyle\leq\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha_{1}[x_{1},x_{0}])+\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha_{2}[x_{2},x_{0}])

\displaystyle 2c\log\left(1+\frac{\text{ diam}(\gamma[x_{1},x_{2}]}{\text{ dist}(\gamma,\delta G)}\right)+2b.

Now the proof follows from the fact that every sub-arc of a
c-
cone arc is again a
c-
cone arc.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G\subsetneq E
be a diameter
c-
John domain such that any two points in
G
can be joined by diameter
c-
John arc which satisfies the
\psi-
natural condition. Then
G
is length
c^{\prime}-
John for some constant
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(c,\psi)
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in G
and
\gamma
be a diameter
c-
John arc joining
x
and
y
satisfying the
\psi
-natural condition. Further, let
x_{0}
be the midpoint of
\gamma
. That is,

(4.4)

\text{ diam}\left(\gamma[x,x_{0}]\right)=\text{ diam}\left(\gamma[x_{0},y]\right)\leq cd(x_{0}).

We need to show that there is a
c^{\prime}-
cone arc joining
x
and
y
. By symmetry it suffices to show that there exists an arc
\sigma
joining
x
to
x_{0}
satisfying

(4.5)

l\left(\sigma[x,z]\right)\leq c^{\prime}d(z)\text{ for every }z\in\sigma.

Set
\alpha=\gamma[x,x_{0}].

We divide the proof into two cases.

Case 1:
2d(x)\geq d(x_{0})
.

Clearly,

(4.6)

\frac{d(x_{0})}{2}\leq d(x)\leq d(z)+|z-x|\leq(1+c)d(z)\text{ for every }z\in\alpha.

From the naturality of
\gamma
, (
4.4
) and (
4.6
), it follows that

(4.7)

k(x,x_{0})\leq\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha)\leq\psi\left(\frac{\text{ diam}(\alpha)}{\text{ dist}(\alpha,\delta G)}\right)\leq\psi(2c(1+c)).

Hence there exists a curve
\sigma
joining
x
and
x_{0}
such that

l_{k}(\sigma)\leq 2k(x,x_{0})\leq 2\psi(2c(1+c)):=A

Then for all
z\in\sigma
we obtain

\left|\log\left(\frac{d(z)}{d(x_{0})}\right)\right|\leq k(z,x_{0})\leq l_{k}(\sigma)\leq A.

Therefore,

e^{-A}d(x_{0})\leq d(z)\leq e^{A}d(x_{0})\text{ for every }z\in\sigma

which together with (
2.3
) implies that

(4.8)

l(\sigma)\leq e^{l_{k}(\sigma)}d(x_{0})\leq e^{A}d(x_{0})\leq e^{2A}d(z)\text{ for every }z\in\sigma

which is the desired result (
4.5
).

Case 2:
2d(x)<d(x_{0})
.

Let
m
be the least integer with
m\geq\log_{2}\left(\frac{d(x_{0})}{d(x)}\right)
.

Our aim is to find points
x=x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{m},x_{m+1}=x_{0}
in the curve
\alpha
and construct arcs
\beta_{i}
between them having uniformly bounded quasihyperbolic length.
Let
x_{2}\in\alpha
be the first point such that
d(x_{2})=2d(x_{1})<d(x_{0})
while travelling through the arc from
x
to
x_{0}
.

Now for every
z\in\alpha[x_{1},x_{2}]
, we obtain

(4.9)

d(x_{1})\leq d(z)+|x_{1}-z|\leq(1+c)d(z).

It is easy to see that,

(4.10)

\text{ diam}\left(\alpha[x_{1},x_{2}]\right)\leq cd(x_{2})=2cd(x_{1})

From (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and by the naturality of the curve
\gamma
, we obtain

k(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha[x_{1},x_{2}])\leq\psi\left(\frac{\text{ diam}(\alpha[x_{1},x_{2}])}{\text{ dist}(\alpha[x_{1},x_{2}],\delta G)}\right)\leq\psi(2c(1+c)).

Therefore, there exists a curve
\sigma_{1}
joining
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
such that

l_{k}(\sigma_{1})\leq 2k(x_{1},x_{2})\leq 2\psi(2c(1+c)):=A.

Therefore, a similar calculation as in Case 1, we obtain

\left|\log\left(\frac{d(z)}{d(x_{1})}\right)\right|\leq k(z,x_{1})\leq l_{k}(\sigma_{1})\leq A\text{ for every }z\in\sigma_{1}

which yields

l(\sigma_{1})\leq e^{A}d(x_{1}).

Now if
2d(x_{2})\geq d(x_{0})
, we construct a curve
\sigma_{2}
joining
x_{2}
and
x_{3}=x_{0}
as in Case 1 with
l(\sigma_{2})\leq e^{A}d(x_{2})
.
Otherwise, we continue this process letting
x_{3}\in\alpha
be the first point such that
d(x_{3})=2d(x_{2})
.
Since any subarc of a diameter
c-
John arc is again a diameter
c-
John arc a similar calculation as before yields us an arc
\sigma_{2}
joining
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
with
l(\sigma_{2})\leq e^{A}d(x_{2})
.
We continue this process by letting
x_{i+1}
as the first point of
\alpha
with
d(x_{i+1})=2d(x_{i})
and thereby obtain an arc
\sigma_{i}
joining
x_{i}
to
x_{i+1}
with

(4.11)

l(\sigma_{i})\leq e^{A}d(x_{i}).

Since,
d(x_{i})=2^{i-1}d(x_{1})
the process will end as soon as
i\geq\log_{2}\left(d(x_{0})/d(x_{1})\right)
.
Let
x_{m}\in\alpha
be the point such that
2d(x_{m})\geq d(x_{0})=d(x_{m+1})
. Also

\text{ diam}(\alpha[x_{m},x_{0}])\leq cd(x_{0})\leq 2cd(x_{m})

and

d(x_{m})\leq d(z)+|x_{m}-z|\leq(1+c)d(z).

Therefore, we have

k(x_{m},x_{0})\leq\psi(2c(1+c)).

Choose an arc
\sigma_{m}
joining
x_{m}
to
x_{m+1}
satisfying

(4.12)

l_{k}(\sigma_{m})\leq 2\psi(2c(1+c)).

Similarly as in Case 1 we obtain

(4.13)

l(\sigma_{m})\leq e^{A}d(x_{m}).

We claim that
\sigma=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}
is a cone arc.

Let
z\in\sigma
then there is
1\leq j\leq m
so that
z\in\sigma_{j}
.
Further,

\left|\log\left(\frac{d(x_{j})}{d(z)}\right)\right|\leq k(z,x_{j})\leq l_{k}(\sigma_{j})\leq A.

Therefore, we obtain,

(4.14)

d(x_{j})\leq e^{A}d(z).

From (
4.11
), (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) it follows that

l\left(\sigma[x,z]\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}l(\sigma_{j})\leq e^{A}\sum_{i=1}^{j}d(x_{j})\leq 2e^{A}d(x_{j})\leq 2e^{2A}d(z),

which implies (
4.5
).
This completes the proof.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.16
.

Let
x^{\prime}=f(x)
and
y^{\prime}=f(y)
be any two points in
G^{\prime}
. Since,
x,y\in G
there exists a
c-
cone arc
\alpha
joining
x
and
y
. Clearly,
\alpha
is a diameter
c-
John arc and by Theorem
1.14

\alpha
satisfies the
\psi-
natural condition for some increasing function
\psi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
.
By Theorem
1.14
it is enough to prove that
\alpha^{\prime}=f(\alpha)
is a diameter
c^{\prime}-
John arc and it satisfies
\psi^{\prime}-
natural condition for some increasing function
\psi^{\prime}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
.

We see from Remark
3.3
that
\alpha^{\prime}
is diameter
c^{\prime}-
John.
Before we prove that
\alpha^{\prime}
satisfies the natural condition we claim that for any connected set
A^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
with
\text{ dist}(A^{\prime}\delta G^{\prime})>0
, we have

(4.15)

\frac{\text{ diam}(A)}{\text{ dist}(A,\delta G)}\leq 6\mu^{\prime}\left(\frac{\text{ diam}(A^{\prime})}{\text{ dist}(A^{\prime},\delta G^{\prime})}\right)

where
A^{\prime}=f(A)
,
\mu^{\prime}(t)=1+\eta^{\prime}(1+t)
and
\eta^{\prime}(t)=\left(\eta^{-1}(t^{-1}\right)^{-1}
.

Let
x\in A
and
z\in\delta G
be two points such that
|x-z|\leq 2\text{ dist}\left(A,\delta G\right)
. Choose
y\in A
such that
\text{ diam}(A)\leq 3|x-y|
.
Since
f^{-1}
is
\eta^{\prime}
-quasisymmetric relative to
\delta G^{\prime}
with
\eta^{\prime}(t)=\left(\eta^{-1}(t^{-1}\right)^{-1}
we obtain

\frac{\text{ diam}(A)}{\text{ dist}(A,\delta G)}\leq 6\frac{|x-y|}{|x-z|}\leq 6\left(1+\eta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|y-z|}{|x-z|}\right)\right)\leq 6\left(1+\eta^{\prime}\left(1+\frac{\text{ diam}(A^{\prime})}{\text{ dist}(A^{\prime},\delta G^{\prime})}\right)\right),

which is the desired result.
It follows from (
4.15
) and from the fact that
f
is
(M,C)-
CQH that for
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in\alpha^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{ diam}_{k^{\prime}}(\alpha^{\prime}[u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])

\displaystyle\leq M\text{ diam}_{k}(\alpha[u,v])+C

\displaystyle\leq M\psi\left(\frac{\text{ diam}(\alpha[u,v])}{\text{ dist}(\alpha[u,v],\delta G)}\right)+C

\displaystyle\leq M\psi\left(6\mu^{\prime}\left(\frac{\text{ diam}(\alpha^{\prime}[u^{\prime},v^{\prime}])}{\text{ dist}(\alpha[u^{\prime},v^{\prime}],\delta G^{\prime})}\right)\right)+C.

The proof is complete by letting
\psi^{\prime}(t)=M\psi(6\mu^{\prime}(t))+C
.

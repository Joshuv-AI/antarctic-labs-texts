Obstructing Two-torsion in the Rational Knot Concordance Group
Jaewon Lee

Abstract
. It is well known that there are many 2-torsion elements in the classical knot concordance group. On the other hand, it is not known if there is any torsion element in the rational knot concordance group 𝒞 ℚ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}} . Cha defined the algebraic rational concordance group 𝒜 ​ 𝒞 ℚ \mathcal{AC}_{\mathbb{Q}} , an analogue of the classical algebraic concordance group, and showed that 𝒜 ​ 𝒞 ℚ ≅ ℤ ∞ ⊕ ℤ 2 ∞ ⊕ ℤ 4 ∞ \mathcal{AC}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\infty}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{\infty} . The knots that represent 2-torsions in 𝒜 ​ 𝒞 ℚ \mathcal{AC}_{\mathbb{Q}} potentially have order 2 2 in 𝒞 ℚ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}} . In this paper, we provide an obstruction for knots of order 2 2 in 𝒜 ​ 𝒞 ℚ \mathcal{AC}_{\mathbb{Q}} from being of finite order in 𝒞 ℚ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}} . Moreover, we give a family consisting of such knots that generates an infinite rank subgroup of 𝒞 ℚ \mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}} . We also note that Cha proved that in higher dimensions, the algebraic rational concordance order is the same as the rational knot concordance order. Our obstruction is based on the localized von Neumann ρ \rho -invariant.

Background

In this section, we first review the definition of von Neumann
\rho
-invariant by using
L^{2}
-signature defect and see its basic properties. Then we briefly recall some ordinary concordance invariants based on the
\rho
-invariant such as the zeroth and first order signatures. After that, we explain the localization of the Alexander module, the extended Blanchfield form, and the corresponding

Notice that the last two subsections will be the main ingredients of our construction. We claim no own result in this section. Throughout this section, every manifold is assumed to be compact and oriented, unless otherwise stated.

2.3.
Localized
\rho
-invariant

From the perspective of obstructing a knot

\rho^{(1)}(K,P)\neq 0

and check a single
\rho
-invariant. A localization technique fits well for this strategy. This subsection is based on the work by Davis
[

]

be a symmetric Laurent polynomial with rational coefficients.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
R_{p}=\left\{\frac{f}{g}\in\mathbb{Q}(t):(p,g)=1,f,g\in{\mathbb{Q}[t^{\pm 1}]}\right\}
. The
localized Alexander module at

where the
{\mathbb{Q}[t^{\pm 1}]}
-tensor product is the usual multiplication.

Definition 2.14
.

Let

be the kernel of the following map:

\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(1)}\rightarrow\frac{\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(1)}}{\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(2)}}=H_{1}(M_{K};\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}])\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}(K)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(K)\otimes R_{p}=\mathcal{A}_{p}(K).

The
localized
\rho
-invariant of

\rho_{p}^{(1)}(K)=\rho\left(M_{K},\phi_{p}:\pi_{1}(M_{K})\rightarrow\frac{\pi_{1}(M_{K})}{\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(2)}_{p}}\right).

After localization at

whose order is relatively prime with

is annihilated in
\mathcal{A}_{p}(K)
. Then we may expect some isotropic submodules to be killed. Since

on

\mathcal{B}\ell_{p}(x\otimes f(t),y\otimes g(t))=f(t)\cdot\mathcal{B}\ell(x,y)\cdot g(t^{-1}).

A knot

if

has no nontrivial isotropic submodule with respect to

.

Now

-anisotropy condition for

makes

be enough to obstruct

from being slice. Moreover,

.

[

, Theorem 4.1]

is

-anisotropic, the connected sum

-anisotropic.

The vanishing of the signature defect of the slice disk complement bounded by

is anticipated. The essence of localization and

-anisotropy is that we expect all possible Lagrangian (or generally isotropic) submodule of

to be killed in

. The key role of

-anisotropic.

Remark 3.3

Since

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
p\in{\mathbb{Q}[t^{\pm 1}]}
,
c
be a positive integer, and
\Delta_{K}
be the Alexander polynomial of
K
.

(a)

If
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
and
p(t)
are coprime, then
\rho^{(1)}_{c,p}(K)=\rho^{(0)}(K)
.

(b)

If
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})=p(t)
, then
\rho^{(1)}_{c,p}(K)=\rho^{(1)}(K)
.

Proof.

(a)

Let
f(t)
be an element of
\mathcal{A}_{c,p}(K)
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{c}(K)
is annihilated by
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
and
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
is invertible in
\mathcal{A}_{c,p}(K)
,

f(t)=f(t)\otimes 1=f(t)\Delta_{K}(t^{c})\otimes\frac{1}{\Delta_{K}(t^{c})}=0.

Thus,
\mathcal{A}_{c,p}(K)=0
, which implies that the kernel
\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(2)}_{c,p}
is
\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(1)}
. Therefore,

\rho_{c,p}^{(1)}(K)=\rho\left(M_{K},\phi_{0}:\pi_{1}(M_{K})\rightarrow\frac{\pi_{1}(M_{K})}{\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(1)}}\right)=\rho^{(0)}(K).

(b)

Let
g(t)
be an element of the kernel of
\mathcal{A}_{c}(K)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{c,p}(K)
. Then there exists
h(t)\in{\mathbb{Q}[t^{\pm 1}]}
such that

g(t)\otimes 1=g(t)h(t)\otimes\frac{1}{h(t)}=0\otimes\frac{1}{h(t)}=0,

where
(h(t),\Delta_{K}(t^{c}))=1
. Since
g(t)h(t)=0
in
\mathcal{A}_{c}(K)
,
g(t)
has a factor
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
. Thus,
g(t)=0
in
\mathcal{A}_{c}(K)
, which implies that
\mathcal{A}_{c}(K)
injects to
\mathcal{A}_{c,p}(K)
. Then
\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(2)}_{c,p}
is the same as the kernel
\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(2)}
of the quotient
\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(1)}\rightarrow\frac{\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(1)}}{\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(2)}}
. Thus,

\rho_{c,p}^{(1)}(K)=\rho(M_{K},\phi_{1}:\pi_{1}(M_{K})\rightarrow\frac{\pi_{1}(M_{K})}{\pi_{1}(M_{K})^{(2)}})=\rho^{(1)}(K).

∎

4.
Rational Slice Obstruction

In this section, we verify that our invariant defined in Section
3
gives a rational slice obstruction. To use this, we impose a rather strong condition, called
(c,p)
-anisotropy, for the localized
\mathcal{A}_{c,p}
to have no nontrivial isotropic submodule with respect to
\mathcal{B}\ell_{c,p}
. For such a knot
K
, we show that if
K
is rationally slice with complexity
c
, then
\rho_{c,p}(K)
vanishes. Moreover, as Proposition
2.16
, it can obstruct any connected sum of those knots. After providing several sufficient conditions for
(c,p)
-anisotropy, we prove Theorem
1.2
. Throughout this section,

is assume to be a Laurent polynomial with rational coefficients. We also assume that
p
be symmetric so that the rational Blanchfield form
\mathcal{B}\ell_{c}
naturally extends to
\mathcal{B}\ell_{c,p}
on the localized Alexander module.

Definition 4.1
.

A knot

is
(c,p)
-anisotropic
if
\mathcal{A}_{c,p}(K)
has no nontrivial isotropic submodule with respect to the Blanchfield form
\mathcal{B}\ell_{c,p}
.

.

Let

-anisotropic knots. If

.

Suppose that

with
(c,p)
-anisopropic

. Let
M_{i}=M_{K_{i}}
. Here we similarly go on with the proof of Proposition
3.7
by constructing a 4-manifold

bounded by

satisfying certain properties. More precisely, the resulting

is required to satisfy the condition in the Lemma

\Delta_{K}
be its Alexander polynomial. If each factor of
p
is symmetric and divides
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
with multiplicity at most
1
, then
K
is
(c,p)
-anisotropic.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{A}_{c}(K),\mathcal{B}\ell_{c})
is equivalent with
(\mathcal{A}(K_{c,1}),\mathcal{B}\ell)
, the
(c,p)
-anisotropy condition of
K
is equivalent with the
p
-anisotropy condition of
K_{c,1}
. Also note that
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})=\Delta_{K_{c,1}}(t)
. Thus, the result follows from the fact that
p
-anisotropy of
K_{c,1}
holds by the Lemma
2.17
.
∎

Then the followings are clear.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose that each factor of
p
is symmetric.
K
is
(c,p)
-anisotropic if one of the following holds:

(a)

\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
is squarefree.

(b)

\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
and
p
are coprime.

A stronger but simpler condition is irreducibility for
\Delta_{K}(t^{c})
, which follows from Proposition
4.6
(a). We say a polynomial
p(t)
is
strongly irreducible
if
p(t^{c})
is irreducible for all positive integers
c
.
p(t)
and
q(t)
are said to be
strongly coprime
if
p(t^{c})
and
q(t^{d})
are coprime for all positive integers
c
,
d
.

Proposition 4.7
.

If
\Delta_{K}
is strongly irreducible, then
K
is
(c,\Delta_{K}(t^{c}))
-anisotropic for all
c
.

Now we are ready to prove the obstructive Theorem
1.2
which is stated without
(c,p)
-anisotropy condition.

Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\{K_{i}\}
be a set of knots whose order is finite in
\mathcal{AC}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\Delta_{i}
be the Alexander polynomial of
K_{i}
. If
\Delta_{i}
’s are strongly irreducible and pairwisely strongly coprime, and
\rho^{(1)}(K_{i})\neq 0
, then
\{K_{i}\}
is linearly independent in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

Since
K_{i}
has finite order in
\mathcal{AC}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, the Levine-Tristram signature function
\sigma_{K_{i}}(\omega)=0
by
[
CO93
]
,
[
CK02
]
. Thus, by Theorem
2.8
, the zeroth order signature vanishes:

\rho^{(0)}(K_{i})=0.

Fix any index
j
, and let
p(t)=\Delta_{j}(t^{c})
. Note that
K_{i}
is
(c,p)
-anisotropic for either
i=j
or
i\neq j
by Proposition
4.6
(b) and Proposition
4.7
. By Proposition
3.4
,

\displaystyle\rho_{c,p}(K_{i})

\displaystyle=\rho^{(0)}(K_{i})=0\text{ if }i\neq j.

\displaystyle\rho_{c,p}(K_{i})

\displaystyle=\rho^{(1)}(K_{i})\neq 0\text{ if }i=j.

Given
J_{1},\cdots,J_{n}
arbitrarily chosen in
\{K_{i}\}
, suppose that
a_{1}J_{1}\#\cdots\#a_{n}J_{n}
is rationally slice with some complexity
c
. Let
p(t)=\Delta_{k}(t^{c})
for a fixed
k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Then by Theorem
4.2
, Proposition
3.7
and the above result for
\rho_{c,p}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{c,p}^{(1)}(a_{1}J_{1}\#\cdots a_{n}J_{n})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\rho_{c,p}^{(1)}(J_{1})+\cdots a_{n}\rho_{c,p}^{(1)}(J_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\neq k}a_{i}\rho^{(0)}(J_{i})+a_{k}\rho^{(1)}(J_{k})=0.

Since
\rho^{(0)}(K_{i})=0
and
\rho^{(1)}(K_{i})\neq 0
for all
i
, we have
a_{k}=0
. In this way,
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{n}=0
.
∎

On the other hand, below corollary immediately follows from Remark
4.4
in the exactly same way.

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfies all conditions in above Theorem
1.2
. Then
\{(K_{i})_{c,1}:i\in I,c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\}
is linearly independent in the classical knot concordance group
\mathcal{C}
.

We remark that Corollary
4.8
can be restated in slightly stronger way by replacing
\mathcal{C}
with the
strong rational concordance group
. The latter means the quotient of
\mathcal{C}
by strongly rationally slice knots.

6.
Remark on BPH-slice knots

In this section, we consider smooth category. A knot
K
is called
H-slice
in a closed, smooth, orientable 4-manifold
W
if
K\subset S^{3}=\partial(W-B^{4})
bounds a smoothly embedded nullhomologous disk in
W-B^{4}
.
K
is said to be
0
-positive (negative, resp)
if it is H-slice in a simply-connected positive (negative, resp) definite
W^{+}
(
W^{-}
, resp). The following facts are well known:

Theorem 6.1
.

[
CHH13
]

If
K
is 0-positive, then

(a)

the signature
\sigma(K)\leq 0
,

(b)

the Levine-Tristram signature function
\sigma_{K}(\omega)\leq 0
,

(c)

Ozsváth-Szabó’s
\tau(K)\geq 0
.

(d)

Ozsváth-Szabó’s correction term
d(S^{3}_{p/q}(K),i)\leq d(L(p,q),i)
.

If
K
is 0-negative, then the inequalities above are given in the opposite way.

A knot
K
is called
0
-bipolar
if it is both
0
-positive and
0
-negative. In such a particular case, Hom’s
\epsilon(K)=0

[
CHH13
]
. Since
d(S^{3}_{p/q}(K),i)=d(L(p,q),i)
for all coprime integers
p
and
q
,
\nu^{+}(K)=0
by
[
NW15
, Theorem 2.5]
and
[
HW16
, Proposition 2.3]
. It follows from
\nu^{+}(K)=0
that
\Upsilon_{K}(t)=0
by
[
OSS17
, Proof of Theorem 1.1]
. In summary, if
K
is
0
-bipolar, then we have (see also
[
CK21
]
):

\sigma(K)=\sigma_{K}(\omega)=\tau(K)=\epsilon(K)=\Upsilon_{K}(t)=\nu^{+}(K)=0.

On the other hand, a
0
-bipolar knot is called
biprojective H-slice (or, BPH-slice)
if definite 4-manifolds
W^{\pm}
in which
K
is H-slice are specified as
n\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
n\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
. Recall the Rasmussen’s
s
-invariant
[
Ras10
]
. Recently it has turned out that:

Theorem 6.2
.

[
MMSW23
]

If
K
is H-slice in
n\mathbb{CP}^{2}
(
n\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
, resp) for some
n
, then
s(K)\geq 0
(
\leq 0
, resp). In particular, if
K
is BPH-slice, then

s(K)=0.

By works of Donaldson
[
Don83
]
and Freedman
[
Fre82
]
, every simply-connected positive definite smooth 4-manifold is homeomorphic to
n\mathbb{CP}^{2}
while it is not known if there exists an exotic
n\mathbb{CP}^{2}
or
n\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
. Therefore, it is natural to ask the following questions:

Question 8
.

Does there exist a 0-bipolar but not BPH-slice knot?

If such a knot exists, then it implies that there exists a simply-connected closed exotic definite 4-manifold. For existence of non-simply-connected case, see
[
LLP23
,
SS23
]
. The notion of H-slice and BPH-slice has been introduced recently. H-slice knots can be used to detect an exotic indefinite 4-manifolds in gauge theoretic way
[
MMP24
]
, and BPH-slice knots are used to construct a potential exotic
n\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, including
S^{4}
for the case when
n=0

[
MP23
]
. Such homotopy
n\mathbb{CP}^{2}
’s are shown to be standard
[
Nak23
]
.

It is interesting to compare the set of rationally slice knots and the set of BPH slice knots. We make a remark that every known examples of rationally slice knots are BPH-slice. On the other hand, not all BPH-slice knots are rationally slice knots (see
[
CHH13
]
). As a corollary of Theorem
1.3
, we can even take twist knots as such examples:

Corollary 6.3
.

There are infinitely many BPH-slice twist knots that generate
\mathbb{Z}^{\infty}
subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Note that if
K
is rationally slice, then:

\sigma(K)=\sigma_{K}(\omega)=\tau(K)=\epsilon(K)=\Upsilon_{K}(t)=\nu^{+}(K)=0.

On the other hand, it is not known if the Rasmussen’s
s
-invariant vanishes for rationally slice knots. We end this article with the following question.

Question 9
.

Does there exist a rationally slice but not BPH-slice knot?

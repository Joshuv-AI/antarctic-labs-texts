Twists by Dirichlet characters and polynomial Euler products of L L -functions, II
J.Kaczorowski and A.Perelli

Abstract




In memory of Eduard Wirsing

Abstract.
In a previous paper we proved that if an
L
-function
F
from the Selberg class has degree
2
, its conductor
q_{F}
is a prime number and
F
is weakly twist-regular at all primes
p\neq q_{F}
, then
F
has a polynomial Euler product. In this paper we extend this result to
L
-functions of degree 2 with square-free conductor
q_{F}
, which are weakly twist-regular at all primes
p\nmid q_{F}
.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):
11M41

Keywords:
Twists by Dirichlet characters; Euler products; Selberg class

1.
Introduction

In
[
5
]
we proved, among other things, that if an
L
-function
F
from the Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}
has degree
2
, its conductor
q_{F}
is a prime number, and
F
is weakly twist-regular at all primes
p\neq q_{F}
, then
F
has a polynomial Euler product. In this paper we keep the notation from
[
5
]
but, for the reader’s convenience, we recall some basic definitions in Section
2
. The aim of this paper is to extend the above result as follows.

Theorem 1.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}
be of degree
2
and its conductor
q_{F}
be square-free. If
F
is weakly twist-regular at all primes
p\nmid q_{F}
, then
F
has a polynomial Euler product.

This is not a straightforward generalization; indeed, apart from the use of Theorem 2 below, a non-trivial extension of the method in
[
5
]
is necessary. On the other hand, the present method cannot settle the problem in full generality, i.e. for all integer conductors, and some new ideas will probably be needed to prove the full result.

One of the main tools in the proof is the following transformation formula for linear twists of
L
-functions from the extended Selberg class, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 2.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
with
d_{F}=2
, and let
\alpha>0
. Then for every integer
K>0
there exist polynomials
Q_{0}(s),...,Q_{K}(s)
, with
Q_{0}(s)\equiv 1
, such that

F(s,\alpha)=-i\omega_{F}^{*}(\sqrt{q_{F}}\alpha)^{2s-1+2i\theta_{F}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{K}\big(\frac{iq_{F}\alpha}{2\pi}\big)^{\nu}Q_{\nu}(s)\overline{F}\big(s+\nu+2i\theta_{F},-\frac{1}{q_{F}\alpha}\big)+H_{K}(s,\alpha).

(1.1)

Here
H_{K}(s,\alpha)
is holomorphic for
-K+\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<2
and satisfies

H_{K}(s,\alpha)\ll(|s|+1)^{2K+A}

(1.2)

with a certain constant
A=A(F,\alpha)>0
. Moreover,
\deg Q_{\nu}=2\nu
and

Q_{\nu}(s)\ll\frac{(A(|s|+1))^{2\nu}}{\nu!}\hskip 42.67912pt\text{for}\ 1\leq\nu\leq\min(|s|,K).

(1.3)

This should be compared with Theorem 1.2 in
[
4
]
. Apart from the value
2\theta_{F}
in place of
\theta_{F}
in (
1.1
), due to a slight change in the definition of
\theta_{F}
compared with
[
4
]
(see next section), the main difference is in the estimate for the size of
H_{K}(s,\alpha)
and in the range for
s
in which it holds. Precisely, Theorem 1.2 in
[
4
]
states that

H_{K}(s,\alpha)\ll(AK)^{K}\hskip 42.67912pt\text{for $-K+\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<2$, $|s|\leq 2K$}.

(1.4)

In the proof of Theorem 1, we consider shifts of
L
-functions of the form
F(s+i\tau)
with
\tau\to\infty
and hence reasonable control upon the size of
H_{K}(s,\alpha)
is needed in half-planes rather than in discs. If fact, for
\tau\to\infty
, (
1.4
) gives an estimate which is far too weak for the proof of Theorem 1. In contrast, (
1.2
) secures a polynomial growth which is exactly what is needed. In principle, the main structure of the proof of Theorem 2 is the same as that of Theorem 1.2 in
[
4
]
. Nevertheless, the new situation where
s
is in a half-plane forces the introduction of significant technical changes in the proof.

2.
Definitions

Throughout the paper we write
s=\sigma+it
,
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
and
\overline{f}(s)
for
\overline{f(\overline{s})}
. The extended Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
consists of non-identically vanishing Dirichlet series

F(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}

absolutely convergent for
\sigma>1
, such that
(s-1)^{m}F(s)
is entire of finite order for some integer
m\geq 0
, and satisfying a functional equation of type

F(s)\gamma(s)=\omega\overline{\gamma}(1-s)\overline{F}(1-s),

where
|\omega|=1
and the
\gamma
-factor

\gamma(s)=Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})

has
Q>0
,
r\geq 0
,
\lambda_{j}>0
and
\Re(\mu_{j})\geq 0
. Note that the conjugate function
\overline{F}
has conjugated coefficients
\overline{a(n)}
. The Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}
is the subclass of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
of the functions satisfying the Ramanujan conjecture
a(n)\ll n^{\epsilon}
and with an Euler product of the form

F(s)=\prod_{p}F_{p}(s),\quad\text{where}\quad F_{p}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a(p^{k})}{p^{ks}}

satisfies

\log F_{p}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(p^{k})}{p^{ks}}\quad\text{with $b(p^{k})\ll p^{\vartheta k}$ for a certain $\vartheta<1/\penalty 2$.}

(2.1)

Note that the series in (
2.1
) is absolutely convergent for
\sigma>\vartheta
and hence

F_{p}(s)
is holomorphic and bounded away from 0 for
\sigma>\vartheta^{\prime}
for some
\vartheta^{\prime}<1/2
.

(2.2)

We say that
F\in{\mathcal{S}}
has a polynomial Euler product if for every prime
p
there exist
\partial_{p}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha_{j,p}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

F_{p}(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{\partial_{p}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{j,p}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

In such a case, it follows from the Ramanujan conjecture that
|\alpha_{j,p}|\leq 1
; see p.448-449 of
[
4
]
.

The twist by a Dirichlet character
\chi
(mod
q
) is defined for
\sigma>1
as

F^{\chi}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}.

We say that
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
is weakly twist-regular at
p
if for every primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
(mod
p^{f}
) with
1\leq f\leq m_{q_{F}}(p)
, where
m_{q_{F}}(p)
is the order of
p
(mod
q_{F}
), the twist
F^{\chi}
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
and has the same degree as
F
. Moreover, the linear twist of
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
is defined for
\sigma>1
as

F(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}e(-n\alpha)

with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Degree
d_{F}
, conductor
q_{F}
, root number
\omega_{F}
and
\xi
-invariant
\xi_{F}
of
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
are defined by

\begin{split}d_{F}=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}&,\qquad q_{F}=(2\pi)^{d}Q^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{2\lambda_{j}},\\
\omega_{F}=\omega\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{-2i\Im(\mu_{j})}&,\qquad\xi_{F}=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\mu_{j}-1/2)=\eta_{F}+id_{F}\theta_{F}\end{split}

with
\eta_{F},\theta_{F}\in\mathbb{R}
. We also write

\omega_{F}^{*}=\omega_{F}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(\eta_{F}+1)}\big(\frac{q_{F}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\big)^{i\theta_{F}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{F}=\max_{j=1,\dots,r}\big|\frac{\Im(\mu_{j})}{\lambda_{j}}\big|,

while
m_{F}
denotes the order of the pole of
F
at
s=1
. In
\omega_{F}^{*}
, and in other definitions below, we changed
\theta_{F}
to
2\theta_{F}
compared to the corresponding definitions in
[
4
]
. This is due to the above slightly different definition of the
\xi
-invariant and the fact that we are considering functions of degree
d_{F}=2
. The
H
-invariants of
F
are defined as

H_{F}(n)=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{B_{n}(\mu_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{n-1}}\hskip 42.67912ptn=0,1,...

where
B_{n}(x)
is the
n
-th Bernoulli polynomial. Note that
H_{F}(0)=d_{F}
is the degree and

H_{F}(1)
is the
\xi
-invariant. We refer to our survey papers
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
for further definitions, examples and the basic theory of the Selberg class.

As in
[
4
]
, for
\nu,\mu=1,2,...
we define the polynomials
R_{\nu}(s)=R_{\nu,F}(s)
and
V_{\mu}(s)=V_{\mu,F}(s)
as

\begin{split}R_{\nu}(s)&=B_{\nu+1}(1-2s-2i\theta_{F})+B_{\nu+1}(1)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\nu+1}{\nu+1\choose k}\big((-1)^{\nu}H_{F}(k)s^{\nu+1-k}-\overline{H_{F}(k)}(1-s)^{\nu+1-k}\big)\end{split}

(2.3)

and

V_{\mu}(s)=(-1)^{\mu}\sum_{m=1}^{\mu}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu_{1}\geq 1,...,\nu_{m}\geq 1\\
\nu_{1}+...+\nu_{m}=\mu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{R_{\nu_{j}}(s)}{\nu_{j}(\nu_{j}+1)},

(2.4)

respectively. We also define
Q_{0}(s)\equiv 1
and, for
\nu=1,2,...
, the polynomials
Q_{\nu}(s)
appearing in Theorem 2 by means of the formula

\exp\big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}R_{\nu}(s)}{\nu(\nu+1)}\frac{1}{(w+2s-1+2i\theta_{F})^{\nu}}\big)\approx 1+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q_{\nu}(s)}{(w-1)\cdots(w-\nu)},

and the coefficients
C_{\mu,\ell}
,
\ell\geq\mu\geq 1
, by

\frac{1}{w^{\mu}}\approx\sum_{\ell=\mu}^{\infty}\frac{C_{\mu,\ell}}{(w-1)\cdots(w-\ell)}.

(2.5)

Moreover, we define the coefficients
A_{\mu,\nu}(s)
(
\nu\geq\mu\geq 1
) by

\frac{1}{(w+2s-1+2i\theta_{F})^{\mu}}\approx\sum_{\nu=\mu}^{\infty}\frac{A_{\mu,\nu}(s)}{(w-1)\cdots(w-\nu)}.

(2.6)

Here
\approx
means asymptotic expansion as
w\to\infty
, i.e. cutting the sum on the left hand side at
\nu=N
introduces an error of size
O_{s}(1/|w|^{N+1})
. We refer to Lemmas
8
and
9
below for a more precise meaning of (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).

We write
w=u+iv
and, for a given
s
, define the contour
{\mathcal{L}}(s)
as follows:

{\mathcal{L}}(s)={\mathcal{L}}_{-\infty}(s)\cup{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}(s)

where

{\mathcal{L}}_{-\infty}(s)=(-\sigma+c_{0}-i\infty,-\sigma+c_{0}+it_{0}]\cup[-\sigma+c_{0}+it_{0},-\sigma-c_{0}+it_{0}]

{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}(s)=[-\sigma-c_{0}+it_{0},-\sigma-c_{0}+i\infty).

Here
t_{0}=t_{0}(s)=c_{1}(|s|+1)^{2}
and
c_{0},c_{1}>0
are sufficiently large constants depending on
F
to be chosen later on. Observe a significant difference in the present choice of
t_{0}
compared to the analogous choice in
[
4
]
; here
t_{0}
is much larger, and this is important in the proof of Theorem 2. Moreover, we denote by
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)
the half-line
1-2s-2i\theta_{F}-{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}(s)
taken with the positive orientation, hence

{\mathcal{L}}_{-\infty}^{*}(s)=(1-\sigma+c_{0}-i\infty,1-\sigma+c_{0}-it_{0}^{*}]

with
t_{0}^{*}=t_{0}^{*}(s)=t_{0}+2t+2\theta_{F}
. Further, we let

{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}^{*}(s)=[1-\sigma+c_{0}-it_{0}^{*},N+1]\cup[N+1,N+1+i\infty),

where the positive integer
N
will be chosen later on (see (
5.10
) below), and write

{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(s)={\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)\cup{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}^{*}(s).

We shall also use the notation

G(s,w)=\frac{(2\pi)^{1-r}}{\Gamma(1-w)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}(1-s-w)+\bar{\mu}_{j})\Gamma(1-\lambda_{j}(s+w)-\mu_{j})

and

S(s,w)=\frac{2^{r-1}}{\sin\pi w}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sin\big(\pi(\lambda_{j}(s+w)+\mu_{j}\big).

Finally,
A,B,c,c^{\prime},...
will denote positive constants, possibly depending on
F
(also via a dependence on the above constants
c_{0},c_{1}
), not necessarily the same at each occurrence. The constants in the
\ll
- and
O
-symbols may also depend on
F(s)
(again, also via
c_{0},c_{1}
).

3.
Lemmas

Lemma 1
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
q_{F}
be square-free. Then for
\sigma>1
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q_{F}\\
(a,q_{F})=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}e(-\frac{a}{q_{F}}n)=F(s)\sum_{d|q_{F}}d\mu(\frac{q_{F}}{d})\prod_{p|d}\left(1-F_{p}(s)^{-1}\right).

(3.1)

Proof.
For simplicity we write
q
in place of
q_{F}
. Recall the well-known Kluyver’s formula for the Ramanujan sum

c_{q}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}e(an/\penalty q)=\sum_{d|(q,n)}\mu(\frac{q}{d})d.

Since
c_{q}(n)\in\mathbb{R}
, the left hand side of (
3.1
) equals

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}c_{q}(n)=\sum_{d|q}\mu(\frac{q}{d})d^{1-s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(dn)}{n^{s}}.

(3.2)

Now we compute the inner sum for a generic
d|q
,
d=p_{1}\ldots p_{k}
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(dn)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu_{1}=0}^{\infty}\ldots\sum_{\nu_{k}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
p_{j}^{\nu_{j}}||n\\
(1\leq j\leq k)\end{subarray}}\frac{a(dn)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu_{1}=0}^{\infty}\ldots\sum_{\nu_{k}=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{a(p_{j}^{\nu_{j}+1})}{p_{j}^{\nu_{j}s}}\right)\sum_{(n,p_{1}\ldots p_{k})=1}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu_{1}=0}^{\infty}\ldots\sum_{\nu_{k}=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{a(p_{j}^{\nu_{j}+1})}{p_{j}^{\nu_{j}s}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}F_{p_{j}}(s)^{-1}\right)F(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(s)d^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(1-F_{p_{j}}(s)^{-1}\right).

Inserting this into (
3.2
) we obtain (
3.1
), and the proof is complete. ∎

For
F
in the Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}
we denote by
N_{F}(\sigma,T)
the number of non-trivial zeros
\beta+i\gamma
of
F
in the rectangle
\beta>\sigma
,
|\gamma|\leq T
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}
with

. Then for every

and any fixed

we have

N_{F}(\sigma,T)\ll T^{3/2-\sigma+\epsilon}.

Proof.

]
. ∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
with
d=2
. Then there exists a positive constant
T_{0}=T_{0}(F)
such that

F(s)\asymp\left(\frac{q_{F}}{(2\pi e)^{2}}\right)^{|\sigma|}|s|^{2|\sigma|+1}

uniformly for
|t|\geq T_{0}
and
\sigma\leq-1
.

Proof.
This is a refined version of Lemma 2.1 in
[
4
]
. The proof follows from the functional equation of
F
and the Stirling formula; see also Lemma 3 in
[
5
]
. ∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
p
be a prime number and
\sigma>1
. Then there exist coefficients
c(\chi,p)
, where
\chi
runs over the Dirichlet characters
\chi

(mod
p
)
, such that

F(s,1/p)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(\bmod p)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}c(\chi,p)F^{\chi}(s)+\left(1-\frac{p}{p-1}F_{p}(s)^{-1}\right)F(s).

Proof.
See equation (2.6) of
[
4
]
, observing that
F(s,1/p)=F(s,-(p-1)/p)
. ∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
,
q
be a positive integer and let
\theta<1/\penalty 2
be fixed. Moreover, for every prime
p|q
, let
\varepsilon_{p}
be a complex number with
|\epsilon_{p}|=1
. Then there exist two positive constants
a
and
b
(depending only on
\theta
) and a sequence of positive numbers
\tau_{k}\to\infty
,
k\geq 1
, satisfying the following two conditions

i) for every prime
p|q
we have
|p^{-i\tau_{k}}-\varepsilon_{p}|<1/\penalty k
,

ii)
|F(\sigma+it)|\geq\tau_{k}^{-b}
uniformly for
-1\leq\sigma\leq\theta
and
|t-\tau_{k}|\leq\tau_{k}^{a}.

Proof.
The numbers
\log p
,
p|q
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Thus by a well known version of the classical Kronecker approximation theorem, for every
k\geq 1
there exists a relatively dense set of numbers
\tau
such that

|p^{-i\tau}-\varepsilon_{p}|<\frac{1}{k}\quad\text{for all}\ p|q.

Thus using Lemma
2
we see that there exists a solution
\tau_{k}\geq 2\max(k,T_{0}(F))
, where
T_{0}(F)
is the constant appearing in Lemma
3
, of this system of inequalities such that
F(\sigma+it)\neq 0
for

-1\leq\sigma<(1+2\theta)/\penalty 4\ \quad\text{ and}\quad|t-\tau_{k}|\leq\tau_{k}^{a},

where
a
is any fixed positive number less than
(1-2\theta)/\penalty 4
. For
s
in such a range we use the well known formula

-\frac{F^{\prime}}{F}(s)=\sum_{|\gamma-t|<1}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+O(\log\tau_{k}),\quad\text{therefore}\quad-\frac{F^{\prime}}{F}(s)=O(\log\tau_{k}).

Hence, recalling Lemma
3
, we have

\log|F(\sigma+it)|=\log|F(-1+it)|+\Re\int_{-1}^{\sigma}\frac{F^{\prime}}{F}(u+it)\,du\geq-b\log\tau_{k}

for
-1\leq\sigma\leq\theta
and
|t-\tau_{k}|\leq\tau_{k}^{a}
, and the lemma follows. ∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
R_{\nu}(s)
be as in (

Let

V_{\mu,N}(s)=(-1)^{\mu}\sum_{m=1}^{\mu}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\nu_{1}\leq N,...,1\leq\nu_{m}\leq N\\
\nu_{1}+...+\nu_{m}=\mu\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{R_{\nu_{j}}(s)}{\nu_{j}(\nu_{j}+1)}.

(3.3)

Recalling definition (

for

.

Lemma 7
.

For
s\in\mathbb{C}
and
1\leq\mu\leq c(|s|+1)
we have

V_{\mu,N}(s)\ll\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{2\mu}}{(\mu-1)!},

where
c
and
c^{\prime}
are as in Lemma
6
. Moreover, for all
\mu\geq 1

V_{\mu,N}(s)\ll(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{2\mu}.

Proof.
Thanks to Lemma
6
, for every
\mu\geq 1
we have

\begin{split}|V_{\mu,N}(s)|&\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\mu}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\nu_{1}+...+\nu_{m}=\mu}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{\nu_{j}+1}}{\nu_{j}(\nu_{j}+1)}\\
&\ll(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{\mu}\sum_{m=1}^{\mu}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{m}}{m!}\ll(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{2\mu}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Moreover, recalling that
c^{\prime}\geq c+1
, for
1\leq\mu\leq c(|s|+1)
we have

\sum_{m=1}^{\mu}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{m}}{m!}\leq\mu\max_{1\leq m\leq\mu}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{m}}{m!}=\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{\mu}}{(\mu-1)!},

and the lemma follows from this and (
3.4
). ∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{C}
and
1\leq\mu\leq\nu\leq N
be integers and let

A_{\mu,\nu}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{\nu-\mu}{-\mu\choose k}C_{\mu+k,\nu}(2s-1+2i\theta_{F})^{k}.

Then for
w\in{\mathcal{L}}_{-\infty}^{*}(s)
and
1\leq N\leq|\sigma|+c
we have

\frac{1}{(w+2s-1+2i\theta_{F})^{\mu}}=\sum_{\nu=\mu}^{N}\frac{A_{\mu,\nu}(s)}{(w-1)\cdots(w-\nu)}+O\big(\frac{4^{N}N!|2s-1+2i\theta_{F}|^{N-\mu+1}}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|}\big),

where the constant in the
O
-symbol may depend on
c
.

Proof.
We follow the proof of Lemma 3.14 in
[
4
]
, assuming that the constant
c_{1}
in the definition of
{\mathcal{L}}_{-\infty}^{*}(s)
in Section 2 is sufficiently large and estimating in a different way the error term. Given an integer
P\geq 0
, for

Therefore, from (
5.3
), (
5.5
) and recalling the definition of
q_{F}
and
\omega^{*}_{F}
, for

|\sigma|+c\leq N\leq|\sigma|+c+1

(5.7)

we have

\begin{split}{\mathcal{J}}_{X}(s,\alpha)&=\omega^{*}_{F}\big(\frac{q_{F}}{4\pi^{2}}\big)^{1/2-s-i\theta_{F}}e(-\frac{1}{2}(s+i\theta_{F}))\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}(s)}\overline{F}(1-s-w)\ \times\\
&\qquad\times\Gamma(1-2s-w-2i\theta_{F})\exp\big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{R_{\nu}(s)}{\nu(\nu+1)}\frac{1}{w^{\nu}}\big)\big(\frac{q_{F}z_{X}}{4\pi^{2}}\big)^{-w}\d{w}+O\big(A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2|\sigma|+c^{\prime}}\big).\end{split}

Hence, by the substitution
1-2s-w-2i\theta_{F}\to w
in the above integral and recalling the definition of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)
in Section 2 we obtain

\begin{split}{\mathcal{J}}_{X}&(s,\alpha)=-i\omega^{*}_{F}\big(\sqrt{q_{F}}\alpha-i\frac{\sqrt{q_{F}}}{2\pi X}\big)^{2s-1+2i\theta_{F}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)}\bar{F}(s+w+2i\theta_{F})\Gamma(w)\\
&\times\exp\big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}R_{\nu}(s)}{\nu(\nu+1)}\frac{1}{(w+2s-1+2i\theta_{F})^{\nu}}\big)\big(\frac{q_{F}z_{X}}{4\pi^{2}}\big)^{w}\d{w}+O\big(A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2|\sigma|+c^{\prime}})\big),\end{split}

(5.8)

uniformly as
X\to\infty
.

Writing again
\eta=2s-1+2i\theta_{F}
, from the power series expansion of the exponential function and recalling (
3.3
) we have

\begin{split}\exp\big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}R_{\nu}(s)}{\nu(\nu+1)}\frac{1}{(w+\eta)^{\nu}}\big)&=1+\sum_{\mu=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{\mu,N}(s)}{(w+\eta)^{\mu}}\\
&=1+\sum_{\mu=1}^{N}\frac{V_{\mu}(s)}{(w+\eta)^{\mu}}+O(\sum_{\mu=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{|V_{\mu,N}(s)|}{|w+\eta|^{\mu}}).\end{split}

(5.9)

Since for
w\in{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)
we have
|w+\eta|\geq 2(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{2}
, where
c^{\prime}
denotes the constant in Lemma
7
, using such a lemma we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{\mu=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{|V_{\mu,N}(s)|}{|w+\eta|^{\mu}})&\ll\sum_{\mu=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{2\mu}}{|w+\eta|^{\mu}}\leq\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{2(N+1)}}{|w+\eta|^{N+1}}\sum_{\mu=N+1}^{\infty}2^{-\mu}\\
&\ll A^{|\sigma|}\frac{(|s|+1)^{2N+1}}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|}.\end{split}

Thus using (
5.9
) and Lemmas
7
and
9

\begin{split}&\exp\big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}R_{\nu}(s)}{\nu(\nu+1)}\frac{1}{(w+\eta)^{\nu}}\big)=1+\sum_{\mu=1}^{N}\frac{V_{\mu}(s)}{(w+\eta)^{\mu}}+O(A^{|\sigma|}\frac{(|s|+1)^{2N+1}}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|})\\
&=1+\sum_{\mu=1}^{N}V_{\mu}(s)\sum_{\nu=\mu}^{N}\frac{A_{\mu,\nu}(s)}{(w-1)\cdots(w-\nu)}+O\big(\frac{4^{N}N!}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|}\sum_{\mu=1}^{N}|V_{\mu}(s)||\eta|^{N-\mu+1}\big)\\
&\hskip 182.1207pt+O(A^{|\sigma|}\frac{(|s|+1)^{2N+1}}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|})\\
&=\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}\frac{Q_{\nu}(s)}{(w-1)\cdots(w-\nu)}+O\big(\frac{4^{N}N!(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{N}}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|}\sum_{\mu=1}^{N}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{\mu+1}}{(\mu-1)!}\big)\\
&\hskip 130.08621pt\ +O(A^{|\sigma|}\frac{(|s|+1)^{2N+1}}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|})\\
&=\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}\frac{Q_{\nu}(s)}{(w-1)\cdots(w-\nu)}+O(A^{|\sigma|}\frac{(|s|+1)^{2N+1}}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|})\end{split}

Now we replace the term
\exp(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}...)
in (
5.8
) by the main term of the above formula. This causes a further error of size

\begin{split}&\ll A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2N+1}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)}\frac{|\Gamma(w)|}{|w(w-1)\cdots(w-N)|}e^{\pi|v|/2}|\d{w}|\\
&=A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2N+1}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)}\frac{|\Gamma(w-N)|e^{\pi|v|/2}}{|w|}|\d{w}|.\end{split}

Moreover, we also want
N
such that
\Re(w-N)\leq 0
for
w\in{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)
, i.e. we choose

N=[-\sigma]+k

(5.10)

with a sufficiently large positive integer
k
satisfying (
5.7
). With such a choice of
N
we have
|\Gamma(w-N)|\ll e^{-\pi|v|/2}/|v|^{1/2}
, hence the integral is
\ll 1
. Therefore (
5.8
) becomes, uniformly as
X\to\infty
,

\begin{split}{\mathcal{J}}_{X}(s,\alpha)&=-i\omega^{*}_{F}\big(\sqrt{q_{F}}\alpha-i\frac{\sqrt{q_{F}}}{2\pi X}\big)^{2s-1+2i\theta_{F}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}Q_{\nu}(s)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{-\infty}(s)}\overline{F}(s+w+2i\theta_{F})\ \times\\
&\qquad\times\Gamma(w-\nu)\big(\frac{q_{F}z_{X}}{4\pi^{2}}\big)^{w}\d{w}+O\big(A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2|\sigma|+B}),\end{split}

(5.11)

which is the analog of (3.35) in
[
4
]
.

Replacing the path of integration in (
5.11
) by the whole path
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(s)
causes an error which, since
\nu\leq N
, by Lemma
10
is of size

\ll A^{|\sigma|}\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}\frac{(|s|+1)^{2\nu}}{\nu!}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}^{*}(s)}|F(s+w+2i\theta_{F})\Gamma(w-\nu)\big(\frac{q_{F}z_{X}}{4\pi^{2}}\big)^{w}||\d{w}|.

Let now
w\in{\mathcal{L}}^{*}_{\infty}(s)
. Clearly,
F(s+w+2i\theta_{F})\ll 1
. Moreover, for
0\leq\nu\leq N
we have
-c\leq\Re(w-\nu)\leq N+1-\nu
and hence for
v\neq 0

v\arg(w-\nu)=v\arg(iv)+\arg\left(1+i\frac{\nu-u}{v}\right)=\frac{\pi}{2}|v|+O(|\sigma|+1).

Observing that this estimate holds for
v=0
as well, by Stirling’s formula we get

\log|\Gamma(w-\nu)|=(u-\nu-1/2)\log|w-\nu|-\frac{\pi}{2}|v|+O(|\sigma|+1).

Consequently

\Gamma(w-\nu)\ll A^{|\sigma|}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|v|}(|s|+|v|+1)^{N-\nu+1/2}.

Further,
|z_{X}^{w}|\ll A^{|\sigma|}e^{-v\arg z_{X}}
, thus the above mentioned error is (with a suitable
c^{\prime\prime}>0
)

\ll A^{|\sigma|}\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}\frac{(|s|+1)^{2\nu}}{\nu!}\int_{-t_{0}^{*}(s)}^{\infty}(|s|+|v|+1)^{|\sigma|-\nu+c^{\prime\prime}}e^{-\pi|v|/2-v\arg{z_{X}}}\d{v}\ll A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2|\sigma|+B}.

We also have by Cauchy’s theorem that for
0\leq\nu\leq N

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(s)}\overline{F}(s+w+2i\theta_{F})\Gamma(w-\nu)\big(\frac{q_{F}z_{X}}{4\pi^{2}}\big)^{w}\d{w}\\
&\hskip-14.22636pt=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\sigma|+\nu+2-i\infty}^{|\sigma|+\nu+2+i\infty}\overline{F}(s+w+2i\theta_{F})\Gamma(w-\nu)\big(\frac{q_{F}z_{X}}{4\pi^{2}}\big)^{w}\d{w}\\
&\hskip-14.22636pt=\big(\frac{q_{F}}{4\pi^{2}X}+i\frac{q_{F}\alpha}{2\pi}\big)^{\nu}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{a(n)}}{n^{s+\nu+2i\theta_{F}}}\exp(-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{q_{F}z_{X}}n)\end{split}

since the poles of the integrand lie to the left of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(s)
.
Consequently, (
5.11
) becomes

\begin{split}{\mathcal{J}}_{X}(s,\alpha)&=-i\omega^{*}_{F}\big(\sqrt{q_{F}}\alpha-i\frac{\sqrt{q_{F}}}{2\pi X}\big)^{2s-1+2i\theta_{F}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{N}\big(\frac{q_{F}}{4\pi^{2}X}+i\frac{q_{F}\alpha}{2\pi}\big)^{\nu}Q_{\nu}(s)\\
&\hskip 22.76228pt\times\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{a(n)}}{n^{s+\nu+2i\theta_{F}}}\exp(-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{q_{F}z_{X}}n)+O(A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2|\sigma|+B}),\end{split}

(5.12)

uniformly as
X\to\infty
. Note that, since

-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{q_{F}z_{X}}=\frac{2\pi i}{q_{F}\alpha}-\frac{1}{q_{F}\alpha^{2}}\frac{1}{X+O(1)},

the series in (
5.12
) is absolutely convergent for all
s
, for every
\nu
.

As on p.472 of
[
4
]
, the final step is to make the range of summation of
\nu
in (
5.12
) independent of
\sigma
(recall that
N
depends on
\sigma
, see (
5.10
)). Let
K>0
be a large integer and
\sigma>-K+1/2
. Depending on the relative sizes of
N
and
K
, we add to or withdraw from (
5.12
) the terms with
\nu
between
N+1
and
K
or between
K+1
and
N
, respectively.
In both cases we have that
\sigma+\nu>3/2
for such
\nu
’s (call them
\nu\in{\mathcal{X}}
), hence from Lemma

10
we deduce that

\begin{split}-i\omega^{*}_{F}&\big(\sqrt{q_{F}}\alpha-i\frac{\sqrt{q_{F}}}{2\pi X}\big)^{2s-1+2i\theta_{F}}\sum_{\nu\in{\mathcal{X}}}\big(\frac{q_{F}}{4\pi^{2}X}+i\frac{q_{F}\alpha}{2\pi}\big)^{\nu}Q_{\nu}(s)\times\\
&\times\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{a(n)}}{n^{s+\nu+2i\theta_{F}}}\exp(-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{q_{F}z_{X}}n)\ll A^{|\sigma|}\sum_{\nu\in{\mathcal{X}}}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{2\nu}}{\nu!}\end{split}

(5.13)

uniformly in
X
. If
N<K
this is

\ll A^{|\sigma|}(|s|+1)^{2K}\sum_{\nu\geq N+1}\frac{c^{\prime 2\nu}}{\nu!}\ll\frac{A^{|\sigma|}c^{\prime 2N}}{(N+1)!}(|s|+1)^{2K}\sum_{\nu\geq 0}\frac{c^{\prime 2\nu}}{\nu!}\ll(|s|+1)^{2K},

while if
N>K
we have

A^{|\sigma|}\sum_{\nu\in{\mathcal{X}}}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1))^{2\nu}}{\nu!}\ll A^{|\sigma|}\frac{(c^{\prime}(|s|+1)^{2N}}{(N-1)!}\ll(|s|+1)^{2N}\ll(|s|+1)^{2K+A}.

Thus, in view of (
5.2
),(
5.3
),(
5.12
) and (
5.13
), for
-K+1/2<\sigma<2
we have

\begin{split}F_{X}(s,\alpha)=&-i\omega^{*}_{F}\big(\sqrt{q_{F}}\alpha-i\frac{\sqrt{q_{F}}}{2\pi X}\big)^{2s-1+2i\theta_{F}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{K}\big(\frac{q_{F}}{4\pi^{2}X}+i\frac{q_{F}\alpha}{2\pi}\big)^{\nu}\\
&\times Q_{\nu}(s)F^{*}_{X}(s+\nu+2i\theta_{F},\alpha)+H_{X}(s,\alpha),\end{split}

(5.14)

where

F^{*}_{X}(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{a(n)}}{n^{s}}\exp(-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{q_{F}z_{X}}n)

and

H_{X}(s,\alpha)\ll(|s|+1)^{2K+A}

uniformly as
X\to\infty
. Moreover, since
F_{X}(s,\alpha)
,
F^{*}_{X}(s,\alpha)
and
Q_{\nu}(s)
are entire functions,
H_{X}(s,\alpha)
is also entire. Further, from (
5.14
) we have that for
1<\sigma<2

\lim_{X\to\infty}H_{X}(s,\alpha)=H(s,\alpha)

exists and is holomorphic since this is clearly true for
F_{X}(s,\alpha)
and
F^{*}_{X}(s,\alpha)
. Thanks to (
5.14
), for
1<\sigma<2

we also have that

H(s,\alpha)=F(s,\alpha)+i\omega^{*}_{F}\big(\sqrt{q_{F}}\alpha\big)^{2s-1+2i\theta_{F}}\sum_{\nu=0}^{K}\big(i\frac{q_{F}\alpha}{2\pi}\big)^{\nu}Q_{\nu}(s)\bar{F}(s+\nu+2i\theta_{F},-\frac{1}{q_{F}\alpha}).

(5.15)

Hence by Vitali’s convergence theorem the limit function
H(s,\alpha)
exists and is holomorphic for
-K+1/2<\sigma<2
, and satisfies

H(s,\alpha)\ll(|s|+1)^{2K+A}.

This provides analytic continuation and bounds for the right-hand side of (
5.15
), and Theorem 2 follows.

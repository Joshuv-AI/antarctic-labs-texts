A Mean Field Game between Informed Traders and a Broker
Philippe Bergault, Leandro Sánchez-Betancourt

Abstract
We find closed-form solutions to the stochastic game between a broker and a mean-field of informed traders. In the finite player game, the informed traders observe a common signal and a private signal. The broker, on the other hand, observes the trading speed of each of his clients and provides liquidity to the informed traders. Each player in the game optimises wealth adjusted by inventory penalties. In the mean field version of the game, using a Gâteaux derivative approach, we characterise the solution to the game with a system of forward-backward stochastic differential equations that we solve explicitly. We find that the optimal trading strategy of the broker is linear on his own inventory, on the average inventory among informed traders, and on the common signal or the average trading speed of the informed traders. The Nash equilibrium we find helps informed traders decide how to use private information, and helps brokers decide how much of the order flow they should externalise or internalise when facing a large number of clients. Keywords: market making, algorithmic trading, externalisation, internalisation, mean field games.

Introduction

Liquidity provision plays a key role in financial markets. A large portion of the liquidity provision activity happens in over-the-counter (OTC) markets where broker-client relationships are ubiquitous. Brokers face an important trading problem when deciding how to face the order flow from informed traders. These problems are challenging because one is typically interested in the Nash equilibrium of a stochastic game.

The study of externalisation-internalisation strategies is an active area of research. Externalisation refers to the act of hedging or unwinding a position sent by a client. On the other hand, internalisation refers to the warehousing of risk by the broker, on the hope that either prices move favourably to the broker or that other trades arrive in the opposite direction. This paper delves into the intricate strategy employed by a broker who not only provides liquidity to a large number of informed traders, but also engages in liquidity-taking transactions in a lit market. In our model, both the broker and the informed traders aim to maximise their expected wealth while strategically managing inventory holdings. The broker employs an inventory penalty to safeguard his strategy against inventory risks (especially toxic inventory). Simultaneously, the informed trader uses the inventory penalty to control her exposure to inventory risks stemming from speculative trades based on common and private signals. The problem is modelled as a linear-quadratic major-minor mean field game that we can solve explicitly using a Gâteaux derivative approach. The broker’s strategy (i) determines the optimal externalisation of the flow from informed traders and (ii) guides the interactions with the lit market trading for hedging and speculative purposes. The derived closed-form strategy of the broker involves a linear combination of his inventory, the average informed trader’s inventory, and the common signal. For the representative informed trader, it also involves her private signal as well as her own inventory.

2
The game with
\displaystyle N
informed traders

2.1
Framework

We consider a trading horizon
\displaystyle T>0
and a probability space
\displaystyle\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\big)
under which all the stochastic processes are defined. We set some positive integer
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}
, corresponding to the number of informed traders acting on a market consisting of a single asset whose price process is denoted by
\displaystyle S.
This quantity can be thought as the mid-price of the asset.

We introduce
\displaystyle N+2
independent standard Brownian motions
\displaystyle W^{S},W^{\alpha},W^{1},\ldots,W^{N}
that we employ in the equations below. Under the probability
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
, the price process
\displaystyle(S_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
follows

\mathrm{d}S_{t}=\sigma^{S}\mathrm{d}W^{S}_{t},

where
\displaystyle\sigma^{S}>0
.

Each trader observes a common signal and a private signal. The common or fundamental signal
\displaystyle(\alpha_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfies the following stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\mathrm{d}\alpha_{t}=-k^{\alpha}\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t},

with
\displaystyle k^{\alpha},\sigma^{\alpha}>0
.
On the other hand, the private signal of each trader is not shared, that is, the private signal of one trader is hidden to every other trader. For trader
\displaystyle n\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, we denote their signal by
\displaystyle(\alpha^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, which follows the SDE

\mathrm{d}\alpha^{n}_{t}=-k^{n}\alpha^{n}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{n}\mathrm{d}W^{n}_{t},

with
\displaystyle k^{n},\sigma^{n}
two positive random variables, independent from everything else, with the same joint distribution
\displaystyle\zeta
for all
\displaystyle n
and such that

\mathbb{E}[k^{n}]=\bar{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}[\sigma^{n}]=\bar{\sigma},

where
\displaystyle\bar{k},\bar{\sigma}>0
.

We denote by
\displaystyle(Q^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
\displaystyle(X^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the inventory process and cash process of the
\displaystyle n-
th trader, respectively. She trades with the broker at rate
\displaystyle(\nu^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
(she buys when
\displaystyle\nu^{n}_{t}>0
and sells when
\displaystyle\nu^{n}_{t}<0
). Therefore, her inventory has dynamics

\mathrm{d}Q^{n}_{t}=\nu^{n}_{t}\mathrm{d}t,

and her cash process has dynamics

\mathrm{d}X^{n}_{t}=-\nu^{n}_{t}\left(S_{t}+\eta^{I}\nu^{n}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t,

where
\displaystyle\eta^{I}>0
is a transaction cost charged by the broker to the traders.

We denote by
\displaystyle(Q^{B}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and
\displaystyle(X^{B}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the inventory and cash process of the broker, respectively. The broker receives the order flow from the traders and trades in a lit market at rate
\displaystyle(N\nu^{B}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.

and his cash process has dynamics

\mathrm{d}X^{B}_{t}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\nu^{n}_{t}\left(S_{t}+\eta^{I}\nu^{n}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-N\nu^{B}_{t}\left(S_{t}+\eta^{B}\nu^{B}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t,

where
\displaystyle\eta^{B}>0
corresponds to the execution costs in the lit market.

For each
\displaystyle n\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
generated by
\displaystyle S,\alpha,\alpha^{n},Q^{n},\nu^{B},k^{n},\sigma^{n}

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}^{B}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}

2.2
The problem of the informed traders

Let
\displaystyle b>0
. For
\displaystyle n\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, let us introduce the probability measure
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{n,\nu^{B}}
given by

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{n,\nu^{B}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\right|_{\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t}}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{n}_{u}+\alpha_{u}}{\sigma^{S}}\mathrm{d}W^{S}_{u}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{n}_{u}+\alpha_{u}}{\sigma^{S}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right).

Under this probability measure,
\displaystyle(S_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
has dynamics

\mathrm{d}S_{t}=\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{n}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{S}\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}^{S,n},

where
\displaystyle\tilde{W}^{S,n}
is a standard Brownian motion under
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{n,\nu^{B}}
. In other words, each informed trader observes a common signal
\displaystyle\alpha
on the price, on top of which each one observes an idiosyncratic signal
\displaystyle\alpha^{n}
that is hidden to the other traders. Moreover, the broker has a linear permanent impact on the price due to the trading in the lit market.

For a given
\displaystyle(\nu^{B}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, the
\displaystyle n-
th informed trader maximises the following objective function

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{n,\nu^{B}}\left[X^{n}_{T}+Q^{n}_{T}S_{T}-a^{n}\,\left(Q^{n}_{T}\right)^{2}-\phi^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{n}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right],

over her set of admissible controls
\displaystyle(\nu^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, where
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{n,\nu^{B}}
is the expectation taken under probability
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{n,\nu^{B}}
, and
\displaystyle a^{n},\phi^{n}
two positive random variables, independent from everything else, with the same joint distribution
\displaystyle\xi
for all
\displaystyle n
and such that

\mathbb{E}[a^{n}]=\bar{a}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}[\phi^{n}]=\bar{\phi},

where
\displaystyle\bar{a},\bar{\phi}>0
.

It is easy to see that this amounts to maximising

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{n,\nu^{B}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{Q^{n}_{t}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{n}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)-\eta^{I}\left(\nu^{n}_{t}\right)^{2}-2\,a^{n}Q^{n}_{t}\nu^{n}_{t}-\phi^{n}\left(Q^{n}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right].

2.3
The problem of the broker

We introduce the probability measure
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{B,\nu^{B}}
given by

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{B,\nu^{B}}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\right|_{\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t}}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha_{u}}{\sigma^{S}}\mathrm{d}W^{S}_{u}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha_{u}}{\sigma^{S}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}u\right).

Under this probability measure,
\displaystyle(S_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
has dynamics

\mathrm{d}S_{t}=\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{S}\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}^{S,B},

where
\displaystyle\tilde{W}^{S,B}
is a standard Brownian motion under
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{B,\nu^{B}}
. That is, the broker observes the fundamental signal
\displaystyle\alpha
on the price, and he has linear permanent impact on the price when he trades in the lit market.

For given
\displaystyle(\nu^{1}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\ldots,(\nu^{N}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, the broker wants to maximise the following objective function

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{B,\nu^{B}}\left[X^{B}_{T}+Q^{B}_{T}S_{T}-\frac{a^{B}}{N}\,\left(Q^{B}_{T}\right)^{2}-\frac{\phi^{B}}{N}\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right],

over his set of admissible controls
\displaystyle(\nu^{B}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
, where
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{B,\nu^{B}}
is the expectation taken under probability
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{B,\nu^{B}}
, and
\displaystyle a^{B},\phi^{B}>0
correspond to the risk aversion of the broker. It is easy to see that this amounts to maximising

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{B,\nu^{B}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{Q^{B}_{t}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)+\eta^{I}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\nu^{n}_{t}\right)^{2}-N\eta^{B}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}-2\frac{a^{B}}{N}Q^{B}_{t}\left(N\nu^{B}_{t}-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\nu^{n}_{t}\right)-\frac{\phi^{B}}{N}\left(Q^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right].

Of course, the optimisation problem remains unchanged if we scale the objective function by dividing it by
\displaystyle N
, in which case the broker maximises

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{B,\nu^{B}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)+\eta^{I}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\nu^{n}_{t}\right)^{2}-\eta^{B}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}-2\,a^{B}\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\nu^{n}_{t}\right)-\phi^{B}\left(\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right],

(2.2)

where
\displaystyle\left(\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}=\left(\frac{Q^{B}_{t}}{N}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
, that is,

\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}=\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\nu^{n}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t.

2.4
Limitations of the finite game model and mean field limit

In the above, we described an agent-based model where
\displaystyle N
informed traders trade against a single broker. Solving this multi-agent (
\displaystyle N+1
players) problem boils down to the resolution of a system of Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations, where the state variables are the inventory processes of the broker and the
\displaystyle N
informed traders, as well as their idiosyncratic and common signals. This system of
\displaystyle N+1
HJB equations is intractable in practice for a large number of informed traders.

To obtain an approximate solution to this problem, in the remaining of the paper we propose a mean field game approach. The broker does not face
\displaystyle N
informed traders but infinitely many of them in a mean field interaction, which can be thought as the averaged behaviour of the informed traders. In the next section, we present rigorously the mean field limit of the
\displaystyle N
-players model, and the corresponding optimisation problems of the broker and a representative informed trader.

Propagation of chaos also tells us that at the limit, we should expect that the execution rate associated with each trader will become independent conditionally to
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle\nu^{B}
, therefore it is reasonable to assume that the dynamics of
\displaystyle\bar{Q}^{B}
will converge toward

\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}=\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\bar{\nu}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t,

where
\displaystyle\bar{\nu}_{t}
denotes the expectation of the execution rate of an informed trader at time
\displaystyle t
knowing
\displaystyle\alpha_{t}
and
\displaystyle\nu^{B}_{t}
.

In the next section, we rigorously introduce the mean field version of the problem.

We consider a trading horizon
\displaystyle T>0
. We denote by
\displaystyle\Omega_{c}

to
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\Omega_{c}^{2}

3.2
Admissible controls

The set of admissible strategies for the representative informed trader as well as the broker is given by

\mathcal{A}=\left\{\nu=(\nu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\nu\text{ is }\mathbb{F}-\text{progressively measurable, and }\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\nu_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right]<+\infty\right.\right\}.

Let us denote by
\displaystyle(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the process with values in
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
representing at time
\displaystyle t
the distribution of the execution rates of the (other) informed traders conditionally to
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}
. We introduce the mean field execution rate
\displaystyle(\bar{\nu}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
given for each

For a couple

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I})=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{Q^{I}_{t}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)-\eta^{I}\left(\nu^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}-2\,a^{I}Q^{I}_{t}\nu^{I}_{t}-\phi^{I}\left(Q^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right],

\displaystyle\eta^{I}

Next, taking the mean-field version of problem (

\underset{\nu^{B}\in\mathcal{A}}{\sup}H^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B}),

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B})=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)+\eta^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)-\eta^{B}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}-2\,a^{B}\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\left(\nu^{B}_{t}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{t}(dx)\right)-\phi^{B}\left(\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right],

The informed trader’s optimality condition

In this subsection, we show that (i) the functional
\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}
is strictly concave, (ii) Gâteaux differentiable, and (iii) we characterise the optimal trading strategy of the representative informed trader.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\nu^{B}\in\mathcal{A}
. The functional
\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\cdot):\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
defined in (
3.1
) is strictly concave up to a
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-
null set.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\nu^{B}\in\mathcal{A}
and let
\displaystyle\zeta,\nu\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])
with
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(A)>0
where
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}:=\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t
and for
\displaystyle(\omega,t)\in A
we have that
\displaystyle\eta_{t}(\omega)\neq\nu_{t}(\omega)
. Let
\displaystyle\rho\in(0,1)
, we need to show that

H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)>\rho\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu)\,.

We observe that

Q^{I,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}=\rho\,Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}+(1-\rho)\,Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\,,

where we use the controls in the superscript to draw attention to the process used to define
\displaystyle Q^{I}_{t}
. Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{Q^{I,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\right)-\eta^{I}\left(\rho\,\zeta_{t}+(1-\rho)\,\nu_{t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2\,a^{I}\,Q^{I,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}\,\left(\rho\,\zeta_{t}+(1-\rho)\,\nu_{t}\right)-\phi^{I}\left(Q^{I,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad+\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{-2\,\eta^{I}\,\zeta_{t}\,\nu_{t}-2\,a^{I}\left(Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}\,\nu_{t}+Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\,\zeta_{t}\right)-2\,\phi^{I}\,Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}\,Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad-\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{-\eta^{I}\,\zeta^{2}_{t}-\eta^{I}\,\nu^{2}_{t}-2\,a^{I}\left(Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}\,\zeta_{t}+Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\,\nu_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt-\phi^{I}\left(\left(Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}\right)^{2}+\left(Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\right)^{2}\right)\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\,.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{\eta^{I}\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)^{2}+2\,a^{I}\left(Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}-Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\right)\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)+\phi^{I}\left(Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}+Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\,.

By the definition of
\displaystyle Q^{I}
we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}-Q^{I,\nu}_{t}\right)\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\zeta_{u}-\nu_{u}\mathrm{d}u\right)\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\zeta_{u}-\nu_{u}\right)\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\right]\,,

and clearly

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left(Q^{I,\zeta}_{t}-Q^{I,\nu_{t}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\geq 0\,.

Given that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(A)>0
, the following holds

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]>0\,,

and this together with the above inequalities implies that
\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)>\rho\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu)
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

The functional
\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}
defined in (
3.1
) is everywhere Gâteaux differentiable in
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
. The Gâteaux derivative at a point
\displaystyle\nu^{I}\in\mathcal{A}
in a direction
\displaystyle w^{I}\in\mathcal{A}
is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle DH^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I}),w^{I}\big\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}w^{I}_{t}\left\{-2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}Q^{I}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I}_{u}\right)\mathrm{d}u\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right].

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\nu^{I},w^{I}\in{\cal A}
. The Gâteaux derivative of
\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}
at point
\displaystyle\nu^{I}
in the direction of
\displaystyle w^{I}
, if it exists, is defined as

\big\langle DH^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I}),w^{I}\big\rangle=\underset{\varepsilon\searrow 0}{\lim}\frac{H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I}+\varepsilon w^{I})-H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I})}{\varepsilon}.

Let
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
, and define

\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}w^{I}_{t}\mathrm{d}t,

for all
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
. Then a direct computation gives us

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I}+\varepsilon w^{I})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I})+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\Big(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\Big)-2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I}_{t}w^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}Q^{I}_{t}w^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\nu^{I}_{t}-2\,\phi^{I}Q^{I}_{t}\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-\eta^{I}\left(w^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}-2\,a^{I}\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}w^{I}_{t}-\phi^{I}\left(\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right].

Let us denote by
\displaystyle A
the term

A:=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{-\eta^{I}\left(w^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}-2\,a^{I}\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}w^{I}_{t}-\phi^{I}\left(\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right].

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{H^{I,\nu^{B}}\!(\nu^{I}+\varepsilon w^{I})\!-\!H^{I,\nu^{B}}\!(\nu^{I})}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\Bigg[\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\bigg\{\!\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\Big(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}\Big)-2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I}_{t}w^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}Q^{I}_{t}w^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\nu^{I}_{t}-2\,\phi^{I}Q^{I}_{t}\tilde{Q}^{I}_{t}\!\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\!+\!\varepsilon A.

We can write this as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{H^{I,\nu^{B}}\!(\nu^{I}+\varepsilon w^{I})\!-\!H^{I,\nu^{B}}\!(\nu^{I})}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\Bigg[\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!w^{I}_{t}\bigg\{\!-2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I}_{t}-2\,a^{I}Q^{I}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{I}Q^{I}_{u}\Big)\mathrm{d}u\!\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\!+\!\varepsilon A.

Taking the limit as
\displaystyle\varepsilon\searrow 0
finally yields the result.

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

We have that

\nu^{I,\star}=\underset{\nu^{I}\in\mathcal{A}}{\arg\max}\ H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I})

if and only if
\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}
is the unique strong solution to the FBSDE

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{d}\left(2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}\right)&=\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-\mathrm{d}Z^{I}_{t},\\
2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{T}&=-2\,a^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{T},\end{cases}

(3.4)

where
\displaystyle Z^{I}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{T}
is an
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}-
adapted
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}-
martingale.

Proof.

As in
Casgrain and Jaimungal 2020

[
15
]
, using Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, we can apply the result of
Ekeland and Temam 1999

[
17
]
which states that

\big\langle DH^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I,\star}),w^{I}\big\rangle=0\quad\forall w^{I}\in{\cal A}\iff\nu^{I,\star}=\underset{\nu^{I}\in\mathcal{A}}{\arg\max}\ H^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I}).

Therefore, it only remains to prove that
\displaystyle\big\langle DH^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I,\star}),w^{I}\big\rangle=0\quad\forall w^{I}\in{\cal A}
if and only if
\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}
is solution to the FBSDE (
3.4
).

Let us first assume that
\displaystyle\big\langle DH^{I,\nu^{B}}(\nu^{I,\star}),w^{I}\big\rangle=0
for all
\displaystyle w^{I}\in{\cal A}
. This implies that

\mathbb{E}\left[-2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}-2\,a^{I}Q^{I}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{u}\right)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=0

almost surely for all
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle-2\,\eta^{I}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[2\,a^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{u}\right)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{u}\right)\!\mathrm{d}u+\mathbb{E}\left[\!2\,a^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{T}\!-\!\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{u}\right)\!\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{u}\right)\mathrm{d}u-Z^{I}_{t},

where the process
\displaystyle Z^{I}
given by

Z^{I}_{t}:=-\mathbb{E}\left[2\,a^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{T}\!-\!\int_{0}^{T}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{u}\right)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

is a martingale, by definition. Hence it is clear that
\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}
is solution to the FBSDE (
3.4
).

Conversely, assume that
\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}
is solution to the FBSDE (
3.4
). Then
\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}
can be represented implicitly as

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[-2\,a^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{u}+\alpha^{I}_{u}+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{u}\right)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Bigg].

Plugging this into the expression of the Gâteaux derivative (
3.3
) in Lemma
3.3
, it is clear that it vanishes almost surely for any
\displaystyle w^{I}\in{\cal A}
.
∎

3.5
The broker’s optimality condition

Similar to the above, in this subsection, we show that (i) the functional
\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}
is strictly concave, (ii) Gâteaux differentiable, and (iii) we characterise the optimal trading strategy of the broker.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\displaystyle(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
with values in
\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
be the distribution of the execution rates of the informed traders conditionally to
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}
. The functional
\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}(\cdot):\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
defined in (
3.2
) is strictly concave up to a
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-
null set.

Proof.

Fix
\displaystyle(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and let
\displaystyle\zeta,\nu\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])
with
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(A)>0
where
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}:=\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t
and for
\displaystyle(\omega,t)\in A
we have that
\displaystyle\eta_{t}(\omega)\neq\nu_{t}(\omega)
. Let
\displaystyle\rho\in(0,1)
, we need to show that

H^{B,\mu}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)>\rho\,H^{B,\mu}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{B,\mu}(\nu)\,.

Similar to the proof of strict concavity for the informed trader, observe that

\bar{Q}^{B,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\rho\,\zeta_{u}+(1-\rho)\,\nu_{u}-\bar{\nu}_{u}\right)\mathrm{d}u=\rho\,\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}+(1-\rho)\,\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\,,

where (as before) we use the notation of having the controls in the superscript.
It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{\bar{Q}^{B,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}\left(b\left(\rho\,\zeta_{t}+(1-\rho)\,\nu_{t}\right)+\alpha_{t}\right)+\eta^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)-\eta^{B}\left(\rho\,\zeta_{t}+(1-\rho)\,\nu_{t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-2\,a^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}\left(\rho\,\zeta_{t}+(1-\rho)\,\nu_{t}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{t}(dx)\right)-\phi^{B}\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu}_{t}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho\,H^{B,\mu}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{B,\mu}(\nu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad+\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{+\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}\,b\,\nu_{t}+\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\,b\,\zeta_{t}-2\,\eta^{B}\,\zeta_{t}\,\nu_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-2\,a^{B}\,\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}\,\nu_{t}+\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\,\zeta_{t}\right)-2\,\phi^{B}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad-\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{+\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}\,b\,\zeta_{t}+\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\,b\,\nu_{t}-\eta^{B}\,\left(\zeta^{2}_{t}-\nu^{2}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-2\,a^{B}\,\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}\,\zeta_{t}+\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\,\nu_{t}\right)-\phi^{B}\,\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}\right)^{2}-\phi^{B}\,\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\,.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)=\rho\,H^{B,\mu}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{B,\mu}(\nu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\qquad+\rho\,(1-\rho)\,\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\{\left(2\,a^{B}-b\right)\,\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}-\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\right)\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)+\eta^{B}\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)^{2}+\phi^{B}\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}-\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\right)^{2}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\,.

Similar to above,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}-\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\right)\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\right]\geq 0\,,

and clearly

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\bar{Q}^{B,\zeta}_{t}-\bar{Q}^{B,\nu}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right]\geq 0\,.

Given that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(A)>0
, then

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\zeta_{t}-\nu_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right]>0\,,

and this implies that
\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}(\rho\,\zeta+(1-\rho)\,\nu)>\rho\,H^{B,\mu}(\zeta)+(1-\rho)\,H^{B,\mu}(\nu)
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

The functional
\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}
defined in (
3.2
) is everywhere Gâteaux differentiable in
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
. The Gâteaux derivative at a point
\displaystyle\nu^{B}\in\mathcal{A}
in a direction
\displaystyle w^{b}\in\mathcal{A}
is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big\langle DH^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B}),w^{B}\big\rangle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\!w^{B}_{t}\left\{(b-2\,a^{B})\bar{Q}^{B}_{T}\!-\!2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B}_{t}+\!\int_{t}^{T}\!\!\!\!\left(b\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B}_{u}\right)\!\mathrm{d}u\right\}\!\mathrm{d}t\right]\!.

(3.5)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\nu^{B},w^{B}\in{\cal A}
. The Gâteaux derivative of
\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}
at point
\displaystyle\nu^{B}
in the direction of
\displaystyle w^{B}
, if it exists, is defined as

\big\langle DH^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B}),w^{B}\big\rangle=\underset{\varepsilon\searrow 0}{\lim}\frac{H^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B}+\varepsilon w^{B})-H^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B})}{\varepsilon}.

Let
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
, and define

\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}w^{B}_{t}\mathrm{d}t,

for all
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
. Then a direct computation gives us

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B}+\varepsilon w^{B})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad-2\,a^{B}\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}\Big(\nu^{B}_{t}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)\Big)-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}w^{B}_{t}-\eta^{B}\Big(w^{B}_{t}\Big)^{2}-\phi^{B}\Big(\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}\Big)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right].

Let us denote by
\displaystyle A

A:=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left\{\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}w^{B}_{t}-\eta^{B}\Big(w^{B}_{t}\Big)^{2}-\phi^{B}\Big(\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}\Big)^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}t\right].

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{H^{B,\mu}\!(\nu^{B}+\varepsilon w^{B})\!-\!H^{B,\mu}\!(\nu^{B})}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad-2\,a^{B}\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}\Big(\nu^{B}_{t}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)\Big)-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B}_{t}\tilde{Q}^{B}_{t}\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]\!+\!\varepsilon A.

We can write this as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{H^{B,\mu}\!(\nu^{B}+\varepsilon w^{B})\!-\!H^{B,\mu}\!(\nu^{B})}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}w^{B}_{t}\bigg\{\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\bar{Q}^{B}_{T}-2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(b\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B}_{u}\Big)\mathrm{d}u\bigg\}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg]+\!\varepsilon A.

Taking the limit as
\displaystyle\varepsilon\searrow 0

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

We have that

\nu^{B,\star}=\underset{\nu^{B}\in\mathcal{A}}{\arg\max}\ H^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B})

if and only if
\displaystyle\nu^{B,\star}
is the unique strong solution to the FBSDE

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{d}\left(2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{t}\right)&=\left(b\,\bar{\nu}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-\mathrm{d}Z^{B}_{t},\\
2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{T}&=(b-2\,a^{B})\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T},\end{cases}

(3.6)

where
\displaystyle Z^{B}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{T}
is an
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}-
adapted
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}-
martingale.

Proof.

As before, using Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
, we can apply the result of
Ekeland and Temam 1999

[
17
]
which states that

\big\langle DH^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B,\star}),w^{B}\big\rangle=0\quad\forall w^{B}\in{\cal A}\iff\nu^{B,\star}=\underset{\nu^{B}\in\mathcal{A}}{\arg\max}\ H^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B}).

Therefore, it only remains to prove that
\displaystyle\big\langle DH^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B,\star}),w^{B}\big\rangle=0\quad\forall w^{B}\in{\cal A}
if and only if
\displaystyle\nu^{B,\star}
is solution to the FBSDE in (
3.6
).

Let us first assume that
\displaystyle\big\langle DH^{B,\mu}(\nu^{B,\star}),w^{B}\big\rangle=0\quad\forall w^{B}\in{\cal A}
. This implies that

\mathbb{E}\left[\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T}-2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(b\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{u}\Big)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=0

almost surely for all
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle-2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[-\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}\Big(b\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{u}\Big)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\Big(b\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{u}\Big)\!\mathrm{d}u-\mathbb{E}\left[\!\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T}+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Big(b\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{u}\Big)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\!\Big(b\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{u}\Big)\!\mathrm{d}u-Z^{B}_{t},

where the process
\displaystyle Z^{B}
given by

Z^{B}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\!\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T}+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\Big(b\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{u}\Big)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

is a martingale, by definition. Hence it is clear that
\displaystyle\nu^{B,\star}
is solution to the FBSDE (
3.6
).

Conversely, assume that
\displaystyle\nu^{B,\star}
is solution to the FBSDE (
3.6
). Then
\displaystyle\nu^{B,\star}
can be represented implicitly as

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[\!\Big(b-2\,a^{B}\Big)\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\!\!\!\Big(b\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\,\mu_{u}(\mathrm{d}x)+\alpha_{u}-2\,\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{u}\Big)\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

Plugging this into the expression of the Gâteaux derivative (
3.5
) in Lemma
3.6
, it is clear that it vanishes almost surely for any
\displaystyle w^{B}\in{\cal A}
.
∎

3.6
The mean field FBSDE system

Finally, at equilibrium, we have the following system of coupled FBSDEs

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{d}\left(2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}\right)&=\left(b\,\nu^{B}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\,\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-\mathrm{d}Z^{I}_{t},\\
-\mathrm{d}\left(2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{t}\right)&=\left(b\,\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-\mathrm{d}Z^{B}_{t},\\
2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{T}&=-2\,a^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{T}\\
2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{T}&=-(2\,a^{B}-b)\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T}.\end{cases}

(3.7)

Moreover, we know that at the equilibrium

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{I,\star}\left|\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{t}\right.\right].

(3.8)

3.7
The closed-form solution to the FBSDE

Next, we solve the FBSDE systems we derived. First, we solve the mean-field system of FBSDEs explicitly, and then we solve the FBSDE of an individual informed trader.

3.7.1
The optimal strategy of the broker

In the equilibrium, we solve the FBSDE system

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{d}\left(2\,\eta^{I}\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}\right)&=\left(b\,\nu^{B,\star}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\,\bar{\phi}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-\mathrm{d}\bar{Z}^{I}_{t},\\
-\mathrm{d}\left(2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{t}\right)&=\left(b\,\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-\mathrm{d}Z^{B}_{t},\\
2\,\eta^{I}\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{T}&=-2\,\bar{a}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{T}\\
2\,\eta^{B}\nu^{B,\star}_{T}&=-(2\,a^{B}-b)\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{T}.\end{cases}

(3.9)

Let us make an ansatz, and look for the solution to the above system in the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=g^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nu^{B,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=h^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,,

where
\displaystyle g^{a}_{t},g^{b}_{t},g^{c}_{t}
and
\displaystyle h^{a}_{t},h^{b}_{t},h^{c}_{t}
are deterministic
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{1}
functions, with terminal conditions
\displaystyle g^{a}_{T}=h^{a}_{T}=g^{c}_{T}=h^{b}_{T}=0
,
\displaystyle g^{b}_{T}=-\bar{a}/\eta^{I}
and
\displaystyle h^{c}_{T}=-(2\,a^{B}-b)/2\,\eta^{B}
, and where

\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\,,\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\nu^{B,\star}_{u}-\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{u}\right)\,\mathrm{d}u\,.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}g^{a}_{t}+g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{t}+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}g^{b}_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}g^{c}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}g^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}\alpha_{t}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}g^{b}_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}g^{c}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\,\left(\nu^{B,\star}_{t}-\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{\alpha}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}g^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}\alpha_{t}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}g^{b}_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,\left(g^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}g^{c}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+g^{c}_{t}\,\left(\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)\alpha_{t}+\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{\alpha}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\big\{\mathrm{d}g^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+g^{b}_{t}\,g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\big\}+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\big\{\mathrm{d}g^{b}_{t}+\left(g^{b}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\big\{\mathrm{d}g^{c}_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,g^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\big\}\,+\sigma^{\alpha}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t},

and similarly,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}^{B,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}h^{a}_{t}+h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{t}+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}h^{b}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}h^{c}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}h^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}\alpha_{t}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}h^{b}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}h^{c}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\,\left(\nu^{B,\star}_{t}-\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{\alpha}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}h^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}\alpha_{t}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}h^{b}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,\left(g^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}h^{c}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+h^{c}_{t}\,\left(\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)\alpha_{t}+\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{\alpha}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\big\{\mathrm{d}h^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{b}_{t}\,g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\big\}+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\big\{\mathrm{d}h^{b}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,g^{b}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\big\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\big\{\mathrm{d}h^{c}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,g^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t\big\}\,+\sigma^{\alpha}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}.

Given that
\displaystyle\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}
also satisfies the above FBSDE, we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\left(b\,\nu^{B,\star}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\,\bar{\phi}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{Z}^{I}_{t},

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\left(b\,\left(h^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)+\alpha_{t}-2\,\bar{\phi}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{Z}^{I}_{t},

and similarly for
\displaystyle\bar{\nu}^{B,\star}_{t}
, for which we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\nu^{B,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{B}}\left(b\,\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{B}}\mathrm{d}Z^{B}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{B}}\left(b\,\left(g^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)+\alpha_{t}-2\phi^{B}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{B}}\mathrm{d}Z^{B}_{t}\,.

Combining the derived expressions we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\bigg\{\mathrm{d}g^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+g^{b}_{t}\,g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b\,h^{a}_{t}+1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\bigg\{\mathrm{d}g^{b}_{t}+\left(g^{b}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b\,h^{b}_{t}-2\,\bar{\phi}}{2\,\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\bigg\{\mathrm{d}g^{c}_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,g^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b\,h^{c}_{t}}{2\,\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}\,+\sigma^{\alpha}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{Z}^{I}_{t},

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\bigg\{\mathrm{d}h^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{b}_{t}\,g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b\,g^{a}_{t}+1}{2\,\eta^{B}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\,\bigg\{\mathrm{d}h^{b}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,g^{b}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b\,g^{b}_{t}}{2\,\eta^{B}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\,\bigg\{\mathrm{d}h^{c}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,g^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{b\,g^{c}_{t}-2\,\phi^{B}}{2\,\eta^{B}}\mathrm{d}t\bigg\}\,+\sigma^{\alpha}h^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{B}}\mathrm{d}Z^{B}_{t}.

Then, by setting

\mathrm{d}Z^{B}_{t}=2\eta^{B}\sigma^{\alpha}\bar{h}^{a}(t)\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{d}\bar{Z}^{I}_{t}=2\eta^{I}\sigma^{\alpha}\bar{g}^{a}(t)\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t},

we observe that the system of equations becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}g^{a}_{t}+\left[-k^{\alpha}g^{a}_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\,g^{a}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)+\frac{b\,h^{a}_{t}+1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}h^{a}_{t}+\left[-k^{\alpha}h^{a}_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,g^{a}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t}\right)+\frac{b\,g^{a}_{t}+1}{2\,\eta^{B}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}g^{b}_{t}+\left[\left(g^{b}_{t}\right)^{2}+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)+\frac{b\,h^{b}_{t}-2\,\bar{\phi}}{2\,\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}h^{b}_{t}+\left[h^{b}_{t}\,g^{b}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\right)+\frac{b\,g^{b}_{t}}{2\,\eta^{B}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}g^{c}_{t}+\left[g^{b}_{t}\,g^{c}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)+\frac{b\,h^{c}_{t}}{2\,\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}h^{c}_{t}+\left[h^{b}_{t}\,g^{c}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)+\frac{b\,g^{c}_{t}-2\,\phi^{B}}{2\,\eta^{B}}\right]\mathrm{d}t\,,

with terminal condition
\displaystyle g^{a}_{T}=h^{a}_{T}=g^{c}_{T}=h^{b}_{T}=0
,
\displaystyle g^{b}_{T}=-\bar{a}/\eta^{I}
and
\displaystyle h^{c}_{T}=-(2\,a^{B}-b)/2\,\eta^{B}
. We see that the system for
\displaystyle g^{b}_{t},g^{c}_{t},h^{b}_{t},h^{c}_{t}
is independent of the solution to
\displaystyle g^{a}_{t},h^{a}_{t}
.
Let
\displaystyle\bm{P}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{P}_{t}=-\begin{pmatrix}h^{c}_{t}&h^{b}_{t}\\
g^{c}_{t}&g^{b}_{t}\end{pmatrix}

and let
\displaystyle\bm{U},\bm{Y},\bm{Q},\bm{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{U}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\bm{Y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{b}{2\,\eta^{B}}\\
\frac{b}{2\,\eta^{I}}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\bm{Q}=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{\phi^{B}}{\eta^{B}}&0\\
0&-\frac{\bar{\phi}}{\eta^{I}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\bm{S}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{2\,a^{B}-b}{2\,\eta^{B}}&0\\
0&\frac{\bar{a}}{\eta^{I}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

The system of ODEs for
\displaystyle g^{b}_{t},g^{c}_{t},h^{b}_{t},h^{c}_{t}
can be written as the following matrix Riccati differential equation (MRDE)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}&0=\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{P}_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}+\bm{Y}\,\bm{P}_{t}-\bm{P}_{t}\,\bm{U}\,\bm{P}_{t}-\bm{Q}\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\\
&\bm{P}_{T}=\bm{S}\,.\end{cases}

(3.10)

The above system has a solution as a consequence of Theorem 2.3 in
Freiling et al. 2000

[
20
]
for
\displaystyle\bm{C}=0
and
\displaystyle\bm{D}=\bm{I}_{2}
, where
\displaystyle 0
and
\displaystyle\bm{I}_{2}
denote the zero and the identity matrix in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
. To be more precise, using the notation of
[
20
]

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{11}=0
,
\displaystyle\bm{B}_{12}=-\bm{U}
,

, and

\displaystyle\bm{C}

\displaystyle\bm{D}

\bm{C}+\bm{D}\,\bm{S}+\bm{S}^{\intercal}\,\bm{D}^{\intercal}=2\,\bm{S}>0\,,

that is, it is positive definite given that we assumed that
\displaystyle 2\,a^{B}-b>0
and
\displaystyle a^{I}>0

satisfies that

3.10

3.7.2
The optimal strategy of the informed trader

Finally, we can solve the FBSDE of the representative informed trader:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{d}\left(2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}\right)&=\left(b\,\nu^{B,\star}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\,\phi^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t-\mathrm{d}Z^{I}_{t},\\
2\,\eta^{I}\nu^{I,\star}_{T}&=-2\,a^{I}Q^{I,\star}_{T}.\end{cases}

(3.16)

As before, we make an ansatz and look for a solution with the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}_{t}=f^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+f^{a,I}_{t}\alpha^{I}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}Q^{I,\star}_{t}+f^{c}_{t}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t},

(3.17)

where
\displaystyle f^{a},f^{a,I},f^{b},f^{b,I},f^{c}
are deterministic
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{1}
functions, with terminal conditions
\displaystyle f^{a}_{T}=f^{a,I}_{T}=f^{b}_{T}=f^{c}_{T}=0
and
\displaystyle f^{b,I}_{T}=-a^{I}/\eta^{I}
, and where

Q^{I,\star}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\nu^{I,\star}_{u}\mathrm{d}u.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{a}_{t}+f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}\alpha_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{a,I}_{t}+f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}\alpha^{I}_{t}+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{b}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+Q^{I,\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{b,I}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}Q^{I,\star}_{t}+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{c}_{t}+f^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}\alpha_{t}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\alpha^{I}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{a,I}_{t}-k^{I}\alpha^{I}_{t}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{b}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}\bar{\nu}^{\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+Q^{I,\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{b,I}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{c}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+f^{c}_{t}\left(\nu^{B,\star}_{t}-\nu^{\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}+\sigma^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{I}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}\alpha_{t}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\alpha^{I}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{a,I}_{t}-k^{I}\alpha^{I}_{t}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{b}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}\left(g^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+g^{b}_{t}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+g^{c}_{t}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+Q^{I,\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{b,I}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}\left(f^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+f^{a,I}_{t}\alpha^{I}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}Q^{I,\star}_{t}+f^{c}_{t}\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\mathrm{d}f^{c}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+f^{c}_{t}\left((h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t})\alpha_{t}+(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t})\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t})\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}+\sigma^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{I}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b}_{t}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{c}_{t}(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t})\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{I}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{a,I}_{t}-k^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{b}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}g^{b}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{b}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{c}_{t}(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t})\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+Q^{I,\star}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{b,I}_{t}+\left(f^{b,I}_{t}\right)^{2}\right\}+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{c}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}g^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{c}_{t}(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t})\ dt\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\sigma^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}+\sigma^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{I}_{t}.

Given that
\displaystyle\nu^{I,\star}_{t}
also satisfies the above FBSDE, we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\nu^{I,\star}_{t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\left(b\,\nu^{B,\star}_{t}+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\,\phi^{I}{Q}^{I,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\,\mathrm{d}{Z}^{I}_{t},

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\left(b\,\left(h^{a}_{t}\alpha_{t}+h^{b}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}+h^{c}_{t}\,\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\right)+\alpha^{I}_{t}+\alpha_{t}-2\,\phi^{I}{Q}^{I,\star}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\,\eta^{I}}\,\mathrm{d}{Z}^{I}_{t}.

Combining the derived expressions we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{a}_{t}-k^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b}_{t}g^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{c}_{t}(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\frac{bh^{a}_{t}+1}{2\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{I}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{a,I}_{t}-k^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{\star}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{b}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}g^{b}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{b}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{c}_{t}(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\frac{bh^{b}_{t}}{2\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+Q^{I,\star}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{b,I}_{t}+\left(f^{b,I}_{t}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t-\frac{\phi^{I}}{\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\bar{Q}^{B,\star}_{t}\left\{\mathrm{d}f^{c}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}g^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{c}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+f^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{bh^{c}_{t}}{2\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+\left[\sigma^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}+\sigma^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{I}_{t}-\frac{1}{2\eta^{I}}\mathrm{d}Z^{I}_{t}\right].

Then, by setting

\mathrm{d}Z^{I}_{t}=2\eta^{I}\left[\sigma^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{\alpha}_{t}+\sigma^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}\mathrm{d}W^{I}_{t}\right],

we observe that the system of equations becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}f^{a}_{t}+\left[-k^{\alpha}f^{a}_{t}+f^{b}_{t}g^{a}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{a}_{t}+f^{c}_{t}(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t})+\frac{bh^{a}_{t}+1}{2\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

(3.18)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}f^{a,I}_{t}+\left[-k^{I}f^{a,I}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{a,I}_{t}+\frac{1}{2\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

(3.19)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}f^{b}_{t}+\left[f^{b}_{t}g^{b}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{b}_{t}+f^{c}_{t}(h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t})+\frac{bh^{b}_{t}}{2\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

(3.20)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}f^{b,I}_{t}+\left[\left(f^{b,I}_{t}\right)^{2}-\frac{\phi^{I}}{\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t

(3.21)

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}f^{c}_{t}+\left[f^{b}_{t}g^{c}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}f^{c}_{t}+f^{c}_{t}\left(h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}\right)+\frac{bh^{c}_{t}}{2\eta^{I}}\right]\mathrm{d}t,

(3.22)

with terminal conditions
\displaystyle f^{a}_{T}=f^{a,I}_{T}=f^{b}_{T}=f^{c}_{T}=0
and
\displaystyle f^{b,I}_{T}=-a^{I}/\eta^{I}
.

Notice that Equation (
3.21
) is independent from the rest of the system. A particular solution to this Riccati equation is given by

y_{p}(t)=-\sqrt{\frac{\phi^{I}}{\eta^{I}}}\tanh\left(\sqrt{\frac{\phi^{I}}{\eta^{I}}}(T-t)\right)\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

We then know that the general solution is given by

\displaystyle y_{p}+u
where
\displaystyle u
solves

u^{\prime}=-u^{2}-2y_{p}u

on
\displaystyle[0,T]
. Substituting
\displaystyle z=1/u
yields

z^{\prime}=2y_{p}z+1,

with the terminal condition
\displaystyle f^{b,I}_{T}=-a^{I}/\eta^{I}
now translating as
\displaystyle z(T)=-\eta^{I}/a^{I}
. The solution to this linear ODE is given by

z(t)=-\frac{\eta^{I}}{a^{I}}\exp\left(-2\int_{t}^{T}y_{p}(s)\mathrm{d}s\right)-\int_{t}^{T}\exp\left(-2\int_{t}^{u}y_{p}(s)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}u.

We can finally conclude that the unique solution to Equation (
3.21
) with terminal condition
\displaystyle f^{b,I}_{T}=-a^{I}/\eta^{I}

is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle f^{b,I}_{t}=-\sqrt{\frac{\phi^{I}}{\eta^{I}}}\tanh\left(\sqrt{\frac{\phi^{I}}{\eta^{I}}}(T-t)\right)-\frac{e^{2\int_{t}^{T}y_{p}(s)\mathrm{d}s}}{\eta^{I}/a^{I}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{2\int_{u}^{T}y_{p}(s)\mathrm{d}s}\mathrm{d}u}

for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
.

Once we know
\displaystyle f^{b,I}
, Equation (
3.19
) is a simple linear ODE with terminal condition
\displaystyle f^{a,I}_{T}=0
. Its solution for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
is therefore given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle f^{a,I}_{t}=\frac{1}{2\eta^{I}}\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\int_{t}^{u}\left(k^{I}-f^{b,I}_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s}\mathrm{d}u\,.

Let
\displaystyle\bm{A}^{b,c}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
\displaystyle\bm{b}^{b,c}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be given by

\bm{A}^{b,c}_{t}=-\begin{pmatrix}g^{b}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}&h^{b}_{t}-g^{b}_{t}\\
g^{c}_{t}&h^{c}_{t}-g^{c}_{t}+f^{b,I}_{t}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bm{b}^{b,c}_{t}=-\frac{b}{2\eta^{I}}\begin{pmatrix}h^{b}_{t}\\
h^{c}_{t}\end{pmatrix}.

We introduce the function
\displaystyle\bm{F}^{b,c}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

\bm{F}^{b,c}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}f^{b}_{t}\\
f^{c}_{t}\end{pmatrix}.

Then it is clear from Equations (
3.20
) and (
3.22
) that
\displaystyle\bm{F}^{b,c}
satisfies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\bm{F}^{b,c}_{t}}=\bm{A}^{b,c}_{t}\bm{F}^{b,c}_{t}+\bm{b}^{b,c}_{t}

with terminal condition
\displaystyle\bm{F}^{b,c}_{T}=0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{F}^{b,c}_{t}=-\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\int_{t}^{u}\bm{A}^{b,c}_{s}\mathrm{d}s}\bm{b}^{b,c}_{u}\mathrm{d}u

for
\displaystyle t\in[0,T]
.

Finally, if we define
\displaystyle b^{a}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

b^{a}_{t}=-f^{b}_{t}g^{a}_{t}-f^{c}_{t}(h^{a}_{t}-g^{a}_{t})-\frac{bh^{a}_{t}+1}{2\eta^{I}}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

then the unique solution to the linear Equation (

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the problem of a broker facing many informed traders. Each informed trader observes both a common and an idiosyncratic signal. The broker charges a fixed transaction cost and chooses his externalisation rate based on the common signal and the average behaviour of the traders. Using a Gâteaux derivative approach, we derive a system of coupled forward-backward SDEs driving this optimisation problem. Using a sequence of ansatzes, we solve this FBSDE system in closed form, and obtain the equilibrium strategy of the broker and that of the representative informed trader.

We then illustrate the results of our model using a set of realistic market parameters. As expected, the average trader’s inventory moves with the common signal, and the broker adjusts his externalisation rate accordingly. More interestingly, the individual signal of a trader seems to have little impact on his trading strategy. This is due to the market impact of the broker as he externalises: his externalisation rate is driven by the average trading rate of the traders, which is itself driven by the common signal. Therefore, even in the presence of a private information that contradicts the beliefs of the market, the representative trader still tends to follow the herd, at least for a large enough value of the permanent price impact parameter.

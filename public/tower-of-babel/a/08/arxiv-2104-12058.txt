Abstract
. In this paper, for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , we obtain the L p L^{p} -boundedness of the Hilbert transform H γ H^{\gamma} along a variable plane curve ( t , u ⁡ ( x 1 , x 2 ) ​ γ ​ ( t ) ) (t,u(x_{1},x_{2})\gamma(t)) , where u u is a Lipschitz function with small Lipschitz norm, and γ \gamma is a general curve satisfying some suitable smoothness and curvature conditions.



1.
introduction

Let
u:\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function,
\gamma:\ [-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
with
\gamma(0)=0
be a smooth function that is either even or odd, and is strictly increasing on
[0,1]
, we define

(1.1)

\displaystyle H^{\gamma}f(x_{1},x_{2})={\rm p.\,v.}\int_{-1}^{1}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u(x_{1},x_{2})\gamma(t))\,\frac{dt}{t}.

The main purpose of this article is to study the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Hilbert transform
H^{\gamma}
along the variable plane curve
(t,u(x_{1},x_{2})\gamma(t))
defined in (
1.1
).

Our Hilbert transform
H^{\gamma}
is motivated by Stein’s conjecture
[
34
]
, which correspond to the limiting case
\gamma(t)=t
not covered here. The so-called Stein conjecture can be stated as follows: Denote

H_{\varepsilon_{0}}f(x_{1},x_{2})={\rm p.\,v.}\int_{-\varepsilon_{0}}^{\varepsilon_{0}}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u(x_{1},x_{2})t)\,\frac{dt}{t},

one wishes to know if the operator
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
, where
u:\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough depending on
\|u\|_{\textrm{Lip}}
. It should be mentioned that this Lipschitz regularity imposed on
u
is critical, since a counterexample based on a construction of the Besicovitch-Kakeya set shows that no
L^{p}
-boundednesss of
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
is possible for
C^{\alpha}
regularity with
\alpha<1
, where
1<p<\infty
.

We now state some historical evolutions of this conjecture. For any real analytic function
u
, Stein and Street
[
36
]
obtained the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
for all
1<p<\infty
. For
C^{\infty}
vector fields
u
under certain geometric assumptions, Christ, Nagel, Stein and Wainger
[
12
]
showed that
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
is bounded on
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
. For
u\in C^{1+\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
, Lacey and Li
[
24
]
proved the
L^{2}
-boundedness of
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
, conditioning on
the boundedness of the so-called Lipschitz-Kakeya maximal operator. For some other partial results toward this conjecture, we refer to
[
29
,
5
,
23
]
.

In the last decade further model problems, with the additional simplifying condition about
u
have been considered: Based on the works of Lacey and Li
[
23
,
24
]
, Bateman
[
1
]
proved the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}P_{j}^{2}
, with a bound being independent of
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
u(x_{1},x_{2})=u(x_{1},0)
is only a one-variable measurable function,
P_{j}^{2}

is the Littlewood-Paley projection applied in the second variable and
1<p<\infty
. Later, under the same condition about
u
, Bateman and Thiele
[
2
]
obtained the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
for all
3/2<p<\infty
. Furthermore, Guo
[
18
,
19
]
obtained some similar results under the condition that
u
is constant along a Lipschitz curve, i.e., a perturbation of Bateman and Thiele’s result
[
1
,
2
]
in the critical Lipschitz regularity.

The so-called Stein conjecture is very difficult. However, we tend to think that the presence of the curvature in
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
is another direction of considering this outstanding open problem. Therefore, we here extend the variable straight line
(t,u(x_{1},x_{2})t)
in
H_{\varepsilon_{0}}
to the variable curve
(t,u(x_{1},x_{2})\gamma(t))
in
H^{\gamma}
(see (
1.1
)), and consider the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the operator
H^{\gamma}
.

We now state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

We assume that
\gamma
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

\inf_{0<t\leq 1}\big|(\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}(t)\big|>0;

(ii)

\min_{j\in\{1,2\}}\inf_{0<t\leq 1}\big|t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)/\gamma(t)\big|>0;

(iii)

\sup_{1\leq j\leq N}\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\big|t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)/\gamma(t)\big|<\infty,
for a sufficiently large integer
N
.

Then for every
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\|u\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq 1/(2\gamma(1))
, it holds that

(1.2)

\|H^{\gamma}f\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,p}\|f\|_{p}

for every
1<p<\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
26
]
, for
1<p\leq\infty
, Liu, Song and Yu obtained the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the corresponding maximal function
M^{\gamma}
along the variable curve
(t,u(x_{1},x_{2})\gamma(t))
, under the same assumptions imposed on
u
and
\gamma
as in Theorem
1.1
. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
should be regarded as a natural continuation of
[
26
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

The smoothness assumption and the strict monotonicity assumption on
\gamma
are made to avoid certain technicality. In the assumption
\rm(iii)
of the above theorem, it is more than enough to take
N=10^{3}
. Some examples of curves satisfying all of the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
can be found in
[

satisfy our assumptions in Theorem

, where the notation

stands for either

or sgn
(t)|t|^{\alpha}
,

. Moreover, one can allow the function

to have a larger Lipschitz norm if one is willing to replace the truncation

To further collect some historical evolutions of our operator (

and define the Hilbert transform along the variable curve

, we refer to

For the case

In the end, let us mention the new ingredients that will be used to prove our Theorem
1.1
. It is easy to see that Theorem
1.1
is the generalization of Di Plinio, Guo, Thiele and Zorin-Kranich’s result
[
15
]
for

from the special non-flat homogeneous curve

to more general curve
\gamma(t)
. In the non-flat homogeneous curve case
\gamma(t)=[t]^{\alpha}
, we have the following special property, i.e.,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\gamma(ab)=\gamma(a)\gamma(b),\quad\textrm{for all}\ \ a,b>0,

which implies that one can add
v(x,y)(u(x,y)v^{-1}(x,y))^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}
in the partition of unity to split these operators considered, where

is the largest integer power of
2
less than

, see
[
15
, (2.36)]
for more details. In the general curve case, such a decomposition is not appropriate based on the absence of this property (
1.3
). Therefore, we have to split our operators by the classical partition of unity, i.e.,
1=\Sigma_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{l}t)
, see (
5.10
). However, we still will encounter the difficulty of
\gamma(2^{-l}t)\neq\gamma(2^{-l})\gamma(t)
, even though we have used this classical partition of unity. To overcome this difficulty and separate
2^{-l}
from
\gamma(2^{-l}t)

by
\gamma_{l}(t)=\gamma(2^{-l}t)/\gamma(2^{-l})
(see (
2.5

2.2

\gamma_{l}
behaves “uniformly” in the parameter

.

On the other hand, in
[
15
]
, the authors need to obtain a local smoothing estimate for

\mathcal{A}_{u,t^{\alpha}}f(x,y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-ut,y-ut^{\alpha})\psi_{0}(t)\,dt.

However, for the general curve case considered here, since the lack of this property (
1.3
), we may not reduce our local smoothing estimate for the corresponding operator

, which is one of the main difficulties in the problem. In this paper, we establish a local smoothing estimate for

(see (

. Compared with

will leads to essential difficulties, since the critical point of the phase function in

is independent of

, but the critical point of the phase function in

will depend on

.

Another highlight of this paper is that we generalize the result in Seeger
[
31
]
to a general homogeneous type space associated with curve

2.
some properties about
\gamma

First of all, without loss of generality, we assume that
\gamma(1)=1
. As a consequence, the function
u
satisfies
\|u\|_{{\rm Lip}}\leq 1/2
.
To simplify our future discussion, we extend the domain of the function
\gamma
from
[-1,1]
to
\mathbb{R}
by setting

(2.1)

\gamma(t)=\gamma(t^{-1})^{-1}

for every
|t|\geq 1
. Then we have
\gamma(t)\in C([0,\infty)),

\gamma(t)
is increasing on
[0,\infty)
and
\gamma([0,\infty))=[0,\infty).
From the assumption
\rm(ii)
in Theorem
1.1
with
j=2
, we see that
\gamma
is either convex or concave. These two cases can be handled in exactly the same way, and therefore we without loss of generality assume that
\gamma
is convex, that is,
\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
for every
0<t\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the above assumptions on
\gamma
, we have

(i)

\gamma(t)
satisfies a doubling condition on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, i.e.,

(2.2)

C_{0,L}\leq\frac{\gamma(2t)}{\gamma(t)}\leq C_{0,U}

for some
C_{0,L}>1
and
C_{0,U}>1
that depend only on
\gamma
;

(ii)

There exists a positive constant
C_{1,L}>1
such that

\displaystyle C_{1,L}\leq\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(2t)}{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}

for all
0<t\leq 1/2
.

Proof.

We start with the proof of (i).
Define
h(t)=\ln\gamma(t)
for all
t\in(0,1]
, by the conditions (ii) and (iii) of Theorem

1.1
, we know that there exists
C>1
such that
th^{\prime}(t)\in[C^{-1},C]
. Then for all
t\in(0,1/2],
we have

\displaystyle\ln\frac{\gamma(2t)}{\gamma(t)}=h(2t)-h(t)=h^{\prime}(\theta t)t\in[C^{-1}\theta^{-1},C\theta^{-1}]\subset[2^{-1}C^{-1},C]

for some
\theta\in[1,2],
and therefore

(2.3)

\displaystyle 1<e^{2^{-1}C^{-1}}\leq\frac{\gamma(2t)}{\gamma(t)}\leq e^{C}

for all
t\in(0,1/2]
. By (
2.1
), we know that (
2.3
) also holds
for all
t\in[1,\infty)
.
It remains to consider
t\in[1/2,1]
.
By continuity, there exists
t_{0}\in[1/2,1]
such that
\sup_{t\in[1/2,1]}\gamma(t)\gamma(2^{-1}t^{-1})=\gamma(t_{0})\gamma(2^{-1}t_{0}^{-1})
. Note
t_{0}
or
2^{-1}t_{0}^{-1}
must less than
3/4
, and
\gamma(1)=1
.
Then,
\gamma(t_{0})\gamma(2^{-1}t_{0}^{-1})\leq\gamma(3/4)<1
.
As a result, for all
t\in[1/2,1]
, we have

\frac{\gamma(2t)}{\gamma(t)}=\frac{1}{\gamma(t)\gamma(2^{-1}t^{-1})}\geq\frac{1}{\gamma(3/4)}>1.

Because
\gamma(t)
is increasing on [0,1], for all
t\in[1/2,1]
, we obtain

\frac{\gamma(2t)}{\gamma(t)}=\frac{1}{\gamma(t)\gamma(2^{-1}t^{-1})}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma(1/2)^{2}}.

By setting

(2.4)

C_{0,L}=\min\{e^{2^{-1}C^{-1}},\gamma(3/4)^{-1}\}>1\ \ \textrm{and}\ \ C_{0,U}=\max\{e^{C},\gamma(1/2)^{-2}\}>1,

we can finish the proof of (i). The proof for (ii) is essentially the same as (
2.3
), we omit it.
∎

Let
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, we define

(2.5)

\gamma_{l}(t)=\frac{\gamma(2^{-l}t)}{\gamma(2^{-l})}.

In the following Lemma
2.2
, we would like to prove some estimates for
\gamma_{l}
uniformly in
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. In the model case
\gamma(t)=t^{2}
, it always holds that
\gamma_{l}(t)=t^{2}
. In the general case,
\gamma
is not homogeneous anymore, and therefore we need an uniform control over the rescaled versions of
\gamma
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I=[1/2,2]
, for
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
with
2^{l}\gg 1
, we then have

(i)

|\gamma_{l}^{(k)}(t)|\simeq 1
, for
t\in I
and
k=0,1,2
;

(ii)

|\gamma_{l}^{(k)}(t)|\lesssim 1
, for
t\in I
and
3\leq k\leq N
;

(iii)

|((\gamma_{l}^{\prime})^{-1})(t)|\simeq 1
, for
t\simeq 1
;

(iv)

|((\gamma_{l}^{\prime})^{-1})^{(k)}(t)|\lesssim 1
, for
t\simeq 1
and
0<k<N
,

where
(\gamma_{l}^{\prime})^{-1}
is the inverse function of
\gamma_{l}^{\prime}.
Here the implicit constants depend only on
\gamma
.

Proof.

We start with the proof of (i).
Because
\gamma_{l}(t)
is increasing on
[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{l}(t)\leq\frac{\gamma(2^{-l+1})}{\gamma(2^{-l})}\leq C_{0,U}\ \ \textrm{and }\ \ \gamma_{l}(t)\geq\frac{\gamma(2^{-l-1})}{\gamma(2^{-l})}\geq C_{0,U}^{-1}.

This finishes the proof of (i) with
k=0.
Next for
k=1,2,

by the conditions (ii) and (iii) of Theorem

1.1
, we conclude that

(2.6)

\displaystyle|t^{k}\gamma_{l}^{(k)}(t)|=\bigg|\frac{(2^{-l}t)^{k}\gamma^{(k)}(2^{-l}t)}{\gamma(2^{-l})}\bigg|\simeq\bigg|\frac{\gamma(2^{-l}t)}{\gamma(2^{-l})}\bigg|=|\gamma_{l}(t)|.

Then we have

|\gamma_{l}^{(k)}(t)|\simeq|\frac{\gamma_{l}(t)}{t^{k}}|\simeq 1.
This finishes the proof of (i).

Next we prove (ii). By the condition (iii) of Theorem

1.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle|t^{k}\gamma_{l}^{(k)}(t)|=\bigg|\frac{(2^{-l}t)^{k}\gamma^{(k)}(2^{-l}t)}{\gamma(2^{-l})}\bigg|\lesssim\bigg|\frac{\gamma(2^{-l}t)}{\gamma(2^{-l})}\bigg|=|\gamma_{l}(t)|,

which further leads to

|\gamma_{l}^{(k)}(t)|\lesssim|\gamma_{l}(t)|t^{-k}\simeq 1.

Finally, we prove (iii) and (iv). The case
k=0
follows from the doubling property of
\gamma^{\prime}
in Lemma
2.1
. The case
k>0
follows from (i), (ii) and (iii) via elementary computations involving inverse functions, and we leave out the details.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the above assumptions on
\gamma
, we obtain

(i)

For each
C>0,
we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}(Ct)\lesssim_{C}\gamma^{-1}(t)

for all
t\geq 0
;

(ii)

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0

so that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}(2^{-k})\gamma^{-1}(2^{-j})\lesssim 2^{-c_{1}(k+j)}

for all
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
j+k\geq 0,

and

(2.9)

\displaystyle 2^{-c_{2}(k+j)}\lesssim\gamma^{-1}(2^{-k})\gamma^{-1}(2^{-j})

for all
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
j+k\leq 0,

where
c_{1},c_{2}
depend only on
C_{0,U}
.

Proof.

The first item (i) follows immediately from the doubling property of
\gamma
. We will therefore only present the proof of (ii). Firstly, we will prove
\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\geq C_{0,U}^{j+k}
for all
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\leq 1
. It suffices to show
\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\geq\min\{C_{0,L}^{j+k},C_{0,U}^{j+k}\}
for all
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, by symmetry, we should only to show the above inequality for
j\geq k
. There are the following four cases:

If
j,k\geq 0
, we have
\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\geq C_{0,L}^{j}C_{0,L}^{k}\gamma(1)^{2}=C_{0,L}^{j+k}
;

If
j,k\leq 0
, we have
\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\geq C_{0,U}^{j}C_{0,U}^{k}\gamma(1)^{2}=C_{0,U}^{j+k}
;

If
j\geq 0
,
k\leq 0
and
j+k\geq 0
, we have

\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})=\frac{\gamma(2^{k+j}2^{-j})}{\gamma(2^{-j})}\geq C_{0,L}^{k+j};

If
j\geq 0
,
k\leq 0
and
j+k\leq 0
, we have

\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})=\frac{\gamma(2^{k+j}2^{-j})}{\gamma(2^{-j})}\geq C_{0,U}^{k+j}.

Altogether, we have shown
\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\geq C_{0,U}^{j+k}
holds for all
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\leq 1
.
Then, for all
a,b>0
and
\gamma(a)\gamma(b)\leq 1
, we can choose

such that
a\in[2^{j},2^{j+1})
and
b\in[2^{k},2^{k+1})
. Furthermore, from the fact that

is increasing on

we conclude that

\displaystyle\gamma(a)\gamma(b)

\displaystyle\geq\gamma(2^{j})\gamma(2^{k})\geq C_{0,U}^{j+k}\simeq C_{0,U}^{j+k+2}=(2^{j+1}2^{k+1})^{\log_{2}C_{0,U}}\geq(ab)^{\log_{2}C_{0,U}}.

Therefore, for all
a,b>0
and
\gamma(a)\gamma(b)\leq 1,
we obtain

ab\lesssim(\gamma(a)\gamma(b))^{c_{1}}

with

.
Let
a=\gamma^{-1}(2^{-k})
and
b=\gamma^{-1}(2^{-j})
, for all

and

we then have

\gamma^{-1}(2^{-k})\gamma^{-1}(2^{-j})\lesssim 2^{-c_{1}(k+j)}.
The proof of (
2.9
) is similar as (
2.8
) and we omit it. This finishes the proof of (ii).
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
, we define a function
\rho(x)
on
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the following way: For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\rho(x)=\begin{cases}\gamma(|x_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}+|x_{2}|,&x_{1}\neq 0;\\
|x_{2}|,&x_{1}=0.\end{cases}

For all
r>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we also define
B(x_{0},r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ \rho(x-x_{0})<r\}
as a ball centered at
x_{0}
with radius
r>0
associated with
\rho
.

We have the following lemma, which says that the function
\rho
gives rise to a quasi-distance function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

One has that the following quasi-triangle inequality

(2.10)

\displaystyle\rho(x+y)\leq C_{0,U}(\rho(x)+\rho(y))

holds for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover, we have the following doubling condition

(2.11)

\displaystyle|B(x,2r)|\lesssim|B(y,r)|

holds for all
r>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. As a result,
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\rho,dx)
is a homogeneous type space.

Proof.

We first prove (
2.10
). Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
, by Definition
2.4
, it suffices to show

\gamma(|x_{1}+y_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}\leq C_{0,U}\big(\gamma(|x_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}+\gamma(|y_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}\big).

By monotonicity of
\gamma
, it is enough to prove

\gamma\big((|x_{1}|+|y_{1}|)^{-1}\big)^{-1}\leq C_{0,U}\big(\gamma(|x_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}+\gamma(|y_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}\big).

Without loss of generality, we assume
|x_{1}|\geq|y_{1}|
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma\big((|x_{1}|+|y_{1}|)^{-1}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\big((2|x_{1}|)^{-1}\big)^{-1}\leq C_{0,U}\gamma\big(|x_{1}|^{-1}\big)^{-1}\leq C_{0,U}\big(\gamma(|x_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}+\gamma(|y_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}\big),

where we used the doubling property of
\gamma
in Lemma
2.1
. This finishes the proof of (
2.10
).

Next we show (
2.11
).
Without loss of generality, we assume
x=y=0
.
Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying
\rho(z)<2r
, it suffices to show there exists a constant
C
such that

\rho(z/C)<r.

Note that

\rho(z/2^{j})=\gamma(2^{j}|z_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}+2^{-j}|z_{2}|,
by the doubling property of
\gamma
in Lemma
2.1
, we have
\gamma(2^{j}|z_{1}|^{-1})\geq C_{0,L}^{j}\gamma(|z_{1}|^{-1}).

Then

\rho(z/2^{j})\leq C_{0,L}^{-j}\gamma(|z_{1}|^{-1})^{-1}+2^{-j}|z_{2}|\leq\max\{C_{0,L}^{-j},2^{-j}\}\rho(z).

Choose
j
satisfying
\max\{C_{0,L}^{-j},2^{-j}\}\leq 1/2
, which leads to
\rho(z/2^{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho(z)<r.

∎

\fint_{Q_{x}}\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{|k+r_{x}|>N}\bigg|m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{Q_{x}^{*}}f_{k})(y)-\fint_{Q_{x}}m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{Q_{x}^{*}}f_{k})(z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,dy,

and
\vec{T}(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(x)
can be written as

\fint_{Q_{x}}\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{|k+r_{x}|>N}\bigg|m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(y)-\fint_{Q_{x}}m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,dy.

By the triangle inequality, it suffices to show

(4.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\vec{T}(\{\chi_{Q_{x}^{*}}f_{k}\})(x)\lesssim F\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{\infty}

and

(4.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\vec{T}(\{\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k}\})(x)\lesssim F\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{\infty}.

We first show (
4.11
). From the triangle inequality and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Fubini’s theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle\vec{T}(\{\chi_{Q_{x}^{*}}f_{k}\})(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q_{x}|^{-1/2}\bigg(\sum_{k}\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\big|m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{Q_{x}^{*}}f_{k})(\cdot)\big|\bigg\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim F|Q_{x}|^{-1/2}\bigg(\int_{Q_{x}^{*}}\sum_{k}|f_{k}(y)|^{2}\,dy\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim F\sup_{y}\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}(y)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

We next show (
4.12
). By Lemma
4.2
, for all
y,z\in Q_{x}
and
u\in I,
we have

\displaystyle\big|m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(y)-m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(z)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{k}^{u}(x,y,z)\sup_{w}|f_{k}(w)|\lesssim GC_{0,U}^{l}2^{-\delta|k+r_{x}|}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{\infty}.

As a result, we find that, for all
y\in Q_{x}
and
u\in I,

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{|k+r_{x}|>N}\bigg|m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(y)-\fint_{Q_{x}}m_{k}(u,D)(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{0,U}^{l}G2^{-\delta N}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{\infty},

which in turn implies

\sup_{x}\vec{T}(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(x)\lesssim C_{0,U}^{l}G2^{-\delta N}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{\infty}.

Choose
N=\frac{1}{\delta}\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)
such that
C_{0,U}^{l}G2^{-\delta N}=F
, where
\delta
is the one in (
4.5
). Then we conclude that

\sup_{x}\vec{T}(\chi_{(Q_{x}^{*})^{\complement}}f_{k})(x)\lesssim F\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{\infty}.

This finishes the proof of of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Now we use Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
to prove our key Lemma
4.4
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that there exist constants
F
and
G
such that
a_{k}
satisfies

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\big|a_{k}(u,D)f\big|\bigg\|_{q}\leq F\|f\|_{q}\ \ \textrm{and}\ \ \big|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a_{k}(u,\xi)\big|\leq G

for
q\in\{2,\infty\}
,
u\in I,

\xi\in K_{1}\times K_{2}
,
|\alpha|\leq 6
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{k}\big|m_{k}(u,D)f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg(1+\big(\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)\big)^{1/2-1/p}\bigg)F\|f\|_{p}.

Proof.

By scaling, it suffices to show

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{k}\big|m_{k}(u,D_{1},2^{-v}D_{2})f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg(1+\big(\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)\big)^{1/2-1/p}\bigg)F\|f\|_{p}.

By the fact that

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{k}\big|m_{k}(u,D_{1},2^{-v}D_{2})f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}=\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{k}\big|m_{k-v}(u,D_{1},2^{-v}D_{2})f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p},

we should only to show

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{k}\big|m_{k-v}(u,D_{1},2^{-v}D_{2})f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg(1+\big(\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)\big)^{1/2-1/p}\bigg)F\|f\|_{p}.

Therefore, without loss of generality, we can assume
v=0
and show

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{k}\big|m_{k}(u,D)f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg(1+\big(\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)\big)^{1/2-1/p}\bigg)F\|f\|_{p}.

By Fatou’s lemma, it suffices to show, for all
M\geq 1,

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{|k|\leq M}\big|m_{k}(u,D)f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg(1+\big(\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)\big)^{1/2-1/p}\bigg)F\|f\|_{p},

where the implicit constant is independent of
M
.

By density we can assume
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Define

\vec{T}_{M}f=\{m_{k}(u,D)f\}_{|k|\leq M}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},L^{\infty}l^{2}_{M})\bigcap C(\mathbb{R}^{2},L^{\infty}l^{2}_{M}),

By Lemma
3.7
, it suffices to show

\Big\|M^{\sharp}_{\textrm{dyad}}\vec{T}_{M}f\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg(1+\big(\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)\big)^{1/2-1/p}\bigg)F\|f\|_{p}.

Choose
N=\frac{1}{\delta}\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)
and define

S_{1}f(x)=\fint_{Q_{x}}\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{|k+r_{x}|\leq N}\bigg|m_{k}(u,D)f(y)-\fint_{Q_{x}}m_{k}(u,D)f(z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,dy

and

S_{2}f(x)=\fint_{Q_{x}}\sup_{u\in I}\bigg(\sum_{|k+r_{x}|>N}\bigg|m_{k}(u,D)f(y)-\fint_{Q_{x}}m_{k}(u,D)f(z)\,dz\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,dy.

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we can find a dyadic cube
x\in Q_{x}
such that

M^{\sharp}\vec{T}_{M}f(x)\leq 2S_{1}f(x)+2S_{2}f(x).

It suffices to obtain

\|S_{1}f\|_{p}\lesssim\big(\log_{2}(C_{0,U}^{l}G/F)\big)^{1/2-1/p}F\|f\|_{p}\ \ \textrm{and}\ \ \|S_{2}f\|_{p}\lesssim F\|f\|_{p},

which follows from Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
.
∎

5.
the proof of the main theorem

This section is devoted to the proof of our main Theorem
1.1
. First of all, we will apply Minkowski’s inequality and replace the sharp truncation
[-1,1]
in the definition of
H^{\gamma}
by a smooth truncation. Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
be a small number that is to be determined, its choice depends only on
\gamma
, and let
\widetilde{\varphi}:\ \mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative smooth bump function supported on
(-\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{0})
with
\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=1
for every
|t|\leq\epsilon_{0}/2
. Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
, by Minkowski’s inequality, we observe that

(5.1)

\bigg\|\int_{-1}^{1}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u(x)\gamma(t))(1-\widetilde{\varphi}(t))\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim_{\epsilon_{0}}\|f\|_{p}

for every
p>1
. Therefore, to prove the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
H^{\gamma}
, it suffices to prove the same boundedness of the operator

(5.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u(x)\gamma(t))\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{t},

which, for the sake of simplifying notation, will still be called as
H^{\gamma}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
u>0
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a nonnegative function,
{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}\varphi\subset[-2,2]
and
\varphi=1
on
[-1,1]
. Define
\phi(t)=\varphi(t)-\varphi(2t)
, then

(5.3)

{\rm supp}{\hskip 1.42271pt}\phi\subset\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\ 1/2\leq|t|\leq 2\right\},\

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi_{k}(t)=1
for all
t\neq 0
,

where
\phi_{k}(t)=\phi(2^{-k}t)
. Let
\tilde{\phi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\backslash 0)
such that
\tilde{\phi}=1
on
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\ 1/4\leq|t|\leq 4\}
. We then define
P_{k}^{2}f(x)=(\phi_{k}(\xi_{2})\widehat{f}(\xi))^{\vee}(x)
and
\tilde{P}_{k}^{2}f(x)=(\tilde{\phi}_{k}(\xi_{2})\widehat{f}(\xi))^{\vee}(x)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

To proceed, we will reduce the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
H^{\gamma}
to a square function estimate.
Recall
u:\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz norm
\|u\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq 1/2
. This further implies that
\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq 1/2\
for every
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
1<p<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{k}(1-\tilde{P}^{2}_{k})H^{\gamma}P_{k}^{2}f\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}

for every Schwartz function
f
.

Proof (of Lemma
5.1
).

This lemma follows from Theorem 1.1 in
[
15
]
, which further relies on Jones’ beta numbers
[
22
]
. In the special case where
\gamma
is a monomial, such a lemma was already proved and used in
[
15
]
. The proof of the general case is essentially the same. For the sake of completeness, we still include the proof here.

We first write

(5.4)

(1-\tilde{P}^{2}_{k})H^{\gamma}P^{2}_{k}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\tilde{P}^{2}_{k})\big[P^{2}_{k}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u(x)\gamma(t))\big]\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.

By Minkowski’s inequality, we have

(5.5)

\Big\|(1-\tilde{P}^{2}_{k})H^{\gamma}P^{2}_{k}f\Big\|_{p}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\|(1-\tilde{P}^{2}_{k})\big[P^{2}_{k}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u(x)\gamma(t))\big]\Big\|_{p}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{|t|}.

By
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, the last display can be further bounded by

(5.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\|\|f(x_{1}-t,x_{2})\|_{L^{p}_{x_{2}}}\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\gamma(t)\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{x_{1}}}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{|t|}.

Note that
\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\gamma(t)\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq|\gamma(t)|\|u\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}
. Therefore, (
5.6
) is bounded by

(5.7)

\|f\|_{p}\|u\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\gamma(t)|\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{|t|}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}\|u\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}.

In the last step, we used the fact that
|\gamma(t)|\lesssim|t|^{\log_{2}C_{0,L}}
, which is a simple consequence of (
2.1
).
∎

Lemma
5.1
tells us that in order to prove the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
H^{\gamma}
, it suffices to consider the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the “diagonal” term:

(5.8)

\bigg\|\sum_{k}\tilde{P}^{2}_{k}H^{\gamma}P^{2}_{k}f\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}

for every
p>1
. By the Littlewood-Paley theory and the vector-valued estimates for the maximal operator, it suffices to prove

(5.9)

\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|H^{\gamma}P^{2}_{k}f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

The proof of this square function estimate will occupy the rest of the paper. In order to bound such a square function, we will first apply a dyadic decomposition in the
t
variable. For every
u\in\mathbb{R}
, define

(5.10)

A_{u,l}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\phi(2^{l}t)\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.

We use notations
u(x)=u_{x}
. With these notations, we can write
H^{\gamma}P^{2}_{k}f
as a sum of
A_{u_{x},l}P^{2}_{k}f
over
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. For each fixed
k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we will split the sum over
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
into two parts: A high frequency part and a low frequency part. The intuition behind the splitting is quite standard: If
l
is “large”, that is,
t\simeq 2^{-l}
is “small”, then by the uncertainty principle applied to the second variable, the term
u\gamma
can be treated as a small perturbation since
P^{2}_{k}f
is essentially constant at the scale
2^{-k}
in the second variable; if
l
is “small”, that is,
t
is “large”, then we will be able to make use of oscillations to obtain certain exponential decay in
l
, via the local smoothing estimates that were established in the following Lemma
5.4
.

Let us proceed with the decomposition in
l
described above. For
v\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, define

(5.11)

E_{v}=\{k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}:\ 2^{v+k}\gamma(\epsilon_{0}/2)\geq 1\}.

Define
v_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
2^{-v_{x}}u_{x}\in[1,2)
.
We see that if
k\notin E_{v_{x}}
, then

(5.12)

2^{v_{x}+k}\gamma(\epsilon_{0}/2)\simeq|u(x)|2^{k}\gamma(\epsilon_{0}/2)\lesssim 1,

and as a consequence, for every choice of
l
, the term
u\gamma
can be treated as a perturbation when considering
P^{2}_{k}f
. To be more precise, we have

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
k\notin E_{v_{x}}
, we have the pointwise estimate

(5.13)

\big|H^{\gamma}P_{k}^{2}f(x)\big|\lesssim H_{1}^{*}P_{k}^{2}f(x)+M_{S}P_{k}^{2}f(x).

Here and hereafter,
H_{1}^{*}
denotes the maximal Hilbert transform applied in the first variable,
M_{S}
denotes the composition of some
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, which are the maximal function applied in the first and second variables, respectively.

Next, for
v,k\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, define

(5.14)

l_{0}=l_{0}(v,k)\in\mathbb{Z}\ \text{ such that }\ 2^{v+k}\gamma(2^{-l_{0}})\simeq 1.

There may be multiple choices of
l_{0}
, we here pick an arbitrary one. Similar to Lemma
5.2
, we have the following pointwise estimate, i.e., Lemma
5.3
. All of these two lemmas will be proved in Section
6
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
k\in E_{v_{x}}
, it holds that

(5.15)

\bigg|\sum_{l\geq l_{0}(v_{x},k)}A_{u_{x},l}P^{2}_{k}f(x)\bigg|\lesssim H_{1}^{*}P^{2}_{k}f(x)+M_{S}P^{2}_{k}f(x).

By Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
and the vector-valued estimates for
H_{1}^{*}
and
M_{S}
, to prove (
5.9
), it remains to prove

(5.16)

\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}\chi_{\{k\in E_{v_{x}}\}}\bigg|\sum_{l\leq l_{0}(v_{x},k)}A_{u_{x},l}P^{2}_{k}f(x)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}

for every
p>1
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
P_{k}^{1}f(x)=(\phi_{k}(\xi_{1})\widehat{f}(\xi))^{\vee}(x)
.
Now we carry out a last decomposition, which is a Littlewood-Paley decomposition in the first variable of
f
, write

(5.17)

A_{u_{x},l}P^{2}_{k}f(x)=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}}A_{u_{x},l}P^{1}_{k^{\prime}}P^{2}_{k}f(x).

For each fixed
u
, the Fourier multiplier of the convolution operator
A_{u,l}
is given by

(5.18)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(t\xi+u\gamma(t)\eta)}\phi(2^{l}t)\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(2^{-l}t\xi+u\gamma(2^{-l}t)\eta)}\phi(t)\widetilde{\varphi}(2^{-l}t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.

Assume that
|\xi|\simeq 2^{k^{\prime}}
and
|\eta|\simeq 2^{k}
. In order for the multiplier to have “non-trivial” contributions, it should admit a critical point in the region where
|t|\simeq 2^{-l}
, that is,

(5.19)

\xi+u\gamma^{\prime}(t)\eta=0\ \text{ for some }\ |t|\simeq 2^{-l}.

This leads to

(5.20)

2^{k^{\prime}}\simeq|u|\gamma^{\prime}(2^{-l})2^{k}.

In other words, for fixed
u,k
and
l
, in the sum over
k^{\prime}
in (
5.17
), only those terms satisfying (
5.20
) will have non-trivial contributions; all other terms can be handled via elementary methods like integration by parts. Therefore, to prove (
5.16
), we will only prove

(5.21)

\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}\chi_{\{k\in E_{v_{x}}\}}\bigg|\sum_{l\leq l_{0}(v_{x},k)}A_{u_{x},l}P^{1}_{k_{1}}P^{2}_{k}f(x)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p},

where

(5.22)

k_{1}=k_{1}(v_{x},k,l)\in\mathbb{Z}\ \ \text{is such that}\ \ 2^{k_{1}}\simeq 2^{v_{x}}2^{k}\gamma^{\prime}(2^{-l}).

In the following part, for ease of notation, we will compress the dependence of
k_{1}
on
x,k
and
l
. We will emphasize such dependence whenever necessary. Moreover, if
x
and
k
are clear from the context, we will also leave out the dependence of
l_{0}
on
x
and
k
.

One key step in the proof of (
5.20
) is the following estimate of local smoothing type, which originally only holds for
p>2
. Here we have a same estimate for every
p>1
due to the Lipschitz assumption on
u
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For every
p>1
, it holds that

(5.23)

\Big\|\chi_{\{k\in E_{v_{x}}\}}A_{u_{x},l_{0}-l}P^{1}_{k_{1}}P^{2}_{k}f(x)\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\delta_{p}l}\|f\|_{p}

for every
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, where
\delta_{p}>0
is a small constant,
k_{1}=k_{1}(v_{x},k,l_{0}-l)
and
l_{0}=l_{0}(v_{x},k)
.

Note that by Fubini’s theorem, the desired estimate (
5.21
) at
p=2
follows immediately from Lemma
5.4
.

The proof of Lemma
5.4
will be given in Section
7
, we now make one remark. Recall that the phase function of the multiplier in (
5.18
), given by

(5.24)

2^{-(l_{0}-l)}t\xi+u_{x}\gamma(2^{-(l_{0}-l)}t)\eta.

Note that for every
t
in the support of
\phi
, it holds that
|t|\simeq 1
; for every
(\xi,\eta)
in the Fourier support of
P^{1}_{k_{1}}P^{2}_{k}f
, it holds that

(5.25)

|\xi|\simeq 2^{k_{1}}\simeq|u_{x}|2^{k}\gamma^{\prime}(2^{-(l_{0}-l)})\ \ \textrm{and}\ \ |\eta|\simeq 2^{k}.

Let us show that both of the terms in (
5.24
) have scales bigger than one. This is another way of saying that our phase function (
5.24
) may oscillate very fast; local smoothing estimates of Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge
[
27
]
allow us to explore this high oscillation and obtain a favourable decay estimate as in the above lemma. That both terms in (
5.24
) are of scale bigger than one is trivial in the case when
\gamma
is a monomial. In the general case, the assumptions we made on
\gamma
in Theorem
1.1
will appear. Let us be more precise. First, note that

(5.26)

|u_{x}|\gamma(2^{-l_{0}+l}t)|\eta|\geq|u_{x}|\gamma(2^{-l_{0}})|\eta|\gtrsim 1,

which follows from the monotonicity assumption on
\gamma
and the definition of
l_{0}
in (
5.14
). The other term is the more interesting one. We need to show that

(5.27)

2^{-l_{0}+l}|u_{x}|2^{k}\gamma^{\prime}(2^{-l_{0}+l})\gtrsim 1,

which follows from applying item (ii) in the statement of Theorem
1.1
with
j=1
.

So far we have finished the proof of (
5.21
) at
p=2
, which leads to the main Theorem
1.1
at
p=2
. Next, we consider
p\neq 2
. It turns out that the case
p<2
and the case
p>2
require entirely different tools. The case
p<2
is easier, and can be handled essentially via the interpolation and the bootstrapping argument of Nagel, Stein and Wainger
[
28
]
. The case
p>2
is much more complicated, and would require a vector-valued version of the
L^{p}
orthogonality principle of Seeger
[
31
]
, we here will prove this vector-valued result in a general homogeneous type space instead of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Homogeneous spaces were introduced
by Coifman and Weiss
[
13
]
, who discovered that the “spaces of
homogeneous type” are the metric spaces to which the Calder
\acute{\textrm{o}}
n-Zygmund
theory extends naturally. It would be interesting to find an approach that works for every
p
simultaneously.

Let us first look at the easier case
p<2
. By interpolation with the exponential decay estimate in Lemma
5.4
and the Littlewood-Paley inequality, it suffices to prove that

(5.28)

\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}\chi_{\{k\in E_{v_{x}}\}}\bigg|A_{u_{x},l_{0}-l}P^{1}_{k_{1}}P^{2}_{k}f(x)\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|P^{2}_{k}f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}

for every
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. Here
k_{1}=k_{1}(v_{x},k,l_{0}-l)
and
l_{0}=l_{0}(v_{x},k)
. In order to prove the above vector-valued estimates, we will appeal to the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the maximal operators that were established in
[
26
]
. Indeed, one can also apply Lemma
5.4
and recover the above result. Define

(5.29)

M^{\gamma}f(x)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}|f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u(x)\gamma(t))|\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,dt.

It was proven in
[
26
]
that, under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
, it holds that

(5.30)

\|M^{\gamma}f\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}

for every
p>1
. Moreover, one can upgrade such scalar-valued bound into a vector-valued bound for free. To be more precise, we also have that

(5.31)

\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|M^{\gamma}f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}

for every
1<p\leq 2
. To show (
5.28
), we observe the following pointwise estimate

(5.32)

\big|A_{u_{x},l_{0}-l}P^{1}_{k_{1}}P^{2}_{k}f(x)\big|\lesssim M^{\gamma}M_{S}P^{2}_{k}f(x).

Therefore, the desired estimate (
5.28
) follows immediately from (
5.31
) and the vector-valued estimates for the strong maximal operator.

In the remaining part, we focus on the case
p>2
. By interpolating with the
L^{2}
bounds in Lemma

Lemma 5.6

and

Let us begin with the proof of Lemma

. We fix

and

From

We write (

(6.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}P^{2}_{k}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-u_{x}\gamma(t)-z)2^{k}\widecheck{\tilde{\phi}}(2^{k}z)\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,dz\,\frac{dt}{t}\\

Recall that

, we see from it that (

) is bounded by the strong maximal function
M_{S}P_{k}^{2}f
. This finishes the proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Next we prove Lemma
5.3
. Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we abbreviate

to

, and then

(6.6)

\sum_{l\geq l_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}P^{2}_{k}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2})\phi_{l}(t)\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,\frac{dt}{t},

(6.8)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\tau\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}(1+\tau)^{-100}\int_{|t|\lesssim 2^{-l_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|P^{2}_{k}f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-z)|2^{k}\chi_{\{\tau 2^{-k}\leq|z|\leq(\tau+1)2^{-k}\}}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)\,dz\frac{2^{k}|u_{x}||\gamma(t)|}{|t|}\,dt\\

. This finishes our proof.

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sup_{u\in[1,2]}\bigg(\sum_{k\in J_{i}\bigcap E_{v}}\big|A_{2^{v}u,l_{0}-l}P_{k_{1}}^{1}P_{k}^{2}g\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim(l+1)^{5}\|g\|_{p}.

Note that

\displaystyle P_{k_{1}}^{1}P_{k}^{2}g=P_{k_{1}}^{1}P_{k}^{2}f~\textrm{for~ all}~k\in J_{i},\ \ \textrm{and}\ \ \ \|g\|_{p}\lesssim\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k\in J_{i}\bigcap E_{v}}\big|\tilde{P}_{k_{1}}^{1}\tilde{P}_{k}^{2}f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}.

For all
i=1,2,3,
we have

\bigg\|\sup_{u\in[1,2]}\bigg(\sum_{k\in J_{i}\bigcap E_{v}}\big|A_{2^{v}u,l_{0}-l}P_{k_{1}}^{1}P_{k}^{2}f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}\lesssim(l+1)^{5}\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{k\in J_{i}\bigcap E_{v}}\big|\tilde{P}_{k_{1}}^{1}\tilde{P}_{k}^{2}f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\|_{p}.

Then (
5.38
) follows by the triangle inequality.

Tensor product of holomorphic discrete series representations of U ⁡ ( p , q ) U(p,q) and quivers
Velleda Baldoni and Michèle Vergne

Abstract
Consider the convex cone of holomorphic orbits in the Lie algebra of U ⁡ ( p , q ) U(p,q) . As in the classical case of Hermitian matrices, the set of elliptic orbits obtained contained in the sum of two holomorphic orbits can be described by “Horn inequalities”. Using representations of quivers, we give another proof of these Horn recursion inequalities obtained by P-E Paradan. We recall the implications of these inequalities for decomposition of tensor product of two representations of the holomorphic discrete series of the group U ⁡ ( p , q ) U(p,q) .

Introduction

Let
(\lambda,\mu)
be a couple of dominant weights for the compact group
G=U(n)
.
One wants to describe the irreducible representations
V_{\nu}
of
G
occurring
with non zero multiplicity
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
in
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}
. Here
V_{\lambda},V_{\mu},V_{\nu}
are the irreducible representations of
U(n)
(or
GL(n)
)
with highest weights
\lambda,\mu,\nu
respectively.

Let
\operatorname{Lie}(G)
be the Lie algebra of
G
. Then the space
\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Lie}(G)
is the space of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices.
Let
T
be the Cartan subgroup of
G
consisting of
n\times n
diagonal matrices with entries of modulus
1
.
If

a=(a(1),\ldots,a(n))
is a sequence of
n
real numbers.
we denote also by
a\in\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Lie}(T)
the diagonal
n\times n
Hermitian matrix with coefficients
a(i)
on the diagonal.
Using the inner product
\operatorname{tr}(AB)
on matrices,
we may consider
a
as an element of
(\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Lie}(G))^{*}
.
Then the Weyl chamber
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}=\{a=(a(1)\geq a(2)\geq\cdots\geq a(n))\}
is a set of representatives of the coadjoint orbits of
G
in
(\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Lie}(G))^{*}
.
Denote by
O_{a}
the coadjoint orbit of
a
.
The orbit
O_{a}
is provided with
a
G
-invariant Kähler structure.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda(1)\geq\lambda(2)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda(n))
be a dominant weight, that is an element of
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
with integral coefficients.
Since the representation
V_{\lambda}
of
U(n)
is obtained by geometric quantization of the corresponding coadjoint orbit
O_{\lambda}
, a geometric analogue of the property
V_{\nu}\subset V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}
for
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
a triple of dominant weights
is the property

O_{c}\subset O_{a}+O_{b}
for
(a,b,c)
a triple of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices.
Let
\operatorname{Cone}(n)\subset{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}\oplus{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}\oplus{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
be the set of such triples
(a,b,c)
.
The cone
\operatorname{Cone}(n)
is a polyhedral cone, and A. Horn (
12
) conjectured a series of recursive inequalities describing this cone.
Knutson-Tao (
14
)
proved Horn conjecture together with the famous saturation conjecture: one has
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\neq 0
if and only if
there exists an integer
N\geq 1
such that

c^{N\nu}_{N\lambda,N\mu}\neq 0
.
Equivalently
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\neq 0
if and only if
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\in\operatorname{Cone}(n)
.

Consider now the non compact group
G=U(p,q)\subset GL(p+q)
of pseudo-unitary transformations, and let
n=p+q
.
The group
G
has a compact Cartan subgroup
T
: the space of
n\times n
diagonal matrices with entries of modulus one.
There is a series
\pi_{\lambda}
of unitary irreducible representations of
G

called the holomorphic discrete series associated to a subset of characters of
T
.
Such a representation
\pi_{\lambda}
is similarly indexed by a sequence of slowly decreasing integers

\lambda=(\lambda(1)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda(p)\geq\lambda(p+1)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda(p+q)),

with a gap between
\lambda(p)
and
\lambda(p+1)
(see Section
4
, Ex.
4
).
It is easy to prove that the tensor product of two representations
\pi_{\lambda}
and
\pi_{\mu}
of the holomorphic discrete series decomposes as a sum with finite multiplicities of representations

\pi_{\nu}
belonging to the holomorphic discrete series:

\pi_{\lambda}\otimes\pi_{\mu}=\oplus_{\nu}m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)\pi_{\nu}.

(1)

Thus a natural question is to describe the triples
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
such that

\pi_{\nu}\subset\pi_{\lambda}\otimes\pi_{\mu}
.

Let

A=(A(1)\geq\cdots\geq A(p)\geq A(p+1)\geq\cdots\geq A(p+q))

be a sequence of slowly decreasing real numbers. We also denote by
A\in\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Lie}(T)

the diagonal matrix with coefficients
A(i)
on the diagonal. Using again the bilinear form
\operatorname{tr}(AB)
, we may consider
A
as an element of

(\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Lie}(G))^{*}
.
Let
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{A}
be the corresponding (non compact) coadjoint orbit of
A
by the action of
U(p,q)
. Then
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{A}
is provided with
a
U(p,q)
-invariant Kähler structure and we say that
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{A}
is an holomorphic orbit.
The representation
\pi_{\lambda}
of
U(p,q)
of the holomorphic discrete series is obtained by geometric quantization of the corresponding (non compact) coadjoint holomorphic orbit
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
.
So the geometric analogue of the property
\pi_{\nu}\subset\pi_{\lambda}\otimes\pi_{\mu}
is the property
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{C}\subset{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{A}+{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{B}
.
Let
\operatorname{Cone}_{\operatorname{hol}}(p,q)
be the set of such triples
(A,B,C)
.

In (
17
), P-E Paradan described inductive necessary and sufficient conditions
for
(A,B,C)
to belong to
\operatorname{Cone}_{\operatorname{hol}}(p,q)
, similar to the Horn conditions in the definite case.
Using Derksen-Weyman saturation theorem (
6
), one deduces
that the saturation conjecture holds for discrete series representations: there exists an integer
N\geq 1
such that

m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{N\nu}(N\lambda,N\mu)\neq 0
if and only if

m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)\neq 0
.
So inequalities for the cone
\operatorname{Cone}_{\operatorname{hol}}(p,q)
give inductive necessary and sufficient conditions
for
m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)
to be non zero.

In this article, we give another proof of Paradan conditions by relating
m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)
to quivers.
One benefit is that the inductive conditions appear very naturally in this setting. Furthermore, some symmetries are visible which are not seen in the initial problem (see Proposition
2
).
We also sketch another proof (based on Rossmann formula for Fourier transform of orbits (
4
)) that the cone generated by the triples

(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
with
m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)>0
is equal to the cone
\operatorname{Cone}_{\operatorname{hol}}(p,q)
, as it should be.

Recall the general quiver setting.
Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a quiver, where
Q_{0}
is the finite set of vertices and
Q_{1}
the finite set of arrows.
A
dimension vector
for
Q
is a vector
{\bf{n}}=(n_{x})_{x\in Q_{0}}
of nonnegative integers
(if vertices are labeled
\{1,2,\ldots,q_{0}\}
,
we adopt also the notation
{\bf{n}}=[n_{1},\ldots,n_{q_{0}}]
).
We consider the family of complex vector spaces
{\mathcal{E}}=(E_{x})_{x\in Q_{0}}
with
E_{x}={\mathbb{C}}^{n_{x}}.

The space of
representations
of the quiver
Q
on
{\mathcal{E}}
is given by

{\mathcal{H}}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{a:x\to y\in Q_{1}}\operatorname{Hom}(E_{x},E_{y}).

The Lie group
\operatorname{GL}_{Q}({\bf{n}})=\prod_{x\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{GL}(n_{x})
acts naturally on
{\mathcal{H}}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})
, and so acts naturally on the space
\operatorname{Sym}^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}}))
of polynomial functions on
{\mathcal{H}}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})
.
Decompose

\operatorname{Sym}^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}}))=\bigoplus_{\bm{\lambda}}m_{Q}(\bm{\lambda})V_{\bm{\lambda}},

where
V_{\bm{\lambda}}
denotes the irreducible representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{Q}({\bf n})
with highest weight
\bm{\lambda}
.
If
Q
has no cycles, the multiplicity
m_{Q}(\bm{\lambda})
is finite.

When
Q
is the Horn quiver
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}

1\rightarrow 3\leftarrow 2

(2)

and the dimension vector is
\bm{n}=[n,n,n]
, then
m_{Q}([\lambda,\mu,\nu])=c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu^{*}}
where
\nu^{*}=(-\nu(n)\geq\cdots\geq-\nu(1))

indexes the dual representation
V_{\nu}^{*}
.

One can decide if
m_{Q}(\bm{\lambda})>0
by studying the Schubert positions of general subrepresentations of a representation of
Q
on
{\mathcal{E}}
. One obtains a natural inductive criterium (
3
) which coincides for the quiver
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}

with Belkale inductive criterium (
2
) for intersection of Schubert cells.

The action of the compact group
K=\operatorname{U}_{Q}({\bf{n}})=\prod_{x\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{U}(n_{x})
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})
leads to a moment map with
values in
(\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Lie}(K))^{*}
and to a “moment cone”
\operatorname{Cone}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})
parameterizing coadjoint orbits of
K
contained in the image of the moment map.
The saturation theorem of Derksen-Weyman is equivalent to the statement that
m_{Q}(\bm{\lambda})>0
if and only if
\bm{\lambda}
belongs to the moment cone
\operatorname{Cone}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})
.

2
Space of representations of a quiver

Let

We use the notation

to

We allow

to have multiple arrows between two vertices but no cycles.
Let

be a dimension vector.
We consider a family of complex vector spaces

of dimension vector

Conversely, a family

The space of

of the quiver

on

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{a:x\to y\in Q_{1}}\operatorname{Hom}(E_{x},E_{y}),

For

Let
\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}

Consider the Ringel bilinear form

Let

.
We say that

is a

for
{\mathcal{E}}.

Define the expected dimension

(4)

where

We write

.

The algorithm

A set
{\mathcal{K}}\in\operatorname{Horn}(Q,{\mathcal{E}})
produces always a valid inequality for the cone
\operatorname{Cone}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})
, namely
the inequality

E({\mathcal{K}})=\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}\sum_{k\in K_{x}}\lambda_{x}(k)\leq 0.

Thus to determine the cone
\operatorname{Cone}_{Q}({\mathcal{E}})
, we have to determine the sets
{\mathcal{K}}\subset_{Q,B}[{\bf n}]
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}=(K_{x})_{x\in Q_{0}}
. Let
{\bm{k}}=(k_{x})_{x\in Q_{0}}
be the family of cardinals of the set
K_{x}
.
It is easy to see that if
K_{x}(1)<\dots<K_{x}(k_{x})
are the elements of
K_{x}
, then the dimension of the Schubert variety determined by
{\mathcal{K}}
is

\displaystyle\dim{\bm{\Omega}}=\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{x}}\left(K_{x}(j)-j\right).

(8)

Any family
{\mathcal{L}}\subseteq[\bm{k}]
can be composed with
{\mathcal{K}}

and give rise to a family
{\mathcal{T}}=(T_{x})_{x\in Q_{0}}
of subspaces

T_{x}=\oplus_{j=1}^{l_{x}}e_{K_{x}(L_{x}(j))}\subseteq S_{x},

where
L_{x}(1)<\dots<L_{x}(l_{x})
are the elements of
L_{x}
and
l_{x}=\lvert L_{x}\rvert
.
We have thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{edim}_{Q,B}({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{E}})=\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{x}}\left(K_{x}(L_{x}(j))-j\right)\;-\!\!\!\!\sum_{a:x\to y\in Q_{1}}l_{x}(n_{y}-l_{y})

(9)

with
l_{x}=|L_{x}|
and
l_{y}=|L_{y}|
.

Accordingly, Theorem
2.2
translates into the following inductive numerical criterion:

Theorem 3.1

(
BVW
)

{\mathcal{K}}\subseteq_{Q,B}[\bm{n}]
if and only if

\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{x}}\left(K_{x}(L_{x}(j))-j\right)\;\;\geq\!\!\!\!\sum_{a:x\to y\in Q_{1}}l_{x}(n_{y}-l_{y})

(10)

for all
{\mathcal{L}}\subseteq_{Q,B}[\bm{k}]
.

The criterion in (
10
) is easy to test numerically.
One may further restrict the families
{\mathcal{L}}
that need to be considered as we will see in a moment.

In (
BVW
), we proved that it is sufficient to test on the

{\mathcal{L}}\subseteq_{Q,B}[\bm{k}]
, such that
\operatorname{edim}_{Q,B}({\mathcal{L}},\bm{k})=0.

Given a subset
{\mathcal{A}}
of the set of arrows, we defined

4
Holomorphic discrete series and tensor product

Let
G
be a connected semisimple Lie group, with finite center and maximal compact subgroup
K
. The representation of
G
in
L^{2}(G)

has a discrete series if and only of
\operatorname{rank}(G)=\operatorname{rank}(K)
. If furthermore
G/K
is an hermitian symmetric space,
within the discrete series, we have a series of representations called the holomorphic discrete series.
The realization of a representation belonging to the holomorphic discrete series is very simple both analytically and algebraically. We will though adopt the algebraic point of view.

We thus assume that
G/K
is provided with a
G
-invariant complex structure.
We start by introducing some general notation. We denote by capital latin letters the groups involved and by the corresponding german letters the corresponding Lie algebras.
We use the superscript
∗
for dual spaces and the subscript
C
for complexifications; for example, we write
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
be the Cartan decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
The complex structure of
G/K
determines the
K
-invariant decomposition
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{-}
where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{-}
are abelian subalgebras of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
. There exists
z\in\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}}
central in
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}},[z,X]=X\}
.
This element
z
is uniquely determined modulo an element of the center of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Example
G=U(2,3).

The maximal compact subgroup is
K=U(2)\times U(3)
.
The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{u}}(2,3)
in block form is given as

{\mathfrak{g}}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
b^{*}&d\\
\end{array}\right)\right\}
where
a\in{\mathfrak{u}}(2),d\in{\mathfrak{u}}(3)
,
b
is any
3\times 2
complex matrix and
b^{*}
is the matrix conjugate transpose of
b
.
The Cartan decomposition is given by
{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{u}}(2)\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}(3)=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&d\\
\end{array}\right)\right\}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&b\\
b^{*}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\right\}.

The complexification of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is
{\mathfrak{gl}}(5,{\mathbb{C}})
.
We have
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{-}

where a matrix in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}
have the block form

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&*\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right)
and a matrix in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{-}
have the block form

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
&0\\
\end{array}\right).

Each
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\pm}
is stable under the adjoint action of
K_{\mathbb{C}}=GL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\times GL(3,{\mathbb{C}})
.
More explicitly
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}

identifies to

\operatorname{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}e_{3}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}e_{4}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}e_{5},{\mathbb{C}}e_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}e_{2})

with action of
K_{\mathbb{C}}=GL(2,{\mathbb{C}})\times GL(3,{\mathbb{C}})
given by
g_{1}rg_{2}^{-1}

if
r\in\operatorname{Hom}({\mathbb{C}}e_{3}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}e_{4}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}e_{5},{\mathbb{C}}e_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}e_{2})
. Similarly for
{\mathfrak{p}}_{-}
.

Let
T
be a Cartan subgroup of
K
, thus
T
is also a Cartan subgroup of
G
.
Let
\Lambda\subset(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
be the lattice of weights of
T.
The central element
z
belongs to
\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}}
.
We denote by
W_{K}
the Weyl group of
K
.
Let
\Delta=\Delta({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}},{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}.
We write
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}{\mathfrak{g}}_{\alpha}
for the root space decomposition. If
\alpha\in\Delta
, its coroot
H_{\alpha}
is in
\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}}
and satisfies
\alpha(H_{\alpha})=2.
The roots are called compact or noncompact depending whether the root space is in
{\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathbb{C}}
or in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbb{C}}.
We denote by
\Delta_{c},\Delta_{n}
the set of compact and noncompact roots, with
\Delta_{n}
decomposed in
\Delta_{n}^{+}\cup\Delta_{n}^{-}
, according to the decomposition
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{-}
.
Choose a system
\Delta_{c}^{+}
of positive compact roots, and let
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{c}^{+}\cup\Delta_{n}^{+}
.
Let
\lambda\in(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
such that
\lambda(H_{\alpha})\geq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
. Then

\langle\lambda,z\rangle\geq 0
since
\lambda\in\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\alpha
, and
\alpha(z)=0
for
\alpha
compact,
while
\alpha(z)=1
for
\alpha
non compact positive.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda
be such that
\lambda(H_{\alpha})\geq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{c}^{+}
.
We denote by
V_{\lambda}
the irreducible representation of
K
(or
K_{\mathbb{C}}
) with highest weight
\lambda
.
Consider
\pi_{\lambda}
the generalized Verma module defined as

\pi_{\lambda}={\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes_{{\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{k}}_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{-})}V_{\lambda}

where
V_{\lambda}
is the
K
-module of highest weight
\lambda
and we extend it to be a
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{p}}_{-})
module by making
{\mathfrak{p}}_{-}
acting trivially (here
{\mathcal{U}}
stands for the universal enveloping algebra). Thus
the representation
\pi_{\lambda}
has highest weight
\lambda,
for the system
\Delta_{c}^{+}\cup-\Delta_{n}^{+}
,
and as a
K
-module,

\pi_{\lambda}=\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})\otimes V_{\lambda}.

(11)

Remark that the action of
z
in
\pi_{\lambda}
have non negative eigenvalues, namely the set of eigenvalues is
\{\langle\lambda,z\rangle+n,n\geq 0\}
, and each eigenvalue has finite multiplicity.

If
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\alpha
,
\rho_{c}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{c}^{+}}\alpha
,
\rho_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{+}}
, then the infinitesimal character of
\pi_{\lambda}
is
\lambda+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}.

If
\langle\lambda+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n},H_{\alpha}\rangle>0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
, then
Harish-Chandra has constructed a unitary irreducible representation of
G
with corresponding
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
module
\pi_{\lambda}
.
Such representation belongs to the
\bf{holomorphic\ discrete\ series}
and we denote it still by
\pi_{\lambda}.

The name holomorphic comes from the fact that it can be realized as a subspace of the space of holomorphic sections of the holomorphic bundle
G\times_{K}V_{\lambda}
on
G/K
, the condition
\langle\lambda+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n},H_{\alpha}\rangle>0
assuring the existence of square integrable holomorphic sections.

Example
U(p,q)

Consider
U(p,q)
, with
\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}}
consisting of diagonal
(p+q)\times(p+q)
matrices with real entries.
Let
h_{k}
be the diagonal
(p+q)\times(p+q)
matrix with
1
at the
k
-th place,
0
at other places. Then
(h_{k})_{k=1}^{p+q}
is a basis of
\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}}
.
We denote by
\epsilon^{k}
the dual basis.
The Weyl group
W_{K}
is the product of the permutation groups
\Sigma_{p}\times\Sigma_{q}
.

Consider the standard system of positive roots

\Delta^{+}=\{\epsilon^{i}-\epsilon^{j},1\leq i<j\leq p+q\}.

Then

\Delta_{c}^{+}=\{\epsilon^{i}-\epsilon^{j},1\leq i<j\leq p\}\cup\{\epsilon^{i}-\epsilon^{j},p+1\leq i<j\leq p+q\}.

\Delta_{n}^{+}=\{\epsilon^{i}-\epsilon^{j},1\leq i\leq p,p+1\leq j\leq p+q\}.

Then
\rho_{n}=\frac{1}{2}(q\sum_{i=1}^{p}\epsilon^{i}-p\sum_{j=1}^{q-p}\epsilon^{j+p})

and
(\rho_{n}-\rho_{c})(h_{p}-h_{p+1})=(p+q)-1
.
Let
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{p+q}\lambda_{i}\epsilon^{i}
in
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
with integral coefficients.
The condition
\langle\lambda+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n},H_{\alpha}\rangle>0
for all compact roots gives
separately

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{p},

\lambda_{p+1}\geq\lambda_{p+2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{p+q},

and
\langle\lambda+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n},H_{\epsilon^{p}-\epsilon^{p+1}}\rangle>0

gives the condition
\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{p+1}+p+q-1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be dominant weights for
K
.
Define
m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)
to be the multiplicity of the representation
V_{\nu}
in the representation
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})
of
K

Recall the following theorem
18
,
13
.

Theorem 4.1

The tensor product of two representations
\pi_{\lambda},\pi_{\mu}
belonging to the holomorphic discrete series is a direct sum, with finite multiplicities, of representations belonging to the holomorphic discrete series:

\pi_{\lambda}\otimes\pi_{\mu}=\otimes_{\nu}m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)\pi_{\nu}.

We have:

m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)=m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu).

(12)

In other words, if the parameters
\lambda,\mu
satisfy Harish-Chandra inequalities, and if
m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)
is not zero, then
the parameter
\nu
satisfy also Harish-Chandra inequalities and
m_{\operatorname{hol}}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)=m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu).

(If the parameters
\lambda,\mu
do not satisfy Harish-Chandra inequalities, then the
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
module
\pi_{\lambda}
may not be irreducible, nor unitarizable. However there is a Jordan-Hölder series for
\pi_{\lambda}\otimes\pi_{\mu}
by modules
\pi_{\nu}
and with multiplicity

m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu).
)

Let us give a rapid check (not a proof, but the proof is not difficult) of the consistency of the formula by restricting both members to
K
. The decomposition of the left hand side of Eq.
12
restricted to
K
is

(\pi_{\lambda}\otimes\pi_{\mu})_{|_{\mathfrak{k}}}=\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})\otimes V_{\lambda}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})\otimes V_{\mu}.

The decomposition of the right hand side is

\sum_{\nu}m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)\pi_{\nu}|_{K}=\sum_{\nu}m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)\left(V_{\nu}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})\right)

=\left(\sum_{\nu}m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)V_{\nu}\right)\otimes\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+}).

Now

\sum_{\nu}m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)V_{\nu}=V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})

by definition of
m_{D}^{\nu}(\lambda,\mu)
. So the right hand side is

V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\mu}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})\otimes\operatorname{Sym}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+})

and thus Eq.
12
is true with respect to the
K
action.

5
Geometric analogue

As in the preceding section, we consider an hermitian symmetric space
G/K
, and the choice
of the positive root system
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{c}^{+}\cup\Delta_{n}^{+}
.
Consider
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}=\{\lambda\in(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*},\lambda(H_{\alpha})\geq 0;\alpha\in\Delta^{+}\}
, the corresponding Weyl chamber.

In this section, we do not assume
\lambda
in the lattice of weights
.
If
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
we denote by
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\subset(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
its coadjoint orbit under the action of
G
.
Moment maps for
G
-Hamiltonian spaces (or
K
-Hamiltonian spaces) are valued in
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
(or
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})^{*}
).
In particular, if the compact group
K
acts on a complex vector space
E
provided with a
K
-invariant Hermitian form
\langle,\rangle
that we take antilinear in the first variable,
the moment map
\mu:E\to(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})^{*}
is given by
\mu(r)(X)=\langle r,Xr\rangle
, for
r\in E
and
X\in(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})
.

Recall that
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
is a (non compact)
G
-Hamiltonian manifold, the moment map
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\to(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
being the inclusion. Since
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
is provided with a
G
-invariant Kähler structure, we will call

{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
an holomorphic orbit.
The set
G{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
is a convex cone in
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
, as proved by Vinberg (
22
)
(see
11
). So the sum
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}+{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}
of two holomorphic orbits is an union of holomorphic orbits.

We denote by
{\mathfrak{c}}^{K}_{\geq 0}=\{\lambda\in(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*},\lambda(H_{\alpha})\geq 0;\alpha\in\Delta_{c}^{+}\}
,
the Weyl chamber for
K
.

Let
z
in the center of
\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}}
such that
{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbb{C}},[z,X]=X\}
.
If
f\in(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
, the value
\langle f,z\rangle
is real.
The geometric analogue of the fact that, in a representation
\pi_{\lambda}
of the holomorphic discrete series, the eigenvalues of
z
are positive
and the corresponding eigenspace is with finite multiplicity is the content of
the following proposition (see
7
).

Proposition 3

Let
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
, let
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
its coadjoint orbit, and let
f\in{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\subset(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
.
Then

1.

\langle f,z\rangle\geq\langle\lambda,z\rangle\geq 0
.

2.

For any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the set

{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}(t)=\{\xi\in{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda},\langle f,z\rangle=t\}

is a compact subset of
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
.

In other words, the function
p:{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by
p(f)=\langle f,z\rangle
is bounded from below by

\langle\lambda,z\rangle\geq 0
. Furthermore the fiber of
p
is compact.

The proof uses the decomposition of
G
as
KAK
and Harish-Chandra description of a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{p}}
by strongly orthogonal roots.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
.
The moment map for the diagonal action of
G
on the product
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}
is the addition

\lambda,\mu
in
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
, and

{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}

{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}
.

.

A({\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu})
modulo the action of
G
.

We now consider the case where
\langle\lambda,H_{\alpha}\rangle>0,\langle\mu,H_{\alpha}\rangle>0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{+}

be the (compact) coadjoint
K
-orbits of
\lambda,\mu
.

. This is a

The following proposition, due to P.E. Paradan

.

using Paradan’s proof of the “

” theorem in the case of

16

Proposition 5

Let
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
such that
\langle\lambda,H_{\alpha}\rangle>0
, and
\langle\mu,H_{\alpha}\rangle>0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{+}
.
Then

P(\lambda,\mu)={\rm Kir}(O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}).

Proof

We will work with equivariant volumes.
If
M
is a
G
-Hamiltonian space with Liouville measure
d\beta_{M}
and proper moment map
\Phi:M\to(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
,
the Duistermaat-Heckman measure of the
G
-Hamiltonian space
M
is (by definition)
\Phi_{*}d\beta_{M}
.
The
G
-equivariant volume of
M
is
the generalized function of
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
defined by

I_{M}(X)=\int_{M}e^{\langle\Phi(m),X\rangle}d\beta_{M}=\int_{(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}}e^{\langle\xi,X\rangle}\Phi_{*}(d\beta_{M}),

so is the Fourier transform of
\Phi_{*}(d\beta_{M})
.

Similarly, if
N
is a
K
-Hamiltonian space with Liouville measure
d\beta_{N}
and proper moment map
\phi:N\to(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})^{*}
,
the Duistermaat-Heckman measure of the
K
-Hamiltonian space
N
is
\phi_{*}(d\beta_{N})
.
The
K
-equivariant volume of
N
is
the generalized function of
X\in{\mathfrak{k}}
defined by

I_{N}(X)=\int_{N}e^{\langle\phi(n),X\rangle}d\beta_{N}=\int_{(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})^{*}}e^{\langle\xi,X\rangle}\phi_{*}(d\beta_{N}),

so is the Fourier transform of
\phi_{*}(d\beta_{N})
.

In the case of
M={\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}
(resp.
N=O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}
),
the measure
\Phi_{*}(d\beta_{M})
is easy to compute directly.
Equivalently, it is easy to give explicit formula for its Fourier transform.
Since the function
m\mapsto\langle\Phi(m),z\rangle
on
M
(resp.
n\mapsto\langle\phi(n),z\rangle
on
N
) is a proper map, the restriction to
{\mathfrak{t}}
(even to
{\mathbb{R}}z\subset{\mathfrak{t}}
) of the equivariant volumes of the non compact spaces
M
or
N

are well defined as generalized functions.

We will also use the Fourier transform on
{\mathfrak{t}}
and the following result.
Let
m
be a locally polynomial measure on
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
and let us consider

{\mathcal{F}}(m)(X)=\int_{\xi\in(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}}e^{\langle X,\xi\rangle}dm(\xi)

its Fourier transform as a generalized function on
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Assume that there exists a set
\{l_{k}\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*},k=1,\ldots,s\}
of non zero linear forms on
{\mathfrak{t}}

such that
\prod_{k=1}^{s}l_{k}(X){\mathcal{F}}(m)(X)
is analytic.
Assume furthermore that
m
is supported on the closed halfspace
\langle\xi,z\rangle\geq 0
.
Then the equation
\prod_{k=1}^{s}l_{k}{\mathcal{F}}(m)=0
imply that
{\mathcal{F}}(m)=0
, thus
m=0
.

Let
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}
.
Let

I_{\lambda}(X)=\int_{{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}}e^{\langle\xi,X\rangle}d\beta_{\lambda}

be the equivariant volume of
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
, that is
the Fourier transform of the orbit
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
with its Liouville measure
d\beta_{\lambda}
.
The equivariant volume of the
G
-Hamiltonian space
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}

is the product
I_{\lambda}(X)I_{\mu}(X)
of the equivariant volumes
I_{\lambda}(X)
,
I_{\mu}(X)
of
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}
and
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}
.
The product is well defined since the addition map
A:{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}+{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}\to(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}

is a proper map.
Let
d\beta=d\beta_{\lambda}d\beta_{\mu}
be the product of the Liouville measures.
Then

I_{\lambda}(X)I_{\mu}(X)

=\int_{{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}}e^{\langle f_{1},X\rangle}e^{\langle f_{2},X\rangle}d\beta=\int_{{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}}e^{\langle f_{1}+f_{2},X\rangle}d\beta

=\int_{{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}}e^{\langle A(f_{1},f_{2}),X\rangle}d\beta=\int_{(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}}e^{\langle\xi,X\rangle}DH(\xi)

with
DH=A_{*}(d\beta)
is the Duistermaat-Heckman measure for the

G
-Hamiltonian space
{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}\times{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}
with moment map
A
.
So, by definition of
P(\lambda,\mu)
, the support of
DH
is

{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\lambda}+{\mathcal{O}}^{nc}_{\mu}=G\cdot(P(\lambda,\mu))

with
P(\lambda,\mu)\subset{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}\subset(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
. Remark that for every
\xi\in P(\lambda,\mu)
,
\langle\xi,z\rangle\geq 0
.
We now use the Weyl integration formula on
G{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}\subset(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{g}})^{*}
.
Desintegrating the measure
DH
by the Liouville measures of the
G
-orbits
G\xi
with
\xi\in P(\lambda,\mu)
, we obtain a measure
S
on

P(\lambda,\mu)\subset{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}\subset(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
.
We consider the action of the compact Weyl group
W_{K}
on
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
and the antiinvariant measure
\operatorname{ant}(S)=\sum_{w\in W_{K}}\epsilon(w)w\cdot S
on
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
.
Since
z
is a central element in
\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}}
,
\operatorname{ant}(S)
is still supported on
\langle z,\xi\rangle\geq 0
.
We have the following formula:
For
X\in{\mathfrak{t}}
:

\left(\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle\right)I_{\lambda}(X)I_{\mu}(X)=\int_{\xi\in(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}}e^{\langle\xi,X\rangle}\operatorname{ant}(S)(\xi).

We now consider the
K
-Hamiltonian space
N=O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}
, and its moment map
\phi:N\to(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})^{*}
.
We see similarly that

\langle\phi(n),z\rangle\geq\langle\lambda,z\rangle+\langle\mu,z\rangle
for any

,
and that the moment map
\phi
is proper.

Consider the equivariant volume of
N
:

I_{N}(X)=\int_{N}e^{\langle X,\phi(n)\rangle}d\beta_{N}=\int_{\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}}^{*}}e^{\langle X,\xi\rangle}\phi_{*}(d\beta_{N}).

So
N
gives rise to a
K
-invariant Duistermaat-Heckman measure

on
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})^{*}
supported on

\phi(N)
.
By definition of
{\rm Kir}(O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+})
,

\phi(N)=K\cdot({\rm Kir}(O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}))

with
{\rm Kir}(O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+})\subset{\mathfrak{c}}_{\geq 0}^{K}\subset\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
.

Similarly desintegrating the measure
DH_{N}=\phi_{*}(d\beta_{N})
by the Liouville measures of the
K
-orbits
K\xi
with

\xi\in{\rm Kir}(O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+})
, and using Weyl integration formula on
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{k}})^{*}
,

S^{\prime}
supported on

{\rm Kir}(O_{\lambda}\times O_{\mu}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{+})
.
Let
\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime})
be the anti-invariant measure defined by
\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime})=\sum_{w\in W_{K}}\epsilon(w)w\cdot S^{\prime}
on
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
.
We have the following formula.
For
X\in{\mathfrak{t}}
:

\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{c}^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle I_{N}(X)=\int_{(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}}e^{\langle\xi,X\rangle}\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime})(\xi).

Both measures
\operatorname{ant}(S)
and
\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime})
are supported on the closed halfspace
\langle\xi,z\rangle\geq 0
and are locally polynomial measures on
(\sqrt{-1}{\mathfrak{t}})^{*}
. It is equivalent to prove that
S=S^{\prime}
or that
\operatorname{ant}(S)=\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime})
since
S,S^{\prime}
are both supported in the Weyl chamber
{\mathfrak{c}}^{K}_{\geq 0}
, so no cancelation can occur.
It is easy to see that both
(\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle){\mathcal{F}}(S)(X)

and
(\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle){\mathcal{F}}(S^{\prime})(X)
are analytic (see for example Proposition 32 of
8
).
We thus can prove that
S=S^{\prime}
by proving that
{\mathcal{F}}(\operatorname{ant}(S))(X)={\mathcal{F}}(\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime}))(X)

on the open set of
X\in{\mathfrak{t}}
such that
\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle\neq 0
.
Now, for such
X
, all equivariant volumes occurring can be computed by an (easy) case of the Berline-Vergne localization formula
for the corresponding possibly non compact
Hamiltonian spaces with proper moment maps.
We give the formulae in the case where
\langle\lambda,H_{\alpha}\rangle\neq 0
and
\langle\mu,H_{\alpha}\rangle\neq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
, since they are easier to state.

We have by Rossmann formula (
4
):

I_{\lambda}(X)I\mu(X)

=\left(\sum_{w_{1}\in W_{K}}\epsilon(w_{1})\frac{e^{\langle w_{1}\lambda,X\rangle}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle}\right)\left(\sum_{w_{2}\in W_{K}}\epsilon(w_{2})\frac{e^{\langle w_{2}\mu,X\rangle}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle}\right).

Thus we obtain:

{\mathcal{F}}(\operatorname{ant}(S))(X)=\frac{\sum_{w_{1},w_{2}\in W_{K}\times W_{K}}\epsilon(w_{1})\epsilon(w_{2})e^{\langle w_{1}\lambda+w_{2}\mu,X\rangle}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle}.

(13)

We now compute the equivariant volume of
N
, that is the product of the equivariant volumes of the compact
K
-orbits
O_{\lambda}
,
O_{\mu}
and of
the symplectic space
{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}
. The list of weights of the action of

on

is the list
\Delta_{n}^{+}
.
The equivariant volume of
{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}
is thus given, for

such that

\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle}

-orbits

,
O_{\mu}

{\mathcal{F}}(\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime}))(X)=\frac{1}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle}\left(\frac{\sum_{w_{1},w_{2}\in W_{K}\times W_{K}}\epsilon(w_{1})\epsilon(w_{2})e^{\langle w_{1}\lambda+w_{2}\mu,X\rangle}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{c}^{+}}\langle\alpha,X\rangle}\right)

{\mathcal{F}}(\operatorname{ant}(S))(X)={\mathcal{F}}(\operatorname{ant}(S^{\prime}))(X)
on the open set of

such that
\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}\langle\alpha,X\rangle\neq 0
,

and

and

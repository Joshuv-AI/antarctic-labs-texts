Abstract




Abstract
On a Hermitian manifold, the Chern connection can induce a metric connection on the background Riemannian manifold. We call the sectional curvature of the metric connection induced by the Chern connection the Chern sectional curvature of this Hermitian manifold. First, we derive expression of the Chern sectional curvature in local complex coordinates. As an application, we find that a Hermitian metric is Kähler if the Riemann sectional curvature and the Chern sectional curvature coincide. As subsequent results, Ricci curvature and scalar curvature of the metric connection induced by the Chern connection are obtained.

Key words
Chern connection, metric connection, Chern sectional curvature

L_{\kappa\bar{\lambda}}={\mathfrak{R}}_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}\kappa\bar{\delta}}\xi^{\alpha}\bar{\zeta}^{\delta}
,
L_{1}={\rm Re\,}L
and
L_{2}={\rm Im\,}L
.
Set
K=\left(K_{\kappa{\lambda}}\right)_{1\leq\kappa,\lambda\leq n}=K_{1}+\sqrt{-1}K_{2}
,
where
L_{\kappa{\lambda}}={\mathfrak{R}}_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}\gamma\bar{\kappa}}\xi^{\alpha}\zeta^{\gamma}
,
K_{1}={\rm Re\,}K
and
K_{2}={\rm Im\,}K
.
Being similar to the formula (2.18) in
[
7
]
, we can write as

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}L_{1}-K_{1}&L_{2}-K_{2}\\
-L_{2}-K_{2}&L_{1}+K_{1}\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F{\rm diag}\left\{L,\bar{L}\right\}F^{-1}-\frac{1}{2}{\rm diag}\left\{I,-I\right\}F{\rm diag}\left\{K,\bar{K}\right\}F^{\mathrm{t}}{\rm diag}\left\{I,-I\right\}.

Denote by
G=(g_{ij})
and
H=(h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})
, then

G^{-1}=F{\rm diag}\left\{H^{-1},\bar{H}^{-1}\right\}F^{-1}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle{\rm tr\,}\left[G^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}L_{1}-K_{1}&L_{2}-K_{2}\\
-L_{2}-K_{2}&L_{1}+K_{1}\\
\end{array}\right)\right]={\rm tr\,}\left(H^{-1}L\right)+{\rm tr\,}\left(\bar{H}^{-1}\bar{L}\right)=h^{\bar{\lambda}\kappa}{\mathfrak{R}}_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}\kappa\bar{\delta}}\xi^{\alpha}\bar{\zeta}^{\delta}+\overline{h^{\bar{\lambda}\kappa}{\mathfrak{R}}_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}\kappa\bar{\delta}}\xi^{\alpha}\bar{\zeta}^{\delta}},

where
{\rm tr\,}(\,)
means the trace of a square matrix.
Hence

\boldsymbol{\rm{Ric}}_{D}(u,w)=g^{kl}g\left(\left({D}^{2}u\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}},w\right),\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{l}}\right)={\mathfrak{R}}^{(4)}_{\alpha\bar{\delta}}\xi^{\alpha}\bar{\zeta}^{\delta}+{\mathfrak{R}}^{(3)}_{\alpha\bar{\delta}}\zeta^{\alpha}\bar{\xi}^{\delta}.

This completes the proof.

\square

We recall that the Ricci curvature tensor
{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}
of the connection
{D}
is defined by

{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2n}g\left(\left({D}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right)\left(e_{k},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\right),e_{k}\right)=g^{kl}g\left(\left({D}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\right),\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{l}}\right).

From (
2.14
), we can see
{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}\neq{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ji}
in general.
It follows from (
2.14
) that

{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}={{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ji}
if and only if
{\mathfrak{R}}^{(3)}_{\alpha\bar{\delta}}={\mathfrak{R}}^{(4)}_{\alpha\bar{\delta}}
.
Are there some Hermitian manifolds such that
{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}={{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ji}
?

Let

T_{\beta\gamma}^{\alpha}=\varGamma_{\beta;\gamma}^{\alpha}-\varGamma_{\gamma;\beta}^{\alpha}=h^{\bar{\lambda}\alpha}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{\beta\bar{\lambda}}}{\partial z^{\gamma}}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{\gamma\bar{\lambda}}}{\partial z^{\beta}}\right)

be the torsion tensor of the Chern connection
D
. Set
T_{\beta}=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}T^{\alpha}_{\beta\alpha}
.
Lee
[
5
]
first introduced the following conformal invariant

b_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\frac{\partial T_{\alpha}}{\partial\bar{z}^{\beta}}-\frac{\partial T_{\bar{\beta}}}{\partial{z}^{\alpha}},

(2.15)

where
T_{\bar{\beta}}=\overline{T_{{\beta}}}
. In fact, it is easy to see

b_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}={\mathfrak{R}}^{(4)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}-{\mathfrak{R}}^{(3)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}.

(2.16)

If there exists a positive scalar function
\rho
in
(M,h)
such that
\rho h
is a Kähler metric, we call
(M,h)
a conformally Kähler manifold.
Westlake
[
12
]
proved
b_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=0
on conformally Kähler manifolds. Hence
{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}={{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ji}
on conformally Kähler manifolds
[
1
]
.
Next we provide an example such that
{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}={{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ji}
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:|z|^{2}=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}|z^{\alpha}|^{2}<1\}
be the unit ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\,(n>1)
endowed with a Hermitian metric
h=\displaystyle\frac{4|dz|^{2}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}
.
The symbols put as above.
A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}\mu\bar{\nu}}=

\displaystyle\frac{4\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{4}}\left[(1-|z|^{2})\delta_{\mu\nu}+\bar{z}^{\mu}z^{\nu}\right],

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}^{(3)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\left[(1-|z|^{2})\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\bar{z}^{\alpha}z^{\beta}\right],

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}^{(4)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\left[(1-|z|^{2})\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\bar{z}^{\alpha}z^{\beta}\right],

where
\delta_{\alpha\beta}
are Kronecker symbols. Hence
{\mathfrak{R}}^{(4)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}={\mathfrak{R}}^{(3)}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}
yields

{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}={{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ji}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(M,h)
be a Hermitian manifold with the background Riemannian
metric
g={\rm Re\,}h
. Suppose
{D}
is the metric connection induced by the Chern connection under the bundle isomorphism
o
.
Then
\boldsymbol{s}_{{D}}
is equal to four times of the second Chern scalar curvature
\boldsymbol{s}^{(2)}_{D}
, i.e.,

\boldsymbol{s}_{{D}}=4\boldsymbol{s}^{(2)}_{D}.

(2.17)

Proof.

For simplicity, we denote by

\boldsymbol{\rm{Ric}}_{{D}}(u):=\boldsymbol{\rm{Ric}}_{{D}}(u,u)={{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}u^{i}u^{j},

then

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{s}_{{D}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\left({{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ij}+{{\mathfrak{R}}}_{ji}\right)=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{\rm{Ric}}_{{D}}(u)}{\partial u^{i}\partial u^{j}}=2h^{\bar{\delta}\alpha}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{\rm{Ric}}_{{D}}(u)}{\partial\xi^{\alpha}\partial\bar{\xi}^{\delta}}

\displaystyle=2h^{\bar{\delta}\alpha}h^{\bar{\lambda}\kappa}\left({\mathfrak{R}}_{\alpha\bar{\lambda}\kappa\bar{\delta}}+{\mathfrak{R}}_{\kappa\bar{\delta}\alpha\bar{\lambda}}\right)=4\boldsymbol{s}^{(2)}_{D}.

This completes the proof.

\square

There are various Hermitian connections on the holomorphic tangent bundle in
[

]
.
We can consider the metric connection induced by any Hermitian connection under
the bundle isomorphism
o
.
For example, the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{lc}
on
T^{1,0}M
(the restriction of the complexified Levi-Civita connection
\nabla

T^{1,0}M
, see

).
We denote by
\boldsymbol{s}_{\nabla^{lc}}
the scalar curvature of the metric connection induced by the Levi-Civita connection

Gauduchon

shown that

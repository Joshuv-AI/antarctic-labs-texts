Some curvature properties of perfect fluid spacetimes Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Krishnendu De ∗ , Uday Chand De and Ljubica S. Velimirovic

Abstract
. In this paper we assume that a perfect fluid is the source of the gravitational field while analyzing the solutions to the Einstein field equations. With this new and innovative approach, we investigate some curvature properties of perfect fluid spacetimes. Firstly, we provide a necessary condition for a Lorentzian manifold to be a perfect fluid spacetime. We prove that a conformally flat perfect fluid spacetime is a spacetime of quasi constant sectional curvature. Also, we acquire that a conharmonically flat perfect fluid spacetime represents the radiation era. Next, we show that if a stiff matter fluid obeys Yang’s equations, then the vorticity of the fluid vanishes. Moreover, we find that if the Ricci tensor is Killing, then the perfect fluid spacetime is (i) expansion-free and shear-free, and its flow is geodesic, however, not necessarily vorticity-free, and (ii) σ \sigma and p p are constant. Besides, we establish that Ricci semi-symmetric or Ricci symmetric perfect fluid spacetimes represent either dark matter era, or phantom era. Finally, we acquire that if a perfect fluid is Ricci symmetric, then either the spacetime represents a dark matter era, or a static spacetime.

1.
Introduction

The proudest glory of Applied Mathematics is general relativity (briefly, GR) theory. GR has always been recognised as the most elegant physics theory ever developed and a very challenging theory. The study of GR, which ignores quantum effects, is crucial for understanding cosmology. In GR theory, the matter content of the universe is stated by choosing the suitable energy momentum tensor. Spacetime is referred as a 4-dimensional torsion-free, time-oriented Lorentzian manifold of signature (3, 1) or, alternatively,(1, 3), as a particular subclass of semi-Riemannian manifolds. Being the natural sources of Einstein’s field equations (briefly, EFEs) that are compatible with the Bianchi identities, perfect fluids perform a crucial role in the theory of GR. In numerous areas of physics, including nuclear physics, plasma physics, and astrophysics, relativistic perfect fluid models are of immense interest. In GR theory, hypothetical distributions of matter, such as the inside of a star or, an isotropic universe, are frequently modelled using perfect fluids.

3.
Proof of the Theorems

In this section, we have proved a series of theorems regarding perfect fluid spacetimes.

At first, we consider a Lorentzian manifold obeying an almost Ricci solitons and establish the subsequent result:

Theorem 3.1
.

If a Lorentzian manifold admits an almost Ricci soliton whose potential vector field is a unit timelike torse-forming vector field, then it becomes a perfect fluid spacetime.

Proof :
Suppose that a Lorentzian manifold admits an almost Ricci soliton. Therefore the equation (
1.7
) reveals

(3.1)

\nabla_{h}u_{k}+\nabla_{k}u_{h}+2R_{hk}+2\lambda g_{hk}=0.

If

is a unit timelike torse-forming vector field, then we infer
\nabla_{k}u_{h}=\phi(u_{k}u_{h}+g_{kh})
. Using this result in (
3.1
), we obtain

\phi(u_{k}u_{h}+g_{kh})+\phi(u_{h}u_{k}+g_{hk})+2R_{hk}+2\lambda g_{hk}=0,

which implies

(3.2)

R_{hk}=-(\phi+\lambda)g_{hk}-\phi u_{h}u_{k}.

Thus, the spacetime becomes a perfect fluid spacetime .

Thus, the proof is finished.

A spacetime of quasi constant sectional curvature is a perfect fluid spacetime (proof is given in Example 3). Is the converse true? In this article, we establish that the converse is not true, in general. Also, we know that each and every RW-spacetime represents a perfect fluid spacetime. Is the converse valid? In this article, we show that the converse is usually not valid and demonstrate the following result:

which implies

\nabla_{l}R+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{l}R+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{l}R=3\nabla_{l}R.

From the last equation we infer that
\nabla_{l}R=0
, that is,
R=
constant.

Hence, from equation (
3
) we conclude that
R_{hk}
is Killing.

Proof of the main Theorem:

In

]
, Sharma and Ghosh established that in a perfect fluid spacetime if
T_{hk}
is Killing, then the perfect fluid spacetime is (i) expansion-free and shear-free, and its flow is geodesic, however, not necessarily vorticity-free, and (ii)
\sigma
and
p
are constant. Now applying this result and the above Lemma, we can state that the perfect fluid spacetime is (i) expansion-free and shear-free, and its flow is geodesic, however, not necessarily vorticity-free, and (ii)
\sigma
and
p
are constant.

This accomplishes the proof.

Now we consider a Ricci semi-symmetric perfect fluid spacetime and establish the subsequent result:

Theorem 3.6
.

If a perfect fluid spacetime is Ricci semi-symmetric, then the spacetime represents either dark matter era, or phantom era.

Proof :
Covariant differentiation of the equation (
3.15
) yields

(3.27)

\displaystyle\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}R_{ij}=\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\alpha g_{ij}+\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}\beta u_{i}u_{j}+\beta\{\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}u_{i}u_{j}+u_{i}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}u_{j}\}.

The foregoing equation immediately gives

(3.28)

\displaystyle\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}R_{ij}-\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}R_{ij}=\beta\{u_{j}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}u_{i}-u_{j}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}u_{i}+u_{i}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}u_{j}-u_{i}\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}u_{j}\}.

Let the perfect fluid spacetime be Ricci semi-symmetric, that is,
\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}R_{ij}-\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}R_{ij}=0
. Then using Ricci identity the above equation infers that

(3.29)

\displaystyle 0=\beta\{u_{j}u_{h}R^{h}_{ilm}+u_{i}u_{h}R^{h}_{jlm}\},

which implies either
\beta=0
, or
\beta\neq 0.

Case (i): If
\beta=0
, then we have
p+\sigma=0
. Therefore, the spacetime represents the dark matter era.

Case (ii): If
\beta\neq 0
, then
u_{j}u_{h}R^{h}_{ilm}+u_{i}u_{h}R^{h}_{jlm}=0.
Therefore multiplying the previous relation by
g^{lm}
we have

(3.30)

\displaystyle u_{j}u_{h}R^{h}_{i}+u_{i}u_{h}R^{h}_{j}=0.

Also, from (
1.3
), we acquire

(3.31)

\displaystyle u^{j}R_{ij}=\alpha u_{i}-\beta u_{i}=(\alpha-\beta)u_{i}.

Using (
3.31
) in (
3.30
), we get

(3.32)

\displaystyle(\alpha-\beta)u_{i}u_{j}=0.

From the previous equation we conclude that
\alpha=\beta
which implies
p+3\sigma=0
.

Thus
\frac{p}{\sigma}=-3<-1
. Hence the spacetime represents the phantom era.

This ends the proof.

A spacetime is called conformally semi-symmetric if it fulfills the relation

(3.33)

\nabla_{l}\nabla_{m}C^{h}_{ijk}-\nabla_{m}\nabla_{l}C^{h}_{ijk}=0.

In
[
12
]
, it is established that in dimension 4, conformally semi-symmetric spacetimes are Ricci semi-symmetric spacetimes. Hence, we state the following:

Corollary 3.1
.

If a perfect fluid spacetime is conformally semi-symmetric, then either the spacetime represents dark matter era, or phantom era.

Again, the class of Ricci symmetric spaces (
\nabla_{l}R_{ij}=0
) is a proper subset of Ricci semi-symmetric spaces. Every semi-symmetric space is known to be Ricci semi-symmetric, but the converse is usually not true. In a Riemannian space they are equivalent for dimension three. In
[
32
]
, it has been established that for
n\geq 3
, the above stated relations are equivalent for hypersurfaces having non negative scalar curvature in a Euclidean space
E^{n+1}
.

From the above studies we write the subsequent result:

Corollary 3.2
.

A Ricci symmetric perfect fluid spacetime represents either phantom era, or dark matter era.

Here, we also consider a Ricci symmetric perfect fluid spacetime to establish a different result.

Theorem 3.7
.

If a perfect fluid is Ricci symmetric, then either the spacetime represents a dark matter era, or a static spacetime.

Proof :
For a perfect fluid spacetime, we acquire

(3.34)

R_{hk}=\alpha g_{hk}+\beta u_{h}u_{k}.

Covariant differentiation of the foregoing equation yields

(3.35)

\nabla_{l}R_{hk}=(\nabla_{l}\alpha)g_{hk}+(\nabla_{l}\beta)u_{h}u_{k}+\beta(u_{k}\nabla_{l}u_{h}+u_{h}\nabla_{l}u_{k}).

By hypothesis
\nabla_{l}R_{hk}=0
. Hence from the above we infer

(3.36)

(\nabla_{l}\alpha)g_{hk}+(\nabla_{l}\beta)u_{h}u_{k}+\beta(u_{k}\nabla_{l}u_{h}+u_{h}\nabla_{l}u_{k})=0.

Multiplying by
u^{k}
gives

(3.37)

(\nabla_{l}\alpha)u_{h}-(\nabla_{l}\beta)u_{h}-\beta\nabla_{l}u_{h}=0,

since
u^{i}\nabla_{l}u_{i}=0
.

Again multiplying by
u^{h}
yields

(3.38)

-(\nabla_{l}\alpha)+(\nabla_{l}\beta)=0.

Using (
3.38
) in (
3.37
), we have

(3.39)

\beta\nabla_{l}u_{h}=0.

Then the previous equation entails that either
\beta=0
, or
\nabla_{l}u_{h}=0
.

If
\beta=0
, then
p+\sigma=0
which implies the spacetime represents a dark matter era.

If
\nabla_{l}u_{h}=0
, then for a smooth vector field
v
, we acquire

\pounds_{v}g_{ij}=\nabla_{i}v_{j}+\nabla_{j}v_{i},

in which
\pounds
stands for Lie derivative. Since
\nabla_{l}u_{h}=0
, therefore
\pounds_{u}g_{ij}=0
which infer that
u
is Killing. Also
\nabla_{l}u_{h}=0
implies
u_{h}
is irrotational. Hence the spacetime becomes static
[
31
]
.

This ends the proof.

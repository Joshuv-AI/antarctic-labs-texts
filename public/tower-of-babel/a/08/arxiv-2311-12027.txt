Series over fat partitions: matrix models and discrete ensembles
A.Yu. Orlov

Abstract
We consider series over Young diagrams of products of Schur functions s λ ∪ λ s_{\lambda\cup\lambda} , marked with “fat partitions” λ ∪ λ \lambda\cup\lambda , which appear in matrix models associated with ensembles of symplectic and orthogonal matrices and quaternion Ginibre ensemble. We consider mixed matrix models that also contain complex Ginibre ensembles labeled by graphs and the three ensembles mentioned above. Cases are identified when the series of perturbations in coupling constants turn out to be tau functions of the DKP hierarchy introduced by the Kyoto school. This topic relates matrix models to random partitions - discrete symplectic ensemble and its modifications.

We remind that the Schur function

Appendix A
Partitions. The Schur polynomials
[
25
]

We recall that a nonincreasing set of nonnegative integers
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}\geq 0
,
we call partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})
, and
\lambda_{i}
are called parts of
\lambda
.
The sum of parts is called the weight
|\lambda|
of
\lambda
. The number of nonzero parts of
\lambda

is called the length of
\lambda
, it will be denoted
\ell(\lambda)
. See
[
25
]
for details.
Partitions will be denoted by Greek letters:
\lambda,\mu,\dots
. The set of all partitions is denoted by

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits
. The set of all partitions with odd parts is denoted
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{OP}\nolimits
.
Partitions with distinct parts are called strict partitions, we prefer
letters
\alpha,\beta
to denote them. The set of all strict partitions will be denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{DP}\nolimits
.
The Frobenius coordinated
\alpha,\beta
for partitions
(\alpha|\beta)=\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits
are of usenames
(let me recall that the coordinates
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{DP}\nolimits
consists of the lengths of arms counted
from the main diagonal of the Young diagram of
\lambda
while

\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{DP}\nolimits
consists of the lengths of legs counted
from the main diagonal of the Young diagram of
\lambda
,
k
is the length of the main diagonal of
\lambda
,
see
[
25
]
for details).

To define the Schur function
s_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits
at the first step we introduce the
set of elementary Schur functions
s_{(m)}
by

e^{\sum_{m>0}\frac{1}{m}p_{m}x^{m}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}x^{m}s_{(m)}(\mathbf{p})

where the variables
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},\dots)
are called power sum variables. For

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits

we define

s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})=\mathrm{det}\left[s_{(\lambda_{i}-i+j)}(\mathbf{p})\right]_{i,j>0}

(41)

If we put
p_{m}=p_{m}(X)=\mathrm{tr}X^{m}
where
X
is a matrix we write
s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p}(X))=s_{\lambda}(X)
.
The Schur functions play the role of the character of the linear group.

The following relation is known as the character map relation

s_{\lambda}(X)=s_{\lambda}(1,0,0,\dots)\sum_{\mu\atop|\mu|=|\lambda|}\varphi_{\lambda}(\mu)\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\mathrm{tr}\left(X^{\mu_{i}}\right)

(42)

where
\varphi_{\lambda}(\mu)
are rational numbers which are expressed in terms of
the characters of the symmetric group
S_{d},\,d=|\lambda|
, see
[
25
]
. It follows from
(
42
) that
s_{\lambda}(X)
is a polynomial of degree
d=|\mu|=|\lambda|
in entries
of
X
.

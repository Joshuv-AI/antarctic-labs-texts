On convergence of numerical solutions for the compressible MHD system with weakly divergence-free magnetic field
Yang Li, Bangwei She

Abstract
We study a general convergence theory for the analysis of numerical solutions to a magnetohydrodynamic system describing the time evolution of compressible, viscous, electrically conducting fluids in space dimension d d ( = 2 , 3 ) (=2,3) . First, we introduce the concept of dissipative weak solutions and prove the weak–strong uniqueness property for dissipative weak solutions, meaning a dissipative weak solution coincides with a classical solution emanating from the same initial data on the lifespan of the latter. Next, we introduce the concept of consistent approximations and prove the convergence of consistent approximations towards the dissipative weak solution as well as the classical solution. Interpreting the consistent approximation as the energy stability and consistency of numerical solutions, we have built a nonlinear variant of the celebrated Lax equivalence theorem. Finally, as an application of this theory, we show the convergence analysis of two numerical methods.



Keywords:
magnetohydrodynamic fluids, weak–strong uniqueness, stability, convergence, dissipative weak solution, consistent approximation

Introduction

The time evolution of unsteady, electrically conducting fluids in the presence of magnetic field is described by the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) system. Mathematical theory of MHD is widely applied in astrophysics and thermonuclear reactions, among many others. A simplified and well-accepted model for compressible isentropic MHD system admits the form (see for instance

&\partial_{t}(\varrho\mathbf{u})+{\rm div}(\varrho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})+\nabla p(\varrho)={\rm div}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u})+{\rm Curl}\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{B},\\
&\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}={\rm Curl}(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B})-\alpha{\rm Curl}({\rm Curl}\mathbf{B}),\\
&{\rm div}\mathbf{B}=0,\\

The aim of this paper is to establish a general framework for the convergence analysis of numerical approximations for the compressible MHD system (

A dissipative weak (DW) solution allows concentration and oscillation defects that can be controlled by the dissipation defects of the energy stability. It should be stressed that constructing a weak solution for “small”
\gamma<d/2
is a challenging task for compressible viscous fluids while the dissipative character of the DW solution allows us to work with “small”
\gamma

Though the DW solution is weaker than the standard finite energy weak solution, it satisfies the weak–strong uniqueness principle, meaning that a DW solution coincides with a classical solution as long as the latter exists. By this argument, the convergence of a numerical solution reduces to the convergence towards a DW solution. Upon realizing a DW solution can be obtained by the limit (discretization parameter
h\to 0
) of a consistent approximation interpreting the stability and consistency properties of the numerical solution, we find a generalized Lax equivalence theorem:

convergence (to a DW or classical solution)
\Longleftrightarrow
stability + consistency =:  consistent approximation.

More specifically, our strategy reads:

•

Proving the weak–strong uniqueness principle in the class of DW solution, see Theorem
2.4
.

•

Passing to the limit (
h\to 0
) from the consistent approximation to construct a DW solution in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

•

Showing that a numerical solution is a consistent approximation in the sense of Definition
2.3
that reflects the stability and consistency of the numerical solution.

In this paper, the convergence theory is aimed for the class of numerical schemes that preserves the divergence free of the magnetic field weakly. Other properties such as conservation of the mass, positivity of the density, and stability of the total energy are also expected for the numerical solutions.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Sect.
2

3

In Sect.
4

\mathfrak{C}=\Big(\overline{\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}}-\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{u}\Big)+\alpha\Big(\overline{|{\rm Curl}\mathbf{B}|^{2}}-|{\rm Curl}\mathbf{B}|^{2}\Big),

Knowing the above limit, we are ready to pass to the limit

\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}\,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\varrho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u}+p(\varrho)\mathbb{I}:\nabla\mathbf{v}\Big)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{v}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}({\rm Curl}\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\mathbf{v}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\

4.2
Example-I

In the first example, we propose a mixed FV–FE approximation adapted from the Navier-Stokes solver of Karper
[
11
]
and the magnetic solver of Ding and Mao
[
3
]
with no-slip boundary conditions.
To begin, we introduce the necessary notations.

Mesh.

Let
\Omega_{h}
be a regular and quasi-uniform triangulation of the bounded domain
\Omega
,
\mathcal{E}
be the set of all
(d-1)
-dimensional faces,
\mathcal{E}^{B}=\mathcal{E}\cap\partial\Omega
be the exterior faces,
\mathcal{E}^{I}=\mathcal{E}\setminus\mathcal{E}^{B}
be the interior faces, and
\mathcal{E}(K)
be the set of all faces of an arbitrary element
K
.
We denote
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{E}^{I}
as the common face of two neighbouring elements
K
and
L
.
Further, we denote
{\bf n}_{\sigma}
as the outer normal of a face
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
and
{\bf n}_{\sigma,K}
as the unit normal vector pointing outwards
K
if
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}(K)
.
The size of the mesh (maximal diameter of all elements) is supposed to be a positive parameter
h<1
.

Function spaces.

We define on
\Omega_{h}
discrete function spaces
Q_{h},V_{h},\mathcal{N}_{h}
, that are spaces of piecewise constants, piecewise linear Crouzeix–Ravairt elements, and lowest order
H({\rm curl})
-Nédélec edge elements, respectively.

Q_{h}\equiv\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\middle|\;v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}^{1}_{0}(K)\ K\in\Omega_{h}\right\},

V_{h}\equiv\left\{\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\middle|\;\mathbf{v}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}^{d}_{1}(K)\forall\,K\in\Omega_{h};\int_{\sigma}\left\llbracket\mathbf{v}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S(x)=0\;\forall\,\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}\right\},

\mathcal{N}_{h}\equiv\left\{\mathbf{v},{\rm Curl}\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\middle|\;\mathbf{v}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{d}\oplus\mathcal{P}_{0}^{1}x\;\forall\;K\in\Omega_{h};\int_{\sigma}\left\llbracket\mathbf{v}\times{\bf n}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S(x)=0\;\forall\;\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}\right\},

where
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{n}(K)
(resp.
\mathcal{P}^{1}_{n}(K)
) denotes the space of polynomials of degree not greater than
n
on element
K
for
d
-dimensional vector valued functions (resp. for scalar functions).
The spaces
Q_{h},V_{h}
and
\mathcal{N}_{h}
shall be used for the approximation of the discrete density, velocity and magnetic field, respectively.
In addition, for the purpose of designing weakly divergence-free magnetic field (see Lemma
4.1
), we introduce the following space

W_{h}=\left\{v\in W^{1,2}\cap L^{2}_{0}\middle|\;v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}^{1}(K)\;\forall\;K\in\Omega_{h}\right\}.

It should be mentioned that for any
\psi_{h}\in W_{h}
we have
\nabla\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
.

The interpolation operators associated to the function spaces
V_{h}
,
\mathcal{N}_{h}
and
W_{h}
are given by

\Pi_{V}:\ W^{1,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h},\quad\Pi_{N}:\ W^{1,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{h},\quad\Pi_{W}:\ L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{h}

satisfy the following interpolation estimates

\left\lVert\mathbf{v}-\Pi_{V}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{C^{1}},\;\left\lVert\mathbf{v}-\Pi_{W}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{C^{1}},\;\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\Pi_{N}\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}+\left\lVert{\rm Curl}(\mathbf{u}-\Pi_{N}\mathbf{u})\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\right\rVert_{C^{2}}.

(4.1)

for any
\mathbf{v}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
,
\mathbf{u}\in C^{2}(\Omega)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, see e.g. the monograph of Brezzi et al.
[
2
]
.

For simplicity of notation, we denote
X_{h}=Q_{h}\times V_{0,h}\times\mathcal{N}_{0,h}
, where

V_{0,h}=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in V_{h}\middle|\int_{\sigma}\mathbf{v}\,{\rm d}S(x)=0\ \forall\,\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{B}\right\}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{N}_{0,h}=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\middle|\int_{\sigma}\mathbf{v}\times{\bf n}\,{\rm d}S(x)=0\;\forall\;\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{B}\right\}.

Next, we suppose the time step size
\Delta t\approx h
and denote
t^{k}=k\Delta t
for
k=1,\ldots,N_{T}(=T/\Delta t)
.
For a generic discrete function
v_{h}
at time
t^{k}=k\Delta t
we denote it by
v_{h}^{k}
and write
v_{h}\in L_{\Delta t}(0,T;Y)
if
v_{h}^{k}\in Y
for all
k=1,\dots,N_{T}
with
Y\in\{Q_{h},V_{0,h},\mathcal{N}_{0,h},X_{h}\}
and

v_{h}(t,\cdot)=v_{h}^{0}\mbox{ for }t\leq 0,\ v_{h}(t,\cdot)=v_{h}^{k}\mbox{ for }t\in((k-1)\Delta t,k\Delta t],\ k=1,2,\ldots,N_{T}.

Some discrete operators.

We define the discrete time derivative by the backward Euler method

D_{t}v_{h}=\frac{v_{h}(t)-v_{h}^{\triangleleft}(t)}{\Delta t}\mbox{ for }t\in(0,T)\quad\mbox{ with }\quad v_{h}^{\triangleleft}(t)=v_{h}(t-\Delta t).

For any piecewise continuous function
f
, we define its trace on a generic edge as

\displaystyle f^{\rm in}|_{\sigma}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(x-\delta{\bf n}_{\sigma}),\ \forall\ \sigma\in\mathcal{E},\qquad f^{\rm out}|_{\sigma}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(x+\delta{\bf n}_{\sigma}),\ \forall\ \sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}.

Note that
f^{\rm out}|_{\mathcal{E}^{B}}
is determined by the boundary condition.
Further, we define the jump and average operators at an edge
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}
as

\left\llbracket f\right\rrbracket_{\sigma}=f^{\rm out}-f^{\rm in}\mbox{ and }\left\{\hskip-3.00003pt\left\{f\right\}\hskip-3.00003pt\right\}_{\sigma}=\frac{f^{\rm out}+f^{\rm in}}{2},

(4.2)

respectively, and denote
\Pi_{h}\equiv\Pi_{Q}
as the element-wise constant projection, where

\Pi_{Q}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to Q_{h}.\quad\left.\Pi_{Q}f\right|_{K}=\left.\widehat{f}\right|_{K}\equiv\frac{1}{\left\lvert K\right\rvert}\int_{K}f\,{\rm d}{x},\ \forall\ K\in\Omega_{h}.

Next, we introduce the upwind flux for any function
r_{h}\in Q_{h}
at a generic face
\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}

\displaystyle{\rm Up}[r_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}]=r_{h}^{\rm up}u_{\sigma}=r_{h}^{\rm in}[u_{\sigma}]^{+}+r_{h}^{\rm out}[u_{\sigma}]^{-},

where
\mathbf{u}_{h}\in V_{h}
is the velocity field and

u_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\int_{\sigma}\mathbf{u}_{h}\,{\rm d}S(x)\cdot{\bf n}_{\sigma},\quad[f]^{\pm}=\frac{f\pm|f|}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad r_{h}^{\rm up}=\begin{cases}r_{h}^{\rm in}&\mbox{if }\ u_{\sigma}\geq 0,\\
r_{h}^{\rm out}&\mbox{if }\ u_{\sigma}<0.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we consider a diffusive numerical flux function of the following form for
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle{\rm F}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(r_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})={\rm Up}[r_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}]-h^{\varepsilon}\left\llbracket r_{h}\right\rrbracket.

(4.3)

It is easy to check for any
\varrho_{h}\in Q_{h}
and
\mathbf{u}_{h}\in V_{h}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}}\int_{\sigma}\left({\rm F}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}},\mathbf{u}_{h})\left\llbracket\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}}\right\rrbracket-{\rm F}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})\left\llbracket\frac{|\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}}|^{2}}{2}\right\rrbracket\right)\,{\rm d}S(x)

(4.4)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}}\int_{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{h}^{{\rm up}}\left\lvert u_{\sigma}\right\rvert+h^{\varepsilon}\left\{\hskip-3.00003pt\left\{\varrho_{h}\right\}\hskip-3.00003pt\right\}_{\sigma}\right)\left\lvert\left\llbracket\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}}\right\rrbracket\right\rvert^{2}\,{\rm d}S(x).

For simplicity, we denote

{\rm co}\{a,b\}=\left[\min(a,b),\max(a,b)\right]

and write

a\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}b\mbox{ if }a\leq cb
if
c
is a positive constant that is independent of the mesh size and time step used in the scheme.
We shall frequently use the abbreviation
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{p}}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{p}L^{q}}
for
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{p}\left(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)\right)}
, respectively.

The numerical method.

Using the above notations we propose a mixed FV–FE method for the approximation of the MHD system (
1.1
) named as
Scheme-I
.

Scheme-I.

Given the initial values (
1.4
) we set
(\varrho_{h}^{0},\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0},\mathbf{B}_{h}^{0})=(\Pi_{Q}\varrho_{0},\Pi_{V}\mathbf{u}_{0},\Pi_{N}\mathbf{B}_{0})
and seek
(\varrho_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{B}_{h})\in L_{\Delta t}(0,T;X_{h})
such that

\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\varrho_{h}\phi_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}}\int_{\sigma}{\rm F}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})\left\llbracket\phi_{h}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S(x)=0\quad\mbox{for all }\phi_{h}\in Q_{h};

(4.5a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}D_{t}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}}\int_{\sigma}{\rm F}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}},\mathbf{u}_{h})\cdot\left\llbracket\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S(x)+\mu\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}:\nabla_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}

(4.5b)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\nu{\rm div}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}-p_{h}){\rm div}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}({\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h}^{\triangleleft})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}=0\quad\mbox{for all }\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{0,h};

\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}\mathbf{B}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{C}_{h}+\alpha{\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\cdot{\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{C}_{h}-(\mathbf{u}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h}^{\triangleleft})\cdot{\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{C}_{h}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\quad\mbox{for all }\mathbf{C}_{h}\in\mathcal{N}_{0,h}.

(4.5c)

where

,
the discrete operators
{\rm div}_{h},\nabla_{h}
and
{\rm Curl}_{h}
are the same as the continuous case on each element. Moreover, the artificial diffusion parameter
\varepsilon
follows

\varepsilon>0\mbox{ if }\gamma\geq 2\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\varepsilon\in(0,2\gamma-1-d/3)\mbox{ if }\gamma\in(4d/(1+3d),2).

\varrho\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\varrho)-\mathcal{H}(\varrho)=p(\varrho)

\int_{\Omega}\left(D_{t}\mathcal{H}(\varrho_{h})-p_{h}{\rm div}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}=-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|D_{t}\varrho_{h}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{x}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}}\int_{\sigma}\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)\left\llbracket\varrho_{h}\right\rrbracket^{2}\left(h^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}|u_{\sigma}|\right)\,{\rm d}S(x).

(4.10)

where
\xi\in{\rm co}\{\varrho_{h}^{\triangleleft},\varrho_{h}\}
and
\zeta\in{\rm co}\{\varrho_{h}^{\rm in},\varrho_{h}^{\rm out}\}
are the same as in the renormalized continuity equation (

).

Finally, we finish the proof by summing up the identities (
4.8
)–(
4.10
).

4.2.2
Consistency

Another step towards the consistent approximation is the consistency.
The numerical solution of

satisfies the consistency criteria (
2.9
) of a consistent approximation. More precisely, we have the following consistency formulation.

Theorem 4.3

(Consistency of the
Scheme-I
)
.

Let
(\varrho_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{B}_{h})
be a solution of the discrete problem (
4.5
) on the time interval
[0,T]
with
\Delta t\approx h
and
\gamma>\frac{4d}{1+3d}
.
Then there exists some positive constant
\beta
such that

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{h}\partial_{t}\phi+\varrho_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\phi\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\phi(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}+{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\beta})

(4.12a)

for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{2}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega})
;

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{h}:\nabla\mathbf{v}+p_{h}{\rm div}\mathbf{v}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}):\nabla\mathbf{v}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}({\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h})\cdot\mathbf{v}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}}\cdot\mathbf{v}(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}+{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\beta})\end{split}

(4.12b)

for any
\mathbf{v}\in C^{2}_{c}([0,T)\times{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}^{0}\cdot\mathbf{C}(0,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{B}_{h}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{C}-\alpha{\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\cdot{\rm Curl}\mathbf{C}+(\mathbf{u}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h})\cdot{\rm Curl}\mathbf{C}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t={\mathcal{O}}(h^{\beta})

(4.12c)

for any
\mathbf{C}\in C_{c}^{2}([0,T)\times\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\mathbf{C}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
;

\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}\cdot\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}{x}={\mathcal{O}}(h)

(4.12d)

for any
\psi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

First, recalling
[
8
, Theorem 13.2]
we know (
4.12a
) holds and there exists a
\beta>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{E}^{I}}\int_{\sigma}{\rm F}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}},\mathbf{u}_{h})\cdot\left\llbracket\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}}\right\rrbracket\,{\rm d}S(x)\,{\rm d}t

(4.13)

\displaystyle+\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}:\nabla_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nu{\rm div}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}-p_{h}){\rm div}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{h}^{0}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}^{0}}\cdot\mathbf{v}(0)\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\varrho_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{u}_{h}}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{h}:\nabla\mathbf{v}+p_{h}{\rm div}\mathbf{v}\right]\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\nabla_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h}):\nabla\mathbf{v}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+h^{\beta}.

Then we derive (
4.12b
) by combining (
4.13
) with the following estimates

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(({\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h}^{\triangleleft})\cdot\Pi_{V}\mathbf{v}-({\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h})\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(({\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h})\cdot(\Pi_{V}\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v})+({\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\times(\mathbf{B}_{h}-\mathbf{B}_{h}^{\triangleleft}))\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right\rvert

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}\left\lVert{\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{B}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}h\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{C^{1}}+\Delta t\left\lVert D_{t}\mathbf{B}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{C^{0}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h+\Delta t^{1/2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h^{1/2}.

where we have used Hölder’s inequality, the uniform bounds (
4.11
) as well as the interpolation estimate (
4.1
).
We are left with the proof of (
4.12c
) and (
4.12d
). To proceed, we set
\mathbf{C}_{h}=\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}
as the test function in (
4.5c
) and analyze each term in the following. First, for the time derivative term we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\mathbf{B}_{h}\cdot\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}\,{\rm d}{x}=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}(t)\cdot\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}(t)\,{\rm d}{x}-\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{-\Delta t}^{T-\Delta t}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}(t)\cdot\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}(t+\Delta t)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{T-\Delta t}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}(t)\cdot\Pi_{N}\underbrace{\mathbf{C}(t+\Delta t)}_{=0}\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}(t)\cdot D_{t}\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}^{0}\cdot\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}(t)\,{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}(t)\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{C}\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}^{0}\cdot\mathbf{C}(0)\,{\rm d}{x}+I_{1}+I_{2},

where

I_{1}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}(t)\cdot(\partial_{t}\mathbf{C}-D_{t}\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\quad I_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{B}_{h}(0)\cdot\left(\mathbf{C}(0)-\int_{0}^{\Delta t}\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}(t)\,{\rm d}t\right)\,{\rm d}{x}.

By Hölder’s inequality and the estimates (
4.11

) we derive

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\cdot({\rm Curl}_{h}\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}-{\rm Curl}\mathbf{C})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h\left\lVert\mathbf{C}\right\rVert_{C^{2}}\left\lVert{\rm Curl}_{h}\mathbf{B}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}h,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left((\mathbf{u}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h}^{\triangleleft})\cdot{\rm Curl}_{h}\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}-(\mathbf{u}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h})\cdot{\rm Curl}\mathbf{C}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{h}\times\mathbf{B}_{h}^{\triangleleft})\cdot({\rm Curl}_{h}\Pi_{N}\mathbf{C}-{\rm Curl}\mathbf{C})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}_{h}\times(\mathbf{B}_{h}^{\triangleleft}-\mathbf{B}_{h}))\cdot{\rm Curl}\mathbf{C}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

5
Conclusion

We introduced the concept of DW solution and consistent approximation for multi-dimensional compressible MHD system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
).
We derived the weak–strong uniqueness property for the DW solution, meaning that the DW solution coincides with the classical solution (emanating from the same initial data) as long as the latter exists.
Further, we proved the convergence of the consistent approximation towards the DW solution as well as the classical solution on the lifespan of the latter. Interpreting the consistent approximation as the stability and consistency of a numerical solution, we established a generalized Lax equivalence theory. Finally, we applied this theory for the convergence analysis of two mixed finite volume–finite element methods. These two methods preserve the conservation of mass, positivity of density, stability of total energy, and weakly divergence free of the magnetic field.

Transformation and symmetries for the Andrews-Garvan crank function
Rishabh Sarma

Abstract
. Let R ⁡ ( z , q ) R(z,q) be the two-variable generating function of Dyson’s rank function. In a recent joint work with Frank Garvan, we investigated the transformation of the elements of the p p -dissection of R ⁡ ( ζ p , q ) R(\zeta_{p},q) , where ζ p \zeta_{p} is a primitive p p -th root of unity, under special congruence subgroups of S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℤ ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) , leading us to interesting symmetry observations. In this work, we derive analogous transformation results for the two-variable crank generating function C ⁡ ( z , q ) C(z,q) in terms of generalized eta products. We consider the action of the group Γ 0 ​ ( p ) \Gamma_{0}(p) on the elements of the p p -dissection of C ⁡ ( ζ p , q ) C(\zeta_{p},q) , leading us to new symmetries for the crank function. As an application, we give a new proof of the crank theorem predicted by Dyson in 1944 and resolved by Andrews and Garvan in 1988. Furthermore, we present identities expressing the elements of the crank dissection in terms of generalized eta products for primes p = 11 , 13 , 17 p=11,13,17 and 19 19 .

1.
Introduction

The rank function is an important statistic in the theory of partitions. It is defined as the largest part minus the number of parts in a partition. In 1944, Dyson gave this definition of the rank statistic and conjectured that it decomposes the partitions of
5n+4
and
7n+5
into five and seven equinumerous classes respectively, a conjecture that was proven 10 years later by Atkin and Swinnerton-Dyer. He also prophesied the existence of a “crank” statistic for a similar explanation of the mod
11
congruence of Ramanujan viz.

p(11n+6)\equiv 0\pmod{11},

and it wasn’t until 1988 that the elusive crank was found by Andrews and Garvan
[
1
]
. The crank of a partition is defined as the largest part if there are no ones in the partition and otherwise the number of parts larger than the number of ones minus the number of ones. It is worth mentioning here that after the discovery of the partition crank, Garvan, Kim and Stanton
[
13
]
came up with new statistics on partitions (also called cranks) which combinatorially prove Ramanujan’s congruences for the partition function modulo
5
,
7
,
11
and
25
, giving explicit bijections between equinumerous crank classes. However, the crank statistic that we work on in this paper is the ordinary partition crank defined by Andrews and Garvan which we simply refer to as the crank. Let
M(r,t,n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
with crank congruent to
r
mod
t
. Then they show that

(1.1)

\displaystyle M(r,5,5n+4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}p(5n+4),\,0\leq r\leq 4,

(1.2)

\displaystyle M(r,7,7n+5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{7}p(7n+5),\,0\leq r\leq 6,

(1.3)

\displaystyle M(r,11,11n+6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{11}p(11n+6),\,0\leq r\leq 10,

thus providing an almost combinatorial explanation of all three partition congruences of Ramanujan. Let
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
, where
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
. Let
M(m,n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
with crank
m
. Then the two variable generating function for the crank function, due to Andrews and Garvan (see
[
1
]
) is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}M(m,n)\,z^{m}\,q^{n}=(1-z)q+\dfrac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(zq;q)_{\infty}(z^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}}.

Equivalently we define

C(z,q):=\dfrac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(zq;q)_{\infty}(z^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}}=1+(-1+z+z^{-1})q+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}M(m,n)\,z^{m}\,q^{n}.

In this paper, we look at the transformation and symmetry of this function, when
z
is a primitive
p
-th root of unity
\zeta_{p}
. We find that the crank function satisfies the same type of symmetries as the rank generating function, which was our subject of interest in
[
14
]
.

Throughout this paper we use the standard
q
-notation:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}),

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}},

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{j};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{\infty}(a_{2};q)_{\infty}\dots(a_{j};q)_{\infty}.

We also define a class of generalized eta products and functions that we will use frequently in expressions for our crank identities and to study their transformations in the subsequent sections.

Following Biagioli (see

We also define the weight

for all

Let

The modularity of the rank generating function
R(z,q)
, where
z
is replaced by a primitive
p
-th root of unity for a general prime

[
7

. Building on the groundbreaking work of Zwegers
[

]
and
[
23
]
around the turn of the century, who realised how Ramanujan’s mock theta functions occur naturally as the holomorphic parts of certain real analytic modular forms, Bringmann and Ono showed that

is the holomorphic part a weak Maass form of weight
\frac{1}{2}
on
\Gamma_{1}(576p^{4})
, where the non-holomorphic part is a Mordell integral of a theta function. Garvan
[
12
]
, in 2019, observed that the introduction of a simple multiplier and a generalized correction series factor reduces the modularity of the rank function to larger and more natural congruence subgroups of
\mbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

In a subsequent joint work with Garvan
[
14
]
, we generalized and improved these results of his paper
[
12
]
on transformations for Dyson’s rank function leading us to exciting observations of symmetry among the elements of dissection of the rank generating function. In this paper, we try to implement our ideas in
[
14
]
and those of Garvan
[
12
]
to obtain analogous results for the crank generating function.

Since its discovery, it has been well established that the crank statistic plays a central role in the theory of partitions. Perhaps the most important breakthrough came in 2005 when Mahlburg showed that the crank itself satisfies Ramanujan-type congruences using Hecke’s theory of modular forms. In his seminal paper, Mahlburg establishes congruences for infinitely many non-nested arithmetic progressions modulo powers of primes by tying the crank generating function to the Dedekind eta function and Klein forms. Rewriting his functions in the framework of generalized eta products, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Mahlburg
[
17
, Corollary 3.3]
)
.

Let
p>3
be prime, suppose
1\leq\ell\leq(p-1)
, and define

\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right):=\dfrac{\eta(z)^{2}}{E_{0,\ell}(z)},

where, following Yang
[
21
]
, we consider the generalized eta product

E_{0,\ell}(z)=q^{\frac{1}{12}}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\zeta_{p}^{\ell}q^{m-1})(1-\zeta_{p}^{-\ell}q^{m}).

Then
\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)
is a weakly holomorphic modular form of weight 1 on the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(2p^{2})
.

Mahlburg further goes on to establish a rescaled form of the crank generating function as the sum of a weakly holomorphic function on
\Gamma_{1}(2p^{2})
and a modular form on
\Gamma_{0}(p)
of integral weights (
[
17
]
, Section 4). We show that after multiplying with an appropriate eta product and a power of
q

\mbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, resembling the result for the rank generating function
[
12

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>3
be prime. Then the function

\eta(p^{2}z)\,\mathcal{C}_{p}\left(z\right)

is a weakly holomorphic modular form of weight
1
on the group

This result is analogous to the modularity of the rank generating function that was studied in
[
12
]
. There the author also considered the modularity and transformation of elements of the
p
-dissection of the rank generating function. We note that in the definition for these rank dissection elements
\mathcal{K}_{p,m}(\zeta_{p}^{d};z)
, there are two cases pertaining to whether
-24m
is a quadratic residue or non-residue modulo

[
14

\Phi_{p,a}(q)
as in the definition of the rank dissection elements

where the series
\Phi_{p,a}(q)

[

Definition 1.4
.

For
p>3

s_{p}=\frac{1}{24}(p^{2}-1)

.

Let

2.
Preliminary definitions and Results

In Section
1
, we defined
M(r,t,n)
to be the number of partitions of
n
with crank
congruent to
r
mod
t
, and let
\zeta_{p}=\exp(2\pi i/p)
. Then we have

C(\zeta_{p},q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}M(k,p,n)\,\zeta_{p}^{k}\right)\,q^{n}.

We introduce a class of generalized eta products and its transformation under matrices in
\mbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
due to Yifan Yang. We later show how it relates to the crank function.

Theorem 2.1

(Yang
[
21
]
)
.

Let
p
be prime and
g,h
be arbitrary real numbers not simultaneously congruent to
0
modulo
p
. For
z\in\mathbb{H}
, we define the generalized Dedekind eta functions
E_{g,h}(z)
by

E_{g,h}(z)=q^{B(g/p)/2}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\zeta_{p}^{h}q^{m-1+\frac{g}{p}}\right)\left(1-\zeta_{p}^{-h}q^{m-\frac{g}{p}}\right),

where
B(x)=x^{2}-x+1/6
. Then the following transformations hold :

i)

E_{g+p,h}=E_{-g,-h}=-\zeta_{p}^{-h}E_{g,h}
.

ii)

E_{g,h+p}=E_{g,h}
.

Moreover for
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

iii)

E_{g,h}(z+b)=e^{\pi ibB(g/p)}E_{g,bg+h}(z)
for
c=0
.

iv)

E_{g,h}(\gamma z)=\varepsilon(a,b,c,d)e^{\pi i\delta}E_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}(z)
for
c\neq 0
where

\displaystyle\varepsilon(a,b,c,d)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e^{\pi i(bd(1-c^{2})+c(a+d-3))/6}&\mbox{if $c$ is odd},\\
\\
-ie^{\pi i(ac(1-d^{2})+d(b-c+3))/6}&\mbox{if $d$ is odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}ab+2ghbc+h^{2}cd}{p^{2}}-\frac{gb+h(d-1)}{p},~and

\displaystyle(g^{\prime}\,\,\,\,h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(g\,\,\,\,h)\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

We now use Yang’s transformation results to find the transformation of our function
\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)
defined below under
\Gamma_{0}(p)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
p>3
be prime, suppose
1\leq\ell\leq(p-1)
, and define

\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right):=\dfrac{\eta(z)^{2}}{E_{0,\ell}(z)},

where, following Yang
[
21
]
, we consider the generalized eta product

E_{0,\ell}(z)=q^{\frac{1}{12}}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\zeta_{p}^{\ell}q^{m-1})(1-\zeta_{p}^{-\ell}q^{m}).

Then

\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.=\mu(A,\ell)\,\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\overline{d\ell}}{p};z\right),

where

\mu(A,\ell)=\exp\left(\pi i\left(\frac{\ell(d-1)}{p}+\frac{cd\ell^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{c\ell}{p}\right)\right),~\text{and}~A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(p).

Here
\overline{m}
is the least nonnegative residue of
m\pmod{p}
.

Proof.

Using Theorems
2.1
and
[
16
, Theorem 2, p.51]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.

\displaystyle=\quad\begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{\frac{2\pi ib}{12}}\eta(z)^{2}}{e^{\frac{\pi ib}{6}}E_{0,\ell}(z)}&\mbox{if $c=0$},\\
\\
\dfrac{e^{\frac{\pi i}{6}\left[(a+d)c-bd(c^{2}-1)-3c\right]}\eta(z)^{2}}{e^{\frac{\pi i}{6}\left[bd(1-c^{2})+c(a+d-3)\right]}e^{\pi i\left[\frac{\ell^{2}cd}{p^{2}}-\frac{\ell(d-1)}{p}\right]}E_{\ell c,\ell d}(z)}&\mbox{if $c\neq 0$ and odd},\\
\\
\dfrac{e^{\frac{\pi i}{6}\left[(a+d)c-bd(c^{2}-1)-3c\right]}\eta(z)^{2}}{-ie^{\frac{\pi i}{6}\left[bd(1-c^{2})+c(a+d-3)\right]}e^{\pi i\left[\frac{\ell^{2}cd}{p^{2}}-\frac{\ell(d-1)}{p}\right]}E_{\ell c,\ell d}(z)}&\mbox{if $c\neq 0$ and even},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=e^{\pi i\left[\frac{\ell(d-1)}{p}-\frac{\ell^{2}cd}{p^{2}}\right]}\dfrac{\eta(z)^{2}}{E_{\ell c,\ell d}(z)}.

E_{\ell c,\ell d}(z)=\left(-\zeta_{p}^{-\ell d}\right)^{\frac{\ell c}{p}}E_{0,\ell d}(z)=e^{\pi i\left[\frac{c\ell}{p}-\frac{2\ell^{2}cd}{p^{2}}\right]}E_{0,\overline{d\ell}}(z)
.

Then,

\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.=e^{\pi i\left[\frac{\ell(d-1)}{p}+\frac{cd\ell^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{c\ell}{p}\right]}\dfrac{\eta(z)^{2}}{E_{0,\overline{d\ell}}(z)}

and we have our result.
∎

The following corollary now follows easily and gives a simpler transformation.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
p>3
be prime and suppose
1\leq\ell\leq\frac{1}{2}(p-1)
. Then

\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.=\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right),

where
A\in\Gamma_{0}(p^{2})\cap\Gamma_{1}(p).

Proof.

Let
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(p^{2})\cap\Gamma_{1}(p).
Then
c\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}
and
a\equiv d\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. So

i)

p\ell(d-1)+cd\ell^{2}-c\ell p\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}},

ii)

p\ell(d-1)+cd\ell^{2}-c\ell p\equiv d\ell+\ell+cd\ell+c\ell\equiv\ell(c+1)(d+1)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Therefore,

\mu(A,\ell)=\exp\left(\pi i\left(\frac{\ell(d-1)}{p}+\frac{cd\ell^{2}}{p^{2}}-\frac{c\ell}{p}\right)\right)=1

and we have our result.
∎

3.
Proofs of main results

3.1.
Modularity and transformation results

Let
\mathcal{F}^{*}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)=\dfrac{\eta(p^{2}z)}{\eta(z)}\,\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)
. The generating function of the crank due to Andrews and Garvan is given by

\displaystyle C(z,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m}M(m,n)\,z^{m}\,q^{n}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1-q^{n}}{(1-zq^{n})(1-z^{-1}q^{n})}.

Replacing
z
by
\zeta_{p}^{\ell}
in the above expression and multiplying both sides by
\eta(p^{2}z)
, we can express the crank generating function in terms of our eta functions as

(3.1)

\eta(p^{2}z)\,\mathcal{C}_{p}\left(z\right)=(1-\zeta_{p}^{\ell})\,\mathcal{F}^{*}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right),~\text{where}~\mathcal{C}_{p}\left(z\right)=q^{-\frac{1}{24}}C(\zeta_{p}^{\ell},q).

This leads to our first main result stated in the introduction section. We restate it here.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p>3
be prime. Then the function

\eta(p^{2}z)\,\mathcal{C}_{p}\left(z\right)

is a weakly holomorphic modular form of weight
1
on the group
\Gamma_{0}(p^{2})\cap\Gamma_{1}(p)
.

In the light of Equation (
3.1
), the proof of the theorem is equivalent to showing that
\mathcal{F}^{*}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)
is a weakly holomorphic modular form of weight
1
on the group
\Gamma_{0}(p^{2})\cap\Gamma_{1}(p)
. The transformation condition holds using Corollary
2.3
and the well-known result that
\dfrac{\eta(p^{2}z)}{\eta(z)}
is a modular function on
\Gamma_{0}(p^{2})
when
p>3
is prime. That is

\mathcal{F}^{*}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.=\mathcal{F}^{*}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)

for
A\in\Gamma_{0}(p^{2})\cap\Gamma_{1}(p)
.

We next check the cusp conditions and show that
\mathcal{F}^{*}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.(z)
when expanded as a series in

has only finitely many terms with negative exponents for
A\in\mbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. By Theorems

[

\mathcal{F}^{*}\left(\frac{\ell}{p};z\right)\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.=\dfrac{\eta(p^{2}z)}{\eta(z)}\dfrac{\eta(z)^{2}}{E_{0,\ell}(z)}\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right.=\dfrac{\eta(p^{2}z)}{\eta(z)}\,\left\arrowvert\,\left[A\right]_{1}\right..\varepsilon_{A}\dfrac{\eta(z)^{2}}{E_{\ell c,\ell d}(z)},

where

is a modular function on
\Gamma_{0}(p^{2})
and the definition of

∎

We note that

. In addition, if

\displaystyle=N(1,5,5n+4)=\cdots=N(4,5,5n+4)=\tfrac{1}{5}p(5n+4),

6.
Concluding remarks

Building on our work on symmetries of the rank generating function
[
14
]
, we have found analogous results for the crank generating function in this paper. The transformation and modularity properties as well as the
p
-dissection identities are similar for the rank and crank functions. It would be interesting to examine the underlying theory behind this resemblance more closely. Two other natural problems to consider are finding crank dissection identities for higher primes
p\geq 23
and also exploring such symmetries for other partition statistics and also their overpartition analogues.

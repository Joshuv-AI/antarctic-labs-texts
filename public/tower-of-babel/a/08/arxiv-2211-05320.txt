Regularity of four dimensional Willmore-type hypersurfaces
Yann Bernard, and Peter Olamide Olanipekun

Abstract
A four dimensional conformally invariant energy is studied. This energy generalises the well known two-dimensional Willmore energy. Although not positive definite, it includes minimal hypersurfaces as critical points. We compute its first variation and by applying Noether’s theorem to the invariances, we derive some conservation laws which are satisfied by its critical points and with good analytical dispositions. In particular, we show that its critical points are smooth. We also investigate other possible four dimensional generalisations of the Willmore energy, and give strong evidence that critical points of such energies do not include minimal hypersurfaces.

The main objects of this paper are the four dimensional Willmore energy and its critical points. We highlight the following interesting properties about the Willmore energy which will guide our discussion on four dimensional generalizations of the Willmore energy.

(a).

The Willmore energy is conformally invariant up to Gauss-Bonnet terms.

(b).

The leading order operator in the Willmore equation is linear.

(c).

Minimal surfaces are critical points of the Willmore energy.

(d).

The Willmore energy is non-negative.

1.1
A four dimensional Willmore-type energy

Generalising the Willmore energy to higher dimensions has been a question of interest for a long time. It has been often asked what properties or conditions must be satisfied by an energy that would qualify as a generalised higher dimensional version of the Willmore energy.
This essentially leads us to ask if there is a four dimensional Willmore-type energy preserving the properties (
a
)-(
d
) above.

By considering an energy involving a linear combination of the quantities

(\vec{h}_{0})_{ij}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})^{jk}(\vec{h}_{0})_{kl}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})^{li}\,,\quad(\vec{h}_{0})_{ij}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})^{ij}(\vec{h}_{0})_{kl}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})^{kl}\quad\mbox{and}\,((\vec{h}_{0})_{ij}\cdot(\vec{h}_{0})^{jk}(\vec{h}_{0})_{k}^{i})^{4/3}

one can easily construct a four dimensional conformally invariant energy. However, preliminary computations show that such energies do not preserve the properties (
b
) and (
c
) above (see for instance those considered in
[
39
]
and in Section 5 of
[
37
]
). Thus a different approach, which we shall now describe, is needed.

There is a relationship between the problem of finding a suitable higher dimensional generalisation of the Willmore energy and the singular Yamabe problem. Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with boundary and let
\Sigma
be an hypersurface, the singular Yamabe problem asks for a defining function
u
for the boundary
\Sigma:=\partial M
such that on the interior
\mathring{M}
, the scalar curvature
R_{g}
of the metric
g=u^{-2}\bar{g}
satisfies
R_{g}=-n(n-1)
.
This problem was first considered by Loewner and Nirenberg in
[
33
]
. Later,
Andersson, Chrúsciel and Friedrich
[
2
]
computed a conformal invariant which obstructs the smoothness of the function

. This obstruction to smooth boundary asymptotics for the Yamabe solution was identified by Gover et. al as a Willmore surface invariant in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see
[
19
,
20
,
22
]
). This identification led to the perception that higher dimensional generalisation of the Willmore invariant can be found via obstructions to smooth boundary asymptotics of the singular Yamabe problem on conformally compact manifolds of higher dimension. They further asked if there is a corresponding energy for such invariants, that is, is there a higher dimensional generalisation of the Willmore energy whose Euler-Lagrange equation corresponds to the identified obstruction? It turns out that the answer is positive (see
[
21
,
23
]
). Moreover, the following four dimensional generalisation of the Willmore energy was identified

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Sigma):=\int_{\Sigma}(|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}-|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+7|\vec{H}|^{4})\,\,\,d\textnormal{vol}_{g}

(4)

where notations have been slightly adjusted as follows:
\vec{H}:=\frac{1}{4}g^{ij}\vec{h}_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}\vec{h}^{j}_{j}
,
|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}:=\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{H}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\vec{H}
, where
\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}
is the projection of the Levi-Civita connection, acting on
\vec{H}
, onto the normal bundle,
|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}:=(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}_{ij})(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}^{ij})
and
|\vec{H}|^{4}:=(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{H})^{2}
. This energy was first discussed in codimension one

1.2
Regularity Proof Strategy

1.2.1
General literature on the strategy in two dimensions

The two dimensional Willmore energy is finite as soon as the Willmore immersion
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,\infty}\cap W^{2,2}
. This implies that
\vec{h}\in L^{2}
.
This fact, when incorporated into the Willmore equation, gives no analytical meaning as the cubic curvature terms
(\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h})\vec{h}-2|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}
(or more importantly
\Delta_{\perp}\vec{H}
as per (
2
)) would not even belong to
L^{1}
. However, this issue can be resolved with the help of conservation laws.
In the seminal article
[
40
]
, the author found such conservation laws. It was shown that the fourth-order Willmore equation, associated with the two-dimensional Willmore energy, can be expressed in divergence forms with several analytical implications. These divergence forms were elegantly obtained, in
[
6
]
, by applying Noether’s theorem (a general result applicable in any dimension) to the invariances of the Willmore energy and several other pre-existing results were rediscovered. Indeed, there exist some 1-forms
\vec{V}_{\textnormal{tra}}
,
{V}_{\textnormal{dil}}
and
\vec{V}_{\textnormal{rot}}
such that

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{W}}=d^{\star}\vec{V}_{\textnormal{tra}}=\vec{0}\,\,,\quad d^{\star}{V}_{\textnormal{dil}}={0}\,\,,\quad d^{\star}\vec{V}_{\textnormal{rot}}=\vec{0}

(6)

where
\vec{\mathcal{W}}
is the Willmore invariant. The quantity
\vec{V}_{\textnormal{tra}}
is geometric since it depends on
\vec{\mathcal{W}}
. The quantities
{V}_{\textnormal{dil}}
and
\vec{V}_{\textnormal{rot}}
are also geometric since they depend on
\vec{V}_{\textnormal{tra}}
.
Applying Poincaré lemma to (
6
), one finds the primitives
\vec{L}
,
S
and
\vec{R}
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d^{\star}\vec{L}=\vec{V}_{\textnormal{tra}}\,\,,\quad d^{\star}{S}={V}_{\textnormal{dil}}\,\,,&d^{\star}\vec{R}=\vec{V}_{\textnormal{rot}}\\
d\vec{L}=\vec{0}\,\,,\quad\quad\quad d{S}={0}\,\,,&d\vec{R}=\vec{0}\\
\end{array}\right.

where the Hodge star
\star
, the exterior differential
d
and codifferential
d^{\star}
are understood in terms of the induced metric
g
.
Further computations
[
6
]
yield the following system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|g|^{1/2}\Delta_{g}(\star S)&=\star\big(d(\star\vec{n})\cdot d(\star\vec{R})\big)\\
|g|^{1/2}\Delta_{g}(\star\vec{R})&=\star\big(d(\star\vec{n})\bullet d(\star\vec{R})\big)+\star\big(d(\star\vec{n})\,\,d(\star{S})\big)\\
|g|^{1/2}\Delta_{g}\vec{\Phi}&=\star\big(d(\star\vec{R})\bullet d\vec{\Phi}\big)+\star\big(d(\star{S})\,\,d\vec{\Phi}\big)\\
\end{array}\right.

where

denotes the Gauss map on
\Sigma
. The system (

) possesses a Jacobian structure suitable for analysis

The question of regularity of the two dimensional Willmore surface is already settled in literature. For instance, it was proved in
[
8
]
and
[
40
]
that Willmore immersions associated with the two dimensional Willmore energy are smooth. The proof techniques heavily rely on the structure of the system (
1.2.1
). The regularity assumptions on
\vec{\Phi}
imply that
\vec{n}
,
S
and
\vec{R}
belong to the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}.
Thus it follows that
\Delta_{g}(\star S)
,
\Delta_{g}(\star\vec{R})
and
\Delta_{g}\vec{\Phi}
all belong to
L^{1}
; this information is critical for regularity purpose. However, the Jacobian structure of system (
1.2.1
) encodes an extra regularity. By using Wente estimates (see Chapter 3 of
[
27
]
,
[
8
]
), one improves the regularity of
(\star S)
and
(\star\vec{R})
by showing that they both belong to
W^{1,p}
for some
p>2
.
The third equation in (
1.2.1
) links the primitives
(\star S)
and
(\star\vec{R})
back to the Willmore immersion
\vec{\Phi}
. In fact, it follows that
\vec{\Phi}
belongs to
W^{2,p}
. By applying a bootstrapping argument, one concludes that
\vec{\Phi}
is smooth.

These formulations and results lead us to ask if analogous results can be obtained in four dimensions. Firstly, what conservation laws are satisfied by the critical points of the energy
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)
?
Understanding Willmore-type equations in higher dimensions, from analytical perspective, can be generally a difficult problem.
For instance, the Willmore-type equation associated with (
4
) is a sixth-order nonlinear partial differential equation, the study of which is rare in literature.
Secondly, one asks if the regularity proof procedure in two dimensions can be applied in four dimensions. These questions are clearly answered in this paper. We develop a regularity proof for the critical points of the energy
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)
.

1.2.2
Difficulties of this paper and our proof strategy

We begin with the statement of our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\vec{\Phi}\in W^{3,2}\cap W^{1,\infty}
be a non-degenerate critical point of the energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Sigma):=\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla\vec{H}|^{2}-|\vec{H}\cdot\vec{h}|^{2}+7|\vec{H}|^{2}\right)d\textnormal{vol}_{g}.

(10)

Then
\vec{\Phi}
is smooth with the estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert DH\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}+\frac{1}{r}\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\left\lVert DH\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{1})}\right)\quad\quad\forall\,r<1

where
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
is any ball of radius
r
.

It is interesting to note that the proof techniques employed in four dimensions is remarkably different from the regularity proofs of similar problems in two dimensions (such as the Willmore surfaces, harmonic maps of surfaces etc). Unlike the two dimensional case, the corresponding expressions for
\vec{L}
,
S
and
\vec{R}
are much more intricate. Also, the system of equations does not seem to possess Jacobian structure, hence we do not use Wente estimates in our proof. However with the help of a very deep Bourgain-Brezis type result and classical results of Miranda
[
36
]
and Di Fazio
[
15
]
, we are able to obtain preliminary results needed to begin the regularity proof. It is important to remark that one can bypass the lack of a Jacobian structure by applying Bourgain-Brezis result to conservation laws derived from the invariances of the energy.
This present paper is the first step towards the analytical study of the Willmore-type energy (
4
) which is new in literature.

Let
\Sigma
be a four dimensional hypersurface and let
\Omega\subset\Sigma
be a chart. The conformal invariance of
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)

where the quantities
\vec{V}
and
\vec{M}
depend on the derivatives of
\vec{\Phi}
, up to fifth order.
The Poincaré Lemma then yields the existence of vector valued 2-forms
\vec{L}_{0},\vec{L}
and
\vec{R}_{0}
and a scalar valued 2-form
S_{0}
satsifying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta S_{0}=\textnormal{Jacobian}(d(\star\vec{\eta}),d\vec{R}_{0})\\

Since the energy
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)
is not bounded below, we define a non negative finite energy

which will serve as the upper bound for some of our estimates. The steps are as follows.

•

Regularity of
\vec{V}
,
\vec{L}_{0}
and
\vec{L}
: the energy hypothesis
\vec{\Phi}\in W^{3,2}\cap W^{1,\infty}
and the dual Sobolev injection
\dot{W}^{-1,4/3}\subset\dot{W}^{-2,2}
imply that
\vec{V}\in\dot{W}^{-2,2}\oplus L^{1}

. A Bourgain-Brezis result allows us to find

which solves the first equation in (

. The latter and the fact that
\vec{L}=\star(\vec{L}_{0}-d|\vec{H}|^{2}\wedge d\vec{\Phi})
then gives

with a similar estimate.

•

Regularity of
\vec{u}
,

and
\vec{R}_{0}
: a classical result of Miranda
[

and some standard elliptic estimate helps us find the estimate
\|D\vec{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\lesssim E(\Omega)

. Since

2.1

Definition 2.1

Let

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{W}}=\vec{0}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}(\Delta^{2}H)\vec{n}-\frac{1}{2}(|h|^{2}\Delta H)\vec{n}-4|\nabla H|^{2}\vec{H}+2(\nabla_{i}H\nabla_{j}H)\vec{h}^{ij}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\Delta(H|h|^{2})\vec{n}-2\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}(H^{2}h^{ik})\vec{n}-2H^{2}h^{ik}h_{mk}\vec{h}_{i}^{m}-28|H|^{4}\vec{H}

2.2
The other energies

The energy (
4
) is unbounded from below as observed by Robin-Graham and Reichert in
[
24

but we can add the energy
\mu\int_{\Sigma}|h_{0}|^{4}\,\,d\textnormal{vol}_{g}

Proposition 2.5

(See Proposition 6.6 of
[
24
]
)
.

Let
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{5}
be an immersion. If
\mu\geq\frac{1}{12}
, then the energy
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)+\mu\int_{\Sigma}|h_{0}|^{4}\,\,d\textnormal{vol}_{g}
is nonnegative.

Proof.

By using the formula
(h_{0})_{ij}=h_{ij}-Hg_{ij}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)+\mu\int_{\Sigma}|h_{0}|^{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H|^{2}-|H|^{2}(|h_{0}|^{2}+4|H|^{2})+7|H|^{4}+\mu|h_{0}|^{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H|^{2}+3|H|^{4}-|H|^{2}|h_{0}|^{2}+\mu|h_{0}|^{4}.

Since
\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H|^{2}\geq 0
, a sufficient condition for

\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)+\mu\int_{\Sigma}|h_{0}|^{4}
to be nonnegative is that the quadratic form
3x^{2}-xy+\mu y^{2}
be nonnegative definite. This gives that
3\mu-\frac{1}{4}\geq 0
which implies
\mu\geq\frac{1}{12}
.
∎

Remark 2.6

Among all energies of the form

\displaystyle|h_{0}|^{4}=|h|^{4}-8|Hh|^{2}+16|H|^{4}.

\displaystyle\delta|h|^{4}

\displaystyle=2|\vec{h}|^{2}\delta|\vec{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=A^{s}\nabla_{s}|h|^{4}+8(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{h}^{is})(\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{s})|h|^{2}+4(\vec{h}^{ij}\cdot\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\vec{B})|h|^{2}

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{W}}_{3}:=4\vec{h}^{is}(\vec{h}_{ij}\cdot\vec{h}^{j}_{s})|h|^{2}-4|h|^{4}\vec{H}+4\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}(h^{ij}|h|^{2})\vec{n}.

The critical point of the energy

2.3
Conservation laws

In this section, the left hand side of the Willmore-type equation (
12
) is reformulated in terms of some divergence free quantities with the help of Noether’s theorem.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let the Lagrangian

L:\big\{(x,y,z):(x,y)\in D\times E,\,z\in T_{y}E\otimes T^{*}_{x}D\big\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

be continuously differentiable. By choosing a
C^{1}
density measure
d\mu(x)
on
\Omega
, we can define a functional
\mathcal{L}
on the set of maps
C^{1}(\Omega,E)
via

\mathcal{L}(u):=\int_{\Omega}L(x,u(x),du(x))\,d\mu(x).

A tangent vector
X
is an
infinitesimal symmetry
for
\mathcal{L}
if and only if

\frac{\partial L}{\partial y^{i}}(x,y,z)X^{i}(y)+\frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{i}_{\alpha}}(x,y,z)\frac{\partial X^{i}}{\partial y^{j}}(y)z^{j}_{\alpha}=0.

The vector field
J
defined by

J^{\alpha}:=\rho(x)\frac{\partial L}{\partial z^{i}_{\alpha}}(x,u,du)X^{i}(u),

where
\rho
satisfies
d\mu(x)=\rho(x)dx^{1}\cdots dx^{n}
, is the well known
Noether current
in Physics. We are now ready to state a version of Noether’s theorem relevant for our purpose.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
X
be a Lipschitz tangent vector field on
E
, which is an infinitesimal symmetry for
\mathcal{L}
. If
u:\Omega\rightarrow E
is a critical point of
\mathcal{L}
, then

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{\partial J^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}=0,

where
\{x^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1,...,n}
are coordinates on
\Omega
such that
d\mu(x)=\rho(x)dx^{1}\cdots dx^{n}
.

The following result holds by applying Noether’s theorem to the invariances of the energy
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma).

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\vec{\Phi}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{5}
be a smooth immersion of a 4-dimensional hypersurface
\Sigma
and let
\mathcal{\vec{W}}
be as defined in Proposition
2.2
with
\mathcal{\vec{W}}=\vec{0}
. Define the following quantity

\displaystyle\vec{V}^{j}:

\displaystyle=|\nabla H|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}-2(\nabla^{j}H\nabla_{i}H)\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}(h^{j}_{i}\Delta H)\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{j}\Delta H)\vec{n}

\displaystyle\quad-2\nabla_{i}(H^{2}\vec{h}^{ij})+4\nabla_{i}(H^{2}h^{ij})\vec{n}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{j}(|h|^{2}\vec{H})-H^{2}h^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla^{j}(|h|^{2}H)\vec{n}+7|\vec{H}|^{4}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}+7\nabla^{j}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})-14\nabla^{j}(|\vec{H}|^{2}H)\vec{n}.

Then the following conservation laws hold by the invariance of
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma)
under translation, dilation and rotation respectively.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vec{0}&=\nabla_{j}\vec{V}^{j}\\
0&=\nabla_{j}(\vec{\Phi}\cdot\vec{V}^{j}-\nabla^{j}|\vec{H}|^{2})\\
\vec{0}&=\nabla_{j}(\vec{\Phi}\wedge\vec{V}^{j}+\vec{M}^{j})\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\vec{M}^{j}
denotes the two-vector

\displaystyle\vec{M}^{j}:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta H)\vec{n}\wedge\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}+2(Hh^{ij})\vec{H}\wedge\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}|h|^{2}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}-7|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H}\wedge\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}.

Proof.

Translation.

Consider a translation
\delta\vec{\Phi}=\vec{a}
, where
\vec{a}
is a constant vector in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
. Then

A_{j}=\vec{a}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\vec{B}=\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{a}.

If
\vec{U}
is any vector, then it holds

\vec{U}\cdot\vec{B}=\vec{a}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}

and

\displaystyle\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{B}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}(\vec{B}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U})-\vec{B}\cdot\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}

\displaystyle=\vec{a}\cdot\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}-\vec{a}\cdot\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}

\displaystyle=\vec{a}\cdot\pi_{T}\nabla_{j}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}.

With the help of the latter, we have

\displaystyle\vec{U}\cdot\Delta_{\perp}\vec{B}

\displaystyle=\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\cdot\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}\vec{B}=\nabla^{i}(\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B})-(\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U})\cdot\nabla_{i}\vec{B}

\displaystyle=\vec{a}\cdot\left[\nabla^{i}\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}-\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\vec{U}\right].

Hence

V^{j}=\vec{a}\cdot\vec{V}^{j},

where

\displaystyle\vec{V}^{j}

\displaystyle=|\nabla H|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}(|h|^{2}\nabla^{j}H)\vec{n}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{j}\Delta H)\vec{n}-\frac{1}{2}\pi_{T}\nabla^{j}(\Delta H\vec{n})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{i}\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}(\nabla^{j}H\vec{n})-\frac{1}{2}\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{i}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}\vec{H}-2\pi_{T}\nabla_{i}(H^{2}\vec{h}^{ij})

\displaystyle\quad+2\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla_{i}(H^{2}\vec{h}^{ij})-\frac{1}{2}\pi_{T}\nabla^{j}(|h|^{2}\vec{H})+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}(|h|^{2}\vec{H})-H^{2}h^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\quad+7|\vec{H}|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}+7\pi_{T}\nabla^{j}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})-7\pi_{\vec{n}}\nabla^{j}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})

\displaystyle=|\nabla H|^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}-2(\nabla^{j}H\nabla_{i}H)\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}(h^{j}_{i}\Delta H)\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{j}\Delta H)\vec{n}

\displaystyle\quad-2\nabla_{i}(H^{2}\vec{h}^{ij})+4\nabla_{i}(H^{2}h^{ij})\vec{n}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{j}(|h|^{2}\vec{H})-H^{2}h^{2}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\quad+\nabla^{j}(|h|^{2}H)\vec{n}+7|\vec{H}|^{4}\nabla^{j}\vec{\Phi}+7\nabla^{j}(|\vec{H}|^{2}\vec{H})-14\nabla^{j}(|\vec{H}|^{2}H)\vec{n}.

We note that

\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}\cdot\vec{V}^{j}=\Delta|\vec{H}|^{2}.

Dilation.

\Delta\vec{v}=\frac{5}{3}d\left(|\vec{H}|^{2}d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\right).

∎

3
Regularity Results

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is divided into different subsections of this section. In Subsection
3.1
, we develop generic regularity results applicable to our problem.
The goal of Subsection
3.2
is to understand the regularity of the quantities
\vec{V},\vec{L}_{0},\vec{L},S_{0},\vec{R}_{0}
and
\vec{u}
obtained in Section
2
. A major achievement of this paper is the existence of an invertible operator
\vec{\mathcal{P}}
used in creating new 2-forms:
\vec{L}_{1},S
and
\vec{R}
which are estimated in Subsection
3.3
. Subsection
3.4
answers the important question of how to link the regularity of the primitives

and

3.1
Set up and preliminary results

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a ball. The Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
of measurable functions from
\Omega
into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is defined by

W^{k,p}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\,:\,D^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\,\,\forall\,\,\alpha\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,|\alpha|\leq k\right\}

with the norm

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert D^{\alpha}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

The dual of
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
is
W^{-k,p}(\Omega)
.

The homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{k,p}(\Omega)
is the space of all
k
-weakly differentiable functions
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that
D^{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
|\alpha|=k
.

The closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
is denoted by
W_{0}^{k,p}(\Omega)
.

Suppose that
\vec{\Phi}\in W^{3,2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
is a critical point of the energy
\mathcal{E}(\Sigma).

By hypothesis, the coefficients of the metric tensor given by
g_{ij}:=\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi}\cdot\nabla_{j}\vec{\Phi}
lie in the space
W^{2,2}\cap L^{\infty}
. We are interested in the regularity of Willmore-type manifolds with no branch point, that is the metric does not degenerate. The metric coefficients are uniformly bounded from above and below and satisfy, in a local coordinate chart
\Omega
of
\Sigma
:

\displaystyle g_{ij}\simeq\delta_{ij}\quad\quad\textnormal{as (4 $\times$ 4)-matrices.}

In other words,
g_{ij}
satisfies on
\Omega

\displaystyle c^{-1}\delta_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}\leq g_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}\leq c\delta_{ij}u^{i}v^{j}

for some
c>0
and for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
. We have used
\delta_{ij}
to denote the
(i,j)-
component of the flat metric.

For
A\in\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
and
B\in\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
with local representations

A=\frac{1}{k!}A_{[i_{1}....i_{k}]}dx^{i_{1}}\wedge_{4}\cdots\wedge_{4}dx^{i_{k}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B=\frac{1}{k!}B_{[j_{1}....j_{k}]}dx^{j_{1}}\wedge_{4}\cdots\wedge_{4}dx^{j_{k}}

we define the usual inner product on
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{4}

\displaystyle\langle A,B\rangle:=\frac{1}{(2k)!}\int_{\Omega}g^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots g^{i_{k}j_{k}}A_{[i_{1}...i_{k}}B_{j_{1}...j_{k}]}dx^{1}\wedge_{4}\cdots\wedge_{4}dx^{4}.

Since the metric is controlled by the Euclidean metric on
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\langle A,B\rangle\simeq\langle A,B\rangle_{0}:=\frac{1}{(2k)!}\int_{\Omega}\delta^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots\delta^{i_{k}j_{k}}A_{[i_{1}...i_{k}}B_{j_{1}...j_{k}]}dx^{1}\wedge_{4}\cdots\wedge_{4}dx^{4}

which is the usual inner-product on forms for the flat metric
\delta
. Accordingly, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\equiv\sup_{\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}}=1}\langle A,B\rangle_{0}=\sup_{\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}}}=1}\langle A,B\rangle\quad\quad\forall\,\,p\in[1,\infty)\,,\,\,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

This fact will be used recurrently and tacitly in subsequent subsections, in particular, when calling upon duality arguments.

First, we discuss the regularising properties of an equation of the type

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta u=f&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(34)

Equivalently, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[u]:=\partial_{i}(|g|^{1/2}g^{ij}\partial_{j}u)=|g|^{1/2}f

where
\mathcal{L}
is a second-order partial differential operator. The coefficient
a^{ij}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
a^{ij}:=|g|^{1/2}g^{ij}
is clearly symmetric and
satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition: there exists some
c>0
such that

\displaystyle c^{-1}\delta^{ij}u_{i}v_{j}\leq a^{ij}u_{i}v_{j}\leq c\delta^{ij}u_{i}v_{j}\,,\,\,\forall\,\,u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Unfortunately, the coefficients
a^{ij}
are not Hölder continuous and standard regularity theory tools for elliptic equations cannot be applied but thanks to Claim
3.2
and Proposition
3.5
, we are still able to obtain the desired results.

Claim 3.2
.

The coefficients
a^{ij}
belong to the space
W^{2,2}\cap L^{\infty}
under pointwise multiplication.

By the Sobolev embedding theorem,
W^{2,2}\subset W^{1,4}
. Thus
a^{ij}
belong to
L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,4}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Although it is true that the product of a function in
L^{p}
and any metric component or its determinant remains in
L^{p}
, the same cannot be said of Sobolev spaces (or of their duals). This is because the metric coefficients are not sufficiently differentiable (not Lipschitz). But the coefficients belong to
L^{\infty}\cap W^{2,2}
by Claim
3.2
and the following holds.

Claim 3.4
.

Let
\alpha
be a function in
(L^{\infty}\cap W^{2,2})
. Let
\beta
be a function in
\dot{W}^{-1,2}
. Then the product
\alpha\beta
belongs to
\dot{W}^{-1,2}\oplus L^{4/3}\subset\dot{W}^{-1,2}
.

As a consequence, we have the norm

\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{W_{0}^{1,2}}\equiv\sup_{\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}}=1}\langle A,B\rangle_{0}\simeq\sup_{\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}}=1}\langle A,B\rangle.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a ball. Suppose that
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
u
be a weak solution of the problem

\begin{cases}\Delta u=f&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Then
D^{2}u\in L^{2}
and

||D^{2}u||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim||f||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Clearly
\mathcal{L}[u]=|g|^{1/2}f
belongs to
L^{2}
since
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. A classical result of Miranda
[
36
]
(see also Corollary 1.4 in
[
13
]
) then yields the desired estimate

\left\lVert D^{2}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert|g|^{1/2}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

Suppose that
u\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and
f=d^{\star}B
, where
B\in\Lambda^{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
, then (
34
) has the special structure

\Delta u=d^{\star}B

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[u_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}]\,\,dx^{i_{1}}\wedge_{4}\cdots\wedge_{4}dx^{i_{k}}

\displaystyle=|g|^{1/2}d^{\star}B

\displaystyle=\partial_{a}(|g|^{1/2}g^{ai_{k+1}}B_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k+1}})\,\,dx^{i_{1}}\wedge_{4}\cdots\wedge_{4}dx^{i_{k}}.

This is a decoupled system of
k
-uniformly elliptic equations in flat divergence form with coefficients in
W^{2,2}\cap L^{\infty}
which embeds in
VMO\cap L^{\infty}
. Such equations are studied by Di Fazio in
[
15
]
(see also
[
3
]
).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a ball. Suppose that
B\in\Lambda^{k+1}\otimes L^{p}(\Omega)
for
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Let
u\in\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
be the
k
-form satisfying

\begin{cases}\Delta u=d^{\star}B&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=0&\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Then

\left\lVert Du\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Using Theorem 2.1 in
[
15
]
, each component of
u
satisfies

\left\lVert Du_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert|g|^{1/2}g^{ai_{k+1}}B_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k+1}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

∎

We will require the following corollaries of Theorem 2.1 in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a ball. Suppose that
u\in\Lambda^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies the conditions

d^{\star}u=0\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad du\in\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega).

Then

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert du\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\Psi\in\Lambda^{2}\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. Standard Hodge theory guarantees there exist
\psi\in\Lambda^{3}\otimes W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
\theta\in\Lambda^{1}\otimes W^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that

\Psi=d\theta+d^{\star}\psi\quad\quad\mbox{with}\quad\quad d\psi=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad d^{\star}\theta=0.

We have, per Claim
3.4
,

\mathcal{L}[\psi]=|g|^{1/2}\Delta\psi=|g|^{1/2}d\Psi\,\,\in\,\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega).

Reasoning componentwise (set
\psi|_{\partial\Omega}=0
), we use
[
15
]
to arrive at

\displaystyle\left\lVert D\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert|g|^{1/2}d\Psi\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(35)

Set
\theta|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Observe that

\displaystyle\langle u,\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u,d\theta+d^{\star}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle d^{\star}u,\theta\rangle+\langle u,\theta\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\langle du,\psi\rangle+\langle u,\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=\langle du,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert du\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{cklh}}{\lesssim}\left\lVert du\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence the result

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert du\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}.

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a ball. Any
Q\in\Lambda^{3}(\Omega,\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))\otimes\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)
can be decomposed in the form

Q=dA+d^{\star}B\quad\quad\mbox{with}\quad\quad d^{\star}A=0\,,\,dB=0\,\,,\,\,A\in\Lambda^{2}\,\,,\,\,B\in\Lambda^{4}\,

and

\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\Psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. As in the proof of Proposition
3.7
, we have

\Psi=d\theta+d^{\star}\psi\quad\mbox{with}\quad d^{\star}\theta=0\,,\,d\psi=0,\,\,\theta|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\,\,\psi|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert D\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert|g|^{1/2}d\Psi\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(36)

Since
\star B
is a 0-form and
d\psi=0
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\star B,\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\star B,d\theta+d^{\star}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\star d^{\star}B,\psi\rangle+\langle\star B,\theta\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\langle\star B,\star\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=\langle\star Q-d^{\star}(\star A),\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\star Q,\psi\rangle-\langle\star A,d\psi\rangle-\langle\star A,\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\star Q\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{neededd}}{\lesssim}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

where Claim
3.4
has been used to obtain the last line. Hence

\displaystyle\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\simeq\left\lVert\star B\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}.

(37)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\langle A,\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A,d\theta+d^{\star}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle dA,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Q-d^{\star}B,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Q,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{W_{0}^{1,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}.

(38)

Combining the estimates (
37
) and (
38
) proves the result.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a ball and let

with
d^{\star}V=0.
There exists
w\in\Lambda^{2}\otimes\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)
such that

d^{\star}w=V\quad,\quad dw=0\quad,\quad\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{L^{1}\oplus\dot{W}^{-2,2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, we will need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\Omega
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. For
k\in(0,1)
, we denote by
\Omega_{k}
, the ball with same center as
\Omega
and with radius rescaled by
k
. Suppose
T\in\Lambda^{1}\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies

\left\lVert dT\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert d^{\star}T\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}\lesssim M,

for some constant
M>0
. Then

\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k})}\lesssim M+|\Omega|^{1/4}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a standard smooth cut-off function with

\mu|_{\Omega_{k}}=1\quad,\quad\mu|_{\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\Omega}=0\quad,\quad 0<\mu(x)<1\,\,\,\forall\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\left\lVert d(\mu T)\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert d\mu\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\lVert dT\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Omega|^{1/4}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+M\,,

(39)

where we have used Jensen’s inequality. By the same token, using

d^{\star}(\mu T)=\star(d\mu\wedge_{4}\star T)+\mu d^{\star}T\,,

we find

\displaystyle\left\lVert d^{\star}(\mu T)\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}\lesssim|\Omega|^{1/4}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+M\,.

(40)

Next, let
\Psi\in\Lambda^{1}\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. Consider the problem (componentwise)

\Delta\psi=\Psi\quad,\quad\psi|_{\partial\Omega}=0\quad,\quad\psi\in\Lambda^{1}\,.

Per Proposition
3.5
and the Sobolev embedding theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert d\psi\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert d^{\star}\psi\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert D^{2}\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(41)

Next, we compute

\displaystyle\langle\mu T,\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mu T,\Delta\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mu T,dd^{\star}\psi+d^{\star}d\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle d^{\star}(\mu T),d^{\star}\psi\rangle+\langle d(\mu T),d\psi\rangle+\langle d(\mu T),\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\langle d^{\star}(\mu T),\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{1b1},\eqref{1b2},\eqref{1b3}}{\lesssim}(|\Omega|^{1/4}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+M)\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This shows that

\left\lVert\mu T\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim|\Omega|^{1/4}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+M\,.

Since
\mu\equiv 1
on
\Omega_{k},
the desired conclusion follows.

Proposition 3.11

\displaystyle\left\lVert d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(B)}<\varepsilon

Proof.

3.2
The creation of three characteristic two-forms

\vec{f}=\nabla_{k}(h^{ij}\nabla^{k}H\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi})-\vec{h}^{ij}h_{ik}\nabla^{k}H-(\nabla^{k}H\nabla_{k}h^{ij})\nabla_{i}\vec{\Phi},

Next is to understand the regularity of

Clearly

\Delta\vec{\theta}=\vec{\Theta}\quad\mbox{on}\,\,\Omega\quad\mbox{with}\,\,\,\vec{\theta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\vec{0}.

Proposition
3.5
confirms that

\displaystyle\vec{\left\lVert D\theta\right\rVert}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lesssim\vec{\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert}_{W_{0}^{2,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\vec{\left\lVert\Theta\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(47)

Observe that

\displaystyle\langle\vec{\Theta},\vec{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta\vec{\theta},\vec{u}\rangle=\langle d\vec{\theta},d\vec{u}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle d\vec{\theta},\frac{5}{3}H^{2}d^{\star}\vec{\eta}-d^{\star}\vec{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{5}{3}\langle d\vec{\theta},H^{2}d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert H^{2}d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}\vec{\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert H^{2}d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}\vec{\left\lVert\Theta\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\vec{\left\lVert u\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert H^{2}d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left\lVert d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\overset{\eqref{ssssj}}{\lesssim}\varepsilon E(\Omega).

(48)

We have observed from equation (
33

\vec{u}\in\Lambda^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{5})
satisfies

\Delta\vec{u}=\frac{5}{3}d^{\star}(H^{2}d^{\star}\vec{\eta}),

which is of the type investigated in Proposition
3.6
.

We split
\vec{u}=\vec{u}_{0}+\vec{u}_{1}
where

\begin{cases}\Delta\vec{u}_{0}=\vec{0}&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,\vec{u}_{0}=\vec{u}&\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{cases}\quad\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\begin{cases}\Delta\vec{u}_{1}=\Delta\vec{u}&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega\\
\,\,\,\,\,\vec{u}_{1}=\vec{0}&\mbox{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

The equation for
\vec{u}_{1}
is handled as in Proposition
3.6
. We find

\displaystyle\left\lVert D\vec{u}_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert H^{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert d^{\star}\vec{\eta}\right\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\overset{\eqref{ssssj}}{\lesssim}\varepsilon E(\Omega).

(49)

In particular, the Sobolev embedding theorem gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\vec{u}_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon E(\Omega).

Hence by (
48

\displaystyle\left\lVert\vec{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon E(\Omega).

(50)

On the other hand, the Caccioppoli inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert D\vec{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega^{\prime})}\lesssim\left\lVert D\vec{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})}\lesssim\left\lVert\vec{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\overset{\eqref{pio}}{\lesssim}\varepsilon E(\Omega)\quad\quad\forall\,\,\,\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega

(51)

Together with (
49
), the latter gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert D\vec{u}\right\rVert_{L^{4/3}(\Omega^{\prime})}\lesssim\varepsilon E(\Omega)\quad\quad\forall\,\,\,\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega.

(52)

Using Claim
3.4
and the estimates we have of the quantities
\vec{u}
,
\vec{J}
and
\vec{L}
, we are now ready to state the first regularity property:

dS_{0}\,\,,\,\,d\vec{R}_{0}\in\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)

with the estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert dS_{0}\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}+||d\vec{R}_{0}||_{\dot{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim E(\Omega_{1})\quad\quad\forall\,\,\Omega\Subset\Omega_{1}.

(53)

Since
S_{0}
and
\vec{R}_{0}
are co-closed, by Proposition (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+||\vec{R}_{0}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim E(\Omega_{1})\quad\quad\Omega\Subset\Omega_{1}.

(54)

Proposition 3.13
.

The operator

Proof.

We have

\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{pq}:=\big(\mathcal{\vec{P}}(\vec{\ell})\big)_{pq}=\vec{\ell}_{p}\wedge\nabla_{q}\vec{\Phi}-\vec{\ell}_{q}\wedge\nabla_{p}\vec{\Phi}.

Let

be the normal vector to the Willmore-type hypersurface
\Sigma
, defined on a small patch
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
by

\vec{n}:=\star_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}\star(d\vec{\Phi}\overset{\wedge}{\wedge}_{4}d\vec{\Phi}\overset{\wedge}{\wedge}_{4}d\vec{\Phi}\overset{\wedge}{\wedge}_{4}d\vec{\Phi})\in\Lambda^{0}(\Omega,\Lambda^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{5}))

where

acts on the 4-vectors in the ambient space while
\star
acts on the 4-forms in the parameter space.

We compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{pq}\cdot(\vec{n}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi})

\displaystyle=(\vec{\ell}_{p}\wedge\nabla_{q}\vec{\Phi}-\vec{\ell}_{q}\wedge\nabla_{p}\vec{\Phi})\cdot(\vec{n}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi})

\displaystyle=3\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\ell}_{p}\,,

so that

\displaystyle\vec{\eta}\cdot\vec{\ell}_{p}=\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{pq}\cdot(\vec{n}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi}).

(56)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{pq}\cdot(\nabla^{p}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi})

\displaystyle=(\vec{\ell}_{p}\wedge\nabla_{q}\vec{\Phi}-\vec{\ell}_{q}\wedge\nabla_{p}\vec{\Phi})\cdot(\nabla^{p}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi})

\displaystyle=6\vec{\ell}_{p}\cdot\nabla^{p}\vec{\Phi}\,,

so that

\displaystyle\vec{\ell}_{p}\cdot\nabla^{p}\vec{\Phi}=\frac{1}{6}\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{pq}\cdot(\nabla^{p}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi}).

(57)

Finally, we check that

\displaystyle\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{pq}\cdot(\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi})

\displaystyle=(\vec{\ell}_{p}\wedge\nabla_{q}\vec{\Phi}-\vec{\ell}_{q}\wedge\nabla_{p}\vec{\Phi})\cdot(\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi})

\displaystyle=2\vec{\ell}_{p}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\delta^{i}_{p}\vec{\ell}_{q}\cdot\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{sothat}}{=}2\vec{\ell}_{p}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}+\frac{1}{6}\delta^{i}_{p}\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{sq}\cdot(\nabla^{s}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi}),

so that

\displaystyle\vec{\ell}_{p}\cdot\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{pq}\cdot(\nabla^{i}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi})-\frac{1}{12}\delta^{i}_{p}\mathcal{\vec{P}}_{sq}\cdot(\nabla^{s}\vec{\Phi}\wedge\nabla^{q}\vec{\Phi}).

(58)

According to (

can be totally recovered from

via

That
\mathcal{\vec{P}}^{-1}
maps

onto itself continuously is trivial.

∎

Observe that given any

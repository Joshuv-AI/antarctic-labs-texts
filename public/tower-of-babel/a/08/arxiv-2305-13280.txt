Effective electromagnetic wave properties of disordered stealthy hyperuniform layered media beyond the quasistatic regime Journal: opticajournal
Jaeuk Kim, Salvatore Torquato

Abstract
Disordered stealthy hyperuniform dielectric composites exhibit novel electromagnetic wave transport properties in two and three dimensions. Here, we carry out the first study of the electromagnetic properties of one-dimensional (1D) disordered stealthy hyperuniform layered media. From an exact nonlocal theory, we derive an approximation formula for the effective dynamic dielectric constant tensor 𝜺 e ( 𝐤 q , ω ) \mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q},\omega\right)}} of general 1D media that is valid well beyond the quasistatic regime and apply it to 1D stealthy hyperuniform systems. We consider incident waves of transverse polarization, frequency ω \omega , and wavenumber k q k_{q} . Our formula for 𝜺 e ( 𝐤 q , ω ) \mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q},\omega\right)}} , which is given in terms of the spectral density , leads to a closed-form relation for the transmittance T T . Our theoretical predictions are in excellent agreement with finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) simulations. Stealthy hyperuniform layered media have perfect transparency intervals up to a finite wavenumber, implying no Anderson localization, but non-stealthy hyperuniform media are not perfectly transparent. Our predictive theory provides a new path for the inverse design of the wave characteristics of disordered layered media, which are readily fabricated, by engineering their spectral densities.

2
Theory

2.1
Exact Strong-Contrast Expansion

Here, we briefly summarize the general nonlocal strong-contrast-expansion formalism of the effective dynamic dielectric constant tensor
\mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q},\omega\right)}}
for 3D two-phase media with arbitrary symmetries
[
31
]
.
(The strong-property-fluctuation theory
[
57
,
58
]
corresponds to a special case of our strong-contrast formalism, as detailed in Section 1 of
Supplement 1
.)
We consider a macroscopically large two-phase composite specimen in three dimensions embedded inside an infinitely large reference phase
q

[
59
,
31
]
.
For simplicity, we take the phase
q
to be the matrix phase (i.e.,
q\hskip-2.0pt=1,2)
and assume that phases 1 and 2 are nonmagnetic and dielectrically isotropic with real-valued and frequency-independent dielectric constants.
These assumptions imply the linear dispersion relation in the reference phase [i.e.,
\mathinner{k_{q}\mathopen{\left(\omega\right)}}\equiv\absolutevalue{\vect{k}_q(\omega)}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{q}}\omega/c
], where
c
is the speed of light in vacuum, and thus we henceforth do not explicitly indicate the
\omega
dependence.

The general nonlocal strong-contrast expansion is a series expansion of the linear fractional form of the tensor
\mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}
, given as

\displaystyle\phi_{p}\bm{L}_{p}^{(q)}\cdot\quantity(
\qty{\tens{I} + \tens{D}^{(q)} \cdot\qty[\fn{\tens{\varepsilon}_e}{\vect{k}_q} - {\varepsilon}_q \tens{I}]}
\cdot\qty[\fn{\tens{\varepsilon}_e}{\vect{k}_q} - {\varepsilon}_q \tens{I}]^{-1}
)\cdot\phi_{p}\bm{L}_{p}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\phi_{p}\bm{L}_{p}^{(q)}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathinner{\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{(p)}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}},

(1)

where
p(\neq q)
indicates the polarized phase,
\bm{L}^{(q)}_{p}
is the expansion parameter defined as

\displaystyle\bm{L}^{(q)}_{p}\equiv

\displaystyle\quantity({\varepsilon}_p - {\varepsilon}_q)\quantity[\tens{I} + \tens{D}^{(q)} \qty({\varepsilon}_p - {\varepsilon}_q)]^{-1},

(2)

and
\mathinner{\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{(p)}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}
is a wave-vector-dependent second-rank tensor that is a functional involving the set of correlation functions
S_{1}^{(p)},S_{2}^{(p)},\ldots,S_{n}^{(p)}
and products of the principal part of the dyadic Green’s function
\mathinner{\bm{H}^{(q)}\mathopen{\left({\bf r}\right)}}
; see Section 1 of
Supplement 1
.
The series expansion [(
1
)] has four salient features.
First, (
1
) is derived from a spatially nonlocal averaged constitutive relation
[
60
,
50
,
61
]
, resulting in an expansion that is valid from the long- to intermediate-wavelength regimes.
Second, (
1
) exactly treats multiple scattering to all orders at a given incident wave vector
{\bf k}_{q}
when the nonlocal homogenization theory is valid because the terms
\mathinner{\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{(p)}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}
for
n=2,\ldots
in (
1
) explicitly account for complete microstructural information (the infinite set of
S_{2},S_{3},\ldots
) to infinite order
[
31
]
; see Section 1 of
Supplement 1
for details.
Third, the choice of the shape of the infinitesimal exclusion region (i.e.,
\bm{D}^{(q)}
) leads to a different expansion parameter
\bm{L}_{p}^{(q)}
that determines the convergence properties.
Thus, unlike standard multiple-scattering theories
[
38
,
62
,
63
]
, here one can naturally ‘tune’ the general series expansion to obtain distinctly different approximations suited for certain classes of microstructures.
Fourth, the left side of (
1
) is a linear fractional transformation of
\mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}
rather than
\mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}
itself, which leads to the rapid convergence of the strong-contrast expansions so that its lower-order truncations approximate well higher-order functionals (i.e., multiple scattering) of the exact series to all orders in terms of lower-order diagrams
[
31
]
.

2.2
Multiple-Scattering Approximations for Layered Media

We can now extract from the exact strong-contrast expansion [(
1
)] accurate multiple-scattering approximations for layered media by truncating the expansion at the
n
-point level.
We focus here on such a formula at the two-point level that depends on the spectral density
\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}\right)}}
because it is still accurate and easy to compute.
For simplicity, we restrict ourselves to normally incident waves (Fig.
1
), and thus the effective dielectric constant now depends on the wavenumber
k_{q}
.
We outline the derivation here and provide details in Section 2 of
Supplement 1
.

Since layered media have rotational symmetry about the
z
-axis and translational symmetry in the
x\hskip-2.0pt-\hskip-2.0pty
plane, the spectral density can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}\right)}}=(2\pi)^{2}\mathinner{\delta\mathopen{\left(k_{x}\right)}}\mathinner{\delta\mathopen{\left(k_{y}\right)}}\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(k_{z}\right)}},

(3)

where
\mathinner{\delta\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}
is the one-dimensional Dirac delta function, and
\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(k_{z}\right)}}
is the spectral density of 1D two-phase media.
For 1D packings of identical hard rods of radius
a
and packing fraction
\phi_{2}
,
\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(k_{z}\right)}}=\phi_{2}[2\sin^{2}(k_{z}a)]/({k_{z}}^{2}a)\mathinner{S\mathopen{\left(k_{z}\right)}}

[
54
,
64
]
, where
\mathinner{S\mathopen{\left(k_{z}\right)}}
is the structure factor of the rod centers.
Due to these symmetries, when applying the series [(
1
)] to layered media, we utilize the feature 3 discussed in Section 2.
2.1
by choosing a disk-like exclusion region normal to the
z
-axis
[
31
]
, leading to

\displaystyle\bm{D}^{(q)}=

\displaystyle{\varepsilon_{q}}^{-1}\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}},\quad\bm{L}^{(q)}_{p}=\beta_{pq}\Big[\varepsilon_{p}\quantity(\tens{I}-\uvect{z}\uvect{z})+\varepsilon_{q}\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}}\Big],

(4)

where
\hat{{\bf z}}
is a unit vector along the
z
-direction and
\beta_{pq}
is the one-dimensional counterpart of the
dielectric polarizability
, defined as
\beta_{pq}\equiv 1-\varepsilon_{q}/\varepsilon_{p}
.
Here,
\bm{L}^{(q)}_{p}
is obtained by substituting
\bm{D}^{(q)}
in (
4
) into (
2
).
Using (
4
) and the assumption of normal incidence, the general expression for
\mathinner{\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{(p)}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
simplifies as

\displaystyle\mathinner{\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{2}^{(p)}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{p}\beta_{pq})^{2}\frac{\mathinner{F^{\mathrm{(1D)}}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}}{\varepsilon_{q}}(\bm{I}-\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}}),

(5)

\displaystyle\mathinner{F^{\mathrm{(1D)}}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}=

\displaystyle\frac{{k}^{2}}{\pi}\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{q_z}\frac{\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(q_{z}\right)}}}{{q_{z}}^{2}-{(2k)}^{2}}+\frac{ik}{4}[\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(0\right)}}+\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(2k\right)}}],

(6)

where
\mathrm{p.v.}
stands for the Cauchy principal value.
Note that
\mathinner{F^{\mathrm{(1D)}}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
is the nonlocal attenuation function for 1D two-phase media, whose 2D and 3D counterparts were derived in Ref.
[
31
]
.

Applying (
4
) and (
5
) into the second-order truncation of the series [(
1
)] yields

\displaystyle\frac{(\phi_{p}\varepsilon_{p}\beta_{pq})^{2}}{\varepsilon_{q}(\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}/\varepsilon_{q}-1)}(\bm{I}-\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}})+\frac{(\phi_{p}\varepsilon_{q}\beta_{pq})^{2}}{\varepsilon_{q}(1-\varepsilon_{q}/\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}})}\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{p}\beta_{pq}[\phi_{p}-(\varepsilon_{p}\beta_{pq})\mathinner{F^{\mathrm{(1D)}}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}/\varepsilon_{q}](\bm{I}-\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}})+\phi_{p}\varepsilon_{q}\beta_{pq}\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}},

(7)

where we have decomposed the effective dielectric constant tensor into two orthogonal components
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
and
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
for the transverse and longitudinal polarizations, respectively, as follows:

\mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}=\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}\quantity(\tens{I}-\uvect{z}\uvect{z})+\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}}
.
Now (
7
) provides two independent approximations:

\displaystyle\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{q}\quantity[
1 + \frac{{\phi_p}^2 (\varepsilon_p/\varepsilon_q)\beta_{pq}}{\phi_p - (\varepsilon_p \beta_{pq}) \F{k_q} /\varepsilon_q}
],

(8)

\displaystyle\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{q}(1-\phi_{p}\beta_{pq})^{-1}.

(9)

Note that
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
is dependent on the incident wavenumber
k_{q}
and is complex-valued if
\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(0\right)}}+\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(2k_{q}\right)}}>0
, implying that the media can be lossy due to forward scattering and backscattering from inhomogeneities in the local dielectric constant.
By contrast,
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
is independent of
k_{q}
, reflecting the fact that a traveling longitudinal wave cannot exist under our assumptions.
Hence, we focus on
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
in the rest of this work.
In the static limit, Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
) reduce to the arithmetic and harmonic means of the dielectric constants, respectively:

\displaystyle\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(0\right)}}=

\displaystyle\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle\equiv\phi_{p}\varepsilon_{p}+\phi_{q}\varepsilon_{q},

\displaystyle\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left(0\right)}}=

\displaystyle(\phi_{p}/\varepsilon_{p}+\phi_{q}/\varepsilon_{q})^{-1}.

(10)

Interestingly, these static results are exact for any microstructure
[
54
]
.
Renormalization of the reference phase for the optimal convergence (Section 2 of
Supplement 1
), equivalent to using the effective Green’s function in Ref.
[
31
]
, yields a
scaled
strong-contrast approximation for disordered layered media:

\displaystyle\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}=\varepsilon_{q}\quantity[
1 + \frac{{\phi_p}^2 (\varepsilon_p/\varepsilon_q) \beta_{pq} }{\phi_p - (\varepsilon_p \beta_{pq} ) \F{ k_q \sqrt{\E{\varepsilon}/\varepsilon_q}}/\E{\varepsilon} }
],

(11)

where
\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle
is given in (
10
).
We henceforth focus on this scaled approximation because it is more accurate than (
8
), as shown in Fig. S6 in
Supplement 1
.
As shown in Ref.
[
31
]
, (
11
) satisfies the Kramers-Kronig relations
[
65
]
so that its predictions properly exhibit both
normal dispersion
[i.e., an increase in
\real[\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}]
with
k_{q}
] and
anomalous dispersion
[i.e., a decrease in
\real[\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}]
with
k_{q}
].
Furthermore, satisfying the Kramers-Kronig relations also implies that (
11
) yields qualitatively accurate predictions, even beyond the validity regime (i.e.,
k_{q}/\rho\lesssim 1.5
); see Sections
5
and 6 on the
Supplement 1
for details.

In the scaled approximation [(
11
)], the quantity
\mathinner{F^{\mathrm{(1D)}}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
is generally complex-valued at a given incident wavenumber
k_{q}
, producing a corresponding
\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}(k_{q})
with a nonnegative imaginary part.
Following conventional usage, a composite attenuates waves at a given wavenumber if the imaginary part of the effective dielectric constant is positive.
Such attenuation occurs here only because of multiple scattering effects (not absorption).

Importantly, our strong-contrast approximation [(
11
)] predicts that stealthy hyperuniform layered media can be perfectly transparent (i.e.,
\imaginary[\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}]=0
) in the infinite-volume limit for a finite range of wavenumbers.
Since any stealthy hyperuniform systems completely suppress both forward scattering and backscattering [i.e.,
\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(0\right)}}+\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(2k_{1}\right)}}=0
] for
k_{1}<K/2
,
\imaginary[\mathinner{F^{\mathrm{(1D)}}\mathopen{\left(k_{1}\right)}}]=0
, and substituting this condition into the scaled formula [(
11
)] yields a perfect transparency interval:

0\leq k_{1}<K_{T}\equiv\frac{K}{2\sqrt{\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle}}=\frac{\rho\pi\chi}{\sqrt{\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle}},

(12)

where
\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle
is the arithmetic mean of the local dielectric constant; see (
10
).
Perfect transparency implies an infinite localization length within this spectral range, which is consistent with an estimate that the localization length is inversely proportional to what we call the spectral density
[
40
]
.
We stress that the prediction [(
12
)] is purely theoretical and hence does not rely on simulations or measurements of the spectral density.

Stealthy hyperuniform particle systems are defined by a spectral density that vanishes in a finite range of wavenumbers that includes the origin [
\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}\right)}}=0
for
0<\absolutevalue{\vect{k}}\leq K
].
The degree of stealthiness
\chi
is measured by the ratio of the number of the constrained wave vectors in the reciprocal space to the total degrees of freedom, i.e., in one dimension,
\chi=K/(2\pi\rho)
, where
\rho
is the number density of particles.
For
\chi<0.5
in two and three dimensions or
\chi<1/3
in one dimension, these stealthy hyperuniform systems are highly degenerate and disordered
[
26
]
.
Thus, we consider 1D cases in which
\chi
takes the following values:
\chi=0.1,0.2,0.3
.
Henceforth, we take the characteristic inhomogeneity length scale
\xi
to be the mean particle separation
1/\rho
, which is of the order of the mean nearest-neighbor distance
\ell_{P}
, (i.e.,
\ell_{P}\sim 1/\rho
).
This choice means that the range of validity of our nonlocal theory is
k_{1}/\rho\lesssim 1.5
for the current models, as shown later in Section
5
.

We numerically generate 1D packings of packing fraction
\phi_{2}=0.2
in the following two-step procedure.
First, we generate point configurations of
N
particles in a periodic fundamental cell
\mathfrak{F}

6
Conclusions and Discussion

In summary, we have theoretically and numerically investigated the effective wave properties, including the effective dynamic dielectric constant tensor
\mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}=\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}(\bm{I}-\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}})+\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}\hat{{\bf z}}\hat{{\bf z}}
and transmittance
T
, of 3D statistically anisotropic two-phase layered media made of 1D disordered stealthy hyperuniform packings.
To predict
\mathinner{\bm{\varepsilon}_{e}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
of such exotic disordered models, we derived for the first time a multiple-scattering approximation [(
11
)] for statistically anisotropic media from the strong-contrast-expansion formalism.
Predictions of both the effective dielectric constant
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left(k_{q}\right)}}
and transmittance
T
are in excellent agreement with corresponding results obtained from the FDTD simulations up to
k_{1}/\rho\lesssim 1.5
.
Remarkably, our predictions of
T
are virtually identical to the simulation results within the perfect transparency ranges.
Our multiple-scattering approximation is the first closed-form formula that provides a simple but accurate relation between the effective wave properties of 3D layered media and their spectral density that applies well beyond the quasistatic regime.
Beyond the valid range
(k_{1}/\rho\gtrsim 1.5)
, (
11
) can still provide qualitatively accurate and physically realistic predictions of dielectric responses, since it satisfies the Kramers-Kronig relations.

We applied this newly derived formula [(
11
)] to disordered stealthy hyperuniform layered media at
\chi=0.1,0.2
, and
0.3
.
It is noteworthy that our multiple-scattering formula [(
11
)] predicts that these disordered systems are perfectly transparent in the infinite-volume limit for a finite ratio
\varepsilon_{2}/\varepsilon_{1}
, implying no Anderson localization up to a finite wavenumber proportional
to
\chi
, as given by (
12
).
This observation is remarkable in that such extended states in 1D disordered systems are more difficult to achieve than in higher dimensions
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
17
]
.
If the localization length is actually finite, we expect that it will be extremely large for sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{2}/\varepsilon_{1}
compared to any practically large sample size in the transparency interval in such stratified media, as will be reported elsewhere
[
71
]
.
In contrast, for disordered non-stealthy layered media, hyperuniform or not, our theory shows that there is no spectral range of perfect transparency, implying that localization emerges as the system size grows.

Our findings also have important practical implications.
For example, we clearly demonstrate that disordered stealthy hyperuniform layered media can be employed as low-pass filters that transmit waves up to a selected wavenumber.
Furthermore, combining our theory with the capabilities to generate media with a prescribed spectral density
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
13
]
enables an inverse-design approach
[
56
]
to engineer and fabricate layered dielectric materials with novel wave properties.
One possible design is a layered medium satisfying
\mathinner{\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{V}}\mathopen{\left(k\right)}}=0
for
k<\epsilon
and
2k_{L}<k<2k_{U}
, which transmits waves within a narrow spectrum
k_{L}<k_{1}<k_{U}
.
Notably, such computationally designed layered media as well as other 1D disordered models can be readily fabricated via vacuum deposition
[
72
]
, spin-coating
[
73
]
, and 3D printing techniques
[
74
]
.
Thus, our results offer promising prospects for engineering novel optoelectronic devices.

We note that our formalism can be extended to cases of obliquely incident wavevectors
{\bf k}_{q}
, in which both effective dielectric constants
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{\perp}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}
and
\mathinner{\varepsilon_{e}^{z}\mathopen{\left({\bf k}_{q}\right)}}
become important.
Moreover, it also will be interesting to extend our theory to lossy dielectric or metallic phases whose dielectric constants are now frequency-dependent and complex-valued.

A NOTE ON WEAKLY PROREGULAR SEQUENCES AND LOCAL COHOMOLOGY
Ryoya Ando

Abstract
. In this note, we give an elementary proof of the result given by Schenzel that there are functorial isomorphisms between local cohomology groups and Čech cohomology groups, by using weakly proregular sequences. In [ 3 ] , he used notions of derived category theory in his proof, but we do not use them in this paper.

In this note, we assume all rings are commutative with identity element. Let

an ideal of

for any

This result was generalised by

. For arbitrary ring

is true for any

-module

if and only if

is a weakly proregular sequence. Let

. A system of elements

such that the natural map;

The goal of this note is the explanation of the following result without using notions of derived category theory.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}
be Abelian categories, and

be a ring,
\underline{a}=a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in A
.
\underline{a}
is called
proregular sequence
if for each
1\leq i\leq r
and
n>0
, there is
m\geq n
such that
((a_{1}^{m},\dots,a_{i-1}^{m}):a_{i}^{m}A)\subset((a_{1}^{n},\dots,a_{i-1}^{n}):a_{i}^{m-n}A)
.

Note that a regular sequence is proregular and this phenomenon is characteristic of non-Noetherian rings.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring. For each
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in A
is a proregular sequence.

Proof.

Let
J_{m}=((a_{1}^{m},\dots,a_{i-1}^{m}):a_{i}^{m}A),I_{n,m}=((a_{1}^{n},\dots,a_{i-1}^{n}):a_{i}^{m-n}A)
. Now
\{I_{n,m}\}_{m\geq n}
is an ascending chain of ideals, then there is
m_{0}\geq n
such that for each
m\geq m_{0},I_{n,m_{0}}=I_{n,m}
. Let
m=m_{0}+n
, then for each
a\in J_{m_{0}}
,
aa_{i}^{m-n}=aa_{i}^{m_{0}}\in(a_{1}^{m_{0}},\dots,a_{i-1}^{m_{0}})\subset(a_{1}^{n},\dots,a_{i-1}^{n})
. So
a\in I_{n,m}=I_{n,m_{0}}
.
∎

Corollary 4.8

Let

5.
Local cohomology

Let
A
be a ring and
I
an ideal of
A
. The functor
\Gamma_{I}
is defined by;

\Gamma_{I}(M)=\left\{x\in M\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\text{$I^{n}x=0$ for some $n\geq 0$}\right\}

for an
A
-module
M
. Note that
\Gamma_{I}(M)=\varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A/I^{n},M)
and this isomorphism is functorial in
M
. By the definition,
\Gamma_{I}
is a left exact functor.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A
be a ring and
I
an ideal of
A
.
H^{i}_{I}(-)
denote the derived functor of
\Gamma_{I}(-)
and it is called
local cohomology
.

Note that
H^{i}(M)\cong\varinjlim\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(A/I^{n},M)
. We summarize the relationship between local cohomology and Čech cohomology. First, we note that the 0-th parts of each cohomology are naturally isomorphic.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A
be a ring,
\underline{a}=a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in A
, and
I=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
. For each
A
-module
M
;

\Gamma_{I}(M)\cong\check{H}^{0}(\underline{a},M).

Proof.

Here
\check{H}^{0}(\underline{a},M)
is the kernel of

M\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}M_{a_{i}}e_{i};x\mapsto(x/1)e_{i}.

Then for each
x\in\check{H}^{0}(\underline{a},M)
and
1\leq i\leq r
, there is
n_{i}\geq 0
such that
a_{i}^{n_{i}}x=0
. So we have
x\in\Gamma_{I}(M)
. Similarly the converse is true, so they are equal as submodules of
M
.
∎

With the preparations we have made above, we can prove the results we have been aiming for.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
A
be a ring,
\underline{a}=a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in A
and
I=(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})
.
\underline{a}
is a weakly proregular sequence if and only if for any
i
and
A
-module
M
,
H^{i}_{I}(M)\cong\check{H}^{i}(\underline{a},M)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\underline{a}
is weakly proregular.
\check{H}^{\bullet}(\underline{a},-)
is a
\delta
-functor by
3.2
. Moreover
\check{H}^{\bullet}(\underline{a},-)
is universal by
4.4
and
2.5
. So
H^{i}_{I}(M)\cong\check{H}^{i}(\underline{a},M)
by above lemma. The converse is true by
4.4
.
∎

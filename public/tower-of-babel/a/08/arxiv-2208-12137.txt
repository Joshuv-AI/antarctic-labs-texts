On a generalization of two results of Happel to commutative rings
Tony J. Puthenpurakal

Abstract
. In this paper we extend two results of Happel to commutative rings. Let ( A , 𝔪 ) (A,\mathfrak{m}) be a commutative Noetherian local ring. Let D f b ​ ( mod ⁡ ( A ) ) D^{b}_{f}(\operatorname{mod}(A)) be the bounded derived category of complexes of finitely generated modules over A A with finite length cohomology. We show D f b ​ ( mod ⁡ ( A ) ) D^{b}_{f}(\operatorname{mod}(A)) has Auslander-Reiten(AR)-triangles if and only if A A is regular. Let K f b ​ ( proj ⁡ A ) K^{b}_{f}(\operatorname{proj}A) be the homotopy category of finite complexes of finitely generated free A A -modules with finite length cohomology. We show that if A A is complete and if A A is Gorenstein then K f b ​ ( proj ⁡ A ) K^{b}_{f}(\operatorname{proj}A) has AR triangles. Conversely we show that if K f b ​ ( proj ⁡ A ) K^{b}_{f}(\operatorname{proj}A) has AR triangles and if A A is Cohen-Macaulay or if dim A = 1 \dim A=1 then A A is Gorenstein.

3.
Preliminaries on AR-triangles

The notion of AR-triangles in a triangulated Krull-Schmidt category was introduced by Happel
[
6
, Chapter 1]
.
First note that the notion of split monomorphism and split epimorphism makes sense in any triangulated category. They are called section and retraction respectively in
[
6
]
. However in
[
6
]
AR-triangles are defined only in Hom-finite categories and in this case right AR-triangles are also left AR-triangles (and vice-versa). However we need the notion of AR-triangles (both left and right) in general. So in this section we carefully define the relevant notions.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Krull-Schmidt triangulated category with shift functor
\sum
.

A triangle
N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M\xrightarrow{h}\sum N
in
\mathcal{C}
is called a
right AR-triangle
(ending at
M
) if

(RAR1)
M,N
are indecomposable.

(RAR2)
h\neq 0
.

(RAR3) If
D
is indecomposable then for every non-isomorphism
t\colon D\rightarrow M
we have
h\circ t=0
.

By considering the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(D,-)
it is easy to see that (AR3) is equivalent to

(RAR3l) If
D
is indecomposable then for every non-isomorphism
t\colon D\rightarrow M
there exists
q\colon D\rightarrow E
with
g\circ q=t
.

It is also easy to see that (RAR3l) is equivalent to

(RAR3g) If
W
is not-necessarily indecomposable and
s\colon W\rightarrow M
is not a retraction then there exists
q\colon W\rightarrow E
with
g\circ q=s
.

Dually, a triangle
\sum^{-1}M\xrightarrow{w}N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M
in
\mathcal{C}
is called a
left AR-triangle
(starting at
N
) if

(LAR1)
M,N
are indecomposable.

(LAR2)
w\neq 0
.

(LAR3) If
D
is indecomposable then for every non-isomorphism
t\colon N\rightarrow D
we have
t\circ w=0
.

By considering the functor
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,D)
it is easy to see that (LAR3) is equivalent to

(LAR3l) If
D
is indecomposable then for every non-isomorphism
t\colon N\rightarrow D
there exists
q\colon E\rightarrow D
with
q\circ f=t
.

It is also easy to see that (LAR3l) is equivalent to

(LAR3g) If
W
is not-necessarily indecomposable and
s\colon N\rightarrow W
is not a section then there exists
q\colon E\rightarrow W
with
q\circ f=s
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say a Krull-Schmidt triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
has AR-triangles if for any indecomposable
M\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a right AR-triangle ending at
M
and a left AR-triangle starting at
M
.

In
[
6
, 1.4.2]
it is proved that in a Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt category if

N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M\xrightarrow{h}\sum N
in
\mathcal{C}
is a right AR-triangle ending at
M
then its rotation

\sum^{-1}M\xrightarrow{-\sum h}N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M
is a left AR-triangle starting at
N
. A careful reading of the proof actually shows the following

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M\xrightarrow{h}\sum N
in
\mathcal{C}
is a right AR-triangle ending at
M
. If
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(N,N)
is Artinian then its rotation

\sum^{-1}M\xrightarrow{-\sum^{-1}h}N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M
is a left AR-triangle starting at
N
.

The following dual statement can also be proved similarly (but in our context also see
3.10
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\sum^{-1}M\xrightarrow{w}N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M
in
\mathcal{C}
is a left AR-triangle ending at
M
. If
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,M)
is Artinian then its rotation

N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M\xrightarrow{-\sum w}\sum N

M
.

4.

In this section we prove:

Lemma 4.1
.

be a Noetherian local ring. Then

Proof.

It is elementary that

is a thick subcategory of

. So in particular it is a triangulated category.

Next we prove that idempotents split in
D^{b}_{f}(A)
. Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{f}(A)
. Let

(see
[
1
, 2.10]
or
[
10
, Corollary A]
) we have that there exists
\mathbf{U}_{\bullet}\in D^{b}(A)
and maps

\alpha\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{U}_{\bullet}
and
\beta\colon\mathbf{U}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
such that
\beta\circ\alpha=e
and
\alpha\circ\beta=1_{\mathbf{U}_{\bullet}}
. Taking cohomology we get an inclusion
H^{i}(\beta)\colon H^{i}(\mathbf{U}_{\bullet})\rightarrow H^{i}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that
\mathbf{U}_{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{f}(A)
. Thus idempotents split in
D^{b}_{f}(A)
.

Set
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{-,b}(\operatorname{proj}A)
. It is well-known that
D^{b}(A)\cong\mathcal{K}
. Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
be bounded complexes in

. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a projective resolution of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
\mathcal{F}^{i}
finitely generated free
A
-modules and
\mathcal{F}^{i}=0
for
i\gg 0
.
Then
we know that

\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(A)}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{F},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}).

Let
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{H}om_{A}(\mathcal{F},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})
be the complex of homomorphisms from
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
; see
2.8
. Then it is clear that
\mathcal{E}^{i}=0
for all
i\gg 0
and
\mathcal{E}^{i}
is finitely generated
A
-module for all

(note
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
is a bounded complex). So
H^{i}(\mathcal{E})
is a finitely generated
A
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}))=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{F},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})
is finitely generated
A
-module.
Let

be a prime ideal in

. Set

{\mathcal{K}}_{P}=\mathcal{K}^{-,b}(\operatorname{proj}A_{P})
.

We note that
{\mathcal{E}}_{P}\cong\mathcal{H}om_{A_{P}}({\mathcal{F}}_{P},{\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}}_{P})
.
Furthermore

{\mathcal{F}}_{P}
has zero homology. As it is bounnded above we get that

in
\mathcal{K}_{P}
. Thus

for any prime

is a Hom-finite category

Next we show that any object in

is a finite direct sum of indecomposable objects.
Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in D^{b}_{f}(A)
. Let

. Then it is clear that

cannot be a direct sum of

. Thus

is a finite direct sum of indecomposable objects in

is Hom-finite with split-idempotents it follows that the endomorphism rings of indecomposable objects are local, see

is a Krull-Schmidt category.
∎

Set

6.2

Let

7.
A filtration of certain DG-algebras

We will need to recall a few basic results.
Let
A
be a Noetherian ring. We will recall Tate’s construction of DG-resolution of
A/I
where
I
is an ideal in
A
, see
[
18
]
.

7.1
.

An associative algebra
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
over
A
is called an
non-positive DG-algebra
over
A

if the following hypotheses are satisfied:

(1)

\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
is non-positively graded
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{n\leq 0}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n}
with each
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{i}
a finitely generated
A
-module and
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{i}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{j}\subseteq\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{i+j}
for all
i,j\leq 0
.

(2)

\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
has a unit element
1\in\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}
such that
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}=A1
.

(3)

\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
is strictly skew-commutative;
(for homogeneous element
x\in\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{i}
set
|x|=i
)
For homogeneous elements
x,y
we have

(i)

x.y=(-1)^{|x||y|}yx.

(ii)

x^{2}=0
if
|x|
is odd.

(4)

There exists a skew derivation
d\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
such that

(i)

d(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n})\subseteq\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n+1}
for all
n\leq 0
.

(ii)

d^{2}=0
.

(iii)

For
x,y
homogeneous,

d(xy)=d(x)y+(-1)^{|x|}xd(y).

The map
d
is called skew derivation on
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
.

7.2
.

Next we recall Tate’s process of killing cycles;
[
18
, section 2]
.
Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
be a non-positive DG-algebra. Let
\rho<0
be a negative integer. Let
t\in Z^{\rho+1}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
be a cycle
of degree
\rho+1
.

Case 1:
\rho
is odd. Let
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}=A\oplus AT
be the exterior power algebra with degree of
A
is zero and
\deg T=\rho
and
T^{2}=0

Case 2:
\rho
is even. Let
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{j\leq 0}AT_{j}
with
AT_{j}\cong A
in degree
-j\rho
(with
T_{0}=1
) and multiplication defined by

T_{i}T_{j}=\frac{(|i|+|j|)!}{|i|!|j|!}T_{i+j}.

In both cases
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
is again a non-positive DG-algebra. Furthermore there is a unique skew-derivation

d
on
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
extending that on
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\otimes 1
with
d(T)=t
in case (1) and

d(T_{j})=tT_{j+1}
for all

in case (2). Furthermore we get
H^{q}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})=H^{q}(A)
for

and

H^{\rho+1}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})=H^{\rho+1}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})/At
.

Remark 7.3
.

Following Tate we denote in both cases
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\otimes\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}<T>
with
d(T)=t
.

7.4
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
be a non-positive DG-algebra over
A
. We assume
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{i}
is a finitely generated free
A
-module for all
i\leq 0
and
\ell(H^{*}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})^{n})<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. By a good filtration
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)\}_{i\geq 0}
of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
we mean

(1)

\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)
is a sub-complex of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)^{n}
a direct summand of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n}
for all

.

(2)

\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i+1)
for all
i\geq 0
and
\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
.

(3)

\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)^{n}
is a direct summand of
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i+1)^{n}
for all
n\leq 0
.

(4)

There exists

depending on
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{F}
such that

\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)^{n}\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(j)^{m}\subseteq\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i+j+c)^{n+m}.

(5)

\ell(H^{*}(\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)))<\infty
for all

i\geq 1
.

We call
c
the
parameter
of
\mathcal{F}
.
The main result of this section
is

Theorem 7.5
.

(with hypotheses as in
7.4
.) Let
\rho<0
be such that
H^{i}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})=0
for
0>i\geq\rho+2
. Suppose
H^{\rho+1}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})\neq 0
and let

be a cycle
of degree
\rho+1
such that its residue class in

is non-zero. Let
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}<T>

.
Then
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
also has a good filtration as discussed in
7.4
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a good filtration of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with parameter
c
. Suppose

.
We have to consider two cases.

Case (1):
\rho
is odd.
Note
{\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}}^{n}={\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}}^{n}\oplus\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n-\rho}T
.
Set
\mathcal{G}=\{\mathbf{G}_{\bullet}(i)\}_{i\geq 0}

We first show that
\mathbf{G}_{\bullet}(i)
is a sub-complex of
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
. Let

.
Then note that

We note that

Thus

\mathbf{G}_{\bullet}(i)
is a sub-complex of
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
.
Clearly hypotheses
(1),(2),(3)
is satisfied.
We note that

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\bullet}(i)^{n}\mathbf{G}_{\bullet}(j)^{m}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i+r+c)^{n}\oplus\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(i)^{n-\rho}T)\cdot(\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(j+r+c)^{m}\oplus\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(j)^{m-\rho}T)

Case (2):
\rho
is even.
We note that

\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{n}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n}\oplus\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n-\rho}T_{-1}\oplus\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n-2\rho}T_{-2}\oplus\cdots

\mathbf{G}_{\bullet}(i)

\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}

\displaystyle atT_{l+1}

.
Clearly hypotheses

is satisfied.

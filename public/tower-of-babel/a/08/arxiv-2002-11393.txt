Existence of positive solutions for a singular elliptic problem with critical exponent and measure data
Akasmika Panda, Debajyoti Choudhuri, Ratan Kr. Giri

Abstract
We prove the existence of a positive SOLA (Solutions Obtained as Limits of Approximations) to the following PDE involving fractional power of Laplacian ( − Δ ) s ​ u = 1 u γ + λ ​ u 2 s ∗ − 1 + μ ​ in ​ Ω , u > 0 ​ in ​ Ω , u = 0 ​ in ​ ℝ N ∖ Ω . \begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+\lambda u^{2_{s}^{*}-1}+\mu~\text{in}~\Omega,\\ u&>0~\text{in}~\Omega,\\ u&=0~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{split} (0.1) Here, Ω \Omega is a bounded domain of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , 2 ​ s < N 2s<N , λ , γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \lambda,\gamma\in(0,1) , 2 s ∗ = 2 ​ N N − 2 ​ s 2_{s}^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2s} is the fractional critical Sobolev exponent and μ \mu is a nonnegative bounded Radon measure in Ω \Omega . Keywords: Fractional Sobolev spaces, SOLA, Radon measure, Marcinkiewicz space, critical exponent. 2010 AMS Classification: 35J60, 35R11, 35A15.

Introduction

In this paper, we discuss the following fractional elliptic problem with a singularity, a critical exponent and a Radon measure.

\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}+\lambda u^{2_{s}^{*}-1}+\mu~\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u&>0~\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u&=0~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{split}

(
P_{\lambda}
)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
C^{2}
boundary,
s\in(0,1)
,
N>2s
,
0<\gamma<1
,
\lambda>0
,
\mu
is a nonnegative bounded Radon measure and
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional Laplacian defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u=\text{P. V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy.

Problems involving nonlocal operators have theoretical applications as well as real life applications in various fields of science. The applications of fractional order Laplacian can be found in Lévy stable diffusion process, chemical reactions in liquids, geophysical fluid dynamics, electromagnetism etc (refer
[
2
]
for further details). Nonlocal problems containing singular or irregular data are used in dislocation problems
[
10
]
, quasi-geostrophic dynamics
[
11
]
, image reconstruction problems
[
17
]
etc. The problem of denoising a image is to find a clear image
u
from a noisy
f
. In the deblurring problem, a given image
f
is considered as a blurry version of an unknown exact image
u
, which is to be determined. For further details refer Kinermann et al.
[
19
]
. Readers may refer to the work in
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
.

In general, the presence of a measure data in the problem weakens the class of solution space, i.e. we lose some degrees of differentiability or/and integrability of the solution space. Solutions to problems involving measure data or
L^{1}
data are obtained by approximations and usually by working in Marcinkiewicz spaces. Readers may refer
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
20
]
and the references therein for further readings on these types of problems. Boccardo et al. (
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
) proved that the solution to a nonlinear elliptic equation involving

for every

, where

. Recently, in 2015, Kuusi et al.
[

considered the problem, as in
[

, in a fractional set up with fractional

for every
s_{1}<s<1
,

. Purely singular problems both in the local and nonlocal cases are studied in
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
, etc. and the references therein. In all these articles the choice of a solution space depends on the power

). Further, we refer
[

[

]
,
[

]
, etc. to survey Brezis
-

The problem
(P_{\lambda})
for
\lambda=0
and the limiting case of
s=1
has been analyzed by Panda et al. in
[
25
]
. The authors have guaranteed the existence of a weak solution in

if

and in

if

. Ghosh et al. in
[

extended this result and studied the problem

with

We use the relation among the fractional Sobolev space, Bessel potential space, Marcinkiewicz space to find a solution in a function space weaker than
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
. Such solutions are called as SOLA (see Definition
2.9
). Due to the presence of nonlinearities with a critical exponent, singularity and a measure data, difficulties arise in the study of
(P_{\lambda})
. Thus, it is not easy to directly approach the problem with any commonly used tools like variational method, Nehari manifold method, etc. We study our main problem via a sequence of approximating problems. It is very challenging to prove the existence of a solution to the approximating problems and simultaneously showing the boundedness of the sequence of solution to these approximating problems in
L^{2_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)
. To overcome these difficulties we take the help of two auxiliary problems. For that, we apply the concentration compactness principle as in
[
23
]
and Ekeland’s variational principle as in
[
13
]
. Precisely, we guarantee that the approximating problem admits at least one solution in a complete Hilbert manifold
H=\{u\in H_{0}^{s}(\Omega):\|u\|_{L^{2_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}=1\}
. We follow some of the arguments of
[
25
]
to prove our main result stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
0<\Lambda<\infty
such that for
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
the problem
(P_{\lambda})
admits a positive SOLA
u\in W_{0}^{s_{1},q}(\Omega)
for every
s_{1}<s
and
q<\frac{N}{N-s}
in the sense of Definition
2.9
.

Let
\Omega

,

Further, the space
(W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)})
with

is a reflexive separable Banach space. The following classical theorem will be used frequently in this article.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 6.5,
[
24
]
)
.

Let

and

with

. Then there exists constant

. Moreover, the space
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for every

and compactly embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for every

Denote

\mathbb{S}_{s,p}=\underset{u\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}{\inf}\frac{\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}}

(2.2)

Since our problem, defined in

Definition 2.7

Let

Theorem 2.8

(Ekeland Variational Principle

,

\|\Psi^{\prime}(v)\|^{*}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\delta}

where

, respectively.

We now introduce a suitable notion of solution to

Definition 2.9

(SOLA for (

(2.4)

and for every

(2.5)

Furthermore, there exists a sequence of weak solutions

)

3
Existence of positive weak solution to
(P_{\lambda,n}^{2})

Define a function
g_{n}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{n}(x,t)=&\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(w_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}-\frac{1}{(t+w_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}&\text{if}~t+w_{n}(x)+\frac{1}{n}>0\\

(3.9)

\mathbb{S}_{2,s}\nu_{2,j}^{\frac{2}{2_{s}^{*}}}\leq\nu_{1,j},~\forall j\in I

(3.13)

,

2.4
, we conclude that
(u_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
L^{s,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and hence bounded in
W_{0}^{s_{1},q}(\Omega)
, for every
s_{1}<s
and
q<\frac{N}{N-s}
.
∎

We now pass the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
in the weak formulation
(
2.6
)
to prove the existence of a SOLA to
(P_{\lambda})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
,
0<\gamma<1
and
u_{n}\in H
be a weak solution of
(P_{\lambda,n})
for
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
. According to Lemma
4.1
,
(u_{n})
is bounded in
W_{0}^{s_{1},q}(\Omega)
, for every
0<s_{1}<s
and
q<\frac{N}{N-s}
. Hence, there exists
u\in W_{0}^{s_{1},q}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
weakly in
W_{0}^{s_{1},q}(\Omega)
. Thus,
u_{n}\rightarrow u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
.

Denote

\Phi(x,y)=\phi(x)-\phi(y),~\bar{U}_{n}(x,y)=u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y),

\bar{U}(x,y)=u(x)-u(y)~\text{and}~d\nu=\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}.

For every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, from the weak formulation of
(P_{\lambda,n})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\bar{U}_{n}(x,y)\Phi(x,y)d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}\phi+\int_{\Omega}\lambda u_{n}^{2_{s}^{*}-1}\phi+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{n}\phi.

We can rewrite the above equation as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\bar{U}(x,y)\Phi(x,y)d\nu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(\bar{U}_{n}(x,y)-\bar{U}(x,y))\Phi(x,y)d\nu=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}\phi+\int_{\Omega}\lambda u_{n}^{2_{s}^{*}-1}\phi+\int_{\Omega}\mu_{n}\phi.

(4.29)

Clearly,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mu_{n}\phi=\int_{\Omega}\phi d\mu,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{2_{s}^{*}-1}\phi=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u^{2_{s}^{*}-1}\phi.

On using the Dominated Convergence Theorem we get

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{(u_{n}+\frac{1}{n})^{\gamma}}\phi=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}\phi.

Now the integral on the left hand side of
(
4.29
)
can be expressed as follows.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(\bar{U}_{n}(x,y)-\bar{U}(x,y))\Phi(x,y)d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}(\bar{U}_{n}(x,y)-\bar{U}(x,y))\Phi(x,y)d\nu

\displaystyle~~+\int_{\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)}(\bar{U}_{n}(x,y)-\bar{U}(x,y))\Phi(x,y)d\nu

\displaystyle~~+\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times\Omega}(\bar{U}_{n}(x,y)-\bar{U}(x,y))\Phi(x,y)d\nu

\displaystyle=J_{1,n}+J_{2,n}+J_{3,n}.

Observe
\bar{U}_{n}\rightarrow\bar{U}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since
\Omega
is bounded, using Lemma
4.1
and Vitali’s lemma we have
\bar{U}_{n}\rightarrow\bar{U}
strongly in
L^{1}(\Omega\times\Omega,d\nu)
. Hence,
J_{1,n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Let
(x,y)\in\Omega\times(B_{R}\setminus\Omega)
, then

\underset{(x,y)\in\Omega\times(B_{R}\setminus\Omega)}{\sup}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\leq C<\infty.

Hence, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
J_{2,n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and similarly
J_{3,n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus on passing the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
in
(
4.29
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\bar{U}(x,y)\Phi(x,y)d\nu=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{u^{\gamma}}\phi+\int_{\Omega}\lambda u^{2_{s}^{*}-1}\phi+\int_{\Omega}\phi d\mu.

(4.30)

Thus,
u
is a positive SOLA to
(P_{\lambda})
, in the sense of Definition
2.9
.
∎

Appendix

We now discuss about the multiplicity of solutions to the problem
(P_{\lambda})
for the case
0<\gamma<1
. Let
I_{\lambda}
be the corresponding energy functional of
(P_{\lambda})
given by

I_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}u^{1-\gamma}-\frac{\lambda}{2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\Omega}u^{2_{s}^{*}}-\int_{\Omega}ud\mu.

For
0<\gamma<1
,
I_{\lambda}
is a
C^{1}
functional and
I_{\lambda}(0)=0.
From Theorem
1.1
,
u\in W_{0}^{s_{1},q}(\Omega)
is a positive SOLA to
(P_{\lambda})
for every
s_{1}<s
,
q<\frac{N}{N-s}
. Then
u
is also a Nehari solution of
(P_{\lambda})
, i.e.
u\in N_{\lambda}=\{u\in W_{0}^{s_{1},q}(\Omega):\langle I_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\}
. Here

\langle I_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy-\int_{\Omega}u^{1-\gamma}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u^{2_{s}^{*}}-\int_{\Omega}ud\mu.

Consider the fibre map
\psi:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\psi(t)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy-\frac{t^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}u^{1-\gamma}-\frac{\lambda t^{2_{s}^{*}}}{2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\Omega}u^{2_{s}^{*}}-t\int_{\Omega}ud\mu.

Then

\psi^{\prime}(t)=At-Bt^{-\gamma}-\lambda Ct^{2_{s}^{*}-1}-D

and

\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)=A+\gamma Bt^{-\gamma-1}-(2_{s}^{*}-1)\lambda Ct^{2_{s}^{*}-2}

where
A=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy
,
B=\int_{\Omega}u^{1-\gamma}
,
C=\int_{\Omega}u^{2_{s}^{*}}
and
D=\int_{\Omega}ud\mu
. Since
u\in N_{\lambda}
,
\psi^{\prime}(1)=A-B-\lambda C-D=0.
Clearly
\psi^{\prime}(t)\rightarrow-\infty
as
t\rightarrow 0
and
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Case 1:
If
\Lambda>\lambda>\frac{A+\gamma B}{(2^{*}_{s}-1)C}
, then
\psi^{\prime\prime}(1)<0.
Hence, there exists at least one Nehari solution to
(P_{\lambda})
.

Case 2:
If
\lambda<\min\{\frac{A+\gamma B}{(2^{*}_{s}-1)C},\Lambda\}
, then
\psi^{\prime\prime}(1)>0
and we guarantee the existence of at least three nontrivial Nehari solution to
(P_{\lambda})
.

Case 3:
If
\lambda=\frac{A+\gamma B}{(2^{*}_{s}-1)C}
, then
\psi^{\prime\prime}(1)=0
. Thus, we obtain a saddle point and hence there exists at least one Nehari solution to
(P_{\lambda})
.

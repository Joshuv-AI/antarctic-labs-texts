Gijs Heuts, Markus Land

Abstract
. We study the loop and suspension functors on the category of augmented 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} -algebras. One application is to the formality of the cochain algebra of the n n -sphere. We show that it is formal as an 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} -algebra, also with coefficients in general commutative ring spectra, but rarely 𝔼 n + 1 \mathbb{E}_{n+1} -formal unless the coefficients are rational. Along the way we show that the free functor from operads in spectra to monads in spectra is fully faithful on a nice subcategory of operads which in particular contains the stable 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} -operads for finite n n . We use this to interpret our results on loop and suspension functors of augmented algebras in operadic terms.

The functors

2.
Suspensions and loops of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebras

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let us denote by
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wc}}
the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories which admit weakly contractible colimits and functors which preserve weakly contractible colimits. Via Lurie’s tensor product
[
Lur17
, §4.8.1]
,
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wc}}
acquires a symmetric monoidal structure. Throughout this section, we let
\mathcal{C}
be an object of
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wc}})
and sometimes refer to it as an
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoidal category with weakly contractible colimits. By definition, the tensor product of
\mathcal{C}
preserves weakly contractible colimits in each variable. We denote the monoidal unit in
\mathcal{C}
by
\mathbf{1}
and write
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})_{/\mathbf{1}}
for the category of augmented
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebras. This is a pointed category, with zero object given by the monoidal unit
\mathbf{1}
of
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, Dunn additivity
[
Lur17
, Theorem 5.1.2.2]
induces the equivalence
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{(\mathrm{aug})}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}^{(\mathrm{aug})}(\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C}))
.
We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\mathcal{C}
be in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wc}})
and
0<k<n
. Then
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(\mathcal{C})
is in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-k}}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wc}})
. Moreover, the functor
\mathrm{res}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}
admits a left adjoint
\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}\colon\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

The first statement is
[
Lur17
, Prop. 5.1.2.9]
. For the second,
[
Lur17
, Remark 5.2.2.10]
then implies that the forgetful functor
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C}))\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}(\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C}))
admits a left adjoint. This adjunction induces the required adjunction on augmented objects, using that also
\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}(\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

We note that, as a consequence,
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})
is cocomplete since it admits weakly contractible colimits, as well as an initial object.
2
2

2

This fact is also true for
n=0
.
In particular, it admits a suspension functor which we denote by
\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}
and warn the reader that it is not compatible with the forgetful functors
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})
for
k<n
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
A
in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})
, the relative tensor product
\mathbf{1}\otimes_{A}\mathbf{1}
exists and its formation provides a functor

\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C}),\quad A\mapsto\mathrm{Bar}(A):=\mathbf{1}\otimes_{A}\mathbf{1}

called the Bar construction.

Proof.

We note again the equivalence
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{(\mathrm{aug})}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}^{(\mathrm{aug})}(\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C}))
and that
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})
is a monoidal category with weakly contractible colimits by
Lemma

2.1
. In particular,
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})
is a monoidal category with geometric realizations, so
[
Lur17
, §4.4.2]
provides a functor
\mathrm{Bar}\colon\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})
which induces the required functor on augmented objects as clearly
\mathrm{Bar}(\mathbf{1})\simeq\mathbf{1}
.
∎

Finally, we will also need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoidal category with geometric realizations. Then a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{\prime}}

in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{(\mathrm{aug})}(\mathcal{C})
induces a canonical map
A^{\prime}\otimes_{A}B\to B^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{(\mathrm{aug})}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

The assumptions on
\mathcal{C}
imply that the relative tensor product can be formed and that it provides a functor
-\otimes_{A}B\colon\mathrm{RMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{RMod}_{B}(\mathcal{C})
left adjoint to the forgetful functor. This functor in addition inherits the structure of an
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}
-monoidal functor from the
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-structure on the map
A\to B
. It consequently induces a left adjoint
-\otimes_{A}B\colon\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathrm{RMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C}))\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathrm{RMod}_{B}(\mathcal{C}))
. There is therefore an equivalence

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathrm{RMod}_{B}(\mathcal{C}))}(A^{\prime}\otimes_{A}B,B^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathrm{RMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C}))}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})

and the latter space contains the map
A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
part of the above diagram. We then use the forgetful functor
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathrm{RMod}_{B}(\mathcal{C}))\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})
induced from the unit of
B
and arrive at the claimed canonical map. This construction is natural in maps of squares, which shows that the just constructed map is compatible with augmentations.
∎

2.2.
Suspensions and loops of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebras

The starting point of our paper is the following result.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
\mathcal{C}
be in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n+1}}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wc}})
. Then there is a natural commutative diagram of colimit preserving functors as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n+1}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n+1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bar}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bar}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}}

Here, we assume
n\geq 1
for the lower-left triangle.

Remark 2.5
.

The proof we give in fact shows that for the lower-left triangle to commute as claimed, it suffices that
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoidal.

Proof of
Theorem

2.4
.

We treat the upper-right triangle first. By
Lemma

2.1
, the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})
lies in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wc}})
.
Using the canonical equivalences
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})_{\mathbf{1}//\mathbf{1}}
as well as
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{n+1}}(\mathcal{C})
for all
n\geq 0
,
[
Lur17
, Cor. 5.2.2.13]
says that the composite

\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n+1}}}\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n+1}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Bar}}\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})

is given by the functor which takes an object
C
to the pushout
\mathbf{1}\amalg_{C}\mathbf{1}
. We note that the composite above canonically factors through
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})
since
\mathrm{Bar}(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}
. The same formula then remains true for this refined functor because the forgetful functor is conservative and preserves pushouts. Since
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(\mathcal{C})
is a pointed category, with terminal object
\mathbf{1}
, the functor
C\mapsto\mathbf{1}\amalg_{C}\mathbf{1}
is the suspension functor
\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}
, showing the commutativity of the upper-right triangle.

Now let us show that all functors in question preserve colimits. First, in any pointed and cocomplete
\infty
-category, the suspension functor commutes with colimits, hence
\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}
preserves colimits. Moreover, for all
k\geq 1
, the functor
\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{k-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{k}}
is a left adjoint and hence also preserves colimits. It then suffices to show that for any
k\geq 1
, the functor
\mathrm{Bar}\colon\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{k-1}}(C)
preserves small colimits. By construction,
\mathrm{Bar}
preserves sifted colimits, so in fact it suffices to show that it preserves the initial object and binary coproducts. The initial object is given by the tensor unit
\mathbf{1}
and which is clearly preserved by
\mathrm{Bar}
. Next, we wish to show that for any two objects
A,B
of
\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(\mathcal{C})
, the canonical map

\mathrm{Bar}(A)\amalg\mathrm{Bar}(A^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Bar}(A\amalg A^{\prime})

is an equivalence. Since
\mathrm{Bar}
preserves sifted colimits and every augmented
\mathbb{E}_{k}
-algebra is a sifted colimit of algebras of the form
\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{k-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(B)

[
Lur17
, Prop. 4.7.3.14]
(to apply this proposition, note that the forgetful functor
\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{k-1}}(\mathcal{C})
is conservative and preserves sifted colimits), it suffices to show that the canonical map

\mathrm{Bar}(\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{k-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(B))\amalg\mathrm{Bar}(\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{k-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(B^{\prime}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{Bar}(\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{k-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(B\amalg B^{\prime}))

is an equivalence. By the already established commutativity of the upper-right triangle, this map is equivalent to the canonical map

\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(B)\amalg\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(B^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}(B\amalg B^{\prime})

which is an equivalence since
\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{k}}
preserves colimits as we have already argued.

Finally, we show that the lower-left triangle commutes. To do so, we first observe that the commutative (in fact pushout) diagram in
\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathcal{C})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(A)}

induces a canonical map
\mathrm{Bar}(A)=\mathbf{1}\otimes_{A}\mathbf{1}\to\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(A)
in
\mathrm{Alg}^{\mathrm{aug}}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{C})
, see
Lemma

2.3
used for the above square for
A
, for
\mathbf{1}
, and the canonical map from the former to the latter. By adjunction, we obtain a natural map
\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathrm{Bar}(A))\to\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(A)
. Source and target of this natural map are colimit preserving functors, as we have just argued. The same reasoning as above implies that it therefore suffices to show that this map is an equivalence in case
A=\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(B)
in which case we may again use the established commutativity of the upper-right triangle for

in place of

. The map under investigation then becomes equivalent to the canonical map

\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(B))\longrightarrow\Sigma_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathrm{free}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}^{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(B))

which is an equivalence because the free functor preserves colimits and therefore commutes with suspensions.
∎

The aim of this section is to explain how the results of

The main technical tool in this section is the result that there is no loss of information in passing from the stable

It should be true that the square of operad maps above already exists

3.1.
Preliminaries

We denote by
\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp}):=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq},\mathrm{Sp})
the category of symmetric sequences in spectra. Here
\mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq}
denotes the groupoid of finite sets and bijections, which is the free symmetric monoidal category on a single generator. We will usually write
A(n)
for the value of a symmetric sequence
A
on the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
A symmetric sequence
A
is called non-unital if
A(0)
is equivalent to
0
. The category
\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})
is equipped with the Day convolution symmetric monoidal structure
\otimes
based on the usual symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{Sp}
and the disjoint union symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq}
. By construction, the fully faithful functor
\iota\colon\mathrm{Sp}\to\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})
, sending
X
to the symmetric sequence
\iota(X)(0)=X
and
\iota(X)(n)=0
for
n>0
is canonically symmetric monoidal. In particular,
\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})
is tensored over
\mathrm{Sp}
via this inclusion.

The
\infty
-category
\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})
can be equipped with a further monoidal structure, called the
composition product
, obeying the formula

A\circ B=\bigoplus\limits_{n\geq 0}(A(n)\otimes B^{\otimes n})_{h\Sigma_{n}},

see
[
BCN23
, Section 3.1]
and
[
Hau22
]
.
It follows that
A\circ\iota(X)
is in the essential image of
\iota
for any symmetric sequence
A
, giving rise to an action of
\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})
on
\mathrm{Sp}
, or equivalently, a monoidal functor

\mathrm{free}\colon\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})\to\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Sp}),\quad A\mapsto[\mathrm{free}_{A}\colon X\mapsto\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(A(n)\otimes X^{\otimes n})_{h\Sigma_{n}}]

where we view
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Sp})=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Sp},\mathrm{Sp})
as monoidal via the composition of functors.
It will be convenient to use the abbreviated notation
D_{n}^{A}(X):=(A(n)\otimes X^{\otimes n})_{h\Sigma_{n}}
for the summands of
\mathrm{free}_{A}
.

For non-unital symmetric sequences
A
and
B
, the above formula for the composition product simplifies to the following. For a finite set
I
we have

(A\circ B)(I)\simeq\bigoplus_{E\in\mathrm{Equiv}(I)}A(I/E)\otimes\bigotimes_{J\in I/E}B(J),

where the sum is indexed over the set of equivalence relations
E
on
I
. It will be important for us to make explicit the
\Sigma_{I}
-action on
(A\circ B)(I)
. To that end, first note that
\Sigma_{I}

Let

Definition 3.4

A symmetric sequence

has

if there exists a natural number

, called the order of nilpotence, with the following property: for each

and each subset

is nullhomotopic in the

is said to have nilpotent Euler classes if its underlying symmetric sequence does.

Example 3.5
.

(1)

Suppose that

is a symmetric sequence such that for every

is nonequivariantly null when

.

(2)

The symmetric sequence underlying the operad
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{nu}}
does not have nilpotent Euler classes. For example, taking homotopy orbits of the
\Sigma_{2}
-equivariant map

yields the standard map

\Sigma^{\infty}\mathbb{RP}^{\infty}_{+}\to\Sigma^{\infty}\mathbb{RP}^{\infty}_{k},

which is not nullhomotopic for any (finite) value of

.

Lemma 3.6
.

The class of non-unital symmetric sequences with nilpotent Euler classes is closed under operadic (de)suspensions and contains the monoidal unit. Moreover, if
A
is a non-unital symmetric sequence with nilpotent Euler classes, then so is its composition product

with any non-unital symmetric sequence

. In particular, the class of non-unital symmetric sequences with nilpotent Euler classes forms a monoidal subcategory of
\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})

Proof.

The monoidal unit of
\mathrm{SSeq}(\mathrm{Sp})
is the sequence that associates

to the singleton and

to any finite set of cardinality other than one, and consequently has nilpotent Euler classes.

Now let

for

The goal of this section is to prove the following:

The functors

. The diagonal arrow is an equivalence by Goodwillie’s classification of homogenous functors. It hence remains to show that the lower right vertical arrow is also an equivalence. By definition, it is given by the product over
n\geq 1
of the following composites.

\mathrm{Map}(D_{n}^{A},D_{n}^{B})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Map}(D_{n}^{A},\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}D_{k}^{B})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Map}(D_{n}^{A},P^{n}\mathrm{free}_{B})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Map}(D_{n}^{A},\mathrm{free}_{B})

The first map in this composite is an equivalence since for
k<n
,
D_{k}^{B}
is
k
-excisive and
P_{k}(D^{A}_{n})=0
. The second map is an equivalence by
Lemma

3.9
and the third map is an equivalence since
D_{n}^{A}
is
n
-excisive.
This completes the argument that
\mathrm{free}
is fully faithful on symmetric sequences with nilpotent Euler classes and hence the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.9
.

It will suffice to show that
P^{n}\bigl(\bigoplus_{k>n}D_{k}^{B}\bigr)\cong 0
. We can reduce further to checking that the dual derivative
D^{\ell}\bigl(\bigoplus_{k>n}D_{k}^{B}\bigr)
vanishes for each
\ell\leq n
. Generally, the dual derivative
D^{\ell}F
of a functor
F\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Sp})
may be constructed as follows. First one forms the
cocross effect
of
F
, which is the functor of
\ell
variables defined as the total cofibre

\mathrm{cr}^{\ell}F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell}):=\mathrm{tcof}(F\circ\mathcal{X}),

where
\mathcal{X}
is the cube

\mathcal{X}\colon\mathcal{P}(\ell)\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}\colon T\mapsto\bigoplus_{t\in T}X_{t}.

Here
\mathcal{P}(\ell)
is the power set of
\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
regarded as a poset under inclusion. Now one colinearizes
\mathrm{cr}^{\ell}F
in each variable to obtain a multilinear functor
\partial^{\ell}F
described by

(\partial^{\ell}F)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell}):=\varprojlim_{j}\Sigma^{j\ell}(\mathrm{cr}^{\ell}F)(\Sigma^{-j}X_{1},\ldots,\Sigma^{-j}X_{\ell}).

Finally, the dual derivative is then

D^{\ell}F(X)=\partial^{\ell}F(X,\ldots,X)^{h\Sigma_{\ell}}.

We are analyzing the specific case
F=\bigoplus_{k>n}D_{k}^{B}
. To show that
D^{\ell}F
vanishes, we will argue that
\partial^{\ell}F
vanishes. The cocross effect
\mathrm{cr}^{\ell}F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell})
is easily seen to be the direct sum over
k>n
of the terms

\Bigl(\bigoplus_{f\colon\mathbf{k}\to\mathbf{l}}B(k)\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}X_{i}^{\otimes f^{-1}(i)}\Bigr)_{h\Sigma_{k}}.

Here the sum is indexed over surjections
f\colon\{1,\ldots,k\}\to\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
. As a consequence, the inverse system
\Sigma^{j\ell}(\mathrm{cr}^{\ell}F)(\Sigma^{-j}X_{1},\ldots,\Sigma^{-j}X_{\ell})
is the direct sum over
k>n
of the homotopy
\Sigma_{k}
-orbits of the inverse systems in
j

\bigoplus_{f\colon\mathbf{k}\to\mathbf{l}}B(k)\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{S}^{-j\rho_{f^{-1}(i)}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}X_{i}^{\otimes f^{-1}(i)}.

Here the spectrum
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{S}^{-j\rho_{f^{-1}(i)}}
denotes a smash product of (virtual) representation spheres corresponding to the stabilizer
\Sigma_{f^{-1}(1)}\times\cdots\times\Sigma_{f^{-1}(\ell)}
of a given surjection
f
. We conclude that the expression above is induced, as a spectrum with
\Sigma_{k}
-action, from a Young subgroup of the form
\Sigma_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times\Sigma_{k_{\ell}}
, with
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{\ell}=k
. Since
k>\ell
there exists an
i
with
k_{i}\geq 2
.

The conclusion of the lemma will follow if we argue that there exists a constant
C
, independent of
k
and
f
, such that each of the maps

B(k)\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{S}^{-C\rho_{k_{i}}}\to B(k)

is null in
\mathrm{Fun}(B\Sigma_{k_{1}}\times\cdots\times B\Sigma_{k_{\ell}},\mathrm{Sp})
. But this map admits a factor of the form

B(k)\otimes\mathbb{S}^{-C\rho_{k_{i}}}\to B(k)

with
k_{i}\geq 2
, which is null if
C
is at least the order of nilpotence of the Euler classes of
B
.
∎

Remark 3.13

The ‘wrong-way morphism’

of an operad

. It is given by the levelwise Spanier–Whitehead dual of the operadic bar construction. Then they prove that there is an equivalence of operads in

of the form

Under this equivalence (and the analogous one for

produces a map of operads

; Ching–Salvatore include an explicit description of this map in

]
. This morphism should be equivalent to the
\beta_{n}
we have constructed, although we will not pursue the matter here. It is interesting to note that Ching–Salvatore’s map already exists
unstably
, as a map of operads in pointed spaces. Since we have only dealt with the operadic suspension and the suspension morphism for
\mathbb{E}_{n}
in the stable case, our methods cannot address this unstable version.

Moreover, as indicated in the introduction,
[

also provides a square of operads as in
Theorem

3.11
after tensoring with an ordinary commutative ring

, based on explicit point-set models. Again, our square (after tensoring with

3.5.
The suspension morphism for operads in pointed spaces

We have discussed the operadic suspension in the stable case, i.e. for operads in
\mathrm{Sp}
, but in fact it should already exist in the context of operads in the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category of pointed spaces with smash product, or generally in pointed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories for which the tensor product is compatible with colimits in each variable. As indicated earlier, for the case of pointed spaces there are several versions of this in the literature. Arone–Kankaanrinta
[
AK14
]
construct a
sphere operad

S^{\rho}
, of which the terms
S^{\rho}(n)
are equivalent to the representation spheres
S^{\rho_{n}}
and the composition maps

S^{\rho}(n)\wedge S^{\rho}(k_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge S^{\rho}(k_{n})\to S^{\rho}(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n})

are homotopy equivalences (or even homeomorphisms). For a general non-unital operad in pointed spaces, one can then define its operadic suspension by taking the levelwise smash product with this sphere operad. An alternative construction of a sphere operad and the corresponding operadic suspension is given by Ching–Salvatore
[
CS22
]
.

In order to compare these constructions to each other and to the operadic suspension we have discussed here it is desirable to characterize the operadic suspension by universal properties, rather than focus on any specific construction of it. In this brief section we point out what such properties might be and invite the reader to take this up. We speculate that our factorizations of the suspension morphism
\sigma\colon\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{E}_{n}^{\mathrm{nu}}\to S\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\mathbb{E}_{n}^{\mathrm{nu}}
of
Theorem

3.11
already exist for the suspension morphism
\sigma\colon(\mathbb{E}_{n}^{\mathrm{nu}})_{+}\to S(\mathbb{E}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{nu}})_{+}
of the (unstable) pointed
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-operad, which is essentially obtained by means of the Euler classes. If true, these factorizations should then provide alternative proofs of
Theorem

2.4
(on suspensions of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebras) and
Corollary

2.7
(on loops of
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebras).

We will now describe the desired properties of the operadic suspension. Consider a presentable pointed symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
(\mathcal{C},\wedge)
and assume that the tensor product commutes with colimits in each variable separately. Let
\mathcal{O}
be a non-unital operad in
\mathcal{C}
. Then there should be a suspended operad
S\mathcal{O}
with terms

S\mathcal{O}(n)=\mathcal{O}(n)\wedge S^{{\rho}_{n}}

together with a canonical suspension morphism
\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}\to S\mathcal{O}
induced from the Euler classes
S^{0}\to S^{{\rho}_{n}}
. These data should satisfy the following property:

(F)

There is a canonical equivalence in
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{C})
between
\Sigma\mathrm{free}_{S\mathcal{O}}
and
\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\Sigma
. Its adjoint map
\mathrm{free}_{S\mathcal{O}}\to\Omega\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\Sigma
refines to a map of monads, making the composite

\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{free}_{\sigma}}\mathrm{free}_{S\mathcal{O}}\longrightarrow\Omega\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\Sigma

equivalent to the suspension morphism of the reduced monad
\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Let us briefly explain the relation between the factorization above and the suspension-loop adjunction on the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
itself. Consider the following right adjoint functors:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\,\Omega\,}\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{fgt}}\mathcal{C}.}

The monad on
\mathcal{C}
associated with the composed adjunction is
\Omega\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\Sigma
. The composite of right adjoints must therefore factor over a right adjoint functor
\gamma^{*}\colon\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\Omega\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\Sigma}(\mathcal{C})
. Combining this with property (F) above allows us to construct a diagram of right adjoint functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\Omega\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\Sigma}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{S\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{fgt}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{fgt}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{fgt}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{fgt}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega}

In particular, this shows that the loops functor
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\Omega}\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
factors over the restriction
\sigma^{*}\colon\mathrm{Alg}_{S\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
along the suspension morphism. Dually, the suspension functor of
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{C})
factors over the pushforward functor

. We note that in the case where

is stable, the first two arrows on the top row of the diagram are equivalences of
\infty
-categories and their composite coincides with the shift we have considered earlier. However, in the general case these need not be equivalences. For general
\mathcal{C}
, it would be desirable if
S\mathcal{O}
, together with its suspension morphism

were determined by property (F) described above, or to give some other additional characterizing properties. We invite the interested reader to take this up.

4.
Triviality of spheres

In this section we discuss some examples where our results can be applied and we investigate in what sense our results are optimal.

4.1.
Preliminaries

For this section, let us fix an
\mathbb{E}_{n+1}
-ring spectrum
A
, so that
\mathrm{Mod}(A)
is a presentably
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoidal
\infty
-category. We write
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(A)
for
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathrm{Mod}(A))
and likewise for the augmented and non-unital versions.
In what follows, an object of
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(A)
is called
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-trivial over

if it is equivalent to
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}
of its underlying augmented
\mathbb{E}_{0}
-algebra. For a space

, we write

for the “cochains of

with coefficients in

. A choice of a basepoint on

makes

into an object of
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(A)
.
As a consequence of

, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be a pointed space and

. Then

is
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-trivial over

. In particular,

is

-trivial over

.

The functor

preserves pullbacks and hence sends

to
\Omega^{n}C^{*}(X;A)
. The result then follows from

2.12
.
∎

Let us assume for a moment that

is a discrete commutative ring and let us also denote by

its associated Eilenberg–Mac Lane spectrum. Then
H^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X;A)
is also canonically an object of
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(A)
, via the composite of lax symmetric monoidal functors

(1)

\mathrm{Mod}(A)\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}\mathrm{Mod}(A)^{\heartsuit}_{\mathrm{gr}}\xrightarrow{\,H\,}\mathrm{Mod}(A).

Here,
\mathrm{Mod}(A)^{\heartsuit}_{\mathrm{gr}}
denotes the ordinary symmetric monoidal category of graded discrete
A
-modules, whereas
\mathrm{Mod}(A)
is equivalent to
\mathcal{D}(A)
, the unbounded derived category of

, and the second functor in the above display is the graded Eilenberg–Mac Lane functor.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a space and
A
a commutative ring. We say that

is
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-formal over
A
if there exists an equivalence

in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}^{\mathrm{aug}}(A)
.

Remark 4.3
.

The formal spaces from rational homotopy theory are, in the above notation, the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-formal spaces over
\mathbb{Q}
. This uses the equivalence between commutative differential graded algebras over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Q}))

[
Lur17
, Prop. 7.1.4.11]
. Surprisingly, a space which is
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-formal over
\mathbb{Q}
is already
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-formal over
\mathbb{Q}

[
Sal17
,
CPRNW23
]
so that the notion of
\mathbb{E}_{k}
-formality over
\mathbb{Q}
is independent of
k
once
k\geq 1
. This is very special to fields of characteristic
0
. Indeed, as we shall see below, the same statement fails for non-rational rings.

Our main result implies the following formality result.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a pointed space and
n\geq 0
. If there exists an equivalence
\tilde{C}^{*}(X;A)\simeq\tilde{H}^{*}(X;A)
in
\mathrm{Mod}(A)
, then
\Sigma^{n}X
is
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-formal over
A
.

Proof.

We have observed above that
C^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X;A)\simeq\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\tilde{C}^{*}(X;A))
. Hence it suffices to observe that also
H^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X;A)\simeq\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\tilde{H}^{*}(X;A))
. This follows from the fact that the composite (
1
) commutes with the suspension and loop functors.
∎

Example 4.5
.

It is well-known in rational homotopy theory that suspensions are formal. One way to prove this is to show that any suspension is rationally equivalent to a wedge of spheres, and then to separately show that spheres are formal, and that wedges of formal spaces are formal, see e.g.
[
Ber12
, Theorem 10.28]
under additional finiteness assumptions.
Theorem

4.4
gives the following non-computational proof of the formality of suspensions. Assume that
A
is a commutative ring of global dimension
\leq 1
, e.g. a Dedekind domain or a field. It is well-known that for any object
M\in\mathrm{Mod}(A)
, there exists an equivalence
M\simeq\oplus_{n}\Sigma^{n}\pi_{n}(M)
, see e.g.
[
HLN21
, proof of Cor. 3.8]
which applies verbatim to the case at hand. The right hand side is the value of the composite (
1
) applied to
M
. In particular
\Sigma X
is
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-formal over
A
, and hence as indicated above,
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-formal over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Example 4.6
.

Suppose that
X
is of finite
\mathbb{Z}
-homological type and
A
is a commutative ring. Then the canonical map
C^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A\to C^{*}(X;A)
is an equivalence. The same statement for cohomology in place if cochains is, of course, not correct in general. However, it is true if the cohomology of
X
is in addition free as a graded abelian group. In this case, the same is true for
\Sigma^{n}X
and reasoning as in

one deduces that
\Sigma^{n}X
is
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-formal over any commutative ring

. Examples of such

include spheres, projective spaces over
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}
, loop spaces of spheres, free loop spaces of odd dimensional spheres, as well as products of such spaces.

Remark 4.7
.

There are many examples of formal spaces whose cochain algebras are not trivial algebras, for instance compact Kähler manifolds different from
S^{2}
. Our methods cannot be applied in this situation.

We single out the most basic cases of suspensions in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.8
.

For
n\geq 0
, the
n
-sphere

is
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-formal over any commutative ring.

Coming back to interpreting formality of spheres in terms of triviality, one may wonder whether our results are suboptimal in the sense that

-fold loop objects are even trivial as
\mathbb{E}_{n+1}
-algebras. The above case of cochains on suspensions gives a good test case for such questions.

Observation 4.9
.

A consequence of

and

are

-algebras in

-trivial over

The following result shows that, in general, the conclusion of

is not sharp.

Lemma 4.10

Let

-ring spectrum where

4.4.
Free loop spaces

We finally discuss some applications of our formality results to free loop spaces that might be interesting from the point of view of string topology of spheres.
Let us write
C^{*}(X)
for
C^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
to simplify notation. We recall that for a connected, nilpotent space
X
of finite
\mathbb{Z}
-homological type with finite fundamental group, the free loop space
LX
as well as
X\times X
are again of
\mathbb{Z}
-homological finite type. Under this assumption, we have canonical equivalences in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{Z})

C^{*}(LX)\simeq C^{*}(X)\otimes_{C^{*}(X\times X)}C^{*}(X)\simeq\mathrm{HH}(C^{*}(X))

and that Hochschild homology (relative to
\mathbb{Z}
) is a functor
\mathrm{HH}\colon\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{Z})^{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{T}}
. The above composite equivalence is then in fact
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant. As a consequence of
Theorem

4.1
, for
n\geq 1
, we obtain an equivalence of
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}
-
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras with
\mathbb{T}
-action

\mathrm{HH}(C^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X))\simeq\mathrm{HH}(H^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X)).

Indeed, we have canonical equivalences
C^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X)\simeq\Omega^{n}C^{*}(X)
and
H^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X)\simeq\Omega^{n}H^{*}(X)
, so
Theorem

4.1
implies the claim once we observe that there exists an equivalence of
\mathbb{E}_{0}
-
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras
C^{*}(X)\simeq H^{*}(X)
since the global dimension of
\mathbb{Z}
is one, see the argument in
Example

4.5
. In particular, we obtain an equivalence of
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}
-
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras with
\mathbb{T}
-action

C^{*}(L\Sigma^{n}X)\simeq\mathrm{HH}(H^{*}(\Sigma^{n}X)),

providing a reminiscence of formality for
L(\Sigma^{n}X)
.

As a nice explicit example, for
n\geq 2
and a commutative ring
A
, we find an equivalence of
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}
-
A
-algebras with
\mathbb{T}
-action

C^{*}(LS^{n};A)\simeq\mathrm{HH}_{A}(A[x]/x^{2}),\quad|x|=-n

where
\mathrm{HH}_{A}
denotes Hochschild homology relative to
A
. For instance for
A=\mathbb{Z}
,
additively,
\mathrm{HH}(\mathbb{Z}[x]/x^{2})
is very easy to compute, because it is easy to resolve
\mathbb{Z}[x]/x^{2}
as a module over
\mathbb{Z}[y,z]/(y^{2},z^{2})
(viewed as a graded commutative ring with
|y|=|z|=-n
), where the module action comes from the ring map sending both
y
and
z
to
x
, see e.g.
[
Lod98
, E.4.1.8]
, but the above equivalence also sheds some light on the coherent multiplicative structure in
C^{*}(LS^{n};\mathbb{Z})
, more precisely its
\mathbb{E}_{n-1}
-algebra structure.
There are a number of papers surrounding the relation of the string topology of
S^{n}
with Hochschild (co)homology of
C^{*}(S^{n})
or
H^{*}(S^{n})
, in particular for
n=2
and with coefficients
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, see e.g.
[
Men09
,
PT23
]
. It would be interesting to find a relation between these works and our formality result.

Finally, we mention that in
[
MNO24
]
the authors show, using the the above relation to the free loop space, that

is not formal as a

\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra. It is natural to then ask whether

is formal as a framed

-algebra if and only if

is rational, but we will not address this here.

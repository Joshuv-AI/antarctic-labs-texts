On Stability in Optimistic Bilevel Optimization
Johannes O. Royset

Abstract




Abstract
. Solutions of bilevel optimization problems tend to suffer from instability under changes to problem data. In the optimistic setting, we construct a lifted formulation that exhibits desirable stability properties under mild assumptions that neither invoke convexity nor smoothness. The upper- and lower-level problems might involve integer restrictions and disjunctive constraints. In a range of results, we invoke at most pointwise and local calmness for the lower-level problem in a sense that holds broadly. The lifted formulation is computationally attractive with structural properties being brought out and an outer approximation algorithm becoming available.

Keywords
: Bilevel optimization, optimistic formulation, stability, epi-convergence.

Introduction

Bilevel problems tend to be ill-posed in the sense that small changes to problem data may cause disproportional large changes in solutions. In fact, ill-posedness can be expected as it is well-known from variational analysis that the set of minimizers to a function is stable in a meaningful sense only under strong assumptions. Even when the lower-level problem is seemingly “nice,” a small change in a lower-level constraint may have a large effect on the solution of a bilevel problem;
[
3
]
furnishes an examples with a linear lower-level objective function, a convex lower-level feasible set satisfying the Slater condition, and a unique lower-level solution satisfying a strict complementarity condition for all upper-level feasible decisions. Additional examples appear in
[
8
]
. Since problem data is almost always unsettled in practice, it is clear that blindly solving a bilevel problem without any safeguards may not produce meaningful decisions. In this paper, we provide a lifted formulation of bilevel problems that is fundamentally well-posed in the sense that discrepancies in the problem data still lead to useful decisions and bounds in the limit as the discrepancies vanish. While the lifted formulation may involve a large number of inequality constraints, it preserves structural properties of the upper- and lower-level problems and thus can be approaches by many well-known algorithmic strategies including those from semi-infinite optimization.

We consider the following bilevel problem. For a nonempty set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
, the upper-level problem is to minimize
f(x,y)
over
x\in X
, where the lower-level problem furnishes
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For nonempty sets
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
, a function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, and a mapping
H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
, the lower-level problem amounts to minimizing
g(x,y)
over
y\in Y
while satisfying
H(x,y)\in D
. Since
D
could be the product set of
\{0\}
and
(-\infty,0]
, the inclusion
H(x,y)\in D
may represent any finite number of equality and inequality constraints. Disjunctive constraints emerge from nonconvex
D
. Throughout, we adopt the
optimistic
perspective. (We refer to
[
28
,
29
]
for the pessimistic perspective and to
[
1
,
8
]
for other alternatives.) This leads to the
bilevel problem

(P)

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in X,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~f(x,y)

\displaystyle\mbox{ subject to }~~y\in\tau\mbox{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in Y}\big\{g(x,z)~\big|~H(x,z)\in D\big\},

(1.1)

where
\tau\in[0,\infty)
allows us to also consider near-optimal solutions of the lower-level problem by sometimes setting
\tau>0
; recall that
\varepsilon\mbox{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in Z}\psi(z)=\{\bar{z}\in Z~|~\psi(\bar{z})\leq\inf_{z\in Z}\psi(z)+\varepsilon,\psi(\bar{z})<\infty\}
for any extended real-valued function
\psi
, set
Z
, and tolerance
\varepsilon\in[0,\infty)
. Since
f
is extended real-valued, (P) may have
implicit constraints
in addition to the
explicit constraints

x\in X
and (
1.1
). Thus, its
feasible set

\{(x,y)\in X\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,f(x,y)<\infty,\mbox{\eqref{eqn:argminconstr} holds}\}
may impose coupling constraints between
x
and
y
.

To model unsettled data in (P), we consider
f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
,
g^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
H^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
as approximations of
f,g
, and
H
, respectively. We examine the situation as these approximations become more accurate as indicated by higher values of the superscript
\nu\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dots\}
. As alluded to above, replacing
f,g,H
in (P) by
f^{\nu},g^{\nu},H^{\nu}
produces the
naive problem

\displaystyle\mbox{(NP)}^{\nu}~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in X,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~f^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle\mbox{ subject to }~~y\in\tau\mbox{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in Y}\big\{g^{\nu}(x,z)~\big|~H^{\nu}(x,z)\in D\big\},

which may have solutions far from those of (P) even if the corresponding functions are close, for example in the sense of the sup-norm on
X\times Y
; Section
3
furnishes concrete instances. In this paper, we propose the following
lifted problem
as an alternative:

\displaystyle\mbox{(P)}^{\nu}~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda}~f^{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle+\sigma^{\nu}\|u\|-\theta^{\nu}\alpha

\displaystyle\mbox{subject to }H^{\nu}(x,y)+u

\displaystyle\in D

\displaystyle g^{\nu}(x,y)+\alpha

\displaystyle\leq g^{\nu}(x,z)+\lambda\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,z),D\big)+\tau^{\nu}~~\forall z\in Y^{\nu}

(1.2)

\displaystyle x\in X,~y

\displaystyle\in Y,~u\in\mathbb{R}^{q},~\alpha\in(-\infty,0],~\lambda\in[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}].

The problem involves the nonnegative parameters
\sigma^{\nu},\theta^{\nu},\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}
, and
\tau^{\nu}
. The two first ones are penalty parameters for the auxiliary decision variables
u\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. (Squared and other penalty terms are also possible, but we omit the details.) The auxiliary decision variable
\lambda
is bounded from above by
\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}
. The last parameter,
\tau^{\nu}
, is a counterpart to the tolerance
\tau
for the lower-level problem in (P). Given any point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
\dist(a,D)=\inf_{a^{\prime}\in D}\|a-a^{\prime}\|
is the
point-to-set distance
under an
arbitrary norm

\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{q}
. The norm underpinning
\dist(\cdot,D)
in (P)
ν
matches the norm used in the term
\sigma^{\nu}\|u\|
of the objective function. For example,
if
D=(-\infty,0]^{q}
, then
\dist(H^{\nu}(x,z),D)=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\max\{0,h^{\nu}_{j}(x,z)\}
when
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{1}
and
h_{j}^{\nu}
is the
j
th component of
H^{\nu}
.

Lastly, (P)
ν
involves a set
Y^{\nu}\subset Y
, which could coincide with
Y
. Since
Y
tends to have a high or infinite cardinality, the possibility of considering a smaller set
Y^{\nu}
in (
1.2
) is practically beneficial.

At first glance, (P)
ν
appears only loosely related to (P). However, the next section establishes that solving (P)
ν
, despite the use of approximating data
(f^{\nu},g^{\nu},H^{\nu},Y^{\nu})
, bounds the minimum value of (P) in the limit as
\nu\to\infty
under mild assumptions. We also show that (P)
ν
can produce solutions that in the limit are feasible and even optimal for (P). This takes place in settings where (NP)
ν
fails to produce such convergence properties.

In addition to have desirable limiting properties as
\nu\to\infty
, (P)
ν
is constructed with computations in mind. While there are several nontrivial challenges associated with solving (P)
ν
, it is apparent that any structural properties in
g^{\nu}
and
H^{\nu}
such as convexity or linearity are being brought forward. Moreover,
Y^{\nu}
does not depend on
x
, which facilitates the use of outer approximations as see in Section
4
.

For

\displaystyle y\in\tau\mbox{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in Y}\big\{g(x,z)\,\big|\,H(x,z)\in D\big\}~~~~~~~\Longleftrightarrow~~

\displaystyle y\in Y,~~H(x,y)\in D,~~g(x,y)\leq\inf_{z\in Y}\big\{g(x,z)\,\big|\,H(x,z)\in D\big\}+\tau.

Although just for equality and inequality constraints in the lower-level problem,

utilizes this equivalence as it introduces

While also utilizing (

[

Stability results for bilevel optimization problems extend at least back to
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
. Most significantly,

]
(see also
[

]
) leverages notions of variational convergence in the study of bilevel problems under perturbations and in particular convergence of the min-value function of the lower-level problem. It is recognized that a regularization of the lower-level problem by allowing near-optimal solutions improves stability properties. Still,
[
26
]
invokes arguably strong assumptions including the existence of interior points for the lower-level feasible set and convexity and quasiconvexity for certain components; examples illustrate how these assumptions appear nearly unavoidable. We continue in this tradition, but define (P)

[
26

and elsewhere. The added variables in (P)

There are several issues that are beyond the scope of the paper. We solely focus on the optimistic formulation (P) and thus omit a treatment of its many alternatives. We concentrate on recovering global solutions of (P) by computing near-optimal solutions of (P)
ν
. Local and stationary solutions are more challenging to treat for several reasons including some listed in
[
16
]
. We shy away from quantifying the rate of convergence, which is difficult for methods relying on constraint penalization.

The example also highlights the critical role of

Detail.
Suppose that

and

. Then,

and

. We see that even if
\{\sigma^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded,
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\leq\mathfrak{m}
. The lower bound improves in the limit to coincide with

if

and

; cf. Assumption

(f).

Theorem
2.2
(b) asserts that (P)
ν
may also furnish an upper bound on the minimum value of (P) in the limit. Now,

and

and

. The existence of a compact set

1.2
). Thus, bounds on

(c) establishes that a sequence of near-optimal solutions of (P)

In addition to the fact recorded at the end of the theorem for the case

2.2
Bounds and Optimal Solutions under Calmness at a Point

A challenge when relating (P) to (P)
ν
is the fact that the lower-level problem of the former involves the constraint
H(x,z)\in D
, while the latter has a term
\lambda\dist(H^{\nu}(x,z),D)
. Still, under broad conditions,

\inf_{z\in Y}\big\{g(x,z)\,\big|\,H(x,z)\in D\big\}=\inf_{z\in Y}g(x,z)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,z),D\big)~~\mbox{ for sufficiently large }\lambda

according to the principle of exact penalization. Despite the presence of approximations and other changes, one can glean from the inequality constraints (
1.2
) in (P)
ν
a role for exact penalization. This subsection examines the stronger conclusions that become available under a condition for exactness.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, we adopt the notation

V(x,u)=\inf_{y\in Y}\big\{g(x,y)~\big|~H(x,y)+u\in D\big\},

with
V
representing the
value function
for the lower-level problem in (P) under changes to
u
.

2.4 Definition

(calmness at a point).

The value function
V
is
calm at

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there is
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
such that

V(x,u)\geq V(x,0)-\lambda\|u\|~~~\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{q},

with
\lambda
then being referred to as a
penalty threshold
.

We refer to
[
7
,
6
]
for a classical treatment of calmness in constrained optimization, which is closely related to the development here. As compared to
[
7
,
6
]
, we focus on minimum values instead of locally minimum values and we also need to consider the role of
x
. Thus, our definitions and results about exact penalization deviate slightly from those in the literature and proofs are given in Section
5
.

The norm in Definition
2.4
is the same as the one underpinning the point-to-set distance. This yields the following characterization of calmness at a point.

2.5 Proposition

(characterization of calmness at a point).

For nonempty
D
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the value function
V
is calm at
x
with penalty threshold
\lambda
if and only if

\inf_{y\in Y}g(x,y)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)=\inf_{y\in Y}\big\{g(x,y)~\big|~H(x,y)\in D\big\}.

In view of the proposition, calmness at
x
is therefore exactly the property that is needed for exact penalization of the constraint
H(x,z)\in D
in the lower-level problem of (P) for
x
. We return to a sufficient condition for calmness after the main theorem of this subsection.

2.6 Theorem

(bounds and optimality under calmness).
Suppose that
\theta^{\nu}|\tau^{\nu}-\tau|\to 0
and there exists an optimal solution
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
of
(P)
such that the value function
V
is calm at
x^{\star}
. Under Assumption
2.1
, the following hold:

(a)

If
\{\theta^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded away from zero, then
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\leq\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

Suppose that
\sigma^{\nu},\theta^{\nu}\to\infty
and there is a compact set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m+q+2}
such that for each
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an optimal solution of
(P)
ν
in
B
. Let
\lambda^{\star}\in[0,\infty)
be a penalty threshold for the value function
V
at
x^{\star}
. If
\lambda\in[\lambda^{\star},\infty)
, then there exist a vanishing sequence
\{\varepsilon^{\nu}\in[0,\infty),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
and a sequence
\{w^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+q+2},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that
w^{\nu}
is an
\varepsilon^{\nu}
-optimal solution of
(P)
ν
and

w^{\nu}\to(x^{\star},y^{\star},0,0,\lambda).

Moreover,
\mathop{\rm lim}\nolimits\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}=\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(c)

Suppose that
\sigma^{\nu}\to\infty
,
\theta^{\nu}\to\infty
,
\varepsilon^{\nu}\to 0
, and, for each
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
,
(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})
is an
\varepsilon^{\nu}
-optimal solution of
(P)
ν
. If there is a subsequence
N\subset\mathbb{N}
and
(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{u},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\lambda})
such that

(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{u},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\lambda}),

then
(\hat{x},\hat{y})
is an optimal solution of
(P)
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem
2.6
(a) supplements Theorem
2.2
(a) by not requiring a compact
Y
and allowing
\tau^{\nu}=\tau
for all
\nu
. The requirement about calmness is mild as it only needs to hold at
some

x^{\star}
, and this point does not need to be known when solving (P)
ν
.
Again, an examination of the proof of Theorem
2.6
(a) shows that the liminf-condition in Assumption
2.1
(e) is superfluous; cf. the discussion after Theorem
2.2
.

2.7 Example

(lack of calmness).

For the data in Example
2.3
,
V(x,u)=0
if
u\leq 0
and
V(x,u)=\infty
otherwise. Since the value function
V
in this case is independent of
x
, it is calm at every
x
. The situation changes if
g(x,y)=y
. Then,

V(x,u)=\begin{cases}\infty&\mbox{ if }u>0\\
-\sqrt{-u}&\mbox{ if }u\in[-1,0]\\
-1&\mbox{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

which is not calm at any
x
. This might invalidate the conclusion
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\leq\mathfrak{m}
in Theorem
2.6
(a).

Detail.
Suppose that
g^{\nu}(x,y)=g(x,y)=y
so that
\delta^{\nu}=0
in Assumption
2.1
and
\theta^{\nu}=\nu^{1/3}
,
\sigma^{\nu}=\nu^{2/3}
,
\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}=\nu^{1/3}
, which implies that Assumption
2.1
(f) holds because
\eta^{\nu}=1/\nu
. In this case with
\tau^{\nu}=0
, (P)
ν
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda}\,f^{\nu}(x,y)+\nu^{2/3}|u|-\nu^{1/3}\alpha

\displaystyle\mbox{ subject to }y^{2}+1/\nu+u\leq 0,~x\in X,~y\in[-1,1],~\lambda\in[0,\nu^{1/3}],~\alpha\leq 0

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~y+\alpha\leq z+\lambda\max\{0,z^{2}+1/\nu\}~~~\forall z\in[-1,1].

For
\lambda\geq 1/2
, the last constraints reduce to the single constraint
y+\alpha\leq-1/(4\lambda)+\lambda/\nu
. Since
\lambda=\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}
is always optimal, the constraint simplify further to

y+\alpha\leq-\frac{1}{4\nu^{1/3}}+\frac{1}{\nu^{2/3}}.

While the right-hand vanishes, it has a negative value of order
\nu^{-1/3}
for large
\nu
. The constraint can be satisfied by setting
y=0
, but then
\alpha
is of order
\nu^{-1/3}
, which incurs a nonvanishing penalty in the objective function because
\theta^{\nu}=\nu^{1/3}
. Alternatively,
\alpha=0
and
y
is of order
\nu^{-1/3}
but then
u
must be of order
\nu^{-2/3}
. Since
\sigma^{\nu}=\nu^{2/3}
, we again face a nonvanishing penalty term in the objective function. Consequently, even if
f^{\nu}
is identical to
f
,
\mathfrak{m}
remains strictly below
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}
and the conclusion of Theorem
2.6
(a) fails.

Theorem
2.6
(b) has no counterpart in Theorem
2.2
. It establishes that
any
optimal solution of (P) satisfying the calmness assumption of the theorem can be recovered by solving (P)
ν
. Thus, there is no optimal solution of (P) of this kind that is “isolated” in the sense that it is not approachable by near-optimal solutions of (P)
ν
. The
\lambda
-components of the stipulated sequence
\{w^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are bounded. This partially addresses the concern from the discussion after Theorem
2.2
about unbounded
\lambda
-values.

The conclusion in Theorem
2.6
(c) strengthens that of Theorem
2.2
(c) by guaranteeing optimality in (P), and not merely feasibility. In addition, the minimum values
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}
of (P)
ν
now converge, at least along the subsequence indexed by
N
, to the minimum value of (P).

As an examples of a sufficient condition for calmness at a point, we furnish the following proposition. We denote by

\dist_{2}(y,C)=\inf_{y^{\prime}\in C}\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2}
the
point-to-set distance in the Euclidean norm
.

2.8 Proposition

(sufficient condition for calmness at a point).
For the value function
V
to be calm at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following suffices: There exist
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\in[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\big|g(x,y)-g(x,y^{\prime})\big|\leq\kappa_{1}\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2}~~\forall y,y^{\prime}\in Y

(2.3)

\displaystyle\dist_{2}\big(y,S(x)\big)\leq\kappa_{2}\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)~~\forall y\in Y,\mbox{ where }S(x)=\big\{z\in Y~\big|~H(x,z)\in D\big\}.

(2.4)

The regularity condition (
2.4
) specifies a certain growth rate of the constraint mapping
H(x,\cdot)
as
y
moves away from the lower-level feasible set
S(x)
. This holds trivially if
Y
is finite as in some integer programs. It also holds when
Y
is a polyhedron,
H(x,\cdot)
is affine, and
D
is polyhedral. Then, Hoffman’s lemma
[
21
]
ensures the existence of
\kappa_{2}
such that (
2.4
) holds; see, for example,
[
42
]
for recent advances in quantifying
\kappa_{2}
, which is called the Hoffman’s constant in this case. More generally, (
2.4
) relates to constraint qualifications as discussed in the next subsection.

Growth of the values of a constraint mapping at points moving away from the feasible set
S(x)
is distinct from growth of lower-level objective function values at points moving away from lower-level optimal solutions. The latter is discussed in
[
47
]
under the name uniformly weak sharp minima.

2.3
Optimal Solutions under Local Calmness

We obtain an even stronger conclusion if a
local
calmness property holds at a cluster point of solutions generated from (P)
ν
. This largely eliminates concerns about unbounded
\lambda
-values.

2.9 Definition

(local calmness).

The value function
V
is
locally calm at

\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there is
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
and
\rho\in(0,\infty)
such that

V(x,u)\geq V(x,0)-\lambda\|u\|~~~\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{q},~x\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\rho),

with
\lambda
being referred to as a
penalty threshold
and
\rho
as a
radius of calmness
.

Local calmness at
\bar{x}
would hold, for example, if the constants
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
in Proposition
2.8
apply not only at a point, but also locally near
\bar{x}
. Again, in the polyhedral case, Hoffman’s lemma
[
21
]
enters with the uniformity locally in
x
corresponding to having an upper bound on the Hoffman’s constants as the polyhedral system is perturbed by
x
. This is a relatively mild assumption because, for example, the Hoffman’s constant of a linear system of inequalities is independent of right-hand side perturbations.

In the case of
D=(-\infty,0]^{q}
,
Y=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}~|~g_{i}(y)\leq 0,i=1,\dots,r\}
being compact for smooth
g_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\{y\in Y~|~H(x,y)\in D\}\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
H
is smooth, Theorem 2.10 in
[
43
]
establishes that the value function
V
is locally calm at every
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
under the constraint qualification: for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in Y}\{g(x,z)~|~H(x,z)\in D\}
, one has

\big\{\nabla g_{i}(y),i\in I(y);~~\nabla_{y}h_{j}(x,y),j\in J(x,y)\big\}~~\mbox{ are linearly independent vectors,}

where
I(y)=\{i~|~g_{i}(y)=0\}
,
J(x,y)=\{j~|~h_{j}(x,y)=0\}
, and
h_{j}
is the
j
th component of
H
.

We refer to
[
7
]
and references therein for other constraint qualifications related to local calmness.

2.10 Theorem

(optimality under local calmness).
Suppose that
\sigma^{\nu},\theta^{\nu}\to\infty
while
\varepsilon^{\nu}
and
\theta^{\nu}|\tau^{\nu}-\tau|
vanish, Assumption
2.1
holds, and there exists an optimal solution
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
of
(P)
such that the value function
V
is calm at
x^{\star}
. Moreover, for each
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})
be an
\varepsilon^{\nu}
-optimal solution of
(P)
ν
. Suppose there is a subsequence
N\subset\mathbb{N}
and
(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{u},\hat{\alpha})
such that

(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu})\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{u},\hat{\alpha})

and the value function
V
is locally calm at
\hat{x}
. Then, the following hold:

(a)

If
Y^{\nu}=Y
for all
\nu
sufficiently large, then
(\hat{x},\hat{y})
is an optimal solution of
(P)
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

If
\theta^{\nu}\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\sup_{y\in Y}\dist_{2}(y,Y^{\nu})\to 0
and there are Lipschitz constants
\kappa_{g},\kappa_{H}\in[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\big|g(x,y)-g(x,y^{\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{g}\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2}~~~\forall x\in X,~y,y^{\prime}\in Y

\displaystyle\big\|H(x,y)-H(x,y^{\prime})\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{H}\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2}~~~\forall x\in X,~y,y^{\prime}\in Y,

then
(\hat{x},\hat{y})
is an optimal solution of
(P)
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem
2.10
is distinct from Theorems
2.2
(c) and
2.6
(c) by
not
requiring that the
\lambda
-components of the near-optimal solutions of (P)
ν
are bounded, at least along a subsequence. It is only the
x
-,
y
-,
u
-, and
\alpha
-components that need to have a cluster point in Theorem
2.10
. This is nearly automatic, for example, when
X
and
Y
are compact. Local calmness is only required to hold at the
x
-component of such a cluster point, while pointwise calmness is assumed to hold at
some

x^{\star}
.

When
Y^{\nu}=Y
, no other assumptions are needed to ensure that the
x
- and
y
-components of a cluster point form an optimal solution of (P); see part (a). When
Y^{\nu}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, are strict subsets of
Y
, additional Lipschitz properties enter in (b). While these might be perceived as mild, we also rely on a sufficiently slow growth of
\theta^{\nu}
and
\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}
relative to the convergence of
Y^{\nu}
to

, which could be more restrictive.

Example

ν

3.1 Example

For

3.2 Example

(instability in (P)).

Let
X=[1,2]
,
Y=\{0,1\}
,

,

, and

\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{y\in Y}\{g(x,y)~|~H(x,y)\in D\}=\{1\}

\tau=0
reduces to

(y-1/2)x
over
x\in[1,2]
and
y\in\{0,1\}
while satisfying

. Thus, the unique optimal solution of
(P)
is
(x^{\star},y^{\star})=(1,1)
with minimum value
\mathfrak{m}=1/2
.

For
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that
H(x,y)
is approximated by
H^{\nu}(x,y)=y-1+1/\nu
, while the other components remain unchanged. Thus, parts (a,c,d,e) of Assumption
2.1
hold with
\delta^{\nu}=0
and
\eta^{\nu}=1/\nu
. The naive approach of simply substituting in the approximating quantities produces the following instance of
(NP)
ν
:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in X,y\in\mathbb{R}}~f^{\nu}(x,y)=(y-\tfrac{1}{2})x

\displaystyle\mbox{ subject to }~y\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in Y}\big\{g^{\nu}(x,z)~\big|~H^{\nu}(x,z)\in D\big\}=\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in\{0,1\}}\{-xz~|~z-1+1/\nu\leq 0\},

with unique optimal solution
(\tilde{x}^{\nu},\tilde{y}^{\nu})=(2,0)
and corresponding minimum value
\tilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{\nu}=-1
. As
\nu\to\infty
, neither the optimal solutions nor the minimum values of
(NP)
ν
converge to the corresponding quantities of
(P)
. This occurs despite a seemingly minor discrepancy in
H^{\nu}
relative to
H
.

It turns out that
(P)
ν
is much better behaved.

Detail.
For
Y^{\nu}=Y
and
\tau^{\nu}=0
, it takes the form

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda}~(y-\tfrac{1}{2})x+\sigma^{\nu}|u|

\displaystyle-\theta^{\nu}\alpha

\displaystyle\mbox{ subject to }~y-1+1/\nu+u

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle-xy+\alpha

\displaystyle\leq-xz+\lambda\max\{0,z-1+1/\nu\}~~~\forall z\in\{0,1\}

\displaystyle x\in[1,2],~y

\displaystyle\in\{0,1\},~u\in\mathbb{R},~\alpha\leq 0,~\lambda\in[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}].

In light of Assumption
2.1
and the theorems above, the parameters
\sigma^{\nu}
,
\theta^{\nu}
, and
\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}
can be set to

\sigma^{\nu}=\nu^{1/2}
,
\theta^{\nu}=\nu^{1/3}
, and
\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}=\nu^{1/3}
. Since
\delta^{\nu}=0
and
\eta^{\nu}=1/\nu
, Assumption
2.1
(f) holds while both
\sigma^{\nu}
and
\theta^{\nu}
tend to infinity as
\nu\to\infty
. An optimal solution of this instance of (P)
ν
is determined, for example, by recognizing that
\lambda
can be set to
\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}=\nu^{1/3}
and the two constraints indexed by
z
reduces to the single constraint
-xy+\alpha\leq-x+\nu^{-2/3}
. This leads to the optimal solutions and minimum values:

\displaystyle\mbox{for }\nu=1,2,\dots,6~~~(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=(1,0,0,-1+\nu^{-2/3},\nu^{1/3}),~~\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}=-\tfrac{1}{2}+\nu^{1/3}-\nu^{-1/3}

\displaystyle\mbox{for }\nu=7,8,\dots~~~~~~(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=(1,1,-1/\nu,0,\nu^{1/3}),~~~~~~~~~~\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}+\nu^{-1/2}.

Clearly,
(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\to(x^{\star},y^{\star})
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\to\mathfrak{m}
.

In this instance of (P), the value function
V
is actually locally calm at any

. Thus, there is no need for the

-component of a solution of (P)
ν
to grow beyond bounds and, in fact, it can remain at zero. This insight produces another set
of optimal solutions and minimum values of (P)
ν
:

\displaystyle\mbox{for }\nu=1,2,3~~~~~~\,(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=(1,0,0,-1,0),~~~~~~~~~\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}=-\tfrac{1}{2}+\nu^{1/3}

\displaystyle\mbox{for }\nu=4,5,\dots~~~~(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=(1,1,-1/\nu,0,0),~~~~~~\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}+\nu^{-1/2},

with slightly faster recovery of
(x^{\star},y^{\star})
as
\nu\to\infty
.

The previous example involves a nonconvex set
Y=\{0,1\}
. However, difficulties are not limited to nonconvex lower-level problems as the next example shows.

3.3 Example

(instability of (P) under convex
Y
).

For
g(x,y)=0
,
g^{\nu}(x,y)=y^{2}/\nu
,
Y=Y^{\nu}=[-2,1]
,
H(x,y)=H^{\nu}(x,y)=-y-1
,
D=(-\infty,0]
,
X=[0,1]
, and
f(x,y)=f^{\nu}(x,y)=(y+1/2)x
, we obtain

\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{y\in Y}\big\{g(x,y)~\big|~H(x,y)\in D\big\}=[-1,1],~~~~\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{y\in Y}\big\{g^{\nu}(x,y)~\big|~H^{\nu}(x,y)\in D\big\}=\{0\},

with these lower-level problems being convex. Suppose that
\tau=\tau^{\nu}=0
. Thus, we find that
(P)
has minimum value
\mathfrak{m}=-1/2
, with optimal solution
x^{\star}=1
,
y^{\star}=-1
. The corresponding numbers for the naive problem
(NP)
ν
are 0, 0, and 0, respectively, regardless of
\nu
.

Again,
(P)
ν
is better behaved. If
\theta^{\nu}=\sqrt{\nu}
, then Theorem
2.10
applies with calmness and local calmness holding trivially.

Detail.
For
\sigma^{\nu}>1
, every optimal solution of (P)
ν
has
u=0
. In (
1.2
),
z=0
is an option which makes the right-hand side of those constraint equal to zero. Thus, (P)
ν
reduces to

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x,y,\alpha}~(y+1/2)x-\theta^{\nu}\alpha~\mbox{ subject to }~x\in[0,1],~y\in[-1,1],~y^{2}/\nu+\alpha\leq 0,~\alpha\leq 0.

By inspection,
(0,0,0)
is an optimal solution of this problem for
\nu=1,2,3
. For
\nu\geq 4
, this changes to
x^{\nu}=1
,
y^{\nu}=-1
,
\alpha^{\nu}=-1/\nu
, with minimum value
\mathfrak{m}^{k}=-1/2+1/\sqrt{\nu}
.

We refer to
[
8
, Section 4.4.2]
for other examples.

4
Computational Considerations

Next, we fix
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
and examine the challenges associated with solving (P)
ν
. This provides the basis for an algorithm for (P) that iteratively and approximately solves (P)
ν
for increasingly large
\nu
. We start by establishing sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions before turning to an outer approximation algorithm.

4.1 Proposition

(existence of near-optimal solutions for (P)
ν
).

For
f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
,
g^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
H^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
, suppose that
D
and
X\times Y\cap\dom f^{\nu}
are nonempty and, for all
x\in X
,

\inf_{z\in Y^{\nu}}g^{\nu}(x,z)+\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,z),D\big)>-\infty.

(4.1)

Then, the following hold:

(a)

If
\varepsilon^{\nu}\in(0,\infty)
and
\inf_{x\in X,y\in Y}f^{\nu}(x,y)>-\infty
, then there exists an
\varepsilon^{\nu}
-optimal solution of
(P)
ν
.

(b)

If
\sigma^{\nu}>0
,
\theta^{\nu}>0
, the functions
f^{\nu},g^{\nu}
are lsc,
H^{\nu}
is continuous relative to
X\times Y
, the function
g^{\nu}(\cdot,z)
is continuous relative to
X
for each
z\in Y^{\nu}
, the sets
X,Y,D
are closed, and the set

\big\{(x,y)\in X\times Y~\big|~f^{\nu}(x,y)\leq\gamma\big\}

(4.2)

is bounded for each
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, then there exists an optimal solution of
(P)
ν
.

Proof.
For (a), it suffices to confirm that
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}
is finite. By assumption, there exist
\bar{x}\in X
,
\bar{y}\in Y
,
\bar{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, and
\bar{\alpha}\in(-\infty,0]
such that
f^{\nu}(\bar{x},\bar{y})<\infty
,
H^{\nu}(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\bar{u}\in D
, and

g^{\nu}(\bar{x},\bar{y})+\bar{\alpha}\leq\inf_{z\in Y^{\nu}}g^{\nu}(\bar{x},z)+\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\dist\big(H^{\nu}(\bar{x},z),D\big)+\tau^{\nu}.

Thus,
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{u},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda}^{\nu})
is feasible in (P)
ν
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}<\infty
. Since
\inf_{x\in X,y\in Y}f^{\nu}(x,y)>-\infty
and the last two terms in the objective function of (P)
ν
are nonnegative at every feasible point,
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}>-\infty
.

For (b), we have
\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}<\infty
by following the arguments in (a). The objective function in (P)
ν
is lsc and level-bounded on the feasible set because
\sigma^{\nu}>0
,
\theta^{\nu}>0
, and (
4.2
) is bounded. By
[
44
, Theorem 4.9]
it only remains to show that the feasible set is closed. Since an intersection of closed sets is closed, this also holds straightforwardly; we recall that
u\mapsto\dist(u,D)
is continuous.

The assumptions in the proposition are mild. For example, if
g^{\nu}(x,\cdot)
is lsc and
Y^{\nu}
is compact, then (
4.1
) holds. A lower bound on
f^{\nu}
on
X\times Y
might also be readily available. Thus, a near-optimal solution of (P)
ν
can be expected via (a) and this is all what is needed in the earlier theorems. For general existence results in bilevel optimization, we refer to
[
8
]
.

While the existence of near-optimal solutions of (P)
ν
is reassuring, we are still faced with the nontrivial task of computing them. Naturally, much depends on any additional properties the components of the problem might have and a comprehensive treatment extends beyond the present paper. We provide a generic algorithm based on outer approximations that, in part, addresses the potentially large, even infinite, number of constraints in (
1.2
). We adopt the following notation in our description of (P)
ν
:

\displaystyle C=\big\{(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+q+2}~\big|~x\in X,y\in Y,\alpha\leq 0,\lambda\in[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}],H^{\nu}(x,y)+u\in D,(x,y)\in\dom f^{\nu}\big\}

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)=f^{\nu}(x,y)+\sigma^{\nu}\|u\|-\theta^{\nu}\alpha

\displaystyle\psi_{z}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)=g^{\nu}(x,y)+\alpha-g^{\nu}(x,z)-\lambda\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,z),D\big)-\tau^{\nu},~~~~z\in Y^{\nu}.

Thus, (P)
ν
amounts to minimizing
\psi_{0}(w)
subject
w\in C
and
\psi_{z}(w)\leq 0
for
z\in Y^{\nu}
. Since
\nu
is fixed in this section, we suppress the dependence on
\nu
in
\psi_{0},\psi_{z}
, and
C
.

Outer Approximation Algorithm for (P)
ν
.

Data.

(x^{0},y^{0},u^{0},\alpha^{0},\lambda^{0})\in C
,
Y^{\nu}_{0}=\emptyset
,
\{\varepsilon^{k},\delta^{k}\geq 0,k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Step 0.

Set
k=1
.

Step 1.

Set
Y^{\nu}_{k}=Y^{\nu}_{k-1}\cup\{z^{k}\}
, where
z^{k}\in\delta^{k}\mbox{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{z\in Y^{\nu}}g^{\nu}(x^{k-1},z)+\lambda^{k-1}\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x^{k-1},z),D\big)
.

Step 2.

Compute
(x^{k},y^{k},u^{k},\alpha^{k},\lambda^{k})\in\varepsilon^{k}\mbox{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{w\in C}\big\{\psi_{0}(w)~\big|~\psi_{z}(w)\leq 0,z\in Y_{k}^{\nu}\big\}
.

Step 3.

Replace
k
by
k+1
and go to Step 1.

The initialization of the algorithm is usually straightforward as any
(x,y)\in(X\times Y)\cap\dom f^{\nu}
can be paired with some
u
, which is a vector of slack-type variables, and an arbitrary
\alpha\leq 0
and
\lambda\in[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}]
to produce a point in
C
.
Step 1 identifies a constraint from (
1.2
) to be added to the collection of constraints indexed by
Y^{\nu}_{k-1}
. If
D=(-\infty,0]^{q}
and
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{1}
, then
\dist(H^{\nu}(x^{k-1},z),D)
equals

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\max\big\{0,h^{\nu}_{j}(x^{k-1},z)\big\},

(4.3)

where
h_{j}^{\nu}
is the
j
th component of
H^{\nu}
. Moreover, if
g^{\nu}(x^{k-1},\cdot)
and
h_{j}^{\nu}(x^{k-1},\cdot)
,
j=1,\dots,q
, are affine functions and
Y^{\nu}
is polyhedral, possibly restricted further to integer variables, then the resulting subproblem can be solved using (mixed-integer) linear programming.

Step 2 amounts to solving a master problem similar to (P)
ν
, but with only
k
constraints of the form (
1.2
). Again, we permit an optimality tolerance. A potential challenge is the variable
\lambda
, which is multiplied by
\dist(H^{\nu}(x,z),D)
and thus produces a nonlinearity. However, we know that
\psi_{z}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)\leq\psi_{z}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda^{\prime})
if
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}\geq 0
, which implies that one can fix
\lambda=\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}
in Step 2. (A reason for
not
fixing
\lambda
at its upper limit would be numerical instability issues, which might trigger substeps fixing
\lambda
at a lower level initially and then increase it cautiously.)

A more serious challenge is the term
-\dist(H^{\nu}(x,z),D)
on the left-hand side in the constraint
\psi_{z}(w)\leq 0
. In the example leading to (
4.3
), this produces a max-expression on the “wrong” side of the inequality. However, such terms can be linearized. For additional binary variables
b_{1},\dots,b_{q}
and additional continuous variables
v_{1},\dots,v_{q}
, we find that

\displaystyle\psi_{z}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)\leq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~

\displaystyle g^{\nu}(x,y)+\alpha-g^{\nu}(x,z)-\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{q}v_{j}-\tau^{\nu}\leq 0

\displaystyle\gamma_{j}^{\rm up}b_{j}\geq v_{j}\geq 0,~~-\gamma_{j}^{\rm low}(1-b_{j})+h_{j}^{\nu}(x,z)\geq v_{j}\geq h_{j}^{\nu}(x,z),~~b_{j}\in\{0,1\},~~~j=1,\dots,q

provided that
\gamma_{j}^{\rm low}\leq h_{j}^{\nu}(x,z)\leq\gamma_{j}^{\rm up}
for
j=1,\dots,q
. While there are nontrivial computational challenges associated with the resulting big-M formulation and the growing number of variables and constraints, linearization of this kind provides a concrete path forward to solving the master problem in Step 2 using mixed-integer programming.

We observe that
\psi_{0}(x^{k},y^{k},u^{k},\alpha^{k},\lambda^{k})-\varepsilon^{k}\leq\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}
for any
k
, which can be used to bound the suboptimality of any feasible point for (P)
ν
. Feasible points are readily available because a slight adjustment of
\alpha^{k}
usually leads to a point satisfying (
1.2
). Regardless, as we see next, the algorithm is guaranteed to achieve an optimal solution of (P)
ν
in the limit under mild assumptions.

4.2 Proposition

(outer approximation algorithm).
Suppose that
X,Y,D
are nonempty closed sets,
Y^{\nu}
is nonempty and compact,

g^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
and
H^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
are continuous,
f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is continuous relative to
\dom f^{\nu}
, a closed set, and for each
\bar{x}\in X
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
, there is
\rho\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
z\in Y^{\nu}
and
x\in X\cap\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\rho)
,

\displaystyle\big|g^{\nu}(x,z)-g^{\nu}(\bar{x},z)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon

(4.4)

\displaystyle\big\|H^{\nu}(x,z)-H^{\nu}(\bar{x},z)\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon.

(4.5)

If the outer approximation algorithm generates
(x^{k},y^{k},u^{k},\alpha^{k},\lambda^{k})
using vanishing
\varepsilon^{k}
and
\delta^{k}
, and

(x^{k},y^{k},u^{k},\alpha^{k},\lambda^{k})\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{u},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\lambda})

for some subsequence
N\subset\mathbb{N}
, then
(\hat{x},\hat{y},\hat{u},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\lambda})
is an optimal solution for
(P)
ν
and

\psi_{0}(x^{k},y^{k},u^{k},\alpha^{k},\lambda^{k})\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}.

Proof.
The conclusion follows from convergence statement 6.15 in
[
44
]
after verifying the conditions:

(i)

C
is closed.

(ii)

\psi_{0}
is continuous relative to
C
.

(iii)

\{\psi_{z},z\in Y^{\nu}\}
is locally bounded, i.e., for any
\bar{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+q+2}
, there are
\varepsilon,\rho\in(0,\infty)
such that
|\psi_{z}(w)|\leq\rho
for all
z\in Y^{\nu}
and
w\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{w},\varepsilon)
.

(iv)

\{\psi_{z},z\in Y^{\nu}\}
is equicontinuous relative to
C
, i.e., for any
\bar{w}\in C
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\rho>0
such that
|\psi_{z}(w)-\psi_{z}(\bar{w})|\leq\varepsilon
for all
z\in Y^{\nu}
and
w\in C\cap\mathbb{B}(\bar{w},\rho)
.

(v)

For any
w\in C
, there is a maximizer of
\psi_{z}(w)
over
z\in Y^{\nu}
.

Conditions (i) and (ii) hold trivially under the assumed conditions. (We note that
[
44
, statement 6.15]
requires
\psi_{0}
to be real-valued and continuous, but this is easily overcome by redefining
\psi_{0}
outside
\dom f^{\nu}
using a continuous extension because
\dom f^{\nu}
is closed.)

Condition (iii) holds because
(z,w)\mapsto\psi_{z}(w)
is continuous and
Y^{\nu}
is compact.

For condition (iv), let
\bar{w}=(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{u},\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda})\in C
and
\varepsilon>0
. For any
w=(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)\in C
, one has

\displaystyle\big|\psi_{z}(w)-\psi_{z}(\bar{w})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|g^{\nu}(x,y)-g^{\nu}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big|+|\alpha-\bar{\alpha}|+\big|g^{\nu}(x,z)-g^{\nu}(\bar{x},z)\big|

(4.6)

\displaystyle~~+\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\Big|\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,z),D\big)-\dist\big(H^{\nu}(\bar{x},z),D\big)\Big|+|\lambda-\bar{\lambda}|\dist\big(H^{\nu}(\bar{x},z),D\big).

Since
H^{\nu}
is continuous,
D
is nonempty, and
Y^{\nu}
is compact, one has that
\kappa

=

\sup_{z^{\prime}\in Y^{\nu}}\dist(H^{\nu}(\bar{x},z^{\prime}),D)
is finite. Moreover, by continuity of
g^{\nu}
and
H^{\nu}
and the continuity properties in (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), there exists
\rho\in(0,\min\{\varepsilon/5,\varepsilon/(5\kappa)]
such that for all
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},u^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{w},\rho)
,

\displaystyle\big|g^{\nu}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-g^{\nu}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big|\leq\varepsilon/5,~~~~~~\big|g^{\nu}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})-g^{\nu}(\bar{x},z^{\prime})\big|\leq\varepsilon/5~~~\forall z^{\prime}\in Y^{\nu}

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\Big|\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,z^{\prime}),D\big)-\dist\big(H^{\nu}(\bar{x},z^{\prime}),D\big)\Big|\leq\varepsilon/5~~~\forall z^{\prime}\in Y^{\nu}.

Since
|\alpha^{\prime}-\bar{\alpha}|\leq\varepsilon/5
and
\kappa|\lambda^{\prime}-\bar{\lambda}|\leq\varepsilon/5
when
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},u^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{w},\rho)
and (
4.6
) holds, we find that for
w=(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)\in C\cap\mathbb{B}(\bar{w},\rho)
and
z\in Y^{\nu}
one has
|\psi_{z}(w)-\psi_{z}(\bar{w})|\leq\varepsilon
.

For (v), maximizing
\psi_{z}(w)
over
z\in Y^{\nu}
is equivalent to minimizing
g^{\nu}(x,z)+\lambda\dist(H^{\nu}(x,z),D)
over
z\in Y^{\nu}
, where
w=(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)
. Since
D
is nonempty,
Y^{\nu}
is nonempty and compact, and
g^{\nu}
and
H^{\nu}
are continuous, it follows via
[
44
, Theorem 4.9]
that the minimum is attained.

The assumptions of the proposition are mild. The conditions (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) simply amount to a uniformity across
z\in Y^{\nu}
of the continuity properties for
g^{\nu}(\cdot,z)
and
H^{\nu}(\cdot,z)
. The proposition applies even for
Y^{\nu}=Y
but would then require that
Y
is compact.

5
Intermediate Results and Proofs

In this section, we use the notation: For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
, let

\mu(x,\lambda)=\inf_{y\in Y}g(x,y)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big);~~~\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda)=\inf_{y\in Y^{\nu}}g^{\nu}(x,y)+\lambda\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,y),D\big).

5.1 Lemma

(lower approximation of penalty-based value functions).
Suppose that Assumption
2.1
(a-d) holds. If
x^{\nu}\in X\to x
and
\lambda^{\nu}\in[0,\infty)\to\lambda
, then
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\leq\mu(x,\lambda)
.

Proof.
Let
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
. We consider two cases. (i) If
\mu(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}
, then there exists
y\in Y
such that

g(x,y)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)\leq\mu(x,\lambda)+\gamma.

(5.1)

Since
Y^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle s$}}\hskip 7.0ptY
, there exists a sequence
\{y^{\nu}\in Y^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
converging to
y
. The continuity of
g
relative to
X\times Y
and the vanishing errors in Assumption
2.1
(c) yield

\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\leq\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\big(g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})-g(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\big)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits g(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})=g(x,y).

(5.2)

Similarly, by Assumption
2.1
(d) and continuity of
\dist(H(\cdot,\cdot),D)
relative to
X\times Y
, one has

\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)\leq\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big).

(5.3)

Combining the inequalities in (
5.1
)-(
5.3
) while also incorporating
\lambda^{\nu}
and
\lambda
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\Big(g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+\lambda^{\nu}\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\Big(\lambda^{\nu}\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq g(x,y)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)\leq\mu(x,\lambda)+\gamma.

(ii) If
\mu(x,\lambda)=-\infty
, then there exists
y\in Y
such that
g(x,y)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)\leq-1/\gamma
. Again constructing
\{y^{\nu}\in Y^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
and leveraging (
5.2
)-(
5.3
), we obtain

\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\leq g(x,y)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)\leq-1/\gamma.

Since
\gamma
is arbitrary, the conclusion holds in both cases.

5.2 Lemma

(vanishing error for penalty-based value function).

For nonempty compact set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, nonempty closed set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{q}
, suppose that
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is fixed and
g(x,\cdot)
and
H(x,\cdot)
are continuous relative to
Y
. Then, the following hold:

(a)

If
\lambda^{\nu}\to\infty
, then
\mathop{\rm lim}\nolimits\mu(x,\lambda^{\nu})=\inf_{y\in Y}\{g(x,y)~|~H(x,y)\in D\}
.

(b)

If
\tau\in(0,\infty)
and
\inf_{y\in Y}\{g(x,y)~|~H(x,y)\in D\}<\infty
, then there exist
\bar{y}\in Y
and
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
such that
H(x,\bar{y})\in D
and
g(x,\bar{y})\leq\mu(x,\lambda)+\tau
.

Proof.
Let
\psi,\psi^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be given by

\psi(y)=\iota_{Y}(y)+g(x,y)+\iota_{D}\big(H(x,y)\big);~~~~\psi^{\nu}(y)=\iota_{Y}(y)+g(x,y)+\lambda^{\nu}\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big).

For part (a), we consider two cases. (i) Suppose that
H(x,y)\not\in D
for all
y\in Y
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that there exist
\gamma<\infty
and a subsequence
N^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(x,\lambda^{\nu})\leq\gamma
for all
\nu\in N^{\prime}
.
For each
\nu
, there exists
y^{\nu}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\psi^{\nu}(y)
because
Y
is compact and nonempty and
g(x,\cdot)
and
H(x,\cdot)
are continuous relative to
Y
; see, e.g.,
[
44
, Theorem 4.9]
. By the compactness of
Y
, there exist a point
\bar{y}\in Y
and a subsequence
N\subset N^{\prime}
such that
y^{\nu}\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt\bar{y}
. Since
H(x,\cdot)
is continuous relative to
Y
, this implies that
\dist(H(x,y^{\nu}),D)\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt\dist(H(x,\bar{y}),D)
. The set
D
is closed and therefore
\dist(H(x,\bar{y}),D)>0
and
\{\dist(H(x,y^{\nu}),D),\nu\in N\}
is bounded away from zero. Moreover,
g(x,\cdot)
is continuous relative to
Y
, which implies that
\{g(x,y^{\nu}),\nu\in N\}
is bounded from below. Combining these facts, we conclude that

\mu(x,\lambda^{\nu})=g(x,y^{\nu})+\lambda^{\nu}\dist(H(x,y^{\nu}),D)\,{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-13.0pt\hbox{\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle N$}}\hskip 7.0pt\infty
, which is a contradiction.

(ii) Suppose that
H(x,\bar{y})\in D
for some
\bar{y}\in Y
. Then,
\inf\psi<\infty
. Trivially, we have
\psi(y)>-\infty
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Since
Y
is compact,
\{\psi^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight. If
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
, then
\inf\psi^{\nu}\to\inf\psi
by

. Thus it only remains to verify such epi-convergence.

First, we verify the liminf-condition (
1.4
). Suppose that
y^{\nu}\to y
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
y^{\nu}\in Y
because otherwise
\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})=\infty
. Since
Y
is closed, this implies that
y\in Y
. If
H(x,y)\in D
, then it follows by continuity of

relative to
Y
that

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\geq\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits g(x,y^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\lambda^{\nu}\dist\big(H(x,y^{\nu}),D\big)\geq g(x,y)=\psi(y).

If

, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\dist(H(x,y),D)=\varepsilon
because
D
is closed. The continuity of

relative to

\dist(H(x,y^{\nu}),D)\geq\varepsilon/2
for all

\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\to\infty
and again

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\geq\psi(y)
.

Second, we verify the limsup-condition (
1.5
). Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
y\in Y
and
H(x,y)\in D
because otherwise
\psi(y)=\infty
. We construct
y^{\nu}=y
. Then,

\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\leq g(x,y)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\lambda^{\nu}\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)=g(x,y)=\psi(y).

We conclude that
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
.

For part (b), we observe that
\inf\psi>-\infty
because
Y
is compact,
D
is closed, and
g(x,\cdot)
and
H(x,\cdot)
are continuous relative to
Y
. Moreover,
\inf\psi<\infty
be assumption. Thus, there exists
\bar{y}\in Y
such that
H(x,\bar{y})\in D
and
g(x,\bar{y})\leq\inf\psi+\tau/2
. By part (a), there is
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
such that
\mu(x,\lambda)\geq\inf\psi-\tau/2
. Consequently,
g(x,\bar{y})\leq\inf\psi+\tau/2\leq\mu(x,\lambda)+\tau
as claimed.

Lemma

(a) is false if the compactness of

is removed. For a counterexample with a convex objective function and a convex feasible set, let

,

,

, and

. Then,
\mu(x,\lambda)=-\infty
for any

but
\inf_{y\in Y}\{g(x,y)~|~H(x,y)\in D\}=1
.

For the
remainder of the paper

ν

\varphi^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to(-\infty,\infty]
defined by

\displaystyle\varphi^{\nu}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\iota_{X}(x)+\iota_{Y}(y)+f^{\nu}(x,y)+\sigma^{\nu}\|u\|-\theta^{\nu}\alpha+\iota_{D}\big(H^{\nu}(x,y)+u\big)

\displaystyle~~~+\iota_{(-\infty,\tau^{\nu}]}\big(g^{\nu}(x,y)+\alpha-\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda)\big)+\iota_{(-\infty,0]}(\alpha)+\iota_{[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}]}(\lambda).

\displaystyle\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\iota_{X}(x)+\iota_{Y}(y)+f(x,y)+\iota_{D}\big(H(x,y)+u\big)

\displaystyle~~~+\iota_{(-\infty,\beta]}\big(g(x,y)+\alpha-\mu(x,\lambda)\big)+\iota_{\{0\}^{q}}(u)+\iota_{\{0\}}(\alpha)+\iota_{[0,\infty)}(\lambda).

5.3 Lemma

(liminf property).
Suppose that Assumption
2.1
(a-d) holds and the functions
f,f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
satisfy
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\geq f(x,y)
when
x^{\nu}\in X\to x
and
y^{\nu}\in Y\to y
.
If
\tau^{\nu}\in[0,\infty)\to\beta
and
\sigma^{\nu},\theta^{\nu}\to\infty
, then

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\geq\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)~~~\mbox{ when }~~(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\to(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda).

Proof.
Let
(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\to(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
x^{\nu}\in X
,
y^{\nu}\in Y
,
\alpha^{\nu}\leq 0
,
\lambda^{\nu}\in[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}]
for all
\nu
because other values of
(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})
would cause
\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=\infty
regardless of
u^{\nu}
, which is unproblematic in the inequality we seek to establish. After invoking closedness, we proceed under the assumption that
x\in X,y\in Y,\alpha\leq 0
, and
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
. We consider five cases:

(i) Suppose that
u\neq 0
so that
\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)=\infty
. Since
u^{\nu}\to u
and
\sigma^{\nu}\to\infty
, we find that
\sigma^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|\to\infty
. Thus,
\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\to\infty
because
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\geq f(x,y)>-\infty
.

(ii) Suppose that
\alpha<0
so that
\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)=\infty
. Since
\alpha^{\nu}\to\alpha
and
\theta^{\nu}\to\infty
, we find that
-\theta^{\nu}\alpha^{\nu}\to\infty
. Thus, again,
\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\to\infty
.

(iii) Suppose that
u=0
,
\alpha=0
,
H(x,y)\in D
, and
g(x,y)-\mu(x,\lambda)\leq\beta
. Then, one has

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\geq\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{X}(x^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{Y}(y^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})

\displaystyle~~~+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\sigma^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(-\theta^{\nu}\alpha^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{D}\big(H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+u^{\nu}\big)

\displaystyle~~~+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{(-\infty,\tau^{\nu}]}\big(g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+\alpha^{\nu}-\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\big)+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{(-\infty,0]}(\alpha^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}]}(\lambda^{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq f(x,y)=\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda).

(iv) Suppose that
u=0
,
\alpha=0
, and
H(x,y)\not\in D
. Then,
\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)=\infty
. Since
D
is closed, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\dist(H(x,y),D)=\varepsilon
. We recall that
u^{\nu}\to 0
,
\alpha^{\nu}\to 0
, and Assumption
2.1
(d) holds. Thus, there exists
\bar{\nu}
such that

\dist\big(H(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)\geq 3\varepsilon/4;~~\|u^{\nu}\|\leq\varepsilon/4;~~\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)\geq\dist\big(H(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)-\varepsilon/4~~~\forall\nu\geq\bar{\nu}.

Consequently, for
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
,

\displaystyle\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+u^{\nu},D\big)

\displaystyle\geq\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)-\|u^{\nu}\|

\displaystyle\geq\dist\big(H(x^{\nu},y^{\nu}),D\big)-\varepsilon/4-\varepsilon/4\geq 3\varepsilon/4-\varepsilon/4-\varepsilon/4=\varepsilon/4>0.

It follows that
\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=\infty
for all
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
.

(v) Suppose that
u=0
,
\alpha=0
, and there is
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
such that
g(x,y)-\mu(x,\lambda)=\beta+\varepsilon
. Then,
\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)=\infty
. By invoking Lemma
5.1
, Assumpton
2.1
(c), and the facts that
\alpha^{\nu}\to 0
and
\tau^{\nu}\to\beta
, there exists
\bar{\nu}
such that for all
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
:

\displaystyle\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\leq\mu(x,\lambda)+\varepsilon/6;~~~~g(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\geq g(x,y)-\varepsilon/6;~~~~\alpha^{\nu}\geq-\varepsilon/6

\displaystyle g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\geq g(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})-\varepsilon/6;~~~~\tau^{\nu}\leq\beta+\varepsilon/6.

Thus, for
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
,

\displaystyle g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+\alpha^{\nu}-\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq g(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})-\varepsilon/6-\varepsilon/6-\mu(x,\lambda)-\varepsilon/6

\displaystyle\geq g(x,y)-\varepsilon/6-\varepsilon/6-\varepsilon/6-\mu(x,\lambda)-\varepsilon/6

\displaystyle=\beta+\varepsilon-4\varepsilon/6\geq\tau^{\nu}+\varepsilon/6.

We conclude that
\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=\infty
for all
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
.

5.4 Lemma

(limsup property).

Suppose that Assumption
2.1
holds,
\beta\in[0,\infty)
,
\{\theta^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded away from zero, and
\theta^{\nu}|\tau^{\nu}-\beta|\to 0
. Then, for any
(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exists a sequence

(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\to(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)~\mbox{ such that }~\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\leq\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda).

Proof.
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
x\in X
,
y\in Y
,
u=0
,
\alpha=0
,
H(x,y)\in D
,
g(x,y)-\mu(x,\lambda)\leq\beta
, and
\lambda\geq 0
because otherwise
\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)=\infty
and the conclusion holds trivially with
(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})=(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)
.

We construct
x^{\nu}=x
,
y^{\nu}=y
,
\lambda^{\nu}=\min\{\bar{\lambda}^{\nu},\lambda\}
, and

\displaystyle u^{\nu}

\displaystyle\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{\bar{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\big\{\|\bar{u}\|~\big|~H^{\nu}(x,y)+\bar{u}\in D\big\}

\displaystyle\alpha^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\min\big\{0,g(x,y)-\mu(x,\lambda)-g^{\nu}(x,y)+\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})-\beta+\tau^{\nu}\big\}.

Since
D
is nonempty and closed,
u^{\nu}
is well defined. The construction implies that
H^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+u^{\nu}\in D
. The condition
g(x,y)-\mu(x,\lambda)\leq\beta
ensures that
\mu(x,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
\alpha^{\nu}
is well-defined but with value
-\infty
if
\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})=-\infty
. Trivially,
\alpha^{\nu}\leq 0
and
\lambda^{\nu}\in[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}]
.

Next, we verify that
\sigma^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|\to 0
. Invoking Assumption
2.1
(d), we see that

\|u^{\nu}\|=\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,y),D\big)\leq\dist\big(H(x,y),D\big)+\eta^{\nu}\leq\eta^{\nu}.

Thus,
\|u^{\nu}\|\to 0
and
\sigma^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|\to 0
follow via Assumption
2.1
(d) and
2.1
(f), respectively.

To establish
-\theta^{\nu}\alpha^{\nu}\to 0
, we invoke Assumption
2.1
(c) to obtain

\displaystyle-\alpha^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\max\big\{0,\mu(x,\lambda)-\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})+g^{\nu}(x,y)-g(x,y)+\beta-\tau^{\nu}\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\big\{0,\mu(x,\lambda)-\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})+\delta^{\nu}+|\beta-\tau^{\nu}|\big\}.

(5.4)

The remaining difference on the right-hand side in this inequality is bounded:

\mu(x,\lambda)-\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})\leq\inf_{z\in Y}\Big\{g(x,z)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,z),D\big)\Big\}-\inf_{z\in Y}\Big\{g^{\nu}(x,z)+\lambda^{\nu}\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,z),D\big)\Big\},

(5.5)

where we leverage the fact that
Y^{\nu}\subset Y
. By Assumption
2.1
(c,d),

g(x,z)+\lambda\dist\big(H(x,z),D\big)-g^{\nu}(x,z)-\lambda\dist\big(H^{\nu}(x,z),D\big)\leq\delta^{\nu}+\lambda\eta^{\nu}\leq\delta^{\nu}+\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\eta^{\nu}

regardless of
z\in Y
. We can use this fact in conjunction with (
5.5
) because
\lambda^{\nu}=\lambda
for sufficiently large
\nu
by construction and Assumption
2.1
(f). Thus, for sufficiently large
\nu
,
\mu(x,\lambda)-\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})\leq\delta^{\nu}+\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\eta^{\nu}
. We combine this fact with (
5.4
) to establish that
-\alpha^{\nu}\leq 2\delta^{\nu}+\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}\eta^{\nu}+|\beta-\tau^{\nu}|
for sufficiently large
\nu
. Since
-\alpha^{\nu}\geq 0
by construction, it follows that
-\theta^{\nu}\alpha^{\nu}\to 0
by Assumption
2.1
(f) and the assumed rate by which
\tau^{\nu}\to\beta
.

Since
\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})
is finite for sufficiently large
\nu
, the definition of
\alpha^{\nu}
implies that

\displaystyle g^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})+\alpha^{\nu}-\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq g^{\nu}(x,y)+g(x,y)-\mu(x,\lambda)-g^{\nu}(x,y)+\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})-\beta+\tau^{\nu}-\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq\beta-\beta+\tau^{\nu}=\tau^{\nu}

for such
\nu
. Collecting these facts, we confirm that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\varphi^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\leq\iota_{X}(x)+\iota_{Y}(y)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}(x,y)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\sigma^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits(-\theta^{\nu}\alpha^{\nu})

\displaystyle+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\iota_{D}\big(H^{\nu}(x,y)+u^{\nu}\big)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\iota_{(-\infty,\tau^{\nu}]}\big(g^{\nu}(x,y)+\alpha^{\nu}-\mu^{\nu}(x,\lambda^{\nu})\big)

\displaystyle+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\iota_{(-\infty,0]}(\alpha^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\iota_{[0,\bar{\lambda}^{\nu}]}(\lambda^{\nu})\leq f(x,y)=\varphi_{\beta}(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda),

where Assumption
2.1
(e) also enters. It is apparent that
(x^{\nu},y^{\nu},u^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu})\to(x,y,u,\alpha,\lambda)
;
\alpha^{\nu}\to 0
because
\theta^{\nu}\alpha^{\nu}\to 0
and
\{\theta^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded away from zero. Thus, the conclusion follows.

The liminf-condition of Assumption
2.1
(e) is actually superfluous in the lemma and can be omitted.

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}+\varepsilon^{\nu}+\theta^{\nu}\Big(\mu^{\nu}\big(x^{\nu},\max\{\lambda,\lambda^{\nu}\}\big)-\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda)\Big)=\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}+\omega^{\nu}.

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{\nu}(x^{\nu},\lambda)+\tau^{\nu}.

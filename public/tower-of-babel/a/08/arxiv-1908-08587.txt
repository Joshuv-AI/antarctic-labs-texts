Abstract
. The present paper establishes the first result on the absolute continuity of elliptic measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure for a divergence form elliptic operator with non-smooth coefficients that have a BMO \BMO anti-symmetric part. In particular, the coefficients are not necessarily bounded. We prove that the Dirichlet problem for elliptic equation div ( A ∇ u ) = 0 {\rm div}(A\nabla u)=0 in the upper half-space ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ + n + 1 (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+} is uniquely solvable when n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and the boundary data is in L p ​ ( ℝ n , d ​ x ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx) for some p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . This result is equivalent to saying that the elliptic measure associated to L L belongs to the A ∞ A_{\infty} class with respect to the Lebesgue measure d ​ x dx , a quantitative version of absolute continuity.



1.
Introduction and statement of main results

Motivated by questions about the behavior of solutions of elliptic and parabolic equations with low regularity drift terms, Seregin, Silvestre, Šverák, and Zlatoš (
[
22
]
) investigated equations such as
-\Delta u+\mathbf{c}\cdot\nabla u=0
and
\partial_{t}u+\mathbf{c}\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u=0
, where
c
is a divergence-free vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. They discovered that the divergence-free condition can be utilized to relax the regularity assumptions on
\mathbf{c}
under which one can obtain the Harnack inequality and other regularity results for solutions. It turns out that the interior regularity theory of De Giorgi, Nash, and Moser can be carried over to elliptic equations with
\mathbf{c}\in\BMO^{-1}
, and to parabolic equations with
\mathbf{c}\in L^{\infty}(\BMO^{-1})
. Generalizing to elliptic or parabolic equations in divergence form, this condition is equivalent to assuming that the coefficient matrix
A
of the operator
L=-\divg(A\nabla)
can be decomposed into an
L^{\infty}
elliptic symmetric part and an unbounded anti-symmetric part in a certain function space. To be precise, the anti-symmetric part should belong to the John-Nirenberg space
\BMO
(bounded mean oscillation) in the elliptic case, and to
L^{\infty}(\BMO)
in the parabolic case.
The space
\BMO
plays a key role in two ways. First, this space has the right scaling properties which arise naturally in the iterative arguments of De Giorgi-Nash-Moser. Secondly, the
\BMO
condition on the anti-symmetric part of the matrix enables one to properly define weak solutions. This latter fact follows essentially from the div-curl lemma appearing in the theory of compensated compactness (
[
6
]
), and the details can be found in
[
22
]
or
[
18
]
.

The interior regularity results of Seregin, Silvestre, Šverák, and Zlatoš lead naturally to questions about
boundary regularity. In
[
18
]
, the second and the fourth authors studied the boundary behavior of weak solutions.
It turns out that many results for elliptic operators with bounded, measurable coefficients can be extended to this setting. For example, they proved the boundary Hölder regularity of the solution, established the existence of the elliptic measure
\omega
associated to these operators, and offered multiple characterizations of the mutual absolute continuity of the elliptic measure and the surface measure in Lipschitz domains. This work laid out the background necessary to
launch the investigation into boundary value problems for elliptic operators having a
\BMO
anti-symmetric part.

In the present paper we establish the first result pertaining to absolute continuity of the elliptic measure for operators with
\BMO
anti-symmetric part and well-posedness of the Dirichlet boundary value problem with
L^{p}
data.

In order to frame our results in the context of the currently existing elliptic theory, let us review some historical milestones. In the middle of the 20th century the theory of boundary value problems mainly concentrated on the case when coefficients of the underlying equations and domains exhibit some amount of smoothness. The past 30-40 years have brought great developments in the study of elliptic measure and boundary value problems for operators with non-smooth bounded measurable coefficients. The background theory of weak solutions, Green function estimates, maximum principle, and similar results were extended to all divergence form elliptic operators with bounded measurable coefficients. It turned out, however, that the question of absolute continuity of the resulting elliptic measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the boundary, or, equivalently, of well-posedness of the Dirichlet boundary value problem with boundary data in
L^{p}
, is much more delicate. First of all, examples have been found that show such results can not be expected for all elliptic operators and some regularity of the coefficients in the transversal direction to the boundary is, in fact, necessary
[
5
]
,
[
20
]
. In light of these examples, the initial efforts concentrated on the study of operators whose coefficients are constant in the transverse direction to the boundary. Later results have extended the theory to the optimal regularity of the coefficients, expressed in terms of a
Carleson measure condition
. In this survey, and in this paper, we shall concentrate on the fundamental case where the domain is the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\right\}
and the coefficients of the operator are independent of the transverse direction, that is,
t
-independent. The first breakthrough in this direction was the 1981 paper of Jerison and Kenig
[
14
]
which established well-posedness of the Dirichlet problem and the absolute continuity of the elliptic measure for operators with symmetric bounded measurable
t
-independent coefficients on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and, by a change of variables, above a graph of a Lipschitz function. A seemingly innocent assumption of symmetry turned out to be critical and it took 20 years to extend these results to non-symmetric operators in dimension 2
[
16
]
and more than 30 years to non-symmetric operators in any dimension
[
10
]
. The 1981 work of Jerison and Kenig relied on the beautiful and powerful Rellich identity which roughly speaking says that the
L^{2}
norms of the normal and tangential derivatives of solutions on the boundary are comparable. It is proved by an integration by parts argument invoking the symmetry of the coefficients. However, not only the method of the proof of the Rellich identity, but the
L^{2}
equivalence of the norms of the normal and tangential trace of the solution itself fails when the coefficients are not necessarily symmetric. This has been demonstrated in
[
16
]
, where the authors established extremely useful characterizations of solvability of the Dirichlet problem in
L^{p}
in terms of the square function/non-tangential maximal function estimates (in any dimension), a method that made possible many later developments including the present paper, and resolved the question of absolute continuity of elliptic measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure for
t
-independent non-symmetric operators in dimension 2. Unfortunately, many ingredients in the argument in
[
16
]
rely heavily on the space being 2 dimensional. For example, the 2-d case relies on a change of variable argument that does not carry forward to higher dimensions. Only 15 years later these results have been finally extended to multidimensional setting. In
[
10
]
the authors established the square function/non-tangential maximal function estimates for solutions to the
t
-independent, non necessarily symmetric, operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
for all
n
, and as a result, absolute continuity of the elliptic measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure and well-posedness of the Dirichlet boundary value problem in
L^{p}
. The method involved a new pull-back/push-forward sequence based on the Hodge decomposition of the coefficients, the celebrated solution to the Kato problem
[
2
]
, the square function/non-tangential maximal function estimates for the heat semigroup, and many other elements. The method has later been streamlined in
[
1
]
to avoid an explicit pull-back/push-forward on Lipschitz domain – an important development in our context.

As we mentioned above, all of these results as well as many elements of the surrounding elliptic theory have been restricted to the context of
bounded
measurable coefficients. The present paper pioneers the consideration of the
\BMO
anti-symmetric part, an optimal structural assumption on the coefficients. The lack of boundedness invalidates many of the arguments that we have described above. We shall discuss all the new difficulties and some critical junctures of our proof in Section
2
, after the statement of Theorem
2.2
. These new difficulties include a new Hodge decomposition beyond
L^{2}
, and new estimates for the Riesz transforms, square functions and non-tangential maximal functions associated to the heat semigroup. Changes of variables and other techniques that preserved the boundedness properties of coefficients are lost in the presence of
\BMO
coefficients. There are many other issues which require a more technical discussion and we refer an interested reader to Section
2
.

We now rigorously state our results. Let
A=A(x)
be an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix of real, t-independent coefficients such that

(1)

The symmetric part
A^{s}=\frac{1}{2}(A+A^{\intercal})=\left(A_{ij}^{s}(x)\right)
is
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and satisfies the ellipticity condition

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\left|\xi\right|^{2}\leq\langle A^{s}(x)\xi,\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n+1}A^{s}_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\left\|A^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1},

for some
0<\lambda_{0}<1
.

(2)

The anti-symmetric part
A^{a}=\frac{1}{2}(A-A^{\intercal})=(A^{a}_{ij}(x))
is in the space
\BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with

\left\|A^{a}_{ij}\right\|_{\BMO}:=\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{Q}\left|A^{a}_{ij}-(A^{a}_{ij})_{Q}\right|dx\leq\Lambda_{0}

(1.2)

for some
\Lambda_{0}>0
. Here
(f)_{Q}
denotes the average
\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}f(x)dx
.

We define in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
a second order divergence form operator

L=-\divg_{x,t}(A(x)\nabla_{x,t}),

(1.3)

which is interpreted in the sense of maximal accretive operators via sesquiliniear form.
We say that
u
is a weak solution to the equation
Lu=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
if
u\in W_{\loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
and

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=0

(1.4)

for all
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
.

We consider the
L^{p}
Dirichlet problem
(D)_{p}
for the equation
\divg(A\nabla u)=0
in the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
when
n\geq 2
. We shall denote by
\mu
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Sometimes we simply denote it by
dx
, and the meaning should be clear from context. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, we say the Dirichlet problem for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)

f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
, there is a solution
u\in W_{\loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
such that

(D)_{p}\quad\begin{cases}Lu=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},\\
u\to f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)\text{ non-tangentially }\mu\text{-a.e. on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
Nu\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu).\end{cases}

Here,
N(u)
denotes the non-tangential maximal function of
u

N(u)(x):=\sup_{(y,t):\left|x-y\right|<t}\left|u(y,t)\right|,

(1.5)

and
u
converges to

\lim_{(y,t)\to(x,0),(y,t)\in\Gamma(x)}u(y,t)=f(x),

where
\Gamma(x)=\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}:\left|y-x\right|<t\right\}
.

The main result of this paper is that the
L^{p}
Dirichlet problem for
L
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
is uniquely solvable for some

sufficiently large:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be a matrix of real, t-independent coefficients satisfying (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Then for some
p\in(1,\infty)
, for each
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
, there exists a unique
u
that solves
(D)_{p}
for
L=-\divg(A\nabla)
in the upper half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
when
n\geq 2
.

This result is equivalent to quantitative absolute continuity of elliptic measure with respect to the Lebesgue measure, the
A_{\infty}
property - see the next Section.

For the uniqueness part of the statement, we actually prove the following Fatou-type result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
be an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix of real coefficients. Assume that the symmetric part of
A
is bounded and elliptic, and that the anti-symmetric part is in the space
\BMO(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
. Assume that
(D)_{p}
is solvable for
L=-\divg(A\nabla)
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
. Suppose that
u
satisfies

\begin{cases}Lu=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},\\
Nu\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu).\end{cases}

Then, the non-tangential limit of

exists a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(and is denoted by
u|_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}^{N.T.}
),
u|_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}^{N.T.}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
, and

u(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u|_{\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}^{N.T.}(y)k(X,y)d\mu(y),

where
k(X,y)
is defined in (
2.2
).

One can see that this result is stronger than uniqueness. Notice that in this theorem, we do not assume that
A
is

-independent. Moreover, for

, in contrast to a solution to

, we do not assume a priori that it converges non-tangentially.

2.
An overview of the proof of Theorem
1.1

As mentioned in the introduction, it is shown in
[
15
]
that some Carleson measure estimate implies some quantitative mutual absolute continuity, namely, the
A_{\infty}
condition, between the elliptic measure associated to an elliptic operator with real,
L^{\infty}
coefficients and the Lebesgue measure. In
[
18
]
, it is verified that this result also holds for elliptic operators having a
\BMO
anti-symmetric part. To understand the precise statement and its connection to Theorem
1.1
, we first need some notations and definitions.

For a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote its Lebesgue measure
\mu(E)
by
\left|E\right|
.
For any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x_{Q}
and
l(Q)
be the center and side length of
Q
, respectively. Let
X_{Q}:=(x_{Q},l(Q))
denote the corkscrew point in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
relative to
Q
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, we use
T(x,r):=\left\{Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}:\left|Y-(x,0)\right|<r\right\}
to denote half balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, and
\Delta(x,r):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|y-x\right|<r\right\}
to denote balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We shall simply write
T_{R}
and
\Delta_{R}
for
T(0,R)
and
\Delta(0,R)
, respectively. We use
C_{0}(\Omega)
to denote the set of continuous functions with compact support on
\Omega
.
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
is defined to be the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

In a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, for each
X\in\Omega
, the elliptic measure
\omega^{X}
is constructed in
[
18
]
to be the measure on
\partial\Omega
, such that
u(X)=\int_{\partial\Omega}hd\omega^{X}
solves the Dirichlet problem for continuous boundary data
h\in C(\partial\Omega)
in the sense that
\divg(A\nabla u)=0
in
\Omega
, with
u\in W_{loc}^{1,2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
, and
u=h
on
\partial\Omega
.

The elliptic measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be defined as follows. Let
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\supp f\subset\Delta_{R_{0}}
for some
R_{0}>0
. We define an extension of
f
(still denoted by
f
) which is equal to
0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\setminus T_{R_{0}}
. Then for all
R\geq R_{0}
,
f^{\pm}\in C(\partial T_{R})
, where
f^{\pm}:=\max\left\{\pm f,0\right\}
. For each
X\in T_{R}
, let
\omega_{R}^{X}
be the elliptic measure on
\partial T_{R}
. Then
u_{R}^{\pm}(X):=\int_{\partial T_{R}}f^{\pm}d\omega_{R}^{X}
solves the Dirichlet problem in
T_{R}
with boundary data
f^{\pm}
for all
R\geq R_{0}
. For any
R_{0}\leq R_{1}\leq R_{2}
and
X\in T_{R_{1}}
, the maximum principle (
[
18
]
Lemma 4.7) implies that
u_{R_{1}}^{\pm}(X)\leq u_{R_{2}}^{\pm}(X)\leq\left\|f^{\pm}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Therefore, we can define
u
as the monotone limit

u(X):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\left(u_{R}^{+}(X)-u_{R}^{-}(X)\right)\qquad\forall\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

And we have

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+})}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(2.1)

The mapping
f\mapsto u(X)
is a positive bounded linear functional on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and thus can be extended to a positive bounded linear functional on the set of all continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that converge to
0
uniformly at infinity. The Riesz Representation Theorem implies that there exists a regular Borel measure
\omega^{X}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
u(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}fd\omega^{X}
. This
\omega^{X}
is defined to be the elliptic measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One can show, by Hölder continuity of solutions and Caccioppoli’s inequality, that
u\in W_{loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+})
and solves the Dirichlet problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
with boundary data
f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For any
X
,
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, the Harnack principle implies that
\omega^{X}
and
\omega^{X_{0}}
are mutually absolute continuous. Define the kernel function
K(X_{0},X,y)
to be the Radon-Nikodym derivative
K(X_{0},X,y):=\frac{d\omega^{X}}{d\omega^{X_{0}}}(y)
. And define

k(X,y):=\frac{d\omega^{X}}{d\mu}(y),\qquad\text{for }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.2)

Note that

k(X,y)=K(X_{0},X,y)k(X_{0},y)\qquad\text{for any }X,X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Definition 2.1

(
A_{\infty}
)
.

For any cube
Q_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

(or

.

We note that in the sequel, we shall actually establish

and equals to 1 in

, where

is defined in (

L_{0}
. This observation enables us to work with the equation
L_{0}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, for which we can control the BMO coefficients by the John-Nirenberg inequality.

When
\divg_{x}\mathbf{c}\neq 0
, the situation is more complicated, even when coefficients are in
L^{\infty}
. We define an
n
-dimensional divergence form operator
L_{||}:=\divg A_{||}\nabla
, and its adjoint
L^{*}_{||}:=-\divg A^{*}_{||}\nabla
. We highlight three ingredients in the proof of the

condition for elliptic measure associated to operators with

,

-independent coefficients in
[
10
]
:

(1)

An adapted Hodge decomposition of
\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{b}
.

(2)

L^{p}
estimates for square functions involving the “ellipticized” heat semigroup
\mathcal{P}_{t}:=e^{-t^{2}L_{||}}
associated to
L_{||}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{t}:=e^{-t^{2}L_{||}^{*}}
. Some of these estimates reply heavily on the solution to the Kato problem.

(3)

L^{p}
estimates for the non-tangential maximal function involving
\mathcal{P}_{t}
and
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{*}
, which enables one to construct a set
F
with desired properties.

None of these ingredients comes for free when we move to the elliptic operators having a BMO anti-symmetric part. But fortunately, in a recent paper (
[
13
]
), we were able to obtain the desired
L^{p}
estimates for square functions involving
\mathcal{P}_{t}
and
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{*}
. The arguments for the
L^{p}
estimates rely on the
L^{p}
estimate for the square root operator
\sqrt{L}
, which is also derived in
[
13
]
. We note here that in
[
7
]
, the Kato problem, or the
L^{2}
estimate for
\sqrt{L}
, was solved for elliptic operators having a BMO anti-symmetric part. Previously, the Kato conjecture was proved for operators having the Gaussian property (
[
11
]
) and for elliptic operators in divergence form with complex, bounded coefficients (
[
2
]
).

In Section
3.3
, we deal with the Hodge decomposition. We point out that we need a
W^{1,2+\epsilon}
Hodge decomposition because the BMO coefficients require higher integrability, while for
L^{\infty}
coefficients, a

Hodge decomposition suffices (see
[
12
]
). The
L^{p}
estimates for the non-tangential maximal function involving
\mathcal{P}_{t}
and
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{*}
are presented in Section

.

2.1.
Further reductions of the statement

Recall that our goal is to derive the Carleson measure estimate (
2.6
).
Note that this formulation allows us to assume that
A
is smooth as long as the bounds do not depend on the regularity of the coefficients.

It turns out that we do not need to show (
2.6
) holds for integral over all of the cube
Q
, but only on a subset
F
of
Q
that has a big portion of the measure of
Q
. To be precise, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to
L
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
. Assume that there is a uniform constant
c
, and, for each cube,
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
there is a Borel set
F\subset Q
, with
\left|F\right|\geq c\left|Q\right|
, such that

\int_{0}^{l(Q)}\int_{F}\left|\partial_{t}u(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dx\lesssim\left|Q\right|,

(2.7)

with the implicit constant depending on
c
,
n
,
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
, the ellipticity constants and the BMO semi-norm only, in particular, independent of
Q
and
F
.

Then
u
satisfies the Carleson measure estimate (
2.6
).

The proof of Lemma
2.3
requires two steps of reduction. First, one can show by integration by parts and the Caccioppoli inequality on Whitney cubes that

\int_{0}^{l(Q)}\int_{Q}\left|\nabla u(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dxdt\lesssim\int_{0}^{2l(Q)}\int_{2Q}\left|\partial_{t}u(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dxdt+\left|Q\right|.

(2.8)

The details can be found in
[
10
]
.

Secondly, since the coefficients are independent of
t
,
\partial_{t}u
is also a weak solution of
L
(see Appendix
A
, Remark
A.1
), and thus
\partial_{t}u
satisfies Harnack Principle and interior Hölder estimates (see
[
18
]
). This allows us to apply a well-known result for Carleson measures (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
Lemma 2.14), to deduce from (
2.7
) an apparently stronger bound

\int_{0}^{l(Q)}\int_{Q}\left|\partial_{t}u(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dx\lesssim\left|Q\right|.

Combining this with (
2.8
), Lemma
2.3
follows. This lemma gives us the freedom to choose the set
F
.

The construction of the set
F
is presented in Section
4.1
. Basically, we will construct
F
such that on the set
F
, the non-tangential maximal function involving
\mathcal{P}_{t}=e^{-t^{2}L_{||}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{t}=e^{-t^{2}L^{*}_{||}}
, as well as some other maximal functions are small (see (
4.2
)). We will exploit this property of the set
F
in the proof of the Carleson measure estimate. Namely, as long as a term can be bounded by maximal functions showing up in the definition of
F
, then there is hope to control that term with desired bounds.

It turns out that to prove the Carleson measure estimate (
2.7
), it suffices to prove the following main lemma (see Section
5
).

Lemma 2.4

(Main Lemma)
.

Let
\sigma
,
\eta\in(0,1)
. Then there exists some finite constant
c=c(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)>0
, and some finite constant
\tilde{c}=\tilde{c}(\sigma,\eta,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n)>0
, such that

J_{\eta,\epsilon}\leq(\sigma+c\eta)J_{\eta,\epsilon}+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

Here,

J_{\eta,\epsilon}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt

where
u
is a bounded weak solution to
Lu=0
(and thus also a weak solution to
L_{0}u=0
) in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
, and
\Psi=\Psi_{\eta,\epsilon}
is a cut-off function defined in Section
4.3
.

The main lemma is proved in Section
5
. In the proof, a typical way to deal with the BMO coefficients is to use the anti-symmetry, Hölder’s inequality, and John-Nirenberg’s inequality. This method inevitably increases the exponent of the integrand, and thus requires some
L^{2+\epsilon}
estimates. Besides the
W^{1,2+\epsilon}
Hodge decomposition we mentioned earlier, it is crucial to have an
L^{p}
estimate for the cut-off function
\Psi
(see Lemma
4.5
), and
L^{p}
estimates for the non-tangential maximal functions and square functions that involve semigroups, for
p>2
.

3.2.

Definition 3.1
.

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left\|\sup_{t>0}\left|h_{t}*f\right|\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,

, and

\left\|\partial_{j}u\partial_{i}v-\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},

where the implicit constant depends only on
p
and dimension.

We refer to
[

]
and

]

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

. Let

,

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{t}(x)}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{\frac{t+s}{2}}(x)}\left|\xi\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{\theta}}{(s-t)^{2}}\int_{Q_{s}(x)}\left|\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}+C\theta\int_{Q_{s}(x)}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}+Cs^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\theta}}{(s-t)^{2}}\int_{Q_{\frac{3}{2}R}(x)}\left|\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{s}(x)}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}+CR^{n}.

Then (
3.7
) follows from Lemma
3.2
.

The interior Caccioppoli (
3.6
) can be shown in the same manner if one chooses
\left(\widetilde{\varphi}-(\widetilde{\varphi})_{Q_{\frac{3}{2}R}(x)}\right)\xi^{2}
as a test function in the beginning.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Note that one can replace
(\mathbf{c}^{a})_{2Q}
and
(\mathbf{b}^{a})_{2Q}
in the right-hand side of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively, by any constant vector
\mathbf{C}
without changing the result. This follows from the simple fact that
\int_{5Q}\mathbf{C}\cdot\nabla v=0
for any test function

.

Moser-type interior estimates for the weak solution to the homogeneous equation
-\divg_{x}A_{||}\nabla_{x}u=0
have been shown in
[
18
]
, or
[
22
]
for the parabolic equations. We show that similar estimates hold for weak solutions to the nonhomogeneous equations.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\varphi
and
\widetilde{\varphi}
be as in Proposition
3.4
. Let
B_{2R}=B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset 5Q
. Then for any
p>1
,

\sup_{B_{R}}\left|\widetilde{\varphi}-c_{0}\right|\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{2R}}\left|\widetilde{\varphi}-c_{0}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}+CR(\left\|\mathbf{b}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\mathbf{b}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO}),

(3.10)

where
c_{0}
is any constant, and
C=C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},p)
. Moreover, a similar estimate holds for
\varphi
:

\sup_{B_{R}}\left|\varphi-c_{0}\right|\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{2R}}\left|\varphi-c_{0}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}+CR(\left\|\mathbf{c}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\mathbf{c}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO}).

(3.11)

Proof.

Fix any
p>1
and
\frac{1}{2}<k_{0}<\frac{p}{2}
. Let
\frac{1}{2}<k_{1}<\min\{1,k_{0}\}
and
k\geq k_{0}
. Let
\alpha=2
when
n\geq 3
, and let
\alpha\in(1,2)
when
n=2
. Choose
q\in(2,\frac{n\alpha}{n-\alpha})
. Set
s_{0}=\frac{2q}{q+2}
. Note that
1<s_{0}<\frac{n}{n-2}
when
n\geq 3
and
1<s_{0}<\alpha
when
n=2
.

Define as in
[
18
]
Lemma 3.4, for any
\delta>0
,
N>>1
and
\beta\geq k_{0}
,

H_{\delta,N}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}t^{\beta},&\quad t\in[\delta,N],\\
N^{\beta}+\frac{\beta}{k_{1}}N^{\beta-k_{1}}(t^{k_{1}}-N^{k_{1}}),&\quad t>N.\end{array}\right.

Then

H^{\prime}_{\delta,N}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\beta t^{\beta-1},&\quad t\in(\delta,N),\\
\beta N^{\beta-k_{1}}t^{k_{1}-1},&\quad t>N.\end{array}\right.

Define, furthermore,

G_{\delta,N}(w)=\int_{\delta}^{w}|H^{\prime}_{\delta,N}(t)|^{2}dt,\ w\geq\delta.

Then for
w\geq\delta
,

H(w)\leq w^{\beta},

(3.12)

wH^{\prime}(w)\leq\beta w^{\beta},

(3.13)

and

G(w)\leq\frac{1}{2k_{1}-1}wG^{\prime}(w).

(3.14)

Here and in the sequel we omit the subscripts in
G_{\delta,N}
and
H_{\delta,N}
.

Let
\delta=R(\left\|\mathbf{b}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\mathbf{b}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO})
, and define
\Psi=\left|\widetilde{\varphi}-c_{0}\right|+\delta
, where
c_{0}
is an arbitrary constant. Then
\Psi
is a subsolution to the equation
\divg_{x}(A_{||}\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\varphi})=\divg_{x}(\mathbf{b}\mathbbm{1}_{5Q}-(\mathbf{b}^{a})_{2Q})
. Also, since
\Psi\geq\delta
, one can define
H(\Psi)
,
G(\Psi)
etc.

For any
R\leq r^{\prime}<r\leq 2R
, let
\eta\in C_{0}^{2}(B_{r})
with
\eta=1
in
B_{r^{\prime}}
and
\left|\nabla\eta\right|\lesssim(r-r^{\prime})^{-1}
. Choose
v=G(\Psi)\eta^{2}>0
as a test function. Then since
\Psi
is a subsolution, one has

\int_{B_{r}}A_{||}\nabla\Psi\cdot\nabla v\leq\int_{B_{r}}\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v.

(3.15)

For the left-hand side of (
3.15
), we have (see the proof of Lemma 3.4 of
[
18
]
)

\int_{B_{r}}A_{||}^{s}\nabla\Psi\cdot\nabla v\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\int_{B_{r}}\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}-\frac{C(n,\lambda_{0},k_{0})}{(2k_{0}-1)^{2}}\frac{\beta^{2}r^{n}}{(r-r^{\prime})^{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{\beta q}\right)^{2/q},

and

\int_{B_{r}}A_{||}^{a}\nabla\Psi\cdot\nabla v\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{8}\int_{B_{r}}\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}\\
+\frac{C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},q,k_{0})}{(2k_{0}-1)^{2}}\frac{\beta^{2}r^{n}}{(r-r^{\prime})^{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{\beta q}\right)^{2/q}.

The right-hand side of (
3.15
) equals

\int_{B_{r}}\mathbf{b}^{s}\cdot\nabla\left(G(\Psi)\eta^{2}\right)+\int_{B_{r}}\mathbf{b}^{a}\cdot\nabla\left(G(\Psi)\eta^{2}\right)\\
=\int_{B_{r}}\mathbf{b}^{s}\cdot\nabla H(\Psi)\left|H^{\prime}(\Psi)\right|\eta^{2}+2\int_{B_{r}}\mathbf{b}^{s}\cdot\nabla\eta G(\Psi)\eta\\
+\int_{B_{r}}\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}-(\mathbf{b}^{a})_{B_{r}}\right)\cdot\nabla H(\Psi)\left|H^{\prime}(\Psi)\right|\eta^{2}+2\int_{B_{r}}\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}-(\mathbf{b}^{a})_{B_{r}}\right)\cdot\nabla\eta G(\Psi)\eta\\
=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

Using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, (
3.13
), as well as Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{8}\int\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}+C(n,\lambda_{0})\left\|\mathbf{b}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\beta^{2}\int\Psi^{2\beta-2}\eta^{2}.

Recall, in addition, that
\Psi\geq\delta=R(\left\|\mathbf{b}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\mathbf{b}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO})
and
2<q<\frac{2n}{n-2}
. Then
\left|I_{1}\right|
is bounded by

\frac{\lambda_{0}}{8}\int\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}+C(n,\lambda_{0})\beta^{2}R^{-2}\int\Psi^{2\beta}\eta^{2}\\
\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{8}\int\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}+C(n,\lambda_{0})\beta^{2}R^{-2}r^{n}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta}\right)^{2/q}.

(3.16)

For
I_{2}
, we use (
3.14
) and obtain

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|\mathbf{b}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{r-r^{\prime}}\frac{\beta^{2}}{2k_{1}-1}\int\Psi^{2\beta-1}\left|\eta\right|\leq\frac{C(n,k_{0})\beta^{2}}{(r-r^{\prime})(2k_{0}-1)R}\int_{B_{r}}\Psi^{2\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n,k_{0})\beta^{2}r^{n}}{(r-r^{\prime})(2k_{0}-1)R}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta}\right)^{2/q}.

(3.17)

Turning to
I_{3}
, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|I_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{r}}\left|\mathbf{b}^{a}-(\mathbf{b}^{a})_{B_{r}}\right|^{s_{0}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{0}^{\prime}}}\left(\int\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\left|H^{\prime}(\Psi)\right|^{q}\eta^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,q)\left\|\mathbf{b}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO}r^{\frac{n}{s_{0}^{\prime}}}\left(\int\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}\right)^{1/2}\beta\left(\int\Psi^{q\beta-q}\eta^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{8}\int\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\eta^{2}+\frac{C(n,\lambda_{0},q)\beta^{2}r^{n}}{R^{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta}\right)^{2/q},

(3.18)

where
s_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{s_{0}}{s_{0}-1}
.
Finally, for
I_{4}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|I_{4}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B_{r}}\left|\mathbf{b}^{a}-(\mathbf{b}^{a})_{B_{r}}\right|^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left(\int\left|G(\Psi)\right|^{q/2}\left|\nabla\eta\right|^{q/2}\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,q)\left\|\mathbf{b}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO}r^{\frac{(q-2)n}{q}}\frac{1}{2k_{1}-1}\frac{\beta^{2}}{r-r^{\prime}}\left(\int_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta-\frac{q}{2}}\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,q,k_{0})\frac{\beta^{2}r^{n}}{(2k_{0}-1)(r-r^{\prime})R}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta}\right)^{2/q}.

(3.19)

Combining (
3.15
)–(
3.3
), we get

\frac{\lambda_{0}}{8}\fint_{B_{r^{\prime}}}\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\leq C\beta^{2}\left((r-r^{\prime})^{-2}+(r-r^{\prime})^{-1}R^{-1}+R^{-2}\right)\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta}\right)^{2/q}.

(3.20)

Furthermore, since
\alpha=2
when
n\geq 3
and
\alpha\in(1,2)
when
n=2
, by
Sobolev embedding

\left(\fint_{B_{r^{\prime}}}H(\Psi)^{\frac{n\alpha}{n-\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n\alpha}}\lesssim\left(\fint_{B_{r^{\prime}}}H(\Psi)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+r^{\prime}\left(\fint_{B_{r^{\prime}}}\left|\nabla H(\Psi)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now by (
3.20
), (
3.12
), and letting
N
go to infinity, we obtain

\left(\fint_{B_{r^{\prime}}}\Psi^{\beta\frac{n\alpha}{n-\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n\alpha}}\leq\left(\fint_{B_{r^{\prime}}}\Psi^{2\beta}\right)^{1/2}\\
+C\beta r^{\prime}\left((r-r^{\prime})^{-2}+(r-r^{\prime})^{-1}R^{-1}+R^{-2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta}\right)^{1/q}\\
\leq C\left(1+\beta\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r-r^{\prime}}+\frac{r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{(r-r^{\prime})R}}+\frac{r^{\prime}}{R}\right)\right)\left(\fint_{B_{r}}\Psi^{q\beta}\right)^{1/q}.

Letting
l=\frac{n\alpha}{(n-\alpha)q}>1
,
\beta=\beta_{i}=kl^{i}
,
r=r_{i}=R+\frac{R}{2^{i}}
and
r^{\prime}=r_{i+1}
for
i=0,1,2,\dots
, one finds

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{r_{i+1}}}\Psi^{kl^{i+1}q}\right)^{\frac{1}{kl^{i+1}q}}

\displaystyle\leq(Ckl^{i})^{\frac{1}{kl^{i}}}\left(\fint_{B_{r_{i}}}\Psi^{kl^{i}q}\right)^{\frac{1}{kl^{i}q}}\leq\dots

\displaystyle\leq(Ck)^{\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{1}{kl^{j}}}l^{\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{j}{kl^{j}}}\left(\fint_{B_{2R}}\Psi^{kq}\right)^{\frac{1}{kq}}.

Letting
i\to\infty
, we have
\sup_{B_{R}}\Psi\leq C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},q,k_{0})\left(\fint_{B_{2R}}\Psi^{kq}\right)^{\frac{1}{kq}}
, and thus

\sup_{B_{R}}\left|\widetilde{\varphi}-c_{0}\right|\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{2R}}\left|\widetilde{\varphi}-c_{0}\right|^{kq}\right)^{\frac{1}{kq}}+C\delta,

where
C=C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},q,k_{0})
. Choosing
k
and
q
such that
kq=p
yields (
3.10
). The proof of (
3.11
) is similar and thus omitted.
∎

3.4.
Weak Solutions of the Parabolic Equation

We introduce
\mathcal{P}_{t}:=e^{-t^{2}L_{||}}
and
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{t}:=e^{-t^{2}L_{||}^{*}}
, the “ellipticized” heat semigroup associated to
L_{||}=-\divg A_{||}\nabla
and to its adjoint
L_{||}^{*}
, respectively. In this subsection, we shall derive Moser-type estimates for
\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}f
(and
\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}f
), as well as reverse Hölder estimate for
\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}f
(and
\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}f
).

Notation.

In the rest of this section, since we only work with the
n
-dimensional operator
L_{||}
and its adjoint
L_{||}^{*}
instead of the operator
L
defined in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, we shall simply write
L
for
L_{||}
, and the same for its adjoint. For the same reason, we shall write
\nabla
for
\nabla_{x}
, and
\divg
for
\divg_{x}
.
We denote by
\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}
the space of bounded semilinear functionals on
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
u(x,t)=e^{-tL}(f)(x)
, for some
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then by Proposition
A.1
,
u(x,t)
is the weak solution to the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\divg(A\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=f(x).\end{cases}

That is,
u(x,t)\in L_{\loc}^{2}\left((0,\infty),W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)\cap C\left([0,\infty),L^{2}\right)
, and satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x,T)\overline{\varphi(x,T)}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x,0)\overline{\varphi(x,0)}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\overline{\langle\partial_{t}\varphi,u\rangle}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}

for any
T>0
, any
\varphi\in L^{2}\left([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
with
\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}\left([0,T],\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
.

Moreover, since
A
depends only on
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\partial_{t}u
is a weak solution to
\partial_{t}v-\divg(A\nabla v)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
(see the remark after Proposition
A.1
). By
[
13
]
, Theorem 4.9 and its remark,
\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}_{\loc}\left((0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
and
\partial_{t}\nabla u\in L^{2}_{\loc}\left((0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
. Finally, by the Gaussian estimate for the kernel of
\partial_{t}e^{-tL}
(see
[
13
]
Theorem 4.8), one can show that

\partial_{t}u\in L^{\infty}\left([\delta_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\quad\forall\,\delta_{0}>0.

(3.21)

These facts enable us to prove the following estimate for
\partial_{t}u
using Moser iteration.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a cube with
l(Q)=R_{0}
. Then

\sup_{Q\times(R_{0}^{2},(2R_{0})^{2}]}\left|\partial_{t}u(x,t)\right|\leq CR_{0}^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\left(\int_{\frac{3}{2}Q}\int_{\frac{R_{0}^{2}}{2}}^{(2R_{0})^{2}}\left|\partial_{t}u(x,t)\right|^{2}dtdx\right)^{1/2},

(3.22)

for some
C=C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0})
.

Proof.

Let
v(x,t)=\partial_{t}u(x,t)
. Then by the definition of weak solution and Lemma
A.1
(ii), we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}v(x,t)\varphi(x,t)dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi=0,

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}\left([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
with
\supp\varphi\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T]
. By considering
v^{\pm}
we can assume
v\geq 0
, and that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}v(x,t)\varphi(x,t)dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi\leq 0,

(3.23)

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}\left([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
with
\supp\varphi\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T]
and
\varphi\geq 0
a.e.

Now for any
0\leq s\leq 1
, define

Q_{s}=(1+s)Q,\quad I_{s}=((1-s)R_{0}^{2},(2R_{0})^{2}],\quad\mbox{and}\quad C_{s}=Q_{s}\times I_{s}.

Fix
l\in\mathbb{N}
, define
q_{l}=2k_{0}^{l}
, where
k_{0}=\frac{n+2}{n}
. Note that
q_{0}=2
.
Furthermore, for any fixed

\frac{4}{3}\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}\leq s_{0}<s_{1}\leq\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{2^{l+2}},

choose
\Psi_{s_{0},s_{1}}\in C_{0}^{2}(C_{\frac{s_{0}+s_{1}}{2}})
and
\widetilde{\Psi}_{s_{0},s_{1}}\in C_{0}^{2}(C_{s_{1}})
such that
\Psi_{s_{0},s_{1}}=1
in
C_{s_{0}}
,
\widetilde{\Psi}_{s_{0},s_{1}}=1
in
C_{\frac{s_{0}+s_{1}}{2}}
,

0\leq\Psi_{s_{0},s_{1}},\widetilde{\Psi}_{s_{0},s_{1}}\leq 1
, and

\left|\nabla\Psi_{s_{0},s_{1}}\right|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\Psi_{s_{0},s_{1}}\right|+\left|\nabla\widetilde{\Psi}_{s_{0},s_{1}}\right|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}\widetilde{\Psi}_{s_{0},s_{1}}\right|\lesssim\frac{R_{0}^{-2}}{(s_{1}-s_{0})^{2}}.

We omit the subscript
s_{0},s_{1}
in
\Psi_{s_{0},s_{1}}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}_{s_{0},s_{1}}
from now on.

Let
t\in I_{s_{0}}
. Recalling (
3.21
), one can take
\varphi=v^{q_{l}-1}\Psi^{2}
as a test function. Then (
3.23
) gives

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}vv^{q_{l}-1}\Psi^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla v\cdot\nabla(v^{q_{l}-1}\Psi^{2})\leq 0.

(3.24)

For the first term, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}vv^{q_{l}-1}\Psi^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{l}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{q_{l}}(x,t)\Psi^{2}(x,t)dx-\frac{1}{q_{l}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{q_{l}}\partial_{t}(\Psi^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{q_{l}}\int_{Q_{s_{0}}}v^{q_{l}}(x,t)dx-\frac{CR_{0}^{-2}}{q_{l}(s_{1}-s_{0})^{2}}\int_{C_{\frac{1}{2^{l+1}}}}v^{q_{l}}.

The second term in (
3.24
) is split, as usual, corresponding to the symmetric and antisymmetric part of
A
. Working with
A^{s}
, we estimate

\quad\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(v^{q_{l}-1}\Psi^{2})\\
=\frac{4(q_{l}-1)}{q_{l}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}})\cdot\nabla(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}})\Psi^{2}\\
+\frac{4}{q_{l}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}})\cdot\nabla\Psi v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\Psi\\
\geq\frac{2\lambda_{0}(q_{l}-1)}{q_{l}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q_{s_{0}}}\left|\nabla v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\right|^{2}dxdt-\frac{C(n,\lambda_{0})R_{0}^{-2}}{q_{l}(s_{1}-s_{0})^{2}}\int_{C_{\frac{1}{2^{l+1}}}}v^{q_{l}}.

Turning to
A^{a}
, note that
A^{a}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v\,\Psi^{2}=0
due to anti-symmetry, so that

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(v^{q_{l}-1}\Psi^{2})=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\Psi^{2})v^{q_{l}-1}\\
=\frac{1}{q_{l}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla(v^{q_{l}})\cdot\nabla(\Psi^{2})=\frac{1}{q_{l}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{s}\nabla(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\widetilde{\Psi}v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\widetilde{\Psi})\cdot\nabla(\Psi^{2}).

By Proposition
3.3
,

\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A^{a}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(v^{q_{l}-1}\Psi^{2})\right|\\
\leq\frac{C_{n}\Lambda_{0}}{q_{l}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla\Psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\widetilde{\Psi}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|\nabla\left(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\widetilde{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}dt\\
\leq\frac{C_{\theta}\Lambda_{0}}{q_{l}}\frac{R_{0}^{-2}}{(s_{1}-s_{0})^{2}}\int_{C_{\frac{1}{2^{l+1}}}}v^{q_{l}}dxdt+\frac{\theta}{q_{l}}\int_{C_{s_{1}}}\left|\nabla v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\right|^{2}.

Combining these estimates with (
3.24
), we have

\int_{Q_{s_{0}}}v^{q_{l}}(x,t)dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{Q_{s_{0}}}\left|\nabla v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\right|^{2}\\
\leq\frac{CR_{0}^{-2}}{(s_{1}-s_{0})^{2}}\int_{C_{\frac{1}{2^{l+1}}}}v^{q_{l}}dxdt+C\theta\int_{C_{s_{1}}}\left|\nabla\left(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}dxdt,

where
C=C(n,\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},\theta)
.

Choosing
\theta
to be sufficiently small, and then taking supremum in
t\in I_{s_{0}}
, we obtain

\sup_{t\in I_{s_{0}}}\int_{Q_{s_{0}}}v^{q_{l}}(x,t)dx+\int_{C_{s_{0}}}\left|\nabla v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\right|^{2}dxdt\\
\leq\frac{CR_{0}^{-2}}{(s_{1}-s_{0})^{2}}\int_{C_{\frac{1}{2^{l+1}}}}v^{q_{l}}dxdt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{C_{s_{1}}}\left|\nabla\left(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}dxdt,

which implies

\sup_{t\in I_{\frac{4}{3}\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}}}\int_{Q_{\frac{4}{3}\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}}}v^{q_{l}}(x,t)dx+\int_{C_{\frac{4}{3}\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}}}\left|\nabla v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\right|^{2}dxdt\\

by Lemma

\int_{C_{\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}}}v^{q_{l}k_{0}}\leq\int_{C_{\frac{4}{3}\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}}}(v^{\frac{q_{l}}{2}}\Psi_{l})^{2k_{0}}\\

Then (

Proof.

Let

3.6.
L^{p}

Definition 3.2
.

The non-tangential maximal function is defined as

N^{\alpha}(u)(x):=\sup_{t>0}\sup_{(y,t):\left|x-y\right|<\alpha t}\left|u(y,t)\right|.

(3.33)

The integrated non-tangential maximal function is defined as

\widetilde{N}^{\alpha}(u)(x):=\sup_{t>0}\sup_{(y,t):\left|x-y\right|<\alpha t}\left(\fint_{\left|y-z\right|<\alpha t}\left|u(z,t)\right|^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}.

(3.34)

Again, we shall simply write
L
for the
n
-dimensional operator
L_{||}
in this section. We consider functions such as
N^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}e^{-t^{2}L}f)
, where we think of

\partial_{t}e^{-t^{2}L}f(x)
as a function of
x
and
t
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\eta>0
,
\alpha>0
. Then

\left\|\eta^{-1}N^{\eta\alpha}(\partial_{t}e^{-(\eta t)^{2}L}f)\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\alpha,p}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}

for all
p>1
, and
f\in W^{1,p}
. The constant
C_{\alpha,p}
also depends on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and
n
, but not on
\eta
.

Proof.

Fix any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
(y,t)\in\Gamma_{\eta\alpha}(x)
so that
\left|x-y\right|<\eta\alpha t
. We claim that for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\left|\eta^{-1}\partial_{t}e^{-(\eta t)^{2}L}f(y)\right|\leq C_{\alpha}M(\nabla f)(x).

(3.35)

Let
V_{t}(x,y)
be the kernel associated to
\partial_{t}e^{-t^{2}L}
. Then by
[
13
]
Theorem 4.8, we have

\left|V_{t}(x,y)\right|\lesssim t^{-n-1}e^{-\frac{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{ct^{2}}},\qquad\left|\eta^{-1}V_{\eta t}(x,y)\right|\lesssim(\eta t)^{-n-1}e^{-\frac{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}{c(\eta t)^{2}}}

where the implicit constant depends on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and
n
.
We write

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}\partial_{t}e^{-(\eta t)^{2}L}f(y)

\displaystyle=\eta^{-1}\partial_{t}e^{-(\eta t)^{2}L}\Bigl(f-\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}f\Bigr)(y)

\displaystyle=\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta^{-1}V_{\eta t}(y,z)\Bigl(f-\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}f\Bigr)(z)dz.

Then the estimate for the kernel entails the bound

\left|\eta^{-1}\partial_{t}e^{-(\eta t)^{2}L}f(y)\right|\lesssim\int_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}\frac{1}{(\eta t)^{n+1}}e^{-\frac{\left|y-z\right|^{2}}{c(\eta t)^{2}}}\left|f(z)-\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}f\right|dz\\
+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}B_{\eta\alpha t}(x)\setminus 2^{k}B_{\eta\alpha t}(x)}\frac{1}{(\eta t)^{n+1}}e^{-\frac{\left|y-z\right|^{2}}{c(\eta t)^{2}}}\left|f(z)-\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}f\right|dz\\
=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, we trivially bound
e^{-\frac{\left|y-z\right|^{2}}{c(\eta t)^{2}}}
by 1, and then the Poincaré inequality gives

I_{1}\lesssim\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\eta t}\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}\left|f(z)-\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}f\right|dz\lesssim\alpha^{n+1}\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}\left|\nabla f\right|\\
\lesssim\alpha^{n+1}M(\nabla f)(x),

where the implicit constants depend only on
n
.

For
I_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\eta t)^{n+1}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(2^{k}-1)^{2}\alpha^{2}}{c}\right\}\int_{2^{k+1}B_{\eta\alpha t}(x)}\left|f(z)-\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}f\right|dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n(k+1)}\alpha^{n}}{\eta t}\exp\left\{-\frac{(2^{k}-1)^{2}\alpha^{2}}{c}\right\}\fint_{2^{k+1}B_{\eta\alpha t}(x)}\left|f(z)-\fint_{B_{2\eta\alpha t}(x)}f\right|dz.

Breaking the integrand into sum of terms containing
\fint_{2^{l+1}B_{\eta\alpha t}(x)}f-\fint_{2^{l}B_{\eta\alpha t}(x)}f
and using the Poincaré inequality again, we obtain

I_{2}\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\left\{-\frac{4k^{2}\alpha^{2}}{c}\right\}2^{n(k+1)}\alpha^{n+1}\sum_{l=2}^{k+1}2^{l}M(\nabla f)(x)\lesssim_{\alpha}M(\nabla f)(x),

and thus (
3.35
) follows. By the choice of
(y,t)
, this implies

\eta^{-1}N^{\eta\alpha}(\partial_{t}e^{-(\eta t)^{2}L}f)(x)\leq C_{\alpha}M(\nabla f)(x),

so that

\left\|\eta^{-1}N^{\eta\alpha}(\partial_{t}e^{-(\eta t)^{2}L}f)\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\alpha,p}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\forall\,p>1,\ f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then the proposition follows from a standard limiting argument.
∎

Proposition 3.12

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(x)\nabla u(x,\tau)\cdot\nabla\left((u(x,\tau)-\bar{u})\Psi^{2}\right)dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{\tau}u(x,\tau)(u(x,\tau)-\bar{u})\Psi^{2}dx

\tau>0
.

3.7
, one can write

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A(x)\nabla u(x,\tau)\cdot\nabla\left((u(x,\tau)-\bar{u})\Psi^{2}\right)dx\\

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{\tau}u(x,\tau)(u(x,\tau)-\bar{u})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim(s^{\prime}-s)^{2}(\eta t)^{2}\int_{B_{\frac{s+s^{\prime}}{2}}}\left|\partial_{\tau}u(x,\tau)\right|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{(s^{\prime}-s)^{2}(\eta t)^{2}}\int_{B_{\frac{s+s^{\prime}}{2}}}\left|u(x,\tau)-\bar{u}\right|^{2}.

\int_{B_{s}}\left|\nabla u(x,\tau)\right|^{2}dx\leq\frac{C}{(s^{\prime}-s)^{2}(\eta t)^{2}}\int_{B_{s^{\prime}}}(u(x,\tau)-\bar{u})^{2}dx\\

Choosing

3.2

\fint_{B(y,\eta t)}\left|\nabla u(x,\tau)\right|^{2}dx\\
\lesssim\frac{1}{(\eta t)^{2}}\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|u(x,\tau)-\bar{u}\right|^{2}dx+(\eta t)^{2}\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|\partial_{\tau}u(x,\tau)\right|^{2}dx,

for any
\tau>0
.

Let
w(z,t)=u(z,\eta^{2}t^{2})

\left\|\widetilde{N}^{\eta}(\nabla_{x}w)\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\forall\,p>2.

To this end, let
\tau=\eta^{2}t^{2}

3.36

\partial_{t}w(z,t)=2\eta^{2}t\partial_{t}u(z,\eta^{2}t^{2})

\fint_{B(y,\eta t)}\left|\nabla w(z,t)\right|^{2}dz\\
\lesssim\frac{1}{(\eta t)^{2}}\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|w(z,t)-\bar{w}\right|^{2}dz+\eta^{-2}\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|\partial_{t}w(z,t)\right|^{2}dz

(3.37)

where
\bar{w}=\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}w(z,0)dz
.

The expression
\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|\partial_{t}w(z,t)\right|^{2}dz
on the right-hand side of (
3.37
) can be controlled by

M\left(N^{\eta}(\partial_{t}w)^{2}\right)(x)
. To estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.37

\left|w(z,t)-\bar{w}\right|\leq\left|w(z,t)-w(z,0)\right|+\left|w(z,0)-\bar{w}\right|.

Using Poincaŕe inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\eta t)^{2}}\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|w(z,0)-\bar{w}\right|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|\nabla w(z,0)\right|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim M\left(\left|\nabla w(\cdot,0)\right|^{2}\right)(x)=M\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}\right)(x).

Since

\left|w(z,t)-w(z,0)\right|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{\tau}w(z,\tau)\right|d\tau\leq t\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\left|\partial_{\tau}w(z,\tau)\right|,

we conclude that

\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\left|w(z,t)-w(z,0)\right|^{2}dz\leq t^{2}\fint_{B(y,\frac{3}{2}\eta t)}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\left|\partial_{\tau}w(z,t)^{2}\right|dz\\
\leq t^{2}M(N^{\eta}(\partial_{t}w)^{2})(x).

Therefore, we have obtained the estimate

\fint_{B(y,\eta t)}\left|\nabla w(z,t)\right|^{2}dz\lesssim M\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}\right)(x)+\eta^{-2}M\left(N^{\eta}(\partial_{t}w)^{2}\right)(x)

for any
(y,t)\in\Gamma_{\eta}(x)
. This implies, by the definition of the integrated non-tangential maximal function
\widetilde{N}^{\eta}
, that

\widetilde{N}^{\eta}(\nabla_{z}w)(x)\lesssim\left(M\left(\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}\right)(x)\right)^{1/2}+\eta^{-1}\left(M\left(N^{\eta}(\partial_{t}w)^{2}\right)(x)\right)^{1/2},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Thus for any
p>2
,

\left\|\widetilde{N}^{\eta}\left(\nabla e^{-\eta^{2}t^{2}L}f\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}+\left\|\eta^{-1}N^{\eta}\left(\partial_{t}e^{-\eta^{2}t^{2}L}f\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
\leq C_{p}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where we have used Proposition
3.11
with
\alpha=1
.
∎

4.
Construction of F and sawtooth domains associated with F

4.1.
The set F

We define the following maximal differential operator

D_{p}f(x):=\sup_{r>0}\left(\fint_{\left|x-y\right|<r}\left(\frac{\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|}\right)^{p}dy\right)^{1/p}.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.1
.

\left\|D_{p_{1}}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{p,p_{1},n}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{p}}\qquad\forall\ 1\leq p_{1}<p<\infty.

This lemma follows from a Morrey type inequality

\frac{\left|f(x)-f(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|}\leq M(\nabla f)(x)+M(\nabla f)(y)\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and the
L^{p}
bound for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function.

We introduce a few notations. Recall that we use
\mathcal{P}_{t}
to denote
e^{-t^{2}L_{||}}
, and
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{t}=e^{-t^{2}L_{||}^{*}}
. Define

\Lambda_{1}:=\eta^{-1}N^{\eta}(\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi)+N(\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{t}\varphi)+\widetilde{N}^{\eta}(\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi)+\left(M(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2})\right)^{1/2},

\Lambda_{2}:=\eta^{-1}N^{\eta}(\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi})+N(\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{t}\widetilde{\varphi})+\widetilde{N}^{\eta}(\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi})+\left(M(\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2})\right)^{1/2},

where
\varphi
and
\widetilde{\varphi}
are as in Proposition
3.4
, and the non-tangential maximal operator
N
in the second terms on the two right hand sides in defined with respect to the cones of aperture 1.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\kappa_{0}\gg 1
be given. Fix
p_{1}\in(1,2)
and define the set
F
as follows

F:=\left\{x\in Q:\Lambda_{1}(x)+\Lambda_{2}(x)+D_{p_{1}}\varphi(x)+D_{p_{1}}\widetilde{\varphi}(x)\leq\kappa_{0}\right\}.

(4.2)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\epsilon_{0}
be as in Proposition
3.4
. Then

\left|Q\setminus F\right|\lesssim\kappa_{0}^{-2-\epsilon_{0}}\left|Q\right|

(4.3)

uniformly in
\eta
.

Proof.

By Chebyshev’s inequality,

\kappa_{0}^{2+\epsilon_{0}}\left|Q\setminus F\right|\leq\int_{Q\cap\left\{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+D_{p_{1}}\varphi+D_{p_{1}}\widetilde{\varphi}>\kappa_{0}\right\}}\left(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}+D_{p_{1}}\varphi+D_{p_{1}}\widetilde{\varphi}\right)^{2+\epsilon_{0}}dx.

We apply Proposition
3.11
, Propostion
3.12
, and Proposition
4.1
, and their analogs for the adjoint operators, with
p=2+\epsilon_{0}
, to see that the right-hand side is bounded by

C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2+\epsilon_{0}}+M\left(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2+\epsilon_{0}}{2}}+\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2+\epsilon_{0}}+M\left(\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2+\epsilon_{0}}{2}}dx,

which, in turn, is bounded by

C\left|Q\right|\fint_{5Q}\left(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2+\epsilon_{0}}+\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2+\epsilon_{0}}\right).

Then the lemma follows from (
3.5
).
∎

We can now choose
\kappa_{0}
, depending only on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and
n
, such that

\left|Q\setminus F\right|\leq\frac{1}{1000}\left|Q\right|.

(4.4)

This completes the construction of
F
and from now on
\kappa_{0}
is fixed.

4.2.
Sawtooth domains and related estimates

Define
\Omega_{0}
to be the sawtooth domain

\Omega_{0}:=\bigcup_{x\in F}\Gamma_{\eta}(x).

(4.5)

Define

\theta_{t}:=\varphi-\mathcal{P}^{*}_{t}\varphi,\qquad\widetilde{\theta}_{t}:=\widetilde{\varphi}-\mathcal{P}_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}.

(4.6)

We observe that

\theta_{\eta t}(x)=-\int_{0}^{\eta t}\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{s}\varphi(x),\text{ and}\quad\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}(x)=-\int_{0}^{\eta t}\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{s}\widetilde{\varphi}(x).

So by the definition of the set
F
,

\left|\theta_{\eta t}(x)\right|\leq\eta t\kappa_{0},\qquad\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}(x)\right|\leq\eta t\kappa_{0}\qquad\forall\,(x,t)\in F\times(0,\infty).

(4.7)

We show that such estimates also hold in the truncated sawtooth domain. Note that we shall eventually choose
\eta>0
to be small, so we can assume in the sequel that
\eta<1/2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Retain the notation above. The following estimates hold:

\left|\theta_{\eta t}(x)\right|\lesssim\eta t\kappa_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}(x)\right|\lesssim\eta t\kappa_{0},\qquad\forall\,(x,t)\in\Omega_{0}\cap\left(2Q\times(0,4l(Q))\right).

Proof.

We only show the estimate for
\theta_{\eta t}
, for the proof for
\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}
is similar. Let
(x,t)\in\Omega_{0}\cap\left(2Q\times(0,4l(Q))\right)
. Then there exists
x_{0}\in F
such that
\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq\eta t
. Since
t<4l(Q)
, and
\eta<\frac{1}{2}
, we have
2B(x_{0},\eta t)\subset 5Q
.
We write

\displaystyle\left|\theta_{\eta t}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x_{0})\right|+\left|\theta_{\eta t}(x_{0})\right|+\left|\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\left(\varphi-\left(\varphi\right)_{2B_{\eta t}(x_{0})}\right)(x_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\left(\varphi-\left(\varphi\right)_{2B_{\eta t}(x_{0})}\right)(x)\right|

(4.8)

where
\left(\varphi\right)_{2B_{\eta t}(x_{0})}=\fint_{2B_{\eta t}(x_{0})}\varphi
. Note that we have used the conservation property, and
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\left(\varphi\right)_{B_{\eta t}(x_{0})}
is a constant.

By Proposition
3.5
, the first term on right-hand side of (
4.2
) is bounded by

C\left(\fint_{2B_{\eta t}(x_{0})}\left|\varphi-\varphi(x_{0})\right|^{p_{1}}\right)^{1/{p_{1}}}+C\eta t(\left\|\mathbf{c}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\mathbf{c}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO}).

By the definition of
D_{p_{1}}
and the set
F
, this is bounded by

C\eta t\left(D_{p_{1}}\varphi(x_{0})+\lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{0}\right)\leq C\eta t(\kappa_{0}+\lambda_{0}+\Lambda_{0})\leq C\eta t\kappa_{0},

with
C=C(\lambda_{0},\Lambda_{0},n,p_{1})
.

By (
4.7
), the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.2
) is also bounded by
C\eta t\kappa_{0}
. Now we take care of the last two terms in (
4.2
). We claim that for any
(y,s)\in\Gamma_{\eta}(x_{0})
,

\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta s}\left(\varphi-\left(\varphi\right)_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\right)(y)\lesssim\eta sM(\nabla\varphi)(x_{0})\lesssim\eta s\kappa_{0}.

Consider the kernel
K_{(\eta s)^{2}}^{*}
associated to
\mathcal{P}_{\eta s}^{*}
. Then by the Gaussian estimate for the kernel of the semigroup,

\left|K_{(\eta s)^{2}}^{*}(y,z)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\eta s)^{n}}e^{-\frac{c\left|y-z\right|^{2}}{(\eta s)^{2}}}.

Then, for
(y,s)\in\Gamma_{\eta}(x_{0})
,

\quad\left|\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta s}\left(\varphi-\left(\varphi\right)_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\right)(y)\right|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(\eta t)^{n}}e^{-\frac{c\left|y-z\right|^{2}}{(\eta s)^{2}}}\left|\varphi(z)-(\varphi)_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\right|dz\\
\lesssim\int_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{(\eta s)^{n}}\left|\varphi(z)-(\varphi)_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\right|dz\\
\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}B_{\eta s}(x_{0})\setminus 2^{k}B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{(\eta s)^{n}}e^{-\frac{c\left|y-z\right|^{2}}{(\eta s)^{2}}}\left|\varphi(z)-(\varphi)_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\right|dz.

Since
\left|y-x_{0}\right|\leq\eta s
,
\left|y-z\right|\geq(2^{k}-1)\eta s
for
z\in 2^{k+1}B_{\eta s}(x_{0})\setminus 2^{k}B_{\eta s}(x_{0})
. Therefore,

\left|\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta s}\left(\varphi-\left(\varphi\right)_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\right)(y)\right|\lesssim(\eta s)\fint_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla\varphi(z)\right|dz\\
+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k+1}B_{\eta s}(x_{0})\setminus 2^{k}B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{(\eta s)^{n}}e^{-c(2^{k}-1)^{2}}\left|\varphi(z)-(\varphi)_{2B_{\eta s}(x_{0})}\right|dz\\
\lesssim(\eta s)M(\nabla\varphi)(x_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k(n+1)}e^{-c(2^{k}-1)^{2}}\eta sM(\nabla\varphi)(x_{0})\\
\lesssim\eta sM(\nabla\varphi)(x_{0})\lesssim\eta s\left(M\left(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\right)(x_{0})\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\eta s\kappa_{0}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Retain the notation above. The following estimates hold:

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\theta_{\eta t}(x)\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t^{3}}\lesssim\eta^{2}\left|Q\right|,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}(x)\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t^{3}}\lesssim\eta^{2}\left|Q\right|,

where the implicit constants only depend on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and
n
.

Proof.

We only prove the estimate for
\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}
, for the proof for
{\theta}_{\eta t}
is similar.
We have the following weighted Hardy’s inequality:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s)\right|ds\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(t)\right|^{p}\frac{dt}{t},\quad\forall\,1<p<\infty.

(4.9)

A short and direct proof of (
4.9
) is provided at the end.
Recall that

\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{\eta t}\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{s}\widetilde{\varphi}ds\right|\leq\int_{0}^{\eta t}\left|\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{s}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|ds,

so that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|\right)^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{\eta t}\left|\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{s}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|ds\right)^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\\
=\eta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{s}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|ds\right)^{2}\frac{dt}{t}.

By (
4.9
), the last term is bounded by
\eta^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}
.
Then Proposition
3.8
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t^{3}}\right)^{p/2}dx\leq\eta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{p/2}dx\lesssim\eta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{p}dx

for any
p\geq 2
. In particular, with
p=2
, we obtain

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}(x)\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t^{3}}\lesssim\eta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}dx\lesssim\eta^{2}\fint_{5Q}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}dx\left|Q\right|\lesssim\eta^{2}\left|Q\right|.

Proof of (
4.9
).
By Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s)\right|ds\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s)\right|^{p}ds\right)^{1/p}t^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.

And so

\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s)\right|ds\right)^{p}\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s)\right|^{p}ds.

Integrating in
t
and using Fubini, we obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s)\right|ds\right)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\left|f(s)\right|^{p}ds\frac{dt}{t^{2}}\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(s)\right|^{p}\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}dtds=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(s)\right|^{p}\frac{ds}{s}.

∎

4.3.
The cut-off function

In this subsection, we define the cut-off function adapted to a thinner sawtooth domain. Define

\Omega_{1}=\bigcup_{x\in F}\Gamma_{\frac{\eta}{8}}(x).

(4.10)

Let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
0\leq\Phi\leq 1
,
\Phi(r)=1
if
r\leq\frac{1}{16}
, and
\Phi(r)=0
if
r>\frac{1}{8}
.
Define

\Psi(x,t):=\Psi_{\eta,\epsilon}:=\Phi\left(\frac{\delta(x)}{\eta t}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{t}{32l(Q)}\right)\left(1-\Phi\left(\frac{t}{16\epsilon}\right)\right),

(4.11)

where
\delta(x):=\dist(x,F)
.

Then
\Psi
has following properties. First,

\Psi=1\quad\mbox{on}\quad\bigcup_{x\in F}\Gamma_{\frac{\eta}{16}}\cap\left\{2\epsilon<t\leq 2l(Q)\right\}.

Secondly,
\supp\Psi\subset\Omega_{1}\cap\left\{\epsilon<t<4l(Q)\right\}
. And finally,

\supp\nabla\Psi\subset E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup E_{3},

where

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,t)\in 2Q\times(0,4l(Q)):\frac{\eta t}{16}\leq\delta(x)\leq\frac{\eta t}{8}\right\},

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,t)\in 2Q\times(2l(Q),4l(Q)):\delta(x)\leq\frac{\eta t}{8}\right\},

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,t)\in 2Q\times(\epsilon,2\epsilon):\delta(x)\leq\frac{\eta t}{8}\right\}.

In addition, a direct computation shows

\left|\nabla\Psi(x,t)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\eta t}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{1}}+\frac{1}{l(Q)}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{3}}.

(4.12)

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the assumptions above,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|^{\alpha}t^{\alpha-1}dt\right)^{p}dx\leq C(\eta,\alpha,p,n)\left|Q\right|,

(4.13)

for any
\alpha>0
,
p>0
, and

\underset{\supp\nabla\Psi}{\iint}\frac{dxdt}{t}\leq C_{n}\left|Q\right|.

(4.14)

Proof.

Using (
4.12
), we compute

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|^{\alpha}t^{\alpha-1}dt\right)^{p}dx\lesssim_{n}\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{\frac{8\delta(x)}{\eta}}^{\frac{16\delta(x)}{\eta}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta t}\right)^{\alpha}t^{\alpha-1}dt\right)^{p}dx\\
+\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{2l(Q)}^{4l(Q)}\left(\frac{1}{l(Q)}\right)^{\alpha}t^{\alpha-1}dt\right)^{p}dx+\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{\epsilon}^{2\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\alpha}t^{\alpha-1}dt\right)^{p}dx\\
\lesssim_{n}\frac{1}{\eta^{\alpha p}}\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{\frac{8\delta(x)}{\eta}}^{\frac{16\delta(x)}{\eta}}\frac{1}{t}\,dt\right)^{p}dx+C_{\alpha,p}\left|2Q\right|\\
\leq C(\alpha,p,n)\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta^{\alpha p}}\right)\left|Q\right|\leq C(\eta,\alpha,p,n)\left|Q\right|.

This shows (
4.13
), and (
4.14
) can be derived similarly:

\displaystyle\underset{\supp\nabla\Psi}{\iint}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{E_{1}}\frac{dxdt}{t}+\iint_{E_{2}}\frac{dxdt}{t}+\iint_{E_{3}}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2Q}\int_{\frac{8\delta(x)}{\eta}}^{\frac{16\delta(x)}{\eta}}\frac{dt}{t}\,dx+\int_{2Q}\int_{2l(Q)}^{4l(Q)}\frac{dt}{t}\,dx+\int_{2Q}\int_{\epsilon}^{2\epsilon}\frac{dt}{t}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\left|Q\right|,

as desired.
∎

5.
Proof of the Carleson measure estimate

Throughout this section, let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be fixed, and construct
F\subset Q
and the cut-off function
\Psi
as in Section
4
. Recall that
\kappa_{0}
is fixed to ensure that (
4.4
) holds.

Recall that we have the matrix
A=A(x)
whose entries are functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or, independent of
t
, and we write

A=\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix}.

Write the
n\times 1
vector
\mathbf{b}
as
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}_{1}+\mathbf{b}_{2}
, with
\divg_{x}\mathbf{b}_{2}=0
. We define a new matrix
A_{1}
as follows:

A_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix}

and define
L_{1}=-\divg A_{1}\nabla
. Then
L_{1}
and
L
actually define the same operator. To be precise, we have the following

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
and
v\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla v(x,t)dxdt=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{1}(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla v(x,t)dxdt.

(5.1)

In particular, a weak solution to
Lu=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
is also a weak solution to
L_{1}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
.

Proof.

We first show (
5.1
) for
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
and
v\in C_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
. To this end, we write

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla v(x,t)dxdt\\
=\iint A_{||}\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}v+\left(\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\intercal}+\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}v\,\partial_{t}u+\mathbf{c}\cdot\nabla_{x}u\,\partial_{t}v+d\,\partial_{t}u\,\partial_{t}v\,dxdt,

(5.2)

and

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\cdot\nabla_{x}v\,\partial_{t}u\,dxdt=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}\left(\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\cdot\nabla_{x}v\right)u\,dxdt\\
=-\iint\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\cdot\nabla_{x}(\partial_{t}v)\,u\,dxdt=-\iint\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\cdot\nabla_{x}(\partial_{t}v\,u)dxdt\\
+\iint\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\cdot\nabla_{x}u\,\partial_{t}v\,dxdt=\iint\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\cdot\nabla_{x}u\,\partial_{t}v\,dxdt,

where in the second equality we have used the facts that
\mathbf{b}_{2}
is
t
-independent and that
v\in C^{2}
, and in the last equality we have used the divergence-free property of
\mathbf{b}_{2}
. Then (
5.2
) is further equal to

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}v+\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\intercal}\cdot\nabla_{x}v\,\partial_{t}u+\left(\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\intercal}\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}u\,\partial_{t}v+d\,\partial_{t}u\,\partial_{t}v\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{1}(x)\nabla u(x,t)\cdot\nabla v(x,t)dxdt.

Now since
C_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
, a limiting argument shows that (
5.1
) holds for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
,
v\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})
.
∎

Define

A_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix},

(5.3)

where
\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}\right)_{2Q}=\fint_{2Q}\mathbf{b}^{a}
,
and let
L_{0}:=-\divg A_{0}\nabla
.

Note that
\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}\right)_{2Q}^{\intercal}=-\left(\mathbf{c}^{a}\right)_{2Q}
by definition of
\mathbf{b}^{a}
and
\mathbf{c}^{a}
. Also,
\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}\right)_{2Q}
is a constant vector so we of course have
\divg_{x}\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}\right)_{2Q}=0
. Hence, we can apply the lemma with
\mathbf{b}_{2}=\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}\right)_{2Q}
. Moreover, observe that

A_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix},

where
\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix}
is the symmetric part of
A_{0}
, which is the same as the symmetric part of
A
, and

\begin{bmatrix}\vbox{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{\vbox{\vbox{}\hrule width=0.0pt,height=0.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern 0.0pt\hbox{}}}\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vbox{}\hss}\vbox{}}}\end{bmatrix}
is anti-symmetric,
\BMO
, with the same
\BMO
semi-norm as
A^{a}
. We summarize these observations in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

A weak solution to
Lu=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
is also a weak solution to
L_{0}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
. Moreover, the operator
L_{0}
has the same ellipticity constant and
\BMO
semi-norm as
L
.

Let
u
be a bounded weak solution to
Lu=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
with
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Then
u
is also a bounded weak solution to
L_{0}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
.
Recall that

J_{\eta,\epsilon}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt.

Then by ellipticity of
A_{0}
and the support property of
\Psi
, we have

J_{\eta,\epsilon}\geq\lambda_{0}\int_{2\epsilon}^{l(Q)}\int_{F}\left|\nabla u(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dxdt.

The goal of this section is to prove Lemma
2.4
.
Once it is proved, we choose
\sigma
and
\eta
to be sufficiently small, so that

J_{\eta,\epsilon}\leq 2\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

(5.4)

Now that
\eta
is fixed, and
\tilde{c}
is independent of
\epsilon
, we let
\epsilon\to 0
and thus obtain

\int_{0}^{l(Q)}\int_{F}\left|\nabla u(x,t)\right|^{2}t\,dxdt\leq 2\tilde{c},

as desired.

Let us further reduce the statement to the case of smooth coefficients before we prove Lemma
2.4
. We claim that we can assume that
A
is smooth (and thus
A_{0}
is smooth) in Lemma
2.4
, as long as all bounds depend on
A
only through its ellipticity constant and
\BMO
semi-norm. If
A
is not smooth, we take
A_{\delta}=\xi_{\delta}*A_{0}
, where
\xi_{\delta}(X)=\delta^{-n-1}\xi(\frac{X}{\delta})
is an approximate identity. Then
A_{\delta}
converges to
A_{0}
locally in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for all
1\leq p<\infty
as
\delta\to 0
, and

\left\|A^{a}_{\delta}\right\|_{\BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq\left\|A_{0}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

(5.5)

See e.g.
[
21
]
Proposition 3.3 for a proof of (
5.5
). Then the desired result in the non-smooth case follows from a limiting argument.
To see this, fix any
u
that satisfies
Lu=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+})}\leq 1
. Then fix a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define the cutoff function
\Psi=\Psi_{Q,\epsilon,\eta}
as in (
4.11
). Take cubes
\widetilde{Q}_{0}
and
\widetilde{Q}_{1}
such that

\supp\Psi\subset\subset\widetilde{Q}_{0}\subset\subset\widetilde{Q}_{1}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}.

Now let
u_{\delta}
satisfy
L_{\delta}u_{\delta}=-\divg(A_{\delta}\nabla u_{\delta})=0
in
\widetilde{Q}_{0}
and
u_{\delta}=u
on
\partial\widetilde{Q}_{0}
. Let furthermore

J_{\delta}=J_{\eta,\epsilon,\delta}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{\delta}\nabla u_{\delta}\cdot\nabla u_{\delta}\,\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt.

Since
A_{\delta}
is smooth, we can use the result in the smooth case and have
J_{\delta}\leq C\left|Q\right|
by (
5.4
). The constant
C
is independent of
\epsilon
, and can be independent of
\delta
because of (
5.5
). Hence, it remains to show that
\left|J_{\delta}-J\right|\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
, where

J=J_{\eta,\epsilon}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt.

Notice that
A^{a}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\delta}\cdot\nabla u_{\delta}=A^{a}_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=0
by anti-symmetry, and thus

\left|J_{\delta}-J\right|=\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A^{s}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\delta}\cdot\nabla u_{\delta}\,\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{0}^{s}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt\right|.

We write

A^{s}_{\delta}\nabla u_{\delta}\cdot\nabla u_{\delta}-A_{0}^{s}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\\
=A^{s}_{\delta}\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)\cdot\nabla u_{\delta}+A^{s}_{\delta}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)+(A_{\delta}^{s}-A_{0}^{s})\nabla u\cdot\nabla u,

and get

\left|J_{\delta}-J\right|\leq\,\sup_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\,(\Psi^{2}t)\bigg\{\left|\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}A^{s}_{\delta}\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)\cdot\nabla u_{\delta}\,dxdt\right|\\
+\left|\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}A^{s}_{\delta}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)dxdt\right|+\left|\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}(A_{\delta}^{s}-A_{0}^{s})\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,dxdt\right|\bigg\}\\
\leq C\Big\{\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)\right|(\left|\nabla u_{\delta}\right|+\left|\nabla u\right|)dxdt+\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|A_{\delta}^{s}-A_{0}^{s}\right|\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dxdt\Big\}\\
=:C(I_{1}+I_{2}).

Using ellipticity of
A_{\delta}
, and taking
u-u_{\delta}
as a test function to both
L_{\delta}u_{\delta}=0
and
L_{0}u=0
in
\widetilde{Q}_{0}
, one can get

\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)\right|^{2}dxdt\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1}\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}A_{\delta}\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)\cdot\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)dxdt\\
=\lambda_{0}^{-1}\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}(A_{0}-A_{\delta})\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)dxdt.

Then by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)\right|^{2}dxdt\leq\lambda_{0}^{-2}\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|A_{0}-A_{\delta}\right|^{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dxdt.

Using Hölder inequality, reverse Hölder inequality for
\nabla u
, and the fact that

\left\|A_{0}-A_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
(
p
will be large), we obtain

\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|\nabla(u_{\delta}-u)\right|^{2}dxdt\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\delta\to 0.

(5.6)

Notice that

\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|\nabla u_{\delta}\right|^{2}dxdt\leq\lambda_{0}^{-4}\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{0}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dxdt,

and thus
I_{1}\to 0
by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
5.6
). The second term,
I_{2}
, converges to 0 by the dominated convergence theorem.

This justifies the claim that we only need to prove Lemma
2.4
for
A
smooth. Notice that in this case,
\varphi
,
\widetilde{\varphi}
,
\mathcal{P}_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}
,
\mathcal{P}^{*}_{t}\widetilde{\varphi}
and
u
are all smooth by interior regularity of elliptic equations.

Now we are ready for the

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

In the sequel, we shall simply write
J
for
J_{\eta,\epsilon}
. We shall not distinguish a column vector and a row vector, namely, we shall not use the sign of transposition, as it should be clear from the context. We denote by
c
some constant depending only on
\lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
and
n
, and by
\tilde{c}
a constant depending additionally on
\sigma
and
\eta
.

Since
u
is a weak solution to
L_{0}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
,

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u\,\Psi^{2}t)\,dxdt=0,

where we have chosen
u\,\Psi^{2}t
to be the test function. Therefore,

J=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt\\
=-\iint A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt-\iint A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\nabla t\,u\Psi^{2}dxdt=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

The first term

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=-\iint A_{||}\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt-\iint(\mathbf{b}-\left(\mathbf{b}^{a}\right)_{2Q})\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Psi^{2})\partial_{t}u\,ut\,dxdt

\displaystyle-\iint(\mathbf{c}-\left(\mathbf{c}^{a}\right)_{2Q})\cdot\nabla_{x}u\,\partial_{t}(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt-\iint d\,\partial_{t}u\,\partial_{t}(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt

\displaystyle=:J_{11}+J_{12}+J_{13}+J_{14}.

For
J_{11}
, we claim that

J_{11}=-\iint\left(A_{||}-(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt.

(5.7)

This is because

\iint(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt=\frac{1}{2}\iint(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}\nabla_{x}(u^{2})\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Psi^{2}t)dxdt,

and the last integral is 0 because
(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}
is a constant anti-symmetric matrix, and
\Psi^{2}t
is
C^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle J_{11}

\displaystyle=-\iint A_{||}^{s}\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt-\iint\left(A_{||}^{a}-(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}(\Psi^{2})ut\,dxdt

\displaystyle=:J_{111}+J_{112}.

For
J_{111}
, we have

\left|J_{111}\right|=2\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}^{s}\nabla_{x}u\,\Psi\,u\nabla_{x}\,\Psi\,t\,dxdt\right|\\
\leq\frac{2}{\lambda_{0}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|\left|\Psi\right|t^{1/2}\left|\nabla_{x}\Psi\right|t^{1/2}dxdt\\
\leq\sigma\lambda_{0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\Psi^{2}t\,dxdt+\frac{c}{\sigma}\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|^{2}t\,dxdt\leq\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|,

where in the first inequality we have used
\left\|A_{||}^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1}
and
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
, and in the last step we have used Lemma
4.5
. For
J_{112}
, by Hölder’s inequality,

\frac{1}{2}\left|J_{112}\right|=\left|\int_{2Q}\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left(A_{||}^{a}-(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}{\Psi}(u\Psi t)dtdx\right|\\
\leq\left(\int_{2Q}\left|A_{||}^{a}-(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}\right|^{\alpha^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{2Q}\Big(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla u\right|\left|\Psi t^{1/2}\right|\left|\nabla\Psi\right|t^{1/2}dt\Big)^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\\
\leq c\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left\|A_{||}^{a}\right\|_{\BMO}\Big\{\int_{2Q}\Big(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\Psi^{2}t\,dt\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|^{2}t\,dt\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

(5.8)

where
\alpha
is any number between 1 and 2. Now we use Hölder inequality with
p=\frac{2}{\alpha}
to bound (
5.8
) by

\displaystyle c\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{2Q}\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\Psi^{2}t\,dtdx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{2Q}\Big(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|^{2}t\,dt\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2\alpha}},

which by Lemma
4.5
can then be bounded by

\tilde{c}J^{1/2}\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{2-\alpha}{2\alpha}}=\tilde{c}J^{1/2}\left|Q\right|^{1/2}.

Then Young’s inequality gives

\left|J_{112}\right|\leq\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

Note that
J_{12}
and
J_{13}
can be estimated similar as (
5.7
). So both of then are bounded by
\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|
. Since
\left\|d\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1}
,
J_{14}
can be also bounded by
\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|
using Young’s inequality and Lemma
4.5
.

For
J_{2}
, we compute

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{0}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n+1}\,u\Psi^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(\mathbf{c}-(\mathbf{c}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}u(u\Psi^{2})dxdt-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}d\,\partial_{t}u(u\Psi^{2})dxdt

\displaystyle=:J_{21}+J_{22}.

For
J_{22}
, since
d
is
t
-independent, integration by parts gives

J_{22}=-\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}d\,\partial_{t}(u^{2})\Psi^{2}dxdt=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}d\,u^{2}\Psi\partial_{t}\Psi\,dxdt.

Thus
\left|J_{22}\right|\leq\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|
again by Lemma
4.5
.
For
J_{21}
, we write

J_{21}=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(\mathbf{c}-(\mathbf{c}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)dxdt\\
+\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(\mathbf{c}-(\mathbf{c}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\Psi(u^{2}\Psi)dxdt=:J_{211}+J_{212}.

Going further,

J_{212}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\mathbf{c}^{s}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Psi(u^{2}\Psi)dxdt+\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(\mathbf{c}^{a}-(\mathbf{c}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\Psi(u^{2}\Psi)dxdt\\
=:J_{2121}+J_{2122}.

Then again by Lemma
4.5
,
\left|J_{2121}\right|\leq\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|
. For
J_{2122}
,

\displaystyle\left|J_{2122}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2Q}\left|\mathbf{c}^{a}-(\mathbf{c}^{a})_{2Q}\right|\left(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla_{x}\Psi(u^{2}\Psi)\right|dt\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{2Q}\left|\mathbf{c}^{a}-(\mathbf{c}^{a})_{2Q}\right|^{\alpha^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|\left|u^{2}\Psi\right|dt\right)^{\alpha}\right)^{1/{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left|Q\right|^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla\Psi\right|dt\right)^{\alpha}dx\right)^{1/{\alpha}}\leq\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

For
J_{211}
, we use (
3.3
) to get

J_{211}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A^{*}_{||}\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)dxdt.

Recall that we defined
\theta_{\eta t}=\varphi-\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi
in Section
4.2
. We compute

J_{211}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}^{*}\nabla_{x}\theta_{\eta t}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Big(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\Big)dxdt+\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}^{*}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\Big(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\Big)dxdt\\
=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(A_{||}^{*}-(A_{||}^{*a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}\theta_{\eta t}\cdot\nabla_{x}\Big(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\Big)dxdt\\
+\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}^{*}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\Big(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\Big)dxdt=:J_{2111}+J_{2112},

where in the second equality we have used the assumption that the coefficients are smooth, which implies that
u^{2}
is smooth, and thus
(A_{||}^{*a})_{2Q}
being a constant anti-symmetric matrix gives

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}(A_{||}^{*a})_{2Q}\nabla_{x}\theta_{\eta t}\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)dxdt=0.

For
J_{2112}
, integration by parts with respect to
t
gives

J_{2112}=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}\left(A_{||}^{*}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)\right)t\,dxdt\\
=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}^{*}\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)t\,dxdt\\
-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}^{*}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)t\,dxdt=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

By the same reasoning as for (
5.7
), we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(A_{||}^{*}-(A_{||}^{*a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)t\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}A_{||}^{*s}\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)t\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(A_{||}^{*a}-(A_{||}^{*a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\right)t\,dxdt

\displaystyle=:I_{11}+I_{12}.

Then, applying Proposition
3.9
to the operator
L^{*}_{||}=-\divg A^{*}_{||}\nabla
, with
p=2
,

\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\right|^{2}t\,dxdt\right)^{1/2}\leq c\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

So by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and by (
3.5
),

\displaystyle\left|I_{11}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\right|^{2}t\,dxdt\right)^{1/2}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})\right|^{2}t\,dxdt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left|Q\right|^{1/2}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|^{2}\Psi^{2}\,t\,dxdt+\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}\Psi\right|^{2}t\,dxdt\right)^{1/2}.

Then Lemma
4.5
and Young’s inequality give

\displaystyle\left|I_{11}\right|\leq c\left|Q\right|^{1/2}\left(J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|\right)^{1/2}\leq\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

For
I_{12}
, we use Hölder inequality to get

\left|I_{12}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{2Q}\left|A_{||}^{*a}-(A_{||}^{*a})_{2Q}\right|^{\alpha^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\\
\times\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}^{*}\varphi\right|\left|\nabla_{x}\left(u^{2}\Psi^{2}\right)\right|t\,dt\right)^{\alpha}dx\Big\}^{1/{\alpha}}\\
\leq c\left|Q\right|^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}t\,dt\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}dx\right)^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\\
\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla_{x}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})\right|^{2}t\,dtdx\right)^{1/2}.

Letting
\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}=\frac{2+\epsilon_{0}}{2}
, then by Proposition
3.9
, (
3.5
) and Lemma
4.5
,

\displaystyle\left|I_{12}\right|\leq c\left|Q\right|^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left|Q\right|^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\left(J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|\right)^{1/2}\leq\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

For
I_{2}
, by the definition of
L^{*}_{||}
, we can write
I_{2}
as

I_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}L_{||}^{*}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\,\partial_{t}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})dx\,t\,dt.

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|\leq c\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|L^{*}_{||}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}t\,dtdx\right)^{1/2}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\partial_{t}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})\right|^{2}t\,dtdx\right)^{1/2}.

By Proposition
3.8
,

\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|L^{*}_{||}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}t\,dtdx\right)^{1/2}\leq\tilde{c}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

So by (
3.5
) and Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\partial_{t}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})\right|^{2}t\,dtdx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|^{1/2}(J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|)^{1/2}\leq\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

We now return to
J_{2111}
. Write

\displaystyle J_{2111}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(A_{||}^{*}-(A_{||}^{*a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}\theta_{\eta t}\cdot\nabla_{x}u(u\Psi^{2})dxdt

\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|t^{2}L_{||}^{*}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\leq c\eta^{-2}\left|Q\right|.

(5.18)

We write

III_{113}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\nabla_{x}(\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}u^{2}\Psi^{2})\cdot A^{*}_{||}\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\\
-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\nabla_{x}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})\cdot\left(A^{*}_{||}-(A_{||}^{a*})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\\
=\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}u^{2}\Psi^{2}L^{*}_{||}\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\\
-\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\nabla_{x}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})\cdot\left(A^{*}_{||}-(A_{||}^{a*})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi=:III_{1131}+III_{1132}.

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, Lemma
4.4
and (

),

\displaystyle\left|III_{1131}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t^{3}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|t^{2}L_{||}^{*}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c\left|Q\right|.

By (
5.15
),
\left|III_{1132}\right|
is bounded by

c\kappa_{0}\eta\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|A^{*}_{||}-(A_{||}^{a*})_{2Q}\right|\left|\nabla_{x}(u^{2}\Psi^{2})\right|\left|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|t\,dxdt,

which is bounded by
\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|
using the same method of estimating
I_{12}
.

Now it remains to estimate
III_{111}
. Note that the integration is over the support of
\Psi
instead of support of
\nabla\Psi
, so we cannot use the same method as estimating
II_{2}
. Like before, we only deal with the term involving
A_{||}^{a}-(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}
, as the term with the symmetric matrix

is easier to estimate. We have

\left|III^{a}_{11}\right|=\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left(A^{a}_{||}-(A_{||}^{a})_{2Q}\right)\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\cdot\nabla_{x}(u^{2})\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\,\Psi^{2}\right|\\
\leq c\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{2Q}\left(\int_{0}^{4l(Q)}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|\left|\nabla_{x}u\right|\Psi^{2}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|dt\right)^{\alpha}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\\
\leq c\left|Q\right|^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\left(\sigma J+\tilde{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(5.19)

We write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\\
\left\|\xi\right\|^{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-2}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\xi(x)\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dxdt}{t}\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}.

As before, let
\mathcal{D}^{\eta}_{k}
be the grid of dyadic cubes such that (
5.10
) holds. Then

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\xi(x)\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dxdt}{t}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{k}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\eta}_{k}}\int_{Q^{\prime}}\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{-k+1}}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\xi(x)\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dtdx}{t}.

(5.20)

By Corollary
3.1
, we bound (
5.20
) by

c\eta\sum_{k}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\eta}_{k}}\left(\int_{2Q^{\prime}}\int_{2^{-k-1}}^{2^{-k+1}}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t}\right)\\
\times\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{-k+1}}\frac{1}{\left|Q^{\prime}\right|}\int_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\left|\xi(x)\right|\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dtdx}{t}.

(5.21)

We now estimate the integral in the second line of (
5.21
).

Let
r=1+\epsilon
with
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small. We use Hölder’s inequality, then definition of
\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}
, and Corollary
3.2
as well as the reverse Hölder estimates for
\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}
, to get

\displaystyle\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{-k+1}}\frac{1}{\left|Q^{\prime}\right|}\int_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2}\left|\xi(x)\right|\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dtdx}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{-k+1}}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\nabla_{x}\widetilde{\theta}_{\eta t}\right|^{2r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\xi(x)\right|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\xi(x)\right|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{-k+1}}\Big\{\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}+\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}\Big\}\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\xi(x)\right|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}\int_{\frac{1}{2^{k}}}^{\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}}\Big\{\fint_{2Q^{\prime}}\left|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}dx+\eta^{-2}\left(\fint_{2Q^{\prime}}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\fint_{2Q^{\prime}}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}\Big\}\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\xi(x)\right|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}\int_{\frac{1}{2^{k}}}^{\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}}\Big\{\fint_{B(x_{0},\eta 2^{-k})}\left|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta^{-2}\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},\eta 2^{-k})}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{1/{r^{\prime}}}+\fint_{B(x_{0},\eta 2^{-k})}\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}dx\Big\}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\supp\Psi}dt}{t},

where in the last inequality we have used (
5.11
), with
x_{0}\in F
. Therefore, we can bound this by

c\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\xi(x)\right|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{-k+1}}\widetilde{N}^{\eta}(\nabla_{x}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi})(x_{0})^{2}+\eta^{-2}N^{\eta}(\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}_{\eta t}\widetilde{\varphi})(x_{0})^{2}\\
+M\left(\left|\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}\right|^{2}\right)(x_{0})\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq c\kappa_{0}^{2}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\xi(x)\right|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}.

So (
5.21
) is bounded by

\displaystyle c\eta\sum_{k}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\eta}_{k}}\left(\int_{2Q^{\prime}}\int_{2^{-k-1}}^{2^{-k+1}}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi\right|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t}\right)\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}\left|\xi(x)\right|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq c\eta\sum_{k}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\eta}_{k}}\int_{2Q^{\prime}}\left(M(\left|\xi\right|^{r})\right)^{1/r}(y)\int_{2^{-k-1}}^{2^{-k+1}}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi(y)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}dy

\displaystyle\leq c\eta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M(\left|\xi\right|^{r})(y)^{1/r}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi(y)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}dy

\displaystyle\leq c\eta\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M(\left|\xi\right|^{r})(y)^{q/r}dy\right)^{1/q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi(y)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/{q^{\prime}}}.

Choosing

, the above is bounded by

c\eta\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\xi\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-2}}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha-2}{\alpha}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\partial_{t}\mathcal{P}^{*}_{\eta t}\varphi(y)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}dy\right)^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}\\
\leq\tilde{c}\left\|\xi\right\|_{L^{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-2}}}\left|Q\right|^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}},

where in the last step we have used Proposition
3.8
.
Combining these estimates with (
5.19
), we obtain

\left|III_{111}^{a}\right|\leq c\left|Q\right|^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}(\sigma J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|)^{1/{\alpha}}\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}J+\tilde{c}\left|Q\right|.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.

6.
Proof of Uniqueness and Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove the uniqueness part in the statement of Theorem
1.1
. One can prove the uniqueness of
L^{p}
Dirichlet problem in bounded domains as in
[
17
]
Theorem 1.7.7. But that argument can not be modified to work for unbounded domains. We present here a different and simpler proof that works in a rather general setting.

Recall that we have proved that for any cube
Q_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\omega^{X_{Q_{0}}}\in A_{\infty}(Q_{0})
, which implies that there is some
q\in(1,\infty)
such that the Radon-Nikodym derivative
k(X_{Q_{0}},\cdot)
satifies the reverse Hölder inequality (
2.4
).
We now show that we have the following non-tangential maximal function estimate:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p\geq q^{\prime}
, where
q
is the exponent in the reverse Hölder inequality (
2.4
). If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
and
u(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)k(X,y)d\mu(y)
, then

\left\|Nu\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)}.

(6.1)

Moreover,
u
converges non-tangentially
\mu
- a.e. to
f
.

Proof.

We first note that (
6.1
) may be obtained as in the proof of Lemma 5.32 in
[
12
]
. Indeed, the argument in
[
12
]
relies only on Hölder continuity of solutions, Harnack principle, and comparison
principle. The coefficients (in
\BMO
) do not affect the argument since the equation is not used explicitly.
It therefore suffices to show that
u
converges non-tangentially
\mu
-a.e. to
f
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, choose
f_{\epsilon}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\left\|f-f_{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}<\epsilon
. Define
u_{\epsilon}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\epsilon}(y)k(X,y)d\mu(y)
. Then
u_{\epsilon}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}})
and
u_{\epsilon}=f_{\epsilon}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We note that the latter fact may be gleaned from the analogous fact on bounded domains (see
[
18
]
), the construction at the beginning of Section 2 (applied with
u=u_{\epsilon}
), and an equicontinuity argument using
[
18
]
Lemma 3.9, and Lemma 4.5.
So

\lim_{\Gamma(x)\ni(y,t)\to(x,0)}u_{\epsilon}(y,t)=f_{\epsilon}(x)\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since we have the non-tangential convergence for a dense class, the non-tangential convergence of
u
follows from (
6.1
) and a standard argument. In fact, we have

\limsup_{\Gamma(x)\ni(y,t)\rightarrow(x,0)}|u(y,t)-f(x)|\leq\left|N\left(u-u_{\epsilon}\right)(x)\right|+\left|\left(f-f_{\epsilon}\right)(x)\right|\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For any
\lambda>0
, we apply Chebyshev’s inequality and (
6.1
) to get

\displaystyle\mu\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\limsup_{\Gamma(x)\ni(y,t)\rightarrow(x,0)}|u(y,t)-f(x)|>\lambda\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:N\left(u-u_{\epsilon}\right)(x)>\lambda/2\right\}\right)+\mu\left(\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|\left(f-f_{\epsilon}\right)(x)\right|>\lambda/2\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-p}\left(\left\|N(u-u_{\epsilon})\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)}+\left\|f-f_{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-p}\left\|f-f_{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)}^{p}\lesssim\epsilon\lambda^{-p}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, it shows that
\lim_{\Gamma(x)\ni(y,t)\rightarrow(x,0)}u(y,t)=f(x)
for
\mu
- a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

The
L^{p}
boundedness of the non-tangential maximal function implies certain decay properties. To be precise, we have the following

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
u(x,t)
be a function in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
. If there exists some constant
C
such that
\left\|Nu\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<C
for some
p>0
, then
u
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\left|u(x,t)\right|<C^{\prime}t^{-\frac{n}{p}}
for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, where the constant
C^{\prime}
only depends on
n
and
C
.

(2)

For any
\epsilon>0
, any
\delta>0
, there exists some
R_{0}=R_{0}(u,\epsilon,\delta)>1
such that for all
\left|x\right|\geq R_{0}
and
t\geq\delta
, we have
\left|u(x,t)\right|<\epsilon
.

Proof.

To see (
1
), we observe for any fixed
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, for all
y\in\Delta(x,t)
,
(x,t)\in\Gamma(y)
. So we have

\displaystyle\left|u(x,t)\right|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{\left|\Delta(x,t)\right|}\int_{\Delta(x,t)}Nu(y)^{p}d\mu(y)\leq C_{n}C^{p}t^{-n}.

We prove (
2
) by contradiction. If this is not true, then there exist some
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
\left|x_{k}\right|\geq k
and
t_{k}\geq\delta
, for which
\left|u(x_{k},t_{k})\right|\geq\epsilon
. Since
t_{k}\geq\delta
,
(x_{k},t_{k})\in\Gamma(y)
for all
y\in\Delta(x_{k},\delta)
. This implies that

Nu(y)\geq u(x_{k},t_{k})\geq\epsilon\qquad\forall\,y\in\Delta(x_{k},\delta).

Choose a subsequence
x_{k_{j}}
so that the collection of surface balls
\{\Delta(x_{k_{j}},\delta)\}
is pairwise disjoint. Then

C^{p}>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|Nu(y)\right|^{p}d\mu(y)\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\Delta(x_{k_{j}},\delta)}\epsilon^{p}d\mu(y)=C_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\epsilon^{p}\delta^{n}=\infty,

which yields a contradiction.
∎

We now prove the uniqueness of the
L^{p}
Dirichlet problem.

Proof of uniqueness.

Fix
q
so that
k(X,\cdot)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
as in (
2.4
), and let
p=\frac{q}{q-1}
. We show that if
u
is a solution of
(D)_{p}
, that is,

\begin{cases}Lu=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1},\\
u\to f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)\text{ non-tangentially }\mu\text{-a.e. on }\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
Nu\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu),\end{cases}

then

u(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(y)k(X,y)d\mu(y)\qquad\text{for some }g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu).

(6.2)

Then by Lemma
6.1
,
u
converges non-tangentially
\mu
-a.e. to
g
. This implies that

u(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)k(X,y)d\mu(y)
, which proves that the solution is unique. We now show (
6.2
).

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, set
f_{m}(x):=u(x,\frac{1}{m})
. Note that by the interior estimates for weak solutions,
f_{m}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover,

\left\|f_{m}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sup_{t>0}\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\left\|Nu\right\|_{L^{p}}<\infty.

(6.3)

Since
Nu\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can apply Lemma
6.2
(2) and get

\lim_{\left|x\right|\to\infty}f_{m}(x)=0,\qquad\left\|f_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

(6.4)

We define

u_{m}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{m}(y)k\left((x,t),y\right)d\mu(y),\text{ and }\,\delta_{m}(x,t):=u(x,t+\frac{1}{m})-u_{m}(x,t).

Since
f_{m}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and satisfies (
6.4
), from the definition of elliptic measures it follows that

\left\|u_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+})}\leq\left\|f_{m}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(6.5)

Moreover, we claim that
u_{m}
is a solution to the continuous Dirichlet problem, with
data
f_{m}
; in particular,
u_{m}(x,0)=f_{m}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To see this, for
R>0
and large, let

\Phi_{R}
be a smooth
cut-off function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, identically
1
in
\Delta(0,R)
, supported in
\Delta(0,2R)
,
with
0\leq\Phi_{R}\leq 1
. Set
f_{m,R}:=f_{m}\Phi_{R}
, and let
u_{m,R}
be the elliptic measure
solution with data
f_{m,R}
. Then
u_{m,R}(\cdot,0)=f_{m,R}
continuously, since the data
belongs to
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,
u_{m,R}(x,0)=f_{m}(x)
for all
|x|<R
.
Given
\epsilon>0
, we note that by Lemma
6.2
(2),
|f_{m}(x)-f_{m,R}(x)|\leq\epsilon
, for
all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, provided that
R
is large enough, hence also
|u_{m,R}(x,t)-u_{m}(x,t)|\leq\epsilon
,
since elliptic measure has total mass
1
. The claim now follows.
This means that

\delta_{m}(x,0)=0\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(6.6)

Notice that
\delta_{m}
is a solution to
Lv=0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, which vanishes continuously on
\{t\equiv 0\}
. We claim that
\delta_{m}\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
. To prove this claim, we observe that by the maximum principle, it suffices to show that

\lim_{|x|+t\to\infty}\big|u\big(x,t+\frac{1}{m}\big)\big|+|u_{m}(x,t)|=0.

For
u\big(x,t+\frac{1}{m}\big)
, this follows immediately from Lemma
6.2
and our assumption that
Nu\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To see that decay to
0
holds for
u_{m}
,
we define
f_{m,R},u_{m,R}
as above.
Given
\epsilon>0
, fix
R
so that
\left\|f_{m}-f_{m,R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\epsilon
, hence also
\left\|u_{m}-u_{m,R}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1})}<\epsilon
.
By Hölder continuity at the boundary, we may choose
\delta>0
small enough
that for
|x|>3R
, and
t<\delta
,
we have

|u_{m,R}(x,t)|\lesssim\delta^{\alpha}||f_{m}||_{\infty}<\epsilon,

and thus also
|u_{m}(x,t)|<2\epsilon
. Moreover, with this value of
\delta
now fixed, it
follows immediately from (
6.3
), the definition of
u_{m}
and (
6.1
), and Lemma
6.2
, that

\lim_{|x|+t\to\infty}|u_{m}(x,t)|\mathbbm{1}_{[\delta,\infty)}(t)=0.

We conclude
that
\delta_{m}\equiv 0
. In turn, the latter is equivalent to

u(x,t+\frac{1}{m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{m}(y)k((x,t),y)d\mu(y),\qquad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{N}.

(6.7)

Since
\sup_{m}\left\|f_{m}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\left\|Nu\right\|_{L^{p}}<\infty
, there is some
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
and
\{f_{m^{\prime}}\}
such that
f_{m^{\prime}}
converges to
g
weakly. Note that
k(X,\cdot)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
(see (
2.5
)), so by letting
m^{\prime}
go to infinity in (
6.7
) we obtain (
6.2
).

From the proof of uniqueness, one can see that we have actually proved the stronger result, Theorem
1.2
. In fact, we did not use
u\to f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
non-tangentially
\mu
-a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to obtain (
6.2
). Once we express
u
as in (
6.2
), we apply Lemma
6.1
to conclude that the non-tangential limit of
u
exists
\mu
- a.e. and is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},d\mu)
.

Appendix A
Appendix: Weak solution of parabolic equations

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose
u,v\in L^{2}\left((0,T),W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
with
\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}\left((0,T),\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
. Then

(1)

u\in C\left([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
;

(2)

The mapping
t\mapsto\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is absolutely continuous, with

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=2\Re\langle\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t),u(\cdot,t)\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

As a consequence,

\frac{d}{dt}\left(u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t)\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\langle\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t),v(\cdot,t)\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}+\overline{\langle{\partial_{t}v(\cdot,t),u(\cdot,t)\rangle}}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\,\text{a.e.}

For the proof see, e.g.,
[
8
]
, Section 5.9.2, Theorem 3.

Suppose that
A=A(x)=A^{s}(x)+A^{a}(x)
is a real,
n\times n
matrix, with
A^{s}
being symmetric, elliptic with constant
\lambda_{0}>0
,
\left\|A^{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1}
, and
A^{a}
being anti-symmetric and
\left\|A^{a}\right\|_{\BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\Lambda_{0}
.

Proposition A.1
.

For any
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\divg(A\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(A.1)

has a unique weak solution
u(x,t)=e^{-tL}(u_{0})(x)
. Here,
\divg=\divg_{x}
and
\nabla=\nabla_{x}
.

Proof.

Existence.

Since the domain of
L
(denoted by
D(L)
) is dense in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and thus dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can find a sequence
\left\{u_{0,\epsilon}\right\}\subset D(L)
such that
u_{0,\epsilon}
converges to
u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Denote
u_{\epsilon}(x,t):=e^{-tL}(u_{0,\epsilon})(x)
. Then by semigroup theory,

\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}+Lu_{\epsilon}=0\quad\text{in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

(A.2)

For any
0<\tau<T
, and any
\varphi\in L^{2}\left((0,T),W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
, with
\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}\left((0,T),\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
, (
A.2
) implies

\int_{\tau}^{T}\left(\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon},\varphi\right)_{L^{2}}dt+\int_{\tau}^{T}\left(Lu_{\epsilon},\varphi\right)_{L^{2}}dt=0.

(A.3)

Since
\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}\in L^{2}_{\loc}\left((0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
(see
[
13
]
Theorem 4.9), and by Lemma
A.1
(ii), (
A.3
) can be written as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{\epsilon}(x,T)\overline{\varphi(x,T)}dx+\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}dxdt\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{\epsilon}(x,\tau)\overline{\varphi(x,\tau)}dx+\int_{\tau}^{T}\overline{\langle\partial_{t}\varphi,u_{\epsilon}\rangle}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}.

(A.4)

Notice that
u_{\epsilon}\to u
in
C((\tau,T),W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
(see
[
13
]
Theorem 4.9), and so letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x,T)\overline{\varphi(x,T)}dx+\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}dxdt\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x,\tau)\overline{\varphi(x,\tau)}dx+\int_{\tau}^{T}\overline{\langle\partial_{t}\varphi,u\rangle}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}.

(A.5)

Letting
\varphi=u_{\epsilon}
in (
A.4
) and applying Lemma
A.1
(ii) again, one obtains

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u_{\epsilon}(x,T)\right|^{2}dx+2\Re\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla u_{\epsilon}\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{\epsilon}}dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|u_{\epsilon}(x,\tau)\right|^{2}dx.

By ellipticity and the definition of
u_{\epsilon}
, we have

2\lambda_{0}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\right|^{2}dxdt\leq\left\|e^{-\tau L}(u_{0,\epsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\\
\leq 2\left\|e^{-\tau L}(u_{0,\epsilon}-u_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+2\left\|e^{-\tau L}(u_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
,
\tau\to 0^{+}
,
T\to\infty
, we obtain
\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dxdt\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty
. This enables us to take limit as
\tau
go to
0^{+}
on both sides of (
A.5
) and get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x,T)\overline{\varphi(x,T)}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}dxdt\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x,0)\overline{\varphi(x,0)}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\overline{\langle\partial_{t}\varphi,u\rangle}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}},

i.e.
u(x,t)
is a weak solution of (
A.1
).

Uniqueness.

Let
v
be a weak solution of (
A.1
). We first show that

\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}\left((0,T),\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
for any
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Define a semilinear functional
F
on
L^{2}\left([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
as follows:
for any
\varphi\in L^{2}\left([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
, let

\langle F,\varphi\rangle:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla v\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}dxdt.

Obviously,

\left|\langle F,\varphi\rangle\right|\leq C\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)}.

Then by Riesz representation theorem, there exists
w(x,t)\in L^{2}\left([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
such that

\displaystyle\langle F,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla w\cdot\nabla\overline{\varphi}+w\overline{\varphi})dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle-\Delta w(\cdot,t)+w(\cdot,t),\varphi\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}dt,

and

\left\|-\Delta w+w\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T],\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)}\leq\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)}.

Choose
\varphi(x,t)=\Psi(x)\overline{\eta}(t)
as a test function in (
A.1
), where
\Psi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\eta\in C_{0}^{1}\left((0,T)\right)
. Then since
v
is a weak solution, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(v(\cdot,t),\Psi\right)_{L^{2}}\eta^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla v\cdot\overline{\nabla\Psi}\eta(t)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle-\Delta w(\cdot,t)+w(\cdot,t),\Psi\rangle_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}\eta(t)dt.

Since
\Psi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is arbitrary,

\int_{0}^{T}v(x,t)\eta^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{T}(-\Delta w+w)\eta(t)dt\qquad\text{in }\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which gives
\partial_{t}v=\Delta w-w\in L^{2}\left((0,T),\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
. Therefore, we can take
\varphi=v
as a test function in (
A.1
) and get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(x,T)\right|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla v\cdot\nabla\overline{v}dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\overline{\langle\partial_{t}v,v\rangle}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(x,0)\right|^{2}dx.

Using this and Lemma
A.1
(ii), we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(x,T)\right|^{2}+2\Re\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla v\cdot\nabla\overline{v}dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(x,0)\right|^{2}dx.

So we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(x,T)\right|^{2}+2\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\nabla v\right|^{2}dxdt\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|v(x,0)\right|^{2}dx,

which implies that if
v(x,0)=0
then
v\equiv 0
.
∎

Remark A.1
.

Let
u(x,t)
be the weak solution to (
A.1
). Since the coefficients are independent of
t
, a standard argument shows that
\partial_{t}u
is a weak solution to
\partial_{t}v-\divg(A\nabla v)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
. That is, for any
T>0
, any
\varphi\in L^{2}\left([0,T],W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
with
\partial_{t}\varphi\in L^{2}\left([0,T],\widetilde{W}^{-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
and
\varphi=0
when
0\leq t\leq\varepsilon
for some
0<\varepsilon<T
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}u(x,T)\overline{\varphi(x,T)}dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A\nabla(\partial_{t}u)\cdot\overline{\nabla\varphi}dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\overline{\langle\partial_{t}\varphi,\partial_{t}u\rangle}_{\widetilde{W}^{-1,2},W^{1,2}}dt.

Moreover, since
\partial_{t}^{l}u\in L^{2}_{\loc}\left((0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
and
\partial_{t}^{l}\nabla u\in L^{2}_{\loc}\left((0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
one can show that for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\partial_{t}^{l}u
is a weak solution to
\partial_{t}v-\divg(A\nabla v)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
.

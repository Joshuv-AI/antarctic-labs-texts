On the tensor nuclear norm and the total variation regularization for image and video completion
A.H Bentbib, A. El Hachimi, K. Jbilou, A. Ratnani

Abstract
In the present paper we propose two new algorithms of tensor completion for three-order tensors. The proposed methods consist in minimizing the average rank of the underlying tensor using its approximate function namely the tensor nuclear norm and then the recovered data will be obtained by using the total variation regularisation technique. We will adopt the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADM), using the tensor T-product, to solve the main optimization problems associated to the two algorithms. In the last section, we present some numerical experiments and comparisons with the most known image completion methods.



keywords

ADM, Tensor completion, Tensor nuclear norm, T-product, T-SVD.

In this work, we will be interested in the problem of tensor completion, with the aim of estimating the missing values from an observed data, e.g., inpainting color images

The main idea behind the completion problem is to find a low-rank data containing the main information of the original data. For matrices, the problem of completion is mathematically formulated as follows

\displaystyle\underset{A}{min}

\displaystyle\;rank(A)

(1)

\displaystyle s.t

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}(A)=P_{\Omega}(M),

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
is the underlying matrix,
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
is the observed matrix,
\Omega
is the set of known pixels and
P_{\Omega}
is the projection operator onto
\Omega
. This optimization problem is not easy to solve because of the non-convexity of the rank function . For that reason, Fazel
[
6
]
, and Kurucz
[
13
]
proposed to solve the problem (
1
) by using the convex surrogate of the rank and the SVD decomposition. In
[
9
]
, Candes and Recht proved theoretically that under certain conditions, the following optimization problem

\displaystyle\underset{A}{min}

\displaystyle\;\left\|A\right\|_{*}

(2)

\displaystyle s.t

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}(A)=P_{\Omega}(M)

recovers well the data, where
\left\|A\right\|_{*}
is the nuclear norm of
A
which will be defined later. Since tensors are the generalization of matrices, the problem of tensor completion can be formulated as follows

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{A}}{min}

\displaystyle\;rank(\mathcal{A})

(3)

\displaystyle s.t

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{M})

where
\mathcal{A}
is the underlying tensor of order 3,
\mathcal{M}
is the observed tensor,
\Omega
is the set of the known data and
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
is the projection operator defined by

\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A})_{i,j,k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{A}_{i,j,k}&\;,\;(i,j,k)\in\Omega\\
0&\;\;otherwise.\end{array}\right.

As stated in
[
22
]
, the optimization problem (
3

2

Notice that the notion of tensor rank is complicated as compared to the matrix rank and many tensor rank definitions and procedures such as Tucker-rank
[

[

We will show how to use the tensor nuclear norm in combination with the TV regularisation procedure to derive the new completion algorithms. In the two approaches, we will use the well known tensor T-product. Some numerical experiments with comparisons to the most well known methods are presented in Section

2.4
The tensor nuclear norm.

We first recall the matrix nuclear norm.
Let

be a matrix, then the nuclear norm denoted by

\left\|B\right\|

We notice that we also have

\|A\|_{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}

where
\left\{\sigma_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{r}

A
and
r

A

Definition 5
.

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

Also, from (
7

The tensor nuclear norm is an extension of the matrix nuclear norm to tensors and is defined in the following definition

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

The proposed methods

Our proposed approaches are based on the minimization of the average rank of a three-order tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. The problem can be formulated as follows

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{A}}{min}

\displaystyle\;{\tt rank_{a}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

(22)

\displaystyle s.t

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{M}).

where
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
is the underlying tensor,
\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
is the observed tensor and
\Omega
is the set of the known pixels.
As stated in
[
1
]
and thanks to Theorem
7
, we can replace the problem (
22
) by the following one

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{A}}{min}

\displaystyle\;\left\|\mathcal{A}\right\|_{*}

(23)

\displaystyle s.t

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{M})

where
\left\|.\right\|_{*}
is the tensor nuclear norm defined above.
It is known that in real world, the problem (
23
) can be very ill-conditioned and one needs regularisation techniques such as the
(\textbf{TV})
-regularisation which we will consider in the present paper. As in the matrix case, other regularization procedures are also possible.

3.1
Tensor completion using the tensor nuclear norm and the first order total variation

For our first approach, we consider the TV-regularized problem

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{A}}{min}

\displaystyle\;\left\|\mathcal{A}\right\|_{*}+\lambda\textbf{TV}_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

(24)

\displaystyle s.t

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(\mathcal{M})

where
\lambda
is a regularization parameter, and

\textbf{TV}_{1}(\mathcal{A})=\left[TV_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)})|\,TV_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{(2)}|\,...|\,TV_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{(n_{3})})\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}},

with

TV_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{(n)})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}\sqrt{\left(D_{1}^{1}\mathcal{A}^{(n)}\right)_{i,j}^{2}+\left(D_{1}^{2}\mathcal{A}^{(n)}\right)_{i,j}^{2}},\;n\in\{1,2,...,n_{3}\}

and
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are the derivative operators in the first and the second direction, respectively, with

D_{1}^{1}\mathcal{A}^{(n)}=\mathcal{A}^{(n)}C_{n_{2}}^{1},\;\;\;\;D_{1}^{2}\mathcal{A}^{(n)}=C_{n_{1}}^{2}\mathcal{A}^{(n)}

where
C_{1}
and

\displaystyle L(\mathcal{A}^{*},\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle\leq L(\mathcal{A}^{*},\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{Q}^{*},\mathcal{B}^{*})\leq L(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{Q}^{*},\mathcal{B}^{*})

(48)

\displaystyle\forall\left(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{B}\right)\in\mathbb{E}.

The next theorem gives some convergence results on the sequences obtained from Algorithm
4
.

Theorem 11
.

Assume that
(\mathcal{A}^{*},\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{Q}^{*},\mathcal{B}^{*})
is a saddle point of
L
. The sequence
(\mathcal{X}^{k},\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{W}^{k},\mathcal{Q}^{k},\mathcal{B}^{k})
generated by Algorithm
4
satisfies:

1.

\underset{k\to+\infty}{lim}F(\mathcal{Z}^{k})+G(\mathcal{W}^{k})=F(\mathcal{Z}^{*})+G(\mathcal{W}^{*}).

2.

\underset{k\to+\infty}{lim}\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{k}\right\|=0.

3.

\underset{k\to+\infty}{lim}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{k}\right\|=0.

Proof 1
.

From the first inequality of (
48
) we get

\left<\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{Q}\right>+\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{B}\right>\leq\left<\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\right>+\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{B}^{*}\right>\;\;\forall(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{W})

(49)

which gives

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{A}^{*}=\mathcal{Z}^{*}\\
\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{*}=\mathcal{W}^{*}.\end{array}\right.

Let us define the following quantities

\bar{\mathcal{X}}^{k}=\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{A}^{*},\;\bar{\mathcal{Z}}^{k}=\mathcal{Z}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{*},\;\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}=\mathcal{W}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{*},\;\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k}=\mathcal{Q}^{k}-\mathcal{Q}^{*},\;\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k}=\mathcal{B}^{k}-\mathcal{B}^{*}.

The main idea of the proof is to show that the sequence
\left(\beta_{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{1}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)_{k\geq 0}
is decreasing. Notice that

\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k+1}=\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k}-\beta_{1}\left(\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}\right),\;\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k+1}=\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}-\beta_{2}\left(\mathcal{D}_{1}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k}\right).

We have

\displaystyle\left(\left\|\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)-\left(\left\|\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\left\|\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\beta_{1}\left<\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k},\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}\right>-2\beta_{1}\left<\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k},\mathcal{D}_{1}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}\right>

(50)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\beta_{2}^{2}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

From the second inequality of (
48
) we obtain the following inequalities for
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W})=(\mathcal{A}^{k},\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{W}^{k})

\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\left<\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\right>+\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{B}^{*}\right>+\beta_{1}\left<\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{*},\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{A}^{*}\right>+\beta_{2}\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{A}^{*})\right>\geq 0

F(\mathcal{Z}^{k})-F(\mathcal{Z}^{*})+\left<\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{Y}^{k},\mathcal{Q}^{*}\right>+\beta_{1}\left<\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{Y}^{k},\mathcal{Y}^{*}-\mathcal{Y}^{k}\right>\geq 0.

G(\mathcal{W}^{k})-F(\mathcal{W}^{*})+\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{k},\mathcal{B}^{*}\right>+\beta_{2}\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{k},\mathcal{W}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{k}\right>\geq 0.

Using (
28
), we get for
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W})=(\mathcal{A}^{k},\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{W}^{k})
that

\left<\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{Q}^{k}\right>+\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{k},\mathcal{B}^{k}\right>+\dfrac{\beta_{1}}{2}\left<\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{Z}^{*}-\mathcal{Z}^{k}\right>+\beta_{2}\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{k},\mathcal{W}^{*}-\mathcal{W}^{k}\right>\geq 0

F(\mathcal{Z}^{*})-F(\mathcal{Z}^{k})+\left<\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Y}^{*},\mathcal{Q}^{k}\right>+\beta_{1}\left<\mathcal{X}^{k}-\mathcal{Y}^{*},\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{A}^{*}\right>\geq 0.

G(\mathcal{W}^{*})-G(\mathcal{W}^{k})+\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{B}^{k}\right>+\beta_{2}\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{*},\mathcal{D}_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{A}^{*})\right>\geq 0.

By regrouping terms we get

-\left<\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k},\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}\right>-\left<\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k},\mathcal{D}_{1}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}\right>\geq\beta_{1}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{2}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}

then

\left(\beta_{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{1}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)-\left(\beta_{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{1}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k+1}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)\geq\beta_{1}^{2}\beta_{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\beta_{1}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Thus, the sequence
\left(\beta_{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{1}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{B}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)_{k\geq 0}
is decreasing, which gives

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\beta_{1}^{2}\beta_{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\beta_{1}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\bar{\mathcal{A}}^{k}-\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)\leq\beta_{2}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{0}\right\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{1}\left\|\bar{\mathcal{W}}^{0}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Therefore

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\left(\mathcal{Q}^{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}\;and\;\left(\mathcal{B}^{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}\;are\;bounded\\
\underset{k\to+\infty}{lim}\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{k}\right\|=0.\\
\underset{k\to+\infty}{lim}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{k}\right\|=0.\end{array}\right.

In addition, by using again the second inequality of (
48
) for
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{W})=(\mathcal{A}^{k},\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{W}^{k})
we obtain

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{Z}^{*})+G(\mathcal{W}^{*})\leq F(\mathcal{Z}^{k})+G(\mathcal{W}^{k})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{Q}^{k}\right>+\dfrac{\beta_{1}}{2}\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{k},\mathcal{B}^{k}\right>+\dfrac{\beta_{2}}{2}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2},

and

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{Z}^{*})+G(\mathcal{W}^{*})\geq F(\mathcal{Z}^{k})+G(\mathcal{W}^{k})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{k},\mathcal{Q}^{k}\right>+\dfrac{\beta_{1}}{2}\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{Z}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{k},\mathcal{B}^{k}\right>+\dfrac{\beta_{2}}{2}\left\|\mathcal{D}_{1}\mathcal{A}^{k}-\mathcal{W}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Hence

\underset{k\to+\infty}{lim\,sup}\;F(\mathcal{Z}^{k})+G(\mathcal{W}^{k})\leq F(\mathcal{A}^{*})+G(\mathcal{W}^{*})\leq\underset{k\to+\infty}{lim\,inf}F(\mathcal{Z}^{k})+G(\mathcal{W}^{k}).

\dfrac{\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k+1}-\mathcal{A}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}}{\left\|\mathcal{A}^{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}}\leq 10^{-4}.

6
Conclusion

In this paper we proposed two methods for image completion by combining the tensor nuclear norm and the total variation regularization approaches. We showed how to compute the different tensor sequences obtained from different optimisation problems and gave some convergence theoretical results. The numerical experiments show that our two approaches are efficient and very competitive as compared to other recent completion methods.

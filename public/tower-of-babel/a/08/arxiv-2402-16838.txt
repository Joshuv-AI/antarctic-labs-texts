Abstract
. We study the topological recurrence phenomenon of actions of locally compact abelian groups on compact metric spaces. In the case of ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} -actions we develop new techniques to analyze Bohr recurrence sets. These techniques include finding and manipulating correlations between the coordinates of the set of recurrence. Using this, we show that Bohr recurrence sets are recurrence sets for ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} -Weyl systems. This family encompasses, for example, all ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} -affine nilsystems. To our knowledge, this is the first result towards a positive answer to Katznelson question in the case of ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} -actions.

Introduction

The study of the phenomenon of recurrence in ergodic theory has a rich history. Its origins can be traced back to H. Poincaré’s work in the late 19th century on the three-body problem, where he discovered that trajectories in certain dynamical systems tend to return to their starting points even in chaotic or unpredictable scenarios. Poincaré’s Recurrence Theorem formalizes this observation, stating that for a measure-preserving transformation
T
on a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
and a set
A\in\mathcal{X}
with
\mu(A)>0
, for almost any point
x\in A
there exists a sequence
(n_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
T^{n_{i}}x\in A
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

The recurrence phenomenon has served to prove various deep results in different areas of mathematics. Since the works of Furstenberg
[
12
]
, and Furstenberg and Weiss
[
13
]
, there has been a profound and fruitful connection between dynamical systems and number theory through recurrence properties, and, more specifically, the notion of set of recurrence (see
3.1
) playing a central role in both areas.
Within this context, the class of nilsystems has proved to be particularly important. Nilsystems have been used, for example, in the ergodic context of multiple convergence along arithmetic progressions
[
20
]
and in the topological context to construct explicit examples of sets with multiple recurrence

2.
Notation, terminology, and prerequisites

2.1.
Topological Dynamical Systems

A topological dynamical system, or just an
H
-system, is a pair
(X,H)
where
X
is a compact metric space and
H
is a locally compact group of homeomorphisms of the space
X
into itself, which for our purposes we will suppose abelian. The action of an element
g\in H
on a point
x\in X
is denoted by
g(x)
or
gx
, and the orbit of a point
x
under the action of
H
is represented by
\mathcal{O}_{H}(x)=\{hx:h\in H\}
(or simply
\mathcal{O}(x)
if the acting group is clear).

A topological dynamical system
(X,H)
is
transitive
if there is a point
x\in X
which orbit
\mathcal{O}_{H}(x)
is dense in
X
. We call such points
transitive points
. A stronger condition than transitivity is
minimality
. A system
(X,H)
is said to be
minimal
if every point in
X
is transitive. In other words, there are no nonempty closed
H
-invariant proper subsets of
X
. A subset
Y\subseteq X
is
H
-invariant if
HY=Y
.

We say that
(Y,H)
is a
subsystem
of
(X,H)
if
Y
is a nonempty closed
H
-invariant subset of
X
. An essential fact is that every dynamical system has a minimal subsystem.

A system
(X,H)
is
equicontinuous
if for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
x,y\in X
,
d_{X}(x,y)<\delta
implies
d_{X}(hx,hy)<\epsilon
, for all
h\in H
(
d_{X}
denotes a metric in
X
). A pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
is called
proximal
if there exists a sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq H
such that

d(h_{n}x,h_{n}y)\to 0
as
n
goes to infinity. If there is no such sequence, the pair
(x,y)
is said to be
distal
. A system is distal if it does not have any nontrivial proximal pairs.

A factor map between topological dynamical systems
(X,H)
and
(Y,H)
is an onto continuous map
\pi\colon X\to Y
such that
\pi\circ h=h\circ\pi
for all
h\in H
. In this case,
X
is called an extension of
Y
and
Y
a factor of
X
. If
\pi
is also injective, we say that
(X,H)
and
(Y,H)
are topologically conjugate (or just conjugate), and
\pi
is called a conjugacy. An extension map
\pi:X\to Y
is called
proximal
if every pair of point
(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X
with
\pi(x_{1})=\pi(x_{2})
are proximal.

Let
\{(X_{n},H)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of topological dynamical systems such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is a factor map
\pi_{n+1}:(X_{n+1},H)\to(X_{n},H)
. The
inverse limit
of
\{(X_{n},H)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of these factors is defined as the set

\Bigl\{(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}~:~\pi_{n+1}(x_{n+1})=x_{n}~\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\Bigr\},

equipped with the product topology and the diagonal action of
H
given by
h(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=(hx_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for every
h\in H
.

A measure preserving system (m.p.s. for short)
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,H)
consists of a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
endowed with a
\mathcal{X}
-measurable and measure preserving action of
H
. That is, for any
A\in\mathcal{X}
and
h\in H
,
hA\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mu(hA)=\mu(A)
. A set
A\in\mathcal{X}
is
invariant
if
\mu(A\Delta hA)=0
for all
h\in H
. A m.p.s.
(X,\mu,H)
is called
ergodic
if there are no nontrivial invariant sets, meaning that if
A\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mu(A\Delta hA)=0
for all
h\in H
, then
\mu(A)\in\{0,1\}
.

2.2.
Nilsystems

Let
G
be a group. For
a,b\in G
we define their commutator as
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
. Given subgroups
A,B\subseteq G
, we write
[A,B]
to denote the subgroup of
G
generated by
\{[a,b]:a\in A,b\in B\}
. We define recursively the commutator subgroups
G_{j}
of
G
as:

G_{1}=G,~~~G_{j+1}=[G,G_{j}],~~j\geq 1.

We say that a group
G
is nilpotent of order
s
or
s
-step nilpotent if
G_{s+1}=\{e_{G}\}
, where
e_{G}
is the identity of
G
.

Let
G
be an
s
-step nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
a discrete cocompact subgroup of
G
(i.e.,
\Gamma
is countable and
G/\Gamma
is compact). Then, we say that the compact nilmanifold
X=G/\Gamma
is an
s
-step nilmanifold.
Let
H\subseteq G
be a subgroup and consider the action of
H
given by left translations. Then
(X,H)
is a
H
-system, also called an
s
-step nilsystem, or to be more precise, an
s
-step
H
-nilsystem. We will always consider the Haar measure
\mu
of
(X,H)
(the unique invariant measure for the action of left translations).

Example 2.1

(Rotations)
.

Let
X
be a compact abelian group,
H
be a group, and
\varphi\colon H\to X
be a homomorphism. Consider the action of
H
on
X
induced by
\varphi
, that is,
h\cdot x=\varphi(h)+x
, for all
h\in H
and
x\in X
, where the group operation of

is written in additive notation. Then
(X,H)
is a
1
-step nilsystem, and this system is usually called a rotation.

For a nilpotent Lie group
G
we denote by
G_{0}
the connected component of the identity
e_{G}
in
G
. Then,
G_{0}
is an open normal subgroup of
G
(see
[
22
, Section 4.1]
). Various representations of nilsystems are available, some of them specifically tailored to address specific problems and contexts. One possible choice allows us to assume that
G
is simply connected, which means
that

is simply connected (see
[
21
, Chapter 10, Theorem 13]
). Another property of nilsystems is that they are all distal (
[
3

).

Now we give some important structural properties of nilsystems that will be useful in the next chapters.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
28
]
)

Let
(X=G/\Gamma,H)
be an
s
-step nilsystem.

(1)

The groups

.

(2)

Let

Proposition 2.3

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
(X=G/\Gamma,H)
be an

Corollary 2.4

(
[

Let

A nilsystem

2.3.
Weyl Systems

In this section, we introduce a central object of this work, the Weyl systems. Weyl systems are usually defined for

Definition 2.6

Let

It is a known fact that for any

(X=G/\Gamma,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})

A concrete example of Weyl systems are affine systems. An

-nilsystem

are defined by

3.
Bohr Recurrence for locally compact abelian groups

This section focuses on the concept of recurrence for systems and families of systems, together with the notion of Bohr recurrence, which is established for locally compact abelian groups using Pontryagin duality. We extend many known properties for
G=\mathbb{Z}
. Although these properties might be considered classical, we were unable to find a proper reference in the literature, so we include them for the sake of completeness. Finally, we state Katznelson’s question in this context and generalize the fact that proximal extensions and inverse limits lift up recurrence.

3.1.
Bohr Recurrence

For a system
(X,H)
and sets
U,V\subseteq X
we denote

N_{H}(V,U)=\{h\in H:V\cap h^{-1}U\neq\emptyset\}.

In the case
U=V
we just write
N_{H}(U)=N_{H}(U,U)
, and in the case
V=\{x\}
we write

N_{H}(x,U)=\{h\in H:hx\in U\}.

Definition 3.1
.

A set
R\subseteq H
is a set of recurrence for a system
(X,H)
if for any nonempty open set
U\subseteq X
,
R\cap N_{H}(U)\neq\emptyset
. Additionally, if
\mathcal{F}
is a family of
H
-systems, a set
R\subseteq H
is a set of recurrence for the family
\mathcal{F}
if for any minimal system
(X,H)
in the family
\mathcal{F}
,
R
is a set of recurrence for
(X,H).

When we say that
R\subseteq H
is a
H
-set of recurrence
without specifying any particular family, we mean that
R
is a set or recurrence for all minimal
H
-systems.

Remark 3.2
.

If
R
is a set of recurrence for a system
(X,H)
, then there exist
x\in X
and a sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq R
such that
h_{n}x\to x
as
n
goes to
\infty
. Indeed, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
A_{n}=\{x\in X:\inf_{h\in R}d(hx,x)<1/n\}
is open and dense, and therefore any point in the dense
G_{\delta}
set
\cap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}
satisfies the aforementioned property.

Now, we introduce the generalities for studying sets of recurrence in the family of rotations. We begin by discussing duality in locally compact abelian groups in order to define Bohr recurrence. For the purposes of this discussion, we will denote the unit circle by
\mathbb{S}^{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
.

Given a locally compact abelian group
H
, a character of
H
is a continuous homomorphism
\chi\colon H\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and the set
\widehat{H}
of characters of
H
is an abelian group under pointwise multiplication. For a compact subset
F\subseteq H
and
\epsilon>0
, let

P(F,\epsilon)=\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:|\chi(h)-1|<\epsilon,\forall h\in F\}
. The sets
P(F,\epsilon)
, where
F
and
\epsilon
range over all compact sets and positive numbers respectively, is an open basis at the identity
e_{\widehat{H}}
, and
\widehat{H}
is a locally compact abelian topological group (
[
19
, Theorem 23.15]
).

Let
H
be a locally compact abelian group and consider
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}\in\widehat{H}
. The Bohr neighborhood of
e_{H}
or just
0
in
H
determined by
\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}\}
and radius
\epsilon>0
is given by

\text{Bohr}(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k};\epsilon)=\{h\in H:|\chi_{i}(h)-1|<\epsilon,~~\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\}.

Remark 3.3
.

If
H
is compact, then as
\widehat{\widehat{H}}
can be identified with
H
(see
[
19
, Theorem 24.3]
), the topology generated by the basis of
e_{H}
given by the sets
Bohr(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k};\epsilon)
, for
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}\in\widehat{H}
and
\epsilon>0
, coincides with the topology in
H
.

Let
H
be a locally compact abelian group. We say that
V\subseteq H
is a
H
-Bohr
0
set if
V
contains a Bohr neighborhood of
0
. Additionally, we say that
W\subseteq H
is a set of
H
-Bohr recurrence (denoted as
W\in H
-Bohr
{}_{0}^{*}
) if for every
V\in\text{Bohr}_{0}
,
W\cap V\neq\emptyset
. We will usually drop the prefix
H
- from
H
-Bohr
0
,
H
-Bohr
{}_{0}^{*}
, and
H
-Bohr recurrence if the locally compact abelian group
H
is clear from the context. The family of
H
-Bohr
0
sets is a filter, which means that it is upward closed and closed under intersections. Additionally, the family of
H
-Bohr
{}_{0}^{*}
sets is
partition regular
, this is, for every
A\in H\text{-Bohr}_{0}^{*}
, if we partition
A
into
N
sets
(A_{n})_{n=1}^{N}
, then there is an
n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that
A_{n}\in H\text{-Bohr}_{0}^{*}
. This is well-known for the case
G=\mathbb{Z}
(see for example
[
14
]
) but the proof is identical for the general case.

A classic example of a
H
-Bohr
0
set which is not trivial is the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(X,H)
be a rotation and consider
x\in X
and
U\subseteq X
a neighborhood of
x
. Then,
N(x,U)
is a Bohr
0
set.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
x=e_{X}
, given that every neighborhood of
x
is a translation of a neighborhood of
e_{X}
. Let
\varphi\colon H\to X
be the homomorphism associated to the action of
H
on
X
. For an open neighborhood
U
of
e_{X}
we have to prove that

N(e_{X},U)=\{h\in H:\varphi(h)\in U\}=\varphi^{-1}(U)

is a
H
-Bohr
0
set. By
3.3
, there exist
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}\in\widehat{X}
and
\epsilon>0
are such that
Bohr(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k};\epsilon)\subseteq U
from which we deduce
Bohr(\chi_{1}\circ\varphi,\ldots,\chi_{k}\circ\varphi;\epsilon)\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(U)
. This means that
N(e_{X},U)
is a
H
-Bohr
0
set and we conclude.
∎

As any orbit in an equicontinuous system is, modulo conjugacy, a minimal rotation, we can extend
3.4
to any equicontinuous system. In particular, we have that sets of Bohr recurrence are precisely the family of sets of recurrence for equicontinuous systems and rotations.

3.2.
Katznelson’s question

In this section, we state Katznelson’s question regarding general group actions.

Katznelson’s question:
Given an action
H
, is every set of Bohr recurrence a set of (topological)
H
-recurrence?

Notice that in the context of nonabelian groups, one could still formulate Katznelson’s problem replacing Bohr recurrence by equicontinuous recurrence. That is, say that
S\subseteq H
is a set of equicontinuous recurrence if for any minimal equicontinous system
(X,H)
and nonempty open set
U\subseteq X
, there exists
g\in S
such that
U\cap g^{-1}U\neq\emptyset
. For abelian groups, as we have seen, equicontinuous recurrence is equivalent to Bohr recurrence. However, one key advantage of equicontinuous recurrence is that it does not rely on the theory of characters, which is not readily available for nonabelian groups. The Katznelson’s problem in this context would be asking if sets of equicontinuous recurrence are necessarily sets of recurrence. It is easy to construct groups for which this formulation of Kaztnelson problem is false, as the following example shows for instance.

Example 3.5
.

Consider a nonrecurrent map
T
on the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
(e.g.,
T(x)=x^{2}
) and let
S
be an irrational rotation. We have that
(\mathbb{S}^{1},\langle T,S\rangle)
is a minimal system and the set
\mathbb{N}\times\{0\}
(here we identify this set with
\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, since all powers of
T
are different) is a set of equicontinous recurrence but not of recurrence for the system
(\mathbb{S}^{1},\langle T,S\rangle)
.

For that reason we restrict ourselves to abelian groups and focus our attention on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
later on. As we pointed out in the introduction, a strategy to give a positive answer to Katznelson’s question for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions is to prove that Bohr recurrence can be lifted up through certain types of extensions for particular classes of systems. This idea also holds for
H
-actions in our context, and the following propositions show that recurrence can be lifted up through inverse limits and proximal extensions. The proof for inverse limits is elemental and the proof for proximal extensions is a straightforward adaptation of the original argument in
[
22
, Proposition 3.8]
, so we will omit them.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\{(X_{n},H)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a collection of minimal topological dynamical systems with factor maps
\pi_{n+1}\colon(X_{n+1},H)\to(X_{n},H)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
R
is a set of recurrence for
(X_{n},H)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, it is also a set of recurrence
for the inverse limit of these systems with respect to the factors.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,H)\to(Y,H)
be a proximal extension between minimal systems and let
R
be a set of recurrence for
(Y,H)
. Then,
R
is a set of recurrence for
(X,H)
.

Unlike proximal extensions and inverse limits, it is not easy to generalize the fact that sets of Bohr recurrence are set of recurrence for
s
-step nilsystems. In the next sections, we focus on doing this for the family of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Weyl systems by developing certain techniques over sets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.

The following results show that Bohr recurrence is preserved under homomorphisms of locally compact groups. We present these in generality, but they will be mostly used in the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Related results can be found in
[
26
,
15
]
. The following can be found in
[
6
, Proposition 2.10]
, with the Bohr case being a straightforward adaptation of that proof.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\phi\colon H\to H^{\prime}
be a group homomorphism between the locally compact abelian groups
H
and
H^{\prime}
, and let
R\subseteq H
be a set of
H
-recurrence (
H
-Bohr recurrence). Then

\phi(R)
is a set of
H^{\prime}
-recurrence (resp
H^{\prime}
-Bohr recurrence).

Note that if
H
and
H^{\prime}
are locally compact abelian groups with
H\leq H^{\prime}
,
3.8
applied to the injection map tells us that any set of
H
-recurrence (
H
-Bohr recurrence) is a set of
H^{\prime}
-recurrence (resp
H^{\prime}
-Bohr recurrence). We state a converse statement for the Bohr case under a finite index condition.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
H\leq H^{\prime}
be locally compact abelian groups and assume that
H
is closed and of finite index in
H^{\prime}
. If
R\subseteq H^{\prime}
is a set of Bohr-
H^{\prime}
-recurrence, then
R\cap H
is a set of
H
-Bohr recurrence.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for any
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}\in\widehat{H}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
g\in R\cap H
such that
g\in Bohr(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k};\epsilon)
. As
H
is closed, we may extend
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}
to characters in
\widehat{H^{\prime}}
(see for instance
[
29
, Theorem 2.1.4]
). Consider
((\mathbb{S}^{1})^{k},H^{\prime})
the rotation by these characters in
(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{k}
, that is,
h
maps
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
to
(\chi_{1}(h)x_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}(h)x_{k})
. Consider the finite system
(H^{\prime}/H,H^{\prime})
, where the action is the natural one, and take
Y
to be a minimal subsystem of
((\mathbb{S}^{1})^{k}\times H^{\prime}/H,H^{\prime})
. Let
V\subseteq Y
an open set with
V\subseteq U\times\{e\}
, where
U
is an
\epsilon/2
-neighborhood of
1
and
e
is the identity in
H^{\prime}/H
. As
R
is a set of Bohr-
H^{\prime}
recurrence, there exists
g\in R
such that
g^{-1}V\cap V\neq\emptyset
. This implies that
g\in R\cap H
and
g\in Bohr(\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k};\epsilon)
, as desired.

∎

4.
Bohr Recurrence for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions

In this section we establish several properties of sets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence, introducing the notion of Bohr correlations.

4.1.
Basic properties of sets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
[d]=\{1,\ldots,d\}
and for
x\in\mathbb{R}
we denote the maximum integer
z
such that
z\leq x
by
\lfloor x\rfloor
, the decimal part of
x
by
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor\in[0,1)
, and the torus norm of
x
by
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}}=\min\{\{x\},1-\{x\}\}
, which is the distance of
x
to the nearest
integer. We generalize this notation for
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
as follows:

\lfloor\boldsymbol{x}\rfloor=(\lfloor x_{i}\rfloor)_{i\in[r]},~~\{\boldsymbol{x}\}=(x_{i}-\lfloor x_{i}\rfloor)_{i\in[r]}\in[0,1)^{r},~~\text{and}~~\left\lVert\boldsymbol{x}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left\lVert x_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}}.

When it is clear, we will simply use
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{x}\right\rVert
instead of
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{x}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}
. With this notation we observe that
V\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is a Bohr
0
set if there exist
\epsilon>0
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
such that

\{\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}:\left\lVert n_{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\right\rVert<\epsilon,\forall i\in[d]\}\subseteq V.

Definition 4.1

(Ramsey property)
.

A property of subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is Ramsey if for any set
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that has this property and any partition
R=A\cup B
, at least one of
A
or
B
has this property.

In other words, a family possesses the Ramsey property if and only if it is partition regular. As we pointed out in
Section

3.1
(right after
3.3
), the family of sets of
H
-recurrence is partition regular for each locally compact abelian group
H
. Consequently, we have the following proposition regarding the family of sets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.

Proposition 4.2
.

The sets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence have the Ramsey property.

Analogously to the one dimensional case where sets of recurrence are usually considered in
\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
to avoid
0
, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.3

(Essentiality)
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. We say that
R
is essential if
\forall\boldsymbol{n}\in R
,
\forall j\in[d]
,
n_{j}\neq 0
.

From now one we make the abuse of notation denoting
\pi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the permutation of coordinates induced by a permutation
\pi:[d]\to[d]
.

The name “essential” is just to emphasize that this is the only relevant study case. In fact, the next proposition shows that every relevant set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence can be reduced to an essential set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. Then, there exist
d^{\prime}\leq d
, a permutation
\pi\colon[d]\to[d]
, and an essential set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-Bohr recurrence
R^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
such that
R^{\prime}\times\{0\}^{d-d^{\prime}}\subseteq\pi(R)
.

Proof.

For
J\subseteq[d]
denote

R_{J}=\{(n_{j})_{j\in[d]}\in R:n_{j}\neq 0\iff j\in J\}.
Notice that, as
0\notin R
, we have that

R=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[d]\\
J\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}R_{J}.

By the Ramsey property, there exists
J\subseteq[d]
nonempty such that
R_{J}
is a set of Bohr
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-recurrence. Now, consider a permutation
\pi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
taking the coordinates
J
to
\{1,\ldots,d^{\prime}\}
maintaining their order, where
d^{\prime}=|J|
. If we set
R^{\prime}=p(R_{J})
, where
p\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}^{J}
is the canonical projection, then
R^{\prime}\times\{0\}^{d-d^{\prime}}\subseteq\pi(R)
and
R^{\prime}
is an essential set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-Bohr recurrence.
∎

In particular, we can always reduce to the case in which
R
is essential when studying recurrence in the family of nilsystems.

Corollary 4.5
.

Every set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence is a set of recurrence for the family of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystems if and only if every essential set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence is a set of recurrence for the family of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystems.

Proof.

We prove the nontrivial direction. Let
\mathscr{X}=(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a minimal nilsystem and
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. We may assume
0\notin R
, otherwise the result is immediate. By
4.4
there exist
d^{\prime}\leq d
, a permutation
\pi\colon[d]\to[d]
and an essential set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-Bohr recurrence
R^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
such that
R^{\prime}\times\{0\}^{d-d^{\prime}}\subseteq\pi(R)
. Consider the minimal nilsystem

\mathscr{Y}=(\overline{\mathcal{O}(e_{X})},T_{\pi(1)},\ldots,T_{\pi(d^{\prime})}).

Notice that for all
n\in R^{\prime}
if
m\in\{n\}\times\{0\}^{d-d^{\prime}}\subseteq\pi(R)
we have that

T_{\pi(1)}^{n_{1}}\cdots T_{\pi(d^{\prime})}^{n_{d^{\prime}}}=T_{1}^{m_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\cdots T_{d}^{m_{\pi^{-1}(d)}}.

In this way, since
R^{\prime}
is a set of recurrence for
\mathscr{Y}
, it follows that
R
is a set of recurrence for
\mathscr{X}
.
∎

The following property enables us to consider coordinates of an essential set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence with arbitrary magnitudes. This is the intuitive generalization of the fact for sets of
\mathbb{Z}-
Bohr recurrence we can always eliminate a finite amount of elements without losing the property of Bohr recurrence.

Proposition 4.6

(Bands Property)
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence,
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
i\in[d]
. Set
B_{k}^{i}=\{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:n_{i}=k\}
, then the set
R_{0}=R\setminus B_{k}^{i}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.

Proof.

We may assume
i=1
, since all the other cases are analogous. It suffices to show that
\{k\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
is not a set of recurrence. Consider the system
(X=\mathbb{Z}_{|k|+1},T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{|k|+1}
denotes the cyclic group with
(|k|+1)
elements,
T_{1}(x)=x+1\text{ mod }(|k|+1)
, and
T_{j}={\rm id}
for
j\geq 2
. It is immediate to check that
\{k\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
is not a set of recurrence for this system. The Ramsey property allows us to conclude that
R_{0}
is a set of recurrence.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

We remark that in the previous proof one can also construct systems where all the transformations act in a non-trivial way.

Next, we prove that the property of being a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence is invariant under isomorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence and
M
an invertible matrix with rational coefficients of dimension
d
. Then, the set defined by

M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\{n=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:Mn\in R\},

is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.

Proof.

By
3.8
, the set
M^{-1}R
is a set of
M^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-recurrence. As
M
has rational coefficients,
3.9
implies that
M^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-recurrence.
∎

When dealing with sets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence, it is common to encounter situations where some coordinates are rationally dependent. For instance, it is not difficult to demonstrate that if
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a set of Bohr recurrence, then so is
R^{\prime}=\{(n_{1},n_{2},n_{1}+n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\mid(n_{1},n_{2})\in R\}
.
Naturally, the intrinsic recurrence behavior resides in the first two coordinates, rendering the third coordinate seemingly redundant. The following definition formalizes this concept, which will be important in the subsequent section for the purpose of eliminating certain cases.

Definition 4.9

(Redundancy)
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. We say that
R
is redundant if there is
d^{\prime}<d
, a morphism
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-Bohr recurrence
R^{\prime}
such that
\phi(R^{\prime})\subseteq R
.

Remark 4.10
.

Notice that
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is redundant if and only if there is
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
such that
R_{\boldsymbol{v}}=\{\boldsymbol{n}\in R:\boldsymbol{v}^{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. In this case,
v
is any non zero vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
orthogonal to the subspace
\phi(\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}})
, where
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the morphism associated to
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
being redundant. We also observe that taking
\boldsymbol{v}
in
\mathbb{Q}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
instead of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
leads to the same definition.

We can provide similar definitions in the context of locally compact abelian groups; however, for clarity, we will limit our discussion to cases involving
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or images of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by matrices with rational coefficients. Note that in such cases, a morphism is just a matrix multiplication.

To end this subsection, we prove that the property of being non-redundant is preserved under the transformation previously described in
4.8
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a non redundant set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence and
M
be an invertible real matrix with rational coefficients of dimension
d
. Then,
M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is non redundant as well.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose that
M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is redundant, then there exists
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\boldsymbol{v}}=\{\boldsymbol{n}\in M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\boldsymbol{v}^{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}=0\},

is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. Hence, by
4.8
,
M(M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\boldsymbol{v}}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. If we define

R_{M^{-T}\boldsymbol{v}}=\{\boldsymbol{n}\in R:(M^{-T}\boldsymbol{v})^{T}\boldsymbol{n}=0\},

then
M(M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})_{\boldsymbol{v}}\subseteq R_{M^{-T}\boldsymbol{v}}
and therefore
R_{M^{-T}\boldsymbol{v}}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. As
M^{-T}\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, we have that
R
is redundant, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.2.
Bohr Correlations

In this section we introduce the notion of Bohr correlation, which in simple words is a slope of the line around which a specific set of Bohr recurrence accumulates. To illustrate this notion first we provide some examples.

Example 4.12
.

If
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}
-Bohr recurrence, then for every
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have that

\tilde{R}=\{(\lfloor n\alpha_{1}+1/2\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor n\alpha_{d}+1/2\rfloor)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:n\in R\},

is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.

In
4.12
the set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence
\tilde{R}
is approximately near the line of slope
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})
, in the sense that for
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\tilde{R}
and
i,j\in[d]
we have that
n_{i}/n_{j}\approx\alpha_{i}/\alpha_{j}
as
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lfloor n\alpha_{i}+1/2\rfloor/\lfloor n\alpha_{j}+1/2\rfloor=\alpha_{i}/\alpha_{j}
.

Proof of
4.12
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}
-Bohr recurrence and consider
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

\tilde{R}=\{(\lfloor n\alpha_{1}+1/2\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor n\alpha_{d}+1/2\rfloor)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:n\in R\}.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N},

\epsilon\in(0,1)
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\beta}_{d}\in[0,1)^{r}
. Consider the Bohr neighborhood of
0

B=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:~\left\lVert n\alpha_{i}\right\rVert\leq\epsilon/2,~\left\lVert n(\alpha_{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i})\right\rVert\leq\epsilon/2,~\forall i\in[d]\}.

As the family of Bohr neighborhood of
0
is a filter, we have that
R\cap B
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}
-Bohr recurrence. Taking
n\in R\cap B
we have that for every
i\in[d]

\left\lVert\lfloor n\alpha_{i}+1/2\rfloor\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}\right\rVert=\left\lVert n\alpha_{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}/2-\{n\alpha+1/2\}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}\right\rVert\\
\leq\left\lVert n\alpha_{i}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert n\alpha_{i}\right\rVert\cdot|\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}|\leq\epsilon/2+\epsilon/2=\epsilon.

Therefore
\tilde{R}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.
∎

For the next example, we need the following proposition.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Denote
R_{1}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\exists m\in\mathbb{Z},(n,m)\in R\}
and for
n\in R_{1}
denote by
R(n,\bullet)=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}:(n,m)\in R\}.

Suppose
R_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}
-Bohr recurrence. For
n\in R_{1}
denote
L_{n}
the length of the largest interval contained in
R(n,\bullet)
. If
L_{n}\to\infty
as
n
goes to infinity, then
R
is a set of Bohr recurrence.

Proof.

If
(X,g_{1},g_{2})
is a minimal rotation, we can find an arbitrarily large
n_{1}\in R_{1}
such that
d(g_{1}^{n_{1}}e_{X},e_{X})\leq\epsilon/2
. Observe that the set
N_{g_{2}}(e_{X},B(e_{X},\epsilon/2))
is syndetic (i.e., it has bounded gaps) by the fact that the system
(X,g_{2})
is distal, so
(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{g_{2}}(e_{X})},g_{2})
is minimal. Taking
n_{1}
such that
L_{n_{1}}>L
(with
L
the syndetic constant associated to
B(e_{X},\epsilon/2)
), we have that there exists
n_{2}\in R(n_{1},\bullet)
such that
d(g_{2}^{n_{2}}e_{X},e_{X})\leq\epsilon/2
. Thus, there exists
(n_{1},n_{2})\in R
such that
d(g_{1}^{n_{1}}g_{2}^{n_{2}}e_{X},e_{X})\leq\epsilon
.
∎

Example 4.14
.

From
4.13
, the set
R=\{(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}:n_{1}^{2}\leq n_{2}\leq 2n_{1}^{2}\}
is a set of Bohr recurrence such that is approximately near a line of slope
\infty
, in the sense that for every
\epsilon>0
, there is
M>0
such that for all
(n_{1},n_{2})\in R\cap B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,M)^{c}
we have that
|n_{1}/n_{2}|<\epsilon.

To formalize the notion illustrated in the examples, we first show that when studying
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence, we can assume, without loss of generality, that the coordinates of the set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence being studied are ordered. Although this assumption is not strictly necessary, it significantly simplifies the notation, making it easier to manipulate.

We call
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
an
ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence
if
R
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence such that

\forall\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in R,~~|n_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|n_{d}|.

Remark 4.15
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence and
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
a system. Consider
S_{d}
the symmetric group and notice that

R=\bigcup_{\varphi\in S_{d}}R\cap\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|n_{\varphi(1)}|\geq\cdots\geq|n_{\varphi(d)}|\}.

By the Ramsey property, there is
\varphi\in S_{d}
such that

R^{\prime}=R\cap\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|n_{\varphi(1)}|\geq\cdots\geq|n_{\varphi(d)}|\},

is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. Note that recurrence in
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
with
R^{\prime}
is equivalent to recurrence in
(X,T_{\varphi(1)},\ldots,T_{\varphi(d)})
with

R^{\prime\prime}=\{(n_{\varphi(1)},\ldots,n_{\varphi(d)}):(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in R^{\prime}\},

which is an ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. Additionally, notice that if
R
is essential, then so is
R^{\prime\prime}
.

Definition 4.16

(Bohr Correlations)
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be an essential and ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence and
I\subseteq\{(i,j)\in[d]^{2}:j\geq i\}
. We define a vector of
I
-Bohr correlations for
R
as
\boldsymbol{P}=(P_{i,j})_{j\geq i}\in[-1,1]^{d\times(d+1)/2}
such that
\forall\epsilon>0
,

R_{\boldsymbol{P},I,\epsilon}=\Bigl\{\boldsymbol{n}\in R:\left|\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}-P_{i,j}\right|\leq\epsilon,~\forall(i,j)\in I\Bigr\}

is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence.
In the case
I=\{(i,j)\in[d]^{2}:j\geq i\}
we drop the prefix
I
and say that
\boldsymbol{P}
is a vector of Bohr correlations. We denote by
\mathcal{BC}(R)
the set of Bohr correlations of
R
.

For example, for
d=2
, a vector of Bohr correlations is just the slope of a line for which many points (a set of Bohr recurrence amount of points) are inside a cone of angle
\epsilon
around such line. Notice that it is not always possible to take
\epsilon=0
(for instance, this is not possible in the set of recurrence from
4.14
).

The existence of Bohr correlations is not immediate from the definition, nor is the possibility of extending a vector of
I
-Bohr correlations to a vector of Bohr correlations. We address both issues in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be an essential and ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. Consider
I\subseteq\{(i,j)\in[d]^{2}:j\geq i\}
and
\boldsymbol{Q}=(Q_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}\in[-1,1]^{I}
a vector of
I
-Bohr correlations. Then, there exists
\boldsymbol{P}\in\mathcal{BC}(R)
that extends
(Q_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in I}
, in the sense that
P_{i,j}=Q_{i,j}
,
\forall(i,j)\in I
. In particular, for
I=\emptyset
we have that
\mathcal{BC}(R)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Notice that
(\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}})_{j\geq i}\in[-1,1]^{d(d+1)/2}
,
\forall\boldsymbol{n}\in R
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we can cover
[-1,1]^{d(d+1)/2}
with finitely many intervals of the form
\prod_{j\geq i}(P_{i,j}-\frac{1}{m},P_{i,j}+\frac{1}{m})
, for
(P_{i,j})_{j\geq i}\in([-1,1]\cap\mathbb{Q})^{d(d+1)/2}
. For every
\boldsymbol{n}\in R_{\boldsymbol{Q},I,1/m}
we have that
(\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}})_{j\geq i}\subseteq[-1,1]^{d(d+1)/2}
. Hence, using the Ramsey property (after turning the aforementioned covering into a partition), we obtain
\boldsymbol{P}^{m}=(P_{i,j}^{m})_{j\geq i}\in([-1,1]\cap\mathbb{Q})^{d(d+1)/2}
such that
R_{\boldsymbol{Q},I,1/m}\cap R_{\boldsymbol{P^{m}},1/m}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence, where we define for each
m\in\mathbb{N}

R_{\boldsymbol{P}^{m},1/m}:=\Bigl\{\boldsymbol{n}\in R~:~\Bigl|\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}-P_{i,j}^{m}\Bigr|<\frac{1}{m},~\forall j\geq i\Bigr\}.

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
(\boldsymbol{P}^{m_{l}})_{l\geq 1}
converges to
\boldsymbol{P}\in[-1,1]^{d(d+1)/2}
. We claim that

R_{\boldsymbol{P},\epsilon}:=\Bigr\{\boldsymbol{n}\in R~:~\Bigl|\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}-P_{i,j}\Bigl|<\epsilon,~\forall j\geq i\Bigl\},

is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence for all
\epsilon>0
and that
\boldsymbol{P}
coincides with
\boldsymbol{Q}
in
I
.
Indeed, for the first part notice that as
\boldsymbol{P}^{m_{l}}\to\boldsymbol{P}
when
l\to\infty
, we can take
l
large enough so that
(P^{m_{l}}_{i,j}-1/m_{l},P^{m_{l}}_{i,j}+1/m_{l})\subseteq(P_{i,j}-\epsilon,P_{i,j}+\epsilon),\forall j\geq i.

Therefore,
R_{\boldsymbol{P}^{m_{l}},1/m_{l}}\subseteq R_{\boldsymbol{P},\epsilon}
and since
R_{\boldsymbol{P}^{m_{l}},1/m_{l}}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence, so is
R_{\boldsymbol{P},\epsilon}
. We conclude that
\boldsymbol{P}\in\mathcal{BC}(R)
.
Now for the second part, let
\epsilon>0
and take
l\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
|P_{i,j}^{m_{l}}-P_{i,j}|\leq\epsilon
,
\forall j\geq i
, and such that
\frac{1}{m_{l}}\leq\epsilon
. For all
n\in R_{\boldsymbol{Q},I,1/m_{l}}\cap R_{\boldsymbol{P}^{m_{l}},1/m_{l}}
, and
(i,j)\in I
,

|P_{i,j}-Q_{i,j}|\leq\left|\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}-P_{i,j}^{m_{l}}\right|+\left|P_{i,j}^{m_{l}}-P_{i,j}\right|+\left|\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}-Q_{i,j}\right|\leq\frac{3}{m_{l}}\leq 3\epsilon.

Taking
\epsilon\to 0
yields
P_{i,j}=Q_{i,j}
,
\forall(i,j)\in I
, concluding.
∎

Now we show that Bohr correlations are consistent, in the sense that if
1\leq i\leq j\leq l\leq d
, then the correlation between the
i
-th coordinate and the
l
-th coordinate is the product of the correlations between the
i
-th coordinate and the
j
-th coordinate, and the correlation between the
j
-th coordinate and the
l
-th
coordinate.

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be an essential and ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence, and
\boldsymbol{P}\in\mathcal{BC}(R)
. Then, for all
i,j,l\in[d]
with
i\leq j\leq l
,

P_{i,l}=P_{i,j}P_{j,l}.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
, we have that
\forall\boldsymbol{n}\in R_{\boldsymbol{P},\epsilon}

\displaystyle|P_{i,l}-P_{i,j}P_{j,l}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{n_{l}}{n_{i}}-P_{i,l}\right|+\left|\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\frac{n_{l}}{n_{j}}-P_{i,j}P_{j,l}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\left|\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\frac{n_{l}}{n_{j}}-P_{i,j}\frac{n_{l}}{n_{j}}\right|+\left|P_{i,j}\frac{n_{l}}{n_{j}}-P_{i,j}P_{j,l}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\left|\frac{n_{l}}{n_{j}}\right|\epsilon+\left|P_{i,j}\right|\epsilon\leq 3\epsilon.

Letting
\epsilon
tend to
0
we get that
P_{i,l}=P_{i,j}P_{j,l}
.
∎

Now, we define the property of complete independence, which comprises the case where the correlations are not rational.

Definition 4.19
.

Let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be an essential and ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. We say that
R
has the property of complete independence if there is
\boldsymbol{P}\in\mathcal{BC}(R)
such that for all
i\in[d]
, the set

\{P_{i,j}:j\geq i,P_{i,j}\neq 0\}
is rationally independent.

The main consequence of the property of complete independence is that it allows us to approximate multiple coordinates with just one variable, which is the statement of
B
. For the readers’ convenience, we state
B
again below.

Theorem B
.

Let
R
be an essential and ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence with the property of complete independence. Then, for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
there exists a set of Bohr recurrence
R_{\epsilon}\subseteq R
such that for any
\boldsymbol{n}\in R_{\epsilon}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
there is
y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
such that

\displaystyle||y||_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}<\epsilon~~\text{ and }~~||(n_{1}y,\ldots,n_{d}y)-(\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d})||_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}<\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{P}\in\mathcal{BC}(R)
such that for all
i\in[d]
,

\{P_{i,j}:j\geq i,P_{i,j}\neq 0\}
are rationally independent. Let
\epsilon>0
and for
l\in[d]
denote
I_{l}=\{j\in[d]:j>l,P_{l,j}\neq 0\}
. As
\{P_{l,j}\}_{j\in I_{l}\cup\{l\}}
is rationally independent, we can find
N\geq r
large enough such that for all
l\in[d]
,
\{(nP_{l,j})_{j\in I_{l}}:n\in[-N,N]\}
, is
\epsilon
-dense in
\mathbb{T}^{|I_{l}|}
.

Let
M>N/\epsilon
. Take
\boldsymbol{n}\in R_{\boldsymbol{P},\epsilon/M}\cap B(0,M)^{c}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
. We will assume without loss of generality that
\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d}\in[0,1)^{r}
. Define
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\cdot r}
by
\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})=(n_{1}\boldsymbol{x},\ldots,n_{d}\boldsymbol{x})
and consider
\boldsymbol{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}}{n_{i}},
where we define
\{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
inductively as follows: first we define
\boldsymbol{y}_{d}=\boldsymbol{w}_{d},
and note that
||\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{d}}{n_{d}}||_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}\leq|\frac{1}{n_{d}}|\leq\epsilon.
Next, suppose we have defined for
1\leq l<d
:
\boldsymbol{y}_{l+1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{y}_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\cap B(0,C)
, where
C=N+d+1
. Take
\boldsymbol{k}_{l}\in[-N,N]^{r}
such that for all
j>l

(1)

||P_{l,j}\boldsymbol{k}_{l}+P_{l,j}(\boldsymbol{w}_{l}-\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\{\frac{n_{l}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\})||_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}\leq\epsilon.

The existence of each coordinate of
\boldsymbol{k}_{l}
is guaranteed by the definition of
N
and the fact that such condition is trivial for
j\notin I_{l}
. Define

\boldsymbol{y}_{l}=\boldsymbol{w}_{l}+\boldsymbol{k}_{l}-\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\{\frac{n_{l}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\cap B(0,C).

In this way, we have that
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{y}_{l}/n_{l}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}\leq C/|n_{l}|\leq(1+\frac{d+1}{N})\epsilon\leq(d+2)\epsilon.
For each
j\in[d]
denote
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\in(\mathbb{R}^{r\cdot r})^{d}
the vector which
j
-th coordinate is the identity matrix on
\mathbb{R}^{r}
and each other coordinate is a matrix full of zeros. In other words, we have that for each
\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_{d}\in(\mathbb{R}^{r})^{d}
,

(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{x}_{d})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{x}_{j}.

We claim that

\left\lVert\varphi(y)-w\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}=\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}.

For this we will prove by induction that for every
l=0,...,d
we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi(y)-w\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}=\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}}{n_{i}})+\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}.

Indeed, we start with
l=d
for which by definition we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi(y)-w\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}=\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}}{n_{i}})-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}.

Now assume that for
1\leq l\leq d
we have proved that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi(y)-w\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}=\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}}{n_{i}})+\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}.

Using the definition of
\boldsymbol{y}_{l}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}}{n_{i}})+\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}(\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}}{n_{i}})+\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{l}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{l}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}(\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\frac{\boldsymbol{y}_{i}}{n_{i}})+\sum_{i=l}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\sum_{i=l}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}},

in where the last equality comes from splitting the last summation into
j>l
and
j<l
, integrating the former terms into the second summation and the later terms into the third summation, namely:

\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq l\end{subarray}}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{l}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{l}=\sum_{i=l}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\sum_{i=l}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}.

This concludes the induction, and using the case
l=0
we get that

\left\lVert\varphi(y)-w\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}=\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}.

Moreover, by (
1
) we have that for each
l\in[d]

\displaystyle||P_{l,j}\boldsymbol{y}_{l}||_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|P_{l,j}\boldsymbol{w}_{l}+P_{l,j}\boldsymbol{k}_{l}-P_{l,j}\sum_{i=l+1}^{d}\{\frac{n_{l}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\}\Big\|_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}\leq\epsilon.

Therefore, we found
y\in B_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}(0,\epsilon d(d+2))
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi(y)-w\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}=\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}P_{i,j}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\left\lVert(\frac{n_{j}}{n_{i}}-P_{i,j})\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j>i}\left\lVert P_{i,j}\boldsymbol{y}_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}+\frac{\epsilon Cd^{2}}{M}

\displaystyle\leq d^{2}\epsilon+d^{2}\epsilon^{2}(d+2)\leq(d+3)^{3}\epsilon.

We just proved that for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
, there is
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
\boldsymbol{n}\in R_{P,\epsilon/M}\cap B(0,M)^{c}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
there is
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
satisfying

||\boldsymbol{y}||_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}<d(d+2)\epsilon~~\text{ and }~~||(n_{1}\boldsymbol{y},\ldots,n_{d}\boldsymbol{y})-(\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d})||_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}<(d+3)^{3}\epsilon.

Given that
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary in the previous statement, we can replace
\epsilon
with
\epsilon/(d+3)^{3}
getting
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
\boldsymbol{n}\in R_{\epsilon}:=R_{P,\epsilon/((d+3)^{3}M)}\cap B(0,M)^{c}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{r}
there is
y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
such that

||\boldsymbol{y}||_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}<d(d+2)\epsilon/(d+3)^{3}<\epsilon~~\text{ and }~~||(n_{1}\boldsymbol{y},\ldots,n_{d}\boldsymbol{y})-(\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{d})||_{\mathbb{T}^{d\cdot r}}<\epsilon,

concluding.
∎

5.
Recurrence in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Weyl systems

In this section, our objective is to lift
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence to the broader class of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Weyl systems. First, we begin with a brief outline of the method. Then, we introduce the concept of total ergodicity for actions of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-groups. Notably, this property is characterized within nilsystems through connectedness. Subsequently, we demonstrate that when extending Bohr recurrence to nilsystems, we can consistently simplify the problem by focusing on scenarios where the set of Bohr recurrence has the property of complete independence. Finally, we use this reduction to establish that sets characterized by
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence are sets of recurrence for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Weyl systems.

5.1.
Pivotal Lemma

In order to establish
A
,
B
gives as a corollary a pivotal technical lemma that comes into play. Specifically, when dealing with a set of Bohr recurrence with the property of complete independence, this lemma facilitates the approximation of the final commutator group within a nilspace. This approximation is achieved by applying the powers of commutators from the dynamics, all of which are drawn from the aforementioned set of Bohr recurrence, to a single variable.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
s\geq 2
and let
(X=G/\Gamma,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-affine system, and
R
an essential and ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence with the property of complete independence. Then, for all
\epsilon>0
there is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence
R_{\epsilon}\subseteq R
such that for all
v\in G_{s}
and all
\boldsymbol{n}\in R_{\epsilon}
there exist
h\in G_{s-1}
such that

(2)

d_{G}(h,e_{G})<\epsilon,\text{ and }d_{X}([h,\tau_{1}^{n_{1}}]\cdots[h,\tau_{d}^{n_{d}}]\Gamma,v\Gamma)<\epsilon.

Proof.

Writing
X=\mathbb{T}^{p}
and
T_{i}x=\boldsymbol{M}_{i}x+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}
where
\boldsymbol{M}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{M}_{d}
are unipotent matrices and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{p}
, we recall from
Section

2.3
that we consider
\Gamma
the group (with composition) generated by the transformations given by the matrices
\boldsymbol{M}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{M}_{d}
; and
G
corresponds to the group generated by
\Gamma
and the transformations obtained by translations of elements in
\mathbb{T}^{p}
. Moreover, we have that
G_{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{p}
and thus
G=G_{0}\Gamma
.

We will use vector notation
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{T}^{p}
for the representatives of elements
g\in G
that correspond to the rotation by
\boldsymbol{g}
.
We use multiplicative notation for the operation on
G
, but notice that such operation reduces to addition for the representatives in
\mathbb{T}^{p}
of elements in
G
. We recall that by
2.2
the group
G_{j}
can be seen as a subgroup of
\mathbb{T}^{p}
for each
j\geq 2
. Moreover, for
j\geq 2

(3)

G_{j}\cong\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\boldsymbol{M}_{i}-\boldsymbol{I})\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\mid\boldsymbol{g}_{i}\in G_{j-1}\cap G_{0},\text{ for each }i\in[d]\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{p},

in where the intersection with

only plays a role when

, because in such case

, and
G_{j-1}\subseteq G_{0}
otherwise.

Denote by
\Psi\colon(G_{s-1}/G_{s-1}\cap\Gamma)^{d}\to G_{s}

\Psi(g_{1}\Gamma,\ldots,g_{d}\Gamma)=[g_{1},\tau_{1}]\cdots[g_{d},\tau_{d}],~\text{ for }g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\in G_{s-1}\cap G_{0},

which is well defined due to the fact that

, and therefore from the definition of

.
Following the comments in

, for each

.

Observe that there is

such that

if

\mathbb{T}^{p}

so the same applies for

be such an embedding. Thus, the function

corresponds to

\Psi(\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{d})=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\boldsymbol{M}_{i}-\boldsymbol{I})\Phi(\boldsymbol{g}_{i}),~\text{ for }\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{d}\in\mathbb{T}^{q},

The linear map

is a uniformly continuous morphism. Let

and take

be a constant of uniform continuity for

for

Therefore, we get that

\left\lVert\Psi(n_{1}\boldsymbol{h},\ldots,n_{d}\boldsymbol{h})-\Psi(\boldsymbol{h}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{h}_{d})\right\rVert_{\mathbb{T}^{p}}<\epsilon,

or equivalently

d_{G_{s}}([h,\tau_{1}^{n_{1}}]\cdots[h,\tau_{d}^{n_{d}}],[h_{1},\tau_{1}]\cdots[h_{d},\tau_{d}])<\epsilon.

In consequence

As every element of

can be represented as

∎

5.1
allows us to prove

in the case where

is an essential and ordered set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence with the property of complete independence and

is connected. In what follows, we will show how to reduce to such a case and finally show how it can be used to prove

.

5.2.
Connectedness in Nilsystems

Notice that in
(X=G/\Gamma,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
we can always assume that
G
is spanned by
G_{0}
and the elements
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d}
defining the dynamics. Indeed, setting
G^{\prime}=\langle G_{0},\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d}\rangle
, we have that
G^{\prime}
is an open subgroup of
G
, and since for all
a\in X
the map
g\to g\cdot a
is open, the sets
G^{\prime}\cdot a
,
a\in X
, are open subsets of
X
that are pairwise equal or disjoint. Since these sets cover
X
, they are closed in
X
, hence compact. Moreover, given that
X
is compact, there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in X
such that
\{X_{i}=G^{\prime}\cdot a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
covers
X
. For
i=1,\ldots,k
, let
\Gamma_{i}
denote the stabilizer of
a_{i}
in
G
, that is,
\Gamma_{i}=g_{i}\Gamma g_{i}^{-1},

where
g_{i}\in G
is an element such that
g_{i}\cdot e_{X}=a_{i}
. Note that
\Gamma_{i}\cap G^{\prime}
represents the stabilizer of
a_{i}
in
G^{\prime}
, and
X_{i}
can be viewed as the nilmanifold
G^{\prime}/(\Gamma_{i}\cap G^{\prime})
. Since
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d}\in G^{\prime}
,
X_{i}
is a
\langle\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d}\rangle
-invariant set and
(X_{i},T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is a nilsystem. In this light, we obtain a partition of
X
into finitely many nilsystems, and each one can be studied separately. Thus, without loss of generality, we can substitute
G

by
G^{\prime}
and assume that
G
is spanned by
G_{0}
and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d}
.

In order to characterize connectedness in nilsystems, we will first need the following result that characterizes the ergodicity in a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-torus rotation.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{T}^{N},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d})
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-rotation. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

(\mathbb{T}^{N},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d})
is ergodic,

(2)

\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
\exists i\in[d]
such that
k\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\notin\mathbb{Z}
,

(3)

\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
\exists(t_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
k\cdot(\sum_{i=1}^{d}t_{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i})\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

((1)
\Longrightarrow
(2)) We prove the contrapositive assertion. Suppose that
\exists\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\forall i\in[d]
we have
\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
f(x)=e^{2\pi i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot x}
is an invariant function in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
that is not constant, and thus
(\mathbb{T}^{N},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d})
is not ergodic.

((2)
\Longrightarrow
(3)) This implication is direct.

((3)
\Longrightarrow
(1)) Suppose that
(\mathbb{T}^{N},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d})
is not ergodic. Then, there exists a
\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d}\rangle
-invariant function
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
which is not constant. Writing
f
in the classic basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})

f(x)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}c_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{2\pi i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot x},

and using the continuity of the operation
x\to g+x
,
\forall g\in\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d}\rangle
, we have that

f(gx)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}c_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{2\pi i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot g}e^{2\pi i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot x}.

By the invariance of
f
, we conclude that
\forall\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
,
c_{\boldsymbol{k}}=c_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{2\pi i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot g}.
As
f
is not constant, it exists
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
c_{\boldsymbol{k}}\neq 0
, and therefore
\boldsymbol{k}\cdot g\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\forall g\in\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d}\rangle
which contradicts the hypothesis.
∎

In what follows, we recall total ergodicity and show that
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-torus rotations are totally ergodic.

Definition 5.3
.

A dynamical system
(Y,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is totally ergodic if for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
the system
(Y,T_{1}^{m_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{m_{d}})
is ergodic. Equivalently, if the action of any finite index subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is ergodic.

5.2
immediately gives:

Proposition 5.4
.

An ergodic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-rotation
(\mathbb{T}^{N},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d})
is totally ergodic.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Suppose that
(\mathbb{T}^{N},m_{1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d})
is not ergodic. Then, by
5.2
, there is
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\forall i\in[d],~~\boldsymbol{k}\cdot m_{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular,

\forall i\in[d],~~(\boldsymbol{k}\prod_{j=1}^{d}m_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},

and by
5.2
,
(\mathbb{T}^{N},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{d})
is not ergodic, which is a contradiction.
∎

We will prove that total ergodicity is equivalent to connectedness in nilsystems. We need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(X=G/\Gamma,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem and
X_{0}
be the connected component of
e_{X}
. Then, there exist
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
X_{0}
is a
(T_{1}^{p_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{p_{d}})
-invariant clopen subset of
X
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be the Haar measure on
X
, which has full support. As
X_{0}
is open, we have that for each
i\in[d]
, there exists a minimal period
p_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
for which
\tau_{i}^{p_{i}}X_{0}=X_{0}
, otherwise the sets
(\tau_{i}^{k}X_{0})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are pairwise disjoint and with the same measure
\mu(X_{0})
, which is a contradiction. Given that
\{\tau_{i}^{j}X_{0}\}_{j=1}^{p_{i}}
partitions
X
,
and that
X_{0}
is open, the set
X_{0}
is also closed. Finally, as
\tau_{i}^{p_{i}}X_{0}=X_{0}
, we have the invariance, and we conclude.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Notice that
(X_{0},T_{1}^{p_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{p_{d}})
is a connected
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem. Furthermore,
X
is the union of finitely many isomorphic copies of
(X_{0},T_{1}^{p_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{p_{d}})
, which are permuted under the dynamics.

Finally, we have the following characterization of total ergodicity in a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be an ergodic
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem for the Haar measure
\mu
. Then,
X
is connected if and only if
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is totally ergodic for
\mu
.

Proof.

If
X
is connected, then by
2.4
we have that for
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
,
(X,T_{1}^{k_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{k_{d}})
is ergodic for
\mu
if and only if the rotations by
\tau_{1}^{k_{1}},\ldots,\tau_{d}^{k_{d}}
are ergodic in the maximal factor torus, which is always true given that such system is totally ergodic for
\mu
by
5.4
. On the other hand, if the system is totally ergodic for
\mu
, by
5.5
, we derive that
X_{0}
is a
(T_{1}^{p_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{p_{d}})
-invariant closed subset of
X
. But, as
(X,T_{1}^{p_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{p_{d}})
is ergodic for
\mu
, thus minimal by
2.5
, we have that
X=X_{0}
, so
X
is connected.
∎

The following lemma will be useful in subsequent proofs.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be an
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem and
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
a real matrix with integer entries. Define
S_{i}=T_{1}^{K_{i,1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{d}^{K_{i,d}},
for each
i\in[d]
. Then,
(X,S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})
is an
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem. In addition, if
\boldsymbol{K}
is invertible and
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is connected and minimal, then
(X,S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})
is connected and minimal as well.

Proof.

Given a matrix
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d\times d}
and
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
an
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem, it is clear that
(X,(T_{1}^{K_{i,1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{d}^{K_{i,d}})_{i=1}^{d})
is still an
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem, as
X
is an
s
-step nilmanifold and the transformations
(T_{1}^{K_{i,1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{d}^{K_{i,d}})_{i=1}^{d}
are given by the corresponding rotations. For the second part of the statement, suppose that
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is connected and minimal, and that
\boldsymbol{K}
is invertible. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
N\cdot K^{-T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d\times d}
. Then, notice that the action of
S_{i}
corresponds to the action generated by the vector
\boldsymbol{s}_{i}=\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, so
N\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{i}=N\cdot\boldsymbol{K}^{-T}\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th canonical vector of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence,

\langle S_{1},\ldots,S_{d}\rangle=\langle(T_{1}^{K_{i,1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{d}^{K_{i,d}})_{i=1}^{d}\rangle\supseteq\langle T_{1}^{N},\cdots,T_{d}^{N}\rangle,

and thus the action of
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})
is minimal, given that the system
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is totally ergodic by
5.7
.
∎

To finish this subsection, we prove the proposition that allows us to reduce to the connected case in general.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem. Then, there exists an invertible matrix
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathbb{N}^{d\times d}
such that
(X_{0},(T_{1}^{K_{i,1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{d}^{K_{i,d}})_{i=1}^{d})
is a connected minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem, where
X_{0}
is the connected component of
e_{X}
.

Proof.

By
5.5
and
5.8
, there are
p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(X_{0},T_{1}^{p_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{p_{d}})
is a nilsystem. However,
(X_{0},T_{1}^{p_{1}},\ldots,T_{d}^{p_{d}})
is not necessarily minimal. To fix this, we will define an upper triangular matrix
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathbb{N}^{d\times d}
as follows. Set

K_{i,i}=\min\{k_{i}\in[p_{i}]:\exists k_{i+1},\ldots k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z},\tau_{j}^{k_{i}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{k_{d}}X_{0}=X_{0}\},

and for
j>i
, assuming that we have defined
K_{i,l}
for every
l\in\{i,\ldots,j-1\}
, we define
K_{i,j}
by

K_{i,j}=\min\{k_{j}\in[p_{j}]:\exists k_{j+1},\ldots k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z},\tau_{i}^{K_{i,i}}\cdots\tau_{j-1}^{K_{i,j-1}}\tau_{j}^{k_{j}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{k_{d}}X_{0}=X_{0}\}.

We claim that
(X_{0},(T_{1}^{K_{i,1}}\circ\cdots\circ T_{d}^{K_{i,d}})_{i=1}^{d})
is a minimal nilsystem. As
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is a minimal nilsystem, we only need to prove that

\{\tau_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{l_{d}}:l_{1},\ldots,l_{d}\in\mathbb{Z},\tau_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{l_{d}}X_{0}=X_{0}\}\subseteq H=\langle(\tau_{1}^{K_{i,1}}\circ\cdots\circ\tau_{d}^{K_{i,d}})_{i=1}^{d}\rangle.

We affirm that for all
i\in[d]
, there are
l_{i,i},l_{i,i+1},\ldots,l_{i,d}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\tau_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{l_{d}}\equiv\tau_{i}^{l_{i,i}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{l_{i,d}}~\text{ mod }H.

Indeed, we assume that this holds for
i<d
, and let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\in\{0,\ldots,K_{i,i}-1\}
such that
l_{i,i}=mK_{i,i}+r
. We define
l_{i+1,j}=l_{i,j}-mK_{i,j}
for all
j\geq i+1
and, as
K_{i,j}=0
for all
j<i
, we have that

\tau_{i}^{l_{i,i}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{l_{i,d}}\equiv\tau_{i}^{r}\tau_{i+1}^{l_{i+1,i+1}}\cdots\tau_{i+1}^{l_{i+1,d}}~\text{ mod }H.

Note that
\tau_{i}^{r}\tau_{i+1}^{l_{i+1,i+1}}\cdots\tau_{i+1}^{l_{i+1,d}}X_{0}=X_{0}
, hence, by the minimality of
K_{i,i}
, we obtain that
r=0
, concluding the induction. In this light

\tau_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{l_{d}}\equiv 0~\text{ mod }H,

which implies that
\tau_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d}^{l_{d}}\in H
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

The previous proof is based in the idea that there is a parallelepiped that generates all the mesh associated to the dynamics of
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
in
X_{0}
(that parallelepiped repeats itself periodically in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
).

With this and
4.8
, it will be possible to reduce to the case in that
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is an
s
-step nilsystem with

connected.

5.3.
Nilsystems and the property of complete independence

This section is devoted to showing how we reduce to the case where the set of Bohr recurrence has the property of complete independence. First, we prove that if we have a redundant set of Bohr recurrence we can reduce the dimensionality of the system.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
(X=G/\Gamma,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem and let
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. If
R
is redundant, then there exist
R^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
, a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
-Bohr recurrence, and a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
-nilsystem
(Y=G^{Y}/\Gamma^{Y},T^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,T^{\prime}_{d-1})
such that
G^{Y}
is a subgroup of
G
and if
R^{\prime}
is a set of recurrence for
(Y,T^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,T^{\prime}_{d-1})
, then
R
is a set of recurrence for
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
.

Proof.

Suppose
R\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is redundant and let
\boldsymbol{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
given by the definition of redundancy. Replacing
R
by
R_{v}=\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in R:\boldsymbol{v}^{T}\cdot n=0\}
, we may assume without loss of generality that for all
n\in R
,
\boldsymbol{v}^{T}\cdot n=0
. Furthermore, as
v\neq 0
we assume without loss that
v_{d}=1
. Define the integer matrix
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{d\times d}
by

M_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }i=j\\
v_{j}&\text{ if }i=d\text{ and }j\neq d\\
0&\text{ else }\\
\end{cases}.

Clearly,
M
is invertible. Note that
M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence by
4.8
. Set
R^{\prime}=\{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}:\exists\boldsymbol{m}\in M^{-1}R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d},~n_{i}=m_{i}\text{ for each }i\in[d-1]\}
,

S_{i}=T_{i}T_{d}^{-v_{i}}
for each
i\in[d]
,
and
Y=\overline{\mathcal{O}_{S_{1},\ldots,S_{d-1}}(e_{X})}
.
Clearly,
(Y,S_{1},\ldots,S_{d-1})
is a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
-nilsystem, and by inspecting the proof of
[
21
, Chapter 11, Theorem 9]
, we know that

can be represented as

with

a subgroup of
G
. Finally, since, for all

such that

T_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{n_{d}}=S_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots S_{d}^{m_{d}},

we have that if
R^{\prime}
is a set of recurrence for
(Y=G^{Y}/\Gamma^{Y},S_{1},\ldots,S_{d-1})
then
R
is a set of recurrence for
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
.
∎

It is immediate that we can apply
5.11
until we get a non-redundant set of recurrent, as the following corollary shows.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
(X=G/\Gamma,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
be a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-nilsystem and

be a set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-Bohr recurrence. Then, there exist

,

\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-Bohr recurrence and
(Y=G^{Y}/\Gamma^{Y},T^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,T^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}})
a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-nilsystem such that
G^{Y}
is a subgroup of
G
and if

is a set of recurrence for
(Y,T^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,T^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}})
then

is of recurrence for
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
.

Proof.

By applying
5.11
iteratively, we get

such that there are
R^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
a non-redundant set of
\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-Bohr recurrence and
(Y=G^{Y}/\Gamma^{Y},T^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,T^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}})
a minimal
s
-step
\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
-nilsystem such that

-step

-Weyl systems. Let

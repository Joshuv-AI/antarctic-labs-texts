Estimates of size of cycle in a predator-prey system II
Niklas L.P. Lundström, Gunnar J. Söderbacka

Abstract
We prove estimates for the maximal and minimal predator and prey populations on the unique limit cycle in a standard predator-prey system. Our estimates are valid when the cycle exhibits small predator and prey abundances and large amplitudes. The proofs consist of constructions of several Lyapunov-type functions and derivation of a large number of non-trivial estimates, and should be of independent interest. This study generalizes results proved by the authors in [ 11 ] to a wider class of systems and, in addition, it gives simpler proofs of some already known estimates. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 34D23, 34C05, 37N25. Keywords: Locating limit cycle; Locating attractor; Size of limit cycle; Lyapunov function; Lyapunov stability

We consider the predator-prey system

\displaystyle\frac{ds}{d\tau}=(h(s)-x)s,

\displaystyle\frac{dx}{d\tau}=m(s-\lambda)x,\quad\text{where}\quad h(s)=(1-s)(s+a),

Biologically, our main results imply that the modeled population exhibits very small predator and prey abundances
during a long portion in time of the cycle,
indicating that the population suffers a relatively high risk of going extinct because of random perturbations.
That the modelled predator and prey populations survives even though reaching extremely low densities may be unrealistic,
but it should be noted that in reality, predators may survive even though prey biomass is very small by feeding on alternative resources.
From (

The Rosenzweig-MacArthur system is a very simplified model of reality,

The case of

To analyze the trajectory in the region

\displaystyle\frac{ds}{dx}=\frac{(h(s)-x)s}{mx(s-\lambda)},

\displaystyle x_{min}\approx x_{max}e^{-\frac{x_{max}}{a}}\quad\text{in which}\quad x_{max}\approx\frac{(1+a)^{2}}{4}.

\displaystyle+\left(e^{\frac{\lambda}{s_{\gamma}}}\frac{s_{\gamma}+a}{1-s_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)^{\frac{m}{k}}\left(\frac{\partial z_{i}}{\partial a}\tilde{x}_{1}+z_{i}\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}-z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}\right),

in which the first term yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\left(e^{\frac{\lambda}{s_{\gamma}}}\frac{s_{\gamma}+a}{1-s_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)^{\frac{m}{k}}z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{k}\left(e^{\frac{\lambda}{s_{\gamma}}}\frac{s_{\gamma}+a}{1-s_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)^{\frac{m}{k}-1}\left(e^{\frac{\lambda}{s_{\gamma}}}\frac{1}{1-s_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\left(1-\frac{s_{\gamma}+a}{a+\lambda}\right)\right)z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\frac{\lambda}{s_{\gamma}}}\frac{s_{\gamma}+a}{1-s_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)^{\frac{m}{k}}\frac{m}{k}\left(\frac{1}{s_{\gamma}+a}-\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)}}\frac{\partial\bar{\eta}}{\partial a}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\frac{\lambda}{s_{\gamma}}}\frac{s_{\gamma}+a}{1-s_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)^{\frac{m}{k}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\partial z_{i}}{\partial a}\tilde{x}_{1}+z_{i}\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}-z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}+\frac{m}{k}\left(\frac{1}{s_{\gamma}+a}-\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}\right),

and so it suffices to ensure that the following expression is nonnegative:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial z_{i}}{\partial a}\tilde{x}_{1}+z_{i}\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}+z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}\left(-\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}+\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{s_{\gamma}+a}-\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right).

(4.27)

Recall
y=\tilde{x}_{1}/h(\lambda)
and so

\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}=-\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)^{2}}+\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}\frac{1}{h(\lambda)}

(4.28)

and since
Y=ye^{-y}
we also conclude,
for
i=0,2
, that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial z_{i}}{\partial a}

\displaystyle=\frac{dz_{i}}{dY}\frac{dY}{dy}\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}=(1-y)e^{-y}\frac{dz_{i}}{dY}\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}

(4.29)

Inserting (
4.28
) and (
4.29
) into (
4.27
) gives

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{1}(y-1)e^{-y}\frac{dz_{i}}{dY}\left(-\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}\frac{1}{h(\lambda)}+\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)^{2}}\right)+z_{i}\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}

\displaystyle+z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}\left(-\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}\frac{1}{h(\lambda)}+\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)^{2}}+\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{s_{\gamma}+a}-\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right).

(4.30)

We begin with the case
m<0.3
. Then from (
4
) we have
\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}=0
and hence (
4
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{1}(y-1)e^{-y}\frac{dz_{i}}{dY}\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)^{2}}+z_{i}\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)^{2}}+\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{s_{\gamma}+a}-\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right).

which is nonnegative (recall
y>1
and (
4.26
)) if

\displaystyle\frac{m}{k}\leq\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{a+\lambda}.

Plugging in
k\geq 1/3
and
m\leq 0.3
we see that it suffices to have
\tilde{x}_{1}\leq a+\lambda
which holds by assumption.

When
m\geq 0.3
it follows from (
4
) that
\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}=0.2
and hence (
4
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{1}(y-1)e^{-y}\frac{dz_{i}}{dY}\left(-\frac{1}{5h(\lambda)}+\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)^{2}}\right)+z_{i}\frac{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}{\partial a}

\displaystyle+z_{i}\tilde{x}_{1}\left(-\frac{1}{5h(\lambda)}+\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)^{2}}+\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{s_{\gamma}+a}-\frac{m}{k}\frac{1}{a+\lambda}\right)

which is nonnegative if

\frac{1}{5}+\frac{m}{k}\frac{h(\lambda)}{a+\lambda}\leq\frac{\tilde{x}_{1}}{h(\lambda)}=\frac{h(0.8)+m\,(0.8-\lambda\,(1-\ln\lambda+\ln 0.8))}{h(\lambda)}

which is implied by

\frac{1}{5}+\frac{m}{k}\leq\frac{\frac{1}{5}(a+\lambda)+0.6m}{a+\lambda}

and, thanks to assumptions
k\geq 1/3
and
a+\lambda\leq 0.2
, we conclude the proof.

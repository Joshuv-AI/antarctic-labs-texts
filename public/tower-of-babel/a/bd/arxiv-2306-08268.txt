Wavefront sets and descent method for finite unitary groups
Zhifeng Peng, Zhicheng Wang ∗

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . In the 1980s, Kawanaka introduced the generalized Gelfand-Graev representations (GGGRs for short) of the finite group G F G^{F} in the case where q q is a power of a good prime for G F G^{F} . An essential feature of GGGRs is that they are very closely related to the (Kawanaka) wavefront sets of the irreducible representations π \pi of G F G^{F} . In [ L7 , Theorem 11.2] , Lusztig showed that if a nilpotent element X ∈ G F X\in G^{F} is “large” for an irreducible representation π \pi , then the representation π \pi appears with “small” multiplicity in the GGGR associated to X X . In this paper, we prove that for unitary groups, if X X is the wavefront of π \pi , the multiplicity equals one, which generalizes the multiplicity one result of usual Gelfand-Graev representations. Moreover, we give an algorithm to decompose GGGRs for U n ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) and calculate the U 4 ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathrm{U}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) case by this algorithm.

be the group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

Let

G

G^{F}
(resp.
H^{F}

\langle\pi,\sigma\rangle_{H^{F}}=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{H^{F}}(\pi,\sigma).

If

By abuse of notation, for a representation

In the representation theory of finite groups of Lie type, Gelfand-Graev representations have become extremely useful in various contexts, especially for constructing cuspidal representations. For example, the Gelfand-Graev representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

have a multiplicity-free decomposition into generic representations. In particular, every cuspidal representation appears in the decomposition. Then we can construct modules of cuspidal representations via the Gelfand-Graev representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. However, in general, the Gelfand-Graev representation does not contain all cuspidal representations. It leads us to study a generalization of Gelfand-Graev representation.

Generalized Gelfand-Graev representations (or GGGRs for abbreviation) of the finite group were introduced firstly by Kawanaka
[
K1
,
K2
,
K3
]
, when
q
is a power of a good prime for
G
. His original purpose is to prove Ennola’s conjecture. Now, GGGRs have been extremely useful in many other contexts of representation theory; see a survey
[
Ge2
]
.
We may associate a nilpotent element
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{F}
with a
{\mathfrak{sl}}
-2 triple
\gamma=\{X,H,Y\}
,

[H,X]=2X,\qquad[H,Y]=-2Y,\qquad[X,Y]=H

and a representation
\Gamma_{\gamma}
of
G^{F}

called the associated GGGR (see
[
L7
, Section 2]
or section
5

[
L7
, Theorem 11.2]
, Lusztig showed that if a nilpotent element
X
is “large” for an irreducible representation
\pi
, then the representation
\pi
appears with “small” multiplicity in the GGGR
\Gamma_{\gamma}
associated to
X
. The “largest” nilpotent element
X

gives us an important invariant, which is the so-called “wavefront set”. However, the multiplicity has not been worked out even in the “largest” case, the wavefront set case.

Howe firstly introduced the notion of the wavefront set in 1981
[
H2
]
for archimedean local fields. In the finite case, wavefront sets were first introduced by Kawanaka. Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G^{F}
. The
Kawanaka wavefront set

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}

of

is defined to be an
F
-stable nilpotent orbit
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
satisfying

(WF1)
\langle\pi,\Gamma_{\gamma}\rangle\neq 0
for some
\gamma=\{X,H,Y\}
with
X\in\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}^{F}
;

(WF2)
\langle\pi,\Gamma_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rangle\neq 0
for some
\gamma^{\prime}=\{X^{\prime},H^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\}
with
X^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime F}
implies
\mathrm{dim}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{dim}\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
.

If
\gamma
is trivial, we have
\langle\pi,\Gamma_{\gamma}\rangle=\mathrm{dim}\pi\neq 0

[
L2
]
and Kawanaka
[
K2
]
, respectively. If this conjecture holds, we say the wavefront set of

is well defined. When

support. The existence of the unipotent support was established for

good by Geck

]

by Geck and Malle,
[

]

Kawanaka also conjectured a description of the wavefront sets via Lusztig correspondence. This conjecture was proved by Lusztig
[
L7
]
and was solved in the later work of Achar and Aubert
[
AA
]
and Taylor
[
T
]
with a geometric refinement of the condition (WF2).
This result implies a deep connection between the irreducible representations of
G^{F}
and the geometry of the algebraic group
G
.

In the p-adic case, the study of wavefront sets is related to the theory of automorphic forms (via their Fourier coefficients) and has found important applications in both areas. For example see

This paper is motivated by D, Jiang, D, Liu, and L Zhang’s work

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with Frobenius map

.
Let

be the dual group of

For a semisimple element

\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s)=\{\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F}):\langle\pi,R_{T^{*},s}^{G}\rangle\neq 0\textrm{ for some }T^{*}\textrm{ containing }s\}

is the Deligne-Lusztig correspondence to pair

And

\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F})=\coprod_{(s)}\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s)

where

runs over the conjugacy classes of semisimple elements. Moreover, there is a bijection (Lusztig correspondence)

\mathcal{L}_{s}:\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s)\to\mathcal{E}(C_{G^{*F}}(s),1).

The bijection
\mathcal{L}_{s}
is uniquely determined in the unitary groups case. Let

and
[a]:=\{a^{{(-q)}^{k}}|k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

are of form

C_{G^{*F}}(s)=\prod_{[a]}G^{*F}_{[a]}(s),

where
G^{*F}_{[a]}(s)
is either a general linear group or unitary group (see subsection

\mathcal{L}_{s}:\mathcal{E}(G^{F},s)\to\prod_{[a]}\mathcal{E}(G^{*F}_{[a]}(s),1).

The classification of the representations in
\mathcal{E}(G^{*F}_{[a]}(s),1)
was given by Lusztig and Srinivasan in
[

]
. So we can associate each
\pi[a]
with a partition

, and we obtained the following classification of irreducible representations:

\begin{matrix}{\mathrm{Irr}}(\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))&\longrightarrow&\left\{\cal{L}:\cal{S}\to\cal{P}\Big|\sum_{[a]}\#[a]\cdot|\cal{L}([a])|=n\right\}\\
\\
\pi&\longrightarrow&\cal{L}_{\pi}\end{matrix}

where

is the set of
[a]
and
\cal{P}
is the set of partitions, and
\cal{L}_{\pi}(a)=\lambda[a]
for each

For an irreducible representation
\pi

{\ell}{(\pi)}

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
,
\ell(\pi)=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\cdots)
be the descent index of
\pi
(see section
5
for detail). Then we have

\ell_{i}=\sum_{[a]}\#[a]\cdot\cal{L}_{\pi}([a])^{t}_{i}

and

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi)}}(\pi)=1,

where
\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi)}}(\pi)
is the generalized Whittaker model of
\pi
with respect to
F
-rational nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi)}
. Moreover, the
F
-stable nilpotent orbit by
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\ell(\pi)}
is the wavefront set of
\pi
.

In our previous work, we calculated the wavefront sets for some irreducible representations of finite symplectic groups. However, we can not get the wavefront set of each irreducible representation by the descent method. The descent indexes might not even be a partition. The reason is that we only have the Fourier-Jacobi descent in the symplectic groups case, which gives us descent indexes only consisting of even integers. However, many irreducible representations with wavefront sets correspond to a partition consisting of some odd integers. For example, the wavefront set of the trivial representation of symplectic groups corresponds to the partition
(1,1,\cdots,1)
. But the descent index of the trivial representation is
(0,2,0,2\cdots,0,2)
. Fortunately, the descent index is not far from the wavefront set. We can get the partition of the wavefront set for some irreducible representations by “taking the average of two adjacent numbers of the descent index”
[
PW
]
. In the unitary groups case, we have both the Fourier-Jacobi and Bessel descent. So the descent indexes would consist of both odd and even integers. Roughly speaking, in this paper, we would consider one of the Fourier-Jacobi descent and the Bessel descent, which lives in a smaller group, and call it for the first descent. The first descent is always an irreducible representation of a unitary group which is different from the symplectic groups case. The irreducibility is crucial in our proof and implies to the multiplicity one result in Theorem
1.1
. Actually, consider the trivial representation symplectic group, the Fourier-Jacobi descent is not irreducible. That’s why “
(0,2)
” appears in the descent index instead of
(1,1)
. However, in the unitary case, the first descent of the trivial character of
\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the trivial character of
\mathrm{U}_{n-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and the descent index is precisely the partition
(1,1\cdots,1)
.

Let us outline the strategy of the proof. For a nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})}
of
\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, consider the following nilpotent orbits:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathcal{O}_{1}:=&\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,1,\cdots,1)}&\subset&\mathrm{U}_{n_{1}}:=&\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\mathcal{O}_{2}:=&\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{2},1,1,\cdots,1)}&\subset&\mathrm{U}_{n_{2}}:=&\mathrm{U}_{n-\lambda_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\mathcal{O}_{3}:=&\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{3},1,1,\cdots,1)}&\subset&\mathrm{U}_{n_{3}}:=&\mathrm{U}_{n-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\mathcal{O}_{k}:=&\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{k})}&\subset&\mathrm{U}_{n_{k}}:=&\mathrm{U}_{\lambda_{k}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\\
\end{array}.

Since the stabilizer of the orbits
\mathcal{O}_{i}
in
\mathrm{U}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the next unitary group
\mathrm{U}_{n_{i+1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the generalized Whittaker model

\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(\pi)

is a representation of
\mathrm{U}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then we can define the generalized Whittaker model of generalized Whittaker model

\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(\pi))

and inductively

\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\cdots\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(\pi))).

We shall apply a finite field analog of the root exchanging technique of Ginzburg-Rallis-Soudry
[
GRS1
,
GRS2
]
to prove the following equation (Section
6
):

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi)=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}(\cdots\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(\pi))).

Therefore, we only have to calculate this kind of generalized Whittaker model:

\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell,1,1,\cdots,1}}(\pi).

Then the calculation of this kind of generalized Whittaker model has been completed in previous works
[
LW2
,
Wang2
]
. In particular, if
\ell
is the first occurrence index of
\pi
(see Section

), then the generalized Whittaker model

is irreducible, which brings us to the multiplicity one result. In general, for an arbitrary nilpotent orbit

, the root exchanging technique still works even if

is not the first occurrence index. So, in principle, one can calculate the dimension of the generalized Whittaker model for any nilpotent orbits. We shall calculate the
\mathrm{U}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

However, in the general case, although the generalized Whittaker model

still can be calculated explicitly, it contains a large number of irreducible constitutes, and the description of the composition of generalized Whittaker models would be very complicated.

In

Let

(Lusztig)
.

There is a bijection

5.
Gan-Gross-Prasad problem and descents for finite unitary groups

5.1.
Construction of descents

Roughly speaking, the Gan-Gross-Prasad problem concerns the generalized Whittaker model of an irreducible representation
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F})
on an
F
-rational nilpotent orbits
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
with
\lambda=(\ell,1,1,\cdots,1)
.
According to the parity of
\ell

G^{F}=\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):=\left\{x\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\ F(x)=x\right\}

where

F:(x_{i,j})\to(x^{q}_{n+1-j,n+1-j})^{-1}.

Now, we give the explicit construction of

for above unitary groups.

(1)

. Assume that
\ell
is even, and let

. Let

\mathcal{O}^{**}=\bigtriangledown_{{m-\ell-1},{n-\ell}}(\mathcal{O}_{(d_{2},\cdots,d_{s})})=\mathcal{O}_{(d_{2}+1,\cdots,d_{s}+1,1,\cdots,1)}.

Note that
m
and
\ell+1
are both even.
By Lemma
6.2
, we have

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{**}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}(\Theta_{n,m}(\pi^{\vee})))=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\Theta_{n,m}(\pi^{\vee})).

It follows from Theorem
4.7
that

\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\Theta_{n,m}(\pi^{\vee}))\cong\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi).

So

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\Theta_{n,m}(\pi^{\vee}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{**}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}(\Theta_{n,m}(\pi^{\vee})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{**}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}(\Theta_{n,m}(\pi^{\vee}))).

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume that
G=\mathrm{U}_{n}
, and
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
G^{F}
.
Let
\cal{O}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{(\ell,d_{2},\cdots,d_{s})}
be an
F
-rational nilpotent orbit of
\mathfrak{g}^{F}
. Then

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi)=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(d_{2},\cdots,d_{s})}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\ell,1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)).

Proof.

We have proved this lemma for odd
\ell
in Lemma
6.3
and for both
n
and
\ell
even in Lemma
6.2
.
We only need to prove the case where
\ell
is even, and
n
is odd. This case follows from the odd
\ell
case and similar argument in the proof of Lemma
6.3
.
∎

7.
Proof of main results

In this section, we prove our main theorems. Theorem
7.1
contains a explicit description of the wavefront set and a multiplicity one result. We mention that the multiplicity one result follows from that fact: the first descent of an irreducible representation is also an irreducible representation.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that
G=\mathrm{U}_{n}
is a unitary group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G^{F})
. Let
\ell(\pi)=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\cdots)
be the descent index sequence of
\pi
.

(i) We have

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi)}}(\pi)=1.

(ii)
The nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi)}
is the wavefront set of
\pi
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by induction on
n
.

(i) By Corollary
5.5
, we know that

•

\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\ell_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)\cong{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell_{1}}(\pi)=\pi^{\prime},

where
\pi^{\prime}
is an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{U}_{n-\ell_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
defined in Corollary
5.5
;

•

the descent index sequence
\ell(\pi^{\prime})
of
\pi^{\prime}
is equal to
(\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\cdots)
.

By our induction assumption on
n
, we have

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi^{\prime})}}(\pi^{\prime})=1.

According to Lemma
6.4
, we have

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi)}}(\pi)=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\cdots)}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\ell_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi))=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi^{\prime})}}(\pi^{\prime})=1.

(ii) Assume that
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots)}
be the wavefront set of
\pi
. In
[
T
, Section 1.5]
, there is a
geometric refinement of the condition (WF2) in the definition of wavefront sets as follows:

(WF
2^{\prime}
)
\langle\pi,\Gamma_{\gamma^{\prime}}\rangle\neq 0
for some
\gamma^{\prime}=\{X^{\prime},H^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\}
with
X^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{F}
implies
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
.

In the unitary groups case,
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
is equivalent to
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda
, where
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda
is the parabolic ordering, for each
k
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}.

Recall that we have already proved that
\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell(\pi^{\prime})}}(\pi^{\prime})=1
. So
\lambda_{1}\geq\ell_{1}
. If
\lambda_{1}>\ell_{1}
, then by Lemma
6.4
,

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots)}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi))=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi)\neq 0,

which implies that
\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)\neq 0
. This contradicts the definition of
\ell(\pi)
. So
\lambda_{1}=\ell_{1}
and

\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)=\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\ell_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)=\pi^{\prime}

is an irreducible representation of
\mathrm{U}_{n-\ell_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. It immediately follows from Lemma
6.4
that
(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots)
is the wavefront set of
\pi
. Then by our induction assumption on
n
, we have
(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots)=(\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\cdots)
, which completes the proof.

∎

If the nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}
is not the wavefront of
\pi
, in principle, we can still calculate the dimension of
\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)
in a similar way. The following theorem is the opposite of (WF
2^{\prime}
).

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\pi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
,
\ell(\pi)=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\cdots)
be the descent index of
\pi
. If
\lambda\leq\ell(\pi)
, then

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi)>0.

Proof.

Suppose that
\pi\in\cal{E}(G^{F},s)
, and the image of
\pi
under the Lusztig correspondence is

\cal{L}_{s}(\pi)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\pi{[a_{i}]},

where
\pi{[a_{i}]}
is the unipotent representation corresponding to partition
\lambda[a_{i}]
for each
[a_{i}]
.

If
k=n
, then
\pi{[a_{i}]}
is a character of
\mathrm{U}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Therefore, for each
i
,
\pi{[a_{i}]}
is a generic representation and so is
\pi
. There is nothing to prove.

Assume that
k<n
. We prove the theorem by induction on
n
. By our assumption that the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is sufficient, we can pick an element
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
different from
\{a_{i}\}
. Denote by
\pi^{\prime}:={\mathcal{D}}_{\ell_{1}}(\pi)\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{U}_{n-\ell_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),s^{\prime})
. Then the set of eigenvalues of the semisimple element
s^{\prime}
is a subset of
\{a_{i}\}
. Let

\cal{L}_{s^{\prime}}(\pi^{\prime})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\pi^{\prime}{[a_{i}]}.

Consider the semisimple element

s_{b}:=\mathrm{diag}(b,\cdots,b,s^{\prime})\in\mathrm{U}_{n-\lambda_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Let
\pi_{b}\in\cal{E}(\mathrm{U}_{n-\lambda_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),s_{b})
such that the image of
\pi
under the Lusztig correspondence is

\pi[b]\otimes\prod_{i=1}^{k}\pi^{\prime}{[a_{i}]}

where
\pi[b]\cong\pi_{(1,1,\cdots,1)}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{U}_{\ell_{1}-\lambda_{1}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
is the component corresponding to
[b]
. By Corollary
5.5
, we have

\ell(\pi_{b})=(\ell_{2}+\ell_{1}-\lambda_{1},\ell_{3},\ell_{4},\cdots)\geq(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots).

By our induction assumption, we have

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots)}}(\pi_{b})>0.

According to Corollary
5.5
, we know that
\pi_{b}
is an irreducible constitute of the generalized Whittaker model
\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi)=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots)}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi))\geq\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots)}}(\pi_{b})>\ 0.

∎

8.
Calculation of Generalized Gelfand-Graev Representations

Rainbolt studies the GGGRs of
\mathrm{U}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
in
[
Ra
]
. In the general case, little is known. In this section, we induce an algorithm to calculate the generalized Gelfand-Graev representations of unitary groups. This algorithm is based on our calculation in section
5.2
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k})}
be a nilpotent orbit in
\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and
\Gamma_{\lambda}
be the generalized Gelfand-Graev representation corresponding to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
. To calculate the generalized Gelfand-Graev representation
\Gamma_{\lambda}
, we only need to calculate

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{U}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\pi,\Gamma_{\lambda})=\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi).

By Lemma
6.4
, we know that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\pi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots})}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4},\cdots)}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{2},1,\cdots,1)}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{k},1,\cdots,1)}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{k-1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{k-2},1,\cdots,1)}}(\cdots(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{(\lambda_{1},1,\cdots,1)}}(\pi)))

So we can reduce the calculation to this kind of generalized Whittaker model

\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell,1,\cdots,1}}(\pi).

Let
\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{U}_{n-\ell}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))
. In Section
5
, we give a explicit description of

m(\pi,\pi^{\prime}):=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{U}_{n-\ell}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}(\mathrm{Wh}_{\mathcal{O}_{\ell,1,\cdots,1}}(\pi),\pi^{\prime}).

So, in principle, one can calculate the dimension
of arbitrary generalized Whittaker models. However, in general, generalized Whittaker models contain
a large number of irreducible constituents, and the calculation would be very complicated. For example, we will use this approach to calculate the
\mathrm{U}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
case.

Abstract
. Response solutions are quasi-periodic ones with the same frequency as the forcing term. The present work is devoted to constructing response solutions for d d -dimensional nonlinear plate models with nonlocal energy damping, which are closely related to damping phenomena in flight structures. For such models, the main characteristic is that the dissipation rate depends on the energy strength. By considering a small parameter ϵ \epsilon in the domain excluding the origin and imposing a small quasi-periodic forcing with a Diophantine frequency vector, we demonstrate the persistence of the corresponding response solution. We provide an alternative approach to the contraction mapping principle (cf. [ 7 , 33 ] ) through a combination of reduction together with the Nash–Moser iteration technique. The reason behind this approach lies in the derivative losses caused by the nonlocal nonlinearity.

1.
Introduction

Problem formulation and a related literature overview

The present work is devoted to the study of the vibrations of a nonlinear plate model associated with the instantaneous energy

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

with
\lambda>0
being a structural positive constant, which is affected by the displacement
u
and the velocity
u_{t}
(see
[
31
]
). For an integer
d\geq 1
, denote by
\mathbb{T}^{d}
the
d
-dimensional torus. Let
\Delta=\sum^{d}_{j=1}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}}
represent the Laplace operator. More precisely, we consider the following plate model with nonlocal nonlinear damping and quasi-periodic external forcing:

\displaystyle u_{tt}+\lambda\Delta^{2}u-\mu\Delta u+\epsilon u_{t}+\alpha\Big(\lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)u_{t}=\epsilon^{2}g(\omega t,x)

(1.1)

for
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Here,
\mu,\alpha
are positive constants,
0<\epsilon\ll 1
is a sufficiently small parameter,
\omega=(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
is a
\nu
-dimensional forcing frequency vector for some integer
\nu>1
, and
g:\mathbb{T}^{\nu}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes an external forcing term with zero average, namely,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{\nu+d}}g(\varphi,x)\mathrm{d}\varphi\mathrm{d}x=0.

From a mathematical perspective, Tavares et al.
[
31
]

investigated the long-time dynamics including the existence of global attractors and established its quasi-stability.
Due to the influence of the strength of the energy on the dissipation rate and the wide application of this model, such as in aeroelasticity (see NASA–Air Force reports
[
1
,
2
]
), this model has attracted significant interest. Other related models, where the damping term
u_{t}
is replaced by
u_{xxt}
in nonlinear nonlocal damping, were originally proposed by Balakrishnan and Taylor on flight structures in the one dimensional case; see
[
2
]
. For further references on extensible beams with nonlocal nonlinear damping of Balakrishnan–Taylor type, we can refer to
[
20
]
and the references included in it.

Additionally, a class of new models related to beam equations with nonlocal nonlinear damping has attracted significant recent interest. For example,
this type of model with a nonlocal nonlinearity of Kirchhoff type was proposed by Lange and Perla Menzala in
[
21
]
, which are closely connected to nonlinear Schrödinger equations with time-dependent dissipation.
They derived the global existence of classical solutions by imposing suitable initial conditions. Since then, many works associated with other partial differential equations (PDEs) with nonlocal nonlinear damping have been presented in literature. Cavalcanti et al.
[
9
]
established the global existence and uniform decay rates of solutions to viscoelastic beam equations. Moreover, the authors
[
10
]
investigated both the well-posedness and the exponential stability of solutions for wave equations. Pucci and Saldi
[
26
]
investigated the asymptotic stability of solutions for fractional
p
-Laplacian equations. Additionally, there has been growing interest in the exploration of nonlinear beam equations; see
[
11
,
12
,
35

15

19

17

Motivated by the aforementioned physical and mathematical studies, the present paper focuses on the existence of response solutions for beam equations with nonlocal nonlinear damping under Diophantine frequency conditions. A solution of equation (
1.1
) is referred to as a response solution if it is quasi-periodic with the same frequency vector
\omega
as that of the forcing function
g
(cf.
[
7
]
), which signifies the simplest harmonic responses to the external forcing.

Other related literature overview

Numerous studies have addressed the existence of response solutions in various contexts. The research on response solutions was initially focused on ordinary differential equations (ODEs), i.e., quasi-periodic forced harmonic oscillator systems with positive damping coefficients. Stoker
[
29
]
first formulated this problem and established the proof of existence, while simultaneously raising the question of whether such solutions persist as damping coefficients approach zero. Subsequently, Moser
[
25
]
answered this question in certain cases for sufficiently small damping coefficients; see also the similar conclusions obtained by Braaksma and Broer
[
6
]
together with Friedman
[
16
]
. Recently, Si et al. showed that the response solution persists in the cases with sufficiently large damping coefficients. We can also refer to
[
22
,
34
,
23
]
for other studies related to the existence of response solutions in the case where damping coefficients vanish. One of the challenges we face when discussing the problem related to the preservation of response solutions in quasi-periodically forced systems is the so-called small denominator problems. Hence, the proof of the above results relies on Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser (KAM) theory if the frequency vector is of Diophantine or Liouvillean type. Moreover, Calleja et al.
[
8
]
and Gentile and Vaia
[
18
]
found alternative approaches to KAM theory using the contraction mapping principle and the properties of continued fractions, respectively.

Before introducing some related work on response solutions to PDEs, we have to refer to the pioneering bifurcation result given by Rabinowitz
[
27
,
28
]
, for fully nonlinear forced wave equations with small damping. The main challenges encountered in the study of response solutions to PDEs are inconsistent with ODEs. Unlike ODEs, there are two main approaches to deal with response solutions to Hamiltonian PDEs. The first approach involves applying the infinite-dimensional KAM theory; see
[
32
,
30
]
. The other one, known as the Craig–Wayne–Bourgain (CWB) method, is founded on the Lyapunov–Schmidt procedure as well as the Nash–Moser theorem; see
[
5
,
3
,
14
]
. Additionally, these studies related
to response solutions to PDEs with the Kirchhoff term could be found in
[
13
,
24
]
. Recent years have seen growing interest in response solutions for damped PDEs. Concerning PDEs subject to very strong damping, Calleja et al.
[
7
]
were inspired by the procedure adopted in
[
8
]
and established the existence of response solutions for wave equations. Correspondingly, Wang and de la Llave
[
33
]
presented such solutions for ill-posed Boussinesq equations indeed exist.

Mathematical challenge

The primary objective of this work is to investigate the persistence of response solutions to equation (
1.1
). Before addressing the technical challenges, we first examine the limitations of the methods developed
[
7
,
33
]
.
In the analytic or differentiable case, the authors transformed the original equation into the fixed point equation of the form
v_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}(v_{\epsilon})
, considered
\epsilon

in a domain excluding the origin
\epsilon=0
, and employed the contraction mapping principle since the corresponding operator
\mathcal{T}_{\epsilon}
was just from a certain function space to itself. However, the techniques and methods developed in
[
7
,
33
]
can not be directly applied to our model (
1.1
). The main challenge in performing these methods is the loss of derivatives caused by the nonlinearity. To overcome this fundamental difficulty, we employ an alternative approach based on Nash-Moser iteration. The key ingredient is to verify the invertibility of the linearized operator and to provide an appropriate estimate for its inverse operator.
To achieve this goal, we are devoted to constructing an invertible transformation, which conjugates the linearized operator to an operator
\mathcal{D}
with constant coefficients plus a remainder
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
. In this process, we encountered the challenge known as the characteristic “small divisors problem” arising from resonance phenomena. In order to overcome this difficulty, we have to assume that the frequency vector is Diophantine. On the other hand, the principal part
\mathcal{D}
admits a diagonal representation with respect to Fourier bases in both temporal and spatial variables. Through careful analysis, we invert
\mathcal{D}
and give the regularising nature of
\mathcal{D}^{-1}
for
\epsilon
in a domain that does not include the origin
\epsilon=0
. As a consequence, there is a straightforward consequence that
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is relatively bounded with respect to
\mathcal{D}^{-1}
.
In our study, the mathematical analysis presents significant technical difficulties and challenges.
The theoretical framework developed here can be extended to establish the existence of response solutions to the singular perturbation structure PDEs with nonlocal energy
damping such as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}u_{t}+\alpha\Big(\lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)u_{t},

which exhibits singular behavior as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

Structure of the paper

The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
states the main theorem. In Section
3
, we construct an invertible transformation to reduce the linearized operator to an operator with constant coefficients plus a remainder under non-resonance conditions. Subsequently, by considering
\epsilon
in a domain that excludes the origin
\epsilon=0
and a priori bounds for the high Sobolev norms of the solution, we prove the invertibility of the linearized operator, which is the crucial ingredient in the Nash–Moser method. Then we provide a proper estimate for its inverse. In Section
4
, we present the Nash–Moser iteration scheme and complete the proof of the main theorem.

3.
Reducibility and invertability of the linearized operator

In this section, we are devoted to verifying the invertibility of the linearized operator and establishing estimates of its inverse operator.
Due to the variable coefficients in the linearized operator, we need to construct an invertible transformation which conjugates it to a constant-coefficient operator plus a remainder term.

According to Lemma

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\epsilon,\omega,u)h

\displaystyle=\mathrm{D}\mathcal{F}(\epsilon,\omega,u)[h]

The following lemma corresponds to the invertibility of the main part

Lemma 3.3

Let
\rho>0,s>0

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{-1}h\|_{\rho,s}\leq K_{0}\delta^{-1}\|h\|_{\rho,s}.

Proof.

It is straightforward that for

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}h=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu},j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\Theta_{\epsilon}(\omega,j)h_{k,j}e^{\mathrm{i}(k\cdot\varphi+j\cdot x)},

\displaystyle\Theta_{\epsilon}(\omega,j)=-(\omega\cdot k)^{2}+\lambda|j|^{4}+\mu|j|^{2}+\mathrm{i}(\epsilon^{2p}\iota(\omega\cdot k)+\epsilon(\omega\cdot k))

\displaystyle|\Theta_{\epsilon}(\varsigma,j)|^{2}=(\lambda|j|^{4}+\mu|j|^{2}-\varsigma^{2})^{2}+(\epsilon^{2p}\iota\varsigma+\epsilon\varsigma)^{2},

In the subsequent analysis, a positive integer

\mathbf{Case~a}

\displaystyle\varsigma^{2}=\lambda|j|^{4}+\mu|j|^{2}\in[1,J_{0}].

Define two regions in
\varsigma\in\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle I^{-}_{1}

\displaystyle:=[-(1+10^{-3}){J}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0},-(1-10^{-3})],

\displaystyle I^{+}_{1}

\displaystyle:=[1-10^{-3},(1+10^{-3})J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0}].

For

\displaystyle\text{either}\quad 0<|\varsigma|<1-10^{-3}\quad\text{or}\quad|\varsigma|>(1+10^{-3})J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0}.

If

In the latter, one obtains that for

\displaystyle|\Theta_{\epsilon}(\varsigma,j)|^{2}\geq(\lambda|j|^{4}+\mu|j|^{2}-\varsigma^{2})^{2}\geq(((1+10^{-3})J_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}-J_{0})^{2}\geq 10^{-6}J_{0}^{2}.

Moreover, we can derive that for

\displaystyle 1-10^{-3}\leq|\varsigma|\leq(1+10^{-3})J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0}.

Since

3.4

\displaystyle|\Theta_{\epsilon}(\varsigma,j)|^{2}\geq(\epsilon^{2p}\iota\varsigma+\epsilon\varsigma)^{2}\geq\epsilon^{2}\varsigma^{2}(1-\epsilon^{2p-1}|\iota|)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{16}\delta^{2}(1-10^{-3})^{2}.

\mathbf{Case~b}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}J_{0}<\lambda|j|^{4}+\mu|j|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}J_{0}\leq\varsigma^{2}\leq\lambda|j|^{4}+\mu|j|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}J_{0}.

\displaystyle|\mathrm{D}^{l}u|_{z,\rho}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},|\alpha|=l}|\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u|_{z,\rho}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},|\alpha|=l}\max_{z\in\mathbb{T}^{m}_{\rho}}|\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u(z)|.

(A.8)

Moreover, we fix
s_{1}>{m}/{2}
in the Appendix.

In the following lemma, we are devoted to discussing the relationship between the Sobolev norm
\|\cdot\|_{z,\rho,s}
and the max norm
|\cdot|_{z,\rho,s}
(cf.
[
4
,
30
]
).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
u
be a real analytic function on
\mathbb{T}^{m}_{\rho}
with
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}u(z)\mathrm{d}z=0
. For
\rho>0,s\geq 0
,
there exists a constant
{C}>0
such that for
u\in H^{\rho,s+s_{1}}_{z,0}
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle|u|_{z,\rho,s}\leq{C}\|u\|_{z,\rho,s+s_{1}},\quad\|u\|_{z,\rho,s}\leq C|u|_{z,\rho,s+s_{1}}.

(A.9)

Proof.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}u(z)\mathrm{d}z=0
, which implies
u_{0}=0
, we can obtain

\displaystyle|u|_{z,\rho,s}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}|u_{k}|\langle k\rangle^{s}e^{\rho|k|}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}|u_{k}|^{2}\langle k\rangle^{2(s+s_{1})}e^{2\rho|k|})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\frac{1}{\langle k\rangle^{2s_{1}}})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{z,\rho,s+s_{1}}.

Therefore, the first inequality in (
A.9
) holds.

It remains to verify the second inequality in (
A.9
). Since
u
is analytic on
\mathbb{T}^{m}_{\rho}
, the Fourier coefficients
u_{k}
have the following decay property:

\displaystyle|u_{k}|\leq|u|_{z,\rho}e^{-\rho|k|}.

Moreover,
\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u
is also analytic on
\mathbb{T}^{m}_{\rho}
with

\displaystyle(\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u)_{k}=u_{k}(\mathrm{i}k)^{\alpha},\quad(\mathrm{i}k)^{\alpha}=(\mathrm{i}k_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(\mathrm{i}k_{m})^{\alpha_{m}}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle|(\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u)_{k}|=|u_{k}||(\mathrm{i}k)^{\alpha}|\leq|\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u|_{z,\rho}e^{-\rho|k|}.

According to (
A.8
), if
|\alpha|=s+s_{1}
, then

\displaystyle|u_{k}||\mathrm{i}k|^{s+s_{1}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=s+s_{1}}|u_{k}||(\mathrm{i}k)^{\alpha}|=\sum_{|\alpha|=s+s_{1}}|(\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u)_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=s+s_{1}}|\mathrm{D}^{\alpha}u|_{z,\rho}e^{-\rho|k|}=|\mathrm{D}^{s+s_{1}}u|_{z,\rho}e^{-\rho|k|}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{z,\rho,s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{2\rho|k|}\langle k\rangle^{2s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\backslash\{0\}}|u_{k}|^{2}e^{2\rho|k|}|k|^{2(s+s_{1})}|k|^{-2s_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{D}^{s+s_{1}}u|^{2}_{z,\rho}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\backslash\{0\}}|k|^{-2s_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C|u|^{2}_{z,\rho,s+s_{1}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
u
be a real analytic function on
\mathbb{T}^{m}_{\rho}
with
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}u(z)\mathrm{d}z=0
. For
0\leq\breve{\rho}<\rho,\sigma>0,s\geq 0
, if
u\in H^{\rho,s}_{z,0}
, then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\rho-\breve{\rho},s+\sigma}\leq\big(\frac{\sigma}{e}\big)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{\breve{\rho}^{\sigma}}\|u\|_{\rho,s}.

(A.10)

Proof.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}u(z)\mathrm{d}z=0
and

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta^{\sigma}}{e^{\breve{\rho}\zeta}}\leq\big(\frac{\sigma}{e}\big)^{\sigma}\frac{1}{\breve{\rho}^{\sigma}},\quad\forall\zeta\geq 0,

we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{\rho-\breve{\rho},s+\sigma}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}|u_{k}|^{2}\langle k\rangle^{2(s+\sigma)}e^{2(\rho-\breve{\rho})|k|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},k\neq 0}|u_{k}|^{2}|k|^{2s}e^{2\rho|k|}\frac{|k|^{2\sigma}}{e^{2\breve{\rho}|k|}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\frac{\sigma}{e}\big)^{2\sigma}\frac{1}{\breve{\rho}^{2\sigma}}\|u\|^{2}_{\rho,s}.

This ends the proof of the lemma.
∎

Team MMSE Precoding with Applications to Cell-free Massive MIMO
Lorenzo Miretti, Emil Björnson, David Gesbert

Abstract
This article studies a novel distributed precoding design, coined team minimum mean-square error (TMMSE) precoding, which rigorously generalizes classical centralized MMSE precoding to distributed operations based on transmitter-specific channel state information (CSIT). Building on the so-called theory of teams , we derive a set of necessary and sufficient conditions for optimal TMMSE precoding, in the form of an infinite dimensional linear system of equations. These optimality conditions are further specialized to cell-free massive MIMO networks, and explicitly solved for two important examples, i.e., the classical case of local CSIT and the case of unidirectional CSIT sharing along a serial fronthaul. The latter case is relevant, e.g., for the recently proposed radio stripe concept and the related advances on sequential processing exploiting serial connections. In both cases, our optimal design outperforms the heuristic methods that are known from the previous literature. Duality arguments and numerical simulations validate the effectiveness of the proposed team theoretical approach in terms of ergodic achievable rates under a sum-power constraint.

Introduction

Inter-cell interference is a major limiting factor of wireless communication systems capitalizing on aggressive spectrum reuse and network densification to increase capacity. To mitigate this effect, future generation systems are expected to implement advanced cooperative communication techniques, in particular by letting geographically distributed base stations jointly serve their users. However, the practical deployment of cooperative wireless networks is currently prevented by the severe scalability issue arising from network-wide processing

network MIMO

I-A

Cooperative transmission with distributed CSIT

In this work we explore a downlink (DL) cooperation regime with full data sharing and general

[

8
,
9

This assumption is relevant, e.g., for all service situations where, compared to data sharing, CSIT sharing needs to be performed within much tighter time constraints, and hence may dominate the fronthaul overhead. For instance, it is suitable in case of rapidly varying channels due to user mobility, where full CSIT sharing may result in outdated information or occupy an excessive portion of the coherence time, or when delay-tolerant data is proactively made available at the TXs using caching techniques (see

However, due to space limitations and to better focus on limited CSIT sharing, in this work, we do not consider network clusterization. In particular, we do not cover complementary service situations where CSIT can be more easily shared than data, hence entering the realm of interference

Nevertheless, if combined with network clustering techniques, the results presented in this study can be seen as a first step towards a more general theory jointly covering limited data and CSIT sharing.

Summary of contributions and related works

Although the importance of cooperative transmission schemes based on limited CSIT sharing has been acknowledged in the literature, a satisfactory understanding of systems with distributed CSIT is still missing. II-C

Performance metric

We measure the network performance under the specified transmission scheme by using Shannon (ergodic) rates

III
Team MMSE precoding

In this work, we study the following novel
team
MMSE precoding design criterion: given a vector of nonnegative weights
\mathbf{w}:=[w_{1},\ldots,w_{K}]^{\mathsf{T}}
belonging to the simplex
\mathcal{W}:=\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\;|\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_{k}=K\}
, we consider the functional optimization problem

\underset{\mathbbm{t}_{k}\in\mathcal{T}}{\text{minimize}}\;\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{t}_{k}):=\mathsf{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{W}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{H}\mathbbm{t}_{k}-\mathbf{e}_{k}\right\|^{2}+\dfrac{\|\mathbbm{t}_{k}\|^{2}}{P}\right],

(5)

where
\mathbf{W}:=\mathrm{diag}(w_{1},\ldots,w_{K})
,
\mathbf{e}_{k}
is the
k
-th column of
\mathbf{I}_{K}
, and
P:=P_{\mathrm{sum}}/K
. A solution to the above problem can be recognized as a distributed version of the classical centralized MMSE precoding design
[
22
]
. For
P\to\infty
, it can be interpreted as the ‘closest’ distributed approximation of the ZF solution. By means of team theoretical arguments
[
24
,
25
]
, this section provides rigorous yet practical guidelines for optimally solving Problem (
5
). Before providing the main results of this section, we also revisit the effectiveness of the MSE criterion in terms of network performance, which is well-known for centralized precoding.

.

Hereafter, with the exception of Section

, we consider w.l.o.g.
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{I}
. The general case will readily follow by replacing
\mathbbm{H}_{l}

III-A

Achievable rates via uplink-downlink duality

This section discusses the formal connection between the objective of Problem (
5
) and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{hard}}
by revisiting UL-DL duality under a general distributed CSIT assumption.

Theorem 1
.

Consider an arbitrary set of distributed precoders
\{\mathbbm{t}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
and weights
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
. Then, any rate tuple
(R_{1},\ldots,R_{K})\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}
such that

R_{k}\leq\log(\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{t}_{k}))^{-1}

(6)

belongs to
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{hard}}
. The power allocation policy
\{p_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
achieving the above inner bound is given in Appendix
A-A
, and satisfies
\sum_{l}\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{x}_{l}\|^{2}]=P_{\mathrm{sum}}
. Furthermore, if
\mathbbm{t}_{k}
solves Problem (
5
)
\forall k\in\mathcal{K}
, then
(R_{1},\ldots,R_{K})
with
R_{k}=\log(\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{t}_{k}))^{-1}
is Pareto optimal, and every rate tuple in
\partial\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{hard}}
is obtained for some
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on connecting Problem (
5
) to the problem of ergodic rate maximization in a dual UL channel, where
\mathbf{w}
is an UL power allocation vector,
\mathbbm{t}_{k}
is a distributed UL combiner, and where achievable rates are measured by using the so-called
use-and-then-forget
(UatF) bound
[
22
, Theorem 4.4]
. The details are given in Appendix
A-A
.
∎

Theorem
1
states that the Pareto boundary of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{hard}}
can be parametrized by
K-1
nonnegative real parameters, i.e., by the weights
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
. A similar parametrization was already known for deterministic channels (see, e.g.,
[
15
]
), or, equivalently, for fading channels with perfect CSIT and CSIR. This work extends the aforementioned results to imperfect and possibly distributed CSIT, and no CSIR. In theory,

is often fixed heuristically (e.g., from the real UL powers), while the network utility is optimized a posteriori by varying the DL power allocation policy

From a precoding design point of view, Theorem
1
generalizes the duality-based argument behind classical MMSE precoding given by
[
22
]
. While
[
22
]
motivates the MMSE solution as the optimal combiner maximizing a dual UL ergodic rate bound based on coherent decoding, the proof of Theorem
1
directly relates the MSE criterion to the more conservative UatF bound. This last point is particularly relevant under distributed CSIT, where an optimal solution to the coherent ergodic rate maximization problem is not known in general. We recall that, in turn,
[
22
]
generalizes classical duality-based arguments for deterministic channels to fading channels. As a concluding remark, we stress that the inner bound (
6
) should not be confused with the well-known inner bound based on the notion of (weighted) MSE on the DL channel
[
33
]
, where the precoders for

\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{t}_{k}^{\star})<\infty
and if the following set of equalities hold

\nabla_{\mathbf{t}_{l,k}}\mathsf{E}\left[c_{k}(\mathbbm{H},\mathbbm{t}_{-l,k}^{\star},\mathbf{t}_{l,k})\middle|S_{l}\right]\bigg|_{\mathbf{t}_{l,k}=\mathbbm{t}_{l,k}^{\star}(S_{l})}=\mathbf{0}\quad\mathrm{a.s.},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

(8)

where
(\mathbbm{t}_{-l,k},\mathbf{t}_{l,k}):=(\mathbbm{t}_{1,k},\ldots,\mathbbm{t}_{l-1,k},\mathbf{t}_{l,k},\mathbbm{t}_{l+1,k},\ldots,\mathbbm{t}_{L,k})
.

By evaluating the stationary conditions (
8
) using standard results on differentiation of real-valued quadratic forms over a complex domain, we obtain that a stationary solution may be given by any solution to the following feasibility problem:

\text{find }\mathbbm{t}_{k}\in\mathcal{T}\text{ s.t. }\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,l}|S_{l}]\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{l,k}(S_{l})+\sum_{j\neq l}\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,j}\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{j,k}|S_{l}]-\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{g}_{l,k}|S_{l}]=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{a.s.},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

(9)

where
\mathbbm{Q}_{l,l}:=\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{H}_{l}+\frac{1}{P}\mathbf{I}
,
\mathbbm{Q}_{l,j}:=\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{H}_{j}
for
j\neq l
, and
\mathbbm{g}_{l,k}:=\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{e}_{k}
, provided that all expectations are finite.
Since the considered quadratic cost
c_{k}
is convex and differentiable a.s. in each of the
\mathbf{t}_{l,k}
, under some mild technical assumptions the notion of stationarity can be interpreted as enforcing each function
\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{l,k}(S_{l})
to be optimal while keeping the functions
\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{j,k}(S_{j})
of all the other TXs
j\neq l
fixed. This is reminiscent of the game theoretical notion of
Nash equilibrium
, with the difference that here all the TXs share the same objective, and hence they act as a team. Similarly to Nash equilibria, stationary solutions may be in general inefficient, i.e., lead to a local optimum. However, a stronger result holds for quadratic teams:

Theorem 2
.

If
\mathbbm{Q}
is uniformly bounded above, i.e., there exists a positive scalar
B<\infty
such that
\mathbbm{Q}\prec B\mathbf{I}
a.s., then Problem (
5
) admits a unique optimal solution, which is also the unique stationary solution solving Problem (
9
).

Proof.

Theorem 2.6.6 of
[
25
]
.
∎

Theorem
2
and Problem (
9
) are of fundamental theoretical importance since they concisely identify the two key ingredients for optimal distributed precoding design:

1.

Robustness against local channel estimation errors, captured by
\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,l}|S_{l}]
and
\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{g}_{l,k}|S_{l}]
;

2.

Robustness against the effect of the “decisions” taken at the other TXs, captured by
\sum_{j\neq l}\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,j}\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{j,k}|S_{l}]
. This is the main new difficulty which is introduced while moving from centralized to distributed precoding design.

From a practical perspective, the above results also provide a very powerful tool to solve the difficult distributed precoding design problem. Specifically, they provide a set of
optimality conditions
for Problem (
5
) in the form of a standard infinite dimensional
linear
feasibility problem, for which many approximate solution methods are available. For instance, the optimal TMMSE precoders may be approached via one of the iterative methods surveyed in
[
25
]
based on interpreting the solution to (
9
) as the unique fixed point of a linear map. Other promising methods may also include finite dimensional approximations of (
9
) obtained, e.g., by sampling the CSI process
(\mathbbm{H},S_{1},\ldots,S_{L})
and by interpreting the sampled version of (
9
) as a classical function interpolation problem from a finite set of linear measurements
[
34
]
. Further discussions on approximate solution methods are left for future work, and most parts of this study will focus on cell-free massive MIMO networks and in particular on special cases where (
9
) can be solved explicitly. However, we remark that the content of this section can be readily applied to study general networks with distributed CSIT as described in Section
II
, not necessarily restricted to cell-free massive MIMO networks.

Before moving to the aforementioned results, we focus on a rather technical yet important weakness of Theorem
2
. The assumption of Theorem
2
is essentially used to ensure the existence of all the expectations in the steps of the proof, and is satisfied for any fading distribution with bounded support. However, it is not satisfied for the classical Gaussian fading model. Despite being unrealistic, since physically consistent fading distributions cannot have unbounded support, Gaussian fading is a very common model in the literature due to its analytical treatability, for example in deriving simple channel estimation error models
[
30
,
22
]
. Furthermore, except for the tails of the distribution, it usually fits measurements well. Therefore, in the following we derive more general optimality results covering this case.

Theorem 3
.

If
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{Q}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{F}}]<\infty
,
then Problem (
5
) admits a unique optimal solution, which is also the unique stationary solution solving Problem (
9
).

Proof.

The proof is given in Appendix
A-B
.
∎

Finally, we conclude this section by observing that the optimality conditions (
9
) are not only useful to characterize the optimal TMMSE solution, but also to evaluate the suboptimality of its approximations. This can be done via an appropriate measure of violation of the optimality conditions. Specifically, we have the following result:

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{Q}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{F}}]<\infty
holds, and let
\mathbbm{t}_{k}^{\star}\in\mathcal{T}
be the unique solution to Problem (
5
). Furthermore, define
\mathbbm{z}_{k}:=[\mathbbm{z}_{1,k}^{\mathsf{T}},\dots,\mathbbm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}_{L,k}]^{\mathsf{T}}
, where
\mathbbm{z}_{l,k}=\mathbbm{z}_{l,k}(S_{l})
is given by the left-hand side of the stationary conditions in (
9
) with
\mathbbm{t}_{k}^{\star}
replaced by an arbitrary
\mathbbm{t}_{k}\in\mathcal{T}
.
If
\mathbbm{z}_{k}\in\mathcal{T}
, i.e., if
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{z}_{l,k}(S_{l})\|^{2}]<\infty
, the following optimality bounds hold:

\displaystyle\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{t}_{k})-\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{t}_{k}^{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{E}\left[\|\mathbbm{Q}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbbm{z}_{k}\|^{2}\right]

(10)

\displaystyle\leq P\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbbm{z}_{k}\|^{2}\right],

(11)

Proof.

The proof is given in Appendix
A-C
.
∎

Clearly,
\mathbbm{z}_{k}(S_{1},\ldots,S_{L})=\mathbf{0}
a.s. gives the optimality conditions in (
9
), and in fact it corresponds to a zero optimality gap in (
11
). Intuitively, the bounds in (
11
) can be quite tight if
\mathbbm{z}_{k}(S_{1},\ldots,S_{L})\approx\mathbf{0}
with high probability. However, if this is not satisfied, we remark that both bounds can be looser than other trivial bounds obtained, e.g., by assuming a centralized information structure, or even output negative estimates of
\mathrm{MSE}_{k}(\mathbbm{t}_{k}^{\star})
. As already mentioned, we leave further studies on suboptimal solutions for future work, and we use (
11
) only in Section
IV-C
for getting analytical insights into a particular setup.

IV
Applications to cell-free massive MIMO

In this section we specialize the theory of Section
III
to cell-free massive MIMO networks, and explicitly derive optimal TMMSE precoders for two practical examples. In the scope of this study, the important feature of the cell-free massive MIMO paradigm is the exploitation of time division duplex operations and channel reciprocity to efficiently acquire estimates
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}
of the local channel
\mathbbm{H}_{l}
at each TX
l
via over-the-uplink training
[
10
]
.
These estimates may be subsequently shared through the fronthaul according to some predefined CSIT sharing mechanism, forming at each TX
l
a side information about the global channel
\mathbbm{H}
of the type

S_{l}:=\left(\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l},\bar{S}_{l}\right)

(12)

where
\bar{S}_{l}
denotes the side information about the other channels
\{\mathbbm{H}_{j}\}_{j\neq l}
collected at TX
l
. Depending on the CSIT sharing mechanism,
\bar{S}_{l}
may be a function of the other local channel estimates
\{\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}\}_{j\neq l}
(e.g., in case of error-free digital signalling), or include additional noise (e.g., in case of random events such as protocol delays). Consistently with the above discussion, we consider the following assumptions:

Assumption 1

(Local channel estimation)
.

For every
l\in\mathcal{L}
, let
\mathbbm{E}_{l}:=\mathbbm{H}_{l}-\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}
be the local channel estimation error for the local channel. Assume that
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}
and
\mathbbm{E}_{l}
are independent. Furthermore, assume
\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{E}_{l}]=\mathbf{0}
, and that
\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{E}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{E}_{l}]=:\mathbf{\Sigma}_{l}
has finite elements. Finally, assume that
(\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l},\mathbbm{E}_{l})
and
(\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j},\mathbbm{E}_{j})
are independent for
l\neq j
.

Assumption 2

(CSIT sharing mechanism)
.

For every
(l,j)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
s.t.
l\neq j
, assume the following Markov chain:
\mathbbm{H}_{l}\to\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\to S_{l}\to S_{j}\to\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}\to\mathbbm{H}_{j}
.

Assumption
1
is widely used in the wireless communication literature and it holds, e.g., for pilot-based MMSE estimates of Gaussian channels
[
10
,
18
]
. Assumption
2
essentially states that all the available information about
\mathbbm{H}_{l}
is fully contained in
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}
at TX
l
, and that TX
j
can only obtain a degraded version of it. We now rewrite the optimality conditions given by (
9
) in light of the considered model:

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
, Assumption
2
, and the assumption of Theorem
3
hold. Then, the unique TMMSE solution to Problem (
5
) is given by the unique
\mathbbm{t}_{k}^{\star}\in\mathcal{T}
satisfying

\mathbbm{t}_{l,k}^{\star}(S_{l})=\mathbbm{F}_{l}\left(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\sum_{j\neq l}\mathsf{E}\left[\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{j,k}\Big|S_{l}\right]\right)\quad\mathrm{a.s.},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

(13)

where
\mathbbm{F}_{l}:=\left(\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}+\mathbf{\Sigma}_{l}+P^{-1}\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}
.

Proof.

The first term of the stationarity conditions (
9
) is evaluated by letting

\begin{split}\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,l}|S_{l}]&=\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{H}_{l}|S_{l}]+P^{-1}\mathbf{I}\\
&=\mathsf{E}[(\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}+\mathbbm{E}_{l})^{\mathsf{H}}(\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}+\mathbbm{E}_{l})|\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}]+P^{-1}\mathbf{I}\\
&=\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}+\mathbf{\Sigma}_{l}+P^{-1}\mathbf{I},\end{split}

(14)

where we used the Markov chain
\mathbbm{H}_{l}\to\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\to S_{l}
and Assumption
1
. Then, for
j\neq l
:

\begin{split}\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,j}\mathbbm{t}_{j}^{\star}|S_{l}]&=\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{H}_{j}\mathbbm{t}_{j,k}^{\star}|S_{l}]\\
&\overset{(a)}{=}\mathsf{E}\left[\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{H}_{j}|S_{l},S_{j}]\mathbbm{t}_{j,k}^{\star}\middle|S_{l}\right]\\
&\overset{(b)}{=}\mathsf{E}\left[\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}|S_{l},S_{j}]\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{H}_{j}|S_{l},S_{j}]\mathbbm{t}_{j,k}^{\star}\middle|S_{l}\right]\\
&\overset{(c)}{=}\mathsf{E}\left[\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{H}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}|\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}]\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{H}_{j}|\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}]\mathbbm{t}_{j,k}^{\star}\middle|S_{l}\right]\\
&\overset{(d)}{=}\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{E}[\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}\mathbbm{t}_{j,k}^{\star}|S_{l}],\end{split}

(15)

where
(a)
follows from the law of total expectation and
\mathbbm{t}_{j,k}=\mathbbm{t}_{j,k}(S_{j})
,
(b)
from the Markov chain
\mathbbm{H}_{l}\to(S_{l},S_{j})\to\mathbbm{H}_{j}
,
(c)
from the Markov chain
\mathbbm{H}_{l}\to\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\to(S_{l},S_{j})
, and
(d)
from Assumption
1
. Note that all the aforementioned Markov chains are implied by Assumption
2
. The proof is concluded by using
\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{g}_{l,k}|S_{l}]=\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{e}_{k}
and by rearranging the terms.
∎

The above lemma reveals the following structure of the optimal TMMSE solution: the matrix
\mathbbm{F}_{l}
can be recognized as a
local
MMSE precoding stage (studied, e.g., in
[
18
]
), that is, a centralized MMSE solution
[
22
]
assuming that there are no other TXs than TX
l
; the remaining part can be then interpreted as a ‘corrective’ stage which takes into account the effect of the other TXs based on the available CSIT and long-term statistical information.

IV-A

No CSIT sharing

As an important example, we assume that no local channel estimate is shared along the fronthaul. This corresponds to the original cell-free massive MIMO setup studied in
[
10
]
. Specifically, we let
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}
as in Assumption
1
and

S_{l}=\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L}.

(16)

Theorem 4
.

The TMMSE precoders solving (
13
) under no CSIT sharing (
16
) are given by

\mathbbm{t}_{l,k}^{\star}(S_{l})=\mathbbm{F}_{l}\mathbf{C}_{l}\mathbf{e}_{k},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

(17)

for some matrices of coefficients
\mathbf{C}_{l}\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times K}
. Furthermore, the optimal
\mathbf{C}_{l}
are given by the unique solution of the linear system

\mathbf{C}_{l}+\sum_{j\neq l}\mathbf{\Pi}_{j}\mathbf{C}_{j}=\mathbf{I}
,
\forall l\in\mathcal{L}
, where
\mathbf{\Pi}_{l}:=\mathsf{E}\left[\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\mathbbm{F}_{l}\right]
.

Proof.

Substituting (
17
) into the optimality conditions (
13
), we need to show that

\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\left(\mathbf{C}_{l}+\sum_{j\neq l}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}\mathbbm{F}_{j}\mathbf{C}_{j}\middle|S_{l}\right]-\mathbf{I}\right)\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathbf{0}\quad\mathrm{a.s.},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L}.

(18)

By the independence between
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}
and
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}
, we can drop the conditioning on
S_{l}
and obtain
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\left(\mathbf{C}_{l}+\sum_{j\neq l}\mathbf{\Pi}_{j}\mathbf{C}_{j}-\mathbf{I}\right)\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathbf{0}
a.s.,
\forall l\in\mathcal{L}.
The proof is concluded by observing that
\mathbf{C}_{l}+\sum_{j\neq l}\mathbf{\Pi}_{j}\mathbf{C}_{j}=\mathbf{I}
,
\forall l\in\mathcal{L}
, always has a unique solution, as shown in Appendix
A-D
.
∎

The optimal solution (
17
) corresponds to a two-stage precoding scheme composed by a local MMSE precoding stage
\mathbbm{F}_{l}
preceded by a statistical precoding stage
\mathbf{C}_{l}
. By letting the rows
\hat{\mathbbm{g}}_{l,k}^{\mathsf{H}}
of
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}
to be independent and distributed as
\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{K}_{l,k})
, corresponding for instance to a non-line-of-sight (NLoS) scenario with no pilot contamination
[
18
]
, it can be shown that the matrices
\mathbf{\Pi}_{l}
are diagonal. Hence, (
17
) takes the simpler form

\mathbbm{t}_{l,k}^{\star}(S_{l})=c_{l,k}\mathbbm{F}_{l}\mathbf{e}_{k},

(19)

which, by mapping the optimal
c_{l,k}
to the optimal large-scale fading decoding coefficients in a dual UL channel, was already studied in
[
28
]
. However, if the channels have non-zero mean, such as in line-of-sight (LoS) models, (
17
) may provide significantly higher rates than (
19
). To see this, let
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\approx\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{l}
for some fixed matrix
\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{l}
,
\forall l\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, since
\bar{\mathbf{H}}_{l}
is statistical information known to all TXs, the TMMSE precoders should take a form similar to a ‘long-term’ centralized MMSE solution, which cannot be implemented using (
19
). Finally, we point out that a suboptimal variation of (
17
) called
optimal bilinear equalizer
(OBE), with
\mathbbm{F}_{l}
replaced by
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}
, was already proposed in
[
26
]
as a low-complexity alternative to centralized MMSE precoding which maintains robustness against pilot contamination.

Theorem 5

The TMMSE precoders solving (
13

\mathbbm{t}_{l,k}^{\star}(S_{l})=\mathbbm{F}_{l}\mathbbm{V}_{l}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\right]\mathbf{e}_{k},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L},

•

\mathbbm{V}_{l}:=\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi}_{l}\mathbbm{P}_{l}\right)^{-1}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{\Pi}_{l})

•

\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{l}:=\mathbf{I}-\mathbbm{P}_{l}\mathbbm{V}_{l}

•

\mathbbm{P}_{l}:=\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}\mathbbm{F}_{l}

•

\mathbf{\Pi}_{l}:=\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{P}_{l+1}\mathbbm{V}_{l+1}]+\mathbf{\Pi}_{l+1}\mathsf{E}[\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{l+1}]

Proof.

We first assume that all the matrix inverses involved in the following steps exist. Substituting (
21

\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{l}^{\mathsf{H}}\left(\mathbbm{V}_{l}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}+\sum_{j\neq l}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\middle|S_{l}\right]-\mathbf{I}\right)\mathbf{e}_{k}=\mathbf{0}\quad\mathrm{a.s.},\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L}.

(22)

To verify the above statement, we rewrite the first two terms inside the outer brackets as:

\left(\mathbbm{V}_{l}+\sum_{j>l}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=l+1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\right]\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{l}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}+\sum_{j<l}\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i},

(23)

where we use the fact that
\mathbbm{P}_{j}
,
\mathbbm{V}_{j}
, and
\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{j}
are deterministic functions of
\hat{\mathbbm{H}}_{j}
only, hence they are independent from
S_{l}
for
j>l
, while they are deterministic functions of
S_{l}
otherwise. Furthermore, since
\mathbbm{P}_{j}
,
\mathbbm{V}_{j}
, and
\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{j}
are independent from
\mathbbm{P}_{i}
,
\mathbbm{V}_{i}
, and
\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}

\forall i\neq j

\begin{split}\sum_{j>l}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=l+1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\right]&=\sum_{j>l}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\right]\prod_{i=l+1}^{j-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{l+1}\mathbbm{V}_{l+1}\right]+\sum_{j>l+1}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\right]\prod_{i=l+1}^{j-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\right]\\

(24)

The second and last term of the above chain of equalities define a recursion terminating with

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{L}\mathbbm{V}_{L}\right]+\mathbf{0}\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{L}\right]=\mathbf{\Pi}_{L-1}
. This recursion gives precisely
\sum_{j>l}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\right]=\mathbf{\Pi}_{l}
. Together with the property
\mathbbm{V}_{l}+\mathbf{\Pi}_{l}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{l}=\mathbf{I}

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}+\sum_{j<l}\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}&=\left(\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{l-1}+\mathbbm{P}_{l-1}\mathbbm{V}_{l-1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{l-2}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}+\sum_{j<l-1}\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{l-2}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}+\sum_{j<l-1}\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i},\end{split}

(25)

where the last equation follows from the definition of
\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{l}
, and where we identify another recursive structure among the remaining terms. By continuing until termination, we finally obtain
\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}+\sum_{j<l}\mathbbm{P}_{j}\mathbbm{V}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\bar{\mathbbm{V}}_{i}=\mathbf{I}
, which proves the main statement under the assumption that all the matrix inverses involved exist. This assumption is indeed always satisfied, as shown in Appendix
A-E
.

Remark 2

VI
Concluding remarks

This work provides novel guidelines for distributed precoding design in systems with distributed CSIT such as cell-free massive MIMO networks. By assuming full data sharing and a sum-power constraint, the proposed optimal approach outperforms previously known heuristic methods in several setups of interest. Going beyond the two chosen examples where the optimal precoders can be derived explicitly, the proposed approach can be potentially applied, exactly or using standard numerical approximations, to a wide range of practical setups. Furthermore, since it exploits the UL-DL duality principle, we remark that the proposed approach also provides optimal distributed combiners for UL operations. Although not covered for simplicity, the proposed approach may be also extended to limited data sharing, for example using network clustering techniques. Other promising lines of research include the extension to different power constraints, the revisitation of cell-free massive MIMO performance analysis involving LoS models or pilot contamination, and the development of new efficient algorithms exploiting fronthaul architectures such as tree or star topologies.

Lemma 6

(Necessity of stationarity)
.

Suppose that
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{Q}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{F}}]<\infty

\mathbbm{t}^{\star}\in\mathcal{T}

Proof.

[
24
,
25

\mathrm{MSE}(\mathbbm{t}^{\star})=\underset{\mathbbm{t}_{l}\in\mathcal{T}_{l}}{\text{min}}\mathrm{MSE}(\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{-l},\mathbbm{t}_{l}),\quad\forall l\in\mathcal{L}.

(41)

We relax the the above conditions by letting
\mathcal{T}_{l,\mathrm{unc}}
be the unconstrained version of
\mathcal{T}_{l}
, i.e., where we remove the constraint
\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{t}_{l}\|^{2}]<\infty
. We then have

\begin{split}\infty>\mathrm{MSE}(\mathbbm{t}^{\star})&=\underset{\mathbbm{t}_{l}\in\mathcal{T}_{l}}{\text{min}}\mathrm{MSE}(\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{-l},\mathbbm{t}_{l})\\

&=\underset{\mathbbm{t}_{l}\in\mathcal{T}_{l,\mathrm{unc}}}{\text{min}}\mathsf{E}\left[\mathsf{E}\left[c\left(\mathbbm{H},\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{-l},\mathbbm{t}_{l}\right)\middle|S_{l}\right]\right]\\

(42)

where
\mathbbm{t}_{l}^{\star\star}
is given by the first-order optimality condition
\nabla_{\mathbf{t}_{l}}\phi_{l}(S_{l},\mathbf{t}_{l})=\mathbf{0}
a.s. applied to the convex function
\phi_{l}(s_{l},\mathbf{t}_{l}):=\mathsf{E}\left[c(\mathbbm{H},\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{-l},\mathbf{t}_{l})\middle|S_{l}=s_{l}\right]

\mathbbm{t}^{\star\star}_{l}(S_{l}):=(\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,l}|S_{l}])^{-1}\left(\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{g}_{l}|S_{l}]-\sum_{j\neq l}\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{Q}_{l,j}\mathbbm{t}^{\star}_{j}|S_{l}]\right)\in\mathcal{T}_{l,\mathrm{unc}},

(43)

\mathbbm{t}_{l}^{\star\star}\in\mathcal{T}_{l}

\mathsf{E}[\|\mathbbm{t}^{\star\star}_{l}\|^{2}]<\infty

5

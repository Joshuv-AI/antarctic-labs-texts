Abstract
The performance of a previously developed analog combining network (ACN) of phase shifters for vehicle-to-vehicle communication is investigated. The original ACN was designed to maximize the sum of the signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) for K K consecutive, broadcast, periodic cooperative awareness messages when communication is over a dominant path whose angle of arrival (AOA) is constant over the duration of K K packets. In this work, we relax this scenario by allowing the dominant path AOA and path-loss (PL) to be time-variant. Assuming a highway scenario with line of sight (LOS) propagation between vehicles, we use affine approximations to model the time variation of different path quantities, including the PL , the relative distance-dependent phase shift between antennas, and the AOA -dependent far-field function of the antennas. Using these approximations, we analytically derive the ACN sum- SNR as each one of these quantities vary over the duration of K K packets. Moreover, we suggest a phase slope design rule that is robust against time variation of the dominant path and optimal under time-invariant conditions. Finally, we validate this design rule using numerical computations and an example of vehicular communication antenna elements.

IV-C

Phase Slope Design Under Time-Varying
\Omega
(
a_{\mathrm{\Omega}}\neq 0
)

To derive a design rule to choose the most robust phase slope in
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
under channel phase variation, we start by characterizing the trends of the loss function,
L_{\mathrm{\Omega}}(x)
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}
, and assume that
a_{\mathrm{\Omega}}\neq 0
. At the points
x=(\alpha-a_{\mathrm{\Omega}})T/2=q\pi
the loss function is at its maximum value
L_{\mathrm{\Omega}}(x)=1
.
On the other hand, as can be seen in Fig.
6
, in the range
[\pi/K,(K-1)\pi/K]
the loss function exhibits
K-2
lobs. These lobes have decreasing maxima in the range
[\pi/K,\pi/2]
.
That follows since
\sin(x)
is increasing over the interval, while
|\sin(Kx)|
is periodic with
\pi/K
(which is equal to the lobes width). Due to the symmetry around
\pi/2
, the lobes maxima are increasing as we get far from the symmetry point in the range
[\pi/2,(K-1)\pi/K]
.
In addition, the maximum loss within
[\pi/K,(K-1)\pi/K]
is bounded following

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{\Omega}}(x)\leq\frac{1}{K\sin(\pi/K)},\quad x\in\bigg[\frac{\pi}{K},(K-1)\frac{\pi}{K}\bigg],

(27)

since
|\sin(Kx)|\leq 1
and
\sin(\pi/K)\leq\sin(x)
where
x\in[\pi/K,(K-1)\pi/K]
.
This bound is decreasing with
K
and it quantifies to
L_{\mathrm{\Omega}}(x)\leq 0.34
for
K=5
, and
L_{\mathrm{\Omega}}(x)\leq 0.32
for
K=10
.
Substituting with the bound in (
21
) we deduce that the sum-
SNR
when
x\in[\pi/K,(K-1)\pi/K]
is at worst
-1.8
dB, and
-1.7
dB below the zero-loss sum-
SNR
(
\inf_{y}S_{\mathrm{\Omega}}(x,y)=K
) for
K=5
, and
K=10
, respectively.
Hence, in summary, the loss in sum-
SNR
is moderate within the interval
[\pi/K,(K-1)\pi/K]
and it gets lower as we approach the point of symmetry
\pi/2
of the loss function. On the other hand, the loss in sum-
SNR
is severe in the intervals centered around the points
(\alpha-a_{\mathrm{\Omega}})T/2=q\pi
.

Guided by these trends in the loss function

, we aim to pick the phase slope

that handles a wide range of error due to channel variation

.
This is equivalent to picking the phase slope that has the largest phase distance from the points with the most severe loss,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\star}\triangleq\arg\min_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}}|\alpha T/2-\pi/2|.

Besides having the largest phase distance from the points with the most severe loss, the

with
\alpha^{\star}
ensures that the effective phase slope

is within the region with moderate loss in sum-

for the widest range of
a_{\mathrm{\Omega}}
compared to any other phase slope in
\mathcal{A}^{\star}

\alpha^{\star}
is the most robust phase slope in
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
when
\Omega
is time-varying.
The solutions to (
28

\displaystyle\alpha^{\star}\in\begin{cases}\bigg\{\displaystyle\frac{K}{2}\frac{2\pi}{KT}\bigg\},&K\text{ even }\\
\bigg\{\displaystyle\frac{K-1}{2}\frac{2\pi}{KT},\displaystyle\frac{K+1}{2}\frac{2\pi}{KT}\bigg\},&K\text{ odd }\end{cases}

V-A

Pathloss Model

The Pathloss is typically modeled following a power law
[
16
]
, and so does its inverse, the path gain, which will be the one explicitly used in the analysis to follow. A generic model of the path gain
[

\displaystyle A(t)=A_{0}\times\bigg(\frac{d_{\mathrm{ref}}}{d(t)}\bigg)^{n_{\mathrm{e}}}10^{-X_{\sigma}(d)/10},~d\geq d_{\mathrm{ref}},

(30)

where

and

is the path loss exponent,

is a reference distance with path gain
A_{0}

is a zero-mean Gaussian random process with standard deviation
\sigma_{\textrm{SH}}
corresponding to shadowing (large scale fading).
Shadowing is a spatially correlated process with autocorrelation
\mathbb{E}\{X_{\sigma}(d)X_{\sigma}(d+\Delta d)\}
, that can be modeled using a decaying exponential function with parameter
d_{\mathrm{c}}

[
17
]
. The decorrelation distance
d_{\mathrm{c}}
, represents the distance difference at which the autocorrelation is equal to
\mathrm{e}^{-1}
. In context of highway scenario, it is reported to be
23.3\leq d_{\mathrm{c}}\leq 32.5~
m in
[
16
]
and
d_{\mathrm{c}}=25~
m in WINNER+B1 pathloss model
[
15
]
. Taking this into account, and for simplicity, we assume that shadowing is a block-type fading over the duration of
KT
s,

\displaystyle X_{\sigma}\triangleq X_{\sigma}(d|_{t=(K-1)T/2}),~0\leq t\leq KT.

Then, assuming that
2
2

2

When
d<d_{\mathrm{ref}}
the model (
30
) is not valid. In such case, the path gain can be assumed to be equal to that at
d_{\mathrm{ref}}
as done, for example, in WINNER+B1 model
[
15
, Table A.1.4-1]
.

d(0)>d_{\mathrm{ref}}
and
d\big((K-1)T\big)>d_{\mathrm{ref}}
, we can approximate the average path gain following

\displaystyle\overline{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\{A(t)\}=\mu_{X}A_{0}\bigg(\frac{d_{\mathrm{ref}}}{d(t)}\bigg)^{n_{\mathrm{e}}}\approx b_{\mathrm{PL}}+a_{\mathrm{PL}}t,

(31)

where

VIII
Conclusion and Future Works

ACN

[
4
]
and
ABN

[
6
]
are robust multiple antenna schemes that maximize the sum-
SNR
of
K
consecutive broadcast, periodic packets, under a worst-case propagation scenario, of a single dominant path with non-varying
AOA
,
path-loss (PL)
, and phase shift between antennas
\Omega
, over the duration of
K
packets. In this work, we investigated the performance of a
1\times 2

ACN
(
2\times 1

ABN
) when the three quantities of the dominant component are time-varying instead. The main findings of this work follow.

•

The phase slopes that yield identical optimal sum-
SNR
when the
AOA
, the path-loss, and
\Omega
are non-varying, yield different sum-
SNR
when any of theses three quantities vary over the duration of
K
packets.

•

Time variation of
\Omega
results in shifting the
ACN
phase slope, and incurs a loss in sum-
SNR
that depends on the phase slope used, how fast the time variation of
\Omega
is, and the antenna separation. In particular, the smaller the antenna separation, the less susceptible
ACN
is to these variations.

•

To mitigate the loss in sum-
SNR
under variation of
\Omega
at medium to large distances, we proposed a design rule (
28
) that yields a phase slope that is robust against time variations and optimal under time-invariant conditions.

•

Time variation of the
PL
does not induce a shift to the
ACN
phase slope, but it attenuates the sum-
SNR
achieved using any phase slope compared to the sum-
SNR
achieved under time-invariant conditions. The sum-
SNR
loss incurred is minor compared to the loss incurred due to time variation of
\Omega
. The derived design rule (
28
) is found to yield a phase slope that is robust against variation of
PL
at medium to large distances, as well.

•

Variation of
AOA
induces a variation of phase and amplitude of the antenna far-field functions, which has equivalent effects to the combined
\Omega
and
PL
time variation effects, with an extent that depends on the antennas employed. The design rule (
28
) yields a robust phase slope in
\mathcal{A}^{\star}
in this case too.

In this study, we investigated the performance of
ACN
/
ABN
after relaxing the worst-case propagation scenario assumed when designing them. The next step is to study the performance of
ACN
/
ABN
under rich multipath propagation, which represents a full relaxation of the worst-case propagation assumption and is left for future work.

Define the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

(55)

It follows that

\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\iff x+\pi\in\mathcal{X},

(57)

\displaystyle(\pi/2-x)\in\mathcal{X}

The first statement follows since if

, then
\pi/2\pm x=m\pi
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then multiplying both sides by
-1

\pi

\pi/2\mp x=(1-m)\pi

For fixed positive integer

f_{1}:x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and

following

\displaystyle f_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{\sin(Kx)}{\sin(x)},

(59)

\displaystyle f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{K\cos(Kx)}{\sin(x)}-\frac{\sin(Kx)}{\sin(x)}\cot(x).

(60)

Lemma 3
.

Let
f_{1}
be as defined in (
59
), then

|f_{1}(x)|
and

are periodic with period
\pi
, and symmetric around
\pi/2
.

Proof.

From (
59
), and
\sin(K\pi)=0
it follows that

\displaystyle f_{1}(x+\pi)=\frac{\sin(Kx+K\pi)}{\sin(x+\pi)}=-\frac{\sin(Kx)\cos(K\pi)}{\sin(x)}.

Hence
|f_{1}(x+\pi)|=|f_{1}(x)|
and
|f_{1}^{2}(x+\pi)|=|f_{1}^{2}(x)|
, and the periodicity property follows.
To prove the symmetry property, we show that

\displaystyle|f_{1}(\pi/2+x)|=|f_{1}(\pi/2-x)|.

(61)

We have
|f_{1}(\pi/2+x)|
is given by

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\sin(K\frac{\pi}{2})\cos(Kx)+\cos(K\frac{\pi}{2})\sin(Kx)}{\cos(x)}\bigg|.

(62)

On the other hand,
|f_{1}(\pi/2-x)|
is given by

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\sin(K\frac{\pi}{2})\cos(Kx)-\cos(K\frac{\pi}{2})\sin(Kx)}{\cos(x)}\bigg|.

(63)

If

is even, it follows from (
62
) and (
63

|f_{1}(\pi/2+x)|=|\sin(Kx)/\cos(x)|=|f_{1}(\pi/2-x)|
. If
K
is odd, it follows from the same equations that
|f_{1}(\pi/2+x)|=|\cos(Kx)/\cos(x)|=|f_{1}(\pi/2-x)|
. Hence
|f_{1}(x)|
is symmetric around
\pi/2
. Since
f_{1}
is a real-valued function,
f_{1}^{2}(x)=|f_{1}(x)|^{2}
, and thus
f_{1}^{2}(x)
is symmetric around
\pi/2
too, and the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
f_{2}
be as defined in (
60
), then

f_{2}^{2}(x)
is periodic with period
\pi
, and symmetric around
\pi/2
.

Proof.

Employing trigonometric identities and noting that

, we have

\displaystyle f_{2}(x+\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{K\cos(Kx)\cos(K\pi)}{\sin(x+\pi)}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\sin(Kx)\cos(K\pi)}{\sin^{2}(x+\pi)}\cos(x+\pi)

\displaystyle=-\cos(K\pi)

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg(\frac{K\cos(Kx)}{\sin(x)}-\frac{\sin(Kx)}{\sin^{2}(x)}\cos(x)\bigg).

Hence,

is periodic with period

.

) and (

) we can conclude that

, and symmetric around

when

. The same statement is true when

, since by (
67

L(x)=\sqrt{c_{1}^{2}f_{1}^{2}(x)+c_{2}^{2}f_{2}^{2}(x)}/(c_{1}K)
, and since both

and Lemma

, respectively.

(iii)

To show (
68
), we first proof that
L(x)\leq 1
. To that end, observe that
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(b+akT)=c_{1}K
. Then using (
65
) we deduce that

\displaystyle-c_{1}K\leq

\displaystyle J(x,y)\leq c_{1}K.

(74)

Then, it follows that
L(x)=-\inf_{y}J(x,y)/(c_{1}K)\leq 1
, which is achievable when
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Second, to show the lower bounds in (
68
), we employ (
67
) and the fact that
c_{1}>0
to deduce that

\displaystyle L(x)=0

\displaystyle\iff x\notin\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\iff\big(f_{1}^{2}(x)=0,c_{2}^{2}f_{2}^{2}(x)=0\big).

(75)

Since
f_{1}(x)=0
if and only if
x\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, and since

\displaystyle f_{2}^{2}(x)=\bigg(K\frac{\cos(Kx)}{\sin(x)}\bigg)^{2}>0,\quad x\in\mathcal{X}^{\star},

(76)

we conclude that

0<L(x)
when
a\neq 0
, (since
c_{2}=aT/2
), and
0\leq L(x)
, when
a=0
.

(iv)

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
, from the definition (
56

x=m\pi/K
, where

,
q\in\mathbb{Z}
, hence
x\notin\mathcal{X}
. Substituting in (

\displaystyle L(x)=\frac{|c_{2}f_{2}(x)|}{c_{1}K}

\displaystyle=\bigg|c_{2}K\frac{\cos(m\pi)}{c_{1}K\sin(x)}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}\sin(x)}\bigg|.

(77)

(v)

From (
67
) it follows that, for

,

\displaystyle L(x)=\frac{1}{K}\sqrt{f_{1}^{2}(x)+\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}f_{2}^{2}(x)}.

From the definitions of

\displaystyle=\frac{aT/2}{b+a(K-1)T/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sqrt{f_{1}^{2}(x)+g(w)\frac{f_{2}^{2}(x)}{(K-1)^{2}}}

It is easily seen that, for a fixed

\displaystyle g(0)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{w\to-A}g(w)

\displaystyle=\lim_{w\to\infty}g(w)=1,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dw}g(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{4Aw}{(w+2A)^{3}}=\begin{cases}\leq 0,&-A<w\leq 0\\
>0,&w>0\end{cases}

\inf_{w>-A}g(w)=0

\displaystyle L(x;w)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{w>-A}L(x,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{(K-1)^{2}f_{1}^{2}(x)+f_{2}^{2}(x)}}{K(K-1)},

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{w>-A}L(x,w)=\frac{|f_{1}(x)|}{K}.

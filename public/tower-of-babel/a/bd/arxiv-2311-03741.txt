Beyond Traditional Beamforming: Singular Vector Projection for MU-MIMO
Md Saheed Ullah, Rafid Umayer Murshed, Mohammad Saquib, and Md. Forkan Uddin

Abstract
This letter introduces a low-complexity beamforming approach for MU-MIMO systems with multiple data streams per user, minimizing inter-user interference and improving spectral efficiency (SE). The Interference-Optimized Singular Vector Beamforming (IOSVB) algorithm is developed by correlating inter-user interference with channel singular vectors. It blends interference minimization and SE maximization by identifying ideal singular vectors. Extensive simulations demonstrate that IOSVB provides near-optimal SE performance, closely matching exhaustive search results while reducing the computational overhead. This novel approach in MU-MIMO systems is a promising option for future 6G wireless communication networks due to its excellent performance and reduced complexity.



I
Introduction

The realm of wireless communication is poised for a revolutionary leap with the advent of 6G, where Multi-User Multiple Input Multiple Output (MU-MIMO) systems emerge as pivotal. MU-MIMO systems are at the forefront by harnessing an intricate array of antennas at both transmitters and receivers, promising to meet the escalating demand for higher data rates and network capacity
[
1
]
. Integral to this evolution is the adoption of spatial multiplexing. This technique amplifies data transmission efficiency across the spatially distributed user spectrum, thereby marking a significant stride towards achieving the lofty throughput aspirations of 6G
[
2
]
.

However, integrating spatial multiplexing within MU-MIMO systems is challenging, with effective interference management
[
3
]
. The complexity of effectively managing interference, particularly in environments where beam selection becomes crucial, cannot be overstated. As systems navigate the complexities of spectral efficiency (SE) and capacity maximization, the precision in beamforming vector selection becomes imperative. This precision directly influences the mitigation of inter-user interference and the enhancement of data throughput, thus emphasizing the crucial need for breakthroughs in beam-selection methodologies
[
4
]
.

In reviewing MU-MIMO beamforming optimization, it is clear that traditional methods, such as fractional programming and Hungarian algorithms, struggle with managing multiple data streams per user. Fractional programming, while effective for power control, falls short when applied to the complex spatial multiplexing of MU-MIMO, as it does not adequately address dynamic inter-user interference
[
5
]
. Similarly, the Hungarian method, designed for assignment problems, cannot easily handle the interdependencies between beam optimizations, making it unsuitable for MU-MIMO beamforming where interference between beams is dynamic
[
6
,
7
]
. DFT codebook-based approaches, although efficient in practical systems, are limited by static, predefined beam patterns that cannot adapt to instantaneous channel conditions
[
8
]
. These methods are unable to fully optimize beamforming in real-time, reducing their effectiveness in complex, high-interference environments like MU-MIMO. In light of the limitations highlighted, there is a clear need for novel approaches that effectively manage interference while supporting multiple data streams per user.

Motivated by the identified challenges and limitations, this study introduces the Interference-Optimized Singular Vector Beamforming (IOSVB) algorithm, a reduced-complexity solution for interference management in MU-MIMO systems. Traditional algorithms
[
9
]
, which iteratively optimize the rate or directly manage inter-user interference, are computationally intensive and impractical for real-time applications. In contrast, IOSVB leverages an upper bound of total system interference as a computationally efficient metric for iterative optimization. Notably, the combiner at the user end requires no knowledge of other users’ channels. This strategic design centralizes the computational burden at the base station (BS), ensuring minimal load on user devices with limited computational resources. Empirical results demonstrate that IOSVB achieves near-optimal SE with significantly reduced complexity, providing a feasible solution for MU-MIMO.

III
Proposed Beamforming Approach

Here, we introduce the IOSVB algorithm to iteratively yield near-optimal beamforming matrices,
\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{k}
and
\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}
. IOSVB uses channel singular vector correlations to reduce inter-user interference to select the most effective vectors within candidate matrices
\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}
. Since our system iteratively searches, we need a metric to identify the best beamforming vectors. The rate equation (
1
) or system interference (
3
) would be too computationally intensive for this iterative approach. This leads us to derive an alternate representation of the interference, an upper bound of the total system interference.

The algorithm iteratively selects a subset of optimized categorical column indices,
\mathcal{I}_{k}
, for each user
k
, containing
N_{s}
values corresponding to
N_{s}
beamforming directions. Each element of the set
\mathcal{I}_{k}
uniquely identifies a particular beam-direction. The aggregation of these subsets across all
K
users forms the global index set
\mathcal{I}
, defined as
\mathcal{I}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{I}_{k}
. The relationship
\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}[\mathcal{I}]=\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}
succinctly captures the key essence of our approach, signifying that the optimal matrices
\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}=[\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{k},\cdots,\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{K}]
are a strategic selection from the broader candidate set
\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}
.

Upon performing SVD on the channel matrix, two orthonormal matrices
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{r}\times N_{r}}
,
\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times N_{t}}
and a diagonal matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{r}\times N_{t}}
, i.e.
\mathop{\mathrm{SVD}}(\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k})=\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}
. Given the sparse nature of channels at mmWave, utilizing a search space of all
N_{t}
vectors results in extensive computational complexity at the expense of minimal performance gain. Thus, we narrow down the search domain by discarding vectors in
\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}
associated with minimal channel gain. We select only the first
N_{c}
columns from
\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}
, where
N_{c}
is chosen to be close to the rank of the channel matrix. This selection is informed by the channel gains in
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}
and compresses the search space to
N_{\text{ex}}={\binom{N_{c}}{N_{s}}}^{K}
possible combinations instead of
{\binom{N_{t}}{N_{s}}}^{K}
, considerably simplifying the optimization challenge. We derive
\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times N_{c}}
by extracting the first
N_{c}
columns from
\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}
for all the users. We also form
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}^{\prime}_{k}
by selecting the first
N_{c}
columns and first
KN_{c}
rows from
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}
.
The concatenated candidate matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}=[\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}_{k},\cdots,\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}_{K}]
represents the collation of selected matrices for all users.
Similarly, the aggregated singular vector matrix for all users, denoted as
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}=[\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}^{\prime}_{1},\cdots\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}^{\prime},\cdots,\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{K}^{\prime}]
, spans a dimension of
KN_{c}\times KN_{c}
. This diagonal matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}
can also be represented as
\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\cdots,\sigma_{KN_{c}})}
. The correlation matrix for all combinatorial selections, symbolized by
\bm{\sLambda}_{c}
, is formulated as

\bm{\sLambda}_{c}={\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}}\;\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}^{\dagger}\;\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}},

(4)

where,
\bm{\sLambda}_{c}
is a
KN_{c}\times KN_{c}
matrix. From
\bm{\sLambda}_{c}
, we generate
N_{\text{ex}}
combinations of matrices, each with dimensions
KN_{s}\times KN_{s}
. A particular combination matrix within this set is represented by
\bm{\sLambda}
, expressed as

\bm{\sLambda}=\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}},\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}]\;{\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}]\;\bm{\mathsfbr{\tilde{F}}}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}].

(5)

Note that
\bm{\sLambda}
is a
KN_{s}\times KN_{s}
square matrix that can be extracted from
\bm{\sLambda}_{c}
using
\bm{\sLambda}=\bm{\sLambda}_{c}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}},\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}]
.

V
Conclusion

While significant research has focused on SU-MIMO beamforming, a notable gap exists in studies dedicated to MU-MIMO beamforming. Current MU-MIMO approaches often fall short in SE due to inadequate interference management. This letter introduced the concept of using the correlation of channel singular vector projections to calculate total interference. Furthermore, we developed IOSVB, a novel beamforming algorithm for MU-MIMO systems designed to identify beams that minimize interference. Through extensive simulations using the NYUSIM channel, we demonstrated that IOSVB outperforms existing algorithms, offering superior performance and reduced computational times. The IOSVB algorithm matches the performance of Exhaustive Search without incurring prohibitive computational costs, making it a promising candidate for next-generation MU-MIMO systems in 6G. Even though the NYUSIM channel is quite realistic for practical settings, imperfect channels obtained from real-world testbeds will be used to evaluate the proposed algorithm’s performance in future works. We also intend to perform a thorough performance analysis of the IOSVB under different channel correlation conditions, which will provide more detailed insights.

For an orthonormal matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}
, the product
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}
results in a sparse non-square identity matrix,
\bm{\mathsfbr{I}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
, i.e.,
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}=\bm{\mathsfbr{I}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
.

Proof:
Since
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}
is orthonormal,
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}^{\dagger}=\bm{\mathsfbr{I}}
. The matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
is
N_{s}\times N_{r}
, with orthonormal rows. Thus,
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}
yields a non-square identity matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{I}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
of size
N_{s}\times N_{r}
, containing
N_{s}
ones.

Lemma 1:
Prove that

||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}^{{\dagger}}\right)\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}||\\
=||\sum_{j\neq k}\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}({\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}})^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}||.\

(16)

Proof:

By changing the position of the summation, we get

\displaystyle||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\left(\sum_{j\neq k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}^{{\dagger}}\right)\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}||

\displaystyle=||\sum_{j\neq k}(\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j})(\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}^{{\dagger}}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}^{\mathrm{{\dagger}}}\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k})||.

(17)

Using the property of a Hermitian matrix in (
17
), equation (
16
) can be obtained.

Lemma 2:
If ‘
j
’ is an interferer, ‘
k
’ is the intended user,

{||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}||}^{2}={||\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k},\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{j}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{j}]||}^{2}

(18)

Proof:

Using
\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}=\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}
, we can write

{||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}||}^{2}={||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}||}^{2}.

(19)

Here, by definition,
\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]=\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}
and
\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{j}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{j}]=\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}.

Hence, (
19
) can be rewritten as,

{||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}||}^{2}={||\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{U}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{j}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{j}]||}^{2}.

(20)

Now, using
Corollary 1
, we can write,

{||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}||}^{2}={||\bm{\mathsfbr{I}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{j}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{j}]||}^{2}.

(21)

In (
21
),
\bm{\mathsfbr{I}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
is a matrix of size
N_{s}\times N_{r}
with
N_{s}
number of ones that corresponds to indices of
\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}
. Using matrix multiplication, (
21
) can be written as

{||\bm{\mathsfbr{W}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{k}\bm{\mathsfbr{F}}_{j}||}^{2}={||\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{j}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{j}]||}^{2}.

(22)

Here,
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
is a matrix of size
N_{s}\times N_{r}
with
N_{s}
non-zero elements, which can also be indicated as
\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{N_{s}}).
Now, we can form a square matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k},\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
of size
N_{s}\times N_{s}
by extracting the non-zero columns from
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}].

Here, the matrix product
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}
is analogous to
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k},\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]\bm{\mathsfbr{V}}_{k}^{\dagger}[\mathsfbr{\mathcal{I}}_{k}]
as in the latter one we are just disregarding the multiplication with the columns of
\bm{\mathsfbr{S}}_{k}
that have only zero values. Thus, using above formulations equation (
18
) can be obtained.

Conditioning continuous-time Markov processes by guiding
Marc Corstanje 1 Frank van der Meulen 1 Moritz Schauer 2

Abstract
. A continuous-time Markov process X X can be conditioned to be in a given state at a fixed time T > 0 T>0 using Doob’s h h -transform. This transform requires the typically intractable transition density of X X . The effect of the h h -transform can be described as introducing a guiding force on the process. Replacing this force with an approximation defines the wider class of guided processes. For certain approximations the law of a guided process approximates – and is equivalent to – the actual conditional distribution, with tractable likelihood-ratio. The main contribution of this paper is to prove that the principle of a guided process, introduced in Schauer et al., 2017a for stochastic differential equations, can be extended to a more general class of Markov processes. In particular we apply the guiding technique to jump processes in discrete state spaces. The Markov process perspective enables us to improve upon existing results for hypo-elliptic diffusions. Keywords: Markov processes, jump processes, Doob’s h h -transform, conditional process, landmark dynamics, diffusions, guided process

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Problem description and motivation

Continuous-time Markov processes are widely used for modelling phenomena that evolve over time. Examples include Brownian motion, the Poisson process, Lévy processes in general, and diffusion processes generated by a stochastic differential equation.
Many applications require sampling corresponding bridge processes, that is sampling the Markov process
X
conditional on the value of its trajectory at some time
T
. For example in the statistical context, with
X_{t}
the state of the process at time
t
, the process is typically only observed at times
t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}
. Based on these observations one may be interested in estimating a parameter
\theta
appearing in the forward description of the process
X
. In this setting, likelihood based inference is difficult if the transition densities of the process are intractable. However, if the process were observed continuously rather than discretely, then likelihood computations for the continuously observed process on
[0,t_{n}]
would typically be easier. This observation has led many authors to employ a data-augmentation scheme, where one samples iteratively
(i)

(X_{t},t\in[0,t_{n}])
conditional on
\{X_{t_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{n}
and
\theta
;
(ii)

\theta
conditional on
(X_{t},t\in[0,t_{n}])
.
Clearly, step
(i)
requires a way to sample
(X_{t},\,t\in(t_{i-1},t_{i}))
conditional on
X_{t_{i-1}}
and
X_{t_{i}}
, i.e. a
bridge
process. More generally, both data augmentation approaches and approaches based on particle samplers naturally lead to the problem of sampling a bridge process. Whereas for Brownian motion the corresponding bridge process, the Brownian bridge, is fully tractable, for general continuous-time Markov processes this is not the case. It is the aim of this paper to provide a general framework for this.
Without loss of generality, we can state the problem as simulating
(X_{t},\,t\in[0,T])
conditional on
(X_{0},X_{T})=(x_{0},x_{T})
. When feasible, we sometimes consider generalisations of this setting, such as conditioning on
(X_{0},LX_{T})=(x_{0},v)
for a given matrix
L
.

1.2.
Approach: conditioning by guiding

Our approach builds upon earlier work in the specific setting where
X
is a diffusion process generated by a stochastic differential equation (SDE). That is,
X
satisfies the equation

\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x_{0}.

In this case the problem of bridge sampling has attracted much attention over the past two decades. The approach that we adopt here consists of
guiding
, the terminology originating from
Papaspiliopoulos & Roberts, 2012
, the underlying ideas going back to
Clark, 1990
;
Delyon & Hu, 2006
. Guiding refers to adjusting the dynamics of the process
X
to ensure that it hits
x_{T}
at time
T
. This can be done in multiple ways. Following
Clark, 1990
,
Delyon & Hu, 2006
proposed to superimpose the drift of a Brownian bridge to the original drift, leading to the process

\,\mathrm{d}X^{\circ}_{t}=b(t,X^{\circ}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{x_{T}-X_{t}^{\circ}}{T-t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t,X^{\circ}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\qquad X_{0}=x_{0}.

If
\sigma\sigma^{\prime}
is strictly positive definite, then indeed
X^{\circ}_{T}=x_{T}
, provided certain smoothness and boundedness conditions on
b
and
\sigma
are satisfied. Equally importantly, they derived an expression for the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the law of the conditioned process,
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
, with respect to the law of
X^{\circ}=(X^{\circ}_{t},\,t\in[0,T])
, which we denote by
\mathbb{P}^{\circ}
. If
\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{t}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\circ}_{t}
denote the restrictions of
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\circ}
to
[0,t]
, respectively, then proving
\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{t}\ll\mathbb{P}^{\circ}_{t}
is relatively easy, but the limiting operation
t\uparrow T
requires careful arguments.

Schauer et al., 2017a
;
Bierkens et al., 2020
considered different, more flexible, guiding terms which can also handle hypo-elliptic diffusions. A major effort in these papers consists of formulating sufficient conditions that justify taking the limit
t\uparrow T
.
For diffusions on manifolds, introducing guiding terms has recently been introduced in
Bui et al., 2021
;
Arnaudon et al., 2019
. While these works contain numerically convincing results of absolute continuity, no proof is given. Recently
Jensen & Sommer, 2021
proved that the approach of
Delyon & Hu, 2006
can be extended to simulating Brownian Bridges on Riemannian manifolds.

1.3.
Contribution

A first contribution of this paper is to define guided processes by means of an exponential change of measure, rigorously studied in
Palmowski & Rolski, 2002
. The beauty of this approach is that it is not restricted to diffusion processes, but applies generally to continuous-time Markov processes, including for example jump processes. Our main result, Theorem
3.3
, gives a simple expression for
\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\star}/\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\circ}
and states sufficient conditions to justify the aforementioned limiting operation
t\uparrow T
. These conditions are designed to facilitate this operation in specific examples. We first illustrate the power of our approach to non-homogeneous jump processes and a continuous-time process evolving over a Delaunay triangulation. Secondly, we apply this to diffusion processes, thereby lifting restrictions on the dynamics of the process from
Bierkens et al., 2020
which then enable us to fully theoretically justify the bridge simulations in
Arnaudon et al., 2021
. Some technical proofs are gathered in the appendix.

is denoted by

λ_{\min}(A)

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert

.

2.
General setting, Doob’s
h
-transform and Guided processes

Throughout we assume existence of an underlying probability space
\left(Ω,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathbb{P}}\right)
. Let
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
be a Markov process on a Polish space
{\mathcal{S}}
equipped with a
σ
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
and define the filtration
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}=σ(\{X_{s}\colon s\leq t\})
. Let
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Throughout this paper, we denote
{\mathbb{P}}_{t}={\mathbb{P}}\rvert_{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}}
,
\mathbb{E}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\rvert_{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}}
and assume all probabilities and expectations are taken conditional on
X_{0}=x_{0}
. For a
{\mathbb{P}}
-Markov process starting at
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}
and generated by a family of operators
{\mathcal{L}}=\{{\mathcal{L}}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
defined on the same domain
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{L}})
, it holds that

M_{t}^{f}:=f(X_{t})-f(x_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{L}}_{s}f(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\in(0,T),

is a local martingale for any function
f\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{L}})
. We denote the space-time generator of the space-time process
\{(t,X_{t})\}_{t\in[0,T]}
by
{\mathcal{A}}f(t,x)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(t,x)+{\mathcal{L}}_{t}f(t,x)
and we extend
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{L}})
to the domain
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})
of
{\mathcal{A}}
. For simplicity, we omit the subscript and write
{\mathcal{L}}f
for
f\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{L}})
. Observe that for
g\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})
, the process

(2.1)

M_{t}^{g}:=g(t,X_{t})-g(0,x_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{A}}g(s,X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad t\in(0,T),

defines a local martingale as well.

Definition 2.1

(
S
-good function)
.

Let
S\leq T
. We call
h\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})
an
S
-good function if
h
is positive and

(2.2)

D_{t}^{h}=\frac{h(t,X_{t})}{h(0,x_{0})}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\mathcal{A}}h}{h}(s,X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right),\qquad 0\leq t\leq S,

is a martingale adapted to the filtration
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,S]}
.

The following proposition is an adaptation of Proposition 3.2 of
Palmowski & Rolski, 2002
and provides conditions for verifying that
h
is an
S
-good function

Proposition 2.2

(Adaptation of Proposition 3.2 of
Palmowski & Rolski, 2002
)
.

Suppose that
h\in{\mathcal{D}}\left({\mathcal{A}}\right)
is such that
h
and
\frac{{\mathcal{A}}h}{h}
are bounded and measurable on
[0,S]\times{\mathcal{S}}
. Then
h
is an
S
-good function.

Definition 2.3

(Conditioned process)
.

Let
S\leq T
and
h
be an
S
-good function. Define the change of measure

(2.3)

\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{t}=D_{t}^{h}\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}_{t},\qquad t\in[0,S].

We refer to the new measure
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{t}
as the conditioned measure induced by
h
and the process
X
under
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
is referred to as the conditioned process induced by
h
. We denote expectations with respect to
\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{t}
by
{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\star}
.

The transformation of measures is known as Doob’s
h
-transform. The function
h
is typically chosen such that the process
X
has particular properties under
\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{t}
, which it does not possess under
\mathbb{P}_{t}
. The following example is a key example to illustrate this.

Example 2.4
.

Suppose the transition kernel of
X
admits a transition density with respect to a measure
ν
on
{\mathcal{S}}
, i.e.
{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t}\in A\mid X_{s}=x\right)=\int_{A}p(s,x;t,y)\,\mathrm{d}ν(y)
for
s\leq t
and
A\in{\mathcal{B}}
. For
T>0
, let the measure
{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
be the measure induced by
h(t,x)=\int p(t,x;T,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)
for a probability measure
\mu
under which
p
is also measurable. Since
{\mathcal{A}}h=0
, we have for measurable
f

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\star}f(X_{t})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left(f(X_{t})\frac{{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(0,x_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle=\int f(x)p(0,x_{0};t,x)\frac{\int p(t,x;T,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\int p(0,x_{0};T,y^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y^{\prime})}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int f(x)\frac{p(0,x_{0};t,x)p(t,x;T,y)}{p(0,x_{0};T,y)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\right)\frac{p(0,x_{0};T,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\int p(0,x_{0};T,y^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\int{\mathbb{E}}\left(f(X_{t})\mid X_{T}=y\right)\,\mathrm{d}\xi(y),

where

\,\mathrm{d}\xi(y)=\frac{p(0,x_{0};T,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)}{\int p(0,x_{0};T,y^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y^{\prime})}.

In particular, for
x_{T}\in{\mathcal{S}}
, the measure
\mu=\delta_{x_{T}}
induces the process
X^{\star}=\left(X\mid X_{T}=x_{T}\right)
. Measurement error on the value at the endpoint can be incorporated using
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=q_{x_{T}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}(y)
, for a a probability density function
q_{x_{T}}
and dominating measure
\bar{\mu}
. Lastly, this approach can also be used when forcing diffusions to have a certain distribution at time
T
, such as seen in
Baudoin, 2002
. Here one can choose
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(y)=\frac{q(y)}{p(0,x_{0};T,y)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)

Unfortunately, many interesting choices of
h
require the transition density
p
of the Markov process to be tractable, which is usually not the case. Instead, one can try to use a tractable approximation
\tilde{h}
to
h
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.5

(Guided process)
.

Suppose
\tilde{h}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})
is an
S
-good function for
S\leq T
and define the change of measure

\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{t}=D_{t}^{\tilde{h}}\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}_{t},\qquad t\in[0,S].

The process
X
under the law
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
is denoted by
X^{\circ}
and is referred to as the guided process induced by
\tilde{h}
. We denote expectations with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{t}
by
{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}
.

By formula (1.2) in
Palmowski & Rolski, 2002
the extended generators of
X^{\star}
and
X^{\circ}
are given by

(2.4)

{\mathcal{A}}^{\star}f=\frac{1}{{h}}\left[{\mathcal{A}}\left(f{h}\right)-f{\mathcal{A}}{h}\right]\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}f=\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\left[{\mathcal{A}}\left(f\tilde{h}\right)-f{\mathcal{A}}\tilde{h}\right],

which characterises the dynamics of
X^{\star}
and
X^{\circ}
, respectively.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose
h
and
\tilde{h}
are
S
-good functions for some
S\leq T
and that
h
is space-time harmonic for
{\mathcal{A}}
, i.e.
{\mathcal{A}}h=0
. Assume
D_{t}^{\tilde{h}}>0
on
[0,S]
. Then, for all
t\leq S
,
{\mathbb{P}}_{t}^{\star}\sim{\mathbb{P}}_{t}^{\circ}
and

(2.5)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{t}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{t}}\left(X\right)=\frac{h(t,X_{t})}{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}\frac{\tilde{h}(0,x_{0})}{h(0,x_{0})}Ψ_{t}(X),\qquad t\in[0,S],

where

(2.6)

Ψ_{t}(X)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\mathcal{A}}\tilde{h}}{\tilde{h}}(s,X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

Proof.

Note that, since
h
and
D_{t}^{\tilde{h}}
are positive, both
\mathbb{P}_{t}^{\star}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\circ}_{t}
are equivalent to
{\mathbb{P}}_{t}
. Moreover,
\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}_{t}=\left(D_{t}^{\tilde{h}}\right)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{t}
and thus

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{t}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{t}}=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{t}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}_{t}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}_{t}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{t}}=\frac{D_{t}^{h}}{D_{t}^{\tilde{h}}}.

The proof now follows from substituting (
2.2
) and using
{\mathcal{A}}h=0
.
∎

In applications, we typically obtain a candidate for
\tilde{h}
via an auxiliary process.

Definition 2.7

(Auxiliary process)
.

Let
\tilde{X}
be a Markov process with generator
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}
and space-time generator
\tilde{{\mathcal{A}}}
. When we consider a guided process induced by a tractable
\tilde{h}
that satisfies
\tilde{{\mathcal{A}}}\tilde{h}=0

\tilde{X}

If
\tilde{h}
is obtained from an auxiliary process then
Ψ

(2.7)

Ψ_{t}(X)=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left({\mathcal{L}}-\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}\right)\tilde{h}}{\tilde{h}}(s,X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

In the setting of Example

and
\tilde{h}

3.
Conditions and proof of absolute continuity of
X^{\star}
and
X^{\circ}
on
[0,T]

Assume
h
and
\tilde{h}
are
S
-good functions for all
S<T
. By
2.6
, the processes
X^{\star}
and
X^{\circ}
are absolutely continuous on
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
for
t<T
. In this section, we provide conditions to ensure that absolute continuity also holds in the limit
t\uparrow T
.

To prove this, we fix a constant
t_{0}\in[0,T)
and impose the following assumptions.

Assumption 3.1
.

There exists a positive continuous scaling function
κ\colon[t_{0},T)\to(0,\infty)
and a family of
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}
-measurable events
\left\{A_{k}(t)\right\}_{k}
for each
t\in[t_{0},T)
so that the following assumptions hold

(
3.1
a)

For all
k
and
t_{0}\leq s\leq t\leq T
,
A_{k}(t)\subseteq A_{k+1}(t)
and
A_{k}(t)\subseteq A_{k}(s)
.

(
3.1
b)

The transition kernel of
X
admits a transition density
p
under
{\mathbb{P}}
with respect to a dominating measure
\nu
, that is
{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}\in A\mid X_{s}=x)=\int_{A}p(s,x;t,y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y)
for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,
A\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and
x\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Moreover, for all
s<T
and
y\in{\mathcal{S}}
,

\lim_{t\uparrow T}h^{\sharp}(t;s,x)=h(s,x),

where

(3.1)

h^{\sharp}(t;s,x)=\int κ(t)\tilde{h}(t,y)p(s,x;t,y)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y).

(
3.1
c)

For all
t_{0}\leq s<t<T
, the random variable
h^{\sharp}(t;s,X_{s})
is
{\mathbb{P}}
-almost surely bounded.

(
3.1
d)

κ
is such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\star}_{t}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)}\right)=\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{T}(A(T)),

where, for
t\in[t_{0},T)
,
A(t)=\bigcup_{k}A_{k}(t)
.

(
3.1
e)

For all fixed
k
,
Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)}
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
-almost surely uniformly bounded in
t
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Define
A_{k}(T)=\cap_{t<T}A_{k}(t)
. Under
3.1
, there exists a random variable
Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)
so that for all
k
,
Ψ_{T}^{\kappa}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(T)}=\lim_{t\uparrow T}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)}

Proof.

Note that
\log Ψ
is an integral and
κ
is continuous, and thus, as
A_{k}(T)\subseteq A_{k}(t)
for all
t
and by
(e)
, the map
t\mapsto Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)
is continuous and bounded on
A_{k}(T)
under
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
. Hence
\lim_{t\uparrow T}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(T)}
exists in the
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
-almost sure sense. Clearly, a random variable
\Psi_{T}^{\kappa}(X)
also exists so that
\lim_{t\uparrow T}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(T)}=\Psi_{T}^{\kappa}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(T)}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\lvert Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)}-Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(T)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)-Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\right\rvert\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(T)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lvert Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\right\rvert\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)\setminus A_{k}(T)}

It follows from the preceding that the first term tends to
0
while the second term is bounded by
(e)
and the indicator tends to
0
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
3.1
is satisfied. Then for any measurable function
f
,

(3.2)

{\mathbb{E}}_{T}^{\star}\left(f(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}\right)={\mathbb{E}}_{T}^{\circ}\left(f(X)\frac{\tilde{h}(0,x_{0})}{h(0,x_{0})}Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}\right)

In particular, if
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{T}(A(T))=1
, the measures are equivalent with

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{T}}{\,\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{P}}_{T}^{\circ}}(X)=\frac{\tilde{h}(0,x_{0})}{h(0,x_{0})}\Psi_{T}^{\kappa}(X).

Proof of
3.3
.

For simplicity, we denote
\bar{h}=\tilde{h}(0,x_{0})/h(0,x_{0})
. The proof is structured as follows. First we show that
{\mathbb{E}}_{T}^{\circ}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}\right)={\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{T}(A(T))
, then we show that
{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\to{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{T}(A(T))
as
t\uparrow T
. Finally, we finish the proof via Scheffé’s lemma.

First note that for any fixed
k
, it follows from dominated convergence, combined with
(e)
,
3.2
and
2.6
that

{\mathbb{E}}_{T}^{\circ}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(T)}\right)=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)}\right)=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\star}_{t}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)}\right).

We now send

on both sides and find that, by

combined with monotone convergence, the left hand side tends to

while the right hand side tends to

by

. Hence
{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{T}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}\right)={\mathbb{P}}^{\star}_{T}(A(T))

Now note that for any fixed

, it follows from

that

\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\geq\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{m}(t)}\right)=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\star}_{t}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A_{m}(t)}\right).

Upon sending
m\to\infty
, we have, by
(d)
,
\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{T}(A(T))
. For the other inequality, we note that for any

, by

,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\leq\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(s)}\right)

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\star}_{t}\left(\mathbf{1}_{A(s)}\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}κ(t)\right)=\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{s}(A(s))

where the last equality follows upon taking
g_{s}=\mathbf{1}_{A(s)}
in
B.1
. Upon sending
s\uparrow T
, we find by monotonicity of measures,
\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{T}(A(T))
.

We thus conclude that
{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\to{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{T}\left(\bar{h}Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}\right)
. Now note that upon taking
\lim_{k\to\infty}
as well in

and interchanging

and
\lim_{t\uparrow T}
, we have that
Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\to Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}
in the
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
-almost sure sense. Here the interchange of limits is allowed as the sequence is monotone in
k
. Hence, by Scheffé’s lemma,
Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\to Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}
in

.

Now let
s<T
and let
f_{s}
be any bounded positive
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
-measurable function such that the support of

is contained in

. Then it follows from

convergence and

\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}_{T}^{\circ}\left(f_{s}(X)\bar{h}Ψ_{T}^{κ}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}\right)&=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}\left(f_{s}(X)\bar{h}Ψ_{t}(X)κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\\
&=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\star}\left(f_{s}(X)\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)\\
\end{aligned}.

Since

has support only on

and for

,

, we have that

. Hence, upon applying

to

,

\begin{aligned} \lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\star}\left(f_{s}(X)\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(t)}\right)&=\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\star}\left(f_{s}(X)\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}κ(t)\mathbf{1}_{A(s)}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{s}^{\star}\left(f_{s}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(s)}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{T}^{\star}\left(f_{s}(X)\mathbf{1}_{A(T)}\right)\end{aligned}.

For the equivalence, it suffices to show that

. Note that by monotonicity

. Now for all

∎

3.1.
Discussion of

The order of verifying the assumptions in

is often as follows. We first choose the function
κ
so that

holds, then we compute

and find

so that

is bounded whenever

is bounded to ensure

via

and, if possible, eliminate the indicator in the expression for the Radon-Nikodym derivative and thereby proof equivalence by showing that

is almost surely bounded.

The function

is incorporated to enable compensating for a possible difference of smoothness in

and

. In
Section

, we consider an example where

is a process in a discrete state space, while

.

Proposition 3.4
.

If the events
A_{k}(t)
are defined by

(3.5)

A_{k}(t)=\left\{\sup_{t_{0}\leq s<t}V(x,X_{s})\leq k\right\}

for a nonnegative function
V\colon[t_{0},T)\times{\mathcal{S}}\to[0,\infty)
,
then
(a)
is satisfied. If also
\sup_{t}V(t,X_{t})
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
-almost surely bounded, then the Radon-Nikodym derivative in

can be simplified since
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{T}(A(T))=1
and thus the measures are equivalent.

The following proposition gives a condition for verifying
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
-almost sure boundedness of
V
and thus showing
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}_{T}(A(T))=1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}V\leq 0
, then there exists a random variable
C
such that
\sup_{t_{0}\leq t<T}V(t,X_{t})\leq C
and
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}(C\geq λ)\leq λ^{-1}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}}V\left(t_{0},X_{t_{0}}\right)
for any
λ>0
.

Proof.

We first show that
\{V(t,X_{t})\}_{t_{0}\leq t<T}
is a supermartingale. Note that by (
2.1
), a
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
-local martingale
M^{V}
exists so that under
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}

V(t,X_{t})=V\left(t_{0},X_{t_{0}}\right)+M_{t}^{V}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}V(s,X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
V
is nonnegative and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}V\leq 0
, it follows that
M^{V}
is bounded from below by
-V(t_{0},X_{t_{0}})
. Hence, by
B.4
,
M^{V}
is a supermartingale. Moreover, it follows that
V(t,X_{t})
is integrable for all

since

{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}\left\lvert V(t,X_{t})\right\rvert={\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}V(t,X_{t})\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{t_{0}}^{\circ}V(t_{0},X_{t_{0}})+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}M_{t}^{V}

The supermartingale property follows as for

,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\circ}_{t}\left(V(t,X_{t})\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\right)-V(s,X_{s})={\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}\left(M_{t}^{V}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\right)-M_{s}^{V}+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\circ}\int_{s}^{t}{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}V(u,X_{u})\,\mathrm{d}u\leq 0

Doob’s supermartingale inequality, see e.g. Theorem 1.3.6 of

, now states that for all

,

λ{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}\left(\sup_{t_{0}\leq t<T}V(t,X_{t})\geq λ\right)\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{t_{0}}^{\circ}V\left(t_{0},X_{t_{0}}\right).

Hence, a random variable
C
exists so that
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}
-almost surely
\sup_{t_{0}\leq t<T}V(t,X_{t})\leq C
where
{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}(C\geq λ)\leq λ^{-1}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\circ}}V\left(t_{0},X_{t_{0}}\right)
.
∎

Inhomogeneous Poisson process

Let

be an inhomogeneous Poisson process with state-dependent rate

{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t+Δ}=x+1\mid X_{t}=x\right)=λ(x)Δ+o(Δ).

The infinitesimal generator of

{\mathcal{L}}f(x)=λ(x)\left(f(x+1)-f(x)\right).

We assume

Hence, since

.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assumptions

and

are satisfied.

Proof.

First note that

Lemma 4.4
.

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{t\uparrow T}{\mathbb{E}}_{t}^{\star}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}(t,X_{t})}{h(t,X_{t})}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}(t)}\right)=\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{T}(A(T)).

Proof.

We first show that for all

,

Hence, for all trajectories

A_{k}(t)\setminus A_{k}(T)=\left\{\sup_{t\leq s<T}\frac{x_{T}-X_{s}}{\tilde{\lambda}(T-s)}>k\right\}

-almost surely uniformly bounded in

Observe that for

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left({\mathcal{L}}-\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}\right)\tilde{h}}{\tilde{h}}(s,X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

Lemma 4.6
.

{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}V(t,x)\leq 0
for all

and

.

Proof.

The result follows from a direct computation.

and for

,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}^{\circ}V(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}(t,x)+\lambda^{\circ}(t,x)\left[V(t,x+1)-V(t,x)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}(t,x)+\lambda(x)\frac{\tilde{h}(t,x+1)}{\tilde{h}(t,x)}\left[V(t,x+1)-V(t,x)\right]

4.2.
Jump process on a Delaunay triangulation

Here, we consider a toy example to model electric flow through a city. In
Doyle & Snell, 2000
, electricity is modeled as a random walk on a discrete grid and here we slightly alter this model by considering jump processes on a triangulation of a random set of points in the plane
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. The network is constructed by first sampling points in the plane according to a planar Poisson process, followed by adding connections according to a Delaunay triangulation (we recap its definition below). We assume that at a given location electricity moves to a randomly chosen neighbour. We condition the model by a starting point and a final point on the grid and model the flow of electricity between the two points.

Definition 4.7

(Voronoi diagram and Delaunay triangulation)
.

Let
P
be a set of points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The Voronoi diagram associated with
P
is the collection of Voronoi cells

{\mathcal{V}}_{P}(x):=\left\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\colon\left\lvert y-x\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert y-z\right\rvert,\text{ for all }z\in P\right\},\qquad x\in P.

The Delaunay triangulation
D(P)
of
P
is the dual graph of the Voronoi diagram. It has
P
as vertex set and there is an edge between
x
and
y
in
D(P)
if
{\mathcal{V}}_{P}(x)
and
{\mathcal{V}}_{P}(y)
share a
(d-1)
-dimensional face. An example of a Delaunay traingulation and a Voronoi diagram can be found in
Figure

1
.

Figure 1.
The blue points indicate a realization of a unit rate planar Poisson process with (left) the Delaunay triangulation and (right) the Voronoi diagram of the set.

In this section, we consider a realization
ξ
of a unit rate planar Poisson process that satisfies the following properties:

•

for all compact
A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the set
A\cap\xi
is finite.

•

There is a constant
K
such that for any pair
x,y
of vertices in
D(\xi)
satisfies
\left\lvert y-x\right\rvert\leq K
.

Note that properties are events of probability
1
.

Denote the Delaunay triangulation of
\xi
by
D(ξ)
. Consider a unit rate jump process
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
D(ξ)
defined through the generator

(4.7)

{\mathcal{L}}f(x)=\sum_{y\in N_{ξ}(x)}(f(y)-f(x)),\qquad x\in ξ,

where
N_{ξ}(x)
denotes the set of neighbors to
x\in ξ
in
D(ξ)
. For fixed
x_{T}\in ξ
and
T>0
, we consider the process conditioned
X^{\star}=\left(X\mid X_{T}=x_{T}\right)
induced by
h(t,x)={\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{T}=x_{T}\mid X_{t}=x\right)
. By Theorem 1 of
Rousselle, 2015
, the time-changed scaled process
X^{ε}=\{εX_{t/ε^{2}}\}_{t\geq 0}
converges in law to a scaled Brownian motion as
ε\downarrow 0
. Moreover, the scale parameter
\tilde{\sigma}>0
of the Brownian motion does not depend on the realization of
ξ
, but it does depend on the rate of
\xi
. We thus have an immediate candidate for
\tilde{h}
in

(4.8)

\begin{gathered}\tilde{h}(t,x)=η(t)\exp\left(-H(t,x)\right),\quad\text{with}\\
η(t)=\frac{1}{2π{\tilde{a}(T-t)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad H(t,x)=\frac{\left\lvert x_{T}-x\right\rvert^{2}}{2\tilde{a}(T-t)},\end{gathered}

5.3.
Application: stochastic landmarks registration

In this section we apply our results to a class of models that has recently appeared in shape analysis. Suppose a shape is characterised by a finite set of points, referred to as landmarks. One problem in landmarks registration consists of finding a flow of diffeomorphisms mapping an ordered set of landmarks of one shape to that of another shape, assuming both shapes are summarised by an equal number of landmarks (see for instance
Younes, 2010
). Whereas traditional models assume the flow to be generated by an
ordinary
differential equation,
stochastic
differential equations have been proposed more recently. Here, we consider specifically the model proposed in
Arnaudon et al., 2019
.
Suppose
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
denotes a configuration of
n
distinct landmarks
q_{i}\in Ω
in a domain
Ω\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Suppose that to each position
q_{i}
a momentum vector
p_{i}
is attached. Define the Hamiltonian

H(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}p_{i}^{T}K(q_{i},q_{j})p_{j},

the kernel

typically being chosen as Gaussian.
The Eulerian model proposed by
Arnaudon et al., 2019
specifies a flow on landmark positions induced by the system of stochastic differential equations

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\,\mathrm{d}q_{i}&=(\partial_{p_{i}}H)(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{l=1}^{J}\sigma_{l}(q_{i})\circ\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{l}\,,\\
\,\mathrm{d}p_{i}&=-(\partial_{q_{i}}H)(q,p)\,\mathrm{d}t-\sum_{l=1}^{J}\left(\partial_{q_{i}}\langle p_{i},\sigma_{l}(q_{i})\rangle\right)(q_{i},p_{i})\circ\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{l}\,,\end{split}

and

for noise-amplitudes

and kernel
\Lambda_{τ}
with length-scale

. For more explanation and details on the derivation of this model, we refer the reader to
Arnaudon et al., 2019
.

We illustrate the stochastic landmarks registration problem using Figure

Lemma B.1

Lemma B.2

(Exponential martingale bound)
.

Suppose
\{N_{t}\}_{t\geq t_{0}}
is a martingale. Then an almost surely finite random variable
C
exists so that
N_{t}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{t}\leq C\log\left(\frac{1}{T-t}\right)
.

Proof.

First note that, for any fixed
t<T
and positive
λ
, Doob’s maximal inequality yields

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{t_{0}\leq s\leq t}\exp\left\{N_{s}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{s}\right\}>e^{λ}\right)\leq e^{-λ}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\exp\left\{N_{t}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{t}\right\}\right)=k_{0}e^{-λ},

where
k_{0}=\exp\left\{N_{t_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{t_{0}}\right\}
is a bounded random variable. It follows that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\{N_{s}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{s}\right\}>λ\right)\leq k_{0}e^{-λ}.

Now set
t_{n}=T-\frac{1}{n}
and assume
n
is sufficiently large so that
t_{n}>0
. It follows from the preceding that we can choose an arbitrary positive sequence
\{λ_{n}\}_{n}
and have that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{n+1}}\left\{N_{s}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{s}\right\}>λ_{n}\right)\leq k_{0}e^{-λ_{n}}.

Upon choosing
λ_{n}=2\log n
, one has
\sum_{n}e^{-λ_{n}}<\infty
and thus, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\{\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{n+1}}\left\{N_{s}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{s}\right\}>λ_{n}\right\}\right)=0.

We can thus almost surely find a random variable
n_{0}(ω)
so that for all
n\geq n_{0}(ω)

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{n+1}}\left\{N_{s}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{s}\right\}\leq λ_{n}

Now notice that for any
t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1}]
, one has
λ_{n}\leq 2\log\left(\frac{1}{T-t}\right)
and therefore

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<T}\frac{N_{t}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{t}}{\log\left(\frac{1}{T-t}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{n}\sup_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}\frac{N_{t}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{t}}{\log\left(\frac{1}{T-t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{n_{0}}}\frac{N_{t}-\frac{1}{2}[N]_{t}}{\log\left(\frac{1}{T-t}\right)}\vee\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}\sup_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}\frac{λ_{n}}{\log\left(\frac{1}{T-t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\vee\sup_{n}\frac{2\log n}{\log\left(\frac{1}{T-t_{n+1}}\right)}

\displaystyle=C\vee 2\sup_{n}\frac{\log n}{\log(n+1)}

\displaystyle\leq C\vee 2,

where
C
is a random variable depending on
n_{0}
, which is finite since
t_{n_{0}}
is almost surely bounded away from
T
.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

Suppose
M
is a local martingale bounded from below with
{\mathbb{E}}M_{0}<\infty
, then
M
is a supermartingale.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
M
is bounded from below by
0
. Now let
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence of stopping times such that
\tau_{n}\uparrow\infty
and
\{M_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\}_{t}
is a martingale for all
n
. It follows from Fatou’s lemma that

{\mathbb{E}}\left\lvert M_{t}\right\rvert={\mathbb{E}}M_{t}={\mathbb{E}}\left(\liminf_{n\to\infty}M_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\right)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}M_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}={\mathbb{E}}M_{0}<\infty.

Hence,
M
is integrable. Moreover, for
s\leq t
, if also follows from Fatou’s lemma that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(M_{t}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left(\liminf_{n\to\infty}M_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\right)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\left(M_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\mid{\mathcal{F}}_{s}\right)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}M_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}=M_{s}

∎

Anisotropic relativistic fluid spheres with a linear equation of state
A. K. Prasad, J. Kumar

Abstract
In this work, we present a class of relativistic and well-behaved solution to Einstein’s field equations for anisotropic matter distribution. We perform our analysis by using the Buchdahl ansatz for the metric function g r ​ r g_{rr} . Three different classes of new exact solution are found for anisotropy factor Δ \Delta . We have analyzed our model with various physical aspects such as pressure (radial as well as transverse), energy density, anisotropy factor, mass, compactness parameter, adiabatic index γ \gamma and surface redshift. A graphical analysis of energy conditions, TOV equation and causality condition indicates the model are well behaved. The physical acceptability of the model has verified by considering compact objects with similar mass and radii, such as 4U 1820-30,Vela X-1, PSR J1614-2230, LMC X-4, SMC X-1, 4U 1538-52, Her X-1, Cgy X-2, PSR B1913+16 and PSR J1903+327.

Due to the highly nonlinear behavior of Einstein’s field equations, one of the most important problems in General Relativity is to obtain exact solution of Einstein equations. Exact solution play important role in the development of many areas of gravitational significance such as the study of effects of gravitation in solar system, black hole,star,gravitational collapse,cosmology and dynamic of early universe. Causality condition and Herrera’s cracking method

For any relativistic star, radial and transverse velocity of sound should satisfy
v_{r}^{2},v_{t}^{2}\leq 1
and this is known as the causality condition. On the other hand, Le Chatelier’s principle demands that the speed of sound be positive, i.e.,
v_{r}^{2},v_{t}^{2}\geq 0
. Combining the above two inequalities, one gets
0<v_{r}^{2},v_{t}^{2}\leq 1
, where the radial and transverse velocity of sound are obtained as

\displaystyle v_{r}^{2}=\frac{dp_{r}}{d\rho}=\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}/\frac{d\rho}{dr}

(28)

\displaystyle v_{t}^{2}=\frac{dp_{t}}{d\rho}=\frac{dp_{t}}{dr}/\frac{d\rho}{dr}

(29)

where
\dfrac{d\rho}{dr}=\dfrac{2Cr\,(1-K)(5+Cr^{2})}{K(1+Cr^{2})^{3}}
, the expressions of
=\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}~~\textrm{and}~~\frac{d\rho}{dr}
are as follows

Case I

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2Cr}{K}\left[\frac{K(Cr^{2}-1)-2Cr^{2}}{\left(1+Cr^{2}\right)^{3}}+\frac{1+Cr^{2}}{\sqrt{K+Cr^{2}}}-2\sqrt{K+Cr^{2}}-\frac{\left(1+Cr^{2}\right)}{(\sqrt{K+Cr^{2}}+(B_{1}/A_{1})\sqrt{K-1})}\right]

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{t}}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}+\frac{Cr}{2K(1+Cr^{2})^{3}}\left[(4K-7)Cr^{2}+\frac{1-2Cr^{2}(4KCr^{2}-7Cr^{2}-K-2)}{1+Cr^{2}}\right]

(31)

Case II

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2Cr}{K}\Bigg[\frac{K(Cr^{2}-1)-2Cr^{2}}{\left(1+Cr^{2}\right)^{3}}+\frac{\Phi\left(1-2K-Cr^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{K-1}\sqrt{K+Cr^{2}}\left(1+Cr^{2}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{e^{\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}-(B_{2}/A_{2})\,e^{-\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}}{e^{\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}+(B_{2}/A_{2})\,e^{-\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle+\frac{4\Phi^{2}(B_{2}/A_{2})}{(K-1)(1+Cr^{2})\Big(e^{\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}+(B_{2}/A_{2})\,e^{-\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}\Big)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{t}}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}+\frac{2Cr}{K(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}\left[\frac{(K-1)5Cr^{2}}{4(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1-2Cr^{2}(4KCr^{2}-7Cr^{2}-K-2)}{4(1+Cr^{2})}-\frac{\Phi^{2}(1+Cr^{2})}{1-K}\right]

(33)

Case III

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2Cr}{K}\Bigg[\frac{K(Cr^{2}-1)-2Cr^{2}}{\left(1+Cr^{2}\right)^{3}}+\frac{\Phi\left(1-2K-Cr^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{K-1}\sqrt{K+Cr^{2}}\left(1+Cr^{2}\right)^{2}}F(r)

(34)

\displaystyle+\frac{-\Phi^{2}(1+(B_{3}/A_{3})^{2})}{(K-1)(1+Cr^{2})\Big(\cos\Big(\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}\Big)+(B_{3}/A_{3})\,\sin\Big(\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}\Big)\Big)}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{t}}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}+\frac{2Cr}{K(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}\left[\frac{(K-1)5Cr^{2}}{4(1+Cr^{2})^{2}}+\frac{1-2Cr^{2}(4KCr^{2}-7Cr^{2}-K-2)}{4(1+Cr^{2})}+\frac{\Phi^{2}(1+Cr^{2})}{1-K}\right]

(35)

With the help of graphical representation we have shown that our model satisfies the causality condition (please see Fig.

Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) equations

Now we are interested in checking the effect of three different forces, viz. gravitational, hydrostatic and anisotropic force,on our given system. The governing generalized Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff(TOV) equation
[

\displaystyle-\frac{M_{G}(\rho+p_{r})}{r^{2}}e^{\frac{\lambda-\nu}{2}}-\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}+\frac{2(p_{t}-p_{r})}{r}=0,

where
M_{G}
is the effective gravitational mass given by:

\displaystyle M_{G}(r)=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\nu^{\prime}e^{(\nu-\lambda)/2}.

(37)

From the equation(

M_{G}(r)
in equation (
36

\displaystyle-\frac{\nu^{\prime}}{2}(\rho+p_{r})-\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}+\frac{2(p_{t}-p_{r})}{r}=0,

(38)

The equation (
38

(F_{g})
, hydrostatic
(F_{h})
and anisotropic force
(F_{a})
from an equilibrium point of view, which are defined as:

\displaystyle F_{g}=-\frac{\nu^{\prime}}{2}(\rho+p),~~~~F_{h}=-\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}~~~~\textrm{and}~~~~F_{a}=\frac{2(p_{t}-p_{r})}{r}=\frac{2\Delta}{C}

(39)

Here, we can derive the hydrostatic force
(F_{h})
from eqns.(
30
),(
32
), (
34
) and anisotropic force
(F_{a})=\Delta_{i}
, where
i=1,2,3
for case I, case II and case III. Now the expression of gravitational force

is written in an explicit form:

Case I

\displaystyle F_{g}=\frac{Cr}{1-K}\left[\frac{1-K}{2(1+Cr^{2})}-\frac{\sqrt{\frac{K-1}{K+Cr^{2}}}}{\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}+(B_{1}/A_{1})}\right]

(40)

Case II

\displaystyle F_{g}=\frac{Cr}{1-K}\left[\frac{1-K}{2(1+Cr^{2})}-\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K-1}{K+Cr^{2}}}\left(\frac{e^{\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}-(B_{2}/A_{2})\,e^{-\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}}{e^{\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}+(B_{2}/A_{2})\,e^{-\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K+Cr^{2}}{K-1}}}}\right)\right]

(41)

\displaystyle F_{g}=\frac{Cr}{1-K}\left[\frac{1-K}{2(1+Cr^{2})}-\Phi\sqrt{\frac{K-1}{K+Cr^{2}}}F(r)\right]

(42)

Adiabatic index

The stability of the relativistic as well as non relativistic compact object
likewise relies on the adiabatic index
\gamma
. Heintzmann and
Hillebrandt
[
14
]
recommended that the adiabatic index must be more than
\frac{4}{3}
at all interior point of relativistic compact object. In other side the non-relativistic and isotropic case (Newtonian fluids), neutron spherical object system has no upper
mass limit for the adiabatic index
\gamma>\frac{4}{3}

[
68
]
. So, the relativistic radial adiabatic index is defined by
[
69
]

\gamma_{r}=\Big(\dfrac{\rho+p_{r}}{p_{r}}\Big)\dfrac{dp_{r}}{d\rho}

Chan et al.
[
69
]
obtained the collapsing condition for the ratio of specific heat for an anisotropic fluid distribution as

\gamma\leq\frac{4}{3}\Bigg[1-max\Bigg(\dfrac{p_{r}-p_{t}}{r\mid p_{r}\mid}\Bigg)\Bigg]

From above expression, we can say that the unstable profile of
\gamma
depend on the anisotropic factor i.e,
\gamma
will increase when
\Delta>0
and decrease when
\Delta<0
. Also it is possible that sign of
\Delta
may be change within the configuration. If it is happen then there is existence of both stable and unstable profile of the object which could lead to fragmentation of the sphere
[
69
]
.
We can see from Fig.
7
that the value of radial adiabatic index is more than
\frac{4}{3}
at all interior points for each different compact
star model.

we get the turning point between stable and unstable region. For this model, we obtained

p_{r}
and the tangential pressure
p_{t}
are completely finite and
positive quantities within the stellar configuration, which is shown in Fig.
1

2

2

model is satisfy the following dominant energy conditions(see Fig.

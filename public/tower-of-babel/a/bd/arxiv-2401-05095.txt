Dixmier-type traces on semifinite symmetric spaces


Abstract
We prove that a normalised linear functional on certain semifinite symmetric spaces respects tail majorisation if and only if it is a Dixmier-type trace.

Introduction

In 1966, J. Dixmier has discovered traces, vanishing on finite-rank operators
[
7
]
. Precisely, for a positive convex increasing to infinity sequence
\{\psi(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
such that
\psi(2n)/\psi(n)\to 1
as
n\to\infty
, he considered the Lorentz space

\mathcal{M}_{\psi}=\left\{A\ \text{is compact}:\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{\psi(n+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu(n,A)<\infty)\right\},

here
\{\mu(n,A)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is the sequence of
s
-numbers of a compact operator
A
on a separable Hilbert space. On the positive cone of this Lorentz space, Dixmier defined a functional

{\rm Tr}_{\omega}(A)=\omega\left(\frac{1}{\psi(n+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu(n,A)\right),\ 0\leq A\in\mathcal{M}_{\psi}

and proved that it extends by linearity to a trace on the whole space, provided that
\omega
is a dilation invariant extended limit on the space
\ell_{\infty}
of all bounded sequences. Nowadays such traces are termed Dixmier traces.

In late 1980s, A. Connes employed Dixmier traces in his noncommutative geometry to construct an analogue of the integral in the noncommutative context
[
5
,
6
]
. Various problems in noncommutative geometry have stimulated the study of Dixmier traces. In particular, the development of semifinite noncommutative geometry unavoidably led to a semifinite counterpart of the Dixmier construction
[
3
,
4
]
.

In 1998, F. Sukochev jointly with P. Dodds, B. de Pagter and E. Semenov studied fully symmetric functionals on fully symmetric spaces (Lorentz spaces are particular examples of them)
[
9
]
. It follows directly from the definition that Dixmier traces are fully symmetric functionals (see Definition (
2.3
) below). Theorem 3.4 of
[
9
]
establishes a criterion for existence of non-trivial symmetric functionals on Lorentz spaces.
In 2011, this direction in the theory of singular traces has resulted in a joint paper of F. Sukochev with N. Kalton and A. Sedaev, showing that Dixmier traces are the only fully symmetric functionals on Lorentz spaces
[
16
]
.

Fedor Sukochev and his co-authors were not the only group studying Dixmier (singular) traces at that period. Starting from late 80s, A. Pietsch has been developing the theory of traces in the general context of operators between Banach spaces. By mid 2010s this work gained its ultimate and elegant form
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

In 1990s, there was another direction in the study of singular traces – the investigation of the traceability of operators
[
26
,
13
,
1
]
. The idea was to determine whether there exist traces taking non-zero value on a given compact operator. It turned out that this problem is equivalent to the existence of non-trivial singular traces on the principal ideal generated by this operator.

In this note we consider a counterpart of Lorentz spaces - symmetric spaces
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, which are closed with respect to a so-called tail majorisation. As shown in
[
2
]
, tail majorisation is fundamental for characterisation of interpolation spaces in the the couple
(\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{1})
. Following
[
13
,
1
]
we construct analogues of Dixmier traces on these spaces. Further, we prove that they are the only symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
which respect tail majorisation. We present general semifinite version of this results.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall main notions of the theory of noncommutative integration.

In what follows,
H
is a complex separable Hilbert space and
B(H)
is the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on
H
, equipped with the uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, and

\mathbf{1}
is the identity operator on
H
.

For a self-adjoint operator
X
in
H
, we denote by
E^{X}
the spectral measure of
X
. For any closed densely-defined operator
X:\mathfrak{D}(X)\to H
with the domain
\mathfrak{D}(X)
, the absolute value of
X
is defined as
|X|=(X^{*}X)^{1/2}.

For more details on von Neumann algebra
theory the reader is referred to e.g.
[
8
,
14
,
15
]

or
[
24
]
.

Throughout the present paper we assume that
\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra, that is a
*
-subalgebra of
B(H)
containing
\mathbf{1}
and closed with respect to the weak operator topology.

The collection of all (self-adjoint) projections in
{\mathcal{M}}
is denoted by
P({\mathcal{M}})
. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
24
, Proposition 1.1]
) that
P({\mathcal{M}})
is a complete lattice. The supremum (respectively, infimum) of two projection
P,Q\in P({\mathcal{M}})
is denoted by
P\vee Q
(respectively,
P\wedge Q
).
Two projections
P,Q\in P({\mathcal{M}})
are said to be equivalent (with respect to
{\mathcal{M}}
), denoted by
P\sim Q
, if there exists a partial isometry
v\in{\mathcal{M}}
such that
P=v^{*}v
and
Q=vv^{*}
.

A trace
\tau
on a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
is a functional on the positive cone
{\mathcal{M}}^{+}
with values in
[0,\infty]
which is additive, positively homogeneous and satisfies

\tau(XX^{*})=\tau(X^{*}X),\quad X\in{\mathcal{M}}.

It is said that a trace
\tau:{\mathcal{M}}^{+}\to[0,\infty]
is

•

faithful if for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}
,
\tau(X)=0
implies that
X=0
;

•

semifinite if for every nonzero
X\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}
there exists
0\leq Y\leq X
, such that
0<\tau(Y)<\infty
;

•

normal if
\tau(\sup_{i\in I}X_{i})=\sup_{i\in I}\tau(X_{i})
for every bounded increasing net
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
{\mathcal{M}}^{+}
.

•

finite if
\tau(X)<\infty
for all
X\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}
.

The extension of a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
on the whole algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
is denoted by
\tau
too.

Throughout this paper,
{\mathcal{M}}
is assumed to be a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
.

Next, we recall the basics of the theory of
\tau-
measurable operators and generalized singular value function.
General facts concerning measurable operators may
be found in
[
18
,
22
]
(see also
[
25
, Chapter
IX]
) and the upcoming book
[
10
]
. For the convenience of the reader, some of the basic definitions are recalled here.

A linear operator
X:\mathfrak{D}\left(X\right)\rightarrow H
is said to be
affiliated
with
\mathcal{M}

if
YX\subseteq XY
for all
Y\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is the commutant of
\mathcal{M}
, defined as
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\{T\in B(H):TS=ST\quad\forall S\in\mathcal{M}\}
. If
X
is a self-adjoint operator affiliated with
{\mathcal{M}}
, then
E^{X}(B)\in{\mathcal{M}}
for any Borel set
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. If
X
is a closed densely defined operator affiliated with
{\mathcal{M}}
, then the projections
n(X),r(X)
and
s(X)
belong to
{\mathcal{M}}
(see e.g.
[
10
, Chapter 2]
).

A closed
densely defined
operator
X:\mathfrak{D}\left(X\right)\rightarrow H
affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
is termed

measurable
with respect to
\mathcal{M}
if there exists a
sequence
\left\{P_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset P\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
, such
that
P_{n}\uparrow\mathbf{1}
,
P_{n}(H)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}\left(X\right)

and
\mathbf{1}-P_{n}
is a finite projection (with respect to
\mathcal{M}
)
for all
n
. It should be noted that the condition
P_{n}\left(H\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}\left(X\right)
implies that

XP_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
. The collection of all measurable
operators with respect to
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
. It is a unital
\ast
-algebra
with respect to the strong sums and products (denoted simply by
X+Y
and
XY
for all
X,Y\in S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
)
[
22
, Corollary 5.2]
.

An operator
X\in S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
is called
\tau-
measurable if there exists a sequence

\left\{P_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
P\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
such that

P_{n}\uparrow\mathbf{1},

P_{n}\left(H\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}\left(X\right)
and

\tau(\mathbf{1}-P_{n})<\infty
for all
n.
The collection of all
\tau
-measurable
operators is a unital
\ast
-subalgebra of
S\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
and is denoted by
S\left(\mathcal{M},\tau\right)

[
18
, Theorem 4]
. It is well known that an operator
X
belongs to
S\left(\mathcal{M},\tau\right)
if and only if
X\in S(\mathcal{M})

and there exists
\lambda>0
such that
\tau(E^{|X|}(\lambda,\infty))<\infty

[
18
, Theorem 2 (ii)]
. Alternatively, an operator
X

affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
is
\tau
-measurable (see

[
12
]
) if and only if

\tau\left(E^{|X|}\bigl(n,\infty\bigr)\right)\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

The generalized singular value function
\mu(X):t\rightarrow\mu(t,X)
,
t>0
, of an operator
X\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)

is defined (see
[
12
, Definition 2.1]
) by setting

\mu(t,X)=\inf\{\|XP\|_{\infty}:\ P\in P(\mathcal{M}),\,\tau(\mathbf{1}-P)\leq t\}.

(2.1)

There exists an equivalent definition which involves the
distribution function of the operator
X
. For every
operator
X\in S(\mathcal{M},\tau),
define the distribution function
d_{X}
by setting

d_{X}(t)=\tau(E^{|X|}(t,\infty)),\quad t>0.

Then (see
[
12
, Proposition 2.2]
) the singular value function
\mu(X)
is the right-continuous inverse of
d_{X}(\cdot)
, that is

\mu(t,X)=\inf\{s\geq 0:\ d_{X}(s)\leq t\}.

In particular,
\mu(X)
is supported on
[0,\tau(\mathbf{1})]
.
For
X,Y\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
we have
[
12
, Lemma 2.5 (v)]

\mu(2t,X+Y)\leq\mu(t,X)+\mu(t,Y),\quad t>0.

Note also that
\mu(0,X)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\mu(t,X)<\infty
if and only if
X
is bounded, in which case
\mu(0,X)=\|X\|_{\infty}
(see e.g.
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
).

In the special case when
\mathcal{M}=L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
is the von Neumann algebra of all
Lebesgue measurable essentially bounded functions on
(0,\infty)
acting via multiplication on the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=L_{2}(0,\infty)
, with the trace given by integration
with respect to Lebesgue measure
m
, the algebra
S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
can be identified with the algebra

S(0,\infty)=\{f\text{ is measurable:}\ \exists A\in\Sigma,m((0,\infty)\setminus A)<\infty,f\chi_{A}\in L_{\infty}(0,\infty)\},

where
\chi_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of a set
A\subset(0,\infty)
, and
\Sigma
is the
\sigma
-algebra of Lebesgue measurable sets on
(0,\infty
).
In this case (see e.g.
[
12
, Section 2.3.2]
), the singular value function
\mu(f)
defined above is precisely the
decreasing rearrangement
f^{*}
of the function
f\in S(0,\infty)
given by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{s\geq 0:\ m(\{|f|>s\})\leq t\}.

If
\mathcal{M}=B(H)
and
\tau
is the
standard trace
{\rm tr}
, then it is not difficult to see that

S(\mathcal{M})=S(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\mathcal{M}
and
for
X\in S(\mathcal{M},\tau)
we have

\mu(t,X)=\mu(n,X),\quad t\in[n,n+1),\quad n\geq 0.

For a compact operator
X
the sequence
\{\mu(n,X)\}_{n\geq 0}
is precisely the sequence of singular values of
X.

The notion of singular value function naturally leads to various notions of majorisation of
\tau
-measurable operators.
For
0\leq A,B\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
we say that
A
is majorised by
B
(in the sense of Hardy-Littlewood) if for all
0<t<\tau(\mathbf{1})
we have

\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,A)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,B)\,ds.

In this case, we write
A\prec\prec B
.

In the present paper we are mainly interested in another majorisation introduced in
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
0\leq A,B\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
we say that
A
is
tail majorised
by
B
if for all
0<t<\tau(\mathbf{1})
we have

\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A)\,ds\leq\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,B)\,ds.

In this case, we shall write
A\prec\prec_{\rm tl}B
.

Following
[
17
, Section 2.4]
we introduce the notion of Calkin spaces.

Definition 2.2
.

A linear subspace
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
of
S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is called a
Calkin space
if
B\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
,
A\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
and
\mu(A)\leq\mu(B)
implies that
A\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
. In the special case when
{\mathcal{M}}=L_{\infty}(0,b)
,
0<b\leq\infty
(respectively,
{\mathcal{M}}=\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{N}})
) we use the term Calkin function (respectively, sequence) space instead and denote
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
by
E(0,b)
(respectively,
E({\mathbb{N}})
).

A classical example of a Calkin space is the space
S_{0}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
of all
\tau
-compact operators, defined as

S_{0}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)=\{X\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\ \mu(\infty,X)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu(t,X)=0\}.

Further examples of Calkin spaces are the noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces, defined as

L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau):=\{X\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\mu(X)\in L_{p}(0,\infty)\},\quad 0<p<\infty,

where
L_{p}(0,\infty)
are the classical Lebesgue
L_{p}
-spaces.

A Calkin space is said to be fully symmetric if
A\prec\prec B\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
implies that
A\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. It is straightforward that Lorentz spaces from Introduction are fully symmetric. We say that a Calkin space
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is closed with respect to the tail majorisation if
A\prec\prec_{\rm tl}B\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
implies that
A\in E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Next we introduce the main objects of interest in the present paper, linear functionals that respect tails.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
be a Calkin space.

•

A linear functional
\phi
on
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is said to be a
symmetric functional
, if
\phi(X)=\phi(Y)
for any
0\leq X,Y\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
with
\mu(X)=\mu(Y).

•

A positive linear functional
\varphi
on
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is said to be
fully symmetric

if
\varphi(A)\leq\varphi(B)
provided that
0\leq A,B\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
are such
A\prec\prec B
.

•

A positive linear functional
\varphi
on
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is said to be
respecting tails

if
\varphi(A)\leq\varphi(B)
provided that
0\leq A,B\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
are such
A\prec\prec_{\rm tl}B
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is easy to see that every positive linear functional on
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
that respects tails is necessarily symmetric.

On the space
L_{\infty}(0,b)
,
0<b\leq\infty
define the dilation operator

(\sigma_{1/2}f)(t)=\begin{cases}f(2t),\ 0<t<b/2\\
0,t\geq b/2\end{cases},\ f\in L_{\infty}(0,b).

It follows from
[
9
, Proposition 2.3]
that for every symmetric functional
\phi
on a Calkin space
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
the following equality holds:

\phi(\sigma_{1/2}X)=\phi(X),\ X\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau),

(2.2)

where
\sigma_{1/2}X
stands for any operator such that
\mu(\sigma_{1/2}X)=\sigma_{1/2}\mu(X)
.

In the present paper we consider the following type of Calkin spaces.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
. Let
\Omega
denote the set of all convex decreasing functions
h
on

(0,\infty)
.
For
h\in\Omega
, we define
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
by setting

\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{A\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau):\|A\|_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}=\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A)\,ds<\infty\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is a Calkin space, which is closed with respect to the tail majorisation.
At the same time,
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is not fully symmetric.
Indeed, for
g(t)=\frac{2}{(t+1)^{2}}
,
f(t)=\frac{\log(1+t)}{(t+1)^{2}}
,
t>e^{2}-1
and
f(t)=\frac{2}{e^{4}}
,
0<t\leq e^{2}-1
we have
f\prec\prec g
. However,
setting
h(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}g(s)ds
, we obtain

\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}f(s)\,ds=\frac{1}{2}(1+\log(1+t))\to\infty,\ t\to\infty.

Thus,
f\notin\mathcal{I}_{h}(0,\infty)
.

In the case when
h
is a bounded function we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(s,A)\,ds\leq h(0)\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu(s,A)\,ds<\infty,

and therefore,
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)\subset\mathcal{L}_{1}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
. In terminology of A. Piestch
[
20
, Section 3]
(used for principal ideals in the case
({\mathcal{M}},\tau)=(B(H),{\mathrm{tr}\,})
), this means that the Calkin space
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is of lower type.

Finally, we call a functional
\phi
on
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
normalised if
\phi(X)=1
for every
X\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
such that
\mu(X)=-h^{\prime}
.

3
Main result

In the present section we establish a form of a positive linear functional
\phi
on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, which respects tail majorisation.

We start with defining some properties of linear functionals on
L_{\infty}(0,b)
,
0<b\leq\infty
.

Definition 3.1
.

A linear functional
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,b)
,
0<b\leq\infty
is said to be

(i)

a
state
if it is positive (i.e.,
\omega(f)\geq 0
if
f\geq 0
) and normalised (in a sense that
\omega(\chi_{(0,b)})=1
);

(ii)

dilation invariant
if

\omega(f)=\omega(\sigma_{1/2}f)
for every
f\in L_{\infty}(0,b)
;

(iii)

h
-compatible
, for
h\in\Omega
, if

\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right)=1.

(3.1)

It can be shown that if the function
h
satisfies the following condition:

\lim_{t\to\infty}h(2t)/h(t)=1,

(3.2)

then every dilation invariant state on
L_{\infty}(0,b)
is
h
-compatible.
The following result constructs the analogue of Dixmier traces on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. In the special case when
\mathcal{M}=B(H)
(and
h\in\Omega
satisfies condition (
3.2
)) this result was proved in
[
1
, Theorem 2.12]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
h\in\Omega
and let
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be as in Definition
2.5
.
For every
h
-compatible dilation invariant state
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,\tau(\mathbf{1}))
the functional

\tau_{\omega}(A)=\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A)\,ds\right),\quad 0\leq A\in\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

extends to a normalised
linear functional on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
that respects tails.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.1
) that
\mu(\lambda A)=|\lambda|\mu(A)
for any
A\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
and any
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence,
\tau_{\omega}
is positively homogeneous. We shall show its additivity on the positive cone of
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Let
0\leq A,B\in\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. By
[
17
, Theorem 3.3.3 and Theorem 3.3.4]
for any
0\leq t\leq\tau(\mathbf{1})
we have that

\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}(\mu(s,A)+\mu(s,B))\,ds\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}(\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A+B))(s)\,ds.

(3.3)

Since
A
and
B
are positive and since
\sigma_{1/2}\mu(s,T)=\mu(2s,T)=0
for any
s>\frac{\tau(\mathbf{1})}{2}
and
T\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds

\displaystyle=\|A+B\|_{1}=\tau(A+B)=\tau(A)+\tau(B)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A)ds+\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,B)ds,

and

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\sigma_{1/2}\mu(s,A+B)ds

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})/2}\mu(2s,A+B)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)ds=\|A+B\|_{1},

and so

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\mu(s,A)+\mu(s,B))\,ds

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A+B))(s)\,ds.

Combining the latter inequality with (
3.3
) we obtain that

\displaystyle 2\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A+B))(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\mu(s,A)+\mu(s,B))\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds

for every
0\leq t\leq\tau(\mathbf{1})
.

Dividing all parts by
h(t)
and applying
\omega
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{2}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A+B))(s)\,ds\right)\\
\leq\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\mu(s,A)+\mu(s,B))\,ds\right)\\
\leq\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds\right).\end{split}

(3.4)

Since
\mu(T)
is supported on
[0,\tau(\mathbf{1})]
for any
T\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
, it follows that
\sigma_{1/2}\mu(T)
is supported on
[0,\frac{\tau(\mathbf{1})}{2}]
. Therefore, the function

t\mapsto\frac{2}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A+B))(s)\,ds

is supported on
[0,\frac{\tau(\mathbf{1})}{2}]
. Hence, noting that
t\in[0,\frac{\tau(\mathbf{1})}{2}]
, for the left-hand side of (
3.4
) we have

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\left(t\mapsto\frac{2}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A+B))(s)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{2t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=\omega\left(t\mapsto\left(\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}-1\right)\frac{1}{h(2t)}\int_{2t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{h(2t)}\int_{2t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds\right)

Using
h-
compatibility of
\omega
we obtain

\omega\left(t\mapsto\left(\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}-1\right)\right)=0

and (since
\omega
is positive)

\omega\left(t\mapsto\left(\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}-1\right)\frac{1}{h(2t)}\int_{2t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds\right)=0.

Using dilation invariance of
\omega
we conclude that

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\left(t\mapsto\frac{2}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A+B))(s)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A+B)\,ds\right).

Referring to (
3.4
) we conclude that

\tau_{\omega}(A+B)\leq\tau_{\omega}(A)+\tau_{\omega}(B)\leq\tau_{\omega}(A+B),

that is
\tau_{\omega}
is linear on the positive cone of
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.
Hence, it extends to a linear functional on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. The fact that the extended functional respects tails is straightforward.
∎

In the special case, when the von Neumann algebra is either atomless or atomic with atoms of equal trace we have the converse result that any functional on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
which respect tails has the form
\tau_{\omega}
as in Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a atomless (or atomic with atoms of equal trace)
von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
. Let
h\in\Omega
and let
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be as in Definition
2.5
. If
\varphi
is a normalized linear functional on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
that respects tails, then

\varphi(A)=\tau_{\omega}=\omega\left(\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}\mu(s,A)\,ds\right),\quad 0\leq A\in\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

for some
h
-compatible dilation invariant state
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.4
any linear functional which respects tails is necessarily a symmetric functional.
Since
{\mathcal{M}}
is either atomless or atomic algebra with atoms of equal trace, it follows that there is a bijective correspondence between all symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and all symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(0,\tau(\mathbf{1}))

[
17
, Theorem 4.4.1]
. Here, in the case of atomic algebra with atoms of equal trace, we isometrically embed the space
\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{N}})
into
L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
. Let
\varphi_{0}
on
\mathcal{I}_{h}(0,\tau(\mathbf{1}))
be the corresponding functional, that is a symmetric functional such that
\varphi=\varphi_{0}\circ\mu
.

We firstly note that
\varphi_{0}
respects tails. Indeed, let
f,g\in\mathcal{I}_{h}(0,\tau(\mathbf{1}))
be such that
f\prec\prec_{\rm tl}g
. Then, there exist
A,B\in S({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
, such that
\mu(A)=f^{*}
and
\mu(B)=g^{*}
. In particular,
A,B\in\mathcal{I}_{h}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and
A\prec\prec_{\rm tl}B
. Since
\varphi
respects tails, it follows that

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(f)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(f^{*})=\varphi_{0}(\mu(A))=\varphi(A)\leq\varphi(B)=\varphi_{0}(\mu(B))=\varphi_{0}(g),

showing that
\varphi_{0}
respect tails.

Let
D_{h}
be the linear span of all positive non-increasing functions from
\mathcal{I}_{h}(0,\tau(\mathbf{1}))
. Observe that
x\in D_{h}
if and only if
x=y^{*}-z^{*}
, for
y,z\in\mathcal{I}_{h}(0,\tau(\mathbf{1}))
.

Define a map
T:D_{h}\to L_{\infty}(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})
by the formula

(Tx)(t)=\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}x(s)ds.

Since
T
is injection, one can define a linear functional
\omega_{0}
on
TD_{h}
by the formula
\omega_{0}(Tx)=\varphi_{0}(x).

Choose
f\in TD_{h}
and, so,
x\in D_{h}
such that
f(t)=\frac{1}{h(t)}\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}x(s)ds.
We have

\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}x(s)\,ds\leq\left|\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}x(s)\,ds\right|\leq\|x\|_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}\cdot\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(-h^{\prime}(s))\,ds.

Since
x\in D_{h}
, then it is of the form
x=y^{*}-z^{*}
for some
y,z\in\mathcal{I}_{h}(0,\tau(\mathbf{1}))
. Thus,

\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}y^{*}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}(-\|x\|_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}h^{\prime}(s)+z^{*}(s))\,ds.

Since
\varphi_{0}
is normalized tail-respecting functional, it follows that

\varphi_{0}(y^{*})=\varphi_{0}(y)\leq\|x\|_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}\varphi_{0}(-h^{\prime})+\varphi_{0}(z)=\|x\|_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}+\varphi_{0}(z^{*})

and,

\varphi_{0}(x)\leq\|x\|_{\mathcal{I}_{h}}.
So,
\omega_{0}(f)\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
. By Hahn-Banach Theorem there exists
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})
such that it coincides with
\omega_{0}
on
TD_{h}
and
\omega(f)\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
for every
f\in L_{\infty}(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})
.

Note, that the function
t\mapsto-2h^{\prime}(2t)
belongs to
D_{h}
. Due to (
2.2
), we have

\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right)=\varphi_{0}(t\mapsto-2h^{\prime}(2t))=\varphi_{0}(-h^{\prime})=1.

That is,
\omega
is
h
-compatible. Hence,

\omega(|\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}-1|)=0
.

Let
R
be the smallest
\sigma_{1/2}
-invariant subspace of
L_{\infty}(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})
, containing
TD_{h}
. We claim that
\omega
is
\sigma_{1/2}
-invariant on
R
. It is sufficient to show that
\omega(\sigma_{1/2}f)=\omega(f)
for every
f\in TD_{h}
or, equivalently,
\omega(\sigma_{1/2}Tx)=\omega(Tx)
for every
x\in D_{h}
or, (in view of definition of
\omega
on
TD_{h}
and (
2.2
)) equivalently,
\omega(\sigma_{1/2}Tx)=\omega(T(2\sigma_{1/2}x))
for every
x\in D_{h}
.
Since

\displaystyle|\sigma_{1/2}Tx-T(2\sigma_{1/2}x)|

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right)\frac{1}{h(2t)}\int_{2t}^{\tau(\mathbf{1})}x(u)\,du

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right)\|x\|_{\mathcal{I}_{h}},

we obtain the claim. Now, using the invariant form of the Hahn-Banach Theorem (see, e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 3.3.1]
) we can extend
\omega
(preserving the norm) to a
\sigma_{1/2}
-invariant functional on the whole
L_{\infty}(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})
(we shall denote it by the same letter).
Since
\omega(\chi_{(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})})=\omega_{0}(T(-h^{\prime}))=1
, we conclude that
\omega
is normalised.
Thus,
\omega
is a
h
-compatible dilation invariant state on
L_{\infty}(0,{\tau(\mathbf{1})})
.
∎

In conclusion we discuss existence of symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
which are supported at zero or at infinity. For that we firstly recall necessary definitions.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
be a Calkin space.

•

A symmetric functional
\phi
on
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is said to be
supported at infinity
if
\phi(XE^{|X|}(a,+\infty))=0
for every
a>0
and every
X\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
.

•

A symmetric functional
\phi
on
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is said to be
supported at zero
if
\phi(XE^{|X|}(0,a))=0
for every
a>0
and every
X\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
.

•

A symmetric functional
\varphi
on
E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is said to be
singular
if
\phi(X)=0
for any
X\in E({\mathcal{M}},\tau)\cap{\mathcal{M}}
such that
\tau(s(X))<\infty
.

It follows from
[
13
, Theorem 4.3.2]
that every positive singular symmetric functional on a Calkin space has a unique decomposition as a sum of a positive symmetric functional supported at infinity and that supported at zero.

Definition 3.5
.

(i) A linear functional
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,b)
,
0<b\leq\infty
is said to be

supported at zero
if
\omega(\chi_{(a,b)})=0
for every
0<a\leq b
.

(ii) A linear functional
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
is said to be

supported at infinity
if
\omega(\chi_{(0,a)})=0
for every
0<a<\infty
.

It follows from Theorem
3.2
and
3.3
that a symmetric functional
\tau_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
which respect tails is supported at zero if and only if he corresponding state
\omega
is supported at zero. If, in addition, the trace
\tau
is infinite, these theorems imply that the functional
\tau_{\omega}
is supported at infinity if and only if
\omega
is supported at infinity.

The following result proves a criterion for the existence of tail-respecting functionals on spaces
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a atomless (or atomic with atoms of equal trace)
von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
and let
h\in\Omega
.

1.

The space
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
admits non-zero tail-respecting functionals supported at infinity
if and only if
\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)=0
and

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}=1.

(3.5)

2.

The space
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
admits non-zero tail-respecting functionals supported at zero if and only if

\lim_{t\to 0}h(t)=\infty
and

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}=1.

(3.6)

Proof.

(i). Suppose firstly that the space
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
admits a non-trivial tail-respecting functional
\phi
supported at infinity. Note that for
A\in\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
and every
t>0
we have that
\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu(s,A)\,ds<\infty
and, so,
\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu(s,A)\,ds\to 0
, as
t\to\infty
. Therefore, if
\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)>0
, then by Theorem
3.3
every tail-respecting functional on
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is zero. Thus,
h
must vanish at infinity. To prove (
3.5
) we firstly note that
by Theorem
3.3
there exists an
h
-compatible dilation invariant state
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
, such that
\phi=\tau_{\omega}
. Moreover, this
\omega
is supported at infinity. Hence,
\omega
vanishes on every function with compact support. Since
\omega
is a state, it follows that it vanishes on all functions vanishing at infinity. In other words,
\omega
is an extended limit at infinity. Hence,
\omega(f)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}f(t)
for every
f\in L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
. In particular,

1=\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\leq 1,

since
h
is decreasing. Hence,
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}=1
, as required.

Conversely, let
h
be a convex decreasing function vanishing at infinity and let (
3.5
) be satisfied. Then, the function
s\mapsto\frac{h(st)}{h(t)}
is convex and decreasing. Hence, the function

\alpha(s):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{h(st)}{h(t)},\ s>0

is convex and decreasing. Since
\alpha(1)=1
and
\alpha(2)=1
, the convexity implies that
\alpha(s)=1
for every
s\geq 1
. In particular,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{h(2^{n}t)}{h(t)}=1\ \text{for every}\ n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Let us choose a sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
,
n\to\infty
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(2^{n+1}t_{n})}{h(t_{n})}=1.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
t_{n+1}>2^{n+1}t_{n}
. For every
t_{n}\leq t\leq 2^{n}t_{n}
we have

1\geq\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\geq\frac{h(2^{n+1}t_{n})}{h(t_{n})}\to 1,n\to\infty.

By
[
23
, Theorem 17]
there exists a dilation invariant extended limit
\omega
(at infinity) on
L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
such that

\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right)=p_{D}\left(t\mapsto\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{a\geq 1}\frac{1}{\log(t)}\int_{a}^{at}\frac{h(2s)}{h(s)}\,\frac{ds}{s}.

On the other hand, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we can find a subsequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset\{t_{n}\}
such that

\frac{h(2s)}{h(s)}\geq 1-1/n
for every
s_{n}\leq s\leq 2^{n}s_{n}
. We have

1\geq p_{D}\left(t\mapsto\frac{h(2t)}{h(t)}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log(2^{n})}\int_{s_{n}}^{2^{n}s_{n}}\frac{h(2s)}{h(s)}\,\frac{ds}{s}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}(1-1/n)=1.

Thus, there exists an
h
-compatible dilation invariant state
\omega
on
L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
, supported at infinity. Theorem
3.2
implies that the space
\mathcal{I}_{h}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
admits non-zero tail-respecting functionals supported at infinity.

The proof of second assertion is similar and is, therefore, omitted.
∎

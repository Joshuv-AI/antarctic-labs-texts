Abstract
. We prove a formula for the Bloch–Kato logarithm of the bottom class in the Asai–Flach Euler system associated to a quadratic Hilbert modular form. We show that this can be expressed as a value, outside the interpolation range, of the p p -adic Asai L L -function constructed in the prequel paper [ GLZ23 ] .

11F41, 11F33, 11G18, 14G35

1.
Introduction

The theory of Euler systems – norm-compatible families of cohomology classes for global Galois representations – has played an important role in many discoveries in number theory. A key aspect of this theory is
explicit reciprocity laws
, which relate Euler systems to the special values of
L
-functions; this can be used as a criterion for the non-vanishing of Euler systems, a crucial input in applications to the Bloch–Kato conjecture and related problems.

One strategy for proving such explicit reciprocity laws, introduced in the seminal paper
[
BDP13
]
in the context of Heegner points, proceeds as follows. Supposing we already have an Euler system for some Galois representation
V
, we aim to carry out the following steps:

(1)

Construct a
p
-adic
L
-function, for some
p
-adic family of Galois representations specialising to
V
, whose values in a suitable interpolation range are critical values of the corresponding complex
L
-functions.

(2)

Relate values of this
p
-adic
L
-function at suitable points
outside
the range of interpolation to Bloch–Kato logarithms of Euler system classes.

(3)

Via analytic continuation, deduce a relation between Euler systems and
p
-adic
L
-values at points
inside
the interpolation range, where the
p
-adic
L
-function specialises to the complex
L
-value.

In the present paper we focus on the
Euler system of Asai–Flach classes
, introduced in
[
LLZ18
]
, which is an Euler system for the Asai, or twisted tensor, Galois representation associated to a Hilbert modular form for a real quadratic field. In this setting, step (1) of the above strategy was carried out in the prequel paper
[
GLZ23
]
. The goal of the present paper is to carry out step (2): the main result (
Theorem

7.7.6
) expresses the logarithms of Euler system classes as non-critical values of the
p
-adic
L
-function constructed in
op.cit.
. The remaining step (3), with applications to the Bloch–Kato conjecture and the Iwasawa main conjecture for Asai
L
-functions, will be carried out in a third paper in the series, currently in preparation.

This strategy was successfully carried out for the Euler system of Beilinson–Flach elements (attached to a Rankin–Selberg convolution of modular forms) in
[
BDR15
,
KLZ17
]
; and more recently in the
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
setting in
[
LPSZ21
,
LZ20
]
. The Asai representation can be seen as a “twisted version” of the Rankin–Selberg convolution, and the construction of the Euler system classes is recognisably a direct generalisation of the Beilinson–Flach case; but the techniques used to construct
p
-adic
L
-functions and prove the reciprocity law for Rankin–Selberg convolutions do not straightforwardly generalise, since they rely on the decomposition of the Shimura variety for
\GL_{2}\times\GL_{2}
as a product of two factors, and new techniques are required. Hence the present paper follows a strategy rather closer to the
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
case, relying on the use of
higher Hida theory
, which has been developed for Hilbert modular varieties at split primes by the first author
[
Gro21
]
; and its “overconvergent” version,
higher Coleman theory
. The higher Coleman theory we use is based on the general results of
[
BP20
]
, with some slight modifications to allow
p
-adic interpolation at only one of the primes above
p
, for which we refer to
[
LZ22
]
.

(We remark that a related problem is also considered in the paper
[
LSZ20
]
, where Skinner and two of the present authors computed a pairing which also involves the Bloch–Kato logarithm of the Asai–Flach class, but projected into a different piece of the de Rham cohomology which can be computed using only conventional, rather than higher, Coleman theory. The earlier paper gives an explicitly computable formula in terms of
p
-adic Hilbert modular forms, but one which does not seem to have any straightforward relationship to
p
-adic
L
-values, in contrast to the formula of
Theorem

7.7.6
.)

-ordinary locus

We now consider a modified form of Besser’s finite-polynomial cohomology, namely
Gros fp-cohomology
, for the
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary locus (with compact supports). This cohomology, denoted by
\widetilde{R\Gamma}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
can be defined as the mapping fibre of the map

\widetilde{R\Gamma}_{\mathrm{dR},c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle)\xrightarrow{\ P_{1+j}(p^{-1-j}\varphi)\ }R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c}(X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle).

(As before, this is in fact independent of
j
in the stated range, despite the notations.) Note that although
\varphi_{1}
lifts to
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}
, the full Frobenius
\varphi
does not; so although
R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c}(X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle)
is isomorphic to de Rham cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}
, the action of Frobenius (given by the functoriality of rigid cohomology) cannot be ‘seen’ via this description.

For proper schemes such as
\mathbb{X}_{G}
, there is no difference between Gros fp-cohomology and the usual fp-cohomology, so there is an extension-by-zero map

\widetilde{R\Gamma}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})\to R\Gamma_{\fp,c}(\mathbb{X}_{G},\mathcal{V}_{G}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j}).

Proposition 5.5.1
.

There exists a class

\tilde{\nu}_{\fp}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})

with the following properties:

•

Its image in
H^{2}_{\fp,c}(\mathbb{X}_{G},\mathcal{V}_{G}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
is the
\nu_{\fp}
of
Proposition

4.2.3
.

•

Its image in
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{dR},c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle)
is the class
(\nu^{1-\mathrm{ord}},0)
of
Proposition

5.4.1
.

Remark 5.5.2
.

The reader may be relieved to hear that the class
\tilde{\nu}_{\fp}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}
is “the ultimate among liftings of
\nu
”: all other variants of
\nu
will be images of this one.

Proof.

From the mapping-fibre definition of Gros fp-cohomology we have a long exact sequence

\dots\to H^{1}_{\mathrm{rig},c}(X_{G,0}^{1-\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle)\to\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})\to\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{dR},c}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle)\to\dots,

in which the boundary map is
P(\varphi)\circ\iota
. Moreover, this is compatible under extension-by-zero with the corresponding sequence for fp-cohomology of
\mathbb{X}_{G}
.

We have seen that the image of
(\nu^{1-\mathrm{ord}},0)
under
\iota
is the class
\nu_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}
, which is annihilated by
P(\varphi)
. Hence it lifts to
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c}
. This lift is unique up to the image of an element of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{rig},c}(X_{G,0}^{1-\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle)
; but from
Proposition

5.1.4
it follows that this group has trivial
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}
-eigenspace for the prime-to-
p
Hecke operators, so there is a unique Hecke-equivariant lifting of
(\nu^{1-\mathrm{ord}},0)
to Gros-fp cohomology. The image of this class under extension-by-0 is therefore a Hecke-equivariant lifting of
\nu_{\mathrm{dR}}
to fp-cohomology of
\mathbb{X}_{G}
, so it must be
\nu_{\fp}
.
∎

It is easily checked that
\iota^{-1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}})=\mathcal{X}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
, so we have the following:

Corollary 5.5.3
.

The pairing (
4.1
) is equal to
\left\langle\iota^{[j],*}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{\fp}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}\right),\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn}\right\rangle
, where
\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn}
is the image of
\mathrm{Eis}^{t}_{\syn}
in the Gros fp-cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
.∎

Here we define
\iota^{[j],*}
for classes in Gros fp-cohomology using the quasi-isomorphism from the BGG complex to the full de Rham complex. We shall give explicit formulae in
Section

7.1
below, but first we need to give an explicit form for
\tilde{\nu}_{\fp}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}
, which can only be done after restricting to
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{G}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}
.

6.
Restricting to the fully-ordinary locus

6.1.
Cohomology with partial compact support

We now consider the cohomology of the fully ordinary locus
X_{G,0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
. Since the complement
X_{G,0}-X_{G,0}^{\mathrm{ord}}
is the disjoint union of a closed subvariety
X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ss})}
and the open subvariety
X_{G,0}^{(2-\mathrm{ss})}\cap X_{G,0}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
, we can apply the formalism of
[
LZ20
, §13]
to define “cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}}
with compact support towards
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{(1-\mathrm{ss})}
” (with coefficients in any abelian sheaf on
\mathcal{X}_{G}
). We write this as
R\Gamma_{c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G},-)
. By construction, this comes equipped with a restriction map

R\Gamma_{c}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{1-\mathrm{ord}},-\right)\to R\Gamma_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},-\right),

which fits into a triangle whose third term is the compactly-supported cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{1-\mathrm{ord}}\cap\mathcal{X}_{G}^{2-\mathrm{ss}}
. In particular, by the same argument as
Proposition

5.1.4
, we have isomorphisms

R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle\right)[\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}]\longleftarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{1-\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle\right)[\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}]\longrightarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{rig},c}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle\right)[\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}].

The advantage of working with
\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}}
is that both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
have liftings.

Notation 6.1.1
.

Write
\nu^{\mathrm{ord}}\in H^{1}_{c1}\Big(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\omega^{(-k_{1},k_{2}+2)}(-D)\Big)
for the image of
\nu^{1-\mathrm{ord}}
under the above restriction map.

Note 6.1.2
.

Over the ordinary locus, we have commuting liftings of both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
, which both act on
R\Gamma_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\omega^{(\dots)}\right)
; and the operator
T_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
decomposes as

T_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}=U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}+\varphi_{2},

with
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\circ\varphi_{2}=p^{k_{2}+1}\langle\mathfrak{p}_{2}\rangle
.

Corollary 6.1.3
.

The class
P(\varphi)\cdot\nu^{\mathrm{ord}}
lies in the kernel of the Hecke operator
U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
.

Proof.

We have
\varphi=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}
.
The result follows easily from
Theorem

5.2.1
and Note
6.1.2
, using the fact that
\nu^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}
is a
T_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
-eigenvector.
∎

6.2.
The Poznan spectral sequence

We now recall a spectral sequence (introduced in
[
LZ20
]
) relating Gros fp-cohomology to coherent cohomology. Here Gros fp-cohomology is defined in the same way as for the
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
-ordinary locus above, but now with
c1
-support.

Definition 6.2.1
.

We define groups
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
, for
m,n\geqslant 0
, by

\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\dots)=H^{n}_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},(\tau_{\geqslant 1}\BGG)^{m}(-D)\right)\oplus H^{n}_{c1}\left(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\BGG^{m-1}(-D)\right);

and we define differentials
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\dots)\to\mathscr{C}^{m+1,n}_{\fp,c1}(\dots)
by

(x,y)\mapsto\left(\nabla x,P(\varphi/p^{n})\iota(x)-\nabla y\right),

where
\iota
is the inclusion of
\Fil^{1+j}\BGG^{\bullet}
into
\BGG^{\bullet}
, and
\nabla
the differential of the BGG complex.

Note that
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}
is zero for
m\leqslant 0
(this is obvious for
m\leqslant-1
, and holds for
m=0
since
\Fil^{(1+j)}\BGG^{0}=0
). It is also zero for
n\leqslant 0
, since
H^{0}_{c1}
vanishes for locally-free sheaves.

Proposition 6.2.2
.

There is a first-quadrant spectral sequence, the
Poznań spectral sequence
, with

{}^{\Pz}E_{1}^{mn}=\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j}),

and the differentials on the
E_{1}
page given by the formula above. This spectral sequence abuts to the Gros fp-cohomology
\widetilde{H}^{m+n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
.

Definition 6.2.3
.

We define a
coherent fp-pair
(of degree
(m,n)
, twist
1+j
and
c1
-support) to be an element of the kernel of the differential
\mathscr{C}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}\to\mathscr{C}^{m+1,n}_{\fp,c1}
; we write the group of these as
\mathscr{Z}^{m,n}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
.

Thus an fp-pair is a pair of elements

x\in H^{n}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},{\mathscr{F}il}^{1+j}\BGG^{m}(-D)),\qquad y\in H^{n}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},{\mathscr{F}il}^{1+j}\BGG^{m-1}(-D))

which satisfy

(6.1)

\nabla(x)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla(y)=P(p^{-1-j}\varphi)\iota(x).

Note 6.2.4
.

Given
x
, the equation (
6.1
) does not determine the element
y
uniquely: it is determined up to an element of
H^{n}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{m-1}(-D))^{\nabla=0}
.

Proposition 6.2.5
.

For
0\leqslant j\leqslant\min\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
, the spectral sequence gives rise to an isomorphism

\alpha_{G,\fp}:\mathscr{Z}^{1,1}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}_{G}^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j}).

Proof.

Since the
E_{1}
page of the spectral sequence is zero for
m\leqslant 0
, the term
{}^{\Pz}E_{2}^{1,1}
is the kernel of the differential on
{}^{\Pz}E_{1}^{1,1}
, which is the group of fp-pairs. Since this is the only nonzero term with
m+n=2
, and the incoming and outgoing differentials at
{}^{\Pz}E_{r}^{1,1}
are trivially zero for all
r\geqslant 1
, we conclude that
{}^{\Pz}E_{2}^{1,1}
coincides with
\widetilde{H}^{2}
of the abutment, as required.
∎

Corollary 6.2.6
.

Every cohomology class in
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})
can be uniquely represented by a coherent fp-pair of degree
(1,1)
.

Proof.

Since the
E_{1}
terms of the spectral sequence are supported in the region
m,n\geqslant 1
, there are no other terms of total degree 2 except
(m,n)=(1,1)
; and clearly
E_{2}^{(1,1)}=E_{\infty}^{(1,1)}
since the differentials on the
E_{2}
page and beyond land outside this region.
∎

An exactly analogous argument shows that for the truncated de Rham cohomology groups
\widetilde{H}^{i}_{\mathrm{dR},c1}
(the hypercohomology of
\tau_{\geqslant 1}\BGG^{\bullet}(-D)
), we have an isomorphism

H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{1}(-D))\xrightarrow{\ \alpha_{G,\mathrm{rig}}\ }\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{dR},c1}^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle,1+j).

Lemma 6.2.7
.

Let
0\leqslant j\leqslant\min\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
. We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Z}^{1,1}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-D\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{1}(-D))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}_{\mathrm{dR},c1}^{2}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle,1+j)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{G,\fp}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{G,\mathrm{rig}}}

where the vertical arrows are the natural projection maps.

Proof.

Clear from the constructions.
∎

6.3.
Construction of a coherent fp-pair

Notation 6.3.1
.

Define

\tilde{\nu}^{(\mathrm{ord})}_{\fp}\in\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\fp,c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle;1+j,P_{1+j})

to be the restriction of the class
\tilde{\nu}^{(1-\mathrm{ord})}_{\fp}
constructed above.

Corollary 6.3.2
.

There exists a uniquely determined class

\xi\in H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\omega^{(-k_{1},-k_{2})}(-\mathcal{D})),

which is independent of
j
, such that
(\nu^{(\mathrm{ord})},\xi)
forms an fp-pair representing the class
\tilde{\nu}^{(\mathrm{ord})}_{\fp}
, and such that
\xi
lies in the
\Pi
-eigenspace for the Hecke operators away from
pN
.

Proof.

The existence of
\xi
is immediate from Corollary
6.2.6
and Lemma
6.2.7
; the independence from
j
is clear by construction.

Now, if
\xi^{\prime}
is another element such that
(\nu^{(\mathrm{ord})},\xi^{\prime})
also represents
\tilde{\nu}^{(\mathrm{ord})}_{\fp}
, then

\xi-\xi^{\prime}\in H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{0}(-D))^{\nabla=0}\cong H^{1}_{\mathrm{rig},c1}(X_{G,0},\mathcal{V}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle).

As we have seen, the
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}
-eigenspace in this cohomology is zero, so there is a unique choice of
\xi
which is Hecke-equivariant.
∎

Lemma 6.3.3
.

The element
\xi
has the following properties:

(6.2)

\varphi_{1}.\xi=\alpha_{1}\,\xi\qquad\text{and}\qquad U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}.\xi=0.

Proof.

We first show the latter statement. We deduce from Corollary
6.1.3
that

U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}\cdot\xi\in H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\BGG^{0}(-\mathcal{D}))^{\nabla=0}[\Pi_{f}^{\prime}].

But as observed above, this space is zero. The former statement follows analogously, by considering the element
(\varphi_{1}-\alpha_{1})\cdot\xi
.
∎

We now lift these classes from the BGG complex to the full de Rham complex:

Definition 6.3.4
.

(1)

Write
\dot{\xi}
for the image of
\xi
in
H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},\mathcal{V}(-D))
.

(2)

Similarly, for
0\leqslant j\leqslant\min\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
write
\dot{\nu}^{(\mathrm{ord})}_{j}
for the image of
\nu^{(\mathrm{ord})}
in

H^{1}_{c1}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G},{\mathscr{F}il}^{j}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}\langle-\mathcal{D}\rangle).

Lemma 6.3.5
.

The element
\dot{\xi}
also satisfies

(6.3)

\varphi_{1}.\dot{\xi}=\alpha_{1}\,\dot{\xi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}.\dot{\xi}=0.

The following result will be very important for the regulator evaluation:

Proposition 6.3.6
.

We have

U_{p}\circ\iota^{*}(\dot{\xi})=0.

Proof.

As maps
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{G})\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H})
, we have the following identity:

(6.4)

U_{p}\circ\iota^{*}\circ\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}=\iota^{*}\circ(\langle\mathfrak{p}_{1}\rangle U_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}).

Since
\dot{\xi}
is an eigenvector of
\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
with non-zero eigenvalue, and it is in the kernel of
\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
, the Proposition follows.
∎

7.
Expression via coherent cohomology

7.1.
Relating the BGG and de Rham complexes

We need to recall some formulae relating the de Rham and BGG complexes for
\GL_{2}
. Let
k\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. The BGG complex for weight
k\geqslant 0
is given by
\left[\omega^{-k}\xrightarrow{\Theta}\omega^{k+2}\right]
, where
\Theta
is a differential operator given in terms of
q
-expansions by

\Theta=\tfrac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\theta^{k+1},\qquad\theta=q\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}q}.

(Cf.
[
TX16
, Remark 2.17]
for example.) The map
\BGG^{\bullet}\to\DR^{\bullet}
is given as follows.

•

In degree 1, it is given by the tensor product of the natural inclusion
\omega^{k}=\Fil^{k}(\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{H}
and the Kodaira–Spencer isomorphism
\omega^{2}\cong\Omega^{1}(D)
.

•

In degree 0, a section
s
of
\omega^{-k}
is mapped to the unique section of
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}
whose image in
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}/\Fil^{0}\cong\omega^{-k}
is
s
, and whose image under the differential
\nabla
lands in
\omega^{k}\otimes\Omega^{1}(D)
.

We now recall (and somewhat reformulate) some results from §4 of
[
KLZ20
]
giving a completely explicit description of these maps. We write
\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H}
for the ordinary locus
as a classical rigid space
(not a dagger space), i.e. neglecting overconvergence. Passing to the Igusa tower
\mathscr{I\!G}_{H}
(the canonical
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
-covering of
\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H}
parametrising ordinary elliptic curves with a trivialization of their formal group), we obtain a canonical section
v
of
\omega
, corresponding to the invariant differential form
\tfrac{dT}{T}
on the Tate curve
\mathbf{G}_{m}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
; note that
\varphi^{*}(v)=pv
. If we let
w
be the unique section such that
\nabla(v)=u\otimes\xi
, where
\xi
the local basis of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathscr{X}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{H}}(D)
corresponding to
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}q}{q}
, then
v
and
w
are a basis of sections of
\mathcal{W}_{H}
over the Igusa tower, with
w
spanning the unit-root subspace (we have
\varphi^{*}(w)=w
) and
\nabla w=0
. Hence we obtain a basis of sections
(v^{a}w^{k-a})_{0\leqslant a\leqslant k}
of
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}
in which the actions of
\nabla
and
\varphi
are completely explicit.

In these coordinates, the map from
\omega^{-k}
to
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{W}_{H}
sends a section
\mathcal{G}
of
\omega^{-k}
to the section

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\tfrac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}\theta^{i}(\mathcal{G})\cdot v^{i}w^{k-i}.

One verifies easily that the image of this under
\nabla
is
\theta^{k+1}(\mathcal{G})v^{k}\otimes\xi
, as expected. (Note that it is not
a priori
obvious that such a sum is overconvergent if
\mathcal{G}
is, since neither
\theta
nor the local bases
v^{i}w^{k-i}
have any meaning outside the ordinary locus.)

7.2.
The Eisenstein class as a coherent fp-pair

Recall that the
\GL_{2}
Eisenstein class
\mathrm{Eis}^{t}_{\syn,N}
lies in
H^{1}_{\syn}(\mathbb{Y}_{H},\mathcal{V}_{H}(1+t))
, where
\mathbb{Y}_{H}
is the
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-model of the modular curve
Y_{1}(N)
, and
\mathcal{V}_{H}
is the sheaf corresponding to the
t
-th symmetric power of the standard representation.

If we restrict to the open subscheme
\mathbb{Y}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
given by removing the supersingular points of the special fibre (so
\mathbb{Y}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
has the same generic fibre as
\mathbb{Y}_{H}
), and work with Gros syntomic cohomology, then we have a convenient explicit description: a class
x\in\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\syn}(\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}_{H},1+t)
is given by a pair
(x_{0},x_{1})
, where

x_{0}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}},\mathcal{V}),\qquad x_{1}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{X}_{1}(N)^{\mathrm{ord}},\Fil^{t}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}\langle D\rangle),\qquad\nabla x_{0}=(1-p^{-1-t}\varphi)x_{1}.

The sheaf
\Fil^{t}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1}\langle D\rangle
is simply
\omega^{t+2}
, so
x_{1}
is an overconvergent
p
-adic modular form of weight
t+2
; via this description,
\varphi
acts on overconvergent forms as
p^{t+1}\langle p\rangle V_{p}
, where
\langle p\rangle
is the diamond operator for
p\bmod N
.

We let
\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn,N}
be the image of
\mathrm{Eis}^{t}_{\syn,N}
in this group; we shall now write down an explicit representing pair. The following is a reformulation of Theorem 4.5.7 of
[
KLZ20
]
; the formulations differ because we are using symmetric powers here rather than symmetric tensors (the basis vector
v^{[r,s]}
of
op.cit.
corresponds to
\tfrac{v^{s}w^{r}}{r!s!}
in our present notation) and because we use a slightly different notation for Eisenstein series following
[
Kat04
]
.

Proposition 7.2.1
.

The Eisenstein class
\widetilde{\mathrm{Eis}}^{t,\mathrm{ord}}_{\syn,N}
is represented by the pair of sections
\left(\epsilon_{0}^{t},\epsilon_{1}^{t}\right)
whose restrictions to
\mathscr{X}_{H}^{\mathrm{ord}}
are

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}

\displaystyle=-N^{k}\sum_{u=0}^{t}\frac{(-1)^{t-u}}{u!}\theta^{u}\left(E^{-t,\mathrm{ord}}_{0,1/N}\right)\cdot v^{t-u}w^{u},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{N^{t}}{t!}F^{t+2}_{0,1/N}\cdot v^{t}\otimes\xi.

Here
F^{t+2}_{0,1/N}
is the algebraic Eisenstein series with
q
-expansion

\zeta(-1-t)+\sum_{n>0}q^{n}\sum_{d|n}(\tfrac{n}{d})^{t+1}(\zeta_{N}^{d}+(-1)^{t}\zeta_{N}^{-d}),

and
E^{-t,\mathrm{ord}}_{0,1/N}
is an ordinary
p
-adic Eisenstein series of weight
-t
, satisfying
\theta^{t+1}(E^{-t,\mathrm{ord}}_{0,1/N})=(1-\langle p\rangle V_{p})F^{t+2}_{0,1/N}
.∎

Remark 7.2.2
.

Note that the individual terms
\theta^{u}(E^{-t,\mathrm{ord}}_{0,1/N})
are
p
-adic modular forms, but they are not overconvergent (unless
u=0
). Nonetheless,
\epsilon_{0}
is an overconvergent section of
\mathcal{V}_{H}
; the non-overconvergence arises because the sections
v,w
we are using to trivialize
\mathcal{V}_{H}
over the ordinary locus are not themselves overconvergent.

7.3.
Explicit formulae for the Clebsch–Gordan map

The map

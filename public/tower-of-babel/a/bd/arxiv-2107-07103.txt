On Optimal Approximations for k k -Submodular Maximization via Multilinear Extension
Lingxiao Huang, Baoxiang Wang, Huanjian Zhou

Abstract
We investigate a more generalized form of submodular maximization, referred to as k k -submodular maximization, with applications across social networks and machine learning domains. In this work, we propose the multilinear extension of k k -submodular functions and unified Frank-Wolfe-type frameworks based on that. Our frameworks accomodate 1) monotone or non-monotone functions, and 2) various constraint types including matroid constraints, knapsack constraints, and their combinations. Notably, we attain an asymptotically optimal 1 / 2 1/2 -approximation for monotone k k -submodular maximization problems with knapsack constraints, surpassing the previous 1 / 3 1/3 -approximation [ 15 ] . The foundation for our analysis stems from new insights into specific linear and monotone properties pertaining to the multilinear extension.

Solving these problems all involves maximizing a

Intuitively, the

For instance, consider identifying influential individuals in a social network. For a fixed topic, the newly selected influential individuals will contribute less to the overall user coverage if many influential individuals have already been selected, and more if only a few have been selected.
Similarly, adding additional features in regression problems and placing additional sensors in an area also share such diminishing returns property.

Formally, for an integer

\min_{0}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t})_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mathbf{s}_{i}\mathbf{t}_{i}\neq 0,\mathbf{s}_{i}\neq\mathbf{t}_{i},\\
\min(\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{t}_{i}),&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.\max_{0}(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{t})_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\mathbf{s}_{i}\mathbf{t}_{i}\neq 0,\mathbf{s}_{i}\neq\mathbf{t}_{i},\\
\max(\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{t}_{i}),&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

1.2
Technical overview

We adopt a continuous approach for our algorithms (Algorithms
1
and
2
), utilizing the newly proposed multilinear extension (Definition
3.2
). Our algorithms extend the previous ones
[
2
,
5
]
for submodular maximization, for which we briefly review now.

Continuous methods for submodular maximization via multilinear extension.

Multilinear extension
F
provides a useful relaxation of submodular maximization
f
to continuous space, maintaining coordinate linearity and specific submodular properties. Its favorable properties have led to the application of multilinear extension in various prior works
[
3
,
9
,
5
,
7
]
to design continuous methods for constrained submodular maximization, which typically involve two stages.
In the first stage, it starts with an empty solution
\mathbf{x}(0)
and updates it during the time interval
[0,1]
by
Frank-Wolfe or continuous greedy method to obtain a fractional solution that approximately maximizes the extension
F
.
In the second stage, the fractional solution
\mathbf{x}(1)
is rounded to a feasible integral solution.

Challenges in continuous methods for
k
-submodular maximization.

Designing rounding schemes to
k
-submodular functions is a straightforward process (Lemma
3.5
).
Therefore, we focus on the technical challenges of the first stage. We provide a summary of these challenges below.

1.

The domain of submodular extension is
[0,1]^{n}
, which benefits the closure of the coordinate-wise maximum operation, i.e.,
\mathbf{x}\vee\mathbf{y}\in[0,1]^{n}
for all
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in[0,1]^{n}
.
This property is crucial in the analysis of the approximation ratio of
\mathbf{x}(1)
.
However, the domain of
k
-submodular extension is the corner of the cube
\Delta_{k}^{n}=\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{nk}:\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}\leq 1,\forall i\in[n]\}
, rather than
[0,1]^{nk}
. Consequently, the closure property no longer holds.

2.

Another beneficial property of submodular extension is the Lipschitzness
[
2
]
, which regulates the value change of each step of continuous methods.
However, it is unclear whether this property persists in
k
-submodular extension due to the additional structure of
k
orthants.

Technical idea: Auxiliary points and novel properties of our multilinear extension.

To tackle the first challenge, we shift our focus to the linear combination operation, which involves constructing
a\mathbf{x}+(1-a)\mathbf{y}
(
a\in[0,1]
) for
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Delta_{k}^{n}
instead of coordinate-wise maximum.
This operation has the advantage of being closed in
\Delta_{k}^{n}
, inspiring us to construct auxiliary points for analysis.
Specifically, given
\mathbf{o}^{\star}
as the optimal fractional solution of
k
-submodular extension
F
and
\mathbf{x}(t)\in t\cdot\Delta_{k}^{n}
as the arriving point at time step
t\in[0,1]
, we create an auxiliary point
\mathbf{o}(t)=\mathbf{x}(t)+(1-t)\mathbf{o}^{\star}
, which is guaranteed to be in
\Delta_{k}^{n}
due to the closure property.
The use of auxiliary points has been previously explored in the literature
[
17
,
26
,
28
]
, demonstrating to be useful for analysis purposes.

Then we investigate the relation between
F(\mathbf{x}(t))
and
F(\mathbf{o}(t))
, whose key is to address the aforementioned second challenge.
When
f
is monotone, we demonstrate that
F(\mathbf{x}(t+\delta))-F(\mathbf{x}(t))\gtrsim F(\mathbf{o}(t))-F(\mathbf{o}(t+\delta))
, which directly leads to a conclusion that
F(\mathbf{x}(1))\gtrsim\frac{1}{2}F(\mathbf{o}^{\star})
(Lemma
3.6
).
Theorem
1.1
is a direct corollary of this conclusion and the rounding guarantee (Lemma
3.5
).
We establish this result based on a novel observation of
k
-submodular extension, called
approximate linearity
(Lemma
3.3
), which captures certain Lipschitzness of
F
and allows us to estimate the increment
F(\mathbf{x}(t+\delta))-F(\mathbf{x}(t))
for sufficiently small values of
\delta
.

When
f
is non-monotone, we require an additional property of
F
, called
pairwise monotonicity
(Lemma
3.3
), to reduce the problem to the monotone case. Utilizing pairwise monotonicity, we are able to obtain an approximation
F(\mathbf{x}(1))\gtrsim\frac{1}{3}F(\mathbf{o}^{\star})
(Lemma
4.2
).
Similarly, Theorem
1.2
is a direct corollary of this approximation and the rounding guarantee (Lemma
3.5
).

Overall, we discover novel properties of our
k
-submodular multilinear extension, including approximate linearity and pairwise monotonicity, which are useful in the analysis of auxiliary points.

Comparison with existing combinatorial approaches.

We demonstrate that our approach using continuous optimization methods yields improved approximations for knapsack constraints compared to prior combinatorial methods such as those presented in
[
15
,
34
]
.
We offer intuitive explanations for this improvement and observe that a similar conclusion holds for submodular maximization with
O(1)
knapsack constraints: to the best of our knowledge, no combinatorial method achieves an optimal approximation, whereas an optimal approximation algorithm via multilinear extension has been presented by
[
7
]
.
Our findings may suggest that the flexibility of continuous methods in selecting stepsizes and update directions provides an advantage over combinatorial approaches for handling knapsack constraints.

Preliminaries

In this section, we first define the constrained
k
-submodular maximization problem and then present the notion of conjunction constraints.
Let

Throughout this paper, we assume there exists a value oracle

Constrained
k
-submodular maximization.

We first present the following support constraints.

Definition 2.1

(
Support constraints).

Given a convex down-closed polytope
\mathcal{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
,
1
1

1

“Down-closed” represents that for every
x,y\in[0,1]^{n}
satisfying that
x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for all

, we have
x\in\mathcal{P}
if
y\in\mathcal{P}
.
we say an integral solution
\mathbf{s}\in\{0,\ldots,k\}^{n}
is consistent to
\mathcal{P}
, denoted as
\mathbf{s}\sim\mathcal{P}
, if the identity vector of its support set satisfies constraint
\mathcal{P}
, i.e.,
1_{\mbox{supp}(\mathbf{s})}\in\mathcal{P}
.

Such support constraints are widely studied in the literature
[
26
,
28
]
and have various applications, such as multiple topics influence maximization with finite budget
[
27
,
35
]
.

We are ready to define the
k
-submodular maximization problem with support constraints.

Problem 2.2

(
k
-submodular maximization with support constraints).

Given a
k
-submodular function
f:\{0,\ldots,k\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
together with a value oracle
\mathcal{O}_{f}

and a support constraint
\mathcal{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
, the goal is to find a vector
S\subseteq[n]
with
\mathbf{s}\sim\mathcal{P}
that maximizes
f(\mathbf{s})
.

The most commonly used support constraints are matroid constraints and knapsack constraints; defined as follows.

Definition 2.3

(
Matroids and matroid constraints).

A matroid is a pair
\mathcal{M}=([n],\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}})
where
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq 2^{n}
, such that
1)
\forall B\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}},~A\subset B~\Rightarrow~A\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}}
;
2)
\forall A,B\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}},~|A|<|B|~\Rightarrow~\exists x\in B\setminus A
s.t.
A\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}}
.
The matroid constraint is defined as

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}:=\mbox{conv}\{1_{I}:I\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}}\}
, i.e., the convex hull of all identity vectors
1_{I}
.

Problem
2.2
is called
k
-submodular maximization with a matroid constraint when
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Definition 2.4

(
Knapsack constraints).

Given a non-negative matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{l\times n}
(
l,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
) and a budget vector
b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{l}
,
we call

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{n}:A\mathbf{x}\leq b\}
the intersection of
l
knapsack constraints.
Specifically,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}}
is called a knapsack constraint when
l=1
.

Problem
2.2
is called k-submodular maximization with knapsack constraints when
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}}
.
We may also consider the intersection of multiple matroid constraints
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}_{a}}
and knapsack constraints
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}}
, in which

\mathcal{P}=\left(\mathop{\bigcap}\limits_{i\in[l]}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\right)\cap\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{K}}.

Conjunction constraint and its membership oracle.

For ease of defining multilinear extension (Section
3.1
), we present another way of encoding support constraint
\mathcal{P}
, called conjunction constraint.
For preparation, we define the corner of cube in
[0,1]^{nk}
as
\Delta_{k}^{n}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in[0,1]^{nk}:\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}\leq 1,\forall i\in[n]\}
.
Note that
\Delta_{k}^{n}
can be viewed as a (partition) matroid constraint in
[nk]
.

Definition 2.5

(
Conjunction constraints).

Given a support constraint
\mathcal{P}\subseteq[0,1]^{n}
, we define its corresponding
conjunction constraint

\mathcal{P}^{c}\subseteq\Delta_{k}^{n}
as

\mathcal{P}^{c}=\bigg\{\mathbf{x}\in\Delta_{k}^{n}:\mathbf{s}=\bigg(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{1,j},\ldots,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{n,j}\bigg)\in\mathcal{P}\bigg\}.

For every
\mathbf{s}_{i}\in[0,1]
, we divide it into
k
dimensions
\mathbf{x}_{i,1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{i,k}\in[0,1]
satisfying that
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}=\mathbf{s}_{i}
, i.e., all such vectors
(\mathbf{x}_{i,1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{i,k})
form a (scaled) simplex in
\Delta_{k}
.
Then
\mathcal{P}^{c}
can be viewed as a conjunction of these simplexes.
By definition, we have the following claim.

Claim 2.6
.

Given a support constraint
\mathcal{P}
, the conjunction constraint
\mathcal{P}^{c}
is convex and down-closed.

We also define the membership oracle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{c}}
of
\mathcal{P}^{c}
, that is for any query
\mathbf{x}\in\Delta_{k}^{n}
,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{c}}
answers whether
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}^{c}
or not.
The following lemma shows the existence of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{c}}
, which is useful for our algorithms.

Lemma 2.7

(
Existence of membership oracle
[
8
]
).

Given a support constraint
\mathcal{P}
as the intersection of matroid constraints and knapsack constraints,
there exists an efficient membership oracle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{c}}
of the conjunction constraint
\mathcal{P}^{c}
.

3.1
Multilinear extension for
k
-submodular functions

We first propose the multilinear extension of
k
-submodular functions.

Definition 3.2

(
Multilinear extension of
k
-submodular functions).

Given a
k
-submodular function
f\colon\{0,\ldots,k\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we define its multilinear extension
F\colon\Delta_{k}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
to be

F(\mathbf{x})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{s}\in\{0,\ldots,k\}^{n}}f(\mathbf{s})\prod\limits_{i\in[n]:\mathbf{s}_{i}\neq 0}\mathbf{x}_{i,\mathbf{s}_{i}}\prod\limits_{i\in[n]:\mathbf{s}_{i}=0}\Big(1-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}\Big)\,.

(1)

The proposed definition is a natural generalization of the well-known multilinear extension of submodular functions (
k=1
)
[
5
]
.
The domain
\Delta_{k}^{n}
can be regarded as the extension of
[0,1]^{n}
for submodular when
k=1

\widehat{\partial_{i,j_{2}}F(\mathbf{x}(t))\geq 0}
and Eq. (
3
), we have
\partial_{i,j_{2}}F(\mathbf{x}(t))\geq-\frac{\varepsilon}{kn^{2}}
, which implies that
\langle\widehat{\nabla_{i}F(\mathbf{x}(t))},{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}_{i}(t)\rangle\geq-\frac{\varepsilon}{kn^{2}}
.
Combining the fact that
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v}_{i}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{i}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\langle\nabla_{i}F(\mathbf{x}(t)),{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle\leq\langle\nabla_{i}F(\mathbf{x}(t)),{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+\langle\nabla_{i}F(\mathbf{x}(t)),{\mathbf{v}}^{\prime}_{i}(t)\rangle+{\frac{\varepsilon}{kn^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad 2\langle\nabla_{i}F(\mathbf{x}(t)),\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{i}(t)\rangle+{\frac{\varepsilon}{kn^{2}}}.

Combining these two cases and the approximate linearity, we have

F(\mathbf{o}^{\prime}(t))-F(\mathbf{o}(t+\delta))\leq-\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{o}^{\prime}(t)),\widehat{\mathbf{v}}(t)\rangle\delta+n^{2}\delta^{2}M\leq{\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M},

(4)

and

\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t)),{\mathbf{v}}(t)\rangle\leq 2\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t)),\widehat{\mathbf{v}}(t)\rangle+{\frac{\varepsilon}{kn}}.

(5)

By definition
\mathbf{o}(1)=\mathbf{x}(1)
and
\mathbf{o}(0)=\mathbf{o}^{\star}
. We bound the improvement in every step by the following inequalities.

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{o}(t))-F(\mathbf{o}(t+\delta))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{o}(t))-F(\mathbf{o}^{\prime}(t))+F(\mathbf{o}^{\prime}(t))-F(\mathbf{o}(t+\delta))

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{o}(t))-F(\mathbf{o}^{\prime}(t))+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

(by Eq. (
4
))

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{o}^{\prime}(t)),\mathbf{o}^{\star}\rangle\delta+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

(by submodularity and multilinearity)

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t)),\mathbf{o}^{\star}\rangle\delta+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

(by submodularity)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{o}^{\star}\rangle\delta+\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))-\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{o}^{\star}\rangle\delta+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{v}(t)\rangle\delta+\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))-\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{o}^{\star}\rangle\delta+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

(by choice of
\mathbf{v}(t)
)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\langle{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{v}(t)\rangle\delta+\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))-\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{o}^{\star}\rangle\delta+\langle\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))}-{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{v}(t)\rangle\delta

\displaystyle~+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\langle{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{v}(t)\rangle\delta+\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))-\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{o}^{\star}\right\|_{2}\delta

\displaystyle~+\left\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))-\widehat{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{v}(t)\right\|_{2}\delta+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

(by Cauchy–Schwarz inequality)

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\langle{\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))},\mathbf{v}(t)\rangle\delta+\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\left\|\mathbf{o}^{\star}\right\|_{2}\delta+\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\left\|\mathbf{v}(t)\right\|_{2}\delta+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

(by Lemma
3.4
)

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t)),\mathbf{v}(t)\rangle\delta+2\varepsilon\delta+\frac{4\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t)),\widehat{\mathbf{v}}(t)\rangle\delta+2\varepsilon\delta+\frac{5\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+n^{2}\delta^{2}M

(by Eq. (
5
))

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle 2\left(F(\mathbf{x}(t+\delta))-F(\mathbf{x}(t))\right)+2\varepsilon\delta+\frac{5\varepsilon\delta}{kn}+2n^{2}\delta^{2}M.

(by approximate linearity)

with probability at least
1-\frac{\varepsilon\eta}{Mn^{2}+1}
.
To sum the above inequalities over
t=0,\delta,\ldots,1
and apply the union bound on the probability
, we conclude that

F(\mathbf{o}(0))-F(\mathbf{o}(1))\leq 2\left(F(\mathbf{x}(1))-F(\mathbf{x}(0))\right)+5\varepsilon,

with probability at least
1-\eta
.
∎
Finally, the query complexity of Algorithm
2
is identical to that of Algorithm
1
.
We complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
by combining Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
3.5
.

5
Conclusions and future works

We proposed unified Frank-Wolfe-type frameworks that solve
k
-submodular maximization with various settings. Notably, for single matroid constraint and
O(1)
knapsacks constraint, we obtained an optimal
1/2
-approximation for monotone
k
-submodular functions and an optimal
1/3
-approximation for non-monotone
k
-submodular functions. Our frameworks also work for various constraint types including any combinations of matroid constraints and knapsack constraints.

Our frameworks are based on the multilinear extension of
k
-submodular functions. This provides a new way to design
k
-submodular maximization algorithms with flexible step sizes and flexible update directions. Considering that multilinear extension for submodular functions has obtained optimal results in many settings of submodular maximization, our extension and rounding techniques could be of independent interest for other
k
-submodular problems.

Many interesting directions are for further investigations. One question is whether
k
-submodular maximization algorithms can be derandomized. Such derandomized algorithms could benefit applications for better reproducibility and consistency. Another direction is to investigate optimization problems of pairwise monotone functions for non-monotone
k
-submodular maximization. Such functions are not well understood yet and our algorithm only uses this property in very simple ways.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma

: Existence of an efficient oracle
\mathcal{O}_{F}

Given oracle access to a
k
-submodular function

, the Chernoff bounds
[

]
implies the following theorem which allows us to approximate the value of the multilinear extension

to arbitrary accuracy.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
F
is the multilinear extension of
f
.
Given a point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}^{c}\subseteq\Delta_{k}^{n}
and parameters
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
,
if
\mathbf{s}^{1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}^{t}
are random vectors independently sampled as follows: for each
l\in[t]
, for each item
i\in[n]
,
\mathbf{s}_{i}^{l}=j
for
j\in[k]
with probability
\mathbf{x}_{i,j}
and otherwise,
\mathbf{s}_{i}^{l}=0
, which occurs independently across all items; then for any
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
, we have

\left|\dfrac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(\mathbf{s}^{i})-F(\mathbf{x})\right|\leq\varepsilon_{0}|\max\limits_{\mathbf{s}\in\Delta_{k}^{n}}f(\mathbf{s})|

with probability at least
1-e^{-t\varepsilon_{0}^{2}/4}
.

For any partial derivative
\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x})
at point
\mathbf{x}\in\Delta_{k}^{n}
and direction
\mathbf{e}_{i,j}
, we construct its stochastic estimate
\widehat{\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x})}
as follows.
Consider points
\mathbf{x}^{0},\mathbf{x}^{1}\in\Delta_{k}^{n}
defined as

\mathbf{x}^{0}_{p,q}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mbox{If }p=i,\\
\mathbf{x}_{p,q}&\mbox{Otherwise}.\end{array}\right.\mbox{ and }~~~~\mathbf{x}^{1}_{p,q}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mbox{If }p=i,q\neq j,\\
1&\mbox{If }p=i,q=j,\\
\mathbf{x}_{p,q}&\mbox{Otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We observe that the Hessian elements of
F
satisfy the condition
\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial x_{i,j_{1}}\partial x_{i,j_{2}}}=0
,
for all
i\in[n]
and
j_{1},j_{2}\in[k]
. This implies that
\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x}^{0})=\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x})
. Leveraging the multilinearity of
F
, we deduce that

F(\mathbf{x}^{1})-F(\mathbf{x}^{0})=\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x}^{0})=\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x}).

We consider two sets of independent samples of random vectors,
\mathbf{s}^{0,1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}^{0,t}
and
\mathbf{s}^{1,1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}^{1,t}
, which satisfy the property delineated in Lemma
A.1
for the points
\mathbf{x}^{0}
and
\mathbf{x}^{1}
, respectively.

Define
\widehat{\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x})}=\dfrac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(\mathbf{s}^{1}_{i})-\dfrac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(\mathbf{s}^{0}_{i})
. Then by Lemma
A.1
the concentration property holds as

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x})}-\partial_{i,j}F(\mathbf{x})\right|

\displaystyle~=~\left|\dfrac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(\mathbf{s}^{1}_{i})-F(\mathbf{x}^{1})-\left(\dfrac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(\mathbf{s}^{0}_{i})-F(\mathbf{x}^{0})\right)\right|

\displaystyle~\leq~\left|\dfrac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(\mathbf{s}^{1}_{i})-F(\mathbf{x}^{1})\right|+\left|\dfrac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(\mathbf{s}^{0}_{i})-F(\mathbf{x}^{0})\right|

\displaystyle~\leq~2\varepsilon_{0}|\max\limits_{\mathbf{s}\in\Delta_{k}^{n}}f(\mathbf{s})|

\displaystyle~\leq~2\varepsilon_{0}nM,

with probability at least
1-2e^{-t\epsilon^{2}/4}
.
By setting
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2kn^{3}M}
and
t=\lceil\frac{16k^{2}n^{6}M^{2}\log\left(\frac{Mn^{2}+1}{\varepsilon\eta}\right)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\rceil
we prove the lemma.

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
3.3
: Properties of the multilinear extension for
k
-submodular functions

For ease of analysis, we introduce an equivalent definition of
k
-submodular functions.
Denote
(k+1)^{V}:=\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})\mid X_{i}\subseteq V,\forall i\in[k],X_{i}\cap X_{j}=\emptyset,\forall i\neq j\}
as the family of
k
disjoint sets.

Definition B.1

(
An equivalent definition of
k
-submodular functions).

A function
f\colon(k+1)^{V}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
k
-submodular
if for any
S=(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})
and
T=(T_{1},\dots,T_{k})
in
(k+1)^{V}
,

f(S)+f(T)\geq f(S\sqcap T)+f(S\sqcup T)\,,

where

S\sqcap T=(S_{1}\cap T_{1},\dots,S_{k}\cap T_{k})

and

S\sqcup T=((S_{1}\cup T_{1})\setminus\bigcup_{i\neq 1}(S_{i}\cup T_{i}),\dots,(S_{k}\cup T_{k})\setminus\bigcup_{i\neq k}(S_{i}\cup T_{i}))\,.

We also define the multilinear extension via this definition.

Definition B.2

(
Induced multilinear extension of Definition
B.1
).

Define
F\colon\Delta_{k}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for a
k
-submodular function
f\colon(k+1)^{V}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as

F(\mathbf{x})=\sum\limits_{S_{1}\uplus\dots\uplus S_{k}=S\subseteq V}f(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})\Big(\prod\limits_{j\in[k]}\prod\limits_{i\in S_{j}}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}\Big)\prod\limits_{i\in V\setminus S}\Big(1-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}\Big)\,,

where
n=|V|
, “
\uplus
” denotes disjoint union.

This definition is identical to the Definition
3.2
by the equivalence of the definitions of
k
-submodularity shown in
[
32
]
.

We denote

\Delta_{e,i}f(X)=f(X_{1},\dots,X_{i-1},X_{i}\cup\{e\},X_{i+1},\dots,X_{k})-f(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})

for
\mathbf{x}\in(k+1)^{V},e\not\in\bigcup^{k}_{j=1}X_{j}
, and
i\in[k]
, which represents the marginal gain when adding
e
to the
i

X
.
Then it is straightforward to notice that
k
-submodularity implies orthant submodularity

\Delta_{e,i}f(X)\geq\Delta_{e,i}f(Y),~\forall X,Y\in(k+1)^{V}\mbox{ with }X\leq Y,e\not\in\bigcup^{k}_{l=1}Y_{l},i\in[k]\,,

and pairwise monotonicity

\Delta_{e,i_{1}}f(X)+\Delta_{e,i_{2}}f(X)\geq 0,~\forall X\in(k+1)^{V}\mbox{ with }e\not\in\bigcup^{k}_{l=1}X_{l},i_{1},i_{2}\in[k],i_{1}\neq i_{2}\,.

(6)

Ward and Živný show that the converse is also true

Functions that are submodular in every orthant and are pairwise monotone must be

Similar results are proved for bisubmodular functions earlier by Ando, Fujishige, and Naitoh

Taking derivative of (

) with respect to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial\mathbf{x}_{i,j}}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in S_{j}}f(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})\prod\limits_{t\in[k]}\prod\limits_{l\in S_{t}\atop l\neq i}\mathbf{x}_{l,t}\prod\limits_{l\in V\setminus S}\Big(1-\sum\limits_{t=1}^{k}\mathbf{x}_{l,t}\Big)

Taking the second-order derivative of (

As

Theorem D.1

(

b

There exist instances of
k

Proof.

Consider the monotone
k

is a monotone submodular function,
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

We set

to be sufficiently small such that

This ensures that the optimal solution

satisfies that
\mathbf{o}_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for all

. Otherwise, we can improve the function value by changing the value

from

In other words, maximizing the
k
-submodular function

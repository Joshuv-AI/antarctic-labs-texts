Complete set of quasi-conserved quantities for spinning particles around Kerr
Geoffrey Compère, Adrien Druart

Abstract
We revisit the conserved quantities of the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Tulczyjew equations describing the motion of spinning particles on a fixed background. Assuming Ricci-flatness and the existence of a Killing-Yano tensor, we demonstrate that besides the two non-trivial quasi-conserved quantities, i.e. conserved at linear order in the spin, found by Rüdiger, non-trivial quasi-conserved quantities are in one-to-one correspondence with non-trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensors. We prove that no such stationary and axisymmetric mixed-symmetry Killing tensor exists on the Kerr geometry. We discuss the implications for the motion of spinning particles on Kerr spacetime where the quasi-constants of motion are shown not to be in complete involution.

The conservation of the mass and spin magnitude being taken into account, the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Tulczyjew (MPT) equations can be written in terms of an Hamiltonian system possessing four degrees of freedom

. The energy and angular momentum provide two immediate first integrals of motion. Two additional quantities linear in the spin vector were found by Rüdiger

;
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012

The unanswered question of the existence of other independent quasi-conserved quantities for the MPT equations led to an undetermined status of the role of integrability and by opposition, chaos, in the dynamics of spinning particles around Kerr. While chaos has been established to appear at second order in the spin

, numerical simulations suggest that no chaos occurs at linear order in the spin
Kunst:2015tla
;
Ruangsri:2015cvg
;
Zelenka:2019nyp
. From the solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equations at linear order in the spin, one can infer that chaotic motion at linear order is negligible
Witzany:2019nml
. In this paper, we will relate the existence of new quasi-conserved quantities homogeneously linear in the spin to the existence of a new tensorial structure on the background, that we will refer to as a
mixed-symmetry Killing tensor
.
This result will apply to the Kerr background and more generally to Ricci-flat spacetimes admitting a Killing-Yano tensor. We will demonstrate that under the assumption of stationarity and axisymmetry, no such non-trivial structure exists on the Schwarzschild background and no non-trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensor on Kerr can be constructed from deformations of trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensors on Schwarzschild. In addition, we will derive that Liouville integrability does not hold around the Kerr background at linear order in the spin since the quasi-conserved quantities are not in involution.

We subsequently particularize our setup in Section

The significance of this result is examined for spinning test-particles in Kerr spacetime in the final Section

In the remaining of this section, we will derive a convenient expression of the 4-velocity in terms of the impulsion and the spin vector. Let us first introduce some definitions. Given any tensor

\displaystyle{}^{*}\!A_{\mu\nu\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{p}}

\displaystyle A^{*}_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{p}\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}^{\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}A_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{p}\rho\sigma}.

II.6
MPT equations at linear order in the spin

We take a break and look back at our fundamental motivation: extreme mass-ratio inspirals involving a spinning secondary. The most important parameter for describing an EMRI is the ratio between the mass

Let us assume that the EMRI central object is a Kerr black hole. As detailed e.g. in

\displaystyle=0,

which are, respectively, the forced geodesic equation with force

III
Building conserved quantities

The MPT equations take the form of a set of first order partial differential equations for the impulsions
p^{\mu}
and the spin
S^{\mu}
. However, the very goal of anyone wanting to solve the MPT equations is to obtain the position of the spinning test-particle as a function of the proper time
X^{\mu}(\tau)
. With respect to the positions
X^{\mu}
, the MPT equations are a set of
second order
PDEs. As it is always the case when studying such a dynamical system, much information can be obtained if one is able to build
first integrals
of the motion (or invariants or conserved quantities),
i.e.
functions of the dynamical variables
\mathcal{Q}(p^{\alpha},S^{\alpha})
that are constant along the motion:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{Q}}(p^{\alpha},S^{\alpha})=0,\qquad\dot{}\triangleq\derivative{\tau}.

(57)

As always in General Relativity, the existence of first integrals of the motion will be strongly related with the presence of symmetries of the background spacetime (i.e. the existence of Killing vectors or Killing(-Yano) tensors).

In this Section, we will discuss the construction of invariants for the MPT equations that are at most linear in the spin vector. After a review of the conserved quantities already discussed in the literature, we will formulate the problem of finding an invariant (of the non-linear MPT equations) at most linear in the spin as a set of general constraints, following the method introduced by Rüdiger
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0046
;
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012
. The ideas behind this procedure are conceptually simple, but the computations for the MPT equations turn out to be involved. This is the reason why we will open this section by explaining Rüdiger’s method for the case of the geodesic equations.

III.1
The geodesic case

As a warm-up, let us recall how first integrals can be constructed for geodesic motion, namely when the spin-dipole is vanishing. In this case, the linear momentum is tangent to the worldline,
p^{\mu}=\mu v^{\mu}
and the MPT equations reduce to the geodesic ones:

\frac{Dp^{\mu}}{\differential\tau}=0,\qquad\frac{D}{\differential\tau}\triangleq v^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}.

(58)

In most GR textbooks and lectures, the problem is tackled from the perspective “
symmetry implies conservation
”: one first introduces the notion of Killing vector fields (
\nabla_{(\alpha}\xi_{\beta)}=0
) and
then
prove the well-known property stating that, given any geodesic of 4-impulsion
p^{\mu}
and a Killing vector field
\xi^{\mu}
of the background spacetime, the quantity
C_{\xi}\triangleq\xi_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}
is constant along the geodesic. One also shows that this property generalizes in the presence of a Killing tensor, and that the invariant mass
\mu^{2}=-p_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}
is also constant along the geodesic trajectory. Of course, when we assert that a scalar function
C(p^{\alpha})
is constant along the geodesic motion (or is conserved, or is invariant, or…), we have in mind the statement that it remains constant along the proper time evolution, which is equivalent to the statement that its covariant derivative along the geodesic path vanishes, namely

\dot{{C}}(p^{\alpha})=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad p^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}C(p^{\alpha})=0.

(59)

In what follows, and as a prelude for the MPT case, we will tackle the problem in the opposite way (“
conservation requires symmetry
”): given an arbitrary geodesic, is it possible to construct invariants of the motion that are polynomial quantities of the impulsion,
i.e.
that are composed of monomials of the form

C_{\mathbf{K}}^{(n)}\triangleq K_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}p^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots p^{\alpha_{n}}

(60)

where, at this point,
\mathbf{K}
is an arbitrary, by definition totally symmetric tensor of rank
n
? We will show that requiring the conservation of
C_{\mathbf{K}}^{(n)}
will require either
\mathbf{K}
to be a Killing vector/tensor or either that
C_{\mathbf{K}}^{(2)}
is the invariant mass.

In order to work out the most general constraint on
\mathbf{K}
, we plug the definition of
C_{\mathbf{K}}
(
60
) into the conservation equation (
59
). Using the geodesic equation (
58
) and relabelling the indices, one gets

p^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}K_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}p^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots p^{\alpha_{n}}=0.

(61)

The crucial point is that the dynamical variables
p^{\alpha}
are
independent
among themselves. The above relation must hold for any values of the independent
p^{\alpha}
, yielding the general constraint

\nabla_{(\mu}K_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n})}=0.

(62)

The only possible cases for solving this constraint are the following:

•

for
n=1
,
K_{\mu}
must be a Killing vector,
\nabla_{(\mu}K_{\nu)}=0
;

•

for
n=2
, either
K_{\mu\nu}
must be a rank-2 Killing tensor (
\nabla_{(\mu}K_{\nu\rho)}=0
), either one takes
K_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}
which leads to the conservation of the invariant mass,
C_{\mathbf{g}}^{(2)}=-\mu^{2}
;

•

for any
n\geq 3
,
\mathbf{K}
must be a rank-n Killing tensor.

Before turning to the spinning particle case, let us make a couple of remarks:

1.

As stated above, the viewpoint adopted here is reversed with respect to the ‘traditional’ one: we have proven that the existence of conserverd quantities along geodesic trajectories that are
polynomial
in the impulsions require the existence of symmetries of the background spacetime (except for the invariant mass
\mu
which is always conserved).

2.

Any linear combination of the invariants defined above remains of course invariant. Nevertheless, the conservation can be checked separately at each order in
\mathbf{p}
because the application of the conservation condition (
59
) doesn’t change the order in
\mathbf{p}
of the terms contained in the resulting expression.

3.

The invariant related to a Killing tensor is relevant only if the latter is irreducible,
i.e.
if it cannot be written as the product of Killing vectors. Otherwise, the invariant at order
n
in
\mathbf{p}
is just a product of invariants of lower order.

III.2
The spinning case

We will now turn on the spin, and discuss the invariants that can be build for MPT equations.

III.2.1
State-of-the-art

Several conserved quantities for the MPT equations have been discussed in the literature, most of the time in the context of the background spacetime being given by the Kerr metric
Saijo:1998mn
:

•

When the Tulczyjew SSCs are enforced, the invariant mass

\displaystyle\mu^{2}=-p_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}

(63)

and the spin parameter

\displaystyle S=\sqrt{S_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}}

(64)

are constant of the motion, as asserted by Eqs. (
19
) and (
20
).

•

If there exists a Killing vector field
\xi_{\alpha}
of the background spacetime, one can upgrade the geodesic case construction and show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\xi}\triangleq\xi_{\mu}p^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}S^{\mu\nu}=C_{\xi}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}S^{\mu\nu}

(65)

is an invariant of the motion
Semerak:1999qc
. It naturally arises from generalized Killing equations
Harte:2008xq
.

These are the only “obvious” invariants. To go further, one should apply the general procedure described above: write down the most general expression for the invariant we are looking for and then work out the constraints implied by its conservation along the motion.

In the presence of both
p^{\mu}
and
S^{\mu}
, one can introduce a grading such that
[p^{\mu}]=[S^{\mu}]=[\mu]=[S^{\mu\nu}]=1
and
[g_{\mu\nu}]=0
and consider invariants of order
n
along such grading (
n=1
linear,
n=2
quadratic, etc).
Such a general scheme has been undertaken by R. Rüdiger in the early 80’s, both for linear invariants
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0046
and for quadratic ones
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012
:

•

Restricting to invariants linear in
p^{\mu}
,
S^{\mu}
, Rüdiger found that, if there exists a Killing-Yano (KY) tensor
Y_{\alpha\beta}
of the background spacetime, then the quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{Y}}\triangleq S_{\alpha\beta}\,^{*}Y^{\alpha\beta}

(66)

is a
quasi-invariant
of motion (i.e. conserved at linear order in the spin),

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{Y}}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{2}).

(67)

It would be an invariant of the motion at all orders in the spin provided that
\mathbf{Y}
satisfy a set of additional conditions
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0046
which have been shown by Santos and Batista
Batista:2020cto
to be equivalent to requiring that
\mathbf{Y}
is a covariantly constant Killing-Yano tensor on the background spacetime, which rules out the Kerr background. However, no systematic analysis has been performed yet on deforming the invariant
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{Y}}
with quadratic corrections in order to attempt to construct quasi-invariants at quadratic or higher order while not further restricting the background.

In this work, we will simply consider the quasi-invariant
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbf{Y}}
as one non-trivial quasi-conserved quantity relevant for the description of EMRIs.

•

Similarly, restricting to quadratic order, Rüdiger showed that the quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{R}=-L_{\alpha}L^{\alpha}-2\mu S^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{Z}-2\mu^{-1}L_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}p^{\beta}

(68)

where
L_{\alpha}\triangleq Y_{\alpha\lambda}p^{\lambda}
,
\mathcal{Z}\triangleq\frac{1}{4}Y^{*}_{\alpha\beta}Y^{\alpha\beta}
and
\xi^{\alpha}\triangleq-\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\lambda}Y^{*\lambda\alpha}
is also a quasi-invariant,
i.e.

\dot{\mathcal{Q}}_{R}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{2})
, if
\mathbf{Y}
is a Killing-Yano tensor.

Both non-trivial quasi-invariants appeared in the independent Hamilton-Jacobi method treatment of the equations of motion by Witzany
witzany2019spinperturbed
, which illustrates the relevance of these quasi-invariants for the description of motion.

There is however not yet a proof that Rüdiger’s quadratic invariant
\mathcal{Q}_{R}
is the unique solution to the corresponding set of constraints in the presence of a Killing-Yano tensor. In what follows, we aim to (i) work out and simplify as much as possible Rüdiger’s constraint equations; (ii) characterize the algebraic properties to be solved for obtaining the general solution and (iii) discuss the existence of additional quasi-invariants for the linearized MPT equations in a Kerr background.

III.2.2
General set of constraints for a quadratic invariant

Our principal motivation being the study of the
linearized
MPT equations, let us consider a quadratic invariant that is at most linear in
\mathbf{S}
:

\mathcal{Q}\triangleq K_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}+L_{\mu\nu\rho}S^{\mu\nu}p^{\rho}.

(69)

The tensors
\mathbf{K}
and
\mathbf{L}
, which are by definition independent of
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{S}
, satisfy the algebraic symmetries
K_{\mu\nu}=K_{(\mu\nu)}
and
L_{\mu\nu\rho}=L_{[\mu\nu]\rho}
.

Using the MPT equations (
4
)-(
5
) and the expression (
15
) for the 4-velocity, the conservation equation can be written

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle=\Xi\quantity(p^\lambda+\frac{1}{\mu}\sRs\tud{\lambda}{\kappa\theta\sigma}S^\kappa S^\theta\hat p^\sigma)

\displaystyle\quad\times\quantity[\qty(\nabla_\lambda K_{\mu\nu}-2L_{\lambda\mu\nu})p^\mu p^\nu+ \qty(\nabla_\lambda L_{\alpha\beta\mu}-K_{\mu\rho}R\tud{\rho}{\lambda\alpha\beta})S^{\alpha\beta}p^\mu-\frac{1}{2}L_{\alpha\beta\rho}R\tud{\rho}{\lambda\gamma\delta}S^{\alpha\beta}S^{\gamma\delta}]\overset{!}{=}0,

(70)

with
\dot{\mathcal{Q}}\triangleq\derivative{\tau}Q
and where the coefficient
\Xi\triangleq\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{2}}{\mu^{2}\quantity(1-d/2)}
is non-vanishing. Let us introduce the tensors

\displaystyle U_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle\triangleq\nabla_{\gamma}K_{\alpha\beta}-2L_{\gamma(\alpha\beta)},

(71)

\displaystyle V_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle\triangleq\nabla_{\delta}L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-K_{\lambda\gamma}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}+\frac{2}{3}K_{\lambda\rho}{R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta[\alpha}}^{\rho}g_{\beta]\gamma},

(72)

\displaystyle W_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\triangleq-\frac{1}{2}L_{\alpha\beta\lambda}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\gamma\delta\varepsilon}.

(73)

The tensors
\mathbf{U}
,
\mathbf{V}
and
\mathbf{W}
obey the algebraic symmetries
U_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=U_{(\alpha\beta)\gamma}
,
V_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=V_{[\alpha\beta]\gamma\delta}
,
W_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\varepsilon}=W_{[\alpha\beta]\gamma\delta\varepsilon}
. Notice that the orthogonality conditions
S^{\alpha\beta}p_{\beta}=0
imply

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}K_{\lambda\rho}{R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta[\alpha}}^{\rho}g_{\beta]\gamma}S^{\alpha\beta}p^{\gamma}=0.

(74)

The conservation equation reads as

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{Q}}={\Xi}\quantity(p^\lambda+\frac{1}{\mu}\sRs\tud{\lambda}{\kappa\theta\sigma}S^\kappa S^\theta\hat p^\sigma)\quantity[U_{\mu\nu\lambda}p^\mu p^\nu+V_{\alpha\beta\mu\lambda}S^{\alpha\beta}p^\mu+W_{\alpha\beta\lambda\gamma\delta}S^{\alpha\beta}S^{\gamma\delta}]\overset{!}{=}0.

(75)

We will go through a number of steps in order to express this condition in terms of the independent variables
s_{\alpha}
and
\hat{p}^{\mu}
.
First, let us expand all terms and express the spin-related quantities in terms of the independent variables
s^{\alpha}
. For this purpose, we will make use of the identities

\displaystyle p^{[\alpha}S^{\beta]}

\displaystyle=p^{[\alpha}s^{\beta]},\qquad S^{\alpha\beta}=2S^{[\alpha}\hat{p}^{\beta]*}=2s^{[\alpha}\hat{p}^{\beta]*},\qquad S^{\alpha}=\Pi^{\alpha}_{\beta}s^{\beta}.

(76)

The conservation equation becomes

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle={\frac{\Xi}{\mu}}\Bigg[\mu^{4}U_{\mu\nu\rho}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}+2\mu^{3}{{}^{*}\!V}_{\alpha\mu\nu\rho}s^{\alpha}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}+\mu^{2}\quantity(4\sWs_{\alpha\mu\nu\beta\rho}+\sRs\tud{\lambda}{\kappa\alpha\rho}U_{\mu\nu\lambda}\Pi^\kappa_\beta)s^{\alpha}s^{\beta}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\quad+2\mu{{}^{*}\!R^{*}}^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\kappa\alpha\rho}{{}^{*}\!V}_{\gamma\mu\nu\lambda}\Pi^{\kappa}_{\beta}s^{\alpha}s^{\beta}s^{\gamma}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}+4{{}^{*}\!R^{*}}^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\kappa\alpha\rho}{{}^{*}\!W^{*}}_{\delta\mu\lambda\gamma\nu}\Pi^{\kappa}_{\beta}s^{\alpha}s^{\beta}s^{\gamma}s^{\delta}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}\Bigg]\overset{!}{=}0.

(77)

Second, we will remove the projectors. One has the identity

\displaystyle{{}^{*}\!R^{*}}^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\kappa\alpha\rho}\Pi_{\beta}^{\kappa}s^{\alpha}s^{\beta}=-I^{\lambda\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\lambda\alpha\beta}\rho\sigma\kappa}s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}\hat{p}^{\sigma}\hat{p}^{\kappa}

(78)

where we have defined

\displaystyle I^{\lambda\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\lambda\alpha\beta}\rho\sigma\kappa}\triangleq{{{}^{*}\!R^{*}}^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\kappa\rho}}^{\alpha}\delta_{\sigma}^{\beta}+{{}^{*}\!R^{*}}^{\lambda\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\lambda\alpha\beta}\rho}g_{\sigma\kappa}.

(79)

The proof is easily carried out, using the fact that
\hat{p}_{\mu}\hat{p}^{\mu}=-1
:

\displaystyle{{}^{*}\!R^{*}}^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\kappa\alpha\rho}\Pi_{\beta}^{\kappa}s^{\alpha}s^{\beta}

\displaystyle={{}^{*}\!R^{*}}^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\kappa\alpha\rho}\quantity(\delta^\kappa_\beta+\hat p^\kappa\hat p_\beta)s^{\alpha}s^{\beta}

(80)

\displaystyle=\quantity[\qty(-\hat p^\sigma\hat p^\kappa g_{\sigma\kappa}) \sRs\tud{\lambda}{\beta\alpha\rho}+\sRs\tud{\lambda}{\kappa\alpha\rho}\hat p^\kappa\delta_\beta^\sigma\hat p_\sigma]s^{\alpha}s^{\beta}

(81)

\displaystyle=-\quantity(\sRs{\tud{\lambda}{\kappa\rho}}^\alpha\delta^\beta_\sigma+\sRs\tud{\lambda\alpha\beta}{\rho}g_{\sigma\kappa})s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}\hat{p}^{\sigma}\hat{p}^{\kappa}.

(82)

Using this identity, the conservation equation finally reads as

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{Q}}

\displaystyle={\frac{\Xi}{\mu}}\Bigg[\mu^{4}U_{\mu\nu\rho}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}+2\mu^{3}{{}^{*}\!V}^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\mu\nu\rho}s_{\alpha}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}-\mu^{2}\quantity(I\tud{\lambda\alpha\beta}{\rho\sigma\kappa}U_{\mu\nu\lambda}+4\sWs\tudud{\alpha}{\mu\nu}{\beta}{\rho}\, g_{\sigma\kappa})s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}\hat{p}^{\sigma}\hat{p}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad-2\mu I^{\lambda\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\lambda\alpha\beta}\rho\sigma\kappa}{{}^{*}\!V}^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\mu\nu\lambda}s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}s_{\gamma}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}\hat{p}^{\sigma}\hat{p}^{\kappa}-4I^{\lambda\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\lambda\alpha\beta}\rho\sigma\kappa}{{}^{*}\!W^{*}}^{\gamma\phantom{\mu\lambda}\delta}_{\phantom{\gamma}\mu\lambda\phantom{\delta}\nu}\,s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}s_{\gamma}s_{\delta}\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu}\hat{p}^{\rho}\hat{p}^{\sigma}\hat{p}^{\kappa}\Bigg]\overset{!}{=}0.

(83)

In principle, finding a quantity which is
exactly
conserved along the motion generated by the MPT equations requires condition (
83
) to be satisfied
exactly.
Because the variables
\hat{p}^{\mu}
and
s_{\alpha}
are independent, this requirement is equivalent to the following set of five constraints, each of them arising at a different order in the spin parameter:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{0}):

\displaystyle~U_{(\mu\nu\rho)}=0,

(84)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{1}):

\displaystyle~{{}^{*}\!V}^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}(\mu\nu\rho)}=0,

(85)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{2}):

\displaystyle~I^{\lambda(\alpha\beta)}_{\phantom{\lambda(\alpha\beta)}(\mu\nu\rho}U_{\sigma\kappa)\lambda}+4{{}^{*}\!W^{*}}^{(\alpha\phantom{(\mu\nu}\beta)}_{\phantom{(\alpha}(\mu\nu\phantom{\beta)}\rho}\,g_{\sigma\kappa)}=0,

(86)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{3}):

\displaystyle~I^{\lambda(\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\lambda(\alpha\beta}(\mu\nu\rho}{{}^{*}\!V}^{\gamma)}_{\phantom{\gamma)}\sigma\kappa)\lambda}=0,

(87)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{4}):

\displaystyle~I^{\lambda(\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\lambda(\alpha\beta}(\mu\nu\rho}{{}^{*}\!W^{*}}^{\gamma\phantom{\sigma|\lambda|}\delta)}_{\phantom{\gamma}\sigma|\lambda|\phantom{\delta)}\kappa)}=0.

(88)

However, for physically relevant situations (
e.g.
when the background spacetime is Kerr or Schwarschild), it will not be possible to fulfill all the constraints (
84
) to (
88
). Nevertheless, we will be able to work out an explicit solution to the two first constraints (
84
) and (
85
) for any Ricci-flat spacetime admitting a hidden symmetry encoded under the form of a rank-two Killing-Yano tensor. In this case, the conservation equation (
83
) will
not
be satisfied exactly, but takes the form

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{Q}}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{2}),

(89)

i.e.

\mathcal{Q}
is a quasi-invariant in the sense defined above.

Notice that the
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{0})
constraint (
84
) simply reduces to

\nabla_{(\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma)}=0,

(90)

i.e.

\mathbf{K}
must be a Killing tensor of the background spacetime.

The
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{1})
constraint (
85
) is more difficult to work out. In Section
IV
, we will proceed to a clever rewriting of this constraint, which will then be particularized to spacetimes admitting a Killing-Yano tensor in Section
V
. Section
VI
will aim to solve it generally. Finally, all these results will be particularized to a Kerr background in Section
VII
.

IV
Conservation equation at linear order in the spin

We will now proceed to the aforementioned rewriting of the constraint (
85
) by introducing a new set of variables. The three first parts of this section are devoted to the derivation of preliminary results, that will be crucial for working out the main result.

IV.1
Dual form of
\mathbf{V}

We want to compute the dual form of the tensor

\displaystyle V_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle\triangleq\nabla_{\delta}L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-K_{\lambda\gamma}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}+\frac{2}{3}K_{\lambda\rho}{R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta[\alpha}}^{\rho}g_{\beta]\gamma}

(91)

with respect to its two first indices. One has

\displaystyle{{}^{*}\!V}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\nabla_{\delta}{{}^{*}\!L}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-K_{\lambda\gamma}{R^{*}}^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}+\frac{2}{3}K_{\lambda\rho}R^{\lambda\phantom{\delta[\alpha}\rho}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta[\alpha}g_{\beta]*\gamma}.

(92)

The last term of this equality can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}K_{\lambda\rho}R^{\lambda\phantom{\delta[\alpha}\rho}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta[\alpha}g_{\beta]*\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}K^{\lambda\rho}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\mu\nu}R_{\lambda\delta\mu\rho}g_{\nu\gamma}=\frac{1}{3}K^{\lambda\rho}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\phantom{\mu}\gamma}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\mu}R_{\lambda\delta\mu\rho}

(93)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}K^{\lambda[\mu}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\phantom{\nu]}\gamma}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\nu]}R^{**}_{\lambda\delta\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{3}K^{\lambda[\mu}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\phantom{\nu]*}\gamma}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\nu]*}R^{*}_{\lambda\delta\mu\nu}

(94)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon^{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}\varepsilon_{\sigma\alpha\beta\gamma}K_{\lambda\rho}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\mu\nu}

(95)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\quantity(K_{\lambda\gamma}R\tud{*\lambda}{\delta\alpha\beta}+K_{\lambda\alpha}R\tud{*\lambda}{\delta\beta\gamma}+K_{\lambda\beta}R\tud{*\lambda}{\delta\gamma\alpha})

(96)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}K_{\gamma\lambda}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}+\frac{2}{3}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\gamma[\alpha}K_{\beta]\lambda}.

(97)

This finally yields

\displaystyle\boxed{{{}^{*}\!V}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\nabla_{\delta}{{}^{*}\!L}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-\frac{2}{3}K_{\lambda\gamma}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}+\frac{2}{3}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\gamma[\alpha}K_{\beta]\lambda}.}

(98)

IV.2
Rüdiger variables

Following Rüdiger
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012
, let us introduce

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\triangleq L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-\frac{1}{3}\quantity(\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+g_{\gamma[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]}K),

(99)

where we have made use of the notations

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\triangleq 2\nabla_{[\alpha}K_{\beta]\gamma},\qquad K\triangleq K^{\alpha}_{\;\;\alpha}.

(100)

The irreducible parts
X_{\alpha}
and
X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
of
\tilde{X}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
are defined through the relation

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\triangleq X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}X^{\delta},\qquad\text{ with }X_{[\alpha\beta\gamma]}\overset{!}{=}0.

(101)

They provide an equivalent description, since Eq. (
101
) can be inverted as

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-\tilde{X}_{[\alpha\beta\gamma]},

(102)

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\tilde{X}_{\beta\gamma\delta}.

(103)

Finally, a simple computation shows that the dual of
\mathbf{\tilde{X}}
is given by

\displaystyle{}^{*}\tilde{X}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}={}^{*}X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-2g_{\gamma[\alpha}X_{\beta]}.

(104)

IV.3
The structural equation

This third preliminary part will be devoted to the proof of the
structural equation

doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012

\displaystyle\boxed{\nabla_{\delta}\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=2\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}-R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\gamma[\alpha}K_{\beta]\lambda})+\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}

(105)

with

\displaystyle\boxed{\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\quantity[K_{\beta\gamma;(\alpha\delta)}+K_{\alpha\delta;(\beta\gamma)}-K_{\alpha\gamma;(\beta\delta)}-K_{\beta\delta;(\alpha\gamma)}-3\qty(K_{\lambda[\alpha}R\tud{\lambda}{\beta]\gamma\delta}+K_{\lambda[\gamma}R\tud{\lambda}{\delta]\alpha\beta})].}

(106)

\boldsymbol{\mu}
possesses the same algebraic symmetries than the Riemann tensor.
We remind the reader that
\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\triangleq 2\nabla_{[\alpha}K_{\beta]\gamma}
. We will use indifferently the notations
\nabla_{\alpha}\mathbf{T}
or
\mathbf{T}_{;\alpha}
for the covariant derivative of the tensor
\mathbf{T}
.
The proof goes as a lengthy rewriting of the original expression:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta}\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}

(107)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{(\delta}\nabla_{\alpha)}K_{\beta\gamma}+\nabla_{[\delta}\nabla_{\alpha]}K_{\beta\gamma}-\nabla_{(\delta}\nabla_{\beta)}K_{\alpha\gamma}-\nabla_{[\delta}\nabla_{\beta]}K_{\alpha\gamma}

(108)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{(\alpha}\nabla_{\delta)}K_{\beta\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{(\beta}\nabla_{\delta)}K_{\alpha\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}\quantity(\nabla_{(\alpha}\nabla_{\delta)}K_{\beta\gamma}-\nabla_{(\beta}\nabla_{\delta)}K_{\alpha\gamma})+\frac{1}{2}\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}.

(109)

We proceed to the following rewriting of twice the quantity in brackets contained in the above expression:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\delta}K_{\beta\gamma}+\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\delta}K_{\alpha\gamma}-\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}

(110)

\displaystyle=2\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\delta}K_{\beta\gamma}-2\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\delta}K_{\alpha\gamma}+\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}

(111)

\displaystyle=2\quantity(\nabla_\beta\nabla_\alpha K_{\gamma\delta}+\nabla_\beta\nabla_\gamma K_{\alpha\delta}-\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta K_{\gamma\delta}-\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\gamma K_{\beta\delta})+\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}

(112)

\displaystyle=2\quantity(\nabla_{(\beta}\nabla_{\gamma)}K_{\alpha\delta}-\nabla_{(\alpha}\nabla_{\gamma)}K_{\beta\delta})+\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}

\displaystyle\quad-2\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\gamma\delta}-\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\gamma}K_{\beta\delta}+\commutator{\nabla_\beta}{\nabla_\gamma}K_{\alpha\delta}.

(113)

This yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta}\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\quantity(K_{\beta\gamma;(\alpha\delta)}+K_{\alpha\delta;(\beta\gamma)}-K_{\alpha\gamma;(\beta\delta)}-K_{\beta\delta;(\alpha\gamma)})

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\frac{3}{4}\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-\frac{3}{4}\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\gamma\delta}-\frac{1}{4}\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\gamma}K_{\beta\delta}+\frac{1}{4}\commutator{\nabla_\beta}{\nabla_\gamma}K_{\alpha\delta}}_{\triangleq\heartsuit}.

(114)

The quantity
\heartsuit
can be rearranged in the following way:

\displaystyle 4\heartsuit

\displaystyle=3\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\alpha}K_{\beta\gamma}-3\commutator{\nabla_\delta}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\alpha\gamma}-2\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\beta}K_{\gamma\delta}-\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\gamma}K_{\beta\delta}+\commutator{\nabla_\beta}{\nabla_\gamma}K_{\alpha\delta}

(115)

\displaystyle=\quantity(3 R\tud{\lambda}{\gamma\delta\beta}-R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\beta\gamma})K_{\alpha\lambda}+\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\alpha\gamma}-3R\tud{\lambda}{\gamma\delta\alpha})K_{\beta\lambda}

\displaystyle\quad+\quantity(3R\tud{\lambda}{\alpha\delta\beta}-3R\tud{\lambda}{\beta\delta\alpha}+2R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\alpha\beta})K_{\gamma\lambda}+\quantity(2R\tud{\lambda}{\gamma\alpha\beta}+R\tud{\lambda}{\beta\alpha\gamma}-R\tud{\lambda}{\alpha\beta\gamma})K_{\delta\lambda}

(116)

\displaystyle=5R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+4\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\alpha\gamma}K_{\beta\lambda}-R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\beta\gamma}K_{\alpha\lambda})+3\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\alpha\gamma\delta}K_{\beta\lambda}-R\tud{\lambda}{\beta\gamma\delta}K_{\alpha\lambda}+R\tud{\lambda}{\gamma\alpha\beta}K_{\delta\lambda})

(117)

\displaystyle=5R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+8R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta[\alpha|\gamma}K_{|\beta]\lambda}-6K_{\lambda[\alpha}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\beta]\gamma\delta}+3R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\gamma\alpha\beta}K_{\delta\lambda}\underbrace{-3R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+3R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}}_{=0}

(118)

\displaystyle=8R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}-8R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\gamma[\alpha}K_{\beta]\lambda}-6K_{\lambda[\alpha}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\beta]\gamma\delta}-6K_{\lambda[\gamma}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta]\alpha\beta}.

(119)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\heartsuit=2\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}- R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\gamma[\alpha}K_{\beta]\lambda})-\frac{3}{2}\quantity(K_{\lambda[\alpha}R\tud{\lambda}{\beta]\gamma\delta}+ K_{\lambda[\gamma}R\tud{\lambda}{\delta]\alpha\beta}).

(120)

Inserting this result into Eq. (
114
) gives the structural equation (
105
) and consequently concludes the proof.

We will conclude this section by working out the dual form of the structural equation (
105
). One has

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta}{}^{*}\!\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=2R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}-\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\mu\nu}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\gamma\mu}K_{\nu\lambda}+{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

(121)

\displaystyle=2R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\mu\nu}R^{**\lambda}_{\phantom{**\lambda}\delta\gamma\mu}K_{\nu\lambda}+{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.

(122)

It is now easier to compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta}{}^{*}\!\lambda_{\alpha\beta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\gamma}

\displaystyle=2R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\beta\nu}\varepsilon^{\mu\gamma\rho\sigma}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\rho\sigma}K^{\nu}_{\phantom{\nu}\lambda}+{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\phantom{\gamma}\delta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\gamma}

(123)

\displaystyle=2R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K^{\gamma}_{\phantom{\gamma}\lambda}+3\,\delta^{[\gamma}_{\alpha}\delta^{\rho}_{\beta}\delta_{\nu}^{\sigma]}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\rho\sigma}K^{\nu}_{\phantom{\nu}\lambda}+{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\phantom{\gamma}\delta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\gamma},

(124)

which yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta}{}^{*}\!\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=2R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+3\,g_{\gamma[\alpha|}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta|\beta\nu]}K^{\nu}_{\phantom{\nu}\lambda}+{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

(125)

\displaystyle=2R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+\quantity(R\tud{*\lambda}{\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+R\tudu{*\lambda}{\delta\beta}{\rho}K_{\lambda\rho}g_{\alpha\gamma}-R\tudu{*\lambda}{\delta\alpha}{\rho}K_{\lambda\rho}g_{\beta\gamma})+{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.

(126)

Rearranging the different terms leads to the final expression

\displaystyle\boxed{\nabla_{\delta}{}^{*}\!\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=3R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}-2R^{*\lambda\phantom{\delta[\alpha}\rho}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta[\alpha}g_{\beta]\gamma}K_{\lambda\rho}+{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.}

(127)

IV.4
Reduction of the second constraint

We will now gather the results obtained in the three previous subsections to express the constraint (
85
) in terms of the irreducible variables introduced above. Let us remind that Eq. (
85
) reads as

\displaystyle{{}^{*}\!V}^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}(\beta\gamma\delta)}=0.

(128)

Using Eqs. (
99
), (
104
) and (
127
), we can rewrite

\displaystyle{{}^{*}\!V}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\delta}{}^{*}\!\tilde{X}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\delta}\quantity({}^*\!\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+g_{\gamma[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]*}K)-\frac{2}{3}K_{\lambda\gamma}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}+\frac{2}{3}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\gamma[\alpha}K_{\beta]\lambda}

(129)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\delta}\quantity({}^*\!X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-2g_{\gamma[\alpha}X_{\beta]})+\frac{1}{3}\underbrace{\quantity(R\tud{*\lambda}{\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+2R\tud{*\lambda}{\delta\gamma[\alpha}K_{\beta]\lambda})}_{\triangleq\heartsuit}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\delta}\quantity( g_{\gamma[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]*}K)+\frac{1}{3}{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}-\frac{2}{3}R^{*\lambda\phantom{\delta[\alpha}\rho}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta[\alpha}g_{\beta]\gamma}K_{\lambda\rho}.

(130)

On the one hand, we have

\displaystyle\heartsuit

\displaystyle=R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha\beta}K_{\gamma\lambda}+R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\gamma\alpha}K_{\beta\lambda}-R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\gamma\beta}K_{\alpha\lambda}=R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\beta\gamma}K_{\alpha\lambda}+2R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha[\beta}K_{\gamma]\lambda}.

(131)

And on the other hand, we can write

\displaystyle\nabla_{\delta}\quantity( g_{\gamma[\alpha}\nabla_{\beta]*}K)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\mu\nu}g_{\gamma\mu}\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\nu}K=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta\gamma}\mu}\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\mu}K.

(132)

Gathering these expressions leads to

\displaystyle{{}^{*}\!V}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\delta}{}^{*}\!X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-2g_{\gamma[\alpha|}\nabla_{\delta}X_{|\beta]}-\frac{2}{3}R^{*\lambda\phantom{\delta[\alpha}\rho}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta[\alpha}g_{\beta]\gamma}K_{\lambda\rho}+\frac{1}{3}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\beta\gamma}K_{\alpha\lambda}+\frac{2}{3}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\delta\alpha[\beta}K_{\gamma]\lambda}+\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{\phantom{\alpha\beta\gamma}\mu}\nabla_{\delta}\nabla_{\mu}K+\frac{1}{3}{}^{*}\!\mu_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.

(133)

When symmetrizing the three last indices, the four last terms of this expression vanish. The constraint (
85
) takes the final form

\displaystyle\boxed{{}^{*}\!X_{\alpha(\beta\gamma;\delta)}+X_{\alpha;(\beta}g_{\gamma\delta)}-g_{\alpha(\beta}X_{\gamma;\delta)}+\frac{1}{3}\quantity(g_{\alpha(\beta}R\tudu{*\lambda}{\gamma\delta)}{\rho}-R\tudu{*\lambda}{\alpha(\beta}{\rho}g_{\gamma\delta)})K_{\lambda\rho}=0.}

(134)

Our principal motivation being the motion of spinning particles in Kerr spacetime, we will now focus on spacetimes possessing a Killing-Yano (KY) tensor. It will turn out that the constraint (
134
) can still be dramatically simplified in such a framework.

V
Spacetimes admitting a Killing-Yano tensor

We now particularize our analysis to spacetimes equipped with a Killing-Yano (KY) tensor,
i.e.
a rank-2, antisymmetric tensor
Y_{\mu\nu}=Y_{[\mu\nu]}
obeying the Killing-Yano equation:

\boxed{\nabla_{(\alpha}Y_{\beta)\gamma}=0.}

(135)

In this case, the constraint (
84
) is automatically fulfilled, because

K_{\alpha\beta}\triangleq Y_{\alpha\lambda}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\beta}

(136)

is a Killing tensor.

The aim of this section is twofold. First, we will review some general properties of KY tensors useful for the continuation of our analysis. Even if most of them were previously mentioned in the literature
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0046
;
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0056
, the goal of the present exposition is to provide a self-contained summary of these results and of their derivations. Second, we will work out an involved identity that will become a cornerstone for solving the constraint (
134
). This so-called
central identity
is the generalization of a result mentioned by Rüdiger in
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012
. Rüdiger only quickly sketched the proof of his identity, while we aim here to provide a more pedagogical derivation of this central result.

V.1
Some general properties of KY tensors

Let us review some basic properties of KY tensors, sticking to our conventions and simplifying the notations used in the literature
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0046
;
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0056
.

V.1.1
An equivalent form of the KY equation

The symmetries
\nabla_{(\alpha}Y_{\beta)\gamma}=\nabla_{\alpha}Y_{(\beta\gamma)}=0
ensure the quantity
\nabla_{\alpha}Y_{\beta\gamma}
to be totally antisymmetric in its three indices. Consequently, there exists a vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}
such that

\boxed{\nabla_{\alpha}Y_{\beta\gamma}=\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}.}

(137)

The value of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
can be found by contracting Eq. (
137
) with
\varepsilon^{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}
and making use of the contraction formula for the Levi-Civita tensor (
23
). We obtain

\boxed{\xi^{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\lambda}Y^{*\lambda\alpha}.}

(138)

Notice that Eq. (
137
) with
\boldsymbol{\xi}
given by Eq. (
138
) is totally equivalent to the Killing-Yano equation.

V.1.2
Dual KY equation

Let us derive the equivalent to the Killing-Yano equation for the dual of the KY tensor
\mathbf{Y}^{*}
. One has

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}Y^{*\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\beta\gamma\mu\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}Y_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\beta\gamma}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}

(139)

\displaystyle=-2\delta^{[\beta}_{\alpha}\delta^{\gamma]}_{\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}=-2\delta^{[\beta}_{\alpha}\xi^{\gamma]},

(140)

leading to

\boxed{\nabla_{\alpha}Y^{*}_{\beta\gamma}=-2g_{\alpha[\beta}\xi_{\gamma]}.}

(141)

In turn, this leads to the conformal Killing-Yano equation for the dual tensor
\mathbf{Y}^{*}
.

V.1.3
Integrability conditions for the KY equation

We will work out some necessary conditions for the tensor
\mathbf{Y}
to be a Killing-Yano tensor,
i.e.
relations that must hold for
\mathbf{Y}
to satisfy the KY equation. We will refer to them as
integrability conditions
for the Killing-Yano equation.

We begin by proving some preliminary results:

Lemma 1
.

One has

\displaystyle\boxed{\nabla^{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}=0.}

(142)

Proof.

We proceed by applying the Ricci identity to the expression:

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\lambda}Y^{*\lambda\alpha}=-\frac{1}{6}\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\lambda}Y^{*\lambda\alpha}

(143)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}g^{\lambda\mu}g^{\alpha\nu}\quantity(R\tud{\rho}{\mu\alpha\lambda}Y^*_{\rho\nu}+R\tud{\rho}{\nu\alpha\lambda}Y^*_{\mu\rho})

(144)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}R^{\alpha\beta}Y^{*}_{\alpha\beta}=0.

(145)

∎

Lemma 2
.

For any antisymmetric tensor
A_{\alpha\beta}=A_{[\alpha\beta]}
, one has

\displaystyle\boxed{R^{\alpha\phantom{\mu\nu}\beta}_{\phantom{\alpha}\mu\nu}A^{\mu\nu}=R^{[\alpha\phantom{\mu\nu}\beta]}_{\phantom{[\alpha}\mu\nu}A^{\mu\nu}.}

(146)

Proof.

The proof is straightforward:

\displaystyle R^{\beta\phantom{\mu\nu}\alpha}_{\phantom{\beta}\mu\nu}A^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=R_{\nu\phantom{\alpha\beta}\mu}^{\phantom{\nu}\alpha\beta}A^{\mu\nu}=R^{\alpha\phantom{\mu\nu}\beta}_{\phantom{\alpha}\mu\nu}A^{\nu\mu}

(147)

\displaystyle=-R^{\alpha\phantom{\mu\nu}\beta}_{\phantom{\alpha}\mu\nu}A^{\mu\nu}.

(148)

∎

Lemma 3
.

For any antisymmetric tensor
A_{\alpha\beta}=A_{[\alpha\beta]}
, we have

\displaystyle\boxed{A_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\phantom{\alpha\beta}\nu}_{\phantom{\mu}\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{2}A^{\mu\nu}R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}.}

(149)

Proof.

The proof is again pretty simple:

\displaystyle A_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\phantom{\alpha\beta}\nu}_{\phantom{\mu}\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=A^{\mu\nu}\quantity(R_{\alpha\beta\nu\mu}+R_{\alpha\nu\mu\beta})

(150)

\displaystyle=-A^{\mu\nu}R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}+A^{\mu\nu}R_{\nu\alpha\beta\mu}.

(151)

The conclusion is reached using Lemma
2
.
∎

We can now work out the first integrability condition. One has

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\lambda}Y^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\beta}

(152)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\nabla^{\lambda}\nabla_{\alpha}Y^{*}_{\lambda\beta}-\frac{1}{3}\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\lambda}Y^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\beta}

(153)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\nabla^{\lambda}\quantity(g_{\alpha[\lambda}\xi_{\beta]})-\frac{1}{3}g^{\lambda\rho}\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\lambda}Y^{*}_{\rho\beta}

(154)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\nabla_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-\frac{1}{3}g_{\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{3}\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\alpha}Y^*_{\lambda\beta}+R\tudu{\lambda}{\beta\alpha}{\rho}Y^*_{\rho\lambda}).

(155)

This gives rise to the Killing-Yano equation
first integrability condition
:

\displaystyle\boxed{\nabla_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\alpha}Y^*_{\lambda\beta}+R\tudu{\lambda}{\alpha\beta}{\rho}Y^*_{\lambda\rho})}

(156)

or, equivalently, using Lemma
3
:

\displaystyle\boxed{\nabla_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\alpha}Y^{*}_{\lambda\beta}-\frac{1}{4}R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}Y^{*\mu\nu}.}

(157)

Symmetrizing this equation gives the reduced form

\displaystyle\boxed{\nabla_{(\alpha}\xi_{\beta)}=\frac{1}{2}Y^{*}_{\lambda(\alpha}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\beta)}.}

(158)

In particular, for Ricci-flat spacetimes,
\xi^{\mu}
is a Killing vector.

A second integrability condition can be written as follows.
Taking the derivative of the defining equation
\nabla_{(\alpha}Y_{\beta)\gamma}=0
yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}+\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\gamma}Y_{\beta\delta}=0.

(159)

Out of this equation, we can write the three (equivalent) identities

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}+\nabla_{\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}Y_{\beta\delta}+\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\gamma}Y_{\beta\delta}

\displaystyle=0,

(160)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}Y_{\beta\delta}+\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}Y_{\alpha\delta}+\commutator{\nabla_\gamma}{\nabla_\beta}Y_{\alpha\delta}

\displaystyle=0,

(161)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}Y_{\alpha\delta}+\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}+\commutator{\nabla_\beta}{\nabla_\alpha}Y_{\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle=0.

(162)

Summing the first and the third and subtracting the second leads to

\displaystyle 2\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle=\commutator{\nabla_\gamma}{\nabla_\alpha}Y_{\beta\delta}+\commutator{\nabla_\gamma}{\nabla_\beta}Y_{\alpha\delta}+\commutator{\nabla_\alpha}{\nabla_\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}

(163)

\displaystyle=R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\beta\gamma}Y_{\alpha\lambda}+R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\gamma}Y_{\beta\lambda}+R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\beta\alpha}Y_{\gamma\lambda}-\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\beta\gamma\alpha}+R\tud{\lambda}{\alpha\gamma\beta}+R\tud{\lambda}{\gamma\alpha\beta})Y_{\lambda\delta}

(164)

\displaystyle=2R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\alpha\beta\gamma}Y_{\lambda\delta}+R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\beta\gamma}Y_{\alpha\lambda}+R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\alpha\gamma}Y_{\beta\lambda}+R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta\beta\alpha}Y_{\gamma\lambda}.

(165)

This gives rise to the
second integrability condition

\displaystyle\boxed{\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}=R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\alpha\beta\gamma}Y_{\lambda\delta}+\frac{1}{2}\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\beta\gamma}Y_{\alpha\lambda}+R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\alpha\gamma}Y_{\beta\lambda}+R\tud{\lambda}{\delta\beta\alpha}Y_{\gamma\lambda}).}

(166)

A reduced form can be obtained by contracting the equation above with
g^{\gamma\delta}
. We obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=R^{\lambda\phantom{\alpha\beta}\rho}_{\phantom{\lambda}\alpha\beta}Y_{\lambda\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\quantity(R\tud{\lambda}{\beta}Y_{\alpha\lambda}+R\tud{\lambda}{\alpha}Y_{\beta\lambda}+R\tud{\lambda\rho}{\beta\alpha}Y_{\rho\lambda}).

(167)

Using Lemma
3
shows that the first and the fourth terms of the right-hand side of this relation cancel. We obtain the
reduced form of the second integrability condition

\displaystyle\boxed{Y_{\lambda(\alpha}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\beta)}=0.}

(168)

We can also symmetrize the indices
\gamma\delta
in Eq. (
166
) to obtain the
symmetrized second integrability condition

\displaystyle\boxed{R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\alpha\beta(\gamma}Y_{\delta)\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}Y_{\lambda(\gamma}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta)\alpha\beta}+R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}(\gamma\delta)(\alpha}Y_{\beta)\lambda}=0}.

(169)

V.2
The central identity

Our so-called central identity will consists into a clever rewriting of the expression
K_{\lambda(\beta}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\gamma\delta)\alpha}
. Its reduced version first introduced by Rüdiger
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012
is a rewriting of the contracted expression
K_{\lambda\rho}R^{*\lambda\phantom{\alpha\beta}\rho}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\alpha\beta}
.

V.2.1
The KY scalar

Let us define the scalar quantity

\mathcal{Z}\triangleq\frac{1}{4}Y^{*}_{\alpha\beta}Y^{\alpha\beta}.

(170)

Its first covariant derivative takes the form

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}Y^{*}_{\alpha\beta}Y^{\alpha\beta}

(171)

\displaystyle=\xi_{[\alpha}g_{\beta]\mu}Y^{\alpha\beta}

(172)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\mu}.

(173)

Its second covariant derivative can be expressed as (notice that
\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\mathcal{Z}=\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\mathcal{Z}
)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\nu}+\xi^{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\nu\rho}\,\xi^{\rho}

(174)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\nu}=\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\mu},

(175)

where the last equality follows from symmetry of the right-hand side.

The following identity is also useful:

\displaystyle\boxed{Y_{\alpha[\beta}Y_{\gamma]\delta}=-\frac{1}{2}Y_{\beta\gamma}Y_{\alpha\delta}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Z}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.}

(176)

The proof consists into noticing that the combination
Y_{\alpha\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}-Y_{\alpha\gamma}Y_{\beta\delta}+Y_{\beta\gamma}Y_{\alpha\delta}
is totally antisymmetric in all its indices. It must consequently be proportional to the Levi-Civita tensor:

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}-Y_{\alpha\gamma}Y_{\beta\delta}+Y_{\beta\gamma}Y_{\alpha\delta}=\mathcal{A}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta},

(177)

where the constant
\mathcal{A}
remains to be determined. This is achieved by contracting the equation above with
\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
, yielding

\displaystyle 3\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}Y_{\alpha\beta}Y_{\gamma\delta}=-4!\,\mathcal{A}.

(178)

Using the definition of
\mathcal{Z}
leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=-\mathcal{Z},

(179)

which gives the desired result.

V.2.2
Derivation of the central identity

The trick for deriving the central identity is to define the tensor

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\beta\sigma}\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}Y_{\nu\lambda}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\rho},

(180)

to perform two different rewritings of this expression and finally to equate them.
Notice that we recover the tensor used in Rüdiger’s proof
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012
by contracting the last two indices of Eq. (
180
).

First, applying the Ricci identity to Eq. (
180
) and making use of Eq. (
176
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\beta\sigma}\commutator{\nabla^\alpha}{\nabla^\beta}Y_{\nu\lambda}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\rho}=-\quantity(R\tud{*\kappa}{\nu\mu\sigma}K_{\kappa\rho}+R\tud{*\kappa\lambda}{\mu\sigma}Y_{\nu[\kappa}Y_{\lambda]\rho})

(181)

\displaystyle=R^{*\kappa}_{\phantom{*\kappa}\nu\sigma\mu}K_{\kappa\rho}+\frac{1}{2}R^{*\kappa\lambda}_{\phantom{*\kappa\lambda}\mu\sigma}\quantity(Y_{\kappa\lambda}Y_{\nu\rho}+\mathcal Z\epsilon_{\nu\kappa\lambda\rho}).

(182)

Second, using Eqs. (
138
), (
141
) and Lemma
1
, we rewrite
\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\mu\phantom{\nu\rho}\sigma}_{\phantom{\mu}\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\alpha\beta\sigma}\varepsilon_{\nu\lambda\gamma\delta}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}Y^{*\gamma\delta}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\rho}

(183)

\displaystyle=-12\delta^{[\sigma}_{\nu}Y^{\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\rho}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}Y^{*\alpha\beta]}

(184)

\displaystyle=-\delta^{\sigma}_{\nu}\quantity(Y\tud{\mu}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\alpha\beta}+Y\tud{\alpha}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\beta\mu}+Y\tud{\beta}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\mu\alpha})

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\quantity(Y\tud{\sigma}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\alpha\beta}+Y\tud{\alpha}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\beta\sigma}+Y\tud{\beta}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\sigma\alpha})

\displaystyle\quad-\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}\quantity(Y\tud{\sigma}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\mu\beta}+Y\tud{\mu}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\beta\sigma}+Y\tud{\beta}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\sigma\mu})

\displaystyle\quad+\delta^{\beta}_{\nu}\quantity(Y\tud{\sigma}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\mu\alpha}+Y\tud{\mu}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\alpha\sigma}+Y\tud{\alpha}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta Y^{*\sigma\mu})

(185)

\displaystyle=\delta^{\sigma}_{\nu}\quantity(3Y\tud{\alpha}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\xi^\mu+2Y\tud{\beta}{\rho}\delta^{[\mu}_\beta\nabla_\alpha\xi^{\alpha]}){-}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\quantity(3Y\tud{\alpha}{\rho}\nabla_\alpha\xi^\sigma+2Y\tud{\beta}{\rho}\delta_\beta^{[\sigma}\nabla_\alpha\xi^{\alpha]})

\displaystyle=\delta^{\sigma}_{\nu}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\rho}\nabla_{\lambda}\xi^{\mu}-\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\rho}\nabla_{\lambda}\xi^{\sigma}+Y^{\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\rho}\nabla_{\nu}\xi^{\sigma}-Y^{\sigma}_{\phantom{\sigma}\rho}\nabla_{\nu}\xi^{\mu}.

Equating the two expressions of

\displaystyle R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\nu\mu\sigma}K_{\rho\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}R^{*\kappa\lambda}_{\phantom{*\kappa\lambda}\mu\sigma}\quantity(Y_{\kappa\lambda}Y_{\nu\rho}+\mathcal Z\epsilon_{\nu\kappa\lambda\rho})+g_{\mu\nu}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\rho}\nabla_{\lambda}\xi_{\sigma}-g_{\nu\sigma}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\rho}\nabla_{\lambda}\xi_{\mu}+Y_{\sigma\rho}\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}-Y_{\mu\rho}\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\sigma}.

This is the cornerstone equation for deriving both the central identity and its reduced form.

Non-reduced form.

Fully symmetrizing Eq. (

\displaystyle K_{\lambda(\beta}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\gamma\delta)\alpha}=Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}(\beta}g_{\gamma\delta)}\xi_{\alpha;\lambda}-g_{\alpha(\beta}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\gamma}\xi_{\delta);\lambda}+Y_{\alpha(\beta}\xi_{\gamma;\delta)}.

We make use of the reduced integrability condition (

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}Y_{\alpha(\beta|}Y^{*}_{\lambda|\gamma}R^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\delta)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}Y_{\alpha(\beta}Y^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\gamma}G_{\delta)\lambda}+\frac{R}{4}Y_{\alpha(\beta}Y^{*}_{\gamma\delta)}

(192)

where

\displaystyle\boxed{K_{\lambda(\beta}R^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\gamma\delta)\alpha}=Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}(\beta}g_{\gamma\delta)}\xi_{\alpha;\lambda}-g_{\alpha(\beta}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\gamma}\xi_{\delta);\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}Y_{\alpha(\beta}Y^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\gamma}G_{\delta)\lambda}.}

Reduced form.

Rüdiger’s reduced form of the central identity can be derived by contracting Eq. (

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}R^{*\kappa\lambda\phantom{\mu}\rho}_{\phantom{*\kappa\lambda}\mu}\quantity(Y_{\kappa\lambda}Y_{\nu\rho}+\mathcal Z\epsilon_{\nu\kappa\lambda\rho})+2\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{Z}-g_{\mu\nu}\Delta\mathcal{Z}-Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\nu}Y^{*}_{\rho(\lambda}G^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\mu)}.

The first term of the above equation can be simplified by noticing that, on the one hand, making use of Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}R^{*\kappa\lambda\phantom{\mu}\rho}_{\phantom{*\kappa\lambda}\mu}Y_{\kappa\lambda}Y_{\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\nu}\quantity(4R\tud{\beta}{\mu}Y^*_{\beta\lambda}+4R\tud{\beta}{\lambda}Y^*_{\mu\beta}-2R\tud{\alpha\beta}{\mu\lambda}Y^*_{\alpha\beta}-2RY^*_{\mu\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\nu}\quantity[4\qty(R\tudu{\alpha}{\mu\lambda}{\beta}Y^*_{\alpha\beta}+R\tud{\beta}{\mu}Y^*_{\beta\lambda})+4R\tud{\beta}{\lambda}Y^*_{\mu\beta}-2RY^*_{\mu\lambda}]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{int_1}}{=}2Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\lambda}+R^{\beta}_{\phantom{\beta}\lambda}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\nu}Y^{*}_{\mu\beta}-\frac{1}{2}RY^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\nu}Y^{*}_{\mu\lambda}

Putting all pieces together, we obtain the reduced central identity

\displaystyle\boxed{R^{*\lambda\phantom{\nu\mu}\rho}_{\phantom{*\lambda}\nu\mu}K_{\lambda\rho}=4\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{Z}-g_{\mu\nu}\Delta\mathcal{Z}-Y^{*\lambda}_{\phantom{*\lambda}(\rho}G_{\mu)\lambda}Y^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\nu}+G^{\beta}_{\phantom{\beta}\lambda}Y^{\lambda}_{\phantom{\lambda}\nu}Y^{*}_{\mu\beta}+\mathcal{Z}G_{\mu\nu}.}

VI.1
Simplification of the constraint

Plugging Eq. (
208

\displaystyle{}^{*}\,X_{\alpha(\beta\gamma;\delta)}+\nabla_{(\beta|}\quantity[X_\alpha-\frac{4}{3}\nabla_\alpha\mathcal Z]g_{|\gamma\delta)}-g_{\alpha(\beta}\nabla_{\delta}\quantity[X_{\gamma)}-\frac{4}{3}\nabla_{\gamma)}\mathcal Z]=0.

(209)

It is straightforward to see that it admits the following non-trivial solution

\displaystyle\boxed{X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=0,\qquad X_{\alpha}=\frac{4}{3}\nabla_{\alpha}\mathcal{Z}.}

(210)

As we will see later, this solution will lead to Rüdiger’s invariant (
68
). In what follows, we will seek a more general solution to Eq. (

\displaystyle\quantity[{}^* X_{\alpha(\beta\gamma}+Y_\alpha g_{(\beta\gamma}-g_{\alpha(\beta}Y_\gamma]_{;\delta)}=0

(211)

by introducing the shifted variable

\displaystyle Y_{\alpha}\triangleq X_{\alpha}-\frac{4}{3}\nabla_{\alpha}\mathcal{Z}.

(212)

We subsequently rewrite Eq. (
211
) in order to remove the dual operator from the tensor
\mathbf{X}
. Let us define the Hodge dual
\tilde{Y}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
of

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\triangleq\varepsilon_{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}Y^{\mu}\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad Y_{\alpha}\triangleq-\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{\alpha\mu\nu\rho}\tilde{Y}^{\mu\nu\rho}.

(213)

Contracting Eq. (
211

\varepsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu\rho}
and using the usual properties of the Levi-Civita tensor, we get

\displaystyle-3\delta^{[\mu}_{(\beta}X^{\nu\rho]}_{\phantom{\nu\rho]}\gamma;\delta)}+\tilde{Y}^{\mu\nu\rho}_{\phantom{\mu\nu\rho};(\beta}g_{\gamma\delta)}-\varepsilon_{(\beta}^{\phantom{(\beta}\mu\nu\rho}Y_{\gamma;\delta)}=0.

(214)

Now, using the fact that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\beta\mu\nu\rho}Y_{\gamma;\delta}=4\delta_{\gamma}^{[\beta}\tilde{Y}^{\mu\nu\rho]}_{\phantom{\mu\nu\rho]};\delta},

(215)

the last term of the previous equation reads as

\displaystyle-\varepsilon_{(\beta}^{\phantom{(\beta}\mu\nu\rho}Y_{\gamma;\delta)}=-g_{(\beta\gamma}\tilde{Y}^{\mu\nu\rho}_{\phantom{\mu\nu\rho};\delta)}+3\delta^{[\mu}_{(\gamma}\tilde{Y}^{\nu\rho]}_{\phantom{\nu\rho]}\beta;\delta)}.

Putting all pieces together, Eq. (

\displaystyle\delta_{(\beta}^{[\mu}\quantity(X\tud{\nu\rho]}{\gamma}-\tilde Y\tud{\nu\rho]}{\gamma})_{;\delta)}=0.

It follows from Eq. (
99

is antisymmetric on its two first indices,
X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=X_{[\alpha\beta]\gamma}

Let us decompose

\displaystyle X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=A_{\alpha}g_{\beta\gamma}+B_{\beta}g_{\alpha\gamma}+C_{\gamma}g_{\alpha\beta}+D^{\text{tf}}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}.

(218)

Note that the constraint

Moreover, since
X_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=X_{[\alpha\beta]\gamma}

217

\delta_{(\alpha}^{[\mu}\delta_{\beta)}^{\nu]}=0

\displaystyle\delta^{[\mu}_{(\beta}\quantity(D\tud{\text{tf}\,\nu\rho]}{\gamma}-\tilde Y\tud{\nu\rho]}{\gamma})_{;\delta)}=0.

This implies that the 1-forms

All in all, we can, without loss of generality, set

consider that

reduces to its traceless part. The constraint equation can be written in the short form

\displaystyle\boxed{\delta_{(\beta}^{[\mu}W^{\nu\rho]}_{\phantom{\nu\rho]}\gamma;\delta)}=0,}

(220)

where we have defined

Contracting the constraint (

Given a solution to the simplified constraint (

VI.4
Invariants homogeneously linear in
\mathcal{S}

Non-trivial invariants of the form (
236
) can only be generated by finding a solution to the differential constraint (
221
). As we will see in this section, this problem and the form of the generated invariant can be recast in a very simple form. The first step is to express the invariant (
236
) as a function of
{}^{*}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
only. Contracting Eq. (

) with
g^{\beta\gamma}
yields

\displaystyle\boxed{Y_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{3}\,^{*}W_{\alpha\lambda}^{\phantom{\alpha\lambda}\lambda}.}

(240)

Rearranging Eq. (

) and making use of Eq. (

\displaystyle\boxed{{}^{*}D_{\alpha\beta\gamma}^{\text{tf}}=\,^{*}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+\frac{2}{3}g_{\gamma[\alpha}\,^{*}W_{\beta]\lambda}^{\phantom{\beta]\lambda}\lambda}.}

(241)

Plugging these two expressions into Eq. (

\displaystyle\boxed{\mathcal{Q}(W_{\alpha\beta\gamma})=2\mu\,^{*}W_{\alpha(\beta\gamma)}S^{\alpha}\hat{p}^{\beta}\hat{p}^{\gamma}.}

(242)

Now, because the dualization is an invertible operation, the giving of
{}^{*}W_{\alpha(\beta\gamma)}
is equivalent to the giving of the symmetrized part in its two last indices of a rank-3 tensor
N_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
antisymmetric in its two first indices, such that

\displaystyle N_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\equiv\,^{*}W_{\alpha\beta\gamma},

(243)

which obeys

\displaystyle N_{\alpha(\beta\gamma;\delta)}=0.

(244)

Note that one
cannot
impose that
N_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is also symmetric in
\beta\gamma
otherwise it would vanish because one would have
N_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=-N_{\beta\alpha\gamma}=-N_{\beta\gamma\alpha}=N_{\gamma\beta\alpha}=N_{\gamma\alpha\beta}=-N_{\alpha\gamma\beta}=-N_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
.

The tensor
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\triangleq N_{\alpha(\beta\gamma)}
obeys the cyclic identity

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+T_{\beta\gamma\alpha}+T_{\gamma\alpha\beta}=0

(245)

but since
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is not symmetric in its two first indices, it is not totally symmetric and the condition defining the mixed-symmetry tensor (
244
) is
distinct
from the condition defining a Killing tensor
K_{(\alpha\beta\gamma;\delta)}=0
where
K_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is totally symmetric
K_{(\alpha\beta\gamma)}=K_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
. In terms of representation of the permutation group,
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is a
\{2,1\}
Young diagram.

Consequently, the following statement holds:

Proposition 1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g_{\mu\nu})
be a (3+1)-dimensional Ricci-flat spacetime admitting a Killing-Yano tensor such that there exists on
\mathcal{M}
a mixed-symmetry Killing tensor,
i.e.
, a rank-3 tensor
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
which is a
\{2,1\}
Young tableau,
i.e.
, built as
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=N_{\alpha(\beta\gamma)}
such that
N_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=N_{[\alpha\beta]\gamma}
, satisfying the (differential) constraint

\displaystyle T_{\alpha(\beta\gamma;\delta)}=0.

(246)

Then, the quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\triangleq T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}S^{\alpha}\hat{p}^{\beta}\hat{p}^{\gamma}

(247)

is a (homogeneously linear in
\mathcal{S}
) quasi-invariant for the linearized MPT equations on
\mathcal{M}
,
i.e.

\displaystyle\derivative{\mathcal{N}}{\tau}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{2}).

(248)

VI.5
Trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensors

If a mixed-symmetry Killing tensor
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is found, the only point to be addressed before claiming the existence of a new quasi-invariant is to check its non-triviality,
i.e.
, it should not be the product of two others quasi-invariants. We define a
trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensor

T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
as mixed-symmetry Killing tensor that generates a trivial quasi-invariant,
i.e.
an invariant which can be written as the product of other quasi-invariants.
5
5

5

It remains to be investigated if such a tensor necessarily takes the form of a sum of direct products of tensors such that Eq. (
246
) holds. We have not found any simple argument for proving this assertion.
A
non-trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensor
is a mixed-symmetry Killing tensor which is not trivial.

If the spacetime admits a Killing-Yano tensor and a Killing vector, we can construct a trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensor of the form

In order to gain some intuition about mixed-symmetry Killing tensors, it is useful to derive the general such tensor in Minkowski spacetime. For a Riemann flat spacetime, the covariant derivative becomes a coordinate derivative in Minkowskian coordinates (in any dimension). We can therefore consider the index

Now, Kerr spacetime admits a non-trivial Killing tensor and curvature and further analysis is required. We will address the existence of a mixed-symmetry tensor for Kerr spacetime in the following.

VIII
Perspectives

We reduced the existence of a new quasi-constant of motion of the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Tulczyjew equations,
i.e.
, conserved at linear order in the spin, for Ricci-flat spacetimes admitting a Killing-Yano tensor to the existence of a mixed-symmetry Killing tensor on the background spacetime. The result applies in particular to Kerr spacetime, where it was shown that under the assumption of stationarity and axisymmetry only trivial mixed-symmetry Killing tensors exist which lead to products of known quasi-invariants. The absence of complete integrability in the sense of Liouville prevents us to rule out chaos at linear order in the spin, which is nevertheless suggested from the conclusions of
Ruangsri:2015cvg
;
Zelenka:2019nyp
;
Witzany:2019nml
. An open question is how many of the two independent quasi-conserved quantities at linear order in the spin found by Rüdiger
doi:10.1098/rspa.1981.0046
;
doi:10.1098/rspa.1983.0012
admit a generalization that is conserved at quadratic order. Even more interesting is whether a complete set of invariants can be built for the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon equations including the relevant quadrupolar corrections to the MPT equations
1975CMaPh..42…65B
;
Deriglazov:2017jub
;
Deriglazov:2018vwa
;
Porto:2008jj
;
Steinhoff:2012rw
.

Even though we used the spin supplementary condition (SSC) of Tulczjew to derive our new constraint for the existence of additional non-trivial quasi-invariants, our reasoning also holds using the Mathisson-Pirani SSC
Mathisson:1937zz
;
Pirani:1956tn
or the Kyrian-Semerák SSC
Kyrian:2007zz
as a direct consequence of Eq. (
54
). The explicit connection between the quasi-invariants detailed in this paper with the quasi-invariants of the spinning particle determined by a Grassmann odd spin
Cariglia:2011qb
;
Kubiznak:2011ay
remains to be established.

The occurrence of Einstein’s tensor in the central identity (
193
) that we derived suggests that Rüdiger’s quadratic invariant will admit a generalization to the Kerr-Newmann spacetime which also admits a Killing-Yano tensor, once the Einstein tensor is replaced with the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor. This remains to be investigated.

It also remains to be investigated whether the mixed-symmetry Killing tensors that we defined as trivial necessarily take the form of a sum of direct products of Killing(-Yano) vectors and tensors. Even if we suspect that this is true, we have not found a proof of such an assertion.

The Hamilton-Jacobi equation for the MPT equations was separated in
Witzany:2019nml
with separation constants of motion identical to the energy, angular momentum and the two Rüdiger quasi-invariants. Assuming no resonances, Witzany
Witzany:2019nml
was also able to derive action-angle variables and fundamental frequencies of the MPT system at linear order in the spin. However, the construction did not allow for a separation of the orbital equations of motion. This result is consistent with our findings that Liouville integrability does not hold due to a single non-vanishing Poisson bracket between the two Rüdiger quasi-invariants, even though the explicit relationship between these statements remains to be deepened.

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{S^{ab}}{S^{cd}}

\displaystyle=\eta^{ac}S^{bd}-\eta^{ad}S^{bc}+\eta^{bd}S^{ac}-\eta^{bc}S^{ad}.

(292)

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{S^{ab}}{S_{cd}}=4\delta^{[a}_{[c}S^{b]}_{\phantom{b]}d]}.

A.3
\poissonbracket{\mathcal I_{\hat A}}{E}
-type brackets

\mathcal{I}_{\hat{A}}=\ell
.

Using the identities
\nabla_{\alpha}k^{\mu}=\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha t}
and
\nabla_{\alpha}l^{\mu}=\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\phi}
, the bracket reads

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{\ell}{E}

\displaystyle=\poissonbracket{p_t}{p_\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\poissonbracket{S^{\alpha\beta}}{p_\phi}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\alpha}l_{\beta}\poissonbracket{S^{\alpha\beta}}{p_t}-\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{\alpha}l_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}k_{\delta}\poissonbracket{S^{\alpha\beta}}{S^{\gamma\delta}}

(297)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R_{t\phi\alpha\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}+\quantity(\nabla_\alpha l_\beta\Gamma^\alpha_{\lambda t}-\nabla_\alpha k_\beta\Gamma^\alpha_{\lambda\phi})S^{\lambda\beta}+\nabla_{\alpha}l^{\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}k_{\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}

(298)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R_{t\phi\alpha\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}-\nabla_{\alpha}l^{\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}k_{\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}=0.

(299)

The last equality follows from the fact that the axisymmetry of Kerr spacetime together with the definition of the Riemann tensor enforce the relation

\displaystyle R_{t\phi\alpha\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=2\Gamma^{\alpha}_{t\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\phi\beta}S_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta}=-2\nabla_{\alpha}l^{\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}k_{\beta}S^{\alpha\beta}

(300)

to hold.

\mathcal{I}_{\hat{A}}=\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
.

The axisymmetric character of Kerr spacetime enforces the tetrad
e^{\mu}_{a}
to be independent of
t
and
\phi
. This yields
\poissonbracket{p_{t,\phi}}{e^\mu_a}=-\partial_{t,\phi}e^{\mu}_{a}=0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{p_{t,\phi}}{S^{ab}}=\poissonbracket{p_{t,\phi}}{S^{\mu\nu}}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b}_{\nu}.

(301)

Using this last equation and the fact that
\mathcal{Q}_{Y}
commute with
x^{\mu}
, we get

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{\mathcal Q_Y}{E}

\displaystyle=\poissonbracket{p_t}{\mathcal Q_Y}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{a}k_{b}\poissonbracket{S^{ab}}{\mathcal Q_Y}

(302)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ss_tetrad}}{=}-4\bigg[r\quantity(\Gamma^{[1}_{kt}-\nabla_kk^{[1})S^{0]k}

\displaystyle\quad+a\cos\theta\quantity(\Gamma^{[3}_{kt}-\nabla_kk^{[3})S^{2]k}\bigg]

(303)

\displaystyle=0.

(304)

\mathcal{I}_{\hat{A}}=\mathcal{Q}_{R}
.

Using the identity

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{E^G}{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\lambda t}S^{\lambda\beta}

(305)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\beta}k_{\alpha}\nabla_{\lambda}k^{\alpha}S^{\lambda\beta}=0

(306)

and the chain rule (
289
), one has

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{\mathcal Q_R}{E}

\displaystyle=2p_{\mu}K^{\mu\nu}\poissonbracket{p_\nu}{E}-2\mu\poissonbracket{S^\alpha\partial_\alpha\mathcal Z}{E}

(307)

\displaystyle=-p_{\mu}K^{\mu\nu}\quantity(2\pb{p_\nu}{p_t}+\pb{p_\nu}{\nabla_\alpha k_\beta S^{\alpha\beta}})

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\quantity(2\pb{S^\alpha\partial_\alpha\mathcal Z}{p_t}+\pb{S^\alpha\partial_\alpha\mathcal Z}{\nabla_\mu k_\nu S^{\mu\nu}}).

(308)

Making use of the identity

\displaystyle R_{t\beta\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\beta}\quantity(\nabla_\mu k_\nu)S^{\mu\nu}+2\nabla_{\alpha}k_{\nu}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\lambda}S^{\nu\lambda},

(309)

the two first Poisson brackets of this expression can be shown to cancel mutually, and we are left with

\displaystyle\hskip-6.0pt\poissonbracket{\mathcal Q_R}{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}S_{\gamma\delta}\,\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{Z}\quantity[R_{t\beta\mu\nu}-\partial_\beta\qty(\nabla_\mu k_\nu)]S^{\mu\nu}

(310)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{Z}S_{\gamma\delta}\nabla_{\rho}k_{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\beta}S^{\nu\lambda}

(311)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{S}^{2}).

Given a triangle with radii of inscribed and circumscribed circles of

The similar result for the Euclidean tetrahedron was proved independently by Grace (1918) and Danielsson (1952). Namely, if we replace the inscribed and circumscribed circles with spheres, the following inequality holds:

In 2014 Greg Egan conjectured in

for an

(R-nr)(R+(n-2)r)\geqslant d^{2},

\sin^{2}(R-r)-\sin^{2}r\cos^{2}R=\sin^{2}d.

For any integer

It leads to inequality (

The Main Result

The inequalities above are related to a simplex and its two spheres: inscribed and circumscribed. This leads to a certain asymmetry in the notation. The spherical simplices - in contrast to Euclidean ones - have a convenient concept of
polar simplex
.
The circumscribed sphere of a simplex is complementary to the inscribed sphere of the simplex’s polar (they have the same center and sum of their radii is
\frac{\pi}{2}

Definition 2.1
.

Spherical Simplex

Given
n\geqslant 3

be distinct vectors in the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
. Then, the set
U:=\{u_{i}\}
defines a spherical simplex, which has
n
vertices.

Definition 2.2
.

Center and Radius of a Spherical Simplex’s Circumscribed Sphere

For a spherical simplex

and all

.

Polar Spherical Simplices

Two spherical simplices

and

Lorentz singular value decomposition:

By construction

CAB=\begin{pmatrix}bc\cos\alpha&c\sin\alpha&\cdots&0\\
-b\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The Lorentz singular value decomposition which we would like to apply to its top-left
2\times 2
corner is a representation in the form
\begin{pmatrix}\cosh t&\sinh t\\
\sinh t&\cosh t\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}K&0\\
0&L\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\cosh s&\sinh s\\
\sinh s&\cosh s\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad K,L,s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Notice that not every
2\times 2

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}
hasn’t. Indeed, if it has, then this would imply that

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\tau&\sinh\tau\\
\sinh\tau&\cosh\tau\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\sigma&\sinh\sigma\\
\sinh\sigma&\cosh\sigma\\
\end{pmatrix},\text{ so}

\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\tau&-\sinh\tau\\
-\sinh\tau&\cosh\tau\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\sigma&-\sinh\sigma\\
-\sinh\sigma&\cosh\sigma\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sinh(\tau+\sigma)&\cosh(\tau+\sigma)\\
\cosh(\tau+\sigma)&-\sinh(\tau+\sigma)\\
\end{pmatrix},

which cannot be true for real
\tau,\sigma.

\end{pmatrix}

be an
n\times n
diagonal matrix, where all
m_{i}>0.
If the matrices

U^{T}MU,V^{T}MV

have all diagonal elements equal to 0, and

U^{T}V

is a diagonal matrix with all its diagonal elements positive, then

\Tr M\geqslant 0.

In the case where
n=3
, the inequality becomes an equality.

Proof.

Consider a bilinear form
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},
defined by the matrix
M:

M(x,y):=m_{1}x_{1}y_{1}-m_{2}x_{2}y_{2}-\dots-m_{n}x_{n}y_{n}.

Denote the columns of
U,V
as
\overline{u}_{i},\overline{v}_{i},i=1,\dots,n.
Then

M(\overline{u}_{i},\overline{u}_{i})=M(\overline{v}_{i},\overline{v}_{i})=0,\quad\forall i=1,\dots,n.

First let’s show that
M(\overline{u}_{i},\overline{v}_{i})\geqslant 0.
Let
\overline{u}_{i}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\overline{v}_{i}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),
then

\displaystyle(m_{1}x_{1}y_{1})^{2}=m_{1}x_{1}^{2}\cdot m_{1}y_{1}^{2}=(m_{2}x_{2}^{2}+\dots+m_{n}x_{n}^{2})(m_{2}y_{2}^{2}+\dots+m_{n}y_{n}^{2})\geqslant(m_{2}x_{2}y_{2}+\dots+m_{n}x_{n}y_{n})^{2}.

So, as
m_{1},x_{1},y_{1}>0,
we have
M(\overline{u}_{i},\overline{v}_{i})\geqslant 0.

Now,
U^{T}V
is an invertible diagonal matrix (all its diagonal elements are positive), so both
U
and
V
are invertible. Let’s choose some unit vector
e.
We can write:

\overline{e}=x_{1}\overline{u}_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\overline{u}_{n},\text{ for some }x_{i}\in\mathbb{R},i=1,\dots,n.

(18)

Then, multiplying both sides by
\overline{v}_{j}
for some
j
:

\overline{e}\overline{v}_{j}=x_{j}\overline{u}_{j}\overline{v}_{j},\text{ or}

x_{j}=\frac{\overline{e}\overline{v}_{j}}{\overline{u}_{j}\overline{v}_{j}}.

Now, multiplying both sides of (
18
) by
\overline{e},
we get:

1=\overline{e}^{2}=x_{1}\overline{e}\overline{u}_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\overline{e}\overline{u}_{n}=\frac{\overline{e}\overline{u}_{1}\cdot\overline{e}\overline{v}_{1}}{\overline{u}_{1}\overline{v}_{1}}+\dots+\frac{\overline{e}\overline{u}_{n}\cdot\overline{e}\overline{v}_{n}}{\overline{u}_{n}\overline{v}_{n}}.

This means that:

\Tr M=\frac{M(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{v}_{1})}{\overline{u}_{1}\overline{v}_{1}}+\dots+\frac{M(\overline{u}_{n},\overline{v}_{n})}{\overline{u}_{n}\overline{v}_{n}}.

(19)

As
\overline{u}_{i}\overline{v}_{i}>0,
we have
\Tr M\geqslant 0.

∎

3
An
n
-dimensional Grace-Danielsson Inequality

Corollary 3.1
.

For a spherical simplex
U
with
n+1
vertices, let
\beta,\Gamma
be its radii of the circumscribed and inscribed spheres, and
\alpha
be the distance between their centers. Let

\displaystyle R:=\tan\beta,\quad r:=\tan\Gamma,\quad d:=\tan\alpha.

Then

(R-nr)(R+(n-2)r)>d^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:=\frac{\pi}{2}-\Gamma,
so
\tan\gamma=\frac{1}{\tan\Gamma}=\frac{1}{r}.
As
\gamma
is the circumscribed sphere radius of a simplex polar to
U,
we from Theorem
2.5
have:

\sqrt{\left(R\cdot\frac{1}{r}-1\right)^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha-\left(R+\frac{1}{r}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha}\geqslant n-1.

As
d=\tan\alpha,
we have:

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\alpha=\frac{1}{1+d^{2}},\quad\sin^{2}\alpha=\frac{d^{2}}{1+d^{2}},

and so

\sqrt{\left(R\cdot\frac{1}{r}-1\right)^{2}\frac{1}{1+d^{2}}-\left(R+\frac{1}{r}\right)^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{1+d^{2}}}\geqslant n-1.

Taking the square and multiplying both parts by
r^{2}(1+d^{2})
we get:

(R-r)^{2}-(1+Rr)^{2}d^{2}\geqslant(n-1)^{2}(1+d^{2})r^{2}.

As
1+Rr>1,1+d^{2}>1
it leads to:

(R-r)^{2}-d^{2}>(n-1)^{2}r^{2},\text{ or}

(R-nr)(R+(n-2)r)>d^{2}.

∎

Notice that Corollary

3.1
is proven for a spherical simplex in
S^{n}
of radius 1, but actually holds for a spherical simplex on a sphere of any radius. We will now make these spheres very large.

Theorem 3.2
.

(Egan conjecture; an
n
-dimensional Grace-Danielsson inequality.)

Consider a Euclidean simplex
U
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n+1
vertices. Let the radii of its circumscribed and inscribed spheres
be
R
and
r
respectively, and the distance between their centers be
d.
Then the following inequality holds:

(R-nr)(R+(n-2)r)\geqslant d^{2}.

Proof.

1.

Construct an
n
-dimensional sphere:

Let the circumscribed and inscribed spheres of
U
have centers
O
and
I
respectively.
Let’s consider such space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1},
that
\left<U\right>
is a hyperplane in it.
Set the origin at
O
.
Let the vertices of the simplex
U
be
u_{1},\dots,u_{n+1}\in\left<U\right>\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
So
|u_{i}|=R,i=1,\dots,n+1.

Choose a unit vector
\overline{e},
such that
\overline{e}\perp\left<U\right>.

Let point
P
be such that
\overline{OP}=H\overline{e},\quad H\to\infty.

Let
u^{\prime}_{i}:=u_{i}+\overline{PO}.

As
\overline{OP}\perp u_{i},
all distances from
P
to
u_{i}
are the same:

|u_{i}^{\prime}|=\sqrt{H^{2}+R^{2}},\quad\forall i=1,\dots,n+1.

Now consider an
n
-dimensional sphere
S_{H},
with the center
P
and radius
\sqrt{H^{2}+R^{2}}.
It contains all
u_{1},\dots,u_{n+1}
.

Let’s call
\{u^{\prime}_{i}\}
on the sphere
S_{H}
the spherical simplex
U^{\prime}
(it is a non-degenerate spherical simplex). We will call its centers of circumscribed and inscribed spheres
O_{H},I_{H}.
Their radii
\beta_{H},\Gamma_{H}.
We will call the spherical distance between
O_{H}
and
I_{H}
to be
\alpha_{H}.
Also:

R_{H}:=\tan\beta_{H},\quad r_{H}:=\tan\Gamma_{H},\quad d_{H}:=\tan\alpha_{H}.

2.

O_{H}
converges to
O
:

By construction we can write:

\displaystyle|O_{H}O|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{H^{2}+R^{2}}-H\to 0

\displaystyle R_{H}

\displaystyle=\tan\beta_{H}=\frac{R}{H},

which also leads to

R_{H}\sqrt{H^{2}+R^{2}}\to R\text{, as }H\to\infty.

(20)

3.

I_{H}
converges to
I
:

Denote all full-dimensional faces of
U
as
F_{i},
and their
n-1
-dimensional volumes as
V_{i}:

V_{i}:=\Vol_{n-1}(F_{i}).

Point
I
belongs to
\left<U\right>
and is equidistant from all
F_{i}.

We can write the incenter’s barycentric coordinates for a Euclidean simplex
U
with
n+1
vertex:

I=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}V_{i}u_{i}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}V_{i}}.

Point
I_{H}
belongs to the sphere
S_{H}
and is equidistant from all of
\left<F_{i},P\right>
(
n
-dimensional hyperplanes).
Consider an
n+2-
vertex Euclidean simplex
\{U,P\}
, and the point
L_{H}
at the face
U,
which is equidistant from all of
\left<F_{i},P\right>.

It is the intersection of the bisector of angle
P
with face
U.
We can write the barycentric coordinates of
L_{H}
:

L_{H}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}\Vol_{n}(\Conv(F_{i},P))u_{i}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}\Vol_{n}(\Conv(F_{i},P))}.

Denoting the distances from
O
to
F_{i}
by
d_{i}
we can continue:

\Vol_{n}(\Conv(F_{i},P))=\frac{\Vol_{n-1}(F_{i})\sqrt{H^{2}+d_{i}^{2}}}{n}=\frac{V_{i}\sqrt{H^{2}+d_{i}^{2}}}{n}

L_{H}=\frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}V_{i}\sqrt{H^{2}+d_{i}^{2}}u_{i}}{\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}V_{i}\sqrt{H^{2}+d_{i}^{2}}}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}V_{i}u_{i}\sqrt{1+\frac{d_{i}^{2}}{H^{2}}}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}V_{i}\sqrt{1+\frac{d_{i}^{2}}{H^{2}}}}\to I,\text{ as }H\to\infty.

(21)

We can estimate
|PL_{H}|\geqslant H.
As
|PI_{H}|=\sqrt{H^{2}+R^{2}},
and
L_{H}\in[PI_{H}],
the following holds:

|L_{H}I_{H}|\leqslant\sqrt{H^{2}+R^{2}}-H\to 0,\text{ as }H\to\infty.

(22)

Thus, from the convergence conditions (
21
), (
22

From

As

\sqrt{H^{2}+R^{2}}\sin\Gamma_{H}\to r\text{, so}

(23)

4.

Euclidean inequality is a limit of spherical inequalities:

From Corollary

So,

A Note on Vectorial Boolean Functions as Embeddings
Augustine Musukwa, Massimiliano Sala

Abstract
Let F F be a vectorial Boolean function from 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} to 𝔽 2 m \mathbb{F}_{2}^{m} , with m ≥ n m\geq n . We define F F as an embedding if F F is injective. In this paper, we examine the component functions of F F , focusing on constant and balanced components. Our findings reveal that at most 2 m − 2 m − n 2^{m}-2^{m-n} components of F F can be balanced, and this maximum is achieved precisely when F F is an embedding, with the remaining 2 m − n 2^{m-n} components being constants. Additionally, for partially-bent embeddings, we demonstrate that there are always at least 2 n − 1 2^{n}-1 balanced components when n n is even, and 2 m − 1 + 2 n − 1 − 1 2^{m-1}+2^{n-1}-1 balanced components when n n is odd. A relation with APN functions is shown.

Introduction

Vectorial Boolean functions play a fundamental role in cryptography, particularly in the design of block ciphers and stream ciphers
[
4
]
. They are used as S-boxes (substitution boxes) to provide non-linearity and resistance against linear and differential cryptanalysis. Strong cryptographic properties such as high nonlinearity, differential uniformity, algebraic immunity, and correlation immunity make these functions essential for securing encryption algorithms.

In this note, we study vectorial Boolean functions mapping from a lower-dimensional vector space to a higher-dimensional vector space. Although few papers have been published on these functions, some studies have suggested their possible application in ciphers. Recently, some researchers have considered these functions, with a focus on studying APN functions that satisfy the so-called D-property (see, for example,
[
1
,
13
]
). These functions were also partially studied in
[
2
]
, where the authors examined certain cryptographic properties of Feistel networks using functions from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
as S-boxes. Specifically, they provided an example of a cipher using an injective APN function from 4 bits to 5 bits as an S-box. This suggests further investigation of injective maps, which in this paper we call
“embeddings.”

In our study of these embeddings, we are also motivated by two more reasons. The first is of a geometric type. If we consider an affine map
F
between two vector spaces
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, with
m\geq n
,
F
will have the property that all components are either balanced or constant (we call such an
F

“affine-like”
in this introduction). There is no hope that generic vectorial Boolean functions share this property, but it is well-known that bijections do, since all their components are balanced except for the trivial zero component. Along the same reasoning, we try to understand when a function has many balanced components (which may be seen as a measure of
“balancedness”
). Since bijections have a huge number of balanced components, we attempt to identify a more general property that guarantees high balancedness.
We do that by looking at embeddings and indeed we present in this paper some results along this line. First, whatever the degree of the embedding
F
, if the image happens to be a subspace then
F
is affine-like (Theorem
21
). Second, if
F
is quadratic (or partially-bent), we can show the presence of a huge number of balanced components (especially when
n
is odd), even if others can clearly be anything (Theorem
17
). Finally, coming back to embeddings of any degree,

F
is affine-like whenever

F
has the highest possible number (permitted by the space dimensions
m,n
)
of balanced components
(Corollary
13
) or of constant components
(Corollary
16
).

The last motivation is related to the theory of APN functions. Although cubic APN permutations in odd dimension are known, none is known in even dimension. Certainly, there is none for
n=4
(as experimentally shown in
[
8
]
and formally proved in
[
3
]
)
and for
n=6
(as experimentally shown in
[
9
]
).
The reason for this scarcity is still unclear. For APN functions, suitable restrictions of their derivatives are embeddings. We show in Section
6
that, for such restrictions, at least half of their components must be balanced in the odd case, while in the even case a surprising three quarters of them must be balanced.
This significant difference
might help explain the scarcity of their permutation classes.

This paper consists of six sections and is structured as follows. Section
2
provides an overview of some known results and essential notations for our study. In Section
3
, we present some preliminary results, with a primary focus on the weight of first-order derivatives of any Boolean function. Section
4
presents a study of some quantities for vBfs from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. In Section
5
, we focus on the case
m\geq n
. Here, we investigate the balancedness of vBfs, with a particular emphasis on injective functions, and we identify cases when they are affine-like. Finally, an application to cubic APN functions is given in Section
6
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some definitions, known results, and notations essential to our work. For further details, we recommend referring to
[
4
,
5
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

The field of two elements,
0
and
1
, is denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let
n
and
m
be positive integers. The vector space of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is represented as
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. We use
0_{n}
to denote the zero vector
(0,\ldots,0)
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. The size of any finite set
A
is denoted by
|A|
.

A function
F
from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is referred to as a
vectorial Boolean function (vBf)
. We simply say a
Boolean function
when
m=1
and it is represented with lowercase letters, such as
f
. The set of all Boolean functions on
n
variables is denoted by
B_{n}
.

A Boolean function on
n
variables in Algebraic Normal Form (ANF) is given by:

f(x_{1},...,x_{n})=\sum_{I\subseteq\mathcal{P}}a_{I}\left(\prod_{i\in I}x_{i}\right),

where
\mathcal{P}=\{1,...,n\}
and
a_{I}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
for all
I\subseteq P
. The
algebraic degree
(or simply
degree
) of
f
, denoted by
\deg(f)
, is
\max_{I\subseteq\mathcal{P}}\{|I|:a_{I}\neq 0\}
.

The
weight
of
f\in B_{n}
is defined as
\mathrm{wt}(f)=|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:f(x)=1\}|
. We say that
f
is
balanced
if
|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:f(x)=1\}|=|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:f(x)=0\}|
, that is, if
\mathrm{wt}(f)=2^{n-1}
. The
evaluation
of
f\in B_{n}
is defined by
{\rm ev}(f)=(f(x))_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}
(with an implicit ordering on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
).

A Boolean function
f\in B_{n}
is called
constant
if
\deg(f)=0
,
affine
if
\deg(f)\leq 1
,
linear
if
\deg(f)=1
and
f(0_{n})=0
,
quadratic
if
\deg(f)\leq 2
and
strictly quadratic
if
\deg(f)=2
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
. An affine function is given by
\alpha\cdot x+c
. Let
\alpha\neq 0_{n}
, a linear function is given by
\alpha\cdot x
. Any affine function with
\alpha\neq 0_{n}
is balanced
[
4
]
.

The
Walsh transform
of
f\in B_{n}
is defined as the function
\mathcal{W}_{f}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
given by
\mathcal{W}_{f}(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f(x)+a\cdot x},
for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. We denote
\mathcal{F}(f)=\mathcal{W}_{f}(0_{n})
.

Remark 1
.

[
4
, p.76]

For any
f\in B_{n}
,
\mathcal{F}(f)=2^{n}-2\mathrm{wt}(f).

Therefore,
f
is balanced if and only if
\mathcal{F}(f)=0
.

The
nonlinearity
of
f\in B_{n}
is given by
\mathcal{N}(f)=2^{n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}|\mathcal{W}_{f}(a)|
. It is well-known that
\mathcal{N}(f)\leq 2^{n-1}-2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}

[
4
, p.80]
. A Boolean function
f\in B_{n}
is called
bent
if
\mathcal{N}(f)=2^{n-1}-2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}
and this may happen only when
n
is even. A Boolean function
f\in B_{n}
is called
semi-bent
if
\mathcal{N}(f)=2^{n-1}-2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
and this many happen only when
n
is odd.

We define the
first-order derivative
D_{a}f
of
f\in B_{n}
in the direction of
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
by

D_{a}f(x)=f(x+a)+f(x)
. A Boolean function
f\in B_{n}
is bent if and only if
D_{a}f
is balanced for all nonzero
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

[
4
, p.82]
.

Any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is called a
linear structure
of
f
if
D_{a}f
is constant. The set of all linear structures of
f
, denoted by
V(f)
, is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

[
4
, p.99]
. A Boolean function
f
is
partially-bent
if there exists a linear subspace
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
such that the restriction of
f
to
E
is affine and the restriction of
f
to any complementary subspace
E^{\prime}
of
E
(where
E\oplus E^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
) is bent. This also includes the case
E=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, i.e.,
\deg(f)\leq 1
. It was observed in
[
3
]
that
E=V(f)
for a partially-bent function
f
. It is well-known that every quadratic function is partially-bent
[
4
, p.257]
.

As it can be noted in
[
4
, p.257]
and
[
6
, p.141]
, the quantity
\mathcal{F}(f)
of any unbalanced partially-bent function
f
is linked to
\dim V(f)
. We present this result in the following.

Remark 2
.

For any unbalanced partially-bent function
f\in B_{n}
, let
k=\dim V(f)
. Then
k\equiv n\pmod{2}
and
\mathcal{F}(f)\in\Big\{\pm 2^{\frac{n+k}{2}}\Big\}
.

Therefore, for
n
even, we have that
\dim V(f)=0
if and only if f is bent,

while for
n
odd
\dim V(f)\geq 1
,
\dim V(f)=1
if and only if f is semi-bent.

A vBf
F
from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
can be represented by
F=(f_{1},...,f_{m})
, where
f_{1},...,f_{m}
are Boolean functions on
n
variables called
coordinate functions
. The Boolean functions given by
\lambda\cdot F
, with
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
and
“
\cdot
”
denoting the dot product, are called
component functions
(or
components
) of
F
and they are denoted by
F_{\lambda}
. The components
\{F_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\neq 0_{m}}
are called
nontrivial components
. The degree of
F
is given by
\deg(F)=\max_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{m}}\deg(F_{\lambda})
, which clearly is the maximum degree over the coordinate functions.

The first-order derivative of a vBf
F
in the direction of any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is defined in a similar manner as for Boolean functions, that is, it is given by
D_{a}F(x)=F(x+a)+F(x)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be finite sets. Let
G
be a function from
X
to
Y
. The
pre-image
of
y\in Y
by
G
is defined by
G^{-1}(y)=\{x\in X:G(x)=y\}
. We say that
G
is
injective
if
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
implies
G(x_{1})\neq G(x_{2})
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
. Equivalently, we say
G
is injective if
|G^{-1}(y)|\leq 1
for all
y\in Y
. The
image
of a function
G
is defined as
{\rm Im}(G)=\{G(x):x\in X\}\subseteq Y
. When
F
is a vBf observe that the definition of injectivity is equivalent to stating that
|{\rm Im}(F)|=2^{n}
, which turns out to be very useful in some proofs in this paper. We call an injective vBf an
embedding
.

A vBf
F
is
balanced
if
|F^{-1}(y)|=2^{n-m}
for all
y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. For
n=m
, a balanced vBf is a permutation, which clearly is an embedding. A vBf
F
is balanced if and only if all nontrivial components are balanced
[
4
, p.112]
. Let
B(F)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}:F_{\lambda}\text{ is balanced}\}
. Since
F
is balanced if and only if
|B(F)|=2^{m}-1
, we can view
|B(F)|
as a measure of
balancedness
for
F
.

We call a vBf
F
a
quadratic embedding
if it is an embedding with
\deg(F)=2
. We say that
F
is a
partially-bent embedding
if it is an embedding with
\deg(F)\geq 2
and all nontrivial components are partially-bent.

Remark 3
.

According to our definitions, all quadratic embeddings are partially-bent embeddings. Moreover, a quadratic/partially-bent embedding
F
may have affine nontrivial components, but
F
must also have higher-degree components (with degree two if
F
is quadratic).

If
F
is a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, we define
C(F)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}:F_{\lambda}\text{ is constant}\}
. Clearly,
C(F)
is a vector space. A function
\psi_{n}
from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to itself is called an
affinity
if there is an invertible matrix
M
and a vector
t\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
such that
\psi_{n}(x)=xM+t
, for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
(using row vector notation).
Any two vBfs
F
and
F^{\prime}
are called
affine equivalent
if there are two affinities,
\psi_{n}
and
\psi_{m}
, such that
F^{\prime}=\psi_{m}\circ F\circ\psi_{n}

[
4
]
. Two affine equivalent vBfs share many properties, but for our goals it is enough to know that
|B(F)|=|B(F^{\prime})|
and
|C(F)|=|C(F^{\prime})|
.

3
Preliminary results on Boolean functions

In this section, we focus on Boolean functions and determine the sum of the weights of their first-order derivatives. We are specifically interested in the result for quadratic and partially-bent functions because it is used in the next section. We begin with the following result, which can also be found in
[
5
, p.1496]
. We report this proof to accustom the reader to our proving methods.

Lemma 4
.

For any
f\in B_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}(f)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}f).

Proof.

We have the following:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}(f)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f(x)}\right)^{2}=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f(x)+f(y)}=\sum_{x,a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{f(x)+f(x+a)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{D_{a}f(x)}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{D_{a}f(x)}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}f).\qed

By applying Lemma
4
and Remark
1
, we can easily infer the following.

Corollary 5
.

Let
f\in B_{n}
. Then

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}f)=2^{2n-1}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}^{2}(f).

From Corollary
5
, Remark
1
and the inequality
\mathcal{F}^{2}(f)\geq 0
, we immediately get the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

For any
f\in B_{n}
, we have

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0_{n}\}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}f)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}f)\leq 2^{2n-1}.

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
f
is balanced.

We next determine the value
\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0_{n}\}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}f)
for partially-bent functions.

Corollary 7
.

Let
f\in B_{n}
be partially-bent and let
k=\dim V(f)
. Then

(i)

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0_{n}\}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}f)=2^{2n-1}
if
f
is balanced,

(ii)

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0_{n}\}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}f)=2^{2n-1}-2^{n+k-1}
if
f
is unbalanced.

Proof.

If
f
is balanced, we just apply Lemma
6
.

Otherwise, we just apply Remark
2
to Corollary
5
.
∎

4
Results for general vectorial Boolean functions

In this section, we study some quantities for vBfs from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, where
n
and
m
are any positive integers. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let
F
be a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot(F(x)+F(x+a))}=\begin{cases}2^{n+m}&\text{ if }a=0_{n}\\
2^{m}\,|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:F(x)=F(x+a)\}|&\text{ if }a\neq 0_{n}.\end{cases}

(1)

Proof.

Observe that
F(x)=F(x+a)
implies that
F(x)+F(x+a)=0_{m}
and in this case

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot(F(x)+F(x+a))}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot 0_{m}}=2^{m}.

When
a=0_{n}
, we have a special case of the above equation, since
F(x)+F(x+a)=F(x)+F(x)=0_{m}
.

On the other hand,

F(x)\neq F(x+a)
implies that
F(x)+F(x+a)=\varepsilon\neq 0_{m}
and so

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot(F(x)+F(x+a))}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot\varepsilon}=0.

We get
0
in the preceding equation because
\lambda\mapsto\lambda\cdot\varepsilon
is a linear Boolean function in
\lambda
.

By summing over
x
the previous equalities, we obtain Equation (
1
).
∎

Next we establish a relation that characterizes the embeddings and it is useful in determining the number of balanced components in the next section.

Theorem 9
.

Let
F
be a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Then

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\mathcal{F}^{2}(F_{\lambda})\geq 2^{n+m}.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
m\geq n
and
F
is an embedding.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{F}(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot(F(x)+F(x+a))}
. From Lemma
8
, it is obvious that

i)

\varphi_{F}(a)\geq 0
, for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,

ii)

\varphi_{F}(a)=0
if and only if
F(x)\neq F(x+a)
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

Note that
F
is an embedding if and only if, for any
a\neq 0_{n}
, we have
F(x)+F(x+a)\neq 0_{m}
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Hence,
F
is embedding if and only if
\varphi_{F}(a)=0
for any
a\neq 0_{n}
.

Now we look at the quantity
\sum_{a\neq 0_{n}}\varphi_{F}(a)
.

For any
F
,

\displaystyle\sum_{a\neq 0_{n}}\varphi_{F}(a)=\sum_{a\neq 0_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot(F(x)+F(x+a))}\geq 0,

(2)

while
F
is an embedding if and only if
\sum_{a\neq 0_{n}}\varphi_{F}(a)=0
.

Next, we apply Relation (
2
) and Lemma
4
in the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\mathcal{F}^{2}(F_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})\right)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{D_{a}F_{\lambda}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot\left(F(x)+F(x+a)\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot\left(F(x)+F(x+0_{n})\right)}+\sum_{a\neq 0_{n}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot\left(F(x)+F(x+a)\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}(-1)^{\lambda\cdot 0_{m}}+\sum_{a\neq 0_{n}}\varphi_{F}(a)=2^{n+m}+\sum_{a\neq 0_{n}}\varphi_{F}(a)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{n+m}.

(3)

In Relation (
3
), equality holds if and only if
F
is an embedding.
∎

Remark 10
.

Worth mentioning is the study in
[
7
, p.126]
, where the value
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\mathcal{F}^{2}(F_{\lambda})
was considered and shown to be

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\mathcal{F}^{2}(F_{\lambda})=2^{m}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}|F^{-1}(b)|^{2}.

Theorem
9
could also be proved by using this relation.

We next apply Lemma
4
and Theorem
9
to directly deduce the following.

Corollary 11
.

Let
F
be a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Then we have

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})\geq 2^{n+m}.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
m\geq n
and
F
is an embedding.

5
Results for vectorial Boolean functions with
m\geq n

This section explores vBf’s from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, with
m\geq n
. We investigate the balancedness of their component functions, with a particular emphasis on injective functions (embeddings).

Corollary
11
carries significant implications for the weights of the first-order derivatives of components. This can be seen in the following.

Corollary 12
.

Let
F
be a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
into
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, with
m\geq n
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})\leq 2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-2^{m-n}).

(4)

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
F
is an embedding.

Proof.

We begin by observing that

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{0_{m}})+\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=2^{2n}+\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{\lambda}).

(5)

So, by Corollary
11
and Equation (
5
), we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})\geq 2^{n+m}-2^{2n}=2^{2n}(2^{m-n}-1).

(6)

and equality holds if and only if
F
is an embedding.

By Equation (
6
), we infer the following:

\displaystyle 2^{2n}(2^{m-n}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{F}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\left(2^{n}-2{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\left(2^{2n}-2\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})\right)

\displaystyle=2^{2n}(2^{m}-1)-2\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})

from which we obtain Relation (
4
) and equality holds if and only if
F
is an embedding. ∎

Next, we apply Corollary
12
to determine an upper bound on the number of balanced components for
any
vBf with
m\geq n
.

Corollary 13
.

Let
F
be a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, with
m\geq n
. Then
|B(F)|\leq 2^{m}-2^{m-n}
. Furthermore, equality may be achieved only when
F
is an embedding and in this case, all the other
2^{m-n}
components are constant.

Proof.

Let
F_{\lambda}
be a component. By Lemma
6
, the value
\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})
is exactly
2^{2n-1}
if
F_{\lambda}
is balanced, strictly less otherwise. Considering (
4
), if
|B(F)|=2^{m}-2^{m-n}
then the inequality reaches equality, if
|B(F)|>2^{m}-2^{m-n}
then the inequality is contradicted. Therefore,
F
can have at most
2^{m}-2^{m-n}
balanced components.

When
|B(F)|=2^{m}-2^{m-n}
,
F
must be an embedding since equality holds for (
4
). In this case, exactly
2^{m-n}
vectors
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
are such that
\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}\mathrm{wt}(D_{a}F_{\mu})=0
, which occurs only when the corresponding components
F_{\mu}
are constants.
∎

Remark 14
.

From Corollary
13
, a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, where
m>n
, cannot have all its nontrivial components balanced, since it has at least
2^{m-n}
unbalanced components.

Let
F
be a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Recall that
C(F)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}:F_{\lambda}\text{ is constant}\}
is a vector space.

Lemma 15
.

Let
F
be a vBf from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
with
m\geq n
. Let
h=\dim C(F)
. Then

|{\rm Im}(F)|\leq 2^{m-h}\;.

Proof.

The case
h=0
is obvious.

If
h\geq 1
, let
E
be such that
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}=C(F)\oplus E
. We claim that

\displaystyle\mbox{for any }x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},\qquad\pi_{E}\circ F(x)\neq\pi_{E}\circ F(y)\iff F(x)\neq F(y)\,,

(7)

where
\pi_{E}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\to E
is any surjective function. From (
7
) we have that
|{\rm Im}(\pi_{E}\circ F)|=|{\rm Im}(F)|
. Since
{\rm Im}(\pi_{E}\circ F)\subseteq E
and
|E|=2^{\dim E}=2^{m-h}
, we have

|{\rm Im}(F)|\leq|E|=2^{m-h}.

To prove (
7
), observe that (
\Rightarrow
) comes from
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, while (
\Leftarrow
) follows from the direct sum
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}=C(F)\oplus E
and the equality (for any
x,y
)
\pi_{C(F)}\circ F(x)=\pi_{C(F)}\circ F(y)
.
∎

We again use Corollary
12
to derive the following result on the number of constant components for an embedding.

Corollary 16
.

Let
F
be an embedding from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, with
m\geq n
. Then
|C(F)|\leq 2^{m-n}
.

Furthermore, if equality is achieved, then all the other
2^{m}-2^{m-n}
components are balanced.

Proof.

According to Lemma
15
,
|{\rm Im}(F)|\leq 2^{m-h}
, where
h=\dim(C(F))
. If
|C(F)|>2^{m-n}
, then
h>m-n
and so
|{\rm Im}(F)|<2^{m-(m-n)}=2^{n}
, which is impossible.

If
|C(F)|=2^{m-n}
and
F
is an embedding, then (
4
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus C(F)}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})=2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-2^{m-n}).

(8)

But
|\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus C(F)|=2^{m}-2^{m-n}
and each addend in (
8
) has to be at most
2^{2n-1}
, hence each addend is exactly
2^{2n-1}
. ∎

We next determine a lower bound on the number of balanced components for any partially-bent embedding. Observe that if
m=n
then any embedding is a permutation, which implies that
|B(F)|=2^{n}-1
. So in the following statement, we are only interested in the case where
m
is strictly larger than
n
.

Theorem 17
.

Let
F
be a partially-bent embedding from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
, with
m\geq n+1
. Then

(i)

|B(F)|\geq 2^{n}-1
, for
n
even and equality holds if and only if all the other nontrivial components (i.e., the unbalanced components) are bent,

(ii)

|B(F)|\geq 2^{m-1}+2^{n-1}-1
, for
n
odd and equality holds if and only if all the other nontrivial components (i.e., the unbalanced components) are unbalanced semi-bent.

Proof.

When
F_{\lambda}
is not balanced, we write
k_{\lambda}=\dim V(F_{\lambda})
. Observe from Remark
2
that, for
n
even,
k_{\lambda}
is even with
0\leq k_{\lambda}\leq n
and for
n
odd,
k_{\lambda}
is odd with
1\leq k_{\lambda}\leq n
. By Corollary
7
, we can derive the following equality:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0_{m}\}}

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})=2^{2n-1}|B(F)|+\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}{\rm wt}(D_{a}F_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=2^{2n-1}|B(F)|+\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}(2^{2n-1}-2^{n+k_{\lambda}-1})

\displaystyle=2^{2n-1}|B(F)|+2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-|B(F)|-1)-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{n+k_{\lambda}-1}

\displaystyle=2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-1)-2^{n-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{k_{\lambda}}

(9)

Since
F
is an embedding, by Relation (
9
) and Corollary
12
, we have

2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-1)-2^{n-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{k_{\lambda}}\quad\quad=\quad\quad 2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-2^{m-n})

from which we deduce the following:

\displaystyle 2^{n-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{k_{\lambda}}=2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-1)-2^{2n-1}(2^{m}-2^{m-n})=2^{2n-1}(2^{m-n}-1)

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{k_{\lambda}}=2^{n}(2^{m-n}-1)

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{k_{\lambda}}=2^{m}-2^{n}.

(10)

Suppose that
n
is even. We know that
k_{\lambda}\geq 0
. Thus,

\displaystyle 2^{m}-2^{n}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{k_{\lambda}}\geq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{0}=2^{m}-|B(F)|-1

\displaystyle\implies|B(F)|\geq 2^{n}-1.

Clearly,
|B(F)|=2^{n}-1
if and only if
k_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda\notin B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\}
if and only if
F_{\lambda}
is bent for all
\lambda\notin B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\}
.

Suppose that
n
odd, we know that
k_{\lambda}\geq 1
. Thus,

\displaystyle 2^{m}-2^{n}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{k_{\lambda}}\geq\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus(B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\})}2^{1}=2(2^{m}-|B(F)|-1)

\displaystyle\implies|B(F)|\geq 2^{m-1}+2^{n-1}-1.

|B(F)|=2^{m-1}+2^{n-1}-1
if and only if
k_{\lambda}=1
for all
\lambda\notin B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\}
if and only if
F_{\lambda}
is unbalanced semi-bent for all
\lambda\notin B(F)\cup\{0_{m}\}
.
∎

5.1
Special Case for embeddings

In this subsection, we consider two special cases, where we see that the bound of Corollary
13
can be actually tight.
In the first case we restrict to
m=n+1
. The second case considers a situation where we see a behaviour in the embedding similar to that of an affine map.

We now examine the first case.

Proposition 20
.

Let a vBf
F
from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n+1}
be an embedding. Then

(i)

either there is one and only one constant nontrivial component and the other nontrivial components are balanced, or

(ii)

there is no constant nontrivial component.

Proof.

Since
F
is embedding and
1\leq|C(F)|\leq 2^{n+1-n}=2
, then we only have two cases.

If
|C(F)|=2
, we have one and only one constant nontrivial component, while the remaining nontrivial components must be balanced by Corollary
16
.

If
|C(F)|=1
,
C(F)=\{0_{n+1}\}
and
F
does not have any constant nontrivial component.
∎

Finally, we examine a special case where the image of an embedding
F
from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
forms a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. We present the result in the following.

Theorem 21
.

Let a vBf
F
from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
be an embedding, with
m\geq n
, and
{\rm Im}(F)
be an affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Then,

(i)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
,
F_{\lambda}
is either constant or balanced.

(ii)

F
has precisely
2^{m}-2^{m-n}
balanced components and
2^{m-n}
constant components.

Proof.

Let
J=\{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n},0,\ldots,0):v_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. By elementary linear algebra,
{\rm Im}(F)
is an affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
if and only if there is an affinity
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
such that
{\rm Im}(F)=\psi(J)
. So if we consider
F^{\prime}=\psi^{-1}\circ F
,
F^{\prime}:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
,
{\rm Im}(F^{\prime})=J
. Observe that we can choose
\psi
so that
F^{\prime}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},0\ldots,0)
. With this choice,
F^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is a linear combination of coordinate functions which are either linear or constant, so
|B(F^{\prime})|+|C(F^{\prime})|=2^{m}
, the same holds for
F_{\lambda}
and we have (i).

If
F^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is a linear combination of the last
m-n
coordinate functions, then
F^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is constant and so
|C(F^{\prime})|\geq 2^{m-n}
. By Corollary
16
, we have (ii).
∎

6
Applications to cubic APN functions

Let
F
be a vBF from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to itself. Let
I={(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1},0)}
. We say that
F
is an APN if for any
a\neq 0_{n}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
x\neq y,y+a
,
F(x)+F(x+a)\not=F(y)+F(y+a)

[
4
, p.137]
.
We can suppose w.l.o.g. that
a\not\in I
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}=I\oplus(I+a)
, and
D_{a}F(x)=D_{a}F(x+a)
.

Definition 22
.

Let
F
be a vBF from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to itself. Let
I_{0}={(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1},0)}
and
I_{1}={(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1},1)}
. Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
,
a\neq 0_{n}
. We define
\mathcal{D}_{a}(F):\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
as
\mathcal{D}_{a}(F)(v)=D_{a}F(v,0)
if
a\notin I_{0}
, as
\mathcal{D}_{a}(F)(v)=D_{a}F(v,1)
if
a\notin I_{1}
.

Clearly,
F
is APN if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{a}(F)
is an embedding for any
a\neq 0_{n}
. Observe that if
F
is cubic APN, then
\mathcal{D}_{a}(F)
is quadratic/partially-bent embedding, for any
a\neq 0_{n}
. Hence, by Theorem
17
, the following is deduced.

Corollary 23
.

Let
F
be a cubic APN function from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to itself. Then

(i)

|B(\mathcal{D}_{a}(F))|\geq 2^{n-1}-1
, for
n
odd and equality holds if and only if all the other nontrivial components (i.e., the unbalanced components) are bent,

(ii)

|B(\mathcal{D}_{a}(F))|\geq 3\cdot 2^{n-2}-1
, for
n
even and equality holds if and only if all the other nontrivial components (i.e., the unbalanced components) are unbalanced semi-bent.

Logical Obstruction to Set Agreement Tasks for Superset-Closed Adversaries
Koki Yagi yagi.koki.27v@st.kyoto-u.ac.jp Susumu Nishimura susumu@math.kyoto-u.ac.jp

Abstract
In their recent paper (GandALF 2018), Goubault, Ledent, and Rajsbaum provided a formal epistemic model for distributed computing. Their logical model, as an alternative to the well-studied topological model, provides an attractive framework for refuting the solvability of a given distributed task by means of logical obstruction : One just needs to devise a formula, in the formal language of epistemic logic, that describes a discrepancy between the model of computation and that of the task. However, few instances of logical obstruction were presented in their paper and specifically logical obstruction to the wait-free 2-set agreement task was left as an open problem. Soon later, Nishida affirmatively answered to the problem by providing inductively defined logical obstruction formulas to the wait-free k k -set agreement tasks. The present paper refines Nishida’s work and devises logical obstruction formulas to k k -set agreement tasks for superset-closed adversaries, which supersede the wait-free model. These instances of logical obstruction formulas exemplify that the logical framework can provide yet another feasible method for showing impossibility of distributed tasks, though it is currently being confined to one-round distributed protocols. The logical method has an advantage over the topological method that it enjoys a self-contained, elementary induction proof. This is in contrast to topological methods, in which sophisticated topological tools, such as Nerve lemma, are often assumed as granted.

Introduction

In the last few decades,
the topological theory of distributed computing
has been successful in giving a range of
fundamental insights and results, most notably
the simplicial complex model of distributed tasks,
protocols as simplicial subdivisions, and
the impossibility results on
a significant family of distributed tasks
[
18
,
16
]
.
Though it
has been recognized since
the earliest work by Saks and Zaharoglou
[
25
]

that the topological model is an interpretation of
epistemic knowledge held by distributed processes,
the rigorous connection was established
only very recently by Goubault, Ledent, and Rajsbaum
in
[
13
]
.
They defined the task solvability in terms of
a multi-agent dynamic epistemic logic (DEL)
[
26
]
,
establishing the isomorphism between the topological models and an appropriate class of logical models of epistemic knowledge.

This new logical model
yields a formal means to refute task solvability:
One is obliged to find an epistemic logic formula
\psi
, called

logical obstruction
,
such that
\psi
holds for the model of the task
but does not for the model of the
protocol that is relevant to the system of concern.
However, in
[
13
]
,
concrete instances of logical obstruction are given only for
the wait-free binary consensus task but none for others.
Particularly the logical obstruction to
the general wait-free
k
-set agreement task was
left as an open problem.
Soon later, Nishida settled it positively by
devising concrete logical obstruction formulas
to the wait-free
k
-set agreement tasks
[
24
]
,
where he carried out the proof by elementary inductive argument
on inductively defined formulas.

This paper presents an extension of
Nishida’s result to the computation model
of superset-closed adversaries
[
10
]
,
which generalizes the basic wait-free model.
More specifically, we provide logical obstruction formulas to show
that
k
-set agreement tasks for a sufficiently small
k

are not solvable under superset-closed adversaries,
by a single round execution of the
round operator
[
17
]
.

The logical obstruction we are to present for the adversarial model
is a generalization of the one devised by Nishida
for the wait-free model.
It is somewhat surprising that essentially the same logical obstruction,
up to appropriate generalization,
works well for the models of different levels of complexity.
(See Section
4.2
for a discussion of
how the underlying simplicial structures are different in the two models.)
This is made possible by exploiting
the notion of
permutation subset
,
a combinatorial feature intrinsic to set agreement tasks
[
24
]
.

The impossibility proofs by Nishida’s and ours demonstrate that
the logical method proposed by by Goubault, Ledent, and Rajsbaum

[
13
]
serves as
yet another feasible method of refuting task solvability,
where the logical obstruction is expressed in
a formal language of epistemic logic.
In the logical framework, one just needs to devise a logical
obstruction formula to the task of concern and is obliged
to prove that the formula is indeed an obstruction.
In this paper, we show that a simple elementary inductive argument suffices,
at least the unsolvability of
k
-set agreement tasks
for the adversarial model is concerned.

We have to remark that this paper discusses
task unsolvability is confined to
single round protocols solely
and does not concern multiple round protocols,
i.e., iterated execution of a single round protocol.
This is in contrast to the preceding work
by Herlihy and Rajsbaum
[
17
]

that provides a topological proof for the
unsolvability of set agreement tasks
for multiple round protocols.
We would need a further technical
development for effectively dealing with multiple round protocols in the
logical framework and leave this topic for future investigation.

Related work.

Topological methods have been successfully applied
to show the impossibility of set agreement tasks
for the wait-free model
[
18
]

and for the adversary model
[
17
]
.
The general proof strategy is to find a ‘topological’ obstruction
that detects the topological inconsistency between the models
of the task and the protocol. In the case of set agreement tasks,
the unsolvability comes from the fact that
the image of the carrier map
[
16
]
of the task,
i.e., a functional specification of the task that maps
an input simplex to an output complex, is less connected than
that of the protocol.
In contrast, in the logical framework proposed by
Goubault et al.
[
13
]
,
one is asked to devise a logical obstruction formula
in the
product update model
,
which encodes the input/output relation of a carrier map
by a complex obtained by a relevant product construction.
(See Section
2.3.2
for the formal definition
of product update model.)
It is often difficult to translate a topological inconsistency into
a logical obstruction formula, since
the complex of a product update model tends to have a fairly different,
more complicated simplicial structure than that of the original one.

The impossibility results presented in
[
24
]

and this paper are demonstrated by a self-contained, elementary proof.
Once the semantics of the formal language of epistemic logic is learnt,
one is immediately accessible to every detail of the proof.
In contrast, impossibility proofs in topological methods
tend to resort to sophisticated theorems from topology.
For instance, the impossibility proof of set agreement tasks
given in
[
17
]
critically depends on
the Nerve lemma, which is by no means an elementary topological result.
(See
[
4
]
for a combinatorial proof of Nerve lemma
and
[
20
]
for a proof from the perspective of
modern algebraic topology.)

It should be noticed that the logic-based solution
provided in this paper
does not fully substitute for the topological method.
First, as we mentioned earlier,
our logical obstruction concerns single round protocols only
and is not immediately applicable to multiple round protocols.
This is because a different logical obstruction formula is required
for each specific epistemic structure that varies at every
incremental round step.
In contrast, topological method is more robust to such
incremental evolution in the underlying structure,
resorting to a certain topological invariant that is kept intact
throughout the steps of rounds.
Second,
the product update model of the logical framework is only able to
relate each input facet (i.e., a simplex of maximal dimension)
to an output complex (of the same dimension).
This means the logical framework assumes certain
crash-free systems in which no process execution fails,
as opposed to the usual crash-prone assumption on distributed systems.
The logical framework, however, still allows us to derive
impossibility results for set agreement tasks: Under the asynchronous setting,
faulty processes can be regarded just as processes
which execute so “slowly” that correct processes are
ignorant of them.
[
22
,
11
]

Naturally, the development of the present paper much owes
to Nishida’s original work
[
24
]
.
Our contribution is to generalize
his insight in the construction of logical obstruction formulas
so that it can encompass adversarial models.
This also contributes to refine Nishida’s original proof
with appropriate level of generalization.
We hope the present paper would
help the significant idea in Nishida’s paper,
which is of limited accessibility,
to reach a wider audience of interest.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
reviews the simplicial semantic model for
epistemic logic and the theorem for task solvability in the model of
dynamic update of knowledge,
as introduced in
[
13
]
.
In Section
3
, we reproduce a classical impossibility result
in the DEL framework by means of logical obstruction,
showing that a simple combinatorial argument suffices
for demonstrating the obstruction proof,
without recourse to topological arguments.
In Section
4
, generalizing Nishida’s,
we provide logical obstruction formulas
to
k
-set agreement tasks for superset-closed adversaries
and provides an inductive proof on the cardinality
of adversaries.
Section
5
concludes
the paper with a summary and a discussion on
directions for future research.

2
The DEL Model for Distributed Computing

Throughout the paper,
we assume that a distributed system consists of

n+1
asynchronous processes (
n\geq 0
),
which are given unique ids.

2.1
Simplicial topology for distributed computing

A
simplicial complex
(
complex
for short)
\mathcal{C}

is a family of finite sets of vertexes, called
simplexes
, closed under set inclusion,
that is,
Y\subseteq X
and
X\in\mathcal{C}
implies
Y\in\mathcal{C}
for any pair of simplexes
X
and
Y
.
We say that a complex
\mathcal{D}
is a
subcomplex
of
\mathcal{C}
, if

\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.
A simplex
X\in\mathcal{C}
is of
dimension

n
if
{\mid}X{\mid}=n+1
.
We call a simplex
X\in\mathcal{C}
a
facet
, if
X
is
a maximal simplex in
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,

X\subseteq Y
implies
X=Y
for any
Y\in\mathcal{C}
.
A complex
\mathcal{C}
is called
pure
(of dimension
n
),
if every facet of
\mathcal{C}
has the same dimension
n
.
We write
\mathsf{V}(\mathcal{C})
for the set of all vertexes in
\mathcal{C}

and
\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})
for the set of all facets in
\mathcal{C}
.

In topological methods for
distributed computing
[
16
]
,
a simplex is used for modeling
a system state of the collection of asynchronous processes,
where each vertex stands for a state of an individual process.
A simplicial complex stands for the set of possible states of
a distributed system.
In this paper, we are solely concerned with the so-called

colored
distributed tasks. Colored tasks are modeled
by
chromatic
simplicial complex,
where the coloring function
\chi
assigns a color
\chi(v)

for each vertex
v
so that different vertexes contained in the same simplex
are distinctively colored, that is,
whenever
u,v\in X
for a simplex
X
,

\chi(u)=\chi(v)
implies
u=v
.
The coloring function models the assignment of
unique ids to individual processes.

Throughout the paper, we are solely concerned with
pure
n
-dimensional chromatic complex,
which we simply call
complexes

in the rest of the paper. A chromatic complex is formally denoted by a
pair
\langle{\mathcal{C},\chi}\rangle
. Since
a (pure chromatic) complex
\mathcal{C}

is equally defined by the set of its facets

\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})
, so that
\mathcal{C}=\{Y\mid Y\subseteq X\text{ for some }X\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})\}
,
we may also write a complex as
\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C}),\chi}\rangle
,
or even in the abridged notation
\mathcal{C}
,
leaving the coloring function
\chi
implicit.

A
simplicial map

\mu:\mathsf{V}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathsf{V}(\mathcal{D})
,
where
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are complexes,
is a vertex map such that
\mu(X)
is a simplex of
\mathcal{D}

for any simplex
X
of
\mathcal{C}
.
In addition, we postulate that
\mu
is
color-preserving
, that is,

\chi(v)=\chi(\mu(v))
for every vertex
v\in\mathsf{V}(\mathcal{C})
.
Hence
\mu
preserves the dimension of simplexes, in particular,
it maps a facet of
\mathcal{C}
to a facet of
\mathcal{D}
.
Furthermore, simplicial map commutes with intersection on simplexes,
namely,
\mu(X\cap Y)=\mu(X)\cap\mu(Y)
.

2.2
Epistemic logic and its semantics

The (multi-agent) epistemic logic
[
26
]
is a logic for
formal reasoning of knowledge of an individual agent or a group of agents.
We assume a set
\mathrm{AP}
of atomic propositions:
An atomic proposition is a propositional
symbol that states whether a certain property is qualified.
The following grammar defines the formal language of epistemic logic formulas:

\varphi::=p\mid\varphi\vee\varphi\mid\varphi\wedge\varphi\mid\neg\varphi\mid\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\varphi\mid\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}\varphi\mid\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\varphi,

where
p
ranges over
\mathrm{AP}
,

a
ranges over a finite set
\mathrm{Ag}
of agents, and

A
ranges over the powerset
2^{\mathrm{Ag}}
.
As a usual convention, we write
\mathsf{false}

for abbreviation of
p\wedge\neg p
, where
p
is an arbitrary atomic proposition.

In addition to propositional connectives, epistemic logic formulas
are equipped with
a few modal operators regarding agents’ knowledge:

\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\varphi
is the ‘knowledge’ operator for a single agent,
reading “the agent
a
knows
\varphi
”;

\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}\varphi
and

\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\varphi
are operators defined for a group
A
of agents, called
‘common knowledge’ and ’distributed knowledge’ operators, respectively.
1
1

1

There is another operator worth mentioning, called ‘group knowledge’,
which is of no use in the present paper
and is omitted.

The semantics for epistemic logic formulas
is defined over an appropriate Kripke frame.
A
Kripke frame
is formally
a pair
\langle{S,\sim}\rangle
, where
S
is
a collection of possible worlds and

\sim
is a family of equivalence relations
\{\sim_{a}\}_{a\in\mathrm{Ag}}

over
S
.
Each
\sim_{a}
is called an
indistinguishability relation
, meaning that
the worlds
X
and
Y
are not distinguishable
by the agent
a
iff
X\sim_{a}Y
.
A
Kripke model

\mathcal{M}=\langle{S,\sim,L}\rangle

is a Kripke frame along with
a function
L:S\to 2^{\mathrm{AP}}
,
which assigns a set
L(X)
of atomic propositions that are true
in each world
X\in S
.

In the following, let
\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}}
denote
the reflexive transitive closure of
\bigcup_{a\in A}\sim_{a}
,
that is,
X\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}}Y
iff

X=X_{0}\sim_{a_{0}}X_{1}\sim_{a_{1}}\cdots\sim_{a_{n}}X_{n}=Y

for some
X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in S
and
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A

(
n\geq 0
).
Further,
we define the relation
\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}
by:

X\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}Y
iff
X\sim_{a}Y
for every
a\in A
.
Then for each particular world
X
of Kripke model
\mathcal{M}
,
the semantics, or truth valuation, of an epistemic logic formula
\varphi

is given by the assertion
\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi
,
which is defined below by induction on the structure of
\varphi
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a Kripke model
\mathcal{M}=\langle{S,\sim,L}\rangle
,
the
satisfaction relation

\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi

is defined as follows, by induction on
\varphi
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},X\models p

if
p\in L(X)
;

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi\vee\psi

if
\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi
or
\mathcal{M},X\models\psi
;

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi\wedge\psi

if
\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi
and
\mathcal{M},X\models\psi
;

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},X\models\neg\varphi

if
\mathcal{M},X\not\models\varphi
;

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},X\models\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\varphi

if
\mathcal{M},Y\models\varphi
for every
Y
such that
X\sim_{a}Y
;

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},X\models\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}\varphi

if
\mathcal{M},Y\models\varphi
for every
Y
such that
X\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}}Y
;

\displaystyle\mathcal{M},X\models\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\varphi

if
\mathcal{M},Y\models\varphi
for every
Y
such that
X\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}Y
.

We write
\mathcal{M},X\not\models\varphi
to mean

\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi
does
not
hold.
A formula
\varphi
is called
valid
(in
\mathcal{M}
),
if
\mathcal{M},X\models\varphi
for every
X\in S
;

\varphi
is called
invalid
,
if
\mathcal{M},X\not\models\varphi
for some
X\in S
.

We say an epistemic formula
positive
,
if its every subformula of negated form
\neg\psi
has
no occurrences of
modal operators
\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}
,
\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}
.
For example, assuming
p,q\in\mathrm{AP}
,

\neg q\wedge\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}\neg p
is a positive formula,
while
p\vee\neg(q\vee\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}p)
is not,
because the latter contains a negated subformula
\neg(q\vee\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}p)
,
whose scope of negation embraces a knowledge operator
\mathop{\mathrm{C}_{A}}
.

2.3
Dynamic epistemic logic and its simplicial model

In
[
13
]
,
Goubault et al. have shown that
there is a tight correspondence between
Kripke frame and simplicial complex.
Restricted to
an appropriate class of Kripke models,
they are indeed the dual of each other
in a suitable category theoretic setting
[
14
]
.
In short, a simplicial complex and a Kripke model
are just different representations of the same structure:
colors correspond to agents, facets to possible worlds, and
adjacency of facets to relation over possible worlds.

In the rest of the paper,

\Pi=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
is assumed to be
the set of unique ids given to
n+1
individual processes.
We also write
\mathit{Value}
to
denote the set of possible local values held by the processes.
For convenience, we may often denote a colored vertex
in a simplicial complex by a pair
(a,v)\in\Pi\times\mathit{Value}
,
which is intended to mean a process of id
a
that holds a local value
v
.
The coloring function is defined by
\chi((a,v))=a
.

2.3.1
Simplicial model induced by complex

In this paper, we assume the set of atomic propositions is
given by
\mathrm{AP}=\{\mathsf{input}_{a}^{v}\mid a\in\Pi,v\in\mathit{Value}\}
,
where
each atomic proposition
\mathsf{input}_{a}^{v}
is intended to assert that
the value
v
is held by the agent
a
(that is, the process whose id is
a
).

The Kripke model that is dual to a given complex
\mathcal{C}=\langle{\mathcal{C},\chi}\rangle

is induced as below.

Definition 2.2

(Simplicial model)
.

For any complex
\mathcal{C}
, we can induce a Kripke model

\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C}),\sim,L}\rangle
such that:

•

the set of agents is taken as the set of colors, i.e.,

\mathrm{Ag}=\Pi
,

•

the set of possible worlds are the set of facets
\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})
,

•

the equivalence relation is defined by:

X\sim_{a}Y\text{ iff }a\in\chi(X\cap Y),\text{ where $X,Y\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})$ and $a\in\mathrm{Ag}$, and}

•

L(X)=\{\mathsf{input}_{a}^{v}\mid(a,v)\in X\}
.

We call this induced Kripke model a
simplicial model
.
In abuse of notation,
we also write

\mathcal{C}
for the simplicial model
\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C}),\sim,L}\rangle
.

As for positive epistemic formulas,
it can be shown that the epistemic knowledge never increases along with
an appropriate simplicial map.

Theorem 2.1

(Knowledge gain
[
13
]
)
.

Suppose we are given a pair of simplicial models

\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C}),\sim^{\mathcal{C}},L^{\mathcal{C}}}\rangle
and

\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{D}),\sim^{\mathcal{D}},L^{\mathcal{D}}}\rangle
that are
induced from complexes
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, respectively.
We call a function
\delta:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}

a
morphism
, if it
is a color-preserving simplicial map from
\mathsf{V}(\mathcal{C})
to

\mathsf{V}(\mathcal{D})
such that

L^{\mathcal{D}}(\delta(X))=L^{\mathcal{C}}(X)
for every
X\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})
.

For any morphism
\delta:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and any positive formula
\varphi
,

\mathcal{D},\delta(X)\models\varphi
implies
\mathcal{C},X\models\varphi
,
for every facet
X\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Proof is by induction on
\varphi
. We only examine a few cases that
have not been demonstrated in
[
13
]
.

To prove the case of disjunction
\varphi\vee\psi
,
suppose
\mathcal{D},\delta(X)\models\varphi\vee\psi
.
Without loss of generality,
we may assume
\mathcal{D},\delta(X)\models\varphi
. By induction hypothesis,

\mathcal{C},X\models\varphi
and thus we have
\mathcal{C},X\models\varphi\vee\psi
.

For the case of distributed knowledge operator,
suppose
\mathcal{D},\delta(X)\models\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\varphi
.
In order to show
\mathcal{C},X\models\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\varphi
,
we assume
Y\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})
is an arbitrary facet
such that
X\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}Y
, i.e.,
A\subseteq\chi(X\cap Y)
,
and show
\mathcal{C},Y\models\varphi
.
Since
\delta
is a color-preserving simplicial map,

A\subseteq\chi(X\cap Y)=\chi(\delta(X\cap Y))=\chi(\delta(X)\cap\delta(Y))
, which means

\delta(X)\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}\delta(Y)
. By the assumption
\mathcal{D},\delta(X)\models\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\varphi
, we have

\mathcal{D},\delta(Y)\models\varphi
; hence
\mathcal{C},Y\models\varphi

by induction hypothesis.
∎

From a topological perspective,
it can be understood that
the knowledge gain theorem stems from the fact
that, for any morphism
\delta
,
a positive epistemic formula is less likely to hold in

\delta(\mathcal{D})
, which is a more densely connected model than
\mathcal{C}
.
Thus we can make use of positive epistemic formulas for detecting
discrepancy in connectivity of
simplicial models, as we will demonstrate in later sections.

2.3.2
The action model and product update

The dynamic epistemic logic (DEL)
[
26
]
is an
epistemic logic with possible updates in the knowledge model.
Following
[
13
]
,
we make use of
product update model
generated by
epistemic actions
[
2
,
26
]
for modeling
the change in the knowledge structure incurred by
an action of information exchange among communicating processes.

To define product update model, let us first
define the cartesian product of simplicial complexes.
Suppose
\langle{\mathcal{C},\chi_{\mathcal{C}}}\rangle

and
\langle{\mathcal{D},\chi_{\mathcal{D}}}\rangle
are complexes.
For each pair of simplexes
X\in\mathcal{C}
and
Y\in\mathcal{D}

such that
\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(X)=\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(Y)
,
we define cartesian product
X\times Y
as a simplex whose each vertex
is a pair of vertexes of matching color from original simplexes, namely,

\displaystyle X\times Y

\displaystyle{}=\{(u,v)\mid u\in X,v\in Y,\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(u)=\chi_{\mathcal{D}}(v)\}.

The coloring on vertexes in the cartesian product inherits that on
the original ones, namely,

\chi((u,v))=\chi_{\mathcal{C}}(u)
.
The cartesian product
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
of complexes
of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
is then defined by

\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D})=\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C})\times\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{D})=\{X\times Y\mid X\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{C}),Y\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{D})\}
.

We can define the pair of projection maps

\pi_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
and

\pi_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}

by
\pi_{\mathcal{C}}((u,v))=u
and

\pi_{\mathcal{D}}((u,v))=v
for every
(u,v)\in\mathsf{V}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D})
,
respectively.
They are both color-preserving simplicial maps.

Definition 2.3

(Simplicial action model and product update)
.

A
simplicial action model

\mathcal{D}=\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{D}),\sim^{\mathcal{D}},\mathsf{pre}}\rangle

is an action model, where the possible worlds are facets of some complex
\mathcal{D}

and
\sim^{\mathcal{D}}
is the family of relations
\{\sim^{\mathcal{D}}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathrm{Ag}}

induced from
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.,
X\sim^{\mathcal{D}}_{a}Y
iff

a\in\chi(X\cap Y)
, where
a\in\mathrm{Ag}

and
X,Y\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{D})
,
and
\mathsf{pre}
is a function that assigns
an epistemic logic formula, called a
precondition
,
to each facet
X
of
\mathcal{D}
.

The
product update model
of
an initial simplicial model
\mathcal{C}=\langle{\mathcal{C},\sim^{\mathcal{C}},L}\rangle

by an action model
\mathcal{D}=\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{D}),\sim^{\mathcal{D}},\mathsf{pre}}\rangle

is a simplicial model
\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{D}]=\langle{\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{D}],\sim,L^{\prime}}\rangle
,
where

\langle{\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{D}],\sim}\rangle

is the Kripke frame induced from
the complex

\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{D}]=\{Z\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\mid\mathcal{C},\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(Z)\models\mathsf{pre}(\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(Z))\}

and
L^{\prime}(Z)=L(\pi_{\mathcal{C}}(Z))

for every
Z\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
.

The product update model provides an alternative way for specifying
distributed tasks. In the topological setting, a task is modeled by
a carrier map
\mathcal{C}\to 2^{\mathcal{D}}
, which
maps each input simplex in
\mathcal{C}
to a subcomplex
\mathcal{D}
consisting of
possible output simplexes
[
16
]
.
The product update model
\mathcal{C}[\mathcal{D}]
encodes the carrier map
as a subset of the cartesian product
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
,
where
\mathcal{C},X\models\mathsf{pre}(Y)

determines how an input simplex
X\in\mathcal{C}
maps to an output simplex
Y\in\mathcal{D}
.

This definition of task solvability by product update

]
.
(The cartesian product of complexes is indeed a categorical product,

[
14
]
)

In the rest of this paper,

as for product update models of protocols.

The product update model

For brevity, we write

By the task solvability definition in DEL (Definition
2.4
)

Theorem 3.1

(
[
13
]

Let
\mathcal{I}

\mathcal{C}
be a communicative action model for a protocol, and

\mathcal{T}

but

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{C}]\not\models\varphi
; That is,

\varphi
holds in every world
of
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{T}]
but

\varphi
is falsified for some world in
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{C}]
.

The positive epistemic formula

to the solvability of the task
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{T}]

for the protocol
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{C}]
.

As a demonstration of impossibility proof by means of logical obstruction,
we examine the wait-free distributed binary consensus task
(i.e., 1-set agreement task with exactly 2 possible initial input values).
The well-known fact that the binary consensus task is not wait-free solvable
in the read-write shared-memory model

15

[

1

Here we demonstrate that this well-known result is also reproduced

As Goubault et al.
[

The input to the task has a connected combinatorial structure,
but the output does not.
Here we give a concrete logical obstruction formula in the formal language of
epistemic logic and thereby

without recourse to topological infrastructure.

We assume
\Pi=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}

In

Definition 4.1

We call a nonempty subset

2
2

2

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

U
.
Then there exists a permutation subset
A
for
f
.

Proof.

Trivially,
f
is a monotonic function over
2^{U}
, that is,

A\subseteq A^{\prime}
implies
f(A)\subseteq f(A^{\prime})
.
By the monotonicity, it hollows that
f^{i}(U)\supseteq f^{i+1}(U)\neq\emptyset

from
U\supseteq f(U)
by induction.
Since
U
is finite, there exists
m
such that
f^{i}(U)=f^{i+1}(U)\neq\emptyset

for every
i\geq m
. This implies that the set
A=\bigcap_{i=0}^{\infty}f^{i}(U)

is a nonempty fixed point, i.e.,
f(A)=A
.

A
is indeed the greatest fixed point of
f
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of this lemma,

\mathit{Output}_{X}:\Pi\to\Pi
has a permutation subset
for any facet
X
of
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]
.

Below we give Nishida’s logical obstruction
\Phi

to the wait-free
k
-set agreement task:

\Phi~\triangleq~\bigvee_{a=0}^{n}\neg\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}~\vee~\bigvee_{m=1}^{k}\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq\Pi\\
{\mid}A{\mid}=m\end{subarray}}\Psi^{(m)}_{A},

where
\Psi^{(m)}_{A}
’s are a class of formulas such that

A\subseteq\Pi
and
{\mid}A{\mid}=m
that is defined inductively as follows:

\Psi^{(m)}_{A}~\triangleq~\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\left(\bigvee_{a\in\Pi\setminus A}\!\neg\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}~\vee\bigvee_{a\in\Pi\setminus A}\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\Biggl(\bigvee_{j\in A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{j}\Biggr)\vee\bigvee_{i=m+1}^{n}\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq\Pi\setminus A\\
{\mid}B{\mid}=i-m\end{subarray}}\Psi^{(i)}_{A\cup B}\right).

We refrain from repeating his original proof herein, because
in the rest of this section we provide a generalized form of this
logical obstruction for superset-closed adversaries.
By the appropriate level of generalization,
our presentation is much clearer and more succinct than the original one.

4.2
Logical obstruction to adversarial set agreement task

We study the unsolvability of set agreement tasks under adversary schedulers,
generalizing the basic wait-free scheduler to the ones
that allow nonuniform failures.

We model such an adversary scheduler by a set
\mathcal{A}
,
called an
adversary

[
10
]
,
of possible subsets of correct processes,
that is,
\mathcal{A}
is a nonempty subset of
2^{\Pi}

such that
\emptyset\not\in\mathcal{A}
.
An adversary
\mathcal{A}
is called
superset-closed
, if

P\in\mathcal{A}
and
P\subseteq P^{\prime}\subseteq\Pi
implies
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
.
In this paper, we are solely concerned with the superset-closed adversaries
and a superset-closed adversary is simply called an adversary.

An adversary
\mathcal{A}
has two different characterizations by
survivor sets and cores
[
19
]
.
An adversary
\mathcal{A}
can be specified by survivor sets,
where a
survivor set

S
is a minimal set contained in
\mathcal{A}
,
that is,
S\supsetneq P
for none of
P\in\mathcal{A}
;
Dually, an adversary
\mathcal{A}
can be specified by cores,
where a
core

C
is a minimal subset of
\Pi

satisfying
C\cap P\neq\emptyset
for every
P\in\mathcal{A}
.
Remark that the wait-free scheduler is just an instance of
(uniform) adversary that is characterized by survivor sets

\{\{a\}\mid a\in\Pi\}
.

We write
\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})
to denote the
minimum core size

of an adversary
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
the minimum cardinality of the cores defined by

\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})=\min\{\,{\mid}C{\mid}\mid\text{$C$ is a core of $\mathcal{A}$}\}
.

Following
[
17
]
,
we model a single round
execution of distributed processes against a given adversary
\mathcal{A}

by a protocol specified by the
round operator

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
For each facet
X\in\mathcal{I}
, which corresponds to an initial
configuration of inputs to processes,
the round operator
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
associates
an output facet
\{(a,\mathit{view}_{a})\mid a\in\Pi\}
,
where each
\mathit{view}_{a}
is a subset of
X

assigned to the
a
-colored process
and the set of views satisfy the following properties:

(survival)

For each
a\in\Pi
,

\mathit{view}_{a}
is a subset of
X

that subsumes a survivor set, i.e.,
\chi(\mathit{view}_{a})\in\mathcal{A}
;

(self-inclusion)

For each
a\in\Pi
,
a\in\chi(\mathit{view}_{a})
;

(containment)

Either
\mathit{view}_{a}\subseteq\mathit{view}_{a^{\prime}}

or
\mathit{view}_{a}\supseteq\mathit{view}_{a^{\prime}}
holds, for every
a,a^{\prime}\in\Pi
.

Note that the wait-free immediate snapshot is
further required to satisfy the immediacy condition:
For every
a,a^{\prime}\in\Pi
,

\mathit{view}_{a^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathit{view}_{a}
holds
whenever
a^{\prime}\in\chi(\mathit{view}_{a})
.
Missing the immediacy condition,
the underlying simplicial structure of the round operator is more involved in
general. Even under the wait-free adversary, the round operator
results in a non-manifold, as studied in
[
3
]
.
To cope with this extra complexity,
our impossibility proof refines Nishida’s original one.
Specifically we need to show an additional property, as stated in lemma
4.2
.

We define the round operator
as the product update model
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}]
,
where, in abuse of notation,

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}
denotes
the uniform communicative action model

\langle{\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}),\sim^{\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}},\mathsf{pre}}\rangle

such that:

•

Each facet in
\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}})
is denoted
by
{X}^{\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle}
,
where
X\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{I})
and
the vector
\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle
of subsets of
\Pi

satisfies:

–

for each
a\in\Pi
,
S_{a}
subsumes a survivor set, i.e.,
S_{a}\in\mathcal{A}
,

–

for each
a\in\Pi
,
a\in S_{a}
, and

–

either
S_{a}\subseteq S_{a^{\prime}}
or
S_{a}\supseteq S_{a^{\prime}}
holds,
for every
a,a^{\prime}\in\Pi
;

•

The preconditions are given as in
the definition of uniform action models in Section
2.4
.

In the following, we may occasionally write
\vec{S}
to denote the vector

\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle
for brevity.

For a facet
{X}^{\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle}
,
let us define a pair of functions

\mathit{Input}_{{X}^{\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle}}

and

\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle}}
by

\mathit{Input}_{{X}^{\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle}}(a)=v
if and only if

(a,v)\in X
and also by

\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\langle{S_{0},\ldots,S_{n}}\rangle}}(a)=S_{a}
, respectively.
Then,
{X}^{\vec{S}}\sim^{\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}]}_{a}{Y}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}
holds iff

\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\vec{S}}}(a)=\mathit{View}_{{Y}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}}(a)

and

\mathit{Input}_{{X}^{\vec{S}}}(a^{\prime})=\mathit{Input}_{{Y}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}}(a^{\prime})

for every
a^{\prime}\in\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\vec{S}}}(a)
.
In particular,

{X}^{\vec{S}}\sim^{\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}]}_{a}{X}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}
iff

\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\vec{S}}}(a)=\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}}(a)
.

Assuming an adversary
\mathcal{A}
is fixed,
we define a class of positive epistemic formulas indexed by
A\in 2^{\Pi}
:

\displaystyle\Psi_{A}

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{cases}\mathsf{false}&\text{if no survivor set is subsumed by $\Pi\setminus A$;}\\
\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\psi_{A}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\psi_{A}
’s are formulas given below

\displaystyle\psi_{A}

\displaystyle\triangleq\bigvee_{a\in\Pi\setminus A}\neg\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}~\vee\bigvee_{a\in\Pi\setminus A}\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\Biggl(\bigvee_{j\in A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{j}\Biggl)\vee\bigvee_{B\supsetneq A}\Psi_{B},

defined by
induction on
A
, ordered by inverse set inclusion
(with
\psi_{\Pi}
being the base case of induction).

In what follows,
we claim that the following formula is the logical obstruction
against the adversary
\mathcal{A}
:

\Phi\triangleq\bigvee_{a\in\Pi}\neg\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}~\vee\bigvee_{A\in[\Pi]^{<c}}\Psi_{A},

where
c=\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})
and

[\Pi]^{<c}=\{A\subseteq\Pi\mid 0<{\mid}A{\mid}<c\}
.

Let us write
\mathit{minView}({X}^{\vec{S}})
to denote
the minimum of the views of a facet
{X}^{\vec{S}}\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}])
,
i.e.,
\mathit{minView}({X}^{\vec{S}})=\bigcap_{a\in\Pi}\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\vec{S}}}(a)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose a facet
{X}^{\vec{S}}\in\mathsf{F}(\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}])
satisfies

(i)

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\models\bigwedge_{a\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}
, and

(ii)

for any
a\in\mathit{minView}({X}^{\vec{S}})
,

\mathit{View}_{{X}^{\vec{S}}}(a)=\mathit{minView}({X}^{\vec{S}})
.

Then we have
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\psi_{\Pi\setminus\mathit{minView}({X}^{\vec{S}})}
.

Proof.

Let us write
A
for the minimum view
\mathit{minView}({X}^{\vec{S}})
.
Note that
A
subsumes a survivor set.

We proceed the proof by induction on
A
.
Let us consider the case that
A
is exactly a survivor set.
For any
B
that is a proper superset of
\Pi\setminus A
,

\Pi\setminus B
is a proper subset of
A

and hence contains no survivor set. Thus
by the assumption (
i
),

\psi_{\Pi\setminus A}
is logically equivalent to

\xi_{A}\equiv\bigvee_{a\in A}\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\Bigl(\bigvee_{j\in\Pi\setminus A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{j}\Bigr)
.
We may suppose
A\neq\Pi
, because

\xi_{A}
does not hold otherwise.
To see
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\xi_{A}
,
it suffices to show that there is a facet
{Y}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}

satisfying
{Y}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}\sim_{a}{X}^{\vec{S}}
for every
a\in A
and also

\mathit{Input}_{{Y}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}}(a^{\prime})\in A
for every
a^{\prime}\in\Pi
.
Take
k\in A
arbitrarily.
Then such a facet is instantiated by
{Y}^{\langle{S_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,S_{n}^{\prime}}\rangle}

where
Y=\{(a,a)\mid a\in A\}\cup\{(a,k)\mid a\in\Pi\setminus A\}
,

S_{a}^{\prime}=S_{a}=A
if
a\in A
, and
S_{a}^{\prime}=\Pi
if
a\in\Pi\setminus A
.

Let us then consider the case
A
is a proper superset of a survivor set.
Similarly as above, we can prove

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\bigvee_{i\in A}\neg\mathsf{input}_{i}^{i}~\vee\bigvee_{i\in A}\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{i}}\Bigl(\bigvee_{j\in\Pi\setminus A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{j}\Bigr)
.
Hence it suffices to show that
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\Psi_{\Pi\setminus B}
for every
proper subset
B
of
A
.
For such a proper subset
B
,
let us define
\vec{S^{\prime}}
by

S^{\prime}_{a}=B
if
a\in B
and otherwise
S^{\prime}_{a}=S_{a}
.
Then, the properties (
i
) and (
ii
)
are held by the facet
{X}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}

and hence by induction hypothesis we have

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}\not\models\psi_{\Pi\setminus B}
.
Since
{X}^{\vec{S}}\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{\Pi\setminus B}}}{X}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}
,
we conclude that

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{\Pi\setminus B}}\psi_{\Pi\setminus B}
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}]\not\models\Phi
.

Proof.

Let
{X}^{\vec{S}}
be the facet of
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}]

such that
X=\{(a,a)\mid a\in\Pi\}
and

S_{a}=\Pi
for every
a\in\Pi
.
We show
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\Phi
.
Clearly

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\bigvee_{a\in\Pi}\neg\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}

and thus it suffices to show
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\bigvee_{A\in[\Pi]^{<c}}\Psi_{A}
.
Take any
A\in[\Pi]^{<c}
arbitrarily.
Since
{\mid}A{\mid}<c
,
A
does not subsume a core and thus

A\cap S=\emptyset
for some survivor set
S\in\mathcal{A}
.
This survivor set
S
is subsumed by
\Pi\setminus A

and hence
we are obliged to show
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\models\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\psi_{A}
.

Let us define
\vec{S^{\prime}}
by
S^{\prime}_{a}=\Pi\setminus A
if
a\in\Pi\setminus A

and
S^{\prime}_{a}=\Pi
otherwise.
Then

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}\not\models\psi_{A}
follows from
lemma
4.2
. Since
{X}^{\vec{S^{\prime}}}\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}{X}^{\vec{S}}
,
we have
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\Psi_{A}
. As we have taken

A\in[\Pi]^{<c}
arbitrarily,
this concludes
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}],{X}^{\vec{S}}\not\models\bigvee_{A\in[\Pi]^{<c}}\Psi_{A}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
X\times\vec{d}
is a facet of
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]
.
Then,
for every

a\in\Pi
and
A\subseteq\Pi
,

\mathit{Output}_{X\times\vec{d}}(a)\in A
implies

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],X\times\vec{d}\models\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\bigl(\bigvee_{i\in A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{i}\bigr)
.

Proof.

Let
Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}
be any facet such that

X\times\vec{d}\sim_{a}^{\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]}Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}
.
Then,
\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(a)=\mathit{Output}_{X\times\vec{d}}(a)\in A
.
Since
\mathit{Input}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(\Pi)\supseteq\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(\Pi)
,
it follows that
\mathit{Input}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(a^{\prime})\in A

for some
a^{\prime}\in\Pi
. This implies

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\bigvee_{i\in A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{i}
.
Hence we are done.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be any adversary.
Then,

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]\models\Phi
holds for every
k<\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Let
k<\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})
and

X\times\vec{d}
be any facet of
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]
.
We will show
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],X\times\vec{d}\models\Phi
.
We may suppose
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],X\times\vec{d}\not\models\bigvee_{a\in\Pi}\neg\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}
,
because
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],X\times\vec{d}\models\Phi
immediately
holds otherwise.
By lemma
4.1
, the function
\mathit{Output}_{X\times\vec{d}}

has a permutation subset
A
.
Since
{\mid}A{\mid}\leq\max\{{\mid}\mathit{Output}_{X\times\vec{d}}(Z){\mid}\mid Z\subseteq\Pi\}\leq k<\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})
,
it suffices to show that

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],X\times\vec{d}\models\Psi_{A}

for this permutation subset
A
.
Since
{\mid}A{\mid}<\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})
, by a similar discussion in the proof
of Proposition
4.3
,
we are obliged to show
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],X\times\vec{d}\models\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}\psi_{A}
.

We proceed by induction
on the size of
\Pi\setminus A
.
First, consider the base case
{\mid}A{\mid}=k
.
Let us show that
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\psi_{A}
holds
for any facet
Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}
such that

Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}^{\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]}X\times\vec{d}
.
Since
A=\mathit{Output}_{X\times\vec{d}}(A)=\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(A)\subseteq\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(\Pi)

and
{\mid}\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(\Pi){\mid}\leq k
,
we have
\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(\Pi)\subseteq A
.
Hence by lemma
4.4
,

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\bigl(\bigvee_{i\in A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{i}\bigr)

holds for every
a\in\Pi
.
This entails
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\psi_{A}
.

Next, consider the case
{\mid}A{\mid}<k
. Again,
let us show that
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\psi_{A}

holds for any facet
Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}
such that

Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\sim_{\mathop{\mathrm{D}_{A}}}^{\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]}X\times\vec{d}
.
Since
\mathit{Output}_{X\times\vec{d}}
has
A
as a permutation subset,
so does
\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}
.
We will show that
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\bigvee_{B\supsetneq A}\Psi_{B}
holds,
assuming

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\not\models\bigvee_{a\in\Pi\setminus A}\neg\mathsf{input}_{a}^{a}~\vee\bigvee_{a\in\Pi\setminus A}\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\bigl(\bigvee_{j\in A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{j}\bigr)
.
It follows from

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\not\models\bigvee_{a\in\Pi\setminus A}\mathop{\mathrm{K}_{a}}\bigl(\bigvee_{j\in A}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\Pi}\mathsf{input}_{a^{\prime}}^{j}\bigr)

that
\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(\Pi\setminus A)\subseteq\Pi\setminus A
by lemma
4.4
.
Thus by lemma
4.1
,

\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}
has a permutation subset
A^{\prime}
,
that is,
\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}(A^{\prime})=A^{\prime}\subseteq\Pi\setminus A
.
Therefore
A\cup A^{\prime}
is a
proper superset of
A
and is also a permutation subset for

\mathit{Output}_{Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}}
.
By induction hypothesis we have

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\Psi_{A\cup A^{\prime}}
,
which entails
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}],Y\times\vec{d^{\prime}}\models\bigvee_{B\supsetneq A}\Psi_{B}
.
∎

By proposition
4.3
and
4.5
,

\Phi
is a logical obstruction and hence the
following impossibility result follows.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an adversary. If
k<\mathit{csize}(\mathcal{A})
,
the
k
-set agreement task
\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{SA}_{k}]

is not solvable the protocol

\mathcal{I}[\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{A}}]
,
a single round of the round operator.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

We have applied the logical method
developed by Goubault, Ledent, and Rajsbaum
[
13
]

to show the impossibility of the set agreement tasks for
superset-closed adversaries,
by giving concrete logical obstruction formulas, which are
generalization of Nishida’s logical obstruction for the wait-free model
[
24
]
.
The method based on logical obstruction allows an
elementary inductive proof, without recourse to
sophisticated topological tools.
The instances of logical obstruction
exemplify that logical method
would serve as a feasible alternative to topological method.

There are several topics to pursue that merit further investigation.
First, it is quite interesting what varieties of impossibility
can be proven by devising concrete logical obstruction formulas.
To date, the topological method has been extensively applied to show
impossibility results
and still keep expanding its application area,
e.g., network computing
[
7
,
9
]
.
Finding concrete logical obstruction formulas to
these particular instances would be an interesting topic of its own right.

We would also expect that logical obstruction could
give a deeper logical understanding on the nature of unsolvability
of distributed tasks.
As observed in
[
12
]
,
the original logical framework of
[
13
]
,
whose atomic propositions are allowed to mention input values only,
cannot refute the solvability of the equality negation task
[
23
]
,
while an extended framework that allows atomic propositions to mention
output decision values has logical obstruction.
This seems to suggest that solvability of distributed tasks could be
classified in terms of expressibility of epistemic logic.

As we have mentioned in Introduction,
this paper solely concerns single round protocols,
leaving multiple round protocols for future investigation.
In principle we are able to discuss multiple round protocols in the epistemic
logic setting, but we would need
to work on a different epistemic model for each incremental round step.
It would be a challenging topic to give a remedy for this
by providing, say, a series of obstruction formulas
indexed by the number of round steps.

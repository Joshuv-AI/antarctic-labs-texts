1.
Introduction

The Hilbert-Pólya approach to the Riemann Hypothesis is a quest for an anti-Hermitian operator whose eigenvalues
\lambda
correspond to zeros
{s}=\lambda+\frac{1}{2}
of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta({s})
. Berry and Keating
[
1
,
2
]
matched the asymptotic distribution of eigenvalues of the requisite operator (if it exists) with a semiclassical calculation of the phase-space area (with an appropriate cut-off) for the dilatation operator
x\frac{d}{dx}
, and Bender, Brody and Müller
[
3
]
constructed an explicit operator, albeit not anti-Hermitian, with the requisite spectrum. Operators related to dilatation, acting on various spaces of functions, were also proposed in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
(See
[
10
,
11
]
for a recent review of additional related approaches.)
Connections between the Riemann Hypothesis and hermiticity or unitarity in higher dimensional quantum systems have also recently been proposed in
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
, and a connection between Robin’s criterion and bounds on multiplicities of states in certain gauge theories was suggested in
[
15
]
.)

The goal of this paper is to present an alternative, perhaps simpler, linear criterion for
{s}
to be a zero of
\zeta({s})
in terms of existence of a solution to a sequence of linear equations in the Banach space
{{\ell}}^{p}
(for any
1<p<\infty
).

We will show that for fixed

(and

), if

then

if and only if

One way to arrive at (

1.3.
Motivation

The
{{u}}_{{k}}
’s that solve (
1.2
) are defined as follows. First, instead of the Riemann zeta function, it is more convenient to work with the Dirichlet eta function,

(1.6)

\eta({s})=(1-2^{1-{s}})\zeta({s}),

and zeros of
\zeta({s})
correspond to zeros of
\eta({s})
.
For
\Re{s}>0
, we have
\eta({s})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^{s}}
.
Next, for
{s}\in\mathbb{C}
that is not a positive odd integer, define (the umbral operator)
{{\mathfrak{F}_{{{s}}}}}:\mathbb{C}[x]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

{{\mathfrak{F}_{{{s}}}}}({{\mathbf{P}}}){\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\,}\sum_{j=0}^{n}{{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}\eta({s}-2j)\qquad\text{for a polynomial ${{\mathbf{P}}}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}x^{j}$.}

{{\mathfrak{F}_{{{s}}}}}({{\mathbf{P}}})
can be regarded as a regularized version of the formal, divergent, expression “
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}n^{-{s}}{{\mathbf{P}}}(n^{2})
”, whereby we replace the formal sum “
\sum(-1)^{n+1}n^{2j-{s}}
” [that would appear when expanding “
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}n^{-{s}}{{\mathbf{P}}}(n^{2})
”] with
\eta({s}-2j)

We then define an inner product on
\mathbb{C}[x]
by

(1.7)

{\left\langle{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1}},{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{2}}\right\rangle}{\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\,}{{\mathfrak{F}_{{-1}}}}({{\mathbf{P}}}_{1}\overline{{{\mathbf{P}}}}_{2}),\qquad\text{for ${{\mathbf{P}}}_{1},{{\mathbf{P}}}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}[x]$.}

This turns out to be a positive definite inner product, as will be shown in §
2

Next, we identify a series of orthogonal polynomials
({{\mathbf{Q}}}_{{k}})_{{{k}}=0}^{\infty}
with respect to
{\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle}

satisfies (

The proof of Theorem

2.
The inner product

Let
{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}x^{j}
and
{{\mathbf{P}}}_{2}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}{{\mathbf{b}}}_{j}x^{j}
be polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[x]
. Equation (
1.7
) defines an inner product

(2.1)

{\left\langle{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1}},{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{2}}\right\rangle}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\eta(-1-2j-2k){{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}\overline{{{\mathbf{b}}}}_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n+m}\frac{(4^{1+k}-1){{\mathbf{B}}}_{2k+2}}{2k+2}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}{{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}\overline{{{\mathbf{b}}}}_{k-j}\right)

where
{{\mathbf{B}}}_{n}
is a Bernoulli number.

Let

{\widetilde{\Phi}}_{1}(x,{{t}})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1,k}(x){{t}}^{k},\qquad{\widetilde{\Phi}}_{2}(x,{{t}})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{{\mathbf{P}}}_{2,k}(x){{t}}^{k}

be formal power series in
{{t}}
with coefficients that are polynomials in

. We define

{\left\langle{{\widetilde{\Phi}}_{1}(x,{{t}}_{1})},{{\widetilde{\Phi}}_{2}(x,{{t}}_{2})}\right\rangle}{\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\,}\sum_{k,m=0}^{\infty}{\left\langle{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1,k}},{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1,m}}\right\rangle}{{t}}_{1}^{k}{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}^{m}\in\mathbb{C}[[{{t}}_{1},{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}]].

Lemma 2.1
.

Viewing
\cosh(\sqrt{x}{{t}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n)!}x^{n}{{t}}^{2n}
as a formal power series in
\mathbb{C}[x][[{{t}}]]
, we have the identity

(2.2)

{\left\langle{\cosh(\sqrt{x}{{t}}_{1})},{\cosh(\sqrt{x}{{t}}_{2})}\right\rangle}=\frac{1+\cosh{{t}}_{1}\cosh{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}}{(\cosh{{t}}_{1}+\cosh{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2})^{2}}

Proof.

\displaystyle{\left\langle{\cosh(\sqrt{x}{{t}}_{1})},{\cosh(\sqrt{x}{{t}}_{2})}\right\rangle}=\sum_{k,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{{{t}}_{1}^{2k}{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}^{2m}}{(2k)!(2m)!}{\left\langle{x^{k}},{x^{m}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{{{t}}_{1}^{2k}{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}^{2m}}{(2k)!(2m)!}\frac{(4^{1+k+m}-1){{\mathbf{B}}}_{2k+2m+2}}{2k+2m+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(4^{1+n}-1){{\mathbf{B}}}_{2n+2}}{2(2n+2)(2n)!}\left[({{t}}_{1}+{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2})^{2n}+({{t}}_{1}-{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2})^{2n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\cosh^{2}(\frac{{{t}}_{1}+{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}}{2})}+\frac{1}{8\cosh^{2}(\frac{{{t}}_{1}-{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}}{2})}=\frac{1+\cosh{{t}}_{1}\cosh{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2}}{2(\cosh{{t}}_{1}+\cosh{\overline{{{t}}}}_{2})^{2}}\,.

∎

An integral representation for the inner product is provided as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1}(x)
and
{{\mathbf{P}}}_{2}(x)
be polynomials. Then,

(2.3)

{\left\langle{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1}},{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{2}}\right\rangle}=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{{{\mathbf{P}}}_{1}(x)\overline{{{\mathbf{P}}}}_{2}(x)dx}{2\sinh\left(\pi\sqrt{-x}\right)}

Proof.

Using a well-known identity for the integral of

(see, e.g., §25.5.8 of
[
24
]
), as well as the definition of the Dirichlet eta function (
1.6
), and the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function, we can write for

,

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-{s}}dx}{\sinh x}=2(2^{{s}-1}-1)\Gamma(1-{s})\zeta(1-{s})=-\frac{\pi^{1-{s}}\eta({s})}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi{s}}{2}\right)}\,.

Thus, for integer

,

\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{x^{{{j}}+{{k}}}dx}{2\sinh\left(\pi\sqrt{-x}\right)}=\eta(-1-2{{j}}-2{{k}})={\left\langle{x^{{j}}},{x^{{k}}}\right\rangle}.

Equation (
2.3

∎

3.
Orthogonal polynomials

Given the inner product (
2.1

Definition 3.1
.

The polynomials
{\left({{\mathbf{Q}}}_{{k}}\right)_{{{k}}=0}^{\infty}}
are defined inductively, starting with
{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{0}(x)=1
, by the following three conditions (for

(a)

\deg{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{{k}}={{k}}
;

(b)

{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{{k}}(0)=1

(c)

{\left\langle{{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{{k}}},{{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{{j}}}\right\rangle}=0

For example, the first four are

\begin{array}[]{ll}{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{0}(x)=1,&{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{1}(x)=1+2x,\\
{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{2}(x)=1+\tfrac{10}{3}x+\tfrac{2}{3}x^{2},&{{\mathbf{Q}}}_{3}(x)=1+\tfrac{196}{45}x+\tfrac{14}{9}x^{2}+\tfrac{4}{45}x^{3}.\\
\end{array}

The polynomials

(3.1)

Given the generating function (
1.9

Proposition 5.3
.

Let

be in the range

Suppose also that

) (which is convergent by our assumptions) holds, then

Proof.

Let
{{\ell}}^{q}
be the dual Banach space with

. Let
{\left({{w}}_{{{k}}}\right)_{{{k}}=1}^{\infty}}
be as in Proposition

. Then (

By (
1.13

These relations were formally derived by linear operations on the columns of the matrix in (

), and the relation for
{{k}}
corresponds to the
{{k}}^{th}
row of that matrix. We will now take linear combinations of these expressions with coefficients
{{w}}_{{k}}
.
Consider the sum

(5.43)

0=\sum_{{{k}}=1}^{\infty}{{w}}_{{k}}\left[-\frac{{s}}{2{{k}}-1}\sum_{{{n}}=0}^{{{k}}-2}{{v}}_{{n}}+\sum_{{{n}}=0}^{\infty}\frac{{{v}}_{{{k}}+{{n}}-1}}{2{{n}}+1}\right].

We first need to show that the RHS of (
5.43
) is absolutely convergent since we will need to rearrange terms. Note that (
1.13
) is equivalent to (
1.1
), and the
{s}
-dependent matrix there can be decomposed into upper and lower triangular matrices and written as
{{{B}}}-{s}{{{W}}}

are constant matrices given by:

(5.44)

{{{W}}}{\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\,}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0&0&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
\frac{1}{3}&0&0&0&0&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
\frac{1}{5}&\frac{1}{5}&0&0&0&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
\frac{1}{7}&\frac{1}{7}&\frac{1}{7}&0&0&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
\frac{1}{9}&\frac{1}{9}&\frac{1}{9}&\frac{1}{9}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad{{{B}}}{\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\,}\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{3}&\frac{1}{5}&\frac{1}{7}&\frac{1}{9}&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
0&1&\frac{1}{3}&\frac{1}{5}&\frac{1}{7}&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
0&0&1&\frac{1}{3}&\frac{1}{5}&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
0&0&0&1&\frac{1}{3}&\cdots\\
&&&&&\\
0&0&0&0&1&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\end{pmatrix}\,.

It is a consequence of Hardy’s inequality, which puts a bound on the norm of the operator defined by
\left({{v}}_{{n}}\right)_{{{n}}=1}^{\infty}\mapsto\left(\frac{1}{{{n}}}\sum_{{{k}}=1}^{{n}}{{v}}_{{k}}\right)_{{{n}}=1}^{\infty}

(see for instance

and references therein), that

is a bounded operator on
{{\ell}}^{p}
.
Let

be its norm. Thus, by definition,

\left[\sum_{{{k}}=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2{{k}}-1}\sum_{{{n}}=0}^{{{k}}-2}|{{v}}_{{n}}|\right)^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\|{{{W}}}\|_{p}\left(\sum_{{{k}}=0}^{\infty}|{{v}}_{{k}}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Then, since
{\left({{w}}_{{{k}}}\right)_{{{k}}=1}^{\infty}}\in{{\ell}}^{q}

\sum_{{{k}}=2}^{\infty}\frac{|{{w}}_{{k}}|}{2{{k}}-1}\sum_{{{n}}=0}^{{{k}}-2}|{{v}}_{{n}}|\leq\left(\sum_{{{k}}=1}^{\infty}|{{w}}_{{k}}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left[\sum_{{{k}}=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2{{k}}-1}\sum_{{{n}}=0}^{{{k}}-2}|{{v}}_{{n}}|\right)^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Since we need to show that

This can be seen as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{{{k}}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{{{n}}=0}^{\infty}\frac{|{{w}}_{{k}}||{{v}}_{{{k}}+{{n}}-1}|}{2{{n}}+1}<{{\mathbf{C}}}_{15}\sum_{{{k}}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{{{n}}=0}^{\infty}\frac{|{{v}}_{{{k}}+{{n}}-1}|}{(2{{n}}+1)(2{{k}}+1)}

8.
Discussion

We have constructed a sequence of functionals on
{{\ell}}^{p}
(
\infty>p>1
) that depend linearly on a parameter
{s}
and whose kernels have a common nontrivial vector precisely when
{s}
is a nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function. This statement can be viewed as a variant of the Berry-Keating observation about the role of the dilatation operator in the problem of the Riemann Hypothesis. It might be interesting to explore whether a simple matrix as (
1.1
) can also be constructed for Dirichlet or Artin
L
-functions, or automorphic
L
-functions, and whether the elements of the matrix correspond to any meaningful data of the automorphic representation. For example, the order of a zero at
{s}=1
is easy to translate to a linear algebraic condition, similarly to §
6
, and therefore if a condition in the spirit of (
1.1
) can be found for an
L
-function associated with an elliptic curve, it might be interesting to explore whether additional arithmetic data that appears in the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture is also encoded in the matrix.

We have also explored whether it is possible to convert the matrix equation (

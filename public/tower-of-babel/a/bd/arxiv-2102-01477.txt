Classification of fiber sequences with a prescribed holonomy action
Mario Fuentes

Abstract
We define ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -fibration sequences as fibrations where the holonomy action of the fundamental group of the base on the fiber lies in a given subgroup ℋ {\mathcal{H}} of ℰ ⁡ ( F ) {\mathcal{E}}(F) , where ℰ ⁡ ( F ) {\mathcal{E}}(F) is the homotopy automorphism group of the fiber. Furthermore, we classify these ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -fibration sequences via a universal ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -fibration sequence.

Introduction

From the foundational paper of Stasheff
[
9
]
to the recent reference
[
3
]
of homotopy theoretical flavour, the literature is splashed of results which let us classify fibrations, sometimes of a certain type (see for instance
[
6
,
11
]
), by means of a suitable classifying space. The most general result in the
homotopy category is probably the classical work of May
[
7
]
which classifies fibrations with a given
category of fibers
.

In these works, it is constructed a classifying object
B\operatorname{aut}F
, and a universal quasi-fibration sequence

F\to B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}F,F)\to B\operatorname{aut}(F)

such that, given a space
B
of the homotopy type of a CW-complex, there is a bijection

\Lambda:[B,B\operatorname{aut}F]\to\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}(B,F)

where
[B,B\operatorname{aut}F]
is the set of homotopy classes of maps from
B
to
B\operatorname{aut}F
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}(B,F)
is the set of equivalence classes of fibration sequence over
B
with fiber
F
.

However, this treatment does not readily apply to classify fibrations with a prescribed holonomy action of the fundamental group of the base on the fiber: given a fibration sequence
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
, there is map of groups
\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})\to{\mathcal{E}}(F)=\pi_{0}(\operatorname{aut}F)
. Given a subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}
of
{\mathcal{E}}(F)
, we say that a fibration sequence is an
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence if the image of the holonomy action lies in the subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}
.

The purpose of this text is to define rigorously the concepts above and to construct a classifying object and a universal
{\mathcal{H}}
-quasi-fibration sequence, of the form

F\to B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F,F)\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F

which classifies
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequences. Here
\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F
will denote the submonoid of
\operatorname{aut}F
of self-homotopy equivalences
\varphi
such that
[\varphi]\in{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant{\mathcal{E}}(F)
. Note that when
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{E}}(F)
is the whole group, then
\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F=\operatorname{aut}F
and we recover the usual classifying space and the original result of Stasheff
[
9
]
. The other extreme case
{\mathcal{H}}=1
, makes
B\operatorname{aut}_{{\mathcal{H}}}F
weakly equivalent to the universal cover of
B\operatorname{aut}F
. The rational homotopy type of this last space is well-known (see for instance
[
10
, §VII]
). Other choices of the subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}
yield intermediate situations between the two described above.

Although this classification may be part of the folklore, we are not aware of any detailed reference in the literature and the purpose of this note is to fill this gap in a rigorous way. The resulting classifying space is a well known and interesting object, see for instance
[
4
]
. Particularly interesting examples and applications of this classification, in the rational homotopy category, are being considered in
[
5
]
; there it is shown that for a good choice of the subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}
, the classifying space has the homotopy type of a rational space. Furthermore, its classifying properties are fundamental for obtaining homotopical information about such space.

As a final remark, I stress that this result differs from the classical classification of topological
G
-bundles for a given topological group
G
(see for instance
[
2
]
) in the same extent as the classification of any class of fibrations differs from the corresponding bundle counterpart.

The text is structured as follows: in the first section, the concepts of holonomy action and
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence are introduced. In the second, the geometric-bar construction is used to prove the classification Theorem
2.1
. Finally, in the third part, we give a pointed version of these results.

1
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequences

Throughout this text, any considered topological space is compactly generated and weakly Hausdorff. Also, the fiber
F
and the base
B
shall be always considered connected spaces of the homotopy type of a CW-complex and
b_{0}
is a fixed basepoint in
B
such that its inclusion is a cofibration. By a
fibration sequence
, we mean a sequence

F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B

where
p
is a Hurewicz fibration, and the map
\omega\colon F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq_{w}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{0}=\pi^{-1}(b_{0})
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
A map between two fibration sequences,
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
and
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
, is a homotopy commutative diagram of the form,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}

In particular,
f
is a weak homotopy equivalence and there is no loss of generality if the right triangle is imposed to be strictly commutative.
The maps of fibration sequences generate a equivalence relation (by imposing symmetry and associativity) and we denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}(B,F)
the corresponding quotient set.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that this definition of
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}(B,F)
(which is the one considered in
[
1
]
, for example) differs with the one considered in
[
9
]
or
[
7
]
, where a fibration with fiber
F
is a Hurewicz fibration
\pi:E\to B
whose fiber is weakly equivalent to
F
, but no explicit weak equivalence is given. These two approaches give rise to different equivalence classes and, therefore, to different classifying spaces. However, both spaces are weakly equivalent (see
[
8
, explanation below Theorem 1.2]
). Since we are considering base spaces which are of the homotopy type of a CW-complex, both approaches coincide in our setting.

Recall how the fundamental group of the base acts on the homotopy automorphism of the fiber.
Given a fibration sequence
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
and
\beta\in\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})
, the lifting property of the fibration yields an induced homotopy equivalence
\bar{\beta}\colon F_{0}=\pi^{-1}(b_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\to}}F_{0}
. Together with the bijection
\omega_{*}\colon[F,F]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}[F,F_{0}]
produces the homotopy equivalence
\hat{\beta}={\omega_{*}}^{-1}[\bar{\beta}\circ\omega]\in[F,F]
. That is,
\omega\circ\hat{\beta}\simeq\bar{\beta}\circ\omega
. Here
[X,Y]
indicates free homotopy classes of (non-based) maps from
X
to
Y
.

Let
\operatorname{aut}F
be the topological monoid of self homotopy equivalences of
F
and write
{\cal E}(F)=\pi_{0}(\operatorname{aut}F)
. Fix a subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant{\cal E}(F)
and consider
\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F\subset\operatorname{aut}F
the submonoid of homotopy equivalences
\varphi
such that
[\varphi]\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

In particular, the process described above, gives, for each fibration sequence
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
, a map

\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})\to{\mathcal{E}}(F),\;\;\;[\beta]\mapsto[\hat{\beta}]

which will be called the
holonomy action of
\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})
on the fiber
.

Definition 1.2
.

A fibration sequence
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
is an
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence if the image of the holonomy action is contained in
{\mathcal{H}}
. This means that

\hat{\beta}\in{\mathcal{H}}
for any
\beta\in\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})
.

A direct inspection shows the following proposition.

Proposition 1.3
.

Given two fibration sequences over
B
with fiber
F
and a map of fibration sequences between them, if one of them is an
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence, the other one also is.

In particular, we can consider
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}_{\mathcal{H}}(B,F)

the set of equivalence classes of
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequences over
B
with fiber
F
, which is a subset of
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}(B,F)
.

Example 1.4
.

In the extreme case,
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{E}}(F)
, we recover the usual notion of fibration sequence, since
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}_{\mathcal{H}}(B,F)=\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}(B,F)
. In the other extreme case,
{\mathcal{H}}=1
, an
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence is a fibration sequence with a trivial holonomy action. As an intermediate case, we can take the homology functor (or more generally, any functor from the homotopy category of topological spaces), and take
{\mathcal{H}}
as those automorphisms which are the identity in the homology.

We finish this section recalling two usual constructions to fix the notation.

Definition 1.5
.

Given a fiber sequence
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
and a based map
f:A\to B
, the
pullback fibration sequence
is defined as

F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{*}\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}f^{*}E=\{(x,a)\in E\times A\mid f(a)=\pi(x)\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{*}\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}A

where
f^{*}\omega(x)=(\omega(x),a_{0})
and
f^{*}\pi(x,a)=a
.

It is immediate to check that the pullback fibration sequence of an
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence is an
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence.

Remark 1.6
.

If
\{a_{0}\}\hookrightarrow A
is a cofibration, an arbitrary map
f:(A,a_{0})\to(B,b_{0})
is (free) homotopy equivalent to a based map, so we can always assume that the maps between base spaces are based.

Definition 1.7
.

For an arbitrary map
\pi:E\to B
, we write

\Gamma E=\{(x,\beta)\in E\times B^{I}\mid\beta(0)=\pi(x)\}

\Gamma\pi:\Gamma E\to B,\;\;\;(x,\beta)\mapsto\beta(1)

where
B^{I}
is the space of paths on
B
. The map
\Gamma\pi
is a fibration with
homotopy fiber

\mathcal{F}=(\Gamma\pi)^{-1}(b_{0})=\{(x,\beta)\in E\times B^{I}\mid\beta(0)=\pi(x),\;\beta(1)=b_{0}\}

If the natural homotopy equivalence
i:E\to\Gamma E,\;i(x)=(x,c_{\pi(x)})
, where
c_{\pi(x)}
is the constant path at
\pi(x)
, induces a weak homotopy equivalence
\pi^{-1}(b)\to(\Gamma\pi)^{-1}(b)
for all
b\in B
, we say that
\pi:E\to B
is a
quasi-fibration
.

By a
quasi-fibration sequence
we mean a sequence

F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B

such that
\pi
is a quasi-fibration and
i\circ\omega:F\to\mathcal{F}
is a weak homotopy equivalence. The
associated fibration sequence
is the fibration sequence

F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i\circ\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Gamma\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B

Finally, we say that a quasi-fibration sequence is an
{\mathcal{H}}
-quasi-fibration sequence
if its associated fibration sequence is an
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence.

The classification theorem

We strongly rely in the classical reference
[

]
from which we recall some facts. Let
G
be a topological monoid with the identity element

a strongly nondegenerate basepoint, and let
X

This is a functor from the category of triples

as above, whose morphisms are triples

where

is a map of monoids and

Then if

is a grouplike topological monoid, i.e.

is a group, then

7

asserts that

is a quasifibration with fiber

is a weak homotopy equivalence, where
i(x)=(x,c_{\ast})

is the unique point in the unique 0-simplex and

In particular, whenever

is of the homotopy type of a CW-complex, the
universal fibration sequence

F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Gamma B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}F,F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Gamma p}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\operatorname{aut}F

classifies fibrations with fiber
F
. Explicitly, see

, for any space
B
of the homotopy type of a CW-complex, the map,

\Lambda\colon[B,B\operatorname{aut}F]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}(B,F),\quad\Lambda[f]=f^{*}\Gamma p

is a natural bijection.
Given
{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant{\mathcal{E}}(F)
a subgroup, consider the map

p_{\mathcal{H}}\colon B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F,F)\to B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F,\ast)=B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F.

Finally, let’s recall that whenever

is grouplike, then , there is a natural weak homotopy equivalence ( see

7

\zeta:G\xrightarrow{\simeq_{w}}\Omega BG,\;\;\;g\mapsto\gamma_{g}

where

is a path such that
\gamma_{g}(t)=(g,t)
where this element lies in the 1-skeleton of

Remark 2.2
.

As in the ordinary case, a more convenient expression of the universal
{\mathcal{H}}
-quasi-fibration sequence can be given.
Fix a base point
x_{0}\in F_{0}
whose inclusion is a cofibration and consider the
evaluation fibration

\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}F\hookrightarrow\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\text{ev}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F

where
\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}F
is the submonoid of
\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F
of
self homotopy equivalences which fix the base point and

{\text{ev}}(g)=g(x_{0})
. This yields a weak homotopy equivalence
F\simeq_{w}\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F/\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}F=B(\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}},\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*},*)
which, in turn, produces equivalent fibrations with fiber
F
,

\Gamma B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F,F)\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F/\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}F)\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F.

On the other hand, by

[
7
, Remark 8.9]
, the quasifibrations

B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F/\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}F)\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F\quad\text{and}\quad B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}F\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F

are equivalent.
That is, the fibration sequences,

F\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}^{*}F\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F\quad\text{and}\quad F\to B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F,F)\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F

are equivalent.

Therefore, Theorem
2.1
can be reformulated in the following way:

F\to B\operatorname{aut}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}F\to B\operatorname{aut}_{\mathcal{H}}F

is the universal
{\mathcal{H}}
-quasi-fibration sequence which classifies
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequences.

3
Based fibrations

The previous results can be adapted to the based case. Let’s give a brief exposition of the concepts needed for a rigorous definition.

A fibration sequence
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
is
based
if
\pi
has a section
\sigma\colon B\to E
and the weak equivalence
\omega
sends the basepoint of
F
to
\sigma(b_{0})
. Consider the ‘whisker construction’ (see
[
7
, Addenda]
),

\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{E}\longrightarrow B\quad\text{where}\quad\widetilde{E}=\frac{E\sqcup(B\times[0,1])}{\sigma(b)\sim(b,0),\forall b\in X},\quad\widetilde{\pi}(y)=\pi(y),\quad\widetilde{\pi}(b,t)=b.

for
y\in E,b\in F
and
t\in[0,1]
.
This is a fibration with fiber
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(x_{0})=\widetilde{F}_{0}=F_{0}\vee[0,1]
.
Given
\beta\in\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})
the induced homotopy equivalence
\widetilde{\beta}\colon\widetilde{F}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\to}}\widetilde{F}_{0}
can be taken to send 1 to 1. Since the inclusion of the basepoint in
F
is a cofibration, there is a pointed weak homotopy equivalence
\tilde{\omega}:F\to\tilde{F}_{0}
, sending
x_{0}
to 1, which induces a bijection
\widetilde{\omega}_{*}\colon[F,F]^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}[F,\tilde{F_{0}}]^{*}
between pointed homotopy classes of base-preserving maps. This yields the pointed homotopy equivalence
\dot{\beta}=\widetilde{\omega}_{*}^{-1}[\widetilde{\beta}f]^{*}\in[F,F]^{*}
.

Let
\operatorname{aut}^{*}F
be the topological monoid of pointed self homotopy equivalences of
F
and denote by
{\cal E}^{*}(F)=\pi_{0}(\operatorname{aut}^{*}F)
. Fix a subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant{\cal E}^{*}(F)
and consider
\operatorname{aut}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}F\subset\operatorname{aut}^{*}F
the submonoid of pointed homotopy equivalences
\varphi
such that their (pointed) homotopy classes
[\varphi]^{*}
belong to
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A based fibration sequence
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B
is a based
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequence if
\dot{\beta}\in{\mathcal{H}}
for any
\beta\in\pi_{1}(B,b_{0})
. Denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}(B,F)
the set of equivalence classes of based
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibrations sequences over
B
with fiber
F
.

Again, pullbacks preserve based
{\mathcal{H}}
-fibration sequences and this definition is independent of the equivalence class of the given based fibration sequences

Consider the maps

p\colon B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}^{*}F,F)\to B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}^{*}F,\ast)=B\operatorname{aut}^{*}F

and

p_{\mathcal{H}}\colon B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}F,F)\to B(\ast,\operatorname{aut}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}F,\ast)=B\operatorname{aut}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}F,

both endowed with the section induced by the inclusion of the basepoint in
F
, and
let
\Lambda\colon[B,B\operatorname{aut}^{*}F]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}\operatorname{\mathcal{F}\text{ib}}^{*}(B,F)
be the natural bijection given in
[
7

Ray transform on Sobolev spaces of symmetric tensor fields, II: Range characterization
Venkateswaran P. Krishnan, Vladimir A. Sharafutdinov

Abstract
. The ray transform I I integrates symmetric m m -tensor field in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} over lines. This transform in Sobolev spaces was studied in our earlier work where higher order Reshetnyak formulas (isometry relations) were established. The main focus of the current work is the range characterization. In dimensions n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , the range characterization of the ray transform in Schwartz spaces is well-known; the main ingredient of the characterization is a system of linear differential equations of order 2 ​ ( m + 1 ) 2(m+1) which are called John equations. Using the higher order Reshetnyak formulas, the range of the ray transform on Sobolev spaces is characterized in dimensions n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 in this paper.



1.
Introduction

The ray transform integrates functions or more generally symmetric tensor fields over
lines and the Radon transform integrates functions over hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The ray
transform of functions is the main mathematical tool of computer tomography.
The ray transform of vector fields and that of symmetric second rank tensor fields arise in Doppler tomography
and travel time tomography, respectively. The Radon transform originally arose while trying to decompose a solution of the wave equation in terms of plane waves. In 2-dimensions, the ray transform of functions coincides, up to notation, with the Radon transform. The Radon transform is extensively studied in
[
3
,
10
]
and a systematic treatment of the ray transform of symmetric tensor fields in Euclidean and Riemannian manifold settings is performed in
[
12
]
. The ray and Radon transforms will be denoted by
I
and
R
, respectively, in this paper.

Three questions naturally arise in the study of the Radon transform and ray transform of symmetric tensor fields
(or any other transform with tomographic applications) (i) inversion formulas (ii) stability
and (iii) range characterization. We do not discuss inversion formulas in this paper. Instead, we refer the interested reader to the aforementioned books
[
3
,
10
,
12
]
where explicit inversion formulas are derived for such transforms. The second question is that of stability. As we will see, this is related to the third question of range characterization; the main topic of this paper. For this reason, we begin with a discussion of stability for the Radon transform.
To make the discussion accessible to a broader audience, we have chosen to be informal in the Introduction. Our current work is a companion paper to our prior work on this topic
[
8
]
, and we largely follow the notation from that paper. To avoid repetition, we have chosen to give only a condensed version of the relevant material to make the discussion self-contained. We recommend the interested readers to read this in conjunction with our earlier work as and when required.

The Radon transform
R
integrates a function along hyperplanes. The set of hyperplanes can be parameterized by points of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then
R
is defined by

Rf(\xi,p)=\int\limits_{\langle\xi,x\rangle=p}f(x)\,dx\quad\big((\xi,p)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\big),

where
\langle\cdot\>,\cdot\rangle
is the standard dot-product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
dx
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on the hyperplane
\{x\mid\langle\xi,x\rangle=p\}
. Some condition on
f
should be imposed for the integral above to converge. The Reshetnyak formulas for the Radon transform is a family of isometry relations (the best stability estimates possible) involving certain weighted Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. For the precise definitions of the weighted Sobolev spaces appearing below, we refer the reader to
[
3
,
14
,
13
]
. Section 2.1 in
[
8
]
also provides a good summary of these works. We have the following
family of higher order Reshetnyak formulas
for the Radon transform
[
13
]
:

\lVert f\rVert_{H_{t}^{(r,s)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lVert Rf\rVert_{H^{r,s+(n-1)/2}_{t+(n-1)/2}\left(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\right)},

for arbitrary reals
s,r
and for
t>-n/2
. We discuss a few special cases of this formula. The case when
r=s=t=0
is the original isometry relation between
f
and its Radon transform, due to Reshetnyak in the 1960s. The case when
r=0
,
s
arbitrary and
t>-n/2
was given by the second author in
[
14
]
.

The Reshetnyak formulas are closely related to the range characterization of the Radon transform. The classical Gel’fand-Helgason-Ludwig (GHL) range characterization theorem for the Radon transform on the Schwartz space is the following (below
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})
denotes the Schwartz space of functions on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
):

A function
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})
is the Radon transform of a function
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)
g(\omega,p)=g(-\omega,-p)
,

(2) for any integer
k\geq 0
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{k}g(\omega,p)\,dp
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree at most
k
in
\omega
.

A natural question is the range characterization of the Radon transform in weighted Sobolev spaces
H_{t}^{(r,s)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The following result is obtained in
[
13
]
: For arbitrary reals
r,s
and
t>-n/2
, the Radon transform

R:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})

uniquely extends to a bijective isometry of Hilbert spaces

R:H^{(r,s)}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to H^{(r,s+(n-1)/2)}_{t+(n-1)/2,e}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}).

Hereafter, the index
e
stands for “even”, i.e.,
H^{(r,s}_{t,e}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})
is the subspace of
H^{(r,s}_{t}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})
consisting of functions satisfying
g(\omega,p)=g(-\omega,-p)
.
We make one crucial remark here. The second part of the GHL condition disappears in the range characterization when one extends the Radon transform from the Schwartz space to the weighted Sobolev spaces.
The special case of this result, when
r=s=t=0
can be found in
[
3
]
, and for the case when
r=0
,
s
arbitrary and
t>-n/2
in
[
14
]
. To show the bijectivity of the Radon transform on these spaces, one proceeds as follows. The Reshetnyak formula shows that, being an isometry, the range of the operator
R|_{H^{(r,s)}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is a closed subspace of
H^{(r,s+(n-1)/2)}_{t+(n-1)/2,e}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})
, and the proof is completed by showing that the orthogonal complement of the latter subspace (with respect to the
H^{(r,s+(n-1)/2)}_{t+(n-1)/2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R})
scalar product) is equal to zero.

We next discuss the existence of Reshetnyak formulas for the ray transform. Let
S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
{n+m-1}\choose m
-dimensional complex vector space of rank
m
symmetric tensor fields on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The family of oriented straight lines in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is parameterized by points of the manifold

T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}=\{(x,\xi)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid|\xi|=1,\langle x,\xi\rangle=0\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

that is, the tangent bundle of the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. A point
(x,\xi)\in T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
determines the line
\{x+t\xi\mid t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
. Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the Schwartz space of
S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{S}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
denote the Schwartz space of functions on
T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
The
ray transform

I
is the linear continuous operator

I:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

(1.1)

that is defined, for
f=(f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by

If(x,\xi)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\,\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle f(x+t\xi),\xi^{m}\rangle\,dt\quad\big((x,\xi)\in T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\big).

(1.2)

Here and henceforth, we use the Einstein summation rule: the summation from 1 to
n
is assumed over every index repeated in lower and upper positions in a monomial. We use either lower or upper indices for denoting coordinates of vectors and tensors. Since we work in Cartesian coordinates only, there is no difference between covariant and contravariant tensors. For
m>0
, the ray transform
I
has an infinite dimensional kernel consisting of so-called potential tensor fields. Therefore it is natural to restrict the ray transform to the orthogonal complement of potential tensor fields, called solenoidal tensor fields. Analogous to the higher order Reshetnyak formulas for the Radon transform, the following family of higher order Reshetnyak formulas for the ray transform of symmetric tensor fields was derived recently in our work
[
8
]
: For an integer
r\geq 0
, real
s
and
t>-n/2
, the
r^{\mathrm{th}}
order Reshetnyak formula for the ray transform restricted to solenoidal tensor fields is

\lVert f\rVert_{H^{(r,s)}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lVert If\rVert_{H^{\left(r,s+1/2\right)}_{t+1/2}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

(1.3)

Definitions of the spaces in the formula are given in the next section.
Two special cases of this Reshetnyak formula were derived by the second author. The first was for the case
r=0
in
[
14
]
, and a first order Reshetnyak formula corresponding to
r=1
(with a slightly different definition for the weighted Sobolev spaces) for the case of the ray transform of functions was given in
[
13
]
.

Now we come to the question of range characterization for the ray transform. John
[
5
]
studied the range characterization of the ray transform of Schwartz class functions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and showed that a function is in the range of this transform if and only if it solves a certain second order linear differential equation, the so-called
John equation
. Helgason
[
2
]
generalized this result to an arbitrary
n\geq 3
dimension. A system of linear second order differential equations appears in the case of
n\geq 4
, they are still called John equations. The second author proved a range characterization result for the ray transform of symmetric tensor fields in Schwartz space in dimensions
n\geq 3

[
12
, Theorem 2.10.1]
.

For the
m
-tensor case, a big system of linear differential equations of order
2(m+1)
appears, see equations (
2.5
) below. Observe that the left-hand side of every equation (
2.5
) is actually a product of
m
John operators defined by (
2.6
). Equations (
2.5
) are not independent, there are many linear relations between them since John operators commute with each other. One would like to distinguish a minimal subsystem of (
2.5
) which is still equivalent to the whole system. We do not try to minimize the system (
2.5
) in the present paper. Nevertheless, let us mention that, in the 3-dimensional case, the system (
2.5
) is equivalent to one linear differential equation of order
2(m+1)
if an appropriate parametrization of the family of lines in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
has been chosen
[
9
]
. In an arbitrary dimension, minimization of the system (
2.5
) is discussed in
[
1
]
.

Integral GHL conditions for the Radon transform are very different of John differential equations for the ray transform. Nevertheless, they are related as is shown by Denisiuk
[
1
]
by restricting the ray transform to 2D planes.

Unlike GHL conditions, the John equations persist in the passage from the Schwartz space to weighted Sobolev spaces. In the scalar case, this fact is proved in
[
15
]
. The main goal of the present article is the proof of the latter fact for the ray transform of symmetric tensor fields. Namely, being understood in the distribution sense, the John equations characterize the range of the ray transform on the spaces
H^{(r,s)}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Unlike the case of the Schwartz space, Reshetnyak formulas play an important role in the characterization.

In our opinion, the difference between GHL conditions for the Radon transform and John equations for the ray transform is caused by the following. The ray transform is over-determined in dimensions
n\geq 3
: we try to recover a tensor field depending on
n
variables from a
(2n-2)
-dimensional set of information. On the other hand, in the case of the Radon transform, the problem of recovering
f
from
Rf
is formally determined.

Finally, we shortly discuss the ray transform in the 2-dimensional case. As we have mentioned, the ray transform of functions coincides with the Radon transform in 2-dimensions. For symmetric
m
-tensor fields, the range characterization of the 2-dimensional ray transform in the Scwartz space was obtained by Pantjukhina
[
11
]
; some version of integral GHL conditions participates here. The range characterization of the 2D ray transform in weighted Sobolev spaces of symmetric
m
-tensor fields is not studied so far for
m>0
. The following interesting question remains open: What kind of conditions, either John’s differential equations or GHL integral conditions, should be used in the latter problem?

2.
Preliminaries and statement of the main result

In this section, we state all preliminary definitions and results required for a precise statement of the range characterization.

2.1.
Ray transform of symmetric tensor fields

Recall from the previous section that
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Schwartz space of symmetric
m

\mathbb{R}^{n}
(each component of the tensor field is a Schwartz class function) and we also introduced the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})

\varphi\in C^{\infty}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})

\varphi(x,r\xi)=\varphi(x,\xi)\ (r>0),\quad\varphi(x+r\xi,\xi)=\varphi(x,\xi)\ (r\in{\mathbb{R}}).

We say that a function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
belongs to

\|\varphi\|_{k,\alpha,\beta}=\sup\limits_{(x,\xi)\in T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\left|(1+|x|)^{k}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\varphi(x,\xi)\right|

is finite for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and for all multi-indices
\alpha
and
\beta
. The family of these seminorms defines the topology on

{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
.

Recall that the ray transform (
1.1
) is defined by (
1.2
).
In the case of an even
m
, the ray transform is the linear continuous operator

I:{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{e}(TS^{n-1}),

and in the case of an odd

I:{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{o}(TS^{n-1}),

{\mathcal{S}}_{e}(TS^{n-1})\ ({\mathcal{S}}_{o}(TS^{n-1}))
is the subspace of
{\mathcal{S}}_{e}(TS^{n-1})
consisting of functions satisfying
\varphi(x,-\xi)=\varphi(x,\xi)
(satisfying
\varphi(x,-\xi)=-\varphi(x,\xi)
).
To unite these formulas, let us introduce the

of

\pi(m)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}e\ \mbox{if}\ m\ \mbox{is even},\\
o\ \mbox{if}\ m\ \mbox{is odd}.\end{array}\right.

Then the ray transform can be initially considered as a linear continuous operator

I:{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi(m)}(TS^{n-1}).

2.2.
Fourier slice theorem

The Fourier transform of symmetric tensor fields

F:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad f\mapsto\widehat{f}

is defined component wise (hereafter

\widehat{f}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\textsl{i}\langle y,x\rangle}f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}(x)\,dx.

The Fourier transform
F:\mathcal{S}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\to\mathcal{S}(T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\ \varphi\mapsto\widehat{\varphi}
is defined as the
(n-1)
-dimensional Fourier transform over the subspace
\xi^{\perp}
:

\widehat{\varphi}(y,\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{(n-1)/2}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\perp}}e^{-\textsl{i}\langle y,x\rangle}\varphi(x,\xi)\,dx\quad\big((y,\xi)\in T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\big).

The Fourier slice theorem
[
12

states:

\widehat{If}(y,\xi)=\sqrt{2\pi}\langle\widehat{f}(y),\xi^{m}\rangle\mbox{ for }(y,\xi)\in T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

2.3.
Range characterization of the ray transform on the Schwartz space

We first observe that the right-hand side of (
1.2
) makes sense for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and we use notation
J
to denote the extended operator. Strictly speaking, we define the continuous linear operator

J:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n};S^{m}\mathbb{R}^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})

by

Jf(x,\xi)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\,\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\,dt\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},0\neq\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

(2.3)

This extension allows us to take partial derivatives of
Jf
with respect to variables
x
and
\xi
freely. One can go back and forth between the original definition and the extended one by

Jf|_{T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}=If\quad\mbox{and}\quad Jf(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{m-1}If\left(x-\frac{\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi}{|\xi|^{2}},\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right).

Given a function
\varphi\in T\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, let us define an extension
\psi
of this function to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
as follows:

\psi(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{m-1}\varphi\left(x-\frac{\langle x,\xi\rangle\xi}{|\xi|^{2}},\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\right).

(2.4)

The range characterization theorem for the ray transform of symmetric tensor fields in dimension
n\geq 3
is as follows:

Theorem 2.1
.

A function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})\ (n\geq 3)
belongs to the range of the operator (
1.1
) if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\varphi(x,-\xi)=(-1)^{m}\varphi(x,\xi)
;

(2)

the function
\psi\in C^{\infty}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\big)
, defined by (
2.4
), satisfies the
equations

\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i_{1}}\partial\xi^{j_{1}}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j_{1}}\partial\xi^{i_{1}}}\Big)\dots\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i_{m+1}}\partial\xi^{j_{m+1}}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j_{m+1}}\partial\xi^{i_{m+1}}}\Big)\psi=0

(2.5)

for all indices
1\leq i_{1},j_{1},\dots,i_{m+1},j_{m+1}\leq n
.

We call (

) the
John equations
and the differential operators

J_{ij}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial\xi^{j}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial\xi^{i}}:C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

(2.6)

the
John operators
. (We hope the reader is not confused by similarity between the notation
J
for the operator (
2.3
) and

for John operators; both the notations are standard ones.)
In the case of

2.4.
The vector fields

) is a closed subspace of
H^{(r,s+1/2)}_{t+1/2,\pi(m)}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
be the space of tempered distributions on
T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. By
\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle
we denote the value of a distribution
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
on a test function
\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
. The embedding

{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
is defined by

\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}\varphi(x,\xi)\psi(x,\xi)\,dxd\xi\quad\mbox{for}\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}).

(2.11)

Proposition 2.3
.

For an integer
r\geq 0
, real
s
and
t\in\big(-(n-1)/2,(n-1)/2\big)
, the space
H^{(r,s)}_{t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
consists of tempered distributions. More precisely, the identity map of

{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
extends to a continuous embedding

H^{(r,s)}_{t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
.

Proof.

There exists the continuous embedding

H^{(r,s)}_{t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})\subset H^{s}_{t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
. Therefore it suffices to prove Proposition
2.3
in the case of
r=0
, i.e., to prove that the identical map of
{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
extends to a continuous embedding

H^{s}_{t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
. To this end we will demonstrate the validity of the estimate

|\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle|\leq C_{s,t}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}_{t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}\|\psi\|_{H^{-s}_{-t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}).

With the help of the Plancherel formula, (
2.11
) can be written as

\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}\widehat{\varphi}(y,\xi)\widehat{\psi}(y,\xi)\,dyd\xi.

Writing this in the form

\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}\Big(|y|^{t}(1+|y|^{2})^{(s-t)/2}\widehat{\varphi}(y,\xi)\Big)\Big(|y|^{-t}(1+|y|^{2})^{-(s-t)/2}\widehat{\psi}(y,\xi)\Big)\,dyd\xi

and applying the Cauchy – Bunyakovski inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle\varphi|\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}|y|^{2t}(1+|y|^{2})^{s-t}|\widehat{\varphi}(y,\xi)|^{2}\,dyd\xi\Big)^{1/2}\times

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}|y|^{-2t}(1+|y|^{2})^{t-s}|\widehat{\psi}(y,\xi)|^{2}\,dyd\xi\Big)^{1/2}=C_{s,t}\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}_{t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}\|\psi\|_{H^{-s}_{-t}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})}.

∎

By Proposition
2.3
, we have the linear continuous operator

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{J}}_{i_{1}j_{1}}\Big({\mathcal{J}}_{i_{2}j_{2}}-(\xi_{i_{2}}X_{j_{2}}-\xi_{j_{2}}X_{i_{2}})\Big)\Big({\mathcal{J}}_{i_{3}j_{3}}-2(\xi_{i_{3}}X_{j_{3}}-\xi_{j_{3}}X_{i_{3}})\Big)\dots

(2.12)

\displaystyle\dots\Big({\mathcal{J}}_{i_{m+1}j_{m+1}}-m(\xi_{i_{m+1}}X_{j_{m+1}}-\xi_{j_{m+1}}X_{i_{m+1}})\Big):H^{(r,s+1/2)}_{t+1/2,\pi(m)}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})

under the only restriction
t\in\big(-n/2,(n-2)/2\big)
.

We are now ready to state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.4

(Main theorem)
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
t\in\big(-(n-1)/2,(n-2)/2\big)
. For every integers
m\geq 0,r\geq 0
and every real
s
, the range of the operator (
2.10
) coincides with the intersection of kernels of differential operators (

(2.13)

3.
Range characterization for ray transform of Schwartz class tensor fields using intrinsic John operators

We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume a function
\psi\in C^{\infty}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\big)
to be positively homogeneous of degree
\lambda
in
\xi
and to satisfy

\psi(x+t\xi,\xi)=\psi(x,\xi)\quad(t\in{\mathbb{R}}).

(3.1)

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
be the restriction of
\psi
to
T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
. Then the equality

(J_{ij}\psi)|_{T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}=\big({\mathcal{J}}_{ij}-(\lambda+1)(\xi_{i}X_{j}-\xi_{j}X_{i})\big)\varphi

(3.2)

holds for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Proof.

(Compare with
[
15
, Lemma 2.4]
)
By the very definition of the operators

X_{i}

and

\Xi_{i}
,
the equality

{\mathcal{J}}_{ij}\varphi=(X_{i}\Xi_{j}-X_{j}\Xi_{i})\varphi=({\tilde{X}}_{i}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{j}-{\tilde{X}}_{j}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{i})\psi

(3.3)

holds on

T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.

By the definition of the operators

{\tilde{X}}_{i}

and

{\tilde{\Xi}}_{i}
,

\displaystyle{\tilde{X}}_{i}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{j}-{\tilde{X}}_{j}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}-\xi_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{j}}-x_{j}\xi^{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{q}}-\xi_{j}\xi^{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{q}}\Big)

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}-\xi_{j}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}-x_{i}\xi^{q}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{q}}-\xi_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{q}}\Big).

After opening parentheses, this becomes

\displaystyle{\tilde{X}}_{i}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{j}-{\tilde{X}}_{j}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial\xi^{j}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial\xi^{i}}\Big)+\Big(x_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial x^{p}}-x_{j}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{p}}\Big)

(3.4)

\displaystyle+\Big(\xi_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial\xi^{p}}-\xi_{j}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial\xi^{p}}\Big)-\Big(\xi_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{p}\partial\xi^{j}}-\xi_{j}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{p}\partial\xi^{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle-(x_{i}\xi_{j}-x_{j}\xi_{i})\xi^{p}\xi^{q}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{p}\partial x^{q}}.

We transform the fourth term on the right-hand side as follows:

\displaystyle\xi_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{p}\partial\xi^{j}}-\xi_{j}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{p}\partial\xi^{i}}

\displaystyle=\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{j}}\Big(\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}\Big)-\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}\Big(\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}\Big)+\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}\Big)\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}-\Big(\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\Big).

Substitute this expression into (
3.4
)

\displaystyle{\tilde{X}}_{i}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{j}-{\tilde{X}}_{j}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial\xi^{j}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial\xi^{i}}\Big)+\Big(x_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial x^{p}}-x_{j}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\xi_{i}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{j}\partial\xi^{p}}-\xi_{j}\xi^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{i}\partial\xi^{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle-\Big(\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}\Big)\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}+\Big(\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle-(x_{i}\xi_{j}-x_{j}\xi_{i})\xi^{p}\xi^{q}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{p}\partial x^{q}}.

On using the operators

J_{ij},\ \langle\xi,\partial_{x}\rangle=\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{p}}

\langle\xi,\partial_{\xi}\rangle=\xi^{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{p}}
,
we write this in the form

\displaystyle{\tilde{X}}_{i}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{j}-{\tilde{X}}_{j}{\tilde{\Xi}}_{i}

\displaystyle=J_{ij}+\Big(x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}-x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\Big)\langle\xi,\partial_{x}\rangle+\Big(\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\Big)\langle\xi,\partial_{\xi}\rangle

(3.5)

\displaystyle-\Big(\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{i}}\Big)\langle\xi,\partial_{x}\rangle+\Big(\xi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}-\xi_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\Big)-(x_{i}\xi_{j}-x_{j}\xi_{i})\langle\xi,\partial_{x}\rangle^{2}.

Since
\psi

in

(3.6)

The relation

holds on

in some local coordinates. Returning to Cartesian coordinates in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we write the representation in the form

\widehat{\psi}=(q^{*}\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}

(4.23)

with
\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. The summation over repeated Latin indices in (
4.23
) is performed from 1 to
n
. We will also write (
4.23
) in the traditional form

\widehat{\psi}(y,\xi)=\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\quad(y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\xi\in y^{\bot}\setminus\{0\})

(4.24)

although it is understood in the distribution sense. Of course we can assume
\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}
to be symmetric in all indices, i.e.,
\widehat{\Phi}=(\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

The tensor field
\widehat{\Phi}
is not uniquely determined by (
4.24
) since the equation (
4.24
) holds for
\xi\in y^{\bot}
only (in the distribution sense: the equation (
4.23
) holds on
V_{n}
). Nevertheless, we can state: there exists a unique tensor field

\widehat{\Phi}=(\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfying (
4.24
) and (
4.2
), where (
4.2
) is also understood in the distribution sense. Indeed, let
\widehat{\Phi^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be an arbitrary tensor field satisfying (
4.24
). Define

\widehat{\Phi}=(\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by

\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)=\Big(\delta_{i_{1}}^{j_{1}}-\frac{y_{i_{1}}y^{j_{1}}}{|y|^{2}}\Big)\dots\Big(\delta_{i_{m}}^{j_{m}}-\frac{y_{i_{m}}y^{j_{m}}}{|y|^{2}}\Big)\widehat{\Phi^{\prime}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)\quad(0\neq y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

where
\delta_{i}^{j}
is the Kronecker tensor. The latter formula can be also understood in the distribution sense. The tensor field
\widehat{\Phi}
satisfies (
4.24
) and (
4.2
) and is uniquely determined by these relations. In terms of
[
12
, Lemma 2.6.1]
,

\widehat{\Phi}
is the
tangential component
of
\widehat{\Phi^{\prime}}
.

The projection (
4.20
) is the composition
q=\pi\circ p
, where the smooth maps

V_{n}\ {\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}}\ T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\setminus{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}_{0}\ {\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}}\ {\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

are defined by
p(y,\xi)=(y,\xi/|\xi|)
and
\pi(y,\xi)=y
. Actually
p
and
\pi
are also smooth fiber bundles. The formula (
4.23
) can be written as

\widehat{\psi}=|\xi|^{m}(p^{*}\pi^{*}\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\frac{\xi^{i_{1}}}{|\xi|}\dots\frac{\xi^{i_{m}}}{|\xi|}.

Since
\frac{\xi^{i}}{|\xi|}=\xi^{i}\circ p
, the formula can be also written as

\widehat{\psi}=|\xi|^{m}p^{*}\big((\pi^{*}\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\big).

(4.25)

By the definition (
4.18
),

\widehat{\psi}=|\xi|^{m}p^{*}\widehat{\varphi}.

This gives together with (
4.25
)

p^{*}\widehat{\varphi}=p^{*}\big((\pi^{*}\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\big).

Since
p^{*}
is an injective operator, this implies

\widehat{\varphi}=(\pi^{*}\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}})\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}

or in the traditional form

\widehat{\varphi}(y,\xi)=\widehat{\Phi}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\quad\big((y,\xi)\in T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\setminus{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}_{0}\big).

(4.26)

We have thus proved that the statement (
4.3
) of Lemma
4.1
holds on
T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\setminus{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}_{0}
. By the hypothesis of the lemma,
\widehat{\varphi}\in L^{2,s}_{t,\pi(m)}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
. Together with (
4.26
), this easily implies that

\widehat{\Phi}\in L^{2,s}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and the formula (
4.3
) holds in the conventional sense at almost all points
(y,\xi)\in T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

By the definition of the
H^{s+1/2}_{t+1/2}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
-norm, the equation (
3.14
) is written as follows:

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}|y|^{2t+1}(1+|y|^{2})^{s-t}\widehat{\varphi}(y,\xi)\overline{\widehat{If}(y,\xi)}\,dyd\xi=0\quad\big(f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{sol}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\big).

(4.27)

Applying Lemma
4.1
to the function
\widehat{\varphi}\in L^{2,s+1/2}_{t+1/2.\pi(m)}(T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1})
, we can state the existence of a tensor field
\widehat{\Phi}\in L^{2,s+1/2}_{t+1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfying (
4.2
) and (
4.3
). With the help of (
4.3
), the equation (
4.27
) becomes

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}|y|^{2t+1}(1+|y|^{2})^{s-t}{\widehat{\Phi}}{}^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\overline{\widehat{If}(y,\xi)}\,\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\,dyd\xi=0.

(4.28)

By (
2.2
),

\widehat{If}(y,\xi)=(2\pi)^{1/2}\widehat{f}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\xi^{i_{1}}\dots\xi^{i_{m}}\quad\big((y,\xi)\in T{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\big).

Substituting this expression into (
4.28
), we obtain

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\xi^{\bot}}|y|^{2t+1}(1+|y|^{2})^{s-t}{\widehat{\Phi}}{}^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\overline{\widehat{f}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)}\,\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\xi_{j_{1}}\dots\xi_{j_{m}}\,dyd\xi=0.

Changing the order of integrations with the help of
[
12
, Lemma 2.15.3]
, we write this in the form

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|y|^{2t}(1+|y|^{2})^{s-t}{\widehat{\Phi}}{}^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\overline{\widehat{f}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)}\bigg[\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap\xi^{\bot}}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\xi_{j_{1}}\dots\xi_{j_{m}}\,d^{n-2}\xi\bigg]\,dy=0.

(4.29)

By
[
12
, Lemma 2.15.4]
,

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap\xi^{\bot}}\xi_{i_{1}}\dots\xi_{i_{m}}\xi_{j_{1}}\dots\xi_{j_{m}}\,d^{n-2}\xi=c_{m,n}\varepsilon^{m}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)

with some positive constant
c_{m,n}
, where

\varepsilon_{ij}(y)=\delta_{ij}-y_{i}y_{j}/|y|^{2}

(4.30)

and
\varepsilon^{m}
is the
m^{\mathrm{th}}
symmetric power of
\varepsilon
. The formula (
4.29
) becomes

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|y|^{2t}(1+|y|^{2})^{s-t}\varepsilon^{m}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y){\widehat{\Phi}}{}^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\overline{\widehat{f}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)}\,dy=0.

(4.31)

This equality holds for every
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{sol}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

We write the equation (
4.31
) in the form

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\varepsilon^{m}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)\Big(|y|^{t+1/2}(1+|y|^{2})^{(s-t)/2}{\widehat{\Phi}}{}^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\Big)\Big(|y|^{t-1/2}(1+|y|^{2})^{(s-t)/2}\overline{\widehat{f}^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)}\Big)dy=0.

(4.32)

Since the tensor field
\widehat{\Phi}
belongs to
L^{2,s+1/2}_{t+1/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and satisfies (
4.2
), the tensor field

\tilde{\Phi}=|y|^{t+1/2}(1+|y|^{2})^{(s-t)/2}{\widehat{\Phi}}
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and satisfies

y_{p}\tilde{\Phi}{}^{pj_{2}\dots j_{m}}(y)=0.

(4.33)

Recall that
2t-1>-n
by the hypothesis
t\in\big(-(n-1)/2,(n-2)/2\big)
of Lemma
3.5
. Since
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a solenoidal tensor field, the tensor field
g=|y|^{t-1/2}(1+|y|^{2})^{(s-t)/2}\widehat{f}
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};S^{m}{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and satisfies

y_{p}g^{pj_{2}\dots j_{m}}(y)=0.

(4.34)

The equation (
4.32
) is written in terms of
\tilde{\Phi}
and
g
as follows:

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\varepsilon^{m}_{i_{1}\dots i_{m}j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y){\tilde{\Phi}}{}^{i_{1}\dots i_{m}}(y)\overline{g^{j_{1}\dots j_{m}}(y)}\,dy=0.

In terms of

and

are
tangential
tensor fields.
Let

Since the tensor fields
\tilde{\Phi}

(4.36)

Now, we apply some arguments of

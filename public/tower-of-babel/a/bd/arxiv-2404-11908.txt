1.
Introduction

The study of holomorphic Morse inequalities dates back to Demailly’s classical work
[
6
]
. It was motivated by Grauert-Riemenschneider conjecture
[
17
]
, which stated that if a holomorphic line bundle
L
is semi-positive and positive at least one point, then
L
is big. Inspired by Witten’s analytic proof
[
19
]
of the classical Morse inequality, Demailly replaced the Morse function by the Hermitian metric of
L
and the Hessian of the Morse function by the curvature form of
L
. Since then the holomorphic Morse inequalities have been explored intensively during the last four decades. Bismut
[
3
]
provided a heat kernel proof involving probability theory. Berman
[
2
]
investigated asymptotics of Bergman kernel functions and then deduced a local version of holomorphic Morse inequalities by scaling technique and standard arguments from functional analysis. Ma-Marinescu
[
14
]
gave another heat kernel proof inspired by Bismut-Lebeau
[
4
]
. Bouche
[
5
]
considered the
q
-convex case. Marinescu
[
15
]
established a result on weakly
1
-complete manifold. Recently, Hsiao and his collaborators
[
10
,
9
,
8
,
12
]
obtained variant versions in Cauchy-Riemann manifolds and complex manifolds with boundary. Besides solving the Grauert-Riemenschneider conjecture, the holomorphic Morse inequalities also give some key evidence towards the Green-Griffiths-Lang conjecture
[
7
]
.

The formulas of the holomorphic Morse inequalities on non-compact manifolds
[
13
, Theorem1.2-Theorem1.6]
can not be established globally in general. The key ingredients to deal with such various settings on non-compact manifolds rely on analysis about positivities of bundles or positivities of boundaries of manifolds. Li-Shao-Wang
[
13
]
introduced the concept of optimal fundamental estimate and gave a unified proof of the weak holomorphic Morse inequalities on several non-compact settings. They also studied asymototics of spectral function of lower energy forms and obtained weak holomorphic Morse inequalities for lower energy forms on complete Hermitian manifolds.

In this paper, we mainly focus on weakly
1
-complete manifolds. The motivation is to establish a version of holomorphic Morse inequalities on non-compact setting. Our results could provide a tool to study embedding problems of non-compact manifolds. We will show that the optimal fundamental estimate holds on weakly
1
-complete manifolds and establish a version of Morse inequalities for lower energy forms on such manifolds.

The following is our main result. An asymptotics of dimensions of the lower energy form spaces for weakly
1
-complete manifolds with some specifical line bundle is established. For the notations and definitions, we refer the readers to Section 2 and Section 3.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a weakly
1
-complete manifold of dimension
n
. Let
(L,h^{L})
and
(E,h^{E})
be
holomorphic Hermitian line bundles on

. Assume
K
is a compact subset and
(L,h^{L})
is Griffiths

-positive on

. Then there exists a sequence of relatively compact sets

The paper is organized as follows: we present basic notions and results needed in our proofs in Section 2. In Section 3, we give a proof of asymptotic estimate of dimension of the lower energy form spaces from the optimal fundamental estimate. In Section 4, we are devoted to proving our main theorem.

2.1.
Complex manifolds

Let

for

. We shall also denote such metric by

and denote its pointwise norm by

.
We denote the space of smooth
(p,q)
-forms over

\Omega^{p,q}(M,E)
.
Let
\Omega^{p,q}_{0}(M,E)
be the subspace of

The metric

and the volume form

induce a Hermitian inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
on

by defining

(2.1)

(s_{1},s_{2}):=\int_{M}\langle s_{1}(x),s_{2}(x)\rangle dv_{M}(x).

Write
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}:=(\cdot,\cdot)
.
We let

denote the completion of

.

It is well known that the Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}^{E}:\Omega_{0}^{p,q}(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega_{0}^{p,q+1}(M,E)
satisfies
(\overline{\partial}^{E})^{2}=0
. By abuse of notation, let
\overline{\partial}^{E}:L^{2}_{p,q}(M,E)\rightarrow L^{2}_{p,q+1}(M,E)
denote the associated maximal

-extension, which is a closed, densely defined operator that satisfies
(\overline{\partial}^{E})^{2}=0
. Let
\overline{\partial}^{E*}
denote the Hilbert space adjoint of
\overline{\partial}^{E}
, then the Gaffney extension of Kodaira Laplacian is given by

\displaystyle\Dom(\square^{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{s\in\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{E})\cap\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{E*}):\overline{\partial}^{E}s\in\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{E*}),~\overline{\partial}^{E*}s\in\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{E})\},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\square^{E}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}^{E}\overline{\partial}^{E*}s+\overline{\partial}^{E*}\overline{\partial}^{E}s\quad\mbox{for}~s\in\Dom(\square^{E}),

Which is a positive, self-adjoint operator
[
14
, Proposition 3.1.2]
.
We define the space of harmonic
(p,q)
-forms with values in
E
as

(2.3)

\mathscr{H}^{p,q}(M,E):=\Ker(\square^{E})\cap L^{2}_{p,q}(M,E)=\{s\in\Dom(\square^{E})\cap L^{2}_{p,q}(M,E):\square^{E}s=0\}.

Let the
q
-th
L^{2}
-Dolbeault cohomology be defined as quotient

(2.4)

H^{0,q}_{(2)}(M,E):=(\Ker(\overline{\partial}^{E})\cap L^{2}_{0,q}(M,E))\big/({\rm Im}(\overline{\partial}^{E})\cap L^{2}_{0,q}(M,E)),

while the
q
-th reduced
L^{2}
-Dolbeault cohomology is defined as quotient

(2.5)

\overline{H}^{0,q}_{(2)}(M,E):=(\Ker(\overline{\partial}^{E})\cap L^{2}_{0,q}(M,E))\big/\overline{({\rm Im}(\overline{\partial}^{E})\cap L^{2}_{0,q}(M,E))},

where
\overline{(\,\cdot\,)}
denotes the closure of the space with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Besides, the Dolbeault isomorphism
H^{q}(M,E)\cong H^{0,q}(M,E)
is well known, where
H^{q}(M,E)
is the
q
-th sheaf cohomology of the sheaf
\mathscr{O}_{M}(E)
of holomorphic section of
E
over
M
, and
H^{0,q}(M,E)
is the
q
-th Dolbeault cohomology.

Suppose
(L,h^{L})
is a Hermitian line bundle on
M
, the curvature of Chern connection on
(L,h^{L})
will be denoted by
\Theta_{L}=\overline{\partial}\partial\log|s|^{2}_{h^{L}}
for any local holomorphic trivializing section

, and the associated Chern curvature form
c_{1}(L,h^{L}):=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\Theta_{L}
is a real
(1,1)
-form on
M
.
We identify the two-form
\Theta_{L}
at every
x\in M
with a linear endomorphism

given by

(2.6)

\Theta_{L}(\alpha,\overline{\beta})=\langle Q_{L}\alpha,\overline{\beta}\rangle,\;\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in T^{1,0}_{x}M.

We can check that the numbers of positive, negative and zero eigenvalues of

are independent of the choice of the Hermitian metric

of

.

2.2.
Some estimates

The following estimate should be viewed as Morrey-Kohn-Hörmander estimate that plays an important role in the solution of
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann problem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
14
, Corollary 1.4.22]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth relatively compact domain in a Hermitian manifold
(X,\omega)
. Let
\rho\in C^{\infty}(X)
such that
M=\{x\in X:\rho(x)<0\}
and
|d\rho|=1
on the boundary
bM
. Let
(L,h^{L})
be a holomorphic Hermitian line bundle on
X
. Then for any
s\in B^{0,q}(M,L)
,
0\leq q\leq n
,

(2.7)

\begin{split}\frac{3}{2}\left(||\overline{\partial}^{L}s||^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{L*}s||^{2}\right)&\geq\frac{1}{2}||(\nabla^{\widetilde{L}})^{1,0*}\widetilde{s}||^{2}+\left(\Theta_{L}\otimes K^{*}_{X}(w_{j},\overline{w}_{k})\overline{w}^{k}\wedge i_{\overline{w}_{j}}s,s\right)\\
&+\int_{bM}\mathscr{L}_{\rho}(s,s)dv_{bM}-\frac{1}{2}\left(||\mathcal{T}^{*}\widetilde{s}||^{2}+||\overline{\mathcal{T}}\widetilde{s}||^{2}+||\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\widetilde{s}||^{2}\right),\end{split}

where
\widetilde{L}:=L\otimes K^{*}_{X}
,
\widetilde{s}:=(w^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w^{n}\wedge s)\otimes(w_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{n})
, and
\nabla^{\widetilde{L}}
is the Chern connection;
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a local orthonormal basis of
T^{1,0}X
with dual basis
\{w^{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of
T^{1,0*}X
;
\mathscr{L}_{\rho}(\cdot,\cdot):=(\partial\overline{\partial}\rho)(w_{j},\overline{w}_{k})\langle\overline{w}^{k}\wedge i_{\overline{w}_{j}}\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Lambda^{0,q}\otimes L}
is the Levi form of
bM
;
\mathcal{T}
is the Hermitian torsion operator and
B^{0,q}(M,L)
is defined in Section 4 .

Let
0\leq q\leq n
. We say the
fundamental estimate
about
(0,q)
-forms with values in
E
holds, provided there are compact subset
K\subset M
and
C>0
, such that for any
s\in\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{E})\cap\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{E*})\cap L^{2}_{0,q}(M,E)
,

(2.8)

\|s\|^{2}\leq C\left(\|\overline{\partial}^{E}s\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{E*}s\|^{2}+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}\right).

The above fundamental estimate yields
\overline{H}_{(2)}^{0,q}(M,E)\cong H^{0,q}_{(2)}(M,E)

[
14
, Theorem 3.1.8]
.

From now on, we suppose
\operatorname{rank}(E)=1
. Denoting
\overline{\partial}^{L^{k}\otimes E}
by
\overline{\partial}^{k,E}
, and its Hilbert space adjoint by
\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}
, we say the

about
(0,q)
-forms with values in

, and

, such that for any

\left(1-\frac{C}{k}\right)\|s\|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{k}\left(\|\overline{\partial}^{k,E}s\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}s\|^{2}\right)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}.

The above optimal fundamental estimate was introduced in
[
13
]
; it is clear that optimal fundamental estimate can deduce fundamental estimate for forms with values in

2.3.
Local asymptotics of spectral functions of lower energy forms

For
q=1,\ldots,n
, let
\square^{k,E}
be the Gaffney extension of Kodaira Laplacian
\overline{\partial}^{k,E}\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}+\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}\overline{\partial}^{k,E}
. It is known that the spectrums of
\square^{k,E}
lie in
[0,+\infty)
by
[
14
, Proposition 3.1.2]
. Let
\Omega\subset[0,+\infty)
be a Borel set, and denote by
E^{q}(\Omega):L^{2}_{0,q}(M,L^{k}\otimes E)\rightarrow\operatorname{Im}E^{q}(\Omega)
the spectral projection of
\square^{k,E}
with respect to
\Omega
. We set
\mathcal{E}^{q}(\lambda,\square^{k,E}):=\operatorname{Im}E^{q}\left([0,\lambda]\right)
, and call it the spectral space of
\square^{k,E}
corresponding to energy lower than
\lambda
. Take an orthonormal basis
\{s_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{E}^{q}(\lambda,\square^{k,E})
and set
B^{q}_{\leq\lambda}(x):=\sum_{j}|s_{j}(x)|^{2}_{L^{k}\otimes E}
to be the spectral function, which is independent of the choice of orthonormal basis.

Let
c_{1}({L},h^{{L}})
be the first Chern class of
(L,h^{L})
, and
M(q)
be the subset of
M
consisting of points on which the curvature of
(L,h^{L})
is non-degenerate and has exactly
q
negative eigenvalues. The following two theorems can be deduced by
[
11
, Corollary 1.4]
:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
N_{0}\geq 2n+1
, then the spectral function
B^{q}_{\leq k^{-N_{0}}}(x)
has an asymptotic equality

(2.10)

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}k^{-n}B^{q}_{\leq k^{-N_{0}}}(x)=(-1)^{q}1_{{M}(q)}\frac{c_{1}({L},h^{{L}})^{n}}{{\omega}^{n}}({x}),\end{split}

where
1_{{M}(q)}
is the characteristic function of
M(q)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
K\subset M
be a compact subset. Then there exist
C>0
, and
k_{0}>0
, such that for any
k>k_{0},x\in K
, we deduce that

(2.11)

\begin{split}k^{-n}B^{q}_{\leq k^{-N_{0}}}(x)\leq C.\end{split}

We give some definitions at the end of this section.

Definition 2.4
.

A complex manifold

is said to be weakly
1
-complete
[
16
]
, if there is a plurisubharmonic function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, such that
M_{c}:=\{x\in M:\varphi(x)<c\}\Subset M
for any

. Such

is called an exhaustion function of

.

Definition 2.5
.

A Hermitian line bundle
(L,h^{L})
on a complex manifold

is said to be Griffiths

-positive at

, if the curvature form
\Theta_{L}
has at least
n-q+1

, where

,
1\leq q\leq n
.

Definition 2.6
.

A complex manifold

of dimension

is called

1

\varrho\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})

3.
Asymptotics of spectral functions of lower energy forms

When the optimal fundamental estimate is satisfied,
[
13
, Proposition 4.2]
gives an asymptotic estimate of the dimension of the lower energy form spaces
\dim\mathcal{E}^{q}(k^{-N_{0}},\square^{k,E}),N_{0}\geq 2n+1
as follows

Theorem 3.1
.

[
13
, Proposition 4.2]

Let
0\leq q\leq n
. For
N_{0}\geq 2n+1
, the estimate of dimension of the lower energy form spaces

(3.1)

\dim\mathcal{E}^{q}(k^{-N_{0}},\square^{k,E})\leq\frac{k^{n}}{n!}\int_{K(q)}(-1)^{q}c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}+o(k^{n})

holds, provided there are a compact subset
K\subset M
and
C>0
, such that for any
k\gg 1
and
s\in\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{k,E})\cap\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{k,E*})\cap L^{2}_{0,q}(M,L^{k}\otimes E)

the inequality as follows holds,

(3.2)

\left(1-\frac{C}{k}\right)\|s\|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{k}\left(\|\overline{\partial}^{k,E}s\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}s\|^{2}\right)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}.

Following the proof of
[
13
]
, we give a more detailed proof of this theorem.

Proof.

By the spectral decomposition theorem, using the optimal fundamental estimate, for any
s\in\mathcal{E}^{q}(k^{-N_{0}},\square^{k,E})\subset\Dom(\square^{k,E})\cap L^{2}_{0,q}(M,L^{k}\otimes E)
,

(3.3)

\begin{split}&(1-\frac{C}{k})||s||^{2}\leq\frac{C}{k}(||\overline{\partial}^{k,E}s||^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{E*}_{k}s||^{2})+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}\\
=&\frac{C}{k}(\square^{k,E}s,s)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}=\frac{C}{k}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda dE_{\lambda}^{q}\right)s,s\right)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}\\
=&\frac{C}{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda d(E_{\lambda}^{q}s,s)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}=\frac{C}{k}\int_{[0,k^{-N_{0}}]}\lambda d(E_{\lambda}^{q}s,s)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}\\
\leq&\frac{C}{k}k^{-N_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}d(E_{\lambda}^{q}s,s)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}=Ck^{-N_{0}-1}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}1dE_{\lambda}^{q}\right)s,s\right)+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M}\\
=&Ck^{-N_{0}-1}\|s\|^{2}+\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{M},\end{split}

where
E_{\lambda}^{q}:=E^{q}(-\infty,\lambda)
, is the spectral measure of
\square^{k,E}
.
Thus, it follows that
\|s\|^{2}\leq c_{k}\int_{K}|s|^{2}dv_{X}
, where
c_{k}:=\frac{k^{N_{0}+1}}{k^{N_{0}+1}-Ck^{N_{0}}-C}
, and
c_{k}\to 1
, when
k\to\infty
.
By Fatou’s lemma, Theorem
2.3
, Hölder’s inequality and Theorem
2.2
, we get:

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(k^{-n}\dim\mathcal{E}^{q}(k^{-N_{0}},\square^{k,E})\right)\\
\leq&\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(k^{-n}c_{k}\int_{K}B_{\leq k^{-N_{0}}}^{q}(x)dv_{X}(x)\right)\\
\leq&\left(\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}c_{k}\right)\left(\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{K}k^{-n}B_{\leq k^{-N_{0}}}^{q}(x)dv_{X}(x)\right)\\
\leq&\int_{K}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}k^{-n}B_{\leq k^{-N_{0}}}^{q}(x)dv_{M}(x)\leq\int_{K(q)}(-1)^{q}\frac{c_{1}({L},h^{{L}})^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

∎

Using the canonical isomorphism of the weakly Hodge decomposition
[
14
, (3.1.21)]

\mathscr{H}^{0,q}(M,E)\cong\overline{H}^{0,q}_{(2)}(M,E)

. This allows us to get the weak Morse inequality by Theorem
3.1

(4.15)

||\zeta s||_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{k}(||\overline{\partial}^{k,E}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2}).

Let
\eta:=1-\zeta
and
C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}:=\sup_{x\in M_{i}}|d\eta(x)|_{g^{T^{*}M}}^{2}>0
. Then, for any
s\in B^{0,j}(M_{i},L^{k}\otimes E)
and
k\geq 1
, we now claim that

(4.16)

\frac{1}{k}(||\overline{\partial}^{k,E}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2})\leq\frac{5}{k}(||\overline{\partial}^{k,E}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}s||_{i}^{2})+\frac{12C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{k}||s||_{i}^{2}.

Notice that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta=1
on
\overline{M}_{a}
and
\eta=0
on
\overline{M}_{i}\setminus\overline{M}_{a^{{}^{\prime}}}
, thus
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M_{i})
. So
\eta s\in\Omega^{0,j}_{0}(M_{i},L^{k}\otimes E)\subset B^{0,j}(M_{i},L^{k}\otimes E)
.
For simplifying notations, we use
\overline{\partial}
and
\overline{\partial}^{*}
instead of
\overline{\partial}^{k,E}
and
\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}
respectively.
It follows that
||\overline{\partial}(\eta s)||^{2}=||\overline{\partial}\eta\wedge s+\eta\overline{\partial}s||^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad||\overline{\partial}^{*}(\eta s)||^{2}=||\eta\overline{\partial}^{*}s-i_{\overline{\partial}\eta}s||^{2}
.
And notice that
||\overline{\partial}\eta\wedge s||^{2}\leq C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}||s||^{2}
and
||i_{\overline{\partial}\eta}s||^{2}\leq C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}||s||^{2}
.
By Cauchy’s inequality, we get

(4.17)

\begin{split}&||\overline{\partial}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2}\\
\leq&2(||\overline{\partial}s-\overline{\partial}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s-\overline{\partial}^{*}(\zeta s)||_{i}^{2})+2(||\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})\\
=&2(||\overline{\partial}(\eta s)||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}(\eta s)||_{i}^{2})+2(||\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})\\
=&2(||\overline{\partial}\eta\wedge s+\eta\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\eta\overline{\partial}^{*}s-i_{\overline{\partial}\eta}s||_{i}^{2})+2(||\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})\\
\leq&3(||\eta\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\eta\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})+6(||\overline{\partial}\eta\wedge s||_{i}^{2}+||i_{\overline{\partial}\eta}s||_{i}^{2})+2(||\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})\\
\leq&3(||\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})+6(C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}||s||_{i}^{2}+C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}||s||_{i}^{2})+2(||\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})\\
=&5(||\overline{\partial}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{*}s||_{i}^{2})+12C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}||s||_{i}^{2}.\end{split}

This finishes the proof of (
4.16
).

Next by applying (
4.14
), (
4.15
) and (
4.16
), we obtain

(4.18)

||s||_{i}^{2}-\int_{K^{\prime}}|s|^{2}dv_{M}\leq\frac{5C_{1}}{k}(||\overline{\partial}^{k,E}s||_{i}^{2}+||\overline{\partial}^{k,E*}s||_{i}^{2})+\frac{12C_{1}C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{k}||s||_{i}^{2}.

Since

is dense in

on

, we can complete this proof by taking

∎

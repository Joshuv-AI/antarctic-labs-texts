Effective medium theory for second-gradient elasticity with chirality
Grigor Nika and Adrian Muntean

Abstract
We derive effective models from a heterogeneous second-gradient nonlinear elastic material taking into account chiral scale-size effects. Our classification of the effective equations depends on the hierarchy of four characteristic lengths: The size of the heterogeneities ℓ \ell , the intrinsic lengths of the constituents ℓ SG \ell_{\rm SG} and ℓ chiral \ell_{\rm chiral} , and the overall characteristic length of the domain L {\rm L} . Depending on the different scale interactions between ℓ SG \ell_{\rm SG} , ℓ chiral \ell_{\rm chiral} , ℓ \ell , and L {\rm L} we obtain either an effective Cauchy continuum or an effective second-gradient continuum. The working technique combines scaling arguments with the periodic homogenization asymptotic procedure. Both the passage to the homogenization limit and the unveiling of the correctors’ structure rely on a suitable use of the periodic unfolding and related operators.

Typical engineered materials with microstructure include ceramic matrix composites, fibre-reinforced polymers, and many other advanced functional materials. Auxiliary formulas

For the readers convenience and for the expediency of the our results, we introduce certain formulas that we will make use of in what follows. These formulas can also be found in

For any sufficiently smooth scalar function

\Sigma

\Sigma

(\nabla\xi)_{\tau}{=}-{\boldsymbol{n}}\times({\boldsymbol{n}}\times\nabla\xi){=}\nabla\xi-(\nabla\xi)_{n}{\boldsymbol{n}},\hskip 10.00002pt(\nabla\xi)_{n}{:=}\nabla\xi\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}.

Moreover, we introduce the surface gradient of
\xi
using the projection operator
\Pi{:=}{\rm I}-{\boldsymbol{n}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}}
.

\nabla_{s}\xi{=}({\rm I}-{\boldsymbol{n}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{n}})\nabla\xi{=}\Pi\nabla\xi.

Thus, we can write down a useful integration by parts on surfaces formula,

\int_{\Sigma}\nabla_{s}\xi\,ds=\int_{\Sigma}\xi({\rm div}{\boldsymbol{n}}){\boldsymbol{n}}\,ds+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left\llbracket\xi{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right\rrbracket\,d\ell,

(3.6)

where

\nu_{i}={\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{ijk}t_{j}n_{k},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3,

is a component of the unit normal vector on
\partial\Sigma
and tangent to
\Sigma
,
t_{j}
is a component of the unit tangent vector to
\partial\Sigma
. Lastly, we remark, the jump term on (
3.6
) is on a ridge, i.e., the line on
\Sigma
where the tangent plane of
\Sigma
is discontinuous. The above formulas are used with a high degree of frequency in emulsions and capillary fluids (see, e.g.,
[
52
]
). We refer the reader to the appendix of reference
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
for an excellent exposition of the above formulae and related topics.

Using the above formulas and notation, the heterogeneous medium is then be characterized by the following system (written component-wise) for
i=1,2,3
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -&\partial_{x_{j}}\tau^{\varepsilon}_{ij}=g_{i}&\text{ in }&\Omega,\\
&\tau^{\varepsilon}_{ij}=\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}-\partial_{x_{k}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}&\text{ in }&\Omega,\\
&(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}-\partial_{x_{k}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk})n_{j}-\Pi_{q\ell}\partial_{x_{\ell}}(\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\Pi_{qj})=0&\text{ on }&\Sigma_{\rm 1},\\
&\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}n_{j}=0&\text{ on }&\Sigma_{\rm 1},\\
&u^{\varepsilon}_{i}=0&\text{ on }&\Sigma_{\rm 0},\\
&\frac{\partial u^{\varepsilon}_{i}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{n}}}=0&\text{ on }&\Sigma_{\rm 0},\\
&\left\llbracket\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\nu_{j}\right\rrbracket=0&\text{ on }&\partial\Sigma_{1},\end{aligned}

(3.7)

where
g_{i}
is a component some appropriately scaled body force that belongs in
{\rm L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
\nu_{i}
is a component of the outward unit normal to
\partial\Sigma
, for
i=1,2,3
.

-

A normal double traction:
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}n_{j},

-

3.2
Variational formulation

The primary setting for this work is the Sobolev space
{\rm H}^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})
, the space of functions
{\boldsymbol{u}}:\Omega\mapsto{\sf I\!R}^{3}
such that each coordinate is twice weakly differentiable and all the first and second partial derivatives are in
{\rm L}^{2}(\Omega)
and the subspace
{\rm H}^{2}_{\Sigma_{\rm 0}}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})
which consists functions that vanish along with their derivatives on the part of the boundary of
\Sigma
,
\Sigma_{\rm 0}
(see, e.g.
[
1
]
).

The space
{\rm H}^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})
is a Hilbert space with norm,

\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{{\rm H}^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}=\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|^{2}_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}+\left\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|^{2}_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3\times 3})}+\left\|\nabla\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|^{2}_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3\times 3\times 3})}\right)^{1/2}.

(3.8)

Hence, if we multiply (
3.7
) by
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\{C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},{\sf I\!R}^{3})\mid{\boldsymbol{v}}=0,\,\,\nabla{\boldsymbol{v}}=0\text{ on }\Sigma_{\rm 0}\}
and integrate by parts, then we obtain:

{-}\int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}{-}\partial_{x_{k}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk})n_{j}v_{i}\,ds+\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}{-}\partial_{x_{k}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk})\partial_{x_{j}}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{\Omega}g_{i}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}.

(3.9)

A second integration by parts of the second term on the second integral gives,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {-}\int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}{-}\partial_{x_{k}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk})n_{j}v_{i}\,ds&+\int_{\Omega}\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}x_{k}}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}-\int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\partial_{x_{j}}v_{i}\,ds=\int_{\Omega}g_{i}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}.\end{aligned}

(3.10)

The last term on the left hand side of the above equation requires a second integration by parts. However, we first decompose it into its normal and tangential component (see equation (
3.5
)) as follows,

\int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\partial_{x_{j}}v_{i}\,ds=\int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}n_{j}n_{l}\partial_{x_{l}}v_{i}\,ds+\int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\Pi_{lj}\partial_{x_{l}}v_{i}\,ds

(3.11)

A second integration by parts on surfaces (see equation (
3.6
)) for the last term on the right hand side of the above equation gives,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\Pi_{lj}\partial_{x_{l}}v_{i}\,ds&=\int_{\Sigma_{\rm 1}}(\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\Pi_{qj}({\rm div}~{\boldsymbol{n}})n_{q}-\Pi_{ql}\partial_{x_{l}}(\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\Pi_{qj}))v_{i}\,ds\\
&-\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\rm 1}}\left\llbracket\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\nu_{j}v_{i}\right\rrbracket d\ell.\end{aligned}

(3.12)

We remark immediately,

\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}\Pi_{qj}({\rm div}~{\boldsymbol{n}})n_{q}=(\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}n_{j}-\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}n_{k}n_{q}n_{j}n_{q})({\rm div}~{\boldsymbol{n}})=0.

(3.13)

Hence, using a density argument, the variational formulation of (
3.7
) is: Find
{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\in{\rm H}^{2}_{\Sigma_{\rm 0}}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})
such that,

\int_{\Omega}\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}+\int_{\Omega}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}x_{k}}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{\Omega}g_{i}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}},

(3.14)

for all
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\rm H}^{2}_{\Sigma_{\rm 0}}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})
.

3.3
Existence and uniqueness

Denote by,

{\rm B}[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon},{\boldsymbol{v}}]{:=}\int_{\Omega}\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}\partial_{x_{j}}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}+\int_{\Omega}\mu^{\varepsilon}_{ijk}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}x_{k}}v_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}.

(3.15)

The form
\rm B
is evidently a bilinear form that is continuous in the weak topology of
{\rm H}^{2}\times{\rm H}^{2}
and it remains to show coercivity in order to apply the Lax-Milgram theorem.

3.3.1
Coercivity in HS 1

Using the strong ellipticity conditions in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) together with Cauchy’s inequality with
\delta
we obtain,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \kappa_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\nabla\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3\times 3\times 3)}}^{2}+&c_{1}\left\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3\times 3})}^{2}\\
\leq&{\rm B}[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}]-2\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathsf{S}^{klm}_{ij}(\frac{{\boldsymbol{x}}}{\varepsilon})\frac{\partial^{2}u^{\varepsilon}_{k}}{\partial x_{m}\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial u^{\varepsilon}_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
\leq&{\rm B}[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}]+2\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}||\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}|\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
\leq&{\rm B}[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}]+2\varepsilon^{2}\delta\left\|\nabla\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2\delta}\left\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}.\end{aligned}

(3.16)

Thus,

(\kappa_{1}-2\delta)\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\nabla\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3\times 3\times 3})}^{2}+(c_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2\delta})\left\|\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3\times 3})}^{2}\leq{\rm B}[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}].

(3.17)

By selecting
\delta<\kappa_{1}/4
, using Poincaré’s inequality in
{\rm H^{1}_{\Sigma_{\rm 0}}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}
, and then using the smallness of
\varepsilon
to guarantee
(c_{1}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}}{\kappa_{1}})=:c>0
, we ensure the desired ellipticity:

\min\{\kappa_{1}/2,c\}\,c_{\Omega}\,\varepsilon^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\rm H^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}\leq{\rm B}[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}].

(3.18)

Additionally, starting with (
3.17
), by utilizing Poincaré’s inequality in
{\rm H^{1}_{\Sigma_{\rm 0}}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}
one can obtain the following estimate for the solution (under HS 1):

\Big(\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\rm H^{1}_{\Sigma_{\rm 0}}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}+\varepsilon^{2}\left\|\nabla\nabla{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3\times 3\times 3})}\Big)^{1/2}\leq{\rm const.}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})},

(3.19)

for some generic constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

3.3.2
Coercivity in HS 2

Coercivity in this case can be shown in exactly the same way as in HS 1. We simply write it down and omit the details,

\min\{\kappa_{1}/2,c\}\,c_{\Omega}\,\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\rm H^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}\leq{\rm B}[{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon},{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}].

(3.20)

Naturally, a similar estimate can be obtained under the scheme HS 2,

\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\rm H^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})}\leq{\rm const.}\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\rm L^{2}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})},

again, the constant is a generic constant independent of
\varepsilon

{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\varepsilon}\in\rm{H}^{2}_{\Sigma_{\rm 0}}(\Omega,{\sf I\!R}^{3})
to (
3.14
). We also refer the reader to the works of
[
21
]
,
[
22

Taylor expansion of the stored energy function around the equilibrium

We perform a Taylor expansion of the stored energy function around the equilibrium. In principle we can continue this expansion and obtain any desired degree of accuracy of the nonlinear energy
W
. However, using the scaling introduce previously, we keep only the terms up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3})
leading to,

\displaystyle{\rm W}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{G},\right)=

\displaystyle{\rm{\rm W}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)+\frac{\partial{\rm W}}{\partial F_{ij}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)(F_{ij}-\delta_{ij})+\frac{\partial{\rm W}}{\partial G_{ijk}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)\partial_{x_{k}}F_{ij}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial F_{ij}\partial F_{k\ell}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)(F_{ij}-\delta_{ij})(F_{k\ell}-\delta_{k\ell})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial F_{ij}\partial G_{k\ell m}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)(F_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{x_{m}}F_{k\ell}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial G_{ijk}\partial G_{m\ell p}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)\partial_{x_{k}}F_{ij}\partial_{x_{p}}F_{m\ell}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}).

The potential energy at the equilibrium configuration is zero and, moreover, we assume that the material is stress free at the equilibrium configuration. Hence, the above expansion reduces to the following,

\displaystyle W\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{G}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial F_{ij}\partial F_{k\ell}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)(F_{ij}-\delta_{ij})(F_{k\ell}-\delta_{k\ell})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial F_{ij}\partial G_{k\ell m}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)(F_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\partial_{x_{m}}F_{k\ell}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial G_{ijk}\partial G_{m\ell p}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)\partial_{x_{k}}F_{ij}\partial_{x_{p}}F_{m\ell}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}).

A.1
Mechanical constitutive law for the stress and hyperstress up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})

The first constitutive law for the stress can be obtained from the above energy the following way,

\sigma{=}\frac{\partial{\rm W}}{\partial\mathbb{F}}({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{G}).

In components we have,

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial F_{ij}\partial F_{k\ell}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)(F_{k\ell}-\delta_{k\ell})+\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial F_{ij}\partial G_{k\ell m}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)\partial_{x_{m}}F_{k\ell}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}).

Set,

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{ijk\ell}

\displaystyle{:=}\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial F_{ij}\partial F_{k\ell}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right),\hskip 9.24994pt\mathsf{S}_{ij}^{k\ell m}{:=}\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial F_{ij}\partial G_{k\ell m}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right).

In more compact form we can write,

\sigma_{ij}{=}\mathsf{K}_{ijk\ell}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{\ell}}+\mathsf{S}_{ij}^{k\ell m}\frac{\partial^{2}u_{k}}{\partial x_{m}\partial x_{\ell}}.

(A.1)

The constitutive law for the hyperstress can be obtained,

\mu{=}\frac{\partial{\rm W}}{\partial\mathbb{G}}({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{G}).

In components we obtain,

\mu_{ijk}=\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial F_{n\ell}\partial G_{ijk}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)(F_{n\ell}-\delta_{n\ell})+\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial G_{nk\ell}\partial G_{ijk}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right)\partial_{x_{\ell}}F_{nk}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}).

(A.2)

If we set,

\mathsf{A}^{ijk}_{n\ell p}{:=}\frac{\partial^{2}{\rm W}}{\partial G_{n\ell p}\partial G_{ijk}}\left({\boldsymbol{x}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{0}\right),

(A.3)

then we can compactly write,

\mu_{ijk}{=}\mathsf{A}^{ijk}_{n\ell p}\frac{\partial^{2}u_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}\partial x_{p}}+\mathsf{S}_{n\ell}^{ijk}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{\ell}}.

(A.4)

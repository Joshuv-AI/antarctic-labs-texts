1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra. We say that a sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely additive signed measures on a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
is
pointwise bounded
if
\sup_{n\in\omega}|\mu_{n}(A)|<\infty
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
, and it is
uniformly bounded
if
\sup_{n\in\omega}\|\mu_{n}\|<\infty
(see Section
2
for more definitions).
The Nikodym Uniform Boundedness Theorem implies that if
\mathcal{A}
is
\sigma
-complete, then every pointwise bounded sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
is uniformly bounded. This theorem, due to its numerous applications, is one of the most important results in the theory of vector measures, see Diestel and Uhl
[
4
, Section I.3]
, and hence motivates the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

A Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the
Nikodym property
if every pointwise bounded sequence of finitely additive signed measures on
\mathcal{A}
is uniformly bounded.

Equivalently,
\mathcal{A}
has the Nikodym property if, for every sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely additive signed measures on
\mathcal{A}
such that
\mu_{n}(A)\to 0
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\sup_{n\in\omega}\|\mu_{n}\|<\infty
(see
[
25
, Proposition 2.4]
).

The Nikodym theorem implies that
\sigma
-complete algebras have the Nikodym property. This result has been generalized by many authors who introduced various weaker properties of Boolean algebras that still imply the Nikodym property, see e.g. Haydon
[
15
]
, Schachermayer
[
24
]
, Freniche
[
9
]
.

On the other hand, countable infinite Boolean algebras do not have the Nikodym property. Indeed, the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
of a countable infinite Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
is homeomorphic to an infinite closed subset of the Cantor space
2^{\omega}
, hence it contains a non-trivial sequence
\langle x_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
convergent to some
x\in 2^{\omega}
. Then, the sequence
\big\langle n(\delta_{x_{n}}-\delta_{x})\colon n\in\omega\big\rangle
of Borel measures on
St(\mathcal{A})
induces a pointwise bounded but not uniformly bounded sequence of finitely additive measures on
\mathcal{A}
. This example is a motivation for considering the following type of spaces.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
F
be a free filter on
\omega
. Endow the set
N_{F}=\omega\cup\{p_{F}\}
, where
p_{F}
is a fixed point not belonging to
\omega
, with the topology
\tau_{F}
defined in the following way:

•

every point of
\omega
is isolated in
N_{F}
, i.e.
\{n\}
is open for every
n\in\omega
,

•

a set
U\subseteq N_{F}
is an open neighborhood of
p_{F}
if and only if
U=A\cup\{p_{F}\}
for some
A\in F
.

For every free filter
F
the space
N_{F}
is a countable non-discrete zero-dimensional space (and so a Tychonoff space). The space
N_{Fr}
, where by
Fr
we denote the Fréchet filter on
\omega
(see Section
2
), is homeomorphic to a convergent sequence of points in a given Tychonoff space together with its limit point. Thus, for a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
, the Stone space
St(\mathcal{A})
contains a non-trivial convergent sequence if and only if it contains a homeomorphic copy of
N_{Fr}
. Consequently, if
N_{Fr}
embeds into
St(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property. In this paper we study for which other free filters we also have such an implication.

We need to introduce some notions. We say that a Borel measure
\mu
on a Tychonoff space
X
is
finitely supported
if it can be written in the form
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
for some distinct points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
and some real numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\omega
; in this case we write
\|\mu\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|
(see again Section
2
).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a zero-dimensional (Tychonoff) space. We call a sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely supported measures on
X

anti-Nikodym
, or shortly an
AN-sequence
, if
\|\mu_{n}\|\rightarrow\infty
and
\mu_{n}(A)\rightarrow 0
for every
A\in\clopen(X)
. We say that
X
has the
finitely supported Nikodym property

if there is no anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
X
.

We also say that a free filter
F
on
\omega
has the
Nikodym property
if the space
N_{F}
has a finitely supported Nikodym property. We denote by
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
the class of all ideals on
\omega
whose dual filters do not have the Nikodym property.

We prove that if
\mathcal{A}
is a Boolean algebra,
F
is a free filter on
\omega
which does not have the Nikodym property, and
N_{F}
homeomorphically embeds into
St(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property (Corollary
8.3
). This result generalizes the aforementioned fact about embedding
N_{Fr}
into the Stone spaces of Boolean algebras.

For any non-trivial convergent sequence
\langle x_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
in a given space
N_{F}
, the sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely supported non-negative measures defined for each
n\in\omega
by the equality
\mu_{n}=n\cdot\delta_{x_{n}}
satisfies the conditions
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(\omega)=\infty
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)=0
for every
A\in F
.
We show that the Nikodym property for filters is in fact characterized by similar conditions.

{restatable*}

theoremtheorempositivemeasures
A filter
F
on
\omega
has the Nikodym property if and only if there is no (disjointly supported) sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely supported non-negative measures on
N_{F}
such that:

(1)

\supp(\mu_{n})\subseteq\omega
for every
n\in\omega
,

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(\omega)=\infty
,

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\mu_{n}(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)=0
for every
A\in F
.

Theorem
1
can be translated in the following way, showing a connection between filters without the Nikodym property and exhaustive ideals associated to lower semicontinuous (
lsc
) submeasures on
\omega
.

{restatable*}

be a filter on

. Then, the following are equivalent:

and

;

(3)

\varphi
on
\omega
such that
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
and

.

The Katětov order
\leq_{K}
is an important tool for comparing the structural complexity of ideals on

, see e.g.
[

]
. As a corollary to Theorem

, we get that the class of the ideals dual to filters without the Nikodym property is bounded in this order by the ideal
{\mathcal{Z}}
of subsets of

having

(see Section

).

.

Extending
[

, we present several conditions for a density ideal equivalent to the statement that its dual filter has the Nikodym property (see Sections

We make an analysis of the cofinal structure of the family

ordered by

and we prove that there is a family of size equal to the dominating number

which is
\leq_{K}
-dominating in

, but there are no

-maximal elements in
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
.

.

Using Theorem
1
and results from
[
14
]
and
[

]
, we show that the classes of those filters

on

for which the spaces

(do not) have the Nikodym property are large. First, there are families
{\mathcal{H}}_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
of size continuum
\mathfrak{c}
, consisting respectively of non-isomorphic summable ideals and of non-isomorphic density ideals (Corollaries
7.3
and
7.6
). Next, there are families
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
of size
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, consisting of non-isomorphic filters on

whose dual ideals are tall and which respectively have and do not have the Nikodym property (Corollary

). Those results yield the following corollary.

{restatable*}

There is a family
{\mathcal{F}}
of

many pairwise non-homeomorphic countable non-discrete spaces without non-trivial convergent sequences, and such that for every Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
and

, if

, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property.

Our results also apply to the Grothendieck property of Boolean algebras, which is closely related to the Nikodym property. This property has its origin in Banach space theory, see
[
12
]
.

Definition 1.4
.

A Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
has the
Grothendieck property
if, for every sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely additive signed measures on
\mathcal{A}
such that
\sup_{n\in\omega}\|\mu_{n}\|<\infty
and
\mu_{n}(A)\to 0
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\widehat{\mu}_{n}(B)\to 0
for every Borel subset
B\subseteq St(\mathcal{A})
, where
\widehat{\mu}_{n}
denotes for each
n\in\omega
the Radon extension of
\mu_{n}
onto the space
St(\mathcal{A})
.

The connection between the Grothendieck property and the Nikodym property of Boolean algebras has been a subject of an extensive research (see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
or
[
24
]
), but it is still not fully understood. It is not easy to find an algebra having exactly one of these properties. Schachermayer
[
24
]
showed that the algebra
\mathscr{J}
of Jordan-measurable subsets of the unit interval has the Nikodym property but does not have the Grothendieck property (see also
[
13
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
26
]
). On the other hand, Talagrand
[
30
]
constructed under the assumption of the Continuum Hypothesis a Boolean algebra with the Grothendieck property and without the Nikodym property, but it is still an open problem if one can find such an example in ZFC (cf.
[
27
]
,
[
11
]
).

Marciszewski and Sobota studied in
[
22
]
a property of spaces of the form
N_{F}
that is connected with the Grothendieck property in a similar way as the finitely supported Nikodym property is connected with the Nikodym property of Boolean algebras.
We need again to introduce some piece of notation. For a topological space
X
we denote by
C(X)
the set of all continuous real-valued functions on
X
and by
C_{b}(X)
the set of all bounded continuous real-valued functions on
X
.
If
\mu
is a Borel measure on
X
and
f\in C(X)
, then we write
\mu(f)=\int_{X}fd\mu
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space. A sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely supported measures on
X
is a
bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig sequence
, or shortly a
BJN-sequence
, if
\|\mu_{n}\|=1
for every
n\in\omega
and
\mu_{n}(f)\rightarrow 0
for every
f\in C_{b}(X)
. We say that
X
has the
bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property
, or shortly the
BJNP
, if there is a BJN-sequence of measures on
X
.

We also say that a free filter
F
on
\omega
has the
bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property
, or shortly the
BJNP
, if the space
N_{F}
has the BJNP. We denote by
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
the class of all ideals on
\omega
whose dual filters have the BJNP.

The bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property was introduced in
[
19
]
, as a tool for investigating the linear structure of the spaces
C_{b}(X\times Y)
endowed with the pointwise topology.
It is proved in
[
22
, Corollary D]
that if
\mathcal{A}
is a Boolean algebra,
F
is a free filter on
\omega
that has the BJNP, and
N_{F}
homeomorphically embeds into
St(\mathcal{A})
, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Grothendieck property.

By Proposition
6.1
,
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
is a subclass of
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
. As we mentioned earlier, by Theorem
1
no element of
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
is maximal with respect to the order
\leq_{K}
. In contrast, the class
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
has
\leq_{K}
-maximal elements, e.g. the asymptotic density zero ideal
{\mathcal{Z}}
is such an element (see
[
22
, Theorem C]
). Moreover, Theorem
1
shows that for a density ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
it is equivalent that
{\mathcal{I}}\notin\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
and that
{\mathcal{I}}
is a
\leq_{K}
-maximal element in
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
. For more details on differences between the classes
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
see Section
6
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we describe basic notation and terminology used in this paper.
In Section 3 we analyze basic properties of anti-Nikodym sequences of measures on spaces
N_{F}
. Section 4 is devoted to proving characterizations of the Nikodym property of filters, and studying connections between this property and submeasures on
\omega
. In Section 5 we present several conditions equivalent to
{\mathcal{I}}\not\in\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
for a density ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
, and we investigate the Katětov order on the class
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
, in particular its cofinal structure. In Section 6 we compare the Nikodym property with the bounded Josefson–Nissenzweig property of filters, and also classes of ideals on
\omega
related to these properties. In Section 7 we analyze some families of ideals that are contained in the class
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
, which give us some rich substructures in the order
(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})
. Section 8 is devoted to applications of the Nikodym property of filters to Boolean algebras. The last section provides some remarks and open questions.

2.
Preliminaries

By
\omega
we denote the first infinite (countable) cardinal number. We also denote
\omega_{+}=\omega\mathbin{\backslash}\{0\}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
. For
A,B\subseteq\omega
we write
A\subseteq^{*}B
if
A\mathbin{\backslash}B
is finite. For every
k,n\in\omega
such that
k<n
we set
[k,n]=\{k,k+1,\ldots,n\}
. By
\mathfrak{c}
we denote the cardinality of the real line
\mathbb{R}
, and by
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
—the cardinal successor of
\mathfrak{c}
.

If
A
is a non-empty set, then
{\mathcal{P}}(A)
denotes its power set. A family
F\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}(A)
is a
filter on
A
if
\emptyset\notin F
,
A\in F
and
F
is closed under finite intersections and taking supersets. By
Fr
we denote the
Fréchet filter
on
\omega
, i.e.
Fr=\{B\subseteq\omega\colon\omega\mathbin{\backslash}B
is finite
\}
. A filter
F
on
\omega
is
free
if
Fr\subseteq F
. Throughout this article, all filters on
\omega
are assumed to be free. For a family
S\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}(A)
we put
S^{*}=\{X\colon A\mathbin{\backslash}X\in S\}
—
S^{*}
is said to be
dual to
S
.

A family
{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)
is an
ideal on
\omega
if
{\mathcal{I}}^{*}
is a free filter on
\omega
. Note that this implies
Fin\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}
, where
Fin
is the ideal of all finite subsets of
\omega
. An ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
on
\omega
is a
P-ideal
if for every sequence
\langle A_{n}\in{\mathcal{I}}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
there is
A\in{\mathcal{I}}
such that
A_{n}\subseteq^{*}A
for every
n\in\omega
. We say that an ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
on
\omega
is
tall
if for every infinite
A\subseteq\omega
there exists an infinite
B\subseteq A
such that
B\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

We denote the set of all functions from
\omega
to
\omega
by
\omega^{\omega}
. We define a preorder
\leq^{*}
on
\omega^{\omega}
by setting
f\leq^{*}g
if
f(n)\leq g(n)
for all but finitely many
n\in\omega
. There are two cardinal invariants related to this preorder:

•

\mathfrak{d}
is the smallest size of a family
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
such that for every
g\in\omega^{\omega}
there is
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
with
g\leq^{*}f
(such a family is called
dominating
in
\omega^{\omega}
);

•

\mathfrak{b}
is the smallest size of a family
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
such that for every
g\in\omega^{\omega}
there is
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
with
f\nleq^{*}g
(such a family is called
unbounded
in
\omega^{\omega}
).

The invariants
\mathfrak{d}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are examples of the
cardinal characteristics of the continuum
, i.e. cardinal invariants which are between
\aleph_{1}
and
\mathfrak{c}
. Such an invariants are studied extensively in the field of infinitary combinatorics, see
[
3
]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Boolean algebra.
St(\mathcal{A})
denotes the Stone space of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e. the space of all ultrafilters on
\mathcal{A}
endowed with the standard topology. If
A\in\mathcal{A}
, then
[A]_{\mathcal{A}}
denotes the clopen set in
St(\mathcal{A})
corresponding via the Stone duality to
A
.

A function
\mu\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
measure on a Boolean algebra

\mathcal{A}
if it is a finitely additive function of bounded total variation, that is,

\|\mu\|=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|\colon A,B\in\mathcal{A},A\land B=0_{\mathcal{A}}\big\}<\infty.

Let
X
be a Tychonoff space. By
\clopen(X)
we denote the family of clopen subsets of
X
, i.e. subsets which are both open and closed. When we say that
\mu
is a
measure on

X
, then we mean that
\mu
is a
\sigma
-additive regular signed measure defined on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
Bor(X)
of
X
which has bounded total variation, that is,

\|\mu\|=\sup\big\{|\mu(A)|+|\mu(B)|\colon A,B\in Bor(X),A\cap B=\emptyset\big\}<\infty.

By
|\mu|(\cdot)
we denote the
variation
of
\mu
; note that
\|\mu\|=|\mu|(X)
.
\supp(\mu)
denotes the
support
of
\mu
. If
A\in Bor(X)
, then
\mu\restriction A
is defined by the formula

(\mu\restriction A)(B)=\mu(A\cap B)
for every
B\in Bor(X)
.
\mu
is a
non-negative measure
if
\mu(A)\geq 0
for every
A\in Bor(X)
, and it is a
probability measure
if it is non-negative and
\mu(X)=1
.

We define the
Radon extension

\widehat{\mu}
of a measure
\mu
on a Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
as the unique measure on
St(\mathcal{A})
such that
\widehat{\mu}\big([A]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)=\mu(A)
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

If
X
is a Tychonoff space and
x\in X
, then
\delta_{x}
denotes the one-point measure on
X
concentrated at
x
.
We say that a measure
\mu
on
X
is
finitely supported
if it is of the form
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\cdot\delta_{x_{i}}
for some
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\mathbin{\backslash}\{0\}
and distinct
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
.
It follows that

\supp(\mu)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
,
\|\mu\|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|
, and that for every
A\in Bor(X)
we have
\mu\restriction A=\sum_{i\colon x_{i}\in A}\alpha_{i}\cdot\delta_{x_{i}}
and
|\mu|(A)=\|\mu\restriction A\|=\sum_{i\colon x_{i}\in A}|\alpha_{i}|
.

For a non-negative measure
\mu
supported on a finite set

X
we define the following numbers (cf.
[
5
, Section 1.13]
):

\at^{+}(\mu)=\max\big\{\mu(\{x\})\colon x\in X\big\},

\at^{-}(\mu)=\min\big\{\mu(\{x\})\colon x\in X\big\}.

If
\mu
is a measure on
X
and
f\in C(X)
, then we set
\mu(f)=\int_{X}fd\mu
if the integral exists. Note that if
\mu
is of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\cdot\delta_{x_{i}}
, then
\mu(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\cdot f(x_{i})
.

3.
Anti-Nikodym sequences of measures on spaces
N_{F}

In this section we study the main properties of anti-Nikodym sequences of measures on spaces
N_{F}
.

We start with a remark about the possible generalization of the finitely supported Nikodym property. One may ask whether we could exchange the condition that
\mu_{n}(A)\rightarrow 0
for every
A\in\clopen(X)
for the condition that
\mu_{n}(f)\rightarrow 0
for every
f\in C_{b}(X)
, and in this way generalize the definition of the finitely supported Nikodym property to non-zero-dimensional spaces. However, this property is not satisfied for any Tychonoff space
(X,\tau)
which has a weaker compact Hausdorff topology
\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\tau
, as it would imply that there is a sequence of measures on
(X,\tau^{\prime})
which is weak* convergent to
0
and unbounded in norm in the dual space
C(X,\tau^{\prime})^{*}
of bounded linear functionals on the Banach space
C(X,\tau^{\prime})
—that would contradict the Banach–Steinhaus theorem for
C(X,\tau^{\prime})
.
In particular, for any filter
F
the space
N_{F}
does not satisfy this stronger property, as
F
contains the Fréchet filter
Fr
and so the topology
\tau_{Fr}
is weaker than
\tau_{F}
, and
N_{Fr}
is compact being homeomorphic to a convergent sequence of real numbers together with its limit point.

Remark 3.1
.

If
F\subseteq G
are filters on
\omega
, then an AN-sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of measures on
N_{G}
is also AN-sequence on
N_{F}
, because the topology of
N_{G}
is
finer
than the topology of
N_{F}
, so
\clopen(N_{F})\subseteq\clopen(N_{G})
. The same is true for BJN-sequences of measures.

The following result shows that we can always modify an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on a space
N_{F}
so that the measures have disjoint supports. It is known that the analogous result holds for BJN-sequences of measures on any Tychonoff space (see
[
22
, Lemma 5.1.(3)]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

If a filter
F
on
\omega
does not have the Nikodym property, then there is an AN-sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of measures on
N_{F}
which is disjointly supported, that is,
\supp(\mu_{k})\cap\supp(\mu_{l})=\emptyset
for every
k\neq l\in\omega
.

Proof.

Let
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
be an AN-sequence of measures on
N_{F}
.

Step 1.
We will find an AN-sequence
\langle\theta_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
on
N_{F}
such that
\supp(\theta_{k})\cap\supp(\theta_{l})\cap\omega=\emptyset
for every
k\neq l\in\omega
, i.e. with supports disjoint on
\omega
.

We first define inductively a strictly increasing sequence
\langle n_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
of natural numbers and an increasing sequence
\langle A_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
of subsets of
N_{F}
satisfying
A_{k}=\big[0,\max\big(\bigcup_{i=0}^{k-1}\supp(\mu_{n_{i}})\cap\omega\big)\big]
and
|\mu_{n_{k}}|(A_{k})<1/k
for every
k\in\omega
. We start with
n_{0}=0
and
A_{0}=\emptyset
. Assume now that for some
k\in\omega_{+}
we have defined natural numbers
n_{0}<\ldots<n_{k-1}
and subsets
A_{0}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq A_{k-1}
of
N_{F}
as required.
The set
A_{k}=\big[0,\max\big(\bigcup_{i=0}^{k-1}\supp(\mu_{n_{i}})\cap\omega\big)\big]
is a finite subset of
\omega
.
As
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is an AN-sequence and singletons are clopen, there exists
n_{k}>n_{k-1}
such that
|\mu_{n_{k}}|(A_{k})<1/k
.

We now define a measure

\theta_{k}=\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction(N_{F}\mathbin{\backslash}A_{k})

for every
k\in\omega
. The sequence
\langle\theta_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
of measures on
N_{F}
will meet the requirements, as:

•

for every pair of natural numbers
k<l
we have
\supp(\theta_{k})\cap\omega\subseteq A_{k+1}
and
\supp(\theta_{l})\cap A_{k+1}=\emptyset
, by the definition of the sequence
\langle A_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
,

•

for all
k\in\omega_{+}
we have

\|\theta_{k}\|\geq\|\mu_{n_{k}}\|-\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}\|>\|\mu_{n_{k}}\|-1/k\textrm{, and so }\|\theta_{k}\|\rightarrow\infty,

•

for all
A\in\clopen(N_{F})
we have
\theta_{k}(A)\rightarrow 0
, as
\mu_{n_{k}}(A)\rightarrow 0
and

\big|\theta_{k}(A)-\mu_{n_{k}}(A)\big|\leq\|\theta_{k}-\mu_{n_{k}}\|=\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction A_{k}\|<1/k\rightarrow 0.

Step 2.
In Step
1
we constructed an AN-sequence
\langle\theta_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
on
N_{F}
with supports disjoint on
\omega
, and we will now “remove” the point
p_{F}
from the supports. We have two cases.

a)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\big|\theta_{n}(\{p_{F}\})\big|<\infty
:

We can find a subsequence
\langle\theta_{n_{k}}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
such that
\theta_{n_{k}}\big(\{p_{F}\}\big)\to\alpha
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, hence
\big\langle\theta_{n_{k}}\big(\{p_{F}\}\big)\colon k\in\omega\big\rangle
is a Cauchy sequence. We define a sequence
\langle\nu_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
of measures by setting

\nu_{k}=(\theta_{n_{2k}}-\theta_{n_{2k+1}})\restriction\omega

for every
k\in\omega
. Then,
\langle\nu_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
is a disjointly supported AN-sequence on
N_{F}
, as:

•

for every
k\in\omega
we have

\supp(\nu_{k})=\big(\supp(\theta_{n_{2k}})\cup\supp(\theta_{n_{2k+1}})\big)\cap\omega,

thus
\supp(\nu_{k})\cap\supp(\nu_{l})=\emptyset
for
k\neq l
as
\theta_{n}
’s have disjoint supports on
\omega
,

•

we have
\|\nu_{k}\|=\|\theta_{n_{2k}}\restriction\omega\|+\|\theta_{n_{2k+1}}\restriction\omega\|\rightarrow\infty
, because

\|\theta_{n_{l}}\restriction\omega\|\rightarrow\infty
(which follows from
\|\theta_{n_{l}}\|\rightarrow\infty
and
\|\theta_{n_{l}}\restriction\{p_{F}\}\|\rightarrow|\alpha|
),

•

for all
A\in\clopen(N_{F})
we have:

\nu_{k}(A)=\big(\theta_{n_{2k}}-\theta_{n_{2k+1}}\big)(A\cap\omega)=\big(\theta_{n_{2k}}-\theta_{n_{2k+1}}\big)(A)-\big(\theta_{n_{2k}}-\theta_{n_{2k+1}}\big)(A\cap\{p_{F}\})\rightarrow 0,

as
\langle\theta_{n_{k}}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
is an AN-sequence and
\theta_{n_{2k}}(\{p_{F}\})-\theta_{n_{2k+1}}(\{p_{F}\})\rightarrow 0
holds because
\big\langle\theta_{n_{k}}\big(\{p_{F}\}\big)\colon k\in\omega\big\rangle
is Cauchy.

b)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|\theta_{n}(\{p_{F}\})\big|=\infty
:

Without losing of generality we may assume that the sequence
\big\langle\big|\theta_{n}(\{p_{F}\})\big|\colon n\in\omega\big\rangle
is strictly increasing. If we denote

\alpha_{n}=\frac{\theta_{2n}(\{p_{F}\})}{\theta_{2n+1}(\{p_{F}\})},

then we get that
|\alpha_{n}|<1
for all
n\in\omega
.
We define a sequence
\langle\nu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of measures by setting

\nu_{n}=(\theta_{2n}-\alpha_{n}\cdot\theta_{2n+1})\restriction\omega

for every
n\in\omega
.
Then,
\langle\nu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is a disjointly supported AN-sequence on
N_{F}
, as:

•

for every
n\in\omega
we have

\supp(\nu_{n})=\big(\supp(\theta_{2n})\cup\supp(\theta_{2n+1})\big)\cap\omega,

thus
\supp(\nu_{k})\cap\supp(\nu_{l})=\emptyset
for
k\neq l
as
\theta_{n}
’s have disjoint supports on
\omega
,

•

we have
\|\nu_{n}\|=\|\theta_{2n}\restriction\omega\|+|\alpha_{n}|\cdot\|\theta_{2n+1}\restriction\omega\|\rightarrow\infty
, because for every
k\in\omega
it holds

\|\theta_{k}\restriction\omega\|\geq|\theta_{k}(\omega)|=|\theta_{k}(N_{F})-\theta_{k}(\{p_{F}\})|\rightarrow\infty,

which follows from
\theta_{k}(N_{F})\rightarrow 0
and the assumption
\big|\theta_{k}(\{p_{F}\})\big|\rightarrow\infty
,

•

for all
A\in\clopen(N_{F})
we have:

\nu_{n}(A)=\big(\theta_{2n}-\alpha_{n}\cdot\theta_{2n+1}\big)(A\cap\omega)=\big(\theta_{2n}-\alpha_{n}\cdot\theta_{2n+1}\big)(A)-\big(\theta_{2n}-\alpha_{n}\cdot\theta_{2n+1}\big)(A\cap\{p_{F}\})\rightarrow 0,

as
\theta_{2n}(\{p_{F}\})-\alpha_{n}\cdot\theta_{2n+1}(\{p_{F}\})=0
,
\langle\theta_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is an AN-sequence and
\langle\alpha_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is bounded.

∎

We will now characterize when a sequence of finitely supported measures on a space
N_{F}
is an AN-sequence.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
F
be a filter on
\omega
and
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
a sequence of finitely supported measures on
N_{F}
. Then
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is an AN-sequence on
N_{F}
if and only if the following three conditions simultaneously hold:

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mu_{n}\|=\infty
,

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(N_{F})=0
,

(3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|\mu_{n}\restriction(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)\big\|=0
for every
A\in F
.

Proof.

Let
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
be an AN-sequence on
N_{F}
. Then,
(1)
and
(2)
hold by the definition. By Proposition
3.2
, without losing of generality we may assume that the sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is disjointly supported. Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that for some
A\in F
there is a subsequence
\langle\mu_{n_{k}}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)\big\|=C
for some
C>0
. We may additionally assume that
\big\|\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)\big\|\geq C/2
for every
k\in\omega
. For each
k\in\omega
there exists
F_{k}\subseteq\supp\big(\mu_{n_{k}}\restriction(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)\big)
satisfying
|\mu_{n_{k}}(F_{k})|\geq C/4
. Since
\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A
is a discrete clopen subspace of
N_{F}
, the set
F=\bigcup_{k\in\omega}F_{k}
is clopen in
N_{F}
. But
\lim_{k\to\infty}|\mu_{n_{k}}(F)|\geq C/4
, contradicting the fact that
\langle\mu_{n_{k}}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
is an AN-sequence on
N_{F}
.

Assume now that
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is a sequence of finitely supported measures on
N_{F}
satisfying conditions
(1)
–
(3)
. If
C\in\clopen(N_{F})
is such that
p_{F}\notin C
, then it must be of the form
\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A
for some
A\in F
, and so
\mu_{n}(C)\rightarrow 0
by
(3)
. On the other hand, if
C\in\clopen(N_{F})
is such that
p_{F}\in C
, then we already know that
\mu_{n}(N_{F}\mathbin{\backslash}C)\rightarrow 0
, thus
\mu_{n}(C)=\mu_{n}(N_{F})-\mu_{n}(N_{F}\mathbin{\backslash}C)\rightarrow 0
, by
(2)
.
∎

4.
The Nikodym property and submeasures on
\omega

In this section we prove our main results about filters
F
without the Nikodym property and their corresponding dual ideals. We will first characterize them using a sequences of non-negative measures on
\omega
, and next using particular ideals on
\omega
associated with submeasures. Theorems
1
and
1
, and their proofs, were strongly inspired by similar characterizations of filters with the BJNP due to Marciszewski and Sobota
[
22
, Theorem B and Theorem 6.2]
.

\theorempositivemeasures

Proof.

Assume that the filter
F
does not have the Nikodym property. By Proposition
3.2
, there is an AN-sequence
\langle\theta_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of measures on
N_{F}
with pairwise disjoint supports contained entirely in
\omega
. Let us define a measure
\mu_{n}=|\theta_{n}|
for each
n\in\omega
. It follows that each
\mu_{n}

\mu_{n}(\omega)=\|\theta_{n}\|\rightarrow\infty

\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\mu_{n}(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\big\|\theta_{n}\restriction(\omega\mathbin{\backslash}A)\big\|=0

for every
A\in F

Assume now that there is a sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely supported non-negative measures on
N_{F}
satisfying conditions

–

. For each
n\in\omega

\nu_{n}=\mu_{n}(\omega)\cdot\delta_{p_{F}}-\mu_{n}.

By Proposition

\langle\nu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle

N_{F}
.
∎

Note that conditions

and
(3)
in the above theorem imply
\mu_{n}(A)\to\infty
for
A\in F
.

Let us recall some standard definitions concerning submeasures on
\omega
. A function
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)\to[0,\infty]
is a
submeasure
if
\varphi(\emptyset)=0
,
\varphi(\{n\})<\infty
for every
n\in\omega
,
\varphi(X)\leq\varphi(Y)

X\subseteq Y
, and
\varphi(X\cup Y)\leq\varphi(X)+\varphi(Y)
for every

. A submeasure
\varphi
on

is

if
\varphi(A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(A\cap[0,n])
for every
A\subseteq\omega
. We consider the following ideal associated with an lsc submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
:

\exh(\varphi)=\big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(A\mathbin{\backslash}[0,n])=0\big\},

called the
exhaustive ideal
of
\varphi
.
It is not difficult to show that
\exh(\varphi)
is an
F_{\sigma\delta}
P-ideal for every lsc submeasure
\varphi
. There following result of Solecki characterizes analytic P-ideals on
\omega
in terms of submeasures.

Theorem 4.1

(Solecki
[
28
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be an ideal on
\omega
such that
Fin\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}
. Then
{\mathcal{I}}
is an analytic P-ideal if and only if there is a lsc submeasure
\varphi
such that
{\mathcal{I}}=\exh(\varphi)
.

For submeasures
\varphi
and
\psi
on
\omega
we write
\psi\leq\varphi
if
\psi(A)\leq\varphi(A)
for every
A\subseteq\omega
. Following Farah
[
5
, page 21]
, we call a submeasure
\varphi

non-pathological
if for every
A\subseteq\omega
we have:

\varphi(A)=\sup\{\mu(A)\colon\mu\textrm{ is a non-negative measure on }\omega\textrm{ such that }\mu\leq\varphi\}.

Trivially, every non-negative measure on
\omega
is non-pathological.

The family of density submeasures is an important subfamily of non-pathological lsc submeasures. Recall that a submeasure
\varphi
on
\omega
is a
density submeasure
if there exists a sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of finitely supported non-negative measures on
\omega
with pairwise disjoint supports
such that

\varphi=\sup_{n\in\omega}\mu_{n}.

An ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
on
\omega
is a
density ideal
if there is a density submeasure
\varphi
such that
{\mathcal{I}}=\exh(\varphi)
. We say in this situation that
{\mathcal{I}}
is
generated by the sequence

\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
. Note that
X\in\exh(\varphi)
if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(X)=0
.

A prototypical ideal for the class of density ideals is the
(asymptotic) density zero
ideal
{\mathcal{Z}}=\exh(\varphi_{d})
, where the
asymptotic density submeasure

\varphi_{d}
is defined for every
A\subseteq\omega
by:

\varphi_{d}(A)=\sup_{n\in\omega}\frac{\big|A\cap[2^{n},2^{n+1})\big|}{2^{n}}.

We are now ready to provide a characterization of the Nikodym property of filters in terms of exhaustive ideals.

5.
The Nikodym property and the Katětov order

We will focus in this section on the structure of the Katětov order (and its variant) on the ideals connected with the Nikodym property.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}
be ideals on
\omega
. We say that
{\mathcal{I}}
is
Katětov below

{\mathcal{J}}
, which we denote by
{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
, if there is a function

such that
f^{-1}[A]\in{\mathcal{J}}
for all
A\in{\mathcal{I}}
. We call such a function
f
a
Katětov reduction
.
Similarly, we say that
{\mathcal{I}}
is
Katětov–Blass below

{\mathcal{J}}
, which we denote by
{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{KB}{\mathcal{J}}
, if there is a finite-to-one function
f\colon\omega\to\omega
such that
f^{-1}[A]\in{\mathcal{J}}
for all
A\in{\mathcal{I}}
, and we call such a function
f
a
Katětov–Blass reduction
.
We write
{\mathcal{I}}<_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
if
{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}\nleq_{K}{\mathcal{I}}
. We say that
{\mathcal{I}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}
are
Katětov equivalent
, which we denote by
{\mathcal{I}}\equiv_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
, if
{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
and
{\mathcal{J}}\leq_{K}{\mathcal{I}}
. Trivially,
{\mathcal{I}}\subseteq{\mathcal{J}}
implies
{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{KB}{\mathcal{J}}
, with the identity function being a Katětov–Blass reduction.
We say that
{\mathcal{I}}
is
isomorphic to

{\mathcal{J}}
, which we denote by
{\mathcal{I}}\cong{\mathcal{J}}
, if there is a bijection
f\colon\omega\to\omega
such that
A\in{\mathcal{I}}
if and only if

for all

. It is immediate that
{\mathcal{I}}\cong{\mathcal{J}}
implies

.
We define the Katětov order for filters on
\omega
in the natural way via dual ideals.

m=f(n)
. Next, for all
n\geq 2
we have

n\cdot\big(f(n)\big)^{4}\geq 2n^{2}\cdot f(n)\textrm{, as }\big(f(n)\big)^{3}\geq 2n.

Therefore, by Theorem
\ref{thm_katetov}.(2)
, we get
\Phi(f)\leq_{K}\Phi(g)
.

Let
\langle\lambda_{n}\colon n\in\omega_{+}\rangle
and
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega_{+}\rangle
be the sequences of measures generating the ideals
\Phi(g)
and
\Phi(f)
, respectively, as described in the definition of the operator
\Phi
. Let
A_{n}=\supp(\lambda_{n})
and
B_{n}=\supp(\mu_{n})
for each
n\in\omega_{+}
, so that
|A_{n}|=n^{2}\cdot f(f(n))
and
|B_{n}|=n\cdot f(n)
. We have
\lambda_{n}(x)=1/\big(n\cdot f(f(n))\big)
for every
x\in A_{n}
and
\mu_{n}(x)=1/f(n)
for every
x\in B_{n}
.

To show that
\Phi(g)\nleq_{K}\Phi(f)
, assume for the sake of contradiction that there is a Katětov reduction
\varphi\colon\omega\to\omega
witnessing
\Phi(g)\leq_{K}\Phi(f)
. Since every density ideal is a P-ideal, by Lemma
5.1
we may additionally assume that
\varphi
is finite-to-one. It is enough to find such a subset
X\subseteq\omega
that

X\notin\exh\big(\sup_{n\in\omega_{+}}\mu_{n}\big)=\Phi(f)\textrm{ and }\varphi[X]\in\exh\big(\sup_{n\in\omega_{+}}\lambda_{n}\big)=\Phi(g)

(as
X\subseteq\varphi^{-1}\big[\varphi[X]\big]
, this will imply
\varphi^{-1}\big[\varphi[X]\big]\notin\exh\big(\sup_{n\in\omega_{+}}\mu_{n}\big)
, contradicting that
\varphi
is a Katětov reduction). We proceed in two cases.

Case 1.
There is a strictly increasing sequence
\langle n_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
in
\omega_{+}
such that for each
k\in\omega
there exists
F_{k}\subseteq B_{n_{k}}
satisfying
|F_{k}|=f(n_{k})
and
\varphi[F_{k}]\subseteq\bigcup\big\{A_{i}\colon f(i)\geq n_{k}\big\}
.

Let
X=\bigcup_{k\in\omega}F_{k}
. Since for every
k\in\omega
we have
\mu_{n_{k}}(X)=1
, it follows that
X\notin\exh\big(\sup_{n\in\omega_{+}}\mu_{n}\big)
. Next, for every
m\in\omega_{+}
we have

\varphi[X]\cap A_{m}=\bigcup_{k\in\omega}\varphi[F_{k}]\cap A_{m}\subseteq\bigcup\big\{\varphi[F_{k}]\colon n_{k}\leq f(m)\big\},

and so

\big|\varphi[X]\cap A_{m}\big|\leq\sum\Big\{\big|\varphi[F_{k}]\big|\colon n_{k}\leq f(m)\Big\}\leq\sum_{i\leq f(m)}f(i)=f(f(m))+\sum_{i<f(m)}f(i)\leq 2\cdot f(f(m)),

where the last inequality follows from
f(f(m))\geq\sum_{i<f(m)}f(i)
. Therefore, for every
m\in\omega_{+}
we have:

\lambda_{m}\big(\varphi[X]\big)\leq\frac{2\cdot f(f(m))}{m\cdot f(f(m))}=\frac{2}{m},

which shows that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\lambda_{m}\big(\varphi[X]\big)=0
, and hence that
\varphi[X]\in\exh\big(\sup_{n\in\omega_{+}}\lambda_{n}\big)
.

Case 2.
If we are not in Case
1
, then there exists
N\in\omega_{+}
such that for each
n\geq N
there is no subset
F_{n}\subseteq B_{n}
satisfying
|F_{n}|=f(n)
(i.e.
\mu_{n}(F_{n})=1
) and
\varphi[F_{n}]\subseteq\bigcup\big\{A_{i}\colon f(i)\geq n\big\}
. Then, for every
n\geq N
there must exist
F_{n}\subseteq B_{n}
satisfying
\mu_{n}(F_{n})=n-1
and
\varphi[F_{n}]\subseteq\bigcup\big\{A_{i}\colon f(i)<n\big\}
. Without losing generality we may assume that
N\geq 3
.

First, let us take any
n\geq N
. As
f(i)\geq i^{4}
for every
i\in\omega
, we have

\big|\{i\in\omega_{+}\colon f(i)<n\}\big|<\sqrt[4]{n}\leq\sqrt{n-1}.

Thus, from
F_{n}\subseteq\bigcup\big\{\varphi^{-1}[A_{i}]\colon f(i)<n\big\}
it follows that there exists such a number
i(n)\in\omega_{+}
that
f(i(n))<n
and

\mu_{n}\big(F_{n}\cap\varphi^{-1}[A_{i(n)}]\big)\geq(n-1)\big/\sqrt{n-1}=\sqrt{n-1}.

By
f(i(n))<n
and
f(i(n))\geq i(n)^{4}
, it follows that
n\geq i(n)^{4}+1
and so
\sqrt{n-1}\geq i(n)^{2}
.
For each
n\geq N
let us define

E_{n}=F_{n}\cap\varphi^{-1}[A_{i(n)}].

Then,
E_{n}\subseteq B_{n}
,
\mu_{n}(E_{n})=\sqrt{n-1}
, and
\varphi[E_{n}]\subseteq A_{i(n)}
.

For each
n\in\omega_{+}
let
A_{n}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n^{2}}C_{j}^{n}
be a division of
A_{n}
into
n^{2}
disjoint parts of size
f(f(n))
, i.e.
\lambda_{n}(C_{j}^{n})=1/n
for every
j=1,\ldots,n^{2}
and
C_{j}^{n}\cap C_{i}^{n}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
. As we have

E_{n}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{n^{2}}\varphi^{-1}\Big[C_{j}^{i(n)}\Big],

for every

n\geq N
there exists
j(n)\in\{1,\ldots,n^{2}\}
such that

\mu_{n}\Big(E_{n}\cap\varphi^{-1}\Big[C_{j(n)}^{i(n)}\Big]\Big)\geq\sqrt{n-1}\big/i(n)^{2}\geq 1,

where the last inequality follows from
n\geq i(n)^{4}+1
.
For each
n\geq N
let us denote

D_{n}=E_{n}\cap\varphi^{-1}\Big[C_{j(n)}^{i(n)}\Big].

We have
D_{n}\subseteq B_{n}
,
\mu_{n}(D_{n})\geq 1
, and
\varphi[D_{n}]\subseteq C_{j(n)}^{i(n)}\subseteq A_{i(n)}
.

As
\varphi
is finite-to-one, there is an increasing sequence
\langle n_{k}:\ k\in\omega\rangle
of natural numbers such that
i(n_{k})<i(n_{j})
for every
k<j
. Let

X=\bigcup_{k\in\omega}D_{n_{k}}.

Since for every
k\in\omega
we have
\mu_{n_{k}}(X)\geq 1
, it follows that
X\notin\exh\big(\sup_{n\in\omega_{+}}\mu_{n}\big)
. Finally, we have

\varphi[X]\subseteq\bigcup_{k\in\omega}C_{j(n_{k})}^{i(n_{k})},

where the union is disjoint, and so
\lambda_{i(n_{k})}(\varphi[X])\leq 1/i(n_{k})
for every
k\in\omega
(and
\lambda_{m}(\varphi[X])=0
for other
m\in\omega
). Thus, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\big(\varphi[X]\big)=0
, due to the fact that
i(n_{k})\to\infty
. Therefore, we have
\varphi[X]\in\exh\big(\sup_{n\in\omega_{+}}\lambda_{n}\big)
.

∎

Corollary 5.12
.

For every family
\{{\mathcal{I}}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
, where
\kappa<\mathfrak{b}
, there is
{\mathcal{J}}\in\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
such that
{\mathcal{I}}_{\alpha}<_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
for all
\alpha<\kappa
.

Proof.

If
\{{\mathcal{I}}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
and
\kappa<\mathfrak{b}
, then, by Theorem
1
, for each
\alpha<\kappa
there is
{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
such that
{\mathcal{I}}_{\alpha}<_{K}{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha}
. By Corollary
5.10
, there is
{\mathcal{J}}\in\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
satisfying
{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha}\leq_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
for every
\alpha<\kappa
, thus we have
{\mathcal{I}}_{\alpha}<_{K}{\mathcal{J}}
for all
\alpha<\kappa
.
∎

6.
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
vs
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}

We will now summarize differences between the classes of ideals
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
. As we said in Introduction, these are connected with differences between the Nikodym property and the Grothendieck property of Boolean algebras. We start with the following result, stating that
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
is a subclass of
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
. Note that it actually follows from Theorems
1
and
6.2
, but for the convenience of the reader and the self-containment of the paper we provide here a direct (and more analytic) argument.

Proposition 6.1
.

If a filter
F
does not have the Nikodym property, then it has the BJNP. Consequently,
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

Let
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
N_{F}
. As
\|\mu_{n}\|\rightarrow\infty
, by taking a suitable subsequence we may assume that
\|\mu_{n}\|\geq 1
for each
n\in\omega
. We define a sequence
\langle\lambda_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
of measures by setting
\lambda_{n}=\mu_{n}/\|\mu_{n}\|
for each
n\in\omega
. We have
\|\lambda_{n}\|=1
for all
n\in\omega
and
|\lambda_{n}(A)|=|\mu_{n}(A)|/\|\mu_{n}\|\leq|\mu_{n}(A)|/1\rightarrow 0
for every
A\in\clopen(N_{F})
.

By
[
22
, Lemma 3.8.(2)]
, the Čech–Stone compactification
\beta N_{F}
is the Stone space of some Boolean algebra, thus in particular it is zero-dimensional.
For every
n\in\omega
we define a measure
\overline{\lambda_{n}}
on
\beta N_{F}
by setting
\overline{\lambda_{n}}(A)=\lambda_{n}(A\cap N_{F})
for each
A\in Bor(\beta N_{F})
. By the definition we have
\big\|\overline{\lambda_{n}}\big\|=1
for each
n\in\omega
and

\overline{\lambda_{n}}(A)\rightarrow 0
for every
A\in\clopen(\beta N_{F})
. We will show that, moreover, for every
f\in C(\beta N_{F})
we have
\int_{\beta N_{F}}fd\overline{\lambda_{n}}\to 0
, i.e.
\langle\overline{\lambda_{n}}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is a BJN-sequence of measures on
\beta N_{F}
.

Let us take arbitrary
f\in C(\beta N_{F})
and
\varepsilon>0
. By the zero-dimensionality of
\beta N_{F}
and the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, the linear span of the set
\big\{\chi_{A}\colon A\in\clopen(\beta N_{F})\big\}
is norm dense in
C(\beta N_{F})
with the supremum norm. Therefore, there exist
m\in\omega
, real numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
, and clopen subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
of
\beta N_{F}
such that
\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon/2
. By the convergence of the sequence
\langle\overline{\lambda_{n}}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
on clopen sets, there is
N\in\omega
such that for every
n\geq N
we have:

\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|\cdot|\overline{\lambda_{n}}(A_{i})|<\varepsilon/2.

Then, for every
n\geq N
it holds:

\Big|\int_{\beta N_{F}}fd\overline{\lambda_{n}}\Big|\leq\int_{\beta N_{F}}\Big|f-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}\Big|d\overline{\lambda_{n}}+\int_{\beta N_{F}}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}\Big|d\overline{\lambda_{n}}\leq

\leq\Big\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}\Big\|_{\infty}\cdot\|\overline{\lambda_{n}}\|+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\alpha_{i}|\cdot|\overline{\lambda_{n}}(A_{i})|<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon,

which proves that
\langle\overline{\lambda_{n}}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is a BJN-sequence of measures on
\beta N_{F}
.

To show that
\langle\lambda_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is a BJN-sequence of measures on
N_{F}
, let
f\in C_{b}(N_{F})
. By the universal property of the Čech–Stone compactification, there is a continuous extension
\overline{f}\in C(\beta N_{F})
of
f
. We proved that

\int_{\beta N_{F}}\overline{f}d\overline{\lambda_{n}}\to 0
, and by the definition of
\overline{\lambda_{n}}
’s we have
\int_{\beta N_{F}}\overline{f}d\overline{\lambda_{n}}=\int_{N_{F}}fd\lambda_{n}
for every
n\in\omega
, thus
\int_{N_{F}}fd\lambda_{n}\to 0
.
∎

Let us recall that our characterization of the Nikodym property via exhaustive ideals, theorem
1
, was inspired by the following characterization of the BJNP.

Theorem 6.2

(Marciszewski and Sobota,
[
22
]
)
.

Let
F
be a free filter on
\omega
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

N_{F}
has the BJNP,

(2)

there is a density submeasure
\varphi
such that
F\subseteq\exh(\varphi)^{*}
,

(3)

there is a non-pathological lsc submeasure
\varphi
such that
F\subseteq\exh(\varphi)^{*}
,

(4)

F\leq_{K}{\mathcal{Z}}^{*}.

The only difference between conditions
(2)
and
(3)
of this theorem and the corresponding conditions of Theorem
1
is the requirement in Theorem
1
that the submeasure
\varphi
is not bounded, i.e.
\varphi(\omega)=\infty
. However, this apparently small difference changes significantly the properties of families of ideals, for example the cofinal structure of
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}
is very simple, as
({\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}},\leq_{K})
has a maximal element by Theorem
6.2
, namely
{\mathcal{Z}}
.

By Theorem
6.2
and Corollary
4.4
we get:

Corollary 6.3
.

If
\varphi
is a non-pathological lsc submeasure on
\omega
and the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}=\exh(\varphi)
is totally bounded, then
{\mathcal{I}}\in{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}\mathbin{\backslash}\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
.

In particular, all the density ideals satisfying any of the conditions from Theorem
1
are elements of
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}\mathbin{\backslash}\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
.

Corollary 6.4
.

{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}\mathbin{\backslash}\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
contains
\mathfrak{c}
many Borel pairwise non-isomorphic ideals on
\omega
.

Proof.

By
[
20
, Propositions 5 and 6]
, there is a family of pairwise non-isomorphic Erdős–Ulam ideals of size
\mathfrak{c}
. Every Erdős–Ulam as a density ideal is Borel. The result follows then from Corollary
5.4
.
∎

Another example of an element of
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{P}}\mathbin{\backslash}\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
is the ideal
\tr({\mathcal{N}})
(see e.g.
[
16
, page 580]
), which is of the form
\exh(\varphi)
for some non-pathological submeasure
\varphi
, and is totally bounded by
[
16
, Proposition 3.18]
. The statement of Corollary
5.5
does not hold for this ideal, as
\tr({\mathcal{N}})<_{K}{\mathcal{Z}}
.
It follows from the following facts:

•

by
[
16
, Proposition 3.6]
we have
\tr({\mathcal{N}})\leq_{K}{\mathcal{Z}}
;

•

there cannot hold
\tr({\mathcal{N}})\equiv_{K}{\mathcal{Z}}
, as it would imply that
\cov^{*}\big(\tr({\mathcal{N}})\big)=\cov^{*}({\mathcal{Z}})
by
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
(see
[
16
, Section 1]
for the definition of
\cov^{*}
), but by
[
16
, Theorem 3.15]
this equality does not hold in the random model, and the Katětov order between Borel ideals is absolute (see e.g.
[
18
, Section 1.2]
).

7.
Examples

In this section we present some examples of classes of ideals on
\omega
which are connected to the class
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
.

7.1.
Summable ideals and large families of non-isomorphic filters

Given
f\colon\omega\to[0,\infty)
such that
\sum_{n\in\omega}f(n)=\infty
, the
summable ideal
corresponding to
f
is the ideal

{\mathcal{I}}_{f}=\Big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\sum_{n\in A}f(n)<\infty\Big\}.

The classical example is the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{1/n}
corresponding to the function
f(n)=1/n
. It is easy to see that
{\mathcal{I}}_{f}=\exh(\mu_{f})
, where
\mu_{f}
is a non-negative measure on
\omega
defined for every
A\subseteq\omega
by

\mu_{f}(A)=\sum_{n\in A}f(n).

Since by the definition we have
\mu_{f}(\omega)=\infty
, and
\mu_{f}
is obviously a non-pathological lsc submeasure, by Theorem
1
we get the following corollary

Corollary 7.1
.

The dual filter
{\mathcal{I}}_{f}^{*}
of any summable ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{f}
does not have the Nikodym property.

Let us denote by
\Sigma
the family of all summable ideals. It appears that
(\Sigma,\leq_{K})
—and so, by Corollary
7.1
,
(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})
—has a rich structure.
Recall that
{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)/Fin
is the quotient algebra of subsets of
\omega
modulo finite sets, endowed with the ordering induced by
\subseteq^{*}
. It is well-known that
({\mathcal{P}}(\omega)/Fin,\subseteq^{*})
contains increasing chains of size
\mathfrak{b}
and antichains of size
\mathfrak{c}
. The following result connects
({\mathcal{P}}(\omega)/Fin,\subseteq^{*})
and

(\Sigma,\leq_{K})
.

Theorem 7.2

(Guzmán-González and Meza-Alcántara
[
14
]
)
.

There is an order embedding of

({\mathcal{P}}(\omega)/Fin,\subseteq^{*})
into
(\Sigma,\leq_{K})
.

Corollary 7.3
.

There is an order embedding of
({\mathcal{P}}(\omega)/Fin,\subseteq^{*})
into
(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})
. In particular,
(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})
contains increasing chains of size
\mathfrak{b}
and antichains of size
\mathfrak{c}
, consisting of summable ideals.

Recall that in Theorem
1
and Corollary
5.11
we have shown that
(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})
contains dominating families of size
\mathfrak{d}
and decreasing chains of size
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
.

We now present a sketch of the construction of large families of non-isomorphic filters with and without the Nikodym property (the ideas follow closely
[
22
, Section 6.1]
).

Corollary 7.4
.

There exist families
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
of size
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, each consisting of pairwise non-isomorphic free filters on
\omega
whose dual ideals are tall, such that:

(1)

every
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
does not have the Nikodym property,

(2)

every
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{2}
has the Nikodym property.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
be the family of all ultrafilters on
\omega
and let
F={\mathcal{I}}_{1/n}^{*}
. We put:

{\mathcal{G}}_{1}=\{G\oplus F\colon G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\},

where
G\oplus F
denotes the disjoint sum of
F
and
G
.
By Corollary
4.2
all members of
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
do have the Nikodym property. Since
F
does not have the Nikodym property by Corollary
7.1
, and
G\oplus F\leq_{K}F
for every
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
(see
[
18
, Section 1.2]
), by Proposition
5.2
all members of
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
do not have the Nikodym property. Moreover,
\big(G\oplus F\big)^{*}=G^{*}\oplus F^{*}
is tall for each
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
, as
G^{*}
and
F^{*}
are tall.
As
|{\mathcal{G}}_{2}|=|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}|=2^{\mathfrak{c}}
, and each family of pairwise isomorphic filters has cardinality
\leq\mathfrak{c}
, for each
i=1,2
we can select a subfamily
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
consisting of
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many pairwise non-isomorphic filters.
∎

7.2.
Simple density ideals

The class of simple density ideals was introduced in
[
1
]
(cf. also
[
21
]
). For every function
g\colon\omega\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
g(n)\to\infty
and
n/g(n)\nrightarrow 0
we define the
simple density ideal
corresponding to
g
as:

{\mathcal{Z}}_{g}=\Big\{A\subseteq\omega\colon\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap n|}{g(n)}=0\Big\}.

It is proved in
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
that every
{\mathcal{Z}}_{g}
is a density ideal.
[
21
, Proposition 10]
presents a sufficient condition for a density ideal generated by a sequence of measures
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
to be a simple density ideal. Since the statement of this result is rather long and technical, we omit it here.
The ideal
\Phi(f)
satisfies conditions
(i),(ii)
and
(v)
from
[
21
, Proposition 10]
for any
f\in\omega^{\omega}
. Conditions
(iv)
and
(vi)
are also satisfied for
\Phi(f)
when
f
is non-decreasing and
f(n)\to\infty
, as using the notation from that proposition we have
a_{n}=1/f(n)
. By the definition of
\Phi(f)
we have also
\min\big(\supp(\mu_{n}^{f})\big)=\sum_{i<n}f(i)
for every
n\in\omega
, where
\langle\mu_{n}^{f}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is the sequence of measures generating
\Phi(f)
. Thus, for
condition
(iii)
to be satisfied, we need that

\frac{1}{f(n)}\cdot\sum_{i<n}f(i)\to 0.

Therefore, by
[
21
, Proposition 10]
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
f\in\omega^{\omega}
be non-decreasing and satisfy the condition
f(n)\big/\sum_{i<n}f(i)\to\infty
. Then, the ideal
\Phi(f)
is equal to the simple density ideal
{\mathcal{Z}}_{g}
, where
g\colon\omega\to[0,\infty)
is defined by
g(k)=f(n)
for
k\in\omega
when
k\in\supp(\mu_{n})
.

It is proved in
[
21
, Theorem 3]
that there is a family
\{{\mathcal{Z}}_{g_{\alpha}}\colon\alpha<\mathfrak{c}\}
such that
{\mathcal{Z}}_{g_{\alpha}}
is not
\leq_{K}
-comparable to
{\mathcal{Z}}_{g_{\beta}}
for any
\alpha\neq\beta
. By Theorem
1
, for every density ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
such that
{\mathcal{I}}\notin\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
we have
{\mathcal{I}}\equiv_{K}{\mathcal{Z}}
, and so the
\leq_{K}
-antichain must lie inside the
\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
family:

Corollary 7.6
.

(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})
contains antichains of size
\mathfrak{c}
consisting of density ideals.

8.
The Nikodym property of Boolean algebras

This section is devoted to an analysis of the connection between embedding of a space
N_{F}
without the finitely supported Nikodym property into the Stone space of a given Boolean algebra
\mathcal{A}
and the lack of the Nikodym property of
\mathcal{A}
.

We first need to introduce some notation (cf.
[
22
, Section 7]
). Let
X
be a compact Hausdorff space,
Y
an infinite countable subset of
X
, and
x\in\overline{Y}\mathbin{\backslash}Y
. Let
Z_{X}(x,Y)
be the space
Y\cup\{x\}
endowed with the subspace topology.
By
\mathfrak{N}_{X}(x)
we denote the collection of all
U\subseteq X
such that
x\in\interior U
. We then put:

\mathfrak{J}_{X}(x,Y)=\big\{U\cap Y\colon U\in\mathfrak{N}_{X}(x)\big\}.

Note that since
x\in\overline{Y}\mathbin{\backslash}Y
,
\mathfrak{J}_{X}(x,Y)
is a filter on the infinite countable set
Y
containing all its co-finite subsets. If
f\colon\omega\to Y
is a bijection, then
F=\big\{f^{-1}[V]\colon V\in\mathfrak{J}_{X}(x,Y)\big\}
is a free filter on
\omega
—we will say in this case that
F
is
f-associated
to

\mathfrak{J}_{X}(x,Y)
. We also have a bijective continuous function
\varphi_{f}\colon N_{F}\to Z_{X}(x,Y)
associated with
f
, which is defined by

\varphi_{f}\restriction\omega=f
and
\varphi_{f}\big(p_{F}\big)=x
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is a Boolean algebra. Let
Y
be a countable subset of
St(\mathcal{A})
and
x\in\overline{Y}\mathbin{\backslash}Y
. Let
f\colon\omega\to Y
be a bijection and
F
the filter on
\omega

f
-associated to
\mathfrak{J}_{St(\mathcal{A})}(x,Y)
.

Then, if
F
does not have the Nikodym property, then the algebra
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property.

Proof.

Let
\langle\nu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
be an anti-Nikodym sequence of measures on
N_{F}
. For each
n\in\omega
we define a measure
\mu_{n}
on
St(\mathcal{A})
by setting

\mu_{n}(B)=\nu_{n}\big(\varphi_{f}^{-1}[B]\big)

for every
B\in Bor\big(St(\mathcal{A})\big)
. Then, we get that
\|\mu_{n}\|=\|\nu_{n}\|\to\infty
and that for each
n\in\omega
the support
\supp(\mu_{n})
is finite and contained in
Z_{St(\mathcal{A})}(x,Y)
.

Since
\varphi_{f}
is continuous, for any
A\in\clopen\big(St(\mathcal{A})\big)
we have
\varphi_{f}^{-1}[A]\in\clopen(N_{F})
, hence by the assumption that
\langle\nu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is anti-Nikodym we get:

\mu_{n}(A)=\nu_{n}\big(\varphi_{f}^{-1}[A]\big)\to 0\textrm{ as }n\to\infty.

For each
n\in\omega
we define a measure
\lambda_{n}
on the algebra
\mathcal{A}
by the formula
\lambda_{n}(A)=\mu_{n}\big([A]_{\mathcal{A}}\big)
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
. By the properties of the sequence
\langle\mu_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
we have

\|\lambda_{n}\|\to\infty
and
\lambda_{n}(A)\to 0
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
. Therefore,
\langle\lambda_{n}\colon n\in\omega\rangle
is a pointwise bounded sequence of measures on
\mathcal{A}
which is not uniformly bounded, and hence
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property.
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is a Boolean algebra and
G
is a filter on
\omega
. Let
\varphi\colon N_{G}\to St(\mathcal{A})
be a continuous function such that
\varphi^{-1}\big(\varphi(p_{G})\big)=\{p_{G}\}
(e.g.
\varphi
is an injection).

Then, if
G
does not have the Nikodym property, then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property.

Proof.

Let us define
Y=\varphi[\omega]
and
x=\varphi(p_{G})
. It follows that
Y
is an infinite countable subset of
St(\mathcal{A})
, as if
Y
was finite, in particular closed in
St(\mathcal{A})
, then it would imply that
\varphi^{-1}[Y]=\omega
is closed in
N_{F}
.
We also have
x\in\overline{Y}\mathbin{\backslash}Y
. Let
f\colon\omega\to Y
be a bijection and let
F
be the filter on
\omega

f
-associated to
\mathfrak{J}_{X}(x,Y)
. We define
g\colon\omega\to\omega
by the formula

g(n)=f^{-1}\big(\varphi(n))
for every
n\in\omega
, i.e.
g=f^{-1}\circ(\varphi\restriction\omega)
.

We claim that the function
g
is a witness for
F\leq_{K}G
. Let
A\in F
. Since
F
is
f
-associated to
\mathfrak{J}_{St(\mathcal{A})}(x,Y)
, we have
f[A]\in\mathfrak{J}_{St(\mathcal{A})}(x,Y)
, and thus
f[A]\cup\{x\}
contains an open neighbourhood of
x
in
Z_{St(\mathcal{A})}(x,Y)
. Since
\varphi
is continuous and
\varphi^{-1}(\{x\})=\{p_{G}\}
, the set

\varphi^{-1}\big[f[A]\cup\{x\}\big]=\varphi^{-1}\big[f[A]\big]\cup\{p_{G}\}
contains an open neighbourhood of
p_{G}
in
N_{G}
, and so
g^{-1}[A]=\varphi^{-1}\big[f[A]\big]\in G

Therefore,
F\leq_{K}G
. By Proposition,
5.2
the filter

8.1
the algebra
\mathcal{A}
does not have the Nikodym property.
∎

9.2.
Tukey reductions

The analysis of the cofinal structure of the order
(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})
shows that it is, in some way, similarly complicated as the cofinal structure of
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
. The standard way of comparing cofinal structures of two orders is through Tukey reductions, see e.g.
[
10
]
and
[
29
]
.

Let
(P,\leq_{P})
and
(Q,\leq_{Q})
be partial orders. A function
f\colon P\to Q
is said to be a
Tukey reduction
if for every
q_{0}\in Q
there exists
p_{0}\in P
such that, for every
p\in P
, the following implication holds:

f(p)\leq_{Q}q_{0}\>\Longrightarrow\>p\leq_{P}p_{0},

i.e. if the preimages by
f
of bounded subsets of
Q
are bounded subsets of
P
. We write
P\preceq_{T}Q
if there exists a Tukey reduction from
P
to
Q
. We say that
P
and
Q
are
Tukey equivalent
, which we denote by
P\equiv_{T}Q
, if
P\preceq_{T}Q
and
Q\preceq_{T}P
.

An example of using Tukey reductions for classes of ideals ordered by
\leq_{K}
is the following result about the class of all
F_{\sigma}
ideals on
\omega
.

Theorem 9.2

(Minami and Sakai
[
23
]
)
.

(F_{\sigma}\textit{-ideals},\leq_{K})\equiv_{T}(F_{\sigma}\textit{-ideals},\leq_{KB})\equiv_{T}(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*}).

Using Theorem
5.6
it is not difficult to prove the following reduction.

Proposition 9.3
.

(\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}},\leq_{K})\preceq_{T}(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*}).

Proof.

We define a Tukey reduction
\Psi\colon\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}\to\omega^{\omega}
in the following way. Let
{\mathcal{I}}\in\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
. By Theorem
\ref{thm_katetov}.(1)
there exists
f\in\omega^{\omega}
such that

{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{K}\Phi(f)
. Let us fix any such
f\in\omega^{\omega}
, and put
\Psi({\mathcal{I}})=g
, where
g(n)=2n^{2}\cdot f(n)
for every
n\in\omega
.

To prove that
\Psi
is a Tukey reduction, let us take any
h\in\omega^{\omega}
. We need to show that the following implication holds for every
{\mathcal{I}}\in\mathcal{A}{\mathcal{N}}
:

\Psi({\mathcal{I}})\leq^{*}h\>\Longrightarrow\>{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{K}\Phi(h).

By the definition of
\Psi
, if
\Psi({\mathcal{I}})\leq^{*}h
then there exists
f\in\omega^{\omega}
such that
{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{K}\Phi(f)
and
2n^{2}\cdot f(n)\leq h(n)
holds for all but finitely many
n\in\omega
.
By Theorem
\ref{thm_katetov}.(2)
we get
\Phi(f)\leq_{K}\Phi(h)
, and so
{\mathcal{I}}\leq_{K}\Phi(h)
.
∎

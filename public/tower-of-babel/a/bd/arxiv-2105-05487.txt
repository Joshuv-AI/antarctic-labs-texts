A monolithic fluid-porous structure interaction finite element method Thanks: This work has been supported by the Russian Science Foundation grant 19-71-10094.
Alexander Lozovskiy, Maxim A. Olshanskii, Yuri V. Vassilevski

Abstract
The paper introduces a fully discrete quasi-Lagrangian finite element method for a monolithic formulation of a fluid-porous structure interaction problem. The method is second order in time and allows a standard P 2 − P 1 P_{2}-P_{1} (Taylor–Hood) finite element spaces for fluid problems in both fluid and porous domains. The performance of the method is illustrated on a series of numerical experiments.



1
Introduction

Blood flow in a vessel with permeable walls or penetration of oil through a crack in a porous matrix can be seen
as the interaction of a freely flowing fluid with a fluid-saturated poroelastic structure.
A continuum mechanics description of such fluid-poroelastic phenomena often leads to coupled systems of (Navier–)Stokes and
Biot equations
[
30
,
22
]
.
Recently, there has been a growing interest in the numerical solution of the Stokes–Biot and Navier–Stokes–Biot problems.
Several authors suggested solution strategies based on decomposition of the system into fluid and poroelastic loosely coupled problems
to allow for a computationally efficient time-stepping schemes
[
5
,
8
]
.
For the reason of better stability, monolithic methods for the (Navier–)Stokes–Biot equations have become popular in the literature.
They differ in the form of equations and the numerical treatment of the coupling conditions on the interface
between a free flow domain and a domain occupied by the porous structure.
In
[
2
]
the continuity of fluid fluxes on the interface is imposed weakly with the help of a Lagrange multipier and
in
[
31
]
an interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method is applied to obtain a discrete coupled formulation.
The Nitsche approach is used for coupling fluid and poroelastic finite element formulations in
[
9
,
1
]
.
Combination of the Nitsche approach and unfitted finite elements
[
1
]
adds extra flexibility to the numerical solution.

Many publications on numerical methods for the fluid–poroelastic problem ignore inertia effect in the fluid and
formulate the free fluid problem as a Stokes system. One reason for such simplification is the lack of the energy dissipation principle
for the Navier–Stokes–Biot problem with the common interface conditions, which hinders the analysis in this case.
This issue is well-known already for the Navier–Stokes–Darcy (the Navier–Stokes–Biot problem with rigid structure),
where a local well-posedness of the system is currently known only under a smallness assumption (even in 2D) and
the proof uses involved arguments that work in the absence of
a priori
energy bound
[
4
,
19
]
.
In the context of the Navier–Stokes–Darcy coupling the issue was addressed in
[
10
,
11
]
,
where interface conditions were modified to ensure the thermodynamical consistency of the complete system.
In this report, we follow
[
10
,
11
]
and employ the suggested correction
to the stress balance in the Navier–Stokes–Biot to end up with a dissipative system and stable numerical method.

We consider the Navier–Stokes–Biot system with the Beavers–Joseph–Saffman interface condition and a modified stress interface condition
and discuss its energy balance. For an ALE formulation of the problem we further introduce a monolithic finite element method.
Our finite element method features the formulation of all equations in the reference coordinates encoding all information on geometry deformation
in solution-dependent coefficients. This formulation allows a simple application of the method of lines for the time discretization.
In particular, the second order discretization in time is straightforward.
Such monolithic approach was proved to be efficient for FSI problems with an impermeable elastic
structure
[
20
,
27
,
28
]
, and we extend it here to the case of poroelasticity.
In the spirit of monolithic formulations we apply here the same finite elements to approximate fluid velocity and pressure in both domains.
We choose the Taylor–Hood element (P2-P1) for this purpose, which is a valid Darcy element for applications
where the local mass conservation is not critical
[
21
]
. We use the same P2 element for the structure velocity.
To enforce the continuity of fluid flux through the interface, we use the penalty approach
(the Nitsche approach as in
[
1
]
would be an alternative).

The remainder of the paper is organized in three sections. We formulate the governing equations,
interface and boundary conditions in section
2
. The same section presents the integral formulation, the energy balance of the system,
and an ALE formulation that we use for the discretization. The finite element method is introduced in section
3
.
Section
4
presents results of several numerical experiments.

2
FPSI model

Consider a time-dependent domain
\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
containing fluid and an elastic porous structure.
A subdomain
\Omega^{f}(t)
is entirely occupied by fluid and a subdomian
\Omega^{s}(t)
is occupied by porous elastic solid fully saturated with fluid.
These subdomains are non-overlapping and
\overline{\Omega(t)}=\overline{\Omega^{f}(t)}\cup\overline{\Omega^{s}(t)}
.
Two regions are separated by the interface
\Gamma^{fs}(t):={\partial\Omega}^{f}(t)\cap{\partial\Omega}^{s}(t)
.

In this paper, the equations governing the fluid and solid motion will be written in the reference domains

\Omega_{f}=\Omega^{f}(0),\quad\Omega_{s}=\Omega^{s}(0),\quad\Gamma_{fs}=\Gamma^{fs}(0).

The deformation of the poroelastic part is given by the mapping

\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s}~:~\Omega_{s}\times[0,t]\to\bigcup_{t\in[0,T]}\Omega^{s}(t),

with the corresponding displacement
\mathbf{u}_{s}
,
\mathbf{u}_{s}(\mathbf{x},t):=\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s}(\mathbf{x},t)
and
the velocity of the elastic structure

\mathbf{v}_{s}=\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{s}=-\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s}(\mathbf{x},t)
.

Figure 1
:
Reference and physical domains and boundaries.

The fluid dynamics is described by the velocity vector field
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x},t)
and the pressure function
p(\mathbf{x},t)
defined in
the whole volume
\Omega(t)
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Following
[
22
,
5
]
we represent
\mathbf{v}
in the poroelastic domain
through the velocity of structure and the filtration flux
\mathbf{q}=\phi(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{s})
, where
\phi
is the known porosity coefficient.
We denote the fluid pressure in the poroelastic domain by
p_{d}
, to emphasize its impact on the Darcy filtration, and
in the fluid domain by
p_{f}
.

Denote by
\rho_{s}
and
\rho_{f}
the densities of solid and fluid. Then
\rho_{p}=\rho_{s}(1-\phi)+\rho_{f}\phi
is the density of the saturated porous medium. Denote by
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}
the Cauchy stress
tensors in porous media and fluid, respectively. The poroelastic stress tensor is given by
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}=\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}-\alpha p\mathbf{I}
,
where
\alpha>0
is Biot’s coefficient (typically
\alpha\simeq 1
, so further we set
\alpha=1
).
The porous medium is also characterized by its permeability tensor
K
.
The Biot system in the porous domain and the Navier-Stokes equations in the fluid domain follow from
the momenta balances and mass conservation principles (and neglecting the inertial effect of the matrix):

\begin{split}&\left\{\begin{split}\rho_{p}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{q}}&={\rm div}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}\\
\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\dot{\mathbf{q}}&=-(K^{-1}\mathbf{q}+\nabla p_{d})\\
-s_{0}\dot{p_{d}}&={\rm div}\,(\mathbf{v}_{s}+\mathbf{q})\end{split}\right.\quad\text{in}~\Omega^{s}(t),\\
&\left\{\begin{split}\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}&={\rm div}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\\
{\rm div}\,\mathbb{v}_{f}&=0\end{split}\right.\quad\text{in}~\Omega^{f}(t),\end{split}

(1)

where
1/s_{0}
is Biot modulus or mixture compressibility modulus.

We divide the boundary of
\Omega(t)
into the external boundary of the poroplastic structure
\Gamma^{s0}(t):={\partial\Omega}(t)\cap{\partial\Omega}^{s}(t)
,
fluid Dirichlet and outflow boundaries:
{\partial\Omega}(t)\cap{\partial\Omega}^{f}(t)=\Gamma^{f0}(t)\cup\Gamma^{\rm out}(t)
; cf. Figure
1
.
The governing equations are complemented with boundary conditions

\mathbb{v}^{f}=\mathbb{g}~\text{ on }\Gamma^{f0}(t),\quad\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=\mathbb{0}\;\text{ on }\Gamma^{\rm out}(t),\quad p_{d}=0\;\text{ on }\Gamma^{s0}(t),\quad\mathbb{v}^{s}=\mathbb{0}~\text{ on }\Gamma^{s0}(t)

(2)

and suitable initial conditions.

We now discuss coupling conditions on the interface between the fluid and poroelastic domains.
Denote by
\mathbf{n}
the normal vector on
\Gamma^{fs}(t)
pointing from the fluid to the poroelastic structure.
The balance of normal stresses on
\Gamma^{fs}(t)
is commonly written in terms of the interface conditions:

\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{n}^{T}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=-p_{d}
. This coupling, however, is not known to provide an energy consistent (dissipative)
system. For the pure Darcy–Navier–Stokes coupling a remedy was suggested in
[
10
,
12
]

where the second condition was changed to include a contribution of the fluid kinetic energy.
In this paper, we use the same modification in the poroelasticity context and the two interface conditions read:

\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}\mathbf{n}\qquad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{n}^{T}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=-p_{d}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}~~~\text{on}~\Gamma^{fs}(t).

(3)

Such modification of the stress balance is similar to modifications of outflow boundary conditions
and 1D-3D models coupling conditions in computational fluid dynamics, see e.g.,
[
6
,
7
]
.
The continuity of the normal flux on the fluid-structure interface gives

\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{n}=(\mathbf{v}_{s}+\mathbf{q})\cdot\mathbf{n}\quad\mbox{on}~\Gamma^{fs}(t).

(4)

Finally, the Beavers–Joseph–Saffman condition sets the tangential component of the normal stress proportional
to the fluid “slip” rate along the interface:

\mathbf{P}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=-\gamma\mathbf{P}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})~~~\text{on}~\Gamma^{fs}(t),

(5)

where
\mathbf{P}
is the orthogonal projector on the tangential plane to
\Gamma^{fs}(t)
.

2.1
Integral formulation

In the preparation for the finite element method, we write out an integral (weak) formulation of the FPSI problem (
1
)–(
5
). We take the inner product of the elasticity equation in (
1
) with a sufficiently smooth
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}
such that
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}=0
on
\Gamma^{s0}(t)
, integrate it over
\Omega^{s}(t)
and integrate the stress term by parts (recall that
\mathbf{n}
is inward for
\Omega^{s}
). This adds up with the first Darcy equation multiplied by a sufficiently smooth
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}
and integrated over
\Omega^{s}(t)
to give

\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}(\rho_{p}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}+(\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\dot{\mathbf{q}}+K^{-1}\mathbf{q})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}:\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,dx\\
-\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}p_{d}{\rm div}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}+\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}\mathbf{n})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}p_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,ds=0.

(6)

Further, the fluid momentum equation in (
1
) is multiplied by a smooth vector function
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}
such that
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}=\mathbb{0}
on
\Gamma^{f0}
. Integrating over
\Omega^{s}(t)
and integrating the stress term by parts we obtain

\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}:\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}\,dx-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}^{T}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}\,ds=0.

(7)

We add up boundary terms in (
6
) and (
7
) and use interface conditions (
3
)–(
5
) to reorganize them

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}\mathbf{n})\cdot

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}p_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}^{T}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}\,ds

use (
3
)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n})\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\,ds-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}p_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,ds

\displaystyle\text{\footnotesize split }\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}(\mathbf{n}^{T}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n})(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds+\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}(\mathbf{P}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n})\cdot\mathbf{P}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\,ds

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}p_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,ds

use (
3
), (
5
)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}p_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds+\gamma\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s})\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds.

Summing up (
6
) and (
7
) and using the calculations above we arrive at the integral equality satisfied by
sufficiently smooth FPSI solution
\mathbf{v}_{s}
,
\mathbf{q}
,
\mathbf{v}_{f}
,
p_{d}
,
p_{f}

\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\left[(\rho_{p}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}+(\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\dot{\mathbf{q}}+K^{-1}\mathbf{q})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\right]\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}:\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,dx\\
-\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}p_{d}{\rm div}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}:\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}\,dx+\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\\
+\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}p_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds+\gamma\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s})\,ds=0

(8)

for all sufficiently smooth
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}
such that
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}=0
on
\Gamma^{s0}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}=\mathbb{0}
on
\Gamma^{f0}
.
For the weak formulation, this integral identity should be supplemented by the two continuity equations in (
1
)
and the normal continuity interface condition (
4
).

To obtain the energy balance identity, we assume that
\Gamma^{f0}
and
\Gamma^{\rm out}
are steady
and
\mathbf{g}=0
on
\Gamma^{f0}
.
We further let
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}=\mathbf{v}_{s}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}=\mathbf{q}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}=\mathbf{v}_{f}
and use
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}=\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}-p\mathbf{I}
,
continuity conditions and (
4
) to arrive at the equality:

\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\left[(\rho_{p}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{s}+(\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\dot{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\mathbf{q}+K^{-1}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\right]\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}:\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}s_{0}\dot{p}_{d}p_{d}\,dx\\
+\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{f}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}:\nabla\mathbf{v}_{f}\,dx-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds+\gamma\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})|^{2}\,ds=0.

(9)

Using
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}=\mu_{f}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{v}_{f}-p_{f}\mathbf{I}
,
{\rm div}\,\mathbf{v}_{f}=0
, and rearranging the first two term by substituting
\rho_{p}=\rho_{s}(1-\phi)+\rho_{f}\phi
, we can rewrite the above equality as

\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\left[(1-\phi)\rho_{s}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{s}+\phi\rho_{f}(\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\frac{\dot{\mathbf{q}}}{\phi})\cdot({\mathbf{v}}_{s}+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{\phi})\right]\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}K^{-1}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}:\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,dx\\
+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}s_{0}\dot{p}_{d}p_{d}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{f}\,dx+\mu_{f}\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{v}_{f}|^{2}\,dx\\
-\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds+\gamma\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})|^{2}\,ds=0.

(10)

The integrals with material derivatives can be readily converted to the variations of kinetic energy by application of the Reynolds transport theorem
and recalling that all parts of
\partial\Omega^{f}(t)
are steady except
\Gamma^{fs}(t)
, which normal velocity is
\mathbf{v}_{s}\cdot\mathbf{n}
:

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\,dx&=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}_{f}}{\partial t}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{v}_{s}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\\
&=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}_{f}}{\partial t}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\\
&=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}_{f}}{\partial t}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}{\rm div}\,(|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}{\mathbf{v}}_{f})\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\\
\text{\footnotesize using }{\footnotesize{\rm div}\,{\mathbf{v}}_{f}=0}~&=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}_{f}}{\partial t}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\,ds+\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}((\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{v}}_{f})\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\\
&=\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\cdot{\mathbf{v}}_{f}\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds.\end{split}

We handle the
\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}
integrals containing material derivatives in (
10
) by the same argument
assuming that the elastic structure is incompressible, i.e.
{\rm div}\,\mathbf{v}_{s}=0
, and recalling that the material derivative in
the structure is written in the Eulerian terms as
\partial/{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}_{s}\cdot\nabla
.
Therefore, (
10
) yields

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}(1-\phi)\rho_{s}|\mathbf{v}_{s}|^{2}+\phi\rho_{f}|\mathbf{v}_{f}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}K^{-1}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}:\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,dx\\
+\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega^{s}(t)}s_{0}|{p}_{d}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}\rho_{f}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}\,dx+\mu_{f}\int_{\Omega^{f}(t)}|\mathbf{D}\mathbf{v}_{f}|^{2}\,dx\\
+\gamma\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})|^{2}\,ds=0,

(11)

where we used
\mathbf{v}_{f}=\mathbf{v}_{s}+\frac{\mathbf{q}}{\phi}
in
\Omega^{s}(t)
for the brevity.
We see that the system is dissipative. Without the correction in the stress balance on the interface, the sign indefinite term

-\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}\int_{\Gamma^{fs}(t)}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,ds
appears in the energy equality, and the system is not necessarily dissipative.

2.2
ALE formulation

In this paper, we adopt the Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian formulation by extending
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s}
to an auxiliary mapping in the fluid domain

\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{f}~:~\Omega_{f}\times[0,t]\to\bigcup_{t\in[0,T]}\Omega^{f}(t)

such that
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s}=\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{f}
on
\Gamma_{fs}
, i.e.
\xi
is globally continuous. In general,
\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{f}
does not follow material trajectories. Instead, it is defined by
a continuous extension of the displacement field to the flow reference domain

\mathbf{u}_{f}:=\mbox{Ext}(\mathbf{u}_{s})=\mathbf{x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{f}(\mathbf{x},t)\quad\mbox{in}~\Omega_{f}\times[0,t];\quad\mathbf{u}=\left\{\begin{split}\mathbf{u}_{s}&\text{ in }\Omega_{s}\\
\mathbf{u}_{f}&\text{ in }\Omega_{f}.\end{split}\right.

(12)

The corresponding globally defined deformation gradient is
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{I}+\nabla\mathbf{u}
, and
J:=\mbox{det}(\mathbf{F})
is its determinant.
From now on, for notational simplicity, we will be using the same notation for these fields defined in the reference configuration as
\mathbf{v}_{f}(\mathbf{x},t):=\mathbf{v}_{f}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{f}(\mathbf{x},t),t)
and
p_{f}(\mathbf{x},t):=p_{f}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{f}(\mathbf{x},t),t)
.
We use the notation
\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}\circ\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s}(\mathbf{x}):=\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s}(\mathbf{x}))
.

The governing equations driving the motion of fluid and structure written in the reference domains read as

\left\{\begin{split}\rho_{p}\frac{\partial\mathbb{v}_{s}}{\partial t}+\rho_{f}\frac{\partial\mathbb{q}}{\partial t}=J^{-1}{\rm div}\,(J(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}\circ\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{s})\mathbf{F}^{-T})\quad\text{in}~\Omega_{s},\\
\rho_{f}\frac{\partial\mathbb{v}_{s}}{\partial t}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\frac{\partial\mathbb{q}}{\partial t}=-K^{-1}\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{F}^{-T}\nabla p\quad\text{in}~\Omega_{s},\\
\rho_{f}\frac{\partial\mathbb{v}_{f}}{\partial t}=J^{-1}{\rm div}\,(J(\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\circ\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$\unboldmath}_{f})\mathbf{F}^{-T})-\rho_{f}\nabla\mathbb{v}_{f}(\mathbf{F}^{-1}(\mathbb{v}_{f}-\frac{\partial\mathbb{u}}{\partial t}))\quad\text{in}~\Omega_{f},\end{split}\right.

(13)

and the mass conservation reads as

\left\{\begin{split}{\rm div}\,(J\mathbf{F}^{-1}(\mathbb{v}_{s}+\mathbf{q}))=-s_{0}J\frac{\partial p_{d}}{\partial t}&~~~\text{in}~\Omega_{s},\\
{\rm div}\,(J\mathbf{F}^{-1}\mathbb{v}_{f})=0&~~~\text{in}~\Omega_{f}.\end{split}\right.

(14)

Using the identity
{\rm div}\,(J\mathbf{F}^{-1}\mathbb{v})=J\nabla\mathbf{v}:\mathbf{F}^{-T}
, the last two equations can be written as

\left\{\begin{split}\nabla(\mathbb{v}_{s}+\mathbf{q}):\mathbf{F}^{-T}=-s_{0}\frac{\partial p_{d}}{\partial t}&~~~\text{in}~\Omega_{s},\\
\nabla\mathbf{v}_{f}:\mathbf{F}^{-T}=0&~~~\text{in}~\Omega_{f}.\end{split}\right.

(15)

The deformation of the structure can be found by integrating the kinematic equation

\frac{\partial\mathbb{u}_{s}}{\partial t}=\mathbf{v}_{s}~~~\text{in}~\Omega_{s}.

(16)

The boundary and interface conditions are the same in the ALE formulation.
The normal
\mathbf{n}
(and projector
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^{T}
) to the interface and outflow boundary in the physical domain
can be computed from the reference normal
\widehat{\mathbf{n}}
, i.e.
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{F}^{-T}\widehat{\mathbf{n}}/|\mathbf{F}^{-T}\widehat{\mathbf{n}}|
. We collect all conditions in one place here:

\mathbb{v}_{f}=\mathbb{g}~\text{ on }\Gamma_{f0},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\widehat{\mathbf{n}}=\mathbb{0}\;\text{ on }\Gamma_{\rm out},\quad\mathbb{v}_{s}=\mathbb{0}~\text{ on }\Gamma_{s0},\quad p_{d}=0~\text{ on }\Gamma_{s0}

(17)

for the outer boundaries and

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{p}\mathbf{n},\qquad\mathbf{n}^{T}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=-p_{d}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}~~~\text{on}~\Gamma_{fs},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{n}=(\mathbf{v}_{s}+\mathbf{q})\cdot\mathbf{n}\quad\mbox{on}~\Gamma_{fs},

(19)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}\mathbf{n}=-\gamma\mathbf{P}K^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})~~~\text{on}~\Gamma_{fs}

(20)

on the interface. For the integral formulation in the reference coordinates,
we will use the identities
J\,d\hat{x}=dx
,
J|\mathbf{F}^{-T}\widehat{\mathbf{n}}|\,\,d\hat{s}=ds
,
where
ds
,
d\hat{s}
are elementary areas orthogonal to
\mathbf{n}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{n}}
in physical and reference coordinates, respectively.

The constitutive relation for the Newtonian fluid in the reference domain reads

\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{f}=-p_{f}\mathbf{I}+\mu_{f}(\nabla\mathbf{v}\mathbf{F}^{-1}+\mathbf{F}^{-T}(\nabla\mathbf{v})^{T})~~~\text{in}~\Omega_{f}.

(21)

For the structure we consider the compressible geometrically
nonlinear Saint Venant–Kirchhoff material
with

\mbox{\boldmath$\sigma$\unboldmath}_{s}=\frac{1}{J}\mathbf{F}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{F}^{T},\quad\text{with}~\mathbf{S}=\lambda_{s}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{E}){\mathbf{I}}+2\mu_{s}\mathbf{E},

(22)

where
\mathbf{E}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{F}^{T}\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{I}\right)
is the Lagrange-Green strain tensor and
\lambda_{s},\mu_{s}
are the Lame constants.

Thus, the FPSI problem in the reference coordinates consists in finding pressure distributions
p_{d}
,
p_{f}
, fluid and structure velocity fields
\mathbf{v}_{f}
,
\mathbf{v}_{s}
, fluid flux in the porous medium
\mathbf{q}
and the displacement field

\mathbf{u}
satisfying the set of equations, interface and boundary conditions
(
13
)–(
20
), together with (
21
), (
22
), and
subject to a given extension rule (
12
).

3
Discretization

We now proceed with dicretization of the FPSI problem formulated in the reference domain.
Treating the problem in the reference domain allows us to avoid time-dependent triangulations and finite element function spaces and
apply the standard method of lines to decouple space and time discretizations.
We adopt a finite element method in space and define
an admissible triangulation of the reference domain
\overline{\Omega}(0)
as a collection
{\cal T}_{h}
of shape-regular tetrahedra
such that the triangulation respects the interface
\Gamma_{fs}
. This implies that

{\cal T}_{h}^{a}:=\{T\in{\cal T}_{h}\,:\,T\subset\Omega_{a}\}
,
a\in\{f,s\}
, are admissible triangulations of the fluid and poroelastic reference
domains
\Omega_{a}
,
a\in\{f,s\}
.
We exploit the finite element Taylor–Hood spaces which are popular in incompressible hydrodynamics:

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}_{h}^{a}&=\{\mathbf{v}\in C(\Omega_{a})\,:\,\mathbf{v}|_{T}\in P_{2}(T)^{3}\quad\forall\,T\in{\cal T}_{h}^{a}\},\quad a\in\{f,s\},\\
\mathbb{Q}_{h}^{a}&=\{q\in C(\Omega_{a})\,:\,q|_{T}\in P_{1}(T)\quad\forall\,T\in{\cal T}_{h}^{a}\},\quad a\in\{f,s\}.\end{split}

For trial functions we need also the following subspaces:

\begin{split}\mathbb{V}_{h}^{a,0}&=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{a}\,:\,\mathbf{v}|_{\Gamma_{a,0}}=\mathbf{0}\},\quad a\in\{f,s\},\\
\mathbb{Q}_{h}^{s,0}&=\{q\in\mathbb{Q}_{h}^{s}\,:\,q|_{\Gamma_{s,0}}={0}\}.\\
\end{split}

We note that the Taylor–Hood is not a standard Darcy element for
H(div)
- formulations of the problem.
In particular, it fails to provide elementwise mass conservation. However, for applications where the local mass conservation is not a major concern,
it is a legitimate choice leading to optimal convergence in the Darcy region in product
L^{2}
-velocity–
H^{1}
-pressure norm
[
21
]
.

For the time discretization, we assume a constant time step
\Delta t
and use the notation
f^{k}(\mathbf{x})\approx f(k\Delta t,\mathbf{x})
for all
time-dependent quantities.
The first or second order backward finite difference approximation

\left[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right]^{k}

of the time derivative of
f
at
t=k\Delta t
is

\left[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right]^{k}=\frac{f^{k}-f^{k-1}}{\Delta t}\quad\text{or}\quad\left[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right]^{k}=\frac{3f^{k}-4f^{k-1}+f^{k-2}}{2\Delta t},

respectively.
By
\widetilde{f}^{k}
we denote the extrapolated quantity
f

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}^{k}:=f^{k-1}\quad\text{or}\quad\widetilde{f}^{k}:=2f^{k-1}-f^{k-2}

for the first or second order extrapolation, respectively.

We proceed to multi-linear forms needed for our finite element formulation. For time derivatives, we need the form:

m^{k}(\mathbf{w}_{s},\mathbf{w}_{d},\mathbf{w}_{f},r;\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f},q):=\int_{\Omega_{s}}\tilde{J}^{k}(\rho_{p}\mathbf{w}_{s}+\rho_{f}\mathbf{w}_{d})\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\int_{\Omega_{s}}\tilde{J}^{k}(\rho_{f}\mathbf{w}_{s}+\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\mathbf{w}_{d})\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{s}}s_{0}\tilde{J}^{k}r\,q\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{f}}\rho_{f}\tilde{J}^{k}\mathbf{w}_{f}\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the elasticity part, we define

a_{s}^{k}(\mathbf{w}_{s},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s})=\int_{\Omega_{s}}\mathbf{F}({\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{k})\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{w}_{s},{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{k}):\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad a_{d}^{k}(\mathbf{w}_{d},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s})=\int_{\Omega_{s}}\tilde{J}^{k}K^{-1}\mathbf{w}_{d}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\mathrm{d}x,

where
\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2})=\lambda_{s}\mbox{tr}(\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2}))\mathbf{I}+2\mu_{s}\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2})
,

\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}_{1})^{T}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}_{2})-\mathbf{I}\right\}_{s}
,
\{{\bf A}\}_{s}=\frac{1}{2}({\bf A}+{\bf A}^{T})
denotes the symmetric part of
tensor
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

For the fluid domain we need the viscous term form

a_{f}^{k}(\mathbf{w}_{f},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})=\int_{\Omega_{f}}2\mu_{f}\tilde{J}^{k}\mathbf{D}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{k}}(\mathbf{w}_{f}):\mathbf{D}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{k}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\,\mathrm{d}x

and inertia form

c_{f}^{k}(\mathbf{w}_{f};\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$\unboldmath}_{f},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})=\int_{\Omega_{f}}\rho_{f}\tilde{J}^{k}\left(\nabla\mathbf{w}_{f}\mathbf{F}^{-1}({\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{k})\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$\unboldmath}_{f}\right)\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{v})=\{(\nabla\mathbf{v})\mathbf{F}^{-1}(\mathbf{u})\}_{s}
.

For handling the mass conservation constraints, we introduce

b_{a}^{k}(q,\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath})=\int_{\Omega_{a}}q\tilde{J}^{k}\mathbf{F}^{-T}({\tilde{\mathbf{u}}}^{k}):\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad a\in\{s,f\}.

Next, we collect the interface terms:

\begin{split}d^{k}(\mathbf{w}_{s},\mathbf{w}_{d},\mathbf{w}_{f},p_{d};\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})=\tau\int_{\Gamma_{fs}}\tilde{J}^{k}_{s}\left((\mathbf{w}_{f}-\mathbf{w}_{s}-\mathbf{w}_{d})^{T}\mathbf{n}\right)\left((\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d})^{T}\mathbf{n}\right)\,ds\\
+\int_{\Gamma_{fs}}\tilde{J}^{k}_{s}p_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds+\int_{\Gamma_{fs}}\tilde{J}^{k}_{s}\frac{\rho_{f}}{2}|{\mathbf{v}}_{f}|^{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\\
+\gamma\int_{\Gamma_{fs}}\tilde{J}^{k}_{s}\ K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{v}_{f}-\mathbf{v}_{s})\right)\cdot\left(\mathbf{P}(\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}-\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s})\right)\,ds,\end{split}

with
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{F}^{-T}\widehat{\mathbf{n}}/|\mathbf{F}^{-T}\widehat{\mathbf{n}}|
,
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^{T}
, and
\tilde{J}^{k}_{s}=\tilde{J}^{k}|\mathbf{F}^{-T}\widehat{\mathbf{n}}|
.
Parameter
\tau
is a penalty parameter which forces the finite element solution to satisfy approximately the normal velocity continuity condition.
The third term on the right-hand side appears due to the additional term in the stress balance interface condition.

The finite element method with the backward difference time discretization reads:
Given
\mathbf{u}^{k-1}
,
\mathbf{v}^{k-1}_{f}
,
\mathbf{v}^{k-1}_{s}
,
\mathbf{q}^{k-1}
,
p^{k-1}_{d}

find
\mathbf{v}^{k}_{f}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{f}
,
\mathbf{v}^{k}_{s}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{s,0}
,
\mathbf{q}^{k}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{s}
,
p^{k}_{f}\in\mathbb{Q}_{h}^{f}
,
p^{k}_{d}\in\mathbb{Q}_{h}^{s,0}
such that

\mathbf{v}^{k}_{f}=\mathbf{g}_{h}(\cdot,(k+1)\Delta t)
on
\Gamma_{f0}
,
and the following identity holds:

\begin{split}m^{k}\left({\small\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{s}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}},\right.&\left.{\small\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{q}}{\partial t}\right]^{k},\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{f}}{\partial t}\right]^{k},\left[\frac{\partial p_{d}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}};\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f},q_{d}\right)\\
&+a_{s}^{k}(\mathbf{v}^{k}_{s},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s})+a_{d}(\mathbf{q}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s})+a_{f}^{k}(\mathbf{v}^{k}_{f},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})+c_{f}^{k}(\mathbf{v}^{k}_{f},\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}_{f}^{k}-\widetilde{\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\\
&+d^{k}(\mathbf{v}^{k}_{s},\mathbf{q}^{k},\mathbf{v}^{k}_{f};\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})\\
&-b_{s}^{k}(p_{d},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d})-b_{f}^{k}(p_{f},\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f})+b_{s}^{k}(q_{d},\mathbf{v}_{s}^{k}+\mathbf{q}^{k})+b_{f}^{k}(q_{f},\mathbf{v}_{f}^{k})=0\end{split}

(23)

for all
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{f}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{f,0}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{s}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{s,0}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$\unboldmath}_{d}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}^{s}
,
q_{f}\in\mathbb{Q}_{h}^{f}
,
q_{d}\in\mathbb{Q}_{h}^{s,0}
.
In addition, we relate the finite element displacement and the velocity field in the porous structure through the kinematic equation

\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}=\mathbf{v}^{k}_{s}\quad\text{in}~\Omega_{s}.

(24)

Equations (
23
)–(
24
) subject to the initial conditions and an equation for continuous extension of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
from
\Omega_{s}
onto

\Omega_{f}
define the discrete problem.
The continuous extension of
\mathbf{u}
in (
12
) is provided by the elasticity equation written for the velocity of the displacement
[
24
]
:

-{\rm div}\,\left[J\left(\lambda_{m}\text{tr}\left(\nabla\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}\mathbf{F}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{I}\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.+\mu_{m}\left(\nabla\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}\mathbf{F}^{-1}+\left(\nabla\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}\mathbf{F}^{-1}\right)^{T}\right)\right)\mathbf{F}^{-T}\right]=0~~~\text{in}~\Omega_{f}

(25)

satisfying the boundary condition
\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}\right]^{k}=\mathbf{v}^{k}
on the interface
\Gamma_{fs}
.
The space dependent elasticity parameters are

\mu_{m}=\mu_{s}|\Delta_{e}|^{-1.2}
,
\lambda_{m}=16\mu_{m}
,
where
|\Delta_{e}|
denotes the physical volume of a mesh tetrahedron
\Delta_{e}
subjected to displacement from the previous time step
[
24
]
.

Although the system is strongly coupled, only
a linear algebraic system should be solved on each time step
.

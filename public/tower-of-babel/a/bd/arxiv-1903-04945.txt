Partial Isometries, Duality, and Determinantal Point Processes Dedicated to Professor Hirofumi Osada on the occasion of his 60th birthday
Makoto Katori

Abstract
A determinantal point process (DPP) is an ensemble of random nonnegative-integer-valued Radon measures Ξ \Xi on a space S S with measure λ \lambda , whose correlation functions are all given by determinants specified by an integral kernel K K called the correlation kernel. We consider a pair of Hilbert spaces, H ℓ , ℓ = 1 , 2 H_{\ell},\ell=1,2 , which are assumed to be realized as L 2 L^{2} -spaces, L 2 ​ ( S ℓ , λ ℓ ) L^{2}(S_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}) , ℓ = 1 , 2 \ell=1,2 , and introduce a bounded linear operator 𝒲 : H 1 → H 2 {\cal W}:H_{1}\to H_{2} and its adjoint 𝒲 ∗ : H 2 → H 1 {\cal W}^{\ast}:H_{2}\to H_{1} . We show that if 𝒲 {\cal W} is a partial isometry of locally Hilbert–Schmidt class, then we have a unique DPP ( Ξ 1 , K 1 , λ 1 ) (\Xi_{1},K_{1},\lambda_{1}) associated with 𝒲 ∗ ​ 𝒲 {\cal W}^{*}{\cal W} . In addition, if 𝒲 ∗ {\cal W}^{*} is also of locally Hilbert–Schmidt class, then we have a unique pair of DPPs, ( Ξ ℓ , K ℓ , λ ℓ ) (\Xi_{\ell},K_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}) , ℓ = 1 , 2 \ell=1,2 . We also give a practical framework which makes 𝒲 {\cal W} and 𝒲 ∗ {\cal W}^{\ast} satisfy the above conditions. Our framework to construct pairs of DPPs implies useful duality relations between DPPs making pairs. For a correlation kernel of a given DPP our formula can provide plural different expressions, which reveal different aspects of the DPP. In order to demonstrate these advantages of our framework as well as to show that the class of DPPs obtained by this method is large enough to study universal structures in a variety of DPPs, we report plenty of examples of DPPs in one-, two-, and higher-dimensional spaces S S , where several types of weak convergence from finite DPPs to infinite DPPs are given. One-parameter ( d ∈ ℕ d\in\mathbb{N} ) series of infinite DPPs on S = ℝ d S=\mathbb{R}^{d} and ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} are discussed, which we call the Euclidean and the Heisenberg families of DPPs, respectively, following the terminologies of Zelditch. Keywords : Determinantal point processes; correlation kernels; partial isometry; locally Hilbert–Schmidt operators; duality; reproducing kernels; random matrix theory Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 60G55, 60B20, 46E22, 60B10

\|{\cal W}f\|_{H_{2}}=\|f\|_{H_{1}}\quad\mbox{for all $f\in({\rm ker}\,{\cal W})^{\perp}$}.

By the definition (

Although this might be known, we give a proof below.

Lemma 2.2

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}

be separable Hilbert spaces and
{\cal W}:H_{1}\to H_{2}
be a bounded
operator. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

(ii)

{\cal W}^{*}{\cal W}
is a projection on

,
which acts as the identity on
({\rm ker}\,{\cal W})^{\perp}
.

(iii)

{\cal W}={\cal W}{\cal W}^{*}{\cal W}
.

Moreover,
{\cal W}
is a partial isometry if and only if so is

{\cal W}^{*}
.

Proof

When
H_{1}=H_{2}
, this fact is well-known
(cf.
[
25
]
). If we apply it to

{\cal W}&O\end{pmatrix},

the assertion is followed by verifying

\widetilde{{\cal W}^{*}}\widetilde{{\cal W}}={\cal W}^{*}{\cal W}\oplus O

\widetilde{{\cal W}}\widetilde{{\cal W}^{*}}=O\oplus{\cal W}{\cal W}^{*}
and

\widetilde{{\cal W}}\widetilde{{\cal W}}^{*}\widetilde{{\cal W}}=\begin{pmatrix}O&O\\
{\cal W}{\cal W}^{*}{\cal W}&O\end{pmatrix}.

∎

2.3
Basic properties of DPPs

For
v=(v^{(1)},\dots,v^{(d)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

y=(y^{(1)},\dots,y^{(d)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
the inner product of them is given by

v\cdot y=y\cdot v:=\sum_{a=1}^{d}v^{(a)}y^{(a)}
,
and
|v|^{2}:=v\cdot v
.
When
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{d},d\in\mathbb{N}
,

x\in S
has
d
complex components;

x=(x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(d)})
with

x^{(a)}=\Re x^{(a)}+i\Im x^{(a)}
,
a=1,\dots,d
.
In order to describe clearly such a complex structure,
we set

x_{{\rm R}}=(\Re x^{(1)},\dots,\Re x^{(d)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

x_{{\rm I}}=(\Im x^{(1)},\dots,\Im x^{(d)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and write
x=x_{{\rm R}}+ix_{{\rm I}}
in this paper.
The Lebesgue measure is written as

dx=dx_{{\rm R}}dx_{{\rm I}}:=\prod_{a=1}^{d}d\Re x^{(a)}d\Im x^{(a)}
.
The complex conjugate of
x=x_{{\rm R}}+ix_{{\rm I}}

is defined as
\overline{x}=x_{{\rm R}}-ix_{{\rm I}}
.
For
x=x_{{\rm R}}+ix_{{\rm I}}
,

x^{\prime}=x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}+ix^{\prime}_{{\rm I}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
we use the
Hermitian inner product
;

x\cdot\overline{x^{\prime}}:=(x_{{\rm R}}+ix_{{\rm I}})\cdot(x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}-ix^{\prime}_{{\rm I}})=(x_{{\rm R}}\cdot x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}+x_{{\rm I}}\cdot x^{\prime}_{{\rm I}})-i(x_{{\rm R}}\cdot x^{\prime}_{{\rm I}}-x_{{\rm I}}\cdot x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}})

and define

|x|^{2}:=x\cdot\overline{x}=|x_{{\rm R}}|^{2}+|x_{{\rm I}}|^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

For
(\Xi,K,\lambda(dx))
defined
on
S=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
S=\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
or on a space having appropriate
periodicities or symmetries,
we write
\Xi=\sum_{j}\delta_{X_{j}}
and
introduce the following operations.

(Shift)

For
u\in S
,

{\cal S}_{u}\Xi:=\sum_{j}\delta_{X_{j}-u}
,

{\cal S}_{u}K(x,x^{\prime})=K(x+u,x^{\prime}+u),

and
{\cal S}_{u}\lambda(dx)=\lambda(u+dx)
.
We write
({\cal S}_{u}\Xi,{\cal S}_{u}K,{\cal S}_{u}\lambda(dx))

simply as
{\cal S}_{u}(\Xi,K,\lambda(dx))
.

(Dilatation)

For
c>0
, we set

c\circ\Xi:=\sum_{j}\delta_{cX_{j}}

c\circ K(x,x^{\prime}):=K\left(\frac{x}{c},\frac{x^{\prime}}{c}\right),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in cS:=\{cx:x\in S\},

and
c\circ\lambda(dx):=\lambda(dx/c)
.
We define
c\circ(\Xi,K,\lambda(dx)):=(c\circ\Xi,c\circ K,c\circ\lambda(dx))
.

Moreover, we also consider the following operations.

(Square root)

For
(\Xi,K,\lambda(dx))
on
S=[0,\infty)
, we put

\Xi^{\langle 1/2\rangle}:=\sum_{j}\delta_{\sqrt{X_{j}}}
,

K^{\langle 1/2\rangle}(x,x^{\prime}):=K(x^{2},{x^{\prime}}^{2})
,
and
\lambda^{\langle 1/2\rangle}(dx):=(\lambda\circ v^{-1})(dx)
,
where
v(x)=\sqrt{x}
.
We define
(\Xi,K,\lambda(dx))^{\langle 1/2\rangle}

:=(\Xi^{\langle 1/2\rangle},K^{\langle 1/2\rangle},\lambda^{\langle 1/2\rangle}(dx))

on
[0,\infty)
.

(Gauge transformation)

For non-vanishing
u:S\to\mathbb{C}
,
a gauge transformation of
K
by
u
is defined as

K(x,x^{\prime})\mapsto\widetilde{K}_{u}(x,x^{\prime}):=u(x)K(x,x^{\prime})u(x^{\prime})^{-1}.

In particular, when
u:S\to{\rm U}(1)
,
the
{\rm U}(1)
-gauge transformation of
K
is given by

K(x,x^{\prime})\mapsto\widetilde{K}_{u}(x,x^{\prime})=u(x)K(x,x^{\prime})\overline{u(x^{\prime})}.

We will use the following basic properties of DPP.

[Gauge invariance]

For any
u:S\to\mathbb{C}
, a gauge transformation
does not change the probability law of DPP;

(\Xi,K,\lambda(dx))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(law)}}{{=}}(\Xi,\widetilde{K}_{u},\lambda(dx)).

[Measure change]

For a measurable function

(\Xi,K(x,x^{\prime}),g(x)\lambda(dx))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(law)}}{{=}}(\Xi,\sqrt{g(x)}K(x,x^{\prime})\sqrt{g(x^{\prime})},\lambda(dx)).

(2.6)

[Mapping and scaling]

For a one-to-one measurable mapping

, if we set

\widehat{\Xi}:=\sum_{j}\delta_{h(X_{j})},\quad\widehat{K}(x,x^{\prime}):=K(h^{-1}(x),h^{-1}(y)),\quad\widehat{\lambda}(dx):=(\lambda\circ h^{-1})(dx),

.

2.5
Orthonormal functions and correlation kernels

In addition to
L^{2}(S_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})
,
\ell=1,2
,
we introduce
L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)
as a parameter space
for functions in
L^{2}(S_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}),\ell=1,2
.
Assume that there are two families of measurable
functions
\{\psi_{1}(x,\gamma):x\in S_{1},\gamma\in\Gamma\}
and

\{\psi_{2}(y,\gamma):y\in S_{2},\gamma\in\Gamma\}

such that
two bounded operators
{\cal U}_{\ell}:L^{2}(S_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})\to L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)

given by

({\cal U}_{\ell}f)(\gamma):=\int_{S_{\ell}}\overline{\psi_{\ell}(x,\gamma)}f(x)\lambda_{\ell}(dx),\quad\ell=1,2,

are well-defined.
Then, their adjoints
{\cal U}_{\ell}^{*}:L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)\to L^{2}(S_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell}),\ell=1,2

are given by

({\cal U}_{\ell}^{*}F)(\cdot)=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{\ell}(\cdot,\gamma)F(\gamma)\nu(d\gamma).

A typical example of
{\cal U}_{1}
is the Fourier transform,
i.e.,
\psi_{1}(x,\gamma)=e^{ix\gamma}
. In this case, for
any
\gamma
, the function
\psi_{1}(\cdot,\gamma)
is
not
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
.
Now we define
{\cal W}:L^{2}(S_{1},\lambda_{1})\to L^{2}(S_{2},\lambda_{2})
by

{\cal W}={\cal U}_{2}^{*}{\cal U}_{1}
, i.e.,

({\cal W}f)(y)=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{2}(y,\gamma)({\cal U}_{1}f)(\gamma)\nu(d\gamma).

(2.13)

Let
I_{\Gamma}
be an identity in
L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)
.
We can see the following.

Lemma 2.7

If
{\cal U}_{\ell}{\cal U}_{\ell}^{*}=I_{\Gamma}
for
\ell=1,2
, then
both
{\cal W}
and
{\cal W}^{*}
are partial isometries.

Proof

By the assumption, we see that

{\cal W}{\cal W}^{*}{\cal W}=({\cal U}_{2}^{*}{\cal U}_{1})({\cal U}_{1}^{*}{\cal U}_{2})({\cal U}_{2}^{*}{\cal U}_{1})={\cal U}_{2}^{*}{\cal U}_{1}={\cal W}.

From Lemma
2.2
,
{\cal W}
is a partial isometry.
By symmetry, the assertion for
{\cal W}^{*}
also follows.
∎

We note that

{\cal W}^{*}{\cal W}={\cal U}_{1}^{*}{\cal U}_{1}
and

{\cal W}{\cal W}^{*}={\cal U}_{2}^{*}{\cal U}_{2}
. Hence,

{\cal U}_{\ell},\ell=1,2
are also partial isometries.
In addition,

{\cal W}^{\ast}{\cal W}
is a locally trace
class operator if and only if so is
{\cal U}_{1}^{\ast}{\cal U}_{1}
.
Therefore,
{\cal W}
is of locally Hilbert–Schmidt class if and only if
so is
{\cal U}_{1}
.

Now we rewrite the condition for

{\cal U}_{1}
to be of locally Hilbert–Schmidt class in terms of the function

\psi_{1}(x,\gamma),x\in S_{1},\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.8

Let
\Psi_{1}(x):=\|\psi_{1}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)}
,
x\in S_{1}

and assume that

\Psi_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S_{1},\lambda_{1})
.
Then, the operator
{\cal U}_{1}
is of locally Hilbert–Schmidt class.

Proof
For a compact set
\Lambda\subset S_{1}
, we see that

\displaystyle|{\cal P}_{\Lambda}{\cal U}_{1}^{*}{\cal U}_{1}{\cal P}_{\Lambda}f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)\int_{\Gamma}\nu(d\gamma)\psi_{1}(x,\gamma)\int_{S_{1}}\overline{\psi_{1}(x^{\prime},\gamma)}\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})\lambda_{1}(dx^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)\Psi_{1}(x)\int_{S_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x^{\prime})\Psi_{1}(x^{\prime})|f(x^{\prime})|\lambda_{1}(dx^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq{\cal P}_{\Lambda}\Psi_{1}(x)\|{\cal P}_{\Lambda}\Psi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(S_{1},\lambda_{1})}\|{\cal P}_{\Lambda}f\|_{L^{2}(S_{1},\lambda_{1})}.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

{\cal P}_{\Lambda}{\cal U}_{1}^{*}{\cal U}_{1}{\cal P}_{\Lambda}f(x)=\int_{S_{1}}\lambda_{1}(dx^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})\left(\int_{\Gamma}\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)\psi_{1}(x,\gamma)\overline{\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x^{\prime})\psi_{1}(x^{\prime},\gamma)}\nu(d\gamma)\right)

and hence

\|{\cal U}_{1}{\cal P}_{\Lambda}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}}={\rm Tr}\,({\cal P}_{\Lambda}{\cal U}_{1}^{*}{\cal U}_{1}{\cal P}_{\Lambda})=\int_{S_{1}}\lambda_{1}(dx)\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda}(x)\left(\int_{\Gamma}|\psi_{1}(x,\gamma)|^{2}\nu(d\gamma)\right)=\|{\cal P}_{\Lambda}\Psi_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(S_{1},\lambda_{1})}<\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we put the following.

Assumption 3

For
\ell=1,2
,

(i)

,

(ii)
\Psi_{\ell}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\Gamma(\nu+1,1)}(dy)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}y^{\nu}e^{-y}{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(y)dy,

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(y)

\displaystyle=\phi_{n}^{(\nu)}(y)=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(n+\nu+1)}}\,L^{(\nu)}_{n}(y),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.5)

with parameter
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
.
Here

\{H_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
are the
Hermite polynomials
,

\displaystyle H_{n}(x)

\displaystyle:=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{-x^{2}}

\displaystyle=n!\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}\frac{(-1)^{k}(2x)^{n-2k}}{k!(n-2k)!},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.6)

where
[a]
denotes the largest integer not greater than

a\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\{L^{(\nu)}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
are
the
Laguerre polynomials
,

\displaystyle L^{(\nu)}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n!}x^{-\nu}e^{x}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\Big(x^{n+\nu}e^{-x}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(\nu+k+1)_{n-k}}{(n-k)!k!}(-x)^{k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad\nu\in(-1,\infty),

(3.7)

where
(\alpha)_{n}:=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)=\Gamma(\alpha+n)/\Gamma(\alpha)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\alpha)_{0}:=1
.
The correlation kernels (
3.2
) are written
in the
Christoffel–Darboux form
as,

K^{(N)}_{\varphi}(x,x^{\prime})=K_{\rm Hermite}^{(N)}(x,x^{\prime})=\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\varphi_{N}(x)\varphi_{N-1}(x^{\prime})-\varphi_{N}(x^{\prime})\varphi_{N-1}(x)}{x-x^{\prime}},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

and

\displaystyle K^{(N)}_{\phi}(y,y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=K_{\rm Laguerre}^{(\nu,N)}(y,y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{N(N+\nu)}\frac{\phi^{(\nu)}_{N}(y)\phi^{(\nu)}_{N-1}(y^{\prime})-\phi^{(\nu)}_{N}(y^{\prime})\phi^{(\nu)}_{N-1}(y)}{y-y^{\prime}},\quad y,y^{\prime}\in[0,\infty).

When
x=x^{\prime}
or
y=y^{\prime}
, we make sense of the above formulas
by using L’Hôpital’s rule.
The former is called the
Hermite kernel

and the latter is the
Laguerre kernel
.

By definition,
for a finite DPP
(\Xi,K,\lambda(dx))
with
N
points in
S
,
the probability density with respect to

\lambda^{\otimes N}(dx_{1}\cdots dx_{N})
is given by

\rho^{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\det_{1\leq j,k\leq N}[K(x_{j},x_{k})],\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in S^{N}
.
Using the
Vandermonde determinantal formula
,

\det_{1\leq j,k\leq N}(z_{k}^{j-1})=\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}(z_{k}-z_{j})
,
which is also given as the type
{\it A}_{N-1}
of
Weyl denominator formula (
A.1
)
in Appendix
A
,
we can verify that
the probability densities
of the DPPs

(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/2)}(dx))

and

(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{\rm Laguerre},\lambda_{\Gamma(\nu+1,1)}(dy))

with respect to
the Lebesgue measures

d\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}dx_{j}
and

d\mbox{\boldmath$y$}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}dy_{j}
are given
as

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}(x_{k}-x_{j})^{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}e^{-x_{\ell}^{2}},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{(\nu,N)}_{\rm Laguerre}(\mbox{\boldmath$y$})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{(\nu,N)}_{\rm Laguerre}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}(y_{k}-y_{j})^{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}y_{\ell}^{\nu}e^{-y_{\ell}},\quad\nu>-1,\quad\mbox{\boldmath$y$}\in[0,\infty)^{N},

(3.8)

with the normalization constants

Z^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite}
and

Z^{(\nu,N)}_{\rm Laguerre}
.

The DPP
(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/2)}(dy))

describes
the eigenvalue distribution of

N\times N
Hermitian random
matrices in the
Gaussian unitary ensemble
(GUE).
When
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
the DPP

(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{\rm Laguerre}
,
\lambda_{\Gamma(\nu+1,1)}(dx))

describes the distribution of the nonnegative square roots
of eigenvalues of
M^{\dagger}M
,
where
M
is
(N+\nu)\times N
complex
random matrix in the

chiral Gaussian ensemble
(chGUE)
and
M^{\dagger}
is its Hermitian conjugate.
The probability density (
3.8
)
can be extended to any
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
and it
is called the
complex Laguerre ensemble

or the
complex Wishart ensemble
.
Many other examples of one-dimensional DPPs
are given as eigenvalue ensembles
of Hermitian random matrices in the literature of
random matrix theory (see, for instance,
[
43
,
21
,
36
]
).

3.1.2
Duality relations between
DPPs in continuous and discrete spaces

We consider the simplified setting (
3.3
) of
W

with
\Gamma=\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
If we set

\Lambda_{1}=[r,\infty)\subset S_{1}=\mathbb{R},r\in\mathbb{R}
and

\Lambda_{2}=\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}\subset S_{2}=\Gamma=\mathbb{N}_{0},N\in\mathbb{N}

in (
2.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle K_{\mathbb{R}}^{\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{n}(x^{\prime})},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle K^{[r,\infty)}_{\mathbb{N}_{0}}(n,n^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{\infty}\overline{\varphi_{n}(x)}\varphi_{n^{\prime}}(x)\lambda_{1}(dx),\quad n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.9)

When
\lambda_{1}(dx)
and
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

are given by (
3.4
) or by (
3.5
),
the kernels (
3.9
) are given by

\displaystyle K_{{\rm DHermite}^{+}(r)}(n,n^{\prime})=(\pi 2^{n+n^{\prime}}n!n^{\prime}!)^{-1/2}\int_{r}^{\infty}H_{n}(x)H_{n^{\prime}}(x)e^{-x^{2}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad=-(\pi n!n^{\prime}!2^{n+n^{\prime}+2})^{-1/2}e^{-r^{2}}\frac{H_{n+1}(r)H_{n^{\prime}}(r)-H_{n}(r)H_{n^{\prime}+1}(r)}{n-n^{\prime}},

and, provided
r>0
,

\displaystyle K_{{\rm DLaguerre}^{+}(r,\nu+1)}(n,n^{\prime})=\left(\frac{n!n^{\prime}!}{\Gamma(n+\nu+1)\Gamma(n^{\prime}+\nu+1)}\right)^{1/2}\int_{r}^{\infty}L^{(\nu)}_{n}(x)L^{(\nu)}_{n^{\prime}}(x)x^{\nu}e^{-x}dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\frac{n!n^{\prime}!}{\Gamma(n+\nu+1)\Gamma(n^{\prime}+\nu+1)}\right)^{1/2}r^{\nu+1}e^{-r}\frac{L^{(\nu+1)}_{n-1}(r)L^{(\nu)}_{n^{\prime}}(r)-L^{(\nu)}_{n}(r)L^{(\nu+1)}_{n^{\prime}-1}(r)}{n-n^{\prime}},

with the convention that
L^{(\nu)}_{-1}(r)=0
,
respectively (see Propositions 3.3 and 3.4 in
[
15
]
).
Borodin and Olshanski called
the correlation kernels
K_{{\rm DHermite}^{+}(r)}
and

K_{{\rm DLaguerre}^{+}(r,\nu+1)}
the
discrete Hermite kernel

and the
discrete Laguerre kernel
, respectively
[
15
]
.
Theorem
2.6
gives

{\bf P}(\Xi_{1}^{\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}}([r,\infty))=m)={\bf P}(\Xi_{2}^{[r,\infty)}(\{0,1,\dots,N-1\})=m),\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.10)

where LHS denotes the probability that
the number of points in the interval
[r,\infty)

is
m
for the
N
-point continuous DPP on
\mathbb{R}
such as

(\Xi_{1},K^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite},\lambda_{N(0,1/2)}(dx))

or
(\Xi_{1},K^{(N)}_{\rm Laguerre},\lambda_{\Gamma(\nu+1,1)}(dx))
,

\nu\in(-1,\infty)
,
while RHS does the probability that
the number of points in
\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
is
m
for
the discrete DPP on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
such as

(\Xi_{2},K_{{\rm DHermite}^{+}(r)})

or
(\Xi_{2},K_{{\rm DLaguerre}^{+}(r,\nu+1)})
,
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
.
The
duality between continuous and discrete ensembles

of Borodin and Olshanski (Theorem 3.7 in
[
15
]
) is
a special case with
m=0
of the equality (
3.10
).

3.2
Finite DPPs in intervals
related with classical root systems

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the four types of

classical root systems
denoted by
{\it A}_{N-1},{\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N}
, and
{\it D}_{N}

(see Appendix
A
).
We set

S^{{\it A}_{N-1}}=\mathbb{S}^{1}=[0,2\pi)
, the unit circle,
with a uniform measure

\lambda^{{\it A}_{N-1}}(dx)=\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx):=dx/(2\pi)
, and

S^{{\it R}_{N}}=[0,\pi]
, the upper half-circle, with

\lambda^{{\it R}_{N}}(dx)=\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx):=dx/\pi
for

{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N},{\it D}_{N}
.

For a fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the four sets of
functions
\{\varphi^{{\it R}_{N}}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
on
S^{{\it R}_{N}}
defined as

\displaystyle\varphi^{{\it R}_{N}}_{n}(x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{e^{-i({\cal N}^{{\it A}_{N-1}}-2J^{{\it A}_{N-1}}(n))x/2}},&{\it R}_{N}={\it A}_{N-1},\cr\displaystyle{\sin\big[({\cal N}^{{\it R}_{N}}-2J^{{\it R}_{N}}(n))x/2\big]},&{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N},\cr\displaystyle{\cos\big[({\cal N}^{{\it D}_{N}}-2J^{{\it D}_{N}}(n))x/2\big]},&{\it R}_{N}={\it D}_{N},\end{cases}

where

{\cal N}^{{\it R}_{N}}:=\begin{cases}N,\quad&{\it R}_{N}={\it A}_{N-1},\\
2N-1,\quad&{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},\\
2(N+1),\quad&{\it R}_{N}={\it C}_{N},\\
2(N-1),\quad&{\it R}_{N}={\it D}_{N}.\end{cases}

(3.11)

and

J^{{\it R}_{N}}(n):=\begin{cases}n-1/2,\quad&{\it R}_{N}={\it A}_{N-1},\\
n-1,\quad&{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it D}_{N},\\
n,\quad&{\it R}_{N}={\it C}_{N}.\end{cases}

(3.12)

It is easy to verify that they satisfy the following
orthonormality relations,

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{{\it A}_{N-1}}_{n},\varphi^{{\it A}_{N-1}}_{m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{nm},

\displaystyle\langle\varphi^{{\it R}_{N}}_{n},\varphi^{{\it R}_{N}}_{m}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,\pi],\lambda_{[0,\pi]})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{nm},\quad{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N},{\it D}_{N},\quad\mbox{if $n,m\in\{1,\dots,N\}$}.

We put
\Gamma=\{1,\dots,N\},N\in\mathbb{N}
and

L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)=\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
.
By the argument given in Remark 3 in Section
3.1.1
,
Assumption 3 is verified, and hence
Corollary
2.9
gives the four types of DPPs;

(\Xi,K^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx))
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
and

(\Xi,K^{{\it R}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))
on
[0,\pi]
,

{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N},{\it D}_{N}
, with the correlation kernels,

\displaystyle K^{{\it R}_{N}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varphi^{{\it R}_{N}}_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi^{{\it R}_{N}}_{n}(x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\sin\{N(x-x^{\prime})/2\}}{\sin\{(x-x^{\prime})/2\}}},&{\it R}_{N}={\it A}_{N-1},\cr\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\sin\{N(x-x^{\prime})\}}{\sin\{(x-x^{\prime})/2\}}-\frac{\sin\{N(x+x^{\prime})\}}{\sin\{(x+x^{\prime})/2\}}\right]},&{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},\cr&\cr\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\sin\{(2N+1)(x-x^{\prime})/2\}}{\sin\{(x-x^{\prime})/2\}}-\frac{\sin\{(2N+1)(x+x^{\prime})/2\}}{\sin\{(x+x^{\prime})/2\}}\right]},&{\it R}_{N}={\it C}_{N},\cr&\cr\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\sin\{(2N-1)(x-x^{\prime})/2\}}{\sin\{(x-x^{\prime})/2\}}+\frac{\sin\{(2N-1)(x+x^{\prime})/2\}}{\sin\{(x+x^{\prime})/2\}}\right]},&{\it R}_{N}={\it D}_{N}.\cr\end{cases}

By Lemma
A.1

in Appendix
A
,
the probability densities for these finite DPPs
with respect to
the Lebesgue measures,

d\mbox{\boldmath$x$}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}dx_{j}
are given
as

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{{\it A}_{N-1}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{{\it A}_{N-1}}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\sin^{2}\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}}{2},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in[0,2\pi)^{N},

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{{\it B}_{N}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{{\it B}_{N}}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left(\sin^{2}\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{x_{k}+x_{j}}{2}\right)\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\sin^{2}\frac{x_{\ell}}{2},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in[0,\pi]^{N},

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{{\it C}_{N}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{{\it C}_{N}}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left(\sin^{2}\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{x_{k}+x_{j}}{2}\right)\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\sin^{2}x_{\ell},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in[0,\pi]^{N},

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{{\it D}_{N}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{{\it D}_{N}}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\left(\sin^{2}\frac{x_{k}-x_{j}}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{x_{k}+x_{j}}{2}\right),\quad\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in[0,\pi]^{N},

with the normalization constants
Z^{{\it R}_{N}}
.

The DPP,
(\Xi,K^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx))
is known as
the
circular unitary ensemble
(CUE)
in random matrix theory (see Section 11.8 in
[
43
]
).
These four types of DPPs,

(\Xi,K^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx))
,

(\Xi,K^{{\it R}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))
,

{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N},{\it D}_{N}

are realized as the eigenvalue distributions of
random matrices in the
classical groups
,

{\rm U}(N)
,
{\rm SO}(2N+1)
,
{\rm Sp}(N)
, and
{\rm SO}(2N)
, respectively.
(See Section 2.3 c) in
[
58
]

and Section 5.5 in
[
21
]
.)

Finite DPPs in intervals

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{2\pi/N}(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm(law)}}{{=}}(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}),

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}^{{\it R}_{N}}(0)=0,\quad{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it C}^{\vee}_{N},{\it BC}_{N},

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}^{{\it R}_{N}}(0)=\rho_{\tau}^{{\it R}_{N}}(\pi)=0,\quad{\it R}_{N}={\it B}^{\vee}_{N},{\it C}_{N}.

In
[
33
]
, it was proved that
these seven types of DPPs
are realized as the particle configurations
at the middle time
t=t_{\ast}/2

of the

noncolliding Brownian bridges

in time duration
[0,t_{\ast}]
,
provided
t_{\ast}=4\pi\Im\tau>0
, whose
initial configurations at
t=0
and
final configurations at
t=t_{\ast}
are fixed
to be specially chosen configurations
depending on the types
{\it R}_{N},N\in\mathbb{N}
.

As
\Im\tau\to\infty
, the temporal inhomogeneity
in such noncolliding Brownian bridges vanishes.
Associated with such limit transitions,
the following degeneracies are observed
in the weak convergence of DPPs
from the seven types of affine root systems
to the four types of classical root systems,

\displaystyle(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Im\tau\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx)),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it B}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\cr\displaystyle{(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it C}^{\vee}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\cr\displaystyle{(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it BC}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Im\tau\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K^{{\it B}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx)),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it B}^{\vee}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\cr\displaystyle{(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it C}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Im\tau\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K^{{\it C}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx)),

\displaystyle(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it D}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Im\tau\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K^{{\it D}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx)),

(3.22)

where the DPPs,
(\Xi,K^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)})

and
(\Xi,K^{{\it R}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]})
,

{\it R}_{N}={\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N},{\it D}_{N}

were given in Section
3.2
.

3.4
Infinite DPPs in
\mathbb{R}

associated with classical orthonormal functions

Here we give examples of infinite DPPs obtained
by Corollary
2.10
.

(i)  DPP with the
sinc kernel
:
We set
S_{1}=\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda_{1}(dx)=dx
,

\Gamma=(-1,1)
,
\nu(dy)=\lambda_{2}(dy)=dy
, and put

\psi_{1}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ixy}.

We see that

\Psi_{1}(x)^{2}\equiv 1/\pi,x\in\mathbb{R}
and thus Assumption 3’(ii) is satisfied.
The correlation kernel
K_{S_{1}}
is given by

K_{\rm sinc}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{iy(x-x^{\prime})}dy=\frac{\sin(x-x^{\prime})}{\pi(x-x^{\prime})},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}.

(ii)  DPP with the
Airy kernel
:
We set
S_{1}=\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda_{1}(dx)=dx
,

\Gamma=[0,\infty)
,
\nu(dy)=\lambda_{2}(dy)=dy
,
and put

\psi_{1}(x,y)={\rm Ai}(x+y),

where
{\rm Ai}(x)
denotes the
Airy function

[
45
]

{\rm Ai}(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos\left(\frac{k^{3}}{3}+kx\right)dk.

We see that

\Psi_{1}(x)^{2}=-x{\rm Ai}(x)^{2}+{\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x)^{2},x\in\mathbb{R}

and thus Assumption 3(ii) is satisfied.
The correlation kernel
K_{S_{1}}
is given by

K_{\rm Airy}(x,x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm Ai}(x+y){\rm Ai}(x^{\prime}+y)dy=\frac{{\rm Ai}(x){\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})-{\rm Ai}(x^{\prime}){\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x)}{x-x^{\prime}},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

where
{\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x):=d{\rm Ai}(x)/dx
.

(iii)  DPP with the
Bessel kernel
:
We set

S_{1}=[0,\infty)
,
\lambda_{1}(dx)=dx
,

\Gamma=[0,1]
,

\nu(dy)=\lambda_{2}(dy)=dy
.
With parameter
\nu>-1
we put

\psi_{1}(x,y)=\sqrt{xy}J_{\nu}(xy),

where
J_{\nu}
is the

Bessel function of the first kind
defined by

J_{\nu}(x):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!\Gamma(\nu+n+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2n+\nu},\quad x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0).

(3.23)

We see that

\Psi_{1}(x)^{2}=x\{J_{\nu}(x)^{2}-J_{\nu-1}(x)J_{\nu+1}(x)\}/2
,

x\in[0,\infty)

and thus Assumption 3’(ii) is satisfied.
The correlation kernel
K_{S_{1}}
is given by

\displaystyle K_{\rm Bessel}^{(\nu)}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{xy}J_{\nu}(xy)\sqrt{x^{\prime}y}J_{\nu}(x^{\prime}y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{xx^{\prime}}}{x^{2}-(x^{\prime})^{2}}\Big\{J_{\nu}(x)x^{\prime}J_{\nu}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})-xJ_{\nu}^{\prime}(x)J_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\Big\},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in[0,\infty),

(3.24)

where
J_{\nu}^{\prime}(x):=dJ_{\nu}(x)/dx
.

These three kinds of infinite DPPs,

(\Xi,K_{\rm sinc},dx)
,
(\Xi,K_{\rm Airy},dx)
,
and
(\Xi,K_{\rm Bessel}^{(\nu)},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx)
,
are obtained as the scaling limits
of the finite DPPs,

(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/2)}(dx))

and
(\Xi,K^{(\nu,N)}_{\rm Laguerre}
,
\lambda_{\Gamma(\nu+1,1)}(dx))
,
given in Section
3.1

as follows.

(i)
Bulk scaling limit
,

\sqrt{2N}\circ(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/2)}(dx))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm sinc},dx).

(ii)
Soft-edge scaling limit
,

\sqrt{2}N^{1/6}\circ{\cal S}_{\sqrt{2N}}\,(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{\rm Hermite},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/2)}(dx))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm Airy},dx).

(iii)
Hard-edge scaling limit
,
for
\nu>-1
,

4N\circ\Big((\Xi,K^{(\nu,N)}_{\rm Laguerre},\lambda_{\Gamma(\nu+1,1)}(dx))^{\langle 1/2\rangle}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K^{(\nu)}_{\rm Bessel},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx).

See, for instance,
[
43
,
21
,
8
,
32
]
,
for more details.

The DPPs with the sinc kernel and
the Bessel kernel with the special values of
parameter
\nu
can be obtained as the bulk
scaling limits of the DPPs,

(\Xi,K^{{\it R}_{N}},\lambda^{{\it R}_{N}}(dx))
,

{\it R}_{N}={\it A}_{N-1},{\it B}_{N},{\it C}_{N},{\it D}_{N}

given in Section
3.2
as

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}\circ(\Xi,K^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm sinc},dx),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}N\circ(\Xi,K^{{\it B}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))\cr N\circ(\Xi,K^{{\it C}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm Bessel}^{(1/2)},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx),

\displaystyle N\circ(\Xi,K^{{\it D}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm Bessel}^{(-1/2)},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx),

(3.27)

where

\displaystyle K_{\rm Bessel}^{(1/2)}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x-x^{\prime})}{\pi(x-x^{\prime})}-\frac{\sin(x+x^{\prime})}{\pi(x+x^{\prime})},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle K_{\rm Bessel}^{(-1/2)}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x-x^{\prime})}{\pi(x-x^{\prime})}+\frac{\sin(x+x^{\prime})}{\pi(x+x^{\prime})},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in[0,\infty).

Since
J_{1/2}(x)=\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\sin x
and

J_{-1/2}(x)=\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\cos x
,
the above correlation kernels
are readily obtained from (
3.24
)
by setting
\nu=1/2
and
-1/2
, respectively.

3.5
Infinite DPPs in
\mathbb{R}

associated with orthonormal theta functions

Let
S=\mathbb{R}
with

\lambda^{A}(dx)=dx
,
and

\lambda^{R}(dx)={\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx

for the types
R=B,C,D
.
Here we assume that
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
is pure imaginary.
We put

\displaystyle\psi^{A}(x,\gamma;\tau)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\Theta^{A}(\gamma,x/\pi,\tau)}{\sqrt{\pi\vartheta_{2}(2\tau\gamma;2\tau)}},

\displaystyle\psi^{R}(x,\gamma;\tau)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\Theta^{R}(\gamma/2,x/\pi,\tau)}{\sqrt{2\pi\vartheta_{2}(\tau\gamma;2\tau)}},\quad R=B,C,D,

and
\Gamma=(0,1)
with

\nu(d\gamma)=d\gamma
,
where
\Theta^{R},R=A,B,C,D
are given by (
3.13
).

Using the equalities

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2i\{(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})+(n-m)\}x}dx=\delta_{nm}\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\{(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})+2(n+m-1)\}x}dx=0,\quad\mbox{for $n,m\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma$},

we can show the orthonormality relations
[
33
]
;

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{A}(\cdot,\gamma;\tau),\psi^{A}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime};\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)}

\displaystyle=\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{R}(\cdot,\gamma;\tau),\psi^{R}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime};\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx)}

\displaystyle=\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}),\quad R=A,B,C,D,\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma.

We can also evaluate the upper bounds for

\Psi^{R}(x;\tau)^{2}:=\|\psi^{R}(x,\cdot;\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)}^{2}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

R=A,B,C,D
,
and confirm that Assumption 3(ii) is also satisfied.
Hence by Corollary
2.9
,
we obtain the
four types of infinite DPPs

(\Xi,K^{A}_{\tau},dx)
,

(\Xi,K^{R}_{\tau},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx)
,

R=B,C,D
.
The correlation kernels are written as follows,

\displaystyle K^{A}_{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}e^{2i(x-x^{\prime})\gamma}\frac{\vartheta_{2}(x/\pi+\tau\gamma;\tau)\vartheta_{2}(x^{\prime}/\pi-\tau\gamma;\tau)}{\vartheta_{2}(2\tau\gamma;2\tau)}d\gamma,\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle K^{B}_{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i(x-x^{\prime})\gamma}\frac{\vartheta_{1}(x/\pi+\tau\gamma/2;\tau)\vartheta_{1}(x^{\prime}/\pi-\tau\gamma/2;\tau)}{\vartheta_{2}(\tau\gamma;2\tau)}d\gamma\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i(x+x^{\prime})\gamma}\frac{\vartheta_{1}(x/\pi+\tau\gamma/2;\tau)\vartheta_{1}(x^{\prime}/\pi+\tau\gamma/2);\tau)}{\vartheta_{2}(\tau\gamma;2\tau)}d\gamma\right],\quad x,x^{\prime}\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle K^{C}_{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i(x-x^{\prime})\gamma}\frac{\vartheta_{2}(x/\pi+\tau\gamma/2;\tau)\vartheta_{2}(x^{\prime}/\pi-\tau\gamma/2;\tau)}{\vartheta_{2}(\tau\gamma;2\tau)}d\gamma\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.-\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i(x+x^{\prime})\gamma}\frac{\vartheta_{2}(x/\pi+\tau\gamma/2;\tau)\vartheta_{2}(x^{\prime}/\pi+\tau\gamma/2);\tau)}{\vartheta_{2}(\tau\gamma;2\tau)}d\gamma\right],\quad x,x^{\prime}\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle K^{D}_{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i(x-x^{\prime})\gamma}\frac{\vartheta_{2}(x/\pi+\tau\gamma/2;\tau)\vartheta_{2}(x^{\prime}/\pi-\tau\gamma/2;\tau)}{\vartheta_{2}(\tau\gamma;2\tau)}d\gamma\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\int_{-1}^{1}e^{i(x+x^{\prime})\gamma}\frac{\vartheta_{2}(x/\pi+\tau\gamma/2;\tau)\vartheta_{2}(x^{\prime}/\pi+\tau\gamma/2);\tau)}{\vartheta_{2}(\tau\gamma;2\tau)}d\gamma\right],\quad x,x^{\prime}\in[0,\infty).

(3.28)

If we change the integral variables appropriately,
the above become the correlation kernels

{\cal K}_{t_{\ast}/2}^{R}
,
R=A,B,C,D
,
with
t_{\ast}=4\pi\Im\tau
, given in
Lemma 3.5 in
[
33
]
.
Using the quasi-periodicity (
B.3
)
of the theta functions, we can show that
the DPP
(\Xi,K^{A}_{\tau},dx)
has a periodicity of
\pi
;

{\cal S}_{\pi}K^{A}_{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})=K^{A}_{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})
,
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
.
By the symmetry (
B.2
) of the theta functions,
we see that
\rho^{R}_{\tau}(0)=K^{R}_{\tau}(0,0)=0
,

R=B,C
.

The infinite DPPs associated with the
above correlation kernels (
3.28
) are
obtained as the bulk scaling limits
of the finite DPPs in intervals
studied in Section
3.3

[
33
]
;

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}\circ(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(dx))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{A},dx),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{N\circ(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it B}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\cr\displaystyle{N\circ(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it B}^{\vee}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{B},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{N\circ(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it C}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\cr\displaystyle{N\circ(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it C}^{\vee}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\cr\displaystyle{N\circ(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it BC}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))}\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{C},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx),

\displaystyle N\circ(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{{\it D}_{N}},\lambda_{[0,\pi]}(dx))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{D},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx).

(3.34)

If we take the limit
\Im\tau\to\infty
in (

),

\displaystyle(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{A},dx)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Im\tau\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm sinc},dx),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{B},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx)\cr(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{C},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx)\end{array}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Im\tau\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm Bessel}^{(1/2)},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx),

\displaystyle(\Xi,K_{\tau}^{D},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Im\tau\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}(\Xi,K_{\rm Bessel}^{(-1/2)},{\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx),

4
Examples in Two-dimensional Spaces

4.1
Infinite DPPs on
\mathbb{C}
: Ginibre and Ginibre-type DPPs

4.1.1
Three types of Ginibre DPPs

Let

\lambda_{{\rm N}(m,\sigma^{2};\mathbb{C})}(dx)
denote
the
complex normal distribution
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{{\rm N}(m,\sigma;\mathbb{C})}(dx)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\pi\sigma^{2}}e^{-|x-m|^{2}/\sigma^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi\sigma^{2}}e^{-(x_{{\rm R}}-m_{{\rm R}})^{2}/\sigma^{2}-(x_{{\rm I}}-m_{{\rm I}})^{2}/\sigma^{2}}dx_{{\rm R}}dx_{{\rm I}},

m\in\mathbb{C},m_{{\rm R}}:=\Re m,m_{{\rm I}}:=\Im m,\sigma>0
.
We set

S=\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\lambda(dx)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx)=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-|x|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/2)}(dx_{{\rm R}})\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/2)}(dx_{{\rm I}}),

and

\displaystyle\psi^{A}(x,\gamma)

\displaystyle:=e^{-(x_{{\rm R}}^{2}-x_{{\rm I}}^{2})/2+2x\gamma},

\displaystyle\psi^{C}(x,\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{2}\sinh(2x\gamma)e^{-(x_{{\rm R}}^{2}-x_{{\rm I}}^{2})/2},

\displaystyle\psi^{D}(x,\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{2}\cosh(2x\gamma)e^{-(x_{{\rm R}}^{2}-x_{{\rm I}}^{2})/2}.

It is easy to confirm that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{A}(x,\gamma)\overline{\psi^{A}(x,\gamma^{\prime})}e^{-x_{{\rm I}}^{2}}dx_{{\rm I}}

\displaystyle=e^{-(x_{{\rm R}}^{2}-4x_{{\rm R}}\gamma)}\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{R}(x,\gamma)\overline{\psi^{R}(x,\gamma^{\prime})}e^{-x_{{\rm I}}^{2}}dx_{{\rm I}}

\displaystyle=e^{-x_{{\rm R}}^{2}}\cosh(4x_{{\rm R}}\gamma)\times\begin{cases}\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})-\delta(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}),&R=C,\cr\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})+\delta(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}),&R=D.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{A}(\cdot,\gamma),\psi^{A}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})})}\nu(d\gamma)=\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})d\gamma,\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{A}:=\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{R}(\cdot,\gamma),\psi^{R}(\cdot,\gamma^{\prime})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})})}\nu(d\gamma)=\delta(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})d\gamma,\quad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{R}:=(0,\infty),\quad R=C,D,

with

\nu(d\gamma)=\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/4)}(d\gamma)=\sqrt{2/\pi}e^{-2\gamma^{2}}d\gamma
.
We also see that

\Psi^{A}(x)^{2}:=\|\psi^{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{A},\nu)}^{2}=e^{|x|^{2}}
,

\Psi^{C}(x)^{2}:=\|\psi^{C}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{C},\nu)}^{2}=\sinh|x|^{2}
,
and

\Psi^{D}(x)^{2}:=\|\psi^{D}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{D},\nu)}^{2}=\cosh|x|^{2}
,

x\in\mathbb{C}
.
Thus Assumption 3 is satisfied and we can
apply Corollary
2.9
.
The kernels (
2.14
) of obtained DPPs are given as

\displaystyle K^{A}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\{(x_{{\rm R}}^{2}-x_{{\rm I}}^{2})+({x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}}^{2}-{x^{\prime}_{{\rm I}}}^{2})\}/2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\{\gamma^{2}-(x+\overline{x^{\prime}})\gamma\}}d\gamma,

\displaystyle K^{C}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\{(x_{{\rm R}}^{2}-x_{{\rm I}}^{2})+({x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}}^{2}-{x^{\prime}_{{\rm I}}}^{2})\}/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\gamma^{2}}\sinh(2x\gamma)\sinh(2\overline{x^{\prime}}\gamma)d\gamma,

\displaystyle K^{D}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-\{(x_{{\rm R}}^{2}-x_{{\rm I}}^{2})+({x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}}^{2}-{x^{\prime}_{{\rm I}}}^{2})\}/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\gamma^{2}}\cosh(2x\gamma)\cosh(2\overline{x^{\prime}}\gamma)d\gamma.

The integrals are performed and we obtain

K^{R}(x,x^{\prime})=e^{ix_{{\rm R}}x_{{\rm I}}}K^{R}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x,x^{\prime})e^{-ix_{{\rm R}}^{\prime}x_{{\rm I}}^{\prime}}
,

R=A,C,D
,
with

\displaystyle K^{A}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=e^{x\overline{x^{\prime}}},

(4.1)

\displaystyle K^{C}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sinh(x\overline{x^{\prime}}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle K^{D}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\cosh(x\overline{x^{\prime}}),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}.

(4.3)

Due to the gauge invariance of DPP mentioned in Section
2.3
,
the obtained three types of infinite DPPs on
\mathbb{C}
are written as

(\Xi,K_{\rm Ginibre}^{R},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx))
,
R=A,C,D
.
The DPP,
(\Xi
,
K_{\rm Ginibre}^{A}
,
\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx))

with (
4.1
)
describes the eigenvalue distribution of the
Gaussian random complex matrix in the bulk scaling limit,
which is called the
complex Ginibre ensemble

[
23
,
43
,
28
,
29
,
21
,
52
]
.
This density profile is uniform
with the Lebesgue measure
dx
on
\mathbb{C}
as

\rho_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x)dx=K^{A}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x,x)\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx)=\frac{1}{\pi}dx_{{\rm R}}dx_{{\rm I}},\quad x\in\mathbb{C}.

On the other hands, the Ginibre DPPs
of types
C
and
D

with the correlation kernels (
4.2
)
and (
4.3
)
are rotationally symmetric around the origin,
but non-uniform on
\mathbb{C}
.
The density profiles
with the Lebesgue measure
dx
on
\mathbb{C}
are given by

\displaystyle\rho^{C}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x)dx

\displaystyle=K^{C}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x,x)\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(1-e^{-2|x|^{2}})dx_{{\rm R}}dx_{{\rm I}},\quad x\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle\rho^{D}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x)dx

\displaystyle=K^{D}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(x,x)\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx)=\frac{1}{2\pi}(1+e^{-2|x|^{2}})dx_{{\rm R}}dx_{{\rm I}},\quad x\in\mathbb{C}.

They were first obtained in
[
34
]

4.1.2
Ginibre and Ginibre-type DPPs
as examples of Weyl–Heisenberg ensembles

Let
d=1
and consider the following window function,

G(x_{{\rm R}})=2^{1/4}e^{-\pi x_{{\rm R}}^{2}},\quad x_{{\rm R}}\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.4)

It is obvious that Assumption 4
is satisfied,
\|G\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx_{{\rm R}})}=1
.
In this case (
2.19
) becomes
[
1
,
3
,
2
]

K_{\rm WH}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{e^{\pi ix_{{\rm R}}x_{{\rm I}}}}{e^{\pi ix_{{\rm R}}^{\prime}x_{{\rm I}}^{\prime}}}K_{{\rm Ginibre}}^{A}(\sqrt{\pi}x,\sqrt{\pi}x^{\prime})e^{-\pi(|x|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2})/2}.

By taking into account the direct decomposition

L^{2}(\mathbb{R})=L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}_{\rm even}(\mathbb{R}),

we have

{\cal W}^{\ast}_{\rm WH}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))={\cal W}^{\ast}_{\rm WH}(L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{R}))\oplus{\cal W}^{\ast}_{\rm WH}(L^{2}_{\rm even}(\mathbb{R})).

When
G(-y)=G(y)
, we have

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{\ast}_{\rm WH}(L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{R}))\subset\{F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}):F(-x)=-F(x),x\in\mathbb{C}\}=:L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{C}),

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{\ast}_{\rm WH}(L^{2}_{\rm even}(\mathbb{R}))\subset\{F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}):F(-x)=F(x),x\in\mathbb{C}\}=:L^{2}_{\rm even}(\mathbb{C}).

We consider the restriction of operator

{\cal W}_{\rm WH}\Big|_{L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{C})}:L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{C})\to L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{R}).

and its adjoint

{\cal W}^{\ast}_{\rm WH}\Big|_{L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{R})}:L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{C}).

Then, the kernel of the operator

{\cal W}_{\rm WH}^{*}\Big|_{L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{R})}{\cal W}_{\rm WH}\Big|_{L^{2}_{\rm odd}(\mathbb{C})}

is given by

K_{\rm WH}^{\rm odd}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(K_{\rm WH}(x,x^{\prime})-K_{\rm WH}(x,-x^{\prime})).

Similarly, we have the kernel,

K_{\rm WH}^{\rm even}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(K_{\rm WH}(x,x^{\prime})+K_{\rm WH}(x,-x^{\prime})).

When the window function
G
is given by (
4.4
),
we obtain

\displaystyle K_{\rm WH}^{\rm odd}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\pi ix_{{\rm R}}x_{{\rm I}}}}{e^{\pi ix_{{\rm R}}^{\prime}x_{{\rm I}}^{\prime}}}K^{C}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(\sqrt{\pi}x,\sqrt{\pi}x^{\prime})e^{-\pi(|x|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2})/2},

\displaystyle K_{\rm WH}^{\rm even}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\pi ix_{{\rm R}}x_{{\rm I}}}}{e^{\pi ix_{{\rm R}}^{\prime}x_{{\rm I}}^{\prime}}}K^{D}_{{\rm Ginibre}}(\sqrt{\pi}x,\sqrt{\pi}x^{\prime})e^{-\pi(|x|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2})/2},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C},

where
K^{C}_{{\rm Ginibre}}
and
K^{D}_{{\rm Ginibre}}

are
given by (
4.2
) and (
4.3
),
respectively.

The Ginibre DPP of type A is extended to

Ginibre-type DPPs
indexed by
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
which are introduced in
[
52
]
and
also known as the infinite pure
polyanalytic ensembles
(cf.
[
2
]
).
Each Ginibre-type DPP with index

is associated with the correlation kernel

K^{(q)}_{\mathrm{Ginibre\text{-}type}}(x,x^{\prime}):=L_{q}^{(0)}(|x-x^{\prime}|^{2})K_{\mathrm{Ginibre}}^{A}(x,x^{\prime}),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C},

(4.5)

where

and

(

This DPP can be viewed as

,

using the

Indeed, we see that

See
[
2
]
for more details about the Weyl–Heisenberg
aspect of finite polyanalytic ensembles.
Other examples of the Weyl–Heisenberg ensembles
are given in
[

,

,
2

4.1.3

We consider an application of Corollary

and
S_{2}=\mathbb{N}_{0}

(4.6)

Note that

of the

on
\mathbb{C}

We assume that
\Gamma=S_{2}=\mathbb{N}_{0}

We can see that

\|\varphi_{\cdot}(x)\|_{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|x|^{2n}/n!=e^{|x|^{2}}
,
x\in\mathbb{C}
.
Hence Assumption 3’ is satisfied.
By Corollary
2.10
,
we obtain the DPP on
\mathbb{C}
in which

with respect to
\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1:\mathbb{C})}

\displaystyle K_{\rm BF}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{n}(x^{\prime})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x\overline{x^{\prime}})^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{x\overline{x^{\prime}}},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}.

This is the reproducing kernel in the Bargmann–Fock space
and obtained DPP is identified with

(\Xi,K^{A}_{\rm Ginibre},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx))
.
See
[
52
,
16
,
2
]
.

If we set
\Gamma=2\mathbb{N}_{0}+1=\{1,3,5,\dots\}
or

\Gamma=2\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,2,4,\dots\}
,
we will obtain the DPPs with the following kernels

\displaystyle K_{\rm BF}^{\rm odd}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x\overline{x^{\prime}})^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}=\sinh(x\overline{x^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle K_{\rm BF}^{\rm even}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x\overline{x^{\prime}})^{2k}}{(2k)!}=\cosh(x\overline{x^{\prime}}),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}.

(\Xi
,
K^{C}_{\rm Ginibre}
,
\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx))

and
(\Xi
,
K^{D}_{\rm Ginibre}
,
\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx))
,
respectively.

The correlation kernel of Ginibre-type DPP
(
4.5
) admits the similar representation
in terms of the
complex Hermite polynomials
defined by

H_{p,q}(\zeta,\overline{\zeta}):=(-1)^{p+q}e^{\zeta\overline{\zeta}}\frac{\partial^{p}}{\partial\overline{\zeta}^{p}}\frac{\partial^{q}}{\partial\zeta^{q}}e^{-\zeta\overline{\zeta}},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{C},\quad p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

]

We note that their generating function is given by

\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}H_{p,q}(\zeta,\overline{\zeta})\frac{s^{p}t^{q}}{p!q!}=\exp(\zeta s+\overline{\zeta}t-st)

and the set
\{H_{p,q}(\zeta,\overline{\zeta})/\sqrt{p!q!}:p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
forms a complete orthonormal system of

L^{2}(\mathbb{C},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(d\zeta))

Let
S_{1}=\mathbb{C}
and
S_{2}=\mathbb{N}_{0}
with

\lambda_{1}(dx)=\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx)

forms

to the
q
-th
Landau level
, which coincides with
the Bargmann–Fock space when

.
Since the following formula is known

L_{q}^{(0)}(|\zeta-\eta|^{2})e^{\zeta\overline{\eta}}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p!q!}H_{p,q}(\zeta,\overline{\zeta})\overline{H_{p,q}(\eta,\overline{\eta})},\quad\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb{C},\quad q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

we obtain the following expansion formula for (
4.5
),

K^{(q)}_{\mathrm{Ginibre\text{-}type}}(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi^{(q)}_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi^{(q)}_{n}(x^{\prime})},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C},\quad q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The obtained DPPs are identified with

(\Xi
,
K^{(q)}_{\mathrm{Ginibre\text{-}type}},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx))
,
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

constructed as Weyl–Heisenberg ensembles
in Section
4.1.2
.

4.2

\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
where

denotes the

\mathbb{R}^{3}

We will use the following coordinates for

on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

x^{(1)}=\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\quad x^{(2)}=\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\quad x^{(3)}=\cos\theta,\quad\theta\in[0,\pi],\quad\varphi\in[0,2\pi).

(4.7)

We consider the case that

S_{1}=\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
S_{2}=\mathbb{N}_{0}
, in which
we assume that
\lambda_{1}(dx)

is given by
the
Lebesgue surface area measure

on

For
n\in\{0,1,\dots,N-1\},N\in\mathbb{N}
, put

\varphi_{n}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x)=\varphi_{n}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\theta,\varphi):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{n}}}e^{-in\varphi}\sin^{n}\frac{\theta}{2}\cos^{N-1-n}\frac{\theta}{2},\quad\theta\in[0,\pi],\quad\varphi\in[0,2\pi),

(4.8)

with

h_{n}=h_{n}^{(N)}:=\frac{4\pi}{N}{\binom{N-1}{n}}^{-1}.

It is easy to confirm the following orthonormality relations on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

\langle\varphi_{n}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\cdot),\varphi_{m}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};d\sigma_{2})}=\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\,\varphi_{n}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\theta,\varphi)\overline{\varphi_{m}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\theta,\varphi)}d\sigma_{2}(\theta,\varphi)=\delta_{nm},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We set
\psi_{1}(\cdot,n)=\varphi_{n}^{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\cdot)
,

n\in\Gamma:=\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
By the argument given in Remark 3 in Section

,
we see Assumption 3’ is satisfied.
Then Corollary
2.10
gives
the DPP with
N

\mathbb{S}^{2}

(\Xi,K_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{(N)},d\sigma_{2}(x))

\displaystyle K^{(N)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=K^{(N)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}((\theta,\varphi),(\theta^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{4\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\binom{N-1}{n}\left(e^{-i(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{n}\left(\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{N-1-n}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{4\pi}\left(e^{-i(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}+\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{N-1}.

(4.9)

The density of points with respect to

\rho(x)=K^{(N)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(x,x)=\frac{N}{4\pi}=\mbox{constant},\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

For two points

and

on

,

\displaystyle\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi-\sin\theta^{\prime}\cos\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+(\sin\theta\sin\varphi-\sin\theta^{\prime}\sin\varphi^{\prime})^{2}+(\cos\theta-\cos\theta^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=|\Phi(x-x^{\prime})|^{2},

with

\displaystyle\Phi(x-x^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=2\left[\sin\frac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{2}\cos\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}}{2}-i\sin\frac{\theta+\theta^{\prime}}{2}\sin\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}e^{i(\varphi+\varphi^{\prime})/2}\left[e^{-i\varphi}\tan\frac{\theta}{2}-e^{-i\varphi^{\prime}}\tan\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right].

{\bf p}^{(N)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})=\frac{1}{Z^{(N)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\|x_{k}-x_{j}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{2},

with

Z^{(N)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\frac{2^{N(N+1)}\pi^{N}}{(N!)^{N-1}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}(j-1)!\right)^{2}.

Since
\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}^{2}=2-2x\cdot x^{\prime}

for

, we have the equality

\frac{1}{2}(1+x\cdot x^{\prime})=\left|e^{-i(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\sin\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}+\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\cos\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right|^{2}.

Hence the absolute value of (
4.9

for

\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
following Corollary
2.10
given in
Section
2.5
.

Let
S_{1}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
S_{2}=\Gamma=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(dx)

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{d}\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C})}(dx^{(a)})=\frac{1}{\pi^{d}}e^{-|x|^{2}}=\frac{1}{\pi^{d}}e^{-(|x_{{\rm R}}|^{2}+|x_{{\rm I}}|^{2})}

\displaystyle=:\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C}^{d})}(dx),

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(dy)

\displaystyle=\nu(dy)=\prod_{a=1}^{d}\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1/4)}(dy^{(a)})=\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{d/2}e^{-2|y|^{2}},

and

\psi_{1}(x,y)=e^{-(|x_{{\rm R}}|^{2}-|x_{{\rm I}}|^{2})/2+2(x_{{\rm R}}\cdot y+ix_{{\rm I}}\cdot y)},\quad x=x_{{\rm R}}+ix_{{\rm I}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We see that

\Psi_{1}(x)^{2}:=\|\psi_{1}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\nu)}^{2}=e^{|x|^{2}}
,

x\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Hence Assumption 3’ is satisfied
and then, by Corollary
2.10
,
we obtain the DPP on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
with the correlation kernel,

\displaystyle K^{(d)}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{d/2}e^{-\{(|x_{{\rm R}}|^{2}-|x_{{\rm I}}|^{2})+(|x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}|^{2}-|x^{\prime}_{{\rm I}}|^{2})\}/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2[|y|^{2}-\{(x_{{\rm R}}+ix_{{\rm I}})+(x^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}-ix^{\prime}_{{\rm I}})\}\cdot y]}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ix_{{\rm R}}\cdot x_{{\rm I}}}}{e^{ix^{\prime}_{{\rm R}}\cdot x^{\prime}_{{\rm I}}}}K_{\rm Heisenberg}^{(d)}(x,x^{\prime})

with

K^{(d)}_{\rm Heisenberg}(x,x^{\prime})=e^{x\cdot\overline{x^{\prime}}},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

The kernels in this form on
\mathbb{C}^{d},d\in\mathbb{N}
have been
studied by Zelditch and his coworkers
(see
[

,
14
]
and references therein),
who identified them with the Szegö kernels
for the reduced Heisenberg group
[

,
59
,
24
]
.
Here we call the DPPs associated with

the
Heisenberg family of DPPs
on
\mathbb{C}^{d},d\in\mathbb{N}
.
This family includes the Ginibre DPP of type A as the
lowest dimensional case with
d=1
.

Definition 5.1

The Heisenberg family of DPPs is a one-parameter

(
d\in\mathbb{N}
)
family of

\Big(\Xi,K^{(d)}_{\rm Heisenberg},\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C}^{d})}(dx)\Big)

with

K^{(d)}_{\rm Heisenberg}(x,x^{\prime}):=e^{x\cdot\overline{x^{\prime}}},\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

Since

K^{(d)}_{\rm Heisenberg}(x,x)\lambda_{{\rm N}(0,1;\mathbb{C}^{d})}(dx)=\frac{1}{\pi^{d}}dx,\quad x\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

every DPP in the Heisenberg family
is uniform on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and the density with respect to
the Lebesgue measure
dx
is given by
1/\pi^{d}
.
Hyperuniformity
[
62
]
of the Heisenberg family of DPPs
has been studied in
[
3
,
42
]
.

5.2
Finite DPPs on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

First we recall basic properties of
spherical harmonics on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

[
44
]
.
For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let

{\cal P}={\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
be a vector space of all complex-valued
polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
and
{\cal P}_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, be its subspaces
consisting of homogeneous polynomials of degree
k
;

p(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
,

c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C},x=(x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(d+1)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
where we have used the notations

x^{\alpha}:=\prod_{a=1}^{d+1}(x^{(a)})^{\alpha_{a}}

with
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d+1})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}
,

|\alpha|:=\sum_{a=1}^{d+1}\alpha_{a}
.
The vector space of all harmonic functions in
{\cal P}
is
denoted by
{\cal H}=\{p\in{\cal P}:\Delta p=0\}

and let
{\cal H}_{k}={\cal H}\cap{\cal P}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Now we consider a unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
denoted by

\mathbb{S}^{d}
, in which we use the polar coordinates
for
x=(x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(d+1)})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

\displaystyle x^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sin\theta_{d}\cdots\sin\theta_{2}\sin\theta_{1},

\displaystyle x^{(a)}

\displaystyle=\sin\theta_{d}\cdots\sin\theta_{a}\cos\theta_{a-1},\quad a=2,\dots,d,

\displaystyle x^{(d+1)}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta_{d},\quad\mbox{with $\theta_{1}\in[0,2\pi),\quad\theta_{a}\in[0,\pi],\quad a=2,\dots,d$}.

(5.1)

Note that
\|x\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}:=\sum_{a=1}^{d+1}{x^{(a)}}^{2}=1
.
For
d=2
, if we put

\theta_{1}=\pi/2-\varphi
and
\theta_{2}=\theta
,
the polar coordinates (
4.7
)
used in Section
4.2
are obtained.
The standard measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is given
by the
Lebesgue area measure
expressed as

d\sigma_{d}(x)=\sin^{d-1}\theta_{d}\sin^{d-2}\theta_{d-1}\cdots\sin\theta_{2}d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{d},\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}.

(5.2)

The total measure of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is calculated as

\omega_{d}:=\sigma_{d}(\mathbb{S}^{d})=\frac{2\pi^{(d+1)/2}}{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}.

(5.3)

We write the space of harmonic polynomials in
{\cal H}_{k}

restricted on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
as

{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}=\left\{h\Big|_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}:h\in{\cal H}_{k}\right\},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We can see that

D(d,k):=\dim{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}=\frac{(d+2k-1)(d+k-2)!}{(d-1)!k!}=\frac{2}{(d-1)!}k^{d-1}+{\rm o}(k^{d-1}).

(5.4)

Consider an orthonormal basis

\{Y^{(d,k)}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{D(d,k)}
of
{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}

with respect to
d\sigma_{d}
;

\langle Y^{(d,k)}_{n},Y^{(d,k)}_{m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d},d\sigma_{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}Y^{(d,k)}_{n}(x)\overline{Y^{(d,k)}_{m}(x)}d\sigma_{d}(x)=\delta_{nm},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(5.5)

Then, if we put

K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{D(d,k)}Y^{(d,k)}_{j}(x)\overline{Y^{(d,k)}_{j}(x^{\prime})},\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

then
\{K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})\}_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}

give the reproducing kernel in
{\cal Y}^{(d,k)}
in the sense that

Y(x^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}Y(x)\overline{K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})}d\sigma_{d}(x),\quad\forall Y\in{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}.

For
\lambda>-1/2
, we define

P^{\lambda}_{k}(x):={{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(-k,k+2\lambda;\lambda+\frac{1}{2};\frac{1-x}{2}\right),

where
{{}_{2}}F_{1}
denotes the
Gauss hypergeometric function
,

{{}_{2}}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma;z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\{(\alpha)_{n}(\beta)_{n}/\gamma)_{n}\}z^{n}/n!
,
with

(\alpha)_{n}:=\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+n-1)=\Gamma(\alpha+n)/\Gamma(\alpha)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\alpha)_{0}:=1
.
Then the following equality is established,

K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{D(d,k)}{\omega_{d}}P^{(d-1)/2}_{k}(x\cdot x^{\prime}),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

where
\omega_{d}
and
D(d,k)
are
given by (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), respectively,
and
x\cdot x^{\prime}:=\sum_{a=1}^{d+1}x^{(a)}{x^{\prime}}^{(a)}
.

We see that
K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})
is
{\rm O}(d+1,\mathbb{R})
-invariant in the sense

K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(gx,gx^{\prime})=K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime}),\quad\forall g\in{\rm O}(d+1,\mathbb{R}),\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d+1}\}
be the standard basis of

\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

and
L_{0}
be
the stabilizer subgroup of

{\rm SO}(d+1,\mathbb{R})
at
e_{d+1}
represented as

L_{0}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}A&{\bf 0}\cr{{}^{t}{\bf 0}}&1\end{array}\right):A\in{\rm SO}(d,\mathbb{R})\right\}.

We define

{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}^{L_{0}}=\{Y\in{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}:Y(\ell x)=Y(x),\quad\forall\ell\in L_{0},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\},

Then
K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}\in{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}^{L_{0}}
.
The function
P^{\lambda}_{k}(s)
is called
the
ultraspherical polynomial

[
45
]
.
The space
{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}^{L_{0}}
is a one-dimensional vector space
generated by
P_{k}^{(d-1)/2}(x\cdot e_{d+1})
.
In general, any
L_{0}
-invariant function is a constant
for each
L_{0}
-orbit,

{\cal O}=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:x\cdot e_{d+1}=a\},-1\leq a\leq 1
,
and hence functions in
{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}^{L_{0}}
is called
the
zonal harmonics
of degree
k
.
Note that, when we set

C_{k}^{\lambda}(x):=\binom{k+2\lambda-1}{k}P^{\lambda}_{k}(x),

we call
C_{k}^{\lambda}(s)

the
Gegenbauer polynomial
of degree
k

[
45
]
.

Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then, if we consider the case that

S_{1}=\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

S_{2}=\mathbb{N}
with

\lambda_{1}(dx)=d\sigma_{d}(x)
,

L^{2}(\Gamma,\nu)=\ell^{2}(\{1,\dots,D(d,k)\})\subset S_{2}
,
and
\psi_{1}(x,n)=Y^{(d,k)}_{n}(x)
,
then (
5.5
)
with Remark 3 in Section
3.1.1

guarantees Assumption 3’.
Hence Corollary
2.10
determines
a unique DPP on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, in which
the correlation kernel is given by
[
13
]

\displaystyle K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{D(d,k)}{\omega_{d}}P^{(d-1)/2}_{k}(x\cdot x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{d-1+2k}{(d-1)\omega_{d}}C^{(d-1)/2}_{k}(x\cdot x^{\prime}).

It is obvious that the obtained DPP is
rotationally invariant on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, since the kernel

K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})
depend only on the inner product
x\cdot x^{\prime}
.
The density of points is uniform on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

and is given with respect to
\sigma_{d}(dx)
by

\displaystyle\rho^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}

\displaystyle=K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x)=\frac{D(d,k)}{\omega_{d}}P^{(d-1)/2}_{k}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{D(d,k)}{\omega_{d}}=\frac{2k^{d-1}}{(d-1)!\omega_{d}}+{\rm o}(k^{d-1}),

where we have used the fact that

P^{\lambda}_{k}(1)={{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(-k,k+2\lambda,\lambda+1/2;0\right)=1,\lambda>-1/2

[
45
]
.

Next we consider the DPP on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
for
fixed
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that
the correlation kernel is given
by the following finite sum
[
13
]
,

\displaystyle K^{(N(d,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}K^{{\cal Y}_{(d,k)}}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\omega_{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}D(d,k)P^{(d-1)/2}_{k}(x\cdot x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\frac{d-1+2k}{d-1}C^{(d-1)/2}_{k}(x\cdot x^{\prime}),

(5.6)

where the total number of points on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

is given by

\displaystyle N(d,L)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}D(d,k)=\frac{2L+d-2}{d}\binom{d+L-2}{L-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{d!}L^{d}+{\rm o}(L^{d}).

(5.7)

The DPP
(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},d\sigma_{d}(x))

is rotationally invariant in
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
is called the
harmonic ensemble

in
\mathbb{S}^{d}
with
N
points by Beltrán
et al.

[
13
]
.
We note the recurrence relation of the Gegenbauer polynomials
(see, Eq.(18.9.7) in
[
45
]
),

(n+\lambda)C^{\lambda}_{n}(x)=\lambda(C^{\lambda+1}_{n}(x)-C^{\lambda+1}_{n-2}(x)).

This implies that

\frac{d-1+2k}{d-1}C^{(d-1)/2}_{k}(x)=C^{(d+1)/2}_{k}(x)-C^{(d+1)/2}_{k-2}(x),\quad k\geq 2.

Since
C^{\lambda}_{0}(x)=1,C^{\lambda}_{1}(x)=2\lambda x
,
we obtain the following expression for the
correlation kernel,

K^{(N(d,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\omega_{d}}\left[C^{(d+1)/2}_{L-1}(x\cdot x^{\prime})+C^{(d+1)/2}_{L-2}(x\cdot x^{\prime})\right].

If we introduce the
Jacobi polynomials

defined as
[
45
]

P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x):=\frac{(\alpha+1)_{n}}{n!}{{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(-n,n+\alpha+\beta+1;\alpha+1;\frac{1-x}{2}\right),

and use the contiguous relation,

(b-a)\,{{}_{2}}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)+a\,{{}_{2}}F_{1}(a+1,b;c;z)-b\,{{}_{2}}F_{1}(a,b+1;c;z)=0
,
the above is written as follows
[
13
]
,

K^{(N(d,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\omega_{d}}\frac{N(d,L)}{\binom{L+d/2-1}{L-1}}P^{(d/2,(d-2)/2)}_{L-1}(x\cdot x^{\prime}),

(5.8)

where
\binom{L+d/2-1}{L-1}:=\Gamma(L+d/2)/\{(L-1)!\Gamma(d/2+1)\}=P^{(d/2,(d-2)/2)}_{L-1}(1)
.

In particular, when
d=1
, for

x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)
,

x^{\prime}=({x^{\prime}}^{(1)},{x^{\prime}}^{(2)})=(\sin\theta^{\prime},\cos\theta^{\prime})\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi)
, we have

x\cdot x^{\prime}=\cos(\theta-\theta^{\prime})

and

\displaystyle K^{(N(1,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}(x,x^{\prime})d\sigma_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}{{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-(2L-1)}{2},\frac{1+(2L-1)}{2};\frac{3}{2};\sin^{2}\frac{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}{2}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\{(2L-1)(\theta-\theta^{\prime})/2\}}{\sin\{(\theta-\theta^{\prime})/2\}}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\{N(\theta-\theta^{\prime})/2\}}{\sin\{(\theta-\theta^{\prime})/2\}}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},

(5.9)

where we have used the fact that

N(1,L)=2L-1
given by (
5.7
).
This verifies the identification of the 1-sphere case
of the present DPP with the CUE,

(\Xi,K^{{\it A}_{N-1}},\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(d\theta))
,
given in Section
3.2
.

On the other hand, when
d=2
,
(
5.7
) gives

N(2,L)=L^{2}
and

\displaystyle K^{(N(2,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{2}}{4\pi}{{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(-L+1,L+1;2;\frac{1-x\cdot x^{\prime}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{4\pi}{{}_{2}}F_{1}\left(-\sqrt{N}+1,\sqrt{N}+1;2;\frac{\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}{4}\right),

which is different from
K_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}^{(N)}(x,x^{\prime})

given by (
4.9
) in Section
4.2
.

5.3
Euclidean family of infinite DPPs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

We consider the vicinity of the north pole
e_{d+1}=(0,\dots,0,1)

on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

and put
\theta_{d}=R/L
,
R\in[0,\infty)
.
Then, as
L\to\infty
,
the polar coordinates (
5.1
) behave as

\displaystyle x^{(1)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{R}{L}\sin\theta_{d-1}\cdots\sin\theta_{2}\sin\theta_{1}=:\frac{1}{L}\widetilde{x}^{(1)},

\displaystyle x^{(a)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{R}{L}\sin\theta_{d-1}\cdots\sin\theta_{a}\cos\theta_{a-1}=:\frac{1}{L}\widetilde{x}^{(a)},\quad a=2,\dots,d,

\displaystyle x^{(d+1)}

\displaystyle\sim 1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R}{L}\right)^{2}.

In this case, for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

x\cdot x^{\prime}=\sum_{a=1}^{d+1}x^{(a)}{x^{\prime}}^{(a)}=1-\frac{1}{2L^{2}}\|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{x}^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}+{\rm o}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right),\quad\mbox{as $L\to\infty$},

where
\widetilde{x},\widetilde{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence we can conclude that

x\cdot x^{\prime}=\cos\left(\frac{r}{L}\right)+{\rm o}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right),\quad\mbox{with $r:=\|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{x}^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}$},\quad\mbox{ as $L\to\infty$}.

(5.10)

In this limit, the measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

given by (
5.2
) behaves as

\displaystyle d\sigma_{d}(x)

\displaystyle\sim\left(\frac{R}{L}\right)^{d-1}\sin^{d-2}\theta_{d-1}\cdots\sin\theta_{2}\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{d-1}\frac{dR}{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{d}}d\sigma_{d-1}(\widehat{x})R^{d-1}dR=\frac{1}{L^{d}}d\widetilde{x},\quad\widehat{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\quad\widetilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The following limit is proved for the correlation kernel

K^{(N(d,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
given by (
5.6
).

Lemma 5.2

When
(
5.10
)
holds,
the limit

k^{(d)}(r)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{d}}K^{(N(d,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}(x,x^{\prime})

exists and has the following expressions,

\displaystyle k^{(d)}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{d/2}(r)}{(2\pi r)^{d/2}}

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}r^{(d-2)/2}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{d/2}J_{(d-2)/2}(rs)ds,

(5.12)

where
J_{\nu}(z)
is
the Bessel function of the first kind
with index
\nu
defined by

(
3.23
)
.

Proof

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
,
the following formula of Mehler–Heine type
is known (Theorem 8.1.1 in
[
61
]
);
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-\alpha}P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}\left(\cos\frac{r}{n}\right)=\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(r),

where the limit is uniform on compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
.
Then under (
5.10
), (
5.8
) gives

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{d}}K^{(N(d,L))}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{2}{\omega_{d}d!}\Gamma(d/2+1)\left(\frac{2}{r}\right)^{d/2}J_{d/2}(r).

By (
5.3
) and the equality

\Gamma(2z)=\{2^{2z}/(2\sqrt{\pi})\}\Gamma(z)\Gamma\left(z+1/2\right)
,
we can confirm that

2\Gamma(d/2+1)/(\omega_{d}d!)=1/\{(2\sqrt{\pi})^{d}\}
.
Hence (
5.11
) is proved.
By the integral formula (see, for instance, Eq.(10.22.1) in
[
45
]
),

\int z^{\nu+1}J_{\nu}(z)dz=z^{\nu+1}J_{\nu+1}(z),

we can derive (
5.12
) from (
5.11
).
The proof is complete. ∎

we obtain an infinite-dimensional DPP on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that it is uniform and isotropic on

and

K^{(d)}(x,x^{\prime})=k^{(d)}(\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(5.13)

where

is given by (

) and (

).

We can give the following alternative expression
for
K^{(d)}
.

Lemma 5.3

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the correlation kernel

given by
(
5.13
)

with

)

K^{(d)}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf 1}_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}(y)e^{i(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}dy=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}e^{i(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}dy,

\mathbb{B}^{d}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y|\leq 1\}
.

Proof

The statement is proved for

and 2 by direct calculation as follows.
For

k^{(1)}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{r}{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{1/2}J_{-1/2}(rs)ds.

Then

which gives (

) with

,
if we put

as
\mathbb{B}^{1}
.
For
d=2
, (
5.12
) gives

k^{(2)}(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}sJ_{0}(rs)ds.

(5.15)

We use the following integral representation for

J_{0}
given as Eq.(10.9.1) in
[
45
]

J_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(z\cos\varphi)d\varphi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{iz\cos\varphi}d\varphi.

Hence (

k^{(2)}(r)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}ds\,s\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\,e^{irs\cos\theta}.

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}ds\,s^{d-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta_{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta_{2}\,\sin\theta_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\cdots\times\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta_{d-2}\sin^{d-3}\theta_{d-2}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta_{d-1}\sin^{d-2}\theta_{d-1}e^{irs\cos\theta_{d-1}}.

\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta_{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta_{2}\,\sin\theta_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta_{d-2}\sin^{d-3}\theta_{d-2}=\sigma_{d-2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-2})=\omega_{d-2},

If we use the following integral representation

Definition 5.4

The Euclidean family of DPPs is
a one-parameter

\displaystyle K^{(d)}_{\rm Euclid}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{J_{d/2}(\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}})}{\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{d/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{1}{\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{(d-2)/2}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{d/2}J_{(d-2)/2}(\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf 1}_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}(y)e^{i(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}dy=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}e^{i(x-x^{\prime})\cdot y}dy,\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

]

Proposition 5.5

The following is established for

\left(\frac{d!}{2}\right)^{1/d}N^{1/d}\circ\Big(\Xi,K^{(N)}_{{\rm harmonic}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},d\sigma_{d}(x)\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N\to\infty}}{{\,\Longrightarrow\,}}\Big(\Xi,K^{(d)}_{\rm Euclid},dx\Big).

We see that

K^{(d)}_{\rm Euclid}(x,x)=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{J_{d/2}(r)}{r^{d/2}}=\frac{1}{2^{d}\pi^{d/2}\Gamma((d+2)/2)}.

\mathbb{R}^{d}

For lower dimensions, the correlation kernels

\displaystyle K^{(1)}_{\rm Euclid}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x-x^{\prime})}{\pi(x-x^{\prime})}=K_{\rm sinc}(x,x^{\prime})\quad\mbox{with}\quad\rho^{(1)}_{\rm Euclid}=\frac{1}{\pi},

\displaystyle K^{(2)}_{\rm Euclid}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle K^{(3)}_{\rm Euclid}(x,x^{\prime})

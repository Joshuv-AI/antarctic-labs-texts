A note on modified J J -Flow with the Calabi Ansatz
P. Sivaram

Abstract
. We study the modified J J -flow introduced in [ 15 ] , particularly the singularities of the flow using the Calabi symmetry. In [ 20 ] , on toric manifolds the convergence of modified J J -flow to the smooth solution was proven under the assumption of positivity of certain intersection numbers. In the case of the Calabi ansatz we show that if some of those intersection numbers are not positive, then the modified J J -flow blows up along some variety and away from the variety we prove the convergence to the solution. As in [ 10 ] , we also prove that the convergence behavior of the modified J J -flow with Calabi symmetry depends on the topological constants c c and the minimum of the Hamiltonian function.



1.
Introduction

There has been considerable progress in recent years on solvability of inverse Hessian equations going beyond the more classically studied complex Monge-Ampere equations. An equation that has been of particular interest, in part due to its relevance to constructing constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) metrics, is the
J
-equation. The
J
-equation on Kähler manifolds was introduced by Donaldson
[
9
]
in the moment map setting and by X. Chen
[
3
]
as the Euler-Lagrange equation for a functional that appears in his formula for the Mabuchi functional. Let
(X,\omega)
be a Kähler manifolds, and let
\chi
be another Kähler metric. The
J
-equation seeks a Kähler metric
\chi_{\varphi}:=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi
in the class of
[\chi]
satisfying

\Lambda_{\chi_{\varphi}}\omega:=n\frac{\omega\wedge\chi_{\varphi}^{n-1}}{\chi_{\varphi}^{n}}=c,

where
c
is necessarily given by

c=n\frac{[\chi]^{n-1}\cdot[\omega]}{[\chi]^{n}}.

A consequence of Chen’s formula for the Mabuchi energy (cf.
[
17
]
) is that solvability of the
J
-equation in the class
[\chi]=c_{1}(K_{M})
implies existence of constant scalar curvature metrics.

In
[
4
]
, X. Chen introduced a natural flow, the so-called
J
-flow, to study existence of solutions to the
J
-equation and proved it’s long-time existence and convergence under a bi-sectional curvature lower bound. The
J
-flow is defined as follows:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=c-\Lambda_{\chi_{\varphi}}\omega\\
\varphi_{t}|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{H},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{H}=\{\varphi:\chi_{\varphi}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi>0\}.
Note that on Kähler surfaces, the
J
-equation reduces to a complex Monge-Ampere equation, and hence by Yau’s celebrated resolution of the Calabi conjecture (cf.
[
23
]
), a solution exists if and only if
[c\chi-\omega]
is a Kähler class. More generally, in
[
17
]
, Song and Weinkove proved that a solution to the
J
-equation in the class
[\chi]
exists if and only if there exists a
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
such that

c\chi_{\varphi}^{n-1}-(n-1)\omega\wedge\chi_{\varphi}^{n-2}>0.

The interested readers can refer to
[
21
,
22
]
for earlier results, and
[
18
]
for an extension of this theorem to more general inverse Hessian equations. While this is an optimal PDE result, the condition above (called the cone condition) is a
pointwise
condition, and is generally difficult to verify. Inspired by the work of Demailly and Paun
[
8
]
and the close analogy between complex Monge-Ampere equations and the
J
-equation, Lejmi and Szekelyhidi
[
13
]
conjectured that a solution to the
J
-equation exists if and only if the following Nakai type criteria holds:

\int_{V}(c\chi^{p}-p\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1})>0,

for any
p
-dimensional sub-variety
V
of the manifold. Note that the above condition is a numerical or topological condition, and at least in principle, much easier to verify. A uniform version of this conjecture was proved by G. Chen in
[
2
]
. Based on the work of Chen, the full Lejmi-Szekelyhidi conjecture was established by Datar-Pingali
[
7
]
on projective manifolds and Song
[
16
]
for general Kähler manifolds.

In analogy with the role of the
J
-equation in studying the Mabuchi functional, the modified
J
-equation was introduced by Li-Shi
[
15
]
to study the modified Mabuchi functional. The setting is as follows: Suppose now, that
\omega
and
\chi
are invariant under the action of a real torus
T\subset Aut_{0}(X)
. Let

\mathcal{H}^{T}=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})^{T}:\chi_{\varphi}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi>0\}

be the
T
-invariant Kähler potentials of
\chi.
Let
\xi
be a holomorphic vector field with
Im(\xi)\in\mathfrak{t}.
We define the Hamiltonian function of
\xi
with respect to the metric
\chi
as the real valued function uniquely determined by the following properties:

i_{\xi}\chi=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\overline{\partial}\theta_{\xi}(\chi)\text{ and }\int_{X}\theta_{\xi}(\chi)\chi^{n}=0.

The modified
J
-equation is defined as

(1.2)

n\frac{\omega\wedge\chi_{\varphi}^{n-1}}{\chi_{\varphi}^{n}}=c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\varphi}),

where
c
is as before. Note that the above equation reduces to the
J
-equation if
T
is trivial.
In
[
15
]
Li-Shi proved that if there exist a
\hat{\chi}\in[\chi]
such that

(c+\theta_{\xi}(\hat{\chi}))\hat{\chi}^{n-1}-(n-1)\omega\wedge\hat{\chi}^{n-2}>0,

then there exist a unique
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{T}
which satisfies the modified
J
-equation (
1.2
). They proved the result using the parabolic flow method by defining the modified
J
-flow by adapting the arguments in
[
17
]
.The modified
J
flow is defined as

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\varphi})-n\frac{\omega\wedge\chi_{\varphi}^{n-1}}{\chi_{\varphi}^{n}}\\
\varphi_{t}|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{T}.\end{cases}

In
[
20
]
Takahshi conjectured that the modified
J
-equation has solution in the class
[\chi]
if and only if
c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi)>0
and the following Nakai criteria holds: For all
p
-dimensional toric sub-varieties
V\subset M
,

\int_{V}((c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))\chi^{p}-p\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1})>0,

and verified this conjecture for toric manifolds (cf.
[
5
]
for an analogous result for the
J
-equation).

The main goal of this note is to study the behaviour of the modified
J
-flow on the blow-ups
X_{n}=\mathrm{Bl}_{x_{0}}\mathbb{P}^{n}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. By Takahashi’s result, the existence problem is completely settled, and so we focus instead on the case when the Nakai criteria
fails
. These are also examples of ruled surfaces. Indeed
X_{n}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O})
, and one can study the modified
J
-flow using the Calabi ansatz. For the
J
-flow, Fang and Lai obtained a complete description of the behaviour of the
J
-flow on these manifolds using the Calabi ansatz in
[
10
]
. In particular, that even when the Nakai criteria fails, the flow still converges on the complement of the exceptional divisor
E
to a solution of the
J
-equation on
X_{n}\setminus E
with a different slope. Our main goal is to extend these results to the modified
J
-flow on these manifolds.

To state our main result, we introduce some notation. Given real numbers
a,b>1
and
k\geq 0
, we let

c_{k}=n\frac{ab^{n-1}-1}{b^{n}-1}-\frac{nk}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-1}{b^{n}-1}.

When
k\geq 0
, we characterise the behaviour of the modified
J
flow using the constant
c_{k}+k
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X_{n}=\mathbb{P}^{n}\#\overline{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
at one point with two Kähler metrics
\omega\in a[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
and
\chi\in b[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
, where
E_{0}
and
E_{\infty}
be the exceptional divisor and the pull-back of the divisor associated to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
respectively and
\xi=kw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}
,where
w
is the fiberwise coordinate of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)
and
k\geq 0
. And let
\chi_{t}
be the solution of the modified
J
-flow.
Then the following three cases characterize the convergence behavior of the modified
J
-flow:

(1)

If
c_{k}+k>n-1
, then
\chi_{t}\rightarrow\chi_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty
on
X_{n}
smoothly and
\chi_{\infty}
is the solution of the modified
J
equation.

(2)

If
c_{k}+k=n-1,
then
\chi_{t}\rightarrow\chi_{\infty}
as
t\to\infty
on
X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0}
smoothly, where
\chi_{\infty}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi,\text{ for some }\varphi\in L^{\infty}(X_{n}),
a singular Kähler metric has a conical singularity along
E_{0}
and smooth everywhere else. And the flow converges to the solution of the equation

n\frac{\omega\wedge\chi_{\infty}^{n-1}}{\chi_{\infty}^{n}}=c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\infty}),\text{ on }X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0}.

(3)

If
0<c_{k}+k<n-1
, then
\chi_{t}\to\hat{\chi}_{\infty}+(\lambda-1)[E_{0}]
, a Kähler current as
t\to\infty
on
X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0}
smoothly, where
\lambda\in(1,b)
is unique such that

n\frac{ab^{n-1}-\lambda^{n-1}}{b^{n}-\lambda^{n}}-\frac{nk}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-\lambda^{n+1}}{b^{n}-\lambda^{n}}+k\lambda=\frac{n-1}{\lambda}.

And the flow converges to the solution of the equation

n\frac{\omega\wedge\hat{\chi}_{\infty}^{n-1}}{\hat{\chi}_{\infty}^{n}}=n\frac{ab^{n-1}-\lambda^{n-1}}{b^{n}-\lambda^{n}}+\theta_{\xi}(\hat{\chi}_{\infty}),\text{ on }X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0}.

To conclude this section, we make a few remarks on some ongoing work, and possible future directions. Firstly we note that in a work in progress, we introduce a notion of Futaki invariant for the modified
J
-equation, and prove a Atiyah-Bott type lower bound for an
L^{2}
-energy in terms of the Futaki invariant, obtaining a modified
J
-equation analogue of a similar result due to Lejmi and Szekelyhidi. As in
[
13
]
, using the above convergence result, we can improve the lower bound to an equality in the case of the manifolds
X_{n}
. In another direction, one can also obtain a similar convergence result for more general projective bundles where the zero section may have a higher co-dimension. In
[
6
]
, the authors develop a program for obtaining weak solutions to the
J
-equation even when the Nakai criteria fails, and obtain weak solutions on Kähler surfaces. It would be interesting to develop an analogous program for the modified
J
-equation. Unlike in the case of the
J
-equation, finding smooth solutions to the modified equation on Kähler surfaces is already non-trivial (cf.
[
15
]
). In light of this, one would expect additional difficulties in adapting the arguments in
[
6
]
to the modified setting.

2.
Modified
J
-flow with the Calabi Ansatz

2.1.
Modified
J
-equation with the Calabi Ansatz:

We first review the ansatz of Calabi to construct Kähler metrics on certain ruled surfaces. The interested reader can refer to
[
1
,
12
,
19
]
for more details. Let
X_{n}
denote blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
at one point. Equivalently,
X_{n}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O})
. Let
h
be a metric on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-1)
with curvature
-\sqrt{-1}2\pi\omega_{FS}
, and write
s=\log|.|_{h}
for the log of the fiberwise norm. Denote by
[E_{0}]
and
[E_{\infty}]
the zero section (exceptional divisor) and the infinity section respectively. The Kähler cone is then given by

\mathcal{K}_{X_{n}}=\{\beta[E_{\infty}]-\alpha[E_{0}]:\beta>\alpha>0\}.

For an appropriate choice of strictly increasing convex function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
we can write down a Kähler metric

\omega=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f(s)

on
X_{n}\smallsetminus(E_{0}\cup E_{\infty})\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}.
At a point choose local coordinates
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-1})
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
and a fiberwise coordinate
w
such that
d\log h(z)=0.
At this point we then have that

\omega=\sqrt{-1}f^{\prime}(s)\omega_{FS}+f^{\prime\prime}(s)\frac{\sqrt{-1}dw\wedge d\bar{w}}{|w|^{2}}.

In order to extend
\omega
to a smooth Kähler metric on
X_{n}
, the following asymptotic properties of
f
are required:

(i)

F_{0}(t):=f(\log t)-\alpha\log t
extends to a smooth function at
t=0
, and
F_{0}^{\prime}(0)>0.

(ii)

F_{\infty}(t):=f(-\log t)+\beta\log t
extends to a smooth function at
t=0
, and
F_{\infty}^{\prime}(0)>0.

By the asymptotic behavior of
f,
we have

\lim_{s\to-\infty}f^{\prime}(s)=\alpha,\lim_{s\to\infty}f^{\prime}(s)=\beta,

and the Kähler class of
\omega
is then given by
\beta[E_{\infty}]-\alpha[E_{0}].

We can repeat this construction for a different convex function
g\in\textbf{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and consider

\chi=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}g(s).

We normalize the Kähler classes so that

\omega\in a[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}],\chi\in b[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}];\quad a,b>1.

We now write down the modified
J
-equation using Calabi ansatz.
We introduce a moment map coordinate
\tau=f^{\prime}(s),
and define the strictly increasing function
\psi\colon[1,b]\to[1,a]
by letting

\psi(f^{\prime}(s))=g^{\prime}(s),\forall~s\in\mathbb{R}.

We consider the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
action on
X_{n}
of the form

e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\cdot(z,w)=(z,e^{\sqrt{-1}k\theta}w).

The vector field generating the action is given by
\xi=kw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}.
Since

i_{\xi}\chi=i_{\xi}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(s)\frac{\sqrt{-1}dw\wedge d\bar{w}}{|w|^{2}}\right)=\sqrt{-1}~\overline{\partial}(kf^{\prime}(s)),

the normalized Hamiltonian function in the momentum coordinate is

\theta_{\xi}(\chi)=k\left(\tau-\frac{n}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-1}{b^{n}-1}\right)

then the modified
J
equation can be written as

(2.1)

\psi^{\prime}(\tau)+(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau)}{\tau}=c_{k}+k\tau,

where

c_{k}=c-\frac{kn}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-1}{b^{n}-1}\text{ and }c=n\frac{ab^{n-1}-1}{b^{n}-1}.

A solution must be strictly increasing and also satisfy the boundary conditions
\psi(1)=1
and
\psi(b)=a.
If we take the derivative of the equation (
2.1
) with respect to
\tau
then we get a second order ODE associated to the modified
J
equation

(2.2)

\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\tau)}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau)}{\tau^{2}}-k=0.

With the boundary conditions
\psi(1)=1
and
\psi(b)=a,
we obtain a unique solution

(2.3)

\tilde{\psi}(\tau)=\frac{k\tau^{2}}{n+1}+\frac{c_{k}}{n}\tau+\left(\frac{n}{n+1}-\frac{c_{k}}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\tau^{n-1}}.

We will use the equation (
2.2
) to define the modified
J
-flow and we also see that the presence of the quadratic term in the solution (
2.3
) makes the analysis of the equation different from the
J
-flow ODE. Note that if we take
k=0
, then we recover the
J
-equation.

From now onwards we assume that
k\geq 0
.

Remark 2.1
.

(a)

For the
J
-equation with the Calabi ansatz, solvability of the equation depends on the topological constant
c=n\frac{[\omega]\cdot[\chi]^{n-1}}{[\chi]^{n}}
. Analogous to that for the modified
J
-equation with Calabi ansatz, solvability depends on the minimum of the normalized hamiltonian and the constant
c
, that is we get a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a smooth solution to the modified
J
-equation from

\int_{E_{0}}\left((c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))\chi^{n-1}-(n-1)\omega\wedge\chi^{n-2}\right)=c_{k}+k-(n-1)>0

and

c_{k}+k=\min_{X_{n}}\big(c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi)\big).

(b)

By Lemma 2.1 of
[
20
]
, the constant
\min_{X_{n}}(c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))
is independent of the metric
\chi
we pick from the class
[\chi]
in general.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X_{n}
be a blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
at one point and
\omega\in a[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
and
\chi\in b[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
to be two Kähler classes. If
c_{k}+k>n-1,
then there exist a solution to the modified
J
-equation (
2.2
).

Proof.

The solution of the ODE (
2.2
) is given by

(2.4)

\psi(\tau)=\frac{k\tau^{2}}{n+1}+\frac{c_{k}}{n}\tau+\left(\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}-\frac{c_{k}}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\tau^{n-1}}.

Given that
c_{k}>n-(k+1).
Then

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{2k\tau}{n+1}+\frac{c_{k}}{n}-(n-1)\left(\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}-\frac{c_{k}}{n}\right)\frac{1}{\tau^{n}}

\displaystyle>\frac{2k}{n+1}+\frac{n-(k+1)}{n}-(n-1)\left(\frac{n+k+1}{n(n+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

This implies that
\psi
has an inverse. So,
f^{\prime}(s)=\psi^{-1}(g^{\prime}(s)),\text{ for all }s\in\mathbb{R}.
Since
f
satisfies the properties of the Calabi ansatz we can get a metric which solves the modified
J
-equation (
1.2
).
∎

As a consequence we have the following rotationally symmetric version of Conjecture 1.4 of
[
20
]
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
X_{n}
be the blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
at one point and
\omega\in a[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
and
\chi\in b[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
to be two Kähler classes. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

The modified
J
-equation (
1.2
) has a solution.

(2)

c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi)>0
and for each
p\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
and
p
-dimensional irreducible sub-variety
V^{p}\subset X
,

\int_{V^{p}}\left((c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))\chi^{p}-p\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1}\right)>0.

(3)

c_{k}+k>n-1.

Proof.

For
(1)
implies
(2)
, by assumption there exist
\chi\in b[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
which solves the modified
J
-equation (
1.2
)

n\frac{\omega\wedge\chi^{n-1}}{\chi^{n}}=c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi).

Let us choose a coordinate at a point
y\in X_{n}
such that
\omega_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
and
\chi_{ij}=\mu_{i}\delta_{ij},\mu_{i}>0\text{ for }1\leq i,j\leq n.

At this point the modified
J
-equation will be of the form

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}=c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi).

This implies that
c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi)>0
, since
y\in X_{n}
was arbitrary and for any
\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{p}\}\subset\{1,2,\dots,n\}
we have

\sum_{r=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\mu_{i_{r}}}<c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi)

So
(c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))\chi^{p}-(p-1)\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1}>0.

And if we integrate the form
(c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))\chi^{p}-(p-1)\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1}
over any
p
-dimensional irreducible sub-variety
V^{p}
we get

\int_{V^{p}}(c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))\chi^{p}-(p-1)\omega\wedge\chi^{p-1}>0.

For
(2)
implies
(3)
, we have
[E_{\infty}]|_{E_{0}}\equiv 0
and
[E_{0}]|_{E_{0}}\equiv-\omega_{FS}
and we can take
V^{n-1}=E_{0}.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{E_{0}}\left((c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi))\chi^{n-1}-(n-1)\omega\wedge\chi^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{0}}\left((c_{k}+k)\chi^{n-1}-(n-1)\omega\wedge\chi^{n-2}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{k}+k-(n-1).

Thus
c_{k}+k>n-1
follows from the given hypothesis.
And
(3)
implies
(1)
follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

(a)

An important point to note here is that for the
J
-equation if
\omega
and
\chi
belong to the same Kähler class, then we can always solve the
J
-equation. But this is not the case for the modified
J
-equation.
For example on
X_{2}
with
k=1
, there is no
\omega,\chi\in 3[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
such that

2\frac{\omega\wedge\chi}{\chi^{2}}=2+\theta_{\xi}(\chi).

Since
c_{1}=\frac{-1}{6}<0
in this case.
Also consider
\omega\in 7[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
and
\chi\in 6[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
in the blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at a point. Then
c_{1}<0.
Thus the modified
J
-equation can not have a solution in
[\chi]
. Hence
a\geq b
does not guarantee the solution for the modified
J
equation, unlike the
J
-equation.

(b)

If
(\omega,\chi)
is
J
-unstable, that is
c<n-1
, then we can not have solution for the modified
J
-equation on that particular pair for any
k\geq 0
, since

k\left(1-\frac{n}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-1}{b^{n}-1}\right)<0.

(c)

When
k\geq 0,
we have
c_{k}+k>n-1
as a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of the solution of the modified
J
-equation. This provides the upper bound on the value of
k
, that is

0\leq k<\left(1+n\frac{ab^{n-1}-1}{b^{n}-1}-n\right)\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-1}{b^{n}-1}-1\right)^{-1}.

(d)

If we choose the
k
to be negative, then to get a sufficient condition for the existence of the increasing function we have to integrate over the sub-variety
E_{\infty}.

Now we turn our attention to the unstable case, that is when
c_{k}+k<n-1.

In this case the general solution(
2.4
) of equation (
2.2
) is not increasing in
[1,b]
.
Instead we consider the following auxiliary family of equations

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\tau)}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau)}{\tau^{2}}-k=0,&\tau\in(s,b)\\
\psi(s)=1\text{ and }\psi(b)=a,&\text{ where }s\in(1,b).\end{cases}

Solution of the above equation is

\psi_{s}(\tau)=\frac{\tau^{2}}{n+1}+\frac{C_{k}(s)}{n}\tau+\frac{A_{k}(s)}{\tau^{n-1}}.

Using the boundary conditions to solve for
C_{k}(s)\text{ and }A_{k}(s)
, we get

(2.6)

C_{k}(s)=n\frac{ab^{n-1}-s^{n-1}}{b^{n}-s^{n}}-\frac{kn}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-s^{n+1}}{b^{n}-s^{n}}\text{ and }A_{k}(s)=s^{n-1}\frac{n+1-ks^{2}}{n+1}-s^{n}\frac{C_{k}(s)}{n}.

Let

\mathcal{S}=\{s\in(1,b):\text{ there exist }\psi_{s}\text{ solves }\eqref{sjeqn}\text{ with }\psi_{s}^{\prime}(\tau)>0,\text{ for all }\tau\in[s,b]\},

and

\lambda=\inf\mathcal{S}.

Since the solution (
2.4
)
\psi
of the ODE (
2.2
) is convex and
\psi^{\prime}(1)<0
in the case
c_{k}+k<n-1
there exist a unique
\bar{s}
such that
\psi^{\prime}(\bar{s})=0
and there exist a unique
s\in(\bar{s},b)
such that
\psi(s)=1.
Hence by definition
s
belong to the set
\mathcal{S}
and
1<\lambda\leq s
.

Then
\lambda
satisfies the following equation

C_{k}(\lambda)+k\lambda=\frac{n-1}{\lambda}

which follows from the equation
\psi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
, by the definition of
\lambda.

The left hand side of the above equation (in analogy to the
J
-equation) is related to the topological constant of the modified
J
-equation as follows:

Let
\chi_{\lambda}\in b[E_{\infty}]-\lambda[E_{0}]
and
\omega\in a[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
. Then

C_{k}(\lambda)+k\lambda=\min_{X_{n}}\left(n\frac{[\omega]\cdot[\chi_{\lambda}]^{n-1}}{[\chi_{\lambda}]^{n}}+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\lambda})\right).

Suppose for
[\chi]=b[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}],[\omega]=a[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
and vector field
\xi=kw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}(k\geq 0)
, we get that
0<c_{k}+k<n-1
, we claim that we can perturb the Kähler class of
\chi
so that we can solve the modified
J
-equation in the perturbed class. And that perturbation is measured by the constant
\lambda
that we introduced above.

For any
s\in(1,b)
, let
\chi_{s}\in b[E_{\infty}]-s[E_{0}]
be any Kähler metric and we know that

C_{k}([\chi_{s}],[\omega])+ks=\min_{X_{n}}\left(n\frac{[\omega]\cdot[\chi_{s}]^{n-1}}{[\chi_{s}]^{n}}+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{s})\right).

2.2.
Geometric description of the constant
\lambda
in the case
c_{k}+k<n-1
:

In this subsection we give a geometric description of the constant
\lambda
.

Let
\chi_{s}\in b[E_{\infty}]-s[E_{0}]

and

\mathcal{S}=\left\{s\in(1,b):C_{k}([\chi_{s}],[\omega])+ks>\frac{n-1}{s}\right\}.

Let us look at the constant
C_{k}([\chi_{s}],[\omega])+ks
,

C_{k}([\chi_{s}],[\omega])+ks=n\frac{ab^{n-1}-s^{n-1}}{b^{n}-s^{n}}-\frac{nk}{n+1}\frac{b^{n}+b^{n-1}s+\cdots+s^{n-1}}{b^{n-1}+b^{n-2}s+\cdots+s^{n-1}}+ks.

As
s\to b
the first term in the above sum goes to infinity and the second and third term are bounded for all
s\in(1,b)
, the set
\mathcal{S}
is nonempty.

We claim that the condition
C_{k}([\chi_{s}],[\omega])+ks>\frac{n-1}{s}
is the condition for the existence of the solution of the modified
J
-equation in the Kähler class
[\chi_{s}]
.
Let
\alpha_{s}\in b_{1}[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}],
where
b_{1}=bs^{-1}
for
s\in\mathcal{S}
be any Kähler metric,
k_{1}=s^{2}k
and
\xi^{\prime}=k_{1}w\frac{\partial}{\partial w}=s^{2}\xi
. Then

C_{k}([\chi_{s}],[\omega])+ks>\frac{n-1}{s}\text{ if and only if }C_{k_{1}}([\alpha_{s}],[\omega])+k_{1}>n-1.

By Theorem
2.2
, there exist
\hat{\alpha}_{s}\in[\alpha_{s}]
such that

(2.7)

n\frac{\omega\wedge\hat{\alpha}_{s}^{n-1}}{\hat{\alpha}_{s}^{n}}=n\frac{[\omega]\cdot[\hat{\alpha}_{s}]^{n-1}}{[\hat{\alpha}_{s}]^{n}}+\theta_{\xi^{\prime}}(\hat{\alpha}_{s}).

Letting
\hat{\chi}:=s\hat{\alpha}_{s}\in b[E_{\infty}]-s[E_{0}]
, if we multiply the equation (
2.7
) by
s^{-1}
we get

n\frac{\omega\wedge\hat{\chi}^{n-1}}{\hat{\chi}^{n}}=n\frac{[\omega]\cdot[\hat{\chi}]^{n-1}}{[\hat{\chi}]^{n}}+\theta_{\xi}(\hat{\chi}),

which proves our claim. In particular by the definition of
\lambda
we have

C_{k}(\lambda)+k\lambda=\frac{n-1}{\lambda}\text{ and }\lambda=\inf\mathcal{S}.

Note that since
c_{k}+k<n-1
by Corolloary
2.3
we also have that
\lambda>1.

Moreover, if we take any
s\in(1,\lambda)
, then by definition of
\lambda
we get that

C_{k}([\chi_{s}],[\omega])+ks<\frac{n-1}{s}

and this is equivalent to

C_{k_{1}}([\alpha_{s}],[\omega])+k_{1}<n-1.

Thus we can not have a smooth solution to the modified
J
-equation in the class
[\chi_{s}].

2.3.
Modified
J
-flow with the Calabi ansatz:

Recall that the modified
J
-flow is defined as

(2.8)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=c-n\frac{\omega\wedge\chi_{\varphi}^{n-1}}{\chi_{\varphi}^{n}}+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\varphi})\\
\varphi_{t}|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{T},\end{cases}

where
\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\varphi})
is the Hamiltonian function of the action of
\xi
with respect to the metric
\chi_{\varphi}
which is normalized so that
\int_{X}\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\varphi})\chi_{\varphi}^{n}=0
and

Differentiating equation (
2.8
) with respect to

, we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right)=\tilde{\Delta}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right)+\xi\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right),

where
\tilde{\Delta}f=h^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{i}\partial_{\bar{j}}f
and
h^{i\bar{j}}=\chi^{i\bar{k}}g_{l\bar{k}}\chi^{l\bar{j}}.

Then the maximum principle implies that

\min_{X_{n}}\left.\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}\leq\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\leq\max_{X_{n}}\left.\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}.

In particular,

(2.9)

\Lambda_{\chi_{\varphi}}\omega\leq\max_{X_{n}}\Lambda_{\chi_{0}}\omega+\max_{X_{n}}\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\phi})-\min_{X_{n}}\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{0}).

By Corollary 5.3 of
[
24
]
,
\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\varphi})
is uniformly bounded. Then the right hand side of the above equation is bounded above by strictly positive constant
A.

Thus

(2.10)

\chi_{\varphi}\geq A\omega,

as long as the flow exists.

Let
\omega=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}g(s)
and
\chi_{t}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f(s,t)
, for
t\geq 0
satisfy the Calabi Ansatz from the previous section. Then the modified
J
flow (
2.8
) becomes

(2.11)

\frac{\partial f(s,t)}{\partial t}=c-(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{f^{\prime}(s,t)}-\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(s)}{f^{\prime\prime}(s,t)}+kf^{\prime}(s,t)-\frac{kn}{n+1}\frac{b^{n+1}-1}{b^{n}-1}.

As before, we define strictly increasing functions in the variable
\tau\in[1,b]
for all
t\geq 0
, that is

\psi:[1,b]\times[0,\infty)\to[1,a]
by

\psi(f^{\prime}(s,t),t)=g^{\prime}(s).

Proposition 2.5
.

The function
\psi=\psi(\tau,t)
defined above is a classical solution of the initial-boundary value problem

(2.12)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=Q(\psi)P[\psi],\\
\psi(1,t)=1,\quad\psi(b,t)=a,\forall t\in(0,\infty)\end{cases}

where

(2.13)

P[\psi]=\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau,t)+(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\tau,t)}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau,t)}{\tau^{2}}-k,

and
Q\in\textbf{C}^{\infty}([1,a],[0,\infty))
is uniquely determined by
g
such that
Q(x)=0
if and only if
x=1
and
x=b
.
Moreover, the initial data
\psi(\tau,0)=\psi_{0}(\tau)
is any smooth monotone function satisfying the above boundary conditions.

Proof.

We have the equation
\psi(f^{\prime}(s,t))=g^{\prime}(s).
Take derivative with respect to the variable
t.

(2.14)

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=0

And if we take derivative of the equation (
2.11
) with respect to the variable
s
, we get

\frac{\partial f^{\prime}}{\partial t}=-(n-1)\left(\frac{g^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}-\left(\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{\prime}+kf^{\prime\prime}(s,t)

Using this in equation (
2.14
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\psi^{\prime}(f^{\prime}(s,t))\left[(n-1)\left(f^{\prime\prime}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\tau)}{f^{\prime}}-\frac{\psi(\tau)}{f^{\prime 2}}f^{\prime\prime}\right)+\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)f^{\prime\prime}-kf^{\prime\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=\psi^{\prime}(f^{\prime}(s,t))f^{\prime\prime}(s,t)\left[(n-1)\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\tau)}{\tau}-\frac{\psi(\tau)}{\tau^{2}}\right)+\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)-k\right]

\displaystyle=g^{\prime\prime}(s)\left(\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)+(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\tau)}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau)}{\tau^{2}}-k\right)

Since
g
is convex and
g^{\prime}>0
, we can get that
g^{\prime\prime}(s)=g^{\prime\prime}((g^{\prime})^{-1}(\psi))
.
Let
Q(\psi)=g^{\prime\prime}((g^{\prime})^{-1}(\psi)).

Then by the asymptotic properties of

, we can get that
Q(1)=Q(b)=0
and

, for all
\tau\in(1,b).

∎

3.
Convergence of the flow with the special initial data

In this section we prove a weaker version of our main theorem for a special choice of initial condition.
For the most part, our proof runs parallel to the arguments in
[
10
]
and
[
6
]
. There is
however one key point of difference that we now wish to explain. Recall that the proof of
[
10
]
and
[
6
]
also proceeds by first proving convergence for a special initial Kähler form, namely
the Kähler form for which the function
\psi
is linear. With this choice of initial data one can then prove a comparison principle along the flow and a monotonicity property along the flow. Unfortunately, as we explain below (cf. Remark
3.3
), for the modified
J
-flow, the choice of a linear initial function does not quite work. We instead have to choose another concave, strictly increasing function. The next lemma summarises the key properties that we need from the initial function.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
c_{k}+k\leq n-1
, there exists a smooth, strictly monotonic and concave function
\psi_{0}:[1,b]\rightarrow[1,a]
with the following properties:

(1)

\psi_{0}\geq\sigma
, where we define

(3.1)

\sigma(\tau)=\begin{cases}1,&1\leq\tau\leq\lambda;\\
\psi_{\lambda}(\tau),&\lambda\leq\tau\leq b,\end{cases}\text{ and $\lambda\in(1,b)$ satisfies }C_{k}(\lambda)+k\lambda=\frac{n-1}{\lambda}.

(2)

P[\psi_{0}]\leq 0
, where
P
is the
2^{nd}
order linear differential operator given by (
2.13
).

Proof.

We define
\psi_{0}
an increasing, smooth and concave function by

\psi_{0}(\tau)=\frac{a-1}{b^{-n}-1}(\tau^{-n}-1)+1.

We know that the
\lambda
satisfies the equation
C_{k}(\lambda)+k\lambda=\frac{n-1}{\lambda}
this implies that

\frac{n+1-k\lambda^{2}}{n+1}-\lambda\frac{C_{k}(\lambda)}{n}>0,

therefore
\sigma
is convex on
[1,b].
Since
\psi_{0}
is concave we have

1\leq\sigma(\tau)<\psi_{0}(\tau)<a,\text{ for all }\tau\in(1,b).

Next, we compute

\displaystyle P[\psi_{0}]

\displaystyle=\frac{a-1}{b^{-n}-1}n(n+1)\frac{1}{\tau^{n+2}}-(n-1)\frac{a-1}{b^{-n}-1}\frac{n}{\tau^{n+2}}-(n-1)\frac{\psi_{0}(\tau)}{\tau^{2}}-k

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n(a-1)}{1-b^{-n}}\frac{1}{\tau^{n+2}}-(n-1)\frac{\psi_{0}(\tau)}{\tau^{2}}-k

\displaystyle<0

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
0<c_{k}+k\leq n-1
and suppose
\psi(\tau,t)
be the solution of the equation (
2.13
) with the initial value
\psi_{0}(\tau)=\psi(\tau,0)
, constructed in the previous lemma, then

(3.2)

\psi(\tau,t)\xrightarrow[C_{loc}^{1}((\lambda,b))]{C^{0}}\sigma(\tau)=\begin{cases}1,&1\leq\tau\leq\lambda\\
\psi_{\lambda}(\tau),&\lambda\leq\tau\leq b\end{cases},

where
\psi_{\lambda}
is a solution of the ODE (
2.2
) with
\psi_{\lambda}(\lambda)=1
and
\psi_{\lambda}(b)=a
and
\lambda
can be described as

\lambda=\inf\{s:\text{ there exist }\psi_{s}\text{ satisfies }\eqref{ode},\psi_{s}(s)=1,\psi_{s}(b)=a\text{ and }\psi_{s}^{\prime}(\tau)>0,\text{ for all }\tau\in(s,b)\}.

Proof.

The proof consists of several steps.

•

First we claim that
\psi(\tau,t)\geq\sigma(\tau).

Define
h(\tau,t)=\psi(\tau,t)-\sigma(\tau)
and it satisfies the following equation on
[\lambda,b]

(3.3)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=Q(\psi)\left(h^{\prime\prime}(\tau,t)+(n-1)\frac{h^{\prime}(\tau,t)}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{h(\tau,t)}{\tau^{2}}\right).

Since
\psi(\tau,t)
is an increasing function for each
t
,
\psi(\tau,t)>1
, for all
(\tau,t)\in(1,b]\times[0,\infty),
hence the claim is equivalent to proving that

\inf_{[\lambda,b]\times[0,T]}h(\tau,t)\geq 0,\text{ for all }T>0.

Suppose the claim is not true, that is, there exist a
T>0
such that
\inf_{[\lambda,b]\times[0,T]}h(\tau,t)<0.
Let
h(x,t_{0})=\inf_{[\lambda,b]\times[0,T]}h(\tau,t),
for some
(x,t_{0})\in[\lambda,b]\times[0,T].

Since
h(\tau,0)=\psi_{0}(\tau)-\sigma(\tau)\geq 0
(by Lemma
3.1
),
h(b,t)=0
and
h(\lambda,t)>0
we can see that
t_{0}>0
and
x\in(\lambda,b).
And we have

Q(\psi(x,t_{0}))>0,\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(x,t_{0})\leq 0,h^{\prime}(x,t_{0})=0\text{ and }h^{\prime\prime}(x,t_{0})\geq 0.

Hence, at
(x,t_{0})
we have

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}-Q(\psi)\left(h^{\prime\prime}+(n-1)\frac{h^{\prime}}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{h}{\tau^{2}}\right)\Bigg)(x,t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)Q(\psi(x,t_{0}))\frac{h(x,t_{0})}{x^{2}}

\displaystyle<0

which is a contradiction to the equation (
3.3
) satisfied by
h
on
[\lambda,b].

Hence we have

\psi(\tau,t)\geq\sigma(\tau),\text{ for all }\tau\in[1,b].

•

Next we claim that
\psi(\tau,t)
is decreasing in
t.
Let
H(\tau,t)=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}(\tau,t)
.
Firstly note that by our choice of
\psi_{0}
we have that by Lemma
3.1

H(\tau,0)=Q(\psi_{0})P[\psi_{0}]\leq 0.

The claim is equivalent to proving that

\sup_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}H(\tau,t)\leq 0,\text{ for all }T>0.

Suppose this is not true, then there exist a
T>0
such that
\sup_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}H(\tau,t)>0.
Let
H(x,t_{0})=\sup_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}H(\tau,t)
and consider

F(\tau,t)=e^{-Ct}H(\tau,t),\text{ where }C=\sup_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}Q^{\prime}(\psi(\tau,t))\frac{H(\tau,t)}{Q(\psi(\tau,t))}.

Since

\frac{H(\tau,t)}{Q(\psi(\tau,t))}=\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau,t)+(n-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(\tau,t)}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau,t)}{\tau^{2}}-k,

the constant
C
is finite.

Let
F(y,t_{1})=\sup_{[1,b]\times[0,T]}F(\tau,t).
Since
F(\tau,0)=H(\tau,0)\leq 0
and
F(1,t)=F(b,t)=0
it should be the case that
t_{1}>0
and
y\in(1,b)
. Then at
(y,t_{1})
we have

\displaystyle F(y,t_{1})>0,Q(\psi(y,t_{1}))>0,H(y,t_{1})>0,\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(y,t_{1})\geq 0,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(y,t_{1})=H^{\prime}(y,t_{1})=0,F^{\prime\prime}(y,t_{1})\leq 0\text{ and }H^{\prime\prime}(y,t_{1})\leq 0.

This implies that

0\leq\Big(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}-Q(\psi)F^{\prime\prime}\Big)(y,t_{1})=e^{-Ct_{1}}\left(-CH(y,t_{1})+\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}(y,t_{1})-Q(\psi(y,t_{1}))H^{\prime\prime}(y,t_{1})\right)

so

0\leq-e^{-Ct_{1}}\Bigg(H(y,t_{1})^{2}\frac{Q^{\prime}(\psi)}{Q(\psi)}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}(y,t_{1})\\
+Q(\psi)\Big(H^{\prime\prime}(y,t_{1})+(n-1)\frac{H^{\prime}(y,t_{1})}{y}-(n-1)\frac{H(y,t_{1})}{y^{2}}\Big)+(n-1)Q(\psi)\frac{H(y,t_{1})}{y^{2}}\Bigg)

Thus

H(y,t_{1})^{2}\frac{Q^{\prime}(\psi)}{Q(\psi)}-\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}(y,t_{1})+Q(\psi)\Big(H^{\prime\prime}(y,t_{1})\\
+(n-1)\frac{H^{\prime}(y,t_{1})}{y}-(n-1)\frac{H(y,t_{1})}{y^{2}}\Big)\leq-(n-1)Q(\psi)\frac{H(y,t_{1})}{y^{2}}<0

which is a contradiction to the fact that
H
actually satisfies the following equation

\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}(\tau,t)=H(\tau,t)^{2}\frac{Q^{\prime}(\psi)}{Q(\psi)}+Q(\psi)\Big(H^{\prime\prime}(\tau,t)+(n-1)\frac{H^{\prime}(\tau,t)}{\tau}-(n-1)\frac{H(\tau,t)}{\tau^{2}}\Big).

Hence

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\leq 0,

and
\psi(\tau,t)
is a decreasing function in
t
.

•

Now we prove the
C^{0}
convergence. The proof is very similar to that of
[
6
]
, so we give only sketch of the proof and for the details we refer the reader to
[
6
]
. Since
\Lambda_{\chi_{\varphi(t)}}\omega=\psi^{\prime}(\tau,t)+(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau,t)}{\tau}
and by the inequality (
2.9
) we get that
0<\psi^{\prime}(\tau,t)<C
for some positive constant C, together this with the previous claim implies that
\psi(\tau,t)
converges uniformly on
[1,b]
to its pointwise limit
\psi_{\infty}(\tau)
, an increasing continuous function.

•

Next, we prove the
C^{1}_{loc}
convergence of
\psi(\tau,t)
and differentiability of
\psi_{\infty}
on
(s,b)
, where

s=\sup\{x:\psi_{\infty}(x)=1\}.

Since
\psi(\tau,t)\geq\sigma(\tau),
we get
\psi_{\infty}(\tau)\geq\sigma(\tau)
.
Next, as in
[
6
]
, we consider the evolving point-wise slope

\eta(\tau,t):=\psi^{\prime}(\tau,t)+(n-1)\frac{\psi(\tau,t)}{\tau}-k\tau.

Then

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}(\tau,t)=Q(\psi)\eta^{\prime}(\tau,t).

So for each
t
,
\eta(\tau,t)
is decreasing on
[1,b].
Let us fix
0<\delta<<1
and
T>0
. Since
\psi(\tau,t)
converges uniformly to
\psi_{\infty}
we get
Q(\psi(\tau,t))
is uniformly bounded below, for all
(\tau,t)\in[s+\delta,b-\delta]\times[T,\infty)
, and this implies

(3.4)

\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{s+\delta}^{b-\delta}\left|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\right|d\tau dt=\int_{s+\delta}^{b-\delta}\Big(\psi(\tau,T)-\psi(\tau,\infty)\Big)d\tau\leq D

for some constant

independent of

This implies that

(3.5)

\int_{T}^{\infty}\Big(\eta(1+\delta,t)-\eta(b-\delta,t)\Big)dt=-\int_{T}^{\infty}\int_{s+\delta}^{b-\delta}\eta^{\prime}(\tau,t)d\tau dt\leq C(\delta),

for some
C(\delta)>0
.
Therefore, there exist a sequence
\{t_{j}\}
such that
\eta(\tau,t_{j})
converges to
\eta_{\infty}
and by the monotinicity of
\eta
the limit
\eta_{\infty}
will be a constant. And the uniform convergence of
\psi(\tau,t)
gives the uniform convergence of
\psi^{\prime}(\tau,t_{j})
on
(s,b).

Now, let
x,\tau\in(s,b)
, then

\eta_{\infty}(\tau-x)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{x}^{\tau}\eta(y,t_{j})dy=\psi_{\infty}(\tau)-\psi_{\infty}(x)+\int_{x}^{\tau}\Big((n-1)\frac{\psi_{\infty}(y)}{y}-ky\Big)dy.

Hence
\psi_{\infty}
is differentiable on
(s,b)
and it satisfies the following equation

\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau)+(n-1)\frac{\psi_{\infty}(\tau)}{\tau}-k\tau=\eta_{\infty}.

•

Next we claim that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\psi(\tau,t)=\psi_{\infty}(\tau)=\sigma(\tau).

Since
\psi_{\infty}(\tau)\geq\sigma(\tau)

and
1\leq\sigma(\tau)<\psi_{0}(\tau)<a,\text{ for all }\tau\in(1,b)
the function
\psi_{\infty}
should be of the following form

\psi_{\infty}(\tau)=\begin{cases}1,&1\leq\tau\leq s;\\
\rho(\tau),&s\leq\tau\leq b,\end{cases}

where

and

is a solution of the ODE (
2.2
) in the interval

with the boundary condition

\rho^{\prime}(\tau)>0,\text{ for all }\tau\in(s,b)
which follows from the last claim and the fact that

is increasing. This implies that

From the definition of
\lambda
we can get that

And the uniqueness of the solution of the ODE (

\psi_{\infty}(\tau)=\sigma(\tau).

Therefore

converges in

, follows from the fact that

Finally, since

) on

Remark 3.3

in the time variable. For instance,

and

, then in this case

Remark 3.4
.

For the case
c_{k}+k>n-1,
the solution of the ODE (
2.2
),
\tilde{\psi}
can be neither convex nor concave. For example, on
X_{3}
, if we take

Uniqueness and higher order estimates

Uniqueness and higher order estimates for the

-flow with the Calabi ansatz in the unstable case is done in
[

for this we need some results which are very similar to the results proved in

]
with necessary modifications. So, just for the readers convenience we state those results and give sketch of the proof for some of the results which needed the necessary modifications.

In the following proposition we prove that on a given compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{R}
the normalized ansatz Kähler potentials actually converge. Let

\Phi:[1,a]\times[0,\infty)\to[1,b],\text{ defined by }\Phi(\tau,t):=\psi^{-1}(\tau,t).

That is
f^{\prime}(s,t)=\Phi(g^{\prime}(s),t)
and we define
\Phi_{\infty}:=(\psi_{\infty}|_{[\lambda,b]})^{-1}:[1,a]\to[\lambda,b].

Proposition 4.1
.

For any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}
,

f^{\prime}(s,t)\to(\Phi_{\infty}\circ g^{\prime})(s),

increasingly in
L^{\infty}(K)
as
t\to\infty.

Proof.

For a given compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{R}
we can find a
B\subset\subset(\lambda,b)
such that
K=(\Phi_{\infty}\circ g^{\prime})^{-1}(B)
, and we have
\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\tau)>0,
for all
\tau\in B.
Then by Proposition
3.2
, there exists
C,T>0
such that for any
t>T
and
\tau\in B
, we have

C^{-1}<\psi^{\prime}(\tau,t)<C.

Since
\psi
decreases in
t
and by definition of
\Phi
we get that
\Phi(.,t)
converges uniformly to
\Phi_{\infty}
on
\Phi_{\infty}^{-1}(B)
increasingly in
t
. Thus, for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}
,
f^{\prime}(s,t)
converges increasingly to
(\Phi_{\infty}\circ g^{\prime})(s)
, for
s\in K.

∎

We define the normalized ansatz potentials by

\hat{f}(s,t):=f(s,t)-f(0,t)=\int_{0}^{s}f^{\prime}(r,t)dr

and

f_{\infty}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}(\Phi_{\infty}\circ g^{\prime})(r)dr.

By the above proposition we get that
\hat{f}(s,t)
converges uniformly to
f_{\infty}(s)
on any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}
as
t\to\infty.

Lemma 4.2
.

For all
t\geq 0,
we have

\hat{f}(s,t)\leq f_{\infty}(s)\text{ when }s\geq 0,\text{ and }\hat{f}(s,t)\geq f_{\infty}(s)\text{ when }s\leq 0.

Proof.

Since
\hat{f}(s,t)-f_{\infty}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}(f^{\prime}(r,t)-(\Phi_{\infty}\circ g^{\prime})(r))dr
, the lemma follows from the Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Let

\phi(s,t):=f(s,t)-f_{0}(s),\phi_{\infty}(s):=f_{\infty}(s)-f_{0}(s),

where
f_{0}
is the global potential for
\chi_{0}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f_{0}(s)\in b[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
so
f^{\prime}_{0}(-\infty)=1
and
f_{0}^{\prime}(\infty)=b
also since,
f_{\infty}
satisfies the modified
J
equation on
[\lambda,b]
with
f^{\prime}_{\infty}(-\infty)=\lambda
and
f^{\prime}_{\infty}(\infty)=b
,
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\infty}\in b[E_{\infty}]-\lambda[E_{0}].

Therefore
\phi(\cdot,t)
is a Kähler potential with respect to the metric
\chi_{0}
, for all
t\geq 0
and it satisfies the following equation

\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(s,t)=c_{k}-(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{f^{\prime}(s,t)}-\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(s)}{f^{\prime\prime}(s,t)}+kf^{\prime}(s,t)

Let
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to(-\infty,0]
be a smooth increasing function satisfying

\displaystyle\nu(s)=(\lambda-1)s\text{ when }s\in(-\infty,-1)\text{ and }

\displaystyle\nu(s)=0\text{ when }s\in(0,\infty).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

\hat{\phi}_{\infty}:=\phi_{\infty}-\nu,

then
\hat{\phi}_{\infty}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By definition
\hat{\phi}_{\infty}=f_{\infty}-(f_{0}+\nu)
and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f_{\infty}\in b[E_{\infty}]-\lambda[E_{0}]
is a Kähler current and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}(f_{0}+\nu)
is a Kähler metric, thus
\hat{\phi}_{\infty}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

There exist a
C>0
such that for all
(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
, we have

\nu(s)-C\leq\phi(s,t)-\phi(0,t)\leq C

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(0,t)=c_{k}-C_{k}(\lambda).

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
f_{0}(0)=0
and by the definition of
\phi(s,t)
, we have

\phi(s,t)-\phi(0,t)=f(s,t)-f_{0}(s)-[f(0,t)-f_{0}(0)]=\hat{f}(s,t)-f_{0}(s).

and the first equation of the lemma follows from Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
. And by Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
3.2
for any
K\subset\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime\prime}}+(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}}-kf^{\prime}-C_{k}(\lambda)\right|_{L^{\infty}(K)}=0.

Hence

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(0,t)=c_{k}-C_{k}(\lambda).

∎

We chose
\phi(s,t)
as a solution of the flow with the special initial metric, now let
\varphi
be the solution of the modified
J
flow with any initial data
\varphi(.,0)
with Calabi symmetry. And let

\vartheta(s,t)=\varphi(s,t)-f_{0}(s)\text{ and }\chi_{0}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}f_{0}.

Then
\vartheta(.,t)
are Kähler potentials of
[\chi_{0}]
and the modified
J
flow is

\frac{\partial\vartheta}{\partial t}=c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\vartheta})-\Lambda_{\chi_{\vartheta}}\omega.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a
C>0
such that on
X_{n}\times[0,\infty)
,

\phi_{\infty}(s)-C\leq\vartheta(s,t)-\phi(0,t)\leq C.

Proof.

We know that
\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\varphi})=\theta_{\xi}(\chi)+\xi(\varphi).

If we take difference between the flows with initial values
\phi(\cdot,0)
and
\vartheta(\cdot,0)
, then
\phi(x,t)-\vartheta(x,t)
satisfies the following equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\phi(x,t)-\vartheta(x,t)\right)=F^{i\bar{j}}[(1-s_{t})\chi_{\phi(t)}+s_{t}\chi_{\vartheta(t)}](\phi(t)-\vartheta(t))_{i\bar{j}}+\xi(\phi(t)-\vartheta(t)),

where
0<s_{t}<1.
By the maximum principle we have

|\phi(x,t)-\vartheta(x,t)|_{C^{0}}\leq|\phi(x,0)-\vartheta(x,0)|_{C^{0}}\leq C,\text{ for all }t\in[0,\infty).

This together with the last lemma we can see that

\nu(s)-C_{1}\leq\vartheta(s,t)-\phi(0,t)\leq C_{1}.

And by Lemma
4.3
we can get that
\nu(s)-C_{2}\leq\phi(s,t)\leq\nu(s)+C_{2}
this implies that

\phi_{\infty}(s)-C\leq\vartheta(s,t)-\phi(0,t)\leq C.

∎

The Kähler potential
\phi_{\infty}
of
\chi_{0}
is not a strict subsolution of the modified
J
equation for the new Kähler classes
[\chi_{\infty}]=b[E_{\infty}]-\lambda[E_{0}]
and
[\omega]
and the constant
C_{k}(\lambda),
because
\chi_{\infty}
blows up in the normal direction to
E_{0}.

Let
f_{\infty}
be the limiting solution corresponding to the new Kähler classes. Then
f_{\infty}^{\prime}(-\infty)=\lambda>1
and

\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(s)}{f_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}(s)}+(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{f_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)}-kf_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)=C_{k}(\lambda)

this implies that

(n-2)\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{f_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(s)}{f_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}(s)}-kf_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)\leq C_{k}(\lambda)-\frac{1}{b},~(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{f_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)}-kf_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)\leq C_{k}(\lambda)

and the equality holds at
-\infty
in the second inequality as

\lim_{s\to-\infty}e^{-s}f_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\infty.

Our aim is to get a strict subsolution so we modify
f_{\infty}
using the barrier function
\nu
which is increasing and smooth and defined in the following way

\nu(s)=s\text{ when }s\leq-1\text{ and }\nu(s)=0\text{ when }s>0.

Let

V_{\infty}(s)=f_{\infty}(s)+\varepsilon\nu(s)

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0.
Then
V_{\infty}
defines a Kähler metric
\Omega=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}V_{\infty}\in b[E_{\infty}]-(\lambda+\varepsilon)[E_{0}].

Further, we can choose a constant
d>0
so that

(4.1)

\begin{split}(n-2)\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{V^{\prime}_{\infty}(s)}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(s)}{V^{\prime\prime}_{\infty}(s)}-kV_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)<&C_{k}(\lambda)-2d\varepsilon\\
\text{ and }\quad(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{V^{\prime}_{\infty}(s)}-kV_{\infty}^{\prime}(s)<&C_{k}(\lambda)-2d\varepsilon.\end{split}

We will use this subsolution to prove the second order estimate, as Lemma
4.7
.

Now, let

\hat{\vartheta}(s,t):=\vartheta(s,t)-\phi(0,t).

Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\vartheta}}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle c+\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\hat{\vartheta}(t)})-\Lambda_{\chi_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\hat{\vartheta}}(\omega)-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(0,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{k}(\lambda)+\delta(t)+k\vartheta^{\prime}(s,t)+kf_{0}^{\prime}(s)-\Lambda_{\chi_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\hat{\vartheta}}(\omega),

where
\delta(t):=c_{k}-C_{k}(\lambda)-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}(0,t)\to 0

as
t\to\infty
by Lemma
4.4
.

Let

u:=\hat{\vartheta}-(V_{\infty}-f_{0}).

Then
u(s,t)=\hat{\vartheta}(s,t)-(f_{\infty}(s)+\varepsilon\nu(s)-f_{0}(s))=\hat{\vartheta}(s,t)-(\phi_{\infty}(s)-\varepsilon\nu(s)),

thus
u
is smooth on
X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0}
and tends to
\infty
along
E_{0}.

Lemma 4.6
.

There exist a constant
C>0
such that

\inf_{X_{n}\times[0,\infty)}u>-C.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
we have

u(s,t)=\hat{\vartheta}(s,t)-(\phi_{\infty}(s)-\varepsilon\nu(s))\geq\phi_{\infty}(s)-C-(\phi_{\infty}(s)-\varepsilon\nu(s))\geq-C.

∎

The following lemma is analogous to Lemma 3.1 of Song-Weinkove in
[
17
]
which guarantee us the second order estimates away from the exceptional divisor.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\chi=\chi_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\vartheta
be the Kähler metrics corresponding to the solution of the
J
flow. Then there exist
A,C>0
such that on
X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0},
we have

\Lambda_{\omega}\chi\leq Ce^{Au}.

Proof.

As in
[
6
]
, we can apply the maximum principle argument to
H=\log\Lambda_{\omega}\chi-Au
with the perturbed heat operator and then use the subsolution construction to get the required estimate.

The evolution for
\log\Lambda_{\omega}\chi
is given by the following general estimate

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{t}\right)\log\Lambda_{\omega}\chi\leq-\frac{n}{\Lambda_{\omega}\chi}\left(h^{k\bar{l}}R_{k\bar{l}}^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{i\bar{j}}-\chi^{k\bar{l}}R_{k\bar{l}}\right),

where
h^{i\bar{j}}=\chi^{i\bar{l}}\omega_{k\bar{l}}\chi^{k\bar{j}},\Delta_{t}:=n^{-1}h^{i\bar{j}}\partial_{j}\overline{\partial}_{j},
and
R
is the curvature tensor for
\omega.

Also

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\xi-\Delta_{t}\right)u=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\vartheta}}{\partial t}-\xi(\hat{\vartheta})+\xi(V_{\infty}-f_{0})-\Delta_{t}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\infty}+\delta(t)-\Lambda_{\chi_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\hat{\vartheta}}(\omega)+k\vartheta^{\prime}+kf_{0}^{\prime}-k\vartheta^{\prime}

\displaystyle+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}-kf_{0}^{\prime}-h^{i\bar{j}}\vartheta_{i\bar{j}}+h^{i\bar{j}}\Omega_{i\bar{j}}-h^{i\bar{j}}f_{0_{i\bar{j}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\infty}+\delta(t)-2\chi^{i\bar{j}}\omega_{i\bar{j}}+\chi^{i\bar{l}}\omega_{k\bar{l}}\chi^{k\bar{j}}\Omega_{i\bar{j}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}

Suppose the maximum of
H
on
X_{n}\times[0,t_{0}]
is achieved at
(p_{0},t_{0}),
with
t_{0}>0
, otherwise the estimate follows trivially. Then applying maximum principle, we have at
(p_{0},t_{0})

(4.2)

0\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\xi-\Delta_{t}\right)H\\
\leq-(n\Lambda_{\omega}\chi)^{-1}(h^{k\bar{l}}R_{k\bar{l}}^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{i\bar{j}}-\chi^{k\bar{l}}R_{k\bar{l}}+n^{-1}\xi(\Lambda_{\omega}\chi))\\
-A(\eta_{\infty}+\delta(t)-2\chi^{i\bar{j}}\omega_{i\bar{j}}+\chi^{i\bar{l}}\omega_{k\bar{l}}\chi^{k\bar{j}}\Omega_{i\bar{j}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime})

Since
\Lambda_{\omega}\chi
is uniformly bounded below(by inequality (
2.10
)), for a given
2d\varepsilon>0
we can choose sufficiently large
A
such that at
(p_{0},t_{0}),
we have

\eta_{\infty}+\delta(t)+h^{i\bar{j}}\Omega_{i\bar{j}}-2\chi^{i\bar{j}}\omega_{i\bar{j}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}\leq-(nA\Lambda_{\omega}\chi)^{-1}(h^{k\bar{l}}R_{k\bar{l}}^{i\bar{j}}\chi_{i\bar{j}}-\chi^{k\bar{l}}R_{k\bar{l}}-n\xi(\Lambda_{\omega}\chi))<2d\varepsilon

As
\delta(t)\to 0
when
t\to\infty
, we can find a
T
such that
|\delta(t)|<d\varepsilon,
for all
t>T.
And, we can take
t_{0}>T
, otherwise, the estimate follows trivially. This implies that

(4.3)

\eta_{\infty}+h^{i\bar{j}}\Omega_{i\bar{j}}-2\chi^{i\bar{j}}\omega_{i\bar{j}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}<d\varepsilon

From (
4.1
) we already have that

2d\varepsilon<\eta_{\infty}-(n-2)\frac{g^{\prime}}{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}-\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}\text{ and }2d\varepsilon<\eta_{\infty}-(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}}{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}.

Note that
[h^{i\bar{j}}]=diag\left[\frac{g^{\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}},\dots,\frac{g^{\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}},\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime\prime})^{2}}\right]
, if we apply this in (
4.2
) and using (
4.3
) and the above inequality we get

\displaystyle d\varepsilon\geq

\displaystyle\eta_{\infty}+(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime\prime})^{2}}-2(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}}-2\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime\prime}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)g^{\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime\prime})^{2}}-2\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime\prime}}+\left(\eta_{\infty}-(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}}{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2d\varepsilon-2\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime\prime}}

and

\displaystyle d\varepsilon\geq

\displaystyle\eta_{\infty}+(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime\prime})^{2}}-2(n-1)\frac{g^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}}-2\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime\prime}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-2)g^{\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{f^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+g^{\prime\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{f^{\prime\prime}}-\frac{1}{V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}}\right)^{2}+\frac{g^{\prime\prime}V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime\prime})^{2}}-2\frac{g^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\eta_{\infty}-(n-2)\frac{g^{\prime}}{V_{\infty}^{\prime}}-\frac{g^{\prime\prime}}{V_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}}+kV_{\infty}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2d\varepsilon-2\frac{g^{\prime}}{f^{\prime}}

Combinig the above estimates, we have at
(p_{0},t_{0})
,

\Lambda_{\omega}\chi=(n-1)\frac{f^{\prime}}{g^{\prime}}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime\prime}}\leq\frac{2n}{d\varepsilon}

and so for any
p\in X_{n}
and
t\in[0,t_{0}]
we have

\displaystyle\log\Lambda_{\omega}\chi(p,t)=

\displaystyle H(p,t)+Au(p,t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\Lambda_{\omega}\chi(p_{0},t_{0})-Au(p_{0},t_{0})+Au(p,t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\log d\varepsilon+\log 2n+Au(p,t)-A\inf_{X_{n}}u(p,t_{0}).

Taking exponential on both sides and using the Lemma
4.6
we get the required inequality.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
K\subset\subset X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0}
and

there exists
C:=C(K,l)
such that

\|\hat{\vartheta}\|_{C^{l}(K)}\leq C.

Proof.

In section 3 of
[
17
]
, Song and Weinkove derived the

estimate by sequence of lemmas using just the

estimate and not any equation. As we have
C^{2}
estimate in Lemma
4.7
, using the arguments from
[

∎

We got all the necessary estimates to pass the limit, now we prove in the next theorem that irrespective of the initial value the flow always converges to a unique limiting solution.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\vartheta(t)
be the solution of the modified
J
-flow, then
\Lambda_{\chi_{\vartheta(t)}}\omega-\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\vartheta(t)})
converges to
C_{\lambda}
smoothly on
X_{n}\smallsetminus E_{0}
as
t\to\infty,
where
C_{\lambda}=n\frac{ab^{n-1}-\lambda^{n-1}}{b^{n}-\lambda^{n}}.

Proof.

Let us define a modified
J
-energy functional as

E(t)=\int_{X_{n}}\left(\Lambda_{\chi_{\vartheta(t)}}\omega-\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\vartheta(t)})\right)^{2}\chi_{\vartheta(t)}^{n}.

In
[
15
]
Li-Shi proved that the energy decreases along the modified
J
-flow by showing that the energy functional along the flow satisfies the following equation:

\frac{d}{dt}E(t)=-2\int_{X_{n}}\left|\nabla^{\chi_{\vartheta(t)}}\left(\Lambda_{\chi_{\vartheta(t)}}\omega-\theta_{\xi}(\chi_{\vartheta(t)})\right)\right|_{\omega}^{2}\chi_{\vartheta(t)}^{n}

The proof follows from the similar arguments of the proof of the result in
[
6
]
.
Hence, the modified
J
-flow always converges to a unique limit and it satisfies the modified
J
-equation in the new Kähler class.
∎

.

Remark 5.1
.

Suppose we are given with two Kähler classes
\chi\in b[E_{\infty}]-b_{0}[E_{0}]
,
\omega\in a[E_{\infty}]-a_{0}[E_{0}]
and the vector field
\xi_{k}=kw\frac{\partial}{\partial w}(k\geq 0)
, then we consider the new Kähler classes
\frac{b}{b_{0}}[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
and
\frac{a}{a_{0}}[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}]
and let
f_{1}(s,t)
be the solution of the modified
J
-flow for the scaled classes with
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}g_{1}(s)\in\frac{a}{a_{0}}[E_{\infty}]-[E_{0}].

As the modified
J
-equation has the normalized Hamiltonian on its right side we have to scale the vector field by

that is
\xi^{\prime}=\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{a_{0}}\xi.

Then the usual parabolic scaling gives us the flow for the original Kähler classes. That is if we take

then this satisfies the modified

and

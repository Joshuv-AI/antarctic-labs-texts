A Ruelle dynamical zeta function for equivariant flows
Peter Hochs, Hemanth Saratchandran

Abstract
For proper group actions on smooth manifolds, with compact quotients, we define an equivariant version of the Ruelle dynamical ζ \zeta -function for equivariant flows satisfying a nondegeneracy condition. The construction is based on an equivariant generalisation of Guillemin’s trace formula, obtained in a companion paper. This formula implies several properties of the equivariant Ruelle ζ \zeta -function. We ask the question in what situations an equivariant generalisation of Fried’s conjecture holds, relating the equivariant Ruelle ζ \zeta -function to equivariant analytic torsion. We compute the equivariant Ruelle ζ \zeta -function in several examples, including examples where the classical Ruelle ζ \zeta -function is not defined. The equivariant Fried conjecture holds in the examples where the condition of the conjecture (vanishing of the kernel of the Laplacian) is satisfied.



1
Introduction

The Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function is an expression involving the lengths of closed flow curves of a flow on a compact manifold. The Fried conjecture states that, for a reasonable class of flows, the value of this function at zero is equal to the analytic torsion of the manifold. In this paper, we construct an equivariant generalisation of the Ruelle
\zeta
-function for proper, cocompact group actions. We give examples where an equivariant generalisation of the Fried conjecture does and does not hold, and ask the question under what hypotheses this generalisation is true.

Background and motivation

Let
M
be a compact manifold, and
\varphi
a flow on
M
. Suppose that
\varphi
is
Anosov
, i.e. that the tangent bundle of
M
decomposes into the image of the generating vector field of
\varphi
, and two sub-bundles on which the derivative of
\varphi
is exponentially contractive as time goes to plus or minus infinity, respectively. An example of an Anosov flow is
the geodesic flow on the unit sphere bundle of the tangent bundle of a compact
Riemannian manifold with negative sectional curvature.

If a flow curve
\gamma
of
\varphi
satisfies
\gamma(l)=\gamma(0)
, then the linearised Poincaré map

P_{\gamma}\colon T_{\gamma(0)}M/\mathbb{R}\gamma^{\prime}(0)\to T_{\gamma(0)}M/\mathbb{R}\gamma^{\prime}(0),

(1.1)

induced by
T_{\gamma(0)}\varphi_{l}
,
does not have
1
as an eigenvalue. Therefore,
\sgn(\det(1-P_{\gamma}))
is well-defined; we then say that the flow
\varphi
is
nondegenerate
.
Let
\nabla^{F}
be a flat connection on a vector bundle
F\to M
. For
\gamma
as above, let
\rho(\gamma)
be the parallel transport map with respect to

\nabla^{F}
along
\gamma
from
0
to
l
. Let

T^{\#}_{\gamma}:=\min\{t>0;\gamma(t)=\gamma(0)\}

(1.2)

be the primitive period of
\gamma
. Then the
Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function
for
\varphi
with respect to
\nabla^{F}
is defined by

R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma):=\exp\left(\sum_{\gamma}\frac{e^{-\sigma l}}{l}\sgn(\det(1-P_{\gamma}))\tr(\rho(\gamma))T^{\#}_{\gamma}\right),

(1.3)

where the sum is over closed flow curves
\gamma
, and
l
is the period of such a curve
\gamma
. (See
[
20
]
for the version introduced by Ruelle, or Definition 2.7 in
[
25
]
for a more general definition.) For Anosov flows, the set of such curves is discrete, and the sum on the right in (
1.3
) converges for
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
with large real part. This follows from an exponential bound on the number of flow curves with periods less than a given value; see (8) in
[
15
]
or Lemma 2.2 in
[
3
]
.

Let
\Delta_{F}:=(\nabla^{F})^{*}\nabla^{F}+\nabla^{F}(\nabla^{F})^{*}
be the Laplacian on differential forms twisted by
F
associated to
\nabla^{F}
.
Let
N
be the number operator on such forms, equal to
p
times the identity on
p
-forms. Let
P
be the orthogonal projection onto the kernel of
\Delta_{F}
. The
Ray–Singer
analytic torsion
of
\nabla^{F}
is defined by

T(\nabla^{F}):=\exp\left(\frac{-1}{2}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}\Tr((-1)^{N}e^{-t\Delta_{F}}-P)\,dt\right).

The
Fried conjecture
states
[
5
]
that if
\Delta_{F}
has trivial kernel, then
R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}
has a meromorphic extension to
\mathbb{C}
that is regular at zero, and

|R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(0)|=T(\nabla^{F}).

(1.4)

This was proved by Fried for the geodesic flow on hyperbolic manifolds
[
4
]
, and has since been proved in several other cases, see for example
[
1
,
2
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
31
,
32
]
. This includes versions for non-Anosov flows, such as geodesic flows on unit sphere bundles of compact manifolds whose sectional curvature is non-positive, but not everywhere negative. For such flows a more general version of the definition of the Ruelle
\zeta
-function is used, see for example Definition 2.7 in
[
25
]
.
The Fried conjecture does not hold in general,
see the end of Section 2 in
[
25
]
, and
[
23
,
30
]
.

In this paper, we define a Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function for flows
\varphi
satisfying an equivariant nondegeneracy condition, on manifolds
M
with proper actions by groups
G
such that
M/G
is compact. The basic idea is to replace flow curves
\gamma
satisfying
\gamma(l)=\gamma(0)
by flow curves
\gamma
satisfying

\gamma(l)=g\gamma(0)

(1.5)

for a fixed element
g\in G
.
We study several properties of such a
\zeta
-function, and verify an equivariant version of the Fried conjecture in some examples.

Construction and results

Let
M
,
\varphi
and
\nabla^{F}
be as before, but in this section we don’t assume
M
is compact and that
\varphi
is Anosov. Suppose that a unimodular, locally compact group

acts properly on
M
and that
M/G
is compact. Suppose
\varphi
is
G
-equivariant, and that the
group action lifts to

and preserves
\nabla^{F}
. Fix an element
g\in G
such that the quotient of

by the centraliser of

has a nonzero

-invariant Borel measure. The Anosov or weaker nondegeneracy condition on

is replaced by the assumption that for all flow curves
\gamma
such that (

) holds, the map

T_{\gamma(0)}\varphi_{l}\circ g^{-1}\colon T_{\gamma(0)}M\to T_{\gamma(0)}M

has one-dimensional eigenspace for eigenvalue
1
(spanned by
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
). Then we call the flow
g
-nondegenerate
.

As mentioned, the idea behind the definition of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function
R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}
is to replace closed flow curves in (

) by flow curves
\gamma
satisfying (
1.5
). We now also allow negative

1.5
) may not hold for any positive

, see Example 2 below Remark
2.3
. More importantly, allowing negative

seems to be essential for obtaining
a natural equivariant generalisation of Fried’s conjecture; see Remarks

and
4.3
.

There are straightforward generalisations of the maps

(defined in (

)) and
\rho(\gamma)
to such flow curves.
The appropriate generalisation of the primitive period
T^{\#}_{\gamma}
in (

) is less obvious, however, particularly for non-discrete groups. This factor

T^{\#}_{\gamma}

is replaced by a certain integral involving a cutoff function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
, see (
2.1
) and (
2.4
). The final definition of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function also involves an integral over the conjugacy class of
g
, making it conjugation-invariant. See Definition
2.7
for details.

We obtain an expression for the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function in terms of a distributional
flat
g
-trace

\Tr_{g}^{\flat}
introduced in
[
10
]
, see Definition
3.1
. With slight abuse of notation (treating distributions as functions), Theorem
3.4
is the equality

R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{g}(\sigma)=\exp\left(-\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\Tr_{g}^{\flat}\bigl((-1)^{N}N\varphi_{t}^{*}\bigr)\frac{e^{-\sigma|t|}}{|t|}\,dt\right).

(1.6)

The proof is based on Theorem
3.2
, an equivariant generalisation of Guillemin’s trace formula
[
7
]
, obtained in
[
10
]
.

Applications of and motivation for Theorem
3.4
include the following.

1.

It implies that the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function is independent of the cutoff function
\chi
used; see Proposition
3.6
.

2.

It implies a decomposition of the classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function for a flow on a compact manifold into equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-functions for the action by the fundamental group of the manifold on its universal cover; see Proposition
3.18
.

3.

As in the classical case, Theorem
3.4
may be a basis for proofs of further properties of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function, such as meromorphic continuation (see Section 2.2 in
[
6
]
, or
[
3
]
) and homotopy invariance (see Theorem 2 in
[
2
]
).

4.

As in the classical case, the expression (
1.6
) for the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function suggests similarities with equivariant analytic torsion; see Remark
3.5
.

Replacing the left hand side of (
1.4
) by the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function (with no absolute value), and the right hand side by the equivariant analytic torsion
T_{g}(\nabla^{F})
of
\nabla^{F}

[
9
]
, we obtain an equivariant version of Fried’s conjecture:

R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(0)=T_{g}(\nabla^{F})^{2}.

(1.7)

See Question
2.12
. If
M
is compact and
G
is trivial, then this reduces to the classical Fried conjecture (
1.4
). We do not expect (
1.7
) to hold in general, and only ask the question under what conditions it may be expected to hold. Analogously to the non-equivariant case, vanishing of the
L^{2}
-kernel of
\Delta_{F}
is a condition.

A relevant class of examples where the equivariant Fried conjecture does not hold is the action by the fundamental group of a compact manifold on its universal cover. Already in Fried’s proof of his conjecture for hyperbolic manifolds
[
4
]
, and also in later proofs of other cases (see the references below (
1.4
)), the strategy is to decompose both sides of (
1.4
) into factors corresponding to conjugacy classes in the fundamental group. But only the total expressions are equal, not the factors for individual conjugacy classes. This is easiest to see for the trivial conjugacy class. Then the corresponding factor in the expression for analytic torsion is
L^{2}
-analytic torsion
[
13
,
16
]
and is generally different from
1
, whereas the corresponding factor for the Ruelle
\zeta
-function is always
1
.

In several other cases, however, we compute the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function explicitly, and find that its value at zero equals equivariant analytic torsion. These cases are:

1.

the real line acting on itself, see Lemma
4.2
;

2.

the circle acting on itself, see Lemmas
4.6
and
4.8
;

3.

certain discrete subgroups of the Euclidean motion group acting on the unit sphere bundle of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with geodesic flow; see Proposition
5.15
.

For geodesic flow on the unit sphere bundle of the
n
-sphere, acted on by
\SO(n+1)
, we compute the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function in Proposition
6.10
for
n=2
and in Proposition
6.14
for
n=3
. In this case,
the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function does not extend meromorphically to zero, so the left hand side of
(
1.7
) is not defined. Now the kernel of the Laplacian
\Delta_{F}
is nonzero, so the conditions of the equivariant Fried conjecture are not satisfied.

For geodesic flow on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function equals
1
, since there are no closed geodesics. For geodesic flow on spheres, this function is not defined, as there are uncountably many closed geodesics. It may be of interest that the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function is defined, and nontrivial, in these cases, for suitable group elements.

As another relation between the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function and equivariant analytic torsion, we show that these quantities
in many cases have the same behaviour with respect to restriction to subgroups; see Lemma
3.10
.

These results, together with the suggestive similarities between the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function and equivariant analytic torsion in Remark
3.5
, make us wonder if general conditions can be formulated under which the equivariant Fried conjecture is true. We plan to investigate this in the future.

In forthcoming work by the first author and Pirie, we obtain positive results on the equivariant Fried conjecture for the suspension flow of an equivariant isometry of a Riemannian manifold.

2.2
An equivariant Fried conjecture

Suppose for now that
M
is a compact, oriented Riemannian manifold, and

is trivial. Let

In this setting, the Fried conjecture states that, for suitable classes of flows

\zeta

(2.8)

This conjecture has been proved in several cases, see for example

,
2
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]

The equality (

[

]
and
[

,
30
]
.

In
[
9
]
, we defined an equivariant version of analytic torsion for proper group actions with compact quotients. It is therefore natural to ask under what conditions an equivariant version of (
2.8
) holds.

To state this question more explicitly, we return to the equivariant setting considered before. We now also assume that
M
has an orientation and a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric, and that
F
has a
G
-invariant Hermitian metric preserved by
\nabla^{F}
.
Consider the Laplacian

\Delta_{F}^{p}:=(\nabla^{F})^{*}\nabla^{F}+\nabla^{F}(\nabla^{F})^{*}\colon\Omega^{p}(M;F)\to\Omega^{p}(M;F),

where we view
\nabla^{F}
as an operator on the space
\Omega^{*}(M;F)
of differential forms on
M
, twisted by
F
, and
(\nabla^{F})^{*}
is its formal adjoint, defined in the usual way in terms of an extension of the Hodge
*
-operator extended to differential forms twisted by
F
. Let
P_{p}
be the orthogonal projection from the Hilbert space of square integrable
p
-forms twisted by
F
onto the kernel of
\Delta_{F}^{p}
inside this space.

For a
G
-equivariant bounded operator
T
on the space of
L^{2}
-sections of a Hermitian
G
-vector bundle over
M
, we define its
g
-trace
as

\Tr_{g}(T):=\int_{G/Z}\Tr(hgh^{-1}T\chi)\,d(hZ),

(2.9)

if
T\chi
is trace-class and the integral converges. This trace has been used in various places besides
[
9
]
, see for example
[
11
,
12
,
14
,
29
]
.

Assuming that the following expressions converge, we write

\mathcal{T}_{g}(t):=\sum_{p=1}^{\dim(M)}(-1)^{p}p\Tr_{g}(e^{-t\Delta_{F}^{p}}-P_{p})

(2.10)

for
t>0
, and

T_{g}(\nabla^{F}):=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{s-1}\mathcal{T}_{g}(t)\,dt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-1}\mathcal{T}_{g}(t)\,dt\right).

(2.11)

In the first term, we use meromorphic continuation from
s
with large real part to
s=0
, again assuming the resulting expression is well-defined. Then
T_{g}(\nabla^{F})
is the
equivariant analytic torsion
of
\nabla^{F}
at
g
. For results on convergence and meromorphic extension of the integrals involved, see Section 3 of
[
9
]
. For independence of the Riemannian metric and other properties, see Sections 4 and 5 of
[
9
]
. For metric independence, a condition is that
\Delta_{F}^{p}
has trivial kernel for all
p
.

Question 2.12

(Equivariant Fried conjecture)
.

Suppose that
M
is odd-dimensional, and
\Delta_{F}^{p}
has trivial
L^{2}
-kernel for all
p
. Under what further conditions does

1.

the expression (
2.7
) for
R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)
converge when
\sigma
has large real part;

2.

this
have a meromorphic extension to the complex plane that is regular in a neighbourhood of zero; and

3.

the equality

R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(0)=T_{g}(\nabla^{F})^{2}

(2.12)

hold?

Remark 2.13
.

The square on the right hand side of (
2.12
), and also the squares in
Lemma
3.17
and Proposition
3.18
, can be avoided if one changes the definition of the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function by introducing a factor
1/2
in front of the integral in (
2.7
). However, the resulting square root that is taken may be an obstruction to meromorphic continuation of this function. For this reason, we have chosen not to include this factor
1/2
.

Remark 2.14
.

The definition of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function does not involve a Riemannian metric, but the definition of equivariant analytic torsion does. We expect equivariant analytic torsion to be independent of the choice of this metric, see Section 4 in
[
9
]
for results in this direction. Then the equality (
2.12
) for one
G
-invariant Riemannian metric implies this equality for all such metrics.

Example 2.15
.

In the classical setting, where
G
is trivial and
M
is compact, the left hand side of (
2.12
) is the absolute value squared of the classical Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function at zero by Lemma
3.17
, while the right hand side is the square of classical Ray–Singer analytic torsion. So in this case, the equality (
2.12
) is the usual Fried conjecture (
2.8
). Then the answer to Question
2.12
is
yes
in cases where the classical Fried conjecture was proved.

Slightly more generally, if
G
, and hence
M
, is compact, and
g=e
, then (
2.12
) is equivalent to the classical Fried conjecture.

Remark 2.16
.

In the non-equivariant case, analytic torsion is a positive number. This is consistent with the absolute value on the left-hand side of (
2.8
). In the equivariant case, analytic torsion need not be positive; see the examples in Sections
4
and
5
. Allowing negative
l
in the definition of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function lets us formulate the equivariant Fried conjecture (
2.12
) without absolute values. For a version of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-functions that only includes positive
l
, the analogue of (
2.12
) does not even hold with
absolute values on both sides in basic cases; see Remark
4.3
for an example.

Remark 2.17
.

In Question
2.12
, we assume that
M
is odd-dimensional, because the question then reduces to the classical Fried conjecture if
G
is trivial, as in Example
2.15
.

If
M
is even-dimensional, then by Lemma
3.17
, the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function for trivial groups becomes the square of the phase of the classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function, rather than the square of its absolute value. It then follows that (
2.12
) does not reduce to the classical Fried conjecture.
For
M
even-dimensional, as in the classical case, the equivariant analytic torsion of
M
is trivial by Proposition 5.6 in
[
9
]
, so the case where
M
is odd-dimensional is the most interesting. In the important example of the geodesic flow on the sphere bundle
M
on a Riemannian manifold, the manifold
M
is odd-dimensional.

Example 2.18
.

In several proofs of the Fried conjecture in different settings, both sides of (
2.8
) are decomposed as sums of terms corresponding to conjugacy classes in the fundamental group. The term in the decomposition of the Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function corresponding to a conjugacy class is an equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function by Lemma
3.19
, in the case of the geodesic flow on a negatively curved manifold. The terms on the two sides of the Fried conjecture corresponding to the same conjugacy class are not equal in general, even if the sums over all conjugacy classes are equal.

This is perhaps clearest for the trivial conjugacy class
\{e\}
. In the setting of Proposition
3.18
, the number
T_{e}(\nabla^{F_{X}})
is the
L^{2}
-analytic torsion
of
X
with respect to
\rho

[
13
,
16
]
. This is generally different from
1
. However, the lift of a suitable flow on
X
to
M
may not have any closed loops, so that
R^{e}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}
is the constant
1
. In fact, in Fried’s proof of his conjecture in the case of hyperbolic manifolds
[
4
]
, this difference exactly compensates for a functional relation satisfied by certain
\zeta
-functions related to
R_{\varphi_{X},\rho}
, see (FE) and (FE)’ on page 531 of
[
4
]
.
In such cases, the answer to Question
2.12
is
no
for the action by
G=\pi_{1}(X)
on

, for
g=e
and at least one other conjugacy class.

For examples where the answer to Question
2.12
is
yes
, see Sections
4
and
5
. For geodesic flow on the two- and three-spheres, discussed in Section

, the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function is defined for

with large real parts for suitable group elements, but does not continue meromorphically to

.
So
the equality (

3
Properties of the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function

3.1
An equivariant Guillemin trace formula

We recall the equivariant version of Guillemin’s trace formula obtained in
[
10
]
. This is
Theorem
3.2
, which involves the distributional
flat
g
-trace
introduced in
[
10
]
.
We then use this to express the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function in terms of this flat
g
-trace, see Theorem
3.4
below. That expression is then used to prove independence of the Ruelle
\zeta
-function of choices made in its definition, and a relation with the classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function (Propositions
3.6
and
3.18
).

We recall the situation of Section 2.1 of
[
10
]
.
Let
E\to M
be a
G
-equivariant vector bundle. We fix a
G
-invariant nonvanishing density, and use it to identify the space
\Gamma^{-\infty}(E)
of generalised sections with the continuous dual of
\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E^{*})
. (And to make similar idenfications for other vector bundles.) Let
\kappa\in\Gamma^{-\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})
be the Schwartz kernel of a
G
-equivariant, continuous linear operator
T\colon\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
. If
x\in G
, then we write
x\cdot\kappa
for the Schwartz kernel of
x\circ T
. Let
p_{2}\colon M\times M\to M
be the projection onto the second factor. Let
\Delta(M)\subset M\times M
be the diagonal. We have a fibrewise trace
\tr
of elements of
\Gamma^{-\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*}|_{\Delta(M)})
, see Definition 2.1 in
[
10
]
. Recall that we have chosen a cutoff function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
as in (
2.1
).

Definition 3.1
.

The operator
T
is
flat
g
-trace class
if

1.

for all
h\in G
, the wave front set
\WF(hgh^{-1}\cdot\kappa)
is disjoint from the conormal bundle to
\Delta(M)\subset M\times M
; and

2.

the integral

\int_{G/Z}\bigl\langle\tr(hgh^{-1}\cdot\kappa)|_{\Delta(M)},(p_{2}^{*}f)|_{\Delta(M)}\bigr\rangle\,d(hZ)

converges for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
, and defines a distribution on
M
.

In that case, the
flat
g
-trace
of
T
is

\Tr_{g}^{\flat}(T):=\int_{G/Z}\bigl\langle\tr(hgh^{-1}\cdot\kappa)|_{\Delta(M)},(p_{2}^{*}\chi)|_{\Delta(M)}\bigr\rangle\,d(hZ).

This definition is independent of
\chi
by Proposition 2.9 in
[
10
]
.

Now let
\Phi
be a
G
-equivariant flow in
E
that lifts
\varphi
. If
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
, then we define the operator
\Phi_{\psi}^{*}
on
\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
by

(\Phi_{\psi}^{*}s)(m):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)\Phi_{-t}s(\varphi_{t}(m))\,dt,

for
s\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E)
and
m\in M
. Let
A\in\End(E)
be
G
-equivariant, and suppose that it commutes with
\Phi
.
If
A\Phi^{*}_{\psi}
is flat
g
-trace class for
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
, then we write

\langle\Tr_{g}^{\flat}(A\Phi^{*}),\psi\rangle:=\Tr_{g}^{\flat}(A\Phi^{*}_{\psi}).

(3.1)

If
x\in G
,
l\in L_{x}(\varphi)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{l}^{x}(\varphi)
, then we write

\tr(Ax\Phi_{-l}|_{\gamma}):=\tr(Ax(\Phi_{-l})_{\gamma(0)}).

Theorem 3.2

(Equivariant Guillemin trace formula)
.

Suppose that the flow
\varphi
is
g
-nondegenerate. Then the operator
A\Phi^{*}_{\psi}
is flat
g
-trace class for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
. Furthermore, (
3.1
) defines a distribution on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, equal to

\Tr^{\flat}_{g}(A\Phi^{*})=\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{hgh^{-1}}_{l}(\varphi)}\frac{\tr\bigl(Ahgh^{-1}\Phi_{-l}|_{\gamma}\bigr)}{|\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}})|}T_{\gamma}\delta_{l}\,d(hZ).

(3.2)

Here
\delta_{l}
is the Dirac
\delta
distribution at
l
.

This result is Theorem 2.28 in
[
10
]
.

3.2
An expression for the equivariant Ruellle dynamical
\zeta
-function

We will use some basic linear algebra.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space, and
A\in\End(V)
. If
\dim(\ker(1-A))=1
, then

\sum_{j=1}^{\dim(V)}(-1)^{j}j\tr(\wedge^{j}A)=-\det((1-A)|_{V/\ker(1-A)}).

(3.3)

Proof.

For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sum_{j=1}^{\dim(V)}(-1)^{j}\tr(\wedge^{j}tA)=\det(1-tA).

(3.4)

The derivative at
t=1
of the left hand side is the left hand side of (
3.3
). If
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\dim(V)}
are the eigenvalues of
A
, repeated by multiplicities, then the derivative at
t=1
of the right hand side of (
3.4
) is

-\sum_{j=1}^{\dim(V)}\lambda_{j}\prod_{k\not=j}(1-\lambda_{k}).

If exactly one of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
equals
1
, then this is the right hand side of (
3.3
).
∎

Recall the notation
\tau^{\nabla^{F}}
for parallel transport with respect to
\nabla^{F}
in Definition
2.1
(e).
Define the flow
\Phi
on
\textstyle{\bigwedge}T^{*}M\otimes F
lifting
\varphi
by

\Phi_{t}(\omega\otimes v)=(\wedge T_{\varphi_{t}(m)}\varphi_{-t})^{*}\omega\otimes\tau^{\nabla^{F}}_{t}v,

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in M
,
\omega\in\textstyle{\bigwedge}T^{*}_{m}M
and
v\in F_{m}
.
Let
N
be the number operator on
\textstyle{\bigwedge}T^{*}M
, acting as the scalar
p
on
\textstyle{\bigwedge}^{p}T^{*}M
. As before, we assume that
\varphi
is
g
-nondegenerate.

Consider the smooth function
\psi_{\sigma}(t)=e^{-\sigma|t|}/|t|
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
. It is not compactly supported, so a priori the pairing
\langle\Tr_{g}^{\flat}((-1)^{N}N\Phi^{*}),\psi_{\sigma}\rangle
is not defined. We define this number, if it exists, by the property that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there is an
r>0
such that for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
with values in
[0,1]
, for which
\psi(t)=1
if
\frac{1}{r}<|t|<r
, we have

\left|\langle\Tr_{g}^{\flat}((-1)^{N}N\Phi^{*}),\psi_{\sigma}\rangle-\langle\Tr_{g}^{\flat}((-1)^{N}N\Phi^{*}),\psi\psi_{\sigma}\rangle\right|<\varepsilon.

The following fact is an equivariant generalisation of a well-known expression for the Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function in terms of the flat trace. See e.g. (5.4) in
[
2
]
, (2.5) in
[
3
]
or (6.17) in
[
25
]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
L_{g}(\varphi)
is countable. Then the pairing

\langle\Tr_{g}^{\flat}((-1)^{N}N\Phi^{*}),\psi_{\sigma}\rangle

is well-defined if and only if (
2.7
) converges, and then

R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{g}(\sigma)=\exp\left(-\langle\Tr_{g}^{\flat}((-1)^{N}N\Phi^{*}),\psi_{\sigma}\rangle\right).

(3.5)

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.3
that for all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
,
h\in G
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{l}^{hgh^{-1}}(\varphi)
,

\begin{split}\tr((-1)^{N}Nhgh^{-1}\Phi_{-l}|_{\gamma})&=\tr\bigl((-1)^{N}N(\wedge T_{\gamma(0)}hg^{-1}h^{-1}\varphi_{l})^{*}\bigr)\tr((hgh^{-1}\circ\tau^{\nabla}_{-l})_{\gamma(0)})\\
&=-\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}})\tr(\rho_{hgh^{-1}}(\gamma)).\end{split}

(3.6)

Let
r>0
be such that
\pm r\not\in L_{g}(\varphi)
, and
|l|>1/|r|
for all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
.
Let
\psi_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
be such that
\psi_{r}(t)=1
if
\frac{1}{r}<|t|<r
, and
\psi(l)=0
for all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\setminus[-r,r]
.

From (
3.6
),
Theorem
3.2
implies that

\langle\Tr_{g}^{\flat}((-1)^{N}N\Phi^{*}),\psi_{r}\psi_{\sigma}\rangle=\\
-\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\cap[-r,r]}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{hgh^{-1}}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{hgh^{-1}}(\gamma))T_{\gamma}\,d(hZ).

The theorem follows in the limit
r\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The equality (
3.5
) may be written suggestively as

\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{g}(\sigma)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\Tr_{g}^{\flat}\bigl((-1)^{N}N\Phi_{t}^{*}\bigr)\frac{e^{-\sigma|t|}}{|t|}\,dt.

(3.7)

If
\Delta_{F}
has trivial kernel, then (
2.11
) means that
2\log T_{g}(\nabla^{F})
is a regularisation of the divergent integral

-\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr_{g}\bigl((-1)^{N}Ne^{-t\Delta_{F}}\bigr)\frac{1}{t}\,dt.

(3.8)

The superficial similarity between (
3.7
) and (
3.8
)
is an equivariant generalisation of an analogous similarity in the classical case, compare for example (1.8) and (6.18) in
[
25
]
. This may be viewed as evidence
that an equivariant Fried conjecture (
2.12
) may hold in certain cases.

The individual numbers
T_{\gamma}
in (
2.7
) depend on the function
\chi
in general, but Theorem
3.4
implies that the expression (
2.7
) does not.

Proposition 3.6
.

If the expression (
2.7
) converges, then it does not depend on the choices
of representatives of classes in the sets
\Gamma_{l}^{hgh^{-1}}(\varphi)
, the intervals
I_{\gamma}
in (
2.4
), or
the function
\chi
satisfying (
2.1
) for all
m
.

Proof.

The right hand side of (
3.5
) does not involve choices of representatives of classes in the sets
\Gamma_{l}^{hgh^{-1}}(\varphi)
, or intervals
I_{\gamma}
as in (
2.4
). And
Proposition 2.9 in
[
10
]
states that the flat
g
-trace, and hence the right hand side of (
3.5
), does not depend on
\chi
.
∎

3.3
Alternative expressions

The integrand in (
2.7
) is clearly invariant under right multiplication of
h
by an element of
Z
, so that it is well-defined as a function on the quotient
G/Z
. We reformulate (
2.7
) in a way that makes this right
Z
-invariance property less obvious, but which can be easier to evaluate in practice.

Lemma 3.7
.

The limit

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\cap[-r,r]}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(h\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(hZ)

(3.9)

converges if and only if the right hand side of (
2.7
) does, and then the two are equal.

Proof.

Let
h\in G
.
By
G
-equivariance of
\varphi
and
\nabla^{F}
,

\begin{split}T_{h\gamma(0)}(\varphi_{l}\circ hg^{-1}h^{-1})&=T_{\gamma(0)}h\circ T_{\gamma(0)}(\varphi_{l}\circ g^{-1})\circ T_{h\gamma(0)}h^{-1}\\
\rho_{hgh^{-1}}(h\gamma)&=h\circ\rho_{g}(\gamma)\circ h^{-1}.\end{split}

These equalities imply that

\begin{split}\det(1-P_{h\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}})&=\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\\
\tr(\rho_{hgh^{-1}}(h\gamma))&=\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma)),\end{split}

respectively. Together with (
2.6
) and the fact that
L_{hgh^{-1}}(\varphi)=L_{g}(\varphi)
, these imply the claim.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

It is immediate from Lemma
3.7
that
if the integral over
G/Z
in (
3.9
) may be interchanged with the sums over
L_{g}(\varphi)
and
\Gamma_{l}^{g}(\varphi)
, then

R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{g}(\sigma)=\\
\exp\left(\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))T^{g}_{\gamma}\right),

(3.10)

where

T^{g}_{\gamma}:=\int_{G/Z}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(h\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(hZ)

plays the role of a
g
-delocalised primitive period of
\gamma
. Here a Borel section of
G\to G/Z
is used, and the total expression (
3.10
) does not depend on this section.
This is true in particular if (
2.7
) converges,
L_{g}(\varphi)
is finite, and
\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)
is finite for all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
.

The definition of the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function involving
the limit
r\to\infty
in (
2.7
) is to ensure that Theorem
3.4
holds. This then implies various other properties of the Ruelle function, such as independence of the choices made (see Proposition
3.6
). In certain cases, however, a more direct definition is possible.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that either

(a)

L_{g}(\varphi)
is finite; or

(b)

g
is central in
G
; or

(c)

M
and
G
are compact; or

(d)

G/Z
is compact, the number

\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{l}^{g}(\varphi);l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\cap[-r,r]\}

grows at most exponentially in
r
, and there is a
C>0
such that for all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
,
\gamma\in\Gamma_{l}^{g}(\varphi)
and
h\in G
,

\left|\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(h\gamma(s))\,ds\right|\leq C|l|.

Then for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
for which any of these expressions converge,

\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{g}(\sigma)=\\
\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{hgh^{-1}}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{hgh^{-1}}(\gamma))T_{\gamma}\,d(hZ)\\
=\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(h\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(hZ).

(3.11)

Proof.

Cases (a) and (b) follow directly from the definition and Lemma
3.7
.

If
G
(and hence
M
) is compact, then we may take
\chi
to be the constant function
1
. Then the right hand sides of (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) both equal

\vol({G/Z})\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))T^{\#}_{\gamma}.

So case (c) follows by (the proof of) Lemma
3.7
.

In case (d), the right hand side of (
3.11
) converges absolutely, and the claim follows by Lemma
3.7
and dominated convergence.
∎

3.4
Restriction to subgroups

The equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function behaves naturally with respect to restriction to cocompact subgroups. This is analogous to the corresponding property of equivariant analytic torsion, Proposition 2.4 in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
H<G
be a cocompact, unimodular, closed subgroup containing
g
. Let
Z_{H}:=Z\cap H
be the centraliser of
g
in
H
, and suppose that
H/Z_{H}
has an
H
-invariant measure.
The expression (
2.7
) for fixed
r
converges absolutely if and only if the same expression for the restricted action by
H
on
M
converges absolutely. If this is true for all
r>0
, then the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function
R^{g,H}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}
for the action by
H
is related to the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function for the action by
G
by

R^{g,H}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}=(R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}})^{\vol(Z/Z_{H})}.

(3.12)

Here the volume
\vol(Z/Z_{H})
is computed with respect to the measure compatible with the Haar measures on
G
and
H
and the given measures on
G/Z
and
H/Z_{H}
.

If
G
is compact, then the same statement is true with absolute convergence replaced by convergence.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G)
be such that for all
x\in G
,

\int_{H}\psi(xh^{-1})\,dh=1.

Define
\chi_{H}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
by

\chi_{H}(m):=\int_{G}\psi(x)\chi(xm)\,dx

for
m\in M
.
Then (
2.1
) holds for all
m\in M
, with
\chi
replaced by
\chi_{H}
and
G
replaced by
H
.

Let
r>0
.
By right
Z
-invariance of the integrand,

\int_{G/Z}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\cap[-r,r]}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(h\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(hZ)\\
=\frac{1}{\vol(Z/Z_{H})}\int_{G/{Z_{H}}}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\cap[-r,r]}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(x\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(xZ_{H}).

This expression converges absolutely if and only if the following expression does, and then the two are equal:

\frac{1}{\vol(Z/Z_{H})}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\cap[-r,r]}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))\int_{G/Z_{H}}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(x\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(xZ_{H}).

(3.13)

For all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)
,

\int_{G/Z_{H}}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(x\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(xZ_{H})=\int_{H/Z_{H}}\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi_{H}(h\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(hZ_{H}).

(3.14)

The maps
P_{\gamma}^{g}
and
\rho_{g}(\gamma)
are the same for the actions by
G
and
H
. So by Lemma
3.7
,

\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{g,H}(\sigma)=\\
\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{H/Z_{H}}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)\cap[-r,r]}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi_{H}(h\gamma(s))\,ds\,d(hZ_{H}).

(3.15)

By (
3.14
), the right hand side of (
3.15
) converges absolutely if and only if (
3.13
) does, and then
(
3.13
)
equals
1/\vol(Z/Z_{H})
times (
3.15
). This implies the claim for possibly noncompact
G
.

If
G
, and hence
M
and
H
, is compact, then we may use constant cutoff functions
\chi=1/\vol(G)
and
\chi_{H}=1/\vol(H)
(these volumes may be normalised to
1
). Then Lemma
3.7
and Example 2 below Remark
2.3
imply that, whenever the following expressions converge,

\begin{split}\log R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)&=\frac{\vol(G/Z)}{\vol(G)}\sum_{l\in L_{g}(\varphi)}\frac{e^{-|l|\sigma}}{|l|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{g}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))T_{\gamma}^{\#}\\
&=\frac{\vol(G/Z)}{\vol(G)}\frac{\vol(H)}{\vol(H/Z_{H})}\log R^{g,H}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)\\
&=\frac{1}{\vol(Z/Z_{H})}\log R^{g,H}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma),\end{split}

which implies the claim.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

If
G
and
M
are compact,
g=e
, and
\varphi
is Anosov, then the last claim in Lemma
3.10
and the exponential bound on the number of flow curves with periods less than a given value
[

]
imply that (
2.7
) converges for
\sigma
with large real part.

Remark 3.12
.

If
G
is noncompact, then absolute convergence is important in Lemma
3.10
. See Remark
5.12
for an example where convergence is not absolute and the conclusion of Lemma
3.10
does not hold.

In the setting of
Lemma
3.10
, the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function has the same behaviour with respect to restriction to cocompact subgroups as equivariant analytic torsion, see Proposition 2.4 in
[
9
]
. This has the following immediate consequence.

Proposition 3.13
.

In the setting of Lemma
3.10
, the answer to Question
2.12
is
yes
for the action by
G
on
M
if and only if the answer is
yes
for the action by the cocompact subgroup
H
.

Example 3.14
.

If
G
is compact, then Question
2.12
is equivalent to the same question for the action by the closure of the subgroup generated by
g
.

Example 3.15
.

Suppose that
G
is a Lie group,
K<G
is maximal compact, and

is discrete and cocompact, and contains

. Suppose that

.
If the equivariant Ruelle

3.5
Relations with the classical Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function

Suppose for now that
G=\{e\}
is trivial and
M
is compact and orientable. (We will drop this assumption again in the text leading up to Proposition
3.18
.) Now
\chi\equiv 1
.
For
l\in L_{e}(\varphi)
, we write

H(l):=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{e}_{l}(\varphi)}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{e})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{e}(\gamma))T_{\gamma}.

We fix a Hermitian metric on
F
, and say that
\rho_{e}
is unitary if
\rho_{e}(\gamma)
is unitary for all
\gamma\in\Gamma^{e}_{l}(\varphi)
.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
l\in L_{e}(\varphi)
. Then
-l\in L_{e}(\varphi)
.
If
\varphi
is orientation-preserving and
\rho_{e}
is unitary, then

H(-l)=(-1)^{\dim(M)-1}\overline{H(l)}.

Proof.

The first claim follows from the fact that for all
m\in M
and
l\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\varphi_{l}(m)=m
if and only if
\varphi_{-l}(m)=m
.
This also shows that for all
l\in L_{e}(\varphi)
, the sets
\Gamma_{l}^{e}(\varphi)
and
\Gamma_{-l}^{e}(\varphi)
are equal.

Recall that the maps
P_{\gamma}^{e}
and
\rho_{e}(\gamma)
depend on
l
, if
\gamma\in\Gamma_{l}^{e}(\varphi)
. Fix
l\in L_{e}(\varphi)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{l}^{e}(\varphi)=\Gamma_{-l}^{e}(\varphi)
. We write
(P_{\gamma}^{e})_{\pm l}
for the map
P_{\gamma}^{e}
, and
\rho_{e}(\gamma)_{\pm l}
for the map
\rho_{e}(\gamma)
, associated to
\gamma
when viewed as an element of
\Gamma_{\pm l}^{e}(\varphi)
. Then

\begin{split}(P_{\gamma}^{e})_{-l}&=(P_{\gamma}^{e})_{l}^{-1};\\
\rho_{e}(\gamma)_{-l}&=\rho_{e}(\gamma)_{l}^{-1}.\end{split}

If
\rho_{e}
is unitary, then the second equality implies that

\tr(\rho_{e}(\gamma)_{-l})=\overline{\tr(\rho_{e}(\gamma)_{l})}.

(3.16)

Now for any invertible endomorphism
A
of a finite-dimensional vector space
V
,

\sgn\det(I-A^{-1})=(-1)^{\dim(V)}\sgn(\det(A))\sgn\det(I-A).

We apply this to
A=(P_{\gamma}^{e})_{l}
,
and note that this map has positive determinant because
T\varphi_{l}
preserves the orientation on the quotient of
TM
by the span of the image of
u
. This yields

\sgn\det(I-(P^{e}_{\gamma})_{-l})=(-1)^{\dim(M)-1}\sgn\det(I-(P^{e}_{\gamma})_{l}).

The claim follows from this equality, (
3.16
), and the fact that
T_{\gamma}
does not depend on
l
.
∎

We write
L^{+}(\varphi):=L_{e}(\varphi)\cap(0,\infty)
. The classical Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function
R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}
for Anosov flows is defined by

R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma):=\exp\left(\sum_{l\in L^{+}(\varphi)}\frac{e^{l\sigma}}{l}H(l)\right),

for
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
for which the expression converges. (See also Example 2 below Remark
2.3
.)

Lemma 3.17
.

If
\varphi
is orientation-preserving and
\rho_{e}
is unitary, then on the real line,

R^{e}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}|^{2}&\text{if $\dim(M)$ is odd};\\
\left(\frac{R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}}{|R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}|}\right)^{2}&\text{if $\dim(M)$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

(3.17)

This equality means that the left hand side converges at
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if the right hand side does, and then the two are equal.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
.
By Lemma
3.16
,

\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{e}(\sigma)=\sum_{l\in L^{+}(\varphi)}\frac{e^{-l\sigma}}{l}\left(H(l)+(-1)^{\dim(M)-1}\overline{H(l)}\right).

(3.18)

Suppose that
\sigma
is real. If
\dim(M)
is odd, then (
3.18
) becomes

\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{e}(\sigma)=2\Real\bigl(\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)\bigr).

(3.19)

If
\dim(M)
is even, then (
3.18
) becomes

\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{e}(\sigma)=2i\Imag\bigl(\log R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)\bigr).

(3.20)

The equalities (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) can be rewritten as (
3.17
).
∎

There is a more interesting and useful relation between the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function and its classical counterpart than Lemma
3.17
.
Suppose that
X
is a compact manifold, and let
M
be the universal cover of
X
. Let

F_{X}=M\times_{\rho}\mathbb{C}^{r}\to X
be the flat vector bundle associated to a representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\to\GL(r,\mathbb{C})
.
Consider the trivial connection
\nabla^{F}=d
on
F=M\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
.

Let
\varphi_{X}
be a flow on
X
, and
\varphi
its lift to a
\Gamma
-equivariant flow on
M
.
Let
R_{\varphi_{X},\rho}
be the classical Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function associated to
\varphi_{X}
and
\rho
.

Proposition 3.18
.

On the real line,

\prod_{(g)}R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|R_{\varphi_{X},\rho}|^{2}&\text{if $\dim(M)$ is odd};\\
\left(\frac{R_{\varphi_{X},\rho}}{|R_{\varphi_{X},\rho}(\sigma)|}\right)^{2}&\text{if $\dim(M)$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

(3.21)

Here
\prod_{(g)}
is the product over all conjugacy classes
(g)\subset\pi_{1}(X)
, and the equality means that the left hand side converges at
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if the right hand side does, and then the two are equal.

Proof.

Let
N_{X}
be the number operator on
\textstyle{\bigwedge}^{*}T^{*}X
.
Proposition 2.16 in
[
10
]
implies that

\Tr^{\flat}((-1)^{N_{X}}N_{X}\Phi_{X}^{*})=\sum_{(\gamma)}\Tr^{\flat}_{\gamma}((-1)^{N}N\Phi^{*}).

(3.22)

Let
\nabla^{F_{X}}
be the connection on
F_{X}
induced by
\nabla^{F}=d
. Then
by Theorem
3.4
and (
3.22
),

R_{\varphi_{X},\nabla^{F_{X}}}^{e}=\prod_{(\gamma)}R_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}^{\gamma}.

Hence the claim follows from Lemma
3.17
.
∎

3.6
The classical decomposition of the Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function for geodesic flow

The classical Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function for geodesic flows on the sphere bundle of a compact, oriented, negatively curved Riemannian manifold
X
can be decomposed into contributions from different conjugacy classes of the fundamental group of
X
, analogously to Proposition
3.18
. These contributions are equal to the relevant equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-functions, as we now discuss.

Let
X
be a compact, oriented Riemannian manifold with negative sectional curvature. Let
\varphi_{X}
be the geodesic flow on the unit sphere bundle
S(TX)
. Let
\tilde{X}
be the universal cover of
X
. Let
\varphi
be the lift of
\varphi_{X}
to
M=S(T\tilde{X})
; this is the geodesic flow on the universal cover. Consider the natural action by
G=\pi_{1}(X)
on
M
. Let
g\in G
be nontrivial. From the assumption that
X
has negative sectional curvature, it follows that there is a unique geodesic
\gamma
in
X
in the conjugacy class of
g
. Let
l
be the period of
\gamma
, and let

T^{\#}_{\gamma}=\min\{t>0;\gamma(t)=\gamma(0)\}

be the primitive period of
\gamma
.

Let
\rho\colon G\to\U(r)
be a unitary representation. Consider the trivial vector bundle
M\times\mathbb{C}^{r}\to M
, on which
G
acts by

x\cdot(m,v)=(xm,\rho(x)v),

for
x\in G
,
m\in M
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
. Let
\nabla^{F}=d\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}
be the natural flat connection on
F
.

Lemma 3.19
.

For all
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
,

R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)=\exp\left(\frac{e^{-l\sigma}}{l}\tr(\rho(g))T^{\#}_{\gamma}\right).

(3.23)

Proof.

In this case
L_{g}(\varphi)=\{l\}
, so (
3.11
) holds.
Then

\bigcup_{h\in G}\Gamma_{l}^{hgh^{-1}}(\varphi)=\{\gamma\}.

As
X
is oriented,

\sgn(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{hgh^{-1}}))=1

(3.24)

for all
h\in G
. Because
parallel transport with respect to
\nabla^{F}
is trivial, we have

\rho_{hgh^{-1}}(\gamma)=\rho(hgh^{-1})

for all
h
, so

\tr(\rho_{hgh^{-1}}(\gamma))=\tr(\rho(g)).

(3.25)

The primitive period
T_{\gamma}
a priori may depend on the choice of the cutoff function
\chi
. As the left hand side of (
3.23
) does not depend on
\chi
, by Proposition
3.6
, we are free to choose this function in a suitable way.
We choose
\chi
to be an approximation of the indicator function of a suitable fundamental domain
Y\subset M
, in the following way.

The subset
\gamma([0,T^{\#}_{g}))\subset M
has the property that for all
m\in\gamma([0,T^{\#}_{g}))
and
x\in G\setminus\{e\}
, the point
xm
lies outside
\gamma([0,T^{\#}_{g}))
. Therefore, the set
\gamma([0,T^{\#}_{g}))
is contained in a fundamental domain
Y
for the action by
G
on
M
. We choose such a fundamental domain
Y
such that

1.

\gamma(\mathbb{R})\cap Y=\gamma([0,T^{\#}_{g}))
; and

2.

the closure of
Y
equals the closure of the interior of
Y
.

By the second property, the indicator function
1_{Y}
can be approximated by compactly supported smooth functions arbitrarily close in the
L^{1}
-norm. As
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
approaches
1_{Y}
in the
L^{1}
-norm,

T_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(\gamma(s))\,ds\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{Y}(\gamma(s))\,ds=T^{\#}_{\gamma}.

(3.26)

By (
3.24
), (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), the left hand side of (
3.23
) equals
the right hand side of (
3.23
).
∎

Remark 3.20
.

Lemma
3.19
means that
R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}
is the contribution to the classical Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function associated to the conjugacy class of
g
, see for example Definition 5.4 in
[
24
]
. This can be used to prove Proposition
3.18
in the case of geodesic flow.

4
The line and the circle

In the rest of this paper, we compute the equivariant Ruelle dynamical
\zeta
-function in several classes of examples. We will see that this function is defined for some flows for which the classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function is not defined. We will also see that the equivariant Fried equality (
2.12
) holds in the examples where both sides are defined.

We start with the most basic, one-dimensional examples: the line and the circle.
We will then show that the equivariant Fried conjecture is true, by explicit computations.

4.1
The real line

Consider the action of
G=\mathbb{R}
on
M=\mathbb{R}
by addition, and the flow

\varphi_{t}(x)=x+t

on
\mathbb{R}
. Consider the trivial line bundle
F=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
\alpha\in i\mathbb{R}
, and consider the connection
\nabla^{F}:=d+\alpha dx
on
F
. We let
\mathbb{R}
act trivially on the fibres of
F
.

Remark 4.1
.

One obtains the same result for the trivial connection
d
and the nontrivial action by
\mathbb{R}
on
F
given by

g\cdot(x,z)=(x+g,e^{\alpha g}z),

for
g,x\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. See Remark 6.9 in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
g\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, then for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
,

R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}=\exp\left(\frac{e^{\alpha g-|g|\sigma}}{|g|}\right),

(4.1)

and

R^{0}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}=1.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the unique flow curve of
\varphi
modulo time shifts:

\gamma(t)=t.

Suppose first that
g\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
L_{g}(\varphi)=\{g\}
, and
\Gamma^{g}_{g}(\varphi)=\{\gamma\}
. So (
3.11
) implies that

R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}=\exp\left(\frac{e^{-|g|\sigma}}{|g|}\sgn\bigl(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g})\bigr)\tr(\rho_{g}(\gamma))T_{\gamma}\right).

Now
u(x)^{\perp}=\{0\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, so the flow is nondegenerate, and

\sgn(\det(1-P^{g}_{\gamma}))=1.

For all
a<b
,
parallel transport with respect to
\nabla^{F}
along
\gamma|_{[a,b]}
is given by

z\mapsto e^{(b-a)\alpha}z,

for
z\in\mathbb{C}=F_{a}
. So

\rho_{g}(\gamma)z=e^{\alpha g}.

Now
T_{\gamma}=1
by Example 2 below Remark
2.3
, so
(
4.1
) follows.

For
g=0
, we have
L_{0}(\varphi)=\emptyset
, which implies (
4.2
).
∎

Proposition 6.2 in
[
9
]
states that the equivariant analytic torsion of
\nabla^{F}
equals

T_{g}(\nabla^{F})=\exp\left(\frac{e^{\alpha g}}{2|g|}\right),

(4.3)

for
g\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, while

T_{0}(\nabla^{F})=1.

It follows by Lemma
4.2
that the answer to Question
2.12
is
yes
in this example, and the equivariant Fried conjecture holds.

Remark 4.3
.

The two sides of (
2.12
) are generally not real in this example. The same is true in other cases; see Lemma
4.6
and Proposition
5.11
. This shows that it is desirable not to have absolute values in (
2.12
).

A version of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function involving only positive
l
would be equal to
1
for negative
g
in this example, see Example 2 below Remark
2.3
. Because equivariant analytic torsion (
4.3
) is different from
1
for such
g
, the equivariant Fried conjecture would not hold for such a definition of the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function. The two sides of (
2.12
) do not even have the same absolute value in that case.

4.2
The circle

The example in this subsection generalises Example 2.10 in
[
25
]
.

We will use the following fact from complex analysis.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, and
\alpha\in i\mathbb{R}
. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
with positive real part, define

F(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{e^{\alpha(n+r)-|n+r|z}}{|n+r|}.

Then for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\pm\alpha+2\pi i\mathbb{Z})
, there is a neighbourhood of
z
to which
F
continues analytically. In particular, if
\alpha\not\in 2\pi i\mathbb{Z}
, then
F
has an analytic continuation to a neighbourhood of zero.

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
r\in(0,1)
.
If
\Real(z)>0
, then

F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(n+r)(\alpha-z)}}{n+r}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(-n+r)(\alpha+z)}}{-n+r}+\frac{e^{r(\alpha+z)}}{r}.

(4.4)

The last term is analytic, so it is enough to show that the two series on the right hand side have the desired properties. These series can be written in terms of the hypergeometric function
{{}_{2}F_{1}}
. Using the Pochhammer symbol
(w)_{n}=w\cdot(w+1)\cdots(w+n-1)
, and
(w)_{0}=1
, we have for all integers
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\frac{(1)_{n}(r)_{n}}{(r+1)_{n}}\frac{1}{n!};

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{-n+r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\frac{(1)_{n}(-r)_{n}}{(-r+1)_{n}}\frac{1}{n!}.

(4.6)

The equality (
4.5
) implies that the first series on the right hand side of (
4.4
) equals

\frac{e^{r(\alpha-z)}}{r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{n}(r)_{n}}{(r+1)_{n}}\frac{\bigl(e^{\alpha-z}\bigr)^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{r(\alpha-z)}}{r}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,r;r+1;e^{\alpha-z}).

Similarly, (
4.6
) implies that the second series on the right hand side of (
4.4
) equals

\frac{e^{r(\alpha+z)}}{r}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,-r;-r+1;e^{-\alpha-z}).

The function
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;\relbar)
has an analytic continuation to a neighbourhood of any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}
; this is a standard fact, see e.g. Section 5.2 of
[
28
]
. This implies the claim. We point out that
the condition
\Real(c-a-b)>0
for the function
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;\relbar)
to extend to
z=1
does not apply in our situation, as
c-a-b=0
for both combinations of
a
,
b
and
c
that occur.
∎

We consider the action by the circle
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
on itself by left multiplication, and the flow
\varphi
given by

\varphi_{t}(x+\mathbb{Z})=x+t+\mathbb{Z}

for
x,t\in\mathbb{R}
.
We consider the connection
\nabla^{F}=d+\alpha dx
on the trivial line bundle
F\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, with the trivial action by

5
Geodesic flow on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

The classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function is trivial for the geodesic flow on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S^{n-1}=S(T\mathbb{R}^{n})
, because there are no closed geodesics. We show that this flow is
g
-nondegenerate
for the action by the Euclidean motion group on
S(T\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for group elements
g
satisfying a regularity property. The expression for the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function diverges, however. We show that for certain discrete subgroups of the Euclidean motion group, the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function does converge, and we compute it explicitly.
In that case, there are no issues with convergence and meromorphic continuation, because the sums that occur are finite. We conclude in Proposition
5.15
that the equivariant Fried conjecture is true in this case.

5.1
Geodesic flow on
S(T\mathbb{R}^{n})

Consider the natural action by the oriented Euclidean motion group
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\SO(n)
on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, given by

(y,r)\cdot x=rx+y

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r\in\SO(n)
. Let
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S^{n-1}
, viewed as the unit sphere bundle of

.
For the rest of this section, the letters
x
and
y
denote elements of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

denotes an element of
S^{n-1}
,

an element of
\SO(n)
and

and

The lift of the action to

is given by

(y,r)\cdot(x,v)=(rx+y,rv).

The exponential map of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\exp_{x}(v)=x+v.

So the geodesic flow

on

\varphi_{t}(x,v)=\bigl(\exp_{x}(tv),\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}\exp_{x}(sv)\bigr)=(x+tv,v).

The generating vector field

(5.1)

Consistently with Example 1 on page

\varphi_{t}((y,r)(x,v))=(r(x+tv)+y,rv)=(y,r)\varphi_{t}(x,v).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

(r-I)w=lv-y,

where

is the component of

orthogonal to

.

Proof.

Write

, with

and

. Then

\begin{split}\varphi_{l}(x,v)&=(\lambda v+w+lv,v);\\
(y,r)(x,v)&=(\lambda rv+rw+y,rv).\end{split}

The right hand sides are equal if and only if
rv=v
and

w+lv=rw+y.

The latter condition is equivalent to (

).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g=(y,r)\in G
, for

and
r\in\SO(n)

-nondegenerate.

Proof.

Let

Let

, and suppose that

. By Lemma

, we have

, so by (

u(x,v)^{\perp}=(v_{0},0)^{\perp}=v_{0}^{\perp}\times v_{0}^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times v_{0}^{\perp}=T_{(x,v)}M.

and

\begin{split}T_{(x,v)}(\varphi_{-l}\circ g)(a,b)&=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\bigl(r(x+ta)+y-lr(v+tb),r(v+tb)\bigr)\\
&=(ra-lrb,rb).\end{split}

(5.3)

We find that, as a map from

This product formula involves an equivariant Euler characteristic, see Definition 5.1 in

Here

Let

6
Geodesic flow on spheres

For geodesic flow on the sphere bundle on a sphere, the classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function is not defined, because there are uncountably many closed geodesics. This flow is not Anosov (which would imply that the Ruelle
\zeta
-function is well-defined), in contrast to the geodesic flow in negative curvature. In this section,
we onsider the action by
\SO(n+1)
on the sphere bundle
S(TS^{n})
. For
n=2
and
n=3
, we show that this flow is
g
-nondegenerate for suitable group elements, and compute the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function for the trivial connection on the trivial line bundle. We expect these computations to generalise to arbitrary
n
, with some additional combinatorics involved. In these cases, the kernel of
\Delta_{F}
is nonzero, so the hypotheses of the equivariant Fried problem, Question
2.12
, are not satisfied.

6.1
Homogeneous spaces of compact Lie groups

We assume in this subsection that
G
is a Lie group, and
H<G
is a closed subgroup. Consider the action by
G\times H
on
G
given by

(x,h)\cdot y=xyh^{-1},

for
x,y\in G
and

.
Let
X_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}
be such that
\ad(X_{0})|_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
. For

, we define
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\colon G\to G
by

\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(x)=x\exp(tX_{0}),

for

. Because

is

-equivariant, it descends to a map
\varphi_{t}\colon G/H\to G/H
.

Example 6.1
.

Geodesic flow on the sphere bundle of a sphere occurs in this way, see Lemma
6.3
.

Fix

. If

is a linear subspace, then we denote the map from

Lemma 6.2
.

Let

and

be such that
\varphi_{l}(xH)=gxH
. Then the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{T_{xH}G/H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{T_{xH}(\varphi_{l}\circ g^{-1})}
\textstyle{T_{xH}G/H}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}/\Ad(x)\mathfrak{h}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Ad(g^{-1})}
\scriptstyle{T_{eH}l_{x}\circ\Ad(x)^{-1}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}/\Ad(x)\mathfrak{h}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{T_{eH}l_{x}\circ\Ad(x)^{-1}}

Let

and

Since the map

Let

in the natural way. Let

Consider the subspace

For a given

-equivariant diffeomorphism

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}x\exp(tX)p=xXp,

Lemma 6.3
.

Under the diffeomorphism (

We have

\exp_{e\SO(n)}(X)=\exp(X)\SO(n),

\varphi_{t}(p,Xp)=(\exp(tX)p,\exp(tX)Xp)

From now on, we make the specific choice

Let

Lemma 6.4

We have

Remark 6.5

Lemma 6.6

for an element

\omega\in\mathfrak{so}(n)^{*}
, viewed as a left-invariant one-form on
M\cong\SO(n)
. For
\nabla^{F}
to be flat, one needs
d\omega=0
. This is the case if and only if
\omega
is zero on
[\mathfrak{so}(n),\mathfrak{so}(n)]=\mathfrak{so}(n)
.
∎

6.3
The case
n=2

In the rest of this section, we assume that the powers of
g
are dense in
T
.

Suppose for this subsection that
n=2
. Then
M=G=\SO(3)
, and
H
is trivial. Since
g
is regular, it follows from Lemma
6.2
that for all
l\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
x\in G
such that
\varphi_{l}(xH)=gxH
,

\dim(\ker(T_{xH}(\varphi_{l}\circ g^{-1})-1))=\dim(\ker(\Ad(g^{-1})-1))=1.

So the flow
\varphi
is
g
-nondegenerate.

Lemma 6.7
.

Suppose that
g
is a rotation by an angle
\theta
.
Then

L_{g}(\varphi)=\theta+2\pi\mathbb{Z}\cup-\theta+2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

(6.8)

Proof.

By Lemma
6.4
, it is enough to show that the right hand side of (
6.8
) is contained in the left hand side.
If
l\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\SO(3)
, then
\varphi_{l}(x)=gx
if and only if
x^{-1}gx=\exp(lX_{0})
. If
l\in\theta+2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, then
\exp(lX_{0})=g
, so taking
x=e
shows that
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
. And if
l\in-\theta+2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, then
\exp(lX_{0})=g^{-1}
, and taking

x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}

shows that
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
g\in\SO(3)
be a rotation over an angle
\theta
. For all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
, the set
\Gamma_{l}^{g}(\varphi)
consists of a single flow curve.

Proof.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\SO(3)\cong M
, and suppose that
\varphi_{l}(x)=gx
and
\varphi_{l}(x^{\prime})=gx^{\prime}
.
Then
x^{-1}gx=\exp(lX_{0})=x^{\prime-1}gx^{\prime}
.
Since the powers of
g
are dense in
\SO(2)
, it follows that for all
y\in\SO(2)
,

x^{-1}yx=x^{\prime-1}yx^{\prime}.

This implies that
x^{-1}x^{\prime}
lies in the centraliser of
\SO(2)
, which is
\SO(2)
itself.
We therefore find that

x^{\prime}=x\exp(tX_{0})

for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
, so
x^{\prime}
and
x
lie on the same flow curve of
\varphi
.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

In Lemma
6.8
(and also in Lemma
6.13
below), it is crucial that the powers of
g
are dense in
T
. In the opposite extreme case where
g
is the identity element, the set
L_{e}(\varphi)=2\pi\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
is still countable, but
\Gamma_{l}^{e}(\varphi)
is uncountable for all
l\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
if
n\geq 2
. This is because there is a closed geodesic though every point in
S^{n}
. This is why the classical Ruelle
\zeta
-function is not defined for the geodesic flow on spheres, whereas the equivariant version is defined for
g
with dense powers in
\SO(2)
, as we will see for
n=2
and
n=3
.

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
F=M\times\mathbb{C}\to M
be the trivial line bundle, and
\nabla^{F}=d
the trivial connection.
The flow
\varphi
is
g
-nondegenerate, the set
L_{g}(\varphi)
is countable, and for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
with positive real parts,

\log R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)=2\pi\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{e^{-|\theta+2\pi n|\sigma}}{|\theta+2\pi n|}+\frac{e^{-|-\theta+2\pi n|\sigma}}{|-\theta+2\pi n|}\right).

(6.9)

Proof.

In this case,
\sgn(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g}))=1
for all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{g}^{l}(\varphi)
, because
S^{2}
is oriented. The constant function
1
is a cutoff function because
G
is compact, while one can take
I_{\gamma}=[0,2\pi]
for any
g
-periodic flow curve
\gamma
. So for all such curves
\gamma
and
x\in\SO(3)
,

\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(x\gamma(s))\,ds=2\pi.

Normalising the measure on
G/Z
to give it unit volume, and applying Lemmas
3.7
,
6.7
and
6.8
, we conclude that (
6.9
) holds.

∎

In this example, the kernel of
\Delta_{F}
is the cohomology of
\SO(3)
, which is not zero. Furthermore, the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function does not extend to
\sigma=0
; Lemma
4.4
does not apply, since
\alpha=0
in the current case. So we are not in the situation of the equivariant Fried conjecture.

6.4
The case
n=3

In this subsection, we suppose that
n=3
, and remind the reader that we are assuming the powers of
g
are dense in
T
.
Then
H=\{I_{2}\}\times\SO(2)
is the copy of
\SO(2)
embedded into
\SO(4)
in the bottom right corner.

Consider the matrices

w_{1}:=I_{2},\quad w_{-1}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\in\OO(2).

Then for all
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

r(\varepsilon\alpha)=w_{\varepsilon}r(\alpha)w_{\varepsilon}.

(6.10)

Lemma 6.11
.

We have

L_{g}(\varphi)=(\theta_{1}+2\pi\mathbb{Z})\cup(-\theta_{1}+2\pi\mathbb{Z})\cup(\theta_{2}+2\pi\mathbb{Z})\cup(-\theta_{2}+2\pi\mathbb{Z}).

(6.11)

Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
.
If
l\in\varepsilon\theta_{1}+2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\SO(4)
, then the condition
\varphi_{l}(xH)=gxH
is equivalent to

x=\begin{pmatrix}aw_{\varepsilon}&0\\
0&dw_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix},

(6.12)

for
a,d\in\SO(2)
. If
l\in\varepsilon\theta_{2}+2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\SO(4)
, then the condition
\varphi_{l}(xH)=gxH
is equivalent to

x=\begin{pmatrix}0&bw_{\varepsilon}\\
cw_{-\varepsilon}&0\end{pmatrix},

(6.13)

for
b,c\in\SO(2)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.4
, the equality (
6.11
) follows if the right hand side is contained in the left hand side. This follows from the characterisations (
6.12
) and (
6.13
) of the elements
x\in\SO(4)
such that
\varphi_{l}(xH)=gxH
, for
l
in the right hand side of (
6.11
). We will prove these characterisations.

Let
l\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
x\in\SO(4)
be such that
\varphi_{l}(xH)=xgH
. As in the proof of Lemma
6.4
, this is equivalent to

xgx^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}r(l)&0\\
0&r(\alpha)\end{pmatrix},

(6.14)

where
l=\pm\theta_{1}
and
\alpha=\pm\theta_{2}
, or

l=\pm\theta_{2}
and
\alpha=\pm\theta_{1}
, where all equalities (here and also below) are modulo
2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. It is immediate that if
x
is of the form (
6.12
) and (
6.13
) in the respective cases, then (
6.14
) holds. So it remains to prove the converse implication.

Since, by assumption, the powers of
g
are dense in
T
, the equality (
6.14
) implies that for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

x^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}r(\alpha_{1})&0\\
0&r(\alpha_{2})\end{pmatrix}x=\begin{pmatrix}r(l)&0\\
0&r(\alpha)\end{pmatrix}

(6.15)

for
l=\pm\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha=\pm\alpha_{2}
, or

l=\pm\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha=\pm\alpha_{1}
.

Write
x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
, for
a,b,c,d\in M_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the left hand side of (
6.15
) equals

\begin{pmatrix}a^{T}r(\alpha_{1})a+c^{T}r(\alpha_{2})c&a^{T}r(\alpha_{1})b+c^{T}r(\alpha_{2})d\\
b^{T}r(\alpha_{1})a+d^{T}r(\alpha_{2})c&b^{T}r(\alpha_{1})b+d^{T}r(\alpha_{2})d\end{pmatrix}.

(6.16)

We first consider the case where
l=\pm\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha=\pm\alpha_{2}
. Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
be such that
l=\varepsilon_{1}\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha=\varepsilon_{2}\alpha_{2}
.
Then by (
6.10
), we have
r(l)=w_{\varepsilon_{1}}r(\alpha_{1})w_{\varepsilon_{1}}
and
r(\alpha)=w_{\varepsilon_{2}}r(\alpha_{2})w_{\varepsilon_{2}}
. Since the diagonal entries of (
6.16
) equal the diagonal entries of the right hand side of (
6.15
), we have for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}a^{T}r(\alpha_{1})a+c^{T}r(\alpha_{2})c&=w_{\varepsilon_{1}}r(\alpha_{1})w_{\varepsilon_{1}};\\
b^{T}r(\alpha_{1})b+d^{T}r(\alpha_{2})d&=w_{\varepsilon_{2}}r(\alpha_{2})w_{\varepsilon_{2}}.\end{split}

(6.17)

So
b=c=0
,
and
x=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}
, for
a,d\in\OO(2)
.
The products
\tilde{a}:=aw_{\varepsilon_{1}}
and
\tilde{d}:=dw_{\varepsilon_{2}}
both centralise
\SO(2)
by (
6.17
), so they lie in
\SO(2)
.
We find that
x=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{a}w_{\varepsilon_{1}}&0\\
0&\tilde{d}w_{\varepsilon_{2}}\end{pmatrix}
, for
\tilde{a},\tilde{d}\in\SO(2)
. Since
x
has determinant
1
, we have
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}
.

In the case where
l=\varepsilon_{1}\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha=\varepsilon_{2}\alpha_{1}
for
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we can argue similarly. Then the equality between (
6.16
) and the right hand side of (
6.15
) is equivalent to
a=d=0
, and
x=\begin{pmatrix}0&\tilde{b}w_{\varepsilon_{2}}\\
\tilde{c}w_{\varepsilon_{1}}&0\end{pmatrix}
with
\tilde{b}:=bw_{\varepsilon_{2}}
and
\tilde{c}:=cw_{\varepsilon_{1}}
lie in
\SO(2)
. The fact that
x
has determinant
1
now implies that
\varepsilon_{1}=-\varepsilon_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 6.12
.

The flow
\varphi_{l}
is
g
-nondegenerate.

Proof.

Let
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
and
x\in\SO(4)
be such that
\varphi_{l}(xH)=gxH
. If
x
is of the form (
6.12
), then
\Ad(x)\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}
, so it follows from Lemma
6.2
and regularity of
g
that the kernel of
T_{xH}(\varphi_{l}\circ g^{-1})
is one-dimensional.

If
x
is of the form (
6.13
), then
\Ad(x)\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{so}(2)\oplus\{0\}
, and a similar argument applies.
∎

Lemma 6.13
.

For all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
, the set
\Gamma_{l}^{g}(\varphi)
consists of a single flow curve.

Proof.

Let
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
. Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\SO(4)
be such that
\varphi_{l}(xH)=gxH
and
\varphi_{l}(x^{\prime}H)=gx^{\prime}H
.

First , suppose that
l\in\varepsilon\theta_{1}+2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, for
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then by Lemma
6.11
, there are
a,d,a^{\prime},d^{\prime}\in\SO(2)
such that

x=\begin{pmatrix}aw_{\varepsilon}&0\\
0&dw_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix},\qquad x^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon}&0\\
0&d^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix},

so that

x^{-1}x^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{\varepsilon}a^{T}a^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon}&0\\
0&w_{\varepsilon}d^{T}d^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix}.

As
w_{\varepsilon}a^{T}aw_{\varepsilon}\in\SO(2)
, it follows that
x^{\prime}H=x\exp(tX_{0})H
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Next, suppose that
l\in\varepsilon\theta_{2}+2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, for
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then by Lemma
6.11
, there are
b,c,b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in\SO(2)
such that

x=\begin{pmatrix}0&bw_{\varepsilon}\\
cw_{-\varepsilon}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad x^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&b^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon}\\
c^{\prime}w_{-\varepsilon}&0\end{pmatrix},

which implies that

x^{-1}x^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}w_{-\varepsilon}c^{T}c^{\prime}w_{-\varepsilon}&0\\
0&w_{\varepsilon}b^{T}b^{\prime}w_{\varepsilon}\end{pmatrix}.

As in the previous case, it follows that
x^{\prime}H=x\exp(tX_{0})H
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Proposition 6.14
.

The flow
\varphi
is
g
-nondegenerate, the set
L_{g}(\varphi)
is countable, and
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
with positive real parts,

\log R^{g}_{\varphi,\nabla^{F}}(\sigma)=2\pi\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{e^{-|(-1)^{k}\theta_{j}+2\pi n|\sigma}}{|(-1)^{k}\theta_{j}+2\pi n|}

(6.18)

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
6.10
,
\sgn(\det(1-P_{\gamma}^{g}))=1
. We can choose
\chi
to be the constant
1
, so that

\int_{I_{\gamma}}\chi(x\gamma(s))\,ds=2\pi

for all
l\in L_{g}(\varphi)
,
\gamma\in\Gamma_{g}^{l}(\varphi)
and

x\in\SO(4)
.
Using Lemmas
6.11
,
6.13
, we obtain (
6.18
).
∎

As in the case
n=2
, the equivariant Ruelle
\zeta
-function does not have a meromorphic continuation to
\sigma=0
in this case, and the kernel of
\Delta_{F}
is nonzero. This implies that the hypotheses of the equivariant Fried conjecture are not satisfied.

Coupling of different solvable ensembles of random matrices
E. N. Antonov, A.Yu. Orlov, D.V. Vasiliev

Abstract
Explicit expressions for multimatrix models with complex and unitary matrices allows to couple these models with well-known unitary, orthogonsl and sympletic ensembles. We consider examples of such mixed ensembles which are solvable in the sense that the partition functions of such ensembles can be considered as tau functions of the classical integrable equations.

Appendix A
Partitions. The Schur polynomials

We recall that a nonincreasing set of nonnegative integers
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k}\geq 0
,
we call partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})
, and
\lambda_{i}
are called parts of
\lambda
.
The sum of parts is called the weight
|\lambda|
of
\lambda
. The number of nonzero parts of
\lambda

is called the length of
\lambda
, it will be denoted
\ell(\lambda)
. See
[
20
]
for details.
Partitions will be denoted by Greek letters:
\lambda,\mu,\dots
. The set of all partitions is denoted by

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits
. The set of all partitions with odd parts is denoted
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{OP}\nolimits
.
Partitions with distinct parts are called strict partitions, we prefer
letters
\alpha,\beta
to denote them. The set of all strict partitions will be denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{DP}\nolimits
.
The Frobenius coordinated
\alpha,\beta
for partitions
(\alpha|\beta)=\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits
are of usenames
(let me recall that the coordinates
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{DP}\nolimits
consists of the lengths of arms counted
from the main diagonal of the Young diagram of
\lambda
while

\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{DP}\nolimits
consists of the lengths of legs counted
from the main diagonal of the Young diagram of
\lambda
,
k
is the length of the main diagonal of
\lambda
,
see
[
20
]
for details).

To define the Schur function
s_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits
at the first step we introduce the
set of elementary Schur functions
s_{(m)}
by

e^{\sum_{m>0}\frac{1}{m}p_{m}x^{m}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}x^{m}s_{(m)}(\mathbf{p})

where the variables
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},\dots)
are called power sum variables. For

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{P}\nolimits

we define

s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p})=\mathrm{det}\left[s_{(\lambda_{i}-i+j)}(\mathbf{p})\right]_{i,j>0}

(41)

If we put
p_{m}=p_{m}(X)=\mathrm{tr}X^{m}
where
X
is a matrix we write
s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{p}(X))=s_{\lambda}(X)
.

Appendix B
Matrix ensembles

Unitary matrices.

The Haar measure on
\mathbb{U}_{N}
in the explicit form is written as

d_{*}U=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\prod_{1\leq i<k\leq N}|e^{\theta_{i}}-e^{\theta_{k}}|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}d\theta_{i},\hskip 9.24994pt-\pi<\theta_{1}<\cdots<\theta_{N}\leq\pi

(42)

Hermitian matrices.

This case can be considered in the same way as the prevous one.
It is natural
[
19
]
to take the following measure on the space
\mathfrak{H}_{N}
of Hermitian
N\times N
matrices:

d\Omega_{N,w}(X)=C_{N}\prod_{i\leq j\leq N}e^{-w\left(\Re X_{i,j}\right)^{2}}d\Re X_{i,j}\prod_{i<j\leq N}e^{-w\left(\Im X_{i,j}\right)^{2}}d\Im X_{i,j}

=c_{N}\left(\Delta(\mathbf{x})\right)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{N}e^{-wx^{2}_{i}}dx_{i}d_{*}U

where
w>0
is a parameter and where we use

X=U\mathrm{diag}\left(\,\,\,0\hskip 9.24994ptx_{i}\atop-x_{i}\,\,0\right)U^{-1}
,
U\in\mathbb{U}_{N}
.

Remark 2
.

In many applied problems in which random matrices are used, it is very convenient to assume
that the parameter
w
is proportional to the size of the matrices
N
.

Orthogonal matrices.

The Haar measure on
\mathbb{O}_{N}
is

d_{*}O=\begin{cases}\frac{2^{(n-1)^{2}}}{\pi^{n}}\prod_{i<j}^{n}\left(\cos(\theta_{i})-\cos(\theta_{j})^{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n},\hskip 9.24994ptN=2n\\
\frac{2^{n^{2}}}{\pi^{n}}\prod_{i<j}^{n}\left(\cos(\theta_{i})-\cos(\theta_{j})^{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sin^{2}\frac{\theta_{i}}{2}d\theta_{i},\hskip 9.24994ptN=2n+1\end{cases}

(43)

where

d\theta_{i},\hskip 9.24994pt0\leq\theta_{i}<\cdots<\theta_{n}\leq\pi
. The prefactors are chosen to provide

\int_{\mathbb{O}_{N}}d_{*}O=1
.

By
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
we denote the space of
N\times N
real skew-symmetric matrices.
Recall that the eigenvalues of real skew fields are purely imaginary;
the eigenvalues occur in pairs
\pm x_{i}\sqrt{-1},\,i=1,\dots,n
in the case
N=2n
while
in the case
N=2n+1
there is an additional eigenvalue
x_{2n+1}=0
.

Skew-symmetric matrices.

For any
X\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
there exists such

X=O\mathrm{diag}\left\{\left(\,\,\,0\hskip 9.24994ptx_{i}\atop-x_{i}\,\,0\right)_{i=1,\dots,n}\right\}O^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}_{2n}
,
where

,

The measure on

d\omega_{N}(X)=\pi^{\frac{1}{2}(n-n^{2})}\prod_{i<j}^{n}e^{-X_{i,j}^{2}}dX_{i,j}

c_{2}x^{2}_{i},\hskip 9.24994ptN=2n+1\end{cases}

[
19
]

It is known

c_{2}\frac{\mathrm{det}\left[2\sinh(2x_{i}y_{i})\right]_{i,j}}{\Delta(\mathbf{x}^{2})\Delta(\mathbf{y}^{2})\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}},\hskip 9.24994ptN=2n+1\end{cases}

Complex matrices.

The measure on the space of complex matrices is defined as

d\Omega(Z)=c_{N}\prod_{a,b=1}^{N}d\Re Z_{ab}d\Im Z_{ab}\text{e}^{-N|Z_{ab}|^{2}}

We will consider integrals over

where the integration domain is

and where

is the normalization

.

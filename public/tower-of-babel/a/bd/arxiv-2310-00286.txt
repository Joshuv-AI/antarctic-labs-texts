Linear stability of the elliptic relative equilibria for the restricted N N -body problem: two special cases
Jiashengliang Xie, Bowen Liuand, Qinglong Zhou

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the elliptic relative equilibria of the restricted N N -body problems, where the N − 1 N-1 primaries form an Euler-Moulton collinear central configuration or a ( 1 + n ) (1+n) -gon central configuration. We obtain the symplectic reduction for the general restricted N N -body problem. For the first case, by analyzing the relationship between these restricted N N -body problems and the elliptic Lagrangian solutions, we distinguish the linear stability of the restricted N N -body problem by the ω {\omega} -Maslov index.Through numerical computations, we also determine the stability conditions in terms of the mass parameters for N = 4 N=4 and the symmetry of the central configuration. For the second case, there exist three positions S 1 , S 2 S_{1},S_{2} and S 3 S_{3} for the massless body (up to rotations of angle 2 ​ π n \frac{2\pi}{n} ). For m 0 m {m_{0}\over m} sufficiently large, we show that the elliptic relative equilibria is linearly unstable if the eccentricity 0 ≤ e < e 0 0\leq e<e_{0} and the massless body lies at S 1 S_{1} or S 2 S_{2} ; while the elliptic relative equilibria is linearly stable if the massless body lies at S 3 S_{3} .

Introduction and main results

In the classical planar
N
-body problems of celestial mechanics, the position vectors of the
N
-particles are denoted by
q_{1},\dots,{q_{N}}\in{\bf R}^{2}
, and the masses are represented by

m_{1},\dots,{m_{N}}>0
. By Newton’s second law and the law of universal
gravitation, the system of equations is

\displaystyle m_{i}\ddot{q_{i}}=\frac{\partial U}{\partial q_{i}},\quad i=1,{\dots,N},

(1.1)

where
U(q)=U(q_{1},{\dots,q_{N}})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq{N}}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|}

is the potential function and
|\cdot|
is the standard norm of vector in
{\bf R}^{2}
.
Suppose the configuration space is

\hat{\chi}:=\left\{q=(q_{1},{\dots,q_{N}})\in({\bf R}^{2})^{{N}}\;|\sum_{i=1}^{{N}}m_{i}q_{i}=0,q_{i}\neq q_{j},\forall i\neq j\right\}.

For the period
T
, the corresponding action functional is

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(q)=\int_{0}^{T}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{{N}}\frac{m_{i}|\dot{q}_{i}(t)|^{2}}{2}+U(q(t))\right]{\mathrm{d}}t,

(1.2)

which is defined on the loop space
W^{1,2}({\bf R}/T{\bf Z},\hat{\chi})
. The periodic solutions
of (
1.1
) correspond to critical points of the action functional (
1.2
).
Let
p_{1},{\dots,p_{N}}\in{\bf R}^{2}
be the momentum vectors of the particles respectively. It is well-known that (
1.1
) can be reformulated as a Hamiltonian system by

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{i}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_{i}},\;\dot{q}_{i}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}},\quad\mbox{for}\;i=1,{\dots,N},

(1.3)

with the Hamiltonian function

\displaystyle H(p,q)=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}}\frac{|p_{i}|^{2}}{2m_{i}}-U(q_{1},{\dots,q_{N}}).

(1.4)

One special class of periodic solutions to the planar
N
-body problem is the elliptic relative equilibrium (ERE for short)
[
22
]
. It is generated by a central configuration and the Keplerian motion.
A central configuration (C.C. for short) is formed by
N
position vectors
\left(q_{1},\ldots,q_{N}\right)=\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\right)
which satisfy

\displaystyle-\lambda m_{i}q_{i}=\frac{\partial U}{\partial q_{i}},\forall\;1\leq i\leq N,

(1.5)

where
\lambda=U(a)/{I(a)}>0
and
I(a)={\sum_{i=1}^{N}}m_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}
is the moment of inertia.
A planar central configuration of the
N
-body problem gives rise to a solution of (
1.1
) where each particle moves on a specific Keplerian orbit while the totality of the particles move according to a homothetic motion.

The linear stability of the ERE is determined by the eigenvalues of the linearized Poincaré map.
Let
{\bf U}
denote the unit circle in the complex plane. The ERE is spectrally stable if all eigenvalues of linearized Poincaré map are on
{\bf U}
; it is linearly stable if Poincaré map is semi-simple and spectrally stable; it is linearly unstable if at least one pair of eigenvalues are not on
{\bf U}
.
Since the nineteenth century
[
24
]
, the researches on the stability have always been active in celestial mechanics because it reveals the dynamics near the period orbits.
However, it has always been one difficult task to obtain the linear stability of ERE, because the linearized Hamiltonian systems are non-autonomous, especially for the elliptic orbits.
Many results on linear stability of the three-body problems have been obtained over the past decades by numerical methods
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
, bifurcation theory
[
23
]
and the index theory
[
7
,
4
,
6
,
26
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, the
{\omega}
-Maslov index theory is the only analytical method to obtain the full picture of the stability and instability to the ERE, such as the elliptic Lagrangian solution
[
7
,
4
,
6
]
, and the elliptic Euler solution
[
26
,
27
]
.

When it comes to

-body problems with

To the best of our knowledge,
for the general case of
n
bodies, the elliptic Euler
-

The central configuration coordinates

For the given masses

x

F(x)=\int_{0}^{T}L(t,x,\dot{x})dt,\qquad\forall\,\,x\in W^{1,2}({\bf R}/T{\bf Z},{\bf R}^{n}),

where
L\in C^{2}(({\bf R}/T{\bf Z})\times{\bf R}^{2n},{\bf R})
and satisfies the
Legendrian convexity condition
L_{p,p}(t,x,p)>0

that
x

equation:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}L_{p}(t,x,\dot{x})-L_{x}(t,x,\dot{x})=0,

(A.18)

\displaystyle x(0)=x(T),\qquad\dot{x}(0)=\dot{x}(T).

(A.19)

For such an extremal loop, define

\displaystyle P(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{p,p}(t,x(t),\dot{x}(t)),

\displaystyle Q(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{x,p}(t,x(t),\dot{x}(t)),

\displaystyle R(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{x,x}(t,x(t),\dot{x}(t)).

Note that

F\,^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{d}{dt}(P\frac{d}{dt}+Q)+Q^{T}\frac{d}{dt}+R.

(A.20)

For
\omega\in{\bf U}
, set

D(\omega,T)=\{y\in W^{1,2}([0,T],{\bf C}^{n})\,|\,y(T)=\omega y(0)\}.

(A.21)

We define the
\omega

\phi_{\omega}(x)

\langle F\,^{\prime\prime}(x)y_{1},y_{2}\rangle,\qquad\forall\;y_{1},y_{2}\in D(\omega,T),

(A.22)

Then

\overline{D}(\omega,T)
.
We also define the
{\omega}

Note that we only use

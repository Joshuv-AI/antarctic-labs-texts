Godbillon-Vey type functional for almost contact manifolds
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
Many contact metric manifolds are critical points of curvature functionals restricted to spaces of associated metrics. The Godbillon-Vey functional was never considered in a variational context in Contact Geometry. Recently we extended this functional from foliations to arbitrary plane fields on a 3-dimensional manifold, so, the following question arises: can one use the Godbillon-Vey functional to find optimal almost contact manifolds? In the paper, we introduce a Godbillon-Vey type functional for a 3-dimensional almost contact manifold, present it in Reinhart-Wood form and find its Euler-Lagrange equations for all variations preserving the Reeb vector field. We construct critical (for our functional) 3-dimensional almost contact manifolds having a double-twisted product structure, these solutions belong to the class C 5 ⊕ C 12 C_{5}\oplus C_{12} according to Chinea-Gonzalez classification. Keywords : almost contact manifold, Godbillon-Vey functional, double-twisted product, variation, Chinea-Gonzalez classes Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) Primary 53C12; Secondary 53C21

Introduction

D. Chinea and C. Gonzalez
[
5
]
decomposed the space of certain 3-tensors on
an almost contact metric manifold
M^{2n+1}(\varphi,\omega,T,g)
into irreducible invariant components
under the action of the structural group
U(n)\times 1
and developed a Gray-Hervella type classification for almost contact metric (a.c.m.) manifolds.
They obtained 12 classes of a.c.m. manifolds, which in dimension three are reduced to five classes:

C_{5}
of
\beta
-Kenmotsu manifolds,
C_{6}
of
\alpha
-Sasakian manifolds,
C_{9}
-manifolds,
C_{12}
-manifolds
(called generalized Sasakian space forms) and
|C|=C_{5}\cap C_{12}
of cosymplectic manifolds.
Many works on a.c.m. manifolds are devoted to normal structures, that is to the first three Chinea-Gonzalez classes.
Some authors investigate
C_{5}\oplus C_{12}
-manifolds, consisting of integrable, non-normal manifolds because
\nabla_{T}T\neq 0
, see
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection.
The elements of
\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq 5}C_{i}\oplus C_{12}
are a.c.m. manifolds that are locally a double-twisted product
B\times_{(u,v)}I
,
where
B
is an almost Hermitian manifold,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval and
u,v
are smooth positive functions on
B\times I
.
The first result in this topic states that any Kenmotsu manifold, i.e.,
(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y=g(\varphi X,Y)\,T-\omega(Y)\,\varphi X
,
is locally a warped product
B\times_{u}I
,
where
u\in C^{\infty}(I)
and
B
is a Kähler manifold, see
[
8
]
.
Note that any two-dimensional almost complex manifold is complex, moreover, it is Kähler.
Thus, in dimension three, the class
\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq 5}C_{i}\oplus C_{12}
reduces to

C_{5}\oplus C_{12}
, whose elements are locally a double-twisted product
B\times_{(u,v)}I
,
where
B
is a 2-dimensional Kähler manifold, see
[
4
,
6
]
.

Finding critical metrics of certain functionals can be considered as an approach to searching for the best metric for a given manifold.
Many contact metric manifolds are critical points of curvature functionals restricted to spaces of associated metrics;
for example, symplectical manifolds are critical for the integral of scalar curvature and
K-contact manifolds are critical for the integral of Ricci curvature in the
T
-direction, e.g.,
[
2
, Sect. 5]
.
The Godbillon-Vey functional was introduced for foliations of codimension one in
[
7
]
,
its changes under infinitesimal deformations of the foliation were studied, for example, in
[
1
]
,
but this functional was never considered in a variational context in Contact Geometry.
In
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
we extended the Godbillon-Vey functional from foliations to arbitrary plane fields on a 3-dimensional manifold
and used the calculus of variations to characterize critical metrics in distinguished classes of almost-product manifolds.
So, a question arises:
can one use a Godbillon-Vey type functional to find optimal 3-dimensional a.c.m. manifolds,
e.g., in
C_{5}\oplus C_{12}
according to Chinea-Gonzalez classification
?
To answer the question, we define a new Godbillon-Vey type functional,
see (
3
),
present it in Reinhart-Wood form and derive its Euler-Lagrange equations for variations preserving the Reeb vector field
T
(Theorem
1
).
For clarity of calculations, we divide variations into two types, each of which has a geometric meaning.
These allow us to find new solutions to the problem:
what a.c.m. manifolds are in some sense optimal
?
Since a.c.m. manifolds with the geodesic vector field
T
are critical for our functional,
it is interesting to study the case when the curvature
k
of
T
-curves is non-zero.
We construct such critical 3-dimensional a.c.m. manifolds having a double-twisted product structure, i.e., solutions belonging to

C_{5}\oplus C_{12}
(Theorem
2
).

2
The Reinhart-Wood type formula

An almost contact structure
(\varphi,\omega,T)
on a smooth
manifold
M^{2n+1}

consists of an endomorphism
\varphi
of
TM
, a 1-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{1}(M)
and a vector field
T
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi^{2}(X)=-X+\,\omega(X)\,T\quad(X\in TM),\qquad\omega(T)=1.

(1)

The plane field
\ker\omega
is called the contact distribution.
By (
1
), we get
\omega\circ\varphi=0
and
\varphi\,T=0
.

Let
T
be a nonzero vector field on a smooth
orientable three-dimensional manifold
M
.
Then for any 1-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{1}(M)
such that
\omega(T)=1
there exists a unique tensor
\varphi\in End(TM)

such that the restriction of
\varphi
on

\ker\omega
specifies a right-hand rotation and

M^{3}(\varphi,\omega,T)
is an almost contact manifold.
In
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
, the Godbillon-Vey functional

gv=\int_{M}\eta\wedge d\eta
, where

\eta=\iota_{T}\,d\omega=d\omega(T,\cdot)
,
was extended from foliations to arbitrary plane fields on
M^{3}
. Note that
\eta(T)=0
.
We use the formula
d\omega(X,Y)=X\omega(Y)-Y\omega(X)-\omega([X,Y])
for
\omega\in\Lambda^{1}(M)
and
X,Y\in TM
.

Definition 1
.

Given an almost contact structure
(\varphi,\omega,T)
on
M^{3}
, define a 1-form
\eta^{*}\in\Lambda^{1}(M)
by

\displaystyle\eta^{*}(X):=(\iota_{T}\,d\omega)(\varphi X)=d\omega(T,\varphi X)\quad(X\in TM),

(2)

i.e.,
\eta^{*}=\eta\circ\varphi
.
Using
\eta^{*}
, we introduce (similarly to
gv
) the following functional:

\displaystyle gv^{*}=\int_{M}\eta^{*}\wedge d\eta^{*}.

(3)

If an almost contact manifold
M^{3}(\varphi,\omega,T)
admits a Riemannian metric
g=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
such that

\langle\varphi X,\varphi Y\rangle=\langle X,Y\rangle-\omega(X)\,\omega(Y),

(4)

then
g
is called a compatible metric and we get an a.c.m. manifold.
Such a structure (
4
) is induced on any hypersurface of an almost Hermitian manifold.
Putting
Y=T
in (
4
), we get
\omega(X)=\langle X,T\rangle
;
thus,
T
is
g
-orthogonal to
\ker\omega
.
An a.c.m. manifold such that

g(X,\varphi Y)=d\omega(X,Y)

is called a contact metric manifold. Such manifolds have a geodesic vector field
T
.

Given a unit vector field
T
on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
, the unit normal
N
, the binormal
B=T\times N
and the torsion
\tau
of
T
-curves
are defined on an open subset
U
of
M^{3}
, where the curvature
k
of
T
-curves is nonzero.
Further, we assume that
U
is
dense, so the set
M\setminus U
can be neglected during integration over
M^{3}
.
The 1-form in the Godbillon-Vey functional
is given by
\eta=kN^{\flat}
(i.e.,
\eta(X)=k\langle N,X\rangle
for
X\in TM
) on
U
and
\eta=0
on
M\setminus U
, see
[
11
,
12
]
.
Thus, for the 1-form
\eta^{*}
in (
2
), using
d\omega(T,\varphi X)=-\omega([T,\varphi X])=k\,\langle N,\varphi X\rangle

and skew-symmetry of
\varphi
, we get

\displaystyle\eta^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-k(\varphi N)^{\flat}&\mbox{ on }U,\\
0&\mbox{ on }\ M\setminus U.\\
\end{array}\right.

If
T
is a geodesic vector field (i.e.,
k=0
) then
\eta=\eta^{*}=0
, hence
gv^{*}=0
.

Recall that the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of a metric
g=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is given by:

2\,\langle\nabla_{X}\,Y,Z\rangle=X\langle Y,Z\rangle+Y\langle X,Z\rangle-Z\langle X,Y\rangle+\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle-\langle[X,Z],Y\rangle-\langle[Y,Z],X\rangle.

(5)

The non-symmetric second fundamental form
h
of the distribution
\ker\omega
is defined by

\displaystyle h_{X,Y}=\langle\nabla_{X}Y,T\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{X}T,Y\rangle\qquad(X,Y\in\ker\omega).

(6)

If the distribution
\ker\omega
in
TM^{3}
is integrable,
then the tensor

2\,{\mathcal{T}}_{X,Y}=\langle[X,Y],T\rangle

vanishes; in this case, the 2-form
h
is symmetric.
The mean curvature of
\ker\omega
is defined by
H=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}_{g}h
.
The distribution
\ker\omega
is said to be
totally umbilical (or, totally geodesic) if Sym
(h)=Hg
(
h=0
, respectively).
Here,

{\rm Sym}(h)(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\,(h(X,Y)+h(Y,X))

is the symmetric second fundamental form of the distribution
\ker\omega
.
The following Frenet-Serret formulas are true on
U
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}\,T=kN,\quad\nabla_{T}\,N=-kT+\tau B,\quad\nabla_{T}\,B=-\tau N.

(7)

Using (
7
), we obtain
k=\langle\nabla_{T}\,T,N\rangle
and
\tau=-\langle\nabla_{T}\,B,N\rangle
.
Recall, see
[
13
, Eq. 139]
,

\displaystyle d\eta(T,B)=k(\tau-h_{B,N}),\quad d\eta(T,N)=T(k)-kh_{N,N}.

(8)

With the volume form
{\rm d}\,{\rm vol}_{g}
in mind, we derive formulas similar to the following one, see
[
11
,
12
]
:

gv=-\int_{M}k^{2}(\tau-h_{B,N})\,{\rm d}\,{\rm vol}_{g}
,
obtained for foliations by B.L. Reinhart and J.W. Wood in
[
10
]
with opposite sign by convention.

Proposition 1
.

We obtain

\displaystyle gv^{*}=-\!\int_{M}k^{2}(\tau+h_{N,B})\,{\rm d}\,{\rm vol}_{g}.

(9)

Proof.

We have
\varphi N=B
and
\varphi B=-N
. From Frenet-Serret formulas
and
\eta^{*}=\eta\circ\varphi
,
we find

\displaystyle d\eta^{*}(T,B)

\displaystyle=T(\eta(\varphi B))-B(\eta(\varphi T))-\eta(\varphi[T,B])=-T(\eta(N))-k\,\langle\varphi[T,B],N\rangle

\displaystyle=-T(k)+k\langle\nabla_{T}\,B-\nabla_{B}\,T,B\rangle=-T(k)+k\,h_{B,B},

\displaystyle d\eta^{*}(T,N)

\displaystyle=T(\eta(\varphi N))-N(\eta(\varphi T))-\eta(\varphi[T,N])=T(\eta(B))-k\,\langle\varphi[T,N],N\rangle

\displaystyle=k\,\langle B,\nabla_{T}\,N-\nabla_{N}\,T\rangle=k\,(\tau+h_{N,B}).

Thus, using (
8
),
\eta^{*}(T)=\eta^{*}(N)=0
and
\eta^{*}(B)=-k
, we calculate

\displaystyle(\eta^{*}\wedge d\eta^{*})(T,N,B)=\eta^{*}(B)\,d\eta^{*}(T,N)=-k^{2}(\tau+h_{N,B}).

Applying to the above the volume form
{\rm d}\,{\rm vol}_{g}
on
(M^{3},g)
, we get (
9
).
∎

3
The first variation of
gv^{*}

Let a smooth orientable 3-dimensional manifold
M
be equipped with a non-vanishing vector field
T
.
Then for any Riemannian metric
g
on
M
such that
g(T,T)=1
there exist a unique 1-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{1}(M)

(given by
\omega=g(T,\,\cdot)
)
and a unique
(1,1)
-tensor
\varphi\in End(TM)
such that
the restriction of
\varphi
on the plane
\ker\omega
specifies a right-hand rotation and

M^{3}(\varphi,\omega,T,g)
is an a.c.m. manifold.
Let
{\cal C}(M,T)
denote the set of all such a.c.m. structures on
(M,T)
.

Let
M^{3}(\varphi,\omega,T,g)
be an almost contact manifold, and
g_{t}\ (|t|<\varepsilon)
a family of Riemannian metrics on
M^{3}
such that
g_{0}=g
and
g_{t}(T,T)\equiv 1
. By the above, there exists a unique family
(\varphi_{t},\omega_{t},T,g_{t})
of a.c.m. structures in
{\cal C}(M,T)

such that
\varphi_{0}=\varphi
and
\omega_{0}=\omega
.
Denote by dot the
t
-derivative at
t=0
of any quantity on
M
.
Since
\overset{\centerdot}{T}=0
and
\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{T,T}=0
are true, the symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor
\overset{\centerdot}{g}=(dg_{t}/dt)|_{t=0}
has five independent components
on a domain
U\subset M
(where
k\neq 0
):

\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{T,N},\quad\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{T,B},\quad\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N},\quad\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B},\quad\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}.

Such variations
g_{t}
that generate on
U
only nonzero components
\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N},\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}
and
\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}
, are called
g^{\top}
-variations.
They preserve
T
and
\ker\omega
; thus produce trivial Euler-Lagrange equations for the functional
gv
,
see
[
11
,
12
]
.
In contrast, the
g^{\top}
-variations are essential for the functional
gv^{*}
and they will be considered in Section
3.1
.

Variations
g_{t}
of
g
that generate on
U
only nonzero components

\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{T,N}
and
\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{T,B}
, are called
g^{\pitchfork}
-variations,
see
[
11
,
12
]
.
The
g^{\pitchfork}
-variations
will be considered in Section
3.2
.

Recall the
general variational formula for the volume form, see
[
13
, p. 162]
:

({\rm d}\,{\rm vol}_{g})^{\centerdot}=\frac{1}{2}\,(\operatorname{Tr}_{g}\overset{\centerdot}{g})\,{\rm d}\,{\rm vol}_{g}\,.

(10)

Applying
variation
g_{t}
to (
5
), one can find how the
connection changes, e.g.,
[
13
, p. 158]
:

2\,\langle(\overset{\centerdot}{\nabla}_{X}\,Y),Z\rangle=(\nabla^{t}_{X}\,{\overset{\centerdot}{g}})(Y,Z)+(\nabla^{t}_{Y}\,{\overset{\centerdot}{g}})(X,Z)-(\nabla^{t}_{Z}\,{\overset{\centerdot}{g}})(X,Y),\quad X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

(11)

The main goal of this section is the following

Theorem 1
.

The Euler-Lagrange equations on
U
of the functional
gv^{*}
with respect to all variations in
{\cal C}(M,T)
are the following:

\displaystyle\tau+h_{N,B}=0,

\displaystyle T(k)-k\,h_{B,B}=0,

\displaystyle(h_{N,B}+h_{B,N})\,(h_{N,N}+h_{B,B})=0,

\displaystyle h^{2}_{N,N}-h^{2}_{B,B}-h_{N,N}h_{B,B}-T(h_{N,N})-\frac{1}{4}\,k^{2}=0.

(12)

Proof.

Using Euler-Lagrange equations (
14
) for
g^{\top}
-variations (Section
3.1
), we simplify Euler-Lagrange equations (
16
) for
g^{\pitchfork}
-variations (Section
3.2
), and get (
12
).
∎

3.1
The
g^{\top}
-variations

Lemma 1
.

For
g^{\top}
-variations of metric on
U
, we obtain
\overset{\centerdot}{\eta}=0
and

\displaystyle\overset{\centerdot}{N}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}\,N-\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}\,B,\qquad\overset{\centerdot}{B}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}\,B,\qquad\overset{\centerdot}{k}=-\frac{k}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N},

\displaystyle(\tau+h_{N,B})^{\centerdot}=(h_{N,N}-h_{B,B})\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}-T(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}).

(13)

Proof.

Using
\overset{\centerdot}{T}=0
,
we find

\displaystyle\langle\overset{\centerdot}{N},T\rangle=\langle\overset{\centerdot}{B},T\rangle=0,\quad\langle\overset{\centerdot}{N},N\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N},

\displaystyle\langle\overset{\centerdot}{B},B\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B},\quad\langle\overset{\centerdot}{N},B\rangle+\langle\overset{\centerdot}{B},N\rangle=-\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}.

Differentiating
\langle\nabla_{T}\,T,N\rangle=k
,
\langle\nabla_{T}\,T,B\rangle=0
and using (
7
) and (
11
), we find

\displaystyle\overset{\centerdot}{k}=\frac{k}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}+(\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,N}-\frac{1}{2}\,(\nabla_{N}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,T},

\displaystyle\langle\overset{\centerdot}{B},N\rangle=-\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}-\frac{1}{k}\,(\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,B}+\frac{1}{2\,k}\,(\nabla_{B}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,T}.

By the above,
\langle\overset{\centerdot}{N},B\rangle=\frac{1}{k}\,(\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,B}-\frac{1}{2\,k}\,(\nabla_{B}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,T}
is true.
Thus,

\displaystyle\overset{\centerdot}{N}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}\,N+\frac{1}{k}\,\big((\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,B}-\frac{1}{2}\,(\nabla_{B}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,T}\big)\,B,

\displaystyle\overset{\centerdot}{B}=\big(-\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}-\frac{1}{k}\,(\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,B}+\frac{1}{2\,k}\,(\nabla_{B}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,T}\big)\,N-\frac{1}{2}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}\,B.

Next, using (
7
) and
\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{T,X}=0
, we find

\displaystyle(\nabla_{N}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,T}=(\nabla_{B}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,T}=0,\quad(\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,B}=-k\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B},\quad(\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{T,N}=-k\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N},

\displaystyle(\nabla_{T}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{N,N}=T(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N})-2\,\tau\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B},\quad(\nabla_{N}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g})_{N,T}=h_{N,N}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}+h_{N,B}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}.

From the above we find

\overset{\centerdot}{N}
,
\overset{\centerdot}{B}
and
\overset{\centerdot}{k}
in (
13
).
Using (
6
) and (
7
), we calculate

\tau+h_{N,B}=-\langle\nabla_{T}\,B,N\rangle+\langle\nabla_{N}\,B,T\rangle=\langle\nabla_{T}\,N,B\rangle-\langle\nabla_{N}\,T,B\rangle=\langle[T,N],B\rangle.

Since the Lie bracket does not depend on metric, we get

\displaystyle(\tau+h_{N,B})^{\centerdot}=\langle[T,N],B\rangle^{\centerdot}=\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{[T,N],B}+\langle[T,\overset{\centerdot}{N}],B\rangle+\langle[T,N],\overset{\centerdot}{B}\rangle

\displaystyle=h_{N,N}\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}-\langle[T,B],B\rangle\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}-T(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B})+\frac{1}{2}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B},

from which the expression of
(\tau+h_{N,B})^{\centerdot}
in (
13
) follows.
∎

Our main result in Section
3.1
is the following.

Proposition 2
.

An a.c.m. manifold
M^{3}(\varphi,\omega,T,g)
is critical for

gv^{*}
with respect to
g^{\top}
-variations
if and only if the following Euler-Lagrange equations hold on
U
:

\displaystyle\tau+h_{N,B}=0,\qquad T(k)-k\,h_{B,B}=0.

(14)

Proof.

Using (
9
) and (
10
), we find

\displaystyle(gv^{*})^{\centerdot}=-\int_{M}\big\{(k^{2}(\tau+h_{N,B}))^{\centerdot}+\frac{k^{2}}{2}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}+\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B})\big\}d\,{\rm vol}_{g}.

By (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle(k^{2}(\tau+h_{N,B}))^{\centerdot}=2\,k\,\overset{\centerdot}{k}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})+k^{2}(\tau+h_{N,B})^{\centerdot}

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{2}}{2}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}-k^{2}(\tau+h_{N,B})\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}+k^{2}(h_{N,N}-h_{B,B})\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}-k^{2}T(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}).

Using the equalities
T(f)=\operatorname{div}(f\cdot T)-f\cdot\operatorname{div}T

for any function
f\in C^{1}(M)
and
{\rm div}\,T=-\operatorname{Tr}_{g}h=-(h_{N,N}+h_{B,B})
, see
[
13
]
, we get

\displaystyle k^{2}T(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B})={\rm div}(k^{2}\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}\,T)+\big(k^{2}(h_{N,N}+h_{B,B})-2\,k\,T(k)\big)\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(gv^{*})^{\centerdot}=-\int_{M}\Big\{-k^{2}(\tau+h_{N,B})\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}+k^{2}(h_{N,N}-h_{B,B})\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}+\frac{k^{2}}{2}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}

\displaystyle-\big(k^{2}(h_{N,N}+h_{B,B})-2\,k\,T(k)\big)\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}+\frac{k^{2}}{2}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N}+\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B})\Big\}d\,{\rm vol}_{g}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\Big\{\frac{k^{2}}{2}\,(\tau+h_{N,B})\,(\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}-\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N})+2\,(k\,T(k)-k^{2}h_{B,B})\,\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B}\Big\}d\,{\rm vol}_{g}.

Since
\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,N},\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{N,B},\overset{\centerdot}{g}_{B,B}
are arbitrary functions on
M
, this completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\{T,N,B\}
be an orthonormal frame on a Riemannian manifold
(M^{3},g)
such that the plane field
Span(T,N)
is tangent to a Riemanian foliation,

\nabla_{T}\,T
is nonzero and parallel to
N
and each
T
-curve has constant curvature.
Set
\omega=T^{\flat}

and define
\varphi
by
\varphi(T)=0
,
\varphi(N)=B
and
\varphi(B)=-N
.
Then
(\varphi,\omega,T,g)
is critical for
gv^{*}
with respect to
g^{\top}
-variations.

Proof.

Since the plane field
Span(T,N)
is integrable, the
first equation of (
14
) holds.
Since the foliation is Riemannian, we have
\nabla_{B}\,B=0
. By this,
the second
equation of (
14
) holds.
∎

Example 1
.

We find solutions of (
14
) with
k\neq 0
using orthogonal coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

5
Conclusion

In the article, we applied the calculus of variations approach to finding best a.c.m. structures for a given manifold.
We defined a new Godbillon-Vey type functional
gv^{*}
for a 3-dimensional a.c.m. manifold,
found its Euler-Lagrange equations for all variations preserving the Reeb vector field and
constructed critical 3-dimensional a.c.m. manifolds having a double-twisted product structure, i.e., solutions
belonging to the class
C_{5}\oplus C_{12}
according to Chinea-Gonzalez classification.

In further work, we intend to study the critical a.c.m. manifolds (for
gv^{*}
) with nonintegrable distribution
\ker\omega
.
The following tasks also seem interesting:
study counterparts
gv^{*}_{1}=\int_{M}\eta\wedge d\eta^{*}
and
gv^{*}_{2}=\int_{M}\eta^{*}\wedge d\eta
of
gv^{*}
;
calculate the second variations of
gv^{*}
and
gv^{*}_{i}
and find their extrema.

We also intend to study the multidimensional case of
gv^{*}
.
For any a.c.m. manifold of dimension

, one may define one-form
\eta^{*}=\eta\circ\varphi=-k\,(\varphi N)^{\flat}

and analogously to the functionals

,
12
]
,
consider the following functionals:

gv^{*}_{s}=\int_{M}\eta^{*}\wedge(d\eta^{*})^{p}\wedge(d\omega)^{n-p},\quad 1\leq p\leq n.

A question arises:
what a.c.m. manifolds,
e.g., in

due to Chinea-Gonzalez classification,
are optimal for functionals (

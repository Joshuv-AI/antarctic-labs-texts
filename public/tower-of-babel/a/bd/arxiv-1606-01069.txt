The Geometry of Almost Einstein ( 𝟐 , 𝟑 , 𝟓 ) \boldsymbol{(2,3,5)} Distributions
Katja SAGERSCHNIG † and Travis WILLSE ‡

Abstract
We analyze the classic problem of existence of Einstein metrics in a given conformal structure for the class of conformal structures inducedf Nurowski’s construction by (oriented) ( 2 , 3 , 5 ) (2,3,5) distributions. We characterize in two ways such conformal structures that admit an almost Einstein scale: First, they are precisely the oriented conformal structures 𝐜 \mathbf{c} that are induced by at least two distinct oriented ( 2 , 3 , 5 ) (2,3,5) distributions; in this case there is a 1 1 -parameter family of such distributions that induce 𝐜 \mathbf{c} . Second, they are characterized by the existence of a holonomy reduction to SU ⁡ ( 1 , 2 ) {\rm SU}(1,2) , SL ⁡ ( 3 , ℝ ) {\rm SL}(3,{\mathbb{R}}) , or a particular semidirect product SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) ⋉ Q + {\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})\ltimes Q_{+} , according to the sign of the Einstein constant of the corresponding metric. Via the curved orbit decomposition formalism such a reduction partitions the underlying manifold into several submanifolds and endows each ith a geometric structure. This establishes novel links between ( 2 , 3 , 5 ) (2,3,5) distributions and many other geometries – several classical geometries among them – including: Sasaki–Einstein geometry and its paracomplex and null-complex analogues in dimension 5 5 ; Kähler–Einstein geometry and its paracomplex and null-complex analogues, Fefferman Lorentzian conformal structures, and para-Fefferman neutral conformal structures in dimension 4 4 ; CR geometry and the point geometry of second-order ordinary differential equations in dimension 3 3 ; and projective geometry in dimension 2 2 . We describe a generalized Fefferman construction that builds from a 4 4 -dimensional Kähler–Einstein or para-Kähler–Einstein structure a family of ( 2 , 3 , 5 ) (2,3,5) distributions that induce the same (Einstein) conformal structure. We exploit some of these links to construct new examples, establishing the existence of nonflat almost Einstein ( 2 , 3 , 5 ) (2,3,5) conformal structures for which the Einstein constant is positive and negative.

In the watershed article
[

, Bailey, Eastwood, and Gover showed that the existence of such a metric in a conformal structure (here, and always in this article, of dimension

on the original manifold. The generalization to the almost Einstein setting has substantial geometric consequences: The zero locus itself inherits a geometric structure, which can be realized as a natural limiting geometric structure of the metric on the complement. This arrangement leads to the notion of conformal compactification, which has received substantial attention in its own right, including in the physics literature
[
Susskind
]

In this subsubsection we follow

. For each

, the corresponding

is
(\eta_{V})_{u}:=\partial_{t}|_{0}[u\cdot\exp(tV)]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For a Lie group
G
and a closed subgroup
P
(with respective Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{p}
), a

of type
(G,P)
on a manifold

is a pair
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
, where
\mathcal{G}\to M
is a
P
-principal bundle and
\omega
is a

, that is, a section of

satisfying

1)

(right equivariance)
\omega_{u\cdot p}(T_{u}R^{p}\cdot\eta)=\Ad(p^{-1})(\omega_{u}(\eta))
for all
u\in\mathcal{G}
,
p\in P
,
\eta\in T_{u}\mathcal{G}
,

2)

(reproduction of fundamental vector fields)
\omega(\eta_{V})=V
for all
V\in\mathfrak{p}
, and

3)

(absolute parallelism)
\omega_{u}\colon T_{u}\mathcal{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an isomorphism for all
u\in\mathcal{G}
.

The
(flat) model
of Cartan geometry of type
(G,P)
is the pair
(G\to G/P,\omega_{\textrm{MC}})
, where
\omega_{\textrm{MC}}
is the Maurer–Cartan form on

defined by
(\omega_{\textrm{MC}})_{u}:=T_{u}L_{u^{-1}}
(here

denotes left multiplication by

). This form satisfies the identity

. We define the

(
form
)

to be the section

and say that

. This is the case iff around any point

that pulls back

.

One can show that the curvature

is horizontal (it is annihilated by vertical vector fields), so invoking the absolute parallelism and passing to the quotient defines an equivalent object

.

2.3.2
Holonomy

Given any Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
of type
(G,P)
, we can extend the Cartan connection
\omega
to a unique principal connection
\smash{\hat{\omega}}
on
\smash{\hat{\mathcal{G}}:=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}G}
characterized by (1)
G
-equivariance and (2)
\smash{\iota^{*}\hat{\omega}=\omega}
, where
\smash{\iota\colon\mathcal{G}\hookrightarrow\hat{\mathcal{G}}}
is the natural inclusion
u\mapsto[u,e]
. Then, to any point
\smash{\hat{u}\in\hat{\mathcal{G}}}
we can associate the holonomy group
\smash{\Hol_{\hat{u}}(\hat{\omega})\leq G}
. Different choices of
\smash{\hat{u}}
lead to conjugate subgroups of
G
, so the conjugacy class
\smash{\Hol(\hat{\omega})}
thereof is independent of
\smash{\hat{u}}
, and we define the
holonomy
of
\omega
(or just as well, of
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
) to be this class.

2.3.3
Tractor geometry

Fix a pair
(G,P)
as in Section
2.3.1
, denote the Lie algebra of
G
by
\mathfrak{g}
, and fix a
G
-representation
\mathbb{U}
. Then, for any Cartan geometry
(\pi\colon\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
of type
(G,P)
, we can form the associated
tractor bundle

\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathbb{U}\to M
, which we can also view as the associated bundle
\smash{\hat{\mathcal{G}}\times_{G}\mathbb{U}\to M}
. Then, the principal connection
\smash{\hat{\omega}}
on
\smash{\hat{\mathcal{G}}}
determined by
\omega
induces a vector bundle connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{U}}
on
\mathcal{U}
.

Of distinguished importance is the
adjoint tractor bundle

\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathfrak{g}
. The canonical map
\Pi_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathcal{A}\to TM
defined by
(u,V)\mapsto T_{u}\pi\cdot\omega_{u}^{-1}(V)
descends to a natural isomorphism
\smash{\mathcal{G}\times_{P}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}TM}
, and via this identification
\Pi_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}
is the bundle map associated to the canonical projection
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}
.

Since the curvature
\Omega
of
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is also
P
-equivariant, we may regard it as a section
K\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{A})
, and again we call it the
curvature
of
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
.

2.3.4
Parabolic geometry

In this article we will mostly (but not exclusively) work with geometries that can be realized as a special class of Cartan geometries that enjoy additional properties, most importantly suitable normalization conditions on
\omega
that guarantee (subject to a usually satisfied cohomological condition) a correspondence between Cartan geometries satisfying those conditions and geometric structures on the underlying manifold. We say that a Cartan geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
of type
(G,P)
is a
parabolic geometry
iff
G
is semisimple and
P
is a parabolic subgroup. For a detailed survey of parabolic geometry, including details of the below, see the standard reference
[
CapSlovak
]
.

Recall that a parabolic subgroup
P<G
determines a so-called
|k|
-grading on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
: This is a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-k}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+k}
compatible with the Lie bracket in the sense that
[\mathfrak{g}_{a},\mathfrak{g}_{b}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{a+b}
and minimal in the sense that none of the summands
\mathfrak{g}_{a}
,
a=-k,\ldots,k
, is zero. The grading induces a
P
-invariant filtration
(\mathfrak{g}^{a})
of
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{a}:=\mathfrak{g}_{a}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+k}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+k}
. We denote by
G_{0}<P
the subgroup of elements
p\in P
for which
\Ad(g)
preserves the grading
(\mathfrak{g}_{a})
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and by
P_{+}<P
the subgroup of elements
p\in P
for which
\Ad(p)\in\End(\mathfrak{g})
have homogeneity of degree
>0
with respect to the filtration
(\mathfrak{g}^{a})
; in particular, the Lie algebra of
P_{+}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{+}=\mathfrak{g}^{+1}=\mathfrak{g}_{+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+k}
.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, its Killing form is nondegenerate, and it induces a
P
-equivariant identification
(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})^{*}\leftrightarrow\mathfrak{p}_{+}
. Via this identification, for any
G
-representation
\mathbb{U}
we may identify the Lie algebra homology
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{p}_{+},\mathbb{U})
with the chain complex

\displaystyle\cdots\to\Lambda^{i+1}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})^{*}\otimes\mathbb{U}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial^{\ast}}}{{\to}}\Lambda^{i}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})^{*}\otimes\mathbb{U}\to\cdots.

The
Kostant codifferential

\partial^{*}
is
P
-equivariant, so it induces bundle maps
\partial^{*}\colon\Lambda^{i+1}T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{U}\to\Lambda^{i}T^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{U}
between the associated bundles.

The normalization conditions for a parabolic geometry
(\mathcal{G}\to M,\omega)
are that

1)

(normality) the curvature
\kappa
satisfies
\partial^{*}\kappa=0
, and

2)

(regularity) the curvature
\kappa
satisfies
\kappa(u)(\mathfrak{g}^{i},\mathfrak{g}^{j})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{i+j+1}
for all
u\in\mathcal{G}
and all
i
,
j
.

Finally, tractor bundles associated to parabolic geometries inherit additional natural structure: Given a
G
-representation
\mathbb{U}
,
P
determines a natural filtration
(\mathbb{U}^{a})
of
\mathbb{U}
by successive action of the nilpotent Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{+}<\mathfrak{g}
, namely

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}_{+}\cdot\mathbb{U}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}_{+}\cdot(\mathfrak{p}_{+}\cdot\mathbb{U})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\{0\}.

(2.14)

Since the filtration
(\mathbb{U}^{a})
of
\mathbb{U}
is
P
-invariant, it determines a bundle filtration
(\mathcal{U}^{a})
of the tractor bundle
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathbb{U}
.

For the adjoint representation
\mathfrak{g}
itself, this filtration (appropriately indexed) is just
(\mathfrak{g}^{a})
, and the images of the filtrands
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathfrak{g}^{-k}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq\mathcal{G}\times_{P}\mathfrak{g}^{-1}
under the projection
\Pi_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}
comprise a canonical filtration
TM=T^{-k}M\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq T^{-1}M
of the tangent bundle.

2.3.5
Oriented conformal structures

The group
\SO(p+1,q+1)
,
p+q\geq 3
, acts transitively on the space of isotropic rays in the standard representation
\mathbb{V}
, and the stabilizer subgroup
\bar{P}
of such a ray is parabolic. There is an equivalence of categories between regular, normal parabolic geometries of type
(\SO(p+1,q+1),\bar{P})
and oriented conformal structures of signature
(p,q)

[
CapSlovak
, Section 4.1.2]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
conformal structure

(M,\mathbf{c})
is an equivalence class
\mathbf{c}
of metrics on
M
, where we declare two metrics to be equivalent if one is a positive, smooth multiple of the other. The signature of
\mathbf{c}
is the signature of any (equivalently, every)
g\in\mathbf{c}
, and we say that
(M,\mathbf{c})
is
oriented
iff
M
is oriented. The
conformal holonomy
of an oriented conformal structure
\mathbf{c}
is
\Hol(\mathbf{c}):=\Hol(\omega)
, where
\omega
is the normal Cartan connection corresponding to
\mathbf{c}
.

We can choose a basis of
\mathbb{V}
for which the nondegenerate, symmetric bilinear form
H
preserved by
\SO(p+1,q+1)
has block matrix representation

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&\Sigma&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2.15)

(With respect to the basis
(E_{a})
, the matrix representation
[H_{\Phi}]
(
2.6
) of the bilinear form
H_{\Phi}
determined by the explicit expression (
2.5
) for
\Phi
has the form (
2.15
).) The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(p+1,

q+1)
consists of exactly the elements

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}b&Z&0\\
X&B&-\Sigma^{-1}Z^{\top}\\
0&-X^{\top}\Sigma&-b\end{pmatrix},

where
B\in\mathfrak{so}(\Sigma)
,
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p+q}
,
Z\in(\mathbb{R}^{p+q})^{*}
. The first element of the basis is isotropic, and if we take choose the preferred isotropic ray in
\mathbb{V}
to be the one determined by that element, the corresponding Lie algebra grading on
\mathfrak{so}(p+1,q+1)
is the one defined by the labeling

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{g}_{0}&\mathfrak{g}_{+1}&0\\
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}&\mathfrak{g}_{0}&\mathfrak{g}_{+1}\\
0&\mathfrak{g}_{-1}&\mathfrak{g}_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.16)

Since the grading on
\mathfrak{so}(p+1,q+1)
induced by
\bar{P}
has the form
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+1}
, any parabolic geometry of this type is regular. The normality condition coincides with Cartan’s normalization condition for what is now called a Cartan geometry of this type
[
CapSlovak
, Section 4.1.2]
.

2.3.6
Oriented
\boldsymbol{(2,3,5)}
distributions

The group
\G_{2}
acts transitively on the space of
H_{\Phi}
-isotropic rays in
\mathbb{V}
, and the stabilizer subgroup
Q
of such a ray is parabolic
[
Sagerschnig
]
. The subgroup
Q
is the intersection of
\G_{2}
with the stabilizer subgroup
\bar{P}<\SO(3,4)
of the preferred isotropic ray in Section
2.3.5
. In particular, the first basis element is isotropic, and if we again choose the preferred isotropic ray to be the one determined by that element, the corresponding Lie algebra grading on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is the one defined by the block decomposition (
2.7
) and the labeling

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{g}_{0}&\mathfrak{g}_{+1}&\mathfrak{g}_{+2}&\mathfrak{g}_{+3}&0\\
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}&\mathfrak{g}_{0}&\mathfrak{g}_{+1}&\mathfrak{g}_{+2}&\mathfrak{g}_{+3}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{-2}&\mathfrak{g}_{-1}&0&\mathfrak{g}_{+1}&\mathfrak{g}_{+2}\\
\mathfrak{g}_{-3}&\mathfrak{g}_{-2}&\mathfrak{g}_{-1}&\mathfrak{g}_{0}&\mathfrak{g}_{+1}\\
0&\mathfrak{g}_{-3}&\mathfrak{g}_{-2}&\mathfrak{g}_{-1}&\mathfrak{g}_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

There is an equivalence of categories between regular, normal parabolic geometries of type
(\G_{2},Q)
and so-called oriented
(2,3,5)
distributions
[
CapSlovak
, Section 4.3.2]
.

On a manifold
M
, define the bracket of distributions
\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F}\subseteq TM
to be the set
[\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F}]:=\{[\alpha,\beta]_{x}\colon x\in M;\alpha\in\Gamma(\mathbf{E}),\beta\in\Gamma(\mathbf{F})\}\subseteq TM
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
(2,3,5)
distribution
is a
2
-plane distribution
\mathbf{D}
on a
5
-manifold
M
that is maximally nonintegrable in the sense that (1)
[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]
is a
3
-plane distribution, and (2)
[\mathbf{D},[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]]=TM
. A
(2,3,5)
distribution is
oriented
iff the bundle
\mathbf{D}\to M
is oriented.

An orientation of
\mathbf{D}
determines an orientation of
M
and vice versa. The appropriate restrictions of the Lie bracket of vector fields descend to natural vector bundle isomorphisms
\smash{\mathcal{L}\colon\Lambda^{2}\mathbf{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]/\mathbf{D}}
and
\smash{\mathcal{L}\colon\mathbf{D}\otimes([\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]/\mathbf{D})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}TM/[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]}
; these are components of the
Levi bracket
.

For a regular, normal parabolic geometry
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
of type
(\G_{2},Q)
, the underlying
(2,3,5)
distribution
\mathbf{D}
is
T^{-1}M=\mathcal{G}\times_{Q}(\mathfrak{g}^{-1}/\mathfrak{q})
, and
[\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}]
is
T^{-2}M=\mathcal{G}\times_{Q}(\mathfrak{g}^{-2}/\mathfrak{q})
.

Conformal geometry

In this subsection we partly follow
[
BEG

2.4.1
Conformal density bundles

A conformal structure
(M,\mathbf{c})
of signature
(p,q)
(denote
n:=p+q
), determines a family of natural
(
conformal
)
density bundles
on
M
: Denote by
\mathcal{E}[1]
the positive
(2n)
th root of the canonically oriented line bundle
(\Lambda^{n}TM)^{2}
, and its respective
w
th integer powers by
\mathcal{E}[w]
;
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}[0]
is the trivial bundle with fiber
\mathbb{R}
, and there are natural identifications

. Given any vector bundle
B\to M
, we denote
B[w]:=B\otimes\mathcal{E}[w]
, and refer to the sections of
B[w]
as
sections of
B
of conformal weight
w

We may view
\mathbf{c}
itself as the canonical
conformal metric
,
\mathbf{g}_{ab}\in\Gamma(S^{2}T^{*}M[2])
. Contraction with
\mathbf{g}_{ab}
determines an isomorphism
TM\to T^{*}M[2]
, which we may use to raise and lower indices of objects on the tangent bundle at the cost of an adjustment of conformal weight. By construction, the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
of any metric
g\in\mathbf{c}
preserves
\mathbf{g}_{ab}
and its inverse,
\mathbf{g}^{ab}\in\Gamma(S^{2}TM[-2])
.

We call a nowhere zero section
\tau\in\Gamma(\mathcal{E}[1])
a
scale
of
\mathbf{c}
. A scale determines
trivializations

\smash{B[w]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}B}
,
b\mapsto\underline{b}:=\tau^{-w}b
, of all conformally weighted bundles, and in particular a representative metric
\tau^{-2}\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{c}
.

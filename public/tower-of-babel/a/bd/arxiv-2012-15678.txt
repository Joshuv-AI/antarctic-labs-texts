Abstract
. This study develops a non-asymptotic Gaussian approximation theory for distributions of M-estimators, which are defined as maximizers of empirical criterion functions. In existing mathematical statistics literature, numerous studies have focused on approximating the distributions of the M-estimators for statistical inference. In contrast to the existing approaches, which mainly focus on limiting behaviors, this study employs a non-asymptotic approach, establishes abstract Gaussian approximation results for maximizers of empirical criteria, and proposes a Gaussian multiplier bootstrap approximation method. Our developments can be considered as extensions of the seminal works (Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and Kato (2013, 2014, 2015)) on the approximation theory for distributions of suprema of empirical processes toward their maximizers. Through this work, we shed new lights on the statistical theory of M-estimators. Our theory covers not only regular estimators, such as the least absolute deviations, but also some non-regular cases where it is difficult to derive or to approximate numerically the limiting distributions such as non-Donsker classes and cube root estimators.



1.
Introduction

This study focuses on non-asymptotic approximations for distributions of M-estimators. Let
Z_{1},...,Z_{n}
be independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables in a measurable space
(\mathcal{Z},\mathscr{Z})
with a generating measure
P_{Z}
. Let
\Theta
be a Banach space equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|
, and
f_{\theta}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable criterion function for
\theta\in\Theta
. The M-estimator
\widehat{\theta}
maximizes the empirical criterion

\displaystyle Q_{n}(\theta):=\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}f_{\theta}(Z_{i}),

(1)

over the parameter space
\Theta
. We are interested in approximating the distribution of
\widehat{\theta}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\theta}\in A)
for any Borel subset
A\subset\Theta
. In particular, we establish a Gaussian approximation result for the distribution of the M-estimator:

\displaystyle\sup_{A}|\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\theta}\in A)-\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A)|=O(r_{n}),

for some positive sequence
r_{n}
decaying to zero, where
\widehat{\theta}_{G}
maximizes a Gaussian process
G(\theta)
with the same mean and covariance of
Q_{n}(\theta)
.

The approximation theory for distributions of M-estimators is one of central topics in mathematical statistics. Its general theory is summarized in
[
38
,
37
,
20
]
for example. Also, there are numerous specific applications
[
8
,
29
,
28
,
23
,
16
,
3
,
18
]
and generalizations
[
19
,
34
,
33
,
7
,
21
]
, among many others. In the statistics literature, it is common to employ limiting approximations. In a regular setup where the empirical criterion is sufficiently smooth, the M-estimator is typically asymptotically normal with the
\sqrt{n}
convergence rate. Its properties as a distributional approximation is well investigated
[
24
,
25
]
. For certain non-regular setups or models, the M-estimators yield more intricate limiting distributions, such as the Chernoff distribution
[
8
]
. More generically, the argmax continuous mapping theorem gives limiting distributions constructed by Gaussian processes
[
14
,
31
]
. For more general parameter spaces, Donsker’s theorem takes Brownian bridges as limiting distributions of empirical processes
[
13
]
. These asymptotic approximation results have been widely used in statistics to construct inference methods for the parameters.

In contrast to the existing approaches, this study develops a non-asymptotic approximation theory for the distributions of M-estimators, which does not require knowledge of the limiting distribution. Specifically, we derive a tractable random variable
\widehat{\theta}_{G}
whose distribution is sufficiently close to that of the target M-estimator
\widehat{\theta}
for each
n
. The random variable
\widehat{\theta}_{G}
used in the approximation consists of the maximizer of a Gaussian process on the parameter space.

Our theoretical results are summarized as follows. First, we develop an abstract non-asymptotic approximation theorem for the distributions of M-estimators (Theorem
1
). We present sufficient conditions for the main theorem and also provide a convergence analysis for M-estimators (Corollaries
1
and
2
). Second, we develop a useful multiplier bootstrap method based on the non-asymptotic approximation theorem and show its validity (Theorem
2
). A hypothesis testing method based on the bootstrap theorem is also discussed (Proposition
2
). Third, we extend our approximation theory to semiparametric M-estimators with nuisance parameters (Theorem
3
), as well as M-estimators with non-Donsker parameter spaces (Theorem
4
). For the non-Donsker case, we provide a coupling inequality with an additional smoothness assumption. Finally, we illustrate usefulness of our non-asymptotic approximation theory by three examples: a cubic root estimator, least absolute deviation, and minimum volume prediction.

Compared to the conventional asymptotic approach, there are at least three attractive features of our non-asymptotic approximation approach. First, it may work under weaker conditions than those for deriving the limit distributions. For regular cases, it is often the case that the criterion functions are sufficiently smooth. For some non-regular cases, it is not trivial to derive the limiting distributions of the M-estimators, such as criteria defined by non-Donsker classes. Also, the naive nonparametric bootstrap is known to be asymptotically invalid
[
1
,
22
,
32
]
. In contrast, our non-asymptotic approach does not require differentiability of the criterion functions, even for their expectations. Moreover, our non-asymptotic approximation and associated bootstrap theory may work even for such non-regular cases. Second, based on the non-asymptotic approximation, our bootstrap method is computationally attractive in some non-regular cases. For example, the Chernoff distribution is given by the maxima of a Gaussian process, which requires a lot of samplings to calculate their quantiles, as noted by
[
17
]
. Our method can avoid such the difficulty by the Gaussian multiplier bootstrap. Third, our method is applicable regardless of whether the limiting distribution is available, e.g., the distribution may not be available for a large-scale parameter space, such as a non-Donsker class. Even in such scenarios, our method can approximate the distribution of the M-estimator.

Our theoretical developments build upon the seminal works by Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and Kato
[
9
,
12
,
10
,
11
]
, which develop the approximation theory for distributions of suprema of empirical processes. We extend their various techniques to be adapted to investigate argmax values as well. In particular, our own technical contributions are summarized as follows. First, we provide a representation of the distribution of the M-estimator based on a transformation of the distribution of suprema of empirical processes. This representation allows us to apply the Gaussian approximation theory from
[
11
]
with a smooth transformation of Gaussian variables. In this process, we also derive a new Stein-type equality to relax existing conditions on moments. Second, we develop a conditional anti-concentration inequality for Gaussian variables that prevents degeneracy of the argmax distribution. This is an extension of the anti-concentration inequality used to study the Gaussian approximation. Since our approximation of argmax uses a difference between two Gaussian processes, we need to develop a conditional version of the conventional anti-concentration inequality. Third, we develop an eigenvalue inequality to ensure that several specific M-estimators satisfy the conditions of our theory. Since our non-asymptotic theory employs relatively different regularity conditions from the conventional asymptotic theories, we provide a detailed evaluation for eigenvalues of certain covariance matrices based on the criterion function.

2.
Gaussian Approximation for M-estimator

In this section we develop a Gaussian approximation for the distribution of the M-estimator. With i.i.d. observations
Z_{1},...,Z_{n}
from
P_{Z}
on
\mathcal{Z}
and a criterion function
f_{\theta}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
for
\theta\in\Theta
, we consider the empirical criterion
Q_{n}(\theta)
as in (
1
) and the M-estimator
\widehat{\theta}:=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}Q_{n}(\theta)
. If there are multiple maxima of
Q_{n}(\theta)
, we arbitrarily pick one of them.

The aim of this study is to approximate the distribution of
\widehat{\theta}
for each
n
. To this end, we use a maximizer of a Gaussian process as an approximator. In particular, we consider a tight Gaussian process
\{G(\theta)\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
with its mean
\mathbb{E}_{G}[G(\theta)]=\mathbb{E}_{Z}[f_{\theta}(Z)]
and covariance function
\Cov_{G}(G(\theta),G(\theta^{\prime}))=\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta}(Z),f_{\theta^{\prime}}(Z))
for
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
. Existence of this Gaussian process will be shown under the conditions below. In this section, we establish an approximation result for the distribution of
\widehat{\theta}
by that of

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}_{G}:=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}G(\theta).

(2)

By Theorem 1 in
[
4
]
, a maximizer of a Gaussian process on a separable space is known to be unique almost surely.

2.1.
Basic Assumption

Letting

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{k}:=\mathbb{E}_{Z}\left[\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|f_{\theta}(Z)-\mathbb{E}_{Z}[f_{\theta}(Z)]|^{k}\right],

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we impose the following basic conditions.

Assumption 1

(Basic)
.

The following conditions hold:

(A1)

\Theta
is separable and pointwise measurable; there exists a countable set
\tilde{\Theta}\subset\Theta
such that for any
\theta\in\Theta
there exists a sequence
\{\widetilde{\theta}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\tilde{\Theta}
satisfying
\widetilde{\theta}_{i}\to\theta
as
i\to\infty
.

(A2)

For any
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
, it holds
\mathbb{E}_{Z}[|f_{\theta}(Z)-f_{\theta^{\prime}}(Z)|]\leq c_{\ell}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|^{q}
with a constant
c_{\ell}>0
and
q\in(0,1]
.

(A3)

There exist constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
such that

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\Theta,\|\cdot\|)\leq C\varepsilon^{-\alpha},

holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

(A4)

There exists an envelope function
F:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F(z)\geq\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|f_{\theta}(z)|
for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}
, and
\|F\|_{L^{2}(P_{Z})}\vee\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(P_{Z})}\leq b<\infty
for some
b>0
, and
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{3}
is finite.

The condition (A1) deals with the nonmeasurability of suprema of uncountable sets (see p.108 in
[
38
]
). Based on this assumption, we can characterize
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}Q_{n}(\theta)
by using
\sup_{\theta\in\widetilde{\Theta}}Q_{n}(\theta)
, which is a measurable map from
\mathcal{Z}
to
\mathbb{R}
. The measurability of the supremum will be used to handle the maximizer of
Q_{n}(\theta)
.

The condition (A2) is the Lipschitz (or Hölder) continuity of
\mathbb{E}_{Z}[|f_{\theta}(Z)|]
in
\theta
. Since the continuity is imposed on the expected value, this condition may hold even when
f_{\theta}(\cdot)
is discontinuous in
\theta
. In addition, our condition does not require differentiability in
\theta
, only the continuity. Hence, it is a relatively mild condition in the literature for M-estimators (see
[
33
,
21
]
for recent examples), which typically requires (twice) differentiability of the expected criterion function. See our illustrations for the least absolute deviation in Section
6
.

The condition (A3) is on the metric entropy of
\Theta
. Similar conditions are frequently used in the studies that used the empirical process theory (see
[
38
]
for an overview). This condition is satisfied for a compact finite-dimensional parameter space and an infinite-dimensional functional space with sufficient smoothness. In our analysis, the condition (A3) is employed to guarantee the existence of the Gaussian process
G(\theta)
, which is shown by Lemma
8
(see also Lemma 2.2 in
[
11
]
) using the notion of pre-Gaussianity in
[
38
]
. We will relax this condition in Section
5
.

The condition (A4) is on integrability of the envelope function
F
and the third moment of
f_{\theta}(Z)
. This moment condition is utilized to apply approximation techniques using Stein’s identity.

2.2.
Covariance Assumption

We additionally impose an assumption on positive definiteness of the covariance function
\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta}(Z),f_{\theta^{\prime}}(Z))
. For a finite parameter subset
T\subset\Theta
, consider a matrix
\Sigma=\Var_{Z}((f_{\theta}(Z))_{\theta\in T})\in\mathbb{R}^{|T|\times|T|}
whose
(i,j)
-th element is
\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta_{i}}(Z),f_{\theta_{j}}(Z))
with
\theta_{i},\theta_{j}\in T
. We introduce the following terminology.

Definition 1

(Coherently positive definite covariance)
.

A
|T|\times|T|
covariance matrix
\Sigma
is
coherently positive definite with

\underline{\sigma}^{2}
, if for any
A\subset T
with
A^{c}=T\backslash A
and
\Sigma=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma_{A}&\Sigma_{AA^{c}}\\
\Sigma_{A^{c}A}&\Sigma_{A^{c}}\end{array}\right]
, the diagonal elements of the matrix
\Sigma_{A}-\Sigma_{AA^{c}}\Sigma_{A^{c}}^{-1}\Sigma_{A^{c}A}
are no less than
\underline{\sigma}^{2}
.

We impose the following assumption on coherent positive definiteness of covariance matrices induced by
f_{\theta}(Z)
.

Assumption 2

(Coherent covariance function)
.

For any
\delta\in(0,1]
, there exists a
\delta
-packing set
T
of
\Theta
and constant
c>0
such that a covariance matrix
\Sigma=\Var_{Z}((f_{\theta}(Z))_{\theta\in T})
is coherently positive definite with
\underline{\sigma}^{2}\geq c\delta^{2\kappa}
for some
\kappa\in(0,1)
.

This condition can be considered as strengthening of positive definiteness of the covariance function, which avoids degeneracy of the covariance matrices. Based on this condition, we can guarantee that the maximum value of the approximating Gaussian process does not degenerate to a single point
[
11
,
10
]
, and thus its maximizer has sufficient variations. In Section
6
, we provide some examples that satisfy this assumption.

To understand the nature of this condition, we provide a sufficient condition for coherent positive definiteness. Let
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)
be the smallest eigenvalue of a matrix
\Sigma
.

Proposition 1

(Coherent eigenvalue condition)
.

If a symmetric positive definite matrix
\Sigma
satisfies
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\geq\underline{\sigma}^{2}
, then
\Sigma
is coherently positive definite with
\underline{\sigma}^{2}
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

It follows by Corollary 2.3 (page 50) in
[
40
]
.
∎

This result suggests that Assumption
2
is closely related to minimum eigenvalues of covariance matrices.

2.3.
Main Theorem

We now present our main result of this paper, a Gaussian approximation for the distribution of the M-estimator. Let

\displaystyle H(\varepsilon):=\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\Theta,\|\cdot\|),\mbox{~and~}J(\varepsilon):=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\sqrt{1+H(\delta)}d\delta,

for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be the metric entropy and its integration. Our abstract approximation theorem is presented as follows.

Theorem 1

(Gaussian approximation)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. Then, for any Borel subset
A\subset\Theta
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, and
n\geq\underline{n}
with an existing
\underline{n}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|\leq C\Biggl\{\varepsilon+\frac{H(\varepsilon)}{n^{5/8}}+\frac{\Delta(n,\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^{\kappa}}(\sqrt{H(\varepsilon)}+1)\Biggr\},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{1},\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{3},c_{\ell},q
, and
b
, and
\Delta(n,\varepsilon)
is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta(n,\varepsilon)=\varepsilon+\frac{J(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}}{n^{1/2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}+{\log(1/\varepsilon)}}{n}.

This theorem characterizes the approximation error for the distribution on the M-estimator
\widehat{\theta}
under the generating distribution
\mathbb{P}_{Z}
by using its Gaussian counterpart
\widehat{\theta}_{G}
under
\mathbb{P}_{G}
. Applications of this theorem for common finite- and infinite-dimensional parameter spaces are presented as follows.

Corollary 1

(Finite-dimensional M-estimator)
.

Let
\Theta
be a compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\|\cdot\|
be the Euclidean norm. Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|=O\left(n^{-5/8}\log n\right).

Proof of Corollary
1
.

By the setting of
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and Lemma
7
, we have

\displaystyle H(\varepsilon)\lesssim\log C_{\Theta}+\log(1/\varepsilon^{d}),\mbox{~and~}J(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)},

for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Combining these bounds with Theorem
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|\lesssim\varepsilon+\frac{{\log(1/\varepsilon)}}{n^{5/8}}+\varepsilon^{1-\kappa}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}.

By setting
\varepsilon=n^{-5/(8-8\kappa)}
, the conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 2

(Infinite-dimensional M-estimator)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
hold with
\alpha/2+\kappa<1
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|=O\left(n^{-5/8}\vee n^{-(1-\alpha/2-\kappa)/\alpha}\vee n^{-(5-5\kappa-\alpha)/(8-8\kappa)}\right).

Proof of Corollary
2
.

By the condition (A3) in Assumption
1
and Lemma
7
, we have
J(\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-\alpha/2}
. Combining this with Theorem
1
yields

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|\lesssim\varepsilon^{1-\alpha/2-\kappa}+\frac{\varepsilon^{-\alpha}}{n^{5/8}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha-\kappa}}{n^{1/2}}.

By setting
\varepsilon=n^{-5/(8+4\alpha-8\kappa)}\vee n^{-\alpha}\vee n^{-(5-5\kappa-\alpha)/(8-8\kappa)}
, the conclusion follows.
∎

2.4.
Sketch of Proof for Theorem
1

The proof of Theorem
1
consists of three main steps: (i) discretization of
\Theta
, (ii) smoothing and the Stein approximation, and (iii) conditional anti-concentration. In this subsection, we provide a rough overview. The rigorous full proof will be provided in the Appendix.

(i) Discretization of
\Theta
:
We consider a finite subset
\Theta_{M}\subset\Theta
with
|\Theta_{M}|=M
and approximate the distribution of
\widehat{\theta}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}Q_{n}(\theta)
by that of
\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta_{M}}Q_{n}(\theta)
.

Fix
A\subset\Theta
and define its complement
A^{c}=\Theta\backslash A
.
Let
\Theta_{M}\subset\Theta
be the finite subset such that
\Theta_{M}
is an
\varepsilon
-cover of
\Theta
, that is, for any
\theta\in\Theta
there exists
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{M}
such that
\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|\leq\varepsilon
.
We also define their discretized analogs
A_{M}:=A\cap\Theta_{M}
and
A_{M}^{c}:=A^{c}\cap\Theta_{M}
.
When
A_{M}
and
A_{M}^{c}
are non-empty, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}\in A\right)

\displaystyle\approx\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\max_{\theta\in A_{M}}Q_{n}(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in A^{c}_{M}}Q_{n}(\theta)+n^{-1/2}\Delta(n,\varepsilon)\geq 0\right)=:P_{1}.

To obtain this approximation, we evaluate the effect of the discretization for the parameter space
\sup_{(\theta,\theta^{\prime}):\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|\leq\varepsilon}\{Q_{n}(\theta)-Q_{n}(\theta^{\prime})\}
by the empirical process technique (Lemma 2.2 in
[
11
]
).
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\max_{\theta\in A}G(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in A^{c}}G(\theta)\geq 0\right)

\displaystyle\approx\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\max_{\theta\in A_{M}}G(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in A^{c}_{M}}G(\theta)+n^{-1/2}\Delta(n,\varepsilon)\geq 0\right)=:P_{2}.

(ii) Smoothing and the Stein approximation
:
In this step, evaluate the difference between the probabilities
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.
The key technique is the approximation by Stein’s identity
[
11
,
9
,
15
]
.
That is, for a smooth function
h\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{M})
and i.i.d. random vectors
X_{1},...,X_{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{M}
with
\bar{X}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in[n]}X_{i}
, we can obtain
\mathbb{E}_{X}[h(\bar{X})]\approx\mathbb{E}_{W}[h(W)]
, where
W
is an
M
-dimensional Gaussian random vector such that
\mathbb{E}_{W}[W]=\mathbb{E}_{X}[\bar{X}]
and
\Var_{W}(W)=\Var_{X}(\bar{X})
.
We can rewrite the probability
P_{1}
as

\displaystyle P_{1}=\mathbb{E}_{Z}\biggl[\underbrace{\textbf{1}\left\{\max_{\theta\in A_{M}}Q_{n}(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in A^{c}_{M}}Q_{n}(\theta)+{n^{-1/2}}\Delta(n,\varepsilon)\geq 0\right\}}_{=:h_{1}}\biggr],

and
P_{2}
with a similar form.
Here, owing to the discretization step,
h_{1}
is a function of
n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in[n]}X_{i}
with
X_{i}=(f_{\theta_{m}}(Z_{i}))_{m\in[M]}
and the same part for
P_{2}
is a function of a sum of the Gaussian vector
W_{i}=(G_{\theta_{m}})_{m\in[M]}
. Thus, the approximation technique may show
P_{1}\approx P_{2}
.

To apply the technique, we approximate the part
h_{1}
by a smooth function of
\bar{X}
. By using the softmax approximation and soft-step function by
[
9
]
, we can show that

\displaystyle|P_{1}-P_{2}|=O\left(\frac{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{3}H(\varepsilon)}{n^{5/8}}\right).

In this step, we develop the local coupling technique (Lemma
3
) and reduce some restrictions such as truncation or higher moments in
[
9
]
.

(iii) Conditional anti-concentration
:
To finish the proof, we move the term
n^{-1/2}\Delta(n,\varepsilon)
from the inside of
P_{2}
to its outside, that is, we show

\displaystyle P_{2}\approx\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\max_{\theta\in A_{M}}G(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in A^{c}_{M}}G(\theta)\geq 0\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/2}\underline{\sigma}}\Delta(n,\varepsilon)\right),

where
\underline{\sigma}
is a lower bound of covariance appearing in Assumption
2
. To this aim, the anti-concentration inequality
[
10
,
9
]
is often utilized to show non-degeneracy of the maximum of the Gaussian vectors. However, the existing inequality does not work in this setting because of the multiple maxima. Therefore we develop the conditional anti-concentration inequality (Lemma
5
) to adapt to our setting.

By combining these steps, we get the result in Theorem
1
. ∎

3.
Bootstrap Approximation

In this section we discuss a multiplier bootstrap method to approximate the distribution of the Gaussian counterpart
\widehat{\theta}_{G}
. Since computing maximizers of Gaussian processes is costly (as discussed in
[
17
]
), the bootstrap approach provides a practical alternative to approximate the distribution of the M-estimator.

Let
e_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
for
i\in[n]
be standard normal multipliers which are independent of
Z_{1},...,Z_{n}
, and
P_{n}(\theta):=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{\theta}(Z_{i})
be the empirical criterion function. Define the multiplier bootstrap process as

\displaystyle B_{n}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=P_{n}(\theta)+E_{n}(\theta),

where

\displaystyle E_{n}(\theta):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}(f_{\theta}(Z_{i})-P_{n}(\theta)).

Then our bootstrap counterpart for
\widehat{\theta}
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}_{B}:=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}B_{n}(\theta).

Let
\mathbb{P}_{e|Z}
be the conditional distribution of
e_{1},...,e_{n}
given
Z_{1},...,Z_{n}
. We obtain the following result for the bootstrap approximation.

Theorem 2

(Bootstrap approximation)
.

Let
\Theta
be a compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\|\cdot\|
be the Euclidean norm. Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. Then, for any Borel subset
A\subset\Theta,\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, and
n\geq\underline{n}
with an existing
\underline{n}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{e|Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{B}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|\leq C\left\{\varepsilon+\frac{H(\varepsilon)}{n^{5/8}}+\frac{\Delta(n,\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^{\kappa}}(\sqrt{H(\varepsilon)}+1)\right\},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\underline{\sigma},\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{1},\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{3},c_{\ell}
, and
b
.

This theorem characterizes the approximation error for the distribution of the Gaussian counterpart
\widehat{\theta}_{G}
under
\mathbb{P}_{G}
by the Gaussian multiplier bootstrap distribution. Owing to this result, we can give analogous corollaries for the finite- and infinite-dimensional parameter spaces.

Corollary 3

(Bootstrap for finite-dimensional
\Theta
)
.

Let
\Theta
be a compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\|\cdot\|
be the Euclidean norm. Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
, and the setting in Corollary
1
hold. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{e|Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{B}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|=O\left(n^{-5/8}\log n\right).

Corollary 4

(Bootstrap for infinite-dimensional
\Theta
)
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
with
\alpha/2+\kappa<1
, and the setting in Corollary
2
hold. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{e|Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{B}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{G}\in A\right)\right|=O\left(n^{-5/8}\vee n^{-(1-\alpha/2-\kappa)/\alpha}\vee n^{-(5-5\kappa-\alpha)/(8-8\kappa)}\right).

The proofs of these corollaries are omitted because they are almost same as those of Corollaries
1
and
2
.

3.1.
Illustration: Hypothesis Testing

Theorems
1
and
2
can be used to conduct hypothesis testing on the true parameter value defined as

\displaystyle\theta_{0}:=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb{E}_{Z}[f_{\theta}(Z)].

We assume that
\theta_{0}
is unique, and consider the testing problem for the null hypothesis
H_{0}:\theta_{0}=\theta^{*}
against the alternative
H_{1}:\theta_{0}\neq\theta^{*}
, where
\theta^{*}\in\Theta
is a hypothetical value. Suppose
\Theta
contains the origin
\{0\}
.

Here we present a testing procedure by sample splitting. We split the observations
\{Z_{1},...,Z_{n}\}
into disjoint sets
Z^{\prime}
and
Z^{\prime\prime}
. Pick a significance level
s\in(0,1)
. Based on
Z^{\prime}
, we compute
\widehat{\theta}_{B}
repeatedly and find a set
\bar{A}_{s}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{e|Z^{\prime}}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{B}\in\bar{A}_{s}\right)=1-s.

The set
\bar{A}_{s}
satisfying this property is not unique, but a reasonable choice is the one with the minimum volume. Then by using the other observations
Z^{\prime\prime}
, we compute
\widehat{\theta}
and construct an acceptance region for
H_{0}
as

\displaystyle\widehat{A}_{s}^{*}:=\{\theta^{*}-\widehat{\theta}+\theta_{s}\mid\theta_{s}\in\bar{A}_{s}\}.

The validity of this acceptance region is established as follows.

Proposition 2

(Hypothesis testing)
.

Suppose that the setting in Theorem
2
and
\widehat{A}_{s}^{*}\subset\Theta
hold. Then under the null hypothesis
H_{0}:\theta_{0}=\theta^{*}
and for any
s\in(0,1)
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\theta_{0}\in\widehat{A}_{s}^{*}\right)\to 1-s,\mbox{~as~}n\to\infty.

The proof is contained in Appendix. By considering different hypothetical values for
\theta^{*}
, we can also construct a confidence set for
\theta_{0}
.

4.
M-estimator with Nuisance Parameters

Our approximation theory can be extended to the case where the criterion function contains nuisance parameters. This extension is particularly useful to allow for semiparametric models. Let
\theta\in\Theta
be our parameters of interest and
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
be nuisance parameters. Let
\Theta\times\mathcal{H}
be a joint parameter space with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\Theta\times\mathcal{H}}:=\|\cdot\|+\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. For
(\theta,\eta)\in\Theta\times\mathcal{H}
, let
f_{\theta,\eta}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
be a criterion function and define its empirical mean

\displaystyle S_{n}(\theta,\eta):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{\theta,\eta}(Z_{i}).

Then we consider the semiparametric M-estimator

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}_{S}:=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}\max_{\eta\in\mathcal{H}}S_{n}(\theta,\eta),

where
\eta
is profiled out. To approximate the distribution of
\widehat{\theta}_{S}
, we introduce a tight Gaussian process
\{\Gamma(\theta,\eta)\mid(\theta,\eta)\in\Theta\times\mathcal{H}\}
with its mean
\mathbb{E}_{\Gamma}[\Gamma(\theta,\eta)]=\mathbb{E}_{Z}[f_{\theta,\eta}(Z)]
and covariance function
\Cov_{\Gamma}(\Gamma(\theta,\eta),\Gamma(\theta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}))=\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta,\eta}(Z),f_{\theta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}(Z))
for all
(\theta,\eta),(\theta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in\Theta\times\mathcal{H}
.
Its existence is shown in Lemma
8
in Appendix. The Gaussian counterpart of
\widehat{\theta}_{S}
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}_{\Gamma}:=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}\max_{\eta\in\mathcal{H}}\Gamma(\theta,\eta).

We note that this setup covers semiparametric M-estimators, where
\Theta
is finite-dimensional and
\mathcal{H}
is infinite-dimensional.

We now give some assumptions to characterize approximation errors for the distribution of
\widehat{\theta}_{S}
by that of
\widehat{\theta}_{\Gamma}
. Most assumptions are analogous to the ones for the no nuisance parameter case in Section
2
. Define

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{S,k}:=\mathbb{E}_{Z}\left[\sup_{(\theta,\eta)\in\Theta\times\mathcal{H}}|f_{\theta,\eta}(Z)-\mathbb{E}_{Z}[f_{\theta,\eta}(Z)]|^{k}\right].

We impose the following assumptions.

Assumption 3

(Basic for nuisance case)
.

The following conditions hold:

(A1)

\Theta\times\mathcal{H}
is separable and pointwise measurable.

(A2)

For any
(\theta,\eta),(\theta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in\Theta\times\mathcal{H}
,
\|f_{\theta,\eta}-f_{\theta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(P_{Z})}\leq c_{\ell}^{q}\|(\theta,\eta)-(\theta^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\|_{\Theta\times\mathcal{H}}^{q}
holds with constants
c_{\ell}>0
and
q\in(0,1]

(A3)

There exist constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
such that

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\Theta\times\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\Theta\times\mathcal{H}})\leq C\varepsilon^{-\alpha},

holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

(A4)

There exists an envelope function
F_{S}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F_{S}(z)\geq\sup_{(\theta\times\eta)\in\Theta\times\mathcal{H}}|f_{\theta,\eta}(z)|
for any
z\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\|F_{S}\|_{L^{2}(P_{Z})}\vee\|F_{S}\|_{L^{\infty}(P_{Z})}\leq b<\infty
with a constant
b>0
, and
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{S,3}
is finite.

Assumption 4

(Coherent covariance function for nuisance case)
.

For any
\delta>0
, there exists a
\delta
-packing set
T
of
\Theta\times\mathcal{H}
in
\|\cdot\|_{\Theta\times\mathcal{H}}
and a constant
c>0
such that a covariance matrix
\Sigma_{A}=\Var_{Z}((f_{\theta,\eta}(Z))_{(\theta,\eta)\in T})
is coherently positive definite with
\underline{\sigma}^{2}\geq c\delta^{2\kappa}
for some
\kappa\in(0,1)
.

Similar comments to Assumptions 1 and 2 in Section
2
apply. Our assumptions cover semiparametric settings, where either
\theta
or
\eta
is infinite-dimensional. The case where both are finite-dimensional is covered as a special case. A Gaussian approximation for the semiparametric M-estimator
\widehat{\theta}_{S}
is established as follows.

Theorem 3

(Semiparametric M-estimator)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
3
and
4
are satisfied. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{Z}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{S}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{\Gamma}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{\Gamma}\in A\right)\right|=O\left(n^{-5/8}\vee n^{-(1-\alpha/2-\kappa)/\alpha}\vee n^{-(5-5\kappa-\alpha)/(8-8\kappa)}\right).

This result holds for a broad class of semiparametric estimators. For the semiparametric estimator, the Gaussian multiplier bootstrap also provides an analogous approximation result as in Theorem
2
. Since the configuration of the proof is nearly identical, we omit it here.

5.
M-estimator with Non-Donsker Class

Additional interest is to conduct a distributional approximation to the M-estimator when
\Theta
is not a Donsker class, e.g., a square root of the metric entropy of
\Theta
is not integrable.
In other words, the condition (A3) in Assumption
1
can be violated. One typical example of the non-Donsker class is the set of bounded Lipschitz functions on a
d
-dimensional space,
BL([0,1]^{d}):=\{f:[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\mid\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})|\leq C\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{2},\forall x,x^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{d},x\neq x^{\prime}\}
with a finite constant
C>0
.
When the input dimension
d
is no less than
2
, its covering number is
\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,BL([0,1]^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}})=O(\varepsilon^{-d})
(see Section 8 in
[
13
]
), so the integral of the covering number diverges and hence Theorem
1
is not applicable.

To handle the non-Donsker parameter space, we consider a sieve approximation. For
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider a sieve space
\Theta_{K}\subset\Theta_{K+1}\subset\cdots\subset\Theta
.
A typical example of the sieve space is a (closure of) linear hull of orthogonal bases, i.e.,
\Theta_{K}:=\{\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{K}w_{j}\phi_{j}|w_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j\in[K]\}
with the basis function
\phi_{j}
such as the trigonometric basis.
For the bounded Lipschitz case, we can find
\Theta_{K}
by splines such that

\sup_{f\in BL([0,1]^{d})}\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{K}}\|f-\theta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}=O(K^{-1/d})
(for an overview, see Section 13 in
[
30
]
).

We define the argmax of the Gaussian process over the sieve space as

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}_{K}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta_{K}}G(\theta).

For this case, we impose the following assumption on the sieve space.

Assumption 5

(Metric entropy of sieve space)
.

\Theta_{K}
is a Banach space and there exists
C>0
which satisfies

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon,\Theta_{K},\|\cdot\|)\leq C\log(1/\varepsilon^{K}),

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
.
Further, there exist constants
c_{\lambda},\lambda>0
which satisfy

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}(K):=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{K}}\|f_{\theta}-f_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(P_{n})}\leq c_{\lambda}K^{-\lambda},

for any
K\in\mathbb{N}
, where
P_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{Z_{i}}
is the empirical measure.

The first condition naturally holds with the typical sieve space
\Theta_{K}=\{\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{K}w_{j}\phi_{j}\}
since each
\theta\in\Theta_{K}
is characterized by
K
-dimensional parameters. The second condition is on an the approximation property of the sieve space. For example, if
\Theta
is the Sobolev space on
[0,1]^{d}
with an order
\alpha
and
\phi_{j}
is a trigonometric basis, then the second condition holds true with
\lambda=\alpha/d
.

We additionally introduce a smoothness assumption on the distribution of argmax values. For a set
A\subset\Theta
, we define
A^{\delta}:=\{\theta\in\Theta\mid\inf_{\theta^{\prime}\in A}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|\leq\delta\}
with
\delta>0
.

Assumption 6

(Smooth maximum)
.

There exist
\bar{\delta},C>0
which satisfy

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\max_{\theta\in A}G(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in A^{c}}G(\theta)+\delta\geq 0\right)-\mathbb{P}_{G}\left(\max_{\theta\in A^{\delta}}G(\theta)-\max_{\theta\in(A^{\delta})^{c}}G(\theta)\geq 0\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta,

for any
\delta\in(0,\bar{\delta})
and Borel subset
A\subset\Theta
.

This assumption requires that when the set
A
for calculating the maximum value varies slightly by
\delta
, the value only changes by about
O(\delta)
. This assumption is satisfied if, for example, a mean function of
G(\theta)
is sufficiently smooth.

Under these assumptions, we obtain the following coupling inequality:

Theorem 4
.

Suppose the conditions (A1) (A2) (A4) in Assumption
1
, and Assumptions
2
,
5
, and
6
hold. Further, assume that
\Theta
is a pre-Gaussian class, that is, there exists a Gaussian process
G
on
\Theta
, and set
K=n^{1/3}
. Then, with probability at least
1-C^{\prime}((n^{-1/8}\vee n^{-\lambda/3})\log n)
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\widehat{\theta}-\widehat{\theta}_{K}|\leq C^{\prime}(n^{-1/8}\vee n^{-\lambda/3})\log n,

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
.

This coupling result is slightly weaker than the one used for showing consistency of the distribution function in the sense of the Kolmogorov distance like Theorem
1
. However, it can be strengthened if the supremum of the empirical process can be bounded as in Lemma 2.3 in
[
12
]
.

6.
Examples

We provide several examples to illustrate usefulness of our non-asymptotic approximation theorems. In this section, we give only an overview of the results, and technical details are presented in Appendix.

6.1.
Cube Root Estimator

As a non-trivial example, we consider a relative frequency estimator, which is a prototype of the mode estimator and converges at the cube root rate (see
[
19
]
). The criterion function is

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(z)=\textbf{1}\{\theta-1\leq z\leq\theta+1\},

(3)

with the parameter space
\Theta=[0,1]
. For simplicity, assume that
Z_{i}
follows the uniform distribution on
[0,1]
, and consider the M-estimator for this criterion.

In this setup, it is easy to verify Assumption
1
. the conditions (A1), (A3), and (A4) are trivially satisfied. The condition (A2) also holds with
c_{\ell}=1
and
q=1
. On the other hand, it is not trivial to verify Assumption
2
. To this aim, we consider equally-spaced parameter grids
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j}\subset\Theta
, where
\theta_{j}=-1+\delta(j-1/2)
for
j=1,2,...,\lfloor 2/\delta\rfloor
. Note that
\|\theta_{j}-\theta_{j^{\prime}}\|\geq\delta>0
for
j\neq j^{\prime}
. We have
\mathbb{E}[f_{\theta_{j}}(Z)]=1/2
and
\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta_{j}}(Z),f_{\theta_{j^{\prime}}}(Z))=7/4-\delta|j-j^{\prime}|
. Let
p=\lfloor 2/\delta\rfloor-1
and consider the covariance matrix
\Sigma=\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta_{1}}(Z),...,f_{\theta_{p+1}}(Z))
, which is written as

\displaystyle\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}c&c-\delta&\ldots&c-(p-1)\delta&c-p\delta\\
c-\delta&c&\ldots&c-(p-2)\delta&c-(p-1)\delta\\
c-2\delta&c-\delta&\ddots&&c-(p-2)\delta\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
c-p\delta&c-(p-1)\delta&\ldots&c-\delta&c\end{pmatrix},

(4)

and
c=7/4
. To study the coherency of
\Sigma
, the following result is useful.

Proposition 3
.

For each
\delta>0
,
\Sigma
defined in (
4
) is coherently positive definite with
\underline{c}\delta
for some
\underline{c}>0
.

This result shows that the criterion (
3
) satisfies Assumption
2
with
\kappa=1/2
, and we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5
.

In the setting of this subsection, the criterion function (
3
) satisfies Assumptions
1
and
2
with
\kappa=1/2
.

All details including the proof of Proposition
3
are contained in Section
G.1
.

6.2.
Least Absolute Deviation Regression

As an example of
\sqrt{n}
-consistent estimators, we consider the least absolute deviation regression (see
[
26
]
). Let
z=(y,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, and consider the criterion function

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(y,x)=|y-x\theta|.

(5)

It is easy to verify Assumption
1
. Suppose
\Theta
and the support of
x
are compact. Then the conditions (A1), (A3), and (A4) are satisfied. Also a simple calculation shows the condition (A2) holds with
q=c_{\ell}=1
.
Our condition (A2) holds even when
P_{Z}
contains a discrete measure, e.g.
P_{Z}
is a mixture of Gaussian and Dirac measure, while the existing general frameworks
[
19
,
33
,
21
]
is not applicable because
\mathbb{E}_{Z}[f_{\theta}(Z)]
is not differentiable in
\theta
.

For Assumption
2
, we analyze its covariance matrix by applying Proposition
3
. To specify
\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta}(Z),f_{\theta^{\prime}}(Z))
, a detailed calculation shows that there exist positive functions
c_{0}(\theta),c_{1}(\theta)
, and
c_{2}(\theta)
satisfying

\displaystyle\Cov_{Z}(f_{\theta}(Z),f_{\theta^{\prime}}(Z))

\displaystyle=c_{0}(\theta)-c_{1}(\theta)|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|+c_{2}(\theta)|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|^{2}+c^{\prime}|\theta^{\prime}-\theta|^{3},

with some constant
c^{\prime}>0
. Based on this formulation, we can decompose the covariance matrix
\Sigma
to satisfy coherent positive definiteness, and then obtain the following corollary. Details are provided in Section
G.2
in Appendix.

Corollary 6
.

The criterion function (
5
) satisfies Assumptions
1
and
2
with
\kappa=1/2
.

6.3.
Minimum Volume Prediction

This subsection considers a minimum volume prediction region estimation developed by
[
27
]
. Let
\mathcal{Z}=[0,1]^{2}
be a sample space as a product of input and output spaces. Suppose we observe a random sample
Z_{i}=(X_{i},Y_{i}),i=1,...,n
. A purpose of the method is to construct a prediction interval of the output
Y
given
X=x
with minimizing the volume of the interval. With a level
a\in[0,1]
, the estimated interval is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{I}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{S\subset[0,1]}\lambda(S),\mbox{~s.t.~}\widehat{P}(S)\geq a,

where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure and
\widehat{P}(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{1}(Y_{i}\in S)K((X_{i}-x)/h_{n})/K((X_{i}-x)/h_{n})
. Here,
K:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a kernel function and
h_{n}>0
is a bandwidth depending on
n
. We can rewrite the interval optimization problem by an optimization in terms of its center
\theta
and width
\iota
so that
{I}=[{\theta}-{\iota},{\theta}+{\iota}]
.
We set
\Theta=[0,1]
and consider the following problem

\displaystyle\min_{\theta\in\Theta}\hat{P}([\theta-{\iota},\theta+{\iota}]),\mbox{~s.t.~}{\iota}=\inf\left\{\iota^{\prime}>0:\sup_{\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta}\widehat{P}([\theta^{\prime}-\iota^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}+\iota^{\prime}])\geq a\right\}.

To discuss the property of the estimator, we assume that there exists the unique shortest interval
[\theta^{*}-\iota^{*},\theta^{*}-\iota^{*}]
the conditional density function
p(y|x)
is bounded, positive, and symmetric, and satisfies
\partial p(\theta^{*}-\iota^{*}|x)>\partial p(\theta^{*}+\iota^{*}|x)
. Furthermore,
K(x^{\prime})=o(1/x^{\prime})
holds as
x^{\prime}\to\infty

We discuss approximation for the distribution the estimator of
\theta
with given
{\iota}
. By
[
19
]
, the estimator
\iota
is known to converge to
\iota^{*}
by the rate
O_{P}(1/(nh_{n})^{1/2}+h_{n}^{2})
. To discuss the approximation, we can rewrite the estimation problem for
\theta
as
\widehat{\theta}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{\theta\in\Theta}n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{\theta}(Z_{i})
with the following criterion function with the fixed input
x
:

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(Z)=K\left(\frac{X-x}{h_{n}}\right)\textbf{1}\{Y\in[\theta-{\iota},\theta+{\iota}]\}\}.

(6)

We verify this criterion satisfies the assumptions of our theory. For Assumption
1
, the conditions (A1) and (A3) are satisfied trivially.
The condition (A2) and (A4) are also verified, owing to finite of moments of
K((X_{i}-x)/h_{n})
by the condition of
K
. For Assumption
2
, we derive the explicit covariance matrix and find that it has a similar form as (
4
). Hence, applying Proposition
3
provides the following result. Details are presented in Section
G.3
in Appendix.

Corollary 7
.

The criterion function (
6
) satisfies Assumptions
1
and
2
with
\kappa=1/2
.

7.
Conclusion

This study develops a general non-asymptotic approximation theory which is applicable to a wide range of M-estimators. In some cases, derivations of limiting distributions for complicated M-estimators are highly non-trivial; to solve this issue, this study proposed a method that can be used universally.
This result is based on the non-asymptotic approximation scheme proposed in a previous studies
[
9
,
12
]
.
We also develop several techniques to extend the scheme for distributional approximation of argmax values of empirical criteria.

Proofs for Main Result (Section
2
)

A.1.
Proof of Theorem
1

For a measurable subset
A\subset\Theta
, let us introduce additional notations as
Q_{A}^{\vee}:=\sup_{\theta\in A}Q_{n}(\theta)
, and
G_{A}^{\vee}:=\sup_{\theta\in A}G(\theta)
.

To prove Theorem
1
, we consider a discretized version of
Q_{n}(\theta)
.
Firstly, we define a finite subset of
\Theta
.
For each
\varepsilon>0
, we consider a
2\varepsilon
-packing set as satisfying Assumption
2
. We note that
\tilde{\Theta}
is also a
\varepsilon
-covering set for
\Theta
.
Here, cardinality of
\widetilde{\Theta}
is described as

\displaystyle M(\varepsilon):=|\widetilde{\Theta}|\leq{\mathcal{N}}(\varepsilon,\Theta,\|\cdot\|_{\Theta}),

where the inequality follows that a cardinality of a
2\varepsilon
-packing set is bounded by that of a
\varepsilon
-covering set.

We also consider discretization of a subset of
A
.
Consider a measurable non-empty set
A\subsetneq\Theta
and define
A^{c}:=\Theta\backslash A
.
If
A\cap\tilde{\Theta}\neq\emptyset
, we set
A_{M}:=A\cap\widetilde{\Theta}
and
A^{c}_{M}:=\widetilde{\Theta}\cap A^{c}
.
Here, without loss of generality, we assume that
A\cap\tilde{\Theta}
is non-empty.
If
A\cap\tilde{\Theta}=\emptyset
, we set
A_{M}=\{\underline{\theta}\}
where
\underline{\theta}
is the closest element of
\tilde{\Theta}
to
A
, and define
A_{M}^{c}:=\tilde{\Theta}\backslash\{\underline{\theta}\}
.
For any
A
, we can find

\displaystyle|A_{M}|\leq M(\varepsilon).

Further, we consider discretization of a subset of
\Theta
.
Let us define

\Delta>0
, we define a function
f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}(x):=g_{\delta}\left(h_{\beta,A}(x)-h_{\beta,A^{c}}(x)+\Delta\right).

(20)

We will show that
f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}(x)
approximates the event (
17
) with suitable selected parameters
\beta,\delta
and
\Delta
in the next step.

Step 2: Approximate probability
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A)
.

We rewrite the probability
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A)
for an arbitrary fixed non-empty
A\subsetneq[M]
by utilizing
f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}
.
In this step, we provide an upper bound for
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A)
associated with
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{m}_{W}\in A)
.
An opposite lower bound is yielded by the similar way, hence we omit it.

Let us introduce notations
\bar{X}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in[n]}X_{i}
and
\bar{W}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in[n]}W_{i}
as
M
-dimensional random vectors.
Now, we provide an upper bound for
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A)
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{X}\left(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{X}\left(\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{m\in[M]}\bar{X}_{m}\in A\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{X}\left(\max_{m\in A}\bar{X}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{X}_{m}\geq 0\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{X}\left(h_{\beta,A}(\bar{X})-h_{\beta,A^{c}}(\bar{X})+\Xi(M,\beta)\geq 0\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}[\textbf{1}\{h_{\beta,A}(\bar{X})-h_{\beta,A^{c}}(\bar{X})+\Xi(M,\beta)\geq 0\}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}[f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\bar{X})],

(21)

where the second equality follows (
17
), and the first inequality follows the bound (
18
) for the approximation error of
h_{\beta,A}
.
The last inequality follows (
19
) and the definition of
f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}
with
\Delta=\Xi(M,\beta)
.

Now, we approximate the bound
\mathbb{E}_{X}[f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\bar{X})]
with a transformation of the Gaussian
\bar{W}
, namely,
\mathbb{E}_{Y}[f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\bar{W})]
.
We apply an approximation technique associated with a Gaussian approximation and the Stein’s identity.
For a preparation, we consider a modified version of
f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}
and
\bar{X}
.
Let
\tilde{X}
be a centered version of
\bar{X}
as
\widetilde{X}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in[n]}\widetilde{X}_{i}
where
\widetilde{X}_{i}=(\widetilde{X}_{i,1},...,\widetilde{X}_{i,M})=(X_{i,m}-\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m}])_{m\in[M]}
.
Similarly, we define
\widetilde{W}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in[n]}\widetilde{W}_{i}
where
\widetilde{W}_{i}=(\widetilde{W}_{i,1},...,\widetilde{W}_{i,M})=(W_{i,m}-\mathbb{E}_{W}[W_{i,m}])_{m\in[M]}
.
Also, consider a mapping
\tau:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
such as
\tau(x)=(x+\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m}])_{m\in[M]}
.
Let us define a modified function for the centered vectors as

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}(x):=f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Delta}\circ\tau(x).

Then, we utilize the centered random vectors and rewrite the following difference as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}[f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\bar{X})]-\mathbb{E}_{W}[f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\bar{W})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{X}}[\widetilde{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\widetilde{X})]-\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{W}}[\widetilde{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\widetilde{W})].

Now, we are ready to apply Lemma
3
.
Since
\tilde{X}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{X}}[\widetilde{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\widetilde{X})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{W}}[\widetilde{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\widetilde{W})]+\frac{C_{S}(\mathcal{M}_{3}+4\overline{\sigma}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{1})}{4n^{1/2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}\left|\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\widetilde{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{W}}[\widetilde{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\widetilde{W})]+\underbrace{\frac{C_{U}(\mathcal{M}_{3}+4\overline{\sigma}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{1})}{4n^{1/2}}\left(\delta^{-3}+\delta^{-2}\beta+\delta^{-1}\beta^{2}\right)}_{=:\Delta_{f}},

where
C_{S},C_{U}>0
are universal constants.
The last inequality follows a bound for the derivatives for
{f}_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}
which are derived in Lemma
6
.

Then, by the property of
g_{\delta}
as (
19
), we continue the upper bound for
\mathbb{P}_{X}\left(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A\right)
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}[f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\bar{X})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{W}[f_{\beta,\delta,A,\Xi(M,\beta)}(\bar{W})]+\Delta_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{W}[\textbf{1}\{h_{\beta,A}(\bar{W})-h_{\beta,A^{c}}(\bar{W})+\delta+\Xi(M,\beta)\geq 0\}]+\Delta_{f}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{W}(h_{\beta,A}(\bar{W})-h_{\beta,A^{c}}(\bar{W})+\delta+\Xi(M,\beta)\geq 0)+\Delta_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(\max_{m\in A}\bar{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{W}_{m}+\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta)\geq 0\right)+\Delta_{f},

(22)

where the last inequality follows the similar argument.

To bound the probability term in the last line (
22
), we apply the anti-concentration inequality for this setting.
We apply Lemma
5
with setting
r=-(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta))/2
and
\varepsilon=(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta))/2
, then it provides

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(\max_{m\in A}\bar{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{W}_{m}+\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta)\geq 0\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(\max_{m\in A}\bar{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{W}_{m}\geq 0\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(0\geq\max_{m\in A}\bar{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{W}_{m}\geq-(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(\max_{m\in A}\bar{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{W}_{m}\geq 0\right)+\underbrace{\frac{1}{\underline{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2\log M}+2)}_{=:\Delta_{h}}(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta)).

Now, we can continue the inequality (
22
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(\max_{m\in A}\bar{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{W}_{m}+\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta)\geq 0\right)+\Delta_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(\min_{m\in A}\bar{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{W}_{m}\geq 0\right)+\Delta_{h}(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta))+\Delta_{f}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(\widehat{m}_{W}\in A\right)+\Delta_{h}(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta))+\Delta_{f}.

We can obtain an opposite version of the inequality to bound
\mathbb{P}(m_{W}^{\vee}\in A)
, then we obtain that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}_{W}(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A)-\mathbb{P}_{W}(\widehat{m}_{Y}\in A)|\leq\Delta_{h}(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta))+\Delta_{f}.

Step 3: Combine the results.

Combining the result of the steps with the bounds for
\Delta_{f}
and
\Delta_{h}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}_{X}(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A)-\mathbb{P}_{W}(\widehat{m}_{W}\in A)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(\sqrt{2\log M}+2)}{\underline{\sigma}}(\delta+2\Xi(M,\beta))+\frac{\mathcal{M}_{3}+4\overline{\sigma}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{1}}{n^{1/2}}\left(\delta^{-3}+\delta^{-2}\beta+\delta^{-1}\beta^{2}\right),

where
C_{\mathcal{M}}>0
be a constant depends on up to a third moment of
\bar{X}
and
\overline{\sigma}
.
We set

\displaystyle\beta=n^{1/8}\log^{1/2}M,\mbox{~and~}\delta=n^{-1/8},

then we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}_{X}(\widehat{m}_{X}\in A)-\mathbb{P}_{W}(\widehat{m}_{W}\in A)|\leq\frac{\log M}{n^{1/8}}\left(\frac{6\sqrt{2}+12}{\underline{\sigma}}+\frac{\mathcal{M}_{3}+4\overline{\sigma}^{2}\mathcal{M}_{1}}{2}\right).

By adjusting coefficients, we obtain the statement.

∎

Appendix B
Technical Results: Stein approximation and anti-concentration

We provide a Gaussian approximation technique for an expectation of smooth functions via the Stein’s identity.
The technique has been developed by several studies
[
5
,
39
]
.
The following lemma is a straightforward application of the result by
[
15
]
.

Lemma 3

(Approximation by Stein Identity)
.

Let
X_{1},...,X_{n}
be independent
M
-dimensional random vectors such as
\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m}^{2}]=\widetilde{\sigma}_{m}^{2}
for all
m\in[M]
.
Define
\mu_{k}:=\max_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{E}_{X}[\max_{m\in[M]}|X_{i,m}|^{k}]
for
k=1,3
, and
\widetilde{X}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
.
Also, for each
i=1,...,n
, let
W_{i}
be a
M
-dimensional Gaussian random vector such as
\mathbb{E}_{W}[W_{i}]=\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i}]
and
\Var_{W}(W_{i})=\Var_{X}(X_{i})
, and we define
\widetilde{W}:=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
.
Then, for any bounded
f\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{M})
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{X}[f(\widetilde{X})]-\mathbb{E}_{W}[f(\widetilde{W})]|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{S}(\mu_{3}+4\mu_{1}\max_{m\in[M]}\widetilde{\sigma}_{m}^{2})}{4n^{1/2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}\left|\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}{f}(x)\right|.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

We apply the multivariate version of the Stein’s equation
[
35
]
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}[f(\widetilde{X})]-\mathbb{E}_{W}[f(\widetilde{W})]=\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}\mathbb{E}_{X}\left[\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})-\widetilde{X}_{m^{\prime}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})\right].

(23)

Here,
\check{f}:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be a solution of the identity which is induced from
f
as

\displaystyle\check{f}(\widetilde{w}):=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{W}}\left[{f}(e^{-s}\widetilde{w}+\sqrt{1-e^{-2s}}\widetilde{W})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{W}[{f}(\widetilde{W})]ds,

for
\widetilde{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
.

We evaluate the term
\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})-\widetilde{X}_{m^{\prime}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})]
for each
m^{\prime}\in[M]
.
Let us define
\widetilde{X}_{-i}:=\widetilde{X}-n^{-1/2}X_{i}
.
Then, we consider the first-order Taylor expansion of
\mathbb{E}_{X}[\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})]
around
\widetilde{X}_{-i}
for all
i\in[n]
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{X}[\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})]

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{E}_{X}[\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}_{\beta,\delta,A}(\widetilde{X}_{-i})]+\sum_{i\in[n]}\underbrace{\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m^{\prime}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\check{f}(\bar{X}_{i,m^{\prime}})]}{n^{3/2}}}_{=:R_{1,m^{\prime},i}}.

Note that
\bar{X}_{i,m^{\prime}}
is an existing random variable as an inter point between
\tilde{X}_{-i}
and
\tilde{X}
.
By an iterative use of the Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}R_{1,m^{\prime},i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{X}[\max_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}|X_{i,m^{\prime}}|\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}|\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\check{f}(\bar{X}_{i,m^{\prime}})|]}{n^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\mu_{1}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}|\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\check{f}(x)|}{n^{3/2}},

for any
i\in[n]
.

Similarly, the first-order Taylor expansion for
\partial_{m^{\prime}}\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{X})
around
\tilde{X}_{-i}
provides

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widetilde{X}_{m^{\prime}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{E}_{X}[{X}_{i,m^{\prime}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}\check{f}(\widetilde{X})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{1/2}}\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{E}_{X}[{X}_{i,m^{\prime}}\partial_{m^{\prime}}\check{f}(\widetilde{X}_{-i})]+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{E}_{X}[{X}_{i,m^{\prime}}^{2}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}(\widetilde{X}_{-i})]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\sum_{i\in[n]}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m^{\prime}}^{3}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\check{f}(\bar{X}_{i,m^{\prime}})]}{2!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{j=0,1}\frac{1}{j!n^{j/2+1/2}}\mathbb{E}_{X}[{X}_{i,m^{\prime}}^{j+1}]\mathbb{E}_{X}[\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{j+1}\check{f}(\widetilde{X}_{-i})]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i\in[n]}\underbrace{\frac{\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m^{\prime}}^{3}\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\check{f}(\bar{X}_{i,m^{\prime}})]}{4n^{3/2}}}_{=:R_{2,m^{\prime},i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[n]}\mathbb{E}_{X}[{X}_{i,m^{\prime}}^{2}]\mathbb{E}_{X}[\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}(\widetilde{X}_{-i})]+\sum_{i\in[n]}R_{2,m^{\prime},i}.

Here, the second equality holds since
{X}_{i}
and
\widetilde{X}_{-i}
are independent by its definition, and the last equality follows
\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m}]=0
.
Similar to the bound for
|\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}R_{2,m^{\prime},i}|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}R_{2,m^{\prime},i}\right|\leq\frac{\mu_{3}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}|\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\check{f}(x)|}{4n^{3/2}},

for each
i\in[n]
.

Substituting the Taylor expansions into (
23
).
Let
\mathcal{M}_{k}:=\max_{m\in[M]}\mathbb{E}_{X}[|X_{1,m}-\mathbb{E}[X_{1,m}]|^{k}]
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{X}[f(\widetilde{X})]-\mathbb{E}_{W}[f(\widetilde{W})]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}\sum_{i\in[n]}\frac{1}{n}|\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{X}[{X}_{i,m^{\prime}}^{2}]||\mathbb{E}_{X}[\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\check{f}(\widetilde{X}_{-i})]|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i\in[n]}\left(\left|\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}R_{1,m^{\prime},i}\right|+\left|\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}R_{2,m^{\prime},i})\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{3}+4\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\mu_{1}}{4n^{1/2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}\left|\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}\check{f}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{S}(\mu_{3}+4\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}\mu_{1})}{4n^{1/2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}\sum_{m^{\prime}\in[M]}\left|\partial_{m^{\prime}}^{3}{f}(x)\right|,

where
c_{S}>0
is a universal constant.
Here, the second inequality follows
\mathbb{E}_{X}[X_{i,m^{\prime}}^{2}]=\widetilde{\sigma}_{m^{\prime}}^{2}
, and the last inequality follows Proposition 2.1 in
[
15
]
.
∎

We further provide an anti-concentration inequality for a difference of two Gaussian maxima.
Our result depends on the following inequality for a single Gaussian maxima by
[
10
]
.

Lemma 4

(Anti-Concentration of Gaussian Maxima: Lemma 4.3 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let
(X_{1},...,X_{M})^{\top}
be a (possibly non-centered)
M
-dimensional Gaussian random vector such as
\sigma_{m}^{2}:=\Var_{X}(X_{m})
for
m\in[M]
.
Also, we consider
\underline{\sigma}:=\min_{m\in[M]}\sigma_{m}
and
\overline{\sigma}:=\max_{m\in[M]}\sigma_{m}
, then suppose
0<\underline{\sigma}<\overline{\sigma}<\infty
holds.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{X}\left(r-\varepsilon\leq\max_{m\in[M]}X_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon\right)\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\underline{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2\log M}+2).

We apply the anti-concentration inequality to our setting.

Lemma 5

(Conditional Anti-Concentration)
.

Let
(W_{1},...,W_{M})^{\top}
be a (possibly non-centered)
M
-dimensional Gaussian random vector such as
\sigma_{m}^{2}:=\Var_{X}(W_{m})
for
m\in[M]
.
Suppose that a covariance matrix
\Sigma
of
(W_{1},...,W_{M})^{\top}
is coherently positive definite (Definition
1
).
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
and non-empty
A\subsetneq[M]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(r-\varepsilon\leq\max_{m\in A}{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}{W}_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon\right)\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\underline{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2\log M}+2),

where
A^{c}:=[M]\backslash A
.

Proof of Lemma
5
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and an non-empty
A\subsetneq[M]
arbitrary.
Let us define
\bar{W}_{A}:=(W_{m})_{m\in A}
and and
\bar{W}_{A^{c}}:=(W_{m})_{m\in A^{c}}
.
For some
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we decompose the probability as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(r-\varepsilon\leq\max_{m\in A}W_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}W_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|A^{c}|}}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{W}_{A}}\left(r-\varepsilon\leq\max_{m\in A}{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}\bar{w}_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon\mid\bar{W}_{A^{c}}=\bar{w}_{A^{c}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\phi_{\bar{W}_{A^{c}}}(\bar{w}_{A^{c}})d\bar{w}_{A^{c}},

(24)

where
\phi_{\bar{W}_{A^{c}}}
is a density function of
\bar{W}_{A^{c}}
, i.e., it is a density function of a multivariate Gaussian distribution with mean
\mu_{A^{c}}:=\mathbb{E}_{W}[\bar{W}_{A^{c}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{|A^{c}|}
and covariance matrix
\Sigma_{A^{c}}:=\Var_{W}(\bar{W}_{A^{c}})\in\mathbb{R}^{|A^{c}|\times|A^{c}|}
.
Here, we set a non-random vector
\bar{w}_{A^{c}}:=(w_{m})_{m\in A^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|A^{c}|}
.

We analyze a conditional distribution of
\bar{W}_{A}
with fixed
\bar{W}_{A^{c}}=\bar{w}_{A^{c}}
.
Let
\mu_{A}:=\mathbb{E}_{W}[\bar{W}_{A}]\in\mathbb{R}^{|A|}
. Since
(\bar{W}_{A},\bar{W}_{A^{c}})
is a joint Gaussian random variable, the conditional distribution is regarded as a Gaussian distribution such as

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{A}|\bar{W}_{A^{c}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{A}+\Sigma_{AA^{c}}\Sigma_{A^{c}}^{-1}(\bar{w}_{A^{c}}-\mu_{A^{c}}),\Sigma_{A}-\Sigma_{AA^{c}}\Sigma_{A^{c}}^{-1}\Sigma_{AA^{c}}).

Note that
\Sigma_{A^{c}}^{-1}
exists by the assumption of coherently positive definiteness.
Then, we rewrite the probability (
24
) as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|A^{c}|}}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{W}_{A}}\left(r-\varepsilon\leq\max_{m\in A}{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}{w}_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon\mid\bar{W}_{A^{c}}=\bar{w}_{A^{c}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\phi_{\bar{W}_{A^{c}}}(\bar{w}_{A^{c}})d\bar{w}_{A^{c}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|A^{c}|}}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{W}_{A}|\bar{W}_{A^{c}}}\left(r-\varepsilon+\max_{m\in A^{c}}{w}_{m}\leq\max_{m\in A}W_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon+\max_{m\in A^{c}}{w}_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\phi_{\bar{W}_{A^{c}}}(\bar{w}_{A^{c}})d\bar{w}_{A^{c}},

(25)

where the last inequality follows since
\bar{W}_{A^{c}}
and
\bar{W}_{A}|\bar{W}_{A^{c}}
are independent, because they are uncorrelated Gaussian random variables.
Then, we bound the probability in the integral.
By Lemma
4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bar{W}_{A}|\bar{W}_{A^{c}}}\left(r-\varepsilon+\max_{m\in A^{c}}{w}_{m}\leq\max_{m\in A}{W}_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon+\max_{m\in A^{c}}{w}_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\underline{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2\log(|A|)}+2)\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\underline{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2\log M}+2).

Substituting the inequality into (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{W}\left(r-\varepsilon\leq\max_{m\in A}{W}_{m}-\max_{m\in A^{c}}{W}_{m}\leq r+\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\underline{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2\log M}+2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|A^{c}|}}\phi_{Y_{A^{c}}}(\bar{w}_{A^{c}})d\bar{w}_{A^{c}}=\frac{2\varepsilon}{\underline{\sigma}}(\sqrt{2\log M}+2).

Then, we achieve the statement.
∎

Appendix C
Proof for Bootstrap Algorithm (Section
3
)

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We will consider the discretization of the process
B_{n}(f)
.
To the end, for a set
A\subset\Theta
, we define notations as
B_{A}^{\vee}=\sup_{\theta\in A}B(\theta)
.
Similarly, we define
P_{A}^{\vee}
and
E_{A}^{\vee}
, respectively.

We bound the effect of discretization of
G(\theta)
and
B_{n}(\theta)

From Lemma

, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq G_{A}^{\vee}-G_{A_{M}}^{\vee}\leq\mu(n,\varepsilon,\tau),\mbox{~and~}0\leq G_{A^{c}}^{\vee}-G_{A_{M}^{c}}^{\vee}\leq\mu(n,\varepsilon,\tau),

with probability at least
1-\exp(-\tau)

To discretize the bootstrap process
B_{n}(f)
, we define

\displaystyle\Delta B_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}:=\sup_{(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\in\Theta_{\varepsilon}}B_{n}(\theta)-B_{n}(\theta^{\prime}).

We also define
\Delta E_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}
and
\Delta P_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}

Then, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq B_{A}^{\vee}-B_{A_{M}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta B_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|\leq|\Delta P_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|+|\Delta E_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|,

(26)

by the definition of
B_{n}(\theta)
.

We will bound the terms
|\Delta P_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|
and
|\Delta E_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|
.
About the first term
|\Delta P_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|
, Lemma
1
yields

\displaystyle|\Delta P_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|=|\Delta Q_{\Theta_{\varepsilon}}^{\vee}|\leq\nu(n,\varepsilon,\tau),

with probability at least
1-\exp(-\tau)
.
About the second term

Lemma 6

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=2}^{p-1}(c-i\delta)^{2}(2^{p-1}c\delta^{p-2}-(p-1)2^{p-2}\delta^{p-1})

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}(c-i\delta)(c-(i+1)\delta)-2^{p-2}c\delta^{p-2}+(p-1)2^{p-3}\delta^{p-1}

\displaystyle\quad+2(c-\delta)(c-p\delta)2^{p-3}\delta^{p-1}

\displaystyle=2^{p-2}\delta^{p-1}(2c^{2}-(3+p)c\delta+2p\delta^{2}).

By combining this result with (
40
), we achieve

\displaystyle c-(b^{(p)})^{\top}(M^{(p)})^{-1}b^{(p)}

\displaystyle=c-\frac{2^{p-2}\delta^{p-1}(2c^{2}-(3+p)c\delta+2p\delta^{2})}{|M^{(p)}|}

\displaystyle=c-\frac{2^{p-2}\delta^{p-1}(2c^{2}-(3+p)c\delta+2p\delta^{2})}{2^{p-1}c\delta^{p-1}-(p-1)2^{p-2}\delta^{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\delta(2c-p\delta)}{2c-(p-1)\delta}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\delta.

Then, we achieve the statement.
∎

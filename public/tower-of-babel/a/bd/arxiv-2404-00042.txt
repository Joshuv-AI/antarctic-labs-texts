Stochastic Optimization with Constraints: A Non-asymptotic Instance-Dependent Analysis


Abstract
We consider the problem of stochastic convex optimization under convex constraints. We analyze the behavior of a natural variance reduced proximal gradient (VRPG) algorithm for this problem. Our main result is a non-asymptotic guarantee for VRPG algorithm. Contrary to minimax worst case guarantees, our result is instance-dependent in nature. This means that our guarantee captures the complexity of the loss function, the variability of the noise, and the geometry of the constraint set. We show that the non-asymptotic performance of the VRPG algorithm is governed by the scaled distance (scaled by N \sqrt{N} ) between the solutions of the given problem and that of a certain small perturbation of the given problem — both solved under the given convex constraints; here, N N denotes the number of samples. Leveraging a well-established connection between local minimax lower bounds and solutions to perturbed problems, we show that as N → ∞ N\rightarrow\infty , the VRPG algorithm achieves the renowned local minimax lower bound by Hàjek and Le Cam up to universal constants and a logarithmic factor of the sample size.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the problem of stochastic convex optimization problem under constraints. Concretely, we are interested in problems of the form

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\;f(x)

\displaystyle\equiv{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim\mathbb{P}_{0}}f(x,z)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{where}\;\;\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in\real^{d}\;:\;g_{i}(x)\leq 0\;\;\text{for}\;\;i=1,\ldots,m\right\}.

(1)

Here,
g_{i}
are convex and twice continuously differentiable functions, and the function
f
is twice continuously differentiable,
\mu
-strongly convex and
\mathrm{L}
-smooth for some
0<\mu\leq\mathrm{L}
. Given
N
iid samples
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{N}\sim\mathbb{P}_{0}
, we are interested in designing computationally efficient algorithm that outputs an approximate solution of the problem (
1
).

Constraint stochastic optimization problems of the form (
1
) are ubiquitous in statistics and optimization, and the study of these problems dates backs to more than 70 years.
There are two standard strategies for solving the problem (
1
): (a) M-estimators under constraints (b) Stochastic approximation methods.

1.1
M-estimators under constraints:

The first approach, popular in the statistics community, provides an approximate solution of problem (
1
) by solving a constrained M-estimation problem. Given
N
iid samples
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{N}\sim\mathbb{P}_{0}
, one outputs the solution of the following M-estimation problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x,z_{i}).

(2)

The asymptotic behavior of the above problem is well understood by now. The study of this problem has its roots in the works of Dupacoka and Wets
[
6
]
, Shapiro
[
19
]
, and King and Rockafellar
[
14
]
. In a recent work Duchi and Ruan
[
5
]
and Davis et al.
[
2
]
show that the solution of the solution obtained from (
2
) is asymptotically locally minimax optimal in the sense of Hájek and Le Cam
[
15
,
7
,
16
,
20
,
23
]
. In simple words,
in a local shrinking neighborhood (shrinking as the number of the sample size increases) of the problem,
there is no other algorithm that performs better than the solution of the problem (
2
). Contrary to global minimax bounds which capture the difficulty of the hardest problem in the problem class, the local minimax lower bounds from
[
5
]
captures a more finer notion of difficulty of the specific problem at hand.

1.2
Stochastic approximation methods:

While the asymptotic performance of constrained M-estimator problems are optimal asymptotically, one drawback of such methods is that they may be expensive to compute. This motivated people to design computationally attractive stochastic approximation schemes that converge to the solution of problem (
1
).

Suboptimality of dual-averaging:

The problem of stochastic optimization under constraints has been has been studied in the optimization community for some time; see the work of and King
[
13
]
.
The behavior stochastic algorithms are more nuanced due to presence of constraint set
\mathcal{X}
. For instance, Duchi and Ruan
[
5
]
show that stochastic approximation with
dual averaging
[
17
]
— a natural algorithm in this case — is not always optimal. Concretely, in Section 5.2 of their paper Duchi and Ruan argue that the dual averaging algorithm is optimal when the the constraint set
\mathcal{X}
consists of linear constraints, but the same algorithm is sub-optimal when
\mathcal{X}
contains non-linear constraints.

Asymptotic optimality via Polyak-Ruppert averaging:

In a surprising new result, Davis, Drusvyatskiy, and Jiang
[
2
]
show that vanilla stochastic approximation algorithm when combined with a Polyak-Ruppert (PR) averaging step is asymptotically local-minimax optimal. Staring with an
x_{1}\in\mathcal{X}
, the updates of the PR-averaged stochastic approximation as obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\texttt{(SA-step)}\qquad x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\text{Proj}_{\mathcal{X}}\left(x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\cdot\nabla f(x_{k},z_{k})\right).

\displaystyle\texttt{(PR-averaging)}\qquad\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k+1}x_{\ell}

Davis et al.
[
2
]
argue that under some mild assumptions of the loss function
f
and the constraint set
\mathcal{X}
, the scaled difference
\sqrt{N}(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{N}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})
converges to a normal distribution with the optimal covariance matrix. Here,
x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}
is the (unique) solution to the problem (
1
), and
N
is the number of samples used (or the number of steps in the algorithm). The authors also argue that
\sqrt{N}{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{N}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|_{2}
matches a local minimax lower bound suggested by the Hájek Le Cam theory
[
7
,
15
,
5
]
.

1.3
Non-asymptotic bounds

While the asymptotic performances of both M-estimator and stochastic approximation algorithms are quite well understood, not much is known
about the non-asymptotic instance-dependent performance of either methods for the problem (
1
) when constraint set
\mathcal{X}\neq\real^{d}
. In a recent work
[
4
]
, the authors provide a non-asymptotic lower bounds for the problem (
1
) under some growth condition on an appropriate modulus of continuity. In particular, motivated by the work of
[
1
]
, the authors in the work
[
4
]
construct a non-asymptotic local minimax lower bound for problem (
1
) by comparing problem (
1
) with its hardest alternative in a local shrinking neighborhood (shrinking with sample size
N
) of the problem. The authors also argue that in one dimension, a grid search based algorithm attains this non-asymptotic local lower bound. Unfortunately, in higher dimensions, grid search algorithms become computationally prohibitive. Thus it is not clear whether such results can be extended to higher dimension.

1.4
Contribution and organization

Motivated from the recent success of instance dependent understanding of stochastic approximation algorithms, our goal in this paper is to have a non-asymptotic understanding of stochastic approximation algorithms in a instance-dependent sense. Put simply, we would like to derive non-asymptotic upper-bounds that reflect the structure of the loss function
f
, the variability of the noise, and the structure of the constraint set
\mathcal{X}
. In Section
2
, we motivate non-asymptotic instance dependence benchmark for the problem
1
. This benchmark is motivated from the well-studied connection between the local minimax lower bounds and solutions of perturbed problem
[
5
,
2
]
. Section
3
is devoted to a devoted to a detailed description of the problem that we consider.
In Section
4
we state the variance reduced proximal gradient (VRPG) algorithm that we study (see Algorithm
1
), and in Theorem
1
, we provide non-asymptotic instance-dependent upper bound for
the VRPG algorithm. Finally, we argue that VRPG is asymptotically locally minimax optimal up to a factor which is logarithmic in the number of sample size
N
and universal constants.

2
An instance-dependent local minimax lower bound

We start with a discussion on the statistical limit of the constrained stochastic optimization problem (
1
). Our lower bounds are motivated from the local minimax theory of Le Cam and Hájek
[
15
,
7
]
. Contrary to ,standard minimax guarantees — which capture the difficulty of the worst case problem in a problem class, local minimax guarantees captures a problem specific difficulty of the problems. In other words, these lower bounds are adaptive to the structure of the function
f
as well as the constrained set
\mathcal{X}
in problem (
1
).

The idea is to consider a small neighborhood of the given problem
\mathcal{P}_{0}
— shrinking with the sample size — and consider worst case difficulty in this local neighborhood. Intuitively, in a small local neighborhood of a given problem
\mathcal{P}_{0}
, problem difficulty does not change drastically. Consequently, the worst case difficulty in the local neighborhood of the problem
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is a finer notion of difficulty of problem
\mathcal{P}_{0}
compared to the worst case problem difficulty.

Focusing on our problem (
1
), let
\mathbb{P}
is any other distribution over the randomness
z
such that
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{P}_{0})
is small to ensure

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{E}}_{z\in\mathbb{P}}f(x,z)\quad\text{is strongly convex}.

(3)

Let
x_{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}
the unique solution to the perturbed problem:

\displaystyle x_{\mathbb{P}}^{\star}=\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\;\left\{f_{\mathbb{P}}(x)\equiv{\mathbb{E}}_{z\sim\mathbb{P}}f(x,z)\right\}.\qquad

(4)

Given access to
N
iid samples
z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
from the distribution
\mathbb{P}
, the following result from the paper
[
5
, Corollary 1]
characterizes the local minimax lower bound for the problem (
1
).

Proposition 1

(Informal)
.

Let
\widehat{x}_{N}
be the output of
any
algorithm with access to
N
iid samples
z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
. Then under suitable smoothness condition on the function
f
and the constraint functions
g_{i}^{\prime}s
in problem (
1
), we have the following asymptotic lower bound:

\displaystyle\liminf_{c\rightarrow\infty}\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\mathbb{P}:\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{P}||\mathbb{P}_{0})\leq c/N}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\left[\|\sqrt{N}(\widehat{x}_{N}-x_{\mathbb{P}}^{\star})\|_{2}^{2}\right]\geq{\mathbb{E}}\|Z\|_{2}^{2}

(5)

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}H^{\star}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}\Sigma^{\star}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}H^{\star}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}})
.

A few comments regarding the last lower bound are in order. The lower bound depends on the property of three matrices
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}},H^{\star}
and
\Sigma^{\star}
. Let us understand these three matrices one at a time. We use
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\cdot)
as the Lagrangian of the problem (
1
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,\beta)=f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{i}g_{i}(x).

(6)

(a)

The matrix
H^{\star}
in the bound (
5
) is the hessian of the Hessian of the Lagrangian (
6
) at optima
x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}

\displaystyle H^{\star}=\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star},\beta^{\star})=\nabla^{2}f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{i}^{\star}\nabla^{2}g_{i}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})

(7)

where
\beta_{i}^{\star}\geq 0
with
\beta_{i}^{\star}=0
when
\nabla g_{i}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})<0
are unique scalars satisfying

\displaystyle\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{i}^{\star}g_{(}x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})=0

See the paper
[
5
, Section 2]
, and the book
[
8
, Chapter VII.2]
for more a more detailed discussion on existence of such
\beta^{\star}
.
Put simply, the Hessian matrix
H^{\star}
captures the structures of the loss function
f
and the constraint-set
\mathcal{X}
near optima
x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}
.

(b)

The matrix

\Sigma^{\star}=\mathrm{Cov}_{z\in\mathbb{P}_{0}}(\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star},z))
is the covariance matrix of the function gradient evaluated at the optima
x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}
. This term captures the noise present in the problem (
1
).

(c)

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{X}}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})
denote the critical tangent cone at
x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{X}}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})

\displaystyle:=\left\{w\in\real^{d}\;:\;\nabla g_{i}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})^{\top}w=0\;\;\text{for all}\;\;i\;\;\text{with}\;\;g_{i}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\cap_{i=1}^{m_{0}}\left\{w\in\real^{d}\;:\;w\in\real^{d}\;:\;\nabla g_{i}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})^{\top}w=0\right\}

Here, we assumed, without loss of generality, that the first
m_{0}
constraints
\left\{g_{i}(x)\leq 0\right\}
are active at the optima
x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}
. The matrix
\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the projection matrix to this tangent cone
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{X}}(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})
. This term captures the structure of the constraint set
\mathcal{X}
near the optima
x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}
.

The lower bound from Proposition
1
provides an asymptotic lower bound for any procedure, and it is not immediately clear what could be a non-asymptotic benchmark. To motivate our non-asymptotic benchmark we discuss an alternative description of the lower bound in Proposition
1
. We do so by connecting the lower bound (
5
) with solutions of a class of perturbed problems obtained from the original problem (
5
).

2.1
Perturbed problems and connections to local minimax lower bounds

Before we discuss the connection between perturbations of the problem (
1
) and the local minimax lower bound (
5
), it is helpful to first understand which distributions
\mathbb{P}
with
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb{P}||\mathbb{P}_{0})

\displaystyle d\mathbb{P}_{u}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\left\langle u,\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star},z)-\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})\right\rangle}{C_{0}}\cdot d\mathbb{P}_{0}(z)\quad\text{where}

\displaystyle\quad C_{0}

\displaystyle=1+\int\left\langle u,\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star},z)-\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})\right\rangle\cdot d\mathbb{P}_{0}(z).

(8)

and taking supremum over the set

2.1.1
A particular perturbation of interest

A particular perturbation of interest is the following.
Given

\displaystyle f_{N}(x)=f(x)+\left\langle x,\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})-\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})\right\rangle\quad\text{where}\quad\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star},z_{i})

\displaystyle\text{(M-estimator:)}\qquad\qquad\widehat{x}^{M}:=\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{\widehat{f}_{N}(x):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(x;z_{i})\right\},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\nabla\widehat{f}_{N}(x)=\nabla f_{N}(x)+o_{p}(1)\cdot\left(x-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\right)

The above relation ensures that the the asymptotic behavior of the M-estimator
\widehat{x}^{M}
is same as that of

from (
9

[
5
]
and also the paper
[

]
for details. The perturbed problem

from (

[

22
,
10

11

Intuitively, as

\displaystyle x_{N}^{\star}=x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}+\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}H^{\star}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}v+o(\|v\|)\quad\text{where}\quad v=\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})-\nabla f(x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}).

Also, see the book by

Under the assumptions of Proposition

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{N}(x_{N}^{\star}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}H^{\star}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}\Sigma^{\star}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}}H^{\star}\mathrm{P}_{\mathcal{T}})

Combining the statement (

A non-asymptotic instance-dependent benchmark:

Motivated from the discussion in the last two sections, we use
\delta_{N}
as the scaled difference between

4.1
Variance-reduced projected stochastic gradient

Our proposed algorithm is a variance reduced stochastic projected gradient descent algorithm and is stated in Algorithm
1
. Just like standard variance reduction schemes

our algorithm proceeds in epochs.

(a)

At the beginning of epoch, we create an approximation of the true constraint optimization problem (

(b)

Within each epoch, we run a proximal stochastic gradient algorithm on the approximate problem constructed step (a).

Concretely, at epoch

we start with an estimate
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{m}

of the problem (
13
), and
T
iid sample
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{T}\}
samples from the distribution

A few comments regrading Theorem

Non asymptotic nature of upper bound:

We point out that our upper bound is non-asymptotic in nature, and the bound holds whenever the number of samples
N\gtrsim\frac{\mathrm{L}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}
, up to some logarithmic factors. The lower bound
\frac{\mathrm{L}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}
is needed to distinguish a
\mu

21
) is
\frac{\log N}{N}\cdot\delta^{2}\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)
— which differs from our aimed upper bound (
12
) of
\frac{\delta^{2}(N)}{N}
by logarithmic factors. Our bound (
21
) also involves a term which depends on the initial-gap
\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{1}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}
; this decencies on the initialization-dependent term decay to zero at a rate
\frac{1}{N^{2}}
.

Choice of step-sizes:

Theorem
1
analyzes a variance reduced algorithm with a constant stepsize
\lambda
. A similar analysis can be performed with appropriate polynomially decaying stepsizes, i.e,
\lambda=\frac{c_{\omega}}{k^{\omega}}
for
\omega\in(0,1)
, and
c_{\omega}
is an appropriate universal constant. See for instance the analysis in
[
21
]
.

Comparison to standard variance reduction methods:

As we mentioned before, our algorithm is motivated from standard
variance reduction schemes
[
9
,
24
]
and proceeds in epochs. In usual variance reduction schemes
[
9
,
24
]
, the number of samples used in epochs increases exponentially with the number of epoch
m
. In our algorithm, however, the number of samples used in each epoch remains same. This change in our algorithm is due to some technical reasons and it contributes to additional factors of
\log(N)
in our upper bounds. This produces a leading term of
\frac{\log N}{N}\cdot\delta^{2}\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\right)
instead of
\delta^{2}\left(N\right)/N
. It would be interesting to know if these additional logarithmic factors can be removed.

Removing logarithmic terms in asymptotic limit:

It is natural to ask what is the asymptotic behavior of the output of Algorithm
1
. Assuming the output

remain bounded, taking number of samples

in the bound (

, and a universal constant. It is interesting to ask whether, these logarithmic factor can be removed, at least asymptotically. Indeed, assuming the iterates

\displaystyle c_{1}\leq\frac{\delta^{2}(N)}{N}\leq c_{2}\qquad\text{for all}\;\;N\geq N_{0}.

\left(1-\frac{\mu^{2}}{6\cdot 8^{2}\mathrm{L}^{2}}\right)^{k}\leq 1/120
for
k\geq\frac{\log(120)}{\log(1/(1-\mu^{2}/6\cdot 8^{2}\cdot\mathrm{L}^{2}))}
. It remains to prove the bound (
25
).

Proof of bound (
25
):

The bound follows from the following
key
lemma on the progress of proximal operators.

Lemma 2
.

Given a function For any stepsize
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{L}}
we have

\displaystyle f(y)+\mathrm{R}(y)\geq f(x^{+})+\mathrm{R}(x^{+})+g^{\top}(y-x)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}+\Delta^{\top}(x^{+}-y)

(30)

where
x^{+}=\mathrm{prox}_{\mathrm{R}}(x-\lambda v)
and
\Delta=v-\nabla f(x)
.

Given this lemma at hand, we first note that the perturbed function
\tilde{f}
is

\mu
-strongly convex and
\mathrm{L}
-smooth, and the stepsize
\lambda=\frac{\mu}{16\mathrm{L}}\cdot\frac{1}{\mathrm{L}}\leq 1/\mathrm{L}
. Now applying Lemma
2

on the function
\tilde{f}
,
y=\widehat{x}
,
x=x_{k-1}
,
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
and
v=\widetilde{\nabla}f_{k,m}(x_{k-1})
yields the bound (
25
). We provide a proof of Lemma
2
in Appendix
A.1
.

5.2
Lipschitz property of perturbed problem solutions:

The second ingredient in the proof is a Lipschitz property of the perturbed problem. Consider two following perturbed problems

\displaystyle x_{\tilde{f}}=\arg\min_{x}\left\{f(x)+\left\langle x,\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})-\nabla f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})\right\rangle+\mathrm{R}(x)\right\}\equiv\arg\min_{x}\tilde{f}(x)\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle x_{\tilde{g}}=\arg\min_{x}\left\{f(x)+\left\langle x,\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(x^{\star})-\nabla f(x^{\star})\right\rangle+\mathrm{R}(x)\right\}\equiv\arg\min_{x}\tilde{g}(x)

Here,
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}
is any point independent of the randomness in
\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f
, and
x^{\star}
is the (unique) solution of the problem (
1
).
The
\mu
-strong convexity of the function
f
ensures that the function
\tilde{f}
satisfies the following two growth condition

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{f}(x_{\tilde{f}})+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-x_{\tilde{f}}\|^{2}\qquad\text{for all}\;\;x.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{\tilde{g}}-x_{\tilde{f}}\|^{2}\leq\tilde{f}(x_{\tilde{g}})-\tilde{f}(x_{\tilde{f}})

\displaystyle=\left\{\tilde{f}(x_{\tilde{g}})-\tilde{g}(x_{\tilde{g}})\right\}+\underbrace{\left\{\tilde{g}(x_{\tilde{g}})-\tilde{g}(x_{\tilde{f}})\right\}}_{\leq 0}-\left\{\tilde{f}(x_{\tilde{f}})-\tilde{g}(x_{\tilde{f}})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\tilde{f}(x_{\tilde{g}})-\tilde{g}(x_{\tilde{g}})\right\}-\left\{\tilde{f}(x_{\tilde{f}})-\tilde{g}(x_{\tilde{f}})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\langle x_{\tilde{g}}-x_{\tilde{f}},[\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})-\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(x^{\star})]-[\nabla f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})-\nabla f(x^{\star})]\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{\tilde{g}}-x_{\tilde{f}}\|\cdot\|[\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})-\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(x^{\star})]-[\nabla f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})-\nabla f(x^{\star})]\|

Rearranging, last bound and taking expectation we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|x_{\tilde{g}}-x_{\tilde{f}}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4{\mathbb{E}}\|[\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})-\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 2.05pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[8.12498pt]{5.96916pt}{0.43057pt}}{\nabla}f(x^{\star})]-[\nabla f(\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x})-\nabla f(x^{\star})]\|^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\leq\frac{16\mathrm{L}^{2}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{T\mu^{2}}

(31)

The last inequality above follows from the
\mathrm{L}
-Lipschitz property of the gradient
\nabla f
.

5.3
Between epoch progress:

We complete the proof by quantifying the progress over epochs. Recalling the definition of
\widehat{x}_{m}
and
x_{N/\log N}^{\star}
from (
22
) and (
15
), respectively, we deduce the following bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{m+1}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{m+1}-\widehat{x}_{m}\|^{2}+2{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\leq}}\frac{2{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{m}-\widehat{x}_{m}\|^{2}}{20}+2{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}}{5}+\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}}{5}+2{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}}{5}+\frac{11}{5}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

(32)

Inequality
(i)
follows from Lemma
1
. Next, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}\|\widehat{x}_{m}-x_{N/\log N}^{\star}\|^{2}+2{\mathbb{E}}\|x_{N/\log N}^{\star}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\cdot 16\mathrm{L}^{2}}{T\mu^{2}}\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{m}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}+2{\mathbb{E}}\|x_{N/\log N}^{\star}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

The second last line above used the bound (
31
) and noting that

. The last line uses the fact
T=\frac{N}{\log N}\geq\frac{60\cdot 32\cdot\mathrm{L}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}
. Combining bounds (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{M+1}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\hskip 1.29167pt\hskip 0.0pt\rule[5.59721pt]{4.2024pt}{0.43057pt}}{x}_{M}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}}{4}+5\cdot{\mathbb{E}}\|x_{N/\log N}^{\star}-x_{\mathbb{P}_{0}}^{\star}\|^{2}

.

6
Discussion

In this paper we study the non-asymptotic performance of a natural a variance-reduced algorithm (VRPG) for a stochastic approximation problem under constraints. An attractive feature of our results are that they are instance-dependent in nature. Meaning our guarantees reflect the complexity of the loss function, the variability of the noise, the geometry constraint set. Additionally, we show that when the number of samples
N\rightarrow\infty
, our upper bound matches the celebrated Há jek-Le Cam local minimax lower bound up to a factor of
\log(N)
and some universal constants.

Our work leaves open a number of open questions. For instance, there is a significant body of work which construct a non-asymptotic local minimax lower bound by comparing the given problem with its hardest alternative in a local shrinking neighborhood of the given problem
[
1
]
. Indeed, in absence of the constraint set (i.e.
\mathcal{X}=\real^{d}
), variance reduced schemes — the ones similar to the one studied in our paper — achieve such non-asymptotic local minimax lower bound
[
11
,
12
]
. It would be interesting to see
if such results can be extended to our setting where
\mathcal{X}\neq\real^{d}
.

A.1
Proof of Lemma
2
:

This Lemma is directly borrowed from

. We provide a proof of this result for completeness. Recall
that

\displaystyle x^{+}=\arg\min_{y}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\|y-(x-\lambda v)\|^{2}+\mathrm{R}(y)\right\}

Thus, there exists
u\in\partial\mathrm{R}(x^{+})
such that

\displaystyle x^{+}=(x-\lambda v)-\lambda\cdot u

(34)

Now using
\mu
-strong convexity of
f
and convexity of the penalty function
\mathrm{R}
we have

\displaystyle f(y)+\mathrm{R}(y)\geq f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}+\mathrm{R}(x^{+})+u^{\top}(y-x^{+})

Additionally, using
\mathrm{L}
smoothness of the function
f
, and the expression
x-x^{+}=\lambda g
we have

\displaystyle f(y)+\mathrm{R}(y)

\displaystyle\geq f(x^{+})+\mathrm{R}(x^{+})-\frac{L\lambda^{2}}{2}\|x^{+}-x\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\nabla f(x^{+})(x^{+}-x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)+u^{\top}(y-x^{+})

\displaystyle=f(x^{+})+\mathrm{R}(x^{+})-\frac{L\lambda^{2}}{2}\|x^{+}-x\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+g^{\top}(y-x)+\lambda\|g\|^{2}+\Delta^{\top}(x^{+}-y)

(35)

The last line above uses the following simplification using the relations
\Delta=v-\nabla f(x)
,
u=g-v
, and
x-x^{+}=\lambda g

\displaystyle-\nabla f(x^{+})(x^{+}-x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)+u^{\top}(y-x^{+})

\displaystyle=g^{\top}(y-x^{+})+(v-\nabla f(x))^{\top}(x^{+}-y)

\displaystyle=g^{\top}(y-x)+\lambda\|g\|^{2}+\Delta^{\top}(x^{+}-y)

Finally, using the stepsize condition
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{L}}
in the bound (
35
) we conclude

\displaystyle f(y)+\mathrm{R}(y)\geq f(x^{+})+\mathrm{R}(x^{+})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g\|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x^{+}\|^{2}+\Delta^{\top}(x^{+}-y).

This completes the proof of Lemma
2
.

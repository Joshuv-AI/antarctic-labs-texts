Some Fock spaces with depth two action Thanks: This work was supported in part by a Simons Foundation Collaboration Grant.
Michael Anshelevich, Jacob Mashburn

Abstract
. The subject of this paper are operators represented on a Fock space which act only on the two leading components of the tensor. We unify the constructions from [ Ans07 , LS08 , BL09 , BL11 ] , and extend a number of results from these papers to our more general setting. The results include the quadratic relation satisfied by (the kernel of) the free cumulant generating function, the resolvent form of the generating function for the Wick polynomials, and classification results for the case when the vacuum state on the operator algebra is tracial. We handle the generating functions in infinitely many variables by considering their matrix-valued versions.

1.
Introduction

Symmetric, anti-symmetric, and full Fock spaces have been studied for almost a century. They have appeared in quantum mechanics and the theory of operator algebras; in combinatorics, representation theory, and the study of orthogonal polynomials; and in other fields. Over the last few decades, there has been a proliferation of other Fock spaces, with accompanying structures which to a greater or lesser degree share the properties of the classical ones. Such structures include commutation relations; Gaussian-type and Poisson-type operators; Wick products and orthogonal polynomials; Itô calculus, etc. Among these numerous constructions, we will highlight three, all of which appeared roughly at the same time a decade ago. While quite different, they all share a common feature, which one might call “depth two action” or “nearest neighbor coupling”. In this paper we study a more general construction extending the three above, which retains this feature.

To be specific, let
\mathcal{B}
be a unital
\ast
-algebra, with a positive faithful linear functional
\phi
, and form the algebraic Fock space
\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\otimes n}
. For each
b\in\mathcal{B}
, on this space we will define operators
a^{+}(b),a^{-}(b),a^{0}(b)
and
X(b)=a^{+}(b)+a^{0}(b)+a^{-}(b)
. Here the creation operator
a^{+}(b)
is defined in the usual way, but the annihilation operator acts on simple tensors as

a^{-}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=(\gamma+\phi)[bu_{1}]u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n},

where
\gamma+\phi\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
is some completely positive map. Note that this operator couples together the first two components of the tensor. This should be compared with, on the one hand, the free Fock space, where the annihilation operator acts only on the first component of the tensor; and, on the other hand, with the
q
-Fock space, where its action involves all components of the tensor. Note also that if
\gamma=0
, this is just the standard free annihilation operator on the full Fock space
\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathcal{B},\phi))
. The preservation operator has the form

a^{0}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=\Lambda(b)u_{1}\otimes u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}

or more generally

a^{0}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=\Lambda(b\otimes u_{1})\otimes u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}

for a map
\Lambda
satisfying a symmetry condition, and acts only on the first component of the tensor.

Before proceeding further, we now outline the three earlier constructions mentioned above. We only list some of the results about these constructions; each of the papers below contains other important results not pertinent to this introduction. It can easily be seen that these constructions all fit in the setting of the preceding paragraph.

In
[
LS08
]
, Lenczewski and Sałapata constructed a deformation of the free Fock space of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with the annihilation operator of the form

a^{-}(f)g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\int f(x_{1})w(x_{1},x_{2})g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,dx_{1}

for a specific function
w(s,t)
of two variables. They studied combinatorial properties of operators
X(f)
on this space, in particular showing that they have the Kesten distribution. In
[
BL09
,
BL11
]
, Bożejko and Lytvynov constructed, in the setting of standard triples, a Fock space based on the annihilation operator

a^{-}(f)g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\int f(x_{1})\eta(x_{1})g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,dx_{1}

for some function
\eta
on
\mathbb{R}
. They studied general (Lévy-type) processes on such a Fock space. Perhaps most importantly, they gave a characterization among these, by a certain continuity property, of what is natural to call the
Free Meixner class
. The paper also contains several formulas for free cumulants of the distributions of such processes. In the second paper, they constructed a generating function for a system of polynomials (in infinitely many variables) associated to such a process in terms of certain operator-valued functions. Finally, in
[
Ans07
]
, the first author studied the free Meixner states. Here the Fock space was based on a finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the annihilation operator is

a^{-}_{i}(e_{u(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{u(n)})=C_{u(1),u(2)}\left\langle{e_{i}},{e_{u(1)}}\right\rangle e_{u(2)}\otimes\ldots e_{u(n)}.

The key results involved equations satisfied by the free cumulant generating function and the generating function for the Wick products (which can be identified with monic orthogonal polynomials in
d
variables), as well as the constructions in which the vacuum state is tracial on the algebra of these polynomials.

Motivated by these three paper, our primary interest in this article is in

•

The joint distributions of
\left\{X(u):u\in\mathcal{B}\right\}
, typically expressed in terms of appropriate cumulants.

•

The Wick polynomials, which are polynomials in
\left\{X(u):u\in\mathcal{B}\right\}
such that

W(u_{1}\otimes u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})\Omega=u_{1}\otimes u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}
.

•

The algebras generated by
\left\{X(u):u\in\mathcal{B}\right\}
.

Free and Boolean cumulant generating functions for free Meixner families
[
BB06
,
Ans07
]
satisfy second-order equations. Depth two action on the Fock space results in such equations being satisfied not by the scalar-valued free cumulant generating function
R(u)
itself, but by its
\mathcal{B}
-valued “kernel”
R^{\prime}(u)
with
R(u)=\phi[uR^{\prime}(u)u]
. In fact,

(1)

R^{\prime}(u)v=v+R^{\prime}(u)\gamma\left[uR^{\prime}(u)u\right]v+R^{\prime}(u)\Lambda(u\otimes v).

This result easily generalizes to a finite family of variables
\left\{u_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{d}
. To make sense of a generating function for joint free cumulants of infinitely many variables
\left\{u_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, we take an approach different from
[
BL11
]
. We form an infinite matrix which contains all the information about joint free cumulants of
\left\{u_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, and still satisfies (an appropriate version of) equation (
1
). In the case when
\Lambda(u\otimes v)=\Lambda(u)v
, for
\left\{u_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
uniformly small, this matrix corresponds to a genuine bounded operator. The analysis is similar in style to, but different from, computations with fully matricial free cumulants
[
PV13
]
.

We perform a similar analysis for the joint generating function of Wick polynomials. It can be interpreted as an infinite matrix, and under appropriate assumptions as a bounded operator. As in
[
Ans07
,
BL11
]
, it has a resolvent-type form

W(u)=(B(u)-X(u))^{-1}(B(u)-\phi[u^{2}]),

where
B(u)=1+\Lambda(u)+(\gamma+\phi)[u^{2}]
. See Section
4
.

Finally, we investigate the situation when the vacuum state on the algebra generated by all operators
\left\{X(u):u\in\mathcal{B}\right\}
is tracial. The depth two nature of the action allows us to write down explicit conditions on
\phi,\gamma,\Lambda
which guarantee this. In the case when
\gamma=0
, the Fock space is the full Fock space, but the circular operators
X(u)
are deformed by a non-trivial
\Lambda
. We show that one can always use
\Lambda
to define a new multiplication on
\mathcal{B}
, so that the representation splits into a semicircular and a free compound Poisson parts. More generally, if
\gamma[u]=\eta u
for
\eta
central (related to the construction from
[
BL09
]
) then one has a similar decomposition, but with the third component on which
\Lambda(u\otimes v)=\lambda uv
for
\lambda
central.

The paper
[
Ans07
]
contained another example of a “depth two action” algebra with a tracial vacuum state, which naturally corresponded to the free multinomial distribution. This example also generalizes to our setting here. It is described in a forthcoming paper
[
AM22
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. After the Introduction, in Section
2
we present the main construction. In Section
3
, we prove formulas for joint moments, and Boolean and free cumulants, of the operators
\left\{X(u):u\in\mathcal{B}\right\}
. We also compare and contrast these formulas with the operator-valued results of
[
AW18
]
. In particular, unlike in
[
AW18
]
, the inner product in this paper is scalar-valued rather than
\mathcal{B}
-valued. In Section
4
, we discuss Wick polynomials, and matricial generating functions for them and for the free cumulants. In Section
5
, we provide conditions under which operators
X(u)
, as well as various generating functions, are bounded. In Section
6
we derive the conditions for the vacuum state to be tracial. The results in Sections
4
and
5
are proven in the setting of
\Lambda(u\otimes v)=\Lambda(u)v
; in contrast, the results in Section
6
are of main interest for general
\Lambda
. Finally, in Section
7
, we describe in detail the examples which motivated this paper, as well as their generalizations covered by our construction. We also prove a representation theorem under the assumption that the vacuum state is tracial.

2.
The construction

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
be
\ast
-algebras. An element
b\in\mathcal{B}
is positive if
b=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}^{\ast}u_{i}
for some
k
and
u_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
. A map
T:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is positive if for each
u\in\ \mathcal{B}
,
T(u^{\ast}u)
is positive in
\mathcal{C}
. It is faithful if
T(u^{\ast}u)=0
only for
u=0
. It is completely positive if for each
n
, the map

T_{n}:M_{n}(\mathcal{B})\rightarrow M_{n}(\mathcal{C}),\,\,\,T_{n}\left([a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}\right)=[T(a_{ij})]_{i,j=1}^{n}

is positive, where we use the usual
\ast
-structure on
M_{n}(\mathcal{B})
.

Construction 2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a unital *-algebra, equipped with star-linear maps
\phi:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
\gamma:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
, and
\Lambda:\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\text{alg}}\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
such that
\phi
is positive and faithful,
\gamma+\phi=\gamma+\phi\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
is completely positive (semi-definite), and
\Lambda
satisfies

(2)

\phi[v^{\ast}\Lambda(b\otimes u)]=\phi[\Lambda(b^{\ast}\otimes v)^{\ast}u],\quad\gamma[v^{\ast}\Lambda(b\otimes u)]=\gamma[\Lambda(b^{\ast}\otimes v)^{\ast}u],

On the algebraic Fock space,

\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})=\mathbb{C}\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}^{\otimes n},

define the inner product by the linear extension of

(3)

\left\langle{u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}},{v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{k}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}\\
=\delta_{n=k}\phi\left[v_{n}^{\ast}(\gamma+\phi)[v_{n-1}^{\ast}(\gamma+\phi)[\ldots(\gamma+\phi)[v_{1}^{\ast}u_{1}]\ldots]u_{n-1}]u_{n}\right].

This inner product is positive semi-definite but not, in general, positive definite. Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\phi}(\mathcal{B})
the completion of the quotient
\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})/\mathcal{N}
by the subspace
\mathcal{N}
of elements of zero seminorm. Next, for each
b\in\mathcal{B}
, consider operators on
\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})

a^{+}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=b\otimes u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n},

a^{-}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=(\gamma+\phi)[bu_{1}]u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n},

a^{-}(b)(u_{1})=\phi[bu_{1}]\Omega,

a^{0}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=\Lambda(b\otimes u_{1})\otimes u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n},

a^{-}(b)(\Omega)=a^{0}(b)(\Omega)=0,

and

X(b)=a^{+}(b)+a^{-}(b)+a^{0}(b).

Several of the results below address the questions of when these operators are defined on
\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})/\mathcal{N}
or on
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\phi}(\mathcal{B})
.

Denote

\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)=\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(X(b):b\in\mathcal{B})=\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(X(b):b\in\mathcal{B}^{sa})

and define on it the
vacuum state

A\mapsto\left\langle{A\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle
.

Remark 3
.

The following observations are straightforward.

•

If for some
t<1
,
\gamma+t\phi
is still completely positive, then the inner product (
3
) is positive definite.

•

If the operators in Construction
2
are bounded with respect to the semi-norm
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{\gamma,\phi}
over
\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})
, then they are well-defined over
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\phi}(\mathcal{B})
and are also norm-bounded.

•

For
\vec{\zeta},\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})
,

\left\langle{a^{+}(b)\vec{\zeta}},{\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}=\left\langle{\vec{\zeta}},{a^{-}(b^{\ast})\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},\quad\left\langle{a^{0}(b)\vec{\zeta}},{\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}=\left\langle{\vec{\zeta}},{a^{0}(b^{\ast})\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}.

In particular, for self-adjoint
b
,
X(b)
is symmetric. In the case when
\Lambda(b\otimes u)=\Lambda(b)u
for
\Lambda:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
, assumption (
2
) simplifies to
(\Lambda(b))^{\ast}=\Lambda(b^{\ast})
.

3.
Moments, free cumulants, Boolean cumulants

In this section, we give combinatorial expressions for the moments and free cumulants of the variables
\{X(u_{i})\}
. For any family of bounded operators
X_{1},...,X_{n}
over a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a state
\psi
over
B(\mathcal{H})
, their
moments
are the numbers

\psi[X_{i(1)}...X_{i(k)}].

Combinatorial background.

A
partition

\pi
of a subset
S\subset\mathbb{N}
is a collection of disjoint subsets of
S
(called
blocks
of
\pi
) whose union equals
S
. We will use
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}j
to say that
i
and
j
are in the same block of
\pi
. In this paper, we will only be concerned with partitions of
[n]:=\{1,2,...,n\}
.

Let NC
(n)
denote the set of
noncrossing partitions
over
[n]
, that is, those partitions
\pi
such that there are no
i<j<k<\ell
such that
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}k
and
j\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}\ell
unless all four are in the same block.
If
i\in[n]
is the first element of its block, we will call it an
opening element
, while the last of its block will be called a
closing element
. If
i
is neither, it will be called a
middle element
.

For distinct blocks
V
and
W
of a noncrossing partition
\pi
,
V
is said to be
inner with respect to

W
if
o_{W}<o_{V}<c_{V}<c_{W}
, where
o_{V}
and
o_{W}
are the opening elements of
V
and
W
, respectively, while
c_{V}
and
c_{W}
are their closing elements. We will simply say a block is
inner
if it is inner with respect to some block, and
outer
if it is not.

Finally, let Int
(n)
denote the
interval partitions
over
[n]
, that is, those partitions
\pi
such that whenever
i<j
and
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}j
, we have
i\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}k
for all
i<k<j
.

The
free cumulants
of
X_{1},...,X_{n}
are defined inductively via the
moment-cumulant formula

\psi[X_{i(1)},...,X_{i(k)}]=\sum_{\pi\in\text{NC}(n)}R_{\pi}[X_{i(1)},...,X_{i(k)}],

where

R_{\pi}[X_{1},...,X_{k}]=\prod_{V\in\pi}R[X_{V(1)},...,X_{V(|V|)}].

Their
Boolean cumulants
are defined in almost exactly the same manner:

\psi[X_{i(1)},...,X_{i(k)}]=\sum_{\pi\in\text{Int}(n)}B_{\pi}[X_{i(1)},...,X_{i(k)}],

where

B_{\pi}[X_{1},...,X_{k}]=\prod_{V\in\pi}B[X_{V(1)},...,X_{V(|V|)}].

Notation 4
.

Denote
\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)
the set of noncrossing partitions of
[n]
with no singleton blocks. For
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, in the moment and Boolean cumulant formulas below, we will assign to each
\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)
the weight operator on
\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})

W_{M}(\pi)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(u_{i}),

where

a_{i}=\begin{cases}a^{+},\text{ if }i\text{ is a closing element,}\\
a^{-},\text{ if }i\text{ is an opening element,}\\
a^{0},\text{ if }i\text{ is a middle element.}\end{cases}

Then we have the following standard expansion.

Proposition 5
.

Given
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, we have the following mixed moment formula:

(4)

\left\langle{X(u_{1})...X(u_{n})\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},

if
n\geq 2
and zero if
n=1
.

Notation 6
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}(n)=\{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(n)|1\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}n\}
, and similarly, let

\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)=\{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(n)|1\overset{\pi}{\thicksim}n\text{ and }\pi\text{ has no singleton blocks}\}.

Lemma 7
.

For
n\geq 2
, given
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, we have the following mixed Boolean cumulant formula:

(5)

B_{n}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]=\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},

and for
n=1
, the cumulant is zero.

Proof.

n=1
is clear. Assume the result holds for all natural numbers less than some
n
, and take
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
. By Proposition
5
, we have

\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Int}(n)}B_{\pi}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]=\left\langle{X(u_{1})...X(u_{n})\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}.

For convenience, denote by
\mathrm{NC}_{ns,mo}(n)
the noncrossing, no-singleton partitions of
[n]
that have more than one outer block. After isolating the
n
th cumulant (corresponding to the partition
\hat{1}_{n}
consisting of a single block), we get

\displaystyle B_{n}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}-\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Int}(n)\backslash\{\hat{1}_{n}\}}B_{\pi}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}-\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Int}(n)\backslash\{\hat{1}_{n}\}}\prod_{V\in\pi}\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(|V|)}\langle W_{M}(\sigma)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}-\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{NC}_{ns,mo}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{M}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},

where the second equality follows from the induction hypothesis.
∎

Notation 8
.

Define the operator
a_{\gamma}^{\sim}(b)
(
b\in\mathcal{B}
) by linear extension of

a_{\gamma}^{\sim}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=\gamma[bu_{1}]u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}\text{ for }n\geq 2,

a_{\gamma}^{\sim}(b)(u_{1})=0\text{ for }n=1,\text{ and}

a_{\gamma}^{\sim}(b)(\Omega)=0.

The operator
a_{\phi}^{\sim}(b)
is defined in a similar manner (for
n\geq 2
, apply
\phi
, and
a_{\phi}^{\sim}(b)(\Omega)=0
), with one critical exception:

a_{\phi}^{\sim}(b)(u_{1})=\phi[bu_{1}]\Omega\text{ for }n=1.

Thus
a^{-}(b)=a_{\gamma}^{\sim}(b)+a_{\phi}^{\sim}(b)
.

For
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, in the free cumulant formula below, we will assign to each
\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(n)
the weight operator on
\mathcal{F}_{alg}(\mathcal{B})

W_{C}(\pi)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(u_{i}),

where

a_{i}=\begin{cases}a^{+},\text{ if }i\text{ is a closing element,}\\
a_{\gamma}^{\sim},\text{ if }i\neq 1\text{ and is an opening element,}\\
a_{\phi}^{\sim},\text{ if }i=1\text{, or}\\
a^{0},\text{ if }i\text{ is a middle element.}\end{cases}

Proposition 9
.

For
n\geq 2
, given
u_{1},...,u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
, we have the following mixed free cumulant formula:

(6)

R_{n}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]=\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)}\langle W_{C}(\pi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},

and for
n=1
, the cumulant is zero.

Proof.

By Theorem 1 in
[
BN08
]
and Lemma
7
,

(7)

\begin{split}\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)}R_{\pi}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]&=B_{n}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]\\
&=\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)}\sum_{\ell\in\text{Block labelings}(\gamma,\phi)}\langle W(\pi,\ell)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},\end{split}

where
\ell
is a labeling of the blocks of
\pi
with either a
\gamma
or
\phi
such that the block containing 1 and
n
is labeled
\phi
, and
W(\pi,\ell)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(u_{i})
, where

a_{i}=\begin{cases}a^{+},\text{ if }i\text{ is a closing element,}\\
a_{\gamma}^{\sim},\text{ if }i\text{ is an opening element of a block labeled }\gamma,\\
a_{\phi}^{\sim},\text{ if }i\text{ is an opening element of a block labeled }\phi,\\
a_{0},\text{ if }i\text{ is a middle element.}\end{cases}

This splitting of terms follows from linearity and the fact that the opening of each block is weighted with
(\gamma+\phi)
of a product involving the operator in that position.

Since the first moment (and thus free cumulant) is zero, for
n=2
, we have

R_{2}[X(u_{1}),X(u_{2})]=M_{2}[X(u_{1})X(u_{2})]=\langle a^{-}(u_{1})a^{+}(u_{2})\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},

and for
n=3
, we have

R_{3}[X(u_{1}),X(u_{2}),X(u_{3})]=M_{3}[X(u_{1})X(u_{2})X(u_{3})]=\langle a^{-}(u_{1})a^{0}(u_{2})a^{+}(u_{3})\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}.

For induction, assume for all natural numbers less than some
n
, the formula holds.

Let
\ll
denote the partial order on NC
(n)
defined by

\sigma\ll\pi\,\,\,\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,\,\,\,\sigma\leq\pi\,\text{ and }\,\forall\,V\in\pi,\,\,\min(V)\overset{\sigma}{\thicksim}\max(V).

Then the inductive hypothesis implies the left-hand side of (
7
) equals

R_{n}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]+\sum_{\pi\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)\backslash\{1_{n}\}}\sum_{\sigma\ll\pi}\langle W(\sigma)\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},

where each
\sigma
is labeled such that for all
V\in\pi
,
\sigma|_{V}
has its unique outer block labeled
\phi
, while the rest (that is, the inner blocks) are labeled
\gamma
.

By Remark 2.14 in
[
BN08
]
,

\{\pi\in\mathrm{NC}(n):\sigma\ll\pi\}\simeq\{S\subset\sigma:S\text{ contains the outer blocks of }\sigma\}=:S_{\sigma}.

With this bijection, the above sum can be rewritten as

R_{n}[X(u_{1}),...,X(u_{n})]+\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathrm{NC}}_{ns}(n)\backslash\{1_{n}\}}\sum_{S\in S_{\sigma}}\langle W(\sigma,\ell_{S})\Omega,\Omega\rangle_{\gamma,\phi},

where
\ell_{S}
is the labeling constructed by giving blocks in
S
the label
\phi
and the rest
\gamma
. Subtracting the sum from both sides gives the result.

4.1.
Wick Polynomials

Definition 19
.

For
u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}\in\mathcal{B}^{\otimes n}
, define the operator
W(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})
on the algebraic Fock space by
the recursion

\begin{split}&W(b\otimes u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})\\
&\quad=X(b)W(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})-W(a^{0}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}))-W(a^{-}(b)(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}))\\
&\quad=X(b)W(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})-W(\Lambda(b\otimes u_{1})\otimes u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})-W((\gamma+\phi)[bu_{1}]u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})\end{split}

with the initial conditions

W(\emptyset)=I,\quad W(u_{1})=X(u_{1}),\quad W(u_{1}\otimes u_{2})=X(u_{1})W(u_{2})-W(\Lambda(u_{1}\otimes u_{2}))-\phi[u_{1}u_{2}].

It is easy to check using the recursion that

W(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})\Omega=u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}.

Since each
W(\vec{\xi})\in\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
, it follows that
\Omega
is cyclic for this algebra.
W(\vec{\xi})
is called a
Wick polynomial
. In particular, we denote
W_{n}(u)=W(u^{\otimes n})
, and note that
W_{n}(u)\Omega=u^{\otimes n}
.

For the remainder of this section, we assume that
\Lambda
has the special form
\Lambda(u\otimes v)=\Lambda(u)v
.

The next proposition states that formally, the generating function for univariate Wick polynomials is a rational function in
X(u)
. In Section
5
, we will give conditions under which the series defining the generating function is convergent and corresponds to a well defined operator.

Proposition 20
.

Denote
W(u)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}W_{n}(u)
and

b(u)=1+\Lambda(u)+(\gamma+\phi)[u^{2}].

Then

(b(u)-X(u))W(u)=b(u)-\phi[u^{2}].

Proof.

X(u)W_{n}(u)=W_{n+1}(u)+(\gamma+\phi)[u^{2}]W_{n-1}(u)+\Lambda(u)W_{n}(u),

X(u)W_{1}(u)=W_{2}(u)+\phi[u^{2}]+\Lambda(u)W_{1}(u),

X(u)=W_{1}(u).

So

X(u)W(u)=W(u)-1+\phi[u^{2}]+(\gamma+\phi)[u^{2}](W(u)-1)+\Lambda(u)(W(u)-1).\qed

\psi_{\vec{\xi}}[u^{\ast}u]=\left\langle{m(u)\vec{\xi}},{m(u)\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}\geq 0
. Therefore

\left\langle{a^{+}(b)\vec{\xi}},{a^{+}(b)\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}=\psi_{\vec{\xi}}[(\gamma+\phi)[b^{\ast}b]]\leq\left\|{(\gamma+\phi)[b^{\ast}b]}\right\|\psi_{\vec{\xi}}[1]=\left\|{(\gamma+\phi)[b^{\ast}b]}\right\|\left\|{\vec{\xi}}\right\|_{\gamma,\phi}^{2}.

Hence,
\|a^{+}(b)\|_{\gamma,\phi}\leq\sqrt{\max(\phi[b^{\ast}b],\left\|{(\gamma+\phi)[b^{\ast}b]}\right\|)}
.

Since
a^{-}(b)
is the adjoint of
a^{+}(b)
, their operator norms are equal, so the above inequality also applies to
a^{-}
.

The argument for
a^{0}(b)
, with
\Lambda
of the special form, is similar.
∎

Proposition 29
.

Denote
c_{\gamma,\phi}=\sqrt{\max(\left\|{\phi}\right\|,\left\|{\gamma+\phi}\right\|)}
and

K=\max\left(1,(\sqrt{2}-1)\left(2+\frac{\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|}{c_{\gamma,\phi}}\right)\right).

Then for
(u_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{B}
,

\left\|{W(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})}\right\|\leq(\sqrt{2}+1)^{n}c_{\gamma,\phi}^{n}K\left\|{u_{1}}\right\|\ldots\left\|{u_{n}}\right\|.

Proof.

\left\|{W(\emptyset)}\right\|=1\leq K
, and by Proposition
28
,

\left\|{W(u_{1})}\right\|=\left\|{X(u_{1})}\right\|\leq(2c_{\gamma,\phi}+\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|)\left\|{u_{1}}\right\|\leq(\sqrt{2}+1)c_{\gamma,\phi}K\left\|{u_{1}}\right\|.

For
n=2
,

W(u_{1}\otimes u_{2})=X(u_{1})W(u_{2})-\Lambda(u_{1})W(u_{2})-\phi[u_{1}u_{2}]=(a^{+}(u_{1})+a^{-}(u_{1}))W(u_{2})-\phi[u_{1}u_{2}],

and

\begin{split}\left\|{W(u_{1}\otimes u_{2})}\right\|&\leq 2c_{\gamma,\phi}\ \left\|{u_{1}}\right\|\left\|{W(u_{2})}\right\|+\left\|{\phi}\right\|\ \left\|{u_{1}}\right\|\ \left\|{u_{2}}\right\|,\end{split}

while for
n\geq 3
,

W(u_{1}\otimes u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=(a^{+}(u_{1})+a^{-}(u_{1}))W(u_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})-(\gamma+\phi)[u_{1}u_{2}]W(u_{3}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}).

and

\left\|{W(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n})}\right\|\leq 2c_{\gamma,\phi}\ \left\|{u_{1}}\right\|\left\|{W(u_{2},\ldots,u_{n})}\right\|+\left\|{\gamma+\phi}\right\|\ \left\|{u_{1}}\right\|\ \left\|{u_{2}}\right\|\ \left\|{W(u_{3},\ldots,u_{n})}\right\|.

Since
(\sqrt{2}+1)^{n}=2(\sqrt{2}+1)^{n-1}+(\sqrt{2}+1)^{n-2}
, the result follows by induction.
∎

Let’s turn our attention back to the infinite matrix
W
whose structure we established in the previous section, particularly the decomposition into a sum of matrices with a single non-zero diagonal. The following more general proposition will be combined with this fact to obtain conditions under which
W
is a bounded operator. The following result must be standard, although we do not have a reference for it.

Lemma 30
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\ell^{2})
, which we can write as an infinite matrix with entries
T_{ij}\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\left\|{T}\right\|\leq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{k\geq\max(1,1-i)}\left\|{T_{k,k+i}}\right\|.

Corollary 31
.

For
\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|<\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{\max(\left\|{\phi}\right\|,\left\|{\gamma+\phi}\right\|)}}
,

(a)

W
is a bounded operator.

(b)

B-X
is invertible, and

W=(B-X)^{-1}(B-\Phi).

Proof.

For (a), by Proposition
29
,
\left\|{W_{i,i+n}}\right\|\leq K\left((\sqrt{2}+1)c_{\gamma,\phi}\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\right)^{n}
.
The result follows from Lemma
30
.

For (b), let
Y=B-X-1=A^{0}+\Gamma+\Phi-X
. Note that
Y
has non-zero entries on only two diagonals:

\displaystyle Y_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{+}(u_{i})+a^{-}(u_{i})

\displaystyle Y_{i,i+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma+\phi)[u_{i}u_{i+1}].

So apply Lemma
30
and Proposition
28
to get the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|{Y}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\left\|{a^{+}}\right\|+\left\|{a^{-}}\right\|)\left(\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\right)+\left\|{\gamma+\phi}\right\|\left(\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2c_{\gamma,\phi}\left(\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\right)+c_{\gamma,\phi}^{2}\left(\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\right)^{2}<1.

Therefore
(B-X)
is invertible. The formula follows from Proposition
21
.
∎

Finally, we return to the question of convergence for the cumulant generating function.

Proposition 32
.

Let
K=\max\{\sqrt{\left\|{\gamma}\right\|},\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|\}
. Then
\left\|{R_{n}^{\prime}[u]}\right\|\leq C_{n}K^{n}\left\|{u}\right\|^{n}
, where
C_{n}
is the Catalan number. If
\left\|{u}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{4K}
, then the generating function
R^{\prime}(u)
converges, and thus the cumulant generating function does as well.

Proof.

By Lemma
12
,
R^{\prime}_{0}[u]=1
,
\left\|{R^{\prime}_{1}[u]}\right\|\leq\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|\left\|{u}\right\|\leq K\left\|{u}\right\|
, and

\left\|{R^{\prime}_{2}[u]}\right\|\leq\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{\gamma}\right\|\leq 2K^{2}\left\|{u}\right\|^{2}.

Recursively,

\begin{split}\left\|{R^{\prime}_{n}[u]}\right\|&\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\left\|{R^{\prime}_{i}[u]}\right\|\left\|{R^{\prime}_{n-i-2}[u]}\right\|\left\|{\gamma}\right\|\left\|{u}\right\|^{2}+\left\|{R^{\prime}_{n-1}[u]}\right\|\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|\left\|{u}\right\|\\
&\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}C_{i}C_{n-i-2}K^{n-2}\left\|{\gamma}\right\|\left\|{u}\right\|^{n}+C_{n-1}K^{n-1}\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|\left\|{u}\right\|^{n}\\
&\leq C_{n}K^{n}\left\|{u}\right\|^{n}\end{split}

since
C_{n-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}C_{i}C_{n-i-2}
and
C_{n}\geq 2C_{n-1}
. So the generating function has norm bounded by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\|{R^{\prime}_{n}[u]}\right\|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}K^{n}\left\|{u}\right\|^{n}.

A well-known approximation of the Catalan numbers via Stirling’s formula is
C_{n}\sim\frac{4^{n}}{n^{3/2}\sqrt{\pi}}
. So this series converges by assumption.
∎

For the generating function
\mathcal{R}[U]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{n}[X,...,X]
for matricial cumulants, we also have the following corollary:

Corollary 33
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
32
, with the assumption
\left\|{u}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{4K}
replaced with
\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{4K}
, the infinite matrix
\mathcal{R}[U]
is bounded, with

(20)

\left\|{\mathcal{R}[U]}\right\|\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left\|{\phi}\right\|C_{n-2}K^{n-2}\left(\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\right)^{n}.

Proof.

Using Corollary
25
, the
i
th diagonal of
\mathcal{R}[U]
has the bound
\left\|{\phi}\right\|C_{n-2}K^{n-2}\left(\sup_{i}\left\|{u_{i}}\right\|\right)^{n}
, so the convergence of the sum follows from Lemma
30
and the same argument from the proof of Proposition
32
.
∎

6.
Traciality

Recall that a state
\psi
on a non-commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}
is
tracial
, or a
trace
, if for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
,

\psi[xy]=\psi[yx].

In this section, we give conditions under which the vacuum state is tracial. We start with an auxiliary result.

Definition 34
.

On
\mathcal{F}_{\text{alg}}(\mathcal{B})
, define an anti-linear involution by the linear extension of

S(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})=u_{n}^{\ast}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{1}^{\ast}.

For
b\in\mathcal{B}
, denote
X_{r}(b)=SX(b^{\ast})S
. Explicitly, for
n\geq 2
,

\begin{split}X_{r}(u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n})&=u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n}\otimes b+u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n-1}\otimes\Lambda(b^{\ast}\otimes u_{n}^{\ast})^{\ast}\\
&\quad+u_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes u_{n-1}(\gamma+\phi)[u_{n}b],\end{split}

with appropriate modifications for
n=1
and
n=0
. Denote
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi;r)=S\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)S
the algebra generated by
\left\{X_{r}(b):b\in\mathcal{B}\right\}
. For
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{alg}}(\mathcal{B})
a simple tensor, denote
W_{r}(\vec{\xi})=SW(\vec{\xi})S
. Then
W_{r}(S(\vec{\xi}))\Omega=\vec{\xi}
, so that
\Omega
is cyclic for
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi;r)
.

Proposition 35
.

(a)

Suppose that
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
and
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi;r)
commute. Then
W
extends to a linear map on
\mathcal{F}_{\text{alg}}(\mathcal{B})
,

\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)=\left\{W(\vec{\xi}):\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{alg}}(\mathcal{B})\right\},\quad\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi;r)=\left\{W_{r}(\vec{\xi}):\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{alg}}(\mathcal{B})\right\},

and for
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{alg}}(\mathcal{B})
,

W(\vec{\xi})^{\ast}=W(S(\vec{\xi})),

Therefore for
A\in\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
,
S(A\Omega)=A^{\ast}\Omega
.

(b)

In addition to the assumption in (a), suppose that
\phi
is tracial on
\mathcal{B}
. Then the vacuum state is tracial on
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
.

Proof.

(a)

Since
\Omega
is cyclic for
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
and
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi;r)
, it is separating for them (and the von Neumann algebras they generate). Using the recursion and induction, for
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{alg}}(\mathcal{B})

\begin{split}W(a^{+}(b)(\vec{\xi}))\Omega&=X(b)W(\vec{\xi})\Omega-W(a^{0}(b)(\vec{\xi}))\Omega-W(a^{-}(b)(\vec{\xi}))\Omega\\
&=X(b)\vec{\xi}-a^{0}(b)(\vec{\xi})-a^{-}(b)(\vec{\xi})\\
&=a^{+}(b)(\vec{\xi}).\end{split}

In particular, if
\vec{\xi}=0
, then
W(\vec{\xi})\Omega=0
, so
W(\vec{\xi})=0
, and the map
W
is well defined.

Clearly,
W(\vec{\xi})\in\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
. On the other hand, using induction on the number of factors, a monomial
X(u_{0})X(u_{1})\ldots X(u_{n})
is a linear combination of terms of the form
X(u_{0})W(\vec{\xi})
, each of which is a linear combination of the terms of the form
W(\vec{\xi^{\prime}})
by the recursion.

Next, note that

X_{r}(b)\Omega=X(b)\Omega

and

W(b)^{\ast}=X(b)^{\ast}=X(b^{\ast})=W(b^{\ast}).

Using induction,

\begin{split}W(a^{+}(b)(\vec{\xi}))^{\ast}&=W(\vec{\xi})^{\ast}X(b)^{\ast}-W(a^{0}(b)(\vec{\xi}))^{\ast}-W(a^{-}(b)(\vec{\xi}))^{\ast}\\
&=W(S(\vec{\xi}))X(b^{\ast})-W(S(a^{0}(b)(\vec{\xi})))-W(S(a^{-}(b)(\vec{\xi}))).\end{split}

On the other hand, if
X_{r}(b)
commutes with
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
,

\begin{split}W(S(\vec{\xi}))X(b^{\ast})\Omega&=W(S(\vec{\xi}))X_{r}(b^{\ast})\Omega=X_{r}(b^{\ast})W(S(\vec{\xi}))\Omega\\
&=SX(b)SS(\vec{\xi})=SX(b)(\vec{\xi}).\end{split}

Since
\Omega
is separating for
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
, this implies that

W(S(\vec{\xi}))X(b^{\ast})=W(SX(b)(\vec{\xi}))=W(S(a^{+}(b)(\vec{\xi})))+W(S(a^{0}(b)(\vec{\xi})))+W(S(a^{-}(b)(\vec{\xi}))).

Thus

W(a^{+}(b)(\vec{\xi}))^{\ast}=W(S(a^{+}(b)(\vec{\xi}))).

(b)

We first show that
X_{r}(b)^{\ast}\Omega=X(b)^{\ast}\Omega
. Indeed, for
\vec{\xi}\in\mathcal{B}^{\otimes n}
,
\left\langle{X_{r}(b)(\vec{\xi})},{\Omega}\right\rangle=0
if
n\neq 1
. For
u\in\mathcal{B}
, using the fact that
\phi
is a trace,

\begin{split}\left\langle{X_{r}(b)(u)},{\Omega}\right\rangle&=\left\langle{Sa^{-}(b^{\ast})(S(u))},{\Omega}\right\rangle=\overline{\phi[b^{\ast}u]}=\phi[ub]=\phi[bu]=\left\langle{u},{b^{\ast}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{u},{X(b)^{\ast}\Omega}\right\rangle\end{split}

To check that the vacuum state is tracial on
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
, it suffices to verify that

\begin{split}\left\langle{W(\vec{\xi})X(b)\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle&=\left\langle{W(\vec{\xi})X_{r}(b)\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle=\left\langle{X_{r}(b)W(\vec{\xi})\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle{W(\vec{\xi})\Omega},{X_{r}(b)^{\ast}\Omega}\right\rangle=\left\langle{W(\vec{\xi})\Omega},{X(b)^{\ast}\Omega}\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle{X(b)W(\vec{\xi})\Omega},{\Omega}\right\rangle.\qed\end{split}

Theorem 36
.

Suppose the operators
X(u):u\in\mathcal{B}
are bounded. Denote

\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)=W^{\ast}(X(u):u\in\mathcal{B})=W^{\ast}(X(u):u\in\mathcal{B}^{sa}).

Consider the conditions

(21)

\Lambda(v^{\ast}\otimes u^{\ast})^{\ast}=\Lambda(u\otimes v),

(22)

u\gamma[yv]-\gamma[uy]v=\Lambda(u\otimes\Lambda(y\otimes v))-\Lambda(\Lambda(u\otimes y)\otimes v),

(23)

\Lambda(u\otimes y\gamma[zv])-\Lambda(u\otimes y)\gamma[zv]=\Lambda(\gamma[uy]z\otimes v)-\gamma[uy]\Lambda(z\otimes v)

and

(24)

\phi[\gamma[u]v]=\phi[u\gamma[v]].

(a)

Each
X_{r}(u)
commutes with
\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
if and only if conditions (
21
), (
22
), (
23
), and (
24
) hold. Note that if
\gamma
is scalar-valued, the third condition is true automatically.

(b)

If the vacuum state is tracial on
\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
, then conditions (
21
), (
22
), and (
23
) hold, and
\phi
is tracial.

(c)

If
\phi
is tracial and each
X_{r}(u)
commutes with
\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
, then the vacuum state is tracial on
\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
.

Under the assumptions in (c), all the conclusions in Proposition
35
hold, the map
S
extends to an anti-linear isometry on
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\phi}(\mathcal{B})
, and the map
A\mapsto SA^{\ast}S
implements the canonical anti-isomorphism between
\Gamma_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
and its commutant.

Proof.

For part (a), note that since we are working on a Fock space with depth two action, to show that
X(u)X_{r}(v)=X_{r}(v)X(u)
it suffices to consider their actions on tensors of length
0
,
1
, and
2
. A calculation shows that this is equivalent to conditions (
21
)-(
24
) together with the previously assumed (
2
).

For part (b), if the vacuum state is tracial, the joint free cumulants are cyclically symmetric. Using Proposition
9
for cumulants of order up to
5
, we obtain the conditions (
21
)-(
23
) and traciality of
\phi
.

Part (c), and the rest of the statements, follow from Proposition
35
and general theory.
∎

7.
Examples

In this section we show how the motivating examples, and their generalizations, fit into the general setting of this paper.

Example 37
.

Let

\mathcal{B}=L^{\infty}([0,1]),\quad\phi[f]=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,dx,

and
\eta
positive a.e. and
\lambda
a real-valued function. Define

\gamma[f](x)=\eta(x)f(x),\quad\Lambda(b\otimes f)(x)=\lambda(x)b(x)f(x).

Then the construction is closely related to the setting in
[
BL09
,
BL11
]
(see also
[
BLR15
]
).

More generally, for an algebra
\mathcal{B}
, let
\eta,\lambda
be central elements in
\mathcal{B}
such that
\eta+1
is positive and
\lambda
self-adjoint. Then we may take

\gamma[f]=\eta f,\quad\Lambda(b\otimes f)=\lambda bf.

If
\eta+1
is invertible, the inner product is positive definite. The operators are typically unbounded. The vacuum state is always tracial.

The free cumulants are given by an explicit formula

R[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]=\sum_{\pi\in\cNC}\phi[\eta^{\left|\pi\right|-1}\lambda^{n-2\left|\pi\right|}f_{1}f_{2}\ldots f_{n}].

In particular, in this case
R^{\prime}[(X(f_{2}),\ldots,X(f_{n-1})]
may be identified with an element of
\mathcal{B}
, and their generating function satisfies a quadratic relation

(25)

R^{\prime}(f)=1+\lambda R^{\prime}(f)f+\eta R^{\prime}(f)fR^{\prime}(f).

In the case where
\mathcal{B}=L^{\infty}([0,1])
, we may take
R^{\prime}(f)
to be the operator of pointwise multiplication by a function
R^{\prime}(f)(x)
which satisfies

R^{\prime}(f)(x)=1+R^{\prime}(f)(x)\lambda(x)f(x)+R^{\prime}(f)(x)^{2}\eta(x)f(x),

and so is a solution of a quadratic equation for each
x
. The free cumulant generating function can then be found via

R(f)=\int f(x)^{2}R^{\prime}(f)(x)\,dx.

Finally, if
fg=gf=f^{\ast}g=fg^{\ast}=0
, then
X(f)
and
X(g)
are free, and if
f
is self-adjoint with
\eta f=\lambda f=0
, then
X(f)
is semicircular. See also the end of the section for related results.

Example 38
.

Let

\mathcal{B}=L^{\infty}([0,1]),\quad\phi[f]=\int_{0}^{1}f(x)\,dx,

and

w(x,y)=\begin{cases}p&\text{if }0<x<y,\\
q&\text{if }0<y<x,\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
p,q\geq 0
. Define

\gamma[f](x)=\int w(x,y)f(y)\,dy

and

\Lambda(b\otimes f)(x)=f(x)\int w(x,y)b(y)\,dy+f(y)b(y).

We obtain the construction from
[
LS08
]
.

The specific form of a function
w
above allowed for explicit combinatorial calculations, but
\gamma
can be defined by the same formula as long as
(w+1)
is non-negative a.e., and it is natural to take

\Lambda(b\otimes f)(x)=\int\lambda(x,y,z)b(y)f(z)\,dy\,dz

or

\Lambda(b)(x)=\int b(y)\lambda(x,dy).

In the first case, necessary conditions for the vacuum state to be tracial imply that
\lambda(x,y,z)
is conjugate-symmetric in its arguments, and
w(x,y)
is symmetric in its arguments. These conditions are not sufficient, and typically the vacuum state is not tracial.

In the second case, we may identify
R^{\prime}(f)
with a function satisfying

R^{\prime}(f)(x)=\frac{1}{1-\int\lambda(x,y)f(y)\,dy-\int w(x,y)R^{\prime}(f)(y)f(y)^{2}\,dy}.

One can generalize this construction further. Instead of the map
\gamma
, it suffices to use a bilinear map
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\gamma}:\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
which is star-linear in each argument and such that
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\gamma}+\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\phi}
is positive semi-definite. Let
w\in\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B}
satisfy
w+(1\otimes 1)\geq 0
, and define

\left\langle{f},{g}\right\rangle_{\gamma}=(\phi\otimes I)[(f\otimes 1)w(g\otimes 1)].

Then the corresponding inner product on the Fock space is positive semi-definite. If
w+1\otimes 1
is invertible, the inner product is positive definite.

Note that

\left\langle{\vec{\zeta}},{\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}=\left\langle{\vec{\zeta}},{K\vec{\xi}}\right\rangle,

where
K
is the multiplication operator by

[(1+w)\otimes 1^{\otimes(n-2)}]\cdot[1\otimes(1+w)\otimes 1^{\otimes(n-3)}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[1^{\otimes(n-2)}\otimes(1+w)].

In the commutative case we may identify

K(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots s_{n})=(1+w(s_{1},s_{2}))\ldots(1+w(s_{n-1},s_{n})).

So Lemma
27
applies. Moreover,
a^{-}(b)=\ell^{\ast}(b)[(1+w)\otimes 1^{\otimes(n-2)}]
, where
\ell^{\ast}
is the free annihilation operator. It follows that for
w\in\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\min}\mathcal{B}
and
\left\|{\Lambda(b\otimes b_{1})}\right\|_{\phi}\leq\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|\ \left\|{b}\right\|\ \left\|{b_{1}}\right\|_{\phi}
,

\left\|{a^{-}(b)}\right\|_{\gamma,\phi}=\left\|{a^{+}(b)}\right\|_{\gamma,\phi}\leq\left\|{b}\right\|_{\phi}\sqrt{\left\|{w}\right\|+1},\quad\left\|{a^{0}(b)}\right\|_{\gamma,\phi}\leq\left\|{\Lambda}\right\|\ \left\|{b}\right\|.

In particular, the operators extend to
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\phi}(\mathcal{B})
.

Example 39
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
with component-wise multiplication and the standard basis
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{d}
,

\phi[e_{j}]=1,\quad\gamma[e_{j}]=\sum_{i=1}^{d}C_{ij}e_{i},\quad\Lambda(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{d}B_{ij}^{k}e_{k},

with
C_{ij}\geq-1
and certain relations between the coefficients. This is the setting of
[
Ans07
]
(possibly with slightly different notation). Embedding
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into
L^{\infty}[0,1]
using functions constant on cubes, relates this example to the preceding one.

If all
C_{ij}>-1
, the inner product is positive definite. The operators are clearly bounded, and so extend to
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\phi}(\mathcal{B})
. Particular parameters for which the vacuum state is tracial were given in
[
Ans07
]
.

Example 40
.

Choose
\mathcal{B}
,
\phi
,
\Lambda
to be general, but let
\gamma=\psi-\phi
be scalar-valued, where
\psi
is positive semi-definite. Then the inner product simplifies to

\left\langle{f_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes f_{n}},{g_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes g_{k}}\right\rangle_{\gamma,\phi}=\delta_{n=k}\phi[g_{n}^{\ast}f_{n}]\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\psi[g_{i}^{\ast}f_{i}],

so that

\mathcal{F}_{\gamma,\phi}(\mathcal{B})\simeq\mathbb{C}\Omega\oplus\left(L^{2}(\mathcal{B},\phi)\otimes\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathcal{B},\psi))\right)

with the standard inner product. A necessary condition for the vacuum state to be tracial is that
\psi
is a multiple of
\phi
. For
\psi\neq\phi
and
\Lambda=0
, the vacuum state is not tracial. In
[
AM22
]
, we investigate a choice of
\Lambda
which does lead to a tracial vacuum state.

Example 41
.

Let
\gamma=0
and
\Lambda(f\otimes g)=fg
. For any
(\mathcal{B},\phi)
, the corresponding algebra of operators on the Fock space is the free (compound) Poisson algebra, a particular case of the constructions in the Appendix of
[
Ans04
]
(for
q=0
) or Proposition 23 in
[
Ans20
]
(for the scalar case). See also
[
GSS92
]
. In particular, for
f\in\mathcal{B}^{sa}
,
X(f)
has a centered free compound Poisson distribution, and a centered free Poisson distribution if
f
is a projection. Moreover, if
f\cdot g=g\cdot f=0
then
X(f)
and
X(g)
are free.

We finish the paper with two structure results under the assumption that the vacuum state is tracial.

Proposition 42
.

Assume that
\mathcal{B}
is unital, and the vacuum state is tracial. Suppose that
\Lambda(u\otimes v)=\Lambda(u)v
. Then
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{B},\phi)
is as in Example
37
, with
\Lambda(u)=\lambda u
for some central
\lambda
, and
\gamma[u]=\eta u
for some central
u
.

Proof.

If
\Lambda(u\otimes v)=\Lambda(u)v
, then conditions (
2
), (
21
), and (
22
) imply that
u\Lambda(v)=\Lambda(u)v
and
u\gamma[yv]=\gamma[uy]v
. Therefore
\Lambda(u)=\lambda u=u\lambda
for
\lambda=\Lambda(1)
, and
\gamma[u]=\eta u=u\eta
for
\eta=\gamma[1]
.
∎

Theorem 43
.

Assume that
\mathcal{B}
is unital, and the vacuum state is tracial. Suppose that
\gamma[f]=\eta f
for
\eta
central, and for some
M
, and all
f,g\in\mathcal{B}
,

(26)

\left\langle{\Lambda(f\otimes g)},{\Lambda(f\otimes g)}\right\rangle\leq M\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}\left\langle{g},{g}\right\rangle.

Then we have an orthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{B},\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\phi})=\mathcal{N}\oplus\mathcal{N}^{\perp}=\mathcal{Z}\oplus\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathcal{N}^{\perp},

such that

•

\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{N}^{\perp},\phi)
is as in Example
37
, with
\Lambda(f\otimes g)=\lambda fg
for some central
\lambda
.

•

\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{Z},\phi)
is generated by free semicircular elements.

•

\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{P},\phi)
is a free Poisson algebra as in Example
41
, where the multiplication on
\mathcal{P}
is given by
\Lambda
(rather than inherited from
\mathcal{B}
).

Moreover, the subalgebras
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{N}^{\perp},\phi)
,
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{Z},\phi)
, and
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{P},\phi)
are free.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}
the inner product space
\mathcal{B}
with the involution and the inner product
\left\langle{f},{g}\right\rangle=\phi[g^{\ast}f]
satisfying the relation

\left\langle{f},{g}\right\rangle=\left\langle{g^{\ast}},{f^{\ast}}\right\rangle.

Denote

(27)

f\cdot g=\Lambda(f\otimes g).

The notation is meant to suggest that we consider the binary operation
\Lambda
as a multiplication on
\mathcal{H}
(different from a multiplication it may have inherited from
\mathcal{B}
). Indeed, equations (
21
), (
22
), and the first condition in (
2
) say that

(f\cdot g)^{\ast}=g^{\ast}\cdot f^{\ast},\quad(f\cdot g)\cdot h=f\cdot(g\cdot h),\quad\left\langle{f\cdot g},{h}\right\rangle=\left\langle{g},{f^{\ast}\cdot h}\right\rangle.

That is,
(\mathcal{H},\cdot,\ast)
is an associative star-algebra (linearity/distributivity is immediate), which is represented on
\mathcal{H}
by left multiplication operators.

Second condition in equation (
2
) reads

(28)

\eta g^{\ast}\Lambda(b\otimes f)=\eta\Lambda(b^{\ast}\otimes g)^{\ast}f=\eta\Lambda(g^{\ast}\otimes b)f.

Therefore
\eta\Lambda(b\otimes f)=\eta\lambda bf
, where
\lambda=\Lambda(1\otimes 1)
.

Let

\mathcal{N}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{B}:\eta f=0\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{N}
is a self-adjoint subspace and an ideal. Restricted to
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
, multiplication by
\eta
is injective. Since by (
21
),
\eta\lambda bg=\eta bg\lambda^{\ast}
, it follows that
\lambda
, restricted to
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
, is self-adjoint and central.
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
is self-adjoint and a left ideal with respect to
\cdot
, so
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{N}^{\perp},\phi)
is a star-subalgebra.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
, with at least one element from
\mathcal{N}
. It follows from equation (
28
) that
\mathcal{N}
is also an ideal with respect to
\cdot
. Combined with the definition of
\mathcal{N}
, this implies that in the expansion for the joint free cumulant in Proposition
9
, elements of
\mathcal{N}
can only appear in the (unique) outer block of
\pi
. Since each inner block is a multiple of
\eta
, it follows that in fact,
\pi
has a single block so that

(29)

R[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]=\left\langle{f_{1}},{f_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{n}}\right\rangle.

Since the vacuum state is tracial, the free cumulants are cyclically symmetric. So if

f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\perp}

with at least one element from each of
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
, we may assume that
f_{1}\in\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
and
f_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{n}\in\mathcal{N}
. Then their inner product is zero, which implies freeness by (
29
).

Next, denote

\mathcal{Z}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{N}:\forall g\in\mathcal{N},g\cdot f=0\right\}

and

\mathcal{P}=\overline{\Span(\left\{f\cdot g:f,g\in\mathcal{N}\right\})}.

Clearly both of them are subspaces (and in fact ideals with respect to
\cdot
). Also, using the properties of
\cdot
at the beginning of the proof, one sees that
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{P}
are orthogonal complements of each other, and
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{Z}\oplus\mathcal{P}
.
\mathcal{P}
is clearly self-adjoint, therefore so is
\mathcal{Z}
.

For
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}\cup\mathcal{P}
with at least one of them in
\mathcal{Z}
, using the cyclic property again,

R[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]=\left\langle{f_{1}},{f_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{n}}\right\rangle=\delta_{n=2}\left\langle{f_{1}},{f_{2}}\right\rangle

and is zero unless both
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}
. It follows that for
f\in\mathcal{Z}^{sa}
,
X(f)
are semicircular, with orthogonal
f
corresponding to free
X(f)
, and are also free from
X(g)
with
g\in\mathcal{P}
.

Under the boundedness assumption (
26
),
\mathcal{P}
satisfies all the axioms of a Hilbert algebra (Chapters 5 and 6 of
[
Dix81
]
). Then we can complete
\mathcal{P}
to a von Neumann algebra represented on
\mathcal{N}
(since it acts as zero on
\mathcal{Z}
, it is also represented on
\mathcal{P}
) by left multiplication. Moreover, we have a semi-finite trace
\psi
on this von Neumann algebra which is finite on
\mathcal{P}
and satisfies
\left\langle{f},{g}\right\rangle=\psi[g^{\ast}\cdot f]
. So in this case for self-adjoint elements and
n\geq 2
,

R[X(f_{1}),\ldots,X(f_{n})]=\psi[f_{1}\cdot f_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot f_{n}].

It follows that
\Gamma^{alg}_{\gamma,\Lambda}(\mathcal{P},\phi)
is the free compound Poisson algebra of
(\mathcal{P},\psi)
.
∎

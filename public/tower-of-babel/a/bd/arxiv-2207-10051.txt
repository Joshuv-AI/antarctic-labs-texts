Dot Product Bounds in Galois Rings
David Crosby

Abstract




ABSTRACT

We consider a problem related to the Erdős Unit Distance Conjecture: How often can a single dot product configuration or a multiple dot product configuration occur over a Galoi Ring? We also find a bound on an inverse vector matrix multiplication problem.

KEYWORDS
: Dot Product, Erdős Unit Distance Conjecture, Galois Rings

1
Introduction

In 1946, Paul Erdős asked how many unit distances may occur in the plane with
n
points? Erdős proved that
n
points in
R^{2}
determines
\Omega(n^{1/2})
many unit distances and conjectured that there are
\Omega(n^{c})
for any
c<1
(see
[
7
]
). The best known result is
\Omega(n/\log(n))
by Guth and Katz
[
9
]
in 2015. For
R^{d}
, Erdős proved that
n
points determines
\Theta(n^{2/d})
unit distances and in 2008 Solymosi and Vu proved the bound of
\Omega(n^{2/d-2/(d(d+2))})

[
15
]
.
The idea of the unit distance problem has since been extended to various rings by using a distance like functional. We see this in Covert, Iosevich and Pakianathan’s 2011 work
[
4
]
where they study integers modulo a prime power and also Iosevich and Rudnev’s 2008 work
[
12
]
over finite fields.

A common extension to this question is the single dot product problem: “How often can a specified dot product occur between
n
points in some ambient space?”. This has been studied in
[
10
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
, and more, where different bounds and configurations are studied.

1.1
Focus of the article

This article will focus on various configurations of the dot product problem in the ambient space of Galois rings.
Galois rings are a set of finite rings that contain both all of
F_{p^{l}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{l}}
. They are constructed as

R_{e,k}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{e}}[x]/(f)
where
f
is of degree
k
and irreducible in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
under the mod
p
map.
The configurations that we consider are single dot products, pairs of dot products, and general forest configurations. We further show an application of the forest configuration leads to a bound on the number of solutions to the matrix equation
aA=b
where
b
is a given column and
a
and
A
are our variables.

Galois rings are finding applications in computer networking through coding theory. For more on Galois rings in coding theory see
[
2
, §8.1.3]
.
Another reason to study finite rings is that they provide a test bed for complicated analysis problems as integrals are always convergent. The prototypical example of this is Zeev Dvir’s paper “On the size of Kakeya sets in finite fields”
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, where he proved the Kakeya Conjecture (relates to subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{l}}
that contain a line in every direction) via simple means.
We study forest configurations as they encompass all dot product configurations that contain no cycles.

1.2
Results

Our first result follows in form the proof by Covert, Iosevich, and Pakianathan
[
4
, Theorem 1.3.2]
. There they study the Erdős distance problem over the integers modulo an odd prime to a power.
We define our counting function
\nu
, which counts how many pairs of points give a specified dot product.

Definition 1
.

Let
E\subset R_{e,k}^{d}
. Let
p,e,k,d
be given natural numbers with
p
prime,
e\geq 5
,
d\geq 2
, and
k\geq 1
.
Define

\nu(t)=\left|\{(x,y)\in E\times E:x\cdot y=t\}\right|.

Covert et al. in
[
4
]
show that
\nu(t)=|E|^{2}/p^{l}+R(t)
, where

|R(t)|<l|E|p^{l\left(\frac{d-1}{2}(2-1/l)\right)}<|E|^{2}/{p^{l}}

whenever
|E|>lp^{ld-d/2+1/2}
.

Our first result gives that the number of pairs of points that give a specified dot product must be no more than twice the expected value whenever the size of our point set is large enough.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E\subset R_{e,k}^{d}
. Let
p,e,k,d
be given natural numbers with
p
prime,
e\geq 5
,
d\geq 2
, and
k\geq 1
.
Let
\nu(t)=|\{(x,y)\in E\times E:x\cdot y=t\}|
.
Then

\nu(t)\leq 2|E|/p^{ek}

for any
t\in R_{e,k}
whenever
|E|\geq\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}
.

As
|E|\leq|R^{d}|=p^{dek}
, this result is non-trivial when
d\geq e+1+\frac{\log_{p}(6+3e)}{k}
. Our second results gives that the number of triplets of points
(x,y,z)
that give specified dot products (
x\cdot y=\alpha,\,y\cdot z=\beta
) must be no more than twice the expected value whenever the size of our point set is large enough. Graphically, this is given in Figure
1
.
We define a new counting function to count these triplets of points.

Definition 2
.

Let
d\geq 3,\ e\geq 5,\ k\geq 1
. Let
E\subset(R_{e,k})^{d}
and suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in R_{e,k}
. We define

\Pi_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=\{(x,y,z)\in E^{3}:x\cdot y=\alpha,x\cdot z=\beta\}.

The proof follows and extends the paper “Pairs of Dot Products in Finite Fields and Rings” by David Covert and Steven Senger
[
5
]
, where they consider both
\mathbb{F}_{p^{l}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{l}}
.
Covert and Senger find

\left|\Pi_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\right|=\frac{|E|^{3}}{p^{2l}}(1+o(1))

whenever
|E|>cp^{l\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)}
for
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{p^{l}}^{d}
and
|E|>cp^{dl-d/2+1}
when
E\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p^{l}}^{d}
for some constant
c
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E\subset R_{e,k}^{d}
. Let
d\geq 3,\ e\geq 5,\ k\geq 1
. Let
E\subset(R_{e,k})^{d}
and suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in R_{e,k}
. We have the bound

|\Pi_{\alpha,\beta}(E)|\leq\frac{2|E^{3}|}{p^{2ek}}

whenever

|E|\geq\sqrt{35e/8}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}.

This result is non-trivial when
d\geq e+1+\log_{p}(35e/8)/k
.
Our third result is similar, but for tree configurations.
We extend the results of
[
3
]
by Blevins, Lynch, Senger, and the author from chains over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{l}
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{l}
to forests over Galois rings.
We create a third counting function as follows:

.

Let

. Let
T
be a forest with vertices
V
with
|V|=m
where each vertex represents an element of
E
and edges
\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{F}|=n
where each edge
(e_{i},e_{j})
represents that we have specified
e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\alpha_{ij}
where
\alpha=\{\alpha_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{F}}
is given. An example graph is visualized in Figure

. We define

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3,\ e\geq 5,\ k\geq 1,\ n\geq 3
. Let
E\subset R_{e,k}^{d}
.
Let
T
be a forest with vertices
V
with
|V|=m
where each vertex represents an element of
E
and edges
\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{F}|=n
where each edge
(e_{i},e_{j})
represents that we have specified
e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\alpha_{ij}
where
\alpha=\{\alpha_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{F}}
is given. Then

\Pi_{\alpha}(T)\leq\frac{2|E|^{m}}{p^{nek}}

whenever

|E|\geq\max\left\{2n\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek-dk/2+3ek/2+k/2},\sqrt{14}ep^{dek-dk/2+nek/2+n/2-ek/2+k/2}\right\}.

When
n\geq 4
, the bound on
|E|
reduces to
|E|\geq\sqrt{14}enp^{dek-dk/2+nek/2+n/2-ek/2+3k/2}.
This is nontrivial (with
n\geq 4
) when
d\geq(n-1)e+3+\frac{n+\log_{p}(14en^{2})}{k}
.
An application of Theorem
1.1
gives a bound on how many pairs of row-vectors and matrices there are that multiply to a specified column-vector.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 3,\ e\geq 5,\ k\geq 1,\ n\geq 4
. Let
E\subset R_{e,k}^{d}
.
Let
aA=b
be an equation where
a\in E
, the columns of
A
are in
E
, and
b
is a given row-vector of length
n
. Whenever
|E|>en\sqrt{14}p^{dek-dk/2+nek/2+n/2-ek/2+k}
, the number of solutions

S=\left|\left\{(a,A)\mid a\in E,A\in E^{n},\ aA=b\right\}\right|

has

S\leq\frac{2|E|^{n+1}}{p^{nek}}.

We now compare this bound to the known number for when
e=k=1
and
E=R^{d}_{e,k}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. From Hodges 1966 work
[
16
]
, where he gives an expression for the number of represntations of a bilinear form (
\bmod\ p^{a}
), we are able to find that the number of such solutions
S
is
p^{dn-n}(p^{d}-1)
which is less than twice the expected value of
\frac{2p^{dn+d}}{p^{n}}
.
The proof for Theorem
1.2
works by showing a similarity of structures. So
should there be a bound on the matrix-matrix multiplication problem
AB=C
, then its bound should give a bound on a corresponding dot product configuration with cycles.

1.3
Background

For proofs of the below statements see
[
2
]
,
[

]
, and
[

]
.

Definition 4

(
\chi_{e,k}
)
.

The canonical additive character
\chi_{e,k}:R_{e,k}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}^{\times}
is defined as

\chi_{e,k}(z)=e^{2\pi i\operatorname{Tr}_{e,k}(z)/p^{e}}.

Lemma 5

(Orthogonality)
.

Let
e,k
be given. For any
a\in R
,

\sum_{z\in R}\chi(az)=\begin{cases}|R|,&a=0\\
0,&a\neq 0\end{cases}

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\beta
be a generator of the subgroup of
R_{e,k}^{\times}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}-1}
. Let
T_{e,k}=\{0,1,\beta,\ldots,\beta^{p^{k}-2}\}
. Every
z\in R
has a unique
p
-adic representation

z=z_{0}+pz_{1}+\cdots+p^{e-1}z_{e-1},\quad z_{i}\in T_{e,k}.

Definition 6
.

We will use the notation
[p^{i}]
to mean
(p^{i})\setminus(p^{i+1})
.

Lemma 7
.

Any
s\in[p^{i}]
has the form
s=p^{i}u
where
u
is the uniquely determined unit of the form
u_{1}+pu_{2}+\cdots+p^{e-i-1}u_{e-i-1}
where
u_{i}\in T_{e,k}
.

Corollary 8
.

If
a\in R_{e,k}
is given, then

\sum_{z\in p^{i}R_{e,k}}\chi_{e,k}(az)=\sum_{w\in R_{e-i,k}}\chi_{e-i,k}(\rho_{i}(a)w).

Where
\rho_{i}(a_{0}+pa_{1}+\cdots+p^{e-1}a_{e-1})=a_{0}+pa_{1}+\cdots+p^{e-1-i}a_{e-1-i}
with
a_{0}+pa_{1}+\cdots+p^{e-1}a_{e-1}
being the
p
-adic expansion of
a
.

Proof.

By definition of
\chi_{e,k}
,
\operatorname{Tr}
, and characteristic of a ring, this is immediate. Let
(az)_{0}+\cdots+p^{e-1}(az)_{e-1}
be the
p
-adic expansion of
az
. Note,

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in p^{i}R_{e,k}}\chi_{e,k}(az)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in R_{e,k}}\exp\left(2\pi i\left[p^{i}az+\left(p^{i}(az)_{0}^{p}+\cdots+p^{e-1+i}(az)^{p}_{e-1}\right)+\cdots+\left(p^{i}(az)_{0}^{p^{k}}+\ldots p^{e-1+i}(az)^{p^{k}}_{e-1}\right)\right]/p^{e}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in R_{e,k}}\exp\left(2\pi i\left[az+\left(p^{i}(az)_{0}^{p}+\cdots+p^{e-1-i}(az)^{p}_{e-1}\right)+\cdots+\left((az)_{0}^{p^{k}}+\cdots+p^{e-1}(az)^{p^{k}}_{e-1}\right)\right]/p^{e-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in R_{e-i,k}}\chi_{e-i,k}(\rho_{i}(a)w).

∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
e,k
be given and
R=R_{e,k}
. If
n
is a given natural number and
0<n<e
, then

\sum_{z\in R^{\times}}\chi(p^{n}z)\leq 0.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in R^{\times}}\chi(p^{n}z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in R}\chi(p^{n}z)-\sum_{z\in(p)}\chi(p^{n}z)

\displaystyle=I+II.

By orthogonality (Lemma
5
),
I=0
.
So
\sum_{z\in R^{\times}}\chi(p^{n}z)=-\sum_{z\in(p)}\chi(p^{n}z)
. Thus, when
n+1=e
,
\sum_{z\in R^{\times}}\chi(p^{n}z)=-|(p)|=-|pR_{e,k}|
.
In the case that
n+1\neq e
, we apply Corollary
8
,

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in R^{\times}}\chi(p^{n}z)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{pz\in pR_{e,k}}\chi(p^{n+1}z)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{w\in R_{e-1,k}}\chi_{e-1,k}(p^{n}w).

By orthogonality in
z
, this is
0
(as
n\neq e-1
). Thus,
\sum_{z\in R^{\times}}\chi(p^{n}z)\leq 0
when
n<e
. Further,

\sum_{z\in R^{\times}}\chi(p^{n}z)=\begin{cases}-|pR_{e,k}|&,\ n+1=e\\
0&,\ n+1<e\end{cases}

∎

For more properties of character sums over Galois rings, see F. Shuqin and H. Wenbao
[
14
]
.

2
Proof of Single Dot Product Result

Theorem
1.1
is an application of the more technical Theorem
2.1
whose proof we delay until later.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
e,k
be given natural numbers greater than 0. Let
E\subset R_{e,k}^{d}
. Let
\nu(t)=|\{(x,y)\in E\times E:x\cdot y=t\}|
. For any
t\in R
,

\nu(t)<|E|^{2}/p^{ek}+D(t).

Further, the discrepancy
D(t)
, has
D(t)<|E|^{2}/p^{ek}
whenever

|E|\geq p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}.

We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Label

F=p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}.

(1)

By Theorem
2.1
, we know that

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\geq F.

Examining
F
, we see

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}

\displaystyle=p^{ek+dek/2}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}.

So,

\displaystyle\frac{F}{p^{ek}p^{dek/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}p^{dik/2-ik/2}\sqrt{2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}}.

For getting a cleaner bound for Theorem
2.1
, we now apply Cauchy–Schwarz, which loosens our bound to

\displaystyle\frac{F}{p^{ek}p^{dek/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}(p^{dik/2-ik/2})^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}p^{dik-ik}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}}.

This is a sum of geometric series or constants. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{F}{p^{ek}p^{dek/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{1-p^{dek-ek}}{1-p^{dk-k}}\left(2p^{-k}\frac{1-p^{-ek}}{1-p^{-k}}+e+ep^{-ek}\right)}.

The above fraction in parenthesis is maximized when
e
grows large,
k=1
, and
p=2
, giving
\frac{1-p^{-ek}}{1-p^{-k}}\leq 2
.
Ergo,

\displaystyle\frac{F}{p^{ek}p^{dek/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{1-p^{dek-ek}}{1-p^{dk-k}}\left(4p^{-k}+e+ep^{-ek}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{1-p^{dk-k}}-\frac{p^{dek-ek}}{1-p^{dk-k}}\right)\left(4p^{-k}+e+ep^{-ek}\right)}.

Let
C=1/(1-p^{dk-k})
. We may bound
-\frac{p^{dek-ek}}{1-p^{dk-k}}
by
2p^{dek-ek-dk+k}
as
\frac{-1}{1-B}\leq\frac{2}{B}
for any
B\geq 2
. This gives,

\displaystyle\frac{F}{p^{ek}p^{dek/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(C+2p^{dek-ek-dk+k})\left(4p^{-k}+e+ep^{-ek}\right)}.

Since
e\geq 5
,
k\geq 1
, and
p\geq 2
, we have
4p^{-k}+e+ep^{-ek}\leq 69/32+e
. So,

\displaystyle\frac{F}{p^{ek}p^{dek/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(C+2p^{dek-ek-dk+k})\left(69/32+e\right)}.

As
C=1/(1-p^{dk-k})
,
p^{dk-k}\geq 2
,
|C|\leq 1
, and
p^{dek-ek-dk+k}\geq 2
, we have
C+2p^{dek-ek-dk+k}\leq(|C|/2+2)p^{dek-ek-dk+k}
. So,

\displaystyle\frac{F}{p^{ek}p^{dek/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(1/2+2)p^{dek-ek-dk+k}\left(69/32+e\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek/2-dk/2-ek/2+k/2}.

Thus

F\leq\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}

(2)

and so
\nu(t)\leq 2|E|/p^{ek}
whenever

|E|>\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}.

∎

As
|E|\leq|R^{d}|=p^{dek}
, this result is non-trivial when

\displaystyle p^{dek}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2},

which simplifies to

\displaystyle p^{dk/2}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{6+3e}p^{ek/2+k/2}

\displaystyle dk

\displaystyle\geq ek+k+\log_{p}(6+3e).

So whenever
d\geq e+1+\frac{\log_{p}(6+3e)}{k}
, then we may take a proper subset of
R^{d}
for Theorem
1.1
.
We now get to the heart of this paper; the technical result for the number of pairs of points in a Galois ring that have a specified dot product. The proof of Theorem
2.1
is given below.

Proof.

Recall that by Lemma
5
,
\sum_{r\in R}\chi(ra)=0
if
a\neq 0
and
p^{ek}
if
a=0
. Since we are after
x\cdot y=t
, we examine
\sum_{s\in R}\sum_{x,y\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y-t))
. This sum gives out a
p^{ek}
precisely when
x\cdot y=t
and 0 when
x\cdot y\neq t
. Thus, by multiplying this sum by
p^{-ek}
we get the number of
x,y\in E
such that
x\cdot y=t
. We rewrite
\nu(t)
(Definition
1
) as below and split
\nu(t)
into the following parts

\displaystyle\nu(t)

\displaystyle=p^{-ek}\sum_{s\in R}\sum_{x,y\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y-t))

\displaystyle=\nu_{0}(t)+\cdots+\nu_{e}(t),

where

\nu_{i}(t)=p^{-ek}\sum_{s\in[p^{i}]}\sum_{x,y\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y))\chi(-st).

For
\nu_{e}(t)
, we have

\nu_{e}(t)=p^{-ek}\sum_{x,y\in E}\chi(0(x\cdot y))\chi(-0t)=|E|^{2}/p^{ek}.

The discrepancy is

D(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\nu_{i}(t)=p^{-ek}\sum_{s\in R\setminus\{0\}}\sum_{x,y\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y-t)).

(3)

We now examine
\nu_{i}(t)
for some
i\neq e
. Recall,

\displaystyle\nu_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=p^{-ek}\sum_{s\in[p^{i}]}\sum_{x,y\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y))\chi(-st).

We will prepare to use Cauchy-Schwarz by recognizing that

\displaystyle\nu_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=p^{-ek}\left(\sum_{x\in E}\left(1\right)\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{s\in[p^{i}]}\chi(s(x\cdot y))\chi(-st)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in E}\left(1\right)\sum_{y\in E}p^{-ek}\sum_{s\in[p^{i}]}\chi(s(x\cdot y))\chi(-st)\right).

By applying Cauchy-Schwarz with
a_{x}=1
and
b_{x}=\sum_{y\in E}p^{-ek}\sum_{s\in[p^{i}]}\chi(s(x\cdot y))\chi(-st)
,

\displaystyle|\nu_{i}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{-2ek}\left(\sum_{x\in E}1\bar{1}\right)\left(\sum_{x\in E}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{s\in[p^{i}]}\chi(s(x\cdot y))\chi(-st)\overline{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in E}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in[p^{i}]}\chi(s^{\prime}(x\cdot y^{\prime}))\chi(-s^{\prime}t)}\right).

Because
z\bar{z}\geq 0
for any
z\in\mathbf{C}
, we may dominate the second sum of
x\in E
by
x\in R^{d}
, obtaining,

\displaystyle|\nu_{i}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{-2ek}|E|\sum_{x\in R^{d}}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}\in E}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}\in[p^{i}]}\chi(s(x\cdot y-t))\chi(-s^{\prime}(x\cdot y^{\prime}-t)).

By Lemma
7
,
s\in[p^{i}]
is the same as
p^{i}u\in[p^{i}]
where
u\in T_{e,k}
.
Thus as
s,s^{\prime}\in[p^{i}]
, we let
s=p^{i}u
and
s^{\prime}=p^{i}v
. Using
\overline{0}
to be the zero element of
R_{e,k}^{d}
this gives,

|\nu_{i}(t)|^{2}\leq p^{-2ek}|E|\sum_{x\in R^{d}}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}}\sum_{p^{i}u,p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]}\chi(p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})\cdot x)\chi(p^{i}t(u-v))

(4)

\displaystyle=p^{-2ek}|E|\sum_{y\in E}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}u,p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]\\
p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})=\overline{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{x\in R^{d}}\chi(p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})\cdot x)\chi(p^{i}t(u-v))\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}u,p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]\\
p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})\neq\overline{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{x\in R^{d}}\chi(p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})\cdot x)\chi(p^{i}t(u-v))\right).

By orthogonality in
x
, whenever
p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})=\bar{0}
we get a factor of
p^{ek}
for each component of
x
. When
p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})\neq 0
, we get a factor of zero. Thus,

\displaystyle|\nu_{i}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y^{\prime}\in E\\
p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
p^{i}u,p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}t(v-u)).

We now split this sum into

\displaystyle|\nu_{i}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq I+II,

where
I
has

has
u\neq v
.

Lemma 10
.

For
I
, we have

|I|\leq|E|^{2}p^{dek+ikd-ek-ik}.

Proof.

For
I
, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y^{\prime}\in E\\
p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
p^{i}u=p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}t(v-u)),

which as
u=v
,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y^{\prime}\in E\\
p^{i}u(y-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
p^{i}u\in[p^{i}]\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}t(0)).

Let
E(y)=1
when
y\in E
and
E(y)=0
when
y\not\in E
. As
u
is a unit,
p^{i}u(y-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}
is the same as
p^{i}(y-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}
. As
\chi(p^{i}t0)=1
, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\left|[p^{i}]\right|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y^{\prime}\in R^{d}\\
p^{i}(y-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\end{subarray}}E(y)E(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\left(p^{(e-i)k}-p^{(e-i-1)k}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y^{\prime}\in R^{d}\\
p^{i}(y-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\end{subarray}}E(y)E(y^{\prime}).

Note

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y^{\prime}\in R^{d}\\
p^{i}(y-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\end{subarray}}E(y)E(y^{\prime})

is the count of
(y,y^{\prime})\in E^{2}

. This we may bound above by taking an arbitrary

|p^{e-i}R|=p^{ik}

. This gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y,y^{\prime}\in R^{d}\\

p^{i}(y-y^{\prime})=\bar{0}\end{subarray}}1\leq|E|p^{ikd}.

Thus,

|I|\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek}\left(p^{(e-i)k}-p^{(e-i-1)k}\right)|E|p^{ikd}\leq|E|^{2}p^{dek+ikd-ek-ik}.

∎

For
II
we have
v\neq u
. So,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}u,p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]\\
p^{i}(uy-vy^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}t(v-u)).

Let

and

. This gives us,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}u,p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]\\
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
u\neq v\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}tb(1-a))

\displaystyle=II_{u}+II_{n},

where
II_{u}
has

being a unit and
II_{n}
has
1-a
being a non-unit.

Lemma 11
.

For
II_{u}
we have,

|II_{u}|\leq|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-ik}.

Proof.

For
II_{u}
, we have that as
1-a
is a unit,

\displaystyle II_{u}

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}u,p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]\\
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
u\neq v\\
1-a\in R_{e-i,k}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}tb(1-a)).

As
b=v
and
p^{i}v\in[p^{i}]
,

by Lemma

. So, summing over

.
As
a=u/v
and
1-a\neq 0
, it must be that
u\neq v
(allowing us to drop it from the restriction on the summation). This gives,

\displaystyle|II_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ b\in R^{\times}_{e-i,k}\\
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
1-a\in R_{e-i,k}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\chi_{e-i,k}(tb(1-a)).

Since
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\bar{0}
, and
b
is a unit, we must have
ay-y^{\prime}\in(p^{e-i})^{d}
, so

\displaystyle|II_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ b\in R^{\times}_{e-i,k}\\
ay-y^{\prime}\in(p^{e-i})^{d}\\
1-a\in R_{e-i,k}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\chi_{e-i,k}(tb(1-a)).

As
a
is a unit, and
ay-y^{\prime}\in(p^{e-i})^{d}
, it must be that for each choice of

, that
y^{\prime}
is in the same coset of
(p^{e-i})^{d}
.
Being the case that
\chi_{e-i,k}(tb(1-a))
does not depend on
y
nor
y^{\prime}
,
we can bound
ay-y^{\prime}\in(p^{e-i})^{d}
by summing
y\in E
and summing
y^{\prime}\in-ay+(p^{e-i})^{d}
. This gives
|(p^{e-i})^{d}|=p^{idk}
choices for
y^{\prime}
. Pulling out this factor of

, we get

\displaystyle|II_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek+idk}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1-a\in R_{e-i,k}^{\times}\end{subarray}}\sum_{b\in R^{\times}_{e-i,k}}\chi_{e-i,k}(tb(1-a)).

As
b
sums over

, by Lemma
9
, we have that
II_{u}=0
when

.
When

, we have, by Lemma

,

\displaystyle|II_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq\left||E|p^{dek-2ek+idk}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1-a\in R_{e-i,k}^{\times}\end{subarray}}-\left|pR_{e-i,k}\right|\right|,

which then simplifies to

\displaystyle|II_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|E\right|p^{dek-2ek+idk}\left|E\right|\left|R^{\times}_{e-i,k}\right|\left|pR_{e-i,k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}.

In the final case that

, we have

, and so

\displaystyle|II_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek+idk}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1-a\in R_{e-i,k}^{\times}\end{subarray}}|R_{e-i,k}^{\times}|.

\displaystyle|II_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|E\right|p^{dek-2ek+idk}\left|E\right|\left|R^{\times}_{e-i,k}\right||R_{e-i,k}^{\times}|

|II_{u}|\leq|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-ik}.

∎

Lemma 12
.

For
II_{n}
, we have

|II_{n}|\leq|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}.

Proof.

Recall
v=b
and
a=u/v
. For
II_{n}
, we have
1-a
being a non-unit, hence
p^{i}(1-a)\in(p^{i+1})
. This requirement makes

satisfied. Thus,

\displaystyle II_{n}

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}v,p^{i}u\in[p^{i}]\\
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
p^{i}(1-a)\in(p^{i+1})\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}tb(1-a)).

As summing over
p^{i}u\in[p^{i}]
is the same as summing over
p^{i}u/v\in[p^{i}]
, we have,

\displaystyle II_{n}

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}b,p^{i}a\in[p^{i}]\\
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\\
p^{i}(1-a)\in(p^{i+1})\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}tb(1-a)).

Recall
p^{i}a\in[p^{i}]
means that

for some unit
u_{0}
and

by Lemma

. As

is not a unit,

for some

restricts

to the subset

. Thus,

\displaystyle II_{n}

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}b\in[p^{i}],\ p^{i}a\in p^{i}+(p^{i+1})\\
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\end{subarray}}\chi(p^{i}tb(1-a)).

By letting

, we have

\displaystyle II_{n}

\displaystyle=|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in E,\ p^{i}b\in[p^{i}],\ c\in(p^{i+1})\\
p^{i}b(ay-y^{\prime})=\overline{0}\end{subarray}}\chi(tbc).

As

means

and no where else do we depend on
y^{\prime}
, we bound this sum by summing

. This gives,

\displaystyle|II_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y^{\prime}\in(p^{e-i})^{d},\ p^{i}b\in[p^{i}],\ c\in(p^{i+1})\end{subarray}}\chi(tbc).

Which, by Corollary
8
(supressing
\rho_{i}
) and pulling out

, is

\displaystyle|II_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek}\left|(p^{e-i})^{d}\right|\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{i}b\in[p^{i}]\end{subarray}}\sum_{c\in R_{e-i-1,k}}\chi(tbc).

By orthogonality in

, Lemma
5
, we have
II_{n}=0
when

. Otherwise,

\displaystyle|II_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|p^{dek-2ek}\left|(p^{e-i})^{d}\right|\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{i}b\in[p^{i}]\end{subarray}}\sum_{c\in R_{e-i-1,k}}1.

We simplify this to

\displaystyle|II_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}.

∎

Recall,

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq|II_{u}|+|II_{n}|,

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq\left(|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-ik}\right)+|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}

\displaystyle\leq 2|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-ik}.

and by our bounds on

),

|\nu_{i}(t)|^{2}\leq 2|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-ik}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+ikd-ek-ik}.

(5)

Thus the discrepancy,

, has

\displaystyle D(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{2|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-2ik-k}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-ik}+|E|^{2}p^{dek+ikd-ek-ik}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{|E|^{2}p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}.

So as we want

\displaystyle\geq|E|\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle\geq p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}.

.
∎

3.1
Proof of Pairs of Dot Products Result

Theorem
1.2
relies on the more technical Theorem
3.1
whose statement is below and whose proof is given later in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
e,k\geq 1
. Let
d\geq 3
,
E\subset(R_{e,k})^{d}
and suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in R_{e,k}
. We have the bound

|\Pi_{\alpha,\beta}(E)|=\frac{|E^{3}|}{p^{2ek}}(1+o(1))

whenever

|E|\geq\max\left(p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)},\sqrt{2G}\right)

where

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=p^{dek+2ek}e\left(2p^{-k}\frac{p^{dek-2ek}-1}{p^{dk-2k}-1}+(1+p^{-ek})\frac{p^{dek-ek}-1}{p^{dk-k}-1}\right).

We now begin the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

Let
e\geq 5,d\geq 3
, and
k\geq 1
. By
Theorem
3.1

we know that

|\Pi_{\alpha,\beta}(E)|=\frac{|E^{3}|}{p^{2ek}}(1+o(1))

is true whenever

|E|\geq\max\left(p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)},\sqrt{2G}\right)

where

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=p^{dek+2ek}e\left(2p^{-k}\frac{p^{dek-2ek}-1}{p^{dk-2k}-1}+(1+p^{-ek})\frac{p^{dek-ek}-1}{p^{dk-k}-1}\right).

By
Equations
1
and
2
, we know

p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}\leq\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}

whenever
e\geq 5,\ d\geq 2
, and
k\geq 1
, which is given. We focus now on getting a nice upper bound on
G
. Recall

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=p^{dek+2ek}e\left(2p^{-k}\frac{p^{dek-2ek}-1}{p^{dk-2k}-1}+(1+p^{-ek})\frac{p^{dek-ek}-1}{p^{dk-k}-1}\right).

As
1/(B-1)\leq 2/B
for any
B\geq 2
,

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{dek+2ek}e\left(2p^{-k}\frac{p^{dek-2ek}-1}{p^{dk-2k}}+(1+p^{-ek})\frac{p^{dek-ek}-1}{p^{dk-k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{2dek+ek-dk+k}e\left(3p^{-ek}+1\right).

Recall,
e\geq 5,p\geq 2,k\geq 1
, so the part in parentheses is maximized when
e=5,\ p=2,
and
k=1
. Thus,

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{2dek+ek-dk+k}e\left(3(2^{-5})+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq p^{2dek+ek-dk+k}e(35/16).

Thus, this lemma is true whenever

|E|\geq\max\left(\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2},\sqrt{2(35/16)ep^{2dek+ek-dk+k}}\right).

As
\sqrt{35e/8}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}\geq\sqrt{6+3e}p^{dek+ek/2-dk/2+k/2}
since

, we have that

|E|\geq\sqrt{35e/8}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}.

∎

This is non-trivial when
|E|<p^{dek}
, giving

p^{dek}\geq\sqrt{35e/8}p^{dek-dk/2+ek/2+k/2}.

Hence, this result is non-trivial when

.
We now begin the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof.

For the sake of brevity, we will use
R
to stand for
R_{e,k}
. Let
\chi
denote the canonical additive character of

(Definition
4
).
Recall that,

\displaystyle|\Pi_{\alpha,\beta}(E)|

\displaystyle=|\{(x,y,z)\in E\times E\times E:x\cdot y=\alpha,x\cdot z=\beta\}|.

As we want
x\cdot y=\alpha
and
x\cdot z=\beta
, the character sum becomes

\displaystyle|\Pi_{\alpha,\beta}(E)|

\displaystyle=p^{-2ek}\sum_{s,t\in R}\sum_{x,y,z\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y-\alpha))\chi(t(x\cdot z-\beta))

\displaystyle=I+II+III,

where
I
has
s=t=0
,
II
has
s
or
t
equal to zero but not both, and

has
s\neq 0
and

.

For

, we see

I=p^{-2ek}\sum_{s=t=0}\sum_{x,y,z\in E}\chi(-0\alpha)\chi(0\beta)\chi(x\cdot(0y-0z))=p^{-2ek}|E|^{3}.

(7)

With

and

, we will use Theorem

from Section

, which requires that

|E|\geq p^{ek}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\sqrt{p^{dek+dik-ik}\left(2p^{-ik-k}+1+p^{-ek}\right)}

For

, as either

or

but not both, we may split the sum as two sums where either

or

,

(8)

Notice that this is the sum of two
\nu(t)
from Theorem

. Thus by Theorem

, we have

.

Considering

, we have as

,

|III|\leq\left|p^{-2ek}\sum_{s,t\in R_{e,k}\setminus\{0\}}\sum_{x,y,z\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y-\alpha))\chi(t(\beta-x\cdot z))\right|

(9)

which we then prepare for Cauchy-Schwarz. So,

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq\left|p^{-2ek}\sum_{x\in E}\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{s\neq 0}\chi(s(x\cdot y-\alpha))\sum_{t\neq 0}\sum_{z\in E}\chi(t(\beta-x\cdot z))\right|

\displaystyle\leq p^{-2ek}\sum_{x\in R^{d}}\left|\sum_{y\in E}\sum_{s\neq 0}\chi(s(x\cdot y-\alpha))\right|\left|\sum_{t\neq 0}\sum_{z\in E}\chi(t(\beta-x\cdot z))\right|.

|III|\leq p^{-2ek}\left(\sum_{x\in R^{d}}\left|\sum_{s\neq 0}\sum_{y\in E}\chi(s(x\cdot y-\alpha))\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{x\in R^{d}}\left|\sum_{t\neq 0}\sum_{z\in E}\chi(t(x\cdot z-\beta))\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(10)

Which we relabel as

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq p^{-2ek}III_{\alpha}\cdot III_{\beta}.

Notice that

and

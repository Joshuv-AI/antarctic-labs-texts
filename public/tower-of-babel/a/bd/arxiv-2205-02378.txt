Regularity criteria and Liouville Theorem for 3D inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes flows with vacuum
Jae-Myoung Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the 3D inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes flows with vacuum, and obtain regularity criteria and Liouville type theorems in the Lorentz space if a smooth solution ( ρ , 𝐮 ) (\rho,\mathbf{u}) satisfies suitable conditions.

1.
Introduction

We consider the existence for solutions
(\rho,u,\pi):Q_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
to the three-dimensional
incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\
\rho\displaystyle u_{t}-\Delta u+\rho(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi=0\\
\\
\displaystyle\text{div}\ u=0,\\
\end{array}\right.\,\,\,\mbox{ in }\,\,Q_{T}:=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\,T).

(1.1)

Here
\rho
is the density function,
u
is the flow velocity and

\displaystyle\pi
is the pressure. We consider the initial value
problem of (
1.1
), which requires initial

\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.2)

Kazhikov
[
9
]
proved that the inhomogeneous
Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) have at least
one global weak solution in the energy space for smooth data with no
vacuum. After that, Ladyzhenskaya and Solonnikov
[
12
]
first
established the unique solvability for the system with smooth
initial data that has no vacuum, global well-posedness in two
dimensions and local well-posedness in three dimensions. Moreover,
if he initial data is small enough, then global well-posedness holds
true. Simon
[
20
]
constructed global weak solutions to the
system with finite energy with the statues containing vacuum. (see
e.g. Danchin
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
for the almost
critical Sobolev spaces and Abidi, Gui and Zhang
[
1
]
for
axi-symmetric initial data and Mucha, Xue and Zheng
[
16
]
and
references therein).

Regarding the regularity criteria for system
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
), Kim
[
10
]
established the
following Serrin type condition

u\in L^{t}(0,T;L^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\frac{3}{s}+\frac{2}{t}=1,s\in(3,\infty],

in the framework of Lorentz space (see e.g. Ye and Zhang

[
23
]
, Sun and Qian
[
21
]
for Besov space)

Recall first the definition of Lorentz spaces. Let
m(\varphi,t)

be the Lebesgue measure of the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\varphi(x)|>t\}
,

i.e.

m(\varphi,t):=m\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\varphi(x)|>t\}.

We denote by the
Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
1\leq p
,
q\leq\infty
with
the norm
[
22
]

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}(m(\varphi,t))^{q/p}\ \frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/q}<\infty,\quad\mbox{for }\ 1\leq q<\infty,\\
&\sup_{t\geq 0}\{t(m(\varphi,t))^{\frac{1}{p}}\}<\infty,\,\hskip 42.67912pt\ \ \quad\mbox{for }\ q=\infty\end{aligned}\right.

Note that
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=L^{r,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset L^{r,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
1<r<q\leq+\infty
.

In this direction, first result is stated as

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be the unique local strong solution in time
interval
[0,T)
to the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with
initial data (1.2). Then there exists a positive constant

\varepsilon
such that
(\rho,u)
is a regular solution on
(0,T]

provided that one of the following two conditions holds

(A)

u\in L^{q,\infty}(0,T;L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and

\|u\|_{L^{q,\infty}(0,T;L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq\varepsilon,~\text{with}~~\frac{3}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1,~3<p<\infty;

(B)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}<\infty,~\text{with}~~\frac{3}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=2,~\frac{3}{2}<p<\infty;

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be the unique local strong solution in time
interval
[0,T)
to the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with
initial data (1.2). Suppose that
u
satisfies the following
conditions holds

\|u\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}<\infty,~\text{with}~~\frac{3}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1,~3<p<\infty,

then the solution pair
(\rho,u)
can be extended beyond time

T>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.1
seem to hold for bounded
domains with smooth boundary with Dirichlet or slip type boundary
conditions.

On the other hands, regarding Liouville theorems for the fluid flows
contains the density function, in particular, compressible
Naiver-Stokes equations, Chae
[
3
]
showed if the smooth
solution
(\rho,u)
satisfies
\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\infty.
then
u\equiv 0
and
\rho=constant
(see Li and Yu
[
14
]
).
In the Lorentz framework, very recently, Li and Niu
[
13
]

proved if
(\rho,u)
is a smooth solution with
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
3<p<\frac{9}{2}
,
3\leq q\leq\infty
or

p=q=3
. Then
u\equiv 0
and
\rho=constant
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
For the equations (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), to the best of my
knowledge, There is no known result so far, even in
L^{p}
-space. In
this direction, parallel to the result of Jarrín

[
11
]
, second result is stated as

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be a weak
solution to the stationary equation corresponding to (
1.1
).
If
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u\in L^{\frac{9}{2},q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\frac{9}{2}\leq q<\infty
, then we
have
u\equiv 0
and
\rho\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Comparing to
[
11
]
, when
\frac{9}{2}<r
, it is not
shown Liouville Theorem because it is difficult to control for

\nabla u
due to the low regularity for density function
\rho
. in
this case, it is not yet known whether the result is valid for the
same reason.

Preliminaries

In this section we introduce some the definition of functional space
and its properties. For
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we denote the usual
Sobolev spaces by
W^{k,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\{u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,:\,D^{p}u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),0\leq|p|\leq k\}
. When
q=2
, we
write
W^{k,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We review some useful
estimates for the proof of Theorems.

We need the Hölder inequality in Lorentz spaces (see
[
17
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
1\leq p_{1}
,
p_{2}\leq\infty
,
1\leq q_{1}
,
q_{2}\leq\infty
and
u\in L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)
,
v\in L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\Omega)
.
Then
uv\in L^{p_{3},q_{3}}(\Omega)
with

\frac{1}{p_{3}}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
\frac{1}{q_{3}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
, and the inequality

\|uv\|_{L^{p_{3},q_{3}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\Omega)}

(2.1)

is valid.

Recall the following useful Gronwall lemma required in our proof,
which is first shown by
[
2
]
(see e.g.
[
18
]
,

[
15
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\phi
be a measurable positive function defined on the interval
[0,T]
. Suppose that there exists
\kappa_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\kappa<\kappa_{0}
and a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,
\phi
satisfies the inequality

\frac{d}{dt}\phi\leq\mu\lambda^{1-\kappa}\phi^{1+2\kappa},

where
0<\lambda\in L^{1,\infty}(0,T)
and
\mu>0
with

\mu\|\lambda\|_{L^{1,\infty}(0,T)}<\frac{1}{2}
.
Then
\phi
is bounded on
[0,T]
.

Next, to obtain the result of Liouville theorem, we start by
introducing the test functions
\varphi_{R}
and
\omega_{R}
as
follows: for a fixed

\varphi_{R}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

such that for
|x|\leq\frac{R}{2}
we
have
\varphi_{R}(x)=1
, for
|x|\geq R
we have

\varphi_{R}(x)=0
, and

\|\nabla\varphi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{c}{R}.

(3.7)

From Lemma
III.3.1
in
[
7

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi_{R}(x)

\omega_{R}

where

\|\omega_{R}\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{r})}

.

For second result, it is studied the following Caccioppoli type
estimate:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:R/2<|x|<R\}
. If the solution
(u,b)

verifies

and
\nabla u\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}R^{2-\frac{9}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

(3.9)

Proof.

A proof is almost same to that
[
11
]
, we give a sketch of

the test functions
\varphi_{R}
and

(3.10)

Once we have defined the functions
\varphi_{R}
and

above, we

First of all, since

is a solution of problem (
3.8
)

Thus by this inequality and the identity above we can write the

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{B_{R}}\partial_{j}u_{i}(\partial_{j}\varphi_{R})u_{i}dx+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{B_{R}}(\partial_{j}u_{i})\partial_{j}(W_{R})_{i}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{R}}(\rho u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\left(\varphi_{R}u-W_{R}\right)dx:=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{K}_{i}.

The term
\mathcal{K}_{2}
in (
3

Applying Hölder’s inequality and (

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|\nabla\textbf{W}_{R}|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|\nabla\varphi_{R}\cdot u|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|\nabla u|^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}R^{2-\frac{9}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Now We study each term in sequence. Due to the property for the
function

, we know that if

Applying the Holdër inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

For

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{3}

Again, we estimate each term separately. In term
\mathcal{J}_{1}
,
in the previous way, it is easily checked that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}R^{2-\frac{9}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}(R/2,R)}|u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

Next, to estimate the term

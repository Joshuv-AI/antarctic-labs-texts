Stationary Anisotropic Stokes, Oseen, and Navier-Stokes Systems: Periodic Solutions in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n}
Sergey E. Mikhailov

Abstract
First, the solution uniqueness, existence and regularity for stationary anisotropic (linear) Stokes and generalised Oseen systems with constant viscosity coefficients in a compressible framework are analysed in a range of periodic Sobolev (Bessel-potential) spaces in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . By the Galerkin algorithm and the Brower fixed point theorem, the existence of solution to the stationary anisotropic (non-linear) Navier-Stokes incompressible system is shown in a periodic Sobolev space for any n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . Then the solution uniqueness and regularity results for stationary anisotropic periodic Navier-Stokes system are established for n ∈ { 2 , 3 , 4 } n\in\{2,3,4\} .



MSC-class:

35J57, 35Q30, 46E35, 76D, 76M

Keywords:

anisotropic Stokes, Oseen, and Navier-Stokes equations; relaxed ellipticity; periodic Sobolev spaces; existence, uniqueness, and regularity; higher dimensions

Analysis of Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations is an established and active field of research in the applied mathematical analysis, see, e.g.,

In

In this paper, we present some further results in this direction considering space-periodic solutions in

n\geq 2

The periodic setting is interesting on its own, modelling fluid flow in periodic composite structures, and is also a common element of homogenisation theories for inhomogeneous fluids.

This paper, particularly, extends to the Oseen system and to the Navier-Stokes system for

2
Anisotropic Stokes, Navier-Stokes and Oseen systems

Let

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}
denote the second-order differential operator represented in the component-wise divergence form as

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}})_{k}:=\partial_{\alpha}\big(a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}})\big),\ \ k=1,\ldots,n,

(2.1)

were
{\mathbf{u}}\!=\!(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})^{\top}
,
E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}})\!:=\!\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}u_{\beta}+\partial_{\beta}u_{j})
are the entries of the symmetric part
{\mathbb{E}}({\mathbf{u}})
of
\nabla{\mathbf{u}}
(the gradient of
{\mathbf{u}}
),
and
a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}
are constant components of the viscosity coefficient tensor

{\mathbb{A}}:=\!\left({a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}}\right)_{1\leq i,j,\alpha,\beta\leq n}
, cf.
[
Duf78
]
.
We also denoted
\partial_{j}:=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
.
Here and further on, the Einstein summation convention in repeated indices from
1
to
n
is used unless stated otherwise.

The following symmetry conditions are assumed (see
[
OSY92
, (3.1),(3.3)]
),

\displaystyle a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}=a_{\alpha j}^{k\beta}=a_{k\beta}^{\alpha j}.

(2.2)

In addition, we require that tensor
{\mathbb{A}}
satisfies the (relaxed) ellipticity condition in terms of all
symmetric
matrices in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
with
zero matrix trace
, see
[
KMW21a
,
KMW21b
]
.
That is, we assume that there exists a constant
C_{\mathbb{A}}>0
such that,

\displaystyle{C_{\mathbb{A}}a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{k\alpha}\zeta_{j\beta}\geq|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|^{2}}\,,\ \

\displaystyle\forall\ \boldsymbol{\zeta}=(\zeta_{k\alpha})_{k,\alpha=1,\ldots,n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}

\displaystyle\mbox{ such that }\,\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\mbox{ and }\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta_{kk}=0,

(2.3)

where
|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|^{2}=\zeta_{k\alpha}\zeta_{k\alpha}
, while the superscript
\top
denotes the transpose of a matrix.

The tensor
{\mathbb{A}}
is endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{A}}\|:=\max\left\{|a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}|:k,j,\alpha,\beta=1\ldots,n\right\}\,.

(2.4)

Symmetry conditions (
2.2
) lead to the following equivalent form of the operator
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}

\begin{array}[]{lll}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}})_{k}=\partial_{\alpha}\big(a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}u_{j}\big),\ \ k=1,\ldots,n.\end{array}

(2.5)

Let
{\mathbf{u}}
be an unknown vector field,
p
be an unknown scalar field,
{\mathbf{f}}
be a given vector field and
g
be a given scalar field.
Then the linear equations

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p={\mathbf{f}},\ {\rm{div}}\ {\mathbf{u}}=g\end{array}

(2.6)

determine the
anisotropic stationary Stokes system with viscosity tensor coefficient
{\mathbb{A}}=\left(A^{\alpha\beta}\right)_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}
in a compressible framework
.

The nonlinear system

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p+{({\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}}={\mathbf{f}}\,,\ \ {\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}}=g

(2.7)

is called the
anisotropic stationary Navier-Stokes system with viscosity tensor coefficient
{\mathbb{A}}\!=\!\left(A^{\alpha\beta}\right)_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}
in a compressible framework
.

In addition, we will also consider a linearised version of the anisotropic stationary Navier-Stokes system (
2.7
),

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p+{({\mathbf{U}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}}={\mathbf{f}}\,,\ \ {\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}}=g,

(2.8)

where
\mathbf{U}
is a given function.
We will call (
2.8
) the stationary anisotropic Oseen system.
If
{\mathbf{U}}\equiv 0
, then (
2.8
) reduces to the Stokes system (
2.7
).

If
g=0
in (
2.6
), (
2.7
), and (
2.8
), then these equations are reduced, respectively, to the
incompressible anisotropic stationary Stokes, Navier-Stokes, and Oseen systems
.

In the
isotropic case
, the tensor
{\mathbb{A}}
reduces to

\displaystyle a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}=\lambda\delta_{k\alpha}\delta_{j\beta}+\mu\left(\delta_{\alpha j}\delta_{\beta k}+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{kj}\right),\ 1\leq i,j,\alpha,\beta\leq n\,,

(2.9)

where
\lambda
and
\mu
are real constant parameters with

\mu>0
(cf., e.g., Appendix III, Part I, Section 1 in
[
Te01
]
)
and
\delta_{k\alpha}
is the Kronecker symbol.
Then (
2.5
) becomes

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}=(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}\,\mathbf{u}+\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}.

(2.10)

Then it is immediate that condition (
2
) is fulfilled (cf.
[
KMW21b
]
) and thus our results apply also to the Stokes, Navier-Stokes, and Oseen systems in the
isotropic case
.
Assuming
\lambda=0
,
\mu=1
we arrive at the classical mathematical formulations of isotropic Stokes, Navier-Stokes, and Oseen systems.

Some periodic function spaces

Let us introduce some function spaces on torus and periodic function spaces (see, e.g.,

[
Agm65
, p.26]
,
[
Agr15
]
,
[
McL91
]
,
[
RT10
, Chapter 3]
,
[
RRS16
, Section 1.7.1]

[
Te95
, Chapter 2]
,
for more details).

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and
{\mathbb{T}}
be the
n
-dimensional flat torus that can be parametrized as the semi-open cube
{\mathbb{T}}=[0,1)^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, cf.
[
Zy02
, p. 312]
.
In what follows,
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{T}})=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
denotes the space of infinitely smooth real or complex functions on the torus.
As usual,
{\mathbb{N}}
denotes the set of natural numbers,
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
the set of natural numbers augmented by 0, and
\mathbb{Z}
the set of integers.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional vector with integer components.
We will further need also the set

\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.

Extending the torus parametrisation to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, it is often useful to identify
{\mathbb{T}}
with the quotient space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Then the space of functions
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
on the torus can be identified with the space of
{\mathbb{T}}
-periodic (1-periodic) functions

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\#}=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\#}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
that consists of functions
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\xi})=\phi(\mathbf{x})\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ \forall\,\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(3.1)

Similarly, the Lebesgue space on the torus
L_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, can be identified with the periodic Lebesgue space
L_{p\#}=L_{p\#}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
that consists of functions
\phi\in L_{p,\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, which satisfy the periodicity condition (
3.1
) (for a.e.
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
).

The space dual to
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{T}})
, i.e., the space of linear bounded functionals on
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{T}})
, called the space of torus distributions, is denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}})
and can be identified with the space of periodic distributions
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\#}
acting on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\#}
.

The toroidal/periodic Fourier transform
mapping a function
g\in\mathcal{C}_{\#}^{\infty}
to a set of its Fourier coefficients
\hat{g}
is defined as (see, e.g.,
[
RT10
, Definition 3.1.8]
)

\displaystyle\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=[{\cal F}_{{\mathbb{T}}}g](\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}e^{-2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}g(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x},\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

and can be generalised to the Fourier transform acting on a distribution
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\#}
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
|\boldsymbol{\xi}|:=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}^{2})^{1/2}
be the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let us denote

\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=(1+|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2})^{1/2}.

Evidently,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2}\leq|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}\leq\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2}\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

(3.2)

Similar to
[
RT10
, Definition 3.2.2]
, for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
we define the
periodic/toroidal Sobolev (Bessel-potential) spaces

H_{\#}^{s}:=H_{\#}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
,
which consist of the torus distributions
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}})
, for which the norm

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H_{\#}^{s}}:=\|\rho^{s}\widehat{g}\|_{\ell_{2}}:=\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2s}|\widehat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(3.3)

is finite, i.e., the series in (
3.3
) converges.
Here
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{2}}
is the standard norm in the space of square summable sequences.
By
[
RT10
, Proposition 3.2.6]
,
H_{\#}^{s}
are Hilbert spaces.

The dual product between
g\in H_{\#}^{s}
and
f\in H_{\#}^{-s}
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, is defined (cf.
[
RT10
, Definition 3.2.8]
) as

\displaystyle\langle g,f\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}:=\langle\hat{g},\hat{f}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}:=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\hat{f}(-\boldsymbol{\xi}).

(3.4)

If
s=0
, i.e.,
g,f\in L_{2\#}
, then (
3.4
) reduces to

\langle g,f\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}g(\mathbf{x})f(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}.

Hence for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the space
H_{\#}^{-s}
is adjoint (dual) to
H_{\#}^{s}
, i.e.,
H_{\#}^{-s}=(H_{\#}^{s})^{*}
.
Similar to, e.g.,
[
McL00
, p.76]
one can show that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H_{\#}^{s}}=\sup_{f\in H_{\#}^{-s},f\neq 0}\frac{|\langle g,f\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}|}{\|f\|_{H_{\#}^{-s}}}.

(3.5)

For
g\in H_{\#}^{s}
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, let us consider the partial sums

g_{m}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},|\boldsymbol{\xi}|\leq m}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}.

Evidently,
g_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{\#}^{\infty}
,
\hat{g}_{m}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
if
|\boldsymbol{\xi}|\leq m
and
\hat{g}_{m}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=0
if
|\boldsymbol{\xi}|>m
.
This implies that
\|g-g_{m}\|_{H_{\#}^{s}}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
and hence we can write

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}},

(3.6)

where the Fourier series converges in the sense of norm (
3.3
).
Moreover, since
g
is an arbitrary distribution from
H_{\#}^{s}
, this also implies that the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
is dense in
H_{\#}^{s}
for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
(cf.
[
RT10
, Exercise 3.2.9]
).

There holds the compact embedding
H_{\#}^{t}\hookrightarrow H_{\#}^{s}
if
t>s
, embeddings
H_{\#}^{s}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\#}^{m}
if
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
s>m+n/2
, and moreover,
\bigcap_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}H_{\#}^{s}={\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
(cf.
[
RT10
, Exercises 3.2.10, 3.2.10, and Corollary 3.2.11]
). Note also that the periodic norms on
H_{\#}^{s}
are equivalent to the corresponding standard (non-periodic) Bessel potential norms on
{\mathbb{T}}
as a cubic domain, see, e.g.,
[
Agr15
, Section 13.8.1]
.

By (
3.3
),

\|g\|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}}=|\widehat{g}(\mathbf{0})|^{2}+|g|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}},

where

\displaystyle|g|_{H_{\#}^{s}}:=\|\rho^{s}\widehat{g}\|_{\dot{\ell}_{2}}:=\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2s}|\widehat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

is the seminorm in
H_{\#}^{s}
.

For any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let us also introduce the space

\displaystyle\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}:=\{g\in H_{\#}^{s}:\langle g,1\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}=0\}.

(3.7)

The definition implies that if
g\in\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}
, then
\widehat{g}(\mathbf{0})=0
and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}}=\|g\|_{H_{\#}^{s}}=|g|_{H_{\#}^{s}}=\|\rho^{s}\widehat{g}\|_{\dot{\ell}_{2}}\ .

(3.8)

Denoting

\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}:=\{g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}:\langle g,1\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}=0\}
,
then
\bigcap_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}=\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
.

Definition (
3.7
) also implies that the space adjoint to
\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}
can be expressed as the quotient space,

(\dot{H}_{\#}^{s})^{*}=(H_{\#}^{s})^{*}/{\mathbb{R}}=H_{\#}^{-s}/{\mathbb{R}}.

Identifying the quotient space
H_{\#}^{-s}/{\mathbb{R}}
with the space
\dot{H}_{\#}^{-s}
, we then identify the space
\dot{H}_{\#}^{-s}
with the space dual to
\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}
.

The corresponding spaces of
n
-component vector functions/distributions are denoted as
\mathbf{L}_{q\#}:=(L_{q\#})^{n}
,
\mathbf{H}_{\#}^{s}:=(H_{\#}^{s})^{n}
, etc.

Note that the norm
\|\nabla(\cdot)\|_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-1}}

is an equivalent norm in
\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}
.
Indeed, by (
3.6
)

\displaystyle\nabla g(\mathbf{x})=2\pi i\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\boldsymbol{\xi}e^{2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\quad\widehat{\nabla g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=2\pi i\boldsymbol{\xi}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\forall\,g\in\dot{H}_{\#}^{s},

and
then (
3.2
) and (
3.8
) imply that

2\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}}=2\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}}=2\pi^{2}|g|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}}\leq\|\nabla g\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-1}}\\
\leq 4\pi^{2}|g|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}}=4\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}}=4\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}}\quad\forall\,g\in\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}.

(3.9)

The vector counterpart of (
3.9
) takes the form

2\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}}=2\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}}\leq\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{(H_{\#}^{s-1})^{n\times n}}\\
\leq 4\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}}=4\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}}\quad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}.

(3.10)

We will further need the first Korn inequality

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\bf v}\|^{2}_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}\leq 2\|\mathbb{E}({\bf v})\|^{2}_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}\quad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}

(3.11)

that can be easily proved by adapting, e.g., the proof in
[
McL00
, Theorem 10.1]
to the periodic Sobolev space; cf. also
[
OSY92
, Theorem 2.8]
.

Let us also define the Sobolev spaces of divergence-free functions and distributions,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\bf w}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}:{{\rm div}}\,{\bf w}=0\right\},\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.12)

endowed with the same norm (
3.3
).
Similarly,
{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#\sigma}
and
\mathbf{L}_{q\#\sigma}
denote the subspaces of divergence-free vector-functions from

{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
and
\mathbf{L}_{q\#}
, respectively, etc.

4
Stationary anisotropic periodic Stokes system

Let
n\geq 2
. In this section, we generalise to the isotropic and anisotropic (linear) Stokes systems in compressible framework and to a range of Sobolev spaces the analysis available in
[
Te95
, Section 2.2]
.

For the unknowns
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}
and the given data

({\mathbf{f}},g)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-2}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let us consider the Stokes system

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f},

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=g,

(4.2)

that should be understood in the sense of distributions, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p,\boldsymbol{\phi}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\phi}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\phi}\in{{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle{\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}},\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}

\displaystyle=\langle g,\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\quad\forall\,\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}.

(4.4)

For
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, let us employ
\bar{e}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\mathbf{x})=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}
as
\phi
in (
4.4
) and
\bar{e}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\mathbf{x})
, multiplied by the unit coordinate vector, as
\boldsymbol{\phi}
in (
4.3
).
Then recalling (
2.5
), we arrive for each
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
at the following algebraic system for the Fourier coefficients,
\hat{u}_{j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
,
k=1,2,\dots,n
, and
\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
.

\displaystyle\hskip-15.00002pt4\pi^{2}\xi_{\alpha}a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\beta}\hat{u}_{j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})+2\pi i\xi_{k}\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\hat{f}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\ k=1,2,\dots,n

(4.5)

\displaystyle 2\pi i\xi_{j}\hat{u}_{j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\ \forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

(4.6)

System (
4.5
)-(
4.6
) can be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\left(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\atop\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)=\left(\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\atop\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

(4.7)

where
\mathfrak{S}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
is the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix with entries

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\ell j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\begin{cases}4\pi^{2}\xi_{\alpha}a_{\ell j}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\beta},&\ell,j=1,\ldots,n;\\
-2\pi i\xi_{\ell},&\ell=1,\ldots,n,\ j=n+1;\\
-2\pi i\xi_{j},&\ell=n+1,\ j=1,\ldots,n;\\
0,&\ell=j=n+1.\end{cases}

(4.8)

Here
i^{2}=-1
, and
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
.
The matrix
\mathfrak{S}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
, is in fact the toroidal/periodic symbol (cf.
[
RT10
, Section 4.1.1]
) of the anisotropic Stokes system (
4.1
)-(
4.2
).

LEMMA 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and condition (
2
) hold. Then the matrix
\mathfrak{S}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
is non-singular for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and hence the solution of the algebraic system (
4.5
)-(
4.6
) can be represented in terms of the inverse matrix
\mathfrak{S}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
as

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\atop\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)=\mathfrak{S}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\left(\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\atop\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

(4.9)

Moreover, the following estimates hold,

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\leq\widehat{C}_{uf}\frac{|\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{|2\pi\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}}+\widehat{C}_{ug}\frac{|\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{2\pi|\boldsymbol{\xi}|},

(4.10)

\displaystyle|\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\leq\widehat{C}_{pf}\frac{|\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{2\pi|\boldsymbol{\xi}|}+\widehat{C}_{pg}|\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

(4.11)

where

\widehat{C}_{uf}=2C_{\mathbb{A}},\ \widehat{C}_{ug}=\widehat{C}_{pf}=1+2C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbb{A}\|,\ \widehat{C}_{pg}=\|\mathbb{A}\|(1+2C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbb{A}\|).

Proof.

Introducing the new variables
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}=2\pi\widehat{\mathbf{u}}
,
\hat{q}=i\hat{p}
, the algebraic system (

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\left(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\atop\hat{q}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)=\left(\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})/(2\pi)\atop-i\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{\ell j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\begin{cases}\xi_{\alpha}a_{\ell j}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\beta},&\ell,j=1,\ldots,n;\\
\xi_{\ell},&\ell=1,\ldots,n,\ j=n+1;\\
\xi_{j},&\ell=n+1,\ j=1,\ldots,n;\\
0,&\ell=j=n+1.\end{cases}

In order to show that

is non-singular for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, we
use Theorem

.
To this end, for a fixed

, we consider the bilinear forms
a_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and

\displaystyle a_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{w}},\widehat{\mathbf{v}}):=\widehat{w}_{\ell}\xi_{\alpha}a_{\ell j}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\beta}\hat{v}_{j}\quad\ \forall\,\widehat{\mathbf{w}},\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,,

(4.14)

\displaystyle b_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}},\hat{q}):=\xi_{j}\hat{v}_{j}\hat{q}\hskip 55.00008pt\forall\,\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\,\hat{q}\in{\mathbb{R}},

(4.15)

as well as the closed subspace

of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
given by

\displaystyle V_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=\left\{\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:b_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}},\hat{q})=0,\ \forall\,\hat{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}\right\}=\left\{\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\xi_{j}\hat{v}_{j}=0\right\}.

\displaystyle|a_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{w}},\widehat{\mathbf{v}})|\leq\|{\mathbb{A}}\||\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{w}}|\,|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}|,\ \ |b_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}},\hat{q})|\leq|\boldsymbol{\xi}|\,|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}|\,|\hat{q}|\quad\forall\,\widehat{\mathbf{w}},\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ \forall\,\hat{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The symmetry conditions (
2.2
) allow us to write the bilinear form
a_{0}
as

\displaystyle a_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{w}},\widehat{\mathbf{v}})=a_{\ell j}^{\alpha\beta}(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}\otimes\boldsymbol{\xi})_{\ell\alpha}^{s}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\otimes\boldsymbol{\xi})^{s}_{\beta j}\,,

where

\displaystyle(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}\otimes\boldsymbol{\xi})^{s}_{\ell\alpha}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widehat{w}_{\ell}\xi_{\alpha}+\widehat{w}_{\alpha}\xi_{\ell}\right),\ \ \ell,\alpha=1,\ldots,n\,.

\displaystyle a_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}},\widehat{\mathbf{v}})\geq c_{{\mathbb{A}}}^{-1}|(\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\otimes{\boldsymbol{\xi}})^{s}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}c_{{\mathbb{A}}}^{-1}|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}|^{2}|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}|^{2}\quad\forall\,\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mbox{ such that }\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}=0\,,

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{q}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{\widehat{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}}\frac{b_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{v}},\hat{q})}{|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}|\,|\hat{q}|}\geq|\boldsymbol{\xi}|\,,

) and (

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\leq\widehat{C}_{uf}\frac{|\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{2\pi|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}}+\widehat{C}_{ug}\frac{|\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{|\boldsymbol{\xi}|},

\displaystyle|\hat{q}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\leq\widehat{C}_{pf}\frac{|\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{2\pi|\boldsymbol{\xi}|}+\widehat{C}_{pg}|\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

\displaystyle\geq\pi^{2}C_{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}\|{\bf w}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}^{2}\quad\forall\,{\bf w}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}.

(5.16)

On the other hand, since
{\mathbf{U}}\in{\mathbf{L}}_{\theta\#\sigma}
, Section

(iii) implies that

\displaystyle\left\langle({\mathbf{U}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{w}},{\bf w}\right\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\forall\,{\bf w}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}.

Hence

\displaystyle a_{{\mathbb{T}};\mathbf{U}}({\bf w},{\bf w})\geq\pi^{2}C_{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}\|{\bf w}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}^{2}\quad\forall\,{\bf w}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}.

(5.17)

Inequality (

) shows that the bilinear form

is coercive.

The boundedness of the divergence operator

implies that the bilinear form
b_{\mathbb{T}}:\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}\times\dot{L}_{2\#}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded as well.
One can also check that for any

, the divergence PDE (

.
The PDE is equivalent to
the algebraic equation (

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\frac{\boldsymbol{\xi}}{2\pi i|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\ \forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Moreover, then the divergence operator

is an isomorphism,
cf. also, e.g.,

7.1
with

(ii)

Stationary anisotropic periodic Navier-Stokes system

6.1
Existence of a weak solution to anisotropic incompressible periodic Navier-Stokes system

In this section, using the Galerkin approximation we show the existence of a weak solution of the anisotropic Navier-Stokes system in the incompressible case, with general data in
L_{2}
-based Sobolev spaces on a flat torus
{{\mathbb{T}}}
, for
n\geq 2
.

Let us consider the Navier-Stokes system

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p+({\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{f},

(6.1)

\displaystyle{\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}}=0,

(6.2)

for the couple of unknowns
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{0}
and the given data

\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}
.
As for the Stokes system, the incompressible Navier-Stokes system (
6.1
)-(
6.2
) can be re-written as one vector equation

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p+({\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{f}

(6.3)

for the unknowns
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\times\dot{H}_{q\#}^{0}
, with some
q>1
, and the given data

\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}
.

Next we show the existence of a weak solution of the Navier-Stokes equation, generalising to anisotropic case the Galerkin approximation arguments from
[
Te01
, Chapter 2]
, cf. also
[
Li69
, Chapter 1, Section 7]
.

First of all, let us define the space
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}
and its norm as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}=\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\cap\mathbf{L}_{n\#},\qquad\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}}=\left(\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}+\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}_{n\#}}\right)^{1/2}.

(6.4)

For the adjoint operator, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}^{*}=(\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\cap\mathbf{L}_{n\#})^{*}=(\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1})^{*}\cup\mathbf{L}_{n/(n-1)\#}.

(6.5)

If
n\in\{2,3,4\}
, then
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}=\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
;
otherwise
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}
is a proper subspace of
\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
.
The space
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}
is also the closure of
\dot{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#\sigma}
in the norm (
6.4
).
Taking into account the mapping properties of operator (
7.16
), we give, similar to
[
Te01
, Chapter 2, Eq. (1.25)]
the following variational formulation of the Navier-Stokes system (
6.1
)-(
6.2
), i.e., equation (
6.3
), for any
n\geq 2
:

For
\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}
, find
{\mathbf{u}}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
such that

\displaystyle\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}}),E_{i\alpha}({\mathbf{v}})\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}+\langle({\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}=\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\quad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}.

(6.6)

Since
\dot{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#\sigma}\subset\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}
, any
{\mathbf{u}}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
satisfying the variational problem (
6.6
) is also a distributional solution of the Navier-Stokes system (
6.1
)-(
6.2
) in the sense of Leray (i.e., for any
\mathbf{v}\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#\sigma}
in (
6.6
)).

Now we are in a position to prove the following assertion.

THEOREM 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and condition (
2
) hold.
If
\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}
, then
the anisotropic Navier-Stokes equation (
6.3
)
has a solution
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\times\dot{H}_{q\#}^{0}
(in the sense of distributions), where

for

, and

for
n\geq 5
.
Moreover, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}\leq\pi^{-2}C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}.

(6.7)

Proof.

As in
[
Te01
, Chapter 2, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
Li69
, Chapter 1, Theorem 7.1]
, we will use the Galerkin approximation.
First of all, let
\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l},\ldots
be a system of linearly independent functions from

\dot{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#\sigma}
that is complete in
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}
.

For each integer
m\geq 1
, let us look for a solution

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{m}=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\eta_{l,m}\mathbf{w}_{l},\quad\eta_{l,m}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(6.8)

of the following discrete analogue of the variational problem (

\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}}_{m}),E_{i\alpha}(\mathbf{w}_{k})\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}+\langle({\mathbf{u}}_{m}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}_{m},\mathbf{w}_{k}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\\

For a fixed

,

Existence of a real solution of this system follows from Lemma

.
Indeed, let
\eta:=\{\eta_{l,m}\}_{l=1}^{m}
,

denote the

).
Note that

Then by representation (
6.8
), equality (
7.25
), the ellipticity condition (
2

3.11

\displaystyle(Q(\eta),\eta)

\displaystyle=\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}}_{m}),E_{i\alpha}(\mathbf{u}_{m})\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}+\langle({\mathbf{u}}_{m}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}_{m},\mathbf{u}_{m}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}-\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{u}_{m}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{u}_{m}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}\|\mathbf{u}_{m}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}.

(6.12)

Thus, for any

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{m}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}\leq\pi^{-2}C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}.

(6.13)

This means that the sequence

and in
\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
and thus there exists a subsequence

After

has a unique solution
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{0}
.

Proof.

Assume that the Navier-Stokes equation (
6.3
) and thus the variational problem (
6.6
) has two solutions
({\bf u}^{(1)},p^{(1)})
and
({\bf u}^{(2)},p^{(2)})
in the space

\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{0}
.
Note that
\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}=\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
if
n\in\{2,3,4\}
.
Then the first Korn inequality (
3.11
), the ellipticity condition (
2
), the variational formulation (
6.6
) and the norm equivalence inequality (
3.10
) give for
k\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\|^{2}_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|{\mathbb{E}}({\mathbf{u}^{(k)}})\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{\mathbb{A}}\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}}^{(k)}),E_{i\alpha}(\mathbf{u}^{(k)})\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}

\displaystyle=2C_{\mathbb{A}}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{u}^{(k)}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\leq 2C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}\|\mathbf{u}^{(k)}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}\|\nabla{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}.

(6.17)

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\mathbf{u}^{(k)}}\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}},\quad k\in\{1,2\}.

(6.18)

The variational formulation (
6.6
) also implies,

\big\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}),E_{i\alpha}({\bf v})\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\\
+\left\langle({\bf u}^{(1)}\cdot\nabla){\bf u}^{(1)}-({\bf u}^{(2)}\cdot\nabla){\bf u}^{(2)},{\bf v}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=0\quad\forall\ {\bf v}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}.

(6.19)

Then by choosing
{\bf v}={\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}
in (
6.19
), we obtain

\big\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}),E_{i\alpha}({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\big\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\\
=-\left\langle\left(({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\cdot\nabla)\right){\bf u}^{(1)},{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\\
-\left\langle({\bf u}^{(2)}\cdot\nabla)({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}),{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\,.

(6.20)

Due to the membership of
{\bf u}^{(1)}
and
{\bf u}^{(2)}
in
\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
, relation (
7.25
) yields

\displaystyle\left\langle({\bf u}^{(2)}\cdot\nabla)({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}),{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}=0\,,

(6.21)

which shows that equation (
6.20
) reduces to

\!\!\!\!\left\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}),E_{i\alpha}({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\!\\
=\!-\left\langle\big(({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\cdot\nabla\big){\bf u}^{(1)},{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\,.

(6.22)

On the other hand, in view of condition (
2
) and the first Korn inequality (
3.11
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}^{2}\,\leq 2C_{\mathbb{A}}\left\langle a_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}),E_{i\alpha}({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\,.

(6.23)

Thus, by
inequalities (
7.20
), (
7.10
), (
3.9
), and (
6.18
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\left(({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\cdot\nabla\right){\bf u}^{(1)},{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{T}}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\|{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}_{4\#}}\|\nabla{\bf u}^{(1)}\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C_{4\#}^{2}\|{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}^{2}\|\nabla{\bf u}^{(1)}\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi^{3}}C_{\mathbb{A}}C_{4\#}^{2}\|\nabla({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}.

(6.24)

Then equalities (
6.22
)-(
6.2
) imply that

\displaystyle\|\nabla({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}^{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi^{3}}C_{\mathbb{A}}^{2}C_{4\#}^{2}\|\nabla({\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)})\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}\,.

(6.25)

Assumption (
6.16
) shows that estimate (
6.25
) is possible only if
{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}={\bf 0}
.

Hence, equation (
6.3
) implies
\nabla(p^{(1)}-p^{(2)})=0
in
\Omega
. Then
p^{(1)}-p^{(2)}
is a constant, i.e.,
p^{(1)}=p^{(2)}
in
\dot{H}_{\#}^{0}
.
∎

Note that in the second inequality in (
6.2
) we used that by the Sobolev embedding theorem,

\|{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{4\#}}\leq C_{4\#}\|{\bf u}^{(1)}-{\bf u}^{(2)}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}
,
which is however not available for the dimensions
n>4
. This limited our uniqueness proof to the cases
n\in\{2,3,4\}
only.

6.3
Solution regularity for the anisotropic periodic Navier-Stokes system

In this section, we show that the regularity of a solution of the anisotropic incompressible Navier-Stokes system on
{{\mathbb{T}}}^{n}
,
n\in\{2,3,4\},
is completely determined by the regularity of its right-hand side, as for the Stokes system.
To prove this we use the inclusions of the nonlinear term
({\mathbf{u}}^{(1)}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}
given by Theorem
7.5
and the unique solvability of corresponding (linear) Stokes system along with the bootstrap argument.
This is sufficient to prove the regularity for
n\in\{2,3\}
. However, to prove the regularity for
n=4
, we needed to accommodate more subtle norm estimates from
[
Ge1979
]
first.

THEOREM 6.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and condition (
2
) hold.

(i) Let
s_{1}>{-1+n/2}
.
If
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s_{1}}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s_{1}-1}
is a solution of the anisotropic Navier-Stokes equation (
6.3
) with a right hand side
\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s_{2}-2}
, where
s_{2}>s_{1}
,
then
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s_{2}}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s_{2}-1}
.

(ii) Moreover, if
{\mathbf{f}}\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}_{\#}^{\infty}

then
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}_{\#}^{\infty}\times\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
.

Proof.

(i) Let
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s_{1}}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s_{1}-1}
be a solution of (
6.3
) with
\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s_{2}-2}
.
Then by Theorem
7.5
, for the nonlinear term we have the inclusion
({\mathbf{u}}^{(1)}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{t_{1}}
with
t_{1}=2s_{1}-1-n/2
if
s_{1}<n/2
, with
t_{1}=s_{1}-1
if
s_{1}>n/2
, and with any
t_{1}\in(s_{1}-2,s_{1}-1)
(and we can further use
t_{1}=s_{1}-3/2
for certainty) if
s_{1}=n/2
.
Hence the couple
({\mathbf{u}},p)
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p=\mathbf{f}^{(1)}

(6.26)

with
\mathbf{f}^{(1)}:=\mathbf{f}-({\mathbf{u}}^{(1)}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s^{(1)}-2}
, where
s^{(1)}=\min\{s_{2},t_{1}+2\}
.
By Corollary
4.4
(i), the linear equation (
6.26
) has a unique solution in

\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}
for any
s\leq s^{(1)}
and thus

({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s^{(1)}}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s^{(1)}-1}
.
If
s^{(1)}=s_{2}
, which we call case (a), this proves item (i) of the theorem.

Otherwise we have case (b), when
s^{(1)}<s_{2}
, i.e.,
s^{(1)}=t_{1}+2
,
by the definition of
s^{(1)}
.
Then we arrange an iterative process by replacing
s_{1}
with
s^{(1)}=t_{1}+2
on each iteration until we arrive at case (a), thus proving item (i) of the theorem.
Note that in case (b),

\displaystyle s^{(1)}-s_{1}\geq\delta:=\min\{s_{1}+1-n/2,1,1/2\}>0

in the first iteration, and
\delta
does not decrease in the next iterations since
s_{1}
increases.
This implies that the iteration process will reach the case (a) and stop after a finite number of iterations.

(ii) If
{\mathbf{f}}\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}_{\#}^{\infty}
, then for any
s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have
\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s_{2}-2}
and item (i) implies that
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s_{2}}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s_{2}-1}
.
Hence
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}_{\#}^{\infty}\times\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
.
∎

Combining Theorems
6.1
and
6.3
, we obtain the following assertion on existence and regularity of solution to the Navier-Stokes system on torus for
n\in\{2,3\}
.

THEOREM 6.4
.

Let
n\in\{2,3\}
and condition (
2
) hold.

(i) If
\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-2}
,

6.3

({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}

(ii) Moreover, if
{\mathbf{f}}\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}_{\#}^{\infty}
then equation (
6.3
) has a solution

({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}_{\#}^{\infty}\times\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
.

Note that in the

(
2.9
) with
\lambda=0
, similar results for the Navier-Stokes system in flat torus as well as in domains of

are available, e.g., in

RRS16

So01

One can easily check that the arguments leading to the regularity Theorem

are at this stage not applicable for

since to apply them we would need the existence of

in

THEOREM 6.5
.

Let
n\in\{2,3,4\}
and condition (
2
) hold.
If
\mathbf{f}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{0}
, then the anisotropic Navier-Stokes equation (
6.3
) has a solution

({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{2}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{1}

and the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{2}}+\|p\|_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{1}}\leq C_{1}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{0}}+C_{-1}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}

(6.27)

holds for some constants

Proof.

Let
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{0}

6.3
) provided by Theorem
6.1
and thus satisfying (
6.7
).
Let
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in\dot{\mathbf{C}}^{\infty}_{\#\sigma}
be a sequence
converging to
\mathbf{u}
in
\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
and let us consider the following Oseen equation (a linearised version of equation (
6.3
)) for

,

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}+\nabla\widetilde{p}_{k}=\mathbf{f}-({\mathbf{u}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}.

(6.28)

By Theorem
5.2
, for every

there exists a solution

Moreover, since

implies that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}\leq C_{\mathbb{A}}\pi^{-2}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}.

(6.29)

In addition, considering (

we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{2}}+\|\widetilde{p}_{k}\|_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{1}}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{0}}+\|({\mathbf{u}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{0}}\right)

(6.30)

with

Let
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
be such that
2<\theta<\infty
if

\theta=n
if

.
Employing Lemma
7.7
with

for

\nabla\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}
for

\widetilde{C}_{\delta}{=C_{\delta}(K_{\theta\#})}>0

\leq 2\pi\delta\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{2}_{\#}}+2\pi\widetilde{C}_{\delta}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#}}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{\#}}\\
\leq 2\pi\delta\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{2}_{\#}}+2\pi^{-1}C_{\mathbb{A}}C_{\theta\#}\widetilde{C}_{\delta}\|{\mathbf{u}}_{k}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{\#}}\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{-1}}.

independent of

, such that

\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#}}\leq C_{\theta\#}\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{\#}}

{\mathbf{u}}_{k}
converges to

7
Some auxiliary results

7.1
Abstract mixed variational formulations

Let us produce the well-posedness result for the abstract mixed formulation, related to Babuška
[
Bab73
]
and Brezzi
[
Bre74
, Theorem 1.1]

(see also Theorem 2.34 and Remark 2.35(i) in Ern & Guermond
[
EG04
]
, and Brezzi & Fortin
[
BF1991
]
).

THEOREM 7.1
.

Let
X
and
{\mathcal{M}}
be two real Hilbert spaces. Let
a(\cdot,\cdot):X\times X\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
b(\cdot,\cdot):X\times{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded bilinear forms. Let
f\in X^{\prime}
and
g\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
. Let
V
be the subspace of
X
defined by

\displaystyle V:=\left\{v\in X:b(v,q)=0\quad\forall\,q\in{\mathcal{M}}\right\}.

(7.1)

Assume that
a(\cdot,\cdot):V\times V\to{\mathbb{R}}
is coercive, which means that there exists a constant
C_{a}>0
such that

\displaystyle a(w,w)\geq C_{a}^{-1}\|w\|_{X}^{2}\quad\forall\,w\in V,

(7.2)

and that
b(\cdot,\cdot):X\times{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
the Babuška-Brezzi condition

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in{\mathcal{M}}\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{v\in X\setminus\{0\}}\frac{b(v,q)}{\|v\|_{X}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}}}\geq C_{b}^{-1}\,,

(7.3)

with some constant
C_{b}>0
. Then the mixed variational formulation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a(u,v)+b(v,p)&=f(v)\quad\forall\,v\in X,\\
b(u,q)&=g(q)\quad\forall\,q\in{\mathcal{M}},\end{array}\right.

(7.4)

has a unique solution
(u,p)\in X\times{\mathcal{M}}
and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X}\leq C_{a}\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}+C_{b}(1+\|a\|C_{a})\|g\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}},

(7.5)

\displaystyle\|p\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}\leq C_{b}(1+\|a\|C_{a})\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}+{\|a\|}C_{b}^{2}(1+\|a\|C_{a})\|g\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}},

(7.6)

where
\|a\|
is the norm of the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
.

7.2
Brower fixed point theorem application

In the main text we need the following well-known result that follows from the Brower fixed point theorem (see, e.g.,
[
Li69
, Chapter 1, Lemma 4.3]
,
[
Ga11
, Lemma IX.3.1]
).

LEMMA 7.2
.

Let
\eta\to Q(\eta)
be a continuous map of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
to itself, such that for some
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle(Q(\eta),\eta)\geq 0\quad\forall\eta:\ |\eta|=\rho.

(7.7)

Here for
\eta=\{\eta_{j}\},\zeta=\{\zeta_{j}\}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
we denote

(\eta,\zeta):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}\zeta_{j},\quad|\eta|:=(\eta,\eta)^{1/2}.

Then there exists
\eta
such that
|\eta|\leq\rho
and
Q(\eta)=0
.

7.3
Advection term properties

Let the quadratic operator
\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}:{\mathbf{w}}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}\mathbf{w}
be defined as

\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}{\mathbf{w}}:=({\mathbf{w}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{w}}.

Let in this section the dimension
n\geq 2
.
To formulate assertions valid both for
n=2
and
n>2
, let us define the set
I_{n}
as

\displaystyle I_{2}:=(2,\infty),\quad I_{n}:=[n,\infty)\mbox{ if }n>2.

(7.8)

Let
\theta\in I_{n}
and let us denote
q_{\theta}:=2\theta/(\theta-2)
. Then

\displaystyle 2<2\theta/(\theta-2)<\infty\mbox{ if }n=2;\quad 2<2\theta/(\theta-2)\leq 2n/(n-2)\mbox{ if }n>2.

(7.9)

By the Sobolev embedding theorem (see, e.g.,
[

, Section 2.2.4, Corollary 2]
), for any
\theta\in I_{n}

the space
{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}
is continuously embedded in the space
\mathbf{L}_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#}

and there exists a constant
C_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#}>0
independent of
\mathbf{v}
, such that

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#}}\leq C_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#}\|{\mathbf{v}}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{\#}}\quad\forall\ \mathbf{v}\in{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{\#},\ \forall\,\theta\in I_{n}.

(7.10)

7.3.1

By the Hölder inequality, for any
\theta\in I_{n}

|\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{1}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{v}}_{2},{\mathbf{v}}_{3}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}|\!\!\leq\!\|{\mathbf{v}}_{1}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#}}\|\nabla{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\|_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{3}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#}}\\
\forall\ {\mathbf{v}}_{1}\in\mathbf{L}_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#},\ {\mathbf{v}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1},\ {\mathbf{v}}_{3}\in\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#},\ \forall\,\theta\in I_{n}.

(7.11)

Due to (
7.10
) and (
3.10
), inequality (

) gives

|\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{1}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{v}}_{2},{\mathbf{v}}_{3}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}|\!\!\leq\!{2\pi}C_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{1}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\|_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{3}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#}}\\
\forall\ {\mathbf{v}}_{1},{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1},\ {\mathbf{v}}_{3}\in\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#},\ \forall\,\theta\in I_{n}.

(7.12)

This implies that the trilinear form
\langle({\mathbf{v}}_{1}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{v}}_{2},{\mathbf{v}}_{3}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}
is bounded and continuous on
{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}\times{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}\times\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#},\ \forall\,\theta\in I_{n}
.
Taking into account that the space
\mathbf{L}_{\theta/(\theta-1)\#}
is dual to the space
\mathbf{L}_{\theta\#}
,
this implies that if

and the following estimate holds for the bilinear term

\displaystyle\|({\mathbf{v}}_{1}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{v}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\theta/(\theta-1)\#}}\leq{2\pi}C_{2\theta/(\theta-2)\#}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{1}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\#}}\|{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}_{\#}},\ \forall\,\theta\in I_{n}.

This means that the quadratic operator

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}}:{\mathbf{H}}^{1}_{\#}\to\mathbf{L}_{\theta/(\theta-1)\#},\ \forall\,\theta\in I_{n}

(7.13)

is bounded and continuous.

Moreover, if
{\mathbf{v}}_{1}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}
and
{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}({\mathbf{v}}_{1}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{v}}_{2}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\partial_{i}({v}_{1,i}{\mathbf{v}}_{2})dx-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}({\rm div}\,{\mathbf{v}}_{1}){\mathbf{v}}_{2}dx=0,

and hence

(7.15)

For

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}}:\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1}\to\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_{\#\sigma}^{*}=(\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{1})^{*}\cup\mathbf{L}_{n/(n-1)\#}.

(7.16)

is also bounded and continuous.

(7.17)

Assume now that
{\mathbf{v}}_{3}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}

{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}

This implies that the trilinear form

is bounded and continuous on
{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}\times{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}\times{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}

Taking into account that the space

THEOREM 7.5
.

Let

.

(i) If

(ii) If

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}}:{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}\to{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-1},

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}}:\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s}\to\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-1}

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathit{B}}{\mathbf{w}}\right\|_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-1}}\leq C_{n,s}\|{\mathbf{w}}\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}}\quad\forall\ {\mathbf{w}}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}.

\mathbf{w}

7.3.6

The following assertion is essentially an adaptation of a result from
[
Ge1979
, Lemma and Remark 2.3(ii)]
to the periodic setting for the particular case of
L_{2}
-based Sobolev spaces.

LEMMA 7.6
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\theta\in I_{n}
.
Then for any
u\in L_{\theta\#}
and any
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
c_{\epsilon}(u)>0
depending only on
u
,
n
, and
\epsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}\,|w|^{2}dx\leq\epsilon\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\#}}+c_{\epsilon}(u)\|w\|^{2}_{L_{2\#}}\quad\forall\ w\in H^{1}_{\#}.

(7.34)

Proof.

Let us recall that
{q_{\theta}}:=2\theta/(\theta-2)>2
, see (
7.8
) and (
7.9
).
Due to the Sobolev embedding theorem, the space
H^{1}_{\#}
is continuously embedded in
L_{{q_{\theta}}\#}
and hence
w\in L_{{q_{\theta}}\#}
.
By the Hölder inequality this implies that
|u|^{2}\,|w|^{2}\in L_{1\#}
and integral in the left hand side of (
7.34
) is bounded.

Let us employ the contradiction argument and assume that there exist
u\in L_{\theta\#}
and
\epsilon>0
such that for any constant
c>0
there exists
w_{c}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}\,|w_{c}|^{2}dx>\epsilon\|w_{c}\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\#}}+c\|w_{c}\|^{2}_{L_{2\#}}.

(7.35)

Inequality (
7.35
) does not hold if
w_{c}=0
a.e. on
{\mathbb{T}}
, hence we can assume that
\|w_{c}\|_{H^{1}_{\#}}\neq 0
.
Inequality (
7.35
) then implies that for any
c
it will also hold for

\widetilde{w}_{c}=w_{c}/\|w_{c}\|_{H^{1}_{\#}}
.
We evidently have

\|\widetilde{w}_{c}\|_{H^{1}_{\#}}=1
and by the Sobolev embedding theorem
\|\widetilde{w}_{c}\|_{L_{{q_{\theta}}\#}}
is bounded by a constant that does not depend on
c
.
By the Hölder inequality for
u\in L_{\theta\#}
and
\widetilde{w}_{c}\in L_{{q_{\theta}}\#}
, we have,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L_{\theta\#}}\|\widetilde{w}_{c}\|^{2}_{L_{{q_{\theta}}\#}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}\,|\widetilde{w}_{c}|^{2}dx>\epsilon+c\|\widetilde{w}_{c}\|^{2}_{L_{2\#}}.

(7.36)

Choosing
c\in{\mathbb{N}}
and taking limit of (
7.36
) as
c\to\infty
, the inequality implies that

\displaystyle{\||\widetilde{w}_{c}|^{2}\|_{L_{1\#}}=\|\widetilde{w}_{c}\|^{2}_{L_{2\#}}}\to 0\quad\mbox{as }c\to\infty.

(7.37)

Note that
{q_{\theta}}/2>1
.
The boundedness of sequence
\|\widetilde{w}_{c}\|_{L_{{q_{\theta}}\#}}

is equivalent to the boundedness of the sequence

\||\widetilde{w}_{c}|^{2}\|_{L_{{q_{\theta}}/2\#}}
and
implies that there exists a subsequence of
\{|\widetilde{w}_{c}|^{2}\}
weakly converging in
L_{{q_{\theta}}/2\#}
(and hence in
L_{1\#}
) to a function
W\in L_{{q_{\theta}}/2\#}
,
and then by (
7.37
),
W=0
.
Then the integral in inequality (
7.36
) converges to zero, and the inequality implies that
\epsilon=0
,
which contradicts the
assumption
\epsilon>0
.
This implies (
7.34
).
∎

Let us now prove a stronger version of Lemma
7.6
, cf.
[
Ge1979
, Remark 2.3(iii)]
.

.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\theta\in I_{n}
.
Let
K_{\theta\#}
be a compact subset of
L_{\theta\#}
.
Then for
any
\delta>0
there exist constants
\widetilde{C}_{\delta}(K_{\theta\#})>0
and

(depending only on

,
\theta
,

and

\displaystyle\|uw\|_{L_{2\#}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|w\|_{H^{1}_{\#}}+\widetilde{C}_{\delta}{(K_{\theta\#})}\|u\|_{L_{\theta\#}}\|w\|_{L_{2\#}}

(7.38)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|w\|_{H^{1}_{\#}}+C_{\delta}(K_{\theta\#})\|w\|_{L_{2\#}}.

(7.39)

Proof.

Let us first prove that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist a constant
\tilde{c}_{\epsilon}>0
, depending only on
n
,
\theta
, and
\epsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}\,|w|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\#}}+\tilde{c}_{\epsilon}\|u\|^{2}_{L_{\theta\#}}\|w\|^{2}_{L_{2\#}}.

(7.40)

Let us recall that
{q_{\theta}}:=2\theta/(\theta-2)>2
, cf. (
7.8
) and (
7.9
).
For
u=0
or

inequality (
7.40
) evidently holds.
To prove that it holds also for
\|u\|_{L_{\theta\#}}\neq 0
and
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{\#}}\neq 0

In general, the distributional behavior of a high degree polynomial more closely resembles

For this discussion, we need to introduce yet another special class of polynomials.

Definition 1.5

The conclusion of Corollary

First, we allow for different sets

rather than a single set

Second, we make no assumptions about the constant terms of the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
.
These features are related to each other in the following way: if one takes
A_{i}=A_{0}+P_{i}(0)
for each

then

is equivalent to

having zero constant term.
So, the ability to take

is reflective of the fact that the result is invariant
under shifts of the polynomials

The independence of Corollary

stands in stark contrast with the polynomial Szemerédi theorem.
In
[

with zero constant terms
and any

consisting of polynomials of degree

there exists a nontrivial configuration

The role of zero constant term is to avoid “local obstructions.”
Indeed, a necessary (though weaker) assumption in order
for the conclusion of the polynomial Szemerédi theorem to hold
is that the polynomials

be

meaning that for every

have a common root mod

To see this, one may consider the sets

In the finite field setting in Corollary

because any element

is invertible in

The absence of local obstructions is behind the phenomenon of

Since local obstructions are not a factor in our setting,

for which the conclusion of Corollary
1.13
fails.
Let
P(y)=y^{p}-y
.
The polynomial
P
has
p
roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
In particular, every element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a root of
P
.
Moreover,
P
is an additive polynomial, so the image
H_{q}=P(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a subgroup of index
p
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}

for every
q=p^{k}
.
Taking
A_{0}=H_{q}
and
A_{1}
to be a nontrivial coset of
H_{q}
, it follows that

C=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}:x\in A_{0},x+P(y)\in A_{1}\}=\emptyset
.
This disagrees with the prediction from Corollary
1.13
that
C
should have cardinality

|C|=p^{-2}q^{2}+O_{q\to\infty;P}\left(q^{2-\gamma}\right)
.

1.3.
Proof strategy

The main technical ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.11
is the following general criterion,
which can be seen as a finitary version of the joint ergodicity criterion of Frantzikinakis
[
F23
]
(see also
[
BF22
]
),
based on the work of Peluse
[
P19
]
and Peluse and Prendiville
[
PP22a
]
:

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite abelian groups, and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}:G_{2}\to G_{1}
.
Suppose there exist
C>0
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

(i)

for any
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, any
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
, and any
\chi_{l+1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq C\left\|f_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}^{\alpha}+\delta_{1},

and

(ii)

for any
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
, not all the trivial character,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\chi_{i}(a_{i}(y))}}\right|\leq\delta_{2}.

Then there exist
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
such that for any
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\E_{z\in G_{1}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\ll_{m,C,s,\alpha}\delta_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}+\delta_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}.

We prove Theorem
1.14
in Section
2
.

It then remains to establish properties (i) and (ii) when
G_{1}=G_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
a_{i}=P_{i}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t])[y]

(with
\delta_{j}=q^{-\beta_{j}}
for some
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}>0
).

In the case that all of the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
have degree
d<p
,
property (i) follows from a standard PET induction argument; see, e.g.
[
Pr17
,
P19
]
.
Property (ii) follows from elementary estimates of exponential sums over finite fields given in
[
BBI21
]
;
see
[
BBI21
, Lemma 3]
and the final inequality appearing in its proof at the top of page 713 in
[
BBI21
]
.

Proving properties (i) and (ii) in the high degree case is more challenging.
In order to establish property (i), we use a new variant of PET induction
that is based on the derivational degrees of the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
.
This argument is carried out in Section
3
.
For condition (ii), we use a Furstenberg–Sárközy-type result proved in
[
AB23
]
;
see item (iv) of Theorem
1.10
above.

2.
Joint ergodicity criterion over finite abelian groups

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.14
, restated here:

See
1.14

Remark 2.1
.

The conclusion of Theorem
1.14
implies conditions (i) and (ii) for appropriate constants.
Suppose

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\E_{z\in G_{1}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq\varepsilon

for every
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
.
Then by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}}+\varepsilon\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq m}{\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}}+\varepsilon,

so (i) holds with
C=1
,
s=1
,
\alpha=1
, and
\delta_{1}=\varepsilon
.
Specializing to
f_{i}=\chi_{i}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
immediately gives (ii) with
\delta_{2}=\varepsilon
.

2.1.
Outline of the proof

In order to carry out a proof of Theorem
1.14
, we will prove the following statement,
which gives Theorem
1.14
in the case
l=m
:

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}:G_{2}\to G_{1}
satisy (i) and (ii).
Let
l\in\{0,\dots,m\}
.
There exist
\gamma_{l,1},\gamma_{l,2}>0
such that
for any
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and any
\chi_{l+1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\chi=1}\prod_{i=1}^{l}{\E_{z\in G_{1}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\ll_{l,C,s,\alpha}\delta_{1}^{\gamma_{l,1}}+\delta_{2}^{\gamma_{l,2}}.

The base case
l=0
follows from condition (ii).

We now briefly describe how to prove Proposition
2.2
for
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
given the result for
l-1
.
By the triangle inequality, it suffices to bound the quantity

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}

under the additional assumption that
\E_{z\in G_{1}}{f_{l}(z)}=0
.
Suppose the quantity in (
2.1
) is large.
We first show (Lemma
2.5
) that this remains true upon replacing
f_{l}
by a function of the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)=\E_{y\in G}{f_{0}(x-a_{l}(y))\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+(a_{i}-a_{l})(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}((a_{j}-a_{l})(y))}}

with
\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}
very small
(with the bound coming from the
l-1
case of Proposition
2.2
).
The condition (i) then implies that
\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}
is large.
The goal is then to show that the quantity
\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s-1}(G_{1})}
is also large
and to iterate the degree lowering process until we arrive at a contradiction
by showing that
\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}
must be large.

The degree lowering process is roughly as follows.
Largeness of the
U^{s}
-norm implies that (Proposition
2.6
)

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G_{1}^{s-2}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}

is large for some family
\left(\chi_{\mathbf{h}}\right)_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s-2}}
of characters on
G
.
We then swap the order of averaging in the definition of
\widetilde{f}_{l}
with the differencing operator
and deduce with the help of Lemma
2.8
that

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in G_{1}^{s-2}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{0}(x-a_{l}(y))\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{i}(x+(a_{i}-a_{l})(y))}\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}(x)}}\right|}

is large, where

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}=\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s-2}}{C^{|\omega|}\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{\omega}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{h}^{\omega}=\left(h^{\omega_{1}}_{1},\dots,h^{\omega_{s-2}}_{s-2}\right).

Replacing
x
by
x+a_{l}(y)
and using the induction hypothesis, this implies

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\left\{(\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1})\in G_{1}^{2(s-2)}:\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}=1\right\}\right|}{|G_{1}|^{2(s-2)}}

is large.
From the definition of
\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}
, this allows us to write the map
\mathbf{h}\mapsto\chi_{\mathbf{h}}

as a product of functions of fewer variables (at least on a large subset of the domain
G_{1}^{s-2}
).
Utilizing this extra structure of the family of characters
\left(\chi_{\mathbf{h}}\right)_{\mathbf{h}\in G_{1}^{s-2}}
,
we are able to conclude (by applying Lemma
2.7
) that
\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s-1}(G_{1})}
is large.

For a full proof of Proposition
2.2
,
we need to give precise meaning to “large” and “small” in the preceding discussion
and to obtain quantitative control of the losses in each step of the argument.
We gather the required technical lemmas in Subsections
2.2
–
2.6

and put everything together to prove Proposition
2.2
in Subsection
2.7
.

and

For each

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite abelian groups, and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}:G_{2}\to G_{1}
.
Let
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Suppose that for any
f_{1},\dots,f_{l-1}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and any
\chi_{l},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\chi=1}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\E_{z\in G_{1}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq\varepsilon.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and
\chi_{l+1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
.
Assume each of the functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{l-1}
is either constant or has mean zero.
If

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\geq\delta,

then there exists
f_{0}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\widetilde{f}_{l}(x+a_{l}(y))\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\geq\frac{\delta^{3}}{4}.

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)=\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x-a_{l}(y))\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+(a_{i}-a_{l})(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}((a_{j}-a_{l})(y))}}.

Moreover, if
\E_{x\in G_{1}}{f_{l}(x)}=0
, we may choose
f_{0}
so that

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)}\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle f_{0}(x)=\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{\overline{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\overline{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}}

so that

\displaystyle\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}}=\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}^{2}\geq\delta^{2}.

Decompose
f_{l}=f_{s}+f_{u}
according to Lemma
2.4
for the seminorm

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}f(x+a_{l}(y))\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}

Then by the triangle inequality and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle f_{s},\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\rangle\right|=\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}f_{s}(x+a_{l}(y))\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}}\right|\geq\delta(\delta-c).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle f_{s},\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\rangle\right|\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f_{s}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{*}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq c^{-1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\geq c\delta(\delta-c).

Taking
c=\frac{\delta}{2}
yields the desired bound.

We now compute
\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)}\right|
directly from the definition of
\widetilde{f}_{l}
:

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)}\right|=\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}}\right|.

If at least one of the functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{l-1}
has mean zero,

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}}\right|\leq\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq\varepsilon.

\displaystyle\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)}=\E_{x\in G_{1}}{f_{0}(x)}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{c_{i}}\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}},

and

\displaystyle\E_{x\in G_{1}}{f_{0}(x)}=\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\overline{c}_{i}}\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\overline{f}_{l}(x+a_{l}(y))\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\overline{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}}}.

2.4.
Gowers norms and correlations with characters

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and let
f:G\to\mathbb{D}
.
There is a family
\left(\chi_{\mathbf{h}}\right)_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}
of characters on
G
such that

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\geq\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+2}(G)}^{2^{s+2}}.

Proof.

By definition,

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+2}(G)}^{2^{s+2}}=\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left\|\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f\right\|_{U^{2}(G)}^{4}}.

Now, for each
\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f\right\|_{U^{2}(G)}^{4}=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}{\left|\widehat{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f}(\chi)\right|^{4}}\leq\sup_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}{\left|\widehat{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f}(\chi)\right|^{2}}\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2},

so we may choose
\chi_{\mathbf{h}}\in\widehat{G}
satisfying

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|^{2}\geq\left\|\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f\right\|_{U^{2}(G)}^{4}.

Using the fact that
f
is 1-bounded, we then have

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\geq\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|^{2}}\geq\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left\|\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f\right\|_{U^{2}(G)}^{4}}=\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+2}(G)}^{2^{s+2}}.

∎

If the map
\mathbf{h}\mapsto\chi_{\mathbf{h}}
is of low rank, then the
U^{s+1}
norm must also be large:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and let
f:G\to\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}:G^{s-1}\to\widehat{G}
,
m\leq s
, and define

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathbf{h}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\varphi_{i}\left((h_{j})_{j\neq i}\right)}

for
\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}
.
Then

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G)}^{2^{s-m}}.

Proof.

For
m=0
,

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)}\right|}\leq\left(\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)}\right|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}=\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G)}^{2^{s}}.

Now let
m\geq 1
.
Letting
\psi(\mathbf{h})
be the conjugate of the phase of the absolute value on the left hand side, we have

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s}}

\displaystyle{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}

\displaystyle=\E_{h_{2},\dots,h_{s}\in G}{\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{h_{2},\dots,h_{s}}\overline{f(x)}\varphi_{1}(h_{2},\dots,h_{s})(x)\E_{h_{1}\in G}{\Delta_{h_{2},\dots,h_{s}}f(x+h_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{m}{\varphi_{i}\left((h_{j})_{j\neq i}\right)(x)}\psi(h_{1},\dots,h_{s})}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\E_{h_{2},\dots,h_{s}\in G}{\E_{x\in G}{\left|\E_{h_{1}\in G}{\Delta_{h_{2},\dots,h_{s}}f(x+h_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{m}{\varphi_{i}\left((h_{j})_{j\neq i}\right)(x)}\psi(h_{1},\dots,h_{s})}\right|^{2}}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\E_{h_{2},\dots,h_{s}\in G}{\E_{h_{1},h^{\prime}_{1}\in G}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{h_{1}-h^{\prime}_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{s}}f(x)\prod_{i=2}^{m}{\varphi_{i}\left((h_{j})_{j\neq i}\right)(x)\overline{\varphi_{i}\left((h^{\prime}_{j})_{j\neq i}\right)(x)}}}\right|}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{h^{\prime}_{1}\in G}{\E_{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{s}\in G}{\left|\E_{x\in G}{\Delta_{h_{1}-h^{\prime}_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{s}}f(x)\prod_{i=2}^{m}{\varphi_{i}\left((h_{j})_{j\neq i}\right)(x)\overline{\varphi_{i}\left((h^{\prime}_{j})_{j\neq i}\right)(x)}}}\right|}}\right)^{1/2}.

By induction, this quantity is bounded above by

\displaystyle\left(\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G)}^{2^{s-(m-1)}}\right)^{1/2}=\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G)}^{2^{s-m}}.

∎

2.5.
Difference interchange lemma

A version of the following lemma appears as the “dual-difference interchange” lemma
in
[
PP22b
, Lemma 6.3]
and
[
Pr20
, Lemma 6.3]
:

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite abelian groups.
Let
f:G_{1}\times G_{2}\to\mathbb{D}
, and define
F(x)=\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f(x,y)}
.
For any
s\geq 0
, any collection
\left(\chi_{\mathbf{h}}\right)_{\mathbf{h}\in G_{1}^{s}}
of characters on
G_{1}
,
and any subset
\mathcal{H}\subseteq G_{1}^{s}
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}F(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\right)^{2^{s}}\leq\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{2s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f(x,y)\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}(x)}}\right|},

where

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}=\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{C^{|\omega|}\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{\omega}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{h}^{\omega}=\left(h^{\omega_{1}}_{1},\dots,h^{\omega_{s}}_{s}\right).

Proof.

We prove the inequality (
2.2
) by induction.

When
s=0
, both sides of (
2.2
) are equal to

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{F(x)\chi(x)}\right|=\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f(x,y)\chi(x)}}\right|.

Suppose
s\geq 1
.
Note that for
h\in G_{1}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}F(x)=\E_{y,y^{\prime}\in G_{2}}{f(x+h,y)\overline{f(x,y^{\prime})}}.

We break up the sum over
\mathcal{H}
into an iterated sum (we write
\mathcal{H}^{h}=\left\{\mathbf{h}\in G^{s-1}:(\mathbf{h},h)\in\mathcal{H}\right\}
)
and apply the induction hypothesis with the functions
f_{h}:G_{1}\times G_{2}^{2}\to\mathbb{D}
given by

f_{h}(x,y,y^{\prime})=f(x+h,y)\overline{f(x,y^{\prime})}
:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}F(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\right)^{2^{s}}

\displaystyle=\left(\E_{h\in G_{1}}{\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{s-1}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{h}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}\Delta_{h}F(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h},h}(x)}\right|}}\right)^{2^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\E_{h\in G_{1}}{\left(\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{s-1}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{h}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}\Delta_{h}F(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h},h}(x)}\right|}\right)^{2^{s-1}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\E_{h\in G_{1}}{\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{2(s-1)}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{h}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y,y^{\prime}\in G_{2}}{\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{h}(x,y,y^{\prime})\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h}(x)}}\right|}}\right)^{2}.

Let
\psi\left(\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h\right)
be the conjugate of the phase of the absolute value.
Then we move the average over
h
to the inside and apply Cauchy–Schwarz:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}F(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\right)^{2^{s}}

\displaystyle~=\left(\E_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in G_{1}^{s-1}}{\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y,y^{\prime}\in G_{2}}{\frac{1}{|G_{1}|}\sum_{(\mathbf{h}^{i},h)\in\mathcal{H}}{\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{h}(x,y,y^{\prime})\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h}(x)\psi\left(\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h\right)}}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle~\leq\E_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in G_{1}^{s-1}}{\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\left|\frac{1}{|G_{1}|}\sum_{(\mathbf{h}^{i},h)\in\mathcal{H}}{\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f(x+h,y)\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h}(x)\psi\left(\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h\right)}\right|^{2}}}},

since

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in G_{1}^{s-1}}{\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y^{\prime}\in G_{2}}{\left|\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f(x,y^{\prime})\right|^{2}}}}\leq 1.

Expanding out the squared term,

\displaystyle\left(\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G_{1}^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}F(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\right)^{2^{s}}

\displaystyle~\leq\E_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in G_{1}^{s-1}}{\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{2}}\sum_{(\mathbf{h}^{i},h_{j})\in\mathcal{H}}{\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f(x+h_{0},y)\overline{f(x+h_{1},y)}}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{{{{\left(\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h_{0}}\overline{\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h_{1}}}\right)(x)\psi\left(\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h_{0}\right)\overline{\psi\left(\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1},h_{1}\right)}}}}}

\displaystyle~\leq\E_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in G_{1}^{s-1}}{\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{2}}\sum_{(\mathbf{h}^{i},h_{j})\in\mathcal{H}}{\left|\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}\Delta^{(1)}_{h_{0}-h_{1}}f(x,y)\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},h_{0};\mathbf{h}^{1},h_{1}}(x)\right|}}.

∎

2.6.
Degree lowering

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite abelian groups, and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}:G_{2}\to G_{1}
.
Let
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Suppose that for any
f_{1},\dots,f_{l-1}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and any
\chi_{l},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\chi=1}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\E_{z\in G}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq\varepsilon.

Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and
\chi_{l+1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
, and put

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)=\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x-a_{l}(y))\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+(a_{i}-a_{l})(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}((a_{j}-a_{l})(y))}}.

If

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+2}(G_{1})}\geq\delta,

then

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}\geq\left(\frac{\delta^{2^{2s+4}}}{2^{2^{s+2}}}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)\cdot\frac{\delta^{2^{s+2}}}{2}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.6

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G_{1}^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\geq\delta^{2^{s+2}}

for some family
\left(\chi_{\mathbf{h}}\right)_{\mathbf{h}\in G_{1}^{s}}
of characters on
G_{1}
.
Therefore, there exists a subset
\mathcal{H}\subseteq G_{1}^{s}
of size
|\mathcal{H}|\geq\frac{\delta^{2^{s+2}}}{2}|G_{1}|^{s}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|\geq\frac{\delta^{2^{s+2}}}{2}

for every
\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Applying Lemma
2.8
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{2s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{0}(x-a_{l}(y))\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{i}(x+(a_{i}-a_{l})(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\widetilde{\chi}_{j}(a_{j}(y))}\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}(x)}}\right|}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\delta^{2^{s+2}}}{2}\right)^{2^{s+1}}=\frac{\delta^{2^{2s+3}}}{2^{2^{s+1}}},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}_{j}=\begin{cases}\chi_{j},&s=0\\
1,&s\geq 1.\end{cases}

After the substituting
x+a_{l}(y)
for
x
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{2s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{0}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\widetilde{\chi}_{j}(a_{j}(y))}\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}(x+a_{l}(y))}}\right|}\geq\frac{\delta^{2^{2s+3}}}{2^{2^{s+1}}}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and 1-boundedness of
f_{0}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{2s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}}{\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\left|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\widetilde{\chi}_{j}(a_{j}(y))}\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}(a_{l}(y))}\right|^{2}}}\geq\frac{\delta^{2^{2s+4}}}{2^{2^{s+2}}}.

By the hypothesis, for each
\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\left|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\widetilde{\chi}_{j}(a_{j}(y))}\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}(a_{l}(y))}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{\chi}=1}\mathbbm{1}_{\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}=1}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\E_{z\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}^{0}-\mathbf{h}^{1}}f_{i}(z)}}\right|^{2}}\leq\varepsilon^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\left\{(\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1})\in\mathcal{H}^{2}:\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}=1\right\}\right|\geq\left(\frac{\delta^{2^{2s+4}}}{2^{2^{s+2}}}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)\cdot|G_{1}|^{2s},

so there exists
\mathbf{h}^{1}\in\mathcal{H}
such that the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\left\{\mathbf{h}^{0}\in\mathcal{H}:\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}=1\right\}

has cardinality

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right|\geq\left(\frac{\delta^{2^{2s+4}}}{2^{2^{s+2}}}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)\cdot|G_{1}|^{s}.

Now, for
\mathbf{h}^{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0}}=\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0}}\overline{\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0},\mathbf{h}^{1}}}=\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}}{\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{\omega}}}.

Since
\mathbf{h}^{1}
is fixed, (
2.3
) represents
\chi_{\mathbf{h}^{0}}

as a product of functions depending on at most
s-1
of the variables
(h^{0}_{1},\dots,h^{0}_{s})
.
Define
\chi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{h}}
by the expression on the right hand side of (
2.3
).
We have
\chi_{\mathbf{h}}=\chi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{h}}
for
\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, and the map
\mathbf{h}\mapsto\chi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{h}}

satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2.7
with
m=s
.
Thus, by Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}\geq\E_{\mathbf{h}\in G_{1}^{s}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\geq\frac{1}{|G_{1}|^{s}}\sum_{\mathbf{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}{\left|\E_{x\in G_{1}}{\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f(x)\chi_{\mathbf{h}}(x)}\right|}\geq\left(\frac{\delta^{2^{2s+4}}}{2^{2^{s+2}}}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)\cdot\frac{\delta^{2^{s+2}}}{2}.

∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be finite abelian groups, and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}:G_{2}\to G_{1}
.
Let
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Suppose that for any
f_{1},\dots,f_{l-1}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and any
\chi_{l},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\chi=1}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{\E_{z\in G_{1}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq\varepsilon.

Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and
\chi_{l+1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
, and put

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)=\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x-a_{l}(y))\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+(a_{i}-a_{l})(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}((a_{j}-a_{l})(y))}}.

For any
s\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
(depending only on
s
) such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}\ll_{s}\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}^{1/m}+\varepsilon^{1/n}.

Proof.

For
s=1
, there is nothing to show.
Suppose (
2.4
) holds for some
s\geq 1
.
By Lemma
2.9
,

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}^{4^{s+1}}}{2^{2^{s+1}}}-\varepsilon^{2}\right)\frac{\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}^{2^{s+1}}}{2}

\displaystyle\gg_{s}\left(\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}-2^{1/2^{s+1}}\varepsilon^{1/2^{2s+1}}\right)^{4^{s+1}}\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}^{2^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}-2^{1/2^{s+1}}\varepsilon^{1/2^{2s+1}}\right)^{4^{s+1}+2^{s+1}}.

Rearranging, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}\ll_{s}\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}^{1/m^{\prime}}+\varepsilon^{1/n^{\prime}}

for

\displaystyle m^{\prime}=4^{s+1}+2^{s+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad n^{\prime}=2^{2s+1}.

By the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}^{1/m^{\prime}}\ll_{s}\left(\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}^{1/m}+\varepsilon^{1/n}\right)^{1/m^{\prime}}\leq\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}^{1/(mm^{\prime})}+\varepsilon^{1/(nm^{\prime})}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G_{1})}\ll\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}^{1/(mm^{\prime})}+\varepsilon^{1/n^{\prime\prime}},

where
n^{\prime\prime}=\max\{nm^{\prime},n^{\prime}\}
.
∎

2.7.
Proof of joint ergodicity criterion

We now carry out the argument outlined in Subsection
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Suppose
l=0
.
We want to show: for any
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\chi=1}\right|\leq\varepsilon_{0}

with
\varepsilon_{0}=O\left(\delta_{1}^{\gamma_{0,1}}+\delta_{2}^{\gamma_{0,2}}\right)
.
If
\chi_{1}=\dots=\chi_{m}=1
, then the left hand side of (
2.5
) is equal to 0.
If, on the other hand, at least one of the characters
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}
is nontrivial,
(
2.5
) reduces to property (ii) with
\varepsilon_{0}=\delta_{2}
.

Now suppose
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and Proposition
2.2
holds for
l-1
.
Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
and
\chi_{l+1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
.
We want to show

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\chi=1}\prod_{i=1}^{l}{\E_{z\in G_{1}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\leq\varepsilon_{l}

with
\varepsilon_{l}=O\left(\delta_{1}^{\gamma_{l,1}}+\delta_{2}^{\gamma_{l,2}}\right)
.
Suppose (
2.6
) fails.
Then for
f^{\prime}_{l}=f_{l}-\E_{x\in G_{1}}{f_{l}(x)}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}f^{\prime}_{l}(x+a_{l}(y))\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\geq\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l-1},

where
\varepsilon_{l-1}=O\left(\delta_{1}^{\gamma_{l-1},1}+\delta_{2}^{\gamma_{l-2},2}\right)

is the bound coming from the induction hypothesis.
Combining with Lemma
2.3
, we may assume that each of the functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{l-1}

is either constant or has mean zero,
f_{l}
has mean zero, and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l-1}}{2^{2l-1}}.

(Note that the extra factor of
2^{l}
when compared with the statement of Lemma
2.3

comes from dividing the modified functions by
2
in order to preserve 1-boundedness of the functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}
.)

By Lemma
2.5
, there exists
f_{0}:G_{1}\to\mathbb{D}
such that the function

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{l}(x)=\E_{y\in G_{2}}{f_{0}(x-a_{l}(y))\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+(a_{i}-a_{l})(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}((a_{j}-a_{l})(y))}}

satisfies

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{1}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}\leq\varepsilon_{l-1}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in G_{2}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}{f_{i}(x+a_{i}(y))}\widetilde{f}_{l}(x+a_{l}(y))\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(a_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(G_{1})}\geq\frac{\delta^{3}}{4}

with

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{\varepsilon_{l}-\varepsilon_{l-1}}{2^{2l-1}}.

By property (i), it follows that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}\geq C^{-1/\alpha}\left(\frac{\delta^{3}}{4}-\delta_{1}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

On the other hand, by Corollary
2.10
, there are
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(G_{1})}\ll_{s}\left\|\widetilde{f}_{l}\right\|_{U^{1}(G_{1})}^{1/m}+\varepsilon_{l-1}^{1/n}.

Combining with (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\delta^{3}-4\delta^{1}\right)^{1/\alpha}\ll_{C,s,\alpha}\varepsilon_{l-1}^{1/m}+\varepsilon_{l-1}^{1/n}\ll\varepsilon_{l-1}^{1/r},

where
r=\max\{m,n\}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{l}\ll\varepsilon_{l-1}+\delta\ll\varepsilon_{l-1}^{\alpha/3r}+\delta_{1}^{1/3}.

By induction, it follows that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{l}\ll\delta_{1}^{\gamma_{l,1}}+\delta_{2}^{\gamma_{l,2}}

for

\displaystyle\gamma_{l,1}=\min\left\{\frac{\alpha}{3r}\gamma_{l-1,1},\frac{1}{3}\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma_{l,2}=\frac{\alpha}{3r}\gamma_{l-1,2}.

∎

Remark 2.11
.

It follows from the proof that in the conclusion of Theorem
1.14
, we can take

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{\alpha}{3r}\right)^{s-2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma_{2}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{3r}\right)^{s-1}

with
r
depending exponentially on
s
.

3.
Gowers norm estimates

Will we prove Theorem
1.11
using the joint ergodicity criterion
(Theorem
1.14
).
As a first step, we therefore must control averages of the form

\displaystyle\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}

by an appropriate Gowers norm.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t])[y]
be nonconstant, essentially distinct polynomials.
There exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
such that
for any
q=p^{k}
and any
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq C_{1}\min_{1\leq i\leq m}{\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}}+C_{2}q^{-\beta}.

The key estimates in the proof of Proposition
3.1
are given by the next two lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and let
H\leq G
be a subgroup.
For any
f:G\to\mathbb{D}
and any
s\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\E_{h\in H}{\left\|\Delta_{h}f\right\|_{U^{s}(G)}^{2^{s}}}\leq[G:H]\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G)}^{2^{s+1}}.

Proof.

Suppose
s=1
.
Expanding the left hand side and taking a Fourier transform, we have

\displaystyle\E_{h\in H}{\left\|\Delta_{h}f\right\|_{U^{1}(G)}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\E_{h\in H}{\E_{v\in G}{\E_{u\in G}{f(u+v+h)\overline{f(u+v)}\overline{f(u+h)}f(u)}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3},\chi_{4}\in\widehat{G}}{\widehat{f}(\chi_{1})\overline{\widehat{f}(\chi_{2}}\overline{\widehat{f}(\chi_{3})}\widehat{f}(\chi_{4})\E_{h\in H}{(\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{2})(h)}\E_{v\in G}{(\chi_{1}\overline{\chi_{3}})(v)}\E_{u\in G}{(\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{2}\overline{\chi}_{3}\chi_{4})(u)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}{\left(\sum_{\lambda\in H^{\perp}}{\left|\widehat{f}(\chi\lambda)\right|^{2}}\right)\left|\widehat{f}(\chi)\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}{\left|\widehat{f}(\chi)\right|^{4}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}{\left(\sum_{\lambda\in H^{\perp}}{\left|\widehat{f}(\chi\lambda)\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|H^{\perp}\right|\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}{\left|\widehat{f}(\chi)\right|^{4}}

\displaystyle=[G:H]\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}(G)}^{4}.

Now suppose
s\geq 2
.
Then applying the
s=1
case above, we have

\displaystyle\E_{h\in H}{\left\|\Delta_{h}f\right\|_{U^{s}(G)}^{2^{s}}}

\displaystyle=\E_{h\in H}{\E_{\mathbf{v}\in G^{s-1}}{\left\|\Delta_{\mathbf{v}}\Delta_{h}f\right\|_{U^{1}(G)}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\E_{\mathbf{v}\in G^{s-1}}{\left(\E_{h\in H}{\left\|\Delta_{h}\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{v}}f\right)\right\|_{U^{1}(G)}^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle=[G:H]\cdot\E_{\mathbf{v}\in G^{s-1}}{\left\|\Delta_{\mathbf{v}}f\right\|_{U^{2}(G)}^{4}}

\displaystyle=[G:H]\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s+1}(G)}^{2^{s+1}}.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t])[y]
be nonconstant, essentially distinct polynomials.
Let
q=p^{k}
.
Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{D}
.
For
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{u}=\{P_{i}(y):1\leq i\leq m\}\cup\{P_{i}(y+u):\text{d-}\deg{P_{i}}>1\}=\{P_{u,1},\dots,P_{u,l}\},

and define

\displaystyle f_{u,j}(x)=\begin{cases}f_{i}(x),&\text{if}~P_{u,j}(y)=P_{i}(y+u)~\text{and}~\text{d-}\deg{P_{i}}>1;\\
\overline{f_{i}(x)},&\text{if}~P_{u,j}(y)=P_{i}(y)~\text{and}~\text{d-}\deg{P_{i}}>1;\\
f_{i}(x+P_{i}(u)-P_{i}(0))\overline{f_{i}(x)}&\text{if}~P_{u,j}(y)=P_{i}(y)~\text{and}~\text{d-}\deg{P_{i}}=1\end{cases}

for
j=2,\dots,l
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2}\leq\E_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{j=2}^{l}{f_{u,j}\left(x+(P_{u,j}-P_{u,1})(y)\right)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}.

Proof.

Expanding the left hand side,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2}=\E_{x,y,u}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y+u))}\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\overline{f_{j}(x+P_{j}(y))}}}.

Using the definition of
f_{u,j}
and then shifting by
P_{u,1}(y)
, this is equal to

\displaystyle\E_{x,y,u}{\prod_{j=1}^{l}{f_{u,j}(x+P_{u,j}(y))}}

\displaystyle=\E_{u}{\E_{x}{\left(f_{u,1}(x)\cdot\E_{y}{\prod_{j=2}^{l}{f_{u,j}\left(x+(P_{u,j}-P_{u,1})(y)\right)}}\right)}},

and the desired inequality follows by an application of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality together with the bound

\left\|f_{u,1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq\left\|f_{u,1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq 1
.
∎

We prove Proposition
3.1
using a version of PET induction.
The appropriate version of PET induction in our context depends on the derivational degrees
of the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
rather than their degrees.
We therefore need a notion of leading term that is different from the usual meaning.
In particular, for a polynomial
P(y)\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t])[y]
, we write
P(y)=P_{k}(y)+P_{<k}(y)
,
where each monomial in
P_{k}(y)
is of derivational degree
k=\text{d-}\deg{P}

and
P_{<k}
has derivational degree
\text{d-}\deg{P_{<k}}<k
.
We then call
P_{k}
the
derivational leading term
of
P
.

To any family of polynomials
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\}\subseteq(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t])[y]
,
we assign a
weight

w=w(\mathcal{P})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathbb{N}}
,
where
w_{i}
is the number of distinct derivational leading terms of deriviational degree
i
.
We order weight vectors using the anti-lexicographic ordering.
That is,
w<w^{\prime}
if there is some
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
w_{d}<w^{\prime}_{d}
and
w_{i}=w^{\prime}_{i}
for
i>d
.
We will induct on the weight of the family
\mathcal{P}
in order to prove Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Since the statement of Proposition
3.1
is symmetric in the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
,
it suffices to prove

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq C_{1}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}q^{-\beta}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
.

For a family of polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
, let
\text{d-}\deg{(P_{1},\dots,P_{m})}=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}{\text{d-}\deg{P_{i}}}
.
Following Leibman
[
L05
]
, we say that a family of essentially distinct polynomials is
standard

if
\text{d-}\deg{P_{1}}=\text{d-}\deg{(P_{1},\dots,P_{m})}
.
We will first establish the inequality (
3.1
) for standard families.

Suppose
P(y)\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t])[y]
is an additive polynomial, say
P(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}{a_{j}p^{j}}
.
Let
H=\left\{P(y):y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Since
P
is a polynomial of degree
p^{N}
, it has at most
p^{N}
roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Hence,
[\mathbb{F}_{q}:H]\leq p^{N}
.
Expanding a function
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{D}
as a Fourier series, one can show

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f\left(x+P(y)\right)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2}=\sum_{\chi\in H^{\perp}}{\left|\widehat{f}(\chi)\right|^{2}},

where
H^{\perp}=\left\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}}:\chi|_{H}=1\right\}
.
By
[
R90
, Section 2.1]
, we have an isomorphism
H^{\perp}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}/H}
, so
|H^{\perp}|=[\mathbb{F}_{q}:H]
.
Then by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\chi\in H^{\perp}}{\left|\widehat{f}(\chi)\right|^{2}}\right)^{2}\leq|H^{\perp}|\sum_{\chi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\left|\widehat{f}(\chi)\right|^{4}}\leq p^{N}\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{4}.

Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
),

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f\left(x+P(y)\right)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq p^{N/4}\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}.

This proves the base case corresponding to the weight vector
(1,0,\dots,0)
.

Suppose (
3.1
) holds for all standard families with weight
<w(\mathcal{P})
.
Let
i_{0}\in\{2,\dots,m\}
such that
P_{i_{0}}
has minimal degree
and, if possible, such that the derivational leading term of
P_{i_{0}}
is different from the derivational leading term of
P_{1}
.
We apply Lemma
3.3
with
P_{u,1}=P_{i_{0}}
and
P_{u,2}(y)=P_{1}(y+u)
to obtain the estimate

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2}\leq\E_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{j=2}^{l}{f_{u,j}\left(x+(P_{u,j}-P_{u,1})(y)\right)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}.

(In the case that all of the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
have
\text{d-}\deg{P_{i}}=1
, we take
P_{u,2}=P_{1}
instead,
and the remainder of the argument works out the same.)

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u}=\left\{P_{u,2}-P_{u,1},\dots,P_{u,l}-P_{u,1}\right\}

be the family of polynomials appearing on the right hand side of (
3.4
).
We claim
w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u})<w(\mathcal{P})
.
Fix
i\in\{2,\dots,l\}
.
If
\text{d-}\deg{P_{u,i}}>\text{d-}\deg{P_{u,1}}
, then the derivational leading term of
P_{u,i}-P_{u,1}

is the same as the derivational leading term of
P_{u,i}
.
If
\text{d-}\deg{P_{u,i}}=\text{d-}\deg{P_{u,1}}
but
P_{u,i}
and
P_{u,1}
have different derivational leading terms,
then the derivational leading term of
P_{u,i}-P_{u,1}
is equal to the difference
of the derivational leading terms of
P_{u,i}
and
P_{u,1}
.
Finally, if
P_{u_{i}}
and
P_{u,1}
have the same derivational leading term,
then
\text{d-}\deg{(P_{u,i}-P_{u,1})}<\text{d-}\deg{P_{u,i}}
.
Therefore, the
j
th coordinate of the weight vector
w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u})
agrees with
the
j
th coordinate of
w(\mathcal{P}_{u})
for
j>\text{d-}\deg{P_{i_{0}}}
and is one smaller for
j=\text{d-}\deg{P_{i_{0}}}
.
Noting that
w(\mathcal{P}_{u})=w(\mathcal{P})
, we have
w(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u})<w(\mathcal{P})
as claimed.

The family
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u}
is also standard:

\text{d-}\deg{P_{u,2}}=\text{d-}\deg{P_{1}}=\text{d-}\deg{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u}}
, unless all of the polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}

have the same derivational leading term, in which case
\text{d-}\deg{P_{u,2}}=\text{d-}\deg{P_{1}}-1=\text{d-}\deg{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u}}
.

Therefore, by the induction hypothesis, for any
u\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y}{\prod_{j=2}^{l}{f_{u,j}\left(x+(P_{u,j}-P_{u,1})(y)\right)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}1)}\leq C_{1}\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}q^{-\beta}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q}\sum_{u\neq 0}{\left(C_{1}\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}q^{-\beta}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\E_{u}{\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}\right)^{\alpha}+2C_{2}q^{-\beta}.

It remains to bound
\E_{u}{\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
in terms of a Gowers norm of
f_{1}
.
If
\text{d-}\deg{P_{1}}>1
and
P_{u,2}(y)=P_{1}(y+u)
, then
f_{u,2}=f_{1}
,
so in particular,
\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}=\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq C_{1}^{1/2}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha/2}+\sqrt{2}C_{2}^{1/2}q^{-\beta/2}.

Suppose instead that
\text{d-}\deg{P_{1}}=1
and
P_{u,2}=P_{1}
.
Write
P_{1}(y)=\eta(y)+c
with
\eta(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}{a_{i}y^{p^{i}}}
an additive polynomial of degree
p^{N}
.
Then
f_{u,2}(x)=f_{1}(x+\eta(u))\overline{f_{1}(x)}=\Delta_{\eta(u)}f_{1}(x)
, so

\displaystyle\E_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}=\E_{h\in H}{\left\|\Delta_{h}f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}\leq\left(\E_{h\in H}{\left\|\Delta_{h}f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2^{s}}}\right)^{1/2^{s}},

where
H=\eta(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Note that
[\mathbb{F}_{q}:H]=\left|\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:\eta(y)=0\right\}\right|\leq p^{N}
.
Hence, by Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\E_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}\leq\left(p^{N}\cdot\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2^{s+1}}\right)^{1/2^{s}}=p^{N/2^{s}}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq p^{\alpha N/2^{s+1}}C_{1}^{1/2}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s+1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}+\sqrt{2}C_{2}^{1/2}q^{-\beta/2}.

We have shown that for any standard family
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\}
,
there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq C_{1}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}q^{-\beta/2}.

Now suppose
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\}
is not standard.
Let
i_{0}\in\{2,\dots,m\}
such that
\text{d-}\deg{P_{i_{0}}}=\text{d-}\deg{\mathcal{P}}>1
.
By Lemma
3.3
with
P_{u,1}=P_{i_{0}}
and
P_{u,2}=P_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{2}\leq\E_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{j=2}^{l}{f_{u,j}\left(x+(P_{u,j}-P_{u,1})(y)\right)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}.

The family

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u}=\left\{P_{u,2}-P_{u,1},\dots,P_{u,l}-P_{u,1}\right\}

is standard since
\text{d-}\deg{(P_{u,2}-P_{u,1})}=\text{d-}\deg{(P_{1}-P_{i_{0}})}=\text{d-}\deg{P_{i_{0}}}=\text{d-}\deg{\mathcal{P}}\geq\text{d-}\deg{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{u}}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y}{\prod_{j=2}^{l}{f_{u,j}\left(x+(P_{u,j}-P_{u,1})(y)\right)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq C_{1}\left\|f_{u,2}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}q^{-\beta}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
.
Taking an average over
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and arguing as above,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{U^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha^{\prime}}+C^{\prime}_{2}q^{-\beta^{\prime}}.

∎

4.
Asymptotic joint ergodicity for polynomial sequences

We are now set to prove Theorem
1.11
.

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

We will use Theorem
1.14
.
First, by Proposition
3.1
,
there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\alpha,\beta_{1}\in(0,1]
such that
for any
q=p^{k}
, any
l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, any
f_{1},\dots,f_{l}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{D}
,
and any
\chi_{l+1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}\prod_{j=l+1}^{m}{\chi_{j}(P_{j}(y))}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq C_{1}\left\|f_{l}\right\|_{U^{s}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}^{\alpha}+C_{2}q^{-\beta_{1}}.

That is, property (i) in Theorem
1.14
is satisfied, with
\delta_{1}=C_{2}q^{-\beta_{1}}
.

Next, by
[
AB23
, Theorem 1.10]
,
there exist
C_{3}>0
and
\beta_{2}\in(0,1]
such that, for any linear combination
P(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{s_{i}P_{i}(y)}
,
any
q=p^{k}
, and any
f:\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f(x+P(y))}-\E_{z\in H_{q}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})}{f(x+a_{0}+z)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\leq C_{3}q^{-\beta_{2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})},

where
a_{0}=P(0)
and
H_{q}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

generated by
\left\{P(y)-a_{0}:y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}
.
Given
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{m}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, there exist
s_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]
such that
\chi_{i}(x)=e(s_{i}x/Q)
,

Q(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]^{+}
irreducible with
|Q|=q
.
Taking

, we then have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\chi_{i}(P_{i}(y))}}-e(a_{0}/Q)\mathbbm{1}_{H_{q}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})^{\perp}}(1)\right|\leq C_{3}q^{-\beta_{2}}.

We claim
H_{q}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})=\mathbb{F}_{q}
for all sufficiently large
q
and all
s_{1},\dots,s_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
not all zero.
Suppose not.
Let

\displaystyle A=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}((t^{-1}))/\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]\right)^{m}:e\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{P_{i}(y)x_{i}}\right)=1~\text{for all}~y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]\right\}.

Since
x\mapsto e(x)
is a continuous homomorphism, the set
A
is a closed subgroup.
The assumption that
H_{q}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})\neq\mathbb{F}_{q}
for arbitrarily large
q
and
s_{1},\dots,s_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
not all zero
means that
A
contains infinitely many rational points.
Since
A
is a compact group, it follows that
A
is uncountable.
In particular,
A
contains a point
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})
with at least one
\alpha_{i}
irrational.
This contradicts the assumption that
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\}
is good for irrational equidistribution,
so the claim holds.

By the claim, the inequality (
4.1
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\left|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\chi_{i}(P_{i}(y))}}\right|\leq C_{3}q^{-\beta_{2}}.

Hence, property (ii) in Theorem
1.14
is satisfied, with
\delta_{2}=C_{3}q^{-\beta_{2}}
.

By Theorem
1.14
, there exist
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\E_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\ll_{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}}(C_{2}q^{-\beta_{1}})^{\gamma_{1}}+(C_{3}q^{-\beta_{2}})^{\gamma_{2}}

for any

.
Taking

gives the desired inequality:

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{f_{i}(x+P_{i}(y))}}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\E_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}{f_{i}(z)}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\ll_{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}}q^{-\gamma}.

∎

Power saving bound

We now deduce a power saving bound for the polynomial Szemerédi theorem over finite fields
when the family of polynomials is good for irrational equidistribution.
Recall the statement of Corollary
1.13
from the introduction:

See
1.13

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle N(A_{0},\dots,A_{m})=\left|\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}:x\in A_{0},x+P_{1}(y)\in A_{1},\dots,x+P_{m}(y)\in A_{m}\right\}\right|.

\displaystyle\left\|\E_{y}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{i}}(x+P_{i}(y))}}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}{\alpha_{i}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\ll q^{-\gamma}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left|N(A_{0},\dots,A_{m})-q^{-(m-1)}\prod_{i=0}^{m}{|A_{i}|}\right|\ll|A_{0}|^{1/2}q^{3/2-\gamma}.

Suppose
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
contains no nontrivial pattern
\{x,x+P_{1}(y),\dots,P_{m}(y)\}
.
Then by the union bound,

\displaystyle N(A,\dots,A)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}:x\in A,\left|\{0,P_{1}(y),\dots,P_{m}(y)\}\right|\leq m\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|A|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\left|\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:P_{i}(y)=0\right\}\right|}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}{\left|\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:(P_{j}-P_{i})(y)=0\right\}\right|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(m+\binom{m}{2}\right)d|A|,

where
d=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}{\deg{P_{i}}}
.
Therefore, by the above,

\displaystyle q^{-(m-1)}|A|^{m+1}-D|A|\ll|A|^{1/2}q^{3/2-\gamma},

where we have put
D=\left(m+\binom{m}{2}\right)d=\binom{m+1}{2}d
.
Multiplying by
|A|^{-1/2}q^{m-1}
and keeping only the dominant term, we have

\displaystyle|A|^{m+1/2}\ll q^{m+1/2-\gamma}.

∎

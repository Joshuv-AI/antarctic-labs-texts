On the regularization of sequences and associated weight functions Thanks: This research was funded in whole by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) project 10.55776/P33417
Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We revisit and generalize the geometric procedure of regularizing a sequence of real numbers with respect to a so-called regularizing function. This approach was studied by S. Mandelbrojt and becomes useful and necessary when working with corresponding classes of ultradifferentiable functions defined via weight sequences and analogous weighted spaces. In this note we also study non-standard situations for the construction yielding the (log-)convex minorant of a sequence and allow a “blow-up” for the regularizing function.

26A12, 26A48, 26A51

1.
Introduction

Spaces of
ultradifferentiable classes
are certain weighted subclasses of smooth functions; the weight is measuring the growth of the derivatives of the functions under consideration. Classically there do exist two, in general mutually distinct approaches: the more original method using a weight sequence
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
, see e.g.
[
11
]
, and the more recent technique using a weight function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor from
[
5
]
. Concerning these function spaces we refer also to
[
4
]
and
[
15
]
and the citations therein. Moreover, analogous weighted structures are studied: We mention
weighted spaces of sequences of complex numbers
or
weighted spaces of formal power series,
see e.g.
[
14
]
and
[
7
]
,
spaces of ultraholomorphic functions,
see e.g.
[
23
]
and
[
8
]
, generalized
Gelfand-Shilov classes
see
[
2
]
, and
weighted spaces of entire functions;
see
[
22
]
and
[
17
]
and in each instance also the references in these works. In all these settings growth and regularity assumptions on the weights are required and unavoidable and it turns out that crucial properties can be viewed as certain regularities of the weights. For a sequence
\mathbf{M}
one of the most basic and important requirements is
log-convexity:

(1.1)

\forall\;p\geq 1:\;\;\;M_{p}^{2}\leq M_{p-1}M_{p+1},

in the literature also denoted by
(M.1)
; see
[
11
]
. It turns out that this property is crucial for technical estimates in many proofs and, in particular, we mention that log-convexity implies that the ultradifferentiable classes are closed under the point-wise product; see e.g.
[
18
, Prop. 2.0.8]
.

A further useful fact is that log-convex sequences
\mathbf{M}
can be expressed in terms of the corresponding
associated function

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
via (
2.6
) and (
3.12
); see Section
2.4
for more details about
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
. This function appears frequently in both the weight sequence and weight function setting crucially. In the latter case
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is also serving as a (counter-)example and is becoming relevant for comparison results between these settings; see
[
4
]
.

The intimate relation between
\mathbf{M}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
expressed in terms of (
2.6
) resp. (
3.12
) admits also applications in different contexts. We mention that it is unavoidable when proving the characterization of inclusion relations and stability properties in terms of the defining weights: For the ultradifferentiable setting see
[
15
, Prop. 4.6 & Thm. 4.11]
, for the weighted formal power series classes see
[
7
, Sect. 4.2]
, for the Gelfand-Shilov spaces see
[
3
, Sect. 4 & 6]
, and for the weighted entire setting we refer to
[
22
]
and
[
17
]
.

Motivated by the importance of log-convexity one may ask:

(*)

When
\mathbf{M}
violates (
1.1
), is it possible to replace
\mathbf{M}
by a log-convex sequence
\mathbf{L}
such that the corresponding weighted function classes coincide? And how are the functions
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{L}}
related?

(*)

More generally, when given an “irregular behaving”
\mathbf{M}
, can it be replaced by a “more regular sequence” without changing the corresponding weighted class?

(*)

How can such a more regular sequence be obtained resp. computed from the given sequence
\mathbf{M}
?

S. Mandelbrojt treated these questions in
[
13
, Chapitre I]
by developing a purely geometric and abstract procedure: Consider a sequence
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
, formally one can even allow
a_{p}=+\infty
for finitely many indices
p\geq 1
, and a so-called
regularizing function

\phi:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
which satisfies some basic growth properties; see Definition
5.1
. Then construct the regularized sequence
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}=(a^{\phi}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
, to be precise
(p,a^{\phi}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is obtained from the given set
(p,a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
. The crucial correspondence between
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{a}
and their regularized counterparts is given by
a_{p}=\log(M_{p})
,
M^{\phi}_{p}=\exp(a^{\phi}_{p})
; i.e.

(1.2)

\mathbf{a}=\log(\mathbf{M}),\hskip 30.0pt\mathbf{M}^{\phi}=\exp(\mathbf{a}^{\phi}).

This construction yields, as a “by-product”, the notion of the so-called
trace function.
The cases
\phi=+\infty
, i.e.
\phi(t)=+\infty
for all
t\geq 0
and which is formally not a regularizing function, and
\phi\neq+\infty
were considered separately since the behavior of the regularized sequences is different. In the construction it turned out that for
\phi=+\infty
the requirement
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=+\infty
avoids technical complications and via (
1.2
) this corresponds to
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
. Moreover,
\phi=+\infty
yields the most prominent example the so-called
log-convex minorant

\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
; see Section
2.3
. In the case
\phi\neq+\infty
the example
\phi(t):=e^{t}
is useful; this case is called
“régularisation exponentielle”
in
[
13
, Chapitre I]
.

Indeed,
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
answers the first question above; see
[
15
, Thm. 2.15]
and the citations there for ultradifferentiable classes and
[
9
, Thm. 5.7]
deals with the ultraholomorphic setting. It also turns out that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}=\omega_{\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}}
which is useful when working with this function. Concerning the second question we mention the classical work
[
6
]
and
[
13
, Chapitre I, Sect. 1.9]
. In the decomposition result from
[
12
]
the regularization w.r.t.
\phi(t)=e^{t}
appeared crucially. Moreover, for
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
the trace function is closely connected to
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
, see (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), and hence to a weight function in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor.

The aim of this paper is to study this construction again in detail, to give a more complete picture in several directions and to extend Mandelbrojt’s techniques:

(A)

When studying again
[
13
, Chapitre I]
in detail we found that the construction is formally not complete in the sense that the first term
a_{0}
of
\mathbf{a}
was not considered and that only non-negative slopes of the crucial straight lines in the construction were used; see the explanations on
[
13
, Chapitre I, p. 2]
and the normalization condition
\phi(0)\geq 1
there. We close this technical gap and present a complete construction and in order to do so we are dealing now with a slightly more general notion for
\phi
being a regularizing function; i.e. extending it to whole
\mathbb{R}
, see Definition
5.1
. Note that
a_{0}
corresponds to
M_{0}
and this value contains crucial information in the weighted settings.

(B)

If
\phi=+\infty
, then study the situation when Mandelbrojt’s basic assumption
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=+\infty
is violated.

(C)

We focus in detail on the case when
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e. allowing a “blow-up-phenomenon”, since this was only mentioned briefly on
[
13
, Chapitre I, p. 2]
.

Indeed, these questions stem from very recent research.
(A)
is motivated by
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
: In these works the inclusion relations for Gelfand-Shilov classes are characterized. In order to proceed, for general
anisotropic resp. multi-index sequences

(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
in
[
1
]
, the definition and the construction of the log-convex minorant has to be generalized accordingly. It turned out that the higher-dimensional case requires a delicate induction argument and during the preparation of
[
1
]
we detected the gap mentioned in
(A)
.

(B)
was mainly inspired by
[
9
]
: In the main results in
[
9
, Sect. 5]
log-convexity and the log-convex minorant of sequences are becoming crucial. In particular, in view of
[
9
, Thm. 5.8]
the behavior of
p\mapsto(M_{p})^{1/p}
and the failure of Mandelbrojt’s basic assumption are interesting. Note that B. Rodríguez-Salinas in
[
16
]
considered for ultraholomorphic classes also “non-standard situations” but focusing on the properties of the function classes and not giving an explicit construction for
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
; we refer to our detailed comments
[
9
, Rem. 5.5 & 5.9]
.

Moreover, the following crucial fact is related to
(B)
: H. Komatsu used
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
in
[
11
]
but without assuming Mandelbrojt’s basic assumption; i.e. not requiring necessarily
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
. But in this case
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
violates crucial growth requirements and, in particular, cannot be considered as weight function in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor.

Summarizing, problems
(A)-(C)
admit, on the one hand, a more detailed understanding and further applications in weighted settings and, on the other hand, can become relevant in general when studying the growth and regularity behavior of sequences of real numbers from an purely abstract point of view.

The paper is structured as follows: First, in Section
2
we introduce the notion of weight sequences
\mathbf{M}
and study basic growth requirements for
\mathbf{M}
and its consequences for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
. In particular, we study Komatsu’s setting in Section
2.5
and recall and prove representation formulas for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
under very general assumptions on
\mathbf{M}
. Section
3
is dedicated to the study of
(A)
for
\phi=+\infty
and hence we revisit the construction of the (log-)convex minorant
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
of a given sequence
\mathbf{M}
and provide a comparison with the results obtained by S. Mandelbrojt; see Section
3.3
. In Section
4
we treat
(B)
and study two “degenerate/non-standard” situations for the construction of
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
. The final Section
5
focuses on
(A)
and
(C)
for general regularizing functions and we give comparisons with the case
\phi=+\infty
. It turns out that
(C)
and the non-standard case in Section
4.2
are similar.

Finally, we wish to emphasize that this note is purely investigating the geometric regularization approach of
\mathbf{a}
resp.
\mathbf{M}
being related via (
1.2
) and the correspondence to the trace function and so to
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
. It is known that more specific growth properties for
\mathbf{M}
used in the theories of weighted settings transfer to properties for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
and vice versa; see e.g.
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
,
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
and the references therein.

2.
Weight sequences and associated functions

2.1.
Basic notation

We write
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{>0}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
. Occasionally, we put
\mathbb{R}_{>0}:=(0,+\infty)
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:=[0,+\infty)
. Frequently we use the conventions
0^{0}:=1
,
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
,
0\cdot(-\infty):=0
and
p\cdot(-\infty):=-\infty
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.

2.2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a given sequence of positive real numbers. Moreover, let us set
\mu_{p}:=\frac{M_{p}}{M_{p-1}}
for
p\geq 1
and
\mu_{0}:=1
. Hence we have
\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}=\mu_{p}\cdots\mu_{1}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
; the case
p=0
gives the empty product.
\mathbf{M}
is called
normalized
if
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}
. For concrete applications and examples it is convenient to introduce the following class of sequences:

\hypertarget{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}:=\{\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\;\mathbf{M}\;\text{is normalized, log-convex},\;\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty\}.

Note:
For each
\mathbf{M}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
we have
\mu_{0}=1\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\dots
and
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
. In particular
\mathbf{M}
is non-decreasing and in fact there exists a one-to-one correspondence between sequences belonging to the class
\mathcal{LC}
and sequences
\mu_{0}=1\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\dots
and
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
via setting
M_{p}:=\prod_{i=0}^{p}\mu_{i}
.

We write
\mathbf{M}\leq\mathbf{N}
if
M_{p}\leq N_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
are called
equivalent
if

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\frac{1}{C^{p+1}}N_{p}\leq M_{p}\leq C^{p+1}N_{p};

i.e. if
0<\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}\leq\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{N_{p}}\right)^{1/p}<+\infty
. Equivalence preserves the corresponding weighted classes; therefore it is no restriction to assume always
M_{0}=1
since otherwise
\mathbf{M}
can be replaced by
\widetilde{\mathbf{M}}
with
\widetilde{M}_{p}:=M_{p}/M_{0}
. Similarly, for each
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
we can find an equivalent sequence
\mathbf{N}
which is even normalized.

(*)

Log-convexity for
\mathbf{M}
is equivalent to the fact that
\mu=(\mu_{p})_{p\geq 1}
is non-decreasing, hence
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}
exists in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

(*)

Moreover, it is known, see e.g.
[
18
, Lemma 2.0.4]
, that for log-convex
\mathbf{M}
one has that
p\mapsto(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}
is non-decreasing. Thus also
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}
exists in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

(*)

Let
\mathbf{M}
be log-convex, then

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}=\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p}\leq\mu_{p}^{p}\Longrightarrow(M_{p})^{1/p}\leq(M_{0})^{1/p}\mu_{p},

and so
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}\leq\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}
. In particular,
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
implies
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
and the converse is valid for any
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
; see e.g.
[
15
, p. 104]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
and assume that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=:C<+\infty
exists. Then we get

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}.

Summarizing, for any log-convex
\mathbf{M}
one has
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}

and both limits exist in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

Proof.

By assumption

\forall\;\epsilon>0\;\exists\;p_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;p>p_{\epsilon}:\;\;\;C-\epsilon\leq\mu_{p}\leq C+\epsilon\Leftrightarrow M_{p-1}(C-\epsilon)\leq M_{p}\leq(C+\epsilon)M_{p-1},

hence iterating this gives
M_{p_{\epsilon}}(C-\epsilon)^{i}\leq M_{p_{\epsilon}+i}\leq(C+\epsilon)^{i}M_{p_{\epsilon}}
and so

\forall\;i\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;(M_{p_{\epsilon}})^{1/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}(C-\epsilon)^{i/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}\leq(M_{p_{\epsilon}+i})^{1/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}\leq(C+\epsilon)^{i/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}(M_{p_{\epsilon}})^{1/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}.

When
\epsilon>0
is now arbitrary and fixed, then as
i\rightarrow+\infty
we get
(M_{p_{\epsilon}})^{1/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}(C-\epsilon)^{i/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}\rightarrow C-\epsilon
and
(C+\epsilon)^{i/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}(M_{p_{\epsilon}})^{1/(p_{\epsilon}+i)}\rightarrow C+\epsilon
. Finally, since
\epsilon>0
can be taken arbitrary,
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=C
is verified.
∎

However, note that for Lemma
2.1
the existence of the limits is crucial: Let
0<c<1<d<+\infty
and set
M_{0}:=1
,
M_{p}:=c^{p}
for all
p\geq 1
odd and
M_{p}:=d^{p}
for all
p\geq 1
even and then

0=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}<\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=c<d=\limsup_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}<\limsup_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty.

2.3.
The log-convex minorant

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given. The
log-convex minorant

\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
is defined to be the point-wise, i.e. w.r.t. relation
\leq
, largest sequence among all log-convex sequences
\mathbf{L}
satisfying
\mathbf{L}\leq\mathbf{M}
.

So, if
\mathbf{L}
is log-convex and
\mathbf{L}\leq\mathbf{M}
, then already
\mathbf{L}\leq\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}(\leq\mathbf{M})
is valid.

Obviously,
\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
if and only if
\mathbf{M}
is log-convex.

2.4.
Associated function

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given. Then, the
associated function

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined as follows:

(2.1)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t):=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\log\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}},\qquad t\geq 0,

with the convention that
0^{0}:=1
. This ensures
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)\geq 0
for any
t\geq 0
since
\frac{t^{0}M_{0}}{M_{0}}=1
for all
t\geq 0
. (
2.1
) corresponds to
[
11
,
(3.1)
]
and we immediately have that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is non-decreasing and satisfying
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty
. In the literature
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is often extended to
t\in\mathbb{R}
resp. even to
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
t\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
in a radial-symmetric way; i.e. replacing
t
by
|t|
.

Moreover, we set
\varphi_{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}}:=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\circ\exp
and define the
Young-conjugate

(2.2)

\varphi^{*}_{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}}(t):=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\{ts-\varphi_{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}}(s)\},\;\;\;t\geq 0.

Note:
In the literature frequently this conjugate is defined by restricting to
s\geq 0
and both definitions coincide if
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is
normalized;
i.e. if
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,1]
. In any case,
\varphi^{*}_{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}}
is a convex function.

2.5.
On H. Komatsu’s setting for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}

In
[
11
,
(3.1)
]
it was mentioned that

(2.3)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}:=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}>0

is crucial and basic for the definition of
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
according to (
2.1
): Under this assumption
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is non-decreasing, continuous, increasing faster than
t\mapsto\log(t)^{p}
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, hence in particular
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty
, and
\log(t)=o(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

However,
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
was not considered necessarily in
[
11
]
and, in general, (
2.3
) is
not sufficient
to avoid extreme situations. The next result illustrates this fact and should be compared with Section
4.2
; in particular with Example
4.3
there.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and set
\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}=:\mathbf{M}_{\inf}\leq\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
. Then we get:

(i)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
implies

\forall\;0\leq t\leq\mathbf{M}_{\inf}:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=0.

(ii)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
implies

\forall\;t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty.

(iii)

If
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
, then
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)<+\infty
for all
t\geq 0
.

(ii)
and
(iii)
together yield that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
is equivalent to
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)<+\infty
for any
t\geq 0
; see also
[
2
, Rem. 1]
even in the
anisotropic situation.

Proof.

(i)

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>0
holds if and only if
\mathbf{M}_{\inf}>0
since
M_{p}>0
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. In this situation

\forall\;0<c\leq\mathbf{M}_{\inf}\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{p}\geq M_{0}c^{p},

which implies
\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\leq\left(\frac{t}{c}\right)^{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
and proves the desired property.

(ii)
Let
t>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
and choose
\epsilon>0
such that
t>\epsilon>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
. Then, by definition we can find infinitely many
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\epsilon\geq(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}\Leftrightarrow\frac{M_{0}}{M_{p}}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{p}
and so
\log\left(\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right)\geq p\log\left(\frac{t}{\epsilon}\right)
for infinitely many
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Hence this part is shown.

(iii)

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
gives

\forall\;t\geq 0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;t^{p}\leq CM_{p}\Leftrightarrow\log\left(\frac{t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right)\leq\log(C),

hence by definition
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)\leq\log(M_{0})+\log(C)=\log(M_{0}C)
for all
t\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In view of Lemma
2.2
the “extreme example”
M_{p}:=d^{p}
,
d>0
, yields

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=0,\;\;\;t\in[0,d],\hskip 30.0pt\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty,\;\;\;t\in(d,+\infty).

For the sake of completeness we mention that in
[
19
, Lemma 7.2]
a “converse extreme situation” was considered: The function
t\mapsto\log(1+t)
cannot be considered as an associated function for any
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
; but when allowing
M_{p}=+\infty
for infinitely many
p\in\mathbb{N}
, then it is possible to treat this “limiting function” as well.

2.6.
Representation formulas for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}

We recall now the explicit representation for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
for more regular sequences; see also
[
13
, 1.8 III, 1.8 V]
and
[
18
, Lemma 8.2.4, Prop. 8.2.5]
but there we have assumed
\mu_{1}\geq 1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be log-convex and assume that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
. Then we get

(2.4)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=0,\;\text{for}\;t\in[0,\mu_{1}],\;\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\log\left(\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right)\;\text{for}\;t\in[\mu_{p},\mu_{p+1}],\;p\geq 1.

In particular,
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(\mu_{p})=\log\left(\frac{M_{0}\mu_{p}^{p}}{M_{p}}\right)
for all
p\geq 1
. This formula implies that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is continuous, non-decreasing and
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=+\infty
. Moreover,
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is increasing faster than
t\mapsto\log(t)^{p}
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
which gives
\log(t)=o(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Finally,
t\mapsto\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(e^{t})=\varphi_{\omega_{\mathbf{M}}}(t)
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

First, by assumption recall that we get
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=+\infty
as well; see Lemma
2.1
and Section
2.2
. Now let
t\geq 0
be given such that
\mu_{p}\leq t\leq\mu_{p+1}
for some
p\geq 1
. Then for any
q>p
we have

\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\geq\frac{M_{0}t^{q}}{M_{q}}\Leftrightarrow\frac{M_{q}}{M_{p}}\geq t^{q-p}\Leftrightarrow\mu_{p+1}\cdots\mu_{q}\geq t^{q-p},

which is clearly satisfied since
p\mapsto\mu_{p}
is non-decreasing by log-convexity. Similarly, if
q<p
, then

\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\geq\frac{M_{0}t^{q}}{M_{q}}\Leftrightarrow\frac{M_{p}}{M_{q}}\leq t^{p-q}\Leftrightarrow\mu_{q+1}\cdots\mu_{p}\leq t^{p-q},

which holds by analogous reasons. For
p=1
we have
q=0
and get
\frac{M_{0}t}{M_{1}}\geq 1\Leftrightarrow\frac{M_{1}}{M_{0}}=\mu_{1}\leq t
. Note that for these arguments it is not necessary to assume that
\mu_{p+1}>\mu_{p}
.

Finally, we have
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(\mu_{1})=\log(\mu_{1}M_{0}/M_{1})=\log(M_{1}M_{0}/(M_{0}M_{1}))=0
. Let
t\in[0,\mu_{1}]
, then
\frac{t^{p}M_{0}}{M_{p}}\leq 1\Leftrightarrow t^{p}\leq\frac{M_{p}}{M_{0}}=\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{p}
for all
p\geq 1
and for
p=0
we have
t^{0}/M_{0}=1
. Altogether this verifies that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
vanishes on
[0,\mu_{1}]
. Alternatively, since by definition
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
is non-decreasing for this property it suffices to check
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(\mu_{1})=0
.
∎

By using this result we give a proof of the useful integral representation formula for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
stated in
[
11
,
(3.11)
]
; see
[
13
, 1.8. III, 1.8 V]
and
[
18
, Prop. 8.2.5]
under slightly stronger normalization assumptions on the sequence.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be log-convex and such that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
. We define the
counting function

\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}
by

\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(t):=|\{p\geq 1:\;\;\;\mu_{p}\leq t\}|,\;\;\;t\in[0,+\infty),

and get the following integral representation formula:

(2.5)

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(s)}{s}ds=\int_{\mu_{1}}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(s)}{s}ds.

Proof.

By definition of
\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}
it is clear that
\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(s)}{s}ds=0
for all
0\leq t<\mu_{1}
. For any
p\geq 1
we get

\int_{\mu_{p}}^{\mu_{p+1}}\frac{\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(s)}{s}ds=p\int_{\mu_{p}}^{\mu_{p+1}}\frac{1}{s}=p\log(\mu_{p+1}/\mu_{p}),

which equals
0
if
\mu_{p}=\mu_{p+1}
. Thus, if
\mu_{p}\leq t\leq\mu_{p+1}
with
p\geq 1
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(s)}{s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mu_{1}}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(s)}{s}ds=\sum_{q=1}^{p-1}q\log(\mu_{q+1}/\mu_{q})+\int_{\mu_{p}}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}(s)}{s}ds=\sum_{q=1}^{p-1}q\log(\mu_{q+1}/\mu_{q})+p\log(t/\mu_{p})

\displaystyle=p\log(t)-\sum_{q=1}^{p}\log(\mu_{q})=p\log(t)-\log(M_{p}/M_{0})=\log\left(\frac{t^{p}M_{0}}{M_{p}}\right).

By Lemma
2.4
we are done.
∎

Indeed, when inspecting the proofs of Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, then by taking into account Lemma
2.1
and the comments from Section
2.2
we get the following version:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be log-convex and assume that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{p}=:C<+\infty
. Then:

(i)

(
2.4
) holds for all
0\leq t<C
.

(ii)

(
2.5
) is valid for all
0\leq t<C
and note that we have to restrict
\Sigma_{\mathbf{M}}
to
[0,C)
.

2.7.
Associated weight sequence

Given
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
is valid let us introduce the sequence
\underline{\mathbf{M}}
via

(2.6)

\underline{M}_{p}:=M_{0}\sup_{t>0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))},\;\;\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}.

Note:
Since
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0
, in (
2.6
) we can also write
\sup_{t\geq 0}
and
\underline{M}_{0}=M_{0}
is clear because
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)\geq 0
,
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0
, and so
\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}=1
.

We have the following properties for
\underline{\mathbf{M}}
:

(i)

\underline{\mathbf{M}}
is log-convex since

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}=\exp\left(\sup_{t>0}\{p\log(t)-\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)\}\right)=\exp\left(\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\{ps-\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(e^{s})\}\right)=\exp(\varphi^{*}_{\mathbf{M}}(p));

recall (
2.2
).

(ii)

We have

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\underline{M}_{p}\leq M_{p},

since by definition:

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M_{0}\sup_{t>0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}=M_{0}\sup_{t>0}\frac{t^{p}}{\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}M_{0}t^{q}/M_{q}}\underbrace{\leq}_{q=p}M_{p}.

(iii)

If
\mathbf{M}
is log-convex, then
\mathbf{M}\equiv\underline{\mathbf{M}}
, i.e.

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\underline{M}_{p}\geq M_{p},

because as seen above
\underline{M}_{0}=M_{0}
is clear and by Lemma
2.4
we get for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
that

M_{0}\sup_{t>0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}\geq M_{0}\frac{\mu_{p}^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(\mu_{p}))}=M_{p}.

Gathering, by
(i)
and
(ii)
we immediately see that
\underline{\mathbf{M}}\leq\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
and in the next section we prove that in fact equality holds.

Analogous statements hold if
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=C\in(0,+\infty)
instead: Then in (
2.6
) we have to replace
\sup_{t>0}
by
\sup_{0<t<C}
and use Lemma
2.6
instead of Lemma
2.4
in
(iii)
.

3.
Constructing the (log-)convex minorant

A sequence
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
is called
convex,
if the epigraph of the set
\{(p,a_{p}):p\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The
convex minorant

\mathbf{a}^{c}
of
\mathbf{a}
is defined to be the largest sequence w.r.t. relation
\leq
, i.e. point-wise, among all convex sequences
\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{a}
.

3.1.
Geometric construction of the convex minorant

Let
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and assume in this section that

(3.1)

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=+\infty.

Moreover let us set
S_{p}:=(p,a_{p})
for
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{S}:=\{S_{p}:p\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Note:

(a)

The forthcoming geometric construction is not changing when we formally allow
a_{p}=+\infty
for
finitely many

p\geq 1
.

(b)

On the other hand,
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=+\infty
implies
a_{p}\geq p
for all
p
sufficiently large and hence, in particular, that

(3.2)

\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}a_{p}>-\infty.

In any case, (
3.2
) implies

(3.3)

\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}>-\infty,

because
a_{p}\leq\frac{a_{p}}{p}\Leftrightarrow 1\leq p
for all
p\geq 1
such that
a_{p}\leq 0
and
\frac{a_{p}}{p}\geq 0
for all
p\geq 1
such that
a_{p}>0
.

(c)

The value
\frac{a_{p}}{p}
is precisely the slope of the straight line connecting the origin
(0,0)
and the point
S_{p}
,
p\geq 1
.

In order to construct the convex minorant of
\mathbf{a}
the idea is to project down vertically each
a_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, to a set of straight lines
\{D_{k}:k\in\mathbb{R}\}
called “supporting lines”. Here
D_{k}
is a straight line having slope
k\in\mathbb{R}
and is given by the formula

D_{k}:t\mapsto tk+d_{k},\hskip 15.0ptk\in\mathbb{R}.

Formally, we can also consider the slope
k=-\infty
with
D_{-\infty}
denoting the
y
-axis
\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb{R}\}
.
D_{k}
are obtained iteratively as follows:

(1)

Let the starting point
S_{0}=(0,a_{0})
be given and consider all straight lines passing through this point with slope
k
. For sufficiently small values
k>-\infty
all points
S_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, will lie strictly above these lines because we have (
3.3
); see comment
(b)
before. So the only point belonging to
\mathcal{S}
and lying on these lines is
S_{0}
and the “limiting case”
k=-\infty
corresponds to the
y
-axis
\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Then we let
k
increase and we find some minimal slope
k_{1}
and a minimal index
p_{1}\geq 1
such that
S_{p_{1}}
lies on the straight line passing through
S_{0}
and with slope
k_{1}
.

We denote this line by
D_{k_{1}}
and its slope is given by
k_{1}=\frac{a_{p_{1}}-a_{0}}{p_{1}}
which might be negative but
k_{1}>-\infty
. Thus we get

D_{k_{1}}:t\mapsto\frac{a_{p_{1}}-a_{0}}{p_{1}}t+a_{0}.

Let us set
p_{0}:=0
and let
D_{k}
, with
-\infty<k\leq k_{1}
, be the lines passing through
S_{0}
with slope
k
.

(2)

Then consider all straight lines passing through
S_{p_{1}}
and having slope
k\geq k_{1}
. When increasing
k
we can find, similarly as before, a minimal slope
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
and a minimal index
p_{2}\geq p_{1}+1
such that
S_{p_{2}}
lies on the straight line passing through
S_{p_{1}}
and having slope
k_{2}
.

This line is denoted by
D_{k_{2}}
, its slope is given by
k_{2}=\frac{a_{p_{2}}-a_{p_{1}}}{p_{2}-p_{1}}
, which might be negative again, but it is well-defined since
p_{2}\geq p_{1}+1
. Note that
k_{2}=k_{1}
might be valid and in this case
S_{p_{2}}
already lies on
D_{k_{1}}
. Similarly, for
k_{1}<k<k_{2}
let
D_{k}
be the lines passing through
S_{p_{1}}
and having slope
k
. When
k_{1}=k_{2}
this set of lines is empty and
D_{k_{1}}=D_{k_{2}}
.

(3)

Then proceed by iteration, so when given a point
S_{p_{i}}
we can find a point
S_{p_{i+1}}
with
p_{i+1}\geq p_{i}+1
and a straight line
D_{k_{i+1}}
with slope
k_{i+1}\geq k_{i}
and passing through the points
S_{p_{i}}
and
S_{p_{i+1}}
.
D_{k}
with
k_{i}<k<k_{i+1}
are defined to be the lines with slope
k
and passing through
S_{p_{i}}
.

(4)

So
D_{k}:t\mapsto kt+d_{k}
and the lines
D_{k_{i}}
are given by
t\mapsto tk_{i}+d_{k_{i}}
with

k_{i}=\frac{a_{p_{i}}-a_{p_{i-1}}}{p_{i}-p_{i-1}},\hskip 30.0ptd_{k_{i}}=\frac{p_{i}a_{p_{i-1}}-p_{i-1}a_{p_{i}}}{p_{i}-p_{i-1}},\;\;\;i\geq 1,

because

d_{k_{i}}=a_{p_{i}}-\frac{a_{p_{i}}-a_{p_{i-1}}}{p_{i}-p_{i-1}}p_{i}=\frac{p_{i}a_{p_{i}}-p_{i-1}a_{p_{i}}-p_{i}a_{p_{i}}+p_{i}a_{p_{i-1}}}{p_{i}-p_{i-1}}=\frac{p_{i}a_{p_{i-1}}-p_{i-1}a_{p_{i}}}{p_{i}-p_{i-1}}.

As mentioned before one might have
k_{i+1}=k_{i}
but
\lim_{i\rightarrow+\infty}k_{i}=\lim_{i\rightarrow+\infty}p_{i}=+\infty
since
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=+\infty
; see again assumption (
3.1
). Moreover, for sufficiently large
i
we have that
d_{k_{i}}<0
.

Then consider the set
\widetilde{S}=(\widetilde{S}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\widetilde{S}_{p}=(p,\widetilde{a}_{p})
and the
y
-coordinate of each point is obtained by projecting the points
S_{p}
vertically down onto the set of straight lines
D_{k}
. This yields a sequence
\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}=(\widetilde{a}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
and we get:

(*)

\widetilde{a}_{p_{i}}=a_{p_{i}}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

(*)

and for
p\geq 1
with
p_{i}<p<p_{i+1}
:

\widetilde{a}_{p}=pk_{i}+d_{k_{i}}=\frac{a_{p_{i}}-a_{p_{i-1}}}{p_{i}-p_{i-1}}p+\frac{p_{i}a_{p_{i-1}}-p_{i-1}a_{p_{i}}}{p_{i}-p_{i-1}}.

In other words, by the geometric construction we have

(3.4)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\widetilde{a}_{p}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\{kp+d_{k}\}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\{kp-A(k)\},

with

(3.5)

A(k):=-d_{k}.

A:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called the
trace function
and
-A(k)
is precisely the
y
-coordinate of the unique intersection point of
D_{k}
with
\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb{R}\}
; i.e. with the
y
-axis. Obviously
A
is continuous and non-decreasing and we put as limiting case
A(-\infty):=-a_{0}
since
k=-\infty
corresponds to the
y
-axis itself and
S_{0}
is the only point lying on this line. With the conventions
0\cdot(-\infty):=0
and
p\cdot(-\infty):=-\infty
for any
p\geq 1
we get that in (
3.4
) we can consider
k\in\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}
.

Note that from (
3.4
) it follows that

(*)

\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}\equiv\mathbf{a}^{c}
,

(*)

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{p}}{p}=+\infty
and

(*)

\widetilde{a}_{0}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\{d_{k}\}=a_{0}
since each
D_{k}
with
-\infty<k\leq k_{1}
intersects the
y
-axis at
S_{0}
.

(*)

We have

\forall\;i\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\widetilde{a}_{p_{i}}=a^{c}_{p_{i}}=a_{p_{i}},

and so the regularized sequence coincides with the original given one for infinitely many indices since
\lim_{i\rightarrow+\infty}p_{i}=+\infty
by basic assumption (
3.1
). In particular, we always have
\widetilde{a}_{0}=a^{c}_{0}=a_{0}
.

For any fixed
k\in\mathbb{R}
we see that
d_{k}
can be expressed in terms of the given sequence
\mathbf{a}
by

(3.6)

d_{k}=\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{a_{p}-pk\}.

This holds since the line passing through
S_{p}
and having slope
k
intersects the
y
-axis at the point
(0,a_{p}-pk)
and by construction and definition of the lines
D_{k}
we have (
3.6
). For
p=0
we get as intersection point
(0,a_{0})
and clearly all lines passing through
S_{0}
intersect the
y
-axis at
S_{0}
. Thus one has

(3.7)

\forall\;k\in\mathbb{R}:\;\;\;A(k)=-d_{k}=-\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{a_{p}-pk\}=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{pk-a_{p}\}.

Note:
The conventions
0\cdot(-\infty):=0
and
p\cdot(-\infty):=-\infty
for any
p\geq 1
give that (
3.7
) even holds for
k=-\infty
since then
A(-\infty)=-a_{0}
.

3.2.
Obtaining the log-convex minorant

Let now
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and note that in applications for weighted structures the sequence
\mathbf{M}
is the crucial one. The idea is to apply the geometric construction from the previous Section
3.1
to

(3.8)

a_{p}:=\log(M_{p}),

and so in order to guarantee (
3.1
) we have to assume
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
. Note that formally we can also allow
M_{p}=+\infty
for finitely many indices
p\geq 1
.

The sequence
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
is then given by

(3.9)

M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=\exp(\widetilde{a}_{p}),

so the log-convex minorant is obtained by considering
\mathbf{M}\mapsto\mathbf{a}\mapsto\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}\mapsto\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
via (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and the regularization from the previous Section. First, by (
3.7
) we get for any
k\in\mathbb{R}
:

A(k)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{pk-a_{p}\}=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{pk-\log(M_{p})\}=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{e^{pk}}{M_{p}}\right)=:\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(e^{k})(=\varphi_{\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}}(k)),

i.e.

(3.10)

A=\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}\circ\exp,

and (
3.9
) and (
3.4
) yield

\displaystyle\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}

\displaystyle=\exp(\widetilde{a}_{p})=\exp\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\{kp-A(k)\}\right)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{kp}}{\exp(A(k))}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{kp}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(e^{k}))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s>0}\frac{s^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s))}.

For
p=0
we point out that
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{0}=M_{0}=\sup_{s>0}\frac{1}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s))}
since
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}
is by definition non-decreasing and continuous and
\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}A(t)=-a_{0}=-\log(M_{0})
. Moreover, we can consider in the supremum all values
s\geq 0
.

Let us now compare the sequence
\underline{\mathbf{M}}
defined in (
2.6
) with
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
:

(*)

If
M_{0}=1
, i.e.
a_{0}=0
, then
\underline{\mathbf{M}}
coincides with
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
because
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\equiv\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}
.

(*)

If
M_{0}\neq 1
, then for sufficiently small values
s\geq 0
one might have
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)<0
. More precisely, by definition
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\geq 0
if and only if
a_{0}=\log(M_{0})\leq 0
.

Thus, if
M_{0}>1
then we see that
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)
is negative for all sufficiently small
s\geq 0
and hence, in particular,
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}
is not a weight function in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor; see
[
5
]
.

(*)

However, by recalling (
2.1
) in any case we have

(3.11)

\forall\;s\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=\log(M_{0})+\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s),

and so

(3.12)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=\sup_{s>0}\frac{s^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s))}=M_{0}\sup_{s>0}\frac{s^{p}}{\exp(\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s))}=\underline{M}_{p};

see also
[
11
,
(3.2)
, Prop. 3.2]
.

(
3.10
) and (
3.11
) yield the important connection between the associated function and the trace function appearing “naturally” in the geometric construction.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
. This approach also implies
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}\equiv\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}}
and
M_{p_{i}}=M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p_{i}}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

In view of (
3.11
) and by
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{0}=M_{0}
one has
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}\equiv\omega_{\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}}
as well and Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
can be applied to the sequence
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
.

3.3.
Comparison with Mandelbrojt’s approach

In
[
13
, Chapitre I]
by giving purely geometric arguments and constructions as in Section
3.1
it was stated that

(3.13)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t))},

with

(3.14)

\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t):=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\log\frac{t^{p}}{M_{p}},\;\;\;t\geq 1.

We refer to
[
13
, p. 17]
and there the following functions were introduced:

T:=\exp\circ\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}},\hskip 15.0ptA:=\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}\circ\exp,

with
A
denoting again the
trace function
from (
3.4
), (
3.5
). However, in
[
13
]
the function
A
was only studied on
[0,+\infty)
which is corresponding to lines having non-negative slopes. Moreover, only indices
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
were considered; concerning this issue please see the footnote
(1)
on
[
13
, p. 1, Chapitre I]
.

Of course, the definition of
\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}
in (
3.14
) can be extended to
t\in(0,1]
. But the value
t=0
is excluded and we see that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=-\infty
. Thus
\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}
also
cannot
be considered on
[0,+\infty)
as a weight function in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor.

However, provided that
\mathbf{M}
satisfies the regularity assumptions from Lemma
2.4
we show that for all sufficiently large values the functions
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
and
\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}
differ in general only by adding a real constant.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be log-convex and assume that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
. Then we get

(3.15)

\forall\;t\geq\max\{\mu_{1},1\}:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\log(M_{0})+\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t),

which implies

(3.16)

\forall\;t\geq\max\{\mu_{1},1\}:\;\;\;\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t).

Proof.

Let
t\geq 1
be given such that
\mu_{p}\leq t\leq\mu_{p+1}
for some
p\geq 1
. Then, analogously as in Lemma
2.4
for any
q>p
we have
\frac{t^{p}}{M_{p}}\geq\frac{t^{q}}{M_{q}}
and, if
p\geq 2
, for any
q<p
we have
\frac{t^{p}}{M_{p}}\geq\frac{t^{q}}{M_{q}}
, too. If
p=1
, then according to the definition in (
3.14
) it is only necessary to consider all
q>p
.

So far we have shown that
\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\log\left(\frac{t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right)
for all
t\geq 1
satisfying
\mu_{p}\leq t\leq\mu_{p+1}
for some
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and hence (
3.15
) follows by taking into account (
2.4
). Finally, (
3.16
) holds by combining (
3.15
) and (
3.11
).
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
be given. Then we get
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t)
for all
t\geq\mu_{1}
.

Proof.

In this case, apart from the basic assumptions on
\mathbf{M}
in Lemma
3.2
, we also have
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}\Leftrightarrow 1\leq\mu_{1}
and so (
3.15
), (
3.16
) yield the assertion.
∎

Let now
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given such that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
and set

\underline{\underline{M}}_{p}:=\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}.

Then we obtain, compare this with Section
2.7
:

(i)

First,

\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}=\exp\left(\sup_{s\geq 0}\{ps-\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(e^{s})\}\right)=\exp\left(\varphi^{*}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}}(p)\right),

and so
\underline{\underline{M}}_{p}
is log-convex.

(ii)

For all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
we get
\underline{\underline{M}}_{p}\leq M_{p}
because

\underline{\underline{M}}_{p}=\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}=\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{p}}{\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{t^{q}}{M_{q}}}\underbrace{\leq}_{q=p}M_{p}.

(iii)

Finally, if
\mathbf{M}
is in addition log-convex, then the proof of Lemma
3.2
yields that

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0},\;\mu_{p}\geq 1:\;\;\;\underline{\underline{M}}_{p}=\sup_{t\geq 1}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}\geq\frac{\mu_{p}^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(\mu_{p}))}=\frac{\mu_{p}^{p}}{\mu_{p}^{p}/M_{p}}=M_{p};

i.e.
\underline{\underline{M}}_{p}=M_{p}
for all
p
sufficiently large.

Summarizing, Corollary
3.3
, Section
2.7
and the previous comments yield the following information:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
be given with
1=M_{0}=M_{1}
; i.e.
\mu_{1}=1
. Then

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=M_{p}=\underline{M}_{p}=\underline{\underline{M}}_{p},

and

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;\mu_{p}\leq t\leq\mu_{p+1}:\;\;\;\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\widetilde{\widetilde{\omega}}_{\mathbf{M}}(t)=\log\left(\frac{t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right);

hence all appearing associated resp. trace functions coincide on
[1,+\infty)
.

Moreover, we note:

(*)

In Section
3.2

(*)

In this case, by changing if necessary the finitely many

M^{n}_{p}:=1,\;\;\;\forall\;0\leq p\leq q_{0}-1,\hskip 30.0ptM^{n}_{p}:=M_{p},\;\;\;\forall\;p\geq q_{0},

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\frac{1}{C}M^{n}_{p}\leq M_{p}\leq CM^{n}_{p}.

(*)

However, even if all points

and follow the proof given in

(*)

On the other hand, if each

Let

4.
Non-standard situations for the convex regularization

The aim is to see how the geometric approach described before is changing when
\mathbf{a}
is violating the basic requirement (
3.1
) in Section
3.1
. In view of
(b)
in Section
3.1
we can distinguish between two cases and in both situations formally we could even allow that
M_{p}=+\infty

for infinitely many
p\geq 1
.

4.1.
Case 1

Let
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and assume that

(4.1)

\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=-\infty;

i.e. (
3.3
) fails. Consequently, by
(b)
in Section
3.1
also
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=+\infty
is violated. When using the notation from this Section we see that in this case, since
\frac{a_{p}}{p}
is the slope of the straight line connecting the points
(0,0)
and
S_{p}
, we cannot find a straight line
D_{k}
with
k>-\infty
such that
D_{k}
is passing through the point
S_{0}
and such that all
S_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, are lying strictly above
D_{k}
.

Thus in the geometric approach we can only consider the
y
-axis as the limiting case which does have “slope”
-\infty
. In order to give the regularized sequence
\widetilde{S}=(\widetilde{S}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
a meaning, we have to put

\widetilde{a}_{0}=a_{0},\hskip 30.0pt\widetilde{a}_{p}=-\infty,\;\;\;\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.

In this case the set
\{D_{k}:k\in\mathbb{R}\}
is empty and formally one can only consider
D_{-\infty}
which corresponds to the set
\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb{R}\}
.
S_{0}
is the only point belonging to
\mathcal{S}
and lying on this line. Thus, the trace function in (
3.5
) takes the form
A(-\infty)=-d_{k}=-a_{0}
and it is not defined for
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,
\widetilde{a}_{0}=a_{0}
but
\widetilde{a}_{p}<a_{p}
for all
p\geq 1
and hence the sequences
\mathbf{a}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}
differ at all indices
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Indeed, we have
\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}\notin\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Moreover, when
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
is given, then via (
3.8
) condition (
4.1
) precisely means

\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=0;

i.e. (
2.3
) is violated. In this case, by (
3.9
), the sequence
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
is given by

M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{0}=M_{0},\hskip 30.0pt\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=0.

Using and combining this information, formally we see that the formulas in Section
3.2
involving the trace function
A
, and so
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}
,
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
, for computing
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
fail because here we have
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=A(-\infty)=-a_{0}=-\log(M_{0})
and
A
is not defined on
\mathbb{R}
. However, recall
(ii)
in Lemma
2.2
: We have
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(0)=0
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
for all
s>0
and so (
3.11
) formally only makes sense for
s=0
. But when “extending” this identity now to
(0,+\infty)
by setting
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
for all
s\in(0,+\infty)
, using this and the conventions
0^{0}:=1
and
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
too, then the formula
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(t))}
makes sense for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, via (
3.10
) we can set
A(k):=+\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{R}
.

4.2.
Case 2

Let
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and assume that

(4.2)

-\infty<\mathbf{a}_{\iota}:=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}<+\infty.

We use again the notation from Section
3.1
and start with the geometric construction as described there since we can find straight lines
D_{k}
with
k>-\infty
and such all
S_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, are lying strictly above
D_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{R}
sufficiently small. Then we proceed with the construction described in Section
3.1
. Since
\frac{a_{p}}{p}
is the slope of the straight line connecting
(0,0)
and
S_{p}
and since

(4.3)

\mathbf{a}_{\iota}<+\infty,

we have that (
3.4
) turns into

(4.4)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\widetilde{a}_{p}=\sup_{k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}\{kp+d_{k}\}=\sup_{k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}\{kp-A(k)\},

where again
A(k)=-d_{k}
is denoting the
trace function;
see (
3.5
). Note that also here
A
is continuous and non-decreasing, that
\widetilde{a}_{0}=\sup_{k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}\{d_{k}\}=a_{0}
and we put again
A(-\infty):=-a_{0}
. By the conventions
0\cdot(-\infty):=0
and
p\cdot(-\infty):=-\infty
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
we see that in (
4.4
) one can consider
k\in\{-\infty\}\cup(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
.

(
3.6
) transfers to
d_{k}=\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{a_{p}-pk\}
for all
k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
and consequently (
3.7
) takes the following form:

\forall\;k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota}):\;\;\;A(k)=-d_{k}=-\inf_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{a_{p}-pk\}=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{pk-a_{p}\}.

A
is now naturally defined on the open interval
(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
.

Given
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
we apply this to the sequence
a_{p}:=\log(M_{p})
and so (
4.2
) precisely means that

0<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=\exp(\mathbf{a}_{\iota})<+\infty.

Consequently, we get for any
k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
that

A(k)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{pk-a_{p}\}=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{pk-\log(M_{p})\}=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{e^{pk}}{M_{p}}\right)=\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(e^{k}),

thus (
4.4
), recall also (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), yields

\displaystyle\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}

\displaystyle=\exp(\widetilde{a}_{p})=\exp\left(\sup_{k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}\{kp-A(k)\}\right)=\sup_{k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}\frac{e^{kp}}{\exp(A(k))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}\frac{e^{kp}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(e^{k}))}=\sup_{s\in(0,\exp(\mathbf{a}_{\iota}))}\frac{s^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s))}=\sup_{s\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})}\frac{s^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s))}.

We summarize:

(a)

M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{0}=M_{0}
holds since

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=\lim_{k\rightarrow-\infty}A(k)=\lim_{k\rightarrow-\infty}-d_{k}=-a_{0}=-\log(M_{0}).

Moreover, in the supremum above we can consider all
s\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
. Using the correspondence with the trace function
A
we see that (
3.11
) is valid for all
s\in[0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota}]
with considering the limit at the right-end point of this interval. Note that, analogously to the previous Section
4.1
, by taking into account
(ii)
in Lemma
2.2
, formally we can put
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)=+\infty
for all
s>\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
, i.e. set
A(k)=+\infty
for all
k\in(\mathbf{a}_{\iota},+\infty)
, and use the convention
\frac{1}{+\infty}=0
in order to consider
\sup_{s\geq 0}
instead of
\sup_{s\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})}
in the above representation for
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}
.

(b)

We have the estimate

(4.5)

\forall\;p\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=\sup_{s\in(0,\mathbf{M}_{\iota})}\frac{s^{p}}{\exp(\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s))}\leq\frac{\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{p}}{\exp(-a_{0})}=M_{0}\mathbf{M}_{\iota}^{p},

since
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}
is non-decreasing and for
p=0
we even have equality.

Thus, up to a constant,
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
is lying below a sequence which is increasing geometrically if
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}>1
and decreasing geometrically if
0<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<1
. If
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=1
, then
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
is uniformly bounded by

.

(c)

(

) is not excluding

and in this case for infinitely many indices

the difference between

and

can be seen to be very large: More precisely, by (

) one has

\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{p}}{M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}}\right)^{1/p}\geq\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{(M_{p}/M_{0})^{1/p}}{\mathbf{M}_{\iota}}=+\infty,

thus
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
and
\mathbf{M}
are not equivalent.

Comment
(c)
yields the following consequence.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

0<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<\mathbf{M}_{\sigma}=+\infty.

Then there does not exist a log-convex sequence
\mathbf{L}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{L}
are equivalent.

This result is in contrast to the case where

0<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<\mathbf{M}_{\sigma}<+\infty,

because here
C^{p+1}\leq M_{p}\leq D^{p+1}
for some constants
0<C<1<D
and all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and so
\mathbf{M}
is equivalent to the constant sequence
\mathbf{1}:=(1)_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be a log-convex sequence such that
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{L}
are equivalent. So
\frac{1}{C^{p+1}}L_{p}\leq M_{p}
for some
C\geq 1
and all
p\in\mathbb{N}
is satisfied. Obviously,
\mathbf{L}^{1/C}:=\left(\frac{1}{C^{p+1}}L_{p}\right)_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is also log-convex and since
\mathbf{L}^{1/C}\leq\mathbf{M}
this implies by definition of
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
that
\mathbf{L}^{1/C}\leq\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
, too. However, by assumption clearly
\mathbf{L}^{1/C}
is equivalent to
\mathbf{M}
as well, a contradiction to (
4.6
).
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
0<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}<+\infty
. Then, with
\mu^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}:=\frac{M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}}{M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p-1}}
we get that

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}=\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p})^{1/p}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}

is valid and hence Lemma
2.6
can be applied to
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
and to
C:=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
2.1
and the comments in Section
2.2
applied to
\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
we have that both limits
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\mu^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}
and
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p})^{1/p}
exist and coincide.

Let us show that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p})^{1/p}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}
:

Since
(M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p})^{1/p}\leq(M_{p})^{1/p}
for all
p\geq 1
we clearly get
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p})^{1/p}\leq\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}
. If
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p})^{1/p}<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
, then there exist
C>C_{1}>0
such that
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}<C_{1}^{p}<C^{p}<M_{p}
for all
p
sufficiently large and choosing
D\geq 1
large enough we have
D^{-1}C^{p}\leq M_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
. The auxiliary sequence
\mathbf{L}:=(D^{-1}C^{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
is clearly log-convex, satisfies
\mathbf{L}\leq\mathbf{M}
and so
\mathbf{L}\leq\mathbf{M}^{\operatorname{lc}}
holds. But this yields
D^{-1}C^{p}\leq M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{p}<C_{1}^{p}
for all
p
sufficiently large, a contradiction as
p\rightarrow+\infty
.
∎

We present now examples which illustrate the fact that in general we do not have

resp.

for infinitely many

Example 4.3
.

We consider “almost affine linear” sequences.

(i)

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
be given as follows:

(4.7)

a_{0}:=0,\hskip 30.0pta_{1}:=-1,\hskip 30.0pta_{p}:=cp,\;\;\;p\geq 2,\;c\geq 0.

Then
\widetilde{a}_{0}=a_{0}
,
\widetilde{a}_{1}=a_{1}
, but
\widetilde{a}_{p}<a_{p}
for all

. Indeed, for all
p\geq 2
the points

are projected vertically down onto the line
D_{c}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{c-\varepsilon}
with
D_{c-\varepsilon}
being the straight line passing through

and having slope

.

(ii)

On the other hand, if
a_{1}:=c
instead and all other terms remain unchanged, then
\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}\equiv\mathbf{a}
and so the convex minorant coincides with
\mathbf{a}
. Note that the points
S_{p}=(p,a_{p})
are lying on the straight line having slope

and passing through

and so
\mathbf{a}
is clearly convex. However, the difference compared with Section

is that “during the geometric construction” we do not get infinitely many points where the regularized sequence coincides with the given one and the equality, for infinitely many indices, is finally obtained in the limit: Each
S_{p}
is projected vertically down onto the line
D_{c}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{c-\varepsilon}
with
D_{c-\varepsilon}
denoting the straight line passing through
(0,0)
and having slope
c-\varepsilon
. But
S_{0}=(0,0)
is the only point belonging to the set
\mathcal{S}
and lying on
D_{c-\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
small but arbitrary.

In both cases (
4.2
) is valid with the same value since
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}=c=\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
. Via (
1.2
) we see that
(i)
corresponds to
M_{0}=1
,
M_{1}=e^{-1}
, and
M_{p}=e^{cp}
,
p\geq 2
, resp.
M_{1}=e^{c}
instead in
(ii)
. In both cases
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}=e^{c}
, but in
(i)
we have
\mathbf{M}_{\inf}=e^{-1}<\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
whereas
\mathbf{M}_{\inf}=e^{c}=\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
in
(ii)
.

Using this we show that in general
\mathbf{M}_{\inf}
cannot be replaced by
\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
in
(i)
in Lemma
2.2
: The first example yields
A(k)=0
for
k\in(-\infty,-1]
but then
A(k)\rightarrow c+1
as
k\rightarrow c
resp.
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)\rightarrow c+1
as
s\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
because
(0,-1-c)
is the intersecting point of the
y
-axis with the straight line having slope
c
and passing through
S_{1}=(1,-1)
. More precisely,
A(k)>0
for all
k\in(-1,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
resp.
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{M}}(s)>0
for all
s\in(e^{-1},\mathbf{M}_{\iota})
. By taking into account (
3.11
) we get
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)\rightarrow c+1
as
s\rightarrow\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(s)>0
for all
\mathbf{M}_{\inf}<s\leq\mathbf{M}_{\iota}
.

We close by showing that in the situation studied in this Section it can also appear that one has equality between
\widetilde{\mathbf{a}}
and
\mathbf{a}
for infinitely many indices.

Example 4.4
.

Let
a_{0}:=0
and each
a_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, is assumed to lie on the straight line passing through
S_{0}=(0,0)
and having slope
c_{p}
; i.e.
a_{p}=c_{p}p
. Let
c_{1}:=1
and fix
c>1
. We claim to define iteratively a strictly increasing sequence
(c_{p})_{p\geq 1}
such that

(4.8)

(p+1)c_{p+1}-pc_{p}<c,\;\;\;p\geq 1,\hskip 30.0pt\frac{2(p+1)}{p+2}c_{p+1}-\frac{p}{p+2}c_{p}<c_{p+2},\;\;\;p\geq 1.

The first relation gives that the slope of the line connecting
S_{p}=(p,a_{p})
and
S_{p+1}=(p+1,a_{p+1})
is strictly smaller than the value
c
for any
p\geq 1
. Note that the case for
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
is clear since this slope is given by
c_{1}=1<c
. The second relation in (
4.8
) precisely means that the slope of the line connecting
S_{p}
and
S_{p+1}
is strictly smaller than the one connecting
S_{p+1}
and
S_{p+2}
for all
p\geq 1
. For
p=0
we have to compare the slope
c_{1}
with
2c_{2}-c_{1}
and
c_{1}<2c_{2}-c_{1}\Leftrightarrow c_{1}<c_{2}
since the sequence is strictly increasing as verified below.

Consequently, when applying the geometric approach from before, for which it suffices to consider non-negative slopes, then in fact
\widetilde{a}_{p}=a_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
but
\limsup_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}\leq c<+\infty
.

We introduce
(c_{p})_{p\geq 1}
inductively by

c_{p+1}:=\frac{c}{2(p+1)}+\frac{2p+1}{2(p+1)}c_{p},\;\;\;p\geq 1;

i.e.
c_{p+1}
denotes the middle point of the interval
[c_{p},\frac{c}{p+1}+\frac{p}{p+1}c_{p}=:d_{p}]
.

First
c_{p}<c
for all
p\geq 1
since this is clear for
p=1
and
c_{p+1}=\frac{c}{2(p+1)}+\frac{2p+1}{2(p+1)}c_{p}<c\Leftrightarrow(2p+1)c_{p}<(2p+1)c\Leftrightarrow c_{p}<c
holds then by induction. Similarly, one can prove that
d_{p}<c\Leftrightarrow c_{p}<c
and, moreover,
c_{p}<d_{p}\Leftrightarrow c_{p}<c
holds and hence inductively the choice for
c_{p+1}
makes sense for all
p\geq 1
.

The sequence
(c_{p})_{p}
is strictly increasing since
c_{p}<c_{p+1}\Leftrightarrow c_{p}<\frac{c}{2(p+1)}+\frac{2p+1}{2(p+1)}c_{p}\Leftrightarrow c_{p}<c
.

By definition, the first requirement in (
4.8
) is equivalent to
c_{p}<c
and hence valid. Let us check the second one: We have

\displaystyle\frac{2(p+1)}{p+2}c_{p+1}-\frac{p}{p+2}c_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(p+1)}{p+2}\frac{c}{2(p+1)}+\frac{2(p+1)}{p+2}\frac{2p+1}{2(p+1)}c_{p}-\frac{p}{p+2}c_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{p+2}+\frac{2p+1}{p+2}c_{p}-\frac{p}{p+2}c_{p}=\frac{4pc+4c}{4(p+1)(p+2)}+\frac{4(p+1)(p+1)}{4(p+1)(p+2)}c_{p}

and

\displaystyle c_{p+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2(p+2)}+\frac{2p+3}{2(p+2)}c_{p+1}=\frac{c}{2(p+2)}+\frac{2p+3}{2(p+2)}\frac{c}{2(p+1)}+\frac{2p+3}{2(p+2)}\frac{2p+1}{2(p+1)}c_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{4cp+5c+(2p+3)(2p+1)c_{p}}{4(p+2)(p+1)}.

Thus the required estimate is equivalent to

4pc+5c+(2p+3)(2p+1)c_{p}>4pc+4c+4(p+1)^{2}c_{p}\Leftrightarrow c+(4p^{2}+8p+3)c_{p}>(4p^{2}+8p+4)c_{p}\Leftrightarrow c>c_{p}
and so this holds inductively as shown above for all
p\geq 1
, too.

5.
Regularizing sequences by a regularizing function

We discuss now the more general approach of regularizing a sequence
\mathbf{a}
w.r.t. a regularizing function
\phi
. Let
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and set again
S_{p}:=(p,a_{p})
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathcal{S}:=\{S_{p}:p\in\mathbb{N}\}
as in Section
3.1
. For the whole procedure described in this Section we can formally allow again that
a_{p}=+\infty

for finitely many indices
p\geq 1
;
when
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then also
for infinitely many
p\geq 1
or
even for all
p\geq 1
sufficiently large.

5.1.
Regularizing function

Definition 5.1
.

A function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}
is called a
regularizing function
if:

(I)

\phi
is non-decreasing,

(II)

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\phi(t)=0
,

(III)

\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\phi(t)=+\infty
for
T\in\mathbb{R}
or
T=+\infty
,

(IV)

\phi
is continuous on
(-\infty,T)
for
T\in\mathbb{R}
or
T=+\infty
, with
T
denoting the value from
(III)
.

We write
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
if
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\phi(T)=+\infty
and
\phi\neq+\infty
if
\phi(t)<+\infty
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
; i.e. if
T=+\infty
.

Note: If
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then we set
\phi(t)=+\infty
for all
t\geq T
. We discuss now the geometric process of regularizing the given sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
or the set
\mathcal{S}
w.r.t. a regularizing function
\phi
. The regularized sequence is then denoted by
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}:=(a^{\phi}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
; similarly write
S^{\phi}_{p}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\phi}
. We work geometrically in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with coordinates
(x,y)
. A special case, formally not within the scope of the basic properties
(I)-(IV)
, is
\phi=+\infty
; i.e.
\phi(t)=+\infty
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The forthcoming construction gives resp. reproves in this special situation the approach from Sections
3
and
4
.

5.2.
Geometric notions

The main idea in order to proceed is to consider the following purely geometric construction depending on
\phi
and
\mathbf{a}
:

(1)

First, we introduce the set

B_{t}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0\leq x\leq\phi(t)\},\;\;\;t\in\mathbb{R};

i.e.
B_{t}
is the stripe on the right-half plane in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with width
\phi(t)
.

(2)

Second, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
we define
D_{t}
to be the intersection of
B_{t}
with the straight line having slope
t
and satisfying the following two properties:

(2.1)

There exists at least one element in the set
\mathcal{S}\cap B_{t}
such that this point also belongs to
D_{t}
and

(2.2)

such that none of the points
S_{p}\in B_{t}
is lying below
D_{t}
.

In other words:
When
t\in\mathbb{R}
is given and fixed, then we move the straight line with slope
t
parallel “from below” until it touches at least one of the points
S_{p}\in B_{t}
. Then the intersection of this line with
B_{t}
is denoted by
D_{t}
.

Occasionally, we consider the “limiting case”
B_{-\infty}
resp.
D_{-\infty}
and both sets correspond to the
y
-axis.

First let us comment on the following fact: The previously defined setting is equivalent to the even more general situation when we consider in the stripe
B_{t}
the segment
D_{\psi(t)}
having slope
\psi(t)
with
\psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
being a strictly increasing and continuous function such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\psi(t)=-\infty
,
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\psi(t)=+\infty
. This follows because in this more general case we consider the reparametrization
\phi\circ\psi^{-1}
, which is clearly again a regularizing function, and apply the framework in this Section to
\phi\circ\psi^{-1}
. Note that such a reparametrization does not change the crucial different behavior of the regularizing function:
\phi=+\infty
,
\phi\neq+\infty
, and
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
.

Next let us see that the mapping
t\mapsto D_{t}
is well-defined in the sense that for each given
t\in\mathbb{R}
we can find at least one point
S_{p}\in D_{t}
.

(*)

If there exists some
T\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi(t)=0
for all
t\in(-\infty,T]
, then
B_{t}
coincides with the
y
-axis for all
t\in(-\infty,T]
. In this case for all such
t
the segment
D_{t}
consists only of its intersection point with the
y
-axis; i.e. we have
D_{t}=S_{0}
. Note that
S_{0}=(0,a_{0})
and
a_{0}>-\infty
.

(*)

For any regularizing function
\phi
, since
\phi\geq 0
, at least
S_{0}
belongs to each
B_{t}
and hence is lying on
D_{t}
.

(*)

If
\phi\neq+\infty
, then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
with
\phi(t)<1
the point
S_{0}
is indeed the only one and whenever
\phi(t)\geq 1
then (at least) a second point
S_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, belongs to
B_{t}
. This comment should be compared with the “normalization condition”
\phi(0)\geq 1
used in
[
13
]
.

For this fact it is crucial to assume naturally that
a_{p}>-\infty
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and which is ensured since
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
: Otherwise, if there exists
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
with
a_{p_{0}}=-\infty
, then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi(t)\geq p_{0}
the geometric idea is not working since we would have to put
D_{t}:y=-\infty
in order to give this case a meaning. However, if “only” (
3.2
) is violated, i.e. if

(5.1)

\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}a_{p}=-\infty,

then as
t\rightarrow+\infty
the lines
D_{t}
are passing through points whose
y
-coordinate is approaching the value
-\infty
. So even this case can be treated for such regularizing functions and the case if (
3.3
) is violated is similar.

(*)

If
\phi=+\infty
, then each
B_{t}
is the whole right half-plane/space and so
D_{t}
is a half-ray and we are allowed to use half-rays with arbitrary slope
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus this case corresponds to the situation treated in Sections
3
and
4
. Hence, for
t\mapsto D_{t}
being well-defined one should avoid (
4.1
) since in this case for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
at least one point will lie strictly below
D_{t}
; see Section
4.1
for this degenerate case.

(*)

If
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then as
t\rightarrow T
the set
B_{t}
is tending to the right-half plane and so
D_{t}
to a half-ray having slope
T
. Here it is also clear that
t\mapsto D_{t}
is well-defined for any
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and, as we are going to see, this situation allows to treat formally also sequences
\mathbf{a}
such that
a_{p}=+\infty
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
sufficiently large. Similarly as for the case
\phi\neq+\infty
, even if (
3.2
) resp. (
3.3
) is violated, the construction makes sense.

5.3.
Principal indices

Now consider the subsequence
(S_{p_{i}})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of points with the following property: There exists some
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
S_{p_{i}}
is lying on
D_{t}
. The corresponding sequence of indices
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is called the sequence of
principal indices
and
S_{p_{i}}
is called a
principal point.

(i)

S_{p_{0}}=S_{0}
holds for any regularizing function
\phi
.

(ii)

If
\phi\neq+\infty
, then
(S_{p_{i}})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
consists of infinitely many points: If there exists an index
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S_{p}\notin D_{t}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and for all
p>p_{i_{0}}
, then
\phi(t)\rightarrow+\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, and hence we are allowed to consider arbitrary large slopes. Consequently, we have
a_{p}=+\infty
for all
p>p_{i_{0}}
. But this is a contradiction to the basic assumption that
a_{p}=+\infty
for possibly at most finitely many
p\geq 1
.

(iii)

If
\phi=+\infty
, then in order to guarantee the existence of infinitely many principal indices we have to assume (
3.1
); i.e. the standard approach treated in Section
3
. If (
3.1
) is violated, then in general one only has finitely many indices
p_{i}
; see Section
4
and, in particular, Example
4.3
: In
(i)
there we can find two indices
p_{0}=0
and
p_{1}=1
, whereas only
p_{0}=0
in
(ii)
. In the situation treated in Section
4.1
there exists only one principal index, namely
p_{0}=0
. Concerning Section
4.2
we point out that the behavior of the sequence
(a_{p}/p)_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
near
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
is crucial, see also Example
4.4
: For sure there exist finitely many principal indices if
\frac{a_{p}}{p}\geq\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
for all but possibly finitely many values
p\geq 1
.

(iv)

If
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then it is also not clear that in general infinitely many principal indices do exist even if
\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}a_{p}<+\infty
: Assume that
p_{i_{0}}
is a principal index, and there exists at least one, namely
p_{0}=0
, but all points
S_{p}
with
p\geq i_{0}+1
are lying on or even strictly above the straight line with slope
T
and which is passing through
S_{p_{i_{0}}}
. This line is given by

(5.2)

\ell_{T}:s\mapsto sT+d_{T}=sT+a_{p_{i_{0}}}-Tp_{i_{0}}=T(s-p_{i_{0}})+a_{p_{i_{0}}},

and so the situation described before holds if

\forall\;p\geq p_{i_{0}}+1:\;\;\;a_{p}\geq T(p-p_{i_{0}})+a_{p_{i_{0}}}.

As
t\rightarrow T
we are only allowed to approach
\ell_{T}
, formally
\ell_{T}=D_{T}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{T-\varepsilon}
, and hence all
S_{p}
,
p\geq i_{0}+1
, will lie strictly above all segments
D_{t}
under consideration and
p_{i_{0}}
is the “last principal index”.

If there exist only finitely many principal indices then we say that eventually
\mathbf{a}
lies on lines having at least slope
T
.

(*)

On the one hand, if
\mathbf{a}
has this property, then
\frac{a_{p}}{p}\geq T(1-\frac{p_{i_{0}}}{p})+\frac{a_{p_{i_{0}}}}{p}
for all
p\geq p_{i_{0}}+1
with
p_{i_{0}}
denoting the last principal index, and hence

(5.3)

\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}\geq T.

Thus,

(5.4)

\liminf_{p\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{p}}{p}<T,

is sufficient to ensure the existence of infinitely many principal indices
p_{i}
.

(*)

On the other hand, if in (
5.3
) we have a strict inequality, then we can find
\epsilon>0
and
p_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{p}\geq Tp+\epsilon p
for all
p\geq p_{\epsilon}
. So eventually each
S_{p}
is lying strictly above the straight line with slope
T
and passing through the origin. In this case there exist finitely many principal indices.

For sure there exist only finitely many principal indices if
a_{p}=+\infty
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
sufficiently large; a formal situation which can be considered only for this case. Moreover, in this setting we can assume that for infinitely many
p\geq 1
we have
a_{p}=+\infty
.

Summarizing,
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
is very similar to
\phi=+\infty
in Section
4.2
: In the first case the admissible slopes are uniformly bounded by
T
and in the latter case by the value
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
. However, in Section
4.2
formally we are allowed to consider
a_{p}=+\infty
for infinitely many
p\geq 1
but
not for all
p\geq 1
sufficiently large
since this contradicts (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).

5.4.
Indices of discontinuity

The following explanations illustrate the difference between
\phi=+\infty
, yielding the (log-)convex minorant, and
\phi\neq+\infty
where so-called
indices of discontinuity
can appear. So let now
\phi\neq+\infty
:

(a)

Take a principal index
p_{i}
, let
t_{1}<t_{2}
and assume that
S_{p_{i}}\in D_{t_{1}}
and that
S_{p_{i}}\in D_{t_{2}}
. Then
S_{p_{i}}\in D_{t}
holds for all
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
as well. So for each
p_{i}
we can associate an interval
I_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that
S_{p_{i}}\in D_{t}
for all
t\in I_{i}
.

(b)

We gather some properties for these intervals:

(b.1)

For each
i\geq 1
the length of
I_{i}
is finite because otherwise this contradicts
a_{p}=+\infty
for possibly at most only finitely many
p\geq 1
.

(b.2)

I_{i}
is, in general, of the form
I_{i}=[t_{i},\tau_{i})
and
t_{i}\in I_{i}
holds because
\phi
is continuous. For
i=0
we have
I_{0}=[-\infty,\tau_{0})
and the slope
-\infty
is formally corresponding to the
y
-axis on which only
S_{0}
is lying.

(b.3)

Clearly
\tau_{i}=t_{i+1}
is valid for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

(b.4)

One might have
I_{i}=t_{i}=t_{i+1}
: If
t_{i}=t_{i+1}=\dots=t_{i+k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, then all points
S_{p_{i}},\dots,S_{p_{i+k}}
are lying on the joint line segment
D_{t_{i}}
with slope
t_{i}
. However, this cannot be the case for
I_{0}
since then
S_{p_{1}}
would be lying on the
y
-axis.

(b.5)

Finally,
t_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
as
i\rightarrow+\infty
is also clear:
\phi(t)\rightarrow+\infty
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
but for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
we only deal with finitely many
S_{p}\in B_{t}
and since
a_{p}=+\infty
for only possibly finitely many
p\geq 1
.

(c)

Let
p_{i}
be a principal index. If
\tau_{i}\notin I_{i}
or if
\tau_{i}\in I_{i}
depends on the following relation between
\phi(\tau_{i})
and
p_{i+1}
:

(c.1)

The case
\phi(\tau_{i})=\phi(t_{i+1})<p_{i+1}
is not possible by continuity of
\phi
, since
\phi
is non-decreasing and by definition of
I_{i}
and
p_{i+1}
: In this situation the requirement
S_{p_{i+1}}\in D_{\tau_{i}}=D_{t_{i+1}}
is impossible.

(c.2)

If
\phi(\tau_{i})=\phi(t_{i+1})>p_{i+1}
, then
I_{i}=[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
since
S_{p_{i+1}}\in B_{t_{i+1}}
.

(c.3)

If
\phi(\tau_{i})=\phi(t_{i+1})=p_{i+1}
, then
I_{i}=[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
if and only if the points
S_{p_{i}}
,
S_{p_{i+1}}
both lie on the segment
D_{t_{i+1}}
. However, this fact is
not clear in general.

Principal indices
p_{i+1}
with this property are called
indices of discontinuity.

(c.4)

We illustrate this situation: Assume that
a_{p_{i}}=a_{p_{{i+1}}}
, and so the points
S_{p_{i}}
,
S_{p_{i+1}}
have the same
y
-coordinate, and that all
S_{p}
,
0\leq p<p_{i+1}
with
p\neq p_{i}
, have the property that they are lying strictly above each segment
D_{t}
,
0<t_{i}\leq t<t_{i+1}
, which is passing through
S_{p_{i}}
and having positive slope
t
.

Then, by definition, for
t=t_{i+1}
the segment
D_{t}
is passing through
S_{p_{i+1}}
but
S_{p_{i}}
is lying strictly above
D_{t_{i+1}}
and so
p_{i+1}
is an index of discontinuity.

(d)

Recall:
t_{i}=t_{i+1}=\dots=t_{i+k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
means that all points
S_{p_{i}},\dots,S_{p_{i+k}}
are lying on a joint line segment and hence
p_{i+1},\dots,p_{i+k}
are not indices of discontinuity.

Note also that
p_{0}=0
is never an index of discontinuity.

Now let us consider the case where
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
. At the beginning, for all sufficiently small
t
we can proceed as above for
\phi\neq+\infty
. Note that there exist at least finitely many principal indices
p_{i}
; indeed there exists at least one such index, namely
p_{0}=0
. As
t\rightarrow T
we have that
\phi(t)\rightarrow+\infty
, i.e. the width of
B_{t}
is tending to
+\infty
, but
D_{t}\rightarrow D_{T}
and so the slopes of the segments
D_{t}
under consideration are uniformly bounded by
T
. Thus we have to distinguish; see
(iv)
in Section
5.3
:

(a)

There exist finitely many principal indices: First, since the set of indices of discontinuity is a subset of all principal indices there exist for sure also at most finitely many indices of discontinuity. When
p_{i_{0}}
is denoting the last principal index, then we set
I_{i_{0}}:=[t_{i_{0}},T)
.

(b)

There exist infinitely many principal indices: Here the situation is analogous to the study of
\phi\neq+\infty
before. However, note that the length of the intervals
I_{i}
is tending to
0
as
i\rightarrow+\infty
since the maximal admissible slope is bounded by
T
.

Finally, let us consider
\phi=+\infty
:

(a)

In any case, the set of indices of discontinuity is empty since
(c.2)
before holds automatically for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
; see Sections
3
and
4
.

(b)

More precisely, in the exotic situation treated in Section
4.1
we have
I_{0}=\{-\infty\}
and this is the only “interval” under consideration.

(c)

In the case treated in Section
4.2
we have to distinguish: If there exist infinitely many principal indices, then to each of these we can assign an interval
I_{i}=[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
and with
I_{0}=[-\infty,t_{1}]
. Here the length of
I_{i}
is tending to
0
since the maximal admissible slope is bounded by
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
. If there exist only finitely many principal indices, say
i_{0}
many, then the last interval is given by
I_{i_{0}}=[t_{i_{0}},\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
.

Note:
In the case of
(i)
in Example
4.3
we have
p_{0}=0
and
p_{1}=1
and this is the last principal index. Then
I_{0}=[-\infty,-1]
and
I_{1}=[-1,c)
and no further intervals appear. In
(ii)
there we have
I_{0}=[-\infty,c)
and no further interval is considered.

5.5.
Definition of the regularized sequence

We define the crucial sequence of line segments
(L_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
as follows; first we focus on
\phi\neq+\infty
and comment then on the other situations:

(I)

If
p_{i}
and
p_{i+1}
are principal indices, then
L_{i}
shall denote the segment of the straight line on
[p_{i},p_{i+1})
, i.e.
\operatorname{pr}_{1}(L_{i})=[p_{i},p_{i+1})
, such that
L_{i}
is having slope
\tau_{i}=t_{i+1}
and it is passing through the point
S_{p_{i}}=(p_{i},a_{p_{i}})
. Thus, we get

L_{i}:s\mapsto t_{i+1}s+a_{p_{i}}-t_{i+1}p_{i}=t_{i+1}(s-p_{i})+a_{p_{i}},\;\;\;p_{i}\leq s<p_{i+1},\;i\in\mathbb{N}.

(II)

If
p_{i+1}
is not an index of discontinuity, then we have
L_{i}\subseteq D_{t_{i+1}}
. If
p_{i+1}
is an index of discontinuity, then
L_{i}
is parallel to
D_{t_{i+1}}
but is strictly lying over
D_{t_{i+1}}
because
\phi(t_{i+1})=\phi(\tau_{i})=p_{i+1}
and
S_{p_{i}}
lies strictly above
D_{t_{i+1}}
. The slopes of
L_{i}
are non-decreasing and tending to
+\infty
as
i\rightarrow+\infty
.

Then consider

(5.5)

\mathcal{L}:=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}L_{i},

and hence
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
consists of a set of straight lines having the following properties:

(*)

At all principal indices, except at
p_{0}=0
, cusps appear and at all indices of discontinuity a jump.

(*)

More precisely, between
p_{0}
and the first index of discontinuity, and then between two consecutive indices of discontinuity, the set of lines is connected, having cusps at all principal indices. Finally it is closed on the left-hand side and open on the right-hand side at the jumps.

We introduce the regularizing mapping
r^{\phi}
by

(5.6)

S_{p}=(p,a_{p})\mapsto S_{p}^{\phi}:=(p,a_{p}^{\phi}),

where
S_{p}^{\phi}
denotes the
vertical projection
of the point
S_{p}
onto the set
\mathcal{L}
; i.e.
a_{p}
is projected vertically down onto
a_{p}^{\phi}
. This gives the regularized set
\mathcal{S}^{\phi}:=\{S_{p}^{\phi}=(p,a_{p}^{\phi}):p\in\mathbb{N}\}
and the regularized sequence
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}:=(a^{\phi}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

(\alpha)

a^{\phi}_{p}\leq a_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\beta)

(\mathbf{a}^{\phi})^{\phi}=\mathbf{a}^{\phi}
,

(\gamma)

a^{\phi}_{p}=a_{p}
if and only if
p
is a principal index. In particular, we have
a^{\phi}_{0}=a_{0}
.

We comment now on the other cases for
\phi
. The corresponding sequence
S_{p}^{\phi}:=(p,a_{p}^{\phi})
is defined analogously via (
5.6
) but the set
\mathcal{L}
can differ from the case
\phi\neq+\infty
.

(a)

Let
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
and distinguish:

(a.1)

If there exist infinitely many principal indices, then the only difference compared with
\phi\neq+\infty
is that the slopes of the lines
L_{i}
are tending to
T
as
i\rightarrow+\infty
.

(a.2)

If there exist finitely many principal indices, say
i_{0}
many, then the set
\mathcal{L}
in (
5.5
) has the form

\mathcal{L}:=\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq i_{0}}L_{i};

i.e. it consists only of
i_{0}+1
lines. The first
i_{0}
lines are as described before and the “last member”
L_{i_{0}}
is defined to be the line with slope
T
and passing through the point
S_{p_{i_{0}}}
; i.e. we get
L_{i_{0}}:=D_{T}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{T-\varepsilon}
.

In view of this definition we see that in this case formally it makes sense to consider
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}
even if
\mathbf{a}=(a_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
a_{p}=+\infty
for all
p\geq 1
sufficiently large: One has only finitely many principal indices and (
5.6
) makes sense since for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
large the
y
-coordinate
+\infty
is “projected vertically down” onto the line
L_{i_{0}}
.

(b)

Let
\phi=+\infty
and distinguish:

(b.1)

If
\mathbf{a}
satisfies (
3.1
), then
\mathcal{L}
is precisely the set of lines described in Section
3
. More precisely,
\mathcal{L}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}L_{i}
is connected since the set of indices of discontinuity is empty and all further properties of
\mathcal{L}
are the same as for
\phi<+\infty
.

(b.2)

In the exotic situation treated in Section
4.1
formally we have
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{0}\}
and
L_{0}
denotes the
y
-axis since this is the only line under consideration. The set of principal indices is equal to
\{p_{0}\}
and the set of indices of discontinuity is empty. However, in order to make the definition (
5.6
) fit with the approach in Section
4.1
we put
\mathcal{L}=\{L_{0},L_{1}\}
with
L_{1}:y=-\infty
since all
S_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, are formally projected down onto this line.

(b.3)

In the situation of Section
4.2
the set of indices of discontinuity is again empty. However, we have either
\mathcal{L}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}L_{i}
, if there exist infinitely many principal indices, or
\mathcal{L}:=\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq i_{0}}L_{i}
else. In the latter case the last member
L_{i_{0}}
is denoting the line passing through the “last principal point”
S_{p_{i_{0}}}
and having slope
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
; i.e.
L_{i_{0}}
coincides with
D_{\mathbf{a}_{\iota}}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{\mathbf{a}_{\iota}-\varepsilon}
on
[p_{i_{0}},+\infty)
.

In all instances properties
(\alpha)
,
(\beta)
,
(\gamma)
hold. However, for
(\beta
) in
(b.2)
recall the convention there, so note that
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}\notin\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and thus
\mathbf{a}\mapsto\mathbf{a}^{\phi}
is not a mapping
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
anymore.

5.6.
Comparison between regularizing functions

Let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed.

(*)

Given two regularizing functions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
with
\phi_{1}\leq\phi_{2}
then we obtain immediately
\mathbf{a}^{\phi_{2}}\leq\mathbf{a}^{\phi_{1}}
.

(*)

Since the “largest possible” or “most extreme” example is given by
\phi=+\infty
we have

(5.7)

\mathbf{a}^{c}\leq\mathbf{a}^{\phi}\leq\mathbf{a},

for all
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and all regularizing functions
\phi
.

(*)

In view of (
5.7
) the constructions treated in Sections
3
and
4
yield the smallest possible sequence within this geometric approach. Indeed, in Section
4.1
one obtains the smallest possible regularized “sequence”.

5.7.
The counting function

Let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\phi
be a regularizing function. Then set

(5.8)

m^{\phi}(t):=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\{S_{p}\in D_{t}\},\;\;\;t\in\{-\infty\}\cup J^{\phi},

where
J^{\phi}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is the natural “interval of definition” depending on
\phi
. Recall that
D_{-\infty}
corresponds to the
y
-axis and let
J^{\phi}_{r}
be the right-end point of
J^{\phi}
; i.e. the limit point. We describe this situation in detail:

(a)

For any regularizing function
\phi
one has
m^{\phi}(t)=0
for all sufficiently small
t
since in this case
S_{0}
is the only point lying on
D_{t}
. We also put
m^{\phi}(-\infty):=0
since
S_{0}
is clearly the only point belonging to
\mathcal{S}
and lying on the
y
-axis.

(b)

If
\phi\neq+\infty
, then
m^{\phi}:\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{>0}
is well-defined; i.e.
m^{\phi}(t)<+\infty
for all
t\in\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}
, because there are only finitely many points
S_{p}
which belong to
B_{t}
and hence are possibly lying on the segment
D_{t}
. Thus we have
J^{\phi}=\mathbb{R}
and
J^{\phi}_{r}=+\infty
.

(c)

Similarly, if
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then
m^{\phi}
is well-defined on
[-\infty,T)
since for each
t\in[-\infty,T)
only finitely many points
S_{p}
can belong to
B_{t}
. In this case
J^{\phi}=(-\infty,T)
and so
J^{\phi}_{r}=T
.

(d)

Let
\phi=+\infty
, then we distinguish:

(d.1)

m^{\phi}
is well-defined on
\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}
provided that
\mathbf{a}
satisfies (
3.1
): When
t\in\mathbb{R}
is fixed, then eventually all
S_{p}
are lying strictly above the half-ray
D_{t}
; see Section
3.1
. Hence
J^{\phi}=\mathbb{R}
and
J^{\phi}_{r}=+\infty
is valid.

(d.2)

If (
3.1
) is violated and
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}>-\infty
, see (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), then
m^{\phi}
is well-defined on
[-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
and so
J^{\phi}=(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
and
J^{\phi}_{r}=\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
; see Section
4.2
. However, note that the value
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
is excluded since in general one might have
m^{\phi}(\mathbf{a}_{\iota})=+\infty
; see
(ii)
in Example
4.3
:

If
c\in\mathbb{R}
is fixed and
a_{p}:=cp
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
, then
m^{\phi}(t)=0
for
t\in[-\infty,c)
and
m^{\phi}(c)=+\infty
. Recall that this sequence is already convex and all
S_{p}
are lying on the straight line passing through the origin and having slope
c
. This is a situation which is excluded by the standard requirement (
3.1
).

(d.3)

If
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}=-\infty
, i.e. (
4.1
) in Section
4.1
, then we consider
m^{\phi}
only at
-\infty
with
m^{\phi}(-\infty):=0
. In this case we have
J^{\phi}=\emptyset
.

Note that in all cases clearly
\lim_{t\rightarrow J^{\phi}_{r}}\phi(t)=+\infty
and for
(d.3)
we have
\phi=+\infty
in any case. Next we summarize some properties for
m^{\phi}
:

(x)

m^{\phi}
is non-decreasing on
J^{\phi}
; for case
(d.3)
this statement is empty: Let
t_{1}<t_{2}
, then we distinguish two cases. If
S_{m^{\phi}(t_{1})}\in D_{t_{2}}
, then obviously
m^{\phi}(t_{1})\leq m^{\phi}(t_{2})
holds. If
S_{m^{\phi}(t_{1})}
lies above the segment
D_{t_{2}}
, then all points
S_{p}
with
0\leq p\leq m^{\phi}(t_{1})
lie above
D_{t_{2}}
as well. Thus we obtain in this case even
m^{\phi}(t_{1})<m^{\phi}(t_{2})
.

(xx)

Now we study the limit case; i.e. the behavior of
m^{\phi}
at the right end point of
J^{\phi}
again except the trivial “constant/one-point” case in
(d.3)
.

(xx.1)

If
\phi\neq+\infty
, then
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}m^{\phi}(t)=+\infty
: Suppose that there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m^{\phi}(t)\leq k
for all
t\in\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}
, then
a_{p}=+\infty
for all
p>k
follows which is a contradiction to the basic assumption on
\mathbf{a}
.

(xx.2)

If
\phi=+\infty
and such that
\mathbf{a}
satisfies (
3.1
), then
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}m^{\phi}(t)=+\infty
holds by similar reasons; see Section
3.1
.

(xx.3)

If
\phi=+\infty
, (
3.1
) is violated and
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}>-\infty
, see case
(d.2)
before, then we have that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\mathbf{a}_{\iota}}m^{\phi}(t)=+\infty
if and only if there exist infinitely many principal indices; see Example
4.4
and
(iii)
in Section
5.3
.

So here this limit might be finite and for this consider Example
4.3
: The sequence given by (
4.7
) in
(i)
yields
\lim_{t\rightarrow\mathbf{a}_{\iota}}m^{\phi}(t)=1
and in
(ii)
we even get
\lim_{t\rightarrow\mathbf{a}_{\iota}}m^{\phi}(t)=0
.

(xx.4)

If
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then similarly the value of
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}m^{\phi}(t)
depends on the existence of finitely or infinitely many principal indices and so on the relation between
T
and
\mathbf{a}
. If
\mathbf{a}
satisfies (
5.4
), then
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}m^{\phi}(t)=+\infty
, but if
\mathbf{a}
eventually lies over lines having slope
T
, then this value is finite and can even be equal to
0
.

(xxx)

m^{\phi}
is continuous from the right on
J^{\phi}
. Indeed, in the case treated in
(d.3)
there is nothing to show. For the segments
D_{t}
we have
D_{t}=D_{t}^{+}
, where

D_{t}^{+}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{t+\varepsilon},

hence the mapping
t\mapsto D_{t}
is right-continuous. If
p_{i}
is not an index of discontinuity, then we obtain
D_{t}=D_{t}^{-}
and recall the notation
D_{t}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{t-\varepsilon}
. Analogously we define
(m^{\phi})^{+}(t)
and with this notation we have to show
m^{\phi}(t)=(m^{\phi})^{+}(t)
for all
t\in J^{\phi}
. Because
m^{\phi}(t)\in\mathbb{N}
for all
t\in J^{\phi}
we have
(m^{\phi})^{+}(t)\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
m^{\phi}(t+\varepsilon)=(m^{\phi})^{+}(t)
for all
\varepsilon>0
with
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. Then
S_{(m^{\phi})^{+}(t)}\in D_{t+\varepsilon}
holds for all those
\varepsilon
, hence
S_{(m^{\phi})^{+}(t)}\in D_{t}
because
D_{t}=D_{t}^{+}
. Finally
(m^{\phi})^{+}(t)\leq m^{\phi}(t)
implies the desired property.

5.8.
The trace function

For each
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and each regularizing function
\phi
we can associate a weight function, the so-called
trace function

A^{\phi}:J^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. In order to lighten notation we suppress the dependence on the given sequence
\mathbf{a}
except in (
5.11
).
A^{\phi}
is defined analogously as in (
3.5
) as follows:

For
t\in J^{\phi}
the value
-A^{\phi}(t)
denotes the
y
-coordinate of the unique intersection point of the segment
D_{t}
with the line
\{(0,y):y\in\mathbb{R}\}
; i.e. with the
y
-axis. Moreover, we extend the definition and put
A(-\infty):=-a_{0}
and so
A^{\phi}:\{-\infty\}\cup J^{\phi}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

If we take an arbitrary straight line
\ell
with slope
t\in\mathbb{R}
and which is passing through some point
S_{p}=(p,a_{p})
with
S_{p}\in B_{t}
, then the
y
-coordinate of the intersection point of
\ell
and the
y
-axis is equal to
a_{p}-pt
.

Let us gather some immediate consequences:

(*)

If
J^{\phi}=\emptyset
, i.e. in the situation of case
(d.3)
in Section
5.7
, then
A(-\infty):=-a_{0}
and
-\infty
the only value for which
A
is defined.

Assume now that
J^{\phi}
is non-empty.

(*)

By definition
A^{\phi}
is strictly increasing on
J^{\phi}
.

(*)

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}A^{\phi}(t)=-a_{0}
holds since for all
t
sufficiently small we have that
S_{0}=(0,a_{0})
is the only point belonging to
B_{t}
; for this recall that
\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\phi(t)=0
and so
m^{\phi}(t)=0
for all
t
sufficiently small. The same is valid if
\phi=+\infty
for the cases
(d.1)
and
(d.2)
in Section
5.7
,

(*)

A^{\phi}
is right-continuous and it is not continuous at
t=t_{i}
if
p_{i}
is an index of discontinuity yielding a jump. Consequently, for
\phi=+\infty
the function
A^{\phi}
is continuous.

(*)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}A^{\phi}(t)=+\infty
holds if the right-end point of
J^{\phi}
is equal to
+\infty
, so if
\phi<+\infty
or if
\phi=+\infty
and
\mathbf{a}
has (
3.1
).

More precisely, in these cases we are allowed to consider arbitrary large slopes
t
and therefore the
y
-coordinates
a_{p}-pt
cannot be uniformly bounded away from
-\infty
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Otherwise, for all
t
large enough at least finitely many points
S_{p}
, with
1\leq p\leq p_{0}
for some
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
depending on this fixed
t
, will lie strictly below
D_{t}
.

(*)

If
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\lim_{t\rightarrow J^{\phi}_{r}}A^{\phi}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow T}A^{\phi}(t)
can be finite or not depending on the growth behavior of
\mathbf{a}
. For example one has
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}A^{\phi}(t)<+\infty
provided there exist finitely many principal indices: Let
p_{i_{0}}
be the last one, then
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}A^{\phi}(t)=-(a_{p_{i_{0}}}-Tp_{i_{0}})=Tp_{i_{0}}-a_{p_{i_{0}}}
; see (
5.2
) in
(iv)
in Section
5.3
.

(*)

Moreover, in the situation of
(d.2)
in Section
5.7
, i.e. if
\phi=+\infty
, (
3.1
) is violated and
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}>-\infty
, then we also have that
\lim_{t\rightarrow J^{\phi}_{r}}A^{\phi}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\mathbf{a}_{\iota}}A^{\phi}(t)<+\infty
: The admissible slope of the lines under consideration is bounded by
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
and the limiting case is a straight line with slope
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
and being parallel to the line
t\mapsto t\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
; see
(i)
in Example
4.3
. The negative value of the
y
-coordinate of the intersection point of this line with the
y
-axis is the limit under consideration. If
a_{p}=cp
for
c\in\mathbb{R}
and all
p\in\mathbb{N}
, then in fact
A(t)=0
for all
t\in(-\infty,c)
and even in the limit we get
A(c)=0
; see
(ii)
in Example
4.3
.

We comment in more detail on the particular cases:

(i)

If
\phi\neq+\infty
, then by definition of
D_{t}
and the trace function we see that
a_{p}-pt\leq-A^{\phi}(t)\Leftrightarrow A^{\phi}(t)\leq pt-a_{p}
for all
0\leq p\leq\phi(t)
and equality holds, in particular, for
p=m^{\phi}(t)
. Thus we summarize, compare with (
3.7
):

(5.9)

A^{\phi}(t)=\max_{p\in\mathbb{N}:\;0\leq p\leq\phi(t)}\{pt-a_{p}\}=m^{\phi}(t)t-a_{m^{\phi}(t)},\;\;\;t\in J^{\phi}=\mathbb{R}.

(ii)

If
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then (
5.9
) holds for all
t\in(-\infty,T)
.

(iii)

Let
\phi=+\infty
, then we distinguish:

(iii.1)

If (
3.1
) holds, then (
5.9
) is valid for all
t\in\mathbb{R}(=J^{\phi})
and
\max_{p\in\mathbb{N}:\;0\leq p\leq\phi(t)}
is replaced by
\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
.

(iii.2)

If (
3.1
) is violated and
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}>-\infty
, then (
5.9
) follows analogously for all
t\in J^{\phi}=(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
and
\max_{p\in\mathbb{N}:\;0\leq p\leq\phi(t)}
is replaced again by
\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
.

(iii.3)

Finally, if (
3.1
) is violated and
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}=-\infty
, i.e. (
4.1
) in Section
4.1
), then we have
J^{\phi}=\emptyset
and recall that we put
A^{\phi}(-\infty):=-a_{0}=-a_{m^{\phi}(-\infty)}
and
-\infty
is the only point at which formally
A^{\phi}
is defined.

Summarizing,
m^{\phi}
and
A^{\phi}
are related by

(5.10)

A^{\phi}(t)=tm^{\phi}(t)-a_{m^{\phi}(t)},\;\;\;t\in\;J^{\phi};

i.e.
m^{\phi}
is the right-derivative of
A^{\phi}
. Indeed, with
A(-\infty):=-a_{0}
, see the comment in
(iii.3)
, and the convention
0\cdot(-\infty):=0
we have that (
5.10
) can be extended to
t=-\infty
in all instances.

To each sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and each regularizing function
\phi
we have constructed a unique trace function
A^{\phi}
. But there exist different sequences which lead to the same trace function. In particular, and this is immediate by the definitions given in Section
5.5
, we have that
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}
yield the same trace function, i.e.

(5.11)

A_{\mathbf{a}^{\phi}}^{\phi}=A_{\mathbf{a}}^{\phi},

for all
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and all regularizing functions
\phi
. This fact even holds in the exotic situation
(iii.3)
since
A^{\phi}_{\mathbf{a}}(-\infty)=-a_{0}=-a^{\phi}_{0}=A_{\mathbf{a}^{\phi}}^{\phi}(-\infty)
and
-\infty
is the only point for which (
5.11
) should be verified but, formally, (
5.11
) can be extended to whole
\mathbb{R}
by setting
A_{\mathbf{a}^{\phi}}^{\phi}(t)=+\infty=A_{\mathbf{a}}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

5.9.
Computing the regularized sequence via the trace function

In this final Section we show how, when
\mathbf{a}
is given, the trace function
A^{\phi}\equiv A_{\mathbf{a}}^{\phi}
can be used to compute
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}
and hence
\mathcal{S}^{\phi}
. Summarizing, one is able to study
\mathbf{a}\mapsto A_{\mathbf{a}}^{\phi}\mapsto\mathbf{a}^{\phi}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
and a regularizing function
\phi
be given with
J^{\phi}\neq\emptyset
. Then, the sequence
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}=(a^{\phi}_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}
can be expressed by

(5.12)

a^{\phi}_{p}=\sup_{t\in J^{\phi}:\phi(t)\geq p}\{pt-A^{\phi}(t)\},\;\;\;p\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore recall that
\lim_{t\rightarrow J^{\phi}_{r}}\phi(t)=+\infty
and so each
p\in\mathbb{N}
can be computed via (
5.12
).

For the particular cases we mention:

(i)

If
J^{\phi}=\emptyset
, i.e. if
\phi=+\infty
and
\mathbf{a}
has (
4.1
), then (
5.12
) is formally not applicable; see also Section
4.1
. But when using the conventions
0\cdot(-\infty):=0
and
p\cdot(-\infty):=-\infty
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and recalling
A^{\phi}(-\infty):=-a_{0}
, then in (
5.12
) one can consider in any case
t\in\{-\infty\}\cup J^{\phi}
. And this gives here again
a^{\phi}_{0}=a_{0}
and
a^{\phi}_{p}=-\infty
for
p\geq 1
.

(ii)

If
\phi=+\infty
and
\mathbf{a}
has (
3.1
), then
\sup_{t\in J^{\phi}:\phi(t)\geq p}
can be replaced by
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

(iii)

If
\phi=+\infty
and
\mathbf{a}
has (
4.2
), then
\sup_{t\in J^{\phi}:\phi(t)\geq p}
can be replaced by
\sup_{t\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}
.

(iv)

If
\phi\neq+\infty
resp.
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\sup_{t\in J^{\phi}:\phi(t)\geq p}
can be replaced by
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}:\phi(t)\geq p}
resp.
\sup_{t\in(-\infty,T):\phi(t)\geq p}
. However, in general we cannot consider all
t\in\mathbb{R}
resp.
t\in(-\infty,T)
since in the regularization procedure segments
D_{t}
with
t
such that
\phi(t)<p
are not considered; see
(c.4)
in Section
5.4
and the arguments in
(a)
resp.
(c)
in the proof below.

(v)

When
J^{\phi}\neq\mathbb{R}
, then let us set
(J^{\phi})^{c}:=\mathbb{R}\backslash J^{\phi}
and, analogously as the conventions used in Sections
4.1
and
4.2
, we put
A^{\phi}(t):=+\infty
for all
t\in(J^{\phi})^{c}
. This allows then to replace
\sup_{t\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})}
resp.
\sup_{t\in(-\infty,T):\phi(t)\geq p}
by
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
resp.
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}:\phi(t)\geq p}
; for the second situation recall that we put
\phi(t)=+\infty
for all
t\geq T
. Finally, in case
(i)
we can also put
A^{\phi}(k):=+\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{R}
and so consider
\sup_{\{-\infty\}\cup\mathbb{R}}
instead; see again Section
4.1
.

Proof.

We distinguish between the different cases.

(a)

Assume that
\phi\neq+\infty
. Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the auxiliary line
\ell_{p}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x=p\}
. For all
t\in J^{\phi}=\mathbb{R}
with
\phi(t)\geq p
consider the segment
D_{t}
. For
p=0
the line
\ell_{p}
is the
y
-axis and we have
\phi(t)\geq 0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence
D_{t}
intersects
\ell_{p}
in
(p,pt-A^{\phi}(t))
but, by definition, the point
S_{p}^{\phi}=(p,a_{p}^{\phi})
is not lying below
D_{t}
which implies
pt-A^{\phi}(t)\leq a_{p}^{\phi}
. Note: For
p=0
we get
-A^{\phi}(t)\leq a_{0}=a_{p}^{\phi}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and equality is attained in the limit
t=-\infty
.

For the converse, first by construction resp. definition of
\mathbf{a}^{\phi}
given in Section
5.5
we see that for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S_{p}^{\phi}\in L_{i}
and that the desired equality holds for any principal index since
a_{p_{i}}=a_{p_{i}}^{\phi}
. Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
p_{i+1}
be now the smallest principal index satisfying
p_{i+1}>p>p_{i}
. Indeed, recall that by
(ii)
in Section
5.3
there exist infinitely many principal indices.

We distinguish now between two cases, see
(c)
in Section
5.4
: If the index
p_{i+1}
is not an index of discontinuity, then we have
L_{i}\subseteq D_{t_{i+1}}
and because
\phi(t_{i+1})=\phi(\tau_{i})\geq p_{i+1}>p
holds we are done since the supremum in (
5.12
) is attained at
t=t_{i+1}
. If
p_{i+1}
is an index of discontinuity, and so necessarily
\phi(\tau_{i})=\phi(t_{i+1})=p_{i+1}
, then
L_{i}\subseteq D_{t_{i+1}}^{-}
where
D_{t_{i+1}}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{t_{i+1}-\varepsilon}
. Thus (
5.12
) holds as well but the supremum is not attained for
t\in\mathbb{R}=J^{\phi}
.

(b)

Assume that
\phi=+\infty
and distinguish:

(b.1)

If
\mathbf{a}
satisfies (
3.1
), then the same arguments from
(a)
apply and note that the set of indices of discontinuity is empty.

(b.2)

Let
\mathbf{a}
have (
4.2
). Again the set of indices of discontinuity is empty. If there exist infinitely many principal indices, then the result follows by repeating the arguments from
(a)
. If there exist only finitely many principal indices, say
i_{0}
many, then for all
p<p_{i_{0}}
we can proceed as in case
(a)
. For
p\geq p_{i_{0}}
note that
S^{\phi}_{p}\in L_{i_{0}}
, so these points lie on the straight line passing through
S_{p_{i_{0}}}
and having slope
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
. Recall that
L_{i_{0}}
coincides with
D_{\mathbf{a}_{\iota}}^{-}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}D_{\mathbf{a}_{\iota}-\varepsilon}
on
[p_{i_{0}},+\infty)
. Thus (
5.12
) holds also for all such
p
when
t\in(-\infty,\mathbf{a}_{\iota})
but the supremum is not attained for any
t
belonging to this set.

(c)

Assume that
\phi(T)=+\infty
for some
T\in\mathbb{R}
. We distinguish:

(c.1)

If there exist infinitely many principal indices, then we can proceed as in case
(a)
above.

(c.2)

If there exist only finitely many principal indices, then the result follows analogously as in case
(b.2)
before with
\mathbf{a}_{\iota}
being replaced by
T
.

Recall that in case
(c)
we have (
5.12
) even if
a_{p}=+\infty
for all
p\geq 1
large.

∎

Shift limits of a non-autonomous system
Dawoud Ahmadi Dastjerdi, Mahdi Aghaee

Abstract
Let t = t 1 t 2 ⋯ t=t_{1}t_{2}\cdots be an element of the full shift with shift map τ \tau on a finite set of characters 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and let Σ = closure ​ { τ i ​ ( t ) : i ∈ ℕ ∪ { 0 } } \Sigma=\text{ closure}\{\tau^{i}(t):\;i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\} . Let f t = f t 1 , ∞ = ⋯ ∘ f t 2 ∘ f t 1 f_{t}=f_{t_{1},\,\infty}=\cdots\circ f_{t_{2}}\circ f_{t_{1}} be a non-autonomous system over a compact metric space X X where t i ∈ 𝒜 t_{i}\in\mathcal{A} . The set 𝐅 t + = { f τ i ​ ( t ) : i ∈ ℕ } {\bf{F}}_{t}^{+}=\{f_{\tau^{i}(t)}:\;i\in\mathbb{N}\} is called the shifted family of f t f_{t} . If t t is a transitive point of the full shift on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} , then by introducing a natural topology, 𝐅 t + ¯ \overline{{\bf{F}}_{t}^{+}} is a classical IFS; otherwise, 𝐅 t + ¯ = { f σ = f σ 1 , ∞ : σ ∈ Σ } \overline{{\bf{F}}_{t}^{+}}=\{f_{\sigma}=f_{\sigma_{1},\,\infty}:\;\sigma\in\Sigma\} is a generalized IFS. 2.1
“Generalized” iterated function systems.

The classical

(IFS) consists of all possible combinations of finitely many continuous self maps
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{0},\,\ldots,\,f_{k-1}\right\}

X

In other words, a classical IFS consists of

\mathcal{A}=\{0,\,1,\cdots,k-1\}

\mathcal{L}(\Sigma_{k})
is the language of
\Sigma_{k}

\Sigma_{k}
with a subshift
\Sigma\subseteq\Sigma_{k}
in (
4
), then it will be a “generalized” iterated function system which we refer to it again as IFS and it is denoted by

\mathfrak{I}=(X,\,\mathcal{F},\,\Sigma),

(5)

where
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{0},\,\ldots,\,f_{k-1}\right\}
.

When
\mathfrak{I}
is the classical IFS; or equivalently, when
\Sigma
is a full shift, then still we use
(
5
)
but the subshift will be shown by
\Sigma_{|\mathcal{F}|}
. The classical IFS is usually denoted by

by other authors.

The

of a point

\sigma
.
The trajectory (or orbit) of

In general,

Notice that when

2.2
Symbolic dynamics

Since we are dealing with a general subshift in an IFS such as (

[

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{0,\,1,\cdots,k-1\}

Let

The map

\tau(\sigma)_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}

on
k

Any invariant closed subspace

is called a

.

A

or a

over

is a finite sequence of symbols from
\mathcal{A}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{n}(\Sigma)

the set of all
admissible
words of length
n
and call

\mathcal{L}(\Sigma):=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{n}(\Sigma)

the
language
of
\Sigma
.
A subshift is characterized by its language or its forbidden words.
For
u\in\mathcal{L}_{k}(\Sigma)
,
let the cylinder
{}_{\ell}[u]_{\ell+k-1}
=_{\ell}\hskip-2.84526pt[u_{\ell}\cdots u_{\ell+k-1}]_{\ell+k-1}
be the set
\{\sigma=\cdots\sigma_{-1}\sigma_{0}\sigma_{1}\cdots\in\Sigma:\sigma_{\ell}\cdots\sigma_{\ell+k-1}=u\}
.
If
\ell=0
, we drop the subscripts and we just write

.
A point
\sigma\in\Sigma
is

\Sigma
appears in
\sigma
infinitely many often and a
subshift
\Sigma
is

iff
\Sigma
has a transitive point. Equivalently,

\Sigma

.

If forbidden set of
\Sigma

is

. A word

is an irreducible shift which has a synchronizing word. Any sofic is synchronized.

A synchronized system
\Sigma
may be represented with a directed labeled graph called

. Points of
\Sigma
is the recording of labels on infinite walks and the closure of all such points. Covers are not unique and there is a “minimal” representation which is called

Any Fischer cover is right resolving: the edges starting at any vertex carry different labels. For sofics, the Fischer cover is a right resolving labeled graph with the fewest vertices among all covers. Also, a subshift is sofic iff it has a finite cover.

The notion of a periodic point in the case of a conventional dynamical system

orbitally periodic

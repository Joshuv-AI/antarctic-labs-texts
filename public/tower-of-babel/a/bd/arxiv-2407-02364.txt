On the stochastic selection of integral curves of a rough vector field
Jules Pitcho

Abstract
. We prove that for bounded, divergence-free vector fields 𝒃 b in L l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( ( 0 , 1 ] , B ​ V ​ ( 𝕋 d , ℝ d ) ) L^{1}_{loc}((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})) , there exists a unique incompressible measure on integral curves of 𝒃 b . We recall the vector field constructed by Depauw in [ 8 ] , which lies in the above class, and prove that for this vector field, the unique incompressible measure on integral curves exhibits stochasticity.



1.
Introduction

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A general principle due to Ambrosio (see Theorem
1.4
) guarantees existence of a measure concentrated on integral curves of
b
, whose 1-marginals at every time is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Question 1
.

Is there a robust selection criterion amongst all such measures?

When
b
lies in
L^{1}((0,1);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, Ambrosio
[
4
]
following on DiPerna and Lions
[
9
]
proved that there exists a unique such measure, thereby answering the above question affirmatively. In this work we give an affirmative answer to the above question for a class of bounded, divergence-free vector fields to which the work of Ambrosio does not apply.
Let us introduce the main objects of study.

All vector fields
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be Borel, essentially bounded and divergence-free, meaning that

\operatorname{div}_{x}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}=0\qquad\text{in the sense of distributions on \;}[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Let us define integral curves of
b
.

Definition 1.1
.

We shall say that a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
is an integral curve of
b
, if it is an absolutely continuous solution to the ODE

\dot{\gamma}(t)={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,\gamma(t)),

which means explicitely that for every
s,t\in[0,1]

\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)=\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{0}^{1}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))|d\tau<+\infty.

We shall further say that
\gamma
is an integral curve starting from
x
at time
s
, if
\gamma(s)=x
and we shall write
\gamma_{s,x}.

Let
\Gamma
denote the metric space
C([0,1];\mathbb{T}^{d})
of continuous paths endowed with the uniform metric, and let
\mathscr{M}
denote the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra. We recall that a selection of integral curves
\{\gamma_{s,x}:x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\}
is measurable, if the map
\mathbb{T}^{d}\ni x\longmapsto\gamma_{s,x}\in\Gamma
is Borel.
Ambrosio proposed the following definition in
[
4
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

A measurable selection
\{\gamma_{s,x}:x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\}
of integral curves of
b
is said to be a
regular measurable selection
, if
there exists
C>0
such that

(1.1)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi(\gamma_{s,x}(t))dx\Big|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\phi(y)|dy\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\;\forall t\in[0,1].

When
b
belongs to
L^{1}((0,1);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and satisfies
\int_{0}^{1}\|[\operatorname{div}_{x}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(s,\cdot)]_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}ds<+\infty
, Ambrosio proved the existence of a regular measurable selection and the following essential uniqueness result: any two regular measurable selection
\{\gamma^{1}_{s,x}\}
and
\{\gamma^{2}_{s,x}\}
1
1

1

From now on, when we omit to specify the indexing set for a family of paths or a family of measures, it will implicitely be understood that the family is indexed by
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
of integral curves of
b

must coincide up to a set of vanishing Lebesgue measure. Moreover, if
b
is divergence-free, essential uniqueness holds amongst measure-preserving measurable selection, that is those which satisfy

(1.2)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi(\gamma_{s,x}(t))dx\Big|=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\phi(y)|dy\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\;\forall t\in[0,1].

Recently, Pappalettera constructed in
[
11
]
a divergence-free vector field
b
for which there does not exist a measure-preserving selection of characteristics. This vector field does not belong to
L^{1}((0,1);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. This, however does not exclude that for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, a probability measure concentrated on integral curves of
b
starting from
x
at time
0
can be uniquely selected by some appropriate criterion. The present work investigates such a selection criterion for divergence-free vector fields in
L_{loc}^{1}((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. For this class of vector fields, a selection criterion for solutions of the continuity equation using regularisation by convolution was already proved by the author in
[
12
]
. We here give a Lagrangian counterpart to this previous result. We begin by defining the measures on integral curves we shall study.

1.1.
Lagrangian representations

All measures are Radon measures in this work. We will denote by

e_{t}:\Gamma\ni\gamma\longmapsto\gamma(t)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
the evaluation map. We will also denote by
\rho:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
a bounded density. It will always be assumed in
C([0,1];w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, which we may do without loss of generality, when the vector field
\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
solves

(PDE)

\operatorname{div}_{t,x}\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})=0\qquad\text{in the sense of distributions on $[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}$}.

A proof of this fact is given in
[
12
]
. Let us now define the main object under consideration in this paper.

Definition 1.3
.

We shall say that a bounded, positive measure
\eta
on
\Gamma
is a Lagrangian representation of the vector field
\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
, if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\eta
is concentrated on the set
\Gamma
of integral curves of
b
,
which explicitely means that for every
s,t\in[0,1]

\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)=0;

(ii)

for every
t\in[0,1]
, we have

(1.3)

\rho(t,\cdot){\mathscr{L}}^{d}=(e_{t})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}.

We now record the following general existence theorem for Lagrangian representations.

Theorem 1.4

(Ambrosio’s superposition principle)
.

In the context of this paragraph, and assuming that
\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
solves (
PDE
), there exists a Lagrangian representation of
\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
.

The above theorem is proved (see for instance
[
3
]
) by a regularisation and compactness argument, where the hypothesis that
\rho
is non-negative plays an essential role.
Let us now introduce the set of Lipschitz paths with constant
L>0

(1.4)

\Gamma_{L}:=\Big\{\gamma\in\Gamma\;:\;|\gamma(s)-\gamma(t)|\leq L|s-t|\quad\forall s,t\in[0,1]\Big\}.

Remark 1.5
.

\Gamma_{L}
is a compact, separable metric space with the metric induced from
\Gamma
.

We now have the following lemma.

Lemma 1.6
.

Any Lagrangian representation
\eta
of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
is concentrated on
\Gamma_{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be a Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
.
Let
D
be a countable dense subset of
[0,1]
. Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)\times(0,1)^{d})
be a standard mollifier. Define

\phi^{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon^{d+1}}}\phi(\frac{t}{\varepsilon},\frac{x}{\varepsilon}),

and denote
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\ast\phi^{\varepsilon}
. Let
s,t\in D
.
Notice that for every
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(1.5)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&\leq\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma}\int_{s}^{t}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&\leq\int_{\Gamma}\int_{s}^{t}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))|d\tau{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma}\int_{s}^{t}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))|d\tau{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,x)|dxd\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|dxd\tau\\
&\leq 2|t-s|\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}},\end{split}

where in the second to last line, we have used Fubini, as well as
(e_{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
, and
in the last line we have used that
\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}
and Hölder’s inequality.
Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem, it holds that

(1.6)

\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)=0,

which implies that there exists a set
N_{s,t}\subset\Gamma
of vanishing
\eta
measure such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma-N_{s,t}
, we have

(1.7)

\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau.

Since
\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}
, it therefore holds that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma-N_{s,t}

(1.8)

|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)|\leq|t-s|\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}.

Define

N:=\bigcup_{s,t\in D}N_{s,t},

which is a set of vanishing
\eta
measure since
D
is countable. As
s,t
were arbitrary in
D
, and by density of
D
in
[0,1]
, we therefore have that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma-N
, it holds

|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)|\leq|t-s|\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\qquad\forall s,t\in[0,1].

This proves the thesis.
∎

Next we present our main tool: the disintegration of a measure with respect to a Borel map and a target measure. In the study of weak solutions of the continuity equation, disintegration has previously been used in
[
1
]
by Alberti, Bianchini and Crippa to establish the optimal uniqueness result for the continuity equation along a bounded, divergence-free, and autonomous in the two-dimensional setting. In
[
5
]
, Bianchini and Bonicatto also used disintegration to prove a uniqueness result for nearly incompressible vector fields in
L^{1}_{t}BV_{x}
. In view of Lemma
1.6
, we will identify a Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
with its restriction to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of the compact set
\Gamma_{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}}
, and thanks to Remark
1.5
, we will be able to perform a disintegration of this measure.

1.2.
Disintegration of measures

Let
X
and
Y
be compact, separable metric spaces,
\mu
a measure on
X
,
f:X\to Y
a Borel map,
\nu
a measure on
Y
such that
f_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}\ll{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}
. Then there exists a Borel family
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}:y\in Y\}
of measures on
X
such that

(i)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}
is supported on the level set
E_{y}:=f^{-1}(y)
for every
y\in Y
;

(ii)

the measure
\mu
can be decomposed as
{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}=\int_{Y}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(y)
, which means that

(1.9)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(A)=\int_{Y}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}(A)d{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(y)

for every Borel set
A
contained in
X
.

If we further assume that
\mu
and
\nu
are positive measures, and that
f_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}
, then there exists a Borel family
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}:y\in Y\}
of
probability
measures on
X
satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
.

Any family satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
is called a
disintegration
of
\mu
with respect to
f
and
\nu
. The disintegration is essentially unique in the following sense: for any other disintegration
\{\tilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}}_{y}:y\in Y\}
there holds
{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}=\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}
for
\nu
-a.e.
y\in Y
.
The above facts are cited from
[
1
]
, and are proven in Dellacherie and Meyer
[
7
]
.

We now give a useful fact.
Let
g:X\to X
a Borel map such that:

(P)

for every
y\in Y
, we have
g^{-1}((f^{-1}(y))^{c})=(f^{-1}(y))^{c}
.

The following is true.

Lemma 1.7
.

In the context of this paragraph, if
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}:y\in Y\}
is a disintegration of
\mu
with respect to
f
and
\nu
, then
\{g_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}:y\in Y\}
is a disintegration of
g_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}
with respect to
f
and
\nu
.

Proof.

Let
y\in Y
. Observe that
g_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}((f^{-1}(y))^{c})=\mu_{y}(g^{-1}(f^{-1}(y))^{c})=\mu_{y}((f^{-1}(y))^{c})=0
where we have used (P) in the second to last equality and that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}
is concentrated on
f^{-1}(y)
in the last equality.
So
g_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}
is supported on
f^{-1}(y)
, and since
y
was arbitrary, this proves
(i)
.

Let
A
a Borel set in
X
. Then, as
g^{-1}(A)
is a Borel set in
X
, it follows that

(1.10)

g_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(A)={\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(g^{-1}(A))=\int_{Y}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}(g^{-1}(A))d{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(y)=\int_{Y}g_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}(A)d{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(y),

which gives
(ii)
.
∎

We also have the following property of the disintegration, which we will use in this paper:

(1.11)

\int_{X}\phi d{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}=\int_{Y}\Big[\int_{E_{y}}\phi d{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}_{y}\Big]d{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(y),

for every Borel function
\phi:X\to[0,+\infty]
.

1.3.
Uniqueness of regular measurable selection

Ambrosio
[
4
]
proved the existence and essential uniqueness of regular measurable selections in the bounded variation setting, thereby extending the work of DiPerna and Lions
[
9
]
. We recall that
\rho:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is assumed to be in
C([0,1];w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. The following can be extracted from Ambrosio
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Assume that
b
belongs to
L^{1}((0,1);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and that
\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
solves (
PDE
). Then, there exists a unique Lagrangian representation
\eta
of
\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
, which further has the following property. For every
s\in[0,1]
, there exists a regular measurable selection
\{\gamma_{s,x}\}
of integral curves of
b
such that

(1.12)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{s,x}}\rho(s,x)dx.

The above theorem implies that, if two bounded vector fields
\rho(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
and
\tilde{\rho}(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
solve (
PDE
) and satisfy
\rho(s,x)=\tilde{\rho}(s,x)
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, then
\rho=\tilde{\rho}
, which is the uniqueness result of Ambrosio for the Cauchy problem for the continuity equation with a vector field in
L^{1}_{t}BV_{x}
.
The essential uniqueness of regular measurable selections can then be deduced. We record it in the following remark.

Remark 1.9
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.8
, if
\{\gamma^{1}_{s,x}\}
and
\{\gamma^{2}_{s,x}\}
are two regular measurable selection of integral curves of
b
, then for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have
\gamma^{1}_{s,x}=\gamma^{2}_{s,x}
. Indeed, let
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
\bar{\rho}\geq 0
, and define the measures

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{i}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma^{i}_{s,x}}\bar{\rho}(x)dx\qquad\text{for \;}i=1,2.

Then, consider the densities
\rho^{1},\rho^{2}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which lie in
C([0,1];w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
and are given by

\rho^{i}(t,\cdot){\mathscr{L}}^{d}=(e_{t})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{i}\qquad\text{for \;}i=1,2.

The vector fields
\rho^{i}(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
both solve (
PDE
). Therefore, by Theorem
1.8
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma^{1}_{s,x}}\bar{\rho}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{s,x}}\bar{\rho}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma^{2}_{s,x}}\bar{\rho}(x)dx.

As
\bar{\rho}
was an arbitrary bounded, nonnegative function, and as the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{M}
of
\Gamma
is countably generated, we have for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}

{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{s,x}^{1}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{s,x}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma^{2}_{s,x}},

which implies that for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}

\gamma_{s,x}^{1}=\gamma_{s,x}=\gamma^{2}_{s,x}.

Given
\tau>0,
we define the truncated versions of
b

(1.13)

{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\tau}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{split}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,x)\qquad&\text{if}\quad t\geq\tau,\\
0\qquad&\text{if}\quad t<\tau.\end{split}\right.

Under the assumption that the bounded variation norm of
b
is not integrable at time zero, the following essential uniqueness of regular measurable selections of integral curves still holds.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
. Assume that
b
belongs to
L_{loc}^{1}((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})),
and consider two regular measurable selections
\{\gamma^{1}_{s,x}\}
and
\{\gamma^{2}_{s,x}\}
of integral curves of
b
starting from
s
. Then
\gamma^{1}_{s,x}=\gamma^{2}_{s,x}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It can be verified directly that the two measurable selections
\{\gamma^{1}_{s,x}(1/k\vee\cdot)\}
and
\{\gamma^{2}_{s,x}(1/k\vee\cdot)\}
are regular measurable selections of integral curves of the vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{1/k}
defined in (
1.13
). Note that this vector field belongs to
L^{1}((0,1);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, so in view of Remark
1.9
, we have
\gamma_{s,x}^{1}(1/k\vee\cdot)=\gamma^{2}_{s,x}(1/k\vee\cdot)
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N_{k}
, where
N_{k}
is a set of vanishing Lebesgue measure.
Define

N:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}N_{k},

which is of vanishing Lebesgue measure.
Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\gamma_{s,x}^{1}(1/k\vee\cdot)=\gamma^{2}_{s,x}(1/k\vee\cdot)
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N,
which implies
\gamma_{s,x}^{1}=\gamma^{2}_{s,x}
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N
by continuity. The thesis follows.
∎

1.4.
Statement of results

In this paper, the vector field
b
will satisfy the hypothesis of Proposition
1.10
. Accordingly, we fix for the rest of the paper

\{\gamma_{1,y}\}
a (essentially unique) regular measurable selection of integral curves of
b
starting from time
1
.
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
denotes the bounded, divergence-free vector field in
L_{loc}^{1}((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}))
constructed by Depauw in
[
8
]
. For completeness we give a construction of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
in the Appendix.
We now state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.11
.

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L_{loc}^{1}((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Then, there exists a unique Lagrangian representation
\eta
of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
, which furthermore has the following properties:

(i)

the family of probability measures
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
;

(ii)

for every smooth sequence
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}\rightarrow{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}
in
L^{1}_{loc}
,
\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}
, and
\operatorname{div}_{x}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}=0
, the unique Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})
converges narrowly to
\eta
as
k\to+\infty
;

(iii)

there exists a Borel family of probability measures
\{\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}\}
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
such that any disintegration
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}\}
of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
satisfies

(1.14)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}(dy),

for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Moreover, for the vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
, the measure
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}
is not a Dirac mass for
{\mathscr{L}}^{2}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

Observe that a sequence satisfying the hypothesis of
(ii)
can be generated by regularising
b
by convolution.
We further ask the following question on the measures
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}
of the above theorem, which capture the stochastic behaviour of the Lagrangian representation
\eta
.

Question 2
.

Can one construct a bounded, divergence-free vector field in
L^{1}_{loc}((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
such that the measures
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}
are absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
?

We finally note that the class of vector field under study in this article has been previously investigated in

.

, the vector field constructed by Depauw in
[
8
]
.

2.
Uniqueness of the Lagrangian representation

In this section
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an essentially bounded, divergence-free Borel vector field satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.11
, namely
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,1];BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

2.1.
Backwards stopping of the Lagrangian representation

Let
\eta
be a Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
, which exists by Theorem
1.4
, and let
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}\}
be a disintegration with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. Let
\{\gamma_{1,y}\}
be a (essentially unique) regular measurable selection of integral curves of
b
. Our goal is now to prove that for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

(2.1)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}={\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}},

which will imply by definition of the disintegration that

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}dy,

from which uniqueness of the Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}),
as well as part
(i)
of Theorem
1.11
will follow. Given two positive real numbers
a
and
b
, we will write
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}.

Let
\tau>0
and
consider the backward stopping map
S^{\tau}:\Gamma\ni\gamma(\cdot)\longmapsto\gamma(\tau\vee\cdot)\in\Gamma
. Note that
S^{\tau}
clearly satisfies (P) of Section
1.2
with
X=\Gamma_{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}}
,
Y=\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
f=e_{1}
, and
g=S^{\tau}
. Therefore, by Lemma
1.7
,
\{(S^{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}\}
is a disintegration with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.
For simplicity, we will write
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}:=(S^{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}^{\tau}:=(S^{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}
.
Recall that we have defined in (
1.13
) the truncated version
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\tau}
of
b
.
We then have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
\tau>0
, the family
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)}\}
is a disintegration of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.

Proof.

As
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\tau}
belongs to
L^{1}((0,1);BV(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is bounded, and divergence-free, there is an essentially unique regular measurable selection
\{\gamma^{\tau}_{1,y}\}
of integral curves of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\tau}
thanks to Remark
1.9
.
Now, observe that
\{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)\}
is a regular measurable selection of integral curves of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\tau}
, hence for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have that
\gamma^{\tau}_{1,y}(\cdot)=\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)
.
It can be checked directly that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}
is a Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\tau})
. So by Theorem
1.8
,
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma^{\tau}_{1,y}}\}
is a disintegration of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
so that

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma^{\tau}_{1,y}}dy.

Therefore, by essential uniqueness of the disintegration,
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)}\}
is a disintegration of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
so that

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)}dy.

∎

We also have the following simple fact.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\Gamma
.
Then
(S^{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}
converges narrowly to
\mu
as
\tau\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi\in C_{b}(\Gamma)
. For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have
\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}S^{\tau}\gamma=\gamma
. Then we have
\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\Phi(S^{\tau}\gamma)=\Phi(\gamma)
by continuity of
\Phi
. Also, we clearly have

\int_{\Gamma}|\Phi(S^{\tau}\gamma)|{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(d\gamma)\leq\|\Phi\|_{C^{0}}\qquad\forall\tau>0.

So, by dominated convergence, it holds that

\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma)(S^{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(d\gamma)=\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(S^{\tau}\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}}(d\gamma).

Since
\Phi
was arbitrary in
C_{b}(\Gamma)
, the thesis follows.
∎

2.2.
Proof of
(i)
of Theorem
1.11

Proof.

Recall that
\eta
is a Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
and that
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. Recall also that
\{\gamma_{1,y}\}
is a (essentially unique) regular measurable selection of integral curves of
b
. Let us prove (
2.1
), which will yield both uniqueness of the Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
as well as
(i)
of Theorem
1.11
, namely it will show

(2.2)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}dy.

By separability of
C_{c}(\Gamma)
, there exists a countable subset
\mathcal{N}
of
C_{c}(\Gamma)
, which is dense.
Let
\tau>0,
and let
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}\}
be a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. By Lemma
1.7
,
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}^{\tau}\}
is a disintegration of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. By Lemma
2.1
, and by essential uniqueness of the disintegration, we have
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)}={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}^{\tau}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{N}
, and let
B
be a Borel set in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. We then have that

(2.3)

\begin{split}\int_{B}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}(d\gamma)dy&=\int_{B}\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)}(d\gamma)dy,\\
&=\int_{B}\lim_{\tau\downarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\tau}_{1,y}(d\gamma)dy,\\
&=\int_{B}\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}(d\gamma)dy,\end{split}

where in the first equality, we have used that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)}
converges narrowly to
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}
as
\tau\downarrow 0
by Lemma
2.2
. In the second to last equality, we have used that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}(\tau\vee\cdot)}={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}^{\tau}
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, which follows from Lemma
2.1
. In the last equality, we have used Lemma
2.2
.
As
B
was an arbitrary Borel set of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have that there exists a set
N_{\Phi}
of vanishing Lebesgue measure such that for every
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N_{\Phi}
, we have

\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}(d\gamma).

Now, let

N:=\bigcup_{\Phi\in\mathcal{N}}N_{\Phi}.

It is a set of vanishing Lebesgue measure as
\mathcal{N}
is countable, and for every
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N
, we have

\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}(d\gamma)\qquad\forall\Phi\in\mathcal{N}.

By density of
\mathcal{N}
in
C_{c}(\Gamma)
,
for every
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}-N
, we have

\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}(d\gamma)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(\gamma){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}(d\gamma)\qquad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}(\Gamma).

This proves that for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{1,y}={\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}
, which proves (
2.2
). As
\eta
was an arbitrary Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
, this proves both that there exists a unique Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
, and
(i)
of Theorem
1.11
.
∎

We will now prove that the unique Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
can be obtained as the unique limit of Lagrangian representations of suitable regularisations of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
.

2.3.
Proof of
(ii)
of Theorem
1.11

Proof.

Let
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a smooth sequence such that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}\to{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}
in
L^{1}_{loc}
and such that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(2.4)

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}(t,x)|\leq\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}},

and such that
\operatorname{div}_{x}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}=0
.
Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
be the unique flow along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
, namely
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}
solves

(2.5)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,x)&={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}({\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,x)),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(0,x)&=x.\end{split}\right.

The measure defined by

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}(A)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(\cdot,x)}(A)dx,

for every Borel set
A
in
\Gamma
is then the unique Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})
, as we clearly have
(e_{t})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}={\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,\cdot)_{\#}{\mathscr{L}}^{d}={\mathscr{L}}^{d},
since
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
is divergence-free, and also that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}
is clearly concentrated on integral curves of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
.

Step 1.
Compactness. Recall the definition of the space
\Gamma_{L}
of Lipchitz paths with Lipschitz constant
L>0
given in (
1.4
).
In view of Lemma
1.6
, we have that

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}(\Gamma_{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}})=1
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
As
\Gamma_{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}}
is compact by Remark
1.5
, it follows by Prokhorov theorem, that there exists an increasing map
\xi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\xi(k)}
converges narrowly to some probability measure
\eta
on
\Gamma_{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}}
as
k\to+\infty.

Step 2.
Let us prove that
\eta
is a Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
. Let
\phi\in C(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
t\in[0,1]
. We have that
\Gamma\ni\gamma\longmapsto\phi(e_{t}(\gamma))
is in
C_{b}(\Gamma)
. Therefore,

\int_{\Gamma}\phi(e_{t}(\gamma)){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\phi(e_{t}(\gamma)){\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\xi(k)}(d\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\phi(x)dx.

As
\phi
and
t
were arbitrary, this implies that
(e_{t})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
for every
t\in[0,1]
.
We still need to prove that
\eta
is concentrated on integral curves of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}.

Let
s,t\in[0,1]
. We have to check that

(2.6)

\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)=0.

We know that

\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\xi(k)}(d\gamma)=0,

however we cannot pass into the limit
k\to+\infty
in the above equation because the functional

(2.7)

\Gamma\ni\gamma\longmapsto\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|,

need not be continuous since
b
is not continuous. To circumvent this problem, let
\varepsilon>0
and let
{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}:[0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a continuous vector field such that
\int_{s}^{t}|{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,x)-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,x)|d\tau dx<\varepsilon
. We then have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&\overset{1}{\leq}\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&\overset{2}{\leq}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&\overset{3}{=}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))-{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau)))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}({\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau)))d\tau\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&\overset{4}{\leq}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))-{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))\Big|d\tau{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}(d\gamma)+\int_{\Gamma}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))\Big|d\tau{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(d\gamma)\\
&\overset{5}{=}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}(\tau,x)-{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,x)\Big|d\tau dx+\int_{\Gamma}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,x)-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,x)\Big|d\tau dx\\
&\overset{6}{\leq}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}(\tau,x)-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,x)\Big|d\tau dx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,x)-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,x)\Big|d\tau dx\\
&\overset{7}{=}2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}\Big|{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,x)-{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(\tau,x)\Big|d\tau dx<2\varepsilon.\end{split}

1 follows by a triangular inequality, 2 follows because the functional

\Gamma\ni\gamma\longmapsto\Big|\gamma(t)-\gamma(s)-\int_{s}^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}}(\tau,\gamma(\tau))d\tau\Big|

is continuous, 3 follows because
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}
is concentrated on integral curves of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
, 4 follows by bringing the absolute value inside the integral, 5 follows because
(e_{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}=(e_{\tau})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}},
6 follows by a triangular inequality, and 7 follows since
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}\to{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}
in
L^{1}_{loc}
. As
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, (
2.6
) follows. Therefore
\eta
is a Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
. By the first part of Theorem
1.11
we have already proved, we know that there exists a unique Lagrangian representation
\eta
of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
. Therefore, the whole sequence
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{k}
converges narrowly to
\eta
as
k\to+\infty
. This proves the thesis.

∎

3.
Stochasticity

We will now prove part
(iii)
of Theorem
1.11
. Throughout this section,
\eta
is the unique Lagrangian representation of
(1,{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}})
from the first part of Theorem
1.11
.
Recall that we have fixed a regular measurable selection
\{\gamma_{1,y}\}
of integral curves of
b
starting from
1
and that

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}dy.

We also define the measure
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}=(e_{0},e_{1})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}

For every

we define the family of measures on
\Gamma

(3.1)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}:=\left\{\begin{split}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}\qquad&\text{if}\quad\gamma_{1,y}(0)=x,\\
0\qquad&\text{if}\quad\gamma_{1,y}(0)\neq x.\end{split}\right.

Define the projection maps

\pi_{0}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\ni(x,y)\longmapsto x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

Let
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}\}
be a disintegration of
\nu
with respect to
\pi_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
.

3.1.
Disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
\nu

We will now give an expression for disintegrations of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
in terms of
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}\}
. Throughout this section
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. We then define
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}:=(\pi_{1})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. We also define the probability measure

(3.2)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{y}:={\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{(\gamma_{1,y}(0),y)},

on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
for every
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The family
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{y}\}
is a disintegration of
\nu
with respect to
\pi_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}.

Proof.

It is clear that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{y}
is supported on
\pi_{1}^{-1}(y)
.
By part
(i)
of Theorem
1.11
, we know also that
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. Therefore, for every Borel set
A
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(A)=(e_{0},e_{1})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(A)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(e_{0},e_{1})_{\#}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}(A)dy=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{(\gamma_{1,y}(0),y)}(A)dy=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{y}(A)dy,

which proves the thesis.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The family
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}:x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
(e_{0},e_{1})
and
\nu
.

Proof.

It is clear that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}
is supported on
(e_{0},e_{1})^{-1}(x,y)
.
For every Borel set
A
contained in
\Gamma
, we have

(3.3)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}(A){\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(dx,dy)&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\{y\}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}(A)d{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{y}dy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}(A)dy\\
&={\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(A),\end{split}

where in the first equality we have used Lemma
3.1
, as well as (
1.11
). In the second equality we have used the definition (
3.2
) of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{y}
and the definition (
3.1
) of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}
, and in the last equality we have used that
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}\}
is a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
e_{1}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
, which follows from
(i)
of Theorem
1.11
, which we have already proved.
This proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

(3.4)

{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}d\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}.

Proof.

Let
B
be a Borel set contained in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. As
\Gamma
is separable, its Borel
\sigma
-algebra is generated by a countable family
\mathscr{G}
. Let
A\in\mathscr{G}
. We then have

(3.5)

\begin{split}\int_{B}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}(A)dx&\overset{1}{=}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}(A\cap\{\gamma(0)\in B\})dx\\
&\overset{2}{=}{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}(A\cap\{\gamma(0)\in B\})\\
&\overset{3}{=}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}(A\cap\{\gamma(0)\in B\}){\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}(dx,dy)\\
&\overset{4}{=}\int_{B}\Big[\int_{\{x\}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{x,\gamma_{1,y}}(A)d{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}\Big]dx\\
&\overset{5}{=}\int_{B}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\delta$}}_{\gamma_{1,y}}(A)\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}(dy)\Big]dx.\end{split}

In equality
1
, we have used that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}
is supported on
\{\gamma(0)=x\}
for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. In equality
2
, we have used that
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{0,x}\}
is a disintegration of of
\eta
with respect to
e_{0}
and
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. In equality
3
, we have used Lemma
3.2
. In equality
4
, we have used that
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\nu$}}_{x}\}
is a disintegration of

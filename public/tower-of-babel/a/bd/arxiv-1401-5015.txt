Model selection of stochastic simulation algorithm based on generalized divergence measures
Papa NGOM and B. Don Bosco DIATTA

Abstract
MCMC methods (Monte Carlo Markov Chain) are a class of methods used to perform simulations per a probability distribution P P . These methods are often used when we have difficulties to directly sample per a given probability distribution P P . This distribution is then considered as a target and generates a Markov chain ( X n ) n ∈ ℕ (X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}} that, when n n is large we have X n ∼ P X_{n}\sim P . These MCMC methods consist of several simulation strategies including the Independent Sampler (IS) , the Random Walk of Metropolis Hastings (RWMH) , the Gibbs sampler , the Adaptive Metropolis (AM) and Metropolis Within Gibbs (MWG) strategy. Each of these strategies can generate a Markov chain and is associated with a convergence speed. It is interesting, with a given target law, to compare several simulation strategies for determining the best. Chauveau and Vandekerkhove [ 4 ] have compared IS and RWMH strategies using the Kullback-Leibler divergence measure. In our article we will compare our five simulation methods already mentioned using generalized divergence measures. These divergence measures are taken in family of α \alpha -divergence measures [ 6 ] , with a parameter α \alpha . This is the Rényi divergence, Tsallis divergence and D α D_{\alpha} divergence .

Description of divergence measures

Let

p_{i}(x)=\frac{\mu_{i}(dx)}{\lambda(dx)},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2.

Definition 2.1

Kullback-Leibler’s relative divergence (also called relative entropy) between two probability measures
\mu_{1}

K(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\int_{\cal X}p_{1}(x)\log\big(\frac{p_{1}(x)}{p_{2}(x)}\big)\lambda(dx)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{1}}\left[\log\frac{p_{1}(x)}{p_{2}(x)}\right]

(2.1)

We can also write

.

We will focus on in this article to a particular family of

\phi

\alpha
-divergence

\alpha
-divergence

1
]

\alpha

\alpha
-divergence of the discrepancy which we will work.

Definition 2.2
(
\alpha
-divergence)

The
\alpha
-divergence is defined by

D_{\alpha}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(1-\int_{\mathcal{X}}p_{1}^{\alpha}(x)p_{2}^{1-\alpha}(x)\lambda(\mathrm{d}x)\Big),\,\alpha>0\ \hbox{and}\ \alpha\neq 1.

(2.2)

Definition 2.3
(Rényi
\alpha
-divergence)

Rényi (1961) for the first time gave one generalization of the relative entropy given in
2.1
. It is defined by

R_{\alpha}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\int_{\cal X}p_{1}^{\alpha}(x)p_{2}^{1-\alpha}(x)\lambda(dx),\,\alpha>0\ \hbox{and}\ \alpha\neq 1.

(2.3)

Definition 2.4
(Tsallis
\alpha
-divergence)

Tsallis
\alpha

T_{\alpha}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{X}}p_{1}^{\alpha}(x)p_{2}^{1-\alpha}(x)\lambda(\mathrm{d}x)-1\Big),\,\alpha>0\ \hbox{and}\ \alpha\neq 1

Convergence and estimators of divergence measures

3.1
Convergence of
\alpha
-divergence measures

We will use a result of Holden (1998) to show the geometric convergence of the MH algorithm under a minoration condition: if there exists

such that
q(y|x)\geq af(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, then

\forall y\in\mathcal{X},\Big|\frac{p^{n}(y)}{f(y)}-1\Big|\leq(1-a)^{n}\Big\|\frac{p^{0}}{f}-1\Big\|_{\infty}

(3.5)

Theorem 3.1

If the proposal density of the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm satisfies
q(y|x)\geq\delta f(y)
, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
, then with
\alpha\in(1,+\infty)

D_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}(1-(r\nu^{n}+1)^{\alpha}),

(3.6)

R_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}r\nu^{n}

(3.7)

T_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\big((r\nu^{n}+1)^{\alpha}-1\big)

(3.8)

where
r=\|\frac{p^{0}}{f}-1\|_{\infty}>0,
and
\nu=(1-\delta).

Proof.

a)
\alpha
-divergence
D_{\alpha}

D_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)=\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(1-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\frac{p^{n}(y)}{f(y)}-1+1\big)^{\alpha}f(y)\lambda(\mathrm{d}y)\Big)

(3.9)

with
\alpha\in]1,+\infty[
, we have

1-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\frac{p^{n}(y)}{f(y)}-1+1\big)^{\alpha}f(y)\lambda(\mathrm{d}y)\geq 1-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\big|\frac{p^{n}(y)}{f(y)}-1\big|+1\big)^{\alpha}f(y)\lambda(\mathrm{d}y)

Therefore using (
3.5
) we have

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(1-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\big|\frac{p^{n}(y)}{f(y)}-1\big|+1\big)^{\alpha}f(y)\lambda(\mathrm{d}y)\Big)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(1-\int_{\mathcal{X}}(r\nu^{n}+1)^{\alpha}f(y)\lambda(\mathrm{d}y)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\big(1-(r\nu^{n}+1)^{\alpha}\big)

b) Rényi
\alpha
-divergence

We will use (
3.5
) and the same procedure give us

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\frac{p^{n}(y)}{f(y)}\big)^{\alpha}f(y)\lambda(\mathrm{d}y)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\log(r\nu^{n}+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}r\nu^{n}

c) Tsallis
\alpha
-divergence

Here too we have

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\frac{p^{n}(y)}{f(y)}\big)^{\alpha}f(y)\lambda(\mathrm{d}y)-1\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\big(\big(r\nu^{n}+1\big)^{\alpha}-1)\big)

\square

Estimators for the three divergence measures

We can initially think to write the integral like a mathematical expectancy and apply the method of Monte Carlo integration.

3.2.1
k-NN based density estimator

This is a method of density estimation proposed by Loftsgaarden and Quesenberry
[
9
]
. It has a set of data training
E=\left\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N}\right\}
where
x_{i}
are independent observations with common probability density function
g
. For a data
z
, the problem is the estimation of a probability density
g
at
z
. The principle is to find among
x_{i}
the kth nearest neighbor of
z
considering euclidean distance and we are interested in the distance between the two points (
x_{i,k}
and
z
). If
x_{i,k}
and
z
are points of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
this distance
d(z,x_{i,k})
allows us to calculate the volume of the hypersphere with center
z
and radius
r=d(z,x_{i,k})
.

The proposed estimator is as follows

\hat{g}_{k,N}(z)=\frac{k/N}{V(Hs(z,d(z,x_{i,k})))}=\frac{k/N}{\pi^{d/2}r^{d}/\Gamma((d/2)+1)}

where
V(Hs(z,d(z,x_{i,k})))=\pi^{d/2}r^{d}/\Gamma((d/2)+1)
is the volume of d-dimensional ball around
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius
r>0
,
\Gamma(.)
is the Gamma function. Loftsgaarden and Quesenberry (1965) suggested after several experiments to take
k
equals to
\sqrt{N}
. We will round it if necessary to the nearest whole.

We find in
[
11
]
theorems of convergence for density’s estimators (k-NN estimators), showing the consistency of these estimators.

Theorem 3.2
(k-NN density estimators, convergence in probability)

Let
k(n)
an integer that denotes the
k(n)
th nearest neighbor in the sample of size n. If

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}k(n)=\infty
, and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n/k(n)=\infty
, then

\hat{g}_{k(n),N}(x)\longrightarrow_{p}g(x)
for almost all
x
.

Theorem 3.3
(Almost sure convergence in sup norm)

If

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}k(n)/\log(n)=\infty
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n/k(n)=\infty
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x}|\hat{g}_{k(n),N}(x)-g(x)|=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\textrm{almost surely.}

a) Application to
p^{n}

Let
X_{1:N}=(X_{1},...,X_{N})
be a simulated sample according to the law with density

with

i.i.d. We choose one

on the sample. Let
\rho_{k,i}
the euclidean distance between

and its kth nearest neighbor on the sample.

The estimate of

at

is

\hat{p}_{k,n,N}(X_{i})=\frac{k/(N-1)}{V(Hs(X_{i},\rho_{k,i}))}

(3.13)

V(Hs(X_{i},\rho_{k,i}))=c\,\rho_{k,i}^{d}
, where

stands for the volume of a d-dimensional unit ball.

Hence

\hat{p}_{k,n,N}(X_{i})=\frac{k}{(N-1)c\,\rho_{k,i}^{d}}

(3.14)

b) Application to
f

We should also have the i.i.d. simulations according to the density
f
. But the principle in this article is that we don’t know how to do i.i.d. simulations directly from

. Then we apply the MCMC simulation algorithms. After several iterations (large

) we can begin to recover samples. However, we take the precaution that two successive samples are separated by

iterations. We get now the samples

of random variables which are ’independent’ with density

. Let
\gamma_{k,i}
the euclidean distance between

and its kth nearest neighbor on the sample

.

The estimator of

\hat{f}_{k,N}(X_{i})=\frac{k}{M\,c\,\gamma_{k,i}^{d}}

(3.15)

Construction of estimators

Póczos and Schneider (2011) proposed an estimator of the integral part that is common to the three divergence measures that we have presented. However, their first estimator is asymptotically biased. They showed that multiplying by a constant, they obtained an asymptotically unbiased estimator.
Starting from it we construct our estimators for the

a) Estimator for

Begin by noting

Once the densities

and

estimated at

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\alpha,N}(p^{n},f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(1-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{\hat{f}_{k,N}(X_{i})}{\hat{p}_{k,n,N}(X_{i})}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(1-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{(N-1)\rho_{k,i}^{d}}{M\gamma_{k,i}^{d}}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\Big)

(Asymptotic unbiasedness)

4.1

Recall that the asymptotically unbiased estimator of

\hat{D}_{\alpha,N,k}(p^{n},f)=\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(1-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{(N-1)V(Hs(X_{i},\rho_{k,i}))}{mV(Hs(X_{i},\gamma_{k,i}))}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\times B_{k,\alpha}\Big).

Let

h(X_{i})=\Big(\frac{(N-1)V(hs(X_{i},\rho_{k,i}))}{mV(Hs(X_{i},\gamma_{k,i}))}\Big)^{1-\alpha}

and

\bar{Y}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}h(X_{i})

We know that

are i.i.d.
\sim p^{n}
. Function

. We can then apply the central limit theorem. Then we will have

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{Y}_{N}-\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{N})}{\sqrt{Var(\bar{Y}_{N})}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\big(0,1\big)

\displaystyle\bar{Y}_{N}\times B_{k,\alpha}-B_{k,\alpha}\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{N})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\Big(0,\sigma^{2}B^{2}_{k,\alpha}\Big)

with
\sigma^{2}=\lim_{N\to\infty}Var(\bar{Y}_{N})

But
\bar{Y}_{N}

B_{k,\alpha}

. We will have

\bar{Y}_{N}B_{k,\alpha}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}\big(M_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)\,,\sigma^{2}B_{k,\alpha}^{2}\big)

Hence

\hat{D}_{\alpha,N,k}(p^{n},f)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}\Big(D_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)\,,\frac{\sigma^{2}B_{k,\alpha}^{2}}{\alpha^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}}\Big)

(4.25)

with

and
\sigma^{2}<\infty
.

\hat{T}_{\alpha,N,k}(p^{n},f)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}\Big(T_{\alpha}(p^{n},f)\,,\frac{\sigma^{2}B^{2}_{k,\alpha}}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}\Big)

and

We had

h(X_{i})=\Big(\frac{\hat{f}_{k,N}(X_{i})}{\hat{p}_{k,n,N}(X_{i})}\Big)^{1-\alpha}\,\textrm{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\bar{Y}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}h(X_{i})

X_{i}
are i.i.d. and if
h
is continuous, then
h(X_{i})
are also i.i.d.

According to the central limit theorem, we will have

\frac{\bar{Y}_{N}-\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{N})}{\sqrt{Var(\bar{Y}_{N})}}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1)

then,

\sqrt{N}\big(\bar{Y}_{N}-\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{N})\big)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}\big(0,N\sigma^{2}\big),

if
\mathbb{E}(\bar{Y}_{N})<\infty

m=-3
and a variance

that make its density function is shifted to the left (see figure) and is therefore not adequate to cover the support of the target density. So that the chain generated by this simulation strategy will soon converge to the target distribution.

Metropolis Within Gibbs - RWMH

The Rényi divergence measures between the respective densities

This method (MwG) is often used in cases where one wants to use the Gibbs sampler and for some conditional distributions he can not simulate directly. Then one introduce in the algorithm a few steps of RWMH sampler, offering instrumental distributions having for target laws the conditional distributions. In our case (Fig. 5) we used a version that systematically applies the steps of the Metropolis sampler to all conditional distributions, so that the algorithm will delay to converge.

Conclusion

Finally we discussed in Section 6 of the causes that make a simulation strategy is more powerful than the other.

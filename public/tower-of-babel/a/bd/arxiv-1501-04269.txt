On the Tong-type identity and the mean square of the error term for an extended Selberg class
Xiaodong Cao, Yoshio Tanigawa, Wenguang Zhai

Abstract
In 1956, Tong established an asymptotic formula for the mean square of the error term in the summatory function of the Piltz divisor function d 3 ​ ( n ) . d_{3}(n). The aim of this paper is to generalize Tong’s method to a class of Dirichlet series that satisfy a functional equation. As an application, we can establish the asymptotic formulas for the mean square of the error terms for a class of functions in the well-known Selberg class. The Tong-type identity and formula established in this paper can be viewed as an analogue of the well-known Voronoï’s formula.

1
Introduction and main results

Let
a(n)(n\geq 1)
be a sequence of complex numbers, which is an arithmetic function. One of the most basic goals of analytic number theory is to
establish the asymptotic formula for the summatory function of
a(n),
as accurate as possible.
Especially it is important to study the properties of the so-called error term of this asymptotic formula,
such as the upper bound, the moments, the sign changes and

\Omega
-results, etc.

One of the important tools in this
area is Voronoï’s formula of the error term when the sequence
a(n)(n\geq 1)
satisfy
good conditions, for example, they are the coefficients of
L
-functions of any degree.
When
a(n)\ (n\geq 1)
are the coefficients of an
L
-function of degree two, Voronoï’s formula of the corresponding
error term is a very strong tool to study the properties of the error term. A well-known
example is the Dirichlet divisor problem. For a survey of Voronoï’s formula, see for example, Steuding

[
47
]
.

However, when
a(n)\ (n\geq 1)
are the coefficients of an
L
-function of degree
\geq 3
, Voronoï’s formula is not strong enough
to get good results for the properties of the error term. Especially, it fails to give an asymptotic formula
for the mean square of the error term even when the degree is
3.

In 1956, Tong
[
49
]
established an asymptotic formula for the mean square of the error term in the summatory function of the
Piltz divisor function
d_{3}(n).
This is the first result in this area for the case of degree
3
. Tong’s main ingredient is to replace the
error term by a corresponding integral such that the difference between
the error term and this integral is very small on average.

The aim of this paper is to generalize Tong’s method to a class of Dirichlet series that satisfy a
functional equation. Especially as an application, we establish the asymptotic formulas for the mean square of the error terms
for a class of functions in the well-known Selberg class.

1.1
The Selberg class

To study a summatory function of
a(n)
and its error term, we consider the Dirichlet series with coefficients
a(n)
which satisfies
a general functional equation.
There are many results in this direction, see for example, Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
3
]
, Redmond
[
42
]
,
Hafner
[
12
,
13
]
, Ivić
[
20
]
, Meurman
[
36
]
, Lau
[
33
]
, Kanemitsu, Sankaranarayanan and Tanigawa
[
29
]
,
Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
9
]
. We shall study such a problem for
L
-functions in the so-called Selberg class.

The well-known Selberg class
{\cal S}
(see for example
[
26
,
44
,
46
]
) consists of non-vanishing Dirichlet series

{\cal L}(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}},

which satisfies the following hypotheses:

I.
Ramanujan’s conjecture
:
a(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

II.
Analytic continuation
: There exists a non-negative integer
m_{\cal L}
such
that
(s-1)^{m_{\cal L}}{\cal L}(s)
is an entire function of finite order.

III.
Functional equation
:
{\cal L}(s)
satisfies a functional equation of type

(1.1)

\Lambda_{{\cal L}}(s)=\omega\overline{\Lambda_{{\cal L}}(1-\bar{s})},

where

(1.2)

\Lambda_{\cal L}(s):={\cal L}(s)Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{L}\Gamma(\alpha_{j}s+\beta_{j}),

and
Q>0,|\omega|=1
and
\alpha_{j}>0,\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm Re\,}\beta_{j}\geq 0
for all
1\leq j\leq L
.
The number
d=2\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}
is called the degree of
{\cal L}(s).

IV.
Euler product
:
{\cal L}(s)
satisfies

{\cal L}(s)=\prod_{p}\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{b(p^{n})}{p^{ns}}\right)

with suitable coefficients
b(p^{n})
satisfying
b(p^{n})\ll p^{nc}
for some
c<1/2.

Many well-known functions are contained in the Selberg class
{\cal S}
. We recall some examples.
The well-known Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
and Dirichlet
L
-functions are functions in
{\cal S}
of
degree
1.
The product of any
\ell
functions in
{\cal S}
of degree
1
is a function in
{\cal S}
of degree
\ell.

So
\zeta^{2}(s)
is a function in
{\cal S}
of degree
2
and
\zeta^{3}(s)
is of degree
3.

Let
f(z)
be a holomorphic cusp form with respect to
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
. If
f(z)
is an eigenform of all Hecke operators,
the automorphic
L
-function attached to
f(z)
is a function in
{\cal S}
of degree
2
.
The Dedekind zeta-function over an algebraic number field of degree
\kappa\geq 2
is a function in
{\cal S}
of degree
\kappa.

The extended Selberg class
{\cal S}^{\#}
(see
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
for an introduction)
consists of all Dirichlet series
\sum_{n\geq 1}a(n)n^{-s}
which satisfy the conditions
\mathrm{I}^{*}
, II and III, where

\mathrm{I}^{*}
means

\mathrm{I}^{*}
:
\sum_{n\geq 1}a(n)n^{-s}
is absolutely convergent for
\sigma>1.

Consider the sum

A(y):={\sum_{n\leq y\,}}^{\prime}a(n).

Define
Q(y)
as the sum of the residues of the function
{\cal L}(s)y^{s}s^{-1}
and the error term

E(y)
is defined by

E(y):=A(y)-Q(y).

Estimates for
E(x)
and
\int_{0}^{T}|E(y)|^{2}dy
were first studied by Voronoï in 1904 and Cramér in 1922
for the special case of the Dirichlet divisor problem respectively, see
[
52
]
and
[
6
]
. Since then this has been
generalized for larger classes of Dirichlet series.
There are many results in this direction, see for example,
[
4
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
33
,
42
,
43
]
.

Roton
[
43
]
proved that if
L\in{\cal S}^{\#}
is a function of degree
d\geq 2.
Then

E(y)\ll y^{(d-1)/(d+1)+\varepsilon},

which is a generalization of Landau’s classical result
[
32
]
.
She also proved that if

\sum_{n\leq y}|a(n)|^{2}\ll y^{1+\varepsilon},

then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy\ll\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T^{2-1/d},&\mbox{if $0<d<3,$}\\
T^{3-4/d+\varepsilon},&\mbox{if $d\geq 3$.}\end{array}\right.

1.2
Statements of main results

Suppose
0\leq\theta<1
is a real number. Let
{\cal S}^{\theta}
denote the set of all Dirichlet series

{\cal L}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a(n)n^{-s}
which satisfy II, III and

(1.4)

{\mathrm{I}}^{\prime}:\ \ \ |a(n)|\ll n^{\theta+\varepsilon},\ \ \ \sum_{n\leq y}|a(n)|^{2}\ll y^{1+\varepsilon}.

In this paper we assume that
\sum_{j=1}^{L}\beta_{j}
is real for the sake of simplicity.
Obviously
{\cal S}\subset{\cal S}^{\theta}\subset{\cal S}^{\#}.
Let
{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}

denote the set of all functions in
{\cal S}^{\theta}
with real coefficients.

Now suppose
a(n)(n\geq 1)
is a sequence of real numbers such that
its corresponding Dirichlet series
{\cal L}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
and is of degree
d\geq 2.

Without loss of generality, we suppose that
d
is an integer.
Let
1/2\leq\sigma^{*}<1
denote a fixed real number such that the estimate

(1.5)

\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}(\sigma^{*}+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}

holds for any
\varepsilon>0.
We suppose that
\sigma^{*}
satisfies the condition

(1.6)

\sigma^{*}<(d+1)/2d,

which plays an important role in our paper.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
d\geq 2
is a fixed integer,
0\leq\theta\leq 1/2-1/2d
is a real number.
Suppose that
{\cal L}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
is a function of degree

d\geq 2
such that (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) hold. Then we have

(1.7)

\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy=C_{d}T^{2-1/d}+O(T^{2-\frac{3-4\sigma^{*}}{2d(1-\sigma^{*})-1}+\varepsilon}),

where
C_{d}>0
is a positive constant.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
0\leq\theta\leq 1/4
is a real number and
{\cal L}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
is of degree
2,
then we have

(1.8)

\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy=C_{2}T^{3/2}+O_{\varepsilon}(T^{1+\varepsilon}).

Remark 1
.

When
{\cal L}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{0}
is of degree 2, the result obtained by Voronoï’s formula
is sometimes stronger. See for example, Meurman
[
36
]
or Lau and Tsang
[
34
]
in the case of Dirichlet divisor problem.
We note that the error term in the asymptotic formula (
1.8
) is best possible when disregarding
\varepsilon.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose
0\leq\theta\leq 1/3
is a real number.
Let
{\cal L}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
is a function of degree
3
such that

{\cal L}(s)={\cal L}_{1}(s){\cal L}_{2}(s),
where
{\cal L}_{1}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}

is a function of degree
1
, and
{\cal L}_{2}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
is a function of degree
2
.
Then we have

(1.9)

\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy=C_{3}T^{5/3}+O_{\varepsilon}(T^{8/5+\varepsilon}).

Furthermore if we assume that

(1.10)

\int_{0}^{T}|\mathcal{L}_{2}(1/2+it)|^{6}dt\ll T^{2+\varepsilon},

then we have

(1.11)

\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy=C_{3}T^{5/3}+O_{\varepsilon}(T^{14/9+\varepsilon}).

Remark 2
.

The assumption (
1.10
) for degree
2

L
-functions is very natural. The first result in this direction is the twelfth power moment of the
Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
(the sixth moment of
\zeta^{2}(s)
over the critical line) due to Heath-Brown
[
16
]
. Meurmann
[
38
]
proved
the twelfth power moment for any Dirichlet L-functions. From Heath-Brown
[
16
]
and Meurmann
[
38
]
we see that (
1.10
) holds for
Dedekind zeta functions of quadratic fields. From
[
25
]
we know that (
1.10
) holds for
the automorphic
L
-functions attached to holomorphic cusp forms.

Remark 3
.

Tong
[
49
]
first established Theorem 1 for the Piltz divisor problem of dimension
d\geq 3,

which becomes a true asymptotic formula when
d=3.
Fomenko
[
10
]
followed Tong’s method to study the case of the
Dedekind zeta-function of cubic fields.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
d\geq 2
is an integer,
0\leq\theta\leq 1/2-1/2d
is a real number.
Suppose that
{\cal L}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
is a function of degree

d
such that (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) hold. Then the error term
E(t)
has a distribution
function
f(\alpha)
in the sense that, for any interval

we have

The function

and its
derivatives satisfy

\frac{d^{k}}{d\alpha^{k}}f(\alpha)\ll_{A,k}(1+|\alpha|)^{-A}

for
k=0,1,2,\cdots
and

can be extended to an entire

Corollary 3
.

Suppose
0\leq\theta\leq 1/2-1/2d
is a real number.
Suppose that

is a function of degree

2,
or is a function of degree

such that it can be written as a product of

and a function of degree

Theorem 3

Suppose that

Suppose

1.3
A remark on Tong’s method

The most important tool up to now to study the behaviour of
E(y)
is Voronoï’s formula, which is usually of the form

(1.12)

E(y)=c_{1}y^{\frac{d-1}{2d}}\sum_{n\leq N}\frac{a(n)}{n^{\frac{d+1}{2d}}}\cos(c_{2}(ny)^{1/d}+c_{3})+O(y^{\frac{d-1}{d}+\varepsilon}N^{-\frac{1}{d}}),

where
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
are constants and
1\ll N\ll y.
For a proof of the above general formula, see for example
Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
9
]
.
Roughly speaking, this formula gives a good approximation to the error term, which is usually
a finite exponential sum, plus a “permissible small” error.

When
d=2,
the formula (
1.12
) is strong enough for us to study the properties of
E(x).
For example,
its upper bound, power moments, sign changes, etc.
A good example is the Dirichlet divisor problem, see for example, the survey paper Tsang
[
50
]
.

However when
d\geq 3,
the error term in (
1.12
) is always
\gg y^{1-2/d+\varepsilon}
, which is much larger than the expected order

y^{(d-1)/2d}
. So (
1.12
) can’t be used to study the asymptotic behaviour of power moments of
E(y),
even for the mean square.

In
[
48
,
49
]
, Tong developed a method to study the mean square of the error term in the summatory function of the
Piltz divisor function
d_{\ell}(n),
which denotes the number of ways such that
n
can be written as a product of
\ell

natural numbers. When
\ell=3,
Tong’s method gives a true asymptotic formula of the mean square of the error term
\Delta_{3}(y)
.
Tong’s main ingredient is to replace the error term by a corresponding integral such that the difference between
the error term and this integral is very small on average.

The aim of this paper is to generalize Tong’s method to the general case. Actually we shall generalize Tong’s method to
a class of functions much more general than
{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
. As applications, we establish
the asymptotic formula of the mean square of
E(y)
for functions in
{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
.

1.4
Organization of this paper

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2 we shall introduce a class of more general
functions. In Section 3 we give some preliminary lemmas about the so-called generalized Bessel functions.
In Section 4 we shall establish a Tong-type identity for the
corresponding error term
E(y)
. As some simple applications of the Tong’s identity,
in Section 5 we give a lower bound for integrals involving
E(y)
and study the small values of

E(y).
In Section 6 we shall establish a truncated Tong-type formula for an integral involving
E(y).

In Section 7 we shall estimate some exponential integrals, which are important in the proof.
In Section 8 we shall prove Theorem 1. In Section 9 we shall prove Theorem 2 and Theorem 3.
In Section 10, we give some examples.

2
A class of more general arithmetic functions

For future applications in mind (e.g.
[
2
]
), we shall derive a Tong-type identity of the error term for a more general
class of functions than that of Selberg class. In this section, following basically to Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
3
,
4
]

and Hafner
[
12
]
, we introduce such a class of functions.

Definition.
Let
\{a(n)\}
and
\{b(n)\}
be two sequences of complex numbers, not identically zero.
Let
\{\lambda_{n}\}
and
\{\mu_{n}\}
be two strictly increasing sequences of positive numbers tending to infinity. Suppose that the series

\displaystyle\varphi(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n)\lambda_{n}^{-s},\quad\psi(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b(n)\mu_{n}^{-s}

converge in some half-plane and have abscissae of absolute convergence
\sigma_{a}^{*}
and
\sigma_{b}^{*}
, respectively.
Define two gamma factors

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\Gamma(\alpha_{j}s+\beta_{j})

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}(s)=\prod_{h=1}^{N^{\prime}}\Gamma(\alpha_{h}^{\prime}s+\beta_{h}^{\prime}),

where
\alpha_{j}
and
\alpha_{h}^{\prime}
are positive real numbers and
\beta_{j}
and
\beta_{h}^{\prime}
are complex numbers.
We assume throughout that

(2.3)

\alpha:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}=\sum_{h=1}^{N^{\prime}}\alpha_{h}^{\prime}.

Let
r
be real. We say that
\varphi
and
\psi
satisfy the functional equation

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(s)\varphi(s)=\Delta_{2}(r-s)\psi(r-s)

if there exists in the
s
-plane a domain
D
that is the exterior of a compact set
\mathfrak{S}
(we call it a “singularity set”)
and on which there exists a holomorphic function
\chi(s)
(
s=\sigma+it
,
\sigma
and
t
real) such that

\displaystyle\texttt{(i)}\quad\lim_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\chi(\sigma+it)=0

uniformly in every interval
-\infty<\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{2}<+\infty
, and

\displaystyle\texttt{(ii)}\quad\chi(s)=\begin{cases}\Delta_{1}(s)\varphi(s)&\mbox{for $\sigma>\sigma_{a}^{*}$,}\\
\Delta_{2}(r-s)\psi(r-s)&\mbox{for $\sigma<r-\sigma_{b}^{*}$.}\end{cases}.

Finally suppose that both
\varphi(s)
and
\psi(s)
have only a finite number of poles on the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}.

Remark 4
.

When
\Delta_{1}(s)=\Delta_{2}(s),
we get the class of functions defined in Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
3
,
4
]
.
Clearly any function in the extended Selberg class
{\cal S}^{\#}
satisfies the functional equation of the form (
2.4
).

Remark 5
.

There are many other arithmetic functions in the above class but
\Delta_{1}(s)\not=\Delta_{2}(s).
One example is the well-known
asymmetric many-dimensional divisor problem. For a survey of the asymmetric many-dimensional divisor problem, see Krätzel
[
31
]
or
Ivić etc.
[
23
]
.
A forthcoming paper
[
2
]
in this direction is in preparation through the approach of this paper.

From now on we always assume that
a(n),b(n)
are real. We suppose that there are infinitely many
n
such
that
|a(n)|\gg 1
and the same holds for
b(n).

For
y>0
and a real number
\varrho
, we define the summatory function
A_{\varrho}(y)
of the arithmetical function
a(n)
by

\displaystyle A_{\varrho}(y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\varrho+1)}{\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq y}}^{\prime}a(n)(y-\lambda_{n})^{\varrho},

where the symbol
\prime
indicates that the last term has to be halved if
\varrho=0
and
y=\lambda_{n}
.
When
\varrho
is negative,
A_{\varrho}(x)
is defined only for those positive
y
not equal to any
\lambda_{n}
.

Let
s_{0}=\sup\{|s|:s\in\mathfrak{S}\},
where
\mathfrak{S}
is the ”singularity set” in Definition,
and
t_{0}=\max\{|\frac{\beta_{j}}{\alpha_{j}}|,|\frac{\beta_{h}^{\prime}}{\alpha_{h}^{\prime}}|:j=1,2,\ldots,N,h=1,2,\ldots,N^{\prime}\}
.
Choose two constants

Choose the third constant

We choose

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the rectangle with vertices

and

,

\mathcal{C}_{u,v}

,

We define the residual function or the main term

(2.5)

\displaystyle Q_{\varrho}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)y^{\varrho+s}}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+1)}ds.

From our choices of

and

S
. By the residue theorem we have

Q_{\varrho}(y)=\sum_{s_{j}}y^{\varrho+s_{j}}P_{s_{j}}(\log y),

where
s_{j}
runs over all the poles of
\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)y^{\varrho+s}/\Gamma(s+\varrho+1)
inside
\mathcal{C}
,

P_{s_{j}}(t)
is a polynomial of

such that its degree is the order of the pole
s_{j}
minus
1
.

We define the error term in the asymptotic formula for
A_{\varrho}(y)
as

E_{\varrho}(y)=A_{\varrho}(y)-Q_{\varrho}(y).

As for
\psi(s)
we define similarly that

\displaystyle A_{\varrho}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\varrho+1)}{\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq y}}^{\prime}b(n)(y-\mu_{n})^{\varrho},

\displaystyle Q_{\varrho}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\psi(s)y^{\varrho+s}}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+1)}ds,

\displaystyle E_{\varrho}^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=A_{\varrho}^{*}(y)-Q_{\varrho}^{*}(y).

Similarly to (

Q^{*}_{\varrho}(y)=\sum_{s_{j}}y^{\varrho+s_{j}}P_{s_{j}}^{*}(\log y),

where

inside
\mathcal{C}
,

P_{s_{j}}^{*}(t)
is a polynomial such that its degree is the order of the pole
s_{j}
minus
1
.

When
\varrho=0,
for simplicity, we also use the following notation

\displaystyle A(y)

\displaystyle=A_{0}(y),

\displaystyle Q(y)

\displaystyle=Q_{0}(y),

\displaystyle E(y)

\displaystyle=E_{0}(y),

\displaystyle A^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=A_{0}^{*}(y),

\displaystyle Q^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=Q_{0}^{*}(y),

\displaystyle E^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=E_{0}^{*}(y).

We suppose that
\sigma^{*}<\sigma_{b}^{*}
is a real number such that the estimate

We shall prove a fundamental lemma of the asymptotic expansion of the integral which we need later.
First we recall the well-known Stirling’s formula for gamma function.

Lemma 1

|\arg s|<\pi-\delta

, as

.

The constants

\displaystyle a_{k}

Proof.

Consider

By (

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}(s)=\prod_{h=1}^{N^{\prime}}\Gamma(\alpha_{h}^{\prime}s+\beta_{h}^{\prime})\sim(2\pi)^{N^{\prime}/2}\exp(g_{2}(s))\left(1+\frac{b_{1}^{\prime}}{s}+\frac{b_{2}^{\prime}}{s^{2}}+\cdots\right),

where

\displaystyle g_{2}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{N^{\prime}}\left\{\left(\alpha_{h}^{\prime}s+\beta_{h}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\log s+\log\alpha_{h}^{\prime})-\alpha_{h}^{\prime}s\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha s+\mu^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log s+(\tau^{\prime}-\alpha)s+\nu^{\prime}.

Similarly we have, with some constants

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(r-s)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j}\Gamma(-\alpha_{j}s+\alpha_{j}r+\beta_{j})

where

\displaystyle g_{1}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\left(-\alpha_{j}s+\alpha_{j}r+\beta_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)(\log(-s)+\log\alpha_{j})+\alpha_{j}s\right\}

By using

Combing these formulas we find that with some constants

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Delta_{1}(r-s)}s^{\omega}F(s)x^{-s}

\displaystyle\qquad\times s^{\omega+M}\left(d_{0}+\frac{d_{1}}{s}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{(N^{\prime}-N)/2}\exp(g(s))e^{-s\log x}s^{\omega+M}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{p_{n}}{s^{n}}.

By (

\overline{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}\cap\{s|t>0\}}\frac{\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Delta_{1}(r-s)}s^{\omega}F(s)x^{-s}ds}=-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}\cap\{s|t<0\}}\frac{\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Delta_{1}(r-s)}s^{\omega}F(s)x^{-s}ds,

Then

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Delta_{1}(r-s)}s^{\omega}F(s)x^{-s}\sim

\displaystyle C_{0}e^{\pi i(\alpha r+\mu-1/2)}s^{\mu^{\prime}-\mu-\alpha r+\omega+M}\exp(f(s))\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{p_{n}}{s^{n}}

Now let

We define the new parameters

4
The Tong-type identity for the error term

In this section we shall prove the Tong-type identity for the integral (or multiple integral) of the error term
E_{\varrho}(y)
,
which is a generalization of Theorem 1 in Tong
[
48
]
for the error term in the Piltz divisor problem.
The main result of this section is

Theorem 4
.

Let
x\geq 1
,
L\geq 0
. Suppose that

\varrho+k>2\alpha\sigma_{b}^{*}-\alpha r-\frac{1}{2}+\mu^{\prime}-\mu.

Then for
x+ky>0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\cdots\int_{0}^{y}E_{\varrho}(x+y_{1}+\cdots+y_{k})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq L}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{r+\varrho}}\int_{0}^{y}\cdots\int_{0}^{y}f_{\varrho}\left((x+y_{1}+\cdots+y_{k})\mu_{n}\right)dy_{1}\cdots dy_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\sum_{\mu_{n}>L}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{r+\varrho+k}}f_{\varrho+k}\left((x+jy)\mu_{n}\right).

Remark 7
.

Cao
[
1
]
and Fomenko
[
11
]
used Tong’s idea in
[
48
]
to study the asymmetric many-dimensional
problem and mean value theorems for automorphic
L
-functions, respectively.

Proof.

We assume first that
\varrho\geq 0
and
\varrho+k>\alpha(2c-r)+\mu^{\prime}-\mu
.
Then by Perron’s formula (see, Ivić
[
19
, (A.8) and (A.4)]
) we have

\displaystyle A_{\varrho}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)x^{s+\varrho}}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+1)}ds,

where
c
is the constant chosen in Section 2. By the residue theorem and (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle E_{\varrho}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,r-b}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)x^{s+\varrho}}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+1)}ds,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{x}E_{\varrho}(u)du=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,r-b}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)x^{s+\varrho+1}}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+2)}ds+c_{1}

with some constant
c_{1}
. By induction we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}E_{\varrho}(x+y_{1})dy_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{x+y}E_{\varrho}(u)du-\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{x}E_{\varrho}(u)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,r-b}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)\left((x+y)^{s+\varrho+1}-x^{s+\varrho+1}\right)}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+2)}ds,

and

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\cdots\int_{0}^{y}E_{\varrho}(x+y_{1}+\cdots+y_{k})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{y}\cdots\int_{0}^{y}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{k}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,r-b}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)\left((x+y+\sum\limits_{j=2}^{k}y_{j})^{s+\varrho+1}-(x+\sum\limits_{j=2}^{k}y_{j})^{s+\varrho+1}\right)}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+2)}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,r-b}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+2)}\int_{0}^{y}\cdots\int_{0}^{y}\left(\Bigl(x+y+\sum_{j=2}^{k}y_{j}\Bigr)^{s+\varrho+1}-\Bigl(x+\sum_{j=2}^{k}y_{j}\Bigr)^{s+\varrho+1}\right)dy_{2}\cdots dy_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,r-b}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)(x+jy)^{s+\varrho+k}}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+k+1)}ds.

Furthermore, under the condition
\varrho+k>\alpha(2c-r)+\mu^{\prime}-\mu
. we can change the path of integration in (
4.1
) to
\mathcal{C}_{r-c,r-b}
,
and by using the functional equation along with a change of variable from
s
to
r-s
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,r-b}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)u^{s+\varrho+k}}{\Gamma(s+\varrho+k+1)}ds=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,b}}G_{\varrho+k}(s)\psi(s)u^{r+\varrho+k-s}ds\quad(u>0).

Since
b>c>\sigma_{b}^{*}
,
\psi(s)
is expressed as a Dirichlet series. Exchanging the order of summation and integration, we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{r+\varrho+k}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{c,b}}G_{\varrho+k}(s)(u\mu_{n})^{r+\varrho+k-s}ds=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{r+\varrho+k}}f_{\varrho+k}(u\mu_{n})

for
a<c
. Since
f_{\varrho+k}(u\mu_{n})\ll(u\mu_{n})^{\theta_{\varrho+k}}
, the series (
4.2
) converges absolutely if

(4.3)

\varrho+k>2\alpha\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-\alpha r-\frac{1}{2}+\mu^{\prime}-\mu.

Thus under this assumption, the exchange of the order of summation and integration is justified and
the equality

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\cdots\int_{0}^{y}E_{\varrho}\left(x+\sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}\right)dy_{1}\cdots dy_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{r+\varrho+k}}f_{\varrho+k}\left((x+jy)\mu_{n}\right)

is valid under (
4.3
).

Finally we note that if
\min\{u,u+y\}>0
, then for any non-negative integer
j
we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}f_{\varrho+j}\left((u+y_{1})\mu_{n}\right)dy_{1}=\int_{0}^{y}dy_{1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}G_{\varrho+j}(s)\left((u+y_{1})\mu_{n}\right)^{r+\varrho+j-s}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i\mu_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Gamma(r+\varrho+j+2-s)\Delta_{1}(r-s)}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\left(\left((u+y_{1})\mu_{n}\right)^{r+\varrho+j+1-s}-(u\mu_{n})^{r+\varrho+j+1-s}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\left(f_{\varrho+j+1}(u+y)-f_{\varrho+j+1}(u)\right).

By induction we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\cdots\int_{0}^{y}f_{\varrho}\left(\Bigl(x+\sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}\Bigr)\mu_{n}\right)dy_{1}\cdots dy_{k}=\frac{1}{\mu_{n}^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}f_{\varrho+k}\left((x+jy)\mu_{n}\right).

Theorem 4 follows from (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) at once.
∎

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{k}{\rm Re\,}\left\{c_{jm}^{\prime}I\left(\lambda_{0}-\frac{k+m}{2\alpha},N,\infty,y+\frac{j}{x}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\times x^{k}\left(y+\frac{j}{x}\right)^{k+\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\frac{k+m}{2\alpha}},

(6.10)

\displaystyle R_{5}(y;M)\ll

\displaystyle x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}M^{\max(\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{3}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha},0)}\log^{A}M

\displaystyle+x^{-2+\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}M^{\max(\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha},0)}\log^{A}M

\displaystyle+x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{r}{2}}M^{\sigma_{b}^{\ast}+\omega_{1}-r-\frac{1}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle+x^{(4\alpha-1)(\sigma_{b}^{\ast}+\omega_{1})-2k+r-\varrho+2(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)+\frac{2k}{\alpha}-2\alpha r-4\alpha},

(6.11)

\displaystyle R_{6}(y;M)\ll

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if $b(n)\geq 0\ (n\geq 1)$,}\\
x^{\theta_{\varrho}}M^{\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-1+\omega_{0}-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}},&\mbox{if the condition (B1) holds.}\end{cases}

Here
\kappa_{0},c_{0}
and

6.1
Lemmas for generalized Bessel functions

Before proving Theorem 7 we shall prepare some preliminary assertions for
the so-called generalized Bessel functions.

By induction one easily verifies (
r-s\notin-\mathbb{N}
)

(6.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}\tilde{y}^{r-s}d\mathrm{Y}_{k}=x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\binom{k}{l}\frac{(y+lx^{-1})^{k+r-s}}{(1+r-s)(2+r-s)\cdots(k+r-s)}

For
j\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle f_{\varrho,j}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)}{(r+\varrho-s)^{j}\Gamma(r+\varrho+1-s)}\frac{\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Delta_{1}(r-s)}x^{r+\varrho-s}ds

and

g_{\varrho,j}(u)=\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}f_{\varrho,j}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}.

By definition
f_{\varrho}(x)=f_{\varrho,0}(x)
.
We note that
f_{\varrho,j}(x)
converges absolutely if

a<\frac{r}{2}+\frac{\varrho+j-\mu^{\prime}+\mu}{2\alpha}.

Lemma 5
.

Let
x\leq y\leq(1+\delta)x
,
b
is a sufficiently large positive constant.
Then we have

(6.13)

\displaystyle f_{\varrho,j}(x)

\displaystyle\ll x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{j}{2\alpha}},

and

(6.14)

\displaystyle g_{\varrho,j}(u)

\displaystyle\ll x^{2k}(ux)^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{k+j}{2\alpha}}.

Proof.

The first assertion (
6.13
) is obtained immediately by replacing
\varrho
by
\varrho+1
and taking
M=-j
in (
3.14
).
(
M
is the parameter in the definition of

see (
3.9
).)

To prove (
6.14
), substituting the definitions of
f_{\varrho,j}(x)
into
g_{\varrho,j}(u)
and using (
6.12
),
we get

\displaystyle g_{\varrho,j}(u)

\displaystyle=x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\binom{k}{l}\left(y+\frac{l}{x}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)}{(r+\varrho-s)^{j}\Gamma(r+\varrho+k+1-s)}\frac{\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Delta_{1}(r-s)}\left(\left(y+\frac{l}{x}\right)u\right)^{r+\varrho-s}ds.

Replacing
\varrho
by
\lambda=\varrho+k+1
and taking

in (

\displaystyle g_{\varrho,j}(u)\ll x^{2k}(ux)^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{k+j}{2\alpha}}.

∎

We also need the asymptotic behaviour of the function

defined by

\Phi_{\varrho}(y)=\frac{d}{du}\left(\frac{f_{\varrho}(yu)}{u^{r+\varrho}}\right).

Lemma 6
.

Let

be a fixed positive integer and

and

such that

(6.15)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{l}}{dy^{l}}\Phi_{\varrho}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{H}{\rm Re\,}\left\{b_{lm}^{\prime}\frac{(uy)^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}+\frac{l-m}{2\alpha}}}{y^{l}u^{r+\varrho+1}}e^{-ih(uy)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}+O\left(\frac{(uy)^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{l-H}{2\alpha}}}{y^{l}u^{r+\varrho+1}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(y^{r+\varrho-b-l}u^{-1-b}\right),

and

(6.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}\Phi_{\varrho}(\tilde{y})d\mathrm{Y_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left\{\sum_{m=0}^{H}{\rm Re\,}\left(b_{jm}^{\prime\prime}\frac{x^{k}(y+\frac{j}{x})^{k+\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\frac{k+m}{2\alpha}}}{u^{r+\varrho+1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{k+m}{2\alpha}}}e^{-ih((y+\frac{j}{x})u)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+O\left(\frac{x^{2k+\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\frac{k+H+1}{2\alpha}}}{u^{r+\varrho+1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{k+H+1}{2\alpha}}}\right)\right\}+O\left(x^{2k+r+\varrho-b}u^{-1-b}\right).

Proof.

From the definition of

, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varrho}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)\Delta_{2}(s)(-s)}{\Gamma(r+\varrho+1-s)\Delta_{1}(r-s)}y^{r+\varrho-s}u^{-s-1}ds,

hence

\displaystyle\frac{d^{l}}{dy^{l}}\Phi_{\varrho}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y^{l}u^{r+\varrho+1}}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)\Delta_{2}(s)}{\Gamma(r+\varrho+1-s)\Delta_{2}(r-s)}

\displaystyle\quad\times s(s-r-\varrho)(s-r-\varrho+1)\cdots(s-r-\varrho+l-1)(yu)^{r+\varrho-s}ds.

Replacing
\varrho
by
\varrho+1
and taking
M=l+1
in the formula (

), we find that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{l}}{dy^{l}}\Phi_{\varrho}(y)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{y^{l}u^{r+\varrho+1}}\Bigl\{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{lm}(yu)^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}+\frac{l-m}{2\alpha}}\cos(h(yu)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+c_{m}\pi)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+O((yu)^{r+\varrho-b})\Bigr\},

which proves the assertion (

Next we prove (

). From (

) and (

(6.18)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}\Phi_{\varrho}(\tilde{y})d\mathrm{Y_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)\Delta_{2}(s)(-s)}{\Gamma(r+\varrho+1-s)\Delta_{1}(r-s)}u^{-s-1}\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}\tilde{y}^{r+\varrho-s}d\mathrm{Y_{k}}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{k}}{u^{r+\varrho+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\left(y+\frac{j}{k}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)\Delta_{2}(s)(-s)}{\Gamma(r+\varrho+k+1-s)\Delta_{1}(r-s)}\left(\left(y+\frac{j}{x}\right)u\right)^{r+\varrho-s}ds.

Replacing
\varrho
by
\varrho+k+1
and taking
M=1
in (
3.13
), we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{a,b}}\frac{\Gamma(r-s)\Delta_{2}(s)(-s)}{\Gamma(r+\varrho+k+1-s)\Delta_{1}(r-s)}\left(\left(y+\frac{j}{x}\right)u\right)^{r+\varrho-s}ds

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{jm}^{\prime}\left(\left(y+\frac{j}{x}\right)u\right)^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\frac{k+m}{2\alpha}}\cos\left(h\left(\left(y+\frac{j}{x}\right)u\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+c_{m}\pi\right)

\displaystyle\quad+O\left((yu)^{r+\varrho-b}\right)

with certain real constants

. Substituting this expression to (

), we get (

).
∎

6.2
Proof of Theorem 7

Now we turn to the proof of Theorem 7. Let
M\in[1,\sqrt{N}]
be a real number as is chosen before, namely.

M\neq\mu_{n}
for
n=1,2,\ldots
and
\hat{E}(M)=0
if
b(n)\geq 0
for all
n

or
\hat{E}(M)\ll M^{\sigma_{b}^{*}-1+\omega_{0}}
otherwise. We always suppose that there are
infinitely many
n
for which
b(n)\gg 1.

Recall the Tong-type identity:

\displaystyle I:=\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}E_{\varrho}\left(y+\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}}{x}\right)d\mathrm{Y}_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{r+\varrho}}\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}f_{\varrho}\left(\left(y+\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}y_{j}}{x}\right)\mu_{n}\right)d\mathrm{Y}_{k}.

In the right hand side we divide the sum over
n
into two parts, namely

I=\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq M}+\sum_{\mu_{n}>M}=:S_{1}(M)+S_{2}(M).

We first treat
S_{1}(M)
. Let
F(\tilde{y})=f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)
. By Taylor’s formula we have

F(\tilde{y})=F(y)+F^{\prime}(y_{1})(\tilde{y}-y)

with some
y\leq y_{1}\leq\tilde{y}
. Then from the definition
f_{\varrho}(x)
and (
3.15
) of Lemma 4 we have an asymptotic expansion

(6.19)

\displaystyle F(y)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\kappa_{l}(yu)^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{l}{2\alpha}}\cos(h(yu)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+c_{l}\pi)+O((yu)^{r+\varrho-b})

and the upper bound

\displaystyle\ll(yu)^{\theta_{\varrho}}

if we take
b
large. Furthermore since
F^{\prime}(y_{1})=uf_{\varrho-1}(y_{1}u)
, we have

\displaystyle|F^{\prime}(y_{1})(\tilde{y}-y)|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{u}{x}(xu)^{\theta_{\varrho-1}}

\displaystyle=x^{-2}(xu)^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}.

Take out the term corresponding to
l=0
in (
6.19
), we get

\displaystyle S_{1}(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq M}\frac{b(n)}{\mu_{n}^{r+\varrho}}\left\{\kappa_{0}(y\mu_{n})^{\theta_{\varrho}}\cos(h(y\mu_{n})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+c_{0}\pi)+O((y\mu_{n})^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}})\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\left.+O((y\mu_{n})^{r+\varrho-b})+O\left(x^{-2}(x\mu_{n})^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=:R_{1}(y;M)+V_{1}+V_{2}+V_{3}.

The error term
V_{2}
can be negligible by taking
b
large. On the hand, by (B2), we have

(6.20)

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle\ll x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq M}|b(n)|\mu_{n}^{-r-\varrho+\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\ll x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}M^{\max(\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{3}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha},0)}\log^{A}M.

Similarly we have

(6.21)

V_{3}\ll x^{-2+\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}M^{\max(\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha},0)}\log^{A}M.

Since
M\neq\mu_{n}
for all
n
, it is easy to see that
if
K(u)
is any continuously differentiable function such that

K(u)Q^{*}(u)\rightarrow 0,\int_{u}^{\infty}Q^{*}(t)K^{\prime}(t)dt\rightarrow 0\ (u\rightarrow\infty),

then

\int_{M}^{\infty}K(u)d\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq u}b(n)=\int_{M}^{\infty}K(u)dA^{*}(u).

So for
S_{2}(M)
we can write

\displaystyle S_{2}(M)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}\right)d\sum_{\mu_{n}\leq u}b(n)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}\right)dA^{*}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}\right)d\hat{Q}(u)+\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{u^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}\right)d\hat{E}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{Q}^{\prime}(u)}{u^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}du+\left.\frac{\hat{E}(u)}{u^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}\right|_{u=M}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M}^{\infty}\hat{E}(u)du\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}\frac{d}{du}\left(\frac{f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)}{u^{r+\varrho}}\right)d\mathrm{Y}_{k}

\displaystyle=:S_{21}(M)+S_{22}(M)-S_{23}(M).

We shall consider
S_{21}(M)
first. From the definition of
f_{\varrho,j}(x)
, one easily checks that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{du}f_{\varrho,j+1}(yu)=\frac{1}{u}f_{\varrho,j}(yu)\quad(1\leq j\leq k+1).

Let
w_{j}(u)
be the functions defined by

w_{1}(u)=\hat{Q}^{\prime}(u)u^{1-r-\varrho}

and

w_{j+1}(u)=w_{j}^{\prime}(u)u\ (j\geq 1).

Then by repeated integration by parts and the definition of

we have

(6.22)

\displaystyle\int_{M}^{M_{1}}\frac{\hat{Q}^{\prime}(u)}{u^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}du

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{j+1}\,w_{j}(u)g_{\varrho,j}(u)\Big|_{M}^{M_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{k+1}\int_{M}^{M_{1}}w_{k+1}^{\prime}(u)g_{\varrho,k+1}(u)du.

Recall that
\hat{Q}(u)
is the sum of residues of of all poles of
\psi(s)x^{s}s^{-1}
except
0
. Let
\gamma
be
a pole with order
\nu
and
\gamma_{0}
the pole with the maximal real part.
Then the residue at
\gamma
has the form
\hat{Q}_{\gamma}(u)=u^{\gamma}P(\log u)
, where

is
a polynomial of degree
\nu-1
. Let
w_{j,\gamma}(u)
be functions defined by the same way for

\hat{Q}_{\gamma}(u)
. Then we see easily

\displaystyle w_{1,\gamma}(u)

\displaystyle=u^{1-r-\varrho}(\gamma u^{\gamma-1}P(\log u)+u^{\gamma}P^{\prime}(\log u)/u)

\displaystyle\ll u^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma-r-\varrho}\log^{\nu-1}u.

Similarly we have

w_{j,\gamma}(u)\ll u^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma-r-\varrho}\log^{\nu-1}u

for
j\geq 2
.
Hence by (
6.14
), we have

\displaystyle w_{j,\gamma}(M_{1})g_{\varrho,j}(M_{1})\ll M_{1}^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma-r-\varrho}(\log M_{1})^{\nu-1}\,x^{2k}(xM_{1})^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{k+j}{2\alpha}}.

Since
k
is chosen as (
6.2
), the exponent of
M_{1}
is negative,
w_{j,\gamma}(M_{1})g_{\varrho,j}(M_{1})
,
as well as

when
M_{1}\to\infty
.

Next we consider the term with
u=M
. Applying (
6.13
) in this case, we get

(6.23)

\displaystyle|w_{j,\gamma}(M)g_{\varrho,j}(M)|

\displaystyle\leq|w_{j,\gamma}(M)|\int_{E_{k}}|f_{\varrho,j}(\tilde{y}u)|d\mathrm{Y_{k}}

\displaystyle\ll M^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma-r-\varrho}(\log M)^{\nu-1}\,(xM)^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}

\displaystyle=x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{j}{2\alpha}}M^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1+2j}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}}(\log M)^{\nu-1}.

For the last integral of (
6.22
) which corresponds to
\gamma
, (
6.13
) implies that

(6.24)

\displaystyle\int_{M}^{M_{1}}w_{k+1,\gamma}^{\prime}(u)g_{\varrho,k+1}(u)du

\displaystyle\ll\int_{M}^{M_{1}}u^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma-r-\varrho}(\log u)^{\nu-1}\,(xu)^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{k+1}{2\alpha}}du

\displaystyle=x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{k+1}{2\alpha}}M^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}-\frac{k+1}{2\alpha}}(\log M)^{\nu-1}.

From (
6.23
) and (
6.24
) we get the estimate from the pole

. It is easily seen that the term

\gamma_{0}

gives the maximal estimate, namely we have

S_{21}(M)\ll x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}M^{{\rm Re\,}\gamma_{0}-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{3}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}}(\log M)^{A^{\prime}}

with some integer

. Now we note that
{\rm Re\,}\gamma_{0}\leq\sigma_{b}^{\ast}
, hence
S_{21}(M)
is absorbed into the right hand side

Next we consider
S_{22}(M)
.
By the trivial bound
\hat{E}(x)\ll x^{\sigma_{b}^{\ast}+\varepsilon}
and (

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{E}(M_{1})}{M_{1}^{r+\varrho}}\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}M_{1})d\mathrm{Y_{k}}

\displaystyle\ll M_{1}^{\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-r-\varrho+\varepsilon}x^{2k}(yM_{1})^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{k}{2\alpha}}

The exponent of

Consider the term
\frac{\hat{E}(M)}{M^{r+\varrho}}\int_{E_{k}}f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}M)dY

\displaystyle S_{22}(M)

\displaystyle\ll x^{\theta_{\varrho}}M^{\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-1+\omega_{0}-(r+\varrho)+\theta_{\varrho}}

\displaystyle=x^{\theta_{\varrho}}M^{\sigma_{b}^{\ast}-1+\omega_{0}-\frac{r}{2}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}}.

Finally we consider

\displaystyle S_{23}(M)=\left(\int_{M}^{N}+\int_{N}^{\infty}\right)\hat{E}(u)\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}\frac{d}{du}\left(\frac{f_{\varrho}(\tilde{y}u)}{u^{r+\varrho}}\right)d\mathrm{Y_{k}}du=:S_{231}+S_{232}.

(6.25)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varrho}(\tilde{y})=\sum_{l=0}^{J}\left(\frac{d^{l}}{dy^{l}}\Phi_{\varrho}(y)\right)\frac{(\tilde{y}-y)^{l}}{l!}+\left.\frac{d^{J+1}}{dv^{J+1}}\Phi_{\varrho}(v)\right|_{v=v_{0}}\frac{(\tilde{y}-y)^{J+1}}{(J+1)!},

\displaystyle V_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}^{N}\hat{E}(u)du\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}\left.\frac{d^{J+1}}{dv^{J+1}}\Phi_{\varrho}(v)\right|_{v=v_{0}}\frac{(\tilde{y}-y)^{J+1}}{(J+1)!}d\mathrm{Y}_{k}

\displaystyle(ux)^{\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\left(\frac{(ux)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{x^{2}}\right)^{J+1}

\displaystyle\leq x^{-\varepsilon(\frac{r}{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha})}\leq 1.

Therefore

\displaystyle V_{4}

\displaystyle\ll x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{r}{2}}\int_{M}^{N}|\hat{E}(u)|u^{-r-\frac{1}{4\alpha}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}-1}du.

Now by the assumption (B3) we get the upper bound of

(6.26)

V_{4}\ll x^{\theta_{\varrho}-\frac{r}{2}}M^{\sigma_{b}^{\ast}+\omega_{1}-r-\frac{1}{4a}-\frac{\varrho}{2\alpha}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}-\mu}{2\alpha}-1}.

Next we treat the first
J

\Phi_{\varrho}(\tilde{y})
. Their contribution to

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{J}\frac{x^{-l}}{l!}\int_{M}^{N}\hat{E}(u)\frac{d^{l}}{dy^{l}}\Phi_{\varrho}(y)du\int_{\mathbf{E}_{k}}(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{k})^{l}d\mathrm{Y}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{J}c_{l}^{*}x^{-l}\int_{M}^{N}\hat{E}(u)\frac{d^{l}}{dy^{l}}\Phi_{\varrho}(y)du

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{J}\sum_{m=0}^{J}{\rm Re\,}\left\{c_{l}^{*}b_{lm}^{\prime}\int_{M}^{N}\hat{E}(u)\frac{(uy)^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}+\frac{l-m}{2\alpha}}}{(xy)^{l}u^{r+\varrho+1}}e^{-ih(uy)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}du\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(\sum_{l=0}^{J}\int_{M}^{N}|\hat{E}(u)|\frac{(ux)^{\theta_{\varrho}+\frac{l-J}{2\alpha}}}{x^{2l}u^{r+\varrho+1}}du\right)+O\left(\sum_{l=0}^{J}x^{-l+r+\varrho-b-1}\int_{M}^{N}|\hat{E}(u)|u^{-1-b}du\right).

The last
O
-term can be removed by taking
b

Lemma 7

We follow the approach of Ivić
[

]

Suppose that

From the formula (see (A7) of Ivić

(8.43)

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle\ll Y^{1-2\sigma}V^{-2}(\log T)^{2}\sum_{t_{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}}\int_{-\log^{2}T}^{\log^{2}T}|\mathfrak{L}(1/2+it_{r}+iv)|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\ll Y^{1-2\sigma}V^{-2}(\log T)^{2}\int_{T-\log^{2}T}^{2T+\log^{2}T}|\mathfrak{L}(1/2+iv)|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\ll Y^{1-2\sigma}V^{-2}T^{1+\varepsilon},

where in the last inequality we used the mean square estimate (
8.29
) for
\mathfrak{L}(1/2+it)
of degree 2.
Hence as a first estimate we get, from (
8.42
) and (
8.43
),

(8.44)

R\ll T^{\varepsilon}\left(Y^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-2/(3-4\sigma)}+Y^{1-2\sigma}V^{-2}T\right).

We shall derive another estimate for
R_{2}
. It is convenient to consider the conditional case first, so we assume (
8.35
).
This time we use Hölder’s inequality in (
8.41
). Thus we have

\displaystyle V\ll Y^{1/2-\sigma}(\log T)^{5/3}\left(\int_{-\log^{2}T}^{\log^{2}T}|\mathfrak{L}(1/2+it_{r}+iv)|^{6}dv\right)^{1/6},

and

(8.45)

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle\ll Y^{3-6\sigma}V^{-6}(\log T)^{10}\sum_{t_{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}}\int_{-\log^{2}T}^{\log^{2}T}|\mathfrak{L}(1/2+it_{r}+iv)|^{6}dv

\displaystyle\ll Y^{3-6\sigma}V^{-6}(\log T)^{10}\int_{T-\log^{2}T}^{2T+\log^{2}T}|\mathfrak{L}(1/2+iv)|^{6}dv

\displaystyle\ll Y^{3-6\sigma}V^{-6}T^{2+\varepsilon}.

Hence (
8.42
) and (
8.45
) give the second estimate

(8.46)

R\ll T^{\varepsilon}\left(Y^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-2/(3-4\sigma)}+Y^{3-6\sigma}V^{-6}T^{2}\right).

If
V\leq T^{(3-4\sigma)/4}
, we apply (
8.44
) with
Y=T
. Then

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\ll(Y^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-\frac{2}{3-4\sigma}}+TY^{1-2\sigma}V^{-2})T^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll(T^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-\frac{2}{3-4\sigma}})T^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}V^{-\frac{2}{3-4\sigma}}

by recalling the condition
V\leq T^{(3-4\sigma)/4}.

Next if
V\geq T^{(3-4\sigma)/4}
, we apply (
8.46
) with
Y=T^{2/(4\sigma-1)}V^{-4/(4\sigma-1)}+1
and get

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\ll(Y^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-\frac{2}{3-4\sigma}}+T^{2}Y^{3-6\sigma}V^{-6})T^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll(TV^{-\frac{2}{3-4\sigma}}+T^{\frac{4-4\sigma}{4\sigma-1}}V^{-\frac{6}{4\sigma-1}})T^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}V^{-\frac{2}{3-4\sigma}}

if
T^{\frac{5-8\sigma}{4\sigma-1}}\leq V^{\frac{4(5-8\sigma)}{(4\sigma-1)(3-4\sigma)}},

which is true when
V\geq T^{(3-4\sigma)/4}
and
1/2<\sigma\leq 5/8.

Summing up, if
1/2<\sigma\leq 5/8
,

R\ll TV^{-2/(3-4\sigma)}

holds true for all
V
, completing the proof of (
8.36
).

Next we shall prove (
8.33
). We use the fourth-power mean value theorem:

(8.47)

\int_{0}^{T}|\mathfrak{L}(1/2+it)|^{4}dt\ll T^{2+\varepsilon},

which is known to be true unconditionally (see e.g. Kanemitsu, Sankaranarayanan and Tanigawa
[
29
]
).
By the similar way we have

(8.48)

R\ll T^{\varepsilon}\left(Y^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-2/(3-4\sigma)}+Y^{2-4\sigma}V^{-4}T^{2}\right)

from (
8.47
). If
V\leq T^{1-\sigma}
, we take
Y=T
in (
8.44
) and get

R\ll T^{\varepsilon}\left(T^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-2/(3-4\sigma)}\right)\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}V^{-1/(1-\sigma)}.

On the other hand if
V\geq T^{1-\sigma}
, we take
V=(TV^{-1})^{1/\sigma}
in (
8.48
) and get

R\ll T^{2-2\sigma)/\sigma}V^{-2/\sigma}+TV^{-2/(3-4\sigma)}\ll TV^{-1/(1-\sigma)}.

Therefore the estimate

R\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}V^{-1/(1-\sigma)}

holds true for all
V
for
1/2<\sigma<2/3
. This completes the proof of (
8.33
).

Now we prove (
8.30
) and (
8.31
).
Recall that
{\cal L}(s)={\cal L}_{1}(s){\cal L}_{2}(s)
, where
{\cal L}_{1}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}

is a function of degree
1
and
{\cal L}_{2}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
is a function of degree
2.

By the work of Kaczorowski and Perelli
[
27
]
, it is known that the functions
of degree one in the Selberg class are the Riemann zeta-function
\zeta(s)
and the shifts

L(s+i\vartheta,\chi)
of Dirichlet
L
-functions attached to primitive characters
\chi
with

\vartheta\in{\mathbb{R}}.

So we have

\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}_{1}(5/8+it)|^{8}dt\ll T\log^{4}T

and

\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}_{2}(5/8+it)|^{8/3}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

Hence by Hölder’s inequality and (
8.34
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}(5/8+it)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}_{1}(5/8+it){\cal L}_{2}(5/8+it)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll\left(\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}_{1}(5/8+it)|^{2\cdot 4}dt\right)^{1/4}\left(\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}_{2}(5/8+it)|^{2\cdot 4/3}dt\right)^{3/4}

\displaystyle\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

Assume that (
1.10
) holds. Then by

\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}_{1}(7/12+it)|^{6}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}

and

\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}_{2}(7/12+it)|^{3}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}

(this comes from (

\int_{0}^{T}|{\cal L}(7/12+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}

10
Some applications of our main results

In this section we consider some applications of our results.

10.1
Some functions of degree
2

Example 1:
The Dirichlet divisor problem.
The first example is
\zeta^{2}(s)\in{\cal S}^{0}_{real}.

Since

\zeta^{2}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)n^{-s}\qquad{\rm Re\,}s>1,

where
d(n)
is the divisor function, this is the Dirichlet divisor problem. This problem is fundamental for
all theory and hence has a long history. Let us review some of them.
Dirichlet first proved that the error term

\Delta(x):=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n\leq x}d(n)-x\log x-(2\gamma-1)x,\hskip 8.53581ptx\geq 2

satisfies

\Delta(x)=O(x^{1/2}).

The latest result is due to Huxley
[
17
]
, who showed that

\Delta(x)\ll x^{131/416}(\log x)^{26947/8320}.

Cramér
[
6
]
proved the classical mean-square result

\int_{1}^{T}\Delta^{2}(x)dx\;=\;\frac{(\zeta(3/2))^{4}}{6\pi^{2}\zeta(3)}T^{3/2}+F(T)

with
F(T)=O(T^{5/4+\varepsilon}).

The estimate
F(T)=O(T^{5/4+\varepsilon})
was improved to
O(T\log^{5}T)
by Tong
[
49
]
,
from which the present work originates. Tong’s result was improved to
O(T\log^{4}T)
by
Preissmann
[
40
]
, and recently to
O(T\log^{3}T\log\log T)
by
Lau and Tsang
[
34
]
. For the asymptotic formulae of higher power moments of
\Delta(x)

see, for example, the papers of Ivić and Sargos
[
24
]
,
Tsang
[
51
]
and Zhai
[
55
]
.
For a survey of the Dirichlet divisor problem, see for example, Krätzel
[
31
]
, Ivić
[
19
]
or Tsang
[
50
]
.

Corollary 1 gives
F(T)=O(T^{1+\varepsilon})
. Since our setting of the problem is of general nature, it is weaker than the
estimate of the form
F(T)\ll T\log^{c}T,
but is much stronger than Cramér’s result
F(T)=O(T^{5/4+\varepsilon}).

Example 2:
The automorphic
L
-function attached to holomorphic cusp forms over
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
.
Let
f
be a primitive holomorphic cusp form of weight
k\geq 1
for the full modular group

SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}).
(For the sake of simplicity we only consider the form for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.)
Let

(10.1)

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{(k-1)/2}e(nz),

where
e(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
, be its normalized Fourier expansion at the cusp
\infty
. Then the automorphic
L
-function

{\cal L}(f,s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{-s}

is an
L
-function of degree
2
satisfying the functional equation

(2\pi)^{-s}\Delta(s){\cal L}(f,s)=(-1)^{k/2}(2\pi)^{-(1-s)}\Delta(1-s){\cal L}(f,1-s)

with the gamma factor
\Delta(s)=\Gamma\left(s+\frac{k-1}{2}\right)
.
Deligne
[
7
]
proved that
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)
, so we have
{\cal L}(f,s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{0}
.
It is well-known that

A(y)=\sum_{n\leq y}\lambda_{f}(n)\ll y^{1/3}

and
(see Rankin
[
41
]
or Selberg
[
45
]
)

\sum_{n\leq y}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}=c_{f}y+O(y^{3/5})

for some positive constant
c_{f}.

Walfisz
[
53
]
proved that

\int_{1}^{T}A^{2}(y)dy=C_{A}T^{3/2}+O(T\log^{2}T)

holds for some positive constant
C_{A}>0
.
From Corollary 1 we have a weaker result

\int_{1}^{T}A^{2}(y)dy=C_{A}T^{3/2}+O(T^{1+\varepsilon}).

Example 3:
The automorphic
L
-function attached to Maass forms for
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
.

Let
\varphi
be a primitive Maass form for
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, which
is an eigenfunction of the Laplace operator with an eigenvalue

\lambda=1/4+r^{2},
where
r\in\mathbb{R}.
Write its Fourier expansion at infinity in the form

\varphi(z)=\sqrt{v}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\rho(n)K_{ir}(2\pi|n|v)e(nu)\quad(\ z=u+iv,u\in{\mathbb{R}},v>0),

where
K_{ir}
is the modified Bessel function of the third kind. The corresponding automorphic
L
-function is

{\cal L}(\varphi,s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho(n)n^{-s},

which is a function of degree
2
, entire on
{\mathbb{C}},
with the functional equation

\pi^{-s}\Delta(s){\cal L}(\varphi,s)=(-1)^{\delta}\pi^{-(1-s)}\Delta(1-s){\cal L}(\varphi,1-s),

where
\Delta(s)=\Gamma\left((s+\delta+ir)/2\right)\Gamma\left((s+\delta-ir)/2\right),

and
\delta
is the parity of
\varphi
defined by
\delta=0
if
\varphi
is even and
\delta=1
if
\varphi
is odd.
Kim and Sarnak
[
30
]
proved that

(10.2)

|\rho(n)|\leq d(n)n^{7/64}.

The Rankin-Selberg method implies that

(10.3)

\sum_{n\leq y}|\rho(n)|^{2}\ll y.

So we have
{\cal L}(\varphi,s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{\theta}
with
\theta=7/64.

Hafner and Ivić
[
14
]
proved that

A_{r}(y)=\sum_{n\leq y}\rho(n)\ll y^{2/5}.

Meurman
[
37
]
proved that

A_{r}(y)\ll y^{\frac{1+\mu}{3}+\varepsilon},

where
\mu\geq 0
is any real number such that
\rho(n)\ll n^{\mu}.

From Corollary 1 we get

\int_{1}^{T}A_{r}^{2}(y)dy=C_{A_{r}}T^{3/2}+O(T^{1+\varepsilon}),

where
C_{A_{r}}>0
is a positive constant.

3

Example 1:
Let
{\cal D}_{r}

-functions corresponding to real primitive Dirichlet

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{1f}=\{{\cal L}(f,s)L(s,\chi):\ L(s,\chi)\in{\cal D}_{r}\},

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{1\varphi}=\{{\cal L}(\varphi,s)L(s,\chi):\ L(s,\chi)\in{\cal D}_{r}\},

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{r}^{3}=\{L(s,\chi_{1})L(s,\chi_{2})L(s,\chi_{3}):\ L(s,\chi_{j})\in{\cal D}_{r},j=1,2,3\},

Whence (

) holds.

\int_{1}^{T}|{\cal L}(\varphi,1/2+it)|^{6}dt\ll T^{2+\varepsilon}.

we have

\int_{1}^{T}|{\cal L}(7/12+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon},

) holds for

Example 2:
Let
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
be a number field of degree

with discriminant

. The Dedekind zeta-function defined by

the number of pairs of

The Dedekind zeta-function
\zeta_{K}(s)
has a simple at

From now we suppose that
\kappa=3.
Let

E_{K}(y):=\sum_{n\leq y}a(n)-\lambda y,

where

denotes the number of ideals in
\mathfrak{L}
of norm

and
\lambda
is the residue
of
\zeta_{K}(s)

Weber and Landau showed that

In 1964, Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan

In 1999, Y.-K. Lau
[

]
improved the above result to

\int_{1}^{T}E_{K}^{2}(y)dy=O\left(T^{5/3}\log^{2}T\right).

Fomenko
[
10
]
followed Tong’s approach and proved that the asymptotic formula

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}E_{K}^{2}(y)dy=C_{K}T^{5/3}+O\left(T^{8/5+\varepsilon}\right)

holds for some positive constants
C_{K}>0
with the help of the estimate

(10.4)

\int_{1}^{T}|\zeta_{K}(\sigma+it)|^{2}\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}

with
\sigma=5/8.

When
\kappa=3
, we can show that the Dedekind zeta-function
\zeta_{K}(s)
can be written as

\zeta_{K}(s)=\zeta(s){\cal L}_{2}(s)
such that where
{\cal L}_{2}(s)\in{\cal S}_{real}^{0}
is a function of degree
2

satisfying the condition (
1.10
).
When
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
is a normal extension, from Lemma 1 of W. Müller
[
39
]

we know that

\zeta_{K}(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{1})L(s,\overline{\chi_{1}})
for some primitive Dirichlet character
\chi_{1}.

So we have
\zeta_{K}(s)=\zeta(s){\cal L}_{2}(s),
where

{\cal L}_{2}(s)=L(s,\chi_{1})L(s,\overline{\chi_{1}})
satisfies (
1.10
) via Meurman
[
38
]
.

When
K/{\mathbb{Q}}
is a not normal extension, from the formula (1) of Fomenko
[
10
]
we have

\zeta_{K}(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,F),
where

F
is a holomorphic primitive cusp form of weight
1
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(|d_{K}|)
and

L(s,F)
is its corresponding automorphic
L
-function of degree
2.
We have the estimate

\int_{1}^{T}|L(1/2+it,F)|^{6}dt\ll T^{2+\varepsilon}.

The proof is also due to Jutila
[
25
]
, who proved it in the case of a holomorphic cusp form

F
of even weight with respect to
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}).
His proof can be applied to
L(s,F),
too.

From Lemma
12
we see that (
10.4
) holds for
\sigma=7/12.
So
from Corollary 2 we get

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}E_{K}^{2}(y)dy=C_{K}T^{5/3}+O\left(T^{14/9+\varepsilon}\right),

which is a slight improvement to Fomenko’s result.

10.3
Some examples of degree
4

Example 1.

Let
{\cal D}_{r}
denote the set of all Dirichlet
L
-functions corresponding to real primitive Dirichlet
characters,
{\cal D}_{c}
denote the set of all Dirichlet
L
-functions corresponding to complex primitive Dirichlet
characters,

f
denote a primitive holomorphic cusp form and
\varphi
denote a primitive Maass form for
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})

for some fixed an eigenvalue
\lambda=1/4+r^{2}(r\in\mathbb{R}),
respectively. Define

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{2f}=\{{\cal L}(f,s)L(s,\chi_{1})L(s,\chi_{2}):\ L(s,\chi_{j})\in{\cal D}_{r},\quad j=1,2\},

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{2\varphi}=\{{\cal L}(\varphi,s)L(s,\chi_{1})L(s,\chi_{2}):\ L(s,\chi_{j})\in{\cal D}_{r},\quad j=1,2\},

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{r}^{4}=\{\prod_{j=1}^{4}L(s,\chi_{j}):\ L(s,\chi_{j})\in{\cal D}_{r},1\leq j\leq 4\},

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{r}^{2}{\cal D}_{c}\overline{{\cal D}_{c}}=\{L(s,\chi_{1})L(s,\chi_{2})L(s,\chi_{3})L(s,\overline{\chi_{3}}):\ L(s,\chi_{1}),L(s,\chi_{2})\in{\cal D}_{r},L(s,\chi_{3})\in{\cal D}_{c}\},

\displaystyle({\cal D}_{c}\overline{{\cal D}_{c}})^{2}=\{\prod_{j=1}^{2}L(s,\chi_{j})L(s,\overline{\chi_{j}}):\ L(s,\chi_{j})\in{\cal D}_{c},j=1,2\}.

Suppose
{\cal L}(s)
is any function in the set

{\cal D}_{2f}\cup{\cal D}_{2\varphi}\cup{\cal D}^{4}\cup{\cal D}_{r}^{2}{\cal D}_{c}\overline{{\cal D}_{c}}\cup({\cal D}_{c}\overline{{\cal D}_{c}})^{2}\cup\{{\cal L}^{2}(f,s),{\cal L}^{2}(\varphi,s),{\cal L}(f,s){\cal L}(\varphi,s)\}.

Obviously
{\cal L}(s)
is a function of degree
4.
From Lemma
12
it is easy to see that

(10.5)

\int_{1}^{T}|{\cal L}(5/8+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

For the mean square of
E(y)
corresponding to
{\cal L}(s)
we have

(10.6)

T^{7/4}\ll\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy\ll T^{7/4+\varepsilon}.

The left inequality in (
10.6
) follows from Theorem 5 directly. The proof of the right inequality in (
10.6
)
is the same as the case
k=4
in Theorem 13.4 of Ivić
[
19
]
with the help of (
10.5
).
So we omit the details. We note that the upper bound in (
10.6
) is already proved in Ivić
[
22
]

when
{\cal L}(s)={\cal L}^{2}(f,s).

If we could find a number
1/2\leq\sigma_{0}<5/8
such that

\int_{1}^{T}|{\cal L}(\sigma_{0}+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon},

then from Theorem 1 the asymptotic formula

(10.7)

\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy=C_{4}T^{7/4}+O(T^{7/4-\delta_{\cal L}})

would hold, where
C_{4}>0
and
\delta_{\cal L}>0
are positive constants. The formula (10.7) implies that the estimate
(10.6) is best possible.

Example 2:
The error term in the Rankin-Selberg problem.

Let
f
be a primitive holomorphic cusp form of weight
k
for the full modular group

SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
and its normalized Fourier expansion at the cusp
\infty
can be written as (
10.1
).
The corresponding Rankin-Selberg zeta function defined by

{\cal L}(f\otimes f,s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c(n)n^{-s},\ c(n)=\sum_{m^{2}|n}|\lambda_{f}(n/m^{2})|^{2},\ ({\rm Re\,}s>1)

is a function of degree
4
satisfying the functional equation

(2\pi)^{-2s}\Delta(s){\cal L}(f\otimes f,s)=(2\pi)^{-2(1-s)}\Delta(1-s){\cal L}(f\otimes f,1-s)

with the gamma factor
\Delta(s)=\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+k-1)
.

Rankin
[
41
]
and Selberg
[
45
]
proved independently that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq y}c(n)=C_{f}y+E(y),\ E(y)=O(y^{3/5}),

where
C_{f}>0
is a positive constant. From Deligne’s estimate
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)
we have

(10.8)

c(n)\leq d^{2}(n)d(1,2;n)\leq d^{3}(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon},

where
d(1,2;n)=\sum_{n=m^{2}d}1\leq d(n).
We write

c(n)=\sum_{n=m^{2}d}|\lambda_{f}(d)|^{2}.
By Cauchy’s inequality we have

c^{2}(n)\leq d(1,2;n)\sum_{n=m^{2}d}|\lambda_{f}(d)|^{4}\ll n^{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=m^{2}d}|\lambda_{f}(d)|^{4},

where we used
d(1,2;n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}.
So we have

(10.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq y}c^{2}(n)

\displaystyle\ll y^{\varepsilon}\sum_{m^{2}d\leq y}|\lambda_{f}(d)|^{4}

\displaystyle\ll y^{\varepsilon}\sum_{m\leq y^{1/2}}\sum_{d\leq y/m^{2}}|\lambda_{f}(d)|^{4}

\displaystyle\ll y^{\varepsilon}\sum_{m\leq y^{1/2}}y/m^{2}\ll y^{1+\varepsilon},

where we used the estimate in Lü
[
35
]

\sum_{n\leq y}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{4}\ll y.

The estimates (
10.8
) and (
10.9
) show that (
1.4
) holds.

Ivić
[
21
]
proved that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy\ll T^{1+2\beta+\varepsilon},

where
\beta=2/(5-2\mu(1/2))
with
\mu(1/2)
satisfying
\zeta(1/2+it)\ll(|t|+1)^{\mu(1/2)+\varepsilon}.
Huxley’s best bound
[
18
]

\mu(1/2)\leq 32/205
implies
\beta\leq 410/961=0.4266\cdots.
The Lindelöf Hypothesis implies
\beta\leq 0.4.

In
[
20
]
Ivić even conjectured

(10.10)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy\ll T^{\frac{7}{4}+\varepsilon}.

The estimate (
10.10
), if it is true, is best possible. Since from Theorem 5 we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy\gg T^{\frac{7}{4}}.

If we could find a
\sigma_{0}<\frac{5}{8}
such that

\int_{1}^{T}|{\cal L}(f\otimes f,\sigma_{0}+it)|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon},

then from Theorem 1 we would have the asymptotic formula

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}E^{2}(y)dy=C_{f}T^{\frac{7}{4}}+O\left(T^{\frac{7}{4}-\delta_{f}}\right)

for some positive constants
C_{f}>0,\delta_{f}>0.

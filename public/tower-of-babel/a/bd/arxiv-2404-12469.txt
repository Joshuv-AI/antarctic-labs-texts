Uncertainty for convolutions of sets
I.D. Shkredov

Abstract




Annotation.

The paper obtains the optimal form of the uncertainty principle in the special case of convolution of sets.

1
Introduction

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an
abelian group and
\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}
its dual group.
For any function
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\xi\in\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}
define the Fourier transform of
f
at
\xi
by the formula

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{g\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(g)\overline{\xi(g)}\,.

(1)

A
number
of uncertainty principles on
{\mathbf{G}}
assert, roughly, that a function on
{\mathbf{G}}
and its Fourier transform cannot be simultaneously highly concentrated.
This topic is quite popular
see, e.g., papers
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,

[
10
]
.
The most basic example is an inequality which connects the sizes of the supports of
f
and
\widehat{f}
to the size of the finite group
{\mathbf{G}}
, namely,

|\mathsf{supp}f|\cdot|\mathsf{supp}\widehat{f}|\geqslant|{\mathbf{G}}|\,,

(2)

and, of course, it is worth mentioning the classical Heisenberg inequality for
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{R}
, which states that for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x-a)^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi-b)^{2}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\geqslant\frac{\|f\|^{4}_{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\,.

(3)

For more background on the Fourier transform on abelian groups and the uncertainty principle we refer to
[
11
]
and the excellent surveys

[
3
]
and
[
12
]
.

This paper considers the case when the function
f
has a special form, namely,
f
is the convolution of the characteristic function of a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
(all required definitions can be found in Section
2
).
This allows us to obtain a new optimal form of the uncertainty principle in terms of the difference set
A-A
(we also consider the asymmetric case, see Theorem
19
below).
Given two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
, define
the
sumset

of
A
and
B
as

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

In a similar way we define the
difference sets
and the
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
2A-A
is
A+A-A
.
Consider two quantities

\rho(A):=\max_{x\neq 0}|A\cap(A+x)|\,,

(4)

and

M(A):=\max_{\xi\neq 0}|\widehat{A}(\xi)|\,,

(5)

where we use the same capital letter to denote a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and its characteristic function
A:{\mathbf{G}}\to\{0,1\}
.

Theorem 1

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|A|=\delta|{\mathbf{G}}|
, and let
|A-A|=K|A|
.
Suppose that
|A|\to\infty
,
\log^{2}K=o(\log|A|)
and

K^{2}\delta=o(1)\,.

(6)

Then

M^{2}(A)\rho(A)\geqslant\frac{|A|^{3}}{K}\cdot(1-o(1))\,.

(7)

In other words, if
f
is the convolution of the characteristic function of a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
, then

\max_{x\neq 0}|f(x)|\cdot\max_{\xi\neq 0}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\geqslant\frac{|A|^{3}}{K}\cdot(1-o(1))\,.

(8)

It is easy to see that bound (
7
) is tight (also, see Remark
6
below).
Indeed, take
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, let
H
be a proper subspace of
{\mathbf{G}}
and let
A\subseteq H
be a random set of positive density. Then with high probability

K|A|:=|A-A|=|H|(1-o(1)),\,\quad\quad\rho(A)\leqslant\frac{|A|}{K}\cdot(1-o(1))

and
\mathsf{E}(A,H)=|A|^{2}|H|
.
The last
identity
implies
M(A)=|A|(1-o(1))

and the same
asymptotic formula
follows from our bound (
7
).
As for condition (
6
), we definitely need that
K\delta=o(1)
(consider the case of a random set
A
).
Finally, note that in our proof we make extensive use of the form of the function
f
(for example, we widely use of the concept of higher sumsets/higher energies, see
[
8
]
)
and it seems like any reasonable analogue of (
8
) for general
f

fails,
see
Remark
5
below.

We thank Vsevolod F. Lev for useful discussions and valuable comments.

2
Definitions and notation

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group and we denote the cardinality of
{\mathbf{G}}
by
N
.
Given a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and a positive integer
k
, we put

\Delta_{k}(A):=\{(a,a,\dots,a)~:~a\in A\}\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}^{k}\,.

Also, let

\Delta_{k}(x):=\Delta_{k}(\{x\})
,
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
.
Now we have

A-A:=\{a-b~:~a,b\in A\}=\{s\in{\mathbf{G}}~:~A\cap(A+s)\neq\emptyset\}\,.

(9)

A natural generalization of the last formula in (
9
)
is the set

\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in{\mathbf{G}}^{k}~:~A\cap(A+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(A+x_{k})\neq\emptyset\}=A^{k}-\Delta_{k}(A)\,,

(10)

which is called the
higher difference set
(see
[
8
]
).
Given a positive integer
k
, we put as in
[
6
]

R^{(k+1)}_{A}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=|A\cap(A+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(A+x_{k})|\,,\quad\quad x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in{\mathbf{G}}\,.

Thus,
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in A^{k}-\Delta_{k}(A)
iff
R^{(k+1)}_{A}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})>0
.
For any two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
the
additive energy
of
A
and
B
is defined by

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A\times A\times B\times B~:~a_{1}-b_{1}=a_{2}-b_{2}\}|\,.

If
A=B
, then we simply write
\mathsf{E}(A)
for
\mathsf{E}(A,A)
.
Similarly,

\mathsf{T}_{k}(A):=|\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{k})\in A^{2k}~:~a_{1}+\dots+a_{k}=a^{\prime}_{1}+\dots+a^{\prime}_{k}\}|\,.

(11)

Also, one can define the
higher energy
(see
[
8
]
)

\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)=\sum_{x}(A\circ A)^{k}(x)=\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1}}R^{(k)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1})^{2}:=\mathsf{E}_{k,2}(A)\,.

(12)

It is known and easy to check (see, e.g.,
[
6
, proof of Theorem 4]
) that
\mathsf{E}_{k,l}(A)=\mathsf{E}_{l,k}(A)
for any integers
k,l>1
.
Note that the first formula in (
12
) can be thought of as the definition of
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)
for any real
k>1
.

Let
\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}
be its dual group.
For any function
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\xi\in\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}
we define its Fourier transform using the formula (
1
).
The Parseval identity is

N\sum_{g\in{\mathbf{G}}}|f(g)|^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}}\big|\widehat{f}(\xi)\big|^{2}\,.

(13)

If
f,g:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
are some functions, then

(f*g)(x):=\sum_{y\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(y)g(x-y)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad(f\circ g)(x):=\sum_{y\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(y)g(y+x)\,.

One has

\widehat{f*g}=\widehat{f}\widehat{g}\,.

(14)

Having a function
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
and a positive integer
k>1
, we write
f^{(k)}(x)=(f\circ f\circ\dots\circ f)(x)
, where the convolution
\circ
is taken
k-1
times.
For example,
A^{(4)}(0)=\mathsf{E}(A)
.
Also, let
\mathsf{T}_{k}(f)=\sum_{x}(f^{(k)}(x))^{2}
.

We need a particular case of Corollary 1 from
[
9
]
.

Lemma 2

Let
k\geqslant 2
be an even integer,
l\geqslant 2
be any integer and
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set.
Then

\sum_{x}(A^{(k)}(x))^{l}=\mathsf{T}_{k/2}(R^{(l)}_{A})\,.

(15)

Also, we need
the generalized triangle inequality
[
8
, Theorem 7]
.

Lemma 3

Let
k_{1},k_{2}
be positive integers,
W\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}^{k_{1}}
,
Y\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}^{k_{2}}
and
X,Z\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
.
Then

|W\times X||Y-\Delta_{k_{2}}(Z)|\leqslant|W\times Y\times Z-\Delta_{k_{1}+k_{2}+1}(X)|\,.

(16)

We have defined the quantity
\rho(A)
and
M(A)
in formulae (
4
), (
5
) above.
More generally,
consider

\rho^{(k)}(A):=\max_{x\neq 0}A^{(k)}(x)\,.

Thus
A^{(2)}(x)=(A\circ A)(x)=|A\cap(A+x)|
.
Similarly to formula (
4
) we put for any positive integer
l>1

\rho_{l}(A):=\max_{|X|=l}|A_{X}|\,,

where
A_{X}:=(A+x_{1})\cap(A+x_{2})\cap\dots\cap(A+x_{l})
and
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{l}\}
is a set of the cardinality
l
. Thus
\rho_{2}(A)=\rho(A)
.
If
X=\{0,x\}
, where
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
, then we write
A_{\{0,x\}}=A_{x}=A\cap(A+x)
.
The inclusion of Katz–Koester
[
4
]
is

B+A_{x}\subseteq(A+B)_{x}\,.

(17)

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
All logarithms are to base
e
.

3
The proof of the main result

We are ready to obtain our first main result, which implies Theorem
7
from the introduction.

Theorem 4

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group,
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|A|=\delta N
,
\delta\in(0,1]
, further
|B|=\zeta|A|
,
|A-A|=K|A|
,
|B-B|=K_{*}|B|
, and let
|A+B|=\omega|A-A|
, where
\zeta,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Suppose that
|A|\geqslant(2K\cdot\max\{1,\omega\})^{8}
.
Then

M^{2}(B)\rho(A)\geqslant\frac{|A|^{2}|B|^{2}}{|A+B|}\cdot\left(1-\frac{6\log K\cdot\log(\zeta KK^{*})}{\log|A|}-(\omega K)^{2}\delta\right)\,,

(18)

and, similarly,

M(A-A)\rho^{2}(A)\geqslant\frac{|A|^{3}}{K}\cdot\left(1-\frac{16\log^{2}(2K)}{\log|A|}-K^{3}\delta\right)\,.

(19)

P r o o f.

Let
\rho=\rho(A)
,
M=M(B)
,
D=A-A
,
S=A+B
,
|D|=K|A|
,
|S|=\omega|D|
, and
\kappa:=K\delta\omega\leqslant 1
.
By the usual triangle inequality for sets (take
k_{1}=k_{2}=1
in Lemma
16
), we have
|A||B-B|\leqslant|A+B|^{2}
and hence

\zeta K_{*}\leqslant\omega^{2}K^{2}\,.

(20)

Also, let
n\geqslant 2
be a positive integer and put

|A^{n-1}-\Delta_{n-1}(A)|=\varepsilon_{n-1}|D|^{n-1}\,,

(21)

where

K^{-n}\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}\leqslant 1\,.

(22)

Then by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the second equality in
(
12
) one has

|A|^{2n}\leqslant|A^{n-1}-\Delta_{n-1}(A)|\mathsf{E}_{n}(A)\leqslant\varepsilon_{n-1}|D|^{n-1}(\rho^{n-1}|A|^{2}+|A|^{n})

(23)

=\varepsilon_{n-1}|D|^{n-1}\rho^{n-1}|A|^{2}+\varepsilon_{n-1}K^{n-1}|A|^{2n-1}\,.

(24)

Thus, we get

\rho\geqslant\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\cdot\frac{|A|}{K}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon_{n-1}K^{n-1}}{|A|}\right)^{1/(n-1)}>\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\cdot\frac{|A|}{K}\left(1-\frac{2\varepsilon_{n-1}K^{n-1}}{(n-1)|A|}\right)\,,

(25)

provided
n\leqslant 2^{-1}\log_{K}|A|
.
Now using Lemma
16
, we obtain for an arbitrary set
M\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and any positive integer
m

\varepsilon_{m-1}|M||D|^{m-1}=|M||A^{m-1}-\Delta_{m-1}(A)|\leqslant|A^{m}-\Delta_{m}(M)|\leqslant|A-M|^{m}\,,

and thus replacing
M
to
(-M)
, we derive

|A+M|\geqslant\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}\cdot\left(\frac{|M|}{|D|}\right)^{1/m}|D|\,.

(26)

Finally, we repeat the argument from
[
5
]
.
Using (
26
), the inclusion (
17
) and the Hölder inequality, one
obtains

\sigma:=\sum_{x}|B_{x}||S_{x}|\geqslant\sum_{x}|B_{x}||A+B_{x}|\geqslant\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}|D|^{1-1/m}\sum_{x}|B_{x}|^{1+1/m}

\geqslant\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}K|A||B|^{2}(\zeta KK_{*})^{-1/m}\,.

(27)

Applying the Fourier transform, formula (
14
) and the Parseval identity (
13
), we get

\sigma=N^{-1}\sum_{\xi}|\widehat{B}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{S}(\xi)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{\omega^{2}K^{2}|B|^{2}|A|^{2}}{N}+M^{2}\cdot(\omega K|A|-\omega^{2}K^{2}|A|^{2}/N)

<\omega\kappa K|A||B|^{2}+\omega KM^{2}|A|\,.

(28)

Combining the last two bounds, we
have

\omega M^{2}>\left(\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}(\zeta KK_{*})^{-1/m}-\omega\kappa\right)|B|^{2}\,.

(29)

Taking
m=n=[2^{-1}\log_{K}|A|]\geqslant 2
and multiplying (
25
), (
29
), we obtain in view of estimates (
20
), (
22
) that

\omega M^{2}\rho\geqslant\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{K}\cdot\left(1-\frac{2\varepsilon_{n-1}K^{n-1}}{(n-1)|A|}\right)\left(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n(n-1)}}_{n-1}(\zeta KK_{*})^{-1/n}-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\omega\kappa\right)

>\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{K}\cdot\left(1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{|A|}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\log(\zeta KK^{*})}{n}-K\kappa\omega\right)

\geqslant\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{K}\cdot\left(1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{|A|}}\right)\left(1-\frac{4\log K\cdot\log(\zeta KK^{*})}{\log|A|}-K\kappa\omega\right)

>\frac{|A||B|^{2}}{K}\cdot\left(1-\frac{6\log K\cdot\log(\zeta KK^{*})}{\log|A|}-K\kappa\omega\right)

(30)

as required.

It remains to obtain (
19
).
In this case we use calculations similar to (
27
) (now
B=A
) and consider

\sigma_{*}=\sum_{x\in D}|D_{x}|\geqslant\sum_{x\in D}|A-A_{x}|\geqslant\varepsilon_{m-1}^{1/m}|D|^{1-1/m}\sum_{x\in D}|A_{x}|^{1/m}

>\varepsilon_{m-1}^{1/m}K\rho^{-1}|A|^{3}(|D|/\rho)^{-1/m}-\varepsilon_{m-1}^{1/m}K\rho^{-1}|A|^{2}(|D|/\rho)^{-1/m}\,.

As in (
28
) putting
\kappa_{*}=\delta K^{2}\rho|A|^{-1}\leqslant\delta K^{2}
, we get

\sigma_{*}=N^{-1}\sum_{\xi}\widehat{D}(\xi)|\widehat{D}(\xi)|^{2}\leqslant\frac{K^{3}|A|^{3}}{N}+M(D)\cdot(K|A|-K^{2}|A|^{2}/N)

<\kappa_{*}K|A|^{3}\rho^{-1}+KM(D)|A|\,.

Combining the last two bounds, we derive

M(D)\rho>\left(\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}(|D|/\rho)^{-1/m}(1-|A|^{-1})-\kappa_{*}\right)|A|^{2}\geqslant\left(\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}(2K)^{-2/m}(1-|A|^{-1})-\kappa_{*}\right)|A|^{2}\,.

Here we have used
the simple bound

\rho\geqslant|A|/(2K)
.
Taking
m=n=[2^{-1}\log_{K}|A|]\geqslant 2
and multiplying the last estimate by (
25
), we obtain the desired bound (
19
).
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Let us discuss obtained inequalities (
7
), (
18
).
First of all,
one can check
that the both bounds
are tight, see the construction after
formula
(
8
).
Secondly, it is easy to make sure that
our uncertainty principle does not hold for an arbitrary function.

Remark 5

Inequalities (
7
), (
18
) have no place for any function
f
, namely, the bounds

\|\widehat{f}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|f\|_{\infty}\cdot|\mathsf{supp}f|\geqslant\|f\|_{1}^{2}\,,

(31)

\max_{\xi\neq 0}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\cdot\max_{x\neq 0}|f(x)|\cdot|\mathsf{supp}f|\geqslant\|f\|_{1}^{2}\,,

(32)

fail.
Indeed, let
p
be a prime number,
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, and
f(x)=A(x)-|A|/p
be the balanced function of a random set
A
of size
|A|>p^{2/3+\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon>0
(one can even take the set of quadratic residues to get a constructive example).
Then the right–hand side of (
31
), (
32
) is
|A|^{2}
but the left–hand side of (
31
), (
32
) is
O(\sqrt{|A|}p^{1+o(1)})=o(|A|^{2})
.

Remark 6

It is easy to see that
the argument of the proof of Theorem
19
gives us

\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)\mathsf{E}^{k}(A,D)\geqslant\frac{|A|^{4k+1}}{K}\,,

(33)

and the last bound is tight up to constants.
Indeed, let
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and let
A=H+\Lambda
,
|A|=|H||\Lambda|
, where
H\leqslant{\mathbf{G}}
and
|\Lambda|=K
be a basis (for
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathbb{Z}
a similar construction can be found in
[
6
, Claim 1]
). Then for
x\in H
one has
|A_{x}|=|A|
and for
x\in D\setminus H
the following holds
|A_{x}|=2|A|/K
.
Thus
\mathsf{E}(A,D)=O(|A|^{3})
,
\mathsf{E}_{k}(A)=\frac{|A|^{k+1}}{K}(1+o_{K}(1))
and (
33
) is sharp (up to constants).

The example above shows (also, see
[
6
, Claim 1]
) that set of large
A_{x}
has measure zero. Similarly,
\widehat{A}(\xi)=|H|H^{\perp}(\xi)\widehat{\Lambda}(\xi)
and thus the set of large Fourier coefficients of
A
has measure zero as well.
Therefore, inequality (
18
) is rather delicate and has place on a set of measure zero.

Using the arguments similar to
[
6
]
(also, see
[
8
]
) we obtain an analogue of Theorem
19
for the quantity
\rho_{l}(A)
.
Here and below, for simplicity, we consider the symmetric case
A=B
.

Theorem 7

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|A|=\delta N
,
l>1
be a positive integer,
and let
|A-A|=K|A|
.
Suppose that
|A|\geqslant K^{8(l-1)}
, and
|A|>4l^{4}
.
Then

M^{2(l-1)}(A)\rho_{l}(A)\geqslant\frac{|A|^{2l-1}}{K^{l-1}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{2l^{2}}{\sqrt{|A|}}\right)\left(1-\frac{12(l-1)\log^{2}K}{\log|A|}-K^{2}\delta\right)^{l-1}\,.

(34)

P r o o f.

We use the notation and the argument of the proof of Theorem
19
.
Let
\rho_{l}=\rho_{l}(A)
.
Applying
the Hölder inequality, we obtain an analogue of calculations in (
23
), (
24
) (see
[
6
, proof of Theorem 4]
)

|A|^{ln}=\left(\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}}R^{(n)}_{A}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\right)^{l}\leqslant|A^{n-1}-\Delta_{n-1}(A)|^{l-1}\mathsf{E}_{l,n}(A)

\leqslant\varepsilon^{l-1}_{n-1}|D|^{(l-1)(n-1)}\mathsf{E}_{n,l}(A)\leqslant\varepsilon^{l-1}_{n-1}|D|^{(l-1)(n-1)}\left(\rho^{n-1}_{l}|A|^{l}+l^{2}|A|^{n+l-2}\right)

and hence

\rho_{l}\geqslant\varepsilon^{-\frac{l-1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\cdot\frac{|A|}{K^{l-1}}\left(1-\frac{l^{2}\varepsilon^{l-1}_{n-1}K^{(l-1)(n-1)}}{|A|}\right)^{1/(n-1)}\geqslant\varepsilon^{-\frac{l-1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\cdot\frac{|A|}{K^{l-1}}\left(1-\frac{2l^{2}\varepsilon^{l-1}_{n-1}K^{(l-1)(n-1)}}{(n-1)|A|}\right)\,,

provided
(l-1)n\leqslant 2^{-1}\log_{K}|A|
.
After that we repeat the proof of Theorem
19
, namely, we put
m=n=[(2(l-1))^{-1}\log_{K}|A|]\geqslant 2

and obtain

M^{2(l-1)}\rho_{l}\geqslant\frac{|A|^{2l-1}}{K^{l-1}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{2l^{2}\varepsilon^{l-1}_{n-1}K^{(l-1)(n-1)}}{(n-1)|A|}\right)\left(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n(n-1)}}_{n-1}K^{-2/n}-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\kappa\right)^{l-1}

\geqslant\frac{|A|^{2l-1}}{K^{l-1}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{2l^{2}}{\sqrt{|A|}}\right)\left(1-\frac{12(l-1)\log^{2}K}{\log|A|}-K\kappa\right)^{l-1}\,,

where we have used inequality (
29
).
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

4
Further generalizations

Now we obtain an analogue of Theorem
19
for the quantity
\rho^{(k)}(A)
,
k>2
.
First of all, we give a trivial lower bound for
\rho^{(k)}(A)
.
Let
k=2s\geqslant 4
for simplicity.
Using
the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality,
one has

\frac{|A|^{k+2}}{|sA\pm A|}\leqslant\mathsf{T}_{s+1}(A)=\sum_{x}A^{(k)}(x)A^{(2)}(x)\leqslant\mathsf{T}_{s}(A)|A|+\rho^{(k)}(A)|A|^{2}\,.

(35)

Hence, if we ignore the first term in (
35
) (it is easy to see that it is negligible for small
|sA\pm A|
),
then we derive

\rho^{(k)}(A)\geqslant\frac{|A|^{k}}{|sA\pm A|}\cdot(1-o(1))

(36)

and this
estimate
can be considered as a trivial lower bound for the quantity
\rho^{(k)}(A)
.

Theorem 8

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and let
k=2s\geqslant 2
be a positive integer.
Also, let

|A|=\delta N
,

|sA\pm A|=K|A|
and
|sA-sA|=K_{*}|A|
.
Suppose that

|A|^{nk-n+1}\geqslant K^{8}_{*}\mathsf{T}^{n}_{s}(A)\,,

and

|A|>4
.
Then

M^{2}(A)\rho^{(k)}(A)\geqslant\frac{|A|^{k+1}}{K}\cdot\left(1-\frac{14\log^{2}K_{*}}{\log|A|}-\delta KK_{*}\right)\,.

(37)

P r o o f.

We use
the argument of the proof of Theorem
19
.
Let

\rho=\rho^{(k)}(A)
,
D=A-A
,
|sD|=K_{*}|A|
and put

|s(A^{n-1}-\Delta_{n-1}(A))|=|(sA)^{n-1}-\Delta_{n-1}(sA)|:=\varepsilon_{n-1}|sD|^{n-1}\,,

where

\frac{1}{K^{n}_{*}}\leqslant\frac{|sA|^{n}}{|sD|^{n}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}\leqslant 1\,.

Then by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Lemma
15
one has

|A|^{nk}\leqslant|(sA)^{n-1}-\Delta_{n-1}(sA)|\mathsf{T}_{s}(R^{(n)}_{A})=\varepsilon_{n-1}|sD|^{n-1}\cdot\sum_{x}(A^{(k)}(x))^{n}

(38)

\leqslant\varepsilon_{n-1}|sD|^{n-1}(\rho^{n-1}|A|^{k}+\mathsf{T}^{n}_{s}(A))\,,

(39)

and hence

\rho\geqslant\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\cdot\frac{|A|^{k-1}}{K_{*}}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon_{n-1}K^{n-1}_{*}\mathsf{T}^{n}_{s}(A)}{|A|^{nk-n+1}}\right)^{1/(n-1)}\geqslant\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}_{n-1}\cdot\frac{|A|^{k-1}}{K_{*}}\left(1-\frac{2\varepsilon_{n-1}K^{n-1}_{*}\mathsf{T}^{n}_{s}(A)}{(n-1)|A|^{nk-n+1}}\right)\,,

provided
n\leqslant 2^{-1}\log_{K_{*}}(|A|^{nk-n+1}\mathsf{T}^{-n}_{s}(A))
.
Now let
Q=sA\pm A
,
|Q|=K|A|
.
Then the inclusion of Katz–Koester (
17
) gives us

sA\pm A_{x}\subseteq Q_{\pm x}
.
Hence as in the proof of Theorem
19
, we get

\sigma:=\sum_{x}|A_{x}||Q_{x}|\geqslant\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}|sD|^{1-1/m}\sum_{x}|A_{x}|^{1+1/m}\geqslant\varepsilon^{1/m}_{m-1}K_{*}|A|^{3}K_{*}^{-2/m}\,.

On the other hand, there is the upper bound (
28
) for
\sigma
.
Putting

m=n=[2^{-1}\log_{K_{*}}(|A|^{nk-n+1}\mathsf{T}^{-n}_{s}(A))]\geqslant 2

we can repeat the
argument
of the proof of Theorem
19
.

Theorem 10

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,

|A|=\delta N
, and
\mathsf{E}(A)=|A|^{3}/K
.
Suppose that

|A|\geqslant 8K^{3}
,
and

\delta^{3}L^{28}K^{25}\ll 1\,.

(41)

Then

\rho^{7}(A)M^{4}(A)\log^{7}|A|\gg\frac{|A|^{11}}{K^{7}}\,.

(42)

P r o o f.

Let
L=\log|A|
,
\rho=\rho(A)
and
M=M(A)
.
Also, let
\Delta>0
be a real number and
P=\{s~:~\Delta<|A_{s}|\leqslant 2\Delta\}
be a set such that
\Delta^{10/7}|P|\gg\mathsf{E}_{10/7}(A)/L
.
It is easy to see that the number
\Delta
and the set
P
exist by the pigeonhole principle.
Clearly, we have

\mathsf{E}(A)\rho^{-4/7}L^{-1}\ll\mathsf{E}_{10/7}(A)L^{-1}\ll\Delta^{10/7}|P|\ll|A|^{2}\Delta^{3/7}\,,

and thus

\Delta\gg\frac{|A|^{7/3}}{(KL)^{7/3}\rho^{4/3}}\,.

(43)

Computing the eigenvalues of the operator
M(x,y):=P(x-y)A(x)A(y)
(see, e.g.,
[
8
, Section 8]
or
[
9
, Theorem 6.3]
), we have

(\Delta|P|)^{8}\ll|A|^{8}\mathsf{E}_{4}(A)\mathsf{E}(P)

and hence using the simple bound
\rho\geqslant|A|/(2K)
as well as the assumption
|A|\geqslant 8K^{3}
, we obtain in view of (
13
), (
14
)

\Delta^{10}|P|^{8}\ll|A|^{8}\mathsf{E}_{4}(A)\sum_{x}P^{(2)}(x)A^{(4)}(x)\leqslant|A|^{8}\left(|A|^{4}+\rho^{3}|A|^{2}\right)\cdot N^{-1}\sum_{\xi}|\widehat{P}(\xi)|^{2}|\widehat{A}(\xi)|^{4}

\ll\rho^{3}|A|^{10}\cdot\left(N^{-1}|P|^{2}|A|^{4}+M^{4}|P|\right)

(44)

If the second term in (
44
) dominates, then thanks to our choice of the set
P
,
we get

\mathsf{E}^{7}(A)\rho^{-4}L^{-7}\ll\mathsf{E}^{7}_{10/7}(A)L^{-7}\ll\Delta^{10}|P|^{7}\ll|A|^{10}\rho^{3}M^{4}

and (
42
) follows.
If the first term in (
44
) is the largest one, then we derive

\Delta^{10}|P|^{6}\ll\delta\mathsf{E}_{4}(A)|A|^{11}\ll\delta|A|^{11}\rho^{18/7}\mathsf{E}_{10/7}(A)\,,

and therefore in view of (
43
)

\left(\frac{|A|^{7/3}}{(KL)^{7/3}\rho^{4/3}}\right)^{10/7}\mathsf{E}^{5}(A)\rho^{-20/7}\ll\Delta^{10/7}\mathsf{E}^{5}_{10/7}(A)\ll\delta|A|^{11}\rho^{18/7}L^{6}\,.

It follows that

\delta^{3}L^{28}K^{25}\gg 1

and this is a contradiction with (
41
).
This completes the proof.

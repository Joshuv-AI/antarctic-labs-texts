Convolutional double copy in (Anti) de Sitter space
Qiuyue Liang, Silvia Nagy

Abstract
The double copy is a remarkable relationship between gauge theory and gravity that has been explored in a number of contexts, most notably scattering amplitudes and classical solutions. The convolutional double copy provides a straightforward method to bridge the two theories via a precise map for the fields and symmetries at the linearised level. This method has been thoroughly investigated in flat space, offering a comprehensive dictionary both with and without fixing the gauge degrees of freedom. In this paper, we extend this to curved space with an (anti) de Sitter background metric. We work in the temporal gauge, and employ a modified convolution that involves the Mellin transformation in the time direction. As an example, we show that the point-like charge in gauge theory double copies to the (dS-) Schwarzschild black hole solution.

To achieve this, we need a convolution in (A)dS space. Recall that the convolution on a flat background

[f\star g](x)=\int d^{4}yf(0+x)g(y-x)\ ,

II
BRST convolution in flat space with a general gauge-fixing functional

We begin with a review of the main properties of the flat space convolutional double copy. This has been previously constructed in the BRST context with a Lorenz gauge fixing functional. Here we will extend this to more general gauge choices, and in particular we will allow ourselves to break Lorentz covariance.

A crucial aspect of the convolutional product is that it does not respect the Leibniz rule:

\partial_{\mu}(f\star g)=(\partial_{\mu}f)\star g=f\star(\partial_{\mu}g)\ .

(5)

This will be important in deriving the gravitational symmetries via the double copy, and will be another crucial consideration in the extension to curved backgrounds.

We also introduce the
\circ
product, defined as

A_{\mu}\circ A_{\nu}\equiv A_{\mu}^{a}\star\left(\Phi^{-1}\right)^{aa^{\prime}}\star A_{\nu}^{a^{\prime}}\ ,

(6)

where we have taken the convolution inverse of the biadjoint scalar field
\Phi^{aa^{\prime}}
. This is the appropriate building block for the double copy, as it connects with the amplitudes formulation, as further detailed in
Anastasiou et al. 2018
;
Borsten et al. 2020
;
Luna et al. 2020
. It also gives the correct mass dimension, upon normalising all physical fields to have dimension 1. This is easiest seen by going to momentum space, where the convolution becomes a product.

We will be working in the BRST formalism, with the YM BRST complex

(A_{\mu},c,\bar{c})
, where
c
and
\bar{c}
denote the ghost and anti-ghost, respectively. The Lagrangian, upon integrating out the Lautrup-Nakanishi Lagrange multiplier field, can be written as

\mathcal{L}=\text{Tr}\left(-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2\xi}\left(n^{\mu}A_{\mu}\right)^{2}-\bar{c}\,n^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}c-j_{\mu}A^{\mu}-\bar{c}j_{\bar{c}}-cj_{c}\right)\ .

(7)

Note that we have been agnostic in our choice of the BRST gauge-fixing functional:

G[A_{\mu}]=n^{\mu}A_{\mu}\ ,

(8)

where
n^{\mu}
denotes either a differential operator or a (Lorentz-breaking) constant vector. For the Lorenz gauge fixing functional, we will have
n^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}
, whereas the axial gauge or temporal gauge will be denoted by a constant vector
n^{\mu}
. We also note that the Lagrangian is a slightly modified version of the standard BRST one, as it includes sources for the ghosts. These were shown to be important in disentangling the degrees of freedom of the gravitational fields resulting from the double copy
Anastasiou et al. 2018
;
Borsten et al. 2020
;
Luna et al. 2020
.

The Lagrangian (
7

\mathcal{Q}A_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}c,\quad\mathcal{Q}c=0,\quad\mathcal{Q}\bar{c}=\frac{1}{\xi}n^{\mu}A_{\mu}\ ,

V
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we have shown that the convolutional double copy can be extended to (anti) de Sitter backgrounds. We found that arbitrary gravitational fluctuations in temporal gauge, as well as their residual symmeteries, can be constructed from counterparts in the YM theory. As an example, we demonstrated how the Schwarzschild solution in a dS background follows from an electromagnetic potential sourced by a point charge. Encouragingly, this agrees with the known results in the flat space limit.

We have found the temporal gauge to be necessary in formulating the simplest extension of the flat-space convolution to de Sitter. Of course, it would be interesting to construct a more general convolution product on an (A)dS background, which could deal with arbitrary gauge choices.

On a more practical note, it would also be worthwhile to explore more general spacetimes of interest to cosmology. For example, we note that the subset of FLRW metrics with vanishing scalar curvature of the 3-space is conformally flat. The conformal factor is a simple generalisation of that in Eq.(
39
), again depending only on the time coordinate, thus making it a good candidate for a similar double copy construction in the temporal gauge. It is known that the non-spinning black hole solution in a general conformal spacetime can be described by the McVittie metric
McVittie 1933
, and it would be very interesting to reproduce this via the double copy.

Another direction for generalising our work is to go beyond the point like charge sources to higher multipole moments and study the corresponding gauge/gravity response. Additionaly, as (A)dS spacetime has an interesting asymptotic behavior, it would be of interest to generalize the results obtained in
Campiglia and Nagy 2021
;
Adamo and Kol 2022
for flat spacetime. Finally, in flat space, there has been progress in understanding how double copy formulations for fields and classical solutions connect with the amplitudes formulation.
Another possible direction would then be to connect with the correlator double copy programme
Mei 2023
;
Armstrong et al. 2021
;
Albayrak et al. 2021
;
Alday et al. 2021
;
Diwakar et al. 2021
;
Sivaramakrishnan 2022
;
Cheung et al. 2022
;
Herderschee et al. 2022
;
Drummond et al. 2023
;
Farrow et al. 2019
;
Lipstein and McFadden 2020
;
Jain et al. 2021
;
Zhou 2021
;
Armstrong et al. 2022
;
Lipstein and Nagy 2023
, which we leave for future work.

. The expressions involving vector indices are derived by taking the derivative with respect to the spatial coordinate,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int\frac{d^{d}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\left(\vec{k}^{2}\right)^{\alpha}}&=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{\Gamma(d/2-\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{\vec{r}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-d/2}\\
\int\frac{d^{d}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{k_{i}}{\left(\vec{k}^{2}\right)^{\alpha}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}&=ix_{i}\frac{\Gamma(d/2-\alpha+1)}{2(4\pi)^{d/2}\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{\vec{r}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-d/2-1}\\
\int\frac{d^{d}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{\left(\vec{k}^{2}\right)^{\alpha}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}&=\frac{\Gamma(d/2-\alpha+1)}{(4\pi)^{d/2}\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}+(\alpha-d/2-1)\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{\vec{r}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\vec{r}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-d/2-1}\end{aligned}\ ,

(80)

where the second equation is obtained by taking derivative
-i\partial_{i}=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
on both sides of the first equation, and similarly for the third equation. It is worth pointing out that the above standard Fourier transforms only hold when we are away from the origin,
r=0
. As is well known, the Laplacian acting on the
1/r
gives us the
\delta
function, which is zero everywhere away from the origin. When acting with derivatives
-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}
on the first equation, one should also recover the
\delta
-behavior when taking the trace. This leads to a special formula that we will use in Eq.(
65
),

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{r^{3}}-\frac{3x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{5}}\right)+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{3}\delta^{3}(\vec{x})\ ,

(81)

where the
\delta^{3}(\vec{x})
function can also be moved to the left hand side as a constant.

The inverse Fourier transformation takes the form,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int d^{d}\vec{x}\frac{e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\left(\vec{x}^{2}\right)^{\alpha}}&=\pi^{d/2}\frac{\Gamma(d/2-\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{\vec{k}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-d/2}\\
\int d^{d}\vec{x}\frac{\vec{x}_{i}}{\left(\vec{x}^{2}\right)^{\alpha}}e^{-i\vec{x}\cdot\vec{k}}&=ik_{i}\pi^{d/2}\frac{\Gamma(d/2-\alpha+1)}{2\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{\vec{k}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-d/2-1}\\
\int d^{d}\vec{x}\frac{\vec{x}_{i}\vec{x}_{j}}{\left(\vec{x}^{2}\right)^{\alpha}}e^{-i\vec{x}\cdot\vec{k}}&=\pi^{d/2}\frac{\Gamma(d/2-\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}+(\alpha-d/2-1)\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\vec{k}^{2}}{4}\right)^{\alpha-d/2-1}\end{aligned}\ .

(82)

As to the Fourier transformations involving time, we treat them in the following way,

\displaystyle f(t)=t^{2}\ ,f(t)=\int\frac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}e^{ik_{0}t}\tilde{f}(k_{0})\ ,

(83)

inversely,

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(k_{0})=\int dte^{-ik_{0}t}f(t)\ .

(84)

We have

\displaystyle 2=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}f(t)=\int\frac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}e^{ik_{0}t}\tilde{f}(k_{0})=-\int\frac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}k_{0}^{2}e^{ik_{0}t}\tilde{f}(k_{0})\ .

(85)

Since the constant under the Fourier transformation gives us the
\delta
function, we can express
\tilde{f}(k_{0})
as
-4\pi\delta(k_{0})/k_{0}^{2}
. Though this expression is not well-defined as a distribution function, one should think of it from the perspective of the derivative operation. Similarly. for
f(t)=t
, we have

\displaystyle 1=\frac{d}{dt}f(t)=\int\frac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}\frac{d}{dt}e^{ik_{0}t}\tilde{f}(k_{0})=i\int\frac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}k_{0}e^{ik_{0}t}\tilde{f}(k_{0})\ ,

(86)

Its Fourier transformation is
2\pi\delta(k_{0})/(ik_{0})
.

Appendix B
Temporal Gauge

B.1
flat space

In this section, we show how to apply gauge transformations in order to write our solutions in temporal gauge. We start with the flat space. Here we will leave the gauge index implicit, as it does not affect the calculations at linear level.

We start from the solution in Lorenz gauge Eq.(
56
), and perform the following gauge transformation,

\displaystyle A_{0}^{\prime}=A_{0}+\partial_{0}\Lambda=0,\ \Lambda=-\frac{g~t}{4\pi r}+f(r),\ A_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{g~t}{4\pi}\frac{x_{i}}{r^{3}}+f^{\prime}(r)\frac{x_{i}}{r}\ .

(87)

where
f(r)
denotes the redundant gauge degree of freedom that will be set to zero for simplicity here, and
′
denotes the derivative with respect to
r
.

In flat space, the gravitational perturbation transforms under the linearized diffeomorphism through

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}=h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}\ .

(88)

To go to the temporal gauge where
h_{0\mu}^{\prime}
vanishes, we first apply the transformation on the temporal component such that

\displaystyle h_{00}^{\prime}=h_{00}+2\partial_{0}\xi_{0}=0\ .

(89)

This is gives a differential equation for the gauge parameter
\xi_{0}
, with solution

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\int dt~h_{00}+F(r)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{r_{s}}{r}t+F(r)\ ,

(90)

where
F(r)
as a function of spatial coordinates serves as the integration constant, and represents the redundant gauge. The next step is to transform
h^{\prime}_{0i}
to zero,

\displaystyle h_{0i}^{\prime}=h_{0i}+\partial_{0}\xi_{i}+\partial_{i}\xi_{0}=0\ ,

(91)

which gives

\displaystyle\xi_{i}=-\int dt~h_{0i}-\int dt~\partial_{i}\xi_{0}=-\frac{t^{2}}{4}\frac{r_{s}x_{i}}{r^{3}}-tF^{\prime}(r)\frac{x_{i}}{r}+F_{i}(r)\ ,

(92)

where the
F_{i}(r)
are functions of
r
and serve as the redundant gauge in spatial components. This gives the spatial graviton after the gauge transform,

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{\prime}=\frac{r_{s}}{r}\delta_{ij}-\frac{t^{2}r_{s}}{2}\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{r^{3}}-\frac{3x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{5}}\right)\ ,

(93)

where we have set the redundant gauges
F(r)
and
F_{i}(r)
to zero.

B.2
de Sitter space

In de Sitter space, the diffeomorphism involves the covariant derivatives,

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}\to h^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}\ .

(94)

\displaystyle\delta h_{00}=2\partial_{0}\xi_{0}+\frac{2}{\eta}\xi_{0},\delta h_{0i}=\partial_{0}\xi_{i}+\partial_{i}\xi_{0}+\frac{2}{\eta}\xi_{i},\delta h_{ij}=\partial_{i}\xi_{j}+\partial_{j}\xi_{i}+\frac{2\delta_{ij}}{\eta}\xi_{0}\ .

(95)

Now we want
h^{\prime}_{00}=0
, which means

\displaystyle 2\partial_{0}\xi_{0}+\frac{2}{\eta}\xi_{0}=\delta h_{00}=-h_{00}=\frac{r_{s}}{r}\frac{1}{H\eta}\frac{\left(1+\frac{r^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)}{\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)^{2}}\ ,

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=\frac{r_{s}}{2Hr}\left(1+\frac{r^{2}}{r^{2}-\eta^{2}}\right)-\frac{r_{s}}{H\eta}\tanh^{-1}\frac{\eta}{r}+\frac{F(x)}{\eta}\ .

(96)

The next step is to make
h_{0i}^{\prime}=0
, this implies that

\displaystyle\frac{2r_{s}}{r}\frac{x_{i}}{H\eta^{2}}\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\right)^{-2}+\partial_{0}\xi_{i}+\frac{2}{\eta}\xi_{i}+\partial_{i}\xi_{0}=0\ ,

(97)

\displaystyle\xi_{i}=\frac{r_{s}x_{i}}{2r(-H\eta)}-\frac{r_{s}x_{i}(-\eta)}{6Hr^{3}}-\frac{r_{s}x_{i}r}{2(-H\eta)(\eta^{2}-r^{2})}+\frac{r_{s}x_{i}}{H\eta^{2}}\tanh^{-1}\frac{\eta}{r}-\frac{x_{i}F^{\prime}(r)}{2r}+\frac{F_{i}(r)}{\eta^{2}}\ .

(98)

With the
\xi_{\mu}
obtained above, we get

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{\prime}=h_{ij}+\partial_{i}\xi_{j}+\partial_{j}\xi_{i}=\frac{r_{s}x_{i}x_{j}(-\eta)}{Hr^{5}}-\frac{r_{s}\delta_{ij}(-\eta)}{3Hr^{3}}+4\pi\frac{r_{s}\eta}{9H}\delta_{ij}\delta^{3}(\vec{x})\ ,

(99)

which are the new spatial components in temporal gauge used in Eq.(
72
).

One can check that the above solutions in temporal gauge satisfy the equations of motion of gauge fields and gravitational fields,

\displaystyle H^{2}\eta^{2}\left(\partial_{0}^{2}A_{i}-\partial_{j}^{2}A_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}A_{j}\right)=j_{i}\ ,

\displaystyle H^{2}\eta^{2}\partial_{0}\partial_{i}A_{i}=j_{0}\ ,

(100)

where the second line should be treated as a constraint equation from the EoM of the temporal component
A_{0}
.

In a similar way, we can obtain the EoM for the graviton as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{2}h-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h_{ij}\right)+\frac{2}{\eta^{2}}h+\frac{1}{\eta}\partial_{0}h=\frac{T_{00}}{H^{2}\eta^{2}}\ ,

(101)

where
h\equiv\sum_{k=1,2,3}h_{kk}
is the trace of the graviton. For
h_{0i}
, the EoM is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}\left(\partial_{i}h-\partial_{j}h_{ij}\right)+\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\partial_{i}h-\partial_{j}h_{ij}\right)=\frac{T_{0i}}{H^{2}\eta^{2}}\ .

(102)

For the spatial part
h_{ij}
, the EoM is

\displaystyle h_{ij}-\eta\partial_{0}h_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\partial_{0}^{2}h_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\left(\partial_{k}\partial_{i}h_{jk}+\partial_{k}\partial_{j}h_{ik}-\partial_{i}\partial_{j}h-\partial_{k}^{2}h_{ij}\right)

(103)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}\left(-h+\eta\partial_{0}h+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\partial_{0}^{2}h-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\partial_{l}^{2}h+\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\partial_{k}\partial_{l}h_{kl}\right)=\frac{T_{ij}}{H^{2}}\ .

Appendix C
dilaton derivation

In this section, we recover the vanishing dilaton in flat space without fixing the temporal gauge in the dictionary itself, but rather treating the physical solution as one that happens to have a vanishing
A_{0}
. The dilaton dictionary contains the contribution

\displaystyle A\circ A-\frac{1}{\square}\partial A\circ\partial A=\frac{g\pi}{4}\frac{\delta(k_{0})}{k_{0}^{2}}+\frac{1}{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}\frac{g^{2}\pi}{4}\frac{\delta(k_{0})}{k_{0}^{2}}k^{2}=\frac{g^{2}\pi}{4}\frac{\delta(k_{0})}{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}\ ,

(104)

where we utilize Eq.(
63
).
Therefore, to get a vanishing dilaton, the ghost-antighost term should behave as

\displaystyle\xi\partial_{0}c\circ\bar{c}=\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}{2\mathfrak{b}_{2}}\frac{g\pi}{4}\frac{\delta(k_{0})}{k_{0}^{2}-k^{2}}\ .

(105)

Notice under the inverse Fourier transformation,
\delta(k_{0})
picks up the
k_{0}=0
phase, and the above expression is equivalent to

\displaystyle\xi\partial_{0}c\circ\bar{c}=\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}{2\mathfrak{b}_{2}}\frac{g\pi}{4}\frac{\delta(k_{0})}{-k^{2}}\ ,

(106)

which will behave like
1/r
in coordinate space. Plugging this ghost-antighost term back in the graviton’s dictionary, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{1}A_{i}\circ A_{j}+2\mathfrak{a}_{123}\left(\frac{\partial_{i}\partial j}{\partial_{0}^{2}}-\delta_{ij}\right)=\left(\mathfrak{a}_{1}-\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}\mathfrak{a}_{123}\right)\frac{g\pi}{4}\frac{\delta(k_{0})}{k_{0}^{2}}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}\mathfrak{a}_{123}\frac{g\pi}{4}\frac{\delta_{ij}\delta(k_{0})}{k^{2}}\ .

(107)

To map with the Fourier transformation of the graviton

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(h_{ij})=r_{s}8\pi^{2}\frac{\delta(k_{0})}{k_{0}^{2}}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}+r_{s}8\pi^{2}\frac{\delta_{ij}\delta(k_{0})}{k^{2}}\ ,

(108)

obtained from Eq.(
61
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{g\pi}{4}\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}\mathfrak{a}_{123}=8\pi^{2}r_{s}\ ,\quad\frac{g\pi}{4}\mathfrak{a}_{1}=16\pi^{2}r_{s}\ .

(109)

This gives an example of a convolution double copy through a general dictionary.

Inexact Proximal Methods for Weakly Convex Functions
Pham Duy Khanh, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Vo Thanh Phat, Dat Ba Tran

Abstract




Abstract
. This paper proposes and develops inexact proximal methods for finding stationary points of the sum of a smooth function and a nonsmooth weakly convex one, where an error is present in the calculation of the proximal mapping of the nonsmooth term. A general framework for finding zeros of a continuous mapping is derived from our previous paper on this subject to establish convergence properties of the inexact proximal point method when the smooth term is vanished and of the inexact proximal gradient method when the smooth term satisfies a descent condition. The inexact proximal point method achieves global convergence with constructive convergence rates when the Moreau envelope of the objective function satisfies the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property. Meanwhile, when the smooth term is twice continuously differentiable with a Lipschitz continuous gradient and a differentiable approximation of the objective function satisfies the KL property, the inexact proximal gradient method achieves the global convergence of iterates with constructive convergence rates.

Key words
: inexact proximal methods, weakly convex functions, forward-backward envelopes, Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property, global convergence, linear convergence rates, proximal points

Introduction

{sec:intro}

This paper is devoted to numerical variational analysis of the following class of optimization problems:

\displaystyle{\rm minimize}\quad\varphi(x):=f(x)+g(x)\quad\text{ subject to }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},

(1.1)

where
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
is a continuously differentiable (
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth) function, not necessary convex, and where
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) function. Since
g
can be chosen as an indicator function of a closed set, the setting of (
1.1
) encompasses various problems problems of constrained optimization, which are studied with numerous applications. This type of problem also appear in many practical models arising, e.g., in machine learning
[
37
,
46
]
, compressed sensing
[
39
]
, and image processing
[
4
,
34
]
. The
proximal-type methods
of our primal interest here are among the most exploited tools for tackling problem (
1.1
) when
g
is convex. More specifically, Martinet first introduced in
[
27
]
the proximal point method to deal with this problem when
f\equiv 0
while the proximal gradient method is designed in
[
6
,
31
]
to solve (
1.1
) when
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth with a Lipschitz continuous gradient. Each iteration of these methods requires
exact
calculations for the
proximal mapping

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x):=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\big\{{g(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}\big\},

(1.2)

where
\lambda>0
is a proximal parameter chosen in advance. Some choices of
g
allow us to find an explicit form for the proximal mapping in (
1.2
); see e.g.,
[
5
, Section 6.9]
and the repository in
[
15
]
. However, in many cases important for practical applications, such a form either doesn’t exist, or is very expensive to be determined computationally; see, e.g.,
[
14
,
18
]
and the discussions therein. For these reasons, some proximal methods that only require
inexact
information for the proximal mapping in (
1.2
) are designed. Let us mention the following major developments in this vein:

\bullet
Rockafellar proposes in
[
40
]
an
inexact proximal point method
(IPPM) for minimizing proper, l.s.c., convex functions with justifying its global convergence. A linear convergence rate of the method is achieved when the inverse of the subgradient mapping associated with the objective function is Lipschitz continuous around the origin; see also
[
9
]
.

\bullet
Schmidt et al.
[
42
]
study an
inexact proximal gradient method
(IPGM) for (
1.1
) when
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth and convex with a Lipschitz continuous gradient while
g
is proper, l.s.c., and convex.
A linear convergence of function values achieved by the average of iterates is established in
[
42
]
. If in addition
g
is strongly convex, then it is shown in
[
42
]
that the convergence is global.

\bullet
Bonettini et al.
[
13
]
design the
inexact forward-backward
(iFB)
algorithm
for (
1.1
) and verify its convergence when
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth (possibly nonconvex) with the Lipschitz continuous gradient, when
g
is proper, l.s.c., convex, and when
\varphi
is bounded from below. When the boundedness of the iterative sequence is assumed, stationarity for all accumulation points of the iterative sequence is established. If in addition
f
is real analytic and
g
is subanalytic, then the iterative sequence has finite length and thus converges to a stationary point of
\varphi
.

The main goal of this paper is to develop new
inexact versions
of both
proximal point
and
proximal gradient
methods in the case where
f
in (
1.1
) is a smooth and generally nonconvex functions, while
g

is
weakly convex
. The latter notion was introduced by Nurminskii
[
33
]
as a weak counterpart of
strongly convex
functions in the sense of Polyak
[
38
]
. We are not familiar with inexact versions of the proximal point method for weakly convex function, while referring the reader to the recent paper
[
10
]
, where an
exact
version of the proximal gradient method has been designed for weakly convex functions.

After completing this paper that extends the scheme and results of inexact reduced first-order methods from smooth optimization in our previous paper
[
24
]
to nonsmooth problems of type (
1.1
) with weakly convex functions

, we got familiar with preprint
[
7
]
, which addresses problems this type assuming that

[
7
]

, the class of weakly convex functions is the

to study the proximal-type methods requiring the

of the proximal mapping. Observe also that, to the best of our knowledge,
no convergence rate
for the iterative sequence generated by IPGM his been established when

is
nonconvex
. Furthermore, the assumptions on the boundedness from below of

[
13
]
seem to be restrictive.

Having in mind the above discussion, we propose novel
inexact proximal methods
to solve (
1.1
) when
g
is a weakly convex function and
f
is smooth but not necessarily convex. The main ideas for these methods come mostly from a general framework for finding zeros of continuous mappings, which is an extension of the

(IRGM) for finding stationary points of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth functions introduced in
[
24
]
. Specifically, such a framework allows us to establish the global convergence with convergence rates of IPPM. The basic convergence properties of our novel version of IPGM for
(
1.1
) are also obtained by employing this general framework together by employing the new notion of
weak
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
for weakly convex functions and techniques for dealing with approximate proximal mappings used in
[
42
]
. Our
major contributions
can be briefly summarized as follows:

\bullet
Finding
stationary accumulation points of IPPM
for a weakly convex function. Global convergence results with explicit convergence rates for this method are obtained under the KL property for the Moreau envelope of the objective function.

\bullet
Finding
stationary accumulation points of IPGM
for structured sums (
1.1
) of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth functions
f
satisfying a descent condition and extended-real-valued weakly convex functions
g
. Global convergence results with convergence rates are obtained for this method when the smooth part is twice continuously differentiable (
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth) with a Lipschitz continuous gradient while an approximation of the objective function satisfies the KL property.

Regarding
numerical aspects
in comparison with the iFB algorithm in
[
13
]
when
g
is convex, our new IPGM method exhibits some advantages in errors control. More specifically, iFB uses the decreasing errors
\varepsilon_{k}=k^{-p}
with
p>2
in the approximation procedure of
[
13
]
for the proximal mapping (
1.2
). When the number of iteration

is large, the error
\varepsilon_{k}
becomes too small, and thus the approximation procedure needs to run longer or even cannot stop due to the round-off error of computers. On the other hand, our method adapts the type of error introduced in
[
24
]
, which does not need to be decreased after each iteration. In minimization problems for smooth functions, this error is automatically adjusted following the magnitude of the exact gradient as illustrated numerically in
[
24
, Section 6]
. By using this type of errors, our IPGM overcomes the numerical disadvantage of iFB mentioned above.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2

3
, which is split into three subsections. This major section also provides a comparison between the algorithm descriptions and theoretical results obtained by IPGM and iFB in
[
13
]
. Numerical illustrations for these methods demonstrating advantages of IPGM in errors control are presented and discussed in the concluding Section

Basic Definitions and Preliminaries

{sec:2}

In this section, we recall some basic definitions and notation needed below and present their properties that are used in what follows. All our considerations are given in
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
with the Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
and inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
. Denoting
{\rm I\!N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
the collections of natural numbers and taking any nonempty subset
I\subset{\rm I\!N}
, the symbol
x^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I}}{{\to}}\bar{x}
means that
x^{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
with
k\in I
. We recall the following relaxation of the Lipschitz continuity of the gradient
\nabla f
employed in
[
2
,
24
]
.

Definition 2.1

(descent condition)
.

A continuously differentiable function
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
is said to satisfy the
descent condition
with constant
L>0
if

\displaystyle f(x)\leq f(y)+\left\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\right\rangle+\dfrac{L}{2}\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}\;\text{ for all }\;x,y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(2.1)

Remark 2.2
.

{weakly convex of ldescent}

The descent condition (
2.1
) is equivalent to the convexity of
\frac{L}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}-f(\cdot)
by the first-order characterization of convex functions
[
32
, Theorem 2.1.2]
. This property is strictly weaker than the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f
as discussed in
[
24
, Section 2]
. It is also shown in
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]
that the constant in the descent condition for
f
can be smaller than the constant of the Lipschitz continuity for
\nabla f
if
f
is a Moreau envelope (
2.5
) of a weakly convex function (Definition
2.4
). Relationships between the two properties for
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth functions are given below.

Proposition 2.3
.

{prop characterize l descent}

Let
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth function, and let
L>0
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\left\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\right\|\leq L\left\|x-y\right\|
for all
x,y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
.

(ii)

\left|f(x)-f(y)-\left\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\right\rangle\right|\leq\dfrac{L}{2}\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(iii)

\left\|\nabla^{2}f(x)\right\|\leq L
for all
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
.

[
Proof.]
The proof can be deduced from
[
32
, Lemmas 1.2.2 and 1.2.3]
and
[
43
, Lemma 8 of Ch. 5]
.

Next we recall the subdifferential constructions for proper extended-real-valued functions
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
taken from
[
28
,
29
,
41
]
and used below. The (Fréchet)
regular subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x}):=\Big\{v\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\Big|\;\liminf_{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\left\langle v,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle}{\left\|x-\bar{x}\right\|}\geq 0\Big\},

(2.2)

while the (Mordukhovich)
limiting subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\left\{v\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\big|\;\exists\,x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x},\ \varphi(x^{k})\rightarrow\varphi(\bar{x}),\ \widehat{\partial}f(x^{k})\ni v^{k}\rightarrow v\right\}.

(2.3)

When
\varphi
is of class of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth functions, then both subdifferentials in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) reduce to the gradient
\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})
. A necessary (but not sufficient) condition for
\bar{x}\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
to be a local minimizer of
\varphi
is

\displaystyle 0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x}).

(2.4)

A point that satisfies (
2.4
) is known in the literature as an
M
(
ordukhovich
)
-stationary
(or
limiting-stationary
) point of
\varphi
. Since this paper does not deal with any other stationary concepts, we simply use the
stationary
point term in what follows.

Let
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be a proper l.s.c. function. Given a parameter value
\lambda>0
, the
Moreau envelope

e_{\lambda}g(\cdot):{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
and the
proximal mapping

\textit{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(\cdot):{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{n}
are defined, respectively, as

e_{\lambda}g(x):=\inf_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\Phi_{\lambda,x}(y),

(2.5)

\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x):=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\Phi_{\lambda,x}(y),

(2.6)

where the minimizing function
\Phi_{\lambda,x}:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda,x}(y):=g(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}.

(2.7)

A function
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is called
prox-bounded
if there exists
\lambda>0
such that
e_{\lambda}g(x)>-\infty
for some
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.
The supremum of all such
\lambda
is the
threshhold
\lambda_{g}
of prox-boundedness
for
g
.
When
g
is a convex function, it is prox-bounded with the threshhold
\lambda_{g}=\infty
. Moreover, its proximal mapping
\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}
is single-valued and continuous in this case, while its Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}g
is convex and
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth for any
\lambda>0
; see, e.g.,
[
41
, Theorem 2.26]
. These remarkable properties can be extended for the class of
weakly convex
functions introduced by Nurminskii
[
33
]
in the way equivalent to the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

{weakly convex def}

A proper l.s.c. function
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is called
\varrho
-weakly convex
for some
\varrho\geq 0
if the quadratically shifted function
g(\cdot)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
is convex.

It has been well recognized in the literature that the class of weakly convex functions is rather large including any
{\cal C}^{2}
-smooth function as well as many important settings in nonsmooth and nonconvex constrained optimization; see, e.g.,
[
10
,
47
]
and the references therein.

Remark 2.5
.

{remark weakly descent}

(i)
If
g
is
\varrho
-weakly convex with
\varrho\geq 0
, then it is also
\rho
-weakly convex for all
\rho\geq\varrho.
As a consequence, any convex function is
\varrho
-weakly convex for any
\varrho\geq 0
.

(ii)

g
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth, it follows from Remark
2.2
that its
\varrho
-weak convexity is equivalent to the descent property with constant
\varrho
of
-g
.

(iii)
Suppose that
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is a
\varrho
-weakly convex function and that
\lambda\in(0,\varrho^{-1})
. Then for any
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, the minimizing function
\Phi_{\lambda,x}(y)
in (
2.7
) is represented by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda,x}(y)=g(y)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}+\dfrac{\lambda^{-1}-\varrho}{2}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2},

being
strongly convex
with constant
\lambda^{-1}-\varrho>0
since
g(y)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}
is convex.

(iv)
If
g
is weakly convex, the regular and limiting subdifferentials agree for all
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, i.e.,
g
is
lower regular
on
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
in the sense of
[
28
]
.

The next result taken from
[
47
, Proposition 3.13]
shows that the
weak convexity
of a function is a
necessary and sufficient
condition for the associated
proximal mapping
(
1.2
) to be
single-valued
.

Proposition 2.6
.

{prox lipschitz}

Let
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be proper, l.s.c., and prox-bounded with the threshold
\lambda_{g}\in(0,\infty]
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)
For all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{g})
, the mapping
\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}
is single-valued on
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
.

(ii)

g
is
\lambda_{g}^{-1}
-weakly convex, where we use the convention
\infty^{-1}=0
.

In this case, the mapping
\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}
is Lipschitz continuous on
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{g})
.

Let us now recall the notion of
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
broadly used in convex analysis, optimization, and their numerous applications; see, e.g.,
[
19
,
30
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

{epsilon subdif}

Let
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be a proper l.s.c. function, and let
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Then the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
of
g
at some
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,g
is denoted by

\displaystyle{\partial}_{\varepsilon}g(\bar{x}):=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\left\langle v,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq g(x)-g(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\right\}.

(2.8)

For convex quadratic functions important for proximal-type considerations, the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential is explicitly calculated from the definition; see
[
19
, Example 1.2.2]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

{sub grad quadratic}

Let
A
be any
n\times n
symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix, let
b\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, and let
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
be a convex quadratic function defined by
g(x):=\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle+\left\langle b,x\right\rangle
for all
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}g(x)=\Big\{A(x+e)+b\;\Big|\;\dfrac{1}{2}\left\langle Ae,e\right\rangle\leq\varepsilon\Big\}\;\text{ for all }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},\;\varepsilon\geq 0.

A direct consequence of Proposition
2.8
is the following formula, which plays an important role in convergence analysis of inexact proximal gradient methods for convex functions conducted in
[
42
]
.

Corollary 2.9
.

{sub grad norm sq}

Given
\bar{x}\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
,
\lambda>0
, and
g(x):=(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|x-\bar{x}\right\|^{2}
, we have

\partial_{\varepsilon}g(x)=\big\{\lambda^{-1}(x-\bar{x}+e)\;\big|\;\|e\|^{2}\leq 2\lambda\varepsilon\big\}\;\text{ for all }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},\;\varepsilon\geq 0.

[
Proof.]
Apply Proposition
2.8
with
A:=I/\lambda
and
b:=-\bar{x}/\lambda
.

Observe that the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential (
2.8
) may be
empty
for simple weakly convex functions. Indeed, the function
g:{\rm I\!R}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
given by
g(x):=-x^{2}
on
{\rm I\!R}
is obviously
2-
weakly convex while

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}g(0)=\left\{v\in{\rm I\!R}\;\big|\;vx\leq-x^{2}+\varepsilon\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}\right\}=\emptyset\;\text{ for all }\;\varepsilon\geq 0.

For our purposes in this paper, we propose to the following proximal-type modification of
\varepsilon
-subgradients that allows us to deal with
\varrho
-weakly convex functions.

Definition 2.10
.

{approximate sub}

Let
\varrho
and
\varepsilon
be the nonnegative numbers. The
weak
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
of a
\varrho
-weakly convex function
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,g
is defined by

\partial_{\varepsilon,\varrho}g(\bar{x}):=\big\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq g(x)-g(\bar{x})+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\varepsilon\;\text{ for all }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\big\}.

(2.9)

Remark 2.11
.

{prop subset}

It is straightforward to deduce from the definition that:

(i)
If
\varrho=0,
i.e.,
g
is a convex function, then

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon,0}g(\bar{x})=\partial_{\varepsilon}g(\bar{x}).

(ii)
We have the equalities

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon,\varrho}g(\bar{x})=\bigcap_{\delta>0}\partial_{\varepsilon,\varrho+\delta}g(\bar{x})=\bigcap_{\delta>0}\partial_{\varepsilon+\delta,\varrho}g(\bar{x}).

(iii)

0\in\partial_{\varepsilon,\varrho}g(\bar{x})
if and only if
\bar{x}
is an

\varepsilon
-solution
for minimizing the convex function
\widetilde{g}(\cdot):=g(\cdot)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot-\bar{x}\right\|^{2}
. The latter means that

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(\bar{x})\leq\widetilde{g}(x)+\varepsilon\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

The next proposition represents the
\varepsilon-
weak subdifferential of a
\varrho
-weakly convex function
g
via the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential of the quadratic shifted function
g(\cdot)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
and provides a sum rule for this new type of approximate subdifferentials.

Proposition 2.12
.

{prop 2.2}

Let
\varrho,\rho,\varepsilon
be nonnegative numbers with
\nu:=\varrho+\rho
, and let
g,h:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be
\varrho
-weakly convex and
\rho
-weakly convex, respectively. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)
For any
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,g
, we have the equality

\displaystyle{\partial}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}g(\bar{x})=\partial_{\varepsilon}(g+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})-\varrho\bar{x}.

(ii)
For any
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,g\cap\mbox{\rm dom}\,h
, it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon,\nu}(g+h)(\bar{x})\supset\bigcup\big\{\partial_{\varepsilon_{1},\varrho}g(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2},\rho}h(\bar{x})\;\big|\;\varepsilon_{i}\geq 0,\;\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon\big\}.

(2.10)

When
{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,g)\cap{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,h)\neq\emptyset
, the equality in (
2.10
) is satisfied, and as a consequence we get

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon,\nu}(g+h)(\bar{x})\subset\partial_{\varepsilon,\varrho}g(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon,\rho}h(\bar{x}).

(2.11)

[
Proof.]
(i) Since
\varphi(\cdot):=g(\cdot)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
, is convex, it follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon,\varrho}g(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\left\langle v,x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq g(x)-g(\bar{x})+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|x-\bar{x}\right\|^{2}+\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\left\langle v+\varrho\bar{x},x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq g(x)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|x\right\|^{2}-g(\bar{x})-\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\bar{x}\right\|^{2}+\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\big|\;\left\langle v+\varrho\bar{x},x-\bar{x}\right\rangle\leq\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon,\ \forall x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon}\varphi(\bar{x})-\varrho\bar{x}\;\mbox{ whenever }\;\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,g.

(ii) We deduce from the
\varrho
-weak convexity of
g
and
\varrho
-weak convexity of
h
that
g(\cdot)+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
and
h(\cdot)+\dfrac{\rho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
are convex functions. Using the sum rule for
\varepsilon
-subdifferentials from
[
19
, Theorem 3.1.1]
gives us

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}(g+h+\dfrac{\nu}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon}(g+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}+h+\dfrac{\rho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\supset\bigcup\left\{\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}(g+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}(h+\dfrac{\rho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})\;\big|\;\varepsilon_{i}\geq 0,\;\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon\right\},

(2.12)

where the equality holds if
{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(g+\frac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}))\cap{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,(h+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}))\neq\emptyset
, or equivalently if
{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,g)\cap{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,h)\neq\emptyset
. Observe by (i) that

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}(g+h+\dfrac{\nu}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})=\partial_{\varepsilon}(g+h)(\bar{x})+\nu\bar{x},

and that for all
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
with
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon_{1}}(g+\dfrac{\varrho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2}}(h+\dfrac{\rho}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon_{1},\varrho}g(\bar{x})+\varrho\bar{x}+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2},\rho}h(\bar{x})+\rho\bar{x}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\varepsilon_{1},\varrho}g(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2},\rho}h(\bar{x})+\nu\bar{x}.

Then the inclusion in (
2
) implies that

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon,\nu}(g+h)(\bar{x})\supset\bigcup\left\{\partial_{\varepsilon_{1},\varrho}(g)(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2},\rho}(h)(\bar{x})\;\big|\;\varepsilon_{i}\geq 0,\;\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon\right\},

(2.13)

where the equality holds if
{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,g)\cap{\rm ri}(\mbox{\rm dom}\,h)\neq\emptyset
. When the latter condition is satisfied, we deduce from Remark
2.11
(iii) that

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon,\nu}(g+h)(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\bigcup\left\{\partial_{\varepsilon_{1},\varrho}g(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon_{2},\rho}h(\bar{x})\;\big|\;\varepsilon_{i}\geq 0,\;\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\partial_{\varepsilon,\varrho}g(\bar{x})+\partial_{\varepsilon,\rho}h(\bar{x}),

which completes the proof of the proposition.

In the remainder of this section, we recall and discuss the

Kurdyka-Łojasiewciz
(KL)
property
as formulated in
[
3
]
, which plays an important in establishing global convergence with convergence rates of many optimization methods; see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
among other references.

Definition 2.13
.

{KL ine}

We say that a function
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
enjoys the
KL property
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\partial f
if there exist
\eta\in(0,\infty]
, a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
. and a concave continuous function
\psi:[0,\eta)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that:

(i)

\psi(0)=0
.

(ii)

\psi
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth on
(0,\eta)
.

(iii)

\psi^{\prime}>0
on
(0,\eta)
.

(iv)
For all
x\in U
with
f(\bar{x})<f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\eta
, we have

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}\big(f(x)-f(\bar{x})\big){\rm dist}(0,\partial f(x))\geq 1.

(2.14)

It has been realized that the KL property is satisfied in broad settings. In particular, it holds at every
nonstationary point
of
f
; see
[
3
, Lemma 2.1 and Remark 3.2(b)]
. Furthermore, it is proved in the original paper by Łojasiewicz
[
26
]
that any analytic function
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
satisfies the KL property at every point
\bar{x}
with
\psi(t)=~Mt^{1-q}
for some
q\in[0,1)
. This property also holds for continuous
semialgebraic functions, continuous subanalytic functions, and functions definable in an o-minimal structure
[
11
,
12
]
. The notions of subanalytic sets and sub-analytic functions can be found in
[
12
,
17
]
while the discussion on semialgebraic functions can be found in
[
2
,
8
]
. For the reader convenience, we recall some fundamental properties of subanalytic and semialgebraic functions in the next two remarks.

Remark 2.14
.

{remark subana}

Some basic properties of subanalytic functions and subanalytic sets in
[
17
, p. 597]
and
[
12
, p. 1208]
are summarized as follows:

(i)
The class of subanalytic sets is closed under finite unions and intersections.

(ii)
The Cartesian product of subanalytic sets is subanalytic.

(iii)
If
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}^{p}
and
g:{\rm I\!R}^{p}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}^{m}
are subanalytic mappings, with
f
being continuous, then the composition
g\circ f
is subanalytic. In particular, the class of continuous subanalytic functions is closed under algebraic operations.

(iv)
Analytic functions are subanalytic.

(v)
The inverse image of a subanalytic set under a subanalytic mapping is a subanalytic set.

It follows from (i), (ii), and (v) that if the functions
f_{i}:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
are subanalytic, then the mapping
F:=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
is subanalytic as well.

Remark 2.15
.

{semi algebraic}

The following properties of semialgebraic functions are useful in the subsequent sections:

(i)
Finite sums and products of semialgebraic functions are semialgebraic.

(ii)
Composition of semialgebraic functions or mappings are semialgebraic.

(iii)
Functions of the type
f(x):=\inf_{y\in S}h(x,y)
, where
h
and
S
are semialgebraic, are semialgebraic.

(iv)
Semialgebraic functions are subanalytic.

(v)
Both regular subdifferential (
2.2
) and limiting subdifferential (
2.3
) of semialgebraic functions are
semialgebraic set-valued mappings.

The properties listed in (i)-(iii) can be found in
[
3
,
8
]
. Property (iv) is mentioned in
[
11
, p. 1208]
while the proof of (v) follows from
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
. As a consequence of (i) and (iii), the Moreau envelope of a proper, l.s.c., semialgebraic function is also semialgebraic, and thus it is subanalytic.

3.1
A General Framework for Finding Zeros of Continuous Mappings

Here we propose the following inexact algorithm to solve the problem
G(x)=0
, where

is a single-valued continuous mapping.

Algorithm 1
.

{scheme continuous mapping}

Step 0

(initialization)
.

{scheme step 0}

Select a starting point
x^{1}\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, initial radii
\varepsilon_{1},r_{1}>0
, radius reduction factors
\mu,\theta\in(0,1)
. Set
k:=1.

Step 1

(inexact mapping)
.

{scheme step 1}

Find some
g^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|g^{k}-G(x^{k})\right\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}.

(3.1)

Step 2

(radius update)
.

{scheme step 2}

If
\left\|g^{k}\right\|\leq r_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}
then
r_{k+1}:=\mu r_{k},

\varepsilon_{k+1}:=\theta\varepsilon_{k}
and
d^{k}:=0
. Otherwise, set
r_{k+1}:=r_{k},\;\varepsilon_{k+1}:=\varepsilon_{k}
, and

\displaystyle d^{k}:=-{\rm Proj}(0,\mathbb{B}(g^{k},\varepsilon_{k}))=-\dfrac{\left\|g^{k}\right\|-\varepsilon_{k}}{\left\|g^{k}\right\|}g^{k}.

(3.2)

Step 3

(stepsize)
.

{scheme step 3}

Choose a stepsize
t_{k}>0.

Step 4

(iteration update)
.

{scheme step 4}

Set
x^{k+1}:=x^{k}+t_{k}d^{k}.
Increase
k
by
1
and go back to Step
1
.

Following the analysis in
[
24
, Section 4]
, we introduce the notion of null iterations of Algorithm
1
and provide some fundamental properties related to this notion.

Definition 3.1
.

{nul iter}

The
k^{\rm th}
iteration of Algorithm
1
is called a

if
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
. The set of all null iterations is denoted by

\mathcal{N}:=\left\{k\in{\rm I\!N}\;\big|\;x^{k+1}=x^{k}\right\}.

Remark 3.2
.

{null remark 2}

We have the following properties of null iterations for Algorithm
1
:

(i)

k\in\mathcal{N}
if and only if either one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

\displaystyle d^{k}=0\Longleftrightarrow r_{k+1}=\mu r_{k}\Longleftrightarrow\varepsilon_{k+1}=\theta\varepsilon_{k}\Longleftrightarrow\left\|g^{k}\right\|\leq r_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}.

(3.3)

(ii)

\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
if and only if
r_{k}\downarrow 0
, which is equivalent to the set
\mathcal{N}
being infinite.

(iii)
Considering
k\notin\mathcal{N}
, it follows from
\left\|g^{k}\right\|>r_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}
and (
3.2
) in Step

that

\left\|d^{k}\right\|=\left\|g^{k}\right\|-\varepsilon_{k}>r_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{k}=r_{k}.

As a consequence, if
\varepsilon_{k}\leq r_{k},
we get

\varepsilon_{k}\leq\left\|d^{k}\right\|,
and hence

\displaystyle\left\|G(x^{k})\right\|\leq\left\|g^{k}\right\|+\varepsilon_{k}=\left\|d^{k}\right\|+2\varepsilon_{k}\leq 3\left\|d^{k}\right\|.

(3.4)

(iv)
If
\mathcal{N}
is finite, observe that for any natural number
k\geq N:=\max{\mathcal{N}}+1
, the
k^{\rm th}
iteration is not a null one and then deduce from (iii) that

\displaystyle r_{k}=r_{N},\;\varepsilon_{k}=\varepsilon_{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|g^{k}\right\|>r_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}=r_{N}+\varepsilon_{N},\;\left\|d^{k}\right\|>r_{k}=r_{N}.

This tell us that
\left\{g^{k}\right\}
and
\left\{d^{k}\right\}
are bounded away from
0.

The next result reveals relationships between sequences of inexact mappings, directions and radii generated by Algorithm
1
. The proofs for (i) and (ii) are similar to
[

, while the last assertion of this proposition is deduced from (i), (ii), the continuity of

Proposition 3.3
.

{goes to 0}

Let
\left\{g^{k}\right\},\;\left\{d^{k}\right\},\;\left\{\varepsilon_{k}\right\}
, and
\left\{r_{k}\right\}
be sequences generated by Algorithm

. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
if and only if there is an infinite set

such that

.

(ii)
For any infinite set
I\subset{\rm I\!N}
, we have the equivalence

\displaystyle g^{k}\overset{I}{\rightarrow}0\Longleftrightarrow d^{k}\overset{I}{\rightarrow}0.

If there exists some infinite set
I\subset{\rm I\!N}
such that either

or

G(x^{k})\overset{I}{\rightarrow}0.

Now we recall the classical results that describe important properties of accumulation points generated by a sequence satisfying Ostrowski’s limiting condition. Asserting (i) of Lemma

Lemma 3.4

{lemma: ostrowski}

Let

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|=0.

Then the following assertions hold:

(i)
If the sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is bounded, then the set of accumulation points of
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is nonempty, compact, and connected in
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
.

(ii)
If
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
has an isolated accumulation point, then this sequence converges to it.

The next theorem, which is a nonsmooth extension of
[

Theorem 3.5
.

{corollary 2.6}

Let
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
be the sequence of iterates generated by Algorithm
1
. Assume that
\left\{t_{k}\right\}
is bounded from above and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}<\infty

Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
and
r_{k}\downarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty.

Every accumulation point of
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is a zero of
G
, and we have
\displaystyle{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}<\infty
.

(ii)
If the sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is bounded, then the set of accumulation points of
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is nonempty, compact, and connected.

(iii)
If
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
has an isolated accumulation point, then the entire sequence converges to this point.

[
Proof.]
Since

as

by taking into account the continuity of

which implies (ii). The latter yields

{0 prop non null sequence}

3.2
Inexact Proximal Point Method

Now we are ready to introduce and develop the
inexact proximal point method
for finding stationary points of a
\varrho
-
weakly convex
function

Algorithm 2

(Inexact proximal point method for weakly convex functions)
.

{ppm}

Step 0

(initialization)
.

Select a proximal parameter
\lambda\in\big(0,\varrho^{-1}\big)
and some
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
satisfying the conditions
0<\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}<2\lambda
. Choose a starting point
x^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, initial radii
\varepsilon_{1},r_{1}>0
, and radius reduction factors
\mu,\theta\in(0,1)
. Set
k:=1.

Step 1

(inexact proximal mapping)
.

{step 1 ppm}

Find some
p^{k}
and define
g^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|p^{k}-\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x^{k})\right\|\leq\lambda\varepsilon_{k}\text{ and }g^{k}:=\lambda^{-1}(x^{k}-p^{k}).

(3.7)

Step 2

(radius update)
.

If
\left\|g^{k}\right\|\leq r_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}
, then
r_{k+1}:=\mu r_{k},\varepsilon_{k+1}:=\theta\varepsilon_{k},d^{k}:=0
. Otherwise, set
r_{k+1}:=r_{k},\;\varepsilon_{k+1}:=\varepsilon_{k}
, and

\displaystyle d^{k}:=-\dfrac{\left\|g^{k}\right\|-\varepsilon_{k}}{\left\|g^{k}\right\|}g^{k}.

Step 3

(stepsize)
.

Choose a stepsize
t_{k}\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}].

Step 4

(iteration update)
.

Set
x^{k+1}:=x^{k}+t_{k}d^{k}
. Increase
k
by
1
and go back to Step

.

We recall some important properties of Moreau envelopes of weakly convex functions that are useful in the convergence analysis of Algorithm
2
.

Proposition 3.9
.

{weakly and descent}

Let
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be a
\varrho
-weakly convex function, and let
\lambda\in(0,\varrho^{-1})
. Then the following assertions holds:

(i)
The Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}g
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth and its gradient calculated by

\displaystyle\nabla e_{\lambda}g(x)=\lambda^{-1}(x-\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x)).

(3.8)

(ii)
The Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}g
satisfies the descent condition with constant
\lambda^{-1}
meaning that

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}g(y)\leq e_{\lambda}g(x)+\left\langle\nabla e_{\lambda}g(x),y-x\right\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}\text{ for all }x,y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(3.9)

(iii)
Every stationary point of
e_{\lambda}g
is a stationary point of
g
.

[
Proof.]
Assertions (i) and (ii) follow from
[
16
, Lemma 2.5]
. Taking any
x
with
\nabla e_{\lambda}g(x)=0
, we deduce from (
3.8
) that
x=\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x)
. By (
2.6
),
x
solves the problem

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\big\{g(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}\big\}.

Applying the generalized Fermat rule and the subdifferential sum rule to the function
y\mapsto g(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}
gives us
0\in\partial g(x)
, which verifies assertion (iii).

Remark 3.10
.

{reduce prop}

Proposition
3.9
(i) allows us to conclude that the inexact proximal point method in Algorithm
2
is a special case of the general scheme to find zeros of continuous functions proposed in Algorithm
1
. Indeed, we can equivalently rewrite condition (
3.7
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|g^{k}-\nabla e_{\lambda}g(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\lambda^{-1}(x^{k}-p^{k})-\lambda^{-1}(x^{k}-{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x^{k}))\right\|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\left\|{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x^{k})-p^{k}\right\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}.

Therefore, Algorithm
1
reduces to Algorithm
2
with
G:=\nabla e_{\lambda}g
and
t_{k}\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
.

The next theorem, which is based on Proposition
3.9

3.3
Inexact Proximal Gradient Method

In this subsection, we derive our major results on the new
inexact proximal gradient method
to solve optimization problems of the type

\displaystyle{\rm minimize}\quad\varphi(x):=f(x)+g(x),

(3.10)

where
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}

\varrho
-weakly convex function with
\varrho\geq 0
. As mention in Section
1
, the formally unconstrained framework of (

) covers problems of constrained optimization via

Define the
composite gradient
mapping
T_{\lambda}:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}^{n}
as
\lambda\in(0,\varrho^{-1})
with
0^{-1}:=\infty
by

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}(x)=\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(F_{\lambda}(x)),

where
F_{\lambda}(x):=x-\lambda\nabla f(x)
. The mapping
T_{\lambda}
can be expressed by (
2.7
) as

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}(x)=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x)}(y),

providing the following
quadratic approximation
of
\varphi
(see
[
6

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\big\{g(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-(x-\lambda\nabla f(x))\right\|^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\big\{(\lambda/2)\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\left\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\right\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}+g(y)\big\}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\big\{f(x)+\left\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\right\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}+g(y)\big\}.

(3.12)

Define further the
gradient
mapping
G_{\lambda}:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}^{n}

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(x):=\lambda^{-1}(x-T_{\lambda}(x)).

(3.13)

Remark 3.13
.

{prop: prox property}

By Proposition

,
\mbox{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}

It follows from (

\displaystyle 0\in\nabla f(x)+\partial g(x)=\partial\varphi(x).

Now we are ready to propose the
inexact proximal gradient method
(IPGM) to find stationary points of the class of functions

given (

Algorithm 3

(Inexact proximal gradient method)
.

{pgm}

{Step 1}

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}(p^{k})\leq\inf\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}+\min\left\{\dfrac{\lambda(1-\lambda\varrho)\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}{2},\rho_{k}\right\}

\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq\varphi(x^{k})-\varphi(x^{k+1})+C_{2}\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\left\|d^{k}\right\|+\rho_{k}.

(3.29)

Summing up both sides of the latter inequality over
k=1,2,\ldots
gives us

\displaystyle C_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq\varphi(x^{1})-\varphi(x^{m+1})+C_{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\left\|d^{k}\right\|+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\rho_{k}\;\text{ for all }\;m\in{\rm I\!N}.

Since
\inf_{k\in{\rm I\!N}}\varphi(x^{k})>-\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_{k}<\infty
, we get

\displaystyle C_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq C_{3}+C_{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\left\|d^{k}\right\|\;\text{ for all }\;m\in{\rm I\!N},

where
C_{3}:=\varphi(x^{1})-\inf_{k\in{\rm I\!N}}\varphi(x^{k})+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_{k}<\infty
.
It implies by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle C_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq C_{3}+C_{2}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\rho_{k}}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}+C_{2}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_{k}}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}}=C_{3}+C_{4}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}},

where
C_{4}:=C_{2}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_{k}}
. Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}}-\dfrac{C_{4}}{2C_{1}}\right)^{2}-\dfrac{C_{4}^{2}}{4C_{1}}=C_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}-C_{4}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}}\leq C_{3}\;\text{ for all }\;m\in{\rm I\!N},

which gives us the desired condition
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}<\infty
.

Suppose now that
\rho_{k}=\min\left\{\dfrac{C_{1}^{2}}{4C_{2}^{2}},\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\right\}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}
for any
k\in{\rm I\!N}
and show that (
3.29
) yields

\displaystyle\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq\varphi(x^{k})-\varphi(x^{k+1})\;\text{ whenever }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

(3.30)

If
k\in\mathcal{N}
, inequality (
3.30
) is obviously satisfied since both sides are zeros. Otherwise, we get

\displaystyle\left\|d^{k}\right\|=\left\|g^{k}\right\|-\varepsilon_{k}>r_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{k}=r_{k}\geq\varepsilon_{k}.

Therefore, the choice of
\left\{\rho_{k}\right\}
leads us to the inequalities

\displaystyle C_{2}\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\left\|d^{k}\right\|+\rho_{k}\leq C_{2}\dfrac{C_{1}}{2C_{2}}\varepsilon_{k}\left\|d^{k}\right\|+\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\leq\dfrac{3C_{1}}{4}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}.

Combining this with (
3.29
), we arrive at

\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq\varphi(x^{k})-\varphi(x^{k+1})+\dfrac{3C_{1}}{4}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2},

which implies in turn the fulfillment of (
3.30
). Summing up both sides of (
3.30
) over
k=1,2,\ldots
with taking into account that
\inf_{k\in{\rm I\!N}}\varphi(x^{k})>-\infty
verifies
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}<\infty
and completes the proof.

{remark descent}

(3.31)

generated by Algorithm

. First we recall the notion of

Definition 3.19

(
forward-backward envelopes).

Let
\varphi=f+g
be given as in (
3.10
) and let
\lambda\in(0,\varrho^{-1})
. The
forward-backward envelope
(FBE) of
\varphi
is given by

\varphi_{\lambda}(x):=\inf_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\big\{f(x)+\left\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\right\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}+g(y)\big\}.

The FBE
\varphi_{\lambda}
is majorized by
\varphi
, i.e.,
\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\leq\varphi(x)
for all
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
. This definition can be rewritten via the Moreau envelope (
2.5
) of
g
as

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=f(x)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+\inf_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\big\{g(y)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|y-(x-\lambda\nabla f(x))\right\|^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle=f(x)-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+\inf_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x)}(y)

\displaystyle=f(x)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+e_{\lambda}g(x-\lambda\nabla f(x)).

When
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth, it follows from the latter equality and Proposition
3.9
that the FBE
\varphi_{\lambda}
of
\varphi
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth with the gradient given by

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}(I-\lambda\nabla^{2}f(x))(x-\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda g}(x-\lambda\nabla f(x)))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}(I-\lambda\nabla^{2}f(x))(x-T_{\lambda}(x)).

(3.32)

Consider the following approximation of the objective function
\varphi
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\times{\rm I\!R}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R},\\
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(x,\varepsilon)=\varphi_{\lambda}(x)+{C}\varepsilon^{2}\text{ for all }x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},\;\varepsilon\in{\rm I\!R},\end{cases}

(3.33)

where
{C}=\min\left\{\dfrac{\lambda(1-\lambda\varrho)}{2},\dfrac{C_{1}^{2}}{4C_{2}^{2}},\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\right\}
with
C_{1},C_{2}
defined in (
3.19
).

The next proposition provides important properties of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
related to
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
, which are used below to establish convergence results for the iterative sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
.

Proposition 3.20
.

{properties surrogate}

Let
\varphi
be defined in (
3.10
), where
f
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth function with
L
-Lipschitz gradient, and let
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
3
with
\varepsilon_{1}\leq r_{1},\theta\leq\mu
and
\rho_{k}=C\varepsilon_{k}^{2}
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
, where
C
is taken from
Theorem
3.17
. Then for all
k\notin{\rm I\!N}
, there are constants
a_{1},a_{2}>0
such that:

(i)

\varphi(x^{k+1})\leq\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(x^{k},\varepsilon_{k})\leq\varphi(x^{k})+{C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}
.

(ii)

\varphi(x^{k})-\varphi(x^{k+1})\geq a_{1}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}
.

(iii)

\left\|\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(x^{k},\varepsilon_{k})\right\|\leq a_{2}\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|
.

[
Proof.]
Pick
k\notin\mathcal{N}
and deduce from the second inequality in (i) that
\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{k})\leq\varphi(x^{k})
. It follows by the constructions of
{C},C
, and
\rho_{k}
that

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\dfrac{\lambda(1-\lambda\varrho)}{2}\varepsilon_{k}^{2},\rho_{k}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\min\Big\{\dfrac{\lambda(1-\lambda\varrho)}{2}\varepsilon_{k}^{2},\dfrac{C_{1}}{4C_{2}^{2}}\varepsilon_{k}^{2},\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\Big\}={C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}.

(3.34)

Since
k\notin\mathcal{N}
, the vector
x^{k+1}=p^{k}
is a
{C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}
-solution to subproblem (
3.14
). Combining this with (
3.14
), (
3.34
), and the
L
-descent property of
f
as in (
2.1
), we get that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{k})

\displaystyle=f(x^{k})-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|^{2}+\inf_{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}(y)

\displaystyle\geq f(x^{k})-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|^{2}+\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}(x^{k+1})-{C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x^{k})-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})\right\|^{2}+g(x^{k+1})+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x^{k+1}-(x^{k}-\lambda\nabla f(x^{k}))\right\|^{2}-{C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x^{k})+\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}+g(x^{k+1})-{C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq f(x^{k+1})-\dfrac{L}{2}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}+g(x^{k+1})-{C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\varphi(x^{k+1})-{C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2},

where the last inequality is deduced from
\lambda^{-1}>L
. Therefore, (i) is justified.

It follows from
\varepsilon_{1}\leq r_{1}
and
\theta\leq\mu
that
\varepsilon_{k}\leq r_{k}
. By Proposition
3.14
(iii), we have
t_{k}\leq 2\lambda.

Combining this with (
3.31
) and the relation
x^{k+1}=x^{k}+t_{k}d^{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\varphi(x^{k})-\varphi(x^{k+1})

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\left\|d^{k}\right\|^{2}=\dfrac{C_{1}}{4t_{k}^{2}}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{C_{1}}{16\lambda^{2}}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|^{2},

which justifies (ii) with
a_{1}:=C_{1}/16\lambda^{2}
.

To verify (iii), we first observe that
\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(x^{k},\varepsilon_{k})=(\nabla\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{k}),2{C}\varepsilon_{k}).
Since
\nabla f
is
L
-Lipschitz, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that
\left\|\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})\right\|\leq L
, and hence

\displaystyle\left\|I-\lambda\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})\right\|\leq\left\|I\right\|+\lambda\left\|\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})\right\|\leq 1+\lambda L.

(3.35)

Using the representation of
\nabla\varphi_{\lambda}
in (

) and the latter estimate give us

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\left\|(I-\lambda\nabla^{2}f(x^{k}))(x^{k}-T_{\lambda}(x^{k}))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-1}\left\|I-\lambda\nabla^{2}f(x^{k})\right\|\cdot\left\|x^{k}-T_{\lambda}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-1}(1+\lambda L)\left\|x^{k}-T_{\lambda}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda^{-1}+L)\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|+\lambda^{-1}(1+\lambda L)\left\|x^{k+1}-T_{\lambda}(x^{k})\right\|.

(3.36)

Furthermore, it follows from
x^{k+1}=p^{k}
and Proposition

(i) that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{k})\right\|\leq(\lambda^{-1}+L)\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|+(1+\lambda L)\varepsilon_{k}.

(3.37)

Remembering that

(iii) that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}\leq\left\|d^{k}\right\|=t_{k}^{-1}\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|\leq\lambda^{-1}\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|.

(3.38)

Combining finally (

gives us

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(x^{k},\varepsilon_{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{k})\right\|+2{C}\varepsilon_{k}

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda^{-1}+L)\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|+(1+\lambda L)\varepsilon_{k}+2{C}\varepsilon_{k}

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda^{-1}+L)\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|+(1+\lambda L+2{C})\lambda^{-1}\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|

\displaystyle=2(\lambda^{-1}+L+{C}\lambda^{-1})\left\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\|,

which justifies (iii) with

and thus completes the proof.

The next result inspired by
[
3
, Theorem 2]
is useful to establish the convergence rates below.

Lemma 3.21
.

{lemma KL fran}

Let
\left\{s_{k}\right\}
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers, and let
\alpha>1
and
\beta>0
be some constants. Suppose that
s_{k}\downarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
, and that for all large
k\in{\rm I\!N}
we have

\displaystyle s_{k}^{\alpha}\leq\beta(s_{k-1}-s_{k}).

(3.39)

Then there exists a number
\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle s_{k}\leq\gamma k^{-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\;\text{ whenever }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}\;\text{ is sufficiently large}.

[
Proof.]
Let
N\in{\rm I\!N}
be such that (
3.39
) holds for all
k\geq N
. Defining

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}:=\min\left\{\dfrac{\alpha-1}{2\beta},(2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}-1)s^{1-\alpha}_{N-1}\right\}>0,

we are going to show that

\displaystyle s_{k}^{1-\alpha}-s_{k-1}^{1-\alpha}\geq\bar{\mu}\;\text{ for all }\;k\geq N.

(3.40)

Consider the two cases for
k.

Case 1
:
s_{k}^{-\alpha}>2s_{k-1}^{-\alpha}
. Since
1-\alpha<0
, we have
s_{k}^{1-\alpha}>2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}s^{1-\alpha}_{k-1}
. Then the decreasing property of
\left\{s_{k}\right\}
yields the desired result:

\displaystyle s_{k}^{1-\alpha}-s_{k-1}^{1-\alpha}>(2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}-1)s_{k-1}^{1-\alpha}\geq(2^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}-1)s^{1-\alpha}_{N-1}\geq\bar{\mu}.

Case 2
:
s_{k}^{-\alpha}\leq 2s_{k-1}^{-\alpha}
. It follows from (
3.39
) and the decreasing property of
\left\{s_{k}\right\}
that

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq\beta(s_{k-1}-s_{k})s_{k}^{-\alpha}\leq 2\beta(s_{k-1}-s_{k})s_{k-1}^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 2\beta\int_{s_{k}}^{s_{k-1}}s^{-\alpha}ds=\dfrac{2\beta}{1-\alpha}(s_{k-1}^{1-\alpha}-s_{k}^{1-\alpha}),

which implies by
\alpha>1
that

\displaystyle s_{k}^{1-\alpha}-s_{k-1}^{1-\alpha}\geq\dfrac{\alpha-1}{2\beta}\geq\bar{\mu}.

Fixing some
K>2N
and summing up (
3.40
) over
k=N+1,\ldots,K
, we get that
s_{K}^{1-\alpha}-s_{N}^{1-\alpha}\geq\bar{\mu}(K-N)
. This allows us to verify the estimates

\displaystyle s_{K}\leq\left[s_{N}^{1-\alpha}+\bar{\mu}(K-N)\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\leq\left(\dfrac{\bar{\mu}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}K^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}

and therefore complete the proof of the lemma.

, and since
\psi
is continuous at
0
with
\psi(0)=0
, suppose without loss of generality that

\displaystyle\left\|z^{k_{1}-1}-\bar{x}\right\|<\nu/4,\quad\left\|z^{k_{1}}-z^{k_{1}-1}\right\|<\nu/8,\;\mbox{ and }\;a_{2}a_{1}^{-1}\psi(\varphi(z^{k_{1}})-\varphi(\bar{x}))<\nu/4.

(3.48)

We claim that if
k\geq k_{1}
and
\left\|y^{k-1}-\bar{y}\right\|<\nu
, then

\displaystyle 2\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|\leq\phi_{k}+\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|,

(3.49)

where
\phi_{k}:=a_{2}a_{1}^{-1}\left[\psi(\varphi(z^{k})-\varphi(\bar{x}))-\psi(\varphi(z^{k+1})-\varphi(\bar{x}))\right]
. To verify this, fix such
k
. Since
\left\|y^{k-1}-\bar{y}\right\|<\nu
and
k\geq k_{1}
, it follows from the first assertion in (
3.47
) that inequality (
3.46
) holds for
y=y^{k-1},
i.e.,

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{k-1})-\varphi(\bar{x}))\left\|\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{k-1})\right\|\geq 1.

(3.50)

The latter implies by (
3.43
) that

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{k-1})-\varphi(\bar{x}))\geq\dfrac{1}{\left\|\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{k-1})\right\|}\geq\dfrac{1}{a_{2}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|}.

(3.51)

Since
\psi
is concave,
\psi^{\prime}
is nonincreasing. It follows from (
3.41
) that

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{k-1})-\varphi(\bar{x})\geq\varphi(z^{k})-\varphi(\bar{x})
, and the nonincreasing property implies therefore that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{a_{2}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|}\leq\psi^{\prime}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{k-1})-\varphi(\bar{x}))\leq\psi^{\prime}(\varphi(z^{k})-\varphi(\bar{x})).

Combining the latter with the concavity of
\psi
and condition (
3.42
) brings us to the inequalities

\displaystyle\psi(\varphi(z^{k})-\varphi(\bar{x}))-\psi(\varphi(z^{k+1})-\varphi(\bar{x}))\geq\psi^{\prime}(\varphi(z^{k})-\varphi(\bar{x}))(\varphi(z^{k})-\varphi(z^{k+1}))

\displaystyle\geq\psi^{\prime}(\varphi(z^{k})-\varphi(\bar{x}))a_{1}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}\geq\dfrac{a_{1}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}}{a_{2}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|}.

This can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|^{2}\leq\phi_{k}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|,

which implies in turn by using
2\sqrt{uv}\leq u+v
that

\displaystyle 2\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|\leq\phi_{k}+\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|

and thus verifies the above claim.

Now we show that inequality (
3.49
) holds for all
k\geq k_{1}
by induction. This is obviously true for
k=k_{1}
since
\left\|y^{k_{1}-1}-\bar{y}\right\|\leq\left\|z^{k_{1}-1}-\bar{x}\right\|+\sigma_{k_{1}}<\nu/2
. Suppose that it holds for
k_{1},k_{1}+1,\ldots,K-1.
Summing up inequality (
3.49
) over
k=k_{1},k_{1}+1,\ldots,K-1
, we get

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{K-1}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{K-1}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|+\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{K-1}\phi_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{K-1}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|+\left\|z^{k_{1}}-z^{k_{1}-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\psi(\varphi(z^{k_{1}})-\varphi(\bar{x})),

which implies in turn that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{K-1}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|\leq\left\|z^{k_{1}}-z^{k_{1}-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\psi(\varphi(z^{k_{1}})-\varphi(\bar{x})).

(3.52)

Employing the triangle inequality gives us

\displaystyle\left\|z^{K-1}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|z^{k_{1}}-\bar{x}\right\|+\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{K-1}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|z^{k_{1}-1}-\bar{x}\right\|+2\left\|z^{k_{1}}-z^{k_{1}-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\psi(\varphi(z^{k_{1}})-\varphi(\bar{x}))<\dfrac{3\nu}{4}.

Since
\sigma_{K-1}<\nu/4
by (
3.47
), we get that

\left\|y^{K-1}-\bar{y}\right\|\leq\left\|z^{K-1}-\bar{x}\right\|+\sigma_{K-1}<\nu,

and thus inequality (
3.49
) holds for
k=K
. Letting
K\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.49
) gives us

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|<\infty.

The latter means that
\left\{z^{k}\right\}
is a Cauchy sequence, and hence it converges to
\bar{x}
. Therefore,
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
converges to
\bar{x}
as well, and moreover we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|+\sum_{k\notin\mathcal{N}}\left\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\rm I\!N}}\left\|x^{j_{k}+1}-x^{j_{k}}\right\|=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|<\infty,

which verifies assertion (i).

(ii) For any
p\in{\rm I\!N},
set
s_{p}:=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|
, which is a finite number. The triangle inequality yields
\left\|z^{p}-\bar{x}\right\|\leq s_{p}
, it suffices to establish the convergence rates for
s_{p}
. To proceed, take any
p\geq k_{1}
, where
k_{1}
satisfies (
3.47
) and (
3.48
). It follows from the proof of (i) that

\displaystyle 2\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|\leq\phi_{k}+\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|\text{ for all }k\geq p.

Summing up the above inequality over
k=p,p+1,\ldots
, we get

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\left\|z^{k}-z^{k-1}\right\|+\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\phi_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|+\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\psi(\varphi(z^{p})-\varphi(\bar{x})),

which implies in turn that

\displaystyle s_{p}\leq\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(\varphi(z^{p})-\varphi(\bar{x}))^{1-q}.

(3.53)

It follows from (
3.50
) by using
\psi^{\prime}(t)=(1-q)Mt^{-q}
for all
t>0
that

(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{p-1})-\varphi(\bar{x}))^{q}\leq M(1-q)\left\|\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{k-1})\right\|.

Combining this with
\varphi(z^{p})\leq\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{p-1})
from (
3.41
) and
q\in(0,1),
we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(\varphi(z^{p})-\varphi(\bar{x}))^{1-q}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{p-1})-\varphi(\bar{x}))^{1-q}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(M(1-q)\left\|\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{p-1})\right\|)^{\frac{1-q}{q}}.

The latter together with (
3.53
) and (
3.43
) gives us

\displaystyle s_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(M(1-q)\left\|\nabla\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(y^{p-1})\right\|))^{\frac{1-q}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(M(1-q)a_{2}\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|)^{\frac{1-q}{q}}\text{ for all }p\geq k_{1}.

(3.54)

We consider the two possible cases for
q.

Case 1
:
q\in(0,1/2]
, or equivalently,

(1-q)/q\geq 1
. Since
\left\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\right\|\rightarrow 0
in this case, we can assume that
M(1-q)a_{2}\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|<1
for all
k\geq p_{1}
and thus deduce from (
3.54
) that

\displaystyle s_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(M(1-q)a_{2}\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\dfrac{M(1-q)a_{2}^{2}}{a_{1}}\right)\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|=\alpha(s_{p-1}-s_{p}),

where
\alpha:=\left(1+\dfrac{M(1-q)a_{2}^{2}}{a_{1}}\right)
. The above estimate can be rewritten as

\displaystyle s_{p}\leq\dfrac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}s_{p-1}\text{ for all }p\geq k_{1},

which implies that
\left\{s_{k}\right\}
linearly converges to
0.
Therefore,
\left\{z^{k}\right\}
linearly converges to
\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
.

Case 2
:
q\in(1/2,1),

or equivalently,

(1-q)/q\in(0,1)
. It follows from (
3.54
) in this case that

\displaystyle s_{p}\leq\left(1+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(M(1-q)a_{2})^{\frac{1-q}{q}}\right)\left\|z^{p}-z^{p-1}\right\|^{\frac{1-q}{q}},

which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle s_{p}^{\frac{q}{1-q}}\leq\beta(s_{p-1}-s_{p})\text{ for all }p\geq k_{1},\;\mbox{ with }\;\beta:=\left[1+\dfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}(M(1-q)a_{2})^{\frac{1-q}{q}}\right]^{\frac{q}{1-q}}.

By Lemma
3.21
with
\alpha:=q/(1-q)
, there exists a positive constant
\gamma
such that

\displaystyle s_{k}\leq\gamma k^{-\frac{1-q}{2q-1}}\;\text{ for sufficiently large }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

Remark 3.23

(comments on the KL assumption)
.

{remark KL}

Let us now discuss some efficient conditions on
f
and
g
, which guarantee the KL property of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
given in (
3.33
) by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(x,\varepsilon)=f(x)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+e_{\lambda}g(x-\lambda\nabla f(x))+{C}\varepsilon^{2}.

(i)

f
is analytic and
g
is semialgebraic.
Indeed, it follows from Remark
2.15
that
x\mapsto e_{\lambda}g(x)
is semialgebraic, and thus is subanalytic. Since
f
is analytic,
[
25
, Proposition 2.2.3]
tells us that all the partial derivatives of
f
is analytic. Using
[
25
, Proposition 2.2.8]
, we deduce that
x\mapsto\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}
is analytic, and thus
(x,\varepsilon)\mapsto f(x)-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x)\right\|^{2}+{C}\varepsilon^{2}
is analytic. Since all the partial derivatives of
f
is analytic, Remark
2.14
also tells us that
\nabla f
is
subanalytic. Since the functions
x\mapsto e_{\lambda}g(x)
and
x\mapsto x-\lambda\nabla f(x)
are continuous, we deduce from Remark
2.14
(iii) that
e_{\lambda}g(x-\lambda\nabla f(x))
is subanalytic, continuous, and so is
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
. By the continuity of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
, the KL property of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
is satisfied for
\psi(t):=Mt^{1-q}
with
M>0
and
q\in[0,1)
.

(ii)

f
and
g
are semialgebraic functions.
Indeed, similarly to (i),
e_{\lambda}g
is a semialgebraic function. By Remark
2.15
(v), the mapping
x\mapsto\nabla f(x)
is also semialgebraic. Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
is a polynomial function, Remark
2.15
(i,ii) tells us again that
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
is semialgebraic. Thus it is subanalytic by Remark
2.15
(iv). Therefore, the KL property of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
is satisfied for
\psi(t):=Mt^{1-q}
with
M>0
and
q\in[0,1)
.

Remark 3.24

(comparison between IPGM and iFB)
.

Let us now compare the major issues in the algorithm description and the obtained results for IPGM given in Theorems
3.17
and
3.22
with that of the
inexact forward-backward
(iFB) algorithm in
[
13
, Theorem 8]
to solve problem (
3.10
) when the function
g
is
convex
. The iFB algorithm from
[
13
]
can be written as follows:

Algorithm 4

(inexact forward-backward (iFB))
.

{ifb}

Step 0
.

Choose
\lambda\in(0,L^{-1})
, a starting point
x^{1}
and a sequence of errors
\omega_{k}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\omega_{k}}<\infty
. Set
k:=1
.

Step 1
.

Compute
p^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}(p^{k})\leq\inf\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}+\omega_{k},

(3.55)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),p^{k}-x^{k}\right\rangle+(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|p^{k}-x^{k}\right\|^{2}+g(p^{k})<g(x^{k}).

(3.56)

Step 2
.

Set
x^{k+1}:=p^{k}.
Increase
k
by
1
and go back to Step 1.

We comments on the following issues.

(i)

Algorithm description
. The main difference between IPGM and iFB is the choice of
stopping criterion
for the subproblem of finding approximate solution
p^{k}
to
T_{\lambda}(x^{k})
at each iteration. Although both algorithms use the condition (
3.55
) for finding
p^{k}
, the selections of
\omega_{k}
are different. The error
\omega_{k}
in iFB needs to satisfy the condition
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\omega_{k}}<\infty
. On the other hand, it follows from Algorithm
3
and Theorem
3.17
that IPGM considers two selections for
\omega_{k}
which are either

\displaystyle\omega_{k}:

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\dfrac{\lambda\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}{2},\rho_{k}\right\}\;\text{ with }\;\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_{k}<\infty,\;\text{ or }

(3.57)

\displaystyle\omega_{k}:

\displaystyle={C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}\;\text{ with }\;{C}:=\min\left\{\dfrac{\lambda}{2},\dfrac{C_{1}^{2}}{4C_{2}^{2}},\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\right\},

(3.58)

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are defined in Proposition
3.16
. Step
2
of Algorithm
3
shows that
\varepsilon_{k}

doesn’t need to decrease
after an iteration. This number is reduced only when
\left\|g^{k}\right\|
is sufficiently small, which holds when
x^{k}
is near the exact solution of problem (
3.10
). When
k\rightarrow\infty
, the convergence rate to
0
of
\left\{\rho_{k}\right\}
in the first choice (
3.57
) is slower than that of iFB. In the second selection (
3.58
), the error
\omega_{k}
of the subproblem is independent with
k
. The approximate solution
p^{k}
for the subproblem of iFB needs to satisfy condition (
3.56
), which ensures the sufficient decrease property of the sequence
\left\{\varphi(x^{k})\right\}
. This property is guaranteed for IPGM if the second choice of error (
3.58
) is employed; see
Remark
3.18
.

(ii)

Stationary accumulation points
. Theorem 8(i) of
[
13
]
requires that the smooth part
f
has a Lipschitz gradient, that the function
\varphi
is bounded from below, and that the sequence
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is bounded. On the other hand, our Theorem
3.17
requires that the smooth part satisfies only the
L
-descent condition, which is strictly weaker than the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f
. This allows us to work with broader classes of functions by replacing the usual Euclidean distance by the
Bregman distance
. The boundedness of
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
and the boundedness from below of
\varphi
are not required in Theorem
3.17
. The latter property is relaxed to
\inf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(x^{k})>-\infty
.

(iii)

Global convergence.
Theorem 8(ii) of
[
13
]
requires that the smooth part
f
is analytic and that the nonsmooth part
g
is subanalytic. On the other hand, the global convergence analysis in our Theorem
3.22
is based on the KL property of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
. It follows from Remark
3.23
that the latter property holds when
g
is
semialgebraic
and
f
is
either analytic
, or
semialgebraic
.

(iv)

Convergence rate.
The rate of convergence for
\left\{x^{k}\right\}
is established in Theorem
3.22
under the KL property of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
, while not any type of convergence rate for iFB is given in
[
13
]
. Instead,
[
13
, Theorem 9]
presents a convergence rate for function values in the variable metric inexact linesearch-based algorithm.

{sec:6}

In this section, we compare the numerical efficiency of our IPGM with iFB in Algorithm
4
for a variant of the
nonconvex image restoration problem
taken from
[
44
]
. To proceed, given a vector
b\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, matrices
A\in{\rm I\!R}^{n\times n}
, and
B\in{\rm I\!R}^{m\times n}
together with a number
\gamma>0
, we consider the following problem:

\text{minimize }\quad\varphi(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(1+(Ax-b)_{i}^{2})+\gamma\|Bx\|_{1}\quad\text{subject to}\quad x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},

(4.1)

where
\left\|u\right\|_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|u_{i}|
for any
u\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

It can be seen thatn(
4.1
) is a special case of (
3.10
) with
f(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(1+(Ax-b)_{i}^{2})
and
g(x):=\gamma\|Bx\|_{1}
. Indeed,
g
is clearly convex while
f
is continuously differentiable with the gradient

\displaystyle\nabla f(x)=2A^{T}u,\;\text{ where }\;u_{i}=\frac{(Ax-b)_{i}}{1+(Ax-b)_{i}^{2}},\;i=1,\ldots,n,\quad\text{for all }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

The smooth part
f
is also considered in
[
13
, Section 4.1]
for an image deblurring and denoising problem. It follows from
[
13
, (4.2)]
that
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L=2\left\|A\right\|_{1}\left\|A\right\|_{\infty}
, where

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|_{1}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|,\quad\left\|A\right\|_{\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|,\quad A=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=\overline{1,n}}.

Moreover,
f
is analytic it is expressed as finite sums and compositions of analytic functions,
g
is semialgebraic and
\varphi
is bounded from below by
0
Therefore, the assumptions on the objective function in Theorem
3.22
and
[
13
, Theorem 8]
are satisfied, and so both IPGM and iFB are applicable to solve (
4.1
).

Problem (
4.1
) is studied in
[
44
]
when the matrix
B
is square and orthogonal. In this section, we consider
A\in{\rm I\!R}^{n\times n},\;b\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},\;B\in{\rm I\!R}^{m\times n}
generated randomly, with i.i.d. (identically and independent distributed) standard Gaussian entries, where
m,n
need not to be equal. The number
\gamma
is chosen as either
10^{-3}
, or
10^{-6}
. We provide two numerical experiments to illustrate how the difference in the stopping criteria for the subproblems of iFB and IPGM affect their performance.

Now we describe the setting for both algorithms in the tests. Firstly, the proximal parameter is chosen as

to ensure that
\lambda\in(0,L^{-1})
for both algorithms. The starting point is chosen as
x^{1}:=0_{{\rm I\!R}^{n}}
. At any iteration of each algorithm, the step of finding an approximation proximal point
p^{k}
satisfying (
3.55
) is equivalent to solving approximately the optimization problem

\min\quad\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}(p):=(2\lambda)^{-1}\left\|p-F_{\lambda}(x^{k})\right\|^{2}+\gamma\left\|Bx\right\|_{1}.

(4.2)

The dual problem of (
4.2
) is given as

\displaystyle\max\quad\Psi_{\lambda,k}(y):

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\lambda}{2}\left\|B^{*}y\right\|^{2}+\left\langle BF_{\lambda}(x^{k}),y\right\rangle-\delta_{\gamma\mathbb{B}^{\infty}}(y),

(4.3)

where
\mathbb{B}^{\infty}:=\left\{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{m}\;|\;\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}|y_{i}|\leq 1\right\}
. In our tests, the dual sequence
\{y^{l}\}
to solve problem (
4.3
) is generated by means of the Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (FISTA) from
[
6
]
and the primal sequence in it is chosen as
p^{l}:=-\lambda B^{*}y^{l}+F_{\lambda}(x^{k})
. The following main stopping criterion is used in both IPGM and iFB:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda,F_{\lambda}(x^{k})}(p^{l})-\Psi_{\lambda,k}(y^{l})\leq\omega_{k},

(4.4)

where the choice of
\omega_{k}
depends on the method. Namely, the settings for IPGM and iFB are as follows:

•

IPGM:
\omega_{k}={C}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}
, where
{C}:=\min\left\{\dfrac{\lambda(1-\lambda\varrho)}{2},\dfrac{C_{1}^{2}}{4C_{2}^{2}},\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\right\}
with
C_{1},C_{2}
defined in Proposition
3.16
. Since
g
is convex, we get

5
Conclusions and Further Research

{sec:concl}

In this paper, we propose and develop inexact proximal point and inexact proximal gradient methods to solve problems of weakly convex optimization and nonconvex composite optimization. These methods achieve stationary accumulation points and, under additional assumptions on the KL property of the envelopes, the global linear convergence. The convergence analysis of the developed algorithms are based on the general framework of finding zeros of continuous mappings when only inexact information for such mappings is accessible. A new approximate subdifferential for weakly convex functions is employed to simplify the convergence analysis of the aforementioned methods.

Our future research includes detailed and efficient numerical analysis and experiments to solve subproblems of the proposed methods. We also intend to find conditions ensuring the convergence to local/global minimizers of our inexact methods. The obtained results would allow us to develop new applications in important classes of models in machine learning, statistic, and related disciplines.

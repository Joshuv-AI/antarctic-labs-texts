The W s , p W^{s,p} -boundedness of stationary wave operators for the Schrödinger operator with inverse-square potential
Changxing Miao, Xiaoyan Su, Jiqiang Zheng

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the W s , p W^{s,p} -boundedness for stationary wave operators of the Schrödinger operator with inverse-square potential ℒ a = − Δ + a | x | 2 , a ≥ − ( d − 2 ) 2 4 , \mathcal{L}_{a}=-\Delta+\tfrac{a}{|x|^{2}},\quad a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}, in dimension d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . We construct the stationary wave operators in terms of integrals of Bessel functions and spherical harmonics, and prove that they are W s , p W^{s,p} -bounded for certain p p and s s which depend on a a . As corollaries, we solve some open problems associated with the operator ℒ a \mathcal{L}_{a} , which include the dispersive estimates and the local smoothing estimates in dimension d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . We also generalize some known results such as the uniform Sobolev inequalities, the equivalence of Sobolev norms and the Mikhlin multiplier theorem, to a larger range of indices. These results are important in the description of linear and nonlinear dynamics for dispersive equations with inverse-square potential.



Key Words:
Schrödinger operator; stationary wave operator; inverse-square potential; dispersive estimate; local smoothing estimate; uniform Sobolev inequality; multiplier theorem; equivalence of Sobolev norms

58J50, 42B20, 35J10, 33C05

1.
Main results and applications

1.1.
Background

For a real-valued potential
V
, the time-dependent wave operators for the pair of self-adjoint operators
H_{0}=-\Delta
and
H\coloneq H_{0}+V
are defined as

\displaystyle W_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\slim_{t\to{\pm\infty}}e^{itH}e^{-itH_{0}},

(1.1)

\displaystyle W_{\pm}^{*}

\displaystyle=\slim_{t\to{\pm\infty}}e^{itH_{0}}e^{-itH},

(1.2)

where
\slim
indicates the strong limit in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In
[
18
]
, Kato and Kuroda derived the following stationary formula for the wave operators,

\displaystyle W_{\pm}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E(\lambda)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}(H-\lambda)R_{0}(\lambda\pm i0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle W_{\pm}^{*}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{0}(\lambda)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}(H_{0}-\lambda)R(\lambda\pm i0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(1.4)

where
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E(\lambda),\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{0}(\lambda),R(\lambda\pm i0),R_{0}(\lambda\pm i0)
are the spectral measures and resolvents associated to
H
and
H_{0}
, respectively. The authors in
[
18
]
proved that under some restrictive assumptions, the wave operators defined in time-dependent form (
1.1
),(
1.2
) and stationary form (
1.3
), (
1.4
) are identical.

For suitable potentials
V
e.g. short range potentials, one can prove that the time-dependent
W_{\pm}
and
W_{\pm}^{*}
exist and are complete, see Agmon
[
2
]
. This implies that they are isometries from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and there is no singular continuous spectrum. It is natural to ask: for what
p\neq 2
are the wave operators
W_{\pm},W_{\pm}^{*}
bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, or bounded in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
? This is precisely the question that this paper tries to answer.

The
W^{s,p}
-boundedness of the wave operators was first investigated by Yajima
[
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
48
,
49
]
under the assumption that zero energy is neither a eigenvalue nor a resonance. These results are established by using the perturbation method and the resolvent identity. However, the above results only apply to a restricted class of potentials. To relax the conditions, Beceanu and Schlag recently
[
3
]
established a structure formula for wave operators using an abstract version of Wiener’s theorem, which implies the
L^{p}
-boundedness for more general potentials.

In this paper, we study the
W^{s,p}
-boundedness of the stationary wave operators associated to the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}=-\Delta+\tfrac{a}{|x|^{2}}
for
a\geq-\smash{\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}}
in dimensions
d\geq 2
, for suitable
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. We define
\mathcal{L}_{a}
as the Friedrichs extension of the operator
-\Delta+\tfrac{a}{|x|^{2}}
defined on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\})
. We refer the readers to
[
36
, Section X.3]
for the general theory of such extensions. The restriction
a\geq-{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}}
guarantees positivity of
\mathcal{L}_{a}
; indeed, the associated form is

\displaystyle Q(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\nabla f+\frac{\sigma x}{|x|^{2}}f\Big|^{2}\,dx,\quad\text{where}\quad\sigma\coloneq\frac{d-2}{2}-\Big[\Big(\frac{d-2}{2}\Big)^{2}+a\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.5)

The elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}
has appeared frequently in the literature. For example, the heat and Schrödinger flows associated with the elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}
have been studied in the theory of combustion (see
[
42
]
), and in quantum mechanics (see
[
17
]
). It is known
[
8
,
32
]
that the time-dependent wave operators of
-\Delta
and
\mathcal{L}_{a}
exist and are complete for
a>-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
in dimensions
d\geq 3
. For the case
d=2
and
d\geq 3
with
a=-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
, there is no scattering theory for
\mathcal{L}_{a}
. However, we can still define the stationary wave operators using the formula (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), provided we have the spectral measures.

One of the main motivations for investigating the
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-boundedness for the stationary wave operators is to derive the Strichartz and dispersive estimates.
Burq, Planchon, Stalker, and Tahvildar-Zadeh
[
9
,
10
]
proved the Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger and wave equations with inverse-square potentials, in space dimensions
d\geq 2
and
a>-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
. The endpoint case
a=-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
was proven by Mizutani
[
30
]
. Later, Fanelli, Felli, Fontelos, and Primo proved
[
13
,
14
]
the dispersive estimates for certain Schrödinger evolution, including the inverse-square potential in dimensions
d=2
and
3
. The dispersive estimates and Strichartz estimates play an important role in the description of well-posedness and long-time behavior for the nonlinear dispersive equation with potentials, see
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
28
,
29
,
50
,
51
]
.
However, to the best of our knowledge, the dispersive estimates for the Schrödinger evolution
e^{it\mathcal{L}_{a}}
in higher dimensions
d\geq 4
, and wave propagator
\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}
in all dimensions are still open. Once we have the
W^{s,p}
-boundedness, by the intertwining property, we may transfer the dispersive estimates and Strichartz estimates of the operator
-\Delta
to the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}
.

Another motivation for our work is to obtain Sogge’s local smoothing estimates for
e^{it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}
.
This states that the space regularity of half-wave propagator will be improved if we average in time.
Using the
W^{s,p}
-boundedness, we will establish the corresponding estimates for
e^{it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}
, under the assumption that local smoothing estimates have been proven for
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}
.

Our results also imply the uniform Sobolev inequality for
\mathcal{L}_{a}
, the equivalence of Sobolev norms adapted to
\mathcal{L}_{a}
, and a Mikhlin-type multiplier theorem for
\mathcal{L}_{a}
. These results improve the corresponding results in
[
33
,
22
]
to a more general range of indices. See Subsection
1.3
for more details.

One thing we want to mention is that a number of other estimates related to
\mathcal{L}_{a}
also can be directly deduced from the
W^{s,p}
-boundedness of the stationary wave operators, but to keep our paper at a reasonable length, we omit the details.

1.2.
Main results

Main result 1.

First, we give the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the stationary wave operators, which is also the first step to establish the
W^{s,p}
-boundedness.

Theorem 1.1

(
L^{p}
-boundedness)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
. Then, the stationary wave operators
W_{\pm}
and
W_{\pm}^{*}
are bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\max\big\{0,\tfrac{\sigma}{d}\big\}<\tfrac{1}{p}<\min\big\{1,\tfrac{d-\sigma}{d}\big\},

(1.6)

where
\sigma
is as in (
1.5
).

Remark 1.2
.

The condition (
1.6
) is equivalent to

\begin{cases}\phantom{{}_{0}}1<p<+\infty&\text{if}\quad a>0,\\
p_{0}^{\prime}<p<p_{0}&\text{if}\quad a<0,\end{cases}

(1.7)

where
p_{0}=\frac{d}{\sigma}
.

Proof sketch for Theorem
1.1

To prove the above results, we cannot use the perturbative method as Yajima and Beceanu did, due to the strong singularity at zero. However, notice that the potential term
V=\smash{\frac{a}{|x|^{2}}}
is homogeneous of degree
-2
, which has the same scaling as
-\Delta
. This key property has been employed in
[
9
,
10
,
35
]
to prove the dispersive and Strichartz estimates for the solutions of Schrödinger equation and wave equation with inverse square potentials.
In particular,
[
35
]
proved Strichartz estimates for the wave equation by constructing some intertwining operators which enabled them to transfer the results for
-\Delta
to
\mathcal{L}_{a}
, but under the radial assumption. Our contribution is to remove this assumption, as we now explain.

Using the special structure of our operator, on each subspace of spherical harmonics, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}
is equivalent to a Bessel operator which can be diagonalized using the eigenfunction expansion in terms of spherical waves. This eigenfunction expansion serves as the “distorted Fourier transform” for
\mathcal{L}_{a}
. It is known
[
27
]
that once the distorted Fourier transforms
\mathscr{F}_{\pm}
are well-defined, the wave operators can be given by
W_{\pm}=\mathscr{F}_{\pm}^{*}\mathscr{F}
. This formula inspires us to construct the stationary wave operators directly.

Hence, we construct two intertwining operators
W
and
W^{*}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
-\Delta
and
\mathcal{L}_{a}
using the decomposition into spherical harmonics,

\displaystyle Wf(r,\omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}K_{k}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r},

(1.8)

\displaystyle W^{*}f(r,\omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}K_{k}^{*}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r},

(1.9)

where
K_{k}(r,s),
and
K_{k}^{*}(r,s)
are given by integrals involving two Bessel functions, and the projection operator
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is defined as in (
2.14
) below.
It is easy to check that
W
and
W^{*}
are unitary in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In addition, we are able to prove that
W=W_{-}
and
W^{*}=W_{-}^{*}
defined by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

To verify that
W
and
W^{*}
are bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
it suffices to prove two modified operators
\widetilde{W}
and
\widetilde{W}^{*}
are bounded in
L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)
.
One of the key ingredients in proving the
L^{p}
-boundedness is a discrete multiplier theorem (Theorem
2.12
below) for spherical harmonics.
If for any fixed
r
and
s
, the coefficients
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)

satisfy the assumptions of this theorem, then it follows that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{W}f(r,\omega)\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}\leq M(r,s)\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)},

where
M(r,s)
is the upper bound for
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
uniformly in
k
. If
M(r,s)
is bounded in
L^{1}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})
on the diagonal
r=s
, then by Young’s inequality, we would have that
\widetilde{W}
is bounded in
L^{p}\big(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega\big)
.
However, this is
not
true. In fact, as we will see from (
4.31
) below, for each fixed
k
,
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
has a singularity of the type

\displaystyle c(r-s)^{-1}

as
r
approaches
s
, which cannot be controlled by a
L^{1}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})
function on the diagonal. This fact also suggests that the intertwining operators
\widetilde{W}
and
\widetilde{W}^{*}
are Calderón–Zygmund singular integral operators on the radial part in each subspace. Hence, our second ingredient in proving the
L^{p}
-boundedness is the Calderón–Zygmund theory.

If we directly estimate each
k
th part in
r
using Calderón–Zygmund theory and Minkowski’s inequality, we would have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{W}f(r,\omega)\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big\|\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)\big\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})}.

That is, the
L^{p}
norm would go inside of the sum, making it impossible to recover the
L^{p}
norm of
f
. However, if we estimate the angular part first, the resulting bound is expressed in terms of
1/|r-s|
instead of
1/(r-s)
and we will not be able to take advantage of the cancellation in
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
.

One special case which can be easily proved is when the kernels are of the variable-separated form
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)=F(r,s)M(k)
, where
F(r,s)
defines a Calderón–Zygmund operator and
\{M(k)\}_{k\geq 0}
satisfies the condition of Theorem
2.12
. In this situation, the operator
\widetilde{W}
can be written as

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}f(r,\omega)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(r,s)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}M(k)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}.

Using Calderón–Zygmund theory, we have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{W}f(r,\omega)\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})}\leq\Big\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}M(k)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)\Big\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})}.

Furthermore, by Theorem
2.12
, we would get the boundedness in
L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)
. This easy case motivates us to approximate the
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
with variable-separated kernels to capture the singularity in the radial part. The remainder term has an
L^{1}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})
kernel which guarantees that we can estimate the angular part first.

Having the previous ideas in mind, the main difficulty in proving Theorem
1.1
is in checking that the conditions for the discrete multiplier theorem are valid. To obtain this, we need to use the power series expansion for hypergeometric functions, and the asymptotic expansions for Gamma functions and its derivatives. This step forms the main part of the proof.

Main result 2.

Now we give our second theorem, which improves the previous theorem to the scale of Sobolev spaces. Define

\displaystyle\nu_{0}=\sqrt{\big(\tfrac{d-2}{2}\big)^{2}+a}.

(1.10)

Theorem 1.3

(
W^{s,p}
-boundedness)
.

Assume that
d\geq 2
and
a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
.

(1)

Let
-d<\alpha<2+2\nu_{0}
. The stationary wave operators
W_{\pm}
are bounded in
{W}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if
p
satisfies

\max\Big\{0,\frac{\sigma}{d},\frac{\sigma+\alpha}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{d},\frac{d+\alpha}{d}\Big\}.

(1.11)

(2)

Let
-2\nu_{0}-2<\beta<d
. The stationary wave operators
W_{\pm}^{*}
are bounded in
{W}^{\beta,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if
p
satisfies

\max\Big\{0,\frac{\sigma}{d},\frac{\beta}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{d},\frac{d-\sigma+\beta}{d}\Big\}.

(1.12)

Proof sketch for Theorem
1.3

To prove the
W^{s,p}
-boundedness of the wave operators, we introduce the following Riesz-type operators
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
formally given by

\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\mathcal{L}_{a}^{-\alpha/2},\quad-d<\alpha<2\nu_{0}+2,\ \alpha\neq 0,

and their inverse operators

\mathcal{R}^{-\beta}=\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\beta/2}(-\Delta)^{-\beta/2},\quad-2-2\nu_{0}<\beta<d,\ \beta\neq 0.

The precise definitions are given in Section
5
.

For these operators, we have the following
L^{p}
-boundedness results.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
.

(1)

Let
-d<\alpha<2+2\nu_{0}
and
\alpha\neq 0
. The operator
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
given by (
5.1
) is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\max\Big\{0,\frac{\sigma+\alpha}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{d},\frac{d+\alpha}{d}\Big\}.

(1.13)

(2)

Let
-2\nu_{0}-2<\beta<d
and
\beta\neq 0
. The operator
\mathcal{R}^{-\beta}
given by (
5.2
) is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\max\Big\{0,\frac{\beta}{d},\frac{\sigma}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma+\beta}{d}\Big\}.

(1.14)

Once we prove Proposition
1.4
, combining it with the
L^{p}
-boundedness of wave operators immediately gives the
W^{s,p}
-boundedness for
W_{\pm}
and
W^{*}_{\pm}
: for
s\neq 0
satisfying
-d<s<2\nu_{0}+2
, and

p
as in (
1.11
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|W_{\pm}f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&=\big\||\nabla|^{s}W_{\pm}|\nabla|^{-s}|\nabla|^{s}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&=\big\||\nabla|^{s}\mathcal{L}_{a}^{-s/2}W_{\pm}|\nabla|^{s}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\lesssim\|W_{\pm}|\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{aligned}

(1.15)

This proves the result, because boundedness in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
implies boundedness in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, to prove Theorem
1.3
, we only need to prove Proposition
1.4
.

The operators
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{R}^{-\beta}
are essentially Mellin multipliers on each subspace of spherical harmonics. The kernel functions of
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{R}^{-\beta}
are given by Fox
H
-functions
H_{4,4}^{2,2}(k;r,s)
, which plays the same role as the hypergeometric functions in the study of wave operators. Using the residue theorem,
H_{4,4}^{2,2}
can be written as power series when
0<\frac{r}{s}<1
and when
\frac{r}{s}>1
. At the point
r=s
, we can prove that they have a singularity of type

\displaystyle c(r-s)^{-1},

see Lemma
5.4
below. These facts allow us to essentially re-use the proof of the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
W_{\pm}
and
W^{*}_{\pm}
to show the boundedness of
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{R}^{-\beta}
. The details are given in Section
5
.

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{R}^{-\beta}
is also closely related to the equivalence of Sobolev norms as studied in
[
22
]
, where the authors utilized generalized Gaussian kernel estimates for the operator
e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{a}}
and a perturbative method to obtain the equivalence in a restricted range
\alpha\in(0,2)
. In this paper, we directly handle these operators using spherical harmonics and Fox
H
-functions, which enable us to extend the range of the fractional powers to
-2-2\nu_{0}<\beta<d
and
-d<\alpha<2+2\nu_{0}
. These bounds on
\alpha
and
\beta
arise naturally in order to guarantee that the Fox
H
-functions are well-defined. In particular, the equivalence of Sobolev norms hold for a larger range of
\alpha
. To be precise, we have the following:

Corollary 1.5

(Equivalence of Sobolev norms)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
a\geq-(\frac{d-2}{2})^{2}
.

(1)

Let
-d<\alpha<2+2\nu_{0}
and
\alpha\neq 0
. Further assume that
p
satisfies

\max\Big\{0,\frac{\sigma+\alpha}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{d},\frac{d+\alpha}{d}\Big\}.

Then for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
,

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.16)

(2)

Let
-2\nu_{0}-2<\beta<d
and
\beta\neq 0
. Further assume that p satisfies

\max\Big\{0,\frac{\beta}{d},\frac{\sigma}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma+\beta}{d}\Big\}.

Then for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\beta/2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{\beta/2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.17)

Remark 1.6
.

We want to emphasize part
(2)
of Corollary
1.5
in dimension
d=2
: in this case,
a\geq 0
, and our result simply says that
\mathcal{R}^{-1}
is
L^{p}
-bounded for
1<p<2
. In particular, this implies that for
1<p<2

\displaystyle\big\||x|^{-1}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(1.18)

This is consistent with the fact that Hardy’s inequality, which is (
1.18
) at the
p=2
endpoint, is false. In this sense, the range for which we proved the inequality (
1.17
) is sharp in dimension two.

1.3.
Applications

Since
\mathcal{L}_{a}
is a positive self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By the spectral theorem, for any bounded Borel function
F
, we can define the operator
F(\mathcal{L}_{a})
by the formula

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{L}_{a})=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\lambda)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\mathcal{L}_{a}}(\lambda).

By the intertwining property
W_{\pm}(-\Delta)=\mathcal{L}_{a}W_{\pm}
and the spectral theorem, we have
W\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{-\Delta}(\lambda)=\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\mathcal{L}_{a}}(\lambda)W
. Hence,

F(\mathcal{L}_{a})=W_{\pm}F(H_{0})W_{\pm}^{*}.

(1.19)

Application 1: Dispersive estimates.

Let
e^{it\mathcal{L}_{a}}f
be the solution of the free Schrödinger equation with inverse-square potential and initial data
f
.
Using (
1.19
) and the
L^{p}
-
L^{p^{\prime}}
estimates for the free Schrödinger operator
e^{-it\Delta}
,

\big\|e^{-it\Delta}f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim|t|^{-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\forall\;t\neq 0

with
1\leq p\leq 2,
and by Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the dispersive estimates for the Schrödinger operator
e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{a}}
as follows:

Corollary 1.7

(Dispersive estimate for Schrödinger equations)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
and
\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{p}<\min\big\{1,\tfrac{d-\sigma}{d}\big\}
. Then, there holds

\displaystyle\big\|e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{a}}f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim

\displaystyle|t|^{-d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\forall\;t\neq 0.

(1.20)

For the wave propagator, the following dispersive estimate

\Big\|\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta})}{\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\Big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim|t|^{-(d-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\theta_{p,d},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\forall\;t\neq 0,

(1.21)

is valid, where

\displaystyle\theta_{p,d}=\tfrac{d+1}{p}-\tfrac{d+3}{2}.

(1.22)

This inequality together with Theorem
1.3
yields the dispersive estimates for the wave propagator
\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}
.

Corollary 1.8

(Dispersive estimate for wave equations)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
and
\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\tfrac{d-\sigma}{d}\Big\}
. If further assume that

\frac{1}{p}>\frac{3}{2}-\frac{d}{2}+\sigma
, then there holds

\displaystyle\Big\|\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}f\Big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim

\displaystyle|t|^{-(d-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\big\|f\big\|_{\dot{W}^{\theta_{p,d},p}},\quad\forall\;t\neq 0.

(1.23)

Proof.

By (
1.19
), we have

\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}=W_{\pm}\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta})}{\sqrt{-\Delta}}W^{*}_{\pm}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Big\|\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}})}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}f\Big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|W_{\pm}\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta})}{\sqrt{-\Delta}}W_{\pm}^{*}f\Big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\tfrac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta})}{\sqrt{-\Delta}}W_{\pm}^{*}f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(1.24)

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-(d-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|W_{\pm}^{*}f\|_{\dot{W}^{\theta_{p,d},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-(d-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\theta_{p,d},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.25)

where in (
1.24
), we used that
\max\Big\{0,\frac{\sigma}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{d}\Big\}
, and in (
1.25
), we used that

-2\nu_{0}-2<\theta_{p,d}<d,\max\Big\{0,\tfrac{\theta_{p,d}}{d},\tfrac{\sigma}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{p}<\min\Big\{1,\tfrac{d-\sigma}{d},\tfrac{d-\sigma+\theta_{p,d}}{d}\Big\}.\qed

Application 2: Strichartz estimates.

Using the classical Strichartz estimates for the free Schrödinger operator

(see Keel–Tao

[

]
)

\displaystyle\|e^{-it\Delta}f\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

with
(q,r)\in\Lambda_{0}\coloneq\big\{(q,r):\;q,r\geq 2,\;\tfrac{2}{q}=d\big(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{r}\big),\;(q,r,d)\neq(2,\infty,2)\big\}
, we recover the Strichartz estimates for
e^{it\mathcal{L}_{a}}

Corollary 1.10

(Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger equations)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
a\geq-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
. Then for any
(q,r)\in\Lambda_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{a}}f\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.26)

Proof.

Using the condition
\frac{1}{r}\in\left(\frac{\sigma}{d},\frac{d-\sigma}{d}\right)
, the spectral theorem gives that

\displaystyle\|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{a}}f\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|We^{-it\Delta}W^{*}f\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{-it\Delta}W^{*}f\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad

\displaystyle\lesssim\|W^{*}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\qed

Let
I
be a time interval and let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Schwartz solution to the homogeneous wave equation
\partial_{tt}u-\Delta u=0
with the initial data
(f,g)
. Then we have the estimates

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{s-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

whenever
s\geq 0
,
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
2\leq r<\infty
obey the scaling condition

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d}{2}-s,

and the wave admissibility conditions

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d-1}{2r}\leq\frac{d-1}{4}.

One could also obtain the following Strichartz estimates for the wave equation with inverse-square potential using Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.11

(Strichartz estimates for wave equation)
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
a\geq-\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}
, and
u
be the solution of wave equation
\partial_{tt}u-\mathcal{L}_{a}u=0
with Cauchy data
(f,g)\in\dot{W}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\dot{W}^{s-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
0<s<\frac{d}{2}
. Let
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
2\leq r<\infty
such that
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d-1}{r}\leq\frac{d-1}{2}\ (q>2,\ \text{if}\ d=3\ \text{and}\ q>4,\ \text{if }\ d=2)
. Then,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{s-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.27)

where
s=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{d}{r}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{u}
be the solution of the wave equation without potential with Cauchy data
(W^{*}f,W^{*}g)
, then
(W^{*}f,W^{*}g)\in\dot{W}^{s,2}\times\dot{W}^{s-1,2}
. By

-boundedness of
W
and
W^{*}
, since we have

\max\Big\{0,\frac{\sigma}{d},\frac{s}{d}\Big\}<\frac{1}{r}<\min\Big\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{d},\frac{d-\sigma+s-1}{d}\Big\},

it follows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{t}L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle=\|W\tilde{u}\|_{L^{q}_{t}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{q}_{t}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|W^{*}f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|W^{*}g\|_{\dot{W}^{s-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|g\|_{\dot{W}^{s-1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}).\qed

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first recall some basic properties of gamma, Bessel and hypergeometric functions and Fox
H
-functions. Then, we prove the
L^{p}
-boundedness of a singular integral operator, and recall a discrete multiplier theorem for spherical harmonics which is crucial in proving our main result.

2.1.
Gamma function and reciprocal gamma function.

For a complex number
z
with
\Re z>0
, the gamma function
\Gamma(z)
is defined by

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}\;dt,

and analytically continued to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dots\}
.
Let
b,c
be two positive constants. Then the following asymptotic expression is a generalization of the Stirling formula (see
[
41
]
),

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(z+b)}{\Gamma(z+c)}\simeq z^{b-c}\left[1+C_{1}\frac{1}{z}+C_{2}\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\ldots\right]

(2.1)

as
z\rightarrow\infty
along any curve joining
z=0
and
z=\infty
provided
z\neq-b,-b-1,-b-2,\ldots,
and
z\neq-c,-c-1,-c-2,\ldots,
where

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(b-c)(b+c-1)}{2},

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\binom{b-c}{2}\big[3(b+c-1)^{2}-(b-c+1)\big].

The reciprocal Gamma function
\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}
is an analytic function near
z=0
with the Taylor expansion

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}=z+\gamma z^{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}C_{j}z^{j},

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant, see
[
1
, p. 256]
.

2.2.
Polygamma function

As in
[
1
, p. 258, 260]
, polygamma functions
\psi^{(m)}(x)
of order
m
are defined as the
(m+1)
-th derivative of the logarithm of the gamma function:

\displaystyle\psi^{(0)}(x)

\displaystyle\mathrlap{\vcentcolon}=\psi(x)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\Gamma(x)},

\displaystyle\psi^{(m)}(x)

\displaystyle\mathrlap{\vcentcolon}=\frac{d^{m}\psi(x)}{dx^{m}},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 2.1
.

The polygamma function has the following asymptotic expansion as
|z|\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\psi^{(m)}(z)

\displaystyle\sim(-1)^{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+m-1)!B_{k}}{k!z^{k+m}},\quad m\geq 1,

\displaystyle\psi^{(0)}

\displaystyle\sim\ln(z)-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{k}}{kz^{k}},

where
B_{k}
are the Bernoulli numbers with
B_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}
.

2.3.
The Bessel functions

The Bessel function of the first kind of order
\nu

(
\Re\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
) is defined by (see
[
43
]
)

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(z)=\big(\frac{1}{2}z\big)^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{\big(\frac{1}{4}z^{2}\big)^{k}}{k!\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}.

(2.2)

The Bessel function of the second kind (a.k.a. Weber’s function) of order
\nu
is

\displaystyle Y_{\nu}(z)=\frac{J_{\nu}(z)\cos(\nu\pi)-J_{-\nu}(z)}{\sin(\nu\pi)}.

When
\nu
is an integer, the right hand side is given by the limiting value

\displaystyle Y_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\partial J_{\nu}(z)}{\partial\nu}\big|_{\nu=n}+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\pi}\frac{\partial J_{\nu}(z)}{\partial\nu}\big|_{\nu=-n}.

The Bessel function of the third kind (a.k.a. Hankel function) of order
\nu
is

\displaystyle H^{(1)}_{\nu}(z)=J_{\nu}(z)+iY_{\nu}(z),\quad H^{(2)}_{\nu}=J_{\nu}(z)-iY_{\nu}(z).

Lemma 2.2

(Watson
[
43
, p. 429]
)
.

The following equality is valid

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{t^{2}-r^{2}}J_{\nu}(at)J_{\nu}(bt)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2}\pi\mathrm{i}J_{\nu}(br)H_{\nu}^{(1)}(ar),b<a;\\
\frac{1}{2}\pi\mathrm{i}J_{\nu}(ar)H_{\nu}^{(1)}(br),a<b.\end{array}\right.

Another formula we need is the indefinite integral for products of two Bessel functions (see
[
34
, p. 241]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}_{\nu}(z)
and
\mathscr{D}_{\nu}(z)
be any nontrivial linear combination of
J_{\nu}(z),Y_{\nu}(z),
and
H_{\nu}^{(1)}(z)
(not necessarily different). Then

The Mellin transform of a function is defined by

We denote

\{Y_{k,l}\}_{k,l}

F(\omega)

F_{k,l}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}F(\omega)Y_{k,l}(\omega)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma({\omega})
.
We write
\mathbb{P}_{k}F
for the projection of
F
to the spherical harmonics of degree
k

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k}F(\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}F_{k,l}Y_{k,l}(\omega).

(2.14)

By making use of zonal harmonics
Z^{k}
,
F(\omega)
can also be written as

\displaystyle F(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}F(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Z^{k}(\theta\cdot\omega)F(\theta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(\theta).

(2.15)

See
[
39
, Chapter IV]
for more information about spherical harmonics and zonal harmonics.

Define the forward finite difference operator acting on sequences
(\mathcal{C}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
by

\mathfrak{D}\mathcal{C}_{k}\coloneq\mathcal{C}_{k+1}-\mathcal{C}_{k}

and inductively define the
N
th order forward finite difference operator

\mathfrak{D}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{k}\coloneq\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}^{N-1}\mathcal{C}_{k}),\quad N\in\mathbb{N}.

For the decomposition (
2.15
), we have the following multiplier theorem on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, which is crucial in proving our main result.

Theorem 2.12

(Bonami and Clerc,
[
5
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{C}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of complex numbers satisfying the conditions

(BCi)

\sup_{k}\left|\mathcal{C}_{k}\right|\lesssim 1
,

(BCii)

\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum\limits_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\left|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{k}\right|\lesssim 1
for
N=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1
.

Then for
1<p<\infty
, there holds

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}_{k}\mathbb{P}_{k}F\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}F\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}.

(2.16)

Remark 2.13
.

Notice that if (
BCi
) and (
BCii
) are valid, by interpolation, we have for all
1\leq m\leq N
,

\displaystyle\sup_{j}2^{j(m-1)}\sum\limits_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\left|\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{k}\right|\lesssim 1.

The following lemma gives a sufficient condition for (
BCii
) if the sequence can be viewed as a smooth function of
k
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Suppose that a bounded sequence
\mathcal{C}_{k}=\mathcal{C}(k)
for some smooth function
\mathcal{C}:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. If one has

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\mathcal{C}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\right|\lesssim_{N}\frac{1}{k^{N}},\quad N=\lfloor\tfrac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1,

then, there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{k}k^{N}|\mathfrak{D}{}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{k}|\lesssim_{N}1.

This estimate implies (
BCii
) for the sequence
\{\mathcal{C}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and Remark
2.13
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}{}\mathcal{C}(k)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{k}^{k+1}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}(y)dy\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{k}.

By induction, we have for
N=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1
,

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}{}^{N}\mathcal{C}(k)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{k}^{k+1}\int_{y_{1}}^{y_{1}+1}\dots\int_{y_{N-1}}^{y_{N-1}+1}\mathcal{C}^{(N)}(z)dzdy_{N-1}\dots dy_{1}\right|\lesssim k^{-N}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}|\mathfrak{D}{}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{k}|

\displaystyle<\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}2^{j}\sup_{2^{j}\leq k\leq 2^{j+1}}|\mathfrak{D}{}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{k}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{k}k^{N}|\mathfrak{D}{}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{k}|<\infty.\qed

Later, we need to determine whether the product of sequences satisfies the conditions (
BCi
) and (
BCii
) or not. This is achieved by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.15
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{G}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
both satisfy (
BCi
),
(\mathcal{G}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
satisfies (
BCii
), and
(\mathcal{F}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
satisfies the stronger condition that

\displaystyle\sup_{k}k^{m}\left|\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{F}_{k}\right|\lesssim 1,

(2.17)

for
1\leq m\leq N=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1
, then

\{\mathcal{C}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\coloneq\{\mathcal{G}_{k}\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
also satisfies the assumptions (
BCi
) and (
BCii
) in Theorem
2.12
.

Proof.

For
N=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1
, using the Leibniz rule for finite difference, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}^{N}(\mathcal{F}_{k}\mathcal{G}_{k})=\sum_{m=0}^{N}\binom{N}{m}\mathfrak{D}^{N-m}\mathcal{F}_{k}\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{G}_{k+N-m}.

Hence, by Remark
2.13
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}|\mathfrak{D}^{N}(\mathcal{F}_{k}\mathcal{G}_{k})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\left|\sum_{m=0}^{N}\binom{N}{m}\mathfrak{D}^{N-m}\mathcal{F}_{k}\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{G}_{k+N-m}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{j}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\left|\sum_{m=0}^{N}\binom{N}{m}2^{j(N-m)}\mathfrak{D}^{N-m}\mathcal{F}_{k}\ 2^{j(m-1)}\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{G}_{k+N-m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{N}\max_{1\leq m\leq N}\sup_{j}2^{jm}\left|\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{F}_{2^{j}}\right|\sup_{j}\sum_{k=2^{j}+N-m}^{2^{j+1}+N-m}\Big|\sum_{m=0}^{N}\ 2^{j(m-1)}\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{G}_{k}\Big|\lesssim_{N}1.\qed

3.
Construction of stationary wave operators

In this section, we construct two operators
W
and
W^{*}
which “intertwine”
-\Delta
and
\mathcal{L}_{a}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Later, we prove that these two intertwining operators are the wave operators
W_{-}
and
W_{-}^{*}
as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) defined by the stationary method which is introduced by Kato and Kuroda
[
18
]
for
d\geq 2
.

3.1.
Spherical harmonics decomposition of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Using the spherical harmonics expansion, for any function
f(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we can decompose it as

\displaystyle f(r,\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega)\eqqcolon\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)

(3.1)

where
r=|x|,\omega=\frac{x}{|x|}
, and each
f_{k,l}(r)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r)
.
For this decomposition, we have the following identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f_{k,l}(r)\right|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r}.

This gives an isometric isomorphism between
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the countable direct sum of
\mathscr{H}_{k,l}=\{f(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega)|\;f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r})\}\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r})
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{k,l}\mathscr{H}_{k,l}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\{f_{k,l}\}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k<\infty\\
1\leq l\leq d_{k}\end{subarray}}.

3.2.
Bessel transform and Hankel transform

Each
\mathscr{H}_{k,l}
reduces
-\Delta
to

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}\

\displaystyle\mathrlap{\vcentcolon}=-\Delta|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}=-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r^{2}}-\frac{d-1}{r}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(k(k+d-2)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r^{2}}-\frac{d-1}{r}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{d-2}{2}+k\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}\right].

If we denote
\mu_{k}=\frac{d-2}{2}+k
, then
\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}
has the generalized eigenfunction,

\displaystyle e_{k}(r,\lambda)=(\lambda r)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}J_{\mu_{k}}\left(\lambda r\right)\quad\text{with eigenvalue }\lambda^{2}>0

in the sense

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}e_{k}(r,\lambda)=\lambda^{2}e_{k}(r,\lambda),

(3.2)

where the
J_{\mu_{k}}
are Bessel functions of the first kind of order
\mu_{k}
as in Subsection
2.3
.
Now we define the
Bessel transform
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r})
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f(\lambda)\coloneq\langle f(r),e_{k}(r,\lambda)\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(r)(\lambda r)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}J_{\mu_{k}}(r\lambda)r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r},

(3.3)

here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r})
.

Lemma 3.1

(Properties of the Bessel Transform)
.

There holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f(\lambda)|^{2}\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r};

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}

\displaystyle=I;

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}(\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}f)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f(\lambda);

(3.6)

Proof.

Using the integral formula

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda J_{\nu}(s\lambda)J_{\nu}(r\lambda)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda=\frac{\delta(r-s)}{s},

we can easily obtain (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) by the definition of Bessel transform.

Since

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}(\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}f)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}f(r),e_{k}(r,\lambda)\rangle=\langle f(r),\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}e_{k}(r,\lambda)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f(r),\lambda^{2}e_{k}(r,\lambda)\rangle=\lambda^{2}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f(\lambda),

we get (
3.6
).
∎

Restricting the operator
\mathcal{L}_{a}
to
\mathscr{H}_{k,l}
, we have

{\mathbb{H}_{k,l}}\coloneq\mathcal{L}_{a}|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}=-\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r^{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{d-2}{2}+k\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}+a\right],

with the generalized eigenfunction

\displaystyle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)=(\lambda r)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}J_{\nu_{k}}\left(\lambda r\right)\text{, with eigenvalue }\lambda^{2}>0,

such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{k,l}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)=\lambda^{2}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda).

(3.7)

Here
\nu_{k}=\sqrt{(\frac{d-2}{2}+k)^{2}+a}
.
We define the Hankel transform on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r})
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}f(\lambda):=\langle f(r),\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\lambda)(r\lambda)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r}.

(3.8)

By the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we have the following properties of the Hankel transform.

Lemma 3.2

(Properties of the Hankel transform)
.

One has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}f(\lambda)|^{2}\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r};

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}

\displaystyle=I;

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}(\mathbb{H}_{k,l}f)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}f(\lambda).

(3.11)

3.3.
Construction of intertwining operators

Now we define the operators
W_{k}=\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}
and
W^{*}_{k}=\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}
on each
\mathscr{H}_{k,l}
. Both operators are unitary on
\mathscr{H}_{k,l}
by (
3.4
) and (
3.9
), in the sense that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|W_{k}f(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r},

(3.12)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|W_{k}^{*}f(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r}.

(3.13)

Next, we utilize the spherical harmonics decomposition to define two operators
W,W^{*}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\displaystyle Wf(r,\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}W_{k}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}W_{k}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega),

(3.14)

\displaystyle W^{*}f(r,\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}W^{*}_{k}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}W_{k}^{*}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega),

(3.15)

where
\mathbb{P}_{k}
is as in (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.3

(
L^{2}
boundedness of wave operator and intertwining property)
.

The operators
W
and
W^{*}
given by (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) are unitary on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and have the following intertwining property

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}W=W(-\Delta),\quad W^{*}\mathcal{L}_{a}=(-\Delta)W^{*}.

(3.16)

Proof.

By (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\|Wf(r,\omega)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|W_{k}f_{k,l}(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f_{k,l}(r)|^{2}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{r}

\displaystyle=\|f(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

This implies that
W
is a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

To prove
\mathcal{L}_{a}W=W(-\Delta),
note that for any
f(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle W(-\Delta)f(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=W(-\Delta)(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega))

\displaystyle=W(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\left[\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}\mathbb{H}_{0;k,l}f_{k,l}(\lambda)\right](r)Y_{k,l}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\left[\lambda^{2}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f_{k,l}(\lambda)\right](r)Y_{k,l}(\omega).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a}Wf(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{a}W(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{a}(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbb{H}_{k,l}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\mathbb{H}_{k,l}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f_{k,l}(r)Y_{k,l}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathscr{H}_{\nu_{k}}\left[\lambda^{2}\mathscr{B}_{\mu_{k}}f_{k,l}(\lambda)\right](r)Y_{k,l}(\omega).

Hence,
W(-\Delta)=\mathcal{L}_{a}W
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By the same argument, we obtain the desired result for the operator
W^{\ast}
.

Therefore, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

3.4.
The equivalence to the wave operators

In this subsection, we prove that the intertwining operators
W
and
W^{*}
defined by (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) are in fact the stationary wave operators
W_{-}
and
W_{-}^{*}
given by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), i.e.

W=W_{-},\quad W^{*}=W_{-}^{*}.

(3.17)

We use
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{-\Delta}}(\lambda)
and
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}(\lambda)
to denote the spectral measure for
\sqrt{-\Delta}
and
\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}
. By previous eigenfunction expansions (see also
[
18
]
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{-\Delta}}(\lambda)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}\bigg|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}f(r)=\langle e_{k}(r,\lambda),f(r)\rangle e_{k}(r,\lambda)\lambda^{d-1},

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}(\lambda)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}\bigg|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}f(r)=\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),f(r)\rangle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\lambda^{d-1}.

The spectral measures for
-\Delta
and
\mathcal{L}_{a}
are given by

\displaystyle{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{{-\Delta}}(\lambda^{2})}={\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{-\Delta}}(\lambda)},\text{ and}\quad\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\mathcal{L}_{a}}(\lambda^{2})={\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}(\lambda)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{-\Delta}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}(\lambda^{2})=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{-\Delta}}(\lambda)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda},\ \text{and}\quad\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}(\lambda^{2})=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}(\lambda)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}.

Thus, the wave operator
W_{-}
is also given by

\displaystyle W_{-}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\mathcal{L}_{a}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}(\lambda^{2})(\mathcal{L}_{a}-\lambda^{2})R_{0}(\lambda^{2}-i0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\lambda^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}_{a}}(\lambda)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda}(\mathcal{L}_{a}-\lambda^{2})R_{0}(\lambda^{2}-i0)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(3.18)

By the functional calculus, on each
\mathscr{H}_{k,l}
, we have

\displaystyle R_{0}(\lambda^{2}-i\varepsilon)|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}f(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle e_{k}(r,\mu),f(r)\rangle e_{k}(r,\mu)\mu^{d-1}}{\mu^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-i\varepsilon)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu.

(3.19)

To prove
W=W_{-}
and
W^{*}=W_{-}^{*}
, it suffices to prove that on each spherical harmonic subspace
\mathscr{H}_{k,l}
,

\displaystyle W_{-}|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}=W_{k},\ \text{and},\ W_{-}^{*}|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}=W_{k}^{*}.

(3.20)

Using the following limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon f(\mu)}{(\mu^{2}-\lambda^{2})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{f(\lambda)}{\lambda},

(3.21)

and by (
3.18
), (
3.19
), we have

\displaystyle\quad W_{-}|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}f(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),(\mathbb{H}_{k,l}-\lambda^{2})R_{0}(\lambda^{2}-i0)f(r)\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),(\mathbb{H}_{k,l}-\lambda^{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{\langle e_{k}(r,\mu),f(r)\rangle e_{k}(r,\mu)\mu^{d-1}}{\mu^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-i\varepsilon)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\right\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{\langle e_{k}(r,\mu),f(r)\rangle(\mu^{2}-\lambda^{2}+V(r))e_{k}(r,\mu)\mu^{d-1}}{\mu^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-i\varepsilon)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\right\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),\int_{0}^{\infty}\tfrac{\langle e_{k}(r,\mu),f(r)\rangle(\mu^{2}-\lambda^{2}+V(r))e_{k}(r,\mu)\mu^{d-1}}{\mu^{2}-\lambda^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\right\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{i\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),{\langle e(r,\lambda),f(r)\rangle V(r)e_{k}(r,\lambda)\lambda^{d-2}}\right\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\textup{I}+\textup{II}-\textup{III},

where

I

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),\int_{0}^{\infty}{\langle e_{k}(r,\mu),f(r)\rangle e_{k}(r,\mu)\mu^{d-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\right\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda,

II

\displaystyle={\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle e_{k}(r,\mu),f(r)\rangle V(r)e_{k}(r,\mu)\mu^{d-1}}{\mu^{2}-\lambda^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\right\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda},

III

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda)\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{k}(r,\lambda),{\langle e(r,\lambda),f(r)\rangle V(r)e_{k}(r,\lambda)\lambda^{d-2}}\right\rangle\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Using the properties (
3.5
) and (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\textup{I}=f(r).

(3.22)

To estimate
II
, by Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

II

\displaystyle=r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\rho J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)J_{\nu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)

\displaystyle\quad\times{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu}{\mu^{2}-\lambda^{2}}J_{\mu_{k}}(s\mu)J_{\mu_{k}}(\rho\mu)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu}V(\rho){\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda f(s)\lambda s^{d/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\frac{i\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\rho J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho J_{\nu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)\Big\{J_{\mu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)H_{\mu_{k}}^{(1)}(s\lambda){{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\rho<s\}}}

\displaystyle\quad+J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)H_{\mu_{k}}^{(1)}(\rho\lambda){{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\rho>s\}}}\Big\}V(\rho){\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda f(s)s^{d/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

Using the equality (

\displaystyle\frac{i\pi a}{2}J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\nu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)J_{\mu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}

\displaystyle-\frac{a\pi}{2}\left[\int_{0}^{s}Y_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)J_{\mu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)J_{\nu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}+\int_{s}^{\infty}J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)Y_{\mu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)J_{\nu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\pi a}{2}J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\nu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)J_{\mu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}+J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)-J_{\nu_{k}}(s\lambda).

Putting
\textup{II}^{\text{in}}(s,\lambda)
into

gives

\displaystyle=\frac{i\pi a}{2}r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\nu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)J_{\mu_{k}}(\rho\lambda)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}f(s)s^{d/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)f(s)s^{d/2}\lambda\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)J_{\nu_{k}}(s\lambda)f(s)s^{d/2}\lambda\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\textup{III}+W_{k}f(r)-f(r).

Summing
\textup{I},\textup{II}
and

, we have

\displaystyle W_{-}|_{\mathscr{H}_{k,l}}=W_{k}.

Hence the operator

defined by (

) equals to

. A similar calculation shows that

.

4.
Proof of the
L^{p}
-boundedness

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. Since

\displaystyle\|W_{+}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\overline{W_{+}f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|{W_{-}\overline{f}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and

\displaystyle\|W_{+}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|\overline{W_{+}^{*}f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|{W_{-}^{*}\overline{f}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

it suffices to show the
L^{p}
-boundedness for
W_{-}
and
W_{-}^{*}
.
By (
3.17
), it is equivalent to prove the
L^{p}
-boundedness for
W
and
W^{*}
. We only give the proof for
W
, since the proof for
W^{*}
is exactly the same.

By definition (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle Wf(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\lambda s)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)s^{d-1}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}(\lambda r)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}J_{\nu_{k}}(\lambda r)\lambda^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{1-\frac{d}{2}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{d}{2}}\lambda J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}}\;\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{k}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s},

with

\displaystyle K_{k}(r,s)=\frac{s^{\frac{d}{2}+1}}{r^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}.

To prove

\|Wf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

it is equivalent to show

\displaystyle\big\|r^{\frac{d}{p}}Wf(r,\omega)\big\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}\leq\big\|r^{\frac{d}{p}}f(r,\omega)\big\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}.

(4.1)

We define the modified kernels

\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)=\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{-\frac{d}{p}}K_{k}(r,s),

and the modified wave operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}f(r,\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

(4.2)

Then, it suffices to prove that

\big\|\widetilde{W}f(r,\omega)\big\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}\lesssim\|f(r,\omega)\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}.

(4.3)

In order to give the explicit formula for the kernel functions
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
, we introduce
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
, and give some estimates for of
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
and its derivatives. Recall
\mu_{k}=\frac{d-2}{2}+k
and
\nu_{k}=\sqrt{(\frac{d-2}{2}+k)^{2}+a}
. We denote

\displaystyle a_{k}=\frac{\mu_{k}+\nu_{k}}{2},\quad b_{k}=\frac{\mu_{k}-\nu_{k}}{2},

(4.4)

and then we have the following estimates.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
2\leq N\leq\big\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\big\rfloor+1
,

\displaystyle|a_{k}|\lesssim k,\quad\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}\right|\lesssim 1,\quad\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}a_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{k^{N}},

(4.5)

and for
0\leq N\leq\big\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\big\rfloor+1
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}b_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

(4.6)

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\mu_{k}
and
\nu_{k}
satisfy
\mu_{k},\nu_{k}\lesssim k
. Also,
|\mu_{k}-\nu_{k}|=|\frac{-a}{\mu_{k}+\nu_{k}}|\lesssim\frac{1}{k}
, and
\frac{d\nu_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}=\frac{\mu_{k}}{\nu_{k}}
, thus (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) are valid. ∎

Explicit formula for the kernel functions

By Lemma
2.4
,

\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
can be written in terms of
{}_{2}F_{1}
as follows:

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)=\frac{s^{\frac{d}{2}+1-\frac{d}{p}}}{r^{\frac{d}{2}-1-\frac{d}{p}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda J_{\mu_{k}}(s\lambda)J_{\nu_{k}}(r\lambda)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}+1+\mu_{k}}\frac{\Gamma(a_{k}+1)}{\Gamma(a_{k}+b_{k}+1)\Gamma(-b_{k})}\ {{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(a_{k}+1,b_{k}+1;a_{k}+b_{k}+1;\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2}\right),\\
\hfill 0<s<r;\\
2{\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{d}{2}+1+\nu_{k}}}\frac{\Gamma(a_{k}+1)}{\Gamma(a_{k}-b_{k}+1)\Gamma(b_{k})}\ {{}_{2}F_{1}}\left(a_{k}+1,1-b_{k};a_{k}-b_{k}+1;\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{2}\right),\\
\hfill 0<r<s;\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}+1+\mu_{k}}\frac{\sin(-\pi b_{k})}{\pi}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a_{k}+n+1)\Gamma(b_{k}+n+1)}{\Gamma(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)\Gamma(n+1)}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2n},&0<s<r;\\

(4.7)

For ease of notation, we define for

Then the kernels

can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)=\begin{cases}2\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}+1+\mu_{k}}\frac{\sin(-\pi b_{k})}{\pi}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{k,n}^{+}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2n},&0<s<r;\\

Using the definition of
{}_{2}F_{1}

Lemma 4.2
.

The kernel functions
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
have the following asymptotic behavior:

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)\sim\begin{cases}\frac{2\Gamma(\frac{\mu_{k}+\nu_{k}}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\mu_{k}+1)\Gamma(\frac{\nu_{k}-\mu_{k}}{2})}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}+1+\mu_{k}},&\frac{s}{r}\to 0;\\

The asymptotic behavior of

Case 3:
\frac{1}{2}<\frac{s}{r}<2

Now we deal with the term

. From Lemma

, we know that the kernels have a singularity of type

at

, and we cannot get a uniform bound in

for

. Instead, we can use the theory of Calderón–Zygmund operators to prove the

\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
, we define an approximate kernel
\widetilde{K}_{k}^{\text{ap}}(r,s)
with the property that the index
k
and variables

are separated, and that they also have the same singularity as
\widetilde{K}_{k}(r,s)
. For the approximating operator, we first estimate the radial part. After that, we use the multiplier theorem for spherical harmonics to get the boundedness for the angular part. For the error term, we will estimate the angular part first, then the radial part using Young’s inequality.

In the remainder of this paper, we use the shorthand notation
\chi_{+}=\chi_{\{r/2<s<r\}}

and

Before we give the details for the proof, we introduce a few lemmas to estimate the finite differences.

Lemma 4.3
.

The following sequences

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}^{+}(r,s)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k}}\chi_{+},

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{k}}}^{+}(r,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(-\pi b_{k})}{\pi}\frac{1}{1-\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{{\mu_{k}-\mu_{0}}}-1\right]\chi_{+},

satisfy (
BCi
) and (
BCii
) with implicit constants that do not depend on
r,s
.

Proof.

Boundedness
: Notice that
\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k}}=(1-(1-\frac{s}{r}))^{\mu_{k}}\lesssim\min\{1,\mu_{k}(1-\frac{s}{r})\}
as
k
goes to
\infty
, this
gives the uniform bound for
\mathcal{T}_{k}^{+}(r,s)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{k}^{+}(r,s)
. Therefore, we only need to estimate their finite difference.

Finite difference estimate for
\mathcal{T}_{k}^{+}(r,s)
.
Notice that
\mu_{k+1}=\mu_{k}+1
, direct computation gives

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{k}^{+}|=\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k}}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{N}.

Thus, taking
N=\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{k}^{+}|\lesssim\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2^{j}}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{N}\frac{1-\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2^{j}}}{\left[1-\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)\right]}\lesssim 1.

The above bound is valid because
x^{N}k^{N}(1-x)^{k}
is uniformly bounded for
x\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by

\displaystyle x^{N}k^{N}(1-x)^{k}\leq(xk)^{N}e^{-kx}\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}t^{N}e^{-t}\lesssim_{N}1.

Finite difference estimate for
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{k}^{+}(r,s)
.
To estimate this term, let us denote

\mathcal{F}_{k}=\frac{\sin\pi b_{k}}{\pi}
and
\mathcal{G}_{k}=\frac{1}{1-\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{{\mu_{k}-\mu_{0}}}-1\right]\chi_{+}
. Using (

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\mathcal{F}_{k}|\leq\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\mathcal{F}_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

(4.16)

Hence, by the Leibniz rule for the finite difference, we have

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{k}^{+}|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{m=0}^{N}\binom{N}{m}\mathfrak{D}^{N-m}\left(\frac{\sin\pi b_{k}}{\pi}\right)\mathfrak{D}^{m}\mathcal{G}_{k+N-m}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{1-(\frac{s}{r})^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{N-m+1}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k+N-m}-\mu_{0}}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1-(\frac{s}{r})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{N+1}\left[\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k+N}-\mu_{0}}-1\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{1-(\frac{s}{r})^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{N-m+1}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k+N-m}-\mu_{0}}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{m}+k^{-N}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{k}^{+}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-(\frac{s}{r})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{N-m+1}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k+N-m}-\mu_{0}}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{m}+1

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{1-(\frac{s}{r})^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{N-m+1}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k+N-m}-\mu_{0}}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{m}+1

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{1-(\frac{s}{r})^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}(2^{-j})^{N-m+1}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{m}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2^{j}}\frac{1-\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{2^{j}}}{1-\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)}+1

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sup_{k}k^{(m-1)}\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)^{m-1}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{k}+1<\infty.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Later, we need to estimate the finite differences for the sequence

However,

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\gamma_{k}|\lesssim\frac{C}{k^{N+1}}.

(4.17)

In order to estimate the finite difference for some sequence which involves

, we first introduce the following lemma which can be proved by induction.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\theta\in(1/2,1)
and define
F(t)\coloneq 1-\theta^{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
N\geq 1
, there exist
L\in\mathbb{N}
and finite sequences
a^{N}_{\ell},b^{N}_{\ell,j},c^{N}_{\ell},d^{N}_{\ell,i},e^{N}_{\ell,i}
(
\ell=1,2,\dots,L
) such that (dropping the
N
index for legibility)

\displaystyle d_{\ell,i}\geq 1,

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=0}^{c_{\ell}}d_{\ell,i}=N,

(4.18)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}^{N}\theta^{\gamma_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}a_{\ell}\bigg(\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\theta^{b_{\ell,j}\gamma_{k+j}}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{i=0}^{c_{\ell}}F(\mathfrak{D}^{d_{\ell,i}}\gamma_{k+e_{\ell,i}})\bigg).

(4.19)

Proof.

This follows from repeated application of the following basic calculations: if
s_{k}
is any sequence, then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\theta^{s_{k}})

\displaystyle=\theta^{s_{k}}F(\mathfrak{D}s_{k}),

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}F(s_{k})

\displaystyle=-\theta^{s_{k}}F(\mathfrak{D}s_{k});

(4.21)

and the following Leibniz rule for the finite difference of product of
n

\mathfrak{D}(a^{1}_{k}\dots a^{n}_{k})=\sum_{r=1}^{n}a^{1}_{k}\dots a^{r-1}_{k}(\mathfrak{D}a^{r}_{k})a^{r+1}_{k+1}\dots a^{n}_{k+1}.

The base case
N=1
is (
4.20
) which is of the correct form (
4.19
). For
N=2
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}^{2}(\theta^{\gamma_{k}})=\mathfrak{D}(\theta^{\gamma_{k}}F(\mathfrak{D}\gamma_{k}))

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{D}\theta^{\gamma_{k}})F(\mathfrak{D}\gamma_{k+1})+\theta^{\gamma_{k}}\mathfrak{D}{}F(\mathfrak{D}{}\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle=\theta^{\gamma_{k}}F(\mathfrak{D}\gamma_{k})F(\mathfrak{D}\gamma_{k+1})-\theta^{\gamma_{k}}\theta^{\mathfrak{D}\gamma_{k}}F(\mathfrak{D}^{2}\gamma_{k})

(4.22)

which is again of the form (
4.19
). Inductively, if a derivative falls on a term
\theta^{\gamma_{k+j}}
, it creates a term
F(\mathfrak{D}\gamma_{k+j})
, or else it falls on a term
F(\mathfrak{D}^{d_{\ell,i}}\gamma_{k+e_{\ell,i}})
; in either case we obtain the new coefficients
d^{N+1}_{\ell,i}\geq 1
sum to
N
, as claimed.
∎

By Lemma
4.4
, (
4.17
) and the fact that for

\displaystyle|F(t)|\lesssim t(1-\theta),

we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}^{N}\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\gamma_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

), we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

The coefficients
h_{k,1n}^{\alpha},h_{k,2n}^{\alpha},C_{k}^{\alpha},A_{k,1n}^{\alpha},A_{k,2n}^{\alpha}
are uniformly bounded in
k
and
n
,

\displaystyle|h_{k,1n}^{\alpha}|,\ |h_{k,2n}^{\alpha}|,\ |C_{k}^{\alpha}|,\ |A_{k,1n}^{\alpha}|,\ |A_{k,2n}^{\alpha}|\lesssim 1.

(5.26)

For
C_{k}^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{N}C_{k}^{\alpha}}{dk^{N}}\lesssim_{N}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}\ \text{for all}\quad N\geq 0.

(5.27)

For each fixed
k
and as
n\to\infty
,
A^{\alpha}_{k,1n},A^{\alpha}_{k,2n}
have the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle A_{k,1n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=1+O_{\alpha,k}\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle A_{k,2n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=1+O_{\alpha,k}\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}\right).

By

, we mean that the implicit
O(\cdot)

\alpha
and
k
.

5.2.
Singularity at
r=s
of
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
.

To find the singularity at
r=s
, we notice that the Mellin multipliers
\mathcal{H}_{4,4}^{2,2}(k,\alpha;z)
are uniformly bounded in
k,n
, we conjecture that the singularity of
H_{4,4}^{2,2}(k,\alpha;r/s)
is
\frac{1}{r-s}
. In the following lemma, we give the proof.

Lemma 5.4
.

As
r\to s
, we have

\displaystyle K_{k}^{\alpha}(r,s)\sim\begin{cases}\frac{C_{k}^{\alpha}}{1-(\frac{s}{r})^{2}}\left\{\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\nu_{k}+{\lambda_{0}}+2}-\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k}+{\lambda_{0}}+\alpha+2}\right\},\ \text{as}\ \frac{s}{r}\to 1^{-};\\
\frac{C_{k}^{\alpha}}{1-(\frac{r}{s})^{2}}\left\{\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\mu_{k}-{\lambda_{0}}}-\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\nu_{k}-{\lambda_{0}}-\alpha}\right\},\ \text{as}\ \frac{r}{s}\to 1^{-}.\end{cases}

(5.28)

Proof.

For a Gauss series
G(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
,
assume that the convergence radius is
1
, and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=a
, then by Abel’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 1^{-}}(1-z)G(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\to 1^{-}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(a_{n+1}-a_{n})z^{n+1}+a_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(a_{n+1}-a_{n})+a_{0}=a.

From this result and the fact that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{k,1n}^{\alpha}=1,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{k,2n}^{\alpha}=1,

(5.29)

the lemma follows.
∎

This lemma implies that as
r
close to
s
,
K^{\alpha}_{k}(r,s)
has a singularity of type

\displaystyle H_{4,4}^{2,2}(k,\alpha;r,s)\sim(r-s)^{-1}\quad(r\to s).

(5.30)

5.3.
Proof of Proposition
1.4

Here we only give the details for
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
, as the proof for
\mathcal{R}^{-\beta}
is similar. We may also assume that
\alpha\neq 2m
where
m
is an integer; when
\alpha=2m
, the details are much simpler.
To obtain the
L^{p}
-boundedness, we use the power series representation (
5.15
) and the discrete multiplier theorem (Theorem
2.12
) for decompositions into spherical harmonics.
Similarly to the modified kernels in Section
4
, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k}(r,s)\coloneq\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{-\frac{d}{p}}K^{\alpha}_{k}(r,s),

and the modified operator

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\alpha}f(r,\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

Then
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\alpha}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)
. Due to the singularity at
r=s
of the kernel functions, we divide the operator into three parts

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\alpha}f(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{2}}\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{2r}\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{2r}^{\infty}\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}

\displaystyle=:\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\alpha}_{1}f(r,\omega)+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\alpha}_{2}f(r,\omega)+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\alpha}_{3}f(r,\omega).

We will prove that
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\alpha}_{i}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)
for
i=1,2,3
and for suitable
p
.

Case 1:
0<\frac{s}{r}<\frac{1}{2}
.

By Lemma
5.3
,
h_{k,1n}^{\alpha},h_{k,2n}^{\alpha}
are uniformly bounded in
k
and
n
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k,1}(r,s)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\nu_{k}+{\lambda_{0}}+2-\frac{d}{p}}+\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\nu_{0}+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}+\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{0}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}.

For the finite difference of
\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k,1}(r,s)
with
1\leq N\leq\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor+1
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{j}2^{j(N-1)}\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}|\mathfrak{D}^{N}\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k,1}(r,s)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{N-1}|\widetilde{K}^{\alpha}_{k,1}(r,s)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\ 2^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{N-1}\left[\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\nu_{k}+{\lambda_{0}}+2-\frac{d}{p}}+\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{k}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{N}\left[\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\nu_{0}+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}+\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{0}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}\right].

For the angular part, using the multiplier theorem, Theorem
2.12
,

\displaystyle\quad\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{K}_{k,1}^{\alpha}(r,s)\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\nu_{0}+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}+\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{0}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}\right]\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}.

To estimate the radial part, if

\nu_{0}+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}>0\ \text{and}\ \mu_{0}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}>0

(5.31)

then
\left[r^{\nu_{0}+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}+r^{\mu_{0}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}\right]\chi_{\{0<r<1/2\}}\in L^{1}\left(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\right)
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{1}^{\alpha}f(r,\omega)\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{0}^{r/2}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\nu_{0}+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}\right\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\int_{0}^{r/2}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\mu_{0}+\alpha+\lambda_{0}+2-\frac{d}{p}}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(s,\omega)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}{s}\right\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{k}f(r,\omega)\right\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}=\|f(r,\omega)\|_{L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)}.

That is, if
\frac{1}{p}<\min\{\frac{d+\alpha}{d},\frac{d-\sigma}{d}\}
, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{1}^{\alpha}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r}{r}\cdot\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega)
.

Lemma 5.5

For

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}E_{k,1n}^{\alpha}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{1}{k^{N}}\frac{1}{n+1},\quad\bigg|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}E_{k,2n}^{\alpha}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{1}{k^{N}}\frac{1}{n+1}.

Proof.

∎

Using Lemma

, a direct computation gives

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}E_{k,1}^{\alpha,+}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\bigg|,\ \bigg|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}E_{k,2}^{\alpha,+}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\bigg|

Appendix A

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

We only give the details for
A_{k,n}^{+}
, as the estimate for
A_{k,n}^{-}
is similar.

For
N=1
, using the definition of polygamma functions, we have

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{k,n}^{+}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}

\displaystyle=A_{k,n}^{+}\bigg[(\psi(a_{k}+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1))\frac{da_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(\psi(b_{k}+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)\frac{db_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}\bigg]

\displaystyle\eqqcolon A_{k,n}^{+}G_{n,k}.

The Leibniz rule gives

\displaystyle\frac{d^{N+1}A_{k,n}^{+}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{N}\binom{N}{m}\frac{d^{m}A_{k,n}^{+}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{m}}\frac{d^{N-m}G_{n,k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N-m}}=A_{k,n}^{+}\sum_{i=0}^{M(N)}C_{i,N}\prod_{j=0}^{N}(G_{n,k}^{(j)})^{\alpha_{i,j,N}},

where
\alpha_{i,j,N}
are integers depends on
i,j,N
and satisfying
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N}\alpha_{i,j,N}(1+j)=N+1
, and
C_{i,N},M(N)
are some constants which depends on
N
.

For
G_{n,k}
, we have the following estimate, with
z\in(a_{k}+n+1,a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)

and
\widetilde{z}\in(b_{k}+n+1,a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)
,

\displaystyle|G_{n,k}|\leq|\psi^{\prime}(z)b_{k}|\cdot\big|\tfrac{da_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}\big|+|\psi^{\prime}(\widetilde{z})a_{k}|\cdot\big|\tfrac{db_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}\big|.

To estimate the derivatives of
G_{n,k}
with respect to
k
, we denote

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{b}\psi(a_{k}+n+1)=\psi(a_{k}+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{a}\psi(b_{k}+n+1)=\psi(b_{k}+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1).

Then

\displaystyle G_{n,k}=\mathfrak{D}_{b}\psi(a_{k}+n+1)\frac{da_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}+\mathfrak{D}_{a}\psi(b_{k}+n+1)\frac{db_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}.

(A.1)

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}_{b}\psi(a_{k}+n+1)|\bigg|\frac{da_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}\bigg|\leq\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{n+1},\frac{1}{k}\bigg\}\frac{1}{k},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}_{a}\psi(b_{k}+n+1)|\bigg|\frac{db_{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k}\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k}.

By the Faá di Bruno formula, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\mathfrak{D}_{b}\psi(a_{k}+n+1)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}C_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)}+b_{k}^{(j)})^{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=\hskip-8.00003pt\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}}\hskip-15.00002ptC_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\!\big[\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}{+}n{+}1)-\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}{+}b_{k}{+}n{+}1)\big]\!\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\hskip-15.00002pt\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}}\hskip-13.00005ptC_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=0\end{subarray}}^{n_{j-1}}\!C_{\alpha_{j},n_{j}}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{\alpha_{j}}(b_{k}^{(j)})^{n_{j}-\alpha_{j}}.

Thus, by (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\mathfrak{D}_{b}\psi(a_{k}+n+1)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})+1}(z)|b_{k}|\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=0\end{subarray}}^{n_{j-1}}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}j\alpha_{j}+(j+1)(n_{j}-\alpha_{j})-\alpha_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})+1}}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}-m_{1}}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N\end{subarray},\alpha_{1}<N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})-1}}\frac{1}{k^{N+N-\alpha_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

(A.2)

Similarly, for
\mathfrak{D}_{a}\psi(b_{k}+n+1)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\mathfrak{D}_{a}\psi(b_{k}+n+1)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(b_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}C_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)}+b_{k}^{(j)})^{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(b_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}\hskip-10.00002ptC_{n_{1}\dots n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(a_{k}+b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=0\\
\end{subarray}}^{n_{j}}C_{\alpha_{j},n_{j}}a_{k}^{(j)(n_{j}-\alpha_{j})}b_{k}^{(j)\alpha_{j}}).

Again, (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) yields

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}\mathfrak{D}_{a}\psi(b_{k}+n+1)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}(j+1)m_{j}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N\ \\
\alpha_{1}\leq n_{1}\leq N\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{N}-1}}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}-(n_{1}-\alpha_{1})}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(n+1)k^{N-1}}.

(A.3)

Using Leibniz rule for (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle G_{n,k}^{(N)}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\!\binom{N}{j}\!\left((\mathfrak{D}_{b}\psi(a_{k}+n+1))^{(j)}a_{k}^{(N-j+1)}\!+\!(\mathfrak{D}_{a}\psi(b_{k}+n+1))^{(j)}b_{k}^{(N-j+1)}\right).

By (
A
) and (
A
), we get

\displaystyle|G_{n,k}^{(N)}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{j+1}}\frac{1}{k^{N-j+1}}+\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{j-1}}\frac{1}{k^{N-j+2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

(A.4)

In view of the boundedness of
A_{k,n}^{+}
, we have

A_{k,n}^{+(N+1)}\lesssim\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Appendix B

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

Using the asymptotics of the Gamma function, we know that
E_{k,1n}^{\alpha},E_{k,2n}^{\alpha}
are uniformly bounded in
n
and
k
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{dE_{k,1n}^{\alpha}}{dk}

\displaystyle=E_{k,1n}^{\alpha}\Big[\left\{(\psi(a_{k}+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1))\right\}\frac{da_{k}}{dk}

\displaystyle+\left\{(\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}-\alpha/2+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}-\alpha/2+n+1))\right\}\frac{da_{k}}{dk}

\displaystyle+\left\{\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)-\psi(-b_{k}+n+1)\right\}\frac{db_{k}}{dk}

\displaystyle+\left\{\psi(-b_{k}-\alpha/2+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}-\alpha/2+n+1)\right\}\frac{db_{k}}{dk}\Big]

\displaystyle\coloneq E_{k,1n}^{\alpha}G_{k,1n}^{\alpha}.

By induction, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{N}E_{k,1n}^{\alpha}}{dk^{N}}=E_{k,1n}^{\alpha}\sum_{i=0}^{M(N)}C_{i,N}\prod_{j=0}^{N}\left(\frac{d^{j}G_{k,1n}^{\alpha}}{dk^{j}}\right)^{\alpha_{i,j,N}}

(B.1)

where
\alpha_{i,j,N}
are integers that depend on
i,j,N
, satisfying
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N}\alpha_{i,j,N}(1+j)=N+1
; and
C_{i,N},M(N)
are some constants which depend on
N
.
It is easy to check that

\displaystyle|G_{k,1n}^{\alpha}|\lesssim\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{n+1}.

(B.2)

Next, we compute the derivatives of
G_{k,1n}^{\alpha}
.

Using the Faá di Bruno formula, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}{\left\{(\psi(a_{k}+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1))\right\}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}C_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)}-b_{k}^{(j)})^{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=\hskip-8.00003pt\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}}\hskip-15.00002ptC_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\!\big[\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}{+}n{+}1)-\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}{-}b_{k}{+}n{+}1)\big]\!\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\hskip-15.00002pt\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}}\hskip-13.00005ptC_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=0\end{subarray}}^{n_{j-1}}\!C_{\alpha_{j},n_{j}}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{\alpha_{j}}(b_{k}^{(j)})^{n_{j}-\alpha_{j}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}{\left\{(\psi(a_{k}+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1))\right\}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})+1}(z)|b_{k}|\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=0\end{subarray}}^{n_{j-1}}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}j\alpha_{j}+(j+1)(n_{j}-\alpha_{j})-\alpha_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})+1}}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}-m_{1}}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N\end{subarray},\alpha_{1}<N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})-1}}\frac{1}{k^{N+N-\alpha_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

An analogous calculation gives that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{N}{\left\{(\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}-\alpha/2+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}-\alpha/2+n+1))\right\}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}k^{N}}\lesssim\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

For
\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)-\psi(-b_{k}+n+1)
, Faá di Bruno formula gives

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d^{N}\{\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)-\psi(-b_{k}+n+1)\}}{dk^{N}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}C_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(a_{k}^{(j)}-b_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}C_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(-b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(-b_{k}^{(j)})^{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=\hskip-8.00003pt\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N}}\hskip-15.00002ptC_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}}\!\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j})}(a_{k}{-}b_{k}+n{+}1)\!\prod_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{\alpha_{j}=0}^{m_{j}}C_{\alpha_{j},m_{j}}(a_{k}^{(j)})^{\alpha_{j}}(b_{k}^{(j)})^{m_{j}-\alpha_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad-\hskip-15.00002pt\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}}\hskip-13.00005ptC_{n_{1},\dots,n_{N}}\psi^{(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j})}(-b_{k}+n+1)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(-b_{k}^{(j)})^{n_{j}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\quad\left|\frac{d^{N}\{\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)-\psi(-b_{k}+n+1)\}}{dk^{N}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jm_{j}=N,\alpha_{j}\leq m_{j}}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}-1}}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=0}^{N}j\alpha_{j}+(j+1)(m_{j}-\alpha_{j})-\alpha_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\sum_{j=1}^{N}jn_{j}=N}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{\sum_{j=1}^{N}(j+1)n_{j}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N-1}}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\quad\left|\frac{d^{N}\{\psi(-b_{k}-\alpha/2+n+1)-\psi(a_{k}-b_{k}+n+1)\}}{dk^{N}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N-1}}.

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d^{N}G_{k,1n}^{\alpha}}{dk^{N}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

(B.3)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d^{N}E_{k,1n}^{\alpha}}{dk^{N}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{1}{k^{N+1}}.

(B.4)

The estimates for
E_{k,2n}^{\alpha}
are similar, so we omit the details.

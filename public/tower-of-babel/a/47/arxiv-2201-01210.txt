Matrix inequalities and majorizations around Hermite-Hadamard’s inequality
Jean-Christophe Bourin and Eun-Young Lee

Abstract




Abstract.
We study the classical Hermite-Hadamard inequality in the matrix setting.
This leads to a number of interesting matrix inequalities such as the Schatten
p
-norm estimates

\left(\|A^{q}\|_{p}^{p}+\|B^{q}\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\|(xA+(1-x)B))^{q}\|_{p}+\|(1-x)A+xB)^{q}\|_{p}

for all positive (semidefinite)
n\times n
matrices
A,B
and
0<q,x<1
. A related decomposition, with the assumption
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=XX^{*}+YY^{*}=I
, is

(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)\oplus(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{2n}U_{k}(A\oplus B)U_{k}^{*}

for some family of
2n\times 2n
unitary matrices
U_{k}
. This is a majorization which is obtained by using the Hansen-Pedersen trace inequality.

Keywords.
Positive definite matrices, block matrices, convex functions, matrix inequalities.

2010 mathematics subject classification.
15A18, 15A60, 47A30.

1
Elementary scalar inequalities

Extending basic scalar inequalities, for instance
|a+b|\leq|a|+|b|
, to matrices lies at the very heart of matrix analysis. Here, we are interested in the elementary inequality which supports the Hermite-Hadamard inequality.
This classical theorem can be stated as follows:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
f(t)
be a convex function defined on the interval
[a,b]
. Then,

f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)\ \leq\int_{0}^{1}f((1-x)a+xb)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2}.

In spite of its simplicity, the Hermite-Hadamard inequality is a powerful tool for deriving a number of important inequalities; see the nice paper
[
12
]
and references therein.

The first inequality immediately follows from the convexity assumption

f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)\leq\frac{f((1-x)a+xb)+f(xa+(1-x)b)}{2}

(1.1)

The second inequality is slightly more subtle; it follows from the sums property

f((1-x)a+xb)+f(xa+(1-x)b)\leq f(a)+f(b)

(1.2)

which requires the convexity assumption twice.
This is the key for Proposition
1.1
and it has a clear geometric interpretation; (
1.2
) is equivalent to the increasingness of

\varphi(t):=f(m+t)+f(m-t)

with
m=(a+b)/2
and
t\in[0,b-m]
. In fact, if we assume that
f(t)
is
C^{2}
and observe that for
t\in[0,b-m]
,

\varphi^{\prime}(t)=f^{\prime}(m+t)-f^{\prime}(m-t)=\int_{m-t}^{m+t}f^{\prime\prime}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s,

we can estimate
\varphi^{\prime}(t)
with
f^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0
.

The extremal property (
1.2
) of
f(t)
says that for four points in
[a,b]
,

p\leq s\leq t\leq q,\ p+q=s+t\Rightarrow f(s)+f(t)\leq f(p)+f(q).

(1.3)

Let us see now what can be said for matrices.
Important matrix versions of (
1.1
) are well-known. Let
\mathbb{M}_{n}
denote the space of
n\times n
matrices and
\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
its self-adjoint (Hermitian) part with the usual order
\leq
induced by the positive semidefinite cone
\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
. We recall
[
6
, Corollary 2.2]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
with spectra in
[a,b]
and let
f(t)
be a convex function on
[a,b]
. Then, for some unitaries
U,\,V\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
,

f\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\{U\frac{f(A)+f(B)}{2}U^{*}+V\frac{f(A)+f(B)}{2}V^{*}\right\}.

If furthermore
f(t)
is monotone, then we can take
U=V
.

Theorem
1.2
is a major improvement of the classical trace inequality of von Neumann (around 1920),

{\mathrm{Tr\,}}f\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}\frac{f(A)+f(B)}{2}

which entails the following trivial extension of Proposition
1.1
.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
f(t)
be a convex function defined on the interval
[a,b]
and let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
with spectra in
[a,b]
. Then,

{\mathrm{Tr\,}}f\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\ \leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}\int_{0}^{1}f((1-x)A+xB)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}\frac{f(A)+f(B)}{2}.

What about matrix versions of the equivalent scalar inequalities (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) ? There is no hope for (
1.3
) : in general the trace inequality

{\mathrm{Tr}\,}f(P)+{\mathrm{Tr}\,}f(Q)\leq{\mathrm{Tr}\,}f(S)+{\mathrm{Tr\,}}f(T)

does not hold for all Hermitian matrices
P,Q,S,T
with spectra in
[a,b]
and such that

P\leq S\leq T\leq Q,\quad P+Q=S+T.

In the matrix setting (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are not equivalent. This paper aims to establish two matrix versions of the extremal inequality (
1.2
). Doing so, we will obtain several new matrix inequalities.

For an operator convex functions
h(t)
on
[a,b]
, and
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
with spectra in this interval, the matrix version of (
1.2
) (as well as Proposition
1.1
) obvioulsy holds,

h((1-x)A+xB)+h(xA+(1-x)B)\leq h(A)+h(B)

(1.4)

for any
0<x<1
. Our results hold for much more general convex/concave functions and have applications to eigenvalue inequalities that cannot be derived from
(
1.4
), even in the case of the simplest operator convex/concave function
h(t)=t
.

We often use a crucial assumption: our functions are defined on the positive half-line and we deal with positive semidefinite matrices. In the matrix setting, the interval of definition of a function may be quite important; for instance the class of operator monotone functions on the whole real line reduces to affine functions.

2
The sums property for matrices

For concave functions, the inequality (
1.2
) is reversed. Here is the matrix version. An isometry

U\in\mathbb{M}_{2n,n}
means a
2n\times n
matrix such that
U^{*}U=I
, the identity of
\mathbb{M}_{n}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
, let
0<x<1
, and let
f(t)
be a monotone concave function on
[0,\infty)
with
f(0)\geq 0
. Then, for some isometry matrices
U,V\in\mathbb{M}_{2n,n}
,

f(A)\oplus f(B)\leq Uf((1-x)A+xB)U^{*}+Vf(xA+(1-x)B)V^{*}.

Proof.

Let
x=\sin^{2}\theta
,
1-x=\cos^{2}\theta
, consider the unitary Hermitian matrix

R:=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{1-x}I&\sqrt{x}I\\
\sqrt{x}I&-\sqrt{1-x}I\end{bmatrix}

and note the unitary congruence

R\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{bmatrix}R=\begin{bmatrix}(1-x)A+xB&\star\\
\star&xA+(1-x)B\end{bmatrix}

(2.1)

where the stars hold for unspecified entries.

Now, recall the decomposition
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
: Given any positive semidefinite matrix
\begin{bmatrix}C&X\\
X^{*}&D\end{bmatrix}
partitioned in four blocks in
\mathbb{M}_{n}
, we have

\begin{bmatrix}C&X\\
X^{*}&D\end{bmatrix}=U_{0}\begin{bmatrix}C&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}U_{0}^{*}+V_{0}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&D\end{bmatrix}V_{0}^{*}

for some unitary matrices
U_{0},V_{0}\in\mathbb{M}_{2n}
. Applying this to (
2.1
), we obtain

\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{bmatrix}=U_{1}\begin{bmatrix}(1-x)A+xB&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}U_{1}^{*}+V_{1}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&xA+(1-x)B\end{bmatrix}V_{1}^{*}

(2.2)

for two unitary matrices
U_{1},V_{1}\in\mathbb{M}_{2n}
.

Next, recall the subadditivity inequality
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
: Given any pair of positive semidefinite matrices
S,T\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
, we have

f(S+T)\leq U_{2}f(S)U_{2}^{*}+V_{2}f(T)V_{2}^{*}

for two unitary matrices
U_{2},V_{2}\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Applying this to (
2.2
) yields

\begin{bmatrix}f(A)&0\\
0&f(B)\end{bmatrix}\leq U\begin{bmatrix}f((1-x)A+xB)&0\\
0&f(0)I\end{bmatrix}U^{*}+V\begin{bmatrix}f(0)I&0\\
0&f(xA+(1-x)B)\end{bmatrix}V^{*}

for two unitary matrices
U,V\in\mathbb{M}_{2n}
. This proves the theorem when
f(0)=0
.

To derive the general case, we may assume that
f(t)
is continuous (a concave function on
[0,\infty)
might be discontinuous at
0
). Indeed, it suffices to consider the values of
f(t)
on a finite set, the union of the spectra of the four matrices
A
,
B
,
(1-x)A+xB
and
xA+(1-x)B
. Hence we may replace
f(t)
by a piecewise affine monotone concave function
h(t)
with
h(0)\geq 0
. Now, since
h(t)
is continuous, a limit argument allows us to suppose that
A
and
B
are invertible. Therefore, letting
\lambda_{n}^{\downarrow}(Z)
denote the smallest eigenvalue of
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
,

r:=\min\{\lambda_{n}^{\downarrow}(A),\lambda_{n}^{\downarrow}(B)\}>0.

We may then replace
h(t)
by
h_{r}(t)
defined as
h_{r}(t):=h(t)
for
t\geq r
,
h_{r}(0):=0
and
h_{r}(s):=h(r)\frac{s}{r}
for
0\leq s\leq r
. The function
h_{r}(t)
is monotone concave on
[0,\infty)
and vanishes at
0
, thus the case
f(0)=0
entails the general case.
∎

Let
\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(Z)
,
j=1,2,\ldots n
, denote the eigenvalues of
Z\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
arranged in the nonincreasing order.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
, let
0<x<1
, and let
f(t)
be a nonnegative concave function on
[0,\infty)
. Then, for
j=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,

\lambda_{1+2j}^{\downarrow}\left(f(A\oplus B)\right)\leq\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left(f(xA+(1-x)B)\right)+\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left(f((1-x)A+xB)\right)

and

\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left\{\left(f(xA+(1-x)B)\right)+\left(f((1-x)A+xB)\right)\right\}\leq 2\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left(f\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\right).

Proof.

The first inequality is a straighforward consequence of Theorem
2.1
combined with the inequalities of Weyl
[
2
, p. 62]
:
For all
S,T\in\mathbb{M}_{d}^{s.a}
and
j,k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
such that
j+k+1\leq d
,

\lambda_{1+j+k}^{\downarrow}(S+T)\leq\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}(S)+\lambda_{1+k}^{\downarrow}(T).

The second inequality is not new; it follows from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
, let
0<x<1
, and let
f(t)
be nonnegative concave function on
[0,\infty)
. Then, for all
p\geq 1
,

\left(\|f(A)\|_{p}^{p}+\|f(B)\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\|f(xA+(1-x)B))\|_{p}+\|f((1-x)A+xB)\|_{p}.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.1
we have

\|f(A)\oplus f(B)\|_{p}\leq\|Uf((1-x)A+xB)U^{*}+Vf(xA+(1-x)B)V^{*}\|_{p}.

The triangle inequality for
\|\cdot\|_{p}
completes the proof.
∎

Corollary
2.3
with
f(t)=t^{q}
reads as the following trace inequality.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
and
0<x<1
. Then, for all
p\geq 1\geq q\geq 0
,

\left\{{\mathrm{Tr\,}}A^{pq}+\mathrm{Tr\,}B^{pq}\right\}^{1/p}\leq\left\{{\mathrm{Tr\,}}(xA+(1-x)B)^{pq}\right\}^{1/p}+\left\{{\mathrm{Tr\,}}((1-x)A+xB))^{pq}\right\}^{1/p}.

Choosing in Corollary
2.4

q=1
and
x=1/2
yields McCarthy’s inequality,

{\mathrm{Tr\,}}A^{p}+{\mathrm{Tr\,}}B^{p}\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}(A+B)^{p}.

This shows that Theorem
2.1
is already significant with
f(t)=t
.
Our next corollary, for convex functions, is equivalent to Theorem
2.1
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
, let
0<x<1
, and let
g(t)
be a monotone convex function on
[0,\infty)
with
g(0)\leq 0
. Then, for some isometry matrices
U,V\in\mathbb{M}_{2n,n}
,

g(A)\oplus g(B)\geq Ug((1-x)A+xB)U^{*}+Vg(xA+(1-x)B)V^{*}.

Since the Schatten
q
-quasinorms
\|\cdot\|_{q}
,
0<q<1
, are superadditive functionals on
\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
(see
[
4
, Proposition 3.7]
for a stronger statement), Corollary
2.5
yields the next one.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
, let
0<x<1
, and let
g(t)
be a nonnegative convex function on
[0,\infty)
with
g(0)\leq 0
. Then, for all
0<q<1
,

\left(\|g(A)\|_{q}^{q}+\|g(B)\|_{q}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\geq\|g(xA+(1-x)B))\|_{q}+\|g((1-x)A+xB)\|_{q}.

From the first inequality of Corollary
2.2
we also get the following statement.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
and let
f(t)
be a nonnegative concave function on
[0,\infty)
. Then, for
j=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,

\lambda_{1+2j}^{\downarrow}\left(f(A\oplus B)\right)\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{1+j}^{\downarrow}\left(f(xA+(1-x)B)\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

Up to now we have dealt with convex combinations
(1-x)A+xB
with scalar weights. It is natural to search for extensions with matricial weights (
C^{*}
-convex combinations). We may
generalize Theorem
2.1
with commuting normal weights.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
and let
f(t)
be a monotone concave function on
[0,\infty)
with
f(0)\geq 0
. If
X,Y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
are normal and satisfy
XY=YX
and
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=I
, then, for some isometry matrices
U,V\in\mathbb{M}_{2n,n}
,

f(A)\oplus f(B)\leq Uf(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)U^{*}+Vf(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)V^{*}.

Proof.

The proof is quite similar to that of Theorem
2.1
except that we first observe that the
2n\times 2n
matrix

H:=\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\\
Y&-X\end{bmatrix}

is unitary. Indeed for two normal operators,
XY=YX
ensures
X^{*}Y=YX^{*}
and a direct computation shows that
H^{*}H
is the identity in
\mathbb{M}_{2n}
.
We then use the unitary congruence

H^{*}\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{bmatrix}H=\begin{bmatrix}X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY&\star\\
\star&Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\end{bmatrix}

(2.3)

where the stars hold for unspecified entries.
∎

Hence, in the first inequality of Corolloray
2.2
and in the series of Corollaries
2.3
-
2.6
, we can replace the scalar convex combinations
(1-x)A+B
and
xA+(1-x)B
by
C^{*}
-convex combinations
X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY
and
Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX
with commuting normal weights. Here we explicitly state the generalization of Corollary
2.5
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
and let
g(t)
be a monotone convex function on
[0,\infty)
with
g(0)\leq 0
. If
X,Y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
are normal and satisfy
XY=YX
and
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=I
, then, for some isometry matrices
U,V\in\mathbb{M}_{2n,n}
,

g(A)\oplus g(B)\geq Ug(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)U^{*}+Vg(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)V^{*}.

3
Majorization

The results of Section 2 require two essential assumptions : to deal with positive matrices and with subadditive (concave) or superadditive (convex) functions. Thanks to these assumptions, we have obtained operator inequalities for the usual order in the positive cone.

The results of this section will consider Hermitian matrices and general convex or concave functions. We will obtain majorization relations. We also consider
C^{*}
-convex combinations more general than those with commuting normal weights.

We recall the notion of majorization. Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
.
We say that
A
is weakly majorized by
B
and we write
A\prec_{w}B
, if

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(A)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}^{\downarrow}(B)

for all
k=1,2,\ldots,n
. If
furthemore the equality holds for
k=n
, that is
A
and
B
have the same trace, then we say that
A
is majorized by
B
, written
A\prec B
. See
[
2
, Chapter 2]
and
[
9
]
for a background on majorization. One easily checks that
A\prec_{w}B
is equivalent to
A+C\prec B
for some
C\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
. We need two fundamental principles:

(1)

A\prec B\Rightarrow g(A)\prec_{w}g(B)
for all convex functions
g(t)
.

(2)

A\prec_{w}B\iff{\mathrm{Tr\,}}f(A)\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}f(B)
for all nondecreasing convex functions
f(t)
. Equivalently
A\prec B\iff{\mathrm{Tr\,}}g(A)\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}g(B)
for all convex functions
g(t)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
and let
g(t)
be a convex function defined on an interval containing the spectra of
A
and
B
. If
X,Y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
satisfy
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=XX^{*}+YY^{*}=I
, then,

{\mathrm{Tr\,}}\left\{g(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)+g(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)\right\}\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}\left\{g(A)+g(B)\right\}.

Proof.

By the famous Hansen-Pedersen trace inequality
[
8
]
, see also
[
6
, Corollary 2.4]
for a generalization,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Tr\,}}

\displaystyle\left\{g(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)+g(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}\left\{X^{*}g(A)X+Y^{*}g(B)Y)+Y^{*}g(A)Y+X^{*}g(B)X\right\}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr\,}}\left\{(g(A)+g(B))(XX^{*}+YY^{*})\right\}={\mathrm{Tr\,}}\left\{g(A)+g(B)\right\}

where the first equality follows from the cyclicity of the trace.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
and
X,Y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
. If
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=XX^{*}+YY^{*}=I
, then, for some unitary matrices
\{U_{k}\}_{k=1}^{2n}
in
\mathbb{M}_{2n}
,

(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)\oplus(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{2n}U_{k}(A\oplus B)U_{k}^{*}.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
, we have the trace inequality

{\mathrm{Tr\,}}g\left((X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)\oplus(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)\right)\leq{\mathrm{Tr\,}}g(A\oplus B)

for all convex functions defined on
(-\infty,\infty)
. By a basic principle of majorization, this is equivalent to

(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)\oplus(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)\prec A\oplus B.

(3.1)

By
[
7
, Proposition 2.6]
, the majorization in
\mathbb{M}_{d}^{s.a}
,
S\prec T
, ensures that (and thus is equivalent to)

S\leq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}TV_{j}^{*}

for
d
unitary matrices
V_{j}\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
. Applying this to (
3.1
) completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We can prove the majorization (
3.1
) in a different way by oberving that our assumption on
X
and
Y
ensures that the map
\Phi
, defined on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
, (here
d=2n
),

\Phi\left(\begin{bmatrix}A&C\\
D&B\end{bmatrix}\right):=\begin{bmatrix}X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY&0\\
0&Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\end{bmatrix},

is a positive linear map unital and trace preserving. Such maps are also called doubly stochastic. It is a classical result (see Ando’s survey
[
1
, Section 7]
and references therein) that we have

\Phi(Z)\prec Z

(3.2)

for every doubly stochastic map on
\mathbb{M}_{d}
and Hermitian
Z
. Here, our map is even completely positive (it is a so called doubly stochastic map, or a unital quantum channel). It is well known in the litterature (
[
1
, Theorem 7.1]
) that we have then

\Phi(Z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_{j}U_{j}^{*}ZU_{j}

for some convex combination
0<t_{j}\leq 1
,
\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_{j}=1
, and some unitary matrices
U_{j}
(these scalars
t_{i}
and matrices
U_{i}
depend on
Z
). In 2003, Zhan
[
14
]
noted that we can take
m=d
. That we can actually take an average,

\Phi(Z)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}U_{j}^{*}ZU_{j},

follows from the quite recent observation
[
7
, Proposition 2.6]
mentioned in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. For unital quantum channels, one has the Choi-Kraus decomposition (see
[
3
, Chapter 3]
)

\Phi(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}K_{i}AK_{i}^{*}

for some weights
K_{i}\in\mathbb{M}_{d}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}K_{i}K_{i}^{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}K_{i}^{*}K_{i}=I
. So, the proof of
Lemma 3.1 shows that, for unital quantum channels, one may derive the fundamental majorization (
3.2
) from two results for convex functions: the basic principle of majorisation and Hansen-Pedersen’s trace inequality.

Theorem
3.2
combined with a variation of Theorem
1.2
,
[
6
, Corolloray 2.4]
, provide a number of interesting operator inequalities. The next corollary can be regarded as another matrix version of the scalar inequality (
1.2
). We will give a proof independent of
[
6
, Corollary 2.4]
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{s.a}
and let
f(t)
be a convex function defined on an interval containing the spectra of
A
and
B
.
If
X,Y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
satisfy
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=XX^{*}+YY^{*}=I
, then, for some unitary matrices
\{U_{k}\}_{k=1}^{2n}
in
\mathbb{M}_{2n}
,

f\left(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY\right)\oplus f\left(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\right)\leq\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{2n}U_{k}f\left(A\oplus B\right)U_{k}^{*}.

In particular, for the absolute value, we note that

\left|X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY\right|\oplus\left|Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\right|\leq\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{2n}U_{k}\left|A\oplus B\right|U_{k}^{*}.

Proof.

The majorization (
3.1
) and the basic principle of majorizations show
that

f\left(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY\right)\oplus f\left(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\right)\prec_{w}f(A\oplus B)

for all convex functions defined on an interval containing the spectra of
A
and
B
.
Thus

f\left(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY\right)\oplus f\left(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\right)+C\prec f(A\oplus B)

for some positive semidefinite matrix
C\in\mathbb{M}_{2n}^{+}
. Hence, as in the previous proof,

f\left(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY\right)\oplus f\left(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\right)+C\leq\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{2n}U_{k}f\left(A\oplus B\right)U_{k}^{*}

for some family of unitary matrices
U_{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{2n}
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
. If
X,Y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
satisfy
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=XX^{*}+YY^{*}=I
, then,

\det(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY)\det(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX)\geq\det A\det B.

Proof.

Since the classical Minkowski functional
Z\mapsto\det^{1/2n}Z
is concave on
\mathbb{M}_{2n}^{+}
, the result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

The case
A=B
and
X,Y
are two orthogonal projections reads as the classical Fisher’s inequality.

3.4

Symmetric anti-norms

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
f(t)
and
g(t)
be two nonnegative functions defined on
[a,b]
and let
A,B\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be Hermitian with spectra in
[a,b]
. If
X,Y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
satisfy
X^{*}X+Y^{*}Y=XX^{*}+YY^{*}=I
, then :

(i)

If
f(t)
is concave, then, for all symmetric antinorms
\|\cdot\|_{!}
,

\left\|f\left(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY\right)\oplus f\left(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\right)\right\|_{!}\geq\left\|f\left(A\oplus B\right)\right\|_{!}.

(ii)

If
g(t)
is convex, then, for all symmetric norms
\|\cdot\|
,

\left\|g\left(X^{*}AX+Y^{*}BY\right)\oplus g\left(Y^{*}AY+X^{*}BX\right)\right\|\leq\left\|g\left(A\oplus B\right)\right\|.

Proof.

Since symmetric antinorms are unitarily invariant and superadditive, the first assertion follows from the version of Corollary
3.4
for concave version. The second assertion is an immediate consequence of Corollary
3.4
. ∎

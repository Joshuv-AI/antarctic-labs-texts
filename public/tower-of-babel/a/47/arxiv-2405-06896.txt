Energy momentum tensor on and off the light cone: exposition with scalar Yukawa theory
Xianghui Cao, Siqi Xu, Yang Li, Guangyao Chen, Xingbo Zhao, Vladimir A. Karmanov, James P. Vary

Abstract
We compute the gravitational form factors A i A_{i} , D i D_{i} and c ¯ i \bar{c}_{i} of the scalar Yukawa theory using both the light-cone and covariant perturbation theory at the one-loop level. The light-cone formalism provides a potential approach to access these form factors beyond the perturbative regime. However, unlike the covariant formulation, the Poincaré symmetry on the light cone is not manifest. In this work, we use perturbation theory as a benchmark to extract the gravitational form factors from the light-front energy-momentum tensor. By comparing results on and off the light cone, we identify T + ⁣ + , T + a , T + ⁣ − , T 12 T^{++},T^{+a},T^{+-},T^{12} as the “good currents” that are properly renormalized and can be used to extract the gravitational form factors.



I
Introduction

The energy-momentum tensor (EMT)
T^{\mu\nu}
is an observable of fundamental significance. It encodes the internal energy, momentum, angular momentum and stress distributions of a system
Teryaev:2016edw
. Therefore, this quantity is particularly interesting for unraveling the structures of hadrons, quarks and gluons bound together by the strong force
Polyakov:2018zvc
;
Burkert:2023wzr
.
The hadronic matrix element (HME) of the EMT can be parametrized in terms of Lorentz tensors
Kobzarev:1962wt
;
Pagels:1966zza
. The scalar functions that are specific to each hadron are known as the gravitational form factors (GFFs). These quantities then probe the internal dynamics of the hadron
Ji:1995cu
;
Ji:1996ek
.

Since QCD is strongly-coupled at the hadron scale, non-perturbative methods are needed to access the GFFs. Among various approaches, the light-front Hamiltonian formulation is poised as a natural framework to describe hadrons as relativistic self-bound states
Namyslowski:1985zq
;
Zhang:1994ti
;
Burkardt:1995ct
;
Brodsky:1997de
;
Carbonell:1998rj
;
Heinzl:2000ht
;
Miller:2000kv
;
Bakker:2013cea
;
Hiller:2016itl
;
Brodsky:2022fqy
;
Gross:2022hyw
.
This approach is demonstrated in one of our recent works for a strongly-coupled scalar Yukawa theory
Cao:2023ohj
. In that work, we extracted GFFs
A(Q^{2})
and
D(Q^{2})
from
T^{++}
and
T^{+-}
, two tensor components of the EMT. We showed that these two components can be renormalized using the same counterterms obtained from solving the light-front Schrödinger equation, i.e. the renormalization of the light-front Hamiltonian
Li:2014kfa
;
Li:2015iaw
;
Li:2015zah
;
Karmanov:2016yzu
.

Further quantities of interest are the GFFs associated with each constituent, which allow us to decompose a hadronic density into contributions from each constituent, e.g. quarks and gluons
Ji:1994av
;
Ji:1995sv
;
Lorce:2018egm
;
Lorce:2021xku
. For the scalar theory, apart from GFFs
A_{i}(Q^{2})
and
D_{i}(Q^{2})
, where
i
enumerates over the constituents, there is an additional GFF,
\bar{c}_{i}(Q^{2})

Polyakov:2018zvc
. It represents the force between constituent
i
and the rest of the system. Current conservation
\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
implies that the total
\bar{c}
vanishes,

\bar{c}(Q^{2})\equiv\sum_{i}\bar{c}_{i}(Q^{2})=0\,.

(1)

Namely, all inter-particle forces balance out for an isolated system – the quantum version of Newton’s third law.
To extract
\bar{c}_{i}(Q^{2})
, using the above two currents
T_{i}^{++}
and
T^{+-}_{i}
are not sufficient, and we need additional currents such as
T^{12}_{i},T^{11}_{i},T^{22}_{i}
. However, these operators contain uncanceled divergences and lead to a violation of the current conservation. These difficulties are caused by the breaking of the Poincaré symmetry due to truncation within the light-front formalism. This effect is better encapsulated in the explicitly covariant formulation of light-front dynamics (CLFD) (see Ref.
Carbonell:1998rj
for a review). In CLFD,
T^{12},T^{11},T^{22}
are contaminated by the spurious contributions that depend on the orientation of the light front
Cao:2023ohj
. The most general Lorentz structures of the EMT taking into account CLFD are given by Lorcé
Lorce:2015lna
.

Facing these difficulties in the non-perturbative calculation, we turn to perturbation theory for guidance.
Recently, More et. al. evaluated the EMT of a quark dressed by gluons using leading order (LO) light-cone perturbation theory (LCPT)
More:2021stk
;
More:2023pcy
. Their results can be compared with the recent LO QED calculations in covariant perturbation theory (CPT)
Metz:2021lqv
;
Freese:2022ibw
;
Freese:2022jlu
;
Eides:2023uox
. More et. al. suggested to use spin-flip HMEs to extract GFFs
D_{i}(Q^{2})
and
\bar{c}_{i}(Q^{2})
, which are unfortunately not available in the scalar case. Furthermore, they obtained a non-vanishing
\bar{c}(Q^{2})
except at
Q=0
. They attributed the violation of current conservation to zero modes
More:2023pcy
.
In this work, we compute the EMT of the scalar Yukawa theory using both covariant and LCPT at the one-loop level.
Scalar theory is among the simplest yet non-trivial quantum field theories in 3+1 dimensions

;
Nieuwenhuis:1996mc
;
Gross:2001ha
;
Hwang:2004mr
;
Emami-Razavi:2010pmi

Chabysheva:2021yse

Chabysheva:2022duu

By comparing the covariant and light-cone perturbation theory for the scalar Yukawa model, we identify the sources of the divergences and explore how to properly extract GFFs

II
Scalar Yukawa theory

In this work, we consider the scalar Yukawa theory. The classical Lagrangian density of this model reads
Li:2014kfa
;
Li:2015iaw
;
Li:2015zah
;
Karmanov:2016yzu
,

\mathscr{L}=\partial_{\mu}N^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}N-m^{2}N^{\dagger}N\\
+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\pi\partial^{\mu}\pi-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\pi^{2}+gN^{\dagger}N\pi\,.

(2)

This Lagrangian describes a complex scalar field
N
interacting with a real scalar field
\pi
via a Yukawa type coupling. It can be used as a rudimentary model for the nucleon-pion interaction. Therefore, we will refer to the complex scalar field
N
as the (mock) nucleon field and tentatively assign the mass of the nucleon
m=0.94\,\mathrm{GeV}
to its physical mass. Similarly, the real scalar field
\pi
will be referred to as the (mock) pion field, and tentatively assigned the pion mass
\mu=0.14\,\mathrm{GeV}
. The coupling
g
is dimensionful. Hence, this model is super-renormalizable. However, there are still divergences within the one-loop radiative correction. It is useful to introduce a dimensionless coupling
\alpha=g^{2}/(16\pi m^{2})
. This coupling is the Yukawa coupling appearing in the tree-level Yukawa potential,

V(r)=-\frac{\alpha}{r}e^{-\mu r}\,.

(3)

Specifically, we consider the nucleon dressed by the pion up to order
O(\alpha)
.
We adopt the bare renormalization scheme
Peskin:1995ev
, i.e. the field operator is not normalized,

\langle 0|N^{\dagger}(x)|p\rangle=\sqrt{Z}e^{ip\cdot x}
.
Instead, the state vector will be normalized to unity,
\langle p^{\prime}|p\rangle=2p^{+}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(p-p^{\prime})
.
The normalization of the state vector is easier to be generalized to the non-perturbative regime in the light-front Hamiltonian formulation
Karmanov:2016yzu
.
Within this scheme, the Lagrangian can be written as,

\mathscr{L}=\partial_{\mu}N^{\dagger}\partial^{\mu}N-m^{2}N^{\dagger}N\\
+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\pi\partial^{\mu}\pi-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\pi^{2}+g_{0}N^{\dagger}N\pi+\delta m^{2}N^{\dagger}N+\frac{1}{2}\delta\mu^{2}\pi^{2}\,,

(4)

where,
\delta m^{2}
and
\delta\mu^{2}
are mass counterterms and
g_{0}
is the bare coupling. These parameters can be obtained from the mass and coupling constant renormalization.

Figure 1:
Leading order diagram for the self-energy of the scalar Yukawa model

We adopt two types of regularization schemes, the dimensional regularization (DR) and Pauli-Villars regularization (PV). The former is the standard choice in covariant perturbation theory and the latter is useful for both the covariant and light-cone perturbation theory
Brodsky:2001ja
;
Brodsky:2002tp
;
Brodsky:2005yu
;
Chabysheva:2009aw
;
Chabysheva:2009vm
;
Chabysheva:2010vk
. Then the nucleon self-energy up to the LO order (see Fig.
1
) is,

\Sigma(p^{2})=-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\ln\frac{4\pi}{e^{\gamma_{E}}}-\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\ln\big[(1-x)\mu^{2}+xm^{2}-x(1-x)p^{2}\big],&\text{DR}\\
\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\ln\frac{(1-x)\mu^{2}_{\textsc{pv}}+xm^{2}-x(1-x)p^{2}}{(1-x)\mu^{2}+xm^{2}-x(1-x)p^{2}},&\text{PV}\end{cases}

(5)

where,
\epsilon=2-d/2
, and
d
is the spacetime dimension.
\gamma_{E}\approx 0.577216
is the Euler’s constant.
With the on-shell scheme, the mass counterterm is then
\delta m^{2}=\Sigma(m^{2})
. The
Z
-factor is,

Z=\Big[1-\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\Sigma(p^{2})}{{\mathrm{d}}p^{2}}\Big]^{-1}\Big|_{p^{2}\to m^{2}}.

(6)

Note that
Z
is not divergent in the scalar Yukawa model.
The bare coupling is obtained from imposing the physical coupling
g
to the nucleon-pion scattering amplitude at the on-shell point. And the result, according to the Lehmann-Symanzik-Zimmermann reduction formula
Peskin:1995ev
, is,

g_{0}=\frac{g}{Z\sqrt{Z^{\prime}}}\,,

(7)

where
Z^{\prime}
is the field renormalization constant of the pion,

Z^{\prime}=\Big[1+\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{x(1-x)}{m^{2}-x(1-x)\mu^{2}}\Big]^{-1}\,.

(8)

III
Energy-momentum tensor

The EMT of the scalar Yukawa theory is,

T^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\{\mu}N^{\dagger}\partial^{\nu\}}N-g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\rho}N^{\dagger}\partial^{\rho}N-m^{2}N^{\dagger}N)-g^{\mu\nu}g_{0}N^{\dagger}N\pi-g^{\mu\nu}\delta m^{2}N^{\dagger}N\\
+\partial^{\mu}\pi\partial^{\nu}\pi-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\sigma}\pi\partial^{\sigma}\pi-\mu^{2}\pi^{2})-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\delta\mu^{2}\pi^{2}\,.

(9)

Here,
a^{\{\mu}b^{\nu\}}\equiv a^{\mu}b^{\nu}+a^{\nu}b^{\mu}
. This EMT is symmetric, and there is no distinction between the canonical EMT and the Belinfante–Rosenfeld-Hilbert-Einstein EMT.
In analogy to mass decomposition in QCD
Ji:1994av
;
Ji:1995sv
, we split the EMT
into two parts,

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{N}=\,

\displaystyle\partial^{\{\mu}N^{\dagger}\partial^{\nu\}}N-g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\rho}N^{\dagger}\partial^{\rho}N-m^{2}N^{\dagger}N)-g^{\mu\nu}gN^{\dagger}N\pi-g^{\mu\nu}\delta m^{2}N^{\dagger}N\,,

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}_{\pi}=\,

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\pi\partial^{\nu}\pi-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\sigma}\pi\partial^{\sigma}\pi-\mu^{2}\pi^{2})-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\delta\mu^{2}\pi^{2}\,,

(10)

i.e. the nucleon EMT contains the interaction.
The HMEs of the EMT for a spin-0 particle can be parametrized by the following Lorentz tensors
Polyakov:2018zvc
,

t^{\alpha\beta}_{i}=\langle p^{\prime}|T^{\alpha\beta}_{i}(0)|p\rangle=2P^{\alpha}P^{\beta}A_{i}(-q^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\big(q^{\alpha}q^{\beta}-g^{\alpha\beta}q^{2}\big)D_{i}(-q^{2})+2M^{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\bar{c}_{i}(-q^{2})\,.

(11)

Here,
P=(p^{\prime}+p)/2
,
q=p^{\prime}-p
.
M
is the mass of the scalar, i.e.
p^{2}=p^{\prime 2}=M^{2}
. The Lorentz scalar functions
A_{i}(-q^{2})
,
D_{i}(-q^{2})
and
\bar{c}_{i}(-q^{2})
are the GFFs that encode the internal dynamics of the physical particle. The subscript
i=N,\pi
enumerates over the constituents. Summing over all constituents gives the total EMT. Accordingly,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}A_{i}(-q^{2})=\,

\displaystyle A(-q^{2}),

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}D_{i}(-q^{2})=\,

\displaystyle D(-q^{2}),

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\bar{c}_{i}(-q^{2})=\,

\displaystyle\bar{c}(-q^{2})\,.

(14)

Current conservation

\partial_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(15)

implies

\bar{c}(-q^{2})=0\,.

(16)

Physically,
\bar{c}_{i}
represents the force between the
i
-th constituent and the rest of the system. Eq. (
16
) is nothing but Newton’s third law applied to hadrons: the total force vanishes for a self-bound system. Current conservation also protects the EMT
T^{\alpha\beta}
against the quantum fluctuation. Consequently, the GFFs
A(-q^{2})
and
D(-q^{2})
do not depend on the renormalization scheme or scale. By contrast, the GFFs
A_{i}(-q^{2})
,
D_{i}(-q^{2})
and
\bar{c}_{i}(-q^{2})
for each constituent are renormalization scheme and scale dependent. Unless otherwise stated, these dependences are suppressed for simplicity. Despite this arbitrariness, the decomposition of the EMT provides insights into the system. For example, in QCD, mass decomposition based on the EMT provides answers to the origin of the proton mass in terms of quark mass, quark kinetic energy, gluon kinetic energy and the anomalous mass
Ji:1994av
;
Ji:1995sv
;
Lorce:2018egm
;
Lorce:2021xku
.

Figure 2:
Leading-order diagrams for the energy-momentum tensor. The solid lines with an arrow represent the nucleon. The wavy lines represent the pion. The cross represents the mass counterterm. The circled crosses represent the operator insertion.

Figure
2
shows the LO diagrams for the EMT. Diagrams (
a
)–(
e
) contribute to
T^{\mu\nu}_{N}
while diagram (
f
) is the contribution to
T^{\mu\nu}_{\pi}
. We first evaluate these diagrams in covariant perturbation theory and extract the relevant GFFs. These results will serve as a benchmark for the LCPT.

III.1
Covariant perturbation theory

The covariant calculation is straightforward. The results are,

\displaystyle t^{\mu\nu}_{a}=\,

\displaystyle Z\Big[2P^{\mu}P^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}(q^{\mu}q^{\nu}-q^{2}g^{\mu\nu})\Big],

(17)

\displaystyle t^{\mu\nu}_{b}=\,

\displaystyle-t^{\mu\nu}_{c}=-t^{\mu\nu}_{d}=-Zg^{\mu\nu}\Sigma(m^{2}),

(18)

\displaystyle t^{\mu\nu}_{e}=\,

\displaystyle Zg_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{4}\ell_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{e}}{(\ell_{E}^{2}+\Delta_{e})^{3}},

(19)

\displaystyle t^{\mu\nu}_{f}=\,

\displaystyle Zg_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{4}\ell_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{f}}{(\ell_{E}^{2}+\Delta_{f})^{3}}\,,

(20)

where,
\Delta_{e}=(x+y)^{2}m^{2}+(1-x-y)\mu^{2}-xyq^{2}
,
\Delta_{f}=(1-x-y)^{2}m^{2}+(x+y)\mu^{2}-xyq^{2}
and

\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{e}=g^{\mu\nu}\ell^{2}_{E}+2(1-x-y)^{2}2P^{\mu}P^{\nu}+((x-y)^{2}-1)(q^{\mu}q^{\nu}-q^{2}g^{\mu\nu})+2g^{\mu\nu}\Big\{\big[1-(1-x-y)^{2}\big]m^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}-x+y^{2}-y)q^{2}\Big\}
,

\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}_{f}=g^{\mu\nu}\ell^{2}_{E}+2(1-x-y)^{2}2P^{\mu}P^{\nu}+\big[(x-y)^{2}-1\big](q^{\mu}q^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}q^{2})+2g^{\mu\nu}\big\{\mu^{2}-(1-x-y)^{2}m^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}-x+y^{2}-y)q^{2}\big\}
.

From these HMEs, we can extract the GFFs. It turned out, GFFs
A_{i}
and
D_{i}
are divergence free,

\displaystyle A_{N}(Q^{2})=\,

\displaystyle Z+Z\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\frac{(1-x-y)^{2}}{\Delta_{e}},

(21)

\displaystyle A_{\pi}(Q^{2})=\,

\displaystyle Z\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\frac{(1-x-y)^{2}}{\Delta_{f}},

(22)

\displaystyle D_{N}(Q^{2})=\,

\displaystyle-Z-Z\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\frac{1-(x-y)^{2}}{\Delta_{e}},

(23)

\displaystyle D_{\pi}(Q^{2})=\,

\displaystyle-Z\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\frac{1-(x-y)^{2}}{\Delta_{f}}\,.

(24)

On the other hand, GFFs
\bar{c}_{i}
are UV divergent. The results with dimensional regularization are,

\bar{c}_{N}^{(\overline{\textsc{ms}})}(Q^{2},\mu^{2}_{R})=-\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{64\pi^{2}m^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+1\Big)+\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\ln\frac{(1-x)\mu^{2}+x^{2}m^{2}}{\mu^{2}_{R}}-\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\ln\frac{\Delta_{e}}{\mu^{2}_{R}}\\
+\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{64\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\frac{2(x+y)(2-x-y)m^{2}+(x^{2}-x+y^{2}-y)q^{2}}{\Delta_{e}},

(25)

and,

\bar{c}_{\pi}^{(\overline{\textsc{ms}})}(Q^{2},\mu_{R}^{2})=\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{64\pi^{2}m^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}-1\Big)-\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\ln\frac{\Delta_{f}}{{\mu}^{2}_{R}}\\
+\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{64\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int\limits_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\frac{2\mu^{2}-2(1-x-y)^{2}m^{2}+(x^{2}-x+y^{2}-y)q^{2}}{\Delta_{f}}.

(26)

Here,
\mu_{R}
is the renormalization scale in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme.
The GFFs
\bar{c}_{i}
can also be obtained with the Pauli-Villars regularization. The results are,

\bar{c}_{N}^{(\textsc{pv})}(Q^{2},\mu_{\textsc{pv}}^{2})=-\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\ln\frac{(1-x)\mu^{2}_{\textsc{pv}}+x^{2}m^{2}}{(1-x)\mu^{2}+x^{2}m^{2}}+\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\ln\frac{\Delta_{e}^{(1)}}{\Delta_{e}}\\
+\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{64\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\frac{2(x+y)(2-x-y)m^{2}+(x^{2}-x+y^{2}-y)q^{2}}{\Delta_{e}^{(j)}},

(27)

Here, the index “
j
” runs over 0 and 1 for the original field and the Pauli-Villars field respectively.
And,

\bar{c}_{\pi}^{(\textsc{pv})}(Q^{2},\mu_{\textsc{pv}}^{2})=\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{32\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\ln\frac{\Delta_{f}^{(1)}}{\Delta_{f}}\\
+\frac{Zg_{0}^{2}}{64\pi^{2}m^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int_{0}^{1-x}{\mathrm{d}}y\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\frac{2\mu_{j}^{2}-2(1-x-y)^{2}m^{2}+(x^{2}-x+y^{2}-y)q^{2}}{\Delta_{f}^{(j)}}.

(28)

In these expressions,
\Delta_{e}^{(0)}=\Delta_{e}
, and
\Delta_{e}^{(1)}
is obtained from
\Delta_{e}
by replacing
\mu
with
\mu_{1}=\mu_{\textsc{pv}}
. Similarly,
\Delta_{f}^{(0)}=\Delta_{f}
, and
\Delta_{f}^{(1)}
is obtained from
\Delta_{f}
by replacing
\mu
with
\mu_{1}=\mu_{\textsc{pv}}
.

The obtained
\bar{c}_{i}(Q^{2})
for both the nucleon and the pion is constant, i.e. independent of
Q^{2}
, consistent with the ultra-local character of the scalar Yukawa theory.
The total
\bar{c}(Q^{2})=\bar{c}_{N}(Q^{2})+\bar{c}_{\pi}(Q^{2})
, including the divergences, vanishes with both the dimensional (DR) and Pauli-Villars (PV) regularizations, as required by current conservation.

Comparing the results from the dimensional regularization and the Pauli-Villars regularization, it is readily established that the Pauli-Villars results can be obtained from the dimensional regularization with the following replacement,

\frac{1}{\epsilon}\;\leftrightarrow\;\ln\frac{\mu^{2}_{\textsc{pv}}}{\mu_{R}^{2}}-\frac{3}{2},

(29)

in the physical limit (
\mu_{\textsc{pv}}\to\infty
and
\epsilon\to 0
).

III.2
Light-cone perturbation theory

We adopt the Pauli-Villars regularization for calculations in LCPT
Brodsky:2001ja
;
Brodsky:2002tp
;
Brodsky:2005yu
. This regularization scheme can be extended to the non-perturbative light-front Hamiltonian formalism including gauge theories
Chabysheva:2009aw
;
Chabysheva:2009vm
;
Chabysheva:2010vk
;
Chabysheva:2015vga
. In LO LCPT, the obtained counterterms are the same as the counterterms in covariant perturbation theory with Pauli-Villars regularization, i.e. Eqs. (
5
–
8
).

For evaluating the HMEs of the EMT, we adopt the Drell-Yan frame, in which the longitudinal momentum of the graviton vanishes
q^{+}=0

Drell:1969km
. The vacuum creation and annihilation are suppressed in this frame. Mathematically,
q^{+}=0
is equivalent to integrating out the longitudinal
x^{-}
direction on the light cone. In the Drell-Yan frame, the diagrams needed for the EMT are the same as Fig.
2
, except all diagrams are now ordered in light-front time
x^{+}
. Applying the diagrammatic rules
Brodsky:1997de
, we obtain the HMEs as,

\displaystyle t_{a}^{\mu\nu}=\,

\displaystyle Z\left[2P^{\mu}P^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}(q^{\mu}q^{\nu}-q^{2}g^{\mu\nu})\right],

(30)

\displaystyle t_{b}^{\mu\nu}=\,

\displaystyle-t_{c}^{\mu\nu}=-t_{d}^{\mu\nu}=-Zg^{\mu\nu}\Sigma(m^{2}),

(31)

\displaystyle t_{e}^{\mu\nu}=\,

\displaystyle Zg_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}x}{2x(1-x)}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}k_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\frac{\frac{{1}}{2}q^{2}g^{\mu\nu}+p_{2}^{\{\mu}p_{2}^{\prime\nu\}}}{(1-x)(s_{2}^{j}-m^{2})(s_{2a}^{j}-m^{2})},

(32)

\displaystyle t_{f}^{\mu\nu}=\,

\displaystyle Zg_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}x}{2x(1-x)}\int\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{2}k_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\frac{\frac{1}{2}q^{2}g^{\mu\nu}+p_{1j}^{\{\mu}p_{1j}^{\prime\nu\}}}{x(s^{j}_{2}-m^{2})(s^{j}_{2b}-m^{2})},

(33)

V
Summary and outlooks

In this work, we compare the gravitational form factors of the scalar Yukawa theory obtained from covariant perturbation theory and from light-cone perturbation theory at the one-loop level. Our intention is to find a reliable way to extract the GFFs in light-front dynamics beyond the perturbative regime. In the light-front formalism, the full Poincaré symmetry is not manifest. As such, GFFs extracted from some current components of the EMT contain uncanceled divergences, which further lead to violation of conservation laws and known sum rules.
Traditionally, the “good currents” are used to extract physical form factors. In the case of the GFFs, the good currents
T^{++}
(and
T^{+a}
) are not sufficient to obtain the

we found that hadronic matrix elements

and

in light-cone perturbation theory reproduce those obtained from covariant perturbation theory whereas

contains uncanceled divergences. Based on these analysis, we proposed a new covariant Lorentz decomposition of the energy-momentum tensor on the light front (
49

\omega^{\mu}
when quantizing the theory.

The new CLFD decomposition avoids the mixing of the uncanceled spurious divergence within the physical GFFs. In particular, the light-front energy density

does not contain spurious contributions, which is consistent with our previous derivation that this operator can be properly renormalized using the same set of counterterms that renormalize the light-front Hamiltonian
Cao:2023ohj

One immediate application is the same system, the scalar Yukawa theory, in the non-perturbative regime. The present work overcomes the divergence issues encountered in the mass decomposition of the strongly coupled scalar nucleon

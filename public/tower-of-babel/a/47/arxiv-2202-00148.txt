On the degree of approximation of continuous functions by a linear transformation of their Fourier series
Xhevat Z. Krasniqi

Abstract
In this paper, we have proved four theorems on the degree of approximation of continuous functions by matrix means of their Fourier series which is expressed in terms of the modulus of continuity and a non-negative mediate function.



keywords
Degree of approximation, Fourier series, modulus of continuity, mediate function.

1
Introduction

By
S_{n}(f;x)
we denote the

n
-th partial sum of the Fourier series of a
2\pi
-periodic continuous function
f(x)
at
x
and
\omega(\delta)=\omega(\delta,f)
the modulus of continuity of
f
.
In short, we denote by
A:=(a_{n,k})
,
(k,n=0,1,\dots)
a lower triangular infinite matrix of real numbers and the
A
-transform of
\{S_{n}(f;x)\}
by

T_{n,A}(f;x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n,k}S_{k}(f;x),\quad(n=0,1,\dots).

Throughout this paper we write
u=\mathcal{O}(v)
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that
u\leq Cv
, and
\|\cdot\|
denotes the sup-norm.
P. Chandra
[
CH1
]
,
[
CH2
]
was the first who estimated the deviation
T_{n,A}(f)-f
, in sup-norm, when

\{a_{n,k}\}
is a monotonic sequences with respect to
k
. Many years later, L. Leindler
[
L
]
replaced the monotonicity condition with the so-called
rest bounded variation sequences
and
“head bounded variation sequences”
(these will be recalled later in this paper).
Chandra’s theorems are the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{a_{n,k}\}
satisfy the following conditions:

a_{n,k}\geq 0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n,k}=1,

(1)

a_{n,k}\leq a_{n,k+1}\quad(k=0,1,\dots,n-1;n=0,1,\dots).

(2)

Suppose
\omega(t)
is such that

\int_{u}^{\pi}t^{-2}\omega(t)dt=\mathcal{O}\left(H(u)\right)\quad(u\to+0),

(3)

where
H(u)\geq 0
and

\int_{0}^{t}H(u)du=\mathcal{O}\left(tH(t)\right)\quad(t\to+0).

(4)

Then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H(a_{n,n})\right).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) hold. Then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega(\pi/n)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H(\pi/n)\right).

If, in addition,
\omega(t)
satisfies (
4
) then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H(\pi/n)\right).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let us assume that (
1
) and

a_{n,k}\geq a_{n,k+1}\quad(k=0,1,\dots,n-1;n=0,1,\dots)

(5)

hold.
Then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega(\pi/n)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-1}\omega(\pi/k)\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}a_{n,r})\right).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let (
1
), (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
) hold. Then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,0}H(a_{n,0})\right).

To obtain his results, L. Leindler
[
L
]
used the following classes of sequences.
A sequence
\textbf{c}:=\{c_{n}\}
of nonnegative numbers tending to zero is called of Rest Bounded Variation, or briefly
\textbf{c}\in RBVS
, if it has the property

\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}|c_{n}-c_{n+1}|\leq K(\textbf{c})c_{m}

for all natural numbers
m
, where
K(\textbf{c})
is a constant depending only on
c
.
A sequence
\textbf{c}:=\{c_{n}\}
of nonnegative numbers will be called of Head Bounded Variation, or briefly
\textbf{c}\in HBVS
, if it has the property

\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}|c_{n}-c_{n+1}|\leq K(\textbf{c})c_{m}

for all natural numbers
m
, or only for all
m\leq N
if the sequence
c
has only finite nonzero terms, and the last nonzero term is
c_{N}
.
To go further it is clear that condition
0<K(\textbf{c})\leq K
needs to be assumed, where
K
is a positive constant.
Assuming that for all
n
and
0\leq m\leq n

\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}|a_{n,k}-a_{n,k+1}|\leq Ka_{n,m}

(6)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|a_{n,k}-a_{n,k+1}|\leq Ka_{n,m}

(7)

hold, where
K
is a positive constant, Leindler
[
L
]
proved the following:

Theorem 1.5
.

The statements of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
hold with (
7
)
in place of (
2
), and with (
6
) in place of (
5
), respectively; naturally maintaining all the other assumptions.

These results are extended further in
[
XhK
]
, then they are generalized in
[
XhK1
]
, and are treated again in
[
XhK2
]
using some other means (see also
[
BD
]
). Very recently, W. Lenski and B. Szal
[
LS
]
, used (for trigonometric approximation in the variable space
L^{p(x)}
and
m=n
) the following condition

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),\qquad(\beta\geq 0),

(8)

assumed on the sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}
,
0\leq m\leq n
. To my best knowledge, such a condition appears for the first time in the mathematical literature, which for
\beta=0
coincides with condition (
7
).
Motivated by the above condition (for
a_{n,m}=\frac{1}{n+1}
and
m=n
this condition has been introduced in
[
LS
]
) we introduce another new condition

\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),\qquad(\beta\geq 0),

(9)

on the sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}
,
0\leq m\leq n
, which for
\beta=0
coincide with condition (
6
).

Remark 1.6
.

Note that for
\beta=0
these two conditions coincides with (
7
) and (
6
), respectively.
Also, it was shown in
[
LS
]
that if
\{{(k+1)}^{-\beta}a_{n,k}\}\in HBVS
, then condition
(
8
)
holds true. Moreover, it easy to verify that condition
(
9
)
implies
\{{(k+1)}^{-\beta}a_{n,k}\}\in RBVS
. Furthermore, some examples, which supports these conditions, are given there as well.

Now we are concerned weather these two conditions on the sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}
can be used in Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
, instead of (
2
) and (
1.1
), which is in fact the aim of this paper.

2
Auxiliary Results

The next lemmas play a helpful role for the proof of our results.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
CH2
]

If (
3
) and (
4
) hold then

\int_{0}^{v}t^{-1}\omega(t)dt=\mathcal{O}\left(vH(v)\right)\quad(v\to+0).

Lemma 2.2
.

If (
3
) and (
4
) hold then

\int_{0}^{\pi/m}\omega(t)dt=\mathcal{O}\left(m^{-2}H(\pi/m)\right).

Proof 2.3
.

Using Lemma
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/m}\omega(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/m}\frac{t\omega(t)}{t}dt\leq\frac{\pi}{m}\int_{0}^{\pi/m}\frac{\omega(t)}{t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{m}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\pi}{m}H\left(\frac{\pi}{m}\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(m^{-2}H(\pi/m)\right),

which completes the proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
LS
]

If
\beta\geq 0
and
0<t\leq\pi
, then

\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}{(j+1)}^{\beta}\frac{\sin\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|\leq\frac{\pi^{2}{(m+1)}^{\beta}}{t^{2}}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\beta\geq 0
be a real number.
If for a fixed
n
, the sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}
satisfies the condition

\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),

for
0\leq m\leq n
, then uniformly in
0<t\leq\pi
,

|K_{n}(t)|:=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n,k}\frac{\sin\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|=\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1}A_{n,\tau}\right),

where
\displaystyle A_{n,\tau}:=\sum_{r=0}^{\tau}a_{n,r}
and
\tau
denotes the integer part of
\,\frac{\pi}{t}
.
If
\{a_{n,k}\}
satisfies the condition

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),

for
0\leq m\leq n
, then

|K_{n}(t)|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{a_{n,n}}{t^{2}}\right).

Proof 2.6
.

Assume that
n\geq\tau
. Applying summation by parts, the inequalities
|\sin\gamma|\leq 1
and
\pi\sin\gamma\geq 2\gamma
, (
\gamma\in[0,\pi/2]
), we have

\displaystyle\left|K_{n}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}\frac{a_{n,k}}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}+\left|\sum_{k=\tau+1}^{n}a_{n,k}\frac{\sin\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{2t}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}a_{n,k}+\left|\sum_{k=\tau+1}^{n}\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\frac{\sin\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{n,\tau+1}}{{(\tau+2)}^{\beta}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{\tau}{(j+1)}^{\beta}\frac{\sin\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{2t}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}a_{n,k}+\frac{a_{n,n}}{{(n+1)}^{\beta}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}{(j+1)}^{\beta}\frac{\sin\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum_{k=\tau+1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{k}{(j+1)}^{\beta}\frac{\sin\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|.

Now, using Lemma
2.4
and our assumption, we get

\displaystyle\left|K_{n}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{n,\tau+1}}{{(\tau+2)}^{\beta}}\frac{\pi^{2}{(\tau+1)}^{\beta}}{t^{2}}+\frac{\pi}{2t}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}a_{n,k}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{a_{n,n}}{{(n+1)}^{\beta}}\frac{\pi^{2}{(n+1)}^{\beta}}{t^{2}}+\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|\frac{\pi^{2}{(k+1)}^{\beta}}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2t}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}a_{n,k}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{t^{2}}\mathcal{O}(a_{n,\tau}+a_{n,n}).

But (by assumption) we also get

\displaystyle a_{n,n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=\tau}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,\tau}).

Therefore,

\left|K_{n}(t)\right|=\frac{\pi}{2t}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}a_{n,k}+\frac{\pi}{t}\mathcal{O}((\tau+1)a_{n,\tau})=\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}a_{n,k}\right),

since for
0\leq k\leq\tau
we have

\displaystyle a_{n,\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=\tau}^{\infty}{(i+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,i}}{{(i+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,i+1}}{{(i+2)}^{\beta}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}{(i+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,i}}{{(i+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,i+1}}{{(i+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,k}).

Similarly, we have obtained

\displaystyle\left|K_{n}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{n,n}}{{(n+1)}^{\beta}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}{(j+1)}^{\beta}\frac{\sin\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{k}{(j+1)}^{\beta}\frac{\sin\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)t}{2\sin\frac{t}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{n,n}}{{(n+1)}^{\beta}}\frac{\pi^{2}{(n+1)}^{\beta}}{t^{2}}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|\frac{\pi^{2}{(k+1)}^{\beta}}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{a_{n,n}}{t^{2}}\right).

The proof is now complete.

3
Main Results

We start with the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{a_{n,k}\}
satisfy the conditions (
1
) and

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),

for all
m=0,1,\dots,n;\,(n=0,1,\dots)
.
Suppose
\omega(t)
is such that (
3
) and (
4
) hold, then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H\left(a_{n,n}\right)\right).

(10)

Proof 3.2
.

Writing

\psi_{x}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\left\{f(x+t)+f(x-t)-2f(x)\right\},

we get

T_{n,A}(f;x)-f(x)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\psi_{x}(t)K_{n}(t)\,dt

and

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\int_{0}^{a_{n,n}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt+\int_{a_{n,n}}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt\right).

(11)

By the well-known inequality
\sin\theta\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\theta
for
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
,
|\sin\theta|\leq 1
, and Lemma
2.1
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a_{n,n}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1)\int_{0}^{a_{n,n}}t^{-1}\omega(t)dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H\left(a_{n,n}\right)\right).

(12)

Now using Lemma
2.5
and (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{a_{n,n}}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}\right)\int_{a_{n,n}}^{\pi}t^{-2}\omega(t)dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H\left(a_{n,n}\right)\right).

(13)

Substituting (
3.2
) and (
3.2
) into (
11
), this implies (
10
).
The proof is now complete.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\{a_{n,k}\}
satisfy the conditions (
1
) and

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),

for all
m=0,1,\dots,n;\,(n=0,1,\dots),
and (
3
).
Then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)+a_{n,n}H\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)\right).

(14)

If, in addition,
\omega(t)
satisfies (
4
) then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)\right).

(15)

Proof 3.4
.

Reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
we have

\displaystyle\|T_{n,A}-f\|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)\int_{0}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)\left(\!\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt+\!\int_{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt\right).

(16)

Using the inequalities
\sin\theta\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\theta
for
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
,
\displaystyle|\sin t|\leq t
and (
1
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(n+1)\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}\omega(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)\right).

(17)

Lemma
2.5
and (
3
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}\right)\int_{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}^{\pi}t^{-2}\omega(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)\right).

(18)

Putting (
3.4
) and (
3.4
) into (
3.4
) we obtain (
14
).
Now we prove (
15
). It is clear that for
0\leq l\leq n-1
and by our assumption we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(a_{n,n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq{(l+1)}^{\beta}\sum_{k=l}^{n-1}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq{(l+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,l}}{{(l+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,n}}{{(n+1)}^{\beta}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq{(l+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,l}}{{(l+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,n}}{{(l+1)}^{\beta}}\right|\geq a_{n,l}-a_{n,n},

from which we conclude that

a_{n,l}=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}\right).

Using the last relation we get

\displaystyle 1=\sum_{l=0}^{n}a_{n,l}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)\sum_{l=0}^{n}a_{n,n}=\mathcal{O}\left((n+1)a_{n,n}\right)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\frac{1}{n+1}=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}\right).

(19)

By Lemma
2.2
, (
3.4
) and (
3.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(n+1)\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}\omega(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(n+1\right){(n+1)}^{-2}H\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left({(n+1)}^{-1}H\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,n}H\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)\right).

(20)

Thus (
3.4
) and (
3.4
) imply (
15
), which completes the proof.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let us assume that (
1
) and

\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),

for all
m=0,1,\dots,n;\,(n=0,1,\dots)
.
Then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)+\sum_{v=1}^{n}v^{-1}\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{v}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{v}a_{n,j}\right).

Proof 3.6
.

Once again, we start from (
3.4
). For the first integral we use (
3.4
):

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt=\mathcal{O}\left(\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{n+1}\right)\right).

(21)

Applying Lemma
2.5
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\pi}{n+1}}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{v=1}^{n}\int_{\frac{\pi}{v+1}}^{\frac{\pi}{v}}t^{-1}\omega(t)A_{n,\tau}dt\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{v=1}^{n}(v+1)\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{v}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{v+1}a_{n,j}\int_{\frac{\pi}{v+1}}^{\frac{\pi}{v}}dt\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\sum_{v=1}^{n}v^{-1}\omega\left(\frac{\pi}{v}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{v}a_{n,j}\right),

since

\int_{\frac{\pi}{v+1}}^{\frac{\pi}{v}}dt=\frac{\pi}{v(v+1)}.

This with (
21
) and (
3.4
) completes the proof.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\{a_{n,k}\}
satisfy the conditions (
1
) and

\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,m}),

for all
m=0,1,\dots,n;\,(n=0,1,\dots),

and suppose that
\omega(t)
is such that (
3
) and (
4
) hold. Then

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,0}H\left(a_{n,0}\right)\right).

(22)

Proof 3.8
.

We have seen in the proof of Lemma
2.5
that

\left|K_{n}(t)\right|\leq\frac{\pi^{2}a_{n,n}}{t^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{t^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|.

Based on our assumption, for
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle a_{n,n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}(a_{n,0}),

and thus

\left|K_{n}(t)\right|\leq\frac{\pi^{2}a_{n,0}}{t^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{t^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{(k+1)}^{\beta}\left|\frac{a_{n,k}}{{(k+1)}^{\beta}}-\frac{a_{n,k+1}}{{(k+2)}^{\beta}}\right|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{a_{n,0}}{t^{2}}\right).

Now we write

\|T_{n,A}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\int_{0}^{a_{n,0}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt+\int_{a_{n,0}}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt\right).

(23)

Similarly, as in (
3.2
)), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a_{n,0}}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1)\int_{0}^{a_{n,0}}t^{-1}\omega(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,0}H\left(a_{n,0}\right)\right).

(24)

Moreover, as in (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{a_{n,0}}^{\pi}|\psi_{x}(t)||K_{n}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,0}\right)\int_{a_{n,0}}^{\pi}t^{-2}\omega(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(a_{n,0}H\left(a_{n,0}\right)\right).

(25)

Putting (
3.8
) and (
3.8
) into (
23
), we get (
22
), which completes the proof.

4
Corollaries

Assume that
A:=(a_{n,k})
is a matrix defined by

a_{n,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{p_{n-k}}{P_{n}},&0\leq k\leq n\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\{p_{k}\}_{k=0,1,\dots,n}
, is a sequence of non-negative numbers with
P_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}\neq 0
. In this case, the transform
T_{n,A}(f;x)
reduces to polynomials of the form

T_{n,A}(f;x)\equiv N_{n}(f;x)=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n-k}s_{k}(f;x).

Consequently, we have the following.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let all conditions of Theorem
3.7
be satisfied. Then

\|N_{n}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{p_{n}}{P_{n}}H\left(\frac{p_{n}}{P_{n}}\right)\right).

If the matrix
A:=(a_{n,k})
is defined as follows:

a_{n,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{p_{k}}{P_{n}},&0\leq k\leq n\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\{p_{k}\}_{k=0,1,\dots,n}
, is a sequence of non-negative numbers with
P_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}\neq 0
. Now, the transform
T_{n,A}(f;x)
are the polynomials

T_{n,A}(f;x)\equiv R_{n}(f;x)=\frac{1}{P_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}s_{k}(f;x).

Therefore, we have:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let all conditions of Theorem
3.1
be satisfied. Then

\|R_{n}(f)-f\|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{p_{n}}{P_{n}}H\left(\frac{p_{n}}{P_{n}}\right)\right).

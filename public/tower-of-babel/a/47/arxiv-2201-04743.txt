A Green’s function for the source-free Maxwell equations on A ​ d ​ S 5 × 𝕊 2 × 𝕊 3 AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
Damien Gobin, Niky Kamran

Abstract
We compute a Green’s function giving rise to the solution of the Cauchy problem for the source-free Maxwell’s equations on a causal domain 𝒟 \mathcal{D} contained in a geodesically normal domain of the Lorentzian manifold A ​ d ​ S 5 × 𝕊 2 × 𝕊 3 AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3} , where A ​ d ​ S 5 AdS^{5} denotes the simply connected 5 5 -dimensional anti-de-Sitter space-time. Our approach is to formulate the original Cauchy problem as an equivalent Cauchy problem for the Hodge Laplacian on 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and to seek a solution in the form of a Fourier expansion in terms of the eigenforms of the Hodge Laplacian on 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} . This gives rise to a sequence of inhomogeneous Cauchy problems governing the form-valued Fourier coefficients corresponding to the Fourier modes and involving operators related to the Hodge Laplacian on A ​ d ​ S 5 × 𝕊 2 AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2} , which we solve explicitly by using Riesz distributions and the method of spherical means for differential forms. Finally we put together into the Fourier expansion on 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} the modes obtained by this procedure, producing a 2 2 -form on 𝒟 ⊂ A ​ d ​ S 5 × 𝕊 2 × 𝕊 3 \mathcal{D}\subset AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3} which we show to be a solution of the original Cauchy problem for Maxwell’s equations. Keywords . Maxwell’s equations, Cauchy problem, Anti-de-Sitter space-time, Hodge Laplacian. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primaries 81U40, 35P25; Secondary 58J50.

Introduction and statement of the main result

Our goal in this paper is to compute a Green’s function for the source-free Maxwell equations on the
10
-dimensional symmetric Lorentzian manifold
({\mathcal{M}},g):=AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, where the metric
g
is the product of the
5
-dimensional anti-de Sitter metric
g_{AdS^{5}}
with the canonical round metrics
g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
and
g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, that is

g=g_{AdS^{5}}\oplus g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\oplus g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\,.

(1.1)

In the above expression,
AdS^{5}
stands for the complete simply connected Lorentzian
5
-manifold of constant sectional curvature
-\kappa
, with
\kappa>0
being fixed, and is thus diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{5}
. If
\theta=(\theta^{1},\theta^{2},\theta^{3})\in[0,\pi]\times[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
denote the
standard coordinates on the
3
-sphere, the metric on
AdS^{5}
can be expressed globally as

g_{AdS^{5}}:=\frac{-dt^{2}+dx^{2}+\cos(x)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}{\kappa\sin(x)^{2}},

(1.2)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in(0,\pi/2]
and where

g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}:=(d\theta^{1})^{2}+\sin^{2}(\theta^{1})(d\theta^{2})^{2}+\sin^{2}(\theta^{1})\sin^{2}(\theta^{2})(d\theta^{3})^{2},

is the canonical round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

While the problem of computing a Green’s function for Maxwell’s equations in this setting is of intrinsic geometric interest, it also carries a natural physical motivation coming from the fact that the symmetric manifold
({\mathcal{M}},g)
is an exact solution of the field equations of Type IIA supergravity which enjoys natural T-duality properties (we refer to
[
36
]
for a classification of all symmetric
AdS^{n}
solutions of Type II supergravity and a discussion of their duality properties). Green’s functions for hyperbolic differential operators are of course closely related to the solution of the initial value problem. More precisely, we shall see that for the specific class of symmetric Lorentzian manifolds
({\cal{M}},g)
considered in this paper, the Green’s function computed in our paper provides indeed the solution to a natural initial value problem for the source-free Maxwell equations.

The approach we take to the computation of the Green’s function makes use on the one hand of Riesz distributions and of generalizations to differential forms of the method of spherical means, and on the other of Fourier decompositions of differential forms in terms of eigenforms of the Hodge Laplacian. Our paper can thus be viewed as a natural continuation of earlier work by one of us in which Green’s functions were constructed for the hyperbolic Hodge Laplacian on classes of Lorentzian
symmetric spaces given by products of simply connected Lorentzian manifolds of constant sectional curvature with simply connected Riemannian surfaces of constant Gaussian curvature
[
11
]
.

\Omega_{0,\delta}^{k}(\mathcal{M})=\{\omega\in\Omega_{0}^{k}(\mathcal{M})\,|\,\delta\omega=0\}.

Unless otherwise stated, we write

We can now formulate the Cauchy problem for the Maxwell equations with initial data prescribed on

In order to solve the above Cauchy problem we follow the approach of

Definition 1.1

1.

In order to state our main theorem, we need to make a technical assumption about the structure of the causal domain
\mathcal{D}

\mathcal{D}
factors as the product of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(2-k)}+\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)})F_{\beta_{k}}=0&\\
F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,\beta_{k}}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,\beta_{k}}\\

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
, where

Second, we need to introduce a particular smooth

.

Assume that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}dF=0&\text{and}&\delta F=0\\

F=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sum_{\beta_{k}\in B_{k}}F_{\beta_{k}}\wedge\eta_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)},

F_{\beta_{k}}(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}F_{+,\,\beta_{k}}(t,x)&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\
F_{-,\,\beta_{k}}(t,x)&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{-}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\

F_{\pm,\,\beta_{k}}(t,x)=\hat{F}_{\beta_{k}}(t,x)-H_{\pm,\,\beta_{k}}(t,x),

,

,

in order to prove the equivalence between the Cauchy problem
(

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(2)}(F)=0&\\
F_{|\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{|\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\end{array},\right.

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(2)}=-(d\delta+\delta d)
is the positive Hodge Laplacian acting on

-forms
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}
are the restrictions to
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
of well chosen elements of
\Omega_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{D})
depending on
E

and

\Delta^{(2)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{(0)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\oplus\Delta^{(2)}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}&0&0\\

where the blocks act on
\Omega^{0}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\otimes\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
,
\Omega^{1}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\otimes\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})

\Omega^{2}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\otimes\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{3})

using the basis of eigenforms introduced above for the Hodge

-Laplacian on the

, by:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(2-k)})F_{\beta_{k}}=0&\\

\end{array},\right.

(1.8)

where as mentioned in introduction
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
is a copy of a subspace of

\mathcal{D}
,

(\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)})_{\beta_{k}}
is the spectrum of the Hodge

-Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(k)}

\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}

\mathbb{S}^{3}

\mathcal{F}_{0,\,\beta_{k}}

The paper is organized as follows. First, we show in Section

the equivalence between the Cauchy problem for the Maxwell equations and the Cauchy problem for
the Hodge Laplacian.
Next, in Section

There are also two appendices in this paper. Appendix

Proof of the main theorem

2.1
Reduction to a wave equation

Our aim in this section is to prove the following Proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a causal domain contained in a geodesically normal domain of
(\mathcal{M},g)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}dF=0&\text{and}&\delta F=0\\

E\in\Omega_{0,\delta}^{1}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}})

\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}=\Sigma\cap\mathcal{D}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(2)}(F)=0&\\
F_{|\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{|\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\end{array},\right.

(2.11)

where
\mathcal{F}_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}

\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}

\Omega_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{D})

]

]

First, it is easy to check that if

\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(2)}(F)=-(d\delta+\delta d)(F)=0.

We now need to prove that we can always select suitable initial data for the wave equation so that a solution of (

)

29

and
\mathcal{F}_{1}

into
\Omega_{0}^{2}(\mathcal{\mathcal{D}})
such that

\mathcal{F}_{0\,|V\cap\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=n_{0}E_{j}d\phi^{0}\wedge d\phi^{j}-\frac{1}{2}B_{ij}d\phi^{i}\wedge d\phi^{j},

and

\mathcal{F}_{1\,|V\cap\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=n^{0}\nabla_{i}\mathcal{F}_{0j}d\phi^{i}\wedge d\phi^{j}-n_{0}g^{ij}\nabla_{i}\mathcal{F}_{jk}d\phi^{0}\wedge d\phi^{k},

where
V
is a coordinate patch of
\mathcal{D}
that intersects
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
in a non empty set endowed with a local chart
\phi
and
n_{\mu}
is the unit normal vector
to
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
. Concerning the initial data we refer the reader to the proof of Proposition 2.1 of
[
9
]
and to
[
29
]
for additional details.
Let us now prove that a solution
F
of (
2.11
)
is also a solution (
2.10
). Assume that

\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(2)}(F)=0.

Since,
[\Delta,\delta]=[\Delta,d]=0
, we can deduce from the previous equality that

\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(\delta F)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(dF)=0.

We also have, thanks to our choice of initial data, that

dF_{|V\cap\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{n}dF_{|V\cap\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=0.

The quantity
dF
is then a solution of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}(dF)=0&\\
dF_{|\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=0&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}dF_{|\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}}=0\\
\end{array}.\right.

Since we restricted our analysis to a causal domain
\mathcal{D}
contained in a geodesically normal domain of
\mathcal{M}
,
our framework is exactly the one used in the Proposition 4.1 p. 201 of
[
20
]
. We can thus
conclude that
dF=0
(see
[
20
]
, Proposition 4.1 p.283). Using the same argument
we can prove in the same way that
\delta F=0
. We have thus proved that a solution
F
of (
2.11
) is actually also a solution
of (
2.10
).
∎

2.2
Decomposition of the Hodge Laplacian and sequence of Cauchy problems

The purpose of this section consists in using the decomposition of the Hodge Laplacian on the product manifold
\mathcal{M}=AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
in order to
simplify the Cauchy problem (
2.11
). More precisely, we shall use the eigenmodes on the
3
-sphere to transform this complete Cauchy problem into a
sequence of Cauchy problems indexed by the spectrum of the Hodge Laplacian. Our motivation for reducing the complete Cauchy problem into Cauchy
problems on
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
lies in the fact that this framework was studied in
[
11
]
and we want to use this work
in order to construct explicitly solutions of these Cauchy problems.

First, let us note that, thanks to the product structure of the manifold
\mathcal{M}=AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, the Hodge Laplacian acting on
\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{M})
can be decomposed in a convenient way. Indeed, it follows from the product structure of
{\cal M}=AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
that this operator can be expressed in matrix form as

\Delta^{(2)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{(0)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\oplus\Delta^{(2)}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}&0&0\\
0&\Delta^{(1)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\oplus\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}&0\\
0&0&\Delta^{(2)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\oplus\Delta^{(0)}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\end{pmatrix},

where the blocks act on
\Omega^{0}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\otimes\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
,
\Omega^{1}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\otimes\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
and

\Omega^{2}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\otimes\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
, respectively.

Let us now introduce the spectrum and the eigenforms of the Hodge
k
-Laplacians
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(k)}
. We refer the reader to Appendix
A
for more details on this point and also
to the references
[
5
,
26
,
28
]
. Thanks to these works we can decompose for
k=0,1,2
the spaces of
k
-forms on the
3
-sphere without multiplicity
in the following way:

\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{3})=\bigoplus_{\beta_{0}\in B_{0}}<\eta_{\beta_{0}}^{(0)}>\quad\text{where}\quad\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(0)}\eta_{\beta_{0}}^{(0)}=\lambda_{\beta_{0}}^{(0)}\eta_{\beta_{0}}^{(0)},\quad\forall\beta_{0}\in B_{0},

\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})=\bigoplus_{\beta_{1}\in B_{1}}<\eta_{\beta_{1}}^{(1)}>\quad\text{where}\quad\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(1)}\eta_{\beta_{1}}^{(1)}=\lambda_{\beta_{1}}^{(1)}\eta_{\beta_{1}}^{(1)},\quad\forall\beta_{1}\in B_{1},

and

\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})=\bigoplus_{\beta_{2}\in B_{2}}<\eta_{\beta_{2}}^{(2)}>\quad\text{where}\quad\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(2)}\eta_{\beta_{2}}^{(2)}=\lambda_{\beta_{2}}^{(2)}\eta_{\beta_{2}}^{(2)},\quad\forall\beta_{2}\in B_{2}.

Note that the spectral theory of the Hodge Laplacian on the space of
3
-forms on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\Omega^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
, is completely equivalent to the spectral theory on

\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
thanks to the Hodge duality. We also emphasize that the previous decompositions
are given without mutliplicity, meaning that an eigenvalue
\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)}
,
k\in\{0,1,2\}
, can be repeated.

Thus, restricted to a triplet
(\eta_{\beta_{2}}^{(2)},\eta_{\beta_{1}}^{(1)},\eta_{\beta_{0}}^{(0)})
, the operator
\Delta_{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}
is given by

\Delta^{(2)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{(0)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}+\lambda_{\beta_{2}}^{(2)}&0&0\\
0&\Delta^{(1)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}+\lambda_{\beta_{1}}^{(1)}&0\\
0&0&\Delta^{(2)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}+\lambda_{\beta_{0}}^{(0)}\end{pmatrix}.

We remark that on the first block we can use the eigenmodes on the
2
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in the same way in order to obtain the scalar operator

\Delta^{(0)}_{AdS^{5}}+\mu+\lambda_{\beta_{2}}^{(2)}
, where
\mu
is an eigenvalue of
\Delta^{(0)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
, as was
studied in
[
12
]
, leading to a representation of the solution.

Using these eigenforms we then obtain a sequence of Cauchy problems indexed by the spectrum of the Hodge Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, given on

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\subset AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
by:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(2-k)}+\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)})F_{\beta_{k}}=0&\\
F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,\beta_{k}}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,\beta_{k}}\\
\end{array},\quad k\in\{0,1,2\},\right.

where
F_{\beta_{k}}\in\Omega^{2-k}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
is defined on
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
,
\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
is the restriction of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
on

AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
\mathcal{F}_{0,\,\beta_{k}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1,\,\beta_{k}}
are the corresponding initial data.
To keep the notation simple, in the next three sections we will omit some subscripts and we thus rewrite these Cauchy problems as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{k}=0&\\
F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}\\
\end{array},\quad k\in\{0,1,2\},\right\}

(2.12)

where
\gamma^{(k)}:=\lambda_{\beta_{2-k}}^{(2-k)}
and
F_{k}:=F_{\beta_{k}}\in\Omega^{k}(\tilde{\mathcal{D}})
is defined on the causal domain
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}

contained in a geodesically normal domain of
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. The goal is now to solve each of the Cauchy problems
(
2.12
) on
\Omega^{k}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. As mentioned earlier, this fits precisely into the framework studied in
[
11
]
in which Green’s functions were computed for Hodge Laplacians on products of simply connected Lorentzian symmetric spaces of constant sectional curvature with simply connected Riemannian surfaces of constant Gaussian curvature.

Solution of the inhomogeneous problem

For our purpose of computing a Green’s function for Maxwell’s equations in

For the convenience of the reader we first give the definitions and some useful properties of the Riesz distributions and the spherical means used in
[
11
]
and
previously given in
[
20
,
33

for the induced area measure on

Definition 2.1

(
[
11
]
, Definition 2)
.

The Riesz distribution at

with parameter

is the distribution on
D
defined for
\text{Re}(\alpha)\geq n
by the integral

\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha}(\phi)(x):=C_{\alpha}\int_{J_{D}^{+}(x)}\rho(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha-n}\phi(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime},

where
\phi
is an arbitrary smooth function compactly supported in
D

stands for the Lorentzian volume element and

C_{\alpha}:=\frac{\pi^{1-\frac{n}{2}}2^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha-n}{2}+1)}.

The map
\alpha\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha}(\phi)
is thus an entire function for any
smooth function
\phi
with compact support in
D
. Let us recall the following fundamental result on Riesz distributions.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
20
,
33
]
)
.

For any fixed
x\in D
, the map
\alpha\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha}
can be holomorphically extended to the whole complex plane. Moreover,

\Delta_{M}\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha-2}
and
\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{0}=\delta_{x}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

For any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(D)
,
u(x)=\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{2}(\phi)
solves the hyperbolic equation
\Delta_{M}u=\phi
in
D
.

In order to define the spherical means on
M
,
we now need to introduce bundle-valued distributions
\tau_{p}
and
\hat{\tau}_{p}
over

using the Lorentzian distance

[

,
20

Given a degree

\tau_{p}
and

by setting

\tau_{0}(x,x^{\prime}):=1,\quad\tau_{1}(x,x^{\prime}):=-\frac{\sin(\sqrt{k_{M}}r)}{\sqrt{k_{M}}}dd^{\prime}\rho(x,x^{\prime}),

\tau_{p}(x,x^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{p}\tau_{p-1}(x,x^{\prime})\wedge\wedge^{\prime}\tau_{1}(x,x^{\prime})\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq p\leq n,

and

\hat{\tau}_{0}(x,x^{\prime}):=0,\quad\hat{\tau}_{1}(x,x^{\prime}):=-d\rho(x,x^{\prime})d^{\prime}\rho(x,x^{\prime}),

\hat{\tau}_{p}(x,x^{\prime}):=\hat{\tau}_{1}(x,x^{\prime})\wedge\wedge^{\prime}\hat{\tau}_{p-1}(x,x^{\prime})\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq p\leq n,

at the points
(x,x^{\prime})\in D\times D
where the distance function is smooth, and where the unaccented and accented operators

and
d^{\prime}
are evaluated at
x
and
x^{\prime}

These distributions are thus maps
\tau_{p},\,\hat{\tau}_{p}:D\times D\rightarrow\Lambda^{p}(T^{\star}M)\boxtimes\Lambda^{p}(T^{\star}M)

\Lambda^{p}(T_{x}^{\star}M)\otimes\Lambda^{p}(T_{x^{\prime}}^{\star}M)

The point-wise left and right inner products of a section

\Gamma(\Lambda^{p}(T^{\star}M)\boxtimes\Lambda^{p}(T^{\star}M))\times\Omega^{p}(M)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\Omega^{p}(M)

Left and right products can likewise be defined for distributions
\Psi:D\times D\rightarrow\Lambda^{p}(T^{\star}M)\boxtimes\Lambda^{p}(T^{\star}M)

and
\omega\in\Omega^{p}(M)
.

The double-differential forms we just defined enjoy the notable property that if

\tau_{p}(x,x^{\prime})+\hat{\tau}_{p}(x,x^{\prime})

Definition 2.2

(
[

Let

Proposition 2.4

The spherical means satisfy:

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\int r^{\alpha-n}\left(\mathcal{M}_{r}+\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{r}\right)\phi(x)d\mu_{M}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}\int_{J_{D}^{+}(x)}\rho(x,x^{\prime})^{\alpha-n}\left(\tau(x,x^{\prime})+\hat{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})\right)\cdot^{\prime}\phi(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime},

where
\phi\in\Omega_{0}^{p}(M)
. For fixed
x
and
\lambda
, this distribution is holomorphic for
\text{Re}(\alpha)>n
. The function

\alpha\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{x,D}^{\alpha}
has an entire extension and as
\tau(x,x^{\prime})+\hat{\tau}(x,x^{\prime})=\text{id}_{\Lambda^{p}(T_{x}^{\star}M)}

we can deduce from Theorem
2.2
that

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{x,D}^{0}(\phi)=\phi(x).

(2.18)

We now set

f_{\alpha}(r,\lambda):=\Phi_{\alpha,\text{sign}(k_{M})}\left(\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{k_{M}}r}{2}\right),\frac{\lambda}{k_{M}}\right),

with
\Phi_{\alpha,\pm}
as in Proposition
2.8
and define
\hat{f}_{\alpha}
thanks to (
2.15
)-(
2.16
) in the following way

\hat{f}_{\alpha}(r,\lambda):=\frac{L_{M}f_{\alpha}(r,\lambda)+pk_{M}\left(2\csc^{2}(\sqrt{k_{M}}r)+n-p-1\right)f_{\alpha}(r,\lambda)-C_{\alpha-2}r^{\alpha-n-2}}{2pk_{M}\cos(\sqrt{k_{M}}r)\csc^{2}(\sqrt{k_{M}}r)}.

We then define another vector-valued distribution by

\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha,\lambda}(\phi):=\int\left(f_{\alpha}(r,\lambda)\mathcal{M}_{r}+\hat{f}_{\alpha}(r,\lambda)\hat{\mathcal{M}}_{r}\right)\phi(x)d\mu_{M}(r),\quad\phi\in\Omega_{0}^{p}(M),

for
\text{Re}(\alpha)>n-2
. Thanks to Proposition
2.4
, (
2.15
)-(
2.16
) and (
2.18
) it follows that the previous distributions satisfy

(\Delta_{M}+\lambda+\gamma)\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha,\lambda+\gamma}=\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{x,D}^{\alpha-2}.

The function
\alpha\mapsto\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha,\lambda+\gamma}
admits an holomorphic extension to
\mathbb{C}
(see for instance
[
4
,
33
]
).
We then have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.9

(
[
11
]
, Proposition 4)
.

The distribution
\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{\alpha,\lambda+\gamma}
is a holomorphic function of
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
for fixed
x\in D
,
\lambda\in\text{spec}(L_{q})

and
\gamma\geq 0
. Moreover, for any
\phi\in\Omega_{0}^{p}(D)
the differential form
\Phi(x):=\mathcal{R}_{x,D}^{2,\lambda+\gamma}(\phi)
solves the equation

(\Delta_{M}+\lambda+\gamma)\Phi=\phi,\quad\text{in}\quad D.

Remark 2.10
.

In
[

this result was obtained in the case
\gamma=0

2.9

\gamma

thus nonnegative by the structure of the spectrum of the Hodge Laplacian on

when replacing

We note that the action of

The differential form

) for any compactly
supported

.

The proof is identical to the one given for Theorem 3 of

]
, with

.

2.4
Construction of a smooth differential form matching the initial data

The aim of this Section is to construct a
2
-form
\hat{F}_{k}:=\hat{F}_{\beta_{k}}
on the causal domain
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{D}_{|AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}

which is contained in a geodesically normal domain of
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}

such that
(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(\hat{F}_{k})
vanishes at infinite order along the Cauchy surface

\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
which is the restriction of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
on
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Such a
2
-form has been already constructed in
[
4
,
20
]
and we follow here these constructions.

In our analysis, we trivialize the bundle
E

over
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
and identify sections on
E
with
\mathbb{C}^{r}
-valued functions where
r
is the rank of
E
.
We also introduce the function
\beta:\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\star}
such that the metric takes the form
-\beta dt^{2}+g_{t}
, i.e., by (
1.2
), that is

\beta(x)=\frac{1}{\kappa\sin(x)^{2}}.

We now follow the procedure of
[
4
]
, Proposition 3.2.5. Assume first that
F_{k}
is a solution to the Cauchy problem (
2.12
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{k}=0&\\
F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}\\
\end{array},\right.

(2.19)

of the form

F_{k}(t,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{j}f_{j,k}(x),

where
x\in\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
. We write

\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)}=\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+Y,

where
Y
is a differential operator containing
t
-derivatives only up to order
1
. Therefore, the equation

0=(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{k}=\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+Y\right)F_{k}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}j(j-1)t^{j-2}f_{j,k}+Yu,

(2.20)

evaluated at
t=0
gives

\frac{2}{\beta(x)}f_{2,k}(x)=-Y(\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}+t\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k})(0,x),

for
x\in\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
. We see that
f_{2,k}
is determined by
f_{0,k}:=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}
and
f_{1,k}:=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}
.
We can then differentiate (
2.20
) with respect to
t
and repeat the same procedure to prove that each

f_{j,k}
can be recursively determined by
f_{0,k}
,…,
f_{j-1,k}
.

We now do not assume anymore that
F_{k}
is a
t
-power series but we still define the
f_{j,k}
,
j\geq 2
, by these recursive relations. Then,

\text{supp}(f_{j,k})\subset\text{supp}(\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k})\cup\text{supp}(\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k})
for all
j
. Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth
function such that
\sigma_{|[-1/2,1/2]}\equiv 1
and
\sigma\equiv 0
outside
[-1,1]
. We can find a sequence
\epsilon_{j}\in(0,1)
such that

\hat{F}_{k}(t,x):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma\left(\frac{t}{\epsilon_{j}}\right)t^{j}f_{j,k}(x),

defines a smooth section with compact support that can be differentiating term-wise and by construction one sees that

\text{supp}(\hat{F}_{k})\subset J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}(\text{supp}(\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k})\cup\text{supp}(\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}))

(see
[
4
]
).
The form
\hat{F}_{k}
is then the form we are interested in since, by the choice of
f_{j,k}
, the section
(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(\hat{F}_{k})

vanishes to infinite order along
\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
.

2.5
Solution of the sequence of Cauchy problems

The aim of this Section is to use Sections
2.3
and
2.4
in order to construct an explicit solution
F_{k}=F_{\beta_{k}}
of the Cauchy problem (
2.12
), which we recall:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{k}=0&\\
F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}\\
\end{array}.\right.

(2.21)

To this effect, we follow the procedure of
[
20
]
also used in
[
4
]
. First, we recall that, thanks to the results of Section
2.4
, we know that

(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(\hat{F}_{k})
vanishes along
\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
to infinite order. Let us thus set

G_{+,\,k}(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(\hat{F}_{k})&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\
0&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{-}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\
\end{array},\right.

(2.22)

which defines a smooth section. We now introduce the inhomogeneous problem

(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(H_{+,\,k})=G_{+,\,k}.

(2.23)

Then, using the results of Section
2.3
, we can explicitly construct a smooth solution

H_{+,\,k}\in\Omega^{2}(J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}))
of the above problem and this
solution satisfies
\text{supp}(H_{+,\,k})\subset\text{supp}(G_{+,\,k})\subset J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})
.

We now consider the “future” Cauchy problem on
\Omega^{2}(J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}))
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{+,\,k}=0&\\
F_{+,\,k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{+,\,k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}\\
\end{array},\quad F_{+,\,k}\in\Omega^{2}(J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})).\right.

(2.24)

We set

F_{+,\,k}=\hat{F}_{k}-H_{+,\,k},

where
\hat{F}_{k}
was explicitly constructed in Section
2.4
and
H_{+,\,k}
was also explicitly constructed in Section
2.3
. The function
F_{+,\,k}
is smooth and
since
H_{+,\,k}=0
on
J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{-}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})
,
F_{+,\,k}=\hat{F}_{k}
to infinite order on
\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
.
In particular,

F_{+,\,k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{n}F_{+,\,k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{+,\,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(\hat{F}_{k}-H_{+,\,k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(\hat{F}_{k})-(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(H_{+,\,k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{+,\,k}-G_{+,\,k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\quad\text{on}\quad J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}).

Therefore,
F_{+,\,k}
is a solution of the “future” Cauchy problem (
2.24
).

Similarly, by considering the form

G_{-,\,k}(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\
(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(\hat{F}_{k})&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{-}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\
\end{array},\right.

(2.25)

we then obtain a solution of the “past” Cauchy problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{-,\,k}=0&\\
F_{-,\,k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{-,\,k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}\\
\end{array},\quad F_{-,\,k}\in\Omega^{2}(J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{-}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})),\right.

(2.26)

by setting

F_{-,\,k}(t,x)=\hat{F}_{k}(t,x)-H_{-,\,k}(t,x),

where
H_{-,\,k}
is the solution of the inhomogeneous problem

(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})(H_{-,\,k})=G_{-,\,k},

constructed in Section
2.3
.

Finally, we put

F_{k}(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}F_{+,\,k}(t,x)&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{+}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\
F_{-,\,k}(t,x)&\text{if}&\quad x\in J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{-}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})\\
\end{array}.\right.

(2.27)

Then, since
F_{+,\,k}
and
F_{-,\,k}
coincide to infinite order on
\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
, we conclude that
F_{k}
is a smooth
2
-form on

J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}})
,
and in particular that

F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla_{n}F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}.

Moreover, by construction

(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{k}=0.

The section
F_{\beta_{k}}=F_{k}\in\Omega^{2}(J_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}(\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}))
, explicitly constructed above, is thus a solution of the
Cauchy problem (
2.21
).

2.6
Solution of the full Cauchy problem

In the previous section we have explicitly constructed in (
2.27
) for each
k\in\{0,1,2\}
a solution
F_{k}=F_{\beta_{k}}
of the (
2.21
)
Cauchy problems restricted to a Fourier mode of the Hodge Laplacian on the
3
-sphere:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(k)}+\gamma^{(k)})F_{k}=0&\\
F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,k}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{k\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,k}\\
\end{array},\right.

which is a concise formulation of the sequence of Cauchy problems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{(2-k)}+\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)})F_{\beta_{k}}=0&\\
F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,\beta_{k}}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,\beta_{k}}\\
\end{array},\right.

where
F_{\beta_{k}}\in\Omega^{2-k}(AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
is defined on
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
,
\Sigma_{\tilde{\mathcal{D}}}
is the restriction of
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
on

AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
\mathcal{F}_{0,\,\beta_{k}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1,\,\beta_{k}}
are the corresponding initial data.
In order to obtain a solution of the complete Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\Delta_{\mathcal{D}}^{(2)}(F)=0&\\
F_{|\Sigma}=\mathcal{F}_{0}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{|\Sigma}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\end{array},\right.

we then only need to add the preceding solutions in the following way:

F=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sum_{\beta_{k}\in B_{k}}F_{\beta_{k}}\wedge\eta_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)}.

(2.28)

In order to complete our work we need to check the convergence of the series (
2.28
), the smoothness the the
2
-form defined as its of its sum and check that this
2
-form is a solution of the source-free Maxwell equations. The proof of convergence of the Fourier expansion in terms of the eigenforms of the Hodge Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
is straightforward and uses the Bessel inequality as in the standard scalar case. Concerning the smoothness of the
2
-form
F
defined in (
2.28
) this is a consequence of the fact that the solutions
F_{\beta_{k}}
of the sequence of Cauchy problems are smooth and the eigenforms of the Hodge Laplacian on the
3
-sphere
\eta_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)}
are also smooth. Finally,
F
is, by construction, a solution of the Maxwell equations since it is a solution of the Cauchy problem for the Hodge Laplacian introduced in Section
2.1
. Indeed, using the decomposition of the Hodge Laplacian studied in Section
2.2
and the
construction in Section
2.5
of the solutions
F_{\beta_{k}}
of the sequence of Cauchy problems, we immediately obtain that
F
is a solution of the Cauchy problem for the Hodge Laplacian.

In conclusion, the
2
-form
F\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{D})
defined by (
2.28
) is an explicit solution of Cauchy problem for the source-free Maxwell equations in a causal domain
\cal D
contained in a geodesically normal domain of the Lorentzian manifold
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, expressed in terms of the forms
F_{\beta_{k}}
constructed above in (
2.27
) and the eigenforms
\eta_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)}
of the Hodge Laplacian on the
3
-sphere, which are explicitly recalled in Appendix
A
. It thus provides an explicit representation of the solution of the Green’s function.

Appendix A
Spectral theory of the Hodge Laplacian on the 3-sphere

The aim of this Appendix is to recall some results on the spectral theory of the Hodge Laplacian on the
3
-sphere. In particular we are interested in the spectrum
and explicit orthonormal eigenmodes of
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(k)}
for
k\in\{0,1,2,3\}
with respect to the Hodge scalar product.
This question has been largely studied and we will, in this Appendix, simply and strictly recall the results
contained in the well-written and self-contained paper
[
5
]
. We also refer the reader to the references
[
1
,
8
,
18
,
22
,
25
,
28
,
34
,
35
]
given in this last paper.

We first study the scalar harmonics on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. We introduce the Höpf coordinates

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}x^{1}&=\sin(\alpha)\cos(\varphi)\\
x^{2}&=\sin(\alpha)\sin(\varphi)\\
x^{3}&=\cos(\alpha)\cos(\theta)\\
x^{4}&=\cos(\alpha)\sin(\theta)\\
\end{array},\right.

where
\alpha\in[0,\pi/2]
,
(\theta,\varphi)\in[0,2\pi[^{2}
and we study the Laplace-de Rham operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(k)}:=-(d\delta+\delta d)
acting on the
scalar functions on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, i.e. in the case
k=0
. Let us thus consider the normalized scalar modes for this operator, i.e. the solutions of the eigenvalue equation

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(0)}\Phi_{i}=\lambda_{i}\Phi_{i},

where
\Phi_{i}
are the modes corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}=-L(L+2)
,
L\in\mathbb{N}
. The index
i=(L,m_{+},m_{-})
allows us to label the modes in the following
way:
|m_{\pm}|\leq\frac{L}{2}
and
\frac{L}{2}-m_{\pm}\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\Phi_{i}=T_{L,m_{+},m_{-}}(\alpha,\varphi,\theta):=C_{L,m_{+},m_{-}}e^{i(S\varphi+D\theta)}(1-x)^{\frac{S}{2}}(1+x)^{\frac{D}{2}}P_{\frac{L}{2}-m_{+}}^{(S,D)}(x),

where
P_{n}^{(a,b)}
is a Jacobi polynomial,
x=\cos(2\alpha)
,
S:=m_{+}+m_{-}
,
D:=m_{+}-m_{-}
and

C_{L,m_{+},m_{-}}:=\frac{1}{2^{m}+\pi}\sqrt{\frac{L+1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{(L/2+m_{+})!(L/2-m_{+})!}{(L/2+m_{-})!(L/2-m_{-})!}}.

Before studying the eigenforms of
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(1)}
we need to introduce some extra quantities. To this effect, let us introduce the two Killing vectors

\xi:=X_{12}+X_{34}=\partial_{\varphi}+\partial_{\theta},

\xi^{\prime}:=X_{12}-X_{34}=\partial_{\varphi}-\partial_{\theta},

where
X_{ij}:=x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i}
are the generators of the
so(4)
algebra.

Let us now study the eigenvalue equation

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(1)}\alpha=\lambda\alpha,

where
\alpha
is a one-form on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Using the Hodge decomposition we can prove that the space of solutions of this equation is the direct sum of two orthogonal
subspaces containing the exact and the co-exact one-forms, respectively. Concerning the exact one-forms, the eigenvalues are
-L(L+2)
,
L\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, the
eigenforms are given by the exterior derivatives of the scalar modes and the dimension of the associated proper subspace
\varepsilon_{L}^{E}
is
d^{E}=(L+1)^{2}
. The
eigenvalues for the co-exact one-forms are given by
-L^{2}
,
L\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0,1\}
and the dimension of the associated proper subspaces

\varepsilon_{L}^{CE}
is
d^{CE}=2(L-1)(L+1)
.

We now construct an explicit orthonormal basis of eigenforms. For this purpose let us define:

A_{i}:=d\Phi_{i},

B_{i}:=*d\Phi_{i}\tilde{\xi},\quad B_{i}^{\prime}:=*d\Phi_{i}\tilde{\xi^{\prime}},

C_{i}:=*dB_{i},\quad C_{i}^{\prime}:=*dB_{i}^{\prime},

where
\tilde{\xi}
is the one-form associated with the vector
\xi
, i.e.
\tilde{\xi}:=\flat\xi
. In these expressions
A_{i}
is an exact one-form whereas
B_{i}
,
C_{i}
,

B_{i}^{\prime}
and
C_{i}^{\prime}
are co-exact one-forms. Finally, we introduce the combinations

E_{i}:=(L+2)B_{i}+C_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{i}^{\prime}:=(L+2)B_{i}^{\prime}-C_{i}^{\prime},

where
i=(L,m_{+},m_{-})
(respectively
i=(L,m_{+}^{\prime},m_{-}^{\prime})
). The main result of
[
5
]
is then the following:

Theorem A.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

1.

The one-forms
E_{L,m_{+},m_{-}}
and
E_{L,m_{+}^{\prime},m_{-}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
satisfy

\Delta E_{L,m_{+},m_{-}}=-L^{2}E_{L,m_{+},m_{-}},

\Delta E_{L,m_{+}^{\prime},m_{-}^{\prime}}^{\prime}=-L^{2}E_{L,m_{+}^{\prime},m_{-}^{\prime}}^{\prime},

for
L\geq 2
.

2.

The family of one-forms

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}E_{L,m_{+},m_{-}},\quad L\geq 2,\quad|m_{+}|\leq\frac{L}{2}-1,\quad|m_{-}|\leq\frac{L}{2},\\
E_{L,m_{+}^{\prime},m_{-}^{\prime}}^{\prime},\quad L\geq 2,\quad|m_{-}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{L}{2}-1,\quad|m_{+}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{L}{2},\\
\end{array}\right.

once normalized, form an orthonormal basis of the corresponding proper subspace of co-exact one-forms
\varepsilon_{L}^{CE}
.

3.

The whole set of one-forms: exact
\{A_{L,m_{+},m_{-}}\}
, for
L\geq 1
, and co-exact
\{E_{L,m_{+},m_{-}},E_{L,m_{+}^{\prime},m_{-}^{\prime}}^{\prime}\}
, as above, form a complete orthonormal
set of modes for the Laplace-de Rham operator on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

Remark A.2
.

Using the Hodge star operator we also obtain explicit orthonormal basis of eigenforms for
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(2)}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(3)}
from the previous ones.

Hodge decomposition and perspectives

The aim of this Appendix is to give some remarks and ideas on the Cauchy problem we study in this paper in order to suggest another way to solve it and also to propose some
generalizations of our result, which was initially motivated by the Cauchy problem studied in
[
12
]
.

As we did before in our paper we study the Cauchy problem for the Hodge Laplacian given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\Delta_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(2)}(F)=0&\\
F_{|\Sigma}=\mathcal{F}_{0}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{|\Sigma}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\\
\end{array},\right.

where
\Delta^{(2)}=-(d\delta+\delta d)
is the Hodge Laplacian acting on
2
-forms of
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}

and
\Sigma
is a Cauchy hypersurface of
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}=AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
is a product manifold we can use a decomposition of the Hodge Laplacian which is different from the one we used in Section

2.2
that allows us to obtain equations only on
AdS^{5}
. Precisely, we know that

\Delta^{(2)}_{AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta^{(0)}_{AdS^{5}}\oplus\Delta^{(2)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}&0&0\\
0&\Delta^{(1)}_{AdS^{5}}\oplus\Delta^{(1)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}&0\\
0&0&\Delta^{(2)}_{AdS^{5}}\oplus\Delta^{(0)}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}\end{pmatrix}.

We can then use the explicit eigenmodes on the
2
and
3
-spheres given for instance in
[
5
,
26
,
28
]
in order to simplify the part corresponding to the
Hodge Laplacian on the product of spheres
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Using a well-chosen basis of eigenforms on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
we obtain
a new countable family of Cauchy problems on each mode, given, for
k\in\{0,1,2\}
, by:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&(\Delta_{AdS^{5}}^{(k)}+\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(2-k)})F_{\beta_{k}}=0&\\
F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\tilde{\Sigma}}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\,\beta_{k}}&\text{and}&\nabla_{n}F_{\beta_{k}\,\,|\tilde{\Sigma}}=\mathcal{F}_{1,\,\beta_{k}}\\
\end{array},\right.

where
(\lambda_{\beta_{k}}^{(k)})_{\beta_{k}}
is the spectrum of the Hodge
k
-Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}}^{(k)}
,
\tilde{\Sigma}
is the
restriction of
\Sigma
on
AdS^{5}
and
F_{\beta_{k}}\in\Omega^{2}(AdS^{5})
.

First of all, let us note that the scalar block
(\Delta_{AdS^{5}}^{(0)}+\gamma^{(0)})F_{\beta_{0}}
, where
\gamma^{(k)}=\lambda_{\beta_{2-k}}^{(2-k)}
, has been studied in

[
23
]
and that we can therefore use this latter work to obtain an explicit representation of
F_{\beta_{0}}
. Indeed, in
[
12
]
the authors obtain a scalar ODE in terms of
(t,x)

that falls within the framework of the important contribution
[
23
]
where a representation of the solution of an equation of the form

\partial_{tt}^{2}\Phi=-A\Phi,

(B.29)

for a positive and symmetric operator
A
in the variable
x
, is given.

Regarding the other blocks, we could use a moving frame and the Hodge decomposition on differential forms to eliminate the
\mathbb{S}^{3}
-contribution from the
AdS^{5}
geometry (see (
1.2
)) and then obtain an
operator in terms of
(t,x)\in(N^{2},g_{N^{2}})
, where
g_{N^{2}}=\frac{-dt^{2}+dx^{2}}{\kappa\sin(x)^{2}}
. More precisely, on this
2
-manifold
N^{2}
we obtain from the previous
equations on the Hodge k-Laplacians of
AdS^{5}
two kinds of equations given by:

(\Delta_{N^{2}}^{(0)}+\gamma^{(0)})F^{(0)}(t,x)=0,

which is of the previous form and can be explicitly solved by
[
23
]
, and

(\Delta_{N^{2}}^{(1)}+\gamma^{(1)})F^{(1)}(t,x)=0,

(B.30)

which is slightly different. Indeed, there are two differences between (
B.30
) and Equation (
B.29
) of
[
23
]
. First, Equation (
B.30
) is a matrix equation but this point should not be an obstacle. More important is the fact that
Equation (
B.30
) contains a matrix-valued term of order one in time that a priori prevents us from using
[
23
]
.
This last term is the reason why we did not use directly
[
23
]
in our work. Nevertheless, we could probably still use a similar analysis to obtain a
representation of the solution of the Maxwell equations in our framework in the spirit of
[
23
]
.

We would also like to mention the work
[
24
]
in which the Maxwell
equations are shown to be of the form (
B.29
) on globally hyperbolic manifolds using potential forms. The reason why we did not use this work lies
in the fact that, even if we restricted our analysis to a geodesically normal domain, we do not want to introduce such potential forms because of possible extensions to the entire non-globally hyperbolic manifold
\mathcal{M}=AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

Finally, for future work, we are interested in obtaining a representation of the solution of the Maxwell equations on
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
similar to the one given in
[
23
]

because we would like
to generalize this result to the
AdS^{5}\times Y^{p,q}
solutions of type IIB supergravity discovered in
[
17
]
, where
Y^{p,q}
is a certain irregular cohomogeneity one Sasaki-Einstein structure on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
, for which the representation of the solution of the Klein-Gordon equation in terms of a Green’s function is given in
[
12
]
. If we could use the procedure coming from
[
12
,
23
]
on
AdS^{5}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
we would then need to obtain a spectral representation of the Hodge Laplacian on
Y^{p,q}
in order to adapt our result on the manifold
AdS^{5}\times Y^{p,q}
. This question is being currently investigated.

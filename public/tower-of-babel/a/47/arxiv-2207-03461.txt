Regulators in the Arithmetic of Function Fields Quentin Gazda Affiliation: Max Planck Institut für Mathematik, Vivatsgasse 7, 53111 Bonn, Germany. Mail: quentin@gazda.fr.


Abstract




Abstract

As a natural sequel for the study of
A
-motivic cohomology, initiated in
[
Gaz22
]
, we develop a notion of regulator for rigid analytically trivial mixed Anderson
A
-motives. In accordance with the conjectural number field picture, we define it as the morphism at the level of extension modules induced by the exactness of the Hodge-Pink realization functor. The purpose of this text is twofold: we first prove a finiteness result for
A
-motivic cohomology and, under a weight assumption, we then show that the source and the target of regulators have the same dimension. It came as a surprise to the author that the image of this regulator might not have full rank, preventing the analogue of a renowned conjecture of Beilinson to hold in our setting.

1
Introduction

Very recently in
[
ANDTR22
]
, Anglès-Ngo Dac-Tavares-Ribeiro made the tremendous exploit of establishing a class formula for a large family of Anderson
A
-modules. This achievement sits at the top of a long list of work, dating back to Carlitz
[
Car35
]
in 1935, and culminating with the breakthroughs of Taelman
[
Tae12
]
and V. Lafforgue
[
Laf09
]
.

In the classical picture of number fields, class formulas in this level of generality are rather stated in terms of mixed motives in the form of Beilinson’s conjectures. Those are far reaching conjectures on special
L
-values formulated by Beilinson in the eighties in two celebrated papers
[
Bei84
]
,
[
Bei86
]
. The whole picture is rooted over the notion of a
Beilinson regulator
, conjecturally defined as follows. Let
M
be a mixed motive over the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
. Here, "motive" is understood in the wording of Deligne (e.g.
[
Del89
, §1]
). Consider the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbbm{1},M)
of
1
-fold extension of the neutral motive
\mathbbm{1}
by
M
in the category of mixed motives over
\mathbb{Q}
. It is expected that one can defined a natural subspace
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbbm{1},M)
consisting of
extensions having everywhere good reduction
(e.g.
[
Sch91
]
). The Hodge realization functor
\mathscr{H}^{+}
, from the category of mixed motives and with values in the category of mixed Hodge structures enriched with infinite Frobenii, is expected to be exact and, in this respect, should induce a morphism of the extension spaces:

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbbm{1},M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{R}-\operatorname{Hdg}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(M)).

(1.1)

Observe that the right-hand side is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of finite dimension. The above map is - conjecturally - the
Beilinson’s regulator of
M
. We denote it
\textrm{{Reg}}(M)
. The following is expected, although far from being proved.

(I)

The space
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbbm{1},M)
has finite dimension over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(II)

If
M
is pure of weight
<-2
, then
\textrm{{Reg}}(M)
has dense image.

(III)

There is a
\mathbb{Q}
-structure
V(M)
on the target of
\textrm{{Reg}}(M)
, natural in
M
, such that we have
\det(\im\textrm{{Reg}}(M))=L^{*}(M,0)\cdot\det V(M)
as
\mathbb{Q}
-structures on
\det\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{R}-\operatorname{Hdg}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(M))
.

Conjectures
(II)
and
(III)
are referred to as Beilinson’s conjectures. This text is concerned with the function field analogue of
(I)
and
(II)
, so we do not further comment on the (conjectural) definition of the special
L
-value
L^{*}(M,0)
nor on the
\mathbb{Q}
-structure
V(M)
. We rather refer the reader to the survey
[
Nek94
]
for a complete account of Beilinson’s conjectures and their history.

The present work grew out as an attempt to bridge these two pictures, and understand how Beilinson’s conjectures could be stated in the language of Anderson
A
-motives. The study of motivic cohomology for
A
-motives was initiated in
[
Gaz22
]
and this text consists in a natural sequel. Our primary interest is the definition of a
Beilinson’s regulator
in this context, and the study of the counterpart of conjectures
(I)
and
(II)
. We hope to make conjecture
(III)
the subject of a subsequent work.

Anderson
A
-motives.
Let
(C,\pazocal{O}_{C})
be a geometrically irreducible smooth projective curve over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
, and fix
\infty
a closed point of
C
. The
\mathbb{F}
-algebra

A=H^{0}(C\setminus\{\infty\},\pazocal{O}_{C})

consists of functions of
C
that are regular away from
\infty
. We denote by
K
its fraction field. The notion of
A
-motives dates back to the pioneer work of Anderson
[
And86
]
, and generalizes prior ideas of Drinfeld
[
Dd74
]
. Let us state the definition of an
Anderson
A
-motives
, leaving details for Section
3
. Throughout this text, unlabeled tensor or fiber products are over
\mathbb{F}
.

Definition

(
3.2
)
.

An
Anderson
A
-motive
\underline{M}
over
K
consists of a finite locally-free
A\otimes K
-module
M
together with an isomorphism of
A\otimes K
-modules

\tau_{M}:(\tau^{*}M)|_{(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes K)\setminus V(\mathfrak{j})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M|_{(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes K)\setminus V(\mathfrak{j})},

where
\tau:A\otimes K\to A\otimes K
is the ring endomorphism acting as the identity on
A
and as the
|\mathbb{F}|
th-power map on
K
, and where
V(\mathfrak{j})
is the effective Cartier divisor on
\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes K
associated to the locally-free ideal
\mathfrak{j}:=\ker(A\otimes K\to K,~a\otimes r\mapsto ar)
.

Remark 1.1
.

In general,
A
-motives do not carry a weight filtration; those that do are called
mixed
(see
[
Gaz22
, §3]
). However, as the main results of this text do not require mixedness - aside from obvious analogy purposes with the number field theory - we voluntarily forget the notion of mixedness. Yet, that of
weights of an
A
-motive
(see
[
Gaz22
, Def. 3.20]
) plays a major role.

A crucial assumption throughout this text however is that of
rigid analyitc triviality
(Subsection
3.2
for details). Given an
A
-motive
\underline{M}
over
K
, we consider the
A
-module

\underline{M}_{B}:=\{f\in M\otimes_{A\otimes K}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle~|~f=\tau_{M}(\tau^{*}f)\}.

It is the
Betti realization of
\underline{M}
. Here,
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle
is an affinoid algebra over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, the completion of an algebraic closure of the local field
K_{\infty}
of
(C,\pazocal{O}_{C})
at
\infty
(see
[
HJ20
, §2.3.3]
,
[
GM22b
, §3]
or Section
3
below for details). To ensure good properties of the functor
\underline{M}\mapsto\underline{M}_{B}
(e.g. exactness) we focus on
rigid analytically trivial

A
-motives; i.e. those
\underline{M}
for which
\underline{M}_{B}
generates
M\otimes_{A\otimes K}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle
(Definition
3.8
). The category of
rigid analytically trivial

A
-motives over
K
, denoted by
A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}^{\operatorname{rat}}
along this text, is an exact
A
-linear category which shall play the role of the classical category of mixed motives over
\mathbb{Q}
and the functor
\underline{M}\mapsto\underline{M}_{B}
that of the singular realization. The reader is invited to consult
[
BGHP20
]
for surveys on this analogy.

Let
\underline{M}
be an object of
A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}^{\operatorname{rat}}
. The
A
-module
\underline{M}_{B}
carries a natural action of the Galois group
G_{\infty}:=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}^{s}|K_{\infty})
,
K_{\infty}^{s}
being the separable closure of
K_{\infty}
inside
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. We have (see Proposition
3.11
and Corollary
3.17
):

Proposition
.

The profinite group
G_{\infty}
acts continuously on the discrete
A
-module
\underline{M}_{B}
. Further, the functor
\underline{M}\mapsto\underline{M}_{B}
, having for source the category
A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}^{\operatorname{rat}}
and for target the category of continuous
A
-linear representation of
G_{\infty}
, is exact.

The action of
G_{\infty}
on
\underline{M}_{B}
should be interpreted as the counter-part of the complex conjugation acting on the Betti realization of classical mixed motives, although here
G_{\infty}
is a way more convoluted that its number field avatar
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}|\mathbb{R})
.

In
[
Gaz22
]
, we established the definition of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
, a natural
A
-module consisting of
integral
extensions of the neutral
A
-motive
\mathbbm{1}
by
\underline{M}
in the category
A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}^{\operatorname{rat}}
. However, there are at least two reasons why this module is not finitely generated in general, preventing the naive analogue of conjecture
(I)
to hold in our context:

1.

The first reason, mentioned in details in
[
Gaz22
, §5]
, is related to the fact that taking Hodge filtrations is not an exact operation on the full class of exact sequences. This is solved by the notion of
regulated extensions
, introduced in Definition 5.7 in
loc. cit.
The sub-
A
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\text{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
of regulated extensions, however, might still not be finitely generated.

2.

The second reason, more subtle, is due to the infinite nature of the absolute Galois group
G_{\infty}
. We solve this second point by introducing the notion of
analytic reduction at
\infty
defined as follows.

The exactness of the Betti realization functor induces a morphism at the level of extension modules:

r_{B}:\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\text{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},\underline{M}_{B})

(1.2)

where the target denotes the continuous Galois cohomology module. We say that an extension
[\underline{E}]
in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\text{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
has
analytic reduction at
\infty
if it lies in the kernel of
r_{B}
; equivalently, if
[\underline{E}]
splits as representations of
G_{\infty}
. We denote by
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\text{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
the kernel of
r_{B}
, and by
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{M})
its cokernel. Our first main theorem is the following (repeated from Theorem
4.1
).

Theorem

(
4.1
)
.

The
A
-modules
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
and
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{M})
are finitely generated. If in addition the weights of
\underline{M}
are all negative, then
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{M})
is finite.

Let us throw some comments on Theorem
4.1
.

-

The above theorem should be understood as the analogue of conjecture
(I)
for rigid analytically trivial Anderson
A
-motives. Indeed, all extensions of classical mixed motives are
regulated
in the obvious sense, and hence (
1.2
) corresponds classically to the morphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces

r_{B}:\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbbm{1},M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}|\mathbb{R}),M_{B})

induced by the exactness of the Betti realization (above,
M
is a mixed motive over
\mathbb{Q}
,
M_{B}
its Betti realization). Yet, in the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear category of mixed motives, the right-hand side is zero, which amounts to say that
all
extensions in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbbm{1},M)
have
analytic reduction at
\infty
.

-

As a second remark, let us mention that Theorem
4.1
also shows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\text{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
is almost
never
finitely generated. To wit, its size is approximately the same as
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},\underline{M}_{B})
. Yet,
G_{\infty}
is not topologically finitely generated: by class field theory, its wild inertia group is topologically isomorphic to the group of
1
-unit in
\pazocal{O}_{\infty}
, itself isomorphic to a countable product of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

-

Finally, let us mention the work of Mornev
[
Mor18
, Thm. 1.1]
, supersiding prior results of Taelman in
[
Tae10
]
, where he obtained a similar version of Theorem
4.1
for Drinfeld modules having everywhere good reduction. We strongly suspect a relation with our work.

This discussion hints that the module
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
is the
right
source of a regulator. This speculation is in chords with recent computations obtained jointly with Maurischat
[
GM22a
]
for the Carlitz tensor powers. The side of Hodge-Pink structures, that we portray next, seems to confirm this insight.

Hodge-Pink structures.
In an innovative unpublished monograph
[
Pin97
]
, Pink defined and studied the general theory of Hodge structures in function fields arithmetic. The right object of study, highlighted in
loc. cit.
and nowadays called
Hodge-Pink structures
, consists in couples
\underline{H}=(H,\mathfrak{q}_{H})
, where

1.

H
is a finite dimensional
K_{\infty}
-vector space,

2.

\mathfrak{q}_{H}
is a
K_{\infty}^{s}[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-lattice in the
K_{\infty}^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
-vector space
H_{K_{\infty}^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}:=H\otimes_{K_{\infty},\nu}K_{\infty}^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)

(see Section
2
for details). Here
K_{\infty}^{s}[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
denotes the completion of
A\otimes K_{\infty}^{s}
for the
\mathfrak{j}
-adic topology,
K_{\infty}^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
its fraction field, and
\nu:K_{\infty}\to K_{\infty}^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
the morphism
a\mapsto a\otimes 1
(which is well-defined by
[
Gaz22
, Lem. 5.1]
).

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
Pin97
]
, Pink is rather concerned with
mixed
Hodge-Pink structures which are triples
(H,WH,\mathfrak{q}_{H})
, where
W=WH
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-graded finite filtration of
H
by subspaces, satisfying a certain
semi-stability
condition.

Classically, Hodge structures arising from motives naturally carry an involution called
the infinite Frobenius
which is obtained functorially from the action of the complex conjugation. We suggest the following function field counterpart:

Definition

(
2.9
)
.

An
infinite Frobenius
for
\underline{H}
is a
K_{\infty}
-linear continuous representation
\phi:G_{\infty}\to\operatorname{End}_{K_{\infty}}(H)
,
H
carrying the discrete topology, such that for all
\sigma\in G_{\infty}
,

\phi(\sigma)\otimes_{A}\sigma:H_{K_{\infty}^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\longrightarrow H_{K_{\infty}^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}

preserves the Hodge-Pink lattice
\mathfrak{q}_{H}
. We denote by
\mathbf{HPk}^{+}
the category of pairs
(\underline{H},\phi_{H})
where
\underline{H}
is an Hodge-Pink structure and
\phi_{H}
is an infinite Frobenius for
\underline{H}
.

Let
\underline{H}^{+}
be a mixed Hodge-Pink structure equipped with a infinite Frobenius, and let
\mathbbm{1}^{+}
denote the neutral object in
\mathbf{HPk}^{+}
. Contrary to the number field picture, yet similar to what we observed for mixed
A
-motives, the space of extensions
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}^{+}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
is generally not of finite dimension over
K_{\infty}
. Reasons are almost identical to those for
A
-motives: taking Hodge filtrations is not an exact operation, and this space of extensions is intertwined with the Galois cohomology of the profinite group
G_{\infty}
. Using Pink’s notion of Hodge additivity - which inspired that of
regulated extensions
in
[
Gaz22
]
- we considered the subspace

\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}^{+}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})

(1.3)

of Hodge additive extensions. We denote by
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}^{+},\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
, or simply
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\infty}
when the category
\mathbf{HPk}^{+}
is clear from context, the subspace of (
1.3
) of extensions whose infinite Frobenius splits.

From Proposition
3.26
below, there is an exact functor
\mathscr{H}^{+}:A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}^{\operatorname{rat}}\to\mathbf{HPk}^{+}
(see Hartl-Juschka
[
HJ20
]
for mixedness analogue of this statement), playing the role of the Hodge realization functor (see Definition
3.25
). As a corollary of Theorem
3.28
below, we record:

Proposition
.

The space
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(\underline{M}))
is finite dimensional over
K_{\infty}
.

The exactness of
\mathscr{H}^{+}
induces an
A
-linear morphism

r_{\mathscr{H}^{+}}:\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}^{\operatorname{rat}}}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}^{+}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(\underline{M}))

which, almost by design, maps
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
to
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(\underline{M}))
(Lemma
4.2
). All our efforts are worth to justify the next definition.

Definition

(
4.3
)
.

We call the
regulator of
\underline{M}
, and denote it
\textrm{{Reg}}(\underline{M})
, the
A
-linear morphism

\textrm{{Reg}}(\underline{M}):\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(\underline{M}))

induced by
r_{\mathscr{H}^{+}}
.

It is the counterpart of Beilinson’s regulator in this setting. Our second main result is the following.

Theorem

(
4.4
)
.

Assume that the weights of
\underline{M}
are all negative. The rank of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
over
A
equals the dimension of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(\underline{M}))
over
K_{\infty}
.

In view of conjecture
(II)
and the above, it is natural to ask whether the image of
\textrm{{Reg}}(\underline{M})
has full rank in its target. It is surprisingly false stated as it is, even in the simplest case of the
p
th Carlitz twist (function field analogue of Tate twists); this will be the subject of a subsequent work. The right formulation of Conjectures
(II)
and
(III)
in this context still is an open problem to the author and source of current investigations.

Methods.
Our proof of the main theorems took strong inspirations in the work of Lafforgue
[
Laf09
]
and Mornev
[
Mor18
]
, and hinges on the concept of
shtuka models
. We associate non-canonically to
\underline{M}
- which sits at the level of the affine curve
\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes K
- a
shtuka model

\underline{\pazocal{M}}_{0}=(\pazocal{M}_{0},\pazocal{N}_{0},\tau_{0})
over the surface
(\operatorname{Spec}A)\times C
(Proposition
5.2
). Attached to it is a complex of
A
-modules

is in fact defined differently in Definition
4.5
below, but is quasi-isomorphic to the next definition by Proposition

.
sitting in degrees

and

:

which only depends on
\underline{M}
up to quasi-isomorphisms. It plays a similar role to that of
shtuka cohomology
as defined and studied extensively in
[

]
. We show in Proposition

that there is a natural long exact sequence of

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\to\underline{M}_{B}^{G_{\infty}}\to\operatorname{H}^{0}(G_{\underline{M}})\\
\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r_{B}}}{{\to}}\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},\underline{M}_{B})\to\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\underline{M}})\to 0

where

is the map defined in (

). The first part of Theorem

is implied by the perfectness of the complex

, which itself follows from finiteness of coherent cohomology of the proper

-scheme

.

To some extent, the proof of Theorem
4.4
and the second part of Theorem

is similar but more involved. When the weights of
\underline{M}
are non-positive, we further associate to
\underline{M}
a

\underline{\pazocal{M}}

(Theorem

). An incarnation of this gadget in the context of Drinfeld modules with everywhere good reduction already appeared in

. The miracle ingredient of our proof is the surprising link between the behaviour of

at

and the space of Hodge additive extensions of Hodge-Pink structures having analytic reduction at

(Corollary

): this link materializes by a distinguish triangle of complexes of

\underline{M}_{B}^{G_{\infty}}[0]\otimes_{A}K_{\infty}\to\operatorname{R}\!\Gamma\left(\operatorname{Spf}\pazocal{O}_{\infty}\hat{\times}C,\pazocal{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pazocal{N}\right)\otimes_{\pazocal{O}_{\infty}}K_{\infty}\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}(\underline{M}))[0]\to[1].

Hodge-Pink structures and their extensions

In this section we provide all the necessary material on Hodge-Pink structures and their extension modules. Our main source of inspiration is Pink’s monograph
[
Pin97
]
. An innovation of ours is the introduction of
infinite Frobenii
in this context.

2.1
Hodge-Pink structures

Definitions

Let
(C,\pazocal{O}_{C})
is a geometrically irreductible smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{F}
and
\infty
is a closed point of
C
. The
\mathbb{F}
-algebra

A=H^{0}(C\setminus\{\infty\},\pazocal{O}_{C})

consists of functions of
C
that are regular away from
\infty
.

Let
v
be a closed point of
C
and denote by
K_{v}
the local function field at
v
. Let
R
be a Noetherian subring of
K_{v}
which contains
A
and such that
R\otimes_{A}K
is a field (which is then identified with a subfield of
K_{v}
). The ring
R
will play the role of a
coefficient ring
. In practice,
R
should be either
A
,
K
or
K_{v}
. Let
L
be a separable extension of
K_{v}
:
L
will play the role of a
base field
.

If
k
is a field which is an
A
-algebra through a morphism
\kappa
, we let
\mathfrak{j}=\mathfrak{j}_{\kappa}
denote the maximal ideal of
A\otimes k
generated by the set of differences
\{a\otimes 1-1\otimes a|a\in A\}
(equivalently, the kernel of
A\otimes k\to k
). We denote by
k[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
the completion of
A\otimes k
along the ideal
\mathfrak{j}_{\kappa}
, and by
k(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
its field of fractions.
k[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{j}
and residual field
k
. By a
k[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-lattice
\mathfrak{h}
in a finite dimensional
k(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
-vector space
V
we shall mean a finitely generated sub-
k[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-module of
V
that contains a basis.

Given an
R
-module
M
, there are (at least) two ways to obtain an
L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-module out of
M
: the
base
-wise way would be
M\otimes_{R}L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
seing
R
as a subfield of
L
; the
coefficient
-wise way would be to consider
L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
as an
A
-module via the morphism
\nu:A\to A\otimes L
,
a\mapsto a\otimes 1
. By
[
Gaz22
, Lem. 5.1]
,
\nu
extends uniquely to a morphism
\nu:K_{v}\to L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
which coincides modulo
\mathfrak{j}
with the inclusion
K_{v}\to L
, and it thus makes sense to consider the module
M\otimes_{R,\nu}L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
.

Following Pink’s
[
Pin97
, Def. 3.2]
, we define:

Definition 2.1
.

An
Hodge-Pink structure
\underline{H}
with base field
L
and coefficient ring
R
consists of the data of
(H,\mathfrak{q})
where

1.

H
is a finitely generated
R
-module,

2.

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{q}_{H}
is an
L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-lattice in the
L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
-vector space
H_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}:=H\otimes_{R,\nu}L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
.

We call
\mathfrak{q}
the
Hodge-Pink lattice of
\underline{H}
. We let
\mathfrak{p}_{H}:=H\otimes_{R,v}L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
and name it the
tautological lattice of
\underline{H}
.

We gather Hodge-Pink structures into an
R
-linear category
{}_{R}\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
(the leftscript
"R"
should disappear from the notations when clear from the context). A
morphism from
\underline{H}=(H,W,\mathfrak{q})
to
\underline{H}^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},W^{\prime},\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})
in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
is a morphism
f:H\to H^{\prime}
of
R
-modules such that
f_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}:=f\otimes_{R,\nu}\operatorname{id}_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}
preserves the Hodge-Pink lattices; i.e.
f_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}(\mathfrak{q})\subset\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
. We call
f

strict
whenever we have:

f_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}\cap\im f_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}.

Extensions

We are interested in computing extension modules in the category
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
. However,
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
is not abelian and we have to clarify the notion of exact sequences we would like to consider.

Definition 2.2
.

A sequence
S:0\to\underline{H}^{\prime}\to\underline{H}\to\underline{H}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
is called
exact
if its underlying sequence of
R
-module is, and
strict
if morphisms of
S
are.

It is formal to check that the category
\mathbf{HPk}
together with the notion of strict exact sequences form an exact category. Given
\underline{X}
,
\underline{Y}
two Hodge-Pink structures, we denote by
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})

the
R
-module of classes of strict exact sequences of the form
0\to\underline{Y}\to\underline{H}\to\underline{X}\to 0
.

We turn to the description of those extension modules. We write
\underline{X}=(X,\mathfrak{q}_{X})
and
\underline{Y}=(Y,\mathfrak{q}_{Y})
. Given an
L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
-linear morphism
f:X_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\to Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}
, consider the object

\underline{E}_{f}=\left(Y\oplus X,\{(q_{y}+f(q_{x}),q_{x})~|~(q_{y},q_{x})\in\mathfrak{q}_{Y}\oplus\mathfrak{q}_{X}\}\right)\in\operatorname{Ob}\mathbf{HPk}_{L}.

(2.1)

Let also
S_{f}
be the canonical short sequence
0\to\underline{Y}\to\underline{E}_{f}\to\underline{X}\to 0
with obvious morphisms. A simple computation shows that
S_{f}
is strict exact. In particular, the assignment
f\mapsto[S_{f}]
defines a map

\varphi:\operatorname{Hom}_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\left(X_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)},Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\right)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})

(2.2)

The following proposition suffices to describe the extension module when the underlying module of
\underline{X}
is projective:

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
X
is a projective
R
-module. The morphism
\varphi
is a surjective
R
-linear morphism whose kernel is

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(X,Y)+\operatorname{Hom}_{L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]}(\mathfrak{q}_{X},\mathfrak{q}_{Y}).

Here, the subscripts
"\mathfrak{q}"
refers to the submodule of morphisms compatible with the Hodge-Pink lattice.

Proof.

Let
f
and
g
be elements of
\operatorname{Hom}_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\left(X_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)},Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\right)
. A formal computation shows that the Baer sum of
[S_{f}]
and
[S_{g}]
is
[S_{f+g}]
, and that the pullback of
[S_{f}]
by
a\cdot\operatorname{id}:\underline{X}\to\underline{X}
, for
a\in R
, is
[S_{af}]
. This proves that
\varphi
is
R
-linear.

To show that
\varphi
is surjective, fix an extension
[S]
of
\underline{X}
by
\underline{Y}
. By our assumption that
X
is projective, the underlying sequence of
R
-modules splits. In other words,
S
is equivalent to an exact sequence of the form

0\longrightarrow\underline{Y}\longrightarrow\left(Y\oplus X,\mathfrak{q}\right)\longrightarrow\underline{X}\longrightarrow 0

(2.3)

where
\mathfrak{q}\subset Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\oplus X_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}
is an
L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-lattice. Let us define a morphism
f:X_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\to Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}
as follows. As morphisms of (
2.3
) are strict, the underlying sequence of lattices is exact:

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{q}_{Y}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{q}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{q}_{X}\longrightarrow 0.

For
q_{x}\in\mathfrak{q}_{X}
, choose one of its lift
\tilde{q}_{x}
in
\mathfrak{q}
, unique up to an element in
\mathfrak{q}_{Y}
. The assignement
\bar{f}(q_{x}):=\tilde{q}_{x}+\mathfrak{q}_{Y}
defines an
L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-linear morphism
\bar{f}:\mathfrak{q}_{X}\to Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}/\mathfrak{q}_{Y}
. As
\mathfrak{q}_{X}
is a projective
L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
-module,
\bar{f}
lifts to an
L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
-linear morphism
f:X_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\to Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}
. It is clear from construction that
S
is isomorphic to
S_{f}
, hence
[S]=\varphi(f)
.

It remains to describe the kernel of
\varphi
. Observe that
S_{f}
is equivalent to
S_{g}
if and only if there exists an
R
-linear map
u:X\to Y
such that
f=g+u
. Besides,
S_{f}
splits if and only if
f
preserves the Hodge-Pink lattices. This proves that

\ker\varphi=\operatorname{Hom}^{W}_{R}(X,Y)+\operatorname{Hom}_{L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]}(\mathfrak{q}_{X},\mathfrak{q}_{Y})

as desired.
∎

Let
\mathbbm{1}
be the Hodge-Pink structure over
L
whose underlying
R
-module is
R
itself and whose mixed Hodge-Pink lattice is
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathbbm{1}}=\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbbm{1}}=L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]
. We call
\mathbbm{1}
the
neutral Hodge-Pink structure
. We end this paragraph with the following corollary:

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\underline{H}
be a Hodge-Pink structure over
L
. We have a natural isomorphism of
R
-modules

\varphi:\frac{H_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}}{H+\mathfrak{q}_{H}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{H}).

Hodge-additive extensions

Contrary to the number fields setting, the
R
-module
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
is almost never finitely generated over
R
, even when
R=K_{v}
. This is an issue regarding regulators, which classically are morphism of finite dimensional vector spaces. Following Pink, we now discuss the notion of
Hodge addivity
on extensions which solve this nuisance.

Let
\underline{H}
be an Hodge-Pink structure over
L
. We first recall how to associate a finite decreasing filtration - the
Hodge filtration
- on
H_{L}
. For
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\operatorname{Fil}^{p}H_{L}
denote the image of
\mathfrak{p}_{H}\cap\mathfrak{j}^{p}\mathfrak{q}_{H}
through the composition:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}_{H}=H\otimes_{R,\nu}L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H\otimes_{R}L=H_{L}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{mod}~\mathfrak{j}}

We call
\operatorname{Fil}H_{L}=(\operatorname{Fil}^{p}H_{L})_{p}
the
Hodge filtration
of
\underline{H}
. The
Hodge polygon
of
\underline{H}
is defined as the polygon of the filtration
\operatorname{Fil}H_{L}
.

Definition 2.5

(
[
Pin97
, §8]
)
.

A strict exact sequence
0\to\underline{H}^{\prime}\to\underline{H}\to\underline{H}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
is said to be
Hodge additive
if the Hodge polygon of
\underline{H}
coincide with that of
\underline{H}\oplus\underline{H}^{\prime}
.

As the Hodge polygon is invariant under isomorphism, the property of being Hodge additive respects equivalences of extensions. Hence, the following definition makes sense:

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\underline{X}
,
\underline{Y}
be objects in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
. We denote by
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
the subset of Hodge additive extensions of
\underline{X}
by
\underline{Y}
in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
.

One easily shows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}
is a sub-
R
-module. The following result due to Pink shows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}
is finitely generated.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
Pin97
, Prop. 8.7]
)
.

Let
\underline{X}
,
\underline{Y}
be objects in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}
where the underlying module of
\underline{X}
is projective. Let
f:X_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\to Y_{L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}
be an
L(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
-linear map. Then, the sequence
S_{f}
is Hodge additive if and only if
f
preserves the tautological lattices. In particular,
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
is an sub-
R
-module of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
, and
\varphi
induces an isomorphism:

\frac{\operatorname{Hom}_{L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]}(\mathfrak{p}_{X},\mathfrak{p}_{Y})}{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(X,Y)+\operatorname{Hom}_{L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]}(\mathfrak{q}_{X},\mathfrak{q}_{Y})\cap\operatorname{Hom}_{L[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]}(\mathfrak{p}_{X},\mathfrak{p}_{Y})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y}).

In the particular case of
\underline{X}
being
\mathbbm{1}
, we get:

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\underline{H}
be a mixed Hodge-Pink structure over
L
. The morphism
\varphi
of Corollary
2.4
induces:

\varphi:\frac{\mathfrak{p}_{H}}{H+(\mathfrak{q}_{H}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{H})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{H}).

2.2
Infinite Frobenii

Before introducing
infinite Frobenii
for Hodge-Pink structures, let us shortly recall the classical story.

The classical picture

According to Nekovar
[
Nek94
, (2.4)]
and Deligne
[
Del89
, §1.4 (M7)]
, an
infinite Frobenius

\phi_{\infty}
for a mixed Hodge structure
(H,WH,\operatorname{Fil}H_{\mathbb{C}})
(with coefficients
\mathbb{R}
and base
\mathbb{C}
) is an involution of the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
H
compatible with
WH
, and such that
\phi_{\infty}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}c
preserves
\operatorname{Fil}H_{\mathbb{C}}
. Mixed Hodge Structures arising from the singular cohomology groups of a variety
X
over
\mathbb{R}
are naturally equipped with an infinite Frobenius, induced by functoriality of the action of the complex conjugation on the complex points
X(\mathbb{C})
.

We let
\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
be the category whose objects are pairs
(\underline{H},\phi_{\infty})
where
\underline{H}
is a mixed Hodge structures and
\phi_{\infty}
is an infinite Frobenius for
\underline{H}
. Morphisms in
\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
are the morphisms of mixed Hodge structures over
\mathbb{C}
which commute to infinite Frobenii.

Extension modules in the category
\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{C}}
of mixed Hodge structures over
\mathbb{C}
are well known. Given an object
\underline{H}
of
\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{C}}
, the complex of
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces

\left[W_{0}H\oplus F^{0}W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
\text{-}1\end{smallmatrix}\right)}}{{\longrightarrow}}W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}}\right]

represents the cohomology of
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{H})
(e.g.
[
Bei86
, §1]
,
[
Car80
, Prop. 2]
,
[
PS08
, Thm. 3.31]
). We obtain an
\mathbb{R}
-linear morphism

\frac{W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}}}{W_{0}H+F^{0}W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{H}).

(2.4)

If now
\underline{H}^{+}
denotes an object in the category
\mathbf{MHS}^{+}_{\mathbb{R}}
with infinite Frobenius
\phi_{\infty}
, the complex
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
is rather represented by

\left[(W_{0}H)^{+}\oplus(F^{0}W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}})^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
\text{-}1\end{smallmatrix}\right)}}{{\longrightarrow}}(W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}})^{+}\right]

where the subscript
+
means the corresponding
\mathbb{R}
-subspace fixed by
\phi_{\infty}\otimes c
(e.g.
[
Bei86
, §1]
,
[
Nek94
, (2.5)]
). We obtain an
\mathbb{R}
-linear morphism

\frac{(W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}})^{+}}{(W_{0}H)^{+}+(F^{0}W_{0}H_{\mathbb{C}})^{+}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{MHS}_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{H}).

(2.5)

Infinite Frobenii for Hodge-Pink structures

We keep notations from the previous subsection. Assume from now on that
L
is a finite separable extensions of
K_{v}
(hence complete), and fix
L^{s}
a separable closure of
L
. In this subsection, we enrich Hodge-Pink structures with a compatible continuous action of the profinite Galois group
G_{L}:=\operatorname{Gal}(L^{s}|L)
. In several cases, we compute extensions.

Let
\underline{H}=(H,\mathfrak{q}_{H})
be an Hodge-Pink structure with coefficient ring
R
and base field
L^{s}
.

Definition 2.9
.

An
infinite Frobenius
for
\underline{H}
is an
R
-linear continuous representation
\phi:G_{L}\to\operatorname{End}_{R}(H)
,
G_{L}
carrying the profinite topology and
H
the discrete topology, such that, for all
\sigma\in G_{L}
,

\phi(\sigma)\otimes_{A}\sigma:H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}\longrightarrow H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}

preserves the Hodge-Pink lattice
\mathfrak{q}_{H}
.

Remark 2.10
.

Above, we denoted
\sigma
its extension to
L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
, i.e. obtained by the functoriality of the assignment
k\mapsto k(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
, from the category of
A
-fields to rings, applied to
\sigma:L^{s}\to L^{s}
.

We let
{}_{R}\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}
(or
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}
) be the category whose objects are pairs
(\underline{H},\phi_{H})
where
\underline{H}
is a mixed Hodge-Pink structure over
L^{s}
and
\phi_{H}
is an infinite Frobenius for
\underline{H}
. Morphisms in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}
are the one in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L^{s}}
commuting the infinite Frobenii.

Definition 2.11
.

A sequence
S:0\to\underline{H}^{\prime}\to\underline{H}\to\underline{H}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}
is called
exact
(resp.
strict
) if the underlying sequence in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L^{s}}
is exact (resp. strict) as in Definition
2.2
.

As well,
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}
with the above class of strict exact sequences is an exact category and we now study its extension modules. While the ingenious analogue of (
2.4
) holds for Hodge-Pink structures (this was Corollary
2.8
), a description similar as (
2.5
) does not hold in our setting as the action of the complex conjugation is replaced by that of the (infinite) profinite group
G_{L}
. Therefore, the extension modules are intertwined with the Galois cohomology of
G_{L}
, preventing an isomorphism as simple as (
2.5
) to exist. In order to clarify how Galois cohomology interferes with the computation of extension spaces, we introduce next an
R
-linear morphism
d_{\underline{H}^{+}}
.

By definition, we have a forgetful functor from
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}
to the category of
R
-linear continuous representation of
G_{L}
, assigning
\underline{H}^{+}=(\underline{H},\phi_{H})
to
\phi_{H}
. Being exact, it induces a natural
R
-linear morphism at the level of extensions:

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}}(\underline{G}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{G_{L}}(\phi_{G},\phi_{H}),

(2.6)

the right-hand side denoting extensions of continuous
R
-linear
G_{L}
-representations.

Definition 2.12
.

An extension
[\underline{E}]\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}}(\underline{G}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
is said to have
analytic reduction
if it splits as a representation of
G_{L}
; i.e. lies in the kernel of (
2.6
). We denote the latter by
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+},v}(\underline{G}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
or simply by
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{v}(\underline{G}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
.

Let
\mathbbm{1}
denote the neutral mixed Hodge-Pink structure over
L^{s}
, and denote by
\mathbbm{1}^{+}
the object of
\mathbf{HPk}^{+}_{L}
given by the pair
(\mathbbm{1},\phi_{\mathbbm{1}})
, where
\phi_{\mathbbm{1}}:G_{L}\to R
,
\sigma\mapsto 1
is the neutral representation.

Definition 2.13
.

Given an object
\underline{H}^{+}
of
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}
, we denote by

d_{\underline{H}^{+}}:\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{L},H)

the
R
-linear morphism (
2.6
) with
\underline{G}^{+}=\mathbbm{1}^{+}
, where
\operatorname{H}^{1}
here denotes the continuous group cohomology.

Recall that
H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}=H\otimes_{R,\nu}L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)
is endowed with a continuous action of
\sigma\in G_{L}
given by
\phi_{H}(\sigma)\otimes\sigma
. For
S
a subset of
H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)}
we denote by
S^{+}
the subset of elements fixed under this action of
G_{L}
. Let
\varphi
be the isomorphism of Corollary
2.8
. There is an
R
-linear morphism:

\varphi^{+}:\frac{(H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)})^{+}}{H^{+}+\mathfrak{q}_{H}^{+}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}^{+}_{L}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+}),

mapping the class of
h\in(H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)})^{+}
to the extension
(\varphi(h),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{H}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right))
. By definition, the image of
\varphi^{+}
lands in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{v}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
. Under some assumption, we can say more.

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{L},\mathfrak{q}_{H})
is trivial. Then, the sequence of
R
-modules:

0\longrightarrow\frac{(H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)})^{+}}{H^{+}+\mathfrak{q}_{H}^{+}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi^{+}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}^{+}_{L}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{\underline{H}^{+}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{L},H)\longrightarrow 0

is exact. In particular, under the same assumption,
\varphi^{+}
induces an isomorphism of
R
-modules:

\varphi^{+}:\frac{(H_{L^{s}(\!(\mathfrak{j})\!)})^{+}}{H^{+}+\mathfrak{q}_{H}^{+}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{v}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+}).

Remark 2.15
.

It will appear that the condition
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{L},\mathfrak{q}_{H})=(0)
is always satisfied for mixed Hodge-Pink structures arising from rigid analytically trivial mixed
A
-motives. We refer to Lemma
3.30
below.

Proof.

We first show that
d_{\underline{H}^{+}}
is surjective. Let
c:G_{L}\to H
be a cocycle. We denote by
[c]
the
R
-linear
G_{L}
-representation of
H\oplus R
given by

[c]:G_{L}\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{R}(H\oplus R),\quad\sigma\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{H}(\sigma)&c(\sigma)\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Because
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{L},\mathfrak{q}_{H})=0
, there exists
h\in\mathfrak{q}_{H}
such that
c(\sigma)=h-(\phi_{H}(\sigma)\otimes\sigma)(h)
. Recall that
\varphi(h)
is represented by the extension of
\mathbbm{1}
by
\underline{H}
whose middle term is

\underline{E}_{h}:=\left(H\oplus R,\begin{pmatrix}1&h\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mathfrak{q}_{H}\oplus L^{s}[\![\mathfrak{j}]\!]\right).

It is formal to check that
[c]
defines an infinite Frobenius for
\underline{E}_{h}
so that the extension given by the pair
(\underline{E}_{h},[c])
defines an element of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})
. Its image through
d_{\underline{H}^{+}}
is
c
, as desired.

Before computing the kernel of
d_{\underline{H}^{+}}
, we begin with an observation. Let
c
be a cocycle
G_{L}\to H
such that
(\underline{E}_{h},[c])
defines an extension of
\mathbbm{1}^{+}
by
\underline{H}^{+}
in
\mathbf{HPk}_{L}^{+}

3.1
Definitions

In this subsection we review the usual setup of
A
-motives. Recall that
(C,\pazocal{O}_{C})
is a geometrically irreductible smooth projective curve over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
with
q
elements,
\infty
is a closed point of
C
and
A
is the
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
\pazocal{O}_{C}(C\setminus\{\infty\})
.

Let
R
be an
A
-algebra through a
\mathbb{F}
-algebra morphism
\kappa:A\to R
. We denote by
A\otimes R
the tensor product over
\mathbb{F}
, and we let
\mathfrak{j}=\mathfrak{j}_{\kappa}
be the kernel of the multiplication map
A\otimes R\to R
,
a\otimes r\mapsto\kappa(a)r
. The following observation appears in
[
Har19
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The ideal
\mathfrak{j}
is a projective
A\otimes R
-module of rank
1
. In particular,
V(\mathfrak{j})
defines a Cartier divisor on
\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes R
.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}
the kernel of the multiplication map
A\otimes A\to A
. Being projective of rank
1
is a property stable by base-change, and as
\mathfrak{j}_{\kappa}=\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}\otimes_{A\otimes A}(A\otimes R)
, it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}
is projective of rank
1
over
A\otimes A
.

We first observe that
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}
is finite projective. Indeed,
A\otimes A
is a Noetherian domain, so it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}
is flat. We use Bourbaki’s flatness criterion:
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}
is torsion free, and
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}/\mathfrak{j}^{2}_{\operatorname{id}}
is flat as it is isomorphic to
\Omega_{A/\mathbb{F}}
itself flat over
A\cong(A\otimes A)/\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}
. The criterion applies to show that
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}
is flat. To conclude that it has constant rank
1
, it suffices to observe that
\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes A)
is connected and that
\mathfrak{j}_{\operatorname{id}}/\mathfrak{j}^{2}_{\operatorname{id}}
has rank
1
over
A
.
∎

Let
\tau
be the ring endomorphism of
A\otimes R
acting as the identity on
A
and as raising to the
q
th power on
R
(i.e.
\tau(a\otimes r)=a\otimes r^{q}
).

Definition 3.2
.

An
A
-motif of rank

over

is a pair
\underline{M}=(M,\tau_{M})
where

is a locally-free module over
A\otimes R
together with a
\tau
-linear isomorphism outside the zero locus

of

:

\tau_{M}:(\tau^{*}M)|_{(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes R)\setminus V(\mathfrak{j})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M|_{(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes R)\setminus V(\mathfrak{j})}.

We call
\underline{M}

effective
whenever
\tau_{M}
comes from the pullback by
\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes R\to(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes R)\setminus V(\mathfrak{j})
of a morphism
\tau^{*}M\to M
.

A morphism
(M,\tau_{M})\to(N,\tau_{N})
of
A
-motives over
R
is an
A\otimes R
-linear morphism

such that
f\circ\tau_{M}=\tau^{*}f\circ\tau_{N}
. We let
A\mathbf{Mot}_{R}
denote the
A
-linear category of
A
-motives over
R
.

Remark 3.3
.

Along this text, we shall denote by

the module

. This agrees with the convention taken in
[

]
, where

is rather described as the submodule of

- where

denotes the localization of

at the non zero-divisor - consisting in elements

for which there exists a positive integer

such that
\mathfrak{j}^{n}x\in M
.

Most of the results in this text are concern with
A
-motives over
K
(with
\kappa:A\to K
being the inclusion). For those, one has a notion of weights that we recall briefly (the reader will find all details in
[
Gaz22
, §3]
). There is an (exact) functor

A\mathbf{Mot}_{K}\longrightarrow{}_{\infty}\mathbf{Isoc}_{K},\quad\underline{M}\longmapsto\pazocal{I}_{\infty}({\underline{M}})

where the targeted category is that of

-isocrystals over

. The category

admits a

in the sense of

, hence any object admits a unique slope filtration (the Harder-Narasimhan filtration)

, Thm. 3.9]
. It is said that

has weights

3.2
The Betti realization functor

We let

be the fraction field of

(equivalently, the function field of

of an

-motive (Definition

) and discuss

(Definition

Tate algebras

Let

.

We denote by

-algebra given by the completion of

Remark 3.5
.

The notation

The following preliminary lemma will be used next, in the definition of the Betti realization functor.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\kappa:A\to L
be an

-algebra morphism with discrete image. We have
\mathfrak{j}_{\kappa}L\langle A\rangle=L\langle A\rangle
.

Proof.

Because

is discrete in

, it contains an element

of norm

. Let

be such that

. Then,

and the series

converges in

to the inverse of

.
∎

The following proposition rephrases

Let

For effective

is zero. The above is isomorphic to
M\otimes_{K[t]}\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},K_{\infty}^{s}\langle t\rangle)
, hence it suffices to show that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},K_{\infty}^{s}\langle t\rangle)
vanishes. By continuity, it is enough to show the vanishing of
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},L\langle t\rangle)
for any subfield
L\subset K_{\infty}^{s}
that is a finite Galois extension of
K_{\infty}
. For such
L
, we denote by
H
the finite Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L|K_{\infty})
. By the additive version of Hilbert’s 90 Theorem
[
Ser68
, x.§1, Prop. 1]
, the module
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},L)=\operatorname{H}^{1}(H,L)
vanishes and thus
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},L[\![t]\!])
is zero. Therefore, there is a long exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle\longrightarrow K_{\infty}[\![t]\!]\longrightarrow\left(L[\![t]\!]/L\langle t\rangle\right)^{G_{\infty}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},L\langle t\rangle)\to 0.

In particular, for any continuous cocycle
c:G_{\infty}\to L\langle t\rangle
, we can find
f\in L[\![t]\!]
such that

\forall\sigma\in G_{\infty}:\quad c(\sigma)=f^{\sigma}-f.

Because
L
is separable over
K_{\infty}
its trace forme is non-degenerated, that is, there exists
\alpha\in L
such that
\eta:=\sum_{\sigma\in H}{\alpha^{\sigma}}\in K_{\infty}
is nonzero. Thus,
f
can be written as

f=\left(\eta^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in H}{\alpha^{\sigma}f^{\sigma}}\right)-\left(\eta^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in H}{\alpha^{\sigma}c(\sigma)}\right)\in K_{\infty}[\![t]\!]+L\langle t\rangle.

It follows that
c
is trivial, and that
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},L\langle t\rangle)=0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

3.3
Analytic continuation

To associate an Hodge-Pink structure to a rigid analytically trivial

-motive
\underline{M}
, it is crucial to understand the behaviour of elements in
\underline{M}_{B}
– which can be seen as

over the affinoid subdomain
\operatorname{Spm}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle

with values in

– near the point
\mathfrak{j}
. However, the latter does not belong to the maximal spectrum of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle A\rangle
, as one deduces from Lemma
3.6
. Hence it is necessary to extend elements of
\underline{M}_{B}
to a larger domain. In this subsection, we show that elements of
\underline{M}_{B}
can be

continuated to the whole rigid analytification of the affine curve
\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, with their only poles supported at
\mathfrak{j}
and its iterates
\tau\mathfrak{j}
,
\tau^{2}\mathfrak{j}
, … . In the
\deg(\infty)=1
-case, this is treated in
[
HJ20
, §2.3.4]
, for special functions of Anderson
A
-modules this is the subject of
[
GM22b
]
.

We recall some material from
[
GM22b
, §3]
. let
L
be any complete subfield of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
that contains
K_{\infty}
and let
|\cdot|
be the norm on
L
it inherits as a subfield of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. We define Gauss norms on
A\otimes L
: let

and let
\rho:=|c|>0
. For

, we set:

\|f\|_{\rho}:=\inf\left(\max_{i}\{|\ell_{i}|\rho^{\deg a_{i}}\}\right)

where the infinimum is taken over all representations of

as finite sums
\sum_{i}{a_{i}\otimes\ell_{i}}
in
A\otimes L
. The application
\|\cdot\|_{\rho}
is indeed a Gauss norm
[
GM22b
, Prop. 3.2]
, and we denote by
L\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
the affinoid algebra over
L
obtained by completing
A\otimes L
with respect to it. Observe that
L\langle A\rangle
as in Definition
3.4
coincides with
L\langle A\rangle_{1}
(see
[
GM21
, Prop. 2.2]
). If
\rho<\rho^{\prime}
, there is a canonical inclusion
L\langle A\rangle_{\rho^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow L\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
and we set:

L\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}A\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}:=\varprojlim_{\rho}L\langle A\rangle_{\rho}

where
\rho
runs over
|L^{\times}|
. If
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{L}
denote the rigid analytic variety
(\operatorname{Spec}A\otimes L)^{\text{rig}}
over

, then
\mathfrak{X}
is isomorphic to the glueing
\varinjlim_{\rho}\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}
where
\mathfrak{X}_{\rho}:=\operatorname{Sp}L\langle A\rangle_{\rho}
. In particular,
L\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}A\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}
is the ring of global sections of
\pazocal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}
.

As
\|\tau(f)\|_{\rho}=\|f\|_{\rho^{1/q}}^{q}
for

,
\tau
induces a morphism
L\langle A\rangle_{\rho}\to L\langle A\rangle_{\rho^{1/q}}
which are compatible: we still denote by
\tau
the resulting morphism
L\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}A\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}\to L\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}A\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}
and

\tau:\mathfrak{X}_{L}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{L}.

For
m\geq 1
, we denote by
J_{m}
the divisor corresponding to
\mathfrak{j}+\tau\mathfrak{j}+...+\tau^{m}\mathfrak{j}
on
\mathfrak{X}
and by

the rigid analytic divisor
J_{\infty}=\mathfrak{j}+\tau\mathfrak{j}+...
. For
n\geq 0
, we introduce

\pazocal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J):=\operatorname{colim}_{m}\pazocal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J_{m})

as the sheaf of meromorphic functions on
\mathfrak{X}
with a pole of order at most
n
at the support of
J
.

Example 3.20
.

Let
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}
and let
\infty
be the point
[0:1]
. We identify
A
with
\mathbb{F}[t]
and the tensor product
A\otimes K
with
K[t]
. We let
\theta\in K
denote the image of
t
so that
\tau^{i}\mathfrak{j}
corresponds to the principal ideal
(t-\theta^{q^{i}})
of
K[t]
. We have

L\langle A\rangle=L\langle t\rangle=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}t^{n}}~\bigg|~a_{n}\in L;~\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}\to 0\right\},

L\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}A\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=L\mathopen{\hbox{${\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\langle}$}}t\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}t^{n}}~\bigg|~a_{n}\in L;~\forall\rho>1:~\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}\rho^{n}\to 0\right\}.

(3.7)

The ring

corresponds to series converging in the

unit disc, whereas

acts on both rings by mapping

Now,
\pazocal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}}(n\cdot J)(\mathfrak{X})
is identified with the subring of

consisting of elements

such that

By Lemma

,

is not supported on

given by sheaf restriction. We are now in position to prove the main result of this subsection (compare with

Theorem 3.21
.

Let
\underline{M}
be a rigid analytically trivial
A
-motive over
K
. Let

Before engaging in the proof, we set some definitions. Let

be a non-constant element and denote by

the element

in

. As

is finite flat, the multiplication maps

in

.

Let

be such that

and

is a

Regulators and finiteness theorems

4.1
Regulators of
A
-motives

Let
\underline{M}=(M,\tau_{M})
be an

-motive over
K
. Recall from
[

, §4]
that there exists a unique
A\otimes A
-submodule

of

over

[

(4.1)

In

, we introduced the sub-

-module of
integral
and
regulated
extensions of
\mathbbm{1}
by
\underline{M}
in

, denoted

\iota:\frac{N_{A}}{(\operatorname{id}-\tau_{M})(M)}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\text{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M}),

(4.2)

assigning to the class of

the extension whose middle object has underlying module

and
\tau

Some computations suggested that

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
\underline{M}
is rigid analytically trivial. Both

The proof of Theorem

(and Theorem

below) is postponed to the last section. Let us comment the above statement.

-

Classically, the

-vector space

over
\mathbb{Q}

[

, §III]
). First observe that, in our analogy, Theorem

is the function field counterpart of this expectation: classically, the analogue of

-group

denoting the Betti realization of

-

A second observation, already announced and corroborating the analogy made in the above paragraph, is that the

is typically not finitely generated. To wit, Theorem

roughly tells that a set of generators has - up to a finite set - the same cardinality as one for

-

The modules

\underline{M}

4.3
Strategy of proofs

In the following section, we develop the theory of shtuka models for
\underline{M}
. Roughly speaking, they are the data of
(\pazocal{M},\pazocal{N},\tau{M})
where
\pazocal{M}\hookrightarrow\pazocal{N}
are two coherent sheaves on
\operatorname{Spec}A\times C
, and where
\tau_{\pazocal{M}}:\tau^{*}\pazocal{M}\to\pazocal{N}
is a morphism which restricts to
\tau_{M}:\tau^{*}M_{A}\to N_{A}
on the affine part
\operatorname{Spec}A\times\operatorname{Spec}A
together with some vanishing condition along
\infty
(Definition
5.1
). Shtuka models for
\underline{M}
always exist (Proposition
5.2
) though they are far from being unique. If the weights of
\underline{M}
are negative, our construction extends to sheaves on
C\times C
(Definition
5.4
, Theorem
5.6
).

One of the main step towards the proof of the theorems is that there is a quasi-isomorphism

G_{\underline{M}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{R}\!\Gamma\!\left(\operatorname{Spec}A\times C,\pazocal{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota-\tau{M}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pazocal{N}\right)\quad(\text{Proposition~}\ref{prop:ge-in-terms-of-cohomology}).

(4.8)

That
C
is proper over
\mathbb{F}
leads to the perfectness of
G_{\underline{M}}
. The fundamental exact sequence of Proposition
4.6
then implies that both
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
and
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{M})
are finitely generated
A
-modules, thus completing the proof of the first part of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem
4.4
and the second part of Theorem
4.1
follow from subtler observations on
C\times C
-shtuka models along
\operatorname{Spf}\pazocal{O}_{\infty}\hat{\times}\operatorname{Spf}\pazocal{O}_{\infty}
. Denoting by
\hat{\pazocal{M}}
and
\hat{\pazocal{N}}
the corresponding formal sheaves and by
\hat{\pazocal{M}}_{\infty}
and
\hat{\pazocal{N}}_{\infty}
the
\pazocal{O}_{\infty}\otimes\pazocal{O}_{\infty}
-modules of their global sections, the
coup de théâtre
of Subsection
5.3
is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\underline{M}_{B}^{+}\otimes_{A}K_{\infty}\longrightarrow\frac{\hat{\pazocal{N}}_{\infty}}{(\iota-\tau_{\pazocal{M}})(\hat{\pazocal{M}}_{\infty})}\otimes_{\pazocal{O}_{\infty}}K_{\infty}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\mathscr{H}^{+}_{K_{\infty}}(\underline{M}))\longrightarrow 0

(see Corollary
5.19
). The material used to construct this sequence resembles much to the techniques employed by V. Lafforgue in
[
Laf09
, §4]
. Although we do use the theory of function fields Fontaine rings as developed in
[
GL11
]
or
[
Har09
]
, the results involved might be reminiscent of such a theory at the place
\infty
.

Now, using classical derived algebro-geometric methods, we show that there is a quasi-isomorphism of complexes of
K_{\infty}
-vector spaces:

\operatorname{R}\!\Gamma\!\left(\operatorname{Spf}\pazocal{O}_{\infty}\hat{\times}C,\hat{\pazocal{M}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota-\tau{M}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{\pazocal{N}}\right)\cong\frac{\hat{\pazocal{N}}_{\infty}}{(\iota-\tau_{\pazocal{M}})(\hat{\pazocal{M}}_{\infty})}[0].

(4.9)

The last ingredient in our proof is Grothendieck’s comparison Theorem between Zariski and formal coherent cohomology, which amounts to a quasi-isomorphism among the left-hand side of (

) tensored along

and the right-hand side of (

) tensored along

.

This reads both that

sits in degree

- hence that
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{M})
is torsion - and that the dimension of

is the same as that of
\operatorname{Ext}^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\otimes_{A}K_{\infty}

. Let

-linear automorphism of

Proof.

The statement for

and two positive integers

To show that

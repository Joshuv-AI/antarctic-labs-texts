Abstract
. We generalize the completely monotone conjecture ( [ CG15 ] ) from Shannon entropy to the Tsallis entropy up for orders up to at least four. To this end, we employ the algorithm ( [ Jün16 , JM06a ] ) which employs the technique of systematic integrations-by-parts.



1.
Introduction

Suppose that
X
is a continuous random variable with probability density function
g(x)
. Given
\alpha>0
, we introduce the entropy defined by

(1)

H_{\alpha}[X]=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(y)\,e^{-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2\,t}}\,dy\right)^{\alpha}\,dx\right).

By reversing the order of integration, it is readily seen that

(2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(y)\,e^{-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2\,t}}\,dy\right)\,dx

holds. Applying L’Hôpital’s rule gives

(3)

\lim_{\alpha\to 1}H_{\alpha}[X]=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\,\log u\,dx:=\mathcal{S}[u],

where
\mathcal{S}[u]
is the Shannon entropy (
[
Sha48
]
) and
u=u(x,t)
is given by

(4)

u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(y)\,e^{-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2\,t}}\,dy.

Now we can rewrite
H_{\alpha}[X]
as

(5)

H_{\alpha}[X]=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u(x,t)\right)^{\alpha}\,dx\right),

which we call a generalized Tsallis entropy as when
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\,dx=1
,
H_{\alpha}[X]
in (
5
) reduces to the Tsallis entropy (
[
Tsa88
]
).

Definition 1.1

(Tsallis Entropy)
.

Let
w=w(x)
be a probability density function. The Tsallis entropy is defined by

(6)

H_{\alpha}[w]=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(1-\int_{\mathbb{R}}w^{\alpha}\,dx\right),

where
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
is a constant.

The Tsallis entropy introduced in ?THM?
generalizes Shannon entropy in the sense of

(7)

\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(1-\int_{\mathbb{R}}w^{\alpha}\,dx\right)\to-\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\,\log w\,dx

as
\alpha\to 1
.

From the viewpoint of partial differential equations (PDEs), (
4
) can be interpreted as the solution formula to the Cauchy problem for the heat equation, i.e.
u=u(x,t)
given by (
4
) satisfies

(8)

\begin{cases}\vskip 8.53581pt\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\dfrac{1}{2}\,\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}},\ \ &(x,t)\in(-\infty,\infty)\times(0,\infty),\\
u(x,0)=g(x),\ \ &x\in(-\infty,\infty),\end{cases}

where
g\in C(\mathbb{R})\bigcap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is the initial condition. Also, (
4
) can be viewed as a convolution formula

(9)

u(x,t)=(g\ast\phi)(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(y)\,\phi(x-y,t)\,dy=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,t}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(y)\,e^{-\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2\,t}}\,dy,

where
\phi(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,t}}\,e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2\,t}}
is the fundamental solution to the heat equation
u_{t}=\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}
. Henceforth, we assume that
g\in C(\mathbb{R})\bigcap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a probability density function, unless otherwise stated.

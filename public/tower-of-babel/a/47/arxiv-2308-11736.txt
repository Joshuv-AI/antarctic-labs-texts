Smooth min-entropy lower bounds for approximation chains
Ashutosh Marwah and Frédéric Dupuis

Abstract
For a state ρ A 1 n ​ B \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B} , we call a sequence of states ( σ A 1 k ​ B ( k ) ) k = 1 n (\sigma_{A_{1}^{k}B}^{(k)})_{k=1}^{n} an approximation chain if for every 1 ≤ k ≤ n 1\leq k\leq n , ρ A 1 k ​ B ≈ ϵ σ A 1 k ​ B ( k ) \rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}\approx_{\epsilon}\sigma_{A_{1}^{k}B}^{(k)} . In general, it is not possible to lower bound the smooth min-entropy of such a ρ A 1 n ​ B \rho_{A_{1}^{n}B} , in terms of the entropies of σ A 1 k ​ B ( k ) \sigma_{A_{1}^{k}B}^{(k)} without incurring very large penalty factors. In this paper, we study such approximation chains under additional assumptions. We begin by proving a simple entropic triangle inequality, which allows us to bound the smooth min-entropy of a state in terms of the Rényi entropy of an arbitrary auxiliary state while taking into account the smooth max-relative entropy between the two. Using this triangle inequality, we create lower bounds for the smooth min-entropy of a state in terms of the entropies of its approximation chain in various scenarios. In particular, utilising this approach, we prove approximate versions of the asymptotic equipartition property and entropy accumulation. In the companion paper [ MD24 ] , we show that the techniques developed in this paper can be used to prove the security of quantum key distribution in the presence of source correlations.

Introduction

One-shot information theory investigates the behaviour of tasks in communication and cryptography under general unstructured processes, as opposed to independent and identically distributed (i.i.d) processes, where the states or the tasks themselves have a certain tensor product structure. This is crucial for information theoretically secure cryptography, where one cannot place any kind of assumption on the actions of the adversary (see, for example,
[

In this section, we derive a simple entropic triangle inequality (Lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\rho
and
\eta
be subnormalised states and
Q
be a positive operator, then for
\alpha>1
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho||Q)\leq\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\eta||Q)+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}D_{\max}(\rho||\eta)+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{\tr(\eta)}{\tr(\rho)}

and for
\alpha<1
if one of
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\eta||Q)
and
D_{\max}(\rho||\eta)
is finite (otherwise we cannot define their difference), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho||Q)\geq\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\eta||Q)-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}D_{\max}(\rho||\eta)-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\frac{\tr(\eta)}{\tr(\rho)}.

Proof.

If
D_{\max}(\rho||\eta)=\infty
, then both statements are true trivially. Otherwise, we have that
\rho\leq 2^{D_{\max}(\rho||\eta)}\eta
and also
\rho\ll\eta
. Now, if
\rho\not\ll Q
then
\eta\not\ll Q
. Hence, for
\alpha>1

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho||Q)=\infty

, then the Lemma is also trivially satisfied. For the remaining cases we have,

\displaystyle 2^{(\alpha-1)\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho||Q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tr\left(Q^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}\rho Q^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}}{\tr(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tr\left(Q^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}2^{D_{\max}(\rho||\eta)}\eta Q^{-\frac{\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}}{\tr(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tr(\eta)}{\tr(\rho)}2^{\alpha D_{\max}(\rho||\eta)}2^{(\alpha-1)\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\eta||Q)}

where we used the fact that

is monotone increasing if the function

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}:=\max_{\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho_{AB})}\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\tilde{\rho}}.

(18)

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\alpha\in(1,\infty]
and
\epsilon\in[0,1)
, and states
\rho_{AB}
and
\eta_{AB}

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}D^{\epsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{AB}||\eta_{AB})-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\rho}_{AB}\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho_{AB})
be a subnormalised state such that

. Using Lemma

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\tilde{\rho}_{AB}||\Id_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\eta_{AB}||\Id_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})+\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}D^{\epsilon}_{\max}({\rho}_{AB}||\eta_{AB})+\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{2}}

(19)

where we used the fact that

which implies that

. Since, the above bound is true for arbitrary states

, we can multiply it by

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\tilde{\rho}}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}D^{\epsilon}_{\max}(\rho_{AB}||\eta_{AB})-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{2}}.

The desired bound follows by using the fact that
\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,\epsilon}(A|B)_{{\rho}}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\tilde{\rho}}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For a state
\rho_{AB}

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon+\delta}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}-\frac{g_{0}(\delta)}{\alpha-1}

Proof.

First, note that

(20)

To prove this, consider a

Using this, we have

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\tilde{\rho}\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho_{AB})}\left\{\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\tilde{\rho}}-\frac{g_{0}(\delta)}{\alpha-1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,\epsilon}(A|B)_{\rho}-\frac{g_{0}(\delta)}{\alpha-1}

where we have used

(5)

We can use the asymptotic equipartition theorem for smooth min-entropy and max-relative entropy

Corollary 3.6

For
\alpha\in(1,2]

and

4
Approximately independent registers

In this section, we introduce our technique for using the smooth min-entropy triangle inequality for considering approximations by studying a state
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
such that for every
k\in[n]

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B}-\rho_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{A_{1}^{k-1}B}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon.

(23)

We assume that the registers
A_{k}
all have the same dimension equal to
|A|
. One should think of the registers
A_{k}
as the secret information produced during some protocol, which also provides the register
B
to an adversary. We would like to prove that
H^{f(\epsilon)}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)
is large (lower bounded by
\Omega(n)
) under the above
approximate independence conditions
for some reasonably small function
f
of
\epsilon
and close to
nH(A_{1})
, if we assume the states
\rho_{A_{k}}
are identical. Let us first examine the case when the states above are completely classical. To show that in this case the smooth min-entropy is high, we will show that the set where the conditional probability
\rho(a_{1}^{n}|b):=\frac{\rho(a_{1}^{n}b)}{\rho(b)}
can be large, has a small probability using the Markov inequality. We will use the following lemma for this purpose.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
p,q
are probability distributions on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|p-q\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, then
S\subseteq\mathcal{X}
defined as
S:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:p(x)\leq(1+\epsilon^{1/2})q(x)\}
is such that
q(S)\geq 1-\epsilon^{1/2}
and
p(S)\geq 1-\epsilon^{1/2}-\epsilon
.

Proof.

For
S^{c}:=\mathcal{X}\setminus S
, where
S
is the set defined above we have that

\displaystyle\epsilon\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|p-q\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\max_{H\subseteq\mathcal{X}}|p(H)-q(H)|

\displaystyle\geq q(S^{c})\left|\frac{p(S^{c})}{q(S^{c})}-1\right|

\displaystyle\geq q(S^{c})\left(\frac{p(S^{c})}{q(S^{c})}-1\right)

\displaystyle=q(S^{c})\left(\frac{\sum_{x\in S^{c}}p(x)}{\sum_{x\in S^{c}}q(x)}-1\right)

\displaystyle\geq q(S^{c})\left(\frac{\sum_{x\in S^{c}}(1+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}})q(x)}{\sum_{x\in S^{c}}q(x)}-1\right)

\displaystyle\geq q(S^{c})\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}

which implies that
q(S^{c})\leq\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Now, the statement of the Lemma follows.
∎

We will also assume for the sake of simplicity that
\rho_{A_{k}}
are identical for all
k\in[n]
. Using the Lemma above, for every
k\in[n]
, we know that the set

\displaystyle B_{k}:

\displaystyle=\left\{(a_{1}^{n},b):\rho(a_{1}^{k},b)>(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\rho(a_{1}^{k-1},b)\rho(a_{k})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(a_{1}^{n},b):\rho(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)>(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\rho(a_{k})\right\}

satisfies
\Pr_{\rho}(B_{k})\leq 2\sqrt{\epsilon}
. We can now define
L=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\chi_{B_{k}}
, which is a random variable that simply counts the number of bad sets
B_{k}
an element
(a_{1}^{n},b)
belongs to. Using the Markov inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Pr_{\rho}\left[L>n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\right]\leq\frac{\Expect_{\rho}[L]}{n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}\leq 2\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}.

We can define the bad set
\mathcal{B}:=\left\{(a_{1}^{n},b):L(a_{1}^{n},b)>n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\}
, then we can define the subnormalised distribution
\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}^{n}B}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}^{n}B}(a_{1}^{n},b)=\begin{cases}\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}(a_{1}^{n},b)&(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in\mathcal{B}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}.

We have
P(\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}^{n}B},\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B})\leq\sqrt{2}\epsilon^{1/8}
. Further, note that for every
(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in\mathcal{B}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(a_{1}^{n}|b)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\rho(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}}\rho(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\in B_{k}}\rho(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{n}\prod_{k:(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}}\rho_{A_{k}}(a_{k})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{n}2^{-n(1-\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})H_{\min}(A_{1})}

where in the third line we have used the fact that if
(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}
, then
\rho(a_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b)\leq(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\rho_{A_{k}}(a_{k})
and in the last line we have used the fact that for
(a_{1}^{k},b)\not\in\mathcal{B}
, we have
|\{k\in[n]:(a_{1}^{n},b)\not\in B_{k}\}|=n-L(a_{1}^{n},b)\geq n(1-\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})
, that all the states
\rho_{A_{k}}
are identical and
2^{-H_{\min}(A_{k})}=\max_{a_{k}}\rho_{A_{k}}(a_{k})
. Note that we have essentially proven and used a
D_{\max}
bound above. This proves the following lower bound for the smooth min-entropy of
\rho

\displaystyle H^{\sqrt{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B)\geq n(1-\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})H_{\min}(A_{1})-n\log(1+\sqrt{\epsilon}).

(24)

The right-hand side above can be improved to get the Shannon entropy
H
instead of the min-entropy
H_{\min}
. However, we will not pursue this here, since this bound is sufficient for the purpose of our discussion.

Although, we are unable to generalise the classical argument above to the quantum case, it provides a great amount of insight into the approximately independent registers problem. Two important examples of distributions, which satisfy the approximate independence conditions above were mentioned in Footnotes
(2)
and
(3)
earlier. To create the first distribution, we flip a biased coin
B
, which is
0
with probability
\epsilon
and
1
otherwise. If
B=0
, then
A_{1}^{n}
is set to the constant all zero string otherwise it is sampled randomly and independently. For this distribution, once the bad event (
B=0
) is removed, the new distribution has a high min-entropy. On the other hand, for the second distribution,
Q_{A_{1}^{2n}B_{1}^{2n}}
, we have that the random bits
B_{i}
are chosen independently, with each being equal to
0
with probability
\epsilon
and
1
otherwise. If the bit
B_{i}
is
0
, then
A_{i}
is set equal to
A_{i-1}
otherwise it is sampled independently. In this case, there is no small probability (small as a function of
\epsilon
) event, that one can simply remove, so that the distribution becomes i.i.d. However, we expect that with high probability the number of
B_{i}=0
is close to
2n\epsilon
. Given that the distribution samples all the other
A_{i}
independently, the smooth min-entropy for the distribution should be close to
2n(1-\epsilon)H(A_{1})
. The above argument shows that any distribution satisfying the approximate independence conditions in Eq.
23
can be handled by combining the methods used for these two example distributions, that is, deleting the bad part of the distribution and recognising that the probability for every element in the rest of the space behaves independently on average.

The above classical argument is difficult to generalise to quantum states primarily because the quantum equivalents of Lemma
4.1
are not as nice and simple. Furthermore, quantum conditional probabilities themselves are also difficult to use. Fortunately, the substate theorem serves as the perfect tool for developing a smooth max-relative entropy bound, which we can then use with the min-entropy triangle inequality. The quantum substate theorem
[
JRS02
,
JN11
]
provides an upper bound on the smooth max relative entropy
D^{\epsilon}_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)
between two states in terms of their relative entropy
D(\rho||\sigma)
.

Theorem 4.2

(Quantum substate theorem
[
JN11
]
)
.

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be two states on the same Hilbert space. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle D^{\sqrt{\epsilon}}_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)\leq\frac{D(\rho||\sigma)+1}{\epsilon}+\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}.

(25)

In this section, we will also frequently use the multipartite mutual information
[
Wat60
,
Hor94
,
CMS02
]
. For a state
\rho_{X_{1}^{n}}
, the multipartite mutual information between the registers
(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n})
is defined as

\displaystyle I(X_{1}:X_{2}:\cdots:X_{n})_{\rho}:=D(\rho_{X_{1}^{n}}||\rho_{X_{1}}\tensor\rho_{X_{2}}\tensor\cdots\tensor\rho_{X_{n}}).

(26)

In other words, it is the relative entropy between
\rho_{X_{1}^{n}}
and
\rho_{X_{1}}\tensor\rho_{X_{2}}\tensor\cdots\tensor\rho_{X_{n}}
. It can easily be shown that the multipartite mutual information satisfies the following identities:

\displaystyle I(X_{1}:X_{2}:\cdots:X_{n})_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(X_{k})_{\rho}-H(X_{1}\cdots\ X_{n})_{\rho}

(27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{n}I(X_{k}:X_{1}^{k-1}).

(28)

Going back to proving a bound for the quantum approximately independent registers problem, note that using the Alicki-Fannes-Winter (AFW) bound
[
AF04
,
Win16
]
for mutual information
[
Wil13
, Theorem 11.10.4]
, Eq.
23
implies that for every
k\in[n]

\displaystyle I(A_{k}:A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}\leq\epsilon\log|A|+g_{2}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)

(29)

where
g_{2}(x):=(x+1)\log(x+1)-x\log(x)
. With this in mind, we can now focus our efforts on proving the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let registers
A_{k}
have dimension
|A|
for all
k\in[n]

\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}
is such that for every

we have

\displaystyle I(A_{k}:A_{1}^{k-1}B)_{\rho}\leq\epsilon

for some

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}+\epsilon}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k})_{\rho}-3n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\log(1+2|A|)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\epsilon^{3/4}}-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\epsilon^{1/4}}\left(\log(1-\sqrt{\epsilon})+g_{1}(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})\right)

(31)

where
g_{1}(x,y):=-\log(1-\sqrt{1-x^{2}})-\log(1-y^{2})
. In particular, when all the states
\rho_{A_{k}}
are identical, we have

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}+\epsilon}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq n\left(H(A_{1})_{\rho}-3\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\log(1+2|A|)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\epsilon^{3/4}}-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\epsilon^{1/4}}\left(\log(1-\sqrt{\epsilon})+g_{1}(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})\right).

(32)

Proof.

First note that we have,

\displaystyle I(A_{1}:A_{2}:\cdots:A_{n}:B)

\displaystyle=D(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}I(A_{k}:A_{1}^{k-1}B)

\displaystyle\leq n\epsilon.

Using the substate theorem, we now have

\displaystyle D_{\max}^{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}\middle\|\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{B}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{B})+1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}-\log(1-\sqrt{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\leq n\sqrt{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}-\log(1-\sqrt{\epsilon}).

(33)

We now define the auxiliary state
\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B}:=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{A_{k}}\otimes\rho_{B}
. Using Lemma
3.5
, for
\alpha\in(1,2)
, we can transform the smooth min-entropy into an
\alpha
-Rényi entropy on the auxiliary product state

as follows:

\displaystyle H

{}_{\min}^{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}+\epsilon}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\eta}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}D^{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\max}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B})-\frac{g_{1}(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})}{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k})_{\rho}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}D^{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{\max}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B}||\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B})-\frac{g_{1}(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})}{\alpha-1}

In the third line above, we have used

for which the above

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}+\epsilon}(A_{1}^{n}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k})_{\rho}-3n\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}\log(1+2|A|)-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\epsilon^{3/4}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\epsilon^{1/4}}\left(\log(1-\sqrt{\epsilon})+g_{1}(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4}})\right).

Then, we have that for
\delta=\epsilon\log\left(|A||B|\right)+g_{2}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)
such that

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}+\delta}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k}|B_{k})_{\rho}-3n\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}\log(1+2|A|)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\delta^{3/4}}-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\delta^{1/4}}\left(\log(1-\sqrt{\delta})+g_{1}(\delta,\delta^{\frac{1}{4}})\right)

where

and
g_{2}(x)=(x+1)\log(x+1)-x\log(x)
. In particular, when all the states

are identical, we have

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}+\delta}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq n\left(H(A_{1}|B_{1})_{\rho}-3\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}\log(1+2|A|)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\delta^{3/4}}-\frac{2\log(1+2|A|)}{\delta^{1/4}}\left(\log(1-\sqrt{\delta})+g_{1}(\delta,\delta^{\frac{1}{4}})\right).

(39)

Proof.

To prove this, we use the auxiliary state
\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}:=\bigotimes\rho_{A_{k}B_{k}}\otimes\rho_{E}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle D(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}||\eta_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E})

\displaystyle=I(A_{1}B_{1}:A_{2}B_{2}:\cdots:A_{n}B_{n}:E)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}I(A_{k}B_{k}:A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq n\left(\epsilon\log\left(|A||B|\right)+g\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\right)=n\delta

where we used the AFW bound for mutual information in the last line
[
Wil13
, Theorem 11.10.4]
. The rest of the proof follows the proof of Theorem
4.3
, only difference being that now we have
\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\eta}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k}|B_{k})_{\rho}
.
∎

We call this generalisation

because the smoothing term (
\delta

. In Appendix

4.2
Simple security proof for sequential device independent quantum key distribution

The approximately independent register scenario and the associated min-entropy lower bound can be used to provide simple “proof of concept” security proofs for cryptographic protocols. In this section, we will briefly sketch a proof for sequential device independent quantum key distribution (DIQKD) to demonstrate this idea. The protocol for sequential DIQKD used in
[
AF20
]
is presented as Protocol
1
.

Sequential DIQKD protocol

Parameters:

•

\omega_{\text{exp}}
is the expected winning probability for the honest implementation of the device

•

n\geq 1
is the number of rounds in the protocol

•

\gamma\in(0,1]

Protocol:

1.

For every
0\leq i\leq n

(a)

(using

{Protocol}

We consider a simple model for DIQKD, where Eve (the adversary) distributes a state
\rho^{(0)}_{E_{A}E_{B}E}
between Alice and Bob at the beginning of the protocol. Alice and Bob then use their states sequentially as given in Protocol
1
. The
k
th round of the protocol produces the questions
X_{k},Y_{k}
and
T_{k}
, the answers
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
and transforms the shared state from
\rho^{(k-1)}_{E_{A}E_{B}E}
to
\rho^{(k)}_{E_{A}E_{B}E}
.

Given the questions and answers of the previous rounds, the state shared between Alice and Bob and their devices in each round can be viewed as a device for playing the CHSH game. Suppose in the
k
th round, the random variables produced in the previous
k-1
rounds are
r_{k-1}:=x_{1}^{k-1},y_{1}^{k-1},t_{1}^{k-1},a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1}
and that the state shared between Alice and Bob is
\rho^{(k-1)}_{E_{A}E_{B}E|r_{k-1}}
. We can then define
\Pr[W_{k}|r_{k-1}]
to be the winning probability of the CHSH game played by Alice and Bob using the state and their devices in the
k
th round. Note that Alice’s device cannot distinguish whether the CHSH game is played in a round or is used for key generation. We can further take an average over all the previous round’s random variables to derive the probability of winning the
k
th game

\displaystyle\Pr[W_{k}]=\Expect_{r_{k-1}}\left[\Pr[W_{k}|r_{k-1}]\right].

(40)

Alice and Bob randomly sample a subset of the rounds (using the random variable
T_{k}
) and play the CHSH game on this subset. If the average winning probability of CHSH game on this subset is small, they abort the protocol. For simplicity and brevity, we will assume here that the state
\rho^{(0)}_{E_{A}E_{B}E}
distributed between Alice and Bob at the start of the protocol by Eve has an average winning probability at least
\omega_{{\text{exp}}}
, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Pr[W_{k}]\geq\omega_{{\text{exp}}}-\delta

(41)

for some small
\delta>0
. Using standard sampling arguments it can be argued that either this is true or the protocol aborts with high probability.

For any shared state
\sigma_{E_{A}E_{B}E}
(where
E_{A}
is held by Alice,
E_{B}
is held by Bob and
E
is held by the adversary) and local measurement devices, if Alice and Bob win the CHSH game with a probability
\omega\in(\frac{3}{4},\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}]
, then Alice’s answer
A
to the game is random given the questions
X,Y
and the register
E
held by adversary. This is quantified by the following entropic bound
[
PAB+09
]
(see
[
AF20
, Lemma 5.3]
for the following form)

\displaystyle H(A|XYE)\geq f(\omega)=\begin{cases}1-h\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{16\omega(\omega-1)+3}\right)&\text{if }\omega\in[\frac{3}{4},\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}]\\
0&\text{if }\omega\in[0,\frac{3}{4})\end{cases}

(42)

where
h(\cdot)
is the binary entropy. The function
f
is convex over the interval
\left[0,\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}\right]
. We plot it in the interval
[\frac{3}{4},\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}]
in Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
The lower bound in Eq.
42
for the interval
[\frac{3}{4},\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}]

For
\epsilon>0
, we choose the parameter
\omega_{\text{exp}}\in[\frac{3}{4}+\delta,\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{4}]
to be large enough so that

\displaystyle 1-f(\omega_{\text{exp}}-\delta)=h\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{16(\omega_{\text{exp}}-\delta)(\omega_{\text{exp}}-\delta-1)+3}\right)\leq\epsilon^{4}.

(43)

We will now use Eq.
42
to bound the von Neumann entropy of the answers given Eve’s information for the sequential DIQKD protocol. We have

\displaystyle H(A_{1}^{n}|X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}T_{1}^{n}E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}T_{1}^{n}E)

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k}Y_{1}^{k}T_{1}^{k}E)

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{=}\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k}|X_{k}Y_{k}R_{k-1}E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Expect_{r_{k-1}\sim R_{k-1}}\left[H(A_{k}|X_{k}Y_{k}E)_{\rho^{(k)}_{|r_{k-1}}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(3)}{\geq}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Expect_{r_{k-1}\sim R_{k-1}}\left[f\left(\Pr[W_{k}|r_{k-1}]\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}f\left(\Pr[W_{k}]\right)

\displaystyle\geq nf\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Pr[W_{k}]\right)

\displaystyle\geq nf(\omega_{\text{exp}}-\delta)\geq n(1-\epsilon^{4})

where in (1) we have used the fact that the questions sampled in the rounds after the
k
th round are independent of the random variables in the previous rounds, in (2) we use the fact that Alice’s answers are independent of the random variable
T_{k}
given the question
X_{k}
and we also grouped the random variables generated in the previous round into the random variable
R_{k-1}:=A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}X_{1}^{k-1}Y_{1}^{k-1}T_{1}^{k-1}
, in (3) we use the bound in Eq.
42
, and in the next two steps we use convexity of
f
. If instead of the von Neumann entropy on the left-hand side above we had the smooth min-entropy, then the bound above would be sufficient to prove the security of DIQKD. However, this argument cannot be easily generalised to the smooth min-entropy because a chain rule like the one used in the first step does not exist for the smooth min-entropy (entropy accumulation
[
DFR20
,
MFSR22
]
generalises exactly such an argument). We can use the argument used for the approximately independent register case to transform this von Neumann entropy bound to a smooth min-entropy bound.

This bound results in the following bound on the multipartite mutual information

\displaystyle I(A_{1}:\cdots:A_{n}:X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}T_{1}^{n}E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k})+H(X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}T_{1}^{n}E)-H(A_{1}^{n}X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}T_{1}^{n}E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k})-H(A_{1}^{n}|X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}T_{1}^{n}E)

\displaystyle\leq n-n(1-\epsilon^{4})=n\epsilon^{4}

where we have used the dimension bound
H(A_{k})\leq 1
for every
k\in[n]
. This is the same as the multipartite mutual information bound we derived while analysing approximately independent registers in Theorem
4.3
. We can simply use the smooth min-entropy bound derived there here as well. This gives us the bound

\displaystyle H^{2\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|X_{1}^{n}Y_{1}^{n}T_{1}^{n}E)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}H(A_{k})-3n\epsilon\log 5-O\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=n(1-3\epsilon\log 5)-O\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\right)

(44)

where we have used the fact that the answers
A_{k}
can always be assumed to be uniformly distributed
[
PAB+09
,
AF20
]
. For every
\epsilon>0
, we can choose a sufficiently large
n
so that this bound is large and positive.

We note that this method is only able to provide “proof of concept” or existence type security proofs. This proof method couples the value of the security parameter for privacy amplification
\epsilon
with the average winning probability, which is not desirable. The parameter
\epsilon
is chosen according to the security requirements of the protocol and is typically very small. For such values of
\epsilon
, the average winning probability of the protocol will have to be extremely close to the maximum and we cannot realistically expect practical implementations to achieve such high winning probabilities. However, we do expect that this method will make it easier to create “proof of concept” type proofs for new cryptographic protocols in the future.

5
Approximate entropy accumulation

Figure 3:
The setting for entropy accumulation and Theorem

In general, it is very difficult to estimate the smooth min-entropy of states produced during cryptographic protocols. The entropy accumulation theorem (EAT)
[
DFR20
]
provides a tight and simple lower bound for the smooth min-entropy
H^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho}
of sequential processes, under certain Markov chain conditions. The state
\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}
in the setting for EAT is produced by a sequential process of the form shown in Figure
3
. The parties implementing the protocol begin with the registers
R_{0}
and
E
. In the context of a cryptographic protocol, the register
R_{0}
is usually held by the honest parties, whereas the register
E
is held by the adversary. Then, in each round
k\in[n]
of the process, a channel
\cM_{k}:R_{k-1}\rightarrow A_{k}B_{k}R_{k}
is applied on the register
R_{k-1}
to produce the registers
A_{k},B_{k}
and
R_{k}
. The registers
A_{1}^{n}
usually contain a partially secret raw key and the registers
B_{1}^{n}
contain the side information about
A_{1}^{n}
revealed to the adversary during the protocol. EAT requires that for every
k\in[n]
, the side information
B_{k}
satisfies the Markov chain
A_{1}^{k-1}\leftrightarrow B_{1}^{k-1}E\leftrightarrow B_{k}
, that is, the side information revealed in the
k
th round does not reveal anything more about the secret registers of the previous rounds than was already known to the adversary through
B_{1}^{k-1}E
. Under this assumption, EAT provides the following lower bound for the smooth min-entropy

\displaystyle H^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\inf_{\omega_{R_{k-1}\tilde{R}}}H(A_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R})_{\cM_{k}(\omega)}-c\sqrt{n}

(45)

where the infimum is taken over all input states to the channels
\cM_{k}
and
c>0
is a constant depending only on
|A|
(size of registers
A_{k}
) and
\epsilon
. We will state and prove an approximate version of EAT. Consider the sequential process in Figure
3
again. Now, suppose that the channels
\cM_{k}
do not necessarily satisfy the Markov chain conditions mentioned above, but each of the channels
\cM_{k}
can be
\epsilon
-approximated by
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
which satisfy the Markov chain
A_{1}^{k-1}\leftrightarrow B_{1}^{k-1}E\leftrightarrow B_{k}
for a certain collection of inputs. The approximate entropy accumulation theorem below provides a lower bound on the smooth min-entropy in such a setting. The proof of this theorem again uses the technique based on the smooth min-entropy triangle inequality developed in the previous section. In this setting too, we have a chain of approximations. For each
k\in[n]
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}

\displaystyle=\tr_{R_{k}}\circ\cM_{k}\left(\cM_{k-1}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})\right)

\displaystyle\approx_{\epsilon}\tr_{R_{k}}\circ\cM^{\prime}_{k}\left(\cM_{k-1}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})\right):=\sigma^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}.

According to the Markov chain assumption for the channels
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
, the state
\sigma^{(k)}_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E}
, satisfies the Markov chain
A_{1}^{k-1}\leftrightarrow B_{1}^{k-1}E\leftrightarrow B_{k}
. Therefore, we expect that the register
A_{k}
adds some entropy to the smooth min-entropy
H^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho}
and that the information leaked through
B_{1}^{n}
is not too large. We show that this is indeed the case in the approximate entropy accumulation theorem.

The approximate entropy accumulation theorem can be used to analyse and prove the security of cryptographic protocols under certain imperfections. For example, the entropy accumulation theorem can be used to prove the security of sequential device independent quantum key distribution (DIQKD) protocols
[
AFDF+18
]
. In these protocols, the side information
B_{k}
produced during each of the rounds are the questions used during the round to play a non-local game, like the CHSH game. In the ideal case, these questions are sampled independently of everything which came before. As an example of an imperfection, we can imagine that some physical effect between the memory storing the secret bits
A_{1}^{k-1}
and the device producing the questions may lead to a small correlation between the side information produced during the
k
th round and the secret bits
A_{1}^{k-1}
(also see
[
JK23
,
Tan23
]
). The approximate entropy accumulation theorem below can be used to prove security of DIQKD under such imperfections. We do not, however, pursue this example here and leave the applications of this theorem for future work. In Sec.

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\cM_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})||\cM^{\prime}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\prime}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E}))+\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon^{\prime})}{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\leq n\epsilon+O(1).

This bound can subsequently be used in Lemma
3.5
to relate the smooth min-entropy of the real state
\cM_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})
with the
\alpha-
Rényi conditional entropy of the auxiliary state
\cM^{\prime}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\prime}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})
, for which we can use the original entropy accumulation theorem.

In order to prove Theorem
5.1
, we broadly follow this idea. However, the condition
\left\|\cM_{k}-\cM^{\prime}_{k}\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon
does not lead to any kind of bound on
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\text{reg}}
or any other channel divergence. We will get around this issue by instead using mixed channels
\cM^{\delta}_{k}:=(1-\delta)\cM^{\prime}_{k}+\delta\cM_{k}
. Also, instead of trying to bound channel divergence in terms of
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}^{\text{reg}}
, we will bound the
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
(defined in the next section) channel divergence and use its chain rule. We develop the relevant
\alpha
-Rényi divergence bounds for this divergence in the next two subsections and then prove the theorem above in Sec
5.3
.

5.1
Divergence bound for approximately equal states

We will use the sharp Rényi divergence
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
defined in Ref.
[
FF21
]
(see
[
BSD21
]
for the following equivalent definition) in this section. For
\alpha>1
and two positive operators
P
and
Q
, it is defined

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(P||Q):=\min_{A\geq P}\hat{D}_{\alpha}(A||Q)

(53)

where
\hat{D}_{\alpha}(A||Q)
is the
\alpha
-Rényi geometric divergence
[
Mat18
]
. For
\alpha>1
, it is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\alpha}(A||Q)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\tr\left(Q\left(Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}AQ^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)&\text{if }A\ll Q\\
\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(54)

A
in the optimisation above is any operator
A\geq P
. In general, such an operator
A
is unnormalised. We will prove a bound on
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
between two states in terms of the distance between them and their max-relative entropy. In order to prove this bound, we require the following simple generalisation of the pinching inequality (see for example
[
Tom16
, Sec. 2.6.3]
).

Lemma 5.2

(Asymmetric pinching)
.

For
t>0
, a positive semidefinite operator
X\geq 0
and orthogonal projections
\Pi
and
\Pi_{\perp}=\Id-\Pi
, we have that

\displaystyle X\leq(1+t)\Pi X\Pi+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)\Pi_{\perp}X\Pi_{\perp}.

(55)

Proof.

We will write the positive matrix
X
as the block matrix

\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}\ X_{2}\\
X_{2}^{\ast}\ X_{3}\end{pmatrix}

where the blocks are partitioned according to the direct sum
\im(\Pi)\oplus\im(\Pi_{\perp})
. Then, the statement in the Lemma is equivalent to proving that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}\ &X_{2}\\
X_{2}^{\ast}\ &X_{3}\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}(1+t)X_{1}\ &0\\
0\ &0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\ &0\\
0\ &\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)X_{3}\end{pmatrix}

which is equivalent to proving that

\displaystyle 0\leq\begin{pmatrix}tX_{1}\ &-X_{2}\\
-X_{2}^{\ast}\ &\frac{1}{t}X_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

This is true because

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}tX_{1}\ &-X_{2}\\
-X_{2}^{\ast}\ &\frac{1}{t}X_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-t^{1/2}\ &0\\
0\ &t^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}\ &X_{2}\\
X_{2}^{\ast}\ &X_{3}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-t^{1/2}\ &0\\
0\ &t^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0

since
X\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
,
\rho
and
\sigma
be two normalised quantum states on the Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho-\sigma\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
and also
D_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)\leq d<\infty
, then we have the bound

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)\leq\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\log\left((1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}+\left(2^{\alpha d}\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}\right).

(56)

Note:
For a fixed
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, this upper bound tends to zero as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. On the other hand, for a fixed
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the upper bound tends to infinity as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
(that is, the bound becomes trivial). In Appendix
B
, we show that a bound of this form for
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
necessarily diverges for
\epsilon>0
as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
.

Proof.

Since,
D_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)<\infty
, we have that
\rho\ll\sigma
. We can assume that
\sigma
is invertible. If it was not, then we could always restrict our vector space to the subspace
\text{supp}(\sigma)
.

Let
\rho-\sigma=P-Q
, where
P\geq 0
is the positive part of the matrix
\rho-\sigma
and
Q\geq 0
is its negative part. We then have that
\tr(P)=\tr(Q)\leq\epsilon
.

Further, let

\displaystyle\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\ket{x_{i}}\bra{x_{i}}

(57)

be the eigenvalue decomposition of
\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Define the real vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

\displaystyle q(i):=\bra{x_{i}}\sigma\ket{x_{i}}.

Note that
q
is a probability distribution. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{I\sim q}\left[\lambda_{I}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\bra{x_{i}}\sigma\ket{x_{i}}

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\sigma\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\ket{x_{i}}\bra{x_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\sigma\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\tr(P)

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon.

Also, observe that
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[n]
because
\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0
. Let’s define

\displaystyle S:=\{i\in[n]:\lambda_{i}\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}\}.

(58)

Since,
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[n]
, we can use the Markov inequality to show:

\displaystyle\Pr_{q}(I\in S^{c})

\displaystyle=\Pr_{q}(\lambda_{I}>\sqrt{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Expect_{I\sim q}\left[\lambda_{I}\right]}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}.

Thus, if we define the projectors
\Pi:=\sum_{i\in S}\ket{x_{i}}\bra{x_{i}}
and
\Pi_{\perp}:=\sum_{i\in S^{c}}\ket{x_{i}}\bra{x_{i}}=\Id-\Pi
, we have

\displaystyle\tr(\sigma\Pi_{\perp})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S^{c}}\bra{x_{i}}\sigma\ket{x_{i}}

\displaystyle=\Pr_{q}(I\in S^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}.

(59)

Moreover, by the definition of set
S
(Eq.
58
) we have

\displaystyle\Pi\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Pi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S}\lambda_{i}\ket{x_{i}}\bra{x_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}\Pi

(60)

and using
D_{\max}(\rho||\sigma)\leq d
, we have that

\displaystyle\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{d}\Id.

(61)

Now, observe that since
\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0
, for an arbitrary
t>0
, using Lemma
5.2
we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)\Pi\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Pi+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)\Pi_{\perp}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Pi_{\perp}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)\Pi\left(\Id+\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\Pi+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)2^{d}\Pi_{\perp}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\Pi+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)2^{d}\Pi_{\perp}

where we have used
\rho\leq\sigma+P
to bound the first term and Eq.
61
to bound the second term in the second line, and Eq.
60
to bound
\Pi\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Pi
in the last step.

We will define
A_{t}:=(1+t)(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\Pi\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)2^{d}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\Pi_{\perp}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Above, we have shown that
A_{t}\geq\rho
for every
t>0
. Therefore, for each
t>0
,
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)\leq\hat{D}_{\alpha}(A_{t}||\sigma)
. We will now bound
\hat{D}_{\alpha}(A_{t}||\sigma)
for
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
as:

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\alpha}(A_{t}||\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\tr\left(\sigma\left(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}A_{t}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\tr\left(\sigma\left((1+t)(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})\Pi+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)2^{d}\Pi_{\perp}\right)^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\tr\left(\sigma\left((1+t)^{\alpha}(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\alpha}\Pi+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\alpha}2^{d\alpha}\Pi_{\perp}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left((1+t)^{\alpha}(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\alpha}\tr\left(\sigma\Pi\right)+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\alpha}2^{d\alpha}\tr\left(\sigma\Pi_{\perp}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left((1+t)^{\alpha}(1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\alpha}+\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\alpha}2^{d\alpha}\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)

where in the last line we use
\tr(\sigma\Pi)\leq 1
and
\tr(\sigma\Pi_{\perp})\leq\sqrt{\epsilon}
(Eq.
59
). Finally, since
t>0
was arbitrary, we can choose the
t>0
which minimizes the right-hand side. For this choice of
t_{\min}=\left(\frac{2^{\alpha d}\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\left(1+\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}
, we get

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\alpha}(A_{t_{\min}}||\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\log\left((1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}+2^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}d}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2(\alpha+1)}}\right)

which proves the required bound.
∎

5.2
Bounding the channel divergence for two channels close to each other

Suppose there are two channels
\cN
and
\cM
mapping registers from the space
A
to
B
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\cN-\cM\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon
. In general, the channel divergence between two such channels can be infinite because there may be states
\rho
such that
\cN(\rho)\not\ll\cM(\rho)
. In order to get around this issue, we will use the
\delta-
mixed channel,
\cM_{\delta}
. For
\delta\in(0,1)
, we define
\cM_{\delta}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\delta}:=(1-\delta)\mathcal{M}+\delta\mathcal{N}.

This guarantees that
D_{\max}(\cN||\cM_{\delta})\leq\log\frac{1}{\delta}
, which is enough to ensure that the divergences we are interested in are finite. Moreover, by mixing
\cM
with
\cN
, we only decrease the distance:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\delta}-\cN\right\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|(1-\delta)\cM+\delta\cN-\cN\right\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\frac{1}{2}\left\|\cM-\cN\right\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\delta)\epsilon.

(62)

We will now show that
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN||\cM_{\delta})
is small for an appropriately chosen
\delta
. By the definition of channel divergence, we have that

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN||\cM_{\delta})=\sup_{\rho_{AR}}D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN(\rho_{AR})||\cM_{\delta}(\rho_{AR}))

where
R
is an arbitrary reference system (
\cN,\cM_{\delta}
map register
A
to register
B
). We will show that for every
\rho_{AR}
,
D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN(\rho_{AR})||\cM_{\delta}(\rho_{AR}))
is small. Note that

\displaystyle\cM_{\delta}(\rho_{AR})

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)\cM(\rho_{AR})+\delta\cN(\rho_{AR})

\displaystyle\geq\delta\cN(\rho_{AR})

which implies that
D_{\max}(\cN(\rho_{AR})||\cM_{\delta}(\rho_{AR}))\leq\log\frac{1}{\delta}
. Also, using Eq.
62
have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\delta}(\rho_{AR})-\cN(\rho_{AR})\right\|_{1}\leq(1-\delta)\epsilon.

Using Lemma
5.3
, we have for every
\alpha\in(1,\infty)

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN(\rho_{AR})||\cM_{\delta}(\rho_{AR}))\leq\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\left(1+\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}}{\delta^{\alpha}}\right)^{{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}}\right).

Since, this is true for all
\rho_{AR}
, for every
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
we have

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN||\cM_{\delta})\leq\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\left(1+\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}}{\delta^{\alpha}}\right)^{{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}}\right).

Note that since
\delta
was arbitrary, we can choose it appropriately to make sure that the above bound is small, for example by choosing
\delta=\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4\alpha}}
, we get the bound

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN||\cM_{\delta})\leq\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\log\left((1+\sqrt{\epsilon})^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4(\alpha+1)}}\right)

which is a small function of
\epsilon
in the sense that it tends to
0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. We summarise the bound derived above in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Suppose channels
\cN
and
\cM
from register
A
to
B
are such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\cN-\cM\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon
. For
\delta\in(0,1)
, we can define the mixed channel
\cM_{\delta}:=(1-\delta)\mathcal{M}+\delta\mathcal{N}
. Then, for every
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, we have the following bound on the channel divergence

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\alpha}(\cN||\cM_{\delta})\leq\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\left(1+\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}}{\delta^{\alpha}}\right)^{{\frac{1}{\alpha+1}}}\right).

(63)

5.3
Proof of the approximate entropy accumulation theorem

We use the mixed channels defined in the previous section to define the auxiliary state
\cM^{\delta}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\delta}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})
for our proof. It is easy to show using the divergence bounds in Sec.
5.2
and the chain rule for
D^{\#}_{\alpha}
entropies that the relative entropy distance between the real state and this choice of the auxiliary state is small. However, the state
\cM^{\delta}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\delta}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})
does not necessarily satisfy the Markov chain conditions required for entropy accumulation. Thus, we also need to reprove the entropy lower bound on this state by modifying the approach used in the proof of the original entropy accumulation theorem.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Using Lemma
5.4
, for every
\delta\in(0,1)
and for each
k\in[n]
we have that for every
\beta>1
, the mixed maps
\cM^{\delta}_{k}:=(1-\delta)\cM^{\prime}_{k}+\delta\cM_{k}
satisfy

\displaystyle D^{\#}_{\beta}(\cM_{k}||\cM^{\delta}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta+1}{\beta-1}\log\left(\left(1+\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{(1-\delta)\epsilon}}{\delta^{\beta}}\right)^{{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}}\right)

\displaystyle:=z_{\beta}(\epsilon,\delta)

(64)

where we defined the right-hand side above as
z_{\beta}(\epsilon,\delta)
. This can be made “small” by choosing
\delta=\epsilon^{\frac{1}{4\beta}}
as was shown in the previous section. We use these maps to define the auxiliary state as

\displaystyle\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}:=\cM^{\delta}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\delta}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E}).

(65)

Now, we have that for
\beta>1
and
\epsilon_{1}>0

\displaystyle D^{\epsilon_{1}}_{\max}

\displaystyle(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E})

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{D}_{\beta}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E})+\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\leq D^{\#}_{\beta}(\rho_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}||\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E})+\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta-1}

\displaystyle=D^{\#}_{\beta}(\cM_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})||\cM^{\delta}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\delta}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E}))+\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\leq D^{\#}_{\beta}(\cM_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})||\cM^{\delta}_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\delta}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E}))+D^{\#}_{\beta}(\cM_{n}||\cM^{\delta}_{n})+\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}D^{\#}_{\beta}(\cM_{k}||\cM^{\delta}_{k})+\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta-1}

\displaystyle\leq nz_{\beta}(\epsilon,\delta)+\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta-1}

(66)

where the first line follows from
[
Tom16
, Proposition 6.5]
, the second line follows from
[
FF21
, Proposition 3.4]
, fourth line follows from the chain rule for
D^{\#}_{\beta}

[
FF21
, Proposition 4.5]
, and the last line follows from Eq.
64
.

For
\epsilon_{2}>0
and
\alpha\in(1,1+\frac{1}{\log(1+2|A|)})
, we can plug the above in the bound provided by Lemma
3.5
to get

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\sigma}-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}nz_{\beta}(\epsilon,\delta)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(g_{1}(\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{1})+\frac{\alpha g_{0}(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta-1}\right).

(67)

We have now reduced our problem to lower bounding
\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\sigma}
. Note that we cannot directly use the entropy accumulation here, since the mixed maps
\cM^{\delta}_{k}=(1-\delta)\cM^{\prime}_{k}+\delta\cM_{k}
, which means that with
\delta
probability the
B_{k}
register may be correlated with
A_{1}^{k-1}
even given
B_{1}^{k-1}E
, and it may not satisfy the Markov chain required for entropy accumulation.

The application of the maps
\cM^{\delta}_{k}
can be viewed as applying the channel
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
with probability
1-\delta
and the channel
\cM_{k}
with probability
\delta
. We can define the channels
\cN_{k}
which map the registers
R_{k-1}
to
R_{k}A_{k}B_{k}C_{k}
, where
C_{k}
is a binary register. The action of
\cN_{k}
can be defined as:

1.

Sample the classical random variable
C_{k}\in\{0,1\}
independently.
C_{k}=1
with probability
1-\delta
and
0
otherwise.

2.

If
C_{k}=1
apply the map
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
on
R_{k-1}
, else apply
\cM_{k}
on
R_{k-1}
.

Let us call
\theta_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}E}=\cN_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cN_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})
. Clearly
\tr_{C_{1}^{n}}\left(\theta_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}E}\right)=\sigma_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta}.

(68)

We will now focus on lower bounding
\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta}
. Using
[
Tom16
, Proposition 5.1]
, we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log\sum_{c_{1}^{n}}\theta(c_{1}^{n})\exp\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{n}}}\right).

We will show that for a given
c_{1}^{n}
, the conditional entropy
\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{n}}}
accumulates whenever the “good” map
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
is used and loses some entropy for the rounds where the “bad” map
\cM_{k}
is used. The fact that
c_{1}^{n}
contains far more
1
s than
0
s with a large probability then allows us to prove a lower bound on
\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta}
.

Claim 5.5
.

Define
h_{k}:=\inf_{\omega}\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{k}|B_{k}\tilde{R}_{k-1})_{\cM^{\prime}_{k}(\omega)}
where the infimum is over all states
\omega_{R_{k-1}\tilde{R}_{k-1}}
for a register
\tilde{R}_{k-1}
, which is isomorphic to
R_{k-1}
, and
s:=\log(|A||B|^{2})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{n}}}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\delta({c_{k},1})h_{k}-\delta({c_{k},0})s\right)

(69)

where
\delta({x,y})
is the Kronecker delta function (
\delta({x,y})=1
if
x=y
and
0
otherwise).

Proof.

We will prove the statement

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k}|B_{1}^{k}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{k}}}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{k-1}}}+\left(\delta({c_{k},1})h_{k}-\delta({c_{k},0})s\right)

then the claim will follow inductively. We will consider two cases: when
c_{k}=0
and when
c_{k}=1
. First suppose,
c_{k}=0
then
\theta_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}E|c_{1}^{k}}=\tr_{R_{k}}\circ\cM_{k}^{R_{k-1}\rightarrow R_{k}A_{k}B_{k}}\left(\theta_{R_{k-1}A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E|c_{1}^{k}}\right)
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k}|B_{1}^{k}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{k}}}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{k}}}-\log|A|

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{k}}}-\log\left(|A||B|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{\theta_{|c_{1}^{k-1}}}-s

where in the first line we have used the dimension bound in Lemma
D.1
, in the second line we have used the dimension bound in Lemma
D.3

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho-\tilde{\rho}\right\|_{1}\leq P(\rho,\tilde{\rho})\leq\epsilon_{1}
. Note that the probability of
\Omega
under the auxiliary state

cancels out.
∎

5.5
Limitations and further improvements

As we pointed out previously, the dependence of the entropy loss per round on
\epsilon
is very poor (behaves as
\sim\epsilon^{1/24}
) in Theorem
5.1
. The classical version of this theorem has a much better dependence of
O(\sqrt{\epsilon})
on
\epsilon
(see Theorem
F.1
). The reason for the poor performance of the quantum version is that our bound on the channel divergence (Lemma
5.4
) is very weak compared to the bound we can use classically. It should be noted, however, that if Lemma
5.4
were to be improved in the future, one could simply plug the new bound into our proof and derive an improvement for Theorem
5.1
.

A better bound on the channel divergence would have an additional benefit. It could simplify the proof and the Markov chain assumption in our theorem. In particular, it would be much easier to carry out the proof if the mixed channels
\cM^{\delta}_{k}
were defined as
(1-\delta)\cM^{\prime}_{k}+\delta\tau_{A_{k}B_{k}}\otimes\tr_{A_{k}B_{k}}\circ\cM_{k}
(which is what is done classically), where
\tau_{A_{k}B_{k}}
is the completely mixed state on registers
A_{k}B_{k}
. Here, instead of mixing the channel
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
with
\cM_{k}
, we mix it with
\tau_{A_{k}B_{k}}\otimes\tr_{A_{k}B_{k}}\circ\cM_{k}
, which also keeps
D_{\max}(\cM_{k}||\cM^{\delta}_{k})
small enough. Moreover, this definition ensures that the registers
B_{k}
produced by the map
\cM_{k}^{\delta}
always satisfy the Markov chain conditions. If it were possible to show that the divergence between the real state
\cM_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})
and the auxiliary state
\cM^{\delta}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\delta}_{1}(\rho^{(0)}_{R_{0}E})
is small for this definition of
\cM_{k}^{\delta}
, then one could directly use the entropy accumulation theorem for lower bounding the entropy for the auxiliary state. We cannot do this in our proof as this definition of the mixed channel
\cM^{\delta}_{k}
also increases the distance from the original channel
\cM_{k}
to
\epsilon+2\delta
and this makes the upper bound in Lemma
5.3
large (finite even in the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
).

It seems that it should be possible to weaken the assumptions for approximate entropy accumulation. The classical equivalent of this theorem (Theorem
F.1
) for instance can be proven very easily and requires a much weaker approximation assumption. It would be interesting if one could remove the “memory” registers
R_{k}
from the assumptions required for approximate entropy accumulation, since these are not typically accessible to the users in applications.

Another troubling feature of the approximate entropy accumulation theorem seems to be that it assumes that the size of the side information registers
B_{k}
is constant. One might wonder if this is necessary, since continuity bounds like the Alicki-Fannes-Winter (AFW) inequality do not depend on the size of the side information. It turns out that a bound on the side information size is indeed necessary in this case. We show a simple classical example to demonstrate this in Appendix
E
. The necessity of such a bound also rules out a similar approximate extension of the generalised entropy accumulation theorem (GEAT).

\nu_{A_{2}B_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}}=\rho_{A_{2}B_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}}
. For this it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We have that

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{B_{1}\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}}\left(\eta_{B_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j\in J}\tr_{c_{j}}\left(\Pi_{a_{j}}\otimes\Pi_{c_{j}}\eta_{B_{1}}\Pi_{a_{j}}\otimes\Pi_{c_{j}}\right)\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j\in J}q(j)\omega_{a_{j}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}

where we have defined the probability distribution
q(j):=\tr(\Pi_{a_{j}}\otimes\Pi_{c_{j}}\eta_{B_{1}})
and states
\omega_{a_{j}}=\frac{1}{q(j)}\Pi_{a_{j}}\tr_{c_{j}}\left(\Pi_{c_{j}}\eta_{B_{1}}\Pi_{c_{j}}\right)\Pi_{a_{j}}
for every
j\in J
.

Since
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}||\Id_{A_{1}}\otimes\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}})=-\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B_{1}B_{2})_{\rho}\leq\log|A_{1}|<\infty
, we have that

\displaystyle\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}\ll\Id_{A_{1}}\otimes\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}\ll\Id_{A_{1}}\otimes\bigoplus_{j\in J}q(j)\omega_{a_{j}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\text{for every }j\in J^{\prime}:\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}\ll\Id_{A_{1}}\otimes\omega_{a_{j}}\text{ and }q(j)>0.

(121)

This decomposition can be used to evaluate
\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}
as follows

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{-\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigoplus_{j\in J}q(j)^{-\alpha^{\prime}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\otimes\rho^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{c_{j}B_{2}}\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)q(j)^{-\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\rho^{1-\alpha^{\prime}}_{c_{j}B_{2}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)^{\alpha}q(j)^{1-\alpha}\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}

for
N:=\tr\left(\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B_{1}B_{2}}^{-\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}
. Further, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}q(j)^{-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}q(j)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{0}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{0}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

where in the last line we have used that the projector
\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{0}
is equal to the projector
\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{0}
for every
j\in J^{\prime}
(here
P^{0}
is the projector onto the image of positive semidefinite operator
P
). This can be seen since for every
j\in J^{\prime}
we first have

\displaystyle\im\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\subseteq\im\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}\right).

(122)

Second, we have that Eq.
121
above implies that
\omega_{a_{j}}^{0}\rho_{Aa_{j}}^{0}=\rho_{Aa_{j}}^{0}
for every
j\in J^{\prime}
. Now, for

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq m\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{0}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=m\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}

where

is the minimum non-zero eigenvalue of

(this action is operator monotone)

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{0}

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{0}.

(123)

Eq.

and
123

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega^{-\alpha^{\prime}}_{a_{j}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{0}=\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{0}.

Finally, we have that

\displaystyle\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}p(j)^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{j\in J^{\prime}}\rho_{A_{1}a_{j}}^{0}\otimes\rho_{c_{j}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{c_{j}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This proves that

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(124)

and hence

\displaystyle\nu_{A_{2}B_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}}

\displaystyle=\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{A_{1}B_{1}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{A_{2}B_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}}

where we have used the fact that
\nu_{A_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}=\rho_{A_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}B_{2}}
and Eq.
124
. We can now modify Eq.

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}A_{2}|B_{1}B_{2})_{\rho}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B_{1}B_{2})_{\rho}+\inf_{\nu}\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{2}|A_{1}B_{1}B_{2})_{\nu}

\nu
such that

. We can use the data processing inequality to get

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B_{1}B_{2})_{\rho}

Together with the above inequality this proves the Lemma.

Appendix D
Dimension bounds for conditional Rényi entropies

Lemma D.1

(Dimension bound)
.

For
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty]
, a state
\rho_{A_{1}A_{2}B}
, the following bounds hold for the sandwiched conditional entropies

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B)_{\rho}-\log|A_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}A_{2}|B)_{\rho}\leq\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B)_{\rho}+\log|A_{2}|

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B)_{\rho}-\log|A_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}A_{2}|B)_{\rho}\leq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B)_{\rho}+\log|A_{2}|.

For
\alpha\in[0,2]
and a state
\rho_{A_{1}A_{2}B}
, the following bounds hold for the Petz conditional entropies

\displaystyle\bar{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}A_{2}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B)_{\rho}+\log|A_{2}|

\displaystyle\bar{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}A_{2}|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A_{1}|B)_{\rho}+\log|A_{2}|.

Proof.

For the sandwiched conditional entropies, we simply use the corresponding chain rules (Corollary
C.2
or Corollary
C.3
) along with the fact that for all states
\nu
,
\tilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A_{2}|A_{1}B)_{\nu}\in[-\log|A_{2}|,\log|A_{2}|]

[
Tom16
, Lemma 5.2]
.

For the Petz conditional entropies, we will make use of the Jensen’s inequality for operators
[
Bha97
, Theorem V.2.3]
. Suppose,
\{\ket{e_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{|X|}
is an orthogonal basis for the space
X
. Then, we have for a positive operator
P_{XY}
and
\alpha\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\tr_{X}P_{XY}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{|X|}\Id_{Y}\otimes\bra{e_{i}}_{X}P_{XY}^{\alpha}\Id_{Y}\otimes\ket{e_{i}}_{X}

\displaystyle\leq|X|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{|X|}\frac{1}{|X|}\Id_{Y}\otimes\bra{e_{i}}_{X}P_{XY}\Id_{Y}\otimes\ket{e_{i}}_{X}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=|X|^{1-\alpha}P_{Y}^{\alpha}

(126)

where in the second step we have used the operator Jensen’s inequality with the operators
\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{|X|}}\Id_{Y}\otimes\ket{e_{i}}_{X}\right\}_{i=1}^{|X|}
along with the fact that the map
X\mapsto X^{\alpha}
is operator concave. For
\alpha\in[1,2]
and positive operator
P_{XY}
, we can use the same argument as above and the fact that
X\mapsto X^{\alpha}
is operator convex in this regime and derive

\displaystyle\tr_{X}P_{XY}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq|X|^{1-\alpha}P_{Y}^{\alpha}.

(127)

To prove the dimension bound, observe that for a positive state
\sigma_{B}
and
\alpha\in[0,2]
, we have

\displaystyle-\bar{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{A_{1}A_{2}B}||\Id_{A_{1}A_{2}}\otimes\sigma_{B})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\tr\left(\rho_{A_{1}A_{2}B}^{\alpha}\sigma_{B}^{1-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\tr\left(\tr_{A_{2}}\left(\rho_{A_{1}A_{2}B}^{\alpha}\right)\sigma_{B}^{1-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\tr\left(|A_{2}|^{1-\alpha}\rho_{A_{1}B}^{\alpha}\sigma_{B}^{1-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=-\bar{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{A_{1}B}||\Id_{A_{1}}\otimes\sigma_{B})+\log|A_{2}|.

We can now take a supremum over
\sigma_{B}
to prove the dimension bound for
\bar{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}
or choose
\sigma_{B}=\rho_{B}
to prove the dimension bound for
\bar{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}
.
∎

[
MLDS+13
, Proposition 8]
. We reproduce the proof argument here.

Lemma D.2
.

For
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty]
, a state
\rho_{ABC}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|BC)_{\rho}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(AC|B)_{\rho}-\log|C|

(128)

and for
\alpha\in[0,2]

\displaystyle\bar{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|BC)_{\rho}\geq\bar{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(AC|B)_{\rho}-\log|C|

(129)

Proof.

By the definition of the sandwiched conditional entropy, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|BC)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\eta_{BC}\in D(BC)}-\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho_{ABC}||\Id_{A}\otimes\eta_{BC})

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\eta_{B}\in D(B)}-\tilde{D}_{\alpha}\left(\rho_{ABC}||\Id_{A}\otimes\frac{\Id_{C}}{|C|}\otimes\eta_{B}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\eta_{B}\in D(B)}-\tilde{D}_{\alpha}\left(\rho_{ABC}||\Id_{AC}\otimes\eta_{B}\right)-\log|C|

\displaystyle=\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(AC|B)-\log|C|

where we simply restrict the supremum in the second line to states of the form
\eta_{BC}=\eta_{B}\otimes\frac{\Id_{C}}{|C|}
to derive the inequality. The same proof also works with
\bar{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}
entropy.
∎

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|BC)_{\rho}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\rho}-2\log|C|.

Further, if the register

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|BC)_{\rho}\geq\tilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\rho}-\log|C|.

(131)

Proof.

and Lemma

[
Tom16

.

Appendix E
Necessity for constraints on side information size for approximate AEP and EAT and its implication for approximate GEAT

It turns out that it is necessary to place some sort of bound on the size of the side information for an approximate entropy accumulation theorem of the form in Theorem
5.1
. The following classical example demonstrates this. This example also demonstrates the necessity for a bound on the size of the side information in an approximate asymptotic equipartition of the form in Theorem
4.5
.

Let there be
n
rounds. For
k\in[n]
, the map
\cM_{k}:A_{1}^{k-1}\rightarrow A_{k}B_{k}C_{k}
. This map sets the variables as follows:

1.

Measure
A_{1}^{k-1}
in the standard basis.

2.

Let
A_{k}\in_{R}\{0,1\}
be a randomly chosen bit.

3.

Let
C_{k}=0
with probability
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
and
C_{k}=1
otherwise.

4.

In the case that
C_{k}=1
, let
B_{k}\in_{R}\{0,1\}^{n}
be a randomly chosen
n
-bit string. Otherwise, let
B_{k}=A_{1}^{k}R_{k}
, where
R_{k}
is an
(n-k)
bit randomly chosen string from
\{0,1\}
.

Let
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
be the map which always chooses
B_{k}
to be a random
n
-bit string. It is easy to see that in this case, we have
H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n})_{\cM^{\prime}_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM^{\prime}_{1}(1)}=n
whereas
H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n})_{\cM_{n}\circ\cdots\circ\cM_{1}(1)}=O(1)
even though for every
k\in[n]
, the maps
\cM_{k}
are
\epsilon-
close in diamond norm distance to the maps
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
. This proves that a bound on the size of the side registers is indeed necessary for approximate entropy accumulation. We show these facts formally in the following.

Lemma E.1
.

Suppose
\Phi:R\rightarrow A
and
\Phi^{\prime}:R\rightarrow A
are two channels which take a register
R
and measure it in the standard basis and map the resulting classical register
C
to the classical register
A
. Then, for every
\rho_{RR^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi(\rho_{RR^{\prime}})-\Phi^{\prime}(\rho_{RR^{\prime}})\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|P^{\Phi}_{AC}-P_{AC}^{\Phi^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}

(132)

where
P^{\Phi}_{AC}
and
P^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{AC}
are the classical distributions produced when the maps
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
are applied to the state
\rho_{RR^{\prime}}
respectively.

Proof.

Let
\{\ket{c}\bra{c}\}_{c}
represent the measurement in the standard basis. Since, both the channels first measure register
R
in the standard basis, they produce the state

\displaystyle\rho_{CR^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c}\ket{c}\bra{c}_{C}\otimes\tr_{R}\left(\ket{c}\bra{c}_{R}\rho_{RR^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c}p(c)\ket{c}\bra{c}_{C}\otimes\rho_{R^{\prime}|c}

where we have defined
p(c):=\tr\left(\ket{c}\bra{c}_{R}\rho_{R}\right)
and
\rho_{R^{\prime}|c}:=\frac{1}{p(c)}\tr_{R}\left(\ket{c}\bra{c}_{R}\rho_{RR^{\prime}}\right)
. Now, the action of channel
\Phi
on register
C
can be represented using the conditional probability distribution
p^{\Phi}_{A|C}
and the action of channel
\Phi^{\prime}
on register
C
can be similarly represented using
p^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{A|C}
. We can define the states

\displaystyle\rho^{\Phi}_{ACR^{\prime}}:=\sum_{ac}p^{\Phi}_{A|C}(a|c)p(c)\ket{a,c}\bra{a,c}\otimes\rho_{R^{\prime}|c}

\displaystyle\rho^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{ACR^{\prime}}:=\sum_{ac}p^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{A|C}(a|c)p(c)\ket{a,c}\bra{a,c}\otimes\rho_{R^{\prime}|c}.

Note that
\tr_{C}\left(\rho^{\Phi}_{ACR^{\prime}}\right)=\Phi(\rho_{RR^{\prime}})
and
\tr_{C}\left(\rho^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{ACR^{\prime}}\right)=\Phi^{\prime}(\rho_{RR^{\prime}})
. Further, we can view the
R^{\prime}
register of
\rho^{\Phi}_{ACR^{\prime}}
and
\rho^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{ACR^{\prime}}
as being created by a channel which measures the register
C
and outputs the state
\rho_{R^{\prime}|c}
in the register
R^{\prime}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi(\rho_{RR^{\prime}})-\Phi^{\prime}(\rho_{RR^{\prime}})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\rho^{\Phi}_{ACR^{\prime}}-\rho^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{ACR^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\rho^{\Phi}_{AC}-\rho^{\Phi^{\prime}}_{AC}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|P^{\Phi}_{AC}-P_{AC}^{\Phi^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}.

∎

We can use the above lemma to evaluate the distance between the channels
\cM_{k}
and
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
. Using the above lemma, it is sufficient to suppose that the input of the channels are classical. We can suppose that the registers
A_{1}^{k-1}
are classical and distributed as
P_{A_{1}^{k-1}}
. Let
P_{A_{1}^{k}B_{k}C_{k}}
be the output of
\cM_{k}
on this distribution and
Q_{A_{1}^{k}B_{k}C_{k}}
be the output of applying
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left\|P_{A_{1}^{k}B_{k}C_{k}}-Q_{A_{1}^{k}B_{k}C_{k}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1}^{k},c_{k}}P(a_{1}^{k-1})P(a_{k})P(c_{k})\left\|P_{B_{k}|a_{1}^{k},c_{k}}-Q_{B_{k}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1}^{k}}P(a_{1}^{k-1})P(a_{k})\left(\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\left\|P_{B_{k}|a_{1}^{k},c_{k}=1}-Q_{B_{k}}\right\|_{1}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left\|P_{B_{k}|a_{1}^{k},c_{k}=0}-Q_{B_{k}}\right\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a_{1}^{k}}P(a_{1}^{k-1})P(a_{k})\epsilon

\displaystyle=\epsilon

where in the first line we have used the fact that
A_{k}
and
C_{k}
are chosen independently with the same distribution in both the maps and the fact that
B_{k}
is chosen independently in
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
, for the third line we have used the fact that
B_{k}
is independent and has the same distribution as
Q_{B_{k}}
when
c_{k}=1
. Since, this is true for all input distributions, we have
\left\|\cM_{k}-\cM^{\prime}_{k}\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon
.

Now, let
R_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}}
be the probability distribution created when the maps
\cM_{k}
are applied sequentially
n
times and
S_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}}
be the probability distribution created when the maps
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
are applied sequentially
n
times. Since,
B_{k}
and
C_{k}
are independent of
A_{k}
in the distribution
S
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n})_{S}=n.

We will show that
H_{\min}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n})_{R}=O(1)
as long as
\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{4}
. Let
l:=\frac{2}{\epsilon}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\prime}}
. Let
E
be the event that there exists a
k>n-l
such that
C_{k}=0
. For our choice of
l
, we have
p(E)\geq 1-\epsilon^{\prime}
.

Lemma E.2
.

Let
P_{AB}
be a subnormalised probability distribution such that
A=f(B)
for some function
f
(that is,
P(a,b)>0
only if
a=f(b)
). Then,
H^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A|B)_{P}\leq\log\frac{1}{\tr(P)-\sqrt{2\epsilon}}
.

Proof.

Let
P^{\prime}_{AB}
be a distribution
\epsilon
-close to
P
in purified distance. Then, it is
\sqrt{2\epsilon}
close to
P
in trace distance. We have that

\displaystyle 2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{P^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=P^{\prime}_{\text{guess}}(A|B)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{b}P^{\prime}_{AB}(f(b),b)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{b}P_{AB}(f(b),b)-\sqrt{2\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\tr(P)-\sqrt{2\epsilon}

which implies that
H_{\min}(A|B)_{P^{\prime}}\leq\log\frac{1}{\tr(P)-\sqrt{2\epsilon}}
. Since, this is true for every distribution
\epsilon
-close to
P
, it also holds for
H^{\epsilon}_{\min}(A|B)_{P}
.
∎

We then have that

\displaystyle H^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n})_{R}

\displaystyle\leq H^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}\wedge E)_{R}

\displaystyle\leq H^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{\min}(A_{1}^{n-l}|B_{1}^{n}C_{1}^{n}\wedge E)_{R}+l

\displaystyle\leq\log\frac{1}{p(E)-\sqrt{2\epsilon^{\prime}}}+l

\displaystyle\leq\log\frac{1}{1-\epsilon^{\prime}-\sqrt{2\epsilon^{\prime}}}+l

\displaystyle\leq l+\log 8/3=O(1)

where in the first line we have used
[
TL17
, Lemma 10]
in the first line, dimension bound (can be proven using Lemma
D.1
) in the second line, Lemma
E.2
in the third line and the fact that
p(E)\geq 1-\epsilon^{\prime}
.

Also, note that the example given here satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|P_{A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{k}C_{1}^{k}}-P_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}C_{1}^{k-1}}P_{A_{k}B_{k}C_{k}}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon

for every
k
. This also proves that a bound on the size of the side information registers (
B_{k}C_{k}
here), as we have in Theorem
4.5
, is necessary for an approximate version of AEP.

Further, this example also rules out the possibility of a natural approximate extension to the generalised entropy accumulation theorem (GEAT)
[
MFSR22
]
where the maps
\cM_{k}\approx_{\epsilon}\cM^{\prime}_{k}
and the maps
\cM^{\prime}_{k}
satisfy the non-signalling conditions because one can write the entropy accumulation scenario in the form of a generalised entropy accumulation scenario where Eve’s information contains the side information
B_{1}^{k}E
in each step. Thus, it would not be possible to prove a meaningful bound on the smooth min-entropy without some sort of bound on the information transferred between the adversary’s register
E_{i}
and the register
R_{i}
.

Appendix F
Classical approximate entropy accumulation

We present a simple proof for the approximate entropy accumulation theorem for classical distributions. This result also requires a much weaker assumption than Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem F.1
.

Let
p_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}
be a classical distribution such that for every
k\in[n]
, and
a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1}
and
e

\displaystyle\left\|p_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}-q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon

(133)

where
\left\|v\right\|_{\infty}:=\max_{i}|v(i)|
and the
q^{(k)}_{B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}=q^{(k)}_{B_{k}|b_{1}^{k-1},e}
or equivalently
q^{(k)}
satisfies the Markov chain
A_{k}\leftrightarrow B_{1}^{k-1}E\leftrightarrow B_{k}
. Also, let
|A_{k}|=|A|
,
|B_{k}|=|B|
for every
k\in[n]
.

Then, for
\epsilon^{\prime}\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\left(1,1+\frac{1}{\log(1+2|A|)}\right)
, we have that

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\inf_{q}H(A_{k}|B_{k}A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}q_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-n(\alpha-1)\log^{2}(2|A|+1)-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}n\log(1+\epsilon|A||B|)-\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon^{\prime})}{\alpha-1}.

(134)

where
g_{0}(x):=-\log(1-\sqrt{1-x^{2}})
. The infimums are taken over all possible input probability distributions.

For
\alpha=1+\sqrt{\epsilon}
(assuming
\sqrt{\epsilon}\leq 1+\frac{1}{\log(1+2|A|)}
), and using
\alpha\leq 2
and
\log(1+x)\leq x
as long as
x\geq 0
, the above bound gives us

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}(A_{1}^{n}|B_{1}^{n}E)_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\inf_{q}H(A_{k}|B_{k}A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E)_{q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}q_{A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-n\sqrt{\epsilon}\left(\log^{2}(2|A|+1)-2|A||B|\right)-\frac{g_{0}(\epsilon^{\prime})}{\alpha-1}

(135)

Proof.

For every
k\in[n]
, we modify
q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}
to create the distributions
r^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}
, which are defined as follows

1.

Choose a random variable
C_{k}
from
\{0,1\}
with probabilities
\left(\frac{|A||B|\epsilon}{1+|A||B|\epsilon},\frac{1}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}\right)
.

2.

If
C_{k}=1
, then choose random variables
A_{k},B_{k}
using
q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}
else choose
A_{k},B_{k}
randomly with probability
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|}
.

That is, we have

\displaystyle r^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}:=\frac{1}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}+\frac{|A||B|\epsilon}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}u_{A_{k}B_{k}}

where
u_{A_{k}B_{k}}
is the uniform distribution on the registers
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
.

For every
k,a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1}
, and
e
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|p_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}-q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\

\displaystyle p_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}\leq q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}+\epsilon\Id_{A_{k}B_{k}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\

\displaystyle p_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}\leq q^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}+\epsilon|A||B|u_{A_{k}B_{k}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\

\displaystyle p_{A_{k}B_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1},b_{1}^{k-1},e}\leq(1+|A||B|\epsilon)r^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}

Define the distribution

\displaystyle r_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}r^{(k)}_{A_{k}B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}p_{E}.

(136)

Note that for every

\displaystyle r_{B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}(b_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b_{1}^{k-1}e)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}q^{(k)}_{B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}(b_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b_{1}^{k-1}e)+\frac{\epsilon}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}q^{(k)}_{B_{k}|B_{1}^{k-1}E}(b_{k}|b_{1}^{k-1}e)+\frac{\epsilon}{1+|A||B|\epsilon},

which implies

\displaystyle r_{B_{k}|B_{1}^{k-1}E}(b_{k}|b_{1}^{k-1}e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{a}_{1}^{k-1}}r_{A_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k-1}E}(\bar{a}_{1}^{k-1}|b_{1}^{k-1}e)r_{B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}(b_{k}|\bar{a}_{1}^{k-1}b_{1}^{k-1}e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{a}_{1}^{k-1}}r_{A_{1}^{k-1}|B_{1}^{k-1}E}(\bar{a}_{1}^{k-1}|b_{1}^{k-1}e)\left(\frac{1}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}q^{(k)}_{B_{k}|B_{1}^{k-1}E}(b_{k}|b_{1}^{k-1}e)+\frac{\epsilon}{1+|A||B|\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=r_{B_{k}|A_{1}^{k-1}B_{1}^{k-1}E}(b_{k}|a_{1}^{k-1}b_{1}^{k-1}e).

Thus, for every

,
r

\displaystyle=(1+\epsilon|A||B|)^{n}r_{A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{n}E}(a_{1}^{n},b_{1}^{n},e)

which shows that

.

Appendix G
Lemma to bound distance after conditioning

The following Lemma relates the distance of two states conditioned on an event to the distance between them without conditioning.

Lemma G.1
.

Suppose
\rho_{XA}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)\ket{x}\bra{x}\otimes\rho_{A|x}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{XA}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\tilde{p}(x)\ket{x}\bra{x}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}
are classical-quantum states such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{XA}-\tilde{\rho}_{XA}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
. Then, for
x\in\mathcal{X}
such that
p(x)>0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{A|x}-\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{p(x)}

(137)

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{XA}-\tilde{\rho}_{XA}\right\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|p(x)\rho_{A|x}-\tilde{p}(x)\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon

This implies that for
x\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|p(x)\rho_{A|x}-\tilde{p}(x)\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|p(x)-\tilde{p}(x)|\leq\epsilon.

Using these inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{A|x}-\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{A|x}-\frac{\tilde{p}(x)}{p(x)}\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left|1-\frac{\tilde{p}(x)}{p(x)}\right|\left\|\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(x)}\frac{1}{2}\left\|p(x)\rho_{A|x}-\tilde{p}(x)\tilde{\rho}_{A|x}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{p(x)}\frac{1}{2}\left|p(x)-\tilde{p}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\epsilon}{p(x)}.

∎

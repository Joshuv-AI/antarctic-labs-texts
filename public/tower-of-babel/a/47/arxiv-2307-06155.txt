where

This graph invariant was introduced by Shannon in 1956 and

The Haemers number of a graph

In this paper, we are interested in a variant of the property in (

\displaystyle\alpha(G^{\boxtimes n})\leq\alpha(H^{\boxtimes n})\Gamma(G,H)^{n}

Observe that (

\displaystyle\Gamma(G,H)\geq\sup_{n}\left(\frac{\alpha(G^{\boxtimes n})}{\alpha(H^{\boxtimes n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha(G^{\boxtimes n})}{\alpha(H^{\boxtimes n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}=\frac{\mathscr{C}(G)}{\mathscr{C}(H)}

To the best of our knowledge, the problem of finding functions
\Gamma
satisfying (
3
) has not been studied before for general graphs. We point out that the No-Homomorphism Lemma partially addresses this question for a special class of graphs

and

H
to
G

\frac{\alpha(G)}{\alpha(H)}\leq\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|}.

It is clear that if there is a homomorphism from

to

. Therefore,

\frac{\alpha(G^{\boxtimes n})}{\alpha(H^{\boxtimes n})}\leq\left(\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|}\right)^{n},\qquad\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Gamma(G,H)=\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|}

(5)

will satisfy (

H
to

We discuss two different approaches for constructing such a function
\Gamma

The first approach yields a function
satisfying (
3

3
) in terms of a maximum. Rather surprisingly, we show that the two approaches are equivalent and correspond to the dual forms of a linear programming problem. For reasons that will become clear later, the common solution obtained through either approach is named “the relative fractional independence number of a graph
G

\alpha^{*}(G|H).

For the special case of
G

\alpha^{*}(G|H)

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{\alpha(G^{c}\boxtimes H)}.

Moreover, if in addition to

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{\alpha(G^{c}\boxtimes H)}\leq\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|},

If both

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\frac{\chi_{f}(G^{c}\boxtimes H)}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|},

(6)

where

Our characterization of

\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{C}(G)}{\mathscr{C}(H)}\leq\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{\alpha(G^{c}\boxtimes H)}.

Next, we discuss the relation of

For a given graph

One application of our results is the extension of the No-Homomorphism Lemma. Assume that there is a homomorphism from

to

\frac{\alpha(G^{\boxtimes n})}{\alpha(H^{\boxtimes n})}\leq\left(\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|}\right)^{n},\qquad\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

The above equation also immediately implies that

\frac{\mathscr{C}(G)}{\mathscr{C}(H)}\leq\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|}.

However, what if we replace

and

\frac{X(G)}{X(H)}\leq\alpha^{*}(G|H)\leq\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)|}{|\mathcal{V}(H)|}

where

or Szegedy’s variants of the Lovász number of the graph

Finally, there are also other results included in the paper, sometimes serving as examples for our discussions. In particular, we find new lower bounds on the Shannon capacity of certain Johnson graphs, enabling us to determine the exact value of their Haemers number for the first time. We also establish that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}4n+2-\mathscr{C}(J(4n+2,3))=0

Theorem 1

Proof.

Example 2

3.2
The second approach

Motivated by the auxiliary receiver approach
[

]
, we consider two graphs,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{C}(G)}{\mathscr{C}(H)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha(G^{\boxtimes n})}{\alpha(H^{\boxtimes n})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

(9)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha(G^{i}\boxtimes H^{n-i})}{\alpha(G^{i-1}\boxtimes H^{n-i+1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha(G\boxtimes W_{i})}{\alpha(H\boxtimes W_{i})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

(10)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{W}\frac{\alpha(G\boxtimes W)}{\alpha(H\boxtimes W)}

(11)

where in (

W_{i}=G^{i-1}\boxtimes H^{n-i}
and the supremum in (

W
.

Define

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\sup_{W}\frac{\alpha(G\boxtimes W)}{\alpha(H\boxtimes W)},

(12)

where the supremum is over
all
graphs
W
.

By considering the special case of
W
having a single vertex, it is clear that

\alpha^{*}(G|H)\geq\frac{\alpha(G)}{\alpha(H)}.

Consequently, to show that
\alpha^{*}
satisfies
(

\alpha^{*}(G|H)^{n}\geq\alpha^{*}(G^{\boxtimes n}|H^{\boxtimes n}),\qquad\forall n.

This claim follows from the following lemma:

Lemma 1
.

For any graphs
G_{1},G_{2},H_{1}
and
H_{2}
we have:

\alpha^{*}(G_{1}\boxtimes G_{2}|H_{1}\boxtimes H_{2})\leq\alpha^{*}(G_{1}|H_{1})\alpha^{*}(G_{2}|H_{2}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G_{1}\boxtimes G_{2}|H_{1}\boxtimes H_{2})

\displaystyle=\sup_{W}\frac{\alpha(W\boxtimes G_{1}\boxtimes G_{2})}{\alpha(W\boxtimes H_{1}\boxtimes H_{2})}=\sup_{W}\frac{\alpha(W\boxtimes G_{1}\boxtimes G_{2})}{\alpha(W\boxtimes H_{1}\boxtimes G_{2})}\cdot\frac{\alpha(W\boxtimes H_{1}\boxtimes G_{2})}{\alpha(W\boxtimes H_{1}\boxtimes H_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{W}\frac{\alpha((W\boxtimes G_{2})\boxtimes G_{1})}{\alpha((W\boxtimes G_{2})\boxtimes H_{1})}\cdot\sup_{W}\frac{\alpha((W\boxtimes H_{1})\boxtimes G_{2})}{\alpha((W\boxtimes H_{1})\boxtimes H_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{*}(G_{1}|H_{1})\alpha^{*}(G_{2}|H_{2}).

∎

Consider the special case of graph
H
having a single vertex. In this case, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\sup_{W}\frac{\alpha(G\boxtimes W)}{\alpha(W)}.

(13)

The optimization problem in (

[

who showed that the supremum in (

of a graph

), which is defined via a linear program
as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G):=\max\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}(G)}w_{v}

(14)

where the maximum is over all weights
w_{v}\geq 0
such that
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{S}}w_{v}\leq 1
for every clique
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{V}(G)
.
6
6

6

Some papers define the fractional independence number differently as the
maximum sum of non-negative weights in
[0,1]
that can be assigned to the vertices of a graph while ensuring that the total weight on any edge does not exceed 1. Here, we require the stronger condition that the total weight of any arbitrary clique does not exceed 1.

In other words, the fractional independence number of

is the fractional clique number of

which is also equal to the fractional chromatic number of

.

Since
\alpha^{*}(G|H)
is equal to
\alpha^{*}(G)
, the

of

of

with respect to

]

3.3
Equivalence of the two approaches

In this section, we provide a characterization of
\alpha^{*}(G|H)
as defined in (
13
). Observe that the third part of this characterization implies that
\Gamma_{1}(G,H)=\alpha^{*}(G|H)
.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
G
is a graph with
k
vertices and
\mathcal{V}(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{k}\}
. Then, we have

(i)

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\max_{\mathbf{w}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i},

(15)

where the maximization over
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{k})
is subject to
w_{i}\geq 0
and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}|f(v_{i})|\leq 1

for any
f\in\mathcal{F}(H,G)
. In other words, we require
\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}|\mathcal{T}_{i}|\leq 1
for any collection of sets
\mathcal{T}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{T}_{k}\in\mathcal{I}(H)
such that
\mathcal{T}_{i}
and
\mathcal{T}_{j}
are disconnected in
H
if there is no edge between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
in
G
.

(ii)

\frac{1}{\alpha^{*}(G|H)}=\min_{w_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}=1}\max_{f}\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}|f(v_{i})|,

where the maximum is over all
f\in\mathcal{F}(H,G)
.

(iii)

Given a probability distribution
p(f)
over
f\in\mathcal{F}(H,G)
, we can consider a random
F\in\mathcal{F}(H,G)
. We have

\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\min\max_{v\in\mathcal{V}(G)}\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[|F(v)|]},

where the minimum is over all possible distributions on the set
\mathcal{F}(H,G)
.

(iv)

For every pair of graphs
G
and
H
, there is some graph
W
such that

\alpha^{*}(G|H)=\frac{\alpha(G\boxtimes W)}{\alpha(H\boxtimes W)}.

In other words, the supremum in the definition of
\alpha^{*}(G|H)
is a maximum.

We give two different proofs for the above theorem. While the first proof given in Section
7.1
is longer, it provides insights into the form of the linear program. On the other hand, if we already know the form of the linear program, its correctness can be verified directly via the second proof presented in Appendix B.

Let us outline the first proof.
We would like to maximize

\alpha(W\boxtimes G)/\alpha(W\boxtimes H)
.
Take some arbitrary graph
W
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a maximum independent set for
W\boxtimes G
,
i.e.,

|\mathcal{B}|=\alpha(W\boxtimes G)
. Then, for every vertex
u
of
W
, we form the set

\mathcal{B}_{u}=\{v\in\mathcal{V}(G):(u,v)\in\mathcal{B}\}
.
Next, we partition the vertices of
W
according to
\mathcal{B}_{u}
(vertices with the same set
\mathcal{B}_{u}
will be placed in the same class). Next, using this partition, we add some edges to the graph
W
such that
\mathcal{B}
is still a maximum independent set for
W\boxtimes G
, but
\alpha(W\boxtimes H)
can potentially decrease. Once the edges are added, the graph
W
will have a particular structure from which we can explicitly compute
\alpha(W\boxtimes H)
and optimize over
W
.

For a universal graph

Theorem 3

For any graphs

which implies that

\alpha(G\boxtimes H)\alpha(H^{c}\boxtimes G^{c})\leq|\mathcal{V}(G)||\mathcal{V}(H)|.

Since

\alpha^{*}((G^{c}\boxtimes H^{c})^{c})=\frac{|\mathcal{V}(G)||\mathcal{V}(H)|}{\alpha(G^{c}\boxtimes H^{c})}

we deduce that

\alpha^{*}((G^{c}\boxtimes H^{c})^{c})\geq\alpha(G\boxtimes H)

Observe that

(G^{c}\boxtimes H^{c})^{c}=G\star H
. This completes the proof.

∎

Observe that

4.2.1
The Expand Operations

Finally, we provide another definition that is useful in studying
\alpha^{*}(G|H)
.

Definition 5

(Expand Operations)
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary graph. We define
Expand(G)
as the set of all graphs that can be obtained by a sequence of the following three operations on
G
:

(I)

Remove a vertex
v
from
G
.

(II)

Replace a vertex
v
of
G
by a clique of arbitrary size. In other words, put a clique of size
k
instead of vertex
v
and connect these
k
new vertices to all neighbors of
v
.

(III)

Add a new edge to
G
.

Remark 5
.

Note that by the above operations, one can merge (contract) any two vertices
v
and
u
in
G
. To achieve this, we only need to delete one of the vertices, for instance
v
, by applying an operation of type (I). Then, we add new edges between
u
and the neighbors of
v
by applying an operation of type (III). This allows us to merge the two vertices
v
and
u
into a single vertex.

Remark 6
.

Definition
5
gives three types of operations.
To reach a graph
H
from a given graph
G
using the Expand operations, we can always assume that the operations occur in the following order: first a series of operations (I), then a series of operations (II), and finally a series of operations (III). To see this, observe that the operations of adding an edge (type (III)) can always be postponed to the very end. Then, if we have a sequence of operations of type (I) or (II), the operations of removing a vertex (type (I)) can all be performed first at the beginning.

One can easily verify that all the above Expand operations, when applied to a graph
G
, will not increase
\alpha(G\boxtimes W)
for any choice of graph
W
. Consequently, we obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 4
.

If
G^{\prime}\in Expand(G)
and
H^{\prime}\in Expand(H)
, then we have

\alpha^{*}(G^{\prime}|H)\leq\alpha^{*}(G|H)\leq\alpha^{*}(G|H^{\prime}).

Corollary 5
.

If
G\in Expand(H)
, then
\alpha^{*}(G|H)\leq\alpha^{*}(H|H)=1
.

Corollary 6
.

If
G\in Expand(H)
and
\alpha(G)\geq\alpha(H)
, then
\alpha^{*}(G|H)=1
.

Proof.

Since
G\in Expand(H)
, we have
\alpha^{*}(G|H)\leq 1
. On the other hand,
\alpha^{*}(G|H)\geq\frac{\alpha(G)}{\alpha(H)}\geq 1
.

∎

5.1
Bounds on the independence number

In
[
35
]
, the structure of odd cycles is used to show that for any natural number
d
,
m\geq 3
and even number
\beta>0
,

\displaystyle\alpha(C^{d}_{m+\beta})\geq\alpha(C^{d}_{m}){\left(\dfrac{m+\beta}{m}\right)}^{d}

(22)

where
C^{d}_{m}
is the
d
-th power of
C_{m}
. One can also obtain this result directly using the No-Homomorphism lemma. In Appendix A, we prove that
\alpha^{*}(C_{n}|C_{m})=n/m
for odd
m
and
n
satisfying
n<m
. Thus, we recover the result of
[
35
]
. More generally, observe that for any natural number

(n/m)^{k}=\frac{\alpha^{*}(C_{n}^{\boxtimes k})}{\alpha^{*}(C_{m}^{\boxtimes k})}\leq\alpha^{*}(C_{n}^{\boxtimes k}|C_{m}^{\boxtimes k})\leq(\alpha^{*}(C_{n}|C_{m}))^{k}=(n/m)^{k}.

Thus, for odd

and

, we deduce

\alpha^{*}(C_{n}^{\boxtimes k}|C_{m}^{\boxtimes k})=(n/m)^{k},\qquad\forall k.

So, we conclude that

is the fundamental limit of the ratio one can obtain by the technique of assigning independent sets of
C_{m}^{\boxtimes k}
to vertices of
C_{n}^{\boxtimes k}
for any arbitrary
k
.

In the next sub-section, we discuss the more general class of Cayley graphs and compute
\alpha^{*}(G|H)
. This provides a different generalization of (
22
).

By Theorem
4
, the inequality

\frac{\mathscr{C}(G)}{\mathscr{C}(H)}\leq\alpha^{*}(G|H)

yields a non-trivial upper bound on the ratio of Shannon number of
G

and
H
. One can compare this bound by the individual bounds on
\mathscr{C}(G)
and
\mathscr{C}(H)
. In particular, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{C}(G)}{\mathscr{C}(H)}\leq\frac{\mathscr{C}(G)}{(\alpha(H^{d}))^{1/d}}

(23)

for any natural number

and then use a known upper bound on

(such as the Lovász number) to compute an upper bound on the ratio of the capacities of

and
H
. However, calculating
\alpha(H^{d})
for large values of

5.2
The ratio of Shannon capacities of two Cayley graphs

As a concrete example, we compute

for two Cayley graphs obtained by cyclic groups.

Theorem 10

Let

H=Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{m},\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots,\pm k)

Proof of the above theorem is given in Section
7.8
. It utilizes the idea of defining a homomorphism from

to
G

implies

for

for some integers

To compare this bound with the previously known bounds, one can use individual bounds on

and
\mathscr{C}(H)
to write the following upper bound.
In
[

\alpha(Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots\pm k)\boxtimes Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots\pm k))=\left\lfloor\frac{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k+1}\right\rfloor n}{k+1}\right\rfloor.

\frac{\mathscr{C}(Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots k))}{\mathscr{C}(Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{m},\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots k))}\leq\frac{\alpha^{*}(Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots\pm k))}{\alpha(Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{m},\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots\pm k)^{2})}=\frac{n/(k+1)}{\sqrt{\left\lfloor\frac{\left\lfloor\frac{m}{k+1}\right\rfloor m}{k+1}\right\rfloor}},

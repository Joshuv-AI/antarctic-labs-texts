Spontaneous Peccei-Quinn symmetry breaking renders sterile neutrino, axion and 𝝌 \chi boson to be candidates for dark matter particles
She-Sheng Xue

Abstract
We study the Peccei-Quinn (PQ) symmetry of the sterile right-handed neutrino sector and the gauge symmetries of the Standard Model. Due to four-fermion interactions, spontaneous breaking of these symmetries at the electroweak scale generates top-quark Dirac mass and sterile-neutrino Majorana mass. The top quark channel yields massive Higgs, W ± W^{\pm} and Z 0 Z^{0} bosons. The sterile neutrino channel yields the heaviest sterile neutrino Majorana mass, sterile Nambu-Goldstone axion (or majoron) and massive scalar χ \chi boson. Four-fermion operators effectively induce their tiny couplings to SM particles. We show that a sterile QCD axion is the PQ solution to the strong CP problem. The lightest and heaviest sterile neutrinos ( m N e ∼ 10 2 m_{N}^{e}\sim 10^{2} keV and m N τ ∼ 10 2 m_{N}^{\tau}\sim 10^{2} GeV), a sterile QCD axion ( m a < 10 − 8 m_{a}<10^{-8} eV, g a ​ γ < 10 − 13 ​ GeV − 1 g_{a\gamma}<10^{-13}{\rm GeV}^{-1} ) and a Higgs-like χ \chi boson ( m χ ∼ 10 2 m_{\chi}\sim 10^{2} GeV) can be dark matter particle candidates, for the constraints of their tiny couplings and long lifetimes inferred from the W W -boson decay width, Xenon1T and precision fine-structure-constant experiments. The axion and χ \chi boson couplings to SM particles are below the values reached by current laboratory experiments and astrophysical observations for directly or indirectly detecting dark matter particles.

7.1

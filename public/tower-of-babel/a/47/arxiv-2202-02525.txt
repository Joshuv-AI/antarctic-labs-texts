Abstract
. Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a connected finite graph. We study the existence of solutions for the following generalized Chern-Simons equation on G G Δ ​ u = λ ​ e u ​ ( e u − 1 ) 5 + 4 ​ π ​ ∑ s = 1 N δ p s , \Delta u=\lambda\mathrm{e}^{u}\left(\mathrm{e}^{u}-1\right)^{5}+4\pi\sum_{s=1}^{N}\delta_{p_{s}}\quad, where λ > 0 \lambda>0 , δ p s \delta_{p_{s}} is the Dirac mass at the vetex p s p_{s} , and p 1 , p 2 , … , p N p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{N} are arbitrarily chosen distinct vertices on the graph. 2.
Preliminary results

For each edge
xy\in E
, We suppose that its weight
w_{xy}>0
and that
w_{xy}=w_{yx}
. Set
\mu:V\to(0,+\infty)
be a finite measure. For any function
u:V\to\mathbb{R}
, the Laplacian of
u
is defined by

(2.1)

\Delta u(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{yx}(u(y)-u(x)),

where
y\sim x
means
xy\in E
. The gradient form of
u
reads

(2.2)

\Gamma(u,v)(x)=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

We denote the length of the gradient of
u
by

|\nabla u|(x)=\sqrt{\Gamma(u)(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Denote, for any function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, an integral of
u
on
V
by
\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\mu(x)u(x)
. Denote
|V|
=
\text{Vol}(V)=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\mu(x)
the volume of
V
. For
p\geq 1
, denote
||u||_{p}:=(\int\limits_{V}|u|^{p}d\mu)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. Define a sobolev space and a norm on it by

W^{1,2}(V)=\left\{u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu<+\infty\right\},

and

\|u\|_{H^{1}(V)}=\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(V)}=\left(\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

To prove our main result, we need the following Sobolev embedding, Poincaré inequality and Maximum principle on graphs.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 5]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. The sobolev space
W^{1,2}(V)
is precompact. Namely, if
{u_{j}}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}(V)
, then there exists some
u\in W^{1,2}(V)
such that up to a subsequence,
u_{j}\to u
in
W^{1,2}(V)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 6]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. For all functions
u:V\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=0
, there
exists some constant
C
depending only on
G
such that
\int\limits_{V}u^{2}d\mu\leq C\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
, where
V
is a finite set, and
K\geq 0
is constant. Suppose a real function
u(x):V\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
(\Delta-K)u(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then
u(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in V
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. For all functions
u:V\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=0
and
p\geq 1
, there
exists a constant
C=C(G,p)
such that

||u||_{p}\leq C||\nabla u||_{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, there exists
C=C(G)
such that

M:=\max\limits_{x\in V}|u(x)|\leq(\frac{C}{\min\limits_{V}\mu}\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus we have
||u||_{p}\leq(\frac{C}{\min\limits_{V}\mu}\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}|V|^{1/p}:=C_{2}||\nabla u||_{2}
.
∎

3.
The proof of Theorem 1.1

Since
\int\limits_{V}-\frac{4\pi N}{\text{Vol}(V)}+4\pi\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\delta p_{j}d\mu=0
, we can choose a solution
u_{0}
of the equation

(3.1)

\Delta u_{0}=-\frac{4\pi N}{\text{Vol(V)}}+4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{p_{j}}.

Letting
u=u_{0}+v
, the equation (
1.3
) can be reduced to the following question

(3.2)

\Delta v=\lambda e^{u_{0}+v}\left(e^{u_{0}+v}-1\right)^{5}+\frac{4\pi N}{\text{Vol}(V)}.

Set
F(y):=(e^{y}-1)^{5}e^{y}
on
\mathbb{R}
, it is clear that
F
has a unique minimal value
-\frac{5^{5}}{6^{6}}.

Thus, it follows from (
3.2
) that

0=\int\limits_{V}\Delta vd\mu\geq\lambda\int\limits_{V}-\frac{5^{5}}{6^{6}}d\mu+4\pi N=-\frac{5^{5}}{6^{6}}\lambda\text{Vol}(V)+4\pi N.

This implies that

\lambda\geq\frac{6^{6}}{5^{5}}\frac{4\pi N}{|V|},

which is a necessary condition for the existence of solutions to (
1.3
).

To solve (
3.2
), for a constant
K\geq\lambda
, we define a sequence
\{w_{n}\}
by a monotone iterative scheme:

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\Delta-K)W_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda e^{u_{0}+W_{n-1}}\left(e^{u_{0}+W_{n-1}}-1\right)^{5}-KW_{n-1}+\frac{4\pi N}{\operatorname{Vol}(V)},~n=1,2,\ldots

\displaystyle W_{0}

\displaystyle=-u_{0}.

Next, we establish a solution to (
3.2
) by a supersolution and subsolution method.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
u
on
V
is called a subsolution of (
3.2
) if

\Delta u\geq\lambda e^{u_{0}+u}\left(e^{u_{0}+u}-1\right)^{5}+\frac{4\pi N}{\operatorname{Vol}(V)}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{W_{n}}
be a
sequence defined by scheme (
3.3
) with
K\geq\lambda
. Then

(3.4)

W_{-}\leq\dots\leq W_{n}\leq\dots\leq W_{2}\leq W_{1}\leq W_{0}

for any subsolution
W_{-}
of (
3.2
).

Proof.

By (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we have

(3.5)

(\Delta-K)(W_{1}-W_{0})=4\pi\sum_{s=1}^{N}\delta_{p_{s}}>0,x\in V.

By Lemma
2.3
, we deduce that

(W_{1}-W_{0})(x)\leq 0

for all
x\in V
.
Suppose that

W_{0}\geq W_{1}\geq\dots\geq W_{k}.

By (
3.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle(\Delta-K)\left(W_{k+1}-W_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{k}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{k}}-1\right)^{5}-\lambda\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{k-1}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{k-1}}-1\right)^{5}-K\left(W_{k}-W_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\lambda\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+\xi}\left(\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+\xi}-1\right)^{4}\left(6\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+\xi}-1\right)-K\right]\left(W_{k}-W_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda-K)\left(W_{k}-W_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where
W_{k}\leq\xi\leq W_{k-1}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we see that
W_{k+1}\leq W_{k}
on
V
.

Set
(W_{-}-W_{0})(x_{0})=\max\limits_{x\in V}(W_{-}-W_{0})(x)
, we claim that

(W_{-}-W_{0})(x_{0})\leq 0.

Otherwise,
(W_{-}-W_{0})(x_{0})>0
. It follows that

\Delta(W_{-}-W_{0})(x_{0})\geq\lambda e^{u_{0}+W_{-}(x_{0})}(e^{u_{0}+W_{-}(x_{0})}-1))^{5}+\frac{4\pi N}{\text{Vol}(V)}+\Delta u_{0}>0.

By the definition of Laplace operator, we obtain

\Delta(W_{-}-W_{0})(x_{0})\leq 0.

This is a contradicion. Thus we have

(W_{-}-W_{0})(x_{0})\leq 0,

which implies that

W_{-}-W_{0}\leq 0

on
V
. Assume that
W_{-}-W_{k}\leq 0
for some integer
k\geq 0
. Thanks to
W_{-}
is a subsolution of (
3.2
) and
K\geq\lambda
, we deduce that

\displaystyle(\Delta-K)\left(W_{-}-W_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\left[\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{-}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{-}}-1\right)^{5}-\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{k}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+W_{k}}-1\right)^{5}\right]-K\left(W_{-}-W_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left[\lambda\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+\eta}\left(\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+\eta}-1\right)^{4}\left(6\mathrm{e}^{u_{0}+\eta}-1\right)-K\right]\left(W_{-}-W_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda-K)\left(W_{-}-W_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where
W_{-}\leq\eta\leq W_{k}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have
W_{-}\leq W_{k+1}
on
V
.

We now complete the proof.
∎

A
. For any integer
n\geq 1
, we see that

(3.14)

\inf\left\{I_{\lambda}(w)\mid w\in W^{1,2}(V),\|w-v_{\lambda}\|_{W^{1,2}(V)}\leq\frac{1}{n}\right\}=\varepsilon_{n}<I_{\lambda}(v_{\lambda}).

By a similar argument as above, we can deduce that there exists
\{v_{n}\}\subset W^{1,2}(V)
satisfying

\|v_{n}-v_{\lambda}\|_{W^{1,2}(V)}\leq\frac{1}{n}

and

I_{\lambda}(v_{n})=\epsilon_{n}.

Thus we conclude that, by passing to a subsequence,
v_{n}\to v_{\lambda}
in
V
as
n\to+\infty
, and hence that
v_{n}>U
for sufficiently large
n
. Therefore, we obtain
I_{\lambda}(v_{n})\geq I_{\lambda}(v_{\lambda})
. This is a contradiction.
∎

We now prove that
I_{\lambda}(v)
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition.

Lemma 3.6
.

Every sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset W^{1,2}(V)
satisfying

(3.15)

I_{\lambda}(v_{n})\to\alpha~\text{and}~\|I^{{}^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(v_{n})\|\to 0~\text{as}~n\to+\infty

has a convergent subsequence.

Proof.

From (
3.15
), we have

(3.16)

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla v_{n}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{6}\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{6}\mathrm{~d}x+\frac{4\pi N}{|V|}\int\limits_{V}v_{n}\mathrm{~d}x=\alpha+o(1),\text{as}~n\to+\infty

(3.17)

\left|\int\limits_{V}\Gamma(v_{n},\varphi)\mathrm{d}x+\lambda\int\limits_{V}e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{5}\varphi\mathrm{d}x+\frac{4\pi N}{|V|}\int\limits_{V}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\varepsilon_{n}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,2}(V)},~\epsilon_{n}\to 0

as
n\to+\infty,

\varphi\in H^{1}(V)
. By taking
\varphi=1
in (
3.17
), we have

\lambda\int\limits_{V}e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{5}\mathrm{~d}x+4\pi N\leq\varepsilon_{n}|V|^{1/2},

from which we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{n}|V|^{1/2}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4\pi N}{\lambda}+\int\limits_{V}e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{5}\mathrm{~d}\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi N}{\lambda}+\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu+\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{5}\mathrm{~d}\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4\pi N}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{6}|V|+\frac{1}{6}\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu.

This implies that there exists a constant
C=C(\epsilon_{n},\lambda,|V|)>0
such that

(3.18)

\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu\leq C.

Hence, we can find
C_{2}>0
such that

(3.19)

\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}\mathrm{d}\mu=\int\limits_{V}\left[\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)+1\right]^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu\leq 2^{6}\left[\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu+|V|\right]\leq C_{2}.

Then by H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality, there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

(3.20)

\int\limits_{V}e^{2\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\left(\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}|V|^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq C_{3}.

Similarly,
\int\limits_{V}e^{4\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C_{4}
for a suitable constant
C_{4}>0
.
Decompose
v_{n}=v_{n}^{\prime}+c_{n}
, where
\int\limits_{V}v_{n}^{\prime}d\mu=0
and
c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
for
n=1,2,\dots
. Substituing it in (
3.16
), we conclude that

(3.21)

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{6}\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}^{\prime}+c_{n}}-1\right)^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu+4\pi Nc_{n}\rightarrow\alpha,

as
n\to+\infty
, and hence that
c_{n}
is bounded from above. By (
3.16
), we see that there exists an integer
N
such that

\alpha-1<I_{\lambda}(v_{n})<\alpha+1

for
n\geq N
. This implies that

(3.22)

\alpha-1<\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{6}\int\limits_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}^{\prime}+c_{n}}-1\right)^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu+4\pi Nc_{n}<\alpha+1.

From (
3.18
) and (
3.22
), we conclude that

(3.23)

\alpha-1+\frac{4\lambda\pi N}{5}-\left(\frac{\lambda}{6}+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{5}\right)|V|<\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}+4\pi Nc_{n}<\alpha+1.

Next we show that
c_{n}
is bounded from below. Taking
v_{n}^{\prime}
in (
3.17
), by Lemma
2.2
, we can find a constant
C_{5}
such that

(3.24)

\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\int\limits_{V}e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}-1\right)^{5}v_{n}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\varepsilon_{n}\left\|v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(V)}\leq C_{5}\varepsilon_{n}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}.

This implies that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\|\nabla

\displaystyle v_{n}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+c_{n}\right)}\left(e^{6v_{n}^{\prime}}-1\right)v_{n}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+c_{n}\right)}v_{n}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mu+C_{5}\varepsilon_{n}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle+C_{6}\int\limits_{V}e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}\left(e^{4\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}+e^{3\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}+e^{2\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}+e^{u_{0}+v_{n}}+1\right)\left|v_{n}^{\prime}\right|\mathrm{d}\mu.

By Lemma

2.2
, Lemma

2.4
and H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality, we deduce that

(3.26)

\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+c_{n}\right)}v_{n}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C_{7}\left\|v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\leq C_{8}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2},

and

(3.27)

\int\limits_{V}e^{5\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}\left|v_{n}^{\prime}\right|\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\left(\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+v_{n}\right)}\mathrm{d}\mu\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}\left(\int\limits_{V}\left|v_{n}^{\prime}\right|^{6}\mathrm{~d}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\leq C_{9}\left\|v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{6}\leq C_{10}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}

for suitable positive constants
C_{7}-C_{10}
.
Similarly, we can get all the other terms on the right hand side of (
3.25
) can be bounded by
\hat{C}||\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}||_{2}
, where
\hat{C}>0
is a constant.
Thus, there exists constant
C_{11}>0
such that

(3.28)

\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+c_{n}\right)}\left(e^{6v_{n}^{\prime}}-1\right)v_{n}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mu\leq C_{11}\left\|\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}.

Clearly,

(3.29)

\int\limits_{V}e^{6\left(u_{0}+c_{n}\right)}\left(e^{6v_{n}^{\prime}}-1\right)v_{n}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\mu\geq 0.

Hence by (
3.28
), we have
||\nabla v_{n}^{\prime}||_{2}\leq C_{12}
for a suitable constant
C_{12}>0
. Therefore, by (
3.23
), we deduce that
c_{n}
is bounded from below.

Thus
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(V)
. Thus, there exists
v\in H^{1}(V)
such that, by passing to a subsequence,
v_{n}(x)\to v(x)
for all
x\in V
.

Next, we find the second solution of (
3.2
). From now on, we suppose that
v_{\lambda}
is the local minimum as defined by Lemma

3.5
(if not, we could have already found our second solution). Thus there exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that

I_{\lambda}(v_{\lambda})\leq I_{\lambda}(v)

for all
v:||v-v_{\lambda}||_{H^{1}(V)}\leq\rho_{0}.
For
c>0
, we have

(3.30)

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}\left(v_{\lambda}-c\right)-I_{\lambda}\left(v_{\lambda}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{6}\int\limits_{V}\left[\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{\lambda}-c}-1\right)^{6}-\left(e^{u_{0}+v_{\lambda}-1}\right)^{6}\right]d\mu-4\pi Nc

\displaystyle<\frac{\lambda}{6}|V|C_{13}-4\pi Nc\rightarrow-\infty\text{ as }c\rightarrow+\infty.

There are two possibilities: (I)
v_{\lambda}
is not a strict local minimum for
I_{\lambda}
, (II)
v_{\lambda}
is a strict local minimum for
I_{\lambda}.
If case (I) happens, then we deduce that

\inf\limits_{\left\|v-v_{\lambda}\right\|_{H^{1}(V)}=\rho}I_{\lambda}=I_{\lambda}\left(v_{\lambda}\right)=:\alpha_{\lambda}

for all
0<\rho<\rho_{0}.
It follows that there exists a local minimum
v_{\rho}\in H^{1}(V)
such that

||v_{\rho}-v_{\lambda}||=\rho,

and
I_{\lambda}(v_{\rho})=\alpha_{\lambda}
for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
. Therefore, in this situation, we get a one-parameter family of solutions of (
3.2
). If case (II) happens, we can find
\rho_{1}\in(0,\rho_{0})
such that

(3.31)

\inf_{\left\|v-v_{\lambda}\right\|_{H^{1}(V)}=\rho_{1}}I_{\lambda}(v)>I_{\lambda}\left(v_{\lambda}\right)=\alpha_{\lambda}.

By (
3.30
), we deduce that

I_{\lambda}\left(u_{\lambda}-c_{0}\right)\leqslant I_{\lambda}\left(u_{\lambda}\right)-1<I_{\lambda}\left(v_{\lambda}\right)

for some
c_{0}>|V|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{1}
.

Define

\mathcal{P}=\left\{\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow H^{1}(V)\right.

|\gamma
is continuous and satisfies
\left.\gamma(0)=v_{\lambda},\gamma(1)=v_{\lambda}-c_{0}\right\}
and

\alpha=\inf\limits_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\lambda}(\gamma(t)).

From (
3.31
),
we conclude that

\alpha>I_{\lambda}\left(v_{\lambda}\right)\geqslant\max\left\{I_{\lambda}(\gamma(0)),I_{\lambda}(\gamma(1))\right\}~\forall\gamma\in\mathscr{P}.

Thus, by Lemma
3.6

,
I_{\lambda}
satisfies the hypothesis of the mountain-pass theorem. Thus
\alpha
is a critical point of
I_{\lambda}
. By virtue of
\alpha>I_{\lambda}(v_{\lambda})
, we get a second solution of (
3.2
).

We now complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

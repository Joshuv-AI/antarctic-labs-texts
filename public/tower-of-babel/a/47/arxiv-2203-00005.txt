Introduction

A classical result of Birkhoff
[
2
]
, which goes back to 1929, says that there are entire functions of which the integer translates are dense in the space of the entire functions endowed with the
{\cal{T}}_{u}
topology of local uniform convergence (see also Luh
[
8
]
for a more general statement). Birkhoff’s proof was constructive.

Much later, during the 80’s, Gethner and Shapiro
[
6
]
and independently Grosse-Erdmann
[
7
]
showed that Birkhoff’s result can be recovered as a particular case of a much more general theorem, through the use of Baire’s category theorem.

This approach simplified Birkhoff’s argument substantially and, in addition, it gave us precise information on the topological size of these functions. In particular, Grosse-Erdmann proved that for every fixed sequence of complex numbers
(w_{n})
with
w_{n}{\rightarrow}\infty
, the set

\big\{f\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})|\overline{\{f(z+w_{n}):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}={\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})\big\}

is
G_{\delta}
and dense in
{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
, and hence “large” in terms of topology.

More recently, Costakis and Sambarino
[
4
]
established a notable strengthening of Birkhoff’s result. Namely, they showed that, for almost all entire functions
f
, in the context of Baire’s category theorem, the set of the translates of
f
with respect to
na
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is dense in the space of all entire functions for every non-zero complex number
{\alpha}
. The significant new element here is the uncountable range of
{\alpha}
.

More specifically, the set

\bigcap_{{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}{\smallsetminus}\{0\}}\big\{f\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})|\overline{\{f(z+n{\alpha}):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}={\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})\big\}

is residual in
{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
, that is, it contains a
G_{\delta}
and dense subset of
{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
.

In particular, it is non-empty. Subsequently, Costakis
[
3
]
examined a similar result, where
n
can be replaced by more general sequences
({\lambda}_{n})
of non-zero complex numbers, so that
{\lambda}_{n}{\rightarrow}\infty
.

In this direction, Costakis
[
3
]
proved that if the sequence
({\lambda}_{n})
satisfies a certain condition, then the desired conclusion is reached, provided that we focus on
{\alpha}\in C(0,1):=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}/|z|=1\}
. Under this condition, it is proved that the set

\bigcap_{{\alpha}\in C(0,1)}\big\{f\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})|\overline{\{f(z+{\lambda}_{n}{\alpha}):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}={\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})\big\}

is residual in
{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
.

The proof of this result follows a similar method to the one used to prove a similar result in
[
3
]
. In the same article
[
3
]
, Costakis examined a simpler and more specific case of the above result. In particular:

In the above set, the request is to find an entire function
f
, so that:

\overline{\{f(z+{\lambda}_{n}{\alpha}):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}={\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})\ \ \text{for every}\ \ {\alpha}\in C(0,1).

Let
C\subseteq{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
be the set of constant functions of
H({\mathbb{C}})
, that is

C=\big\{g:{\mathbb{C}}{\rightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}|\ \ \text{there exists}\ \ {\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\ \ \text{such that}\ \ g(z)={\alpha}\ \ \text{for every}\ \ z\in{\mathbb{C}}\big\}

Firstly, Costakis proved in

that there is some

is a subset of
\overline{\{f(z+{\lambda}_{n}{\alpha}):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}
for every

This result offered a different and notable proof. In fact, he proved something stronger in this case, by adding a stronger condition of convergence. More specifically, Costakis proved the following result:

Let

be a sequence of non-zero complex numbers, so that

\displaystyle{\varPsi}_{n}((a,z)){\rightarrow}G(z),\ \ \text{for every}\ \ (a,z)\in K\times{\mathbb{C}}.

(15)

We shall consider the functions
{\varPsi}_{m,n}:K\times{\overline{D}}_{m}{\rightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}
, so that

{\varPsi}_{m,n}((a,z))={\varPsi}_{n}((a,z))\ \ \text{for every}\ \ n\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \ (a,z)\in K\times{\overline{D}}_{m},

that is
{\varPsi}_{m,n}={\varPsi}\upharpoonright K\times{\overline{D}}_{m}
is the restriction of
{\varPsi}_{n}
on
K\times{\overline{D}}_{m}
for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We set
{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}=\{{\varPsi}_{m,n}|n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Because
{\mathfrak{D}}
is equicontinuous on
K\times{\mathbb{C}}
we take that the sets
{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}
are equicontinuous on
K\times{\overline{D}}_{m}
for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Given that
{\varGamma}
is locally bounded we take that for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is
M_{m}>0
so that:

{\parallel}{\varPsi}_{n}{\parallel}_{K\times{\overline{D}}_{m}}<M_{m},\ \ \text{for every}\ \ n=1,2,{\ldots}\;.

This gives that
{\varPsi}_{m,n}\in B(0,M_{n})
for every
n=1,2,{\ldots}
, on
C(K\times{\overline{D}}_{m})
for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

So, we have
{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\subseteq B(0,M_{m})
for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore, the
{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}
is a bounded subset on metric space
(C(K\times{\overline{D}}_{m},{\parallel}\cdot{\parallel}\infty))
. So, the
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m}
set is a closed and bounded subset of
C(K\times{\overline{D}}_{m}),{\parallel}\cdot{\parallel}\infty))
, for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
).

We shall prove now that the set
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m}
is equicontinuous on
K\times{\overline{D}}_{m}
, for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We fix
m_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
w_{2}\in K\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}}
. We fix
{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
. We shall prove that
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m_{2}}
is equicontinuous on
w_{2}
. Because of the equicontinuity of
{\mathfrak{D}}_{m_{2}}
on
w_{2}
we take that for
\frac{{\varepsilon}_{1}}{3}>0
there is some
{\delta}_{2}>0
, so that for every
w\in K\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}}
,
\rho((w,w_{2}))<{\delta}_{2}
we have:

\displaystyle|{\varPsi}_{m_{2},n}(w)-{\varPsi}_{m_{2},n}(w_{0})|<\frac{{\varepsilon}_{1}}{3}\ \ \text{for every}\ \ n=1,2,{\ldots}\;.

(16)

Let
h\in\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m_{2}}
. Then, there is subsequence
(k_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of natural numbers, so that
{\varPsi}_{m_{2},k_{n}}{\rightarrow}h
uniformly on
K\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}}
as
n{\rightarrow}+\infty
.

Thus, there is
m_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq m_{3}
has as follows:

\displaystyle{\parallel}{\varPsi}_{m_{2},k_{2}}-h{\parallel}_{k\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}}}<\frac{{\varepsilon}_{1}}{3}.

(17)

We apply now (
16
) for
n=k_{m_{3}}
and (
17
) for
n=m_{3}
we take that for every
w\in K\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}}
,
\rho((w,w_{2}))<{\delta}_{2}
we have:

|h(w)-h(w_{2})|=|h(w)-{\varPsi}_{m_{2},k_{m_{3}}}(w)+{\varPsi}_{m_{2},k_{m_{3}}}(w)-{\varPsi}_{m_{2},k_{m_{3}}}(w_{2})+{\varPsi}_{m_{2},k_{m_{3}}}(w_{2})-h(w_{2})|<{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Because of the fact that number
{\delta}_{2}
depends only on
m_{2}
,
w_{2}
,
{\varepsilon}_{1}
and not on
h
, we take that the set
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m_{2}}
is equicontinuous on
w_{2}
, so the set
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m_{2}}
is equicontinuous on
K\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}}
.

We have proved that the set
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m_{2}}
is a closed, bounded and equicontinuous subset of
(C(K\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}}),{\parallel}\cdot{\parallel}\infty)
. Based on Ascoli’s-Arzela Theorem, we take that the set
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m_{2}}
is a compact subset of
C(K\times{\overline{D}}_{m_{2}})
. Thus, we take that the set
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{m}
is a compact subset of
C(K\times{\overline{D}}_{m})
for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We set
{\overline{G}}:K\times{\mathbb{C}}{\rightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}
, where
{\overline{G}}((a,z))=G(z)
for every
(a,z)\in K\times{\mathbb{C}}
. Of course,
{\overline{G}}\in C(K\times{\mathbb{C}})
.

We shall prove now that
{\varPsi}_{n}{\rightarrow}{\overline{G}}
on the space
C(K\times{\mathbb{C}})
endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of
K\times{\mathbb{C}}
.

For the purposes of proof by contradiction, we suppose that the sequence
({\varPsi}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
does not converge to
{\overline{G}}
on the space
C(K\times{\mathbb{C}})
.

Then, there is some
\nu_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, some positive number
{\varepsilon}_{2}>0
and some subsequence
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of natural numbers, so that:

\displaystyle{\parallel}{\varPsi}_{a_{n}}-{\overline{G}}{\parallel}_{K\times{\overline{D}}_{\nu_{1}}}\geq{\varepsilon}_{2}\ \ \text{for every}\ \ n=1,2,{\ldots}\;.

(18)

The sequence
({\varPsi}_{\nu_{1},a_{n}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence in
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{\nu_{1}}
. Because of the fact that
\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}_{\nu_{1}}
is compact, sequence
({\varPsi}_{\nu_{1},a_{n}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a subsequence
({\varPsi}_{\nu_{1},a_{\rho_{n}}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
that converges to some
h_{1}\in C(K\times{\overline{D}}_{\nu_{1}})
.

Therefore, we have:

\displaystyle{\varPsi}_{\nu_{1},a_{\rho_{n}}}{\rightarrow}h_{1}\ \ \text{uniformly on}\ \ K\times{\overline{D}}_{\nu_{1}}.

(19)

Based on (
15
), we have

{\varPsi}_{n}((a,z)){\rightarrow}G(z)\ \ \text{for every}\ \ (a,z)\in K\times{\mathbb{C}}.

This gives us:

\displaystyle{\varPsi}_{a_{\rho_{n}}}(w){\rightarrow}{\overline{G}}(w)\ \ \text{for every}\ \ w\in K\times{\overline{D}}_{\nu_{1}}.

(20)

Based on (
19
), we have:

\displaystyle{\varPsi}_{\nu_{1},a_{\rho_{n}}}(w){\rightarrow}h_{1}(w)\ \ \text{for every}\ \ w\in K\times{\overline{D}}_{\nu_{1}}.

(21)

Based on (
20
) and (
21
), we have:

\displaystyle h_{1}(w)={\overline{G}}(w)\ \ \text{for every}\ \ w\in K\times{\overline{D}}_{\nu_{1}}.

(22)

Based on (
19
) and (
22
), we have:

\displaystyle{\varPsi}_{\nu_{1},a_{\rho_{n}}}{\rightarrow}{\overline{G}}\ \ \text{uniformly on}\ \ K\times{\overline{D}}_{\nu_{1}}.n

(23)

Based on (
18
) and (
23
) we end up to a contradiction. So, we have proved that
{\varPsi}_{n}{\rightarrow}{\overline{G}}
uniformly on compact subsets of
K\times{\mathbb{C}}
, or else:

\sup_{(a,z)\in K\times L}|{\varPsi}_{n}((a,z))-G(z)|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty

for very compact set
L\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(m_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of complex numbers, so that
m_{n}{\rightarrow}\infty
,
{\theta}\in(0,1)
and
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
a\neq 0
.

Then, there is no entire function
f
, so that:

\sup_{t\in[0,{\theta}]}|f(m_{n}e^{2\pi it})-a|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{and}

\int_{[m_{n},m_{n}e^{2\pi i{\theta}}]}f(z)dz{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty.

Proof
.

To take a contradiction we suppose that there exists an entire function
f
that satisfies the above two convergence.

We have the curves
{\gamma}_{n}:[0,{\theta}]{\rightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}
, where
{\gamma}_{n}(t)=m_{n}e^{2\pi it}
for every
t\in[0,{\theta}]
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We also have
{\gamma}^{\ast}_{n}:={\gamma}_{n}([0,{\theta}])
for
n=1,2,{\ldots}\;.

Because
m_{n}{\rightarrow}\infty
, we use only the terms
m_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
m_{n}\neq 0
.

Based on Cauchy’s Theorem we have:

\displaystyle\int_{[m_{n},m_{n}e^{2\pi i{\theta}}]}f(z)dz=\int_{{\gamma}^{\ast}_{n}}f(z)d(z)\ \ \text{for every}\ \ n=1,2,{\ldots}\;.

(1)

We have:
A:{\mathbb{C}}{\rightarrow}{\mathbb{C}}
for the constant function, so that
A(z)=a
for every
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

We also have:

\displaystyle\int_{[m_{n},m_{n}e^{2\pi i{\theta}]}}A(z)dz=a(m_{n}e^{2\pi i{\theta}}-m_{n})\ \ \text{for every}\ \ n=1,2,{\ldots}\;.

(2)

We fix
{\varepsilon}_{0}\in\Big(0,\dfrac{|a|\cdot|e^{2\pi i{\theta}}-1|}{2\pi}\Big)
.

We can write down the first of the two convergences of hypothesis as follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in{\gamma}^{\ast}_{n}}|f(z)-a|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty.

(3)

By our hypothesis (
1
) and (
3
) we take that there is some
\nu_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq\nu_{0}
has as follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in{\gamma}^{\ast}_{n}}|f(z)-a|<{\varepsilon}_{0}\ \ \text{and}

(4)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{{\gamma}^{\ast}_{n}}f(z)dz\bigg|<{\varepsilon}_{0},\ \ m_{n}\neq 0.

(5)

Based on (
2
), Cauchy’s Theorem, (
4
) and the simple properties of the complex integrals, we have:

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{{\gamma}^{\ast}_{n}}f(z)dz-a(m_{n}e^{2\pi i{\theta}}-m_{n})\bigg|<2\pi|m_{n}|{\varepsilon}_{0}.

(6)

Based on (
4
), (
5
), (
6
), triangle inequality and the specific of
{\varepsilon}_{0}
we assume that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq\nu_{0}
, the following applies:

\displaystyle|m_{n}|<\frac{{\varepsilon}_{0}}{|a|\,|e^{2\pi i{\theta}}-1|-2\pi{\varepsilon}_{0}}

(7)

(where
|a|\,|e^{2\pi i{\theta}}-1|-2\pi{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
from the certain choice of
{\varepsilon}_{0}
).

Inequality (
7
) and the fact that
m_{n}{\rightarrow}\infty
gives a contradiction and this completes the proof of this lemma.
\quad\blacksquare

4
The main result

In order to prove the main result, we also need the following corollary of Lemma
3.2
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
(m_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of complex numbers,
{\theta}
be a positive number and
f
,
g
be two entire functions.

We suppose that for every compact subset
L
of
{\mathbb{C}}
the following shall apply:

\sup_{(t,z)\in[0,{\theta}]\times L}|f(z+m_{n}e^{2\pi it})-g(z)|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty.

Then, for every compact subset
L
of
{\mathbb{C}}
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}

\sup_{(t,z)\in[0,{\theta}]\times L}|f^{(\nu)}(z+m_{n}e^{2\pi it})-g^{(\nu)}(z)|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty.

Proof
.

It is simple implication of Lemma
3.2
by induction.

Now, we are ready to prove the main result of this article.
\quad\blacksquare

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\lambda}=({\lambda}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of non-zero complex numbers, so that
{\lambda}_{n}{\rightarrow}\infty
,
{\theta}\in(0,1)
and
G\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
, where
G
is not a constant function.

Then, we have:
{\cal{U}}({\lambda},{\theta},G)=\emptyset
.

Proof
.

We shall prove the Theorem by distinguishing some cases.

•

Case 1

G(0)=0
and
G^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
.

The result is supported by Proposition 1.

•

Case 2

G(0)=0
and
G^{\prime}(0)=0
.

We shall distinguish two cases here:

a)
G^{(\nu)}(0)=0
for every
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Provided that
G\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
we have
G(z)=\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{\nu=0}\dfrac{G^{(\nu)}(0)}{\nu!}z^{\nu}
for every
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
, so we have
G(z)=0
for every
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
, which is false because
G
is not a constant function in our hypothesis.

b) There is a
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\nu\geq 2
so that
G^{(\nu)}(0)\neq 0
.

Let
\nu_{0}=\min\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}|G^{(\nu)}(0)\neq 0\}
, that is
\nu_{0}
is the smallest natural number, so that
G^{(\nu_{0})}(0)\neq 0
. Of course,
\nu_{0}\geq 2
.

Therefore, we have
G^{(\nu_{0}-1)}(0)=0
and
G^{(\nu_{0})}(0)\neq 0
.

We suppose that
{\cal{U}}({\lambda},{\theta},G)\neq\emptyset
. Let
f\in{\cal{U}}({\lambda},{\theta},G)
. Then, there is a sequence
(m_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, so that
m_{n}\in\{{\lambda}_{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where for every compact subset
L\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}

\sup_{(t,z)\in[0,{\theta}]\times L}|f(z+m_{n}e^{2\pi it})-G(z)|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty.

Based on the above Corollary
4.1
, we take that for every compact subset
L\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,z)\in[0,{\theta}]\times L}|f^{(\nu_{0}-1)}(z+m_{n}e^{2\pi it})-G^{(\nu_{0}-1)}(z)|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty.

(1)

Because
G^{(\nu_{0}-1)}(0)=0
and
G^{(\nu_{0})}(0)\neq 0
we take that the function
G^{(\nu_{0}-1)}\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
is not a constant function. Of course, the function
f^{(\nu_{0}-1)}\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Based on (
1
) and Proposition
2.1
we have a contradiction, because, according to (
1
), we have
f^{(\nu_{0}-1)}\in{\cal{U}}({\lambda},{\theta},G^{(\nu_{0}-1)})
, that is
{\cal{U}}({\lambda},{\theta},G^{(\nu_{0}-1)})\neq\emptyset
that is false by Proposition
2.1
.

•

Case 3

G(0)\neq 0

We have function
g=G-G(0)
. Of course, we have
g\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
and
g(0)=0
.

We suppose that
{\cal{U}}({\lambda},{\theta},G)\neq\emptyset
. Let
f\in U({\lambda},{\theta},G)
.

Then, there is a sequence
m=(m_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of complex numbers, where
m_{n}\in\{{\lambda}_{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so that for every compact subset
L\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,z)\in[0,{\theta}]\times L}|f(z+m_{n}e^{2\pi it})-G(z)|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty.

(2)

We have function
F=f-G(0)
. Of course,
F\in{\cal{H}}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Based on (
2
) we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,z)\in[0,{\theta}]\times L}|F(z+m_{n}e^{2\pi it})-g(z)|{\rightarrow}0\ \ \text{as}\ \ n{\rightarrow}+\infty,\ \ \text{for every compact set}\ L\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}.

(3)

Based on (
3
) we have:
F\in{\cal{H}}({\lambda},{\theta},g)
where
g
is not a constant, according to its definition, that is,
{\cal{U}}({\lambda},{\theta},g)\neq\emptyset
, which is false by the above Cases 1 and 2, because
g(0)=0
.

The proof of our main result is complete now.
\quad\blacksquare

Boundary Green’s functions and Minkowski content measure of multi-force-point SLE ( ρ ¯ ) κ {}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
Dapeng Zhan

Abstract
We consider a transient chordal SLE ( ρ 1 , … , ρ m ) κ {}_{\kappa}(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}) curve η \eta in ℍ \mathbb{H} from w w to ∞ \infty with force points v 1 > ⋯ > v m v_{1}>\cdots>v_{m} in ( − ∞ , w − ] (-\infty,w^{-}] , which intersects and is not boundary-filling on ( − ∞ , v m ) (-\infty,v_{m}) . The main result is that there is an atomless locally finite Borel measure μ η \mu_{\eta} on η ∩ ( − ∞ , v m ] \eta\cap(-\infty,v_{m}] such that for any v < v m v<v_{m} , the d d -dimensional Minkowski content of η ∩ [ v , v m ] \eta\cap[v,v_{m}] exists and equals μ η ​ [ v , v m ] \mu_{\eta}[v,v_{m}] , where d = ( ∑ ρ j + 4 ) ​ ( κ − 4 − 2 ​ ∑ ρ j ) 2 ​ κ d=\frac{(\sum\rho_{j}+4)(\kappa-4-2\sum\rho_{j})}{2\kappa} is the Hausdorff dimension of η ∩ [ v , v m ] \eta\cap[v,v_{m}] . In the case that all ρ j = 0 \rho_{j}=0 , this measure agrees with the covariant measure derived in [ 2 ] for chordal SLE κ up to a multiplicative constant. We call such measure a Minkowski content measure, extend it to a class of subsets of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , and prove that they satisfy conformal covariance. To construct the Minkowski content measure on η ∩ [ v , v m ] \eta\cap[v,v_{m}] , we follow the standard approach to derive the existence and estimates of the one- and two-point boundary Green’s functions of η \eta on ( − ∞ , v m ) (-\infty,v_{m}) , which are the limits of the rescaled probability that η \eta passes through small discs or open real intervals centered at points on ( − ∞ , v m ) (-\infty,v_{m}) .



1
Introduction

The Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) introduced by Oded Schramm is a
one-parameter (
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
) family of random fractal curves growing in plane domains, which satisfy conformal invariance and domain Markov property. Due to its close relation with two-dimensional statistical lattice models, Brownian motion, Gaussian free field and Liouville quantum gravity, SLE has received a lot of attention over the past two decades. We refer the reader to Lawler’s textbook
[
9
]
for basic properties of SLE.

Hausdorff dimension and Minkowski content are central objects in the study of the fractal properties of SLE. It is known that the Hausdorff dimension of an SLE
κ
curve is
2\wedge(1+\frac{\kappa}{8})
(
[
20
,
4
]
), and the corresponding Minkowski content of any bounded SLE
κ
arc exists (
[
10
]
). The Hausdorff dimension of the intersection of an SLE
κ
curve with the domain boundary is
0\vee(1\wedge(2-\frac{8}{\kappa}))
(
[
3
]
), and the corresponding Minkowski content of the intersection restricted to any compact boundary arc also exists (
[
7
]
).

The Minkowski content of SLE agrees with the natural parametrization of SLE developed in
[
12
,
14
]
, which was constructed using the Doob-Meyer decomposition. Alberts and Sheffield used that idea to construct a conformally covariant measure on the intersection of a
chordal
SLE
κ
(one important type of SLE) curve with
\mathbb{R}
, and conjectured that their measure has close relation with the Minkowski content of the intersection (
[
2
]
).

In this paper, we work on chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
, which are natural variants of chordal SLE
κ
. The growth of a chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve is affected by the force values
\underline{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m})
and force points
(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
in addition to the initial point and target point of the curve. One-force-point SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\rho)
was introduced in
[
11
]
for the construction of conformal restriction measures. Multi-force-point SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
was extensively studied in
[
15
]
in the framework of imaginary geometry. Using imaginary geometry, the authors of
[
16
]
proved that for a (one-force-point) chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\rho)
curve
\eta
in
\mathbb{H}
started from
w
with force point
v<w
, the Hausdorff dimension of
\eta\cap(-\infty,v)
is
\frac{(\rho+4)(\kappa-4-2\rho)}{2\kappa}
, when
\rho\in((-2)\vee(\frac{\kappa}{2}-4),\frac{\kappa}{2}-2)
.

The goal of this paper is to prove the existence of the Minkowski content of the intersection of a multi-force-point chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve
\eta
with some boundary arc of the domain. We assume that the curve
\eta
grows in
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Imm z>0\}
from a point
w\in\mathbb{R}
to
\infty
, and the force points lie on the same side of
w
, say
v_{m}<\cdots<v_{1}<w
. Moreover, we assume that the force values are such that a.s.
\eta
intersects but does not contain
(-\infty,v_{m})
. By the work of
[
16
]
, the Hausdorff dimension of
\eta\cap(-\infty,v_{m}]
is
d:=\frac{(\rho_{\Sigma}+4)(\kappa-4-2\rho_{\Sigma})}{2\kappa}
, where
\rho_{\Sigma}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\rho_{j}
.
We are interested in the
d
-dimensional Minkowski content of
\eta\cap I
for intervals
I\subset(-\infty,v_{m}]
.

To establish the Minkowski content, we follow the standard approach in
[
10
]
and
[
7
]
to study the existence of the one-point Green’s function

G_{1}(z_{1}):=\lim_{r_{1}\to 0^{+}}r_{1}^{d-1}\mathbb{P}[\dist(z_{1},\eta\cap\mathbb{R})\leq r_{1}]

and the two-point Green’s function

G_{2}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\lim_{r_{1},r_{2}\to 0^{+}}r_{1}^{d-1}r_{2}^{d-1}\mathbb{P}[\dist(z_{j},\eta\cap\mathbb{R})\leq r_{j},j=1,2],

for
z_{1}\neq z_{2}\in(-\infty,v_{m})
, and derive some estimates.

The first result on SLE Green’s function in the literature (cf.
[
8
]
) is about the one-point Green’s function for a chordal SLE at an interior point, but with the Euclidean distance replaced by the conformal radius. The two-point conformal radius Green’s function for a chordal SLE at interior points was proved to exist in
[
13
]
. The existence of the Eucliean distance version (the original definition) of these Green’s functions was proved in
[
10
]
. The one- and two-point Green’s functions for a chordal SLE at boundary points were derived in
[
7
]
.

We use the ideas in
[
7
]
to study the boundary Green’s functions of a chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve
\eta
.
Due to the force points, the proofs here involve more technical details than those in
[
7
]
. We prove the existence of the one-point and two-point Green’s functions for
\eta
on
(-\infty,v_{m})
as well as some upper bounds and convergence rates. We also obtain the exact formula of the one-point Green’s function up to a multiplicative constant.

Using the estimates of these Green’s functions, we then prove that, for any bounded measurable set
S\subset(-\infty,v_{m}]
, the limit

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}r^{1-d}\lambda(\{u\in S:\dist(u,\eta\cap\mathbb{R})\leq r\})

converges both a.s. and in
L^{2}
, where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. We let
\xi_{S}
denote the limit and prove that whenever
\partial S
has zero Minkowski content,
\xi_{S}
is the Minkowski content of
\eta\cap{S}
.

Furthermore, we use these
\xi_{S}
to prove that there is a random measure
\mu_{\eta}
supported by
\eta\cap(-\infty,v_{m}]
such that for any compact interval
I\subset(-\infty,v_{m}]
,
\mu_{\eta}(I)=\xi_{I}
. We call such
\mu_{\eta}
the Minkowski content measure on
\eta\cap(-\infty,v_{m}]
. The Minkowski content measures could be defined on a more general family of subsets of Euclidean spaces. We prove that they satisfy the conformal covariance and restriction property, and use these properties to show that the increasing function
\mu_{\eta}(\eta[0,t])
could act as one component in the Doob-Meyer decomposition of some supermartingale. When all
\rho_{j}=0
, the SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
reduces to the standard chordal SLE
κ
. The above statement shows that the
\mu_{\eta}
in this special case agrees with the covariant measure developed in
[
2
]
for chordal SLE
κ
, and so proves the conjecture there.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review chordal Loewner equations, SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
processes, extremal length, and a family of Bessel-like diffusion processes, which will be needed later in this paper. In Section
3
we derive some one-point estimates of the SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve
\eta
, i.e., some upper bounds of the probability that
\eta
gets near a marked point in
(-\infty,v_{m}]
. In Sections
4
and
5
we obtain the existence and some estimates of the one- and two-point Green’s functions for
\eta
on
(-\infty,v_{m})
. In Section
6.1
we define the notion of Minkowski content measure, and develop a framework of this new concept. Finally, in Section
6.2
we use the Green’s functions from Sections
4
and
5
to construct the Minkowski content measure on
\eta\cap(-\infty,v_{m}]
and prove the conjecture of
[
2
]
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Chordal Loewner equation

A bounded relatively closed subset
K
of
\mathbb{H}
is called an
\mathbb{H}
-hull if
\mathbb{H}\setminus K
is a simply connected domain.
For an
\mathbb{H}
-hull
K
, there is a unique conformal map
g_{K}
from
\mathbb{H}\setminus K
onto
\mathbb{H}
such that
g_{K}(z)=z+O(\frac{1}{z})
as
z\to\infty
.
If
K=\emptyset
,
g_{K}=\id
. Suppose
K\neq\emptyset
. Let
K^{\doub}=\overline{K}\cup\{\overline{z}:z\in{\overline{K}}\}
, where
\overline{K}
is the closure of
K
, and
\overline{z}
is the complex conjugate of
z
. By Schwarz reflection principle,
g_{K}
extends conformally to
\mathbb{C}\setminus K^{\doub}
. Let
S_{K}=\mathbb{C}\setminus g_{K}(\mathbb{C}\setminus K^{\doub})
. Then
S_{K}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
. Let
a_{K}=\min(\overline{K}\cap\mathbb{R})
,
b_{K}=\max(\overline{K}\cap\mathbb{R})
,
c_{K}=\min S_{K}
,
d_{K}=\max S_{K}
. Then
g_{K}
maps
\mathbb{C}\setminus(K^{\doub}\cup[a_{K},b_{K}])
conformally onto
\mathbb{C}\setminus[c_{K},d_{K}]
(cf.
[
24
, Section 5.2]
).

Let
W\in C([0,T),\mathbb{R})
for some
T\in(0,\infty]
. The chordal Loewner equation driven by
W
is

\partial_{t}g_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-W(t)},\quad 0\leq t<T;\quad g_{0}(z)=z.

For every
z\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\tau^{*}_{z}
be the first time that the solution
t\mapsto g_{t}(z)
blows up; if such time does not exist, then set
\tau^{*}_{z}=\infty
.
For
t\in[0,T)
, let
K_{t}=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:\tau^{*}_{z}\leq t\}
. It turns out that for each
t\geq 0
,
K_{t}
is an
\mathbb{H}
-hull,
K_{0}=\emptyset
,
K_{t}\neq\emptyset
for
t>0
, and
g_{t}
agrees with
g_{K_{t}}
. We call
g_{t}
and
K_{t}
the chordal Loewner maps and hulls, respectively, driven by
W
. We write
g^{W}_{t}
and
K^{W}_{t}
to emphasize the dependence of
g_{t}
and
K_{t}
on
W
, when necessary. Let
c^{W}_{t}=c_{K^{W}_{t}}
and
d^{W}_{t}=d_{K^{W}_{t}}
for
t>0
; and
c^{W}_{0}=d^{W}_{0}=W(0)
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
w\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathbb{R}_{w}
be the set
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{w\}\cup\{w^{+},w^{-}\}
, where
w^{+}
and
w^{-}
are two symbols lying outside
\mathbb{R}
, which satisfy the inequalities
w^{-}<w<w^{+}
,
w^{+}<x
, and
w^{-}>y
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
x>w>y
.
Let
W,g^{W}_{t},\tau^{*}_{z},c^{W}_{t},d^{W}_{t}
be as above. The modified chordal Loewner maps
\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}
driven by
W
are functions defined on
\mathbb{R}_{W(0)}
for
0\leq t<T
such that

\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g^{W}_{t}(v),&\mbox{if }0\leq t<\tau^{*}_{v};\\
d^{W}_{t},&\mbox{if }t\geq\tau^{*}_{v}\mbox{ and }v\geq W(0)^{+};\\
c^{W}_{t},&\mbox{if }t\geq\tau^{*}_{v}\mbox{ and }v\leq W(0)^{-}.\end{array}\right.

Here
\tau^{*}_{W(0)^{\pm}}
are understood as
0
, and so
\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}(W(0)^{+})=d^{W}_{t}
and
\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}(W(0)^{-})=c^{W}_{t}
.

We remark that the
\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}
agrees with the
g^{w}_{K}
in
[
23
, Definition 2.11]
when
w=W(0)
and
K=K^{W}_{t}
. It is useful to describe the force point processes of SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
with boundary force points. See the next subsection.

For
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and
S\subset\mathbb{C}
, let
\rad_{z_{0}}(S)=\sup\{|z_{0}-z|:z\in S\cup\{z_{0}\}\}
. The following proposition is well known (cf.
[
9
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
K_{t}
and
g_{t}
,
0\leq t\leq\delta
, be chordal Loewner hulls and maps driven by
W
. Suppose
\rad_{{W(0)}}(K_{\delta})\leq r
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
r>0
. Then we have

(i)

|W(\delta)-W(0)|\leq 2r
;

(ii)

|g_{\delta}(z)-z|\leq 3r
for any
z\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}\setminus\overline{{K_{\delta}}}
;

(iii)

|\ln(g_{\delta}^{\prime}(z))|\leq 5(\frac{r}{|z-x_{0}|})^{2}
for any
z\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}
with
|z-x_{0}|\geq 10r
.

We use
f_{t}=f^{W}_{t}
to denote the continuation of
(g^{W}_{t})^{-1}
from
\mathbb{H}
to
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
, when the continuation exists. If for every
t\in[0,T)
,
f^{W}_{t}
exists, and
\eta(t):=f^{W}_{t}({W(t)})
is continuous on
[0,T)
, then we say that
\eta
is the chordal Loewner curve driven by
W
. Such
\eta
may not exist in general; and when it exists,
\eta
is a curve in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
started from
W(0)
, and
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t}
is the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta[0,t]
for all
t
.

2.2
Chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}({\underline{\rho}})
processes

When the driving function
W
equals
\sqrt{\kappa}B(t)
, where
\kappa>0
and
B
is a standard Brownian motion, the chordal Loewner curve driven by
W
is known to exist (
[
20
]
) and is called a chordal SLE
κ
curve in
\mathbb{H}
from
0
to
\infty
.
The chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
processes are natural variants of chordal SLE
κ
, where one keeps track of additional marked points. We now review the definition and properties of chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
developed in
[
15
]
that will be needed in this paper.

Let
\kappa>0
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
,
w\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{w}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{w\}\cup\{w^{+},w^{-}\}
. We require that for
\sigma\in\{+,-\}
,
\sum_{j:v_{j}=w^{\sigma}}\rho_{j}>-2
. We write
\underline{\rho}
and
\underline{v}
respectively for
(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m})
and
(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
.
The (chordal) SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{{\rho}})
process in
\mathbb{H}
started from
w
with force points
\underline{v}
is the chordal Loewner process driven by the function
W(t)
,
0\leq t<T
, which solves the SDE

dW(t)=\sqrt{\kappa}dB(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbbm{1}_{\{W(t)\neq\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}(v_{j})\}}\frac{\rho_{j}}{W(t)-\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}(v_{j})}\,dt,\quad W(0)=w,

where
B(t)
is a standard Brownian motion, and
\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}
are as in Definition
2.1
.

The SDE should be understood as an integral equation, i.e.,
W-w-\sqrt{\kappa}B
is absolutely continuous with the derivative being the summation in the SDE. The solution exists uniquely up to the first time
T
(called a continuation threshold) that
\sum_{j:g^{W}_{t}(v_{j})=W(t)}\rho_{j}\leq-2
.
If there does not exist a continuation threshold, then the lifetime is
\infty
.

We call
V_{j}(t):=\widehat{g}^{W}_{t}(v_{j})
the force point process started from
v_{j}
. It is absolutely continuous with initial value
V_{j}(0)=v_{j}
and derivative
\mathbbm{1}_{\{W(t)\neq{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\widehat{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{g}}}^{W}_{t}(v_{j})\}}\frac{2}{V_{j}(t)-W(t)}
. In fact, the set
\{t:W(t)={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\widehat{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}g}}^{W}_{t}(v_{j})\}
has Lebesgue measure zero. So we will omit the factors
\mathbbm{1}_{\{W(t)\neq{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\widehat{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}g}}^{W}_{t}(v_{j})\}}
. Thus,
W
and
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
together solve the following system of SDE-ODE:

dW=\sqrt{\kappa}dB+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\rho_{j}dt}{W-V_{j}},\quad W_{j}(0)=w_{j};

(2.1)

dV_{j}=\frac{2dt}{V_{j}-W},\quad V_{j}(0)=v_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

(2.2)

Here we omit “
(t)
” for simplicity.
Moreover,
V_{j}\geq W
if
v_{j}\geq w^{+}
; and
V_{j}\leq W
if
v_{j}\leq w^{-}
. If
V_{j}(t_{0})=V_{k}(t_{0})
at some point
t_{0}
, then
V_{j}(t)=V_{k}(t)
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. If
v_{j}\geq v_{k}
, then
V_{j}\geq v_{k}
. If
v_{j}=v_{k}
for some
j\neq k
, then
V_{j}=V_{k}
, and so
v_{j}
and
v_{k}
may be treated as a single force point with force value
\rho_{j}+\rho_{k}
.

An SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
process a.s. generates a chordal Loewner curve
\eta
, which satisfies the following DMP (domain Markov property). Let
{\cal F}=({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the complete filtration generated by
\eta
. We write
\underline{V}
for
(V_{1},\dots,V_{m})
. If
\tau
an
{\cal F}
-stopping time, then on the event
\{\tau<T\}
, there is a curve
\eta^{\tau}
in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
such that
\eta(\tau+\cdot)=f_{\tau}(\eta^{\tau})
, and the law of
\eta^{\tau}
conditionally on
{\cal F}_{\tau}
is an SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve started from
W(\tau)
with force points
\underline{V}(\tau)
, where when
V_{j}(\tau)=W(\tau)
, then
V_{j}(\tau)
as a force point is treated as
W(\tau)^{+}
(resp.
W(\tau)^{-}
) if
v_{j}\geq w^{+}
(resp.
v_{j}\leq w^{-}
).

2.3
Extremal length

Extremal length is a nonnegative quantity
\lambda(\Gamma)
associated with a family of (piecewise
C^{1}
) curves (cf.
[
1
, Definition 4-11]
).
The extremal distance between
E
and
F
in
\Omega
, denoted by
d_{\Omega}(E,F)
, is the extremal length of the family of curves in
\Omega
connecting
E
and
F
.

Extremal length satisfies conformal invariance (cf.
[
1
, Section 4-1]
) and comparison principle (
[
1
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
). The conformal invariance means that if
\Gamma
is a family of curves in a domain
D
, and
f
is a conformal map on
D
, then
\lambda(f(\Gamma))=\lambda(\Gamma)
. The comparison principle means that if every curve in
\Gamma
contains a curve in
\Gamma^{\prime}
, then
\lambda(\Gamma)\geq\lambda(\Gamma{{}^{\prime}})
. If every curve in
\Gamma
contains a curve in
\Gamma_{1}
and a curve in
\Gamma_{2}
, and
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
are respectively contained in
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
, which are disjoint open sets, then
\lambda(\Gamma)\geq\lambda(\Gamma_{1})+\lambda(\Gamma_{2})
.

Suppose
R
is a rectangle of dimension
a\times b
, and
I_{1},I_{2}
are sides of
R
with length
a
. Then
d_{R}(I_{1},I_{2})=b/a
(cf.
[
1
, Section 4-2]
). By conformal invariance, we get the following result.

Example 2.3
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
, let
A_{\mathbb{H}}(x,r_{1},r_{2})
denote the semi-annulus
\{z\in\mathbb{H}:r_{1}<|z-x|<r_{2}\}
. Let
I_{j}=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:|z-x|=r_{j}\}
,
j=1,2
. Then
d_{A_{\mathbb{H}}(x,r_{1},r_{2})}(I_{1},I_{2})=\frac{1}{\pi}\ln(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be a disjoint pair of connected nonempty closed subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
that intersect
\mathbb{R}
. Then we have

\prod_{j=1}^{2}\Big(\frac{\diam(S_{j})}{\dist(S_{1},S_{2})}\wedge 1\Big)\leq 144e^{-\pi d_{\mathbb{H}}(S_{1},S_{2})}.

If
S_{2}
is unbounded, then for any
z_{0}\in S_{1}
,

\frac{\rad_{z_{0}}(S_{1})}{\dist(z_{0},S_{2})}\wedge 1\leq 144e^{-\pi d_{\mathbb{H}}(S_{1},S_{2})}.

Proof.

The first statement in the special case that
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are bounded is exactly
[
17
, Lemma 2.9]
. The general case follows from the special case by replacing
S_{j}
with
S_{j}\cap\{|z|\leq n\}
and letting
n\to\infty
. The second statement follows immediately from the first one.
∎

2.4
Transition density of some diffusion processes

In this subsection we review some results about the transition density of a family of Bessel-like diffusion processes, which will be used in the proofs of Theorems
3.3
and
4.6
. See Remarks
3.2
and
4.7
.
We first briefly review Jacobi polynomials (cf.
[
18
, Chapter 18]
). For indices
\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-}>-1
,
Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})}(x)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, are a class of orthogonal polynomials w.r.t. the weight
w(s):=(1-s)^{\alpha_{+}}(1+s)^{\alpha_{-}}
. They satisfy

P_{0}^{(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})}\equiv 1;

(2.3)

\int_{-1}^{1}w(s)P_{n}^{(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})}(s)P_{m}^{(\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-})}(s)ds=0,\quad\mbox{if }n\neq m;

(2.4)

(2.5)

\widehat{W},\widehat{U},\widehat{R},\widehat{D}_{u},\widehat{V}_{j},\widehat{Q}_{j},\widehat{H}_{j}
on
[0,\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*})
, to be respectively the time-changes of
W,U,R,D_{u},V_{j},Q_{j},H_{j}
via
\Phi
. For example,
\widehat{R}(t)=R(\Phi^{-1}(t))
,
0\leq t<\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}
. By (
3.6
,
3.9
),
\widehat{H}_{j}
satisfies the following ODE on
[0,\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*})
:

\frac{d\widehat{H}_{j}}{\widehat{H}_{j}}=\frac{2}{\kappa}\cdot\frac{1}{\widehat{R}}\cdot\frac{\widehat{V}_{0}-\widehat{V}_{j}}{\widehat{W}-\widehat{V}_{j}}dt=\frac{2}{\kappa}\cdot\frac{1-\widehat{Q}_{j}}{\widehat{R}}dt,\quad 0\leq j\leq m-1.

(3.11)

By (
3.8
,
3.10
), we have

\widehat{H}_{j}(t)=\frac{\Upsilon_{j}(\Phi^{-1}(t))}{\Upsilon_{0}(\Phi^{-1}(t))}\asymp\frac{|v_{j}-u|\wedge(|w-u|e^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}t})}{|w-u|e^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}t}}.

(3.12)

From (
3.7
,
3.9
), for some standard Brownian motion
\widehat{B}
,
\widehat{R}
satisfies the following SDE:

d\widehat{R}=\sqrt{\widehat{R}(1-\widehat{R})}d\widehat{B}+\frac{\kappa-2-\rho_{m}}{\kappa}(1-\widehat{R})dt-\frac{2}{\kappa}dt-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{\rho_{j}}{\kappa}\widehat{Q}_{j}dt.

(3.13)

By Itô’s formula, for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

\frac{d\widehat{R}^{c}}{\widehat{R}^{c}}=c\sqrt{\frac{1-\widehat{R}}{\widehat{R}}}d\widehat{B}+\Big(c\frac{\kappa-2-\rho_{m}}{\kappa}+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\Big)\frac{1-\widehat{R}}{\widehat{R}}dt-\frac{2c}{\kappa\widehat{R}}dt-\frac{c}{\widehat{R}}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{\rho_{j}}{\kappa}\widehat{Q}_{j}dt

=c\sqrt{\frac{1-\widehat{R}}{\widehat{R}}}d\widehat{B}+\Big(c\frac{\kappa-4-\rho_{\Sigma}}{\kappa}+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\Big)\frac{dt}{\widehat{R}}-\Big(c\frac{\kappa-2-\rho_{m}}{\kappa}+\frac{c(c-1)}{2}\Big)dt+c\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{\rho_{j}}{\kappa}\frac{1-\widehat{Q}_{j}}{\widehat{R}}dt.

Let
\widehat{E}(t)=e^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}t}
.
By taking
c=2\gamma
in the above SDE and using (
3.11
), we find that

\widehat{M}:=\widehat{E}^{-\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\widehat{R}^{2\gamma}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\widehat{H}_{j}^{-\gamma\rho_{j}}

is a positive local martingale on
[0,\widehat{\tau}^{*}_{u})
and satisfies the SDE

\frac{d\widehat{M}}{\widehat{M}}=\frac{2\rho_{\Sigma}+8-\kappa}{\kappa}\frac{1-\widehat{R}}{\sqrt{\widehat{R}(1-\widehat{R})}}d\widehat{B}.

(3.14)

Let

\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}=\frac{\widehat{H}_{j+1}}{\widehat{H}_{j}}=\frac{\widehat{V}_{j+1}-\widehat{U}}{\widehat{V}_{j}-\widehat{U}}\in(0,1]
,
0\leq j\leq m-2
. By (
3.11
), each
\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}
satisfies
\frac{d\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}}{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}}=\frac{2}{\kappa}\frac{\widehat{Q}_{j}-\widehat{Q}_{j+1}}{\widehat{R}}\geq 0
, and so is increasing.
Since
\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{j-1}=\rho_{j}
and
\widehat{H}_{0}=1
, we have

\widehat{M}=\widehat{E}^{-\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\widehat{R}^{2\gamma}\prod_{j=0}^{m-2}\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}^{\gamma\sigma_{j}-\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}.

(3.15)

We will apply Girsanov theorem. Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the current measure. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\tau_{n}
be the maximum element in
[0,\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}]
such that
|\ln(\frac{\widehat{M}(t)}{\widehat{M}(0)})|\leq n
on
[0,\tau_{n})
. Then
\widehat{M}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{n})
is a uniformly bounded martingale on
[0,\infty)
. Here when
\tau_{n}=\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}
and
t\geq\tau_{n}
,
\widehat{M}(t\wedge\tau_{n})
is understood as
\lim_{t\uparrow\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}}\widehat{M}(t)
, which a.s. converges on the event
\{\tau_{n}=\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}\}
. So we get
\mathbb{E}[\frac{\widehat{M}(\tau_{n})}{\widehat{M}(0)}]=1
. Now we define a new probability measure
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
by
\frac{d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\frac{\widehat{M}(\tau_{n})}{\widehat{M}(0)}
.
By Girsanov theorem and (
3.13
,
3.14
), under the new measure
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
,
\widehat{R}
satisfies the following SDE on
[0,\tau_{n})
:

d\widehat{R}=\sqrt{\widehat{R}(1-\widehat{R})}d\widetilde{B}-\frac{2}{\kappa}dt-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{\rho_{j}}{\kappa}\widehat{Q}_{j}dt-\frac{2\rho_{\Sigma}+6-\rho_{m}}{\kappa}(\widehat{R}-1)dt,

where
\widetilde{B}
is a standard Brownian motion under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
. Since
\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{j-1}=\rho_{j}
,
\sigma_{0}=2
,
\widehat{Q}_{0}=1
, and
\widehat{Q}_{m-1}=1-\widehat{R}
, we may rewrite the above SDE as

d\widehat{R}=\sqrt{\widehat{R}(1-\widehat{R})}d\widetilde{B}-\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\kappa}(\widehat{Q}_{j}-\widehat{Q}_{j+1})dt-\frac{\rho_{\Sigma}+4}{\kappa}(\widehat{R}-1)dt.

(3.16)

Since
\widehat{Q}_{j}\geq\widehat{Q}_{j+1}
and
\sigma_{j}\leq\sigma^{*}
, we get

\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\kappa}(\widehat{Q}_{j}-\widehat{Q}_{j+1})\leq\frac{\sigma^{*}}{\kappa}(1-\widehat{Q}_{m-1})\leq\Big(\frac{\sigma^{*}}{\kappa}\vee\frac{1}{2}\Big)\widehat{R}
.
Thus, under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
,
\widehat{R}
up to
\tau_{n}
satisfies the property of
\widetilde{X}
in Lemma
2.7
with
\delta_{-}:=\frac{4}{\kappa}(\rho_{\Sigma}+4)>2
and
\delta_{+}:=\frac{4}{\kappa}\sigma^{*}\vee 2\geq 2
. By Lemma
2.7
, under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{N}
, up to
\tau_{n}
,
\widehat{R}
stochastically dominates the process
X
, which starts from
1
, and satisfies the SDE (
2.15
).
By Proposition
2.5
(i),
X
has a transition density
p_{t}(x,y)
, which by (
2.13
) satisfies

p_{t}(x,y)\lesssim y^{\frac{\delta_{-}}{2}-1}=y^{2\gamma}.

(3.17)

Let
L>0
be such that
|w-u|e^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}L}=\frac{r}{4}
. By (
3.10
), we get (
3.4
) if

\mathbb{P}[\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}>L]\lesssim\prod_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\Big(\frac{|v_{j+1}-u|}{|v_{j}-u|}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{j}}.

(3.18)

Since
\frac{d\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\frac{\widehat{M}(\tau_{n})}{\widehat{M}(0)}
and
\widehat{M}(\cdot\wedge\tau_{n})
is a uniformly bounded martingale under
\mathbb{P}
, we get

\mathbb{P}[L<\tau_{n}\wedge\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}]=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{L<\tau_{n}\wedge\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}\}}{\widehat{M}(0)}/{\widehat{M}(L\wedge\tau_{n}\wedge L)}]=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{L<\tau_{n}\wedge\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}\}}{\widehat{M}(0)}/{\widehat{M}(L)}].

Define
\widehat{N}(t)=\prod_{j=0}^{m-2}\Big(\frac{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(t)}{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(0)}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{m-1}-\gamma\sigma_{j}}
.
Since
\widehat{E}(L)=e^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}L}\asymp\frac{r}{|w-u|}
, by (
3.15
),

\frac{\widehat{M}(0)}{\widehat{M}(L)}=\widehat{E}(L)^{\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\widehat{R}(L)^{-2\gamma}\widehat{N}(L)\asymp\Big(\frac{r}{|w-u|}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\widehat{R}(L)^{-2\gamma}\widehat{N}(L).

Since
0<\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(0)\leq\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(L)\leq 1
and
\sigma^{*}\geq\sigma_{j}\geq 0
, by (
3.12
) and the fact that
|v_{j_{0}}-u|=r\asymp|w-u|e^{-\frac{2}{\kappa}L}
, we have

\widehat{N}(L)\leq\prod_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\Big(\frac{1}{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(0)}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\cdot\prod_{j=j_{0}}^{m-2}\Big(\frac{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(L)}{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(0)}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\Big(\frac{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(L)}{\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(0)}\Big)^{-\gamma\sigma_{j}}

\leq\widehat{H}_{m-1}(0)^{-\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\Big(\frac{\widehat{H}_{m-1}(L)}{\widehat{H}_{j_{0}}(L)}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\widehat{H}_{j_{0}}(L)^{-\gamma\sigma_{*}}\prod_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\widehat{H}_{j,j+1}(0)^{\gamma\sigma_{j}}

\asymp\Big(\frac{r}{|w-u|}\Big)^{-\gamma\sigma_{m-1}}\prod_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\Big(\frac{|v_{j+1}-u|}{|v_{j}-u|}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{j}}.

Here we used that
\widehat{H}_{m-1}(L)/\widehat{H}_{m-1}(0)\asymp|w-u|/r
and
\widehat{H}_{j_{0}}(L)\asymp 1
.
Since
\widehat{R}
under
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
up to
\tau_{n}
stochastically dominates the process
X
with transition density
p_{t}(x,y)
, and
-2\gamma<0
, by (
3.17
) we get

\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}_{n}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{L<\tau_{n}\wedge\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}\}}\widehat{R}(L)^{-2\gamma}\Big]\leq\int_{0}^{1}y^{-2\gamma}p_{L}(1,y)dy\lesssim 1.

By the last four displayed formulas we get

\mathbb{P}[L<\tau_{n}\wedge\widehat{\tau}_{u}^{*}]\lesssim\prod_{j=0}^{j_{0}-1}\Big(\frac{|v_{j+1}-u|}{|v_{j}-u|}\Big)^{\gamma\sigma_{j}}
, which implies the desired estimate (
3.18
) as
n
tends to
\infty
.
∎

4
One-point Green’s Function

Let
\kappa,\rho_{\Sigma},\sigma^{*},\sigma_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy that
\kappa>0
,
\rho_{\Sigma}>(-2)\vee(\frac{\kappa}{2}-4)
and
\sigma^{*}\geq 2\geq\sigma_{*}>0
.
In this section, a constant depends only on
\kappa,\rho_{\Sigma},\sigma^{*},\sigma_{*}
.
We will use the following positive constants:

\gamma=\frac{2\rho_{\Sigma}+8-\kappa}{2\kappa},\quad\alpha=(\rho_{\Sigma}+2)\gamma,\quad\alpha_{*}=\sigma_{*}\gamma,\quad\alpha^{*}=\sigma^{*}\gamma.

(4.1)

We write
X\lesssim Y
or
Y\gtrsim X
if there is a constant
C>0
such that
X\leq CY
; and write
X\asymp Y
if
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
. We write
X=O(Y)
as
R\to 0^{+}
if there are constants
C,c>0
such that when
0<R<c
, we have
X\leq CY
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\underline{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfy that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\rho_{j}=\rho_{\Sigma}
and
\sigma_{j}:=2+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\rho_{k}\in[\sigma_{*},\sigma^{*}]
,
0\leq j\leq m-1
. We also define
\sigma_{m}=2+\rho_{\Sigma}=2+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\rho_{j}
, but do not require
\sigma_{m}\in[\sigma_{*},\sigma^{*}]
. Define

\alpha_{0}=2\gamma,\quad\alpha_{j}=\rho_{j}\gamma,\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

(4.2)

Note that
\alpha=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\alpha_{j}
.
Let
w\in\mathbb{R}
and
v_{m}\leq\cdots\leq v_{1}\in(-\infty,w)\cup\{w^{-}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{w}
. We write
\underline{v}
for
(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})
. Let
\eta
be an SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve in
\mathbb{H}
started from
w
with force points
\underline{v}
. The assumption that
\sum_{k=1}^{j}\rho_{k}=\sigma_{j}-2\geq\sigma_{*}-2>-2
,
1\leq j\leq m-1
, and
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\rho_{k}=\rho_{\Sigma}>-2
implies that the continuation threshold is never met, and the lifetime of
\eta
is
\infty
.

Define
G:\{(w,\underline{v},u)\in\mathbb{R}^{m+2}:w\geq v_{1}\geq\cdots\geq v_{m}>u\}\to(0,\infty)
by

G(w,\underline{v};u)=|w-u|^{-\alpha_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}|v_{j}-u|^{-\alpha_{j}}

(4.3)

=\Big(\frac{|v_{1}-u|}{|w-u|}\Big)^{\sigma_{0}\gamma}\cdot\Big(\frac{|v_{2}-u|}{|v_{1}-u|}\Big)^{\sigma_{1}\gamma}\cdots\Big(\frac{|v_{m}-u|}{|v_{m-1}-u|}\Big)^{\sigma_{m-1}\gamma}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{|v_{m}-u|}\Big)^{\sigma_{m}\gamma}.

(4.4)

For
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
r>0
, and
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{C},\mathbb{R}\}
, let
B_{\mathbb{F}}(u,r)=\{z\in\mathbb{F}:|z-u|\leq r\}
, and
\tau^{u}_{r;\mathbb{F}}=\inf(\{t:\eta(t)\in B_{\mathbb{F}}(u,r)\}\cup\{\infty\})
. Then
\eta\cap B_{\mathbb{F}}(u,r)\neq\emptyset
iff
\tau^{u}_{r;\mathbb{F}}<\infty
. We also define
\tau^{u}_{r}=\tau^{u}_{r;\mathbb{C}}
.

The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(One-point Green’s Function)
.

Suppose either
\rho_{\Sigma}\in((-2)\vee(\frac{\kappa}{2}-4),\frac{\kappa}{2}-2)
and
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
, or
\rho_{\Sigma}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}-2
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.
There are a constant
C_{\mathbb{F}}>0
depending only on
\mathbb{F},\kappa,\rho_{\Sigma}
, and a constant
\beta\in(0,1)
depending only on
\kappa,\rho_{\Sigma},\sigma_{*}
such that, for any
u\in(-\infty,v_{m})
, as
r/{|v_{m}-u|}\to 0^{+}
,

\mathbb{P}[\eta\cap B_{\mathbb{F}}(u,r)\neq\emptyset]=C_{\mathbb{F}}G(w,\underline{v};u)r^{\alpha}+O\Big(\Big(\frac{|v_{m}-u|}{|w-u|}\Big)^{\alpha_{*}}\Big(\frac{r}{|v_{m}-u|}\Big)^{\alpha+\beta}\Big).

(4.5)

Recall that the implicit constants in the
O
symbol depend only on
\kappa,\rho_{\Sigma},\sigma^{*},\sigma_{*}
.

Remark 4.2
.

The theorem does not include the case that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and
\rho_{\Sigma}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}-2
because in that case
\eta
a.s. does not hit
(-\infty,v_{m})
.
We do not have exact formulas of
C_{\mathbb{F}}
in general (unless
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and
\rho=0
), as happened in
[
7
,
10
]
. We do have an exact formula of
\beta
:
\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{\rho_{\Sigma}+4}{\rho_{\Sigma}+3}+\frac{\alpha_{*}+1}{\alpha_{*}}
, but it is very unlikely to be sharp. To prove the theorem, we will prove Theorem
4.6
after some lemmas, which is the “
m=1
” case of Theorem
4.1
. The general case of Theorem
4.1
follows from the special case and the fact that the force point functions tend to merge together.

From now on, we fix
u\in(-\infty,v_{m})
. Let
L_{0}=|w-v_{m}|\geq 0
and
L_{1}=|v_{m}-u|>0
. Using that
\sigma^{*}\geq\sigma_{k}\geq\sigma_{*}>0
,
|w-u|\geq|v_{1}-u|\geq\cdots\geq|v_{m}-u|>0
, and (
4.1
,
4.4
), we get

\Big(\frac{L_{1}}{L_{0}+L_{1}}\Big)^{\alpha^{*}}\Big(\frac{r}{L_{1}}\Big)^{\alpha}\leq G(w,\underline{v};u)r^{\alpha}\leq\Big(\frac{L_{1}}{L_{0}+L_{1}}\Big)^{\alpha_{*}}\Big(\frac{r}{L_{1}}\Big)^{\alpha}\leq\Big(\frac{r}{L_{1}}\Big)^{\alpha}.

(4.6)

By treating
u
as a force point of
\eta
with force value
0
, we may apply Theorem
3.3
to conclude that

\mathbb{P}[\dist(\eta,u)\leq r]\lesssim({L_{1}}/{(L_{0}+L_{1})})^{\alpha_{*}}({r}/{L_{1}})^{\alpha},\quad\mbox{for any }r\in(0,L_{1}),

(4.7)

which implies that

\mathbb{P}[\dist(\eta,u)\leq r]\lesssim({r}/{L_{1}})^{\alpha}\quad\mbox{for any }r>0.

(4.8)

When
v_{m}<w^{-}
, i.e.,
L_{0}>0
, by Theorem
3.3
,

\mathbb{P}[\dist(\eta,v_{m})\leq r]\lesssim({r}/{L_{0}})^{\alpha_{*}},\quad\mbox{for any }r>0.

(4.9)

Let
K_{t},g_{t},{\cal F},W,\underline{V}=(V_{1},\dots,V_{m})
be as in the previous section. Let
U
and
D_{u}
be defined by (
3.1
).
We have the following estimate.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
R>r\in(0,|v_{m}-u|)
. Let
\tau
be an
{\cal F}
-stopping time.
Then

\mathbb{P}[\tau^{u}_{r}<\infty|{\cal F}_{\tau},\{\tau\leq\tau^{u}_{R}\}\}]\lesssim(r/R)^{\alpha}.

(4.10)

Proof.

We may assume that
r<R/2
since the conditional probability is bounded by
1
. We only need to prove (
4.10
) with
E:=\{\tau\leq\tau^{u}_{R}\}
replaced by
E_{0}:=\{\tau<\tau^{*}_{u}\wedge\tau^{u}_{R}\}
since the conditional probability on
E\setminus E_{0}
is
0
. Suppose
E_{0}
occurs. By DMP of SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
, the conditional probability equals the probability that an SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve
\eta^{\tau}
in
\mathbb{H}
started from
W(\tau)
with force points
\underline{V}(\tau)
visits
g_{\tau}(B_{\mathbb{C}}(u,r))
. Recall that
g_{\tau}
maps
\mathbb{C}\setminus(K_{\tau}^{\doub}\cup[v_{m},\infty))
, which contains
u
, conformally onto
\mathbb{C}\setminus[V_{m}(\tau),\infty)
.
By Koebe
1/4
theorem,
|V_{m}(\tau)-U(\tau)|\geq D_{u}(\tau)R/4
. Since
r<R/2
, by Koebe distortion theorem,
\rad_{U(\tau)}(g_{\tau}(B_{\mathbb{C}}(u,r)))\leq 4D_{u}(\tau)r
. So
\rad_{U(\tau)}(g_{\tau}(B_{\mathbb{C}}(u,r)))/|V_{m}(\tau)-U(\tau)|\leq 16r/R
. By (
4.8
), the conditional probability that
\eta^{\tau}
visits
g_{\tau}(B_{\mathbb{C}}(u,r))
is bounded by a positive constant
times
(r/R)^{\alpha}
. So we have (
4.10
).
∎

For
t\geq 0
, we write
\partial_{\mathbb{H}}^{+}K_{t}
for the union of the “right side” of
\partial K_{t}
and the real interval
[b_{K_{t}},\infty)
. More precisely,
\partial_{\mathbb{H}}^{+}K_{t}
is the
g_{t}^{-1}
image of the interval
[W(t),\infty)
as a set of prime ends of
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t}
.
We have the following estimate.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\tau
be an
{\cal F}
-stopping time. On the event
\tau<\infty
, let
I
be an
{\cal F}_{\tau}
-measurable crosscut of
{\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{\tau}}
, which disconnects
g_{\tau}^{{-1}}(V_{m}(\tau))=v_{m}\wedge a_{K_{\tau}}
, viewed as a prime end of
{\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{\tau}}
, from
\partial^{+}_{\mathbb{H}}K_{\tau}
in
{\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{\tau}}
. Then

\mathbb{P}[\eta\mbox{ visits }I|{\cal F}_{\tau},\{\tau<\infty\}]\lesssim\exp(-\pi\alpha_{*}d_{\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{\tau}}(I,\partial^{+}_{\mathbb{H}}K_{\tau})).

(4.11)

Proof.

By DMP the conditional probability equals the probability that an SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
curve
\eta^{\tau}
in
\mathbb{H}
started from
W(\tau)
with force points
\underline{V}(\tau)
visits
g_{\tau}(I)
. By the assumption of
I
,
g_{\tau}(I)
is a crosscut of
\mathbb{H}
that disconnects
V_{m}(\tau)
from
[W(\tau),\infty)
.
By conformal invariance of extremal length,
d_{\mathbb{H}}(g_{\tau}(I),[W(\tau),\infty))=d_{\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{\tau}}(S,\partial^{+}_{\mathbb{H}}K_{\tau})
. By Proposition
2.4
,
1\wedge\frac{\rad_{V_{m}(\tau)}(g_{\tau}(I))}{|W(\tau)-V_{m}(\tau)|}\lesssim\exp({-\pi d_{\mathbb{H}}(g_{\tau}(I),[W(\tau),\infty))|})
. So we get the conclusion using (
4.9
).
∎

The following lemma is the first two-point estimate in this paper.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume
v_{m}<w
. Then for any
r_{0},r_{1}>0
,

\mathbb{P}[\dist(v_{m},\eta)\leq r_{0},\dist(u,\eta)\leq r_{1}]\lesssim({r_{0}}/{L_{0}})^{\alpha_{*}}({r_{1}}/{L_{1}})^{\alpha}.

(4.12)

Proof.

If
r_{1}\geq L_{1}/6
, then we get (
4.12
) by applying (
4.9
) to bound
\mathbb{P}[\dist(v_{m},\eta)\leq r_{0}]
. If
r_{0}\geq(L_{0}\wedge L_{1})/6
and
r_{1}<L_{1}/6
, then we get (
4.12
) by applying (
4.7
) to bound
\mathbb{P}[\dist(u,\eta)\leq r_{1}]
and using the fact that
\frac{L_{1}}{L_{0}+L_{1}}\asymp\frac{L_{0}\wedge L_{1}}{L_{0}}
.
So we may now assume that
r_{0}<(L_{0}\wedge L_{1})/6
and
r_{1}<L_{1}/6
.
WLOG, we may assume that
r_{1}=e^{-N}L_{1}/2
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\tau^{0}=\tau^{v_{m}}_{r_{0}}
and
\tau^{1}_{n}=\tau^{u}_{L_{1}e^{-n}/2}

,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then (
4.12
) is equivalent to

\mathbb{P}[\tau^{0}<\infty,\tau^{1}_{N}<\infty]\lesssim({r_{0}}/{L_{0}})^{\alpha_{*}}e^{-\alpha N}.

(4.13)

By (
4.9
,
4.7
),

\mathbb{P}[\tau^{0}<\infty]\lesssim(r_{0}/L_{0})^{\alpha_{*}};

(4.14)

\mathbb{P}[\tau^{1}_{n}<\infty]\lesssim({L_{1}}/({L_{0}}+L_{1}))^{\alpha_{*}}e^{-\alpha n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.15)

By (
4.10
),

\mathbb{P}[\tau^{1}_{N}<\infty|{\cal F}_{\tau^{1}_{n}},\tau^{1}_{n}<\infty]\lesssim e^{-\alpha(N-n)},\quad n\leq N.

(4.16)

Note that, when
\tau^{1}_{n}<\tau^{0}
, every curve in
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{\tau^{1}_{n}}
connecting
I:=\{z\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}:|z-v_{m}|=r_{0}\}
with

13
]
:

G_{\mathbb{F}}(w,\underline{v};u_{1},u_{2})=G_{0;\mathbb{F}}(u_{1})\mathbb{E}_{*}[G_{\tau^{*}_{u_{1}};\mathbb{F}}(u_{2})],

where
\mathbb{E}_{*}
stands for the expectation w.r.t. the SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho},\kappa-8-2\rho_{\Sigma})
curve started from
w
with force points
(\underline{v},u_{1})

6
Minkowski Content Measure

Let
\eta
be as in Theorems
4.1
and
5.3
. The purpose of this section is to use those theorems to construct a covariant Borel measure, called the Minkowski content measure, on
\eta\cap(-\infty,v_{m}]
, which is closely related to the Minkowski contents of subsets of
\eta\cap(-\infty,v_{m}]
.

6.1
General theory

In this subsection, we first review the Minkowski contents, and then define the Minkowski content measures, and derive some basic properties.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\lambda^{n}
denote the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Fix
d\in(0,n)
. Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
r>0
, let
B(S,r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\dist(x,S)\leq r\}
, and
\Cont(S;r)=r^{d-n}\lambda^{n}(B(S,r))
. The upper and lower (
d
-dimensional) Minkowski content of
S
are respectively defined by

\overline{\Cont}(S):=\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\Cont(S;r),\quad\underline{\Cont}(S):=\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\Cont(S;r).

When
\overline{\Cont}(S)=\underline{\Cont}(S)
, the common value, denoted by
\Cont(S)
or
\Cont_{d}(S)
, is called the (
d
-dimensional) Minkowski content of
S
.

Remark 6.1
.

The definition here differs from that in
[
5
, 3.2.37]
in two aspects. First, we allow
d
to be not an integer; second, we omit a multiplicative constant:
\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-d}{2}+1)}{\pi^{\frac{n-d}{2}}}
for simplicity.

For
S_{1},S_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, from
\lambda^{n}(B(S_{1}\cup S_{2},r))\leq\lambda^{n}(B(S_{1},r))+\lambda^{n}(B(S_{2},r))
, we get

\overline{\Cont}(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\leq\overline{\Cont}(S_{1})+\overline{\Cont}(S_{2}).

(6.1)

\underline{\Cont}(S_{1}\cup S_{2})\leq\underline{\Cont}(S_{1})+\overline{\Cont}(S_{2}).

(6.2)

Lemma 6.2
.

For any compact sets
S_{1},S_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\underline{\Cont}(S_{1})+\underline{\Cont}(S_{2})\leq\underline{\Cont}(S_{1}\cup S_{2})+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\overline{\Cont}(S_{1}\cap B(S_{1}\cap S_{2},\varepsilon)).

(6.3)

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
S_{1}\cap B(S_{2},1/k)\downarrow S_{1}\cap S_{2}
, there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S_{1}\cap B(S_{2},1/k_{0})\subset B(S_{1}\cap S_{2},\varepsilon)
. Let
r\in(0,1/(2k_{0}))
. Suppose
x\in B(S_{1},r)\cap B(S_{2},r)
. Then there exist
y_{j}\in S_{j}
such that
|x-y_{j}|\leq r
,
j=1,2
. Then
|y_{1}-y_{2}|\leq 2r\leq 1/k_{0}
. So
y_{1}\in S_{1}\cap B(S_{2},1/k_{0})\subset S_{1}\cap B(S_{1}\cap S_{2},\varepsilon)
, which implies that
x\in B(S_{1}\cap B(S_{1}\cap S_{2},\varepsilon),r)
. So we get

B(S_{1},r)\cap B(S_{2},r)\subset B(S_{1}\cap B(S_{1}\cap S_{2},\varepsilon),r)
. Thus,

\Cont(S_{1};r)+\Cont(S_{2};r)\leq\Cont(S_{1}\cup S_{2};r)+\Cont(S_{1}\cap B(S_{1}\cap S_{2},\varepsilon);r).

We then get (
6.3
) by first sending
r
to
0^{+}
and then sending
\varepsilon
to
0^{+}
.
∎

For
T\subset S\subset\mathbb{R}
, we write
\partial_{S}T
for the relative boundary of
T
in
S
, i.e.,
S\cap\overline{T}\cap\overline{S\setminus T}
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
{\cal M}
denote the family of all sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that can be expressed as the intersection of an open set with a closed set.
Fix
S\in\cal M
. A
d
-dimensional Minkowski content measure on
S
is a Borel measure
\mu
on
S
which satisfies that, for any compact set
K\subset S
, (i)
\mu(K)<\infty
, and (ii) whenever
\mu(\partial_{S}K)=0
,
\Cont_{d}(K)
exists and equals
\mu(K)
.

Remark 6.4
.

A Minkowski content measure
\mu
on any
S\in\cal M
is
\sigma
-finite since
S
can be expressed as a countable union of compact sets. Given such
\mu
, for
1\leq k\leq n
, let
E_{\mu,k}
denote the set of points
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mu\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{k}=c\}>0
. Then
E_{\mu,k}
is countable. Let
\cal R_{\mu}
denote the family of
n
-dimensional rectangles
R=\prod_{k=1}^{n}I_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that each
I_{k}
is a compact interval with
\partial I_{k}\cap E_{\mu,k}=\emptyset
. Then
\mu(\partial R)=0
for
R\in\cal R_{\mu}
, and every compact
n
-dimensional rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
could be approximated by elements in
\cal R_{\mu}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

The Minkowski content measure on any
S\in\cal M
is unique , when it exists.

Proof.

Let
S=F\cap G\in\cal M
, where
F
is closed and
G
is open. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
be Minkowski content measures on
S
. Let
{\cal R}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}^{G}
be the family of
R\in{\cal R}_{\mu_{1}}\cap{\cal R}_{\mu_{2}}
that are contained in
G
. For any
R\in{\cal R}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}^{G}
, from
\mu_{j}(\partial R)=0
and
\partial_{S}(R\cap F)\subset\partial R
, we get
\mu_{j}(R)=\mu_{j}(R\cap F)=\Cont(R\cap F)
,
j=1,2
, which implies that
\mu_{1}(R)=\mu_{2}(R)
. Since every compact rectangle in
G
can be approximated by elements in
{\cal R}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}}^{G}
, we get
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

(i) If
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S\in\cal M
, then for any compact set
K\subset S
,
\overline{\Cont}(K)\leq\mu(K)
. (ii) If
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is compact, and
\mu
is a measure on
S
such that
\Cont(S)=\mu(S)<\infty
, and for any compact set
K\subset S
,
\overline{\Cont}(K)\leq\mu(K)
, then
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
.

Proof.

(i) Let
S=F\cap G
, where
F
is closed and
G
is open. Let
K
be a compact subset of
S
. Then for any
k>0
,
K
can be covered by finitely many rectangles in
\cal R_{\mu}
that are contained in
B(K,1/k)\cap G
. Let
K_{k}
be the union of these rectangles. Then
K_{k}
is a compact subset of
B(K,1/k)\cap G
, and
\mu(\partial K_{k})=0
. Now
K_{k}\cap S=K_{k}\cap F
is a compact subset of
S
, and
\partial_{S}(K_{k}\cap S)\subset\partial K_{k}
. So
\mu(\partial_{S}(K_{k}\cap S))=0
. Thus,
\overline{\Cont}(K)\leq\overline{\Cont}(K_{k}\cap S)=\mu(K_{k}\cap S)\leq\mu(B(K,1/k))
. Sending
k
to
\infty
, we get
\overline{\Cont}(K)\leq\mu(K)
.

(ii) Let
K
be a compact subset of
S
such that
\mu(\partial_{S}K)=0
. Let
L=\overline{S\setminus K}
. Then
L
is also a compact subset of
S
,
S=K\cup L
, and
K\cap L=\partial_{S}K
. From
\mu(\partial_{S}K)=0
we get
\Cont(S)=\mu(S)=\mu(K)+\mu(L)
.
Thus, by the assumption and (
6.2
),

\mu(S)=\Cont(S)\leq\underline{\Cont}(K)+\overline{\Cont}(L)\leq\overline{\Cont}(K)+\overline{\Cont}(L)\leq\mu(K)+\mu(L)=\mu(S).

So we get
\Cont(K)=\mu(K)
, and conclude that
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
S\in\cal M
and
\mu
be a measure on
S
. Suppose there exists a family of compact subsets
\{F_{\iota}\}_{\iota\in I}
of
S
such that (i) for every
\iota\in I
,
\Cont(F_{\iota})=\mu(F_{\iota})<\infty
; (ii) for every compact
K\subset S
, there exists
\iota\in I
such that
K\subset F_{\iota}
; and (iii) every compact
K\subset S
can be expressed as
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}J_{n}
, where
(J_{n})
is a decreasing sequence of sets such that each
J_{n}
is a finite disjoint union of elements in
\{F_{\iota}\}
. Then
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
.

Proof.

Let
K_{0}\subset S
be compact. By (ii) there is
\iota\in I
such that
K_{0}\subset F_{\iota}
. So we have
\mu(K_{0})\leq\mu(F_{\iota})<\infty
by (i). Suppose further that
\mu(\partial_{S}K_{0})=0
. Let
\mu_{F_{\iota}}=\mu(\cdot\cap F_{\iota})
be a measure on
F_{\iota}
. Then
\Cont(F_{\iota})=\mu_{F_{\iota}}(F_{\iota})<\infty
. Let
K\subset F_{\iota}
be compact. By (iii) we express
K=\bigcap J_{n}
, where
(J_{n})
is a decreasing sequence of sets each of which is a finite disjoint union of elements in
\{F_{\iota}\}
. By (
6.1
) and (i), each
J_{n}
satisfies
\overline{\Cont}(J_{n})\leq\mu(J_{n})
. Since
\mu(J_{n})\downarrow\mu(K)
, we get
\overline{\Cont}(K)\leq\mu(K)
.
By Lemma
6.6
(ii),
\mu_{F_{\iota}}
is the Minkowski content measure on
{F_{\iota}}
. Since
K_{0}
is a compact subset of
{F_{\iota}}
, and
\mu_{F_{\iota}}(\partial_{F_{\iota}}K_{0})\leq\mu(\partial_{S}K_{0})=0
, we get
\Cont(K_{0})=\mu_{F_{\iota}}(K_{0})=\mu(K_{0})
. Thus,
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
.
∎

Example 6.8
.

Let
n=1
. Let
B
be a
1

L_{t}
be the usual local time of

at
0
(cf.
[

]
). Let
Z
be the zero set
\{t:B_{t}=0\}
. There is a deterministic constant
C_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any
t_{2}\geq t_{1}\geq 0
,
\Cont_{1/2}(Z\cap[t_{1}{,}t_{2}])=C_{0}(L_{t_{2}}-L_{t_{1}})

[

]

6.7
with

Example 6.9
.

Let
n=2
. Let
\kappa\in(0,8)
and

. Let
\eta
be an SLE
κ
curve in
\mathbb{H}
from

to
\infty
. The natural parametrization of

is a random continuously strictly increasing function
\theta
that satisfies that for any
{t_{2}\geq t_{1}\geq 0}

(

with

Example 6.10
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Let

be an

C_{n}\in(0,\infty)
such that for any

,
\Cont_{2}(B[t_{1},t_{2}])=C_{n}(t_{2}-t_{1})
(cf.

C_{n}B_{*}{(}\lambda^{1}|_{[0,\infty)}{)}

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be compact. For
r>0
, let
\mu_{r}
be the measure such that
d\mu_{r}=r^{d-n}\mathbbm{1}_{B(S,r)}d\lambda^{n}
. Then
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
iff
\mu_{r}\to\mu
weakly as
r\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

First, suppose
\mu_{r}\to\mu
weakly as
r\to 0^{+}
. Since
\mu_{r}
is supported by
B(S,r)
,
\mu
is supported by
S
. Since
|\mu_{r}|=\Cont(S;r)
by definition, we get
|\mu|=\lim|\mu_{r}|=\Cont(S)
.
Let
K
be a compact subset of
S
. Fix
r_{0}>0
. Then for any
r\in(0,r_{0})
,
\Cont(K;r)\leq\mu_{r}(B(K;r_{0}))
. Sending
r
to
0^{+}
, we get
\overline{\Cont}(K)\leq\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\mu_{r}(B(K,r_{0}))\leq\mu(B(K,r_{0}))
since
B(K,r_{0})
is closed. Sending
r_{0}
to
0^{+}
, we get
\overline{\Cont}(K)\leq\mu(K)
. By Lemma
6.6
(ii),
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
.

Second, suppose
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
. Then
|\mu|=\Cont(S)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}|\mu_{r}|
. Thus, the family
(\mu_{r})_{r\in(0,1]}
is pre-compact w.r.t. the weak convergence. Suppose
\mu_{r_{n}}\to\mu^{\prime}
weakly for some sequence
r_{n}\to 0
. Then
\mu^{\prime}
is supported by
S
, and
|\mu^{\prime}|=\lim|\mu_{r_{n}}|=\lim\Cont(S;r_{n})=\Cont(S)
.
Let
K
be a compact subset of
S
such that
\mu(\partial_{S}K)=0
. Then
\Cont(K)=\mu(K)
. Fix
r_{0}\in(0,1]
. Choose
n
big enough such that
r_{n}<r_{0}
. Then
\mu_{r_{n}}(B(K,r_{0}))\geq\Cont(K,r_{n})
. First sending
n
to
\infty
and then sending
r_{0}
to
0^{+}
, we get
\mu^{\prime}(K)\geq\Cont(K)=\mu(K)
. Now let
K
be any compact subset of
S
. Then we could find a decreasing sequence of compact subsets
(K_{n})
of
S
such that
K=\bigcap_{n}K_{n}
and
\mu(\partial_{S}K_{n})=0
for each
n
. Then
\mu^{\prime}(K)=\lim\mu^{\prime}(K_{n})\geq\lim\mu(K_{n})=\mu(K)
. Since this holds for any compact
K\subset S
and
|\mu^{\prime}|=|\mu|
, we get
\mu^{\prime}=\mu
. This means that any subsequential weak limit of
(\mu_{r})
as
r\to 0^{+}
is
\mu
. Thus,
\mu_{r}\to\mu
weakly as
r\to 0^{+}
.
∎

Theorem 6.12

(Restriction Property)
.

Let
\mu
be the Minkowski content measure on
S\in\cal M
. Let
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be closed and
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. Suppose
\mu(\partial_{S}(S\cap F))=0
. Let
S^{\prime}=S\cap F\cap G
and
\mu^{\prime}=\mu|_{S^{\prime}}
. Then
\mu^{\prime}
is the Minkowski content measure on
S^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We have
S^{\prime}\in\cal M
since
\cal M
is closed under finite intersections. Let
K\subset S^{\prime}
be compact. Then
K
is also a compact subset of
S
, and so
\mu^{\prime}(K)=\mu(K)<\infty
. Suppose
\mu^{\prime}(\partial_{S^{\prime}}K)=0
. Then
\mu(\partial_{S^{\prime}}K)=0
. Let
S_{F}=S\cap F
. Since
S^{\prime}=S_{F}\cap G
is relatively open in
S_{F}
, and
K\subset S^{\prime}
is compact, we get
\partial_{S^{\prime}}K=\partial_{S_{F}}K
. From
K\subset S_{F}\subset S
, we get
\partial_{S}K\subset\partial_{S_{F}}K\cup\partial_{S}S_{F}
. Thus,
\mu(\partial_{S}K)\leq\mu(\partial_{S^{\prime}}K)+\mu(\partial_{S}S_{F})=0
. Since
\mu
is the Minkowski content measure on
S
, we get
\Cont(K)=\mu(K)=\mu^{\prime}(K)
. So
\mu^{\prime}
is the Minkowski content measure on
S^{\prime}
.
∎

The following theorem asserts that the Minkowski content measures are preserved under covariant transforms
[
2
, Definition 1.1]
. For an open set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that a map
\phi:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is conformal if it is
C^{1}
and the Jacobian matrix at every
x\in G
is some positive number
s(x)
times an orthogonal matrix, and the
|\phi^{\prime}(x)|
is understood as such (unique) number
s(x)
. When
n=1
, this means that
\phi
is
C^{1}
and
\phi^{\prime}\neq 0
. When
n=2
,
\phi
is holomorphic or anti-holomorphic on each component of
G
with nonzero complex derivatives. When
n\geq 3
, by Liouville’s theorem,
\phi
is a Möbius transformation on each component of
G
.

Theorem 6.13

(Conformal Covariance)
.

Let
\mu
be the Minkowski content measure on
S\in\cal M
, which is relatively closed in an open set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\phi:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be conformal and injective.
Then
\nu:=\phi_{*}(|\phi^{\prime}|^{d}\cdot\mu)
is the Minkowski content measure on
\phi(S)
, where

|\phi^{\prime}|^{d}\cdot\mu
is the measure
\mu
weighted by
|\phi^{\prime}|^{d}
, and
\nu
is the pushforward of
|\phi^{\prime}|^{d}\cdot\mu
under
\phi
.

Proof.

Since
S
is relatively closed in
G
and
\phi
is a homeomorphism on
G
, both
\phi(G)
and
\phi(G\setminus S)
are open. So
\phi(S)=\phi(G)\setminus\phi(G\setminus S)\in\cal M
.

Let
K
be any compact subset of
G
. Let
m_{K}=\min_{x\in K}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|
and
M_{K}=\max_{x\in K}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|
. Let
c>1
. Then there is
\delta>0
such that
|\phi^{\prime}(x)|<cM_{K}
for
x\in B(K,\delta)
, and
|((\phi)^{-1})^{\prime}(y)|\leq c/m_{K}
for
y\in B(\phi(K),\delta)
. Thus, for
r>0
small enough, we have

\phi(B(K,\frac{r}{cM_{K}}))\subset B(\phi(K),r)\subset\phi(B(K,\frac{r}{m_{K}/c})),

which then implies that

\frac{(m_{K}/c)^{n}}{(cM_{K})^{n-d}}\Cont(K;\frac{r}{cM_{K}})\leq\Cont(\phi(K);r)\leq\frac{(cM_{K})^{n}}{(m_{K}/c)^{n-d}}\Cont(K;\frac{r}{m_{K}/c}).

First sending
r
to
0^{+}
and then sending
c
to
1^{+}
, we get

{m_{K}^{n}}{M_{K}^{d-n}}\cdot\underline{\Cont}(K)\leq\underline{\Cont}(\phi(K))\leq\overline{\Cont}(\phi(K))\leq{M_{K}^{n}}{m_{K}^{d-n}}\cdot\overline{\Cont}(K).

(6.4)

Now assume
K\subset S
and
\mu(\partial_{S}K)=0
. Since
\Cont(K)=\mu(K)
and
m_{K}\leq|\phi^{\prime}|\leq M_{K}
on
K
, we get

\frac{m_{k}^{n}}{M_{K}^{n}}\int_{K}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{d}\mu(dx)\leq\underline{\Cont}(\phi(K))\leq\overline{\Cont}(\phi(K))\leq\frac{M_{K}^{n}}{m_{K}^{n}}\int_{K}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{d}\mu(dx).

(6.5)

Let
K_{\phi}\subset\phi(S)
be compact. Let
K=\phi^{-1}(K_{\phi})
. Then
K\subset S
is compact. So
\mu(K)<\infty
. Since
|\phi^{\prime}|
is bounded on
K
,
\nu(K_{\phi})<\infty
. Suppose
\nu(\partial_{\phi(S)}K_{\phi})=0
. We want to show that
\Cont(K_{\phi})=\nu(K_{\phi})
. Since
\partial_{S}K=\phi^{-1}(\partial_{\phi(S)}K_{\phi})
, we get
\mu(\partial_{S}K)=0
.

Fix
c>1
. We may find
R_{1},\dots,R_{N}\in{\cal R}_{\mu}
which intersect each other only at their boundaries such that
K\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}R_{j}\subset G
, and for each
1\leq j\leq N
,
\max_{x\in R_{k}}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq c\min_{x\in R_{k}}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|
. Let
K_{j}=K\cap R_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
. Then
\mu(\partial_{S}K_{j})\leq\mu(\partial_{S}K)+\mu(\partial R_{j})=0
. By (
6.5
),

c^{-n}\int_{K_{j}}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{d}\mu(dx)\leq\underline{\Cont}(\phi(K_{j}))\leq\overline{\Cont}(\phi(K_{j}))\leq c^{n}\int_{K_{j}}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{d}\mu(dx).

(6.6)

Summing up the last inequality of (
6.6
) over
1\leq j\leq N
, and using (
6.1
) and that
\mu(K_{j}\cap K_{k})\leq\mu(R_{j}\cap R_{k})=0
when
j\neq k
, we get

\overline{\Cont}(K_{\phi})\leq c^{n}\int_{K}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{d}\mu(dx)
.

Let
1\leq k<j\leq N
. Since
\mu(K_{j}\cap K_{k})=0
, we get
\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\overline{\Cont}(K_{j}\cap B(K_{j}\cap K_{k},\delta))=0
by Lemma
6.6
(i). By (
6.4
),
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\overline{\Cont}(\phi(K_{j})\cap B(\phi(K_{j})\cap\phi(K_{k}),\varepsilon))=0
. By (
6.1
), we then get

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\overline{\Cont}(\phi(K_{j})\cap B(\phi(K_{j})\cap\bigcup_{k=1}^{j-1}\phi(K_{k}),\varepsilon))=0
for
2\leq j\leq n
. By (
6.3

\underline{\Cont}(K_{\phi})\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\underline{\Cont}(\phi(K_{j}))
, which together with the first inequality of (

\Cont(K_{\phi})=\int_{K}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{d}\mu(dx)=\nu(K_{\phi})

.
∎

Remark 6.14
.

It’s easy to extend the notions of Minkowski content and Minkowski content measure to Riemannian manifolds. The propositions in this subsection will still hold.

Remark 6.15
.

Most well-known deterministic fractal sets do not have non-trivial Minkowski contents. However, we may define the average Minkowski content by

\Cont_{d}^{\aaa}(S)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Cont_{d}(S;e^{-r})dr,

and use this to define the average Minkowski content measure. All results in this subsection hold with the word “average” added before “Minkowski” and
\Cont
replaced by
\Cont^{\aaa}
except that Theorem
6.11
requires moderate modification. The correct statement is: for any compact set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mu
is the average Minkowski content measure on
S
iff
\mu
is the weak limit of
\mu_{t}
as
t\to\infty
, where
\mu_{t}
is the measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfies
d\mu_{t}/d\lambda^{n}=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(n-d)s}\mathbbm{1}_{B(S,e^{-s})}ds
.

Example 6.16
.

Let
S\subset[0,1]
be the
1/3
-Cantor set. Let

. Then
\Cont_{d}(S,e^{-t})
,

, has period
\ln(3)
, and is not constant. So the Minkowski content
\Cont_{d}(S)
does not exist, but the average Minkowski content

for some

. Then the average Minkowski content measure on

exists and equals

is the Haar probability measure on

Remark 6.18

We emphasize here that the a.s. existence of the Minkowski content measure

(6.10)

By (

(6.11)

Combining (

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[J_{r_{1}}(u_{1})J_{r_{2}}(u_{2})]-G_{2}(u_{1},u_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|v_{m}-u_{1}|^{-\alpha}|u_{1}-u_{2}|^{-\alpha}\Big(1\wedge\Big(\frac{r_{1}\vee r_{2}}{|v_{m}-u_{1}|\wedge|u_{1}-u_{2}|}\Big)^{\zeta}\Big).

(6.12)

Fix a bounded measurable set
S\subset(-\infty,v_{m}]
. Let
A=\sup\{|v_{m}-u|:u\in S\}

Y_{r}=\int_{S}J_{r}(u)\lambda(du)=r^{-\alpha}\lambda(B_{\mathbb{R}}(\eta\cap\mathbb{R},r)\cap S).

(6.13)

From (

\int_{S}\!\int_{S}G_{2}(u_{1},u_{2})\lambda(du_{1})\lambda(du_{2})\lesssim\int_{0}^{A}L_{1}^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{A-L_{1}}L_{2}^{-\alpha}\lambda(dL_{2})\lambda(dL_{1})<\infty.

The finiteness of this integral together with (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(Y_{r_{1}}-Y_{r_{2}})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{S}J_{r_{1}}(u)\lambda(du)-\int_{S}J_{r_{2}}(u)\lambda(du)\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2}\int\!\!\int_{\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in S^{2}:u_{1}>u_{2}\}}|\mathbb{E}[J_{r_{j}}(u_{1})J_{r_{k}}(u_{2})]-G(u_{1},u_{2})|\lambda(du_{1})\lambda(du_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{A}\!\!\int_{0}^{A}L_{1}^{-\alpha}L_{2}^{-\alpha}\Big(1\wedge\Big(\frac{r_{1}\vee r_{2}}{L_{1}\wedge L_{2}}\Big)^{\zeta}\Big)\lambda(dL_{1})\lambda(dL_{2}),

where in the last line we used a change of variables:
L_{1}=v_{m}-u_{1}
and
L_{2}=u_{1}-u_{2}
. Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

,
we can easily bound the last integral and get

\|Y_{r_{1}}-Y_{r_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim A^{2-2\alpha-\zeta}(r_{1}\vee r_{2})^{\zeta}.

(6.14)

This implies that

is Cauchy in

as

By (

By Borel-Cantelli lemma, with probability one,

for sufficiently large

, which implies that a.s.

since

in

. A similar argument shows that, for any

, a.s.

as

.

Let
E_{0}
be the event that

for every

. Then

and

By Fubini Theorem,

\int_{S}\mathbb{E}[J_{r}(u)]\lambda(du)\to\mathbb{E}[\xi_{S}],\quad\int_{S}\!\int_{S}\mathbb{E}[J_{r}(u_{1})J_{r}(u_{2})]\lambda(du_{1})\lambda(du_{2})\to\mathbb{E}[\xi_{S}^{2}].

(6.15)

We now define

,
u\in(-\infty,v_{m}]
. Then for

has a continuous version on

and if

6.8

Theorem 6.21

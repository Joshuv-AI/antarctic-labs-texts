Mean first-passage time of a random walker under Galilean transformation
Marcus Dahlenburg, Gianni Pagnini

Abstract
We consider a continuous-time random walk model with finite-mean waiting-times and we study the mean first-passage time (MFPT) as estimated by an observer in a reference frame 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , that is co-moving with a target, and by an observer in a reference frame 𝒮 ′ \mathcal{S}^{\prime} , that is in uniform motion with respect to the target through a Galilean transformation. We found that the simple picture emerging in 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , where the mean first-passage time depends on the whole jump distribution but only on the mean value of the waiting-times, does indeed not hold in 𝒮 ′ \mathcal{S}^{\prime} where the estimation depends on the whole jump distribution and also on the whole distribution of the waiting-times. We derive the class of jump-size distributions such that the dependence of the MFPT on the mean waiting-time only is conserved also in 𝒮 ′ \mathcal{S}^{\prime} . However, if the MFPT is finite, the dependence on the specific waiting-time distribution disappears in 𝒮 ′ \mathcal{S}^{\prime} when the initial position is sufficiently far-away from the target. While the MFPT emerges to be Galilean invariant with both two-sided and one-sided jump distributions with finite moments, the MFPT is not a Galilean invariant for one-sided jump distribution with power-law tails (one-sided Lévy distributions).

.
While a large number of theoretical results
are still derived both in the framework of the classical Brownian motion

[

,

,

,

,

]

and in the framework of the anomalous diffusion processes
within the diffusing-diffusivity approach

[

]

or Lévy-like motions

[

,

,

,

,
the quantitative experimental analysis of first-passage time distributions
is becoming accessible in biology only recently

[

,

]

thanks to the increasing availability of single-molecule measurements

[
25
,
26
]
.

At the same time,
foundation of statistical mechanics has been recently tested
against the principle of Galilean invariance

[
27
]
and, actually,
within the Zwanzig approach in the Hamiltonian framework,
the notion of thermal equilibrium emerges to be not frame invariant
because the required properties of the random force are
peculiar of a specific reference frame and the noise must acquire
different statistics in a different reference frame

[
27
]
.
The consequence is that present coarse-grain diffusive models
meet Galilean invariance in the phase-space only
- and therefore in a weak sense only -
because the stochastic forcing represented by the noise is indeed
always stated with the same properties
as independent of any reference frame transformation

[
27
]
.

Hence, motivated by the (yet) unavoidable failure
of Galilean invariance in stochastic processes
[
27
]

and the emerging of experimental investigation
of first-passage time statistics in biological systems,
we want to study the effect of a Galilean transformation by a mean drift

[
28
,
29
]

on the measurements of the mean first-passage time (MFPT)
with the aim to provide some indications for
quantifying and eventually for removing this effect.

The continuous-time random walk (CTRW) approach

[
30
,
31
]

emerged to be successful for understanding,
reproducing and explaining the main features of
the anomalous diffusion observed in living systems

[
32
,
33
]
.
In a CTRW model,
at any iteration occurring after a random waiting-time,
the walker’s displacement is an instantaneous jump and then
it is independent of any reference frame transformation,
which, with finite-mean waiting-times,
causes weakly Galilean invariance because the process is local in time;
in opposition to the CTRW with infinite-mean waiting-times
that converges to time-fractional anomalous diffusion
that is nonlocal in time
and it is not Galilean invariant
[
27
]
.

Here, we consider a one-dimensional CTRW model in continuous space
with finite-mean waiting-times
and asymmetric or one-sided distribution of jumps,
which allows for a
finite
MFPT.
We remind that the MFPT of a CTRW with symmetric jumps
is infinite both in continuous

[
34
,
35
]

and in discrete space with nearest-neighbour jumps

[
36
,
37
,
38
]
but, in opposition,
the MFPT of a symmetric diffusion process is finite when
particle stochastically resets to its initial position at a constant rate

[
39
]
.
Within this CTRW modelling setting,
we study the estimation of a
finite
MFPT
by an observer in a reference frame
\mathcal{S}
,
that is co-moving with the target,
and by an observer in a reference frame
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
,
that is in uniform motion with respect to the target
according to a Galilean transformation.

The present research can also be re-phrased as a study of
the MFPT over uniform moving boundaries for a CTRW model,
a similar problem for the CTRW, but with an immobile target only,
was considered by Franke & Majumdar
[
40
]
,
and the case with a constant time-step but
general random-moving boundaries
has been recently analysed for Lévy flights
by Denisov
et al.

[
41
]
.

We found that in
\mathcal{S}

the MFPT results to be dependent on the jump distribution
and on the mean value only of the waiting-times, but
this simple picture emerged in
\mathcal{S}

is actually broken in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

where the estimation depends on the whole jump distribution
and also on the whole distribution
of the waiting-times.

Different estimations of the MFPT emerge according to the
direction of the relative motion between
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and the target.
In this setting,
the averaged relative shift between

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and the target is provided by
the frame velocity, the mean waiting-time and the mean jump-size.
In particular,
for initial position sufficiently far-away from the target,
the finiteness of the MFPT is determined solely by
a negative value of the averaged relative shift.
In this limit,
the two estimations in
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are reconciled
only with an asymmetric random walk when the jump-size distribution
is governed by a power-law distribution towards the target,
which has an infinite mean,
and an arbitrary jump-size distribution in the direction opposite
to the target, which has a finite mean.
In this case, the MFPT is not a Galilean invariant.
We provide also under which conditions the two estimations
of the MFPT in
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are finite but different, namely,
when the MFPT meets the Galilean invariance.
Moreover, we derive the class of jump-size distributions such
that the dependence of the MFPT on the mean waiting-time only is conserved also
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, for any initial position, in analogy with
\mathcal{S}
.
This occurs in the special case of the weighted superposition of
an exponential jump-size distributions towards a departing target
together with a non-null probability to rest and
an arbitrary jump-size distribution with a finite mean in the
direction opposite to the target.

In the following, we first provide notions and notation
in next Section
2

and later we derive and discuss
the conditions for a finite MFPT in the reference frame
\mathcal{S}

and in the reference frame
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

in Section
3
and
4
, respectively.
Conclusions are reported in Section
5
.

2
Notions and notation

Let the two reference frames
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be
represented by
the one-dimensional coordinate systems
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
respectively,
and then be related by
the Galilean transformation with constant velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

x^{\prime}=x+v\,t\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,

(1)

where
t
is the time-lag.

A CTRW model for the walker’s position
after
N\in\mathbb{N}
iterations, and here denoted by
X_{t_{N}}
,
is defined in the two reference frames

\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
as follows

\displaystyle{\rm in}\,\,\mathcal{S}:\quad X_{t_{N}}=x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta x_{i}\,,\quad t_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta t_{i}\,,

(2)

{\rm in}\,\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\quad X^{\prime}_{t_{N}}=X_{t_{N}}+v\,t_{N}=x_{0}+v\,t_{N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta x_{i}\,,

(3)

with initial data

t_{0}=0
,
X_{0}=x_{0}
,
X^{\prime}_{0}=X_{0}
, and
where
\{\delta x_{i}\}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{\delta t_{i}\}\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}

are the jumps and the waiting-times that are independently drawn
at each iteration from the probability density function (PDF)

q(\xi)
and
\psi(\tau)
, respectively.
In general, density
q(\xi)
can be also asymmetrical.
Then, peculiar characteristic of the CTRW is that at the
observation time
t=t_{N}
each realisation of the
walker’s trajectory has performed a corresponding
number
N
of jumps.
Moreover, let the symbols
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
be the probability measure
in the frame
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, respectively, then it holds

\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\leq x)=\mathbb{P}^{\prime}(X^{\prime}_{t}\leq x^{\prime})\,.

(4)

By definition we have that

\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\leq x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}P(z;t)\,dz\,,

(5)

where
P(x;t)
is the PDF estimated in
\mathcal{S}

for a walker to be in
x
at time
t
, such that

\displaystyle{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P(x;t)dx=1}
and
P(x;t)>0

for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,+\infty)

with initial datum
P(x;0)=P_{0}(x)
.
The same definition holds for
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
:

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}(X^{\prime}_{t}\leq x^{\prime})=\int_{-\infty}^{x^{\prime}}P^{\prime}(y;t)\,dy\,.

(6)

Hence, equality (
4
) reads

\int_{-\infty}^{x}P(z;t)\,dz=\int_{-\infty}^{x^{\prime}}P^{\prime}(y;t)\,dy\,.

(7)

Equality (
7
) is important for
establishing the relation between PDFs in
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

and then for the comparison between MFPT estimations
presented in the following.
In particular,
by applying transformation (
1
) to (
7
),
it emerges that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{x}P(z;t)\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{x^{\prime}}P^{\prime}(y;t)\,dy

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{x+vt}P^{\prime}(y;t)\,dy=\int_{-\infty}^{x}P^{\prime}(z+vt;t)\,dz\,,

and then

P(x;t)=P^{\prime}(x+vt;t)\,\,\text{and}\,\,P(x-vt;t)=P^{\prime}(x;t)\,,

(9)

that takes into account Galilean transformation (
1
)
for particles’ density.

3
Analysis in the co-moving reference frame
\mathcal{S}

3.1
Derivation of the MFPT formula

Within the CTRW approach for particle diffusion,
the determination of the walker’s PDF
is a two-step procedure
[
42
]

that in
\mathcal{S}
is given by the formulae

\Omega(x;t)=P_{0}(x)\psi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q(x-\xi)\Omega(\xi;\tau)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

(10a)

P(x;t)=P_{0}(x)\Psi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\Psi(t-\tau)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q(x-\xi)\Omega(\xi;\tau)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

(10b)

where
\Omega(x;t)
is the outgoing normalised flow of
particles that counts the amount of walkers are leaving
the point
x
per unit of time at the instant
t
,
and
\displaystyle{\Psi(t)=1-\int_{0}^{t}\psi(\tau)d\tau}
is
the probability for a walker to stay in the same location
until time
t
.

By applying Fourier and Laplace transformations to
(
10a
) and to (
10b
), and
by solving with respect to
P(x;t)
, we obtain

\widehat{\widetilde{P}}(\kappa;\lambda)=\frac{\widehat{P}_{0}(\kappa)\widetilde{\Psi}(\lambda)}{1-\widehat{q}(\kappa)\widetilde{\psi}(\lambda)}\,,

(11)

that is the Montroll–Weiss equation

[
42
,
43
]
,
where
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
is the wavenumber parameter
in the Fourier domain and

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is the frequency parameter in the Laplace domain.
In the physical domain, it holds

P(x;t)=\Psi(t)P_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q(x-\xi)P(\xi;\tau)\,d\xi d\tau\,.

(12)

The walker’s PDF
P(x;t)
can be stated in terms of the propagator, namely
of the conditional probability density
p(x;t|x_{0})
, i.e.,

P(x;t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}p(x;t|x_{0})P_{0}(x_{0})\,dx_{0}\,,

(13)

and
from (
12
) we obtain

p(x;t|x_{0})=\Psi(t)\delta(x-x_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q(x-\xi)p(\xi;\tau|x_{0})\,d\xi d\tau\,,

(14)

where spatial homogeneity of the process has been assumed,
i.e.,
p(x;t|x_{0})=p(x-x_{0};t|0)
.

In order to estimate the MFPT for the observer in
\mathcal{S}
,
we locate an absorbing boundary at the target position
x=x_{T}

and we compute the survival probability of the walker,
i.e., the probability for the walker to be not beyond the target.
Without loss in generality,
since the process is assumed spatially homogeneous,
we set
x_{0}>x_{T}=0
and we look for the equation
governing the walker’s PDF laying in
x\geq 0
.
In particular, we stress that we analyse the problem at the level
of the equation and not at the level of the solution,
thus the method of imagines - or similar - does not apply
but proper initial and boundary conditions are needed.
In our notation, due to the absorbing events,
we look for the propagator provided only by the non-absorbed particles
that means provided by those walker’s realisations
that at the previous iteration were in the positive semi-axis by taking
advantage of the renewal nature of the CTRW.
With reference to the integral over
\xi
in (
14
),
by using the properties of convolution integrals,
we first replace the integrand
q(x-\xi)p(\xi;\tau|x_{0})
with

q(\xi)p(x-\xi;\tau|x_{0})
and
later we introduce the jump-size with respect to the starting point
by the shifting
\xi\to\xi-x_{0}
such that the integrand
turns out to be
q(\xi-x_{0})p(x;\tau|\xi)
.
Moreover,
in analogy with a killing process,
we remove the contribution from the sourcing points
\xi

that are beyond the target by taking the integral interval
over the positive semi-axis only. In formulae:

\displaystyle p_{\text{abs}}(x;t|x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(t)p_{\text{abs}}(x;0|x_{0})+

(15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}q(\xi-x_{0})p_{\text{abs}}(x;\tau|\xi)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

with
(x,t)\in[0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
,

p_{\text{abs}}(x;0|x_{0})=\delta(x-x_{0})
and

p_{\text{abs}}(0;t|x_{0})=0
.
The effect of the absorbing boundary is embodied by the non-normalisation
of
p_{\text{abs}}(x;t|x_{0})
.

Therefore,
the survival probability - hereinafter denoted by
\Lambda(x_{0},t)
-
results to be

\displaystyle\Lambda(x_{0},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{\text{abs}}(x;t|x_{0})\,dx

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\!\psi(t-\tau)\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!q(\xi-x_{0})\Lambda(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

that meets the conditions
\Lambda(x_{0},0)=1
and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\Lambda(x_{0},t)=0
.
We highlight that if the waiting-times are all equal and not distributed,
namely
\psi(\tau)=\delta(\tau-\Delta t)
,
with
\Delta t>0
, and

t=n\Delta t
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\Psi(0)=1
and

\displaystyle{\Psi(t\geq\Delta t)=1-\int_{0}^{t}\delta(\tau-\Delta t)\,d\tau=0}
,
then from (
16
)
the equation for the survival probability with discrete time
is recovered, see, e.g.,
[
1
, equation (10)]

and
[
35
, equation (18)]
.
In particular, in the discrete-time case,
the survival probability is governed by
a homogeneous Wiener–Hopf equation.
Moreover, we stress here that the results by
Sparre Andersen
[
44
,
45
]

as well as those by
Pollaczek
[
46
]
and Spitzer

[
47
,
48
,
49
]

for the determination of the survival probability

\Lambda(x_{0},t)
hold for Markovian random walks with discrete time
and that the CTRW
is indeed Markovian if and only if the
waiting-times distribution is exponential

[
50
,
51
]
.

The PDF of the FPT is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}_{x_{0}}(t)=-\frac{\partial\Lambda(x_{0},t)}{\partial t}}

and then the MFPT - hereinafter denoted by
T(x_{0})
-
is here estimated by the following limit

\displaystyle T(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\mathcal{P}_{x_{0}}(t)\,dt=-\int_{0}^{\infty}t\,\frac{\partial\Lambda(x_{0},t)}{\partial t}\,dt

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Lambda(x_{0},t)\,dt=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\widetilde{\Lambda}(x_{0},\lambda)\,,

where
\widetilde{\Lambda}(x_{0},\lambda)
is the Laplace transform of
the survival probability that from (
16
) is determined by the
equation

\widetilde{\Lambda}(x_{0},\lambda)=\widetilde{\Psi}(\lambda)+\widetilde{\psi}(\lambda)\int_{0}^{\infty}q(\xi-x_{0})\widetilde{\Lambda}(\xi,\lambda)\,d\xi\,.

(18)

By definitions, it holds

\widetilde{\Psi}(\lambda)=(1-\widetilde{\psi}(\lambda))/\lambda

and
\widetilde{\psi}(0)=1
so,
by assuming a finite-mean waiting-times distribution, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\langle\tau\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau\,\psi(\tau)\,d\tau<\infty}
,
we have that
\widetilde{\psi}(\lambda)\sim 1-\langle\tau\rangle\lambda+o(\lambda)

and then the MFPT (
17
)
estimated by an observer in co-motion with
the target is

T(x_{0})=\langle\tau\rangle+\int_{0}^{\infty}q(\xi-x_{0})T(\xi)\,d\xi\,,\quad x_{0}>0\,.

(19)

In particular, from (
19
),
we observe that the MFPT as estimated in
\mathcal{S}
depends
on the full distribution of the jumps’ size
but on the mean value only of the waiting-times,
actually,
T(x_{0})
is independent of the specific form of the PDF
of the waiting-times
\psi(\tau)
.

Formula (
19
) is an non-homogeneous Wiener–Hopf integral equation
that admits solution
T(x_{0})=\mathcal{O}({\rm e}^{bx_{0}})
as
x_{0}\to\infty

whenever
q(\xi)=\mathcal{O}({\rm e}^{-a|\xi|})
as
\xi\to\infty
with

a>b
given that
\langle\tau\rangle=\mathcal{O}({\rm e}^{bx_{0}})

when
x_{0}\to\infty
, see
[
52
, p. 345]
.
Nevertheless, not all solutions are also MFPT solutions

[
35
]
.

3.2
Two-sided jumps

By remembering that the MFPT for symmetric diffusive processes
is infinite,
a finite MFPT solution can be obtained with
a two-sided asymmetric jump-size distribution with exponential tails that,
by taking into account the above conditions on the existence of the
solution of (
19
), is indeed quite a general case.
In particular, such MFPT solutions can be explicitly derived by taking

[
35
]

q(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-b)\exp(\xi)\,,&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{-}_{0}\,,\\
\\
ab\exp(-a\xi)\,,&\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\,,\end{array}\right.

(20)

with
b\in[0,1]
and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, where the mean value is

\langle\xi\rangle=\left(\frac{1+a}{a}\right)b-1\,,

(21)

and it holds
[
35
]

T(x_{0})=-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle}(1+x_{0})\,,\quad x_{0}\,,\langle\tau\rangle\,,-\langle\xi\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\,.

(22)

We highlight that the MFPT is infinite in some asymmetric cases, too,
whenever
\langle\xi\rangle=0
which corresponds to

b=a/(1+a)
.
Formula (
22
) in the asymptotic limit

x_{0}\gg 1
reduces to the known result

[
34
]
,

T(x_{0})\sim-\frac{x_{0}}{u}\,,\quad u=\frac{\langle\xi\rangle}{\langle\tau\rangle}<0\,,

(23)

for a starting point far-away enough from the boundary.

We report that the emergence of Wiener–Hopf equations
in boundary problems for diffusive processes is,
as a matter of fact, the proper mathematical scenario, see, e.g.,

[
1
,
18
,
48
,
49
,
53
,
54
,
55
,
56
]
.

The non-universality of the MFPT for starting points near
the boundary has been recently discussed by these authors

[
35
]
.
In fact, if we pass to the spatial-dimensional setting through the length-scale

\ell
we have

x^{{\rm{D}}}_{0}=\ell\,x_{0}
and
\xi^{{\rm{D}}}=\ell\,\xi
such that

\langle\xi^{{\rm{D}}}\rangle=\ell\,\langle\xi\rangle

from which it follows
\ell=\langle\xi_{\rm{D}}\rangle/\langle\xi\rangle
.
Hence, even if the spatial-dimensional processes have the same
mean jump-size
\langle\xi_{\rm{D}}\rangle
,
the length-scale of the systems
\ell
can be in general different.
In fact,
\ell
depends also on
\langle\xi\rangle
(
21
)
that is a function of the specific values of parameters
a
and
b

building-up the jump-size distribution (
20
).
More quantitatively, in the spatial-dimensional setting,
formula (
22
) reads

T(x_{0})=-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle}\left[1+\frac{x_{0}^{{\rm{D}}}}{\ell}\right]=-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle}\left[1+\langle\xi\rangle\frac{x_{0}^{{\rm{D}}}}{\langle\xi^{{\rm{D}}}\rangle}\right]\,,

(24)

with
x_{0}^{{\rm{D}}}
,
\langle\tau\rangle
,
-\langle\xi\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
from which it emerges that the universal asymptotic limit (
23
),
that in spatial-dimensional form is

T(x_{0})\sim-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi^{{\rm{D}}}\rangle}\,x_{0}^{{\rm{D}}}\,,

(25)

is obtained when

x_{0}^{{\rm{D}}}\gg\frac{\langle\xi^{{\rm{D}}}\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle}\,,

(26)

that is non-universal in spite of equal spatial-dimensional mean jump-size

\langle\xi^{{\rm{D}}}\rangle
because
\langle\xi\rangle

depends on the values of
a
and
b
that are proper of each
jump-size PDF (
20
).

Summarising,
the length-scale discriminating
between starting-points near and far the boundary is not universal.
The family of asymmetric exponential-distributions
that we have studied (
20
) defines a common length-scale
for a sub-family of PDFs with the same mean (
21
)
for different values of parameters
a
and
b

after which the MFPT is universal for all the processes of the sub-family,
while the MFPT is not universal for starting-points less than such length-scale.

To conclude,
from formula (
22
) it emerges that
when the jump-PDFs
q(\xi)
are two-sided with exponential tails
then the MFPT is finite if
\langle\xi\rangle<0
,
i.e., in the direction of the target.
Thus the asymmetry of the processes does not guarantee a finite MFPT
because those with
\langle\xi\rangle\geq 0
lead indeed
to an infinite MFPT together with symmetric processes.

Similarly, also in the case of
two-sided jump-PDFs with power-law tails
the asymmetry of the processes does not guarantee a finite MFPT.
In fact, when the resulting random walk is a Lévy flight
with stable index

\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
and asymmetry parameter

\beta\in(-1,1)
then the MFPT is finite when

\alpha\in(1,2)

[
19
,
20
]
.
See in Appendix
A
its determination.
In the other asymmetric case, namely
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
together with the symmetric ones, the MFPT is infinite

[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.
It is important to report that
without an absorbing boundary the PDF decreases according
to the following limit behaviours on the left of the starting point:
when
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
\beta=1
the PDF is two-sided
and it has a right power-law tail
x^{-1-\alpha}
when
x\to+\infty
and
a left exponential tail lighter than the Gaussian when
x\to-\infty
.
In these cases the MFPT is finite with an
absorbing boundary at
x_{T}<x_{0}
.
When
\beta=-1
the properties of the tails are exchanged
and then the MFPT is finite when the absorbing boundary is at

x_{T}>x_{0}
.

3.3
One-sided jumps

We consider now one-sided jumps in the direction of the target
and the MFPT emerges to be finite both with a jump PDF
with finite moments and with a power-law jump PDF.
Within this framework, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{0}q(\xi)\,d\xi=1\,,\quad q(\xi)>0\quad{\rm for}\,\,{\rm all}\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{-}_{0}\,,

(27)

and the MFPT results to be governed by the following
Volterra equation of the second kind

T(x_{0})=\langle\tau\rangle+\int_{0}^{x_{0}}q(\xi-x_{0})T(\xi)\,d\xi\,,

(28)

rather than by the non-homogeneous Wiener–Hopf equation (
19
).
From (
28
) we have that

\widetilde{T}(s)=\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{s\,[1-\widetilde{\rho}(s)]}\,,

(29)

where

\widetilde{\rho}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-s\theta}\,\rho(\theta)\,d\theta\,,\quad\text{where}\quad\rho(\theta)=\rho(-\xi)=q(\xi)\,,

(30)

with
-\xi=\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
.
Hence, since it holds
\widetilde{\rho}(s)<1
when
s\neq 0
,
then formula (
29
) can be re-written as

\widetilde{T}(s)=\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\widetilde{\rho}(s)\right]^{n}\,,

(31)

such that by anti-transformation we have

T(x_{0})=\langle\tau\rangle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\rho_{n}(\theta)\,d\theta\,,

(32)

where

\displaystyle{\rho_{n}(\theta)=\int_{0}^{\theta}\rho_{n-1}(\chi)\rho(\theta-\chi)\,d\chi}

with
\rho_{0}(\theta)=\delta(\theta)
.
Formula (
32
) is the unique solution of (
28
)
and it meets all the constraints for actually being a MFPT,
in opposition with the two-sided case governed by (
19
)
where solutions not consistent with a MFPT can emerge

[
35
, Appendix A]
.

If we consider a jump PDF with existing moments such that
in the limit
s\to 0
it holds

\widetilde{\rho}(s)\sim 1+\langle\xi\rangle s
,
with
\langle\xi\rangle<0
, then from (
29
) we obtain

T(x_{0})\sim-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle}\,x_{0}\,,\quad x_{0}\to\infty\,.

(33)

We observe that also in this case with one-sided jump-PDFs
the MFPT depends on the mean value only of the waiting times
but on the whole distribution of the jump-sizes because
when we move to the dimensional case the discussion around formula
(
26
) still applies.

If we consider a one-sided Lévy distribution for the jumps then

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\rho}(s)={\rm e}^{-s^{\alpha}}}
,
with
0<\alpha<1
, and from (
29
) it holds

\displaystyle{\widetilde{T}(s)=\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{s\,[1-{\rm e}^{-s^{\alpha}}]}\,,}

(34)

that in the limit
s\to 0
gives

\widetilde{T}(s)\sim\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{s^{1+\alpha}}\,,\quad s\to 0\,,

(35)

and then in the asymptotic case
x_{0}\to+\infty
it results

T(x_{0})\sim\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\,x_{0}^{\alpha}\,,\quad 0<\alpha<1\,,\quad x_{0}\to+\infty\,,

(36)

that is consistent with literature results, inter alia,

[
20
, formula (56)]
.

4
Analysis in the uniform moving reference frame
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

4.1
Derivation of the MFPT formula

We study now as the same process is described
by an observer in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
Actually, by using the identity (
9
)
for deriving formulae (
10a
) and (
10b
)
for
\Omega^{\prime}(x;t)
and
P^{\prime}(x;t)
,
the corresponding Montroll–Weiss equation (
11
) results to be

\widehat{\widetilde{P^{\prime}}}(\kappa;\lambda)=\frac{\widehat{P}_{0}(\kappa)\widetilde{\Psi}(\lambda-i\kappa v)}{1-\widehat{q}(\kappa)\widetilde{\psi}(\lambda-i\kappa v)}\,,

(37)

see also Reference
[
57
, equation (12)]
,
and in the physical space it holds

P^{\prime}(x;t)=\Psi(t)P_{0}(x-vt)+\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q(x-\xi-v\tau)P^{\prime}(\xi;\tau)\,d\xi d\tau\,.

(38)

By applying again formula (
13
)
in the reference frame
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

where identity (
9
) has been used,
from formula (
38
)
the conditional probability density
p^{\prime}(x;t|x_{0})
is
determined by

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(x,t|x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(t)\delta(x-x_{0}-vt)+

(39)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q(x-\xi-v\tau)p^{\prime}(\xi;\tau|x_{0})\,d\xi d\tau\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(t)\delta(x-x_{0}-vt)+

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}q(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau)p^{\prime}(x;\tau|\xi)\,d\xi d\tau\,.

When an absorbing boundary is located at
x_{T}=0
,
in analogy with (
15
) the walker’s PDF from
(
39
) turns into

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055ptp^{\prime}_{\text{abs}}(x;t|x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(t)\Theta(x_{0}+vt)\delta(x-x_{0}-vt)+

(40)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\Theta(x_{0}+v\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}q(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau)p^{\prime}_{\text{abs}}(x;\tau|\xi)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

where again we have removed the contribution from the sourcing points

\xi
that are beyond the target by taking the integration interval
over the positive semi-axis only and
we have removed also the contribution from those walker’s realisation
that did not jump yet but they moved beyond the target
because of the frame translation
by employing the function
\displaystyle{\Theta(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\max\{0,x\}}
.
Therefore the corresponding survival probability is

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\Lambda^{\prime}(x_{0},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi(t)\Theta(x_{0}+vt)+

(41)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{0}^{t}\psi(t-\tau)\Theta(x_{0}+v\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}q\left(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau\right)\Lambda(\xi,\tau)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

and its Laplace transform

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt\widetilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}(x_{0},\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda t)\Psi(t)\Theta(x_{0}+vt)dt+

(42)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda\tau)\psi(\tau)\Theta(x_{0}+v\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!q\left(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau\right)\widetilde{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\xi,\lambda)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

such that, finally, the MFPT estimated by an observer in
uniform motion with respect to the target is

\displaystyle T^{\prime}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta(x_{0}+vt)\Psi(t)dt+

(43)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\Theta(x_{0}+v\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}q(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau)T^{\prime}(\xi)\,d\xi d\tau\,.

From equation (
43
),
we observe that in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

the MFPT is no more dependent only
on the mean value of the waiting-times as in
\mathcal{S}
, see formula (
19
),
but it depends on its whole distribution
\psi(\tau)

and the related
\Psi(\tau)
.
However, when
v=0
we recover formula (
19
) as expected.

In particular, we distinguish the cases
v<0
and
v\geq 0

that we denote
T^{\prime}_{<}(x_{0})
and
T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})
, respectively,
such that (
43
) turns into

T^{\prime}_{<}(x_{0})=\int_{0}^{x_{0}/|v|}\hskip-8.5359pt\Psi(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{x_{0}/|v|}\hskip-8.5359pt\psi(\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}\hskip-5.69046ptq(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau)T^{\prime}_{<}(\xi)\,d\xi d\tau\,,\quad v<0\,,

(44)

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})=\langle\tau\rangle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}q(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau)T^{\prime}_{>}(\xi)\,d\xi d\tau\,,\quad v\geq 0\,.

(45)

Since it holds

\int_{0}^{x_{0}/|v|}\Psi(\tau)\,d\tau<\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(\tau)\,d\tau=\langle\tau\rangle\,,

(46)

and also

\int_{0}^{x_{0}/|v|}\,\,\psi(\tau)\,d\tau<\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\,d\tau=1\,,

(47)

it results that

T^{\prime}_{<}(x_{0})<T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})\,,\quad v\neq 0\,.

(48)

4.2
Two-sided jumps

We consider also in this case the exponential PDF defined in (
20
).
A solution of equation (
44
) can be obtained
and we have the explicit result

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{<}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\widetilde{\Psi}(|v|s)}{s}-\frac{ab}{a-s}\widetilde{\psi}(|v|s)\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{<}(a)\right]

(49)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(a-s)(1+s)}{(a-s)(1+s)-\widetilde{\psi}(|v|s)[(1-b)(a-s)+ab(1+s)]}\,,

where we observe that the MFPT depends on the whole distribution
of the waiting-times, and not on the mean value only,
and that it is finite and it remains finite even in the
case with
\langle\xi\rangle=0
, i.e.,
b=a/(1+a)
, and
in particular in the case of a symmetric jump-size distribution,
i.e.,
a=1
and
b=1/2
.
In the limit
s\to 0
we have

\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{<}(s)\sim-\frac{1}{s^{2}[-|v|+\langle\xi\rangle/\langle\tau\rangle]}+\frac{b\,\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{<}(a)}{s\langle\tau\rangle[-|v|+\langle\xi\rangle/\langle\tau\rangle]}\,,\quad s\to 0\,,

(50)

from which,
by remembering that it holds
T^{\prime}_{<}(0)=0
and that the inverse Laplace
transformation of
1/s
is a constant,
the asymptotic behaviour is

T^{\prime}_{<}(x_{0})\sim-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle-|v|\langle\tau\rangle}\,x_{0}\,,\quad-|v|\langle\tau\rangle+\langle\xi\rangle<0\,,\quad x_{0}\to\infty\,.

(51)

A solution of equation (
45
) can indeed be obtained
in the case of the asymmetric exponential PDF (
20
)
by assuming

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{0}^{n}\,,

(52)

that from (
45
) gives

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})=-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{v\langle\tau\rangle+\langle\xi\rangle}\,(1+x_{0})\,,\quad v\langle\tau\rangle+\langle\xi\rangle<0\,,

(53)

and in the asymptotic and universal limit tends to

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})\sim-\langle\tau\rangle\frac{x_{0}}{\langle\xi\rangle+v\langle\tau\rangle}\,,\quad\langle\xi\rangle+v\langle\tau\rangle<0\,,\quad x_{0}\gg 1\,,

(54)

that is consistent with the case
v<0
(
51
).

We remark that, in (
51
) and (
54
),
the MFPT depends on the mean value only of the jump-sizes
\langle\xi\rangle

but, when we move to the dimensional case,
they indeed depend on the whole distribution of the jump-sizes in analogy with
the discussion around formula (
26
). Moreover,
also in cases with
\langle\xi\rangle=0
,
and in particular in the symmetric case,
the MFPT is finite with an arriving target because
the condition in (
51
) is always met while
for a departing target the condition in (
54
)
is never met.

Furthermore, we underline the fact that in the asymptotic limit
for a far-away initial position
x_{0}\to+\infty
,
then a far-away length-scale exists that takes into account the
time-scale of the waiting-times in terms of the mean value such that
the universal behaviour of the MFPT is recovered
in both reference frames
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
In particular, in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

the dependence on the whole distribution of the waiting-times
reduces to the dependence on the mean value only in analogy with
\mathcal{S}
.

We observe that by comparing formula (
23
) in
\mathcal{S}
and
formulae (
51
) and (
54
) in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
the MFPT is
Galilean invariant for two-sided jumps distribution with existing moments
and far-away initial position.

If the jump distribution decays with a power-law,
then a finite MFPT is obtained when the condition

\langle\xi\rangle+v\langle\tau\rangle<0
is met.
In particular,
the MFPT is finite in both cases
v<0
and
v>0

whenever the jump-distribution is skewed
in the direction of the target such that
\langle\xi\rangle\to-\infty
.
This implies that the tail in the direction of the target
decreases according to
|x|^{-(\alpha+1)}
with
0<\alpha\leq 1

while in the opposite direction it decreases exponentially or
with a power-law
|x|^{-(\alpha^{\prime}+1)}
with
1<\alpha^{\prime}<2
.
In analogy with the one-sided case studied in the next subsection,
we have that for
x_{0}\to+\infty

the MFPT goes like
x_{0}^{\alpha}
with
0<\alpha\leq 1

(where the extremal value
\alpha=1
is included
for taking into account when the jumps
in the direction of the target are distributed according to the left-side
of the Cauchy distribution, which leads to an infinite negative mean value).
A noteworthy two-sided special case is provided by symmetric
Lévy densities with
1<\alpha<2
when
v<0
.
In fact, in this case we have
\langle\xi\rangle=0
and
a finite MFPT proportional to
x_{0}
follows
from the uniform motion of the target.

4.3
One-sided jumps

In order to guarantee a finite MFPT
also in the case with power-law jump distribution,
in analogy with the analysis in
\mathcal{S}
, see formula (
28
), we assume
one-sided jumps and the equation (
43
) for
T^{\prime}(x_{0})
becomes

\displaystyle T^{\prime}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\Psi\left(\tau\right)\Theta\left(v\tau+x_{0}\right)d\tau

(55)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\psi\left(\tau\right)\Theta\left(v\tau+x_{0}\right)\int_{0}^{x_{0}+v\tau}\!\!q\left(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau\right)T^{\prime}\left(\xi\right)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

such that formulae (
44
) and (
45
)
now read

T^{\prime}_{<}\left(x_{0}\right)=\int_{0}^{x_{0}/|v|}\hskip-14.22636pt\Psi\left(\tau\right)d\tau+\int_{0}^{x_{0}/|v|}\hskip-14.22636pt\psi\left(\tau\right)\int_{0}^{x_{0}-|v|\tau}\hskip-14.22636ptq\left(\xi-x_{0}+|v|\tau\right)T^{\prime}_{<}(\xi)d\xi d\tau\,,\,v<0\,,

(56)

T^{\prime}_{>}\left(x_{0}\right)=\langle\tau\rangle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\tau\right)\int_{0}^{x_{0}+v\tau}q\left(\xi-x_{0}-v\tau\right)T^{\prime}_{>}\left(\xi\right)d\xi d\tau\,,\quad v\geq 0\,.

(57)

Therefore, from the comparison against (
28
),
we observe that the MFPT estimated by an observer in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

requires the knowledge of the whole waiting-times PDF
\psi(\tau)
in
opposition to the estimation by an observer in
\mathcal{S}
.
In particular,

T^{\prime}(x_{0})=T^{\prime}_{<}\left(x_{0}\right)=T^{\prime}_{>}\left(x_{0}\right)=\langle\tau\rangle+\int_{0}^{x_{0}}q\left(\xi-x_{0}\right)T^{\prime}\left(\xi\right)d\xi d\tau\,,\quad{\rm for}\quad v=0\,,

(58)

that is equal to the corresponding formula (
28
)
in
\mathcal{S}
, when
x_{0}>0
, as it is expected.

4.3.1
One-sided jumps with arrival target:
v<0

In the case of an arrival target, namely the case when
v<0
,
we can derive an explicit solution of
T_{<}\left(x_{0}\right)

from formula (
56
) for a one-sided jump PDF in analogy
with (
32
).
In fact, by applying Laplace transformation to (
56
)
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{<}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Psi}\left(s|v|\right)}{s\,\left[1-\widetilde{\psi}(s|v|)\widetilde{\rho}(s)\right]}

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Psi}\left(s|v|\right)}{s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\widetilde{\psi}\left(s|v|\right)\widetilde{\rho}(s)\right]^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{\Psi}_{n}\left(s|v|\right)\widetilde{\rho}_{n}(s)\,,

and then

T^{\prime}_{<}(x_{0})=\frac{1}{|v|}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\int_{0}^{x_{0}-z}\Psi_{n}\left(\frac{y}{|v|}\right)\rho_{n}(x_{0}-z-y)\,dydz\,,

(60)

where
\rho(-\xi)=q(\xi)
and
\rho_{n}(\xi)
are
the same as in (
32
), and

\Psi_{n}(\tau)
is the probability of
n
jumps in
[0,\tau]
,
i.e.,
\displaystyle{\Psi_{n}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau}\Psi_{n-1}(\tau-\chi)\psi(\chi)d\chi}

with
\Psi_{0}(\tau)=\Psi(\tau)
.

By comparing (
60
) and (
32
),
we obtain that the MFPT in the reference frame
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}

depends on the whole waiting-times PDF
\psi(\tau)
,
more precisely on the probability
\Psi(\tau)
,
in opposition to the MFPT in the reference frame
\mathcal{S}
that
depends on the mean value only of the waiting-times.
We stress that when the target is arriving,
i.e.,
v<0
,
there is not any special case of the jump PDF
such that the MFPT can be determined by the mean waiting-times only
rather than by the whole PDF
\psi(\tau)
in analogy with the cases
in
\mathcal{S}
and, as we will see in the next section,
in the case with departing target, i.e.,
v>0
,
and one-sided jumps.

If we consider a jump PDF with existing moments such that in the
limit
s\to 0
it holds
\widetilde{\rho}(s)\sim 1+\langle\xi\rangle s
,
with
\langle\xi\rangle<0
, then from the first line of (
59
)
we obtain

T^{\prime}_{<}(x_{0})\sim-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle-|v|\langle\tau\rangle}\,x_{0}\,,\quad x_{0}\to\infty\,.

(61)

If, if in analogy with §
3.3
,
we consider also a one-sided Lévy distribution
for the jumps then
\widetilde{\rho}(s)={\rm e}^{-s^{\alpha}}
,
with
0<\alpha<1
, then from the first line of (
59
) it holds

\displaystyle{\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{<}(s)=\frac{\widetilde{\Psi}(|v|s)}{s\,[1-\widetilde{\psi}(|v|s)\,{\rm e}^{-s^{\alpha}}]}\,,}

(62)

that in limit
s\to 0
gives

\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{<}(s)\sim\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{s^{1+\alpha}}\,,\quad s\to 0\,,

(63)

where we have used the fact that

\widetilde{\Psi}(|v|s)=(1-\widetilde{\psi}(|v|s))/(|v|s)
and
the limit behaviour

\widetilde{\psi}(|v|s)\sim 1-|v|\langle\tau\rangle s

for
s\to 0

such that in the asymptotic case
x_{0}\to+\infty
it results

T^{\prime}_{<}(x_{0})\sim\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\,x_{0}^{\alpha}\,,\quad 0<\alpha<1\,,\quad x_{0}\to+\infty\,,

(64)

that is equal to formula (
36
) in
\mathcal{S}
for
the similar case of one-sided jumps in the direction of the absorbing boundary.

By comparing (
61
) against (
33
) and
(
64
) against (
36
)
it follows that, when the target is arriving, i.e.,
v<0
,
the MFPT is a Galilean invariant for one-sided jumps
with existing moments and such invariance is indeed not met
for one-sided jumps with power-law distribution.

4.3.2
One-sided jumps with departing target:
v>0

In the case of a departing target, namely the case when
v>0
,
we can not use the technique of the Laplace transform.
Therefore, first set formula (
57
) for a one-sided jump PDF in analogy
with (
32
), i.e.,

T^{\prime}_{>}\left(x_{0}\right)=\langle\tau\rangle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\tau\right)\int_{0}^{x_{0}+v\tau}\rho\left(x_{0}+v\tau-\xi\right)T^{\prime}_{>}\left(\xi\right)\,d\xi d\tau\,,

(65)

and later we split the problem as follows

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{>}\left(x_{0}\right)=\langle\tau\rangle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\tau\right)h(x_{0}+v\tau)\,d\tau\,,

(66a)

\displaystyle h(y)=\int_{0}^{y}\rho\left(y-\xi\right)T^{\prime}_{>}\left(\xi\right)\,d\xi\,.

(66b)

In this case we can not derive the explicit form of
T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})

in analogy with (
32
) and (
60
).
But, we can study when the MFPT
T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})
depends
on the mean value only of the waiting-times and not on the
whole PDF
\psi(\tau)
, see Appendix
B
for the detailed
study. As we have already seen, this is analog to study the asymptotic
behaviour when
x_{0}\to+\infty
.
From (
66a
), we observe that

T^{\prime}_{>}\left(x_{0}\right)
depends
on the mean value only of the waiting-times if

h(y)=a_{3}y+a_{2}\,,

(67)

where
a_{3}
and
a_{2}
are constants to be determined
(see, Appendix
B
)
and from definition (
66b
) we have that
h(y)\geq 0
with
y\geq 0
.
Moreover,
if we disregard the possibility for the walker to stay in the actual position
we have that
h(0)=0

and therefore it follows that
a_{3}>0
and
a_{2}=0
.
Otherwise, if the walker may stay in the actual position with
non null probability then

a_{2}=-c/(\langle\xi\rangle/\langle\tau\rangle+v)\neq 0

(see notation and formula (
B.19
) in Appendix
B
).

By plugging (
67
) into (
66a
) then

T^{\prime}_{>}\left(x_{0}\right)=a_{3}x_{0}+(1+a_{3}v)\langle\tau\rangle\,,

(68)

and the Laplace transform reads

\widetilde{T}^{\prime}_{>}\left(s\right)=\frac{a_{3}+(1+a_{3}v)\langle\tau\rangle\,s}{s^{2}}\,.

(69)

To conclude, from equalling the Laplace transforms of
formulae (
66b
) and (
67
) and by using (
69
)
we have that
\widetilde{\rho}(s)
emerges to be

\widetilde{\rho}(s)=\frac{a_{3}}{a_{3}+\left(1+a_{3}v\right)\langle\tau\rangle\,s}\,.

(70)

By remembering the setting stated in (
30
),
parameter
a_{3}
is determined by fixing a finite-mean jump PDF
through the formula

\displaystyle{d\widetilde{\rho}(s)/ds|_{s=0}=-\langle\theta\rangle=\langle\xi\rangle<0}

which provides

a_{3}=-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle+v\langle\tau\rangle}>0\,,\quad\langle\xi\rangle+v\langle\tau\rangle<0\,,\quad\langle\xi\rangle<0\,,

(71)

that inserted into (
68
) gives

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})\sim-\frac{\langle\tau\rangle}{\langle\xi\rangle+v\langle\tau\rangle}\,x_{0}\,,\quad x_{0}\gg-\langle\xi\rangle\,.

(72)

On the other side, if we insert (
71
) into (
70
)
we obtain

\widetilde{\rho}(s)=\frac{1}{1-\langle\xi\rangle\,s}\,,

(73)

and, by remembering the notation setting (
30
)
with
\theta=-\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
such that

\langle\theta\rangle=-\langle\xi\rangle
,
the jump PDF takes the exponential form

\rho(\theta)=\frac{1}{\langle\theta\rangle}\,{\rm e}^{-\theta/\langle\theta\rangle}=-\frac{1}{\langle\xi\rangle}\,{\rm e}^{-\xi/\langle\xi\rangle}=q(\xi)\,,\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\,,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{-}_{0}\,.

(74)

Hence, an exponential distribution of the jump-lengths
allows for an estimation of the MFPT that is
dependent on the mean-value only of the waiting-times,
and it is independent of the whole distribution
\psi(\tau)
,
in analogy with the estimations in the reference frame
\mathcal{S}
.

In the case of a one-sided power-law distribution of jump-sizes,
i.e.,
\widetilde{\rho}(s)={\rm e}^{-s^{\alpha}}
,
the estimation of the MFPT can be derived as follows.
Motivated by the case analysed in §
4.3.1
,
we assume that
T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})\sim A_{1}\,x_{0}^{\alpha}
, with

A_{1}\propto\langle\tau\rangle>0
,
and then from the Laplace transform of (

we have

\widetilde{h}(s)\sim A_{1}\,\frac{1-A_{0}\,s^{\alpha}}{s^{\alpha+1}}\,,\quad s\to 0\,,

where
A_{0}>0
is a constant to be determined.
From (
75
) we have that

h(y)\sim A_{1}\,y^{\alpha}-A_{1}A_{0}\,,\quad y\to+\infty\,,

(76)

and if this is plugged in (
66a
) we obtain

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})\sim\langle\tau\rangle+A_{1}x_{0}^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\left[1+\frac{v\tau}{x_{0}}\right]^{\alpha}\,d\tau-A_{1}A_{0}\,.

(77)

We introduce now the parameter
\tau_{*}
such that

v\tau/x_{0}\ll 1
when
\tau<\tau_{*}
and thus

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\langle\tau\rangle+A_{1}x_{0}^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\tau_{*}}\psi(\tau)\left[1+\alpha\frac{v\tau}{x_{0}}\right]\,d\tau

(78)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+A_{1}x_{0}^{\alpha}\int_{\tau_{*}}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\left[1+\frac{v\tau}{x_{0}}\right]^{\alpha}\,d\tau-A_{1}A_{0}\,.

Since
\tau_{*}\to+\infty
when

then the contribution of the second integral can be neglected
and we finally get

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})\sim\langle\tau\rangle+A_{1}B_{1}\,x_{0}^{\alpha}+A_{1}B_{0}\alpha v\,x_{0}^{\alpha-1}-A_{1}A_{0}\,,

(79)

where
\displaystyle{B_{1}=\int_{0}^{\tau_{*}}\psi(\tau)d\tau\sim\mathcal{O}(1)}
and

\displaystyle{B_{0}=\int_{0}^{\tau_{*}}\tau\psi(\tau)d\tau\sim\mathcal{O}(\langle\tau\rangle)}
.
To conclude,
by setting
\langle\tau\rangle\propto A_{1}A_{0}
we have

A_{0}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
such that

T^{\prime}_{>}(x_{0})\sim\langle\tau\rangle\,x_{0}^{\alpha}\,,\quad x_{0}\to+\infty\,,

(80)

that was the assumed estimation of the MFPT.

By comparing (
72
) against (
33
) and
(
80
) against (
36
)
it follows that, when the target is departing, i.e.,
v>0
,
the MFPT is a Galilean invariant for one-sided jumps
with existing moments but such invariance is indeed broken
for one-sided jumps with a power-law distribution.

5
Summary and Conclusion

In the view of the failure of present
diffusive models regarding the Galilean invariance

[
27
]
,
we have analysed such failure with respect to the MFPT
in the case of the CTRW approach

[
30
,
31
]
,
that
is one of the most suitable stochastic processes for
studying diffusion in biological systems

[
32
,
33
]

where the MFPT finds one of the most important applications

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

The main result is that, for an observer co-moving with the target,
the MFPT does not depend on the specific waiting-times distribution
except for its mean value while,
for an observer in uniform motion with respect the target,
the specific distribution of the waiting-times plays a role.
However, there exists a length-scale determined by the
time-scale as well that defines a far-away initial position such
that the universal behaviour is observed in both reference frames
and the Galilean invariance is met for the MFPT when it exists.
The estimations in the two reference frames are reconciled
with respect to the independence of the whole waiting-times
distribution, only when the relative velocity between
the target and the observer is positive and
the (two-sided) jump-distribution in the moving reference frame is
of a special form combining a one-sided exponential
and a delta-distribution
together with an arbitrary jump-size distribution with finite mean
in the opposite direction with respect to the target
(see the derivation in Appendix
B
,
in particular the general definition of the jump-distribution
(
B
), its setting
in (
B.6
) and (
B.7
)
and finally formula (
B.21
)).

Summarising our results we found that:

In the reference frame
\mathcal{S}
co-moving with a target,
the MFPT depends on the jump PDF and on the mean value only
of the waiting-times:
this occurs with both two-side and one-side jumps’ distribution.
When a jump PDF with one-sided jumps in the direction of the target
is considered, the MFPT emerges to be finite also in the case of a
jump PDF with a power-law tail, namely a one-side Lévy density,
and it grows with a sublinear law in
opposition to the linear law of the case with an exponential jump PDF.
When the two-sided jump PDF decreases with exponential tails
then the MFPT is finite if the finite mean jump-size
is negative, as well as in the case with power-law tails for
which the mean jump-size is negative but infinite.
However also in this case the MFPT can turn into infinite
if the mean jump-size is non-negative, as it occur with
an asymmetric or symmetric jump PDF.

In the reference frame
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, that moves with a uniform motion with
respect to the target through a Galilean transformation,
the MFPT depends on both the PDFs of jump-sizes and waiting-times
and in both two-sided and one-sided case.
Nevertheless, for initial positions sufficiently far-away from the
target, the dependence on the exact waiting-times distribution
disappears and only the dependence on the mean waiting-time remains.
For an arriving target,
the MFPT is finite even when the mean jump-size is zero,
which includes the case of a symmetric jump distribution,
because condition (
51
) is always met.
On the contrary, for a departing target the corresponding condition (
54
)
is never met when the mean jump-size is zero.
For an observer that is moving in both directions with respect to the target,
it emerges that the MFPT is finite
only in the case of an asymmetric jumps-size distribution with finite mean
and if the averaged relative shift between the observer
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and the target is negative.
In this setting the MFPT meets Galilean invariance.
In the case of power-law tails, the MFPT is finite
whenever the jump-distribution is skewed: which implies
a condition on the decaying of the tail in the direction of the
target and also in the opposite direction.
However, a noteworthy special case with finite MFPT emerges
when a symmetric jump distribution with
1<\alpha<2

is consider with arrival target,
because in this setting the MFPT is determined by the uniform
motion of the reference frame.
Moreover, we report that in the one-sided case with
power-law distributed jumps in the direction of the target,
the MFPT does not differ from the MFPT for a co-moving
observer that is characterised through the same power-law distribution of jumps
towards the target.
This fact highlights that the MFPT of a CTRW model is not
a Galilean invariant.

In a long perspective,
under the assumption that the CTRW is the proper approach
for modelling a phenomenon, see, e.g.,

[
58
,
59
,
60
,
61
]
,
this theoretical result can be understood as a method for an
indirect measurement of the waiting-times distribution,
in opposition to existing studies on the MFPT of a CTRW
in a co-moving reference frame
\mathcal{S}
,
see, e.g.,
[
35
]
,
that allow indeed only for the measurement of the mean waiting-time.

Appendix A
Finite MFPT for Lévy flights

The MFPT for Lévy flights is finite whenever it holds

[

,
mutatis mutandis
from formulae (46) and (47)]

\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}<\arctan\left\{\beta\tan\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}\right\}\,,\quad\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)\,,\quad\beta\in(-1,1)\,.

When

therefore condition (
A.1

When

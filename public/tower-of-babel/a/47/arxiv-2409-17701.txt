On isometric universality of spaces of metrics
Yoshito Ishiki and Katsuhisa Koshino

Abstract
. A metric space ( M , d ) (M,d) is said to be universal for a class of metric spaces if all metric spaces in the class can be isometrically embedded into ( M , d ) (M,d) . In this paper, for a metrizable space Z Z possessing abundant subspaces, we first prove that the space of bounded metrics on Z Z is universal for all bounded metric spaces (with restricted cardinality). Next, in contrast, we show that if Z Z is an infinite discrete space, then the space of metrics on Z Z is universal for all separable metric spaces. As a corollary of our results, if Z Z is non-compact, or uncountable and compact, then the space of metrics on Z Z is universal for all compact metric spaces. In addition, if Z Z is compact and countable, then there exists a compact metric space that can not be isometrically embedded into the space of metrics on Z Z .

Backgrounds

For a metrizable space

X
,

The continuity of a

1.2.
Main results

In this paper,

1.2.1.
Spaces possessing abundant subspaces

Throughout this paper,
we often use the

The symbol

\omega_{0}

stands for the first infinite
ordinal,
which is equal to

\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as a set.
The discrete topology of

\omega_{0}
is the same to
the order topology on

\omega_{0}
.
Moreover,
for two ordinals
\alpha,\beta
,

\alpha\in\beta
.
In this paper,
we will deal with
both the discrete topology and

\kappa
.
We will
clarify which topology is being used

that no confusion can arise.

Let

\kappa

be a

We denote by

\mathscr{S}_{\kappa}

the class of

of weight

\kappa
.
For example,

\mathscr{S}_{\aleph_{0}}=\mathscr{S}
.
For a class

\mathscr{C}

of
metric spaces,
we denote by

\mathrm{B}(\mathscr{C})

the all bounded metric spaces
belonging to

\mathscr{C}
.
For example,
the class

\mathrm{B}(\mathscr{S})

consists of
all separable bounded metric spaces.

For two topological spaces
X
and
Y
,
we denote by

X\oplus Y

the topological sum of
X
and
Y
.
Let
\{pt\}

denote
the one-point space.

The next theorem is our first result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Z

be a metrizable space,
and
let

(X,d)

be a
metric space such that:

(U1)

there exists
a metric space

(Y,D)
containing
X

as a subspace
such that

D|_{X^{2}}=d
,
and there exists
a closed
topological embedding from

Y\oplus\{pt\}

to

Z
.

Then
there
exists an isometric embedding

J\colon(X,d)\to(\mathrm{Met}(Z),\metdis_{Z})
.
Moreover, if
d
is bounded,
we can choose
J
so that

J
is a map from
(X,d)
into

\mathrm{BMet}(Z)
.

As our second result,
Theorem

1.1

yields
the following theorem
stating that
if a metric space

contains abundant spaces as subspaces,
then

\mathrm{Met}(Z)

is universal.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

\mathscr{C}

be a class of
metrizable spaces and

Z

be a metrizable space.
Assume that the following condition
is satisfied:

(2)

for each

(X,d)\in\mathscr{C}
,
there exists
a metric space

(Y,D)

containing
X

as a subspace
such that

D|_{X^{2}}=d
,

Y\oplus\{pt\}

.

Then

(\mathrm{Met}(Z),\metdis_{Z})

,
and

(\mathrm{BMet}(Z),\metdis_{Z})

\mathrm{B}(\mathscr{C})
.

We denote by
\mathbf{Q}

(resp.
\Gamma
)
the Hilbert cube,

(resp. the Cantor set, i.e., the countable power of

).
Namely,

\mathbf{Q}=[0,1]^{\aleph_{0}}

\Gamma=\{0,1\}^{\aleph_{0}}
.
For each
infinite cardinal

\kappa
,
we also denote
by

\ell^{2}(\kappa)

the Hilbert space of weight

(see
[
35
, p.16]
).
Let

be the countable power of

.
Namely,

,

We denote by

,

Theorem 1.3
.

The following statements are true:

(1)

For every infinite cardinal

\kappa
,

(\mathrm{Met}(\ell^{2}(\kappa)),\metdis_{\ell^{2}(\kappa)})

.

There exists

Due to the same argument as Theorem

.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space,

contains

1.2.2.
Discrete spaces

In the theorems
explained above,
we focus on a space

that
contains

In the contrast,

For a set

Theorem 1.5

Let

(1)

(2)

Corollary 1.6
.

Let

\kappa

Assume that

(1)

the space

(2)

has

,

Then

(\mathrm{Met}(Z),\metdis_{Z})

is universal for

,

(\mathrm{BMet}(Z),\metdis_{Z})

for

\mathrm{B}(\mathscr{S}_{\kappa})
.

Concentrating on
the universality for
separable or compact metric spaces,
we summarize

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
X
be an infinite metric space.

(1)

If

X

is

(\mathrm{Met}(X),\metdis_{X})

is
universal
for

\mathscr{T}
.

(2)

If

(\mathrm{Met}(X),\metdis_{X})

is universal for

.

As a consequence,

Case of compact and countable spaces

Based on

Let

Namely,

Let

2.1.
Basic facts on spaces of metrics

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

X
be a
metrizable
space.
Then
we have

\mathrm{CPM}(X)+\mathrm{Met}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{Met}(X).

Namely,
if

d\in\mathrm{CPM}(X)
and

e\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
then

d+e\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
Moreover,
if both
d
and
e
are bounded,
the so is

d+e
.

Proof.

As is easily observed,

d+e

is a metric on

X
.
Define
a
map

f\colon(X,d+e)\to(X,e)

by

f(x)=x
.
Since

d+e
is continuous on

X\times X
,
all open balls of

(X,d+e)

are
also
open in

X
.
Thus
the map

f

is an open map.
By

e(f(x),f(y))\leq(d+e)(x,y)

for all

x,y\in X
,
the map

f

is

1
-Lipschitz,
in particular, it is continuous.
This implies that

f

is a homeomorphism.
Hence

d+e\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let

\mathscr{C}

be a
class of metric spaces.
If

X
is a
metrizable space,
and
the space
\mathrm{CPM}(X)

(resp.
\mathrm{BCPM}(X)
)
is
universal for

\mathscr{C}
,
then
so is
the space

\mathrm{Met}(X)

(resp.
\mathrm{BMet}(X)
).

Proof.

We will show that
for every

(Z,w)\in\mathscr{C}
,
there exists
an isometric embedding

f\colon Z\to\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
Using the assumption,
we obtain
an isometric embedding

g\colon(Z,w)\to(\mathrm{CPM}(X),\metdis_{X})
.
Take a metric

d

in

\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
and define a map

f\colon Z\to\mathrm{CPM}(X)

by

f(z)=g(z)+d
.
Lemma
2.1

implies that

f

is
actually
a map into

\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
Since we have

\metdis_{X}(f(x),f(y))=\metdis_{X}(g(x),g(y))
,
we conclude that

f
is an isometric embedding.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let

\mathscr{C}

be a
class of metric spaces,
and

X

be a
metrizable space.
Then
the space

\mathrm{CPM}(X)

(resp.
\mathrm{BCPM}(X)
)
is universal for

\mathscr{C}
if
and only if
so is
\mathrm{Met}(X)

(resp.
\mathrm{BMet}(X)
).

Let
X
and
Y

be a
metrizable spaces,
and

f\colon X\to Y

be a
continuous map.
Then
for each

d\in\mathrm{CPM}(Y)
,
we define a pseudometric

f^{*}d
on
X
by

f^{*}d(x,y)=d(f(x),f(y))
.
We denote by

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{CPM}(Y)\to\mathrm{CPM}(X)

the map defined by

d\mapsto f^{*}d
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

X

and

Y

be
metrizable spaces,
and let

f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous map.
Assume that

f

is surjective.
Then the following statements are true:

(1)

The map

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{CPM}(Y)\to\mathrm{CPM}(X)

is an isometric embedding
such that

f^{*}(\mathrm{BCPM}(Y))\subseteq\mathrm{BCPM}(X)
.

(2)

There exists an isometric embedding

h\colon\mathrm{Met}(Y)\to\mathrm{Met}(X)

such that

h(\mathrm{BMet}(Y))\subseteq\mathrm{BMet}(X)
.

(3)

For a class

\mathscr{C}

of metric spaces,
if
\mathrm{Met}(Y)

(resp.
\mathrm{BMet}(Y)
)
is universal for

\mathscr{C}
,
then so is

\mathrm{Met}(X)

(resp.
\mathrm{BMet}(X)
).

Proof.

First let us verify

(1)
.
Since

f
is surjective,
for every pair

d,e\in\mathrm{CPM}(Y)

we have

\displaystyle\metdis_{X}(f^{*}d,f^{*}e)=\sup_{x,y\in X}|f^{*}d(x,y)-f^{*}e(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x,y\in X}|d(f(x),f(y))-e(f(x),f(y))|

\displaystyle=\sup_{u,v\in Y}|d(u,v)-e(u,v)|=\metdis_{Y}(d,e).

This proves

(1)
.
Next, we show

(2)
.
Take

\rho\in\mathrm{BMet}(X)

and define
a map
h\colon\mathrm{Met}(Y)\to\mathrm{Met}(X)
by

h(d)=f^{*}d+\rho
.
Then the map

h
is as desired.
Statement

(3)
is deduced from
Statement

(2)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space,
and

H

be a dense subset of

X
.
Then the map

r\colon\mathrm{Met}(X)\to\mathrm{Met}(H)

defined by

r(d)=d|_{H^{2}}

is isometric embedding
and

r(\mathrm{BMet}(X))\subseteq\mathrm{BMet}(H)
.

Proof.

For every pair

d,e\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
define a
map

s\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)

by

s(x,y)=|d(x,y)-e(x,y)|
.
Then it is
continuous.
Since
H
is dense in

X
,
we see that

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

Then

Proof.

For each

d_{u}(x,y)=\begin{cases}d(x,y)&\text{if $x,y\in X$;}\\

d(y,u)+1&\text{if $x=pt$ and $y\in X$;}\\

Let us verify that

is an isometric embedding.
Fix any

By the definition of

4.
The countable discrete spaces

The purpose of
this section is
to prove
Theorem

1.5

and
Corollaries

1.6

and

1.7
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a set.
If a map

w\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)

satisfies:

(1)

for
every
pair

x,y\in X
,
we have

d(x,y)=0
if and only if

x=y
;

(2)

for every
pair

x,y\in X
,
we have

w(x,y)=w(y,x)
;

(3)

there exists

L\in(0,\infty)

such that
for every
distinct pair

x,y\in X
,
we have

w(x,y)\in[L,2L]
,

then

w

is a metric and
it generates the discrete topology on

X
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

w

satisfies the triangle inequality.
For every
triple

x,y,z\in X
,
the condition
(3)

implies that

w(x,y)\leq 2L=L+L\leq w(x,z)+w(z,y).

Thus,
the map

w
satisfies the
triangle inequality.
∎

For a set

S
,
we denote by

[S]^{2}

the set

\{\,\{p,q\}\mid p,q\in S,p\neq q\,\}
.
Namely,

[S]^{2}

is the set of all subsets of

S

consisting of exact two points.
Note that if
S
is infinite,
then

\card(S)=\card([S]^{2})
,
where
“
\card
”
means the cadinality.
Let us provide the
proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

For each

t\in[0,\infty)
,
we define a map

R_{t}\colon\mathbb{R}\to[-t,t]

by

R_{t}(x)=\begin{cases}t&\text{if $t\leq x$;}\\
x&\text{if $x\in[-t,t]$;}\\
-t&\text{if $x\leq-t$.}\end{cases}

Note that

R_{t}

is

1
-Lipschitz,
and

R_{t}
is the identity on

[-t,t]
.
Hence,
if

x,y\in[-t,t]
,
then we have

|R_{t}(x)-R_{t}(y)|=|x-y|
.

Take a family

\{S_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

consisting
of mutually disjoint subspaces of

\kappa

such that

\card(S_{i})=\kappa

and

\kappa=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}S_{i}
.
Using

\card([S_{i}]^{2})=\kappa
,
for each

i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
we also take a surjective
map

\tau_{i}\colon[S_{i}]^{2}\to\kappa
.
For each

i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
we define
a map

F_{i}\colon\ell^{\infty}(\kappa)\to\ell^{\infty}([S_{i}]^{2})

by

F_{i}(a)(\{p,q\})=R_{2^{i}}\circ a(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\}))+3\cdot 2^{i}.

Note that for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
we have

F_{i}(a)(\{p,q\})\in\left[2^{i+1},2\cdot 2^{i+1}\right]

for all

a\in\ell^{\infty}(\kappa)

and

\{p,q\}\in[S_{i}]^{2}
.
We next
define a map

G_{i}\colon\ell^{\infty}(\kappa)\to\mathrm{Met}(S_{i})

by

G_{i}(a)(p,q)=\begin{cases}F_{i}(a)(\{p,q\})&\text{if $p\neq q$;}\\
0&\text{if $p=q$.}\end{cases}

Since

F_{i}(a)(\{p,q\})\in\left[2^{i+1},2\cdot 2^{i+1}\right]
,
Lemma

4.1

(
L=2^{i+1}
)
establishes
that
the function

G_{i}(a)

actually
belongs to

\mathrm{Met}(S_{i})
.

Now we define a map

H\colon\ell^{\infty}(\kappa)\to\mathrm{Met}(\kappa)
by

H(a)(p,q)=\begin{cases}G_{i}(a)(p,q)&\text{if $p,q\in S_{i}$;}\\
\max\{2^{i+2},2^{j+2}\}&\text{if $i\neq j$ and
$p\in S_{i}$ and $q\in S_{j}$.}\end{cases}

Note that
each

H(a)

becomes a
metric and
it
belongs to

\mathrm{Met}(\kappa)

since
the diameter

\yodiam_{G_{i}(a)}(S_{i})\leq 2^{i+2}

for all
a\in\ell^{\infty}(\kappa)
and

i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Now let us verify that

H
is an isometric embedding,
i.e.,
we will show

\metdis_{\kappa}(H(a),H(b))=\lVert a-b\lVert_{\ell^{\infty}}

for
arbitrary maps

a,b\in\ell^{\infty}(\kappa)
.
Since

H(a)(p,q)=H(b)(p,q)

for all
p,q
such that
p\in S_{i}

and
q\in S_{j}
for distinct
i
and
j
,
we have

\displaystyle\metdis_{\kappa}(H(a),H(b))=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\sup_{p,q\in S_{i}}|H(a)(p,q)-H(b)(p,q)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\sup_{p,q\in S_{i}}|F_{i}(a)(p,q)-F_{i}(b)(p,q)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\sup_{p,q\in S_{i}}|R_{2^{i}}(a(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\})))-R_{2^{i}}(b(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\})))|.

This means that

(A1)

\displaystyle\metdis_{\kappa}(H(a),H(b))=

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\sup_{p,q\in S_{i}}|R_{2^{i}}(a(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\})))-R_{2^{i}}(b(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\})))|.

For every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
from the fact that

R_{2^{i}}
is

1
-Lipschitz,
it follows that

\displaystyle|R_{2^{i}}(a(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\})))-R_{2^{i}}(b(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\})))|

\displaystyle\leq|a(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\}))-b(\tau_{i}(\{p,q\}))|\leq\lVert a-b\lVert_{\ell^{\infty}}

for every
\{p,q\}\in[S_{i}]^{2}
.
According to the inequality (
A1
),
we conclude that

\metdis_{\kappa}(H(a),H(b))\leq\lVert a-b\lVert_{\ell^{\infty}}.

Next, we will show the opposite inequality.
Since
a
and
b
are bounded functions,
we can take a sufficiently large number

N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

such that

\{a(s),b(s)\}\subseteq[-2^{N},2^{N}]

for all

s\in\kappa
.
Notice that
N
is depending on
a
and
b
.
Then, since
R_{2^{N}}
is the identity on

[-2^{N},2^{N}]
,
for every
\{p,q\}\in[S_{N}]^{2}
,
due to
(
A1
),
we have

\displaystyle|a(\tau_{N}(\{p,q\}))-b(\tau_{N}(\{p,q\}))|

\displaystyle=|R_{2^{N}}(a(\tau_{N}(\{p,q\})))-R_{2^{N}}(b(\tau_{N}(\{p,q\})))|\leq\metdis_{\kappa}(H(a),H(b)).

Since

\tau_{N}

is surjection,
we observe that

\displaystyle\lVert a-b\lVert_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq\metdis_{\kappa}(H(a),H(b)).

Then we conclude that

\metdis_{\kappa}(H(a),H(b))=\lVert a-b\lVert_{\ell^{\infty}}
.
Thus, the space

\ell^{\infty}(\kappa)

is a metric subspace of

\mathrm{Met}(\kappa)
.

2.8
completes the proof of

(1)
in

\kappa

2.8
.

for all

and

(2)

in

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

The proof is similar to
that of
Theorem
1.5
.
Take a compact subset

K

of

c_{0}(\omega_{0})
.
Under the assumption,

X

is
a compact and countably infinite
metrizable space,
and
hence

contains an

\omega_{0}+1
.
Thus

2.6

or

\mathrm{Met}(\omega_{0}+1)

It remains to verify that

can be isometrically embedded into

\mathrm{Met}(\omega_{0}+1)
,
where we regard

\omega_{0}+1
as an ordered topological space,
thus it is homeomorphic to
the one-point compactification of
the
countably infinite discrete space.
We define
the function

L\colon\omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R}

by

L(s)=\inf_{f\in K}f(s)
.
Since

K

is compact,
for each

\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,
we can take a finite

\epsilon
-net

T_{\epsilon}

of

K
.
We also define

h_{\epsilon}\colon\omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R}

by

h_{\epsilon}(s)=\min_{f\in T_{\epsilon}}f(s)
.
Since

T_{\epsilon}

is
a finite

\epsilon
-net,
the map

h_{\epsilon}

belongs to

c_{0}(\omega_{0})

and

\lVert h_{\epsilon}-L\rVert\leq\epsilon
.
Then
we conclude that

L\in c_{0}(\omega_{0})
.
In this setting,
the map

l\in c_{0}(\omega_{0})

l(s)=|L(s)|+2^{-s}

c_{0}(\omega_{0})
.
We may assume that

for all

s\in\omega_{0}

by replacing

K
with

K+l=\{\,f+l\mid f\in K\,\}

if necessary.
Put

.
Since

K
is

we see that

and

U(s)>0

.
Put
S_{k}=\{2k,2k+1\}
.
We then define

G(f)(p,q)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $p=q$;}\\
f(k)&\text{if $p\neq q$ and $p,q\in S_{k}$;}\\
\max\{U(k),U(l)\}&\text{if $p\in S_{k}$, $q\in S_{l}$,and $k\neq l$;}\\
U(k)&\text{if $p=\omega_{0}$, and $q\in S_{k}$;}\\
U(k)&\text{if $p\in S_{k}$, and $q=\omega_{0}$.}\end{cases}

Under this definition,

\mathrm{Met}(\omega_{0}+1)

[

Then,

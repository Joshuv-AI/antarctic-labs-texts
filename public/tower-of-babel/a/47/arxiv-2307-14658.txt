Central Extensions and Cohomology
Rohit Joshi, Steven Spallone

Abstract
. Let G G be a group which is topologically a CW-complex, B ​ G BG a classifying space for G G , and A A a discrete abelian group. To a central extension of G G by A A , we can associate a cohomology class in H 2 ​ ( B ​ G , A ) H^{2}(BG,A) . We prove this association is injective, and bijective in many cases. A homomorphism of such groups lifts to a central extension iff the pullback of the associated cohomology class vanishes.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a discrete abelian group. When
G
is a discrete group, there is a well-known natural correspondence between central extensions of
G
by
A
and the quadratic group cohomology
H^{2}(G,A)
.
From this, one can systematically find cohomological criteria for lifting problems. In this paper we extend this correspondence to the topological group setting, when
G
is topologically a CW-complex, for instance a Lie group.

Let
\mathbb{E}(G,A)
be the set of (equivalence classes of) central extensions of
G
by
A
, and let
BG
be a classifying space for
G
. A construction of Milgram-Steenrod gives a natural map

\alpha_{G}:\mathbb{E}(G,A)\to H^{2}(BG,A).

When
G
is discrete and
A=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
(with
p
prime), the map
\alpha_{G}
agrees with the classical bijection.
Here is our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

The map
\alpha_{G}
is an injection. It is an isomorphism when
G
is connected. When
A=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and
G
is the semidirect product of a discrete group and a connected group, then
\alpha_{G}
is an isomorphism.

Note that orthogonal groups satisfy the latter hypothesis on
G
. From the injectivity of
\alpha_{G}
we deduce the following lifting criterion.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varphi:G^{\prime}\to G
be a homomorphism,
A
a discrete abelian group, and
p:E\to G
an extension of
G
by
A
. Then
\varphi
lifts to
E
iff

\varphi^{*}(\alpha_{G}(E,i,p))=0.

(Here
i:A\to E
is the inclusion.)

For the orthogonal group
G=\Or(V)
of a quadratic space, we describe its three inequivalent double covers, denoted by
\Pin^{\pm}(V)
and
\widetilde{\Or}(V)
, and then describe
\alpha_{G}
for each.
Finally, we give an application of our work to Stiefel-Whitney classes of orthogonal representations.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a topological group which is a CW-complex. An orthogonal representation
\pi:G\to\Or(V)
lifts to

(1)

\widetilde{\Or}(V)
iff
w_{1}(\pi)^{2}=0
.

(2)

\Pin^{+}(V)
iff
w_{2}(\pi)=0
.

(3)

\Pin^{-}(V)
iff
w_{2}(\pi)+w_{1}(\pi)^{2}=0
.

When
\pi
lifts, the set of lifts of
\pi
is acted on simply transitively by the group of continuous linear characters
\chi:G\to\{\pm 1\}
.

The equivalence of
\mathbb{E}(G,A)
and
H^{2}(BG,A)
was observed in the ’70s. A proof is given in
[
Wig73
, Theorem 4]
by comparing two spectral sequences in simplicial sheaf cohomology. We have nonetheless written this manuscript because for a result of this importance, it is useful to have alternate proofs. When
G
is connected, our correspondence essentially agrees with the obstruction class construction in
[
NWW13
]
, according to Remark 7.17 in that paper and Proposition
10.3
below.

The layout of the paper is as follows. After reviewing definitions for topological groups in Section
2
, we examine the category of their central extensions in Section
3
. The theory of (principal)
G
-bundles is reviewed in Section
4
.
In Section
5
we review the theory of classifying spaces, and in particular the Milgram-Steenrod model and its functorial properties. In Section
6
we define a correspondence
\beta_{G}
, rather than
\alpha_{G}
, when
G
is connected by means of covering space theory. We also review basic properties of the Eilenberg-Maclane spaces
K(A,2)
.
A certain square arises from the connecting homomorphisms of these constructions, which we prove
anticommutes
in Section
7
. This is perhaps the least straightforward part of this paper. For disconnected groups, we incorporate the exact sequence for the component group
\pi_{0}(G)
in Section
8
. In Section
9
we use the
K(A,2)
-spaces to define a natural map from the bundles of Section
5
to the quadratic cohomology. By Section
10
everything is in place for our definition of
\alpha_{G}
, and Theorem
1.2
follows by gathering the earlier work.

Then we focus on the orthogonal groups. In Section
11
we review Stiefel-Whitney classes, and in Section
12
we describe the double covers of the orthogonal group, culminating in Theorem
1.3
.

2.1.
Groups

When

is a group, we write

for the identity, and

for the abelianization of

. When

is an abelian group, we may use additive notation, so the identity would be

.
If

Let

be the group of

. Let

be the cyclic group of order

.
Throughout this paper

2.2.
Topological Groups

Throughout this paper we will consider various hypotheses on spaces and topological groups. For convenience we discuss relevant definitions here.
For us, topological groups will always be Hausdorff. For a topological group
G
, denote by
G^{\circ}
the path component of
G
containing the identity. It is a normal subgroup of
G
; write
\pi_{0}(G)=G^{\circ}\backslash G
for the quotient. Write
\Aut(G)
for the (continuous) automorphisms of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

A space is
locally path-connected
(LPC), provided it has a base of path-connected sets.

When
G
is locally path-connected,
G^{\circ}
is open in
G
, and so
\pi_{0}(G)
is discrete
[
Mun00
, Theorem 4.2, page 162]
.
Of course, Lie groups are LPC.

Example 2.2
.

The additive group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of
p
-adic integers is not LPC. The
p
-adic solenoid
\mathbb{T}_{p}
(see
[
HM20
, page 20]
) is connected, but not LPC.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
B
be a topological space.
We say that
B
is
semilocally simply connected
, provided for all
b\in B
, there is a neighborhood
U\ni b
so that the induced homomorphism
\pi_{1}(U,b)\to\pi_{1}(B,b)
is trivial. We say that
B
is
nice
, provided it is Hausdorff, locally path-connected, semilocally simply connected, and nonempty.

The significance for us is that when
B
is nice and path-connected, it has a universal cover. If
p:E\to B
is a cover with
B
nice, then
E
is automatically nice.

Definition 2.4
.

A topological group
G
is a
CW-group
, provided the underlying topological space is homeomorphic to a CW complex.

For instance Lie groups are CW-groups. The additive group
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
of finite sequences is a CW-group. A CW-group is necessarily LPC and nice.

2.3.
Topology on Products

Subtle topological issues arise for products of spaces, when neither is locally compact. For example, if
X
and
Y
are infinite-dimensional CW-complexes, then the space
X\times Y
with the usual product topology may not be a CW complex. Topologists prefer to work with “compactly generated” spaces, which include CW-complexes.

Definition 2.5
.

A space
X
is
compactly generated
(CG), provided a subset of
X
is closed iff its intersection with every compact subset of
X
is closed.

Unfortunately, when
X
and
Y
are CG spaces, then
X\times Y
with the product topology may not be CG.
There is a standard refinement of the product topology, sometimes called the
CG-topology
on
X\times Y
, which remedies this. We will write this as “
X\times_{k}Y
”. When either
X
or
Y
is locally compact, then the topologies coincide, i.e.,
X\times_{k}Y=X\times Y
. A standard reference is
[
ML13
, Chapter VII, Section 8]
. The continuity condition for a
right
G
-action
on a space
X
will be
that it defines a continuous map
X\times_{k}G\to X
. On the other hand, when
G
is a topological group, the multiplication map is continuous when
G\times G
is given the product topology.

3.
Central Extensions

In this section we study the category of central extensions of topological groups by discrete groups. Throughout,
G
is a topological group.

Definition 3.1
.

A
central extension of
G
by
A
is triplet
\mathscr{E}=(E,i,p)
, where
E
is a topological group,
i:A\to E
and
p:E\to G
are continuous homomorphisms. We require that

is an injection onto a discrete central subgroup of
E
, that
p
is open and surjective, and that
\ker p=\im i
.

Thus
\mathscr{E}
can be identified with the exact sequence

(1)

\mathscr{E}:0\to A\overset{i}{\to}E\overset{p}{\to}G\to 1,

of continuous homomorphisms of topological groups. Another central extension

\mathscr{E}^{\prime}:0\to A\overset{i^{\prime}}{\to}E^{\prime}\overset{p^{\prime}}{\to}G\to 1

is
equivalent
to (
1
) when there is a homomorphism
\varphi:E\to E^{\prime}
with
\varphi\circ i=i^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime}\circ\varphi=p
. (It is necessarily an isomorphism.)
In this case we write
\mathscr{E}\cong\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
. Let
{\bf E}(G,A)
be the category of central extensions of
G
by
A
, and let
\mathbb{E}(G,A)
be the set of central extensions of
G
by
A
, modulo equivalence.

3.1.
Functorial Properties

A morphism
\varphi:G^{\prime}\to G
gives rise to a “pullback” morphism
\varphi^{*}:\mathbb{E}(G,A)\to\mathbb{E}(G^{\prime},A)
as follows:
Given
\mathscr{E}=(E,i,p)\in{\bf E}(G,A)
, set

E^{\prime}=\{(g^{\prime},e)\mid\varphi(g^{\prime})=p(e)\},

viewed as a closed subgroup of the product

Define

by

, and let

be the first projection. Finally put
\varphi^{*}\mathscr{E}=(E^{\prime},i^{\prime},p^{\prime})

In particular, the group

acts on

by pullback.

Similarly, a homomorphism

3.3.
Relation to Lifting Problems

The following is clear:

Proposition 3.4

Let

The group

of

For

4.1.

Let

4.3.

See

4.5.

Let

Lemma 4.6

Proof.

It is enough to see that the action of

Let

∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let

Steenrod defines in

•

•

6.
Homotopy Theory of Classifying Spaces

In this section let

be a CW-group.

6.1.
The Long Exact Sequence

Applying Theorem
4.4
to the
G
-bundle

gives a long exact sequence

\cdots\to\pi_{n}(G)\to\pi_{n}(EG)\to\pi_{n}(BG)\to\pi_{n-1}(G)\to\cdots,

which is natural in

is contractible, this gives isomorphisms

(8)

\partial_{n}=\partial_{n}^{G}:\pi_{n}(BG)\overset{\sim}{\to}\pi_{n-1}(G),

for

. The set of path components
\pi_{0}(G,1)=G/G^{\circ}
has a group structure, and

(9)

\partial_{1}:\pi_{1}(BG)\to\pi_{0}(G,1)

is an isomorphism. By Hurewicz’s theorem, this gives rise to natural isomorphisms

(10)

\partial_{1}:H_{1}(BG)\overset{\sim}{\to}(G^{\circ}\backslash G)^{\ab},

and

\partial_{1}^{*}:\Hom(H_{1}(BG),A)\to\Hom_{c}(G,A).

Since

is discrete,

\Hom_{c}(G,A)=\Hom((G^{\circ}\backslash G)^{\ab},A).

By the UCT, we have

0\to\Ext^{1}(H_{0}(BG),A)\to H^{1}(BG,A)\overset{q^{1}}{\to}\Hom(H_{1}(BG),A)\to 0.

Since

is free, the

gives an isomorphism from

to
\Hom(H_{1}(BG),A)
.
Combining this with (

) gives:

Proposition 6.1
.

The composition

m^{1}=\partial_{1}^{*}\circ q^{1}:H^{1}(BG,A)\to\Hom_{c}(G,A)

is a natural isomorphism.

The Hurewicz map
h_{2}:\pi_{2}(BG)\to H_{2}(BG)
dualizes to a homomorphism

h^{2}=h_{2}^{*}:\Hom(H_{2}(BG),A)\to\Hom(\pi_{2}(BG),A).

Moreover the Universal Coefficient Theorem gives an exact sequence

(11)

0\to\Ext^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{1}(BG),A)\to H^{2}(BG,A)\overset{q^{2}}{\to}\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(H_{2}(BG),A)\to 0.

The map
m^{2}:H^{2}(BG,A)\to\Hom(\pi_{1}(G),A)
defined by
m^{2}=(\partial_{2}^{*})^{-1}\circ h^{2}\circ q^{2}
is the composition of natural maps and hence is natural itself.

6.2.
Case of

Proposition 6.2

When

Proof.

By (

The first equivalence follows from Proposition

∎

12.1.
Pin Groups

Let

Say

unit vector
provided
Q(u)=1
, and an
antiunit vector
, provided
Q(u)=-1
.
Note that if
u
is a unit vector, then
iu
is an antiunit vector.

The Clifford algebra
C(V)
is the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(V)
by the two-sided ideal generated by the set

\{v\otimes v-Q(v):v\in V\}.

It contains
V
as a subspace. Write
C(V)^{\times}
for the group of invertible members of
C(V)
.

Definition 12.1
.

Write
\Pin^{-}(V)
for the subgroup of
C(V)^{\times}
generated by the antiunit vectors in
V\subset C(V)
, and

\Pin^{+}(V)
for the subgroup generated by the unit vectors.

There are unique homomorphisms
\rho^{-}:\Pin^{-}(V)\to\Or(V)
and
\rho^{+}:\Pin^{+}(V)\to\Or(V)
. Here
\rho^{-}
(resp.,
\rho^{+}
) sends an antiunit (resp., unit) vector
u
to the reflection of
V
determined by
u
. The kernels of
\rho^{+}
and
\rho^{-}
are both equal to
\mu_{2}
.
Since
\Or(V)
is generated by reflections,
\rho^{+}
and
\rho^{-}
are surjective. Thus they are extensions of
\Or(V)
by
\mu_{2}
. See
[
FH13
, Chapter 20]
and
[
Frö85
, Appendix 1]
for details.

From fermionic spin-Calogero–Sutherland models to the Haldane–Shastry spin chain by freezing
Jules Lamers, Didina Serban

Abstract
The Haldane–Shastry spin chain is a long-range model known to enjoy a myriad of remarkable properties, including Yangian symmetry and, for spin 1 / 2 1/2 , explicit highest-weight eigenvectors featuring Jack polynomials, or more precisely its zonal spherical special case. These properties stem from the spin-Calogero–Sutherland model, which reduces to Haldane–Shastry in a special ‘freezing’ limit. In this work we clarify some points that, to the best of our knowledge, were missing in the literature. We have two main results. First, we show that freezing the fermionic spin-Calogero–Sutherland naturally accounts for the form of the explicit Haldane–Shastry wave functions. Second, we give a proof of the claim of Bernard, Gaudin, Haldane and Pasquier that the Yangian highest-weight eigenvectors of the 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 r \mathfrak{gl}_{r} version of the Haldane–Shastry spin chain arise from freezing 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 r − 1 \mathfrak{gl}_{r-1} spin-Calogero–Sutherland eigenvectors at the zonal spherical point α ⋆ = 1 / 2 \alpha^{\star}=1/2 .

A few isolated models were solved, some just partially, by using their connection with more general dynamical models

The long-range interaction can be understood to arise from stripping out the charge degrees of freedom in a fermionic system, and the excitations left are purely magnetic, the magnons in the ferromagnetic regime and the spinons in the antiferromagnetic ones. In this system, the magnons do not form bound states, and the magnons and the spinons have purely (fractional) statistical interaction.

Spin-Calogero–Sutherland model

The (trigonometric, quantum) Calogero–Sutherland model describes
N
particles moving on a circle while interacting in pairs with a periodic inverse-square potential

\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}[(x_{i}-x_{j})/2]}=\frac{z_{i}\,z_{j}}{z_{ij}\,z_{ji}}=\frac{-z_{i}\,z_{j}}{(z_{i}-z_{j})^{2}}=\frac{1}{|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}}\,,\qquad z_{j}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mspace{1mu}x_{j}}\,,

(2.1)

z_{ij}\equiv z_{i}-z_{j}

|z_{j}|=1

[

\displaystyle\text{scalar case:}\qquad\quad\begin{aligned} H^{\textsc{cs}}_{\pm}&=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}+\beta\,(\beta\mp 1)\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}[(x_{i}-x_{j})/2]}\\

(2.2)

2.3
Fermionic spin Calogero–Sutherland model

Now consider

Consider the fermionic

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathrm{tot}}_{-}\equiv\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\!\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,P_{\sigma}\,s_{\sigma}\,,\qquad\bigl(\Pi^{\mathrm{tot}}_{-}\bigr)^{2}=N!\;\Pi^{\mathrm{tot}}_{-}\,.

Haldane–Shastry spin chain

The spin-1/2 Haldane–Shastry spin chain is quantum integrable in the sense that it has abelian symmetries, nonabelian symmetries, and is exactly solvable: the Hamiltonian (

)

•

, each of which

•

[

]

The striking parallel with the properties of the spin-Calogero–Sutherland model, cf. the start of §

3.1
Freezing

We are interested in the limit
\beta\to\infty
of the effective spin-Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonian (
2.36
). The dominant term is the part linear in
\beta
. The term with derivatives,

\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{z_{i}+z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\,\bigl(z_{i}\,\partial_{z_{i}}-z_{j}\,\partial_{z_{j}}\bigr)=\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Biggl(\,\sum_{i(\neq j)}^{N}\frac{z_{i}+z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\Biggr)z_{j}\,\partial_{z_{j}}\,,

(3.1)

vanishes when the
z_{j}
are fixed, up to an irrelevant common factor, to distinct
N
th roots of unity. These are classical equilibrium positions. Indeed, defining the ‘evaluation’ (specialisation)
11
11

11

More properly
[
47
]
, evaluation corresponds to taking the quotient of the physical space, e.g. (
2.18
) or (
2.33
), by the ideal generated by
p_{1}(\bm{{z}}),\dots,p_{N-1}(\bm{{z}}),p_{N}(\bm{{z}})-N
where
p_{k}(\bm{{z}})=\sum_{i}z_{i}^{k}
are the power sums.

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\colon z_{j}\mapsto\omega^{j}\,,\qquad\omega\equiv\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/N}\,,

(3.2)

we have the identity

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\sum_{i(\neq j)}^{N}\frac{z_{i}+z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}=-\mathrm{i}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\cot\frac{\pi\,n}{N}=0\,,

(3.3)

since the summand is odd, while the sum may be taken over a set symmetric around

(

H^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}=\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\partial_{\beta}\big|_{\beta=0}\widetilde{H}_{-}^{\prime\mspace{2mu}\textsc{cs}}=\sum_{i<j}^{N}\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\frac{z_{i}\,z_{j}}{z_{ij}\,z_{ji}}\,(1+P_{ij})\,.

(3.4)

We then get the possible eigenvalues for free from the linear part in
\beta
of the effective spin-Calogero–Sutherland energy (
2.28
), cf. (
2.63

E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}(\bar{\bm{{k}}})=\partial_{\beta}\big|_{\beta=0}E^{\prime\mspace{2mu}\textsc{cs}}(\bar{\bm{{k}}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,(N-2\mspace{1mu}i+1)\,\bar{k}_{i}\,.

(3.5)

This result needs to be supplemented by a rule that selects which

are allowed, and thus which of the infinitely many possible energies of the form (

related by a (Galilean) boost for any

[cf. (
3.13

\bar{k}_{N}=0

–

The abelian symmetries of the Haldane–Shastry spin chain also arise from freezing. The (total) momentum operator (

3.1.1
Strong coupling vs classical limit

To understand freezing in more detail consider the (ordinary, i.e. not effective) spin-Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonian (
2.3
). Reinstate Planck’s constant by setting
\beta=g/\hbar
and multiplying
\widetilde{H}^{\textsc{cs}}_{-}
by
\hbar^{2}
. In these terms
\beta\to\infty
can be interpreted either as a strong coupling limit (
g\to\infty
) or as a classical limit (
\hbar\to 0
).

In the classical limit the momenta become
\hat{p}_{j}\coloneqq-\mathrm{i}\,\hbar\,\partial_{x_{j}}\to p_{j}
, while in the potential energy
g\,(g-\hbar\,P_{ij})\to g^{2}
the distinction between bosons and fermions is lost. This yields (the identity on spins times) the
Calogero–Sutherland–Moser
model,

H^{\textsc{csm}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}^{2}+g^{2}\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}[(x_{i}-x_{j})/2]}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{j}^{2}+g^{2}\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{z_{i}\,z_{j}}{z_{ij}\,z_{ji}}\,,

(3.6)

with equations of motion

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}

\displaystyle=\{x_{i},H^{\textsc{csm}}\}=\hphantom{+}\frac{\partial H^{\textsc{csm}}}{\partial p_{i}}=p_{i}\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{j}

\displaystyle=\{p_{j},H^{\textsc{csm}}\}=-\frac{\partial H^{\textsc{csm}}}{\partial x_{j}}=-g^{2}\,\partial_{x_{j}}\!\sum_{i(\neq j)}^{N}\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}[(x_{i}-x_{j})/2]}\,.

The classical equilibrium, where these Poisson brackets vanish, is
p_{j}=0
and
x_{j}=j/N
up to permutations and a common shift of the positions. This leads us to the evaluation (
3.2
).

The energy at classical equilibrium only receives a contribution from the potential energy. This is also the dominant (most divergent) term for
g\to\infty
. To find it we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\sum_{i\neq j}^{N}\frac{z_{i}\,z_{j}}{z_{ij}\,z_{ji}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{(1-\omega^{n})(1-\omega^{-n})}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2}\oint_{\bar{C}_{1}}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\;\frac{1}{(1-y)(1-y^{-1})}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\log\frac{y^{N}-1}{y-1}\;,

where
\bar{C}_{1}
is a counter-clockwise contour encircling all the poles at the roots of unity
y=\omega^{n}
except
y=1
. By deforming the integration contour and taking into account that the integral over the contour at infinity vanishes, we compute

\displaystyle\oint_{\bar{C}_{1}}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\;\frac{1}{(1-y)(1-y^{-1})}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\log\frac{y^{N}-1}{y-1}

\displaystyle=-\!\oint_{C_{1}}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\;\frac{1}{(1-y)(1-y^{-1})}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\log\frac{y^{N}-1}{y-1}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\res\limits_{y=1}\,\frac{1}{(1-y^{-1})(y^{N}-1)}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\frac{y^{N}-1}{y-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}-1}{12}\;,

where
C_{1}
is a counter-clockwise contour encircling
y=1
.
Hence the classical equilibrium energy is given by

g^{2}\,\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{z_{i}\,z_{j}}{z_{ij}\,z_{ji}}=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\,E^{0}\,,

(3.10)

where
E^{0}
was defined in (
2.13
). This value coincides (up to a factor of
\hbar^{2}
) with the contribution proportional to
\beta^{2}
in the quantum-mechanical energy, cf. (
2.28
)–(
2.30
).

Now expand the spin-Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonian one order further in
\hbar
:

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\,\widetilde{H}^{\textsc{cs}}_{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,(\hbar\,z_{j}\,\partial_{z_{j}})^{2}+g\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{z_{i}\,z_{j}}{z_{ij}\,z_{ji}}\,(g+\hbar\,P_{ij})

(3.11)

\displaystyle=H^{\textsc{csm}}\,\mathbbm{1}+\hbar\,g\sum_{i<j}^{N}\frac{z_{i}\,z_{j}}{z_{ij}\,z_{ji}}\,P_{ij}+O(\hbar^{2})\,.

At classical equilibrium the
semiclassical limit
, i.e. the coefficient of
\hbar
, becomes (
g
times) the Haldane–Shastry Hamiltonian (
3.4
) up to a shift by the constant (
3.10
).
We conclude that the Haldane--Shastry spin chain can be obtained both from the original or from the effective spin-Calogero--Sutherland Hamiltonian by freezing.

The physical picture is that the freezing limit is dominated by the potential term, so that the particles slow down and come to a halt at their equally spaced classical equilibrium positions to form the Haldane–Shastry spin chain.

3.2
Haldane–Shastry eigenvectors

As we have just seen, the Haldane–Shastry hamiltonian and its eigenvalues can be obtained by freezing. The same holds for the abelian symmetries (with a little more effort) and nonabelian symmetries (see §
B.2
). Unfortunately it is not as simple to derive the Haldane–Shastry
eigenvectors
directly from those of the spin-Calogero–Sutherland model by freezing. There are several obstacles.
First, one needs to compute limit
\beta\to\infty
of non-symmetric Jack polynomials, or rather their partial symmetrisation (
2.49
). Second, for many states the result vanishes upon evaluation. Third, the wave functions of the surviving states require further work. The reason for this is that evaluation allows to rewrite partially symmetric polynomials from
\mathbb{C}[\bm{{z}}]^{S_{M}\times S_{N-M}}
as symmetric polynomials in only
M
variables. Indeed, consider the evaluation of the power sums
p_{n}(\bm{{z}})\equiv\sum_{i}z_{i}^{n}
,

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,p_{n}(\bm{{z}})=N\,\delta_{N\mid n}\,,\qquad\delta_{N\mid n}\equiv\begin{cases}1&n\in N\,\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,,\\
0&\text{else}\,.\end{cases}

(3.12)

Hence
p_{n}(z_{M+1},\dots,z_{N})=p_{n}(\bm{{z}})-p_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})
coincides with
N\,\delta_{N\mid n}-p_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})
upon evaluation. In principle, any element in
\mathbb{C}[\bm{{z}},\bm{{z}}^{-1}]^{S_{M}\times S_{N-M}}
can be reduced to a polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{M}]^{S_{M}}
in this way. Carrying out this procedure in practice, however, is not straightforward in general.

The exception is formed by the simple eigenstates
\Delta(\bm{{z}})\,P_{\bm{{\lambda}}}(\bm{{z}})\,\lvert\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle
and their spin-flipped counterparts. Here we only need to recall that
P_{\bm{{\lambda}}}(\bm{{z}})\to e_{\bm{{\lambda}}^{\prime}}(\bm{{z}})\equiv e_{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}(\bm{{z}})\,e_{\lambda^{\prime}_{2}}(\bm{{z}})\cdots
when
\beta\to\infty
, and note that by Newton’s identities (
3.12
) implies

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\ e_{n}(\bm{{z}})=\delta_{n,0}+(-1)^{N-1}\delta_{n,N}\,.

(3.13)

Thus the only
M=0
states that survive evaluation correspond to partitions
\bm{{\lambda}}=(l^{N})
for some
l=\ell(\bm{{\lambda}}^{\prime})\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
. By (
2.23
) their wave function
P_{(l^{N})}(\bm{{z}})=e_{N}(\bm{{z}})^{l}
is independent of
\beta
. Upon evaluation all these states reduce to
\lvert\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle
up to a phase.
That is, all states with
\bar{\bm{{k}}}=(l^{N})+\bm{{\delta_{N}}}
can, in the freezing limit, be identified with the ferromagnetic state
\lvert\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle
. Their energy is

E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}(\bm{{\delta_{N}}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,(N-2\mspace{1mu}i+1)\,(N-i)=\frac{N(N^{2}-1)}{12}=E^{0}\,,

(3.14)

where we used the boost invariance mentioned following (
3.5
) to set the last momentum to zero. This eigenvalue is consistent with the evaluation of (
3.4
) at
P_{ij}=1
as appropriate for symmetric spin vectors.

For arbitrary
M
the spin-Calogero–Sutherland eigenvectors are not as simple, and it is much less easy to follow them in the freezing limit. We do not know how to get the simple Haldane–Shastry wave functions directly by freezing. Luckily there are other approaches to derive this result.

3.2.1
Frozen wedges

Upon evaluation many single-particle basis vectors
u_{k}
from (
2.50
) become the same,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,u_{k+2\mspace{1mu}N}=\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,u_{k}\,,

(3.15)

and the wedges become linearly dependent. In order to evaluate
\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}^{(M)}
from (
2.62
) we need to evaluate the Vandermonde determinants as well as the Schur polynomials. For the former we use that
\Delta(\bm{{z}})
can be evaluated in two ways. On the one hand

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\Delta(\bm{{z}})=(-1)^{(N+2)(N-1)/4}\,N^{N/2}

(3.16)

is just a constant
13
13

13

The value is found by noticing that
(-1)^{N(N-1)/2}\,\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\Delta(\bm{{z}})^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\omega^{i}\prod_{j(\neq i)}^{N}(1-\omega^{j-i})=N^{N}\,\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,e_{N}(\bm{{z}})=(-1)^{N-1}N^{N}
, where the middle equality uses (
3.18
).
.
On the other hand we can split the Vandermonde factor as

\Delta(\bm{{z}})=\Delta(\bm{{z}}_{I^{\mathrm{c}}})\,\Delta(\bm{{z}}_{I})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i\in I\end{subarray}}^{N}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
j\in I\end{subarray}}^{N}(z_{i}-z_{j})\,,

(3.17)

where
\Delta(\bm{{z}}_{I})^{-1}
compensates for the spurious factors in the two products. These products can be explicitly evaluated: since

\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-\omega^{j})=\lim_{u\to 1}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(u-\omega^{j})=\lim_{u\to 1}\frac{u^{N}-1}{u-1}=N

(3.18)

we compute, setting
|I|\equiv\sum_{i\in I}i
,

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i\in I\end{subarray}}^{N}(\omega^{i}-\omega^{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
j\in I\end{subarray}}^{N}(\omega^{i}-\omega^{j})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<j\\
i\in I\end{subarray}}^{N}(\omega^{i}-\omega^{j})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i\\
i\in I\end{subarray}}^{N}(\omega^{j}-\omega^{i})

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I}\biggl((-1)^{i-1}\!\prod_{j(\neq i)}^{N}\!\!(\omega^{i}-\omega^{j})\biggr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|I|-M}\prod_{i\in I}\biggl(\omega^{(N-1)i}\!\prod_{j(\neq i)}^{N}\!(1-\omega^{j-i})\biggr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|I|-M}N^{M}\prod_{i\in I}\omega^{-i}\,.

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\Delta(\bm{{z}}_{I^{\mathrm{c}}})=(-1)^{(N+2)(N-1)/4}\,N^{N/2-M}\times(-1)^{|I|-M}\,\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\Delta(\bm{{z}}_{I})\,e_{M}(\bm{{z}}_{I})\,,

(3.20)

where
e_{M}(\bm{{z}}_{I})=\prod_{i\in I}z_{i}
.
Thus we may convert the factor
\Delta(\bm{{z}}_{I^{\mathrm{c}}})
in (
2.62
) into a constant times
(-1)^{|I|}\,\Delta(\bm{{z}}_{I})\,e_{M}(\bm{{z}}_{I})
upon evaluation.
Regarding the Schur polynomials, by the evaluation (
3.12

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,p_{n}(\bm{{z}}_{I})=-\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,p_{n}(\bm{{z}}_{I^{\mathrm{c}}})=\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\Omega\,p_{n}(-\bm{{z}}_{I^{\mathrm{c}}})\quad{\rm for}\ n<N\,,

(3.21)

where

(3.22)

Upon evaluation the wedge (

\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}^{(M)}=\mathrm{cst}_{N,M,\bm{{\lambda}}^{(\uparrow)}}\times\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,N\}\\

\displaystyle=\mathrm{cst}_{N,M,\bm{{0}}}\times\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,\dots,N\}\\

3.2.2
Connection to scalar Calogero–Sutherland model

The Haldane–Shastry Hamiltonian (
3.4
) acts on the frozen
M
-magnon wedges (
3.24
) as

H^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\ \mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}^{(M)}=\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\Bigl(\partial_{\beta}\big|_{\beta=0}\widetilde{H}_{-}^{\prime\mspace{2mu}\textsc{cs}}\ \widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}^{(M)}\Bigr)\,.

(3.26)

Due to (
2.63
) and (
3.5
) the linearised spin-Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonian acts by

\partial_{\beta}\big|_{\beta=0}\widetilde{H}_{-}^{\prime\mspace{2mu}\textsc{cs}}\;\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}^{(M)}=E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl(\bar{\bm{{k}}}\bigr)\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}+\sum_{i<j}^{N}\widetilde{h}_{ij}\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}\,.

(3.27)

The squeezing operators
\widetilde{h}_{ij}
kill the
\uparrow
s with ‘fully packed’ momenta

\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}=\pm\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}^{(\downarrow)}}\wedge\widehat{u}_{2\,\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}}:\qquad\widetilde{h}_{ij}\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}}=\pm\Bigl(\widetilde{h}_{ij}\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}^{(\downarrow)}}\Bigr)\!\wedge\widehat{u}_{2\,\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}}\,.

(3.28)

Hence the
\uparrow
s are spectators in (
3.27
), effectively reducing the rank of the wedges by one, that is, from spin-1/2 to the scalar case.

In full detail, the reduction to the scalar case goes as follows. Recall, cf. (
2.56
), that fully polarised wedges have the simple form
\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}^{(\downarrow)}}=a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,\lvert\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle
. Using this along with the definition (
2.64
) of the squeezing operators we find

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{mn}\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}^{(\downarrow)}}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\sum_{p=1}^{\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{m}-\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{n}-1}\!\!\!\bigl(\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{m}-\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{n}-p\bigr)\,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}^{(\downarrow)}-2\,p\,(\bm{{\varepsilon_{m}}}-\bm{{\varepsilon_{n}}})}

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\bigl(h_{mn}\,a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\bigr)\,\lvert\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle\,,

where we defined a scalar version of the squeezing operator,

h_{mn}\,a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\equiv\sum_{p=1}^{\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{m}-\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{n}-1}\!\!\!\bigl(\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{m}-\bar{k}^{(\downarrow)}_{n}-p\bigr)\,a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}-p\,(\bm{{\varepsilon_{m}}}-\bm{{\varepsilon_{n}}})}\,,

(3.30)

which acts on antisymmetric polynomials in
M
variables.
To get back to the spin case (
3.28
) we just multiply (
3.30
) by
\lvert\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle=\lvert 1,\dots,M\rangle\!\rangle
and take the wedge produce with
\widehat{u}_{2\,\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}}=a_{\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N-M})\,\lvert\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle=\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{N-M})\,\lvert\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle
, keeping in mind to shift the coordinates associated tot the
\uparrow
s as
z_{j}\mapsto z_{j+M}
when taking the wedge product.
Just as the squeezing operator (
3.28
) is captured by the scalar action (
3.30
), the linearised spin-Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonian can be reduced by a scalar operator
H^{\text{red}}
that acts on
\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{M}]^{S_{M}}
. Namely,

\partial_{\beta}\big|_{\beta=0}\widetilde{H}_{-}^{\prime\mspace{2mu}\textsc{cs}}\ \,\widehat{u}_{\bm{{k}}^{(\downarrow)}}\wedge\widehat{u}_{2\,\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}}=\Bigl(\bigl(H^{\text{red}}\;a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\bigr)\,\lvert\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle\Bigr)\wedge\widehat{u}_{2\,\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}}\,,

(3.31)

where

H^{\text{red}}\;a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\equiv E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl(\bar{\bm{{k}}}\bigr)\,a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})+\sum_{m<n}^{M}h_{mn}\,a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,.

(3.32)

Since the
a_{\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})
form a basis for
\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{M}]^{S_{M}}
we obtain

H^{\text{red}}=E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl(\bar{\bm{{k}}}\bigr)+\sum_{m<n}^{M}h_{mn}\,.

(3.33)

This starts to look like the effective Hamiltonian of the Calogero–Sutherland model for
N^{\star}\equiv M
spinless fermions. To verify that this is indeed the case and identify the parameter
\beta
it remains to massage the diagonal term into a more convenient form.

At this point we need to make an assumption about
\bar{\bm{{k}}}
, following
[
20
]
(cf. Appendix
A
),
[
3
]
, and §3.2.3 of
[
26
]
. The evaluations allow us, without loss of generality, to take
\bm{{\lambda}}^{(\downarrow)}
to be a partition with nonnegative parts. Thus
\lambda^{(\downarrow)}_{M}\geq 0
, i.e.
\nu_{M}\geq 1
. Since
\omega^{N}=1
it furthermore seems reasonable to ask for the maximum degree of the polynomial in each variable to be less than
N
. By symmetry of (
3.25
) we may consider its degree in
z_{1}
. As the prefactor has degree
2\,(M-1)
we thus restrict our attention to
\bm{{\nu}}
with largest part bounded by
\nu_{1}\leq N-2M+1
.
15
15

15

We stress that due to the evaluation one can always take either
\nu_{M}\geq 1
(as we do, provided one allows for parts
\geq N
) or
\nu_{1}\leq N-2M+1
(allowing for negative parts). Since both conditions at the same time in particular requires
M\leq N/2
, at the very least we are missing all descendants ‘beyond the equator’. We will look at descendants in more detail in §
3.2.3
.

The conditions
\nu_{1}\leq N-2M+1
and
\nu_{M}\geq 1
imply that the integers
\bm{{\mu}}
defined in (
3.25
) obey
0<\mu_{m}<N
are strictly increasing and non consecutive, i.e.

0<\mu_{m}<N\,,\qquad\mu_{m+1}>\mu_{m}+1\,.

(3.34)

Such an
M
-tuple
\bm{{\mu}}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{M})
is called a
motif
, or more precisely an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-
motif
.
In terms of the partitions,
\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\uparrow)}
and
\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}
the conditions
\bm{{\lambda}}^{(\uparrow)}=\bm{{0}}
and
\nu_{1}\leq N-2M+1
mean that

\bar{k}_{j}^{(\uparrow)}=N-M-j\,,\qquad\bar{k}_{1}^{(\downarrow)}\leq N-M-1\,.

(3.35)

Hence the momenta associated with
\uparrow
s are fixed to their minimal values
\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\uparrow)}=\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}
, while the momenta
\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}
associated with the magnons
\downarrow
are a subset of
\bm{{\delta_{N-M}}}
.
The full
N
-tuple of coordinate integers
\bar{\bm{{k}}}
and the motif are related by

\bar{k}_{j}=N-j-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!\theta(N-j-\mu_{m})\,,\qquad\theta(x)\equiv\begin{cases}0&x<0\,,\\
1&x\geq 0\,,\end{cases}

(3.36)

i.e.
\bar{\bm{{k}}}+(M^{N})=\bm{{\delta_{N}}}+\bigl((\bm{{\mu}}^{+})^{\prime})^{-}
, where
\bigl((\bm{{\mu}}^{+})^{\prime})^{-}
is the reverse of the conjugate
(\bm{{\mu}}^{+})^{\prime}
to the partition
\bm{{\mu}}^{+}
that is itself obtained from the motif
\bm{{\mu}}
by reversal. That is,
\bm{{\mu}}
records positions of duplicates in
\bar{\bm{{k}}}
, counted from the right (since
\bar{\bm{{k}}}
decreases). See Table
1
for an example. Since the Haldane–Shastry energy (
3.5
) is linear we have

E^{0}=E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}(\bm{{\delta_{N}}})=E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl(\bar{\bm{{k}}}+\bigl((\bm{{\mu}}^{+})^{\prime}\bigr)^{-}\bigr)=E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl(\bar{\bm{{k}}}\bigr)+E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl(\bigl((\bm{{\mu}}^{+})^{\prime}\bigr)^{-}\bigr)\,.

(3.37)

The last term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl(\bigl((\bm{{\mu}}^{+})^{\prime}\bigr)^{-}\bigr)=E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}\bigl((\bm{{\mu}}^{+})^{\prime}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,(\mu^{+})^{\prime}_{i}\,(N-2\,i+1)

(3.38)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{(\mu^{+})^{\prime}_{i}}(N-2\,i+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{\mu_{m}}\,(N-2\,i+1)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mu_{m}\,(N-\mu_{m})\,,

where in the third equality we use the standard trick of switching from summing row by row to column by column. Thus we can rewrite the Haldane–Shastry energy as

E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}(\bar{\bm{{k}}})=E^{0}-E^{\textsc{hs}}(\bm{{\mu}})\,,\qquad E^{\textsc{hs}}(\bm{{\mu}})\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mu_{m}\,(N-\mu_{m})\,.

(3.39)

The summand of difference
E^{\textsc{hs}}(\bm{{\mu}})
with the ferromagnetic energy (
3.14
) is the well-known quadratic dispersion relation of magnons for the Haldane–Shastry spin chain, and its sum gives their (strictly additive) correction to the energy (
3.14
) of the ferromagnetic vector
\lvert\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle
.

From (
3.25
) we have
\mu_{m}=\bar{k}_{M-m+1}^{(\downarrow)}+m
. Plugging this into (
3.39
) we get

\displaystyle E^{\textsc{hs}}_{-}(\bar{\bm{{k}}})

\displaystyle=\Biggl(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigl(\bar{k}_{m}^{(\downarrow)}\bigr)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigl(M-2\,m+1\bigr)\,\bar{k}_{m}^{(\downarrow)}\Biggr)-\frac{1}{2}\,(N-M-1)\bigl|\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}\bigr|

(3.40)

\displaystyle+E^{0}-\frac{1}{6}\,M\,(M+1)\,(3\,N-2\,M-1)

\displaystyle=\bigl[E^{\prime\mspace{2mu}\textsc{cs}}\bigl(\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}\bigr)\bigr]_{N^{\star}=M,\,\beta^{\star}=1}-\frac{1}{2}\,(N-M-1)\,\bigl|\bar{\bm{{k}}}^{(\downarrow)}\bigr|+\mathrm{cst}_{N,M}\,,

where we recognised the Calogero–Sutherland energy (
2.28
) for
N^{\star}=M
particles with effective coupling
\beta^{\star}=1
, shifted by a multiple of the (total) momentum and a constant.

Putting everything together the action (
3.27
) of the linearised spin-Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonian reduces to the scalar operator

H^{\text{red}}=\bigl[H^{\prime\mspace{2mu}\textsc{cs}}_{-}\bigr]_{N^{\star}=M,\,\beta^{\star}=1}-\frac{1}{2}\,(N-M-1)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!z_{m}\,\partial_{z_{m}}+\mathrm{cst}_{N,M}

(3.41)

defined on the space
\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\dots,z_{M}]^{S_{M}}
of antisymmetric polynomials in
M
variables. Here we recognised the action of the effective Calogero–Sutherland Hamiltonian for spinless bosons [see e.g. (
4.13
)–(
4.14
) with
r=1
] with
N^{\star}=M
particles and coupling
\beta^{\star}=1
, up to an abelian symmetry proportional to the total momentum
\sum_{m=1}^{M}z_{m}\,\partial_{m}
and a constant.
From §
2.2
, see (
2.32
), we know that the eigenfunctions (
3.41
) are given by

\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,[P_{\bm{{\lambda}}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})]_{\beta=\beta^{\star}+1}=\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,P_{\bm{{\lambda}}}^{\star}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,,\quad P_{\bm{{\lambda}}}^{\star}\equiv P_{\bm{{\lambda}}}\big|_{\beta=2}\,,

(3.42)

where we simplified
\bm{{\lambda}}\equiv\bm{{\lambda}}^{(\downarrow)}
.
The final step is to evaluate as in (
3.26
), taking into account the contribution coming from
\Delta(z_{M+1},\dots,z_{N})
following §
3.2.1
. The conclusion is that the Haldane–Shastry spin chain has wave functions
\Psi_{\bm{{\mu}}}^{\textsc{hs}}(i_{1},\dots,i_{M})=\mathrm{ev}_{\omega}\,\widetilde{\Psi}_{\bm{{\mu}}}^{\textsc{hs}}(z_{i_{1}},\dots,z_{i_{M}})
with

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}^{\textsc{hs}}_{\bm{{\mu}}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})

\displaystyle=\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})^{2}\,e_{M}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})\,P_{\bm{{\lambda}}}^{\star}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})

(3.43)

\displaystyle=\Delta(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})^{2}\,P_{\bm{{\nu}}}^{\star}(z_{1},\dots,z_{M})

\displaystyle=m_{\bm{{\mu}}^{+}}+\text{lower}\,,

which is of the form (
3.25
) as it should be. The corresponding motif is

\mu_{m}=\lambda_{M-m+1}+2\,m-1=\nu_{M-m+1}+2\,(m-1)\,,

(3.44)

in terms of which the energy is given by (
3.39
). The motif conditions (
3.34
) ensure that the degree of (
3.43
) in each variable is

In the preceding discussion we had to assume

(A.11)

A.3
Into the difference equation

As a warm up we start with the

This is the quadratic dispersion of Haldane–Shastry. The sign comes from (

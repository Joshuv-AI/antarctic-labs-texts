A classification of neighborhoods around leaves of a singular foliation
Simon-Raphael Fischer, Camille Laurent-Gengoux

Abstract
. We classify singular foliations admitting a given leaf and a given transverse singular foliation.



Introduction

Although much less studied than regular foliations, singular foliations are extremely common in differential geometry. After an intense debate in the 1970s about what their very definition should be
[

We think that most of the work which is done here should hold true as well using the alternative definition of singular foliations as in

Non-commutative geometry, presently the most active area of research that makes use of singular foliations uses examples associated to differential operators (see,

We intend here to complete a classification along a leaf

To understand what such a semi-local classification can achieve, recall that a basic result for singular foliations states that ”if one travels along a leaf, one always sees the same landscape through the window”.
More precisely, there is a splitting theorem: For a leaf of codimension

We address the following question:

Also, there are cases for which the answer to Question

What is the relation between YMCs and singular foliations?
Geometrically, the existence of a YMC is linked to the following phenomenon (to our knowledge, first noticed by
[

Consider a path

with transverse singular foliation

That is, it is a Yang-Mills connection with respect to a subgroup bundle of symmetries. Of course, since we work in the formal case, such an interpretation should be considered as an heuristic idea only. But it can be made precise:
We will show that any

(1)

Let

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{formal}}(\mathcal{U})\simeq C^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})[[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]].

\displaystyle=\left[\mathrm{PT}^{P}_{\gamma}(p),g\right]\cdot\left[\mathrm{PT}^{P}_{\gamma}(p),v\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{PT}^{c_{H}(P)}_{\gamma}\bigl([p,g]\bigr)\cdot\mathrm{PT}^{T}_{\gamma}\bigl([p,v]\bigr)

i.e.

\mathrm{PT}^{P}_{\gamma}(p\cdot g)=\mathrm{PT}^{P}_{\gamma}(p)\cdot g

Now assume that

Observe now that
[p,q_{\gamma}]
depends by construction only on
\gamma

[p,v]
; the dependency of
q_{\gamma}
on
p

p
over

is the same as for

Corollary A.7

(Bracket of horizontal and vertical vectors)
.

Assume a compatible Yang-Mills connection on
T
, then this satisfies a connection and curvature property:

(1)

The horizontal lift
\mathbb{H}
on
T
satisfies

\left[\mathbb{H}(X),\overline{\nu}\right]=\overline{\nabla^{\mathcal{G}}_{X}\nu}

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(L)
and
\nu\in\Gamma(\mathcal{g})
.

(2)

There is a unique
\zeta\in\Omega^{2}(L;\mathcal{g})
generating the curvature of
\mathbb{H}

\bigl[\mathbb{H}\left(X\right),\mathbb{H}\left(X^{\prime}\right)\bigr]-\mathbb{H}\bigl([X,X^{\prime}]\bigr)=\overline{\zeta(X,X^{\prime})}

for all
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{X}(L)
; in particular,
\zeta
is the curvature/field strength of

.

[
Proof.]We have

\left[\mathbb{H}(X),\overline{\nu}\right]=\left[\overline{A(X)},\overline{\nu}\right]=\overline{\left[A(X),\nu\right]}_{\mathrm{at}(P)}=\overline{\nabla^{\mathcal{G}}_{X}\nu}

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(L)
and
\nu\in\Gamma(\mathcal{g})
. The curvature equation follows similarly.

Proposition A.10

(Field redefinitions of connections are connections)

[

Remark A.11

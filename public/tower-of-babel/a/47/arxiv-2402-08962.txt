Let

be a DVR such that

\displaystyle\psi^{G}_{H}

\displaystyle\colon R^{H}\rightarrow R^{G},

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{|G\colon H|}\sum_{gH\in G/H}gr.

See

Recall we say a graded ring

Next we show

Lemma 3.5
.

(with hypotheses as in

). If

is Cohen-Macaulay then

is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{M}_{G}
be the

-maximal ideal of

. Notice as

is a finite

-module we get

the

-maximal ideal of

. As

is Cohen-Macaulay it follows that

. We have a sequence of

such that the composite is identity. As local-cohomology is

Next we show that Cohen-Macaulay property of ring of invariants behave well with respect to base change. More precisely let

be two ring of integers of global fields such that

is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

We note that

is a finitely generated

-module.
Let

be a maximal homogeneous ideal of

be a maximal ideal of

with

We prove Theorem

by induction on order of

4.1

Let
(\mathcal{O},(\pi))
be a DVR. By
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}
we denote a class of rings

with following properties.

(1)

R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n}
is a graded Cohen-Macaulay ring with

.

(2)

\dim R=3
.

(3)

are domains.

(4)

Set

.

Example: The polynomial ring
\mathcal{O}[X,Y]\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{O}}

The following result is useful.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

. Then
H^{i}_{R_{+}}(R)=0
for
i\neq 2
and
H^{2}_{R_{+}}(R)\neq 0

Proof.

We note that
R/\pi R
is a two dimensional finitely generated algebra over a field
\mathcal{O}/(\pi)
. It is also Cohen-Macaulay. So we have
H^{i}_{(R/\pi R)_{+}}(R/\pi R)=0
for

and
H^{2}_{(R/\pi R)_{+}}(R/\pi R)\neq 0
. We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow R\xrightarrow{\pi}R\rightarrow R/\pi R\rightarrow 0.

Taking cohomology with respect to

we get that for

and for all

we have

H^{i}_{R_{+}}(R)_{n}\xrightarrow{\pi}H^{i}_{R_{+}}(R)_{n}\rightarrow 0.

As

is finitely generated

-module, see (see
[

, 15.1.5]
), it follows from Nakayama’s Lemma that

for all

(when

).

We also have a sequence

It follows that
H^{2}_{R_{+}}(R)\neq 0
.
∎

In this section we describe the Ellingsrud-Skjelbred spectral sequences, see

(we follow the exposition given in

5.1
.

Let
S
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Let

be the category of left
A
-modules.

(1) Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal in

be the torsion functor associated to
\mathfrak{a}

(2) If

then note
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\in Mod(S[G])
.

5.2
.

Ellingsrud-Skjelbred spectral sequences are constructed as follows: Consider the following sequence of functors

(i)\quad Mod(S[G])\xrightarrow{(-)^{G}}Mod(S)\xrightarrow{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}}Mod(S),

(ii)\quad Mod(S[G])\xrightarrow{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}}Mod(S[G])\xrightarrow{(-)^{G}}Mod(S)

We then notice

(a)

The above compositions are equal.

(b)

It is possible to use Grothendieck spectral sequence of composite of functors to both (i) and (ii) above; see

[
9
, 8.6.2]
.

Following Ellingsrud-Skjelbred we let
H^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(G,-)
denote the
n^{th}
right derived functor of this composite
functor. So by (i) and (ii) we have two first quadrant spectral sequences for each
S[G]
-module
M

(\alpha)\colon\quad\quad E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathfrak{a}}(H^{q}(G,M))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathfrak{a}}(G,M),\ \text{and}

(\beta)\colon\quad\quad\mathcal{E}_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(G,H^{q}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathfrak{a}}(G,M).

Remark 5.3
.

Ellingsrud-Skjelbred spectral sequences have an obvious graded analogue.

.

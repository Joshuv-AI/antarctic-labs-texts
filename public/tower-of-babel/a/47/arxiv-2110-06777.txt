Incremental Ensemble Gaussian Processes
Qin Lu, Georgios V. Karanikolas, and Georgios B. Giannakis

Abstract
Belonging to the family of Bayesian nonparametrics, Gaussian process (GP) based approaches have well-documented merits not only in learning over a rich class of nonlinear functions, but also in quantifying the associated uncertainty. However, most GP methods rely on a single preselected kernel function, which may fall short in characterizing data samples that arrive sequentially in time-critical applications. To enable online kernel adaptation, the present work advocates an incremental ensemble (IE-) GP framework, where an EGP meta-learner employs an ensemble of GP learners, each having a unique kernel belonging to a prescribed kernel dictionary. With each GP expert leveraging the random feature-based approximation to perform online prediction and model update with scalability , the EGP meta-learner capitalizes on data-adaptive weights to synthesize the per-expert predictions. Further, the novel IE-GP is generalized to accommodate time-varying functions by modeling structured dynamics at the EGP meta-learner and within each GP learner. To benchmark the performance of IE-GP and its dynamic variant in the adversarial setting where the modeling assumptions are violated, rigorous performance analysis has been conducted via the notion of regret, as the norm in online convex optimization. Last but not the least, online unsupervised learning for dimensionality reduction is explored under the novel IE-GP framework. Synthetic and real data tests demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed schemes.

Introduction

Gaussian processes (GPs) cross-fertilize merits of kernel methods and Bayesian models to benefit several learning tasks, including regression, classification, ranking, and dimensionality reduction
[
1
]
. In GP-based approaches, a Gaussian

is assumed over a learning function

with covariance (kernel) capturing similarities among

dependent on inputs
\{\mathbf{x}_{t}\}

\{y_{t}\}

, Bayes rule produces the
posterior
distribution of
f(\cdot)

Besides learning functions with rich expressiveness, the Bayesian framework of GP-based approaches further quantifies uncertainty of the function estimate, which is of utmost importance in safety-critical applications. For instance in medical diagnosis
[
2
]
, human intervention would be called for when machine operated decisions are accompanied by high uncertainty.

In spite of the intriguing performance, applicability of plain-vanilla GPs in the big data regime is discouraged by the cubic computational complexity in the number of training samples
[
1
]
. To relieve the scalability issue, various attempts have been made, including efficient numerical operation
[

,
4
]
, and structured approximants of the kernel matrix
[

,
6

. Of special interest to us is the random feature (RF) based approach, which, leveraging the spectral properties of stationary kernels, converts the nonparametric GP paradigm to a parametric one
[
7
,
8

Such a parametric approach readily accommodates online processing of data samples
[
9
]
, which is necessitated in time-critical applications. For instance, the detection of spam emails is performed on an email-by-email basis in real time. Albeit accommodating online operation, the performance of existing RF-based GP methods hinges on the single
preselected
kernel, that may fall short in characterizing upcoming data. Henceforth, online kernel adaptation is essential to real-time decision making.

On the theoretical horizon, to benchmark performance of online approaches, analysis is usually conducted via the notion of regret, the norm in online convex optimization
[
10
]
and online learning with experts
[
11
]
, to combat with the adversarial setting where the generative assumptions are violated. Although several scalable GP approaches have been developed for the online operation

, regret analysis has not been touch upon except for the plain-vanilla GP

In accordance with the aforementioned desiderata, the goal of the current work is to pursue algorithmic developments of scalable GPs that could enable kernel adaptation to cope with function dynamics in the online scenario, as well as benchmark performance of the resultant approaches via regret-based analysis.

1-B

Contributions

Relative to the aforementioned past works, the present paper aims at bringing together the fields of scalable GPs and online learning with expert advice
[
11
]
.
The pursuit lies in algorithmic development, as well as performance analysis via the measure of regret to account for the violations of the generative models. The detailed contributions are highlighted as follows.

c1)

Towards online kernel adaptation, the present work advocates an incremental (I) approach based on a weighted ensemble (E) of GP learners with scalable RF-based kernel approximations. The novel IE-GP learns the unknown function and jointly adapts to the appropriate EGP kernel on-the-fly.

c2)

To cope with learning nonstationary functions, dynamic IE-GP variants have been devised to capture structured dynamics at the EGP meta-learner and individual GP learners via a hidden Markov model and the state-space models, respectively.

c3)

To account for data being adversarially chosen in the online setting, the performances of IE-GP and its dynamic variant are compared with some benchmark functions with data in hindsight via static and switching regret analyses. In both cases, the cumulative regrets over
T
slots are of order
\mathcal{O}(\log T)
, implying no regret on average.

c4)

Complementary to the supervised function learning task, online unsupervised learning for dimensionality reduction (a.k.a. latent variable model) is investigated under the proposed IE-GP paradigm.

c5)

Extensive experimental results are provided to validate the merits of the proposed methods in regression, classification and dimensionality reduction tasks.

Relative to the conference precursor
[
40
]
, the novelty lies in the following four aspects: 1) A switching (S) IE-GP approach is devised by modeling dynamics at the EGP meta-learner via a first-order Markov chain; 2) The performance of the proposed SIE-GP is analysed via the notion of switching regret; 3) EGP-based online unsupervised learning with RFs for scalability is further explored; 4) Experimental section has been significantly expanded via the inclusion of classification and dimensionality reduction tests.

\displaystyle=p(y|{\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}}_{\mathbf{v}}^{m\top}(\mathbf{x}){\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m})\;.

(14b)

Focusing on the incremental (I) setting, each expert

based on

, and update of the parameter posterior upon the arrival of

per slot.
To assess the per-expert contribution, the EGP meta-learner relies on the posterior probability
w_{t}^{m}:={\rm Pr}(i=m|\mathbf{y}_{t};\mathbf{X}_{t})
. As we shall see next, the resultant IE-GP proceeds in two steps, namely prediction and correction, by propagating per-expert weights and posterior pdfs
\{w_{t}^{m},{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})\}_{m=1}^{M}
from slot to slot.

Prediction.

Upon receiving
\mathbf{x}_{t+1}
, each expert
m
constructs the RF vector using
{\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}}_{\mathbf{v}}^{m}(\mathbf{x}_{t+1})
as in (

{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})
available from slot

y_{t+1}
can be obtained by invoking the sum-product probability rule

\displaystyle{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},i=m,\mathbf{X}_{t+1})\!\!=\!\!\int\!\!p(y_{t+1}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}\!,\mathbf{x}_{t})p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}.

(15)

Leveraging again the sum-product rule, the EGP meta-learner seeks the ensemble predictive pdf as

\displaystyle{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!\!{\rm Pr}(i\!=\!m|\mathbf{y}_{t},\!\mathbf{X}_{t}){p}(y_{t+1}\!|\mathbf{y}_{t},\!i\!=\!m,\!\mathbf{X}_{t+1}\!)

\displaystyle=\!\!\sum_{m=1}^{M}w_{t}^{m}{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\!i\!=\!m,\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

(16)

which takes an intuitive form as a weighted combination of predictions from the individual GP experts.
Having available the predictive pdf, we are ready to update the posterior pdf of the RF model parameter vector.

Correction.

With the arrival of
y_{t+1}
, each expert
m

{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}
via Bayes’ rule as

{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t+1},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})=\frac{{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t}\!){p}(y_{t+1}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m},\mathbf{x}_{t+1})}{{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},i=m,\mathbf{X}_{t+1})}

(17)

where
{p}(y_{t+1}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m},\mathbf{x}_{t+1})
is the known likelihood (cf. (
14b
)), and
{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})
is available from slot
t
. For later use, the so-termed Bayesian loss incurred by expert
m
at slot
t+1
is (cf.
[
43
]
)

\displaystyle l_{t+1|t}^{m}:=-\log{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},i=m,\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

(18)

whose ensemble version is given by

\displaystyle\ell_{t+1|t}

\displaystyle:=-\log{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

\displaystyle\ =-\log\!\!\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!\!w_{t}^{m}\exp\!\left(-l_{t+1|t}^{m}\!\right).

(19)

Simultaneously, the EGP meta-leaner obtains the updated weight
w_{t+1}^{m}:={\rm Pr}(i=m|\mathbf{y}_{t+1},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})
as

\displaystyle w_{t+1}^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm Pr}(i=m|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t}){p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},i=m,\mathbf{X}_{t+1})}{{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})}

\displaystyle=w_{t}^{m}\exp(\ell_{t+1|t}-l_{t+1|t}^{m})

(20)

where
w_{t}^{m}
is available from slot

. Intuitively, large

implies small

, and thus

relative to the rest will be smaller than that at slot

3-B

Laplace approximation for non-Gaussian likelihood

When the likelihood is non-Gaussian as in classification, Poisson regression or ordinal regression, the per-expert parameter posterior is no longer available in closed form. Fortunately, the so-termed Laplace approximation
[
44
]
can be leveraged to carry out the prediction and correction steps with tractability in IE-GP. Specifically, each expert maintains a Gaussian approximant for the parameter vector as
p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})\approx\mathcal{N}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m};\hat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}}^{m}_{t},{\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}_{t}^{m})
, which readily allows the predicted pdf of
y_{t+1}
(
15
) to be calculated via Monte-Carlo sampling, or through probit approximation for binary classification with the logistic likelihood; see, e.g.,
[
45
, Chapter 8.4.4.2]
.

Given
y_{t+1}
, the EGP meta-learner updates the per-expert weight as in (
20
) with the per-expert loss at hand from the prediction step. Also, each expert relies on Laplace approximation to seek an updated Gaussian approximant as

{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t+1},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})\approx\mathcal{N}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m};\hat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}}_{t+1}^{m},{\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}^{m}_{t+1})
,
where
\hat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}}^{m}_{t+1}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}^{m}_{t+1}
are respectively the mode of the log posterior and the corresponding information matrix inverse, that can be obtained by solving the following optimization problem using Newton’s iteration
[
45
]

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\hat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}}_{t+1}^{m}=\underset{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}}{\arg\max}\log{p}({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})+\log p(y_{t+1}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m},\mathbf{x}_{t+1})

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt({\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}^{m}_{t+1})^{-1}=({\mbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}}^{m}_{t})^{-1}\!\!-\!\nabla^{2}_{\theta^{m}}\log p(y_{t+1}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}\!\!,\mathbf{x}_{t+1})\Big\rvert_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}=\hat{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}}_{t+1}^{m}}.

4
Regret analysis

The pdf
{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

, from which its mean and variance can be also obtained (even in closed form, cf. (

f

of these pdfs (the norm in adversarial settings), regret analysis is well motivated along the lines of online convex optimization

To this end, let
\mathcal{L}(f(\mathbf{x}_{\tau});y_{\tau}):=-\log p(y_{\tau}|f(\mathbf{x}_{\tau}))
be the per-slot negative log-likelihood (NLL). For any fixed function estimator
\hat{f}^{*}(\cdot)
, the incurred loss over
T

13
), the best function estimate (benchmark) with data

available in hindsight, are obtained with the optimal weights

in the EGP prior by maximizing the batch function posterior,

, as

\displaystyle(\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{T},\{\hat{w}^{m}\})=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{f}_{T},\{w^{m}\}\\
\sum_{m}w^{m}=1\end{subarray}}{\arg\max}p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|\mathbf{f}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})\!\!\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!w^{m}p(\mathbf{f}_{T}|i\!=\!m,\mathbf{X}_{T})

whose solution is
\hat{w}^{m^{*}}=1
and
\hat{w}^{m}=0
for

. This implies that only one GP expert
m^{*}
is active in the benchmark function estimate for
\tau=1,\ldots,T
. The optimal estimate by expert
m^{*}
are then given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{f}}_{T}=\underset{\mathbf{f}_{T}}{\arg\max}\ \ p(\mathbf{f}_{T}|i=m^{*},\mathbf{X}_{T})p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|\mathbf{f}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})\;.

(29)

As every positive semidefinite kernel
\kappa^{m}

is associated with a unique RHKS

[
14
]
, the optimal function estimator

is extracted from (

\displaystyle m^{*}\in

\displaystyle\>\underset{m\in\mathcal{M}}{\arg\min}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}{\mathcal{L}}({\hat{f}}^{m}(\mathbf{x}_{\tau});y_{\tau})+\frac{1}{2}\|{\hat{f}}^{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m}}^{2}

where the optimal function estimator per expert

\displaystyle{\hat{f}}^{m}(\cdot)\in

\displaystyle\>\underset{f^{m}\in\mathcal{H}^{m}}{\arg\min}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}{\mathcal{L}}(f^{m}(\mathbf{x}_{\tau});y_{\tau})+\frac{1}{2}\|f^{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m}}^{2}\>.

(as1)

5
EGP for dynamic learning

In the proposed IE-GP, the EGP meta-learner relies on the posterior probability of a
static
random variable
i
to assess contributions of the GP experts, each of which models the learning function via the
time-invariant
parameter vector
{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}
. Such a stationary setting implies that IE-GP handles no dynamics in the unknown function. This is also manifested in Sec.
4
that with batch data in hindsight the optimal function estimate is associated with one of the GP experts (cf. (
29
)). To further enable learning for dynamic functions, the rest of this section will explore extensions to accommodate
time-varying

i_{t}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}_{t}
for the EGP meta-learner and individual GP learners, respectively.

5-A

Dynamics at EGP meta-learner

Capitalizing on time-dependent
i_{t}\in\mathcal{M}
to denote the index of the contributing expert, the EGP meta-learner models the evolution of
i_{t}
via a Markov chain with prior transition probability
q_{mm^{\prime}}:={\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}=m|i_{t}=m^{\prime})
for
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
. Such a dynamic model allows the learning function to jump among the candidate spaces associated with the GP experts, yielding the so-termed switching (S) IE-GP hereafter. The values of
\{q_{mm^{\prime}}\}_{m,m^{\prime}}\in[0,1]
are user-defined parameters. It is worth pointing out that IE-GP can be regarded as a special case of SIE-GP with
q_{mm}=1
.

The novel SIE-GP differs from IE-GP in the weight update. To illustrate this, the per-expert posterior weight in SIE-GP is first adapted as
w_{t|t}^{m}:={\rm Pr}(i_{t}=m|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})
given time-varying
i_{t}
. Before propagating to
w_{t+1|t+1}^{m}
, the EGP meta-learner leverages the aforementioned Markov transition model to predict the weight for GP model
m
at slot
t+1
via
w_{t+1|t}^{m}:={\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}\!=\!m|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})
, which, with
\{w_{t|t}^{m}\}
available, can be obtained as

\displaystyle w_{t+1|t}^{m}

\displaystyle=\!\!\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{M}\!{\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}=m,i_{t}=m^{\prime}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})

\displaystyle=\!\!\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{M}{\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}\!=\!m|i_{t}\!=\!m^{\prime},\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t}){\rm Pr}(i_{t}\!=\!m^{\prime}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t})

\displaystyle=\!\!\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{M}q_{m,m^{\prime}}w_{t|t}^{m^{\prime}}

where the last equality holds since the evolution of
i_{t}
is independent from
\mathbf{y}_{t}
and
\mathbf{X}_{t}
. With non-zero
\{q_{mm^{\prime}}\}
, the prediction rule in (

) allows for activation of the previously inactive expert

(
w_{t|t}^{m}\approx 0
), thereby accommodating switching among candidate GP models or function spaces.

With the predicted weights (
35
) and the per-expert predictive pdf (

y_{t+1}

\displaystyle{p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t+1})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}{p}(y_{t+1},i_{t+1}=m|\mathbf{y}_{t},\!\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}{\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}=m|\mathbf{y}_{t},\mathbf{X}_{t}){p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},i_{t+1}\!=\!m,\!\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}w_{t+1|t}^{m}\ {p}(y_{t+1}|\mathbf{y}_{t},i_{t+1}\!=\!m,\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

(36)

where

Switching regret analysis

With the underlying assumption that the active model per slot changes over time, the notion of switching or shifting regret
[
11

with data in hindsight, yielding the switching regret defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\rm SW}(T):=\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau|\tau-1}^{\rm SW}-\underset{i_{1},\ldots,i_{T}}{\min}\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\mathcal{L}(\hat{f}^{i_{\tau}}(\mathbf{x}_{\tau});y_{\tau})

(39)

where, as in static regret (
31

(as4)

5-B

Dynamics within each GP expert

The aforementioned SIE-GP accounts for dynamics of expert switching at the EGP meta-leaner. To further handle a dynamic learning function within each expert

8
Conclusions

This paper put forth an incremental scheme that leverages an ensemble of scalable RF-based parametric GP learners to jointly infer the unknown function along with its performance, and a data-driven kernel combination. Dynamic function learning was enabled through modeling structured dynamics for the EGP meta-learner and individual GP learners. On the theoretical aspect, regret analysis was conducted to benchmark even in adversarial settings the novel IE-GP and its dynamic variant relative to benchmark strategies with data in hindsight. Further, EGP-based latent variable model is devised for online kernel-adaptive dimensionality reduction. Extensive experimental results are provided to illustrate the superior performance of the novel IE-GP schemes.

Proof of Lemma 1

To prove Lemma 1, we will first upper bound the cumulative online Bayesian loss associated with IE-GP,
\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau|\tau-1}
, relative to that incurred by any RF-based GP expert
m
, namely
\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{m}

20

\tau=1
to
T

\exp(-\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau|\tau-1}+\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{m})=1/(Mw_{T}^{m})

yielding

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau|\tau-1}\!-\!\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{m}\!=\log M\!+\!\log w_{T}^{m}\!\overset{(a)}{\leq}\!\log M

(56)

where
(a)
holds because

Next, we will bound the difference between
\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{m}

and the cumulative loss incurred by a fixed strategy
{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}_{*}

m\in\mathcal{M}

Upon defining the cumulative loss over

\theta

, the expected cumulative loss over any pdf
q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})
is
[
43
]

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\theta}}:=\mathbb{E}_{q}[\mathcal{L}_{\theta}]=\int_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}}q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\mathcal{L}_{\theta}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}\;.

On the other hand, the following equality holds for the cumulative online Bayesian loss based on Bayes’ rule

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau|\tau-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}-\log p(y_{\tau}|\mathbf{y}_{\tau-1},\mathbf{X}_{\tau})=-\log p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|\mathbf{X}_{T})\;.

Let

with the variational parameter

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\theta}}=\int q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\log\frac{p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}},\mathbf{X}_{T})}{p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|\mathbf{X}_{T})}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\int q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\log\frac{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}|\mathbf{y}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})}{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}

\displaystyle=\int q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\log\frac{q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})}{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}-\int q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\log\frac{q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})}{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}|\mathbf{y}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}\ \

\displaystyle=\!{\rm KL}(q(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\|p(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\!)\!-\!{\rm KL}(q(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\|p(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}|\mathbf{y}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})\!)\!\overset{(b)}{\leq}\!{\rm KL}(q(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\|p(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\!)

\displaystyle=2n_{\text{RF}}\log\sigma_{\theta}+\frac{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}_{*}\|^{2}+2n_{\text{RF}}\xi^{2}}{2\sigma_{\theta}^{2}}\!-\!n_{\text{RF}}\!-\!2n_{\text{RF}}\log\xi

(57)

where
(a)

{\rm KL}(q(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})\|p(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}|\mathbf{y}_{T},\!\mathbf{X}_{T})\!)\geq 0
.

The last step towards bounding
\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{*}}
is to establish an upper bound for
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\theta}}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{*}}
. To this end,
let

and

around

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(z_{\tau};y_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(z_{\tau}^{*};y_{\tau})+\frac{d\mathcal{L}(z_{\tau}^{*};y_{\tau})}{dz_{\tau}}(z_{\tau}-z_{\tau}^{*})

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\frac{d^{2}}{dz_{\tau}^{2}}\mathcal{L}(h(z_{\tau});y_{\tau})\frac{(z_{\tau}-z_{\tau}^{*})^{2}}{2}

(58)

where

is some function lying between
z_{\tau}
and
z_{\tau}^{*}
. Taking the expectation of (

q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}})
, leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{q}[\mathcal{L}(z_{\tau};y_{\tau})]\!-\!\mathcal{L}(z_{\tau}^{*};y_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dz_{\tau}^{2}}\mathcal{L}(h(z_{\tau});y_{\tau})\frac{(z_{\tau}-z_{\tau}^{*})^{2}}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}c\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(z_{\tau}-z_{\tau}^{*})^{2}}{2}\right]\overset{(b)}{\leq}\frac{c\xi^{2}}{2}

where

Summing (

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\theta}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\theta_{*}}+\frac{Tc\xi^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Tc\xi^{2}}{2}\!+\!2n_{\text{RF}}\log\sigma_{\theta}\!+\!\frac{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}_{*}\|^{2}+2n_{\text{RF}}\xi^{2}}{2\sigma_{\theta}^{2}}\!-\!n_{\text{RF}}\!-\!2n_{\text{RF}}\log\xi\;.

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau}^{m}\!-\!\!\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\mathcal{L}\!\left(\!{\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}}^{m\top}_{\mathbf{v}}\!(\mathbf{x}_{\tau}\!){\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}_{*};y_{\tau}\!\right)\!\leq\!\frac{\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}_{*}\|^{2}}{2\sigma_{\theta^{m}}^{2}}\!+\!n_{\text{RF}}\log\!\left(\!1\!+\!\frac{Tc\sigma_{\theta^{m}}^{2}}{2n_{\text{RF}}}\!\right)

\bar{w}_{t+1}^{i_{t+1}}=w_{t+1|t}^{i_{t+1}}
by induction. Towards this, we will first leverage (
70
) to obtain

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{t}(\mathbf{i}_{T})\propto\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{T})\exp\left(\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}-l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{i_{\tau}}\right)

based on which, (
72
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\bar{w}_{t+1}^{i_{t+1}}

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}}\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{t+1})\exp\left(\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}-l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{i_{\tau}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{t}}\frac{\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{t+1})}{\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{t})}e^{-l_{t|t-1}^{i_{t}}}\hskip-11.38092pt\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t-1}}\hskip-8.5359pt\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{t})\exp\left(\sum_{\tau=1}^{t-1}-l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{i_{\tau}}\right)

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{i_{t}}{\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}|i_{t})\exp\left(-l_{t|t-1}^{i_{t}}\right)\bar{w}_{t}^{i_{t}}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\sum_{i_{t}}{\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}|i_{t})\exp\left(-l_{t|t-1}^{i_{t}}\right){w}_{t|t-1}^{i_{t}}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\propto}\sum_{i_{t}}{\rm Pr}(i_{t+1}|i_{t})w_{t|t}^{i_{t}}\overset{(c)}{=}w_{t+1|t}^{i_{t+1}}

(73)

where
(a)
follows due to the induction assumption that
\bar{w}_{t}^{i_{t}}=w_{t|t-1}^{i_{t}}
; and,
(b)
and
(c)
are based on (
38
) and (
35
), respectively.

With
\bar{\ell}_{t+1|t}={\ell}_{t+1|t}^{\rm SW}
being established according to (
73
), multiplying (
70
) from
t=1
to
T
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau+1|\tau}^{\rm SW}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau+1|\tau}^{i_{\tau+1}}

\displaystyle=\log\bar{w}_{T}(\mathbf{i}_{T})-\log\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{T})

\displaystyle\leq-\log\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{T})\;.

(74)

Since
\bar{w}_{0}(\mathbf{i}_{T})\!=\!{\rm Pr}(i_{1})\prod_{\tau=2}^{T}{\rm Pr}(i_{\tau}|i_{\tau-1})\!=\!\frac{1}{M}q_{0}^{T-s}q_{1}^{s}
with
s
denoting the number of switches in
\mathbf{i}_{T}
, (
74
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau+1|\tau}^{\rm SW}\!\!-\!\!\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau+1|\tau}^{i_{\tau+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\log\!M\!-\!(T\!-\!s)\log\!q_{0}\!-\!s\log q_{1}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\log\!M\!-\!T\log\!q_{0}\!+\!S\!\log\frac{q_{0}}{1-q_{0}}

(75)

where the inequality in
(a)
results from
s\leq S
and
q_{0}\geq q_{1}=1-q_{0}
based on (as4)-(as5). As the RHS of (
75
) is a convex function of
q_{0}
, the following holds true upon setting it to its minimal value taken at
q_{0}^{*}=(T-S)/T

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\ell_{\tau+1|\tau}^{\rm SW}\!-\!\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau+1|\tau}^{i_{\tau+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\log M\!-\!S\log\frac{S}{T}\!+\!(T\!-\!S)\log\frac{T}{T-S}

which, upon leveraging
(T-S)\log\frac{T}{T-S}\leq(T-S)\frac{S}{T-S}=S
for

, yields Lemma 2.

Proof of Lemma 3

For any sequence
\mathbf{i}_{T}

{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}:=\{{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}^{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\Theta}}^{\mathbf{i}_{T}}

\displaystyle:=\!\!\int\!\!\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}^{\mathbf{i}_{T}}q({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}})d{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\!=\!\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\!\int\!\!\mathcal{L}({\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}}^{i_{\tau}\top}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{x}_{\tau}){\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{i_{\tau}};y_{\tau})q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{i_{\tau}})d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{i_{\tau}}

which can be re-expressed as
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\Theta}}^{\mathbf{i}_{T}}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\Theta}}^{m}
, where

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\Theta}}^{m}:=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{m}}\int\mathcal{L}({\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}}^{m\top}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{x}_{\tau}){\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m};y_{\tau})q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m})d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}

with
\mathcal{T}_{m}
collecting the
T_{m}
slot indices when GP model from expert
m
is in action, that is,
\mathcal{T}_{m}:=\{\tau|i_{\tau}\!=\!m,1\!\leq\!\tau\!\leq\!T\}
.

On the other hand, the cumulative online loss for any
\mathbf{i}_{T}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l^{i_{\tau}}_{\tau|\tau-1}\!\!=\!\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\!-\!\log p(y_{\tau}|\mathbf{y}_{\tau-1},i_{\tau},\!\mathbf{X}_{\tau})\!=\!-\log p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|\mathbf{i}_{T},\!\mathbf{X}_{T}).

)

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{i_{\tau}}-\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{q_{\Theta}}^{\mathbf{i}_{T}}=\int q({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}})\log\frac{p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}},\mathbf{i}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})}{p(\mathbf{y}_{T}|\mathbf{i}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}

\displaystyle=\int q({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}})\log\frac{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}|\mathbf{y}_{T},\mathbf{i}_{T},\mathbf{X}_{T})}{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}})}d{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\int q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m})\left(\log\frac{q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m})}{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m})}-\log\frac{q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m})}{p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}|\mathbf{y}^{m},\mathbf{X}^{m})}\right)d{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}KL(q({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m})\|p({\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{m}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!\!\left(\!2n_{\text{RF}}\!\log\frac{\sigma_{\theta^{m}}}{\xi_{m}}\!+\!\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\theta}^{2}}\left(\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}_{*}^{m}\|^{2}\!+\!2n_{\text{RF}}\xi_{m}^{2}\right)\!-\!n_{\text{RF}}\!\right).

the sum of which over
m
in conjunction with (
76
) results in

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}l_{\tau|\tau-1}^{i_{\tau}}-\sum_{\tau=1}^{T}\mathcal{L}({\mbox{\boldmath$\phi$}}^{i_{\tau}\top}_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{x}_{\tau}){\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}^{i_{\tau}}_{*};y_{\tau})\leq

(77)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\!\!\!\left(\!2n_{\text{RF}}\!\log\frac{\sigma_{\theta^{m}}}{\xi_{m}}\!+\!\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\theta}^{2}}\left(\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\theta$}}_{*}^{m}\|^{2}\!+\!2n_{\text{RF}}\xi_{m}^{2}\right)\!-\!n_{\text{RF}}+\!\frac{T_{m}c\xi_{m}^{2}}{2}\!\right).

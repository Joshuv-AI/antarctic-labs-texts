On 3D Hall-MHD equations with fractional Laplacians: global well-posedness
Huali Zhang, Kun Zhao

Abstract
. The Cauchy problem for 3D incompressible Hall-magnetohydrodynamics (Hall-MHD) system with fractional Laplacians ( − Δ ) 1 2 (-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}} is studied. The well-posedness of 3D incompressible Hall-MHD equations remains an open problem with fractional diffusion ( − Δ ) 1 2 (-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}} . First, global well-posedness of small-energy solutions with general initial data in H s H^{s} , s > 5 2 s>\frac{5}{2} , is proved. Second, a special class of large-energy initial data is constructed, with which the Cauchy problem is globally well-posed. The proofs rely upon a new global bound of energy estimates involving Littlewood-Paley decomposition and Sobolev inequalities, which enables one to overcome the 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -order derivative loss of the magnetic field.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

This paper is oriented towards the Cauchy problem for 3D incompressible Hall-MHD equations with fractional Laplacians:

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla P-\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{b}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\beta}\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\times\left(\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}\right)\times\mathbf{b}\right)=\mathbf{0},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0;\\
\mathbf{u}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0},\ \ \mathbf{b}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{b}_{0},\end{cases}

(1.1)

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t>0
. The object is to identify initial conditions under which (
1.1
) is globally well-posed.

1.2.
Background

The classical 3D magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) equations with magnetic diffusion:

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{t}+(-\Delta)\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla P-\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{b}_{t}+(-\Delta)\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0,\end{cases}

(1.2)

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t>0
, describe the macroscopic behavior of electrically conducting incompressible fluids in a magnetic field. Here, the unknown functions
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
P=P(\mathbf{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
denote, respectively, the fluid velocity, pressure, and magnetic field.

In magnetic fusion theory, if a so-called “Hall current” perpendicular to the magnetic field is present in the equation for the current and if the magnetic field is very large, then the gyrofrequency of electrons greatly exceeds the average frequency of electron collision. This reflects that the Hall current effect has a greater influence on MHD fluids than the electrical resistivity does. However, in ideal MHD theory, the Hall-current term is routinely neglected in order to simplify the underlying mathematical analysis. In the 1960 paper entitled “
Studies on Magnetohydrodynamic Waves and Other Anisotropic Wave Motions
”
[
29
]
, M.J. Lighthill criticized the neglect of the Hall term in ideal MHD theory, whose opinion implies that such a negligence breaks down the ideal MHD theory near the boundary region of any high-density plasma. As a remedy, Lighthill brought the Hall term back to the classical MHD equations, resulting in the standard Hall-MHD equations:

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{t}+(-\Delta)\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla P-\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{b}_{t}+(-\Delta)\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla\times\left(\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}\right)\times\mathbf{b}\right)=\mathbf{0},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0.\end{cases}

(1.3)

Comparing with the classical MHD equations, (
1.2

1.3

1.3.
Motivation

We observe that all of the previous studies on (
1.1
) are concerned with the case when
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
. Since smaller
\beta
generates weaker magnetic diffusion, analysis of (
1.1
) when
\beta\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
is more challenging than the case when
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
. As a matter of fact, even in the case when
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
, well-posedness (local or global) of classical solutions to (
1.1
) still remains open. Main difficulty stems from the
\frac{1}{2}
-order regularity loss of the magnetic field. To explain the difficulty, let us consider the simple situation when
\mathbf{u}\equiv\mathbf{0}
. In this case, the equation for the magnetic field reads

\mathbf{b}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}+\nabla\times\left(\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}\right)\times\mathbf{b}\right)=\mathbf{0}.

By standard commutator estimates, we can show that

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\\
\lesssim\ &\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Lambda^{s}((\nabla\times\mathbf{b})\times\mathbf{b})\cdot\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\times\mathbf{b})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\big|+\text{lower order terms,}\\
\lesssim\ &\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\Lambda^{s}((\nabla\times\mathbf{b})\times\mathbf{b})-\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\times\mathbf{b})\times\mathbf{b}\big)\cdot\Lambda^{s}(\nabla\times\mathbf{b})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\big|+\text{lower order terms,}\\
\lesssim\ &\|\mathbf{b}\|_{H^{s}}\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{H^{s}}+\text{lower order terms},\end{split}

where
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
s>0
. From above calculations we see that a global energy bound of the solution can not be achieved if one remains in the
H^{s}
-framework. However, if one could manage to show that

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\text{lower order terms},\end{split}

then a global energy bound can be achieved at least in the regime of small-energy solutions. Motivated by such an observation, we set our goal of this paper to identify initial conditions under which classical solutions to (
1.1
) with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
are globally well-posed.

Preliminaries

We first introduce some commutator estimates.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{H},\mathbf{X},\mathbf{W}
be divergence free vectorfields in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For
j\geq-1

I=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[{\Delta}_{j},\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla]\mathbf{X}\cdot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{W}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle|I|\lesssim\

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{X}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\mathbf{X}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}}{\sum_{k\geq j-1}}2^{j-k}\|{{\Delta}}_{k}\mathbf{X}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.1)

Proof.

By phase decomposition, we first write
I
as

, where

\begin{split}&I_{1}=\sum_{|k-j|\leq 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left({\Delta}_{j}(S_{k-1}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla{\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{X})-S_{k-1}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla{\Delta}_{j}{\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{X}\right)\cdot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{W}\mathrm{d}{\bf x},\\
&I_{2}=\sum_{|k-j|\leq 2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left({\Delta}_{j}({\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla S_{k-1}\mathbf{X})-{\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla{\Delta}_{j}S_{k-1}\mathbf{X}\right)\cdot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{W}\mathrm{d}{\bf x},\\
&I_{3}=\sum_{k\geq j-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big({\Delta}_{j}({\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla{\tilde{\Delta}}_{k}\mathbf{X})-{\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla\Delta_{j}{\tilde{\Delta}}_{k}\mathbf{X}\big)\cdot{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{W}\mathrm{d}{\bf x},\end{split}

where

and
{\tilde{\Delta}}_{k}:={\Delta}_{k-1}+{\Delta}_{k}+{\Delta}_{k+1}
. Using Hölder’s inequality and commutator estimate, we can show that

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\sum_{|k-j|\leq 2}}\|\nabla S_{k-1}\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{k}\mathbf{X}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta_{j}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{j}\mathbf{X}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta_{j}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.2)

Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim{\sum_{|k-j|\leq 2}}\|\nabla S_{k-1}\mathbf{X}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta_{k}\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{j}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\mathbf{X}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta_{j}\mathbf{H}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta_{j}\mathbf{W}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.3)

Since

I_{3}=\sum_{k\geq j-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[\Delta_{j},\Delta_{k}\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla]\tilde{\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{X}\cdot\Delta_{j}\mathbf{W}\mathrm{d}{\bf x},

Substituting (

(2.6)

Combining (

|\bm{\xi}|\leq 1-2\varepsilon
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}({\bf x},t)=\alpha_{1}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left(e^{-|\bm{\xi}|^{2\alpha}t}\gamma(\bm{\xi})\right)\ast\mathbf{v}_{0},

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}({\bf x},t)=\alpha_{2}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left(e^{-|\bm{\xi}|t}\gamma(\bm{\xi})\right)\ast\mathbf{v}_{0},

(2.11)

where
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\cdot)
denotes the inverse Fourier transform. Since
\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{0}
, then we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{U})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbf{B}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbf{B}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}.

By Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{U})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbf{B}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}\|_{W^{s+\frac{1}{2},\infty}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbf{B}\|_{W^{s+\frac{1}{2},\infty}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}.

(2.12)

Using the information about the support of
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{B}
(pertaining to the support of
\mathbf{v}_{0}
), we can show that

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{U}\|_{W^{s+\frac{1}{2},\infty}}+\|\mathbf{B}\|_{W^{s+\frac{1}{2},\infty}}&\lesssim\gamma(\bm{\xi})\cdot|\bm{\xi}|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot(\|\widehat{\mathbf{U}}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}}+\|\widehat{\mathbf{B}}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}})\\
&\lesssim\gamma(\bm{\xi})\cdot(\alpha_{1}e^{-|\bm{\xi}|^{2\alpha}t}+\alpha_{2}e^{-|\bm{\xi}|t})\cdot|\bm{\xi}|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}}\\
&\lesssim(e^{-\frac{t}{2^{2\alpha}}}+e^{-\frac{t}{2}})\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we can show that

\begin{split}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\nabla\times\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}&\lesssim\gamma(\bm{\xi})\cdot(|\bm{\xi}|-1)^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\|\widehat{\mathbf{B}}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}+\|\widehat{\mathbf{U}}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}})\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(e^{-\frac{t}{2^{2\alpha}}}+e^{-\frac{t}{2}})\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}.\end{split}

Substituting the above inequalities into (
2
), we obtain

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{U}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{U})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}+\|\mathbf{B}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(e^{-\frac{t}{2^{2\alpha}}}+e^{-\frac{t}{2}})^{2}\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}}.\end{split}

(2.13)

Hence, (
2.9
) is proved.

To show (
2.10
), we observe that since
\text{supp}\,\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}\subseteq\text{supp}\,\widehat{\mathbf{U}}+\text{supp}\,\widehat{\mathbf{B}}
, it holds that

\text{supp}\,\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}\subseteq\left\{\bm{\xi}:|\bm{\xi}|\leq 2+2\varepsilon\right\},\ 0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(2.14)

Hence, we have

\|\nabla\times(\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\|\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}.

(2.15)

By a direct calculation, we can show that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}t}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta})\times e^{-|\bm{\eta}|t}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\eta})\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-t(|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}+|\bm{\eta}|)}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\eta})\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}.

(2.16)

On the other hand, using the convolution theorem, we can show that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-|\tilde{\bm{\eta}}|^{2\alpha}t}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\times e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}}|t}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{\eta}}.

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-t(|\tilde{\bm{\eta}}|^{2\alpha}+|\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}}|)}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{\eta}}.

(2.17)

Since
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})=-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\tilde{\bm{\eta}})
, we obtain from (
2
) that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-t(|\tilde{\bm{\eta}}|^{2\alpha}+|\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}}|)}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{\eta}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-t(|{\bm{\eta}}|^{2\alpha}+|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|)}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\eta})\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta}.

(2.18)

Adding (
2
) and (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-|\bm{\xi}-{\bm{\eta}}|^{2\alpha}t}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-{\bm{\eta}})\times e^{-|\tilde{\bm{\eta}}|t}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}({\bm{\eta}})\mathrm{d}{\bm{\eta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q(t,\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta})\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\eta})\mathrm{d}\bm{\eta},

(2.19)

where

Q(t,\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta}):=e^{-t(|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}+|\bm{\eta}|)}-e^{-t(|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|+|\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha})}.

Note that within the support of
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\eta})
, it holds that
|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|,|\bm{\eta}|\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]
, where
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, using Taylor’s theorem, we can show that

\displaystyle|Q(t,\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta})|\leq\

\displaystyle e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}t}\big|e^{-|\bm{\eta}|t}-e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|t}\big|+e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|t}\big|e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}t}-e^{-|\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}t}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle t\,e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}t}\big||\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|-|\bm{\eta}|\big|+C\,t\,e^{-|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|t}\big||\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}-|\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}t}\frac{\big||\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|-|\bm{\eta}|\big|}{|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}}+Ce^{-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|t}\frac{\big||\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}-|\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}\big|}{|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle(e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|^{2\alpha}t}+e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta}|t})\,\varepsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim\

\displaystyle(e^{-\frac{1}{2}\cdot{(\frac{1}{2}})^{2\alpha}t}+e^{-\frac{1}{2}\cdot{\frac{1}{2}}t})\varepsilon.

(2.20)

Integrating (
2.15
) with respect to time and using (
2
), we derive that

\begin{split}\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\nabla\times(\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\mathrm{d}t&\lesssim\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\widehat{\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B}}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}\mathrm{d}t\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q(t,\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta})\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\xi}-\bm{\eta})\times\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}(\bm{\eta})d\bm{\eta}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

By Young’s inequality and (
2
), we conclude that

\begin{split}\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\nabla\times(\mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{B})\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\mathrm{d}t&\lesssim\int^{\infty}_{0}\|Q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}}\mathrm{d}t\\
&\lesssim\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\varepsilon\big((e^{-\frac{1}{2}\cdot{(\frac{1}{2}})^{2\alpha}t}+e^{-\frac{1}{2}\cdot{\frac{1}{2}}t}\big)\mathrm{d}t\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\bm{\xi}}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{\bm{\xi}}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

&+\int^{t}_{0}\big(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau,\end{split}

where

K(t)=\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{u}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{b}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\alpha+s}\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\mathbf{b}(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using Young’s inequality for series convolution, we obtain

\begin{split}{\sum_{j\geq-1}}2^{(2s+1)j}({\sum_{k\geq j-1}}2^{j-k}\|{\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}&={\sum_{j\geq-1}}({\sum_{k\geq j-1}}2^{(j-k)(s+\frac{3}{2})}2^{(s+\frac{1}{2})k}\|{\Delta}_{k}\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\\
&\lesssim{\sum_{j\geq-1}}2^{(2s+1)j}\|{\Delta}_{j}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Consequently, we obtain

\begin{split}K(t)\lesssim&\ \|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{u}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{b}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&+\int^{t}_{0}\big(\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\big(\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

Using the Sobolev inequalities:

\begin{split}\|\nabla\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{b}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{s}},\ s>\frac{5}{2},\end{split}

and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities:

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2s}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\mathbf{b}\|^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{\alpha}{s}}\|\Lambda^{\alpha+s}\mathbf{u}\|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{s}}_{L^{2}},

(3.17)

we update the estimate of
K(t)
as

\displaystyle K(t)\lesssim\

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{u}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{s}\mathbf{b}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{b}\|_{H^{s}}\|\Lambda^{s+\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}\big(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\mathbf{b}\|_{H^{s}}\big)\big(\|\Lambda^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2\alpha}{s}}\|\Lambda^{\alpha+s}\mathbf{u}\|^{2-\frac{2\alpha}{s}}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{s}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\mathbf{b}\|^{2-\frac{1}{s}}_{L^{2}}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.18)

Set

E(t)=\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\big(\|\mathbf{u}(t)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}+\|\mathbf{b}(t)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}\big)+\int^{t}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}(\tau)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Adding (
3.1
) and (
3.1
) implies
E(t)\lesssim E(0)+E^{\frac{3}{2}}(\tau)
, from which and standard continuation argument the global bound of
E(t)
follows when
E(0)
is sufficiently small. The uniqueness of the solution follows from a routine argument and the energy estimates derive above. We omit the details for brevity.

3.2.
Local existence with small energy

After obtaining the global energy bounds, we now give a proof of local existence for small data. For (
1.1
) is a quasilinear system, so we will seek a local solution in a weaker Sobolev spaces, and then improve it’s regularity by combining the global bounds of energy. We will obtain the local existence through an approximation procedure, where the approximation procedure has been shown in
[
9
]
. For each positive integer
n
, we define

\widehat{J_{n}f}(\xi)=\chi_{B_{n}}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi),

where
B_{n}
denotes the closed ball of radius
n
centered at
0
and
\chi_{B_{n}}
denotes the characteristic functions on
{B_{n}}
. For
a\geq 0
, we denote

H^{a}_{n}=\{f\in H^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\mathrm{supp}\hat{f}\subseteq B_{n}\}.

We now seek a solution
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{b})\in H^{s}_{n}
satisfying

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}+J_{n}(J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{u})-J_{n}(J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{b})=\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{b}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\beta}\mathbf{b}+J_{n}(J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{b})-J_{n}(J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{u})\\
\ \ \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+J_{n}({\nabla\times\left(\left(\nabla\times J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{b}\right)\times J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{b}\right)})=\mathbf{0},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0;\\
\mathbf{u}|_{t=0}=J_{n}\mathbf{u}_{0},\ \ \mathbf{b}|_{t=0}=J_{n}\mathbf{b}_{0},\end{cases}

(3.19)

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the projection onto divergence-free vector fields. For each fixed integer
n\geq 1
, it is not very hard, although tedious, to verify that the right hand side of (
3.19
) satisfies the Lipschitz condition in
H^{s}_{n}
and, by Picard’s theorem, (
3.19
)
has a unique global (in time) solution. The uniqueness implies that

J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u},\quad J_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}.

and ensures the divergence-free conditions
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0
. Then, (
3.19
) is
simplified to

\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}+J_{n}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u})-J_{n}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b})=\mathbf{0},\\
\mathbf{b}_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}+J_{n}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{b})-J_{n}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u})+J_{n}({\nabla\times\left(\left(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}\right)\times\mathbf{b}\right)})=\mathbf{0},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf{b}=0;\\
\mathbf{u}|_{t=0}=J_{n}\mathbf{u}_{0},\ \ \mathbf{b}|_{t=0}=J_{n}\mathbf{b}_{0},\end{cases}

(3.20)

We denote the solution of (
3.20
) as
(\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{b}^{n})
. Set the total energy

\begin{split}E^{s}(t)[\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{b}^{n}]=&\|\mathbf{u}^{n}(t)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}+\|\mathbf{b}^{n}(t)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}+\int^{t}_{0}(\|\Lambda^{\alpha}\mathbf{u}^{n}(\tau)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}^{n}(\tau)\|^{2}_{{H}^{s}})\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

From section 3.1, we can get the following global bound

E^{s}(1)[\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{b}^{n}]\lesssim\epsilon.

(3.21)

We then find a limit of the sequence
\{(\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{b}^{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
in a appropriate functional spaces. To do that, let us now estimate the lower-order energy

E^{0}(1)[\mathbf{u}^{n}-\mathbf{u}^{m},\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}].

By (
3.20
), we only need to see the quasi-linear part

\begin{split}Q=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\nabla\times\big((\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{n})\times\mathbf{b}^{n}\big)-\nabla\times\big((\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m})\times\mathbf{b}^{m}\big)\right)\cdot(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})dx\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\times\big((\nabla\times(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}))\times\mathbf{b}^{n}\big)\cdot(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\times\big((\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m})\times(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\big)\cdot(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})dx\\
=&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big((\nabla\times(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}))\times\mathbf{b}^{n}\big)\cdot\nabla\times(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\times\big((\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m})\times(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\big)\cdot(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})dx\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\times\big((\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m})\times(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\big)\cdot(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})dx\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\cdot\nabla(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m})-(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m})\cdot\nabla(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\right\}\cdot(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})dx.\end{split}

Using Hölder’s inequality, we can derive that

\begin{split}|Q|\lesssim&\|\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m})\|_{L^{6}}\|\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\|\nabla\times\mathbf{b}^{m}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\|_{L^{4}}\|\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\lesssim&(\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}^{m}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}^{n}\|^{2}_{H^{s}})\|\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{100}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

These uniform bounds allow us to show that

\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}^{m}\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{b}^{n}\|^{2}_{H^{s}})\|\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Integrating the above inequality on
[0,t]
, we can deduce that by Gronwall’s inequality

E^{0}(1)[\mathbf{u}^{n}-\mathbf{u}^{m},\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}]\leq CE^{0}(0)[\mathbf{u}^{n}-\mathbf{u}^{m},\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}].

As a result, we have

E^{0}(1)[\mathbf{u}^{n}-\mathbf{u}^{m},\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}^{m}]\rightarrow 0,\quad\mathrm{if}\ n,m\rightarrow\infty.

Let
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{b})
be the limit. Then
E^{s}(1)[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{b}]\lesssim\epsilon.
By (
3.21
) and the interpolation inequality

\|f\|_{H^{a}}\leq\|f\|^{1-\frac{a}{s}}_{L^{2}}\|f\|^{\frac{a}{s}}_{H^{s}},\quad 0<a<s,

we further obtain the strong convergence

E^{a}(1)[\mathbf{u}^{n}-\mathbf{u},\mathbf{b}^{n}-\mathbf{b}]\rightarrow 0,n\rightarrow\infty,\quad 0<a<s.

Consequently,
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{b})\in C([0,1];H^{a})
. This strong convergence makes it easy to
check that
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{b})
satisfies the Hall-MHD equation (
1.1
). Combining the local existence and global bounds in Section 3.2 and Section 3.1 respectively, we can conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
\square

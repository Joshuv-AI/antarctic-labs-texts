Abstract
. The Motsch-Tadmor (MT) model is a variant of the Cucker-Smale model with a normalized communication weight function. The normalization poses technical challenges in analyzing the collective behavior due to the absence of conservation of momentum. We study three quantitative estimates for the discrete-time MT model considering the first-order Euler discretization. First, we provide a sufficient framework leading to the asymptotic mono-cluster flocking. The proposed framework is given in terms of coupling strength, communication weight function, and initial data. Second, we show that the continuous transition from the discrete MT model to the continuous MT model can be made uniformly in time using the finite-time convergence result and asymptotic flocking estimate. Third, we present uniform-in-time stability estimates for the discrete MT model. We also provide several numerical examples and compare them with analytical results.

Introduction

Emergent collective behaviors are ubiquitous in many biological complex systems such as aggregation of bacteria, flocking of birds, swarming of fish, synchronization of fireflies, and herding of sheep, or others. Among such systems, we are mainly interested in the flocking in which self-organized particles (agents) evolve into an ordered motion with the same velocity using limited environmental information and communication between particles. To model such flocking behaviors, Cucker and Smale
[
7
,
8
]
introduced an analytically treatable particle model that resembles the Newton-type
N
-body system with the weighted average of relative velocities as an internal force. Let us first introduce the key components of the Cucker-Smale (CS) model, which will then lead us to its variant, the Motsch-Tadmor (MT) model.

Let
x_{i}
and
v_{i}
be the spatial position and velocity of the
i
-th particle. Then, the dynamics of the CS particle are governed by the following first-order system of ordinary differential equations for
(x_{i},v_{i})
:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{i}=v_{i}\,,\quad t>0,~~i\in[N]:=\{1,\cdots,N\},\\
\displaystyle{\dot{v}}_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a(\|x_{j}-x_{i}\|)(v_{j}-v_{i})\,.\end{cases}

Here,
\kappa>0
and
N
denote the uniform coupling strength and system size, respectively, and the scalar quantity
a_{ij}=a(\|x_{j}-x_{i}\|)
represents the communication weight between the
i
-th and
j
-th particles.
For instance in
[
8
]
, the following explicit form of communication weight function was used:

a(r)=\frac{1}{(1+r^{2})^{\beta}}\quad\text{ for some }\beta\geq 0\,.

The CS model (
1.1
) has been extensively studied from various perspectives, to name a few, stochastic effects
[
1
,
14
]
, collective dynamics of an infinite CS ensemble
[
3
,
4
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
,
asymptotic flocking dynamics of the kinetic CS model

[
5
]
, time-delay
[
6
]
, general and specific network topologies
[
19
,
9
,
23
]
, rigorous mean-field limit
[
10
]
and external forcing
[
24
]
, etc. Right after Cucker-Smale’s seminal work
[
8
]
, Motsch and Tadmor
[
21
]
introduced a generalized CS model by replacing the factor
N
appearing in the momentum equation
\eqref{eq: CS}_{2}
by the sum of weights exerted on the
i
-th particle:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{i}=v_{i},\quad t>0,~~i\in[N],\\
\displaystyle{\dot{v}}_{i}=\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}(v_{j}-v_{i}),\end{cases}

where the normalized communication weight function
\phi_{ij}
is given by

\displaystyle{\phi}_{ij}:=\frac{a(\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a(\|x_{i}-x_{k}\|)}.

Then, it is easy to see that
\phi_{ij}
is non-negative, unit sum with respect to the second index, and non-symmetric in indices
i
and
j
: there may exist
i_{0},j_{0}
such that
\phi_{i_{0}j_{0}}\neq\phi_{j_{0}i_{0}}
and

\phi_{ij}\geq 0,\quad\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}=1,\quad\forall~i,j\in[N].

From now on, we call the system (
1.2
) as the Motsch-Tadmor model (see also
Section

2.1
). Compared to the vast aforementioned literature for the CS model, the emergent dynamics of (
1.2
) have been investigated only in recent literature, e.g., mono-cluster and multi-cluster flocking
[
17
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
13
]
and collective dynamics of an infinite MT ensemble
[
15
]
.

In general, to derive a numerical scheme from the continuous model, we often implement a suitable time-discretization. Given that, we employ the first-order forward Euler scheme with a time-step
h=\Delta t
, and replace derivatives
{\dot{x}}_{i}
and
{\dot{v}}_{i}
by the corresponding Newton quotients in this work:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle x_{i}(n+1)=x_{i}(n)+hv_{i}(n)\,,\quad n=0,1,\ldots,\\
\displaystyle v_{i}(n+1)=v_{i}(n)+h\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\phi}_{ij}(n)\big(v_{j}(n)-v_{i}(n)\big),\end{cases}

where the normalized communication weight
\phi_{ij}(n)
is given by

(1.4)

{\phi}_{ij}(n):=\frac{a(\|x_{i}(n)-x_{j}(n)\|)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a(\|x_{i}(n)-x_{k}(n)\|)}.

Then, it is easy to see that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\phi}_{ij}(n)=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{j\neq i}\phi_{ij}(n)\leq 1.

In this setting, the following are three questions that we address in this paper:

•

(Flocking dynamics): Does the proposed discrete model (
1.3
) exhibit emergent behaviors? More precisely, what conditions for system parameters and initial data are needed to show the emergent flocking dynamics?

•

(Uniform-in-time transition): Formally, the discrete model (
1.3
) converges to the continuous model (
1.2
) as the time-step tends to zero. Can we verify this smooth transition uniformly in time?

•

(Uniform-in-time stability): Can we bound the distance between two solutions of the discrete model (
1.3
) with that of two initial data vectors uniformly in time?

The main purpose of this paper is to provide affirmative answers to the aforementioned questions. Before we move on to the descriptions of the main results, we introduce several handy notations for the analysis. These will be used throughout the paper. For
\forall~i,j\in[N]
, we set

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle x_{i}=(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{d}),\quad v_{i}=(v_{i}^{1},\ldots,v_{i}^{d}),\quad X:=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\quad V:=\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\|x_{i}\|:=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{d}|x_{i}^{k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\|v_{i}\|:=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{d}|v_{i}^{k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
\displaystyle\Delta^{x}_{ij}:=x_{i}-x_{j},\quad\Delta^{v}_{ij}:=v_{i}-v_{j},\quad\Delta^{x}:=[\Delta^{x}_{ij}],\quad\Delta^{v}:=[\Delta^{v}_{ij}],\\
\displaystyle\|\Delta^{x}\|_{F}:=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\|\Delta^{x}_{ij}\|^{2}}\,,\quad\|\Delta^{v}\|_{F}:=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\|\Delta^{v}_{ij}\|^{2}}.\end{cases}

The main results of this paper are three-fold. First, we show that the discrete model (
1.3
) exhibits a mono-cluster flocking under a suitable set of conditions using the nonlinear functional approach. More precisely, let
(X,V)=(X(n),V(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a global solution to (
1.3
). Then, the pair of scalar-valued functionals
(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F},\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|^{2}_{F})
satisfies the following set of recursive inequalities (see
Proposition

3.4
):

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}+h\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\,,\\
\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v}(n+1)\|_{F}\leq\left[1-h\kappa\left(1-\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}N\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\right]\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\,,\end{cases}

Based on these relations, the mono-cluster flocking estimate follows (see
Theorem

3.5
): If system parameters and initial data satisfy

\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}<\frac{1}{4N\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}}=:M\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}<\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}(1-\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}Ns)ds,

then we have asymptotic mono-cluster flocking: there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\sup_{0\leq n<\infty}\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\leq M\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}e^{-C\kappa(1-\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}NM)nh},\quad\forall~n\geq 0.

Second, we are concerned with the uniform-in-time transition from the discrete model to the continuous model as
h\to 0
using a finite-time convergence result and asymptotic flocking estimate. Let
(X,V)
and
(X^{h},V^{h})
be global solutions to the continuous and discrete models, respectively, with the same initial data. Then we have for some constant
C>0
,

\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n<+\infty}\|\Delta^{x,h}(n)-\Delta^{x}(nh)\|_{F}\leq C\,,\quad\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n<+\infty}\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|=0\,,

where
\Delta^{x,h}
and
\Delta^{x}
refer to the shape discrepancies in positions for the discrete and continuum evolutions, respectively.
We refer to
Theorem

3.13
for details.

Third, we present a version of uniform-in-time stability of the discrete solution under the following sufficient conditions on initial data, coupling strength, and communication weight function:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\max\left\{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F},\|\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}\right\}<M,\\
\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}<\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}\psi(s)ds,\quad\|\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}<\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}\psi(s)ds.\end{cases}

Under the above conditions, we show that there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
only depending on initial data such that for small
h>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\right\|_{F}+\left\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\right\|_{F}

(1.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\left\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\right\|_{F}+\left\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\right\|_{F}+C(n,h)\,,

where
\lim_{h\to 0}C(n,h)=C_{0}
for all
n\geq 0
.
Throughout this paper, we refer to
\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\|_{F}
and
\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}
as a
shape discrepancy functional
(in position and velocity, respectively) of
(X(n),V(n))
and
(\overline{X}(n),\overline{V}(n))
since both measure how far the particles are from one another as an ensemble (in terms of position or velocity) at the
n
th step. If the shape discrepancy is zero, then two configurations coincide exactly, and shape discrepancy is invariant under rigid body motion.
Note that (
1.6
) is different from what is usually referred to as a stability estimate as we are considering shape discrepancy rather than the difference between two solutions. This is analogous to orbital stability in hyperbolic conservation laws; see
Section

4
for details.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we briefly discuss motivations for the MT model and recall previous results on the emergent dynamics of the continuous MT model. In
Section

3
, we study asymptotic mono-cluster flocking of the discrete MT model, and using this flocking estimate and finite-time results on the transition from the discrete model to the continuous model, we verify that the continuous transition can be made uniformly in time as the time-step tends to zero. In
Section

4
, we present the rigorous statement and proof of the uniform-in-time stability result (
1.6
).
In
Section

5
, we provide several numerical examples for the discrete MT model to illustrate the analytical results obtained in previous sections.
Section

6
concludes with a brief summary of our main results.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly review how the MT model (
1.2
) can be motivated from the Cucker-Smale model (
1.1
), and summarize previous results on the emergent behaviors of the model (
1.2
).

2.1.
From the CS model to the MT model

In this subsection, we discuss the motivation for the normalized communication weights in the MT model. For this, we begin with the CS model:

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{i}=v_{i},\quad i\in[N],\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle{\dot{v}}_{i}=\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{a_{ij}}{N}(v_{j}-v_{i}),\end{cases}

where
a_{ij}
is the pairwise communication weight depending only on the distance between particles
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
:

a_{ij}:=a(\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|),\quad\forall~i,j\in[N].

In what follows, we consider a situation in which two groups of agents are separated far apart from each other, and the number of agents occupying the region of each flocking group varies widely in size, one being significantly larger than the other. In this case, the communication weight between two agents, one in the smaller group and another in the larger group will be close to zero. Moreover, for agent
i
in the smaller group, we can observe

{\dot{v}}_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{ij}(v_{j}-v_{i})\approx\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{j\in\text{ smaller group}}a_{ij}(v_{j}-v_{i})\,,

where
N
is the total system size which is the sum of the sizes of the smaller group and the larger group. In the case of
N\gg 1
, the dynamics for such particles (agents) in a smaller group will be described as being almost static
\frac{dv_{i}}{dt}\approx 0
. Thus, the model (
1.1
) does not capture the actual phenomenon accurately. To overcome this limitation in (
1.1
), Motsch and Tadmor proposed a slightly modified variant (
1.2
), which we will refer to as the Motsch-Tadmor (MT) model throughout this paper. By normalizing the communication weight
a_{ij}
, the new resulting model can now describe the above setting more accurately compared to the CS model.
As a trade-off, the communication weight matrix
(\phi_{ij})
loses the symmetry. In the MT model, the symmetric communication weight
a_{ij}
is now replaced by the normalized one

\phi_{ij}:=\frac{a_{ij}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik}},\quad\forall~i,j\in[N]\,,

leading to the continuous MT model:

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{i}=v_{i},\quad i\in[N]\,,\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle{\dot{v}}_{i}=\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}(v_{j}-v_{i})\,.\end{cases}

Now, we apply the first-order forward Euler scheme to (
2.2
) with
h=\Delta t
: for
t=nh

({\dot{x}}_{i}(t),{\dot{v}}_{i}(t))\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Big(\frac{x_{i}(n+1)-x_{i}(n)}{h},\frac{v_{i}(n+1)-v_{i}(n)}{h}\Big)

to derive the discrete counterpart. Note that the continuous and discrete models (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) do not have obvious conserved quantities, except the total number of particles. This lack of conservation laws causes lots of technical difficulties in the analysis.

Next, we explain why the rigorous flocking analysis for the Cucker-Smale model based on energy estimates is difficult to translate to the discrete and continuuum Motsch-Tadmor models. To see this, we first begin with the flocking analysis for the CS model. We sum up
\eqref{B-0}_{2}
over all
i\in[N]
, and use the index exchange transformation
i~\leftrightarrow~j
to find

(2.3)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(a_{ij}-a_{ji})(v_{j}-v_{i}).

Then, we use the symmetry
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
to derive the conservation of total momentum:

(2.4)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}=0,\quad t>0.

Secondly, one can take the inner product
\eqref{B-0}_{2}
with
2v_{i}
and sum up the resulting relation over
i
using the symmetry
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
to derive an energy dissipation estimate:

(2.5)

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v_{i}\|^{2}\Big)=-\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\|v_{j}-v_{i}\|^{2}\leq 0.

These estimates imply exponential velocity alignment for the fluctuation around the average initial velocity under suitable conditions on
a
and the initial configuration: there exists a positive constant
\Lambda
such that

(2.6)

\Big\|v_{i}(t)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}(0)\Big\|\lesssim e^{-\Lambda t}\Big\|v_{i}(0)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}v_{j}(0)\Big\|,\quad\mbox{as $t\to\infty$},\quad\forall~i\in[N].

For details on how to derive this exponential decay from (
2.5
) see

[
11
]
.
Obviously, this exponential velocity alignment also implies spatial cohesiveness:

(2.7)

\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\Big\|x_{i}(t)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}(0)\Big\|<\infty,\quad\forall~i\in[N].

Next, we return to the MT model (
2.2
). Unlike the CS model, the MT model does not preserve the total momentum due to the non-symmetric property of
\phi
: in general,

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}(t)\neq 0,\quad t>0.

Hence, these energy estimates cannot be used in the MT model. This is one of the main technical difficulties for flocking analysis. Before we close this subsection, we list several assumptions on
a
and the time-step
h
used throughout the paper:

2.1
.

Suppose that the unnormalized communication weight
a(r)
and the time-step
h
satisfy the following structural conditions:

(1)

There exists positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

(2.8)

c_{1}\leq a(r)\leq c_{2},\quad\forall~r\geq 0.

(2)

There exists a positive Lipschitz constant
L_{a}
such that

(2.9)

|a(r_{1})-a(r_{2})|\leq L_{a}|r_{1}-r_{2}|,\quad r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0.

(3)

The time-step
h
is sufficiently small so that

0<h<\min\Big\{1,\frac{1}{\kappa}\Big\},

where
\kappa
is the coupling strength in (
1.3
).

2.2
.

Below, we comment on the conditions in Assumption
2.1
and put them in context to the current state-of-the-art. Conditions (1) and (2) can be compared to those in
[
12
]
for the original Cucker-Smale model. More precisely, in
[
12
]
the authors assumed
0<a(r)\leq 1
in order to show a uniform-in-time transition from the discrete to continuum Cucker-Smale model. In
[
14
]
, which is concerned with a stochastic version of the Cucker-Smale model,
a(r)
is just assumed to be non-negative, non-increasing, and radially symmetric.
Further, monotonicity (or being non-increasing) is a common assumption on
a(r)

[
21
,
22
,
14
]
. We can think of monotonicity as a special case of almost everywhere Lipschitz continuity. This is because a monotonic function is differentiable almost everywhere with a derivative bounded almost everywhere over a bounded domain; hence in the setting where the spatial diameter is bounded (as we will show in our flocking result), a monotone
a(r)
can be considered Lipschitz almost everywhere. Condition (2) is also required to guarantee the well-posedness of the model and can be considered a standard assumption
[
2
,
18
,
16
]
.
In some sense, Condition (1) can be viewed as corresponding to the variable all-to-all communication weight case which is always long-ranged. Whether we can relax Condition (1) as is the case for the original CS model remains a challenging open problem.
Condition (3) is not very restrictive, as it is always possible to take small enough time steps
h
.

2.2.
Previous results

In this subsection, we review the emergent dynamics of the continuous MT model (
2.2
) for comparison with the new flocking estimate for the discrete MT model in the next section. Although the energy estimates for the MT model cannot be employed due to a lack of conservation laws as explained in the previous subsection, we can still use the nonlinear functional approach as applied in
[
10
]
.

For a given configuration
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}
, we define spatial and velocity diameters:

{\mathcal{D}}(X):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|,\quad{\mathcal{D}}(V):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\|v_{i}-v_{j}\|.

Next, we recall the concept of the mono-cluster flocking as follows.

2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}:=\{(x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a global solution to (
2.2
). Then, configuration
{\mathcal{Z}}
exhibits an asymptotic mono-cluster flocking if and only if the following two conditions hold.

(2.10)

\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}{\mathcal{D}}(X(t))<\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{D}}(V(t))=0.

By following calculations in
[
21
,
22
]
,
we can show these diameters satisfy a system of dissipative differential inequalities (SDDI):

(2.11)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{D}}(X(t))\Big|\leq{\mathcal{D}}(V(t)),\quad t>0,\\
\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{D}}(V(t))\leq-\kappa a({\mathcal{D}}(X(t))){\mathcal{D}}(V(t)).\end{cases}

Note that once we can show the uniform boundedness of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
, the differential inequality
\eqref{B-0-5}_{2}
yields the exponential decay of
{\mathcal{D}}(V)
. To derive the estimates (
2.7
) for the continuous MT model, we introduce nonlinear functionals
{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}
:

(2.12)

{\mathcal{H}}^{\pm}(t):={\mathcal{D}}(V(t))\pm\kappa\int_{0}^{{\mathcal{D}}(X(t))}a(\eta)d\eta,\quad t\geq 0,

where we used the following notation for initial configurations:

X^{0}:=X(0),\quad V^{0}:=V(0).

Since diameter functionals are Lipschitz continuous,
\mathcal{H}^{\pm}
is Lipschitz continuous as well. Hence, they are almost differentiable and satisfy the stability estimate:

\mathcal{H}^{\pm}(t)\leq\mathcal{H}^{\pm}(0),\quad t\geq 0,

which implies (following a similar argument as in
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
):

{\mathcal{D}}(V(t))+\kappa\int_{0}^{{\mathcal{D}}(X(t))}a(\eta)d\eta\leq{\mathcal{D}}(V^{0}),\quad t\geq 0.

Now we recall the results in
[
19
,
21
,
22
]
on the emergence of mono-cluster flocking.

2.4
.

[
19
,
21
,
22
]

Suppose that initial data and system parameters satisfy either one of the following conditions:

(1)

either

(2.13)

{\mathcal{D}}(V^{0})\leq\kappa\int_{{\mathcal{D}}(X^{0})}^{\infty}a(s)ds,

(2)

or

(2.14)

a(s)=(1+s)^{-\beta},~\beta\geq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{D}}(V^{0})\leq\frac{\kappa}{N}\int_{{\mathcal{D}}(X^{0})}^{\infty}\phi(s)ds,

and let
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}
be a global solution of (
2.2
). Then, mono-cluster flocking emerges asymptotically, i.e., there exists
{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}
such that

(2.15)

\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}{\mathcal{D}}(X(t))\leq{\mathcal{D}}^{\infty},\quad{\mathcal{D}}(V(t))\leq{\mathcal{D}}(V^{0})\exp\Big(-\kappa a({\mathcal{D}}^{\infty})t\Big),\quad\forall~t\geq 0.

2.5
.

Under the same setting as in
Theorem

2.4
, there exists a set of asymptotic velocities
\{v_{i}^{\infty}\}
such that

v_{i}^{\infty}:=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}v_{i}(t)\quad\text{ with }\quad\|v_{i}^{\infty}\|<\infty,\quad\forall~i\in[N]\,,

where

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|v_{i}(t)-v_{i}^{\infty}\|\leq\frac{N\mathcal{D}(V(0))}{a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})}\,e^{-\kappa a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})t}\,.

Proof.

Note that for all
i\in[N]
,
v_{i}(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle v_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=v_{i}(0)+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{ij}(s)(v_{j}(s)-v_{i}(s))ds\,,

where
\phi_{ij}(s):=\frac{a_{ij}(\|x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(s)\|)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik}(\|x_{i}(s)-x_{j}(s)\|)}.

Since
\phi_{ij}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|v_{i}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{i}(0)\|+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}|\phi_{ij}(s)|\left\|v_{j}(s)-v_{i}(s)\right\|ds

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{i}(0)\|+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(V(s))ds\,.

Now, we set

\displaystyle v_{i}^{\infty}

\displaystyle:=v_{i}(0)+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{ij}(s)(v_{j}(s)-v_{i}(s))ds\,.

By
Theorem

2.4
,
\mathcal{D}(V(s))
decays exponentially to zero, and so
v_{i}^{\infty}
above is well-defined. Indeed, note that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|v_{i}^{\infty}\|&\leq\|v_{i}(0)\|+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(V(s))ds\\
&\leq\|v_{i}(0)\|+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(V(0))\exp\left(-\kappa a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})s\right)ds\\
&\leq\|v_{i}(0)\|+\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\mathcal{D}(V(0))}{\kappa a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})}=\|v_{i}(0)\|+\frac{N\mathcal{D}(V(0))}{a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})}\,.\end{aligned}

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\|v_{i}^{\infty}-v_{i}(t)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left|\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t}^{\infty}\phi_{ij}(v_{j}(s)-v_{i}(s))ds\right|\leq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(V(s))ds

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(V(0))\exp(-\kappa a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty}s))ds\leq\frac{N\mathcal{D}(V(0))\exp(-\kappa a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})t)}{a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})}\,,

and so the result follows.
∎

2.6
.

Estimations in
Theorem

2.4
, and
Corollary

2.5
follow
[
22
]
, which are improved versions from the original estimates with
a^{2}
instead of
a
from
[
21
]
.

2.7
.

Thanks to the exponential decay of

derived in Theorem

, the asymptotic velocities are well-defined, and they are the same:

Note that asymptotic velocities depend on the whole particle trajectory of the initial configuration, unlike the CS model in which the asymptotic flocking velocity is completely determined by the average initial velocity.

3.
Mono-cluster flocking and uniform-in-time transition

In this section, we study the emergence of mono-cluster flocking for (

h
tends to zero.

3.1.
Preparatory lemmas

In this subsection, we derive several lemmas to be used in the proof of mono-cluster flocking.

3.1
.

Let
{\phi}_{ij}
be the communication weight defined in (
1.4
). Then

(3.1)

|{\phi}_{il}-{\phi}_{jl}|\leq\|{\phi}\|_{\text{Lip}}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|,\quad\forall~i,j,l\in[N],

where
\|{\phi}\|_{\text{Lip}}:=\frac{L_{a}}{Nc_{1}}\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)
depends on the constants from
2.1
.

Proof.

We use (
1.4
) and (
2.9
) to find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |{\phi}_{il}-{\phi}_{jl}|&=\left|\frac{a_{il}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}-a_{jl}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}}\right|\\
&=\left|\frac{a_{il}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}-a_{jl}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}+a_{jl}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik})}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}}\right|\\
&\leq\frac{L_{a}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|}{Nc_{1}}+\frac{N|\phi_{jl}|\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|L_{a}}{N^{2}c_{1}^{2}}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{L_{a}}{Nc_{1}}+\frac{L_{a}c_{2}}{Nc_{1}^{2}}\right)\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|:=\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|.\end{aligned}

∎

3.2
.

For
X=\{x_{i}\}
and
V=\{v_{i}\}
, we have

\sum_{1\leq i,j,l\leq N}\|\Delta_{li}^{v}\|\cdot\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}\|\cdot\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}\|\leq N\|\Delta^{x}\|_{F}\|\Delta^{v}\|_{F}^{2}\,.

Proof.

We use (
1.5
) to get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{1\leq i,j,l\leq N}\|\Delta_{li}^{v}\|\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}\|\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}\|\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{N}|\Delta_{li}^{v}|\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta_{ij}^{v}||\Delta_{ij}^{x}|\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(N\sum_{l=1}^{N}|\Delta_{li}^{v}|^{2}\right)\left(N\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\Delta_{ij}^{v}|^{2}|\Delta_{ij}^{x}|^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq N\left[\left(\sum_{1\leq i,l\leq N}|\Delta_{li}^{v}|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\Delta_{ij}^{v}|^{2}\sum_{1\leq r,s\leq N}|\Delta_{rs}^{x}|^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=N\|\Delta^{x}\|_{F}\|\Delta^{v}\|_{F}^{2}\,.\end{aligned}

Here, we used the Hölder inequality and Jensen’s inequality for the first and second inequalities. In the third inequality, we use the following inequality:

\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}a_{ij}b_{ij}\leq\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}a_{ij}\sum_{1\leq r,s\leq N}b_{rs}.

∎

3.3
.

By the same argument as in the proof of

\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\|\Delta^{v}_{ij}\|^{2}\|\Delta^{x}_{ij}\|^{2}\leq\|\Delta^{v}\|_{F}^{2}\|\Delta^{x}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\|\Delta^{v}\|_{F}^{2}\|\Delta^{x}\|_{F}^{2}\,.

Next, we provide recursive relations for

and
\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}

3.4
.

Let
(X,V)
be a global solution to (
1.3
). Then, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}+h\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\,,\quad n\geq 0,\\
&\|\Delta^{v}(n+1)\|_{F}\leq\left[1-h\kappa\left(1-\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}N\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\right]\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

(i) It follows from (
1.3
) that

x_{i}(n+1)=x_{i}(n)+hv_{i}(n)\,,\quad x_{j}(n+1)=x_{j}(n)+hv_{j}(n)\,.

These imply

\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n+1)=\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)+h\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\,,

and so

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n+1)\|^{2}&=\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)\|^{2}+h^{2}\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\|^{2}+2h\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)\cdot\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\\
&\leq\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)\|^{2}+h^{2}\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\|^{2}+2h\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)\|\cdot\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\|.\end{aligned}

We sum up (
3.3

i,j\in[N]
to derive

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|^{2}_{F}&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\Big(\|\Delta^{x}_{ij}(n)\|^{2}+h^{2}\|\Delta^{v}_{ij}(n)\|^{2}+2h\|\Delta^{x}_{ij}(n)\|\cdot\|\Delta^{v}_{ij}(n)\|\Big)\\
&\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\left(\|\Delta^{x}_{ij}(n)\|^{2}+h^{2}\|\Delta^{v}_{ij}(n)\|^{2}+2h\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\|\Delta^{x}_{ij}(n)\|^{2}\sum_{r,s=1}^{N}\|\Delta^{v}_{rs}(n)\|^{2}}\right)\\
&=\left(\sqrt{\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)\|^{2}}+h\sqrt{\sum_{r,s=1}^{N}\|\Delta_{rs}^{v}(n)\|^{2}}\right)^{2}=\Big(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}+h\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\Big)^{2}.\end{aligned}

This yields the desired estimate for
\eqref{New-2}_{1}
.

(ii) It follows from (
1.3
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} v_{i}(n+1)&=v_{i}(n)+h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}{\phi}_{il}(n)\big(v_{l}(n)-v_{i}(n)\big),\\
v_{j}(n+1)&=v_{j}(n)+h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}{\phi}_{jl}(n)\big(v_{l}(n)-v_{j}(n)\big).\end{aligned}

These yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n+1)=v_{i}(n)-v_{j}(n)+h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\phi_{il}(n)(v_{l}(n)-v_{i}(n))-\phi_{jl}(n)(v_{l}(n)-v_{j}(n))\Big)\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt=v_{i}(n)-v_{j}(n)+h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Big[\phi_{il}(n)(v_{l}(n)-v_{i}(n))-\phi_{jl}(n)\Big(v_{l}(n)-v_{i}(n)+v_{i}(n)-v_{j}(n)\Big)\Big]\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt=(1-h\kappa)(v_{i}(n)-v_{j}(n))+h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}(\phi_{il}(n)-\phi_{jl}(n))(v_{l}(n)-v_{i}(n))\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt=(1-h\kappa)\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)+h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}(\phi_{il}(n)-\phi_{jl}(n))\Delta^{v}_{li}(n).\end{aligned}

Here in the last equality, we used the unit sum relation
\sum_{l=1}^{N}\phi_{jl}=1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n+1)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-h\kappa)^{2}\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\|^{2}+2h\kappa(1-h\kappa)\sum_{l=1}^{N}(\phi_{il}(n)-\phi_{jl}(n))\cdot\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)\cdot\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)

\displaystyle\qquad+h^{2}\kappa^{2}\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{N}(\phi_{il}(n)-\phi_{jl}(n))\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-h\kappa)^{2}\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\|^{2}+2h\kappa(1-h\kappa)\sum_{l=1}^{N}\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}\cdot\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)\|\cdot\|\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)\|\cdot\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)\|

(3.4)

\displaystyle\qquad+h^{2}\kappa^{2}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{N}\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}\cdot\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)\|\cdot\|\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)\|\right]^{2}\,,

where we used the Lipschitz property of
\phi
from
Lemma

3.1
.

Next, we sum up (
3.4
) over
i,j
, and use
Lemma

3.2
for the second term to find

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v}(n+1)\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-h\kappa)^{2}\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}^{2}+2h\kappa N(1-h\kappa)\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+h^{2}\kappa^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}^{2}\|\Delta^{x}_{i,j}(n)\|^{2}\Big\|\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Delta^{v}_{li}(n)\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}^{2}\Big((1-h\kappa)^{2}+2h\kappa N(1-h\kappa)\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}+h^{2}\kappa^{2}N^{2}\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}^{2}\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big[1-h\kappa\left(1-\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}N\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\Big]^{2}\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}^{2}\,,

where we used the following relation by the Jensen inequality:

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\|\Delta^{x}_{ij}(n)\|^{2}\Big\|\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Delta^{v}_{li}(n)\Big\|^{2}\leq N\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}^{2}\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}^{2}\,.

∎

3.2.
Asymptotic flocking dynamics

In this subsection, we develop a suitable framework leading to mono-cluster flocking. First, we set

M:=\frac{1}{4N\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi(s):=1-\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}Ns.

3.5
.

Suppose that initial data and coupling strength satisfy

(3.5)

\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}<M\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}<\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}\psi(s)ds,

and let
(X,V)\in\mathbb{R}^{2dN}
be a global solution to (
1.3
). Then

(i)

it holds

(3.6)

\sup_{0\leq n<\infty}\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\leq M\,,

(ii)

and for any constant
0<C<1
there exists a sufficiently small
h>0
such that

(3.7)

\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)nh},\quad\forall~n\geq 0.

3.6
.

Our estimates for the discrete MT model only provide a conditional flocking guarantee, and it cannot be extended to an unconditional flocking theorem with our current proof techniques. This is different from the results for the continuous MT model. In
[
22
, Proposition 2.9]
, an unconditional flocking result is presented.
Numerically, we will indeed observe unconditional flocking, see
Section

5
. Generalizing our results to obtain unconditional flocking is an interesting open question.

Proof of
Theorem

3.5
.

If
\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}=0
then (i) and (ii) are trivially true since (
3.2
) guarantees
\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}=0
and
\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{n}(n)\|_{F}
for all
n\geq 0
. Therefore from now on we assume
\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}>0
. Showing (i) is the most involved step; once established, (ii) follows as a direct consequence.

(i) (Uniform bound in position shape discrepancy):
Suppose the contrary holds, i.e., there exists a positive constant
n^{\infty}=n^{\infty}(M)>0
such that

(3.8)

\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\leq M\quad\forall~n<n^{\infty}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}>M.

By assumption
\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}<M
in (
3.5
), and so

n^{\infty}>0
. From
\eqref{New-2}_{2}
we have

(3.9)

\|\Delta^{v}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\leq-h\kappa\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|\leq 0\,.

Summing (
3.9
) over all
n=0,\cdots,n^{\infty}-1
and using
\eqref{New-2}_{1}
yields

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\Delta^{v}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}&\leq-\sum_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}-1}h\kappa\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})\\
&\leq-\sum_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}-1}\kappa\left(\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})\,.\end{aligned}

Here, we used that (
3.8
) guarantees
\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})>0
for all
n=0,\cdots,n^{\infty}-1
.
Since
\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}
is not monotonic with respect to
n
, we reindex the set

\{\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}:\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\geq\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}\}_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}}

into
\{\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\}_{q=0}^{K}
so that it satisfies the following indexing rule:

\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}_{0}\|_{F},\quad\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}_{K}\|_{F},\quad\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}<\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}.

If there exist
n\neq m
such that

\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(m)\|_{F},

we set

\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(m)\|_{F}\quad\mbox{for some}~~q\in[K].

Hence,
\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}
with the same value is relabelled and merged as sharing the same index.

Note that the left-hand side of (
3.10
) is strictly negative. We would like to interpret the right-hand side of (
3.10
) an approximation of the integral

\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}}\kappa\psi(s)ds\,.

By
Lemma

A.1
in
Appendix

, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{q=0}^{K-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\right)\kappa\psi(\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F})\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt\leq\sum_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\kappa\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})\,.\end{aligned}

where the right-hand side of (
3.11
) is strictly positive. Combining with (
3.10
), we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{q=0}^{K-1}(\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}-\|\Delta_{q}^{x}\|_{F})\kappa\psi(\|\Delta_{q}^{x}\|_{F})\,.

Moreover, it follows from (
3.9
) that

\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}\quad\mbox{for all}~0\leq n<n^{\infty}\,,

and so (
3.2
) implies

(3.13)

\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}h\leq\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}h\quad\mbox{for $0\leq n<n^{\infty}$}.

By condition (
3.5
), we have
\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}<M

and

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}

\displaystyle<\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}\psi(s)ds=\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}(1-N\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}s)ds

\displaystyle=\kappa\left(M-\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}-\frac{N\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}}{2}(M^{2}-\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}^{2})\right)<\kappa M\,.

Then applying (
3.13
) to
n=n^{\infty-1}
we see

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}

\displaystyle<\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}h+M<\kappa hM+M<2M

as long as
h<\frac{1}{\kappa}
, which holds from
2.1
. It follows that
\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F})>0
.
Since
\psi
is decreasing, we can estimate the integral from
\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}
to
\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}
from above by a Riemann sum:

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{K-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\right)\kappa\psi(\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F})>\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}}\kappa\psi(s)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt>\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}}\kappa\psi(s)ds-\left(\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}\kappa\psi(s)ds-\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\int_{M}^{\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}}\kappa\psi(s)ds+\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}>\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}\,.

We substitute this bound into (
3.12
) to get

\|\Delta^{v}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}<-\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}\,.

We arrived at a contradiction since obviously, it must hold that
\|\Delta^{v}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}\geq 0
. Therefore, we have

n^{\infty}=+\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{0\leq n<\infty}\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\leq M.

(ii) (Exponential decay of velocity shape discrepancy): Since
\psi
is decreasing and
\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}
is uniformly bounded by
M
from (i), we have
\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})\geq\psi(M)
.
Therefore, it follows from
\eqref{New-2}_{2}
that

\|\Delta^{v}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\leq-h\kappa\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})\leq-h\kappa\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\psi(M)\,.

This yields

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\Delta^{v}(n+1)\|_{F}&\leq\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}(1-h\kappa\psi(M))\\
&\leq\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}(1-h\kappa\psi(M))^{n+1}\\
&=\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}\exp\left[(n+1)\log(1-h\kappa\psi(M))\right]\\
&=\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}\exp\left[(n+1)h\left(\frac{\log(1-h\kappa\psi(M))}{h}\right)\right].\end{aligned}

On the other hand, note that

(3.15)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log(1-h\kappa\psi(M))}{h}=-\kappa\psi(M).

Finally, we combine (
3.14
) and (

) to obtain

\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)nh},

for any
0<C<1
and

small enough.
∎

In
Section

2
, we mention that if solutions to the continuous MT model follow flocking dynamics, then the particles converge to exhibit a uniform constant velocity as they are evolved by the dynamical system. Although such velocity is not an invariant quantity as it is determined by the initial setup, it is notable that there indeed exists a limiting velocity. In
[
22
]
, this is referred to as an
emergent
flocking velocity. We can conclude the analogous result for the discrete MT model based on
Theorem

3.5
.

3.7
.

Under the same setting as in
Theorem

3.5
, there exists a set of asymptotic velocities
\{v_{i}^{\infty}\}
such that

v_{i}^{\infty}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}v_{i}(n)\quad\text{ with }\quad\|v_{i}^{\infty}\|<\infty,\quad\forall~i\in[N]\,,

and

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|v_{i}^{\infty}-v_{i}(n)\|\leq\frac{h\kappa\sqrt{N}\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)hn}}{1-e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)h}}\,.

Proof.

Note that
v_{i}(n)
satisfies

\displaystyle v_{i}(n)=v_{i}(0)+h\kappa\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}(m)\left(v_{j}(m)-v_{i}(m)\right).

Now, we set

(3.17)

v_{i}^{\infty}:=v_{i}(0)+h\kappa\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}(m)\left(v_{j}(m)-v_{i}(m)\right),\quad\phi_{ij}(m):=\frac{a_{ij}(m)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{ik}(m)}.

Since
\phi_{ij}\leq 1
and the summand in the summation decays exponentially to zero by
Theorem

3.5
, the right-hand side of (

) is well-defined. More specifically, we denote the summand by
b_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and note that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|b_{m}\|^{2}&=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}(m)\left(v_{j}(m)-v_{i}(m)\right)\right\|^{2}\leq N\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\Delta^{v}_{ij}(m)\|^{2}\leq N\|\Delta^{v}(m)\|_{F}^{2}\\
&\leq N\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}^{2}e^{-2C\kappa\psi(M)mh}\,.\end{aligned}

Hence,
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}
is absolutely convergent and so
v_{i}^{\infty}
in (
3.17
) is well-defined.
We can then compute directly

\displaystyle\|v_{i}^{\infty}-v_{i}(n)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|h\kappa\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}(m)\left(v_{j}(m)-v_{i}(m)\right)\right\|\leq h\kappa\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\|v_{j}(m)-v_{i}(m)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq h\kappa\sqrt{N}\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{v}(m)\|_{F}\leq h\kappa\sqrt{N}\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)mh}

\displaystyle=\frac{h\kappa\sqrt{N}\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)hn}}{1-e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)h}}\,.

Hence, for fixed
h>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{i}^{\infty}-v_{i}(n)\|=0\,.

∎

3.8
.

The asymptotic flocking velocity exists, although it cannot be expressed explicitly in terms of the initial configuration. This is similar to the continuous MT model as seen in

2.5
.

3.3.
Uniform-in-time continuous limit

In this subsection, we study the uniform-in-time transition from the discrete model:

(3.18)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle x^{h}_{i}(n+1)=x^{h}_{i}(n)+hv^{h}_{i}(n)\,,\quad n\geq 0,\quad i\in[N],\\
\displaystyle v^{h}_{i}(n+1)=v^{h}_{i}(n)+h\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\phi}^{h}_{ij}(n)\big(v^{h}_{j}(n)-v^{h}_{i}(n)\big),\end{cases}

to the continuous model:

(3.19)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\dot{x}}_{i}=v_{i}\,,\quad t>0,\quad i\in[N],\\
\displaystyle{\dot{v}}_{i}=\kappa\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{ij}(v_{j}-v_{i}),\end{cases}

as the time-step
h
vanishes. For notational simplicity, we set

X^{h}:=(x_{1}^{h},\ldots,x_{N}^{h}),\quad V^{h}:=(v_{1}^{h},\ldots,v_{N}^{h}).

We first establish the continuous transition for
h\to 0
in any finite time interval (see
Corollary

3.12
below) : for any fixed
T>0
,

\lim_{h\to 0+}\sup_{0\leq n<\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}\Big(\|X^{h}(n)-X(nh)\|+\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|\Big)=0,

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the largest integer less than equal to
x
.

In order to extend this result to a uniform-in-time statement, we cannot hope to achieve decay to zero for
\|X^{h}(n)-X(nh)\|
uniformly in time due to the fact that our uniform-in-time flocking result only holds in shape discrepancy and not for positions and velocities directly. Therefore, we introduce the following definition.

3.9
.

The discrete system (
1.3
) converges to (
1.2
)
uniformly in time
if for some class of solutions
(X^{h},V^{h})
and
(X,V)
to (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), respectively, there exists a constant
c\geq 0
only depending on the model parameters, choice of communication function
a
and initial condition
(X(0),V(0))=(X^{h}(0),V^{h}(0))
such that

\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n<+\infty}\|\Delta^{x,h}(n)-\Delta^{x}(nh)\|_{F}\leq C\,,\quad\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n<+\infty}\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|=0\,.

In order to derive such a result for an infinite time interval, we can use the flocking theorem (
Theorem

3.5
) for the discrete MT model, and its continuous counterpart
[
22
, Proposition 2.9]
. This was also the approach that the authors in
[
12
]
used to show the discrete to continuous uniform-tin-time transition for the Cucker-Smale model.

We start by recalling a general finite-time convergence result. Consider the following Cauchy problems for a general first-order ODE system and its corresponding discrete system:

(3.20)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dt}=f(y),\quad 0<t<T,\\
\displaystyle y(0)=y_{0},\end{cases}\quad\begin{cases}y_{n+1}=y_{n}+hf(y_{n}).\quad 0<n<\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\rfloor,\\
y(0)=y_{0}.\end{cases}

For
R>0
, we set

\Omega:=\{y:\|y-y_{0}\|<R\}.

Suppose that the vector field
f
and discrete solution satisfy the following conditions:

•

{(\mathcal{A}}1)
: The vector field
f
is Lipschitz continuous on the open set
\Omega
:

\mathcal{L}_{f}:=\sup_{y_{1}\neq y_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\Omega}\frac{|f(y_{2})-f(y_{1})|}{|y_{2}-y_{1}|}<\infty\,.

•

{(\mathcal{A}}2)
: The discrete solution
(y_{n})
satisfies

\|y_{n}-y_{0}\|\leq R,\quad\forall~n=0,1,\cdots,\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor.

In the following proposition, we recall finite-time convergence from the discrete model to its continuous counterpart stated in (
3.20
).

3.10
.

Suppose that the conditions
{(\mathcal{A}}1)
and
{(\mathcal{A}}2)
hold for some
T>0
. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

For the truncation error
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n):=\left\|\frac{dy}{dt}\bigg\rvert_{t=nh}-\frac{y((n+1)h-y(nh)}{h}\right\|\,.

we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\max_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor T/h\right\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n)=0\,.

(2)

The error
\mathcal{E}_{2}^{h}(n)
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}^{h}(n):=\|y(nh)-y_{n}\|

can be controlled by the truncation error
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n):

(3.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}^{h}(n)\leq\frac{\max_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor T/h\right\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n)}{\mathcal{L}_{f}}\left(e^{\mathcal{L}_{f}nh}-1\right)\,,\quad 0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor\,.

Proof.

For the proof, refer to
[
25
, Chapter 12,Theorem 12.2]
.
∎

In fact, in some situations, the condition
{(\mathcal{A}}2)
follows from the condition
{(\mathcal{A}}1)
directly, as can be seen in the following lemma.

3.11
.

If
{(\mathcal{A}}1)
is satisfied for

R>\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{f}}\left((1+\mathcal{L}_{f}h)^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}-1\right)\|f(y_{0})\|
,
then
{(\mathcal{A}}2)
holds.

Proof.

Indeed, the relation

\|f(y_{k})\|\leq\mathcal{L}_{f}\|y_{k}-y_{0}\|+\|f(y_{0})\|

implies

\displaystyle\|y_{n}-y_{0}\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\mathcal{L}_{f}h)^{k}\|y_{n-k}-y_{0}\|+h\|f(y_{0})\|\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(1+\mathcal{L}_{f}h)^{l}

\displaystyle=h\|f(y_{0})\|\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}(1+\mathcal{L}_{f}h)^{l}\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{f}}\left((1+\mathcal{L}_{f}h)^{\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}-1\right)\|f(y_{0})\|\,.

∎

To apply the above results to the MT model, we consider (
3.20
) with

y=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{N}\\
v_{1}\\
\vdots\\
v_{N}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2Nd}\,,\quad f(y)=\begin{pmatrix}0_{N}&\operatorname{Id}_{N}\\
0_{N}&\kappa(A(X)-\operatorname{Id}_{N})\end{pmatrix}\cdot y\,,\quad A_{ij}(X)=\frac{a(\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a(\|x_{i}-x_{k}\|)}\,.

3.12
Finite-time transition.

Let
(X(t),V(t))
and
(X^{h}(n),V^{h}(n))
be the global solutions to the continuous MT model (
3.19
) and the discrete MT model (
3.18
), respectively, such that the initial condition
\|y_{0}\|
for the discrete model is sufficiently small (depending on
\kappa,h,c_{1},c_{2},L_{a}
). Then, for all
0\leq T<\infty
, we have the following finite-time transition from discrete to continuum:

(3.23)

\lim_{h\to 0+}\sup_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}\Big(\|X^{h}(n)-X(nh)\|+\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|\Big)=0\,.

Proof.

To show that the assumptions for
Proposition

3.10
are satisfied, note that for any two-particle ensembles
y,\bar{y}\in\Omega
, it holds

\displaystyle\|f(y)-f(\bar{y})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|V-\bar{V}\|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\|A(X)(V-\bar{V})\|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\|(A(X)-A(\bar{X}))\bar{V}\|^{2}+\kappa^{2}\|V-\bar{V}\|^{2}\,.

Using condition (1) in
2.1
, we have

\|A(X)(V-\bar{V})\|^{2}\leq(c_{2}/c_{1})^{2}\|V-\bar{V}\|^{2}\,.

Thanks to condition (2), the relation

|A_{ij}(X)-A_{ij}(\bar{X})|\leq\frac{L_{a}}{Nc_{1}}\left[\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)\|x_{i}-\bar{x}_{i}\|+\|x_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}\|+\frac{c_{2}}{Nc_{1}}\sum_{k}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}_{k}\|\right]

implies

\displaystyle\|(A(X)-A(\bar{X}))\bar{V}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}(X)-A_{ij}(\bar{X})|^{2}\|\bar{v}_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{L_{a}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}(R+\|y_{0}\|)^{2}\|X-\bar{X}\|^{2}\,.

Hence, the hypothesis
{(\mathcal{A}}1)
is satisfied with

\mathcal{L}_{f}=\left(\max\left\{\left(1+\kappa^{2}+\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}\right),4\kappa^{2}\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{L_{a}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}(R+\|y_{0}\|)^{2}\right\}\right)^{1/2}<\infty\,.

Next, we use
Lemma

3.11
, and denote
R^{*}
by

\displaystyle R^{*}:=\frac{\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}+\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}}}{2\kappa\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)\frac{L_{a}}{c_{1}}}-\|y_{0}\|\,,

which is positive for sufficiently small
\|y_{0}\|
. Then for any
0<R\leq R^{*}
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{f}=\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}+\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}}\,.

For the condition in
Lemma

3.11
to hold and choosing
R=R^{*}
, we require

\displaystyle R^{*}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{f}}{2\kappa\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)\frac{L_{a}}{c_{1}}}-\|y_{0}\|>\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{f}}\left((1+\mathcal{L}_{f}h)^{\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\rfloor}-1\right)\|f(y_{0})\|\,,

which is indeed satisfied for sufficiently small
\|y_{0}\|
and
\|f(y_{0})\|
. We conclude that
{(\mathcal{A}}2)
holds, noting that

\|f(y_{0})\|^{2}\leq\left(1+\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}\right)\|V_{0}\|^{2}
thanks to condition (1) in
2.1
.
Hence, it follows from
Proposition

3.10
that for all
0\leq n\leq\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\rfloor,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}^{h}(n)

\displaystyle:=\left(\|X^{h}(n)-X(nh)\|^{2}+\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{\max_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor T/h\right\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n)}{\mathcal{L}_{f}}\left(e^{\mathcal{L}_{f}nh}-1\right)

by (
3.22
). Thus, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{h}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}\frac{\max_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n)}{\mathcal{L}_{f}}\left(e^{\mathcal{L}_{f}nh}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\max_{0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor\frac{T}{h}\right\rfloor}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{h}(n)}{\mathcal{L}_{f}}\left(e^{\mathcal{L}_{f}T}-1\right)=0

by (
3.21
).
∎

Note that in the above estimation, there is an exponential dependence on the upper bound of the error term with respect to the time horizon
T
. Hence, such analysis does not hold for an infinite time interval. On the other hand, if we consider the shape discrepancy for positions instead of positions themselves, we can achieve the uniform-in-time transition from the discrete MT model to the continuous one as
h\to 0
for an infinite time interval as stated in
Definition

3.9
. We present this result in the following theorem.

3.13
uniform-in-time transition.

Let
(X^{h},V^{h})
and
(X,V)
be global solutions to (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), respectively. Assume the condition from
Theorem

2.4
hold for
(X(0),V(0))
and the conditions from
Theorem

3.5
hold for
(X^{h}(0),V^{h}(0)):=(X(0),V(0))
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n<+\infty}\|\Delta^{x,h}(n)-\Delta^{x}(nh)\|_{F}\leq c_{0}\mathcal{D}(V(0))\,,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad\limsup_{h\rightarrow 0}\sup_{0\leq n<+\infty}\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|=0

for some constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(\kappa,N,a)
independent of
n
and
h
.

Proof.

By
Corollary

2.5
and
Corollary

3.7
, we have

\exists~V^{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}V(t),\quad\exists~V^{h,\infty}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}V^{h}(n).

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Then by the flocking estimates for (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), there exist times
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
such that for any
h>0
,

(3.24)

\|V^{h}(n)-V^{h,\infty}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{9}\quad\forall~n\geq\left\lfloor\frac{T_{1}}{h}\right\rfloor\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|V(nh)-V^{\infty}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{9}\quad\forall~n\geq\left\lfloor\frac{T_{2}}{h}\right\rfloor.

The second statement above follows directly as the solution itself does not depend on
h
. For the first statement, however, we need to check carefully the interplay between
n
and
h
. Indeed, from (
3.16
) we have

\displaystyle\|V^{h}(n)-V^{h,\infty}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v_{i}^{h}(n)-v_{i}^{h,\infty}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{h^{2}\kappa^{2}N\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|^{2}_{F}e^{-2C\kappa\psi(M)hn}}{(1-e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)h})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{2}\kappa^{2}N^{2}\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}^{2}e^{-2C\kappa\psi(M)hn}}{(1-e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)h})^{2}}.

For this expression to be controlled by
(\epsilon/9)^{2}
, we require

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{C\kappa\psi(M)h}\,\left[\log\frac{9}{\epsilon}+\log\left(\frac{h\kappa N\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}}{1-e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)h}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2C\kappa\psi(M)h}\,\left[\log\frac{9}{\epsilon}+\log\left(\kappa N\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}\right)+\log\left(\frac{h}{C\kappa\psi(M)h+O(h^{2})}\right)\right]=O\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)\,,

and therefore such a
T_{1}
exists for small enough
h
as claimed.
Now, we set

\bar{T}:=\max\{{T_{1},T_{2}}\}.

Then we use the finite-time result in
Corollary

3.12
to see that there exists a small enough
h_{0}=h_{0}(\epsilon)>0
such that

(3.25)

\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|<\frac{\epsilon}{9}

for all
0<h<h_{0}
and
0\leq n\leq\left\lfloor\bar{T}/h\right\rfloor
. Choosing
\tilde{n}=\left\lfloor\bar{T}/h\right\rfloor,
we can estimate the error between the discrete and continuum limit points via

(3.26)

\|V^{h,\infty}-V^{\infty}\|\leq\|V^{h,\infty}-V^{h}(\tilde{n})\|+\|V^{h}(\tilde{n})-V(\tilde{n}h)\|+\|V(\tilde{n}h)-V^{\infty}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{3}

for all
0<h<h_{0}
.
Finally, we combine (
3.24
), and (
3.26
) to find the uniform-in-time transition in velocities:

(3.27)

\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|\leq\|V^{h}(n)-V^{h,\infty}\|+\|V^{h,\infty}-V^{\infty}\|+\|V^{\infty}-V(nh)\|<\epsilon

for all
n\geq\left\lfloor\bar{T}/h\right\rfloor
, and
0<h<h_{0}
. Combining with (
3.25
), we get

\displaystyle\|V^{h}(n)-V(nh)\|<\epsilon

for all
n\geq 0
, and
0<h<h_{0}
.

Now, consider the shape discrepancy for positions:

(3.28)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\Delta^{x,h}_{ij}(n)-\Delta^{x}_{ij}(nh)\|&=\|((x_{i}^{h}(n)-x_{j}^{h}(n))-(x_{i}(nh)-x_{j}(nh))\|\\
&=\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}h[(v_{i}(kh)-v_{i}^{h}(k))-(v_{j}(kh)-v_{j}^{h}(k))]\right\|+O(h^{2})\\
&\leq h\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|\Delta_{ij}^{v,h}(k)-\Delta_{ij}^{v}(kh)\|+O(h^{2})\\
&\leq h\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{ij}^{v,h}(k)-\Delta_{ij}^{v}(kh)\|+O(h^{2}).\end{aligned}

Note we can use
Theorem

3.5
and
Theorem

2.4
to obtain that for any
K\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v,h}_{ij}(k)-\Delta^{v}_{ij}(hk)\|\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{v,h}_{ij}(k)\|+\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{v}_{ij}(hk)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{v,h}(k)\|_{F}+\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(V(hk))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\|\Delta^{v,h}(0)\|_{F}e^{-C\kappa\psi(M)kh}+\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}(V^{0})e^{-\kappa a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})hk}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F},\mathcal{D}(V(0))\right\}\frac{e^{-\tilde{c}hK}}{1-e^{-\tilde{c}h}}\leq\frac{1}{h}\left(\frac{N\mathcal{D}(V(0))}{\tilde{c}}+O(h)\right)\,,

where

\tilde{c}=\min\left\{C\kappa\psi(M),\kappa a(\mathcal{D}^{\infty})\right\}
.
Together with
Corollary

3.12
to bound the case
k\in\{0,...,K-1\}
, we conclude that for fixed
K\in\mathbb{N}
and
h
small enough

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{x,h}_{ij}(n)-\Delta^{x}_{ij}(nh)\|\leq\frac{2N\mathcal{D}(V(0))}{\tilde{c}}=:c_{0}\mathcal{D}(V(0))\,,

and so
\|\Delta^{x,h}(n)-\Delta^{x}(nh)\|_{F}

is uniformly bounded for all
n\geq 0
and small enough
h>0
.
∎

4.
Uniform-in-time stability estimates

In this section, we study a version of uniform
\|\cdot\|_{F}
-stability for the discrete MT model with respect to initial data. As briefly discussed in the introduction, our estimate is not the typical type of stability estimate. Typical stability estimates bound the difference between two pairs of solutions over all time in terms of the difference in the initial data. Due to the loss of conservation of momentum in (
1.3
), comparing two pairs of solutions directly is technically challenging. Hence, we consider instead the difference in shape discrepancies
\Delta^{x},\Delta^{v}
between solution pairs. In
Section

3
, we derived the flocking estimate for these quantities and concluded uniform-in-time discrete to continuous transition based on this estimate. The flocking estimate is also a key ingredient to obtain uniform-in-time stability for the discrete MT model. Our stability estimate will guarantee that the structure of the solution is preserved if the initial shape discrepancies are close enough. Such structural preservation of solutions is analogous to orbital stability in hyperbolic conservation laws, also see
Remark

4.8
for more details.

We start by introducing two assumptions needed for uniform stability.

•

(
\mathcal{F}1
): Initial data
(X^{0},V^{0})
and
(\overline{X}^{0},\overline{V}^{0})
satisfy

\max\left\{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F},\|\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}\right\}<M=\frac{1}{4N\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}}.

•

(
\mathcal{F}2
): Coupling strength
\kappa
is sufficiently large such that

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}<\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}\psi(s)ds\,,\qquad\|\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}<\kappa\int_{\|\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}}^{M}\psi(s)ds\,.

By
Theorem

3.5
, the framework
(\mathcal{F}1)-(\mathcal{F}2)
implies the emergence of asymptotic flocking for
(X,V)
,
(\overline{X},\overline{V})
and their shape discrepancies.

4.1
.

(Uniform-in-time stability)

Suppose that the initial data
(X^{0},V^{0})
and
(\overline{X}^{0},\overline{V}^{0})
and coupling strength satisfy the framework
(\mathcal{F}1)-(\mathcal{F}2)
, and
let
(X,V)=(X(n),V(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\overline{X},\overline{V})=(\overline{X}(n),\overline{V}(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be solutions to (
1.3
) with initial data
(X^{0},V^{0})
and
(\overline{X}^{0},\overline{V}^{0})
, respectively.
For sufficiently small
h>0
, there exist
C(n,h)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\right\|_{F}+\left\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\left\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\right\|_{F}+\left\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\right\|_{F}+C(n,h),\quad n\geq 1,

where
\lim_{h\to 0}C(n,h)=C_{0}
for some constant
C_{0}>0
, only depending on the initial conditions
V(0)
and
\overline{V}(0)
.

Proof.

Since the proof is rather lengthy, we give here an overview of the main ingredients and provide rigorous proofs of all necessary auxiliary results in the next subsections. We split the proof in three steps:

\bullet
Step A: we claim that for any
0<\epsilon<1
and sufficiently small
h>0
there exists positive constants
b_{1}(\epsilon,h),\,b_{2}(h)
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n+1)\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\right\|_{F}+h\left\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\right\|_{F},\\
&\|\Delta^{v}(n+1)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n+1)\|_{F}\leq(1-\epsilon)\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}+b_{1}(\epsilon,h)e^{-b_{2}(h)n},\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle b_{1}(\epsilon,h)

\displaystyle:=\left(\bar{c}_{0}\epsilon+\bar{c}_{1}h+\bar{c}_{2}h^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,,\qquad b_{2}(h):=C\kappa\psi(M)h\,.

\diamond
(Derivation of
\eqref{D-0-0}_{1}
): It follows from (
1.3
) that

\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n+1)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{x}}(n+1)=\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{x}}(n)+h\left(\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{v}}(n)\right).

This yields

\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n+1)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{x}}(n+1)\|\leq\|\Delta_{ij}^{x}(n)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{x}}(n)\|+h\|\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{v}}(n)\|.

Now, we sum up the above relation over all
i,j\in[N]
to find the desired estimate
\eqref{D-0-0}_{1}
.

\diamond
(Derivation of
\eqref{D-0-0}_{2}
): The proof of the second estimate
\eqref{D-0-0}_{2}
requires several auxiliary results; we postpone it to
Lemma

4.6
.

\bullet
Step B: We estimate
\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}
and
\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\|_{F}
in terms of quantities depending only on the initial data.

\diamond
(Bound on
\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}
): We claim that for any
0<\epsilon<1
and
h
small enough, we have

(4.2)

\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}\leq(1-\epsilon)^{n}\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}+\frac{b_{1}e^{b_{2}}}{1-(1-\epsilon)e^{b_{2}}}e^{-b_{2}n}.

See
Corollary

4.7
for details.

\diamond
(Bound on
\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\|_{F}
): By (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\|_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}+h\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|\Delta^{v}(k)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(k)\|_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}+h\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left((1-\epsilon)^{k}\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}+\frac{b_{1}e^{b_{2}}}{1-(1-\epsilon)e^{b_{2}}}e^{-b_{2}k}\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}+h\frac{1-(1-\epsilon)^{n}}{\epsilon}\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}+h\frac{b_{1}e^{b_{2}}}{1-(1-\epsilon)e^{b_{2}}}\frac{1-e^{-b_{2}n}}{1-e^{-b_{2}}}\,.

\bullet
Step C: Choosing
0<h<\epsilon<1
, we combine (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) to find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\|_{F}+\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}\\
&\leq\max\Big\{1\,;\,\underbrace{\frac{h}{\epsilon}\left[1-(1-\epsilon)^{n}\right]+(1-\epsilon)^{n}}_{<\,1}\Big\}\Big(\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}+\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}\Big)\\
&\qquad+h\frac{b_{1}e^{b_{2}}}{1-(1-\epsilon)e^{b_{2}}}\frac{1-e^{-b_{2}n}}{1-e^{-b_{2}}}+\frac{b_{1}e^{b_{2}}}{1-(1-\epsilon)e^{b_{2}}}e^{-b_{2}n}\\
&\leq\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}+\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}+C(n,h)\end{aligned}

with

\displaystyle C(n,h):=h\frac{b_{1}(\epsilon,h)e^{b_{2}(h)}}{1-(1-\epsilon)e^{b_{2}(h)}}\frac{1-e^{-b_{2}(h)n}}{1-e^{-b_{2}(h)}}+\frac{b_{1}(\epsilon,h)e^{b_{2}(h)}}{1-(1-\epsilon)e^{b_{2}(h)}}e^{-b_{2}(h)n}\,.

It follows that for fixed
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1-e^{-b_{2}(h)n}}{1-e^{-b_{2}(h)}}=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{C\kappa\psi(M)hn+O(h^{2})}{C\kappa\psi(M)h+O(h^{2})}=n\,.

Hence for any
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}C(n,h)=\sqrt{\frac{\bar{c}_{0}}{\epsilon}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\sqrt{\|\Delta^{v}(0)\|_{F}^{2}+\|\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}^{2}}\,.

∎

4.1.
Propagation of velocity shape discrepancy

Our main goal in this subsection is to control the shape discrepancy in velocities between pairs
(X,V)
and
(\overline{X},\overline{V})
of solutions by bounding how their difference propagates via the discrete MT dynamics. For this, we define
\lambda=\lambda(n)
as

(4.4)

\lambda:=\min_{1\leq i,j\leq N}(\overline{\phi}_{ij}+\overline{\phi}_{ji})>0\,,\qquad\overline{\phi}_{ij}=\frac{a\left(\|\overline{x_{i}}-\overline{x_{j}}\|\right)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}a\left(\|\overline{x_{i}}-\overline{x_{k}}\|\right)}\,.

and
\alpha=\alpha(n)
as

(4.5)

\alpha:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\alpha_{ij}=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\left(1-\overline{\phi}_{ij}-\overline{\phi}_{ii}\right)^{2}.

4.2
.

Suppose the framework
(\mathcal{F}1)-(\mathcal{F}2)
holds, and let
(X,V)
and
(\overline{X},\overline{V})
be global solutions to (
1.3
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{v}(n+1)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n+1)\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}(n,h)\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}^{2}+\mathcal{C}_{2}(n,h)\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}+\mathcal{C}_{3}(n,h)\|\Delta^{v}(n)\|_{F}^{2}\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(n,h)

\displaystyle:=(1-h\kappa\lambda(n))^{2}+4h^{2}\kappa^{2}\left(\alpha(n)+\frac{L_{a}^{2}M^{2}}{Nc_{1}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sqrt{2}h\kappa(1-h\kappa\lambda(n))\left(\alpha(n)+\frac{L_{a}^{2}M^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}(n,h)

\displaystyle:=\frac{8h\kappa L_{a}M}{c_{1}}(1-h\kappa\lambda(n))\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}(n,h)

\displaystyle:=\frac{32h^{2}\kappa^{2}L_{a}^{2}M^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\right)^{2}\,.

We will prepare this result with several a priori estimates.

4.3
.

Suppose the framework
(\mathcal{F}1)-(\mathcal{F}2)
holds, and let
(X,V)
and

be global solutions to (
1.3

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n+1)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{v}}(n+1)=(1-h\kappa\lambda_{ij}(n))(\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{v}}(n))+\mathcal{I}^{ij}_{1}+\mathcal{I}^{ij}_{2}\,,\end{aligned}

where
\lambda_{ij}=\overline{\phi}_{ij}+\overline{\phi}_{ji}>0

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{ij}_{1}

\displaystyle:=h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}(\phi_{il}-\overline{\phi}_{il})\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)-h\kappa\sum_{l=1}^{N}(\phi_{jl}-\overline{\phi}_{jl})\Delta_{lj}^{v}(n)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{ij}_{2}

\displaystyle:=h\kappa\sum_{l\neq i,j}\Big[\overline{\phi}_{il}\left(\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{li}^{\overline{v}}(n)\right)-\overline{\phi}_{jl}\left(\Delta_{lj}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{lj}^{\overline{v}}(n)\right)\Big]\,.

Proof.

It follows from (
1.3
) that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n+1)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{v}}(n+1)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\big(v_{i}(n+1)-v_{j}(n+1)\big)-\big(\overline{v}_{i}(n+1)-\overline{v}_{j}(n+1)\big)\\

Note that the second summation on the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{l=1}^{N}\overline{\phi}_{il}\left(\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{li}^{\overline{v}}(n)\right)\\

Thus, we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Big[\phi_{il}\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)-\overline{\phi}_{il}\Delta_{li}^{\overline{v}}(n)\Big]=-\left(\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{v}}(n)\right)+\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(\phi_{il}-\overline{\phi}_{il}\right)\Delta_{li}^{v}(n)\\

Similarly, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Big[\phi_{jl}\Delta_{lj}^{v}(n)-\overline{\phi}_{jl}\Delta_{lj}^{\overline{v}}(n)\Big]=-\left(\Delta_{ji}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{ji}^{\overline{v}}(n)\right)+\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(\phi_{jl}-\overline{\phi}_{jl}\right)\Delta_{lj}^{v}(n)\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum_{l\neq i}\overline{\phi}_{jl}\left(\Delta_{lj}^{v}(n)-\Delta_{lj}^{\overline{v}}(n)-\Delta_{ij}^{v}(n)+\Delta_{ij}^{\overline{v}}(n)\right).\end{aligned}

Subtracting (
4.9

4.8

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Big[\phi_{il}\Delta_{li}^{v}-\overline{\phi}_{il}\Delta_{li}^{\overline{v}}-\phi_{jl}\Delta_{lj}^{v}+\overline{\phi}_{jl}\Delta_{lj}^{\overline{v}}\Big]\\

4.4

For

4.8
.

In
Theorem

4.1
, we showed that the shape discrepancy of two pairs of solutions remains uniformly bounded by the shape discrepancy between two sets of initial data. The conditions
\mathcal{F}1
and
\mathcal{F}2
for this result are imposed to guarantee the formation of mono-cluster flocking. We can view our stability result as a kind of orbital stability of solution structure. To see this, we recall the analogous result of viscous conservation laws. In viscous conservation laws, the speed of the shock structure (traveling wave connecting two constant far-field states) is determined by the Rankine-Hugoniot condition. Moreover, the perturbed shock structure converges to the shifted original shock structure with the same shape. This phenomenon is called the orbital stability of the shock structure and the shift is also determined by the mass of a perturbation via the conservation law. In our case, the MT model does not have momentum conservation, so we cannot know the asymptotic velocity a priori. Nevertheless, our uniform stability result illustrates that the differences in the shape of the configurations can be uniformly controlled by that of the initial data. This is why we can interpret our uniform stability results as a kind of orbital stability of solution structure.

\frac{\|\Delta^{x}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(n)\|_{F}+\|\Delta^{v}(n)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(n)\|_{F}}{\|\Delta^{x}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{x}}(0)\|_{F}+\|\Delta^{v}(0)-\Delta^{\overline{v}}(0)\|_{F}}

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have addressed three quantitative estimates for the discrete MT model, corresponding to a forward Euler discretization of the continuous MT model. Compared to the CS model in which the forcing term is skew-symmetric in the index exchange map, the flocking force in the MT model does not have this skew-symmetric property. Hence, the total momentum for the MT model is not conserved. This lack of conservation law poses technical challenges for the flocking and stability analysis. For example, the standard energy estimates cannot be used for the continuous MT model as it is.

Fortunately, however, the nonlinear functional approach introduced in Ha-Liu
[
10
]
can be applied with the spatial and velocity diameters as suitable nonlinear functionals. In
[
22
]
, such a nonlinear functional approach was adapted to prove the flocking result for the continuous MT model.

For the discrete MT model, we provide the following three quantitative estimates. First, we show that the discrete model can exhibit asymptotic flocking under a suitable framework in terms of initial data and system parameters. Second, we show that the discrepancy between the discrete and continuous solutions tends to zero uniformly in time. For this uniform transition from the discrete model to the continuous model, we used the standard finite-time transition result as well as the asymptotic flocking estimates for both the discrete and the continuum models.
Since the flocking estimate on the discrete level is a conditional result, the uniform-in-time transition relies on the same constraints for the initial data. In contrast, the finite-time transition can be shown for a general class of initial data. Thus, there is a trade-off between using a uniform-in-time transition with a limited choice of admissible initial data and a finite-time transition with a limited time horizon. Finally, we show that the difference in shape discrepancy between two solutions measured in the
\ell^{2}
-distance can be controlled by their initial value.

Our numerical investigations in Section
5
suggest that the conditions for flocking to occur may be weakened. Noting that unconditional flocking for a slowly decaying communication function
\phi
was obtained for the continuous model
[
22
]
in Proposition 2.9, it is a natural next step to seek an unconditional flocking result in the discrete setting as well.

Here, we have chosen an explicit first-order Euler discretization scheme as a starting point for developing the methodology for uniform-in-time flocking and stability. Other discretizations are of interest such as implicit schemes and higher-order schemes; a fourth-order Runge-Kutta scheme for instance is practically desirable from an implementation perspective. This work opens up avenues to develop a uniform-in-time theory for much more general classes of numerical algorithms.

Appendix A
Reindexing estimate

In this appendix, we provide a detailed proof of (
3.11
) in
Section

3
. The argument follows the proof strategy in
[
12
, Appendix A]
.

A.1
.

Consider the scalar sequence
\{\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given via solutions to the discrete MT model (
1.3
).
Assume there exists a positive constant
n^{\infty}=n^{\infty}(M)>0
such that

(A.1)

\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\leq M\quad\forall~n<n^{\infty}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}>M\,.

Then it is possible to reindex the set

\{\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}:\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\geq\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}\}_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}}

into
\{\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\}_{q=0}^{K}
so that it satisfies

\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}_{0}\|_{F},\quad\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}_{K}\|_{F},\quad\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}<\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}.

and

\sum_{q=0}^{K-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F})\leq\sum_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}).

Proof.

Recall that
\psi(s):=1-\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}}Ns
is a decreasing function. Now, we claim that the sequence
\{\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\}_{q=0}^{K}
is a monotone increasing sequence, if necessary, by re-indexing the set

\Big\{\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}:\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\geq\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}\Big\}_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}}.

In other words, we have

(A.2)

\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}_{0}\|_{F}<\|\Delta^{x}_{1}\|_{F}<\cdots<\|\Delta^{x}_{K}\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}.

In what follows, we verify (
A.2
) in two steps.

\bullet

Step A
(Refinement): Note that the finite sequence
\{\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\}_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}}
might not be monotonic. So when we move from
\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}
to
\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}
, we might fly over other values in
\{\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\}_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}}
. To get rid of such situations, we refine the discrete-time steps
\{0,h,\ldots,n^{\infty}h\}
as follows.

Suppose there exists some
n^{\prime}<n^{\infty}
such that

(A.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mbox{Either}~~\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}<\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\prime})\|_{F}<\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt\text{or}\quad\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}>\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\prime})\|_{F}>\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}.\end{aligned}

Then we add a new grid point
\overline{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that

\|\Delta^{x}(\overline{n})\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\prime})\|_{F}.

and remove
n^{\prime}
from the previous index set. Note that we do not require that
\|\Delta^{x}(\overline{n})\|_{F}
corresponds to the shape discrepancy of a set of particle solutions
\{x_{i}(\overline{n})\}_{i=1}^{N}
of the discrete MT model. Here, we are simply relabeling a set of scalars.
We repeat this process until the relation (
A.3
) does not happen for a new index set
\{l\}_{0}^{I}
with

0\leq l\leq I,\quad\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}_{0}\|_{F}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\Delta^{x}(I)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}.

Note that
\{\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\}_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}}
and
\{\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\}_{l=0}^{I}
are identical as sets. For the new index set
\{l\}_{0}^{I}
and corresponding sequence
\{\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\}_{l=0}^{I}
, we claim

(A.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{l=0}^{I-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F})\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\sum_{n=0}^{n^{\infty}-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}).\end{aligned}

If we have a new grid point
\overline{n}\in(n,n+1)
, then we have

\displaystyle\left(\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(\overline{n})\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(\overline{n})\|_{F})+\left(\|\Delta^{x}(\overline{n})\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq\left(\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(\overline{n})\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})+\left(\|\Delta^{x}(\overline{n})\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\left(\|\Delta^{x}(n+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(n)\|_{F}).

Here we used that
\psi
is a decreasing function. We can conclude for (
A.4
) using this observation several times. It then remains to show that

(A.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{K-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F})\leq\sum_{l=0}^{I-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F})\,.

We split the set
\{l\}_{0}^{I}
into two subsets as follows:

\mathcal{N}^{+}:=\{l:\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\geq\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}\},\quad\mathcal{N}^{-}:=\{l:\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\leq\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}\}.

By the construction of
\{l\}_{0}^{I}
, two consecutive indices
l
and
l+1
should belong to either
\mathcal{N}^{+}
or
\mathcal{N}^{-}
, except when

\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}.

Note that the latter is of little importance since in this case

\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F})=0.

Therefore, the summation in the left-hand side of (
A.4
) can be rewritten as

(A.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{l=0}^{I-1}\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F})\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt=\left(\sum_{l,(l+1)\in\mathcal{N}^{+}}+\sum_{l,(l+1)\in\mathcal{N}^{-}}\right)\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}).\end{aligned}

\bullet

Step B
(Estimate): We denote
\mathcal{T}:=\{\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\}_{q=0}^{K}
.
Then, we can define a surjective function
\alpha:l\in{\mathcal{N}}^{+}~~\mapsto~~\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\in\mathcal{T}
and a bijective function
\beta:~\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\in\mathcal{T}~~\mapsto~~q\in\{0,\dots K\}
.
Now we define
\gamma:\mathcal{N}^{+}\to\{q\}_{0}^{K}
as
\gamma:=\beta\circ\alpha
, the composition of
\beta
and
\alpha
, which is obviously surjective. From the construction of the sequence
\{l\}_{0}^{I}
, we know that
\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}
and
\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}
are two adjacent elements in
\mathcal{T}=\{\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\}_{q=0}^{K}
. In simpler terms, we have

\gamma(l+1)-\gamma(l)=\pm 1.

Therefore, we denote the pre-image of
\gamma
as
\gamma^{-1}
, and we can rewrite the first summation of (
A.6
) as

(A.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{l,(l+1)\in\mathcal{N}^{+}}\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\sum_{q=0}^{K-1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\gamma^{-1}(q)\\
l+1\in\gamma^{-1}(q+1)\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l+1\in\gamma^{-1}(q)\\
l\in\gamma^{-1}(q+1)\end{subarray}}\right)\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}).\end{aligned}

Next, we note that both
\{\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\}_{l=0}^{I}
and
\{\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}\}_{q=0}^{K}
construct a path from
\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}
to
\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}=\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F},&l\in\gamma^{-1}(q)\text{ and }l+1\in\gamma^{-1}(q+1),\\

In other words, each path from
\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}
to
\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}

\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}
to
\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}
or from
\|\Delta^{x}_{q+1}\|_{F}
to
\|\Delta^{x}_{q}\|_{F}
. Since
\|\Delta^{x}(n^{\infty})\|_{F}>\|\Delta^{x}(0)\|_{F}
we can check that for fixed

\left|\{l:l\in\gamma^{-1}(q)~\text{and }l+1\in\gamma^{-1}(q+1)\}\right|=\left|\{l:l\in\gamma^{-1}(q+1)~\text{and }l+1\in\gamma^{-1}(q)\}\right|+1,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in\gamma^{-1}(q)\\

l\in\gamma^{-1}(q+1)\end{subarray}}\right)\left(\|\Delta^{x}(l+1)\|_{F}-\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F}\right)\psi(\|\Delta^{x}(l)\|_{F})

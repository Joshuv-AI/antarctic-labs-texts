The Loewner Energy via the Renormalised Energy of Moving Frames
Alexis Michelat, Yilin Wang

Abstract
We obtain a new formula for the Loewner energy of Jordan curves on the sphere, which is a Kähler potential for the essentially unique Kähler metric on the Weil-Petersson universal Teichmüller space, as the renormalised energy of moving frames on the two domains of the sphere delimited by the given curve.

53C42, 30C35.

Background on Weil-Petersson quasicircles

In

In this article, we sometimes view Jordan curves as curves on
S^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and give new characterisations of the Loewner energy in terms of the moving frames on
S^{2}
.
Note that in this article,
S^{2}
refers to the sphere of radius
1
centred at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
equipped with the induced round metric
g_{0}
from its embedding into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Therefore,
S^{2}
is isometric to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
endowed with the metric

\displaystyle g_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}=\frac{4|dz|^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}

by the stereographic projection.
To distinguish the two setups, we will let
\gamma
denote a Jordan curve in

\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and let
\Gamma
denote a Jordan curve in

. Let us
first list a few equivalent definitions of Weil-Petersson quasicircles that are

Theorem 1.1

(Cui,

Let
\gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
be a Jordan curve,
\Omega
be the bounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\gamma

f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\Omega
and
g:\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
be biholomorphic maps such that
g(\infty)=\infty
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a quasiconformal extension of
g

\mathbb{C}
such that the Beltrami coefficient
\mu=\dfrac{\partial_{\overline{z}}g}{\partial_{z}g}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\mu(z)|^{2}\frac{|dz|^{2}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}<\infty

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla\log|f^{\prime}(z)||^{2}|dz|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}<\infty
.

(3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}<\infty
.

(4)

The
(conformal) welding function

H^{1/2}(S^{1})
.

(5)

The curve
\gamma

belongs to
H^{1/2}(\gamma)
.

(6)

Every minimal surface

has finite renormalised area,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{RA}(\Sigma)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\mathrm{Area}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon})-\mathrm{Length}(\partial\Sigma_{\varepsilon})\right)=-2\pi\chi(\Sigma)-\int_{\Sigma}|\mathring{A}|^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}>-\infty,}

(1.1)

where for all

and
\partial\Sigma_{\varepsilon}=\Sigma\cap\left\{(z,t):t=\varepsilon\right\}

If
\gamma

.

The equivalences

In

Not only we can characterize this class of curves qualitatively, as listed above, there is an important quantity associated with each element of the class.
Indeed, after appropriate normalisation, the class of Weil-Petersson quasicircles can be identified with the Weil-Petersson universal Teichmüller space
T_{0}(1)
via conformal welding. Takhtajan and Teo
[

showed that

carries an essentially unique homogeneous Kähler metric and introduced the
universal Liouville action

S_{1}
. They showed that

is a Kähler potential on

which is of critical importance for the Kähler geometry.
We take an analytic instead of a Teichmüller theoretic viewpoint, so we will consider

as defined for Weil-Petersson quasicircles instead of their welding functions.
Explicitly, for a Weil-Petersson quasicircle

\displaystyle S_{1}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}+4\pi\log|f^{\prime}(0)|-4\pi\log|g^{\prime}(\infty)|.

(1.3)

(Y. Wang,

A Jordan curve

(1.4)

We will therefore use interchangeably the terms

“Weil-Petersson quasicircle,”

\displaystyle I^{L}(\gamma)=I^{L}({\mathfrak{i}}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-2\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+\frac{2}{z}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}-2\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{2}{z}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}+4\log|f^{\prime}(0)|-4\log|g^{\prime}(\infty)|.

1.2
Moving Frames and the Ginzburg-Landau Equations

Moving frames, first introduced by Darboux in the late
19
th century to study curves and surfaces, were later generalised by Élie Cartan and permit one to reformulate astutely a wide class of differential-geometric problems. One of the rather recent such use of this theory is found in the work of Frédéric Hélein on harmonic maps (
[
19
]
), where the moving frame pave the way towards new regularity results.

In
[
23
]
, Paul Laurain and Romain Petrides suggest a new approach to relate
the Loewner energy
to the renormalised energy of moving frames using the Ginzburg-Landau energy in a minimal regularity setting (which is of independent interest). Although the Ginzburg-Landau is normally used to construct harmonic maps with values into
S^{1}
under topological constraints where no smooth solutions exist (
[
3
]
), it should be seen—although we will not use this functional here—more generally as a way to construct (singular) moving frames on surfaces. Through this approach, one may hope to link quantatively the Loewner energy and the Willmore energy that can also be written in terms of moving frames (
[
26
]
).

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain, and
\gamma=\partial\Omega
. In
[
23
]
, they show that the Bethuel-Brezis-Hélein (
[
3
]
) analysis carries on for general chorc-arc curves and
H^{1/2}
boundary data. Using this delicate analysis, they obtain the following result, which is the most relevant one in this article.

Theorem 1.3

(Laurain-Petrides,
[
23
]
, Theorem
0.2
, Theorem
0.3
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded simply connected domain such that
\gamma=\partial\Omega
is a Weil-Petersson quasicircle. Then, there exists a harmonic map
\vec{u}:\Omega\setminus\left\{p\right\}\rightarrow S^{1}
with boundary data
\tau:\Gamma\rightarrow S^{1}
which is the unit tangent vector of
\partial\Omega=\Gamma
. Let
\vec{v}=-i\,\vec{u}
and
\omega=\langle\vec{u},d\vec{v}\,\rangle
, then there exists a harmonic function
\mu:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\omega=\ast\,d\left(G_{\Omega}+\mu\right)
, and a conformal map
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\Omega
such that
f(0)=p
, and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}f&=e^{\mu\circ f}\vec{u}\circ f\\
\partial_{r}f&=e^{\mu\circ f}\vec{v}\circ f.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\omega-\ast\,dG_{\Omega}|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mu|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2},

(1.7)

where
G_{\Omega}
is a Green’s function with Dirichlet boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
.

The other main result of
[
23
]
is to identify the renormalised energy in the sense of Bethuel-Brezis-Hélein as an explicit term involving (
1.7
).

Remark 1.4
.

The harmonic function
\mu
is explicitly given by
\mu=\log|\partial_{r}f|\circ f^{-1}=\log\left|\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}f\right|\circ f^{-1}=\log|f^{\prime}|\circ f^{-1}
. The last identities follow
from the conformality of

. We note that in
[
23
]
, the point

is a special point such that any biholomorphic map

with

maximizes

amongst all biholomorphic maps

We see that the frame energy (

) coincides with the first term in (
1.3
). To obtain the second half of the Loewner energy involving

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2},

(1.8)

we cannot easily use the Ginzburg-Landau equation to construct the moving frames since that would force us to work on the non-compact domain
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.
Using the inversion
{\mathfrak{i}}
will not suffice either.
If we choose the biholomorphic map
\widetilde{g}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{i}}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
so that
\widetilde{g}={\mathfrak{i}}\circ g\circ{\mathfrak{i}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{\widetilde{g}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{\widetilde{g}^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\left|\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}-2\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}+\frac{2}{z}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}

which is in general different from (
1.8
).
To overcome this technicality,
we work directly on
S^{2}
to obtain a formula of the Loewner energy in terms of moving frames.

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega_{1}}\partial_{\nu}\left(-\mu_{1}+\theta_{1}\right)d\mathscr{H}^{1}_{g_{0}}+\int_{\partial\Omega_{2}}\partial_{\nu}\left(-\mu_{2}+\theta_{2}\right)d\mathscr{H}^{1}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\int_{\Gamma}k_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-2\pi\right)-2\pi-\left(-\int_{\Gamma}k_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-2\pi\right)-2\pi=0

which proves (
2.23
).

Finally, we deduce by (
2
), (
2
) and (
2.23
) that

\displaystyle-\pi\,I^{L}(\Gamma)=-\int_{\Omega_{1}}|d\mu_{1}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-2\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{\Omega_{1}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-4\pi\mu_{1}(p_{1})+4\pi\log(2)-2\pi

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{2}}|d\mu_{2}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-2\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{\Omega_{2}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-4\pi\mu_{2}(p_{2})+4\pi\log(2)-2\pi

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{1}}|d\mu_{1}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-\int_{\Omega_{2}}|d\mu_{2}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-2\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{\Omega_{1}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}-2\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{\Omega_{2}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle-4\pi\mu_{1}(p_{1})-4\pi\mu_{2}(p_{2})+8\pi\log(2)-4\pi.

(2.26)

Recalling the identity (
2.7
), we finally deduce that

\displaystyle\pi\,I^{L}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}|d\mu_{1}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}|d\mu_{2}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+2\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{\Omega_{1}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+2\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{\Omega_{2}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+4\pi

\displaystyle+4\pi\log|\nabla f_{1}(0)|+4\pi\log|\nabla f_{2}(0)|-12\pi\log(2).

(2.27)

Now we introduce the functional

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}|d\mu_{1}|^{2}_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+\int_{\Omega_{2}}|d\mu_{2}|^{2}_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+2\int_{\Omega_{1}}G_{\Omega_{1}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+2\int_{\Omega_{2}}G_{\Omega_{2}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+4\pi.

(2.28)

We deduce that

\displaystyle I^{L}(\Gamma)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathscr{E}(\Gamma)+4\log|\nabla f_{1}(0)|+4\log|\nabla f_{2}(0)|-12\log(2).

(2.29)

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

We check that equality (
2.29
) holds for the equator
S^{1}
. Using the definition (
1.3
) with the conformal maps
f,g
being the identity maps, we see that
I^{L}(S^{1})
vanishes.

Let us first check that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(S^{1})=0

with the marked points
p_{1}=S=(0,0,-1)
and
p_{2}=N=(0,0,1)
.
This identity justifies the term
4\pi
in the definition of
\mathscr{E}
as we remarked earlier.

For this, since
K_{g_{0}}=1
on
\Omega_{1}=S_{-}^{2}
,
after making a stereographic projection
\pi:S^{2}\setminus\{N\}\to\mathbb{C}
sending
S
to
0
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}_{-}}G_{S^{2}_{-}}K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}G_{\mathbb{D}}(z)\frac{4|dz|^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{4\log|z|}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}|dz|^{2}=8\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r\log r}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}dr

\displaystyle=8\pi\left[-\frac{1}{2(1+r^{2})}\log(r)+\frac{1}{2}\log(r)-\frac{1}{4}\log(1+r^{2})\right]_{0}^{1}=-2\pi\log(2).

(2.30)

We take
f_{1}=\pi^{-1}|_{\mathbb{D}}
which is consistent with
f_{1}(0)=p_{1}=S
. By (
2.4
) we have
\partial_{\nu}\mu=-1
on
S^{1}
and
-\Delta_{g_{0}}\mu=1
in
S^{2}_{-}
which translates to

\displaystyle-\Delta\mu(z)=\frac{4}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}\qquad\text{in}\;\,\mathbb{D}.

We deduce by a direct verification that

\mu(z)=-\log(1+|z|^{2})
. (This is easy to guess since by (
2.6
),
\mu
can be computed from the conformal factor of
f_{1}
.)

Therefore, we have by the conformal invariance of the Dirichlet energy

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}_{-}}\left|\omega-\ast dG_{S^{2}_{-}}\right|^{2}_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{2}_{-}}|d\mu|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla\mu(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{2x}{1+|x|^{2}}\right|^{2}dx=8\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{3}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}dr

\displaystyle=8\pi\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{r}{1+r^{2}}-\frac{r}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\right)dr=8\pi\left[\frac{1}{2}\log(1+r^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}\right]_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle=8\pi\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(2)-\frac{1}{4}\right)=4\pi\log(2)-2\pi.

(2.31)

Finally, by (
2.8
) and (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}_{-}}|\omega-\ast\,dG_{S^{2}_{-}}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+2\int_{S^{2}_{-}}G_{S^{2}_{-}}\,K_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}+2\pi

\displaystyle=\left(4\pi\log(2)-2\pi\right)-4\pi\log(2)+2\pi=0.

Applying the same computation to

with

which is consistent with the choice

.

Now we show that

Since the inverse stereographic projection is given by

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\pi^{-1}(z)=\left(\frac{2\,\mathrm{Re}\,(z)}{1+|z|^{2}},\frac{2\,\mathrm{Im}\,(z)}{1+|z|^{2}},\frac{-1+|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}\right),

we compute directly that

\displaystyle|\nabla f_{1}(0)|=|\nabla f_{2}(0)|=2\sqrt{2}

which concludes the proof of (

) for the circle

.

3
Construction of Harmonic Moving Frames for Weil-Petersson Curves

In the previous section, we showed that in the case of smooth curves, the Loewner energy was equal to a renormalised Dirichlet energy of a specific harmonic moving frame. In this section, we will directly construct harmonic moving frames satisfying appropriate boundary conditions for arbitrary Weil-Petersson quasicircles. In the next section, we will show that Theorem
2.6
holds for non-smooth curves.

Before stating the main theorem of this section, recall an easy lemma on harmonic vector fields.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a smooth surface,
\vec{n}:\Sigma\rightarrow S^{2}
its unit normal, and
g=\iota^{\ast}g_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
be the induced metric. Assume that
\vec{u}:\Sigma\rightarrow S^{2}
is a smooth critical point of the Dirichlet energy amongst
S^{2}
-valued maps such that
\langle{\vec{u}},\vec{n}\rangle=0
.
Then
\vec{u}
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equation:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}\vec{u}=|d\vec{u}|_{g}^{2}\vec{u}+\left(\langle d\vec{u},d\vec{n}\rangle_{g}+\langle\vec{u},\Delta_{g}\vec{n}\rangle\right)\vec{n}.

(3.1)

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
19
]
in Lemme (
1.4.10
), taking variations
\vec{X}
that also satisfy
\langle\vec{X},\vec{n}\rangle=0
.
∎

The following result is the same as Theorem
2.6
, but for a general Weil-Petersson quasicircle.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subset S^{2}
a Weil-Petersson quasicircle and let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
be the two open connected components of
S^{2}\setminus\Gamma
.
For
j=1,2
and for all
p_{j}\in\Omega_{j}
, there exists a harmonic moving frame
(\vec{u}_{j},\vec{v}_{j}):\Omega_{j}\setminus\left\{p_{j}\right\}\rightarrow U\Omega_{j}\times U\Omega_{j}
such that the Cartan form
\omega_{j}=\langle\vec{u}_{j},d\vec{v}_{j}\rangle
admits the decomposition

\displaystyle\omega_{j}=\ast\,d\left(G_{\Omega_{j}}+\mu_{j}\right),

(3.2)

where
G_{\Omega_{j}}=G_{\Omega_{j},p_{j}}:\Omega_{j}\setminus\left\{p_{j}\right\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the Green’s function of the Laplacian
\Delta_{g_{0}}
on
\Omega_{j}
with Dirichlet boundary condition and singularity
p_{j}\in\Omega_{j}
, and
\mu_{j}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{j})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{g_{0}}\mu_{j}&=1\qquad&&\text{in}\;\,\Omega_{j}\\
\partial_{\nu}\mu_{j}&=k_{g_{0}}-\partial_{\nu}G_{\Omega_{j}}\qquad&&\text{on}\;\,\partial\Omega_{j},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

where
k_{g_{0}}
is the geodesic curvature on
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{j}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The Neumann condition for
\mu_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq 2
) is understood in the sense of distributions, since the geodesic curvature is only in
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
in general (see the appendix for more details).

Proof.

Rather than using the moving frame that comes from a Ginzburg-Landau type minimisation as in
[
23
]
—that would have had to be carried in the geometric setting of domains of
S^{2}
—we directly use the uniformisation theorem and the geometric formula of
[
41
]
(that does not require any regularity on the curve
\Gamma
) to construct the relevant moving frame.
We now construct the moving frame on
\Omega_{1}
. The construction for
\Omega_{2}
is similar.

Step 1.
Definition of
(\vec{u}_{1},\vec{v}_{1})
and
\mu_{1}
.

Let
\pi:S^{2}\setminus\left\{N\right\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the standard stereographic projection and assume without loss of generality that
N\in\Omega_{2}
. Let
\Omega=\pi(\Omega_{1})\subset\mathbb{C}
be the image domain and
\gamma=\pi(\Gamma)\subset\mathbb{C}
be the image curve. Thanks to the Uniformisation Theorem, there exists a univalent holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\Omega
such that
f(0)=\pi(p_{1})
.

Now, let
f_{1}=\pi^{-1}\circ f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\Omega_{1}
. Notice that
f_{1}(0)=p_{1}
.
Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\pi^{-1}(f(z))=\left(\frac{2\,\mathrm{Re}\,(f(z))}{1+|f(z)|^{2}},\frac{2\,\mathrm{Im}\,(f(z))}{1+|f(z)|^{2}},\frac{-1+|f(z)|^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\right).

A direct computation show that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}f_{1}=f^{\prime}\left(\frac{(1-\overline{f}^{2})}{(1+|f|^{2})^{2}},\frac{-i(1+\overline{f}^{2})}{(1+|f|^{2})^{2}},\frac{2\overline{f}}{(1+|f|^{2})^{2}}\right).

Now, by analogy with the construction in Section
2
(see also
[
23
]
, Proposition
5.1
), define
\mu_{1}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\vec{u}_{1}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow US^{2}
and
\vec{v}_{1}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow US^{2}
by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{r}f_{1}&=e^{\mu_{1}\circ f_{1}}\vec{v}_{1}\circ f_{1}\\
\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}f_{1}&=e^{\mu_{1}\circ f_{1}}\vec{u}_{1}\circ f_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, we have from direct computations

\displaystyle e^{2\mu_{1}\circ f_{1}}=|\partial_{r}f_{1}|^{2}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{\theta}f_{1}|^{2}=2|\partial_{z}f_{1}|^{2}=\frac{4|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}}.

Therefore, we deduce if
\mu=\mu_{1}\circ f_{1}
that

\displaystyle\mu(z)=\log|f^{\prime}(z)|-\log\left(1+|f(z)|^{2}\right)+\log(2).

(3.4)

Since
\partial_{z}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\,\partial_{y})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{r}f_{1}&=\cos(\theta)\partial_{x}f_{1}+\sin(\theta)\partial_{y}f_{1}=\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\mathrm{Re}\,(\partial_{z}f_{1})-\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\partial_{z}f_{1}\right)\\
\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}f_{1}&=-\sin(\theta)\partial_{x}f_{1}+\cos(\theta)\partial_{y}f_{1}=-\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\mathrm{Re}\,(\partial_{z}f_{1})-\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\partial_{z}f_{1}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

By the elementary identities for all
a,b\in\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Re}\,(a)\mathrm{Re}\,(b)+\mathrm{Im}\,(a)\mathrm{Im}\,(b)&=\mathrm{Re}\,(ab)\\
\mathrm{Re}\,(a)\mathrm{Im}\,(b)+\mathrm{Im}\,(a)\mathrm{Re}\,(b)&=\mathrm{Im}\,(ab),\end{aligned}\right.

we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{r}f_{1}&=\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\partial_{z}f_{1}\right)=\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{\overline{z}}{|z|}\overline{f^{\prime}(z)}\left(\frac{(1-f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}},\frac{i(1+f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}},\frac{2f(z)}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}}\right)\right)\\
\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\theta}f_{1}&=-\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\partial_{z}f_{1}\right)=\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{\overline{z}}{|z|}\overline{f^{\prime}(z)}\left(\frac{(1-f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}},\frac{i(1+f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}},\frac{2f(z)}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}}\right)\right).\end{aligned}\right.

More generally, if
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a smooth complex function, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\varphi=-\mathrm{Im}\,\left(z\right)\left(\partial_{z}+\partial_{\overline{z}}\right)\varphi+\mathrm{Re}\,\left({z}\right)i\left(\partial_{z}-\partial_{\overline{z}}\right)\varphi=i\left(z\,\partial_{z}\varphi-\overline{z}\,\partial_{\overline{z}}\varphi\right).

(3.5)

Since

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}f_{1}|=\frac{1}{r}|\partial_{\theta}f_{1}|=\frac{2|f^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^{2}},

we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \vec{v}_{1}\circ f_{1}&=\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\overline{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{|zf^{\prime}(z)|}}\left(\frac{(1-f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})},\frac{i(1+f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})},\frac{2f(z)}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})}\right)\right)\\
\vec{u}_{1}\circ f_{1}&=\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\overline{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{|zf^{\prime}(z)|}}\left(\frac{(1-f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})},\frac{i(1+f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})},\frac{2f(z)}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})}\right)\right).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.6)

Notice that

\displaystyle F(z)=\left((1-f(z)^{2}),i(1+f(z)^{2}),2f(z)\right)

is a holomorphic null vector,
i.e.

\langle F(z),F(z)\rangle=0
, so we see directly since
|\vec{u}_{1}|=|\vec{v}_{1}|=1
that

\displaystyle\langle\vec{u}_{1},\vec{v}_{1}\rangle=0.

Step 2.
Verification of the system (
3.3
).

Part 1.
Equation on
\Omega_{1}
for
\mu_{1}
.

Since
\mu=\mu_{1}\circ f_{1}
, the equation
-\Delta_{g_{0}}\mu_{1}=1
is equivalent to

\displaystyle-\Delta\mu=e^{2\mu}

(3.7)

Thanks to the explicit expression in (
3.4
), and by harmonicity of
\log|f^{\prime}|
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta\mu=4\,\partial_{\overline{z}}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)\overline{f^{\prime}(z)}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\right)=4\left(\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}-\frac{|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|f(z)|^{2}}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}}\right)=\frac{4|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}}=e^{2\mu}.

Recalling that

\displaystyle g_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}=\frac{4|dz|^{2}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}=(\pi^{-1})^{\ast}g_{0},

we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{4|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|dz|^{2}}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})^{2}}=f^{\ast}g_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}=f^{\ast}((\pi^{-1})^{\ast}g_{0})=(\pi^{-1}\circ f)^{\ast}g_{0}=f_{1}^{\ast}g_{0}.

Therefore, (
3.7
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-\Delta_{f_{1}^{\ast}g_{0}}(\mu_{1}\circ f_{1})=1

or by conformal invariance of the Dirichlet energy

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g_{0}}\mu_{1}=1.

(3.8)

Part 2.
Boundary conditions.

If
{h}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}\partial_{x}{h}+\frac{y}{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}\partial_{y}{h}=\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,(z)}{|z|}\left(\partial_{z}+\partial_{\overline{z}}\right){h}+\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,(z)}{|z|}i\left(\partial_{z}-\partial_{\overline{z}}\right){h}=2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{z}{|z|}\partial_{z}{h}\right).

This implies since
\mu(z)=\log|f^{\prime}(z)|-\log(1+|f(z)|^{2})+\log(2)
by (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mu(z)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\frac{|f(z)|^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\mu=\mathrm{Re}\,\left(z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-2z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\frac{|f(z)|^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\right)

on
\partial\mathbb{D}
in the distributional sense. We will comment on it in Remark
4.6
.
Recall that the geodesic curvature on
\partial\Omega_{1}

is given (see
[
10
]
) by

\displaystyle k_{g_{0}}=\langle\vec{u}_{1},\partial_{\theta}\vec{v}_{1}\rangle.

(3.9)

From (
3.6
) it is natural to define

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=\overline{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{|zf^{\prime}(z)|}}\left(\frac{(1-f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})},\frac{i(1+f(z)^{2})}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})},\frac{2f(z)}{(1+|f(z)|^{2})}\right)=\chi(z)\psi(z),

where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \chi(z)&=\frac{\overline{zf^{\prime}(z)}}{|zf^{\prime}(z)|}=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\overline{zf^{\prime}(z)}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(zf^{\prime}(z)\right)\right)\\
\psi(z)&=\left(\frac{1-f(z)^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}},\frac{i(1+f(z)^{2})}{1+|f(z)|^{2}},\frac{2f(z)}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\right),\end{aligned}\right.

(3.10)

so that
\vec{v}_{1}\circ f_{1}=\mathrm{Re}\,(\varphi)
and
\vec{u}_{1}\circ f_{1}=\mathrm{Im}\,(\varphi)
.
Then, we compute

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{z}\chi&=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+\frac{1}{z}\right)\chi\\
\partial_{\overline{z}}\chi&=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}+\frac{1}{z}\right)}\chi.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.11)

We also get

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{z}\psi&=-\frac{f^{\prime}(z)\overline{f(z)}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\psi+\frac{2f^{\prime}(z)}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\left(-f(z),i\,f(z),1\right)\\
\partial_{\overline{z}}\psi&=-\frac{\overline{f^{\prime}(z)}f(z)}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\psi.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.12)

Since
\langle\psi,\psi\rangle=0
,
we have
\langle\partial_{z}\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle\partial_{\overline{z}}\psi,\psi\rangle=0
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\langle(-f(z),i\,f(z),1),\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\langle(-f(z),i\,f(z),1),(1-f(z)^{2},i(1+f(z)^{2}),2f(z))\rangle=0,

(3.13)

while

\displaystyle\left\langle(-f(z),i\,f(z),1),\left(1-\overline{f(z)}^{2},-i\left(1+\overline{f(z)}^{2}\right),2\overline{f(z)}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-f(z)+\overline{f(z)}|f(z)|^{2}+f(z)+\overline{f(z)}|f(z)|^{2}+2{\overline{f(z)}}=2\overline{f(z)}(1+|f(z)|^{2}),

so that

\displaystyle\langle(-f(z),i\,f(z),1),{\overline{\psi}}\rangle=2\overline{f(z)}.

(3.14)

Therefore, we deduce by (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\partial_{z}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\partial_{\overline{z}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle\partial_{z}\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle\partial_{\overline{z}}\psi,\psi\rangle=0\\
&\langle\partial_{z}\psi,\overline{\psi}\rangle=\frac{2f^{\prime}(z)\overline{f(z)}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\\
&\langle\partial_{\overline{z}}\psi,\overline{\psi}\rangle=-\frac{2\overline{f^{\prime}(z)}f(z)}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.15)

The identities (
3.11
) and (
3.15
) imply that

\displaystyle z\,\partial_{z}\varphi-\overline{z}\,\partial_{z}\varphi=-\left(\mathrm{Re}\,\left(z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+1\right)\varphi+\chi(z)\left(z\,\partial_{z}\psi-\overline{z}\,\partial_{\overline{z}}\psi\right),

and since
|\chi|^{2}=1
and
|\psi|^{2}=2
, we have

\displaystyle\langle z\,\partial_{z}\varphi-\overline{z}\,\partial_{\overline{z}}\varphi,\varphi+\overline{\varphi}\rangle=-2\,\left(\mathrm{Re}\,\left(z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+1\right)

\displaystyle+\chi(z)\left(z\left(\chi(z)\langle\partial_{z}\psi,\psi\rangle+\overline{\chi(z)}\langle\partial_{z}\psi,\overline{\psi}\rangle\right)-\overline{z}\left(\chi(z)\langle\partial_{\overline{z}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle+\overline{\chi(z)}\langle\partial_{\overline{z}}\psi,\overline{\psi}\rangle\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-2\,\left(\mathrm{Re}\,\left(z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+1\right)+2z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\frac{|f(z)|^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}+2\overline{z}\overline{\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}}\frac{|f(z)|^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-2-2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\left(z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-2z\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\frac{|f(z)|^{2}}{1+|f(z)|^{2}}\right),

so that

\displaystyle k_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle\vec{u},\partial_{\theta}\vec{v}\rangle=-\langle\partial_{\theta}\vec{u},\vec{v}\rangle=-\langle\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{Im}\,(\varphi),\mathrm{Re}\,(\varphi)\rangle=-\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\langle\partial_{\theta}\varphi,\mathrm{Re}\,(\varphi)\rangle\right)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{C}\cap\left\{z:\mathrm{Im}\,(z)>0\right\}
be the Poincaré half-plane, and
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a univalent holomorphic map,
\Omega=f(\mathbb{H})
, and assume that
\gamma=\partial\Omega
is a simple curve of finite Loewner energy. Then the geodesic curvature
k_{g_{0}}
of
\gamma
is given in the distributional sense by

\displaystyle k_{g_{0}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\qquad\text{for all}\;\,z\in\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{R}.

(5.1)

Proof.

The geodesic curvature is given by

\displaystyle k_{g_{0}}=\langle\partial_{x}\vec{u},\vec{v}\rangle,

if
(\vec{u},\vec{v})
is the Cartesian frame given by (in the following formulae,
f
is seen as a
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued function)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \vec{u}&=\frac{\partial_{x}f}{|\partial_{x}f|}=\left(\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right),\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)\right)=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\\
\vec{v}&=\frac{\partial_{y}f}{|\partial_{y}f|}=\left(-\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right),\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)\right)=i\,\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}.\end{aligned}\right.

Define
\vec{u}_{z}=\dfrac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\vec{u}_{z}

\displaystyle=\partial_{z}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\vec{u}_{z}

\displaystyle\partial_{\overline{z}}\vec{u}_{z}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\overline{z}}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{\prime}(z)^{2}\overline{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}}{|f^{\prime}(z)|^{3}}=-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)}\vec{u}_{z}.

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{z}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)+\overline{\partial_{\overline{z}}\left(\frac{{f^{\prime}(z)}}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\left(\vec{u}_{z}-\vec{u}_{\overline{z}}\right)=\frac{i}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}}\right)=-\frac{i}{4}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\left(\vec{u}_{z}+\vec{u}_{\overline{z}}\right)=-\frac{i}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)

where we used

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\partial_{z}\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)\right)=-\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\mathrm{Re}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right),

so that

\partial_{x}\vec{u}=\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\vec{v},

and

\displaystyle k_{g_{0}}=\langle\partial_{x}\vec{u},\vec{v}\rangle=\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right),

which concludes the proof of the lemma.

\displaystyle|i+\log(z)|^{2}=|\log|z|+i(1+\arg(z))|^{2}=\log^{2}|z|+(1+\arg(z))^{2}

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}=\frac{(\log|z|-1)^{2}+(1+\arg(z))^{2}}{|z|^{2}\log^{2}|z|(\log^{2}|z|+(1+\arg(z))^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\log^{2}|z|}+\frac{-2\log|z|+1}{\log^{4}|z|}\leq\frac{2}{|z|^{2}\log^{4}|z|}+\frac{2}{|z|^{2}\log^{2}|z|},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}(0,\frac{1}{2})}\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2}|dz|^{2}\leq 4\pi\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{dr}{r\log^{4}(r)}+4\pi\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{dr}{r\log^{2}(r)}=\frac{4\pi}{\log(2)}+\frac{4\pi}{3\log^{2}(2)}<\infty,

which shows that
\gamma
is a Weil-Petersson quasicircle. Then, we have by Lemma
5.1
for

\displaystyle k_{g_{0}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)=\frac{1-\log|z|+\log^{2}|z|}{|z|\log|z|(1+\log^{2}|z|)}=-\frac{1}{|z|(1+\log^{2}|z|)}+\frac{1}{|z|\log|z|},

which concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

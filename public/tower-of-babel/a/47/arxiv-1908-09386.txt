A variational formulation for steady surface water waves on a Beltrami flow
M. D. Groves 1 and J. Horn 1

Abstract
This paper considers steady surface waves ‘riding’ a Beltrami flow (a three-dimensional flow with parallel velocity and vorticity fields). It is demonstrated that the hydrodynamic problem can be formulated as two equations for two scalar functions of the horizontal spatial coordinates, namely the elevation η \eta of the free surface and the potential Φ \Phi defining the gradient part (in the sense of the Hodge-Weyl decomposition) of the horizontal component of the tangential fluid velocity there. These equations are written in terms of a nonlocal operator H ⁡ ( η ) H(\eta) mapping Φ \Phi to the normal fluid velocity at the free surface, and are shown to arise from a variational principle. In the irrotational limit the equations reduce to the Zakharov-Craig-Sulem formulation of the classical three-dimensional steady water-wave problem, while H ⁡ ( η ) H(\eta) reduces to the familiar Dirichlet-Neumann operator.

Introduction

1.1
The main results

Consider an incompressible perfect fluid of unit density
occupying a three-dimensional domain bounded below by a rigid horizontal plane and above
by a free surface. A
steady water wave
is a fluid flow of this kind in which both the velocity field and free-surface profile
are stationary with respect to a uniformly (horizontally) translating frame of reference. Working in this frame of
reference, suppose that the fluid domain is

D_{\eta}=\{(x,y,z):-h<y<\eta(x,z)\}
(so that the free surface is the graph
S_{\eta}

of an unknown function
\eta
), and
the flow is a
(strong) Beltrami flow
whose velocity and vorticity fields
\mathbf{u}
and
\curl\mathbf{u}
are parallel, so
that
\curl\mathbf{u}=\alpha\mathbf{u}
for some fixed constant
\alpha
.
The hydrodynamic problem is to solve the equations

\div\mathbf{u}=0

in
D_{\eta}
,

(1)

\curl\mathbf{u}=\alpha\mathbf{u}

in
D_{\eta}
,

(2)

\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{j}=0

at
y=-h
,

(3)

\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0

at
y=\eta
,

(4)

\displaystyle\parbox{284.52756pt}{$\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+g\eta-\sigma\!\left(\frac{\eta_{x}}{(1+|\nabla\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)_{\!\!\!x}-\sigma\!\left(\frac{\eta_{z}}{(1+|\nabla\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)_{\!\!\!z}=\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{c}|^{2}$}\mbox{at $y=\eta$},

(5)

where
\mathbf{n}
denotes the (upward-pointing) unit normal vector at
S_{\eta}
,
\mathbf{j}=(0,1,0)
,
\nabla=(\partial_{x},\partial_{z})^{\mathrm{T}}
and the physical constants

,

\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},c_{3})^{\mathrm{T}}

are respectively the acceleration due to gravity, the coefficient of surface tension and the wave velocity;
the pressure
p

is recovered using the formula
p(x,y,z)=-\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}(x,y,z)|^{2}-gy
(the variables
\mathbf{u}
and

the stationary Euler equation).
Equations (
4

It is natural to write

as a perturbation of the trivial solution

\eta^{\star}=0,\qquad\mathbf{u}^{\star}=c_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha y\\
0\\
\sin\alpha y\end{pmatrix}+c_{3}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\alpha y\\

\cos\alpha y\end{pmatrix}

\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{u}^{\star}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\star}+g\eta-\sigma\!\left(\frac{\eta_{x}}{(1+|\nabla\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)_{\!\!\!x}-\sigma\!\left(\frac{\eta_{z}}{(1+|\nabla\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)_{\!\!\!z}=0

(11)

\delta\mathcal{L}_{0}(\eta,\xi)=0,

\displaystyle G(\eta)\xi+\mathbf{c}.\nabla\eta=0,

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla\xi|^{2}-\frac{(G(\eta)\xi+\mathbf{c}.\nabla\eta+\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\xi)^{2}}{2(1+|\nabla\eta|^{2})}

\displaystyle\qquad\mbox{}-\mathbf{c}.\nabla\xi+g\eta-\sigma\!\left(\frac{\eta_{x}}{(1+|\nabla\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)_{\!\!\!x}-\sigma\!\left(\frac{\eta_{z}}{(1+|\nabla\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)_{\!\!\!z}=0,

4.1
Hodge-Weyl decomposition

Let us work in the Sobolev spaces

H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):\|u\|_{s}^{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(1+|\mathbf{k}|^{2}\right)^{s}|\hat{u}(\mathbf{k})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{k}<\infty\right\},\qquad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

where

denotes the Fourier transform of

\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):=\{u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}:=\|\nabla u\|_{H^{s-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}<\infty\},\qquad s\geq 0.

For each
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
there exist unique functions
\Phi
,
\Psi\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

such that

\mathbf{f}=\nabla\Phi+\nabla^{\perp}\Psi;

this (obviously orthogonal) decomposition of

is its

The functions
\Phi,\Psi
are characterised as the weak solutions

\Delta\Phi=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f}

\Delta\Psi=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{f}

\Phi
and
\Psi
are the unique functions in

such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\nabla\Phi\cdot\nabla\chi=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{f}\cdot\nabla\chi,\qquad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\nabla\Psi\cdot\nabla\chi=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{f}\cdot\nabla^{\perp}\chi

for all
\chi\in\dot{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, and one accordingly
writes
\Phi=\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f})
,
\Psi=\Delta^{-1}(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{f})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For each
s\geq 0
the formulae

\mathbf{f}\mapsto\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{f})
and
\mathbf{f}\mapsto\Delta^{-1}(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{f})

define continuous linear mappings

.

|\alpha|\in[0,\alpha^{\star}]

Using these inequalities, one finds by straightforward calculations that

\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{1},\|\partial_{x}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{1},\|\partial_{z}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{1}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{0},

and it follows from the equation

\partial_{y}^{2}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})=-\partial_{x}^{2}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})-\partial_{z}^{2}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})-\alpha\curl{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})-\mathbf{H}\\

(45)

that

\|\partial_{yy}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{0}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{0}.

This argument establishes the result for
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
for
m=0
.

Suppose that
\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{s}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
so that

\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m+1}

and obviously

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}\lesssim\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\partial_{x}\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}\mathbf{H}\|_{s}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m+1},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{z}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}\lesssim\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\partial_{z}\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}\lesssim\|\partial_{z}\mathbf{H}\|_{s}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m+1}.

Furthermore, it follows from (
45
) that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{m+3}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||D|\partial_{y}^{m+1}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(|D|\mathbf{H})\|_{0}+\|\partial_{y}^{m+1}{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{1}+\|\partial_{y}^{m+1}\mathbf{H}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(|D|\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}+\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{m+2}+\|\partial_{y}^{m+1}\mathbf{H}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||D|\mathbf{H}\|_{m}+\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m}+\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m+1},

where
|D|={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[|\mathbf{k}|{\mathcal{F}}[\cdot]]
. One concludes that
\|{\mathcal{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{H})\|_{m+3}\lesssim\|\mathbf{H}\|_{m+1}
.

The estimates for
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{3}
are obtained in a similar fashion.
∎

In view of the previous result we henceforth fix
\alpha^{\star}<\frac{\pi}{2h}
and assume
that
|\alpha|\in[0,\alpha^{\star}]
; all results hold uniformly over these values of
\alpha
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
s\geq 2
.
There exists an open neighbourhood
V
of the origin in

such that

)–(

)

.

\tilde{\mathbf{A}}(\eta,\Phi)
depends analytically upon
\eta
and
\Phi
(and linearly upon
\Phi
).

Proof.

Using the estimates

\displaystyle\|f_{1}(\eta)\Delta\eta\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s-2}(D_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f(\eta)\|_{s}\|\Delta\eta\|_{s-\frac{3}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s-1}(D_{0})},

\displaystyle\|f_{2}(\eta,\nabla\eta)\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s-2}(D_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f(\eta,\nabla\eta)\|_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s-2}(D_{0})},

\displaystyle\|f_{2}(\eta,\nabla\eta)\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s-1}(D_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f(\eta,\nabla\eta)\|_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s-1}(D_{0})},

\displaystyle\|\nabla\eta\underline{\smash{\tilde{f}}}\|_{s-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\eta\|_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s}(D_{0})},

\displaystyle\|\nabla\eta\underline{\smash{\tilde{f}}}\|_{s-\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\eta\|_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{H^{s-1}(D_{0})},

where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are analytic mappings of a neighbourhood of the origin in respectively

{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
into
{\mathbb{R}}
,
one finds that the mappings
(\eta,\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\mapsto g^{\eta}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})
,

(\eta,\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\mapsto\mathbf{H}^{\eta}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})
,
(\eta,\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\mapsto\mathbf{h}^{\eta}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})
,
are analytic in
V\times H^{s}(D_{0})^{3}
,
where
V
is an open neighbourhood of the origin in
H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
their target spaces being respectively
H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
H^{s-2}(D_{0})^{3}
and
H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}
. (The above estimates are obtained from standard embedding theorems — see in particular Hörmander

[
13
, Theorem 8.3.1]
, noting that this theorem remains true when
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is replaced by
D_{0}
).
It follows that the formula

{\mathcal{H}}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}},\eta,\Phi)=\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}-\alpha\,\curl{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}-\mathbf{H}^{\eta}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\\
\underline{\smash{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}}\cdot\mathbf{j}-g^{\eta}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\\
(\underline{\smash{\curl{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}}})_{\mathrm{h}}+\alpha\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\cdot\underline{\smash{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}}^{\!\perp}_{\mathrm{h}})-\mathbf{h}^{\eta}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})-\nabla\Phi\end{pmatrix},

defines an analytic mapping

{\mathcal{H}}:S\times V\times\dot{H}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow H^{s-2}(D_{0})^{3}\times H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\times H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2},

where
S=\{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\in H^{s}(D_{0})^{3}:\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\wedge\mathbf{j}\big|_{y=-h}={\mathbf{0}},\tilde{A}_{2y}=0\}
.

Furthermore,
{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbf{0}},0,0)=({\mathbf{0}},0,{\mathbf{0}})
, and the calculation

\mathrm{d}_{1}{\mathcal{H}}[{\mathbf{0}},0,0](\tilde{\mathbf{A}})=\begin{pmatrix}\curl\curl{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}-\alpha\,\curl{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}\\
\underline{\smash{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}}\cdot\mathbf{j}\\
(\underline{\smash{\curl{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}}})_{\mathrm{h}}+\alpha\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\cdot\underline{\smash{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}}}^{\!\perp}_{\mathrm{h}})\end{pmatrix}

and Proposition
4.7
show that

\mathrm{d}_{1}{\mathcal{H}}[{\mathbf{0}},0,0]:S\times H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\times\dot{H}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow H^{s-2}(D_{0})^{3}\times H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\times H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})^{2}

is an isomorphism.
The analytic implicit-function theorem (Buffoni & Toland
[
14
, Theorem 4.5.3]
)
asserts the existence of an open neighbourhoods
W
and
U
of the origin in respectively

H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\times\dot{H}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}})
and

)–(
43
)
has a unique solution

\eta

. Since

one can without loss of generality take

, and clearly

(with

the construction yields a unique solution in a neighbourhood of the origin in

,
which is evidently the zero solution).
∎

Coagulation dynamics under environmental noise: Scaling limit to SPDE Keywords and phrases: scaling limits; coagulation dynamics; stochastic PDE; environmental noise; interacting diffusions; rainfall formation. AMS subject classification (2020): 60K35, 82C21, 60H15, 86A08.
Franco Flandoli and Ruojun Huang

Abstract
We prove that a system of locally interacting diffusions carrying discrete masses, subject to an environmental noise and undergoing mass coagulation, converges to a system of Stochastic Partial Differential Equations (SPDEs) with Smoluchowski-type nonlinearity. Existence, uniqueness and regularity of the SPDEs are also proven.



1
Introduction

Environmental noise in deterministic or stochastic interacting particle systems is a space-dependent noise acting on all particles, opposite to the more common independent noise for each particle. For particles in a fluid, the environmental noise may be an idealized description of a turbulent fluid. An example which motivates the model studied in the present work are the small rain droplets of which clouds are made. Droplets move in the cloud due to the force exerted on them by the surrounding turbulent air, and coagulate when they become sufficiently close to each other. We introduce a particle system modeling these two phenomena and investigate its scaling limit to a continuous density model, which is a stochastic Smoluchowski system. Opposite to independent noise for each particle which becomes a Laplacian in the scaling limit, the environmental noise yields a stochastic transport term in the continuous limit.
Raindrop formation in turbulent fluids has been studied in the Physics literature
[

Remark 3

(a). We have assumed

(b). The reason we chose finitely many mass levels (instead of infinite, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\nabla_{x}q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\theta^{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}+z)dsdx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}

and the fact that due to
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{x}}
uniformly elliptic, the gradient of its
(2d)
-dimensional heat kernel satisfies

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}q_{t}(x,y;x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\frac{1+2d}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{Ct}}

for any
t>0
,
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(cf.
[
26
, Theorem 1]
). Correspondingly, we have that for any
d\geq 1
(cf.
[
14
, Proposition 5, pp. 615]
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla_{x}q_{s}(x,y;x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{C}{s^{\frac{1+2d}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{Cs}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1-2d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{C}}+C1_{\{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}\leq 1\}}.

The rest of the proof is analogous to that for the uniform bounds given above.

4
Bounding various terms

Building on the Itô-Tanaka procedure of Section
3
, still working on the original probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
we complete the proof of Proposition
5
by showing the negligibility of all the minor terms in (
40
).

To prepare, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
let us denote by
[0,\tau_{i}^{N})
the lifespan of particle
i=1,...,N
in our system, namely at the stopping time
\tau_{i}^{N}\in(0,\infty]
particle
i
is removed from the system due to the coagulation rule. Let us also consider on the same probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
an auxiliary “free” particle system
(x_{i}^{f}(t))_{i=1}^{\infty}
, that obeys the same dynamics as (
1
), with
x_{i}^{f}(0)=x_{i}(0)
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and the
same
driving Brownian motions
\{W_{t}^{k}\}_{k\in K}
,
\{\beta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, but without coagulation of masses. In other words,

\displaystyle dx_{i}^{f}(t)=\sum_{k\in K}\sigma_{k}\left(x_{i}^{f}(t)\right)\,\circ\,dW^{k}_{t}+\lambda\,d\beta_{i}(t),\quad i\in\mathbb{N}

(48)

with lifespan
[0,\infty)
. This auxiliary particle system dominates our true system in the following sense:
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle x_{i}^{f}(t)=x_{i}^{N}(t),\quad t\in[0,\tau_{i}^{N}),\;i=1,...,N

(49)

due to the fact that the coefficients of the SDE (
1
) do not depend on the mass parameter.

We start with a lemma proving that any fixed number of particles in the auxiliary system have a joint density that is uniformly bounded and decays exponentially at infinity.
Recall that
\Gamma
is the uniform upper bound on the initial density of an individual particle, and
R
the maximal radius of its compact support.

Proposition 11

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N},d\geq 1
be fixed, and denote

\displaystyle\overline{X}_{t}^{f}

\displaystyle:=\left(x_{1}^{f}(t),...,x_{\ell}^{f}(t)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}

\displaystyle\overline{x}

\displaystyle:=\left(x_{1},...,x_{\ell}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}

where
x_{i}^{f}(t)
are defined above. Then
\overline{X}_{t}^{f}
has a probability density
\mathbf{p}_{t}(\overline{x})
on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}
that satisfies for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{t}(\overline{x})\leq c^{-1}e^{-c|\overline{x}|}

(50)

where
c=c(d,\lambda,\ell,T,R,\Gamma,\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k\in K},)
is a positive constant.

Proof.
Let us denote

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{t}

\displaystyle:=(\beta_{1}(t),...,\beta_{\ell}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}(\overline{x})

\displaystyle:=(\sigma_{k}(x_{1}),...,\sigma_{k}(x_{\ell}))\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}

\displaystyle\left(\nabla\Sigma_{k}\cdot\Sigma_{k}\right)(\overline{x})

\displaystyle:=\left(\left(\nabla\sigma_{k}\cdot\sigma_{k}\right)(x_{1}),...,\left(\nabla\sigma_{k}\cdot\sigma_{k}\right)(x_{\ell})\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}

where
\nabla\sigma_{k}\cdot\sigma_{k}
is defined in (
2
).
Then, by (
48
) the
\ell
-tuple
\overline{X}_{t}^{f}
satisfies the SDE in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}
:

\displaystyle d\overline{X}_{t}^{f}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in K}\Sigma_{k}(\overline{X}_{t}^{f})\,\circ\,dW^{k}_{t}+\lambda\,d\overline{B}_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in K}\Sigma_{k}(\overline{X}_{t}^{f})\,dW^{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in K}\left(\nabla\Sigma_{k}\cdot\Sigma_{k}\right)(\overline{X}_{t}^{f})dt+\lambda\,d\overline{B}_{t}.

This is an Itô diffusion associated with the operator with coefficients of class
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell d})
that acts on functions on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\Delta+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in K}\text{tr}\left(\Sigma_{k}^{T}\Sigma_{k}(\overline{x})D^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in K}\left(\nabla\Sigma_{k}\cdot\Sigma_{k}\right)(\overline{x})\cdot D

thus admits a transition density (i.e. heat kernel)
q^{(\ell)}_{t}(\overline{x},\overline{y})
that is bounded above (and below) by Gaussian kernel (cf.
[
26
, Theorem 1]
), i.e.

q^{(\ell)}_{t}(\overline{x},\overline{y})\leq CQ_{t}(\overline{x}-\overline{y}),\quad\forall t>0,\;\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell d},

where
Q_{t}(\cdot)
is the density of a centered Gaussian vector
\overline{Y}^{0}_{t}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell d}
with covariance matrix
CtI
, for some
C=C\left(d,\ell,\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k\in K},\lambda\right)
finite. Further, the initial condition
\overline{X}_{0}^{f}
has a density
\mathbf{p}_{0}(\overline{x})
bounded above by
\Gamma^{\ell}
, and compactly supported in the ball
\mathbb{B}(0,\sqrt{\ell}R)
, due to Condition
1
.
Then, for any
t\in[0,T]
, the probability density of
\overline{X}_{t}^{f}

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{t}(\overline{x})=\left(q_{t}^{(\ell)}*\mathbf{p}_{0}\right)(\overline{x})\leq\int Q_{t}^{(\ell)}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})\mathbf{p}_{0}(\overline{y})d\overline{y}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{p}_{0}\left(\overline{x}+\overline{Y}_{t}^{0}\right)\right]\leq\Gamma^{\ell}\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{x}+\overline{Y}_{t}^{0}\in\mathbb{B}\left(0,\sqrt{\ell}R\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma^{\ell}\mathbb{P}\left(|\overline{Y}_{t}^{0}|\geq|\overline{x}|-\sqrt{\ell}R\right)\leq c^{-1}e^{-c\left(|x|-\sqrt{\ell}R\right)_{+}},

where we used the Gaussian tail of
\overline{Y}^{0}_{t}
in the last line, and

c=c(d,\lambda,\ell,T,R,\Gamma,\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k\in K})
is a positive constant. Adjusting the value of
c
yields our thesis.

Lemma 12

For any finite
T,d\geq 1
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}dt\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j\in\mathcal{N}(t)}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),x_{j}^{N}(t))\right|=0.

Proof.
Due to (
49
), it is enough to prove the thesis with the auxiliary system replacing the true system, with the former having infinite lifespan, i.e. to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}dt\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j=1}^{N}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x_{i}^{f}(t),x_{j}^{f}(t))\right|=0.

(The same applies to all the subsequent lemmas in this section.)
Consider any fixed pair
(i,j)
, with
1\leq i\neq j\leq N
, and we go on to bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x_{i}^{f}(t),x_{j}^{f}(t))\right|dt.

By Proposition
11
,
(x_{i}^{f}(t),x_{j}^{f}(t))
has a joint density
\mathbf{p}_{t}(x,y)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
satisfying the bound (
50
) with
\ell=2
, implying that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x_{i}^{f}(t),x_{j}^{f}(t))\right|dt

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x,y)\right|\mathbf{p}_{t}(x,y)dtdxdy

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x,y)\right|e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy\,,

(51)

and thus the problem is reduced to prove that

\lim_{|z|\to 0}\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x,y)\right|e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy=0.

By Fatou’s lemma, we have that

\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x,y)\right|e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x,y)\right|e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy

and thus it is sufficient to prove that

\lim_{|z|\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x,y)\right|e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy=0.

By a change of variables, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle v^{\epsilon,z}(T-t,x,y)

\displaystyle=r^{\epsilon,z}(T-t,x,y)-r^{\epsilon,0}(T-t,x,y)

(52)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\left[\theta^{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}+z)-\theta^{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\right]dsdx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left[q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},y^{\prime}+z)-q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right]\theta^{\epsilon}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})dsdx^{\prime}dy^{\prime}

where
q_{t}(x,y;x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
is the
(2d)
-dimensional heat kernel (i.e. fundamental solution) associated with operator
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{x}}
introduced in the proof of Proposition
11
. Hence, as
\epsilon\to 0
,

\displaystyle v^{\epsilon,z}(T-t,x,y)\to\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},x^{\prime}+z)-q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},x^{\prime})\right]dsdx^{\prime}.

Thus, the problem is reduced to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},x^{\prime}+z)-q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},x^{\prime})\right]dsdx^{\prime}\right|e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy=0.

(53)

Since the integrand converges to zero pointwise (see below), to apply Vitali convergence theorem, it suffices to check the uniform integrability in the parameter
|z|\leq 1
, with respect to the finite measure
e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy
, of

A_{x,y,z}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},x^{\prime}+z)dsdx^{\prime}\,;

namely, to prove that for some
\delta>1
, we have that

\sup_{|z|\leq 1}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|A_{x,y,z}|^{\delta}e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy<\infty.

To this end, in the proof of Proposition
8
, we already mentioned that for
d\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}q_{t-s}(x,y;x^{\prime},x^{\prime}+z)ds\leq C\left(|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-z-x^{\prime}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-2d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-z-x^{\prime}|^{2}}{C}}+C1_{\{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-z-x^{\prime}|^{2}\leq 1\}}

(54)

(and the
d=1
case is similar, involving the logarithm).
Let us integrate this expression in the variable
x^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note that, for any
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that

|x-x^{\prime}|\vee|y-z-x^{\prime}|\geq\frac{|x-(y-z)|}{2}.

Thus, for any
x^{\prime}
,

|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|y-z-x^{\prime}|^{2}\geq\frac{|x-y+z|^{2}}{4}.

We separate the integral in
x^{\prime}
into one in the ball
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}\left(\frac{x+(y-z)}{2},|x-y+z|\right)
and the other outside the ball, and then it is easy to show that the first part is upper bounded by
C|x-y+z|^{2-d}
, and the second part by
C|x-y+z|^{2-d}1_{\{d\geq 3\}}+C|\log|x-y+z||1_{\{d=2\}}
. In the case
d=1
, a constant bound can be shown to hold.

Now we need to check, for some
\delta>1
, the uniform finiteness in
|z|\leq 1
of

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|A_{x,y,z}|^{\delta}e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy\leq\begin{cases}C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|x-y+z|^{(2-d)\delta}e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy,\quad d\geq 3\\
C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\log|x-y+z||^{\delta}e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy,\quad d=2\\
C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy,\quad d=1.\end{cases}

Taking
d\geq 3
for instance (the other cases being similar),

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|x-y+z|^{(2-d)\delta}e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy\overset{u=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}},\;v=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2}}}{=}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|\sqrt{2}u+z|^{(2-d)\delta}e^{-\sqrt{|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}}/C}dudv

\displaystyle\leq\iint|\sqrt{2}u+z|^{(2-d)\delta}e^{-(|u|+|v|)/(2C)}dudv\leq C\int|\sqrt{2}u+z|^{(2-d)\delta}e^{-|u|/(2C)}du

\displaystyle\overset{|z|\leq 1}{\leq}C\int_{|u|\leq 2}|\sqrt{2}u+z|^{(2-d)\delta}du+C\int_{|u|>2}e^{-|u|/(2C)}du

\displaystyle\overset{|z|\leq 1}{\leq}C\int_{|w|\leq 4}|w|^{(2-d)\delta}dw+C\leq C^{\prime},

provided we take
\delta\leq d/(d-2)
, where the constant
C^{\prime}
is independent of
|z|\leq 1
.

Note also that
z\mapsto A_{x,y,z}
is continuous at
z=0
by dominated convergence, since LHS of (
54
) is continuous in
z
, a classical fact for Green functions, and RHS of (
54
) is dominated by
C|x-y|^{2-2d}e^{-|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}/C}+C1_{\{|x-x^{\prime}|\leq 1\}}
, for all
|z|\leq|x-y|/4
say (and
x\neq y
), which is integrable in
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

With the integrand of (
53
) converging pointwise to
0
as
|z|\to 0
(i.e. for a.e. fixed
(x,y)
), and uniformly integrable with respect to the finite measure
e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy
, we have by Vitali convergence theorem that

\lim_{|z|\to 0}\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|v^{\epsilon,z}(T-t,x,y)\right|e^{-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}/C}dxdy=0.

Since the cardinality in the double sum in the thesis is (at most)
N^{2}
, this completes the proof.

Lemma 13

For any finite
T,d\geq 1
, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}dt\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j\in\mathcal{N}(t)}\left|\nabla_{x}v^{\epsilon,z}(t,x_{i}^{N}(t),x_{j}^{N}(t))\right|=0.

The same statement also holds for

Proof.
The proof is analogous to Lemma
12
, using the estimate for

(
45

44

Considering any fixed pair of particles
(i,j)
,

Lemma 17

U

\int u_{k}\left(t,x\right)u_{h}\left(t,x\right)dx\leq U.

We have found

\displaystyle d\left\|u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\zeta\int\left|\nabla u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t,x\right)\right|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq Cdt+\left\|u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+2\sum_{k\in K}\left\langle R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right),u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\rangle dW_{t}^{k}+\left(1+\delta^{-1}\right)\sum_{k\in K}\left\|R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

for some constant
C>0
.

5.1.2
Commutator estimate

Let us prove:

Lemma 28

\sum_{k\in K}\left\|R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{Q}\int\left|u_{m}\left(t,x\right)\right|^{2}dx.

for a suitable constant
C_{Q}>0
.

Proof.
Collecting the definitions, we have

\displaystyle R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\cdot\sigma_{k}\left(y\right)u_{m}\left(t,y\right)dy-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\cdot\nabla\int\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)u_{m}\left(t,y\right)dy

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\cdot\left(\sigma_{k}\left(y\right)-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\right)u_{m}\left(t,y\right)dy.

One has

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in K}\left\|R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in K}\iiint\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\cdot\left(\sigma_{k}\left(y\right)-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\right)u_{m}\left(t,y\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)\cdot\left(\sigma_{k}\left(y^{\prime}\right)-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\right)u_{m}\left(t,y^{\prime}\right)dydy^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle\leq U^{2}\iiint\left\|\sum_{k\in K}\left(\sigma_{k}\left(y\right)-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\right)\otimes\left(\sigma_{k}\left(y^{\prime}\right)-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\cdot\left|\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\right|\left|\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)\right|\left|u_{m}\left(t,y\right)\right|\left|u_{m}\left(t,y^{\prime}\right)\right|dydy^{\prime}dx

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|
here denotes the Euclidean matrix norm.
We show below that

\left\|\sum_{k\in K}\left(\sigma_{k}\left(y\right)-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\right)\otimes\left(\sigma_{k}\left(y^{\prime}\right)-\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\right)\right\|\leq C_{Q}\left|x-y\right|\left|x-y^{\prime}\right|

(59)

for some constant
C_{Q}
. Recall the support property of
\rho
; it implies
that
\rho_{\epsilon}
has support in the ball
B\left(0,\epsilon\right)
,
hence the previous expression is bounded by

\displaystyle\leq C_{Q}\epsilon^{-2}\int dx\int_{B\left(x,\epsilon\right)}dy\int_{B\left(x,\epsilon\right)}dy^{\prime}\epsilon^{-2d}\left|\left(\nabla\rho\right)\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\right|\left|\left(\nabla\rho\right)\left(\frac{x-y^{\prime}}{\epsilon}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\cdot\left|x-y\right|\left|x-y^{\prime}\right|\left|u_{m}\left(t,y\right)\right|\left|u_{m}\left(t,y^{\prime}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{Q}\iiint dxdydy^{\prime}\epsilon^{-2d}\left|\left(\nabla\rho\right)\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\right|\left|\left(\nabla\rho\right)\left(\frac{x-y^{\prime}}{\epsilon}\right)\right|\left|u_{m}\left(t,y\right)\right|\left|u_{m}\left(t,y^{\prime}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=C_{Q}\int\left(\int\epsilon^{-d}\left|\left(\nabla\rho\right)\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\right|\left|u_{m}\left(t,y\right)\right|dy\right)^{2}dx.

By Young’s inequality for convolutions, this is bounded by

\leq C_{Q}\int\left|u_{m}\left(t,x\right)\right|^{2}dx.

It remains to prove (
59
). It is equivalent to

\left\|Q\left(y,y^{\prime}\right)-Q\left(y,x\right)-Q\left(x,y^{\prime}\right)+Q\left(x,x\right)\right\|\leq C_{Q}\left|x-y\right|\left|x-y^{\prime}\right|.

It is sufficient to prove a similar estimate componentwise, for the
matrix-value function
Q
. Now

\displaystyle Q_{ij}\left(y,y^{\prime}\right)-Q_{ij}\left(y,x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{2}Q_{ij}\left(y,\alpha\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)+\left(1-\alpha\right)\left(y-x\right)\right)d\alpha\cdot\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)

Q_{ij}\left(x,y^{\prime}\right)-Q_{ij}\left(x,x\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{2}Q_{ij}\left(x,\alpha\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)\right)d\alpha\cdot\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{h}Q_{ij}\left(y,\alpha\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)+\left(1-\alpha\right)\left(y-x\right)\right)-\partial_{h}Q_{ij}\left(x,\alpha\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{2}\partial_{h}Q_{ij}\left(y,\beta\left(\alpha\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)+\left(1-\alpha\right)\left(y-x\right)\right)+\left(1-\beta\right)\left(\alpha\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)\right)\right)d\beta\cdot\left(y-x\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{1}\partial_{h}Q_{ij}(\beta y+(1-\beta)x,\alpha(x-y^{\prime}))d\beta\cdot(y-x).

Collecting these identities, we get the required bound.

5.1.3
Conclusion

From Lemma
28
and the a priori bounds on
u_{m}
we get

\sum_{k\in K}\left\|R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{Q}U\int\left|u_{m}\left(t,x\right)\right|dx\leq C_{Q}U.

Hence

d\left\|u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\zeta\int\left|\nabla u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t,x\right)\right|^{2}dxdt\leq C^{\prime}dt+2\sum_{k\in K}\left\langle R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right),u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\rangle dW_{t}^{k}

for a new constant
C^{\prime}>0
. It follows

\displaystyle\zeta\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int\left|\nabla u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t,x\right)\right|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}T+2\left(\sum_{k\in K}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right),u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\rangle^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}T+2\left(\sum_{k\in K}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}.

As above,
\left\|u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq U
, and
\sum_{k\in K}\left\|R_{m,k}^{\epsilon}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{Q}U
, hence

\zeta\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int\left|\nabla u_{m}^{\epsilon}\left(t,x\right)\right|^{2}dxdt\leq C^{\prime}T+2C_{Q}^{1/2}UT^{1/2}.

The proof of the lemma is complete.

5.2
Proof of Corollary
26

Let
u=\left(u_{1},...,u_{M}\right)
,
u^{\prime}=\left(u_{1}^{\prime},...,u_{M}^{\prime}\right)
be two weak solutions. Set
v\left(t\right)=u\left(t\right)-u^{\prime}\left(t\right)
,
v_{m}\left(t\right)=u_{m}\left(t\right)-u_{m}^{\prime}\left(t\right)
. The regularity of

v
is sufficient to apply Itô formula (cf.
[
31
]
,

[
39
]
,
[
40
]
):

\displaystyle d\left\|v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\mathcal{L}v_{m}\left(t\right),v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\rangle dt+2\left\langle F_{m}\left(u\left(t\right)\right)-F_{m}\left(u^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right),v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle+2\sum_{k\in K}\left\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla v_{m}\left(t\right),v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\rangle dW_{t}^{k}+\sum_{k\in K}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

Since
\left\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla v_{m}\left(t\right),v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\rangle=0
and
\sum_{k\in K}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is bounded above by

-2\left\langle\mathcal{L}v_{m}\left(t\right),v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\rangle
, we get

d\left\|v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\left\langle F_{m}\left(u\left(t\right)\right)-F_{m}\left(u^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right),v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\rangle dt.

Now

F_{m}\left(u\right)-F_{m}\left(u^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\left(u_{n}u_{m-n}-u_{n}^{\prime}u_{m-n}^{\prime}\right)-2\left(u_{m}\sum_{n=1}^{M}u_{n}-u_{m}^{\prime}\sum_{n=1}^{M}u_{n}^{\prime}\right)

and each term of the form

can be

\displaystyle\left|u_{h}u_{k}-u_{h}^{\prime}u_{k}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|u_{h}u_{k}-u_{h}u_{k}^{\prime}\right|+\left|u_{h}u_{k}^{\prime}-u_{h}^{\prime}u_{k}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int\left|v_{n}\left(t,x\right)\right|\left|v_{m}\left(t,x\right)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq 2C\sum_{n=1}^{M}\left\|v_{n}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2CM\left\|v_{m}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Summing over

6
Relative compactness of the empirical measure

In this section, we show the tightness of the sequence of laws of
\{\mu^{N,m}\}_{m\leq M}
taking values in
\mathcal{D}_{T}(\mathcal{M}_{+,1})^{M}
.

6.1
A general compactness criterion

Let
\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
be the set of positive
Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with mass
\leq 1
. Recall that, given

R>0
, the set
\mathcal{K}_{R}\subset\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{R}=\Big\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|x\right|\mu\left(dx\right)\leq R\Big\}

is relatively compact in
\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

endowed with the topology of weak convergence of measures.

The weak convergence on
\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
can
be metrized in the following way. For every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the space
C\left(K\right)
is separable; let
\left(f_{n}^{K}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a dense sequence in
C\left(K\right)
and define the function
\delta_{K}:\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(K\right)^{2}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
as

\displaystyle\delta_{K}\left(\mu,\nu\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\left(\left|\left\langle\mu,f_{n}^{K}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nu,f_{n}^{K}\right\rangle\right|\wedge 1\right).

This is a metric on
\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(K\right)
and the metric space

\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(K\right),d_{K}\right)
is complete and
separable; convergence in this metric is weak convergence of measures. Taking
a sequence of compact sets
K_{m}
with
\cup_{m}K_{m}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
proceeding in a similar way we may define a metric on
\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. Rearranging the double procedure in a
single one, we may claim that there exists a sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, dense in
C\left(K\right)
for every compact set

K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that

\displaystyle\delta\left(\mu,\nu\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\left(\left|\left\langle\mu,f_{n}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nu,f_{n}\right\rangle\right|\wedge 1\right)

is a metric on
\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and the
metric space
\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),\delta\right)
is complete and separable; convergence in this metric is
weak convergence of measures. Finally, we may take the sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, in
C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, by revising the previous construction from the beginning and
using the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
in

C_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.

Given
T>0
, consider the space of càdlàg functions

\displaystyle\mu_{\cdot}:\left[0,T\right]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

where
\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),\delta\right)
is considered as a metric space (hence continuity and limits of
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
are understood in this metric). Denote it by
\mathcal{D}\left(\left[0,T\right];\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\right)
or more shortly as
\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\right)
and
endow it by the Skorohod topology, not recalled here, but denoted by
d

below (cf.
[
8
, Ch. 3, Eq. (5.2)]
).

Criteria of compacteness in
\left(\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\right),d\right)
are usually expressed by means of a modified
modulus of continuity, to account of jumps. When the jumps are very small (as
in our case), the classical modulus of continuity is sufficient, defined as
(
\mu_{\cdot}\in\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\right)
)

\displaystyle\omega_{\gamma}\left(\mu_{\cdot}\right)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in\left[0,T\right]\\
\left|t-s\right|\leq\gamma\end{subarray}}\delta\left(\mu_{s},\mu_{t}\right).

A sufficient condition for a (deterministic) sequence
\left\{\mu_{\cdot}^{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\right)
to be relatively
compact is:

Proposition 29

If (i). the family

\displaystyle\left\{\mu_{t}^{n};n\in\mathbb{N},t\in\left[0,T\right]\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

is relatively compact (see above a sufficient condition); and (ii).

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow 0}\sup_{n}\omega_{\gamma}\left(\mu_{\cdot}^{n}\right)=0,

then
\left\{\mu_{\cdot}^{n}\right\}
is relatively compact in

\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\right)
.

Let now
\left\{\mu_{\cdot}^{n}\right\}
be a family of
random
elements of

\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\right)
and let
\left\{\mathbb{P}^{n}\right\}
be their laws. This sequence of laws is weakly relatively
compact if it is tight, namely if given
\epsilon>0
there exists a compact
set
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}\subset\mathcal{D}_{T}\left(\mathcal{M}_{+,1}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{n}\left(\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\mu_{\cdot}^{n}\in\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}\right)\geq 1-\epsilon.

Proposition
29
yields the following practical sufficient condition:

Proposition 30

If (i). there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|x\right|\mu_{t}^{n}\left(dx\right)\leq C_{1}

for all
n
and
t\in[0,T]
;
(ii). and there exists
\beta>0
such that, for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, there exists
C_{\phi}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle\mu_{t}^{n},\phi\right\rangle-\left\langle\mu_{s}^{n},\phi\right\rangle\right|\right]\leq C_{\phi}\left|t-s\right|^{\beta}

for all
t,s\in\left[0,T\right]
,
then the sequence
\left\{\mathbb{P}^{n}\right\}
is relatively compact.

Application to our case

Let

7
Existence and boundedness of limit density

In this section, we show that

Proposition 32

Any subsequential limit in law of
\{\mu_{t}^{N,m}(dx),t\in[0,T]\}_{m\leq M}

\mathcal{D}_{T}(\mathcal{M}_{+,1})^{M}

\Gamma

.

Denote on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

\mu_{t}^{N}(dx):=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mu_{t}^{N,m}(dx)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}(t)}\delta_{x_{i}^{N}(t)}(dx),\quad t\geq 0.

Let us recall the auxiliary free system of particles
\{x_{i}^{f}(t)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
introduced in Section
4
. For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote their empirical measure

\mu_{t}^{N,f}(dx):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x^{f}_{i}(t)}(dx),\quad t\geq 0.

In addition to (

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for all

\displaystyle\left\{x_{i}^{N}(t):i\in\mathcal{N}(t)\right\}\subset\left\{x_{i}^{f}(t):i=1,...,N\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

and the domination between the empirical measures:
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for any

and

\displaystyle\mu_{t}^{N,m}(B)\leq\mu_{t}^{N}(B)\leq\mu_{t}^{N,f}(B).

(60)

It is also clear that
\mu_{0}^{N,f}(dx)\to\sum_{m=1}^{M}r_{m}p_{m}(x)dx
(weakly), as

, in
\mathcal{M}_{+,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in probability.

We now argue that in order to show Proposition

, it is sufficient to prove that

Proposition 33

For every finite
T
, the sequence of empirical measures of the auxiliary free system,
\{\mu_{t}^{N,f}(dx):t\in[0,T]\}
, converges in law as
N\to\infty
to a limit
\{\overline{\mu}^{f}_{t}(dx):t\in[0,T]\}
, in the space
C([0,T];\mathcal{M}_{+,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. The latter random measure is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, with a density
\overline{u}^{f}(t,x)
uniformly bounded by the deterministic constant
\Gamma
.

We know by Section
6
that the sequence of laws
\{\mathscr{L}^{N}\}_{N}
of
\big\{\{\mu^{N,m}\}_{m\leq M},\mu^{N,f}\big\}

\mathcal{D}_{T}(\mathcal{M}_{+,1})^{M+1}
form a tight sequence hence is weakly relatively compact. Fix any weak subsequential limit
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
of
\mathscr{L}^{N_{j}}
. By Skorohod’s representation theorem, on an auxiliary probability space
(\widehat{\Omega},\widehat{\mathcal{F}},\mathbf{P})
, there exist random variables
\big\{\{\widehat{\mu}^{N_{j},m}\}_{m\leq M},\widehat{\mu}^{N_{j},f}\big\}
,
j\geq 1
and
\big\{\{\overline{\mu}^{m}\}_{m\leq M},\overline{\mu}^{f}\big\}
, having the laws
\mathscr{L}^{N_{j}}
,
j\geq 1
,
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
, respectively, such that
\mathbf{P}
-a.s.

\big\{\{\mu^{N_{j},m}\}_{m\leq M},\mu^{N_{j},f}\big\}\to\big\{\{\overline{\mu}^{m}\}_{m\leq M},\overline{\mu}^{f}\big\},\quad j\to\infty.

In particular,
\overline{\mu}^{f}
satisfies the properties in Proposition
33
.

By (
60

(\widehat{\Omega},\widehat{\mathcal{F}},\mathbf{P})
we have
\mathbf{P}
-a.s. for every

As

\mathbf{P}
-a.s. to

\left\langle\overline{\mu}_{t}^{f}(dx)-\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m}(dx),\phi\right\rangle\geq 0.

This implies that
\mathbf{P}
-a.s. for every open set

,

and

\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m}(A)\leq\overline{\mu}_{t}^{f}(A)=\int_{A}\overline{u}^{f}(t,x)dx.

Recall that any Borel probability measure on a Polish space is regular; namely the measure of a Borel set is the infimum of the measures of open sets larger than the given Borel set. Fix any Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with null Lebesgue measure, and any open set
A\supset B
. By absolute continuity of
\overline{\mu}_{t}^{f}
with respect to Lebesgue, we have that
\overline{\mu}_{t}^{f}(B)=0
, and further

\displaystyle 0\leq\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m}(B)\leq\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m}(A)\leq\overline{\mu}_{t}^{f}(A),\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(61)

Taking infimum over all open sets

0\leq\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m}(B)\leq\inf_{\text{open }A\supset B}\overline{\mu}_{t}^{f}(A)=\overline{\mu}_{t}^{f}(B)=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

That is,

\mathbf{P}
-a.s. Let us denote its density by

\int_{B}\left[\overline{u}^{f}(t,x)-u_{m}(t,x)\right]dx\geq 0

, which implies
\overline{u}^{f}(t,x)-u_{m}(t,x)\geq 0

,
\mathbf{P}
-a.s.

Thus we are left to show Proposition

\\
\mu_{0}(x)&=\sum_{m=1}^{M}r_{m}p_{m}(x)dx.\end{cases}

(see in particular (

8.1
Existence, uniqueness and regularity results

Arguing in the ”free” case as in the more difficult case with interaction, we
can prove that the family of laws
Q_{N}
of the empirical measures are tight
and that every limit point may be seen, on a suitable probability space, as a
random time-dependent probability measure
\mu_{t}
(in the case with
interaction there were only finite measures, but here the empirical measures
have mass equal to one), adapted to the noise
W
, satisfying

\displaystyle\int\phi\left(t,x\right)\mu_{t}\left(dx\right)

\displaystyle=\int\phi\left(0,x\right)u_{0}\left(x\right)dx+\sum_{k\in K}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathbb{\int}\nabla\phi\left(s,x\right)\cdot\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\mu_{s}\left(dx\right)\right)dW_{s}^{k}

(62)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ij}\left(\mathbb{\int}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi\left(s,x\right)\left(\lambda^{2}\delta_{ij}+Q_{ij}\left(x,x\right)\right)\mu_{s}\left(dx\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int\partial_{s}\phi\left(s,x\right)\mu_{s}\left(dx\right)ds

for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1,2}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.

The first result we want to prove is the uniqueness of measure-valued
solutions to this equation. Although potentially several techniques may be
used, we present here one based on SPDE theory in negative order Sobolev
spaces, which may be of interest on its own. The approach presented here is
inspired by
[
10
]
. For the sake of simplicity we develop the theory
in the Hilbert scale
W^{\alpha,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
but,
following
[
30
]
and
[
1
]
, one can work in a Banach scale

W^{\alpha,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
with
p>2
which has the
advantage to reduce the necessary degree of differentiability to have the
Sobolev embedding
W^{\alpha,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\subset C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
(see below) and thus allows one to ask
less differentiability of the coefficients
\sigma_{k}
; since an optimal result
is still not clear, we do not stress this level of generality in this work.

In dimension
d
one has the Sobolev embedding
W^{\frac{d}{2}+\delta,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\subset C_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
for every
\delta>0
; for simplicity of exposition we take
\delta=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore the set of probability measures
\Pr\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is contained in the dual of
W^{\frac{d+1}{2},2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, the negative order Sobolev space
W^{-\frac{d+1}{2},2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
:

\Pr\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\subset W^{-\frac{d+1}{2},2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\text{.}

The set-theoretical inclusion is a continuous embedding of metric spaces when

\Pr\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is endowed of the distance

d\left(\mu,\nu\right)=\sup_{\left\|\phi\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\left|\left\langle\mu,\phi\right\rangle-\left\langle\nu,\phi\right\rangle\right|

where
\left\langle\mu,\phi\right\rangle
denotes, as usual,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi\left(x\right)\mu\left(dx\right)
. Moreover, up
to a constant
C>0
, one has

\left\|\mu\right\|_{W^{-\frac{d+1}{2},2}}\leq C

for all
\mu\in\Pr\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.

A measure-valued solution in the sense above is also a weak solution of class

W^{-\frac{d+1}{2},2}
. Choosing
\phi\left(t,x\right)
related to

e^{t\mathcal{L}^{\ast}}\psi
, with suitable choice of times, one can rewrite
the previous identity as

\left\langle\psi,\mu_{t}\right\rangle=\left\langle e^{t\mathcal{L}^{\ast}}\psi,\mu_{0}\right\rangle-\sum_{k\in K}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}^{\ast}}\psi,\mu_{s}\right\rangle dW_{s}^{k}

for every
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, where we
write
e^{t\mathcal{L}^{\ast}}
so that the duality structure is more clear,
but it is equal to
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
,
\mathcal{L}
as in (
58
). We only remark that, thanks to the

C_{b}^{\infty}
regularity of
\sigma_{k}
, the semigroup

maps

in itself for every

, so that

\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}^{\ast}}\psi
is of
class
W^{\frac{d+1}{2},2}
and thus the duality is well defined under the
stochastic integral (similarly for the initial condition term).

We want to move a step further, namely interpret the previous identity as an
equation of the form

\mu_{t}=e^{t\mathcal{L}}\mu_{0}+\sum_{k\in K}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\mu_{s}dW_{s}^{k}

(63)

in a suitable Hilbert space. For technical reasons which will be clear below,
given

such that
2N+1\geq\frac{d+1}{2}
. Then we consider progressively measurable processes
\mu_{t}

in

with the following regularity:

\sup_{t\in\left[0,T\right]}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mu_{t}\right\|_{W^{-2N,2}}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mu_{t}\right\|_{W^{-2N+1,2}}^{2}dt<\infty.

(64)

Probability measure solutions of the equation above belong to this space.
Under these condition,
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\nabla\mu_{t}\right\|_{W^{-2N,2}}^{2}dt<\infty
(
\nabla
interpreted in the sense
of distributions), the multiplication with
\sigma_{k}\in C_{b}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
remains of the same class; and the operators

e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}
are equibounded (on finite time
intervals) in
W^{-2N,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
by duality, hence

\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\mu_{s}dW_{s}^{k}
is a stochastic process in
W^{-2N,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

63

W^{-2N,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, for solution of class
(
64

Call
a_{ij}\left(x\right):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+Q_{ij}\left(x,x\right)\right)

[
12
]
) that there exists
\eta_{0}\in\left(0,1\right)
such that

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in K}\left(\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\cdot\xi\right)^{2}\leq\eta_{0}\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\left(x\right)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}

for all

. This implies

\sum_{k\in K}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-2\eta_{0}\left\langle\mathcal{L}g,g\right\rangle

and thus

\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\eta_{0}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(65)

for all

because

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq-2\eta_{0}\int_{s}^{T}\left\langle\mathcal{L}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f,e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\rangle dt

\displaystyle=-\eta_{0}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\eta_{0}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For every

, let us endow

by the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}:=\left\|\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)^{n}f\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Lemma 34

Let
\eta\in\left(\eta_{0},1\right)
be
given. For every integer number
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists a constant

C_{n,K}>0
such that

\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}^{2}dt\leq\left(\eta+C_{n,K}\left(T-s\right)\right)\left\|f\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}^{2}

for all
f\in W^{2n,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
.

Proof.
The case
n=0
is already proved above. We give the proof for
n=1
and
n=-1
, the general case being similar.

For every
k\in K
there is a second order differential operator

D_{k}^{\left(2\right)}
, with coefficients of class
C_{b}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, such that

\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)f=\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f+D_{k}^{\left(2\right)}f

(66)

for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. Indeed, the
terms with third order derivatives in
\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)f
and
\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f
coincide. Therefore (case
n=1
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{W^{2,2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\left[\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)-D_{k}^{\left(2\right)}\right]e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

For
\epsilon>0
we use the inequality
\left(a+b\right)^{2}\leq\left(1+\epsilon\right)a^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)b^{2}
to get

\leq\left(1+\epsilon\right)\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|D_{k}^{\left(2\right)}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

The first term is handled by (
65
), the second one by a trivial
bound, to get

\leq\left(1+\epsilon\right)\eta_{0}\left\|\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon,K}\left(T-s\right)\left\|f\right\|_{W^{2,2}}^{2}.

If
\epsilon
satisfies
\left(1+\epsilon\right)\eta_{0}=\eta
, this is
the required bound.

For
n=-1
, we have to prove

\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)^{-1}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\left(\eta+C_{-1,K}\left(T-s\right)\right)\left\|\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)^{-1}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We set
g=\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)^{-1}f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, so that we have to prove

\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)^{-1}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\left(\eta+C_{-1,K}\left(T-s\right)\right)\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By (
66

\leq\left(1+\epsilon\right)\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\sum_{k\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\left(1-\mathcal{L}\right)^{-1}D_{k}^{\left(2\right)}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

Proof.
The function
u_{0}\in W^{2n,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is also of
class
u_{0}\in W^{-2N,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, and the
corresponding mild solution
u
of class
W^{2n,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
is also a distributional solution in the sense of Proposition

35
, hence it is the unique solution of that
negative order Sobolev class. A probability-measure solution
\mu_{t}\left(dx\right)
exists as a subsequential limit of the empirical measures, with
initial condition
\mu_{0}\left(dx\right)=u_{0}\left(x\right)dx
,
hence by the uniqueness statement of Proposition

35
, it coincides with
u
, in the sense of
distributions, which implies
\mu_{t}\left(dx\right)=u\left(t,x\right)dx
.

The previous argument proves only that
\left\|u\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\|_{\infty}
is finite. In the next section we prove that it is
uniformly bounded by a deterministic constant.

8.2
Uniform upper bound

Lemma 39

Let
u_{0}\in W^{2n,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
with
n>d/4
and let

C_{0}>0
be a constant such that

\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}.

Then

\left\|u\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}

a.s. in all parameters.

Proof.
Let us prove only that

u\left(t,x\right)\leq C_{0}.

The proof that
u\left(t,x\right)\geq-C_{0}
is similar. The first remark
is that the process identically equal to
C_{0}
is a solution, in the sense
that it satisfies the weak formulation, with initial condition
C_{0}
. By
linearity, the process

v\left(t,x\right):=u\left(t,x\right)-C_{0}

also satisfies the weak formulation, with initial condition

v_{0}\left(x\right):=u_{0}\left(x\right)-C_{0}\leq 0.

Our thesis is that also

v\left(t,x\right)\leq 0.

Given
\delta>0
, let
\beta_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)

be a
C^{2}
-convex function such that
\beta_{\delta}\left(r\right)=0

for
r<0
,
\beta_{\delta}\left(r\right)=r-\delta
for
r>2\delta
. The
family
\beta_{\delta}
converges uniformly to the function
\beta\left(r\right)=r1_{[0,\infty)}\left(r\right)
. From Itô formula

d\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)=\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)dv\left(t,x\right)+\frac{1}{2}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)d\left[v\left(\cdot,x\right)\right]_{t}.

From a number of intermediate computations that we omit (better understood
formally at the level of Stratonovich calculus), we get

\displaystyle d\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)\Delta v\left(t,x\right)dt+\sum_{k\in K}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)dW_{t}^{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}\left(Q\left(x,x\right)\nabla\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)\right)dt

interpreted of course in integral

\displaystyle\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)\Delta v\left(s,x\right)ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in K}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\cdot\nabla\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)dW_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{div}\left(Q\left(x,x\right)\nabla\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)\right)ds

(but pointwise in
x
, thanks to the regularity of
v
). We have neglected the
term
\beta_{\delta}\left(v_{0}\left(x\right)\right)
because it is
zero, by definition of the objects. Now we want to integrate in
x
this
identity. The first and second derivatives of
v
, being equal to those of
u
, are integrable; hence all terms on the right-hand-side are integrable. The
function
v
itself could not be integrable, becuase the constant
C_{0}
is
not, hence
\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)
a priori is
not integrable. However, it is integrable as a consequence of the identity. We
have also used stochastic Fubini theorem to deal with the stochastic term.

Taking into account that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\cdot\nabla\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)dx=0

because
\operatorname{div}\sigma_{k}=0
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{div}\left(Q\left(x,x\right)\nabla\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)\right)dx=0

(in both cases we use Gauss-Green formula), we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)dx=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)\Delta v\left(s,x\right)dxds.

But

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)\Delta v\left(s,x\right)dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\beta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}\left(v\left(s,x\right)\right)\left|\nabla v\left(s,x\right)\right|^{2}dx\leq 0

because, by convexity,
\beta_{\delta}^{\prime\prime}\left(r\right)\geq 0
.
Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)dx\leq 0.

Since the function
\beta_{\delta}
is non-negative, we deduce

\beta_{\delta}\left(v\left(t,x\right)\right)=0

a.s. and thus, taking the limit as
\delta\rightarrow 0
,
v\left(t,x\right)\leq 0
a.s., completing the proof.

8.3
Proof of Proposition
27

The proof consists in two main steps plus a few remarks. First we show that a
modified version of the system for
\left(u_{m}\right)_{m=1,...,M}
has a
global solution in the regularity class specified by Proposition

27
; this is Step 1 below. Then, in Step 2,
we connect this regular solution with the one provided by Corollary

26
: we show they are the same, hence the solution of
Corollary
26
has the regularity stated by Proposition

27
.

Step 1
. Consider the auxiliary system

\displaystyle u_{m}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=e^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{m}\left(0\right)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)ds+\sum_{k\in K}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}v_{m,k}\left(s\right)dW_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle v_{m,k}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{m}\left(0\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)ds+\sum_{h\in K}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}v_{m,h}\left(s\right)dW_{s}^{h}

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\left(u_{1},...,u_{M}\right)

with
m=1,...,M
,
k\in K
and
\widetilde{F}_{m}
defined as follows. Recall
that
0\leq u_{m}\left(0\right)\leq U
. Taken a smooth compact support
function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\chi\left(a\right)=a\text{ for }\left|a\right|\leq U+1,

we define
\widetilde{F}_{m}
as

\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(t,x\right)\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\chi\left(u_{n}\left(t,x\right)\right)\chi\left(u_{m-n}\left(t,x\right)\right)-2\chi\left(u_{m}\left(t,x\right)\right)\sum_{n=1}^{M}\chi\left(u_{n}\left(t,x\right)\right).

Given
T>0
, consider the space
\mathcal{X}_{T}
of progressively measurable
processes
u_{m},v_{m,k}
in
W^{2n,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
such
that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(u_{m},v_{m,k}\right)_{m=1,...,M,k\in K}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\in\left[0,T\right]}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|u_{m}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{k\in K}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|v_{m,k}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\|_{W^{2n+1,2}}^{2}dt

is finite; the space
\mathcal{X}_{T}
with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}
is a Banach space. In it, let us define
the map
\Gamma_{T}
as follows. We write an element
\left(u_{m},v_{m,k}\right)_{m=1,...,M,k\in K}
of
\mathcal{X}_{T}
in the form

\left(u,v\right)
,
u=\left(u_{m}\right)_{m=1,...,M}
,
v=\left(v_{m,k}\right)_{m=1,...,M,k\in K}
, and similarly we write
\Gamma_{T}\left(u,v\right)
in the form
\left(\Gamma_{T}^{\left(1\right)}\left(u,v\right),\Gamma_{T}^{\left(2\right)}\left(u,v\right)\right)
, with components
\Gamma_{T}^{\left(1\right)}\left(u,v\right)_{m}
,
\Gamma_{T}^{\left(2\right)}\left(u,v\right)_{m,k}
,
m=1,...,M,k\in K
, given by

\Gamma_{T}^{\left(1\right)}\left(u,v\right)_{m}\left(t\right):=e^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{m}\left(0\right)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)ds+\sum_{k\in K}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}v_{m,k}\left(s\right)dW_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}^{\left(2\right)}\left(u,v\right)_{m,k}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{t\mathcal{L}}u_{m}\left(0\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{h\in K}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}v_{m,h}\left(s\right)dW_{s}^{h}.

By Lemma
34
,
\Gamma_{T}
maps
\mathcal{X}_{T}

into itself; the proof is similar to the computation done below to prove the
contraction property and thus it is not duplicated. We only remark a property
of
\widetilde{F}_{m}
for the purpose of proving that
\Gamma_{T}
maps

\mathcal{X}_{T}
into itself: since
\chi
is smooth and compact support,
hence having all derivatives of every order bounded,
\chi\left(u_{m}\left(t,x\right)\right)\chi\left(u_{n}\left(t,x\right)\right)
is of
class

C\left(\left[0,T\right];L^{2}\left(\Omega;W^{2n,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\right)\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\left[0,T\right]\times\Omega;W^{2n+1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\right)

(68)

for every
u_{n},u_{m}
of the same class.

Given two input functions
\left(u,v\right)
,
\left(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\right)
, with the same initial values
u_{m}\left(0\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}^{\left(1\right)}\left(u,v\right)_{m}\left(t\right)-\Gamma_{T}^{\left(1\right)}\left(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\right)_{m}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\left(\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)-\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in K}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\left(v_{m,k}\left(s\right)-v_{m,k}^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)dW_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{T}^{\left(2\right)}\left(u,v\right)_{m,k}\left(t\right)-\Gamma_{T}^{\left(2\right)}\left(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\right)_{m,k}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\left(\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)-\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{h\in K}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\left(v_{m,h}\left(s\right)-v_{m,h}^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)dW_{s}^{h}.

Let us develop the estimates for the second line, the first one being similar
and a little bit easier. We have, from
\left(a+b\right)^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)a^{2}+\left(1+\epsilon\right)b^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{k\in K}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\Gamma_{T}^{\left(2\right)}\left(u,v\right)_{m,k}\left(t\right)-\Gamma_{T}^{\left(2\right)}\left(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\right)_{m,k}\left(t\right)\right\|_{W^{2n+1,2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)T\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{k\in K}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\left(\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)-\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)\right)\right\|_{W^{2n+1,2}}^{2}dtds

\displaystyle+\left(1+\epsilon\right)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{k\in K}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{h\in K}\int_{s}^{T}\left\|\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e^{\left(t-s\right)\mathcal{L}}\left(v_{m,h}\left(s\right)-v_{m,h}^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)\right\|_{W^{2n+1,2}}^{2}dtds

where we have used also Fubini-Tonelli theorem. We apply Lemma

34

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)T\left(\eta+C_{K}T\right)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u\left(s\right)\right)-\widetilde{F}_{m}\left(u^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\left(1+\epsilon\right)\left(\eta+C_{K}T\right)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{h\in K}\left\|v_{m,h}\left(s\right)-v_{m,h}^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}^{2}ds.

\displaystyle\leq C_{\chi}\left(1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)T\left(\eta+C_{K}T\right)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u_{m}\left(s\right)-u_{m}^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\left(1+\epsilon\right)\left(\eta+C_{K}T\right)\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{h\in K}\left\|v_{m,h}\left(s\right)-v_{m,h}^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{2n,2}}^{2}ds.

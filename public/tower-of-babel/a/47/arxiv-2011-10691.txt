Tau Functions of ( n , 1 ) (n,1) curves and Soliton Solutions on Non-Zero Constant Backgrounds
Atsushi Nakayashiki

Abstract
We study the tau function of the KP-hierarchy associated with an ( n , 1 ) (n,1) curve y n = x − α y^{n}=x-\alpha . If α = 0 \alpha=0 the corresponding tau function is 1 1 . On the other hand if α ≠ 0 \alpha\neq 0 the tau function becomes the exponential of a quadratic function of the time variables. By applying vertex opertaors to the latter we obtain soliton solutions on non-zero constant backgrounds.

Introduction

In our previous paper
[
N2020
]
the degeneration of the theta function solution (tau function)
of the KP-hierarchy as a result of what we call one step degeneration of an
(n,s)
curve
for
n=2,3
has been studied. The obtained formula expresses the degenerate tau function as a sum of
combinations of exponential functions with tau functions of a lower genus curve.
Repeating this process we finally come to the tau function associated with an

(n,1)
curve of genus zero. In this paper we derive the explicit formula for the tau function
of an
(n,1)
curve with an arbitrary
n\geq 2
. We use it as a seed solution in the vertex operator construction of solutions of the KP-hierarchy.
In the case of
n=2
the solutions obtained in this way are considered as
solitons on non-zero constant backgrounds.

For
n\geq 2
consider the rational algebraic curve
C_{n}
defined by

\displaystyle y^{n}=x-\alpha,

(1.1)

which we call an
(n,1)
curve.
From this curve a solution of the KP-hierarchy (tau function) is constructed as follows.
To this end we use the Sato Grassmannian which we denote by UGM (=universal Grassmann manifold)
after Sato
[
SS
,
SN
]
. The Sato Grassmannian is the parameter space of solutions of the KP-hierarchy and is
defined as the set of certain subspaces of the vector space
V={\mathbb{C}}((z))
of Laurent series in a variable
z
.

Consider the point
\infty
of
C_{n}
and take the local coordinate
z
around

\infty
such that

\displaystyle x=z^{-n},\hskip 14.22636pty=z^{-1}(1+O(z)).

This choice of the local coordinate is necessary in order to study the degeneration of solutions of the
KP-hierarchy associated with an
(n,s)
curve
[
N2020
]
.
Define the vector space
V_{n}
by

\displaystyle V_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{C}}y^{j}.

By expanding
y^{j}
in the coordinate
z

V_{n}
can be considered as a subspace of
V
.
Then it is known that it belongs to UGM (see
[
N2019
]
for example).
We denote this point of UGM by
V_{n}(z)
meaning that it is obtained from
V_{n}
using the coordinate
z
.

For each point of UGM a solution
\tau({\bf t})
of the KP-hierarchy is constructed, up to constant,
in the form of the Schur function expansion as

\displaystyle\tau({\bf t})=\sum_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}({\bf t}),

where the summation is over all partitions,
s_{\lambda}({\bf t})
is the Schur function corresponding to
\lambda
and

\xi_{\lambda}
the Plücker coordinate of the point of UGM.

In the present case the Schur function expansion of the tau function corresponding to
V_{n}(z)
begins
from a non-zero constant. We define the tau function
\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))
as that corresponding to

V_{n}(z)
normalized as

\displaystyle\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))=1+\sum_{|\lambda|>0}\xi_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(t).

(1.2)

In the case
\alpha=0
we easily have
\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))=1
. So the actual problem here is to compute

\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))
for
\alpha\neq 0
.
The result is

\displaystyle\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left(q({\bf t})+L({\bf t})\right)},

(1.3)

where
q({\bf t})
is a quadratic form in
{\bf t}={}^{t}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},...)
and
L({\bf t})
is a linear
form in
{\bf t}
(see Theorem
3.1
).
It is difficult to prove such a formula directly from the Schur function expansion.
The strategy here to derived the formula (
1.3
) is to consider a coordinate change
of the corresponding wave and the adjoint wave function
[
DJKM
]
.
This idea comes from the paper
[
DN
]
by Dubrovin and Natanzon,
who first studied the effect of a coordinate change in the construction of
solutions of the KP-equation.

The Schur function expansion of the tau function (
1.3
) is also calculated explicitly
by normalizing a frame of
V_{n}(z)
(see Theorem
3.2
).
We use the Giambelli formula for Plücker coordinates
[
EH
,
NOS
]
in this computation.

The second logarithmic derivative of a tau function with respect to
t_{1}
(see (
2.2
)) gives a
solution of the KP-equation (
2.3
). The solution corresponding to
\tau_{0}(t;V_{n}(z))
is
a constant. So (
1.3
) itself does not look an interesting solution of the KP equation, although
it is used to describe the degeneration of theta function solution as mentioned in the beginning.
However this is not the case. It plays a role as a seed to create various solutions of the KP-hierarchy.
In fact it is well known that soliton solutions of the KP-hierarchy are constructed by applying vertex operators
to the trivial solution
1

[
DJKM
]
.
Similarly we also get various solutions by applying vertex operators to
\tau_{0}(t;V_{n}(z))
.
In particular, if
n=2
and
\alpha\neq 0
solutions obtained in this way are considered as solitons
with non-zero constant asymptotics (see Corollary
and Theorem
).
Note that for this computation to work it is indispensable to introduce
the infinite number of time variables.

Finally we mention that in the paper
[
Givental2003
]
the tau function of the form (
1.3
) had been studied in relation with Gromov-Witten invariants and the dispertionless KP-hierarchy. In that paper
all points of UGM corresponding to tau functions of the n-reduced KP-hierarchy, which are expressed in the
form of the exponential of a quadratic form, are determined. The point
V_{n}(z)
is a special family, depending on the parameter
\alpha
, in them. However such explicit formula as in Theorem
3.1
of the tau function was not derived in
[
Givental2003
]
as far as the author understands.

2
Sato Grassmannian

In this section we beriefly review the theory of the Sato Grassmannian and the KP-hierarchy.

Let
\tau({\bf t})
be a function of
{\bf t}={}^{t}(t_{1},t_{2},...)
.
The KP-hierarchy is the equation for
\tau({\bf t})
given by

\displaystyle{\rm Res}_{k=\infty}\tau({\bf t}-{\bf s}-[k^{-1}])({\bf t}+{\bf s}+[k^{-1}])e^{-2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{j}k^{j}}\frac{dk}{2\pi i}=0,

(2.1)

where
{\bf s}={}^{t}(s_{1},s_{2},...)
,
[k]={}^{t}(k,k^{2}/2,k^{3}/3,...)
.
By expanding in
s_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, (
2.1
) gives an
infinite number of differential equations for
\tau({\bf t})
expressed in Hirota’s bilinear form
[
DJKM
]
.
Set
(x,y,t)=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
and,
for a solution
\tau({\bf t})
of (
2.1
) ,

\displaystyle u({\bf t})=2\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\log\tau({\bf t}).

(2.2)

Then
u({\bf t})
is a solution of the KP-equation,

\displaystyle 3u_{yy}+(-4u_{t}+6uu_{x}+u_{xxx})_{x}=0.

(2.3)

The totality of solutions of the KP-hierarchy constitues a certain infinite dimensional Grassmann manifold
called the Sato Grassmannian
[
SS
]
. Let us recall its definition and fundamental properties.

Let
V={\mathbb{C}}((z))
be the vector space of Laurent series in
z
. Define two subspaces of
V
by

V_{\phi}={\mathbb{C}}[z^{-1}]
,
V_{0}=z{\mathbb{C}}[[z]]
.
Then
V=V_{\phi}\oplus V_{0}
. Let
\pi:V\longrightarrow V_{\phi}
be the projection
map. Then the Sato Grassmannian which we denote by UGM (=universal Grassmann manifold) after Sato
[
SS
,
SN
]
, is defined as the set of subspaces
U
of
V
such that

\displaystyle\dim({\rm Ker}\,\pi|_{U})=\dim({\rm Coker}\,\pi|_{U})<\infty.

Example 2.1
.

The subspace
V_{\phi}
belongs to UGM. In this case

\displaystyle\dim({\rm Ker}\,\pi|_{V_{\phi}})=\dim({\rm Coker}\,\pi|_{V_{\phi}})=0.

(2.4)

.

In general a subspace
U
defined by

\displaystyle U=\sum_{j\leq 0}{\mathbb{C}}\xi_{j},\quad\xi_{j}=z^{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\xi_{i,j}z^{i},\quad\xi_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{C}},

(2.5)

satisfies (
2.4
) and belongs to UGM.
The totality of points of UGM corresponding to such frames is denoted by
{\rm UGM}^{\phi}
, which
forms a cell called the big cell of UGM.

A point
U
of UGM can be specified by its frame, that is, a basis of
U
.
If we associate the infinite column vector
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
to an element
\sum a_{n}z^{n}
of
V
,
a frame of
U
can be expressed by an
{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
matrix

\xi=(\xi_{i,j})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}},j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq 0}
denotes
the set of non-positive integers.

In writing the matrix
\xi
we follow the usual convention that the row numbers increase downward and
the column numbers increase rightward.
For example the frame
\xi=(...,\xi_{-1},\xi_{0})
of
U
given by (
2.5
) is represented as

\displaystyle\xi=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\vdots&\vdots\\
\cdots&1&0\\
\cdots&0&1\\
---&---&---\\
\cdots&\xi_{1,-1}&\xi_{1,0}\\
\cdots&\xi_{2,-1}&\xi_{2,0}\\
&\vdots&\vdots\\
\end{array}\right).

In general it is always possible to take a frame satisfying the following condition;
there exists a negative integer
l
such that

\displaystyle\xi_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\text{ if $j<l$ and $i=j$ }\\
0&\text{ if ($j<l$ and $i<j$) or ($j\geq l$ and $i<l$)}.\end{array}\right.

In the sequel we always take a frame which satisfies this condition, although it is not unique.

A Maya diagram
M=(m_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of intergers such that
m_{0}>m_{1}>\cdots
and
m_{j}=-j
for all sufficiently large
j
. For a Maya diagram
M=(m_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
the corresponding partition is defined by

\lambda(M)=(j+m_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
. By this correspondence the set of Maya diagrams and the set
of partitions bijectively correspond to each other.
We identify a partition with the corresponding Maya diagram.

For a frame
\xi
and a Maya diagram
M=(m_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
define the Plücker coordinate of
\xi
corresponding to
M
by

\displaystyle\xi_{M}=\det(\xi_{m_{i},j})_{-i,j\leq 0}.

If
M
corresponds to a partition
\lambda
,
\xi_{M}
is denoted also by
\xi_{\lambda}
.
Due to the condition (
2
) and the condition of the Maya diagram
this infinite determinant can be computed by the finite determinant
\det(\xi_{m_{i},j})_{k\leq-i,j\leq 0}
for
a sufficiently small
k
.

Define the Schur function
s_{(n)}(t)
corresponding to the partition
(n)
by

\displaystyle e^{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{n}\kappa^{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s_{(n)}(t)\kappa^{n}.

and the Schur function
[
Mac1995
]
corresponding to a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{l})
by

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}({\bf t})=\det(s_{(\lambda_{i}-i+j)}(t))_{1\leq i,j\leq l}.

We assign weight
j
to the variable
t_{j}
. Then
s_{\lambda}({\bf t})
becomes homogeneous
of weight
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{l}
.

To a point
U
of UGM take a frame
\xi
of
U
and define the tau function by

\displaystyle\tau({\bf t};\xi)=\sum_{\lambda}\xi_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}({\bf t}),

(2.17)

where the sum is taken over all partitions.
We call it the Schur function expansion of
\tau({\bf t};\xi)
.

If we change the frame
\xi
, Plücker coordinates and consequently
the tau function are multiplied by a non-zero constant due to the property of a determinant.
We call
\tau({\bf t};\xi)
, for any frame
\xi
of
U
, a tau function of
U
.
So tau functions of a point of UGM differ by non-zero constant multiples to each other.

For a frame
\xi
of the form (
2
), (
2.5
)
\xi_{(0)}=1
and the Schur function
expansion takes the form

\displaystyle\tau({\bf t};\xi)=1+\text{p.w.t},

(2.18)

where p.w.t. means positive weight terms. For
U\in{\rm UGM}^{\phi}
we denote
\tau_{0}({\bf t};U)

the tau function normalized as in (
2.18
). We call it the normalized tau function of
U
.

The fundamental theorem of the Sato theory is the following
[
SS
]

(see also
[
SN
,
KNTY
,
Mul1994
]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

[
SS
]

For a frame
\xi
of a point of UGM
\tau({\bf t};\xi)
is a solution of the KP-hierarchy.
Conversely for a
formal power series solution
\tau({\bf t})
of the KP-hierarchy there exists a unique point
U

of UGM such that
\tau({\bf t})
is a tau function of
U
.

The inverse construction from a solution
\tau({\bf t})
of the KP-hierarchy to the point
U
of UGM is given using the wave function as follows
[
SS
,
SN
,
KNTY
,
N2010-2
]
.
The wave function
\Psi^{\ast}({\bf t};z)
and the adjoint wave function
\Psi^{\ast}({\bf t};z)

[
DJKM
]
corresponding to
\tau({\bf t})
are defined by

\displaystyle\Psi({\bf t};z)=\frac{\tau({\bf t}-[z])}{\tau({\bf t})}e^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}z^{-i}}.\quad\Psi^{\ast}({\bf t};z)=\frac{\tau({\bf t}+[z])}{\tau({\bf t})}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}z^{-i}}.

(2.19)

These functions are solutions of the linear problem associated with the KP-hierarchy
[
DJKM
]
.
For the inverse construction we use the adjoint wave function.
Let
\Psi_{i}^{\ast}(z)
be the Laurent series in
z
defined by

\displaystyle\left(\tau({\bf t})\Psi^{\ast}({\bf t};z)\right)|_{t=(x,0,0,0,...)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Psi_{i}^{\ast}(z)x^{i}.

(2.20)

Then

\displaystyle U=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{C}}\Psi_{i}^{\ast}(z).

(2.21)

By this construction the following property follows.
Let
U
be a point of UGM,
\tau({\bf t})
a tau function corresponding to
U
and

f(z)=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\frac{z^{i}}{i}}
an invertible formal power series.
Then
f(z)U
belongs to UGM and a tau function corresponding to it is given by

\displaystyle e^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}t_{i}}\tau({\bf t}).

(2.22)

3
(n,1) curve and main results

In this section the problem is fromulated and main results are stated.

Let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer and
\alpha
a complex number.
Consider the rational curve
C_{n}
defined by

\displaystyle y^{n}=x-\alpha_{j}

(3.1)

which we call
(n,1)
curve.
The point of infinity of
C_{n}
corresponds to
y=\infty
.
Take a local coordinate
z
around
\infty
such that

\displaystyle x=z^{-n},\hskip 28.45274pty=z^{-1}(1-\alpha z^{n})^{1/n}.

(3.2)

This type of the local coordinate
z
was used for a general
(n,s)
curves of genus
g\geq 1

in constructing the multivariate sigma functions
[
BEL1997
,
BEL1999
,
N2010-1
]
and the quasi-periodic
solutions of the
n
-reduced KP-hierarchy
[
BEL1997
,
N2010-2
]
.

Let
V_{n}
be the space of meromorphic functions on
C_{n}
which have a pole
only at
p_{\infty}
.
It is nothing but the vector space generated by
y^{i}
,
i\geq 0
:

\displaystyle V_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{C}}y^{i}.

By expanding
y^{i}
in the local coordinate
z
we consider
V_{n}
as a subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}((z))
.
We denote this subspace by
V_{n}(z)
indicating the choice of the local coordinate
z
.
Later we consider a coordinate change.
Notice that

\displaystyle y^{i}=z^{-i}(1+O(z)),\quad i\geq 0.

By taking linear cominations of them we have a frame of
V_{n}(z)
of the form (
2.5
).
Therefore

\displaystyle V_{n}(z)\in{\rm UGM}^{\phi}.

Let
\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))
be the normalized tau function of
V_{n}(z)
.

Our main theorem is

Theorem 3.1
.

The following formula holds:

\displaystyle\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left(q({\bf t})+L({\bf t})\right)},

\displaystyle q({\bf t})=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}q_{i,j}t_{i}t_{j},\hskip 14.22636ptL({\bf t})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}L_{in}t_{in}

Here,
q_{i,j}=q_{j,i}
for any
i,j
,
q_{i,j}=0
if
j=nm
for some
m\geq 1
or
i+j\neq 0

{\rm mod.}n
and,
for
r\geq 1
,
s\geq 0
,
1\leq p\leq n-1
,
i\geq 1
,

\displaystyle q_{nr-p,ns+p}=\alpha^{r+s}\frac{pr}{r+s}\binom{r-\frac{p}{n}}{r}\binom{s+\frac{p}{n}}{s}.

(3.3)

\displaystyle L_{in}=-\sum_{r+s=i,r\geq 1,s\geq 0}\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\frac{ni}{(nr-p)(ns+p)}q_{nr-p,ns+p},

(3.4)

where, for a non-negative integer
n
,

\displaystyle\binom{x}{n}=\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1)}{n!},\quad n\geq 1,\qquad\binom{x}{0}=1.

The Schur function expansion of
\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))
can also be computed.

Theorem 3.2
.

The following expansion holds.

\displaystyle\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{r_{i},s_{i},p_{i},q_{i}}A_{l}((r_{i}),(s_{i}),(p_{i}),(q_{i}))s_{(nr_{1}-p_{1}-1,...,nr_{l}-p_{l}-1|ns_{1}+q_{1},...,ns_{l}+q_{l})}(t),

\displaystyle A_{l}((r_{i}),(s_{i}),(p_{i}),(q_{i}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{l}(ns_{i}+q_{i}+r_{i})}\alpha^{\sum_{i=1}^{l}(r_{i}+s_{i})}\det\left(\delta_{p_{i},q_{j}}\binom{r_{i}+s_{j}-1}{s_{j}}\binom{s_{j}+\frac{p_{i}}{n}}{r_{i}+s_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq l}.

Here the sumation in the second term is over all
r_{i},s_{i},p_{i},q_{i}
satisfying

\displaystyle 1\leq p_{i},q_{i}\leq n-1,\quad r_{i}\geq 1,\quad s_{i}\geq 0,

\displaystyle nr_{1}-p_{1}>\cdots>nr_{l}-p_{l},

\displaystyle ns_{1}+q_{1}>\cdots>ns_{l}+q_{l},

and
(m_{1},...,m_{l}|m_{1}^{\prime},...,m_{l}^{\prime})
is the Frobenius notation of a partition.

The proofs of theorems are given in subsequent sections.

4
Coordinate change and wave functions

In this section we derive the equation for
\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))
by considering a coordinate change
and wave functions.

Let us take
w=y^{-1}
as another local coordinate around
\infty
.
Then

\displaystyle x-\alpha=w^{-n},\hskip 28.45274pty=w^{-1}.

(4.1)

By expanding elements of
V_{n}
in terms of
w
and identifying the ambient space
V
of UGM
with
{\mathbb{C}}((w))
,
we define the subspace
V_{n}(w)
of
V
.
Then

\displaystyle V_{n}(w)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{C}}w^{-i}=V_{\phi}\in{\rm UGM}^{\phi}.

So
\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(w))=1
.
Consider the corresponding wave and adjoint wave functions,

\displaystyle\Psi(t;w)=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}w^{-i}},\hskip 28.45274pt\Psi^{\ast}(t;w)=e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}w^{-i}}.

(4.2)

Here we change the local coordinate from
w
to
z
.
By (
4.1
)
z
and
w
are connected by

\displaystyle z^{-n}-\alpha=w^{-n}.

Therefore

\displaystyle w=z(1-\alpha z^{n})^{-1/n}.

(4.3)

Expand

\displaystyle(1-\alpha z^{n})^{i/n}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{i,m}z^{m},

(4.4)

where

\displaystyle a_{i,m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(-\alpha)^{k}\binom{\frac{i}{n}}{k},&m=nk\text{ for some $k\geq 0$}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We set
a_{i,j}=0
if
j<0
for the sake of convenience.
Let us decompose
w^{-i}
into two parts corresponding to negative and non-negative powers in
z
,

\displaystyle w^{-i}=\sum_{m=0}^{i-1}a_{i,m}z^{-(i-m)}+f_{i}(z),\quad f_{i}(z)=\sum_{m=i}^{\infty}a_{i,m}z^{m-i}.

(4.8)

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}w^{-i}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}\sum_{m=0}^{i-1}a_{i,m}z^{-(i-m)}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}f_{i}(z).

Define the new set of time variables
\{T_{i}|i\geq 1\}
by

\displaystyle T_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}a_{j,j-i}t_{j}.

(4.9)

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}w^{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}T_{l}z^{-l}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}f_{i}(z).

(4.10)

The coefficient matrix
(a_{j,j-i})_{i,j\geq 1}
in the right hand side of (
4.9
) is an upper triangular matrix whose diagonal entries
are all one. So it has the inverse which is again an upper triangular matrix with the same property.
Therefore
t_{i}
can be written as

\displaystyle t_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{i,j}T_{j},

(4.11)

where
b_{i,j}=0
if
j<i
and
b_{i,i}=1
for
i\geq 1
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}f_{i}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}T_{j}F_{j}(z),

(4.12)

with

\displaystyle F_{j}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}b_{i,j}a_{i,m+i}\right)z^{m}.

(4.13)

Substituting (
4.10
) and (
4.12
) into (

\tau_{0}({\bf t};V_{n}(z))
.

Let

\displaystyle G(z)=(1-\alpha z^{n})^{\frac{1}{n}},\qquad H(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}G(z_{1})-z_{1}G(z_{2}).

(8.1)

Since
H(z_{1},z_{1})=0
,
H(z_{1},z_{2})/(z_{2}-z_{1})
is holomorphic near
(0,0)
.
It has the expansion of the form

\displaystyle\frac{H(z_{1},z_{2})}{z_{2}-z_{1}}=1+\text{h.o.t},

where h.o.t means terms containing
z_{1}^{i}z_{2}^{j}
with
i+j\geq 1
.
Thus
\log(H(z_{1},z_{2})/(z_{2}-z_{1}))
can be expanded to a power series in
z_{1},z_{2}
.

Proposition 8.1
.

The following expansion holds,

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}\log\left(\frac{H(z_{1},z_{2})}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{\infty}q_{i,j}z_{1}^{i-1}z_{2}^{j-1},\quad\partial_{z_{i}}=\partial/\partial z_{i}.

(8.2)

Remark 8.2
.

For
(p_{1},p_{2})\in C_{n}\times C_{n}
,
p_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i})
, the bilinear differential

\displaystyle d_{z_{1}}d_{z_{2}}\log H(z_{1},z_{2})=d_{p_{1}}d_{p_{2}}\log(y_{1}-y_{2})=d_{p_{2}}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}y_{1}^{j}y_{2}^{n-j}}{(x_{1}-x_{2})ny_{1}^{n-1}}dx_{1}

is the genus zero analogue of that of an
(n,s)
curve with positive genus
which is used in the construction of the multi-variate sigma function

[
BEL1997
,
BEL1999
,
N2010-1
]
.

Proof of Proposition
8.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}\log\left(\frac{H(z_{1},z_{2})}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\right)=\partial_{z_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}\log\left(1-\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right)-\frac{1}{(z_{2}-z_{1})^{2}}.

(8.3)

Let us expand the right hand side near
(z_{1},z_{2})=(0,0)
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
assuming

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\right|<1,\quad\left|\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right|<1,\quad z_{1}z_{2}\neq 0.

For example

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(z_{2}-z_{1})^{2}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}mz_{1}^{m-1}z_{2}^{-m-1}.

Since the left hand side of (
8.3
) is holomorphic at
(0,0)
, only non-negative powers
of
z_{1},z_{2}
should remain in the expansion of the right hand side under the assumption above.
Define the symbol
[\quad]_{+}
by

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i,j=-\infty}^{\infty}e_{i,j}z_{1}^{i}z_{2}^{j}\right]_{+}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}e_{i,j}z_{1}^{i}z_{2}^{j}.

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}\log\left(\frac{H(z_{1},z_{2})}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\right)=\left[\partial_{z_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}\log\left(1-\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right)\right]_{+}.

(8.4)

Write

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{z_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}\log\left(1-\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right)\right]_{+}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{\infty}\tilde{q}_{i,j}z_{1}^{i-1}z_{2}^{j-1}.

(8.5)

We shall show that
\tilde{q}_{i,j}=q_{i,j}
.

Expand the logarithmic function as

\displaystyle\log\left(1-\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right)=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\left(\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right)^{m}\left(\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\right)^{m}.

(8.6)

We further expand

\displaystyle\left(\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right)^{m}=(1-\alpha z_{1}^{n})^{-\frac{m}{n}}(1-\alpha z_{2}^{n})^{\frac{m}{n}}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}(-\alpha)^{k+l}\binom{-\frac{m}{n}}{k}\binom{\frac{m}{n}}{l}z_{1}^{nk}z_{2}^{nl}.

(8.7)

Substituting this expression into (
8.6
) and writing
m=ni+p
,
i\geq 0
,
1\leq p\leq n
,
we get

RHS of (
8.6
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{ni+p}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}(-\alpha)^{k+l}\binom{-i-\frac{p}{n}}{k}\binom{i+\frac{p}{n}}{l}z_{1}^{n(k+i)+p}z_{2}^{n(l-i)-p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{ni+p}\sum_{s\geq i,r\geq-i}^{\infty}(-\alpha)^{r+s}\binom{-i-\frac{p}{n}}{s-i}\binom{i+\frac{p}{n}}{r+i}z_{1}^{ns+p}z_{2}^{nr-p}.

Then

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{z_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}\log\left(1-\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\frac{G(z_{2})}{G(z_{1})}\right)\right]_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n}\sum_{r\geq 1,s\geq 0}\tilde{q}_{ns+p,nr-p}z_{1}^{ns+p-1}z_{2}^{nr-p-1},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{ns+p,nr-p}=(-1)^{r+s+1}\alpha^{r+s}\sum_{i=0}^{s}\frac{(ns+p)(nr-p)}{ni+p}\binom{-i-\frac{p}{n}}{s-i}\binom{i+\frac{p}{n}}{r+i}.

By this expression we see that
\tilde{q}_{i,j}=0
if
i+j\neq 0
mod.
n
.

A computation shows

\displaystyle(ns+p)(nr-p)\binom{-i-\frac{p}{n}}{s-i}\binom{i+\frac{p}{n}}{r+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{r+s+i-1}p(ni+p)\frac{r!s!}{(r+i)!(s-i)!}\binom{s+\frac{p}{n}}{s}\binom{r-\frac{p}{n}}{r}.

Then

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{ns+p,nr-p}=pr!s!\alpha^{r+s}\binom{s+\frac{p}{n}}{s}\binom{r-\frac{p}{n}}{r}\sum_{i=0}^{s}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(r+i)!(s-i)!}.

(8.8)

Using Lemma
5.3
we easily have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{s}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(r+i)!(s-i)!}=\frac{1}{(r+s)(r-1)!s!}.

Substituting it to (
8.8
) we get

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{ns+p,nr-p}=\alpha^{r+s}\frac{pr}{r+s}\binom{s+\frac{p}{n}}{s}\binom{r-\frac{p}{n}}{r}=q_{ns+p,nr-p}.

Thus the proposition is proved.
∎

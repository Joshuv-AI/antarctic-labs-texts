Rigidity theorems for best Sobolev inequalities
Francesco Maggi, Robin Neumayer, Ignacio Tomasetti

Abstract
. For n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , p ∈ ( 1 , n ) p\in(1,n) , the “best p p -Sobolev inequality” on an open set Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} is identified with a family Φ Ω \Phi_{\Omega} of variational problems with critical volume and trace constraints. When Ω \Omega is bounded we prove: (i) for every n n and p p , the existence of generalized minimizers that have at most one boundary concentration point, and: (ii) for n > 2 ​ p n>2\,p , the existence of (classical) minimizers. We then establish rigidity results for the comparison theorem “balls have the worst best Sobolev inequalities” by the first named author and Villani, thus giving the first affirmative answers to a question raised in [ MV05 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

The goal of this paper is to answer some basic open questions concerning a “doubly critical” family
\{\Phi_{\Omega}(T)\}_{T\geq 0}
of minimization problems on Sobolev functions, which, in a precise sense to be clarified below, can be interpreted as collectively defining the best Sobolev inequality on an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{1}
-boundary. Given an integer
n\geq 2
and
p\in(1,n)
, these problems are defined as

\Phi_{\Omega}(T)=\inf\Big\{\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}:\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p^{\star}}=1\,,\,\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p^{\#}}=T^{p^{\#}}\Big\}\,,

(1.1)

and their minimizers, whenever they exist, satisfy the Euler–Lagrange equation

\left\{\begin{split}&-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda\,u^{{p^{\star}}-1}\,,\quad\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{on $\Omega$}\,,\\
&|\nabla u|^{p-2}\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{\Omega}}=\sigma\,u^{{p^{\#}}-1}\,,\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}\,,\end{split}\right.

(1.2)

for suitable Lagrange multipliers
\lambda,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. We call
\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p^{\star}}=1
and
\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p^{\#}}=T^{p^{\#}}
the “volume” and “trace” constraints of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
. The critical Sobolev exponents associated to
n
and
p
,
{p^{\star}}
and
{p^{\#}}
, are defined by

{p^{\star}}=\frac{np}{n-p}\,,\qquad{p^{\#}}=\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p}=\frac{n-1}{n}\,{p^{\star}}\,,

and their precise values guarantee the scale invariance of
\Phi_{\Omega}
, i.e.

\Phi_{x+r\,\Omega}(T)=\Phi_{\Omega}(T)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,r>0\,,T\geq 0\,.

When
\Omega
is bounded, the
C^{1}
-regularity of
\partial\Omega
guarantees that every
u\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
with
\nabla u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
lies in the competition class of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
for some
T\geq 0
. In particular,

{\rm Epi}(\Phi_{\Omega})=\big\{(T,G)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:T\geq 0\,,G\geq\Phi_{\Omega}(T)\big\}\,,

(the epigraph of
\Phi_{\Omega}
) collects the best possible information on the range of values achievable by
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
when
\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega)}
is fixed: from this peculiar viewpoint, which is reminiscent of the one adopted in the study of Blaschke–Santaló diagrams,
{\rm Epi}(\Phi_{\Omega})

is
“the best Sobolev inequality on
\Omega

1.2.
Statements of the main results

With this summary on the state of the art for
\Phi_{\Omega}
in mind, there are two fundamental open questions that form the subject of our paper:

Question 1:
When does
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
(
T>0
) admit minimizers?

Question 2:
Does rigidity hold in the comparison theorem (
1.12
)?

The main idea of this paper is attacking these two closely related questions by systematically exploiting the complete characterization of
\Phi_{H}
obtained in
[
MN17
]
.

Concerning Question 1, a classical concentration-compactness argument characterizes the limit behavior of minimizing sequences of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
as the superposition of a standard weak limit plus at most countably many concentration points, located either in the interior of
\Omega
, or on its boundary. By exploiting properties of
\Phi_{H}
we are able to (i): exclude all interior concentrations and all but
at most one
boundary concentration, thus proving existence of minimizers for a suitable “relaxed problem”
\Phi_{\Omega}^{*}(T)
; and (ii): completely exclude concentrations, and thus establish the existence of minimizers of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
, as soon as
\partial\Omega
is of class
C^{2}
and
n>2p\,
. To give precise statements, it is convenient to let
\mathcal{X}_{\Omega}(T)
denote the competition class of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
, and let
\mathcal{Y}_{\Omega}(T)
denote the set of triples
(u,\mathsf{v},\mathsf{t})
with either
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\Omega}(T)
and
\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{t}=0
, or
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
\mathsf{v}\in(0,1]
,
\mathsf{t}\in(0,T]
, and

\mathsf{v}^{p^{\star}}+\int_{\Omega}u^{p^{\star}}=1\,,\qquad\mathsf{t}^{p^{\#}}+\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{p^{\#}}=T^{p^{\#}}\,.

(1.20)

The relaxed problem associated to
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
is then given by

\Phi_{\Omega}^{*}(T)=\inf_{\mathcal{Y}_{\Omega}(T)}\,\mathcal{E}\,,\,\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,\,\mathcal{E}(u,\mathsf{v},\mathsf{t})^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}+\mathsf{v}^{p}\,\Phi_{H}\Big(\frac{\mathsf{t}}{\mathsf{v}}\Big)^{p}\,,

(1.21)

with the convention that
\mathsf{v}^{p}\Phi_{H}(\mathsf{t}/\mathsf{v})^{p}=0
if
(\mathsf{v},\mathsf{t})=(0,0)
.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of minimizers of
\Phi_{\Omega}
)
.

If
n\geq 2
,
p\in(1,n)
, and
\Omega
is a bounded open set with
C^{1}
-boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then:

(i):
for every
T>0
, there is a minimizer
(u,\mathsf{v},\mathsf{t})
of
\Phi_{\Omega}^{*}(T)
, and

\Phi_{\Omega}(T)=\Phi_{\Omega}^{*}(T)\,;

(1.22)

moreover, if
\int_{\Omega}u^{p^{\star}}>0
, then
u/\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega)}
is a minimizer of
\Phi_{\Omega}(\|u\|_{L^{p^{\#}}(\partial\Omega)}/\|u\|_{L^{p^{\star}}(\Omega)})
;

(ii):
if
\Omega
has boundary of class
C^{2}
,
n>2p
,
T>0
, and
(u,\mathsf{v},\mathsf{t})
is a minimizer of
\Phi_{\Omega}^{*}(T)
, then
\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{t}=0
, and thus

is a minimizer of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
.

.

Minimizers of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
solve the Euler–Lagrange equation (
1.2

(u,\mathsf{v},\mathsf{t})

Question 2 is motivated by the various rigidity statements associated to comparison theorems in Riemannian geometry (see, e.g.
[
CE08
]
). In that setting, a certain model space provides a universal bound on a certain global geometric quantity (comparison theorem), which is then shown to be saturated by the model space alone (rigidity statement). With this analogy in mind, we can reformulate more precisely Question 2 as follows:

Question 2, weak form:
Does
\Phi_{\Omega}=\Phi_{B}
on
(0,{\rm ISO}(B)^{1/{p^{\#}}})
imply that
\Omega
is a ball?

Question 2, strong form:
Does
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)=\Phi_{B}(T)
at just one value of
T\in(0,{\rm ISO}(B)^{1/{p^{\#}}})
imply that
\Omega
is a ball?

Concerning the
weak form
of Question 2, through a careful use of the properties of
\Phi_{H}
we answer affirmatively whenever
\Omega
is bounded and connected. These conditions are optimal, as shown by unbounded or disconnected non-rigidity examples presented in
[
MV05
]
. In fact, the argument we propose gives rigidity under the mere assumption that
\Phi_{\Omega}=\Phi_{B}
holds on an open neighborhood of
T=0
. Concerning the
strong form
of Question 2, which was originally formulated in
[
MV05

, we can answer in the affirmative as a direct by-product of our existence result for minimizers of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
(thus, when

-boundary and

) thanks to the following “conditional rigidity” statement, which is proved in
[

]
as a direct by-product of the proof of (

\displaystyle\mbox{{\it if $\Omega$ is connected (possibly unbounded)}}\,,

\displaystyle\mbox{{\it if $\Phi_{\Omega}(T)=\Phi_{B}(T)$ for a value of $T\in(0,{\rm ISO}(B)^{1/{p^{\#}}})$}}\,,

(1.23)

\displaystyle\mbox{{\it and if $\Phi_{\Omega}(T)$ is known to admit minimizers (possibly just for that $T$)}}\,,

\displaystyle\mbox{{\it then $\Omega$ is a ball}}\,.

(We notice for future use an important consequence of (

\Phi_{B}(T)<\Phi_{H}(T)\,,\qquad\forall T\in\big(0,{\rm ISO}(B)^{1/{p^{\#}}}\big)\,;

(Rigidity of “Balls have the worst best Sobolev inequalities”)

1.3.
Strategy of proof

Concentration-compactness arguments and the use of sharp Sobolev-type inequalities (like the Sobolev and Escobar inequalities (
1.3
) and (
1.6
)) are the standard tools of the trade in the analysis of variational problems with critical growth. As seen, if interpreted as assertions about
\Phi_{H}
, (
1.3
) and (
1.6

\Phi_{H}(0)=S(n,p)
” and “
\Phi_{H}(T)\geq E(n,p)\,T

\Phi_{H}
obtained in
[
CL94
,
MN17
]
. We now explain how this characterization is used in this paper.

We have already mentioned how the mere knowledge of the existence of minimizers in
\Phi_{H}(T)
for every
T>0
allows one to reduce the analysis of concentrations to the simplest possible case of a
single
boundary concentration (thus leading to Theorem
1.1
-(i)). Finer properties of
\Phi_{H}
are exploited in the proof of Theorem
1.1
-(ii), which goes as follows. We consider the existence of a minimizer
(u,\mathsf{v},\mathsf{t})

\Phi_{\Omega}^{*}(T)

2

2.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper we always assume that
n\geq 2
and
p\in(1,n)
. We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{n}
and
\mathcal{H}^{k}
the Lebesgue measure and the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, although we simply set
|E|
in place of
\mathcal{L}^{n}(E)
. We denote by
B_{r}(x)
the open ball of center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and radius
r>0
, and set
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
, while
B
denotes a ball of unspecified center and radius.

Following a standard shorthand notation, by “
f(x)=g(x)+{\rm O}_{a,b}(|x|)
for
|x|>R
” we mean that
|f(x)-g(x)|\leq C(a,b)\,|x|
if
|x|>R
; by “
f(x)=g(x)+{\rm o}_{a,b}(|x|)
as
|x|\to 0
” we mean that
\lim_{|x|\to 0}|f(x)-g(x)|/|x|=0
at a rate that is uniform with respect to the parameters
a
and
b
.

In general, we will use capital letters (e.g.
U,V,\Psi
) to denote functions defined on the half space
H
and lowercase letters (e.g.
u,v,\varphi
) to denote functions defined on an open bounded domain
\Omega
.

2.2.
Concentration-compactness

The following lemma is a version of Lions’ celebrated
concentration-compactness lemma and provides a natural starting point to study minimizing sequences of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
.

Lemma 2.1

(Concentration-compactness)
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
p\in(1,n)
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded with
C^{1}
-boundary. If
\{u_{j}\}_{j}
is a sequence in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
,
\{\nabla u_{j}\}_{j}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
as distributions in
\Omega
, then the Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{j}=|\nabla u_{j}|^{p}\,\mathcal{L}^{n}\llcorner\Omega\,,\qquad\nu_{j}=|u_{j}|^{p^{\star}}\,\mathcal{L}^{n}\llcorner\Omega\,,\qquad\tau_{j}=|u_{j}|^{{p^{\#}}}\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial\Omega\,,

(2.1)

have subsequential weak-star limits
\mu
,
\nu
and
\tau
which satisfy

\displaystyle\nu\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!|u|^{{p^{\star}}}\mathcal{L}^{n}\llcorner\Omega+\sum_{i\in I}\mathsf{v}_{i}^{p^{\star}}\,\delta_{x_{i}}\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\tau\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!|u|^{{p^{\#}}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\llcorner\partial\Omega+\sum_{i\in I}\mathsf{t}_{i}^{p^{\#}}\,\delta_{x_{i}}\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu\!

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\!|\nabla u|^{p}\mathcal{L}^{n}\llcorner\Omega+\sum_{i\in I}\mathsf{g}_{i}^{p}\,\delta_{x_{i}},

(2.4)

where
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\overline{\Omega}
is at most countable set,
\mathsf{v}_{i}>0
and
\mathsf{t}_{i}\geq 0
for every
i\in I
,
\mathsf{t}_{i}>0
only if
x_{i}\in\partial\Omega
, and

\mathsf{g}_{i}\geq\mathsf{v}_{i}\,\Phi_{H}\Big(\frac{\mathsf{t}_{i}}{\mathsf{v}_{i}}\Big)\,,\qquad\forall i\in I\,.

(2.5)

In particular,
\mathsf{g}_{i}\geq S\,\mathsf{v}_{i}
whenever
x_{i}\in\Omega
.

Proof.

See appendix
A
.
∎

2.3.
Near-boundary coordinates

In this section, we introduce two types of coordinates for a neighborhood of a boundary point of a domain
\Omega
: one that requires minimal regularity of the boundary of
\Omega
and will suffice in the proofs of Theorem
1.1
(i)
and Theorem
1.3
(i)
, and a second that requires
C^{2}
regularity of the boundary of
\Omega
and will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(ii)
and Theorem
1.3
(ii)
.

Given an open set
\Omega
with
C^{1}
-boundary, we denote by
\nu_{\Omega}
its outer unit normal and by
T_{x}(\partial\Omega)
the tangent space to
x\in\partial\Omega
. When
\Omega
has
C^{2}
-boundary, we denote by
{\rm A}_{\Omega}
and
{\rm H}_{\Omega}
the second fundamental form and the scalar mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
defined by
\nu_{\Omega}
.
To define coordinates near boundary points of
\Omega
, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we set
\mathbf{p}(x)=x-x_{n}\,e_{n}
,
\textbf{{D}}_{r}=\{x:x_{n}=0\,,|\mathbf{p}x|<r\}
, and
\textbf{{C}}_{r}=\big\{x:|x_{n}|<r\,,|\mathbf{p}(x)|<r\big\}
. In particular, if
\Omega
is an open set with
C^{1}
-boundary such that

0\in\partial\Omega\,,\qquad T_{0}\,(\partial\Omega)=\{x_{n}=0\}\,,\qquad\nu_{\Omega}(0)=-e_{n}\,,

(2.6)

then we can find
r_{0}>0
and
\ell:\textbf{{D}}_{r_{0}}\to(-r_{0},r_{0})
such that
\ell(0)=0
,
\nabla\ell(0)=0
, and

\displaystyle\Omega\cap\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\big\{x+t\,e_{n}:x\in\textbf{{D}}_{r_{0}}\,,r_{0}>t>\ell(x)\big\}\,,

\displaystyle(\partial\Omega)\cap\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\big\{x+\ell(x)\,e_{n}:x\in\textbf{{D}}_{r_{0}}\big\}\,.

We then define the maps
F:\textbf{{D}}_{r_{0}}\to\partial\Omega
,
f:\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\hat{f}:\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by setting

\displaystyle F(x)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!x+\ell(x)\,e_{n}\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,x\in\textbf{{D}}_{r_{0}}\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\hat{f}(x)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!F(\mathbf{p}x)+x_{n}e_{n}\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\!x\in\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}\,.

(2.8)

\displaystyle f(x)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!F(\mathbf{p}x)-x_{n}\,\nu_{\Omega}(F(\mathbf{p}x))\,,\qquad x\in\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}\,.

(2.9)

In this way, for every
y\in(\partial\Omega)\cap\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}
, if we set
y=F(\mathbf{p}x)
, then

\nu_{\Omega}(y)=\frac{\nabla\ell(x)-e_{n}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\ell(x)|^{2}}}\,,\qquad{\rm H}_{\Omega}(y)={\rm div}\,\Big(\frac{\nabla\ell}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla\ell|^{2}}}\Big)(x)\,.

Notice that the map
f
need not be of class
C^{1}
if the boundary of
\Omega
is only of class
C^{1}
, while the map
\hat{f}
will be as regular as the boundary of
\Omega
. The following lemma summarizes basic properties about the map
\hat{f}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Near-boundary coordinates, one)
.

If
H=\{x_{n}>0\}
,
\Omega
is an open set with
C^{1}
-boundary and (
2.6
) holds,
then there exist
r_{0}
and
C_{0}
positive such that the map
\hat{f}
in (
2.8
) defines a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism from
\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}
to its image,
taking
\textbf{{D}}_{r_{0}}
into
\partial\Omega
and with

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\textbf{{C}}_{r/C_{0}}\cap H)\subset\Omega\cap B_{r}\subset\hat{f}(\textbf{{C}}_{C_{0}\,r}\cap H)\qquad\forall r<r_{0}/C_{0}\,.

(2.10)

Moreover, letting
\hat{g}=\hat{f}^{-1}
denote the inverse of
\hat{f}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\hat{f}={\rm Id}\,_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+{\rm o}(1)\,,\qquad(\nabla\hat{g})^{*}\circ\hat{f}={\rm Id}\,_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+{\rm o}(1)\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle J\hat{f}=1+{\rm o}(1)\,,\qquad\qquad 1\leq J^{\partial H}\hat{f}\leq 1+{\rm o}(1)\,,\quad\quad\mbox{for $x\in\textbf{{C}}_{r_{0}}$.}

(2.12)

The orders in (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) depend on
\partial\Omega
and on
0\in\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

See appendix
B
.
∎

2.4.
Properties of minimizers

The following lemma gathers some fundamental properties of minimizers of
\Phi_{\Omega}
that will be needed in the sequel.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
n\geq 2
,
p\in(1,n)
,
T>0
,
\Omega
is a bounded open set with
C^{2}
-boundary, and
u
is a minimizer of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
, then
u
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous in
\Omega
, there are
\lambda
and
\sigma
such that the Euler–Lagrange equation (
1.2
) holds in the weak sense, and the balance condition

\lambda\,\int_{\Omega}u^{{p^{\star}}-1}+\sigma\,\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{{p^{\#}}-1}=0\,,

(2.16)

holds.

Proof.

By a standard argument, based on similar considerations to the one presented in Lemma
2.6
below, one sees that a minimizer
u
of
\Phi_{\Omega}(T)
is a
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-distributional solution of the Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.2
) for some
\lambda,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. As soon as
\Omega
is bounded and has Lipschitz boundary, one can exploit (
1.2
) in conjunction with a Moser iteration argument to prove that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
(see, e.g.
[
MW19
, Theorem 3.1]
; their result applies to (
1.2
) by taking, in the notation of their paper,
\mathcal{A}(x,u,{\nabla}u)=|{\nabla}u|^{p-2}{\nabla}u
,
\mathcal{B}(x,u,{\nabla}u)=\lambda u^{{p^{\star}}-1}
, and
\mathcal{C}(x,u)=\sigma u^{{p^{\#}}-1}
). On further assuming that
\partial\Omega
is of class
C^{2}
, then the classical result
[
Lie92
, Theorem 1.7]
can be applied to deduce that
u\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
for a suitable
\beta=\beta(n,p)\in(0,1)
(for more details, see
[
MW19
, Theorem 3.9]
). In particular,
u
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
, as claimed.
∎

2.5.
Volume/trace correcting variations

At various stages in our arguments we will need to slightly modify certain competitors so to restore the volume and trace constraints defining
\mathcal{X}_{\Omega}(T)
. The following lemma describes the basic mechanism used to this end.

Lemma 2.6

(Volume/trace correcting variations)
.

If
n\geq 2
,
p\in(1,n)
,
M>0
,
\Omega
is an open set with
C^{1}
-boundary,
v\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
with
\nabla v\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and if
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
are such that

\mbox{$\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}v^{p^{\star}}$ and $\int_{(\partial\Omega)\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}v^{p^{\#}}$ are positive and finite}\,,

(2.17)

then there exist positive constants
\eta
and
C
, and functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
and
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
, all depending on
n
,
p
,
v
and
M
only, and with the following property.

If
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}\subset L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
is such that, for every
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

\mbox{$v_{\varepsilon}=v$ on $\Omega\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})$}\,,\qquad\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\leq M\,,

(2.18)

then for every
(a,b)
with
|a|,|b|<\eta/C
, we can find
(s,t)
with
|s|,|t|<\eta
such that

w_{\varepsilon}=v_{\varepsilon}+s\,\varphi+t\,\psi

satisfies

\int_{\partial\Omega}|w_{\varepsilon}|^{p^{\#}}=a+\int_{\partial\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p^{\#}}\,,\qquad\int_{\Omega}|w_{\varepsilon}|^{p^{\star}}=b+\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p^{\star}}\,,

(2.19)

\Big|\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{p}-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\Big|\leq C\,\big(|a|+|b|\big)\,.

(2.20)

Proof.

By (
2.17
) there are
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
and
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
such that

\int_{\partial\Omega}v^{{p^{\#}}-1}\,\xi=1\,,\qquad\int_{\Omega}v^{{p^{\star}}-1}\,\psi=1\,.

(2.21)

Setting
\varphi=\xi-(\int_{\Omega}v^{{p^{\star}}-1}\xi)\,\psi
, we have
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0}))
with

\int_{\Omega}v^{{p^{\star}}-1}\,\varphi=0\,,\qquad\int_{\partial\Omega}v^{{p^{\#}}-1}\,\varphi=1\,.

(2.22)

We now define
h_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

h_{\varepsilon}(s,t)\!=\!\Big(\int_{\partial\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}+s\,\varphi+t\,\psi|^{p^{\#}}-\!\int_{\partial\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p^{\#}},\ \int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}+s\,\varphi+t\,\psi|^{p^{\star}}-\!\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{p^{\star}}\Big)\,.

By (
2.18
) we have
h_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
\alpha=\alpha(n,p)\in(0,1)
, with

\sup_{\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}\|h_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})}<\infty\,;

moreover,
h_{\varepsilon}(0,0)=0
and, by (
2.21
) and (
2.22
),

\nabla h_{\varepsilon}(0,0)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c c}{p^{\#}}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{{p^{\#}}-1}\,\varphi&&{p^{\#}}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{{p^{\#}}-1}\,\psi\\
&\\
{p^{\star}}\,\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{{p^{\star}}-1}\,\varphi&&{p^{\star}}\,\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|^{{p^{\star}}-1}\,\psi\end{array}\right)=\Big(\begin{array}[]{c c}{p^{\#}}&0\\
0&{p^{\star}}\end{array}\Big)\,.

We can thus apply the inverse function theorem
uniformly
in
\varepsilon
, to find positive constants
\eta
and
C_{1}
depending on
n
,
p
, and
v
so that each
h_{\varepsilon}
is invertible on
E=\{(s,t):|s|,|t|<\eta\}
, with
\{(a,b):|a|,|b|<\eta/C_{1}\}\subset h_{\varepsilon}(E)
, and
\nabla h_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(a,b)\geq{\rm Id}\,_{2\times 2}/C_{1}
(in the sense of positive definite matrices) for every
(a,b)\in h_{\varepsilon}(E)
. In particular, if we let
(s,t)=h_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(a,b)
for a pair
(a,b)
with
|a|,|b|<\eta/C_{1}
, then the function
w_{\varepsilon}=v_{\varepsilon}+s\,\varphi+t\,\psi
satisfies (
2.19
) and
|(a,b)|=|h_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(s,t)|\geq|(s,t)|/C_{1}
. Moreover, by the elementary inequality

\big||X+Y|^{p}-|X|^{p}\big|\leq p\,\max\big\{|X|,|Y|\big\}^{p-1}\,|Y|\,\qquad\forall X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

we see that, setting
\gamma=\max\big\{|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|,|\nabla\varphi|,|\nabla\psi|\big\}^{p}
,

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{p}&-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\Big|\leq p\,\int_{\Omega}\gamma^{(p-1)/p}\,\big(|s|\,|\nabla\varphi|+|t|\,|\nabla\psi|\big)\\
&\leq C\,\Big(\int_{\Omega}\gamma\,\Big)^{(p-1)/p}\,\Big(\int_{\Omega}\big(|s|^{p}\,|\nabla\varphi|^{p}+|t|^{p}\,|\nabla\psi|^{p}\big)\Big)^{1/p}\\
&\leq C\,|(s,t)|\leq C_{2}\,|(a,b)|\,,\end{split}

(2.23)

for a constant
C_{2}
depending on
n
,
p
,
v
, and
M
. Letting
C=\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}
concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Boundary concentrations

3.1.
Properties of
\Phi_{H}
-minimizers

We recall some facts proved in
[
MN17
]
about
\Phi_{H}
and its minimizers. Recall that we denote by
T_{0}
the minimum point of
\Phi_{H}
, so that

T_{0}\in(0,T_{E})\,,\qquad\Phi_{H}(T_{0})=2^{-1/n}\,S(n,p)\,,

(3.1)

where
T_{E}
is the “Escobar trace” defined in (
1.8
). If we set
H=\{x_{n}>0\}
, the minimizers of
U_{T}
of
\Phi_{H}(T)
for
T>0
are characterized (modulo the obvious scaling and translation invariance of
\Phi_{H}
) as

\displaystyle U_{T}(x)=c_{T}\!\!\left\{\begin{split}&\tau_{t_{T}\,e_{n}}U_{S}(x)=(1+|x-t_{T}\,e_{n}|^{p^{\prime}})^{1-(n/p)}\,,\qquad\!\!\!\!\hskip 8.5359ptT\in(0,T_{E})\,,\\
&\tau_{e_{n}}U_{E}(x)=|x+e_{n}|^{(p-n)/(p-1)}\,,\qquad\!\!\!\!\hskip 48.36958ptT=T_{E}\,,\\
&\tau_{s_{T}\,e_{n}}U_{BE}(x)=(|x-s_{T}\,e_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}-1)^{1-(n/p)}\,,\!\!\!\!\qquad T>T_{E}\,,\end{split}\right.

(3.2)

where the constants
c_{T}
,
t_{T}
and
s_{T}
are chosen in such a way that

\int_{H}\,U_{T}^{p^{\star}}=1\,,\qquad\int_{\partial H}\,U_{T}^{p^{\sharp}}=T^{p^{\sharp}}\,,\qquad\int_{H}|\nabla U_{T}|^{p}=\Phi_{H}(T)^{p}\,.

(3.3)

It is convenient to keep in mind that the various formulas for
U_{T}
listed in (
3.2
) all share the same decay behavior at infinity, that is (see (
3.20
) below), we have

U_{T}(x)\sim|x|^{(p-n)/(p-1)},\qquad|{\nabla}U_{T}|\sim|x|^{(1-n)/(p-1)}\qquad\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.

(3.4)

(where the rate depends on the specific value of
T
under consideration). The constants
t_{T}
and
s_{T}
have the following properties:
T\in(0,T_{E})\mapsto t_{T}
is continuous and strictly decreasing, with
t_{T}>0
if and only if
T\in(0,T_{0})
, and

\lim_{T\to 0^{+}}t_{T}=+\infty\,,\qquad\lim_{t\to(T_{E})^{-}}t_{T}=-\infty\,,\qquad t_{T_{0}}=0\,,

(3.5)

while
T\in(T_{E},\infty)\mapsto s_{T}
is continuous, negative, strictly increasing, with

\lim_{T\to(T_{E})^{+}}s_{T}=-\infty\,,\qquad\lim_{T\to+\infty}s_{T}=-1\,.

(3.6)

Denoting by
\Delta_{p}v={\rm div}\,(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v)
the
p\,

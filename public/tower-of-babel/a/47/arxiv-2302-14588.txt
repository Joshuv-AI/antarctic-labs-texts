Fractional Korn’s inequalities without boundary conditions
D. Harutyunyan, T. Mengesha, H. Mikayelyan, and J.M. Scott

Abstract
Motivated by a linear nonlocal model of elasticity, this work establishes fractional analogues of Korn’s first and second inequalities for vector fields in fractional Sobolev spaces defined over a bounded domain. The validity of the inequalities require no additional boundary condition, extending existing fractional Korn’s inequalities that are only applicable for Sobolev vector fields satisfying zero Dirichlet boundary conditions. The domain of definition is required to have a C 1 \displaystyle C^{1} -boundary or, more generally, a Lipschitz boundary with small Lipschitz constant. We conjecture that the inequalities remain valid for vector fields defined over any Lipschitz domain. We support this claim by presenting a proof of the inequalities for vector fields defined over planar convex domains.



1
Introduction and main results

In this paper, we prove the fractional analogues of Korn’s first and second inequalities in the so-called bounded Lipschitz domains with small Lipschitz constant. For
\displaystyle n\geq 2
, suppose that
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz
boundary.
Classical Korn’s inequalities give a means of controlling the
\displaystyle L^{p}
-norm of the gradient
\displaystyle\nabla\bm{u}
of a Sobolev vector field
\displaystyle\bm{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the symmetric part of its gradient,
\displaystyle e(\bm{u})(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{u}(\bm{x})+\nabla\bm{u}^{\intercal}(\bm{x}))
(and the vector field itself). For
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
, a version of the classical Korn’s first inequality
[
7
,
8
]

states that there is a constant
\displaystyle C=C(\Omega)>0,
such that

\inf_{\bm{A}\in\text{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\nabla\bm{u}-\bm{A}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|e(\bm{u})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{u}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.1)

Here,
\displaystyle\text{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
represents the set of
\displaystyle n\times n
skew symmetric matrices.
Korn’s second inequality reads as follows
[
7
,
8
]
: there is a constant
\displaystyle C=C(\Omega)>0,
such that

\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|e(\bm{u})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}}\right)\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{u}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.2)

These inequalities play a fundamental role in establishing the well posedness of the linear equations of elastostatics, a system of partial differential equations arising from linearized elasticity under various boundary conditions
[
7
,
8
,
6
]
.

By a fractional analogue of these inequalities we mean estimates of these type for vector fields in the fractional Sobolev spaces
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),
for
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
, where
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is in
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if

|\bm{u}|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\bm{u}(\bm{x})-\bm{u}(\bm{y})|^{p}}{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|^{n+ps}}d\bm{x}d\bm{y}<\infty.

The function space
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space with the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+|\cdot|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
. When writing inequalities analogous to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for vector fields in
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one must find proper substitutes for the notion of gradient and symmetric part of the gradient. It is intuitively clear that the difference quotient
\displaystyle\frac{\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{u}(\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}
could be used as a substitute for the gradient of
\displaystyle\nabla\bm{u}
while the seminorm
\displaystyle|\bm{u}|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
replaces
\displaystyle\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
. Noting that for
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in\Omega
and
\displaystyle\bm{y}
close to
\displaystyle\bm{x}
, one has for suffficiently smooth vector fields
\displaystyle\bm{u}(\bm{x})
that

\displaystyle\frac{(\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{u}(\bm{x}))}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\cdot\frac{(\bm{y}-\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\approx e(\bm{u})(\bm{x})\frac{(\bm{y}-\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\cdot\frac{(\bm{y}-\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|},

we will use the projected difference quotient
\displaystyle\frac{(\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{u}(\bm{x}))}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\cdot\frac{(\bm{y}-\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}
as the nonlocal analogue for
\displaystyle e(\bm{u}),
and the seminorm

[\bm{u}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|(\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{u}(\bm{x}))\cdot\frac{(\bm{y}-\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\right|^{p}}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|^{d+ps}}d\bm{y}d\bm{x}

to replace its norm. This weighted norm of the projected difference quotient not only approximates the norm of
\displaystyle e(\bm{u})
but also inherits its zero sets. Indeed, for
\displaystyle L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
vector fields
\displaystyle\bm{u}
, the equality
\displaystyle(\bm{u}(\bm{x})-\bm{u}(\bm{y}))\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{y})=0
holds for a.e.
\displaystyle\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\Omega,
if and only if
\displaystyle\bm{u}
is an infinitesimal rigid motion
[
24
, Proposition 1.2]
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\bm{u}
has the form

\bm{u}(\bm{x})=\bm{A}\bm{x}+\bm{b},\quad\text{for some $\displaystyle\bm{A}\in\text{Skew}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ and $\displaystyle\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Those are exactly Sobolev vector fields that make
\displaystyle e(\bm{u})=\bm{0}

\displaystyle a.e.
in
\displaystyle\Omega,
as can be seen from (
1.1
). We denote this class of vector fields by
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
. Before we are ready to state the desired fractional analogues of Korn’s inequalities, we need to introduce the definition of bounded domains with Lipschitz constant not exceeding a number
\displaystyle L>0.

Definition 1.1
.

An open bounded Lipschitz domain

\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to have a Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle\leq L,
if the boundary of
\displaystyle\Omega
can be covered by finitely many balls (or cylinders)
\displaystyle B_{i},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\dots,m,
so that each portion

\displaystyle\partial\Omega\cap B_{i}
is the graph of a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle\leq L,
upon a rotation of the coordinate system.

Remark 1.2
.

An open bounded domain with
\displaystyle C^{1}
-boundary has a local Lipschitz constant as small as any initially chosen positive constant
\displaystyle\epsilon>0.

Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\displaystyle n\geq 2
,
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty)
. There exists a constant
\displaystyle M_{0}>0,
depending only on
\displaystyle n,p,
and
\displaystyle s,
such that the following holds: for any open bounded Lipschitz set
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle\leq M_{0},
there exist positive constants
\displaystyle C_{1}
and
\displaystyle C_{2},
depending only on
\displaystyle d,s,p
and
\displaystyle\Omega,

such that for all
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one has

\inf_{\bm{r}\in\mathcal{R}}|{\bm{u}}-{\bm{r}}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}[\bm{u}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)},

(1.3)

and

|{\bm{u}}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}([{\bm{u}}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{u}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}),

(1.4)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
is the class of infinitesimal rigid motions.

Some remarks are in order. The same way the classical Korn’s inequalities are linked to the linearized elasticity, so are their fractional analogues to some nonlocal models of elasticity. We discuss here one such model, peridynamics, a continuum nonlocal theory of mechanics of materials initially proposed by Stewart Silling
[
20
,
22
,
21
]
. In bond-based linearized peridynamics, a material occupying a domain
\displaystyle\Omega
is approximated to be a complex mass-spring system where material points interact, at a distance, with each other over a bond joining them. If the material is subject to a deformation
\displaystyle\bm{v}(\bm{x})=\bm{x}+\bm{u}(\bm{x})
, then
\displaystyle\frac{(\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{u}(\bm{x}))}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\cdot\frac{(\bm{y}-\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}
represents a (unit less) linearized nonlocal strain at
\displaystyle\bm{x}
along the bond
\displaystyle\bm{\xi}=\bm{y}-\bm{x}
. The total strain energy is postulated to be proportional to

W_{\rho}(\bm{u})=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\bm{y}-\bm{x})\left|\frac{(\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{u}(\bm{x}))}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\cdot\frac{(\bm{y}-\bm{x})}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|}\right|^{2}d\bm{y}d\bm{x},

where
\displaystyle\rho(\bm{\xi})
is locally integrable and serves as a weight for the long-range interactions. Given an external force
\displaystyle\bm{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the corresponding configuration can be found as a minimizer of the functional

\bm{u}\mapsto W_{\rho}(\bm{u})-\int_{\Omega}\bm{f}(\bm{x})\cdot\bm{u}(\bm{x})d\bm{x}

(1.5)

over an appropriate admissible subset of
\displaystyle L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In fact, existence of minimizers in some subsets of the energy space

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rho}(\Omega)=\{\bm{u}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):W_{\rho}(\bm{u})<\infty\}

is demonstrated in

[
13
]
. See also
[
14
]
for existence of solutions to more generalized models of linearized peridynamics. Except for
\displaystyle n=1
, the question that whether
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rho}(\Omega)
, which is based on the
projected difference-quotient
,
is equal to the space

\{\bm{u}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\rho(\bm{y}-\bm{x})\frac{\left|\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{u}(\bm{x})\right|^{2}}{|\bm{y}-\bm{x}|^{2}}d\bm{y}d\bm{x}<\infty\}

based on the full difference-quotient remains open. The fractional Korn’s inequalities proved in Theorem
1.3
address this question and establish equality of sets for a special case when
\displaystyle\rho(\bm{\xi})=|\bm{\xi}|^{-n-2(s-1)}
, for
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
. In this case,
\displaystyle W_{\rho}(\bm{u})=[\bm{u}]^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,2}(\Omega)}
and
minimizing the functional in (
1.5
) over a weakly closed subset of the smaller
\displaystyle W^{s,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, say,
\displaystyle W^{s,2}_{\omega}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{\bm{u}\in W^{s,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):\omega\subset\Omega,\,\,\bm{u}=\bm{0},\ \text{a.e. in}\ \omega\}
is possible. To apply Hilbert space methods, inequality (
1.4
) is now essential, along with a Poincaré-Korn inequality, see Lemma
2.1
below, to show the coercivity of the functional. We leave the details to the interested readers, see
[
13
,
14
]
.

Via a counterexample

Remark 1.4
.

It is well known that the range of exponent that validates the classical Korn inequalities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) is
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty.
Moreover, for Sobolev vector fields that satisfy zero Dirichlet boundary conditions, one can always choose
\displaystyle\bm{A}=0

in (

) in that range. However, this is no longer true in the fractional setting because despite the fact that the case
\displaystyle ps<1
is included in the validity range for (

) and (
1.4

)
with
\displaystyle\bm{A}=0

\displaystyle ps<1.

As it is clear from the formulation, Theorem

4

As we will show in Section

1.3

1.4
). The main challenge is thus proving (

1.4

[

]
and
[

]
and we will not repeat it here. We would rather focus on obtaining the estimate for epigraphs. That will be accomplished after proving the existence
of an extension operator to extend the vector fields in the epigraph to be defined on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As in
[
15
]
we will use the extension introduced in
[
17
]
which allows us to control the seminorm of the extended vector fields by the seminorm over the epigraph. In this work, we use an improved Hardy-type inequality, Lemma

in Section

[

and restricted the validity (

2
Korn-Poincaré and Hardy-type inequalities

Given an open set
\displaystyle D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
we define the spaces
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}_{0}(D)
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)
to be the closure of
\displaystyle C_{c}^{1}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\displaystyle C^{1}_{c}(\overline{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively, with respect to the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)},
where
\displaystyle C_{c}^{1}(\bar{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the set of
\displaystyle C^{1}
functions whose support is compactly contained in
\displaystyle\overline{D}
.
It is known that for bounded domains with Lipschitz boundary,
\displaystyle C^{1}(\overline{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)
, as shown in
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
.
We begin with the following Korn-Poincaré inequalities that are compatible with the seminorm
\displaystyle[\cdot]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}
.
It is worth mentioning that the fractional Korn-Poincaré inequality is an important component in the proof of the first and second fractional Korn inequalities. This is in contrast to the classical local setting where the Korn-Poincaré inequality is derived as a consequence of Korn’s first inequality after the latter has been established by other means.

Lemma
2.1

(Korn-Poincaré inequalities)
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain. Then for any
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists a positive constant
\displaystyle C
depending only on
\displaystyle n,p,s,
and
\displaystyle\Omega,
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\min_{\bm{r}\in\mathcal{R}}\|{\bf u}-\bm{r}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C[{\bf u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}\quad{\bf u}\in\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega).

Moreover, if
\displaystyle V\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a weakly closed subset such that
\displaystyle V\cap\mathcal{R}=\{\bm{0}\}
, then there exists a constant
\displaystyle\tilde{C}>0,
that in addition may depend on
\displaystyle V,
such that

\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\tilde{C}[{\bf u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}\quad{\bf u}\in V.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion. The proof of the second can be found in
[
14
, Proposition 2.7]
.
We will use a standard contradiction argument adopted by Kondratiev and Oleinik for the classical case in
[
6
]
. Suppose that there is a sequence
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{k}\in\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)
and the corresponding minimizers
\displaystyle\bm{A}_{k}\in\mathrm{skew}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\displaystyle{\bf b}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\|\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\bm{x}-{\bf b}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\quad\text{and}\quad[\bm{u}_{k}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq 1/k,\quad k=1,2,\dots

(2.7)

Upon passing to the fields
\displaystyle\bm{v}_{k}=\bm{u}_{k}-\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\bm{x}-{\bf b}_{k}
we can assume without loss of generality that
\displaystyle\bm{A}_{k}=0
and
\displaystyle{\bf b}_{k}=0
in (
2.7
) for all
\displaystyle k
. Thus we have the minimality conditions

\|\bm{v}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{A}\cdot\bm{x}-{\bf b}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for any}\quad\bm{A}\in\mathrm{skew}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \ {\bf b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ \ k=1,2,\dots

(2.8)

We then have from (
2.7
) that the sequence
\displaystyle{\bf v}_{k}
is bounded in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)
.
We can now apply the compactness theorem in
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
to conclude that
the sequence
\displaystyle\{\bm{v}_{k}\}
is pre-compact in
\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega),
thus we can assume without loss of generality that

\bm{v}_{k}\to\bm{v}\quad\text{in}\quad L^{p}(\Omega),

(2.9)

for some field
\displaystyle\bm{v}\in L^{p}(\Omega).
We have by (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{m}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=([\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{m}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C([\bm{v}_{k}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+[\bm{v}_{m}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C(1/k+1/m+\|\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}),

thus the condition (
2.9
) implies that the sequence
\displaystyle\{\bm{v}_{k}\}
is Cauchy and thus is convergent in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)
and the limit is
\displaystyle{\bf v}
. This gives
\displaystyle\bm{v}_{k}\to\bm{v}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as
\displaystyle k\to\infty
as well.
We thus have from (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle[\bm{v}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq[\bm{v}_{k}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+[\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{k}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 1/k+[\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{k}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}\to 0

as
\displaystyle k\to\infty,
thus
\displaystyle[\bm{v}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}=0,
which gives

\bm{v}(\bm{x})=\bm{A}\cdot\bm{x}+\bm{b},\quad\text{for a.e.}\quad\bm{x}\in\Omega,

(2.10)

for some constant skew-symmetric matrix
\displaystyle\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and some vector
\displaystyle\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
24
, Proposition 1.2]
or
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
).
We then have by (
2.7
), (
2.8
), and (
2.10
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle 1=\|\bm{v}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{A}\cdot\bm{x}-{\bf b}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\|\bm{v}_{k}-\bm{v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\to 0,

as
\displaystyle k\to\infty,
which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.2

We remark that if, for a given

\displaystyle{\bf u}\in\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(Q(\bm{x}_{0},\tau))

, and
\displaystyle s

The following variant of the fractional Hardy-type
inequality is key for proving the boundedness of the extension operator we will define in the next section. For notational convenience, we represent points
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\displaystyle\bm{x}=(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}

Definition 2.3

(Epigraph)
.

Let
\displaystyle f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function.
The set

D=\{(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})\ :\ \bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ x_{n}>f(\bm{x}^{\prime})\}

is called an epigraph supported by the function
\displaystyle f
.
In that case we also denote

D_{-}=\{(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})\ :\ \bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ x_{n}<f(\bm{x}^{\prime})\}.

In what follows,
\displaystyle f
will be a globally Lipschitz function with
\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq M.
Also, capital letter
\displaystyle C
will denote a constant that depends on
\displaystyle n,p,s
and
\displaystyle M,
while small letter
\displaystyle c
will denote a constant that depends only on
\displaystyle n,p
and
\displaystyle s.
For any epigraph
\displaystyle D
and any
\displaystyle\eta>0
, define the mapping
\displaystyle\Phi_{\eta}:{D_{-}}\to D
given by

\Phi_{\eta}(\bm{x})=(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\eta(f(\bm{x}^{\prime})-x_{n})),

(2.12)

which is clearly a Lipschitz diffeomorphism with the inverse

(\Phi_{\eta})^{-1}(\bm{x})=(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{\eta}(f(\bm{x}^{\prime})-x_{n})),

and
\displaystyle\text{det}(\nabla\Phi_{\eta})=-\eta
.
By direct calculation we get

\|\nabla\Phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{-})}=\sqrt{n-1+\eta^{2}+(1+\eta)^{2}{\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}}},

(2.13)

and

\|\nabla(\Phi_{\eta})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}=\sqrt{n-1+\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}+(1+\frac{1}{\eta})^{2}{\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}}}.

(2.14)

Hence, in space dimensions
\displaystyle n\geq 2,
the norms
\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi_{\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{-})}
and

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\Phi_{\eta})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}

are bounded from below by one (independent of the Lipschitz constant of
\displaystyle f
). Moreover, as proved in
Lemma
A.1
, there exists a constant
\displaystyle C>0,

depending only on
\displaystyle n,\eta,
and
\displaystyle M
such that

|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|\leq C|(\Phi_{\eta})^{-1}(\bm{x})-\bm{y}|\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{x},\bm{y}\in D.

(2.15)

Lemma
2.4

(Hardy-type inequality)
.

Let
\displaystyle f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle\leq M,
and let
\displaystyle D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the epigraph supported by
\displaystyle f.
There exist a constant
\displaystyle C=C(n,p,s,M)>0,
and a constant
\displaystyle c_{2}(n)\geq 1
(coming from the Whitney cover of
\displaystyle D
), such that for every
\displaystyle\lambda,\mu\in[1-\delta,1+\delta]
with
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{2c_{2}(n)\sqrt{n}(2+M)}
and every vector field
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)
one has

\int_{D}\frac{|u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\lambda(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})))-u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\mu(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})))|^{p}}{|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{ps}}d\bm{x}\leq C[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}^{p}.

(2.16)

Proof.

Given the epigraph
\displaystyle D
supported by
\displaystyle f
as in the assumption of the lemma,
we consider the sequence of cubes
\displaystyle\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the property that

(i)

\displaystyle D=\cup_{k}Q_{k}
, and the
\displaystyle Q_{k}
are mutually disjoint,

(ii)

the doubled cubes
\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{k}=2\cdot Q_{k}
satisfy the inclusion
\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{k}\subset D
for all
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N},
and that they
have the finite intersection property

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\chi_{\hat{Q}_{k}}(\bm{x})\leq c_{1}(n)\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{x}\in D,\quad\text{and }

(iii)

there exists a constant
\displaystyle c_{2}(n)
such that each of the
\displaystyle c_{2}(n)-
times enlarged cube
\displaystyle c_{2}(n)\cdot Q_{k}
intersects with the graph of
\displaystyle f.

Such a covering of the open set
\displaystyle D
is called a Whitney cover. Given an open set, it is always possible to construct a Whitney cover for it, see
[
23
, Chapter VI, Theorem 1]
for details.
In the above, the constants
\displaystyle c_{1}(n)
and
\displaystyle c_{2}(n)

depend only on the space dimension
\displaystyle n.
Let now
\displaystyle a_{k}>0
be the side length of
\displaystyle Q_{k}.
Observe that on one hand condition (i) in particular implies that

|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|\geq\frac{a_{k}}{2},\quad\text{for all}\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\ \bm{x}\in Q_{k}.

(2.17)

On the other hand for a fixed point
\displaystyle\bm{x}=(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})\in Q_{k},
let
\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\bm{x},\mathrm{Gr}(f))=|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|,

where
\displaystyle\bm{y}=(\bm{y}^{\prime},f(\bm{y}^{\prime}))\in\mathrm{Gr}(f)
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}(f)
is the graph of
\displaystyle f.
We have that

\displaystyle|f(\bm{x}^{\prime})-f(\bm{y}^{\prime})|\leq M|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|
,
thus we can estimate

\begin{split}|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|&=|(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})-(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))|\\
&\leq|(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})-(\bm{y}^{\prime},f(\bm{y}^{\prime}))|+|(\bm{y}^{\prime},f(\bm{y}^{\prime}))-(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))|\\
&=\mathrm{dist}(\bm{x},\mathrm{Gr}(f))+|(\bm{y}^{\prime},f(\bm{y}^{\prime}))-(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))|\\
&\leq\mathrm{dist}(\bm{x},\mathrm{Gr}(f))+\sqrt{1+M^{2}}|\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|\\
&\leq(1+\sqrt{1+M^{2}})\,\mathrm{dist}(\bm{x},\mathrm{Gr}(f))\,.\end{split}

From condition (iii) we have
\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\bm{x},\mathrm{Gr}(f))\leq\frac{c_{2}(n)+1}{2}\sqrt{n}a_{k}\leq c_{2}(n)\sqrt{n}a_{k}
, hence

|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|\leq c_{2}(n)\sqrt{n}(2+M)a_{k}\quad\text{for all}\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\ \bm{x}\in Q_{k}.

(2.18)

By the definition of
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D),
it suffices to take
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in C^{1}_{c}(\overline{D},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
We can then estimate

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{D}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{|u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\lambda(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})))-u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\mu(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})))|^{p}}{|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{ps}}d\bm{x}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{Q_{k}}\frac{|u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\lambda(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})))-u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\mu(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})))|^{p}}{|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{ps}}d\bm{x}.

Setting

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x})=(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\lambda(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})))
for brevity, we aim to prove the inequality

\int_{Q_{k}}\frac{|u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))|^{p}}{|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{ps}}d\bm{x}\leq C[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\hat{Q}_{k})}^{p},

(2.19)

for each cube
\displaystyle Q_{k}.
For every fixed
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
we have by (
2.17
), that

\int_{Q_{k}}\frac{|u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))|^{p}}{|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{ps}}d\bm{x}\leq{2^{ps}}a_{k}^{-ps}\int_{Q_{k}}|u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))|^{p}d\bm{x}.

(2.20)

Next we apply the Korn-Poincaré inequality (
2.11
) to the cube

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{k}
and the vector field
\displaystyle\bm{u}.
Hence, there exists a constant
\displaystyle C>0,
a skew-symmetric matrix
\displaystyle\bm{A}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},
and a vector
\displaystyle\bm{b}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\|\bm{u}(\cdot)-\bm{A}_{k}(\cdot)-\bm{b}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\hat{Q}_{k})}\leq Ca_{k}^{s}[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\hat{Q}_{k})}.

(2.21)

Observe that
\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}\colon Q_{k}\to\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(Q_{k})
is a one-to-one diffeomorphism with the inverse
\displaystyle\Phi_{1/\lambda}^{\ast}
and has Jacobian equal to
\displaystyle\lambda\leq 1+\delta.
Also, due to the inequality (
2.18
), we have that, for every

\displaystyle\bm{x}\in Q_{k}
and every
\displaystyle\lambda\in[1-\delta,1+\delta],

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x})-\bm{x}|=|1-\lambda||x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|\leq\delta c_{2}(n)\sqrt{n}(2+M)a_{k}\leq\frac{a_{k}}{2},

provided
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{2c_{2}(n)\sqrt{n}(2+M)}.
This implies the
inclusion conditions
\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(Q_{k}),\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(Q_{k})\subset\hat{Q}_{k}.
Consequently, noting that by skew-symmetry

\displaystyle(\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x})+\bm{b}_{k})_{n}-(\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x})+\bm{b}_{k})_{n}=0
, and using the bound in (
2.21
), we can estimate that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{Q_{k}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle|u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))|^{p}d\bm{x}

(2.22)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{k}}|u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-(\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x})+\bm{b}_{k})_{n}-(u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-(\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x})+\bm{b}_{k})_{n})|^{p}d\bm{x}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{Q_{k}}|u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-(\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x})+\bm{b}_{k})_{n}|^{p}d\bm{x}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2^{p-1}\int_{Q_{k}}|u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-(\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x})+\bm{b}_{k})_{n}|^{p}d\bm{x}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\|\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\bm{A}_{k}\cdot\bm{y}-\bm{b}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\hat{Q}_{k})}^{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq Ca_{k}^{ps}[\bm{u}(\bm{y})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\hat{Q}_{k})}^{p}.

Putting together now (
2.20
) and (
2.22
) we discover

\int_{Q_{k}}\frac{|u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))|^{p}}{|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{ps}}d\bm{x}\leq C[\bm{u}(\bm{y})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\hat{Q}_{k})}^{p}.

In order to complete the proof of the lemma, one needs to sum (
2.19
) over
\displaystyle k
and keep in mind the finite intersection property in (ii). This completes the proof of Lemma 2.4.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In the special case of the half space, where
\displaystyle D=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
\displaystyle f\equiv 0
, inequality (
2.16
) reduces to

\int_{D}\frac{|u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\lambda x_{n})-u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\mu x_{n})|^{p}}{|x_{n}|^{ps}}d\bm{x}\leq C[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}^{p}.

This inequality was proved in
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
for vector fields in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}_{0}(D)
for particular values of
\displaystyle\lambda
and
\displaystyle\mu
under the extra assumption that
\displaystyle ps\neq 1.
It is now clear from Lemma
2.4
that this requirement is not necessary and that the fractional Korn’s inequality proved in
[
12
]
for vector fields in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}_{0}(D)
is also valid for all
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
(even when
\displaystyle ps=1
). A consequence of this is that the Korn inequality proved in
[
15
]
for vector fields defined on bounded domains with smooth boundary will also be true for the full ranges of
\displaystyle s
and
\displaystyle p
.

We note that we refer to the inequality (
2.16
) as a Hardy-type because the inequality captures the optimal decay rate to zero of a
map near the boundary, say in the case when
\displaystyle D=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
\displaystyle(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})\mapsto u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\lambda x_{n})-u_{n}(\bm{x}^{\prime},\mu x_{n})
, which vanishes on the hyperplane
\displaystyle\partial D=\{x_{n}=0\}
, in terms of an appropriate seminorm. See
[
3
,
10
]
for the standard fractional Hardy-type inequalities.

3
Fractional Korn’s inequalities

3.1
Korn’s second inequality over epigraphs

This section is devoted to the fractional Korn’s second inequality for vector fields defined over epigraphs. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem
3.1

(Korn’s second inequality in epigraphs)
.

Given
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
, there exists a universal constant
\displaystyle M_{0}>0
and another constant
\displaystyle C_{0}>0
depending only on
\displaystyle n,p,s
and
\displaystyle M_{0}
with the following property: For any epigraph
\displaystyle D
supported by
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M_{0}
, one has for all
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)
the inequality

|\bm{u}|_{W^{s,p}(D)}\leq C_{0}[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}.

(3.23)

As we described in the introduction, to prove the fractional Korn’s inequality (
3.23
) for an epigraph
\displaystyle D
, we first prove the existence of an extension operator to extend the vector fields in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)
to be in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in such a way that the seminorm of the extended vector fields is controlled by the seminorm over
\displaystyle D
. As in
[
15
]
, we will show that the extension operator that was used in
[
17
]
for the proof of the classical Korn’s inequality will also be useful to prove the fractional case.

Proposition
3.2

(Extension operator)
.

Let
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle 1\leq p<\infty
and let
\displaystyle D
be an epigraph supported by a Lipschitz function
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}=M.
There exists a bounded extension operator
\displaystyle E:W^{s,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}),
a constant
\displaystyle C>0,
depending only on
\displaystyle n,p,s,
and
\displaystyle M,
and a constant
\displaystyle c>0
depending only on
\displaystyle n,p,
and
\displaystyle s,
with the property that for all
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in W^{s,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n})
one has

[E(\bm{u})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}+c(1+M)^{2+\frac{n}{2p}}M|\bm{u}|_{W^{s,p}(D)}.

(3.24)

Proof.

By density of
\displaystyle C_{c}^{1}(\overline{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
9
, Theorem 6.70]
), it suffices to show the inequality for
\displaystyle C^{1}
vector fields. Following the approach in
[
17
]
, we define the extension operator
\displaystyle E
as follows.
For
\displaystyle\bm{u}=(\bm{u}^{\prime},u_{n})\in C_{c}^{1}(\overline{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and for constants
\displaystyle\lambda
,
\displaystyle\mu
,
\displaystyle k
,
\displaystyle\ell
,
\displaystyle m
, and
\displaystyle q
, set

[\mathrm{E}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})]_{i}:=\begin{cases}u_{i}(\bm{x})\,,&\qquad\bm{x}\in D\,,\quad i=1,2,\ldots n-1,n\,,\\
k\,u_{i}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})+\ell\,u_{i}^{\mu}(\bm{x})\,,&\qquad\bm{x}\in D_{-}\,,\quad i=1,2,\ldots n-1\,,\\
m\,u_{n}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})+q\,u_{n}^{\mu}(\bm{x})\,,&\qquad\bm{x}\in D_{-}\,,\end{cases}

(3.25)

where

\begin{split}u_{j}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}):=u_{j}\big(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\lambda(f(\bm{x}^{\prime})-x_{d})\big)=u_{j}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x})),\\
u_{j}^{\mu}(\bm{x}):=u_{j}\big(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\mu(f(\bm{x}^{\prime})-x_{d})\big)\,=u_{j}(\Phi_{\mu}(\bm{x})).\end{split}

(3.26)

We choose constants
\displaystyle\lambda
,
\displaystyle\mu
,
\displaystyle k
,
\displaystyle\ell
,
\displaystyle m
,
\displaystyle q
, such that

\begin{split}\lambda>0\,,\quad\mu>0\,,\qquad k+\ell=1=m+q\,,\qquad\lambda k=-m\,,\quad\mu\ell=-q\,.\end{split}

(3.27)

For
\displaystyle 0\leq\lambda<\mu
these constants are uniquely
defined and are given by

k=\frac{1+\mu}{\mu-\lambda},\quad\ell=-\frac{1+\lambda}{\mu-\lambda},\quad m=-\frac{\lambda(1+\mu)}{\mu-\lambda},\quad q=\frac{\mu(1+\lambda)}{\mu-\lambda}.

(3.28)

Let now
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{1}{2c_{2}(n)\sqrt{n}(1+M)}
be as in Lemma 2.4, and choose

\lambda=1-\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\mu=1+\delta,

(3.29)

where we note that since
\displaystyle\delta\leq 1/2,

\displaystyle\lambda,\mu\in\left[1/2,3/2\right]
, and
\displaystyle M>0
.
Recalling that the boundary
\displaystyle\partial D
is given by the equation
\displaystyle x_{n}=f(\bm{x}^{\prime})
, it is clear that the operator
\displaystyle E
takes continuous map defined on
\displaystyle D
to continuous maps on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, for
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in C_{c}^{1}(\overline{D},\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\displaystyle E(\bm{u})\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This can be shown following calculations similar to the ones that will be used below estimating
\displaystyle[E(\bm{u})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
to demonstrate the inequality (
3.24
). We split the domain of integration and write

\displaystyle\displaystyle[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=[\bm{u}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2\int_{D_{-}}\int_{D}\frac{\left|(E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})-E(\bm{u})(\bm{y}))\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\right|^{p}}{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|^{n+(s+1)p}}d\bm{y}d\bm{x}.

(3.30)

We need to estimate the second and the third terms. For
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D_{-}
, we write

E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})=E_{\lambda}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})+E_{\mu}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})

where
\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})=(k(\bm{u}^{\prime})^{\lambda},m\,u^{\lambda}_{n})
and
\displaystyle E_{\mu}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})=(\ell(\bm{u}^{\prime})^{\mu},q\,u^{\mu}_{n})
. We have

[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}\leq 2^{p-1}([E_{\lambda}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}+[E_{\mu}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}),

and will estimate each of the summands next. To estimate
\displaystyle[E_{\lambda}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}
, we make the change of coordinates
\displaystyle\bm{z}=\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x})
and
\displaystyle\bm{w}=\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{y})
and recall the discussion about the mapping
\displaystyle\Phi
in (
2.12
)–(
2.14
) to write the integral as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\,[E_{\lambda}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{D}\int_{D}\frac{|k(\bm{u}^{\prime}(\bm{z})-\bm{u}^{\prime}(\bm{w}))\cdot(\bm{z}^{\prime}-\bm{w}^{\prime})+m(u_{n}(\bm{z})-u_{n}(\bm{w}))\cdot([(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{z})]_{n}-[(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{w})]_{n})|^{p}}{|(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{z})-(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{w})|^{n+(s+1)p}}d\bm{z}d\bm{w}.

Notice that

[(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{z})]_{n}-[(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{w})]_{n}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}(z_{n}-w_{n})+\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda}(f(\bm{z}^{\prime})-f(\bm{w}^{\prime})),

and

|\bm{z}-\bm{w}|\leq\|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{-})}\left|(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{z})-(\Phi_{\lambda})^{-1}(\bm{w})\right|.

It then follows using the relation
\displaystyle\lambda\,k=-m,
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle[E_{\lambda}(

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}\leq\frac{2^{p-1}k^{p}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}\|^{n+(s+1)p}_{L^{\infty}(D_{-})}\,[{\bm{u}}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{2^{p-1}m^{p}(1+\lambda)^{p}}{\lambda^{2+p}}\|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}\|^{n+(s+1)p}_{L^{\infty}(D_{-})}\int_{D}\int_{D}{\frac{\left|\big(u_{n}(\bm{z})-u_{n}(\bm{w})\big)\cdot\big(f(\bm{z}^{\prime})-f(\bm{w}^{\prime})\big)\right|^{p}}{|\bm{z}-\bm{w}|^{d+(s+1)p}}}d{\bm{w}}d{\bm{z}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{p-1}k^{p}}{\lambda^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}\|^{n+(s+1)p}_{L^{\infty}(D_{-})}\,\left([{\bm{u}}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}+(1+\lambda)^{p}\|\nabla f\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}}|u_{n}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(D)}\right).

A similar estimate as above also holds for
\displaystyle[E_{\mu}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}
where
\displaystyle\lambda,k
are replaced by
\displaystyle\mu
and
\displaystyle\ell
. We now combine the two estimates, keeping in mind (
2.13
), from which we have
\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi_{\lambda}\|^{n+(s+1)p}_{L^{\infty}(D_{-})}\leq C{(1+M)^{n+2p}}
,
and the explicit formulae (
3.28
) and (
3.29
) which imply
\displaystyle k^{p}\leq C(1+M)^{p}
, to obtain the bound, after some calculations, that

[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}\leq C[{\bm{u}}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}+c(1+M)^{\frac{n}{2}+2p}M^{p}|\bm{u}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(D)}.

It remains is to estimate the third term
\displaystyle\int_{D_{-}}\int_{D}\frac{\left|(E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})-E(\bm{u})(\bm{y}))\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\right|^{p}}{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|^{n+(s+1)p}}d\bm{y}d\bm{x}
in (
3.1
). To that end, we denote the integral by
\displaystyle I_{mix}
and for
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D_{-}
write

E({\bm{u}})(\bm{x})=k\bm{u}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x}))+\ell\bm{u}(\Phi_{\mu}(\bm{x}))+(m-k)(\bm{0}^{\prime},u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x})))+(q-\ell)(\bm{0}^{\prime},u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}(\bm{x}))),

It then follows by algebraic calculations and using the relations (
3.27
)
and (
3.28
) between
\displaystyle k,\ell,m
, and
\displaystyle q
that for
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D_{-}
and
\displaystyle\bm{y}\in D
:

\begin{split}&(E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})-E(\bm{u})(\bm{y}))\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\\
&={k\big(\bm{u}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x}))-\bm{u}(\bm{y})\big)\cdot\big(\bm{x}-\bm{y}\big)+\ell\big(\bm{u}(\Phi_{\mu}(\bm{x}))-\bm{u}(\bm{y})\big)\cdot\big(\bm{x}-\bm{y}\big)}\\
&\quad+{(m-k)\big(u_{n}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x}))-u_{n}(\Phi_{\mu}(\bm{x}))\big)\cdot\big(x_{n}-y_{n}\big)}\\

4
Fractional Korn’s inequality for planar polygonal convex domains

As we discussed in the introduction, we conjecture that the smallness of the Lipschitz constant of the boundary of the domain is not necessary for the validity of the Fractional Korn’s inequalities. In this section, we will support this hypothesis by demonstrating the validity of the inequality
in the case of planar polygonal convex domains.
The argument of the proof mimics the strategy we used for smooth domains. We begin by proving the inequality for angular domains. We then cover the boundary of the convex polygonal domain by balls centered on the boundary. The resulting intersecting sets are either wedges (bounded angular domains) or half balls over which we will have the appropriate estimates. Finally, we use a partition of unity argument to obtain the estimates over the convex polygon.
In this section, vectors defined on the planar domains are represented as
\displaystyle\bm{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
.

\displaystyle D
with an angle of span in the interval
\displaystyle(0,\pi)

\displaystyle D
is given by

D=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ :\ 0<x_{1},\ \ \alpha x_{1}<x_{2}\},

(4.1)

for some
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathbb{R.}

Note that
\displaystyle D
is exactly half of the epigraph supported by the function
\displaystyle f(x_{1})=\alpha x_{1}
defined over
\displaystyle(0,\infty)
.
In that case, we set

D_{-}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ :\ 0<x_{1},\ \ x_{2}<\alpha x_{1}\}.

Notice that
\displaystyle\overline{D\cup D_{-}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}\geq 0}=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\geq 0\}.
We begin by demonstrating the existence of an extension operator to prove that vector fields defined over
\displaystyle D
can be extended to
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}>0}
accompanied with an appropriate control of their nonlocal norm. We use the extension operator defined in
[
17
]
for planar angular domains where it is shown to map
\displaystyle W^{1,2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
\displaystyle W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proposition
4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
, and let
\displaystyle D
be given by (
4.1
). Then, there exists a bounded extension operator
\displaystyle E:{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)\to\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}>0})}
such that
\displaystyle E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})=\bm{u}(\bm{x})
for
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D
. Moreover, there exists a constant
\displaystyle C>0
depending only on
\displaystyle p,s,
and
\displaystyle\alpha,
such that for all
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D),

[E(\bm{u})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}>0})}\leq C([\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}+\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(D)}).

(4.2)

Proof.

As before, it suffices to prove the inequality for
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in C^{1}_{c}(\overline{D};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Following
[
17
]
, we set
\displaystyle E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})=\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})+\tilde{E}_{\mu}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})
, for
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D_{-}
, where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(ku_{1}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})+\alpha k(1+\lambda)u_{2}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}),mu_{2}^{\lambda}(\bm{x}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\mu}(\bm{u})(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(\ell u_{1}^{\mu}(\bm{x})-\alpha k(1+\lambda)u_{2}^{\mu}(\bm{x}),qu_{2}^{\mu}(\bm{x})),

(4.3)

and
\displaystyle E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})=\bm{u}(\bm{x})
, if
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D
.
The constants
\displaystyle\lambda
,
\displaystyle\mu
,
\displaystyle k
,
\displaystyle\ell
,
\displaystyle m
,
\displaystyle q
, satisfy the constraints (
3.27
)-(
3.29
), and the functions
\displaystyle u_{i}^{\lambda}
and
\displaystyle u_{i}^{\mu}
are defined as before in (
3.26
). Note that this is the extension for epigraphs with the additional summand
\displaystyle\alpha k(1+\lambda)(u_{2}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})-u_{2}^{\mu}(\bm{x}))
in the 1st component of

\displaystyle E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})
for
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D_{-}
. The proof of the estimate in (
4.2
) follows the calculations done for the case of the epigraphs. Below we sketch the proof only including those calculations that are new. As before, we begin by decomposing the integral as

\displaystyle\displaystyle[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}>0})}=[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2\int_{D_{-}}\int_{D}\frac{\left|(E(\bm{u})(\bm{x})-E(\bm{u})(\bm{y}))\cdot(\bm{x}-\bm{y})\right|^{p}}{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|^{n+(s+1)p}}d\bm{y}d\bm{x}.

(4.4)

We need to estimate the last two terms. Clearly,

[E(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}\leq 2^{p-1}\left([\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}+[\tilde{E}_{\mu}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}\right).

A simple calculation reveals that the additional summands
\displaystyle\alpha k(1+\lambda)u_{2}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})
and
\displaystyle\alpha k(1+\lambda)u_{2}^{\mu}(\bm{x})
make it possible to simplify further. Indeed, after change of variables
\displaystyle\bm{z}=\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x})=(x_{1},\alpha x_{1}+\lambda(\alpha x_{1}-x_{2}))
and
\displaystyle\bm{w}=(y_{1},\alpha y_{1}+\lambda(\alpha y_{1}-y_{2}))
, we have that

(\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(\bm{u})(\bm{z})-\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(\bm{u})(\bm{w}))\cdot(\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\bm{z})-\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\bm{w}))=k(\bm{u}(\bm{z})-\bm{u}(\bm{w}))\cdot(\bm{z}-\bm{w}),

and hence

[\tilde{E}_{\lambda}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}\leq C(\alpha,\lambda,k,p,s)[\bm{u}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}.

Similar estimates also holds for
\displaystyle[\tilde{E}_{\mu}(\bm{u})]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D_{-})}
, after noting that the relations between the parameters in (
3.27
), implies that
\displaystyle\alpha k(1+\lambda)=-\alpha\ell(1+\mu)
. The point here is that
the additional summand
\displaystyle\alpha\,k(1+\lambda)(u_{2}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})-u_{2}^{\mu}(\bm{x}))
in the first component of the extension facilitates a cancellation of the extra term, which is
\displaystyle|\bm{u}|_{W^{s,p}(D)}
multiplied by the Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle\alpha
, that would appear if we otherwise use the extension operator (
3.25
) treating the domain as a Lipschitz domain. This eliminates the need for the Lipschitz constant to be small so as to absorb the term involving
\displaystyle|\bm{u}|_{W^{s,p}(D)}
. What is left now is estimating the mixed integral
\displaystyle\int_{D_{-}}\int_{D}\dots d\bm{y}d\bm{x}
appearing in (
4.1
). This can be estimated as in the proof of Proposition
3.2
. The only difference is that there will be an additional term due to the new term

\displaystyle\alpha k(1+\lambda)(u_{2}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})-u_{2}^{\mu}(\bm{x}))
. This amounts to estimating the expression

\mathrm{II_{new}}=\int_{D_{-}}\int_{D}\frac{|(u_{2}^{\lambda}(\bm{x})-u_{2}^{\mu}(\bm{x}))\cdot(x_{1}-y_{1})|^{p}}{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|^{2+p+ps}}d\bm{y}d\bm{x}

(4.5)

in terms of the norm of
\displaystyle{\bm{u}}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)
.
To prove (
4.5
), by the change of variable
\displaystyle\bm{z}=\Phi_{1}(\bm{x}),
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{II_{new}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=C\int_{D}\int_{D}\frac{|(u_{2}^{\lambda}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z}))-u_{2}^{\mu}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z})))\cdot(z_{1}-y_{1})|^{p}}{|\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z})-\bm{y}|^{2+p+ps}}d\bm{y}d\bm{z}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\int_{D}I(\bm{z})|u_{2}^{\lambda}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z}))-u_{2}^{\mu}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z}))|^{p}d\bm{z},

where for any fixed
\displaystyle\bm{z}\in D
we have set

I(\bm{z})=\int_{D}\frac{|z_{1}-y_{1}|^{p}}{|\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z})-\bm{y}|^{2+p+ps}}d\bm{y}.

Using Lemma
A.2
from the appendix we have that for each
\displaystyle\bm{z}\in D,

I(\bm{z})\leq\frac{C}{|z_{2}-f(z_{1})|^{ps}},

with a constant
\displaystyle C
that depends only on
\displaystyle p,s,
and the Lipschitz constant of
\displaystyle f(z_{1})=\alpha z_{1}
, which is
\displaystyle\alpha
in this case.
As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{II_{new}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\int_{D}\frac{|u_{2}^{\lambda}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z}))-u_{2}^{\mu}(\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{z}))|^{p}}{|z_{2}-f(z_{1})|^{ps}}d\bm{z}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=C\int_{D}\frac{|u_{2}(z_{1},f(z_{1})+\lambda(z_{2}-f(z_{1})))-u_{2}(z_{1},f(z_{1})+\mu(z_{2}-f(z_{1})))|^{p}}{|z_{2}-f(z_{1})|^{ps}}d\bm{z}.

In order to finish the proof we need to estimate the expression in (
4.6
) by the seminorm
\displaystyle[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}.
This would be straightforward by Lemma
2.4
, if
\displaystyle D
was an epigraph (but
\displaystyle D
is just part of an epigraph). We demonstrate below how the proof of Lemma
2.4
can be adjusted to this situation. To that end, we need to provide an appropriate Whitney-type cover of
\displaystyle D
. Let

\displaystyle F=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ :\ x_{1},x_{2}>0\}
be the first quadrant in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}.
We cover
\displaystyle F
by horizontal rows of identical dyadic cubes as follows: Cover the strip
\displaystyle F\cap\{2^{k}\leq x_{2}\leq 2^{k+1}\}
by closed cubes,
\displaystyle Q_{k}
, of side length
\displaystyle 2^{k},
for every
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{Z}
starting from the
\displaystyle x_{2}-
axis. The resulting cover is exactly the restriction of the Whitney cover of the upper half-space on the first quadrant. Notice here that,
\displaystyle Q_{k}
is
\displaystyle 2^{k}
distant away from the
\displaystyle x_{1}
-axis, and the doubled cubes
\displaystyle\hat{Q}^{+}_{k}
in the direction of the positive axes have a finite intersection property. Now, the domain
\displaystyle D
is the image of
\displaystyle F
under the bi-Lipschitz mapping

\varphi:F\to D\text{ defined by }\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2}{+}\alpha x_{1}).

Each of the dyadic cubes
\displaystyle Q_{k}
(from the covering of
\displaystyle F
) will get mapped to a parallelogram
\displaystyle P_{k}
which will constitute a Whitney-type cover of
\displaystyle D,
by a sequence of dyadic parallelograms. It is not difficult to see that
\displaystyle P_{k}
is a translation of
\displaystyle 2^{k}
times the base parallelogram
\displaystyle\bar{P}_{0}
determined by the points
\displaystyle(0,0),

\displaystyle(0,1)
,
\displaystyle(1,\alpha),
and
\displaystyle(1,1+\alpha)
. This construction gives rise to a perfect cover of
\displaystyle D,
as the parallelograms are essentially disjoint. Moreover, for any
\displaystyle k
, the height of parallelogram
\displaystyle P_{k}
is comparable to its distant away from the line
\displaystyle x_{2}=\alpha x_{1}=f(x_{1})
, and the finite intersection property of enlarged cubes of the initial Whitney cover will also persist under the mapping
\displaystyle\varphi
. We denote the image of the doubled cubes
\displaystyle{\hat{Q}}^{+}_{k}
by
\displaystyle{\hat{P}}^{+}_{k}
. That is,
\displaystyle{\hat{P}}^{+}_{k}=\varphi({\hat{Q}}^{+}_{k})
and, from the construction, these are just translations of
\displaystyle 2^{k}
times
\displaystyle\bar{P}^{+}_{0}
, which is the paralellogram determined by the points
\displaystyle(0,0)
,
\displaystyle(0,2)
,
\displaystyle(2,2\alpha)
,
\displaystyle(2,2+2\alpha)
. With this at hand, we can now repeat the argument in the proof of Lemma
2.4
. Since the argument is almost the same for this construction, we only demonstrate
the analogue of the inequality (
2.22
). To that end, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{P_{k}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle|u_{2}(\underbrace{z_{1},f(z_{1})+\lambda(z_{2}-f(z_{1}))}_{=\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{z})})-u_{2}(\underbrace{z_{1},f(z_{1})+\mu(z_{2}-f(z_{1}))}_{=\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{z})})|^{p}d\bm{z}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{P_{k}}|u_{2}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{z}))-(\tilde{\bm{A}}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{z})+\bm{b}_{k})_{2}|^{p}d\bm{z}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2^{p-1}\int_{P_{k}}|u_{2}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{z}))-(\tilde{\bm{A}}_{k}\cdot\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{z})+\bm{b}_{k})_{2}|^{p}d\bm{z}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\|\bm{u}(\bm{y})-\tilde{\bm{A}}_{k}\cdot\bm{y}-\bm{b}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\hat{P}^{+}_{k})}^{p}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\tilde{a}_{k}^{ps}[\bm{u}(\bm{y})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\hat{P}^{+}_{k})}^{p},

where
\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{k}
is the height of
\displaystyle P_{k}
and, as before, we can show that for appropriately chosen
\displaystyle\lambda
and
\displaystyle\mu
, depending on
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle n
,
\displaystyle\Phi^{*}_{\lambda}(P_{k}),\Phi^{*}_{\mu}(P_{k})\subset{\hat{P}}^{+}_{k}
. Notice that the choice of the infinitesimal rigid displacement
\displaystyle\bm{x}\mapsto\tilde{\bm{A}}_{k}\cdot\bm{x}+\bm{b}_{k}
as well as the last inequality follow from a version of Poincaré-Korn inequality over the parallelogram
\displaystyle\hat{P}^{+}_{k}
(see Remark

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{0}
is 1, then by a simple scaling we have that for any
\displaystyle\tau>0,

\min_{\bm{r}\in\mathcal{R}}\|\bm{u}-\bm{r}\|_{L^{p}(\tau\bar{P}_{0})}\leq C\tau^{s}[\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\tau\bar{P}_{0})},

where
\displaystyle C
independent of
\displaystyle\tau.

Putting together the analogue of (
2.20
) and (
4.7
) we obtain that

\int_{P_{k}}\frac{|u_{2}(\Phi_{\lambda}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))-u_{2}(\Phi_{\mu}^{\ast}(\bm{x}))|^{p}}{|x_{2}-f(x_{1})|^{ps}}d\bm{x}\leq C[\bm{u}(\bm{y})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\hat{P}_{k})}^{p}.

The rest is similar to the proof of Lemma

.

to
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}>0},\mathbb{R}^{2})
. The proposition also implies the fractional Korn’s second inequality for planar angular domains.

\displaystyle\bm{u}\in C^{1}_{c}(\overline{D},\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then by Proposition
4.1
, we can extend
\displaystyle\bm{u}
to
\displaystyle E(\bm{u})\in\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}>0})

such that

[E(\bm{u})]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x_{1}>0})}\leq C([\bm{u}]_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(D)}+\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(D)}).

(4.8)

4.2
The case of planar convex polygonal domains

In this subsection, we show that extension of vector fields defined in planar convex polygonal domains
\displaystyle D
to
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}
with controlled
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}}
norm is possible.
We prove the following extension result.

Proposition
4.3
.

Let
\displaystyle n=2
,
\displaystyle s\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
.
Let
\displaystyle\Omega
be a convex polygonal domain, i.e.
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
is a simple closed curve that is piecewise affine, with finitely many vertices with interior angle in
\displaystyle(0,\pi)
.
Then there exists a positive constant
\displaystyle C
, depending only on
\displaystyle s,p
and
\displaystyle\Omega,

such that for all
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, one has

|{\bm{u}}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C([{\bm{u}}]^{p}_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{u}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of inequality (
1.4
). Choose an open set
\displaystyle\Omega_{0}\Subset\Omega
and open balls
\displaystyle B_{r_{j}}(\bm{y}_{j})
, for
\displaystyle j=1,\ldots,N
with centers
\displaystyle\bm{y}_{j}
at the vertices of
\displaystyle\Omega
such that

1.

\displaystyle\Omega=\cup_{j=0}^{N}\Omega_{j}
where
\displaystyle\Omega_{j}=\Omega\cap B_{r_{j}}(\bm{y}_{j})
for
\displaystyle j=1,\ldots,N
and
\displaystyle\bm{y}_{j}\notin B_{r_{i}}(\bm{y}_{i})
if
\displaystyle i\neq j
.

2.

For every
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq N
, define
\displaystyle T_{j}:B_{r_{j}}(\bm{y}_{j})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
to be the operator consisting of the translation
\displaystyle\bm{y}_{j}\to 0
and a rotation such that
\displaystyle T_{j}(\partial\Omega\cap B_{r_{j}}(\bm{y}_{j}))
coincides with part of an angular planar domain
\displaystyle\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,0<x_{1},\;\alpha_{j}x_{1}<x_{2}\}
, for
\displaystyle\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Set
\displaystyle Q_{j}=T_{j}(B_{r_{j}}(\bm{y}_{j})),
and also define

\begin{split}Q_{j}^{+}:=\{\bm{x}\in Q_{j}\,:\,0<x_{1},\;\alpha_{j}x_{1}<x_{2}\}\,,&\qquad Q_{j}^{-}:=\{\bm{x}\in Q_{j}\,:\,0<x_{1},\;\alpha_{j}x_{1}>x_{2}\}\,,\\
K_{j}^{+}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,\,0<x_{1},\;\alpha_{j}x_{1}<x_{2}\}\,,&\qquad K_{j}^{-}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,0<x_{1},\;\alpha_{j}x_{1}>x_{2}\}\,.\end{split}

We may choose the map
\displaystyle T_{j}
so that
\displaystyle T_{j}(\Omega_{j})=Q_{j}^{+}
. Note that
\displaystyle T_{j}
is a bi-Lipschitz map with Lipschitz constant depending only on
\displaystyle\Omega
. Let
\displaystyle\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
be a
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
partition of unity subordinate to the collection
\displaystyle\{\Omega_{0}\}\cup\{B_{r_{j}}(\bm{y}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{N}
. Then for every
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq N,
we have
\displaystyle\text{supp}(\varphi_{j})\subset B_{r_{j}}(\bm{y}_{j}),

\displaystyle\text{dist}(\bm{y},\text{supp}(\varphi_{j}))\geq\beta_{j}>0
for every
\displaystyle\bm{y}\in\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{j}}
. We also have
\displaystyle\text{supp}(\varphi_{0})\subset\Omega_{0}
,
\displaystyle\text{dist}(\bm{y},\text{supp}(\varphi_{0}))\geq\beta_{0}>0
for every

\displaystyle\bm{y}\in\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{0}},
and
\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{N}\varphi_{j}\equiv 1
on
\displaystyle\Omega
.

Suppose now
\displaystyle\bm{u}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Define
\displaystyle\bm{u}_{j}:=\varphi_{j}\bm{u}
, for
\displaystyle j=0,1,\dots,N.
Following the exact procedure in the proof of Theorem
1.3
we show that

|\bm{u}_{0}|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}

(4.9)

and

|\bm{u}_{j}|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)},\qquad j=1,2,\dots,N,

(4.10)

where
\displaystyle C
is a positive constant that depends on
\displaystyle s,p,

\displaystyle\text{diam}(\Omega)
,
and the partition of unity. Therefore by (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we have

[\bm{u}]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{N}\bm{u}_{j}\Big]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}[\bm{u}_{j}]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{s,p}(\Omega)}.

The estimate for vector fields
\displaystyle\bm{u}
in
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
follows by density. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Some technical lemmas

The following estimate is used in the proof of boundedness of the extension operator in cylindrical epigraphs. The lemma originally appeared in
[
15
]
with the restriction that the base function
\displaystyle f
has a small Lipschitz constant. We prove the lemma without any restriction on
\displaystyle f
here.

Lemma
A.1
.

Let
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle M.
Let
\displaystyle D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an epigraph supported by
\displaystyle f
.
For
\displaystyle\lambda>0
let
\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x})\colon D_{-}\to D
be as in (
2.12
). Then one has

|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|\leq C|\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\bm{x})-\bm{y}|\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{x},\bm{y}\in D,

for some constant
\displaystyle C=C(\lambda,M).

Proof.

We have
\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\bm{x})=(\bm{x}^{\prime},f(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\frac{1}{\lambda}(f(\bm{x}^{\prime})-x_{n})),
hence we can calculate for any
\displaystyle\bm{x},\bm{y}\in D_{-}:

|\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\bm{x})-\bm{y}|^{2}=|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|^{2}+\left|\frac{1}{\lambda}x_{n}+y_{n}-(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})f(\bm{x}^{\prime})\right|^{2}.

In the case
\displaystyle x_{n}\leq y_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}x_{n}+y_{n}-(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})f(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(y_{n}-x_{n})+(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq y_{n}-x_{n}\geq 0

thus we get

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\bm{x})-\bm{y}|\geq|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|.

Assume in the sequel
\displaystyle x_{n}>y_{n}.
Let
\displaystyle\epsilon=\epsilon(M,\lambda)\in(0,1/2]
be a small constant yet to be chosen. If
\displaystyle|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|,
then we are done.
Assuming further
\displaystyle|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|<\epsilon|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|,
we have

|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|<\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}}|x_{n}-y_{n}|<2\epsilon|x_{n}-y_{n}|.

We can then calculate again

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}x_{n}+y_{n}-(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})f(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}(x_{n}-y_{n})+(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})(y_{n}-f(\bm{y}^{\prime}))+(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})(f(\bm{y}^{\prime})-f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}(x_{n}-y_{n})-M(1+\frac{1}{\lambda})|\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\epsilon M(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}))(x_{n}-y_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\lambda}(x_{n}-y_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq 0,

if we choose
\displaystyle\epsilon=\mathrm{min}(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2M(1+\lambda)}).
The proof is now complete.
∎

The following estimate is used in the proof of the existence of a bounded extension operator on planar angular domains.

Lemma
A.2
.

Let

\displaystyle f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
\displaystyle M.
Let
\displaystyle D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an epigraph supported by
\displaystyle f
. For the map
\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(\bm{x}^{\prime},x_{n})=(\bm{x}^{\prime},(1+\lambda)f(\bm{x}^{\prime})-\lambda x_{n})\colon D_{-}\to D
with
\displaystyle\lambda>0
, there exists a constant
\displaystyle C,
depending only on
\displaystyle n,p,s,\lambda
and
\displaystyle M,
such that

I(\bm{x})=\int_{D}\frac{{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|^{p}}}{|\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\bm{x})-\bm{y}|^{n+p+ps}}d\bm{y}\leq\frac{C}{|x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{ps}}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{x}\in D.

Proof.

For simplicity we will present a proof for
\displaystyle\lambda=1,
the general case being completely similar. We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{D}\frac{{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|^{p}}}{|\Phi_{1}^{-1}(\bm{x})-\bm{y}|^{n+p+ps}}d\bm{y}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{D}\frac{{(|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|^{2}+|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2})^{p/2}}}{(|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|^{2}+|y_{n}+x_{n}-2f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{2})^{(n+p+ps)/2}}d\bm{y}.

For
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
yet to be chosen, and for any
\displaystyle\bm{x}\in D
define the complementary subsets of
\displaystyle D
as follows:

E_{1}^{\epsilon}(\bm{x})=\{\bm{y}\in D\ :\ |\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))\},\quad E_{2}^{\epsilon}(\bm{x})=\{\bm{y}\in D\ :\ |\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|<\epsilon(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))\}.

In what follows, the constant
\displaystyle C
may depend only on
\displaystyle n,p,s,M
and
\displaystyle\epsilon.
In the case
\displaystyle\bm{y}\in E_{1}^{\epsilon}(\bm{x}),
substitute
\displaystyle a=x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})>0,

\displaystyle\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{x}^{\prime}=a\bm{w}^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle y_{n}+x_{n}-2f(\bm{x}^{\prime})=at,
where
\displaystyle|\bm{w}^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon
and
\displaystyle\bm{w}^{\prime}
belongs to a subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
\displaystyle t
belongs to a subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}.
We can then estimate using the inequality
\displaystyle(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|^{2}+|t-2|^{2})^{1/2}\leq C(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|+|t|+1)
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{E_{1}^{\epsilon}(\bm{x})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{{(|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|^{2}+|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2})^{p/2}}}{(|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|^{2}+|y_{n}+x_{n}-2f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{2})^{(n+p+ps)/2}}d\bm{y}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a^{ps}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\{\bm{w}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ :\ |\bm{w}^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|^{2}+|t-2|^{2})^{p/2}}}{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|^{2}+|t|^{2})^{(n+p+ps)/2}}d\bm{w}^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a^{ps}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\{\bm{w}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ :\ |\bm{w}^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{1}{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|+|t|)^{n+ps}}{+\frac{1}{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|+|t|)^{n+ps+p}}}d\bm{w}^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a^{ps}}\int_{\{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ |\bm{w}|\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{1}{|\bm{w}|^{n+ps}}{+\frac{1}{|\bm{w}|^{n+ps+p}}}d\bm{w}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{C(\epsilon)}{a^{ps}},

Consider now the case
\displaystyle\bm{y}\in E_{2}^{\epsilon}(\bm{x}).
We have in that case

\displaystyle\displaystyle y_{n}+x_{n}-2f(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(y_{n}-f(\bm{y}^{\prime}))+(f(\bm{y}^{\prime})-f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))+x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq-M|\bm{y}^{\prime}-\bm{x}^{\prime}|+x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon M)(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime})).

Thus, if we choose
\displaystyle\epsilon=1/2M
, we will have
\displaystyle y_{n}+x_{n}-2f(\bm{x}^{\prime})\geq(x_{n}-f(\bm{x}^{\prime}))/2.
Consequently, setting
\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}=a\bm{w}^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle y_{n}+x_{n}-2f(\bm{x}^{\prime})=at,
we will have that
\displaystyle|\bm{w}^{\prime}|<\epsilon
and
\displaystyle\bm{w}^{\prime}
belongs to a subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n-1},
while
\displaystyle t
belongs to a subset of
\displaystyle(1/2,\infty).

We can estimate in a similar manner:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{E_{2}^{\epsilon}(\bm{x})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{{(|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|^{2}+|x_{n}-y_{n}|^{2})^{p/2}}}{(|\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{y}^{\prime}|^{2}+|y_{n}+x_{n}-2f(\bm{x}^{\prime})|^{2})^{(n+p+ps)/2}}d\bm{y}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a^{ps}}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\int_{\{\bm{w}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ :\ |\bm{w}^{\prime}|<\epsilon\}}\frac{{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|^{2}+|t-2|^{2})^{p/2}}}{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|^{2}+|t|^{2})^{(n+p+ps)/2}}d\bm{w}^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a^{ps}}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\int_{\{\bm{w}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\ :\ |\bm{w}^{\prime}|<\epsilon\}}\frac{1}{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|+|t|)^{n+ps}}{+\frac{1}{(|\bm{w}^{\prime}|+|t|)^{n+ps+p}}}d\bm{w}^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a^{ps}}\int_{\{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ :\ |\bm{w}|>1/2\}}\frac{1}{|\bm{w}|^{n+ps}}{+\frac{1}{|\bm{w}|^{n+ps+p}}}d\bm{w}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{a^{ps}}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

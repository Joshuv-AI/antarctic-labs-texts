The a a -numbers of non-hyperelliptic curves of genus 3 with cyclic automorphism group of order 6
Ryo Ohashi, Momonari Kudo, Shushi Harashita

Abstract
. In this paper, we study non-hyperelliptic curves of genus 3 3 with cyclic automorphism group of order 6 6 . Over an algebraically closed field K K of characteristic ≠ 2 , 3 \neq 2,3 , such curves are written as plane quartics C r : x 3 ​ z + y 4 + r ​ y 2 ​ z 2 + z 4 = 0 C_{r}:x^{3}z+y^{4}+ry^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}=0 with one parameter r r . As the first main theorem, we show that r ≠ 0 , ± 2 r\neq 0,\pm 2 and give a necessary and sufficient condition with respect to r r and r ′ r^{\prime} such that C r ≅ C r ′ C_{r}\cong C_{r^{\prime}} . By describing the Hasse-Witt matrix of C r C_{r} in terms of a certain Gauss’ hypergeometric series, we obtain the second main theorem, where we determine the possible a a -number of C r C_{r} , and give the exact number of isomorphism classes over K K of such curves attaining the possible maximal a a -number.

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic
p
.
Throughout this paper, by a curve we mean a non-singular projective variety of dimension one.
A curve
C
of genus
g
over
K
is said to be
superspecial
if
J(C)\cong E^{g}
for a supersingular elliptic curve
E
, where
J(C)
denotes the Jacobian variety of
C
.
There are two well-known equivalent conditions for
C
to be superspecial:
One is that the Frobenius
{\rm Frob}_{C}^{\ast}
on the first cohomology group
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
acts as zero
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
, where
{\rm Frob}_{C}
is the absolute Frobenius morphism on
C
, and the other is
that the
a
-number
a(C)
is equal to the genus
g
,
where the
a
-number of
C
is defined by
a(C):={\rm dim}_{K}\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha_{p},J(C))
with the group scheme
\alpha_{p}
obtained as the kernel of the Frobenius on the additive group
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
.
This equivalence can be generalized as follows:
A matrix
H
representing
{\rm Frob}_{C}^{\ast}
with respect to a suitable basis for
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
is called the
Hasse-Witt matrix
of
C
, and one has

a(C)=g-\mathrm{rank}(H)
(see e.g.,
[
4
, § 2.4]
for an exposition of this fact).
Here, the
a
-number is used for the stratification on the space of curves of given genus
g
.
For this context, determining the
a
-number of a given curve is a very important problem in the study of algebraic curves and their moduli spaces.

This paper focuses on the case where
C
is of genus
g=3
and non-hyperelliptic curve. We here recall that Lercier-Ritzenthaler-Rovetta-Sijsling
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
classified such curves
C
over
K
of characteristic
p\geq 5
into
13
types by structures of the automorphism group of
C
(we write
{\rm Aut}(C)
for this group) as finite groups, and they also gave an explicit defining equation of
C
for each type.
In the following, we denote by
{\rm Z}_{n}
(resp.
{\rm D}_{2n}
) the cyclic (resp. dihedral) group of order
n
(resp.
2n
) and denote by
{\rm S}_{n}
the symmetric group of degree
n
.
Among these 13 types, the families of dimension
1
are the following three types:

{\rm Aut}(C)\cong{\rm Z}_{6},\,{\rm Z}_{4}\times{\rm D}_{4}
or
{\rm S}_{4}
.
Of these, it is known (cf.
[
10
, Sections 4-5]
) that the Jacobian varieties of the latter two curves are
(2,2,2)
-isogenous to the product of three elliptic curves
E_{i}
, and hence the
a
-number of
C
is completely described in terms of those of
E_{i}
(therefore we do not treat the two cases in this paper).

From the above reason, in this paper we study non-hyperelliptic curves
C
of genus
3
with cyclic automorphism group
{\rm Z}_{6}
.
According to
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
, an explicit defining equation of such a curve is given as

C_{r}:x^{3}z+y^{4}+ry^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}=0\quad{\rm for}\ r\in K,

and
r^{2}
is a complete invariant of the curve (over any algebraically closed field), as shown in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
. In Section
2.1
of this paper,
we study such a description of these curves for the reader’s convenience with a different argument from
[
7
]

and we find a condition on
r
so that
C_{r}
is nonsingular and the automorphism group of
C_{r}

is isomorphic to
{\rm Z}_{6}
.

Theorem A
.

Assume that the characteristic of
K
is not
2
nor
3
.
Any non-hyperelliptic curve of genus
3
with automorphism group containing a cyclic group of order
6
is isomorphic to
C_{r}
for an
r\neq\pm 2
, and
the automorphism group of
C_{r}
for
r\neq\pm 2
is a cyclic group of order
6

if and only if
r\neq 0
.
For
r,r^{\prime}\notin\{0,\pm 2\}
, we have
C_{r}\cong C_{r^{\prime}}
if and only if
r^{2}={r^{\prime}}^{2}
. Moreover, if
C_{r}
is superspecial, then
r^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

On the other hand, it is known (cf. Remark
2.2.5
below for details) that non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
3
whose automorphism group contains
{\rm Z}_{6}
can be represented as

M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}:y^{6}=(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})^{3}(x-t_{3})^{4}(x-t_{4})^{4},

where all
t_{i}
’s are distinct elements in
K
. In Subsection
2.2
, we give an invariant for isomorphism classes of these curves by using Theorem A.

Li, Mantovan, Pries and Tang
[
8
, Section 6.1]
determined possible Newton polygons of
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
, which implies that the possible
a
-numbers of
C_{r}
are also determined (see the beginning of Section
3
below). As a different way from the prior work, we
compute the Hasse-Witt matrix of
C_{r}
explicitly by using a Gauss’ hypergeometric series, which gives another proof of the result on the possible
a
-numbers of
C_{r}
. Our method has an advantage: We can determine the exact number of isomorphism classes of
C_{r}
attaining the possible maximal
a
-number:

Theorem B
.

We assume that
p\geq 5
. Then, we have the following:

(1)

(cf.
[
8
, Section 6.1]
) The possible
a
-numbers of non-hyperelliptic curves
C_{r}
of genus 3 such that
\operatorname{Aut}(C_{r})\simeq{{\rm Z}}_{6}
are as follows:

(i)

If
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, we have
a(C_{r})=0
or
2
, and

(ii)

If
p\equiv 5\pmod{6}
, we have
a(C_{r})=1
or
3
.

(2)

In both the cases (i) and (ii), the exact number of isomorphism classes of
C_{r}\!
attaining the maximal
a
-number is
\left\lfloor p/12\right\rfloor
.

We remark that the enumeration result in Theorem
B
for
p\equiv 5\pmod{6}
with
a
-number
=3
is found in Brock’s dissertation
[
1
, Theorem 3.15, II(e)]
, where he used the result by Hashimoto
[
5
]
on the class numbers of quaternion unitary groups. Therefore, the new result is that we obtain the number of isomorphism classes of
C_{r}
such that
a(C_{r})=2
when
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: In Subsection
2.1
, we show that non-hyperelliptic curves of genus 3 with cyclic automorphism group
{\rm Z}_{6}
are written as the form
C_{r}
for
r\neq 0,\pm 2
, and we prove Theorem
A
. In Subsection
2.2
, we examine the relationship between
C_{r}
and
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
. At the beginning of Section 3, we review a part of Li-Mantovan-Pries-Tang’s works
[
8
]
briefly.
After that, we compute explicitly the Hasse-Witt matrices of our curves, dividing into two cases depending on whether
p\equiv 5\pmod{6}
or
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
. Finally, we give a concluding remark in Section 4.

2.
Non-hyperelliptic genus-3 curves with cyclic automorphism group of order 6

As described in Introduction, we study non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
3
with cyclic automorphism groups of order
6
in this paper. Let
K
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
or
p\geq 5
.
In this section, we give a complete description of defining equations of such curves. In Subsection
2.1
below, we study an explicit defining equation of our curves as plane quartics, and we prove Theorem A. In Subsection 2.2, we study another defining equation of our curves as Moonen’s 9th special family, and we examine the correspondence between these two forms.

2.1.
Plane quartic with cyclic automorphism group of order
6

We start with considering the plane quartic given in Case (7) of
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
:

(2.1)

C_{r}:x^{3}z+y^{4}+ry^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}=0,\quad{\rm for}\ r\in K.

The automorphism groups of
C_{r}
contains
{\rm Z}_{6}
for all
r
. Indeed, for a primitive third root
\zeta
of unity, the automorphism

\gamma:C_{r}\rightarrow C_{r}\ ;\,(x:y:z)\mapsto(\zeta x:-y:z)

is of order
6
. We need to determine when
C_{r}
is non-singular,
when
\operatorname{Aut}(C_{r})
is isomorphic to
{\rm Z}_{6}

and when
C_{r}
and
C_{r^{\prime}}
for
r,r^{\prime}\in K
are isomorphic.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

The curve
C_{r}
is non-singular if and only if
r\neq\pm 2
.

Proof.

Using the Jacobian criterion, the only possible singular points are
(x:y:z)\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
satisfying the equation
3x^{2}z=2y(2y^{2}+rz^{2})=x^{3}+2z(2z^{2}+ry^{2})=0
. From this equation and (
2.1
), we have
x=0
,
y\neq 0
and
z\neq 0
. Then, it follows from
2y^{2}+rz^{2}=0
and
2z^{2}+ry^{2}=0
that
(4-r^{2})y^{2}=0
and
(4-r^{2})z^{2}=0
. Hence, there exists a solution without
x=y=z=0
if and only if
r=\pm 2
.
∎

From now on, we assume
r\neq\pm 2
.
It follows from

x^{3}z+y^{4}+ry^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}=\Bigl(y^{2}+\frac{r}{2}z^{2}\Bigr)^{\!2}+x^{3}z-\Bigl(\frac{r^{2}}{4}-1\Bigr)z^{4}

that we have a degree-2 morphism

(2.2)

\rho_{r}:C_{r}\to{E_{r}},\quad(x:y:z)\mapsto\biggl(-\frac{x}{z},\,\frac{y^{2}+\frac{r}{2}z^{2}}{z^{2}}\biggr)=:(X,Y),

where
E_{r}
is the elliptic curve defined as

(2.3)

E_{r}:Y^{2}=X^{3}+(r^{2}/4-1).

Note that the
j
-invariant of
E_{r}
is zero.
This morphism
\rho_{r}:C_{r}\to{E_{r}}
is just the quotient of
C_{r}
by the involution
\gamma^{3}
and it is ramified at
infinity
O
and the three points
(\zeta^{i},r/2)
for
i=0,1,2
.
Note that the set of branch points
is stable under the action of the order-3 group
\langle\gamma\rangle/\langle\gamma^{3}\rangle
.

The assertion of the following lemma has been obtained by
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.
For the reader’s convenience, we will review it, which helps the reader understand the structure of a non-hyperelliptic curve of genus
3
having
{\rm Z}_{6}

as a subgroup of its automorphism group.

Lemma 2.1.2
.

Let
C
be a non-hyperelliptic curve of genus
3
such that a subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
is isomorphic to
{\rm Z}_{6}
. Then
C
is isomorphic to
C_{r}

for some
r\neq\pm 2
.

Proof.

Note that any automorphism of
C
extends to an automorphism of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, as
C
is canonical. Any nontrivial involution in
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(K)
has a representative in
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(K)

which is conjugate to
\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1)
since
{\rm char}(K)\neq 2
.
In particular, any involution
\sigma
is identical on a certain hyperplane
H

(and sends a vector
v
to
-v
).
Hence, the quotient
C\to E
by
\langle\sigma\rangle
is ramified at four points (the intersection of quartic
C
and the hyperplane
H
), which are distinct (otherwise
C
is singular).
Then the Hurwitz’s formula says that
E
is of genus one.

Let
\sigma
be the involution in
G
, and let
E
be the quotient
C/\langle\sigma\rangle
.
Now
{\rm Z}_{3}\cong G/\langle\sigma\rangle

acts on
E
non-trivially and moreover the set of the branch points of the quotient morphism
C\to E
is stable under the action of
{\rm Z}_{3}\cong G/\langle\sigma\rangle
.
The action of
{\rm Z}_{3}
on the
4
branch points
has to be non-trivial (otherwise
{\rm Z}_{3}
acts on
E
trivially), and therefore one is a fixed point and the other 3 points make an orbit of
{\rm Z}_{3}
.
Consider
E
as an elliptic curve with
the fixed point as the origin
O
; then

E
is an elliptic curve of
j
-invariant
0
,
say
E:Y^{2}=X^{3}+1
, and

C
is the double cover of
E
ramified at
O
and the three points

(X,Y)=(\zeta^{i}a,b),\quad i=0,1,2

for a certain
(a,b)
satisfying
b^{2}=a^{3}+1
.
We choose
r
so that
a^{3}=4/(r^{2}-4)
, we identify

E
and
E_{r}
as in (
2.3
)
by the isomorphism

E\to E_{r}
sending
(X,Y)
to
(X/a,rY/2b)
.
Replacing
b
by
-b
by using the hyperelliptic involution
(X,Y)\mapsto(X,-Y)
in
\operatorname{Aut}(E_{0})
if necessary,
the two double covers
C\to E_{r}
and
C_{r}\to E_{r}

have the same branch points.
Hence
C
is isomorphic to
C_{r}
.
∎

Let
D
be the genus-1 curve obtained by forgetting the origin from the elliptic curve
Y^{2}=X^{3}+1
with
j
-invariant
0
.
In the proof of Lemma
2.1.2
, to a cyclic subgroup
G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(C)
of order
6

we associate
a degree-2 morphism
C\to D
,
which is the quotient by the order-2 subgroup of
G
.
We call such a morphism of degree 2
up to the action by
\operatorname{Aut}({D})
,
a
{\rm Z}_{6}
-
elliptic quotient of
C
,
where the action of
\operatorname{Aut}({D})

is defined by the composition of
C\to D
and an automorphism
D\to D
.

Lemma 2.1.3
.

Let
C
be a non-hyperelliptic curve of genus
3
such that a subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
is isomorphic to
{\rm Z}_{6}
. Then we have a unique
{\rm Z}_{6}
-elliptic quotient of
C
.

Proof.

We have shown the existence.
The uniqueness follows from the classification of automorphism groups
of non-hyperelliptic curve
C
of genus
3
. The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(C)

(containing
{\rm Z}_{6}
) is isomorphic to
{\rm Z}_{6}
or a group
{\rm G}_{48}
of order
48
(cf.
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
).
By a tedious computation, we can check that the set of involutions in
subgroups
G
with
G\cong{\rm Z}_{6}
:

\{h^{3}\mid h\in\operatorname{Aut}(C),\ \#\langle h\rangle=6\}

is a singleton, see
[
10
, Section 6]
for an explicit description
of
{\rm G}_{48}
.
∎

Let
\alpha\neq 0
be an element of
K
such that
\alpha^{-6}=r^{2}/4-1
.
Let
\iota_{r}
be the isomorphism
E_{r}\simeq D
over
K
sending
(X,Y)\to(\alpha^{2}X,\alpha^{3}Y)
.
We give a necessary and sufficient condition that
{\rm Aut}(C_{r})
is the cyclic group of order
6
:

Proposition 2.1.4
.

Assume that
r\neq\pm 2
. Then
\operatorname{Aut}(C_{r})\cong{\rm Z}_{6}
for
r\neq 0
, and
\operatorname{Aut}(C_{0})\cong{\rm G}_{48}
.

Proof.

Let
\iota_{r}\circ\rho_{r}:C_{r}\to E_{r}\simeq D
be the
{\rm Z}_{6}
-elliptic quotient
as constructed in (
2.2
).
By using Lemma
2.1.3
,
we have a canonical homomorphism
\operatorname{Aut}(C_{r})\to\operatorname{Aut}({D})
.
The kernel is of order
2
, and the image, say
H
, consists of elements of
\operatorname{Aut}({D})
stabilizing the set of the branch points of
\rho_{r}
. The set of branch points considered in
E_{r}
is
B:=\{O,(1,r/2),(\zeta,r/2),(\zeta^{2},r/2)\}
.

Consider the exact sequence

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r})@>{\subset}>{}>\operatorname{Aut}(D)@>{\varphi}>{}>E_{r}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

Here
\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r})
is the automorphism group of
E_{r}
as an elliptic curve,
which is considered as a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(D)
via
\iota_{r}
,
and
\varphi
sends
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(D)
to
\iota_{r}^{-1}\circ g\circ\iota_{r}(O)
.
Since
H
stabilizes the branch points, we have
\varphi(H)\subset B
.
If
r\neq 0
, then
\varphi(H)=\{O\}
, since
\{O\}

is the unique subgroup of
E_{r}
contained in
B
.
If
r=0
, we have
\varphi(H)=B
, since
B
is the
2
-torsion
subgroup of
E_{r}
and any element of
B
can be lifted to an element of
H
.
As the
j
-invariant of
E_{r}
is
0
, we have
\#\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r})=6
.
If
r\neq 0
, then
H\cap\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r})
contains
(X,Y)\mapsto(\zeta^{i}X,Y)
for
i=0,1,2
but does not contain the hyperelliptic involution
(X,Y)\mapsto(X,-Y)
, whence
\#(H\cap\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r}))=3
. If
r=0
,
then any element of
\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r})
stabilizes
B
, whence

\#(H\cap\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r}))=\#\operatorname{Aut}(E_{r})=6
. Thus
the group
H
is isomorphic to
{\rm Z}_{3}
if
r\neq 0
, and is of order
24
if
r=0
.
∎

To obtain Theorem
A
, it remains to show its latter part:

For
r,r^{\prime}\notin\{0,\pm 2\}
, we have
C_{r}\cong C_{r^{\prime}}
if and only if
r^{2}={r^{\prime}}^{2}
.
Moreover, if
C_{r}
is superspecial, then
r^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Proof.

The “if"-part of the first statement is clear: If
r=-r^{\prime}
, then the map
(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y,\sqrt{-1}z)
defines an isomorphism
C_{r}\rightarrow C_{r^{\prime}}
.
Let us show the “only if"-part.
By Lemma
2.1.3
, the isomorphism class of
C_{r}
is determined by the branch points of
\iota_{r}\circ\rho_{r}:C_{r}\to E_{r}\simeq D
, up to
\operatorname{Aut}(D)
. The branch points are
the origin
O
and the three points on
D:Y^{2}=X^{3}+1
defined by

Y:=\alpha^{3}r/2
and
X^{3}:=4/(r^{2}-4)
.
Hence, if
C_{r}\simeq C_{r^{\prime}}
, then
we have
r^{2}={r^{\prime}}^{2}
looking at the third power of the
X
-coordinates of the branch points, since

\operatorname{Aut}(D)
stabilizes
X^{3}
(or equivalently
Y^{2}
).

Next, we assume that
C_{r}
with
r\neq\pm 2
is superspecial. It is known that any superspecial curve is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, and hence there exists a curve
C^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
such that
C_{r}\simeq{C^{\prime}}\otimes_{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-1.99168pt}K
. Let
{C_{r}}^{\!(\sigma)}
be the fiber product
C_{r}\otimes_{K,\sigma}K
, where
\sigma
is the
p^{2}
-power map from
K
to
K
.
Then we have
C_{r}\cong{C_{r}}^{\!(\sigma)}
. On the other hand
{C_{r}}^{\!(\sigma)}\cong C_{\sigma(r)}
holds clearly, hence we obtain
C_{r}\cong C_{\sigma(r)}
. Since
r^{2}
is an invariant of
C_{r}
,
we have
r^{2}=\sigma(r^{2})
. This means that
r^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, so the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 2.1.5
.

The authors learned from a referee that it had been shown in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
that
r^{2}
is a complete invariant of
C_{r}
over an algebraically closed field.

2.2.
Correspondence to Moonen’s 9th special family

We use the same notation as in the previous subsection, and let
r\in K
with
r\neq\pm 2
.
Since
C_{r}
is a
6
-cyclic cover of the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
via the quotient map
C_{r}\to C_{r}/\langle\gamma\rangle\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, it is isomorphic to

M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}:y^{6}=(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})^{3}(x-t_{3})^{4}(x-t_{4})^{4}

for some pairwise distinct elements
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
,
t_{3}
, and
t_{4}
in
K
, see Remark
2.2.5
below where the isomorphism
C_{r}\cong M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
can be proved without constructing any explicit isomorphism.
Conversely, one can check that
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
is a non-hyperelliptic curve of genus
3
whose automorphism group contains
{\rm Z}_{6}
.
The curve
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
is known as
Moonen’s 9th special family

[
11
, Table 1]
(this is the reason why we use the notation ‘
M
’ for the curve).

In the following, we construct an explicit isomorphism between
C_{r}
and
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
, by which we can compute the equation for
C_{r}
isomorphic to a given
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
, and vise versa.
With this isomorphism, we also obtain analogues of Proposition
2.1.4
and Theorem A for
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

The curve
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
is birational to the curve defined by the equation

(2.4)

\widetilde{C}_{s}:Y^{3}=(X^{2}-1)(X^{2}-s)\ \,{\rm with}\ s:=\frac{t_{4}-t_{2}}{t_{4}-t_{1}}\cdot\frac{t_{3}-t_{1}}{t_{3}-t_{2}}.

Note that
s
is an element of
K
different from 0 or 1.

Proof.

First, we claim that the curve
M_{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})}
is isomorphic to the curve defined by

D_{s}:Y^{6}=X^{3}(X-1)^{4}(X-s)^{4}.

Indeed, the transformation

(3.7)

\displaystyle d_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=(5p-1)/6}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{i}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{j}\alpha^{i}\beta^{j},

\displaystyle d_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=(p-5)/6}\binom{(p-2)/3}{i}\binom{(p-2)/3}{j}\alpha^{i}\beta^{j}.

Since
i,j
with
i+j=(5p-1)/6
and
i,j\leq(2p-1)/3
must satisfy
i,j\geq(p+1)/6
, we can rewrite the right hand side of (
3.7
) as that of (
3.5
), as desired.

(3) Let
G^{(d)}(a,b,c\,|\,t)
be the truncation of Gauss’ hypergeometric series
G(a,b,c\,|\,t)
to degree
d
modulo
p
, that is, we define

G^{(d)}(a,b,c\,|\,t):=\sum_{n=0}^{d}g_{n}t^{n},\quad g_{n}:=\frac{(a\,;n)(b\,;n)}{(c\,;n)(1\,;n)}\ \ \text{mod}\ p

where
(x\,;n)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x+j-1)
.
It follows from (
3.3
) of Proposition
3.2.2
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=(p-1)/2}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{i}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{j}t^{i}

\displaystyle=\binom{(2p-1)/3}{(p-1)/2}G^{(p-1)/2}\left(\frac{1-2p}{3},\frac{1-p}{2},\frac{p+7}{6}\ \middle|\ t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\binom{(2p-1)/3}{(p-1)/2}G^{(p-1)/2}(1/3,1/2,7/6\mid t)\pmod{p}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=(p-5)/6}\binom{(p-2)/3}{i}\binom{(p-2)/3}{j}t^{i}

\displaystyle=\binom{(p-2)/3}{(p+1)/6}\left(\frac{2-p}{3},\frac{5-p}{6},\frac{p+7}{6}\ \middle|\ t\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\binom{(p-2)/3}{(p+1)/6}G^{(p-5)/6}(2/3,5/6,7/6\mid t)\pmod{p}.

Substituting
t=\alpha/\beta
and multiplying
\beta^{(p-1)/2}
or
\beta^{(p-5)/6}
of both sides, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=(p-1)/2}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{i}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{j}\alpha^{i}\beta^{j}

\displaystyle\equiv\beta^{(p-1)/2}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{(p-1)/2}G^{(p-1)/2}(1/3,1/2,7/6\mid\alpha/\beta),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=(p-5)/6}\binom{(p-2)/3}{i}\binom{(p-2)/3}{j}\alpha^{i}\beta^{j}

\displaystyle\equiv\beta^{(p-5)/6}\binom{(p-2)/3}{(p+1)/6}G^{(p-5)/6}(2/3,5/6,7/6\mid\alpha/\beta).

Then, it follows from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\binom{(2p-1)/3}{(p-1)/2}\beta^{(p-1)/2}G^{(p-1)/2}(1/3,1/2,7/6\mid\alpha/\beta)\\
&\equiv(\alpha\beta)^{-(p+1)/6}d_{1}(r)\pmod{p}\end{split}

and

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\binom{(p-2)/3}{(p+1)/6}\beta^{(p-5)/6}G^{(p-5)/6}(5/6,2/3,7/6\mid\alpha/\beta)\\
&\equiv d_{2}(r)\pmod{p}.\end{split}

On the other hand, using Euler’s transformation formula, we obtain

(3.10)

G(a,b,c\,|\,t)=(1-t)^{c-a-b}G(c-a,c-b,c\,|\,t).

Setting
a=(1-2p)/3,\,b=(1-p)/2,\,c=(p+7)/6
and
t=\alpha/\beta
, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&G^{(p-1)/2}(1/3,1/2,7/6\mid\alpha/\beta)\\
&\equiv(1-\alpha/\beta)^{(p+1)/3}G^{(p-5)/6}(5/6,2/3,7/6\mid\alpha/\beta)\pmod{p}.\end{split}

We remark that
G(1/3,1/2,7/6\mid t)
modulo
p
does not have any
t^{n}
-term for
(p-1)/2\leq n\leq p-1
since the values
(1/2\,;n)
for all integers
(p-1)/2\leq n\leq p-1
are divided by
p
. Similarly to this, the values
(5/6\,;n)
for all integers
(p-5)/6\leq n\leq p-1
can be divided by
p
, and hence we see that the

-terms of

modulo
p
for

are all vanishing.
Therefore, by multiplying
\binom{(p-2)/3}{(p+1)/6}\binom{(2p-1)/3}{(p+1)/6}(\alpha\beta)^{(p+1)/6}\beta^{(p-1)/2}
to both sides of (
3.11
), we get the equality

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\binom{(p-2)/3}{(p+1)/6}d_{1}(r)\\
&\equiv\binom{(2p-1)/3}{(p+1)/6}(\alpha\beta)^{(p+1)/6}(\beta-\alpha)^{(p+1)/3}d_{2}(r)\pmod{p}\end{split}

by (
3.8
) and (
3.9
).
Hence
d_{1}(r)
is divided by
d_{2}(r)
as symmetric polynomials in
\alpha
and
\beta
.
∎

Thanks to Proposition
3.2.3
, we can compute the

-number of

by determining whether

For this, we prove the following lemma, which collects properties of

Lemma 3.2.4
.

With notation as above, we have the following:

(1)

As a polynomial in
\alpha
and
\beta
, the polynomial

cannot be divided by
\beta-\alpha

(2)

The polynomial

does not have roots

.

(3)

The polynomial

Proof.

(1) If
\alpha=\beta
, then it follows from (

) in Proposition
3.2.3
(2) together with Vandermonde’s convolution theorem that

\displaystyle d_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=(p-5)/6}\binom{(p-2)/3}{i}\binom{(p-2)/3}{j}\beta^{(p-5)/6}

\displaystyle=\beta^{(p-5)/6}\binom{(2p-4)/3}{(p-5)/6}\neq 0.

(2) Assume that
c_{2}(r)
has a root
r=2
(resp.
r=-2
).
Since the curve

and the curve

are clearly isomorphic, then

has another root

(resp.

can be divided by

, and so does

. This contradicts the statement (1).

(3) Using (
3.4
), it suffices to show the separability of

.
Similarly to Igusa’s proof in
[
6
]
for the separability of the Deuring polynomial, we can prove the assertion as follows:
We first claim that

with

is a separable polynomial.
Indeed, it follows from the statement (1) together with (
3.6
) and (
3.9
) that the roots
t
of

are different from
0
and
1
.
Here, by Euler’s hypergeometric differential equation, we have that

satisfies the differential equation
\mathcal{D}G(a,b,c\,|\,t)=0
with

(3.13)

\mathcal{D}:=t(1-t)\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}+\bigl(c-(a+b+1)t\bigr)\frac{d}{dt}-ab.

Assume that
G(t)
has a multiple root

, then we have
G(t_{0})={G}^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
and
{G}^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})=0
by (
3.13
).
Repeating this inductively, we thus have
{G}^{(n)}(t_{0})=0
for all

, where

denotes the

-th derivative of

with respect to

.
This is a contradiction, and so the roots of

are all simple.

To prove the separability of
d_{2}(r)
, assume for contradiction that
d_{2}(r)
has a multiple root

.
Proposition
3.2.3
(1) implies that

and
-r_{0}
is also a multiple root of

. Hence

has a factor of the form
(r-r_{0})^{2}(r+r_{0})^{2}=(r^{2}-r_{0}^{2})^{2}

and

that we obtain

\displaystyle r^{2}-r_{0}^{2}=(r^{2}-4)-(r_{0}^{2}-4)

\displaystyle=(\beta-\alpha)^{2}-(r_{0}^{2}-4)\alpha\beta

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\biggl\{\biggl(1-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\biggr)^{\!\!2}-(r_{0}^{2}-4)\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\biggr\}.

Recall from

that
r^{2}-r_{0}^{2}=\beta^{2}\{(1-t)^{2}-t(r_{0}^{2}-4)\}
, and hence we obtain

(r^{2}-r_{0}^{2})^{2}=\beta^{4}\{t^{2}+(2-r_{0})t+1\}^{2}.

Thus, we have that

is not separable by (

), and hence this is a contradiction.
Therefore, any root

of

is simple, as desired.
∎

Here, we determine when

has a root with

with

(2)(3) as the degree of

4.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we focused on non-hyperelliptic curves
C_{r}
of genus 3 with cyclic automorphism group of order 6, and gave a necessary and sufficient condition with respect to
r
and
r^{\prime}
such that
C_{r}\cong C_{r^{\prime}}
(the first assertion of Theorem A). We found another proof of the possible
a
-numbers of
C_{r}
, and we obtained the exact number of isomorphism classes of
C_{r}
attaining the possible maximal
a
-number (Theorem B). Moreover, we showed that
r^{2}
belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.85358pt
if
C_{r}
is superspecial (the second assertion of Theorem A). More strongly, the following seems to be true:

Expectation
.

If
C_{r}
is superspecial, then
r\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Any superspecial curve is isomorphic (over
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
) to one defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
Hence, the above Expectation implies that
C_{r}
itself provides a model over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, if it is superspecial.

We confirmed that the above Expectation is true for
17\leq p<10000
with help of computer calculation.
More precisely, we checked that any solution
z
of

(4.1)

G^{(p-5)/6}(5/6,2/3,7/6\mid t)=0

is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
for all
17\leq p<10000
with
p\equiv 5\pmod{6}
by using Magma
[
2
]
.
This assertion implies the correctness of Expectation.
Indeed, if
C_{r}
is superspecial, then we have
p\equiv 5\pmod{6}
and
c_{2}(r)=0
holds as in Lemma
3.2.1
. This is equivalent to (
4.1
) setting
t:=\alpha/\beta
,
where
\alpha+\beta=r
and
\alpha\beta=1
.
If
t=\alpha/\beta\in(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.85358pt)^{2}
holds,
then
\alpha/\beta=\alpha^{2}=\beta^{-2}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.85358pt)^{2}

and therefore
r=\alpha+\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Regardless of whether the above Expectation is correct or not, we find an explicit model over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.85358pt
of a superspecial
C_{r}
, by using the second assertion of Theorem A: The curve
C_{r}
is isomorphic (over

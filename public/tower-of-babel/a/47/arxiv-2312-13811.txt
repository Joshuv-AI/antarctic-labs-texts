The left tail of the subcritical derivative martingale in a branching random walk
Benjamin Bonnefont Vincent Vargas

Abstract
Motivated by the study of the quantum Mabuchi theory [ 8 ] , we obtain in this work a sharp estimate on the left tail of the distribution of the so-called derivative martingale in the L 4 L^{4} phase.

Introduction

Branching random walks (BRW) have a long history in probability theory and its applications. Given a real parameter
\beta
(the inverse temperature in the language of statistical physics), one can associate to a BRW a natural random measure called a multiplicative cascade. Multiplicative cascades were introduced by Mandelbrot in
[
10

Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC), a random measure which models energy dissipation in a turbulent flow. The precise mathematical construction of GMC was established by Kahane in the landmark paper

In both aforementionned models, the main object of interest (a random measure) is obtained as the limit of a positive martingale and it is also very natural to inquire on the existence and the properties of the derivatives of these martingales which form yet another sequence of martingales. In the case of GMC theory, these derivatives appear as a crucial ingredient in the construction of the quantum Mabuchi theory
[
8
]
or as so-called logarithmic fields in conformal field theory
[
13

. In this paper, we will work within the simplified framework of the Gaussian BRW as motivated by
[
8
]
, we will give the first sharp estimates on the left tail of the derivative.

We consider the case of a BRW with binary splitting and independent standard Gaussian increments. The process is indexed by the binary tree
\mathbb{T}=\cup_{n\geq 0}\,\{0,1\}^{n}

1.1
Results

We will divide the presentation of our results in two parts. First we give a lower bound on the left tail in the full regime
\beta\in(0,\beta_{c})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\beta\in\left(0,\beta_{c}\right)
, there exist
c
and
C>0
such that for
x\geq 0

\mathbb{P}(Z_{\beta}<-x)\geq C\mathrm{e}^{-cx^{\gamma}}.

Second we give an upper bound on the left tail in the regime
\beta\in(0,\beta_{c}/2)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{\beta_{c}}{2}\right)
, there exist
c^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}>0
such that for
x\geq 0

\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\beta}<-x\right)\leq C^{\prime}\mathrm{e}^{-c^{\prime}x^{\gamma}}.

We will make two remarks on the limiting cases
\beta=0
and
\beta=\beta_{c}
where much more is known and the behaviour of the left tail is quite different.

Remark 1.3
.

In the case
\beta=0
, one can check that
Z_{0}=\lim\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}X_{u}
is a standard Gaussian random variable.

Remark 1.4
.

The case
\beta=\beta_{c}
has been thoroughly studied in the literature. In this case, the variable

is trivial; however

is non trivial, negative almost surely and the left tail follows the power law

\quad\mathbb{P}(Z_{\beta_{c}}<-x)\sim\frac{C}{x},\quad\text{ as }x\rightarrow+\infty,

Lower bound for the left tail: proof of Theorem

This section is devoted to proving Theorem

2.1
Heuristics

A quick inspection of
Z_{n}(\beta)
as a function of
(X_{u})_{|u|=n}
, namely

Z_{n}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}\big(X_{u}-\beta n\big),

reveals that

\text{ess}\inf Z_{n}(\beta)=-\frac{1}{\beta\mathrm{e}}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}=:-m_{n}.

This value is achieved when all the particles are located at
\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}
. This suggests the following scenario to get a lower bound. Put all the particles around
\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}
to minimize
Z_{n}(\beta)
and then show that, on this event, the rest
Z_{\beta}-Z_{n}(\beta)
is negligible using the decomposition of the derivative martingale

Z_{\beta}=Z_{n}(\beta)+R_{n}(\beta),

with

R_{n}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}Z_{\beta}^{u}+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}\big(X_{u}-\beta n\big)\big(W_{\beta}^{u}-1\big)

(2.1)

and
(Z_{\beta}^{u},W_{\beta}^{u})
are independent copies of
(Z_{\beta},W_{\beta})
independent of
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

To see why this works, suppose for instance that we are in the
L^{2}
phase (
\beta<\beta_{c}/\sqrt{2}
). In this case,
Z^{u}
and
W^{u}-1
are centered with finite variance, thus the central limit theorem ensures that
\sum_{|u|=n}Z_{\beta}^{u}
fluctuates as
2^{n/2}
. If the
X_{u}
are all located near
\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}
, we have

\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}Z_{\beta}^{u}\penalty\ \simeq\penalty\ \left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{n}\frac{1}{2^{\frac{n}{2}}}\sum_{|u|=n}Z_{\beta}^{u}

which is exponentially small thanks to the condition
\beta<\beta_{c}
.

The cost for placing all the particles around
\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}
is of order
\exp(-2^{n})
as we will see and noting that
2^{n}=\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}\right)^{\gamma}
shows that we obtain the expected order.

Remark 2.1
.

The probability that all the particles are near
\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}
at time
n
in the i.i.d. case is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\mathcal{N}(0,n)=\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}+O(1)\Big)^{2^{n}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n2^{n}+o(n2^{n})}.

We will see in the proof that the extra
n
term in the exponential comes from the i.i.d. assumption and it will disappear for the correlated process
(X_{u},|u|=n)
.

Following the above heuristic, we start by giving a lower bound for the left tail of
Z_{n}(\beta)
and then a lower bound for the left tail of
Z_{\beta}
by showing that
Z_{n}(\beta)
and
Z_{\beta}
are close enough when the aforementionned scenario occurs.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{n}(\beta)<-(1-\varepsilon)m_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big((X_{u})_{|u|=n}\in\mathsf{V}_{n}^{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{\,2^{n}}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{n}}}\int_{\mathsf{V}_{n}^{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{t}\Sigma_{n}^{-1}x}\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(Z_{n}(\beta)<-(1-\varepsilon)m_{n}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{\,2^{n}}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{n}}}\int_{\mathsf{\Pi}^{\varepsilon}_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{t}\Sigma_{n}^{-1}x}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}^{\,2^{n}}\sqrt{\det\Sigma_{n}}}\,\lambda\big(\mathsf{\Pi}^{\varepsilon}_{n}\big)\,\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\,\sup_{x\in\mathsf{\Pi}^{\varepsilon}_{n}}x^{t}\Sigma_{n}^{-1}x\right).

It is an exercise to show that

for some positive

A.2
.
Notice also that the biggest eigenvalue of

\displaystyle x^{t}\Sigma_{n}^{-1}x

\displaystyle=\left(\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{2}\mathds{1}^{t}\Sigma_{n}^{-1}\mathds{1}+2\left(\beta n-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\mathds{1}^{t}\Sigma_{n}^{-1}h+h^{t}\Sigma_{n}^{-1}h

Upper bound for the left tail

3.1
Large deviations with the branching property

From now on, we omit the subscript
\beta
to alleviate notations. The branching property gives the decomposition

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}\big(Z^{u}+(X_{u}-\beta n)W^{u}\big).

(3.1)

In order to obtain large deviations bounds, we need to control the Laplace transform of the random variables
Z+aW
. Intuitively, for
a>0
, the fat right tail of
W
should help. For the same reason, it seems hopeless at first sight to obtain a nice bound in the case
a<0
. But there must be some compensation with
Z
because a typical scenario for a large value for
W
leads to a positive large value for
Z
too (the box scenario of Subsection
2.2

\displaystyle Z_{n}+aW_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}\left(X_{u}-\beta n+a\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta a}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta(X_{u}+a)-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}((X_{u}+a)-\beta n).

Therefore
Z+aW=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta a}Z^{[a]}
where
Z^{[a]}
is the limit of the derivative martingale when the initial ancestor starts at
a
. Since the large negative values for
Z_{n}
are obtained when all the particles lie around
\beta n
at time
n
, it seems reasonable to expect that starting from
a<0
makes it harder to achieve. In fact, by exponential tilting, we can shift the mean of the
X_{u}

Lemma 3.1
.

For

Proof.

Recall that
(X_{u})_{|u|=n}

\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}X_{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1-2^{-n})

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{a}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}=Y_{n}^{[a]}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\frac{a}{(1-2^{-n})2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}X_{u}-\frac{a^{2}}{2(1-2^{-n})}},

the variables

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{a}}[X_{u}]=a
and the covariance matrix remains unchanged. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda Z_{n}^{[a]}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta(X_{u}+a)-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}(X_{u}+a-\beta n)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2(1-2^{-n})}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\frac{a}{(1-2^{-n})2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}X_{u}}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}(X_{u}-\beta n)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2(1-2^{-n})}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2a}{(1-2^{-n})2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}X_{u}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}n}(X_{u}-\beta n)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{a^{2}}{2(1-2^{-n})}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda Z_{n}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, applying Fatou’s lemma and conditional Jensen’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda Z^{[a]}}\right]\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{a^{2}}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[Z|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\frac{a^{2}}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda Z}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\displaystyle\leq C(t+1)\mathrm{e}^{\big(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}+\beta\eta\big)t},\quad\text{ for }z\leq(\beta+\eta)t+A.

Thus,

\braket{Q}_{\infty}\leq\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,\infty)}C^{2}(t+1)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{(\beta^{2}+2\beta\eta)t}\,\mathds{1}_{t\leq u\wedge v}\,\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u)\mu(\mathrm{d}v)\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

which proves that
Q
displays Gaussian concentration.
∎

The above approach also provides a useful bound on the Laplace transform of low particles.

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for
a\leq 0
and
\lambda\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda(Z+aW)}\right]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a+C\left(\left(\lambda\mathrm{e}^{-\beta a}\right)^{2}+1\right)}.

Proof.

As in the discrete case, define the derivative martingale when the initial ancestor starts at
a\leq 0
:

Z_{t}^{[a]}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{\lceil t\rceil}}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{t}}\left(a+X_{t}(u)-\beta t\right)\mathrm{e}^{\beta(a+X_{t}(u))-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}t}.

Define as previously

\displaystyle T_{k}^{u,[a]}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{t\geq R_{k-1}^{u}\,:\,a+X_{t}(u)=(\beta+\eta)t+A\right\},

\displaystyle R_{k}^{u,[a]}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{t\geq T_{k}^{u}\,:\,a+X_{t}(u)=\beta t\right\},

where
R_{0}^{u,[a]}\coloneqq 0
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{u,[a]}\coloneqq\bigcup_{k}[R_{k-1}^{u,[a]},T_{k}^{u,[a]}]
and define

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}^{[a]}_{t}

\displaystyle=a\mathrm{e}^{\beta a}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\beta(a+X_{s}(u)-\beta s)\right)\mathrm{e}^{\beta(a+X_{s}(u))-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}s}\,\mathds{1}_{s\in\mathcal{R}^{u,[a]}}\,\mathrm{d}X_{s}(u)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u).

It is a martingale bounded in
L^{2}
by Equation (
3.3
) and we have

Z_{\infty}^{[a]}\geq\tilde{Z}_{\infty}^{[a]}-Q^{[a]},

where

\displaystyle Q^{[a]}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}}Q^{[a]}_{u}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathds{1}_{T_{k}^{u,[a]}<+\infty}\Big(A+\eta T_{k}^{u,[a]}\Big)\mathrm{e}^{\big(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}+\beta\eta\big)T_{k}^{u,[a]}+\beta A}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u).

We are going to prove

\displaystyle\sup_{a\leq 0}\,\mathrm{ess\penalty\ sup}\braket{\tilde{Z}^{[a]}}_{\infty}<\infty,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Q^{[a]}\leq\mathbb{E}Q,

\displaystyle\sup_{a\leq 0}\,\mathrm{ess\penalty\ sup}\braket{Q^{[a]}}_{\infty}<\infty.

(3.3)

Let us admit for a moment those bounds. Then, using
\mathbb{E}\tilde{Z}_{\infty}^{[a]}=a\mathrm{e}^{\beta a}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda Z_{\infty}^{[a]}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda\tilde{Z}_{\infty}^{[a]}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda Q^{[a]}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda\mathbb{E}\tilde{Z}_{\infty}^{[a]}+\text{ess sup}\braket{\tilde{Z}^{[a]}}_{\infty}\lambda^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\lambda\mathbb{E}Q^{[a]}+\text{ess sup}\braket{Q^{[a]}}_{\infty}\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda a\mathrm{e}^{\beta a}+C(\lambda^{2}+1)},

for some
C>0
. And using
Z+aW=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta a}Z^{[a]}
concludes the proof.

Now let us prove the
3
statements in (
3.3
). First, using the fact that
a+X_{s}(u)\leq(\beta+\eta)s+A
when
s\in\mathcal{R}^{u,[a]}
, one obtains the same finite bound as in the case
a=0
, namely

\displaystyle\braket{\tilde{Z}^{[a]}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,\infty)}\left(1+\beta(\eta s+A)\right)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{(\beta^{2}+2\beta\eta)s+2\beta A}\,\mathds{1}_{s\leq u\wedge v}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u)\mu(\mathrm{d}v)\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

Remark 3.5
.

In the case
a>0
, we lose the control on the bracket for
t\in(0,T_{1})
.

The strong Markov property for
(X_{t}(u),t\geq 0)
applied at
T_{1}^{u,[a]}
shows that the conditional law of
\big(T_{k}^{u,[a]}\big)_{k\geq 2}
given
T_{1}^{u,[a]}
is equal to the conditional law of
\big(T_{k}^{u}\big)_{k\geq 2}
given
T_{1}^{u}
. We can thus focus on the first hitting time. Now, recall that the hitting time
T_{\alpha,b}
of a line
s\mapsto\alpha+bs
with
b>0
by a standard Brownian motion has a law given by

\mathds{1}_{t>0}\,\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\pi}t^{\frac{3}{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha b}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{b^{2}}{2}t-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2t}}+\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\alpha b}\right)\delta_{\infty},

see for instance
[
2
, Formula 2.0.2]
, and observe that the density part is decreasing in
\alpha
as soon as
\alpha>\frac{1}{b}
. Thus, if we choose
A>\frac{1}{\beta+\eta}
, we have, for
t\in(0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{1}^{u,[a]}=t\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(T_{1}^{u}=t\right).

Then, with
f^{u,[a]}(t)\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\left(T_{1}^{u,[a]}=t\right)
, one gets

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{u}^{[a]}>x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{u}^{[a]}>x\,\Big|\,T_{1}^{u,[a]}=t\right)\,\mathbb{P}\left(T_{1}^{u,[a]}=t\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathds{1}_{T_{k}^{u,[a]}<+\infty}\Big(A+\eta T_{k}^{u,[a]}\Big)\mathrm{e}^{(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}+\beta\eta)T_{k}^{u,[a]}+\beta A}>x\,\Bigg|\,T_{1}^{u,[a]}=t\right)\,f^{u,[a]}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathds{1}_{T_{k}^{u}<+\infty}\big(A+\eta T_{k}^{u}\big)\mathrm{e}^{(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}+\beta\eta)T_{k}^{u}+\beta A}>x\,\Bigg|\,T_{1}^{u}=t\right)\,f^{u,[a]}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathds{1}_{T_{k}^{u}<+\infty}\big(A+\eta T_{k}^{u}\big)\mathrm{e}^{(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}+\beta\eta)T_{k}^{u}+\beta A}>x\,\Bigg|\,T_{1}^{u}=t\right)\,\mathbb{P}\left(T_{1}^{u}=t\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Q_{u}>x\right).

We thus have the following stochastic dominance

Q_{u}^{[a]}\leq_{s}Q_{u}.

(3.4)

And by linearity

\mathbb{E}Q^{[a]}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}Q^{[a]}_{u}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u)\leq\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}Q_{u}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u)=\mathbb{E}Q.

What remains to be proved is a uniform bound for
\mathrm{ess\,sup}\braket{Q^{[a]}}_{\infty}
over
a
. The proof goes the same way as in
[
8
]
: with

Q_{u}^{[a]}(t)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[Q_{u}^{[a]}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

and

\mathrm{d}Q_{u}^{[a]}(t)=A^{[a]}_{u}(t)\mathrm{d}X_{t}(u),

the Markov property applied to
(a+X_{t}(u))_{t\geq 0}
yields to the same expression for the infinitesimal increment

A^{[a]}_{u}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{1}(t,a+X_{t}(u)),&\mbox{ if }t\in\left(T_{k}^{u,[a]},R_{k}^{u,[a]}\right),\\
f_{2}(t,a+X_{t}(u)),&\mbox{ if }t\in\left(R_{k}^{u,[a]},T_{k+1}^{u,[a]}\right).\end{array}\right.

And since in the first case
a+X_{t}(u)\geq\beta t
and
a+X_{t}(u)\leq(\beta+\eta)t+A
in the second case, one has by
[
8
, Lemma 5.3, Lemma 5.4]

\left|A^{[a]}_{u}(t)\right|\leq C(t+1)\mathrm{e}^{(\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}+\beta\eta)t}.

This yields to the desired uniform bound over
a

\braket{Q^{[a]}}_{\infty}\leq\int_{\partial\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,\infty)}C^{2}(t+1)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{(\beta^{2}+2\beta\eta)t}\,\mathds{1}_{t\leq u\wedge v}\,\,\mu(\mathrm{d}u)\mu(\mathrm{d}v)\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

∎

Lemma A.1
.

If
p\in[1,\gamma\wedge 2)
, then
Z_{\beta},W_{\beta}\in L^{p}
and there exists
B_{p}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg|\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-n\kappa_{\beta}}Z^{u}_{\beta}\bigg|^{p}\,\Bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\leq B_{p}\,\mathbb{E}|Z_{\beta}|^{p}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{p(\beta X_{u}-n\kappa_{\beta})},

\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg|\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-n\kappa_{\beta}}(X_{u}-\beta n)(W^{u}_{\beta}-1)\bigg|^{p}\,\Bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\leq B_{p}\,\mathbb{E}|W_{\beta}-1|^{p}\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{p(\beta X_{u}-n\kappa_{\beta})}|X_{u}-\beta n|^{p}.

Proof.

If
Y_{1},...,Y_{N}
are centered and independent random variables in
L^{p}
, the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality provides a positive
B_{p}
(which does not depend on
Y
) such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N}Y_{k}\right|^{p}\right]\leq B_{p}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N}Y_{k}^{2}\Bigg)^{p/2}\right].

Now the function
x\mapsto x^{p/2}
is subadditive since
p<2
, thus

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N}Y_{k}\right|^{p}\right]\leq B_{p}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{N}|Y_{k}|^{p}\right],

and the proof of the proposition is a consequence of this last inequality applied to the random variables
\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-n\kappa_{\beta}}Z_{\beta}^{u}
and
\mathrm{e}^{\beta X_{u}-n\kappa_{\beta}}(X_{u}-\beta n)(W^{u}_{\beta}-1)
conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

There exists
\theta>0
such that

\log\det\Sigma_{n}=\theta 2^{n}-2\log 2+o(1).

Proof.

\displaystyle\log\det\Sigma_{n}

\displaystyle=\log\left(2^{n}-1\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^{k}\log\left(2^{n-k}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(2^{n}-1\right)+2^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(k\log 2+\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\left(\log 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k}{2^{k}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)+\frac{\log(2^{n}-1)}{2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\left(\theta\underbrace{-\log 2\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{2^{k}}-\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)+\frac{\log(2^{n}-1)}{2^{n}}}_{\frac{-2\log 2}{2^{n}}+O(4^{-n})}\right),

where we set
\theta=2\log 2+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)
and used
\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{2^{k}}=\frac{n+2}{2^{n}}
and

\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)=O(4^{-n})
.
∎

lim Ulrich sequences and Boij-Söderberg cones
Srikanth B. Iyengar, Linquan Ma, Mark E. Walker

Abstract
. This paper extends the results of Boij, Eisenbud, Erman, Schreyer, and Söderberg on the structure of Betti cones of finitely generated graded modules and finite free complexes over polynomial rings, to all finitely generated graded rings admitting linear Noether normalizations. The key new input is the existence of lim Ulrich sequences of graded modules over such rings.

13D02 (primary); 13A35, 13C14, 14F06 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
a polynomial ring in
d
-variables, each of degree one. The
Betti table
of a finitely generated graded
A
-module
M
is the matrix of integers, denoted
\beta(M)
, that records the homological positions and the twists of the graded free modules appearing in the minimal free resolution of
M
. Boij and Söderberg
[
1
]

conjectured an explicit description of the collection of all Betti tables of graded Cohen-Macaulay
A
-modules “up to rational scaling”.
This conjecture was proved by Eisenbud and Schreyer
[
6
]
, and subsequently extended to all graded
A
-modules by Boij and Söderberg
[
2
]
. In detail, for
0\leq c\leq d
, let
\mathbf{B}^{c}(A)
denote the cone
inside the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq d,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
spanned by the Betti tables of all graded
A
-modules of codimension at least
c
. The results of Eisenbud and Schreyer, and Boij and Söderberg, give an equality

\mathbf{B}^{c}(A)=\mathbf{B}^{c}_{d}

where
\mathbf{B}^{c}_{d}
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
-span of certain explicit Betti tables
\beta(\underline{a})
, where
\underline{a}=(a_{0},\dots,a_{l})
ranges over all “degree sequences”, that is to say,
(l+1)
-tuples of increasing integers, for
l\geq c
; see (
5.2
)
below for definition of
\beta(\underline{a})
. A Cohen-Macaulay module
M
whose Betti table is a rational multiple of
\beta(\underline{a})
for some choice of degree sequence
\underline{a}
is called a “pure module”.

In this language, the theorem asserts that there exists a pure module for each possible degree sequence and
\mathbf{B}^{c}(A)
is spanned by the Betti tables of pure modules. Roughly speaking, this means that the Betti tables of all modules are governed by those of the pure modules. In particular, the multiplicity conjecture of Huneke and Srinivasan is a direct consequence
[
1
]
.

The main result of this paper extends these results to all
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded
k
-algebras that admit linear Noether normalizations, that is to say, a linear system of parameters; see
4.4
. We assume
k
is infinite throughout this paper, for the sake of simplicity, so this class of
k
-algebras includes the standard graded ones. For any graded
k
-algebra
R
and integer
0\leq c\leq d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim(R)
, let
\mathbf{B}^{c}(R)
be the cone spanned by Betti tables of modules of codimension at least
c
and of finite projective dimension. We prove:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.2
)
.

For
R
and
\mathbf{B}^{c}_{d}
as above, we have a containment

\mathbf{B}^{c}(R)\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{c}_{d}
, and equality holds when
R
is Cohen-Macaulay.

As in
[
1
]
, one can deduce the following bounds on the multiplicity of a perfect module; that is to say, a finitely generated module whose projective dimension equals its codimension. When
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, perfect modules are precisely those that are Cohen-Macaulay and of finite projective dimension. Thus the statement below generalizes the theorem on multiplicities over polynomial rings.

Corollary 1.2

(Corollary
6.3
)
.

With
R
as above, any perfect
R
-module
M
satisifes

e(R)\frac{t_{1}\cdots t_{c}}{c!}\leq e(M)\leq e(R)\frac{T_{1}\cdots T_{c}}{c!}

where
c
is the codimension of
M
, and the
t_{i}
’s and
T_{i}
’s are the minimal and maximal shifts occurring in the minimal free resolution of
M
.

Eisenbud and Erman
[
4
]
generalized the results in
[
1
,
6
]
to finite free complexes over
A
, and we extend their results to rings
R
as above; see Theorem
6.2
.

The proof of Eisenbud and Schreyer uses a pairing between Betti tables of graded
A
-modules and cohomology tables of coherent sheaves on
\operatorname{Proj}(A)={\mathbb{P}}^{d-1}_{k}
, along with a description of the rational cone spanned by the cohomology tables of such coherent sheaves. Although we do not use such a pairing in this paper, we do establish a result concerning the cone of cohomology tables for coherent sheaves over
\operatorname{Proj}(R)
with
R
as in Theorem
1.1
. When
k
is an infinite perfect field of positive characteristic, we prove
\overline{\mathbf{C}}({\mathbb{P}}^{d-1}_{k})=\overline{\mathbf{C}}(\operatorname{Proj}(R))
, where
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
is the component-wise limit closure of cohomology tables of coherent sheaves; see Theorem
3.4
.

The results stated above are known when
R
admits a graded Ulrich module, or, equivalently, when
\operatorname{Proj}(R)
admits an Ulrich sheaf; see
[
4
]
. But the existence of such modules is known only in a small number of cases. Instead, we use “lim Ulrich sequences” of graded modules, which are sequences of graded modules that asymptotically approximate Ulrich modules. They exist over any graded
k
-algebra admitting a linear Noether normalization, and where
k
is a infinite perfect field of positive characteristic. The relevant results are established in sections
2
and
4
.

2.
Lim Ulrich sequences of sheaves

For any noetherian scheme
X
let
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(X)
be its derived category of coherent sheaves. The tensor product of coherent sheaves on
X
is denoted
-\otimes-
; its derived version is
-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}-
. Throughout this manuscript
k
is a field and
m\geq 1
an integer. Let
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
be the projective space over
k
of dimension
m
; we write
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
when the field
k
needs emphasis.

2.1
.

For each coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
(or a bounded complex of such) on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
set

\gamma_{i,t}({\mathcal{F}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{m},{\mathcal{F}}(t))\quad\text{for $i,t$ in ${\mathbb{Z}}$.}

With
\varepsilon\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\to\operatorname{Spec}(k)
the structure map, for any coherent sheaf on
{\mathcal{F}}
the counit of the adjoint pair
(\varepsilon^{*},\varepsilon_{*})
is a natural map

(2.2)

\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\,.

The following result is well-known; see
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a coherent sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
. The conditions below are equivalent:

(1)

The map (
2.2
) is an isomorphism.

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}^{r}
for some integer
r\geq 0
.

(3)

\gamma_{i,t}({\mathcal{F}})=0
, except possibly when
i=0
and
t\geq 0
, or
i=m
and
t\leq-m-1
.

When these conditions hold,
{\mathcal{F}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}^{r}
for
r=\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}})
. ∎

Any nonzero sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
satisfying the equivalent condition of the result above is said to be an
Ulrich sheaf
. Condition (3) is used to define Ulrich sheaves over arbitrary projective varieties.

Lim Ulrich sequences

We introduce the notion of lim Ulrich sequences of sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
, following
[
15
, §6.6]
.

2.4
.

A
lim Ulrich sequence
of sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
is a sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of coherent sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
for which the following properties hold:

(1)

\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})\neq 0
for each
n\geq 0
;

(2)

There exists an integer
t_{0}
such that
\gamma_{0,t}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})=0
for
t\leq t_{0}
and all
n
;

(3)

There exists an integer
t_{1}
such that
\gamma_{\geqslant 1,t}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})=0
for
t\geq t_{1}
and all
n
;

(4)

Except possibly when
i=0
and
t\geq 0
, or
i=m
and
t\leq-m-1
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{i,t}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})}{\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=0\,.

The range of values of
i
and
t
arising in (4) is precisely the one from Lemma
2.3
. For geometric applications, the key properties are (1) and (4); see, in particular, Theorem
2.8
below. The other conditions become important when considering the corresponding sequence of graded modules; see the proof of Theorem
4.17
.

Clearly, if each
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
is an Ulrich sheaf, then the sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Ulrich. However, there are other lim Ulrich sequences on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
, at least when
k
has positive characteristic, and these will be used to construct lim Ulrich sequences on a much larger family of schemes; see Theorem
2.19
.

Next we present a characterization of lim Ulrich sequences on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
, in the spirit of the original definition of Ulrich sheaves.

2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a coherent sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
. Consider
\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}})
, the mapping cone of the counit map (
2.2
), and the induced exact triangle

(2.6)

\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}})\longrightarrow

in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbb{P}}^{m})
. Observe that this is natural in
{\mathcal{F}}
, but it is not compatible with twists. Keeping in mind that
\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}{\mathcal{F}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}^{r}
, tensoring the exact triangle defining
\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}})
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(t)
yields an exact triangle

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}(t)^{r}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}(t)\longrightarrow\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}})(t)\longrightarrow

The resulting exact sequence in cohomology reads

(2.7)

\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}(t))^{r}\to\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{m},{\mathcal{F}}(t))\to\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{m},\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}})(t))\to\operatorname{H}^{*+1}({\mathbb{P}}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}(t))^{r}\,.

The gist of the next result is that for a lim Ulrich sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
, the counit maps
\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
are asymptotically isomorphisms. If
{\mathcal{F}}
is an Ulrich sheaf, applying it to the sequence
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}
, recovers Lemma
2.3
, but of course the latter result, and its proof, are models for the one below.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be a lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
.
For any
{\mathcal{G}}
in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbb{P}}^{m})
and integers
i,j
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{i,j}(\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}{\mathcal{G}})}{\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=0\,.

The proof only uses conditions (1) and (4) in
2.4
.

Proof.

In what follows we write
{\mathbb{P}}
instead of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
, and
{\mathcal{O}}
for the structure sheaf on it. Set
r_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})
. It helps to consider the collection
{\mathbf{S}}
of sequences
({\mathcal{C}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
with
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbb{P}})
, with the property that

(2.9)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathcal{C}}_{n})}{r_{n}}=0\,.

The desired result is that the sequence with
{\mathcal{C}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}{\mathcal{G}}
is in
{\mathbf{S}}
. In the proof we repeatedly use the following elementary observation: Given sequences
({\mathcal{C}}_{n}^{\prime})_{n\geqslant 0}
,
({\mathcal{C}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
, and
({\mathcal{C}}_{n}^{\prime\prime})_{n\geqslant 0}
, of bounded complexes of coherent sheaves such that for each
n
there is an exact triangle

{\mathcal{C}}_{n}^{\prime}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}_{n}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow

if two of the three sequences are in
{\mathbf{S}}
, then so is the third.

It follows from this observation that given an exact triangle
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}\to
in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbb{P}})
, if the desired result holds for any two of
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
,
{\mathcal{G}}
, and
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}
, then it holds for the third. Since each object in the derived category is equivalent to a bounded complex consisting of twists of
{\mathcal{O}}
, a standard induction using these observations reduces checking the desired result to the case when
{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{O}}(t)
, for
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
; that is to say that the sequence
(\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(t))_{n\geqslant 0}
is in
{\mathbf{S}}
.

When
-m\leq t\leq-1
, one has
\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathcal{O}}(t))=0
so the exact sequence (
2.7
) with
{\mathcal{F}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
gives the first equality below:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}},\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(t))}{r_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(t))}{r_{n}}=0\,.

The second one is by condition
2.4
(4). This is the desired result.

Suppose
t\geq 0
. We argue by induction on
t
that the sequence
(\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(t))_{n\geqslant 0}
is in
{\mathbf{S}}
. The base case is
t=0
. Then since
\operatorname{H}^{i}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathcal{O}}(t))=0
for all
i\geq 1
the exact sequence (
2.7
), with
{\mathcal{F}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
, reduces to the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathcal{O}}(t))^{r_{n}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(t))\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}},\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(t))\longrightarrow 0

When
t=0
the map on the left is an isomorphism in degree
0
, by the definition of
r_{n}
. Thus we get

\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{*}({\mathbb{P}},\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n}))=\sum_{i\geq 1}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{i}({\mathbb{P}},{\mathcal{F}}_{n})

and the desired limit is again immediate from
2.4
(4).

Suppose
t\geq 1
and consider the Koszul resolution of
\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(t)
:

0\to\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(t-m-1)\to\cdots\to\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})^{m+1}(t-1)\to\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(t)\to 0

Since
t-m-1\geq-m
, from the already established part of the result and the induction hypotheses we get that the sequences
(\operatorname{cone}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})(j))_{n\geqslant 0}
are in
{\mathbf{S}}
for
t-m-1\leq j\leq t-1
. The exact sequence above implies that the same holds for
j=t
.

This completes the discussion of the case
t\geq 0
.

Given this one can use the Koszul resolution above and a descending induction on
t
to cover also the case
t\leq-m-1
.
∎

We record a construction of lim Ulrich sequences of sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
from

2.10
.

Let
k

. Let
\rho\colon Z\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
be the quotient by the action of the symmetric group
S_{m}
on
Z
, or any finite flat map. For each

{\mathcal{L}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(p^{n},2p^{n},\dots,mp^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{E}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\rho_{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{n})\,.

The
{\mathcal{L}}_{n}
are line bundles on

, and the

are vector bundles on
{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{m}
. The following result is a special case of
[

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
k
be an infinite perfect field of positive characteristic. Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be a coherent sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
of positive rank and
\gamma_{0,t}({\mathcal{N}})=0
for
t\ll 0
. With
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
the sheaf defined above, set

{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varphi^{n}_{*}({\mathcal{N}}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{n})\quad\text{for $n\geq 0$.}

The sequence

2.12
.

Fix a proper
k
-scheme
X
and
{\mathcal{L}}
a line bundle on
X
. For any coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
and integer
t

{\mathcal{F}}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes t}\,.

Recall that
{\mathcal{F}}
is

if there is a surjection
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{r}\to{\mathcal{F}}
for some
r\geq 0
. The line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
is
ample
if
for each coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
, the sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}(t)
is globally generated for

. The statement below is well-known.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
k
be a field,
X
a proper
k
-scheme, and
{\mathcal{L}}
a line bundle on
X
. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

{\mathcal{L}}
is ample and globally generated;

(2)

There exists a finite map
\rho\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{k}
for some integer
n
with
{\mathcal{L}}\cong\rho^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(1)
.

If in addition

is infinite, with
m\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim X
, these statements are equivalent to

(3)

There exists a finite map
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
and
{\mathcal{L}}\cong\pi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}(1)
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (1) and (2) is proved in
[
9
, Proposition I.4.4 and (the proof of) Proposition 1.4.6]
.

Suppose now that
k
is infinite. The implication (3)
\Rightarrow
(2) is obvious.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3): With
\rho
as in (2), we can find a linear projection
\pi^{\prime}\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
defined on all of
f(X)
, since

is infinite. The map
\pi^{\prime}\circ\rho
has the desired properties.
∎

2.14
.

In what follows, we say
(X,{\mathcal{L}})
is a
Noether pair
to mean that
X
is a proper scheme over an infinite field
k
, and
{\mathcal{L}}
is an ample and globally generated line bundle on
X
. The basic example is the pair
({\mathbb{P}}^{m},{\mathcal{O}}(1))
which we identify with
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
. More generally, if
R
is a graded
k

(\operatorname{Proj}(R),\widetilde{R(1)})
is a Noether pair; see Lemma

.

A morphism
f\colon(X,{\mathcal{L}})\to(X^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
of Noether pairs is a map of
k

f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
such that
f^{*}({\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})\cong{\mathcal{L}}
. By Proposition
2.13
, given a Noether pair
(X,{\mathcal{L}})
there is a finite dominant linear map of Noether pairs

\pi\colon(X,{\mathcal{L}})\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}\quad\text{where $m\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim X$.}

Thus, by the projection formula, for any sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
one has

(2.15)

\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))\cong\operatorname{H}^{i}({\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k},\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}(t))\quad\text{for all $i,t$.}

This allows one to introduce a notion of lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves on
X
; see
2.17
. First we record the result below, which is immediate from the equivalence (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) in Proposition
2.13
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{L}})
be a Noether pair and
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
a finite morphism of
k
-schemes. Then
(X^{\prime},f^{*}{{\mathcal{L}}})
is also a Noether pair. ∎

2.17
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{L}})
be a Noether pair. An
Ulrich
sheaf on
X
is a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
such that the coherent sheaf
\pi_{*}({\mathcal{F}})
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
is Ulrich for some finite dominant map
\pi\colon(X,{\mathcal{L}})\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
of Noether pairs; see
2.1
. Given Lemma
2.3
and (
2.15
), one can characterize this property purely in terms of the cohomology modules of twists of
{\mathcal{F}}
; in particular, it is independent of the choice of
\pi
. In the same vein, a
lim Ulrich sequence
of sheaves on
X
is a sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of coherent sheaves on
X
for which the sequence
(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{\geqslant 0}
is lim Ulrich in the sense of
2.4
. Once again, this can be expressed purely in terms of the cohomology of the twists of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
, which reconciles the definition given here with that in
[
15
, 6.6]
.

The result below serves to clarify that the lim Ulrich property is, to a certain extent, independent of the domain of definition of the sheaves involved.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
f\colon(X,{\mathcal{L}})\to(X^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
be a morphism of Noether pairs where
f
is finite and dominant. A sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of coherent sheaves on
X
is lim Ulrich if and only if the sequence
(f_{*}{\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of coherent sheaves on
X^{\prime}
is lim Ulrich.

Proof.

The stated claim is immediate from the observation that if
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
is a finite dominant linear map, then so is the composition
\pi^{\prime}\circ f
.
∎

The result below is a minor extension of
[
15
, Theorem 7.15]
.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{L}})
be a Noether pair with
\dim X\geq 1
. Assume furthermore that the field
k
is infinite and perfect of positive characteristic. There exists a lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
on
X
such that for each
n\geq 0
one has

0pt({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{x}\geq\begin{cases}1&\text{when $x\in X$ is a closed point}\\
0pt{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}&\text{for all $x$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Set
m\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim X
and let
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
be a finite dominant linear map. We begin by choosing a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{M}}
on
X
satisfying the following conditions

(1)

\operatorname{H}^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k},\pi_{*}{\mathcal{M}}(t))=\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{M}}(t))=0
for
t\ll 0
;

(2)

The sheaf
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{M}}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
has positive rank;

(3)

0pt{\mathcal{M}}_{x}\geq 0pt{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
for each
x\in X
.

To construct such an
{\mathcal{M}}
, consider the closed subset
W
of
X
consisting of closed points
x\in X
such that
0pt{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}=0
. Let
\mathcal{J}
be the sheaf of local sections of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
supported on
W
, and set
{\mathcal{M}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}/\mathcal{J}
. This construction ensures
{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
for
x\not\in W
, and
0pt{\mathcal{M}}_{x}\geq 1
for
x\in W
. This justifies both (1) and (3); see Lemma
2.20
below. Moreover
\dim{\mathcal{M}}=\dim X=\dim{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
, so
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{M}}
has positive rank and (2) holds.

With
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
the sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
defined in
2.10
, set

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varphi^{n}_{*}({\mathcal{M}}\otimes\pi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{n})

where
\varphi
is the Frobenius morphism on
X
. Since the Frobenius map preserves depth and
\pi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
is locally free,
0pt({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{x}=0pt{\mathcal{M}}_{x}
for each
n
and
x\in X
. In particular
0pt({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{x}
has the stated properties.

Using the fact that Frobenius commutes with pushfoward of sheaves, and the projection formula, one gets

\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}=\pi_{*}\varphi^{n}_{*}({\mathcal{M}}\otimes\pi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{n})\cong\varphi^{n}_{*}(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{M}}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{n})\,.

Theorem
2.11
yields that the sequence
(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
of sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
is lim Ulrich. Thus, by definition, the sequence
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is lim Ulrich; see also Lemma
2.18
.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
X
be as in (
2.14
) and
{\mathcal{F}}
a coherent sheaf on
X
. If
{\mathcal{F}}_{x}
has positive depth at every closed point
x\in X
, then
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))=0
for
t\ll 0
.

Proof.

By pushing forward to
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
we may reduce to the case where
X={\mathbb{P}}^{m}
. In this case, Grothendieck-Serre duality gives

\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,{\mathcal{F}}(t))^{*}\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{m}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-t-m-1))

where
(-)^{*}
denotes
k
-linear duals and Ext is computed in the abelian category of coherent sheaves. We have a spectral sequence

\mathrm{E}^{p,q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{H}^{p}(X,\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}^{q}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-t-m-1)))\Longrightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{p+q}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-t-m-1))

where
\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}
denotes sheafified Ext. Since
{\mathcal{F}}_{x}
has positive depth at all closed points and the local rings
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
are regular, we have
\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}^{m}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
; thus
\mathrm{E}^{0,m}=0
. By Serre vanishing
\mathrm{E}^{p,m-p}=0
for all
p\geq 1
for
t\ll 0
. This proves

\mathrm{Ext}^{m}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-t-m-1))\quad\text{for $t\ll 0$.}\qed

3.
Cones of cohomology tables

Throughout this section
k
is an infinite field and
(X,{\mathcal{L}})
a Noether pair over
k
, as in
2.14
, and
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
a finite dominant map with
{\mathcal{L}}\cong\pi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{m}}(1)
; thus
m=\dim X
. We prove that the closure of the cohomology tables of coherent sheaves on
X
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{m}
coincide; see Theorem
3.4
.

3.1
.

Extending the notation from
2.1
, for any
{\mathcal{G}}
in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
set

\gamma_{i,j}({\mathcal{G}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,{\mathcal{G}}(j))\,\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma({\mathcal{G}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\gamma_{i,j})_{i,j}\,.

By the projection formula (
2.15
), which applies also to objects in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
, any
\gamma({\mathcal{G}})
occurs as the cohomology table of a complex in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbb{P}}^{m})
. The result below means that the converse also holds, up to limits.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
({\mathcal{F}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be a lim Ulrich sequence of sheaves on
X
. For each
{\mathcal{G}}
in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k})
there exists a sequence
({\mathcal{G}}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{i,j}({\mathcal{G}}_{n})}{\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=\gamma_{i,j}({\mathcal{G}})\qquad\text{for all $i,j$.}

When
{\mathcal{G}}
is a vector bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
, the
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
can be chosen in
\operatorname{coh}X
.

Proof.

For each
n\geq 0
set

{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{L}{\pi}^{*}{\mathcal{G}}\,.

In particular, when
{\mathcal{G}}
is a vector bundle, each
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
is a coherent sheaf on
X
. The projection formula yields

\pi_{*}({\mathcal{G}}_{n})\cong\pi_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}{\mathcal{G}}\,,

so Theorem
2.8
applied to the lim Ulrich sequence
(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n}))_{n\geqslant 0}
yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{i,j}({\mathcal{G}}_{n})}{\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{i,j}(\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}\pi_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}{\mathcal{G}})}{\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})}\,.

Since one has equalities

\gamma_{i,j}(\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}\pi_{*}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}{\mathcal{G}})=\gamma_{0,0}({\mathcal{F}}_{n})\gamma_{i,j}({\mathcal{G}})

the desired result follows.
∎

3.3
.

Fix an integer
m
and consider the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space

W\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\prod_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\,,

endowed with the topology defined by pointwise convergence.

For any coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{G}}
on
X
we view
\gamma({\mathcal{G}})
, defined in
3.1
, as an element in
W
. This is the
cohomology table
of
{\mathcal{G}}
. The
cone
of cohomology tables is the subspace

\mathbf{C}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{{\mathcal{G}}\in\operatorname{coh}X}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geqslant 0}\gamma({\mathcal{G}})\,,

of
W
. Observe that the definition involves only the coherent sheaves on
X
, and not all of
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. We write
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
for the closure of
\mathbf{C}(X)
in

.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
k
be an infinite perfect field of positive characteristic and
(X,{\mathcal{L}})
a Noether pair over
k
. There is an equality
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)=\overline{\mathbf{C}}({\mathbb{P}}^{m})
for
m\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim X
.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon(X,{\mathcal{L}})\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m}_{k}
be a finite dominant linear map of Noether pairs. It is clear from (
2.15
) that
\mathbf{C}(X)\subseteq\mathbf{C}({\mathbb{P}}^{m})
. We have only to prove that the cohomology table of a coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
is in
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
. By
[
7
, Theorem 0.1]
, one has a convergent sum

\gamma({\mathcal{F}})=\sum_{i\geqslant 1}a_{i}\gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{i})

where each
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
is a coherent sheaf obtained as a push-forward of a vector bundle on a linear subset of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
and each
a_{i}
is a positive real number. We claim that it suffices to verify that each
\gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{i})
is in
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
.

Indeed, assume this is so and for each
n\geq 1
consider the sum

\gamma(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}a_{1}\gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{i})+\cdots+a_{n}\gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{n})\,.

Since each
\gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{i})
is in
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
approximating the

by positive rational numbers we can write

\gamma(n)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\gamma(n)(s)

where each
\gamma(n)(s)
is a positive rational combination of
\gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{1}),\dots,\gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{n})
and hence in
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
. For each

pick an integer
s_{n}\geq 1
such that

|\gamma(n)_{a,b}-\gamma(n)(s_{n})_{a,b}|<1/2^{n}

for all
a,b
with
|a|,|b|<n
. Evidently

\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma(n)(s_{n})=\gamma({\mathcal{F}})

and each
\gamma(n)(s_{n})
is in
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
, so
\gamma({\mathcal{F}})
is in
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
, as claimed.

It thus remains to consider a coherent sheaf of the form
\iota_{*}({\mathcal{E}})
, where
\iota\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{c}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
is a linear subspace and
{\mathcal{E}}
is a vector bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}^{c}
, and verify that
\gamma({\mathcal{E}})
is in the closure of
\mathbf{C}(X)
.

Consider the scheme
X^{\prime}
obtained by the pull-back of
\pi
along
\iota
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}}^{c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}}^{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

With
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\pi^{\prime})^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{c}}(1)
, the pair
(X^{\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime})
is a Noether pair; see Lemma
2.16
. Using the projection formula we identify the cohomology tables of coherent sheaves on
X^{\prime}
and on
{\mathbb{P}}^{c}
as elements of

, from
3.3
. Thus
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X^{\prime})\subseteq\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X)
.

The scheme
X^{\prime}
admits a lim Ulrich sequence. This is clear when
c=0
; for

2.19
. Thus, keeping in mind that
{\mathcal{E}}
is a vector bundle, Lemma

yields that
\gamma({\mathcal{E}})
is in
\overline{\mathbf{C}}(X^{\prime})

∎

4.
Lim Ulrich sequences of graded modules

As before
k
is a field. Let
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{R_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 0}
be a finitely generated, graded
k
-algebra with
R_{0}=k
. Set
\mathfrak{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R_{\geqslant 1}
; this is the unique homogenous maximal ideal of
R
. We write
\operatorname{grmod}R
for the category of finitely generated graded
R
-modules, with morphisms the degree preserving
R
-linear maps. The component in degree
j
of a graded
R
-module
M
is denoted
M_{j}
.

The full subcategory of the derived category of
\operatorname{grmod}R
consisting of complexes
C
with
\operatorname{H}_{i}(C)=0
for
i\ll 0
is denoted
\mathbf{D}^{+}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
. It contains
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
the bounded derived category of finitely generated graded
R
-modules.

4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(u_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
be a sequence of positive integers. We say that a sequence of objects
(C^{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
of
\mathbf{D}^{+}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
is
\boldsymbol{u}
-trivial
if for all integers
i,j
one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(C^{n})_{j}}{u_{n}}=0\,.

A sequence of morphisms
(f^{n}\colon C^{n}\to D^{n})
in
\mathbf{D}^{+}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence
if the sequence of their mapping cones,
\operatorname{cone}(f^{n})
, is
\boldsymbol{u}
-trivial. It is easy to verify that a composition of
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalences is also a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence. This observation will be used often in the sequel.

The proof of the result below is straightforward; see also
[
15
, Lemma 5.3]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

If a sequence of morphisms
(f^{n}\colon C^{n}\to D^{n})
in
\mathbf{D}^{+}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence, then for any
i,j
there is an equality

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(C^{n})_{j}}{u_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(D^{n})_{j}}{u_{n}}\,.

The corresponding statements involving
\liminf
also holds. In particular, if one of the limits exists, so does the other and the two limits coincide. ∎

We say that a complex of graded
R
-modules is
perfect
if it is quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finitely generated free
R
-modules.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(f^{n}\colon C^{n}\to D^{n})
be a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence. For
P
in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
, the induced sequence

(\operatorname{id}\otimes f_{n}\colon P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}C^{n}\to P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}D^{n})

is also an
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence under either of the following conditions:

(1)

The complex
P
is perfect;

(2)

There exists an integer
s
such that
\operatorname{H}_{i}(\operatorname{cone}(f_{n}))=0
for all
i<s
and all
n
.

Proof.

Replacing
P
by its minimal free resolution, we get that
P_{i}
, the component in homological degree
i
, is finite free with
P_{i}=0
for
i\ll 0
. The desired conclusion is that the sequence
(P\otimes_{R}\operatorname{cone}(f_{n}))
is
\boldsymbol{u}
-trivial under either of the two conditions.

When
P
is perfect,
P_{i}=0
also for
i\gg 0
, and the
\boldsymbol{u}
-triviality can be checked by a straightforward induction on the number of nonzero components of
P
.

Suppose condition (2) holds, and let
s
be as given; we may assume
s=0
. Fix an integer
i
. It is easy to see that for each integer
n
, the inclusion
P_{\leqslant i+1}\subseteq P
of sub-complexes induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{H}_{i}(P_{\leqslant i+1}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{cone}(f_{n}))\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes_{R}\operatorname{cone}(f_{n}))\,.

Since
P_{\leqslant i+1}
is perfect, one can invoke the already established part (1).
∎

In the sequel we need the theory of multiplicities for graded modules. Since the rings we work with are not necessarily standard-graded, we begin by recalling the basic definitions and results in the form we need.

4.4
.

Let
R
be a graded
k
-algebra as above. For any finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
and integer
q\geq\dim_{R}M
set

e_{q}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q!\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{R}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}M)}{n^{q}}\,.

This is an integer, and it is equal to
0
when
q\geq\dim_{R}M+1
. The
multiplicity
of
M
is the integer

e(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}e_{q}(M)\quad\text{for $q=\dim_{R}M$.}

This coincides with the multiplicity of
M_{\mathfrak{m}}
as a module over the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

In what follows the focus is on graded
k
-algebras
R
admitting a
linear
system of parameters; that is to say, a system of parameters in
R_{1}
. It is convenient to call the
k
-subalgebra generated by a linear system of parameters a
linear Noether normalization
of
R
. Linear Noether normalizations exist, for instance, when
R
is standard-graded (homogeneous, in the language of
[
3
, §4.1]
) and
k
is infinite. The next results allow one to reduce computing multiplicities over rings admitting linear Noether normalizations to the case of standard graded rings.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
be a finitely generated graded
k
-algebra, and set
\mathfrak{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R_{\geqslant 1}
. For any set of elements
\boldsymbol{x}\subseteq R_{1}
satisfying
\sqrt{(\boldsymbol{x})}=\mathfrak{m}
, the ideal
(\boldsymbol{x})
is a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
; it is a minimal reduction when
\boldsymbol{x}
is a system of parameters for
R
.

Proof.

The claim about minimality follows from
[
13
, Corollary 8.3.6]
once we prove that
(\boldsymbol{x})
is a reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
, for the analytic spread of
\mathfrak{m}
equals
\dim R
.

The desired result is clear when
R
is standard graded because
\mathfrak{m}^{n}=(\boldsymbol{x})\mathfrak{m}^{n-1}
for any
n
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n}\subseteq(\boldsymbol{x})
, and such an
n
exists since
\mathfrak{m}
is the radical of
(\boldsymbol{x})
.

In what follows given a homogenous ideal
I
in
R
such that
\ell_{R}(M/IM)
is finite, we write
e(I,M)
for the multiplicity of
M
with respect to
I
. Namely, the limit of the sequence in
4.4
with
\mathfrak{m}
replaced by
I
; see also
[
3
, Definition 4.6.1]
.

The goal is to prove that
\mathfrak{m}
is the integral closure of
(\boldsymbol{x})
; see
[
13
, Corollary 1.2.5]
. It suffices to verify that this property holds modulo each minimal prime of
R
; see
[
13
, Proposition 1.1.5]
. Thus we may assume
R
is a domain, and then the desired result is equivalent to:
e(\boldsymbol{x},R)=e(R)
; this is by Rees’ theorem
[
13
, Theorem 11.3.1]
.

There exists an integer
s
such that the Veronese subring
R^{(s)}
of
R
is standard-graded, after rescaling. Thus
\boldsymbol{x}^{s}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}x_{1}^{s},\dots,x_{d}^{s}
is a reduction of the maximal ideal
(R_{s})R^{(s)}
of
R^{(s)}
, and hence one gets that
e(\boldsymbol{x}^{s},R^{(s)})=e(R^{(s)})
. This justifies the second equality below:

e(\boldsymbol{x}^{s},R)=s\cdot e(\boldsymbol{x}^{s},R^{(s)})=s\cdot e((R_{s}),R^{(s)})=e((R_{s}),R)

The first and the third equalities hold as the rank of
R
over
R^{(s)}
is
s
; see
[
3
, Corollary 4.7.9]
.
If
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
are homogeneous generators of the ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, then
\mathfrak{m}^{s}
is integral over
(f_{1}^{s},\dots,f_{n}^{s})
, which is contained in the ideal
(R_{s})
, so we get that

e((R_{s}),R)\leq e((f_{1}^{s},\dots,f_{n}^{s}),R)=e(\mathfrak{m}^{s},R)\,.

We conclude that
e(\boldsymbol{x}^{s},R)\leq e(\mathfrak{m}^{s},R)
and hence that
e(\boldsymbol{x},R)\leq e(\mathfrak{m},R)
. The reverse inequality is clear.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
R
be a graded
k
-algebra admitting a linear system of parameters
\boldsymbol{x}
. For each

in
\operatorname{grmod}R
there is an equality

e(M)=(\dim_{R}M)!\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\leqslant n}\operatorname{rank}_{k}(M_{i})}{n^{\dim_{R}M}}\,.

When
\dim_{R}M=\dim R
, one has also an equality

e(M)=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x};M)\,.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
the ideal
(\boldsymbol{x})
is a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal of

, so the multiplicity of

as an

-module coincides with its multiplicity when viewed as a module over the

-subalgebra of

generated by
\boldsymbol{x}
. Therefore, in the remainder of the proof, we assume that

is a standard graded polynomial ring, over indeterminates
\boldsymbol{x}
. Then the fact the multiplicity is the Euler characteristic of the Koszul homology of

with respect to
\boldsymbol{x}
is
[
3
, Theorem 4.7.6]
.

The first equality is also well-known, but we could not find a suitable reference, so we outline a proof: Set
q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim_{R}M
and let

be the function defined on the subcategory of finitely generated
R
-modules of dimension at most

by

e^{\prime}_{q}(N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}q!\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\leqslant n}\operatorname{rank}_{k}(N_{i})}{n^{q}}\,.

The desired result is that
e^{\prime}_{q}(-)=e_{q}(-)
on this subcategory of
R
-modules. It is clear that equality holds when the module is generated in a single degree. Since any finitely generated module admits a finite filtration by
R
-submodules such that the subquotients in the filtrations are generated in a single degree, it remains to note that both invariants are additive on short exact sequences of modules of dimension at most
q
; this is clear for
e^{\prime}_{q}(-)
and for
e_{q}(-)
it is
[
3
, Corollary 4.7.7]
.
∎

4.7
.

Let
R
be a graded
k
-algebra admitting a linear Noether normalization and

a finitely generated graded
R
-module. We write
\nu_{R}(U)
for its minimal number of generators. The
R
-module

is
Ulrich
if it has the following properties:

(1)

U
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay and nonzero;

(2)

e(U)=\nu_{R}(U)
;

(3)

U
is generated in degree
0
.

When

is a standard graded polynomial ring, the only Ulrich modules are
R^{n}
, for some integer
n
; no twists are allowed.

Following
[
15
,
16
]
, we introduce a notion lim Ulrich sequences of graded
R
-modules. To that end, we recall some basic properties of finite free complexes.

4.8
.

Consider a complex

F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0\longrightarrow F_{n}\longrightarrow F_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{1}\longrightarrow F_{0}\longrightarrow 0

of finite free
R
-modules with
\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}(F)
finite and nonzero. The New Intersection Theorem
[
19
]
yields
n\geq\dim R
; if equality holds we say
F
is a
short
complex. For such an

, any maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module

satisfies

\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{R}M)=0\qquad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

This is by the acyclicity lemma
[
3
, Exercise 1.4.24]
. This property characterizes maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules, by the depth sensitivity of Koszul complexes.

Lim Ulrich sequences

Let
R
be a graded
k
-algebra admitting a linear system of parameters. Compare the definition below with that of an Ulrich module
4.7
, and also with that of lim Ulrich sheaves
2.4
.

4.9
.

A sequence
(U^{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
in
\operatorname{grmod}R
is
lim Ulrich
if each
U^{n}
is nonzero and the following properties hold:

(1)

For each finite free short complex
F
with
\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}(F)
finite one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U^{n})}{\nu_{R}(U^{n})}=0\qquad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

(2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}e_{d}(U^{n})/\nu_{R}(U^{n})=1
for
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R
.

(3)

With
\mathfrak{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R_{\geqslant 1}
the unique homogenous maximal ideal of
R
, one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}(U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n})_{0}}{\nu_{R}(U^{n})}=1\,.

It suffices that condition (1) hold for
F
the Koszul complex on a
single
system of parameters for
R
; this can be proved along the lines of
[
15
, Lemma 5.7]
. Moreover, with
F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K(\boldsymbol{x})
the Koszul complex on a linear system of parameters
\boldsymbol{x}
, parts (1) and (2) above can be expressed more succinctly as follows:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x};U^{n})}{\nu_{R}(U^{n})}=\begin{cases}1&\text{when $i=0$}\\
0&\text{when $i\neq 0$.}\end{cases}

Here is an expression of these conditions in the language introduced in
4.1
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
R
be a graded
k
-algebra with a linear system of parameters
\boldsymbol{x}
, and
(U^{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
a lim Ulrich sequence in
\operatorname{grmod}R
. For
\boldsymbol{u}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\nu_{R}(U^{n}))
, the following sequences of canonical surjections are

-equivalences:

K(\boldsymbol{x};U^{n})\twoheadrightarrow U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n}\twoheadrightarrow(U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n})_{0}\,.

Proof.

Consider the canonical morphisms

f^{n}\colon K(\boldsymbol{x};U^{n})\twoheadrightarrow U^{n}/\boldsymbol{x}U^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g^{n}\colon U^{n}/\boldsymbol{x}U^{n}\twoheadrightarrow U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n}\,.

We prove that the sequences
(f^{n})
and
(g^{n})
are
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalences, which implies that the composition
(g^{n}f^{n})
is also a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence, as claimed.

It is easy to see that the homology of the cone of
f^{n}
satisfies

\operatorname{H}_{i+1}(\operatorname{cone}(f^{n}))\cong\begin{cases}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x};U^{n})&\text{for $i\geq 1$.}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Thus
(f^{n})
is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x};U^{n})_{j}}{u_{n}}=0\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$ and $j\in{\mathbb{Z}}$.}

It remains to observe that the lim Ulrich property expressed in condition (
4.9
), in
4.9
, implies the limit above is
0
, that is to say,
(f^{n})
is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence, as desired.

For each
n
one has that

\operatorname{rank}_{k}(\mathfrak{m}U^{n}/\boldsymbol{x}U^{n})=\operatorname{rank}_{k}(U^{n}/\boldsymbol{x}U^{n})-u_{n}

so condition (2) defining the lim Ulrich property of
(U^{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}(\mathfrak{m}U^{n}/\boldsymbol{x}U^{n})}{u_{n}}=0\,.

The cone of the morphism
g^{n}\colon U^{n}/\boldsymbol{x}U^{n}\to U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n}
satisfies

\operatorname{H}_{i}(\operatorname{cone}(g^{n}))\cong\begin{cases}(\mathfrak{m}U^{n})/(\boldsymbol{x}U^{n})&\text{for $i=1$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It follows that the sequence of maps
(g^{n})
is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence.

This completes the proof that the sequence
(g^{n}f^{n})
is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence.

Condition (3) in
4.9
is equivalent to the condition that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}(U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n})_{i\neq 0}}{u_{n}}=0\,.

Since the homology of the mapping cone of the surjection

(U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n})\to(U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n})_{0}

is
(U^{n}/\mathfrak{m}U^{n})_{i\neq 0}
in degree 1 and zero otherwise, it follows that the sequence above is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence, as desired.
∎

4.11
.

It is clear from the proof of Lemma
4.10
that when
(U^{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is a lim Ulrich sequence, the sequences
(f^{n})
and
(g^{n})
are
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalent in the stronger sense that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\operatorname{cone}(f^{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U^{n})}=0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(\operatorname{cone}(g^{n}))}{\nu_{R}(U^{n})}\,.

That is to say, the ranks of the homology modules, and not just their graded pieces, of the cones are asymptotically zero with respect to
(\nu_{R}(U^{n}))
. These conditions are equivalent to the lim Ulrich property of the sequence
(U^{n})_{\geqslant 1}
.

Moreover, it is not hard to verify that if the sequence
(g^{n}f^{n})
is a
\boldsymbol{u}
-equivalence (in either sense) then so are the sequences

(f^{n})
and
(g^{n})
; the converse is clear.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
R
be a graded
k
-algebra with a linear system of parameters
\boldsymbol{x}
, and
(U^{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
a lim Ulrich sequence in
\operatorname{grmod}R

\mathbf{D}^{+}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
and integers

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}K(\boldsymbol{x};U^{n}))_{j}}{\nu_{R}(U_{n})}=\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}k)_{j}\,.

Proof.

Set
u_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\nu_{R}(U^{n})
and
\boldsymbol{u}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(u_{n})
. Set

and let

be the composition of maps

A composition of

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{H}_{i}(P\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{R}G^{n})_{j}}{u_{n}}

The first one holds because the surjection

∎

Boij-Söderberg theory

In this section we recall basics of Boij-Söderberg theory, due mainly to Boij, Eisenbud, Erman, Schreyer, and Söderberg
[
2
,
1
,
4
,
7
,
6
]
.

Codimension sequences and degree sequences

Throughout we fix a non-negative integer
d
, soon to be the dimension of a graded ring. By a
codimension sequence
(for
d
) we mean a non-decreasing sequence
\boldsymbol{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(c_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
where each
c_{i}
is an element of the set
\{\varnothing,0,\dots,d,\infty\}
, with ordering

\varnothing<0<1<\cdots<d<\infty\,.

For any non-negative integer
c
the constant sequence
(c)_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is also denoted
c
. We consider the collection of codimension sequences with the natural partial order:

(c_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\leq(c^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\qquad\text{if $c_{i}\leq c^{\prime}_{i}$ for each $i$.}

A
degree sequence
is an increasing sequence of integers
t_{a}<t_{a+1}<\cdots<t_{a+l}
, with
0\leq l\leq d
. It is convenient to view it as an infinite sequence

\boldsymbol{t}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\dots,-\infty,-\infty,t_{a},\dots,t_{a+l},\infty,\infty,\dots)

with
t_{i}
in position
i
. The integer
l
is the
codimension
of
\boldsymbol{t}
, denoted
\operatorname{codim}(\boldsymbol{t})
; we define
\inf(\boldsymbol{t})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}a
. The sequence
\boldsymbol{t}
is
compatible
with a codimension sequence
\boldsymbol{c}
if

0\leq c_{a}\leq\operatorname{codim}(\boldsymbol{t})\leq c_{a+1}\qquad\text{for $a=\inf(\boldsymbol{t})$.}

In what follows we consider the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space

(5.1)

V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathbb{Q}}^{({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}})}=\bigoplus_{i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}\,.

Each degree sequence
\boldsymbol{t}
determines an element
\beta(\boldsymbol{t})
of
V
given by

(5.2)

\beta(\boldsymbol{t})_{i,j}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}\frac{\prod_{n\neq a}|t_{n}-t_{a}|}{\prod_{n\neq i}|t_{n}-t_{i}|}&\text{for $a\leq i\leq a+l$ and $j=t_{i}$,}\\
0&\text{for all other values of $i,j$.}\end{cases}

Here
a\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\inf(\boldsymbol{t})
and
l=\operatorname{codim}(\boldsymbol{t})
. Given a codimension sequence
\boldsymbol{c}
, we set

(5.3)

\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{cone in $V$ spanned by $\beta(\boldsymbol{t})$ as $\boldsymbol{t}$ ranges}\\
&\text{over degree sequences compatible with $\boldsymbol{c}$}\end{aligned}\right\}\,.

This is the smallest subset of
V
containing the
\beta(\boldsymbol{t})
, for the permitted
\boldsymbol{t}
, and closed under addition and multiplication by non-negative rational numbers.

The finite topology on
V

Given a finite subset
Y\subset{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
we write
V_{Y}
for the subspace of
V
spanned by the corresponding coordinate vectors. Thus a vector
v\in V
is in
V_{Y}
if and only if
v_{i,j}=0
for
(i,j)\notin Y
. We topologize
V
by giving it the finite topology: a subset
U
of
V
is open if and only if its intersection with any finite dimensional subspace of
V
is open in the Euclidean topology. This is equivalent to the condition that
U\cap V_{Y}
is open in
V_{Y}
, equipped with its Euclidean topology, for all finite subsets
Y
of
{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Observe that the cone spanned by vectors
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
in the positive orthant of
V
is contained in
V_{Y}
if and only if each
v^{i}
is in
V_{Y}
. This has the following consequence.

Lemma 5.4
.

With
Y
as above, for any codimension sequence
\boldsymbol{c}
the cone
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}\cap V_{Y}
is spanned by vectors
\beta(\boldsymbol{t})
where
\boldsymbol{t}
is any degree sequence compatible with
\boldsymbol{c}
and satisfying the condition

(n,t_{n})\in Y\qquad\text{for $\inf(\boldsymbol{t})\leq n\leq\inf(\boldsymbol{t})+\operatorname{codim}(\boldsymbol{t})$.}

Thus
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}\cap V_{Y}
is the cone spanned by a finite collection of vectors, and hence the subset
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}\subset V
is closed in the finite topology. ∎

The result above means that if a sequence of elements
(\beta_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
in
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
converges in the finite topology to an element
\beta
of
V
, then
\beta
must also belong to
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
.

Graded modules over graded rings

As in Section
4
, let
k
be a field and
R=\{R_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 0}
a finitely generated graded
k
-algebra with
R_{0}=k
. Set
\mathfrak{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R_{i\geqslant 1}
.

5.5
.

Let
M
be an
R
-complex in
\mathbf{D}^{+}(\operatorname{grmod}R)
. For any pair of integers
(i,j)
, the
Betti number
of
M
in degree
(i,j)
is

\beta^{R}_{i,j}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)_{j}\,.

The
Betti table
of
M
is the array
\beta^{R}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\beta^{R}_{i,j}(M))_{i,j}
.

Finite free complexes

With
R
as above, we write
\mathcal{F}(R)
for the class of finite free complexes of graded
R
-modules, that is to say, complexes of the form

0\longrightarrow F_{b}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{a}\longrightarrow 0

where each
F_{i}
is a finitely generated graded free
R
-module, and the differential is homogenous of degree zero.
Each finite free complex is quasi-isomorphic to a
minimal
one: a finite free complex
F
whose differential
d
satisfies
d(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}F
. When
F
is minimal, one has

F_{i}\cong\bigoplus_{j}R(-j)^{\beta^{R}_{i,j}(F)}\,.

Since
F
is finite free,
\beta^{R}_{i,j}(F)
is nonzero only for finitely many pairs
(i,j)
, so
\beta^{R}(F)
, the Betti table of
F
,
is an element in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
V
from (
5.1
).

Codimension

Let
\operatorname{codim}_{R}M
be the
codimension
of a finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
, namely, the height of its annihilator ideal. Thus
\operatorname{codim}_{R}M=\infty
if and only if
M=0
. Let
\boldsymbol{c}
be a codimension sequence, as defined above. Set

(5.6)

\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{F\in\mathcal{F}(R)\left|\begin{aligned} \text{ $\beta^{R}_{i,j}(F)=0$ when $c_{i}=\varnothing$, and}\\
\text{$\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)\geq c_{i}$ when $c_{i}>\varnothing$}\end{aligned}\right.\right\}\,.

For instance, for the constant sequence
\dim R
the objects in
\mathcal{F}^{\dim R}(R)
are precisely the finite free complexes with homology of finite length. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}^{0}(R)=\mathcal{F}(R)
. Clearly, if
\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}
is another codimension sequence with
\boldsymbol{c}\leq\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\supseteq\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}}(R)
.

Consider the cone in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
V
from (
5.1
), spanned by the Betti tables of the finite free complexes
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)
:

(5.7)

\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geqslant 0}\beta^{R}(F)\,.

Pure complexes

A finite free complex
F
is
pure
if there exists a degree sequence
\boldsymbol{t}
as above such that for
a\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\inf(\boldsymbol{t})
and
l\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{codim}\boldsymbol{t}
, the following conditions hold:

(1)

\beta_{i,j}(F)\neq 0
if and only if
j=\boldsymbol{t}_{i}
, that is to say, the complex
F
is quasi-isomorphic to minimal complex of the form

0\longrightarrow R(-t_{a+l})^{b_{a+l}}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow R(-t_{a})^{b_{a}}\longrightarrow 0\,;

(2)

The
R
-module
\operatorname{H}_{a}(F)
is Cohen-Macaulay with
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{H}_{a}(F)\geq l
;

(3)

\operatorname{H}_{n}(F)=0
for
n\neq a
.

In particular,
\mathsf{\Sigma}^{-a}F
is the free resolution of a finitely generated
R
-module, namely,
\operatorname{H}_{a}(F)
. It follows from the result of Herzog and Kühl
[
11
]
that the Betti table of such an
F
has the form

\beta^{R}(F)=b\cdot\beta(\boldsymbol{t})\,,

where
b\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\beta_{a}^{R}(F)
and
\beta(\boldsymbol{t})
is as in (
5.2
).

Boij-Soderberg theory

Let
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring; thus the degree of each
x_{i}
is
1
. The following result is due to Eisenbud and Erman, and builds on work of Eisenbud and Schreyer, and Boij and Söderberg. The cone
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
is defined in (
5.3
).

Theorem 5.8
.

[
4
, Theorem 3.1]

Each degree sequence has an associated pure object in
\mathcal{F}(S)
. For any codimension sequence
\boldsymbol{c}
for
S
one has
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(S)=\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
.∎

By a codimension sequence
\boldsymbol{c}

for
a graded ring
R
we mean a codimension sequence for the integer
\dim R
. Combining the preceding theorem with Lemma
5.4
gives the following result.

Corollary 5.9
.

For a standard graded polynomial ring
S
, and any codimension sequence
\boldsymbol{c}
for
S
, the subset
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(S)\subset V
is closed in the finite topology. ∎

6.1

Throughout this section

a finitely generated graded

-algebra admitting a linear Noether normalization, say

.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
. If
\boldsymbol{c}
is a codimension sequence for

, then
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
. Equality holds if

for all

, or

is Cohen-Macaulay.

The proof of the theorem when

has positive characteristic is given in

. We record a couple of expected consequences.

When
R
is a standard graded polynomial ring, the bounds given below on the multiplicities (whose definition is recalled in
4.4
) were conjectured by Huneke and Srinivasan, and proved by Eisenbud and Schreyer
[
6
]
as a consequence of their proof of the conjectures of Boij and Söderberg
[

]
.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
, and let
M
be a nonzero finitely generated, graded,
R
-module generated in degree zero, and of finite projective dimension. Then

e(M)\leq\frac{e(R)\beta_{0}(M)}{c!}\prod_{i=1}^{c}\max\{j\mid\beta^{R}_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\}\,,

for
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{codim}M
. When
M
is perfect, one has also the lower bound:

e(M)\geq\frac{e(R)\beta_{0}(M)}{c!}\prod_{i=1}^{c}\min\{j\mid\beta^{R}_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\}\,.

Each of the inequalities above is strict, unless
M
has a pure resolution.

Proof.

We deduce these bounds from Theorem
6.2
following the argument in
[
1
]
.

There is nothing to check if
\dim R=0
so we can assume
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim R\geq 1
. Let
A
be a Noether normalization of
R
as in
6.1
.
It follows from Lemma
4.6
that the multiplicity of a graded
R
-module coincides with its multiplicity as an
A
-module.

Fix an integer
c
with
0\leq c\leq d
. We look at Betti tables of
R
-modules of finite projective dimension and codimension at least
c
. So let
\boldsymbol{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\dots,\varnothing,c,\infty,\dots)
with
c
in position
0
and
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
the corresponding rational cone; see (
5.3
). It is thus the positive rational cone spanned by
\beta(\boldsymbol{t})
where
\boldsymbol{t}
is any degree sequence of the form

(\dots-\infty,t_{0},\dots,t_{l},\infty,\dots)

with
c\leq l\leq\dim R
. We know that
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
, by Theorem
6.2
.

Let
\operatorname{Hilb}_{R}(t)
be the Hilbert series of
R
, and for
\beta\in\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
, set

\operatorname{Hilb}_{\beta}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Hilb}_{R}(t)g_{\beta}(t),\quad\text{where}\quad g_{\beta}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}\beta_{i,j}t^{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t,t^{-1}]\,.

Since
R
admits a linear system of parameters,
\operatorname{Hilb}_{R}(t)=f_{R}(t)/(1-t)^{d}
for some polynomial
f_{R}(t)
and
e(R)=f_{R}(1)
. If
M\in\operatorname{grmod}R
has finite projective dimension, then
\operatorname{Hilb}_{\beta}(t)=\operatorname{Hilb}_{M}(t)
, where
\beta=\beta^{R}(M)
; see
[
3
, Lemma 4.1.13]
.

For
\beta\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\beta(\boldsymbol{t})
with
\boldsymbol{t}
a degree sequence as above we have that
g_{\beta}(t)
is divisible by
(1-t)^{l}
, with
l=\operatorname{codim}(\boldsymbol{t})
, and no higher power. In particular, we have that
g_{\beta}(t)
is divisible by
(1-t)^{c}
for all
\beta\in\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
. It thus makes sense to define

e(\beta)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}e(R)\left(\frac{g_{\beta}(t)}{(1-t)^{c}}\right)_{t=1}\quad\text{for $\beta\in\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}$.}

The function
e(-)
depends on the chosen

and
e(\beta(\boldsymbol{t}))=0
if
\operatorname{codim}(\boldsymbol{t})\geq c+1
.

Recall that one has

\operatorname{codim}_{R}M=\dim R-\dim_{R}M=d-\dim_{R}M

for any
M\in\operatorname{grmod}R
of finite projective dimension; see
[
18
, Théorème 2]
.

Claim
.

If
M\in\operatorname{grmod}R
has finite projective dimension, and codimension at least
c
, then
e(\beta^{R}(M))=e_{d-c}(M)
; see
4.4
.

Indeed, since
M
has dimension at most
d-c
, one has that

\operatorname{Hilb}_{M}(t)=\frac{f_{M}(t)}{(1-t)^{d-c}}

for some polynomial
f_{M}(t)
and then
e_{d-c}(M)=f_{M}(1)
. On the other hand, for
\beta=\beta^{R}(M)
, the discussion above yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}_{M}(t)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hilb}_{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hilb}_{R}(t)g_{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{R}(t)}{(1-t)^{d}}g_{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(f_{R}(t)g_{\beta}(t)/(1-t)^{c}}{(1-t)^{d-c}}\,.

Thus
f_{M}(t)=f_{R}(t)g_{\beta}(t)/(1-t)^{c}
; evaluating this equality at
t=1
yields the claim.

Once we know that the multiplicity of a module can be extracted from its Hilbert series, the rest of the argument is as in
[
1
, Theorem 4.6]
.
∎

We write
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{short}}(R)
for the positive cone in

, defined in (

), spanned by

as

ranges over the short complexes over

; see

.

Corollary 6.4
.

With
R
and
A
as in

, there is an equality
\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{short}}(R)=\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{short}}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{c}
be the codimension sequence
(\dots,\varnothing,\dim R,\dim R,\dots)
with the first occurrence of

is in degree
0
.
Then
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)=\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(A)
, by Theorem

. Intersecting these cones with the cone

yields the stated equality.
∎

In proving Theorem
6.2
it will be expedient to use the notion of depth for complexes, and some results from
[
14
]
. In what follows we state the necessary definitions and results for local rings, with the implicit understanding that the corresponding statements in the graded context also hold.

6.5
.

Let
R
be a local ring with residue field
k
. By the
depth
of an
R
-complex
M
of
R
-modules we mean the least integer
i
, possibly
\pm\infty
, such that
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(k,M)\neq 0
; see
[
8
]
for alternative descriptions. Given a bounded complex
M
let
\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(M)
denote the supremum of integers
i
such that
\operatorname{H}_{i}(M)\neq 0
. For such an
R
-complex
M
there is an inequality

(6.6)

0pt_{R}M\geq-\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(M)\,.

Equality holds if and only if
0pt_{R}\operatorname{H}_{s}(M)=0
for
s\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(M)
. These claims are contained in
[
14
, Proposition 2.7(2) and Theorem 2.3]
.

One version of the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula for complexes reads: If
F
is finite free
R
-complex with
\operatorname{H}(F)\neq 0
, then for any
R
-complex
M
one has

(6.7)

\displaystyle 0pt_{R}(F\otimes_{R}M)

\displaystyle=0pt_{R}M-\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}F

\displaystyle=0pt_{R}M-0ptR+0pt_{R}F\,.

The first equality is
[
14
, Corollary 2.2]
, and the second follows from the first applied with
M=R
.

Lemma 6.8
.

Fix a codimension sequence
\boldsymbol{c}
for
R
, an
R
-complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)
and a finitely generated
R
-module
U
satisfying
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 0ptR_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
. For any integer
i
, if

c_{i}<\infty
, or
0pt_{R}U\geq 0ptR
, then

\operatorname{codim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F\otimes_{R}U)\geq c_{i}\,.

When
R
is a finitely generated graded
k
-algebra, and
F
and
U
are also graded, then it suffices that the condition on depth holds for
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}(R)
.

Proof.

The key observation is the following:

Claim
.

Assume
0pt_{R}U\geq 0ptR
as well. Then
\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(F\otimes_{R}U)\leq\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(F)
.

Indeed, since
\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(F)
does not increase on localization, we can further localize at prime minimal in the support of
\operatorname{H}_{s}(F\otimes_{R}U)
, for
s\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(F\otimes_{R}U)
, and assume it has nonzero finite length. Then the claim about equality in (
6.6
) yields the first equality below:

\displaystyle-\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(F\otimes_{R}U)

\displaystyle=0pt_{R}(F\otimes_{R}U)

\displaystyle=0pt_{R}U-0ptR+0pt_{R}F

\displaystyle\geq 0pt_{R}F

\displaystyle\geq-\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(F)\,.

The second equality is (
6.7
). The first inequality holds by our hypotheses on
U
, and another application of (
6.6
) yields the last one. This settles the claim.

Fix an
i
. The case
c_{i}=\infty
and
0pt_{R}U\geq 0ptR
follows from the claim.

Suppose
c_{i}<\infty
and choose
\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Proj}(R)
such that
0pt\mathfrak{q}<c_{i}
. Since
c_{i}\leq\dim R
it follows that
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{m}
. The choice of
\mathfrak{q}
means that
\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}(F_{\mathfrak{q}})<i
, because
F
is in
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)
, and we have to verify that

\sup\operatorname{H}_{*}((F\otimes_{R}U)_{\mathfrak{q}})<i\,.

This is covered by the claim, applied to the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
, the
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
-complex
F_{\mathfrak{q}}
, and
U_{\mathfrak{q}}
. The applies since
\mathfrak{q}
is a non-maximal prime.

In the graded context, it suffices to observe that the conditions on depth for
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}(R)
imply the condition for all primes in
\operatorname{Spec}R\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
by
[
3
, Theorem 1.5.9]
.
∎

6.9
.

Let
\boldsymbol{c}
be a codimension sequence of the form

\boldsymbol{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\dots,\varnothing,\varnothing,c_{a},\dots,c_{b},\infty,\infty,\dots)

with
0\leq c_{a}\leq c_{b}\leq\dim R
. Consider the sequence

\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\dots,\varnothing,\varnothing,c_{a},\dots,c_{b},d,d,\dots)

where

. Evidently,
\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}\leq\boldsymbol{c}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

With
\boldsymbol{c}
and
\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}
as above, if
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}}(R)\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}}_{d}
, then
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
.

Proof.

Pick
F\in\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)
. Then one has

\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}F=0ptR-0pt_{R}F\leq d+b\,.

Consequently, one has

\beta_{i,j}^{R}(F)=0\quad\text{for $i\geq d+b+1$ and all $j$.}

This fact will be used further below.

Since
\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}\leq\boldsymbol{c}
, we have
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}}(R)
, and also for
A
. Thus, by hypothesis,
\beta^{R}(F)
is in
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}}(A)
. Theorem
5.8
gives a decomposition

\beta^{R}(F)=\sum_{n}r_{n}\beta^{A}(G^{n})

as a finite sum, with
r_{n}>0
and
G^{n}\in\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}}(A)
, with
G^{n}
the shifted resolution of an
A
-module. That is,
\operatorname{H}_{i}(G^{n})=0
for all but one value, say
i_{n}
, of
i
. Moreover, by the definition of
\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}
, we have

\operatorname{codim}_{A}\operatorname{H}_{i_{n}}(G^{n})=\begin{cases}&c_{i_{n}}\text{ or more if $a\leq i_{n}\leq b$}\\
&\dim A\text{ if $i_{n}>b$.}\end{cases}

In particular, when
i_{n}>b
the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula yields

\beta_{d+b+1,j}(G^{n})\neq 0\quad\text{for some $j$.}

But then
\beta^{R}_{d+b+1,j}(F)\neq 0
as well, a contradiction. Thus
a\leq i_{n}\leq b
for each
n
, that is to say,
G^{n}
is in
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(A)
.
∎

The last part of Theorem
6.2
holds over any field

, so we record it separately.

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
and
\boldsymbol{c}
a codimension sequence for
R
.
If
c_{i}\neq\infty
for all
i
, or
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\supseteq\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
.

Proof.

With
A
as in
6.1
, one has
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(A)=\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}_{d}
, by Theorem
5.8
, so the desired inclusion is that
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)\supseteq\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(A)
. When
c_{i}<\infty
, this is verified in the course of the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 9.1]
; see in particular,
[
4
, Lemma 9.5, 9.6]
and
[
17
]
.
We give the argument, for one key equality in the proof was not justified in
op. cit.

One can assume
c_{i}=\varnothing
for
i<0
and
c_{0}
is finite. Since
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(A)
is spanned by (shifts) of the Betti tables of finitely generated graded
A
-modules, it suffices to prove that if
M
is a finitely generated with
\operatorname{codim}_{A}M\geq c_{0}
, then
\beta^{A}(M)
is in
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)
. By
[
17
]
, pulling back
M
along a
k
-algebra automorphism of
A
, one can suppose that the
R
-modules
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(R,M)
have finite length for each
i\geq 1
. Let
F
be the minimal free resolution of
M
over
A
, so the finite free complex of
R
-modules
R\otimes_{A}F
satisfies

\beta^{R}(R\otimes_{A}F)=\beta^{A}(F)=\beta^{A}(M)\,.

Since
\operatorname{H}_{i}(R\otimes_{A}F)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(R,M)
has finite length for
i\geq 1
, and
c_{i}<\infty
, one gets

\operatorname{codim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(R\otimes_{A}F)\geq\dim R\geq c_{i}\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

It remains to observe that

\operatorname{codim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{0}(R\otimes_{A}F)=\operatorname{codim}_{R}(R\otimes_{A}M)=\operatorname{codim}_{A}(M)\,.

The equality on the right is by Lemma
A.1
; this is glossed over in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 9.1]
. This completes the proof in the case when each

is finite.

Suppose that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. Fix an
A
-complex
G
in
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(A)
and consider the
R
-complex
F\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R\otimes_{A}G
.
Evidently
\beta^{R}(F)=\beta^{A}(G)
, so for the desired inclusion one has only to verify that
F
is in
\mathbf{B}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)
. Since
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and finite as an

-module for any finitely generated

Proof of Theorem

for each

.

Let
(U^{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
be a lim Ulrich sequence of graded
R
-modules provided by Theorem
4.17
. This is where we need the hypothesis that the characteristic of
k
is positive. Fix an
R
-complex
F
in
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{c}}(R)
and consider the

F\otimes_{R}U^{n}
.

For any

\operatorname{grmod}R

\operatorname{codim}_{A}M\geq\operatorname{codim}_{R}M

yields that, viewed as an

u_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\nu_{R}(U^{n})

K(\boldsymbol{x};A)\xrightarrow{\sim}k

This proves that

Characteristic zero

Next we explain how to deduce the characteristic zero case of Theorem
6.2
from that of positive characteristic. The key is the following result, whose proof is a standard argument.

Lemma 6.14
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero,
R
a finitely generated graded
k
-algebra, and fix
F
in
\mathcal{F}(R)
. There exists a field
k_{p}
of positive characteristic
p
, a finitely generated graded
k_{p}
-algebra
R_{p}
, and an
R_{p}
-complex
F_{p}
in
\mathcal{F}(R_{p})
such that

\displaystyle\beta^{R_{p}}(F_{p})=\beta^{R}(F)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\operatorname{codim}_{R_{p}}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F_{p})=\operatorname{codim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)\quad\text{for each $i$.}

Moreover, if the
k
-algebra
R
admits a linear normalization, then the
k_{p}
-algebra
R_{p}
can be chosen to admit one as well.

Proof.

Write
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/J
, where
J=(f_{1},\dots,f_{t})
is a homogenous ideal. Pick a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}
-subalgebra
W
of
k
such that the coefficients of the
f_{i}
and the coefficients in the entries in the differentials of
F
are in
W
.
Thus we have a graded ring
R_{W}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(f_{1},\dots,f_{t})
over
W
viewed as a ring concentrated in degree
0
, and such that
R=k\otimes_{W}R_{W}
.

When
R
admits a linear Noether normalization, then by
[
12
, (2,1,8)(h)]
, one can invert elements in
W
to ensure that the inclusion
W[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]\subseteq R_{W}
is module-finite, with cokernel
W
-free. It follows that for all maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
W
base-change along
W\to W/\mathfrak{m}
induces a map

(W/\mathfrak{m})[x_{1},...,x_{d}]\to R_{W/\mathfrak{m}}\,,

that is a linear Noether normalization of
R_{W/\mathfrak{m}}
.

Write
\beta^{R}(F)=(\beta_{i,j})
. Evidently there exists a diagram (not necessarily a complex) of finite free
R_{W}
-modules

F_{W}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\cdots\longrightarrow\bigoplus R_{W}(-j)^{\beta_{i+1,j}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus R_{W}(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus R_{W}(-j)^{\beta_{i-1,j}}\longrightarrow\cdots

such that
F=k\otimes_{W}F_{W}
. With
Q
the field of fractions of
W
one has an isomorphism

k\otimes_{W}F_{W}\cong k\otimes_{Q}(Q\otimes_{W}F_{W})

In particular,
Q\otimes_{W}F_{W}
is a complex of
R_{Q}
-modules and the codimension of the
R_{Q}
-module
\operatorname{H}_{i}(Q\otimes_{W}F_{W})
equals that of the
R
-module
\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)
, for each
i
.

Inverting further elements in
W
, one can ensure that
F_{W}
is complex and for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
W
and integer
i
one has

\operatorname{codim}_{R_{W/\mathfrak{m}}}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F_{W/\mathfrak{m}})=\operatorname{codim}_{R_{W}}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F_{W})=\operatorname{codim}_{R}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)\,.

Indeed, this follows from
[
12
, (2.1.14)(g) last sentence, and (2.3.9)(c)]
; the first reference tells us we can localize at elements in
W
to preserve the annihilators of the homology modules (note that
k\otimes_{W}\operatorname{H}_{i}(F_{W})=\operatorname{H}_{i}(F)
), and the second one says that by
inverting more elements in
W
the height of an ideal is preserved; see (2.3.3) and (2.3.4) in
op. cit.
for notation.

Finally, pick a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
W
such that
\operatorname{H}(F_{W})\otimes_{R_{W}}W/\mathfrak{m}W
is nonzero and set
F_{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W/\mathfrak{m}\otimes_{W}F_{W}
. It is clear the
R_{p}
-complex
F_{p}
has the same Betti numbers as
F
; the codimensions of their homology modules is also the same, by construction. It remains to note that
W/\mathfrak{m}
has positive characteristic
p
.
∎

Appendix A
Codimension

In this section
A\hookrightarrow R
is an integral extension of noetherian rings. For any finitely generated
A
-module
M
, it is easy to see that there is an inequality

\operatorname{codim}_{R}(R\otimes_{A}M)\leq\operatorname{codim}_{A}M\,.

Equality holds when, for instance, the going-down theorem holds for the extension
A\to R
, but not always; see Example
A.2
. Here is one other situation in which equality holds; this comes up in the proof of Proposition
6.11
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
A\to R
as above and
M
a finitely generated
A
-module with
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{A}M
finite and such that

\ell_{R}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(R,M)<\infty\quad\text{for all $i\geq 1$.}

There is an equality
\operatorname{codim}_{R}(R\otimes_{A}M)=\operatorname{codim}_{A}M
.

Proof.

One has to only to verify that
\operatorname{codim}_{R}(R\otimes_{A}M)\geq\operatorname{codim}_{A}M
, that is to say

0pt\mathfrak{q}\geq\operatorname{codim}_{A}M\quad\text{for all $\mathfrak{q}$ in $\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(R\otimes_{A}M)$.}

Let
f\colon\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{Spec}A
be the map induced by
A\to R
. Then

\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(R\otimes_{A}M)=f^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}_{A}M)\,.

Fix
\mathfrak{q}
minimal in
f^{-1}(\operatorname{Supp}_{A}M)
. If
\mathfrak{q}
is the maximal ideal of
R
, then

0pt\mathfrak{q}=\dim R=\dim A\geq\operatorname{codim}_{A}M\,,

as desired. We can thus assume that
\mathfrak{q}
is not maximal in
R
.

Set
\mathfrak{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathfrak{q}\cap A
; this is in
\operatorname{Supp}_{A}M
and so contains a minimal element, say
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
, in
\operatorname{Supp}_{A}M
. It suffices to verify

0pt\mathfrak{q}\geq 0pt\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\,.

Since
\mathfrak{q}
is not maximal in
\operatorname{Spec}R
, the hypotheses yield

\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(R,M)_{\mathfrak{q}}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}_{i}(R_{\mathfrak{q}},M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0\quad\text{for $i\geq 1$.}

Thus the
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
-module
(R\otimes_{A}M)_{\mathfrak{q}}
has finite projective dimension; it is also of nonzero finite length, because
\mathfrak{q}
is minimal in
\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(R\otimes_{A}M)
. This gives the first and the second equalities below:

\displaystyle\dim R_{\mathfrak{q}}

\displaystyle=0ptR_{\mathfrak{q}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R\otimes_{A}M)_{\mathfrak{q}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}M_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\dim A_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}\,.

The fourth one holds as
A_{\mathfrak{p}}\to R_{\mathfrak{q}}
is local and, as
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-modules,
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
are Tor-independent. The inequality is clear. The last equality holds hold because
M_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}
has finite length, since
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is minimal in
\operatorname{Supp}_{A}M
. Thus
0pt\mathfrak{q}\geq 0pt\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
.
∎

Example A.2
.

Let
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[x,y,z]/(x,y)\cap(z)
and
A
the subring
k[x+z,y]
. Then for
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A/yA
one has

\operatorname{codim}_{A}M=1\quad\text{whereas}\quad\operatorname{codim}_{A}(R\otimes_{A}M)=\operatorname{codim}_{R}(R/yR)=0\,.

In geometric language,
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is the union of a line and a plane, both passing through the origin,
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is a plane, and
\operatorname{Spec}(R)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)
any linear map that is finite (any generic such map would do). Then for one “bad” line in
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, its inverse image in
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is a union of the exceptional line in
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
and another line passing through the origin. This gives an example where the codimension in
A
is 1 but the codimension in
R
drops to
0
.

Sufficient conditions for solvability of linear Diophantine equations and Frobenius numbers
Eteri Samsonadze

Abstract
. A linear Diophantine equation ∑ i = 1 n a i ​ x i = b \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}=b is considered, where a 1 , a 2 , … , a n a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n} are coprime natural numbers, b b is an non-negative integer, x i x_{i} ( i = 1 , 2 , … , n ) (i=1,2,...,n) are non-negative integers. It is proved that if [ b M ] ≥ [ n − ∑ i = 1 n a i + r M ] \left[\frac{b}{M}\right]\geq\left[n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+r}{M}\right] , then this equation is solvable (here M M is the least common multiple of the numbers a 1 , a 2 , … , a n a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n} ; r r is the remainder of b b modulo M M ). With the aid of this result, it is shown that the considered equation is solvable if either b ≥ n ​ M − ∑ i = 1 n a i b\geq nM-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i} or b ≥ ( n − 1 ) ​ M b\geq(n-1)M . The case where ∑ i = 1 n a i ≥ ( n − 2 ) ​ M + 2 \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\geq(n-2)M+2 is considered closer. It is proved that, in that case, the equation is solvable if b > ( n − 1 ) ​ M − ∑ i = 1 n a i b>(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i} . If, in addition, r > ( n − 1 ) ​ M − ∑ i = 1 n a i r>(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i} , then it has M n − 1 a 1 ​ a 2 ​ … ​ a n ​ C b ′ + n − 1 n − 1 \frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}C^{n-1}_{b^{\prime}+n-1} integer non-negative solutions; it is also shown that if b ≥ M b\geq M and r ≤ ( n − 1 ) ​ M − ∑ i = 1 n a i r\leq(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i} , then the number of solutions of the equation is greater than or equal to M n − 1 a 1 ​ a 2 ​ … ​ a n ​ C b ′ + n − 2 n − 1 \frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}C^{n-1}_{b^{\prime}+n-2} ; here b ′ = [ b M ] b^{\prime}=\left[\frac{b}{M}\right] (and C m k C^{k}_{m} denotes the binomial coefficient ( m k ) {\begin{pmatrix}m\\ k\\ \end{pmatrix}} ). Moreover, it is proved that if the numbers a 1 , a 2 , … , a n a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n} are coprime, then F ​ r ​ o ​ b ​ ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a n ) ≤ c Frob(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})\leq c , where c c is the smallest among the numbers a i ​ a j − a i − a j a_{i}a_{j}-a_{i}-a_{j} with ( a i , a j ) = 1 (a_{i},a_{j})=1 ( 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n 1\leq i<j\leq n ). Key words and phrases : linear Diophantine equation; Frobenius number. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 11D04, 11D07.



Consider a linear
Diophantine equation

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}=b,

where
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
are coprime natural numbers,
b
is an integer non-negative number,
x_{i}

(i=1,2,...,n)
are integer non-negative numbers.

Proposition 1.
Let
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
be coprime natural numbers. Then we have the inequality

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\leq(n-1)M+1,

where
M
is is the least common multiple of the numbers
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
.

Proof.

We have
a_{i}=\frac{M}{k_{i}}
, where
k_{i}\in\mathbf{N}

(i=1,2,...,n)

(a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq...\leq a_{n})
. Therefore, if
a_{1}\neq M
and
a_{i}=M
for
i>1
, then
a_{i}=1
(as it follows from the fact the numbers
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
are coprime). Hence, in that case, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=(n-1)M+1.

If numbers
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{l}
are different from
M

(2\leq l\leq n)
, then
a_{i}\leq\frac{M}{2}
for
1\leq i\leq l
(at that, at least one of the numbers
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{l}
is less than
\frac{M}{2}
since, again,
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
are coprime). Therefore, in that case, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}<\frac{M}{2}l+(n-l)M=M(n-\frac{l}{2})\leq M(n-1).

∎

Formula (2) implies that

n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+r}{M}\geq\frac{M-1-r}{M},

where
r
is the remainder of
b
modulo
M
. Since
r\leq M-1
, this implies that

(3)

n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+r}{M}\geq 0.

Introduce the following notation:

(4)

s=\left[n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+r}{M}\right]

where
\left[x\right]
denotes the integer part of a number
x
, i.e., the greatest integer that is not greater than
x
.

In
[
2
]
, we have shown that, for the number
P(b)
of integer non-negative solutions of equation (1), the following equality is valid:

(5)

P(b)=\sum_{k=0}^{s}l_{k}\overline{C}_{b^{\prime}+n-1-k}^{n-1}=l_{0}\overline{C}_{b^{\prime}+n-1}^{n-1}+l_{1}\overline{C}_{b^{\prime}+n-1-2}^{n-1}+...l_{s}\overline{C}_{b^{\prime}+n-1-s}^{n-1},

where
b^{\prime}=\left[\frac{b}{M}\right]
,
l_{k}

(k=0,1,...,s)
is the number of integer non-negative solutions of the system

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}t_{i}=r+Mk;~0\leq t_{i}\leq\frac{M}{a_{i}}-1~(i=1,2,...,n),

while
\overline{C}^{t}_{m}
denotes
C^{t}_{m}
if
m\geq t
, and denotes 0 otherwise (here

C^{t}_{m}
is the binomial coefficient
{\begin{pmatrix}t\\
k\\
\end{pmatrix}}
).

Since
l_{i}\geq 0

(i=0,1,...,s)
, formula (5) implies that equation (1) is solvable if and only if there is
l_{k}\neq 0
(
k\leq s
) such that
k\leq b^{\prime}
.

It was shown in
[
2
]
that

l_{0}=P(r),

l_{k}=P(r+kM)-C_{n}^{1}P(r+(k-1)M)+C_{n}^{2}P(r+(k-2)M)+

(6)

...+(-1)^{k}C_{n}^{k}P(r),~(k=1,2,..,s),

and

(7)

\sum_{i=0}^{s}l_{i}=\frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}.

Observe that formula (5) implies the obvious fact that if
P(r)\neq 0
, then equation (1) is solvable.

Since
l_{i}\geq 0

(i=0,1,...,s)
, formula (7) implies that at least one of
l_{0},l_{1},...,l_{s}
is different from zero. It follows that at least one of the numbers

P(r),P(r+M),...,P(r+sM)

is different from zero (otherwise equality (6) would imply that
l_{0}=l_{1}=...=l_{s}=0.)

Theorem 2.
If
\left[\frac{b}{M}\right]\geq\left[n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+r}{M}\right]
, then equation (1) is solvable.

Proof.

If
b^{\prime}\geq s
, then
\overline{C}_{b^{\prime}+n-1-k}^{n-1}\neq 0
, for
k=0,1,...,s
. Since at least one of the numbers
l_{0},l_{1},...,l_{s}
is different from zero, formula (5) implies that
P(b)\neq 0
.
∎

Theorem 3.
If
b\geq nM-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
, then equation (1) is solvable.

Proof.

If
b\geq nM-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
, then
\frac{b}{M}\geq n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}{M}
. This implies that
\left[\frac{b}{M}\right]\geq\left[n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+r}{M}\right]
. Therefore, from Theorem 1 we obtain that equation (1) is solvable.
∎

Theorem 4.
If
b\geq(n-1)M
, then equation (1) is solvable.

Proof.

If
b\geq(n-1)M
, then
\frac{b}{M}\geq n-1
. The inequality
n-1\geq s
implies that
\frac{b}{M}\geq s
and hence
\left[\frac{b}{M}\right]\geq s
. Therefore, by Theorem 1, equation (1) is solvable.
∎

As is well known (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
), if
b>a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2})
, then the equation

a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{a}=b

is solvable if
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are coprime. If, for some integers
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
, one has

a_{1}y_{1}+a_{2}y_{2}=b,

then obviously the
n
-tuple
(y_{1},y_{2},0,0,...0)
is a solution of the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}=b

provided that
n\geq 2
.
These arguments
imply

Theorem 5.
Equation (1) is solvable if, among coprime numbers
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
, one can choose coprime numbers
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
such that

b>a_{i}a_{j}-(a_{i}+a_{j}).

For instance, the equation

3x_{1}+4x_{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}=b

is solvable provided that
b\geq 6
.

Consider the case where

(8)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\geq(n-2)M+2,

or, equivalently,

(9)

(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\leq M-2.

Formula (8) implies that

n-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+r}{M}\leq n-\frac{(n-2)M+2+r}{M}=2-\frac{2+r}{M}\leq 1.

Thus, if condition (8) is satisfied, then
s=0
or
s=1
. As it is not hard to verify,
s=0
if
r>r_{0}
; and
s=1
if
r\leq r_{0}
. Here

(10)

r_{0}=(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}.

Formulas (5) and (7) imply that, for any non-negative integer
b
,

(11)

P(b)=\frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}C^{n-1}_{b^{\prime}+n-1}

if
r>r_{0}
; and

(12)

P(b)=P(r)C^{n-1}_{b^{\prime}+n-1}+(\frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}-P(r))\overline{C}_{b^{\prime}+n-2}^{n-1}

if
r\leq r_{0}
.

Since

\overline{C}^{n-1}_{b^{\prime}+n-2}=\frac{(b^{\prime}+n-2)(b^{\prime}+n-1)~...~b^{\prime}}{(n-1)!},

for any integer non-negative
b
, and

C^{n-1}_{b^{\prime}+n-2}-\frac{(b^{\prime}+n-2)(b^{\prime}+n-1)~...~b^{\prime}}{(n-1)!}=C^{n-2}_{b^{\prime}+n-2},

equality (12) implies that

(13)

P(b)=P(r)C^{n-2}_{b^{\prime}+n-2}+\frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}\overline{C}^{n-2}_{b^{\prime}+n-2},

for
r\leq r_{0}
.

Assume now that
b^{\prime}\geq 1
, or, equivalently,
b\geq M
. Formula (13) implies that equation (1) is solvable if
r\leq r_{0}
, for any integer non-negative
b
. Moreover, formula (11) implies that this equation is solvable also in the case where
r>r_{0}
. Thus, equation (1) is solvable for any
b\geq M
. Formula (10) implies that it is solvable also for
b\in(r_{0},M)
. Therefore, equation (1) is solvable for any
b>r_{0}
.

Thus, formulas (11) and (13) imply

Theorem 6.
If
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\geq(n-2)M+2,
and
b>(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},
then equation (1) is solvable. At that, if
r>(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
, there are precisely
\frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}C_{b^{\prime}+n-1}^{n-1}
integer non-negative solutions. If
b\geq M
, but
r\leq(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
, then the number of solutions is greater or equal to
\frac{M^{n-1}}{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{n}}C_{b^{\prime}+n-2}^{n-2}
.

Thus, under condition (8), if equation (1) has no solution for
b=(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
, then the number
b_{0}=(n-1)M-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
is the greatest integer such that equation (1) has no solution.

In literature, an integer
b_{0}
is called the Frobenius number
Frob(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})
if
b=b_{0}
is the greatest integer such that the equation

(14)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}=b

has no solution. The problem of finding Frobenius numbers is closely related to problems from various fields of mathematics. A lot of papers are devoted to this problem. The explicit formula for Frobenius numbers are found only for the particular cases
n=2
(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
) and
n=3

[
3
]
. Namely, it is proved that

(15)

Frob(a_{1},a_{2})=a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2})

if
(a_{1},a_{2})=1
.

Formula (15) and Theorem 6 imply

Theorem 7.
Let
(a_{1},a_{2})=1
. Then

(16)

Frob(a_{1},a_{2},a_{1}a_{2},a_{1}a_{2},...,a_{1}a_{2})=a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2}).

Proof.

Condition (8) is satisfied for equation

(17)

a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{1}a_{2}x_{3}+a_{1}a_{2}x_{4}+...+a_{1}a_{2}x_{n}=b,

(here
M=a_{1}a_{2}
, and
a_{1}+a_{2}+(n-2)a_{1}a_{2}\geq(n-2)M+2
). Therefore, Theorem 6 implies that this equation is solvable if
b>(n-1)a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2}+(n-2)a_{1}a_{2})
, or, equivalently,
b>a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2})
.

If
b=a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2})
, then equation (17) has no solution since the equality

a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{1}a_{2}x_{3}+a_{1}a_{2}x_{4}+...+a_{1}a_{2}x_{n}=a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2})

implies that
x_{3}=x_{4}=...=x_{n}=0
. Moreover, the equation

a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}=a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2})

has no solution if
(a_{1},a_{2})=1
, as it follows from formula (15).

Thus
b=a_{1}a_{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2})
is the greatest integer such that equation (17) has no solution.
∎

Theorem 5 implies

Theorem 8.
If numbers
a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}
are coprime, then

Frob(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n})\leq c,

where
c
is the smallest among the numbers
a_{i}a_{j}-a_{i}-a_{j}
with
(a_{i},a_{j})=1
(
1\leq i<j\leq n
).

Example 9.

Frob(2,4,5,5,6)\leq 3
since
min(2\cdot 5-2-5,4\cdot 5-4-5,5\cdot 6-5-6)=3
. Moreover, the equation

2x_{1}+4x_{2}+5x_{3}+5x_{4}+...+6x_{5}=b

is solvable for
b=2
. Therefore,
Frob(2,4,5,5,6)=3
.

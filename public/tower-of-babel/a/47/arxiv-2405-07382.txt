Total coloring graphs with large maximum degree
Aseem Dalal, Jessica McDonald, Songling Shan

Abstract
We prove that for any graph G G , the total chromatic number of G G is at most Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 2 ​ ⌈ | V ⁡ ( G ) | Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 ⌉ \Delta(G)+2\lceil\frac{|V(G)|}{\Delta(G)+1}\rceil . This saves one color in comparison with a result of Hind from 1992. In particular, our result says that if Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 1 2 ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | \Delta(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}|V(G)| , then G G has a total colouring using at most Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 4 \Delta(G)+4 colors. When G G is regular and has a sufficient number of vertices, we can actually save an additional two colors. Specifically, we prove that for any 0 < ε < 1 0<\varepsilon<1 , there exists n 0 ∈ ℕ n_{0}\in\mathbb{N} such that: if G G is an r r -regular graph on n ≥ n 0 n\geq n_{0} vertices with r ≥ 1 2 ​ ( 1 + ε ) ​ n r\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+\varepsilon)n , then χ T ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 2 \chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2 . This confirms the Total Coloring Conjecture for such graphs G G .

Introduction

In this paper, all graphs are simple unless otherwise explicitly stated. For terms not defined here, we follow
[
22
]
.

A
total coloring
of a graph
G
is an assignment of colors to the vertices and edges of
G
such that adjacent vertices receive different colors, adjacent edges receive different colors, and if any edge
e
is incident to any vertex
v
, then
e,v
also receive different colors.
The
total chromatic number
of
G
, denoted
\chi_{T}(G)
, is the minimum number of colors needed to totally color
G
. Since a vertex of maximum degree must receive different colors from all its incident edges, every graph
G
satisfies
\chi_{T}(G)\geq\Delta(G)+1
. On the other hand, Behzad
[
1
, Conjecture 1, p.44]
, and independently Vizing
[
21
]
, proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Total Coloring Conjecture)
.

Every graph
G
has
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
.

There has been extensive work towards the Total Coloring Conjecture; even the planar case, which is not yet completely verified, has a significant literature. An early survey of total coloring can be found in the 1995 book of Jensen and Toft
[
14
]
(Section 4.9); see also the survey paper of Geethaa, Narayananb, and Somasundaram
[
9
]
for a more modern account.

In this paper we focus on the Total Coloring Conjecture for graphs with large maximum degree. For any graph
G
with sufficiently large maximum degree, Molloy and Reed
[
16
]
showed that
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+10^{26}
. When the maximum degree is large in comparison with
|V(G)|
, this can be improved. In particular, Hind
[
13
]
proved that
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2\lceil\frac{|V(G)|}{\Delta(G)}\rceil+1
for any graph
G
. Our first result in this paper is to improve Hind’s bound by (at least) one.

Theorem 1
.

Every graph
G
satisfies
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2\left\lceil\frac{|V(G)|}{\Delta(G)+1}\right\rceil
.

When
\Delta(G)\geq\frac{3}{4}|V(G)|
, Hilton and Hind
[
12
]
have already confirmed Conjecture
1
. However, for graphs with
\Delta(G)<\frac{3}{4}|V(G)
, Theorem
1
offers an improved approximation to the Total Coloring Conjecture. In particular, for all graphs with
\Delta(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}|V(G)|
, Theorem
1
says that
G
has a total coloring using at most
\Delta(G)+4
colors.

When the maximum degree of
G
is large in comparison to
|V(G)|

and

G
is regular, more can be said. All
r
-regular graphs
G
with
r>\frac{2}{3}|V(G)|+\frac{23}{6}
are known to satisfy the Total Coloring Conjecture (Xie and He
[
24
]
and Xie and Yang
[
25
]
); this result is built on earlier work by Chetwynd, Hilton and Zaho
[
4
]
and also Chew
[
5
]
. Our second result in this paper is to show that for graphs on a sufficient number of vertices, this fraction of
\tfrac{2}{3}
can be lowered all the way to
\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Theorem 2
.

For any
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that: if
G
is an
r
-regular graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices such that
r\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+\varepsilon)n
, then
\chi_{T}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we use a Vizing-fan type structure to prove two specific
edge-coloring
results: one for a hypergraph that is nearly a graph, and one for a graph that is nearly bipartite. In Section 3 we use the hypergraph edge-colouring result, along with a classic result of Hajnal and Szemeredi
[
11
]
on
equitable vertex-colorings
, to quickly prove Theorem
1
. The nearly-biparitite edge-coloring result is needed (along with the hypergraph edge-coloring result) for our proof of Theorem
2
. Section 4 contains some other needed preliminaries for Theorem
2
, divided into two subsections on matchings (in the complement of a regular graph), and edge-colorings (which are somewhat equitable). At the start of Section 5 we state one previous result by the third author, about a vertex partition that will be essential for us, and then we present our proof of Theorem
2
.

2
Two edge-coloring results

A
hypergraph

H
is a set of vertices
V(H)
and a set of edges
E(H)
, each of which is a subset of
V(H)
of size at least two. In this paper we assume that all hypergraphs are simple, meaning that any two edges intersect in at most one vertex. A hypergraph
H
is said to have rank
c
if the largest set in
E(H)
has size
c
; if all edges in
H
have size 2 then
H
is a graph. Given a hypergraph
H
, a vertex
v
has
degree
, denoted
d_{H}(v)
, equal to the number of edges of
H
it is a member of;
\Delta(H)
denotes the maximum degree of
H
. A
matching

M
in a hypergraph
H
is a set of edges, none of which share any common vertices; a vertex is
saturated
by
M
if it is contained in an edge of
M
;
V(M)
denotes the set of all vertices in
V(H)
that are saturated by
M
, and;
M
is
perfect
if
V(H)=V(M)
. Given an integer
k\geq 1
, a
k
-edge-coloring
of a hypergraph
H
(with at least one edge) is an assignment of the colors
1,\ldots,k
to the edges of
H
such that two edges sharing a common vertex must receive different colors. For a vertex
x
in a hypergraph
H
, by
\delta_{H}(x)
we mean the set of all edges containing
x
in
H
; by
N_{H}(x)
we mean the set of all vertices in an edge with
x
in
H
.

Given a hypgergraph
H
, a partial
k
-edge-coloring of
H
is an edge-coloring of some sub-hypergraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
. For
\varphi
any partial
k
-edge-coloring of
H
, and any vertex
v\in V(H)
, denote by
\overline{\varphi}(v)
the set of colors
missing
at
v
, that is, the set of all colors in
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
that are not used on an edge incident to
v
under
\varphi
. For a vertex set
X\subseteq V(H)
, define
\overline{\varphi}(X)=\bigcup_{v\in X}\overline{\varphi}(v)
to be the set of missing colors of
X
. Given any pair of distinct colors
\alpha,\gamma\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and given a vertex
v\in V(H)
missing at least one of
\alpha,\gamma
under
\varphi
, we can consider the
maximal
(\alpha,\gamma)
-chain
P
beginning at
v
, that is, the component containing
v
of
the sub-hypergraph induced by edges colored
\alpha
or
\gamma
under
\varphi
. The
end-vertices
of such a chain are the vertices that have degree one in the chain.
If
H
is a graph, then a maximal
(\alpha,\gamma)
-chain is a maximal
(\alpha,\gamma)
-alternating path, and its end-vertices are the endpoints of the path. Given such a maximal chain
P
, note that by switching the colors
\alpha,\gamma
on
P
we get a new partial
k
-edge-coloring
\varphi^{\prime}
of
H
where exactly the same set of edges in
H
have been colored; we denote this
\varphi^{\prime}
by
\varphi/P
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
\varphi
be a partial
k
-edge-coloring of
G
. A
multifan
centered at
r
with respect to
\varphi
is a sequence
F_{\varphi}(r,s_{0}:s_{p})=(r,e_{0},s_{0},e_{1},s_{1},\ldots,e_{p},s_{p})
with
p\geq 0
consisting of distinct edges
e_{0},\ldots,e_{p}
with
e_{i}=rs_{i}
for all
i
, where
e_{0}
is left uncolored under
\varphi
,
and
for every edge
e_{i}
with
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, there exists
j\in\{0,\ldots,i-1\}
such that

\varphi(e_{i})\in\overline{\varphi}(s_{j})
.

The above definition of a multifan first appeared in the book of Stiebitz et al.
[
18
]
as a generalization of a
Vizing-fan
, which Vizing used to prove his eponymous theorem.
Vizing required his fans to be simple, while multifans are typically allowed to have parallel edges, although that difference is not relevant for us here, since all graph (and hypergraphs) are simple in this paper. The other difference in the structures is important for us here however: in the italicized part of the definition above, Vizing required
j=i-1
.
We don’t require this, but we will however find it useful to consider multifans which have this property. To this end, first note that if
F_{\varphi}(r,s_{0}:s_{p})
is a multifan, then for any integer
p^{*}\in\{0,\ldots,p\}
,
F_{\varphi}(r,s_{0}:s_{p^{*}})
is also a multifan.
A subsequence
(s_{0}=s_{\ell_{0}},s_{\ell_{1}},s_{\ell_{2}},\ldots,s_{\ell_{t}})
with the property that
\varphi(e_{\ell_{i}})=\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(s_{\ell_{i-1}})
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, is called a
linear sequence
of
F_{\varphi}(r,s_{0}:s_{p})
.
Given such a linear sequence, and given
h\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, we
shift from
s_{\ell_{h}}
to
s_{0}
by recoloring edge
e_{\ell_{i-1}}
with
\varphi(e_{\ell_{i}})
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
.
Note that the result is a partial
k
-edge-coloring where
e_{0}
is colored and
e_{\ell_{h}}
is not. These linear subsequences and shifts have been implicitly used in many papers (see eg.
[
3
]
).

The first of the two edge-coloring results we will prove in this section is as follows.

Lemma 3
.

Let
H
be a hypergraph with rank
c
and suppose that there exists a matching
M
of
H
such that all edges in
E(H)\setminus M
have size two. Suppose further that there exists a vertex
x\in V(H)
with
N_{H}(x)\subseteq V(H)\setminus V(M)
.
If
|M|+|\delta_{H}(x)|\leq\Delta(H)+2c-1,
then
H
has a
(\Delta(H)+2c-1)
-edge-coloring where every edge of
M\cup\delta_{H}(x)
receives a different color.

Note that the
H
in Lemma
3
is a hypergraph, but it is nearly a graph (all but the matching edges are of size two). Our approach will be to find a multifan within the edges of size two, and to consider linear subsequences and shifts of this multifan, but also to use maximal
(\alpha,\gamma)
-chains in the hypergraph which may indeed contain edges of larger sizes.

Proof.

(Lemma
3
)
Let
\Delta=\Delta(H)
and let
M^{*}=M\cup\delta_{H}(x)
. By assumption,

|M^{*}|=|M|+|\delta_{H}(x)|\leq\Delta+2c-1.

Let
\varphi
be a partial
(\Delta+2c-1)
-edge-coloring of
H
with the following properties:

(P1)

every edge in
M^{*}
receives a different color, and;

(P2)

subject to (P1),
\varphi
assigns colors to as many edges as possible.

If every edge in
H
is colored by
\varphi
then we are done, so we may assume there is some edge
e_{0}
that is uncolored by
\varphi
. Since
e_{0}
is uncolored it is not in
M^{*}
, so by assumption
e_{0}=uv
for some
u,v\in V(H)\setminus\{x\}
. Let
F
be a multifan centered at
u
, which is maximal subject to only containing edges of size two.

Since
u\neq x
,
u
is incident with at most one edge from
M^{*}
. Since
\varphi
has
\Delta+2c-1
colors (and
uv
is uncolored), we also know that
|\overline{\varphi}(v)|\geq 2c\geq 4
, and all of these colors must be present at
u
(otherwise
\varphi
could be extended to
e_{0}=uv
).
So there are certainly linear sequences of
F
containing no edge of
M^{*}
. We let
F_{1}\subseteq F
be a multifan induced by all the linear sequences of
F
that don’t contain edges of
M^{*}
.
Suppose
F_{1}=(u,uw_{0},w_{0},uw_{1},w_{1},\ldots,uw_{t},w_{t})
, where
w_{0}=v
.

Claim 1
.

Suppose
F_{2}\subseteq F_{1}
corresponds to the union of any number of linear sequences of
F
. Then
\overline{\varphi}(u)\cap\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})=\emptyset
for any
w_{i}\in V(F_{2})
.

Proof of claim.

We already explained the truth of this for
w_{0}=v
, and can extend this argument to the other
w_{i}
as well. To this end, suppose that
\overline{\varphi}(u)\cap\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})\neq\emptyset
for some
i
. Then there exists a linear sequence
(u,w_{0},w_{i_{1}},\ldots,w_{i_{s}})
of
F_{2}
for which
w_{i_{s}}=w_{i}
. Shift in
(u,w_{0},w_{i_{1}},\ldots,w_{i_{s}})

from
w_{i_{s}}
to
w_{0}
and then color the edge
uw_{i}
by a color in
\overline{\varphi}(u)\cap\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})
. The resulting partial
(\Delta+2c-1)
-edge-coloring
\varphi^{\prime}
of
H
still has every edge in
M^{*}
colored differently, since we excluded the edges of
M^{*}
from
F_{1}
(and
F_{2}
). Hence
\varphi^{\prime}
contradicts (P2).
∎

Claim 2
.

For any color
\gamma\in\{1,\ldots,\Delta+2c-1\}
, there are at most
2c-1
vertices from
\{w_{0},\ldots,w_{t}\}
that are all missing
\gamma
under
\varphi
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose to the contrary that there exists such a
\gamma
, and let
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{2c}
be
2c
distinct vertices of
\{w_{0},\ldots,w_{t}\}
all of which are missing
\gamma
under
\varphi
. Suppose, without loss of generality, that
y_{1}
is the first vertex
in the order
w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{t}
for which
\gamma\in\overline{\varphi}(y_{1})

does not contain any edge that is colored by
\gamma
.
Let
\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(u)
; we know that
\alpha\neq\gamma
by Claim
1
. Consider the set
\mathcal{P}
of maximal
(\alpha,\gamma)
-chains staring at

, respectively.
At most one edge of
M^{*}
is colored by
\alpha
, and at most one edge of
M^{*}
is colored by
\gamma
, and any other edge coloured
\alpha
or
\gamma
has size two. So, if a chain in
\mathcal{P}
contains exactly one edge of
M^{*}
, then it has at most

end-vertices; if it contains exactly two edges of

, then it can have at most
2c
end-vertices. Since there are at least
2c+1
different end-vertices for the chains in
\mathcal{P}
, this means that there is at least one chain
P\in\mathcal{P}
not containing any edges from
M^{*}
. In particular, all edges in this chain will have size two. We let
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi/P
and consider two different cases according to whether or not

is an endpoint of
P
.

Case 1
:
The vertex
u
is an endpoint of
P
.

Suppose first that the other end of
P
is
y_{1}
. Then in
\varphi^{\prime}
,
u
is missing
\gamma
, so in particular no
\gamma
-edges appear in
F_{1}
– if there were any before, they must have been recolored to

. However since these edge all occur after

in the order of

, and

is missing

\varphi^{\prime}
,

is still a multifan in

under
\varphi^{\prime}
. However now Claim

, leading to a contradiction in our choice of
\varphi
.

We may now assume that the other end of
P
is not
y_{1}
. Here, at least
F_{1}(u,w_{0}:y_{1})
is a multifan with respect to
\varphi^{\prime}
containing no edges of

. But now the color
\gamma
is missing at both
u
and
y_{1}
under
\varphi^{\prime}
, so Claim
1
is not satisfied for
\varphi^{\prime}
, leading to a contradiction in our choice of
\varphi
.

Case 2
:
The vertex
u
is not an endpoint of
P
.

In this case we know that
P
does not contain any edges of
F_{1}
.

First suppose that
y_{1}
is not an end of
P
. Then
F_{1}
is still a multifan with respect to
\varphi^{\prime}
containing no edges of

. However then color

is missing at
u
and at least one other vertex from
\{y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}\}
under

. This means that Claim

is not satisfied for
\varphi^{\prime}
, leading to a contradiction in our choice of
\varphi
.

We may now assume that
y_{1}
is an end of
P
. Here, at least
F_{1}(u,w_{0}:y_{1})
is a multifan with respect to
\varphi^{\prime}
containing no edges of
M^{*}
. But now the color
\alpha
is missing at both

and
y_{1}
under
\varphi^{\prime}
, so Claim

is not satisfied for
\varphi^{\prime}
, leading to a contradiction in our choice of
\varphi
.
∎

Claims
1
and
2
tell us that

|\overline{\varphi}(V(F_{1}))|\geq|\overline{\varphi}(u)|+\tfrac{1}{2c-1}\sum_{i=0}^{t}|\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})|.

(
1
)

If we let

be the hypergraph induced by all the colored edges in

, then
|\overline{\varphi}(z)|\geq\Delta+2c-1-d_{H^{\prime}}(z)
for all

. Since

, we know that

for any

. Since the edge

is uncolored, we further know that

. So from (

|\overline{\varphi}(V(F_{1}))|\geq(\Delta+2c-1-d_{H^{\prime}}(u))+\tfrac{1}{2c-1}(2c)+\tfrac{1}{2c-1}((2c-1)t)>\Delta+2c+t-d_{H^{\prime}}(u).

(

)

On the other hand, we know that there are exactly

, so there are exactly

The second edge-coloring result that we prove in this section is as follows. Note that while Lemma
3
is about a hypergraph that is almost a graph, the following result is about a graph that is nearly a bipartite graph. Despite the different settings, the arguments are very similar.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G_{0}
be a bipartite graph with bipartition
(A,B)
and let
M
be a matching in
G_{0}
. Let
G
be a graph obtained from
G_{0}
by adding a new vertex
x
and adding some number of edges between
x

and
V(G)\setminus V(M)
. Let
k=\max\{\Delta(G),k_{B}+1\}
, where
k_{B}=\max\{d_{G}(v):v\in B\}
.
If
k\geq|M|+|\delta_{G}(x)|
,
then
G
has a
k
-edge-coloring where every edge of
M\cup\delta_{G}(x)
receives a different color.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\Delta(G)
and let
M^{*}=M\cup\delta_{G}(x)
. By assumption,
|M^{*}|\leq k.
Let
\varphi
be a partial
k
-edge-colouring of
G
with the following properties:

(P1)

every edge in
M^{*}
receives a different color, and;

(P2)

subject to (P1),
\varphi
assigns colors to as many edges as possible.

If every edge in
G
is colored by
\varphi
then we are done, so we may assume there is some edge
uv
that is uncolored by
\varphi
. Note that
u,v\neq x
, since otherwise
uv
would be in
M^{*}
and hence be colored under
\varphi
. Suppose, without loss of generality, that
u\in A
, and let
F
be a maximal multifan centered at
u
.

We know that
v\in B
(since
v\neq x
) so
k\geq d_{G}(v)+1
. We also know that
uv
is uncolored, therefore
|\overline{\varphi}(v)|\geq 2
. All of these colors must be present at
u
(otherwise
\varphi
could be extended to
e_{0}=uv
). On the other hand, since
u\neq x
,
u
is incident with at most one edge from
M^{*}
. So there are certainly linear sequences of
F
containing no edge of
M^{*}
. We let
F_{1}\subseteq F
be a multifan induced by all the linear sequences of
F
that don’t contain edges of
M^{*}
. Suppose
F_{1}=(u,uw_{0},w_{0},uw_{1},w_{1},\ldots,uw_{t},w_{t})
, where
w_{0}=v
. In particular, note that
x\neq w_{i}
for any

, since its incident edges are all in
M^{*}
.

Claim 3
.

Suppose
F_{2}\subseteq F_{1}
corresponds to the union of any number of linear sequences of
F
. Then
\overline{\varphi}(u)\cap\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})=\emptyset
for any
w_{i}\in V(F_{2})
.

Proof of claim.

This argument is identical to that of Claim
1
within the proof of Lemma
3
.
∎

As

is bipartite, for any distinct
\alpha,\beta\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
with
\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(u),\beta\in\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})

for some

, if there is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-alternating path joining

, then it creates an odd cycle with

, and hence we know that the path must contain

(as an internal vertex). Moreover, this means that the path contains edges from
M^{*}
.
Similarly, if
\beta\in\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})\cap\overline{\varphi}(w_{j})
for

, and there is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-alternating path joining

and
w_{j}
, then this creates an odd cycle with

, and hence we know that the path must contain

(as an internal vertex). Again, we also get that such a path contains edges from

Claim 4

For any

, there is at most one vertex from

\varphi

Proof of claim.

Suppose to the contrary that

are two distinct vertices of
\{w_{0},\ldots,w_{t}\}
, both of which are missing
\gamma
under
\varphi
. Suppose, without loss of generality, that

is the first vertex
in the order

for which
\gamma\in\overline{\varphi}(y_{1})
. In particular, this implies that

does not contain any edge that is colored by
\gamma
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{\varphi}(u)
; we know that
\alpha\neq\gamma
by Claim

. Consider the set of maximal
(\alpha,\gamma)
-alternating paths staring at

. If these three paths are all disjoint,
then one of them, say

, does not contain any edge of

(since there are at most two edges of

with the colors
\alpha,\gamma
). On the other hand, suppose that two of the three paths are in fact the same path

. By our discussion prior to this claim, we know that
P^{*}
must contain

as an internal vertex, and hence there are two
M^{*}
-edges colored
\alpha,\gamma
on the path

. But then again, among the three paths, we find one

that does not contain any edges of

.

We let

and consider two different cases according to whether or not

, does not contain

.

Case 1
:
The vertex
u
is an endpoint of

.

Since the other endpoint of

is outside

,

is a multifan with respect to
\varphi^{\prime}
containing no edges of

. But now the color
\gamma
is missing at both

and

under
\varphi^{\prime}
, so Claim

is not satisfied for

, leading to a contradiction in our choice of

Case 2
:
The vertex

is not an endpoint of

In this case we know that

does not contain any edges of

, and our proof is identical to case 2 of Claim

in Lemma

.
∎

Claims
3
and
4
tell us that

|\overline{\varphi}(V(F_{1}))|\geq|\overline{\varphi}(u)|+\sum_{i=0}^{t}|\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})|.

(
3
)

If we let
G^{\prime}
be the graph induced by all the colored edges in

, then
\overline{\varphi}(z)\geq k-d_{G^{\prime}}(z)
for all

.
We further argued that
|\overline{\varphi}(w_{0})|\geq 2
earlier in this proof, and the same argument (less being incident to an uncolored edge) tells us that
|\overline{\varphi}(w_{i})|\geq 1
for all other

. So from (

(

)

On the other hand, we know that there are exactly

, so there are exactly

that are not included in

. At most one of these excluded colored edges could be in

(since

), while all the rest must have colors that are not in

. Of course, by virtue of being incident to

, none of these excluded colors are in

either. Hence we have found a set of at least

that are not in
\overline{\varphi}(V(F_{1}))
. So we get

|\overline{\varphi}(V(F_{1}))|\leq k-(d_{G^{\prime}}(u)-t-1)<k-d_{G^{\prime}}(u)+2+t,

∎

Proof of Theorem
1

A
k
-coloring
of a graph
G
is an assignment of the colors
1,2,\ldots,k
to the vertices of
G
such that adjacent vertices receive different colors. Given a
k
-coloring
\varphi
, and given any
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we call
V_{i}=\{v\in V(G):\varphi(v)=i\}
the
i
th color class of
\varphi
; if
|V_{i}|,|V_{j}|
differ by at most one for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, then we say that
\varphi
is
equitable
.

Theorem 5

(Hajnal and Szemerédi
[
11
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let

be any integer. Then
G
has an equitable
k
-coloring.

We can now show that Theorem

follows quickly from Lemma

(and Theorem

).

1
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\Delta=\Delta(G)
.
We will show that

has a total coloring using at most
\Delta+2\lceil\frac{|V(G)|}{\Delta+1}\rceil

By Theorem
5
,

has an equitable (
\Delta

\varphi_{0}
. Let

.
By assumption, each color class of
\varphi_{0}
has size
c
or

.

We construct a hypergraph
H
as follows. We let
V(G)=V(H)\cup\{x\}
for one new vertex

. We start with

, and for any color class
V_{i}
of
\varphi_{0}
, if
|V_{i}|\geq 2
, we add
V_{i}
as an edge to

. If there is at least one color class of
\varphi_{0}
that has size one, then for each such color class

, we add

as an edge of size two to

.

Note that
d_{H}(w)=d_{G}(w)+1
for all

, and

d_{H}(x)\leq\Delta+1
as

has exactly
\Delta+1
distinct color classes. Hence

to

4.1
Matchings in the complement of a regular graph

The following result can be easily proved by applying Hall’s Marriage Theorem,
and one can also find a short proof in
[
20
, Lemma 9]
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
G[X,Y]
be bipartite graph with
|X|=|Y|=n
. If
\delta(G)\geq n/2
, then
G
has a perfect matching.

Give a graph
G
and a set
X\subseteq V(G)
, the
deficiency of
X
is defined to be the quantity
\df(G)=\odd(G-X)-|X|
, where for any graph
H
,
\odd(H)
is the number of odd components or components of odd order of
H
.
Note that any matching of
G
must contain at least
\df(X)
unsaturated vertices, for any
X\subseteq V(G)
.
The deficiency of
G
is
\df(G)=\max\{\df(X):X\subseteq V(G)\}
. The Tutte-Berge Formula
[
2
,
19
]
asserts that the maximum size of a matching in
G
is equal to
\tfrac{1}{2}(|V(G)|-\df(G))
. For any
X\subseteq V(G)
, define an auxiliary bipartite multigraph
H(X)
by contracting each component in
G-X
to a single vertex and deleting the edges of
G[X]
. As part of his new proof of the Tutte-Berge Formula and the Gallai-Edmonds Structure Theorem
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, West
[
23
]
defined
H(X)
and proved (1)-(2) of the following. Here, by a graph
G
being
factor-critical
, we mean that
G
does not have a perfect matching, but
G-v
does, for every
v\in V(G)
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
X\subseteq V(G)
be a maximal set with
\df(X)=\df(G)
. Then

(1)

(West
[
23
, Lemma 2]
)
Every component of
G-X
is factor-critical.

(2)

(West
[
23
, Lemma 3]
)
The bipartite multigraph
H(X)
has a matching saturating
X
.

(3)

We may choose the matching
M
in (2) so that if
d
is the maximum degree in
H
over all vertices in
X
, then
M
also saturates all vertices of
V(H(X))\setminus X
having degree at least
d
in
H
.

To see Lemma
7
(3), note first that any bipartite multigraph
H
with bipartition
(X,Y)
where
d
is the maximum degree in
H
over all vertices in
X
, has a matching saturating all vertices of degree at least
d
in
Y
(simply apply Hall’s Theorem to
(X,Y^{\prime})
, where
Y^{\prime}
are those vertices of
Y
having degree at least
d
). Then note that given any
two matchings
M_{1},M_{2}
of

where
M_{1}
saturates
X_{1}\subseteq X
and
M_{2}
saturates
Y_{2}\subseteq Y
, there is a matching
M
of

that saturates both
X_{1}
and
Y_{2}
(look at
M_{1}\cup M_{2}
and delete an appropriate set of edges).

We can now prove the following.

Lemma 8
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
r
-regular graph for some integer
r\geq 0
. Then
\overline{G}
has a matching covering
at least
n-\frac{n}{n-r}
vertices of
\overline{G}
.

Proof
. Note that
\overline{G}
is
(n-1-r)
-regular. If
\overline{G}
has a perfect matching, then we are done, so suppose not. Let
S\subseteq V(G)
be a maximal set with
\df(S)=\df(\overline{G})
, as in Lemma
7
. Then by this lemma, every component of
\overline{G}-S
is factor-critical.

Let
D
of be an odd component of
\overline{G}-S
, and suppose that
|V(D)|\leq n-r-1
. Then the number of edges between
D
and
S
in
\overline{G}
is at least

|V(D)|(n-r-1-(|V(D)|-1)).

The above function is concave down in
|V(D)|
, and given
1\leq|V(D)|\leq n-r-1
, we get that the function is always at least
n-r-1
. Thus by Lemma
7
(3),
\overline{G}
has a matching saturating

and all vertices from the small components of
\overline{G}-S
(i.e. those of size at most

).
As there are at most
\frac{n}{n-r}
components of
\overline{G}-S
with
|V(D)|\geq n-r
, and because each of such component is factor-critical, it
follows that
\overline{G}
has a matching that covers at least

vertices of
\overline{G}
.
∎

The remainder of our proof consists of five steps, through which we construct our desired edge-coloring. Before we provide the details for each of these steps, let us first give an outline, along with several necessary definitions. In fact, we shall give two outlines: first an executive summary of the whole process, and then a detailed outline of the five steps for edge-coloring.

Executive Summary of Edge-Coloring Procedure:

•

Let
k=\left\lceil\tfrac{1}{2}\left(r+2+m^{2/3}\right)\right\rceil+4

\ell=\lceil m^{5/6}\rceil+1

•

In Steps 1-3 we will

•

After Steps 1-3 we will not change any of the color classes

•

In Step 4 we will introduce

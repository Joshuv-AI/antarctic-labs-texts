Abstract




Abstract

In this short paper, we explore relationship between various models of complex networks with pinning controllers.

In the paper [1], we discussed following coupling network with a single pinning controller

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{dx_{1}(t)}{dt}&=f(x_{1}(t),t)+c\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}a_{1j}x_{j}(t)\\
&-c\varepsilon(x_{1}(t)-s(t)),\\
\frac{dx_{i}(t)}{dt}&=f(x_{i}(t),t)+c\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}x_{j}(t),\\
&i=2,\cdots,m\end{array}\right.

where
a_{ij}\geq 0
, if
i\neq j
and
a_{ii}=-\sum_{j\neq i}a_{ij}

It is clear that (1) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d(x_{i}(t)-s(t))}{dt}

\displaystyle=f(x_{i}(t),t)-f(s(t),t)+c\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{a}_{ij}(x_{j}(t)-s(t))

(6)

\tilde{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{11}-\varepsilon&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1m}\\

Now, we will reveal that the results given in [2], [3] are direct consequences of those given in [1].

Firstly, we give following simple proposition on the normal operators in functional analysis.

Proposition 2

The assumption that
\Gamma
and
K
are commutable is equivalent to that
\Gamma
and
K
have same eigenvectors.

In fact, if
\gamma_{i}
is a eigenvalue of
\Gamma
,
x_{i}
is the corresponding eigenvector. Then

\Gamma x_{i}=\gamma_{i}x_{i}

Because
\Gamma K=K\Gamma
, then

\Gamma Kx_{i}=K\Gamma x_{i}=\gamma_{i}Kx_{i}

which means
Kx_{i}
is also an eigenvector of
\Gamma
corresponding to
\gamma_{i}

Kx_{i}=k_{i}x_{i}

\Gamma
and
K
have same eigenvectors.

It is well known that the definite matrix
\Gamma
can be decomposed as
\Gamma=Q^{T}\bar{\Gamma}Q
, where
Q
is an orthogonal matrix and
\bar{\Gamma}=diag[\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}]
is a positive diagonal

Based on Proposition 2,
K
can also be written as
K=Q^{T}\bar{K}Q
,
\bar{K}=diag[k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]
.

Let
\tilde{x}_{i}(t)=Qx_{i}(t)
.
\tilde{s}(t)=Qs(t)
,
and

\tilde{f}(u)=Q(f(Q^{-1}u,t)

It is clear that

\displaystyle(\tilde{x}-\tilde{y})^{\top}(\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})-\tilde{f}(\tilde{y}))=(x-y)^{T}(f(x)-f(y))

(16)

Therefore, in the following, we will discuss following systems

\displaystyle\frac{d({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))}{dt}

\displaystyle={f}({x}_{i}(t))-{f}({s}(t)))+c\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\tilde{G}_{ij}\bar{\Gamma}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))

where
\bar{\Gamma}=diag[\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}]
is a positive diagonal matrix.

The QUAD condition is

\displaystyle({x}-{y})^{\top}({f}({x})-{f}(\tilde{y}))-({x}-{y})^{\top}\bar{K}\bar{\Gamma}({x}-{y})<-\eta({x}-{y})^{\top}({x}-{y})

\bar{K}=diag[k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}]

Proof

\displaystyle V(\tilde{x}(t))=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))^{T}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))

Differentiating, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d(V({x}(t)))}{dt}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))^{T}[{f}({x}_{i}(t))-{f}({s}(t))]

\displaystyle+c\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\xi_{i}\tilde{G}_{ij}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))^{T}\bar{\Gamma}({x}_{j}(t)-{s}(t))

\displaystyle<\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))^{T}\bar{K}\bar{\Gamma}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))

\displaystyle+c\lambda_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))^{T}\bar{\Gamma}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))

\displaystyle-\eta\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))^{T}({x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t))

\displaystyle V({x}(t))=O(e^{-\eta t})

\displaystyle{x}_{i}(t)-{s}(t)=O(e^{-\eta t}),~~i=1,\cdots,m

Conclusions

In this note, we point out that most of the results in papers [2] and [3] have been reported and are direct consequences of previous papers [1], [4]. We also correct the proof given in the papers [2] and [3].

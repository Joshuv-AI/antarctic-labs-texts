The double phase Dirichlet problem when the lowest exponent is equal to 1
Alexandros Matsoukas, Nikos Yannakakis

Abstract
. In this paper we prove an existence and uniqueness result for the double phase Dirichlet problem when the lowest exponent is equal to 1. Our solution is a function of bounded variation that simultaneously lies in a suitable weighted Sobolev space and is found as the limit of a sequence of solutions of intermediate double phase Dirichlet problems whose lowest exponent p p goes to 1. As a result of that, our approach involves the study of some relevant properties of generalized Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary,
f\in L^{n}(\Omega)
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
a(x)\geq 0
. In this paper we address the matter of existence of solutions for the double phase Dirichlet problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divergence(\displaystyle\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)&=&f\,\text{ in }\Omega\\
u&=&0\,\text{ in }\partial\Omega\,.\end{array}\right.

The terminology double phase problem is used in the literature to describe problems as the above, that depend on two competing exponents
1\leq p<q
and exhibit distinct growth behavior in different parts of
\Omega
, depending on which exponent is dominant there. These different parts of the domain are usually called the p and the q-phase respectively and here they are determined by the function
a
; when
a(x)=0
we have the p-phase and when
a(x)>0
the q-phase respectively.

Double phase problems were firstly introduced by Zhikov in
[
20
]
and in recent years they have become the focus of extensive research
(see for example
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
and the references therein). Nevertheless with the exception of
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
it seems that the case
p=1
has been overlooked and hence this paper is a step towards filling this gap.

As is a common theme when dealing with problems that involve the 1-Laplacian the solution to (
1.1
) will be found as the limit for
p\rightarrow 1
of the solutions
(u_{p})
of the intermediate Dirichlet problems

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divergence(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)&=&f\,\text{ in }\Omega\\
u&=&0\,\text{ in }\partial\Omega\,.\end{array}\right.

The difficulty of giving some sense to
\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}
in (
1.1
) is overcomed by using the ideas of Anzellotti from
[
1
]
and the role of the above quotient will be played by a bounded vector field
z
. Note also that due to the coexistence of the 1 and the weighted q-Laplacian in (
1.1
), it is natural to expect that its solution should lie simultaneously in
BV(\Omega)
and in some suitable weighted Sobolev space.

Our approach follows the ideas of
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
and our hypotheses are that the weight
a
is Lipschitz continuous, belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{q}
, is non-zero on the boundary of
\Omega
and the exponents
p,q
satisfy the inequality
\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{1}{n}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Generalized Orlicz spaces

In this part we follow
[
17
]
. Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary,
1<p<q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
a(x)>0
a.e. in
\Omega
. The functions

\theta_{p},\theta_{0}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

defined by

\theta_{p}(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}\text{ and }\theta_{0}(x,t)=a(x)t^{q}\,,

are uniformly convex, generalized
\Phi
functions.
Hence when equipped with the so-called Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{\theta_{r}}=\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho_{\theta_{r}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})\leq 1\}\,,

where

\rho_{\theta_{r}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\theta_{r}(x,u)\,dx\,,

the generalized Orlicz spaces
L^{\theta_{r}}(\Omega)
, for
r=p
and
0
, are reflexive Banach spaces. The same is true for the generalized Orlicz-Sobolev space

W^{1,\theta_{r}}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{\theta_{r}}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L^{\theta_{r}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\}

with norm

\|u\|_{1,\theta_{r}}=\|u\|_{\theta_{r}}+\|\nabla u\|_{\theta_{r}}\,.

As usual we define

W^{1,\theta_{r}}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{1,\theta_{r}}}\,,

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the spaces
L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
are actually weighted Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces respectively. For matters of convenience of notation we may sometimes denote the formers by
L^{s}_{a}(\Omega)
, with
a
the weight and
s
the corresponding exponent.

If
a\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and is non-zero on
\partial\Omega
then we can define a trace on
W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
a\in C(\overline{\Omega})
with
a\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
, such that
a(x)\neq 0
, for all
x\in\partial\Omega
. Then there exists a bounded linear operator

T:W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{q}(\partial\Omega)

such that

Tu=u|_{\partial\Omega}\,,\text{ for all }u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)\,.

Proof.

Since
a(x)\neq 0
, for all
x\in\partial\Omega
, there exists
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
such that
a(x)\geq a(x_{0})
, for all
x\in\partial\Omega
. Hence, since
a
is uniformly continuous we have that there exists
r>0
such that

a(x)\geq a(x_{0})
, for all
x\in\overline{U}
, where

U=\left\{x\in\Omega:\dist(x,\partial\Omega)<r\right\}

and this implies (see
[
10
, p. 17]
) that

W^{1,\theta_{0}}(U)=W^{1,q}(U)\,.

Thus, since
U
has Lipschitz boundary which contains the boundary of
\Omega
, the existence of the trace operator in
W^{1,q}(U)
implies the existence of
c>0
such that

\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\partial U}|u|^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq c\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(U)}\,,\text{ for all }u\in C^{1}(\overline{U})\,,

where by
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
we denote the
n-1
dimensional Hausdorff measure.

Hence the linear operator
S
defined by
Su=u|_{\partial\Omega}
, for
u\in C^{1}(\overline{U})
with values in
L^{q}(\partial\Omega)
is bounded and (by density) can be extended to the whole of
W^{1,q}(U)
. Note that as in the classical case it is easy to see that

Su=u|_{\partial\Omega},\text{ for all }u\in C(\overline{U})\cap W^{1,q}(U)\,.

We now define

T:W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{q}(\partial\Omega)\,,

by

Tu=S(u|_{U})\,.

The operator
T
is bounded since our hypothesis on
a
implies that the operator
u\rightarrow u|_{U}
, from
W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
into
W^{1,q}(U)
is bounded.

Moreover if
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
, then
u|_{\overline{U}}\in C(\overline{U})\cap W^{1,q}(U)
and hence

Tu=S(u|_{U})=u|_{\partial\Omega}\,.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

The hypothesis that
a
is non-zero on the boundary of
\Omega
is not restricting with respect to the double-phase nature of our problem, since the set

\left\{x\in\Omega:a(x)=0\right\}

still determines the p-phase inside
\Omega
.

The above allows us to identify
W^{1,\theta_{0}}_{0}(\Omega)
as the space of functions on
\Omega
with zero boundary values.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
a\in C(\overline{\Omega})
with
a\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
, such that
a(x)\neq 0
, for all
x\in\partial\Omega
. If
T
is the trace operator defined in Proposition
2.2
, then

\ker T=W^{1,\theta_{0}}_{0}(\Omega)\,.

Proof.

The proof follows easily from the classical case.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We have that

W^{1,\theta_{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)

and hence the restriction of
T
on
W^{1,\theta_{p}}(\Omega)
is the trace operator on
W^{1,\theta_{p}}(\Omega)
, whose kernel is the space
W^{1,\theta_{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

If
p<n
then the following Sobolev embedding holds

W_{0}^{1,\theta_{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)

where
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
. In particular

\|u\|_{p^{*}}\leq S(n,p)\|u\|_{1,\theta_{p}}\,,\text{ for all }u\in W_{0}^{1,\theta_{p}}(\Omega)\,,

where
S(n,p)
is the best constant of the classical Sobolev embedding. Moreover, as it was showed in
[
19
]

(2.1)

\lim_{p\to 1}S(n,p)=S(n,1)

and this will be useful in the sequel.

With the additional assumption
\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{1}{n}
the following Poincaré type inequality holds

\|u\|_{\theta_{p}}\leq c\|\nabla u\|_{\theta_{p}}\,,\text{ for all }u\in W_{0}^{1,\theta_{p}}(\Omega)\,,

for some
c>0
.

We will also need the definition of the Muckenhoupt class
A_{q}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A weight
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
a(x)>0
a.e. in
\Omega
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{q}
if

\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}a(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}a(x)^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}dx\right)^{q-1}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.

For more details on generalized Orlicz spaces we refer the interested reader to the book
[
8
]
.

2.2.
Functions of bounded variation and Anzellotti’s theory

A function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
belongs to
BV(\Omega)
if its distributional derivative
Du
is a Radon measure.
The total variation of the measure
Du
is given by

|Du|(\Omega)=\sup\{\langle Du,\phi\rangle:\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega),\|\phi\|\leq 1\}.

When equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{BV}=\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+|Du|(\Omega)\,,

the space
BV(\Omega)
becomes a Banach space and possesses the following important compactness property: if
(u_{n})
is a bounded sequence in
BV(\Omega)
then there exists a subsequence, which for convenience we denote again by
(u_{n})
, and a function
u\in BV(\Omega)
such that

•

Du_{n}\rightarrow Du
,
w^{\ast}
as measures in
\Omega
and

•

u_{n}\rightarrow u
, in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Let
z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a vector field whose
\divergence z
in the sense of distributions belongs to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\langle\divergence z,\phi\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}z\,\nabla\phi\,dx\,,\text{ for all }\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

If
u\in BV(\Omega)
one may define the distribution
(z,Du):C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
as

(2.2)

\langle(z,Du),\phi\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}uz\nabla\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}u\phi\divergence z\,dx.

By Anzellotti’s theory
[
1
]
we can define the weak trace on
\partial\Omega
of the normal component of
z
, denoted by
[z,v]
for which we have the following generalized Green’s formula

(2.3)

\int_{\Omega}u\divergence z\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(z,Du)=\int_{\partial\Omega}u[z,v]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,.

3.
Main results

As usual a function
u\in W^{1,\theta_{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
is said to be a weak solution of the double phase Dirichlet problem (
1.2
) if

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)\nabla vdx=\int_{\Omega}fvdx,\

for all
v\in W^{1,\theta_{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Our hypotheses on the weight function
a
and the exponents
p,q
are

(H_{0}):a\in C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})\cap A_{q}\,,a(x)\neq 0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\,,1<p<q<n\text{ and }\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{1}{n}\,,

where by
C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
we denote the space of Lipschitz functions defined on
\overline{\Omega}
. We have the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
11
, Theorem 1.1]

If
(H_{0})
holds and
f\in L^{n}(\Omega)
, then there exists a unique weak solution of problem (
1.2
), for every
p>1
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
p^{\ast}=\frac{np}{n-p}
. If
f\in L^{p_{\ast}}(\Omega)
with
p_{\ast}
given by
\frac{1}{p_{\ast}}+\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}=1
, then an application of Hölder’s inequality gives us

\|f\|_{p_{\ast}}\leq|\Omega|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{n}\,.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
(H_{0})
holds and
f\in L^{n}(\Omega)
satisfies

\|f\|_{n}(S(n,1)+1)<1\,.

If
(u_{p})
are the weak solutions of the Dirichlet problem (
1.2
), then there exist a function
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\theta_{0}}_{0}(\Omega)
and a vector field
z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
\|z\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
such that, up to subsequences,

\displaystyle u_{p}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle u\,,\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega)\,,

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p}

\displaystyle\overset{w}{\rightharpoonup}

\displaystyle\nabla u\,,\text{ in }L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u

\displaystyle\overset{w}{\rightharpoonup}

\displaystyle z\,,\text{ in }L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

for all
1\leq r<\infty
.

Proof.

Let

\lambda_{p}=\|\nabla u_{p}\|_{\theta_{p}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}(|\frac{\nabla u_{p}}{\lambda}|^{p}+a(x)|\frac{\nabla u_{p}}{\lambda}|^{q})\,dx\leq 1\right\}\,.

Thus

\int_{\Omega}(|\frac{\nabla u_{p}}{\lambda_{p}}|^{p}+a(x)|\frac{\nabla u_{p}}{\lambda_{p}}|^{q})\,dx=1.

Choosing
u_{p}/\lambda_{p}
as a test function in (
3.1
) we get

(3.2)

\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u_{p}|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla u_{p}|^{q})\,dx=\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}}\int_{\Omega}fu_{p}\,dx\,.

By Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding, Remark
3.2
and (
2.1
) for
p
close to
1
we have

\displaystyle I=\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u_{p}|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla u_{p}|^{q})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}}\|f\|_{p_{\ast}}\|u_{p}\|_{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{S(n,p)}{\lambda_{p}}\|f\|_{p_{\ast}}\|\nabla u_{p}\|_{\theta_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(n,p)|\Omega|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{n}\,,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(S(n,1)+1)\|f\|_{n}\,.

To estimate
I
on the left hand side, we see that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\lambda_{p}}^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}|\frac{|\nabla u_{p}|}{\lambda_{p}}|^{p}\,dx+{\lambda_{p}}^{q-1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\frac{\nabla u_{p}}{\lambda_{p}}|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\{{\lambda_{p}}^{p-1},{\lambda_{p}}^{q-1}\}\int_{\Omega}(|\frac{|\nabla u_{p}|}{\lambda_{p}}|^{p}+a(x)|\frac{\nabla u_{p}}{\lambda_{p}}|^{q})\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{{\lambda_{p}}^{p-1},{\lambda_{p}}^{q-1}\}.\

Hence

\min\{{\lambda_{p}}^{p-1},{\lambda_{p}}^{q-1}\}<1\,

for
p
close to
1
and
\lambda_{p}=\|\nabla u_{p}\|_{\theta_{p}}<1
.

Since
L^{\theta_{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega)
and
L^{\theta_{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
we also have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}|\leq C\|\nabla u_{p}\|_{\theta_{p}}\leq C

and

(3.3)

\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u_{p}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\|\nabla u_{p}\|_{\theta_{p}}\leq 1\,.

Thus
(u_{p})
is bounded in
BV(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
and so passing to a subsequence, which for simplicity we denote again by
(u_{p})
, we get a function
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p}

\displaystyle\overset{w^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}

\displaystyle Du\,,\text{ as measures in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p}

\displaystyle\overset{w}{\rightharpoonup}

\displaystyle\nabla u\,,\text{ in }L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle u_{p}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle u\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega)\,,\text{ and a.e. in }\Omega\,.

Let
1\leq r<\infty
. Then since
L^{\theta_{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}|^{(p-1)r}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\Omega|^{1-\frac{(p-1)r}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{p}|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{(p-1)r}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\Omega|^{1-\frac{(p-1)r}{p}}\|\nabla u_{p}\|_{\theta_{p}}^{(p-1)r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\Omega|^{1-\frac{(p-1)r}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|\Omega|+1)\,.

Thus, we have

(3.4)

\||\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq(|\Omega|+1)^{\frac{1}{r}}\,,\text{ for all }1\leq r<\infty

and hence we may pass to a further subsequence, still denoted by

(|\nabla u_{p}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{p})\,,

which converges weakly to a vector field
z_{r}\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proceeding with a diagonal argument we may extract a common subsequence
(|\nabla u_{p}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{p})
and a vector field
z\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, independent of
r
, such that

|\nabla u_{p}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{p}\overset{w}{\rightharpoonup}z\,,\text{ in }L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\

for all
1\leq r<\infty
. Using (
3.4
) and the lower semicontinuiuty of the norm, we get

\|z\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq(|\Omega|+1)^{\frac{1}{r}},

for all
1\leq r<\infty
and hence
z\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Letting
r\to\infty
we have

\|z\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1.

∎

The following Meyers-Serrin type theorem will be important in the sequel. Note that our assumption that the weight function belongs to the Muckenhoupt class is equivalent to the uniform boundedness of the convolution operators and thus, as was pointed out in
[
21
]
, the Meyers-Serrin theorem is valid in the corresponding weighted Sobolev space
W^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
(H_{0})
holds. If
u\in BV(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
, then there exists a sequence
(v_{n})
in
W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle u\,,\text{ in }L^{1}(\Omega)\,,

\displaystyle\int|\nabla v_{n}|\,dx

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle|Du|(\Omega)\,,

\displaystyle\nabla v_{n}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\nabla u\,,\text{ in }L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We can define an open covering of
\Omega
given by a family of open sets
(C_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
defined as in
[
2
, Theorem 10.1.2]
and
(\phi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
a partition of unity subordinate to the covering. We consider also the standard mollifiers
(\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}})_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
. As we have already mentioned since
a\in A_{q}
the Meyers-Serrin theorem is valid in
W^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
. Thus we may choose
\varepsilon_{i}>0
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that

\displaystyle\text{ supp }\left(\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\phi_{i}u)\right)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle C_{i},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\phi_{i}u)-\phi_{i}u|\,dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i}},

\displaystyle|\int_{\Omega}|\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\phi_{i}Du)|\,dx-\int_{\Omega}|\phi_{1}Du||

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(u\nabla\phi_{i})-u\nabla\phi_{i}|\,dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i}},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\nabla(u\phi_{i}))-\nabla(u\phi_{i})|^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{i}}\right)^{q}\,.

Hence, defining
v_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(\phi_{i}u)
and then arguing as in
[
2
, Theorem 10.1.2]
, we have that
v_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega)
and approximates
u
in the sense that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}-u|\,dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon,

\displaystyle|\int_{\Omega}|Dv_{\varepsilon}|-\int_{\Omega}|Du||

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 4\varepsilon\,,

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\varepsilon\,.

Hence letting
v_{n}=v_{\frac{1}{n}}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain the required result.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

By
[
2
, Remark 10.2.1]
we have that the function
u\in BV(\Omega)
and the approximating sequence
(v_{n})
in Proposition
3.4
coincide on the boundary of
\Omega
in the sense of the
BV
-trace. The same is true for the trace in
W_{0}^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
since
v_{\varepsilon}-u
in the above proof is the strong limit in
W_{0}^{1,\theta_{0}}(\Omega)
of the sequence

v_{\varepsilon,k}-u_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\rho_{\varepsilon_{i}}\ast(u\phi_{i})-u\phi_{i})

whose trace is 0 and the result follows from the continuity of the trace operator.

As the following Proposition illustrates, the convergence of the gradients
\nabla u_{p}
to
\nabla u
in
L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
p\rightarrow 1
, is actually strong

Proposition 3.6
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
3.3
we have that

\nabla u_{p}\to\nabla u\text{ in }L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,.

Proof.

We first show that

\lim_{p\to 1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\left(|\nabla u_{p}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{p}-|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\nabla(u_{p}-u)\,dx=0\,.

By Proposition
3.3

\nabla u_{p}\overset{w}{\rightharpoonup}\nabla u\text{ in }L^{\theta_{0}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

and hence

\lim_{p\to 1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\nabla(u_{p}-u)\,dx=0\,.

Thus we only need to show that

\lim_{p\to 1}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u_{p}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{p}\nabla(u_{p}-u)\,dx=0\,.

To this end let
\varepsilon>0
. Then by Proposition
3.4
there exists
v\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}|f||u-v|\,dx+|\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|\,dx-|Du|(\Omega)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

and

(3.5)

(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u-\nabla v|^{q}\,dx)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

Using Remarks
2.5
and
3.5
we may use
u_{p}-v
in the weak formulation (

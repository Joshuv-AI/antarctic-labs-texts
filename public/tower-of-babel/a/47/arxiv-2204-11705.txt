1.
Introduction

1.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra. Almost split sequences, also called Auslander-Reiten sequences, are the building blocks of the Auslander-Reiten theory, AR-theory for short. These non-split short exact sequences contain information about how certain morphisms factor. The theory developed in
[
6
,
7
]
provides an efficient tool for studying the local structure of additive categories. The existence of almost split sequences for
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, the category of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules, was first proved in
[
5
]
.

Because of the importance of the theory, it has been studied in many different settings, such as subcategories of abelian categories
[
9
]
, triangulated categories
[
24
]
, exact categories
[
31
]
, extriangulated categories
[
26
]
, Krull-Schmidt categories
[
30
]
, and submodule categories
[
40
]
.

The theory became more interesting and became the core of the study in modern representation theory after Ringel embedded the almost split sequences into certain quivers known as Auslander-Reiten quivers
[
38
]
. The AR-quiver of a category
\mathscr{C}
, usually denoted by
\Gamma(\mathscr{C})
or
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
, is a valued translation quiver
[
3
, §IV.4]
that contains much of the homological and combinatorial information of the category. Its vertices are the isoclasses
[X]
of indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{C}
and arrows are defined by using a notion of irreducibility.

The shape of the AR-quiver of an algebra not only visualizes the information of the category
\mathscr{C}
but also gives valued information on the category. For instance, one important class of Artin algebras is Artin algebras of finite representation type. Recall that an algebra
\Lambda
is of finite representation type if, up to isomorphism, there are only finitely many indecomposable modules in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. Their module categories are well described by the AR-quiver, see
[
36
]
. For instance, Riedtmann classified all selfinjective algebras of finite representation type by their Auslander-Reiten quivers
[
35
,
37
]
. If
\mathscr{C}
is a Frobenius exact category, then the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathscr{C}
, denoted by
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathscr{C}}
, is obtained by removing all projective-injective vertices of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
.

We point out that the AR-quiver often decomposes into a union of (infinite) components and the structures of such components are studied extensively. For example, in
[
11
]
, tree classes of the components of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver of a quantum complete intersection were completely described. The shape of the components of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver that contain Heller lattices was determined in
[
2
]
and components of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver
{\Gamma}^{s}_{\mathscr{C}}
of a finite group scheme
\mathcal{G}
over a field
k
of characteristic
p
was studied in
[
45
]
.

1.2
.

The study of monomorphism categories, also known as submodule categories, almost has the same age as AR-theory, or rather older. The study of such categories goes back to Birkhoff
[
12
]
. Monomorphism categories allow us to apply methods from homological algebra, combinatorics and geometry to open problems in linear algebra. The monomorphism category of
\Lambda
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda)
, consists of all monomorphisms in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
as objects. The morphisms are defined by commutative diagrams. The study of such categories has been the subject of several recent researches, see e.g.
[
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
50
]
. For the history and details of the theory, please refer to
[
42
,
51
]
and references therein.

In
[
14
]
it is proved that the monomorphism category of a Frobenius abelian category is a Frobenius exact category. In
[
40
]
it is shown that
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda)
has Auslander-Reiten sequences. The authors of
[
48
]
generalized results of
[
40
]
from monomorphism categories to the category
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\Lambda)
, that is, the category of all sequences
A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow A_{n}
in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, such that all morphisms are monomorphisms. Monomorphism categories associated to arbitrary species also had been studied in
[
19
]
. In
[
21
]
, the first author of this paper investigated the monomorphism categories associated to a subcategory.

1.3
.

Auslander and Bridger
[
4
]
introduced finitely generated modules of Gorenstein dimension zero as a natural generalization of finitely generated projective modules.
This notion was further developed by several authors and a definition in full generality for arbitrary modules over associative and unitary rings was proposed by Enochs and Jenda
[
18
]
, by using the terminology of Gorenstein projective modules.

It has been proved that Gorenstein projective modules are strongly related with monomorphism categories. For instance, Gorenstein projective bimodules over the tensor product of two algebras are studied via monomorphism categories
[
25
]
. Quite recently in
[
46
]
, it is shown that there are recollements induced by the monomorphism categories of Gorenstein projective and Ding projective modules.

1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
denote the subcategory of
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda)
consisting of all morphisms
f:X\rightarrow Y
such that all
X,Y
and
{\rm Cok}(f)
lie in
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
. This monomorphism subcategory will be called the
\mathcal{G}
-monomorphism category of
\Lambda
. In this paper we are mainly interested in studying the shape of the components of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
.

The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
is devoted to prepare the necessary background for our main results. In particular, Frobenius categories, Auslander-Reiten sequences, Auslander-Reiten quivers and the category of Gorenstein projective modules are studied. In Section
3
, we introduce and study the
\mathcal{G}
-monomorphism category
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
. In particular, we see that if
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, then
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
has almost split sequences. Then we study the structure of almost split sequences starting with/ending at certain objects in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
. We also investigate the structure of the middle terms of certain almost split sequences in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
.

In Section
4
we define an auto-equivalence
\vartheta
on
\underline{\rm Gprj}\mbox{-}\Lambda
, the stable category of Gorenstein projective modules, and study the
\vartheta
-orbits of indecomposable non-projective Gorenstein projective modules. As a generalization of the boundary modules
[
41
, (5.1)]
, we define the notion of boundary vertices of
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
, the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
, and study those components of
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
that contain a boundary vertex.
In particular, when
\Lambda
is a CM-finite algebra and
\Gamma
is a component of
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
containing a boundary vertex, the shape of
\Gamma
both in the finite and in the infinite case will be discussed, see Theorem
4.12
.
If we consider the boundary vertices of the form
(0\rightarrow G)
, where
G
runs over the set of all pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable non-projective Gorenstein projective modules, then there is a well-defined map between the set of certain
\vartheta
-orbits and the set of all components of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
containing vertices of the form
(0\rightarrow G)
, see Proposition
4.15
.

In Section
5
we study components of
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
with finite cardinality containing a boundary vertex. In particular, we show that if
G
is an indecomposable non-projective Gorenstein projective module such that the component of
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
containing a boundary vertex
(0\rightarrow G)
is finite, then this component contains at most two distinct
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}
-orbits which are different from the
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}
-orbit of the boundary vertex
(0\rightarrow G)
, see Theorem
5.7
.

In the last section of the paper, we investigate an interesting three-periodicity phenomenon. We show that under some mild conditions, the cardinality of the finite components of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
containing a boundary vertex is divisible by 3, see Theorem
6.2
.
As it was mentioned by the referee, this kind of three-periodicity phenomenon, for some of the objects, is happening throughout the paper, see Remark
3.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
\Lambda
will always denote an Artin algebra, and
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
will denote the category of finitely generated right
\Lambda
-modules. By a subcategory we always mean a full subcategory. The subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
consisting of all projective modules is denoted by
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
. An additive subcategory
\mathscr{X}
of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
is called a resolving subcategory if it contains
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
and if it is closed under extensions and kernels of epimorphisms.

2.1
.

Functorially finite subcategories.
Let
\mathscr{X}
be an additive subcategory of an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
. Let
A\in\mathscr{A}
be an object. A right
\mathscr{X}
-approximation of
A
is a morphism
\varphi:X\rightarrow A
, with
X\in\mathscr{X}
satisfying the property that any other morphism
\varphi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow A
with
X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{X}
, factors through
\varphi
. Dually one can define the notion of a left
\mathscr{X}
-approximation of
A
. We say that
\mathscr{X}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory, resp. a covariantly finite subcategory, of
\mathscr{A}
if any object
A
in
\mathscr{A}
admits a right, resp. a left,
\mathscr{X}
-approximation. We say that
\mathscr{X}
is a functorially finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
if it is both a contravariantly finite and a covariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{X}\subseteq{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
be a contravariantly finite subcategory. It is proved in
[
28
, Corollary 2.6]
that if
\mathscr{X}
is a resolving subcategory additionally, then it is also covariantly finite and hence is a functorially finite subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
.

2.3
.

Frobenius categories.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category. A pair
(i,p)
of morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
, depicted by
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\rightarrow}}Z
, is called a kernel-cokernel pair if
i
is a kernel of
p
and
p
is a cokernel of
i
. Let
\mathscr{E}
be a fixed class of kernel-cokernel pairs in
\mathscr{C}
which is closed under isomorphism and satisfies certain axioms
[
13
, Definition 2.1]
. Then
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})
is called an exact category
[
34
]
and the distinguished kernel-cokernel pairs in
\mathscr{E}
are called conflations. In
[
27
, Appendix A]
, it is shown that exact categories are just full and extension-closed subcategories of abelian categories. For the other terminologies, we will follow
[
20
,
27
]
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})
be an exact category. An object
P\in\mathscr{C}
is called a projective object relative to
\mathscr{E}
, or simply an
\mathscr{E}
-projective object, if the functor
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}}(P,-)
from
\mathscr{C}
to
\mathscr{A}b
, the category of abelian groups, is exact. We say that
\mathscr{C}
has enough projective objects if for every object
X\in\mathscr{C}
there exists an admissible epimorphism
P\rightarrow X
such that
P
is
\mathscr{E}
-projective. The notions of
\mathscr{E}
-injective objects and a category having enough
\mathscr{E}
-injective objects are defined dually.

An exact category
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})
is called Frobenius if it has enough
\mathscr{E}
-projective and enough
\mathscr{E}
-injective objects and the classes of
\mathscr{E}
-projective and
\mathscr{E}
-injective objects coincide
[
13
, §13.4]
. Happel
[
24
, Theorem 2.6]
observed that the stable category
\underline{\mathscr{C}}
of a Frobenius category is a triangulated category. Recall that
\underline{\mathscr{C}}
is the category consisting of the same objects as
\mathscr{C}
. For two objects
\underline{X},\underline{Y}\in\underline{\mathscr{C}}
the morphism set
{\rm{Hom}}_{\underline{\mathscr{C}}}(\underline{X},\underline{Y})
is defined to be
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)
modulo the subgroup of
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)
consisting of all morphisms that factors through an
\mathscr{E}
-projective object. The stable Hom set is also denoted by
\underline{{\rm{Hom}}}
.

2.4
.

Auslander-Reiten sequences.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category. A morphism
p:Y\rightarrow Z
in
\mathscr{C}
is said to be right minimal if every morphism
h:Y\rightarrow Y
with
ph=p
is an isomorphism. A morphism
p:Y\rightarrow Z
in
\mathscr{C}
is called a right almost split morphism if it is not a retraction and each morphism
g:W\rightarrow Z
which is not a retraction factors through
p
. A right minimal almost split morphism is a morphism which is right minimal and right almost split in
\mathscr{C}
. Dually, left minimal, left almost split and left minimal almost split morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
are defined.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})
be an exact category. A conflation
\eta:0\rightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\rightarrow}}Z\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{E}
is called an almost split conflation, or almost split sequence, if
i
is a left minimal almost split morphism and
p
is a right minimal almost split morphism. In this case,
\eta
is called an almost split sequence starting with
X
or an almost split sequence ending with
Z
. Throughout, we call
X
,
Y
and
Z
, the left, middle and right term of
\eta
, respectively.

We say that
\mathscr{C}
has almost split sequences if each non-projective indecomposable object appears as the right term of an almost split sequence, and each non-injective indecomposable object appears as the left term of an almost split sequence. In addition, each projective indecomposable object should appear as the right term of a right minimal almost split morphism and each injective indecomposable object should appear as the left term of a left minimal almost split morphism.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\mathscr{C}={\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
and let
\tau_{\Lambda}=D{\rm{Tr}}
be the Auslander-Reiten translation, where
D
is the standard duality functor and
{\rm{Tr}}
stands for the transpose. It is known that the end terms of an almost split sequence are related to
\tau_{\Lambda}
and
\tau_{\Lambda}^{-1}={\rm{Tr}}D
, see e.g.
[
3
, §IV.2]
.

In view of this property, over an exact category
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})
that has almost split sequences, we define
\tau_{\mathscr{C}}
by saying that
\tau_{\mathscr{C}}Z=X
if and only if there exists an almost split sequence
0\rightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\rightarrow}}Z\rightarrow 0
in
\mathscr{E}
.

Remark 2.6
.

One of the old problems in representation theory of algebras is to determine which categories admit almost split sequences. One of the important results in this direction is due to Auslander and Smalø
[
9
, Theorem 2.4]
, showing that if
\mathscr{C}
is an extension closed and functorially finite subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, then
\mathscr{C}
has almost split sequences. Note that
\mathscr{C}
, as an extension closed full subcategory of the abelian category
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, is an exact category.

2.7
.

Auslander-Reiten quivers.

Recall that a quiver
\Delta
is a quadruple
(\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},s,t)
, where
\Delta_{0}
, resp.
\Delta_{1}
, is the set of vertices, resp. the set of arrows, of
\Delta
, and
s,t:\Delta_{1}\rightarrow\Delta_{0}
are two maps, called the source and target maps, which associate each arrow
\alpha
to its source
s(\alpha)
and its target
t(\alpha)
, respectively. Sometimes, when maps
s
and
t
are clear from the context, a quiver will be denoted just by the sets of vertices and arrows.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an exact category that has almost split sequences. The Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathscr{C}
is a quiver recording some important information of
\mathscr{C}
in the form of a quiver. It is a valued translation quiver
[
3
, §IV.4]
whose vertices are modules in
{\rm{ind}}\mathscr{C}
, the subcategory of isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{C}
. For two vertices
[X]
and
[Y]
in
{\rm{ind}}\mathscr{C}
, the arrows
[X]\rightarrow[Y]
are defined by using the notion of irreducible morphisms. These valued arrows of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
describe the minimal left almost split morphisms with indecomposable domains and the minimal right almost split morphisms with indecomposable codomains. The translation is given by the Auslander-Reiten translations
\tau_{\mathscr{C}}
and
\tau^{-1}_{\mathscr{C}}
. The connected components of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
are called Auslander-Reiten components of
\mathcal{C}
. Throughout this paper, the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathscr{C}
will be denoted by
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
.

If
[X]
is a vertex corresponding to an indecomposable projective object
X
, resp. indecomposable injective object
X
, then
[X]
is called a projective vertex, respectively an injective vertex, of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
. If
\mathscr{C}
is a Frobenius exact category, then the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathscr{C}
is denoted by
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathscr{C}}
. It is obtained by removing all projective-injective vertices of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}}
and hence removing all arrows attached to those vertices.

If
\mathscr{C}={\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, then
\Gamma_{{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda}
, resp.
\Gamma^{s}_{{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda}
, will be denoted by
\Gamma_{\Lambda}
, resp.
\Gamma^{s}_{\Lambda}
.

We omit the valuations of the arrows in the Auslander-Reiten quivers if they are not essential. We often identify the isomorphism class of an indecomposable object with its representative, or with the corresponding vertex in the Auslander-Reiten quiver.

For background on the AR-theory of Artin algebras the reader is referred to one of the books
[
8
,
3
]
or
[
44
]
. For details of the AR-theory of Krull-Schmidt categories we refer the reader to
[
30
]
.

2.8
.

Gorenstein projective modules.

Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra. A
\Lambda
-module
G
is called Gorenstein projective if there exists an exact sequence

P^{\bullet}:\cdots\rightarrow P^{-1}\xrightarrow{d^{-1}}P^{0}\xrightarrow{d^{0}}P^{1}\rightarrow\cdots

of finitely generated projective modules which is
{\rm{Hom}}_{\Lambda}(-,\Lambda)
-exact, and
G\cong{\rm{Ker}}d^{0}
. The subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
consisting of all Gorenstein projective modules will be denoted by
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
. In the following remark, we list some of the basic properties of the category
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
. The proof of these facts could be found in
[
10
,
15
]
. Let
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda^{\perp}
be the subcategory

\{M\in{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda\mid{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda}(G,M)=0\ {\rm for\ any}\ G\in{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda\}

of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra and let

{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
be the category of finitely generated Gorenstein projective
\Lambda
-modules.

(1)

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda}(G,P)=0
, for every Gorenstein projective module
G
and every projective module
P
.

(2)

{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda\cap{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda^{\perp}={\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
.

(3)

{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
is a resolving subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
.

(4)

{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
, as the full subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, is closed under extensions. So it inherits a canonical exact structure from
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. Its conflations are all short exact sequences in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
with all terms in
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
.

(5)

{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
is a Frobenius exact category: its class of (relative) projective objects is exactly the class of projective modules in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. This class also plays the role of relative injectives of the exact category
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
, see e.g.
[
15
, Proposition 2.1.11]
.

(6)

By Remark
2.6
,
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
has almost split sequences if it is a functorially finite subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. This is the case, for instance, when
\Lambda
is a Gorenstein algebra, i.e. the injective dimension of
\Lambda
both as a left and as a right
\Lambda
-module is finite
[
15
, Corollary 2.3.6]
. In this case, the Auslander-Reiten translation of
M
in
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
will be denoted by
\tau_{\mathscr{G}}
.

(7)

An Artin algebra
\Lambda
is called selfinjective if
\Lambda
is injective as a
\Lambda
-module, or equivalently, the projective and injective modules in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
coincide. Obviously, when
\Lambda
is a selfinjective algebra, all modules are Gorenstein projective, that is,
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda={\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
.

(8)

An Artin algebra
\Lambda
is said to be of finite Cohen-Macaulay type, CM-finite for short, if
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
is of finite type, that is, up to isomorphism, there are only finitely many indecomposable modules in
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
, see
[
10
]
. It is clear that if
\Lambda
is CM-finite, then
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda\subseteq{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
is functorially finite.

Let
M\in{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. Consider the short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\rightarrow\Omega_{\Lambda}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i_{M}}}{{\rightarrow}}P_{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{M}}}{{\rightarrow}}M\rightarrow 0

in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, where
P_{M}
is the projective cover of
M
. The module
\Omega_{\Lambda}M
is called the first syzygy of
M
. Inductively we can define
\Omega^{n}_{\Lambda}M
, the
n
-th syzygy of
M
, where
n\geq 2
is an integer.

If we assume further that
M
is a Gorenstein projective module, then we can define inductively
\Omega^{n}_{\Lambda}(M)
, for negative integers, using the notion of minimal left
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
-approximation. We need the following property of the syzygy modules.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
M
be an indecomposable non-projective Gorenstein projective module. Then

\Omega_{\Lambda}^{-1}\Omega_{\Lambda}M\simeq M\simeq\Omega_{\Lambda}\Omega^{-1}_{\Lambda}M.

Proof.

By Remark
2.9
,
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\Lambda}(G,P)=0
for every Gorenstein projective module
G
and every projective module
P
. So we can deduce that the morphism
i_{m}
in the short exact sequence (
2.1
) is a left
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
-approximation. It is minimal because
M
is indecomposable. So we obtain the first isomorphism. The second isomorphism is proved dually.
∎

Note that when
\Lambda
is a selfinjective algebra,
\Omega^{n}_{\Lambda}(M)
, with
n
a negative integer, is the same as the
n
-th cosyzygy of
M
in the usual sense, which is defined by using injective envelopes.

3.
Relative monomorphism categories

Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra. The morphism category
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, is a category whose objects are morphisms
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, denoted by
(X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}Y)
as an object in
{\mathcal{H}}(\Lambda)
. A morphism
\alpha:(X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}Y)\rightarrow(X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}Y^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)
, is a pair of morphisms
(\alpha_{1}\ \alpha_{2})
, where
\alpha_{1}:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
and

3.1
.

Let

{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. The relative monomorphism category of

with respect to

, denoted by

\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)

Of particular interest for us is when

Definition 3.2

Let
\Lambda

The following remark collects some of the basic properties of this category.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be an Artin algebra.

(1)

The equivalence
\mathcal{H}(\Lambda)\simeq{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}T_{2}(\Lambda)
in view of
[

or
[

(2)

By

as a subcategory of

. Thus

(3)

Let
{{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
be a contravariantly finite subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda

[

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\Lambda
is an indecomposable algebra, i.e.,
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}\Lambda
is a connected category. This implies that
{\rm{prj}\mbox{-}}T_{2}(\Lambda)

\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
contains

\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
is a connected category. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
is connected and of finite representation type, it follows from

that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{S}}
is connected. It means that there is a walk between any two vertices. Assume that
\Gamma
is an arbitrary component of
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
. Pick a vertex

of
\Gamma.
The connectedness of
\Gamma_{\mathcal{S}}
yields a walk

such that
y
is a projective-injective vertex and for

,
x_{d}
is not a projective vertex. Note that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{S}}
contains all projective-injective vertices. By
x_{d}\longleftrightarrow x_{d+1}
we mean that there is either an arrow
x_{d}\rightarrow x_{d+1}
or an arrow
x_{d+1}\rightarrow x_{d}
in
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
. If

is isomorphic to
(0\rightarrow P)
for some indecomposable projective module
P
, then by Proposition
3.10
,

has to be of the form
(a)
or
(c)
. If
y
is isomorphic to
(P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
for some indecomposable projective module
P
, then by Proposition
3.9
,
x_{1}
has to be of the form
(b)
or
(c)
. So for both cases the vertex

To state our next result we need some notations. Let
G
be an indecomposable non-projective Gorenstein projective
\Lambda
-module. The unique component of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
containing the vertex

will be denoted by

Notation 4.13
.

Let

be the complete set of all pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable non-projective Gorenstein projective modules. Set

\mathcal{O}:=\{[G]_{\vartheta}\mid G\in U\},

where

to be the set of all
\vartheta
-orbits of the stable category
\underline{\rm{Gprj}}\mbox{-}\Lambda
and

to be the set of all components of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
containing a vertex of the form
(0\rightarrow G)
, where

Lemma 4.14
.

There exists a well-defined map
\delta:\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
sending
[G]_{\vartheta}
to
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}
belong to the equivalence class
[G]_{\vartheta}
. Hence there is an integer

such that
\vartheta^{m}(G)=G^{\prime}.
Proposition
4.7
implies that
\tau^{3m}_{\mathcal{S}}(0\rightarrow G)=(0\rightarrow\vartheta^{m}(G))=(0\rightarrow G^{\prime})

and

\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}

and

With the above notation, the following statements hold.

(2)

In this section, let

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field and let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{G}}(\Lambda)
be of finite representation type. Let
\Delta
be a Dynkin diagram different from
\mathbb{D}_{4}
and let
G
be a non-trivial group of weakly admissible automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}\Delta
. If
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\Delta/G
is a component of the stable Auslander-Reiten quiver
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
, then 3 is a divisor of the cardinality of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Assume that
X
is a non-projective indecomposable Gorenstein-projective module. Then Proposition
4.7
implies that three applications of the Auslander-Reiten translation to the object
{\bf X}=(0\rightarrow X)
yield again an object of the same type. Hence the cardinality
d
of the
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}
-orbit of
{\bf X}
is a divisor of 3. In particular,
{\bf X}
is of
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}
-period
d
. Let us fix the following notation throughout the proof.

•

Let
\alpha
denote the possible generator of
G
.

•

For every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and a vertex
a
in
\Delta
, we let
\widetilde{(i,a)}
denote the associated
\alpha
-orbit of the vertex
(i,a)
in
\mathbb{Z}\Delta
, that is, the set of all
\alpha^{j}(i,a)
where
j
runs through
\mathbb{Z}
.

•

Let
{\bf X}
be a vertex of
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\Delta/G
that corresponds to the orbit of a vertex
(0,x)
in
\mathbb{Z}\Delta
, where
x
is a vertex in
\Delta.
Namely,
{\bf X}=\widetilde{(0,x)}
.

•

Let
U(v)
denote the
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}
-orbit of a vertex
v
in
\Gamma^{s}_{\mathcal{S}}
.

We check all possible cases based on Theorem
6.1
and show that for each case
3
is a divisor of
\mid\Gamma\mid
.

(1)
\Delta=\mathbb{E}_{n}
with
n=7,8.
By Theorem
6.1
, we have
\alpha=\tau^{r}
for some
r\geq 1.
According to the existence of the boundary vertex
{\bf X}
with
\tau^{d}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{(0,x)}=\widetilde{(0,x)}
in
\Gamma
, we deduce that
(-d,x)
lies in the
\tau^{r}
-orbit of
(0,x)
. Hence
(-d,x)=(rs,x)
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, and so
r
divides
d
. On the other hand, since
\tau^{r}(0,x)=(-r,x)
in
\mathbb{Z}\Delta
and
(0,x)
and
(-r,x)
have the same
\tau^{r}
-orbits, we have
\tau^{r}_{\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{(0,x)}=\widetilde{(0,x)}
. This implies
d
divides
r
. As both
r
and

are positive we get

. Now
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{E}_{n}/<\tau^{d}>
, and it implies that
\mid\Gamma\mid=nd
.

(2)
\Delta=\mathbb{E}_{6}.
If
\alpha=\tau^{r}
for some
r\geq 1
, then the same argument as the case
(1)
works. Assume

for some

Since

is a boundary vertex, having exactly one immediate predecessor, equivalently exactly one immediate successor,
x
is not possible one of the vertices 2, 3, 5. Assume

is either
1
or

consists of all pairs

, where

. Since

, we observe that

or
(rs,6)
for some

. So, for each of these cases we see that
r
divides
d
. By the definition of
\phi
, we see that the vertices
(j,3)
and
(j,4)
, for any

, are fixed under the automorphism
\phi
. So the
\phi\tau^{r}
-orbits of
(0,3)
and

are the same as the
\tau^{r}
-orbits of
(0,3)
and
(0,4)

for the vertices
(0,3)
and

, we obtain the equalities

and

. Indeed,

is the least number holding those conditions. As
\tau_{\mathcal{S}}
induces an automorphism on

, hence those qualities force to have the equality

, and so

and

{(3)}\ \Delta=\mathbb{D}_{n}
where

. This case is proved in the sprit of the two cases we have already investigated. However, for the convenience of the reader, we repeat the main points of this case.

If

for some

, then again the same argument as the case

works. Assume that

for some

. If the vertex

is the same as the

with

Keeping in mind that

, as required.

(5)

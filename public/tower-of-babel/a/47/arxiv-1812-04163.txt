KAM tori are no more than sticky


Abstract
When a Gevrey smooth perturbation is applied to a quasi-convex integrable Hamiltonian, it is known that the KAM invariant tori that survive are “sticky”, i.e. doubly exponentially stable. We show by examples the optimality of this effective stability.



B. Fayad, D. Sauzin

6 December 2018

1
Introduction

We are interested in effective stability around invariant
quasi-periodic tori of nearly integrable analytic or Gevrey regular
Hamiltonian systems. Under generic non degeneracy assumptions on the
integrable Hamiltonian, KAM theory (after Kolmogorv Arnold Moser)
guarantees the existence of a large measure set of invariant
quasi-periodic tori for the perturbed systems. The invariant tori
given by KAM theory have Diophantine frequency vectors. To study the
diffusion rate of orbits that start near these invariant tori, an
important tool is the Birkhoff Normal Forms (or BNF) at an invariant
torus, that introduce action-angle coordinates in which the system in
small neighborhoods of an invariant Diophantine torus becomes
integrable up to arbitrary high degrees in the Taylor series of the
Hamiltonian (see for example
[
Bi66
]
or
[
SM71
]
).

Exploiting the Diophantine property of the frequency vector of the
invariant torus, it is possible to collect estimates in the successive
BNFs and establish exponential stability of the torus, in the sense
that nearby solutions remain close to the invariant torus for an
interval of time which is exponentially large with respect to some
power of the inverse of the distance
r
to the torus, a power that
depends only on the Diophantine exponent
\tau
of the torus in the
case of real analytic Hamiltonians, and that involves additionally the
degree of Gevrey smoothness in the case of Gevrey smooth Hamiltonians
(
[
PW94
]
,
[
Po00
]
).

Combining BNF estimates with Nekhoroshev theory, Giorgilli and
Morbidelli proved in
[
MG95
]
that for integrable Hamiltonians with a
quasi-convex Hessian, the KAM tori of an analytic perturbation of the
Hamiltonian are doubly exponentially stable: the exponential stability
time
\exp\!\big(r^{-1/(\tau+1)}\big)
is promoted to

\exp\!\big(\!\exp\big(r^{-1/(\tau+1)}\big)\big)
.
Invariant quasi-periodic tori with this strong form of effective
stability are termed
sticky
.

Stickiness of the invariant tori was later extended in
[
BFN17
]
to a
residual and prevalent set of integrable Hamiltonians and to the
Gevrey category. It was proved there that generically, both in a
topological and measure-theoretical sense, an invariant Lagrangian
Diophantine torus of a Hamiltonian system is doubly exponentially
stable. Also, for a residual and prevalent set of integrable
Hamiltonians, for any small perturbation in Gevrey class, there is a
set of almost full Lebesgue measure of KAM tori which are doubly
exponentially
stable.

Our aim here, is to give examples showing that doubly exponential
stability cannot in general be
strengthened. Loosely stated our main result is the following

Theorem.

For arbitrary
N\geq 3
, there exist
quasi-convex Hamiltonian systems in
N
degrees of freedom that can be perturbed in the Gevrey smooth
category so that most of the invariant tori of the perturbed system
are no more than doubly exponentially stable.

The exact statements will be given in Section
3
. The diffusion mechanism we will use in our constructions is the so called Herman synchronized diffusion, that first appeared in
[
MS03
]
where the speed in Arnol’d diffusion is estimated for a class of nearly integrable system. In
[
MS03
]
, completely integrable systems with twist are considered and it is shown that it is possible to construct perturbations of size
{\epsilon}
in Gevrey class, that have orbits diffusing in action at an exponential rate in inverse powers of
{\epsilon}
. The diffusion rate is shown to be almost optimal due to the Nekhoroshev effective stability theory.

Our setting here is quite different, in this that the perturbative
parameter is not an extra parameter
{\epsilon}
but the
action
variable itself
when viewed as the distance
r
of the diffusive
orbit from the invariant torus. In this “singular perturbative
setting”, the nature of the construction is in fact expected to be
different from
[
MS03
]
since the diffusion rate is at best doubly
exponential in an inverse power of
r
, compared to
the simple exponential one can achieve in
the Arnol’d diffusion problem treated in
[
MS03
]
.

The new difficulties that arise in the singular perturbative setting, as well as the novel constructions to overcome them will be commented in the next section where the heuristics of our construction are described in detail.

2
Description of the construction

The construction of the diffusive flows is obtained by suspension from
a perturbative construction in the discrete setting of symplectic maps
on

M=({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}})^{n}\simeq{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
where
{\mathbb{T}}
denotes the torus
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

and
n\geq 2
.

We now explain the main ideas in the discrete construction in
the case
n=2
. We
concentrate on the diffusion rate from the neighborhood of a single
invariant torus. We will be dealing with perturbation of a product of
two twist maps of the annulus
{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, denote them by
F_{0}
and

G_{0}
,
F_{0}(\theta_{1},r_{1})=(\theta_{1}+{\omega}_{1}+r_{1}+{\mathbb{Z}},r_{1})
,

G_{0}(\theta_{2},r_{2})\coloneqq(\theta_{2}+{\omega}_{2}+r_{2}+{\mathbb{Z}},r_{2})
. Set

T_{0}=F_{0}\times G_{0}\colon\thinspace M\righttoleftarrow
. Observe that
T_{0}

has an invariant torus
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}={\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times\{(0,0)\}
, on which the restricted
dynamics is the translation of vector
{\omega}=({\omega}_{1},{\omega}_{2})
.

Let us explain how to perturb
T_{0}
into a map
T
that is tangent to

T_{0}
at
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
and that has pieces of orbits that diffuse away from a
neighborhood of
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
at a doubly exponentially small speed.
More precisely, we obtain a sequence
\rho_{n}\to 0
, points
z_{n}

such that
\operatorname{dist}(z_{n},{\mathcal{T}}_{0})<\rho_{n}
and times
\Theta_{n}
that are
doubly exponentially large in
1/\rho_{n}
, such that

\operatorname{dist}(T^{\Theta_{n}}(z_{n}),{\mathcal{T}}_{0})
and
\operatorname{dist}(T^{-\Theta_{n}}(z_{n}),{\mathcal{T}}_{0})

are both doubly exponentially large in
1/\rho_{n}
.
It will appear clearly from our diffusion mechanism that drifting away
from
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
by an amount
\rho_{n}
, or by an amount that is doubly
exponentially large in
1/\rho_{n}
, both require a doubly exponentially large time.

Herman synchronized diffusion

The diffusion mechanism we will use is the Herman synchronized
diffusion, that first appeared in
[
MS03
]
. Let us explain in some
words what is the synchronized diffusion. It is based on the following
mechanism of coupling of two twist maps of the annulus (the second one
being integrable with linear twist): at exactly one point
p
of a
well chosen periodic orbit of period
q
of the first twist map
in
M_{1}={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
the coupling consists of pushing the orbits in the second annulus up
in
M_{2}={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}

on some fixed vertical
\Delta
by an amount
1/q
that sends an
invariant curve whose rotation number is a multiple of
1/q
exactly
to another one having the same property (due to the linear twist
property).

The dynamics of the
q^{\rm th}
iterate of the coupled map on the
line
\{p\}\times\Delta\subset M_{1}\times M_{2}
will thus drift at a
linear speed : after
q^{2}
iterates the point will have moved by
1

in the second action coordinate
r_{2}
, and after
q^{3}
it will have
moved by
q
.
The diffusing orbits obtained this way are
bi-asymptotic to infinity: their
r_{2}
-coordinates travel from
-\infty

to
+\infty
at average speed
1/q^{2}
.

For this mechanism to be implemented with a Gevrey regular
small coupling of the two twist maps, it is necessary that the
periodic point
p
be isolated from the rest of the points on its
orbit by a distance
\sigma
that is greater than the inverse of some
power of
\ln q
, since
1/q
is the translation amount required from
the coupling that must be exclusively localized around
p
. We call
such periodic points “logarithmically” isolated.

Optimal rates in Arnol’d diffusion of
[
MS03
]

In
[
MS03
]
, only one periodic point is sufficient to have estimates on the Arnol’d diffusion rate in the nearly integrable system. In fact, in
[
MS03
]
, a completely integrable twist map of the annulus such as
F_{0}
is first perturbed to create a hyperbolic saddle point with a saddle connection (a pendulum). Near the separatrix of the pendulum,
one can find periodic orbits of arbitrary high period
q
and isolation
\sigma
that is determined by the hyperbolicity of the saddle point. More precisely, with an
{\epsilon}
perturbation of the integrable twist, the periodic orbits near the separatrix will then have an isolation of order
{\epsilon}^{1/2}
and choosing
q
exponentially large in the inverse of
{\epsilon}
allows to use the coupling mechanism with a second completely integrable linear twist to obtain diffusive orbits at exponential rate in the inverse of
{\epsilon}
.

Doubly exponential diffusion rates in the singular perturbative setting

In our singular perturbative setting, the main obstacles when one attempts to apply the synchronized diffusion mechanism are threefold : 1) the diffusion rate must be calculated from arbitrary small neighborhoods of the invariant torus
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
, hence many perturbations and many diffusive orbits may enter into play as opposed to the single orbit of
[
MS03
]
; 2) each perturbation must not affect
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
and must allow for further perturbations; 3) the Diophantine property on the frequency of
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
imposes, due to averaging, strong restrictions on the period and the isolation properties of the periodic points that come near the invariant torus.

The main step to prepare for the coupling construction is to be able
to perturb
F_{0}
in order to get an annulus map
F

on the first factor
M_{1}={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}

that is tangent to
F_{0}
at the circle
r=0
(we omit the
subscript
1
for
r_{1}
in this paragraph) and that
has a sequence of periodic points
p_{n}
at distance
r_{n}
from the
circle
r=0
and that are
\sigma_{n}
isolated from their
orbits. Since we will work with perturbations of
F_{0}
that are
compactly supported away from
r=0
, we cannot expect larger
isolation
\sigma_{n}
than an exponentially small quantity in the
inverse of
r_{n}
,
and the precise exponent involved in this
exponential is dictated by the Gevrey regularity
\alpha
only.
According to the above description of how the synchronized
diffusion mechanism functions, the period
q_{n}
of the point
p_{n}
,
that will also determine the order of the diffusion rate, should
not be taken smaller than an exponential in the inverse
of
\sigma_{n}
. Hence, the double exponential in
\frac{1}{r_{n}}
!!

Let us now suppose that a map
F
is constructed with such a sequence

p_{n}\in M_{1}
, and let us show how to obtain the coupling with the
map
G_{0}
which lives on the other factor

M_{2}={\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
. The main idea is to couple
F
and
G_{0}

separately at each periodic orbit with compactly supported Gevrey
regular coupling functions. Indeed, while performing locally the
couplings around the product of the orbit of
p_{n}
with
M_{2}
,
we keep the direct product structure of
F
with
G_{0}
in the products
of smaller neighborhoods of the circle
{\mathbb{T}}\times\{0\}\subset M_{1}

with
M_{2}
. Thus, the couplings that yield diffusive orbits involving
the successive points
p_{n}
are done inductively without affecting
each other.

How to perturb the first factor to get a sequence of isolated periodic points

We turn now to the perturbative techniques that allow to
obtain
F
. We put together two tools.
The first one allows us to perturb a periodic circular rotation of
period
P/Q
while fine-tuning the rotation number so as to create circle diffeomorphisms with a

\sigma
-isolated periodic point
p
of arbitrary large period
q
,
where
\sigma
is exponentially small in
Q
for large
Q
but otherwise independent
of
q
.
In particular, we can choose
q
exponentially large in
1/\sigma
(doubly exponentially large in
Q
).
The second tool is a trick due to M. Herman that
allows us to embed the circle dynamics thus obtained inside the phase
portrait of a perturbation of a linear twist map of the
annulus
M_{1}
. In fact, the periodic map will appear in the
neighborhood of the circle of period
P/Q
of the linear twist.
The coupling mechanism will then yield orbits that diffuse at speed

1/q^{2}
in
M_{1}\times M_{2}
. Of course, to conclude we
have to require that
1/Q
be larger than the distance

r_{Q}=\lvert{\omega}_{1}-P/Q\rvert

from the circle
{\mathbb{T}}\times\{0\}
to the periodic orbit of the linear twist near which the
isolated periodic point
p
is embedded.
At that stage, we could assume
{\omega}_{1}
irrational or Diophantine and
then impose that
1/Q
be of order
\sqrt{r_{Q}}
or larger, depending
on the Diophantine exponent of
{\omega}_{1}
, with the hope to refine the estimates on
\sigma
and thus on the
diffusing time.
But, since we want to embed, using Herman’s trick, the isolated
periodic point in
M_{1}
at distance
r

without affecting
the circle

{\mathbb{T}}\times\{0\}
, we must accept
the exponential smallness of the isolation parameter in
M_{1}
to be
dictated in the first place by the Gevrey regularity of our compactly supported perturbations, thus absorbing
the potential gain stemming from arithmetics.
This means that by our technique we cannot tackle the problem of
matching the diffusion rate with the doubly exponential stability
lower bounds obtained in
[
MG95
,
BFN17
]
, that are of the
form
\exp\!\big(\!\exp\big(r^{-\frac{1}{\alpha(\tau+1)}}\big)\big)
,
where
\alpha
refers to the Gevrey regularity class and
\tau
is the
Diophantine exponent of the translation vector.

To emphasize the role that arithmetics should play in optimizing the diffusion speed we may ask the following question that is similar to the one raised in
[
FK18
, Question 24]
for elliptic fixed points.

Question 1
.

Give an example of an analytic or Gevrey smooth Hamiltonian that has a non-resonant invariant torus with positive definite twist that is not more than exponentially stable in time.

It follows from
[
MG95
,
BFN17
,
BFN15
]
, that a super Liouville property must be required on the frequency vector of the invariant torus.

Questions on the optimality of the bounds, on
analytic perturbations and on the genericity of doubly exponential
diffusion

An interesting way to address the question of optimizing the bounds,
as well as to aim at analytic constructions, is to look for a single
analytic (or Gevrey smooth) perturbation of
F_{0}
that yields a map

\tilde{F}
that is tangent to
F_{0}
at
0
to some fixed degree, and
that has a sequence of periodic orbits
p_{n}
with isolation properties
related to the arithmetics of
{\omega}_{1}
(for instance, with
\sigma_{n}

of order
e^{r_{n}^{-1/(\tau^{\prime}+1)}}
, where
\tau^{\prime}
is such that
{\omega}_{1}

is not
\tau^{\prime}
-Diophantine). But even if such a perturbation of
F_{0}

is possible, it would still be a delicate task to perform an analytic
coupling with
G_{0}
since a single analytic intervention to couple the
neighborhood of the orbit of any periodic point
p_{n}
with the second
factor will affect the whole map everywhere and we cannot rely on the
nice direct product structure of
F
with
G_{0}
for further
perturbations as we do in the Gevrey category. One should probably
resort to the theory of normally hyperbolic invariant manifolds to say
that some kind of product structure remains valid at the periodic
orbits of the points
p_{n}
. Even then however, the linear character of the twist of the
second factor will definitely disappear, which will also bring extra
difficulties.

Let us make a last remark concerning the analytic category. In fact, obtaining examples of analytic Hamiltonians having a topologically unstable invariant torus with positive definite twist at the torus is a hard task by itself, let alone the control of the diffusion time that is the object of our investigation here. Real analytic Hamiltonians with unstable invariant tori and elliptic fixed points (with arbitrary frequencies in the case of
4
degrees of freedom) were obtained in
[
Fa18
,
FF18
]
, but these examples do not have positive definite twist.

Finally, besides the analytic question and the question of optimizing the bounds, one can ask whether the upper bounds on the diffusion rates that we obtain in our examples are generic for KAM tori, or for invariant quasi-periodic Diophantine tori in general.

Plan of the paper

Section
3
contains the main statements for
symplectomorphisms and for flows.
In Section 4, we state the main inductive step of the construction,
that yields a diffusive segment of orbit for a perturbation of

F_{0}\times G_{0}
linked to one isolated periodic point that will be
created on the first factor.
In Section 4.1 we explain how the main
inductive step is used to result in a diffusive invariant torus. In
Section 4.2 we elaborate on this to get simultaneously a large measure
set of invariant tori that are diffusive.

Sections 5 and 6 contain the
proof of the main inductive step.
Section 5 is devoted to the perturbation of
F_{0}
in order to get the
map
F
with isolated periodic points. In Section 5.1, we show how to
perturb circular rotations to obtain a periodic orbit with the
required isolation estimate and with arbitrary large period. In
Section 5.2 we show how this periodic orbit can be imbedded in a
perturbation of
F_{0}
. Section 6 introduces the coupling lemma of
F

with
G_{0}
and shows how to use it to get diffusion using the isolated
periodic point of
F
.

In Section 7 we provide the suspension trick that allows to transfer the results from the discrete case of symplectomorphisms to the continuous time context of Hamiltonian flows.

In the Appendix we collect and prove some necessary Gevrey estimates for maps and for flows that are used all along the paper.

3.1
Notations on diffusive dynamics and Gevrey functions

We use the notation

E(\nu)=E_{C,\gamma}(\nu)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{C\nu^{-\gamma}}}\quad\text{for $\nu>0$,}

(3.1)

for some choice of
C,\gamma>0
that we will explicit later.
We will say informally that “
E(\nu)
is doubly exponentially large for small
\nu
”.
Notice that

E(\nu)\gg E({\lambda}\nu)^{\mu}\kern 5.0pt\text{as $\nu\to 0$,}\qquad\text{for every ${\lambda}>1$ and $\mu>0$.}

(3.2)

Definition 3.1
.

Given a transformation
T
(or a flow) on a metric space
(M,d)
and
\nu>0
, we say that:

•

A point
z
of
M
is
\nu
-diffusive

if there exist an initial condition
{\hat{z}}\in M
and
a positive integer (or real)
t

such that
d({\hat{z}},z)\leq\nu
,
t\leq E(\nu)
and
d(T^{t}{\hat{z}},z)\geq E(2\nu)
.

•

A subset
X
of
M
is
\nu
-diffusive
if all points in
X
are

\nu
-diffusive.

•

A subset
X
of
M
is
diffusive
if there exists a sequence
\nu_{n}\to 0
such that

X
is
\nu_{n}
-diffusive for each
n
.

In the latter cases, we also say that
T
is
\nu
-diffusive
on
X
, resp.
diffusive on
X
.

Our goal is to construct examples of diffusive dynamics in
the context of near-integrable Hamiltonian systems and
exact-symplectic maps.
The requirement
d(T^{t}{\hat{z}},z)\geq E(2\nu)
might seem
exaggerated at first look, but, as mentioned earlier, there will be
no essential difference in the order of magnitude of the time needed
to diffuse by an amount
\nu
or by an amount as large as

E(2\nu)
.

We will also use a variant of the above definition: we say that a
point or a set is
\nu
-diffusive-
*
or
diffusive-
*
if the corresponding property
holds with the function
E
replaced by

E^{*}(\nu)\coloneqq E\big(\nu/\lvert\ln\nu\rvert\big)=\exp\big(\nu^{\gamma C\nu^{-\gamma}}\big)\quad\text{for $\nu\in(0,1)$.}

(3.3)

Notice that, as
\nu\to 0
,
E(\nu)\ll E^{*}(\nu)\ll\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{e}^{C^{\prime}\nu^{-\gamma^{\prime}}}}
for any
\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma
and
C^{\prime}>0
.

We will deal with Gevrey smooth functions and maps in several real
variables.
Periodicity may be required with respect to some of these variables,
in which case we will consider that each of the corresponding variables
is an angle, which lives in

{\mathbb{T}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

Recall that, given a real
\alpha\geq 1
, Gevrey-
\alpha
regularity is
defined by the requirement that the partial derivatives exist at all
(multi)orders
\ell
and are bounded by
CM^{\lvert\ell\rvert}\lvert\ell\rvert!^{\alpha}
for some
C
and
M
;
when
\alpha=1
this simply means analyticity, but we shall take
\alpha>1

throughout the article.
Upon fixing a real
L>0
which essentially stands for the inverse of
the previous
M
, one can define a Banach algebra

G^{\alpha,L}(K)\subset C^{\infty}(K)

when
K
is a Cartesian product of closed Euclidean balls and tori;
the elements of
G^{\alpha,L}(K)
are the “uniformly
Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
” functions on
K
.
In the non-compact case of a Cartesian product
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times K
with
K

as above, we define

G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times K)\subset{\mathcal{G}}^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times K)\subset C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times K),

where the smaller space is a Banach algebra, with norm
\lVert\,.\,\rVert_{\alpha,L}
,
consisting of uniformly Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times K
,
while
{\mathcal{G}}^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times K)
is a complete metric space,
with translation-invariant distance
d_{\alpha,L}
,
obtained by covering
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
by an increasing sequence of closed balls and considering
the Fréchet space structure accordingly.
Details are given in Appendix
A.1
.

3.2
Hamiltonian flows

Let
n\geq 2
.
We work in
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, with coordinates
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n},r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
,
or in
{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, with coordinates

(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n},\tau,r_{1},\ldots,r_{n},s)
.
We use the standard symplectic structures
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}\theta_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}r_{j}

or
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}\theta_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}r_{j}+\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge\mathrm{d}s
,
so that it is equivalent to consider a non-autonomous
Hamiltonian
h(\theta,r,t)
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which depends

1
-periodically on the time
t

or a Hamiltonian of the form

H(\theta,\tau,r,s)=s+h(\theta,r,\tau)
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.
Given an arbitrary
{\omega}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we will be interested in
non-autonomous
1
-periodic perturbations of

h_{0}(r)\coloneqq({\omega},r)+{\tfrac{1}{2}}(r,r)

(3.4)

or, equivalently, in certain autonomous perturbations of the integrable
Hamiltonian

H_{0}(r,s)\coloneqq s+h_{0}(r)

(3.5)

for which we denote by
{\mathcal{T}}_{(r,s)}
the invariant torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}\times\{(r,s)\}
associated with any
r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}

(it carries the quasi-periodic motion
\dot{\theta}={\omega}+r
,
\dot{\tau}=1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
L>0
be real. For any
{\epsilon}>0
there is
h\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}})

such that

(1)

d_{\alpha,L}(h_{0},h)<{\epsilon}
,

(2)

the Hamiltonian vector field generated by

H\coloneqq s+h(\theta,r,\tau)
is complete and, for every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the torus
{\mathcal{T}}_{(0,s)}\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{n+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is
invariant and diffusive for
H
.
Here the exponent implied in (
3.1
) is
\gamma=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
.

Note that if
{\omega}
is Diophantine then, for any
h
satisfying (1)
of Theorem
3.1
, we know from
[
MG95
,
BFN17
]
that

{\mathcal{T}}_{(0,s)}
is doubly exponentially stable (because
H_{0}
is quasi-convex).
More precisely, it holds that for any initial condition that is at distance
\rho
from
{\mathcal{T}}_{(0,s)}
, the orbit will stay within distance
2\rho
from
{\mathcal{T}}_{(0,s)}
during time
\exp\!\big(\!\exp\big(r^{-\frac{1}{\alpha(\tau+1)}}\big)\big)
,
where
\tau
is the Diophantine exponent.
Theorem
3.1
shows that we cannot expect in general a
stability better than doubly exponential. Observe that we do not
recover however the factor
1/(1+\tau)
in the exponent
governing our lower bound on the
diffusion time.

Note also that we know from
[
MG95
]
and
[
BFN17
]
that, for
H

such that
d_{\alpha,L}(H_{0},H)<{\epsilon}
, we have a set of invariant tori
that are doubly exponentially stable and fill a set whose complement
has measure going to
0
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
(for
r
in the unit ball for
example).
Our next result gives an example where most of these tori are no more
than doubly exponentially stable.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
L>0
be real. For any
{\epsilon}>0
, there exist
h\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}})

and a closed set

for

.

In fact, the union

3.3
Exact-symplectic maps

Let
{\omega}=({\omega}_{1},{\omega}_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Recall that
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We set
M_{1}\coloneqq{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and
M_{2}\coloneqq{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and
define

and

F_{0}(\theta_{1},r_{1})\coloneqq(\theta_{1}+{\omega}_{1}+r_{1}+{\mathbb{Z}},r_{1}),\quad G_{0}(\theta_{2},r_{2})\coloneqq(\theta_{2}+{\omega}_{2}+r_{2}+{\mathbb{Z}},r_{2}),

(3.6)

We say that a function is

Our first result for the discrete case is that one can find a Gevrey perturbation of the
integrable twist map

From now on,

1

(3.8)

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
L>0
be real.
For any
{\epsilon}>0

and

(1)

u
and

are flat for

,

(2)

\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\lVert v\rVert_{\alpha,L}<{\epsilon}
,

,
with exponent

in (

Here, when we write

with a function

as defined on

(and independent of the

.

and

,

4
The main building brick : localized diffusive orbits

We now fix real numbers

We also fix

as in Section

To prove Theorems

.

Let
\gamma\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
and
b\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{4}
.
There exists

such that,
for any
\nu>0
small enough and

,
there exist

and

(2)

\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\lVert v\rVert_{\alpha,L}{\leq\mathrm{e}^{-c\nu^{-\gamma}}}

(3)

{\mathcal{V}}(\bar{r},\nu)\times M_{2}
is invariant and
\big(3\nu,\tau,\tau^{b}\big)
-diffusive
for
T\coloneqq\Phi^{v}\circ\Phi^{u}\circ T_{0}
,
where

\tau\coloneqq E_{3c\gamma,\gamma}(\nu)
.

In Condition (3) of the statement, we have used a refinement of
Definition
3.1
:
we say that a subset
X
of
M
is
(\tilde{\nu},\tau,A)
-diffusive
for
T
if, for every point

of

{\hat{z}}\in M
and

,

.

The proof of Proposition

is in

.
(The reader will see that our choice of

4.1
Proof that Proposition
4.1

implies Theorem
3.3

We are given
{\epsilon}>0
and, without loss of generality, we can assume
that
{\epsilon}
is small enough so that we can apply
Proposition
4.1
for every
n\geq 1
with the
following values of
\nu
and

:

\nu_{n}\coloneqq(c\gamma)^{1/\gamma}10^{-n}{\epsilon},\quad\bar{r}^{(n)}\coloneqq 2\nu_{n}.

We thus obtain
u_{n}\in G^{\alpha,L}(M_{1})
, supported in
{\mathcal{V}}(\bar{r}^{(n)},\nu_{n})
,
and
v_{n}\in G^{\alpha,L}(M_{1}\times M_{2})
, supported in
{\mathcal{V}}(\bar{r}^{(n)},\nu_{n})\times M_{2}
,
which also satisfy Conditions (2) and (3) of Proposition
4.1
.
Observe that for any two different values of

the supports are

(because
\bar{r}^{(n)}-\nu_{n}>\bar{r}^{(n+1)}+\nu_{n+1}

Since

\mathrm{e}^{x}>x\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{e}^{-x}<x^{-1}\quad\text{for all $x>0$,}

(4.1)

we have

\mathrm{e}^{-c\nu_{n}^{-\gamma}}=\big(\mathrm{e}^{-c\gamma\nu_{n}^{-\gamma}}\big)^{1/\gamma}<\big(c\gamma\nu_{n}^{-\gamma}\big)^{-1/\gamma}=(c\gamma)^{-1/\gamma}\nu_{n},

(4.2)

hence the formulas

and
v\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}v_{n}

define functions
u\in G^{\alpha,L}(M_{1})
and
v\in G^{\alpha,L}(M_{1}\times M_{2})

with
\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\lVert v\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\sum\mathrm{e}^{-c\nu_{n}^{-\gamma}}<{\epsilon}
.
Moreover, since the

’s,
v_{n}
’s and all their partial derivatives
vanish for

, the same is true for
u
and
v
.
The functions
u
and
v
thus satisfy properties (1)–(2) of Theorem

3.3
.

We claim that
T=\Phi^{v}\circ\Phi^{u}\circ T_{0}
also satisfies
property (3) of Theorem
3.3
.
Indeed, the disjointness of the supports implies that
T
coincides
with
\Phi^{v_{n}}\circ\Phi^{u_{n}}\circ T_{0}
on

{\mathcal{V}}(\bar{r}^{(n)},\nu_{n})\times M_{2}
.
Now, for each
z\in{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
and
n\geq 1
, we can pick
\bar{z}\in{\mathcal{V}}(\bar{r}^{(n)},\nu_{n})\times M_{2}
such that

,
and then, by (3) of Proposition
4.1
, we can find

{\hat{z}}\in{\mathcal{V}}(\bar{r}^{(n)},\nu_{n})\times M_{2}
and
t\leq\tau_{n}\coloneqq E_{3c\gamma,\gamma}(\nu_{n})
such that
d({\hat{z}},\bar{z})\leq 3\nu_{n}
and

d(T^{t}{\hat{z}},\bar{z})\geq\tau_{n}^{b}
. We get
d({\hat{z}},z)\leq 5\nu_{n}
and
\tau_{n}=E_{C,\gamma}(5\nu_{n})
if we use
C\coloneqq 3c\gamma\cdot 5^{\gamma}
in the definition (
3.1
) of the doubly exponentially large function
E_{C,\gamma}
.
Then,
d(T^{t}{\hat{z}},z)>\tau_{n}^{b}-2\nu_{n}>\frac{1}{2}\tau_{n}^{b}\gg E_{C,\gamma}(10\nu_{n})
by (
3.2
),
hence
T
is
5\nu_{n}
-diffusive on
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
for every
n
large enough.

4.2
Proof that Proposition
4.1
implies
Theorem
3.4

We are given
{\epsilon}>0
and, without loss of generality, we can assume

{\epsilon}\leq 1
.
Let
\gamma\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
and let
c
be as in Proposition
4.1
.

Here is a definition that we will use from now on:
we say that a subset
Y
of a metric space

is

-dense
if,
for every
z\in X
, there exists
\bar{z}\in Y
such that

.

a)

We first define a fast increasing sequence of integers by

N_{1}\coloneqq\left\lceil\exp({4\kappa/{\epsilon}})\right\rceil,\qquad N_{i}\coloneqq N_{i-1}\left\lceil\exp\big(\exp\big(\tilde{C}(N_{i-1}\ln N_{i-1})^{\gamma}\big)\big)\right\rceil\quad\text{for $i\geq 2$,}

(4.3)

where
\kappa\coloneqq\max\{1,(c\gamma)^{-1/\gamma}\}
and
\tilde{C}\coloneqq\max\{6c\gamma,1/\gamma\}
.
We also set

\tilde{\nu}_{i}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N_{i}},\qquad\nu_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\tilde{\nu}_{i}}{\lvert\ln\tilde{\nu}_{i}\rvert}=\frac{1}{N_{i}\ln N_{i}},\qquad{\tau_{i}\coloneqq E_{3c\gamma,\gamma}(\nu_{i}).}

According to Lemma
B.1
in the appendix, one has

2\nu_{i}N_{i}=\tfrac{2}{\ln N_{i}}\leq 2^{-2i+1}{\epsilon}/\kappa<1.

(4.4)

b)

We now construct a sequence
(Y_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of mutually disjoint
subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
such that, for each

,

(i)

Y_{i}
is a disjoint union of at most
N_{i}
open arcs

,

(ii)

each of these open arcs can be written

Y^{(i,j)}=(r^{(i,j)}-\nu_{i},r^{(i,j)}+\nu_{i})\!\!\mod{\mathbb{Z}}
, with
r^{(i,j)}\in[0,1)
,

(iii)

Y_{i}
is
\tilde{\nu}_{i}
-dense in
({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})-\bigsqcup\limits_{1\leq i^{\prime}\leq i-1}Y_{i^{\prime}}
,

(iv)

is a disjoint union
of
N_{i}
closed arcs of equal length.

To do so, we start with
r^{(1,j)}\coloneqq\frac{j-1}{N_{1}}
for

and define the arcs
Y^{(1,j)}
and the set
Y_{1}

by (i)–(ii)
(the disjointness requirement results from

).
We then go on by induction and suppose that, for a given

,

.
As a consequence of (i)–(ii) and (

), we have

(4.5)

We observe that
M_{i+1}\coloneqq\frac{N_{i+1}}{N_{i}}
is an integer
\geq 3

Inside each closed arc mentioned in (

Indeed, on the one hand

on the other hand, the common length of the closed arcs of (

\mu_{i}=\tfrac{1}{N_{i}}\Big(1-\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}\operatorname{Leb}(Y_{i^{\prime}})\Big)>2^{-i-1}/N_{i}

Labelling all the new open arcs as

their

(note that

is
\frac{\mu_{i}}{M_{i+1}}
-dense in each closed arc
mentioned in (

),
and
\frac{\mu_{i}}{M_{i+1}}<\frac{1}{N_{i}M_{i+1}}=\tilde{\nu}_{i+1}
).

c)

Now, for each

Consider the finite sums

:

,

(4.6)

.

We claim that

Secondly the functions

Lastly, by virtue of the previous point, for each

.
In view of the definition of

, it is sufficient to show that,

is

Definition 5.1

\geq\sigma

5.1
Circle diffeomorphisms with isolated periodic points

We start by constructing a circle diffeomorphism with an isolated periodic point.
For any

we denote by

a function in

satisfying

\text{${\Delta}_{Q}$ is $\tfrac{1}{Q}$-periodic,}\quad 0\leq{\Delta}_{Q}\leq 1,\quad{\Delta}_{Q}(0)=0,\quad\text{${\Delta}_{Q}\equiv 1$ on $\big[\tfrac{1}{4Q},\tfrac{3}{4Q}\big].$}

(5.1)

Since
\alpha>1
, every space
G^{\alpha,L^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}})
contains such a function, and one can choose it so that

\lVert{\Delta}_{Q}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}\leq L^{\prime\alpha}\exp(\tilde{c}\,Q^{\gamma})

(5.2)

with
\tilde{c}=\tilde{c}(\alpha,L^{\prime})
independent of
Q

according to Lemma
A.5
in the appendix
(take

{\Delta}_{Q}(\theta)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\eta_{2Q}\big(\frac{2j-1}{2Q}+\theta\big)

and
\tilde{c}\coloneqq L^{\prime-\alpha}+\frac{1}{\gamma}+2^{\gamma}c_{1}(\alpha,L^{\prime})

with the notation of Lemma
A.5
, using the inequalities

L^{\prime-\alpha}\leq\mathrm{e}^{L^{\prime-\alpha}}
and
1\leq Q\leq\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{\gamma}Q^{\gamma}}
).

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
Q\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
and

be coprime and let

.
Then, for every

,
there exist an integer

and a real

•

\ell\leq q\leq\ell Q
and

,

•

the point
\frac{1}{2Q}+{\mathbb{Z}}

\sigma
-isolated for the circle map

(5.3)

If moreover
\ell\geq 2Q
, then the set
\{f_{\sigma,{\delta}}^{s}(\frac{1}{2Q}+{\mathbb{Z}})\mid s\in{\mathbb{N}},\;Q\leq s\leq 2Q-1\}

\frac{1}{Q}
-dense in
{\mathbb{T}}

Proof.

a)

We define

of
\ell
and
\ell P+1

with

,
hence
\ell\leq q\leq\ell Q
.

The condition

F_{\sigma,{\delta}}(x)\coloneqq x+\frac{P}{Q}+{\delta}+\sigma{\Delta}_{Q}(x)

{\mathbb{R}}
such that

Formula (

of
{\mathbb{T}}

.
We will tune

.

b)

To study the dynamics of

\tilde{G}_{\sigma,0}^{\ell}(X_{0})<X_{0}+1=\tilde{G}_{0,\frac{1}{\ell Q}}^{\ell}(X_{0})\leq\tilde{G}_{\sigma,\frac{1}{\ell Q}}^{\ell}(X_{0})

Going back to the variable

of

We now suppose

Using the second part of (

Since

5.2
Herman imbedding trick of circle diffeomorphisms into twist maps

We will have to imbed the circle dynamics of Proposition
5.2

into the annulus
via
a perturbation of the twist map
F_{0}
. For
this we will use the celebrated technique introduced by Herman in

[
He84
]
to imbed circle dynamics as restricted dynamics on
an invariant graph by a twist map.

Recall that trivial examples of symplectic maps of the annulus are
given by

\Phi^{h}(\theta,r)=\big(\theta+h^{\prime}(r)+{\mathbb{Z}},r\big),\qquad\Phi^{w}(\theta,r)=\big(\theta,r-w^{\prime}(\theta)\big),

(5.11)

where the function
h=h(r)
does not depend on the angle
\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}
,
and the function
w=w(\theta)
does not depend on the action
r
.
In particular,

h(r)\equiv{\omega}_{1}r+{\tfrac{1}{2}}r^{2}\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0pt\Phi^{h}=F_{0}.

(5.12)

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that we are given a circle diffeomorphism of the form

\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}\mapsto f(\theta)=\theta+h^{\prime}\big(\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)\!\!\mod{\mathbb{Z}},

where
h=h(r)
and
{\epsilon}={\epsilon}(\theta)
are smooth functions and

\hat{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the equation

-w^{\prime}={\epsilon}-{\epsilon}\circ f^{-1},

(5.13)

determines a smooth function
w=w(\theta)
up to an additive constant,
and the annulus map

\Phi^{w}\circ\Phi^{h}\colon\thinspace(\theta,r)\mapsto\big(\theta+h^{\prime}(r)+{\mathbb{Z}},r-w^{\prime}(\theta+h^{\prime}(r))\big)

(5.14)

leaves invariant the graph
\big\{\big(\theta,\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)\mid\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}\big\}
, with induced dynamics
\theta\mapsto f(\theta)
on
it.

Proof.

Let us check that the right-hand side of (
5.13
) has zero mean value:

\langle{\epsilon}\circ f^{-1}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\epsilon}\big(f^{-1}\big(\tilde{\theta}\big)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{\theta}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\epsilon}(\theta)f^{\prime}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\quad\text{and}\quad f^{\prime}(\theta)=1+\frac{\mathrm{d}\,}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\big[h^{\prime}\big(\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)\big],

hence
\langle{\epsilon}\rangle-\langle{\epsilon}\circ f^{-1}\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\epsilon}(\theta)\frac{\mathrm{d}\,}{\mathrm{d}\theta}\big[h^{\prime}\big(\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)\big]\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\theta)h^{\prime}\big(\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=0
,
since this is the mean value of the derivative of the periodic
function

h\big(\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)
. Consequently, any primitive of
{\epsilon}-{\epsilon}\circ f^{-1}
induces a smooth
function on
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Now, consider an arbitrary point

(\theta,r)=\big(\theta,\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)

on the graph mentioned in the statement.
Its image by
\Phi^{w}\circ\Phi^{h}
is

(\theta_{1},r_{1})\coloneqq\big(\theta+h^{\prime}(r)+{\mathbb{Z}},r-w^{\prime}(\theta_{1})\big)
.
We have

\theta_{1}=\theta+h^{\prime}\big(\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\big)+{\mathbb{Z}}=f(\theta)

and
r_{1}=\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)-w^{\prime}(\theta_{1})=\hat{r}+{\epsilon}\circ f^{-1}(\theta_{1})-w^{\prime}(\theta_{1})=\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta_{1})
.
∎

Let us have a look at the solutions of (
5.13
)
in the case of Gevrey data, with
h
as in (
5.12
),

i.e.

h^{\prime}(r)\equiv{\omega}_{1}+r
:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
L^{\prime}>L
.
Suppose

f(\theta)=\theta+{\omega}_{1}+\hat{r}+{\epsilon}(\theta)\!\!\mod{\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{for all $\theta\in{\mathbb{T}}$,}\quad\text{and}\quad{\epsilon}={\delta}+{\epsilon}_{*},

where
\hat{r},{\delta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\epsilon}_{*}\in G^{\alpha,L^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}})
satisfies

\lVert{\epsilon}_{*}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}

with
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}={\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}(\alpha,L,L^{\prime})
as in Lemma
A.4
.
Then
f
is a circle diffeomorphism and Equation (
5.13
) has a solution
w
such that

\lVert w\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq(1+2L^{\alpha})\lVert{\epsilon}_{*}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}.

(5.15)

Proof.

We can write
f=(\operatorname{Id}+{\omega})\circ(\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon}_{*})
with
{\omega}\coloneqq{\omega}_{1}+\hat{r}+{\delta}
.
By Lemma
A.4
, we obtain that
\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon}_{*}
is a
diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}
, which (because of periodicity) can be viewed
as the lift of a circle diffeomorphism.
Hence
f
is a circle diffeomorphism and the right-hand side of (
5.13
)
is

g\coloneqq{\epsilon}-{\epsilon}\circ f^{-1}={\epsilon}_{*}-{\epsilon}_{*}\circ f^{-1}={\epsilon}_{*}-{\epsilon}_{*}\circ(\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon}_{*})^{-1}\circ(\operatorname{Id}-{\omega}).

Lemma
A.4
yields

\lVert{\epsilon}_{*}\circ(\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon}_{*})^{-1}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\lVert{\epsilon}_{*}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}
, which easily implies

\lVert g\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq 2\lVert{\epsilon}_{*}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}
.

Now, we already know that

has zero mean value, and a solution
to (

) can be defined by the formula

w(\theta+{\mathbb{Z}})\equiv-\int_{0}^{\theta}g(\theta_{1})\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\quad\text{for $\theta\in(-{\tfrac{1}{2}},{\tfrac{1}{2}}]$.}

One has
\lVert w\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq{\tfrac{1}{2}}\lVert g\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{T}})}
and, for
each

,

\frac{L^{(k+1)\alpha}}{(k+1)!^{\alpha}}\lVert w^{(k+1)}\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{T}})}=\frac{L^{(k+1)\alpha}}{(k+1)!^{\alpha}}\lVert g^{(k)}\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq L^{\alpha}\cdot\frac{L^{k\alpha}}{k!^{\alpha}}\lVert g^{(k)}\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{T}})},

whence
\lVert w\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq({\tfrac{1}{2}}+L^{\alpha})\lVert g\rVert_{\alpha,L}

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Consider the mapping

{\mathcal{S}}_{\omega}\colon\thinspace(\theta,r,t)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}\mapsto\big(\theta+(1-t){\omega}+{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},r\big)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
and
{\Lambda}>0
be real, and

{\Lambda}_{1}\geq{\Lambda}\Big(1+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\lvert{\omega}_{i}\rvert^{1/\alpha}\Big)
.
Then

w\in G^{\alpha,{\Lambda}_{1}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0ptw\circ{\mathcal{S}}_{\omega}\in G^{\alpha,{\Lambda}}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}})\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\lVert w\circ{\mathcal{S}}_{\omega}\rVert_{\alpha,{\Lambda}}\leq\lVert w\rVert_{\alpha,{\Lambda}_{1}}.

Proof.

One can check that, for every
(p,q,s)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}
,

\partial_{\theta}^{p}\partial_{r}^{q}\partial_{t}^{s}(w\circ{\mathcal{S}}_{\omega})=(-1)^{s}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\;\text{s.t.}\;\lvert m\rvert=s}{\omega}_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots{\omega}_{n}^{m_{n}}(\partial_{\theta}^{p+m}\partial_{r}^{q}w)\circ{\mathcal{S}}_{\omega},

whence, with the notation
{\Omega}\coloneqq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\lvert{\omega}_{i}\rvert

\displaystyle\lVert w\circ{\mathcal{S}}_{\omega}\rVert_{\alpha,{\Lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p,q,m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}}\frac{{\Omega}^{\lvert m\rvert}{\Lambda}^{\lvert p+q+m\rvert\alpha}}{p!^{\alpha}q!^{\alpha}\lvert m\rvert!^{\alpha}}\lVert\partial_{\theta}^{p+m}\partial_{r}^{q}w\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,q\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}}A_{\ell}\frac{{\Lambda}^{\lvert\ell+q\rvert\alpha}}{q!^{\alpha}}\lVert\partial_{\theta}^{\ell}\partial_{r}^{q}w\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{T}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\quad\text{with}\kern 5.0ptA_{\ell}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{p,m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\;\text{s.t.}\;p+m=\ell}\frac{{\Omega}^{\lvert m\rvert}}{p!^{\alpha}\lvert m\rvert!^{\alpha}}.

∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Consider an interval

and the mapping

\alpha\geq 1
and

Then

Let

for each

, as in

We fix real numbers
\alpha\geq 1
and
L>0
.

A.1
Gevrey functions and Gevrey maps

Here we adapt definitions and facts taken from
[
MS03
]
,
[
MS04
]
and
[
FMS18
]
.

The Banach algebra
G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)
of uniformly Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
functions

Let
N\geq 1
be integer. We will deal with real functions of
N
variables
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K
, where
M\geq 0
and
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N-M}
is a Cartesian
product of closed Euclidean balls and tori. We define the uniformly Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K
by

G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)\coloneqq\{f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)\mid\lVert f\rVert_{\alpha,L}<\infty\},\\
\lVert f\rVert_{\alpha,L}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}^{N}}\frac{L^{\lvert\ell\rvert\alpha}}{\ell!^{\alpha}}\lVert\partial^{\ell}f\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)}.

(A.1)

We have used the standard notations

\lvert\ell\rvert=\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{N}
,
\ell!=\ell_{1}!\ldots\ell_{N}!
,

\partial^{\ell}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}\ldots\partial_{x_{N}}^{\ell_{N}}
,
and

{\mathbb{N}}\coloneqq\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
.
The space
G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)
turns out to be a Banach algebra,
with

\lVert fg\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\alpha,L}\lVert g\rVert_{\alpha,L}

(A.2)

for all
f
and
g
,
and there are “Cauchy-Gevrey inequalities”:
if
0<L_{0}<L
,
then

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{N};\ |m|=p}\lVert\partial^{m}f\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq\frac{p!^{\alpha}}{(L-L_{0})^{p\alpha}}\lVert f\rVert_{\alpha,L}\quad\text{for all $p\in{\mathbb{N}}$.}

(A.3)

When necessary, we use the notation
\lVert\,.\,\rVert_{\alpha,L,{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K}

instead of
\lVert\,.\,\rVert_{\alpha,L}
to keep track of the domain to
which the norm relates.

The metric space
{\mathcal{G}}^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)

When
M\geq 1
, instead of restricting ourselves to uniformly
Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K
, we may cover the
factor
{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
by an increasing sequence of closed balls and consider a
Fréchet space accordingly.
For technical reasons, we choose the sequences

L_{j}\coloneqq 2^{-\frac{j-1}{\alpha}}L,\quad R_{j}\coloneqq 2^{j}\qquad\text{for $j\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}$,}\vskip-6.45831pt

and set

{\mathcal{G}}^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)\coloneqq\bigcap_{j\geq 1}G^{\alpha,L_{j}}\big(\overline{B}_{R_{j}}\times K\big),\quad d_{\alpha,L}(f,g)\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-j}\min\big\{1,\lVert g-f\rVert_{\alpha,L_{j},\overline{B}_{R_{j}}\times K}\big\}.

(A.4)

Clearly,
G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)\subset{\mathcal{G}}^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{M}\times K)
but the
inclusion is strict,
and the larger space is a complete metric space for the
distance
d_{\alpha,L}
.

This construction is needed in Section
7
only.
In the rest of this appendix, we focus on uniformly Gevrey functions and
maps on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(with
M=N
and no factor
K
).

Composition with uniformly Gevrey-
(\alpha,L)
maps

For
N\geq 1
integer, we define

G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\coloneqq\{F\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\mid\lVert F\rVert_{\alpha,L}<\infty\},\\
\lVert F\rVert_{\alpha,L}\coloneqq\lVert F_{[1]}\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\cdots+\lVert F_{[N]}\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

(A.5)

This is a Banach space.

We also define

{\mathcal{N}}^{*}_{\alpha,L}(f)\coloneqq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}^{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}}\frac{L^{\lvert\ell\rvert\alpha}}{\ell!^{\alpha}}\lVert\partial^{\ell}f\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})},

so that

\lVert f\rVert_{\alpha,L}=\lVert f\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+{\mathcal{N}}^{*}_{\alpha,L}(f)
.

Lemma A.0
.

Let
L_{0}\in(0,L)
. There exists
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{c}}={\epsilon}_{\operatorname{c}}(\alpha,L,L_{0},N)
such that,
for any
f\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})

and
F=(F_{[1]},\ldots,F_{[N]})\in G^{\alpha,L_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, if

{\mathcal{N}}^{*}_{\alpha,L_{0}}(F_{[1]}),\ldots,{\mathcal{N}}^{*}_{\alpha,L_{0}}(F_{[N]})\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{c}},

then
f\circ(\operatorname{Id}+F)\in G^{\alpha,L_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and

\lVert f\circ(\operatorname{Id}+F)\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{\alpha,L}
.

The proof is in Appendix A of
[
FMS18
]
.

A.2
Comparison estimates for Gevrey flows

In Section
4.2
, we use comparison estimates for the flows
of two nearby Gevrey Hamiltonian systems.
We prove them here, building upon some facts which are proved in
[
FMS18
]

about the flows of Gevrey vector fields.

Lemma A.1

(General case)
.

Suppose that
0<L_{0}<L
and
N\geq 1
.
Then there exists
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}={\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}(\alpha,L,L_{0},N)>0
such that, for every vector field
X\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
with
\lVert X\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}
,
the time-
1
map
\Phi
of the flow generated by
X
satisfies

\lVert\Phi-\operatorname{Id}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq\lVert X\rVert_{\alpha,L}

(A.6)

and, if we are given another vector field
\tilde{X}\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
with
\lVert\tilde{X}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}
,
then its time-
1
map
\tilde{\Phi}
satisfies

\lVert\tilde{\Phi}-\Phi\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq 2\lVert\tilde{X}-X\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

(A.7)

Proof.

The first part of the statement is exactly Part (i) of Lemma A.1 from

[
FMS18
]
.
There, the flow
t\in[0,1]\mapsto\Phi(t)
was obtained by considering
the functional
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\xi)
defined by

\mathcal{F}(\xi)(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}X\circ\big(\operatorname{Id}+\xi(\tau)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using an auxiliary
L^{\prime}\in(L_{0},L)
and Lemma
A.0
, it was
shown that, if
\lVert X\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}
small enough, then
\mathcal{F}

maps into itself

\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{\,\xi\in C^{0}\big([0,1],G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\big)\mid\lVert\xi\rVert\leq\lVert X\rVert_{\alpha,L}\,\}

(which is a closed ball in a Banach
space) and has a unique fixed point, none other than

\xi^{*}(t)\coloneqq\Phi(t)-\operatorname{Id}
.

In that proof,
\mathcal{F}
was shown to be
K
-Lipschitz, with

K\coloneqq\max_{i,j}\lVert\partial_{x_{j}}X_{[i]}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}
.
We can ensure
K\leq{\tfrac{1}{2}}
by diminishing
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}
if necessary and using (
A.3
).
Then, for
any

\xi_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
, the fixed point
\xi^{*}
is the
limit of the sequence of iterates
(\mathcal{F}^{k}(\xi_{0}))_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and

\lVert\xi^{*}-\xi_{0}\rVert\leq 2\lVert\mathcal{F}(\xi_{0})-\xi_{0}\rVert
.

Now, suppose we also have
\lVert\tilde{X}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}
.
The time-
t
map of
\tilde{X}
is thus
\tilde{\Phi}(t)=\operatorname{Id}+\tilde{\xi}^{*}(t)
,
with
\tilde{\xi}^{*}
fixed point of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{B}\righttoleftarrow
.
Lemma
A.0
yields

\lVert\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}(\xi)\rVert=\lVert\int_{0}^{t}(\tilde{X}-X)\circ\big(\operatorname{Id}+\xi(\tau)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\rVert\leq\lVert\tilde{X}-X\rVert_{\alpha,L}\quad\text{for any $\xi\in\mathcal{B}$,}

thus we can compare the fixed points
\xi^{*}
and
\tilde{\xi}^{*}
by writing
the former as the limit of the sequence
(\mathcal{F}^{k}(\xi_{0}))_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with

\xi_{0}\coloneqq\tilde{\xi}^{*}
; we get

\lVert\xi^{*}-\tilde{\xi}^{*}\rVert\leq 2\lVert\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\xi}^{*})-\tilde{\xi}^{*}\rVert=2\lVert\mathcal{F}(\tilde{\xi}^{*})-\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\tilde{\xi}^{*})\rVert\leq\lVert\tilde{X}-X\rVert_{\alpha,L},

which yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Hamiltonian case)
.

Suppose that
0<L_{0}<L
and
n\geq 1
.
Then there exist
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{H}},C_{0}>0
such that, for every
u\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
with
\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{H}}
,

\lVert\Phi^{u}-\operatorname{Id}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq C_{0}\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L},

(A.8)

and, given another
\tilde{u}\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
with
\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{H}}
,

\lVert\Phi^{\tilde{u}}-\Phi^{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq C_{0}\lVert\tilde{u}-u\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

(A.9)

Proof.

Let
L^{\prime}\coloneqq(L_{0}+L^{\prime})/2
.
Any
u\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
generates a Hamiltonian vector

X_{u}
which, according to (
A.3
) with

,
satisfies

\lVert X_{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2n};\ |m|=1}\lVert\partial^{m}u\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}\leq(L-L^{\prime})^{-\alpha}\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

Similarly,
\lVert X_{\tilde{u}}-X_{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}\leq(L-L^{\prime})^{-\alpha}\lVert\tilde{u}-u\rVert_{\alpha,L}
.
Thus, with
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{H}}\coloneqq(L-L^{\prime})^{\alpha}{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{f}}(\alpha,L^{\prime},L_{0},2n)
and

we get

\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L},\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{H}}\kern 5.0pt\Rightarrow\kern 5.0pt\lVert\Phi^{u}-\operatorname{Id}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq{\tfrac{1}{2}}C_{0}\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt\lVert\Phi^{\tilde{u}}-\Phi^{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq C_{0}\lVert\tilde{u}-u\rVert_{\alpha,L}.

∎

Corollary A.3

(Iteration of maps of the form
\Phi^{v}\circ\Phi^{u}\circ T_{0}
)
.

Suppose that
n\geq 1
.
Then there exist
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{d}},C_{1}>0
such that, for every
u,v,\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
such that

\lVert u\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\lVert v\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{d}},\quad\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\lVert\tilde{v}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{d}}

(A.10)

and for every

,
the orbits of
z

T\coloneqq\Phi^{v}\circ\Phi^{u}\circ T_{0}
and

\tilde{T}\coloneqq\Phi^{\tilde{v}}\circ\Phi^{\tilde{u}}\circ T_{0}
satisfy

\operatorname{dist}\big(T^{k}(z),\tilde{T}^{k}(z)\big)\leq 3^{k}C_{1}\big(\lVert\tilde{u}-u\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\lVert\tilde{v}-v\rVert_{\alpha,L}\big)\quad\text{for all $k\in{\mathbb{N}}$.}

(A.11)

Proof.

For any
u,v,\tilde{u},\tilde{v}\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
and

, the maps

T\coloneqq\Phi^{v}\circ\Phi^{u}\circ T_{0}
and

\tilde{T}\coloneqq\Phi^{\tilde{v}}\circ\Phi^{\tilde{u}}\circ T_{0}
satisfy

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}\big(T(z),\tilde{T}(z)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{dist}\big(\Phi^{v}(\Phi^{u}(T_{0}(z))),\Phi^{v}(\Phi^{u}(T_{0}(\tilde{z})))\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{dist}\big(\Phi^{v}(\Phi^{u}(T_{0}(\tilde{z}))),\Phi^{v}(\Phi^{\tilde{u}}(T_{0}(\tilde{z})))\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\operatorname{dist}\big(\Phi^{v}(\Phi^{\tilde{u}}(T_{0}(\tilde{z}))),\Phi^{\tilde{v}}(\Phi^{\tilde{u}}(T_{0}(\tilde{z})))\big)

\displaystyle\hskip-50.00008pt\leq(\operatorname{Lip}\Phi^{v})(\operatorname{Lip}\Phi^{u})(\operatorname{Lip}T_{0})\operatorname{dist}(\tilde{z},z)+(\operatorname{Lip}\Phi^{v})\lVert\Phi^{\tilde{u}}-\Phi^{u}\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})}+\lVert\Phi^{\tilde{v}}-\Phi^{v}\rVert_{C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})}.

On the one hand,
\operatorname{Lip}T_{0}=2
.
On the other hand, for any
L_{0}>0
, the Lipschitz constant of a
map
\Psi
such that
\Psi-\operatorname{Id}\in G^{\alpha,L_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n},{\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
is bounded by

1+\operatorname{Lip}(\Psi-\operatorname{Id})\leq 1+L_{0}^{-\alpha}\lVert\Psi-\operatorname{Id}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}

(using the mean value inequality, (
A.1
)
and (
A.5
)).
Applying Lemma
A.2
with
L_{0}=L/2
,
we can thus choose
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{d}}
so that assumption (
A.10
) entails

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}\Phi^{u},\operatorname{Lip}\Phi^{v}\leq 1+2^{\alpha}L^{-\alpha}C_{0}{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{d}}\leq(3/2)^{1/2}

and

\displaystyle\lVert\Phi^{\tilde{u}}-\Phi^{u}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq C_{0}\lVert\tilde{u}-u\rVert_{\alpha,L},\quad\lVert\Phi^{\tilde{v}}-\Phi^{v}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{0}}\leq C_{0}\lVert\tilde{v}-v\rVert_{\alpha,L},

whence

\operatorname{dist}\big(T(z),\tilde{T}(z)\big)\leq 3\operatorname{dist}(\tilde{z},z)+\eta

with
\eta\coloneqq(3/2)^{1/2}C_{0}\big(\lVert\tilde{u}-u\rVert_{\alpha,L}+\lVert\tilde{v}-v\rVert_{\alpha,L}\big)
.
Iterating this, we get

\operatorname{dist}\big(T^{k}(z),\tilde{T}^{k}(z)\big)\leq 3^{k}(\operatorname{dist}(\tilde{z},z)+{\tfrac{1}{2}}\eta)-{\tfrac{1}{2}}\eta
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
thus we can conclude by choosing
C_{1}\coloneqq{\tfrac{1}{2}}(3/2)^{1/2}C_{0}
.
∎

A.3
A Gevrey inversion result

In Section
5.2
,
we use the following

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose
L<L_{1}
. Then there exists
{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}={\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}(\alpha,L,L_{1})
such that, for
every
{\epsilon}\in G^{\alpha,L_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})
, if
\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}
, then

\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon}
is a diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}
and

\displaystyle(\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon})^{-1}=\operatorname{Id}+\tilde{\epsilon}\quad\text{with}\quad\lVert\tilde{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}},

(A.12)

\displaystyle\left\lVert g\circ(\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon})^{-1}\right\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\lVert g\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}}\quad\text{for any $g\in G^{\alpha,L_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})$.}

(A.13)

Proof.

Let
L^{\prime}\coloneqq(L+L^{\prime})/2
.
We use Lemma
A.0
and define

{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}\coloneqq\min\big\{{\tfrac{1}{2}}(L_{1}-L^{\prime})^{\alpha},{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{c}}(\alpha,L^{\prime},L,1),{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{c}}(\alpha,L_{1},L,1)\big\}.

Given
{\epsilon}\in G^{\alpha,L_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{i}}
,
the functional

\mathcal{F}\colon\thinspace f\in\mathcal{B}\mapsto-{\epsilon}\circ(\operatorname{Id}+f),\quad\text{where
$\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{f\in G^{\alpha,L}({\mathbb{R}})\mid\lVert f\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}}\}$,}

is well defined (because
\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{c}}(\alpha,L^{\prime},L,1)
and
{\epsilon}\in G^{\alpha,L^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}})
),
maps
\mathcal{B}
into itself (we even have
\lVert\mathcal{F}(f)\rVert\leq\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}
),
and is
K
-Lipschitz with

K\coloneqq\lVert{\epsilon}^{\prime}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}

(using also (
A.2
) and the mean value inequality).
But (
A.3
) yields

\lVert{\epsilon}^{\prime}\rVert_{\alpha,L^{\prime}}\leq(L_{1}-L^{\prime})^{-\alpha}\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}}\leq{\tfrac{1}{2}}
,
which implies that
\mathcal{F}
is a contraction, and also that
\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon}
is
a diffeomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}
(since its derivative stays
\geq 1/2
).
The unique fixed point
\tilde{\epsilon}
of
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathcal{B}
is

(\operatorname{Id}+{\epsilon})^{-1}-\operatorname{Id}
,
which yields
\lVert\tilde{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L}\leq\lVert{\epsilon}\rVert_{\alpha,L_{1}}\leq{\epsilon}_{\operatorname{c}}(\alpha,L_{1},L,1)

and hence (
A.13
) by another application of
Lemma
A.0
.

∎

Appendix B
Some estimates on doubly exponentially growing sequences

According to (
4.3
), the increasing sequence
(N_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is defined by

N_{1}\coloneqq\left\lceil\exp({4\kappa/{\epsilon}})\right\rceil,\qquad N_{i}\coloneqq N_{i-1}\left\lceil\exp\big(\exp\big(\tilde{C}(N_{i-1}\ln N_{i-1})^{\gamma}\big)\big)\right\rceil\quad\text{for $i\geq 2$,}

where
0<{\epsilon}\leq 1
,

\kappa\geq 1

and
\tilde{C}\coloneqq\max\{6c\gamma,1/\gamma\}
, with
c,\gamma>0
.
Here, we show a few inequalities which are used in
Section
4.2
.
Recall that
\nu_{i}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N_{i}\ln N_{i}}
and
\xi_{i}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{-c\nu_{i}^{-\gamma}}
.

Lemma B.1
.

One has

\displaystyle\ln N_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 4^{i}\kappa/{\epsilon}

for every
i\geq 1
,

(B.1)

\displaystyle N_{i+1}\xi_{i+1}

\displaystyle\leq{\tfrac{1}{2}}N_{i}\xi_{i}

for
i
large enough,

(B.2)

\displaystyle N_{i+1}\xi_{i+1}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{-E_{3c\gamma,\gamma}(\nu_{i})}

for
i
large enough.

(B.3)

Proof.

We have
\ln(N_{1})\geq 4\kappa/{\epsilon}
and,
by virtue of (
4.1
),

N_{1}\geq 4\kappa/{\epsilon}\geq 4
.
Now, for
i\geq 2
, since
\gamma\tilde{C}\geq 1
,
we have

\ln N_{i}\geq\exp\big(\tilde{C}(N_{i-1}\ln N_{i-1})^{\gamma}\big)=\Big[\exp\big(\gamma\tilde{C}(N_{i-1}\ln N_{i-1})^{\gamma}\big)\Big]^{1/\gamma}\geq\Big[\exp(N_{i-1}\ln N_{i-1})^{\gamma}\Big]^{1/\gamma}

and (
4.1
) yields

\ln(N_{i})\geq N_{i-1}\ln N_{i-1}\geq 4\ln N_{i-1}
, whence (
B.1
) follows.

We have
\ln\frac{1}{N_{i}\xi_{i}}=c(N_{i}\ln N_{i})^{\gamma}-\ln N_{i}
and, since

\ln(N_{i})\ll(N_{i}\ln N_{i})^{\gamma}
,

c{\Lambda}_{i}^{\gamma}\geq\ln\frac{1}{N_{i}\xi_{i}}\geq c({\Lambda}_{i}/\sqrt{3})^{\gamma}\quad\text{for~$i$ large enough,}\quad\text{where ${\Lambda}_{i}\coloneqq N_{i}\ln N_{i}=1/\nu_{i}$.}

Inequality (
B.2
),
being equivalent to

\ln\frac{1}{N_{i+1}\xi_{i+1}}\geq\ln\frac{1}{N_{i}\xi_{i}}+\ln 2\quad\text{for~$i$ large enough,}

thus results from
({\Lambda}_{i+1}/\sqrt{3})^{\gamma}\geq{\Lambda}_{i}^{\gamma}+\frac{\ln 2}{c}

(which holds for
i
large enough because
N_{i+1}\geq 3N_{i}
, hence

{\Lambda}_{i+1}=N_{i+1}\ln N_{i+1}>3{\Lambda}_{i}
).

Let
C\coloneqq 3c\gamma
. Inequality (
B.3
), being equivalent to

\ln\frac{1}{N_{i+1}\xi_{i+1}}\geq(\ln 3)E_{C,\gamma}(1/{\Lambda}_{i})\quad\text{for~$i$ large enough,}

results from
{\Lambda}_{i+1}^{\gamma}\geq\frac{3^{\gamma/2}\ln 3}{c}E_{C,\gamma}(1/{\Lambda}_{i})
,
which holds since
E_{C,\gamma}(1/{\Lambda}_{i})=\left\lceil\exp\big(\exp(C{\Lambda}_{i}^{\gamma})\big)\right\rceil
and

{\Lambda}_{i+1}^{\gamma}=N_{i}^{\gamma}(\ln N_{i+1})^{\gamma}\left\lceil\exp\big(\gamma\exp(\tilde{C}{\Lambda}_{i}^{\gamma})\big)\right\rceil,\quad N_{i}^{\gamma}(\ln N_{i+1})^{\gamma}\geq\tfrac{3^{\gamma/2}\ln 3}{c}

and

\gamma\exp(\tilde{C}{\Lambda}_{i}^{\gamma})\geq\exp(C{\Lambda}_{i}^{\gamma})

for
i
large enough since
\tilde{C}>C
.
∎

Prime Avoidance Property of k k -th Powers of Piatetski-Shapiro Primes
Helmut Maier and Michael Th. Rassias

Abstract
. In previous papers the authors established the prime avoidance property of k k -th powers of prime numbers and of prime numbers within Beatty sequences. In this paper the authors consider k k -th powers of Piatetski-Shapiro primes. Key words. Piatetski-Shapiro primes; k k -th powers. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11P32, 11N05, 11A63.



1.
Introduction

The term
``
prime avoidance” first appears in the paper
[
3
]
of K. Ford, D. R. Heath-Brown and S. Konyagin, where they prove the existence of infinitely many “prime-avoiding” perfect
k
-th powers for any positive integer
k
. Their definition is as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

Let

g_{1}(m):=\frac{\log m\>\log_{2}m\>\log_{4}m}{(\log_{3}m)^{2}}\>.

Here
\log_{k}x:=\log(\log_{k-1}x)
. Then an integer
m
, for which
g_{1}(m)\geq m^{(0)}
(with
m^{(0)}>0
being a fixed constant) is called prime-avoiding with constant
c_{0}>0
if
m+u
is composite for all integers
u
satisfying
|u|\leq c_{0}g_{1}(m)\>.
We also say,
m
has the prime avoidance property with constant
c_{0}
.

For the sake of larger flexibility we also consider the following variations:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
h(m)
be defined for
m\geq m^{(1)}
and let
h(m)\rightarrow\infty
for
m\rightarrow\infty
. Let
c_{1}>0
be a fixed constant.

(i)

Then an integers
m\geq m^{(1)}
(with
m^{(1)}>0
being a fixed constant) is called prime-avoiding with constant
c_{1}
and function
h
if
h(m)\geq m^{(1)}
and
m+u
is composite for all integers
u
satisfying
|u|\leq h(m)
. We also say, that
m
has the prime avoidance property with constant
c_{1}
and function
h
.

(ii)

m\geq m^{(1)}
is called prime-avoiding to the right with function
h
and constant
c_{2}
if
m+u
is composite for all integers
u\in[0,c_{2}h(m)]
. We say that
m
has the prime-avoidance property to the right.

An analogous definition is to apply for “prime-avoiding to the left” and “prime avoidance property to the left”.

(iii)

The terms “one-sided prime-avoidance property” is used, if prime-avoidance property to the right, to the left of both hold.

The function
g_{1}
in (1.1) has played a central role for a long time in the study of large gaps between consecutive primes. The connection between these questions can easily be seen as follows:

Let
m
be prime-avoiding with function
h
and constant
c_{3}>0
and let
p_{n}
be the largest prime number
\leq m
, with
p_{n+1}
being the subsequent prime number. Then

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\geq 2c_{1}h(m)\>.

Also the reverse
-
large gaps imply the existence of prime-avoiding numbers can be seen easily.

2.
Large gaps between primes

The fact that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}-p_{n}}{\log p_{n}}=\infty

was first proved by Westzynthius
[
14
]
. Erdős
[
2
]
obtained that infinitely often one has:

p_{n+1}-p_{n}>C_{1}\>\frac{\log p_{n}\>\log_{2}p_{n}}{(\log_{3}p_{n})^{2}}

with appropriate
C_{1}>0
.

The order of magnitude of the lower bound (2.1) was slightly improved by Rankin
[
13
]
who showed that

p_{n+1}-p_{n}>C_{2}\>\frac{\log p_{n}\>\log_{2}p_{n}\>\log_{4}p_{n}}{(\log_{3}p_{n})^{2}}=C_{2}g_{1}(p_{n})\>.

The proof of Rankin differs only slightly from that of Erdős. Rankin is using a better estimate for the number of smooth integers. The approach of Rankin and modifications of it have become known as the Erdős-Rankin method. In
[
2
]
and
[
13
]
these two authors construct a long interval of integers which all have greatest common divisor larger than 1 with

P(x):=\prod_{p<x}p\>.

Their result also implies a prime-avoidance result for most integers of that interval. A modified version of this method was applied by the authors of
[
3
]
on the problem of prime-avoiding
k
-th powers mentioned in the introduction.

In
[
10
]
the authors of the present paper extended this result by proving the existence of infinitely many prime-avoiding
k
-th powers of prime numbers. For the sake of simplicity they only treat one-sided prime avoidance. They prove the following:

There are infinitely many
n
, such that

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\geq C_{3}g_{1}(n)

and the interval
[p_{n},p_{n+1}]
contains the
k
-th power of a prime number.

Their method of proof consists in a combination of the method of
[
3
]
with the matrix method of the first author
[
8
]
. The matrix
\mathcal{M}
employed in this technique is of the form

\mathcal{M}:=(a_{r,u})_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq P(x)^{D-1}\\
u\in\mathcal{B}\end{subarray}}\>,

where
D
is a fixed positive integer, where the
rows

\mathcal{R}(r):=\{a_{r,u}\>:\>u\in\mathcal{B}\}

of the matrix are
translates
– in closer or wider sense – of the
base-row

\mathcal{B}
.

The essential idea of the construction of the base-row
\mathcal{B}
is the Erdőw-Rankin method. Here however – as in all combinations with the matrix method – the base-row is not completely free of numbers not coprime to
P(x)
. The columns

\mathcal{C}(u):=(a_{r,u})_{1\leq r\leq P(x)^{D-1}}\>,\ \ \text{with $(a,P(x))=1$}

are called
admissible columns
.

The number of prime numbers in these columns, which are arithmetic progressions (or in the case of
[
10
]
– shifted powers of elements of arithmetic progressions), can be estimated using theorems on primes in arithmetic progressions.

It was a famous prize problem of Erdős, being open for more that 70 years, to replace the function
g_{1}
in (2.2) by a function of higher order of magnitude. This problem could finally be solved in the paper
[
4
]
by K. Ford, B. J. Green, S. Konyagin and T. Tao and independently in the paper
[
12
]
by J. Maynard. Later all five authors improved on this result in their joint paper
[
5
]
. They proved:

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\geq C_{4}\>g_{2}(p_{n})

infinitely often, where

g_{2}(m):=\frac{\log m\>\log_{2}m\>\log_{4}m}{\log_{3}m}\>.

In the paper
[
10
]
the authors of the present paper combined the methods of the papers
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
to
obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1 of
[
10
]

There is a constant
C_{5}>0
and infinitely many
n
, such that

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\geq C_{5}\>g_{2}(n)

and the interval
[p_{n},p_{n+1}]
contains the
k
-th power of a prime.

Using the Definition
1.2
this can also be phrased as follows:

There is a constant
C_{5}>0
, such that infinitely many
k
-th powers of primes are one-sided prime-avoidant with constant
\frac{1}{2}C_{5}
. In the paper
[
11
]
the authors investigated the prime-avoidance of the
k
-th powers of prime numbers with Beatty sequences.

Definition 2.1
.

For two fixed real numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
, the corresponding non-homogeneous Beatty sequence is the sequence of integers defined by

\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta}:=(\lfloor\alpha n+\beta\rfloor)_{n=1}^{\infty}

(
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function:
\lfloor u\rfloor
the largest integer
\leq u
).

Definition 2.2
.

For an irrational number
\gamma
we define its type
\tau
by the relation

\tau:=\sup\{\rho\in\mathbb{R}\>:\>\lim\inf\>n^{\rho}\|\gamma n\|=0\}\>\ \ \text{(see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{maier_beatty}{}{}]}).}

In the paper
[
11
]
the authors prove:

Theorem 1.3 of
[
11
]
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Let
\alpha,\beta
be fixed real numbers with
\alpha
being a positive irrational and of finite type. Then there is a constant
C_{6}>0
, depending only on
\alpha
and
\beta
, such that for infinitely many
n
we have:

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\geq C_{6}\>g_{2}(n)

and the interval
[p_{n},p_{n+1}]
contains the
k
-th power of a prime
\tilde{p}\in\mathscr{B}_{\alpha,\beta}
.

In this paper we deal with
Piatetski-Shapiro primes
. We prove:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
c_{4}\in(1,18/17)
be fixed,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 2
. Then there is a constant
C_{6}>0
, depending only on
k
and
c_{4}
, such that for infinitely many
n
we have

p_{n+1}-p_{n}\geq C_{6}g_{2}(n)

and the interval
[p_{n},p_{n+1}]
contains the
k
-th power of a prime
\tilde{p}=[l^{c_{4}}]\>.

Using again Definition
1.2
we can phrase Theorem
2.3
as follows:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
c_{4}\in(1,18/17)
be fixed,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 2
. Then there is a constant
C_{7}>0
, such that infinitely many
k
-th powers of primes of the form
\tilde{p}=[l^{c_{4}}]
have the one-sided prime avoidance property with constant
C_{7}
.

Again one can achieve prime avoidance by a slight modification of the proof.

3.
Construction of the matrix
\mathcal{M}

In several papers it was crucial for the estimate of prime numbers in arithmetic progressions
\bmod q
, that
q
was a “good modulus”.

We recall the definition:

Definition 3.1
.

Let us call an integer
q>1
a “good" modulus, if
L(s,\chi)\neq 0
for all characters
\chi\bmod\>q
and all
s=\sigma+it
with

\sigma>1-\frac{C_{8}}{\log\left[q(|t|+1)\right]}.

This definition depends on the size of
C_{8}>0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is a constant
C_{9}>0
, such that in terms of
C_{9}
, there exist arbitrarily large values of
x
, for which the modulus

P(x)=\prod_{p<x}p

is good.

Proof.

This is Lemma 3.7 of
[
10
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q
be a good modulus. Then for
x\geq q^{D}
we have

\sum_{1<l\leq Q}\sum_{\chi}^{*}\left|\sum_{x}^{x+h}\chi(p)\log p\right|\ll h\exp\left(-c_{5}\>\frac{\log x}{\log Q}\right)\>,

provided
x/Q\leq h<x
and

\exp(\log^{1/2}x)\leq Q\leq x^{6}\>,

(where
\displaystyle\sum^{*}
denotes the sum over all primitive characters
\bmod\>l
). The value of the constant
c_{5}
and the constant
D
depends only on the value of
C_{9}
in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

This is Theorem 7 of Gallagher
[
6
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
x
be sufficiently large.

y=C_{10}\>x\>\frac{\log x\log_{3}x}{\log_{2}x}\>,

C_{10}>0
being a sufficiently small constant. Then there is an integer
m_{0}
satisfying

1\leq m_{0}<P(x)

and an exceptional set
V
satisfying

\#V\ll x^{1/2+\epsilon}\>,\ \ \text{$\epsilon>0$ arbitrarily small}

such that

(m_{0}+1)^{k}+u-1

is composite for
2\leq u\leq y
, unless
u\in V
.

Proof.

This is Lemma 3.10 of
[
3
]
.
∎

The construction of
V
, which is due to Ford, Heath-Brown and Konyagin
[
3
]
is described in Lemma 3.5 of
[
3
]
. We now construct the matrix
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Choose
x
, such that
P(x)
is a good modulus. Let
y
satisfy (3.1),
V
satisfy (3.3) and
m_{0}
satisfy (3.2) and (3.4). We let

\mathcal{M}:=(a_{r,u})_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq P(x)^{D-1}\\
1\leq u\leq y\end{subarray}}\>,

where

a_{r,u}:=(m_{0}+1+rP(x))^{k}+u-1

and the constant
D
will be specified later.

For
1\leq r\leq P(x)^{D-1}
we denote by

R(r):=(a_{r,u})_{1\leq u\leq y}

the
r
-th row of
\mathcal{M}
and for
1\leq u\leq y
we denote by

C(u):=(a_{r,u})_{1\leq r\leq P(x)^{D-1}}

the
u
-th column of
\mathcal{M}
.

4.
Piatetski-Shapiro primes in arithmetic progressions

Definition 4.1
.

Let
c_{6}>1
. Let
a
and
d
be coprime integers,
w\geq 1
. Then we let

\pi_{c_{6}}(w;d,a)=\#\{p\leq w\>:\>p\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})},\ p\>\equiv a\bmod\>d\}

\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})}=\{p\ \text{prime}\>:\>p=[l^{c_{6}}]\ \text{for some}\ l\}\>.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
a
and
d
be coprime integers,
d\geq 1
. For fixed
c_{6}\in(1,18/17)
we have (with
\gamma=1/c_{6}
):

\pi_{c_{6}}(w;d,a)=\gamma w^{\gamma-1}\pi(w;d,a)+\gamma(1-\gamma)\int_{2}^{w}u^{\gamma-2}\pi(u;d,a)du+O\left(w^{\frac{17}{39}+\frac{7\gamma}{19}+\epsilon}\right)\>.

Proof.

This is Theorem 8 of
[
1
]
.
∎

5.
Conclusion of the Proof

Definition 5.1
.

We let
l(r):=m_{0}+1+rP(x)
,

\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathcal{M}):=\{r\>:1\leq r\leq P(x)^{D-1},\ l(r)\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})}\}\>,

\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathcal{M}):=\{r\>:1\leq r\leq P(x)^{D-1},\ r\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathcal{M}),\ {R}(r)\ \text{contains a prime number}\}.

We observe that each row
{R}(r)
with
r\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathcal{M})
has as its first element
a_{r,1}
, the
k
-th power of the prime
l(r)\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})}
. We now conclude the proof of Theorem
2.3
by showing that the set
\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathcal{M})\setminus\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathcal{M})
is non-empty.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have

\#\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathcal{M})=\frac{P(x)^{D\gamma}}{\phi(P(x))}\>\left(1+O\left(e^{-c_{7}D}\right)\right)\>.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
4.2
with
P(x)^{D}
instead of
w
,
d=P(x)
,
a=m_{0}+1
and obtain

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\gamma P(x)^{D(\gamma-1)}\pi(P(x)^{D},P(x),m_{0}+1)

(5.1)

\displaystyle\ \ +\gamma(1-\gamma)\int_{2}^{P(x)^{D}}u^{\gamma-2}\pi(u,P(x),m_{0}+1)\>du+O\left(P(x)^{D\left(\frac{17}{39}+\frac{7\gamma}{19}+\epsilon\right)}\right)\>.

We now use Dirichlet-characters to evaluate
\pi(u,P(x),m_{0}+1)
. We use the familiar definition

\theta(u,g,r)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq u\\
p\equiv r\bmod g\end{subarray}}\log p

and obtain

\theta(u,P(x),m_{0}+1)=\frac{1}{\phi(P(x))}\sum_{p\leq u}\log p+\frac{1}{\phi(P(x))}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\bmod P(x)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{p\leq u}\chi(p)\log p\>.

Assume that
\chi\bmod\>r
is induced by the primitive character
\chi^{*}
. Then it is well-known that

\sum_{p\leq u}|\chi(p)\log p-\chi^{*}(p)\log p|\leq\log(ru)^{2}\>.

We obtain:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left|\theta(u,P(x),m_{0}+1)-\frac{1}{\phi(P(x))}\sum_{p\leq u}\log p\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\phi(P(x))}\sum_{1<r\leq P(x)}\sum_{\chi^{*}\bmod\>r}^{*}\left|\sum_{p\leq u}\chi(p)\log p\right|\>,

where
\displaystyle\sum^{*}
denotes the summation over primitive characters.

From Lemma
3.3
we obtain:

\theta(u,P(x),m_{0}+1)=\frac{1}{\phi(P(x))}\left(\sum_{p\leq u}\log p\right)\left(1+O\left(e^{-c_{7}D}\right)\right)\>.

Lemma
5.2
now follows from (5.1), (5.2) and (5.3).
∎

We now estimate
\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathcal{M})
. We have

|\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathcal{M})|\leq\sum_{v\in V}t(v)\>,

where

t(v)=\#\{r\>:\>l(r)\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})},l(r)^{k}+v-1\ \text{prime}\}\>.

To obtain an upper estimate for
t(v)
we replace the condition
l(r)^{k}+v-1
prime by the condition

(l(r)^{k}+v-1,Q(x))=1\>,\ \ \text{where}\ \ Q(x)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<p\leq P(x)\\
p\ \text{prime}\end{subarray}}p\>.

Let

N(v):=\#\{r\>:\>l(r)\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})}:(l(r)^{k}+v-1,Q(x))=1\}\>.

By the sieve of Eratosthenes we have:

N(v)=\sum_{r:l(r)\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})},l(r)\leq P(x)^{D}}\ \ \sum_{\tau\mid(l(r)^{k}+v-1,Q(x))}\mu(\tau)

We now apply a Fundamental Lemma in the theory of Combinatorial Sieves, replacing the Möbius functions by functions of compact support.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\kappa>0
and
y>1
. There exist two sets of real numbers
\Lambda^{+}=(\lambda_{d}^{+})
and
\Lambda^{-}=(\lambda_{d}^{-})
depending only on
\kappa
and

with the following properties:

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\pm}=1\>,

(5.7)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{d}^{\pm}|\leq 1\>,\ \text{if}\ 1<d<y\>,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}^{\pm}=0\>,\ \text{if}\ d\geq y\>,\ \text{and for any integer $n>1$,}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}\lambda_{d}^{-}\leq 0\leq\sum_{d\mid n}\lambda_{d}^{+}\>.

Moreover, for any multiplicative function
g(d)
with
0\leq g(p)<1
and satisfying the dimension condition

\prod_{w\leq p<z}(1-g(p))^{-1}\leq\left(\frac{\log z}{\log w}\right)^{\kappa}\left(1+\frac{K}{\log w}\right)

Proof.

This is Fundamental Lemma 6.3 in
[
7
]
.
∎

For
\tau\mid Q(x)
let
\rho(\tau,v)
be the number of solutions of

The function

\rho(\tau,v)=\prod_{\tilde{p}\mid\tau}\rho(\tilde{p},v)\>.

We now apply Lemma

with

\displaystyle g(\tilde{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}\rho(\tilde{p},v),\ \ \ \text{if $x\leq\tilde{p}\leq P(x)$}\\
0,\ \ \ \text{otherwise}\\
\end{array}\right.

and choose

satisfying (5.7), (5.8), (5.9), where

,

From (5.1) and (5.2), we obtain

N(v)\leq\sum_{\tau\mid Q(x)}\lambda^{+}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\>:\>l(r)\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})},\ l(r)\leq P(x)^{D}\\

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\>:\>l(r)\in\mathcal{P}^{(c_{6})},\ l(r)\leq P(x)^{D}\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(\tau,v)}\bigg(\gamma P(x)^{D(\gamma-1)}\pi\left(P(x)^{D},\tau P(x),z(s)\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ +\gamma(1-\gamma)\int_{2}^{P(x)^{D}}u^{\gamma-2}\pi(u,\tau P(x),z(s))du\bigg)\>.

We write

\pi(u,q,a)=\frac{\theta(u,q,a)}{\log u}\ \left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log u}\right)\right)

and make use of Dirichlet characters.

For the non-principal characters we again apply Lemma

.

In the treatment of the principal character we switch from the function

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\mid Q(x)}\lambda^{+}(\tau)\>\frac{\rho(\tau,v)}{\phi(\tau)\phi(P(x))}P(x)^{D\gamma}\left(1+O\left(e^{-c_{11}D}\right)\right)

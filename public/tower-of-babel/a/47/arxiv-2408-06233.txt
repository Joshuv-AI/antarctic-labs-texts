Generic motives and motivic cohomology of fields
F. Déglise

Abstract
. These notes are devoted to the study of generic motives and their application to the structure of the motivic cohomology of fields. Related to Voevodsky’s homotopy t t -structure on motivic complexes, generic motives are also central to the conjectural motivic t t -structure, as was shown by Beilinson. Morphisms of generic motives are closely related to the functoriality of Rost’s cycle modules. We obtain some new computations of generic motives, and deduce computations of morphisms of generic motives, which draw a conjectural picture. The structure of generic motives reflects into that of motivic cohomology of fields. After giving a new argument for determining weights in the K-theory of number fields using Borel’s isomorphism, we deduce that many motivic cohomology groups of fields have infinite rank, usually of the same cardinality as the field itself. As an example motivic cohomology groups of the real or complex numbers are shown to be uncountable in many degrees and twists. Despite these results, we formulate a hope on their behavior with respect to transcendental elements, providing a counterpart to the Beilinson-Soulé vanishing conjecture.

whose enthusiasm for, and commitment to, Grothendieck’s theory of motives was a driving force for me.

1.
Introduction

One of the acts of birth of motivic cohomology was,
retrospectively, Borel’s 1974 computation of the K-theory of number fields as a corollary of that of the stable real cohomology of arithmetic groups
[
Bor74
]
. Higher K-theory had barely been introduced by Quillen in his celebrated paper
[
Qui73b
]
, and Lichtenbaum had just embarked in a lifetime quest to develop higher regulators and analogs of Dirichlet’s class number formula. Both works were published in the same lecture notes in 1973.
The next step seemed to have been crystallized by two works in the year 1979: the first one is a paper due to Deligne
[
Del79
]
on special values of L-functions attached to motives, and the second one consists of lectures of Spencer Bloch, on algebraic cycles. They both lead to the second era of motives, led by the famous conjectures due to Beilinson, stated in
[
Bi84
]
in 1984. Not long after the statement of these conjecture, Voevodsky’s PhD proposed a new approach via the famous h-topology, and the category of h-motivic complexes. This opened the way to a totally new era, which encompassed mixed motivic complexes into a homotopy theory for algebraic variety, now known as motivic homotopy theory. It led to many success, and most notably the solution to the Milnor and Bloch-Kato conjectures by Voevodsky (
[
Voe11
]
), with important contributions by Rost (
[
Ros96
]
).

At the time being, Beilinson’s conjectures on motivic complexes have been positively answered, with the construction of several suitable categories of mixed motives,
equipped with six functors and an

,

,

3.3.

3.3.1

Generic motives enjoy a rich functorial structure,

We use notation (D1*), (R1a*) and so on to indicate the data and relations

Remark 3.3.2

Using cohomological motives and given

the above construction amounts to giving a covariant functor:

3.3.3

We start with the first two types of functoriality satisfied by generic motives:

(D2*)

5.0.1

5.1.

In this subsection, we recall the following fundamental computation of motivic cohomology,
mainly due to Borel if one neglects weights, which were determined by Beilinson using his

Theorem 5.1.1
.

Let
K
be a number field. Let

and

be respectively the number of real and complex embeddings of

.
Then for any couple of integers

\operatorname{H}_{\mathcyr{B}}^{n,i}(K)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}&n=i=0,\\
K^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}&n=i=1,\\
\mathbb{Q}^{r_{2}}&n=1,i>1,i\text{ even},\\
\mathbb{Q}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}&n=1,i>1,i\text{ odd},\\

If

is the ring of integers of

\operatorname{H}_{\mathcyr{B}}^{n,i}(\mathcal{O}_{K})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}&n=i=0,\\
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}&n=i=1,\\
\mathbb{Q}^{r_{2}}&n=1,i>1,i\text{ even},\\
\mathbb{Q}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}&n=1,i>1,i\text{ odd},\\
\Pic(O_{K})_{\mathbb{Q}}&n=2,i=1,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We will give a proof of this theorem, or rather a reduction to Borel’s computation
of the stable real cohomology of arithmetic groups (

We first recall the arguments in low degree for the comfort of the reader

5.3.
Motivic cohomology with coefficients in abelian varieties

We finish this paper by mentioning an interesting cohomology theory that comes out
of Voevodsky’s theory of motivic complexes.

Definition 5.3.1
.

Let
k
be a perfect field, and
A
be an abelian variety over
k
.
For an integer
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we consider the motivic complex in
\DM(k)
:

\underline{A}(i)=\Sigma^{\infty}(\underline{A})\otimes\mathbbm{1}(i).

Then, given any smooth
k
-scheme of finite type, we define the motivic cohomology of
X

with coefficients in
A
, degree
n
and twists
i
, as the abelian group:

H^{n,i}(X,\underline{A})=\Hom_{\DM(k)}\big(\M(X),\underline{A}(i)\big).

This new cohomology theory inherits all the properties
of the motive
\bM(X)
:

•

It is contravariant with respect to arbitrary morphisms in
X
,
and covariant up to twists and shift with respect to proper smoothable morphisms
(use Gysin morphisms).

•

It satisfies the localization property with respect to a smooth closed pair
(X,Z)
(use the Gysin triangle).

•

It satisfies the projective bundle formula as well as the blow-up formula with smooth center.

According to
4.2.4
,
\underline{A}(-i)=0
for
i>0
. One deduces from the cancellation theorem
that for
i\geq 0
:

H^{n,i}(X,\underline{A}))\simeq H^{n}_{\mathrm{Nis}}(X,\underline{A}(i))

where the left hand-side is the Nisnevich cohomology of the motivic complexes
\underline{A}\otimes\mathbbm{1}(i)\in\DMe(k)
.
According to the property of the derived tensor product in
\DMe(k)
, one deduces
that
\underline{A}(i)
is a complex of Nisnevich sheaves concentrated in cohomological degree
\leq i
.
One deduces:

H^{n,i}(X,\underline{A})=\begin{cases}\underline{A}(X)&n=i=0,\\
0&\text{$i<0$ or $n>2i$ or $n-i>\dim(X)$.}\end{cases}

Finally, one gets for any function field
K/k
the following computation:

H^{n,n}(K,\underline{A})\simeq\operatorname{K}^{SM}_{n}(K;A)

with the notation of
4.2.4
.
Moreover, it follows from
3.3.7
that
\operatorname{K}^{SM}_{*}(-;A)\simeq\widehat{\underline{A}}_{*}
is a cycle module over
k
.
One deduces a differential from Rost’s cycle complex with coefficients in the latter cycle module:

\bigoplus_{y\in X^{(n-1)}}\operatorname{K}^{SM}_{1}(\kappa_{y};A)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{div}_{A}}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(n)}}A(\kappa_{x}).

Definition 5.3.2
.

Consider the above notation.
One defines the codimension
n
algebraic cycles of
X
with coefficients in
A
as the abelian group:

Z^{n}(X;A)=\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(n)}}A(\kappa_{x}).

The map
\mathrm{div}_{A}
defined above will be called the divisor class map. One defines the Chow group of
X

in codimension
n
and coefficients in
A
as the cokernel:

\CH^{n}(X;A):=\coKer(\mathrm{div}_{A})=Z^{n}(X;A)/\sim_{A}.

Here
\sim_{A}
is the obvious equivalence relation,
that will be called the rational equivalence.
As a consequence of the coniveau spectral sequence together with the previous computation,
one gets:

Proposition 5.3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth
k
-scheme and
A
an abelian variety.
Then for any integer
n\geq 0
, there exists a canonical isomorphism:

H^{2n,n}(X,\underline{A})\simeq\CH^{n}(X;A).

Remark 5.3.4
.

(1)

By considering motives of the form
\underline{A}\otimes\underline{B}
in
\DMe(k)
,
one can define exterior products of motivic cohomology with coefficients in abelian varieties.

(2)

Up to inverting the characteristic exponent of
k
, it can be extended to singular
k
-schemes
in a contravariant way. The extension satisfies
\mathrm{cdh}
-descent.

(3)

One can define a compactly supported version by using motive with compact supports.

5.3.5
.

The preceding definition is useful to state the computation of motivic cohomology
of function fields that comes from
4.2.2
.

Proposition 5.3.6
.

Let
K
be a number field,
and
\bar{C}/K
be a smooth projective curve with base point
x_{0}\in\bar{C}_{(0)}
.
We let
L
be the function field of
\bar{C}
, and
A
be the jacobian of pointed curve
(\bar{C},x_{0})
.

Then, there are canonical morphisms

\pi_{L/K}^{n,i}:\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{n-2,i-1}(K,\underline{A})\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{n,i}(L).

which are isomorphisms for any pair
(n,i)
such that
i\geq n
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}-[1,3]
.

For
i>1
, the following map is an isomorphism:

\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{-1,i-1}(K,\underline{A})\oplus\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{1,i}(K)\xrightarrow{(\pi^{1,i}_{L/K},\varphi^{*})}\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{1,i}(L)

Moreover, for
i\geq 3
, the following sequence of abelian groups is exact:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{0,i-1}(K,\underline{A})\xrightarrow{\pi_{L/K}^{2,i}}\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{2,i}(L)\xrightarrow{\sum_{x}\partial_{x}}\bigoplus_{x\in C_{(0)}}\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{1,i-1}(\kappa_{x})\rightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{1,i-1}(K,\underline{A})\xrightarrow{\pi_{L/K}^{2,i}}\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{3,i}(L)\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

This is a faithful translation of the homotopy exact sequence of
4.2.2
,
with the duality result of
4.2.6
, and using the
vanishing of motivic cohomology of the fields
K
and
\kappa_{x}
from
5.1.4
.
∎

5.3.7
.

One can therefore interpret the preceding conjecture for the function field
L

in terms of properties of the cohomology groups
\operatorname{H}_{\mathrm{M}}^{n,i}(K,\underline{A})
,
which are attached to an abelian variety over a number field.
These data might be easier to grasp than the transcendental extension

.

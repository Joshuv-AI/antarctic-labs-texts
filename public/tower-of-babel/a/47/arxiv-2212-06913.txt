Pseudoprocesses related to higher-order equations of vibrations of rods
Manfred Marvin Marchione, Enzo Orsingher

Abstract
In this paper we study Fresnel pseudoprocesses whose signed measure density is a solution to a higher-order extension of the equation of vibrations of rods. We also investigate space-fractional extensions of the pseudoprocesses related to the Riesz operator. The measure density is represented in terms of generalized Airy functions which include the classical Airy function as a particular case. We prove that the Fresnel pseudoprocess time-changed with an independent stable subordinator produces genuine stochastic processes. In particular, if the exponent of the subordinator is chosen in a suitable way, the time-changed pseudoprocess is identical in distribution to a mixture of stable processes. The case of a mixture of Cauchy distributions is discussed and we show that the symmetric mixture can be either unimodal or bimodal, while the probability density function of an asymmetric mixture can possibly have an inflection point.

Introduction

In mathematical physics, the vibrations of rods are described by the partial differential equation

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}=-\kappa^{2}\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{4}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0

(1)

where the constant
\kappa^{2}
is related to the structure of the vibrating rod (see Courant and Hilbert
[
3
]
, pag. 244-246). A probabilistic analysis of equation (
1
) has been performed by Orsingher and D’Ovidio
[
11
]
for
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}
by decomposing the equation into two Schrödinger-type equations

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{i}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{i}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\right)u=0.

(2)

The Schrödinger equation was examined from a probabilistic point of view by Ibragimov et al.
[
5
]
and Platonova and Tsykin
[
13
]
. By treating the Schrödinger-type equations as heat equations with imaginary time, the fundamental solution to (
1
) can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle u(x,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[P(B(s)\in dx)\Big|_{s=it}+P(B(s)\in dx)\Big|_{s=-it}\right]/dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2it}-i\frac{\pi}{4}}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}+\frac{e^{\frac{x^{2}}{2it}+i\frac{\pi}{4}}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\right]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2t}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)

(3)

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion. The probabilistic interpretation of formula (
3
) is that a Brownian motion is started at the origin at time
t=0
and it moves either with increasing or decreasing imaginary time with equal probabilities. The direction of the time is chosen once forever at
t=0
and can’t be changed.

In the same paper, the authors constructed a signed measure
\mathbb{P}
on the space of real-valued measurable functions
x(t),\;t\geq 0
, defined by

\mathbb{P}\left(\cap_{j=1}^{m}\{a_{j}\leq x(t_{j})\leq b_{j}\}\right)=\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\ldots\int_{a_{m}}^{b_{m}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}u(x_{j}-x_{j-1},\;t_{j}-t_{j-1})\;dx_{j}

(4)

with
t_{0}=0
and
x_{0}=0
. The fundamental solution (
3
) can be viewed as a signed density function related to a pseudoprocess
F(t)
which was called the Fresnel pseudoprocess by the authors.
The signed measure
\mathbb{P}
is well defined on the algebra
\mathcal{F}
containing all the cylinder sets and is finitely additive on
\mathcal{F}
. Since
\mathbb{P}
is signed, Kolmogorov’s extension theorem cannot be applied to extend
\mathbb{P}
to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{F}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{P}
has infinite total variation (see Krylov
[
6
]
) and therefore cannot be extended to
\sigma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
.

The construction method described by formula (
4
), which resembles the classical construction of the Wiener measure, was used by different authors for studying pseudoprocesses related to higher-order heat-type equations of the form

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=c_{n}\frac{\partial^{n}u}{\partial x^{n}}

(5)

for suitable coefficients
c_{n}
. The even-order case was studied from a probabilistic point of view by Krylov
[
6
]
. The odd-order case was first examined by Orsingher
[
10
]
and later developed by Lachal
[
7
,
8
]
and Orsingher and D’Ovidio
[
12
]
. The authors obtained the exact distributions of various functionals of pseudoprocesses like the maximum and the sojourn time.

A generalization of equation (
1
) was considered by De Gregorio and Orsingher
[
4
]
who introduced pseudoprocesses related to equations of the form

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\partial^{2n}u}{\partial x^{2n}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0,\;n\geq 2.

(6)

The authors expressed the fundamental solution
u_{2n}(x,t)
to equation (
6
) in the form

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cos\left(\gamma x\right)\cos\left(\gamma^{n}t\right)d\gamma.

(7)

and they pointed out that, for
n=3
, the solution (
7
) can be written as

u_{6}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2(3t)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\left[{\text{Ai}}\left(\frac{x}{(3t)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right)+{\text{Ai}}\left(-\frac{x}{(3t)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right)\right],\;\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0

(8)

where
{\text{Ai}}(x)
is the Airy function of the first kind.

In this paper we show that the representation (
8
) can be extended to all values of
n
by using the generalized Airy functions

{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cos\left(sx+\frac{s^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\right)ds,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;\alpha>1

(9)

The generalized Airy function (
9
), which coincides with the classical Airy function for
\alpha=3
, was first introduced by Ansari and Askari
[
1
]
and Askari and Ansari
[
2
]
in the integer case
\alpha=n
,
n\geq 2
, as a solution to the ordinary differential equation

y^{(n)}(x)+\gamma_{n}xy(x)=0,\qquad\gamma_{n}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}&\text{for even}\;n\\
-1&\text{for odd}\;n\end{cases}

and it was generalized to all possible values of
\alpha>1
by Marchione and Orsingher
[
9
]
which obtained the function (
9
) as the solution to a fractional diffusion equation involving an higher-order Riesz-Feller operator.

The fundamental solution
u_{2n}(x,t)
to equation (
6
) can be expressed in the form

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\left[{\text{Ai}}_{n}\left(\frac{x}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)+{\text{Ai}}_{n}\left(-\frac{x}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)\right].

(10)

However, we observe that a distinction must be made between the even case
n=2r
and the odd case
n=2r+1
. While in the even case only symmetric solutions of the form (
10
) are admitted, for odd values of
n
we obtain asymmetric solutions of the form

u_{2n,p}(x,t)=\frac{1}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\Biggl[p\cdot{\text{Ai}}_{n}\left(-\frac{x}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)+(1-p)\cdot{\text{Ai}}_{n}\left(\frac{x}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)\Biggr]

(11)

Moreover, the probabilistic interepretation we give to formula (
11
) is substantially different from that given by Orsingher and D’Ovidio
[
11
]
which is described by formula (
3
).

Our analysis begins with the study of the symmetric pseudo-density (
10
) which can be expressed in the form

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{n\pi t^{\frac{1}{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{2k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2k+1}{n}\right)}{(2k)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(n+1)}{2n}\right)

Moreover, we show that the pseudo-density admits the probabilistic representation

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\left[\sin\left(a_{n}xG_{n}(1/t)\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(b_{n}xG_{n}(1/t)\right)\right]

(12)

where
G_{\gamma}(\tau)
is a random variable with Weibull distribution having probability density function
g_{\gamma}(y;\tau)=\gamma\frac{y^{\gamma-1}}{\tau}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{\gamma}}{\tau}\right)
with
y,\gamma,\tau>0
,
a_{n}=\cos\frac{\pi}{2n}
and
b_{n}=\sin\frac{\pi}{2n}.

Thus, the function
u_{2n}(x,t)
can be interpreted as the expected value of amplified oscillations with Weibull distributed random parameters.

We then introduce a fractional extension of the Fresnel pseudoprocess by studying the space-fractional partial differential equation

\frac{\partial^{2}u_{2\alpha}}{\partial t^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{2\alpha}u_{2\alpha}}{\partial|x|^{2\alpha}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0

whose solution can be expressed in the form

u_{2\alpha,p}(x,t)=\frac{1}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\Biggl[p\cdot{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}\left(-\frac{x}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)+(1-p)\cdot{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)\Biggr]

where the Airy function
{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}(x)
is defined in formula (
9
).

The function
u_{2\alpha,p}(x,t)
can be interepreted as the density of a fractional Fresnel pseudoprocess
F_{2\alpha}^{(p)}(t)
. Moreover, a probabilistic representation for
u_{2\alpha,p}(x,t)
resembling formula (
12
) holds.

We finally study the fractional Fresnel pseudoprocess
F_{2\alpha}^{(p)}(t)
time-changed with an independent Lévy stable subordinator
S_{\theta}(t)
having exponent
\theta
. We prove that, if the exponent
\theta
is chosen in a suitable way, the time-changed pseudoprocess

Y_{2\alpha,\theta}^{(p)}(t)=F_{2\alpha}^{(p)}(S_{\theta}(t))

becomes a genuine stochastic process. In particular, we obtain that

Y_{2\alpha,\theta}^{(p)}(t)\overset{i.d.}{=}\begin{cases}H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;p\\
-H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;1-p\end{cases}

(13)

where
H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)
is an asymmetric Lévy stable process having suitable parameters which depend on
\alpha
and
\theta
.

A special case of the relationship (
13
) is obtained for
\theta=\frac{1}{\alpha}
. For such a choice of
\theta
, the distribution of the subordinated pseudoprocess is a mixture of Cauchy distributions. The case
\alpha=2,\;p=\frac{1}{2}
was examined by Orsingher and D’Ovidio
[
11
]
who obtained the symmetric probability density function

\mathbb{P}(Y_{4,\frac{1}{2}}(t)\in dx)/dx=\frac{t}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\;\frac{x^{2}+t^{2}}{x^{4}+t^{4}}.

(14)

It can be shown that the probability density (
14
) is bimodal and its two modes coincide with the points
x=\pm t\sqrt{\sqrt{2}-1}
. In general, we discover that, in the symmetric case
p=\frac{1}{2}
, probability density function of the subordinated pseudoprocess

Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t)=F_{2\alpha}(S_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t))

is bimodal only for
1<\alpha<3
. Outside of this interval, that is for
\alpha\geq 3
, the symmetric mixture distribution is unimodal.

In the case of asymmetric superposition of Cauchy densities emerging in the study of the subordinated pseudoprocess
Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}^{(p)}(t)
, the structure of the distribution is considerably more complicated. In this case we show that the probability density function can possibly have an inflection point. We prove this fact by finding a particular choice of
\alpha
and
p
such that the inflection point emerges.

2
The higher-order Fresnel pseudoprocess

We start our analysis by studying the solution to the partial differential equation

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\partial^{2n}u}{\partial x^{2n}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0,\;n\geq 2.

(15)

By expressing equation (
15
) in the form

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(n+1)}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(n+1)}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}\right)u=0

De Gregorio and Orsingher
[
4
]
showed that the fundamental solution
u_{2n}(x,t)
to (
15
) can be expressed as a linear combination of the functions

v_{1,n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-i\gamma x-(-1)^{n}it\gamma^{n}}d\gamma,\qquad v_{2,n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-i\gamma x+(-1)^{n}it\gamma^{n}}d\gamma.

(16)

We emphasize that a distinction must be made whether
n
is even or odd.

For even
n
, say
n=2r
, the functions in (
16
) are complex-valued. A real-valued solution to equation (
15
) is obtained by considering an equally-weighted linear combination of
v_{1,n}(x,t)
and
v_{2,n}(x,t)
. Thus, for
n=2r
, the solution to equation (
15
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle u_{4r}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[v_{1,2r}(x,t)+v_{2,2r}(x,t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-i\gamma x-it\gamma^{2r}}d\gamma+\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-i\gamma x-it\gamma^{2r}}d\gamma\right.

\displaystyle\;\;\;\left.+\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-i\gamma x+it\gamma^{2r}}d\gamma+\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-i\gamma x+it\gamma^{2r}}d\gamma\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cos\left(\gamma x+t\gamma^{2r}\right)d\gamma+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cos\left(-\gamma x+t\gamma^{2r}\right)d\gamma\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(2rt)^{\frac{1}{2r}}}\left[{\text{Ai}}_{2r}\left(\frac{x}{(2rt)^{\frac{1}{2r}}}\right)+{\text{Ai}}_{2r}\left(-\frac{x}{(2rt)^{\frac{1}{2r}}}\right)\right]

(17)

where
{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}(x)
is the generalized Airy function defined in formula (
9
).

For odd
n
, say
n=2r+1
, the functions
v_{1,n}(x,t)
and
v_{2,n}(x,t)
are real-valued and can be expressed in terms of generalized Airy functions:

v_{j,2r+1}(x,t)=\frac{1}{[(2r+1)t]^{\frac{1}{2r+1}}}{\text{Ai}}_{2r+1}\left((-1)^{j}\frac{x}{[(2r+1)t]^{\frac{1}{2r+1}}}\right),\qquad j=1,2.

Thus, any linear combination of the form

\displaystyle u_{2r+1}(x,t)=\frac{1}{[(2r+1)t]^{\frac{1}{2r+1}}}\Biggl[p\cdot

\displaystyle{\text{Ai}}_{2r+1}\left(-\frac{x}{[(2r+1)t]^{\frac{1}{2r+1}}}\right)

\displaystyle+q\cdot

\displaystyle{\text{Ai}}_{2r+1}\left(\frac{x}{[(2r+1)t]^{\frac{1}{2r+1}}}\right)\Biggr],\qquad p,q\in\mathbb{R}

(18)

is a solution to equation (
15
) for
n=2r+1
. For
p=q=\frac{1}{2}
, a symmetric solution analogous to (
17
) is obtained.

For
q=1-p
,
0\leq p\leq 1
the solution (
18
) admits the following probabilistic interpretation. Consider the pseudoprocess
X_{n}(t)
having pseudo-density

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}(t)\in dx)/dx=\frac{1}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\text{Ai}}_{n}\left(\frac{x}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right).

The pseudoprocess
X_{n}(t)
emerges from the analysis of odd-order heat-type equations of the form (
5
) (see Orsingher
[
10
]
and Lachal
[
7
,
8
]
) and their space-fractional extensions (Marchione and Orsingher
[
9
]
). From formula (
11
) it is clear that

F_{2n}^{(p)}(t)\overset{i.d.}{=}\begin{cases}-X_{n}(t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;p\\
X_{n}(t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;1-p.\end{cases}

Thus, we interpret the Fresnel pseudoprocess as a pseudoprocess obtained by randomly choosing at the time
t=0
the sign of
X_{n}(t)
.

In the final section of the paper we will briefly discuss the asymmetric solution (
18
) for
q=1-p
,
0\leq p\leq 1
. Throughout this section we will restrict ourselves to the analysis of the symmetric case

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\left[{\text{Ai}}_{n}\left(\frac{x}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)+{\text{Ai}}_{n}\left(-\frac{x}{(nt)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)\right]

(19)

for both
n
even and odd. The fundamental solution (
19
) arises from the following Cauchy problem.

Theorem 1
.

The function (
19
) is the solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial^{2}u_{2n}}{\partial t^{2}}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\partial^{2n}u_{2n}}{\partial x^{2n}}\\
u_{2n}(x,0)=\delta(x),\;\;\frac{\partial u_{2n}}{\partial t}(x,0)=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

The Fourier transform
\widehat{u}_{2n}(\gamma,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{i\gamma x}u_{2n}(x,t)d\gamma
is the solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{u}_{2n}(\gamma,t)}{\partial t^{2}}=-\gamma^{2n}\widehat{u}_{2n}(\gamma,t)\\
\widehat{u}_{2n}(\gamma,0)=1,\;\;\frac{\partial\widehat{u}_{2n}}{\partial t}(\gamma,0)=0.\end{cases}

Thus, we obtain

\widehat{u}_{2n}(\gamma,t)=\cos(\gamma^{n}t)=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{i\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)|\gamma|^{n}t}+e^{-i\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)|\gamma|^{n}t}\right].

(20)

By taking the inverse Fourier transform of formula (
20
) and having in mind the representation (
9
) the proof is complete.
∎

A power series representation for
u_{2n}(x,t)
can be obtained by expressing the generalized Airy function in the following form (see Marchione and Orsingher
[
9
]
):

{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi\alpha^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}\alpha^{\frac{k}{\alpha}}}{k!}\Gamma\left(\frac{k+1}{\alpha}\right)\sin\left(\pi\frac{(k+1)(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;\alpha>1.

(21)

By combining the expressions (
19
) and (
21
), we have that

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{n\pi t^{\frac{1}{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{2k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2k+1}{n}\right)}{(2k)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(n+1)}{2n}\right)

(22)

For
n=2
, by applying the gamma duplication formula
\frac{\Gamma\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{(2k)!}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{k}\Gamma(k+1)}
and considering separately the odd-order terms and the even-order terms of the power series, formula (
22
) becomes

\displaystyle u_{4}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)^{k}\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}(2k+1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k)!}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)^{2k}\sin\left(3k\pi+\frac{3}{4}\pi\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left.\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)!}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)^{2k+1}\sin\left(3k\pi+\frac{9}{4}\pi\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2k)!}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)^{2k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2k+1)!}\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)^{2k+1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)

(23)

which coincides with formula (
3
) up to a scaling factor.

In the following theorem we show that the power series representation (
22
) can be interpreted as an expected value of amplified oscillations with Weibull distributed random parameters.

Theorem 2
.

The following relationship holds:

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\left[\sin\left(a_{n}xG_{n}(1/t)\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(b_{n}xG_{n}(1/t)\right)\right]

(24)

where
G_{\gamma}(\tau)
is a random variable with Weibull distribution having probability density function

g_{\gamma}(y;\tau)=\gamma\frac{y^{\gamma-1}}{\tau}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{\gamma}}{\tau}\right),\qquad y,\gamma,\tau>0

and

a_{n}=\cos\frac{\pi}{2n},\qquad b_{n}=\sin\frac{\pi}{2n}.

Proof.

We start by writing formula (
22
) in the form

\displaystyle u_{2n}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{2k+1}\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{2k+1}{n}\right)}{(2k+1)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(n+1)}{2n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{nt}{\pi x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-ty^{n}}y^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(xy)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(n+1)}{2n}\right)dy

(25)

where we have used the integral representation of the gamma function. By using the relationship

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\sin((2k+1)\phi)=\sin\left(x\sin\phi\right)\operatorname{cosh}(x\cos\phi)

(26)

formula (
25
) becomes

u_{2n}(x,t)=\frac{nt}{\pi x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-ty^{n}}y^{n-1}\sin\left(xy\cos\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(xy\sin\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)dy

which completes the proof.
∎

In view of formula (
23
), theorem
2
implies that

\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\left[\sin\left(x\sqrt{\frac{Z}{2t}}\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(x\sqrt{\frac{Z}{2t}}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)

(27)

where
Z
is an exponential random variable with mean 1. By expressing the relationship (
27
) in integral form we obtain the unexpected result

\frac{1}{\pi x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\sin\left(x\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(x\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\right)e^{-y}dy=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right).

(28)

Remark 1
.

Formula (
28
) can be obtained also by direct calculation. By observing that

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}we^{-w^{2}+Aw}dw=\frac{A\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\;e^{\frac{A^{2}}{4}},\qquad A\in\mathbb{C}.

we can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\sin\left(x\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(x\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\right)e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8i\pi x}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(e^{ix\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}}-e^{-ix\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}}\right)\left(e^{x\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}}+e^{-x\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}}\right)e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4i\pi x}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}we^{-w^{2}}\left[e^{w\frac{x}{\sqrt{2t}}(1+i)}+e^{w\frac{x}{\sqrt{2t}}(i-1)}-e^{w\frac{x}{\sqrt{2t}}(1-i)}-e^{-w\frac{x}{\sqrt{2t}}(1+i)}\right]dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4i\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2t}}\left[(1+i)e^{i\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}+(i-1)e^{-i\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}\right]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi t}}\left[\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)+\sin\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\cos\left(\frac{x^{2}}{4t}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right).

3
The fractional Fresnel pseudoprocess

We now study a fractional generalization of the higher-order Fresnel pseudoprocess. In particular, we consider the pseudoprocess related to the space-fractional Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial^{2}u_{2\alpha}}{\partial t^{2}}(x,t)=\frac{\partial^{2\alpha}u_{2\alpha}}{\partial|x|^{2\alpha}}(x,t),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0,\;\alpha>1\\
u_{2\alpha}(x,0)=\delta(x),\;\;\frac{\partial u_{2\alpha}}{\partial t}(x,0)=0.\end{cases}

(29)

where the operator
\frac{\partial^{2\alpha}}{\partial|x|^{2\alpha}}
denotes the Riesz fractional derivative. First proposed by Riesz
[
15
]
, the Riesz fractional derivative can be implicitely defined by means of its Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}\left\{\frac{\partial^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial|x|^{\alpha}}\right\}(\gamma)=-|\gamma|^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\{f(x)\}(\gamma).

For a function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
f\in C_{m}(\mathbb{R})
, with derivatives decaying to 0 for
|x|\to+\infty
, the following explicit representation can be given to the Riesz fractional derivative of order
\alpha
,
m-1<\alpha<m

\frac{\partial^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial|x|^{\alpha}}=-\frac{1}{2\Gamma(m-\alpha)\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)}\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{f(z)}{(x-z)^{\alpha-m+1}}dz+(-1)^{m}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{f(z)}{(z-x)^{\alpha-m+1}}dz\right].

In the next theorem we show that the Riesz fractional differential operator is related to a fractional extension of the higher-order Fresnel pseudoprocess examined in the previous section.

Theorem 3
.

The solution to the fractional Cauchy problem (
29
) can be expressed in the form

u_{2\alpha}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\left[{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)+{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}\left(-\frac{x}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)\right],\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0,\;\alpha>1.

(30)

Proof.

The Fourier transform
\widehat{u}_{2\alpha}(\gamma,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{i\gamma x}u_{2\alpha}(x,t)d\gamma
is the solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{u}_{2\alpha}(\gamma,t)}{\partial t^{2}}=-|\gamma|^{2\alpha}\widehat{u}_{2\alpha}(\gamma,t)\\
\widehat{u}_{2\alpha}(\gamma,0)=1,\;\;\frac{\partial\widehat{u}_{2\alpha}}{\partial t}(\gamma,0)=0.\end{cases}

Thus, we obtain

\widehat{u}_{2\alpha}(\gamma,t)=\cos(|\gamma|^{\alpha}t)=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{i\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)|\gamma|^{\alpha}t}+e^{-i\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)|\gamma|^{\alpha}t}\right].

(31)

By taking the inverse Fourier transform of (
31
) and considering formula (
9
) the proof is complete.
∎

By means of the usual construction described in (
4
), the function
u_{2\alpha(x,t)}
can be viewed as the density of a signed measure on the space of real-valued measurable functions. Similarly to the integer case, the pseudo-density admits a power series representation as

u_{2\alpha}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\alpha\pi t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)^{2k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2k+1}{\alpha}\right)}{(2k)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha}\right),\qquad\alpha>1

(32)

which can be easily obtained by using (
21
). Moreover, the pseudo-density (
32
) admits a probabilistic representation in terms of an expected value of amplified oscillations with Weibull distributed random parameters

u_{2\alpha}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\left[\sin\left(a_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(b_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)\right)\right]

(33)

where
G_{\gamma}(\tau)
is a random variable with Weibull distribution as in theorem
2
and
a_{\alpha}=\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}
,
b_{\alpha}=\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}.

We omit the proof of formula (
33
) which coincides with the proof of theorem
2
in view of (
32
).

4
Subordinated Fresnel pseudoprocesses

In this section we study the fractional Fresnel pseudoprocess
F_{2\alpha}(t)
time-changed with an independent stable subordinator
S_{\theta}(t)
having characteristic function

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\gamma S_{\theta}(t)}\right]=e^{-t|\gamma|^{\theta}e^{-i\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma}}.

(34)

Our first result concerns the pseudo-density of the subordinated pseudoprocess, for which a power series representation is obtained in the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
F_{2\alpha}(t)
be the Fresnel pseudoprocess of order
\alpha>1
and
S_{\theta}(t)
an independent stable subordinator of exponent
\theta
with characteristic function (
34
). If
\alpha\theta>1
the following relationship holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(F_{2\alpha}(S_{\theta}(t))\in dx\right)/dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha\theta\pi t^{\frac{1}{\alpha\theta}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha\theta}}}\right)^{2k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2k+1}{\alpha\theta}\right)}{(2k)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha}\right),\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}.

(35)

Proof.

We use the Wright function representation of the probability density function
h_{\theta}(x,t)
of the stable subordinator
S_{\theta}(t)

h_{\theta}(x,t)=\frac{\theta t}{x^{\theta+1}}W_{-\theta,1-\theta}\left(-\frac{t}{x^{\theta}}\right)=\frac{\theta}{x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}t^{k+1}}{x^{\theta(k+1)}k!\;\Gamma(-\theta(k+1)+1)},\qquad x>0,\;t>0.

In light of (
32
) we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(F_{2\alpha}

\displaystyle(S_{\theta}(t))\in dx)/dx=\int_{0}^{+\infty}u_{2\alpha}(x,s)h_{\theta}(s,t)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta t}{\alpha\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}\;\Gamma\left(\frac{2k+1}{\alpha}\right)\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\cdot\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{-\theta-\frac{2k+1}{\alpha}-1}W_{-\theta,1-\theta}\left(-\frac{t}{s^{\theta}}\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha\pi t^{\frac{1}{\alpha\theta}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha\theta}}}\right)^{2k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2k+1}{\alpha}\right)}{(2k)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\cdot\int_{0}^{+\infty}y^{\frac{2k+1}{\alpha\theta}}W_{-\theta,1-\theta}\left(-y\right)dy.

(36)

By using the Mellin transform of the Wright function (see Prudnikov et al.
[
14
]
, pag. 355)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\text{W}_{a,b}(-x)\;x^{\eta-1}dx=\frac{\Gamma(\eta)}{\Gamma(b-a\eta)},\qquad\eta>0,\;a>-1,\;b\in\mathbb{C}

the representation (
35
) is easily obtained. The convergence of the power series for
\alpha\theta>1
can be proved by using the Stirling formula for the gamma function.
∎

The next theorem shows that the probabilistic representation in terms of amplified oscillations with Weibull distributed random parameters is maintained if the Fresnel pseudoprocess is time-changed with an independent stable subordinator.

Theorem 5
.

For
\alpha\theta>1
the following relationship holds:

\mathbb{P}\left(F_{2\alpha}(S_{\theta}(t))\in dx\right)/dx=\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\left[\sin\left(a_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha\theta}(1/t)\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(b_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha\theta}(1/t)\right)\right]

(37)

where
G_{\gamma}(\tau)
is a random variable with Weibull distribution having probability density function

g_{\gamma}(y;\tau)=\gamma\frac{y^{\gamma-1}}{\tau}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{\gamma}}{\tau}\right),\qquad y,\gamma,\tau>0

and
a_{\alpha}=\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha},\qquad b_{\alpha}=\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}.

Proof.

By using the integral representation of the gamma function in formula (
35
) we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(F_{2\alpha}(S_{\theta}(t))\in dx)/dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha\theta}}}\right)^{2k+1}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2k+1}{\alpha\theta}+1\right)}{(2k+1)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(n+1)}{2\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\theta t}{\pi x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-ty^{\alpha\theta}}y^{\alpha\theta-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(yx)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\sin\left(\pi\frac{(2k+1)(\alpha+1)}{2\alpha}\right)dy

By using formula (
26
) as in theorem
2
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}(F_{2\alpha}(S_{\theta}(t))\in dx)/dx=\frac{\alpha\theta t}{\pi x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-ty^{\alpha\theta}}y^{\alpha\theta-1}\sin\left(xy\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\right)\operatorname{cosh}\left(xy\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\right)dy.

∎

We now show that the distribution of the time-changed Fresnel pseudoprocess

Y_{2\alpha,\theta}(t)=F_{2\alpha}(S_{\theta}(t))

is a genuine probability distribution if the exponent of the stable subordinator
S_{\theta}(t)
is chosen in a suitable way. We start by observing that the pseudoprocess
Y_{2\alpha,\theta}(t)
has characteristic function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[e^{i\gamma Y_{2\alpha,\theta}(t)}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\gamma F_{2\alpha}(S_{\theta}(t))}\big\lvert S_{\theta}(t)\right]\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\cos\left(\gamma^{\alpha}S_{\theta}(t)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)|\gamma|^{\alpha}S_{\theta}(t)}+e^{-i\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)|\gamma|^{\alpha}S_{\theta}(t)}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{-t|\gamma|^{\alpha\theta}e^{i\frac{\theta\pi}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma}}+e^{-t|\gamma|^{\alpha\theta}e^{-i\frac{\theta\pi}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma}}\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{-t|\gamma|^{\alpha\theta}\cos\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\left(1+i\tan\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma\right)}+e^{-t|\gamma|^{\alpha\theta}\cos\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\left(1-i\tan\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma\right)}\right]

(38)

where we have used formulas (
20
) and (
34
).

The characteristic function of a stable process
H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)
of exponent
\nu\neq 1
can be expressed in the form

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\gamma H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)}\right]=e^{-t\sigma^{\nu}|\gamma|^{\nu}\left(1-i\beta\operatorname{sgn}\gamma\tan\frac{\pi\nu}{2}\right)+i\mu t\gamma}

(39)

where
0<\nu\leq 2,\;\nu\neq 1
is the exponent of the stable process,
\sigma>0
is the dispersion parameter,
\beta\in[-1,1]
is the skewness parameter and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is the drift parameter.

Denote by
Z_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)
the random variable

Z_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)=\begin{cases}H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;\frac{1}{2}\\
-H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

(40)

where where
H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)
is a stable random variable with characteristic function (
39
).
By setting

\nu=\alpha\theta,\qquad\beta=-\frac{\tan\frac{\pi\theta}{2}}{\tan\frac{\pi\alpha\theta}{2}},\qquad\sigma=\left(\cos\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}},\qquad\mu=0

(41)

formula (
38
) implies that

Y_{2\alpha,\theta}(t)\overset{i.d.}{=}Z_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)

(42)

Observe that the result (
42
) holds if the constraints
\nu\in(0,2]
and
\beta\in[-1,1]
are satisfied. As discussed by Marchione and Orsingher
[
9
]
, for fixed
\nu\in(0,2)
and
\beta\in(-1,1),\;\beta\neq 0,
it is always possible to choose
\alpha>1
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that the relationships (
41
) are satisfied.

Formula (
40
) shows that the Fresnel pseudoprocess time-changed with a stable subordinator is identical in distribution, for all
t>0
, to a stochastic process which is obtained by randomly choosing at time
t=0
the sign of an asymmetric stable process. Since the positive and negative signs are equiprobable, the resulting random process is symmetric. As stable distributions are unimodal, one may expect the random variable (
42
) to have a bimodal distribution. We will now show that this is not necessarely true.

Consider the particular case of a Fresnel pseudoprocess
F_{2\alpha}(t)
of order
2\alpha,\;\alpha>1
time-changed with a stable subordinator
S_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t)
. The subordinated pseudoprocess

Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t)=F_{2\alpha}(S_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t))

has characteristic function

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\gamma Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t)}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{-t|\gamma|\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\left(1+i\tan\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma\right)}+e^{-t|\gamma|\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\left(1-i\tan\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma\right)}\right]

(43)

whose inverse Fourier transform coincides with the mixture of two asymmetric Cauchy probability density functions. The resulting distribution is symmetric and reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t)\in dx\right)/dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\frac{t\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}{x^{2}+t^{2}-2xt\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}+\frac{t\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}{x^{2}+t^{2}+2xt\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}{\pi}\left[\frac{x^{2}+t^{2}}{x^{4}+t^{4}+2x^{2}t^{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}\right].

(44)

The two modal points of the probability density function (
44
) are located at
x=\pm t\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}
. For
\alpha=2
, our result coincides with formula (5.4) of the paper by Orsingher and D’Ovidio
[
11
]
. For
1<\alpha<3
, the first derivative of the probability density function (
44
) has three roots in
x=0
and
x=\pm t\sqrt{2\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}-1}
. Thus, the density function has two modes in
x=\pm t\sqrt{2\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}-1}
and a minimum at the origin. However, for
\alpha\geq 3
, the only real root of the first derivative is in
x=0
. The mixture density is unimodal and reaches its maximum at the origin.

5
The asymmetric Fresnel pseudoprocess

In section
2
we have introduced asymmetric solutions to equation (
15
) for odd values of
n
. A natural fractional extension of such solutions is obtained by considering the function

u_{2\alpha,p}(x,t)=\frac{1}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\Biggl[p\cdot{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}\left(-\frac{x}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)+(1-p)\cdot{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)\Biggr],\;\;x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0

(45)

where
p\in[0,1]
and
\alpha>1
as usual. It can be checked that the Fourier transform of the pseudo-density (
45
) satisfies the ordinary differential equation

\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{u}_{2\alpha,p}(\gamma,t)}{\partial t^{2}}=-|\gamma|^{2\alpha}\widehat{u}_{2\alpha,p}(\gamma,t)

which implies that, similarly to the symmetric case, the pseudo-density is a solution to the fractional partial differential equation

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{2\alpha}u}{\partial|x|^{2\alpha}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0.

Moreover, by using the following probabilistic representation for the Airy function (Marchione and Orsingher
[
9
]
)

\frac{1}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\text{Ai}}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{(\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\right)=\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\left[\sin\left(a_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)\right)e^{-b_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)}\right]

(46)

we have that

u_{2\alpha,p}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi x}\mathbb{E}\left[\sin\left(a_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)\right)\left(pe^{b_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)}+(1-p)e^{-b_{\alpha}xG_{\alpha}(1/t)}\right)\right]

(47)

where we have used the same notation of theorem
5
. Of course, for
p=\frac{1}{2}
, formula (
47
) coincides with (
33
).

Denote by
F_{2\alpha}^{(p)}(t)
the asymmetric Fresnel pseudoprocess having pseudo-density (
45
) and let
S_{\theta}(t)
be a stable subordinator with characteristic function (
34
) such that
F_{2\alpha}^{(p)}(t)
and
S_{\theta}(t)
are independent. Consider the subordinated pseudoprocess

Y_{2\alpha,\theta}^{(p)}(t)=F_{2\alpha}^{(p)}(S_{\theta}(t)).

It can be verified that
Y_{2\alpha,\theta}^{(p)}(t)
has characteristic function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\gamma Y_{2\alpha,\theta}^{(p)}(t)}\right]=\left[p\cdot e^{-t|\gamma|^{\alpha\theta}\cos\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\left(1+i\tan\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma\right)}+(1-p)\cdot e^{-t|\gamma|^{\alpha\theta}\cos\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\left(1-i\tan\frac{\pi\theta}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\gamma\right)}\right].

(48)

Denote by
Z_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)
the random variable

Z_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)=\begin{cases}H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;p\\
-H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)\qquad&\text{with prob.}\;1-p\end{cases}

(49)

where
H_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)
is a stable random variable with characteristic function (
39
). Formula (
48
) implies that

Y_{2\alpha,\theta}^{(p)}(t)\overset{i.d.}{=}Z_{\nu}(\sigma,\beta,\mu;t)

(50)

where we have used the parametrization (
41
).
Thus, the asymmetric Fresnel pseudoprocess time-changed with a suitable stable subordinator is identical in distribution, for all
t>0
, to a stochastic process obtained by randomly choosing at the initial time
t=0
the sign of an asymmetric stable process. The resulting random process is asymmetric for
p\neq\frac{1}{2}
.

By considering the special case of a mixture of Cauchy distributions, we have shown in the previous section that the symmetric mixture density of the process (
40
) can have one or three stationary points. In the first case the distribution is unimodal while in the second case it is bimodal. We will now discuss a third possibile case which can occur for the asymmetric process (
49
). For suitable choices of the parameters, the probability density function can have exactly two stationary points, one being a maximum point and the other one being an inflection point. Consider now an asymmetric mixture of Cauchy distributions, which emerges from the subordinated pseudoprocess

Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}^{(p)}(t)=F_{2\alpha}^{(p)}(S_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(t)).

By using formula (
48
) for
\theta=\frac{1}{\alpha},\;\alpha>1

it can be shown that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}^{(p)}(t)\in dx\right)/dx=

\displaystyle\frac{t\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}{\pi}\left[\frac{p}{x^{2}+t^{2}-2xt\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}+\frac{1-p}{x^{2}+t^{2}+2xt\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}{\pi}\left[\frac{x^{2}+t^{2}+2(2p-1)xt\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}{x^{4}+t^{4}+2x^{2}t^{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}\right].

(51)

In order to study the stationary points of the density (
51
), we should study in principle its first and second derivatives. By setting
f(x)=\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{2\alpha,\frac{1}{\alpha}}^{(p)}(t)\in dx\right)/dx
, we have that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2t\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}{\pi}\bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{\left(x+(2p-1)t\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\right)\left(x^{4}+t^{4}+2x^{2}t^{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\right)}{\left(x^{4}+t^{4}+2x^{2}t^{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2x\left(x^{2}+t^{2}+2xt(2p-1)\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\right)(x^{2}+t^{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha})}{\left(x^{4}+t^{4}+2x^{2}t^{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\right)^{2}}\bigg].

(52)

The numerator of the expression (
52
) is a fifth-order polynomial in
x
. Its roots are difficult to find out and it is not even guaranteed that they are expressible in terms of radicals. The study of the fifth-order algebraic equation emerging from formula (
52
) is beyond the aim of our paper. However, in order to prove that the density (
51
) can have an inflection point, it is sufficient for us to find a specific combination of
\alpha
and
p
for which the inflection point occurs.

A substantial simplification of formula (
52
) can be obtained if
\alpha
and
p
are chosen in such a way that the terms
x+(2p-1)t\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}
and
x^{2}+t^{2}\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}
in the numerator have the same root. Thus, by setting

-(2p-1)t\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}=\pm t\sqrt{-\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}

(53)

we immediately obtain

p=\frac{\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\mp\sqrt{-\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}}{2\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}.

(54)

This implies that, if
p
satisfies the relationship (
54
), the point

x=\pm t\sqrt{-\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}

(55)

is a root of (
52
) and thus it is a stationary point of (
51
). Observe that, in order for (
55
) to be a real root, we need to impose the constraint
1<\alpha<2
. Moreover, since
\sin\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}>\sqrt{-\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}
for
1<\alpha<2
, the constraint
p\in[0,1]
is satisfied by formula (
54
). Finally, note that the choice of the sign in formula (
55
) is arbitrary as long as the correct sign is then chosen in formula (
54
).

Our next step is to determine the value of
\alpha
such that the stationary point (
55
) is an inflection point. By taking into account formula (
53
), tedious but straightforward calculation permits to prove that

f^{\prime\prime}\left(\pm t\sqrt{-\cos\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}\right)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{3\cos^{2}\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}-1}{2t^{3}\cos\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}\sin^{4}\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}.

(56)

Of course, the second derivative (
56
) vanishes for

\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2\arccos\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)}.

(57)

Moreover, since
\frac{\pi}{4}<\arccos\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)<\frac{\pi}{2}
, the constraint
1<\alpha<2
is satisfied by formula (
57
). Thus, by setting
p=\frac{\sqrt{2}\mp 1}{2\sqrt{2}}
as in formula (
54
), we have found a particular combination of
\alpha
and
p
for which the probability density function (
51
) has an inflection point in

x=\pm\frac{t}{\sqrt{3}}.

For such a choice of
\alpha
and
p
, we can rewrite formula (
52
) as

f^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{2t}{\pi\sqrt{3}}\;\frac{\left(x\mp\frac{t}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{2}\left(x^{3}\mp\frac{t}{\sqrt{3}}x^{2}+t^{2}x\pm\sqrt{3}t^{3}\right)}{\left(x^{4}+t^{4}-\frac{2t^{2}}{3}x^{2}\right)^{2}}

(58)

The expression (
58
) has two coinciding real roots in
x=\pm\frac{t}{\sqrt{3}}
, which represent the inflection point of the density, and a real root which represents the maximum point. Observe that the expression
x^{3}\mp\frac{t}{\sqrt{3}}x^{2}+t^{2}x\pm\sqrt{3}t^{3}
in the numerator of (
58
) is strictly monotonically increasing and thus it has only one real root.

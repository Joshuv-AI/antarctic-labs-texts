Stochastic expansion for the pricing of Asian and basket options Thanks: Date: March 15, 2024.
Fabien Le Floc’h

Abstract
We present closed analytical approximations for the pricing of basket options, also applicable to Asian options with discrete averaging under the Black-Scholes model with time-dependent parameters. The formulae are obtained by using a stochastic Taylor expansion around a log-normal proxy model and are found to be highly accurate for Asian options in practice as well as for vanilla options with discrete dividends.



keywords

Stochastic expansion, Asian option, basket option, Black-Scholes, arithmetic averaging, cash dividends.

1
Introduction

The buyer of a vanilla basket option of strike
K
and maturity
T
on underlying assets
(S_{i})_{i=1,...,n}
receives at maturity
T

\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S_{i}(T)-K\right)
for a call and
\max\left(K-\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S_{i}(T)\right)
for a put. The weights
w_{i}
typically sum to one. The buyer of an Asian call option of strike
K
and averaging dates
(t_{i})_{i=1,...,n}
on an asset
S
receives
\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S(t_{i})-K\right)
. Under the Black-Scholes model with a term-structure of interest rates, dividend yields and volatilities, Asian options with discrete arithmetic averaging can be reduced to an equivalent vanilla basket option with a specific correlation structure.
Asian (basket) options are particularly popular among commodity traders, but is also traded regularly in the foreign exchange and in the equity derivatives spaces.

There is no exact closed analytic formula for the pricing of arithmetic Asian or vanilla basket options. The problem has been widely studied. In the context of the Black-Scholes model, early valuation techniques were based on approximating the arithmetic mean by a geometric mean
[
17
]
, or on moment matching against a lognormal distribution as in Levy
[
12
]
. Later, Curran
[
4
]
introduced a much more accurate pricing based on geometric conditioning which has been more recently further improved, by exploring better integration cutoffs and conditioning variables
[
5
]
. Ju proposed a third-order correction for the Levy moment matching in
[
9
]
, while Zhou
[
19
]
looked at matching four moments with the log extended skew normal distribution. Beyond the Black-Scholes model, but restricted to Asian options, Zhang and Oosterlee propose an efficient method based on the Fourier cosine series for affine models with a known characteristic function in
[
18
]
.

Here, we consider a Black-Scholes setting with time-dependent parameters, and propose a stochastic expansion using the geometric average as a proxy, following the ideas of Etoré and Gobet, who applied their expansion to price vanilla options with discrete dividends under the piecewise-lognormal model in
[
6
]
, with further refinements in
[
11
]
. We start by deriving the first, second and third-order expansions and then evaluate their accuracy on a sample of previously published cases. This allows for a comparison with other accurate approximations.

2
Reductions to vanilla basket options

2.1
From Asian to basket

Let
F(0,t)
be the forward price of the underlying asset from valuation time to the date
t
. In the Black-Scholes model with time-dependent parameters we have

F(0,t)=S(0)e^{\int_{0}^{t}r(s)-q(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\,,

with
r,q
being the instantateneous interest rate and dividend yield and
S(t)
the underlying asset spot at time
t
. Under the risk-neutral measure
\mathbb{Q}
, the forward price follows

\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}F(t,T)=F(t,T)\sigma(t)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W(t)\,,

where
W
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Brownian motion.

The buyer of an arithmetic Asian option of strike
K
and maturity
T
on an underlying
S
with observation dates
(t_{i})_{i=1,...,n}
with
t_{n}=T
, receives at maturity
\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S(t_{i})-K\right)
for a call and
\max\left(K-\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S(t_{i})\right)
for a put. It may be expressed as a basket option of strike
K
and maturity
T
on
n
underlying assets
(S_{i})_{i=1,...,n}
. The covariance between the assets must match the covariance of
S
between different observation times, this means
1
1

1

We use the Itô isometry to obtain Equations
1
and
3
.

\rho_{i,j}\sqrt{v_{i}v_{j}}=v_{i\wedge j}\,,

(1)

where
\rho_{i,j}
denotes the correlation between the assets
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
,
v_{i}=\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\sigma^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
being the total variance up to time
t_{i}
for the asset
S
.
Each underlying asset forward to
T
must match the forwards of the single asset to
t_{i}
, meaning

F_{i}(0,T)=\mathbb{E}[S_{i}(T)]=\mathbb{E}[S(t_{i})]=F(0,t_{i})=S(0)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{i}}(r(s)-q(s))\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\,.

(2)

Thus, the arithmetic Asian option on
S
with observations
t_{i}
corresponds to a vanilla basket option on
S_{i}
with forwards to maturity
F_{i}(0,T)
, total variances
v_{i}
and correlations
\rho_{i,j}
for
i,j=1,...,n
.

2.2
Asian baskets

This may be extended to Asian baskets with payoff at maturity

\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{j}S_{j}(t_{i})-K,0\right)\,,

where
(\mu_{j})_{j=1...m}
are basket weights and
S_{j}
are the different assets with correlation
\rho_{j,l}
. We may define
n\times m
assets
\hat{S}_{i+n(j-1)}
with forward
F_{j}(t_{i})
, total variance
\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\sigma_{j}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
, correlation

\hat{\rho}_{i+n(j-1),k+n(l-1)}=\rho_{j,l}\frac{\int_{0}^{t_{i}\wedge t_{k}}\sigma_{j}(s)\sigma_{l}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\sigma_{j}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\sigma_{l}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}}\,,

(3)

for
(i,k)\in\{1,...,n\}^{2}
,
(j,l)\in\left\{1,...,m\right\}^{2}
. The Asian basket option is equivalent to a vanilla basket option on
\hat{S}_{i+n(j-1)}
with weights
w_{i}\mu_{j}
.

2.3
Average strike options

A floating strike Asian (call) option paying at maturity
T
,
\max\left(S(T)-k\sum_{i=1}^{n}S(t_{i})\right)
, with
k
being a strike percentage, can be expressed as an equivalent fixed strike Asian (put) option
[
15
]
. Through this symmetry, the formulae given in this paper may be used to price floating (also known as average) strike options.

3
Expansions

3.1
The proxy

An early technique, first explored by Vorst
[
17
]
to estimate the price of Asian options with arithmetic averaging is to approximate it by the geometric average option equivalent, because the geometric average of geometric Brownian motions is another geometric Brownian motion and can thus be priced with the usual Black-Scholes formula. This was extended to the more general cases of vanilla basket options by Gentle
[
7
]
.

Under the risk-neutral measure
\mathbb{Q}
, the forward price for each asset follows

\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}F_{i}(t,T)=F_{i}(t,T)\sigma_{i}(t)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}(t)\,,

where
W_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Brownian motion, and we have
\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{j}=\rho_{i,j}\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}t
.
The payoff at maturity of the basket option reads

\displaystyle V(T)

\displaystyle=\left[\eta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}S_{i}(T)-K\right)\right]^{+}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}F_{i}(0,T)\right)\left[\eta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-{K}^{\star}\right)\right]^{+}=A\left[\eta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-{K}^{\star}\right)\right]^{+}

with
\eta=\pm 1
for a call (respectively a put) option and

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{i}=\frac{w_{i}F_{i}(0,T)}{A(T)}\,,\quad A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}F_{i}(0,T)\,,\quad{K}^{\star}=\frac{K}{A}\,,

\displaystyle S_{i}^{\star}(t)=\frac{S_{i}(t)}{F_{i}(0,t)}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{i}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{i}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}(s)}\,.

We approximate the arithmetic average by the geometric average leading to

V(0)\approx B(0,T)A\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\eta\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-{K}^{\star}\right)\right)^{+}\\
\right]

(4)

with
a_{i}=\tilde{a}_{i}
as a specific case.

Let

G(T)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}=e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\frac{1}{2}a_{i}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}a_{i}\sigma_{i}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}W_{i}(s)}

We have

\displaystyle m(T)=\mathbb{E}\left[\ln G(T)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}v_{i}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}^{2}(T)=\mathrm{Var}\left[\ln G(T)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}\rho_{i,j}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s\,.

(6)

with
v_{i}=\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
.

Vorst
[
17
]
, Gentle
[
7
]
propose to adjust the strike price of the geometric average option such that the expectation of the term inside the max function is preserved and use
\tilde{K}^{\star}=K^{\star}-(1-\mathbb{E}\left[G(T)\right])
instead of
K^{\star}
.
A different way to preserve the expectation consists in adjusting the spot side instead of the strike side:

V(0)\approx B(0,T)A\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\eta\left(\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-{K}^{\star}\right)\right)^{+}\right]

(7)

with
\alpha=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[G(T)]}
and
B(0,T)
is the discount factor to the payment date. The latter is found to be more accurate in Borovykh
[
1
]
.

Levy
[
12
]
matches not only the mean but also the variance of the arithmetic average by a lognormal distribution. We have

\nu_{A}^{2}=\ln\left(\mathrm{Var}\left[A(T)\right]\right)=\ln\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}e^{\rho_{i,j}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\right)\,..

(8)

We may adjust the
a_{i}
used in the geometric mean such that the variance of the geometric mean matches exactly the one of the arithmetic mean by using

a_{i}=\tilde{a}_{i}\frac{\nu_{A}}{\tilde{\nu}}\,.

(9)

Through Equation
6
and the definition of the factor
\alpha
, we obtain the following formula for our Vorst Geometric (VG) and Vorst Levy (VL) approximations, using respectively
a_{i}=\tilde{a}_{i}
and
a_{i}
as defined by Equation
9
:

\displaystyle V(0)\approx A\cdot\mathrm{Black}(1,K^{\star},{\nu}^{2},T)

(10)

where
\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)
denotes the Black-76 formula applied to the forward
F
, strike
K
and total variance
v
to maturity
T
and
\nu=\tilde{\nu}
for the geometric approximation,
\nu=\nu_{A}
for the Levy approximation. We have

\mathrm{Black}(F,K,v,T)=\eta B(0,T)\left[F\Phi(\eta d_{1})-K\Phi(\eta d_{2})\right]

(11)

with
d_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{v}}\left(\ln\frac{F}{K}+\frac{1}{2}v\right)
,
d_{2}=d_{1}-\sqrt{v}
.

We will adopt the Vorst geometric formula with adjusted forward as proxy for our stochastic Taylor expansion. The Vorst geometric formula with strike shift would lead to slightly more complicated equations and exhibit a worse accuracy.

The Vorst Levy formula corresponds exactly to the Levy approximation, but defines a stochastic process based on the individual underlying asset processes. This will allow to define the stochastic expansions based on this alternative proxy as well.

3.2
First order expansion

Let
h(x)=\max(\eta x,0)
, we apply a Taylor expansion of order-1 on
h
:

\mathbb{E}\left[B(0,T)h\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-K^{\star}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[B(0,T)h\left(\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\\
+\mathbb{E}\left[B(0,T)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}\right)h^{\prime}\left(\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\\
+\mathrm{Error}_{2}(h)

(12)

where
\mathrm{Error}_{2}(h)\leq_{c}2\left(||\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}||_{3}\right)^{2}
. We may rewrite the second term using a derivative with regards to
K^{\star}
, and interchange derivation and expectation (see Etoré and Gobet
[
6
]
) to obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}\right)h^{\prime}\left(\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\\
=-\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}\right)h\left(\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-K^{\star}\right)\right]

(13)

Let
G^{\star}=\alpha G
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[G^{\star}(T)\right]=1
and
G^{\star}
may be thus expressed as

G^{\star}(T)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)^{2}\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)dW_{G}(T)}

in probability, with
W_{G}
being a Brownian motion and
\beta^{2}(s)=\frac{\partial\nu^{2}(s)}{\partial s}
. We interpret
\frac{G^{\star}(T)}{G^{\star}(0)}
as a change of measure on
\mathcal{F}_{T}
. Under the new induced measure
\mathbb{Q}^{G}
,
\tilde{W}_{G}(T)=W_{G}(T)-\int_{0}^{T}\beta(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
is a Brownian motion. Then
G^{\star}(T)
under
\mathbb{Q}^{G}
has the same law as
G^{\star}(T)e^{\nu^{2}(T)}
under
\mathbb{Q}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{\star}(T)h^{\prime}\left(G^{\star}(T)-K^{\star}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathbb{E}^{G}\left[h\left(\tilde{G}^{\star}(T)e^{\nu^{2}(T)}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\,.

(14)

The right hand side corresponds to the derivative of a vanilla option price under the Black model with forward
e^{\nu^{2}(T)}
, strike
K^{\star}
and total variance
\nu^{2}(T)
.

In similar fashion we use a change of measure
\frac{S_{i}^{\star}(T)}{S_{i}^{\star}(0)}
and define

\tilde{W}_{i}(t)=W_{i}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{i}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s

to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[S_{i}^{\star}(T)h^{\prime}\left(G^{\star}(T)-K^{\star}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathbb{E}^{i}\left[h\left(\tilde{G}^{\star}(T)e^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\,.

(15)

We use Equation
14
and Equation
15
in Equation
13
to obtain the first order expansion

V_{\textsf{VG1}}=A\cdot\mathrm{Black}(1,K^{\star},\nu^{2},T)\\
+A\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\nu^{2}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)-A\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\bar{v}_{i}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)\,.

(16)

with

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{l}v_{i,l}\,,

\displaystyle\quad v_{i,l}=\rho_{i,l}\int_{0}^{T}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{l}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s\,.

(17)

In particular, for an Asian, we have
v_{i,l}=\int_{0}^{t_{i}\wedge t_{j}}\sigma^{2}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s
. For baskets, the volatilities are taken to be constant equal to each underlying implied volatility to maturity in practice and we have
v_{i,l}=\rho_{i,l}\sqrt{v_{i}v_{l}}
.

The relevant derivatives of the Black-76 formula are given in Appendix
A
. The formula with the Vorst Levy proxy is exactly the same, but uses different
\nu
and
a_{i}
.

3.3
Second order expansion

The second-order Taylor expansion involves the additional term

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}\right)^{2}h\left(\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-K^{\star}\right)\right]

We decompose it into three terms. The first term involves only
G^{\star}
, we perform two successive changes of measure around
\frac{G^{\star}(T)}{G^{\star}(0)}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{\star}(T)^{2}h(G^{\star}(T)-K)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{G}\left[\tilde{G}^{\star}e^{\nu^{2}(T)}h\left(\tilde{G}^{\star}e^{\nu^{2}(T)}-K^{\star}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=e^{\nu^{2}(T)}\mathbb{E}^{G^{2}}\left[h\left(\tilde{\tilde{G}}^{\star}e^{2\nu^{2}(T)}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\,.

The second term involves the cross product
S_{i}^{\star}(T)G^{\star}(T)
. We perform two successive changes of measure around
\frac{G^{\star}(T)}{G^{\star}(0)}
and
\frac{S_{i}^{\star}(T)}{S_{i}^{\star}(0)}
to obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[S_{i}^{\star}(T)G^{\star}(T)h(G^{\star}(T)-K)\right]=\\
\mathbb{E}^{i}\left[\tilde{G}^{\star}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}h\left(\tilde{G}^{\star}e^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}-K^{\star}\right)\right]=\\
e^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\mathbb{E}^{i,G}\left[h\left(\tilde{\tilde{G}}^{\star}e^{\nu^{2}(T)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\,.

The third term involves
S_{i}^{\star}(T)S_{j}^{\star}(T)
. We perform two successive changes of measure around
\frac{S_{i}^{\star}(T)}{S_{i}^{\star}(0)}
and
\frac{S_{j}^{\star}(T)}{S_{j}^{\star}(0)}
to obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[S_{i}^{\star}(T)S_{j}^{\star}(T)h(G^{\star}(T)-K)\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}^{i}\left[\tilde{S}_{j}^{\star}(T)e^{\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}h\left(\tilde{G}^{\star}e^{\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{l}\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,l}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{l}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\\
=e^{\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,j}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{j}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s}\mathbb{E}^{i,j}\left[h\left(\tilde{\tilde{G}}^{\star}e^{\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{l}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{i,l}\sigma_{i}(s)\sigma_{l}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}\rho_{j,l}\sigma_{j}(s)\sigma_{l}(s)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}s\right)}-K^{\star}\right)\right]\,.

Our formula for the second order expansion thus reads

V_{\textsf{VG2}}=A\cdot\mathrm{Black}(1,K^{\star},\nu^{2},T)\\
+A\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\nu^{2}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)-A\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\bar{v}_{i}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)\\
+\frac{A}{2}e^{\nu^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{2\nu^{2}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)+\frac{A}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}e^{\bar{v}_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\nu^{2}+\bar{v}_{i}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)\\
-A\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}\tilde{a}_{j}e^{v_{i,j}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\bar{v}_{i}+\bar{v}_{j}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)\,.

(18)

3.4
Third order expansion

The third order expansion adds the term

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}S_{i}^{\star}(T)-\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}\right)^{3}h\left(\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}^{\star}(T)^{a_{i}}-K^{\star}\right)\right]

In similar fashion as for the second order, we perform three consecutive changes of measure involving
\frac{G^{\star}(0)}{G^{\star}(0)}
,
\frac{S_{i}^{\star}(T)}{S^{\star}(0)}
. This leads to our third order expansion
Our formula for the second order expansion thus reads

\displaystyle V_{\textsf{VG3}}

\displaystyle=A\cdot\mathrm{Black}(1,K^{\star},\nu^{2},T)

(19)

\displaystyle+A\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\nu^{2}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)-A\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial K^{\star}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\bar{v}_{i}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{A}{2}e^{\nu^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{2\nu^{2}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)

\displaystyle-A\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}e^{\bar{v}_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\nu^{2}+\bar{v}_{i}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{A}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}\tilde{a}_{j}e^{v_{i,j}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{2}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\bar{v}_{i}+\bar{v}_{j}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{A}{6}e^{2\nu^{2}}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{3}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{3\nu^{2}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{A}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}e^{\nu^{2}+2\bar{v}_{i}}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{3}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{2\nu^{2}+\bar{v}_{i}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{A}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{a}_{i}\tilde{a}_{j}e^{\bar{v}_{i}+\bar{v}_{j}+v_{i,j}}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial{K^{\star}}^{3}}\mathrm{Black}\left(e^{\nu^{2}+\bar{v}_{i}+v_{j}},K^{\star},\nu^{2},T\right)

On those two examples, the third order expansion on the Levy proxy is the most precise, and the second order expansion is more accurate than the third order expansions of Etoré, Gobet and Le Floc’h.

7
Conclusion

For Asian options, the second order expansion on the Vorst Geometric proxy is already very accurate, better than the third order formula of Ju or the Deelstra lower bound approximations. The third order expansion is comparable in accuracy to the more sophisticated Deelstra conditioning with moment matching. The stochastic expansions have the advantage of requiring no numerical solver and no numerical integration; they thus allow for a straightforward stable calculation of the greeks, and facilitate a robust implementation. The Vorst Levy proxy leads to a slightly worse accuracy of the prices.

The expansions do not fare as well on basket options. In particular for very large (not so realistic) volatilities, the expansions misbehave, especially the third order expansion. The expansions on the Vorst Geometric proxy are also not so accurate under mild correlations. The Vorst Levy proxy remediates this latter issue. In the context of commodity options, the assets are typically futures on the same commodity but of different maturities, and have a correlation close to one
[
3
]
. The payoff also often involves the arithmetic average of those futures prices: the contract is effectively an Asian basket option. Given such a correlation structure, the expansions would behave very well on those options.

When applied to price vanilla European options with cash dividends under the piecewise-lognormal model (by reducing the problem to a vanilla basket option pricing problem), the expansions are found to be the most precise against previously published approximations tailored to the specific case of cash dividends.

Further research may apply similar expansions to Asian spread options, using a double geometric approximation as proxy (see also
[
2
]
). Other possible extensions would be for Asian options under discrete dividends assumptions, Asian options under stochastic volatility models with a reasonably fast pricing of European options such as Schöbel-Zhu.

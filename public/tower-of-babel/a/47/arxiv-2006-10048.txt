Abstract
We apply the C ∗ C^{*} -algebraic formalism of topological T-duality due to Mathai and Rosenberg to a broad class of topological spaces that include the torus bundles appearing in string theory compactifications with duality twists, such as nilmanifolds, as well as many other examples. We develop a simple procedure in this setting for constructing the T-duals starting from a commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebra with an action of ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} . We treat the general class of almost abelian solvmanifolds in arbitrary dimension in detail, where we provide necessary and sufficient criteria for the existence of classical T-duals in terms of purely group theoretic data, and compute them explicitly as continuous-trace algebras with non-trivial Dixmier–Douady classes. We prove that any such solvmanifold has a topological T-dual given by a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra bundle of noncommutative tori, which we also compute explicitly. The monodromy of the original torus bundle becomes a Morita equivalence among the fiber algebras, so that these C ∗ C^{*} -algebras rigorously describe the T-folds from non-geometric string theory.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background

T-duality is a symmetry of string theory which relates distinct
spaces that describe the same physics. It has presented a challenge to
mathematics in finding a rigorous framework in which these
‘equivalences’ of spaces is manifest. It was realized early on that
noncommutative geometry provides such a
framework
[
18
,
30
]
, at least in the
simplest cases of tori endowed with a trivial
gerbe, where subsequently it was shown that T-duality is realised as
Morita equivalence of noncommutative
tori
[
5
,
29
,
38
,
47
,
48
]
.

T-duality of spaces which are compactified on tori, or more generally
torus bundles, can be explained topologically
in terms of correspondence spaces which implement a smooth analog of the
Fourier–Mukai transform
[
24
]
. In the correspondence space
picture, T-duality transformations are realised as homeomorphisms in the
mapping class group of the fibres of doubled torus bundles. This gives rise to an
isomorphism of K-theory groups, which are the groups of D-brane
charges on the pertinent space; as this only concerns how topological
data of the space change under T-duality, it is commonly refered to as
‘topological T-duality’, to distinguish it from the more physical
notion of T-duality which also dictates how geometric data on
the space should transform. It was shown by
[
32
]
that
this can be reformulated in terms of the
C^{*}
-algebra of functions on
the space by considering its crossed product by an action of the
abelian Lie group
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, leading to a general T-duality formalism
that can be regarded as a noncommutative version of the topological
aspects of the Fourier–Mukai transform; this version of T-duality is
often called the ‘
C^{*}
-algebraic formulation’ of topological
T-duality.

The story becomes more interesting for spaces that are endowed with a
non-trivial gerbe, which in string theory typically comprise torus
bundles with ‘
H
-flux’. The gerbe can be encoded in the data of a
continuous-trace
C^{*}
-algebra with a non-trivial Dixmier–Douady
class, which is a noncommutative algebra to which the formalism of
topological T-duality was applied originally
in
[
3
,
32
,
33
]
. In addition to relating
spaces with different topologies, T-duality in string theory for such
instances predicts the existence of ‘non-geometric’ spaces, called T-folds
[
25
]
,
which cannot be viewed as conventional Hausdorff topological
spaces. In these instances the correspondence space picture
‘geometrizes’ the action of T-duality. It was shown
by
[
4
,
32
,
33
]
that the T-folds
of
[
25
]
have a rigorous incarnation in noncommutative
geometry as
C^{*}
-algebra bundles of noncommutative tori; necessary
and sufficient conditions for the existence of ‘classical’ T-dual
Hausdorff spaces were developed in terms of topological data, and explicit constructions of ‘non-classical’
T-duals as noncommutative torus bundles were given. These points of view were harmonised
in
[
6
]
, and in
[
7
]
a
C^{*}
-algebraic
version of the correspondence space construction was given. The explicit connections of these
noncommutative torus bundles to the T-folds of
[
25
]
in the
setting of noncommutative gauge theories on D-branes in T-folds
was elucidated
in
[
16
,
19
,
28
,
31
]
. Topological
T-duality and T-folds have also been studied rigorously from other
approaches based on homotopy
theory
[
8
,
9
]
and on higher
geometry
[
36
]
.

In string theory, the simplest examples of torus bundles are sometimes
called ‘twisted tori’
[
13
]
; although this name is a
misnomer, we continue to use it as it is convenient for our
purposes. These are fibrations of
n
-dimensional tori
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
over
a circle
{\mathbb{T}}
which do not carry the extra data of a gerbe; they have
monodromy in the mapping class group
\mathsf{SL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
of the torus
fibers. The simplest examples of these, the Heisenberg nilmanifolds,
are T-dual to torus bundles with
H
-flux and also to T-folds, and they
arise in the
C^{*}
-algebraic constructions
of
[
32
,
33
]
. However, there are other examples
which do not have any classical dual with
H
-flux, and these are
missed by the usual
C^{*}
-algebraic framework which starts from
continuous-trace algebras. The simplest examples of these with
n=2
were studied
in
[
28
]
in the language of noncommutative gauge theories,
where it was shown that the monodromy of the original torus bundles
becomes a non-trivial Morita equivalence of the fiber noncommutative
tori of the dual
C^{*}
-algebra bundle. As far as we are aware, these
are new examples of noncommutative torus bundles which have not been
rigorously studied in the mathematics literature, and the primary
purpose of this paper is to fill this gap: starting from the

C^{*}
-algebra of functions on a twisted torus in any dimension, we
give a rigorous construction of the topological T-duals in the

C^{*}
-algebraic framework and precisely describe the non-classical

C^{*}
-algebra bundles with their Morita equivalence monodromies. This
includes some of the examples from
[
32
,
33
]
based
on topological T-duality applied to continuous-trace algebras, and the
examples of
[
28
]
based on T-duality in noncommutative gauge
theory, while at the same time it produces many new examples. In
particular, we give a unified description of the noncommutative torus bundles
which are T-dual to twisted tori in any dimension.

The noncommutative gauge theory on a D-brane comes with other moduli,
in addition to the noncommutativity parameters, which also generally
transform in a non-trivial way under the monodromies so as to leave the physics
unchanged
[
28
]
. In the absence of other moduli, as in topological
T-duality, the non-trivial Morita equivalences of the fibres of the

C^{*}
-algebra bundles require an interpretation akin to the
topological monodromies, which act as homeomorphisms in the mapping
class group
\mathsf{SL}(n,{\mathbb{Z}})
of the fibres of the original twisted
torus. This is naturally achieved by considering our
C^{*}
-algebras as objects in a
category where both the usual
*
-isomorphisms as well as Morita equivalences are
realised as isomorphisms. This category is well-known to experts and
all of our considerations of topological T-duality in this paper will
take place therein. This perspective will also be advantageous for
eventual rigorous considerations of noncommutative gauge theories on
these
C^{*}
-algebra bundles in terms of projective modules, as well as
for the treatments of D-branes in terms of their K-theory, though we
do not pursue these further aspects in the present paper.

1.2
Summary and outline

In this paper our starting point is a very general definition of a
twisted torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
as the quotient of a locally
compact group
\mathsf{G}
by a lattice
\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
in
\mathsf{G}
; this definition
encompasses the
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
-bundles over
{\mathbb{T}}
discussed above,
along with many other known examples from string theory. We regard
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
as a ‘torus bundle without
H
-flux’, which is
captured simply by the
C^{*}
-algebra of functions
C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})
. This is ultimately the novelty of our
approach, which leads to a simpler perspective on topological
T-duality as compared to the approach of
[
32
,
33
]

based on the more complicated continuous-trace algebras. Our approach
uses similar techniques as those of
[
32
,
33
]
for
evaluating Morita equivalences of cross products by actions of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
though with a much simpler
C^{*}
-algebraic structure. In particular,
in this paper we do not develop any new
C^{*}
-algebraic machinery as such, but instead
we gather a fortuitously existing collection of results that enable us
to explicitly identify both classical and non-classical T-duals of
twisted tori with relatively straightforward algebraic techniques. On
the other hand, the tradeoff for the simplicity of our framework is
the absence of some key constructions
from
[
4
,
32
,
33
]
.

We have endeavoured throughout to provide a fairly self-contained, and at times
pedagogical, presentation. For this reason we have collected all the
key concepts and tools involving cross products of
C^{*}
-algebras and
Morita equivalence in Section
2
. Experts versed in

C^{*}
-algebra theory may safely skip this section.

In Section
3
we give our definition of twisted tori

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
and discuss the
C^{*}
-algebraic formulation of topological
T-duality that we employ in this paper. We describe how T-dual

C^{*}
-algebras are naturally isomorphic when regarded as objects of
the additive category
\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}
that underlies
Kasparov’s bivariant K-theory, and we adapt the construction of
noncommuative correspondences from
[
7
]
as diagrams in
this category. We spell out some simple techniques that we use to
compute classical T-duals with
H
-flux, i.e., the T-dual algebra is a
certain continuous-trace
C^{*}
-algebra with non-trivial
Dixmier–Douady class, and more general techniques based on Green’s
symmetric imprimitivity
theorem which enable the computation of noncommutative T-duals. We
illustrate our scheme on two well-known examples which have classical
T-duals: we reproduce the standard rules for T-duality of tori as well
as the topology changing mechanism for T-duality of orbifolds of
compact Lie groups
\mathsf{G}
.

In Section
4
we come to the main class of
examples and results of this paper. We review the definition and
topology of the special class of twisted tori provided by almost
abelian solvmanifolds, which are
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
-bundles over a circle

{\mathbb{T}}
. Accordingly, we regard the algebra of functions

C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})
as an object in the category

\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{T}}
of
C^{*}
-algebra bundles
over
{\mathbb{T}}
, where in particular fibrewise Morita equivalences are
isomorphisms. T-duality in this category requires fibrewise actions of

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and in particular
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
-actions which act non-trivially on
the base
{\mathbb{T}}
would take the algebra out of the category

\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{T}}
. This means that the ‘essentially
doubled spaces’ of
[
28
]
, which arise from T-duality along
the base circle and require a completely doubled formalism, are not
considered in this paper; they require working in a different
category, which we do not discuss here. We give necessary and
sufficient criteria for the existence of classical T-duals with

H
-flux in this case which are based on simple algebraic data of the
underlying group
\mathsf{G}
, and we explicitly compute the corresponding
continuous-trace
C^{*}
-algebras dual to any almost abelian
solvmanifold
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
satisfying these conditions. We
further show that any such solvmanifold has a non-classical T-dual
that is a
C^{*}
-algebra bundle of noncommutative
n
-tori over

{\mathbb{T}}
, which we also compute explicitly; this rigorously
confirms, in particular, arguments from string theory suggesting that non-geometric
solutions result from T-duality on some six-dimensional almost
abelian solvmanifolds
[
1
]
.

Finally, Section
5
is devoted to explicit examples
of the general formalism of Section
4
. We apply
our results to all three classes of three-dimensional
solvmanifolds. We recover in this way a new perspective on the
well-known T-duals of the Heisenberg nilmanifolds: the three-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
with
H
-flux, and the basic noncommutative principal

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
-bundle over
{\mathbb{T}}
given by the group
C^{*}
-algebra of the integer
Heisenberg group. Our general formalism also rigorously reproduces
the noncommutative torus bundles from
[
28
]
which are T-dual
to Euclidean solvmanifolds for the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
elliptic conjugacy class of

\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
. We extend these results to give new examples of
noncommutative torus bundles dual to Euclidean solvmanifolds for the

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{6}
elliptic conjugacy classes, as well as to the
Poincaré solvmanifolds. In particular, our formalism extends the

C^{*}
-algebraic formulation of topological T-duality to the case of
non-principal torus bundles, which have also been previously
considered in
[
22
]
.

2
Crossed products and duality

In this section we summarise some of the mathematical tools that we
will use in this paper. A good reference for the material covered in
the following is the book
[
52
]
. Throughout this paper,
all topological spaces are assumed to be second countable (hence
separable), locally compact and Hausdorff.

2.1
Dynamical systems and their crossed products

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a locally compact group and let
X
be a
\mathsf{G}
-space, i.e., a
topological space which is acted
upon
homeomorphically by
\mathsf{G}
; we denote the
\mathsf{G}
-action
\mathsf{G}\times X\to X
by
(\gamma,x)\mapsto\gamma\cdot x
. The pair
(X,\mathsf{G})
is
called a
transformation group
.
A related concept is that of a

dynamical system
, which is a triple
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)

consisting of an algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
and a locally compact group
\mathsf{G}
acting
on
{\mathcal{A}}
via a group homomorphism
\alpha\colon\mathsf{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}({\mathcal{A}})
,
denoted
\gamma\mapsto(\alpha_{\gamma}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}})
for
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
. In topological
T-duality one usually requires
{\mathcal{A}}
to be a
C^{*}
-algebra, in which
case
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
is called a
C^{*}
-dynamical
system
. Two dynamical systems
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
and
({\mathcal{B}},\mathsf{G},\beta)
are

equivalent
if there is an algebra isomorphism

\varphi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
which intertwines the
\mathsf{G}
-actions:
\varphi\circ\alpha_{\gamma}=\beta_{\gamma}\circ\varphi
for all
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
.

If
{\mathcal{A}}
is a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra, then we call
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
a

commutative
dynamical system. In that case, by Gelfand duality

{\mathcal{A}}=C_{0}(X)
is the algebra of continuous functions vanishing at
infinity on a
topological space
X

equipped with the
\mathsf{G}
-action
\alpha_{\gamma}^{\dagger}\big|_{X}
for
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
under
the identification of points
x\in X
with irreducible representations
of
C_{0}(X)
, which are one-dimensional and given by the point evaluation
maps
{\rm ev}_{x}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
with
{\rm ev}_{x}(\mathtt{f})=\mathtt{f}(x)
; then

(X,\mathsf{G})
is a transformation group. Conversely, given a transformation
group
(X,\mathsf{G})
, there is an associated commutative dynamical system

(C_{0}(X),\mathsf{G},\alpha)
, where
\alpha_{\gamma}(\mathtt{f})(x)=\mathtt{f}\big(\gamma^{-1}\cdot x\big)
for
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
,
\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X)
and
x\in X
. In other words, there is a one-to-one
correspondence between transformation groups and commutative
C^{*}
-dynamical
systems.

If the
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
is not commutative, then we call

({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
a
noncommutative

C^{*}
-dynamical system.

As usual, it is more useful to work with a representation rather than the
abstract dynamical system itself. A
covariant representation
of
a dynamical system
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
with
multiplier algebra
{\rm M}({\mathcal{B}})
is a
pair
({\Pi},U)
consisting of a homomorphism
{\Pi}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm M}({\mathcal{B}})
and a unitary representation
U\colon\mathsf{G}\to{\rm M}({\mathcal{B}})
,
\gamma\mapsto U_{\gamma}
which satisfy the compatibility condition

{\Pi}\big(\alpha_{\gamma}(a)\big)=U_{\gamma}{\Pi}(a)U_{\gamma}^{-1},

for all
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
and
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. A natural choice is to take

{\mathcal{B}}=\mathcal{K}({\mathcal{H}})
to be the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on a
separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, which gives a
representation
{\Pi}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm B}({\mathcal{H}})
of the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
by
bounded operators
{\rm B}({\mathcal{H}})
on
{\mathcal{H}}
and a unitary representation

U\colon\mathsf{G}\to{\rm B}({\mathcal{H}})
of the group
\mathsf{G}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
; in this case
we call
({\Pi},U)
a covariant representation of
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
on
{\mathcal{H}}
.

When a group
\mathsf{G}
acts on a space
X
, one is naturally interested
in considering the
quotient space
X/\mathsf{G}
of
\mathsf{G}
-orbits on
X
. When
\mathsf{G}
acts
freely and properly on

X
, this is described algebraically by the algebra of functions

C_{0}(X/\mathsf{G})
. More generally, the subalgebra of
\mathsf{G}
-invariant elements

{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathsf{G}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
of a
\mathsf{G}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
can be used to represent the quotient, even for

\mathsf{G}
-actions with fixed points. A more general and systematic way of
dealing with the effective algebraic “quotient” is through the

crossed product
algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
for a dynamical
system
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
. For a transformation group
(X,\mathsf{G})
,
this description is particularly powerful in the cases where the
quotient
X/\mathsf{G}
is not a Hausdorff space, while for a free and proper

\mathsf{G}
-action it gives an algebra with the same spectrum
X/\mathsf{G}

as the algebra
C_{0}(X/\mathsf{G})=C_{0}(X)^{\mathsf{G}}
of

\mathsf{G}
-invariant functions on
X
.

In order to define the crossed product
algebra, we first define

\|f\|_{\rm univ}:=\sup_{({\Pi},U)}\big\|({\Pi}\rtimes_{\alpha}U)(f)\big\|

for compactly supported functions
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})
, where the supremum is taken
over (possibly degenerate) covariant representations

({\Pi},U)
of
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
with

({\Pi}\rtimes_{\alpha}U)(f):=\int_{\mathsf{G}}{\Pi}\big(f(\gamma)\big)U_{\gamma}\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma),

and
\mu_{\mathsf{G}}
denotes the left invariant Haar measure on
\mathsf{G}
. This defines a norm, called the universal norm, on the
space
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})
.
Then the crossed product algebra
{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
is the
completion (in the universal norm) of the algebra
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})

equipped with the convolution product

\displaystyle(f\star f^{\prime})(\gamma):=\int_{\mathsf{G}}\,f(\gamma^{\prime})\alpha_{\gamma^{\prime}}\big(f^{\prime}\big(\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma\big)\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma^{\prime}),

(2.1)

for all
f,f^{\prime}\colon\mathsf{G}\to{\mathcal{A}}
. In general this is a noncommutative
multiplication, even for commutative dynamical systems.
Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, there is a
*
-structure on the
convolution algebra defined by

f^{\dagger}(\gamma):=\Delta_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma)^{-1}\alpha_{\gamma}\big(f\big(\gamma^{-1}\big)^{*}\big),

where
\Delta_{\mathsf{G}}\colon\mathsf{G}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is the modular function of

\mathsf{G}
defined through

\Delta_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma^{\prime})\int_{\mathsf{G}}\,f(\gamma)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathsf{G}}f(\gamma\gamma^{\prime})\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma)

for
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})
and
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{G}
. By the uniqueness of the left
invariant Haar measure
\mu_{\mathsf{G}}
up to a positive
constant,
\Delta_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma^{\prime})
is independent of
f

and
\Delta_{\mathsf{G}}
is easily proven to be a continuous group homomorphism from
\mathsf{G}
to the
multiplicative group
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
; it is trivial for abelian groups and for compact
groups.

When
{\mathcal{A}}=C_{\rm c}(X)

is the algebra of a commutative dynamical system, the convolution
algebra
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G}\times X)
consists of functions
f\colon\mathsf{G}\times X\to{\mathbb{C}}
and the
convolution product reads as

(f\star f^{\prime})(\gamma,x)=\int_{\mathsf{G}}f(\gamma^{\prime},x)f^{\prime}\big(\gamma^{\prime-1}\gamma,\gamma^{\prime-1}\cdot x\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma^{\prime}),

while the
*
-algebra structure is given by

f^{\dagger}(\gamma,x)=\Delta_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma)^{-1}\overline{f\big(\gamma^{-1},\gamma^{-1}\cdot x\big)}.

The crossed product is a generalization of the usual group algebra

C^{*}(\mathsf{G})
, the completion (in the universal norm) of
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G})
which is recovered in the case
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{C}}

(the
C^{*}
-algebra of a point)
wherein
\alpha_{\gamma}={\rm id}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
for all
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
and (
2.1
) recovers the usual convolution product of
functions on the group
\mathsf{G}
. As explained in
Section
2.2
below, the
group
C^{*}
-algebra description illustrates the relation between crossed
products and semi-direct products of groups (see Theorem
2.5
). We also
note that if a group
\mathsf{G}
acts trivially on an algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
, then

{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes\mathsf{G}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}\otimes C^{*}(\mathsf{G})
.

The crossed product can be thought of as a universal
object for covariant representations of the dynamical system

({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
, in the following sense: Define the
universal
covariant representation

({\bm{{\Pi}}},{\bm{U}})
of
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)

in
{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
by

\big({\bm{{\Pi}}}(a)f\big)(\gamma)=af(\gamma)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\big({\bm{U}}_{\gamma^{\prime}}f\big)(\gamma)=\alpha_{\gamma^{\prime}}\big(f\big(\gamma^{\prime}{}^{-1}\gamma\big)\big),

for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{G}
. Then the
universal property defining the crossed product implies that any covariant representation
({\Pi},U)
of

({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
factors through the
universal covariant representation: There exists a unique homomorphism

{\bm{\varphi}}\colon{\rm M}({\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G})\to{\rm M}({\mathcal{B}})
such that

{\Pi}={\bm{\varphi}}\circ{\bm{{\Pi}}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad U_{\gamma}={\bm{\varphi}}({\bm{U}}_{\gamma})

for all
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
.

If
({\Pi},U)
is a covariant representation of the dynamical system

({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, then

{\Phi}_{({\Pi},U)}(f):=({\Pi}\rtimes_{\alpha}U)(f)

defines a representation
{\Phi}_{({\Pi},U)}\colon C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})\to{\rm B}({\mathcal{H}})

of the crossed product
{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
as bounded operators on

{\mathcal{H}}
. This is called the
integrated form
of the covariant
representation
({\Pi},U)
. In particular, it maps the convolution
product (
2.1
) onto the operator product in the
algebra
{\rm B}({\mathcal{H}})
,

{\Phi}_{({\Pi},U)}(f\star g)={\Phi}_{({\Pi},U)}(f){\Phi}_{({\Pi},U)}(g),

and it is covariant in the sense that

{\Phi}_{({\Pi},U)}\big(i_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma)(f)\big)=U_{\gamma}{\Phi}_{({\Pi},U)}(f),

where
\big(i_{\mathsf{G}}(\gamma)(f)\big)(\gamma^{\prime}):=\alpha_{\gamma}\big(f\big(\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}\big)\big)
for each
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathsf{G}
and
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})
.

Example 2.1

(noncommutative two-tori)
.

The noncommutative torus is a fundamental example
of a noncommutative space in both physics and mathematics. Its
original incarnation
[
43
]
is a nice example of a crossed
product construction, which will play a fundamental role later on in this
paper. Let
\big(C({\mathbb{T}}),{\mathbb{Z}},\tau^{\theta}\big)
be the commutative
C^{*}
-dynamical system
where
\tau^{\theta}
is induced through pullback by rotations of the circle
{\mathbb{T}}
through
a fixed angle
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
:

\tau^{\theta}_{n}(\mathtt{f})(z)=\mathtt{f}\big({\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}n\theta}z\big),

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\mathtt{f}\in C({\mathbb{T}})
and
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
. The resulting crossed
product

{\sf A}_{\theta}:=C({\mathbb{T}})\rtimes_{\tau^{\theta}}{\mathbb{Z}}

is a called a
rotation
algebra
, and for irrational values of
\theta
it can be identified as a noncommutative two-torus

{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}^{2}
in the following way.

By definition, the algebra

{\sf A}_{\theta}
is the universal norm completion of the convolution
algebra
C_{\rm c}({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
, whose elements
f=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
can be regarded as
sequences (with only finitely many nonvanishing
terms) of functions
f_{n}\colon{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. The convolution product is
given by

(f\star_{\theta}g)_{n}(z):=\sum_{n^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{n^{\prime}}(z)g_{n-n^{\prime}}\big({\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}n^{\prime}\theta}z\big),

and the
*
-algebra structure is

f^{\dagger}_{n}(z):=\overline{f_{-n}\big({\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}n\theta}z\big)}.

Via the Fourier transformation

f(z,w):=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{n}(z)w^{n}

for
w\in{\mathbb{T}}
, we may regard the convolution algebra
C_{\rm c}({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
as a subspace of the space of functions
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
equipped
with the star-product

\displaystyle(f\star_{\theta}g)(z,w)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(f\star_{\theta}g)_{n}(z)w^{n}.

(2.2)

After a further Fourier transformation

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{m,n}z^{m}

and some simple redefinitions of the Fourier series involved, the
star-product (
2.2
) may be written in the form

(f\star_{\theta}g)(z,w)=\sum_{(m,n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\bigg(\sum_{(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}f_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}g_{m-m^{\prime},n-n^{\prime}}{\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}(m-m^{\prime})n^{\prime}\theta}\bigg)z^{m}w^{n}.

This recovers the usual commutative pointwise multiplication of functions in

C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
for
\theta=0
. For
\theta\neq 0
it realizes the irrational rotation algebra
{\sf A}_{\theta}
as a
deformation of the algebra of functions
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
on a
two-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
; it is equivalent to the usual strict
deformation quantization of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
whose star-product is a twisted
convolution product on
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
.

In the language of covariant representations of the dynamical system

(C({\mathbb{T}}),{\mathbb{Z}},\tau^{\theta})
, the crossed product
{\sf A}_{\theta}
is the
universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by two unitaries
U
and
V

satisfying the relation
[
52
, Proposition 2.56]

\displaystyle UV={\,\rm e}\,^{-2\pi{{\rm i}}\theta}VU.

(2.3)

A concrete representation
of
{\sf A}_{\theta}
on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}={\rm L}^{2}({\mathbb{T}})
is
given by defining

U(\mathtt{f})(z)=z\mathtt{f}(z)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad V(\mathtt{f})(z)=\mathtt{f}\big({\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}\theta}z\big).

Example 2.2

(
Noncommutative
{\bm{d}}
-tori
)
.

The natural higher-dimensional generalization of
Example
2.1
involves a skew-symmetric real
d{\times}d

matrix
\Theta=(\theta_{ij})
, see
[
45
]
. Then the noncommutative
d
-torus

{\sf A}_{\Theta}={\mathbb{T}}_{\Theta}^{d}
is the universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated
by
d
unitaries
U_{1},\dots,U_{d}
satisfying the relations

U_{i}U_{j}={\,\rm e}\,^{-2\pi{{\rm i}}\theta_{ij}}U_{j}U_{i}

for
i,j=1,\dots,d
. By
[
37
, Lemma 1.5]
, every
noncommutative torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Theta}^{d}
can be obtained as an iterated
crossed product by
{\mathbb{Z}}
in the following way. Let

\Theta_{|d-1}=(\theta_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq d-1}
, and let

U_{1},\dots,U_{d-1}
be the standard generators of

{\sf A}_{\Theta_{|d-1}}={\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}_{\Theta_{|d-1}}
. Define a
group homomorphism

\tau^{\vec{\theta}}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}\to\operatorname{Aut}({\sf A}_{\Theta_{|d-1}})
by

\tau_{n}^{\vec{\theta}}(U_{i})={\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}n\theta_{id}}U_{i},

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
\vec{\theta}:=(\theta_{id})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
.
Then there is an isomorphism of
C^{*}
-algebras

\displaystyle{\sf A}_{\Theta}\simeq{\sf A}_{\Theta_{|d-1}}\rtimes_{\tau^{\vec{\theta}}}{\mathbb{Z}}.

(2.4)

In the particular case where
\Theta_{|d-1}={\bm{0}}_{d-1}
, we denote the
corresponding noncommutative
d
-torus by

{\sf A}_{\vec{\theta}}={\mathbb{T}}_{\vec{\theta}}^{d}\,
, and (
2.4
)
shows that it can be obtained by a crossed product of the commutative
algebra of functions on a
d{-}1
-torus by an action of
{\mathbb{Z}}
:

{\sf A}_{\vec{\theta}}\simeq C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}\big)\rtimes_{\tau^{\vec{\theta}}}{\mathbb{Z}}.

2.2
Semi-direct products and group algebras

Most of our considerations later on will focus on spaces that can be
obtained from semi-direct products of groups. We will now explain the
relation between crossed products and semi-direct products which will
be useful for these examples.

There are two ways to think about the semi-direct product construction:

(1)

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a
group with two subgroups
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
such that
\mathsf{N}
is normal. If
\mathsf{N}\cap\mathsf{H}=\{e\}\subset\mathsf{G}
and every
element of
\mathsf{G}
can be written as a product of an element of

\mathsf{N}
with an element of
\mathsf{H}
, then we say that
\mathsf{G}
is a semi-direct
product of its subgroups
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
and we write
\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{N}\,\mathsf{H}
.

(2)

Let
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
be two groups together with a left action

\varphi\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
of
\mathsf{H}
on
\mathsf{N}
by automorphisms, which we denote by

\varphi_{h}(n)={}^{h}n
for
h\in\mathsf{H}
and
n\in\mathsf{N}
; in particular

{}^{h}(nn^{\prime})={}^{h}n\,{}^{h}n^{\prime}
. We write
{}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{N}
to
indicate that
\mathsf{H}
acts on
\mathsf{N}
from the left. The semi-direct product
of
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
is the group
\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}
defined to be the set

\mathsf{N}\times\mathsf{H}
with the product

(n,h)\,(n^{\prime},h^{\prime})=\big(n\,{}^{h}n^{\prime},hh^{\prime}\big).

The inverse is then
(n,h)^{-1}=\big({}^{h^{-1\!}}{n^{-1}},h^{-1}\big)
.

These two definitions are equivalent: Given subgroups
\mathsf{N},\mathsf{H}\subset\mathsf{G}
as
in point (1), it follows that
\mathsf{G}\simeq\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\rm Ad}\mathsf{H}

where
{\rm Ad}
is the adjoint action:
{\rm Ad}_{h}(n)=hnh^{-1}
. On the other
hand, every element of the group
\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}
defined in (2) can be written as
(n,h)=(n,e_{\mathsf{H}})(e_{\mathsf{N}},h)
and the subgroups
\mathsf{N}\times\{e_{\mathsf{H}}\}

and
\{e_{\mathsf{N}}\}\times\mathsf{H}
intersect only in the identity of
\mathsf{G}
.
If the action of
\mathsf{H}
on
\mathsf{N}
is trivial,
i.e.,
\varphi_{h}={\rm id}_{\mathsf{N}}
for all
h\in\mathsf{H}
, then the semi-direct
product reduces to the direct product
\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}=\mathsf{N}\times\mathsf{H}
.

Later on we will need to consider the interplay between semi-direct
products and quotient groups, which is provided by the simple

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}
be a semi-direct
product, and let
{\sf V}\subset\mathsf{N}
be a subgroup which is normal in

\mathsf{G}
. Then the quotient group
\mathsf{G}/{\sf V}
is the
semi-direct product
(\mathsf{N}/{\sf V})\rtimes_{\varphi^{\sf V}}\mathsf{H}
, where

{\varphi^{\sf V}}
is the action
\varphi
of
\mathsf{H}
induced on the
quotient group
\mathsf{N}/{\sf V}
.

If
\mathsf{N}
is a group, we write
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})
for the corresponding group

C^{*}
-algebra, i.e., for the crossed product
{\mathbb{C}}\rtimes\mathsf{N}
. If

\mathsf{N}
is finite, then
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})={\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]
is the linear space
freely generated over
{\mathbb{C}}
by the group elements, made into an algebra by
linearly extending the product from
\mathsf{N}
to
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]
; equivalently
it is the algebra of continuous functions
on
\mathsf{N}
with the convolution product.
Given a
left
\mathsf{H}
-action
\varphi\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
, there is an induced action

\varphi^{*}\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(C^{*}(\mathsf{N}))
via pullback. For
\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{N}
finite the
vector spaces
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\times\mathsf{H}
and
C^{*}(\mathsf{N}\times\mathsf{H})
are canonically
isomorphic, and it is straightforward
to show that the corresponding crossed product and semi-direct product are
related by

Proposition 2.4
.

If
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
are finite groups, then

C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\varphi^{*}}\mathsf{H}\simeq C^{*}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}).

Proof.

Note that
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\varphi^{*}}\mathsf{H}={\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]\rtimes_{\varphi^{*}}\mathsf{H}
is the
vector space of functions
f\colon\mathsf{H}\to{\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]
with convolution product

\big(f\star_{{\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]\rtimes_{\varphi^{*}}\mathsf{H}}f^{\prime}\big)(h)=\sum_{h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{H}}\,f(h^{\prime})\star_{{\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]}\varphi^{*}_{h^{\prime}}\big(f^{\prime}\big(h^{\prime-1}h\big)\big),

and using the convolution product in
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]
this can be
written as

\big(f\star_{{\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}]\rtimes_{\varphi^{*}}\mathsf{H}}f^{\prime}\big)(n,h)=\sum_{h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{H}}\sum_{n^{\prime}\in\mathsf{N}}f(n^{\prime},h^{\prime})f^{\prime}\big({}^{h^{\prime-1\!}}\big(n^{\prime-1}n\big),h^{\prime-1}h\big),

which is easily seen
to coincide with the convolution product in
C^{*}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})={\mathbb{C}}[\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}]
.
∎

A more general result holds if
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
are locally compact groups with
\varphi\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})

a continuous action of
\mathsf{H}
on
\mathsf{N}
via automorphisms
(i.e.,
(h,n)\mapsto\varphi_{h}(n)
is a continuous map from
\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{N}
to
\mathsf{N}
).
In this case the semi-direct product
\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}
is a locally compact
group (in the product topology on
\mathsf{N}\times\mathsf{H}
) with
\mathsf{N}
a closed normal subgroup and
\mathsf{H}
a closed
subgroup (see
[
52
, Proposition 3.11]
with

A={\mathbb{C}}
). The analogue of item (1) above also holds in the
context of locally compact groups if
\mathsf{G}
is
\sigma
-compact, and

\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
are closed subgroups of
\mathsf{G}
.

The action
\beta

defining the
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(C^{*}(\mathsf{N}),\mathsf{H},\beta)
and hence the crossed
product
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}
is the composition
of the pullback
\varphi^{*}
of the action
\varphi\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
with
the action
\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}\colon\mathsf{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(C^{*}(\mathsf{N}))
that enters the
definition of the Haar measure on
\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}
in terms of the Haar
measures on
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
: If
\mu_{\mathsf{N}}
is a (left invariant)
Haar measure on
\mathsf{N}
, then the integral

I_{h}(F)=\int_{\mathsf{N}}F\big({}^{h}n\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n)
for
F\in C^{*}(\mathsf{N})

is left invariant, i.e.,

I_{h}(\lambda_{n^{\prime}}F)=I_{h}(F)
where
(\lambda_{n^{\prime}}F)(n):=F\big(n^{\prime-1}n\big)

for all
n^{\prime}\in\mathsf{N}

(use invariance of the Haar measure under
n\to{}^{h^{-1\!}}n^{\prime}
).
Uniqueness of the Haar measure up to a positive constant then
implies there exists a function
\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}\colon\mathsf{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}(h)\int_{\mathsf{N}}F\big({}^{h}n\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n)=\int_{\mathsf{N}}F(n)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n).

(2.5)

It is straighforward to see that

\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}
is a group homomorphism and that it is
continuous
[
52
, Section 2]
. The Haar measure

\mu_{\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}}
on

\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}
is then given by

\int_{\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}}f(n,h)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}}(n,h):=\int_{\mathsf{H}}\int_{\mathsf{N}}\,f(n,h)\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}(h)^{-1}\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(h).

This is trivially invariant under the left
\mathsf{N}
-action, and
it is also invariant under the left
\mathsf{H}
-action
(n,h)\mapsto(1,h^{\prime})(n,h)=\big({}^{h^{\prime}\!}n,h^{\prime}h\big)
, using (
2.5
) with
F\big({}^{h^{\prime}\!}n\big):=f\big({}^{h^{\prime}\!}n,h^{\prime}h\big)

and recalling that
h
is fixed in (
2.5
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
be locally compact groups
and
\varphi\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
a continuous action of
\mathsf{H}
on
\mathsf{N}
. Define

\beta\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(C^{*}(\mathsf{N}))
by
(\beta_{h^{\prime}}\ell)(n)=\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}(h^{\prime})^{-1}\ell\big({}^{h^{\prime-1\!}}n\big)
for all
h^{\prime}\in\mathsf{H}
and

\ell\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})
. Then

C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}\simeq C^{*}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}).

For a full
proof of Theorem
2.5
that takes into account the topological
and
C^{*}
-algebraic aspects, see
[
52
, Proposition 3.11]
. Here
we shall just show that under the canonical injection
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})\hookrightarrow C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}
, given by
f(n,h)\mapsto f(h)
where

f(h)(n)=f(n,h)
, the convolution product in
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})
is mapped to the convolution product in
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}
.
Let
f,f^{\prime}\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})
, then

\displaystyle\big(f\star_{C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})}f^{\prime}\big)(n,h)=\int_{\mathsf{H}}\int_{\mathsf{N}}f(n^{\prime},h^{\prime})f^{\prime}\big((n^{\prime},h^{\prime})^{-1}(n,h)\big)\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}(h^{\prime})^{-1}\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n^{\prime})\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(h^{\prime}).

(2.6)

On the other hand, for the images of
f
,
f^{\prime}
in
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}
we have

\big(f\star_{C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}}f^{\prime}\big)(h)=\int_{\mathsf{H}}f(h^{\prime})\star_{C^{*}(\mathsf{N})}\beta_{h^{\prime}}\big(f^{\prime}\big(h^{\prime-1}h\big)\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(h^{\prime}).

Using the convolution product in
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})
this can be written as

\displaystyle\big(f\star_{C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}}f^{\prime}\big)(h)(n)=\int_{\mathsf{H}}\int_{\mathsf{N}}f(h^{\prime})(n^{\prime})\beta_{h^{\prime}}\big(f^{\prime}\big(h^{\prime-1}h\big)\big)\big(n^{\prime-1}n\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n^{\prime})\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(h^{\prime}),

which from the definition of the action
\beta
is easily seen to
equal the image
(f\star_{C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})}f^{\prime})(h)(n)
in
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}
of the product
(f\star_{C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})}f^{\prime})(n,h)
in
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H})
from (
2.6
).

More generally we have
[
52
, Proposition 3.11]

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H},\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical
system for the semi-direct product group
\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}
. Then
({\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha|_{\mathsf{N}}}\mathsf{N},\mathsf{H},\beta)
is a
C^{*}
-dynamical
system, where

\beta\colon\ \mathsf{H}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}({\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha|_{\mathsf{N}}}\mathsf{N}),\qquad h\longmapsto\beta_{h}

is defined by

\big(\beta_{h}(f)\big)(n)=\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}(h)^{-1}\alpha_{h}\big(f\big(^{h^{-1\!}}n\big)\big)

for all
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N},{\mathcal{A}})\subset{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha|_{\mathsf{N}}}\mathsf{N}
, with
\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}\colon\mathsf{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
defined by (
2.5
) and
{}^{h^{-1\!}}n=\varphi_{h^{-1}}(n)
.
Moreover, the canonical injection
C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H},{\mathcal{A}})\hookrightarrow C_{\rm c}\big(\mathsf{H},C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N},{\mathcal{A}})\big)

extends to a
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\big(\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathsf{H}\big)\simeq\big({\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha|_{\mathsf{N}}}\mathsf{N}\big)\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}.

Theorem
2.5
is then recovered by setting
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{C}}
.

In the spirit of Theorem
2.5
,
which shows that crossed products are a generalization of
semi-direct products, let us mention the semi-direct
product construction behind Theorem
2.6
. Consider three groups
\mathsf{M}
,
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
with group actions
{}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{N}
and

{}^{\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{M}
; then there are also group actions

{}^{\mathsf{N}}\mathsf{M}
and
{}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{M}
. The associativity
of the triple semi-direct product construction is then easily established
through

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathsf{M}
,
\mathsf{N}
and
\mathsf{H}
be groups with group actions
{}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{M}
,
{}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{N}
and

{}^{\mathsf{N}}\mathsf{M}
satisfying the compatibility conditions

{}^{h}\big({}^{n}m\big)={}^{({}^{h}n)}\big({}^{h}m\big)

for all
m\in\mathsf{M}
,
n\in\mathsf{N}
and
h\in\mathsf{H}
. Then there exists a group
action
{}^{\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{H}}\mathsf{M}
defined by
{}^{(n,h)}m={}^{n}\big({}^{h}m\big)
, and a
group action
{}^{\mathsf{H}}(\mathsf{M}\rtimes\mathsf{N})
defined by
{}^{h}(m,n)=\big({}^{h}m,{}^{h}n\big)
,
which together satisfy the associativity property

\mathsf{M}\rtimes(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{H})=(\mathsf{M}\rtimes\mathsf{N})\rtimes\mathsf{H}.

2.3
Pontryagin duality and Fourier transform

If
\mathsf{N}
is a locally compact
abelian
group we denote by
\widehat{\mathsf{N}}

its Pontryagin dual, i.e., the set of characters
\chi\colon\mathsf{N}\to\mathsf{U}(1)
,
which is also a locally compact abelian group (with the compact-open
topology and with the pointwise multiplication). For example, if

\mathsf{N}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
then
\widehat{\mathsf{N}}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the characters are given by

\chi_{p}(x)={\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}\langle p,x\rangle}
for
x\in\mathsf{N}
and

p\in\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
. The Pontryagin duality theorem states that there is a
canonical isomorphism
\widehat{\widehat{\mathsf{N}}}\simeq\mathsf{N}
, where

n\in\mathsf{N}
is associated to the character
\chi\mapsto\chi(n)
on

\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
.

The Fourier transform shows that the group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})
is
isomorphic to
C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big)
:
Given a Haar measure
\mu_{\mathsf{N}}
on

\mathsf{N}
, the Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}(f)
of
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})
is defined by

{\mathcal{F}}(f)(\chi):=\int_{{\mathsf{N}}}{f}(n)\chi(n)\,{\rm d}\mu_{{\mathsf{N}}}(n)

for
\chi\in\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
. It sends
the convolution product of functions in
C^{*}(\mathsf{N})

to the pointwise product of functions in
C(\widehat{\mathsf{N}})
:

{\mathcal{F}}({f}\star{f^{\prime}})={\mathcal{F}}({f}){\mathcal{F}}({f^{\prime}}),

and extends to an isomorphism
[
52
, Proposition 3.1]

{\cal{F}}\colon\ C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big),

(2.7)

where
C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big)
is the algebra of functions on
\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
vanishing at
infinity. For
\mathsf{N}
separable, Hausdorff and locally compact,

C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big)
is indeed a
C^{*}
-algebra.

Given a continuous left group action
\varphi\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
, which we
also denote as before by
\varphi_{h}(n)={}^{h}n
, consider the induced action

\beta\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}\big(C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\big)
as defined in Theorem
2.5
.
There is also an induced left action
\widehat{\varphi}\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big)
defined by pullback:

\big(\widehat{\varphi}_{h}\chi\big)(n):=\chi\big({}^{h^{-1}}n\big)
, together with its pullback

\widehat{\varphi}^{\,*}\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}\big(C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big)\big)
defined by

\big(\widehat{\varphi}^{\,*}_{h}\widehat{f}\,\big)(\chi)=\widehat{f}\big(\widehat{\varphi}_{h^{-1}}\chi\big)
for all
h\in\mathsf{H}
,
\widehat{f}\in C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big)
and
\chi\in\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
.
The Fourier transform isomorphism (
2.7
) then extends to the isomorphism

Proposition 2.8
.

If
\mathsf{N}
is a locally compact abelian group and

\varphi\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
is a continuous action of a locally
compact group
\mathsf{H}
on
\mathsf{N}
, then

\quad C^{*}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{H}\simeq C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big)\rtimes_{{\widehat{\varphi}}^{\,*}}\mathsf{H}.

Proof.

We show that the triples
(C^{*}(\mathsf{N}),\mathsf{H},\beta)
and
\big(C_{0}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{N}}\big),\mathsf{H},\widehat{\varphi}{}^{\,*}\big)
are equivalent dynamical systems,
see
[
52
, Example 3.16]
.
For this, we prove that the Fourier transform
(
2.7
) is
\mathsf{H}
-equivariant with respect to the
\mathsf{H}
-actions
\beta
and
\widehat{\varphi}^{\,*}
. For
h\in\mathsf{H}
,
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{N})
and

\chi\in\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
we compute

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\big(\beta_{h}(f)\big)(\chi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathsf{N}}\beta_{h}(f)(n)\chi(n)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n)=\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}(h)^{-1}\int_{\mathsf{N}}f\big({}^{h^{-1\!}}n\big)\chi(n)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\mathsf{H}}(h)^{-1}\int_{\mathsf{N}}f\big({}^{h^{-1\!}}n\big)\chi\big({\phantom{I^{I}\!\!\!\!\!}}^{h\!}\big({}^{h^{-1}\!}n\big)\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n)=\int_{\mathsf{N}}f(n)\chi\big({}^{h}n\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{N}}(n)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}(f)\big(\widehat{\varphi}_{h^{-1}}\chi\big)=\big(\widehat{\varphi}_{h}^{\,*}{\mathcal{F}}(f)\big)(\chi),

where in the fourth equality we used (
2.5
) with

F\big({}^{h^{-1}\!}n\big)=f\big({}^{h^{-1\!}}n\big)\chi\big({\phantom{I^{I}\!\!\!\!\!}}^{h\!}\big({}^{h^{-1}\!}n\big)\big)
.
∎

Replacing
\mathsf{N}
with
\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
in
Proposition
2.8
we also obtain the isomorphism

\displaystyle C^{*}\big({\widehat{\mathsf{N}}}\big)\rtimes_{\widehat{\beta}}\mathsf{H}\simeq C_{0}\big({\widehat{\widehat{\mathsf{N}}}}\big)\rtimes_{{\widehat{\widehat{\varphi}}}{}^{\,*}}\mathsf{H}\simeq C_{0}(\mathsf{N})\rtimes_{{\varphi}^{*}}\mathsf{H},

(2.8)

where
\widehat{\beta}\colon\mathsf{H}\to\operatorname{Aut}\big(C^{*}(\widehat{\mathsf{N}})\big)
is defined by

\widehat{\beta}_{h}(\widehat{f}\,)(\chi)=\widehat{\sigma}_{\mathsf{H}}(h)^{-1}\widehat{f}\big(\widehat{\varphi}_{h^{-1}\!}\chi\big)
for
h\in\mathsf{H}
,
\widehat{f}\in C_{\rm c}(\widehat{\mathsf{N}})
and
\chi\in\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
, with
\widehat{\sigma}_{\mathsf{H}}\colon\mathsf{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
defined as in (
2.5
) but using the dual group

\widehat{\mathsf{N}}
instead of
\mathsf{N}
, and in the final isomorphism we
used Pontryagin duality
\widehat{\widehat{\mathsf{N}}}\simeq\mathsf{N}
.

Another important property of crossed products is
Takai
duality

[
52
, Section 7.1]
. If
\mathsf{G}
is a locally compact abelian group and

({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
is a

-dynamical system, then

({\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G},\widehat{\mathsf{G}},\widehat{\alpha})
is a

\widehat{\alpha}\colon\ \widehat{\mathsf{G}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}({\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}),\qquad\chi\longmapsto\widehat{\alpha}_{\chi}

Theorem 2.9

(Takai duality)
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical system where
\mathsf{G}
is a

-algebras

2.4
Morita equivalence and Green’s theorem

Crossed products of algebras provide a host of examples of dualities
which come in the form of various levels of strong and weak
equivalences of algebras, see, e.g.,
[
6
]
. The most
primitive form of such dualities is provided by (strong) Morita
equivalence
[
41
]
. A bimodule for a pair of algebras
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
vector space
{\mathcal{M}}
which is simultaneously
a left
{\mathcal{A}}
-module and a right
{\mathcal{B}}
-module, where the left action
of
{\mathcal{A}}
commutes with the right action of
{\mathcal{B}}
:
(a\cdot\xi)\cdot b=a\cdot(\xi\cdot b)
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
\xi\in{\mathcal{M}}
. If
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are
C^{*}
-algebras,
we say that a bimodule
{\mathcal{M}}
is an
{\mathcal{A}}
–
{\mathcal{B}}

Morita
equivalence bimodule
(or
imprimitivity bimodule
) if it
is equipped with an
{\mathcal{A}}
-valued inner product

{}_{\mathcal{A}}\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
and a
{\mathcal{B}}
-valued inner
product
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}
satisfying the
associativity condition

{}_{\mathcal{A}}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\cdot\xi=\psi\cdot\langle\phi,\xi\rangle_{\mathcal{B}},

for all
\psi,\phi,\xi\in{\mathcal{M}}
, under which
{\mathcal{M}}
is complete in
the norm closures, and such that the ideal
{}_{\mathcal{A}}\langle{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{M}}\rangle
is dense in
{\mathcal{A}}
and
\langle{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{M}}\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}
is dense in
{\mathcal{B}}
. The bimodule
{\mathcal{M}}
establishes a

Morita equivalence
between the algebras
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
, and
in this case we write
{\mathcal{A}}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ {\mathcal{B}}
.

Morita equivalent
C^{*}
-algebras have equivalent categories of
nondegenerate
*
-representations: If
{\Pi}_{\mathcal{B}}\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\rm B}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{B}})
is a
representation of
{\mathcal{B}}
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{B}}
, then we can
construct another Hilbert space

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{A}}:={\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{B}}

which is the quotient of the tensor product
{\mathcal{M}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{B}}
by the relation
(\xi\cdot b)\otimes\psi-\xi\otimes{\Pi}_{\mathcal{B}}(b)\psi=0
identifying the

{\mathcal{B}}
-actions for

\xi\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{B}}
. The inner product
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{A}}
is given by

\big\langle\xi\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\psi\big|\xi^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\psi^{\prime}\big\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{A}}}:=\big\langle\psi\big|{\Pi}_{\mathcal{B}}\big(\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}\big)\psi^{\prime}\big\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{B}}},

and a representation
{\Pi}_{\mathcal{A}}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm B}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{A}})
of the algebra

{\mathcal{A}}
is defined by

{\Pi}_{\mathcal{A}}(a)(\xi\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\psi)=(a\cdot\xi)\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\psi

for
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
\xi\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\psi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{A}}
; this
representation is unitary equivalent to the representation

{\Pi}_{\mathcal{B}}
. Conversely, starting with a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
, we
can use a conjugate
{\mathcal{B}}
–
{\mathcal{A}}
equivalence bimodule

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
to construct a unitary equivalent representation of

{\mathcal{B}}
; then there are surjective bimodule homomorphisms

{\mathcal{M}}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
and

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
which satisfy a certain
transitivity law. As a particular consequence of this equivalence, Morita
equivalent algebras have homeomorphic spectra and isomorphic K-theory groups.

Example 2.10

(noncommutative two-tori)
.

A famous example of Morita equivalence in both mathematics and string
theory is provided by the
noncommutative tori
{\sf A}_{\theta}={\mathbb{T}}_{\theta}^{2}
from
Example
2.1
. Firstly, notice from (
2.3
) that
changing the coset representative

yields an identical
algebra:
{\sf A}_{\theta+m}={\sf A}_{\theta}
for all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Secondly, there
is an obvious

C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

obtained by
interchanging the two generators
U
and
V
. The converse
is also true
[
43
,
44
]

{\sf A}_{\theta^{\prime}}\simeq{\sf A}_{\theta}
if and only if
\theta^{\prime}=\theta\ {\rm mod}~1
. More generally, two irrational rotation

-algebras

and
{\sf A}_{\theta^{\prime}}
are
Morita equivalent if and only if
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
lie in the
same orbit under the action of
\mathsf{GL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
by fractional linear
transformations

\theta^{\prime}={\tt M}[\theta]:=\frac{a\theta+b}{c\theta+d}\qquad\mbox{for}\quad{\tt M}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\bigg)\in\mathsf{GL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}}).

The explicit Morita equivalence bimodules can be found
in
[
43
]
. On the other hand, the rational rotation
algebras

are all Morita equivalent to the commutative
algebra
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
of functions on the
two-torus
[
44
]
.

Example 2.11

(noncommutative
d
-tori)
.

The Morita equivalences of Example
2.10
generalize to
the higher-dimensional noncommutative tori
{\sf A}_{\Theta}={\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{\Theta}

from Example
2.2
in the following
way
[
46
]
. First of all, the algebra
{\sf A}_{\Theta}
is
unchanged if the matrix
\Theta
is written in another basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
: if
B\in\mathsf{GL}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
with transpose
B^{\rm t}
, then there is
a
C^{*}
-algebra isomorphism

{\sf A}_{B^{\rm t}\,\Theta\,B}\simeq{\sf A}_{\Theta}
. More generally, consider the set of real
skew-symmetric
d{\times}d
matrices
\Theta
whose orbits

{\bm{M}}[\Theta]
are defined for all
{\bm{M}}\in{\sf SO}(d,d;{\mathbb{Z}})
, where

{\bm{M}}[\Theta]=(A\,\Theta+B)\,(C\,\Theta+D)^{-1}\qquad\mbox{for}\quad{\bm{M}}=\left(\begin{matrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{matrix}\right)\in{\sf SO}(d,d;{\mathbb{Z}}),

and
A
,
B
,
C
and
D
are
d{\times}d
block matrices satisfying

A^{\rm t}C+C^{\rm t}A=0=B^{\rm t}D+D^{\rm t}B\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad A^{\rm t}D+C^{\rm t}B=\mathbbm{1}_{d}.

The set of all such matrices is dense in the space of all
skew-symmetric real
d{\times}d
matrices, and there is a Morita
equivalence

{\sf A}_{{\bm{M}}[\Theta]}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ {\sf A}_{\Theta}.

However, for
d>2

are algebras
{\sf A}_{\Theta}
and
{\sf A}_{\Theta^{\prime}}
that are isomorphic (and
so Morita equivalent) but for which the matrices
\Theta
and

\Theta^{\prime}
do not belong to the same
{\sf SO}(d,d;{\mathbb{Z}})

orbit
[
46
]
.

We will also need an equivariant version of Morita equivalence in
order to show that Morita equivalent algebras induce Morita
equivalent crossed products according
to
[
14
, Section 5.4]

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
and
({\mathcal{B}},\mathsf{G},\beta)
be

C^{*}
-dynamical systems such that

{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are Morita equivalent. Then the crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras

{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
and
{\mathcal{B}}\rtimes_{\beta}\mathsf{G}
are Morita equivalent if
there exists a
\mathsf{G}
-equivariant
{\mathcal{A}}
–
{\mathcal{B}}
Morita equivalence bimodule
{\mathcal{M}}
,
i.e., if there is a strongly continuous action
U\colon\mathsf{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}({\mathcal{M}})
of
\mathsf{G}
on an

{\mathcal{A}}
–
{\mathcal{B}}
Morita equivalence bimodule
{\mathcal{M}}
such that

U_{\gamma}(a\cdot\xi)=\alpha_{\gamma}(a)\cdot U_{\gamma}(\xi)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad U_{\gamma}(\xi\cdot b)=U_{\gamma}(\xi)\cdot\beta_{\gamma}(b),

and

{}_{{\mathcal{A}}}\langle U_{\gamma}(\xi),U_{\gamma}(\xi^{\prime})\rangle=\alpha_{\gamma}\big({}_{\mathcal{A}}\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle\big)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\langle U_{\gamma}(\xi),U_{\gamma}(\xi^{\prime})\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}=\beta_{\gamma}\big(\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}\big),

for
all
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
,
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
.

In this paper, our main application of Morita equivalence will involve
Green’s symmetric imprimitivity theorem. Let
X
be a locally compact space, and let

\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{K}
be locally compact groups with commuting free and
proper actions on the right and on the left on
X
, respectively. We can lift these
actions to left actions on
C_{0}(X)
by defining

({}^{h}\mathtt{f})(x)=\mathtt{f}\big(h^{-1}\cdot x\big)
and
\big({}^{k}\mathtt{f}\big)(x)=\mathtt{f}(x\cdot k)

for all
\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X)
,
x\in X
,
h\in\mathsf{H}
and
k\in\mathsf{K}
. Commutativity
of the actions of
\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{K}
implies that there are well-defined
induced actions of
\mathsf{H}
and
\mathsf{K}

respectively on the quotient spaces
\mathsf{K}{\setminus}X
and
X/\mathsf{H}
, and hence
respectively on the algebras
C_{0}(\mathsf{K}{\setminus}X)
and

C_{0}(X/\mathsf{H})
which we denote
{\rm rt}
and
{\rm lt}
. Green’s symmetric imprimitivity theorem then reads as
[
52
, Corollary 4.10]

Theorem 2.13
.

There is a Morita
equivalence of
C^{*}
-algebras

\displaystyle C_{0}(\mathsf{K}{\setminus}X)\rtimes_{\rm rt}\mathsf{H}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\rm\tiny M}}\ C_{0}(X/\mathsf{H})\rtimes_{\rm lt}\mathsf{K}

(2.9)

implemented by the Morita equivalence
(
or imprimitivity
)

bimodule
{\mathcal{M}}
which is the completion of
C_{\rm c}(X)
with the
actions

\displaystyle(a\cdot\xi)(x)=\int_{\mathsf{K}}\,a(k,x\cdot\mathsf{H})\xi\big(k^{-1}\cdot x\big)\Delta_{\mathsf{K}}(k)^{1/2}\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{K}}(k),

\displaystyle(\xi\cdot b)(x)=\int_{\mathsf{H}}\xi\big(x\cdot h^{-1}\big)b\big(h,\mathsf{K}\cdot x\cdot h^{-1}\big)\Delta_{\mathsf{H}}(h)^{-1/2}\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(h),

for all
x\in X
,
a\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{K}\times X/\mathsf{H})
,
b\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{H}\times\mathsf{K}{\setminus}X)
and
\xi\in C_{\rm c}(X)
, and the inner products

\displaystyle{}_{\mathcal{A}}\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle(k,x\cdot\mathsf{H})=\Delta_{\mathsf{K}}(k)^{-1/2}\int_{\mathsf{H}}\xi(x\cdot h)\overline{\xi^{\prime}\big(k^{-1}\cdot x\cdot h\big)}\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{H}}(h),

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}(h,\mathsf{K}\cdot x)=\Delta_{\mathsf{H}}(h)^{-1/2}\int_{\mathsf{K}}\overline{\xi\big(k^{-1}\cdot x\big)}\xi^{\prime}\big(k^{-1}\cdot x\cdot h\big)\,{\rm d}\mu_{\mathsf{K}}(k),

for all
x\in X
,
h\in\mathsf{H}
,
k\in\mathsf{K}
and
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in C_{\rm c}(X)
.

Theorem
2.13
has several useful applications and
corollaries, see, e.g.,
[
42
]
. A particularly relevant
special case that we shall use below is when
\mathsf{K}
is the trivial group,
in which case (
2.9
) reduces to the Morita equivalence

\displaystyle C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\rm rt}\mathsf{H}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ C_{0}(X/\mathsf{H}),

illustrating the use of crossed products in describing
quotients. This equivalence can in fact be strengthened to a stable
isomorphism
[
42
]

\displaystyle C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\rm rt}\mathsf{H}\simeq C_{0}(X/\mathsf{H})\otimes\mathcal{K}\big({\rm L}^{2}(\mathsf{H})\big),

(2.10)

where
\mathcal{K}
denotes the algebra of
compact operators.

Example 2.14

(tori)
.

A particularly relevant example for us is the case
X={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with

\mathsf{H}={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
acting by translations
(n,x)\mapsto x+n
for

n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, which realizes the

d
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
as a crossed product:

C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\big)\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ C_{0}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

Let us illustrate the construction explicitly. The convolution algebra

C_{\rm c}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)\!\subset\!C_{0}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
can be
identified with the space of sequences
f=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
of
functions
f_{n}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
with the convolution product

(f\star g)_{n}(x)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}f_{m}(x)g_{n-m}(x-m).

Consider the algebra

{\mathcal{A}}:=\big\{f\in C_{0}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathcal{K}\big(\ell^{2}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\big)\big)\big)\,\big|\,f(x+m)=U_{m}f(x)U_{m}^{-1}\big\},

where
U_{m}
is the unitary shift operator on
\ell^{2}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\big)
defined by

(U_{m}a)_{n}=a_{n-m}
for each
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
.
Define a map
{\Phi}\colon C_{\rm c}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)\to C_{\rm c}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\mathcal{K}\big(\ell^{2}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\big)\big)\big)
by

\big({\Phi}(f)(x)\big)_{mn}=f_{m+n}(x+n).

It is easy to see that
{\Phi}(f)(x+m)=U_{m}{\Phi}(f)(x)U_{m}^{-1}
for all
f\in C_{\rm c}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)
, and if
a=(a_{mn})\in{\mathcal{A}}
then defining

f_{n}:=a_{0n}
gives
{\Phi}(f)=a
. It is also easy to check that

{\Phi}(f\star g)={\Phi}(f){\Phi}(g)
, and consequently

\Phi
gives an algebra isomorphism

{\Phi}\colon\ C_{0}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }{\mathcal{A}}.

The explicit Morita equivalence bimodule is now obtained from the
completion of

{\mathcal{M}}=\big\{\xi\in C_{\rm c}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ell^{2}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\big)\big)\,\big|\,\xi(x+m)=U_{m}\xi(x)\big\}.

The left action of the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}={\Phi}\big(C_{\rm c}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)\big)
is by left
matrix multiplication on
{\mathcal{M}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{M}},

\displaystyle(a,\xi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto a\cdot\xi,\qquad(a\cdot\xi)_{n}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a_{nm}\xi_{m},

while the right action of the algebra
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\big)
is by right pointwise
multiplication on
{\mathcal{M}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}\times C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\big)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{M}},

\displaystyle(\xi,b)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\xi\cdot b,\qquad(\xi\cdot b)_{n}=\xi_{n}b.

The left and right inner
products are respectively given by

\displaystyle{}_{\mathcal{A}}\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\xi\otimes\eta^{*},

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{C({\mathbb{T}}^{d})}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\overline{\xi_{n}}\eta_{n},

for
\xi,\eta\in{\mathcal{M}}
.
Together with the isomorphism
\Phi
, this establishes a
Morita equivalence between the algebras
C_{0}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

and
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\big)
.

and

acting by left multiplication on the

\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H}

,

and the group

This equivalence also follows from the

-algebra isomorphism
[
52
, Theorem 4.29]

C_{0}(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H})\rtimes_{\rm lt}\mathsf{G}\simeq C^{*}(\mathsf{H})\otimes\mathcal{K}\big({\rm L}^{2}(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{H})\big).

3
Topological T-duality and twisted tori

In this section we shall apply the results of Section
2
, and in particular
Green’s theorem, to present a scheme
that will be employed in our study of topological T-duality. We shall
then illustrate how our scheme works to reproduce some standard
(commutative) examples of T-dual spaces.

3.1
Twisted tori and their T-duals

We are interested in formulating a notion of T-duality for
“torus bundles without
H
-flux”, which for our purposes can be
characterised by the following general class of spaces.

Definition 3.1

(twisted tori)
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a locally compact group which admits a
cocompact discrete subgroup
\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
, i.e., a lattice in
\mathsf{G}
, which we let act on
\mathsf{G}
by left
multiplication. The quotient space

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}:=\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\backslash\mathsf{G}

is a
twisted torus
.

By
[
34
, Lemma 6.2]
, only unimodular groups can contain lattices, i.e., groups
\mathsf{G}
whose
modular function
\Delta_{\mathsf{G}}
is identically equal to
1
.

Example 3.2

(tori)
.

Every lattice in the abelian Lie group
\mathsf{G}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
isomorphic to
\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, acting by translations. Then

{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\backslash{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional torus.

Example 3.3

(nilmanifolds)
.

Generalizing Example
3.2
, let
\mathsf{G}
be a connected and simply-connected nilpotent Lie group. Then a theorem of
Malcev
[
40
, Theorem 2.12]
establishes the existence
of a lattice
\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
in
\mathsf{G}
if and only if

\mathsf{G}
can be defined over the rationals,
i.e., there exists a basis for its Lie algebra which has rational
structure constants, and in this case
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}=\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\backslash\mathsf{G}
is a nilmanifold.

Example 3.4

(orbifolds)
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a compact Lie group. Then the lattices in
\mathsf{G}
are
precisely the finite subgroups
\Gamma
of
\mathsf{G}
, and

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash\mathsf{G}
is a smooth orbifold. For instance,
for
\mathsf{G}={\sf SU}(2)
the twisted tori are precisely the three-dimensional
ADE orbifolds
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash\Sphere^{3}

of the three-sphere for a finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset{\sf SU}(2)
;
for
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
a cyclic subgroup of order
n\geq 2
, this recovers
the familiar lens spaces
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\backslash\Sphere^{3}=:\mathbb{L}(n,1)
.

Following
[
32
]
, we come now to a central concept of this paper.

Definition 3.5

(topological T-duality)
.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
be a twisted torus which admits a
non-trivial right action
of the abelian Lie group
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
. The crossed product

C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is a
C^{*}
-algebraic T-dual
of the twisted torus.

If the spectrum of the crossed product algebra
C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a Hausdorff topological space
X
(for instance
if it is Morita
equivalent to a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra
C(X)
), then we say that
X
is T-dual to the
twisted torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
and call
X
a ‘classical T-dual’; otherwise we say that the T-dual of

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
is a noncommutative space.

For Definition
3.5
to be a ‘good’ notion of T-duality, we should first explain

(a)

in
what precise sense
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
and

C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are ‘equivalent’, and

(b)

how
T-duality applied twice returns the original twisted torus.

The answers to both of these points turns out to be provided by
working in a suitable category tailored to our treatment of
topological T-duality.

3.2
T-duality in the category
\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}

The terminology ‘topological T-duality’ refers to a coarse equivalence at
the level of topology; for
C^{*}
-algebras the topology is measured by
K-theory. A more powerful refinement is provided by Kasparov’s bivariant
K-theory which constructs groups
{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
for any pair
of separable
C^{*}
-algebras
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
; when
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{C}}
,
the group
{\rm KK}({\mathbb{C}},{\mathcal{B}})\simeq{\rm K}({\mathcal{B}})
is the K-theory group of
{\mathcal{B}}
. The cycles in
Kasparov’s groups
{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
, called
Kasparaov

{\mathcal{A}}
–
{\mathcal{B}}
bimodules
, are triples
({\mathcal{H}},\phi,T)
where
{\mathcal{H}}

is a right Hilbert
{\mathcal{B}}
-module,
\phi
is a
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{A}}

on
{\mathcal{H}}
, and
T\in{\rm End}_{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is a
{\mathcal{B}}
-linear operator on

{\mathcal{H}}
, subject to certain compactness conditions; we do not
provide further details of the definition here and instead refer to
[
6
]
for a
concise review of KK-theory in the context that we shall use it
in this paper. Kasparov bimodules may be thought of as generalizations
of morphisms between
C^{*}
-algebras, in the sense that any algebra homomorphism
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}

determines a class
[\phi]\in{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
, represented by the

{\mathcal{A}}
–
{\mathcal{B}}
-bimodule
({\mathcal{B}},\phi,0)
.

A key feature of Kasparov’s KK-theory is the composition product

\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\colon\ {\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})\times{\rm KK}({\mathcal{B}},\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{C}),

which is bilinear and associative. This product is compatible with the
composition of morphisms
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
and
\psi\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathcal{C}

of
C^{*}
-algebras:
[\phi]\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}[\psi]=[\psi\circ\phi]
. It
also makes
{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{A}})
into a ring with unit

1_{\mathcal{A}}=[{\rm id}_{\mathcal{A}}]
. We say that an element
\alpha\in{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
is
invertible
if there exists an element

\beta\in{\rm KK}({\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}})
such that

\alpha\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\beta=1_{\mathcal{A}}
and

\beta\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\alpha=1_{\mathcal{B}}
.

An important special instance of Kasparov
bimodules comes from Morita equivalence: Any Morita equivalence

{\mathcal{A}}
–
{\mathcal{B}}
bimodule
{\mathcal{M}}
is also a Kasparov bimodule

({\mathcal{M}},\phi,0)
, with
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm End}({\mathcal{M}})
the left action of

{\mathcal{A}}
, which defines an invertible class
[{\mathcal{M}}]\in{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
with inverse
[\,\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}\,]\in{\rm KK}({\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}})
given by the conjugate
{\mathcal{B}}
–
{\mathcal{A}}
bimodule

\overline{{\mathcal{M}}}
. Generally, if there exists an invertible element

\alpha\in{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
, then the algebras
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}

are said to be
KK-equivalent
, and we write
{\mathcal{A}}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny{KK}}}\ {\mathcal{B}}
. Thus Morita equivalence implies
KK-equivalence, but the converse is not generally true. KK-equivalent algebras have isomorphic
K-theory groups, but not necessarily homeomorphic spectra.

This refinement naturally
suggests an approach to T-duality where the category of separable

C^{*}
-algebras with
*
-homomorphisms is replaced with an additive
category
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}}
, whose objects are again separable
C^{*}
-algebras
but whose morphisms between any two objects
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are
given by the classes in
{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
(see,
e.g.,
[
7
]
). The composition product defines the
composition law, and isomorphic algebras
in this category are precisely the KK-equivalent
algebras; in particular, Morita equivalent algebras are isomorphic
as objects in
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}}
. Our formulation and computations of topological
T-duality will always take place in this category, and in this
setting we can easily provide answers to points (a) and (b) below Definition
3.5

through

Theorem 3.6
.

If
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
is a twisted torus with a
non-trivial right action
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then there are
isomorphisms in the category
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}}
given by the equivalences

(a)

C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny{KK}}}\ C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(
up to a shift of degree

n\ {\rm mod}~2)
, and

(b)

\big(C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\big)\rtimes_{\widehat{\rm rt}}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})
.

Proof.

The KK-equivalence (a) follows from the Connes–Thom isomorphism,
formulated in the language of KK-theory
[
17
]
. The Morita
equivalence (b) follows from Takai duality
(Theorem
2.9
).
∎

Another virtue of the categorical setting is that it enables a
general algebraic reformulation of the correspondence space
construction, which for topological spaces ‘geometrizes’ the action of
topological T-duality. In
[
7

it is proven that, if

{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are separable
C^{*}
-algebras, then any class in

correspondence’. For this, we first recall,
following
[
6

7

definition of Gysin or “wrong way” homomorphisms on K-theory for

C^{*}
-algebras. If

is a
morphism of separable
C^{*}
-algebras, a

is a
functorial assignment of a corresponding element
\phi!\in{\rm KK}({\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}})
. If a K-orientation exists, we say that
\phi
is

and call
\phi!
the associated

\phi_{!}:=(-)\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\phi!\colon{\rm K}({\mathcal{B}})\to{\rm K}({\mathcal{A}})

the definition from
[

to the present context of Theorem
3.6

The crossed product

3.4
Topological T-duality for the torus

Let us now describe how our considerations reproduce the standard

The simplest example of the T-duality scheme (

\mathsf{G}={\mathbb{R}}

Topological T-duality for almost abelian solvmanifolds

A large class of twisted tori of interest as string compactifications

4.1

Let
\mathsf{G}

. Notice also that

\mathsf{G}/{\sf V}={\sf V}\backslash\mathsf{G}
and
\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}/{\sf V}^{\mathbb{Z}}={\sf V}^{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
(because
{\sf V}^{\mathbb{Z}}=\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\cap{\sf V}
is normal in

\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
), and moreover

\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}^{\text{\tiny${\sf V}$}}}\backslash\mathsf{G}^{\text{\tiny${\sf V}$}}=\big({\sf V}^{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\big)\big\backslash({\sf V}\backslash\mathsf{G})=\big(\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}/{\sf V}^{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\big\backslash(\mathsf{G}/{\sf V})
.

4.2
Almost abelian solvmanifolds

In contrast to the case of nilpotent groups, there is no
simple criterion for the existence of a lattice in a general
connected and simply-connected solvable Lie group, as is required to define a corresponding twisted
torus. To formulate such a criterion, we specialise to
almost
abelian solvable groups
: these are the solvable Lie groups
\mathsf{G}
of
dimension
d

whose nilradical
\mathsf{N}
has codimension one and is abelian:

\mathsf{N}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}.

Then
\mathsf{G}
has the
structure of a semi-direct product

\mathsf{G}=\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathbb{R}}

for a continuous one-parameter left group action

\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
; concretely,
\varphi
is given by the
adjoint action of the one-dimensional subgroup
\mathsf{H}={\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathsf{N}

in the group
\mathsf{G}
(cf. Section
2.2
). This exhibits

\mathsf{G}
as a nontrivial group extension

1\longrightarrow\mathsf{N}\longrightarrow\mathsf{G}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow 1.

We can regard the one-parameter group action
\varphi
as a matrix

\varphi_{x}\in\mathsf{GL}(d-1,{\mathbb{R}})
for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
acting on the
vector space
\mathsf{N}
, which we identify with
{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
via a choice
of basis
\vec{e}_{1},\ldots,\vec{e}_{d-1}
. Since

\varphi_{0}=\mathbbm{1}_{d-1}
and
\varphi_{x}
is always non-singular, it
follows from the continuity of
\varphi
and the determinant that

\det\varphi_{x}>0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\mathsf{G}
admits a lattice

\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
if and
only if there exists
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
such that
\varphi_{x_{0}}
is
conjugate to an integer matrix
\mathtt{M}\in\mathsf{SL}(d-1,{\mathbb{Z}})
:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-1}\varphi_{x_{0}}\Sigma=\mathtt{M}

(4.2)

for some

\Sigma\in\mathsf{GL}(d-1,{\mathbb{R}})
.
In this case the twisted torus

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}=\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\backslash\mathsf{G}
is called an
almost abelian solvmanifold
.
The condition (
4.2
) strongly restricts the homomorphisms

\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{N})
; in particular, it requires that the
characteristic polynomial of
\varphi_{x_{0}}
has integer
coefficients. In this case the lattice (in the standard basis
\vec{e}_{1},\ldots,\vec{e}_{d-1}
) is given by

\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}=\Sigma\cdot{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}\rtimes_{\varphi|_{x_{0}\,{\mathbb{Z}}}}x_{0}{\mathbb{Z}},

which correspondingly sits as a nontrivial group extension

1\longrightarrow\Sigma\cdot{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\longrightarrow x_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow 1.

Then
\Lambda_{\mathsf{N}}\setminus\mathsf{N}\simeq{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}
, and the corresponding Mostow bundle realises the twisted torus

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
as a torus bundle over a circle

\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}\mathsf{N}\backslash\mathsf{G}\simeq{\mathbb{T}}
, whose monodromy
is specified by the matrix
\mathtt{M}
in the mapping class group
\mathsf{SL}(d-1,{\mathbb{Z}})
of
orientation-preserving
automorphisms up to homotopy of the torus fibres
{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}
.

For an almost abelian solvmanifold we can make the twisted torus construction more concrete
by choosing the global
coordinates
(z,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
on the group
manifold (associated with the basis
\vec{e}_{1},\ldots,\vec{e}_{d-1}
).
The group multiplication of the semi-direct product

\mathsf{G}={\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathbb{R}}
is

\displaystyle(z,x)(z^{\prime},x^{\prime})=(z+\varphi_{x}\cdot z^{\prime},x+x^{\prime}),

(4.3)

where we used
\varphi_{x}\varphi_{x^{\prime}}=\varphi_{x+x^{\prime}}
, and the
inverse of a group element is

(z,x)^{-1}=(-\varphi_{-x}\cdot z,-x),

where we used
\varphi_{x}^{-1}=\varphi_{-x}
.
The twisted torus is defined as the quotient
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}=\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}{\setminus}\mathsf{G}
which is generated by the equivalence relation
(z,x)\sim(\Sigma\cdot\gamma,x_{0}\,\alpha)\,(z,x)
for all
(\gamma,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
. The global structure
of
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
is generated by the simultaneous local coordinate
identifications under the action of the elements

(\Sigma\cdot\gamma,x_{0}\alpha)
of
\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
given by

\displaystyle(z,x)\longmapsto(z+\Sigma\cdot\gamma,x),

\displaystyle(z,x)\longmapsto(\Sigma{\tt M}^{\alpha}\,\Sigma^{-1}\cdot z,x+x_{0}\,\alpha).

(4.4)

These identifications explicitly exhibit the twisted torus as a torus bundle over a circle, with
local fiber coordinates
z\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}
and base coordinate
x\in{\mathbb{T}}
,
whose monodromy is specified by the matrix
{\tt M}\in\mathsf{SL}(d-1,{\mathbb{Z}})
, and whose periods are given respectively by

\Sigma\in\mathsf{GL}(d-1,{\mathbb{R}})
and
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
.

4.3
C^{*}
-algebra bundles

Mostow bundles and their
C^{*}
-algebraic T-duals can be grouped together
under the general heading of ‘
C^{*}
-algebra bundles’, which encompasses
the notions of
C_{0}(X)
-algebras and
C^{*}
-bundles, as we now
explain; see Section 8.1 and Appendix C of
[
52
]
for
further details.
Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space. There are two equivalent
notions for the
C^{*}
-algebra analogue of a fibre bundle over
X
.

A
C_{0}(X)
-algebra
is a
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}

equipped with a nondegenerate injection
\iota
of
C_{0}(X)

into the centre of its multiplier algebra, called the
structure
map
. For
\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X)
and

a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we abbreviate
\iota(\mathtt{f})a
by
\mathtt{f}\cdot a
. This endows

{\mathcal{A}}
with a
C_{0}(X)
-bimodule
structure.

Given a family
{\mathcal{B}}=({\mathcal{B}}_{x})_{x\in X}
of
C^{*}
-algebras, a

section
of
{\mathcal{B}}
is
a map
s\colon X\to{\mathcal{B}}
such that
s(x)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
for all
x\in X
; we
denote the space of sections of
{\mathcal{B}}
which vanish at infinity by
\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{B}})
. The family
{\mathcal{B}}
is then called a
C^{*}
-bundle
over
X
with fibres
{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
if the following
conditions are satisfied:

•

\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{B}})
is a
C^{*}
-algebra under pointwise operations and the
supremum norm;

•

{\mathcal{B}}_{x}=\{s(x)\,|\,s\in\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{B}})\}
for each

x\in X
;

•

\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{B}})
is closed under multiplication by

C_{0}(X)
; and

•

For each
s\in\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{B}})
, the function
x\mapsto\|s(x)\|

is upper semi-continuous, i.e., the set

\{x\in X\,|\,\|s(x)\|<\epsilon\}
is open in
X
for all
\epsilon>0
.

In this
paper we will only be concerned with
C^{*}
-bundles that have non-zero fibres.

If
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
C^{*}
-bundle over
X
, then its section algebra

\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{B}})
is a
C_{0}(X)
-algebra: its structure map
\iota
from

C_{0}(X)
is defined by
\iota(\mathtt{f})s=\mathtt{f}s
. Conversely, if
{\mathcal{A}}
is a

C_{0}(X)
-algebra, then the fibre
{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
over
x\in X
is
{\mathcal{A}}_{x}:={\mathcal{A}}/\mathcal{I}_{x}
, where

\mathcal{I}_{x}=\{\mathtt{f}\cdot a\,|\,\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X),\,\mathtt{f}(x)=0,\,a\in{\mathcal{A}}\}
is identified as the ideal in

{\mathcal{A}}
of sections vanishing at
x
. If

a\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we write
a(x)=a+\mathcal{I}_{x}
for its image in
{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
. The
function
x\mapsto\|a(x)\|
is upper semi-continuous
and vanishes at infinity with

\|a\|=\sup_{x\in X}\big\|a(x)\big\|

for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. The
elements
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
can in this way be viewed as sections of a

C^{*}
-bundle
({\mathcal{A}}_{x})_{x\in X}
. We will sometimes use the
notation
\coprod_{x\in X}{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
for
{\mathcal{A}}
when we wish to emphasise
its structure as a
C^{*}
-bundle over
X
with fibre
C^{*}
-algebras

{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
. These definitions do not require
local triviality of the bundle nor the fibres of the bundle to be
isomorphic to one another.
C^{*}
-algebra bundles over
X
are objects of a
category whose morphisms are
fibrewise

*
-homomorphisms,
i.e.,
C_{0}(X)
-linear morphisms
\psi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
:
\psi(\mathtt{f}\cdot a)=\mathtt{f}\cdot\psi(a)
for all
\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X)
and
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
; then
\psi

induces
*
-homomorphisms
\psi_{x}\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{x}\to{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
such that

\psi_{x}\big(a(x)\big)=\psi(a)(x)
for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Example 4.5

(trivial
C^{*}
-algebra bundles)
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is any
C^{*}
-algebra, then
{\mathcal{A}}=C_{0}(X,\mathcal{D})\simeq C_{0}(X)\otimes\mathcal{D}
is naturally a
C_{0}(X)
-algebra with structure map

\big(\iota(\mathtt{f})a\big)(x):=\mathtt{f}(x)\,a(x)

for
\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X)
,
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
x\in X
. In this case each fibre

{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
is canonically identified with
\mathcal{D}
and elements of
{\mathcal{A}}

are obviously identified with sections.

Example 4.6

(continuous maps)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be locally compact spaces and
\sigma\colon Y\to X
a continuous surjective map. Then
C_{0}(Y)
is a
C_{0}(X)
-algebra with structure map

\iota(\mathtt{f})\mathtt{g}:=(\mathtt{f}\circ\sigma)\,\mathtt{g}
, for
\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X)
and
\mathtt{g}\in C_{0}(Y)
,
and fibers
C_{0}(Y)_{x}\simeq C_{0}\big(\sigma^{-1}(x)\big)
.

In this paper we are particularly interested in crossed
products of
C^{*}
-algebra bundles. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a
C_{0}(X)
-algebra,
and denote by
\operatorname{Aut}_{X}({\mathcal{A}})
the group of fibrewise
automorphisms of
{\mathcal{A}}
. A
fibrewise action
of a locally compact group
\mathsf{G}
on

{\mathcal{A}}
is then a group homomorphism
\alpha\colon\mathsf{G}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{X}({\mathcal{A}})
. This
implies that
\alpha
induces an action
\alpha^{x}
on each fiber

{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
for
x\in X
, and in
this case we say that the dynamical system
({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
is

C_{0}(X)
-linear
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
X
be a locally compact Hausdorff space, and let

({\mathcal{A}},\mathsf{G},\alpha)
be a
C_{0}(X)
-linear
C^{*}
-dynamical system. Then
the crossed product
{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
is again a

C_{0}(X)
-algebra with fibres

({\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G})_{x}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}_{x}\rtimes_{\alpha^{x}}\mathsf{G},

where

\alpha_{\gamma}^{x}\big(a(x)\big)=\alpha_{\gamma}(a)(x)

for each
x\in X
,
\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
and
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

The structure map
of
{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
is given by precomposing the structure map
of
{\mathcal{A}}
with the natural injection of the center of the multiplier
algebra of
{\mathcal{A}}
into the center of the multiplier algebra of

{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
; it satisfies

(\mathtt{f}\cdot f)(\gamma)=\mathtt{f}\cdot\big(f(\gamma)\big)
for all

\mathtt{f}\in C_{0}(X)
,
f\in C_{\rm c}(\mathsf{G},{\mathcal{A}})
and

\gamma\in\mathsf{G}
. See
[
52
, Theorem 8.4]
for further
details.
∎

Example 4.8

(transformation groups)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{G})
be a second countable transformation group whose quotient
X/\mathsf{G}
is a
Hausdorff space. By
Example
4.6
,

C_{0}(X)
is a
C_{0}(X/\mathsf{G})
-algebra whose fiber over
\mathsf{G}\cdot x
is
isomorphic to

C_{0}(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{G}_{x})
, where
\mathsf{G}_{x}=\{\gamma\in\mathsf{G}\,|\,\gamma\cdot x=x\}
is the stabilizer subgroup at
x\in X
. Then the crossed product
C_{0}(X)\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{G}
is the
section algebra of a
C^{*}
-algebra bundle over
X/\mathsf{G}
whose fiber
over
\mathsf{G}\cdot x
is isomorphic to

C^{*}(\mathsf{G}_{x})\otimes\mathcal{K}\big({\rm L}^{2}(\mathsf{G}/\mathsf{G}_{x})\big)

[
51
]
. In
the special case where

\mathsf{G}={\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathsf{G}_{x}={\mathbb{Z}}
for all
x\in X
, this is contained in
the statement of Proposition
3.9
.

Example 4.9

(principal torus bundles)
.

Let
E\to X
be a principal
{\mathbb{T}}^{r}
-bundle. By
Example
4.8
,
C_{0}(E)
is a
C_{0}(X)
-algebra
with fibers
C_{0}(E)_{x}\simeq C({\mathbb{T}}^{r})
, and by (
2.10
) there is a
stable isomorphism
C_{0}(E)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{T}}^{r}\simeq C_{0}(X)\otimes\mathcal{K}\big({\rm L}^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{r})\big)
. More generally, a

noncommutative principal
{\mathbb{T}}^{r}
-bundle
on
X
is a

C_{0}(X)
-linear
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathbb{T}}^{r},\alpha)
with
an isomorphism

{\mathcal{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{T}}^{r}\simeq C_{0}(X,\mathcal{K})

of
C^{*}
-algebra bundles over
X
. For further details and a
classification of noncommutative principal torus bundles,
see
[
15
,
21
]
.

Example 4.10

(noncommutative correspondences)
.

The noncommutative correspondence
\mathcal{C}=C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
from
Proposition
3.8
is a noncommutative principal

{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
-bundle on

in the sense of
Example
4.9

C^{*}

\mathcal{C}
is naturally equipped with the

{\mathbb{T}}^{n}=\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}
, and the Takai duality

\mathcal{C}\rtimes_{\widehat{{\rm rt}}}{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\simeq C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\otimes\mathcal{K}\big(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\big)
of

C^{*}
-algebra bundles over the twisted torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
.

Remark 4.11
.

There is a natural notion of Morita equivalence of
C^{*}
-algebra bundles over
X
, similar to the notion of
equivariant Morita equivalence from Theorem
2.12
, which
uses the
C_{0}(X)
-bimodule structures: a
C_{0}(X)
-linear Morita equivalence
between two

C_{0}(X)
-algebras is a Morita equivalence which is compatible with the

C^{*}
-bundle structures over
X
. More generally, there is a category

\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{X}
of
C^{*}
-algebra bundles over
X
whose morphisms are
elements of Kasparov’s groups
\mathcal{R}{\rm KK}(X;{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
, see, e.g.,
[
15
]
: the cycles are the usual cycles

({\mathcal{H}},\phi,T)
for Kasparov’s bivariant K-theory
{\rm KK}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
(cf. Section
3.2
) with the
additional requirement that
\phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\rm End}_{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
is

C_{0}(X)
-linear. There is an obvious faithful functor

\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{X}\to\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}
which forgets the

C_{0}(X)
-algebra structures. Isomorphic
C^{*}
-bundles in the category

\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{X}
are precisely the
\mathcal{R}{\rm KK}
-equivalent

C^{*}
-bundles. If
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are isomorphic in

\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{X}
, i.e., there exists an invertible class

\alpha\in\mathcal{R}{\rm KK}(X;{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
, then they are also
isomorphic as

C^{*}
-algebras in the category
\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}
.

4.4
\bm{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
-actions on Mostow bundles

We can now apply the results of Section
3
to the
class of twisted tori given in
Section
4.2
.
The Mostow fibration of any almost abelian solvmanifold identifies

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
as a torus bundle over a circle, hence the
algebra of functions
C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})
is a
C({\mathbb{T}})
-algebra. In other words,
C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})
is an
object of the category
\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{T}}
, and we are
interested in the T-duality isomorphisms of
C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})

in this category. In particular, given a fibrewise right action of the abelian
Lie group
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
on
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
, it follows from
Theorem
4.7
that the
C^{*}
-algebraic T-dual

C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is also a
C({\mathbb{T}})
-algebra, and by
[
15
, Theorem 3.5]
the
C^{*}
-bundles

C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})
and

C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are isomorphic as
C^{*}
-algebras in the
category
\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{T}}
.

In order to have sensible definitions of
T-duality, we need to identify the
homologically non-trivial one-cycles of the twisted torus

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
, which are determined
in
[
2
, Proposition 4.7]
. Write

\mathtt{M}=(m_{ij})

for the
integer matrix elements
m_{ij}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
of the monodromy matrix. Since

\mathsf{G}={\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathbb{R}}
is simply-connected, the
fundamental group of the twisted torus is

\pi_{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\simeq\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
whose abelianisation

\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}/[\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}},\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}]
gives
the first homology group via the presentation

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}},{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\bigg\langle\hat{e}_{1},\dots,\hat{e}_{d-1}\,\Big|\,\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}m_{ji}\hat{e}_{j}=\hat{e}_{i}\ \mbox{for}\ i=1,\dots,d-1\bigg\rangle,

(4.5)

where the first factor of
{\mathbb{Z}}
corresponds to the base circle of the
torus fibration and the generators
\hat{e}_{1},\dots,\hat{e}_{d-1}

correspond to the torus fibres; they are given by

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{i}:=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\Sigma_{ki}\vec{e}_{k},

(4.6)

where

\vec{e}_{1},\ldots,\vec{e}_{d-1}
is the standard basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}
giving the
group law (
4.3
).

We can then apply the structure theorem for finitely-generated

{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules by appealing to some classical matrix algebra.
From the presentation (
4.5
),
{\rm H}_{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}},{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\rm{coker}}(\varphi_{x_{0}}-{\rm{id}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}})

where
(\varphi_{x_{0}}-{\rm{id}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}})\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}
in the
basis
\hat{e}_{1},\ldots,\hat{e}_{d-1}
is given by the integer relation matrix

\displaystyle{\tt A}:=\mathtt{M}-\mathbbm{1}_{d-1}~.

(4.7)

Let
r
be the rank of
\tt A
. This matrix can be brought into its Smith normal form
\tt D

by finding invertible integer matrices
{\tt L},{\tt R}\in\mathsf{GL}(d-1,{\mathbb{Z}})
such that

{\tt D}={\tt L}{\tt A}{\tt R}

is diagonal with entries
m_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for

i=1,\dots,d-1
. The
integers
m_{i}
are the
elementary divisors
of

{\tt A}
. They have the properties that
m_{i}
divides

m_{i+1}
, for
0<i<d-1
, and in particular
m_{i}=0
for
i>r
; they can
be computed explicitly (up to sign) as

m_{i}=\frac{d_{i}({\tt A})}{d_{i-1}({\tt A})},

where the
i
-th determinant divisor

d_{i}({\tt A})
is the greatest common
divisor of all
i{\times}i
minors of the relation matrix
{\tt A}
,
with
d_{0}({\tt A}):=1
. The matrices
{\tt L},{\tt R}\in\mathsf{GL}(d-1,{\mathbb{Z}})
are found by reducing the matrix
{\tt A}
to
its Smith normal form
{\tt D}
through a sequence of elementary row
and column operations over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, see, e.g.,
[
23
]
.

Given the Smith normal form, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{e}_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\,\big({\tt L}^{-1}\big)_{ji}\,\hat{e}_{j}

(4.8)

and observe that
the image of
\tt A
, which is generated over
{\mathbb{Z}}
by the vectors

\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}{\tt A}_{ji}\hat{e}_{j}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d-1}\big({\tt R}^{-1}\big)_{ki}{\tt D}_{lk}\tilde{e}_{k},

is equivalently generated by the
vectors
m_{k}\tilde{e}_{k}
with
k=1,\ldots,r
.
Hence

\displaystyle{\rm{coker}}(\varphi_{x_{0}}-{\rm{id}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}})

\displaystyle=\big\langle\hat{e}_{1},\ldots,\hat{e}_{d-1}\big\rangle\Big/\bigg\langle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-1}{\tt A}_{j1}\hat{e}_{j},\ldots,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-1}{\tt A}_{j\>\!d-1}\hat{e}_{j}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\tilde{e}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{e}_{d-1}\big\rangle\big/\big\langle m_{1}\tilde{e}_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\tilde{e}_{r}\big\rangle

and

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}},{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1-r}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{i}}.

(4.9)

We are exclusively
interested in the natural
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
-actions on
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
which descend
from actions of abelian subgroups
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\subset\mathsf{G}
, acting on
\mathsf{G}

by right multiplication. They can be
organised into three classes associated with the different types of
summands in the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module presentation of the homology group (

), and we only retain those which are fiberwise
actions on the Mostow bundle.
The first summand
{\mathbb{Z}}
in (
4.9
) corresponds to the subgroup

{\mathbb{R}}_{x}=\big\{(0,\xi)\in\mathsf{G}\big\}

acting on
\mathsf{G}
by right multiplication:

(z,x)(0,\xi)=(z,x+\xi)

for all
(z,x)\in\mathsf{G}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Clearly this does not descend
to a fiberwise
action on the twisted torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
, and the crossed
product
C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{x}
is no longer a

C({\mathbb{T}})
-algebra. Thus an
{\mathbb{R}}_{x}
-action takes us out of the
category
\mathcal{R}\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{T}}
, and we will henceforth
discard
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
-actions where
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
contains the subgroup
{\mathbb{R}}_{x}
.

For the remaining types of summands in (

), we can
give explicit descriptions of the
C^{*}
-algebraic T-duals of an almost
abelian solvmanifold. We consider both classes in turn. As the only solvmanifolds in one and two dimensions are tori, which are
already treated by our analysis from
Section

, we assume

for the
remainder of this paper.

4.5
\bm{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}
-actions: Circle bundles with
\bm{H}
-flux

Let us consider the second summand
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1-r}
in

in
\ker\big(\varphi_{x_{0}}-{\rm id}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}}\big)

In terms of the generators

in the kernel of the

Secondly,

In the following we consider a subgroup

.

is the span of a subset of the generators

.

The quotient group
\mathsf{G}^{\text{\tiny${\sf V}$}}:=\mathsf{G}/{\sf V}
is a
(d{-}n)
-dimensional almost
abelian solvable Lie group

where

for
all

and

, with
{\rm pr}_{\text{\tiny{${\sf V}$}}}
the projection
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
to
{\sf V}
and

{\sf V}

{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}
.

\mathsf{G}

be

The first statement follows from Proposition

∎

4.6
\bm{{\mathbb{R}}_{z}}
-actions: noncommutative torus bundles

Let us now come to the torsion summands
{\mathbb{Z}}_{m_{i}}

{\mathbb{Z}}

elementary divisor

for some

{\mathbb{Z}}

{\mathbb{R}}_{z_{0}}

for all

\displaystyle C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{z_{0}}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ C_{0}(\mathsf{G}/{\mathbb{R}}_{z_{0}})\rtimes_{\rm lt}\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}.

(4.11)

Theorem 4.17
.

\displaystyle C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{z_{0}}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ \mbox{$\coprod\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}$}{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}_{\vec{\theta}_{z_{0}}(x)},

Remark 4.19

Recalling that our formulation of topological T-duality takes place in the additive category

5
Three-dimensional solvmanifolds and their T-duals

The goal of this final section is to give some explicit examples in
low dimensions of the general formalism we have developed in
Section
4
, recovering some previously known
results in the literature from a new perspective, as well as providing several new examples.
Note that tori
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
in any
dimension
d
are covered by our general framework of
Section
4.4
for
{\mathbb{R}}_{y}
-actions: when

\varphi_{x}=\mathbbm{1}_{d-1}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the torus bundles

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{T}}^{d-n}
of Proposition
4.13
are

trivial
. Theorem
4.14
then shows that any

C^{*}
-algebraic T-dual
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{y_{0}}
is isomorphic
to
{\sf C}{\sf T}\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{y_{0}},0\big)\simeq C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{T}}_{y_{0}}\big)\otimes\mathcal{K}
. Below we
apply our formalism to the well-known Mostow fibrations of
three-dimensional solvmanifolds.

5.1
Mostow bundles and
\bm{\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})}
conjugacy classes

In three dimensions, solvmanifolds are completely
classified, see, e.g.,
[
2
]
. In particular,
by
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
all solvmanifolds in
this dimension which are based on connected and simply-connected Lie groups

\mathsf{G}
are almost abelian. If
\mathsf{G}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathbb{R}}
is
abelian, i.e.,
\varphi_{x}=\mathbbm{1}_{2}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}}\simeq{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
is a torus, while
the remaining cases correspond to non-trivial one-parameter group actions

\varphi\colon\ {\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}).

Elements of
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
are classified up to conjugacy by trace, so there are three classes, determined by which of the three types
of conjugacy classes of
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
that
the image of the homomorphism
\varphi
lands in: parabolic
(
|{\rm Tr}\,\varphi_{x}|=2
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
), elliptic (
|{\rm Tr}\,\varphi_{x}|<2

for all
x\not=k\,\pi/2
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
),
or hyperbolic (
|{\rm Tr}\,\varphi_{x}|>2
for all

x\not=0
), see, e.g.,
[
26
]
.

The existence of cocompact discrete subgroups
\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}
of
\mathsf{G}
is highly
restrictive on the allowed homomorphisms
\varphi
; a necessary condition is that there exists

x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
such that
{\rm Tr}\,\varphi_{x_{0}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The monodromy matrices

\displaystyle\mathtt{M}=\Sigma^{-1}\,\varphi_{x_{0}}\Sigma=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})

(5.1)

are integer matrices that live in the corresponding conjugacy classes
of
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
. As automorphisms of the torus fibres of the Mostow bundle, in the mapping
class group
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
, they act on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
in the following
way. Let
\big(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\big)
be the eigenvalues of
\mathtt{M}
, which
are the roots of the characteristic polynomial
t^{2}-({\rm Tr}\,\mathtt{M})t+1
. Then
there are three possibilities:

•

\underline{\rm Parabolic}
: In this case

{\rm Tr}\,\mathtt{M}=\pm\,2
and
\lambda=\lambda^{-1}=\pm\,1
. Then
\mathtt{M}
has
an integral eigenvector corresponding to a closed curve on

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
which is invariant under the
associated automorphism, and homeomorphisms of this type are Dehn twists of

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

•

\underline{\rm Elliptic}
: In this case
{\rm Tr}\,\mathtt{M}=0,\pm 1
,
and the eigenvalues
\big(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\big)
are complex of
modulus
1
. Then the associated automorphism has finite order
equal to
2
,
3
,
4
or
6
, and corresponds to a periodic
homeomorphism of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

•

\underline{\rm Hyperbolic}
: In this case

|\lambda|>1>\big|\lambda^{-1}\big|
. The associated automorphism has no
invariant closed curve on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
; it ‘stretches’ the
eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda
and ‘contracts’ the eigenspace
corresponding to
\lambda^{-1}
. These are called Anasov homeomorphisms of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

In this section we shall apply Theorem
4.17
with

z_{0}=\vec{e}_{2}=(0,1)
, which for convenience we combine with
Proposition
4.18
and Remark
4.19
to reformulate it as

Theorem 5.1
.

The
C^{*}
-algebraic T-dual of a three-dimensional
solvmanifold

with respect to an
{\mathbb{R}}_{z}
-action
with

is Morita equivalent to a
C^{*}
-algebra bundle
of noncommutative two-tori over the circle

\displaystyle C({\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda_{\mathsf{G}}})\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ \mbox{$\coprod\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}$}\,{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{\theta(x)},

where the noncommutativity parameters
\theta(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
are given by

\displaystyle\theta(x)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{for}\ x\in\mathbb{F}_{*},\\
\dfrac{\big(\Sigma^{-1}\varphi_{x_{0}x}\big)_{12}}{\big(\Sigma^{-1}\varphi_{x_{0}x}\big)_{22}}&\mbox{for}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{*},\end{cases}

where
\big(\Sigma^{-1}\varphi_{x_{0}x}\big)_{ij}

\Sigma^{-1}\varphi_{x_{0}x}

\displaystyle\theta(x+1)=\mathtt{M}\big[\theta(x)\big]=\frac{a\theta(x)+b}{c\theta(x)+d}.

(5.2)

By Example
2.10
the corresponding fibre noncommutative
tori are Morita equivalent:

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{\theta(x+1)}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ {\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{\theta(x)},

and so isomorphic in the category
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}}
.

The
C^{*}
-algebraic T-dual in these instances has more structure in general than the
original algebra of functions

, as a consequence of

Proposition 5.2
.

The noncommutative torus bundle
\coprod_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta(x)}^{2}
is a
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
-algebra.

Proof.

We can also describe these noncommutative torus bundles via (strict)
deformation quantization: Performing an identical calculation to that of
Example
2.1
, we may describe the fibrewise crossed product (
4.18
) (with
d=3
) explicitly in terms of a
star-product, and hence the
C^{*}
-algebra bundle of Theorem
5.1

as a deformation of the algebra of functions

C\big({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
on the trivial torus bundle over
{\mathbb{T}}
by regarding the convolution algebra
C_{\rm c}({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{Z}})
as the space of functions

C\big({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
equipped with the star-product

\displaystyle(f\star_{\theta}g)(x,v_{1},v_{2})=\sum_{(p_{1},p_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}{\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}(p_{1}v_{1}+p_{2}v_{2})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{(f\star_{\theta} g)(x,v_1,v_2) = \sum_{(p_1,p_2)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^2} }{}\times\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}f_{q_{1},q_{2}}(x)g_{p_{1}-q_{1},p_{2}-q_{2}}(x){\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}\theta(x)(p_{1}-q_{1})q_{2}},

(5.3)

where we used the fibrewise Fourier transformation

In this formulation of the

-algebraic T-dual, it is immediately evident that there is an injection

(or

(or

-algebra

∎

The remainder of this paper is devoted to providing illustrations of

5.1
, through explicit calculations in
each of the three conjugacy classes of
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. The features
differ for each conjugacy class so we consider them individually in

5.2
Parabolic torus bundles

We start with the best
studied example in the literature, which is based on the nilpotent Heisenberg
group. In string theory it is T-dual to the three-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
with
H
-flux by
the standard Buscher rules (see, e.g.,
[
27
]
), and in
topological T-duality it gives the basic example of a noncommutative
principal torus
bundle
[
15
,
21
,
32
]
. Here we shall give a new algebraic
perspective on
both these T-duals by applying our formalism of topological T-duality
directly to the Heisenberg nilmanifold. This class has several
features that make it special among the three-dimensional
solvmanifolds, which we explain in detail.

Heisenberg nilmanifolds

The three-dimensional Heisenberg group
{\sf Heis}(3)
is the nilpotent
Lie group whose Mostow bundle
structure is based on the semi-direct product

{\sf Heis}(3)={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathbb{R}}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\varphi_{x}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

Since
|{\rm Tr}\,\varphi_{x}|=2
, the matrix
\varphi_{x}
parameterizes a
parabolic conjugacy class of
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Here we denote the coordinates on the group manifold of
\mathsf{N}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

(y,z)
. The group multiplication on
{\sf Heis}(3)
is then given by

(x,y,z)(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime}+xz^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}),

and the inverse of a group element is

(x,y,z)^{-1}=(-x,xz-y,-z).

In this case it is clear that for

x_{0}=m

with
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
, the matrix
\varphi_{x_{0}}
is integer-valued and hence
may be taken as monodromy matrix
{\tt M}_{m}
of infinite order with
\Sigma=\mathbbm{1}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\mathtt{M}_{m}=\varphi_{m}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&m\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

(5.4)

The
Heisenberg nilmanifold

{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}
is the compact
space obtained as the quotient of
{\sf Heis}(3)
with respect to the
lattice given by the discrete Heisenberg group

{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}}):=\big\{(m\,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in{\sf Heis}(3)\,\big|\,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}\big\}.

The equivalence relation
is given by the left action of
{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})
, which
leads to the local coordinate identifications under the action of the
generators of
{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})
given by

\displaystyle(x,y,z)\longmapsto(x+m,y+m\,z,z),

\displaystyle(x,y,z)\longmapsto(x,y+1,z),

\displaystyle(x,y,z)\longmapsto(x,y,z+1).

(5.5)

Geometrically, this exhibits the Heisenberg nilmanifold as a
non-trivial principal
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
-bundle

{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\rightarrow{\mathbb{T}}
.
Using the algorithm described in Section
4.4
, the
relation matrix

{\tt A}_{m}=\mathtt{M}_{m}-\mathbbm{1}_{2}

has rank
r=1
with elementary divisors

m_{1}=m
and
m_{2}=0
, and the first homology group of
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}
can thus be presented as the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{1}({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})},{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}),

with respective free generators denoted
e_{x}
and
e_{y}
, and torsion
generator
e_{z}
of order
m
,
me_{z}=0
.

Dually, the topology of the nilmanifold is captured by its algebra of
functions, which can be computed as the subalgebra of invariant
functions on the
Heisenberg group
{\sf Heis}(3)
with respect to the left action of the
lattice
{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})
:

C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)=C_{0}\big({\sf Heis}(3)\big)^{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}.

Harmonic analysis on the nilmanifold can be used to determine the
Fourier decomposition of any function
\mathtt{f}\in C({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})})
. Invariance under the generators
(
5.5
) of
{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})
forces the expansion to take the form

\displaystyle\mathtt{f}(x,y,z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}\sum_{p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathtt{f}_{p,k}(x+mq){\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}py+2\pi{{\rm i}}(k+mpq)z},

(5.6)

for functions
\mathtt{f}_{p,k}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
vanishing at
infinity. From this expression we can easily see the

C({\mathbb{T}})
-algebra structure of
C({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})})
: Restricting (
5.6
) to
y=z=0

determines a function on the circle
{\mathbb{T}}=\big\{\big({\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}x/m},1,1\big)\in{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big\}
and defines a
C^{*}
-algebra monomorphism

C({\mathbb{T}})\hookrightarrow C({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})})
. On the other hand, the evaluation
of (
5.6
) at
z=0

(5.8)

Noncommutative correspondences

We come now to the noncommutative correspondences underlying this
topological T-duality. For this, we recall that both

C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)
and
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)\rtimes_{{\rm Ad}^{*}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{(0,1)}

are
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
-algebras, with
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}

parameterized by the local coordinates
(x,y)
. We will show that the
noncommutative correspondence is given by the balanced tensor product

C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{Z}}_{(0,1)}\simeq C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)\otimes_{C({\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\mbox{$\coprod\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}$}{\mathbb{T}}_{mx}^{2}

over
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)

Note that here the subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(0,1)}
acts non-trivially on the algebra
of functions

, in contrast to our previous examples. We consider elements of the convolution
algebra
C_{\rm c}({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{(0,1)})
as sequences

of functions

with the convolution product

(f\star g)_{\tilde{q}}(x,y,z)=\sum_{\tilde{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{\tilde{p}}(x,y,z)g_{\tilde{q}-\tilde{p}}(x,y-\tilde{p}\,m\,x,z).

We write the Fourier transformation

f(x,y,z,\tilde{z}):=\sum_{\tilde{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{\tilde{q}}(x,y,z){\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}\tilde{q}\tilde{z}}

for functions on
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{z}
, and define the star-product

(f\star g)(x,y,z,\tilde{z})=\sum_{\tilde{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(f\star g)_{\tilde{q}}(x,y,z){\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}\tilde{q}\tilde{z}}.

We further use the harmonic expansion of functions on the nilmanifold
given from (
5.6
) by

f_{\tilde{q}}(x,y,z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}\sum_{p,q\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{\tilde{q};p,k}(x+q){\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}qy+2\pi{{\rm i}}(k+mpq)z}.

After some elementary manipulations, we can then write the star-product on
the space of functions
C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{z}\big)
as

\displaystyle(f\star g)(x,y,z,\tilde{z})=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}\sum_{r,s,\tilde{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}ry+2\pi{{\rm i}}(l+mrs)z}{\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}\tilde{p}\tilde{z}}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}}\sum_{p,q,\tilde{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{\tilde{q};p,k}(x+q)g_{\tilde{p}-\tilde{q};r-p,l-k}(x+q-s){\,\rm e}\,^{2\pi{{\rm i}}mr(q-s)z}{\,\rm e}\,^{-2\pi{{\rm i}}mx(r-p)\tilde{q}}.

(5.11)

We can define an injection
C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)\hookrightarrow C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf Heis}_{m}(3;{\mathbb{Z}})}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{z}\big)
as the space of
functions which are independent of the coordinate
\tilde{z}
. For
these functions the sums in (

, and one recovers the pointwise product of
functions in

5.3
Elliptic torus bundles

We shall now illustrate Theorem
5.1
for the three-dimensional solvmanifolds
based on the Euclidean group in two dimensions, which have no
classical Hausdorff T-duals and whose noncommutative T-dual fibration was first
discussed in
[
28
]
for the case of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
elliptic
monodromy. Here we shall recover this noncommutative geometry rigorously from
our algebraic framework, and moreover extend it to the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{6}
elliptic
monodromies which have not been considered previously.

The Euclidean group
{\sf ISO}(2)
in two dimensions is the
three-dimensional almost abelian solvable Lie group

\displaystyle{\sf ISO}(2)={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathbb{R}}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\varphi_{x}=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos x&\sin x\\
-\sin x&\cos x\end{matrix}\right).

Since
|{\rm Tr}\,\varphi_{x}|<2
for
x\notin\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
, the rotations
\varphi_{x}

parameterize an elliptic conjugacy class of
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Here we shall denote coordinates on the group manifold of
\mathsf{N}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

z=(z_{1},z_{2})
. The group multiplication on
{\sf ISO}(2)
is then

(x,z_{1},z_{2})\,(x^{\prime},z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})=\big(x+x^{\prime},z_{1}+z_{1}^{\prime}\cos x+z_{2}^{\prime}\sin x,z_{2}-z_{1}^{\prime}\sin x+z_{2}^{\prime}\cos x\big)

and the inverse of a group element is

(x,z_{1},z_{2})^{-1}=\big({-}x,-z_{1}\cos x+z_{2}\sin x,-z_{1}\sin x-z_{2}\cos x\big).

The existence of lattices in
{\sf ISO}(2)
is highly
restrictive
[
26
,
28
]
. They require angles

x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
for which
2\cos x_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, i.e.,
\cos x_{0}=0,\pm\frac{1}{2},\pm 1
. For our purposes, there are three
inequivalent choices of non-trivial elliptic monodromies,
which are characterized by matrices
{\tt M}\in\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
of finite
order in the cyclic subgroups
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{6}
. They lead to three
types of
Euclidean solvmanifolds
, according to the order of the monodromy in the fibre of the
associated Mostow bundle. A crucial distinction from the case of the
Heisenberg nilmanifold is that here the Mostow fibrations are not
principal
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
-bundles.
We consider each of the three cases
separately in turn.

Euclidean
\bm{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}
-solvmanifolds

We observe that for

x_{0}=m\pi

with
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
, the matrix
\varphi_{x_{0}}
is integer-valued, and so
may be set equal to
\tt M
with
\Sigma=\mathbbm{1}_{2}
. For
m
even, the
monodromy
{\tt M}=\mathbbm{1}_{2}
is trivial and the corresponding
Mostow bundle is simply the three-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
, which we have
already considered in Section
3.4
. For
m
odd,
which we now assume,
the nontrivial monodromy matrix is given by

\displaystyle{\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}=\varphi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{m\pi}=-\mathbbm{1}_{2}.

(5.12)

The Euclidean

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-solvmanimanifold
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}
is the compact
space obtained as the quotient of
{\sf ISO}(2)
with
respect to the lattice given by the discrete Euclidean group

{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}}):=\big\{(m\pi\alpha,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in{\sf ISO}(2)\,\big|\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big\}.

The quotient is taken by the left action of

{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
, which leads
to the local coordinate identifications under the action of the
generators of
{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
given by

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto(x+m\,\pi,-z_{1},-z_{2}),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto(x,z_{1}+1,z_{2}),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto(x,z_{1},z_{2}+1).

The relation matrix
{\tt A}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}={\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}-\mathbbm{1}_{2}=-2\mathbbm{1}_{2}
has maximal rank
r=2
with elementary divisors

m_{1}=m_{2}=2
, and
the first homology group of

{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}
can thus be presented as the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

{\rm H}_{1}\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})},{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

It follows that the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-solvmanifolds do not possess any classical
T-duals. By symmetry the two possible noncommutative torus bundles
corresponding to the two torsion generators
e_{z_{1}}
and
e_{z_{2}}
of
order two are the same.

Consider the one-parameter subgroup
{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}
of
{\sf ISO}(2)

given by

{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}=\big\{(0,0,\zeta)\in{\sf ISO}(2)\big\},

which acts on the Euclidean group
{\sf ISO}(2)
by
right multiplication

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})(0,0,\zeta)=(x,z_{1}+\zeta\sin x,z_{2}+\zeta\cos x).

In this case
\mathbb{F}_{*}
is the subset of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
where
\cos x=0
, so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{*}=\tfrac{\pi}{2}(2{\mathbb{Z}}+1).

(5.13)

Applying Theorem
5.1
, it follows that the
C^{*}
-algebraic T-dual of the
Euclidean
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-solvmanifold is Morita equivalent to the

C({\mathbb{T}})
-algebra

\displaystyle C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny($1$)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ \mbox{$\coprod\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}$}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{\theta^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(x)},

(5.14)

where

\displaystyle\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{for}\ x\in\frac{1}{m}\big({\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{1}{2}\big),\\
\tan(m\pi x)&\mbox{for}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\frac{1}{m}\big({\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{1}{2}\big).\end{cases}

(5.15)

Recalling that the integer
m
is odd here, it is easily seen that the noncommutativity parameter (
5.15
) is invariant under changing coset representative
x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, which is consistent with its
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
orbit under
the corresponding monodromy matrix (
5.12
):

\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(x+1)=\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(x)={\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}\big[\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(x)\big],

so that fibrewise
{\sf A}_{\theta^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(x+1)}={\sf A}_{\theta^{\text{\tiny(1)}}_{m}(x)}
. Thus the non-trivial
monodromy in the automorphism group of the
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
fibres of the
twisted torus
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}
becomes a
trivial identity action on its T-dual
C^{*}
-algebra bundle.
This is analogous to what we found in the parabolic case. Moreover,
by Proposition
5.2
the
C^{*}
-algebra
bundle (
5.14
) can be described as a deformation of the algebra of functions

C\big({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
on a trivial
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
-bundle over the
circle via a star-product

f\star_{\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}}g
given by (
5.3
). In marked contrast to the parabolic case, however, the algebra of
functions
C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)
is not itself
a
C\big({\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)
-algebra, and the
noncommutative torus bundle cannot be identified with the group

C^{*}
-algebra of the integer Euclidean group

{\sf ISO}_{m}^{\text{\tiny(1)}}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Euclidean
\bm{{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}}
-solvmanifolds

We next observe that for

x_{0}=\frac{m\pi}{2}

with
m
an odd integer, the matrix
\varphi_{x_{0}}
is again integer-valued, and so
may be set equal to
\tt M
with
\Sigma=\mathbbm{1}_{2}
:

\displaystyle{\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}=\varphi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\frac{m\pi}{2}}=\pm\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right).

(5.16)

Without loss of generality, we shall fix the positive sign by assuming that
m\in 4\mathbb{Z}+1
(the choice of negative sign for
m\in 4{\mathbb{Z}}+3
simply
corresponds to a reflection
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(-z_{1},-z_{2})
below).

The Euclidean

{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
-solvmanifold
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}
is the compact
space obtained as the quotient of
{\sf ISO}(2)
with

{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}}):=\big\{\big(\tfrac{m\pi}{2}\alpha,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\big)\in{\sf ISO}(2)\,\big|\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}},\,\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big\}.

The quotient is taken by the left action of

{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
, which leads
to the local coordinate identifications under the action of the
generators of
{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
given by

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\big(x+\tfrac{m\pi}{2},z_{2},-z_{1}\big),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto(x,z_{1}+1,z_{2}),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto(x,z_{1},z_{2}+1).

The relation matrix
{\tt A}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}={\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}-\mathbbm{1}_{2}
has maximal rank
r=2
with elementary divisors
m_{1}=1
and

m_{2}=2
, and the first homology group of

{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}
can thus be presented as the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

{\rm H}_{1}\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})},{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},

where the torsion
generator of order two is
e_{z_{2}}=e_{z_{1}}
. Thus again there is no
classical T-dual, as is well-known in
this case (see, e.g.,
[
28
]
).

Proceeding as in the previous
case, the subset
\mathbb{F}_{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is again given by (
5.13
) and we arrive at the Morita equivalence

C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny($2$)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ \mbox{$\coprod\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}$}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{\theta^{\text{\tiny(2)}}_{m}(x)},

where

\displaystyle\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{for}\ x\in\frac{1}{m}(2{\mathbb{Z}}+1),\\
\tan\big(\frac{m\pi}{2}x\big)&\mbox{for}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\frac{1}{m}(2{\mathbb{Z}}+1).\end{cases}

(5.17)

The corresponding star-product

f\star_{\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}}g
on
C\big({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\big)

is given by (
5.3
) and was originally written down
in
[
28
]
, where it was also pointed out that the
noncommutative torus fibration is not isomorphic to the group

C^{*}
-algebra of
{\sf ISO}_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Compared to the previous cases, the new feature here is that a change of coset
representative
x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
generally has a
non-trivial action on the fibers of the
C^{*}
-bundle
\coprod_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}{\sf A}_{\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(x)}
according to
(
5.2
). We check this explicitly here: Recalling that
m\in 4\,{\mathbb{Z}}+1
in
this case, the fibre over any
x\in\frac{1}{m}{\mathbb{Z}}
is preserved
as then the form of (
5.17
) implies

\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(x+1)=0=\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(x)\qquad\mbox{for}\quad x\in\tfrac{1}{m}{\mathbb{Z}}.

However,
over any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\frac{1}{m}{\mathbb{Z}}
the noncommutativity parameter (
5.17
) changes non-trivally according to the
trigonometric identity
\tan\big(x+\frac{m\pi}{2}\big)=-\cot x
, but in a way which is
consistent with its
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
orbit under the corresponding
monodromy matrix (
5.16
):

\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(x+1)=-\frac{1}{\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(x)}={\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}\big[\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(2)}}(x)\big]\qquad\mbox{for}\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\tfrac{1}{m}{\mathbb{Z}}.

By Theorem
5.1
, the fibre noncommutative tori are Morita
equivalent, and so are isomorphic in the
category
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}}
. A physical picture of the action
of the monodromy on the K-theory group of this
C^{*}
-bundle in terms
of fiber D-branes is given in
[
28
]
.

Euclidean
\bm{{\mathbb{Z}}_{6}}
-solvmanifolds

Finally, we observe that for

x_{0}=\frac{m\pi}{3}

with
m\notin 3{\mathbb{Z}}
, the matrix
\varphi_{x_{0}}
takes four possible forms

\varphi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\frac{m\,\pi}{3}}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}\epsilon^{\prime}&\sqrt{3}\\
-\sqrt{3}&\epsilon^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right)

with independent signs
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}=\pm 1
. It
conjugates to an
integer matrix via the element
\Sigma\in\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
given by

\displaystyle\Sigma=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}\left(\begin{matrix}1&\frac{1}{2}\\
0&\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\end{matrix}\right).

(5.18)

The precise form of
\varphi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{x_{0}}
and hence of the
corresponding monodromy matrix
\tt M
depends on the congruence
class of the integer
m
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
:

\Sigma^{-1}\,\varphi^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{\frac{m\pi}{3}}\Sigma=\begin{cases}\pm\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\hphantom{-}1&1\\
-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),&m\in 3{\mathbb{Z}}+1,\\
\pm\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\hphantom{-}0&\hphantom{-}1\\
-1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right),&m\in 3{\mathbb{Z}}+2.\end{cases}

Without loss of generality, we shall fix
m\in 6\,{\mathbb{Z}}+2
and hence take

\displaystyle{\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-1&-1\end{matrix}\right)

(5.19)

in the following (with the other three possibilities obtained simply
by reflection
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(-z_{1},-z_{2})
and interchange

(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(z_{2},z_{1})
below).

The Euclidean

{\mathbb{Z}}_{6}
-solvmanifold
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}
is the compact
space obtained as the quotient of
{\sf ISO}(2)
with
respect to the lattice given by the discrete subgroup

\displaystyle{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}}):=\left\{\left(\tfrac{m\pi}{3}\alpha,\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}}\gamma_{1}+\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}}\gamma_{2},\sqrt{\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\gamma_{2}\right)\!\in{\sf ISO}(2)\,\Big|\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}},\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\right\}.

The quotient is taken by the left action of

{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
, which leads
to the local coordinate identifications under the action of the
generators of
{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})
given by

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\left(x+\tfrac{m\pi}{3},\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\sqrt{3}z_{2}-z_{1}\big),-\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\sqrt{3}z_{1}+z_{2}\big)\right),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\left(x,z_{1}+\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}},z_{2}\right),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\left(x,z_{1}+\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}},z_{2}+\sqrt{\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\right).

The relation matrix
{\tt A}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}={\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}-\mathbbm{1}_{2}
has maximal rank
r=2
with elementary divisors
m_{1}=1
and

m_{2}=3
, and the first homology group can thus be presented as the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

{\rm H}_{1}\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})},{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{3},

where the torsion generator of order three is
e_{z_{2}}=e_{z_{1}}
. Here too there is no
classical T-dual.

Applying Theorem
5.1
in this case, with the
non-trivial period matrix
\Sigma
from (
5.18
) and the
subset
\mathbb{F}_{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
again given by (
5.13
), gives
the Morita equivalence

C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}^{\text{\tiny($3$)}}_{m}(2;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ \mbox{$\coprod\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}$}{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{\theta^{\text{\tiny(3)}}_{m}(x)},

where

\displaystyle\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{for}\ x\in\frac{1}{m}\big(3{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{3}{2}\big),\\
-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\tan\big(\frac{m\pi}{3}x\big)&\mbox{for}\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\frac{1}{m}\big(3{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{3}{2}\big).\end{cases}

(5.20)

As in the previous case, changing coset representative

x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
generally has a non-trivial action on the fibers of the

C^{*}
-bundle over
{\mathbb{T}}
: Recalling that
m\in 6\,{\mathbb{Z}}+2
here, the
fiber over any
x\in\frac{1}{m}\,\big(3\,{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{3}{2}\big)
is preserved by
the form of (
5.20
):

\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}(x+1)=0=\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}(x)\qquad\mbox{for}\quad x\in\tfrac{1}{m}\big(3{\mathbb{Z}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\big).

On the other hand, over any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\frac{1}{m}\big(3{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{3}{2}\big)

the noncommutativity parameter (
5.20
) changes according to
the trigonometric identity

\tan\big(x+\tfrac{m\pi}{3}\big)=\frac{\tan x-\sqrt{3}}{1+\sqrt{3}\tan x},

but consistently with its
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
orbit under the corresponding
monodromy matrix (
5.19
):

\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}(x+1)=-\frac{1}{\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}(x)+1}={\tt M}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}\big[\theta_{m}^{\text{\tiny(3)}}(x)\big]\qquad\mbox{for}\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\tfrac{1}{m}\big(3{\mathbb{Z}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\big).

By Theorem
5.1
the corresponding fibre noncommutative tori are Morita
equivalent,
and so isomorphic in the category
{\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}}
.

5.4
Hyperbolic torus bundles

Our last application of
Theorem
5.1
is to the final class of
three-dimensional solvmanifolds, which are
hyperbolic analogues of the Euclidean solvmanifolds based on the
Poincaré group, and have also not been
previously studied in the present context.

The Poincaré group
{\sf ISO}(1,1)
in two dimensions is the
three-dimensional almost abelian solvable Lie group which can be presented as

{\sf ISO}(1,1)={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}{\mathbb{R}}\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\varphi_{x}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}\cosh x&\sinh x\\
\sinh x&\cosh x\end{matrix}\bigg).

Since
|{\rm Tr}\,\varphi_{x}|>2
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
, the hyperbolic rotation

\varphi_{x}
parameterizes a hyperbolic conjugacy class of

\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
. Again we denote coordinates on the group
manifold of
\mathsf{N}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}

by
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
, so that the group multiplication on

{\sf ISO}(1,1)
is given by

(x,z_{1},z_{2})(x^{\prime},z_{1}^{\prime},z_{2}^{\prime})=\big(x+x^{\prime},z_{1}+z_{1}^{\prime}\cosh x+z_{2}^{\prime}\sinh x,z_{2}+z_{1}^{\prime}\sinh x+z_{2}^{\prime}\cosh x\big)

and the inverse of a group element is

(x,z_{1},z_{2})^{-1}=\big({-}x,-z_{1}\cosh x+z_{2}\sinh x,z_{1}\sinh x-z_{2}\cosh x\big).

Clearly there is no
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
for which
\varphi_{x_{0}}
is
an integer matrix in this case. However, there is an infinite family of discrete points

\displaystyle x_{0}=\log\left(\frac{m}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)^{2}-1}\right)

(5.21)

labelled by an integer
m>2
with
2\cosh x_{0}=m
, at which
the matrix
\varphi_{x_{0}}
takes the form

\varphi_{x_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}m&\pm\sqrt{m^{2}-4}\\
\pm\sqrt{m^{2}-4}&m\end{matrix}\right).

This matrix has eigenvalues
\big(\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\big)
where

\lambda^{\pm 1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(m\pm\sqrt{m^{2}-4}\right)={\,\rm e}\,^{x_{0}},

and it conjugates to the integer matrix

\displaystyle{\tt M}_{m}=\Sigma^{-1}\varphi_{x_{0}}\Sigma=\left(\begin{matrix}m&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right)

(5.22)

by the element
\Sigma\in\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
given by

\displaystyle\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\lambda^{\pm 1}}{2\big(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\big)}-\lambda^{\mp 1}&\dfrac{\lambda}{2\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)}-1\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\dfrac{\lambda^{\pm 1}}{2\big(\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\big)}+\lambda^{\mp 1}&\dfrac{\lambda}{2\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)}+1\end{pmatrix}.

(5.23)

For definiteness, we choose the positive square root in (
5.21
) (with the choice of negative square root obtained
from the interchange
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(z_{2},z_{1})
below).

The Poincaré
solvmanifold
{\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}_{m}(1,1;{\mathbb{Z}})}
is the compact space
obtained as the quotient of
{\sf ISO}(1,1)
by the lattice

{\sf ISO}_{m}(1,1;{\mathbb{Z}}):=\big\{(x_{0}\,\alpha,\Sigma\cdot\gamma)\in{\sf ISO}(1,1)\,\big|\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}},\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big\}.

The quotient by the left action of this lattice leads to the local
coordinate identifications under the action of the generators given by

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\left(x+\cosh^{-1}\big(\tfrac{m}{2}\big),\tfrac{1}{2}\big(mz_{1}+\sqrt{m^{2}-4}z_{2}\big),\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\sqrt{m^{2}-4}z_{1}+mz_{2}\big)\right),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\left(x,z_{1}+\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2(\lambda^{2}-1)}-\lambda^{-1},z_{2}+\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{2(\lambda^{2}-1)}+\lambda^{-1}\right),

\displaystyle(x,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\left(x,z_{1}+\tfrac{\lambda}{2(\lambda^{2}-1)}-1,z_{2}+\tfrac{\lambda}{2(\lambda^{2}-1)}+1\right).

The relation matrix
{\tt A}_{m}={\tt M}_{m}-\mathbbm{1}_{2}
has maximal rank
r=2
with elementary divisors
m_{1}=1
and

m_{2}=m-2
, so the first homology group of the Poincaré solvmanifold may be
presented as the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

{\rm H}_{1}\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}_{m}(1,1;{\mathbb{Z}})},{\mathbb{Z}}\big)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}_{m-2},

with torsion generator
e_{z_{2}}=-e_{z_{1}}
of order
m-2
.

Consider the one-parameter subgroup

{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}=\big\{(0,0,\zeta)\in{\sf ISO}(1,1)\big\}

acting on the Poincaré group
{\sf ISO}(1,1)
by right multiplication

(x,z_{1},z_{2})(0,0,\zeta)=(x,z_{1}+\zeta\sinh x,z_{2}+\zeta\cosh x).

In this case the set
\mathbb{F}_{*}
consists of a distinguished point
x_{*}\in{\mathbb{R}}
on the base of the

C^{*}
-algebra bundle given by

x_{*}=\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{2\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)-\lambda^{3}}{2\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)+\lambda^{3}}\right).

Applying
Theorem
5.1
, with the periods
(
5.21
) and (
5.23
), then identifies the
Morita equivalence

C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}_{m}(1,1;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)\rtimes_{\rm rt}{\mathbb{R}}_{(0,1)}\ {\bm{\sim}}_{\text{\tiny M}}\ \mbox{$\coprod\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}$}{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta_{m}(x)}^{2}

of
C({\mathbb{T}})
-algebras, where

\theta_{m}(x_{*})=0

and

\displaystyle\theta_{m}(x)=\frac{2\lambda\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)-\lambda^{2}+\lambda\big(2\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)+\lambda\big)\tanh\big(\cosh^{-1}\big(\frac{m}{2}\big)x\big)}{\lambda^{3}-2\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)-\big(\lambda^{3}+2\big(\lambda^{2}-1\big)\big)\tanh\big(\cosh^{-1}(\frac{m}{2})x\big)}

(5.24)

for
x\neq x_{*}
.

This noncommutative torus bundle, along with its topological T-duality with

C\big({\mathbb{T}}_{{\sf ISO}_{m}(1,1;{\mathbb{Z}})}\big)
, has formally the same properties
as the
C({\mathbb{T}})
-algebras described in
Section
5.3
, so
we refrain from repeating the details here. We only mention the
fibre monodromy behaviour anticipated from (
5.2
): Using the hyperbolic identity

\tanh\big(x+\cosh^{-1}\big(\tfrac{m}{2}\big)\big)=\frac{m\tanh x+\sqrt{m^{2}-4}}{m+\sqrt{m^{2}-4}\tanh x},

we find

\theta_{m}(x_{*}+1)=0=\theta_{m}(x_{*})

and
we see explicitly here that the noncommutativity parameter (
5.24
)
changes consistently with its
\mathsf{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
orbit under the monodromy
matrix (
5.22
):

\theta_{m}(x+1)=-\frac{1}{\theta_{m}(x)}-m={\tt M}_{m}\big[\theta_{m}(x)\big]\qquad\mbox{for}\quad x\neq x_{*}.

Thus the fiber noncommutative tori are Morita equivalent by
Theorem
5.1
,
and so are isomorphic
C^{*}
-algebras in the category

\mathscr{K}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathscr{K}
.

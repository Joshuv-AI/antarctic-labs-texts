1.
Introduction and the main result

Standing waves for the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\Delta\psi-\tilde{V}(y)\psi+|\psi|^{p-1}\psi,

(1.1)

are solutions of the form
\psi(t,y)=e^{i\lambda t}u(y),

where
i
denotes the imaginary
part and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},~p>1.

Assuming that
u(y)
is positive and vanishes at infinity, we see
that
\psi
satisfies (
1.1
) if and only if
u
satisfies the following nonlinear elliptic problem

-\Delta u+V(y)u=u^{p},\,\,u>0,\,\,\,\,\,\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow\infty}u(y)=0,

(1.2)

where
V(y)=\tilde{V}(y)-\lambda
. Hereafter, we assume that
V(y)
is bounded and
V(y)\geq 0.

When
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}

in (
1.2
) i.e. the subcritical exponent case, in
[
24
]

Wei and Yan showed the equation has infinitely many non-radial positive solutions when
V(y)
is a radially positive function.
There are various existence results for the subcritical case, such as
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
.

In this paper, we will investigate the critical case i.e.
p=\frac{N+2}{N-2}:

-\Delta u+V(y)u=u^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}},\,\,u>0,\,\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.3)

where
V(y)\geq 0
and
V\not\equiv 0
.
It corresponds to the following well-known Brezis-Nirenberg problem in
S^{N}

-\Delta_{S^{N}}u=u^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}+\mu u,\,\,u>0,\,\,\hbox{on}\,\,S^{N}.

(1.4)

Indeed, after using the stereographic projection, problem (
1.4
) can be reduced to (
1.3
) with

V(y)=\frac{-4\mu-N(N-2)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}},

and
V(y)>0
if
\mu<-\frac{N(N-2)}{4}
.
Problem (
1.4
) has been studied extensively.
In
[
3
]
,
Brezis and Li proved if
\mu>-\frac{N(N-2)}{4},
then the only solution to (
1.4
)
is the constant
u=(-\mu)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}.

When
\mu=-\frac{N(N-2)}{4},

in
[
10
]
Druet (see also Druet and Hebey
[
11
,
12
]
)
proved that the set of positive solutions to (
1.4
)
is compact provided that the energy is bounded.
Furthermore, in
[
1
,
4
]
there have been proved that there are more and more non-radial
solutions as
\mu\rightarrow-\infty.

In
[
7
]
, Chen, Wei and Yan proved that when

\mu<-\frac{N(N-2)}{4}

and
N\geq 5,
there are infinitely many non-radial solutions to (
1.4
)
whose energy can be made arbitrarily large. This implies that the boundedness
of energy in
[
10
,
11
]
is necessary. When
\mu=-\frac{N(N-2)}{2}

and
u^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}
is taken place
of
K(y)u^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}
in (
1.4
)
where
K(y)
being a fixed smooth function, in
[
25
]

Wei and Yan showed that it has infinitely many non-radial positive solutions.
More results on
the existence, multiplicity and qualitative properties of solutions for non-compact elliptic problems can also be found in
[
3
,
9
,
13
,
17
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
and the references therein.

It is not difficult to see that
if
V\geq 0

and

, then the mountain pass value for problem (
1.3
) is not a critical value of the corresponding functional.
Hence all the arguments based on the concentration compactness arguments
[

19

To our best knowledge, the first existence result for (

For the Brezis–Nirenberg problem in

Set

(A.3)

Lemma A.1
.

There holds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial V}{\partial y_{i}}u\eta,

(A.5)

In this section, we mainly study the Green’s function of
L_{m}
(see the definition of (
2.4
)).

For any function
g
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define its corresponding function
g^{\star}\in H_{s}
as follows.

First we define
\textbf{A}_{j}
as

\textbf{A}_{j}z=\Bigl(r\cos\big(\theta+\frac{2j\pi}{m}\big),r\sin\big(\theta+\frac{2j\pi}{m}\big),z^{\prime\prime}\Big),\quad j=1,\cdots,m,

where
z=(z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
z^{\prime}=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
z^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-2}
, while

\textbf{B}_{i}z=\begin{cases}\bigl(z_{1},\cdots,z_{i-1},-z_{i},z_{i+1},\cdots,z_{N}),\quad&i=2,\\
\bigl(z_{1},\cdots,z_{i-1},z_{i},z_{i+1},\cdots,z_{N}),\quad&i=1,3,4,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N.\end{cases}

Let

\hat{g}(y)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}g(\textbf{A}_{j}y),

Then

Noting that
\delta_{x}
is not in
H_{s},
we consider

L_{m}u=\delta_{x}^{\star},\quad u\in H_{s}.

(B.1)

The solution of (
B.1
) is denoted as

. We want to point out that

\delta_{x}^{\star}=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{\textbf{A}_{j}x}+\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{\textbf{B}_{i}\textbf{A}_{j}x}\Bigr).

Proposition B.1
.

Assume that
V(y)\geq 0
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The solution
G_{m}(y,x)
of (
B.1
) satisfies

|G_{m}(y,x)|\leq\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{C}{|y-\textbf{A}_{j}x|^{N-2}}+\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{C}{|y-\textbf{B}_{i}\textbf{A}_{j}x|^{N-2}}\Bigr)

for all

, where

is any fixed large constant.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{1}=\frac{C_{N}}{|y-x|^{N-2}}
, which satisfies
-\Delta\omega_{1}=\delta_{x}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
\omega_{2}
be the solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta\omega+V(y)\omega=(2^{*}-1)u_{m}^{2^{*}-2}\omega_{1},&\text{in}\;B_{2R}(0),\\
\omega=0,&\text{on}\;\partial B_{2R}(0).\end{cases}

Then
\omega_{2}\geq 0
and

\omega_{2}(y)=\int_{B_{2R}(0)}G(z,y)(2^{*}-1)u_{m}^{2^{*}-2}\omega_{1}\leq C\int_{B_{R}(0)}\frac{1}{|y-z|^{N-2}}\frac{1}{|z-x|^{N-2}}\,dz\leq\frac{C}{|y-x|^{N-4}},

where

is the Green’s function of the positive operator

in
B_{2R}(0)
with zero boundary condition.
We can continue this process to find
\omega_{i}
, which is the solution of

\begin{cases}-\Delta\omega+V(y)\omega=(2^{*}-1)u_{m}^{2^{*}-2}\omega_{i-1},&\text{in}\;B_{2R}(0),\\
\omega=0,&\text{on}\;\partial B_{2R}(0),\end{cases}

\begin{split}0\leq\omega_{i}(y)=&\int_{B_{2R}(0)}G(z,y)(2^{*}-1)u_{m}^{2^{*}-2}\omega_{i-1}\\
\leq&C\int_{B_{2R}(0)}\frac{1}{|y-z|^{N-2}}\frac{1}{|z-x|^{N-2(i-1)}}\,dz\leq\frac{C}{|y-x|^{N-2i}}.\end{split}

Let
i
be large satisfying
\omega_{i}\in L^{\infty}(B_{2R}(0))
. Define

\omega=\sum_{l=1}^{i}\omega_{l},

and
\upsilon=G_{m}(y,x)-\xi\omega^{\star}
, where
\xi(y)=\xi(|y|)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{2R}(0))
,
\xi=1
in

and

. Then we have

(B.2)

where

and

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2R}(0)
. Applying Proposition
2.1
, (
B.2
)

.

First, we claim that

.
Indeed, assume that there are

,
\upsilon_{n}
satisfying (
B.2
), with
|g_{n}|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0

. Then,
\upsilon_{n}\to\upsilon

On a Unified and Simplified Proof for the Ergodic Convergence Rates of PPM, PDHG and ADMM
Haihao Lu, Jinwen Yang

Abstract
We present a unified viewpoint of proximal point method (PPM), primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) and alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) for solving convex-concave primal-dual problems. This viewpoint shows the equivalence of these three algorithms upto a norm change, and it leads to a four-line simple proof of their 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}(1/k) ergodic rates. The simple proof technique is not limited to these three algorithms, but can also be utilized to analyze related algorithms, such as gradient descent, linearized PDHG, inexact algorithms, just to name a few.

Introduction

We study three classic algorithms, proximal point method (PPM), primal-dual hybrid gradient method (PDHG) and alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), for solving a convex-concave primal-dual problem,

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\max_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\;L(x,\lambda)\ ,

(1)

where
L(x,\lambda)
is lower semicontinuous convex in the primal variable
x
, upper semicontinuous concave in the dual variable
\lambda
. Such convex-concave primal-dual problems and their primal and/or dual forms have various applications in image processing, statistics, conic programming, etc.

While PDHG and ADMM are extensively studied in the optimization literature and widely used in practice, the proofs of their
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
ergodic convergence rate are still viewed as “technical” with non-trivial mathematical manipulations. These proofs make it difficult to gain intuitions about the extrapolation steps in PDHG and the carefully-chosen cyclic rule in ADMM and to extend these algorithms in other settings.
In this paper, we provide a unified viewpoint of PPM, PDHG and ADMM. This viewpoint clearly shows that PDHG and ADMM are variants of PPM with different choices of norms. Furthermore, this viewpoint leads to a four-line simple and unified proof (Theorem
1
) of the
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
ergodic rate for these three algorithms. Indeed, this viewpoint was also mentioned in
[
8
,
15
]
, which provides an approximate PPM interpretation of PDHG, but such connection was not further explored to obtain convergence analysis. Recently,
[
12
,
10
,
17
]
show the equivalence of ADMM/DRS and PDHG up to a change of norm, which can also be clearly obtained from the such interpretation.

We believe this perspective provides a fundamental understanding of these three algorithms, which can also facilitate developing new variants of these algorithms. Indeed, this viewpoint and the convergence result is not limited to analyze these three algorithms, and can also be easily extended to analyze other first-order methods, such as gradient descent for convex optimization, linearized PDHG, inexact algorithms, just to name a few.

Main results

In this section, we present a unified and simplified proof for ergodic rates of PPM, PDHG and ADMM. We start with presenting a generic algorithm and show that PPM, PDHG and ADMM are special cases of this generic algorithm with a different choice of norm. Then we show the
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
ergodic rate of this generic algorithm. As a direct consequence, this shows the ergodic rate of the three algorithms, and show the equivalence of these algorithms upto a norm.

We denote
z=(x,\lambda)
as the primal-dual solution pair and
\mathcal{F}(z)=\mathcal{F}(x,\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x}L(x,\lambda)\\
-\partial_{\lambda}L(x,\lambda)\end{pmatrix}
as the sub-gradient of the objective (more precisely, the gradient over the primal variable
x
and the negative gradient over the dual variable
\lambda
). We consider a generic algorithm with iterate update rule:

P(z^{k}-z^{k+1})\in\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})\ ,

(5)

where
P\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+n)\times(m+n)}
is a positive semi-definite matrix.

Next, we show that the iterate updates of PPM, PDHG and ADMM all follow with (
5
) with a proper choice of matrix
P
.

Lemma 1
.

The update rule of PPM (Algorithm
1
) for the primal-dual problem (
1
) is an instance of (
5
)
with
P=\frac{1}{\eta}I
.

Proof.

This can be obtained directly from the first-order stationary condition of the update rule of PPM.
∎

Lemma 2
.

The update rule of PDHG (Algorithm
2
) for the primal-dual problem (
2
) is an instance of (
5
)
with
P=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\eta}I&A^{T}\\
A&\frac{1}{\eta}I\end{pmatrix}
.

Proof.

Notice that we have
\mathcal{F}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial f(x)-A^{T}\lambda\\
\partial g^{*}(\lambda)+Ax\end{pmatrix}
for the objective
L(x,\lambda)
defined in (
2
). The first-order optimality condition of update rules in Algorithm
2
are

\displaystyle\begin{split}&0\in\partial f(x^{k+1})+\frac{1}{\eta}(x^{k+1}-x^{k}-\eta A^{T}\lambda^{k})\\
&0\in\partial g^{*}(\lambda^{k+1})+\frac{1}{\eta}(\lambda^{k+1}-\lambda^{k}+\eta A(2x^{k+1}-x^{k}))\ .\end{split}

Rearranging the above update rules, we reach at

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\eta}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})+A^{T}(\lambda^{k}-\lambda^{k+1})\in\partial f(x^{k+1})-A^{T}\lambda^{k+1}\\
&\frac{1}{\eta}(\lambda^{k}-\lambda^{k+1})+A(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\in\partial g^{*}(\lambda^{k+1})+Ax^{k+1}\end{split}

that is,

P(z^{k}-z^{k+1})\in\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})\ .

∎

Lemma 3
.

The update rule of ADMM (Algorithm
3
) for the primal-dual problem (
4
) is an instance of (
5
)
with
P=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\eta A^{T}A&-A^{T}\\
0&-A&\frac{1}{\eta}I\end{pmatrix}
.

Proof.

Notice that we have
\mathcal{F}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial g(y^{k+1})-B^{T}\lambda^{k+1}\\
\partial f(x^{k+1})-A^{T}\lambda^{k+1}\\
Ax^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b\end{pmatrix}
for the objective
L(x,y,\lambda)
defined in (
4
). By utilizing the first-order optimality conditions, we can rewrite the three update rules in Algorithm
2
on
y^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1},x^{k+1}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&\in\partial g(y^{k+1})+\eta B^{T}(Ax^{k}+By^{k+1}-b-\frac{1}{\eta}\lambda^{k})=\partial g(y^{k+1})+\eta B^{T}\frac{1}{\eta}(\lambda^{k}-\lambda^{k+1}-\lambda^{k})=\partial g(y^{k+1})-B^{T}\lambda^{k+1}\ ,\end{split}

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\eta}(\lambda^{k}-\lambda^{k+1})-A(x^{k}-x^{k+1})=By^{k+1}+Ax^{k+1}-b\ ,\end{split}

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&\in\partial f(x^{k+1})+\eta A^{T}(Ax^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b-\frac{1}{\eta}\lambda^{k+1})=\partial f(x^{k+1})+\eta A^{T}(Ax^{k+1}-Ax^{k}+\frac{1}{\eta}\lambda^{k}-\frac{2}{\eta}\lambda^{k+1})\ .\end{split}

(8)

Rearranging (
6
), (
8
) and (
7
), we reach

P(z^{k}-z^{k+1})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\eta A^{T}A&-A^{T}\\
0&-A&\frac{1}{\eta}I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}y^{k}-y^{k+1}\\
x^{k}-x^{k+1}\\
\lambda^{k}-\lambda^{k+1}\end{pmatrix}\in\begin{pmatrix}\partial g(y^{k+1})-B^{T}\lambda^{k+1}\\
\partial f(x^{k+1})-A^{T}\lambda^{k+1}\\
Ax^{k+1}+By^{k+1}-b\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})\ .

∎

The above three lemmas clearly show the connections and differences among PPM, ADMM and PDHG: they share very similar update rules (i.e., (
5
)) with a different choice of the matrix
P
. Indeed, these
P
norm matrices appear in the analysis of ADMM
[
9
]
and PDHG
[
5
]
without many intuitions, and our viewpoint of (
5
) make clear where they come from.

The next theorem is our main result, which shows the ergodic rate of any algorithm with update rule (
5
) when
P
is positive semi-definite.

Theorem 1
.

Consider an iterate update rule (
5
) with a positive semi-definite matrix
P
for solving a convex-concave primal-dual problem (
1
). The iterates
\{z^{k}=(x^{k},\lambda^{k})\}_{k=1,...,\infty}
are obtained from this iterate update rule and the initial solution
z^{0}=(x^{0},\lambda^{0})
. Denote
\bar{z}^{k}=(\bar{x}^{k},\bar{\lambda}^{k})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}z^{i}
as the average iterate. Then it holds for any
k\geq 1
and a primal-dual solution
z=(x,\lambda)
that

L(\bar{x}^{k},\lambda)-L(x,\bar{\lambda}^{k})\leq\frac{\|z-z^{0}\|^{2}_{P}}{2k}\ .

Proof.

Denote
w^{k+1}=P(z^{k}-z^{k+1})\in\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})
. It then holds by convexity-concavity of
L(x,\lambda)
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})\ =L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})+L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})\\
\leq&\left\langle w^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\right\rangle\ =\left\langle z^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\right\rangle_{P}=\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{P}^{2}\\
\ \leq&\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{P}^{2}\ ,\end{split}

(9)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{P}
is a semi-norm. We finish the proof by noticing

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(\bar{x}^{k},\lambda)-L(x,\bar{\lambda}^{k})\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}L(x^{i+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{i+1})\leq\frac{1}{2k}\|z^{0}-z\|_{P}^{2}\ ,\end{split}

where the first inequality comes from convexity-concavity of
L(x,\lambda)
and the telescoping using (
9
).
∎

3
Extensions

The update rules of PPM, PDHG and ADMM require solving a proximal (implicit) oracle, because the right-hand-side of (
5
) depends on
z^{k+1}
. This step in general can be computationally expensive. In this section, we discuss two types of extensions to avoid this issue: linearized methods and inexact methods.

3.1
Linearized methods

Instead of using
F(z^{k+1})
in (
5
), we consider a generic update rule:

P(z^{k}-z^{k+1})\in F^{k+1}\ ,

(11)

where
P\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+n)\times(m+n)}
is a positive semi-definite matrix and
F^{k+1}
is an approximation to the sub-differential
\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})
so that (
11
) is inexpensive to solve.

Two economic examples of the linearized methods are gradient descent for minimization problems and linearized PDHG for primal-dual problems.

•

Gradient descent
[
11
]
.
Consider a minimization problem
\min_{x}f(x)
where
f(x)
is a convex and
L
-smooth function (i.e.,
\nabla f(x)
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous). Gradient descent has update rule
x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\eta\nabla f(x^{k})
, where
\eta
is the step-size of the algorithm. It is easy to see that gradient descent is an instance of (
11
) with
z^{k}=x^{k}
(one can view that the Lagrangian function
L(x,y)
is independent of
y
),
P=\frac{1}{\eta}I
and
F^{k+1}=\nabla f(x^{k})
.

•

Linearized PDHG
[
5
]
.
Linearized PDHG, which is formally stated in Algorithm
4
, solves (
2
) without the need of solving proximal oracles. Similar to the proof of Lemma
2
, we can rewrite the update rule of linearized PDHG as an instance of (
11
) with
P=\frac{1}{\eta}I
and
F^{k+1}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla f(x^{k})-A^{T}\lambda^{k+1}\\
\nabla g^{*}(\lambda^{k})+Ax^{k+1}\end{pmatrix}
.

To guarantee the convergence property of the algorithms,
F^{k+1}
should well approximate
\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})
, which is formalized in the following assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

Consider iterates
\{z^{k}=(x^{k},\lambda^{k})\}
from algorithm with update rule (
11
).

(i)

There exists a matrix
E\succeq 0
such that for any
z=(x,y)
,

L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})\leq\langle F^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{E}^{2}\ .

(12)

(ii)

It holds that
P\succeq E
.

When
F^{k+1}=F(z^{k+1})
, Assumption
1

(i)
holds with
E=0
. The matrix
E
, in principle, measures the difference between
F^{k+1}
and
F(z^{k+1})
. Assumption
1

(ii)
indicates that such difference (i.e.
E
) is not too large compared to the matrix
P
.

The next theorem shows that an algorithm with update rule (
11
) has
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
sublinear rate under Assumption
1
:

Theorem 2
.

Consider an iterate update rule (
11
) with a positive semi-definite matrix
P
for solving a convex-concave primal-dual problem (
2
). The iterates
\{z^{k}=(x^{k},\lambda^{k})\}_{k=1,...,\infty}
are obtained from this iterate update rule (
11
), and the initial solution
z^{0}=(x^{0},\lambda^{0})
. Denote
\bar{z}^{k}=(\bar{x}^{k},\bar{\lambda}^{k})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}z^{i}
as the average iterate. Suppose Assumption
1
holds. Then it holds for any
k\geq 1
and a primal-dual solution
z=(x,\lambda)
that

L(\bar{x}^{k},\lambda)-L(x,\bar{\lambda}^{k})\leq\frac{\|z-z^{0}\|^{2}_{P}}{2k}\ .

Proof.

It holds by Assumption
1
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})&\ \leq\langle F^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{E}^{2}=\langle z^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle_{P}+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{E}^{2}\\
&\ =\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{P}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{E}^{2}\\
&\ \leq\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{P}^{2}\ ,\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from
P\succeq E
. We finish the proof by noticing

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(\bar{x}^{k},\lambda)-L(x,\bar{\lambda}^{k})\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}L(x^{i+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{i+1})\leq\frac{1}{2k}\|z^{0}-z\|_{P}^{2}\ .\end{split}

∎

As a direct consequence of Theorem

Corollary 4
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&f(x^{k+1})-f(x)\ =f(x^{k+1})-f(x^{k})+f(x^{k})-f(x)\\
\ \leq&\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}+\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x\rangle=\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-x\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}\ ,\end{split}

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})&=g^{*}(\lambda^{k+1})-g^{*}(\lambda)-(x^{k+1})^{T}A^{T}(\lambda-\lambda^{k+1})\\
&\leq(\nabla g^{*}(\lambda^{k+1})+Ax^{k+1})^{T}(\lambda^{k+1}-\lambda)+\frac{L}{2}\|\lambda^{k}-\lambda^{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}\ ,\end{split}

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})&=f(x^{k+1})-f(x)-(\lambda^{k+1})^{T}A(x^{k+1}-x)\\
&\leq(\nabla f(x^{k})-A^{T}\lambda^{k+1})^{T}(x^{k+1}-x)+\frac{L}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}\ .\end{split}

(15)

Combine (
14
) and (
15
) and we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})&=L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})+L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})\\
&\leq\langle F^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}\ ,\end{split}

which implies Assumption
1

(i)
holds with
E=LI
. Furthermore, with the choice of stepsize
\eta\leq\frac{1}{L+\|A\|_{2}}
, it holds that
P-E=\begin{pmatrix}\left({\frac{1}{\eta}-L}\right)I&A^{T}\\
A&\left({\frac{1}{\eta}-L}\right)I\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0
. The result follows from application of Theorem
2
.
∎

3.2
Inexact methods

Another typical way to avoid the potentially-expensive proximal steps is to update the iterates by inexactly solving (
5
). In this section, we consider inexact methods with updates,

\mathcal{P}(z^{k}-z^{k+1})+\epsilon^{k+1}\in\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})\ ,

(16)

where
\epsilon^{k+1}
is the error for iteration
k+1
. The next theorem shows that as long as the total error,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\epsilon^{i}\|_{2}
is controllable, then the inexact algorithm obtains a reasonable convergence guarantee when the constraint set of the problem is bounded.

Theorem 3
.

Consider an iterate update rule (
16
) with a positive semi-definite matrix
P
for solving a convex-concave primal-dual problem with bounded region
Z
. Denote
D
the diameter of
Z
. The iterates
\{z^{k}=(x^{k},\lambda^{k})\}_{k=1,...,\infty}
are obtained form the iterate update rule (
16
) and the initial solution
z^{0}=(x^{0},\lambda^{0})
. Denote
\bar{z}^{k}=(\bar{x}^{k},\bar{\lambda}^{k})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}z^{i}
as the average iterate. Then it holds for any
k\geq 1
and a primal-dual solution
z=(x,\lambda)\in Z
that

L(\bar{x}^{k},\lambda)-L(x,\bar{\lambda}^{k})\leq\frac{\|z-z^{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{P}}}{2k}+\frac{D\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\epsilon^{i}\|_{2}}{k}\ .

Remark 2
.

Utilizing Theorem
3
, we can obtain the convergence guarantees for inexact PDHG and inexact ADMM. Notice that
\epsilon^{i}
is essentially the error in (sub-)gradient of the minimization sub-problem. One can utilize standard first-order methods (such as gradient descent and accelerated gradient descent) for the sub-problem with carefully chosen tolerance
\epsilon^{i}
so that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\epsilon^{i}\|_{2}
is bounded.

Proof.

Denote
w^{k+1}=(w_{x}^{k+1},w_{\lambda}^{k+1})\in\mathcal{F}(z^{k+1})
. It then holds by convexity-concavity of
L(x,\lambda)
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})\ =L(x^{k+1},\lambda)-L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})+L(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1})-L(x,\lambda^{k+1})\\
\leq&\left\langle w^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\right\rangle\ =\left\langle z^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\right\rangle_{P}+\langle\epsilon^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z^{k+1}\|_{P}^{2}+\langle\epsilon^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle\\
\ \leq&\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{P}^{2}+\langle\epsilon^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle\\
\ \leq&\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k}-z\|_{P}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{P}^{2}+D\|\epsilon^{k+1}\|_{2}\ ,\end{split}

(17)

where the last inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequlity that
\langle\epsilon^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z\rangle\leq\|\epsilon^{k+1}\|_{2}\|z^{k+1}-z\|_{2}
and
\|z^{k}-z\|_{2}\leq D
by the definition of
D
as diameter of
Z
. We finish the proof by noticing

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(\bar{x}^{k},\lambda)-L(x,\bar{\lambda}^{k})\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}L(x^{i+1},\lambda)-L(x,\lambda^{i+1})\leq\frac{1}{2k}\|z^{0}-z\|_{P}^{2}+\frac{D\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\epsilon^{i}\|_{2}}{k}\ ,\end{split}

where the first inequality comes from convexity-concavity of
L(x,\lambda)
and the second is from the telescoping.
∎

On Parametric Amplification In Discrete Josephson Transmission Line
Eugene Kogan

Abstract
We consider the discrete series-connected lossy Josephson transmission line, constructed from Josephson junctions, capacitors and resistors (one-dimensional array of Josephson junctions). We derive equations describing pump, signal, and idler interaction in the system and calculate the thresholds for the parametric amplification.

II
RSJ Josephson transmission line

We consider a model of the JTL presented in Fig.
1
.

Fig. 1:
Josephson transmission line composed of superconducting grains

We take
as the dynamical variables Josephson phases
and the potentials of the grains
V_{n}
. The circuit equations are
[
23
]

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2e}\frac{d\varphi_{n}}{dt}=V_{n-1}-V_{n},

(1a)

\displaystyle C\frac{dV_{n}}{dt}+GV_{n}=\partial^{2}_{n}\left(\frac{V_{n}}{R_{J}}+C_{J}\frac{dV_{n}}{dt}\right)

\displaystyle+I_{c}\left[\sin\left(\varphi_{n}+\varphi_{0}\right)-\sin\left(\varphi_{n+1}+\varphi_{0}\right)\right].

(1b)

where
C
is the ground capacitance,
I_{c}
is the critical current of the JJ,

G
is the conductance of the ohmic resistor shunting the ground
capacitor,
R_{J}
is the ohmic resistor shunting the JJ;
the second-order discrete
differential operator
\partial^{2}_{n}
is defined as

\partial^{2}_{n}f_{n}\equiv f_{n-1}-2f_{n}+f_{n+1}
.
For the sake of generality we consider d.c. background Josephson current
I_{c}\sin\left(\varphi_{0}\right)
flowing along the JTL,
where
\varphi_{0}
is the d.c. Josephson phase.

Inclusion of the capacitance in parallel to the Josephson elements (inter-island capacitance) would certainly make the model more physically realistic. However it complicates the mathematics (there appears the fourth order derivative in the final equation). In spite of this the parallel capacitance was included in our previous paper dealing with parametric amplification in the JTL in the continuum approximation
[
23
]
. It turned out that such generalization of the model doesn’t lead to any qualitative differences. So in the present paper we decided to give the priority to simplicity over generality.

We can exclude
V_{n}
and obtain closed equation for
\varphi_{n}

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\varphi_{n}}{d\tau^{2}}+Z_{J}G\frac{d\varphi_{n}}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=\partial^{2}_{n}\left[\sin\left(\varphi_{n}+\varphi_{0}\right)+\frac{Z_{J}}{R_{J}}\frac{d\varphi_{n}}{d\tau}+\frac{C_{J}}{C}\frac{d^{2}\varphi_{n}}{d\tau^{2}}\right],

(2)

where we introduced the dimensionless time
\tau=t/\sqrt{L_{J}C}
,

Z_{J}\equiv\sqrt{L_{J}/C}
is the characteristic impedance of the JTL, and
L_{J}=\hbar/(2eI_{c})
.
Let us present (
II
) as

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\varphi_{n}}{d\tau^{2}}-\partial_{n}^{2}\left(\cos\varphi_{0}\cdot\varphi_{n}+\frac{C_{J}}{C}\frac{d^{2}\varphi_{n}}{d\tau^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{n}^{2}\left\{\frac{Z_{J}}{R_{J}}\frac{d\varphi_{n}}{d\tau}+n.l.[\sin\left(\varphi_{n}+\varphi_{0}\right)]\right\}-Z_{J}G\frac{d\varphi_{n}}{d\tau},

(3)

where n.l. stands for the non-linear terms of expansion of the sine function in Taylor series.

If we ignore the dissipation and the non-linear terms, Eq. (
II
) has obvious solution

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}e^{i(k_{\alpha}n-\omega_{\alpha}\tau)}+c.c.,

(4)

where

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}^{2}=\frac{4\cos\varphi_{0}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k_{\alpha}}{2}\right)}{1+\frac{4C_{J}}{C}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k_{\alpha}}{2}\right)},

(5)

and

A_{\alpha}
are arbitrary constant amplitudes.
The nonlinearity and the dissipation we’ll take into account approximately, by changing (
4
) to

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}(n)e^{i(k_{\alpha}n-\omega_{\alpha}\tau)}+c.c.

(6)

and assuming that
the complex amplitudes
A_{\alpha}(n)
slowly change with
n
.
Additionally, because we consider the dissipation and nonlinear terms
as being in some sense small, while calculating the discrete second order derivative of the terms in the parenthesis in (
II
)
we will ignore the corrections which come from the
n
-dependence of the amplitudes, thus the discrete second derivative operator acting on a partial wave with the wave vector
k
just multiplies this partial wave by
-4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)
. For example,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\varphi_{n-1}-2\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle=-2i\sum_{\alpha}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\omega_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}e^{i(k_{\alpha}n-\omega_{\alpha}\tau)}+c.c.,

(7)

However, we’ll do better while calculating the discrete second derivative standing in the l.h.s. of (
II
).
We promote
n
, as argument of the amplitudes, to the continuous variable
Z
and approximate

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(n\pm 1)=A_{\alpha}(Z)\pm\frac{\partial A_{\alpha}(Z)}{\partial Z}.

(8)

As the result, for the discrete second derivative we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi_{n-1}-2\varphi_{n}+\varphi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{\alpha}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k_{\alpha}}{2}\right)A_{\alpha}e^{i(\omega_{\alpha}\tau-k_{\alpha}n)}+c.c.,

\displaystyle+i\sum_{\alpha}\sin(k_{\alpha})\frac{\partial A_{\alpha}}{\partial Z}e^{i(k_{\alpha}n-\omega_{\alpha}\tau)}+c.c.

(9)

III-A

Coupled equations for the amplitudes

Now let us take into account the nonlinear terms.
Consider the case
\varphi_{0}\sim 1
and
\left|\varphi_{n}\right|\ll 1

Expanding the sine function in the r.h.s. of (
II
) in Taylor series and keeping the first two terms of the expansion we have

\displaystyle n.l.\left[\sin\left(\varphi_{n}+\varphi_{0}\right)\right]=-\frac{\sin\varphi_{0}}{2}\varphi_{n}^{2}.

(10)

After we substitute (
4

10

we’ll consider a superposition of only three waves (pump,
signal, and idler), with the frequencies
\omega_{p}
,
\omega_{s}
and
\omega_{i}
respectively, satisfying equation

\displaystyle\omega_{s}+\omega_{i}=\omega_{p}.

(11)

Keeping only the terms which have

\omega_{p,s,i}t

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}A_{s}A_{i}e^{i[(k_{s}+k_{i})n-\omega_{p}t]}+\frac{1}{2}A_{p}A_{i}^{*}e^{i[(k_{p}-k_{i})n-\omega_{s}t]}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}A_{p}A_{s}^{*}e^{i[(k_{p}-k_{s})n-\omega_{i}t]}+c.c.

(12)

Finally, collective all the perturbative terms in (
II
) we
obtain coupled equations for the amplitudes

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{p}}{dZ}+\nu_{p}A_{p}

\displaystyle=i\mu_{p}A_{s}A_{i}e^{i\Delta k\cdot Z}

(13a)

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{s,i}}{dZ}+\nu_{s}A_{s,i}

\displaystyle=i\mu_{s,i}A_{p}A_{i,s}^{*}e^{-i\Delta k\cdot Z},

(13b)

where

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tan(k_{\alpha}/2)}{\omega_{\alpha}}\left[Z_{J}G+4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\frac{Z_{J}}{R_{J}}\right]

(14a)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sin\varphi_{0}\cdot\tan(k_{p}/2)}{\omega_{p}^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k_{s}+k_{i}}{2}\right)

(14b)

\displaystyle\mu_{s,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sin\varphi_{0}\cdot\tan(k_{s,i}/2)}{\omega_{s,i}^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{k_{p}-k_{i,s}}{2}\right)

(14c)

\displaystyle\Delta k

\displaystyle=k_{s}+k_{i}-k_{p}.

(14d)

Comparing Eqs. (

) with the appropriate equations from our previous publication

, we see that
the basic equations systems in the discrete

Only the parameters of the systems are different

III-B

Weak signal: threshold for the parametric amplification

Let us solve Eq. (
13
) in the small (relative to the pump) signal and idler approximation (
|A_{s}|,|A_{i}|\ll|A_{p}|
). In this approximation the
equation for
A_{p}
becomes decoupled from the other two and takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{p}}{dZ}+\nu_{p}A_{p}=0,

(15)

with the obvious solution

\displaystyle A_{p}(Z)=e^{-\nu_{p}Z}A_{p}^{0}.

(16)

Treating the other two equations, we’ll introduce
the local approximation, by treating
A_{p}
in the r.h.s. of Eqs. (
13b
) as a constant. In this approximation the solutions for
A_{s},A_{i}
are

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{s}\\
A_{i}\end{array}\right)=e^{-i\Delta k/2\cdot Z}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dots e^{KZ}\\
\dots e^{K^{*}Z}\end{array}\right),

where dots stand for some constant amplitudes, and
K
is one of the roots of the characteristic polynomial

\displaystyle P(K)\equiv K^{2}+(\nu_{s}+\nu_{i})K

\displaystyle+(\nu_{s}-i\Delta k/2)(\nu_{i}+i\Delta k/2)-\mu_{s}\mu_{i}\left|A_{p}\right|^{2}.

(22)

Parametric amplification of the signal takes place when
one of the roots have positive real part. The boundary between the parametric amplification and no parametric amplification we can find by demanding that
K
for that boundary is purely imaginary. So,
after simple algebra, we obtain
the condition for the parametric amplification:

\displaystyle\mu_{s}\mu_{i}\left|A_{p}\right|^{2}>\nu_{s}\nu_{p}\left[1+\frac{\left(\Delta k\right)^{2}}{(\nu_{s}+\nu_{i})^{2}}\right].

(23)

Looking at Eq. (
23
) we realise that the momenta mismatch
\Delta k
acts in some sense similar to the losses in the system. Both factors together define the threshold for the parametric amplification.

Though it’s not completely consistent, we bring back the coordinate dependence of
A_{p}
, given by Eq. (
16
),
and consider (
23
) as a local condition for the parametric amplification.

Small wave vectors limiting case

Consider the limiting case
k_{p},k_{s},k_{i}\ll 1
. In this case
\Delta k=0
. We assume additionally that
R_{J}=\infty
, hence
\nu_{\alpha}=\nu
(doesn’t depend upon
\alpha

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{p}}{dZ}+\nu A_{p}

\displaystyle=i\mu_{p}A_{s}A_{i}

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{s,i}}{dZ}+\nu A_{s,i}

\displaystyle=i\mu_{s,i}A_{p}A_{i,s}^{*},

(24b)

\displaystyle\mu_{p}=\mu_{s}+\mu_{i}.

(25)

We’ll present the amplitudes as

\displaystyle A_{p}=ie^{-\nu Z}A_{p}^{(r)},\hskip 14.22636ptA_{s,i}=e^{-\nu Z}A_{s,i}^{(r)}.

In the new variables Eqs. (

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{p}^{(r)}}{d\tilde{Z}}

\displaystyle=\mu_{p}A_{s}^{(r)}A_{i}^{(r)}

(27a)

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{s,i}^{(r)}}{d\tilde{Z}}

\displaystyle=-\mu_{s,i}A_{p}^{(r)}A_{i,s}^{(r)},

(27b)

where

\displaystyle d\tilde{Z}\equiv e^{-\nu Z}dZ,

(28)

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}=K-\frac{1}{\nu}e^{-\nu Z},

(29)

K
is an arbitrary constant.

We’ll consider only the real solutions, postponing the general analysis until later.
From (
27a
), (

) follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tilde{Z}}\left[{A^{(r)}_{p}}^{2}+{A^{(r)}_{s}}^{2}+{A^{(r)}_{i}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\mu_{p}-\mu_{s}-\mu_{i}\right)A_{p}^{(r)}A_{s}^{(r)}A_{i}^{(r)}.

(30)

Due to (

\displaystyle A_{p}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=B\cos\theta

(32a)

\displaystyle A_{s}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=B\sin\theta\cos\phi

(32b)

\displaystyle A_{i}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=B\sin\theta\sin\phi,

IV-C

Threshold for the parametric amplification

Equations (
42b
) in the weak signal approximation can be written down as

\displaystyle\frac{dA_{s,i}}{dZ}+\left(\nu_{s,i}-i\gamma_{s,i}|A_{p}|^{2}\right)A_{s,i}=i\eta_{s,i}A_{p}^{2}A_{i,s}^{*}e^{-i\Delta k\cdot Z}.

(54)

In the framework of the local approximation, we treat
A_{p}
in the r.h.s. of Eqs. (

(

K
this time is one of the roots of the characteristic polynomial

\displaystyle P(K)\equiv K^{2}+(\nu_{s}+\nu_{i})K+(\nu_{s}-2i\gamma_{s}|A_{p}|^{2}-i\Delta k/2)

\displaystyle\cdot(\nu_{i}+2i\gamma_{i}|A_{p}|^{2}+i\Delta k/2)-\eta_{s}\eta_{i}\left|A_{p}\right|^{4}.

(55)

We again find the boundary between the parametric amplification and no amplification by demanding that
K
for that boundary is purely imaginary. So,
after simple algebra, we obtain
the condition for the parametric amplification:

\displaystyle\eta_{s}\eta_{i}\left|A_{p}\right|^{4}>\nu_{s}\nu_{i}\left\{1+\frac{\left[\Delta k+2(\gamma_{s}+\gamma_{i})\left|A_{p}\right|^{2}\right]^{2}}{(\nu_{s}+\nu_{i})^{2}}\right\}.

(56)

Here we can only repeat what was said after Eq. (
23

Again, we bring back the coordinate dependence of
A_{p}
, given by Eq. (
44
)
and consider (
56
) as a local condition for the parametric amplification.

V
Discussion

The present paper generalized the previously obtained results
on parametric amplification in JTL
[
5
,
11
,
25
]
to the discrete model of the line. The discrete character of the line leads to the renormalization of the parameters of the coupled amplitude equations.

We formulate the local approximation which allows explicitly find the
threshold for parametric amplification in the case of weak signal.
In addition we present the exact particular solution for the coupled equations for the pump, signal and idler amplitudes in the particular case of small wave vectors and
present the solution graphically.
Finally, we would like to mention that
in real case of
the line of finite length, the frequency dependence of the line impedance leads to matching
problems and hence to the wave reflection (e.g.,
[
26
]
). However we leave the study of the problem until later.

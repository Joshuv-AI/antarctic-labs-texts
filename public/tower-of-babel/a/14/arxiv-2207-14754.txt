The Morrison–Kawamata cone conjecture for singular symplectic varieties
Christian Lehn, Giovanni Mongardi, Gianluca Pacienza

Abstract
. We prove the Morrison–Kawamata cone conjecture for projective primitive symplectic varieties with ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -factorial and terminal singularities with b 2 ≥ 5 b_{2}\geq 5 , from which we derive for instance the finiteness of minimal models of such varieties, up to isomorphisms. To prove the conjecture we establish along the way some results on the monodromy group which may be interesting in their own right, such as the fact that reflections in prime exceptional divisors are integral Hodge monodromy operators which, together with monodromy operators provided by birational transformations, yield a semidirect product decomposition of the monodromy group of Hodge isometries.

14J50, 14E07 (primary), 32J27, 51F15, 53C26, 14B07 (secondary).

1.
Introduction

The celebrated Cone Theorem says that for e.g. a smooth, projective variety
X
the
K_{X}
-negative part of the cone of curves of
X
is
locally rational polyhedral and extremal
K_{X}
-negative rays yield contraction morphisms (see e.g.
[
KM98
, Theorem 3.7]
). Getting information about the part of the cone where
K_{X}
is nonnegative is much more complicated. For instance
K3
surfaces may have round cone of curves or contain infinitely many
(-2)
-curves (see
[
Tot12
]
).
The Morrison–Kawamata cone conjecture provides a beautiful conjectural explanation for those apparent pathologies in terms of the action of the automorphism group.

Morrison
[
Mor93
,
Mor96
]
gave the original statement of the cone conjecture for Calabi–Yau threefolds. The statement was then generalised by Kawamata in
[
Kaw97
]
to families of varieties with numerically trivial canonical bundle, and from there to the so-called klt Calabi–Yau pairs by Totaro, cf.
[
Tot10
]
.
We state it here without boundary (cf.
[
Kaw97
, Conjecture 1.12]
and
[
Tot10
, Conjecture 1.1]
for pairs). Recall that a
K
-trivial fiber space
is a proper surjective morphism
f:X\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
with connected fibers between normal varieties such that
X
has
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and terminal singularities and
K_{X}
is zero in
N^{1}(X/S)
.

Conjecture 1.1

(The Morrison-Kawamata Cone conjecture)
.

Let
X\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
be a
K
-trivial fiber space.

(1)

There exists a rational polyhedral cone
\Pi
which is a fundamental domain for the
action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X/S)
on
{\operatorname{Nef}}^{e}(X/S):={\operatorname{Nef}}(X/S)\cap{\operatorname{Eff}}(X/S)
in the sense that

(a)

{\operatorname{Nef}}^{e}(X/S)=\cup_{g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X/S)}g^{*}\Pi
.

(b)

int(\Pi)\cap int(g^{*}\Pi)=\emptyset
, unless
g^{*}=id
in
\operatorname{GL}(N^{1}(X/S))
.

(2)

There exists a rational polyhedral cone
\Pi^{\prime}
which is a fundamental domain in the sense above for the
action of
{\operatorname{Bir}}(X/S)
on
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}^{e}(X/S):=\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}(X/S)\cap{\operatorname{Eff}}(X/S)
.

Item (2) above is also known as the birational Cone conjecture.

The conjecture was proved for
K3
surfaces by Sterk
[
Ste85
]
, checked for the other
K
-trivial smooth surfaces by Yu. Namikawa in
[
Nam85
]
(see also
[
Kaw97
]
) and more recently extended to two-dimensional klt pairs by Totaro
[
Tot10
]
.
The conjecture has been proved for abelian varieties by Prendergast-Smith
[
PS12
]
. Note that in this case the birational and the automorphism version coincide. Kawamata proved the cone conjecture for all 3-dimensional
K
-trivial fiber
spaces over a positive-dimensional base
[
Kaw97
]
. For Calabi–Yau 3-folds, there are results by Oguiso
[
Ogu01
]
, Szendröi
[
Sze99
]
,
Uehara
[
Ueh04
]
and Wilson
[
Wil94
]
, but we are far from a complete solution, see
[
LOP18
]
for an account and for further references therein.

For irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds, one often considers the analogous conjecture where
{\operatorname{Nef}}^{e}(X)
is replaced by the convex hull
{\operatorname{Nef}}^{+}(X)
of
{\operatorname{Nef}}(X)\cap\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
inside
\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
(and similarly for the convex hull
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}^{+}(X)
of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}(X)\cap\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
). It is easy to see that
{\operatorname{Nef}}^{e}(X)\subset{\operatorname{Nef}}^{+}(X)
(see
[
MY15
, Remark 1.4]
), the other inclusion follows from the SYZ-conjecture and thus holds for all known examples. Therefore, up to the validity of the SYZ-conjecture this is just a different formulation.
The cone conjecture has been solved completely for irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds, due to work of Markman
[
Mar11
]
, Markman–Yoshioka
[
MY15
]
and Amerik–Verbitsky
[
AV15
,
AV17
,
AV20
]
, following a strategy that can be traced back to Sterk
[
Ste85
]
.

In this paper we concentrate on singular holomorphic symplectic varieties, building upon the above works and pursuing, as in the smooth case, Sterk’s strategy.

There are multiple reasons to study the cone conjecture for singular holomorphic symplectic varieties. First of all, the latter are together with complex tori and singular Calabi–Yau varieties the building blocks of “mildly singular” varieties with trivial first Chern class (see
[
GKKP11
,
DG18
,
Dru18
,
GGK19
,
HP19
,
BGL22
]
), which are themselves the conjectural output of the MMP for smooth projective varieties with zero Kodaira dimension.

Secondly, for almost all choices of Mukai vectors, moduli spaces of sheaves with that fixed Mukai vector on symplectic surfaces are singular and give rise to singular holomorphic symplectic varieties (see
[
PR18
]
for all the relevant definitions and Theorem 1.19 therein for the result).

Finally, the cone conjecture is naturally related to the problem of finiteness of (marked) minimal models, see e.g.
[
MY15
, Corollary 1.5]
. Recall that a
minimal model
of a variety
X
is a variety
X^{\prime}
with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities, birational to
X
, and such that the canonical sheaf
\omega_{X^{\prime}}
is nef. A
marked minimal model
of
X
is a minimal model
X^{\prime}
together with a birational map
\phi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
. For both there is an obvious notion of isomorphism.

Finiteness of minimal models is known to hold for varieties of general type, cf.
[
BCHM10
, Corollary 1.1.5]
, while a variety of non-maximal Kodaira dimension can have infinitely many minimal models (see
[
Rei83
, Section 6.8]
,
[
Kaw97
, Example 3.8]
). Yet marked minimal models are conjectured to be finite. The MMP is conceived to produce minimal models — these are mostly singular, hence, if adjunction holds, minimal models should be fibered
f:X\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
in singular
K
-trivial varieties
X_{s}
. Therefore, the cone conjecture for possibly singular
K
-trivial fiber spaces
f:X\xrightarrow{\ \ }S
together with the abundance conjecture could lead to the finiteness of minimal models up to isomorphisms for arbitrary varieties, as it is the case for projective primitive symplectic varieties, see Corollary
1.4
below.

Our main result verifies the conjecture for projective primitive symplectic varieties (cf. Definition
2.2
), which form the largest class of singular holomophic symplectic varieties for which we have a theory similar to the one in the smooth case at our disposal.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities and assume that
b_{2}(X)\geq 5
.

(1)

The cone conjecture
1.1
, item (1), for the
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
-action on
{\operatorname{Nef}}^{+}(X)
holds for
X
.

(2)

The birational cone conjecture
1.1
, item (2), for the
{\operatorname{Bir}}(X)
-action on
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}^{+}(X)
holds for
X
.

As in
[
Tot10
]
,
[
MY15
, Corollary 1.5]
and
[
Ogu14
, Theorem 1.3]
we deduce the following consequences.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities and assume that
b_{2}(X)\geq 5
. Then the number of contractions of
X
is finite, up to automorphisms. In particular the number of Lagrangian fibrations on
X
is finite, up to automorphisms.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities and
b_{2}(X)\geq 5
. Then the number of marked minimal models of
X
is finite.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities,
\rho(X)=2
and assume that
b_{2}(X)\geq 5
. Then:

(1)

Either both boundary rays of
{\operatorname{Nef}}(X)
are rational and
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
is a finite group
or both boundary rays of
{\operatorname{Nef}}(X)
are irrational and
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
is an infinite group.
Moreover, in the second case,
{{\operatorname{Nef}}}(X)=\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}(X)=\overline{{\operatorname{Pos}}}(X)
, where
{{\operatorname{Pos}}}(X)
is the
positive cone with respect to the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form
q_{X}
, and
{\operatorname{Bir}}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
.

(2)

Either both boundary rays of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}(X)
are rational and
{\operatorname{Bir}}(X)

is a finite group or both boundary rays of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}}}(X)
are irrational and
{\operatorname{Bir}}(X)
is an
infinite group.

The hypothesis
b_{2}(X)\geq 5
in the above results is needed in order to apply the Hodge theoretic global Torelli theorem for primitive symplectic varieties (cf. Theorem
4.9
) and in order to ensure that the monodromy group has finite order inside the group of isometries of
H^{2}(X)
(cf.
[
BL18
, Theorem 8.2]
). Of course, the Cone conjecture holds trivially for primitive symplectic varieties with
b_{2}(X)=3
.

One of the keys to prove the conjecture is the study of the monodromy group and in particular the fact that reflections in prime exceptional divisors (i.e.​ prime
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisors having negative square with respect to the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki quadratic form, see Definition
2.18
)
are integral monodromy operators preserving the Hodge structure, a result that seems interesting in its own right. More generally, we summarize below in one single statement our results on the monodromy group.

.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety with terminal singularities.

(1)

(= Theorem
3.7
). Let
\pi:X\xrightarrow{\ \ }\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{X}
be a birational morphism of primitive symplectic varieties. Then the natural morphism

is
finite Galois cover whose Galois group is isomorphic to

\Pi_{B\in\mathcal{B}}W_{B}
, where

is the folded Weil group defined in (

).

(2)

(= Theorem

). Let

be a prime exceptional divisor on

R_{E}:H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\alpha\mapsto\alpha-2\frac{q(E,\alpha)}{q(E,E)}E

is integral and yields a monodromy Hodge isometry.

(3)

(= Theorem

{\mathbb{Q}}

and the group

Notice that Theorem
1.6
, item (1), which is crucial to prove item (2), is false without the terminality assumption, and there are examples, already in dimension 2, where item (2) fails, see Example
3.12
.

As for the proof of the cone conjecture, we follow the strategy adopted in the smooth case, which relies on several layers of different results proven over the years by several authors, that we try to acknowledge in the proofs. In particular, we follow Markman
[
Mar11
]
to show that the birational cone conjecture holds, and then, as in
[
MY15
]
and
[
AV17
]
, we deduce the cone conjecture from it and from a boundedness result for squares of wall divisors which we establish using Amerik’s and Verbitsky’s work on density of orbits
[
AV20
]
.

To overcome the technical difficulties that arise in the singular case we rely upon the recent progress in the moduli theory of singular symplectic varieties by B. Bakker and the first named author
[
BL18
,
BL21
]
as well as on some technical tools developed in the singular case in
[
KMPP19
,
LMP21
,
BGL22
]
.
For Theorem
1.6
, we also substantially rely on ideas and results due to Markman and Namikawa
[
Mar10
,

,

,
Nam11
]
.

Notation and Conventions

The term variety will denote an integral separated scheme of finite type over
{\mathbb{C}}
in the algebraic setting or an irreducible and reduced separated complex space in the complex analytic setting.

2.
Symplectic Varieties

We introduce the relevant classes of singular symplectic varieties and recall basic facts about their second cohomology lattice.

2.1.
Primitive Symplectic Varieties

A
symplectic form
on a smooth variety
U
is a holomorphic
2
-form
\sigma
such that
d\sigma=0
and
\sigma
defines a symplectic form in the sense of linear algebra on the tangent bundle of
U
. Recall that a
symplectic variety
in the sense of Beauville
[
Bea00
]
is a pair
(X,\sigma)
consisting of a normal (complex analytic) variety
X
and a holomorphic symplectic form
\sigma\in H^{0}(X^{\operatorname{reg}},\Omega_{X}^{2})
on the regular part
X^{\operatorname{reg}}
such that there is a resolution of singularities
\pi:Y\xrightarrow{\ \ }X
for which
\pi^{*}\sigma
extends to a holomorphic form on
Y
. Recall that an
irreducible symplectic manifold
is a simply connected compact Kähler manifold such that
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X}^{2})={\mathbb{C}}\sigma
for a symplectic form
\sigma
.

Definition 2.2
.

A normal compact Kähler variety
X
is called
primitive symplectic
if it satisfies
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
and
(H^{0}(X,(\Omega_{X}^{2})^{\vee\vee})=)\ H^{0}(X^{\operatorname{reg}},\Omega_{X}^{2})={\mathbb{C}}\sigma
, where
(X,\sigma)
is a symplectic variety.

An important feature is that the second cohomology group of a primitive symplectic variety carries a pure Hodge structure (of weight two), cf. e.g.
[
BL18
, Corollary 3.5]
.

Remark 2.3
.

(1)

For a normal variety
X
such that
X^{\operatorname{reg}}
has a symplectic form
\sigma
, having canonical singularities is in fact equivalent to Beauville’s condition
[
Bea00
]
that the pullback of
\sigma
to a resolution of
X
extends as a regular
2
-form. By
[
Elk81
]
and
[
KS21
, Corollary 1.7]
this is also equivalent to
X
having rational singularities.

(2)

Let
X
be a normal variety having a symplectic form on its regular part. A resolution of singularities
\pi:Y\xrightarrow{\ \ }X
such that the pullback of the symplectic form extends to a symplectic form on
Y
is referred to as a
symplectic resolution
. A resolution is symplectic if and only if it is crepant.

(3)

An irreducible symplectic manifold is primitive symplectic (see e.g.
[
LMP21
, Section 2]
). More generally, there is the notion of an irreducible symplectic variety, see
[
GKKP11
]
, and those are also primitive symplectic. The converse fails, take e.g. a singular Kummer K3, but a
smooth
primitive symplectic variety is irreducible symplectic by
[
Sch21
]
.

(4)

Note that for a bimeromorphic contraction
\pi:Y\xrightarrow{\ \ }X
from a primitive symplectic variety to a complex variety
X
, the variety
X
is primitive symplectic if and only if it is Kähler.

Example 2.4
.

Let
S
be a K3 surface and let
v=mw\in H^{*}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
be a Mukai vector and let
H
be
v
-general. Then, by
[
PR18
, Theorem 1.19]
the moduli space of Gieseker
H
-semistable sheaves on
S
with Mukai vector
v
is a primitive symplectic variety, if
v\not=(0,mH,0)
or
S
has Picard
rank one. We checked in
[
LMP21
, Theorem 2.12]
that for moduli spaces of objects there is no restriction on
S
or
v
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety and let
G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X)
be a finite group of automorphisms of
X
which fix the symplectic form. Then
X/G
is a primitive symplectic variety.

2.6.
The quadratic form

Recall that the second cohomology of a primitive symplectic variety carries an integral nondegenerate quadratic form
q_{X}

Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki
form (BBF form in what follows). Due to the work of many people, see
[

, §5.1]
and references therein, we know that this form shares many properties with its counterpart for irreducible symplectic manifolds. This is summarized in the following.

.

Let

be a primitive symplectic variety of dimension

and let

be the class of the symplectic form. Then

(2.1)

Remark 2.8
.

As the singularities of a primitive symplectic variety are rational, see Remark
2.3
, the pullback
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
to a resolution is injective by a well-known fact, see e.g.
[
BL21
, Lemma 2.1]
. In particular,
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion free if
X
admits an irreducible symplectic resolution. Otherwise we will sometimes have to work with the torsion free part
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})_{\mathrm{tf}}
.

Remark 2.9
.

A useful consequence of Proposition
2.7
is that we can switch back and forth between (rational) homology and cohomology in degree
2
. If
X
is primitive symplectic and
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
, we write
\alpha^{\vee}\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
for the unique class satisfying

(2.2)

\alpha^{\vee}\cdot\beta=q_{X}(\alpha,\beta)\qquad\forall\beta\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}).

This defines an isomorphism
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
,
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\vee}
. For
c\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
we will also denote by
c^{\vee}\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
the preimage of
c
under this isomorphism so that
c^{\vee\vee}=c
and
\alpha^{\vee\vee}=\alpha
. Notice that if
c
is the class of a curve, then
c^{\vee}
is a
(1,1)
-class. Note that even if
c\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
, we will only have
c^{\vee}\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
. It is worthwhile to point out that our definition of
\alpha^{\vee}
differs from that of Markman
[
Mar13
, p. 357]
by a scalar multiple, which does however not affect any argument.

2.10.
Deformation theory of primitive symplectic varieties

A lot is known about deformations of singular symplectic varieties thanks to the pioneering work of Namikawa in
[
Nam01a
,
Nam06
,
Nam08
]
. Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety. Then mainly due to work of Grauert and Douady
[
Gra74
,
Dou74
]
, there is a universal deformation
{\mathscr{X}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }{\operatorname{Def}}(X)
of
X
over a complex space germ and we refer to
{\operatorname{Def}}(X)
as the Kuranishi space of
X
, see e.g. the beginning of § 4 of
[
BL18
]
for general background on Kuranishi spaces and further references.

For a projective symplectic variety (in the sense of Beauville), Namikawa proved in
[
Nam06
, Theorem 1]
that the Kuranishi space
{\operatorname{Def}}(X)
is smooth. Note that Namikawa’s unobstructedness result is under the additional hypothesis that a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminalization exists, but this is always fulfilled by
[
BCHM10
, Corollary 1.4.3]
. Moreover, by Corollary 2 and Proposition 1 of
[
Nam06
]
if
X
admits a symplectic resolution, the universal deformation is shown to be a smoothing. Thus, looking at all deformations of
X
does not give us information about the singularities. Instead it has proven fruitful to consider
locally trivial
deformations only. We refer the reader to
[
BGL22
, § 2]
for a general reference for locally trivial deformations.

By
[
FK87
, (0.3) Corollary]
there exists a closed complex subspace
{\operatorname{Def}}^{\rm{lt}}(X)\subset{\operatorname{Def}}(X)
of the Kuranishi space parametrizing the locally trivial deformations of
X
. Its tangent space is given by
H^{1}(X,T_{X})
and by
[
BL18
, Theorem 4.7]
it is smooth of dimension
h^{1,1}(X)
. We will write

(2.3)

{\mathscr{X}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }{\operatorname{Def}}^{\rm{lt}}(X)

for the universal locally trivial family of deformations, that is, the restriction of the Kuranishi family to
{\operatorname{Def}}^{\rm{lt}}(X)
.

2.15.
Locally trivial monodromy operators

Let

be a locally trivial family of primitive symplectic varieties. By
[

, Lemma 2.4]
, the torsion free part of the second cohomology of the fibers of

forms a local system so that we have a notion of parallel transport. This is sufficient for our purposes, but it is convenient to use the following strengthening. By
[

, Proposition 5.1]
, a locally trivial deformation is even topologically trivial. Therefore, if

where

. Such operators preserve the Bogomolov–Beauville–Fujiki form.

Definition 2.16
.

Let

be a primitive symplectic variety. By varying

over all loops in all possible locally trivial families, the corresponding parallel transport operators

on

form the

. We denote it by
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}}(X)\subset{\rm O}(H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}),q_{X})
. Note that the monodromy group is contained in the orthogonal group
{\rm O}(H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}),q_{X})
as parallel transport preserves the BBF form. We denote by
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{Hdg}}}(X)\subset{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}}(X)
the group of

, that is, the subgroup of all those isometries that preserve the Hodge structure.

If

, the monodromy group has finite index in the orthogonal group of

by
[

, Theorem 8.2]
.

2.17.
Prime Exceptional Divisors

The bimeromorphic geometry of a primitive symplectic variety will be in large parts determined by the geometry of prime exceptional divisors. In the following sections we will try to transport as much as possible from what is known for irreducible symplectic manifolds to primitive symplectic varieties. Here we simply give the definition.

Definition 2.18
.

Let

be a primitive symplectic variety. A prime Weil divisor

if it is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier and

In particular, a prime exceptional divisor

has a cohomology class

.
Recall that by
[
LMP21
, Theorem 1.2, item (1)]
(which is essentially contained in
[

and
[

, Theorem 3.3]

-factorial primitive symplectic variety can be contracted on a birational model (which is a locally trivial deformation of the initial variety).
This explains why a prime exceptional divisor on a projective primitive symplectic variety is uniruled.

3.

Remark 3.9

4.
Monodromy and cones

4.1.
Monodromy operators induced by birational maps

We will show in this paragraph that birational maps between
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal projective primitive symplectic varieties induce monodromy operators. Note that for those varieties, a birational map induces a morphism between the second cohomology groups (by
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factoriality) which is an isomorphism (by terminality, cf.
[
KM98
, Corollary 3.54]
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\phi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a birational map between projective primitive symplectic varieties with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities. Then
\phi_{*}\in{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{Hdg}}}(X)
.

Proof.

By
[
BL18
, Theorem 6.16]
, there are one-parameter deformations
{\mathscr{X}}
,
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
of
X
,
X^{\prime}
over a disk
\Delta
such that
{\mathscr{X}}
and
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
are birational over
\Delta
such that the birational map is an isomorphism over the pointed disk
\Delta^{\times}
. In particular,
X
and
X^{\prime}
are inseparable in the moduli space. It follows from the proof that
{\mathscr{X}}
,
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
are projective over
\Delta
and the birational map between them induces the map
\phi
on the central fiber. Let us consider the graph
\Gamma\subset{\mathscr{X}}\times_{\Delta}{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
of the birational map. Its central fiber contains the graph of
\phi
but possibly also other things, i.e.

\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma_{\phi}+\sum_{i}Y_{i}

where
Y_{i}\subset X\times X^{\prime}
are cycles of dimension
\dim X
. By construction, the correspondence
\Gamma_{0}
acts on
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{2}(X^{\prime},{\mathbb{Q}})
by a monodromy operator. By
[
Huy99
, Corollary 5.2 item (ii)]
, the
Y_{i}
’s map to subvarieties of positive codimension in
X
and
X^{\prime}
and only if they map to a divisor, they contribute to the action of the correspondence
[\Gamma_{0}]_{*}
on
\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
.
The argument in
[
Huy03
, Proof of Theorem 2.5]
applies
verbatim
here and shows precisely that the
Y_{i}
’s map to codimension
\geq 2
. The conclusion follows.
∎

With this result, the following definition makes sense.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety. We denote by
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{bir}}}(X)
the subgroup of
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{Hdg}}}(X)
consisting of all monodromy operators induced by birational maps.

4.4.
Cones in the real Picard group

Both the birational and the ordinary cone conjecture are statements about cones in the real Picard group which we now define.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety and let
\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}:=\operatorname{Pic}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
be the real Picard group. Inside
\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
we consider the following cones:

(1)

The
ample cone

{\operatorname{Amp}}(X)
of
X
, i.e. the cone generated by all ample (integral) Cartier divisors on
X
.

(2)

The
nef cone

{\operatorname{Nef}}(X)
of
X
, i.e. the closure of the ample cone. Note that this is usually strictly bigger than the cone generated by all nef (integral) divisors).

(3)

The
movable cone

{\operatorname{Mov}}(X)
of
X
, i.e. the cone generated by all effective (integral) divisors without fixed part. This cone is neither closed nor open in general. We denote by
{{\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}}}(X)
its closure and by
{\operatorname{Mov}^{\circ}}(X)
its interior.

(4)

The
positive cone

{\operatorname{Pos}}(X)
of
X
which is the connected component containing
{\operatorname{Amp}}(X)
of the cone of positive vectors of
\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
with respect to the BBF form.

(5)

If
X
has
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities, we define the
birational ample cone

{\operatorname{BAmp}}(X)
of
X
to be the the union of all
f^{*}{\operatorname{Amp}}(X^{\prime})
where
f:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is a birational map to another primitive symplectic variety
X^{\prime}
with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities. Similarly, one defines
{\operatorname{BNef}}(X)
.

(6)

The
effective cone

{\operatorname{Eff}}(X)
of
X
which is the cone spanned by all effective divisors and the
big cone

{\operatorname{Big}}(X)
which is the cone spanned by all big divisors.

We have the following inclusions:

(4.1)

{\operatorname{Amp}}(X)\subset{\operatorname{BAmp}}(X)\subset{\operatorname{Mov}^{\circ}}(X)\subset{\operatorname{Pos}}(X).

The lefthand side inclusion is clear.
The middle inclusion follows from the fact that birational maps between terminal
K
-trivial varieties are isomorphisms in codimension one (cf.
[
KM98
, Corollary 3.54]
).
The righthand side inclusion is given in particular by the following lemma, which we record here also for later use.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety.
If
D
is a divisor in the interior of the movable cone of
X
,
then
q_{X}(D,D^{\prime})>0
for all
D^{\prime}
prime divisors on
X
.

Proof.

To start with recall that the Boucksom-Zariski decomposition holds for any effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
D
on primitive symplectic varieties, by
[
KMPP19
, Theorem 1.1]
. Moreover if
D=P+N
is such a decomposition,
for all integers
k>0
such that
kP
and
kD
are integral,
we have an isomorphism

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(kP))\cong H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(kD)),

which implies that

(4.2)

N\text{ coincides with the divisorial base locus of }D.

Therefore if
D
is a movable line bundle on
X
,
then
D
is
q_{X}
-nef (i.e.
q_{X}(D,D^{\prime})\geq 0
for all
D^{\prime}
prime divisors on
X
).
Now suppose that
q_{X}(D,D^{\prime})=0
for some prime divisor

. Since

lies in the interior of the movable cone, for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and

which yields the following contradiction

q_{X}(D-\varepsilon A,D^{\prime})=-\varepsilon q_{X}(A,D^{\prime})<0.

∎

The following lemma adds another inclusion to chain (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety. Then
{\operatorname{Pos}}(X)\subset{\operatorname{Big}}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
{\operatorname{Pos}}(X)
is open and convex, it is enough to show that rational, even integral classes in
{\operatorname{Pos}}(X)
are big. Let
\alpha
be such a class and let
L_{\alpha}
be the corresponding line bundle. Take a general one parameter deformation in the Hodge locus of
L_{\alpha}
. Then on a nearby fiber

with Picard rank one, the corresponding line bundle

is ample by the projectivity criterion
[
BL18
, Theorem 6.9]
. In particular, the dimension of

grows as a polynomial of degree

in

. By semicontinuity, sections of powers of

grow at least as much and thus
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Big}}(X)
.
∎

Furthermore, let us observe that on any projective variety
X
the big cone is contained in the effective cone

(4.3)

{\operatorname{Big}}(X)\subset{\operatorname{Eff}}(X).

4.8.
A Hodge-theoretic global Torelli

In the proof of the decomposition of the monodromy group
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{Hdg}}}(X)
as a semidirect product of
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{bir}}}(X)
with the subgroup of reflections in prime exceptional divisors we will need the following Hodge-theoretic version of the global Torelli theorem for projective primitive symplectic varieties with

-factorial terminal singularities. For the analogous statement in the smooth case, see
[

, Theorem 1.3, item (2)]
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be projective primitive symplectic varieties with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminal singularities such that they are equivalent under locally trivial deformations.
Let

be a locally trivial parallel transport operator which is a Hodge isometry. Assume

.
Then there is a birational map
f:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
with

if and only if the restriction of

to
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
satisfies

(4.4)

p({\operatorname{BAmp}}(X))\cap{\operatorname{BAmp}}(X^{\prime})\not=\emptyset.

Proof.

The “only if” direction follows from Theorem
4.2
.
Let
\varphi
be a marking of
X^{\prime}
. By hypothesis,
(X^{\prime},\varphi)
and
(X,\varphi\circ p)
lie in the same connected component of the marked moduli space and have the same period. Hence, by
[
BL18
, Theorem 1.1]
(which uses the hypothesis

), the varieties

and
X^{\prime}
are birational so that by
[
BL18
, Theorem 6.16]
, there are one parameter deformations

and
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
of

and

over a disk

which are isomorphic over the pointed disk and such that the graph

of this isomorphism for

degenerates to a cycle

where the correspondence

and

.
Again, as in the proof of Theorem

to deduce that the

Notice that if

is the birational map such that

and

5.
The semidirect product decomposition

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, terminal singularities. The main goal of this section is to show that the group
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{Hdg}}}(X)
of Hodge monodromy operators is equal to the semidirect product of the normal subgroup generated by reflections in prime exceptional divisors (cf. Theorem
3.10
) and of the subgroup
{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{bir}}}(X)
of monodromy
operators induced by birational maps from
X
to itself (cf. Theorem
4.2
).

From such result, as it is done in the smooth case in
[
Mar11
, Section 6]
, we will deduce the birational cone conjecture in Section
6
. We follow Markman’s approach
[
Mar11
, Sections 5 and 6]
and using our results from previous sections we arrive at the proof of the cone conjecture.

Definition 5.1
.

A line bundle
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is called
stably exceptional
if there exists a closed analytic subset
Z\subset{\operatorname{Def}}(X,L)
of positive codimension such that for all

t\in{\operatorname{Def}}(X,L)\setminus Z
the line bundle
L_{t}
equals
\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(aE_{t})
where

E_{t}
is a prime exceptional divisor on
X_{t}
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
the Cartier index of
E_{t}
.

Notice that on special points (i.e. along
Z
) a stably exceptional line bundle
L
may have
h^{0}>1
, see Example
5.10
. Moreover, even when
h^{0}=1
the divisor may be reducible or non-reduced, i.e. not
prime
exceptional, and for this reason we prefer not to use the terminology
stably prime exceptional
introduced in
[
Mar11
, Definition 6.4]
in the smooth case. It is important to notice that the class of a stably exceptional line bundle is in the effective cone by the semicontinuity of
h^{0}
.

5.2.
Moduli spaces of primitive symplectic varieties

Given a lattice
\Lambda
with quadratic form
q

Proposition 5.8

Let

For the first inclusion consider

to a projective primitive symplectic variety

Proposition 5.9

Let

be a projective primitive symplectic variety with terminal and

Proof.

First of all by Proposition

Example 5.10
.

Note that a stably exceptional class does not always specialize to a sum of prime exceptional divisors (if this were the case, the equality
{\mathcal{FE}({E})}={\mathcal{SE}({X})}
would be true for trivial reasons). As a counterexample we may take an elliptic K3 surface with a section and an

-fiber (i.e. two smooth rational curves meeting in two distinct points). Let

be the class of the section,

e^{2}=s^{2}=-2,\quad f^{2}=0=e.f,\quad f.s=1=e.s.

We deduce that

is a primitive class of square
-2

\alpha
is stably exceptional. If

are the

-curves on general nearby fibers in the Hodge locus of

specializes to an element in the linear system corresponding to

Definition 7.1
.

Let
X
be a projective primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and terminal singularities and let

be a Cartier divisor on

if the following conditions hold:

(1)

Negativity:

(2)

\varphi\in{\operatorname{Mon}^{2,{\rm{lt}}}_{\operatorname{Hdg}}}(X)
we have

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
X
be a primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and terminal singularities and let
\pi:\,X\xrightarrow{\ \ }Z
be a relative Picard rank one contraction. Let

be a contracted curve. Then the dual class
D
to

deforms to a wall divisor on all projective deformations
X_{t}\in\operatorname{Hdg}_{[C]}
in the Hodge locus of
C

Proof.

By moving inside the Hodge locus of
C
and deforming
\pi
along the way as in the proof of Proposition
2.12
, we can directly assume that
X_{t}
is projective, as projective deformations are dense in any family which is non trivial in moduli by
[
BL18
, Corollary 6.10]
. We consider a deformation
C_{t}\subset X_{t}
of

as in Corollary
2.14
and denote

. We have to verify that

is a wall divisor.

The negativity condition follows as

is contracted (see
[
BL18
, Lemma 5.21]
) and

is a topological invariant. For the wallness, let

and consider a birational model

such that

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and terminal. Since

is a Hodge isometry

remains of type

deforms over all its Hodge locus, a (non-zero, possibly negative) multiple of

is dual to

Let

Because

Proposition 7.4
.

Let

be a projective primitive symplectic variety with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and terminal singularities and let

Up to changing the birational model and acting with

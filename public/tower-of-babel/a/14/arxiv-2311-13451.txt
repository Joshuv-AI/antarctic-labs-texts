Infinite-dimensional flats in the space of positive metrics on an ample line bundle
Rémi Reboulet, David Witt Nyström

Abstract
. We show that any continuous positive metric on an ample line bundle L L lies at the apex of many infinite-dimensional Mabuchi-flat cones. More precisely, given any bounded graded filtration ℱ \mathcal{F} of the section ring of L L , the set of bounded decreasing convex functions on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of ℱ \mathcal{F} embeds L p L^{p} - isometrically into the space of bounded positive metrics on L L with respect to Darvas’ d p d_{p} distance for all p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) p\in[1,\infty) , and in particular with respect to the Mabuchi metric ( p = 2 p=2 ).



Introduction.

We consider the space of bounded positive metrics on an ample line bundle
L
over a projective manifold
X
. Mabuchi
[
Mab87
]
defined a distance between two
smooth
positive metrics
\phi_{0}
,
\phi_{1}
on
L
, with

d_{2}(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})^{2}:=\inf_{\{\gamma_{t}\}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{X}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{MA}(\gamma_{t})\,dt,

where the infimum is taken over smooth paths
\{\gamma_{t}\}
joining
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
. This distance then extends by continuity to bounded positive metrics.

By work of Chen
[
C+00
]
and Darvas
[
Dar15
]
, this distance in fact makes it into a
geodesic
metric space, which is by itself remarkable: indeed, the Mabuchi distance is highly nonlinear, and geodesics are given as solutions to a degenerate complex Monge–Ampère equation
[
Sem92
]
.

Endowed with the Mabuchi distance, the set
L^{\infty}\cap\mathrm{PSH}(X,L)
of positive bounded metrics on
L
furthermore has
negative curvature
, in the sense of Busemann, i.e. the distance function between two geodesics is convex
[
CC21
]
. In this paper, we will be interested in finding
flat
subspaces of
L^{\infty}\cap\mathrm{PSH}(X,L)
, i.e. isometric embeddings of (subsets of) a real vector space into it. Geodesics are of course examples of
one-dimensional flats
, but in this paper, we show that this space in fact admits
infinite-dimensional
flats.

To construct such subspaces, we will use the method of
quantisation
. Recall that the space of continuous positive metrics is closely related to the spaces of Hermitian norms on
H^{0}(X,kL)
. Indeed, fixing a smooth volume form
dV
on
X
, a continuous positive metric
\phi
induces a Hermitian norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi}
on the space of sections
H^{0}(X,kL)
by

\left\|s\right\|_{k\phi}^{2}:=\int_{X}|s|^{2}_{k\phi}\,dV.

Conversely, a Hermitian norm induces a metric
via
the Fubini–Study map: letting
(s_{k,i})_{i}
be an orthonormal basis for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi}
, one defines the smooth positive metric

\phi_{k}:=k^{-1}\log\sum_{i}|s_{k,i}|^{2}.

It is well-known from results on quantisation
[
Bou90
,
Cat99
,
Tia90
,
Zel98
]
that, at the limit, these metrics recover
\phi
:

\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{k}=\phi.

Chen–Sun
[
CS12
]
indeed show that the space of positive metrics is a Gromov–Hausdorff limit of these spaces of Hermitian norms.

Such spaces of norms admit many flat subspaces: fixing a basis of
H^{0}(X,kL)
, the subclass of Hermitian norms admitting it as an orthogonal basis is in fact isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{\dim H^{0}(X,kL)}
. Those subspaces are called
apartments
and fit more generally into the structure of an
Euclidean building
. It has been conjectured for a long time, see Donaldson
[
Don99
, Section 6(iii)]
and Codogni
[
Cod19
, Section 7]
, that these structures should reflect to an
asymptotic
building structure on the space of Kähler metrics. Our result is in this direction.

We now consider a bounded graded filtration
\mathcal{F}
of the section ring of
L
(in the sense of
[
BC11
]
), which means that on each graded piece
H^{0}(X,kL)
, we have an induced decreasing, left-continuous filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}H^{0}(X,kL)
which satisfies a
submultiplicativity
property, in that

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}H^{0}(X,kL)\cdot\mathcal{F}^{\gamma}H^{0}(X,mL)\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\lambda+\gamma}H^{0}(X,(k+m)L).

To such a filtration are associated its
jumping numbers

\lambda_{i,k}
, which are the (ordered) values of
\lambda
at which the filtration changes dimension. Such filtrations were shown by the second author
[
Nys12
]
to arise from
test configurations
of
(X,L)
, i.e.
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant one-parameter degenerations of
(X,L)
, although they are strictly more general than test configurations
[
Szé15
]
.

Furthermore, to a filtration
\mathcal{F}
one can associate its
Duistermaat–Heckman measure

\sigma(\mathcal{F})

[
BC11
]
, which is given as the weak limit of the measures

\sigma_{k}(\mathcal{F}):=k^{-n}\sum_{i}\delta_{\lambda_{i,k}/k}.

Let us now pick a function
\mathfrak{f}
on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure that is bounded,
convex
and
decreasing
. Note that one can always choose an orthogonal basis
(s_{k,i})
for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi}
which is
adapted
to
\mathcal{F}
, in that
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda_{i}}H^{0}(X,kL)
is spanned by
\{s_{k,j}:j\geq i\}
. We then define a
modified
Fubini–Study metric:

\phi_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}}:=k^{-1}\log\sum_{i}|s_{k,i}|^{2}e^{k\cdot\mathfrak{f}(\lambda_{k,i}/k)}.

Our main result is then the following:

Main Theorem (
3.5
).
Let
\phi
be a continuous positive metric on
L
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a bounded graded filtration on
L
. Then, we have that:

(1)

for any
\mathfrak{f}
bounded, convex and decreasing on the support of
\sigma(\mathcal{F})
, the limit

\phi^{\mathfrak{f}}:=\mathrm{usc}\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}}

exists, and defines a bounded positive metric on
L
;

(2)

given two such functions
\mathfrak{f}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, we have that

d_{2}(\phi^{\mathfrak{f}},\phi^{\mathfrak{g}})=\|f-g\|_{L^{2}(\sigma(\mathcal{F}))}.

Remark 1.
The second statement implies that the map
\mathfrak{f}\mapsto\phi^{\mathfrak{f}}
is an
isometric embedding
of the cone of bounded, convex and decreasing functions on the support of
\sigma(\mathcal{F})
equipped with the
L^{2}_{\sigma(\mathcal{F})}
-metric into the cone of bounded positive metrics on
L
equipped with the Mabuchi metric. Therefore, for
any
bounded graded filtration
\mathcal{F}
, there exists an associated
infinite-dimensional flat cone
with apex
\phi
.

Remark 2.
Phong–Sturm
[
PS07
]
(in the case of test configurations) and Ross–Witt Nyström
[
RN14
]
(in general) constructed a geodesic ray, i.e. a
one-dimensional flat cone
, associated to the data of a bounded graded filtration on the section ring of
L
. One recovers those results by taking the family of functions
\mathfrak{f}_{t}:x\mapsto-t\cdot x
, so that
\{\phi^{\mathfrak{f}_{t}}\}
coincides with that geodesic ray. In fact, our construction can be seen as first
modifying
the filtration
\mathcal{F}
by the data of the function
\mathfrak{f}
, in a way that is
flat
in a certain sense of the metric geometry of filtrations, and then taking
t=1
in this geodesic ray construction.

Remark 3.
Later on, Darvas
[
Dar15
]
introduced Finsler structures
d_{p}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
, on the space of positive metrics, recovering Mabuchi’s metric for
p=2
, and this circle of ideas has blossomed into a field of its own
[
Dar19
]
. Our result also covers the case
p\in[1,\infty)
, in that

d_{p}(\phi^{\mathfrak{f}},\phi^{\mathfrak{g}})=\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(\sigma(\mathcal{F}))}.

Ideas for the proof.
Let us fix a continuous positive metric
\phi
on
L
and a
bounded graded filtration

\mathcal{F}
of the section ring of
L
. While the language of filtrations is likely more familiar to the reader, we prefer to consider instead
bounded graded non-Archimedean norms

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
, which are in one-to-one correspondence with filtrations: indeed, on each piece
H^{0}(X,kL)
of the section ring, a filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}_{k}
induces a non-Archimedean vector space norm by setting

\|v\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}}=\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\,v\in\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}_{k}H^{0}(X,kL)\};

and this construction can be reversed. Due to the fact that we also work with norms in quantisation, it will make proofs and parallels more clear.

We then consider the class of bounded, decreasing convex functions
\mathfrak{f}
on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
(i.e. that of its associated filtration). This class is naturally infinite-dimensional. The convexity property is an analogue of
submultiplicativity
, and the decreasing property is important to preserve the order of the jumping numbers of the filtration, which become values of the logarithm of the non-Archimedean norm at an orthogonal basis. Those two properties then allow us to construct another bounded graded non-Archimedean norm on the section ring of
L
. The crucial aspect of this article is that the construction associating a new norm to such a function
\mathfrak{f}
can in fact be made in a
flat
manner, in a certain sense of metric geometry of graded norms. The flatness on the non-Archimedean side, which can be seen as the
boundary at infinity
of the space of positive metrics on
L
, in turn reflects on the space of positive metrics itself,
via
Phong–Sturm’s construction. We are greatly helped by recent results of Finski
[
Fin22
,
Fin23
]
that give algebraic conditions allowing us to understand the metric geometry of positive metrics
via
the metric geometry of norms on the section ring of
L
, which appear e.g. in quantisation of geodesic rays.

Structure of the paper.
The first section gathers results from the literature concerning spaces of norms and quantisation of positive metrics. It is essentially expository.

The second section recalls notions on non-Archimedean norms and Duistermaat–Heckman measures. We explain how to modify a filtration (equivalently a non-Archimedean norm) using the data of a convex decreasing function on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure, then prove our construction yields a new bounded graded norm (Theorem
2.1
).

The third section is devoted to the proof of our main theorem. We first study how to modify complex norms, and how this operation preserves submultiplicativity. Finally, we prove the main result (Theorem
3.5
). In the last subsection, we discuss the results and list some open questions, in particular related to Okounkov bodies.

1.
Quantisation of positive metrics.

Let
X
be a compact projective complex manifold, endowed with an ample line bundle
L
. The principle of quantisation allows one to construct positive Hermitian
metrics
on
L
coming from sequences of
norms
on the spaces of sections
H^{0}(X,kL)
. The ideal type of sequence of norms is a
submultiplicative
sequence of
Hermitian
norms, as submultiplicative sequences have good asymptotic behaviour, while Hermitian norms are easier to analyse, notably because they always admit orthogonal bases.

Unfortunately, in Kähler geometry, most sequences used in quantisation fail to be both submultiplicative and Hermitian. An important class of examples is given by taking
L^{2}
or
L^{\infty}
norms associated to continuous metrics on the line bundle, as we will explain below. The problem of relating submultiplicativity and Hermitian-ness is at the heart of various problems, including the construction of Mabuchi geodesics
[
PS07
,
Ber09
,
Fin22
]
. Indeed, while one can obtain partial submultiplicativity for sequences of Hermitian norms (coming from the
Bernstein–Markov property

[
BB10
,
Nys14
]
), this is not in general sufficient to yield convergence of the quantisation process, and is harder to control under perturbations. Work of Berman–Berndtsson
[
BB10
]
and recently Finski
[
Fin22
]
provides a way to bridge the gap between those two notions.

1.1.
The space of norms on a complex vector space.

We begin by recollecting facts about spaces of norms. Throughout this section,
V
will be a finite-dimensional vector space of dimension
N
over
\mathbb{C}
. We will denote by
\mathcal{N}(V)
the space of norms on
V
, and by
\mathcal{N}^{H}(V)\subset\mathcal{N}(V)
the space of Hermitian norms on
V
.

Those spaces can be endowed with quasi-metric structures. To that end, we consider two norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(V)
, and define their
successive minima
:

(1.1)

\lambda_{i}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}):=\sup_{W\in\mathrm{Grass}_{i}(V)}\inf_{w\in W^{\times}}\log\frac{\left\|w\right\|^{\prime}}{\left\|w\right\|},

\mathrm{Grass}_{i}(V)
denoting the Grassmannian of
i
-dimensional subspaces of
V
. If both norms admit a common orthogonal basis
\underline{v}=(v_{i})
, which is always the case if for example both norms are Hermitian, then up to reordering one has

(1.2)

\lambda_{i}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})=\log\frac{\left\|v_{i}\right\|^{\prime}}{\left\|v_{i}\right\|},

corresponding to the logarithms of the eigenvalues of the transition matrix between the two norms. For
p\in[1,\infty)
, one then defines

d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})^{p}:=N^{-1}\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})|^{p},

and

d_{\infty}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}):=\max_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})|.

It is then clear that, for
p<q\in[1,\infty]
,

(1.3)

d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})\leq d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})\leq d_{q}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})\leq d_{\infty}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}).

The following fact is well-known for the Hermitian case (e.g.
[
BE21
, Theorem 3.1]
), and is explained in
[
Fin23
, Eq (3.9)]
in the non-Hermitian case:

Proposition 1.1
.

For
p\in[1,\infty]
,
d_{p}
defines a metric on
\mathcal{N}^{H}(V)
. It satisfies a quasi-triangle inequality on
\mathcal{N}(V)
in general: for
\left\|\cdot\right\|
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime\prime}
,

d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})\leq d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime\prime})+d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime\prime},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})+\log N.

The spaces
(\mathcal{N}^{H}(V),d_{p})
are geodesic for
p\in[1,\infty]
and uniquely geodesic for
p\in(1,\infty)
. The unique geodesic is given by picking a common orthogonal basis
\underline{v}
for two norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}
, and defining for
t\in[0,1]
:

\left\|\sum c_{i}v_{i}\right\|_{t}:=\sum|c_{i}|\left\|v_{i}\right\|^{1-t}\left\|v_{i}\right\|^{\prime}{}^{t}.

We also define the
relative volume
of two norms by

\mathrm{vol}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}):=N^{-1}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}).

This is a quantity that measures the discrepancy between the volumes of the unit balls of two given norms. It is closely related to the
d_{1}
distance: indeed, if
\left\|v\right\|\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}[v]
for all
v\in V
, then

d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})=\mathrm{vol}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}).

One can show that this is a particular case of a more general formula using the
max operator
in the Hermitian case. Given two Hermitian norms diagonalised in a common basis
{\underline{v}}
, define
\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})
as the unique norm diagonalised in the basis
{\underline{v}}
and such that

\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})(v_{i})=\max(\left\|v_{i}\right\|,\left\|v_{i}\right\|^{\prime}).

A simple computation then shows that

(1.4)

d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})=d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}))+d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime},\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})).

1.2.
Spaces of sequences of norms.

Let, as in the beginning of this section,
X
be a compact projective complex manifold endowed with an ample line bundle
L
. For clarity, we will denote
V_{k}:=H^{0}(X,kL)
and
N_{k}=\dim H^{0}(X,kL)=h^{0}(X,kL)
. We now take our interest to sequences
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}:k\mapsto\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}
, where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}
is a norm on
V_{k}
.

Let us pick two such sequences
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}
. Let us define, for
p\in[1,\infty]
,

d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}):=\limsup_{k}k^{-1}d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{k}).

We will say that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
is
p
-bounded relative to
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}
if the limit above is finite. We note that this only defines a pseudo-distance on the set of equivalence classes of relatively
p
-bounded sequences of norms; in general, one defines an equivalence relation whereby two such sequences are
p
-equivalent
if and only if their
d_{p}
distance is zero.

We also define the
relative volume
of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}
as

\mathrm{vol}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}):=\limsup_{k}k^{-1}\mathrm{vol}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{k}).

A quick computation shows that
\mathrm{vol}
is
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
d_{1}
, so that the relative volume is well-defined between two relatively
1
-bounded sequences of norms. Likewise, defining the max operator for sequences of Hermitian norms by

\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\prime}):k\mapsto\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{k})

shows that the asymptotic version of (
1.4
) also holds:

(1.5)

d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet})=d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}))+d_{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet},\vee(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet})).

1.3.
Quantisation.

Given a continuous positive metric
\phi
on
L
, one can associate to it two different norms on
V_{k}
: a Hermitian norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi}
, given by

\|s\|_{k\phi}^{2}=\int_{X}|s|e^{-k\phi}\,dV

for a smooth volume form
dV
, and a non-necessarily Hermitian
sup-norm

\|s\|_{k\phi,\infty}:=\sup_{X}|s|e^{-k\phi}.

To any norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|\in\mathcal{N}(V_{k})
, one can associate its
Fubini–Study metric
, a bounded positive metric on
L
, defined at
\ell\in(kL)_{x}
,
x\in X
, by

|\ell|_{\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|)}:=\inf_{s\in V_{k},\,s(x)=\ell}\|s\|.

If
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is furthermore Hermitian, diagonalised in a basis
{\underline{v}}_{k}=(v_{1,k},\dots,v_{N_{k},k})
one recovers
[
Fin22
, Lemma 2.1]
the usual definition

\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|)=\log\sum_{i}|v_{i,k}|^{2}e^{-2\left\|v_{i,k}\right\|}.

Classical results on quantisation
[
Bou90
,
Cat99
,
Tia90
,
Zel98
]
show that

\lim_{k}\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi})=\lim_{k}\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi,\infty})=\phi.

The space of bounded positive metrics on
L
can also be endowed with distances closely related to the
d_{p}
distances on spaces of norms
[
Dar15
]
: given
\phi,\psi
that are positive with respect to a Kähler form
\omega
representing
c_{1}(L)
, one defines

d_{p}(\phi,\psi):=\inf_{t\mapsto\gamma_{t}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{X}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|^{p}\,\mathrm{MA}(\gamma_{t})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}dt,

where the infimum is taken over smooth curves joining the two metrics. The case
p=2
corresponds to the distance introduced by Mabuchi
[
Mab87
]
. The general case was treated by Darvas
[
Dar15
]
. As it turns out, by results of Berndtsson
[
Ber09
]
, Darvas–Lu–Rubinstein
[
DLR20
]
, and Finski
[
Fin22
, Theorem 1.3, Corollary 3.6]
, this quantisation procedure can also be metrised:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\phi,\psi
be bounded positive metrics on
L
. Then,

d_{p}(\phi,\psi)=d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\psi})=d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi,\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\psi,\infty})

for
p\in[1,\infty)
, and also for
p=\infty
if both metrics are continuous (or more generally bounded and regularisable from below,
[
Fin22
, Proposition 2.15]
).

As explained at the start of the section, one easily sees that the sup-norms associated to a continuous positive metric yield a
submultiplicative
sequence of norms on the algebra of sections of
L
. This means that, given
s_{m}\in V_{m}
,
s_{n}\in V_{n}
, one has that

\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|_{(m+n)\phi,\infty}\leq\left\|s_{m}\right\|_{m\phi,\infty}\cdot\left\|s_{n}\right\|_{n\phi,\infty}.

More generally, picking a submultiplicative sequence of norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
always ensures that the Fubini–Study operator

\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}):=\lim_{k}{}^{*}\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k})

converges, by Fekete’s lemma; furthermore, Fekete’s lemma ensures that we are taking the usc regularisation of the supremum of a family of positive metrics, which itself will be positive. To ensure that this operator takes finite values, one must add another condition:

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
be a submultiplicative sequence of norms on the algebra of sections of
L
. We say that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
is a
bounded graded norm
on
R(X,L)
if there exists a bounded positive metric on
L
such that

d_{\infty}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi,\infty})<\infty.

In particular, as a consequence of the previous theorem and the classification results for sequences of norms from
[
Fin22
]
, one has

Theorem 1.4

(
[
Fin22
, Corollary 3.6]
)
.

If
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}
are two bounded graded norms on
L
, then for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,

d_{p}(\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}),\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}))=d_{p}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}).

Importantly, this allows us to work directly with general sequences of norms, which do not
a priori
come from bounded metrics on
L
. The case
p=\infty
is more subtle, but will not be necessary for the purpose of this article.

1.4.
More general sequences of norms.

We explain here how the aforementioned results on quantisation generalise to more abstract norms than those that
a priori
come from positive metrics.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
be a bounded graded norm. We say that a non-necessarily submultiplicative sequence of norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\prime}
is
Bernstein–Markov
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
if, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>1
such that for all
k
,

C_{\varepsilon}^{-1}e^{-\varepsilon k}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{k}\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon k}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k},

or equivalently

d_{\infty}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k},\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{k})\leq k\varepsilon+\log C_{\varepsilon}.

Example 1.6

(
[
BB10
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Given a continuous positive metric
\phi
on
L
, the sequence of
L^{2}
-norms associated to
\phi
(with respect to a smooth volume form) is Bernstein–Markov with respect to the sequence of sup-norms associated to
\phi
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
be a bounded graded norm, and
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}
be a sequence of norms that is Bernstein–Markov with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
. Then,
\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet})
exists as a positive bounded metric, and

\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet})=\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}).

Proof.

Because
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
is submultiplicative, the limit
\lim_{k}k^{-1}\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k})
exists by Fekete’s lemma, and equals
\sup_{k}k^{-1}\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k})
. Boundedness is easily seen to ensure that this supremum is finite, hence
\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet})
is the regularised supremum of a bounded above and below family of positive metrics, hence is itself a bounded positive metric. The equality then follows from the fact that
\left\|\cdot\right\|\mapsto k^{-1}\mathrm{FS}(\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is obviously
1
-Lipschitz with respect to
d_{\infty}
on
\mathcal{N}^{H}(H^{0}(X,kL))
and the supremum norm on
L^{\infty}\cap\mathrm{PSH}(X,L)
, while the Bernstein–Markov property implies that
k^{-1}d_{\infty}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\prime})\to 0
.
∎

2.
Filtrations and Duistermaat–Heckman measures.

2.1.
Bounded graded non-Archimedean norms.

If
V
is as before a complex vector space of dimension
N
, one can also consider the space of
non-Archimedean norms
on
V
. Namely, the trivial absolute value
|\cdot|_{0}
on
\mathbb{C}
, defined by
|z|_{0}=1
if and only if
z\neq 0
, is non-Archimedean: trivially, one sees that

|z+z^{\prime}|\leq\max(|z|,|z^{\prime}|).

A non-Archimedean norm on
V
is a function
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}:V\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the usual norm axioms, but with respect to
|\cdot|_{0}
, and satisfying the
ultrametric
triangle inequality:

\left\|v+w\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}\leq\max(\left\|v\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}},\left\|w\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}).

An exercise in linear algebra shows that such norms can all be constructed by fixing a basis
\underline{v}=(v_{i})
of
V
, real numbers
\underline{a}=(a_{i})
, and setting

\left\|\sum c_{i}v_{i}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}:=\max_{i,c_{i}\neq 0}e^{-a_{i}}.

A basis
\underline{v}
in which
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}
can be expressed as above is called an
orthogonal basis
, in the non-Archimedean sense. Conversely, any non-Archimedean norm on
V
admits an orthogonal basis
[
BE21
, Example 1.11]
.

There is also a trivial norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0}
on
V
, defined by
\|v\|_{0}=1
if and only if
v\neq 0
. It has the property that any basis of
V
is an orthogonal basis.

We denote the space of such norms by
\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{NA}}(V)
. We note that this space can also be metrised in a similar way as in the complex case, but this will not be required for the purpose of this article. In fact, in a strong sense,
\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{NA}}(V)
with such metric structures can be understood as the boundary at infinity of the negatively curved space
(\mathcal{N}^{H}(V),d_{p})
.

Returning now to the geometric setting, where
V_{k}=H^{0}(X,kL)
for an ample line bundle on
X
, and
\dim V_{k}=N_{k}
, one can also define an appropriate notion of a sequence of norms on the section ring of
L
.

We define a
bounded graded non-Archimedean norm
on
L
to be a sequence of non-Archimedean norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
, where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}}
is a non-Archimedean norm on
V_{k}
, satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

submultiplicativity:
for all
s_{m}\in H^{0}(X,mL)
and
s_{n}\in H^{0}(X,nL)
,

\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|^{NA}_{m+n,\mathfrak{f}}\leq\left\|s_{m}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{m,\mathfrak{f}}\left\|s_{n}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{n,\mathfrak{f}};

(2)

boundedness:
there exists
\delta>0
, such that for all
k
,

e^{-\delta k}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0,k}\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k}\leq e^{\delta k}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0,k}.

Here,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0,k}
is the
trivial norm
on
V_{k}
.

Bounded graded norms are in bijection with
bounded graded filtrations
of the section ring, in the sense of
[
Nys12
,
BC11
,
BJ21
]
, as in the introduction. In particular, test configurations
[
BJ21
]
and divisorial valuations
[
BJ23
]
induce examples of bounded graded norms. The setting of filtrations is more likely to be familiar to the reader, but our results will be more easily stated in terms of non-Archimedean norms, justifying our choice of notation.

2.2.
The Duistermaat–Heckman measure and convex functions.

Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
be a bounded graded non-Archimedean norm. To each
k
, one can define a measure
\sigma_{k}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}})
on the real numbers as follows. Let
(s_{i,k})_{i}
be an orthogonal basis for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}}
. We then set

\sigma_{k}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}}):=k^{-n}_{*}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\delta_{-k^{-1}\log\left(\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}\right)}.

By
[
BC11
,
CM15
]
, the measures
\sigma_{k}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}})
converge weakly to a measure
\sigma(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}})
supported on a closed interval of
\mathbb{R}
, independently of the choice of orthogonal basis for each
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}}
. We define the
Duistermaat–Heckman measure
of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
to be this weak limit.

Our main result in this section is the observation that, given a bounded graded non-Archimedean norm and a bounded decreasing convex function
\mathfrak{f}
on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure, one can construct a different bounded graded non-Archimedean norm. This construction will be essential in the proof of our main theorem.

The construction.
Fix
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{\bullet}
, and
(s_{i,k})_{i}
a sequence of orthogonal bases as above. Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a bounded convex, decreasing function on the support of
\sigma(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}})
.

Define now, for each
k
, a non-Archimedean norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k,\mathfrak{f}}
as the unique norm satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k,\mathfrak{f}}
admits
(s_{i,k})_{i}
as an orthogonal basis;

(2)

for each
i=1,\dots,N_{k}
, one has

\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k,\mathfrak{f}}=\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k}e^{k\cdot f\left(\frac{-\log\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k}}{k}\right)}.

Theorem 2.1
.

The sequence
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}
is a bounded graded non-Archimedean norm.

Proof.

It is easy to see that, if
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
is bounded, then its modification by
\mathfrak{f}
is also bounded, with additional constant
\delta=\sup\mathfrak{f}
. We thus only have to show submultiplicativity: for
s_{m}\in H^{0}(X,mL)
and
s_{n}\in H^{0}(X,nL)
, we want to prove that

\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|_{m+n,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}\leq\left\|s_{m}\right\|_{m,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}\left\|s_{n}\right\|_{n,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}.

To that end, we proceed in a similar manner to
[
Reb22
, Theorem 2.6.1]
: we consider first the following special case, where we assume that
s_{m}
belongs to an orthogonal basis for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m}^{\mathrm{NA}}
, and
s_{n}
to an orthogonal basis for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{n}^{\mathrm{NA}}
. Let us write
\alpha=-\log\left\|s_{m}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{m}
, and
\beta=-\log\left\|s_{n}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{n}
, so that
\alpha/m
and
\beta/n
belong to the support of
\sigma(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{\bullet})
. Since
s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\in H^{0}(X,(m+n)L)
, there is a decomposition

s_{m}\cdot s_{n}=\sum a_{j}s_{j,m+n}

according to the fixed orthogonal basis
(s_{j,m+n})
for
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{m+n}
. Let us write

\gamma:=-\log\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}.

Since for all
j
with
s_{j}\neq 0
we have

\left\|s_{j,m+n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}\leq\max_{a_{j}\neq 0}\left\|s_{j,m+n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}=\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}},

then for all
\delta_{j}=-\log\left\|s_{j,m+n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}
with
a_{j}\neq 0
we have

\gamma\leq\delta_{j},

and furthermore since
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{\bullet}
is submultiplicative, one has

(2.1)

\alpha+\beta\leq\gamma\leq\delta_{j}.

Note now that

\frac{\alpha+\beta}{m+n}=\frac{m}{m+n}\frac{\alpha}{m}+\frac{n}{m+n}\frac{\beta}{n}.

Convexity of
\mathfrak{f}
then yields

\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{m+n}\right)\leq\frac{m}{m+n}\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\alpha}{m}\right)+\frac{n}{m+n}\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right),

so that

(2.2)

(m+n)\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{m+n}\right)\leq m\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\alpha}{m}\right)+n\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right).

With those results in hand, we can prove submultiplicativity in this first special case. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}

\displaystyle=\max_{a_{j}\neq 0}(e^{-\delta_{j}}e^{(m+n)\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\delta_{j}}{m+n}\right)}).

By (
2.1
), and the fact that
\mathfrak{f}
is decreasing, we have in particular that

\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}\leq e^{-(\alpha+\beta)}e^{(m+n)\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{m+n}\right)}.

Thus, by (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\alpha}e^{m\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\alpha}{m}\right)}e^{-\beta}e^{n\mathfrak{f}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|s_{m}\right\|_{m,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}\left\|s_{n}\right\|_{n,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}

by definition.

We now show submultiplicativity in general. Let
s_{m}
,
s_{n}
be arbitrary sections, which we decompose in their respective orthogonal bases as

s_{m}=\sum_{i}c_{i}s_{i,m},\,s_{n}=\sum_{j}c_{j}s_{j,n}.

We then have that

\displaystyle\left\|s_{m}\cdot s_{n}\right\|^{NA}_{m+n,mff}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i,c_{i}\neq 0;j,c_{j}\neq 0}\left\|s_{i,m}\cdot s_{j,n}\right\|^{NA}_{m+n,\mathfrak{f}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{i,c_{i}\neq 0}\left\|s_{i,m}\right\|^{NA}_{m,\mathfrak{f}}\right)\cdot\left(\max_{j,c_{j}\neq 0}\left\|s_{j,n}\right\|^{NA}_{n,\mathfrak{f}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|s_{m}\right\|^{NA}_{m,\mathfrak{f}}\left\|s_{n}\right\|^{NA}_{n,\mathfrak{f}}.

The first inequality follows from the non-Archimedean triangle inequality (with redundancies, since the
s_{i,m}\cdot s_{j,n}
generate
H^{0}(X,(m+n)L)
); the second inequality follows from the result proven above in the case of orthogonal sections and splitting the independent maxima; and the equality in the last line is definitional.
∎

The flat cone we will embed in the space of positive metrics is therefore the space of bounded, decreasing convex functions on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of a bounded graded norm. It is easily seen to be infinite-dimensional, and flat since
L^{p}
with
p>1
is a strictly convex norm.

3.
Embedding the flat cone.

3.1.
Rescaling by filtrations, I: the finite-dimensional case.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, and
\underline{v}
be a fixed basis of
V
. We define the
apartment

\mathcal{A}_{\underline{v}}\subset\mathcal{N}^{H}(V)
to be the set of all Hermitian norms admitting
{\underline{v}}
as an orthogonal basis. For
p\in[1,\infty]
, this is easily seen to be isometric to
(\mathbb{R}^{N},L^{p})

via
the following construction: one fixes a Hermitian norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|\in\mathcal{A}_{\underline{v}}
, and one defines for
{\underline{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\iota_{\underline{v}}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,{\underline{a}})

to be the unique Hermitian norm in
\mathcal{A}_{\underline{v}}
such that, for all
i
,

\iota_{\underline{v}}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,{\underline{a}})(v_{i})=\left\|v_{i}\right\|e^{-a_{i}}.

This defines a rescaling map

\iota_{\underline{v}}(\cdot,\cdot):\mathcal{A}_{\underline{v}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{A}_{\underline{v}}.

One would like to extend this definition to non-Hermitian norms. Of course, one cannot in general pick an arbitrary diagonalising basis (as those may not exist in general) for this operation. One instead uses an
envelope
-type construction for rescaling. To that end, we construct a
gauge
non-Archimedean norm as follows. Having fixed a basis
\underline{v}
, and a vector
{\underline{a}}
, let

\left\|\cdot\right\|^{NA}_{{\underline{v}},\underline{a}}:V\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}

be the non-Archimedean norm given by

\left\|\sum\alpha_{i}v_{i}\right\|^{NA}_{\underline{a}}=\max_{i,\alpha_{i}\neq 0}e^{-a_{i}}.

We then define the rescaling of
\left\|\cdot\right\|

via

(3.1)

\iota_{\underline{v}}^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,{\underline{a}})(w):=\inf_{w=\sum w_{i}}\left\{\sum_{i}\left\|w_{i}\right\|\left\|w_{i}\right\|^{NA}_{{\underline{v}},\underline{a}}\right\},

where the infimum ranges over all decompositions of
w
as a sum of vectors
w_{i}\in V
. This gives a rescaling map

\iota_{\underline{v}}^{1}(\cdot,\cdot):\mathcal{N}(V)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathcal{N}(V).

More generally, we will occasionally denote by

\iota^{1}(\cdot,\cdot):\mathcal{N}(V)\times\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{NA}}(V)\to\mathcal{N}(V)

be the map sending a norm to its envelope by a non-Archimedean norm as defined above. In this case,

\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{{\underline{v}},{\underline{a}}})=\iota^{1}_{\underline{v}}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,{\underline{a}}).

Note that, if
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is Hermitian, then there always exists a basis
\underline{v}
which is orthogonal for
\left\|\cdot\right\|
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}
. Defining a vector
\underline{a}
with components
a_{i}=-\log\left\|v_{i}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}
, one then has that

\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}})=\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\underline{a})=\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\underline{a}).

Importantly, the vector
\underline{a}
depends on the choice of such a jointly orthogonal basis.

The following result, which follows from the case
p=\infty
proven in
[
Fin22
, Lemma 2.8]
, shows that these rescaling operations have a very nicely controlled metric distortion. In our applications, where we will consider sequences of vector spaces
V_{k}
whose dimensions will grow at most as
k^{n}
for some fixed
n
, this will imply that this distortion is only growing as
o(k)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\underline{v}
be a basis of
V
,
{\underline{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and let
\left\|\cdot\right\|\in\mathcal{A}_{\underline{v}}(V)
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(V)
. Then, for
p\in[1,\infty]
,

d_{p}(\iota_{\underline{v}}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,{\underline{a}}),\iota_{\underline{v}}^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime},{\underline{a}}))\leq d_{\infty}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})+\log\dim V.

Proof.

For
t\in[0,\infty)
, let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\underline{v}}(V)
be given by

\left\|v_{i}\right\|_{t}:=\left\|v_{i}\right\|e^{-ta_{i}}.

In particular,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}=\iota_{\underline{v}}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,{\underline{a}})
. Applying
[
Fin22
, Lemma 2.8]
at
t=1
then gives

d_{\infty}(\iota_{\underline{v}}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,{\underline{a}}),\iota_{\underline{v}}^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime},{\underline{a}}))\leq d_{\infty}(\left\|\cdot\right\|,\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime})+\log\dim V,

and the result follows since
d_{p}\leq d_{\infty}
.
∎

3.2.
Rescaling by filtrations, II: sequences of norms.

Consider now
\phi
a continuous positive metric on an ample line bundle
L
over
X
. Let
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
be a bounded graded norm on
L
. Then, it is well-known that for each
k
, one can find a basis
\underline{s}_{k}:=(s_{i,k})_{i}
of
V_{k}=H^{0}(X,kL)
which is both orthogonal in the Hermitian sense for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi}
and in the non-Archimedean sense for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}}
. Let
\mathfrak{f}
be a bounded, decreasing convex function on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure
\sigma(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}})
, which gives a bounded graded norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}
as in Theorem
2.1
. We then define the two following modifications:

(1)

\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{f})
is the sequence of Hermitian norms given in degree
k
by

\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\mathfrak{f}):=\iota_{\underline{s}_{k}}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}),

which can be described as the unique Hermitian norm on
V_{k}
admitting
\underline{s}_{k}
as an orthogonal basis and such that for all
i
,

\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\mathfrak{f})(s_{i,k})=\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|_{k\phi}e^{k\mathfrak{f}(-k^{-1}\log\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|_{k}^{\mathrm{NA}})};

(2)

\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi,\infty},\mathfrak{f})
is the sequence of norms given in degree
k
by

\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi,\infty},\mathfrak{f}):=\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi,\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k,\mathfrak{f}}^{\mathrm{NA}}).

Theorem 3.2
.

In the notations above,

(1)

\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi,\infty},\mathfrak{f})
is bounded and submultiplicative;

(2)

\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{f})
is Bernstein–Markov with respect to
\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi,\infty},\mathfrak{f})
.

Proof.

Proof of (1).
The submultiplicativity statement appears in the second paragraph
[
Fin22
, Section 5.1]
. We give here a proof for the convenience of the reader.
Let
s_{m}\in V_{m}
and
s_{n}\in V_{n}
. We have by definition

\displaystyle\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m+n},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}})(s_{m}\cdot s_{n})=\inf_{s_{m}\cdot s_{n}=\sum t_{p}}\left\|t_{p}\right\|_{m+n}\left\|t_{p}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}.

Now, any decomposition
s_{m}=\sum u_{q}\in V_{m}
,
s_{n}=\sum v_{r}\in V_{n}
gives a decomposition
s_{m}\cdot s_{n}=\sum u_{q}\cdot v_{r}\in V_{m+n}
. This implies that

\displaystyle\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m+n},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}})(s_{m}\cdot s_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{s_{m}=\sum u_{q},s_{n}=\sum v_{r}}\left\|u_{q}\cdot v_{r}\right\|_{m+n}\left\|u_{q}\cdot v_{r}\right\|_{m+n}^{\mathrm{NA}}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{s_{m}=\sum u_{q},s_{n}=\sum v_{r}}\left\|u_{q}\right\|_{m}\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{n}\left\|u_{q}\right\|_{m}^{\mathrm{NA}}\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{n}^{\mathrm{NA}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{s_{m}=\sum u_{q}}\left(\left\|u_{q}\right\|_{m}\left\|u_{q}\right\|_{m}^{\mathrm{NA}}\right)\inf_{s_{n}=\sum v_{r}}\left(\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{n}\left\|v_{r}\right\|_{n}^{\mathrm{NA}}\right)

\displaystyle=\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{m}^{\mathrm{NA}})(s_{m})\cdot\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{n},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{n}^{\mathrm{NA}})(s_{n}),

where in the second inequality we have used submultiplicativity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\prime}_{\bullet}
. Boundedness easily follows from the fact that
\mathfrak{f}
satisfies uniform boundedness.

Proof of (2).
Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi}
is Bernstein–Markov with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi,\infty}
, the result then follows from applying Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

The limit
\mathrm{FS}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{f}))=\mathrm{FS}(\iota^{1}(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi,\infty},\mathfrak{f}))
is well-defined as a bounded positive metric on
L
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
1.7
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

It is then clear from the definitions that
\mathrm{FS}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\mathfrak{f}))
corresponds with the metric
\phi_{k}^{\mathfrak{f}}
from the introduction.

3.3.
Proof of the main result.

Given
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
a bounded graded norm, let
\mathcal{C}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}}
be the infinite-dimensional cone of bounded decreasing convex functions on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For all continuous positive metrics
\phi
on
L
and all bounded graded norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
on
L
, there exists an isometric embedding

\displaystyle\iota:(\mathcal{C}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}},L^{p}(\sigma(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}})))

\displaystyle\to(L^{\infty}\mathrm{PSH}(X,L),d_{p})

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathrm{FS}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{f})),

for
p\in[1,\infty)
, mapping the apex of the cone to
\phi
.

Proof.

Let us denote for simplicity by
S
the closed interval that is the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{C}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}}
.
By Corollary
3.3
and Theorem
1.4
, for
p\in[1,\infty)
, one has

(3.2)

d_{p}(\mathrm{FS}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{f})),\mathrm{FS}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{g})))=d_{p}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{f}),\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{g})).

To prove the isometry result, it therefore suffices to show that we have

(3.3)

d_{p}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{f}),\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet\phi},\mathfrak{g}))=\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(\sigma(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}))}.

By definition, one has that

\displaystyle k^{-1}d_{p}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\mathfrak{f}),\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\mathfrak{g}))^{p}

\displaystyle=N_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1,\dots,N_{k}}|f(-k^{-1}\log\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k})-g(-k^{-1}\log\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k})|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{S}|f-g|^{p}\,d\sigma_{k}(\left\|\cdot\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k}).

Indeed, both norms in the left-hand side are diagonalisable in the basis
\underline{s}_{k}
, so that their successive minima are

\lambda_{i}(\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\mathfrak{f}),\iota(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{k\phi},\mathfrak{g}))=\log\frac{\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|_{k\phi}e^{kg(-k^{-1}\log\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k})}}{\left\|s_{k,i}\right\|_{k\phi}e^{kf(-k^{-1}\log\left\|s_{i,k}\right\|^{\mathrm{NA}}_{k})}}.

It then easily follows, since
\sigma_{k}\to\sigma
weakly, that one obtains in the limit the the desired isometry equality (
3.3
). Note that the map in the statement of the theorem is then easily seen to be injective, i.e. defines an isometric
embedding
, for if
f\neq g
while their images define the same metric, then

\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(\sigma(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{NA}}))}=0.

∎

3.4.
Discussion and open questions.

Relation with Okounkov bodies.
The Okounkov body
\Delta(X,L)

[
Oko03
,
LM09
,
KK12
]
is a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
associated to the algebra of sections of
L
, which captures the volume of
L
. Its definition requires a flag, in particular a hypersurface
Y_{1}\subset X
. Such a hypersurface induces a bounded graded filtration
\mathcal{F}_{1}
(
via
its order of vanishing valuation). In
[
Nys14
]
, the second author defines an operation sending a bounded positive metric
\phi
on
L
to a convex function on
\Delta(X,L)
, its
Chebyshev transform

c[\phi]
. By our main result, one can show that a decreasing convex function
\mathfrak{f}
on the support of the measure associated to
\mathcal{F}_{1}
induces a modification
\phi^{\mathfrak{f}}
satisfying
c[\phi^{\mathfrak{f}}]=c[\phi]+\mathfrak{f}
. In particular, geodesics in this cone are characterised by their Chebyshev transform being affine.

In
[
RN17
, Theorem 8.1]
, the authors show that there exists a partial moment map from the space of positive metrics to the first coordinate of the Okounkov body. Namely, if
H_{1}
is the
exhaustion function
in the sense of
[
RN17
, Section 8]
associated to the first divisor
Y_{1}
in the flag used to construct the Okounkov body, then for all continuous positive metrics on
L
, one has

(H_{1})_{*}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}=(p_{1})_{*}d\lambda,

where
d\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure, and
p_{1}
is the projection onto the first variable. Exhaustion functions can be constructed using Legendre transforms of test curves; such test curves also admit Chebyshev transforms which give functions in
\mathcal{C}
under certain singularity assumptions. Finally,
H_{1}
can also be obtained
via
a related, but different, quantisation procedure to ours
[
RN17
, Theorem 8.3]
. It would therefore be interesting to understand better the connection between our results and those of
[
RN17
]
.

The building structure on the space of Kähler metrics.
The apartments we defined in the space of Hermitian norms on a vector space give it the structure of an
Euclidean building
, with the key property that
any two norms share an apartment
: in other words, any two Hermitian norms can be diagonalised by the same basis, and they (and the geodesic joining them) lie in an entire flat isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Our results hint at the existence of such an apartment structure in the space of positive metrics, where the apartments are given by sequences of bases diagonalising a filtration, and the flat model is the space bounded decreasing convex functions on the support of the associated Duistermaat–Heckman measure. This suggests the following, seemingly very difficult open queestion:

Question.
Let
\phi_{0},\phi_{1}
be two continuous positive metrics on
L
. Then, there exists a choice of filtration such that they lie in the image of a same embedding in the sense of our main theorem.

This result would then completely realise the space of positive metrics as an
asymptotic
building, suggesting that the results and techniques from the theory of Euclidean buildings can also be applied to it.

Generalisations.
Much recent work has been devoted to extending pluripotential theory and quantisation procedures both to big or prescribed singularity classes, and to the Kähler setting
[
DX21
,
DDNL23
,
DRN+23
]
. Notions of test configurations (hence possibly filtrations) for Kähler manifolds, more or less algebraic, exist in the literature
[
DR17
,
SD18
,
DXZ23
]
. It is expected that an adequate formulation of our results also hold for big or prescribed singularity classes, although many necessary tools, such as Finski’s results and a satisfactory theory of Duistermaat–Heckman measures, are as of yet missing.

Toric degenerations.
In the toric setting, one can pick a filtration
\mathcal{F}
that is not only submultiplicative but
multiplicative
. Thus, the proof of Theorem
2.1
shows that one can embed the entire space of convex functions on the support of the Duistermaat–Heckman measure in the space of positive metrics; not just the decreasing ones. Assuming that
(X,L)
is not toric, but admits a (metrised) toric degeneration, it would be interesting to understand how the phenomenon of obtaining the entire cone of convex functions arises at the limit.

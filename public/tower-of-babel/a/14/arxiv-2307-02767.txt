Heavy flavor conserved semi-leptonic decay of B s B_{s} in the covariant light-front approach
Yu-Ji Shi, Zhi-Peng Xing

Abstract
We study the heavy flavor conserved semi-leptonic decay B s → B ​ ℓ ​ ν B_{s}\to B\ell\nu in the covariant light front approach. The covariant light front quark model is used to calculate the transition form factors of B s → B ( ∗ ) B_{s}\to B^{(*)} as well as D s → D D_{s}\to D , which are consistent with the leading power predictions from the heavy quark symmetry. The angular distribution analysis on the B s → B ( ∗ ) ​ l ​ ν ¯ B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu} decay is performed by investigating the forward-backward asymmetry of the lepton. We also study the angular distribution of B s → B ∗ ( → B γ ) l ν ¯ B_{s}\to B^{*}(\to B\gamma)l\bar{\nu} decay both through the lepton forward-backward asymmetry and the azimuth angle. The branching fractions of B s → B ​ l ​ ν ¯ B_{s}\to Bl\bar{\nu} and B s → B ∗ ​ l ​ ν ¯ B_{s}\to B^{*}l\bar{\nu} are at the order 10 − 8 10^{-8} and 10 − 9 10^{-9} , respectively. The number of B s → B ​ l ​ ν ¯ B_{s}\to Bl\bar{\nu} events is estimated to be 1.76 1.76 . The branching fraction of B s → B ∗ ( → B γ ) l ν ¯ B_{s}\to B^{*}(\to B\gamma)l\bar{\nu} is at the order 10 − 11 10^{-11} , which is calculated by introducing Breit-Wigner distribution for the intermediate B ∗ B^{*} .



I
Introduction

Heavy flavor physics is an important research area in particle physics due to its crucial role in precisely testing the Standard Model (SM) and searching for new physics (NP). Generally, highly suppressed decay processes in the SM such as the flavor-changing neutral currents (FCNC) processes are widely used as sensitive probes for NP due to their tiny branching fractions. However, besides the FCNC processes, heavy flavor conserved (HFC) processes also have tiny branching fractions, so they enjoy the same advantage for probing NP. Recently, the HFC processes have received increasing attention from both experimentalists
LHCb:2015une
;
LHCb:2020gge
and theorists
Voloshin:2019ngb
;
Niu:2021qcc
;
Cheng:2022kea
;
Cheng:2022jbr
;
Liu:2022mxv
. Particularly, on the theoretical side, the small recoil in HFC processes will effectively constrain the form factor for the transition matrix element. The limited phase space will enhance the amplitudes suppressed by lepton mass in semi-leptonic decays, which is also referred to as helicity suppressed amplitudes. As discussed in Ref.
Datta:2017aue
, when the NP Hamiltonian with
(S-P)
and tensor current are introduced, helicity suppressed amplitudes will appear due to the interference of the NP and SM currents. These amplitudes are sensitive to the NP effects since the NP contribution in positive definite term is highly suppressed by the NP Wilson coefficients. Therefore, these effects are very helpful for NP studies and unique in HFC processes.

The HFC decays of
B_{s}
have highly suppressed branching fractions due to the limited phase space of the
s\to u
transition. This feature makes them ideal platforms for detecting NP, but they have so far not received much attention due to the difficulty of experimental measurement. However, nowadays the abundant production of
B_{s}
in the Belle experiment will improve the situation.
As an electron-positron collider, the Belle detector is currently capable of collecting
711\ {\rm fb}^{-1}
at the energy arround the
\Upsilon(4S)
mass, and
121.4\ {\rm fb}^{-1}
at the energy arround the
\Upsilon(5S)
mass
Dubey:2022lyq
.
\Upsilon(5S)
can decay into
B_{s}^{(*)}
in three ways:
\Upsilon(5S)\to B^{0*}_{s}\bar{B}^{0*}_{s}
,
B^{0*}_{s}\bar{B}^{0}(B^{0}_{s}\bar{B}^{0*}_{s}
) and
B^{0}_{s}\bar{B}^{0}_{s}
with branching fractions 0.87, 0.073 and 0.057, respectively
Belle:2012tsw
. Therefore, the data samples of
B_{s}
generated by the Belle experiment are sufficient for precise measurements of various HFC
B_{s}
decays.

In this work, we will study the semi-leptonic decay
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}
, which is the simplest HFC process of
B_{s}
, using the covariant light-front quark model (LFQM). The traditional LFQM model
Terentev:1976jk
;
Berestetsky:1977zk
;
Jaus:1989au
;
Jaus:1989av
was originally developed based on the light-front formalism
Brodsky:1997de
, which has been widely applied for calculating non-perturbative quantities in terms of hadrons. However, the traditional LFQM loses Lorentz invariance and suffers from the so-called zero mode problem
Choi:1998nf
. The covariant LFQM was first proposed in Refs.
Jaus:1999zv
;
Choi:1998nf
;
Cheng:1997au
with the use of covariant Bethe-Salpeter (BS) approach
Salpeter:1951sz
;
Salpeter:1952ib
, and was then widely applied for meson decays
Jaus:2002sv
;
Cheng:2003sm
;
Bakker:2000pk
;
Bakker:2002mt
;
Bakker:2003up
;
Choi:2005fj
;
Choi:2013mda
;
Chang:2018zjq
;
Chang:2019mmh
;
Zhang:2023ypl
. Recently, the LFQM has also been successfully applied for heavy baryon decays with the help of the quark-diquark picture
Ke:2007tg
;
Wei:2009np
;
Ke:2012wa
;
Zhao:2018mrg
;
Wang:2017mqp
;
Wang:2022ias
;
Liu:2022mxv
;
Zhao:2022vfr
;
Zhang:2022bvl
;
Geng:2022xpn
;
Geng:2020fng
;
Hsiao:2020gtc
;
Liu:2023zvh
or the three quark picture
Zhao:2023yuk
. Therefore, the covariant LFQM must be reliable for our study of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}
decays.

This paper is organized as follows. In section II, we will present the calculation of the transition form factors in the framework of covariant LFQM. The corresponding numerical calculation and the angular distribution analysis will be performed in section III. A brief summary is given in the last section.

II
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}\bar{l}\nu
decay in covariant light-front quark model

In this section, we introduce the covariant calculation for the transition form factors of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}
using LFQM. The effective electroweak Hamiltonian for the
B_{s}\to B^{*}\bar{l}\nu
decay reads as

{\cal H}_{{\rm eff}}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}[\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})s][\bar{l}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu],

(1)

where
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant. With this Hamiltonian, the leptonic part of the decay amplitude can be computed straightforwardly, while the hadronic part are parameterized by the transition form factors of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}
. The corresponding form factors induced by the vector and axial-vector currents are defined as

\displaystyle\langle B(P^{\prime})|\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}s|B_{s}(P)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\bar{P}}_{\mu}-\frac{m_{B_{s}}^{2}-m_{B}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}\right)F_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{m_{B_{s}}^{2}-m_{B}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}F_{0}(q^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle B^{*}(P^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})|\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}s|B_{s}(P)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2V(q^{2})}{m_{B_{s}}+m_{B^{*}}}\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\varepsilon^{\prime}{}^{*\nu}P^{\alpha}P^{\prime\beta},

\displaystyle\langle B^{*}(P^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})|\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}s|B_{s}(P)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2im_{B^{*}}\,\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime*}\cdot q}{q^{2}}\,q_{\mu}A_{0}(q^{2})

\displaystyle+i(m_{B_{s}}+m_{B^{*}})A_{1}(q^{2})\left[\varepsilon_{\mu}^{\prime\prime*}-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime*}\cdot q}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}\right]

\displaystyle-i\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime*}\cdot{\bar{P}}}{m_{B_{s}}+m_{B^{*}}}\,A_{2}(q^{2})\left[{\bar{P}}_{\mu}-\frac{m_{B}^{2}-m_{B^{*}}^{2}}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}\right],

(2)

where
q=P-P^{\prime}
,
{\bar{P}}=P+P^{\prime}
and the Levi-Civita tensor is defined as
\varepsilon_{0123}=1
.

In the LFQM, the light-front decomposition for a four-momentum vector reads as:
P=(P^{-},P^{+},{\vec{P}}_{\perp})

with
P^{\pm}=P^{0}\pm P^{3}
, and thus
P^{2}=P^{+}P^{-}-{\vec{P}}_{\perp}^{2}
. The constituent quark momentums of the initial (final) meson are denoted as
p_{1}^{(\prime)},p_{2}^{(\prime)}
. Note that
P^{(\prime)}
and
p_{1}^{(\prime)},p_{2}^{(\prime)}
cannot be on shell simultaneously. In the covariant LFQM,
P^{(\prime)}
is on-shell, namely
P^{(\prime)2}=M^{(\prime)2}
with
M^{(\prime)}
being the physical mass of the initial (final) meson. However,
p_{1}^{(\prime)},p_{2}^{(\prime)}
are off-shell due to the four-momentum conservation
P^{(\prime)}=p_{1}^{(\prime)}+p_{2}^{(\prime)}
. This situation is different from that in the traditional LFQM, where
p_{1}^{(\prime)},p_{2}^{(\prime)}
are set on shell and only the
+,\perp
components of the momentums are conserved.
The constituent quark momentums can be expressed by the internal variables
(x_{i}^{(\prime)},{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)})
as:

p_{1,2}^{(\prime)+}=x_{1,2}^{(\prime)}P^{(\prime)+},\quad{\vec{p}}_{1,2\perp}^{(\prime)}=x_{1,2}^{(\prime)}{\vec{P}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)}\pm{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)}.

(3)

It is convenient to define several internal quantities during the LFQM calculation:

\displaystyle k_{z}^{(\prime)}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{2}^{(\prime)}M_{0}^{(\prime)}}{2}-\frac{m_{2}^{(\prime)2}+{\vec{p}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)2}}{2x_{2}M_{0}^{(\prime)}},

\displaystyle e_{i}^{(\prime)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m_{i}^{(\prime)2}+{\vec{p}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)2}+k_{z}^{(\prime)2}},

\displaystyle M_{0}^{(\prime)2}

\displaystyle=(e_{1}^{(\prime)}+e_{2}^{(\prime)})^{2}=\frac{{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)2}+m_{1}^{(\prime)2}}{x_{1}^{(\prime)}}+\frac{{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)2}+m_{2}^{(\prime)2}}{x_{2}^{(\prime)}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{0}^{(\prime)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{M_{0}^{(\prime)2}-(m_{1}^{(\prime)}-m_{2}^{(\prime)})^{2}},

(4)

where
i=1,2
,
m_{i}^{(\prime)}
are the constituent quark masses,
M_{0}^{(\prime)}
is the kinetic invariant mass of the initial (final) meson,
k_{z}^{(\prime)}
is the relative momentum of the constituent quark in the
z
-direction and
e_{i}^{(\prime)}
is the energy of quark
i
.

Figure 1:
Feynman diagram for the calculation of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}
transition form factors, where the
cross symbol denotes the transition current and the black dots denote the bound state vertexes.

The calculation of meson transition matrix elements in the covariant LFQM is equivalent to calculating the amplitude of the Feynman diagram shown in Fig.
1
, which can be expressed as

{\cal A}_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}}=\frac{N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}p_{2}\frac{H_{{\cal M}}H_{{\cal M}^{\prime}}}{N_{1}N_{1}^{\prime}N_{2}}iS_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}},

(5)

where
N_{c}=3
,
{\cal M}
and
{\cal M}^{\prime}
denote the initial and final mesons respectively,
H_{{\cal M}},\ H_{{\cal M}^{\prime}}
are the bound-state vertex functions. The
S_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}}
and the denominators are

\displaystyle S_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}[(i\gamma^{0}\Gamma_{{\cal M}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0})(\not{p}_{1}^{\prime}+m_{1}^{\prime})\Gamma_{\mu}(\not{p}_{1}+m_{1})i\Gamma_{{\cal M}}(-\not{p}_{2}+m_{2})],

\displaystyle N_{1}^{(\prime)}

\displaystyle=p_{1}^{(\prime)2}-m_{1}^{(\prime)2}+i\epsilon,\;\;\;N_{2}=p_{2}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}+i\epsilon,

(6)

where
i\Gamma_{{\cal M}^{(\prime)}}
is the vertex operators. The vertex operators for pseudoscalar and vector mesons are

\displaystyle i\Gamma_{P}=-i\gamma_{5},\ \ \ \ i\Gamma_{V}=i\left[\gamma^{\mu}-\frac{\left(k_{1}-k_{2}\right)^{\mu}}{w_{V}}\right]

(7)

with
w_{V}=M_{0}^{\prime}+m_{1}^{\prime}+m_{2}
.

Here we choose a suitable frame where
q^{+}=0
. The
p_{2}^{-}

integration in Eq. (
5
) picks up the pole
p_{2}=\hat{p}_{2}=[({{\vec{p}}_{2\perp}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}})/{p_{2}^{+}},p_{2}^{+},{\vec{p}}_{2\perp}]

which leads to

\displaystyle N_{1}^{(\prime)}\to\hat{N}_{1}^{(\prime)}=x_{1}(M^{(\prime)2}-M_{0}^{(\prime)2}),

\displaystyle H_{{\cal M}}H_{{\cal M}^{\prime}}S_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}}\to h_{{\cal M}}h_{{\cal M}^{\prime}}{\hat{S}}_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\int d^{4}p_{2}\frac{1}{N_{1}N_{1}^{\prime}N_{2}}\to-i\pi\int dx_{2}d^{2}{\vec{k}}_{\perp}\frac{1}{x_{2}{\hat{N}}_{1}{\hat{N}}_{1}^{\prime}}.

(8)

where
{\hat{S}}_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}}
means that all the
p_{i}^{(\prime)}
appearing in
S_{\mu}^{{\cal M}{\cal M}^{\prime}}
are replaced by
{\hat{p}}_{i}^{(\prime)}
. The condition
q^{+}=0
leads to
P^{+}=P^{\prime+},\ p_{1}^{+}=p_{1}^{\prime+}
and so that
x_{1}=x_{1}^{\prime}
. Using
p_{2\perp}=p_{2\perp}^{\prime}+q_{\perp}
, one can obtain
{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{\prime}={\vec{k}}_{\perp}-x_{2}q_{\perp}
.
The explicit form of
h_{P}^{\prime}
was derived in Refs.
Jaus:1999zv
;
Cheng:2003sm
as

h_{{\cal M}^{(\prime)}}=(M^{(\prime)2}-M_{0}^{(\prime)2})\sqrt{\frac{x_{1}^{(\prime)}x_{2}}{N_{c}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\widetilde{M}_{0}^{(\prime)}}\varphi^{(\prime)},

(9)

where
\varphi^{(\prime)}
is the light-front momentum distribution
amplitude for a
s
-wave meson. Generally, it is taken as a Gaussian-type
wave function
Jaus:1999zv
;
Cheng:2003sm
:

\displaystyle\varphi^{(\prime)}(x_{2},{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)})=\

\displaystyle 4\left(\frac{\pi}{\beta^{(\prime)2}}\right)^{3/4}\sqrt{\frac{dk_{z}^{(\prime)}}{dx_{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(-\frac{k_{z}^{(\prime)2}+{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{(\prime)2}}{2\beta^{(\prime)2}}\right),

(10)

where
\beta^{(\prime)}
is a parameter of order
\Lambda_{QCD}
. As shown in Refs.
Jaus:1999zv
;
Cheng:2003sm
, the covariant LFQM suffers from the so-called zero mode contribution. In these literatures a systematic program was derived to include such zero mode contribution, which is realized by the following replacements:

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{1\mu}\to P_{\mu}A_{1}^{(1)}+q_{\mu}A_{2}^{(1)},\;\;\;\hat{N}_{2}\to Z_{2},

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{1\mu}\hat{N}_{2}\to q_{\mu}\left[A_{2}^{(1)}Z_{2}+\frac{q\cdot P}{q^{2}}A_{1}^{(2)}\right].

(11)

The expressions of
A_{i}^{(j)},Z_{2}
are

\displaystyle A_{1}^{(1)}=

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}}{2},\quad A_{2}^{(1)}=A_{1}^{(1)}-\frac{{\vec{k}}_{\perp}\cdot{\vec{q}}_{\perp}}{q^{2}},

\displaystyle A_{1}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle-{\vec{k}}_{\perp}^{2}-\frac{\left({\vec{k}}_{\perp}\cdot{\vec{q}}_{\perp}\right)^{2}}{q^{2}}

\displaystyle Z_{2}=

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{1}+m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}+\left(1-2x_{1}\right)M^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(q^{2}+q\cdot P\right)\frac{{\vec{k}}_{\perp}\cdot{\vec{q}}_{\perp}}{q^{2}}.

(12)

The analytical results of the from factors can be found in the Appendix
A
and are also given in Refs
Jaus:1999zv
;
Cheng:2003sm
, where an alternative parameterization of the form factors as shown in Eq. (
39
) is used.

III
Angular distributions

III.1
Angular Distribution of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}

We firstly perform an angular distribution analysis for the three-body decay
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}
. Introducing an auxiliary vector particle
V
with momentum
q=p_{l}+p_{\bar{\nu}}
and polarization vector
\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V}
, as well as using the following decomposition formula:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V*}(t)\varepsilon_{\nu}^{V}(t)-\sum_{\lambda=\pm,0}\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V*}(\lambda)\varepsilon_{\nu}^{V}(\lambda),

(13)

we can express the helicity amplitudes of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}
induced by the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{B_{s}\to B}^{h_{l}}=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}\Big[A_{t}B^{h_{l}}_{t}-\sum_{\lambda=\pm,0}A_{\lambda}B^{h_{l}}_{\lambda}\Big],

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{B_{s}\to{B^{*}}}^{h_{R},h_{l}}=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}\Big[A_{t}^{h_{R}}B^{h_{l}}_{t}-\sum_{\lambda=\pm,0}A_{\lambda}^{h_{R}}B^{h_{l}}_{\lambda}\Big],

(14)

where the hadronic amplitudes
A_{h_{V}},A_{h_{V}}^{h_{R}}
and the leptonic amplitudes
B^{h_{l}}_{h_{V}}
are defined as

\displaystyle A_{h_{V}}=

\displaystyle\langle B|\bar{u}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})s|B_{s}\rangle\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V*}(h_{V}),

\displaystyle A^{h_{R}}_{h_{V}}=

\displaystyle\langle B^{*}(h_{R})|\bar{u}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})s|B_{s}\rangle\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V*}(h_{V}),

\displaystyle B^{h_{l}}_{h_{V}}=

\displaystyle\langle l(h_{l})\bar{\nu}|\bar{l}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu|0\rangle\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V}(h_{V}),

(15)

with
h_{V}=t,0,\pm
being the helicity of the auxiliary vector particle. The analytical expressions of
A_{h_{V}},A_{h_{V}}^{h_{R}}
and
B^{h_{l}}_{h_{V}}
can be found in the Appendix

In the rest frame of
B_{s}
, we let the lepton pair and the
B^{(*)}
meson move along and against the z-axis, respectively. The angular between the moving direction of the lepton
l
and the z-axis is denoted as
\theta_{l}
. The angular distribution for the differential decay width of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}}}{dq^{2}d{\rm cos}\theta_{l}}=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}^{2}|V_{us}|^{2}\beta_{l}\sqrt{\lambda_{B_{s}}^{(*)}}}{64(2\pi)^{3}m_{B_{s}}^{3}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big[L_{1}^{(*)}+L_{2}^{(*)}\ {\rm cos}\theta_{l}+L_{3}^{(*)}\ {\rm cos}^{2}\theta_{l}\Big],

(16)

where
\beta_{l}=1-{\hat{m}}_{l}^{2}
,
{\hat{m}}_{l}=m_{l}/\sqrt{q^{2}}
and
\lambda_{B_{s}}^{(*)}=[m_{B_{s}}^{2}-(m_{B^{(*)}}+\sqrt{q^{2}})^{2}][m_{B_{s}}^{2}-(m_{B^{(*)}}-\sqrt{q^{2}})^{2}]
. The coefficients
L_{i}^{(*)}
are functions of
q^{2}=(p_{l}+p_{\bar{\nu}})^{2}
and their analytical expressions in terms of the helicity amplitudes are

\displaystyle L_{1}=

\displaystyle 2\beta_{l}q^{2}\left[|A_{0}|^{2}+|A_{t}|^{2}{\hat{m}}_{l}^{2}\right],

\displaystyle L_{2}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle L_{3}=

\displaystyle-2\beta_{l}^{2}q^{2}|A_{0}|^{2}

(17)

for the
B_{s}\to Bl\bar{\nu}
decay and

\displaystyle L_{1}^{*}=

\displaystyle\beta_{l}q^{2}\left[2|A^{0}_{0}|^{2}+2|A^{0}_{t}|^{2}{\hat{m}}_{l}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(1+{\hat{m}}_{l}^{2})(|A_{\parallel}|^{2}+|A_{\perp}|^{2})\right],

\displaystyle L_{2}^{*}=

\displaystyle-4\beta_{l}q^{2}\left[{\rm Re}(A^{0}_{0}A^{0*}_{t}){\hat{m}}_{l}^{2}+{\rm Re}(A_{\parallel}A_{\perp}^{*})\right],

\displaystyle L_{3}^{*}=

\displaystyle-\beta_{l}^{2}q^{2}\left[2|A^{0}_{0}|^{2}-|A_{\parallel}|^{2}-|A_{\perp}|^{2}\right].

(18)

for the
B_{s}\to B^{*}l\bar{\nu}
decay. Here we have made redefinitions:
A_{\parallel}=(A^{+}_{+}+A^{-}_{-})/\sqrt{2}
and
A_{\perp}=(A^{+}_{+}-A^{-}_{-})/\sqrt{2}
. The normalized forward–backward (FB) asymmetry for
\theta_{l}
is defined as

\displaystyle A^{3,l}_{FB}=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\int^{1}_{0}-\int^{0}_{-1}\right)d{\rm cos}\theta_{l}\frac{d\Gamma_{B_{s}\to B^{*}l\bar{\nu}}}{dq^{2}d{\rm cos}\theta_{l}}}{\int^{1}_{-1}d{\rm cos}\theta_{l}\frac{d\Gamma_{B_{s}\to B^{*}l\bar{\nu}}}{dq^{2}d{\rm cos}\theta_{l}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3L_{2}^{(*)}}{6L_{1}^{(*)}+2L_{3}^{(*)}},

(19)

which vanishes for the
B_{s}\to Bl\bar{\nu}
decay.

The helicity amplitude of
B_{s}\to B^{*}(\to B\gamma)l\bar{\nu}
is expressed as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\rm 4bdy}^{h_{\gamma},h_{l}}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}\Big[

\displaystyle H^{\mu}(h_{\gamma})\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V*}(t)B^{h_{l}}_{t}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\lambda=\pm,0}H^{\mu}(h_{\gamma})\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V*}(\lambda)B^{h_{l}}_{\lambda}\Big],

The

\displaystyle H^{\mu}(h_{\gamma})\varepsilon_{\mu}^{V*}(h_{V})=

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{R}=\pm,0}\int\frac{d^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i}{l^{2}-m_{B^{*}}^{2}+im_{B^{*}}\Gamma_{B^{*}}}

\displaystyle\times\langle B,\gamma(h_{\gamma})|B^{*}(l,h_{R})\rangle A^{h_{R}}_{h_{V}}.

denotes the decay width of
B^{*}

B^{*}\to B\gamma
can be parameterized as

\displaystyle\langle B(p_{B}),\gamma(k,h_{\gamma})|B^{*}(p,h_{R})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p-p_{B}-k)D^{h_{\gamma}}_{h_{R}},

(22)

with

\displaystyle D^{h_{\gamma}}_{h_{R}}=\frac{g_{0}}{m_{B^{*}}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\varepsilon^{\gamma*}_{\mu}(k,h_{\gamma})p_{\nu}k_{\rho}\varepsilon^{R}_{\sigma}(p,h_{R}),

\varepsilon^{\gamma}
and
\varepsilon^{R}

is a dimensionless coupling constant. The expressions of

can be found in the Appendix. The helicity amplitude becomes

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\rm 4bdy}^{h_{\gamma},h_{l}}=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{us}\frac{i}{p^{2}-m_{B^{*}}^{2}+im_{B^{*}}\Gamma_{B^{*}}}

\displaystyle\times\left[D^{h_{\gamma}}_{0}A^{0}_{t}B^{h_{l}}_{t}-\sum_{\lambda=\pm,0}D^{h_{\gamma}}_{\lambda}A^{\lambda}_{\lambda}B^{h_{l}}_{\lambda}\right].

(24)

and

and

functions. The differential decay width of

reads as

\displaystyle\frac{d\Gamma_{B_{s}\to B\gamma l\bar{\nu}}}{dp^{2}dq^{2}d{\rm cos}\theta_{l}d{\rm cos}\theta_{\gamma}d\phi}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle{\cal K}\Big[I_{1}+I_{2}\ {\rm cos}2\theta_{l}+I_{3}\ {\rm sin}^{2}\theta_{l}{\rm cos}2\phi

\displaystyle+I_{4}\ {\rm sin}2\theta_{l}{\rm cos}\phi+I_{5}\ {\rm sin}\theta_{l}{\rm cos}\phi+I_{6}\ {\rm cos}\theta_{l}

\displaystyle+I_{7}\ {\rm sin}\theta_{l}{\rm sin}\phi+I_{8}\ {\rm sin}2\theta_{l}{\rm sin}\phi

Conclusion

In this work, we studied the heavy flavor conserved semi-leptonic decay
B_{s}\to B\ell\nu
in the covariant light front approach. We used covariant light front quark model to calculate the transition form factors of
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}
as well as
D_{s}\to D
, and the results are consistent with the leading power predictions from HQS. We performed an angular distribution analysis on the
B_{s}\to B^{(*)}l\bar{\nu}
decay by investigating the forward-backward asymmetry of the lepton. The angular distribution of
B_{s}\to B^{*}(\to B\gamma)l\bar{\nu}
decay is studied both through the lepton forward-backward asymmetry and the azimuth angle. The obtained branching fractions of
B_{s}\to Bl\bar{\nu}
and
B_{s}\to B^{*}l\bar{\nu}
are at the order
10^{-8}
and
10^{-9}
, respectively. The obtained branching fraction of
B_{s}\to B^{*}(\to B\gamma)l\bar{\nu}
is at the order
10^{-11}
. Using the measured cross section of
B_{s}
production, we deduced that the number of
B_{s}\to Bl\bar{\nu}
events is
1.76
, which is possible to be tested in the future with the increasing amount of
B_{s}
samples produced by the updating Belle experiment.

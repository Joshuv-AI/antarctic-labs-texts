A lower semicontinuous time separation function for C 0 C^{0} spacetimes
Eric Ling

Abstract
The time separation function (or Lorentzian distance function) is a fundamental tool used in Lorentzian geometry. For smooth spacetimes it is known to be lower semicontinuous, and in fact, continuous for globally hyperbolic spacetimes. Moreover, an axiom for Lorentzian length spaces – a synthetic approach to Lorentzian geometry – is the existence of a lower semicontinuous time separation function. Nevertheless, the usual time separation function is not necessarily lower semicontinuous for C 0 C^{0} spacetimes due to bubbling phenomena. In this paper, we introduce a class of curves called “nearly timelike” and show that the time separation function for C 0 C^{0} spacetimes is lower semicontinuous when defined with respect to nearly timelike curves. Moreover, this time separation function agrees with the usual one when the metric is smooth. Lastly, sufficient conditions are found guaranteeing the existence of nearly timelike maximizers between two points in a C 0 C^{0} spacetime.

Introduction

The time separation function (or Lorentzian distance function) is a fundamental tool used in causal theory and Lorentzian geometry. For smooth spacetimes, it’s known to be lower semicontinuous, and in fact, continuous if the spacetime is globally hyperbolic
[
31
]
. In synthetic approaches to Lorentzian geometry, a lower semicontinuous time separation function even appears as an axiom, as in the Lorentzian length spaces of
[
21
]
or the recently introduced bounded Lorentzian-metric spaces of
[
30
]
.

However, for
C^{0}
spacetimes, i.e. ones where the metric is only continuous, it’s known that the time separation function – as it’s usually defined – is
not
lower semicontinuous due to bubbling phenomena. (Bubbling occurs when the causal future
J^{+}(p)
is not contained in the closure of the timelike future
I^{+}(p)
of a point
p
in the spacetime.) The goal of this paper is to introduce a time separation function for
C^{0}
spacetimes that is lower semicontinuous and agrees with the usual definition in the smooth setting.

Let us briefly demonstrate what goes wrong in the continuous setting with the usual definition of the time separation function. If
p
is the vertex of a bubbling region (e.g. the origin in
[
9
, Ex. 1.11]
) and
q
is a point in the bubble, then there are causal curves with positive Lorentzian length from
p
to
q
, but any neighborhood of
p
contains points which are not in the causal past of
q
. The time separation between these points and
q
is zero by definition, hence the time separation function is not lower semicontinuous. See the example before Theorem
3.2
.

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we review standard causal theory for
C^{0}
spacetimes. In section
3
, we introduce a new class of curves dubbed
nearly timelike
and the relation:
q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
if there is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
or if
q=p
. It satisfies
I^{+}(p)\subset\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\subset J^{+}(p)
. We define the time separation function with respect to nearly timelike curves instead of causal curves. Specifically, we define

\tau(p,q)\,=\,\sup\{L(\gamma)\mid\gamma\text{ is a nearly timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\}

whenever there is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q

and
\tau(p,q)=0
otherwise. Here
L(\gamma)
is the Lorentzian length of
\gamma
. With this definition, we show that
\tau
is lower semicontinuous in Theorem
3.2
. Moreover,
\tau
satisfies the reverse triangle inequality and
\tau(p,q)>0
if and only if
q\in I^{+}(p)
. Furthermore, our definition agrees with the usual definition for the time separation function whenever the metric is smooth (locally Lipschitz is sufficient). At the end of section
3
, we show how
C^{0}
spacetimes fit into the framework of Lorentzian pre-length spaces. However, they do
not
necessarily fit into the framework of Lorentzian length spaces since they are not locally causally closed: limit curves do not necessarily exist for nearly timelike curves. In section
4
, we find sufficient conditions to prove a limit curve theorem for nearly timelike curves. We use this to prove the existence of
nearly timelike maximizers
between two points under the aforementioned conditions. We end with a discussion and conclusion in section

Fix a smooth complete Riemannian metric

Causal curves can always be reparameterized by

.

The
causal future
of a point

if and only if there is a causal curve

from

to

or if

. The

of a point

that can be reached by a timelike curve starting from

. The
causal past

J^{-}(p)
and

are defined time-dually.
I^{\pm}(p)
are open sets

.

Remark.
Other references use

to denote the timelike future with respect to almost everywhere timelike curves; we use the notation

is said to satisfy the

satisfies the push-up property for

). However, it can fail below this regularity; in this case, pathologies like

can form (i.e. “future bubbling regions” regions where
\text{int}\big[J^{+}(p)\big]\setminus I^{+}(p)
) is nonempty). Examples and discussions of spacetimes with bubbling can be found in

The next proposition gives equivalent formulations of the push-up property for
J
. It motivates the definition of “nearly timelike curves” given in the next section.

Proposition 2.1
.

For a
C^{0}
spacetime
(M,g)
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

I^{+}\big(J^{+}(p)\big)=I^{+}(p)
.

(2)

\emph{int}\big[J^{+}(p)\big]=I^{+}(p)
.

(3)

J^{+}(p)\subset\overline{I^{+}(p)}
.

Proof.

(1) implies (2): Since
I^{+}(p)
is open and contained in
J^{+}(p)
, it’s also contained in the interior of
J^{+}(p)
. For the reverse inclusion, fix
q\in\text{int}\big[J^{+}(p)\big]
.
I^{-}(q)
meets every neighborhood of
q
; hence it meets
\text{int}\big[J^{+}(p)\big]
at some point
r
. Since
r
is in the interior, we can assume
r\neq p
. Therefore there is a causal curve from
p
to
r
and a timelike curve from
r
to
q
. Therefore
q\in I^{+}(p)
by (1).

(2) implies (3): Fix
q\in J^{+}(p)
. Since
I^{+}(q)
is an open set contained in
\text{int}\big[J^{+}(p)\big]
, we have
I^{+}(q)\subset I^{+}(p)
by assumption. Thus
q\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}
since
I^{+}(q)
meets any neighborhood
U
of
q
.

(3) implies (1): Clearly
I^{+}(p)\subset I^{+}\big(J^{+}(p)\big)
. For the reverse inclusion, fix
q\in J^{+}(p)
and
r\in I^{+}(q)
. Since
I^{-}(r)
is an open set containing
q
and
q\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}
, we have
I^{-}(r)
meets
I^{+}(p)
. Therefore
r\in I^{+}(p)
.
∎

Suppose the push-up property for
J
holds on a
C^{0}
spacetime
(M,g)
. From the previous proposition and its time-dual version, it follows that for any causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
, we have

\gamma(t)\,\in\,\overline{I^{+}\big(\gamma(s)\big)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma(s)\,\in\,\overline{I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)}

for all
s<t
in
[a,b]
. It is this property we use to define “nearly timelike curves” in
C^{0}
spacetimes.

3
Nearly timelike curves

In this section, we introduce a relation
\mathcal{J}
between
I
and
J
based on causal curves which never enter bubbling regions. Formally:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
C^{0}
spacetime.

\bullet

Let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
be a causal curve. We call
\gamma
a
a nearly timelike curve
if

\gamma(t)\,\in\,\overline{I^{+}\big(\gamma(s)\big)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma(s)\,\in\,\overline{I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)}

for all
s<t
in
[a,b]
. Clearly, timelike curves are nearly timelike. Moreover, the restriction of nearly timelike curves is nearly timelike, i.e.
\gamma|_{[c,d]}
is nearly timelike for any
[c,d]\subset[a,b]
and any nearly timelike curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
.

\bullet

We define the
nearly timelike future
of a point
p\in M
as the set

\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\,=\,\{q\in M\mid\text{there is a nearly timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\}\cup\{p\}.

The
nearly timelike past

\mathcal{J}^{-}(p)
is defined time-dually. Evidently,

q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\>\>\Longleftrightarrow\>\>p\in\mathcal{J}^{-}(q)\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad I^{+}(p)\subset\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\subset J^{+}(p).

\bullet

The
time separation function

\tau\colon M\times M\to[0,\infty]
will be defined with respect to nearly timelike curves. More precisely, if there is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
, we define

\tau(p,q)\,=\,\sup\{L(\gamma)\mid\gamma\text{ is a nearly timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\},

and
\tau(p,q)=0
otherwise.

Let
\tau_{\rm causal}
denote the usual time separation function as it’s normally defined
[
31
]
. The only difference between
\tau
and
\tau_{\rm causal}
is that the latter is defined with “causal” curves, while the former is defined with “nearly timelike” curves. Clearly
\tau\leq\tau_{\rm causal}
. They are equal whenever the push-up property for
J
holds for a
C^{0}
spacetime
(M,g)
; this follows since, in this case,
J^{+}(p)=\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
for all
p\in M
via Proposition
2.1
in the previous section. The downside of using
\tau_{\rm causal}
instead of
\tau
is that the former is not necessarily lower semi-continuous when the spacetime does not satisfy the push-up property. This is demonstrated in the example below.

Example.
\tau_{\rm causal}
is not necessarily lower semicontinuous:
Let
(M,g)
denote the bubbling spacetime in
[
11
]
. Let
p=(0,0)
denote the origin. Fix a point
q\in\text{int}\big[J^{+}(p)\big]\setminus I^{+}(p)
, i.e.
q
lies in the interior of the bubble. There are causal curves from
p
to
q
with positive Lorentzian length, hence
\tau_{\rm causal}(p,q)>0
. Any neighborhood
U
of
p
contains points
p^{\prime}\in U
such that
p^{\prime}\notin J^{-}(q)
(e.g. take
p^{\prime}\in I^{+}(p)\cap U
); therefore
\tau_{\rm causal}(p^{\prime},q)=0
, which implies
\tau_{\rm causal}
is not lower semicontinuous.

Although
\tau_{\rm causal}
is not necessarily lower semicontinuous for
C^{0}
spacetimes, the next theorem shows that
\tau
is.

Theorem 3.2
.

The following hold for a
C^{0}
spacetime
(M,g)
.

(1)

I^{+}\big(\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\big)=I^{+}(p)
and
I^{-}\big(\mathcal{J}^{-}(p)\big)=I^{-}(p)
for all
p\in M
.

(2)

r\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
whenever
r\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(q)
and
q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
.

(3)

\tau(p,r)\geq\tau(p,q)+\tau(q,r)
whenever
r\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(q)
and
q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
.

(4)

\tau(p,q)>0
if and only if
q\in I^{+}(p)
.

(5)

\tau
is lower semicontinuous.

Remark.
(1) will be referred to as the
push-up property for
\mathcal{J}
.
Unlike the usual push-up property for
J
, the push-up property for
\mathcal{J}

always
holds for
C^{0}
spacetimes. (2) shows that the relation
\mathcal{J}
is transitive. (3) is known as the
reverse triangle inequality
. (4) and (5) are axioms in the definition of a Lorentzian pre-length space
[
21
]
; after the proof of the theorem, we show how
C^{0}
spacetimes fit into the framework of Lorentzian pre-length spaces.

Proof.

(1)

Clearly
I^{+}(p)\subset I^{+}\big(\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\big)
. For the reverse inclusion, fix
q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
and
r\in I^{+}(q)
.
I^{-}(r)
is an open set containing
q
; hence
I^{-}(r)
meets
I^{+}(p)
since
q\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}
. The time-dual statement holds since the definition of a nearly timelike curve is time-symmetric.

(2)

If
r=q
or
q=p
, then the result is trivial, so suppose
r\neq q
and
q\neq p
. Let
\alpha\colon[0,1]\to M
and
\beta\colon[1,2]\to M
be nearly timelike curves from
p
to
q
and
q
to
r
, respectively. Let
\gamma\colon[0,2]\to M
be the concatenation of
\alpha
and
\beta
. We show that
\gamma
is a nearly timelike curve. Fix
s<t
in
[0,2]
. The cases
t\leq 1
or
s\geq 1
are trivial to check since
\gamma(t)
equals
\alpha(t)
or
\beta(t)
, respectively, in these cases. Assume the remaining case:
s<1
and
t>1
. Let
U
be any neighborhood of
\gamma(t)
. Then
U
intersects
I^{+}(q)
since
\gamma(t)=\beta(t)
. Therefore there is a timelike curve from
q
to some
x\in U
. Hence
x\in I^{+}\big(\gamma(s)\big)
by the push-up property for
\mathcal{J}
, hence
\gamma(t)\in\overline{I^{+}\big(\gamma(s)\big)}
. The time-dual of this argument gives
\gamma(s)\in\overline{I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)}
.

(3)

There are four cases to consider.

Case 1
: There is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
and one from
q
to
r
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. There is a nearly timelike curve
\alpha
from
p
to
q
such that
\tau(p,q)\leq L(\alpha)+\varepsilon
. Likewise there is a nearly timelike curve
\beta
from
q
to
r
with
\tau(q,r)\leq L(\beta)+\varepsilon
. If
\gamma
denotes the concatenation of
\alpha
and
\beta
, then

\tau(p,r)\,\geq\,L(\gamma)\,=\,L(\alpha)+L(\beta)\,\geq\,\tau(p,q)+\tau(q,r)-2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, the result follows.

Case 2
: There is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
but none from
q
to
r
. In this case, we must have
q=r
and
\tau(q,q)=0
. Therefore the reverse triangle inequality
\tau(p,r)\geq\tau(p,q)+\tau(q,r)
reduces to
\tau(p,q)\geq\tau(p,q)
, which is clearly true

Case 3
: There is no nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
, but there is one from
q
to
r
. This case is similar to case 2.

Case 4
: There is no nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
and also none from
q
to
r
. In this case, we have
p=q=r
and
\tau(p,p)=0
. The reverse triangle inequality reduces to
0\geq 0
, which is clearly true.

(4)

Clearly
\tau(p,q)>0
whenever
q\in I^{+}(p)
. Conversely, suppose
\tau(p,q)>0
. Then there is a nearly timelike curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
from
p
to
q
with
L(\gamma)>0
. Therefore there is a
t_{0}\in(a,b)
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})
is future-directed timelike. Without loss of generality, we can assume
t_{0}=0
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
is unit, i.e.
g\big(\gamma^{\prime}(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0)\big)=-1
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. From
[
24
, Lem. 2.9]
, there is a coordinate neighborhood
U
with coordinates
x^{\mu}
around
\gamma(0)
such that
\partial_{0}=\gamma^{\prime}(0)
. Set
\gamma^{\mu}=x^{\mu}\circ\gamma
. By definition of the derivative, there is a
\delta>0
such that
0<t<\delta
implies
|\gamma^{0}(t)/t-1|<\varepsilon
and
|\gamma^{i}(t)/t|<\varepsilon
for all
i=1,\dotsc,n
(where
n+1
is the dimension of the spacetime). Therefore, by choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, we can guarantee that for
t_{1}>0
sufficiently close to
0
, we have
\gamma(t_{1})\in I^{+}_{\eta^{\varepsilon}}\big(\gamma(0)\big)
(see Lemma 2.11(1) in
[
24
]
); then Lemma 2.9(5) in
[
24
]
implies
\gamma(t_{1})\in I^{+}\big(\gamma(0)\big)
. Therefore
\gamma(t_{1})\in I^{+}(p)
by an application of the push-up property for
\mathcal{J}
. Similarly, we can find a
t_{2}>t_{1}
sufficiently close to
t_{1}
such that
\gamma(t_{1})\in I^{-}\big(\gamma(t_{2})\big)
. Therefore
\gamma(t_{1})\in I^{-}(q)
by an application of the time-dual push-up property for
\mathcal{J}
. Thus there is a timelike curve from
p
to
\gamma(t_{1})
and one from
\gamma(t_{1})
to
q
, hence
q\in I^{+}(p)
.

(5)

Fix
(p_{0},q_{0})\in M\times M
and
t<\tau(p_{0},q_{0})
. We want to show that there is a neighborhood of
(p_{0},q_{0})
such that
\tau(p,q)>t
for all
(p,q)
in this neighborhood. If
\tau(p_{0},q_{0})=0
, then
M\times M
is such a neighborhood. Now assume
\tau(p_{0},q_{0})\in(0,\infty)
. Set
\varepsilon=\tau(p_{0},q_{0})-t.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
be a nearly timelike curve from
p_{0}
to
q_{0}
with
L(\gamma)>\tau(p_{0},q_{0})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Pick
0<a<b<1
such that
0<L(\gamma|_{[0,a]})<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
and
0<L(\gamma|_{[b,1]})<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
. Set
p^{\prime}=\gamma(a)
and
q^{\prime}=\gamma(b)
. By (4), we have
p^{\prime}\in I^{+}(p_{0})
and
q^{\prime}\in I^{-}(q_{0})
. Put
U=I^{-}(p^{\prime})
and
V=I^{+}(q^{\prime})
. We show that
U\times V
is the desired neighborhood. Indeed, for any
(p,q)\in U\times V
, the reverse triangle inequality implies

\displaystyle\tau(p,q)\,

\displaystyle\geq\,\tau(p,p^{\prime})+\tau(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})+\tau(q^{\prime},q)

\displaystyle>\,\tau(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\,L(\gamma|_{[a,b]})

\displaystyle=\,L(\gamma)-L(\gamma|_{[0,a]})-L(\gamma|_{[b,1]})

\displaystyle>\,\tau(p_{0},q_{0})-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\,t.

Lastly, if
\tau(p_{0},q_{0})=\infty
, then a similar argument as above yields the desired neighborhood. ∎

We use the previous theorem to show how
C^{0}
spacetimes fit into the framework of Lorentzian pre-length spaces. A
Lorentzian pre-length space
is a quintuple
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
satisfying the following four axioms
[
21
]
.

Axioms for a Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
:

1.

(X,d)
is a metric space,

2.

\leq
is a reflexive and transitive relation,

3.

\ll
is a transitive relation contained in
\leq
,

4.

\tau\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty]
is a lower semicontinuous map satisfying

(a)

\tau(x,y)=0
if
x\nleq y
,

(b)

\tau(x,y)>0
if and only if
x\ll y
,

(c)

\tau(x,z)\geq\tau(x,y)+\tau(y,z)
whenever
x\leq y\leq z
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a
C^{0}
spacetime with a complete Riemannian metric
h
on
M
. Define the relations
\ll
and
\leq
via

p\,\ll\,q\quad\text{iff}\quad q\in I^{+}(p)\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad p\,\leq\,q\quad\text{iff}\quad q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p).

Let
\tau
be the time separation function introduced in the beginning of this section. Then
(M,d_{h},\ll,\leq,\tau)
is a Lorentzian pre-length space.

Proof.

We verify the axioms of a Lorentzian pre-length space:

1.

(M,d_{h})
is a metric space. In fact it’s a complete metric space.

2.

\leq
is reflexive since
p\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
by definition. It’s transitive by Theorem
3.2
(2).

3.

That
\ll
is transitive follows immediately from the definition of a timelike curve. That
\ll
is contained in
\leq
means, by definition, that
p\ll q
implies
p\leq q
. This holds since
I^{+}(p)\subset\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
.

4.

\tau
is lower semicontinuous by Theorem
3.2
(5).

(a)

If
p\nleq q
, then there is no nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
. Therefore
\tau(p,q)=0
by definition.

(b)

This follows from Theorem
3.2
(4).

(c)

This follows from Theorem
3.2
(3). ∎

We end this section with some comments on Lorentzian length spaces. A Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
is called
locally causally closed
if for each
x\in U
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
such that if
p_{n},q_{n}\in U
are sequences converging to
p\in\overline{U}
and
q\in\overline{U}
, respectively, with
p_{n}\leq q_{n}
for all
n
, then
p\leq q
, see
[
21
, Def. 3.4]
. See also the “corrected” definition,
weakly locally causally closed
in
[
20
, Def. 2.16]
.
One of the requirements for a Lorentzian pre-length space to be a Lorentzian length space is that
(X,d,\ll,\leq,\tau)
is locally causally closed, see
[
21
, Def. 3.22]
. The following example shows that the Lorentzian pre-length space for a
C^{0}
spacetime
(M,g)
, as defined in Corollary
3.3
, is not necessarily (weakly) locally causally closed even when
(M,g)
is globally hyperbolic.

Example.
(M,d_{h},\ll,\leq,\tau)
is not necessarily locally causally closed:
Let
(M,g)
be the two-dimensional globally hyperbolic
C^{0}
spacetime from
[
11
]
. Let
(t,x)
be the natural coordinates on
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
p=(0,0)
denote the origin. For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
B_{2\varepsilon}
denote the usual open ball with radius
2\varepsilon
centered at
p
. (By “usual” we mean defined with with respect to the Euclidean metric
h=\delta
on
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
.) For
n=1,2,\dotsc
, set the points
p_{n}=(-\varepsilon/n,0)
on the negative
t
-axis. For all

, set
q_{n}=q=(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. Since the spacetime is isometric to Minkowski spacetime for points
t\leq|x|
, the straight line
\gamma_{n}
joining
p_{n}
to
q_{n}
is timelike and lies entirely in
B_{2\varepsilon}
. Therefore
p_{n}\leq q_{n}
. However,
p_{n}\to p
and
q_{n}\to q
but
p\nleq q
since there is no nearly timelike curve joining

to

(see
[

). Thus the corresponding Lorentzian pre-length space

is not locally causally closed. (To show that it’s not weakly locally causally closed, one makes use of Proposition 5.9 in
[

]
.)

The previous example shows that, in general, a limit curve argument will not hold for nearly timelike curves. However, one does exist if additional assumptions are imposed, see Lemma
4.1
in the next section.

4
Nearly timelike maximizers

If
\gamma
is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
such that
L(\gamma)=\tau(p,q)
where
\tau
is the time separation function introduced in the previous section (defined with respect to nearly timelike curves), then we call
\gamma
a
nearly timelike maximizer
from
p
to
q
. Note that
\tau(p,q)<\infty
whenever a nearly timelike maximizer from
p
to
q
exists.

In this section we establish sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of a nearly timelike maximizer between two points
p
and
q
in a
C^{0}
spacetime
(M,g)
, see Theorem
4.2
. We adopt the notation

\mathcal{J}(p,q)\,:=\,\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\cap\mathcal{J}^{-}(q).

Also, we need the following two definitions:

\bullet

(M,g)
is
strongly causal
if for every
p\in M
and every neighborhood
U
of
p
, there is a neighborhood
V\subset U
of
p
such that

\gamma(a),\gamma(b)\in V\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\gamma\subset U

whenever
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
is a causal curve.

\bullet

A subset
E\subset M
is called
causally plain
if

q\in J^{+}(p)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}\>\>\text{ and }\>\>p\in\overline{I^{-}(q)}

for all
p,q\in E
.

Remarks.
An assumption used throughout this section is that
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is causally plain. We emphasize that this does
not
imply that no bubbling from
p
or
q
exists, but it does imply the lack of bubbling from within
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
. Proposition
4.4
outlines the class of bubbling spacetimes to which the results in this section are relevant; a specific example based on the spacetime in
[
11
]
is provided after that proposition. Also, we do not need the full strength of strong causality for the results in this section. It would be sufficient to consider strong causality on
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
, and, in fact, it would be sufficient to consider only nearly timelike curves instead of causal curves in the definition.

The following lemma establishes a limit curve argument for nearly timelike curves. Recall from the example after Corollary
3.3
that a limit curve argument for nearly timelike curves does not hold in general, so additional assumptions need to be imposed.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a strongly causal
C^{0}
spacetime. Assume
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is compact and causally plain. Let
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,b_{n}]\to M
be a sequence of nearly timelike curves from
p_{n}
to
q_{n}
parameterized by
h
-arclength. Assume

p_{n}\to p,\quad q_{n}\to q,\quad p_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p),\quad q_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{-}(q),\quad\text{ and }\quad p\neq q.

Then there is a
b\in(0,\infty)
and a nearly timelike curve
\gamma\colon[0,b]\to M
from
p
to
q
such that for each
t\in(0,b)
, there is a subsequence
\gamma_{n_{k}}
which converges to
\gamma
uniformly on
[0,t]
.

Proof.

We first show
\sup_{n}\{b_{n}\}<\infty
. By assumption,
p_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
. Therefore
\gamma_{n}(t)\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
by Theorem
3.2
(2) for each
t\in[0,b_{n}]
. Likewise
\gamma_{n}(t)\in\mathcal{J}^{-}(q)
. Therefore each
\gamma_{n}
is contained in the compact set
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
. By
[
24
, Prop. 2.17]
, for each
x\in\mathcal{J}(p,q)
, there is a neighborhood
U_{x}
such that
L_{h}(\lambda)\leq 1
for any causal (and hence any nearly timelike) curve
\lambda\subset U_{x}
. By strong causality, there are neighborhoods
V_{x}\subset U_{x}
such that
\lambda\subset U_{x}
whenever
\lambda\colon[a,b]\to M
is a causal curve with endpoints in
V_{x}
. Since
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is covered by
\{V_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{J}(p,q)}
, there is a finite subcover
V_{1},\dotsc,V_{N}
. It follows that any nearly timelike curve with image contained in
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
has
h
-length bounded by
N
. Thus
\sup_{n}\{b_{n}\}\leq N
.

Since every sequence in
\mathbb{R}
contains a monotone subsequence, we can assume
b_{n}
is monotone by restricting to a subsequence. Then either (1)
b_{n}\to\infty
or (2)
b_{n}\to b<\infty
. The first scenario is ruled out by the paragraph above. Therefore the second scenario must hold. Moreover,
b>0
. Indeed, we have
d_{h}(p_{n},q_{n})\leq b_{n}
, and taking
n\to\infty
gives
d_{h}(p,q)\leq b
. Thus the assumption
p\neq q
implies
b>0
.

Extend each
\gamma_{n}
to inextendible causal curves
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
by, for example, concatenating each
\gamma_{n}
with the integral curve of a timelike vector field and then reparamterizing by
h
-arclength. By the usual limit curve theorem
[
24
, Thm. 2.21]
, there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}
) and a causal curve
\tilde{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
with
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=p
such that
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}
converges to
\tilde{\gamma}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. The triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle d_{h}\big(q,\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(b)\big)\,

\displaystyle\leq\,d_{h}\big(q,\gamma_{n}(b_{n})\big)\,+\,d_{h}\big(\gamma_{n}(b_{n}),\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(b)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\,d_{h}\big(q,\gamma_{n}(b_{n})\big)\,+\,|b_{n}-b|.

Since
\gamma_{n}(b_{n})\to q
and
b_{n}\to b
, the right-hand side limits to 0. Thus
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(b)\to q
. Therefore
\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,\,b]}
is a causal curve from
p
to
q
. Set
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,b]}
. Fix
t\in(0,b)
. There is a subsequence (still denoted by
b_{n}
) such that
b_{n}\geq t
for all
n
. Therefore, for this subequence, we have
\gamma_{n}=\tilde{\gamma}_{n}
on
[0,t]
; hence
\gamma_{n}
converges uniformly to
\gamma
on
[0,t]
.

It remains to show that
\gamma
is a nearly timelike curve. Fix
s<t
in
[0,b]
. By compactness, we have
\gamma\subset\mathcal{J}(p,q)
. Therefore
\gamma(t)\in\overline{I^{+}\big(\gamma(s)\big)}
since
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is causally plain. Likewise
\gamma(s)\in\overline{I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)}
. ∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a strong causal
C^{0}
spacetime. Assume
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is compact and causally plain. If
q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
with
q\neq p
, then there is a nearly timelike maximizer
\gamma
from
p
to
q
, i.e.
L(\gamma)=\tau(p,q)
.

Proof.

By definition of
\tau
, there is a sequence of nearly timelike curves
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,b_{n}]\to M
from
p
to
q
satisfying
\tau(p,q)\leq L(\gamma_{n})+1/n
. Assume each
\gamma_{n}
is parameterized by
h
-arclength. Let
\gamma\colon[0,b]\to M
be the nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
appearing in the conclusion of Lemma
4.1
. As in the proof of that lemma, let
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
be the inextendible causal curve extensions of
\gamma_{n}
and let
\tilde{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
be the resulting limit curve so that
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,b]}
.

It suffices to show
L(\gamma)\geq\tau(p,q)
. There is a subsequence
\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}|_{[0,b]}
which converges uniformly to
\gamma
; moreover,
b_{n_{k}}
limits to
b
monotonically as
k\to\infty
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By upper semicontinuity of the Lorentzian length functional
[
24
, Prop. 3.7]
, there is an
N
such that
k\geq N
implies

\displaystyle L(\gamma)+\varepsilon\,

\displaystyle\geq\,L\big(\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}|_{[0,b]}\big)

\displaystyle=\,L(\gamma_{n_{k}})+\int_{b_{n_{k}}}^{b}\sqrt{-g\big(\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\big)}

\displaystyle\geq\,\big(\tau(p,q)-1/n_{k}\big)+\int_{b_{n_{k}}}^{b}\sqrt{-g\big(\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\big)}.

As
k\to\infty
, the above integral limits to zero. This follows since (1)
b_{n_{k}}\to b
and (2) there is a neighborhood
U
of
q
such that
-g(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
is bounded on
U
for any
h
-arclength parmeterized curve
\lambda
contained in
U
. (1) is clear. To prove (2), let
U
be a coordinate neighborhood of
q
with coordinates
x^{\mu}
, and assume
U
is
h
-convex and has compact closure. Using similar triangle inequality arguments as in the proof of
[
24
, Prop. 2.2]
, it follows that the component functions
\lambda^{\mu}=x^{\mu}\circ\lambda
of any
h
-arclength parameterized curve
\lambda
are Lipschitz with the same Lipschitz constant; this proves (2). Thus, taking
k\to\infty
, we have
L(\gamma)+\varepsilon\geq\tau(p,q)
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we have
L(\gamma)\geq\tau(p,q)
.
∎

The next theorem proves a sequential continuity result for
\tau
but only from directions within
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
and under the assumption that
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is compact and causally plain. It could have applications to synthetic approaches of Lorentzian geometry which require a continuous
\tau
as in
[
30
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a strong causal
C^{0}
spacetime. Assume
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is compact and causally plain.
If

p_{n}\to p,\quad q_{n}\to q,\quad p_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p),\quad q_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{-}(q),\quad\text{ and }\quad p\neq q,

then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(p_{n},q_{n})\,=\,\tau(p,q).

Proof.

By lower semicontinuity of
\tau
, we have
\tau(p,q)\leq\liminf\tau(p_{n},q_{n})
. It suffices to show
\tau(p,q)\geq\limsup\tau(p_{n},q_{n})
. Set
t:=\limsup\tau(p_{n},q_{n})
. If
t=0
, then
\tau(p,q)\geq t
is immediate. Therefore we can assume
t>0
. Seeking a contradiction, suppose
\tau(p,q)<t
. Then there are subsequences (still denoted by
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
) and an
\varepsilon>0
such that
\tau(p,q)<\tau(p_{n},q_{n})-2\varepsilon
for all
n
and
\tau(p_{n},q_{n})\to t
as
n\to\infty
. Since
t>0
, we can assume
\tau(p_{n},q_{n})>0
for all
n
by restricting to a further subsequence. Let
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,b_{n}]\to M
be a sequence of nearly timelike curves from
p_{n}
to
q_{n}
such that
L(\gamma_{n})>\tau(p_{n},q_{n})-1/n
. Let
\gamma\colon[0,b]\to M
be the nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
appearing in the conclusion of Lemma
4.1
. As in the proof of that lemma, let
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
be the inextendible causal curve extensions of
\gamma_{n}
and let
\tilde{\gamma}\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
be the resulting limit curve so that
\gamma=\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,b]}
. There is a subsequence
\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}|_{[0,b]}
which converges uniformly to
\gamma
; moreover,
b_{n_{k}}
limits to
b
monotonically as
k\to\infty
. By upper semicontinuity of the Lorentzian length functional
[
24
, Prop. 3.7]
, there is an
N
such that
k\geq N
implies

\displaystyle L(\gamma)+\varepsilon\,

\displaystyle\geq\,L\big(\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}|_{[0,b]}\big)

\displaystyle=\,L(\gamma_{n_{k}})+\int_{b_{n_{k}}}^{b}\sqrt{-g\big(\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\big)}

\displaystyle>\,\big(\tau(p_{n_{k}},q_{n_{k}})-1/n_{k}\big)+\int_{b_{n_{k}}}^{b}\sqrt{-g\big(\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\big)}

\displaystyle>\,\big(\tau(p,q)+2\varepsilon-1/n_{k}\big)+\int_{b_{n_{k}}}^{b}\sqrt{-g\big(\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime},\tilde{\gamma}_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\big)}.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, the integral term vanishes as
k\to\infty
. Therefore, we obtain
L(\gamma)\geq\tau(p,q)+\varepsilon
, which is a contradiction.
∎

The previous results rely on
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
being compact and causally plain.
The following proposition gives sufficient conditions ensuring this and summarizes the results in this section. A specific example for which the proposition applies follows afterwards.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic
C^{0}
spacetime. For an open subset
M^{\prime}\subset M
, assume
g
is smooth on
M^{\prime}
(locally Lipschitz is sufficient) and that
J^{+}(M^{\prime})\subset M^{\prime}
. If
\overline{I^{+}(p)}\setminus\{p\}\subset M^{\prime}
for some
p\in\overline{M^{\prime}}
, then for all
q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
with
q\neq p
, the following hold:

(1)

\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is compact and causally plain.

(2)

There is a nearly timelike maximizer from
p
to
q
.

(3)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(p_{n},q_{n})=\tau(p,q)
if
p_{n}\to p
and
q_{n}\to q
with
p_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
and
q_{n}\in\mathcal{J}^{-}(q)
.

Proof.

(2) and (3) follow from (1) via Theorems
4.2
and
4.3
, respectively, so it suffices to show (1). We first show that
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is causally plain. Fix
x,y\in\mathcal{J}(p,q)
with
y\in J^{+}(x)
. We want to show
y\in\overline{I^{+}(x)}
and
x\in\overline{I^{-}(y)}
. Either
x=p
or
x\neq p
. If
x=p
, then
y\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(x)
and so the result follows. Now assume
x\neq p
. Then
x\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}\setminus\{p\}\subset M^{\prime}
. Therefore any causal curve from
x
to
y
will be contained in
M^{\prime}
since
J^{+}(M^{\prime})\subset M^{\prime}
. The metric is smooth on
M^{\prime}
and so the push-up property holds on
M^{\prime}
from which the result follows. (In fact this argument proves that
\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
is causally plain.)

Now we show
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is compact. First note that
J(p,q):=J^{+}(p)\cap J^{-}(q)
is compact by global hyperbolicity. By the Hopf-Rinow theorem,
J(p,q)
is closed and bounded (with respect to the Riemannian distance function
d_{h}
). Since
\mathcal{J}(p,q)\subset J(p,q)
, it follows that
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is also bounded. Therefore it suffices to show that
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is closed. Let
r
be a limit point of
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
. We can assume
r\neq p,q
. Let
r_{n}\in\mathcal{J}(p,q)
be a sequence with
r_{n}\to r
. Let
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,b_{n}]\to M
be a sequence of
h
-arclength parameterized nearly timelike curves from
p
to
r_{n}
. Since
J(p,q)
is compact,
[
24
, Prop. 3.4]
and its proof imply that there is a
b\in(0,\infty)
with
b_{n}\to\infty
and a causal curve
\gamma\colon[0,b]\to M
from
p
to
r
such that for any
t\in(0,b)
, there is a subsequence of
\gamma_{n}
which converges to
\gamma
uniformly on
[0,t]
.

Claim:
\gamma
is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
r
.

To prove the claim, the following fact will be useful. Fact:
\gamma(t)\in M^{\prime}
for all
t\in(0,b]
.

We first prove the fact. It suffices to show
\gamma(t)\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}\setminus\{p\}
.
\gamma(t)\neq p
since there are no closed causal curves in
M
. Let
U
be any neighborhood of
\gamma(t)
. If
t<b
, then there is a subsequence
\gamma_{n}(t)\to\gamma(t)
. Since
\gamma_{n}(t)\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}
, there are timelike curves starting at
p
and ending in
U
, hence
\gamma(t)\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}
. If
t=b
, then
r_{n}\to\gamma(b)
and the same argument applies.

Now we prove the claim. Fix
s<t
in
[0,b]
. We want to show
\gamma(t)\in\overline{I^{+}\big(\gamma(s)\big)}
and
\gamma(s)\in\overline{I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)}
. Either
s=0
or
s\neq 0
. Consider first
s\neq 0
. Then
\gamma(s),\gamma(t)\in M^{\prime}
, by the fact, and so the result follows since the push-up property applies on
M^{\prime}
. Now assume
s=0
. The proof of the fact shows that
\gamma(t)\in\overline{I^{+}(p)}
.
Now we show
p\in\overline{I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)}
. If
U
is any neighborhood of
p
, choose
0<\varepsilon<t
small enough so that
\gamma(\varepsilon)\in U
. The fact implies
\gamma(\varepsilon)\in\overline{I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)}
, hence
U
intersects
I^{-}\big(\gamma(t)\big)
.

We have proven the claim and so
r\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
. Similarly,
r\in\mathcal{J}^{-}(q)
. ∎

Example.
Let
(M,g)
denote the two-dimensional globally hyperbolic
C^{0}
spacetime from
[

]
. Let
P
denote the null cone

. The metric is smooth on

, and since the lightcones of

. Moreover, for each

, direct calculations as in

\overline{I^{+}(p)}\setminus\{p\}\subset M^{\prime}
. Therefore the previous proposition applies. In particular, for any

and

with
q\neq p
, there is a nearly timelike maximizer
\gamma
from
p
to
q
, that is,
L(\gamma)=\tau(p,q)
.

The spacetime
(M,g)
from
[
11
]
also demonstrates that global hyperbolicitiy does not necessarily imply compactness of
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
for all
p,q
. In
(t,x)
coordinates, let
p=(-1,1)
and
q=(1,1)
. Then
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
is not compact since it does not contain the origin
(0,0)
which is a limit point of
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
.

5
Discussion and conclusion

In this paper, we defined a new class of curves for
C^{0}
spacetimes dubbed “nearly timelike curves” and introduced the relation
\mathcal{J}
via
q\in\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
if there is a nearly timelike curve from
p
to
q
or if
q=p
. It satisfies
I^{+}(p)\subset\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)\subset J^{+}(p)
. The motivation for introducing
\mathcal{J}
was to obtain a lower semicontinuous time separation function
\tau
(Theorem
3.2
). Therefore
C^{0}
spacetimes can fit into the framework of Lorentzian pre-length spaces introduced in
[
21
]
(Corollary
3.3
). However, as demonstrated at the end of section
3
, the Lorentzian pre-length space associated with a
C^{0}
spacetime is not necessarily locally causally closed, which is an extra axiom in the definition of a Lorentzian length space. In summary,
C^{0}
spacetimes fit into the frame work of Lorentzian pre-length spaces but not necessarily Lorentzian length spaces.

In section
4
, we found sufficient conditions guaranteeing a limit curve argument for nearly timelike curves (Lemma
4.1
) and also the existence of a nearly timelike maximizer between two points (Theorem
4.2
), i.e. a nearly timelike curve
\gamma
from
p
to
q
such that
L(\gamma)=\tau(p,q)
. We found sufficient conditions guaranteeing a sequential continuity result,
\tau(p_{n},q_{n})\to\tau(p,q)
(Theorem
4.3
), but we must assume that the sequences
p_{n}\to p
and
q_{n}\to q
are contained in
\mathcal{J}^{+}(p)
and
\mathcal{J}^{-}(q)
, respectively. This may have applications for the bounded Lorentzian metric-spaces introduced in
[
30
]
since they require a continuous time separation function. Our results are applied to a class of spacetimes in Proposition
4.4
and a specific example is provided afterwards.

One can define
\mathcal{J}
-global hyperbolocity
by strong causality and compactness of all
\mathcal{J}(p,q)
. Since
\mathcal{J}
-global hyperbolocity is not implied by the usual notion of global hyperbolocity, it would be interesting to characterize the notion of
\mathcal{J}
-global hyperbolocity. For example, is there an analogous concept of a Cauchy surface? In fact, the hierarchy of the causal ladder is not well understood for
C^{0}
spacetimes; a detailed description of this hierarchy and how
\mathcal{J}
-global hyperbolocity fits into it would be an interesting question.

Other relations satisfying the push-up property have been defined for
C^{0}
spacetimes as in
[
10
]
. It would be interesting to compare the relationship between nearly timelike curves and
\tilde{d}^{+}
-curves in
[
10
]
.
Also, an interesting question would be to determine if nearly timelike maximizers have a causal character, which is the case for causal maximizers in locally Lipschitz spacetimes
[
16
]
. See also
[
23
,
39
]
for related results.

Appendix A
Relating the different definitions for timelike curves

In this appendix, we review the different definitions of timelike curves used in low-regularity causal theory.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to M
be a causal curve from
p
to
q
.

(1)

\gamma
is
timelike
if there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})<-\varepsilon
almost everywhere.

(2)

\gamma
is
almost everywhere timelike
if
g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})<0
almost everywhere.

(3)

\gamma
is
piecewise
C^{1}
timelike
if
\gamma
is piecewise
C^{1}
and
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
is future directed timelike for all
t
including the finite number of break points (understood as one-sided limits).

(4)

\gamma
is
locally uniformly timelike
if there is a smooth Lorentzian metric
\check{g}
such that
\check{g}<g
and
\check{g}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})<0
almost everywhere. (
\check{g}<g
means “
\check{g}(X,X)\leq 0
implies
g(X,X)<0
for all nonzero
X
.”)

The corresponding timelike futures are:

(1)

I^{+}(p)=\{q\mid\text{there is a timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\}.

(2)

I^{+}_{\rm a.e.}(p)=\{q\mid\text{there is an almost everywhere timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\}.

(3)

I^{+}_{C^{1}}(p)=\{q\mid\text{there is a piecewise $C^{1}$ timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\}.

(4)

\check{I}^{+}(p)=\{q\mid\text{there is a locally uniformly timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}\}.

Definition (1) was introduced in
[
24
]
; it’s used in this paper and in
[
26
,
14
]
. A proof showing openness of
I^{+}(p)
is given in
[
24
, Thm. 2.12]
. Definition (2) is not used as much since
I^{+}_{\rm a.e.}(p)
is not necessarily open, see
[
18
, Ex. 3.1]
; however, if the metric is smooth (locally Lipschitz is sufficient), then
I^{+}_{\rm a.e.}(p)=I^{+}(p)
, see Proposition
A.2
below. Definition (3) is used in
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
13
,
15
,
25
,
12
]
. A proof showing openness of
I^{+}_{C^{1}}(p)
is given in
[
36
, Prop. 2.6]
, see also
[
25
, Prop. 2.2]
. Definition (4) was introduced in
[
9
]
. That
\check{I}^{+}(p)
is open is shown in
[
9
, Prop. 1.4]
.

The following proposition shows that definitions (1), (3), and (4) agree with each other. It’s a mild generalization of
[
18
, Lem. 2.7]
.

Proposition A.2
.

For all points
p
in a
C^{0}
spacetime
(M,g)
, we have

I^{+}(p)\,=\,\check{I}^{+}(p)\,=\,I^{+}_{C^{1}}(p).

Moreover, if
g
is smooth (locally Lipschitz is sufficient), then they’re all equal to
I^{+}_{\rm a.e.}(p)
.

Proof.

First recognize that

\check{I}^{+}(p)\,\subset\,I^{+}_{C^{1}}(p)\,\subset\,I^{+}(p).

The first inclusion follows from causal theory for smooth (at least
C^{2}
) metrics; the endpoints of an almost everywhere timelike curve can be joined by a broken timelike geodesic in a smooth spacetime, see
[
8
, Cor. 2.4.11]
. The second inclusion follows from compactness, see
[
24
, Prop. 2.4]
. Conversely, fix
q\in I^{+}(p)
. It suffices to show
q\in\check{I}^{+}(p)
. Let
\gamma\colon[0,b]\to M
be a timelike curve from
p
to
q
and assume
\gamma
is parameterized by
h
-arclength. Then there is an
\varepsilon>0
such that
g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})<-\varepsilon
almost everywhere. By
[
9
, Prop. 1.2]
, there is a smooth Lorentzian metric
\check{g}
satisfying
\check{g}<g
and
d(\check{g},g)<\varepsilon
where

d(\check{g},g)\,=\,\sup_{p\in M,\>0\neq X,Y\in T_{p}M}\frac{|\check{g}(X,Y)-g(X,Y)|}{|X|_{h}|Y|_{h}}.

Since
\gamma
is parameterized by
h
-arclength,
d(\check{g},g)<\varepsilon
implies
\check{g}(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})<\varepsilon+g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})<0
almost everywhere. Thus
q\in\check{I}^{+}(p)
.

Lastly, suppose
g
is smooth. Then the endpoints of an almost everywhere timelike curve can be joined by a broken timelike geodesic, hence
I^{+}_{\rm a.e.}(p)\subset I^{+}_{C^{1}}(p)
. Therefore the four timelike futures are equal in this case. That locally Lipschitz is sufficient follows from Corollary 1.17 and Proposition 1.21 in
[
9
]
.
∎

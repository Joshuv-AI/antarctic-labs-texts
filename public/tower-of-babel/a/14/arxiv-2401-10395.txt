Heegaard Floer Surgery Formula and Cosmetic Surgeries
Alan Du

Abstract
. Two Dehn surgeries on a knot are called cosmetic if they yield homeomorphic three-manifolds. We show for a certain family of null-homologous knots in any closed orientable three-manifold, if the knot admits cosmetic surgeries with a pair of positive surgery coefficients, then the coefficients are both greater than 1 1 . In addition, for this family of knots, we show that 1 / q 1/q Dehn surgery for q q at least 2 2 is not homeomorphic to the original three-manifold. The proofs of these results use the mapping cone formula for the Heegaard Floer homology of Dehn surgery in terms of the knot Floer homology of the knot; we provide a new proof of this formula for integer surgeries in Spin c \Spin^{c} structures with nontorsion first Chern class.



1.
Introduction

Heegaard Floer homology is a powerful invariant of closed oriented three-manifolds introduced by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
OS04b
]
. It has been used to prove many results about three-manifold topology, especially Dehn surgery. In fact, the Heegaard Floer homology of any surgery on a knot can be computed from the knot Floer complex of the knot
[
OS08
,
OS10
,
Zem23
]
.

In this paper, we will use Heegaard Floer homology to study cosmetic surgeries on a certain class of knots in arbitrary closed oriented three-manifolds. We recall the definition of cosmetic surgeries.

Definition 1.1
.

If two Dehn surgeries on a knot yield homeomorphic manifolds, then these two surgeries are
cosmetic
. If in addition, the two surgeries are orientation-preserving homeomorphic, then these two surgeries are
purely cosmetic
.

Cosmetic surgeries are very rare; in fact, the following cosmetic surgery conjecture has been proposed:

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
Kir95
]
Problem 1.81)
.

Suppose
K
is a knot in a closed oriented three-manifold
Y
such that
Y-K
is irreducible and not homeomorphic to the solid torus. If two different Dehn surgeries on
K
are purely cosmetic, then there is a homeomorphism of
Y-K
which takes one slope to the other.

The existence of cosmetic surgeries has been ruled out in many cases for knots in
S^{3}
. For instance, Ozsváth and Szabó proved that if
K\subset S^{3}
admits cosmetic rational surgeries, then either the resulting manifold is an
L
-space or the surgery slopes have opposite signs
[
OS10
]
. Much more is known when considering purely cosmetic surgeries. Hanselman proved that any pair of purely cosmetic Dehn surgeries on knots in
S^{3}
must have slopes that are
\pm 2
or
\pm 1/q
for some integer
q

[
Han19
]
.

Less is known for knots in arbitrary three-manifolds. Ni found an obstruction to cosmetic surgeries on knots in three-manifolds satisfying a certain condition on their Thurston norms
[
Ni10
]
.

The main theorem in this paper is an analogue of Ozsváth and Szabó’s result. We consider a class of knots which were shown by Ni to have special properties on their knot Floer homology.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
Y
be a closed, oriented three-manifold, and
K\subset Y
a null-homologous knot. We say that
K
is
blow-up unknotted
if there exists a taut Seifert surface
F
for
K
, some natural number
N
, and a properly-embedded smooth disk
G
inside
X=(Y\times[0,1])\#N\overline{{\mathbb{C}}P^{2}}
with
\partial G=K\times\{0\}
and
[G]=\iota_{*}[F]\in H_{2}(X,K\times\{0\})
for
\iota:(Y,K)\to(X,K\times\{0\})
the inclusion.

Remark
.

The property of
K
being blow-up unknotted implies that
K
is null homotopic.

For example, by Kirby calculus,
K
is a blow-up unknotted knot if
K
is obtained from the unknot in
Y
by a sequence of crossing changes that change a positive crossing to a negative one.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K\subset Y
be a blow-up unknotted knot, and suppose
0<r<s
are distinct positive rational numbers with the property that
Y_{r}(K)\cong\pm Y_{s}(K)
. Then
r,s>1
or
K
is the unknot.

We also provide progress on the knot complement problem for blow-up unknotted knots.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
K\subset Y
be a nontrivial blow-up unknotted knot and
q\geq 2
, then
Y_{1/q}(K)\ncong\pm Y
. Furthermore, if
g(K)\geq 2
, then
Y_{1}(K)\ncong\pm Y
.

The proofs of these theorems rely on a mapping cone formula that relates the Heegaard Floer homology of Dehn surgery on null-homologous knots in arbitrary closed, oriented three-manifolds with the knot Floer chain complex. This result was proven for Heegaard Floer homology in
\Spin^{c}
structures whose first Chern classes are torsion by Ozsváth and Szabó, and the formula in nontorsion
\Spin^{c}
structures for zero surgeries was proven by Ni
[
OS08
,
OS10
,
Ni20
]
. A recent result by Zemke provides a very general surgery formula via an equivalence of certain Lagrangians with local systems in the Fukaya category
[
Zem23
]
. Perhaps of independent interest, we provide a conceptually-simpler proof of the integer surgery formula for nontorsion
\Spin^{c}
structures, which generalizes Ozsváth and Szabó’s and Ni’s results.

4.
Rational Surgeries

In this section, we sketch the proof of the rational surgery formula:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
K\subset Y

p,q

\mathfrak{t}\in\Spin^{c}(Y)

Ozsváth and Szabó stated and proved this theorem in the case where
\mathfrak{t}
has torsion first Chern class
[
OS10
]
. In this case, they showed this isomorphism also preserves
{\mathbb{Z}}

\Spin^{c}
structure is nontorsion, as long as grading information is ignored.

By Kirby calculus,
Y_{p/q}(K)
can be realized by surgery with coefficient

K\#O_{q/r}\subset Y\#(-L(q,r))
, where

is the greatest integer smaller than or equal to
p/q
,
a=\lfloor p/q\rfloor
, and
p/q=a+r/q
, and

is the knot which is obtained by viewing one component of the the Hopf link as a knot inside the lens space

thought of as
-q/r
surgery on the other component of the Hopf link
[
OS10
]
. Thus, one obtains the rational surgery formula by proving a formula for Morse surgeries on rationally null-homologous knots and then applying a Kunneth principle for connected sums.

Analogously to the case of integer surgeries on null-homologous knots, for
K\subset Y

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
K\subset Y
be a blow-up unknotted knot, and suppose
0<r<s
are distinct positive rational numbers with the property that
Y_{r}(K)\cong\pm Y_{s}(K)
. Then
r,s>1
or
K
is the unknot.

Proof.

Since
H_{1}(Y_{\pm p/q}(K))\cong H_{1}(Y)\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}},
we can fix
p>0
throughout. First, let
q
be relatively prime to
p
and
q\geq p
. Then
\im(\widehat{v}_{j/q})_{*}\cap\im(\widehat{h}_{(j-p)/q})_{*}=\im(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}
for all
j=0,\dots,p-1
. Thus
t_{K}^{p/q}=p\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}
. Therefore, according to equation
5.9
, for positive integral
q
with
0<p/q\leq 1
,
\rank\widehat{HF}(Y_{p/q}(K))
is a nondecreasing function of
q
. The function is strictly increasing unless
(\widehat{v}_{s})_{*}
is an isomorphism for all
s\geq 0
. Hence, by Proposition
5.5
,
K
is the unknot.

Since the total rank of
\widehat{HF}
is an invariant of the underlying (unoriented) three-manifold, the result is true whenever
0<r<s\leq 1
.

Now suppose
q,q^{\prime}
are relatively prime to
p
and
0<q<p<q^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle t_{K}^{p/q}-t_{K}^{p/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\rank(\widehat{v}_{j/q})_{*}-p\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=q}^{p-1}\rank(\widehat{v}_{j/q})_{*}-(p-q)\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}

\displaystyle\leq(p-q)(b-\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\rank\widehat{HF}(Y_{p/q^{\prime}}(K))

\displaystyle-\rank\widehat{HF}(Y_{p/q}(K))=(q^{\prime}-q)(\rank\ker(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}+b-\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*})

\displaystyle+(q^{\prime}-q)\left(2\sum_{s=1}^{g-1}(\rank\ker(\widehat{v}_{s})_{*}+b-\rank(\widehat{v}_{s})_{*})\right)

\displaystyle+2t_{K}^{p/q^{\prime}}-2t_{K}^{p/q}

\displaystyle\geq 2(q^{\prime}-q)(b-\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*})-2(p-q)(b-\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*})

\displaystyle\geq 0,

with equality only if
(\widehat{v}_{s})_{*}
is an isomorphism for all
s\geq 0
, hence
K
is the unknot by Lemma
5.5
.

Since the total rank of
\widehat{HF}
is an invariant of the underlying (unoriented) three-manifold, the result holds.
∎

In the case where
p
is
1
or
2
, the following corollary is immediate, since there do not exist distinct positive integers
q,q^{\prime}
coprime to
p
with
p/q
and
p/q^{\prime}
both greater than
1
.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
K\subset Y
be a blow-up unknotted knot, and suppose
0<q^{\prime}<q
are distinct positive numbers with the property that
Y_{p/q}(K)\cong\pm Y_{p/q^{\prime}}(K)
for
p\in\{1,2\}
, then
K
is the unknot.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
K\subset Y
be a nontrivial blow-up unknotted knot and
q\geq 2
, then
Y_{1/q}(K)\ncong\pm Y
. Furthermore, if
g(K)\geq 2
or
\rank H_{*}(\widehat{A}_{0})>b
, then
Y_{1}(K)\ncong\pm Y
.

Proof.

Let
q\geq 2
, then by Equation
5.9
,

\rank\widehat{HF}(Y_{1/q}(K))\geq(b-\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*})(q-1)+\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}+q\rank\ker(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}\geq b,

and it is strictly greater unless
(\widehat{v}_{s})_{*}
is an isomorphism for all
s\geq 0
. Hence, by Proposition
5.5
,
K
is the unknot.

If
q=1
, then

\rank\widehat{HF}(Y_{1}(K))\geq\rank(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}+\rank\ker(\widehat{v}_{0})_{*}=\rank H_{*}(\widehat{A}_{0})\geq b,

and it is strictly greater unless
\rank H_{*}(\widehat{A}_{0})=b
and
(\widehat{v}_{s})_{*}
is an isomorphism for all
s\geq 1
, which implies
g(K)\leq 1
.

Since the total rank of
\widehat{HF}
is an invariant of the underlying (unoriented) three-manifold, the result holds.
∎

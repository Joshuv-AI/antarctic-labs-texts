Rauzy dimension and finite-state dimension
Verónica Becher, Olivier Carton, Santiago Figueira

Abstract
In a paper of 1976, Rauzy studied two complexity notions, β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\beta}}{}{} and β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\beta}}{}{} , for infinite sequences over a finite alphabet. The function β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\beta}}{}{} is maximum exactly in the Borel normal sequences and β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\beta}}{}{} is minimum exactly in the sequences that, when added to any Borel normal sequence, the result is also Borel normal. Although the definition of β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\beta}}{}{} and β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\beta}}{}{} do not involve finite-state automata, we establish some connections between them and the lower dim ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\operatorname{dim}}}{}{} and upper dim ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\operatorname{dim}}}{}{} finite-state dimension (or other equivalent notions like finite-state compression ratio, aligned-entropy or cumulative log-loss of finite-state predictors). We show tight lower and upper bounds on dim ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\operatorname{dim}}}{}{} and dim ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\operatorname{dim}}}{}{} as functions of β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\beta}}{}{} and β ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\beta}}{}{} , respectively. In particular this implies that sequences with dim ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\operatorname{dim}}}{}{} zero are exactly the ones that that, when added to any Borel normal sequence, the result is also Borel normal. We also show that the finite-state dimensions dim ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\operatorname{dim}}}{}{} and dim ¯ \displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{\operatorname{dim}}}{}{} are essentially subadditive. We need two technical tools that are of independent interest. One is the family of local finite-state automata, which are automata whose memory consists of the last k \displaystyle k read symbols for some fixed integer k \displaystyle k . We show that compressors based on local finite-state automata are as good as standard finite-state compressors. The other one is a notion of finite-state relational (non-deterministic) compressor, which can compress an input in several ways provided the input can always be recovered from any of its outputs. We show that such compressors cannot compress more than standard (deterministic) finite-state compressors.

From the

\displaystyle\alpha_{u}

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(u,i)

\displaystyle i=0,\ldots,m^{\prime}-1

\displaystyle 1-\theta^{\prime}(u,i)

\displaystyle i=0,\ldots,m^{\prime}-1

\displaystyle\alpha_{u}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=0}^{m^{\prime}-1}\theta^{\prime}(u,i)\right)^{\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}}

Combining this inequality with the statement of Lemma

4
Relational
,
functional
and
deterministic compressors

To prove Theorem
2
, we need to work with compressors with
possibly non-functional behaviour. We call them
relational
compressors
. We also introduce, as special case of them, the standard
functional (non-deterministic) and deterministic versions.

A
transducer
with input and output alphabet
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}
is a tuple

\displaystyle\langle Q,\Delta,I,F\rangle
, where
\displaystyle Q
is a finite set of states,

\displaystyle\Delta\subset Q\times{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{*}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{*}\times Q
is a finite set of transitions,
\displaystyle I\subseteq Q
is the
non-empty subset of initial states, and
\displaystyle F\subseteq Q
is the subset of final
states. A transition
\displaystyle(p,u,v,q)
is written
\displaystyle p\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u|v}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u|v$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u|v}}{\xrightarrow{u|v}}q
.

Relational compressors.

A
relational compressor
is a
transducer

\displaystyle\langle Q,\Delta,I,F\rangle
that could
possibly yield several accepting runs for the same input sequence
\displaystyle x
. We
introduce notation to deal with such runs. A
finite run
\displaystyle\eta
from
a state
\displaystyle p
to a state
\displaystyle q
is a sequence of consecutive transitions

\displaystyle p\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{0}|v_{0}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}q_{1}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|u_{1}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{1}|u_{1}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|u_{1}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|u_{1}}}q_{2}\cdots q_{n-1}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}}}q
. Its
label
\displaystyle\Lambda(\eta)
is the pair

\displaystyle(u,v)
where its
input label
\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(\eta)
and
output
label
\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(\eta)
are respectively
\displaystyle u\triangleq u_{0}\cdots u_{n-1}
and

\displaystyle v\triangleq v_{0}\cdots v_{n-1}
.

Similarly, an
infinite run
\displaystyle\eta
is an infinite sequence

\displaystyle q_{0}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{0}|v_{0}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}q_{1}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|v_{1}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{1}|v_{1}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|v_{1}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|v_{1}}}q_{2}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{2}|v_{2}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{2}|v_{2}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{2}|v_{2}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{2}|v_{2}}}q_{3}\cdots
of
consecutive transitions. Its
label
\displaystyle\Lambda(\eta)
is the pair
\displaystyle(x,y)

where its
input label
\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(\eta)
and
output
label
\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(\eta)
are respectively
\displaystyle x\triangleq u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}\cdots
and

\displaystyle y\triangleq v_{0}v_{1}v_{2}\cdots
. Note that both the input and output labels can be finite
as labels of transitions might be empty.

An infinite run
\displaystyle q_{0}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{x|y}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle x|y$}}}}{\xrightarrow{x|y}}{\xrightarrow{x|y}}\infty
is
accepting
if 1) both
\displaystyle x
and

\displaystyle y
are infinite, 2)
\displaystyle q_{0}
is initial and 3)
\displaystyle q_{i}
is final for infinitely
many indices
\displaystyle i
. This is the classical Büchi acceptance condition.
A pair
\displaystyle(x,y)
is
accepted
if it is the label
\displaystyle\Lambda(\eta)
of some

accepting run

\displaystyle\eta
. If
\displaystyle\eta
is either a finite or an infinite run,

\displaystyle\eta[m:n)
is the finite run
\displaystyle q_{m}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{m}|v_{m}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{m}|v_{m}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{m}|v_{m}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{m}|v_{m}}}q_{m+1}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{m+1}|u_{m+1}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{m+1}|u_{m+1}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{m+1}|u_{m+1}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{m+1}|u_{m+1}}}q_{m+2}\cdots q_{n-1}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{n-1}|v_{n-1}}}q_{n}
of
length
\displaystyle n-m
.

Although a
relational compressor
can compress an input in different
ways, it has to be always possible to recover the input from any of its
outputs. So an additional requirement holds for any
relational
compressor
: if
\displaystyle(x,y)
and
\displaystyle(x^{\prime},y)
are
accepted
then
\displaystyle x=x^{\prime}
.
A state is
useful
if it occurs in some accepting run. Since the
realized relation remains unchanged when
useless
states are removed, we
always assume in the sequel that the set
\displaystyle Q
only contains
useful
states.

Let
\displaystyle\eta
be an accepting run
\displaystyle q_{0}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{0}|v_{0}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{0}|v_{0}}}q_{1}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|v_{1}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{1}|v_{1}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|v_{1}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{1}|v_{1}}}q_{2}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u_{2}|v_{2}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u_{2}|v_{2}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{2}|v_{2}}}{\xrightarrow{u_{2}|v_{2}}}q_{3}\cdots
in a
relational compressor

\displaystyle C
. According to the
notation introduced above,
\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(\eta[0:n))
and
\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(\eta[0:n))

are respectively
\displaystyle u_{0}\cdots u_{n-1}
and
\displaystyle v_{0}\cdots v_{n-1}
. The
compression ratios

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta)
and
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\bar{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta)
of
\displaystyle C
along the run
\displaystyle\eta
are
respectively defined by

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\underline{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta)\triangleq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|\Lambda_{1}(\eta[0:n))|}{|\Lambda_{0}(\eta[0:n))|}\quad\text{and}\quad\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\bar{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta)\triangleq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|\Lambda_{1}(\eta[0:n))|}{|\Lambda_{0}(\eta[0:n))|}.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be the class of all
relational compressors
.
The
lower compression ratio

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}}{}{}:{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow[0,1]
and the
upper
compression ratio

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}{\bar{\rho}}}{}{}(x):{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow[0,1]
are respectively
defined by

\immediate\immediate\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\underline{\rho}}{}{}(x)\triangleq\inf_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\inf_{\Lambda_{0}(\eta)=x}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta)\quad\text{and}\quad\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}{\bar{\rho}}}{}{}(x)\triangleq\inf_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\inf_{\Lambda_{0}(\eta)=x}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\bar{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta),

(1)

where
\displaystyle\eta
ranges over all accepting runs in
\displaystyle C
such that
\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(\eta)=x
.

Functional compressors.

A
functional compressor

\displaystyle C
is a
relational compressor
such that the
relation it realizes is actually a function: if
\displaystyle(x,y)
and
\displaystyle(x,y^{\prime})
are

accepted
by
\displaystyle C
then
\displaystyle y=y^{\prime}
. Hence

\displaystyle C(x)=y
. Notice that
\displaystyle C

\displaystyle C(x)
.

If
\displaystyle C

then
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}_{C}}{}{}
and
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\bar{\rho}_{C}}{}{}
are defined
in the same way as for the
relational compressors
and
\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\underline{\rho}^{\rm f}}{}{}
,

\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}{\bar{\rho}^{\rm f}}}{}{}
are defined as in (
1
) except that
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

is the class of all
functional compressors
.

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}_{C}}{}{}

4.1

For
\displaystyle w

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|w|\!|_{u}}{}{}

of
\displaystyle u

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}|w|_{u}}{}{}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}|\!|w|\!|_{u}}{}{}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|0000|_{00}}{}{}=3

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|0000|\!|_{00}}{}{}=2

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|w|\!|_{u}}{}{}

\displaystyle|w|

\displaystyle|u|

Now we consider the notion of

\displaystyle\ell\geqslant 1

\displaystyle 0\log 0=0
. By

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle 0\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}ah_{\ell}}{}{}(w)\leqslant 1

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{ah}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{ah}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\underline{ah}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\triangleq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}ah_{\ell}}{}{}(x[0:n))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\overline{ah}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\triangleq\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{ah}_{\ell}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\quad\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\overline{ah}_{\ell}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\triangleq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}ah_{\ell}}{}{}(x[0:n))

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}h_{\ell}}{}{}(w)\triangleq-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}}f_{u}\log f_{u}\quad\text{where}\quad f_{u}=\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|w|_{u}}{}{}}{|w|-|u|+1}

\displaystyle 0\log 0=0
.
The

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{h}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{h}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle x
are respectively defined as follows.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\underline{h}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\triangleq\liminf_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{h}_{\ell}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\quad\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\underline{h}_{\ell}}{}{}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\triangleq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}h_{\ell}}{}{}(x[0:n))

\displaystyle\displaystyle\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.4844,0.1055,0.1172}\overline{h}}{}{}(x)

Lemma 5
.

Let
\displaystyle C
be a
relational compressor
with
\displaystyle\#Q
states and let
\displaystyle r_{C}
be
the maximum number of symbols written by a single transition of
\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\ell\geqslant 0

\sum_{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}}2^{-{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)}\leqslant(\#Q)^{2}(1+\ell r_{C}).

Proof.

We claim that for each integer
\displaystyle k
the cardinality of the set

\displaystyle\{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}:{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)=k\}
is at most
\displaystyle(\#Q)^{2}2^{k}
. Let
\displaystyle p

and
\displaystyle q
be two states of
\displaystyle C
and
\displaystyle v
be a word. Let us prove that the
set
\displaystyle\{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}:p\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{w|v}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle w|v$}}}}{\xrightarrow{w|v}}{\xrightarrow{w|v}}q\}
has cardinality at

\displaystyle 1
. Suppose that there are
\displaystyle 2
distinct words
\displaystyle w_{1},w_{2}
in this

transducer
is trim, there is a finite run

\displaystyle i\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{u|v^{\prime}}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle u|v^{\prime}$}}}}{\xrightarrow{u|v^{\prime}}}{\xrightarrow{u|v^{\prime}}}p
from an initial state
\displaystyle i
and a final run

\displaystyle q\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{x|y}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle x|y$}}}}{\xrightarrow{x|y}}{\xrightarrow{x|y}}\infty
. It follows that both pairs
\displaystyle(uw_{1}x,v^{\prime}vy)
and

\displaystyle(uw_{2}x,v^{\prime}vy)
are
accepted
and this is a contradiction. If a word
\displaystyle w

belongs to
\displaystyle\{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}:{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)=k\}
, then there are two
states
\displaystyle p,q
and a word
\displaystyle v
of length
\displaystyle k
such that
\displaystyle w
belongs to

\displaystyle\{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}:p\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{w|v}}{\xrightarrow{\smash{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle w|v$}}}}{\xrightarrow{w|v}}{\xrightarrow{w|v}}q\}
. This proves the upper bound
for the cardinality of
\displaystyle\{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}:{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)=k\}
since there
are
\displaystyle(\#Q)^{2}
possible choices for
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle q
and
\displaystyle 2^{k}
possible choices
for
\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}}2^{-{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell r_{C}}\#\{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}:{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)=k\}2^{-k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\ell r_{C}}(\#Q)^{2}=(\#Q)^{2}(1+\ell r_{C}).

∎

Lemma 6

(adapted from
[
9
]
)
.

For every
\displaystyle x\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{ah}}{}{}(x)\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}}{}{}(x)\text{ and }{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{ah}}{}{}(x)\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}{\bar{\rho}}}{}{}(x).

Proof.

We follow the proof that
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{ah}}{}{}(x)\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}^{\rm f}}{}{}(x)

The proof for

\displaystyle C
be a

with
\displaystyle k=\#Q

Without loss of generality, it can be assumed that the length of the input

[

If the length of an input label

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{ah}}{}{}(x)\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\overline{ah}}{}{}(x)\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\bar{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(\eta)

be a fixed integer. Then

\sum_{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|\Lambda_{0}(\eta[0:n))|\!|_{w}}{}{}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)\leqslant|\Lambda_{1}(\eta[0:n))|.

{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}ah_{\ell}}{}{}(\Lambda_{0}(\eta[0:n)))\leqslant\frac{|\Lambda_{1}(\eta[0:n))|}{\ell K(n)}+\frac{1}{\ell}\log\left(\sum_{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}}2^{-{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}L_{C}}{}{}(w)}\right).

Lemma 7
.

Let
\displaystyle x\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\mathbb{N}}
. For each positive real number
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
, there exists an

\displaystyle C
such

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{\rho}_{C}}{}{}(x)\leqslant{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{ah}}{}{}(x)+\varepsilon

Proof.

By definition of
\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\underline{ah}}{}{}(x)
, there exists an integer
\displaystyle\ell\geqslant 1
and an

\displaystyle(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
of integers such that

for each integer
\displaystyle k\geqslant 0
. By replacing the sequence

\displaystyle(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|x[0:\ell n_{k})|\!|_{w}}{}{}/n_{k}

\displaystyle w
of length
\displaystyle\ell
. Let
\displaystyle f_{w}
be this limit for
\displaystyle w
of length
\displaystyle\ell
.
Note that the numbers
\displaystyle f_{w}
satisfy the relations

. Let
\displaystyle m\geqslant 1
be an integer to be fixed later. For each
word
\displaystyle u
of length
\displaystyle\ell m
, let
\displaystyle f_{u}
be defined by
\displaystyle f_{u}\triangleq f_{w_{1}}\cdots f_{w_{m}}

\displaystyle u
is factorized
\displaystyle u=w_{1}\cdots w_{m}
with
\displaystyle|w_{i}|=\ell
for
each
\displaystyle 1\leqslant i\leqslant m
. It is easily checked that
\displaystyle\sum_{u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}}f_{u}=1
. This equality implies that there exists a family
\displaystyle(v_{u})_{u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}}
of words such that the set
\displaystyle\{v_{u}:u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}\}
is prefix-free and that
\displaystyle|v_{u}|\leqslant\lceil-\log f_{u}\rceil
holds
for each word
\displaystyle u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}
. Let us consider the
deterministic
compressor

\displaystyle C
that replaces each word
\displaystyle u
of length
\displaystyle\ell m
by the

\displaystyle v_{u}
. Such a compressor is of course

\displaystyle\{v_{u}:u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}\}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle n_{k}

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\displaystyle|C(x[0:\ell n_{k}))|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|x[0:\ell n_{k})|\!|_{u}}{}{}|v_{u}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|x[0:\ell n_{k})|\!|_{u}}{}{}\lceil-\log f_{u}\rceil

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n_{k}}{m}-\sum_{u\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell m}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|x[0:\ell n_{k})|\!|_{u}}{}{}\log f_{u}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{n_{k}}{m}-\sum_{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|x[0:\ell n_{k})|\!|_{w}}{}{}\log f_{w}.

Dividing by
\displaystyle\ell n_{k}
gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{|C(x[0:\ell n_{k}))|}{\ell n_{k}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\ell m}-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{w\in{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}\mathbb{B}}{}{}^{\ell}}\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0.2969,0.3594}|\!|x[0:\ell n_{k})|\!|_{w}}{}{}}{n_{k}}\log f_{w}.

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
, the proof is complete.

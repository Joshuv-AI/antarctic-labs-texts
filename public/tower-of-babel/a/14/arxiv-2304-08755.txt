Introduction

In
[
10
]
, Stein and Weiss gave a class of integral operators with the nonnegative kernels which satisfy some homogeneity and the rotational invariance. Beckner
[
1
]
represented it as Stein-Weiss lemma and obtained the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
K
is a non-negative kernel defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfies continuous on any domain that excludes the point
(0,0)
, homogeneous of degree
-n,K(\delta u,\delta v)=\delta^{-n}K(u,v)
, and
K(Ru,Rv)=

K(u,v)
for any
R\in SO(n)
. Then
T
is a from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
bounded integral operator defined by

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)dy

with

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq A\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

(1)

holds for
1<p<\infty
, the optimal constant is given by

A=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K\left(x,\hat{e}_{1}\right)|x|^{-n/p^{\prime}}dx

(2)

and
\hat{e}_{1}
is a unit vector in the first coordinate direction.

The inequality (
1
) was given by Stein and Weiss
[
10
]
. In
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, Beckner proved that the constant
A
in (
2
) is the best constant.
Inspired by Theorem
1
, we investigate a class of integral operators on Heisenberg group and obtained sharp bounds in weighted type spaces.

Let us first recall some basic knowledge about the Heisenberg group. The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a non-commutative nilpotent Lie group, with the underlying manifold
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}
with the group law

x\circ y=\left(x_{1}+y_{1},\ldots,x_{2n}+y_{2n},x_{2n+1}+y_{2n+1}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(y_{j}x_{n+j}-x_{j}y_{n+j}\right)\right)

and

\delta_{r}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1}\right)=\left(rx_{1},rx_{2},\ldots,rx_{2n},r^{2}x_{2n+1}\right),\quad r>0,

where
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})
and
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{2n},y_{2n+1})
.
The Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
coincides with the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. We denote the measure of any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
by
|E|
.Then

|\delta_{r}(E)|=r^{Q}|E|,d(\delta_{r}x)=r_{Q}dx,

where
Q=2n+2
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

The Heisenberg distance derived from the norm

|x|_{h}=\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}+x_{2n+1}^{2}\right]^{1/4},

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})
,is given by

d(p,q)=d(q^{-1}p,0)=|q^{-1}p|_{h}.

This distance
d
is left-invariant in the sense that
d(p,q)
remains unchanged when
p
and
q
are both left-translated by some fixed vector on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.Furthermore,
d
satisfies the triangular inequality
[
7
]

d(p,q)\leq d(p,x)+d(x,q),\quad p,x,q\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

For
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,the ball and sphere with center
x
and radius
r
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are given by

B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(x,y)<r\},

and

S(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(x,y)=r\},

respectively.And we have

|B(x,r)|=|B(0,r)|=\Omega_{Q}r^{Q},

where

\Omega_{Q}=\frac{2\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(n/2)}{(n+1)\Gamma(n)\Gamma((n+1)/2)}

is the volume of the unit ball
B(0,1)
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
\omega_{Q}=Q\Omega_{Q}
(see
[
8
]
). For more details about Heisenberg group can be refer to
[
4
]
and
[
11
]
.

The weighted-type space on Euclidean space
H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\alpha>0
consists all measurable functions
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\text{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{\alpha}|f(x)|<\infty.

The function
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is a norm on the space which is
dx
almost everywhere equal.
In this paper, we will consider the weighted-type space on Heisenberg group. Our definition is as follow.

Definition 1
.

The weighted-type space on Heisenberg group
H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
with
\alpha>0
consists of all measurable functions
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}:=\text{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}|f(x)|<\infty,

where
\|\cdot\|_{H_{\alpha}^{\infty}}
is a norm on the space which also is
dx
almost everywhere equal.

In harmonic analysis, the study of multilinear integral operators and their properties is often more desirable and interesting. In
[
12
]
, Wu and Yan extened Theorem
1
to the case of multilinearity, now we utilize their promotional methods to give
m
-linear integral operator with the nonnegative kernels on Heisenberg group.

Definition 2
.

Suppose that
K
is a nonnegative kernel defined on
\mathbb{H}^{nm}
and satisfies the homogenous degree
-mn
,

K(|t|_{h}x,|t|_{h}y_{1},\ldots,|t|_{h}y_{m})=|t|_{h}^{-mn}K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}),

and

K(Rx,Ry_{1},\ldots,Ry_{m})=K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})

for any
R\in SO(n)
, then
K
denotes the kernel of

T_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

(3)

Taking
e_{1}
is a unit vector in the first coordinate direction in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then we have

T_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})f_{1}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

(4)

Next, we will provide our main result of mulitilinear integral operator with kernel
K
in weighted type space on Heisenberg group.

2
Sharp bound for m-linear integral operator

Theorem 2
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
with
\alpha_{j}>0
for any
j=1,\ldots,m
, if

H_{m}:=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}dy_{1},\ldots dy_{m}<\infty,

(5)

then
T(f_{1},\ldots f_{m})(x)
is bounded from
H_{a_{1}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times H_{\alpha_{m}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Moreover, if condition (
4
) is satisfied, then the following formula for the norm of the operator hold

\|T_{m}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=H_{m}.

(6)

Proof of Theorem
2
.

For convenience, we take

T_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{j})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

Using the above identity and the definition of weighted-type space on Heisenberg group
H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we can infer that

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{i})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}dy_{1}\cdots y_{m}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}|x|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}dy_{1}\cdots y_{m}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

for every
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, from which by taking the supremum in
H_{\alpha_{i}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, it follows that

\|T_{m}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq H_{m},

(7)

and we obtain the boundedness of
T_{m}
on
H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Then by taking

f_{\alpha_{j}}(x)=\begin{cases}1/|x|^{\alpha_{j}}_{h},&{x\neq 0},\\
0,&{x=0},\end{cases}

(8)

it is clear that for any
j=1,\ldots,y_{m}
, we have

\|f_{\alpha_{j}}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{j}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=1.

(9)

Bring (
8
) into identity (
4
) and through simple calculations, we have

\displaystyle|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{\alpha_{j}}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{j})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}|\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{j}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{j}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{j}}dy_{1}\cdots y_{m}=H_{m}

for
x\neq 0
, where we used the condition
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
.
From (
10
) and by using the definition of norm in the weighted-type space on Heisenberg group
H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we can infer that

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{\alpha_{j}}(\delta_{\alpha_{j}}y_{j})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|=H_{m}.

Combining (
9
) with (
11
) it follows that

\|T_{m}\|_{\prod_{j=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{j}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\geq H_{m}.

(12)

Through (
7
) and (
12
), it implies that (
6
) holds. We have finished the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

3
Sharp bound for Hardy operator

In this section, we will study
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy operator on Heisenberg group and obtain its sharp bound in weighted-type space on Heisenberg group.

The
n
-dimensional Hardy operator on Heisenberg group is defined by Wu and Fu
[
13
]

Hf(x):=\frac{1}{|B(0,|x|_{h})|}\int_{B(0,|x|_{h})}f(y)dy,x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

(13)

where
f
be a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. And they proved
H
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
,
1<p\leq\infty
.
Moreover,

\|H\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\frac{p}{p-1},1<p\leq\infty.

In
[
5
]
, Fu and Wu introduced the
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy operator, which is defined by

\mathfrak{H}_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}^{m}|x|^{mn}}\int_{|(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|<|x|}f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m}),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
are nonnegative locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Inspired by
[
5
]
, we define the m-liner n-dimensional Hardy operator on Heisenberg group is as follow.

Definition 3
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be nonnegative locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then

\mathfrak{H}_{m}^{h}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}|x|_{h}^{mQ}}\int_{|(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|_{h}<|x|_{h}}f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},

(14)

where
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.

Now, we formulate our main results as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{i}\in H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
,
\alpha>0
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
with
\alpha_{j}>0
for any
j=1,\ldots,m.
If

\mathcal{A}^{h}_{m}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}}\int_{|(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|_{h}<1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}<\infty,

(15)

then
\mathfrak{H}_{m}^{h}
is bounded from
H_{a_{1}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times H_{\alpha_{m}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, and the following formula for the norm of the operator hold

\|\mathfrak{H}_{m}^{h}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\mathcal{A}^{h}_{m}.

According to Theorem
3
, we have the following result holds.

Corollary 1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N},\alpha>0
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
with
\alpha_{i}>0
for any
i=1,\ldots,m
.Then the operator
\mathfrak{H}_{m}^{h}
is bounded from
H_{a_{1}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times H_{\alpha_{m}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
if and only if (
3
) hold. Moreover, if (
15
) hold, the following formula holds

\mathcal{A}^{h}_{m}=\frac{2Q^{m}}{2^{m}(mQ-\alpha)}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{mQ}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

Now, we begin to prove our result.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The proof process in this section is similar to that of Theorem
2
, so we only provide a brief proof and focus on the calculation of the optimal constant.

Combining (
14
) with the definition of weighted-type space on Heisenberg group
H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{H}_{m}^{h}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\text{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}|x|_{h}^{mQ}}\int_{|(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|_{h}<|x|_{h}}f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|

\displaystyle=\text{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}}\int_{|(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})|_{h}<1}f_{1}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}z_{1})\cdots f_{m}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}z_{m})dz_{1}\cdots dz_{m}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}}\int_{|(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|_{h}<1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|x|_{h}^{-\alpha}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{m}^{h}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

This indicates for every
f_{i}\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}(H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}),i=1,\ldots,m
, by taking the supremum in
H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
that

\|\mathfrak{H}_{m}^{h}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq\mathcal{A}_{m}^{h}.

(16)

Next, we take
f_{\alpha_{j}}(x)
defined by (
8
), which satisfies (
9
).
Using (
14
), we can obtain

|x|_{h}^{\alpha}\left|\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}|x|_{h}^{mQ}}\int_{|(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|_{h}<|x|_{h}}f_{\alpha_{1}}(y_{1})\cdots f_{\alpha_{m}}(y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|=\mathcal{A}_{m}^{h}.

(17)

Combining (
9
) with (
17
), it follows that

\|\mathfrak{H}_{m}^{h}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\geq\mathcal{A}_{m}^{h}.

Thus, we have finished the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

In order to obtain the sharp constant, we learned and borrowed the method in
[
5
]
.
For convenience, we take several special cases for separate calculations, and the general situation is a summary of these special cases.

Case 1 when
m=1
.

This situation returns to equation (
13
), here we provide another form of definition, then its norm of sharp bound is

\mathcal{A}^{h}_{1}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}}\int_{|y|_{h}<1}|y|_{h}^{-\alpha}dy<\infty.

Using the polar coordinate transformation formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{h}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}}\int_{|y|_{h}<1}|y|_{h}^{-\alpha}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S(0,1)}r^{-\alpha+Q-1}drdy^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}}{\Omega_{Q}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{-\alpha+Q-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{Q-\alpha}.

At this point, we have obtained the sharp constant when
m=1
, which is

\|\mathfrak{H}_{1}^{h}\|_{H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\frac{Q}{Q-\alpha}.

(18)

Case 2 when
m=2
.

According to Theorem
3
and the polar coordinate transformation formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{h}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{2}}\int_{|(y_{1},y_{2})|_{h}<1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S(0,1)}\int_{S(0,1)}r_{1}^{-\alpha_{1}+Q-1}r_{2}^{-\alpha_{2}+Q-1}dr_{1}dr_{2}dy_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}dy_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}_{Q}}{\Omega^{2}_{Q}}\frac{1}{2Q-\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha_{1}+Q-1)}t^{-\alpha_{2}+Q-1}(1-t^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2}_{Q}}{\Omega^{2}_{Q}}\frac{1}{4Q-2\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha_{1}+Q)-1}x^{\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha_{2}+Q)-1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{Q^{2}}{2(2Q-\alpha)}B(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{Q^{2}}{2(2Q-\alpha)}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})\Gamma(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2})}{\Gamma(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

This indicates that when
m=2
, the sharp constant is

\mathcal{A}^{h}_{2}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2(2Q-\alpha)}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2})\Gamma(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2})}{\Gamma(Q-\frac{\alpha}{2})}

(19)

Case 3 when
m\geq 3
.

The proof of case when
m\geq 3
is similar to that of case 2, we can continue to use the same method.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{h}_{m}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}}\int_{|(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|_{h}<1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\int_{(S(0,1))^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}^{-\alpha_{i}+Q-1}dr_{1}\cdots dr_{m}dy_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\cdots dy_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{\Omega_{Q}^{m}}\frac{2}{2^{m}(mQ-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha_{1}+Q)-1}x^{\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha_{2}+Q)-1}

\displaystyle\times\cdots\times(1-x)^{\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha_{m-1}+Q)-1}x^{\frac{1}{2}(-\alpha_{m}+Q)-1}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2Q^{m}}{2^{m}(mQ-\alpha)}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{mQ}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

When we take
m=1
and
m=2
in (
20
), the result is the same as (
18
) and (
19
).
So we obtained the sharp constant for
\mathcal{A}_{m}^{h}
, which is

\mathcal{A}^{h}_{m}=\frac{2Q^{m}}{2^{m}(mQ-\alpha)}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(\frac{Q}{2}-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{mQ}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

4
Sharp bounds for Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya and
Hilbert operators

In this section, we study
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya and Hilbert operators in weighted-type space on Heisenberg group. First of all, let’s recall their definitions and properties.

The
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya is defined by

P_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nm}}\frac{f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})}{[\text{max}(|x|^{n},|y_{1}|^{n},\ldots,|y_{m}|^{n})]^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

When
n=m=1
, the linear Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator
P_{1}
is considered in
[
6
]
, Hardy obtained that the norm of Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})(1<q<\infty)
, that is

\|P_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))}=\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}.

The Hilbert operator is the essential extension of the classical Hilbert’s inequality , the
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hilbert operator is defined by

P^{*}_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,y_{m})(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nm}}\frac{f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})}{(|x|^{n}+|y_{1}|^{n}+\cdots+|y_{m}|^{n})^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

For
n=1
, the following is a know sharp estimate

\int_{0}^{\infty}T_{1}^{*}f(x)g(x)dx\leq\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi/p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty)}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}(0,\infty)}}.

Now, we give the definitions of
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya and Hilbert operators on Heisenberg group.

Definition 4
.

Suppose that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be nonnegative locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator is defined by

P^{h}_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})}{[\text{max}(|x|_{h}^{Q},|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},\ldots,|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})]^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

(21)

Definition 5
.

Suppose that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be nonnegative locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hilbert operator is defined by

P^{h*}_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})}{(|x|^{Q}_{h}+|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q}+\cdots+|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

(22)

Now, we formulate our main results as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N},f_{i}\in H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n}),\alpha>0
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
with
\alpha_{i}>0
for any
i=1,\ldots,m
. If

\mathcal{B}^{h}_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}}{[\max(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},\ldots,|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})]^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}<\infty,

(23)

then
P^{h}_{m}
is bounded from
H_{a_{1}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times H_{\alpha_{m}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
if and only if the following formula for the norm of the operator hold

\|P_{m}^{h}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\frac{mQ\omega_{Q}^{m}}{\alpha\prod_{j=1}^{m}(Q-\alpha_{j})}.

Theorem 5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{i}\in H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
,
\alpha>0
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
with
\alpha_{i}>0
for any
i=1,\ldots,m
. If

\mathcal{B}^{h*}_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}}{[\max(1+|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q}+\cdots+|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})]^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}<\infty,

(24)

then
P^{h}_{m}
is bounded from
H_{a_{1}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times H_{\alpha_{m}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
H_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
if and only if the following formula for the norm of the operator hold

\|P_{m}^{h*}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\left(\frac{2\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(n/2)}{(n+1)\Gamma(n)\Gamma((n+1)/2)}\right)^{m}\frac{\prod^{m}_{i=1}\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{Q})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{Q})}{\Gamma(m)}.

In the above proof,we have obtained that
\mathcal{B}^{h}_{m}
and
\mathcal{B}^{h*}_{m}
are clearly valid, so we omit their proof and will focus on proving their sharp constants.

Proof of Theorem
4
and
5
.

In order to get the sharp constant of
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator, we will consider the following two scenarios and combine them to obtain our result.

Case 1 when
m=2
.

In this case , we have

\mathcal{B}^{h}_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{\alpha_{2}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},|y_{2}|_{h}^{Q})]^{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}.

By calculation,we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{\alpha_{2}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},|y_{2}|_{h}^{Q})]^{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}<1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}-2Q}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<|y_{2}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{2}-2Q}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}.

\displaystyle I_{0}=

\displaystyle\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}<1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{Q}^{2}}{(Q-\alpha_{1})(Q-\alpha_{2})},

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}-2Q}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{Q}}{Q-\alpha_{2}}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha-Q}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{Q}^{2}}{\alpha(Q-\alpha_{2})}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<|y_{2}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{2}-2Q}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}}{Q-\alpha_{1}}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}>1}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-\alpha-Q}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{2}}{\alpha(Q-\alpha_{1})}.

Thus,we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{\alpha_{2}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},|y_{2}|_{h}^{Q})]^{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2Q\omega_{Q}^{2}}{\alpha(Q-\alpha_{1})(q-\alpha_{2})}.

Case 2 when
m\geq 3
.

Let

\displaystyle E_{0}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{k}|_{h}\leq 1,1\leq k\leq m\};

\displaystyle E_{1}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{1}|_{h}>1,|y_{k}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h},2\leq k\leq m\};

\displaystyle E_{i}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{i}|_{h}>1,|y_{j}|_{h}<|y_{k}|_{h},|y_{k}|_{h}\leq|y_{i}|_{h},1\leq j<i<k\leq m\};

\displaystyle E_{m}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{m}|_{h}>1,|y_{j}|_{h}<|y_{m}|_{h},1<j<m\}.

Obviously, we have

\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}E_{j}=\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n},E_{i}\cap E_{j}=\emptyset.

Taking

K_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{i}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},\ldots,|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})]^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},

then we begin to calculate
K_{j}
with
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.

K_{0}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\int_{|y_{j}|\leq 1}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{j}}dy_{j}=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(Q-\alpha_{j})},

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y_{1}|>1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}-Qm}dy_{1}\prod_{j=2}^{m}\int_{|y_{j}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h}}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{j}}dy_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m-1}}{\prod_{j=2}^{m}(Q-\alpha_{j})}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha-Q}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{\alpha\prod_{j=2}^{m}(Q-\alpha_{j})}.

So we can deduce that

K_{j}=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{\alpha\prod_{1\leq i\leq m,i\neq j}(Q-\alpha_{j})}.

Then we can obtain that

K_{m}=\frac{mQ\omega_{Q}^{m}}{\alpha\prod_{j=1}^{m}(Q-\alpha_{j})}.

Combining the above two cases, we have completed the Theorem
4
.
Next, we follow the method given by Bényi and Oh
[
3
]
to calculate the sharp constant of
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hilbert operator. Using the polar coordinates and making change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{m}^{h*}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{1}}\cdots|y_{m}|_{h}^{-\alpha_{m}}}{(1+|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q}+\cdots+|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\omega_{Q}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r_{1}^{Q-\alpha_{1}-1}\cdots r_{m}^{Q-\alpha_{m}-1}}{(1+r_{1}^{Q}+\cdots+r_{m}^{Q})^{m}}dr_{1}\cdots dr_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{Q^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{\frac{1}{Q}(Q-\alpha_{1})-1}\cdots t_{m}^{\frac{1}{Q}(Q-\alpha_{m})-1}}{(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m})^{m}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{Q^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{Q}}\cdots t_{m}^{-\frac{\alpha_{m}}{Q}}}{(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m})^{m}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{Q^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{-\beta_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{-\beta_{m}}}{(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m})^{\alpha}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}.

Set

I_{m}(\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{-\beta_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{-\beta_{m}}}{(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m})^{\alpha}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}.

By a simple calculate, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\alpha}t^{\beta}}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-t)^{-\beta}t^{\alpha+\beta-2}dt

\displaystyle=B(1-\beta,\alpha+\beta-1).

Using the variable substitution
t_{m}=(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m}-1)q_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{m}(\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{-\beta_{1}}\cdots t_{m-1}^{-\beta_{m-1}}}{(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m-1})^{\alpha-1+\beta_{m}}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+q_{m})^{\alpha}q_{m}^{\beta_{m}}}dq_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(1-\beta_{m},\alpha+\beta_{m}-1)I_{m}(\alpha-1+\beta_{m},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1}).

Using the inductive method combine with the properties of Gamma function, we have

I_{m}(\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Gamma(1-\beta_{i})\Gamma(\alpha-k+\prod_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{i})}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.

Thus, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{m}^{h*}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{Q^{m}}\frac{\prod^{m}_{i=1}\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{Q})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{Q})}{\Gamma(m)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(n/2)}{(n+1)\Gamma(n)\Gamma((n+1)/2)}\right)^{m}\frac{\prod^{m}_{i=1}\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{Q})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{Q})}{\Gamma(m)}.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
4
and
5

On Structural Aspects of Friends-And-Strangers Graphs
Ryan Jeong

Abstract
Given two graphs X X and Y Y with the same number of vertices, the friends-and-strangers graph 𝖥𝖲 ⁡ ( X , Y ) \mathsf{FS}(X,Y) has as its vertices all n ! n! bijections from V ⁡ ( X ) V(X) to V ⁡ ( Y ) V(Y) , with bijections σ , τ \sigma,\tau adjacent if and only if they differ on two elements of V ⁡ ( X ) V(X) , whose mappings are adjacent in Y Y . In this article, we study necessary and sufficient conditions for 𝖥𝖲 ⁡ ( X , Y ) \mathsf{FS}(X,Y) to be connected for all graphs X X from some set. In the setting that we take X X to be drawn from the set of all biconnected graphs, we prove that 𝖥𝖲 ⁡ ( X , Y ) \mathsf{FS}(X,Y) is connected for all biconnected X X if and only if Y ¯ \overline{Y} is a forest with trees of jointly coprime size; this resolves a conjecture of Defant and Kravitz. We also initiate and make significant progress toward determining the girth of 𝖥𝖲 ⁡ ( X , Star n ) \mathsf{FS}(X,\text{Star}_{n}) for connected graphs X X , and in particular focus on the necessary trajectories that the central vertex of Star n \text{Star}_{n} takes around all such graphs X X to achieve the girth.

The friends-and-strangers graph

A more concrete example of an object that friends-and-strangers graphs generalize is the famous

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

Here, we review some common families of graphs and elementary graph theory terminology that we shall refer to throughout this article.

•

[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

•

The vertex and edge sets of a graph
G

and
E(G)
, respectively.

•

Define the disjoint union of a collection of graphs

\bigsqcup_{i\in I}V(G_{i})

Common Families of Graphs

Assume that the vertex set of all graphs is given by

As remarked in

An
open ear decomposition
of a graph
G
is a finite ordered sequence of subgraphs
[P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{r}]
of
G
such that the following properties hold.

•

The edge sets of the
P_{i}
partition the edges of
G
, i.e.

E(G)=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{r}E(P_{i})
.

•

P_{0}
is a simple cycle, and for all
i\geq 1
,
P_{i}
is a path that is not a simple cycle (i.e. its endpoints are not the same vertex in the graph
G
).

•

For
i\geq 1
, each endpoint of
P_{i}
is contained in some previous subgraph
P_{j}
, for
j<i
, while all internal vertices of
P_{i}
are not contained in any such
P_{j}
.

r=3

This yields a natural characterization of all biconnected graphs on
n\geq 3
vertices.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
8
]
)
.

A simple graph

on

vertices has an open ear decomposition if and only if it is biconnected.

The following proposition will be referenced briefly in our study of biconnected graphs with one ear. In particular, it follows immediately from this proposition that any open ear decomposition of a biconnected graph

must have the same number of ears

.

Proposition 3.5

(
[
6
]
)
.

A biconnected graph

with an open ear decomposition with

ears must satisfy
r=|E(G)|-|V(G)|
. (

is sometimes called the Betti number of
G
.)

Proposition 3.8

Let

Proof.

The proposition follows directly from Theorem

in the case that

3.2
Biconnected Graphs with One Ear

We begin by extending Theorem
3.1

for all biconnected graphs that can be decomposed into an open ear decomposition with at most one ear (i.e.
r\leq 1
in Definition
3.3
). We concern ourselves with the
r=1
case (
r=0
is the statement of Theorem
3.1
). The result is immediate for
n=3
(any biconnected graph is necessarily
K_{3}
) and for
n=4
(any biconnected graph with at most one ear is Hamiltonian), so take
n\geq 5
.

Let
[P_{0},\ P_{1}]
denote the open ear decomposition of some biconnected
G
with one ear. Denote the vertices in the simple path
P_{1}
by
\{v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
(as ordered in the path). Let
\{v_{0}^{\prime},\dots,v^{\prime}_{k}\}
with
v_{0}^{\prime}=v_{0}
,
v_{k}^{\prime}=v_{m}
be the shorter path from
v_{0}
to
v_{m}
in
P_{0}
, and
\{w_{0},\dots,w_{\ell}\}
with
w_{0}=v_{m}
,
w_{\ell}=v_{0}
the longer. We can assume

(i.e., there exist inner vertices in
P_{1}
and the shorter path in
P_{0}
), as the connectedness of
\mathsf{FS}(X,Y)
is immediate from

being Hamiltonian if these bounds are not satisfied. We must have the strict inequality
k+m<n
, since
k<|E(P_{0})|-1
,

, and by Proposition
3.5

|E(P_{0})|+|E(P_{1})|=|E(X)|=n+1
. We can also assume, without loss of generality, that
k,m\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor

(the length of the shorter of two paths between the same vertices in
P_{0}
, and
|E(P_{0})|<n
). If this were not true for

(i.e.
m>\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
), construct a different open ear decomposition
[P_{0}^{\prime},P_{1}^{\prime}]
of
G
that includes the vertices in
P_{1}
in the new initial cycle
P_{0}^{\prime}
; by Proposition
3.5
,
|E(P_{0}^{\prime})|+|E(P_{1}^{\prime})|=|E(X)|=n+1
and
|E(P_{0}^{\prime})|>\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1
, so that the length of
P_{1}^{\prime}
,
|E(P_{1}^{\prime})|
, has the desired upper bound.

With these reductions, construct the graph
X
from
\text{Cycle}_{n}
by removing the edge
\{1,n\}
from

and adding the edges
\{n,k\}
and
\{1,n-m+1\}
, where
2\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor<n-m+1\leq n-1
and

. Note that
(n-m+1)-k=n-(k+m)+1\geq 2
, or there must be at least one vertex strictly between the vertices
k
and
n-m+1
in
\text{Cycle}_{n}
. We now have the following observation.

Proposition 3.9
.

Any non-Hamiltonian biconnected graph
G
on
n\geq 5
vertices with one ear is isomorphic to a graph
X
with
V(X)=[n]
and
E(X)=\{\{i,i+1\}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\leq i\leq n-1\}\cup\{1,w\}\cup\{n,v\}
, with the inequalities
2\leq v\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor<w\leq n-1
and
v+[(n+1)-w]<n
.

Proof.

Construct
X
from
G
as in the preceding discussion: here, we have the correspondences
v=k
and
w=n-m+1
, and
k+m+\ell=n+1
(by Proposition
3.5
). Define the mapping
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}[n]=V(X)\to V(G)
by

\displaystyle\varphi(v)=\begin{cases}v^{\prime}_{i}&v=i,\ \ 1\leq i\leq k\\
w_{i}&v=k+i,\ \ 1\leq i\leq\ell-1\\
v_{m-i}&v=n-i+1,\ \ m\geq i\geq 1\end{cases}

One can confirm that

is indeed a graph isomorphism, so

.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Any tree

with

Proposition 3.11
.

Any tree

Proof.

We can assume

\mathcal{T}
and

yield a subgraph of

with central vertex

adjacent to

and not a leaf: take

∎

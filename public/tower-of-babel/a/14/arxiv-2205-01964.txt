1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to study the weighted compactness of the commutators of the Littlewood-Paley square function
g
associated with the Schrödinger operator
L=-\Delta+V
, where
\Delta
is the well-known Laplacian operator and the function
V
is a nonnegative potential enjoying certain reverse Hölder inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 3
).

It is well-known that the Schrödinger operator
L
plays a fundamental role in Harmonic analysis and PDE
[
26
]
. In the past few decades, Great achievements have been made in the study of the operator
L
and its associated operators such as Riesz transforms
\nabla L^{-1/2}
. It is worthy to pointing out that the
estimates of the solutions of
L
and Riesz transforms
\nabla L^{-1/2}
were given in
[
15
,
27
,
39
]
. Great interests have been shown to investigate the properties of Hardy type spaces associated with
L
, such like, the Hardy space
H^{p}_{L}

[
14
,
12
]
for
0<p\leq 1
; the local Hardy spaces
h_{L}

[
38
]
. The semigroup maximal function
e^{-tL}(t>0)
and the Riesz transforms
\nabla L^{-1/2}
on Heisenberg groups were further considered in
[
23
]
.

In order to state more known results, we need to introduce some notations and definitions. Let
V
be a nonnnegative function defined on
{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.
For every ball
B(x,r)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say
V
is in the reverse Hölder class
RH_{q}
(
q>n/2
), if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
V
enjoys the following reverse Hölder inequality

\Big(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}V^{q}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}V(y)dy\Big).

We need to give the definition of a class of more generalized
\rm BMO
space associated with
\rho.

Definition 1.1
.

(
\rm BMO_{\theta}(\rho)({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
space,
[
1
]
)
. For
\theta>0
,
we defined the class
\rm BMO_{\theta}(\rho)({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
of locally integrable functions
f
such that

(1.1)

\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)-f_{B}|dy\leq C\Big(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{\theta},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, where
f_{B}=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f
and the function
\rho

[
27
]
associated with
V(x)\in RH_{q}
is defined by

(1.2)

\rho(x)=\sup_{r>0}\Big\{r:\frac{1}{r^{n-2}}\int_{B(x,r)}V(y)dy\leq 1\Big\}.

For a function
f\in\rm BMO_{\theta}(\rho)({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, the infimum of
the constants in (
1.1
) is denoted by
\|f\|_{\rm BMO_{\theta}(\rho)}
.

We now need tointroduce a more larger weights class than the classical
A_{p}
weights class as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

(
A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
weights class,
[
2
]
)
.
Let
w
be a nonnegative, locally integrable function on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
1<p<\infty
, a weight
w
is said in the class
A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that for all balls
B=B(x,r)
, it holds that

(1.3)

\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(y)dy\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(y)^{-1/(p-1)}dy\Big)^{p-1}\leq C\Big(1+\frac{r}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{\theta p}.

w\in A_{1}^{\rho,\theta}
means that there exists a constant
C
such that for all balls
B
, the following inequality holds

M^{\theta}_{V}(w)(x)\leq Cw(x),\ a.e.\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where

M^{\theta}_{V}(f)(x)=\sup_{x\in B}\frac{1}{\Psi_{\theta}(B)|B|}\int_{B}|f(y)|dy
and

\Psi_{\theta}(B)=(1+r/\rho(x))^{\theta}
for
B=B(x,r)
.

Remark 1.3
.

It is easy to see that
\rm BMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset\rm BMO_{\theta}(\rho)({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset\rm BMO_{\theta^{\prime}}(\rho)({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for
0<\theta<\theta^{\prime}
,
A_{p}\subsetneq A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
and the classes

are increasing with
\theta

.

The Littlewood-Paley
g
function and the semi-group maximal function associated to Schrödinger operator
L
are defined by

(1.4)

g(f)(x)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{d}{dt}e^{-tL}(f)(x)\Big|^{2}tdt\Big)^{1/2},

and

(1.5)

\mathcal{T}^{*}(f)(x)=\sup_{t>0}|e^{-tL}f(x)|=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy\Big|,

where
k_{t}
is the kernel of the operator
e^{-tL}
.
The commutators of
g
and
\mathcal{T}^{*}
with

are defined by

(1.6)

g_{b}(f)(x)=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{d}{dt}e^{-tL}(b(x)-b(\cdot))(f)(x)\Big|^{2}tdt\Big)^{1/2};

and

(1.7)

\mathcal{T}_{b}^{*}(f)(x)=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}k_{t}(x,y)(b(x)-b(y))f(y)dy\Big|.

For

In another paper, Tang

Theorem B
(

,

As was said before, this paper is devoted to studying the weighted compactness for commutators of
Littlewood-Paley function related to Schrödinger operator
L
. Recall that, a linear or sublinear operator
T
is said to be a compact operator from a Banach space
X
to a Banach space
Y
, if the image under
T
of any bounded subset in
X
is a relatively compact subset in
Y.
Given a locally integrable function
b
,
the commutator
[b,T]
of
T
is given by
[b,T](f)(x)=bTf(x)-T(bf)(x).

We now present some known result about the compactness of some classical operators.
In 1975, Corders
[
11
]
first showed that the commutators of the Fourier multipliers and pseudodifferential operators are compact on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
provided
b\in{\rm CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
{\rm CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}
in the
\rm BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
topology. In 1978, Uchiyama
[
32
]

surprisingly proved that the commutator of singular intergral opertors with
{\rm Lip}_{1}({\rm S}^{n-1})
kernels
is compact on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
if and only if

b\in{\rm CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Since then, the study on the compactness of commutators of different operators
has attracted much more attention. We refer the reader to
[
21
,
22
,
33
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
25
]
for the compactness of commutators of
linear operators,

[
3
,
4
,
37
,
30
,
31
]
for multilinear operators and
[
6
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
for extrapolation theory of compactness. In particular, in a recent paper , the result of Corders
[
11
]
was extended to the bilinear case and the the condition
b\in\rm CMO({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
was improved to a more bigger space which is called
\rm{XMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Note that these compactness results are all concerned with the space
\text{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However, Theorem B shows that the
L^{p}
boundedness holds for more larger space
\text{BMO}_{\theta}(\rho)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, rather than
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the weights class
A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
is more larger than
A_{p}
weights class. Let
\text{ CMO}_{\theta}(\rho)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the
\rm BMO_{\theta}(\rho)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
topology. Then, it is quite natural to ask the following question:

Question 1.4
.

Whether the operator
g_{b}
is compact from
L^{p}(w)
to
L^{p}(w)
when
w\in A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
and
b
belongs to the space
\text{ CMO}_{\theta}(\rho)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
?

One may ask the same question for the operator
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{b}
. Very recently, we obtained the following weighted compactness for
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{b}
in
[
34
]
. However, an extra weights condition needs to be assumed.

Theorem C
(
[
34
]
).
Let
1<p<\infty
,
w\in A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
and
b\in\rm CMO_{\theta}(\rho)({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. If

satisfies the following condition

(1.8)

\lim_{A\rightarrow+\infty}A^{-np+n}\int_{|x|>1}\frac{w(Ax)}{{|x|}^{np}}dx=0,

then the operator
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{b}
is a compact operator from
L^{p}(w)
to
L^{p}(w)
.

Therefore, another question arises naturally:

Question 1.5
.

Is it possible to remove condition (
1.8
) from Theorem C ?

The main purpose of this paper is to give firm answers to the above questions. Our results are as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. If
w\in A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
and
b\in\rm CMO_{\theta}(\rho)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
then the operator
g_{b}
is a compact operator from
L^{p}(w)
to
L^{p}(w)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. If
w\in A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
and

,
then the operator
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{b}
defined by (

to

We first recall some notation and lemmas which will be used later. Given

The auxiliary function

(
[

]

There exist

In particular,

(3.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{3}_{13}&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{1}\Big(\int_{|x-y|<\rho(x)}t^{-\frac{n+\delta+1}{2}}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}tdt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{1}t^{-\delta}\Big(t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{|x-y|<\rho(x)}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{1}t^{-\delta}\Big(\rho(x)^{-n}\int_{|x-y|<\rho(x)}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{1}t^{-\delta}dt\Big)^{1/2}M_{V,\eta}f(x)\\
&\leq CM_{V,\eta}f(x).\end{split}

This inequality, combining with (
3.21
), (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) yields that

(3.25)

L_{13}\leq CM_{V,\eta}f(x).

For
L_{14}
, decompose it into the following three parts

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L_{14}&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|<\sqrt{t}}t^{-\frac{n+\delta}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{c|x-y|^{2}}{t}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&\quad\times|b(x+h)-b(y)||f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}tdt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\quad+C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\Big(\int_{\sqrt{t}\leq|x-y|<\rho(x)}t^{-\frac{n+\delta}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{c|x-y|^{2}}{t}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&\quad\times|b(x+h)-b(y)||f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}tdt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\quad+C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{|x-y|<\rho(x)}t^{-\frac{n+\delta}{2}-1}e^{-\frac{c|x-y|^{2}}{t}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&\quad\times|b(x+h)-b(y)||f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}tdt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&=:L^{1}_{14}+L^{2}_{14}+L^{3}_{14}.\end{split}

It is easy to see that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{1}_{14}&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|<\sqrt{t}}t^{-\frac{n+\delta}{2}-1}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}tdt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{-(1+\delta)}\Big(t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{|x-y|<\sqrt{t}}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{-(1+\delta)}dt\Big)^{1/2}M_{V,\eta}f(x)\\
&\leq CM_{V,\eta}f(x).\end{split}

Using (
3.14
) with
M>n+\theta\eta
, we get

(3.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{2}_{14}&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{-(1+\delta)}\Big(t^{\frac{M-n}{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k-1}\sqrt{t}\leq|x-y|<2^{k}\sqrt{t}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{M}}dy\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{-(1+\delta)}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k(M-n-\theta\eta)}}{2^{k\theta\eta}(2^{k}\sqrt{t})^{n}}\int_{|x-y|<2^{k}\sqrt{t}}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{1}^{\rho(x)^{2}}t^{-(1+\delta)}dt\Big)^{1/2}M_{V,\eta}f(x)\\
&\leq CM_{V,\eta}f(x).\end{split}

If
t\geq 1
, then
t^{-\delta/2}\leq 1
. Taking
N=1
, we have
(\sqrt{t}/\rho(x))^{-2N}=\rho(x)^{2}/t
, therefore

(3.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{3}_{14}&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho(x)}{t}\Big)^{2}\Big(\int_{|x-y|<\rho(x)}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho(x)}{t}\Big)^{2}\Big(\rho(x)^{-n}\int_{|x-y|<\rho(x)}|f(y)|dy\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\rho(x)^{2}}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho(x)}{t}\Big)^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}M_{V,\eta}f(x)\\
&\leq CM_{V,\eta}f(x).\end{split}

Sum up (
3.26
)-(
3.29
) in all, we have

(3.30)

L_{14}\leq CM_{V,\eta}f(x).

Inequality (
3.30
) together with (
3.25
), (
3.20
), (
3.19
) and (
3.18
) gives that

(3.31)

L_{1}\leq C|h|^{\delta}M_{V,\eta}f(x).

By Lemma
2.4
for any
p^{\prime}\leq\eta<\infty
, we get

(3.32)

\|L_{1}\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C|h|^{\delta}\|M_{V,\eta}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C|h|^{\delta}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

which together with (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) yields that

\|g_{b}f(h+\cdot)-g_{b}f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C(|h|+|h|^{\delta})\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We will complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
by slightly modifying the proof in
[
34
]
. We only give the proof of the modified part here.
Since the condition (
1.8
) was used only in the step to prove for
w\in A_{p}^{\rho,\theta}
,

(3.33)

\lim_{A\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|x|>A}|\mathcal{T}^{*}_{b,\gamma}f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx=0,

whenever
f\in F
. Therefore it suffices to prove (
3.33
) without the condition (
1.8
). As the proof of Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that for any
N>0
, there is a constant
C_{N}>0
so that

(3.34)

\sup_{t>0}k_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}.

In fact, we may control the left side of (
3.34
) by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{t>0}k_{t}(x,y)&\leq C_{N}\sup_{t>0}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{5t}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(y)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&\leq C_{N}\sup_{\sqrt{t}\geq|x-y|}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{5t}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&\quad+C_{N}\sup_{\sqrt{t}<|x-y|}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{5t}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&=I+J.\end{split}

We need to consider the contributions of
I
and
J
. For
I
, using the estimate
e^{-s}\leq\frac{C}{s^{n/2}}
, we have

I\leq\sup_{\sqrt{t}\geq|x-y|}\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\leq\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}.\\

For
J
, using the estimate
e^{-s}\leq\frac{C}{s^{n/2+N/2}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}J&\leq C_{N}\sup_{\sqrt{t}<|x-y|}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\Big(\frac{t}{|x-y|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}+\frac{N}{2}}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&=\sup_{\sqrt{t}<|x-y|}\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|x-y|}\Big)^{N}\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&\leq\sup_{\sqrt{t}<|x-y|}\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}+\rho(x)}{|x-y|+\rho(x)}\Big)^{N}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{t}+\rho(x)}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}\\
&=\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N}.\end{split}

Therefore

\sup_{t>0}k_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C_{N}}{|x-y|^{n}}\Big(1+\frac{|x-y|}{\rho(x)}\Big)^{-N},

holds for any
N>0
. Then, similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we may show that (
3.33
) holds. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

∎

$100\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}

[
2
]
. The particularly narrow width of the
X(3872)
of
1.19\pm\pm 0.21\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
makes this difference especially stark
[
3
,
4
]
. In addition, the
X(3872)
is known to decay through many isospin violating channels, including
\rho^{0}J/\psi

[
1
]
and
\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}

[
5
]
.

Nearly two decades since its discovery, the
X(3872)
lacks a definitive interpretation although several models have been proposed in order to explain its unusual properties. Due to its close proximity to the
D^{0}\bar{D}^{*0}
threshold, the molecular
D^{0}\bar{D}^{*0}+\bar{D}^{0}D^{*0}
interpretation offers a compelling possibility
[
6
]
. Two other explanations include a compact tetraquark interpretation
[
7
]
or a superposition between a molecular and a conventional charmonium state
[
8
]
. It is predicted that the branching fractions of the decays of the
X(3872)
to
\pi\chi_{cJ}
or
\pi\pi\chi_{cJ}(J=0,1,2)
depend on the internal structure of the
X(3872)

[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. These pionic transitions of the
X(3872)
to
\chi_{cJ}
therefore serve as an excellent way to probe the quark configuration of the
X(3872)
.

A previous BESIII analysis
[
5
]
measured the single pion branching fraction ratio

\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}=0.88^{~+0.33}_{~-0.27}~\pm 0.10,

where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second systematic, and placed upper limits of

\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c0}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}<19

and

\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c2}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}<1.1

at the 90% confidence level. Though these upper limits were consistent with both a conventional interpretation and a four-quark state (i.e. either a compact tetraquark or a hadronic molecule), the measurement on
X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}
was consistent with the four-quark state and disfavored the conventional picture.
Another BESIII analysis
[
12
]
reduced the upper limit on the
J=0
decay to

\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c0}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}<3.6

at the 90% confidence level and placed upper limits on the two-pion decays to
\chi_{cJ}
. However, none of the aforementioned upper limits are stringent enough to rule out additional interpretations for the
X(3872)
.

In this paper, we strengthen these results by searching for the decays
X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1,2}
with BESIII data. For
J=1
, this decay is predicted to be extremely sensitive to the quark configuration of the
X(3872)
. For a conventional charmonium state, the ratio
\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)/\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)
is expected to be approximately 12.5 assuming the single
\pi^{0}
is produced through two gluons
[
9
]
, although recent studies offer a critique of this prediction
[
13
,
14
]
. Meanwhile, for a four-quark state, the same ratio is predicted to be approximately
2.9\times 10^{-3}

[
11
]
. This stark difference indicates that even an upper limit could provide key information disfavoring a conventional interpretation. Additionally, it is predicted that for
J=2
, this ratio is further suppressed and on the order of
10^{-6}

[
10
]
for a molecular state.

We exclusively reconstruct the processes
X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1,2}
, collectively called the search channel. The
X(3872)
is produced
alongside a photon, referred to as
\gamma_{1}
throughout this paper, in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions.
The
\chi_{c1}
and
\chi_{c2}
are reconstructed via
\chi_{c1,2}\to\gamma_{2}J/\psi
and the
J/\psi
is reconstructed via
J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}
or
J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
. We refer to the photon produced alongside the
J/\psi
in
\chi_{c1,2}
decays as
\gamma_{2}
.
The final state in the search channel is therefore
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\pi^{0}\pi^{0}l^{+}l^{-}
, where each
\pi^{0}
is reconstructed from two photons and
l^{+}l^{-}
refers to either an electron or muon pair.

With the same production of
X(3872)
in the decay
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma X(3872)
, the branching fraction of the search channel is normalized to that of
X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
, referred to as the normalization channel throughout this paper. This cancels common systematic uncertainties.

The BESIII detector
[

records symmetric
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions provided by the BEPCII storage ring

The solenoid is supported by an octagonal flux-return yoke with resistive plate counter muon identification modules interleaved with steel.

1~{\textrm{GeV}}/c
is
0.5\%
, and the
{\textrm{d}}E/{\textrm{d}}x
resolution is
6\%

(
5\%

1

[

[

Signal MC samples are generated for the processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma X(3872)
with

and

with

\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)
measured in Ref.
[
5

III
Event Selection

The final states for both the normalization and search channels are fully reconstructed.
For the normalization channel, we use the same event selection criteria as in the single-pion analysis
[
5
]
. Details for the selection of the search channel are described in the following.

Charged tracks detected in the MDC are required to be within a polar angle (
\theta
) range of
|\rm{cos\theta}|<0.93
, where
\theta
is defined with respect to the
z
-axis, which is the symmetry axis of the MDC. Their distance of closest approach to the interaction point (IP) must be less than 10 cm along the
z
-axis,
|V_{z}|
, and less than 1 cm in the transverse plane,
|V_{xy}|
.
To distinguish between electrons and muons, each electron pair is required to have at least one candidate with
E/p>0.85
, while each muon pair requires both candidates to have
E/p<0.25
, where
E
is the energy of the candidate deposited in the EMC and
p
is the momentum of the candidate reconstructed in the MDC.
To select
J/\psi
events, we require the
l^{+}l^{-}
mass to be within
$33\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}
of the nominal
J/\psi
mass.

Photons are identified as showers within the EMC. Energy deposited in nearby TOF counters is added to the shower energy. The deposited energy of each shower must be more than 25 MeV in the barrel region (
|\cos\theta|<0.80
) and more than 50 MeV in the end cap region (
0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92
). To suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the event, the difference between the EMC time and the event start time is required to be within
[0, 700] ns.
The
\pi^{0}
candidates are formed from pairs of photons and are required to satisfy
$107\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}<M(\gamma\gamma)<$163\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$/c^{2}
. In addition, a
\pi^{0}
mass fit is performed separately for each pair and
\chi^{2}<2500
is imposed.

A six constraint (6C) kinematic fit is performed on the final-state particles. Momentum and energy account for four of these constraints, while the remaining two constraints comprise
\pi^{0}
mass constraints.
We require
\chi_{6C}^{2}/\text{d.o.f}<9.0
for
\chi_{c1}
events and
\chi_{6C}^{2}/\text{d.o.f}<8.0
for
\chi_{c2}
events, where
\chi_{6C}^{2}/\text{d.o.f}
is the 6C kinematic fit
\chi^{2}
statistic per degree of freedom. These values were chosen by optimizing over a figure of merit (FOM)

FOM=\sqrt{-2\ln{\frac{P(\mu\tau;N_{\text{bkg}})P(\mu;N_{\text{sig}})}{P(N_{\text{bkg}};N_{\text{bkg}})P(N_{\text{sig}};N_{\text{sig}})}}},

(1)

P

N_{\text{sig}}
is the number of events in the signal region,
N_{\text{bkg}}
is the number of events in the background region, and
\tau
is the expected ratio of background events in the background region to background events in the signal region. The signal and background regions are described in the next section. The free parameter
\mu

N_{\text{sig}}
and
N_{\text{bkg}}
are not integers in exclusive MC, the figure of merit is computed by sampling Poisson distributions with means
N_{\text{sig}}
and
N_{\text{bkg}}

\gamma_{1}
against the CM and extending to the end of the plot window at
4.0\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}

X(3872)

J/\psi

X(3872)

V
Systematic Uncertainties

A variety of sources of systematic uncertainties on the branching fraction ratio are addressed. In addition to quantities such as the cross-section and luminosity, several uncertainties on the branching fraction
\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1,2}\right)
cancel upon computing the ratio. Here we describe these remaining systematic uncertainties on the ratio
R_{\chi_{cJ}}
, given by Eq.
2
.

V.1
Photon detection and charged track efficiencies

Photons and charged tracks in the BESIII detector were previously measured to have systematic uncertainties of 1% per photon
[
44
]
and charged track
[
45
]
. In the ratio, five photons (four from
\pi^{0}
decays and one from the
\chi_{cJ}
decay) and two charged pions are uncanceled. Therefore, the photon detection efficiency systematic uncertainty is
5\%
and the charged track reconstruction efficiency is
2\%
.

V.2
Input branching fractions

The remaining branching fractions
\mathcal{B}\left(\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma\right)
and
\mathcal{B}\left(\chi_{cJ}\to\gamma J/\psi\right)
do not cancel in the branching fraction ratio. These branching fractions and their uncertainties are taken directly from the PDG
[
32
]
.

V.3
Kinematic fit

The systematic uncertainty due to the kinematic fit is addressed by comparing the effect of a loosened
\chi^{2}/\text{d.o.f.}
cut to the nominal value on control samples. The process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta J/\psi
with
\eta\to\gamma\gamma\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
is used to estimate contributions to the uncertainty from the search channel, while the process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma_{\text{ISR}}\psi^{\prime}
, with
\psi(3686)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi
, is used to estimate contributions from the normalization channel. The efficiency of the
\chi^{2}/\text{d.o.f.}
selection is computed in signal MC for the search and normalization channels by dividing the number of events with the nominal
\chi^{2}/\text{d.o.f.}
by the number of events with a loose selection of
\chi^{2}/\text{d.o.f.}<25.0
. The same efficiency is then calculated in data for both channels. The ratio between the search and normalization channels is then computed in MC and in data. The difference between the values of these ratios,
1.7\%
for
\chi_{c1}
and
2.1\%
for
\chi_{c2}
, is taken as the systematic uncertainty.

V.4
E/p
cut

The systematic uncertainty on the
E/p
cut is computed in the same way as the kinematic fitting systematic uncertainty in Section
V.3
. The nominal selection criteria in the search and normalization control samples are relaxed to
E/p<0.5
for both muons and
E/p>0.5
for either electron. Ratios of efficiencies between the search and normalization channels are calculated in MC and data and the difference between these ratios,
2.7\%
for
\chi_{c1}
and
2.4\%
for
\chi_{c2}
, is taken as the systematic uncertainty in the
E/p
selection.

V.5
Decay model

Generating signal MC events containing an
X(3872)
requires an assumption about the decay model used to describe the production mechanism
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma X(3872)
. This results in a systematic uncertainty that we estimate by generating
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma X(3872)
signal MC with four alternative decay models. The nominal model is based on the model for the decay
\psi(3686)\to\gamma\chi_{c1}
; the process is assumed to be
E1
dominant where the photon has the angular distribution
1-\frac{1}{3}\cos^{2}\theta

[
46
]
. The four variations are: (1) phase space; (2)
L=0
,
S=1
; (3)
L=2
,
S=1
; and (4)
L=2
,
S=2
. Here,
S
is the combined spin of the
\gamma
and the
X(3872)
and
L
is the orbital angular momentum between them.

We further introduce two systematic variations on the decay model for
X(3872)\to\rho^{0}J/\psi
used for the normalization channel. These are (1)
L=2
,
S=1
and (2)
L=2
,
S=2
, where
S
is the combined spin of the
\rho^{0}
and
J/\psi
and
L
is the orbital angular momentum between them. These two configurations, alongside the nominal
S
-wave, are the only partial waves allowed for
X(3872)\to\rho^{0}J/\psi
by angular momentum conservation.

The greatest difference between efficiency ratios computed with the nominal model and one of the model variations,
11.0\%
for
\chi_{c1}
and
9.2\%
for
\chi_{c2}
, is taken to be the systematic uncertainty.

V.6
Efficiency ratio energy dependence

In order for the cross section and luminosity to cancel in the ratio given in Eq.
2
, we assume the reconstruction efficiency at each CM energy used in the calculation is identical. In practice, this assumption is not exactly true, resulting in an additional systematic uncertainty caused by some energy dependence on the efficiency ratio.
To assess this uncertainty, the weighted average of the efficiency ratio over the center-of-mass-energy range is calculated, with the weight of each energy point given by the value of the nominal
\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma X(3872))
lineshape. The resulting average is taken as the nominal value. The mass and width parameters of this lineshape are then varied by one sigma in either direction in every possible combination and an average efficiency ratio is calculated for each. The greatest deviation from the nominal value,
0.4\%
for
\chi_{c1}
and
0.5\%
for
\chi_{c2}
, is taken to be the systematic uncertainty.

VI
Upper Limit Calculations

Due to the low significance of the signal yields for both the
\chi_{c1}
and
\chi_{c2}
modes, we calculate upper limits on the number of signal events in the search channel using the Rolke method
[
43
]
, at the
90\%
confidence level. In addition, the total systematic uncertainties given in Table
6
, added in quadrature with the maximum uncertainty on the normalization channel yield, are treated as an uncertainty on the efficiency. These upper limits are then divided by the normalization channel measurement to obtain the upper limit on the ratio. The resulting upper limits on the branching fraction ratios are

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}<1.1

(3)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c2}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}<0.5.

(4)

These upper limits on the branching fraction ratios serve to further disfavor the conventional charmonium hypothesis for the
X(3872)
and are consistent with compact tetraquark and hadronic molecule predictions.

VII
Summary

Our search for the decays
X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1,2}
in BESIII data between
4.15\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
and
4.30\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
did not yield any evidence for these processes. The upper limits of
R_{\chi_{cJ}}
at the 90% confidence level were determined for both channels, with
\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}<1.1
and
\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c2}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}J/\psi\right)}<0.5
. The
\chi_{c1}
result clearly disfavors a conventional charmonium interpretation of the
X(3872)
, which is expected to exhibit a branching fraction ratio of
\frac{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)}{\mathcal{B}\left(X(3872)\to\pi^{0}\chi_{c1}\right)}\approx 12.5

[
9
]
. In addition, both results are consistent with models describing
X(3872)
as a four-quark state
[
10
]
, although the statistics at the moment is insufficient to validate these predictions.

Enrico Le Donne, Luca Nalon

Abstract
. We consider 2 2 -step free-Carnot groups equipped with sub-Finsler distances. We prove that the metric spheres are codimension-one rectifiable from the Euclidean viewpoint. The result is obtained by studying how the Lipschitz constant for the distance function behaves near abnormal geodesics.



1.
Introduction

The regularity of spheres in sub-Riemannian and sub-Finsler structures is related to a peculiar phenomenon of these geometries: the existence of abnormal geodesics, for an introduction to sub-Riemannian geometry and its abnormal curves see
[
10
]
. Indeed, in
[
9
]
, the authors proved that, in the case of sub-Finsler Carnot groups, the absence of non-constant abnormal geodesic implies that spheres are boundaries of Euclidean Lipschitz domains. Conversely, sub-Finsler spheres with cusps do appear in Carnot groups with non-trivial abnormal geodesic. The presence of such cusps is related to the rate of convergence to the asymptotic cones of finitely generated nilpotent groups, see
[
5
]
for details. In this paper we address the regularity of spheres for the class of free-Carnot groups of step 2, in terms of Euclidean rectifiability.

Definition 1.1

(Euclidean rectifiable subset)
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be a smooth manifold of dimension
d
and let
k
be a natural number. A subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{M}
is
Euclidean
k
-rectifiable
, if

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Riem}}^{k}(S)<\infty
and there are countably many measurable subsets
E_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and Lipschitz functions
f_{n}\colon E_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{M}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Riem}}^{k}\left(S\,\,\setminus\,\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}f_{n}(E_{n})\right)=0\,,

where
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is endowed with the Euclidean metric, the space

\mathbb{M}
is endowed with some Riemannian metric,
and the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\mathrm{Riem}}
is taken with respect this Riemannian metric.

We remark that the notion of Euclidean rectifiability for bounded subsets of
\mathbb{M}
is independent of the choice of the Riemannian metric. The main result of this article is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a sub-Finsler free-Carnot group of step
2
. Denote by
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
the corresponding Carnot-Carathéodory distance. Then the unit sphere, defined as

\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}\coloneq\set{g\in\mathbb{G}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g,0_{\mathbb{G}})=1}\,,

is Euclidean
(d-1)
-rectifiable, where
d
is the topological dimension of
\mathbb{G}
and
0_{\mathbb{G}}
is its identity element.

We refer to
Section

2
for the notion of sub-Finsler free-Carnot group of step
2
and Carnot-Carathéodory distance. We refer to
Section

5.2
for the proof of
Theorem

1.2
. In this context, abnormal geodesic are length-minimizing curves with a control that is singular with respect to the end-point map (
16
), see
Section

4
. In Carnot groups of step
2
it is known that the
abnormal set

\mathrm{Abn}
, i.e., the set of points reached by abnormal curves passing through the identity element of the group, is contained in an algebraic variety of co-dimension
3
, see
[
4
]
. Free-Carnot groups of step
2
are a class for which this bound cannot be improved, in this sense they have the largest possible amount of abnormal curves within Carnot groups of step
2
. We stress that very few result concerning Euclidean rectifiability of spheres in sub-Finsler structures with non-trivial abnormal curves are present in the literature, all of which study the problem for a specific sub-Finsler norm, see
[
1
,
2
]
. Nonetheless, the statement of
Theorem

1.2
has been known in the sub-Riemannian case. Indeed, sub-Riemannian structures of step
2
have no Goh extremals, hence, by
[
7
, Proposition 4.2]
, outside of the diagonal the Carnot-Carathéodory distance is Lipschitz with respect to the Riemannian distance. This implies that level sets of the distance from a fixed point, i.e. spheres, are rectifiable sets for almost every value. In Carnot groups, the existence of dilations ensures the result. This strategy, valid for sub-Riemannian
2
-step Carnot groups, does not generalize to sub-Finsler distances, since the distance may not be Euclidean Lipschitz, see
[
5
]
.

The strategy that we follow to prove
Theorem

1.2
starts by considering on every such a
\mathbb{G}
a Riemannian metric
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
coming from specific scalar products (we call them
adequate
, see (
6
)) on
\mathbb{G}
, seen in exponential coordinates as (
2
). Following
[
9
, Section 4.1]
, we interpret the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}
as the graph of the function
d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
, restricted to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
of the distance
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
. We continue by building upon the fact that, in this setting, the abnormal set is an algebraic variety of codimension
3
to get information on its Minkowski content. We use this information to study the asymptotic behaviour of the Hausdorff measure for tubular neighborhoods of
\mathrm{Abn}\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
. The critical part of the argument is to find a local bound for the Lipschitz constant of
d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
at a given point on
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
, in terms of its distance from the abnormal set. In this regard,
Proposition

5.4
states that this Lipschitz constant is bounded (up to a uniform constant) by
\delta^{-1}
, where
\delta
is the distance of that point from the abnormal set. Finally, we combine all the previous results and, by applying a suited version of the area formula for graph functions, we prove
Theorem

1.2
.

The scheme of reasoning that we summarized is rather general as all the results in
Section

3
arbitrarily hold for Carnot groups. Nonetheless, a strong version of the Sard property (as in
[
11
]
) is needed in other to get a result analog to
Proposition

4.3
. However, this kind of results are known only for Carnot groups of small rank and/or step, see
[
4
,
11
,
12
]
, even though no example of Carnot groups where the abnormal set has co-dimension smaller than
3
is known. A possibly harder phenomenon to study in wider classes of Carnot groups is the asymptotic local behaviour of the Lipschitz constant of
d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
, with respect to the distance from the abnormal set. In the latter part of
Section

5
we exhibit an example of a sub-Finsler free-Carnot group of step
2
for which the bound of
Proposition

5.4
is optimal. However, we are currently not able to provide examples of sub-Finsler Carnot groups of step
2
where the local Lipschitz constant of
d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
diverge to infinity faster than the reciprocal of the distance from the abnormal set. We are then lead to state the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a sub-Finsler Carnot group of step
2
, denote by
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
its Carnot-Carathéodory distance and by
\mathrm{Abn}
the abnormal set of
\mathbb{G}
. Fix on
\mathbb{G}
a Riemannian metric
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
. Let
K
be a compact subset that does not contain
0_{\mathbb{G}}
, e.g.,
K=\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
.
Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for every
\delta>0
, the function
d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
is
\left(C\delta^{-1}\right)
-Lipschitz (with respect to the metric
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
) when restricted to the set
\Set{g\in K\colon d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,\mathrm{Abn})\geq\delta}
.

If the latter statement were proved, by combining it with
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
and the arguments of this article, the Euclidean rectifiability of sub-Finsler spheres will be addressed for all sub-Finsler Carnot groups of step
2
. In complete generality, we do not know if one should expect the following:

Conjecture 1.4
.

Metric spheres in sub-Finsler Carnot groups are Euclidean codimension-one rectifiable.

2.
Sub-Finsler free-Carnot groups of step 2

A
Carnot group
is a simply connected Lie group with stratifiable Lie algebra. We recall that a real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
stratifiable of step
s
if it admits a direct sum decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s}\,,

into linear spaces such that
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{i}]=\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}
for every
i=1,\dots,s-1
,
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{s}]=\set{0}
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{s}\neq 0
. Such a decomposition is called
stratification
. Free-nilpotent Lie algebras of step
s
on finite sets are examples of stratifiable Lie algebras. We call
free-Carnot group of step
s
and rank
n
a Carnot group with free-nilpotent Lie algebra of step
s
on a set with
n
elements, they are unique up to isomorphism of Lie groups.

We consider the following model for a free-Carnot group of step
2
and rank
n
:

(2)

\mathbb{G}\coloneq\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),*\right),

where
\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of skew-symmetric contravariant tensors on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of rank
2
, i.e., the vector space generated by the elements of the form
v\wedge w\coloneq v\otimes w-w\otimes v
, with
v
and
w
ranging in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hereafter we will refer to the elements of
\mathbb{G}
both as sums
x+Y
and pairs
(x,Y)
where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Y\in\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The identification between
\mathbb{R}^{n}\oplus\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
remains understood. The product law
*
that we consider in (
2
) is

(3)

(x_{1},Y_{1})*(x_{2},Y_{2})\coloneq\left(x_{1}+x_{2},Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{1}\wedge x_{2})\right).

Such a nilpotent Lie group has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\coloneq(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),[\cdot,\cdot])
given by Lie bracket
[(v_{1},\omega_{1}),(v_{1},\omega_{2})]=(0,v_{1}\wedge v_{2})
for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This Lie algebra is free-nilpotent of step
2
and generated by the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A stratification
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is given by

\mathfrak{g}_{1}\coloneq\mathbb{R}^{n}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{2}\coloneq\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We denote the identity element of the group
\mathbb{G}
by
0_{\mathbb{G}}\coloneq(0,0)
. We stress that is this framework, the exponential map
\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{G}
is the identity map on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We introduce the projection map

\pi\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\pi(x,Y)\coloneq x,

which is a Lie group homomorphism
\pi\colon(\mathbb{G},*)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
.

For every
g=(x,Y)\in\mathbb{G}
, the left-translation map
L_{g}\colon\mathbb{G}_{n}\to\mathbb{G}_{n}
is defined by
L_{g}(h)\coloneq g*h
. Its differential at the identity is the map
\mathrm{d}L_{g}\colon T_{0_{\mathbb{G}}}\mathbb{G}=\mathfrak{g}\to T_{g}\mathbb{G}
such that, for every
(v,\omega)\in\mathfrak{g}
, with
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\omega\in\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it holds

\mathrm{d}L_{g}(v,\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{prod}}}{{=}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(x+tv,Y+t\omega+\tfrac{1}{2}(x\wedge tv)\right)\bigg|_{t=0}=\left(v,\omega+\tfrac{1}{2}(x\wedge v)\right).

We consider
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
as a subspace of the tangent space
T_{0_{\mathbb{G}}}\mathbb{G}
of
\mathbb{G}
at
0_{\mathbb{G}}
and on the distribution

\Delta\coloneq\bigcup_{g\in\mathbb{G}}\mathrm{d}L_{g}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\subseteq T\mathbb{G},

we fix a left-invariant norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
, i.e.,
\lVert\mathrm{d}L_{g}v\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}=\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
for every
g\in\mathbb{G}
and
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. We refer to
\Delta
as the
horizontal distribution
of
\mathbb{G}
.
We refer to
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
as a
Carnot sub-Finsler structure
on
\mathbb{G}
and to such a couple
(\mathbb{G},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}})
, with
\mathbb{G}
an in (
2
) with product (
3
), as
a
sub-Finsler free-Carnot group of step
2
(and rank
n
)
. An absolutely continuous curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}
is said to be
horizontal
if
\dot{\gamma}(t)\in\Delta
for almost every
t\in[0,1]
.

Remark 2.1
.

In every sub-Finsler free-Carnot group

(\mathbb{G},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}})
of step
2
,
there is a correspondence between horizontal curves from the origin and elements of the space
L^{1}\coloneq L^{1}([0,1],\mathfrak{g}_{1})
of integrable curves into the first stratum of
\mathbb{G}
. Namely, on the one hand, for every
u\in L^{1}
we define the
horizontal curve
\gamma_{u}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}
associated to the control
u
as the unique solution to the differential equation

\begin{cases}\gamma(0)=0_{\mathbb{G}}\,,\\
\dot{\gamma_{u}}(t)=\mathrm{d}L_{\gamma(t)}\,u(t)\,,\quad\text{for a.e.\ $t\in[0,1]$}\,.\end{cases}

On the other hand, to each horizontal curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}
such that
\gamma(0)=0_{\mathbb{G}}
, we define the
control
u\in L^{1}
associated to
\gamma
by

u(t)\coloneq\mathrm{d}L_{\gamma(t)^{-1}}\dot{\gamma}(t)\in T_{0_{\mathbb{G}}}\mathbb{G}\,,\quad\text{for a.e.\ $t\in[0,1]$.}

The product law (
3
) allows us to write explicitly the equation for a curve of given control
u
:

(4)

\gamma_{u}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\int^{s}_{0}u(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\wedge u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

We define the length
\ell(\gamma)
of a horizontal curve
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}
of control
u
as

\ell(\gamma)\coloneq\int^{1}_{0}\lVert\dot{\gamma}(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int^{1}_{0}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

We remark that
\ell(\gamma)
is invariant under reparametrization of the interval
[0,1]
.

We now define the so called
Carnot-Carathéodory distance
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
on
\mathbb{G}
induced by the sub-Finsler structure
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
as

(5)

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g,h)\coloneq\inf\Set{\ell(\gamma)\,:\,\text{$\gamma$ horizontal curve with $g,h\in\gamma([0,1])$}},

which induces the manifold topology thanks to the Chow–Rashevskii theorem,
[
10
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Since
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
is left invariant, the distance
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
is left-invariant as well, i.e.,

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(h*g_{1},h*g_{2})=d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g_{1},g_{2})\,,\quad\text{for every $g_{1},g_{2},h\in\mathbb{G}$}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a sub-Finsler free-Carnot group of step
2
and let
g,h\in G
be two points in
\mathbb{G}
. A
geodesic between
g
and
h
is a horizontal curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}
that is length-minimizing from
g
to
h
, i.e.,
\gamma(0)=g,\gamma(1)=h
and

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g,h)=\ell(\gamma)\,.

We stress that the completeness of
\mathbb{G}
ensure the existence of a geodesic between every two elements of
\mathbb{G}
, see
[
10
]
. This means that the infimum in (
5
) is always attained.

In this paper we investigate the rectifiability of the sub-Finsler unit sphere at the origin:

\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}\coloneq\set{g\in\mathbb{G}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},g)=1}\,

with respect to a Riemannian distance on
\mathbb{G}
. Since any two Riemannian metrics are bi-Lipschitz equivalent on compact sets, the result does not depend on the choice of the Riemannian distance. It turns out that, in our argument, it is convenient to consider on
\mathbb{G}
, seen as the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, Euclidean distances given by specific scalar products, as we next introduce.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a free-Carnot group of step
2
. A scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{G}
is said to be
adequate
whenever
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are orthogonal with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and

(6)

\langle v_{1}\wedge w_{1},v_{2}\wedge w_{2}\rangle=\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle\cdot\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle-\langle v_{1},w_{2}\rangle\cdot\langle w_{1},v_{2}\rangle\,,

for every
v_{1},v_{2},w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We observe that, on the one hand, every adequate scalar product on
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is uniquely determined by its restriction to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. On the other hand, we claim that every scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be extended to an adequate scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Indeed, formula (
6
) defines a bilinear form on
\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is positively defined by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\langle v\wedge w,v\wedge w\rangle=\langle v,v\rangle\cdot\langle w,w\rangle-\langle v,w\rangle^{2}\geq 0,

and the equality holds if and only if
v
is parallel to
w
, that is
v\wedge w=0
.

Hereafter
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}
will denote an Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{G}
induced by an adequate scalar product.
The function
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
represents the distance with respect to this Euclidean norm, and

(7)

\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\coloneq\set{g\in\mathbb{G}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},g)=1}

is the corresponding unit sphere centered at
0_{\mathbb{G}}
.

On
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}

are equivalent, in the sense that there is a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

(8)

C_{1}^{-1}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\leq\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}\leq C_{1}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\,,\qquad\text{for every $v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

Moreover,
since for
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the curve
t\mapsto tv
is horizontal, we obtain

(9)

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},v)\leq C_{1}\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\,,\qquad\text{for every $v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}\\

Finally, since
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
is continuous with respect to the manifold topology, it has a minimum and a maximum on the compact set
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
. Therefore it exists
M>0
such that

(10)

M^{-1}\leq d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},g)\leq M\,,\qquad\text{for every $g\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}$.}

We conclude this section by discussing some properties of adequate scalar products.

Remark 2.5
.

For every adequate scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we claim that

(11)

\lVert v\wedge w\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\leq\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\cdot\lVert w\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}},\quad\text{ for every }v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and the equality holds if and only if
v
and
w
are orthogonal. Indeed, by (
6
), we compute

\lVert v\wedge w\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}^{2}=\langle v\wedge w,v\wedge w\rangle=\langle v,v\rangle\cdot\langle w,w\rangle-\langle v,w\rangle^{2}\leq\lVert v\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}^{2}\cdot\lVert w\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}^{2}\,,

and the latter estimate is an equality if and only if
\langle v,w\rangle=0
.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be an adequate scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, hereafter
\perp
will denote its corresponding orthogonality relation. Moreover, if
\mathcal{P}\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{W}\leq\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are linear subspaces, we use the notation:

\mathcal{P}^{\perp_{1}}\coloneq\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\mathcal{W}^{\perp_{2}}\coloneq\mathcal{W}^{\perp}\cap\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

We claim that if
v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{P}\leq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is such that
at least one between
v
and
w
belongs to
\mathcal{P}^{\perp_{1}}
, then
v\wedge w\in\left(\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathcal{P})\right)^{\perp_{2}}
. Indeed, if
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
and
v\in\mathcal{P}^{\perp_{1}}
, then

\langle v\wedge w,x_{1}\wedge x_{2}\rangle=\langle v,x_{1}\rangle\cdot\langle w,x_{2}\rangle-\langle v,x_{2}\rangle\cdot\langle w,x_{1}\rangle=0\,.

The same argument holds if
w\in\mathcal{P}^{\perp_{1}}
.

3.
The sphere as graph

We consider the group
\mathbb{G}
as in (
2
), with product (
3
), and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We define
the dilation of factor
t
as the map

\delta_{t}\colon\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{G}\,,\qquad(x,Y)\mapsto(tx,t^{2}Y)\,.

One can immediately check that
\delta_{t}
is a Lie group isomorphism for every
t\neq 0
. Moreover, once a Carnot sub-Finsler structure
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
is fixed on
\mathbb{G}
, the map
\delta_{t}
is a metric dilation of factor
t
with respect to the Carnot-Carathéodory distance (
5
), i.e.,

(12)

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(\delta_{t}(g),\delta_{t}(h))=td_{\mathrm{cc}}(g,h)\,,\qquad\text{for every $g,h\in\mathbb{G}$ and $t\in\mathbb{R}$.}

For every
g\in\mathbb{G}
, we define
the dilation vector field
as

(13)

\widehat{\delta}(g)\coloneq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\delta_{t}(g)\bigg|_{t=1}\in T_{g}\mathbb{G}\,,\quad\text{for every $g\in\mathbb{G}$.}

Remark 3.1
.

Fix on
\mathbb{G}
an adequate scalar product. We claim that the vector field
g\mapsto\widehat{\delta}(g)
is
transverse to the foliation of
\mathbb{G}\setminus\set{0}
given by:

\Set{\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}(r)}_{r\in(0,+\infty)}\,,

where
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}(r)
is the sphere centered at
0_{\mathbb{G}}
of radius
r
with respect to the distance
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
induced by the scalar product. Indeed, by computing the dilation vector field at
g=(x,Y)
in coordinates, we get

\widehat{\delta}(x,Y)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\delta_{t}(x,Y)\bigg|_{t=1}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(tx,t^{2}Y)\bigg|_{t=1}=(x,2Y)\,.

From
Definition

2.4
, we recall that
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are orthogonal. We then compute the scalar product between
\widehat{\delta}(g)
and the vector orthogonal to
T_{g}\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
, namely

\braket{(x,Y),(x,2Y)}_{\mathrm{eu}}=\lVert x\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{eu}}+2\lVert Y\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{eu}},

which is not null as long as
(x,Y)\neq 0_{\mathbb{G}}
. We conclude that
\widehat{\delta}(g)
is transverse to
T_{g}\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
for every
g\in\mathbb{G}\setminus\set{0}
.

Remark 3.2
.

We claim that the map defined by

(14)

\phi\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{G}\setminus\set{0}\,,\qquad(p,t)\mapsto\delta_{t}^{-1}(p)\,.

is a global diffeomorphism. Indeed, for every
g\in\mathbb{G}\setminus\set{0}
, the set

(15)

r_{g}\coloneq\set{\delta_{t}(g)\,\colon\,t\in(0,+\infty)}

is connected, it has
0_{\mathbb{G}}
as an accumulation point, and it escapes every compact set of
\mathbb{G}
. This implies that
r_{g}\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
is non-empty and therefore
g
is in the image of
\phi
, this proves
\phi
is surjective. Moreover,
the map
t\mapsto\lVert\delta_{t}(g)\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}
is smooth from
(0,+\infty)
to
\mathbb{R}
and by
Remark

3.1
its derivative is never zero, therefore it is injective. This implies that
r_{g}\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
has at most one element, hence
\phi
is injective. Finally,
\phi
is a local diffeomorphism by
Remark

3.1
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
S\coloneq\set{(p,d_{0}(p))\,}{\,p\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}}
be the graph of the function
d_{0}(\cdot)\coloneq d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
restricted to
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
. We claim that, for
\phi
as in (
3.2
), one has

\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}=\phi(S)\,.

Indeed, by
Remark

3.2
, each set
r_{g}
, defined in (
15
), has precisely one point in
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
and, since
\delta_{t}
is a metric dilation of factor
t
that fixes
0_{\mathbb{G}}
, also one point in
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}
. Moreover, for every
p\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
, we have

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\phi(p,d_{0}(p))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{metric_dilation}}}{{=}}d_{0}(p)^{-1}\cdot d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},p)=1\,.

This implies that the map
p\mapsto\phi(p,d_{\mathrm{cc}}(p))
is a bijection from
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
to
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}
.

Hereafter, if
f
is a Lipschitz function between metric spaces, the number
\mathrm{Lip}(f)
will denote the Lipschitz constant of
f
. As already mentioned, the goal of this paper is to study the rectifiability of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}
. Since we have expressed it as the graph of a function, this motivates the need for the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let the group
\mathbb{G}
be as in (
2
), the set
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
as in (
7
) and the map
\phi
as in (
14
). Denote by
d
the topological dimension of
\mathbb{G}
and by
d_{0}(\cdot)
the function
d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
. There exists a constant
C_{2}
such that for every Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
, it holds

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}_{\mathrm{eu}}(\phi(\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{E}))\leq C_{2}\left(\mathrm{Lip}(d_{0}|_{E})+1\right)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}_{\mathrm{eu}}(E)\,,

where
\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{E}\coloneq\set{(x,d_{0}(x))\,}{\,x\in E}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times(0,+\infty)
, the measure
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eu}}
is the Hausdorff measure with respect to
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
, and on
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
we consider the distance function given by the restriction of
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
.

To prove
Proposition

3.4
, we recall the following corollary of the area formula
[
6
, Theorem 3.2.3]
applied to graph functions.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Borel set and
\psi\colon A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Lipschitz function. Then

\mathcal{H}^{d}(\mathrm{gr}\,\psi)\leq\left(\mathrm{Lip}(\psi)+1\right)\mathcal{H}^{d}(A)\,,

where
\mathrm{gr}\,\psi\coloneq\set{(x,\psi(x))\,}{\,x\in A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{d}
is the Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

Proof of
Proposition

3.4
.

This is a simple exercise, which we spell out for completeness. Fix a Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
. We recall from (
10
) that, for some
M>0
, we have that
M^{-1}\leq d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},g)\leq M
for every
g\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
. In particular
\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{E}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times[M^{-1},M]
. By
Remark

3.2
, the map
\phi\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{G}\setminus\set{0}
is a diffeomorphism, therefore
\phi
is Lipschitz on compact sets. We define
L_{1}
to be the Lipschitz constant of
\phi
when restricted to
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times[M^{-1},M]
. We recall that
(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}},d_{\mathrm{eu}})
is locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. By the compactness of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
, we can then consider a finite collection of closed subsets
B_{1},\dots,B_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and bi-Lipschitz maps
h_{i}\colon B_{i}\to h_{i}(B_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
such that

\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\,h_{i}(B_{i})=\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,.

Let
L_{2}\geq 1
be a number greater than
\mathrm{Lip}(h_{i})
and
\mathrm{Lip}(h_{i}^{-1})
for every
1\leq i\leq N
.
For every
1\leq i\leq N
, we consider the functions defined as
\psi_{i}\coloneq d_{0}\circ h_{i}
from
B_{i}
to
[M^{-1},M]
. We consider on
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times(0,+\infty)
the product metric
\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{eu}}^{2}+\lvert\cdot\rvert^{2}}
and the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{d}
with respect to that metric. We observe that the maps defined as

\Psi_{i}\colon\mathrm{gr}\,\psi_{i}\to\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{h_{i}(B_{i})}\,,\qquad(x,\psi_{i}(x))\mapsto(h_{i}(x),\psi_{i}(x))\,,

are
L_{2}
-Lipschitz.

Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
be Borel sets such that
A_{i}\subseteq B_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq N
and

E=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(A_{i})\,.

Finally, we can combine the previous discussion with
Corollary

3.5
to compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}_{\mathrm{eu}}(\phi

\displaystyle(\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{E}))\leq L_{1}^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{E})=L_{1}^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\,\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{h_{i}(A_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle=L_{1}^{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\mathrm{gr}\,d_{0}|_{h_{i}(A_{i})}\right)\leq L_{1}^{d-1}L_{2}^{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\mathrm{gr}\,\psi_{i}|_{A_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}^{d-1}L_{2}^{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\mathrm{Lip}(\psi_{i})+1\right)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(A_{i}\right)\leq L_{1}^{d-1}L_{2}^{d}\left(\mathrm{Lip}(d_{0}|E)+1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(A_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}^{d-1}L_{2}^{2d-2}\left(\mathrm{Lip}(d_{0}|_{E})+1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(h_{i}(A_{i})\right)\leq L_{1}^{d-1}L_{2}^{2d-2}\left(\mathrm{Lip}(\psi)+1\right)\mathcal{H}^{d-1}_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(E\right)\,.

The statement is then proved by setting
C_{2}\coloneq L_{1}^{d-1}L_{2}^{2d-1}
.
∎

4.
Abnormal set and Minkowski content

The
abnormal set

\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})
of the Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
as in (
2
) is, by definition, the set of critical values of the end-point map
\mathrm{End}
defined as

(16)

\mathrm{End}\colon L^{1}([0,1],\mathfrak{g}_{1})\to\mathbb{G},\qquad u\mapsto\gamma_{u}(1),

where
\gamma_{u}
is defined as in (
4
). In this case, the abnormal set can be described using Grassmannians. We recall that the
Grassmannian

\mathrm{Gr}(n,k)
, with

, is the family of all
k
-dimensional vector subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. From
[
11
, Equation 3.9]
, the abnormal set
\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})
can be written as

(17)

\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})=\bigcup\Set{\mathcal{P}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathcal{P})\,\colon\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Gr}(n,n-2)}.

In the following result we summarize some properties of the abnormal set (
17
), referring to
[
11
]
for the details.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
11
, Theorem 3.14, Theorem 3.15]

Let
\mathbb{G}
be as in (
2
), then the abnormal set (
17
) is an algebraic variety of co-dimension
3
within
\mathbb{G}
and it is invariant by dilations, i.e.,

\delta_{t}(\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G}))=\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\,,\qquad\text{for every $t\in(0,+\infty)$.}

We recall from
[
3
, Section 3.4]
that a bounded algebraic variety
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension
m
is the finite union of smooth compact sub-varieties of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension not greater than
m
. In particular
\mathcal{A}
is closed and
m
-rectifiable in the stronger sense of
[
6
, Definition 3.2.14]
. Therefore, by
[
6
, Theorem 3.2.39]
, its
m
-dimensional Minkowski content equals its
m
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, which is finite. The following proposition states that an
m
-rectifiable set has finite
m
-dimensional upper Minkowski content and it is a simple consequence of
[
6
, Theorem 3.2.39]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
0\leq k\leq d
be integer numbers and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
(d-k)
-rectifiable set in the stronger sense of
[
6
, Definition 3.2.14]
, then,
for every
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, there exists

such that

\mathcal{H}^{d}\left(\set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(x,\mathcal{S})\leq\varepsilon}\right)\leq c\cdot\varepsilon^{k}\,,\qquad\text{for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})$.}

A similar result allows us to estimate the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the following set

(18)

A_{\delta}\coloneq\set{p\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G}))\leq\delta}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be as in (
2
) and
d
be its topological dimension. Fix on
\mathbb{G}
an adequate scalar product and denote by
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
the induced distance. With respect to
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
, consider the sphere (
7
) and its subsets (
18
).
Then there exists a constant
C_{3}
such that

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(A_{\delta})\leq C_{3}\delta^{3}\,,\qquad\text{for every $\delta\in(0,1)$.}

Proof.

We firstly remark that is not restrictive to prove the statement for
\delta

smaller than a given
\delta_{0}>0
. We recall from
Remark

3.2
that for every
g\in\mathbb{G}\setminus\set{0}
the set
r_{g}\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
, where
r_{g}
is defined as in (
15
), is a singleton. Denote by
\hat{g}
the element of that singleton. We then define the map

(19)

\sigma\colon\Set{g\in\mathbb{G}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}})\leq 1/2}\to\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,,\qquad g\mapsto\hat{g}\,.

Consider the diffeomorphism
\phi
as in (
14
). We observe that the map
\sigma
is conjugated, thought
\phi
, to the map

f\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times(0,+\infty)\,,\qquad(x,t)\mapsto(x,1)\,,

which is
1
-Lipschitz if we consider on
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\times(0,+\infty)
the product metric
\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{eu}}^{2}+\lvert\cdot\rvert^{2}}
. Since
\phi
is bi-Lipschitz when restricted to compact sets, we conclude that
\sigma
is Lipschitz as well. We denote the Lipschitz constant of
\sigma
by
L_{1}
.

From
Proposition

4.1
, we get that
\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})
is closed and invariant by dilation. Then, for every
\delta\leq 1/2
and
p\in A_{\delta}
, we have
\delta\geq d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G}))=d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,q)
for some
q\in\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})
. The estimate
d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,\sigma(q))=d_{\mathrm{eu}}(\sigma(p),\sigma(q))\leq L_{1}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,q)\leq L_{1}\delta
implies the following inclusion:

(20)

A_{\delta}\subseteq\set{p\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}})\leq L_{1}\delta},\qquad\text{for every $\delta\leq 1/2$.}

Since
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
is compact and locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, we can consider a finite collection of open subsets
B_{1},\dots,B_{N}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and bi-Lipschitz maps
h_{i}\colon B_{i}\to h_{i}(B_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
such that
\Set{h_{i}(B_{i})\,}{\,1\leq i\leq N}
is a covering of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
. Let
\delta_{0}>0
be such that, for every

,

(21)

d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,q)\leq L_{1}\delta_{0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{$p,q\in h_{i}(B_{i})$ for some $1\leq i\leq N$.}

Let
L_{2}\geq 1
be a constant greater than
\mathrm{Lip}(h_{i})
and
\mathrm{Lip}(h_{i}^{-1})
for every
1\leq i\leq N
. We define the set

E_{i,\delta}\coloneq\Set{\bar{p}\in B_{i}\,}{\,d(\bar{p},h_{i}^{-1}(\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap h_{i}(B_{i}))\leq L_{1}L_{2}\delta},

and we claim that, for every
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,

(22)

\set{p\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}})\leq L_{1}\delta}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}\left(E_{i,\delta}\right)\,.

Indeed, fix
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
and
p\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
such that
d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}})\leq L_{1}\delta
. Then
d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,q)\leq L_{1}\delta_{0}
for some

. By (

), there exists

such that
p,q\in h_{i}(B_{i})
. Define
\bar{p}\coloneq h_{i}^{-1}(p)
and
\bar{q}\coloneq h_{i}^{-1}(q)
. Then
\bar{q}\in h_{i}^{-1}(\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}})
and

d(\bar{p},\bar{q})\leq L_{2}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(h_{i}(\bar{p}),h_{i}(\bar{q}))\leq L_{2}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(p,q)\leq L_{1}L_{2}\delta\,.

Therefore
p\in h_{i}\left(E_{i,\delta}\right)
and the inclusion (
22
) is proved.

From
Proposition

4.1
, the set
\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})
is an algebraic variety of dimension
d-3
and invariant by dilations. Moreover, from
Remark

3.1
, the manifold
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
is transverse to the dilation flow (
13

\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
is a bounded algebraic variety of dimension

, hence it is
(d-4)
-rectifiable. We observe that each
h_{i}^{-1}(\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap h_{i}(B_{i}))
is the image by a Lipschitz map of the

-rectifiable set
\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap h_{i}(B_{i})
. By
Proposition

4.2
with
\varepsilon_{0}\coloneq L_{1}L_{2}\delta_{0}
, there exists

such that, for every

,

(23)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(E_{i,\delta})\leq cL_{1}^{3}L_{2}^{3}\delta^{3}\,,\qquad\text{for every $\delta\leq\delta_{0}$.}

Let
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, we can then compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(A_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{a_delta_inclusion}}}{{\leq}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\set{x\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(x,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\cap\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}})\leq L_{1}\delta}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{hi_inclusion}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(h_{i}(E_{i,\delta})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{2}^{d-1}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(E_{i,\delta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{estimate_eidelta}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}cL_{2}^{d-1}L_{1}^{3}L_{2}^{3}\delta^{3}\leq cNL_{1}^{3}L_{2}^{d+2}\delta^{3}\,.

The statement is proved by setting

.
∎

As another consequence of (

d_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(g,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\right)=\inf\Set{d_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(g,\mathcal{P}\times\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathcal{P})\right)\,\colon\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Gr}(n,n-2)}.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{G}
be a horizontal curve with control

. Given

and

Indeed,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(\pi(\gamma(t)),\mathcal{P}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{curve}}}{{=}}d_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\mathcal{P}\right)=d_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}u_{1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}u_{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\mathcal{P}\right)

For the rest of this section, we fix a Carnot sub-Finsler structure
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}
on the group (

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a constant

such that for every

The latter implies that

Let us now write

We use the decomposition

to write

\displaystyle\gamma_{u}(1)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{curve}}}{{=}}\int^{1}_{0}u_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+

\displaystyle+\int^{1}_{0}u_{2}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u_{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

We can then write

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{eu}}

\displaystyle\left(\gamma(1),\mathcal{P}\times\textstyle{\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}}(\mathcal{P})\right)=

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int^{1}_{0}u_{2}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u_{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\int^{1}_{0}u_{2}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u_{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}.

The first term is strictly less than
\varepsilon
thanks to (
27
). We can apply Fubini to the second term and get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u_{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\int^{1}_{0}u_{1}(s)\wedge\int^{1}_{s}u_{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{wedge_norm_ineq}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\left\lVert u_{1}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\cdot\left\lVert\int^{1}_{s}u_{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{integral_norm_estimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\left\lVert u_{1}(s)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\cdot 2\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}s\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{c1M}}}{{\leq}}C_{1}M\varepsilon\,.

A similar estimates allows us to bound the third term, namely,

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\int^{1}_{0}\int^{t}_{0}u_{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\wedge u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{wedge_norm_ineq}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{2}\int^{1}_{0}\left\lVert\int^{t}_{0}u_{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\cdot\left\lVert u_{1}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\mathrm{d}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{c1M},\eqref{integral_norm_estimate}}}{{\leq}}C_{1}M\varepsilon\,.

By combining everything together, we finally get

d_{\mathrm{eu}}\left(\gamma(1),\mathcal{P}\times\textstyle{\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}}(\mathcal{P})\right)<\varepsilon+C_{1}M\varepsilon+C_{1}M\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon\left(1+2C_{1}M\right)\,.

The proposition is then proved by setting
K\coloneq 1+2C_{1}M
.
∎

5.
Minimal height and rectifiability of spheres

The consequence of
Corollary

4.6
can be informally rephrased by saying that the projection of each geodesic is not close to any vector subspace of co-dimension
2
. This property is closely related to the notion of minimal height of a configuration of points, which we next recall from
[
8
]
.

5.1.
Some remarks on minimal height

Hereafter, if
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
is a
m
-tuple of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will denote by
\mathrm{span}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
the subspace generated by those vectors. We will denote by
(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,a_{m})
the
(m-1)
-tuple obtained from
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
by removing the
j
-th element.

Definition 5.1
.

The
m
-dimensional volume
of an
m
-tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
of vectors in the Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d)
is the
m
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{m}
of the parallelotope generated by those vectors, i.e.,

\mathrm{vol}_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\coloneq\mathcal{L}^{m}\left(\set{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}a_{m}:\lambda_{i}\in[0,1]}\right)

The
minimal height
of
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
is the smallest height of the parallelotope generated by those vectors, i.e.,

\mathrm{MinHeight}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\coloneq\min_{j\in\set{1,\dots,m}}d\left(a_{j},\mathrm{span}(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,a_{m})\right)\,.

Equivalently

\displaystyle\mathrm{MinHeight}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})

\displaystyle=\min_{j\in\set{1,\dots,m}}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})}{\mathrm{vol}_{m-1}(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,a_{m})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})}{\max_{j\in\set{1,\dots,m}}\mathrm{vol}_{m-1}(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,a_{m})}\,.

For the next result, one should think that
\Gamma
is the projection, via the map
\pi
, of a geodesic.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a compact subset of the Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d)
,
\delta>0
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Gamma
is not contained in the
\delta
-neighbourhood of any
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Gr}(n,m)
, i.e., for every
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{Gr}(n,m)
there exists
x\in\Gamma
such that
d(x,P)\geq\delta
.
Then there are
x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1}\in\Gamma
such that
\mathrm{MinHeight}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})\geq\delta
.

Proof.

We firstly claim that there exists an
m
-tuple
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
of points in
\Gamma
such that
\mathrm{vol}_{m}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})>0
. Indeed, by assumption
\mathrm{span}(\Gamma)
must have dimension grater than
m
. Hence, any linearly independent points
y_{1},\dots,y_{m}
in
\Gamma
span positive
m
-volume.

Consider
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Gamma^{m}
that maximizes
\mathrm{vol}_{m}
among all
m
-tuples in
\Gamma^{m}
, which exists by compactness and in particular
\mathrm{vol}_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})>0
. By hypothesis there exists
x_{m+1}\in\Gamma
such that
d\left(x_{m+1},\mathrm{span}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\right)\geq\delta
, i.e.,

\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{m+1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})}{\mathrm{vol}_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})}\geq\delta\,.

For every
j\in\set{1,\dots,m+1}
, since
\mathrm{vol}_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
is maximal, we have

\displaystyle d(x_{j},\mathrm{span}(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x}_{j},\dots,x_{m+1})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{m+1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})}{\mathrm{vol}_{m}(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x}_{j},\dots,x_{m+1})}\geq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{m+1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})}{\mathrm{vol}_{m}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})}\geq\delta\,.

This implies that
\mathrm{MinHeight}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})\geq\delta
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
be vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equipped with the Euclidean norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert
. Suppose that
\mathrm{MinHeight}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})>0
, then every
w\in\mathrm{span}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
can be written as
w=\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}a_{m}
with

\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert\leq\frac{\lVert w\rVert}{\mathrm{MinHeight}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})}\,,\qquad\text{for every $j\in\set{1,\dots,m}$}\,.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{MinHeight}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})>0
, then
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
are linearly independent and therefore every
w\in\mathrm{span}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
can be uniquely written as
w=\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}a_{m}
. Let us now fix
j\in\set{1,\dots,m}
, we then have

\displaystyle\lVert w\rVert

\displaystyle\geq d(w,\mathrm{span}(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,a_{m})=d(\lambda_{j}a_{j},\mathrm{span}(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,a_{m})

\displaystyle=\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert\cdot d(a_{j},\mathrm{span}(a_{1},\dots,\widehat{a}_{j},\dots,a_{m})\geq\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert\cdot\mathrm{MinHeight}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\,.

It follows that
\lvert\lambda_{j}\rvert\leq\lVert w\rVert\cdot\mathrm{MinHeight}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})^{-1}
.
∎

The following is the key result:

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\left(\mathbb{G},\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{sf}}\right)
be a sub-Finsler free-Carnot group of step
2
and rank
n

an adequate scalar product and let

denote the induced distance. Consider the sphere (

and a number

such that, for every
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})

In particular, the function

\left(C_{5}\delta^{-1}\right)

Before the proof of

5.4

Lemma 5.5
.

Consider on
\mathbb{G}
, defined as in (
2

be the induced norm. Then there exists a constant
K_{1}
such that, for every
(n-1)

Y=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}\wedge v_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rVert v_{j}\lVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\leq K_{1}\cdot\frac{\rVert Y\lVert_{\mathrm{eu}}}{\varepsilon}\,,\quad\text{for every $j\in\set{1,\dots,n-1}$.}

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and an
(n-1)
-tuple
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
as in the assumptions and define

We claim that it is sufficient to prove that

contains an Euclidean ball in
\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
centered at zero with radius independent of

and

. Indeed, if

It follows that

of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n-1})
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{span}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
. We claim that
\set{e_{i}\wedge e_{j}\,}{\,1\leq i<j\leq n}
, which is an orthonormal basis of
\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

. Indeed, fix
Y\coloneq e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
with
i<j
, then
i\leq n-1
and therefore

. We recall that
\mathrm{MinHeight}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\geq\varepsilon
, therefore, by

, we have that

\left\lvert a_{i}^{k}\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\left\lVert e_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}=\varepsilon^{-1}\qquad\text{for every $k=1,\dots,n-1$}\,.

We can then write

We stress that

symmetric, i.e.,

, and convex as well. It is a general fact in Euclidean geometry that the convex hull of a symmetrised orthonormal basis contains an Euclidean ball of radius

0\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{n-1}
.

We set
g_{i}\coloneq\gamma(t_{i})
, for
i\in\set{1,\dots,n-1}
, and
g_{0}\coloneq\gamma(0)=0_{\mathbb{G}}
. We also now fix
h\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
. We write
g
and
h
in coordinates:

g=(\xi_{1},W_{1})\,,\quad h=(\xi_{2},W_{2})\,.

We then define

q\coloneq\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,\quad Z\coloneq W_{2}-W_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\xi_{2}\wedge\xi_{1})\in\textstyle{\bigwedge^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,.

We have

(33)

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{c1_estiamate}}}{{\leq}}C_{1}\lVert\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\leq C_{1}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)\,.

Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert W_{2}-W_{1}\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})\wedge\xi_{1}\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}\cdot\lVert\xi_{1}\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)+\frac{\lVert\xi_{1}\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}}{2}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)

(34)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)\,,

where we used that the scalar product is adequate and that
g\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
.

By the product rule (
3
), we observe that
h=Z*g*q
. We recall that
d_{\mathrm{cc}}
is left-invariant, so that we infer

(35)

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},h)\leq d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},Z*g)+d_{\mathrm{cc}}(Z*g,Z*g*q)\leq d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},Z*g_{n-1})+d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g_{n-1},g)+d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},q)\,.

Thanks to (
32
), the
n
-tuple
(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})
satisfies the conditions of
Lemma

5.6
with
\varepsilon
replaced by
C_{4}\delta
, so we estimate the first term of (
35
):

d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},Z*g_{n-1})\leq K_{2}\cdot\frac{\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}}{C_{4}\delta}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g_{j-1},g_{j})\,.

We shall use the previous inequality in (
35
) and use that
g_{0},\dots,g_{r-1},g
are consecutive points along the geodesic
\gamma
. If
\delta\leq\delta_{0}\coloneq 2K_{2}/(C_{1}C_{4})
, we get

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},h)

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\cdot\frac{\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}}{C_{4}\delta}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g_{j-1},g_{j})+d_{\mathrm{cc}}(g_{n-1},g)+d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},q)

\displaystyle=d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},g)+\frac{K_{2}}{C_{4}\delta}\cdot\lVert Z\rVert_{\mathrm{eu}}+d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},q)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{dccq}\eqref{dccz}}}{{\leq}}d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},g)+\frac{2K_{2}}{C_{4}\delta}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)+C_{1}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},g)+\frac{4K_{2}}{C_{4}\delta}d_{\mathrm{eu}}(g,h)\,.

The statement is then proved by setting
C_{5}\coloneq\max\set{1,4K_{2}/C_{4}}
.
∎

Here is the proof of the main result:

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

We see
\mathbb{G}
as in (
2
) with an Euclidean distance as in
Section

2
, denoted by
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
, so to have the sphere
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
as in (
7
).
To prove the Euclidean rectifiability of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}
, since
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
is locally bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
we consider the sets
E_{n}
for (
1
) to be subsets of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}
.
With the notation (
18
), we then define

\displaystyle E_{n}\coloneq A_{2^{-n}}\setminus A_{2^{-n-1}}

\displaystyle=\set{x\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,}{\,2^{-n-1}<d_{\mathrm{eu}}(x,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\leq 2^{-n}}\,,\quad\forall n\geq 1\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle E_{0}\coloneq\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\setminus A_{1/2}

\displaystyle=\set{x\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,}{\,d_{\mathrm{eu}}(x,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})>\tfrac{1}{2}}\,.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{Riem}}^{d-1}
the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure with respect to the distance
d_{\mathrm{eu}}
.
We obviously have that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(E_{0})\leq\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}})
.
By
Proposition

4.3
we also get

(36)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(E_{n})\leq\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(A_{2^{-n}})\leq C_{3}\cdot 2^{-3n}.

Let
d_{0}(\cdot)\coloneq d_{\mathrm{cc}}(0_{\mathbb{G}},\cdot)
and
\phi
be the dilating map from
Remark

3.2
.
We consider a sequence of functions
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
f_{n}
is the image by
\phi
of the graphing map of
d_{0}
restricted to
E_{n}
. Namely,

f_{n}\colon E_{n}\to\mathbb{G}\qquad x\mapsto f_{n}(x)\coloneq\phi(x,d_{0}(x))=\delta_{d_{0}(x)}^{-1}(x).

Our aim is to show that the maps
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
give the Euclidean rectifiability of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}
.

We first explain why the maps
f_{n}
are Lipschitz. Notice that each of these maps is the composition between the graph map

x\mapsto(x,d_{0}(x))
, which has Lipschitz constant
\left(1+\mathrm{Lip}(d_{0}|_{E_{n}})\right)
, and the diffeomorphism
\phi
. Moreover, the graph of
d_{0}
is contained in a compact set, on which therefore
\phi
is Lipschitz with constant
L
. We use
Proposition

5.4
, recalling that
C_{5}\geq 1
, to get

(37)

\mathrm{Lip}(f_{n\,|E_{n}})\leq L\left(1+\mathrm{Lip}(d_{0}|_{E_{n}})\right)\leq L\left(1+C_{5}\cdot 2^{n+1}\right)\leq C_{5}L\cdot 2^{n+2}.

Thus, the maps
f_{n}
are Lipschitz.

We next prove that the sets
f_{n}(E_{n})
cover almost all of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}
. Since
\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}E_{n}=\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{eu}}\,\setminus\,\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})
and each function

(38)

\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}\,\setminus\,\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}f_{n}(E_{n})=\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}\cap\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\subseteq\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\,.

Moreover, from
Proposition

4.1
we have that the Hausdorff dimension of

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G}))=0

(39)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}\,\setminus\,\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}f_{n}(E_{n})\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_sfera_dentro}}}{{\leq}}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\mathrm{Abn}(\mathbb{G})\right)=0\,.

It is left to prove that
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}\right)
is finite.
Because of (
39

(40)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}f_{n}(E_{n})\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(f_{n}(E_{n})\right)<\infty\,.

We then check that we have a summable sequence:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(f_{n}(E_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\left(\phi(\mathrm{gr}(d_{0}|_{E_{n}}))\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{d-1}_{\mathrm{eu}}(E_{n})\cdot\left(\mathrm{Lip}(d_{0|_{E_{n}}})+1\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{stima_C4}}}{{\leq}}C_{2}C_{3}\cdot 2^{-3n}\cdot\left(\mathrm{Lip}(d_{0|_{E_{n}}})+1\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{lipn}}}{{\leq}}C_{2}C_{3}C_{5}L\cdot 2^{-3n}\cdot 2^{n+2}=C_{2}C_{3}C_{5}L\cdot 2^{-2n+2}\,,

where in the first inequality we used
Proposition

3.4
. Hence, (
40
) is obtained and the proof of the rectifiability of
\mathbb{S}_{\mathrm{cc}}

is concluded.

Example 5.9

It is easy to show that, if

\gamma(1)=h_{\varepsilon,\delta}

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{\widetilde{u}})=\int^{1}_{0}\lvert u_{12}(t)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{1}_{0}\lvert u_{34}(t)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t=\int^{1}_{0}\lvert u_{12}(t)+u_{34}(t)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t=\int^{1}_{0}\lvert u(t)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}t=\ell(\gamma_{u})\,,

Abstract
. A degree of a module M M is a numerical measure of information carried by M M . We highlight some of Vasconcelos’ outstanding contributions to the theory of degrees, bridging commutative algebra and computational algebra. We present several degrees he introduced and developed, including arithmetic degree, jdeg, homological degree, cohomological degrees, canonical degree and bi-canonical degree. For the canonical and bi-canonical degrees we discuss recent developments motivated by our joint works [ 25 , 19 , 9 ] .



In loving memory of Wolmer Vasconcelos. It has been an honor and a privilege to be his friends and collaborators, and to witness the joy that mathematics brought to him.

1.
Introduction

We discuss some of Vasconcelos’ outstanding results in the theory of degrees and how they have inspired many researches in commutative algebra and beyond.
We present (in chronological order when appropriate) several degrees Vasconcelos introduced and developed, such as arithmetic degree, jdeg, homological degree, cohomological degrees, canonical degree and bi-canonical degree. For more details and proofs on the statements of various degrees we refer the reader to
[
64
]
and to the referenced literature. For canonical degree and bi-canonical degree we focus on our joint work, informal discussions, open questions that were of particular interest to Vasconcelos, and recent developments. A more comprehensive survey is in
[
8
]
and a full treatment is in our papers
[
25
,
19
,
9
]
.
We refer to
[
11
]
for general background and any unexplained terminology.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring (or a Noetherian graded algebra) and let

\mathcal{M}(R)
be the category of finitely generated
R
-modules (or the appropriate category
of graded modules). Broadly speaking, a degree function is a numerical function

d:\mathcal{M}(R)\mapsto\mathbb{N}
. Extensively studied degree functions include the multiplicity
(classical degree) and the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity. The main point is that the value
d(M)
should capture several important elements of the
structure of the
R
-module
M
. A major theme of Vasconcelos’ prolific research is the connection between commutative algebra and computational algebra (see for instance
[
61
]
). Therefore we highlight some issues of particular interest when we look for new degree functions.

Cohen-Macaulay structures can be considered as fundamental blocks (see
[
11
]
for a beautiful treatment of the subject). Vasconcelos wrote in
[
64
]
: “
We hold the view that Cohen-Macaulay structures are ubiquitous in algebra,
are central to commutative algebra and algebraic geometry and represent unique computational
efficiencies and that without an understanding of the ways it appears in
the structure of non Cohen-Macaulay structures the prospects for successful large scale
symbolic computations are rather dim.” Hence it is desirable to seek functions that coincide with the classical multiplicity when the
R
-module
M
is Cohen-Macaulay and to be able to measure the deviation of
M
from being Cohen-Macaulay. It is also important to relate a new degree function to other classical invariants of
M
and to study is its behavior under generic hyperplane sections.

A main topic of Vasconcelos’ research is the study of the complexity of normalization processes of graded algebras. In this context, let
R
be a normal, unmixed integral domain and let
A
be a semistandard graded
R
-algebra (i.e., a finite extension of a standard graded
R
-algebra). Let
\overline{A}
be the integral closure of
A
. Estimating the number of steps that general algorithms must take to build
\overline{A}
can be viewed as an invariant of A. For several interesting classes of algebras, including affine graded algebras over fields and Rees algebras of ideals and modules, multiplicity dependent bounds were derived for instance in
[
14
,
41
,
47
,
54
,
56
]
. One of Vasconcelos’ broad goals was to define new degree functions that provide extensions of such bounds to general graded algebras and to find new techniques to study the complexity.

Now we briefly describe how this manuscript is organized. The first half of the paper focuses on arithmetic degree, jdeg, homological degree, and cohomological degrees.
The definitions, properties and developments of these degrees are in the respective sections.

The
arithmetic degree
is addressed in Section
2
. It is a refinement of the classical multiplicity, but it is defined in a way that collects information about the associated
primes of
M
in all codimensions. A major contribution of Vasconcelos is a formula for the arithmetic degree that does not use a primary decomposition (Theorem
2.2
). Due to his result, software systems such as Macaulay2
[
34
]
or CoCoA
[
1
]
can be used to compute the arithmetic degree.

Section
3
is dedicated to
jdeg
, introduced by Pham and Vasconcelos as a generalization of
j
-multiplicity. One important point is that the
jdeg
provides estimates for the complexity of normalization (Theorem
3.3
). It also extends classical bounds.

In Section
4
we discuss the
homological degree
, which was introduced by Vasconcelos as an extension of
the usual notion of multiplicity, aimed to provide a numerical signature for
a module
M
when
M
is not necessarily Cohen-Macaulay. The difference between the homological degree of
M
and its multiplicity is often called a Cohen-Macaulay deficiency in the literature.
For example, if
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay, this difference is the Stückrad-Vogel invariant of
M
. The homological degree is defined recursively on the dimension of the module, which allows,
in principle, for its calculation by symbolic computer packages. It can be used to bound the Euler characteristic of parameter ideals (Theorem
4.14
).
The homological degree is used to define the
homological torsion
, which plays an important role in the study of Hilbert coefficients. The homological torsion can be used to bound the first Hilbert coefficient
\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)
of a module
M
relative to an ideal generated by a system of parameters
Q
for
M
(Theorem
4.17
). The coefficient
\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)
is often called the Chern number, and it has been extensively studied by Vasconcelos.

In Section
5
we discuss
cohomological degrees
, introduced by Vasconcelos.
The notion of cohomological degree generalizes the homological degree and it is the classical multiplicity if
M
is Cohen-Macaulay. It provides an estimate for the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of a standard graded algebra (Theorem
5.3
). The cohomological degree can also be used to estimate the minimal number of generators of an ideal, generalizing and improving many previously known bounds (Theorem
5.6
).
We also discuss the
bdeg
, a cohomological degree introduced by Vasconcelos’ former student Gunston.

The second half of the manuscript, Sections
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,

11
, deals with more recently introduced degrees, the canonical degree and the bi-canonical degree
[
25
,
19
,
9
]
.
We include topics and developments motivated by our discussions with Vasconcelos.

The overarching goal here is the stratification of Cohen-Macaulay rings. Several researchers
have been interested in finding new significant classes of rings which naturally include Gorenstein rings. We introduce invariants that characterize some of these classes and measure the deviation from
R
being Gorenstein.
Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring that has a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. A canonical ideal is an ideal isomorphic to a canonical module of
R
. The condition that the canonical module is an ideal is equivalent to
R
being generically Gorenstein. We refer to
[
11
, Section 3]
for the background on canonical modules.
The ring
R
is Gorenstein when
\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic to
R
, therefore we look at the properties of
\mathcal{C}
as a way to refine our understanding of
R
.

In Section
6
we discuss the
canonical degree
of
R
,
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
. It is defined so that its vanishing characterizes the Gorenstein property of
R
in codimension one. Furthermore, the minimal value of the canonical degree is assumed on the class of
almost Gorenstein rings
(Proposition
6.9
). Almost Gorenstein rings local rings (AGL for short) were introduced in
[
5
]
, and a rich theory has been developed in
[
29
]
and
[
33
]
. Numerical semigroup rings, in particular the three-generated ones, provide the set-up for many interesting results and examples, which we summarize at the end of the section. In Section
7
we relate the canonical degree to the Sally module, introduced by Vasconcelos. This connection inspired the definition of 2-AGL rings
[
12
]
, as a generalization of AGL rings. We propose a further generalization to 3-AGL rings, we give a characterization in terms of canonical degree when the type of the ring is small, we present supporting examples in three-generated semigroup rings (computed with the aid of Macaulay2), and we conclude with some open questions.

In Section
8
we discuss the
bi-canonical degree
of
R
,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
. Like the canonical degree, the vanishing of the bi-canonical degree characterizes Gorensteinness in codimension one. However, the bi-canonical degree seems more suitable for computations. We recall the important properties and we explore rings with minimal value of bideg.

In Section
9
we discuss the Comparison Conjecture. In
[
19
]
we conjectured that in general,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)\leq\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
. We prove that the conjecture holds in three-generated numerical semigroup rings, using the methods of
[
19
]
, where we had shown a special case. There have been very interesting recent developments for numerical semigroup rings. Using different methods, Herzog and Kumashiro in
[
39
]
studied an inequality that turned out to be equivalent to the Comparison Conjecture. Their results imply that the conjecture is true when the type of
R
is at most three, and they found a counterexample when the type is five.

In Section
10
we summarize how the canonical degree and the bi-canonical degree behave under change of rings, in particular augmented rings,
(\mathfrak{m}:\mathfrak{m})
, and hyperplane sections.

Finally, in Section
11
, we present the main results and open questions from
[
9
]
, our last paper with Vasconcelos. We extended the bi-canonical degree to rings where the canonical module is not necessarily an ideal, and we discussed generalizations to rings without canonical modules but admitting modules sharing some of their properties.

2.
Arithmetic Degree

We begin this section by recalling the definition of multiplicity, which motivated the definition of the arithmetic degree. Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d>0
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
s
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
. We denote length by
\lambda
. We define the Hilbert function of
M
with respect to
I
as

{\mathrm{H}}_{I,M}:n\mapsto{\lambda}(M/I^{n+1}M).

There exists a polynomial
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm P}_{I,M}(X)\in\mathbb{Q}[X]}
of degree
s
such that
{\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}}_{I,M}(n)=\mbox{\rm P}_{I,M}(n)}
for all sufficiently large
n
. This polynomial
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm P}_{I,M}(X)}
is called the Hilbert polynomial of
M
with respect to
I
and can be written as

\mbox{\rm P}_{I,M}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{e}_{i}(I,M){{n+s-i}\choose{s-i}}.

The integers
\mathrm{e}_{i}(I,M)
are called the Hilbert coefficients of
M
with respect to
I
. In particular,
\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)
is called the multiplicity (or degree) of the module
M
with respect to
I
. When
I=\mathfrak{m}
, we write
{\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},M)=\mbox{\rm deg}(M)}
.

There is a chain of submodules
{\displaystyle 0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{r-1}\subset M_{r}=M}
such that
{\displaystyle M_{i}/M_{i-1}\simeq R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
for some
{\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\in\mbox{\rm Spec}(R)}
.
In this case, we obtain

\mbox{\rm deg}(M)=\sum_{\tiny\dim(R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})=d}\mbox{\rm deg}(R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}).

Recall that
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ass}(M)\subseteq\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}}
. If
{\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mbox{\rm Ass}(M)}
with
{\displaystyle\dim(R/{\mathfrak{p}})=d}
, then the length
{\displaystyle{\lambda}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}})}
is the number of times that
{\mathfrak{p}}
occurs in the chain of submodules. That is, we have

\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mbox{\rm Ass}(M)\\
\dim(R/{\mathfrak{p}})=d\end{array}}{\lambda}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}})\,\mbox{\rm deg}(R/{\mathfrak{p}})\leq\mbox{\rm deg}(M).

The arithmetic degree of
M
was defined in a way that collects information about the associated primes of
M
in
all
codimensions.

Definition 2.1
.

[
59
, Definition 1.6]
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module.
The
arithmetic degree
of
M
is the integer

\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)=\sum_{\tiny{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mbox{\rm Ass}(M)}{\lambda}(\Gamma_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}}))\,\mbox{\rm deg}(R/{\mathfrak{p}}),

where
{\displaystyle\Gamma_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}})=H^{0}_{{\mathfrak{p}}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}})}
is the finite length submodule of
M_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
consisting of all elements of
{\displaystyle M_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
with support in
{\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
. (In some literatures, the arithmetic degree is also denoted by
{\displaystyle\mbox{arith-deg}(M)}
.)

If all the associated primes of
M
have the same codimension, then
\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)
is equal to
\mbox{\rm deg}(M)
.
Both
\mbox{\rm deg}(M)
and
\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)
can be defined in the same way as above when
M
is a (graded) finitely generated module over a standard graded algebra
{\displaystyle A=A_{0}[A_{1}]}
with the Artinian local ring
A_{0}
. In this case, the Hilbert function of
M
is defined in terms of the homogeneous maximal ideal of
A
. Bayer and Mumford
[
6
]
gave an extensive survey on
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm adeg}(A)}
and its significance in algebraic geometry, where
{\displaystyle A=k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]/I}
is the quotient of a polynomial ring over a field
k
.

Vasconcelos showed how to compute the arithmetic degree of a graded module without using a primary decomposition. Due to his theorem, software systems such as Macaulay2 or CoCoA can be used to compute the arithmetic degree.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
59
, Proposition 1.11]
,
[
58
, Proposition 5]
)

Let
R=k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]
and
M
a graded
R
-module. Then

\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\mbox{\rm deg}\left(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}\left(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R),R\right)\right).

One of the main characteristics of a new degree function
\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)
(see Section 5) that we wish to obtain is that it behaves well under generic hyperplane sections. That is, if
A=A_{0}[A_{1}]
is a standard graded algebra with the Artinian local ring
A_{0}
and if
h\in A_{1}
is not contained in any minimal prime of
A
, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)\geq\mbox{\rm Deg}(M/hM)}
. Unfortunately, the arithmetic degree fails to obtain this characteristic.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
59
, Theorem 1.14]

Let
A=A_{0}[A_{1}]
be a standard graded algebra with the Artinian local ring
A_{0}
and
M
a finitely generated graded
A
-module. Let
h\in A_{1}
be a regular element on
M
. Then

\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)\leq\mbox{\rm adeg}(M/hM).

In
[
58
]
Vasconcelos explored relations between the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and the arithmetic degree of a standard algebra
A
.
He also studied comparisons between the arithmetic degree and the reduction number of
A
. He extensively studied the reduction number as a measure of the complexity of
A
.
One interesting application of the arithmetic degree is that it can be used to make predictions about the outcome of carrying out a Noether normalization on a graded algebra.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
59
, Theorem 2.11]
,
[
58
, Theorem 7]
)

Let
A
be an affine algebra over an infinite field
k
and let
k[{\mathbf{z}}]
be a Noether normalization of
A
. Let
M
be a finitely generated, graded, and faithful
A
-module. Then every element of
A
satisfies a monic equation over
k[{\mathbf{z}}]
of degree at most
\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)
.

Miyazki, Vogel, and Yanagawa
[
42
]
presented a refined theory on the arithmetic degree of a homogeneous ideal
I
of a polynomial ring
S
over a field. A key point in their work is the description of the arithmetic degree in terms of the Hilbert polynomial of an appropriate
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{S}^{i}(S/I,S)
,
based on Vasconcelos’ ideas
[
16
,
58
]
.

3.
jdeg

The notion of
j
-multiplicity was introduced and developed by Achilles and Manaresi
[
2
]
, Flenner, O’Carroll and Vogel
[
18
]
, and evolved into a rich
extension of the ordinary multiplicity (see for instance
[
43
,
53
]
). Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and let
A
be a finitely generated graded
R
-algebra. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
A
-module. Then
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
is a graded submodule of
M
and is annihilated by a sufficiently large power of
\mathfrak{m}
. Therefore,
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
can be considered as a graded module over the Artinian ring
{\displaystyle A/\mathfrak{m}^{k}A}
for some sufficiently large
k
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and let
A
be a finitely generated graded
R
-algebra. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
A
-module. Then the
j
-multiplicity
of
M
is

\mbox{\rm j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm deg}({\mathrm{H}}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(M))}&\mbox{if}\;\;{\displaystyle\dim({\mathrm{H}}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(M))=\dim_{R}(M)},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

A special case of the
j
-multiplicity can be defined as follows: Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring,
I
an arbitrary
R
-ideal, and
N
a finite
R
-module of dimension
d
. Then the associated graded module
{M=\mbox{\rm gr}_{I}(N)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}I^{j}N/I^{j+1}N}
is a finitely generated graded module over the associated graded ring
{A=\mbox{\rm gr}_{I}(R)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}I^{j}/I^{j+1}}
. In this case, the
j
-multiplicity of
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm gr}_{I}(N)}
is sometimes called the
j
-multiplicity of
I
on
N
. Polini and Xie
[
48
]
established the depth of the associated graded ring in terms of the
j
-multiplicity of an ideal.

Pham and Vasconcelos introduced the
jdeg
, extending the definition of
j
-multiplicity by localization. We note that in contrast to other extensions of the classical multiplicity, such as the arithmetic degree, that requires
R
to be a local ring,
jdeg
places no such restrictions on
R
.

Definition 3.2
.

[
46
, Definition 1.1]
Let
R
be a Noetherian ring and
A
a standard graded
R
-algebra. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
A
-module. For each prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
R
, we define

\mbox{\rm j}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm deg}({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}^{0}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}}))}&\mbox{if}\;\;{\displaystyle\dim({\mathrm{H}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}^{0}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}}))=\dim_{R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}(M_{{\mathfrak{p}}})},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then
jdeg
of
M
is the integer

\mbox{\rm jdeg}(M)=\sum_{\tiny{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mbox{\rm Spec}(R)}\mbox{\rm j}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(M).

If
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is an Artinian local ring, then
\mbox{\rm jdeg}(M)
is equal to the traditional multiplicity
\mbox{\rm deg}(M)
. One important application is that the
jdeg
provides estimates for the complexity of normalization.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
45
, Theorem 2]

Let
R
be a Noetherian domain and
A
a semistandard graded
R
-algebra of finite integral closure
\overline{A}
. Consider a sequence of distinct integral graded extensions

A\subseteq A_{0}\rightarrow A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}\cdots\rightarrow A_{n}=\overline{A},

where the
A_{i}
satisfy the
S_{2}
condition of Serre. Then
n\leq\mbox{\rm jdeg}(\overline{A}/A)
.

In
[
45
]
Pham and Vasconcelos also explored how the values of
jdeg
relate to other well studied invariants of the algebra. For instance, let
A
be a semistandard graded domain over a field
k
. If
A
satisfies
S_{2}
and
A\neq\overline{A}
, then
\mbox{\rm jdeg}(\overline{A}/A)=\mathrm{e}_{1}(A)-\mathrm{e}_{1}(\overline{A})
. Among other interesting results, using
jdeg
the authors generalize bounds of
[
47
]
(see
[
45
, Theorem 3]
). In the sequel
[
46
]
the focus is on
jdeg
of blow-up algebras. We denote the minimal number of generators of a module
M
by
\nu(M)
.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
46
, Proposition 4.1]

Let
{\displaystyle(R,\mathfrak{m})}
be a Noetherian local domain of positive dimension and let
M
be a non-free module of rank r that is free on the punctured spectrum. Let
S
be the symmetric algebra of
M
. Then

\mbox{\rm jdeg}(S)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1+\mbox{\rm j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(S)&\mbox{if}\;\;{\displaystyle\nu(M)\geq\dim(R)+r,}\\
1&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

A more specific bound of
jdeg
of the symmetric algebra with additional conditions on
R
and
M
can be found in
[
46
, Theorem 4.2 and Proposition 4.3]
. An important property of
jdeg
is its invariance property.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
44
, Theorem 4.1.1]

Let
R
be a reduced universally catenary Noetherian ring and
I
an ideal containing a regular element. Let
A=R[It]
be the Rees algebra of
I
and let
\overline{A}
be the integral closure of
A
. Let
B
be a finitely generated graded
A
-algebra defined by a decreasing filtration such that
{\displaystyle A\subsetneq B\subset\overline{A}}
. Then

\mbox{\rm j}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mbox{\rm gr}(A))=\mbox{\rm j}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mbox{\rm gr}(B))\quad\mbox{for every}\;\;{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mbox{\rm Spec}(R).

Moreover, if
B
satisfies the Serre’s condition
({\mathrm{S}}_{2})
, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm jdeg}(\mbox{\rm gr}(A))=\mbox{\rm jdeg}(\mbox{\rm gr}(B))}
.

4.
Homological Degree

The homological degree was introduced by Vasconcelos as an extension of
the usual notion of multiplicity, aimed to provide a numerical signature for
a module
M
when
M
is not necessarily Cohen-Macaulay. It is defined recursively on the dimension of the module, which allows,
in principle, for its calculation by symbolic computer packages.

Definition 4.1
.

[
60
, Definition 2.8]
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
. Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
s
. The
homological degree
of
M
with respect to
I
is defined as follows:

\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle{\lambda}(M)}&\mbox{if}\;\;{\displaystyle s=0,}\\
{\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+\sum_{i=d-s+1}^{d}{{s-1}\choose{i-d+s-1}}\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}\left(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\right)}&\mbox{if}\;\;{\displaystyle s>0.}\end{array}\right.

When
I=\mathfrak{m}
, we simply write
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M)}
.

If
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a Gorenstein local ring, by the Local Duality Theorem
[
11
, Theorem 3.5.8]
, for all integers
i
, there exist natural isomorphisms

\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\simeq\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(H^{d-i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),\,E),

where
E=E(k)
denotes the injective envelope of the residue field
k
.

Goto and Ozeki
[
31
]
introduced an alternative definition of a homological degree.

Definition 4.2
.

[
31
, Definition 1.1]
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring with infinite residue field. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
s
.
The
homological degree
of
M
with respect to
I
is defined as follows:

\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle{\lambda}(M)}&\mbox{if}\;\;{\displaystyle s=0}\\
{\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}{{s-1}\choose{i}}\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}\left(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),E)\right)}&\mbox{if}\;\;{\displaystyle s>0.}\end{array}\right.

By
[
31
, Fact 2.1]
,
{\displaystyle\dim\left(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),E)\right)\leq i}
for each
i
.
Therefore the definition of the homological degree is a recursive definition. Moreover, if
\dim(R)=\dim(M)
, then the definitions
4.1
and
4.2
coincide. Note that if
M
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)}
.

In the original definition of the homological degree
[
60
, Definition 2.8]
, Vasconcelos supposed that
R
is a graded algebra,
S
a Gorenstein graded algebra mapping onto
R
and
M
is a finitely generated graded module of
R
, and he used
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}_{S}^{i}(M,S)}
in the definition.
In
[
31
]
, the authors supposed that
R
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically complete. In this case,
R
is a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein local ring

. Thus, by passing to
S
, without loss of generality, they assumed that
R
is a Gorenstein ring. Throughout this section we may assume that
R
is a Gorenstein local ring, not only for simplicity, but also because it enables us to use both definitions (
[
60
, Definition 2.8]
,
[
31
, Definition 1.1]
) due to the Local Duality Theorem.

Remark 4.3
.

The following are the homological degrees of a module
M
of small dimension.

(1)

Suppose
\dim(M)=1
. Then

\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right).

(2)

Suppose
\dim(M)=2
. Let
{\displaystyle N=\mbox{\rm Hom}(H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),E)}
. Then

\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+{\lambda}\left(N\right)}&\mbox{if}\;\;\dim(N)=0,\\
{\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,N)+{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N)\right)}&\mbox{if}\;\;\dim(N)=1.\end{array}\right.

Also, by
[
59
, Proposition 3.5]
,
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M)=\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)+{\lambda}\left(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{2}_{R}\left(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,R),R\right)\right)}
.

A finitely generated module
M
is said to be
generalized Cohen-Macaulay
if
M_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is Cohen-Macaulay for every non-maximal prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Recall that
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)
is finitely generated for all
i<\dim(M)
. By using the Matlis Duality, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.
If
M
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay module and
\dim(M)=s
, then

\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}{{s-1}\choose{i}}\,{\lambda}\left(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right).

The integer
{\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}{{s-1}\choose{i}}\,{\lambda}\left(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)}
is called the Stückrad-Vogel invariant of
M
and is denoted by
{\displaystyle\mathbb{I}(M)}
. Thus, if
M
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay module, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+\mathbb{I}(M)}
. A family of elements
{\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}}
in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is said to be a
system of parameters
for
M
if
{\displaystyle\dim(M/({\mathbf{x}})M)=0}
, where
s=\dim(M)
. We call an
R
-ideal
Q
a
parameter ideal
for
M
if there exists a system of parameters
{\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}}
for
M
such that
Q=({\mathbf{x}})
.
A finitely generated module
M
is said to be
Buchsbaum
if
{\displaystyle{\lambda}(M/QM)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)=\mathbb{I}(M)}
for every parameter ideal
Q
of
M
. It is well known that a Buchbaum module is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay module. Thus, we obtain the following formula for
\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)
in terms of multiplicities.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.
If
M
is a Buchsbaum module, then for every parameter ideal
Q
of
M
we have

\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+{\lambda}(M/QM)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M).

Properties of homological degrees

Now we observe general properties of the homological degree. Particularly, we are interested in bounds for
hdeg
, how
hdeg
works with short exact sequences, and most importantly how
hdeg
behaves under hyperplane sections.

Proposition 4.6
.

[
63
, Proposition 7.6]
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring and

an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal such that
\mathfrak{m}^{r}\subset I

\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)\leq r^{s}\,\mbox{\rm deg}(M)+r^{s-1}\,\left(\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M)-\mbox{\rm deg}(M)\right).

Proposition 4.7
.

(
[

[

[

Let

\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let

(3)

{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(L)\leq\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)+(s-1)\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(N)}

If

for any generic hyperplane section

{\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}I^{n}t^{n}}

{\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{+}(I)=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}I^{n}t^{n}}

\mbox{\rm Proj}\left(\mathcal{R}(I)\right)=\left\{{\mathfrak{p}}\mid{\mathfrak{p}}\;\mbox{is a graded prime ideal of $\mathcal{R}(I)$ such that $\mathcal{R}_{+}(I)\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}$ }\right\}.

Let
{\displaystyle f:I\rightarrow\mathcal{R}(I)}
be a map given by
{\displaystyle f(x)=xt}
.

.

[

Let

.

[

Brennan and Ciupercă showed when the homological degrees are preserved under modulo operation for modules of small dimension.

Theorem 4.11
.

[
7

Let

\mathfrak{m}
the ideal generated by the elements of degree

. Let

(1)

Suppose

Homological torsion is a combinatorial expression of the terms of homological degrees. It behaves well under hyperplane sections
[
31
, Lemma 2.8]
and plays a very useful role in the study of Hilbert coefficients.

Definition 4.12
.

[
31
, Definition 1.2]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d>0
with infinite residue field
k
. Let
E=E(k)
be the injective envelope of
k
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
s\geq 2
.
The
i
-th
homological torsion
of
M
with respect to
I
is

T_{I}^{i}(M)=\sum_{j=1}^{s-i}{{s-i-1}\choose{j-1}}\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}\left(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),E)\right).

In particular, we write
T_{I}(M)=T^{1}_{I}(M)
and call it the homological torsion. That is,

T_{I}(M)=\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}{{s-2}\choose{j-1}}\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}\left(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M),E)\right).

Proposition 4.13
.

[
63
, Proposition 6.1]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring. Let
M
be a generalized Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module of dimension
s\geq 2
. Let
{\displaystyle Q=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})}
be a parameter ideal for
M
. Then

{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s-1})M)\right)\leq T_{I}(M)

for any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
. Moreover, if
M
is Buchsbaum, then the equality holds.

Proof.

The following is a modification of the proof given in
[
63
]
with added details. We prove by induction on
s
. Suppose
s=2
. We may assume that
x=x_{1}
is
M
-regular. From the exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\rightarrow M/xM\rightarrow 0}
, we obtain

H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0\longrightarrow H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/xM)\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\longrightarrow\cdots.

Thus,
{\displaystyle{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/xM)\right)\leq{\lambda}\left(H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)=T_{I}(M)}
.

Suppose
s\geq 3
and the assertion is true for
s-1
. Let
{\displaystyle N=M/x_{1}M}
,
{\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{s-1}}
, and
{\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}=x_{2},\ldots,x_{s-1}}
. Then by induction hypothesis, we obtain

{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/{\mathbf{x}}M)\right)={\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N/{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}N)\right)\leq T_{I}(N).

It is enough to show that
{\displaystyle T_{I}(N)\leq T_{I}(M)}
. From the exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0}
, we obtain the long exact sequence

\cdots\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N)\longrightarrow H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\longrightarrow\cdots.

Therefore, for each
j=1,\ldots,s-2
, we have

{\lambda}\left(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N)\right)\leq{\lambda}\left(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+{\lambda}\left(H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right).

Now we compute
{\displaystyle T_{I}(N)}
.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\displaystyle T_{I}(N)}&=&{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s-2}{{s-3}\choose{j-1}}{\lambda}\left(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(N)\right)}\\
&\leq&{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s-2}{{s-3}\choose{j-1}}{\lambda}\left(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{s-2}{{s-3}\choose{j-1}}{\lambda}\left(H^{j+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{\lambda}\left(H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+\sum_{j=2}^{s-2}\left({{s-3}\choose{j-1}}+{{s-3}\choose{j-2}}\right){\lambda}\left(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+{\lambda}\left(H^{s-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)}\\
&=&{\displaystyle T_{I}(M).}\end{array}

∎

Euler Characteristic

Let
Q=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})
be a parameter ideal for a module
M
. The
i
th Kozul homology module generated by the system of parameters
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
with coefficients in
M
is denoted by
{\displaystyle H_{i}(Q,M)}
. The (first)
Euler characteristic
of
M
relative to
Q
is defined as

\chi_{1}(Q,M)=\sum_{i\geq 1}(-1)^{i-1}{\lambda}\left(H_{i}(Q,M)\right).

Then by a classical result of Serre
[
4
,
52
]
, we have

\chi_{1}(Q,M)={\lambda}(M/QM)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M).

The Euler characteristic can be also bounded by the homological degree.

Theorem 4.14
.

[
22
, Theorem 7.2]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian complete local ring with infinite residue field and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module with
{\displaystyle\dim(M)=\dim(R)=d\geq 1}
. Then for every parameter ideal
Q
for
R
, we have

\chi_{1}(Q,M)\leq\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M).

Goto and Ozeki proved when the equality holds true. Recall that we denote the Hilbert polynomial of
M
with respect to
Q
by
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm P}_{Q,M}(X)}
.

Theorem 4.15
.

[
31
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian complete local ring with infinite residue field and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module with
{\displaystyle\dim(M)=\dim(R)=d\geq 1}
. Let
Q
be a parameter ideal for
R
. Then

\chi_{1}(Q,M)=\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)

if and only if

(-1)^{i}\mathrm{e}_{i}(Q,M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle T^{\,i}_{Q}(M)}&\mbox{if}\;\;1\leq i\leq d-1,\\
{\displaystyle{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)}&\mbox{if}\;\;i=d\end{array}\right.

for all
1\leq i\leq d
, and
{\displaystyle{\lambda}\left(M/Q^{n+1}M\right)=\mbox{\rm P}_{Q,M}(n)}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Example 4.16
.

[
31
, Example 3.8]

Let
\ell\geq 2
and
m\geq 1
be integers. Let

S=k[\![X_{i},Y_{i},Z_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell,\;1\leq j\leq m]\!]\quad\mbox{and}\quad R=S/(X_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell})\cap(Y_{1},\ldots,X_{\ell}),

where
S
is the formal power series ring over an infinite field
k
. Let
x_{i},y_{i}
, and
z_{j}
denote the images of
X_{i},Y_{i}
, and
Z_{j}
in
R
respectively. Consider the following parameter ideal of
R
:

Q=(x_{i}-y_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell)+(z_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq m).

Then we have

\chi_{1}(Q,R)=\ell-1,\quad\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,R)=2,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(R)=2+{{\ell+m-1}\choose{m+1}}.

In particular, if
\ell=2
, then
{\displaystyle\chi_{1}(Q,R)=\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(R)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,R)}
.

Chern number

The homological torsion can be used as a bound for the first Hilbert coefficient
\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)
of a module
M
relative to an ideal generated by a system of parameters
Q
for
M
. This coefficient
\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)
is often called the Chern number, and it has been extensively studied by Vasconcelos and the authors
[
63
,
26
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
22
]
.

Theorem 4.17
.

[
22
, Theorem 6.5]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian complete local ring with infinite residue field and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module with
{\displaystyle\dim(R)=\dim(M)\geq 2}
. Let
Q
be a parameter ideal for
R
. Then

{\displaystyle-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)\leq T_{Q}(M)}
.

As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.15
, we obtain that if
{\displaystyle\chi_{1}(Q,M)=\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)}
, then

{\displaystyle-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)=T_{Q}(M)}
. The converse is true if
M
is unmixed. We note that
[
31
, Example 4.5]
shows that the unmixedness is necessary.

Theorem 4.18
.

[
31
, Theorem 4.2]

Suppose that
{\displaystyle\dim(R)=\dim(M)\geq 2}
and that
M
is unmixed. Let
Q
be a parameter ideal for
R
. Then

-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)=T_{Q}(M)\;\;\mbox{if and only if}\;\;\chi_{1}(Q,M)=\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M).

Recall that if
M
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)
. Thus, sometimes the integer
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)}
is called a
Cohen-Macaulay deficiency
. Vasconcelos conjectured that the negativity of
\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,R)
, where
Q
is a parameter ideal of
R
, is an expression of the lack of Cohen-Macaulayness of
R

[
63
, Conjecture 3.1]
. Ghezzi, Goto, Hong, Ozeki, Phuong and Vasconcelos settled this conjecture affirmatively for rings in
[
20
]
and for modules in
[
22
]
. Thus,
\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)
can be considered also as a Cohen-Macaulay deficiency. The following proposition shows the relation between these two Cohen-Macaulay deficiencies.

Proposition 4.19
.

[
63
, Corollary 7.7]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian complete local ring with infinite residue field and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module with
{\displaystyle\dim(M)=\dim(R)=d\geq 1}
. Let
Q=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
be a parameter ideal for
R
such that the sequence
{\displaystyle a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}
is superficial relative to
\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)
. Then

-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)\leq\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M).

Proof.

We include a modification of the proof of
[
30
, Lemma 2.4]
. We prove by induction on
d
. If
d=1
, let
Q=(a)
. Let
t
be a sufficiently large integer such that

H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=(0:_{M}Q^{t}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\lambda}(M/Q^{t}M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)t-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M).

Then we obtain the following:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\displaystyle-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)}&=&{\displaystyle{\lambda}(M/Q^{t}M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)t}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{\lambda}(M/Q^{t}M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q^{t},M)}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{\lambda}(M/Q^{t}M)-\left({\lambda}(M/Q^{t}M)-\chi_{1}(Q^{t},M)\right)}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{\lambda}(H_{1}(Q^{t},M))}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{\lambda}((0:_{M}Q^{t}))}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right).}\end{array}

Since
\dim(M)=1
, we have
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)+{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)}
. Thus, the assertion follows.

Suppose
d\geq 2
. Let
{\mathbf{a}}=a_{1},\ldots,a_{d-1}
,
S=R/({\mathbf{a}})
,
q=QS
, and
M^{\prime}=M/({\mathbf{a}})M
. Then as proved in the
d=1
case, we have
{\displaystyle-\mathrm{e}_{1}(q,M^{\prime})={\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{\prime})\right)}
. Therefore,

-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)\leq-\mathrm{e}_{1}(q,M^{\prime})={\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{\prime})\right)\leq\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M),

where the last inequality follows from Corollary
4.10
.
∎

By Theorem
4.14
and Proposition
4.19
, we obtain

\max\{\chi_{1}(Q,M),\;-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)\}\leq\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M).

In
[
27
]
, Goto, Hong, and Vasconcelos established the relationships among
\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q)
and various first Euler characteristics when
Q
is a parameter ideal of
R
. We state one of the main results of
[
27
]
, which is related to the inequality stated above.

Theorem 4.20
.

[
27
, Theorem 4.2]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension

with
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm depth}(R)\geq d-1}
and infinite residue field. Let
Q=({\mathbf{x}})
be a parameter ideal of

. Then
{\displaystyle\chi_{1}(Q,M)=-\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)}
if and only if
{\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}}
is a
d
-sequence.

The
sectional genus

{\displaystyle g_{s}(I,M)}
of
M
with respect to an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal

is defined as

g_{s}(I,M)={\lambda}(M/IM)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,M)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(I,M).

Suppose that
{\displaystyle\dim(R)=\dim(M)}
and
Q
is a parameter ideal for

. Then due to Serre, we have
{\displaystyle\chi_{1}(Q,M)={\lambda}(M/QM)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)}
. Thus, we can rewrite

As consequences of Theorems
4.14
and
4.17
, both
{\displaystyle g_{s}(Q,M)}
and
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)-\,T_{Q}(M)}
are less than or equal to
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(Q,M)}
. Goto and Ozeki showed a direct relation between
{\displaystyle g_{s}(Q,M)}
and
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)-\,T_{Q}(M)}
.

Theorem 4.21
.

[
32
, Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.4]

Suppose
{\displaystyle\dim(R)=\dim(M)\geq 2}
and let
Q
be a parameter ideal for
R
. Then

g_{s}(Q,M)\leq\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(M)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(Q,M)-T_{Q}(M).

The equality holds true if and only if

(-1)^{i}\mathrm{e}_{i}(Q,M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle T^{i}_{Q}(M)}&\mbox{if}\;\;2\leq i\leq d-1,\\
{\displaystyle{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)}&\mbox{if}\;\;i=d,\end{array}\right.

for all
2\leq i\leq d
and

{\lambda}\left(M/QM\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{e}_{i}(Q,M).

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, and
Q
a minimal reduction of
I
.
Rossi proved that, if
{\displaystyle\dim(R)\leq 2}
, then
{\displaystyle g_{s}(I,R)\geq r_{Q}(I)-1}
, where
r_{Q}(I)
is the reduction number of
I
with respect to
Q

[
49
, Corollary 1.5]
. Thus, the homological degree can be used as an upper bound for a reduction number. Ghezzi, Goto, Hong, and Vasconcelos were able to extend Rossi’s result to
2
-dimensional Buchsbaum rings
[
24
, Theorem 4.3]
. We conclude this section with several open questions raised by Vasconcelos.

Question 4.22
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring which is a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein local ring. Let
\mathcal{M}(R)
be the category of all finitely generated
R
-modules. Consider the following set of rational numbers:

\left\{\frac{\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M)-\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M/hM)}{\mbox{\rm deg}(M)}\;\;\vline\;\;M\in\mathcal{M}(R),\;\mbox{and $h$ is a generic hyperplane section}\right\}.

Is this set finite or bounded? Can it be expressed as an invariant of
R
?

Question 4.23
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein local ring and let
Q=({\mathbf{x}})
be a parameter ideal for
R
. If
{\mathbf{x}}
is a
d
-sequence, what is an estimation for
\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{Q}(R)
?

Question 4.24
.

Let
{\displaystyle\varphi:(R,\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow(S,\mathfrak{n})}
be a flat homomorphisms of local rings which are homomorphic images of a Gorenstein local rings. Can we relate
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}(R),\mbox{\rm hdeg}(S)}
, and
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm hdeg}(S/\mathfrak{m}S)}
?

5.
Cohomological Degree

Cohomological degrees were introduced by Vasconcelos and generalize the homological degree.

Definition 5.1
.

(
[
59
, Definition 3.1]
,
[
15
]
)
Let
R
be either a Noetherian local ring or a standard graded algebra with the (irrelevant) maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Let
\mathcal{M}(R)
be the category of finitely generated
R
-modules. The
cohomological degree
(or
extended degree
) on
\mathcal{M}(R)
is a function
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}:\mathcal{M}(R)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}}
satisfying the following three conditions:

(i)

{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)=\mbox{\rm Deg}\left(M/H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
R
has positive depth and that
h\in R
is a regular, generic hyperplane section on
M
. Then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M/hM)\leq\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)}
.

(iii)

If
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay module, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)=\mbox{\rm deg}(M)}
.

If
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, then the homological degree
\mbox{\rm hdeg}_{I}
with respect to
I
is a cohomological degree of
R
(Proposition
4.7
, Corollary
4.10
).

Remark 5.2
.

There is just one cohomological degree in dimension at most 1.

(1)

If
{\displaystyle\dim(M)=0}
, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)={\lambda}(M)}
.

(2)

If
{\displaystyle\dim(M)=1}
, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)=\mbox{\rm deg}(M)+{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)=\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)=\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M)}
.

An important property of the cohomological degree is that it provides an estimate for the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
\mbox{\rm reg}(A)
of a standard graded algebra
A
.

Theorem 5.3
.

[
15
, Theorem 2.4]

Let
A
be a standard graded algebra over an infinite field
k
. Then

\mbox{\rm reg}(A)<\mbox{\rm Deg}(A)
.

Another important property of the cohomological degree is that it bounds the minimal number of generators of a module.

Proposition 5.4
.

[
15
, Proposition 2.1]

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.

(1)

{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)\geq\mbox{\rm deg}(M)}
and the equality holds if and only if
M
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

{\displaystyle\nu(M)\leq\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)}
.

(3)

Let
L
be a submodule of
M
. If
{\displaystyle{\lambda}(L)<\infty}
, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)=\mbox{\rm Deg}(M/L)+{\lambda}(L)}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

[
59
, Proposition 3.9]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. Then

\mbox{\rm deg}(M)\leq\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)\leq\mbox{\rm Deg}(M).

Proof.

We collect the associated primes of
M
by their dimensions:
{\displaystyle\dim(M)=s_{1}>s_{2}>\ldots>s_{n}}
. Then

\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)=a_{s_{1}}(M)+a_{s_{2}}(M)+\cdots+a_{s_{n}}(M),

where
a_{s_{i}}(M)
is the contribution of all primes in
\mbox{\rm Ass}(M)
of dimension
s_{i}
. Also,
{\displaystyle a_{s_{1}}(M)=\mbox{\rm deg}(M)}
, which proves the first inequality. Since both
adeg
and
Deg
are additive on the exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\rightarrow M\rightarrow M/H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\rightarrow 0}
, we may assume that
H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
. Let
h
be a regular hyperplane section on
M
. Then by Theorem
2.3
, the induction hypothesis on
\dim(M)
, and Definition
5.1
, we obtain

\mbox{\rm adeg}(M)\leq\mbox{\rm adeg}(M/hM)\leq\mbox{\rm Deg}(M/hM)\leq\mbox{\rm Deg}(M).\qed

Cohomological degrees can be used to estimate the minimal number of generators of an ideal in an arbitrary local ring. The following theorem generalizes a bound that holds when
R
is Cohen-Macaulay
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
, and it improves previously known bounds (see
[
15
, Section 3]
).

Theorem 5.6
.

[
15
, Theorem 4.6]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
with infinite residue field. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Suppose that
{\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{s}\subseteq I}
. Then, for any cohomological degree
Deg
on
\mathcal{M}(R)
, we have

\nu(I)\leq\mbox{\rm Deg}(R)\,{{s+d-2}\choose{d-1}}+{{s+d-2}\choose{d-2}}.

Gunston introduced the following cohomological degree in his Ph.D. thesis.

Definition 5.7
.

[
35
, Definition 3.1.1]
Let
\mathcal{D}(R)
denote the set of all cohomological degree functions on
\mathcal{M}(R)
. For a finitely generated
R
-module, we define

\mbox{\rm bdeg}(M)=\min\{\mbox{\rm Deg}(M)\mid\mbox{\rm Deg}\in\mathcal{D}(R)\}.

The inductive approach for
bdeg
works.

Theorem 5.8
.

[
35
, Theorem 3.1.2]

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module of positive depth. Then for a generic hyperplane
h
, we have

\mbox{\rm bdeg}(M)=\mbox{\rm bdeg}(M/hM).

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
s
. There exist a generic superficial sequence
{\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}}
for
M
such that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bdeg}(M)}&\leq&{\displaystyle{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{s}{\lambda}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})M)\right)}\\
&\leq&{\displaystyle(s+1)\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M)-s\cdot\mbox{\rm deg}(M)}\end{array}

Proof.

The first inequality is a consequence of Theorem
5.8
and the second follows from Corollary
4.10
.
∎

One of the difficulties of computing cohomological degrees lies on their behavior on short exact sequences. It turns out
bdeg
has more amenable properties.

Proposition 5.10
.

(
[
35
, Proposition 3.2.2]
,
[
13
, Propositions 3.2 and 3.3]
)

Let
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0}
be an exact sequence of finitely generated
R
-modules.

(1)

If
{\displaystyle{\lambda}(N)<\infty}
, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bdeg}(M)\leq\mbox{\rm bdeg}(L)+{\lambda}(N)}
.

(2)

{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bdeg}(M)\leq\mbox{\rm bdeg}(L)+\mbox{\rm bdeg}(N)}
.

(3)

{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bdeg}(L)\leq\mbox{\rm bdeg}(M)+\left(\dim(L)-1\right)\mbox{\rm bdeg}(N)}
.

We denote
{\displaystyle M^{*}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(M,R)}
for an
R
-module
M
.

Theorem 5.11
.

[
13
, Theorems 5.2 and 5.3]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
d
and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. Then

\nu_{R}(M^{*})\leq\mbox{\rm bdeg}_{R}(M^{*})\leq\mbox{\rm hdeg}(M)\left(\mbox{\rm deg}(R)+\frac{d(d-1)}{2}\right).

6.
The canonical degree

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring that has a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. The canonical degree was introduced in
[
25
]
with the overall goal to refine our understanding of
R
and to measure the deviation from
R
being Gorenstein.
The canonical degree requires knowledge of the multiplicity of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals.

We begin with the definition of canonical degree in
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings, which is our main setting. We denote length by
\lambda
, and the multiplicity associated with the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic filtration by
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rule{8.53581pt}{0.56905pt}$\;\!$})
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. The
canonical degree of
R
is the integer

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathcal{C})-{\lambda}(R/\mathcal{C})
. In particular, if
(a)
is a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)={\lambda}(\mathcal{C}/(a))\geq 0
.

We show in
[
25
, Proposition 2.1]
that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
is well defined; that is, the integer
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathcal{C})-{\lambda}(R/\mathcal{C})
is independent of the canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 6.2
.

In
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings the vanishing of
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
is equivalent to
R
being Gorenstein, since we have
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=0
if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is a principal ideal.

We define
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
in full generality as follows.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
that has a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. The
canonical degree of
R
is the integer

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=\sum_{\tiny\mbox{\rm ht}({\mathfrak{p}})=1}\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R_{{\mathfrak{p}}})\mbox{\rm deg}(R/{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{\tiny\mbox{\rm ht}({\mathfrak{p}})=1}[\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{{\mathfrak{p}}})-{\lambda}((R/\mathcal{C})_{{\mathfrak{p}}})]\mbox{\rm deg}(R/{\mathfrak{p}}).

We show in
[
25
, Theorem 2.2]
that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
is a well-defined finite sum independent of the canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. The definition yields a nice formula in terms of multiplicity when
\mathcal{C}
is equimultiple, and several results in dimension one extend to higher dimension when
\mathcal{C}
is equimultiple.

Remark 6.4
.

If the canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
is equimultiple with a minimal reduction
(a)
, then by the associativity formula
[
11
, Corollary 4.7.8]
we have

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mathcal{C}/(a))=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{C}/(a)).

In general, the vanishing of

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
characterizes Gorensteinness in codimension one.

Remark 6.5
.

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)\geq 0
and vanishes if and only if
R
is Gorenstein in codimension
1
.

Remark 6.6
.

If
R
is Gorenstein, it is clear that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=0
. However, the converse does not hold in general. For example, if
R
is a normal domain we have that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=0
, since
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is Gorenstein for every prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
R
with
\mbox{\rm ht}({\mathfrak{p}})\leq 1
. Therefore if
R
is a non-Gorenstein normal domain then
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=0
.

Generalizing the one-dimensional case, we observe that when
\mathcal{C}
is equimultiple the vanishing of
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
is equivalent to
R
being Gorenstein. We denote the type of
R
by
r(R)
.

Corollary 6.7
.

[
25
, Corollary 2.5]

Suppose that the canonical ideal of
R
is equimultiple. Then we have the following.

(1)

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)\geq r(R)-1
.

(2)

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=0
if and only if
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Let
(a)
be a minimal reduction of the canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Then

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},\mathcal{C}/(a))\geq\nu(\mathcal{C}/(a))=r(R)-1.

If
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=0
then
r(R)=1
, which proves that
R
is Gorenstein.
∎

In light of the above corollary, we are interested in examining extremal values of the canonical degree. We see that the minimal value for the canonical degree is assumed on a new class of Cohen-Macaulay rings, called almost Gorenstein rings, introduced
in
[
5
]
. A rich theory has been developed in dimension one by Goto, Matsuoka and Phuong
[
29
]
, and in higher dimension by Goto, Takahashi and Taniguchi
[
33
]
. We recall the definition.

Definition 6.8
.

[
33
, Definition 3.3]

A Cohen-Macaulay local ring
R
with a canonical module
\omega
is said to be an
almost Gorenstein
local ring (AGL for short) if there exists an exact sequence of
R
-modules

{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow R\rightarrow\omega\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0}

such that
\nu(X)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(X)
, where
\nu(X)
is the minimal number of generators of
X
. In particular, if
R
is Gorenstein, then
X=0
, so
R
is almost Gorenstein.

The following result characterizes AGL rings in terms of minimal canonical degree. We include the proof for completeness.

Proposition 6.9
.

[
25
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
.
Then
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=r(R)-1
if and only if
R
is an almost Gorenstein ring.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is not a Gorenstein ring. Suppose that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=r(R)-1
. Let
(a)
be a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
. Consider the exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\to R\overset{\varphi}{\to}\mathcal{C}\to X\to 0,\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;\varphi(1)=a.

Then
\nu(X)=r(R)-1=\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(X)
. Thus,
R
is an almost Gorenstein ring.

For the converse, assume that
R
is almost Gorenstein. There exists an exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\to R\overset{\varphi}{\to}\mathcal{C}\to X\to 0\;\;\mbox{such that}\;\;\nu(X)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(X).

Since
\dim(X)=0
by

[
33
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have
\lambda(X)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(X)=\nu(X)=\lambda(X/\mathfrak{m}X)
, so that
\mathfrak{m}X=(0)
.
Let
a=\varphi(1)\in\mathcal{C}
and set
Q=(a)
. Then
\mathfrak{m}Q\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{C}\subseteq Q
. Therefore, since
R
is not a discrete valuation ring and
{\lambda}(Q/\mathfrak{m}Q)=1
, we get
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{C}=\mathfrak{m}Q
, so that
Q
is a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
.
It follows that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(X)=\nu(X)=r(R)-1
.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
is equimultiple. The proof of Proposition
6.9
shows that if
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=r(R)-1
, then
R
is an almost Gorenstein ring
[
25
, Proposition 3.3]
.
In particular, if
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=1
, then by Corollary
6.7
,
R
is not Gorenstein and
r(R)=2
. Therefore
R
is almost Gorenstein (not Gorenstein).

Under a more technical assumption we can generalize Proposition
6.9
to higher dimension. We refer the reader to
[
25
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Numerical semigroup rings

Several interesting results and examples can be found in numerical semigroup rings. We briefly recall the definitions and basic properties that we will use throughout the manuscript. Our focus will be on
3-generated numerical semigroup rings. For reference we use
[
66
, Section 8.7]
.

Given positive integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
, let
H=<a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}>=\{c_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}a_{n}\mid c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
be the numerical semigroup generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. We assume that
\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=1
. Let
k
be a field and let
k[\![t]\!]
be the formal power series ring over
k
. We denote the subring of
k[\![t]\!]

generated by the monomials
t^{a_{i}}
by
k[\![H]\!]
and we call it the semigroup ring of
H
over
k
. The ring
k[\![H]\!]
is a one-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local domain.

There are several integers playing roles in deriving properties of
k[\![H]\!]
.
There is an integer
s
such that
t^{n}\in k[\![H]\!]
for all
n\geq s
. The smallest such
s
is called the
conductor
of
H
or of
k[\![H]\!]
, which we denote by
c
, and
t^{c}k[\![t]\!]
is the largest ideal of
k[\![t]\!]
contained in
k[\![H]\!]
. Then
c-1
is called the Frobenius number of
k[\![H]\!]
and expresses its multiplicity,
c-1=\mbox{\rm deg}(k[\![H]\!])
. For the computation of the canonical module of
k[\![H]\!]
see for instance
[
12
, Section 6]
.

3-generated numerical semigroup rings

Let
k
be a field, let
a,b,c
be positive integers with
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
,
H=<a,b,c>
and
R=k[\![t^{a},t^{b},t^{c}]\!]
.
Assume
R
is not Gorenstein (i.e.,
H
is not symmetric). Then
r(R)=2
(see
[
29
, Section 4]
).
There exists a surjective homomorphism
\Gamma:S=k[\![X,Y,Z]\!]\rightarrow R
given by
\Gamma(X)=t^{a},\Gamma(Y)=t^{b},\Gamma(Z)=t^{c}
.
Let
P=\mbox{\rm ker}(\Gamma)
. Then there exists a matrix

\left(\begin{matrix}X^{a_{1}}&Y^{b_{1}}&Z^{c_{1}}\\
Y^{b_{2}}&Z^{c_{2}}&X^{a_{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right)

such that
P=I_{2}(\varphi)
, the ideal generated by
2\times 2
minors of
\varphi
. That is,

P=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})=(X^{a_{1}+a_{2}}-Y^{b_{2}}Z^{c_{1}},\;Y^{b_{1}+b_{2}}-X^{a_{1}}Z^{c_{2}},\;Z^{c_{1}+c_{2}}-X^{a_{2}}Y^{b_{1}}).

We call this matrix the Herzog matrix of the semigroup
H

[
36
]
. The computation of the Herzog matrix is also summarized in
[
38
, Section 3]
.

We recall a way to compute the canonical ideal in this case. See also
[
62
, Theorem 6.25]
.

Remark 6.11
.

Let
x,y,z
be the images of
X,Y,Z
in
R\simeq S/P
respectively.
Let
L=(f_{1},f_{2})
. Since
f_{1},f_{2}
form a regular sequence, the canonical module of
R
is

\mathcal{C}=\mbox{\rm Ext}^{2}_{S}(R,S)\simeq\mbox{\rm Ext}^{2}_{S}(S/P,S)\simeq\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S/L}(S/P,S/L)\simeq(L:P)/L=(L:f_{3})/L\simeq(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}}).

Similarly,
\mathcal{C}\simeq(y^{b_{1}},z^{c_{2}})\simeq(x^{a_{2}},z^{c_{1}})
.

Goto, Matsuoka and Phuong proved a nice formula for the canonical degree
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 6.12
.

With notation as above we have

(1)

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=a_{1}b_{1}c_{1}
, if
bb_{2}-aa_{1}>0
;

(2)

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=a_{2}b_{2}c_{2}
, if
bb_{2}-aa_{1}<0
.

Corollary 6.13
.

[
29
, Corollary 4.2]
The ring
k[\![t^{a},t^{b},t^{c}]\!]
is an almost Gorenstein ring if and only if the Herzog matrix is either

{\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}X&Y&Z\\
Y^{b_{2}}&Z^{c_{2}}&X^{a_{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right)}
or
{\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}X^{a_{1}}&Y^{b_{1}}&Z^{c_{1}}\\
Y&Z&X\ \end{matrix}\right)}
.

Example 6.14
.

Let
R=k[\![t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}]\!]
. The Herzog matrix is
{\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}X&Y&Z\\
Y&Z&X^{2}\ \end{matrix}\right)}
, so
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=1
.
R
is almost Gorenstein.

Example 6.15
.

Let
R=k[\![t^{5},t^{7},t^{9}]\!]
. The Herzog matrix is
\left(\begin{matrix}X&Y&Z^{2}\\
Y&Z&X^{4}\ \end{matrix}\right)
, so
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=2
.
R
is not almost Gorenstein.

7.
Canonical index and Sally module

In view of Corollary
6.7
and Proposition
6.9
, the next case to consider is
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=r(R)
. It is open in general. Some examples are provided in
[
25
, Example 4.12]
. In the smallest case,
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=r(R)=2
, we obtained nice relationships among several invariants of
R
in
[
25
, Section 4]
. Inspired by
[
25
]
and
[
65
]
, Chau, Goto, Kumashiro and Matsuoka defined the notion of 2-AGL rings as a generalization of AGL rings of dimension one
[
12
]
. The theory of these rings has then been developed by Goto, Isobe, and Taniguchi
[
28
]
. Both
[
25
]
and
[
12
]
use the Sally module, introduced by Vasconcelos in
[
57
]
. In order to discuss these results, we recall the necessary background.

We showed in
[
25
, Proposition 4.1]
that the reduction number of the canonical ideal of
R
is an invariant of the ring. Therefore we have the following definition.

Definition 7.1
.

[
25
, Definition 4.2]

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. The
canonical index
of
R
is the reduction number of the canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
of
R
and is denoted by
\rho(R)
.

Remark 7.2
.

Assume
d=1
and that
R
is not Gorenstein. Let
(a)
be a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
. Notice that we can not have
\rho(R)=1
, otherwise
\mathcal{C}^{2}=a\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}a^{-1}\subset\mbox{\rm Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})=R
so that
\mathcal{C}=(a)
, a contradiction.
Furthermore, if
R
is almost Gorenstein (i.e.,
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=r(R)-1
), then
\rho(R)=2
, by
[
29
, Theorem 3.16]
. We note that the converse is not true, as shown in the following example.

Example 7.3
.

[
25
, Example 4.9]
Let
S=k[\![X,Y,Z]\!]
, let
I=(X^{4}-Y^{2}Z^{2},Y^{4}-X^{2}Z^{2},Z^{4}-X^{2}Y^{2})
and
R=S/I
. Let
x,y,z
be the images of
X,Y,Z
in
R
. Then
\mathcal{C}=(x^{2},z^{2})
is a canonical ideal of
R
with a minimal reduction
(x^{2})
. We have that
\rho(R)=2
,
\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathcal{C})=16
and
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=8
.

We briefly recall the Sally module associated to
the canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
in rings of dimension
1
. Let
Q=(a)

be a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
and consider the exact sequence of finitely generated
R[QT]
-modules

0\rightarrow\mathcal{C}R[QT]\rightarrow\mathcal{C}R[\mathcal{C}T]\rightarrow S_{Q}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow 0.

Then the Sally module
S=S_{Q}(\mathcal{C})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{C}^{n+1}/\mathcal{C}Q^{n}
of
\mathcal{C}
relative to
Q
is Cohen-Macaulay and, by

[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have

(1)

\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathcal{C})=\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)+\sum_{j=1}^{\rho(R)-1}\lambda(\mathcal{C}^{j+1}/a\mathcal{C}^{j})=\sum_{j=0}^{\rho(R)-1}\lambda(\mathcal{C}^{j+1}/a\mathcal{C}^{j}).

The following property of Cohen-Macaulay rings of type
2
is a useful calculation that we use to characterize rings with minimal canonical index
2
.

Proposition 7.4
.

[
25
, Proposition 4.5]

Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Let
(a)
be a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
. If
\nu(\mathcal{C})=2
, then
{\lambda}(\mathcal{C}^{2}/a\mathcal{C})={\lambda}(\mathcal{C}/(a))
.

From Equation (
1
) and Proposition
7.4
we obtain the following (see also Example
7.3
).

Theorem 7.5
.

[
25
, Theorem 4.6]
Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Suppose that the type of
R
is
2
.
Then
\rho(R)=2
if and only if
{\displaystyle\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathcal{C})=2\,\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)}
.

Remark 7.6
.

Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Then the multiplicity of the Sally module
s_{0}(S)=\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathcal{C})-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathcal{C})+\lambda(R/\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathcal{C})-\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
is an invariant of
R
, by
[
29
, Corollary 2.8]
and
[
25
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Remark 7.7
.

Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Suppose that the type of
R
is
2
. Then by Equation (
1
), Proposition
7.4
and Remark
7.6
we have that
s_{0}(S)=\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
, if
\rho(R)=2
, and
s_{0}(S)=\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)+\sum_{j=2}^{\rho(R)-1}\lambda(\mathcal{C}^{j+1}/a\mathcal{C}^{j})
if
\rho(R)\geq 3
.

It is shown in
[
29
, Theorem 3.16]

that
R
is an almost Gorenstein (but not Gorenstein) ring if and only if
s_{0}(S)=1
. Therefore it is natural to generalize as follows.

Definition 7.8
.

[
12
, Definition 1.3]
Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Then
R
is a
2
-almost Gorenstein local ring
(
2
-AGL for short), if
s_{0}(S)=2
; that is,
\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathcal{C})-\lambda(R/\mathcal{C})+2=\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)+2
.

Remark 7.9
.

We recall the notation of
[
12
, Section 2]
, and we refer the reader there for details. Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension one with canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Let
(a)
be a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
. Set
{\displaystyle K=\dfrac{\mathcal{C}}{a}=\left\{\dfrac{x}{a}\;\vline\;x\in\mathcal{C}\right\}}
in the total ring of fractions of
R
.
Then
K/R\simeq\mathcal{C}/(a)
.
Therefore
{\lambda}(K/R)
that appears in
[
12
]
is
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
.

Among many interesting results in
[
12
]
, Proposition 3.8 characterizes 2-AGL rings of type two. They are rings with minimal canonical degree and minimal canonical index.
We give here an alternative short proof using our set-up.

Proposition 7.10
.

[
12
, Proposition 3.8]
Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal. Suppose that the type of
R
is
2
. The following conditions are equivalent
:

(i)

R
is a
2
-AGL ring
;

(ii)

\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=2
and
\rho(R)=2
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)\geq 2
, since
R
is not almost Gorenstein. The conclusion follows from Remark
7.7
.
∎

In
[

the authors studied 2-AGL numerical semigroup rings. In particular, for 3 generated semigroup rings they obtained the following characterization, in the spirit of Corollary

.

[

The ring

.

(2)

Definition 7.13
.

Let
R
be a

Let

8.
bi-canonical degree

The canonical degree is dependent on finding minimal reductions, which in general is a particularly hard task.
We consider in
[
19
]
a degree that seems more amenable to computation.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. The
bi-canonical degree of
R
is the integer
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\lambda(\mathcal{C}^{**}/\mathcal{C})
, where
{\mathcal{C}}^{**}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{C},R),R)
is the bidual of
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 8.2
.

We note that the bi-canonical degree is an invariant of the ring. We have
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=0
if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is reflexive. Therefore, by
[
40
, Corollary 7.29]
in
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings the vanishing of
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
is equivalent to
R
being Gorenstein.

We define
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
in full generality as follows. We recall that
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rule{8.53581pt}{0.56905pt}$\;\!$})
is the multiplicity defined by the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic filtration.

Definition 8.3
.

[
19
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
that has a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Then the
bi-canonical degree
of
R
is the integer

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mathcal{C}^{**}/\mathcal{C})=\sum_{\tiny\mbox{\rm ht}({\mathfrak{p}})=1}\mbox{\rm bideg}(R_{{\mathfrak{p}}})\mbox{\rm deg}(R/{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{\tiny\mbox{\rm ht}({\mathfrak{p}})=1}[\lambda(R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathcal{C}_{{\mathfrak{p}}})-\lambda(R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}/\mathcal{C}^{**}_{{\mathfrak{p}}})]\mbox{\rm deg}(R/{\mathfrak{p}}).

We show in
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
that
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
is a well-defined finite sum independent of the canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 8.4
.

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)\geq 0
and vanishes if and only if
R
is Gorenstein in codimension
1
.

Remark 8.5
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
.
The
residue
of
R
was introduced by Herzog, Hibi, and Stamate in
[
37
, Section 2]
.
A standard metric is
{\rm res}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\mathcal{C}))
, where
\mbox{\rm tr}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{C}\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}=\{f(x):f\in\mathcal{C}^{*},x\in\mathcal{C}\}
is the trace ideal of
\mathcal{C}
.
In dimension
1
, we have
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\lambda(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\mathcal{C}))={\rm res}(R)

[
19
, Proposition 2.3]
.
They may differ in dimension
d>1
.

Remark 8.6
.

In dimension
1
,
R
is Gorenstein if and only if one of
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
or
{\rm res}(R)
vanishes, in which case all three vanish.
If
R
is not Gorenstein,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)\geq 1
,
{\rm res}(R)\geq 1
. The cases when the minimal values are reached have the following designations.

(1)

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
if and only if
R
is a
Goto ring
.

(2)

{\rm res}(R)=1
if and only if
R
is a
nearly Gorenstein ring
.

We now recall very useful formulas for the computation of duals and biduals.
Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the total ring of fractions of a Noetherian local ring
R
. If
I
is an ideal containing a non zero divisor, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I,R)=(R:_{\mathcal{Q}}I)}
. A difficulty is that computer systems such as Macaulay2
[
34
]
are set to calculate quotients of the form
(A:_{R}B)
for ideals
A,B\subset R
, which is done with calculations of syzygies. But it is possible to compute quotients in the total rings of fractions as follows.

Proposition 8.7
.

[
19
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
I,J
be
R
-ideals and let
a\in I
be a regular element.

(1)

\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I,J)=a^{-1}(aJ:_{R}I)
. In particular,
I^{*}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I,R)=a^{-1}((a):_{R}I)
.

(2)

I^{**}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I,R),R)=((a):_{R}((a):_{R}I))
.

When
R
is
1
-dimensional we discuss relationships between almost Gorenstein rings (i.e., rings with minimal value
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=r(R)-1
) and Goto rings.

Theorem 8.8
.

(
[
19
, Theorem 4.2]
,
[
37
, Proposition 6.1]
)
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
1

with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. If
R
is almost Gorenstein
(
not Gorenstein
)
, then
R
is a Goto ring.

Proof.

From the proof of Proposition
6.9

there exists an exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\rightarrow(a)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0

such that
(a)
is a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{C}
and
X\simeq(R/\mathfrak{m})^{r(R)-1}
. By applying
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\cdot,(a))
to the exact sequence above, we obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{C},(a))\rightarrow R\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(X,(a)).

Since
R
is not Gorenstein, we have
\mathcal{C}\neq(a)
. Therefore the image of
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{C},(a))=(a):_{R}\mathcal{C}
in
R
is a proper ideal of
R
. Since
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{C}\subset(a)
it follows that
\mathfrak{m}=(a):_{R}\mathcal{C}
.

By Proposition
8.7
we have that
\mathcal{C}^{**}=((a):_{R}((a):_{R}\mathcal{C}))=(a):_{R}\mathfrak{m}
. Therefore
\mathcal{C}^{**}/(a)
is isomorphic to the socle of
R/(a)
in
R
and we obtain

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\lambda(\mathcal{C}^{**}/(a))-\lambda(\mathcal{C}/(a))=r(R)-(r(R)-1)=1.\qed

Corollary 8.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
1

with a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. If
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=1
, then
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
.

Proof.

Since
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=1
, by Remark
6.10

R
is almost Gorenstein (not Gorenstein). Therefore
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
by Theorem
8.8
.
∎

The example below shows that the
converse of Theorem
8.8
(and of Corollary
8.9
) does not hold true. Therefore, in dimension one, Goto rings are a larger class than almost Gorenstein rings.

Example 8.10
.

Let
R=k[\![t^{5},t^{7},t^{9}]\!]
as in Example
6.15
. We have that
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=2
, and
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
, by Proposition
9.3
, or by a direct calculation (see
[
19
, Example 4.3]
).

9.
Comparison of canonical and bi-canonical degrees

In this section we discuss the Comparison Conjecture, proposed in
[
19
]
.

Conjecture 9.1
.

(Comparison Conjecture)
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring that has a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
. Then

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)\leq\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R).

Remark 9.2
.

The Comparison Conjecture holds in the following cases:

(1)

R
is Gorenstein in codimension one, since
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=0
and
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=0
.

(2)

R
is a Goto ring, since
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
implies
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)\geq 1
, otherwise
R
would be Gorenstein in codimension one.

(3)

R
is an almost Gorenstein ring of dimension one, by Theorem
8.8
.

The Comparison Conjecture for 3-generated numerical semigroup rings

We prove the conjecture for 3-generated numerical semigroup rings. Notation is as in Section
6
. In
[
19
, Proposition 6.1]
we calculated
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
when
a_{1}\leq a_{2}
,
b_{2}\leq b_{1}
, and
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
. We obtained that
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=a_{1}b_{2}c_{1}
, which supported the Comparison Conjecture, since
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
is either
a_{1}b_{1}c_{1}
or
a_{2}b_{2}c_{2}
, by Theorem
6.12
. Here we complete the calculation to obtain
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
in general.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
R=k[\![t^{a},t^{b},t^{c}]\!]
. Let
\varphi=\left(\begin{matrix}X^{a_{1}}&Y^{b_{1}}&Z^{c_{1}}\\
Y^{b_{2}}&Z^{c_{2}}&X^{a_{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right)

be the Herzog matrix such that
R\simeq k[\![X,Y,Z]\!]/I_{2}(\varphi)
.
Then

{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\min\{a_{1},a_{2}\}*\min\{b_{1},b_{2}\}*\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\}}.

Proof.

We note that there are essentially two (possibly overlapping) cases, after a suitable change of variables.

(1)

Two among
\min\{a_{1},a_{2}\},\min\{b_{1},b_{2}\},\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\}
occur as exponents on the same column of the matrix.

(2)

All
\min\{a_{1},a_{2}\},\min\{b_{1},b_{2}\},\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\}
occur as exponents on the same row of the matrix.

Let
x,y,z
be the images of
X,Y,Z
in
R
respectively. Then we have

x^{a_{1}+a_{2}}=y^{b_{2}}z^{c_{1}},\quad y^{b_{1}+b_{2}}=x^{a_{1}}z^{c_{2}},\quad z^{c_{1}+c_{2}}=x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{1}}.

The ideal
{\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}})}
is the canonical ideal of
R
and
{\displaystyle(x^{a_{1}}):\mathcal{C}=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{1}},z^{c_{1}})}
, since

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle y^{b_{1}}y^{b_{2}}=x^{a_{1}}z^{c_{2}}\in(x^{a_{1}}),}&{\displaystyle z^{c_{1}}y^{b_{2}}=x^{a_{1}+a_{2}}\in(x^{a_{1}}).}\end{array}

Then by Proposition
8.7
, we have

\mathcal{C}^{**}=(x^{a_{1}}):((x^{a_{1}}):\mathcal{C})=(x^{a_{1}}):(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{1}},z^{c_{1}}).

For case (1) we may assume that
a_{1}\leq a_{2}
,
b_{2}\leq b_{1}
, and
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\displaystyle z^{c_{2}}y^{b_{1}}=z^{c_{1}}y^{b_{2}}z^{c_{2}-c_{1}}y^{b_{1}-b_{2}}=x^{a_{1}+a_{2}}z^{c_{2}-c_{1}}y^{b_{1}-b_{2}}\in(x^{a_{1}}),}&{\displaystyle z^{c_{2}}z^{c_{1}}=x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{1}}\in(x^{a_{1}}).}\end{array}

It follows that
{\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{**}=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{2}})}
. Moreover, since
z^{c_{1}+c_{2}}\in(x^{a_{1}})
, we have that
\mathcal{C}=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}})=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{1}+c_{2}})
. Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)}&=&{\displaystyle\lambda(R/\mathcal{C})-\lambda(R/\mathcal{C}^{**})}\\
&=&{\displaystyle\lambda(R/(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{1}+c_{2}}))-\lambda(R/(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{2}}))}\\
&=&{\displaystyle a_{1}b_{2}(c_{1}+c_{2})-a_{1}b_{2}c_{2}}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{a_{1}}{b_{2}}{c_{1}}.}\end{array}

For case (2) we may assume that that
a_{1}\leq a_{2}
,
b_{1}\leq b_{2}
, and
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
.
We have that

\mathcal{C}^{**}=(x^{a_{1}}):((x^{a_{1}}):\mathcal{C})=(x^{a_{1}}):(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{1}},z^{c_{1}})=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{2}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}}),

since

\begin{array}[]{l}z^{c_{2}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}}y^{b_{1}}=z^{c_{2}}y^{b_{2}}=z^{c_{2}-c_{1}}y^{b_{2}}z^{c_{1}}=z^{c_{2}-c_{1}}x^{a_{1}+a_{2}}\in(x^{a_{1}}),\\
\\
z^{c_{2}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}}z^{c_{1}}=z^{c_{1}+c_{2}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}}=x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{1}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}}=x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}}\in(x^{a_{1}}).\end{array}

Since
z^{c_{1}+c_{2}}=x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{1}}\in(x^{a_{1}})
, we have
\mathcal{C}=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{1}+c_{2}})
,
\mathcal{C}^{**}=(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{1}+c_{2}},z^{c_{2}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}})
. Because there are
a_{1}b_{1}c_{1}
monomials of the form
x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}
,
0\leq i\leq a_{1}-1
,
b_{2}-b_{1}\leq j\leq b_{2}-1
,
c_{2}\leq i\leq c_{1}+c_{2}-1
, it follows that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)}&=&{\displaystyle\lambda(R/(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{1}+c_{2}}))-\lambda(R/(x^{a_{1}},y^{b_{2}},z^{c_{1}+c_{2}},z^{c_{2}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}})}\\
&=&{\displaystyle a_{1}b_{2}(c_{1}+c_{2})-\big(a_{1}b_{2}(c_{1}+c_{2})-a_{1}b_{1}c_{1}\big)}\\
&=&{\displaystyle{a_{1}}{b_{1}}{c_{1}}.}\end{array}

∎

From Theorem
6.12
and Proposition
9.3
we have the following.

Recent developments on the Comparison Conjecture for numerical semigroup rings

In the recent paper
[
39
]
, Herzog and Kumashiro studied upper bounds on the colength of the trace of the canonical module in numerical semigroup rings, i.e., upper bounds on the bi-canonical degree. Let
R=K[\![H]\!]
. They study when the inequality
{\lambda}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\mathcal{C}))\leq g(H)-n(H)

holds. In their set-up
g(H)=|\mathbb{N}\setminus H|
and
n(H)=|\{h\in H:h<F(H)\}|
denote
the number of gaps and the number of non-gaps, respectively, and
F(H)

denotes the Frobenius number of
H
; that is, the largest integer in
\mathbb{N}\setminus H
. We refer to
[
39
]
for the background. It follows from
[
39
, Lemma 2.2]
and Remark
7.9
that
g(H)-n(H)=\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
. Therefore the above inequality is equivalent to the Comparison Conjecture. We summarize their results. Recall that
r(R)
denotes the type of
R
.

(1)

Suppose that
r(R)\leq 3
. Then the Comparison Conjecture holds
[
39
, Theorem 2.4]
. See also Proposition 3.2 in the earlier paper
[
38
]
for the case of 3-generated semigroups.

(2)

There is a counterexample to the Comparison Conjecture when
r(R)=5

[
39
, Example 2.5]
. Let
H=<13,14,15,16,17,18,21,23>
.
Then
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=9>\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=8
. The authors note that this example arises from far-flung Gorenstein rings, studied in
[
39
, Section 4]
. It is shown in
[
38
, Corollary 2.2]
that the inequality
{\lambda}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\mathcal{C}))\leq n(H)
always holds.
R
is far-flung Gorenstein if and only if
{\lambda}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\mathcal{C}))=n(H)
. Therefore, far-flung Gorenstein rings are good candidates for counterexamples, since the bi-canonical degree is as big as possible.

(3)

The case
r(R)=4
is open
[
39
, Question 2.6]
. The examples we tried so far support the conjecture.

We conclude this section with the following question.

Question 9.6
.

Can we find other classes of rings for which the Comparison Conjecture holds? What about 2-AGL rings?

Augmented rings

Let

1

\mathcal{C}

Consider the augmented ring

That is,

From the results in

Theorem 10.1
.

(

[

)

(1)

Suppose that

(2)

is an almost Gorenstein ring if and only if

is an almost Gorenstein ring.

(3)

Suppose that

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R\ltimes\mathfrak{m})=2\,\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)-1.

In particular if

.

11.
Generalization of bi-canonical degrees

In our last paper with Vasconcelos
[
9
]
we defined the bi-canonical degree for rings where the canonical module is not necessarily an ideal. We also discussed generalizations to rings without canonical modules but admitting modules sharing some of their properties. In this section we summarize the main results and open questions.

bi-canonical degree of rings with a canonical module

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
that has a canonical module
\omega
. For an
R
-module
E
, we denote the dual of
E
by
{\displaystyle E^{*}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(E,R)}
.
By dualizing the finite free presentation of
\omega

F_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{0}\rightarrow\omega\rightarrow 0,

we obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\omega^{*}\rightarrow F_{0}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{1}^{*}\rightarrow D(\omega)\rightarrow 0,

where
{\displaystyle D(\omega)=\mbox{\rm coker}(\varphi^{*})}
is the Auslander dual of
\omega
.
The module
D(\omega)
depends on the chosen presentation but the values of
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(D(\omega),R)}
, for
i\geq 1
, are independent of the presentation. By
[
3
, Proposition 2.6]
, there exists an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R)\longrightarrow\omega\longrightarrow\omega^{**}\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R)\longrightarrow 0.

Definition 11.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. The
bi-canonical degree
of
R
is

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R))+\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R)),

where
{\displaystyle D(\omega)}
is the Auslander dual of
\omega
and
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rule{8.53581pt}{0.56905pt}$\;\!$})
is the multiplicity associated with the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic filtration.

An
R
-module
E
is said to be
torsionless
if the natural homomorphism
{\displaystyle\sigma:E\rightarrow E^{**}}
is injective.
We show that if
R
has a canonical ideal, then Definition
8.3
and Definition
11.1
coincide.

Proposition 11.2
.

[
9
, Proposition 3.2]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. Then
\omega
is torsionless if and only if
\omega
is isomorphic to an ideal of
R
.

Notice that by Definition
11.1
,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=0
if and only if
\omega
is reflexive. By
[
40
, Korollar 7.29]
, this is equivalent to
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is Gorenstein for all primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
with height
1
. Therefore we can generalize Remark
8.4
.

Proposition 11.3
.

[
9
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. Then
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=0
if and only if
R
is Gorenstein in codimension
1
.

In accordance with Theorem
8.8
, we ask when the minimal value of bideg is attained.

Question 11.4
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
1
with a canonical module
\omega
. When is
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
?

Remark 11.5
.

By Definition
11.1
,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R)=0
and
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R))=1
;

(2)

\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R)=0
and
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R))=1
.

By Proposition
11.2
we have
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R)=0
if and only if
\omega
is isomorphic to an ideal
\mathcal{C}
of
R
. Therefore case (1) is equivalent to
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R))={\lambda}(\mathcal{C}^{**}/\mathcal{C})=1
, i.e.,
R
is a Goto ring. It would be interesting to find an explicit example of case (2).

The following proposition, which follows from classical results in
[
11
]
, is helpful in computing examples with Macaulay2, since it provides an explicit presentation of the Auslander dual as well as a criterion for the canonical module to be an ideal in terms of a matrix.

Proposition 11.6
.

[
9
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
(S,\mathfrak{n})
be a regular local ring. Let
I
be an
S
-ideal of height
g
such that
R=S/I
is Cohen-Macaulay. Let

0\rightarrow G_{g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}G_{g-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow G_{1}\rightarrow G_{0}=S\rightarrow 0

be a minimal free
S
-resolution of
R
. Then:

(1)

{\displaystyle\omega_{R}\simeq\mbox{\rm coker}(\sigma^{T}\otimes R)}
.

(2)

{\displaystyle D(\omega_{R})\simeq\mbox{\rm coker}(\sigma\otimes R)}
.

(3)

Let
{\displaystyle\varphi=\sigma^{T}\otimes R}
and
{\displaystyle G_{g}=S^{p}}
. Then
\omega_{R}
is an ideal if and only if
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ht}(I_{p-1}(\varphi))\geq 1}
and
I_{p}(\varphi)=0
.

Using Proposition
11.6
we compute
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
in a ring when the canonical module is not an ideal.

Example 11.7
.

[
9
, Example 3.5]

Let
S
be a regular local ring with the maximal ideal
(X,Y,Z)
. Let
R=S/I
be given by the minimal resolution

0\longrightarrow S^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{3}\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow 0,\;\mbox{where}\;\sigma=\left(\begin{matrix}X^{2}&Y^{2}\\
YZ&ZX\\
XZ&XY\end{matrix}\right).

Let
x,y,z
be the images of
X,Y,Z
in
R=S/I
respectively. We obtain the free presentation of the canonical module
\omega_{R}
of
R
:

R^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{2}\rightarrow\omega_{R}\rightarrow 0,

where
{\displaystyle\varphi=\sigma^{T}\otimes R=\left(\begin{matrix}x^{2}&yz&xz\\
y^{2}&zx&xy\end{matrix}\right)}
. Since
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ht}(I_{1}(\varphi))=0}
, the canonical module
\omega_{R}
is not an ideal. Moreover, we have

0\rightarrow\omega_{R}^{*}\rightarrow R^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{3}\rightarrow D(\omega_{R})\rightarrow 0,

where
{\displaystyle\varphi^{*}=\sigma\otimes R=\left(\begin{matrix}x^{2}&y^{2}\\
yz&zx\\
xz&xy\end{matrix}\right)}
. By using Macaulay2, we compute the bi-canonical degree:

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega_{R}),R))+\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega_{R}),R))=1+6=7.

Precanonical ideals

We consider a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
R
that does not necessarily have a canonical module. We look for rings that have ideals with properties similar to those of a canonical ideal. Ideals with similar set of conditions have appeared in the literature, notably among them closed ideals, studied by Brennan and Vasconcelos in
[
10
]
, and spherical modules, introduced by Vasconcelos in
[
55
]
. We recall the necessary background.

An ideal
I
is said to be
closed
if
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I,I)=R
. An
R
-module
E
is said to be
spherical
if
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(E,E)=R}
and
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(E,E)=0}
for all
i\geq 1

[
55
, Section 4.3]
. Spherical modules are also called
semidualizing
and have been studied extensively by Sather-Wagstaff. We refer to
[
50
]
for a survey.

We note that the canonical module is semidualizing.
If
R
is not Gorenstein, principal ideals generated by a non-zero divisor are semidualizing. Examples of rings with non trivial semidualizing ideals are constructed in
[
51
]
.

We note an early result of Vasconcelos in this context.

Proposition 11.8
.

[
55
, Proposition 4.9]
. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an Artinian local ring with
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=0
. Let
E
denote the the injective hull of
R/\mathfrak{m}
. Let
P
be a finitely generated
R
-module that satisfies
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(P,P)=R}
and
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{R}(P,P)=0}
. Then
P\simeq R
, or
P\simeq E
.

In the set-up of the above proposition, an example of a module
P
that is neither
R
nor
E
is in
[
55
, Section 4.3]
. We now define a class of ideals in this setting.

Definition 11.9
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. An
R
-ideal
\mathcal{P}
is called a
precanonical
ideal if
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P})=R
and
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P})=0
.

In
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
we prove conditions under which a precanonical ideal is canonical. It would be interesting to find ideals that are precanonical, but not canonical. Next, we propose the following definition.

Definition 11.10
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a precanonical ideal
\mathcal{P}
. The
bi-canonical degree
of
R

relative to

\mathcal{P}
is

\mbox{\rm bideg}_{\mathcal{P}}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mathcal{P}^{**}/\mathcal{P}).

Question 11.11
.

[
9
, Question 4.3]

Which are the properties we can obtain from the bi-canonical degree of
R
relative to
\mathcal{P}
to understand the structures of the ideal and the ring?

Recall that if
\mathcal{C}
is a canonical ideal of a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, then
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\lambda(\mathcal{C}^{**}/\mathcal{C})=0
(i.e.,
\mathcal{C}
is reflexive) if and only if
R
is Gorenstein (i.e.,
\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic to
R
). In
[
9
, Proposition 4.5]
we generalize as follows.

Proposition 11.12
.

Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an ideal that contains a non-zero-divisor. If
I
is closed and reflexive, then
I
is a principal ideal.

Corollary 11.13
.

Let
R
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a precanonical ideal
\mathcal{P}
that contains a non-zero-divisor. Then
\mbox{\rm bideg}_{\mathcal{P}}(R)=0
if and only if
\mathcal{P}
is a principal ideal.

Proof.

If
\mbox{\rm bideg}_{\mathcal{P}}(R)=0
, then
\mathcal{P}
is reflexive and so it is principal by Proposition
11.12
. If
\mathcal{P}
is a principal ideal the conclusion follows by Proposition
8.7
.
∎

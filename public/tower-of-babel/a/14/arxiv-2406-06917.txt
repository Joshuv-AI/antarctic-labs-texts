Monadic ortholattices: completions and duality
Joseph McDonald, Miguel Peinado

Abstract
. We show that the variety of monadic ortholattices is closed under MacNeille and canonical completions. In each case, the completion of L L is obtained by forming an associated dual space X X that is a monadic orthoframe. This is a set with an orthogonality relation and an additional binary relation satisfying certain conditions. For the MacNeille completion, X X is formed from the non-zero elements of L L , and for the canonical completion, X X is formed from the proper filters of L L . The corresponding completion of L L is then obtained as the ortholattice of bi-orthogonally closed subsets of X X with an additional operation defined through the binary relation of X X . With the introduction of a suitable topology on an orthoframe, as was done by Goldblatt and Bimbó, we obtain a dual adjunction between the categories of monadic ortholattices and monadic orthospaces. A restriction of this dual adjunction provides a dual equivalence.

Introduction

Monadic algebras were introduced by Halmos
[
7
]
as an algebraic realization of the one-variable fragment of first-order logic. A monadic algebra is a Boolean algebra with an additional unary operation
\exists

[
7
]
had a family of interrelated quantifiers and played the same role for full first-order logic. At about the same time, Henkin, Monk, and Tarski
[
9

10

introduced the closely related cylindric algebras as algebraic models of first-order logic. These too had a family of quantifiers, related in a somewhat different way than in polyadic algebras. They showed that each monadic algebra and each cylindric algebra can be embedded into a complete, atomic one. These were among the results that grew into the theory of canonical extensions of Boolean algebras with operators
[
11

12

The approach was to show that the quantifier of a monadic algebra yields an equivalence relation on its set of ultrafilters, and the powerset of this relational structure is then a complete atomic monadic algebra extending the original. Including the Stone topology into this process yields a duality between monadic algebras and Stone spaces equipped with a compatible equivalence relation.

\exists[x]=\exists[y]
and
y\subseteq z
.

Lemma 3.7
.

R={\uparrow}S
.

Proof.

Given

, let
\widehat{x}
be the filter generated by
\exists[x]
and note that since

is down-directed,
\widehat{x}
is the upset generated by
\exists[x]
. Then
x\,R\,z
iff
\exists[x]\subseteq z
iff
\widehat{x}\subseteq z
. Since
\exists[x]=\exists[\widehat{x}]
it follows that
x\,R\,z
implies
x\,{\uparrow}S\,z
. Conversely, if
x\,{\uparrow}S\,z
, there is
y
with
\exists[x]=\exists[y]
and
y\subseteq z
. Since
\exists[x]\subseteq y
, then
\widehat{x}\subseteq y
, hence
\widehat{x}\subseteq z
and so

.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
L
be a monadic
ol
. Then
Y=(\mathcal{F}(L),\perp,S)
is a monadic
of
,
S
is an equivalence relation, and

is the canonical extension of

.

Proof.

Since
x\leq y
and
x\perp z
implies
y\perp z
, for any

we have
A^{\perp}=({\uparrow}A)^{\perp}
. Thus
S[\{x\}]^{\perp}=({\uparrow}S[\{x\}])^{\perp}=R[\{x\}]^{\perp}
. Then, since

for any subset

, we have

S[S[\{x\}]^{\perp}]\subseteq R[R[\{x\}]^{\perp}]\subseteq R[\{x\}]^{\perp}=S[\{x\}]^{\perp}.

Here, the second containment uses the fact that

is a monadic
of
. This inequality shows that

is a monadic
of
, and as noted,

is an equivalence relation. Since the mapping

does not depend on the choice of

or

, it is an

embedding into
\mathcal{B}(Y)
that provides a canonical extension of

when considered as a

. To show that it is a canonical extension of

as a monadic

, we show that the quantifiers

and

on

are equal. But

\exists_{R}A=R[A]^{\perp\perp}=({\uparrow}S[A])^{\perp\perp}=S[A]^{\perp\perp}=\exists_{S}A.

This completes the proof.
∎

Our derivation could have been done throughout starting with the relation
S
, but it would have been a bit more complicated.

4.
Orthospaces

Bimbó
[
1
]
placed Goldblatt’s work
[
6
]
on the Stone space of a

in a categorical setting in an attempt to create a duality between the category

of

The focus of this section is to show that what is obtained in
[

]
is a dual adjunction that gives rise to a dual equivalence when the definition of orthospaces is appropriately extended.

Definition 4.1
.

An orthospace (abbrev.:

)
(X,\perp,\leq,\tau)
consists of an
of
with a partial ordering
\leq
and a compact topology
\tau
that satisfies

(1)

if
x\nleq y
then there is
U\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
,

(2)

if
x\perp z
and
x\leq y
, then
y\perp z
,

(3)

if
U\in\mathcal{C}(X)
, then
U^{\perp}\in\mathcal{C}(X)
,

(4)

if
x\perp y
, then there is
U\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
x\in U
and
y\in U^{\perp}
.

Here
\mathcal{C}(X)
is the set of clopen, bi-orthogonally closed subsets of

.

Condition (1) guarantees that every
os

X
is totally-order disconnected and therefore totally disconnected. Since
X
is compact by definition,

is a Stone space.

Lemma 4.2
.

In any
os
we have
x\leq y
iff
y\in\{x\}^{\perp\perp}
.

Proof.

If
x\leq y
then
y\in\{x\}^{\perp\perp}
since (2) implies that each bi-orthogonally closed set is an upset and we always have
x\in\{x\}^{\perp\perp}
. Conversely, if
x\not\leq y
, then by (1) there is
U\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
. Since
U
is bi-orthogonally closed and
x\in U
, we have
\{x\}^{\perp\perp}\subseteq U
, hence
y\not\in\{x\}^{\perp\perp}
.
∎

Using this lemma, one can formulate an equivalent definition of an
os
that does not involve an ordering. Call
(X,\perp,\tau)
an
os
′
if
(X,\perp)
is an
of
with a compact topology
\tau
that satisfies (3) and (4) and additionally has
\mathcal{C}(X)
separate points, that is, it satisfies the following: (1

) if

U\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
U\cap\{x,y\}
containing one element. Clearly if
(X,\perp,\leq,\tau)
is an

, then
(X,\perp,\tau)
is an
os
′
since (1) implies (1
′
).

Proposition 4.3
.

If
(X,\perp,\tau)
is an
os
′
, then setting
x\leq y
iff

, we have that
(X,\perp,\leq,\tau)
is an

Proof.

It is simple to see that

is reflexive and transitive. If
x\neq y
, then by (1
′
)
there is

that separates them, say
x\in U
and
y\not\in U
. It follows that

, hence

. So
\leq
is anti-symmetric, hence a partial order. If
x\nleq y
, then
y\not\in\{x\}^{\perp\perp}
. So there is

with
x\perp z
and

with
x\in U
and

. Suppose

and

∎

If we begin with an

′

(X,\perp,\tau)
, then form an

(X,\perp,\leq,\tau)
as above, then build from it an

to form a partial ordering. By Lemma

are equivalent. We follow Bimbó’s terminology to make it easy to match with her paper, although the notion of an

seems simpler.

Definition 4.4

Let

Let

For an

Proposition 4.5

These assignments on objects extend to contravariant functors

Example 4.6

For

An

Theorem 4.8

The functors
\mathcal{F}\dashv\mathcal{C}
provide an adjunction between

and
{\sf OS}^{op}

Proof.

For

and

(\,\cdot\,)^{-}
and
(\,\cdot\,)^{+}

(\,\cdot\,)^{-}
(\,\cdot\,)^{+}

by setting for

and
\phi:X\to\mathcal{F}(L)

\displaystyle f^{-}(x)

\displaystyle=\{a:x\in f(a)\},

\displaystyle\phi^{+}(a)

\displaystyle=\{x:a\in\phi(x)\}.

Note that

is the composite of

and

Note also that

Thus, the maps

and

between hom-sets are well-defined. Also, we have

\displaystyle f^{-+}(a)

\displaystyle=\{x:a\in f^{-}(x)\}=\{x:x\in f(a)\}=f(a),

\displaystyle\phi^{+-}(x)

\displaystyle=\{a:x\in\phi^{+}(a)\}=\{a:a\in\phi(x)\}=\phi(x).

Thus, for each

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}(\psi)\circ\alpha^{+})(a)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}(\psi)(\{x:a\in\alpha(x)\})

\displaystyle=\{x^{\prime}:\psi(x^{\prime})\in\{x:a\in\alpha(x)\}\}

\displaystyle=\{x^{\prime}:a\in\alpha\psi(x^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle=(\alpha\circ\psi)^{+}(a),

and for the latter, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha^{+}\circ f)(b)

\displaystyle=\{x:f(b)\in\alpha(x)\}

\displaystyle=\{x:b\in(\mathcal{F}(f)\circ\alpha)(x)\}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{F}(f)\circ\alpha)^{+}(b).

5.
Monadic orthospaces

In this section, we extend the results in the previous section to the setting of monadic

Definition 5.1
.

A tuple
(X;\perp,\leq,R,\tau)
is a monadic orthospace if
(X,\perp,\leq,\tau)
is an

,
(X,\perp,R)
is a monadic

, and for each

we have

.

For a monadic

, its bi-orthogonally closed sets

form a monadic

under the quantifier
\exists A=R[A]^{\perp\perp}
. It is clear from the definition of a monadic

that its clopen bi-orthogonally closed sets

form a subalgebra of

, hence form a monadic

.

Definition 5.2
.

Let

be a monadic

, and equip its Goldblatt

(\mathcal{F}(L),\perp,\leq,\tau)
with the relation

iff

of its Goldblatt

. Call this the monadic Goldblatt

and denote it

.

For a monadic

, we have that
\mathcal{F}(L)
is indeed a monadic

. It is clearly an

and also a monadic

. It remains only to show that if

is a clopen and bi-orthogonally closed set of
\mathcal{F}(L)
, then so is

. By Goldblatt’s result,

for some

. In the proof of Proposition

, we saw that

, so

.

Proposition 5.3
.

For

, the map

We know that

Definition 5.4

Proposition 5.5
.

For

To aid readability, we write

Note that by Goldblatt’s result, there is some

Observe that

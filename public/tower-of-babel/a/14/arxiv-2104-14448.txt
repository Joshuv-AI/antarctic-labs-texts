On smoothing of plurisubharmonic functions on unbounded domains
T. Harz

Abstract
. We prove that for every n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , there exists a pseudoconvex domain Ω ⊂ ℂ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n} such that 𝔠 0 ​ ( Ω ) ⊊ 𝔠 1 ​ ( Ω ) \mathfrak{c}^{0}(\Omega)\subsetneq\mathfrak{c}^{1}(\Omega) , where 𝔠 k ​ ( Ω ) \mathfrak{c}^{k}(\Omega) denotes the core of Ω \Omega with respect to 𝒞 k \mathcal{C}^{k} -smooth plurisubharmonic functions on Ω \Omega . Moreover, we show that there exists a bounded continuous plurisubharmonic function on Ω \Omega that is not the pointwise limit of a sequence of 𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}^{1} -smooth bounded plurisubharmonic functions on Ω \Omega .

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a domain. If
\Omega
is bounded, then there always exists a strictly plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
on
\Omega
that is bounded from above; for example,
\varphi(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=|\cdot|^{2}
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, is a function as desired. However, if
\Omega
is unbounded, then in general there does not exist a plurisubharmonic function on
\Omega
that is bounded from above and strictly plurisubharmonic on the whole of
\Omega
. The subset of
\Omega
where all bounded from above plurisubharmonic functions fail to be strictly plurisubharmonic is called the core of
\Omega
, and will be denoted by
\mathfrak{c}(\Omega)
.

Core sets have been introduced and studied in the series of articles
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
. Further results related to cores of complex manifolds were obtained, for example, in
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
. To be more precise, core sets have been studied with respect to different subclasses of (bounded from above) plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
: In
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, the authors consider cores with respect to the class of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth plurisubharmonic functions. In
[
5
]
, also core sets related to
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-smooth plurisubharmonic functions,
k\geq 0
, are investigated. The papers
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
deal with cores related to continuous plurisubharmonic functions, and in
[
2
]
also a core set with respect to a class of plurisubharmonic functions with weak singularities is introduced.

The goal of this paper is to start an investigation on how these different notions of core sets are related. More precisely, we are interested in the question if core sets with respect to certain subclasses of (bounded from above) plurisubharmonic functions do always coincide, or can be different from one another in general. We will show, in particular, that the cores with respect to continuous and with respect to
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth plurisubharmonic functions are not always the same, even on pseudoconvex domains.

Before stating the results of this paper in detail, we need to introduce some notations: We write
\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)
to denote the set of plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
. Moreover,
\mathcal{PSH}^{\ast}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
, where
L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
denotes the set of locally bounded functions on
\Omega
, and
\mathcal{PSH}^{k}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{k}(\Omega)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\cup{\infty}
. A function
\varphi\colon\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty)
,
\varphi\not\equiv-\infty
, is called strictly plurisubharmonic if for every
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth function
\theta\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
the function
\varphi+\varepsilon\theta
is plurisubharmonic on
\Omega
(note that in the case that
\varphi
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth, this definition coincides with the usual definition of strict plurisubharmonicity by means of positive definiteness of the complex Hessian of
\varphi
). We say that
\varphi
is strictly plurisubharmonic near a point
z\in\Omega
, if
\varphi
is strictly plurisubharmonic on some open neighborhood
U\subset\Omega
of
z
.

The set
\mathfrak{c}(\Omega)
related to the function space
\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)
is then defined by

\mathfrak{c}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{z\in\Omega:\begin{minipage}[210.45021pt]\centering every function $\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)$ that is bounded from \\
above fails to be strictly plurisubharmonic near $z$ \@add@centering\end{minipage}\right\}

In the same way, we introduce the sets
\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)
and
\mathfrak{c}^{k}(\Omega)
related to the function spaces
\mathcal{PSH}^{\ast}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{PSH}^{k}(\Omega)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\infty}
, respectively.
It follows immediately from the definition that we have a chain of inclusions as follows,

\mathfrak{c}(\Omega)\subset\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)\subset\mathfrak{c}^{0}(\Omega)\subset\mathfrak{c}^{1}(\Omega)\subset\cdots.

We will show that each of the first three inclusions above can be strict in general. In fact, to see that in some cases
\mathfrak{c}(\Omega)\neq\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)
is easy, and it can be observed, e.g., in the following example.

Example 1
.

For generic
C\in\mathbb{R}
, the set

\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\big\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\log\lvert w\rvert+\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2}<C\big\}

is a strictly pseudoconvex domain with smooth boundary in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Clearly,
\mathfrak{c}(\Omega)=\varnothing
, since
\varphi(z,w)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\log\lvert w\rvert+(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})-C
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
\Omega
. On the other hand, observe that
\Omega
contains the complex line
L\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{C}\times\{0\}
. Hence if
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}^{\ast}(\Omega)
is bounded from above, then, by Liouville’s theorem, there exists a constant
M\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi\equiv M
on
L
. In particular,
\varphi
is not strictly plurisubharmonic at any point in
L
. Thus
L\subset\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)
. In particular,
\mathfrak{c}(\Omega)\neq\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)
.

To see that in general
\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)\neq\mathfrak{c}^{0}(\Omega)
and
\mathfrak{c}^{0}(\Omega)\neq\mathfrak{c}^{1}(\Omega)
, even if
\Omega
is pseudoconvex, is more difficult. This is shown in the following two results of this article.

Theorem 1
.

For every
n\geq 2
, there exist a pseudoconvex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a nonempty open set
\omega\subset\Omega
such that the following assertions hold true:

(1)

There exists a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi<0
on
\Omega
and
\varphi
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
\omega
.

(2)

Every function
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi<0
on
\Omega
is constant on
\omega
.

In particular,
\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)\neq\mathfrak{c}^{0}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 2
.

For every
n\geq 2
, there exist a pseudoconvex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a nonempty open set
\omega\subset\Omega
such that the following assertions hold true:

(1)

There exists a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi<0
on
\Omega
and
\varphi
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
\omega
.

(2)

Every function
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi<0
on
\Omega
is constant on
\omega
.

In particular,
\mathfrak{c}^{0}(\Omega)\neq\mathfrak{c}^{1}(\Omega)
.

The proofs of Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are given in sections 2 and 3, respectively. In section 4 we note a consequence with respect to non-approximability of bounded plurisubharmonic functions on unbounded domains.

Proof of

In what follows,
n\geq 2
is fixed. If
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
and
r>0
, then
\mathbb{D}(z_{0},r)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\lvert z-z_{0}\rvert<r\}
and
\mathbb{B}(w_{0},r)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}:\lvert w-w_{0}\rvert<r\}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{D}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{D}(0,1)
and
\mathbb{B}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{B}(0,1)
.

Let
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a dense subset of
[0,2\pi]
and let
a_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(1+\frac{1}{j})e^{i\theta_{j}}
. Define

\sigma(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{j}\log\lvert z-a_{j}\rvert,

where
(\delta_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of constants
\delta_{j}>0
converging to zero so fast that

(1)

\sigma\colon\mathbb{C}\to[-\infty,\infty)
is subharmonic,

(2)

\lvert\sigma\rvert<1
on
\bar{\mathbb{D}}
.

Further, fix a point
w_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
such that
\lvert w_{0}\rvert=2
, and let

\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\big\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}:\sigma(z)+\log\lvert z\rvert+\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\log\lvert w-w_{0}\rvert+\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2}<4\big\}.

Observe that, after possibly passing to a suitable connected component,
\Omega
is a pseudoconvex domain such that

(3)

(A\cup\{0\})\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\subset\Omega
, where
A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
,

(4)

\mathbb{C}\times\{w_{0}\}\subset\Omega
,

(5)

\bar{\mathbb{D}}\times\bar{\mathbb{B}}\subset\Omega
;

in order to see (5), note that
\log\lvert w-w_{0}\rvert<\log 3<2
if
\lvert w\rvert<1
. Define
\tilde{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to[-\infty,\infty)
as

\tilde{\varphi}(z,w)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sigma(z)+\log\lvert z\rvert+\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\log\lvert w-w_{0}\rvert+\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert w\rvert^{2}.

Then observe that

(6)

\tilde{\varphi}
is strictly plurisubharmonic,

(7)

\tilde{\varphi}>-2
on
\{\frac{1}{2}<\lvert z\rvert<1\}\times\mathbb{B}
, and

(8)

\tilde{\varphi}<-2
on
\Omega\cap\{\lvert w\rvert>4\}
;

in order to see (8), note that
\tilde{\varphi}<4-\frac{1}{2}\lvert w\rvert^{2}
on
\Omega
. Thus
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max(\tilde{\varphi},-2)
is a bounded plurisubharmonic function on
\Omega
such that, according to (6) and (7),
\varphi
is strictly plurisubharmonic on

\omega\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}<\lvert z\rvert<1\}\times\mathbb{B}\subset\Omega.

In particular,
\omega\cap\mathfrak{c}^{\ast}(\Omega)=\varnothing
.

On the other hand, let now
\psi\colon\Omega\to(-\infty,0)
be a continuous plurisubharmonic function. Then, by (3) and (4),
\psi\equiv C
on
(A\cup\{0\})\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
for some constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, by (5), by continuity of
\psi
, and since
b\mathbb{D}\subset\bar{A}
, we conclude that
\psi\equiv C
on
(b\mathbb{D}\cup\{0\})\times\mathbb{B}
. An application of the maximum principle to the functions
\psi(\,\cdot\,,w)|_{\mathbb{D}}
,
w\in\mathbb{B}
, thus implies that
\psi\equiv C
on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{B}
. In particular,
\psi
fails to be strictly plurisubharmonic on
\omega\subset\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{B}
, and hence
\omega\subset\mathfrak{c}^{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof of

The proof of
2
relies on the following simple lemma in dimension 1. Here, for
U\subset\mathbb{C}
open we write
\mathcal{SH}(U)
for the set of subharmonic functions on

.

Lemma 1
.

Fix a dense subset
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset[0,2\pi]
and set
a_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(1+\frac{1}{j})e^{i\theta_{j}}
. Then the following assertions hold true:

(1)

There exists a function
u\in\mathcal{SH}(\mathbb{C})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{C})
such that
u(a_{j})=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
u(z)=\lvert z\rvert^{2}
on
\bar{\mathbb{D}}
.

(2)

For every function
v\in\mathcal{SH}(U)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(U)
, where
U\subset\mathbb{C}
is an open neighborhood of
\bar{\mathbb{D}}
, the following statement is true: if
v(a_{j})=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{j}\in U
, then
v\equiv 1
on
\bar{\mathbb{D}}
.

Proof.

(1) Fix a sequence
(r_{j})
of constants
r_{j}\in(0,1)
such that
\bar{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},r_{j})\cap\bar{\mathbb{D}}=\emptyset
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\bar{\mathbb{D}}(a_{j},r_{j})\cap\bar{\mathbb{D}}(a_{k},r_{k})=\emptyset
for every
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
j\neq k
. Further, choose a smooth function
\chi\colon\mathbb{C}\to[0,\infty)
such that
\SUPP\chi\subset\mathbb{D}
and
\chi\equiv 1
near
0
. Finally, let
(\varepsilon_{j})
be a sequence of constants
\varepsilon_{j}>0
such that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
the function
\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\varepsilon_{j}\chi(\lvert z-a_{j}\rvert/r_{j})\log\lvert z-a_{j}\rvert
is strictly subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}(a_{j},r_{j})
.

Define functions
u_{m}\in\mathcal{SH}(\mathbb{C})\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{C})
by

u_{m}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c@{,\quad}l}\max\Big(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\varepsilon_{j}\chi\Big(\textstyle\frac{\lvert z-a_{j}\rvert}{r_{j}}\Big)\log\lvert z-a_{j}\rvert,\,1\Big)&\text{if }z\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{D}(a_{j},r_{j})\\
\lvert z\rvert^{2}&\text{if }z\notin\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\mathbb{D}(a_{j},r_{j})\end{array}\right..

Note that the sequence
(u_{m})
is monotonically decreasing. Thus
u\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}u_{m}
is a well-defined subharmonic function on
\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, since
\lvert z\rvert^{2}\geq u_{m}(z)\geq 1
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{D}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, it is easy to observe that
u
is continuous. By construction,
u(a_{j})=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2) If
v(a_{j})=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{j}\in U
, then, by the choice of
(a_{j})
and continuity of
v
, it follows that
v\equiv 1
on
b\mathbb{D}
. Since
v
is subharmonic, we conclude that
v\leq 1
on
\mathbb{D}
.

Assume, in order to get a contradiction, that there exists a point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
v(z_{0})<1
. The set
M\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:v(z)=1\}
is closed by continuity of
v
, and it is open, since
v
is subharmonic. Thus
M=\emptyset
, since
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus M
, and hence
v<1
on
\mathbb{D}
.
By the classical Hopf Lemma, see
[
6
]
, it then follows that

v(z)\leq 1-c\DIST(z,b\mathbb{D})\quad\mathrm{for}\ z\in\bar{\mathbb{D}}

for some constant
c>0
. Since
v
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth, this implies that

v(z)>1\quad\mathrm{for}\ z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{\mathbb{D}}\mathrm{\ close\ enough\ to}\ b\mathbb{D}.

This contradicts the fact that
\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{\mathbb{D}}
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\lvert a_{j}\rvert=1
and
v(a_{j})=1
whenever
a_{j}\in U
. Thus
v\equiv 1
on
\bar{\mathbb{D}}
.
∎

In what follows, let
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be the continuous subharmonic function constructed in the proof of
1
. Observe that not only
u(a_{j})=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, but in fact for every

there exists a disc
D_{j}\subset\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\bar{\mathbb{D}}
centered at
a_{j}
such that
u\equiv 1
on
D_{j}
. Define

\sigma(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{j}\log\lvert z-a_{j}\rvert,

where
(\delta_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of constants
\delta_{j}>0
converging to zero so fast that

(1)

\sigma\colon\mathbb{C}\to[-\infty,\infty)
is subharmonic,

(2)

\sigma<\frac{1}{4}
on
\bar{\mathbb{D}}
,

(3)

\sigma\geq-1
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}D_{j}
.

Further, fix a point
w_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
such that
\lvert w_{0}\rvert=4
, and let

\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}:\sigma(z)+\log\lvert w-w_{0}\rvert+\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2}<3\}.

Observe that, after possibly passing to a suitable connected component,
\Omega
is a pseudoconvex domain containing the set

E\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\big(\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\big)\cup(\mathbb{C}\times\{w_{0}\})

(4)

\Omega\cap\{\lvert w\rvert\leq 3\}

(5)

\Omega\cap\{\lvert w\rvert\leq 1\}\supset\bar{\mathbb{D}}\times\bar{\mathbb{B}}
,

(6)

\Omega\cap\{2\leq\lvert w\rvert\leq 3\}\subset\big(\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}D_{j}\big)\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
.

In order to see (4), note that
\sigma(z)>0
for
\lvert z\rvert>\frac{5}{2}
, and
\log\lvert w-w_{0}\rvert\geq 0
for
\lvert w\rvert\leq 3
. For (5), observe that
\sigma<\frac{1}{4}

\log\lvert w-w_{0}\rvert+\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2}\geq 4

Let now
\psi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega)

\psi

\Omega
.

\psi\equiv c

. Since

\psi\equiv c
on
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{B}
.

It remains to show that there exists a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{PSH}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega)

\varphi
is bounded from above on
\Omega
and
\varphi
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
\omega
. To that end, define
\tilde{\varphi}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
by

\tilde{\varphi}(z,w)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c@{,\quad}l}u(z)&\lvert w\rvert<\frac{5}{2}\\
1&\lvert w\rvert\geq\frac{5}{2}\end{array}\right..

By the choice of the discs

in the definition of the function
u
above, and due to property (6), we see that the function
\tilde{\varphi}
is continuous and plurisubharmonic on
\Omega
. Morevoer, by (4), it is bounded from above, and, by the choice of

, we have
\tilde{\varphi}(z,w)=\lvert z\rvert^{2}
on
\omega
. Let
\lambda\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]

\lambda\equiv 1

, and define

. This concludes the proof of the theorem.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\lambda\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
be smooth such that
\{\lambda>0\}=[0,1)
and
\lambda\equiv 1
near
0
. Then for every
R>0
there exists
C>0
such that

S(z_{1},z^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lambda(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\lvert z^{\prime}\rvert^{2}+C\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2}

is plurisubharmonic on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{B}(0,R)
and strictly plurisubharmonic on
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{B}(0,R)
; here,
z=(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
let

H_{S}(z,\xi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial z_{j}\partial{\bar{z}}_{k}}(z)\xi_{j}\bar{\xi}_{k}.

Then

\begin{split}H_{S}(z,\xi)=&\big\{[\lambda^{\prime\prime}(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2}+\lambda^{\prime}(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})]\cdot\lvert z^{\prime}\rvert^{2}+C\big\}\cdot\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{2}\\
&+2\RE\{\lambda^{\prime}(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\bar{z}_{1}\xi_{1}\langle z^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle\}+\lambda(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\cdot\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert^{2},\end{split}

where
\langle z^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{j=2}^{n}z_{j}\bar{\xi}_{j}
. Let
R>0
be fixed and let
z\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{B}(0,R)
. Then

\big\lvert\lambda^{\prime}(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\bar{z}_{1}\xi_{1}\langle z^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle\big\rvert\leq R\lvert\lambda^{\prime}(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\rvert\cdot\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert,

and thus for
C>0
large enough it follows that

H_{S}(z,\xi)\geq\frac{C}{2}\cdot\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{2}-R\lvert\lambda^{\prime}(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\rvert\cdot\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert+\lambda(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\cdot\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert^{2}.

But an easy application of L’Hospital’s rule shows that
(\lambda^{\prime})^{2}\leq L\lambda
for some constant
L>0
, which implies that

0\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(R\sqrt{L}\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert-\sqrt{\lambda(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})}\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert\right)^{2}\leq\frac{R^{2}L}{2}\cdot\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{2}-R\lvert\lambda^{\prime}(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\rvert\cdot\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert+\frac{\lambda(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})}{2}\cdot\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert^{2}.

Hence
H_{S}(z,\xi)\geq\varepsilon(\lvert\xi_{1}\rvert^{2}+\lambda(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2})\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert^{2})
for some constant
\varepsilon>0
, provided that
C
is chosen large enough.
∎

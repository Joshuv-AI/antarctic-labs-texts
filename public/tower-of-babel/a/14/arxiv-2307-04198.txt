Compact monotone tall complexity one T T -spaces
Isabelle Charton, Silvia Sabatini, Daniele Sepe

Abstract
. In this paper we study compact monotone tall complexity one T T -spaces. We use the classification of Karshon and Tolman, and the monotone condition, to prove that any two such spaces are isomorphic if and only if they have equal Duistermaat-Heckman measures. Moreover, we show that the moment polytope is Delzant and reflexive, and provide a complete description of the possible Duistermaat-Heckman measures. Whence we obtain a finiteness result that is analogous to that for compact monotone symplectic toric manifolds. Furthermore, we show that any such T T -action can be extended to a toric ( T × S 1 ) (T\times S^{1}) -action. Motivated by a conjecture of Fine and Panov, we prove that any compact monotone tall complexity one T T -space is equivariantly symplectomorphic to a Fano manifold endowed with a suitable symplectic form and a complexity one T T -action.



1.
Introduction

Fano manifolds play an important role in complex algebraic geometry
and beyond. A compact complex manifold is
Fano
if its anticanonical
bundle is ample. Any such manifold is simply connected (see

[
25
, Corollary 6.2.18]
and
[
37
, Remark 3.12]
),
and its Todd genus equals one (see
[
24
, Section 1.8]
for a definition).
Moreover, in any complex dimension there are finitely many
topological types of Fano manifolds (see
[
32
]
). The Fano
condition can be reformulated in Kähler terms: A compact complex manifold
(Y,J)
is Fano if and only if
there exists a Kähler form
\omega\in\Omega^{1,1}(Y)
such that
c_{1}=[\omega]
, where
c_{1}
is the first Chern class of
(Y,J)
– see, for instance,
[
2
, Sections 5.4 and
7]
. This motivates the following definition
1
1

1

In the literature

manifolds are also called
symplectic Fano (see
[

).
: A
symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is
(positive) monotone
if there exists
(a positive)
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

c_{1}=\lambda[\omega],

where
c_{1}
is the first Chern class of
(M,J)
and
J
is any
almost complex structure that is compatible with
\omega
. If

(M,\omega)
is positive monotone, then
\omega

can be rescaled so as to be equal to
c_{1}
in cohomology.

A driving question in symplectic topology is to determine whether
every compact positive monotone symplectic manifold is
diffeomorphic to a Fano manifold. The answer is affirmative in
(real) dimension up to four by work of McDuff (see

[
41
]
), and is negative starting from dimension twelve by work of Fine and Panov (see

[
14
,
48
]
). To the best of our knowledge, this is an open
problem in the remaining dimensions.

Motivated by a conjecture due to
Fine and Panov (see
[

[

,
7
,
8
,
36

), we study the above question in

Hamiltonian torus action
: An action of a

by symplectomorphisms that is codified by a smooth
T
-invariant map
\Phi:M\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, called moment map (see Section

. We remark

Problem 1.1
.

Find necessary and sufficient conditions for a compact monotone
Hamiltonian
T

variety.

A starting point to attack Problem

Intuitively, the lower the complexity, the larger the

T
-space of complexity

complexity
k

T
-space
. Problem

has been already solved in complexity zero, i.e.,
for compact monotone symplectic toric manifolds. To recall the solution, we
say that two Hamiltonian
T
-spaces are
isomorphic

are symplectomorphic so that the moment maps are intertwined (see
Definition
2.1
for a precise statement). Let
(M,\omega,\Phi)
be a compact monotone symplectic toric manifold. By Delzant’s classification (see
[
9
]
), the
isomorphism class of
(M,\omega,\Phi)
is determined by
the moment polytope
\Phi(M)\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, which is
Delzant
(see Section
2.3.1
). Moreover, if
without loss of generality we
assume that
c_{1}=[\omega]
, then, up to translation,

\Phi(M)
is also
reflexive
(see Definition

3.12
). Reflexive polytopes were introduced by Batyrev
in
[
3
]
in the study of toric Fano varieties and, like Fano
manifolds, enjoy special properties. For
instance, if
\ell\subset\mathfrak{t}
is the standard lattice,
then there are finitely many reflexive polytopes of full dimension in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
up to the
standard action of
\mathrm{GL}(\ell^{*})
– see Corollary

3.14
.

\mathbb{R}^{2}

The combination of Delzant’s classification and this
result above yields finiteness for compact monotone
symplectic toric manifolds up to the following notion of equivalence: Two Hamiltonian
T

(M_{1},\omega_{1},\Phi_{1})
and

(M_{2},\omega_{2},\Phi_{2})

\Psi:(M_{1},\omega_{1})\to(M_{2},\omega_{2})

. More precisely, the following holds.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there are finitely many equivalence
classes of compact symplectic toric manifolds of dimension
2n
with first Chern class equal to the class
of the symplectic form.

Moreover, by Delzant’s classification and the Kähler description of
the Fano condition, the following result solves Problem
1.1
in complexity zero.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a compact monotone symplectic toric manifold, then there exists an integrable
complex structure
J
on
M
that is compatible with
\omega
and
invariant under the torus action such that the Kähler manifold
(M,J)
is Fano.

In fact, Theorem

proves the stronger result that any
compact monotone symplectic toric manifold is

symplectomorphic
to a Fano manifold (endowed with a suitable
symplectic form).

1.1.
The results

In this paper we solve Problem
1.1
for
tall

complexity one
T
-spaces, i.e., those for which no reduced space is a point (see
Definition
2.74
). Such spaces have been
classified by Karshon and Tolman in a series of papers (see

[
28
,
29
,
30
]
). This classification is
more involved than that of compact symplectic
toric manifolds: For instance, there are several invariants, namely
the moment polytope, the genus, the painting and the
Duistermaat-Heckman measure (see Section
2.4
for
more details),
and these invariants satisfy some compatibility conditions (see

[
30
]
).

In order to attack Problem
1.1
in the above setting, first we
study the isomorphism classes of compact monotone tall
complexity one
T
-spaces. Our first main result states that, for
these spaces, the Duistermaat-Heckman measure determines all other
invariants. For our purposes, we codify this measure by the unique
continuous function that represents its Radon-Nikodym derivative with
respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
,
which we call the
Duistermaat-Heckman
function (see Theorem
2.49
and Definition

2.51
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Two compact monotone tall complexity one
T
-spaces are isomorphic if and only if their
Duistermaat-Heckman functions are equal.

Our second main result is
finiteness
of compact monotone tall complexity one

T
-spaces up to equivalence, which is the analog of
Theorem
1.2
. To this end, we observe that the moment polytope of
a space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
with
c_{1}=\lambda[\omega]
is a reflexive Delzant polytope if and only if
c_{1}=[\omega]
and the moment map
\Phi
satisfies the so-called
weight sum formula (see Proposition

3.18
and Lemma

3.20
). If
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is
a compact monotone tall complexity one

T
-space, then we may rescale
\omega
and translate
\Phi

so as to satisfy the above conditions (see Corollary

3.4
and Proposition
3.5
). The next
result is a crucial step towards establishing finiteness.

Theorem 1.5
.

Given a reflexive Delzant polytope
\Delta
, there exist finitely
many isomorphism classes of compact monotone tall
complexity one
T
-spaces with
\Phi(M)=\Delta
.

The following result
is a simple consequence of
Theorem
1.5
and answers a question originally posed to us by Yael Karshon.

Corollary 1.6
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there are finitely many equivalence classes of compact tall
complexity one
T
-spaces of dimension
2n
with first Chern class equal to the class
of the symplectic form.

Our third main result concerns the
extendability
of a tall complexity one

T
-action on a compact monotone symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
to a
toric
(T\times S^{1})
-action. To the best of our
knowledge, there is no criterion to ensure such extendability for
compact tall complexity one
T
-spaces of dimension at least six.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a compact monotone tall
complexity one
T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
, then the Hamiltonian
T
-action
extends to a symplectic toric
(T\times S^{1})
-action.

Figure 1.2.

T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
of dimension six whose moment map image is as in
Figure

. We remark that
the set consisting of the ‘height function’ of each such polytope, i.e., the
difference between the
z

‘bottom’ boundary, is precisely the set of the possible
for the Duistermaat-Heckman functions of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
.

Finally, our last main result is a
solution
to Problem
1.1
for tall complexity one
T
-spaces. We recall that, given a compact torus
T
, there exists a unique
complex Lie group
T_{\mathbb{C}}
such that the Lie algebra of
T_{\mathbb{C}}
is
the complexification of
\mathfrak{t}
and

is a maximal compact subgroup of

(see

). For instance, if

,
then

. The following result
is concerned with the existence of an integrable complex structure.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a compact monotone tall complexity one

T
-space, then there exists a
T
-invariant integrable
complex structure
J
on
M
that is compatible with
\omega
such that the complex manifold
(M,J)
is Fano and the

T
-action extends to an effective holomorphic
T_{\mathbb{C}}
-action.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.8
, the
following result solves a stronger version of Problem
1.1

for compact tall complexity one
T
-spaces.

Corollary 1.9
.

Any compact monotone tall complexity one

-space is equivariantly symplectomorphic to a Fano manifold

-action.

1.2.
Structure of the paper

In Section

2
we recall fundamental properties of (compact)
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces. While many of the notions and results
presented therein are well-known to experts, we also set the
terminology and notation used throughout. Some basic properties of
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces are considered in Section

2.1
, which describes in detail the local models
near any orbit, and introduces the notion of exceptional and regular
points and orbits. These are a generalization of the corresponding
concepts introduced by Karshon and Tolman in complexity one, which
play a key role in their classification. Moreover, we also discuss the notion of
exceptional and regular sheets, which are closely related to the
notion of x-ray (see
[
51
, Definition 2.1]
). In Section

2.2
we take a closer look at the invariants of

compact
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces, starting with the so-called
Convexity Package and its consequences. A large part of Section
2

is taken up by the
existence
of the Duistermaat-Heckman
function of a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space (see Theorem
2.49
and Definition

2.51
). We could not find an appropriate
reference for this result and, hence, included the material for completeness. We
focus on the complexity one case, showing that in this case there is a
polytope in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
that encodes the Duistermaat-Heckman
function (see Corollary
2.64
). In Section

2.3
we introduce compact complexity preserving
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces, a class of spaces that generalizes simultaneously compact symplectic toric
manifolds and compact tall complexity one
T
-spaces. For instance, we
prove that their moment polytopes are Delzant polytopes (see
Proposition
2.73
), and their Duistermaat-Heckman
functions enjoy natural properties (see Corollary
2.71
). These spaces may be of
independent interest. Finally, we review the classification
of compact tall complexity one
T
-spaces due to Karshon and Tolman in
Section
2.4
.

In Section
3
we prove some properties of Hamiltonian

T
-actions on compact monotone symplectic manifolds. We show that the presence of a Hamiltonian
T
-action forces
monotonicity to be positive (see Proposition
3.3
). Hence, the symplectic form of any compact monotone
Hamiltonian
T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
can be rescaled so that it is equal to

c_{1}
in cohomology (see Corollary

3.4
). Moreover, we show that the moment map of any compact
Hamiltonian
T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
with

can be translated to satisfy the so-called
weight sum formula (see Proposition
3.5
). Hence, in
order to study compact monotone
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces, it suffices to consider those that satisfy both aforementioned

normalized monotone. That is the content of Section

3.1
. In Section
3.2
,
we characterize normalized monotone complexity preserving Hamiltonian

T
-spaces as being precisely those that are compact
monotone and have reflexive Delzant polytopes as moment map images
(see Proposition

3.18
and Lemma

3.20
).

Section
4
is the technical heart of the paper
and is where we prove our first main result, Theorem

1.4
. In Section
4.1
, we
recall that the genus of a compact monotone tall complexity one

T
-space is zero, a result proved in
[
49
]
. Moreover, we show
that there is a facet of the moment polytope along
which the Duistermaat-Heckman function is constant and equal to the
minimal value (see Proposition
4.2
). Such a
facet, which we call minimal, plays an important role in the arguments
of Sections
4
and
5
. In
Section
4.2
, we characterize isolated fixed points
in normalized monotone tall complexity one

T
-spaces (see Proposition
4.6
). This is
another fundamental result that we use extensively in Sections

4
and
5
. The space of
exceptional orbits and the painting of a normalized monotone tall complexity one

T
-space are studied in Section
4.3
. We
show that the isotropy data associated to the former can be
reconstructed by ‘looking at the moment polytope’ (see Remark

4.12
for a precise statement). Moreover,
we prove that the painting of a normalized monotone tall complexity one

-space is trivial (see Definition

and Theorem

). In Section

4.4
, we provide an explicit formula for the

-space (see Theorem

). It is given in terms of the number of connected components of the space

). Finally, in Section

we prove

by bringing the above results together.

In Section
5
we prove all our remaining main

5.1
addresses the question of
finding necessary conditions for a function to be the

T
-space (see Proposition
5.1
). This allows us to
prove the finiteness result, namely Theorem
1.5
and Corollary

1.6
. In Section
5.2
, we
prove that the aforementioned necessary conditions are, in fact,

5.12
and Corollary

5.15
). Our method to prove this result leads us

. Finally, in Section

, we

1.8
.

1.3.
Conventions

Tori

Throughout the paper, we identify the integral lattice of
S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{Z}
. This means that the exponential map
\exp:\mathrm{Lie}(S^{1})=i\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
is given by
\exp(ix)=e^{2\pi ix}
, where
z\mapsto e^{z}
is the standard complex exponential
function. Moreover, we often identify
\mathrm{Lie}(S^{1})

and its dual with
\mathbb{R}
tacitly; we trust that this does not cause
confusion.

In this paper,
T
is a compact torus of
dimension
d
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
. We
denote its integral lattice by
\ell
, namely
\ell=\ker(\exp\colon\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow T)
, and the dual of
\ell
by
\ell^{*}
. Moreover, we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the standard pairing between
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and

\mathfrak{t}
. Finally, we fix an inner product on
\mathfrak{t}
once and for all.

Convex polytopes

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be a real vector
space of dimension
d
with full-rank
lattice
\ell\subset\mathfrak{t}
. A
(convex) polytope

\Delta
in

\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is a subset that satisfies either of the following two
equivalent conditions:

•

\Delta
is the convex hull of a finite set of points, or

•

\Delta
is the bounded intersection of a finite set of (closed)
half-spaces of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
,

(see
[
52
, Theorem 2.15]
). Throughout the paper, we assume
that a polytope
\Delta
has dimension equal to that of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. We often
write
\Delta
in its minimal representation, i.e.,

(1.1)

\Delta=\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\,\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu_{i}\rangle\geq c_{i}\}\,

where
\nu_{i}\in\mathfrak{t}
is the
inward normal
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and the
affine hyperplane
\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu_{i}\rangle=c_{i}\}
supports a
facet
of
\Delta
for
i=1,\ldots,l
. A finite non-empty intersection
of facets of
\Delta
is a
face
of
\Delta
. For convenience, we
think of
\Delta
also as a face. The
dimension
of a face
\mathcal{F}
of

\Delta
is the dimension of the affine span of
\mathcal{F}
in

\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. Faces that are 1-dimensional are called
edges
, while
0-dimensional faces are
vertices
.

2.
(Compact) Hamiltonian
T
-spaces and their invariants

2.1.
Definition and first properties

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.
A smooth
T
-action
\psi\colon T\times M\to M
is

Hamiltonian
if it admits a

moment map
, i.e., a smooth
T
-invariant map
\Phi\colon M\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
that satisfies

(2.1)

d\langle\Phi,\xi\rangle=-\iota_{\xi^{\#}}\omega\,\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathfrak{t},

where
\xi^{\#}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
denotes the vector field associated
to
\xi
. In this case, the diffeomorphism
\psi(t,\cdot)\colon M\to M
is a symplectomorphism
for each
t\in T
,
i.e., it preserves
\omega
.
For brevity we denote
\psi(t,p)
by
t\cdot p
.

Definition 2.1
.

•

A
(compact) Hamiltonian
T
-space
is a (compact)
connected symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
endowed with an effective Hamiltonian

T
-action and a moment map
\Phi\colon M\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. We denote such a
space by
(M,\omega,\Phi)
.

•

Two Hamiltonian
T
-spaces
(M_{1},\omega_{1},\Phi_{1})
and

(M_{2},\omega_{2},\Phi_{2})
are
isomorphic
if there exists a symplectomorphism
\Psi:(M_{1},\omega_{1})\to(M_{2},\omega_{2})
such that
\Phi_{2}\circ\Psi=\Phi_{1}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Since
T
is connected, an isomorphism of
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces is necessarily a
T
-equivariant diffeomorphism.

Definition
2.1
includes
\dim T=0
(this is used, for
instance, in Theorem
2.49
). In this case, a Hamiltonian
T
-space is simply a symplectic
manifold and an isomorphism is simply a symplectomorphism.

2.1.1.
Orbital moment map and reduced spaces

We endow the quotient space
M/T
with the quotient
topology. Since
\Phi
is
T
-invariant, it descends to a continuous
map
\bar{\Phi}:M/T\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}

that is called the
orbital moment
map
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\Psi
is an isomorphism between
(M_{1},\omega_{1},\Phi_{1})
and

(M_{2},\omega_{2},\Phi_{2})
, then there is a homeomorphism
\bar{\Psi}:M_{1}/T\to M_{2}/T
such that
\bar{\Phi}_{1}=\bar{\Phi}_{2}\circ\bar{\Psi}
.

The fibers of
\bar{\Phi}
can be canonically identified with the
quotient of the fibers of
\Phi
by the
T
-action. These are known as

reduced spaces
. If
\alpha\in\Phi(M)
is a regular value of

\Phi
, then the reduced space at
\alpha
,
\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)/T
, is
an orbifold that inherits a symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{red}}

(see
[
39
]
and
[
34
, Lemma 3.9]
).

2.1.2.
Complexity of a Hamiltonian

T
-space

Since the
T
-action on

M
is effective and since orbits are
isotropic submanifolds of
(M,\omega)
, we have that
d\leq n
. The
difference
n-d
is a simple, but important invariant of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
.

Definition 2.4
.

The
complexity
of a Hamiltonian
T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is

k:=\frac{1}{2}\dim M-\dim T.

Example 2.5
.

Complexity zero Hamiltonian
T
-spaces are
symplectic toric
manifolds
. Throughout the paper, we refer to torus actions of
complexity zero as
toric
.

Intuitively, the complexity of a Hamiltonian
T
-space is half of the
dimension of a reduced space at a regular value.

2.1.3.
Local model and local normal form

Given
p\in M
, its
stabilizer
is the closed
subgroup
H:=\{t\in T\mid t\cdot p=p\}
. We set
h:=\dim H
. Since
T
is abelian, any two points on the same orbit have equal
stabilizers. Hence, the stabilizer of an orbit is well-defined. If
\mathcal{O}
denotes the
T
-orbit containing
p
, the
infinitesimal symplectic linear action of
H
on
(T_{p}M,\omega_{p})
fixes
T_{p}\mathcal{O}
. Thus there is a symplectic linear action of

H
on the quotient vector space
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
endowed with the quotient linear
symplectic structure. We call the underlying Lie group homomorphism
the
symplectic slice representation of
\boldsymbol{p}
.

Remark 2.6
.

The symplectic slice representations of two points lying on the same
orbit are naturally isomorphic. Hence, the symplectic slice
representation of an orbit is well-defined. This allows us to ‘decorate’ the
quotient space
M/T
by attaching the symplectic slice
representation to every orbit (this data includes the
stabilizer of the orbit).

Let
\Psi
be an isomorphism between
(M_{1},\omega_{1},\Phi_{1})
and

(M_{2},\omega_{2},\Phi_{2})
and let
\bar{\Psi}:M_{1}/T\to M_{2}/T
be
the homeomorphism given by Remark
2.3
. For
any
p\in M_{1}
,
\bar{\Psi}([p])
and
[p]
have equal stabilizers
and symplectic slice representations.

Fix a
T
-invariant
almost complex structure on
(M,\omega)
; the existence of such a
structure is proved in
[
42
, Lemma
5.52]
. We observe that
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
has real dimension

2(h+n-d)=2(h+k)
, where
k
is the complexity of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
. Hence, we
use the above almost complex structure to
identify
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
with
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}

endowed with the standard symplectic form

(2.2)

\frac{i}{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{h+k}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}.

Moreover, under this identification, the linear
H
-action is by
unitary transformations.

Let
\rho:H\to U(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})
be the
associated homomorphism of Lie groups. Since
H
is abelian,

\rho(H)
is contained in a maximal torus of
U(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})
. We denote
the maximal torus of
U(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})
consisting of diagonal
transformations by
(S^{1})^{h+k}
. Hence,
we may assume that
\rho
factors through
a Lie group homomorphism
H\to(S^{1})^{h+k}
that we also denote by

\rho
by a slight abuse of
notation. We
write
\rho_{j}
for the
j
th component of
\rho
, where
j=1,\ldots,h+k
. Let
d_{e}\rho_{j}
denote the derivative at the identity of

\rho_{j}
and set

d_{e}\rho_{1}:=2\pi i\alpha_{1},\ldots,d_{e}\rho_{h+k}:=2\pi i\alpha_{h+k}
. Hence,
\alpha_{j}\in\ell^{*}_{\mathfrak{h}}
for all
j=1,\ldots,h+k
, where
\mathfrak{h}

is the Lie algebra of
H
and
\ell_{\mathfrak{h}}
denotes
the integral lattice in
\mathfrak{h}
. We call

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{h+k}
the
isotropy weights of
\boldsymbol{p}
(for the

\boldsymbol{H}
-action)
. The multiset of isotropy weights of
p
does not depend on the
choice of
T
-invariant almost complex structure on

(M,\omega)
. Moreover, this multiset encodes the action of the
identity component of
H
on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
. Explicitly,

(2.3)

\exp(\xi)\cdot(z_{1},\ldots,z_{h+k})=(e^{2\pi i\langle\alpha_{1},\xi\rangle}z_{1},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\langle\alpha_{h+k},\xi\rangle}z_{h+k})\quad\text{for
every}\;\;\xi\in\mathfrak{h}\,.

In particular, if
H
is connected, then the multiset of isotropy
weights of
p
determine the symplectic slice representation up to
unitary isomorphisms. Finally, by Remark
2.6
, the multisets of
isotropy weights of two points lying on the same orbit are equal.

From the stabilizer
H\leq T
of
p
and the Lie group homomorphism
\rho:H\to(S^{1})^{h+k}
, we construct a symplectic manifold together
with a Hamiltonian
T
-action and a moment map. This is the local model for a
T
-invariant neighborhood of

\mathcal{O}
in
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
. We do this in two equivalent ways, seeing as
one is more convenient for proofs and the other is more convenient for
calculations.

The abstract construction

Let
\Omega
denote the
symplectic form on
T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
given
by taking the sum of the pullbacks of the canonical symplectic form on

T^{*}T
and the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
(see equation
(
2.2
)). Let
H
act (on the right) on
T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}

as follows: On
T^{*}T
it acts by
the cotangent lift of (right) multiplication, while on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
it
acts by
z\cdot h:=\rho(h^{-1})(z)
, for
h\in H
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
. By construction,
the
H
-action on
(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k},\Omega)
is
Hamiltonian. Let
\hat{\Phi}:T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}\to\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the moment map that sends
(0,0)\in T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
to the origin in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Since this
H
-action is
free and proper, the quotient

(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H:=\hat{\Phi}^{-1}(0)/H

is a smooth manifold that inherits a symplectic form

\omega_{\mathrm{red}}
(see
[
42
, Proposition
5.40]
).

Let
T
act (on the left) on
T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
as follows: On

T^{*}T
it acts by the
cotangent lift of (left) multiplication, while on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
it acts
trivially. This
T
-action is Hamiltonian and commutes with the above

H
-action. Hence, it induces a Hamiltonian
T
-action on
((T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H,\omega_{\mathrm{red}})
. As a moment map for
this
T
-action we take the one that sends
[0,0]\in(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H
to the origin in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and we denote it by

\Phi_{\mathrm{red}}
. The desired local model is the triple
((T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H,\omega_{\mathrm{red}},\Phi_{\mathrm{red}})
.

The explicit construction

The choice of inner product on
\mathfrak{t}
induces an
inner product on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
which, in turn, determines an
isomorphism
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\simeq\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Moreover, we choose a trivialization
T^{*}T\cong T\times\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. With these choices, we fix an identification
T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
with
T\times\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
. Under this identification, the above
(right)
H
-action is given by

(t,\alpha,\beta,z)\cdot h=(th,\alpha,\beta,\rho(h^{-1})z),

while the above moment map

\hat{\Phi}
is given by

(t,\alpha,\beta,z)\mapsto\beta-\Phi_{H}(z),

where
\Phi_{H}:\mathbb{C}^{h+k}\to\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is the homogeneous
moment map for the (left)
H
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
given by
h\cdot z=\rho(h)z
. The map

\begin{split}T\times\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}&\to\hat{\Phi}^{-1}(0)\\
(t,\alpha,z)&\mapsto(t,\alpha,\Phi_{H}(z),z)\end{split}

is an
H
-equivariant diffeomorphism, where the left hand side is
equipped with the (right)
H
-action on
T\times\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
given by

(2.4)

(t,\alpha,z)\cdot h=(th,\alpha,\rho(h^{-1})z).

Hence the quotient

(2.5)

Y:=T\times_{H}\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}

is diffeomorphic to
(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H
. We denote by
\omega_{Y}
(respectively
\Phi_{Y}
) the pullback of

\omega_{\mathrm{red}}
(respectively
\Phi_{\mathrm{red}}
) under the above diffeomorphism. The (left)

T
-action on
Y
is given by

(2.6)

s\cdot[t,\alpha,z]=[st,\alpha,z],

while the moment map
\Phi_{Y}
takes the form

(2.7)

\Phi_{Y}([t,\alpha,z])=\alpha+\Phi_{H}(z).

The stabilizer of
[1,0,0]\in Y
is
H
and the symplectic slice
representation of
[1,0,0]
is
\rho:H\to(S^{1})^{h+k}
. Moreover, if the
T
-action is effective, the
complexity of
(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})
is equal to
k
. We refer to

(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})
as the
local model of

\boldsymbol{p}
. By
Remark
2.6
, the local models at two points lying
on the same orbit are equal.

Remark 2.7
.

By a slight abuse of terminology, we also refer to
((T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H,\omega_{\mathrm{red}},\Phi_{\mathrm{red}})
as the local model of
p
. Moreover,
throughout the paper we sometimes state results in terms of

but use
(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H
in the proof. We trust that this does not
cause confusion.

As a consequence of the local normal form
theorem for Hamiltonian actions of compact Lie groups due to Guillemin-Sternberg
[
20
]
and Marle

[
38
]
, any Hamiltonian

T
-space is isomorphic to a local model near an orbit. More
precisely, the following holds.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a Hamiltonian
T
-space. Given
p\in M
, let

(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})
be the local model of
p
. There exist

T
-invariant open neighborhoods
U\subset M
of
p
and
V\subset Y
of
[1,0,0]
and an isomorphism between

(U,\omega,\Phi)
and
(V,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y}+\Phi(p))
that maps
p
to
[1,0,0]
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a Hamiltonian
T
-space. For any
p\in M
with
stabilizer
H
, the
homomorphism
\rho:H\to(S^{1})^{h+k}
is injective.

Proof.

Fix
p\in M
and notation as above. Let
(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})
be
the local model of
p
. Since the
T
-action on
M
is assumed
to be effective, so is the
T
-action on
Y
by Theorem
2.8
(see Remark
2.2
). By definition of

Y
and of the
T
-action on
Y
, the
T
-action on

Y=T\times_{H}\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
is
effective if and only if the Lie group homomorphism
\rho:H\to(S^{1})^{h+k}
is injective, as desired.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Given
p\in M
, let
H
be its stabilizer and let

\{\alpha_{j}\}
be the multiset of isotropy weights of
p
. By
Corollary
2.9
, the
H
-action on
(T_{p}\mathcal{O})^{\omega}/T_{p}\mathcal{O}
is effective. In particular, so is the action
by its identity component. Using equation (

), it follows that the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of

equals

\ell^{*}_{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Remark 2.11
.

The above discussion simplifies significantly if

is a
fixed
point
, i.e., if

. In this case,

so that

, and the isotropy
weights
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
lie in
\ell^{*}
. Moreover,

, the symplectic form
\omega_{Y}
is the standard
symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the
T
-action on
Y
is given by

(2.8)

\exp(\xi)\cdot(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(e^{2\pi i\langle\alpha_{1},\xi\rangle}z_{1},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\langle\alpha_{n},\xi\rangle}z_{n})\quad\text{for
every}\;\;\xi\in\mathfrak{t}\,,

and the moment map
\Phi_{Y}:Y\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is given by

(2.9)

\Phi_{Y}(z)=\pi\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}|z_{j}|^{2},

where

.

2.1.4.
Regular and exceptional local models

Theorem
2.8
allows us to understand a Hamiltonian
T
-space in a
T
-invariant open neighborhood of a
point
p
by studying the local model of
p
. For this reason, in this subsection we
take a closer look at local models.

In what follows, we fix a non-negative integer
k
and a closed subgroup
H\leq T
. As above, we set
d:=\dim T
and
h:=\dim H
. We also fix
an injective Lie group homomorphism
\rho:H\hookrightarrow(S^{1})^{h+k}
. We denote the subspace
of fixed points of the
H
-action induced by
\rho
by
(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})^{H}
. As in Section

2.1.3
, we use
k
,
H
and
\rho
to construct
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces

((T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k})/\!/H,\omega_{\mathrm{red}},\Phi_{\mathrm{red}})\cong(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})

that we refer to as the
local model determined by
\boldsymbol{k}
,
\boldsymbol{H}
and

\boldsymbol{\rho}
(see equations (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and
(
2.7
) for the definition of
Y
, the
T
-action and
\Phi_{Y}

respectively). We remark that
k
is the complexity of
(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})
, that
H
is the stabilizer of
p:=[1,0,0]
and
that
\rho
is the symplectic slice representation of
p
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer, let
H\leq T

be a closed subgroup and let
\rho:H\hookrightarrow(S^{1})^{h+k}

be an injective Lie group homomorphism, where
h=\dim H
. Set
s:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})^{H}
. There exists an isomorphism of
Hermitian vector spaces
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{s}

such that

(2.10)

\rho=(\rho^{\prime},1):H\hookrightarrow(S^{1})^{h+k-s}\times 1\hookrightarrow(S^{1})^{h+k-s}\times(S^{1})^{s}\simeq(S^{1})^{h+k}

and
(\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s})^{H}=\{0\}
. Moreover,
s\leq k
and, if
s=k
, then
\rho
is an isomorphism between
H
and
(S^{1})^{h}
and
the
H
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{h}
is toric.

Proof.

To simplify notation, set
V:=(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})^{H}
. The standard
Hermitian product on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
induces an isomorphism of Hermitian
vector spaces
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}\simeq V^{\perp}\oplus V
, and both
V
and

V^{\perp}
are endowed with the restriction of the standard
Hermitian product. By definition
\rho(H)
fixes
V
pointwise and
is a subgroup of
U(h+k)
. Hence,
\rho
splits as the
direct sum of two
H
-representations that we denote by

\rho_{V}:H\to U(V)
and
\rho^{\prime}:H\to U(V^{\perp})
. By construction,

\rho_{V}
is the trivial representation. Moreover,

\rho^{\prime}(H)
is contained in a maximal torus of
U(V^{\perp})
(see
Section
2.1.3
). Since the Hermitian vector
spaces
V
and
V^{\perp}
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{s}
and to

\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s}
respectively and since
\{0\}=(\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s})^{H}
, this proves the first statement. To prove
the second, we observe that
\rho^{\prime}:H\to U(V^{\perp})
is injective, since
\rho
is injective. Since maximal tori in
U(V^{\perp})

have dimension equal to
h+k-s
and
since
h=\dim H
, it follows at once that
s\leq k
. Finally, if
s=k
,
then, since
\rho^{\prime}
is injective, it follows that the map
H\hookrightarrow(S^{1})^{h}
is an isomorphism of Lie groups.
∎

Lemma
2.12
allows us to ‘decompose’ local models as
follows (see
[
28
, Lemma 4.4]
for a proof in the case
k=s=1
).

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer, let
H\leq T

be a closed subgroup and let
\rho:H\hookrightarrow(S^{1})^{h+k}

be an injective Lie group homomorphism, where
h=\dim H
. Let
s=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})^{H}
and
\rho^{\prime}:H\hookrightarrow(S^{1})^{h+k-s}
be as
in the statement of Lemma
2.12
, so that

(\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s})^{H}=\{0\}
. The local
model determined by
k
,
H
and
\rho
is isomorphic to

(Y^{\prime}\times\mathbb{C}^{s},\mathrm{pr}^{*}_{1}\omega_{Y^{\prime}}+\mathrm{pr}^{*}_{2}\omega_{0},\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}\Phi_{Y^{\prime}}),

where
(Y^{\prime},\omega_{Y^{\prime}},\Phi_{Y^{\prime}})
is the local model determined by

k-s
,
H
and
\rho^{\prime}
,
\omega_{0}
is the standard symplectic form

\mathbb{C}^{s}
, and
\mathrm{pr}_{j}
is the projection from
Y^{\prime}\times\mathbb{C}^{s}

to its
j
th component, for
j=1,2
.

Proof.

In this proof we use the abstract construction of local models (see
Section
2.1.3
and Remark

2.7
). Fix an isomorphism

\mathbb{C}^{h+k}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{s}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s}
as in the statement of
Lemma
2.12
. This induces a symplectomorphism between
(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k},\Omega)

and
((T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s})\times\mathbb{C}^{s},\mathrm{pr}^{*}_{1}\Omega^{\prime}+\mathrm{pr}^{*}\omega_{0})
, where
\Omega^{\prime}
denotes the symplectic form
on
T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s}
and, as above,
\mathrm{pr}_{j}
is the projection from
(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s})\times\mathbb{C}^{s}

to its
j
th component, for
j=1,2
. We endow
(T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s})\times\mathbb{C}^{s}
with the following (right)
H
-action and
(left)
T
-action:

•

(
H
-action): On
T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s}
we consider the (right)
H
-action used
to construct the local model determined by
k-s
,
H
and
\rho^{\prime}
,
while
H
acts trivially on
\mathbb{C}^{s}
, and

•

(
T
-action): On
T^{*}T\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k-s}
we consider the (left)
T
-action used
to construct the local model determined by
k-s
,
H
and
\rho^{\prime}
,
while
T
acts trivially on
\mathbb{C}^{s}
.

The above symplectomorphism is both
H
- and
T
-equivariant. Hence,
there is a
T
-equivariant symplectomorphism between the symplectic
quotients with respect to the
H
-actions, i.e., an isomorphism
between the resulting Hamiltonian
T
-spaces. Once the abstract
constructions are identified with the explicit ones as in Section

2.1.3
, this yields the desired isomorphism of
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Proposition

is particularly simple if

, i.e., if

is

.

Motivated by Theorem

and by Remark

, we introduce the

.

Let

be a complexity

be a

. Let

be the orbit containing

Remark 2.16
.

We observe that Definition

extends
also to orbits (see Remark

); hence,
throughout the paper, we also refer to

Definition 2.17
.

Let

be a Hamiltonian

-space. We define the set of

as the subset of

consisting of exceptional orbits.

Example 2.18
.

A fixed point

in a complexity

T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is regular if
and only if it lies on a fixed submanifold of dimension

. We observe that the latter condition is

The techniques of Example
2.18
and Lemma

, together with Theorem

, can be used to prove the following result.

Lemma 2.19
.

•

Any point with
trivial stabilizer, as well as any point in a complexity zero

-space, is regular.

•

If a Hamiltonian
T
-space has positive complexity, then any isolated fixed

•

The subset of regular points in a Hamiltonian
T
-space is open.

•

If a point
p
is regular, then the stabilizer of

is connected.

We conclude this section with two results in complexity one.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a complexity one

-space. A point

is
isolated if and only if it is exceptional.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.19
, if
p\in M^{T}
is
isolated, then it is exceptional. Conversely, if

is regular, then, by Theorem
2.8

and Example

,

is not isolated.
∎

Finally, as an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.8
and of Proposition
2.13
, the following
characterization of exceptional points holds (cf.
[

, Lemma 4.4]
).

Corollary 2.21
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a complexity one

-space. A point

is
exceptional if and only if every nearby orbit in the
same moment fiber has a strictly smaller stabilizer.

In particular, Definition
2.15
agrees with
the definition of exceptional orbits of
[

]
in complexity one,
and is the appropriate notion for our purposes.

2.1.5.
Regular and exceptional sheets

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a Hamiltonian
T
-space. For any closed subgroup
H\leq T
, the action of
H
on
M
is also
Hamiltonian: Let

\mathfrak{h}
be the Lie algebra of
H
and let
i:\mathfrak{h}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{t}
denote the
inclusion. Then a moment
map for the
H
-action is given by the composition

(2.11)

M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{t}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{h}^{*},

where

is the dual of

. We
denote the set of fixed points of the

-action by

M^{H}=\{p\in M\mid h\cdot p=p\,\text{ for all }h\in H\}.

The following result is used throughout and a proof can be found in
[
42
, Lemma 5.53]
.

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a Hamiltonian
T
-space and let
H\leq T
be
closed. Then any connected component of
M^{H}
is an embedded
symplectic submanifold of
(M,\omega)
.

We refer to the connected components of
M^{T}
as the
fixed submanifolds
of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
.

Remark 2.23

If
N\subset M^{T}
is a fixed submanifold, then for any

,
the isotropy weights of
p
are equal to those at
p^{\prime}
. Hence, the

isotropy weights of the fixed submanifold

are well-defined. Moreover, they can be used to determine
\dim N
: By Theorem
2.8
,

\dim N
equals twice the number of isotropy weights of
N
that are
equal to 0.

Let
H\leq T
be a closed subgroup that is the stabilizer of a point

p\in M
and let
N\subset M^{H}
denote the connected component that
contains
p
. We set
\omega_{N}:=\omega|_{N}
. By Lemma

2.22
,
(N,\omega_{N})
is an embedded
symplectic submanifold of
(M,\omega)
. Moreover, since
T
is
abelian and connected, and since
N
is connected,
N
is

T
-invariant. The
T
-action on
N
induces a

-action on

Theorem 2.32

(

Let

(i)

(Connectedness)
The fibers of the moment map are connected.

(ii)

(Stability)
The moment map is open as a map onto its

(iii)

(Convexity)
The moment map image is the convex hull of the images of
the fixed submanifolds.

We remark that, since the action is effective, the moment map
image of a compact Hamiltonian
T

dimension equal to
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.

Definition 2.33

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space. The image
\Phi(M)

is called the

.

By Theorem
2.32
, the moment polytope of a compact
Hamiltonian
T
-space is convex. In fact, more is true and, in order
to prove this, we need to recall a few notions. We say that a polytope

\Delta\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}

is

if any edge

is of the form

(2.14)

e=\{v+t\alpha\mid t\in[0,l]\}\quad\text{for some }v\in\mathfrak{t}^{*},\,\alpha\in\ell^{*}\text{ and }l\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}.

Moreover, a
subset

is a

if, for all

and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\lambda v\in C
. A cone in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is
proper
if it does not contain any subspace of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
of
positive dimension. The following result provides a local description
of the moment polytope of a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space near the
image of a fixed submanifold.

Corollary 2.34
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space of dimension
2n
. Let

be a fixed submanifold and let
\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{n}
be the isotropy weights of

\mathcal{C}_{N}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\text{-span}\left\{\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{n}\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}^{*},

and let

be the maximal subspace that is contained in

.

(i)

There exist an open neighborhood

of
\Phi(N)
in
\Phi(M)

and an open neighborhood

of

in
\mathcal{C}_{N}
such that

. In particular,
\Phi(M)
is rational.

(ii)

The intersection

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{H}_{N}+\Phi(N)\right)\cap\Phi(M)

is a face of
\Phi(M)
and the dimension of this

\mathcal{H}_{N}
.

In particular,
\Phi(N)
is a vertex of
\Phi(M)
if and only if the cone
\mathcal{C}_{N}
is proper.

Figure 2.1.
The arrows are the non-zero isotropy weights of the fixed
submanifold

\mathcal{C}_{N}
and the straight line spanned by the

.

Proof.

For any

, there exist a

of

and an open neighborhood

of

,

in

. Since

. By

in

. Since the cone

is convex and rational, and
since vertices of the moment polytope are the image of fixed submanifolds

To prove part

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{F}}:=\left\{\xi\in\mathfrak{t}\mid\left\langle x-y,\xi\right\rangle=0\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{F}\right\}.

By part
(i)
of Corollary
2.34
,

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a rational subspace of
\mathfrak{t}
of
dimension equal to the codimension of
\mathcal{F}
in
\Phi(M)
. Hence

\exp(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{F}})
is subtorus of
T
. The subset

M_{\mathcal{F}}:=\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{F})\subset M
is
T
-invariant; we set

(2.18)

H_{\mathcal{F}}:=\{t\in T\mid t\cdot p=p\text{ for all }p\in M_{\mathcal{F}}\}.

Lemma 2.42

(Theorem 3.5.12 in
[
45
]
)
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space and let
\mathcal{F}
be a face of
\Phi(M)
. Then

M_{\mathcal{F}}=\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{F})
is a connected component of

M^{H_{\mathcal{F}}}
and the Lie algebra of
H_{\mathcal{F}}
equals
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Remark 2.43
.

Connectedness of
M_{\mathcal{F}}
can also be proved using the fact
that
the Convexity Package implies that the preimage of any convex set is
connected (see
[
30
, Section 2]
).

Corollary 2.44
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space and let

\mathcal{F}
be a face of
\Phi(M)
. There exists
a connected, open and dense subset of
M_{\mathcal{F}}
whose points
have stabilizer equal to
H_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

By the principal orbit theorem (see
[
12
, Theorem
2.8.5]
) and since
T
is abelian, there exists a subgroup
H

of
T
and a connected, open and dense subset of
M_{\mathcal{F}}

such that
H
is the stabilizer of any point in this subset. Hence,

H_{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq H
. However, since
H
is the stabilizer
of orbits of principal type and since
T
is abelian,
H
is
contained in the stabilizer of any point in

M_{\mathcal{F}}
. Therefore,
H\subseteq H_{\mathcal{F}}
.
∎

By Corollary
2.44
, the preimage of
a face
\mathcal{F}
of
\Phi(M)
gives rise to a sheet in the sense
of Definition
2.24
that is stabilized by

H_{\mathcal{F}}
. We denote it by

(2.19)

(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}).

In particular, since the complexity of
(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
is at most that of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
by Proposition
2.26
, if
the codimension of
\mathcal{F}
is
r
, then
\dim M_{\mathcal{F}}\leq 2n-2r
.

2.2.2.
The Duistermaat-Heckman measure and its density
function

In this section we take a close look at an invariant of compact
Hamiltonian
T
-spaces that is central to this paper. We start by
recalling the following notion.

Definition 2.45
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space of dimension
2n
. The
Duistermaat-Heckman measure of

\boldsymbol{\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)}
is the pushforward of the (normalized)
Liouville measure, i.e., for any Borel set
U\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
,

(2.20)

m_{DH}(U)\colon=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\Phi^{-1}(U)}\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

The Duistermaat-Heckman measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue
measure on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
(see
[
11
, equation (3.4)]
). Therefore its Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect
to the Lebesgue measure is a Lebesgue integrable function
f_{\text{DH}}\colon\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is uniquely defined up to a set of measure zero. Without loss
of generality, we henceforth assume that
f_{\text{DH}}
vanishes identically
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\smallsetminus\Phi(M)
.

In
[
11
]
, Duistermaat and Heckman give an explicit representative of the restriction of

f_{\text{DH}}
to the intersection of the moment polytope with the
set of regular values of
\Phi
. In order to
state this result, we denote the set of regular
values of
\Phi
contained in
\Phi(M)
by

\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Moreover, we recall that for any
x\in\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}
, the reduced space
M_{x}
is an orbifold that
inherits a symplectic form that we denote by
\omega_{x}
(see Section

2.1.1
).

Theorem 2.46

(Proposition 3.2 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact complexity
k

T
-space. The restriction of the
Radon-Nikodym derivative of the Duistermaat-Heckman measure of

\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
to
\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}
can be chosen to be
equal to the function
\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}\to\mathbb{R}

(2.21)

x\mapsto\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\int_{M_{x}}\frac{\omega_{x}^{k}}{k!}=:\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\mathrm{Vol}(M_{x}),\quad x\in\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}.

Remark 2.47
.

By
[
11
, Corollary 3.3]
, the restriction of the
function (
2.21
) to each connected component of
\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}

is a polynomial of degree at most
k
. Moreover, if this
polynomial has positive degree, the coefficients
of the monomials of top degree are integral. This is because the cohomology classes

[\omega_{x}]
vary linearly with
x
on such a connected component
and the variation is controlled by a cohomology class
with integral coefficients (see
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Remark 2.48

Theorem
2.46
is sufficient to calculate the
Duistermaat-Heckman measure, as the set of singular values has
measure zero by Sard’s theorem.

By Remark
2.47
, the function of
(
2.21
) is continuous. The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
2.49

below, which is probably well-known to experts (cf.
[
17
, Theorem
3.2.11]
for linear symplectic actions on vector spaces and

[
46
, Theorem A and Remark 1.2.10]
). However,
since we use it extensively throughout the paper, we include a proof for completeness.

Theorem 2.49
.

Given a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
, there exists a unique
continuous function
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right):\Phi(M)\to\mathbb{R}
that extends the
function of (
2.21
).

Remark 2.50
.

By Remark
2.47
, if the interior of the moment polytope consists solely of regular
values, then Theorem
2.49

is trivial. In this case,
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is the restriction
of a polynomial of degree at most the complexity of

\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
. For instance, the desired function in the
case of compact symplectic toric
manifolds is the indicator function of the moment polytope, since
reduced spaces are connected by Theorem
2.32
, and since
k=\dim M_{x}=0
for all
x\in\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}
.

Theorem
2.49
allows us to introduce the following notion.

Definition 2.51
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space. We call the continuous
map
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right):\Phi(M)\to\mathbb{R}
given by Theorem

2.49

the Duistermaat-Heckman
function
of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
.

Example 2.52
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space and let
H\subset T

be a subtorus. Choose a complementary subtorus
K
of
T
so that

T=H\times K
. This induces an identification
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{h}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{k}^{*}
. We write the Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
as
dxdy
,
where
dx
(respectively
dy
) is the Lebesgue measure on

\mathfrak{h}^{*}
(respectively
\mathfrak{k}^{*}
). Let
\pi:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\to\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the projection induced by the inclusion
H\subset T
. The
H
-action is Hamiltonian with moment map
\Phi^{\prime}:=\pi\circ\Phi
. Since
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is continuous, by Fubini’s theorem, the Duistermaat-Heckman function of

(M,\omega,\Phi^{\prime})
is given by

(2.22)

DH(M,\omega,\Phi^{\prime})(x)=\int\limits_{\Delta_{x}}DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)(x,y)dy\quad\text{for all }x\in\Phi^{\prime}(M),

where
\Delta_{x}=\pi^{-1}(x)\cap\Phi(M)
.

Suppose further that
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is a compact symplectic toric manifold and that

H
has codimension 1. For any

x\in\Phi^{\prime}(M)
,
\Delta_{x}
is an interval in
\{x\}\times\mathfrak{k}^{*}\simeq\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}
that we write as
\{(x,y)\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\oplus\mathbb{R}\mid y\in[p_{min}(x),p_{max}(x)]\}
. By Remark
2.50
,

(2.23)

DH(M,\omega,\Phi^{\prime})(x)=p_{\max}(x)-p_{\min}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\Phi^{\prime}(M).

We observe that, since
\Phi(M)
is a convex polytope, the
difference
p_{max}-p_{min}
is concave (cf. Proposition

2.59
below).

Our proof of Theorem
2.49
uses extensively ideas from
[
17
, Section
3.5]
. Before proceeding with the proof, we need to recall some
facts about singular values of the moment map.

Intermezzo 1: chambers of the moment map

We begin by relating singular points of the moment map of a
Hamiltonian
T
-space (that is not necessarily compact), to sheets
arising from one dimensional stabilizers (see Definition

2.24
). If
K\leq T
is a closed one-dimensional
subgroup that occurs as the stabilizer of some point in
M
, then
every
p\in M^{K}
is a singular point of
\Phi
. In fact, the converse
also holds.

Lemma 2.53
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a Hamiltonian
T
-space. If
K\leq T
is a
one-dimensional closed
subgroup that occurs as the stabilizer of some point
in
M
then
\Phi(M^{K})
is contained in the set of singular values
of
\Phi
. Conversely, if
x\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is a
singular value of
\Phi
, then there exists a one-dimensional closed
subgroup
K\leq T
that occurs as the stabilizer of some point
in
M
such that
x\in\Phi(M^{K})
.

Proof.

Since the action is Hamiltonian, the first statement
holds. Conversely, let
p\in M
be a singular point with
\Phi(p)=x
. Let
H
be the stabilizer of
p
, let
h=\dim H
and let
k

be the complexity of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
. Since the
T
-action is
Hamiltonian and since
p
is a singular point of
\Phi
,
\dim H\geq 1
. If
\dim H=1
, there is nothing to prove. Hence, suppose that

\dim H\geq 2
. By Theorem
2.8
, it suffices to prove the result for the Hamiltonian
T
-action
on the local model of
p
. Hence, we may assume that
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is the
local model
(Y,\omega_{Y},\Phi_{Y})
at
p
and that
p=[1,0,0]
. Moreover, by Corollary
2.9
, the symplectic
slice representation
\rho:H\to(S^{1})^{h+k}
of
p
is injective. The
stabilizer of any point in
Y=T\times_{H}\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
is a subgroup of

H
. In fact, if
\tilde{H}\leq H
is the stabilizer of some
point in
Y
, we have that

Y^{\tilde{H}}=T\times_{H}\mathrm{Ann}(\mathfrak{h})\times(\mathbb{C}^{h+k})^{\tilde{H}},

where
H
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
via
the symplectic slice representation of
p
. Since the
H
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
is linear,

(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\tilde{H}}
is a subspace for any subgroup
\tilde{H}\leq H
. Hence, to prove the result, it suffices to show that there exists a
one-dimensional closed subgroup
K\leq H
that occurs as a
stabilizer of a point in
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
.

Let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{h+k}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the isotropy
weights of
p
. Since the symplectic slice representation of
p
is
injective, the
H
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
is effective. This implies that
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{h+k}

span
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Hence we may assume that there exists
s\geq 1
such that the span of
\eta_{s+1},\ldots,\eta_{h+k}
has codimension
1. Since the
H
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{h+k}
is Hamiltonian with moment map
\Phi_{H}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{h+k}|z_{j}|^{2}\eta_{j}
, it can be checked directly that all
points in

\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{h+k}\mid z_{j}=0\text{ if }j=1,\ldots,s,\,z_{j}\neq 0\text{ if }j\geq s+1\},

have stabilizers of dimension one, as desired.
∎

In other words, the set of singular values of the moment map equals
the union of the moment map image of all sheets that are stabilized by
some one dimensional closed subgroup
of
T
(see Definition
2.24
). Each such image is contained
in some affine hyperplane (see Remark

2.25
and the discussion preceding
it). If, in addition,
M
is compact there are two important
consequences. First, there are only finitely many subgroups of
T
that occur
as the stabilizer of some point in
M
(see
[
22
, Theorem
10.9.1]
). Second, since the action is
Hamiltonian,
\Phi
has some singular
point; hence, by Lemma
2.53
, there exists a
one dimensional closed subgroup of
T
that that occurs
as the stabilizer of some point in
M
. Let
K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}\leq T
be the
collection of such one dimensional closed subgroups. Since
M
is
compact, by Lemma
2.22
,
M^{K_{i}}
is a compact submanifold of
M

for each
i=1,\ldots,r
and, therefore, has finitely
many connected components
N_{i1},\ldots,N_{is_{i}}
. For each
i,j
,
we denote the corresponding sheet by
(N_{ij},\omega_{ij},\Phi_{ij})

and we set
\Delta_{ij}:=\Phi_{ij}(N_{ij})
.
We observe that the union of the
\Delta_{ij}
’s includes the union of all
facets. More precisely, the following holds.

Lemma 2.54
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space. For any facet

\mathcal{F}
of
\Phi(M)
, there exist indices
i,j
as above so
that
\mathcal{F}=\Delta_{ij}
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
be the
sheet corresponding to
\mathcal{F}
(see Corollary
2.44
and
(
2.19
)), and let
H_{\mathcal{F}}
its stabilizer. By Lemma

2.42
, since the codimension of
\mathcal{F}

in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is one, the dimension of
H_{\mathcal{F}}
is also one. By
Corollary
2.44
, it follows that

(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
is one
of the sheets constructed above.
∎

The complement of the union of the
\Delta_{ij}
’s in the moment polytope is precisely
\Phi(M)_{\mathrm{reg}}
. We call the
closure of a connected component of this complement a
chamber of

\boldsymbol{\Phi(M)}
. These chambers partition the moment polytope into
subpolytopes, i.e., the following properties hold (see Figure
2.2
):

(1)

Each chamber is a polytope in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
of full dimension and any two chambers
intersect in a common face.

(2)

Let
F
be a facet of a chamber. The set of
points
x\in F
that are regular values for all the moment maps

\Phi_{ij}
of the sheets

(N_{ij},\omega_{ij},\Phi_{ij})
constructed above is dense in

F
. Moreover this set is contained in the interior of

F
. Conversely, if
x
is a singular value that is a regular value
for all the moment maps

\Phi_{ij}
of the sheets

(N_{ij},\omega_{ij},\Phi_{ij})
constructed above, then
x
lies in
the interior of a facet of a chamber.

(3)

If two chambers
\mathfrak{c}
and

\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
intersect in a face
F
, then there exists a
sequence of chambers
\mathfrak{c}_{0}=\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{c}_{s}=\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
with the
property that the intersection of
\mathfrak{c}_{l}
and

\mathfrak{c}_{l+1}
is a facet that contains
F
for all

l=0,\ldots,s-1
.

Figure 2.2.
The six triangles above are the chambers for
the moment polytope for the standard

(S^{1})^{2}
-action on the complete flag variety in

\mathbb{C}^{3}
with first Chern
class equal to the class of the symplectic form.

Properties
(1)
–
(3)
follow from the above
definition of the
N_{ij}
’s and from Theorems
2.8

and
2.32
(see also
[
21
, Section 14]
).

By Remark
2.50
, Theorem

2.49
follows at once if the interior of
\Phi(M)

consists entirely of regular values. The following result describes
precisely when this happens in terms of exceptional sheets.

Lemma 2.55
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact Hamiltonian
T
-space. There is
precisely one chamber of
\Phi(M)
if and only if there are no
exceptional sheets.

Proof.

If
\Phi(M)
has no exceptional sheets then all sheets are contained
in the boundary of
\Phi(M)
by Lemma

2.35
. This implies that the union of
the
\Delta_{ij}
’s constructed above is contained in the boundary of

\Phi(M)
, which equals the union of the facets of
\Phi(M)
since

\Phi(M)
is a convex polytope. By Lemma

2.54
, the union of all the facets of

\Phi(M)
is contained in the union of the
\Delta_{ij}
’s. Hence,
the union of the
\Delta_{ij}
’s equals the boundary of

\Phi(M)
. Since
\Phi(M)
is a convex polytope of full dimension, the complement of the boundary of

\Phi(M)
in
\Phi(M)
equals the interior of
\Phi(M)
, which is
connected. Hence, there is precisely one chamber of
\Phi(M)
.

Conversely, if
\Phi(M)
has at least two chambers, then
at least one of the
\Delta_{ij}
’s constructed above cannot be
contained in the boundary of
\Phi(M)
. By Lemma

2.35
, the corresponding sheet is exceptional.
∎

Seeing as the case of only one chamber has already been proved, in
what follows (namely, in Intermezzo 2 and in the proof of Theorem

2.49
below), we assume that there are at least
two chambers.

Intermezzo 2: the wall-crossing formula

The main tool that we use
in the proof of Theorem
2.49
is the so-called
wall-crossing
formula
for compact Hamiltonian
T
-spaces (see
[
4
]
,
[
17
, Section
3.5]
and
[
46
]
). We recall it here for completeness and
we draw on the above Intermezzo for notation. Moreover, in this
subsection we consider the closure of the complement of the moment map
as a chamber.

Let

\mathfrak{c}_{\pm}
be two chambers in
\Phi(M)
that intersect in a
facet
F
. Let
\xi\in\ell
be the primitive
element that is normal to the hyperplane supporting
F
and that points out
of
\mathfrak{c}_{-}
(see Figure
2.3
). We fix a point
x\in F
that
has the property that is a regular value for all for all the moment maps

\Phi_{ij}
of the sheets

(N_{ij},\omega_{ij},\Phi_{ij})
constructed in the above Intermezzo;
such a point exists by property
(2)
.

Figure 2.3.
Two chambers,
\mathfrak{c}_{-}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{+}
, separated
by a hyperplane with normal
\xi
.

We use the fixed inner
product on
\mathfrak{t}
to choose a complementary subspace
\mathfrak{k}
to
the span of
\xi
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{t}=\langle\xi\rangle\oplus\mathfrak{k}
. Hence, viewing
\mathfrak{t}
as the space of homogeneous polynomials of
degree one on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we can view polynomials on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
as being
generated by
\xi
and polynomials on
\mathfrak{k}^{*}
. Moreover,
since
\xi\in\ell
, we have that
\exp(\langle\xi\rangle)
is a
circle in
T
that we denote by
S^{1}
. The subspace
\mathfrak{k}
is
isomorphic to the Lie algebra of the quotient
T/S^{1}
. In what follows, we use this identification tacitly since
it is compatible with the identification of Remark
2.25
.

Let
f_{\pm}:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be the polynomial that,
when restricted to the interior of
\mathfrak{c}_{\pm}
, equals
(
2.21
). Since
x
is a regular value of

\Phi_{ij}
, it lies in the interior of a
chamber
\mathfrak{c}_{ij}
of
\Phi_{ij}(N_{ij})
for all
i,j
. Hence, there is a corresponding polynomial

f_{ij}:\mathfrak{k}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
that, when restricted to the interior of the

\mathfrak{c}_{ij}
, equals (
2.21
). (If
x\notin\Phi_{ij}(N_{ij})
, this polynomial is identically zero.)

Finally, for each
i,j
, we let
\kappa_{ij}
be half the codimension
of
N_{ij}
in
M
and, if
i,j
are such that
x\in\Phi_{ij}(N_{ij})
, we let
\alpha_{ij1},\ldots,\alpha_{ij\kappa_{ij}}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the isotropy weights for the
S^{1}
-action on the normal bundle to
N_{ij}
.

Theorem 2.56

(Wall-crossing formula)
.

Set the notation as above. For all
y\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
we have that

(2.24)

f_{+}(y)-f_{-}(y)=\sum\limits_{\{i,j\mid x\in\Phi_{ij}(N_{ij})\}}\xi^{\kappa_{ij}-1}(y-x)\left(\prod\limits_{s=1}^{\kappa_{ij}}\alpha_{ijs}\right)^{-1}\left[\frac{f_{ij}(y-x)}{(\kappa_{ij}-1)!}+P_{ij}(y-x)\right],

where
P_{ij}
is a polynomial depending on
i,j
that is divisible
by
\xi
.

Remark 2.57
.

•

We stress that Theorem
2.56

also holds if, say,
\mathfrak{c}_{-}
is the closure of the complement of
the moment map image.

•

The polynomials
P_{ij}
have been computed explicitly (see

[
46
, Theorem 1.2.9]
). They depend on the symplectic
reduction of
N_{ij}
at
x
by the
T/S^{1}
-action.

Back to the Duistermaat-Heckman function of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)

With the above Intermezzos, we have all the ingredients to proceed with the proof of Theorem
2.49
.

Proof of Theorem
2.49
.

First, we prove existence of a continuous extension. Let
\mathfrak{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{c}_{l}
be the
chambers of
\Phi(M)
and, for each
i=1,\ldots,l
, let
f_{i}:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be the polynomial that equals
(
2.21
) when restricted to the interior of

\mathfrak{c}_{i}
. The result is proved if
we show that the map given by

(2.25)

x\mapsto f_{i}(x)\qquad\text{if }x\in\mathfrak{c}_{i}

is well-defined, i.e., given two chambers
\mathfrak{c}_{i}
and

\mathfrak{c}_{j}
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{j}\neq\emptyset
, the following holds:

(2.26)

f_{i}(x)=f_{j}(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{c}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{j}.

Clearly equation (
2.26
) holds if
i=j
, so suppose that
i\neq j
. Set
F:=\mathfrak{c}_{i}\cap\mathfrak{c}_{j}
; this is
a face of both
\mathfrak{c}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{j}
(see property
(1)
).

We consider
first the special case that
F
is a facet. Since the set of points in
F
that
are regular values for all the sheets

(N,\omega_{N},\Phi_{N})

The Duistermaat-Heckman function of a complexity one
T
-space

In this section we prove some properties of the Duistermaat Heckman
function of a compact complexity one
T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
. We start with the
following special property that fails to hold in higher complexity
(see
[
26
]
).

Proposition 2.59
.

The Duistermaat-Heckman function
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right):\Phi(M)\to\mathbb{R}
of a compact complexity one

T
-space
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is concave.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.49
,
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is
continuous. Hence, it suffices to check that the restriction of
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
to the interior of
\Phi(M)
is concave. Since the complexity
of
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is one, by Remark
2.47
,
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is piecewise
linear. Thus the restriction of
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
to the interior of
\Phi(M)
is concave if and only if it is
log-concave. The result then follows immediately from
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

As observed in
[
49
, Corollary 3.8]
, continuity and concavity of

DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
(see Theorem
2.49

and Proposition
2.59
), together with convexity of
\Phi(M)
(see Theorem
2.32
), immediately
imply the following result.

Corollary 2.60
.

The minimum of the Duistermaat-Heckman function of a compact
complexity one
T
-space is attained at a vertex.

Next we prove two results relating
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
with exceptional orbits
and singular values of
\Phi
.

Lemma 2.61
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact complexity one
T
-space. If
there are no isolated fixed points, then
M_{\mathrm{exc}}=\emptyset
and the Duistermaat-Heckman
function
DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right):\Phi(M)\to\mathbb{R}
is the restriction of an affine
function.

Proof.

Since there are no isolated fixed points, Lemma

2.20
implies that there are no
exceptional fixed
points. Hence, by Lemma

2.36
, there are no
exceptional sheets. By Lemma
2.30
,

M_{\mathrm{exc}}=\emptyset
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.55
, there
is only one chamber of
\Phi(M)
. The result then follows by Remark
2.50
.
∎

Lemma 2.62
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact complexity one
T
-space. If the

DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
is constant, then there are
no singular values in the interior of
\Phi(M)
.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Suppose that there is a singular value
in the interior of
\Phi(M)
. Hence there exist two chambers

\mathfrak{c}_{-}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{+}
of
\Phi(M)
that intersect in
a facet
F
that is not contained in the boundary of
\Phi(M)
. As
in Intermezzo 2, we let
\xi\in\ell
be a primitive element that
is normal to the hyperplane supporting

and points out of
\mathfrak{c}_{-}
. Moreover, we fix
x\in F
that is a regular value for
all the sheets
(N_{ij},\omega_{ij},\Phi_{ij})
constructed in

lies in the interior of

(see

Since

is not contained in the boundary
of
\Phi(M)
, neither is

. In particular, if

, then
\Phi_{ij}(N_{ij})
is not contained in the

\Phi(M)
. Hence,
(N_{ij},\omega_{ij},\Phi_{ij})
is

. By

, the complexity of

is one, the complexity of

is zero. Hence, the codimension of

in

equals 4. Therefore, the

\xi
in the right hand side of equation

\xi\left(\sum\limits_{\{i,j\mid x\in\Phi_{ij}(N_{ij})\}}(\alpha_{ij1}\alpha_{ij2})^{-1}f_{ij}\right),

and the integers

are as in the
discussion leading up to Theorem

Let

In this section, we introduce a class of Hamiltonian

,

and Corollary

with the following result, which extends
[

, Corollary 3.1,
Lemma 3.3 and Remark 3.4]
.

Proposition 2.67
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact complexity
k

T
-space. If
N\subset M^{T}
is a fixed submanifold with
\dim N=2k
, then
\Phi(N)
is a
vertex of
\Phi(M)
. Moreover, for every face
\mathcal{F}
of

\Phi(M)

(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})

Since
\dim N=2k

2.28
,

, the

2.34
,
\Phi(N)
is a vertex of
\Phi(M)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a face of
\Phi(M)
that contains
\Phi(N)
and
let
p\in N\cap M_{\mathcal{F}}

2.44
, there exist points arbitrarily close to

that have stabilizer equal to
H_{\mathcal{F}}
, the stabilizer of

(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
. Since

p
is regular and all stabilizers in a regular local model
are connected,
H_{\mathcal{F}}
is connected by Theorem

. Finally, we observe that, since
(N,\omega_{N})

is a fixed submanifold and
N\subseteq M_{\mathcal{F}}
,
(N,\omega_{N})
is a
sheet in
(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
. Since

\dim N=2k
and
N\subset M^{T/H_{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mathcal{F}}
,
by Proposition
2.26
, the complexity of

(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
is at

. On the other hand,

(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
is a

\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
and the complexity of the latter is
k
. Hence, by
Proposition
2.26
, the complexity of

(M_{\mathcal{F}},\omega_{\mathcal{F}},\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})
is at
most
k
.
∎

.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact complexity

for each face
\mathcal{F}
of
\Phi(M)
, the

;

(ii)

for each face
\mathcal{F}
of
\Phi(M)

For any face
\mathcal{F}
of

and

In this section we use ideas and techniques from equivariant

3.1.
The weight sum formula

In this paper we are mostly concerned with compact Hamiltonian

T
-spaces satisfying the following condition.

Definition 3.1
.

A symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is
monotone
if there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

c_{1}=\lambda[\omega]
, where
c_{1}
is the first Chern class of
(M,\omega)
.
It is
positive monotone
if
\lambda>0
.

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

If

(M,\omega)
is compact and monotone, since
[\omega]\neq 0
,
then
\lambda
in Definition
3.1
is unique.

(2)

Let
(M,\omega)
be a monotone
symplectic manifold and let
\Psi:(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\to(M,\omega)

be a symplectomorphism. Since
\Psi
pulls back almost complex
structures that are compatible with
\omega
to almost complex
structures that are compatible with
\omega^{\prime}
,
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

is monotone. Moreover, if
(M,\omega)
is compact and if
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
are such that
c_{1}=\lambda[\omega]
and
c_{1}^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}[\omega^{\prime}]
, then
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.

If
(M,\omega)
is such that
H^{2}(M;\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
, then it is monotone (e.g.
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
). In general, (positive) monotonicity is very restrictive. In the
presence of a Hamiltonian torus action,
the following result holds.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
(M,\omega)
is compact and monotone, and admits
an effective Hamiltonian
T
-action, then
(M,\omega)
is positive monotone.

Proof.

The proof follows
mutatis mutandis
that of
[
16
, Lemma 5.2]
, in which it is assumed that
M^{S^{1}}
is
discrete (see
[
16
, Definition 3.1]
). Let
H\leq T
be a one
dimensional subtorus and let
\phi:M\to\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the
induced moment map. We identify
H\simeq S^{1}
and consider

(M,\omega,\phi)
as a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-space. We observe that

\phi\colon M\to(\text{Lie}(S^{1}))^{*}
is a Morse-Bott function;
moreover, by
(
2.1
), the isotropy weights in the positive normal bundle to a fixed point are
positive (cf.
[
16
, Proof of Lemma 5.2]
). Therefore, if
F_{\min}
(respectively
F_{\max}
) denotes a fixed component on which
\phi
attains its minimum
(respectively maximum), all the isotropy weights in the normal bundle to
F_{\min}
(respectively
F_{\max}
) are
positive (respectively negative). Moreover, even if some of the isotropy weights of
p_{\min}\in F_{\min}
(respectively at

p_{\max}\in F_{\max}
) are zero, by the effectiveness of the action
some of them must be different from zero.
Hence, the sum of the isotropy weights of
p_{\min}
(resp.
p_{\max}
) is strictly positive (respectively strictly
negative).
To complete the proof, it is enough to consider the equivariant extensions of
[\omega]
and

c_{1}
in the equivariant cohomology ring of
M
, which are respectively

[\omega-\phi]
and
c_{1}^{S^{1}}
,
and to compare them
at
p_{\min}
and
p_{\max}
to deduce that
\lambda
must be positive
(see equation (5.1) in
[
16
, Proof of Lemma 5.2]
).
∎

Throughout this paper, a Hamiltonian

-space

if

The following result is an immediate consequence of Definition

For any reflexive polytope the origin is the only interior lattice point.

Lemma

For a rational polytope

Reflexive Delzant polytopes are in particular rational and they can be
characterized in terms of the weights of their vertices. More
precisely, the following result, proved in various contexts by various

[

,

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\Delta\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be a
d
-dimensional Delzant polytope. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\Delta
is a reflexive polytope.

(ii)

\Delta
satisfies the
weight sum formula
, i.e., for
each vertex
v\in\Delta
,

(3.2)

v=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}\,,

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
are the weights of
v
.

Remark 3.16

In
[
16
, Prop. 2.12]
it is assumed that the origin is an
interior point of
\Delta
to prove that
(ii)
implies
(i)
.
However this follows by (
3.2
). Indeed, consider the multiset
\mathcal{W}
of all the primitive
vectors appearing as weights of vertices of
\Delta
.
Note that, if
\alpha\in\mathcal{W}
has multiplicity
r
, then so
does
-\alpha
.

Hence the sum of all the weights in
\mathcal{W}
is
0\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.
Therefore, if
\Delta
satisfies (
3.2
), then

(3.3)

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}v=0\,,

where
\mathcal{V}
is the set of vertices of
\Delta
. Since
\Delta
is the
convex hull of its vertices, the interior points of
\Delta
are
precisely those that can be written as follows:

(3.4)

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{v}v\quad\text{with }\lambda_{v}>0\;\;\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{V}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{v}=1\,.

Let
\lambda_{v}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}
for all

v\in\mathcal{V}
. Then by (
3.3
), we have
0=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\lambda_{v}v
. Hence, (
3.4
) yields that
0
belongs to the interior of
\Delta
.

The following technical lemma regarding reflexive Delzant polytopes is
used extensively in Sections

and

5

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\Delta
be a reflexive Delzant polytope in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, let

\mathcal{F}
be a facet of
\Delta
supported on the affine

, let

be a vertex of

in

, and let

be the weights of

\mathcal{F}\subset v+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d-1}\rangle.

Then

. Moreover, if

By (

,

satisfies the weight sum formula at

,

\langle\alpha_{d},\nu\rangle=1

If

We finish with the following simple, useful result.

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
be a compact monotone complexity
preserving

-space. If

Proof.

If

The next result plays a key role throughout the paper.

Proposition 4.15
.

\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)

T

. If

Theorem 4.17

-space of dimension

.

•

if

is precisely the number
of isolated fixed points divided by

, and

•

if
e
is an

\Phi(M)

\Phi^{-1}(e)

M_{\mathrm{exc}}
.

First we show that the image of
\{p\in M^{H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}}\mid p\text{ is exceptional}\}
under the quotient map

equals
M_{\mathrm{exc}}
and that number of connected components of
the latter equals the number of exceptional sheets that are
stabilized by
H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}
. Clearly, the image of

\{p\in M^{H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}}\mid p\text{ is exceptional}\}
under the quotient map

M\to M/T
is contained in
M_{\mathrm{exc}}
. Conversely, given an

\mathcal{O}\in M_{\mathrm{exc}}

is exceptional by Remarks

and

.
Fix a point

. By Proposition

4.15
and Lemma

,

. Since
M^{H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}}
is

T
-invariant, it follows that
\mathcal{O}
is contained in

M^{H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}}
. Hence,
M_{\mathrm{exc}}
is contained
in the image of

\{p\in M^{H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}}\mid p\text{ is exceptional}\}
under the quotient map

M\to M/T
and the first claim follows. To prove the second claim, we observe that the connected

\{p\in M^{H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}}\mid p\text{ is exceptional}\}
are
exactly the exceptional sheets that are
stabilized by

. Since sheets are

-invariant and

is connected, the restriction of the quotient map

to
\{p\in M^{H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}}\mid p\text{ is exceptional}\}

.

By Proposition

, the image
of an exceptional sheet that is stabilized by

\Phi(M)\cap\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu_{\min}\rangle=0\}

By the above argument, in order to prove the bulleted statements, it
suffices to show that the number of exceptional sheets that are
stabilized by
H_{\mathcal{F}_{\min}}
is precisely the number of
isolated fixed points divided by

if and only if there are no isolated

Let

. Hence, the cardinality of

Moreover, if

of the intersections

4.5.
The Duistermaat-Heckman function is a complete invariant

We can proceed with the proof of our first main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
and
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
be compact monotone tall complexity one
T
-spaces of
dimension
2n
. If they are isomorphic, then they have equal
Duistermaat-Heckman measures. By Theorem

2.49
, they have equal
Duistermaat-Heckman functions.

Suppose conversely that they have equal Duistermaat-Heckman
functions. First, we show that it suffices to show the result under
the additional assumption that both spaces are normalized. To this
end, assume this special case. Since
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
and
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
are monotone, by Corollary

3.9
, there exist
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}>0
and

v,v^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
such that
(M,\lambda\omega,\lambda\Phi+v)
and

(M^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\omega^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\Phi^{\prime}+v^{\prime})
are normalized monotone. Moreover,
since
\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)
and
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
are tall and have equal
Duistermaat-Heckman functions, there exists a Delzant polytope

\Delta
such that
\Phi(M)=\Delta=\Phi^{\prime}(M^{\prime})
. Since
(M,\lambda\omega,\lambda\Phi+v)
and

(M^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\omega^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\Phi^{\prime}+v^{\prime})
are normalized monotone, by
Proposition
3.18
, the polytopes
\lambda\Delta+v
and
\lambda^{\prime}\Delta+v^{\prime}
are
reflexive Delzant. We observe that there exists at most one
reflexive Delzant polytope obtained from
\Delta
by rescaling and
translating because the vertices of a reflexive Delzant polytope
are determined by the tangent cones to its vertices (see
Proposition
3.15
). Hence,
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
v=v^{\prime}
; in particular,

\lambda\Delta+v=\lambda^{\prime}\Delta+v^{\prime}
. On the other hand, we
observe that, for any
w\in\lambda\Delta+v=\lambda^{\prime}\Delta+v^{\prime}
,

(4.28)

\begin{split}DH(M,\lambda\omega,\lambda\Phi+v)(w)&=\lambda DH\left(M,\omega,\Phi\right)(\lambda w+v)\\
DH(M^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\omega^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}\Phi^{\prime}+v^{\prime})(w)&=\lambda^{\prime}DH(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})(\lambda^{\prime}w+v^{\prime}).\end{split}

This is an immediate consequence of the definition of
the Duistermaat-Heckman function, of the fact that its restriction to
the set of regular values is given by (
2.21

is the number of

The first step towards proving which quadruples are associated to a normalized monotone tall complexity one

and whether the

)

Definition 5.6
.

We say that a quadruple
(\Delta,\mathcal{F},s,k)
consisting of a polytope
\Delta

in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, a facet
\mathcal{F}\subset\Delta
, and integers

Remark 5.7
.

If
(\Delta,\mathcal{F},-1,2)
is admissible, then, by
the proof of Corollary
5.3
, the polytope
\Delta
is
contained in the strip
\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid-1\leq\langle w,\nu\rangle\leq 1\}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
k\in\{1,2\}
. If
(\Delta,\mathcal{F},-1,k)
is admissible,
then for any edge
e
of
\Delta
that intersects the linear
hyperplane
\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle=0\}
, there exists a vertex
v\in\mathcal{F}
and a weight

\alpha_{v}\in\ell^{*}
at

such that

e\cap\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle=0\}=\{v+\alpha_{v}\}.

In particular,
e\cap\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle=0\}
is contained in
\ell^{*}
.

Proof.

Fix such an edge
e
. Since
(\Delta,\mathcal{F},-1,k)
is
admissible and
k>0
,
\Delta
has no vertex on the linear
hyperplane
\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle=0\}
. Hence,
e
intersects
\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle=0\}
in the relative interior of
e
, so that the intersection
e\cap\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle=0\}
consists of one element, which is not a vertex since
(\Delta,\mathcal{F},-1,k)
is
admissible. Let
v\in\Delta
be the vertex that is incident
to
e
and satisfies
\langle v,\nu\rangle<0
. Since
\Delta
is
integral and is contained in the upper-half plane
\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle\geq-1\}
, and since
\nu\in\ell^{*}
is
primitive,
\langle v,\nu\rangle=-1
, i.e.,
v\in\mathcal{F}
. Moreover,
e
is the edge that comes out of

\mathcal{F}
that is incident to
v
. Let
\alpha_{v}\in\ell^{*}
be
the weight of
v
such that
e
is contained in the half-ray
v+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\langle\alpha_{v}\rangle
. By Lemma

3.17
,
\langle v+\alpha_{v},\nu\rangle=0
, whence
e\cap\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu\rangle=0\}=\{v+\alpha_{v}\}
. Since
v,\alpha_{v}\in\ell^{*}
, the result
follows.

Admissible quadruples encode the abstract analogs of the functions
given by (

Definition 5.9
.

Let
(\Delta,\mathcal{F},s,k)
be an admissible quadruple. The

determined
by

is the map

Remark 5.10
.

Let

and

In what follows, we fix the map

by

Definition 5.11

Let

be a polytope in

of

Definition 5.13

Let
\Delta
be a Delzant polytope in

\Delta=\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\,\{w\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\mid\langle w,\nu_{i}\rangle\geq c_{i}\}

Remark 5.14

As remarked in

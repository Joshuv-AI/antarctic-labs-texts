Magnetic fluctuations in Pb 9 ​ Cu ( PO 4 ​ ) 6 ​ O \text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
Makoto Shimizu, Junya Otsuki, Harald O. Jeschke

Abstract
The hope that copper doped lead apatite Pb 9 ​ Cu ( PO 4 ​ ) 6 ​ O \text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O} is a room-temperature superconductor has largely been dashed by global research efforts. Nevertheless, according to the current state of knowledge, the material has interesting magnetic properties, and research groups around the world have prepared high quality samples. We use a fluctuation exchange approximation (FLEX) approach to study the magnetic tendencies in Pb 9 ​ Cu ( PO 4 ​ ) 6 ​ O \text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O} . We find ferromagnetic fluctuations very close to the filling of the stoichiometric compound which can be understood from Fermi surface nesting at the M M point. This is similar to the one-band triangular lattice Hamiltonian at three-quarter filling. Interestingly, the special k z k_{z} dependence of the Pb 9 ​ Cu ( PO 4 ​ ) 6 ​ O \text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O} band structure makes it very sensitive to doping. Only slight charge doping switches between antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic fluctuations. If the material could become superconducting, it might be easily switchable between singlet and triplet superconductivity.

II
Electronic structure

We perform all electron density functional theory (DFT) calculations for
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}

The variations of the magnetic properties between AFM and FM can be understood with the triangular lattice.
However,

IV
Conclusions

We have studied the consequences of the electronic structure of
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
for magnetic fluctuations. We find that electronically,
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
provides an interesting three-quarter filled, two orbital triangular lattice Hamiltonian. As such, it can be partly understood from the one-band triangular lattice Hamiltonian that has previously been studied in the context of organic charge transfer salts and of sodium cobaltate. However, we have worked out two important differences that arise from the three-dimensionality of
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
,
i.e.
from the
k_{z}
dispersion of the electronic structure. One consequence is that the change of doping that triggers the transition between antiferromagnetic and ferromagnetic spin fluctuations is much smaller in
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
compared to the simple triangular lattice. The other consequence is that the ferromagnetic fluctuations in the electron doped region is significantly stabilized in the three-dimensional two-orbital model.

While our FLEX result for the undoped material indicates antiferromagnetic fluctuations, we also find that ferromagnetic fluctuations are reached by very little electron doping. Thus, slightly inhomogeneous samples
\text{Pb${}_{10-x}$Cu${}_{x}$}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
with
x\approx 1
would have ferromagnetic as well as antiferromagnetic regions, and this could explain the levitation observed in some of the samples
Lee et al. 2023b
;
Guo et al. 2023
;
Wu et al. 2023
of
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
.

By focusing on a single site for the position of the doped Cu
2+
ion, we have introduced a significant simplification, just like in many other studies
Si et al. 2023
;
Witt et al. 2023
;
Yue et al. 2023
;
Witt et al. 2023
. It is possible that disorder effects will play an important role in
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}

Sun et al. 2023
. Nevertheless, our study shows that the electronic structure that is present in slightly simplified
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
has very interesting properties. It is characterized by two degenerate orbitals on a triangular lattice, three-quarter filling and a finite
k_{z}
dispersion. As this is a rather generic situation, it could already be realized in other existing materials, or it would not be too hard to design materials with this property. As the hope for superconductivity in
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
is probably in vain, it makes sense to understand why and possibly find or design a similar material that does support a superconducting ground state. A likely disadvantage of
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{O}
is the large ratio between interaction strength on the
d
orbitals and the band width
Witt et al. 2023
; in the search for a better

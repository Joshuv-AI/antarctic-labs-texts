A spectral Lovász–Simonovits theorem
Yongtao Li, Lihua Feng, Yuejian Peng

Abstract
A fundamental result in extremal graph theory is attributed to Mantel’s theorem, which states that every graph on n n vertices with more than ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor edges must contain a triangle. Lovász and Simonovits (1975) provided a supersaturation phenomenon by showing that for any q < n / 2 q<n/2 , every graph with ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + q \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+q edges contains at least q ​ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ q\lfloor n/2\rfloor triangles. This result resolved a conjecture proposed by Erdős in 1962. In this paper, we establish a spectral counterpart of the result of Lovász and Simonovits. Let Y n , 2 , q Y_{n,2,q} be the graph obtained from the bipartite Turán graph T n , 2 T_{n,2} by embedding a matching with q q edges into the partite set of size ⌈ n / 2 ⌉ \lceil n/2\rceil . Using the supersaturation-stability method and the spectral techniques, we firstly prove that for q ≤ 1 11 ​ n q\leq\frac{1}{11}\sqrt{n} , every graph G G on n n vertices with spectral radius λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ λ ⁡ ( Y n , 2 , q ) \lambda(G)\geq\lambda(Y_{n,2,q}) contains at least q ​ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ q\lfloor n/2\rfloor triangles. We also show that the bound q = O ⁡ ( n ) q=O(\sqrt{n}) is tight up to a constant factor, yielding a phenomenon different from that in edge supersaturation. Our result answers a spectral triangle counting problem proposed by Ning and Zhai (2023). Secondly, let T n , 2 , q T_{n,2,q} be the graph obtained from T n , 2 T_{n,2} by embedding a star with q q edges into the partite set of size ⌈ n / 2 ⌉ \lceil n/2\rceil . We show further that T n , 2 , q T_{n,2,q} is the unique extremal graph that contains at most q ​ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ q\lfloor n/2\rfloor triangles and attains the maximum spectral radius. Thirdly, we present an asymptotic spectral stability result under a specific constraint on the triangle covering number. This result could be viewed as a spectral extension of a recent result proved by Balogh and Clemen (2023), and independently by Liu and Mubayi (2022).



Key words:
Spectral graph theory;
counting triangles; adjacency matrix.

1
Introduction

The Turán type problem states that for a graph
F
,
what is the minimum integer
m

such that every
n
-vertex graph with more than

m
edges contains a copy of
F
.
This extremal number is denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
.
It is a longstanding open problem in extremal combinatorics to develop some understanding of the function
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)

for a general graph
F
.
A well-known result of Mantel
(see
[
4
,
36
]
) asserts that
an
n
-vertex graph with more than

\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
edges contains a triangle.
The bound is tight by considering the bipartite Turán graph
T_{n,2}
, which is a complete bipartite graph whose
two parts have sizes as equal as possible.
Shortly afterwards,
there have been various generalizations of Mantel’s theorem;
see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
]
.

1.1
Extremal graph results on triangles

About eighty years ago,
Rademacher (unpublished, see Erdős
[
15
,
18
]
) provided
an interesting extension on Mantel’s theorem by showing that
there are not just one but at least
\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles in
any
n
-vertex graph with more than
\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor
edges.

Theorem 1.1

(Erdős–Rademacher, 1941)
.

If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with

e(G)\geq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor+1
,
then
G
has at least
\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles,
with equality if and only if

G
is the graph obtained from

T_{n,2}
by
adding one edge into the partite set of size
\lceil{n}/{2}\rceil
.

The problem of counting triangles is referred to as the Erdős–Rademacher problem,
which is regarded as a starting point of the supersaturation in graph theory.
Erdős
[
18
]
proved a variant
for a graph with less than
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
edges.
The Erdős result states that
if
\ell\geq 0
and
e(G)\geq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor-\ell
,
and
G
has a triangle,
then it has at least
\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor-\ell-1
triangles.
Setting
\ell=0
,
we see that
if
e(G)\geq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor
, then

G
has at least
\left\lfloor{n}/{2}\right\rfloor-1
triangles,
unless
G=T_{n,2}
.
Moreover,
Erdős
[
16
,
17
]

showed that there exists
c>0
such that if
1\leq q<cn
and
n
is sufficiently large, then
every
n
-vertex graph with
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+q

edges has at least
q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles.
This was further generalized by
Lovász and Simonovits
[
37
,
38
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Lovász–Simonovits, 1975)
.

Let
1\leq q<{n}/{2}
be an integer
and
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with

e(G)\geq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor+q
.
Then
G
contains at least
q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor

triangles.

A quick proof of Theorem
1.2
in the range of
q\leq{n}/{4}
can be found in

[
4
, page 302]
. Incidentally,
we mention that
the condition
q<{n}/{2}
is necessary.
For even
n
and
q={n}/{2}
, one can add
q+1
extra edges
to the larger vertex part of
K_{\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}-1}
such that there is no triangle
among these
q+1
edges.
Hence, we get a graph with
{n^{2}}/{4}+q
edges and
(q+1)({n}/{2}-1)={n^{2}}/{4}-1
triangles, which are less than
q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles.

The stabilities of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

were recently studied by Xiao and Katona
[
59
]
,
Liu and Mubayi
[
34
]
and Balogh and
Clemen
[
3
]
.
A graph is called color-critical if it has an edge
whose deletion reduces its chromatic number.
For example, the complete graphs and odd cycles are color-critical. Apart from the triangles,
the supersaturation problem for color-critical graphs was also
investigated by Mubayi
[
40
]
, Pikhurko and Yilma
[
51
]
, Ma and Yuan
[
39
]
.
For related results, we refer to
[
52
,
32
]

and references therein.

1.2
Eigenvalues and counting triangles

Spectral graph theory aims to apply algebraic properties, such as the eigenvalues of associated matrices, to investigate the structural properties of a graph.
In recent years, significant breakthroughs have been made in understanding graph structure through matrix eigenvalues; see, e.g.,
[
21
,
22
]
.
Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices, denoted by
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.
The adjacency matrix of
G
is denoted by
A(G)=[a_{i,j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
,
where
a_{i,j}=1
if
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are adjacent, and
a_{i,j}=0
otherwise.
The spectral radius
\lambda(G)
is defined as the maximum modulus
of the eigenvalues of
A(G)
. By the Perron–Frobenius theorem,
\lambda(G)
is the largest eigenvalue of
A(G)
and there exists a non-negative eigenvector corresponding to it. The Rayleigh quotient gives

\lambda(G)=\max_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}A(G)\bm{x}}{\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{x}}
, which implies
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}\geq\delta(G)
,
where each equality holds if and only if
G
is a regular graph.

The classical Turán theorem states that

e(G)>(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})n^{2}/2
forces a clique
K_{r+1}
.
Similarly, one may wish to investigate the condition on spectral radius
\lambda(G)
to guarantee certain substructures.
Such problems fall under the umbrella of spectral extremal graph theory, which is one of the most
popular topic in spectral graph theory.
A well-known result on this topic was obtained
in 1986 by Wilf
[
57
]
, who showed that
every
n
-vertex graph
G
with

\lambda(G)>(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})n
has
a clique
K_{r+1}
.
Another significant result due to Nikiforov
[
41
]

states that every
m
-edge graph
G
with
\lambda^{2}(G)>{(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})2m}
contains a clique
K_{r+1}
.
The above spectral results generalize
the aforementioned Turán theorem.
Nikiforov’s result implies Wilf’s result
by invoking Turán’s bound or Rayleigh’s bound
\lambda(G)\geq 2m/n
.
Indeed, if
\lambda(G)>(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})n
,
then
\lambda^{2}(G)>(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})n\cdot(2m/n)=(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})2m
, as desired.
In addition, Wilf’s bound implies the “strong” Turán theorem. To see this, if
G
is a
K_{r+1}
-free graph,
then
\delta(G)\leq\lambda(G)\leq(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{r}})n
by assuming Wilf’s bound. Hence
\delta(G)\leq n-\lceil n/r\rceil=\delta(T_{n,r})
.
Taking a vertex
u\in V(G)
with degree
d(u)=\delta(G)
.
By induction, we get
e(G\setminus\{u\})\leq e(T_{n-1,r})
, which yields
e(G)=e(G\setminus\{u\})+\delta(G)\leq e(T_{n-1,r})+\delta(T_{n,r})=e(T_{n,r})
, as expected.

The spectral Turán problems have been receiving considerable
attention in the last two decades
and it is an important topic in modern graph theory; see, e.g.,

[
41
,
7
,
25
]
for cliques,

[
20
]
for matchings,

[
12
]
for even cycles,

[
23
,
65
,
24
]
for cycles of consecutive lengths,

[
42
]
for color-critical graphs,

[
1
,
45
]
for complete bipartite graphs,

[
54
,
30
]
for planar and
outerplanar graphs,

[
55
,
62
,
8
,
66
]
for graphs without certain minors,

[
43
]
for a spectral Erdős–Stone–Bollobás theorem,

[
44
]
for the spectral stability theorem,

[
11
]
for a spectral Erdős–Sós theorem,

[
60
]
for a spectral Erdős–Pósa theorem,

[
27
]
for a spectral Erdős–Rademacher theorem,

[
28
]
for a spectral Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau theorem,

[
9
,
61
,
28
]
for friendship graphs,

[
26
,

]
for bowties,

[

,

]
for book graphs,

[

for odd wheels
and
[

,

Although there has been a wealth of research
on the spectral extremal problems,
there are
very few
results on the spectral problem
of counting substructures.
In 2007, Bollobás
and Nikiforov
[
7
]
proved that every
n
-vertex graph
G
has at least

\big(\frac{\lambda(G)}{n}-1+\frac{1}{r}\big)\frac{r(r-1)}{r+1}(\frac{n}{r})^{r+1}
copies of
K_{r+1}
.
This result provides a spectral supersaturation for cliques.
The spectral supersaturation for general graphs (see
[
27
, Sec. 5]
) states that
for any
\varepsilon>0
and any graph
F
with
\chi(F)=r+1
, there exist
\delta>0
and
n_{0}
such that if
G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\lambda(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)n
,
then
G
contains at least
\delta n^{|F|}
copies of
F
.

Recall in Theorem
1.1
that
G
has at least

\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles when
e(G)>e(T_{n,2})
. Correspondingly,
it is natural to consider the spectral version:
if
\lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2})
,
whether
G
has at least
\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles.
However, this intuition is not true.
Let
K_{a,b}^{+}
be the graph obtained from
K_{a,b}
by adding an edge to
the vertex set of size
a
.
For even
n
, taking
a=\frac{n}{2}+1
and
b=\frac{n}{2}-1
,
we compute that
\lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}-1}^{+})>\lambda(T_{n,2})
, while
K_{\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}-1}^{+}
has exactly
\frac{n}{2}-1
triangles.
Ning and Zhai
[
48
]
proved that

\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor-1
triangles can be guaranteed.

Theorem 1.3

(Ning–Zhai, 2023)
.

If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2}),

then
G
has at least

\left\lfloor{n}/{2}\right\rfloor-1
triangles,
unless
G
is the bipartite Turán graph
T_{n,2}
.

Ning and Zhai
[
48
]

proposed a problem on finding spectral counting results
for color-critical graphs, including, e.g., the spectral version of Theorem
1.2
.
Interestingly, this problem was also selected as one of the “Unsolved Problems” in spectral graph theory
[
33
, Sec. 9]
.
There are few spectral results on counting substructures in a graph with given order,
including only triangles
[
48
]
, cliques
[
7
]
,
triangular edges
[
28
]

and bowties
[
29
]
.
The main contribution of this paper is to establish
a spectral version of Theorem
1.2
.
To some extent,
our result could be viewed as a partial
resolution of the Ning–Zhai problem.

2
Main results

2.1
A spectral Lovász–Simonovits theorem

Before trying to obtain the spectral counterpart of Theorem
1.2
, the first obstacle we may encounter is how to formulate a spectral hypothesis that corresponds to the conventional assumption on the size of a graph.
More precisely,
we would like to determine an increment function
\delta(q)>0

such that the condition
e(G)\geq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+q
can be replaced with a spectral condition of the form
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2})+\delta(q)
.
For instance, is it sufficient to consider
\delta(q)=2q/n
?
Since every additional edge guarantees
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
additional triangles, it is unlikely that
such spectral increment can create
\lfloor n/2\rfloor

new triangles.
From this point of view, it seems difficult to measure the increment on the spectral radius of a graph.
To get around the difficulty,
Li, Lu and Peng
[
27
]
presented a new way to measure the increment on the spectral radius.
Let
T_{n,2,q}
be the graph obtained from

T_{n,2}
by embedding a star
K_{1,q}
into the larger vertex part; see Fig.
1
.
Under this notation, they define the spectral increment
as
\delta(q)=\lambda(T_{n,2,q})-\lambda(T_{n,2})
, and proposed the following problem.

Problem 2.1

(Li–Lu–Peng, 2024)
.

Let
1\leq q<{n}/{2}
and
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2,q}).

Then
G
has at least
q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles,
with equality if and only if
G=T_{n,2,q}
.

Problem
2.1
could be regarded as
a spectral version of the Lovász–Simonovits theorem.
Especially,
Li, Lu and Peng
[
27
]

confirmed this problem in the initial case of
q=1
.

In the present paper,
our goal is to confirm Problem
2.1
for any given
q\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
t(G)
be the number of triangles in
G
.
We show that

t(G)\geq q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
in Problem
2.1

can be guaranteed by a slightly
weaker
condition.
Let
Y_{n,2,q}
be the graph obtained from

T_{n,2}
by embedding
q
pairwise disjoint edges
into the partite set with larger size; see Fig.
1
.
It is not difficult to verify that

\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\lambda(T_{n,2,q})
for every
q\geq 2
; see Proposition
3.2
.

Figure 1:
The graphs
Y_{n,2,q}
and
T_{n,2,q}
.

In what follows, we give a stronger result than Problem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
1\leq q\leq\frac{1}{11}\sqrt{n}
and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(Y_{n,2,q}),

then there are at least
q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles in
G
.

Remark.

The above bound
q\leq\frac{1}{11}\sqrt{n}
is
tight
up to a constant factor; see Example
3.3
. This reveals a
different
phenomenon from that of the traditional edge supersaturation.
Moreover, the spectral condition in Theorem
2.2
is weaker than the size condition in Theorem
1.2
, since
e(G)>e(Y_{n,2,q})
implies
\lambda(G)>\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
.
Indeed, suppose that
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
. Then, invoking the fact that
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2e(G)}{n}
, we obtain
e(G)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\lambda(G)\rfloor\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})\rfloor=e(Y_{n,2,q})
,
where the last equality follows from
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2q}{n}+\frac{4q}{n^{2}}
(see Lemma
3.1
).
In conclusion, the spectral condition is indeed weaker than the size condition.
In addition, there are many graphs
G
satisfying
\lambda(G)>\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
while
e(G)<e(Y_{n,2,q})
.
From this perspective, the spectral condition of Theorem
2.2
has
wider
applicability.

The Bollobás–Nikiforov bound
[
7
]

states that
t(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{12}\left(\lambda(G)-\frac{n}{2}\right)
. This provides a lower bound for
t(G)
under the assumption of Theorem
2.2
.
For even
n
, we have
\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})>\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2q}{n}
, and then
t(G)>\frac{q}{6}n
; For odd
n
, we obtain
\lambda(G)>\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{2n}+\frac{2q}{n}
, which implies
t(G)>\frac{4q-1}{24}n
. Since
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2q}{n}+\frac{4q}{n^{2}}
for all
n\geq 4q
(see Lemma
3.1
), the lower bound on
t(G)
provided by Bollobás–Nikiforov’s bound does not exceed
\frac{q}{6}n+\frac{q}{3}
. In contrast, Theorem
2.2
offers a
stronger
bound
t(G)\geq q\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

Recall that
\lambda(T_{n,2,q})>\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
.
Applying Theorem
2.2
,
we know that the desired bound
in Problem
2.1
holds
immediately.
Using the double-eigenvector technique, we shall confirm Problem
2.1
in the range
q\leq\frac{1}{11}\sqrt{n}
,
showing that
T_{n,2,q}
is the unique graph that
attains the maximum spectral radius among all
n
-vertex
graphs
with at most
q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles.

Theorem 2.3
.

If
1\leq q\leq\frac{1}{11}\sqrt{n}
and
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2,q}),

then
G
has at least
q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor
triangles,
with equality if and only if
G=T_{n,2,q}
.

Remark.
When embedding
a graph
H
with
q
edges into one partite set
of
T_{n,2}
, we can verify that
the spectral radius of the resulting graph
decreases according to the following order of
H
:
star, complete graph, complete bipartite graph (that is not a star), cycle, path, and matching.
Moreover, it can be verified that
if we embed two matchings of sizes
q_{1}
and
q_{2}

(with
q_{1}+q_{2}=q
) into two partite sets, respectively,
then the resulting graph has larger spectral radius than

Y_{n,2,q}
. In contrast,
if we embed two stars of sizes
q_{1}
and

q_{2}
into two partite sets, respectively, then
the resulting graph has smaller spectral radius than

T_{n,2,q}
.

2.2
A spectral extension of a stability result

The
triangle covering number

\tau_{3}(G)
is the minimum number of vertices in a set that contains at least one vertex of each triangle in
G
.
In particular,
\tau_{3}(G)=1
if and only if all triangles of
G

share a common vertex.
Xiao and Katona
[
59
]
gave a stability of Theorem
1.1
by showing that if
e(G)>\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
and
\tau_{3}(G)\geq 2
, then
G
has at least

n-2
triangles.
Moreover, they proposed a problem to study the stability of Theorem
1.2
.
Recently, this problem was solved by Liu and Mubayi

[
34
]
, as well as Balogh and Clemen

[
3
]
independently.

Before stating their result, we need to define two extremal graphs.
Firstly,
let
G_{1}
be the graph of order
n
, where
V(G_{1})=A\cup B
with
|A|=\lceil{n}/{2}\rceil+a
and

|B|=\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor-a
, where
a\in\mathbb{N}
is chosen later.
Pick
2(s-1)
vertices
x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{s-1},y_{s-1}
in
A

and two vertices
u_{1},u_{2}
in
B
. Adding
s
edges

\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
and
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}

into
K_{|A|,|B|}
, and delete
\alpha
edges

\{u_{1},x_{1}\},\ldots,\{u_{1},x_{\alpha}\}
,
where
\alpha:=s-t-a^{2}-\mathbf{1}_{\{2\nmid n\}}a
.
Then
e(G_{1})=\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+t
and
\tau_{3}(G_{1})=s
.
Secondly,
let
G_{2}
be the graph with the same vertex partition.
Unlike the previous construction, we now
pick
2s
vertices

x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{s},y_{s}\in A
and one vertex
u\in B
,
add the
s
edges
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
into
A
,
and delete the
\alpha
edges

\{u,x_{1}\},\ldots,\{u,x_{\alpha}\}
between
A
and
B
.
Similarly, we have
e(G_{2})=\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+t
and
\tau_{3}(G_{2})=s
.
It is easy to see that

t(G_{1})=(s-1)|B|+|A|-2\alpha
and

t(G_{2})=s|B|-\alpha
.
Now, we choose an appropriate
a\in\mathbb{N}

to minimize the number of triangles in the above constructions.

Theorem 2.4

(Balogh–Clemen, 2023)
.

Let
t,s\in\mathbb{N}
such that

0<t<s
.
Suppose that

G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+t
edges. If
\tau_{3}(G)\geq s
and
n
is sufficiently large, then
G
contains at least
\min\{t(G_{1}),t(G_{2})\}
triangles.

An independent work was also studied by Liu and Mubayi
[
34
, Theorem 1.4]
.
In other words, there are two ways to embed

s
disjoint edges in the partition
in order to minimize the number of triangles, that is,
embedding
s-1
or
s
edges in the larger vertex part, and other edges in the smaller part.
Recently, Li, Feng and Peng
[
29
]

presented a spectral version of the result of Xiao and Katona
[
59
]

by showing that if
G
is an
n

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2})
and
\tau_{3}(G)\geq 2
,
then
G
contains at least
n-3
triangles.
This bound is the best possible and provides a spectral stability of the Ning–Zhai result in Theorem
1.3
.

Next, we shall consider the general case
\tau_{3}(G)\geq s
for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
The third main result in this paper reveals an asymptotically spectral extension of Theorem
2.4
.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
G
is a graph with
n\geq 28s^{2}
vertices with

\lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2})
and
\tau_{3}(G)\geq s
,
then there are at least
\frac{1}{2}sn-5s^{2}
triangles in
G
.

Remark.

Theorem
2.5
gives the stability of Theorem
2.3
.
Applying the fact
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2e(G)}{n}
, we see that

e(G)>e(T_{n,2})
implies
\lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2})
.
As a by-product of Theorem
2.5
,
we find that if
e(G)>e(T_{n,2})
and
\tau_{3}(G)\geq s
,
then
G
contains
\frac{1}{2}sn-O(1)
triangles.
This result could be viewed as an asymptotic version of
Theorem
2.4
, since
t(G_{1})\approx t(G_{2})\approx\frac{1}{2}sn-O(1)
.
With additional efforts, the constant term of Theorem
2.5
can be slightly improved,
but it seems difficult to establish the exact bound and
determine the spectral extremal graphs corresponding to the extremal graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
in Theorem
2.4
.

2.3
Our approach and organization

Our approach.

The main idea in our proofs of Theorems
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.5

uses a simple yet surprisingly powerful technique known as
the supersaturation-stability method.
Informally, every graph with few triangles can be made bipartite by deleting a relatively small number of edges.
Here, “relatively small” means a constant number of edges independent of
n
; see Theorem
3.6
.
This is different from the classical stability method,
which typically requires the removal of roughly
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges for some small
\varepsilon>0
.
Although this description may sound somewhat vague, we will make it more precise in Section
4
.
Unlike the traditional stability method,
our method requires only the condition
n\geq 121q^{2}
, instead of requiring that
q
is fixed and
n
is sufficiently large (or a tower-type function in
q
).
This is because our proof avoids the use of the celebrated triangle removal lemma
*
*

*

The lemma says that for each
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that every graph on
n
vertices with at most
\delta n^{3}
triangles can be made triangle-free by removing at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges. That is, a graph with a subcubic number of triangles can be made triangle-free by removing a subquadratic number of edges; see
[
13
]
.
.
Consequently,
q
is allowed to vary over a wide range and may even grow with
n
.
Hence, we significantly improve the lower bound
on the order
n
of a graph with respect to
q
.
We believe that the supersaturation-stability method used in this paper may be of independent interest and could potentially find further applications elsewhere, much like the classical stability approach, which has now seen widespread use.
Last but not least, in addition to the supersaturation-stability method, we also employ the spectral technique developed by Cioabă, Feng, Tait and Zhang
[
9
]
,
as well as the double-eigenvector technique
applied by Fang, Tait and Zhai
[
19
]
.

Organization.

In Section
3
, we show the tightness of
q
in Theorem
2.2
and introduce preliminaries for counting triangles.
As a warm up, we give a spectral stability result
for graphs with few triangles;
see Theorem
3.6
.
In Section
4
,
we outline the main ideas of our method and
then present the proof of
Theorem
2.2
.
In Section
5
, we employ the double-eigenvector technique to prove Theorem
2.3
.
The proof of Theorem
2.5
is provided
in Section
6
.
Finally,
we propose several related spectral extremal problems for readers.

3
Preliminaries

Recall that
Y_{n,2,q}
is a graph obtained from the bipartite Turán graph
T_{n,2}
by embedding
q
pairwise disjoint edges into the partite set of size
\lceil n/2\rceil
. In what follows, we give an estimate on the spectral radius of
Y_{n,2,q}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(a) If
n
is even, then
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})

is the largest root of

f_{1}(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}-(n^{2}x)/4+n^{2}/4-qn.

(b) If
n
is odd, then
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})

is the largest root of

f_{2}(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}-(n^{2}x)/4+x/4+n^{2}/4-1/4+q-qn.

Consequently, we have
\sqrt{\left\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\right\rfloor+2q}<\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2q}{n}+\frac{4q}{n^{2}}
for every
n\geq 4q
.

Proof.

(a) Let
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{\mathrm{T}}
be the Perron eigenvector of
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
.
Assume that
V(Y_{n,2,q})=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup Y
, where
X_{1}=\{u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2},\ldots,u_{q},v_{q}\}
is the set of vertices of the matching of size
q
,
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
and
Y
are partite sets of
K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}
.
Since two vertices with the same neighborhood have the same value in corresponding coordinates of
\bm{x}
, we may assume that

x_{u_{i}}=x_{v_{i}}=a
for
1\leq i\leq q
,
x_{u}=b
for each
u\in X_{2}
, and
x_{w}=c

for each
w\in Y
. Then

\begin{cases}\lambda a=a+\frac{n}{2}c,\\
\lambda b=\frac{n}{2}c,\\
\lambda c=2qa+(\frac{n}{2}-2q)b.\end{cases}

Thus,
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
is the largest eigenvalue of

B_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\frac{n}{2}\\
0&0&\frac{n}{2}\\
2q&\frac{n}{2}-2q&0\end{bmatrix}.

By calculation, we know that
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
is the largest root of

f_{1}(x):=\det(xI-B_{1})=x^{3}-x^{2}-(n^{2}x)/4+n^{2}/4-qn.

(b) Let
V(Y_{n,2,q})=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup Y
be a partition,
where
X_{1}=\{u_{1},v_{1},\ldots,u_{q},v_{q}\}
spans a matching with

q
edges,
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
and
Y

are partite sets of
K_{\frac{n+1}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}}

satisfying
|X_{1}|+|X_{2}|=\frac{n+1}{2}

and
|Y|=\frac{n-1}{2}
. A similar argument yields that

\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
is the largest eigenvalue of

B_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\frac{n-1}{2}\\
0&0&\frac{n-1}{2}\\
2q&\frac{n+1}{2}-2q&0\end{bmatrix}.

Thus,
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})

is the largest root of

f_{2}(x):=\det(xI-B_{2})=x^{3}-x^{2}+x/4-(n^{2}x)/4+n^{2}/4-1/4-qn+q.

Moreover, it is easy to check that

f_{1}(\sqrt{n^{2}/4+2q})=2q\sqrt{n^{2}/4+2q}-qn-2q<0

and

f_{2}(\sqrt{(n^{2}-1)/4+4})=2q\sqrt{(n^{2}-1)/4+2q}-qn-q<0.

Consequently, we get

\sqrt{\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+4}<\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
. Similarly, we can verify that
f_{1}(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2q}{n}+\frac{4q}{n^{2}})>0
and
f_{2}(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2q}{n}+\frac{4q}{n^{2}})>0
, which implies that

\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2q}{n}+\frac{4q}{n^{2}}
, as desired.
∎

Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
q\geq 2
, we have
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\lambda(T_{n,2,q})
.

Proof.

We start the proof in two cases.
(a) If
n
is even, by a simple computation as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we can obtain that
\lambda(T_{n,2,q})
is the largest root of

\displaystyle h_{1}(x)

\displaystyle:=x^{4}-x^{3}-(n^{2}x^{2})/4-qx^{2}+(n^{2}x)/4-(nx)/2-nqx

\displaystyle\quad-(nq)/2+(n^{2}q)/4-(nq^{2})/2.

Let
f_{1}(x)
be the polynomial defined in Lemma
3.1
. It follows that

xf_{1}(x)-h_{1}(x)=qx^{2}+(nx)/2+(nq^{2})/2+(nq)/2-(n^{2}q)/4.

It is easy to see that for every
x>\frac{n}{2}
, we have

xf_{1}(x)-h_{1}(x)>\tfrac{n}{2}f_{1}(\tfrac{n}{2})-h_{1}(\tfrac{n}{2})=\tfrac{1}{4}(n^{2}+2q(1+q)n)>0.

We conclude that
xf_{1}(x)>h_{1}(x)
for every
x>\frac{n}{2}
.
Note that
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
is the largest root of
xf_{1}(x)
.
Combining with
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q}),\lambda(T_{n,2,q})>\frac{n}{2}
,
we obtain
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\lambda(T_{n,2,q})
.

(b) If
n
is odd, then
\lambda(T_{n,2,q})
is the largest root of

\displaystyle h_{2}(x)

\displaystyle:=x^{4}-x^{3}+x^{2}/4-(n^{2}x^{2})/4-qx^{2}+x/4-(nx)/2+(n^{2}x)/4+qx-nqx

\displaystyle\quad+q/4-(nq)/2+(n^{2}q)/4+q^{2}/2-(nq^{2})/2.

Let
f_{2}(x)
be the polynomial defined in Lemma
3.1
. Then

xf_{2}(x)-h_{2}(x)=qx^{2}+(n-1)x/2+(nq)/2-(n^{2}q)/4+(n-1)q^{2}/2-q/4.

For every
x>\frac{n-1}{2}
, it follows that

xf_{2}(x)-h_{2}(x)>\tfrac{n-1}{2}f_{2}(\tfrac{n-1}{2})-h_{2}(\tfrac{n-1}{2})=\tfrac{1}{4}(n-1)(2q^{2}+n-1)>0.

In this case, observe that
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q}),\lambda(T_{n,2,q})>\frac{n-1}{2}
, so we get
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\lambda(T_{n,2,q})
.
∎

3.1
The tightness of
q
in Theorem
2.2

In this section, we provide an example showing that the bound of
q
in Theorem
2.2
is tight up to a constant factor.
Recall that
Y_{n,2,q}
and
T_{n,2,q}
are obtained from
T_{n,2}
by adding a matching, and a star with
q
edges into the partite set of size
\lceil n/2\rceil
, respectively.

Example 3.3
.

If
q=0.8\sqrt{n}
and
n\geq 110
are integers, then
\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})>\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
.
However, the graph
T_{n,2,q-1}
contains exactly
(q-1)\lfloor n/2\rfloor
triangles.

Proof.

We partition the proof into two cases.
(a) If
n
is even, by a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we know that
\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})
is the largest root of

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)

\displaystyle:=x^{4}-x^{3}-(n^{2}x^{2})/4-qx^{2}+x^{2}+(n^{2}x)/4+(nx)/2-nqx

\displaystyle\quad-(n^{2}/4)+(nq)/2+(n^{2}q)/4-(nq^{2})/2.

Let
f_{1}(x)
be defined in Lemma
3.1
.
Since
q=0.8\sqrt{n}
, by computation, we see that

xf_{1}(x)-g_{1}(x)=(0.8\sqrt{n}-1)x^{2}-0.5nx-0.2n^{5/2}+0.57n^{2}-0.4n^{3/2}.

For every
n\geq 4
and
x>{n}/{2}
, it follows that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(xf_{1}(x)-g_{1}(x))=(1.6\sqrt{n}-2)x-0.5n>0.

Then for every
x>n/2
, we have

\displaystyle xf_{1}(x)-g_{1}(x)\geq\tfrac{n}{2}f_{1}(\tfrac{n}{2})-g_{1}(\tfrac{n}{2})=0.07n^{2}-0.4n^{3/2}>0.

We conclude that
xf_{1}(x)>g_{1}(x)
for every
x>n/2
and
n\geq 33
.
Note that
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})
and
\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})
are the largest roots of
xf_{1}(x)
and
g_{1}(x)
, respectively.
Since
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})>\frac{n}{2}
and
\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})>\frac{n}{2}
, we obtain
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})
.

(b) If
n
is odd, by computation, we get that
\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})
is the largest root of

\displaystyle g_{2}(x)

\displaystyle:=x^{4}-x^{3}+(5x^{2})/4-(n^{2}x^{2})/4-qx^{2}-(3x)/4+(nx)/2+(n^{2}x)/4+qx-nqx

\displaystyle+1/4-n^{2}/4-(3q)/4+(nq)/2+(n^{2}q)/4+q^{2}/2-(nq^{2})/2.

Let
f_{2}(x)
be determined in Lemma
3.1
.
Substituting
q=0.8\sqrt{n}
, we compute that

\displaystyle xf_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=(0.8\sqrt{n}-1)x^{2}-0.5(n-1)x-0.2n^{5/2}+0.57n^{2}

\displaystyle-0.4n^{3/2}-0.32n+0.6\sqrt{n}-0.25.

For every
x>\frac{n-1}{2}
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(xf_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x))=(1.6\sqrt{n}-2)x-0.5(n-1)>0.

It follows that for every
n\geq 110
and
x>\frac{n-1}{2}
,

xf_{2}(x)-g_{2}(x)>\tfrac{n-1}{2}f_{2}(\tfrac{n-1}{2})-g_{2}(\tfrac{n-1}{2})=0.07n^{2}-0.8n^{3/2}+0.68n+0.8\sqrt{n}-0.75>0.

Thus, we get
xf_{2}(x)>g_{2}(x)
for every
x>\frac{n-1}{2}
.
It is easy to see that
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})>\frac{n-1}{2}
and
\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})>\frac{n-1}{2}
. So we conclude that
\lambda(Y_{n,2,q})<\lambda(T_{n,2,q-1})
.
∎

3.2
Counting the number of triangles

In 2007, Bollobás and Nikiforov
[
7
]

established some powerful results on counting the number of
triangles (cliques) of a graph
in terms of the spectral radius.

Lemma 3.4

(See
[
7
,
9
,
48
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with
m
edges. Then

t(G)\geq\frac{\lambda\bigl(\lambda^{2}-m\bigr)}{3}.

The equality holds if and only if
G
is a complete bipartite graph.

As a consequence, Lemma
3.4
implies an interesting result of Nosal
[
50
]
, which states that every
m
-edge graph
G
with
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
contains a triangle; see
[
41
,
47
]
for related results.
The inequality in Lemma
3.4
can be rephrased as the following versions:

\lambda^{3}\leq 3t+m\lambda\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{t\geq\frac{1}{3}\lambda(\lambda^{2}-m)}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad m\geq\lambda^{2}-\frac{3t}{\lambda}.

This inequality was initially obtained by
Bollobás and Nikiforov
[
7
, Theorem 1]
,
and it was independently discovered by Cioabă, Feng,
Tait and Zhang
[
9
]
, as well as Ning and Zhai
[
48
]
.
The case of equality was characterized by Ning and Zhai
[
48
]
.

Moon–Moser’s inequality states that if

G
has
n
vertices and
m
edges,
then

t(G)\geq\frac{4m}{3n}\left(m-\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right).

We refer to
[
4
, p. 297]
and
[
36
, p. 443]
for detailed proofs.
This result gives the supersaturation on triangles
for graphs with more than
n^{2}/4
edges.
Next, we present a generalization for a graph with
less than
n^{2}/4
edges whenever this graph
is far from being bipartite.
More precisely, for
\varepsilon>0
,
we say that a graph
G
is
\varepsilon
-far from being bipartite
if
any subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G

with more than
e(G)-\varepsilon
edges is not bipartite.
In other words,
if
G
is
\varepsilon
-far from being bipartite, then we must
delete at least
\varepsilon
edges from
G
to make it bipartite.

Theorem 3.5

(See
[
2
]
)
.

If
G
is
\varepsilon
-far from being bipartite, then

t(G)\geq\frac{n}{6}\left(m+\varepsilon-\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right).

For the convenience of the reader, we include a proof here.

Proof.

For each vertex
v\in V(G)
, let
N_{v}=N(v)

and
N_{v}^{c}=V(G)\setminus N(v)
.
Since
G
is
t
-far from being bipartite, we must have
e(N_{v})+e(N_{v}^{c})\geq\varepsilon
for every
v\in V(G)
.
Indeed, if
e(N_{v})+e(N_{v}^{c})<\varepsilon
for some
v
, then deleting all edges inside
N_{v}
and
N_{v}^{c}
yields a bipartite subgraph with more than
e(G)-\varepsilon
edges, a contradiction.
For each
v\in V(G)
, we have

\sum_{w\in N_{v}^{c}}d(w)=2e(N_{v}^{c})+e(N_{v}^{c},N_{v})=e(N_{v}^{c})+m-e(N_{v})\geq m+\varepsilon-2e(N_{v}).

Summing this inequality over all vertices
v\in V(G)
gives

\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{w\in N_{v}^{c}}d(w)\geq mn+\varepsilon n-2\sum_{v\in V}e(N_{v})=mn+\varepsilon n-6t(G),

where we used the identity
3t(G)=\sum_{v\in V}e(N_{v})
.
On the other hand, we get

\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{w\in N_{v}^{c}}d(w)=\sum_{v\in V}\left(2m-\sum_{w\in N_{v}}d(w)\right)=2mn-\sum_{w\in V}d^{2}(w).

Combining the two inequalities,
we obtain

6t(G)\geq\varepsilon n-mn+\sum_{w\in V}d^{2}(w)\geq\varepsilon n-mn+\frac{4m^{2}}{n},

where the last inequality follows by Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality.
Note that

{4m^{2}}/{n}\geq 2mn-{n^{3}}/{4}
. Substituting this into the previous inequality gives the desired bound.
∎

The supersaturation on triangles is usually deduced from
Moon–Moser’s inequality.
In fact, Theorem
3.5

can also imply the supersaturation on triangles.
Note that every bipartite graph of order
n
has at most
n^{2}/4
edges.
Thus, if
G
has at least
{n^{2}}/{4}+q
edges,
then it is
q
-far from being bipartite.
Theorem
3.5
implies that

G
contains at least
{qn}/{3}
triangles.
In particular, setting
q=\varepsilon n^{2}
,
we obtain that

G
has at least
{\varepsilon n^{3}}/{3}
triangles.

For dealing with some extremal graph problems,
it is a great advantage that
applying Theorem
3.5
can
help us to get rid of the use of triangle removal lemma so that
we do not require the order of a graph to be sufficiently large.
This approach could be considered to be a key ingredient in our paper, and
it was proven to be effective and feasible in many extremal graph problems.
For other related applications,
we refer the reader to
[
28
,
29
]
.

3.3
A spectral stability for graphs with few triangles

In what follows, we provide a warm-up practice
that has its independent interest.
We concentrate on explaining the main ideas which we
believe have wide applicability.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with

\lambda(G)\geq n/2
and
t(G)\leq kn/2
, then

e(G)>\frac{n^{2}}{4}-3k,

and there exists a vertex partition

V(G)=S\cup T
such that

e(S,T)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}-9k

and

\frac{n}{2}-3\sqrt{k}\leq|S|,|T|\leq\frac{n}{2}+3\sqrt{k}.

Moreover, we have

\frac{n}{2}-12k\leq\delta(G)\leq\lambda(G)\leq\Delta(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}+9k.

Theorem
3.6

gives an effective way to obtain a bipartition of a graph with large spectral radius and few triangles. To some extend,
this theorem indicates how the supersaturation-stability method
can be used to deal with a variety of spectral extremal problems.
The proof of Theorem
3.6
is a well combination of
Lemma
3.4
and Theorem

3.5
.

Proof.

First, if
G
is a complete bipartite graph,
then
e(G)\leq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
.
Since
e(G)=\lambda^{2}(G)\geq n^{2}/4
,
it follows that
n
is even and
G=K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}
. In this case,
the desired result holds immediately.
Now suppose that
G
is not a complete bipartite graph.
Then the inequality in Lemma
3.4
is strict. So we get

, then
all triangles of
G
can be covered by a vertex set of
at most
s-1
vertices, contradicting with
\tau_{3}(G)\geq s
.
Now, suppose that
e(S)+e(T)=k
for some integer
k\geq s+1
. Note that
each edge of
G[S]
or
G[T]
can yield
at least
{n}/{2}-3\sqrt{s}
triangles.
From Claim 1, we know that
|S||T|-e(S,T)\leq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor-e(S,T)<9s
. Then

G[S,T]
misses at most
(9s-1)
edges between

S
and
T
, and each missing edge destroys at most
k
triangles.
So we get
t(G)\geq k(\frac{n}{2}-3\sqrt{s})-(9s-1)k\geq k(\frac{n}{2}-3\sqrt{s}-9s+1)>\frac{1}{2}s(n-1)
,
where the last inequality holds for every
s\geq 3
and
n\geq 28s^{2}
.
This contradicts with the assumption
t(G)\leq\frac{1}{2}s(n-1)
.
Thus, we have
e(S)+e(T)=s
, as claimed.
∎

From the above claims,
we obtain

e(S,T)=e(G)-s>\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor-4s
. Then

\frac{n}{2}-2\sqrt{s}\leq|S|,|T|\leq\frac{n}{2}+2\sqrt{s}.

If
s=3
, then
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-3\leq|S|,|T|\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+3
.
Since there are less than
4s
missing edges in
G[S,T]
, Claim 2 implies that

t(G)\geq s(\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-3)-4s\cdot s\geq\frac{1}{2}sn-5s^{2}
.
If
s\geq 4
, then a similar argument shows that
t(G)\geq s(\frac{n}{2}-2\sqrt{s})-4s^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}sn-5s^{2}
, as needed.
∎

7
Concluding remarks

Recall that
t(G)
denotes the number of triangles in a graph
G
.
A special case of an aforementioned
result of Bollobás and Nikiforov
[
7
]

states that

t(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{12}\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}\right).

In fact, a careful examination indicates that the above inequality holds strictly. Motivated by this observation,
one may ask the following problem.

Problem 7.1
.

What is the best constant
C>0
such that
t(G)\geq Cn^{2}\left(\lambda-\frac{n}{2}\right)
?

Remark.

By the clique density theorem of Reiher
[
52
]
,
we know that if
\varepsilon>0
and
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
e(G)\geq(\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon)n^{2}
, then
G
has at least

(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))\varepsilon n^{3}
triangles, where
o(1)\to 0
whenever

\varepsilon\to 0
. With the aid of this powerful result,
one can easily prove that if
G
has at least
(\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon)n^{2}
edges, where we set
\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\lambda}{n}-\frac{1}{2})
, then
G
has at least
(\frac{1}{4}+o(1))n^{2}(\lambda-\frac{n}{2})
triangles. Moreover, the graph
T_{n,2,1}
shows
that the coefficient
\frac{1}{4}
is achievable.
Recently, Shengtong Zhang told us that taking
G=T_{n,3}
yields
C\leq\frac{2}{9}
.

Another question as to how many triangles appear at least in any
n
-vertex graph with given spectral radius turns out to be interesting.
For instance, for any graph
G
with

\lambda(G)=\gamma n
, where
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3}]
, that is,
\lambda(T_{n,2})<\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(T_{n,3})
,
there might exist a graph, say
L
, whose shape is approximately between
T_{n,2}
and
T_{n,3}

such that
\lambda(L)=\gamma n
and
t(L)\leq t(G)
.
Formally, we now define the minimizer
L
as follows.
For
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>0
,
we define
L_{n,s,\alpha}
as a complete multipartite graph with

V(L_{n,s,\alpha})=V_{1}\cup\cdots V_{s}\cup V_{s+1}
,
where
|V_{1}|=\cdots=|V_{s}|=\frac{n(1+\alpha)}{s+1}
and

|V_{s+1}|=\frac{n(1-s\alpha)}{s+1}
.
Next, we speculate that
L_{n,s,\alpha}

contains the minimum number of triangles
among all graphs with given spectral radius.

Conjecture 7.2

(Spectral triangle density conjecture)
.

Let
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1)

and
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\lambda(G)\geq\gamma n
.
Then
t(G)\geq t(L_{n,s,\alpha})
, where

s\geq 2
is an integer with

\gamma\in(\frac{s-1}{s},\frac{s}{s+1}]
and

\alpha>0
is a maximal number such that

\lambda(L_{n,s,\alpha})\geq\gamma n
.

Nosal
[
50
]

proved that every graph
G
with

m
edges and
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}

contains a triangle; see, e.g.,
[
41
,
47
]
.
In 2023, Ning and Zhai
[
48
]

showed that if

\lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{m}
,
then
t(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{m}-1}{2}\rfloor
, unless
G
is a complete bipartite graph.
We propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 7.3
.

If
G
is a graph with
m
edges and

\lambda(G)\geq\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2},

then
G
has at least
\frac{m-1}{2}
triangles,
with equality if and only if
G=K_{2}\vee\frac{m-1}{2}K_{1}
.

Remark.

Roughly speaking, Conjecture
7.3
reveals that
in a graph
G
with
\lambda(G)\approx\sqrt{m}+\frac{1}{2}
,
the number of triangles
jumps from
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m}

to
\frac{1}{2}m
.
In addition, we can see that if
\lambda(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{m}
,
then
t(G)>\frac{2\varepsilon}{3}m^{3/2}
.
In fact, it is not difficult to obtain a weaker bound than Conjecture
7.3
. Indeed, it follows from Lemma
3.4
that

t(G)>\frac{1}{3}\lambda(\lambda^{2}-m)\geq\frac{m-1}{3}
.
Thus, the difficulty of Conjecture
7.3
lies in
deriving the tight bound
t(G)\geq\frac{m-1}{2}
.

Theorem
1.2
asserts that
every graph
G
with
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+q

edges contains at least
q\lfloor n/2\rfloor
triangles for every
q<n/2
.
In Theorem
2.2
,
we proved a spectral version for all
q\leq\frac{1}{11}\sqrt{n}
.
This bound on
q
is tight up to a constant factor (Example
3.3
).
Under our framework, it seems feasible to improve the constant factor slightly. However, new ideas are needed to determine the exact bound on
q
. It is ambitious to propose the following problem.

Problem 7.4
.

What is the supremum
\delta\in(0,0.8)
such that Theorem
2.2
holds for every integer
q<\delta\sqrt{n}
? Additionally, what is the optimal range of
q
in Theorem
2.3
?

In 2017,
Pikhurko and Yilma
[
51
]

proved that for every color-critical graph

F
with
\chi(F)=r+1
,
there exist
\delta=\delta(F)>0

and
n_{0}
such that
for all
n\geq n_{0}
and
q<\delta n
,
if
G
is an
n
-vertex graph
with
e(T_{n,r})+q
edges and it achieves the minimum
number of copies of
F
,
then
G
is obtained from the Turán graph
T_{n,r}
by adding
q
new edges. Furthermore,
by Theorem
1.2
, we see that
\delta(K_{3})=\frac{1}{2}
. A result of Lovász and Simonovits
[
37
,
38
]
implies that
\delta(K_{r+1})=\frac{1}{r}
for every
r\geq 2
.
Moreover, Pikhurko and Yilma
[
51
]
also determined the exact value of
\delta

for a number of color-critical graphs, such as, odd cycles
\delta(C_{2k+1})=\frac{1}{2}
, and
cliques with one edge removed
\delta(K_{r+2}-e)=\frac{r-1}{r^{2}}
. Similarly, it is also meaningful to determine the exact value of
\delta(F)
for various graphs
F
in the spectral setting.

Improving the error term in the sieve of Eratosthenes
Madieyna Diouf

Abstract
. We have devised an alternative approach to sifting integers in the sieve of Eratosthenes that helps refine the error term. Instead of eliminating all multiples of a prime number p < z p<z in the traditional sieve method, our approach solely eliminates multiples of p p that have the minimum prime factor of p p . By leveraging the density of integers with the least prime factor p p in this sieve technique, we obtain a reduced error term and an upper bound of π ⁡ ( x ) \pi(x) that accurately reflects the prime number theorem.

Introduction And Statement of Results

Sieve theory aims to improve the Sieve of Eratosthenes
[
2
]

We provide a new approach to understanding the sieve of Eratosthenes and overcoming obstacles created by the Möbius function and parity problem. Our result enhances the precision of the error term that is currently,

\displaystyle E=O\left(2^{\pi(z)}\right).

We improve it to:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=O\left(\sum_{p_{i}<z}\left\{\frac{x}{p_{i}}\right\}\prod_{p<p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)=O\left(\pi(z)\right).

The best-known upper limit of

(1.1)

(1.1)

z
to
\log x

\displaystyle\pi(x)

while our result shows that

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

2.
Background

Consider a finite sequence of integers
\mathscr{A}=\{1,2,...,\>x\}
and a set of primes
\mathscr{P}
. For a given
z\geq 2
, let
P(z)=\prod_{p<z,\>p\in\mathscr{P}}p
. Let
S(\mathscr{A,P},z)
denote the number of integers in
\mathscr{A}
that have no prime divisor
p<z
.

Say
\chi[statement]=1
if the statement is true,
0
if false.
Letting
\mu(d)
be the Möbius function, the exact formula of the Sieve of Eratosthenes by Legendre is

\displaystyle\pi(x)-\pi(z)+1=\sum_{d|P(z)}\mu(d)\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d}\right\rfloor.\text{\> \> \> \> \> \> \> }

We obtain the Legendre identity

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|P(z)}\mu(d)\left\lfloor\frac{x}{d}\right\rfloor.

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{d|P(z)}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}-\sum_{d|P(z)}\mu(d)\left\{\frac{x}{d}\right\}.

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{d|P(z)}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}+\sum_{d|P(z)}O(1).

\displaystyle=x\prod_{p\leq z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+O\left(2^{\omega\left(P(z)\right)}\right).

(2.1)

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x\prod_{p\leq z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+O\left(2^{\pi(z)}\right).

But
(2.1)
is of limited use, unless
z
is very small such as
z=\log x
. We propose the following process that allows the sifting level
z
to grow with
x
, ideally at an optimal
\sqrt{x}
rate while considering
\mathscr{A}
as the entire set of integers in
[1,x]
.

3.
Results

3.1.
Sieving

Given the positive integers less than or equal to
x
,

1) Remove the multiples of
2
that have
2
as the smallest prime factor.

2) Remove the multiples of
3
that have
3
as the smallest prime factor.

3) Remove the multiples of
5
with
5
as the smallest prime factor.

Continue removing all remaining positive integers less than or equal to
x
that are multiples of
p_{i}
and have
p_{i}
as the smallest prime factor, where
p_{i}
is the
i
-th prime number, until we reach the largest prime
p_{r}
that is less than
z
, where
z=\sqrt{x}
in the original sieve of Eratosthenes.

After completing the process, we obtain a set of positive integers with no multiples of primes less than
z
. These integers are the prime numbers between
z
and
x
and the number
1
. So, we obtain the relation

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p_{i}\mid n\\
\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\end{subarray}}1.

(3.1)

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x-\mathfrak{S}(x,z)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p_{i}\mid n\\
\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\end{subarray}}1.

It should be noted that the summation

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p_{i}\mid n\\
\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\end{subarray}}1.

represents the number of multiples of
p_{i}
not exceeding
x
with least prime factor
p_{i}
. This value can be estimated with great accuracy. Unlike the traditional sieves, where we remove multiples of primes, and these multiples can overlap, meaning that a multiple of
2
can also be a multiple of
3
or a multiple of
p<z
; Here, each multiple of
2
having the least prime factor as
2
is distinct from any multiple of
3
having the least prime factor as
3
…, which is different from any multiple of
p<z
with least prime factor
p
. The following arguments estimate
\mathfrak{I}(x,z)
.

Given a prime
p_{i}<z
, we have
\left\lfloor\frac{x}{p_{i}}\right\rfloor=\frac{x}{p_{i}}-\left\{\frac{x}{p_{i}}\right\}
or simply
\frac{x}{p_{i}}+O(1)
. By using the latter estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{x}{p_{i}}+O(1)\text{\> integers less than or equal to $x$ that are multiples of $p_{i}$. Among these, }

\displaystyle\frac{x}{p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i-1}}\right)+O(1)\text{ do not divide $p_{i-1}$. Similarly, }

\displaystyle\frac{x}{p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i-1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i-2}}\right)+O(1)\text{ are not divisible by $p_{i-1}$ and not divisible by $p_{i-2}$.}

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\frac{x}{p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i-1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i-2}}\right)...\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right)+O(1)\text{ are not divisible by any prime $p<p_{i}$.}

Therefore, the number of multiples of
p_{i}
less than or equal to
x
, with least prime factor
p_{i}
is estimated to:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}(x,z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p_{i}\mid n\\
\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{p_{i}}\prod_{p<p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+O(1).

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p_{i}\mid n\\
\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\frac{x}{p_{i}}\prod_{p<p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\sum_{p_{i}<z}O(1).

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\frac{x}{p_{i}}\prod_{p<p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+O(\pi(z)).

With
(3.1)
and
(3.3)
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{p_{i}<z}\frac{x}{p_{i}}\prod_{p<p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+O(\pi(z)).

Jared Duker Lichman and Carl Pomerance have shown in
[
14
]
that for primes
p
,
q
and
r
(the authors noted that
r>1
may be any real number), if
g(p)
is the density of the set of integers with least prime factor
p
, then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq r}g(p)=\sum_{p\leq r}\frac{1}{p}\prod_{q\leq p}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)=1-\prod_{p\leq r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right).

We multiply equation
(3.5)
by
x
and perform variable changes to obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{i}<z}\frac{x}{p_{i}}\prod_{p<p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=x-x\prod_{p_{i}<z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right).

By using
(3.6)
in equation
(3.4)
, we get

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x-\left(x-x\prod_{p_{i}<z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)\right)+O(\pi(z)).

(3.7)

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x\prod_{p_{i}<z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)+O(\pi(z)).

4.
An alternative proof

In the following proof, using the same sieve, we will exclusively choose the upper limit
x
of the set of integers
\mathscr{A}=[1,x]
, as a power of
2
for technical reasons.

Our first method in section
3
bypasses the use of the Möbius function, resulting in an error term of
O(\pi(z))
. Let’s examine our results when we incorporate the Möbius function.

After sieving as illustrated in
(3.1)
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x-\mathfrak{S}(x,z).

Where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p_{i}\mid n\\
\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{n\leq x}\chi[p_{i}\mid n]\chi\left[\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\right].

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\chi\left[\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)=1\right]\sum_{n\leq x}\chi[p_{i}\mid n].

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\big|\left(n,\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}\right)\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\sum_{n\leq x}\chi[p_{i}\mid n].

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\chi[d\mid n]\sum_{n\leq x}\chi[p_{i}\mid n].

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\sum_{n\leq x}\chi[p_{i}\mid n]\chi[d\mid n].

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p_{i}\mid n\\
d\mid n\end{subarray}}1.

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
[\>p_{i},d\>]\mid n\end{subarray}}1.

Where
[x,y]
is the least comment multiple of the integers
x
and
y
.

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\left\lfloor\frac{x}{[p_{i},d]}\right\rfloor.

Moreover, since

\displaystyle d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}};

Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle gcd(p_{i},d)=(p_{i},d)=1.

When using the fact that

(4.4)

\displaystyle[p_{i},d].(p_{i},d)=dp_{i},

then
(4.3)
and
(4.4)

(4.5)

\displaystyle[p_{i},d]=dp_{i}.

After substituting
(4.5)
into
(4.2)
, the result is

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(x,z)=\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\left\lfloor\frac{x}{dp_{i}}\right\rfloor.

By inserting
(4.6)
into
(4.1)
, one obtains

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\left\lfloor\frac{x}{dp_{i}}\right\rfloor.

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\left(\frac{x}{dp_{i}}-\left\{\frac{x}{dp_{i}}\right\}\right).

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\frac{x}{dp_{i}}+\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\left\{\frac{x}{dp_{i}}\right\}.

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{p_{i}<z}\frac{x}{p_{i}}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d}+\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\left\{\frac{x}{dp_{i}}\right\}.

(4.7)

\displaystyle=x-\sum_{p_{i}<z}\frac{x}{p_{i}}\prod_{p<p_{i}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\sum_{p_{i}<z}\sum_{d\big|\frac{P(z)}{p_{i}p_{i+1}...p_{r}}}\mu(d)\left\{\frac{x}{dp_{i}}\right\}.

5.
Retrieving the upper bound in Chebyshev’s inequality

Set
z=\sqrt{x}
in this application.

5.1.
Upper bound of
S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

We know that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\prod_{p<z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{p<z}\sum_{m>0}\frac{1}{p^{m}}>\sum_{k<z}\frac{1}{k}>\int_{1}^{z}\frac{dx}{x}=\log z.

By
(5.1)
, we obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle\prod_{p<z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)<\frac{1}{\log z}=\frac{1}{\log(\sqrt{x})}.

As a result of
(5.2)
and
(4.12)
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle<\frac{x}{\log(\sqrt{x})}+O\left(\pi(\sqrt{x})\right).

\displaystyle S(\mathscr{A,P},z)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\log x}.

5.2.
Upper bound of
\pi(x)

\displaystyle\pi(x)

\displaystyle=\big(\pi(x)-\pi(z)\big)+\pi(z).

(5.4)

\displaystyle\pi(x)

\displaystyle\leq S(\mathscr{A,P},z)+\pi(z).

A combination of
(5.3)
and
(5.4)
, gives

\displaystyle\pi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{x}{\log{\sqrt{x}}}+O\left(\pi(\sqrt{x})\right).

(5.5)

\displaystyle\pi(x)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{x}{\log x}.

The sieving technique accurately identifies all relevant multiples and avoids duplication for optimal results.

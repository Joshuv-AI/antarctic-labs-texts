Chain-imprimitive, flag-transitive 2 2 -designs
Carmen Amarra a,b∗, Alice Devillers a, Cheryl E. Praeger a

Abstract
. We consider 2 2 -designs which admit a group of automorphisms that is flag-transitive and leaves invariant a chain of nontrivial point-partitions. We build on our recent work on 2 2 -designs which are block-transitive but not necessarily flag-transitive. In particular we use the concept of the “array” of a point subset with respect to the chain of point-partitions; the array describes the distribution of the points in the subset among the classes of each partition. We obtain necessary and sufficient conditions on the array in order for the subset to be a block of such a design. By explicit construction we show that for any s ≥ 2 s\geq 2 , there are infinitely many 2 2 -designs admitting a flag-transitive group that preserves an invariant chain of point-partitions of length s s . Moreover an exhaustive computer search, using Magma , seeking designs with e 1 ​ e 2 ​ e 3 e_{1}e_{2}e_{3} points (where each e i ≤ 50 e_{i}\leq 50 ) and a partition chain of length s = 3 s=3 , produced 57 57 such flag-transitive designs, among which only three designs arise from our construction – so there is still much to learn.

(c)

In particular, for

Proof.

(a) This follows from

{\mathscr{C}}(C)

[

(b) By the discussion before

2.3.
Array of a subset

The
array function
of a nonempty subset
B
of
{\mathscr{P}}
, with respect to the chain
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
of partitions, is the function
\chi_{B}:\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}{\mathscr{C}}_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
defined by

(7)

\chi_{B}(C):=\left|B\cap C\right|\quad\text{for each $C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i}$ and each $i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}$.}

In particular, for
i=s
we have
\chi_{B}(C)=\chi_{B}({\mathscr{P}})=|B|
. For brevity, and when there is no ambiguity, we shall frequently use the notation
x_{C}:=\chi_{B}(C)
, or, more specifically,

(8)

x_{(\delta_{j})_{j>i}}:=\chi_{B}\big(C_{(\delta_{j})_{j>i}}\big)

for
C_{(\delta_{j})_{j>i}}
as in (
3
). For a subgroup

which leaves invariant each partition in the chain
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
, we say that two array functions
\chi_{B}
and
\chi_{B^{\prime}}
are
equivalent under
G
if
\chi_{B}^{g}=\chi_{B^{\prime}}
for some
g\in G
, where

(9)

\chi_{B}^{g}(C):=\chi_{B}\big(C^{g^{-1}}\big)\quad\text{for all}\ C\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}{\mathscr{C}}_{i}.

By
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
, if
G=S_{e_{1}}\wr\ldots\wr S_{e_{s}}
then the
G
-orbit of
B\subseteq{\mathscr{P}}
consists of all
B^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathscr{P}}
whose array function
\chi_{B^{\prime}}
is equivalent to
\chi_{B}
under
G
.

3.
Point subsets with uniform array functions

The theme of the paper is
G
-flag-transitive designs, where
G
is chain-imprimitive on the point set
{\mathscr{P}}
with respect to a chain
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
as in (
1
). If
B
is a block of such a design then the setwise stabiliser
G_{B}
is transitive on
B
. This means in particular that, for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,s\}
, every
{\mathscr{C}}_{i}
-class
C
which contains elements of
B
must contain a constant number of elements of
B
. In other words, there exist positive integers
y_{i}
, depending only on
i
, such that for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,s\}
and each
C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i}
,
\chi_{B}(C)=\big|B\cap C\big|=x_{C}\in\{0,y_{i}\}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A subset
B
of
{\mathscr{P}}
is
uniform
relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
, with
uniform sequence

(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})
, if for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,s\}
and each
C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i}(B)
,
\big|B\cap C\big|=y_{i}
.

Note that for
i=0
the class
C
has a unique element, say
\delta
, and
\chi_{B}(C)=0
or
1
according to whether
\delta\in B
or
\delta\notin B
, so
y_{0}=1
; while for
i=s
we have
C={\mathscr{P}}
, and so
\chi_{B}({\mathscr{P}})=|B|=k
and thus
y_{s}=k
. Our next result derives further restrictions on a uniform sequence and shows that all uniform subsets with the same uniform sequence are in the same orbit under the stabiliser of the chain
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s,e_{1},\dots,e_{s}
be integers, all at least
2
, let
{\mathscr{P}}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{e_{i}}
, let
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
be a chain of nontrivial partitions of
{\mathscr{P}}
, as in (
1
) with parts as in (
4
), and chain stabiliser
G=S_{e_{1}}\wr\ldots\wr S_{e_{s}}
. For each
i\in\{0,\ldots,s\}
, let
y_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

(a)

There exists a nonempty subset
B
of
{\mathscr{P}}
which is uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
, if and only if the following hold:

(i)
y_{0}=1
,
y_{s}=|B|
,  and  (ii) for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,
y_{i-1}\mid y_{i}
with
\frac{y_{i}}{y_{i-1}}\leq e_{i}
.

(b)

If conditions (i) and (ii) in part (a) hold, then the subset

(12)

B:=\left\{(\delta_{i})_{i=1}^{s}\in{\mathscr{P}}\ \ \vline\ \ 0\leq\delta_{i}\leq\frac{y_{i}}{y_{i-1}}-1\ \text{for }\ 1\leq i\leq s\right\}

is uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
. Moreover, a subset of
{\mathscr{P}}
has these properties if and only if it lies in the
G
-orbit of
B
.

Proof.

(a) Assume that there is a nonempty subset
B
of
{\mathscr{P}}
such that
B
is uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
. Since all
{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
-classes have size
1
and
B
is non-empty, we have that
y_{0}=1
, and since
{\mathscr{C}}_{s}=\{{\mathscr{P}}\}
we also have
y_{s}=|B\cap{\mathscr{P}}|=|B|
. This proves condition (i). Let
1\leq i\leq s
and let
C
be a
{\mathscr{C}}_{i}
-class intersecting
B
non-trivially, so that
x_{C}=y_{i}
. Consider the set
{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C)
of all
{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}
-classes contained in
C
. For each
C^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C)
,
x_{C^{\prime}}\in\{0,y_{i-1}\}
, and
x_{C}=\sum_{C^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C)}x_{C^{\prime}}
. It follows that
y_{i-1}\mid y_{i}
. Recall that
|{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C)|=e_{i}
so, if
C^{\prime}\subset C
is such that
x_{C^{\prime}}=y_{i-1}
, then we have
x_{C}\leq e_{i}x_{C^{\prime}}
and thus
\frac{y_{i}}{y_{i-1}}=\frac{x_{C}}{x_{C^{\prime}}}\leq e_{i}
, which is condition (ii). Therefore (i) and (ii) hold whenever some subset
B
is uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
.

Conversely assume that conditions (i) and (ii) hold. Note that from condition (ii),
0\leq\frac{y_{i}}{y_{i-1}}-1<e_{i}
. Hence if
(\delta_{i})_{i=1}^{s}
satisfies the defining conditions for
B
then
(\delta_{i})_{i=1}^{s}\in\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{e_{i}}={\mathscr{P}}
. The array function
\chi_{B}
of
B
with respect to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
has value
\chi_{B}\big(C_{(\delta_{j})_{j>i}}\big)=\big|\big\{(\varepsilon_{j})_{j=1}^{s}\in B\ \big|\ \varepsilon_{j}=\delta_{j}\ \text{for all}\ j>i\big\}\big|
, which, using conditions (i) and (ii), can be computed as

(13)

\chi_{B}\big(C_{(\delta_{j})_{j>i}}\big)=\begin{cases}\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}\frac{y_{\ell}}{y_{\ell-1}}=y_{i}&\text{if $\delta_{j}<\frac{y_{j}}{y_{j-1}}$ for all $j>i$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Therefore
B
is uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
. This completes the proof of statement (a), and also proves the first part of statement (b).

(b) Let
{\mathscr{B}}=B^{G}
with
B
as in (
12
). Assume first that
B^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{B}}
; so
B^{\prime}=B^{g}
for some
g\in G
. By
[
2
, Lemma 2.5 (a)]
, the array function
\chi_{B^{\prime}}=\chi^{g}_{B}
where
\chi^{g}_{B}
is as defined in (
9
). Hence, for each
C\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}{\mathscr{C}}_{i}
, say
C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i}
so also

, we have
\chi_{B^{\prime}}(C)=\chi_{B}^{g}(C)=\chi_{B}\big(C^{g^{-1}}\big)
which lies in
\{0,y_{i}\}
since
B
has uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
. Thus
B^{\prime}
is also uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
.

Conversely, let
B^{\prime}
be a uniform subset of
{\mathscr{P}}
relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
; we will show that

.

Claim. For every
j\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}
, there exists
g\in G
such that
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}(B)^{g}={\mathscr{C}}_{j}(B^{\prime})
.

We prove the claim by induction on
\ell=s-j
, for
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Suppose first that
\ell=1
, so that
j=s-1
. By (
5
),
|{\mathscr{C}}_{s-1}(B)|
is the number of classes of
{\mathscr{C}}_{s-1}
that intersect
B
nontrivially. Since
B
and
B^{\prime}
both have uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
, it follows from Definition
3.1
and Lemma

that
|B|=|B^{\prime}|=k=y_{s}

. Then since

induces

on

(b)), there exists

maps
{\mathscr{C}}_{s-1}(B)

{\mathscr{C}}_{s-1}(B^{\prime})
. Therefore the claim holds for

, that is, for
\ell=1
.

Let
\ell\in\{2,\ldots,s\}
and assume inductively that the claim holds for
\ell-1
, that is, there exists
g\in G
such that
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}(B)^{g}={\mathscr{C}}_{j}(B^{\prime})
where

. By Proposition

, and we may view

(and hence an element of

. Such an element

.

. Therefore

2.1
(b) we deduce that

H(C_{i})\cong\left(\prod_{C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C_{i})}H(C)\right)\rtimes H(C_{i})^{{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C_{i})}\cong\left(\prod_{C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C_{i})}H(C)\right)\rtimes S_{e_{i}}.

Since
B\cap C_{i}
is the disjoint union over all
C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(B\cap C_{i})
of subsets
B\cap C
, it follows that the orbit
(B\cap C_{i})^{\prod_{C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(C_{i})}H(C)}
is the disjoint union over all
C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(B\cap C_{i})
of orbits
(B\cap C)^{H(C)}
. Hence

(B\cap C_{i})^{H(C_{i})}=\left(\bigcup_{C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(B\cap C_{i})}(B\cap C)^{H(C)}\right)^{S_{e_{i}}}

and therefore, since
|{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(B\cap C_{i})|=y_{i}/y_{i-1}
,

\big|(B\cap C_{i})^{H(C_{i})}\big|=\big|(B\cap C)^{H(C)}\big|^{y_{i}/y_{i-1}}\binom{e_{i}}{y_{i}/y_{i-1}}\quad\text{for any $C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i-1}(B\cap C_{i})$.}

By the induction hypothesis
\big|(B\cap C)^{H(C)}\big|=b_{i-1}
. Substituting this into the equation above, and applying the definition of
b_{i-1}
, we obtain

\big|(B\cap C_{i})^{H(C_{i})}\big|=\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\binom{e_{j}}{y_{j}/y_{j-1}}^{y_{i-1}/y_{j}}\Bigg)^{y_{i}/y_{i-1}}\cdot\binom{e_{i}}{y_{i}/y_{i-1}}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\binom{e_{j}}{y_{j}/y_{j-1}}^{y_{i}/y_{j}}=b_{i}.

Therefore Claim 2 holds by induction.

Finally, observe that
C_{s}={\mathscr{P}}
and
G=H(C_{s})
, so that
{\mathscr{B}}=B^{G}=(B\cap C_{s})^{H(C_{s})}
. Therefore, by Claim 2 and recalling that

, we obtain
|{\mathscr{B}}|=b_{s}=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\binom{e_{j}}{y_{j}/y_{j-1}}^{k/y_{j}}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s,e_{1},\dots,e_{s}
be integers, all at least
2
, let
{\mathscr{P}}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{e_{i}}
, and let
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
be a chain of nontrivial partitions of
{\mathscr{P}}
, as in (
1
) with chain stabiliser
G=S_{e_{1}}\wr\ldots\wr S_{e_{s}}
. Then for any subset
B\subseteq{\mathscr{P}}
which is uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
, the setwise stabiliser
G_{B}
of
B
is transitive on
B
.

Proof.

Let
B\subseteq{\mathscr{P}}
be uniform relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
, with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
.
Since, by Lemma
3.2
(b), every
G
-orbit on uniform subsets of
{\mathscr{P}}
with uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
contains a subset
B
of the form described in (
12
), it is enough to prove the result for such a subset
B
. Observe that the subset
B
in (
12
) can be written as
B=E_{1}\times\ldots\times E_{s}
where, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
,

E_{i}:=\left\{\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{e_{i}}\ \vline\ 0\leq\delta_{i}<\frac{y_{i}}{y_{i-1}}\right\}=\mathbb{Z}_{y_{i}/y_{i-1}}.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, the sets

, for all
C\in{\mathscr{C}}_{i}(B)
, form a partition
{\mathscr{C}}_{i}^{B}
of

, and the partitions
{\mathscr{C}}_{i}^{B}
form a chain
\bm{\mathscr{C}}^{B}:\binom{B}{1}={\mathscr{C}}_{0}^{B}\prec{\mathscr{C}}_{1}^{B}\prec\ldots\prec{\mathscr{C}}_{s}^{B}=\{B\}
. We note that some of the subsets

may contain only a single element, (if

), and hence some of these partitions may be equal. This possible degeneracy does not affect our arguments. By Proposition
2.1
(a) the stabiliser in

of this chain

is the group

. Identify each group

acting naturally on

and fixing

pointwise; thus

and in particular

. We shall show that

is transitive on

. By a result in permutation group theory (see, for instance,
[
19
, Lemma 5.4 (iii)]
), the wreath product

of groups

and

\Gamma
and

is transitive on

. (This is true even if one or other of

, so by
[

is transitive on

. Suppose that

the group

∎

-design is a point-block incidence structure in which every point lies in a constant number of blocks. Clearly, any point-block incidence structure which admits a point-transitive automorphism group is a

-design.

Let

-chain-imprimitive

-designs based on the existence of a sequence of integers

as in (

We first deduce from these properties a divisibility condition for these numbers

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s,k,e_{1},\dots,e_{s}
be integers, all at least
2
such that
k<v
where
v=\prod_{i=1}^{s}e_{i}
. Let

, let

, and for

let

Assume that
v-1
divides
(k-1)\cdot d
.

(a)

The number
u:=\frac{(k-1)d}{v-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and each
y_{i}
is a positive integer coprime to
u
. Moreover
y_{1}-y_{0}=\frac{k-1}{v-1}(e_{1}-1)
, and for
2\leq i\leq s
,
y_{i}-y_{i-1}=\frac{k-1}{v-1}(e_{i}-1)\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}e_{j}
.

(b)

If, in addition, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
the integer
y_{i-1}
divides
(e_{i}-1)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}e_{j}\right)/d
, then for each

,
y_{i-1}
divides
y_{i}
and
1<\frac{y_{i}}{y_{i-1}}<e_{i}
.

Proof.

For
0\leq i\leq s
, let

, with
e_{0}=1
. Then for each
i=1,\dots,s
,

c_{i}-1=c_{i-1}(e_{i}-1)+\dots+c_{1}(e_{2}-1)+(e_{1}-1),

and it follows that

divides

.
By assumption

divides
(k-1)\cdot d

as

(15)

y_{i}=1+\frac{u}{d}\cdot(c_{i}-1)\quad\text{for $0\leq i\leq s$},

and
y_{i}
is coprime to

.
Then, for

, using this equation, and noting that

This proves part (a), noting that the product is ‘empty’ if

Now assume, in addition, that for each

the integer

divides

. Thus, since

, it follows that

divides

, and therefore

divides

.
Since

for all

, the

’s are strictly increasing. Then by (

’s are also strictly increasing, and so

.
Since

), we get that

y_{i-1}e_{i}=e_{i}+u\cdot\frac{(c_{i-1}-1)e_{i}}{d}=e_{i}\left(1-\frac{u}{d}\right)+u\cdot\frac{c_{i-1}e_{i}}{d}=e_{i}\left(1-\frac{u}{d}\right)+u\cdot\frac{c_{i}}{d}.

Now

implies that

, so

. Then, since

,

Therefore

. This proves part (b).

y_{i+1}-y_{i}=u\cdot\frac{e_{i+1}-1}{d}\cdot\prod_{j\leq i}e_{j}.

Since
B
has uniform sequence
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})
relative to
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
by Claim 1,
the parameter
y_{i}
divides
y_{i+1}
by Lemma
3.2
(a), and hence
y_{i}
divides
y_{i+1}-y_{i}
. So
y_{i}
divides
u\cdot\frac{e_{i+1}-1}{d}\cdot\prod_{j\leq i}e_{j}
, and since
y_{i}
is coprime to
u
,
y_{i}
divides
\frac{e_{i+1}-1}{d}\cdot\prod_{j\leq i}e_{j}
. This proves condition (FT2), so Claim 3 is proved.

This proves the main assertion of Theorem
1.2
, and the final assertion follows from Claim 1.
Thus Theorem
1.2
is proved.

.

6.
Explicit constructions for flag-transitive,
s
-chain-imprimitive
2
-designs

Note that, although Construction
4.2
gives a general construction for flag-transitive,
s
-chain-imprimitive
2
-designs based on a collection of integers
y_{i}
satisfying certain conditions, we need to produce integers
y_{i}
with the required conditions before we can guarantee existence of such
2
-designs. We do this in Construction
6.1
, where we give explicit parameters
e_{1},\ldots,e_{s}
, and
k
such that the integers
y_{i}
satisfy the hypotheses of Construction
4.2
.

Construction 6.1
.

Let
s,d
be integers, both at least
2
, and define integers
e_{1},\ldots,e_{s},k
by

(17)

e_{1}=d+1,\quad e_{i}=d+\prod_{j\leq i-1}e_{j},\quad\text{for $2\leq i\leq s$, and}\quad k:=1+\frac{v-1}{d}\text{ where }v=\prod_{j\leq s}e_{j}.

Let
{\mathscr{P}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{e_{i}}
, and let
G=S_{e_{1}}\wr\ldots\wr S_{e_{s}}
, the stabiliser of the partition chain
\bm{\mathscr{C}}
as in (
1
) with partitions as in (
4
). For
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, let
y_{i}
be as in (
2
), let
B
be as in (
12
), and let
\mathscr{D}^{\rm ft}(\mathbf{e};k)
be as in Construction
4.2
, where
\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{s})
.

Proposition 6.2
.

The incidence structure
\mathscr{D}^{\rm ft}(\mathbf{e};k)
of Construction
6.1
is a
G
-flag-transitive,
(G,s)
-chain-imprimitive
2
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design with
G=S_{e_{1}}\wr\ldots\wr S_{e_{s}}
and
\lambda=\frac{bk}{vd}
, where
b=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\binom{e_{i}}{y_{i}/y_{i-1}}^{k/y_{i}}
with
y_{i}=\frac{e_{i+1}-1}{d}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}
, and
y_{s}=k
.

Proof.

First we prove that
d
is equal to
d^{\prime}:=\gcd(e_{1}-1,\dots,e_{s}-1)
. We show inductively that
d
divides
e_{i}-1
for each
i
: by (
17
) we have
d=e_{1}-1
so this holds for
i=1
, and if
1<i\leq s
and
d
divides
e_{j}-1
for all
j<i
, then by (
17
),
e_{i}\equiv d+1\equiv 1\pmod{d}
, so
d
also divides
e_{i}-1
. Thus
d
divides
d^{\prime}
. On the other hand
d^{\prime}\leq e_{1}-1=d
and hence
d=d^{\prime}
.

Thus to prove that
\mathscr{D}^{\rm ft}(\mathbf{e};k)
is a
2
-design, it is sufficient to verify that properties (FT1) and (FT2) in Theorem
1.2
hold, since then all the assertions will follow from Proposition
4.3
. First we note that, by (
17
),
v-1=(k-1)\cdot d
, and hence
v-1
divides
(k-1)\cdot d
, proving property (FT1). The second property (FT2) involves the
y_{i}
for
1\leq i<s
. Since
\frac{k-1}{v-1}=\frac{1}{d}
we have, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}
, that
\prod_{j\leq i}e_{j}=e_{i+1}-d
, so by (
2
),

\displaystyle y_{i}=1+\frac{k-1}{v-1}\left(\left(\prod_{j\leq i}e_{j}\right)-1\right)=1+\frac{1}{d}\left(\left(e_{i+1}-d\right)-1\right)=\frac{d+(e_{i+1}-d-1)}{d}=\frac{e_{i+1}-1}{d}.

This implies firstly that
y_{i}
is a positive integer, since
d
divides
e_{i+1}-1
. Also it implies that
y_{i}
divides
(e_{i+1}-1)\left(\prod_{j\leq i}e_{j}\right)/d
. Thus property (FT2) holds, and therefore
\mathscr{D}^{\rm ft}(\mathbf{e},k)
is a
2
-design.

Recall that
\lambda=\frac{bk(k-1)}{v(v-1)}=\frac{bk}{vd}
, where
b:=|{\mathscr{B}}|
is computed using the formula (
14
) in Lemma
3.3
. Recall
y_{i}=\frac{e_{i+1}-1}{d}
for
i<s
and
y_{s}=k
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Having proved Proposition
6.2
, it is now simple to deduce Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For each
s\geq 2
we have infinitely many choices for the integer
d\geq 2
, and hence, by Proposition
6.2
, Construction
6.1
yields infinitely many flag-transitive,
s
-chain-imprimitive
2
-designs, proving Theorem
1.1
.
∎

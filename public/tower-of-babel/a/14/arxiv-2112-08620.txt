Narrow bands in magnetic field and strong-coupling Hofstadter spectra
Xiaoyu Wang, Oskar Vafek

Abstract
We develop a new, efficient, and general method to determine the Hofstadter spectrum of isolated narrow bands. The method works for topological as well as for trivial narrow bands by projecting the zero 𝐁 \mathbf{B} -field hybrid Wannier states – which are localized in one direction and Bloch extended in another direction – onto a representation of the magnetic translation group in the Landau gauge. We then apply this method to find, for the first time, the Hofstadter spectrum for the exact single particle charged excitations in the strong coupling limit of the magic angle twisted bilayer graphene at the charge neutrality point and at | ν | = 2 |\nu|=2 down to low magnetic fields when the flux through the moiré unit cell is only ∼ 1 / 25 \sim 1/25 of the electronic flux quantum i.e. ∼ 1 \sim 1 T at the first magic angle. The resulting spectra provide a means to investigate Landau quantization of the quasiparticles even if their dispersion is interaction induced.

Appendix B
Hybrid Wannier approach in finite magnetic field and numerical procedure

B.1
Hybrid Wannier wavefunction representation of
\mathbf{B}=0
narrow band

In twisted bilayer graphene, hybrid Wannier wavefunctions have been constructed as basis states for describing the narrow band physics of the Bistritzer-MacDonald (BM) Hamiltonian at
\mathbf{B}=0
. Unlike exponentially localized and symmetric Wannier states in both directions, there are no topological obstructions to constructing hybrid Wannier states, which are exponentially localized along one direction and Bloch extended along the other. Detailed discussions of hybrid Wannier states have been given in Refs.
Song et al. 2019
;
Kang and Vafek 2020
. Here we merely outline the general procedure for constructing hybrid Wannier states. We begin by solving for the two narrow band energy eigenstates of the BM Hamiltonian per valley and spin (Eq. (
S70
)), and construct them to also be eigenstates of the
C_{2}T
operator with eigenvalue
+1
; this fixes their phase up to a sign. Next, the hybrid Wannier states
\ket{w_{c}(n_1,k_2\bg_2)}
are constructed as eigenstates of the projected (periodic) position operator
\hat{P}e^{-i\frac{\mathbf{g}_{1}}{N_{1}}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\hat{P}
, where
N_{1}
is a large integer that discretizes the momentum space along the
\mathbf{g}_{1}=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}L_{m}}(1,0)
direction, and
\hat{P}
is the projector onto the
\mathbf{B}=0
narrow bands. The hybrid Wannier states are labeled by the Chern number
c=\pm 1
, momentum
k_{2}\in[0,1)
along the
\mathbf{g}_{2}\equiv\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}L_{m}}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})
direction, and index
n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
denoting the exponential localization of the hybrid Wannier states near the real space strip
\mathbf{r}=(n_{1}L_{1x},y)
. They can be represented as a one-dimensional Fourier transform of the Chern Bloch states
\ket{\Psi_{c}(k_1\bg_1,k_2\bg_2)}
, smooth in
k_{1}
, as
Kang and Vafek 2020
:

\ket{w_{c}(n_1,k_2\bg_2)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{1}}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{1-\frac{1}{N_{1}}}e^{-i2\pi k_{1}n_{1}}\ket{\Psi_{c}(k_1\bg_1,k_2\bg_2)},

(S58)

where
k_{1},k_{2}\in[0,1)
. The hybrid Wannier states in the opposite valley are related by time reversal symmetry.

B.2
Magnetic translation group eigenstates generated by hybrid Wannier states

In finite magnetic field, we choose the Landau gauge
\mathbf{A}=|\mathbf{B}|x\hat{\mathbf{y}}

\ket{W_{c}(k_1,k_2;n_0)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{s_{1}=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i2\pi k_{1}s_{1}}\hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}^{s_{1}}\ket{w_c(n_0,k_2\bg_2)},

(S59)

where
\hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{1,2}}
are generators of magnetic translations by the primitive vectors of the moire unit cell
\mathbf{L}_{1}=L_{m}(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\frac{1}{2})
and
\mathbf{L}_{2}=L_{m}(0,1)
. They are given by:

\hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}=e^{i\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\hat{T}_{\mathbf{L}_{1}},\ \hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{2}}=\hat{T}_{\mathbf{L}_{2}},

(S60)

where
\hat{T}_{\mathbf{L}_{1,2}}
are usual discrete translation operators, defined via their action on a function
\psi(\mathbf{r})
as
\hat{T}_{\mathbf{L}_{1,2}}\psi(\mathbf{r})=\psi(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{L}_{1,2})
, and we have defined a magnetic translation wavevector:

\mathbf{q}_{\phi}=\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}_{2}\right).

(S61)

For rational fluxes
\phi/\phi_{0}=p/q
, it is straightforward to show that
\commutator{\hat{t}_{\bL_1}}{\hat{t}_{\bL_2}^q}=0
, and:

\hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}\ket{W_{c}(k_1,k_2;n_0)}=e^{-i2\pi k_{1}}\ket{W_{c}(k_1,k_2;n_0)},\ \hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{2}}^{q}\ket{W_{c}(k_1,k_2;n_0)}=e^{-i2\pi qk_{2}}\ket{W_{c}(k_1,k_2;n_0)}.

(S62)

Note however that in general
\commutator{\hat{t}_{\bL_1}}{\hat{t}_{\bL_2}}\neq 0
. We can relabel the MTG eigenstates as:

\ket{W_c(k_1,k_2+\frac{l}{q};n_0)},\ k_{1}\in[0,1),\ k_{2}\in[0,1/q),\ l=0,\dots q-1.

(S63)

Here the magnetic Brillouin zone is defined by the magnetic strip
[0,1)\times[0,1/q)
. MTG eigenstates labeled by different
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
quantum numbers in the magnetic Brillouin zone are orthogonal. For a given index
n_{0}
, there are
2q
states generated from hybrid Wannier states, and are labeled by the Chern number
c
and the index
l
of magnetic strips along the
\mathbf{g}_{2}
direction.

Due to the nontrivial band topology encoded in the hybrid Wannier wavefunctions, the MTG eigenstates defined in Eq. (
S59
) are not guaranteed to be orthonormal. Consider the overlap matrix at a given momentum
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
:

\begin{split}&\Lambda_{W}(c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},c_{2}l_{2}n_{2};k_{1},k_{2})\\

(S64)

where on the last line for notational simplicity we have defined

\bar{\mathbf{p}}=\bar{p}_{1}\mathbf{g}_{1}+(k_{2}+\frac{l_{2}}{q})\mathbf{g}_{2}

M(c_{1}\bar{\mathbf{k}},c_{2}\bar{\mathbf{p}};s_{1})
as the matrix elements of
e^{is_{1}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{r}}
in the Chern Bloch basis. The magnetic wavevector
\mathbf{q}_{\phi}
hybridizes Chern Bloch states at different wavevectors satisfying the following two Diophantine equations:

\bar{k}_{1}=[\bar{p}_{1}+s_{1}\frac{p}{2q}]_{1},\ l_{1}=[l_{2}+s_{1}p]_{q},

(S65)

where
[\dots]_{n}
denotes the modulus with respect to integer
n
.

We first discuss the completeness of the basis states. Note that if we were to fix the indices
n_{1}=n_{2}=0
, the
2q
MTG eigenstates are not linearly independent. This is illustrated in Fig.
S2
(a,c), where we show the eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{W}
at momentum
k_{1}=k_{2}=0
. At
\phi/\phi_{0}=p/q
, there are
2q-p
linearly independent states. An analytical proof of the state-deficiency is given in Sec. I of the SM for the chiral limit
w_{0}/w_{1}=0
using an index theorem. On the other hand, a complete basis set can be generated by enlarging the
n_{1,2}
range and choosing
n_{1},n_{2}\in\{0,1\}
for the trial MTG eigenstates. As illustrated in Fig.
S2
(b,d), this procedure generates an overcomplete basis set (i.e., number of independent states greater than
2q
). We therefore choose
2q
states with largest overlap eigenvalues to represent the narrow band Hilbert space in a finite magnetic field. Note however, that only at low magnetic fields are the
2q
largest eigenvalues well separated from the remaining
2q
states by a well defined spectral gap, i.e. the spillover from remote bands is small at low magnetic fields. However, at larger
\mathbf{B}
the overlap spectral gap closes (e.g. Fig.
S2
(d) with
\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}=\frac{2}{13}
), due to significant spillover from the remote bands, making the
\mathbf{B}=0
hybrid Wannier construction less reliable. This is also seen in Fig.
S4
where we make a quantitative comparison of the Hofstadter spectra for the non-interacting BM Hamiltonian calculated using the hybrid Wannier approach and more conventional Landau-level based approach.

The desired orthonormalized basis set
\{\ket{V_a(k_1,k_2)},\ a=1,\dots 2q\}
is thus obtained via:

\ket{V_a(k_1,k_2)}\equiv\sum_{c,l,n_{0}}\ket{W_c(k_1,k_2+\frac{l}{q};n_0)}U(cln_{0},a;k_{1},k_{2})\frac{1}{\sqrt{D_{a}(k_{1},k_{2})}},

(S66)

where
D_{a}
are the largest
2q
eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{W}
for any given
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
, and
U
is a rectangular matrix satisfying:

\sum_{c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},c_{2}l_{2}n_{2}}U^{*}(c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},a;k_{1},k_{2})\Lambda_{W}(c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},c_{2}l_{2}n_{2};k_{1},k_{2})U(c_{2}l_{2}n_{2},b;k_{1},k_{2})=\delta_{a,b}D_{a}(k_{1},k_{2}).

(S67)

One can straightforwardly check the orthonormality condition
\bra{V_a(k_1,k_2)}\ket{V_b(k_1',k_2')}=\delta_{k_{1},k_{1}^{\prime}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{2}^{\prime}}\delta_{a,b}
.

To quantify how well
\ket{V_a(k_1,k_2)}
describes the
\mathbf{B}\neq 0
narrow band Hilbert space, we follow Ref.
Hejazi et al. 2019
and obtain the narrow band eigenstates
\ket{\Psi_n(k_1,k_2)}
by expanding the BM Hamiltonian in the Landau level basis of monolayer graphene. We define the overlap matrix between states generated via these two procedures :

\Lambda_{LL}(a,n;k_{1},k_{2})\equiv\bra{V_{a}(k_1,k_2)}\ket{\Psi_n(k_1,k_2)}.

(S68)

If the hybrid Wannier approach generates exact eigenstates, then for each
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
the matrix
\Lambda_{LL}(a,n;k_{1},k_{2})
is unitary of size
2q\times 2q
. In Fig.
S3
we show both the SVD eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{LL}
and the projected weight (
\text{Tr}[\Lambda_{LL}\Lambda_{LL}^{\dagger}]/2q
) for a few twist angles and ratios of
w_{0}/w_{1}
. Observe that as the magnetic field decreases, the spillover of
\ket{V_a}
into remote bands also decreases, and
\ket{V_a}
extrapolates to the exact narrow band wavefunctions in the
\mathbf{B}\rightarrow 0

B.3
Matrix elements of the non-interacting BM Hamiltonian in the MTG eigenstates

The matrix elements of the BM Hamiltonian in the orthonormalized MTG eigenstate basis is given by:

\begin{split}&\bra{V_a(k_1,k_2)}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{K}}_{BM}(\mathbf{p}-\frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A})\ket{V_b(k_1,k_2)}\\
=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{D_{a}(k_{1},k_{2})}}U^{\dagger}(a,c_{1}l_{1}n_{1};k_{1},k_{2})H_{BM}(c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},c_{2}l_{2}n_{2};k_{1},k_{2})U(c_{2}l_{2}n_{2},b;k_{1},k_{2})\frac{1}{\sqrt{D_{b}(k_{1},k_{2})}},\end{split}

(S69)

where repeated indices are summed over, and:

\begin{split}&H_{BM}(c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},c_{2}l_{2}n_{2};k_{1},k_{2})\\
\equiv&\bra{W_{c_1}(k_1,k_2+\frac{l_1}{q};n_1)}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{K}}_{BM}(\mathbf{p}-\frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A})\ket{W_{c_2}(k_1,k_2+\frac{l_2}{q};n_2)}\\
=&\sum_{s_{1}}e^{i2\pi k_{1}s_{1}}e^{-i\frac{s_{1}(s_{1}-1)}{2}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{L}_{1}}\bra{w_{c_1}(n_1,(k_2+\frac{l_1}{q})\bg_2)}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{K}}_{BM}(\mathbf{p}-\frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A})e^{is_{1}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{w_{c_2}(n_2+s_1,(k_2+\frac{l_2}{q})\bg_2)}.\end{split}

(S70)

The matrix elements of the BM Hamiltonian can be split into two terms

\hat{H}^{\mathbf{K}}_{BM}(\mathbf{p}-\frac{e}{c}\mathbf{A})=\hat{H}^{\mathbf{K}}_{BM}(\mathbf{p})-\frac{\hbar v_{F}}{\ell}(1_{2}\sigma_{y})\frac{x}{\ell},

(S71)

where
\ell\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{eB}}
is the magnetic length.

We stress that due to exponential localization of the hybrid Wannier states along the
\mathbf{L}_{1}
direction, the matrix elements of the BM Hamiltonian in Eq. (
S70
) are non-neglibile only if
n_{1}
and
n_{2}+s_{1}
are close to each other. Since both
n_{1},n_{2}\in\{0,1\}
, this constrains the summation over
s_{1}
to a few moiré lattice constants. In practice we are able to achieve numerical convergence for
s_{1}\in[-4,4]
. Therefore,
x
in the matrix element is
O(L_{m})
making the second term
O(\hbar v_{F}L_{m}/\ell^{2})
. The first term in Eq. (
S71
) is nominally
O(\hbar v_{F}k_{\theta})
except very near the magic angle where there is an additional suppression of the bandwidth by a factor of
\eta\sim 1/40
(in the chiral limit magic angle
\eta
vanishes). The second term is therefore nominally smaller than the first term at weak magnetic fields by a factor of order
O(L^{2}_{m}/(4\ell^{2}))
, except near the magic angle where this factor has an extra enhancement by
1/\eta
.

The matrix elements of the
\mathbf{B}=0
term are calculated as follows:

\begin{split}&H_{BM}^{(1)}(c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},c_{2}l_{2}n_{2};k_{1},k_{2})\\
=&\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{s_{1}\bar{k}_{1}\bar{p}_{1}}e^{i2\pi k_{1}s_{1}}e^{-i\frac{s_{1}(s_{1}-1)}{2}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{L}_{1}}e^{-i2\pi\bar{k}_{1}n_{1}}e^{i2\pi\bar{p}_{1}(n_{2}+s_{1})}\sum_{c_{3}}\varepsilon_{c_{1},c_{3}}(\bar{\mathbf{k}})M(c_{1}\bar{\mathbf{k}},c_{2}\bar{\mathbf{p}};s_{1}),\end{split}

(S72)

where
\varepsilon_{c_{1},c_{3}}(\bar{\mathbf{k}})
is the matrix elements of the zero field BM Hamiltonian in the Chern Bloch basis, and
M
is defined in Eq. (
S64
).

The matrix elements for the vector potential term is calculated as:

\begin{split}&H_{BM}^{(2)}(c_{1}l_{1}n_{1},c_{2}l_{2}n_{2};k_{1},k_{2})\\
=&-\frac{\hbar v_{F}}{\ell}\frac{1}{N_{1}}\sum_{s_{1}\bar{k}_{1}\bar{p}_{1}}e^{i2\pi k_{1}s_{1}}e^{-i\frac{s_{1}(s_{1}-1)}{2}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{L}_{1}}e^{-i2\pi\bar{k}_{1}n_{1}}e^{i2\pi\bar{p}_{1}(n_{2}+s_{1})}N(c_{1}\bar{\mathbf{k}},c_{2}\bar{\mathbf{p}};s_{1}),\end{split}

(S73)

where we have defined:

N(c_{1}\bar{\mathbf{k}},c_{2}\bar{\mathbf{p}};s_{1})\equiv\frac{1}{N_{1}L_{1,x}}\frac{1}{qN_{2}L_{2}}\int_{-N_{1}L_{1,x}/2}^{N_{1}L_{1,x}/2}\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{qN_{2}L_{2}}\mathrm{d}y\Psi_{c_{1}\bar{\mathbf{k}}}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\left[\sigma_{y}\mu_{0}\frac{x}{\ell}e^{is_{1}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\right]\Psi_{c_{2}\bar{\mathbf{p}}}(\mathbf{r}).

(S74)

Here we write it explicitly as a real space integral. We chose the momentum space mesh to be
k_{1}=\frac{m_{1}}{N_{1}}
where
m_{1}=0,\dots,N_{1}-1
, and
k_{2}=\frac{m_{2}}{N_{2}q}
where
m_{2}=0,\dots,N_{2}-1
. The
x
-integration range in Eq. (
S74
) is necessary to avoid revivals of the hybrid Wannier states since
\ket{w_{c}(n+N_1,k_2\bg_2)}=\ket{w_{c}(n,k_2\bg_2)}
. Eq. (
S74
) is calculated by expressing the Chern Bloch states in the plane wave basis, and perform real-space integration accordingly.

B.4
Matrix elements of the projected density operator

In momentum space, the Coulomb interaction takes the form:

\hat{H}_{int}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}V(\mathbf{q})\delta\rho_{\mathbf{q}}\delta\rho_{-\mathbf{q}}

(S75)

where the projected electron density operator in valley
\mathbf{K}
is given by:

\hat{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}=\sum_{a,k_{1},k_{2};b,p_{1},p_{2}}\bra{V_a(k_1,k_2)}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{V_b(p_1,p_2)}\mathfrak{d}^{\dagger}_{a,k_{1},k_{2}}\mathfrak{d}_{b,p_{1},p_{2}}

(S76)

The density operator in valley
\mathbf{K}^{\prime}
is related via
C_{2}P
symmetry.

The background charge term
\bar{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}
can be calculated from valley
\mathbf{K}
(guaranteed by
C_{2}P

\bar{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}=2\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\mathbf{q},m\mathbf{g}_{1}+n\mathbf{g}_{2}}\sum_{a,k_{1},k_{2}}\bra{V_a(k_1,k_2)}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{V_a(k_1,k_2)}

(S77)

where
2
comes from spin degeneracy (neglecting Zeeman splitting). Eqs. (
S76
) and (
S77
) are calculated in a similar fashion to the matrix elements of the BM Hamiltonian discussed previously. We choose

for numerical convergence.

Appendix C
Solving for the dispersion of charged excitations in the strong coupling limit using Landau-level based approach

C.1
MTG eigenstates generated from Landau level states

In the main text we have focused our attention on the hybrid Wannier approach and solving for the
\mathbf{B}\neq 0
dispersion of charged excitations. Here we also present a calculation based on the Landau-level approach
Bistritzer and MacDonald 2011a
;
Moon and Koshino 2014
;
Hejazi et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2019
, which is much more computationally demanding at low
\mathbf{B}
. Later we provide a consistency check between Landau-level approach and the hybrid Wannier approach.

We begin with a brief discussion of the Landau level eigenstates of the Dirac Hamiltonian of monolayer graphene. For simplicity we consider the following Dirac Hamiltonian in a magnetic field:

\hat{H}^{\mathbf{K}}_{l}(\mathbf{p}-\frac{e\mathbf{A}}{c})=v_{F}\left[\sigma_{x}(p_{x}-K_{l,x})+\sigma_{y}(p_{y}-K_{l,y}-x/\ell^{2})\right].

(S78)

Here
l=1,2
is the layer index, and
\mathbf{K}_{l}=(K_{l,x},K_{l,y})
is the position of the Dirac cone in the reciprocal space. The eigenstates of the Dirac Hamiltonian are solved by going to the harmonic oscillator basis:
x=\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{2}}(a+a^{\dagger})
, and
p_{x}=\frac{1}{i\sqrt{2}\ell}(a-a^{\dagger})
. The particle-hole symmetric Landau level eigenstates are given as:

\bra{\br}\ket{\psi^{(l)}_{n\gamma}(k_2)}=e^{iK_{l,x}x}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{L_{m}}k_{2}y}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{n}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2})\\
-i\gamma\phi_{n-1}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2})\end{pmatrix},

(S79)

where
\epsilon_{n\gamma}=\frac{v_{F}}{\ell}\gamma\sqrt{2n}
is the energy of the Dirac Hamiltonian, labeled by
n=1,2,\dots
, and
\gamma=\pm 1
corresponds to positive and negative energy solutions. In addition, there is an anomalous zero energy state given by

\bra{\br}\ket{\psi^{(l)}_{0}(k_2)}=e^{iK_{l,x}x}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{L_{m}}k_{2}y}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{n}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2})\\
0\end{pmatrix}

(S80)

which lives on the A sublattice.
\phi_{n}(x)
is the eigenfunction of
a^{\dagger}a
, and is given to be:

\phi_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}n!}}e^{-x^{2}/2\ell^{2}}H_{n}(x/\ell),

(S81)

where
H_{n}(x)
is the Hermite polynomial. The shift in the position for a given momentum
k_{2}\mathbf{g}_{2}
is given by:

\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{L_{m}}k_{2}-K_{l,y}\right)\ell^{2}.

(S82)

In the Landau level basis, the eigenstates of the MTG are generated as:

\begin{split}\ket{\Psi_{n\gamma}^{(l)}(k_1,k_2)}&=\sum_{s_{1}=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i2\pi k_{1}s_{1}}\hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}^{s_{1}}\ket{\psi^{(l)}_{n\gamma}(k_2)}\\
&=\sum_{s_{1}}e^{i2\pi(k_{1}-\frac{k_{2}}{2})s_{1}}e^{-i\frac{s_{1}(s_{1}-1)}{2}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{L}_{1}}e^{-is_{1}K_{l,x}L_{1,x}}\ket{\psi^{(l)}_{n\gamma}(k_2+s_1\frac{\phi}{\phi_0})}.\end{split}

(S83)

It is straightforward to check that:

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{n\gamma}^{(l)}(k_1+1,k_2)}

\displaystyle=\ket{\Psi_{n\gamma}^{(l)}(k_1,k_2)},

(S84)

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{n\gamma}^{(l)}(k_1,k_2+\frac{\phi}{\phi_0})}

\displaystyle=e^{iK_{l,x}L_{1,x}}e^{-i2\pi(k_{1}-\frac{k_{2}}{2})}\ket{\Psi_{n\gamma}^{(l)}(k_1,k_2)},

(S85)

\displaystyle\bra{\Psi_{n\gamma}^{(l)}(k_1,k_2)}\ket{\Psi_{n'\gamma'}^{(l')}(p_1,p_2)}

\displaystyle=\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}}\delta_{p_{1},p_{2}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\delta_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}.

(S86)

Therefore, the MTG eigenstates defined in
(k_{1},k_{2})\in[0,1)\times[0,\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}})
form a complete and orthornomal basis set in a finite magnetic field.

The exact eigenstates of the narrow bands for non-interacting BM Hamiltonian are solved by computing the matrix elements in these MTG eigenstates.

C.2
Strong coupling Hofstadter spectra using Landau level approach

We project the strong coupling Hamiltonian onto the
\mathbf{B}\neq 0
narrow band eigenstates, which we label as
\ket{\Psi_{a,\bk}}
where
a=1,\dots 2q
, and
\mathbf{k}=k_{1}\mathbf{g}_{1}+k_{2}\mathbf{g}_{2}
. The Hamiltonian can be written as:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{int}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum V_{\mathbf{q}}\delta\rho_{\mathbf{q}}\delta\rho_{-\mathbf{q}},

(S87)

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta=\mathbf{K},\mathbf{K}^{\prime}}\sum_{s=\uparrow,\downarrow}\sum_{a\mathbf{k},b\mathbf{p}}\bra{\Psi_{\eta s, a,\bk}}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\Psi_{\eta s, b,\bp}}\mathfrak{d}^{\dagger}_{\eta s,a,\mathbf{k}}\mathfrak{d}_{\eta s,b,\mathbf{p}}-\bar{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}},

(S88)

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\mathbf{q},m\mathbf{g}_{1}+n\mathbf{g}_{2}}\sum_{\eta,s}\sum_{a,\mathbf{k}}\bra{\Psi_{\eta s, a,\bk}}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\Psi_{\eta s, a,\bk}}.

(S89)

Here
1_{4}
is the identity operator in the Hilbert space spanned by layer and sublattice degrees of freedom. For completeness we added the valley (
\eta
) and spin (
s
) indices as subscripts to the narrow band eigenstates. The background charge density term
\bar{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}
can be calculated entirely in valley
\mathbf{K}
, due to the
C_{2}P
symmetry relating the eigenstate wavefunctions in the two valleys. To obtain the energy spectrum of excitations at integer fillings we apply the double commutator method discussed in the main text.

The key numerical procedure is to calculate the overlap matrix
\bra{\Psi_{a,\bk}}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\Psi_{b,\bp}}
, which we discuss below. From here onward we also drop the valley and spin indices for notational convenience. We proceed by constructing the matrix in the MTG eigenstate basis:

\bra{\Psi^{(l_1)}_{n_1\gamma_1,\bk}}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\Psi^{(l_2)}_{n_2\gamma_2,\bp}},

(S90)

and then project onto the narrow bands via:

\bra{\Psi_{a,\bk}}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\Psi_{b,\bp}}=U^{\dagger}_{a,n_{1}\gamma_{1}l_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bra{\Psi^{(l_1)}_{n_1\gamma_1,\bk}}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\Psi^{(l_2)}_{n_2\gamma_2,\bp}}U_{n_{2}\gamma_{2}l_{2},b}(\mathbf{p}),

(S91)

where
U
is a rectangular part of the unitary matrix that diagonalizes the BM Hamiltonian. Repeated indices are summed over.

The matrix element defined in Eq. (
S90
) is calculated as follows:

\begin{split}&\bra{\Psi^{(l_1)}_{n_1\gamma_1,\bk}}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\Psi^{(l_2)}_{n_2\gamma_2,\bp}}\\
=&\sum_{s_{1},s_{1}^{\prime}}e^{-i2\pi k_{1}s_{1}}e^{i2\pi p_{1}s_{1}^{\prime}}e^{i2\pi q_{1}s_{1}}\bra{\psi^{(l_1)}_{n_1\gamma_1}(k_2)}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}^{s_{1}^{\prime}-s_{1}}\ket{\psi^{(l_2)}_{n_2\gamma_2}(p_2)}\\
=&\sum_{s_{1}}\delta_{[p_{1}+q_{1}]_{1},k_{1}}e^{i2\pi p_{1}s_{1}}\bra{\psi^{(l_1)}_{n_1\gamma_1}(k_2)}1_{4}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\hat{t}_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}^{s_{1}}\ket{\psi^{(l_2)}_{n_2\gamma_2}(p_2)}\\
=&\delta_{l_{1},l_{2}}\sum_{s_{1}}\delta_{[p_{1}+q_{1}]_{1},k_{1}}e^{i2\pi(p_{1}-\frac{p_{2}}{2})s_{1}}e^{-i\frac{s_{1}(s_{1}-1)}{2}\mathbf{q}_{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{L}_{1}}e^{-is_{1}K_{l,x}L_{1,x}}\bra{\psi^{(l_1)}_{n_1\gamma_1}(k_2)}1_{2}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\psi^{(l_1)}_{n_2\gamma_2}(p_2+s_1\frac{\phi}{\phi_0})}.\end{split}

(S92)

Here we only calculate the expression when both Landau level indices
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
are non-zero. The case where either is zero can be calculated straightforwardly. The expectation value of
1_{2}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}
operator in the Landau level basis of layer
l
is given as follows:

\begin{split}&\bra{\psi^{(l)}_{n_1\gamma_1}(k_2)}1_{2}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\ket{\psi^{(l)}_{n_2\gamma_2}(p_2+s_1\phi/\phi_0)}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\delta_{k_{2},p_{2}+s_{1}\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}+q_{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{n_{1}}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2}),&i\gamma_{1}\phi_{n_{1}-1}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2})\end{pmatrix}1_{2}e^{iq_{x}x}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{n_{2}}(x-(\tilde{k}_{2,l}-q_{y})\ell^{2})\\
-i\gamma_{2}\phi_{n_{2}-1}(x-(\tilde{k}_{2,l}-q_{y})\ell^{2})\end{pmatrix}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\delta_{k_{2},p_{2}+s_{1}\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}+q_{2}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\phi_{n_{1}}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2})e^{iq_{x}x}\phi_{n_{2}}(x-(\tilde{k}_{2,l}-q_{y})\ell^{2})+\right.\\
&\left.\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\phi_{n_{1}-1}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2})e^{iq_{x}x}\phi_{n_{2}-1}(x-(\tilde{k}_{2,l}-q_{y})\ell^{2})\right].\end{split}

(S93)

Note that:

\begin{split}&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\phi_{n_{1}}(x-\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2})e^{iq_{x}x}\phi_{n_{2}}(x-(\tilde{k}_{2,l}-q_{y})\ell^{2})\\
=&e^{iq_{x}\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x{\phi_{n_{1}}(x)}e^{iq_{x}x}{\phi_{n_{2}}(x+q_{y}\ell^{2})}\\
=&e^{iq_{x}\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\phi_{n_{1}}(x)e^{iq_{x}x}e^{ip_{x}q_{y}\ell^{2}}\phi_{n_{2}}(x)\\
=&e^{iq_{x}\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2}}e^{-\frac{i}{2}q_{x}q_{y}\ell^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\phi_{n_{1}}(x)e^{iq_{x}x+iq_{y}\ell^{2}p_{x}}\phi_{n_{2}}(x)\\
=&e^{iq_{x}\tilde{k}_{2,l}\ell^{2}}e^{-\frac{i}{2}q_{x}q_{y}\ell^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x\phi_{n_{1}}(x)e^{c_{-}a+c_{+}a^{\dagger}}\phi_{n_{2}}(x),\end{split}

(S94)

where we have used
e^{X}e^{Y}=e^{X+Y+\frac{1}{2}\commutator{X}{Y}+\dots}
, and:

c_{\pm}=i\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{2}}(q_{x}\pm iq_{y}).

(S95)

The expectation value in the harmonic oscillator basis is calculated as follows:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x{\phi_{n}(x)}e^{c_{-}a+c_{+}a^{\dagger}}{\phi_{m}(x)}=\begin{cases}e^{\frac{1}{2}c_{+}c_{-}}\sqrt{\frac{m!}{n!}}(c_{+})^{n-m}L_{m}^{n-m}(-c_{+}c_{-})&\text{ for }n\geq m,\\
e^{\frac{1}{2}c_{+}c_{-}}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{m!}}(c_{-})^{m-n}L_{n}^{m-n}(-c_{+}c_{-})&\text{ for }n<m,\end{cases}

(S96)

where
L_{n}^{m-n}(x)
is the associated Laguerre polynomial,

\displaystyle L^{m}_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{(N+m)!}{(N-k)!(m+k)!k!}(-x)^{k}.

(S97)

C.3
Comparing Landau level approach and hybrid Wannier approach

As has been pointed out by Ref.
Hejazi et al. 2019
, to achieve numerical convergence for the Hofstadter spectra of the non-interacting BM Hamiltonian, the number of Landau levels to be kept is roughly
25q
, when
\phi/\phi_{0}=1/q
. This makes the calculation of strong coupling Hofstadter spectra prohibitively costly at low magnetic fields. On the other hand, as has already been demonstrated earlier in Fig.
S4
, in the hybrid Wannier approach, only two sets of Chern states are needed, making it much more computationally viable.

In Fig.
S5
we show the magnetic field dependence of
|\bar{\rho}_{\mathbf{g}_{1}}|
defined in Eq. (
S89
), calculated using the Landau-level approach for a sequence of upper Landau level cutoffs
n_{LL}
, as well as the hybrid Wannier approach. This shows that to achieve numerical convergence in the Landau level approach a significant number of Landau levels needs to be kept. Therefore, it is preferable to use the hybrid Wannier approach at low magnetic fields.

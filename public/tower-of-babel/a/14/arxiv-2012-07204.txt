Generalizations of degeneracy second main theorem and Schmidt’s subspace theorem
Si Duc Quang

Abstract
. In this paper, by introducing the notion of “ distributive constant ” of a family of hypersurfaces with respect to a projective variety, we prove a second main theorem in Nevanlinna theory for meromorphic mappings with arbitrary families of hypersurfaces in projective varieties. Our second main theorem generalizes and improves previous results for meromorphic mappings with hypersurfaces, in particular for algebraically degenerate mappings and for the families of hypersurfaces in subgeneral position. The analogous results for the holomorphic curves with finite growth index from a complex disc into a project variety, and for meromorphic mappings on a complete Kähler manifold are also given. For the last aim, we will prove a Schmidt’s subspace theorem for an arbitrary families of homogeneous polynomials, which is the counterpart in Number theory of our second main theorem. Our these results are generalizations and improvements of many previous results.

Introduction and Results

Let
V
be a subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
n>0
. Let
l\geq n
and
q\geq l+1.
Hypersurfaces
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
in
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
are said to be in
l
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
if

Q_{j_{0}}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{j_{l}}\cap V=\emptyset\text{ for every }1\leq j_{0}<\cdots<j_{l}\leq q.

If
l=n
then we say that
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
are in
general position
w.r.t
V
. If
V={\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
, we just say that
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
are in general (resp.
N
-subgeneral) position in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
.

In 1933, H. Cartan
[
2
]
established the most important second main theorem for meromorphic mappings from
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
and hyperplanes as follows (here we use the standard notations from Nevanlinna theory which can be found in Section 2).

Theorem A.

Let
f:{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
be a linearly nondegenerate meromorphic mapping and
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be
q
hyperplanes in general position in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then we have

\|\ (q-(N+1))T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}N^{[N]}(r,f^{*}H_{i})+o(T_{f}(r)).

Here, by the notation “
\|P
” we mean the assertion
P
holds for all
r\in[0,\infty)
excluding a Borel subset
E
of the interval
[0,\infty)
with
\int_{E}dr<\infty
.
The Nevanlinna’s defect of
f
with respect to a hypersurface
Q
of degree
d
truncated to level
M
is defined by

\delta^{[M]}_{f,*}(Q)=1-\lim\mathrm{sup}\dfrac{N^{[M]}(r,f^{*}Q)}{dT_{f}(r)}.

Then, from Theorem A we obtain the estimate of total defect for
f
with family
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
as follows:

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta^{[N]}_{f,*}(H_{i})\leq n+1.

In order to deal with the case of arbitrary meromorphic mapping (not require the linear non-degenerate condition), we have to consider
f
as a linearly nondegenerate mapping into the smallest projective subspace
V(\approx{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}))
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
containing
f({\mathbb{C}}^{m})
and consider
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
as a family of
q
hyperplanes in
N
-subgeneral position in
V
. By introducing the notion of Nochka weight, in 1983 Nochka
[
15
]
gave the following second main theorem for this case.

Theorem B
(cf.
[
15
,
17
]
).
Let
f:{{\mathbb{C}}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a meromorphic mapping and
\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
be hyperplanes in
N-
subgeneral position in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})

(N\geq n)
. Then we have

\|\ (q-(2N-n+1))T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}N^{[n]}(r,f^{*}H_{i})+o(T_{f}(r)).

Hence, the bound above of the total defect obtained from this theorem is
(2N-n+1).

In 1992, A. E. Eremenko and M. L. Sodin
[
6
]
firstly proved a second main theorem for arbitrary holomorphic curve from
{\mathbb{C}}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
intersecting
q
hypersurfaces
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
in general position of the form

\|\ (q-2N)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{\deg Q_{i}}N(r,f^{*}Q_{i})+o(T_{f}(r)).

Then, the total defect obtained from this second main theorem is bounded above by
2N
.

We note that, in 1979, B. Shiffman
[
30
]
conjectured that if
f
is an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphiccurve from
{\mathbb{C}}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
then the total defect of
f
with
q
hypersurfaces
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
in general position is bounded above by
N+1
. This conjectured is proved by M. Ru
[
23
]
in 2004. The result of M. Ru is improved by T. T. H. An and H. T. Phuong
[
1
]
with an explicit truncation level for counting functions. Later on, in 2009, M. Ru
[
24
]
proved a more general second main theorem as follows.

Theorem C
(cf.
[
24
]
)
Let
V
be a smooth projective subvariety of dimension
n\geq 1
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
be
q
hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
in general position w.r.t
V
. Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}\to V
be an algebraically nondegenerate holomorphic map. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\bigl\|\ (q-n-1-\epsilon)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\deg Q_{i}}N(r,f^{*}Q_{i})+o(T_{f}(r)).

We see that, the bound above of the total defect obtained from this theorem is
n+1
, and it is believed to be sharp.

Note that all mentioned above results, the mappings always are assumed to be algebraically nondegenerate. In order to consider the case of algebraically degenerate mappings, we need to consider such mappings as algebraically nondegenerate mappings into the smallest subvariety
V
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
containing their images with a family of hypersurface in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
in
N
-subgeneral position w.r.t

In some years later, by developing the method of M. Ru, many authors have given second main theorems for algebraically nondegenerate meromorphic mappings into

of dimension

with hypersurfaces in
N
-subgeneral position. We list here the name of some authors together their bound above of the total defect:

•

2012, Z. Chen, M. Ru and Q. Yan
[

]

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta^{[M]}_{f,*}(Q_{i})\leq l(n+1)
for some

•

2014, L. Shi and M. Ru

]

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta^{[M]}_{f,*}(Q_{i})\leq\dfrac{l(l-1)(n+1)}{l+n-2}

•

2016, L. Giang
[
10

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta^{[M_{0}]}_{f,*}(Q_{i})\leq l(n+1)

However, these results cannot deduce Theorem C of M. Ru or the classical second main theorem of H. Cartan. The difficulty comes from the fact that there is no Nochka weights for the families of hypersurfaces.

Recently, in
[
19
]
the author proposed the replacing hypersurface technique, which allows to generalize the second main theorem for families of hypersurfaces in general position to the case of families of hypersurfaces in subgeneral posiition without using Nochka’s weight. He proved the following.

Theorem D
(cf.
[
19
]
)
Let
V
be a smooth projective subvariety of dimension
n\geq 1
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
be hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
in
l-
subgeneral position with respect to
V
,
\deg Q_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
. Let
d
be the least common multiple of
d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}
, i.e.,
d=l.c.m.(d_{1},\ldots,d_{q})
. Let
f:{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to V
be an algebraically nondegenerate meromorphic mapping. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\bigl\|\ \left(q-(l-n+1)(n+1)-\epsilon\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d_{i}}N^{[M_{0}]}_{Q_{i}(f)}(r)+o(T_{f}(r)),

where
M_{0}=\left[\deg(V)^{n+1}e^{n}d^{n^{2}+n}(l-n+1)^{n}(2n+4)^{n}(q!)^{n}\epsilon^{-n}\right]
.

Here, the notation
[x]
stands for the biggest integer not exceed the real number
x
.

This technique has been applied by later authors for the analogous problems such as: second main theorem for moving hypersurfaces in subgeneral position, non-integrated defect relation of meromorphic mappings on Kähler manifolds intersecting hypersurfaces in subgeneral position, subspace theorem in number theory for family of homogeneous polynomials in subgeneral position. In particular, in 2019 Q. Ji, Q. Yan and G. Yu
[
11
]
applied this method for the case of families of hypersurfaces in
l
-subgeneral with index

and obtained:

•

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta_{f,*}(Q_{i})\leq\left(\dfrac{l-n}{\max\{1,\min\{l-n,\kappa\}\}}+1\right)(n+1)
, without trucation level.

Here, a family of hypersurfaces
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
is said to be in
l
-general position with index
\kappa
in
V
if it is in
l
-subgeneral position w.r.t
V
and
\dim\bigcap_{j=0}^{s}{\mathbb{Q}}_{i_{j}}\cap V\leq n-s-1
for all

.

Actually, in

Theorem E.

Let

The first main theorem of this paper is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})
be a smooth complex projective variety of dimension
n\geq 1
. Let
\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
be a family of hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})

\Delta

\bigl\|\ \left(q-\Delta(n+1)-\epsilon\right)T_{f}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{d_{i}}N^{[M_{0}]}(r,f^{*}Q_{i})+o(T_{f}(r)),

where
M_{0}=\left[d^{n^{2}+n}\deg(V)^{n+1}e^{n}\Delta^{n}(2n+4)^{n}(n+1)^{n}(q!)^{n}\epsilon^{-n}\right]

Hence, this theorem implies the estimate of the total defect as follows:

•

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta^{[M_{0}]}_{f,*}(Q_{i})\leq\Delta(n+1)

Also, as indicated in Section 3, we have the following remarks:

•

If
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
is in general position then

.

•

If

•

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
generalizes all above mentioned results, also improves the result of Q. Ji, Q. Yan and G. Yu.

Notation and Auxialiary results

2.1. Counting function.
We use the following usual notations:

\displaystyle\|z\|

\displaystyle:=\big(|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{m}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\text{ for }z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{B}}^{m}(r)

\displaystyle:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:\|z\|<r\},

\displaystyle S(r)

\displaystyle:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:\|z\|=r\}\ (0<r<\infty),

\displaystyle v_{m-1}(z)

\displaystyle:=\big(dd^{c}\|z\|^{2}\big)^{m-1},

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}(z)

\displaystyle:=d^{c}\log\|z\|^{2}\land\big(dd^{c}\log\|z\|^{2}\big)^{m-1}\text{on}\quad\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{0\}.

For a divisor
\nu
on a ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{m}(R_{0})
of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, with
R_{0}>0
and a positive integer
M
or
M=\infty
, we define the counting function of

by

\displaystyle\nu^{[M]}(z)=\min\ \{M,\nu(z)\},

\displaystyle n(t)=\begin{cases}\int\limits_{|\nu|\,\cap{\mathbb{B}}^{m}(t)}\nu(z)v_{m-1}&\text{ if }m\geq 2,\\
\sum\limits_{|z|\leq t}\nu(z)&\text{ if }m=1.\end{cases}

Similarly, we define
n^{[M]}(t).

Define

N(r,r_{0},\nu)=\int\limits_{r_{0}}^{r}\dfrac{n(t)}{t^{2m-1}}dt\quad(0<r_{0}<r<R_{0}).

Similarly, define
N(r,r_{0},\nu^{[M]})
and denote it by
N^{[M]}(r,r_{0},\nu)
.

For a meromorphic function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{B}}^{m}(R_{0})
, denote by
\nu_{\varphi}
its divisor of zeros and set

N_{\varphi}(r,r_{0})=N(r,r_{0},\nu_{\varphi}),\ N_{\varphi}^{[M]}(r,r_{0})=N^{[M]}(r,r_{0},\nu_{\varphi})\ (r_{0}<r<R_{0}).

For brevity, we will omit the character
[M]
if
M=\infty
.

2.2. Characteristic function and first main theorem.
Fix a homogeneous coordinates system
(x_{0}:\dots:x_{N})
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
f:\mathbb{{\mathbb{B}}}^{m}(R_{0})\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}(\mathbb{C})
be a meromorphic mapping with a reduced representation
\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{N})
. Set
\|\tilde{f}\|=\big(|f_{0}|^{2}+\dots+|f_{N}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
.
The characteristic function of
f
is defined by

T_{f}(r,r_{0}):=\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\dfrac{dt}{t^{2m-1}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{B}}^{m}(t)}f^{*}\Omega\wedge v^{m-1},\ (0<r_{0}<r<R_{0}),

where
\Omega
is the Fubini-Study form on
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}({\mathbb{C}})

Definition

Remark:

\bullet

\bullet

For more general, we have the following definition.

(b) Case 2:

Repeating the argument in the proof of Theorem

With the assumption of Theorem

Proof.
Repeating the above argument, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{S(r)}

\displaystyle\left|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})^{b}\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{\frac{1}{\Delta}qd-d(n+1)-\frac{dm_{0}l}{u}}|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})(z)|^{b}\cdot\prod_{{\mathrel{\mathop{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}\limits_{{0\leq j\leq n}}}}}|P_{i,j}(\tilde{f})(z)|^{\frac{m_{0}}{u}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{q}|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}}\right|^{t}\sigma_{m}

. By the concavity of the logarithmic function, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}b&\int_{S(r)}\log|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})|\sigma_{m}+\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta}qd-d(n+1)-\dfrac{dm_{0}l}{u}\right)\int_{S(r)}\log\|\tilde{f}\|\sigma_{m}\\

for some positive constant

By Jensen’s formula, this inequality implies that

(4.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\biggl(\dfrac{1}{\Delta}qd&-d(n+1)-\dfrac{dm_{0}l}{u}\biggl)T_{f}(r,r_{0})+bN_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}(r)+\dfrac{m_{0}}{u}\sum_{{\mathrel{\mathop{{0\leq j\leq n+1}}\limits_{{1\leq i\leq n_{0}}}}}}N_{P_{i,j}(\tilde{f})}(r)\\

\|F({\bf x})\|_{v}\leq\|{\bf x}\|^{du}_{v},\text{ i.e., }-\frac{1}{u}\log\|F({\bf x})\|_{v}+d\log\|{\bf x}\|_{v}\geq 0.

This implies that

-\dfrac{1}{u}\sum_{v\in S}\log\|F({\bf x})\|_{v}+d\sum_{v\in S}\log\|{\bf x}\|_{v}\leq-\dfrac{1}{u}\sum_{v\in M_{k}}\log\|F({\bf x})\|_{v}+d\sum_{v\in M_{k}}\log\|{\bf x}\|_{v}.

Then we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{n+1}{u}\sum_{v\in S}\log\|F({\bf x})\|_{v}+d(n+1)\sum_{v\in S}\log\|{\bf x}\|_{v}\leq-\frac{n+1}{u}h(F({\bf x}))+dh({\bf x}).

Since
\|P_{i,j}({\bf x})\|_{v}\leq\|{\bf x}\|^{d}_{v}\cdot\|P_{i,j}\|_{v}
for all
i,j
and
v\in M_{k}\setminus S
, we have

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\log\frac{\|{\bf x}\|_{v}^{d}\cdot\|P_{i,j}\|_{v}}{\|P_{i,j}({\bf x})\|_{v}}\leq\sum_{v\in M(k)}\log\frac{\|{\bf x}\|_{v}^{d}\cdot\|P_{i,j}\|_{v}}{\|P_{i,j}({\bf x})\|_{v}}=dh({\bf x})+h(P_{i,j}).

By the subspace theorem due to Schlickewei
[
28
]
(see also Schmidt
[
29
, Theorem 3]
), for every
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
and all
F({\bf x})
outside a finite union of proper linear subspaces, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\max_{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}_{v}}\log\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}\dfrac{\|F({\bf x})\|_{v}\cdot\|L\|_{v}}{\|L(F({\bf x}))\|_{v}}\leq(H_{Y}(u)+\epsilon^{\prime})h(F({\bf x})).

Combining (
5.9
), (
5.10
), (
5.11
) and this inequality, we have

(5.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\Delta}\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_{Q_{j},v}({\bf x})\leq&\dfrac{\epsilon^{\prime}(n+1)}{uH_{Y}(u)}h(F({\bf x}))\\
&+\left(d(n+1)+\frac{(2n+1)(n+1)l\delta}{u}\right)h({\bf x})+O(1)\\
\leq&\left(d(n+1)+\frac{(2n+1)(n+1)l\delta}{u}+\dfrac{\epsilon^{\prime}(n+1)d}{H_{Y}(u)}\right)h({\bf x})+O(1),\end{split}

for all
{\bf x}\in V
outside a finite union of proper algebraic subsets.

Note that
h(F({\bf x}))\leq uh({\bf x})
. Then by choosing

u\geq 4\Delta(2n+1)(n+1)l\delta\epsilon^{-1}\ \text{ and }\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\frac{H_{Y}(u)\epsilon}{4\Delta(n+1)d},

from (
5.12
) we get

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\dfrac{\lambda_{Q_{j},v}({\bf x})}{d}\leq\left(\Delta(n+1)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)h({\bf x})+C,

for all
{\bf x}\in V
outside a finite union of proper algebraic subsets, where
C
is a positive constant.
Since there only finite points
{\bf x}\in V
such that
h({\bf x})
is bounded above by
\dfrac{2C}{\epsilon}
, then

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\dfrac{\lambda_{Q_{j},v}({\bf x})}{d}\leq\left(\Delta(n+1)+\epsilon\right)h({\bf x})

for all
{\bf x}\in V
outside a finite union of proper algebraic subsets. The theorem is proved.
∎

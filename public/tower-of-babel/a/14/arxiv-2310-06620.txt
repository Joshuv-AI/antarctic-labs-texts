Non-explicit sharp profile for perturbed nonlinear heat equations with gradient terms
Maissâ Boughrara

Abstract
We consider a class of blow-up solutions for perturbed nonlinear heat equations involving gradient terms. We first prove the single point blow-up property for this equation and determine its final blow-up profile. We also give a sharper description of its blow-up behaviour, where we take as a profile some suitably chosen solution of the unperturbed semilinear heat equation. The proof relies on selfsimilar variables with involved arguments to control the gradient term.



1
Introduction and statement of the results

We are concerned with blow-up phenomenon for the following perturbed nonlinear heat equation:

\begin{split}u_{t}=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u+h(u,\nabla u),\\
u(.,0)=u_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{split}

(1.1)

where
1<p
, if
N\geq 3
then
(N-2)p<(N+2)
, and

h\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}),|h(u,v)|\leq C(|u|^{\gamma}+|v|^{\overline{\gamma}}+1),

(1.2)

with
0\leq\gamma<p,0\leq\overline{\gamma}<\frac{2p}{p+1}
and
C>0
.

The unperturbed case (i.e
h\equiv 0
) has been extensively studied in the literature, and non bibliography can be exhaustive. We simply refer to the book by Quittner and Souplet in
[
QS19
]
and the references therein. In this paper, our aim is to consider the perturbed case, and to address the question of refining the blow-up profile. Using standard contraction arguments, the Cauchy problem for equation (
1.1
) is well posed in
W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, locally in time. Therefore, the maximal solution
u(t)
exists for all
t\in[0,T)
, with
T\leq+\infty
.
Then, either it is a global solution, or it exists only up to some finite time
T<+\infty
called the blow-up time, and in this case the solution blows up in the following sense :

||u(t)||_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\longrightarrow+\infty\text{ when }t\rightarrow T.

We say that
a
is a blow-up point of
u
, if there exists a sequence
(x_{n},t_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
such that

(x_{n},t_{n})\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}(a,T)\text{ and }u(x_{n},t_{n})\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty.

Some authors investigated the asymptotic behaviour of blow-up solutions. We may cite Souplet, Tayachi and Weissler in
[
STW96
]
, Alfonsi and Weissler in
[
AW92
]
. In particular, in
[
EZ11
]
, Ebde and Zaag constructed a solution of (
1.1
) which blows up at some point
a
. Moreover, they showed that the constructed solution obeys the following blow-up profile

\left|\left|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(.,t)-f\left(\frac{.-a}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}}\right)\right|\right|_{W^{1,\infty}}=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\log(T-t)|}}),

(1.3)

where

f(z)=\left(p-1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{4p}|z|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(1.4)

However, two issues remain unsolved following that work:

•

Is
a
the only blow-up point?

•

Can we define a notion of final blow-up profile
u_{*}
, defined for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{a\}
such that

u_{*}(x)=\underset{t\rightarrow T}{\lim}u(x,t)?

(1.5)

Can we determine its equivalent near the blow-up point?

As a matter of fact, our first aim in this paper is to answer those open questions. More precisely, we have the following statement.

Our next goal is to see whether we can improve the error estimate in (
1.3
), or whether we can derive a Taylor expansion of the solution. As it was noted by Zaag in
[
Zaa02
]
, any attempt of doing such thing will lead to computations trapped in the scales of powers of

[

In some sense, the problem comes from the fact that we are linearising our solution around the profile

) and in (

[

]
, a good candidate for that sharper version may be simply the solution of the heat equation with a pure power source (i.e.

). However, there is one draw back in this idea: We are replacing an explicit profile by a non-explicit one.

Proof.

Therefore, by projecting (

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\int v^{2}_{+}\rho=\int\mathcal{L}v_{+}.v_{+}\rho+\int\pi_{+}[c(\phi,p)v^{2}]v_{+}\rho+\int\pi_{+}[F(v,\nabla v)]v_{+}\rho.

\overset{.}{z}\geq\frac{1}{2}z-CN-Ce^{-\nu s}.

Using the same arguments with the neutral and the stable component

N\leq\epsilon(x+y+z)+\delta^{\frac{k}{2}}J.

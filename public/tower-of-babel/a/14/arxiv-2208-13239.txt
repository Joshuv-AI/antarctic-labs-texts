Abstract
. It is proved for a strongly pseudoconvex domain D D in ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} with 𝒞 2 , α \mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha} -smooth boundary that any complex geodesic through every two close points of D D sufficiently close to ∂ D \partial D and whose difference is non-tangential to ∂ D \partial D intersect a compact subset of D D that depends only on the rate of non-tangentiality. As an application, a lower bound for the Kobayashi distance is obtained.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32F45

1.
Introduction

In
[
10
]
Huang proved the localization theorem for infinitesimal extremal mappings near
\mathcal{C}^{3}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex points.

This result, which was initially developed to solve a conjecture of
Abate-Vensentini in
[
9
]
, has found many other applications in working on various problems. For instance, it plays a crucial role in solving the homogeneous Monge-Ampère equations
or in establishing various regularity for CR mappings.
Its immediate consequence was also the existence of geodesics with prescribed boundary data, a result previously obtained in
[
5
]
within the class of
\mathcal{C}^{14}
-smooth strictly linearly convex domains.
Recently, Huang
[
11
]
showed that his result remains true for strongly pseudoconvex point with only
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-regularity,
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

In this paper we extend the localization theorem to non-infinitesimal settings, while the estimate we are able to achieve applied to infinitesimal case is sharper.

Some part of our paper is devoted to yet another application: we obtain lower bounds for the Kobayashi distance. Estimates that we are going to present being in the spirit of Balogh-Bonk results
[
2
]
are sensitive in the non-tangential case.

2.
Definitions and main results

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
z,w\in D,
and
X\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.
The Kobayashi distance
k_{D}
is the largest pseudodistance not exceeding the Lempert function

l_{D}(z,w)=\inf\{\tanh^{-1}|\alpha|:\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,D)\hbox{ with }\varphi(0)=z,\varphi(\alpha)=w\},

where
\Delta
is the unit disc and
\tanh^{-1}t=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+t}{1-t}.
This distance is the integrated form of the Kobayashi metric

\kappa_{D}(z;X)=\inf\{|\alpha|:\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,D),\varphi(0)=z,\alpha\varphi^{\prime}(0)=X\}.

Set
\delta_{D}(z)=\mbox{dist}(z,\partial D)
and
h_{D}(z,w)=\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}.

Recall that if
p
is a Dini-smooth boundary point of
D
, then
(see
[
20
, Corollary 8]
)

(1)

k_{D}(z,w)\leq\log\left(1+\frac{2|z-w|}{h_{D}(z,w)}\right),\quad z,w\mbox{ near }p.

The example of the unit ball shows a similar lower bound has to include the term

\frac{|z-w|^{2}}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}.

Let us mention two results in this direction. If
p
is a strongly pseudoconvex boundary point of
D
, then
there is
c>0
such that (see
[
22
, Theorems 1.6 and 1.7]
)

(2)

k_{D}(z,w)\geq\log\left(1+\frac{c|z-w|}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{c|z-w|}{\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}\right),\quad z,w\mbox{ near }p.

For strongly pseudoconvex domain
D,
Balogh and Bonk introduced a positive function
g_{D}
on
D\times D
which depends on
\delta_{D}
and the Carnot-Carathéodory metric on
\partial D
(see
[
2
, formula (1.2)]
) and they proved that
g_{D}-k_{D}
is bounded on
D\times D.
As a consequence of their work, there exists
C>0
such that

(3)

\log\left(1+\frac{|z-w|^{2}}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}\right)-C\leq k_{D}(z,w)

\leq\log\left(1+\frac{|z-w|}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}\right)+C,

which also follows from (
1
) and (
2
).

The following estimate is more sensitive in the non-tangential case.

Proposition 1
.

Let
p
be a strongly pseudoconvex boundary point of a domain
D.

Then there exist a neighborhood
U
of
p
and a constant
c>0
such that

k_{D}(z,w)\geq\log\left(1+\frac{c|(z-w)_{n}|}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}\right),\quad z,w\in D\cap U.

Here
X_{n}
denotes the projection of
X
on the real normal to
\partial D
at the closest point
p(z)\in\partial D

to
z.
Note that
|X_{n}|=2|\mbox{Re}\langle X,g_{D}(z)\rangle|,

where
g_{D}(z)=:\bar{\partial}\tilde{\delta}_{D}(z)=\bar{\partial}\tilde{\delta}_{D}(p(z))
and
\tilde{\delta}_{D}
is the signed distance to
\partial D.

Proposition
1
together with (
2
) leads to the following more precise estimate:

k_{D}(z,w)\geq\log\left(1+c\left(\frac{|(z-w)_{n}|+|z-w|^{2}}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}+\frac{|z-w|}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}}+\frac{|z-w|}{\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}\right)\right).

Actually, it is a consequence of the trivial inequality:
\max\{\log(1+x_{1}),\log(1+x_{2})\}\geq\log\left(1+\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}\right).

One may expect similar results, replacing the real by the complex normal.

Proposition 2
.

Let
p
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex boundary point of a domain
D.

Then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
p
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
one may find a constant
c=c(\varepsilon)>0
such that
if
z,w\in D\cap U
and
|(z-w)_{N}|\geq\varepsilon|z-w|,
then

k_{D}(z,w)\geq\log\left(1+\frac{c|(z-w)_{N}|}{\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}\delta_{D}(w)^{1/2}}\right).

Here and in the sequel
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
X_{N}
denotes the projection of
X
on the complex normal to
\partial D

at the closest point
p(z)\in\partial D
to
z.
Note that
|X_{N}|=2|\langle X,g_{D}(z)\rangle|.

It is natural to ask if this result remains true for
\varepsilon=0,
that is, without the assumption about
(z-w)_{N}.

Proposition
8
below gives a partial answer to this question.

Thy key point in the proof of Proposition
2
is the following result which is also of independent interest. To formulate it let us introduce a class of
FH
-domains
. It is composed of bounded pseudoconvex domains that either have
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth boundary, or their closures posses Stein neighborhood bases. The motivation for the name that we are using here comes from the exposing type theorem that is essentially due to the work of Huang (see
[
9
, Lemma 1]
) for applying a local version of the Fornaess Fornæss embedding theorem
[
8
]
. Note that any strongly pseudoconvex domain is an
FH
-domain.

Theorem 3
.

Let
D
be an
FH
-domain. Suppose that
p
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex boundary point of
D
. Then there is a neighborhood
U
of
p
and a constant
C>0
such

|(z-w)_{N}|\leq C|z-w|\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)

for any extremal mapping
\varphi
through
z
and
w
, where
z\neq w\in D\cap U
.

As we mentioned in the introduction the infinitesimal counterpart of the above result has been obtained in
[
10
,
11
]
. Note that it can be deduced directly from Theorem
3
, as the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 1]
implies that any extremal mapping whose diameter is small enough must be a complex geodesic.

The proof presented here is based on the deep theory of Lempert
[
16
,
17
]
and its extension to
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-case due to Huang and Lempert. Instead of considering additional Lagrange-Euler equations or investigating subtle (e.g. boundary) perturbation of stationary discs, the main point of our argument relies on a careful analysis of the scaling carried out in
[
16
]
.

Theorem
3
together with
[
5
, Proposition 4]
(see also (
5
) below) leads to the following

Corollary 4
.

Let
D
and
p
be as in Theorem
3
.
Then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
there exist a neighborhood
U
of
p
and a compact subset
K
of
D
such that
if
z\neq w\in D\cap U
and
|(z-w)_{N}|\geq\varepsilon|z-w|,
then any extremal mapping through
z
and
w
intersects
K
.

The respective definitions, as well as other facts about complex geodesic, are given in section
3
.

3.
Extremal mappings and complex geodesics

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{d},

z\neq w\in D,
and
0\neq X\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

A mapping
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,D)
is called:

– extremal for
\kappa_{D}(z;X):=\alpha
(in short,
\varphi=\varphi_{z;X}
)
if
\varphi(0)=z
and
\alpha\varphi^{\prime}(0)=X.

– extremal for
k_{D}(z;w):=\alpha
(in short,
\varphi=\varphi_{z,w}
)
if
\varphi(0)=z,

\varphi(\alpha)=w

and
k_{D}(z,w)=\tanh^{-1}\alpha.

– complex geodesic if
k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta),\varphi(\eta))=k_{\Delta}(\zeta,\eta)
for any
\zeta,\eta\in\Delta.

Recall that a bounded
D
is said to be strictly linearly convex if
D
has
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth boundary and, for any
p\in\partial D,
the restriction of the Hessian of
\tilde{\delta}_{D}
on
T_{p}^{{\mathbb{C}}}(\partial D)
is a positive Hermitian form.

According to Lempert’s theory, if
D
is bounded strictly linearly convex, there exist unique extremal mappings for
\kappa_{D}(z;X)
and
k_{D}(z,w),
and they are complex geodesics. Conversely, after a reparametrization,
any complex geodesic
\varphi
is extremal for
\kappa_{D}(\varphi(\zeta);\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta))
and

k_{D}(\varphi(\zeta),\varphi(\eta))
for any
\zeta,\eta\in\Delta.
It is also known (see
[
18
]
) that if
D
has a
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary, then
complex geodesics
extend
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha-}
-smoothly to
\overline{\Delta}
(that is
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha-\varepsilon}
-smoothly for any
\varepsilon>0
).

Let us mention the following tangential version of Theorem
3
.

Theorem 5
.

(
[
9
, Theorem 1]
)
Let
D
be an
FH
-domain.
Suppose that
p
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex boundary point of
D.

Then for any neighborhood
U
of
p,
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
for each extremal mapping
\varphi_{z;X}
with
|z-p|<\varepsilon
and

|X_{N}|<\varepsilon|X|,
one has that
\varphi_{z;X}(\Delta)\subset U
and
\varphi_{z;X}
is a complex geodesic.

(
[
3
, Proposition 2.5]
, see also
[
14
]
) The same remains true for each extremal mapping
\varphi_{z,w}

with
|z-p|<\varepsilon,|w-p|<\varepsilon
and
|z-w|_{N}<\varepsilon|z-w|.

Let us remark that Theorem
5
is stated in
[
9
]
under an additional assumption of
\mathcal{C}^{3}
-smoothness, nevertheless the whole proof works in
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-settings, as well.

The next result can be considered as an infinitesimal version of Theorem
3
.

Theorem 6
.

(
[
11
, Corollary 1.2]
;
[
10
, Theorem 2]
if
\alpha=1
)
Let
p
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth strongly pseudoconvex point of a domain
D,
where

\alpha\in(0,1].
Then there exist a neighborhood
U
of
p
and

such that for each extremal

\varphi_{z;X}
with
\varphi_{z;X}(\Delta)\subset U
one has that
|X_{N}|<C\operatorname{diam}^{\alpha}(\varphi_{z;X}(\Delta))|X|.

As we already said one can deduce Theorem
6
directly from Theorem

with a bit better estimate
|X_{N}|<C\operatorname{diam}(\varphi_{z;X}(\Delta))|X|
. Note that this estimate is almost precise.
Indeed, if

is a ball, then

\operatorname{diam}(\varphi_{z;X}(\Delta))\sim\delta_{D}(z)^{1/2}+|X_{N}|/|X|.\

This observation can be partially extended in a more general setting.

Proposition 7
.

Let
D
be a strongly pseudoconvex domain with

\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary.
There exist
C>c>0
such that for any complex geodesic
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,D)
one has that

(4)

c\left(\delta_{D}(\varphi(\zeta))^{1/2}+\frac{|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|_{N}}{|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|}\right)\leq\operatorname{diam}(\varphi),\quad\zeta\in\Delta,

(5)

c\max(\delta_{D}\circ\varphi)^{1/2}\leq\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)\leq C\max(s\circ\delta_{D}\circ\varphi),

where
s(x)=-x^{1/2}\log x.

In (

) we may assume that
\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)
is small enough; therefore
\varphi(\Delta)

is near some
p\in\partial D
and hence
|\varphi^{\prime}(\zeta)|_{N}
is well-defined.

One may expect that

(6)

\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)\sim\max(\delta_{D}\circ\varphi)^{1/2}\sim\max|\varphi^{\prime}|

for any complex geodesic
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}(\Delta,D)
which is parameterized such that

\delta_{D}(\varphi(0))=\max(\delta_{D}\circ\varphi).

.

Proof of Theorem
3

In the proof we can assume that
D
is strictly convex and
\varphi
is a geodesic. Actually, the result is trivial when the diameter of an extremal mapping passing through
z
and
w
is big. If, in turn, it is small enough, the extremal mapping lies entirely in a neighborhood of some boundary point
p
, which can be exposed globally to a strictly convex one — see
[
9
,
10
]
for details (for another exposing theorem see also
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
). Then this mapping is a stationary disc (see
[
17
]
) and consequently a complex geodesic.

Let us recall here again that the core of the our argument relies on a careful analysis of the so called Lempert theory that was proven in
[
16
]
in
\mathcal{C}^{6}
settings. How to extend it to
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
case was explained in a series of papers by Huang and Lempert, see e.g. discussion
[
11
, Remark B]
and references contained therein. Below we shall briefly sketch these ideas

Remark 2
.

For a strictly linearly convex domain
D
with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
-smooth boundary,
z\in D
and
X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
let
f_{D,z,X}
denote a complex geodesic in
D
such that
f_{D,z,X}(0)=z
and
f^{\prime}_{D,z,X}(0)=\lambda X
for some
\lambda>0
.

As noted by Lempert
[
18
, Lemma 3.1]
(see also
[
6
]
), geodesics
f_{D,z,X}
are
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
smooth on
{\mathbb{T}}
. Using the fact that their
\mathcal{C}^{1/2}
norms are uniformly bounded when
z
is within a compact subset of
K
of
D
(note that this bound remains uniform when
D
is perturbed a bit in
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-topology) Lempert showed in
[
19
, Proposition 3]
that the bound on
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
norm is in fact locally uniform; see also
[
9
, Lemma 4]
for a detailed proof of this fact. Summing up,
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
norms of
f_{D,z,X}
remain locally uniform with respect to
z
in
K
and when
D
is perturbed in
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
topology.

Therefore one can deduce the following: if
D_{n}
converges to a strictly convex domain
D
in
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
topology,
K
is a compact subset of
D
and
f_{n}
is a sequence of geodesics in
D_{n}
such that
f_{n}(0)\in K
, then we can find a subsequence converging in
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
topology to a geodesic in
D
. (Using this together with
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
and uniqueness of geodesics in
D
we can even say more:
f_{D,z,X}
varies continuously, as elements of
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}({\mathbb{T}})
, with respect to
z
,
X
and
D
varying in
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
topology.)

We start the proof of Theorem
3
. Take
\tilde{z}\in D
sufficiently close to
\partial D
and a geodesic
\varphi
passing through it. The case that is interesting for us is when
\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)\to 0
(then, of course,
\tilde{z}\to\partial D
, as geodesics are proper).

We shall use some transformations. Let us mention that they as well as all function that will appear below can be chosen to depend continuously on
\tilde{z}
, and all constants will be uniformly bounded. Recall the notation from the introduction: if
X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, then
|X_{N}|=2|\langle X,g_{D}(\tilde{z})\rangle|
.

Translate the domain
D
so that the closest point to
\tilde{z}
on
\partial D
is
0
. Using unitary map we can make the Levi form at
0
of a defining function
r
of
\partial D
to be a positive diagonal matrix. Replacing the coordinates
z_{j}
by
\lambda_{j}z_{j}
we can additionally assume that it is the identity matrix. Using unitary map again we can assume that the tangent space is
\operatorname{Re}z_{1}=0
, i.e. that
r(z)=2\operatorname{Re}z_{1}+|z|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}P(z)+O(|z|^{2+\alpha})
, where
P
is a quadratic polynomial. All these transformations used so far (denote the composition of all of them by
F
) satisfy the following estimate:

\frac{|(\tilde{z}-w)_{N}|}{|\tilde{z}-w|}\sim\frac{|(F(\tilde{z})-F(w))_{N}|}{|F(\tilde{z})-F(w)|}.

Moreover, the point
\tilde{z}
is mapped a point of the form
(-s,0)
, where
s>0
(in particular,
(\tilde{z}-w)_{N}=\tilde{z}_{1}-w_{1}
and
(F(\tilde{z})-F(w))_{N}=F_{1}(\tilde{z})-F_{1}(w)
). Then composing
F
with an additional transformation
z_{1}\mapsto z_{1}+P(z)
,
z^{\prime}\mapsto z^{\prime}
gives a biholomorphic map
G
in a neighborhood
U
of
0
such that

(13)

\frac{|F_{1}(\tilde{z})-F_{1}(w)|}{|F(\tilde{z})-F(w)|}=\frac{|G_{1}(\tilde{z})-G_{1}(w)|}{|G(\tilde{z})-G(w)|}+O(|\tilde{z}|+|\tilde{z}-w|)

and the boundary of
G(D\cap U)
near
0
is of the form
\{2\operatorname{Re}\zeta_{1}+|\zeta|^{2}+O(|\zeta|^{2+\alpha})<0\}
. To see the estimate in (
13
) it suffices to express
P(\tilde{z})-P(w)=\sum(\tilde{z}_{j}-w_{j})\alpha_{j}(\tilde{z},w)
, where
\alpha_{j}
are linear, which implies that
|P(\tilde{z})-P(w)|\leq|\tilde{z}-w|O(|(\tilde{z},w)|)
. Thus (
13
) follows from the trivial estimate
|w|\leq|\tilde{z}|+|\tilde{z}-w|
.

The translation moves
0
to
e_{1}=(1,0^{\prime})
and
G(D\cap U)
to
\{|\zeta|^{2}+O(|\zeta-e_{1}|^{2+\alpha})<1\}
. Then point
\tilde{z}
is mapped to a point of the form
(1-s+\gamma s^{2},0^{\prime})
, where
\gamma\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Clearly
s\to 0
if
\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)\to 0
. Moreover,
d_{D}(\tilde{z})=s+O(s^{2})
.

If
\varphi
is a geodesic passing through
\tilde{z}
and
w
, then
\operatorname{diam}\varphi\gtrsim|\tilde{z}-e_{1}|,|\tilde{z}-w|
(the first inequality follows from the fact that geodesics are proper and that
|\tilde{z}-e_{1}|\sim\delta_{D}(\tilde{z})
, the second is trivial). Thus it suffices to focus on achieving estimates for the term
|G_{1}(\tilde{z})-G_{1}(w)|/|G(\tilde{z})-G(w)|
.

Summing up our situation boils down to the following one:
D
near
e_{1}
is of the form
\{|\zeta|^{2}+O(|\zeta-e_{1}|^{2+\alpha})<1\}
,
\tilde{z}=(1-s+\gamma s^{2},0)
, where
s
is close to
0
and
\gamma
is uniformly bounded. We then aim at proving that
|\tilde{z}_{1}-w_{1}|\leq C|\tilde{z}-w|\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)
for any geodesic
\varphi
in
D
passing through
\tilde{z}
with small diameter and any
w
in its range.

Let
m_{t}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda+t}{1+t\lambda},

A_{t}(z)=\left(m_{t}(z_{1}),\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\frac{z^{\prime}}{1+tz_{1}}\right)
, and

,

converges to

in the

-topology, as

, when restricted to

(see
[

Take

such that an analytic disc

lies entirely in
\{\operatorname{Re}z_{1}>0\}
and its boundary touches
\{\operatorname{Re}z_{1}=0\}

(14)

\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}=\frac{\operatorname{Re}\varphi_{1}(\eta)}{1+|\varphi_{1}(\eta)|^{2}}.

Note that

when

.
It is also clear that the above discs are stationary maps, and thus geodesics, in
A_{t}^{-1}(D)\cap\{\operatorname{Re}z_{1}>-1/2\}

Claim.
There exists
C_{1}>0
such that

We shall apply Remark
2
to domains
D_{t}\cap\{\operatorname{Re}z_{1}\geq-1/2\}
(using cut-off functions we can extend them to globally strictly convex domains). By the claim, after a possible reparametrization, the geodesic
A_{t}^{-1}\circ\varphi

\mathcal{C}^{1+\alpha-\epsilon}
-topology to a geodesic, say
\psi

t
is close to

\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)

\psi
is contained in
\{\operatorname{Re}z_{1}\geq 0\}
, we get that its range cannot lie on a horizontal line (that is a line of the form
{\mathbb{C}}\times\{b\}
). In particular,

|(\psi(\lambda)-\psi(\mu))_{T}|/|\lambda-\mu|
is a strictly positive constant for
\lambda,\mu\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
.
Here and in the sequel we denote
X_{1}=(X_{1},0)
and

|(A^{-1}_{t}(\varphi(\lambda))-A_{t}^{-1}(\varphi(\mu)))_{1}|<C|(A^{-1}_{t}(\varphi(\lambda))-A_{t}^{-1}(\varphi(\mu)))_{T}|,

\varphi
passes through

, we thus get that for any
w

(15)

\frac{|(A^{-1}_{t}(\tilde{z})-A_{t}^{-1}(w))_{1}|}{|(A^{-1}_{t}(\tilde{z})-A_{t}^{-1}(w))_{T}|}<C,

provided that
\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)
is sufficiently small.

Denoting

,

Let us compute

\frac{|(\tilde{z}-w)_{1}|}{|(\tilde{z}-w)_{T}|}=\frac{|(A_{t}(x)-A_{t}(y))_{1}|}{|(A_{t}(x)-A_{t}(t))_{T}|}=\frac{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}|x_{1}-y_{1}|}{|(1+tx_{1})y_{T}|}\leq C\sqrt{1-t^{2}}.

We shall estimate the right-hand side of the inequality above. It follows from (

\frac{(1-t)^{2}}{1+t^{2}}=\frac{|1-\varphi_{1}(\eta)|^{2}}{1+|\varphi_{1}(\eta)|^{2}}

\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\sim|1-\varphi_{1}(\eta)|\leq|\varphi(\eta)-e_{1}|\leq|\varphi(\eta)-\tilde{z}|+|\tilde{z}-e_{1}|\lesssim\operatorname{diam}(\varphi)

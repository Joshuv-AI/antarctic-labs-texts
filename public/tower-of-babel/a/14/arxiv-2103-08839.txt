Abelian hearts of twin cotorsion pairs on extriangulated categories 0 0 footnotetext: Panyue Zhou was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 11901190) and the Scientific Research Fund of Hunan Provincial Education Department (Grant No. 19B239).
Qiong Huang, Panyue Zhou

Abstract
It was shown recently that the heart of a twin cotorsion pair on an extriangulated category is semi-abelian. In this article, we consider a special kind of hearts of twin cotorsion pairs induced by d d -cluster tilting subcategories in extriangulated categories. We give a necessary and sufficient condition for such hearts to be abelian. In particular, we also can see that such hearts are hereditary. As an application, this generalizes the work by Liu in an exact case, thereby providing new insights in a triangulated case. Keywords: Hearts; Cotorsion pairs; Extriangulated categories; d d -cluster tilting subcategories, Fully rigid subcategories. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10.

Introduction

The notion of cotorsion pairs was first introduced by Scale in
[
S
]
, it has been studied in the representation theory.
A careful look reveals that what is necessary to define a cotorsion pair on a category is the existence of an
{\rm Ext}^{1}
bifunctor with appropriate properties. Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]

formalized the notion of an
extriangulated category
by extracting those properties of
{\rm Ext}^{1}
on exact categories and on triangulated categories that seem relevant from the point-of-view of cotorsion pairs.
Exact categories
and triangulated categories are examples of extriangulated categories, while there are some other examples of
extriangulated categories which are neither exact nor triangulated, see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ
]
. Hence,
many results on exact categories and triangulated categories can be unified in the same framework.
Nakaoka and Palu also defined (twin) cotorsion
pairs on extriangulated categories in
[
NP
]
.
They showed that the heart of a twin cotorsion pair was always semi-abelian and the heart of a single cotorsion pair became abelian.
This unified the similar arguments on (twin) cotorsion pairs in exact categories
[
L1
]
and triangulated categories
[
N
]
.

Recently, Liu
[
L2
]
studied a special kind of hearts of twin cotorsion pairs induced by
d
-cluster tilting subcategories in exact categories.
He gave a sufficient-necessary condition when such hearts become abelian.
We know that extriangulated categories are a simultaneous generalization of exact categories and
triangulated categories. A very natural question is whether these similar results hold in extriangulated categories.
In this article, we give an affirmative answer of this question.

This article is organized as follows: In Section 2, we review some elementary definitions
that we need to use, extriangulated categories, twin cotorsion pairs and
d
-cluster tilting subcategories. In Section 3, we prove our main result, see Theorem
3.13
.

The notion of fully rigid subcategories in an extriangulated category was introduced by Liu and Zhou
[
LZ
]
, it can be regarded as a simultaneous generalization of
[
B
, Definition 5.1]
and
[
L2
, Definition 1.5]
.
We recall it here.

Definition 3.2
.

[
LZ
, Definition 2.6]

A subcategory
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathscr{C}
is called
fully rigid
if

(a)

it admits a rigid cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{Y})
and
\mathcal{M}\neq\mathcal{P}
,

(b)

any indecomposable object in
\mathscr{C}
either belongs to
\mathcal{Y}
or belongs to
\mathcal{H}:=\textrm{CoCone}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})
.

Remark 3.3
.

From
[
LZ
, Remark 1.5]
, we can see that when
\mathcal{M}
is fully rigid, it is contravariantly finite, rigid, and
\mathscr{C}/{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp_{1}}
is an abelian category where
{\mathcal{M}}^{\perp_{1}}=\{X\in\mathscr{C}\mid\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{M},X)=0\}.

Remark 3.4
.

From the definition of full rigid subcategories, we know that cluster tilting subcategories are fully rigid, but fully rigid subcategory are not necessarily cluster tilting, see
[
LZ
, Example 5.1]
. Furthermore,
d
-cluster tilting is not always fully rigid when
d\geq 3
, see
[
L2
, Example 3.1]
.

Liu and Nakaoka showed that a
d
-cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{M}
always admits a twin cotorsion pair
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{Y}),(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{N})
, see
[
LN
, Theorem 5.14]
. The heart of such a twin cotorsion pair is just
\mathscr{C}/\mathcal{Y}
. We denote
\mathscr{C}/\mathcal{Y}
by
\underline{\mathscr{C}}
, for any morphism
f\in\mathscr{C}(X,Y)
, we denote its image in
\underline{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)
by

. From now on to the end of the article, let

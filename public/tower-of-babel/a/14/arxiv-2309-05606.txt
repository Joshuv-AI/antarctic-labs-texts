1
Introduction

An edge colouring of
K_{n}
using
k
colours is a map
c:E(K_{n})\to[k]
, where the colour of an edge
uv
is
c(uv)
. Given a simple graph
H
and an edge colouring of
K_{n}
, we say that this colouring contains a
rainbow copy
of
H
if there exists a subgraph of
K_{n}
isomorphic to
H
, whose edges all have different colours. Otherwise, we say this colouring is
rainbow
H
-free
.

For non-negative integers
e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}
, we say the sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
is
n
-good
iff
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}=\binom{n}{2}
. An edge colouring of the complete graph
K_{n}
using
k
colours is said to have
colour distribution sequence

(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
if exactly
e_{i}
edges have colour
i
for every
i\in[k]
. In this case, we also say that this is an
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
-colouring
of
K_{n}
.

In
[
8
]
, Gyárfás and Simonyi initiated the study of rainbow
K_{3}
-free colourings of
K_{n}
. They named these colourings Gallai colourings, referring to Gallai’s related work
[
5
]
on comparability graphs. Since then, many Ramsey-type results
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
and enumeration results
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
for Gallai colourings have been obtained.

A new avenue of research on the distribution of colours in Gallai colourings was proposed by Gyárfás, Pálvölgyi, Patkós and Wales in
[
6
]
. They proved the following theorem, which says that as long as we have sufficiently many vertices, any colour distribution sequence can be realised as a Gallai colouring.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

For every integer
k\geq 2
, there exists an integer
N
such that for all
n\geq N
and all non-negative integers
e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}=\binom{n}{2}
, there exists a Gallai colouring of
K_{n}
with colour distribution sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
.

In
[
6
]
, Gyárfás, Pálvölgyi, Patkós and Wales denoted the smallest such integer
N
by
g(k)
. They also gave the estimate
2k-2\leq g(k)\leq 8k^{2}+1
, and posed the problem of determining the exact order of magnitude of
g(k)
. The gap was closed considerably when Feffer, Fu and Yan
[
4
]
proved that
\Omega(k^{1.5}/\log k)\leq g(k)\leq O(k^{1.5})
. The first main result of this paper resolves this problem.

Theorem 1.2
.

g(k)=\Theta(k^{1.5}/(\log k)^{0.5})
.

Motivated by this result, we study a generalised version of this problem. We first generalise the quantity
g(k)
to

as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
H
be a graph and let
k
be a positive integer. Define
g(H,k)
to be the smallest integer
N
, such that for all
n\geq N
and all non-negative integers
e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}=\binom{n}{2}
, there exists a rainbow
H
-free colouring of
K_{n}
with colour distribution sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
. If such

does not exist, define
g(H,k)=\infty
.

Note that setting
H=K_{3}
recovers the definition of

. Moreover, we have the following simple property of
g(H,k)
.

Lemma 1.4
.

If
H_{1}
is a subgraph of
H_{2}
, then
g(H_{1},k)\geq g(H_{2},k)
.

Proof.

This follows from the observation that any rainbow
H_{1}
-free graph is also rainbow
H_{2}
-free.
∎

The natural questions of interest regarding
g(H,k)
are the following.

Question 1.5
.

For what graphs
H
and integers
k
is
g(H,k)
finite? If
g(H,k)
is finite, What is the order of magnitude for

It turns out that the behaviour of

Definition 1.6
.

Let
H
be a graph and let
k
be a positive integer.

•

H
is
k

of

has a vertex of degree at most

.

•

The degeneracy of

is the smallest positive integer

H
is
k

As two examples, 1-degenerate graphs are exactly forests, and every graph with degeneracy at least

contains a subgraph of minimum degree at least

. With this definition in mind, the results we will prove in this paper are the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

If

for all sufficiently large

.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
H
is a graph on

g(H,k)=\Theta_{m}(k)
.

Theorem 1.9
.

If
H
is a graph on

, then

\Omega_{m}(k)\leq g(H,k)\leq O(k^{1.5}/(\log k)^{0.5}).

2
Trees

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.7
. Note that any forest is the subgraph of a tree, therefore, by Lemma
1.4
, it suffices to prove Theorem
1.7
in the case when
H
is a tree. In fact, we will prove the following stronger result.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every integer
m\geq 2
, there exists a constant
D=D(m)
such that the following holds: If
H
be any tree on
m
vertices, then any edge colouring of
K_{n}
such that every colour is used to colour at most
\frac{1}{D}\binom{n}{2}
edges contains a rainbow copy of
H
.

Note that the condition in Theorem
2.1
implies implicitly that
\frac{1}{D}\binom{n}{2}\geq 1
, or equivalently
n\geq\Omega(\sqrt{D})
.

We first show that the Theorem
2.1
implies Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
D=D(m)
be the constant in Theorem
2.1
. Let
k\geq\lfloor 2D\rfloor
be an integer. For any sufficiently large integer
n
, assume that
\binom{n}{2}=qk+r
, where
0\leq r\leq k-1
. Set
e_{1}=e_{2}=\cdots=e_{k-r}=q
and
e_{k-r+1}=\cdots=e_{k}=q+1
. Then
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
is
n
-good and

e_{i}\leq 1+\frac{1}{k}\binom{n}{2}\leq\frac{1}{D}\binom{n}{2}

for all
i\in[k]
. Hence, by Theorem
2.1
, any colouring of
K_{n}
with colour distribution sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
contains a rainbow copy of
H
. From definition, this proves
g(H,k)=\infty
.
∎

We will prove Theorem
2.1
with
D(m)=(6m)^{6m}
. Given an edge colouring
c
of
K_{n}
and any vertex
v\in V
, let

C(v)=\bigcup_{v^{\prime}\in V\setminus\{v\}}\{c(vv^{\prime})\}

be the set of colours assigned to edges adjacent to
v
. The following technical lemma shows that at least half of the vertices satisfy
|C(v)|\geq 2m+1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer and let
D=(6m)^{6m}
. Let
c
be an edge colouring of
K_{n}
such that every colour is used to colour at most
\frac{1}{D}\binom{n}{2}
edges. Then there are at most
\frac{1}{2}n
vertices
v
such that
|C(v)|\leq 2m
.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by contradiction. Assume that there is a set
V_{0}
of at least
\frac{1}{2}n
vertices
v\in V(K_{n})
such that
|C(v)|\leq 2m
. We show by induction that for every integer
0\leq j\leq 2m
, we can find a subset
V_{j}
of
V_{0}
such that

\left|\bigcap_{v\in V_{j}}C(v)\right|\geq j\text{\quad and\quad}|V_{j}|\geq\frac{n}{2(6m)^{j}}.

The base case follows from the definition of
V_{0}
. Suppose now we have picked
V_{j}
as required for some
0\leq j\leq 2m-1
. Without loss of generality, assume that

\{1,2,\dots,j\}\subseteq\bigcap_{v\in V_{j}}C(v).

From the assumption, there are at most
\frac{j{\binom{n}{2}}}{D}
edges coloured with one of the colours
1,2,\cdots,j
. Hence, by pigeonhole, there exists a vertex
u\in V_{j}
such that there are at most
\frac{2j\binom{n}{2}}{D|V_{j}|}
edges adjacent to
u
with colour
1,2,\cdots,j
. Note that for any
u^{\prime}\in V_{j}\setminus\{u\}
, the colour
c(uu^{\prime})
of the edge
uu^{\prime}
must belong to the set
C(u)
, which has size at most
2m
. By pigeonhole again, there exists some colour
q\in C(u)\setminus[j]
such that at least

\frac{|V_{j}|-1-\frac{2j\binom{n}{2}}{D|V_{j}|}}{2m-j}

vertices
u^{\prime}\in V_{j}\setminus\{u\}
satisfy
c(uu^{\prime})=q
. Set

V_{j+1}=\{u^{\prime}\mid u^{\prime}\in V_{j}\setminus\{u\}\text{ and }c(uu^{\prime})=q.\}

We verify that
V_{j+1}
works.

Indeed, the first condition holds as

\{1,2,\cdots,j,q\}\subseteq\bigcap_{v\in V_{j+1}}C(v).

For the second condition, we have

\displaystyle|V_{j+1}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|V_{j}|-1-\frac{2j\binom{n}{2}}{D|V_{j}|}}{2m-j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|V_{j}|-1-\frac{2j(n-1)(6m)^{j}}{D}}{2m}

\displaystyle\text{by }|V_{j}|\geq\frac{n}{2(6m)^{j}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|V_{j}|-1-\frac{n}{(6m)^{2m}}}{2m}

\displaystyle\text{by }D=D(m)=(6m)^{6m}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|V_{j}|}{6m}\geq\frac{n}{2(6m)^{j+1}},

\displaystyle\text{by }|V_{j}|\geq\frac{n}{2(6m)^{j}}

as required. Hence, we can pick the sets
V_{0},V_{1},\cdots,V_{2m}
by induction.

Note that for any
u\in V_{2m}
, we have

2m\geq|C(u)|\geq\left|\bigcap_{v\in V_{2m}}C(v)\right|\geq 2m.

Hence, there exists a set
C
of size
2m
such that
C(u)=C
for all
u\in V_{2m}
. In particular, for any two vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in V_{2m}
, the colour of the edge
uu^{\prime}
must belong to this set

. Therefore, we have

\binom{|V_{2m}|}{2}\leq 2m\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{D}.

However,

\binom{|V_{2m}|}{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}|V_{2m}|^{2}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{16(6m)^{4m}}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{(6m)^{5m}}>2m\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{D},

a contradiction. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Now we can prove Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We will show that
D(m)=(6m)^{6m}
works.
Proceed by induction on
m
. The case
m=2
is trivial. Suppose we have already established the theorem for all trees with fewer than
m
vertices. Since
H
is a tree, it has a leaf
v_{0}
. Let the vertices in
H
be
v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{\ell-1}
, and suppose the unique edge adjacent to

is
v_{0}v_{1}
. Let
H^{\prime}=H-v_{0}
.

Let

be the subgraph of

induced by the set of vertices

, then

contains at least

. From assumption, each colour is used at most

\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{D(m)}\leq\frac{\binom{|v(G^{\prime})|}{2}}{D(m-1)}

times in

. Therefore, we may apply induction hypothesis to find a rainbow copy of

in

. Suppose vertex

in

is embedded to vertex

in

Hence, we can find a vertex

3
Graphs with degeneracy at least 3

In this section we look at the case when
H
is a graph with degeneracy at least 3 and prove Theorem
1.8
. We begin with the lower bound.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
n\geq m\geq 3
and
e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}\geq 0
are integers such that
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
is
n
-good and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{e_{i}}{2}<\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{m(m-1)(m-2)}.

Then any
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
-colouring of
K_{n}
contains a rainbow copy of
K_{m}
.

Proof.

Fix an
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
-colouring of
K_{n}
. For each
i\in[k]
, let
E_{i}
be the set of edges in
K_{n}
that has colour
i
. Pick a size
m
subset
U
of
[n]
uniformly at random. For each
i\in[k]
and any distinct edges
e,e^{\prime}\in E_{i}
, the probability that both
e
and
e^{\prime}
are in
U
is either
\frac{\binom{n-3}{m-3}}{\binom{n}{m}}=\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{n(n-1)(n-2)}
if
e
and
e^{\prime}
share a vertex, or
\frac{\binom{n-4}{m-4}}{\binom{n}{m}}=\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}\leq\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{n(n-1)(n-2)}
if they don’t. Using a union bound, we see that the expected number of monochromatic edge pairs in
U
is at most

\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{n(n-1)(n-2)}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{e_{i}}{2}<1.

Hence, there exists a realisation of
U
such that the complete graph induced by the
m
vertices in
U
is rainbow.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
H
be a graph on
m
vertices. Then
g(H,k)\geq k/m^{3}
for all sufficiently large
k
.

Proof.

When
m\leq 2
, the theorem is trivial. When
m\geq 3
, let
n=\lfloor k/m^{3}\rfloor
and assume
k
is large enough so that
\binom{n}{2}\geq k
. Suppose that
\binom{n}{2}=qk+r
, where
0\leq r\leq k-1
. Set
e_{1}=e_{2}=\cdots=e_{k-r}=q
and
e_{k-r+1}=\cdots=e_{k}=q+1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{e_{i}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{k}+1\right)\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\binom{n}{2}^{2}}{k^{2}}<\frac{n^{4}}{4k}\leq\frac{n^{3}}{4m^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{m(m-1)(m-2)}.

So by Lemma
3.1
, we can find a rainbow copy of
K_{m}
, and hence a rainbow copy of
H
in this colouring of
K_{n}
.
∎

For the upper bound, we first prove it under the stronger assumption that
H
has minimum degree at least 3.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
H
be a graph on
m
vertices with minimum degree at least 3. Assume
n\geq 2k
and
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
is an
n
-good sequence. Then there exists a rainbow
H
-free
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
-colouring of
K_{n}
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
k
. Note that every graph
H
with minimum degree 3 contains at least 6 edges, so any colouring using less than 6 colours will be rainbow
H
-free. Thus the statement is trivially true for all
k\leq 5
. Assume now
k\geq 6
.

Without loss of generality, assume
e_{1}\geq\cdots\geq e_{k}>0
. Then
e_{1}\geq\frac{1}{k}\binom{n}{2}\geq n-1
and
e_{k}\leq\frac{1}{k}\binom{n}{2}
. Let
t\geq 1
be the smallest integer satisfying
\binom{t}{2}+t(n-t)\geq e_{k}
. By minimality,
\binom{t-1}{2}+(t-1)(n-t+1)<e_{k}
,
and thus

\binom{t}{2}+t(n-t)-e_{k}<n-t\leq e_{1}.

This means that we can colour
e_{k}
of the
\binom{t}{2}+t(n-t)
edges adjacent to
v_{n-t+1},\cdots,v_{n}
in
K_{n}
with colour
k
, and colour the remaining ones with colour 1.

Also, since

\binom{\frac{n}{k}}{2}+\frac{n}{k}\left(n-\frac{n}{k}\right)=\frac{n}{2k}\left(2n-\frac{n}{k}-1\right)\geq\frac{n}{2k}(n-1)=\frac{1}{k}\binom{n}{2}\geq e_{k},

we have
t\leq\frac{n}{k}
and thus
n-t\geq n(1-\frac{1}{k})\geq 2(k-1)
. Let
e_{1}^{\prime}=e_{1}-\binom{t}{2}-t(n-t)+e_{k}
and
e_{i}^{\prime}=e_{i}
for all
2\leq i\leq k-1
. It is easy to verify that
(e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,e_{k-1}^{\prime})
is an
(n-t)
-good sequence. Hence, we can use induction hypothesis to find a rainbow
H
-free
(e_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,e_{k-1}^{\prime})
-colouring of
K_{n-t}
. Combining this with the colouring above for edges adjacent to
v_{n-t+1},\cdots,v_{n}
, we obtain an
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
-colouring of
K_{n}
.

We claim that this colouring is rainbow
H
-free. Indeed, let
H^{\prime}
be any subgraph of
K_{n}
isomorphic to
H
. If all vertices of
H^{\prime}
are from
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n-t}
, then it is not rainbow by the inductive construction. Otherwise,
H^{\prime}
contains least one of
v_{n-t+1},\cdots,v_{n}
. But edges adjacent to each of these vertices can only have colour 1 or
k
, so
H^{\prime}
not rainbow as
H^{\prime}\cong H
has minimum degree 3.
∎

We can now deduce Theorem
1.8
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
H
be a graph on
m
vertices with degeneracy at least 3. Then
H
must contain a subgraph
H^{\prime}
with minimum degree at least 3. Lemma
3.3
implies that
g(H^{\prime},k)\leq 2k
. So by Lemma
1.4
,
g(H,k)\leq g(H^{\prime},k)\leq 2k
, which gives the upper bound. The lower bound follows from Corollary
3.2
.
∎

4
Graphs with degeneracy 2

In this section, we study the case when
H
is a graph with degeneracy 2 and prove Theorem
1.9
. We only need to prove the upper bound as the lower bound follows from Corollary
3.2
. Let
H
be a graph with degeneracy 2. To prove an upper bound of the form
g(H,k)\leq N
, we need to construct a rainbow
H
-free
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
-colouring of
K_{n}
for every
n\geq N
and every
n
-good sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
. It turns out that repeated suitable applications of the following colouring steps would work.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose we have a complete graph
K_{n}
and a sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\geq\binom{n}{2}
.

•

A standard colouring step on
K_{n}
involves splitting it into
K_{t}
and
K_{n-t}
for some
1\leq t\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
, and colouring all
t(n-t)
edges between them with some colour
i
satisfying
e_{i}\geq t(n-t)
. The size of such a standard colouring step is
t
.

•

A simple colouring step is a standard colouring step of size 1.

We are interested in colourings of
K_{n}
that can be obtained entirely from standard colouring steps.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a complete graph
K_{n}
and a sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}\geq\binom{n}{2}
, we say
K_{n}
has a standard colouring (with respect to
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
) if it has an edge colouring that can be obtained from a sequence of standard colouring steps. More formally,
K_{n}
has a standard colouring if it has an edge colouring that can be obtained using the following stepwise algorithm, where we view each set
S_{\ell}
as a multiset.

•

Initialise by setting
S_{0}=\{K_{n}\}
and
e_{0,j}=e_{j}
for all
j\in[k]

•

For
\ell\geq 0
, if
S_{\ell}=\{K_{1},\cdots,K_{1}\}
, terminate.

Otherwise, pick some
K_{m}\in S_{\ell}
with
m\geq 2
, some
i\in[k]
and some
1\leq t\leq\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor
, such that
t(m-t)\leq e_{\ell,i}
.

•

Set
S_{\ell+1}=(S_{\ell}\setminus\{K_{m}\})\cup\{K_{t},K_{m-t}\}
,
e_{\ell+1,i}=e_{\ell,i}-t(m-t)
and
e_{\ell+1,j}=e_{\ell,j}
for all
j\in[k]\setminus\{i\}
. Note that this correspoinds to a standard colouring step on
K_{m}
of size
t
using colour
i
.

•

Repeat the second and third step.

Similarly, we say
K_{n}
has a simple colouring if it has an edge colouring that can be obtained from a sequence of simple colouring steps.

We first prove a lemma that explains why we are interested in standard colouring steps and standard colourings.

Lemma 4.3
.

Every standard colouring of
K_{n}
is rainbow cycle-free, and therefore rainbow
H
-free for all graphs
H
with degeneracy 2.

Proof.

For every
3\leq k\leq n
, let
C
be an arbitrary cycle of length
k
in
K_{n}
. Let
\ell\geq 1
be the smallest index such that vertices in
C
do not all belong to the same complete graph in
S_{\ell}
. By minimality, they all belong to the same complete graph in
S_{\ell-1}
, say
K_{m}
, that was split into two complete graphs
K_{t}
and
K_{m-t}
by a standard colouring step in the
\ell
-th iteration of the algorithm. From definition, all edges between
K_{t}
and
K_{m-t}
have the same colour. Since at least two edges on
C
go between
K_{t}
and
K_{m-t}
,
C
is not rainbow.
∎

Assume now that the algorithm starts with a complete graph
K_{n}
and an
n
-good sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
. Then throughout the algorithm, there is a relation between the sizes of the complete graphs in
S_{\ell}
and the
e_{\ell,j}
’s.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
\ell\geq 0
, if
S_{\ell}=\{K_{m_{1}},\cdots,K_{m_{r}}\}
, then

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\binom{m_{j}}{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell,j}.

Proof.

We use induction on
\ell
. The case
\ell=0
follows from the assumption that
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})=(e_{0,1},\cdots,e_{0,k})
is
n
-good. Suppose this is true for some
\ell\geq 0
, and without loss of generality assume that the
(\ell+1)
-th standard colouring step is splitting
K_{m_{1}}
into
K_{t}
and
K_{m_{1}-t}
and colouring the
t(m_{1}-t)
edges with colour
1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\binom{t}{2}+\binom{m_{1}-t}{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{r}\binom{m_{j}}{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{t}{2}+\binom{m_{1}-t}{2}-\binom{m_{1}}{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell,j}

\displaystyle=-t(m_{1}-t)+e_{\ell,1}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}e_{\ell,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell+1,j},

as required.
∎

This relation can also be proved by noting that edges that remained to be coloured after
\ell
iterations are exactly those within the complete graphs in
S_{\ell}
.

Using this, we can prove that a simple colouring step can always be performed during the algorithm under a mild size condition.

Lemma 4.5
.

For every
\ell\geq 0
, if
S_{\ell}=\{K_{m_{1}},\cdots,K_{m_{r}}\}
and
m_{1}\geq 2k
, then a simple colouring step can be performed on
K_{m_{1}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\binom{m_{j}}{2}\geq\binom{m_{1}}{2}.

So there exists
i\in[k]
such that
e_{\ell,i}\geq\frac{1}{k}\binom{m_{1}}{2}\geq m_{1}-1
. Hence, we can perform a simple colouring step on
K_{m_{1}}
using colour
i
.
∎

To obtain result analogous to Lemma
4.5
below size
2k
, we need the following important concept of cushion.

Definition 4.6
.

For
\ell\geq 0
, if
S_{\ell}=\{K_{m_{1}},\cdots,K_{m_{r}}\}
, then we say the cushion we have for
K_{m_{1}}
is
\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell,j}-\binom{m_{1}}{2}=\sum_{j=2}^{r}\binom{m_{j}}{2}
, and we say that this cushion is provided by
K_{m_{2}},\cdots,K_{m_{r}}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For all
m\geq 2
, suppose
K_{m}\in S_{\ell}
and the cushion we have for
K_{m}
is at least
\min\{\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}-k),km\}
. Then
m-1
consecutive simple colouring steps can be performed on
K_{m}
to produce a simple colouring.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for each
0\leq t\leq m-2
, it is possible to perform a simple colouring step on
K_{m-t}
. Indeed, performing simple colouring steps on
K_{m},\cdots,K_{m-t+1}
colours
\sum_{j=1}^{t}(m-j)=\frac{1}{2}t(2m-t-1)
edges in total.

If the cushion we have is at least
\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}-k)
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell+t,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell,j}-\frac{1}{2}t(2m-t-1)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{m}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}-k)-\frac{1}{2}t(2m-t-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(m^{2}-m+k^{2}-k-2mt+t^{2}+t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(t-m+k)(t-m+k+1)+k(m-t-1)

\displaystyle\geq k(m-t-1).

If instead the cushion we have is at least
km
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell+t,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{\ell,j}-\frac{1}{2}t(2m-t-1)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{m}{2}+km-\frac{1}{2}t(2m-t-1)

\displaystyle=\binom{m-t}{2}+km

\displaystyle\geq km\geq k(m-t-1).

Hence, in both cases there exists a colour
i
with
e_{\ell+t,i}\geq m-t-1
. So we can perform a simple colouring step on
K_{m-t}
using colour
i
, as required.
∎

We remark that a weaker version of this lemma in the case when
m=2k
was proved by Feffer, Fu and Yan in
[
4
]
using a much more complicated method.

We prove one final lemma before proving Theorem
1.9
. This lemma allow us to perform several consecutive standard colouring steps of the same size, which will be useful for creating cushion.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
n,m,t,k\geq 1
and
e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}\geq 0
be integers satisfying
n>tm
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}e_{i}>tn(m+k)
. Then we can perform
m
consecutive standard colouring steps of size
t
on
K_{n}
.

Proof.

For each
j\in[m]
, the
j
-th size
t
standard colouring step splits
K_{n-(j-1)t}
into
K_{t}
and
K_{n-jt}
, and colour the
t(n-jt)
edges between them using one of the
k
colours. Since
t(n-jt)\leq tn
, colour
i
can be used to perform a size
t
standard colouring step at least
\left\lfloor\frac{e_{i}}{tn}\right\rfloor
times for each
i\in[k]
. Since

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lfloor\frac{e_{i}}{tn}\right\rfloor\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{e_{i}}{tn}-k>m+k-k=m,

it is possible to perform
m
such steps.
∎

We now sketch the upper bound proof in Theorem
1.9
, which proceeds in three stages:

•

Stage 1: We perform
k
consecutive standard colouring steps of size
r=\Theta\left(\frac{k^{0.5}}{(\log k)^{0.5}}\right)
using Lemma

. Let
\mathcal{R}
be the set of
k
complete graphs

split off in these steps. These complete graphs in

will provide a small but still significant amount of cushion.

•

Stage 2: We perform several standard colouring steps of larger size, so that the set
\mathcal{C}
of complete graphs split off in this stage provide a big cushion of at least
\frac{1}{2}k^{2}
.

•

Stage 3: By Lemma
4.5
, we can reduce the size of the largest remaining complete graph down to

. Using the cushion provided by

and Lemma
4.7
, we can find a simple colouring for this

. Then, we use Lemma

again and the cushion provided by

to find simple colourings for each complete graph in

. Finally, we show that the cushion provided by

is enough for us to find simple colourings for the complete graphs in

itself as well.

The idea of this proof is already present implicitly in the upper bound proof in

but with

and

being the same set, which turns out to be less than optimal.

Proof of Theorem

.

The lower bound follows directly from Corollary

\beta=5000000,\text{ }n=\left\lfloor\frac{\beta k^{1.5}}{(\log{k})^{0.5}}\right\rfloor.

Let
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
be any
n

. Initialise the colouring algorithm by setting
e_{0,j}=e_{j}
for all

and
S_{0}=\{K_{n}\}
.

Stage 1: The Small Cushion Steps

Set

. Since

\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{0,j}=e=\binom{n}{2}>\frac{\beta^{2}k^{3}}{3\log k}\geq 10krn>rn(k+k),

k

Stage 2: The Big Cushion Steps

Let

|J_{1}|\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2.25}}{(\log k)^{1.25}}\leq\sum_{j\in J_{1}}e_{k,j}\leq e,

Case 1.

\sum_{j\in J_{1}}e_{k,j}\geq 0.1e
.

Set
c=\left\lfloor\frac{k^{0.75}}{(\log k)^{0.75}}\right\rfloor
. Since

\sum_{j\in J_{1}}e_{k,j}\geq 0.1e\geq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{3}}{30\log k}\geq cn\cdot\frac{\beta k^{0.75}(\log k)^{0.25}}{30}>cn(\left\lceil k^{0.75}(\log k)^{0.25}\right\rceil+|J_{1}|),

we can perform
\left\lceil k^{0.75}(\log k)^{0.25}\right\rceil

4.8
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be this collection of

k^{0.75}(\log k)^{0.25}\binom{c}{2}\geq\frac{k^{2.25}}{3(\log k)^{1.25}}\gg k^{2},

which is enough for us to apply Lemma
4.7
later on. At this point, the set of complete graphs yet to be coloured consists of the complete graphs in
\mathcal{R}
coming from Stage 1, the complete graphs in
\mathcal{C}
coming from Stage 2 and one other large complete graph of size

.

Case 2.
\sum_{j\in J_{1}}e_{k,j}<0.1e
.

Let
J_{2}=\left\{j\in[k]\mid e_{k,j}\leq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{30\log k}\right\}

\sum_{j\in J_{2}}e_{k,j}\leq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{3}}{30\log k}\leq 0.1e
. Let
J_{3}=[k]\setminus(J_{1}\cup J_{2})
. Without loss of generality, assume
J_{3}=[\ell]
. Then
\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{30\log k}<e_{k,j}<\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2.25}}{(\log k)^{1.25}}

and

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}e_{k,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_{k,j}-\sum_{j\in J_{1}}e_{k,j}-\sum_{j\in J_{2}}e_{k,j}\geq 0.9e-0.1e-0.1e=0.7e.

For each
j\in J_{1}\cup J_{2}
, use colour
j
to perform as many simple colouring steps as possible to the largest remaining complete graph. Suppose a total of
N-k
simple colouring steps are performed. Then for each

, we have
e_{N,j}<n
, as otherwise we could perform another simple colouring step using colour
j
. Let
K_{x_{0}}
be the largest complete graph in

. Then

consists of
K_{x_{0}}
,
k
complete graphs

coming from Stage 1, and many

from the simple colouring steps above. Therefore, by Lemma

, we have

\binom{x_{0}}{2}+k\binom{r}{2}=\sum_{j\in J_{1}\cup J_{2}}e_{N,j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}e_{N,j}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}e_{N,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}e_{k,j}\geq 0.7e.

Since

, it follows that
0.5x_{0}^{2}\geq\binom{x_{0}}{2}\geq 0.66e\geq 0.32\frac{\beta^{2}k^{3}}{\log k}
, and thus
x_{0}\geq\frac{4\beta k^{1.5}}{5(\log k)^{0.5}}
.

Now consider the following process.

•

Start with the complete graph
K_{x_{0}}
.

•

In the
j
-th step, if
x_{j-1}<\frac{2\sqrt{\beta}k^{1.25}}{(\log k)^{0.25}}

of maximum possible size

K_{x_{j}}
and

x_{j}=x_{j-1}-c_{j}
.

The maximality of

c_{j}x_{j}\leq e_{N+j-1,j}\text{\quad and \quad}(c_{j}+1)(x_{j}-1)>e_{N+j-1,j}.

From this, it follows that

e_{N+j,j}=e_{N+j-1,j}-c_{j}x_{j}<x_{j}-c_{j}-1\leq x_{j}-1<n,

and

\frac{e_{N+j-1,j}}{2x_{j}}\leq\frac{e_{N+j-1,j}}{x_{j}-1}-1\leq c_{j}\leq\frac{e_{N+j-1,j}}{x_{j}},

where the first inequality holds because

. We claim that

for some

. If instead we performed all

.

Since

. Moreover, we have

2k\ll\frac{\sqrt{\beta}k^{1.25}}{(\log k)^{0.25}}\leq\frac{1}{2}x_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\leq x_{\ell^{\prime}}<\frac{2\sqrt{\beta}k^{1.25}}{(\log k)^{0.25}},

It follows that for

, we have

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the collection

of small complete graphs split off in this process. The amount of cushions provided by

is at least

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}\binom{c_{j}}{2}\geq\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}c_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}c_{j}\frac{e_{N+j-1,j}}{2x_{j}}

by

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}c_{j}\frac{e_{k,j}}{2x_{j}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{180\log k}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{c_{j}}{x_{j}}

\displaystyle\text{by }e_{k,j}\geq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{30\log k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{180\log k}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}\int_{x=x_{j}}^{x_{j-1}}\frac{1}{x_{j}}\text{d}x

\displaystyle\text{by }c_{j}=x_{j-1}-x_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{180\log k}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\prime}}\int_{x=x_{j}}^{x_{j-1}}\frac{1}{x}\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{180\log k}\int_{x=x_{\ell^{\prime}}}^{x_{0}}\frac{1}{x}\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{180\log k}\log{\frac{x_{0}}{x_{\ell^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{180\log k}\log\frac{2\sqrt{\beta}k^{0.25}}{5(\log k)^{0.25}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\beta^{2}k^{2}}{720\log k}(\log k-\log\log k)\gg k^{2},

which is enough for us to apply Lemma
4.7
later on. At this point, the set of complete graphs left to be coloured consists of those in
\mathcal{R}
, those in
\mathcal{C}
and the complete graph

with

.

and
\mathcal{R}

. Recall that, for every

,

If we are in Case 1, then

,

•

.

Note that the

]
by proving Theorem
1.2
. Recall that rainbow

We will use the following fundamental decomposition theorem of Gallai colourings.

Theorem 5.1

(
[

]
)

Suppose we have a Gallai

into

, such that for each

Now we can prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Theorem
1.9

The proof consists of 3 steps.

Step 1: Construct the sequence and describe a splitting process

Consider the colour distribution sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
on

It is easy to verify that

with colour distribution sequence

Suppose for a contradiction that there is such a Gallai colouring, then we can perform the following stepwise splitting process by repeatedly applying Theorem

•

Initialise with

.

•

For each

•

into
K_{y_{m}}
and
K_{x_{i+1}}
, with the latter being the union of
K_{y_{1}},\cdots,K_{y_{m-1}}
. Note that by Theorem
5.1
, every edge between
V(K_{x_{i+1}})
and
V(K_{y_{m}})
is coloured with a base colour.

•

Repeat the second and third step.

Hence, we can find a nested sequence of complete graphs
K_{x_{0}}\supset K_{x_{1}}\supset\cdots\supset K_{x_{\ell}}
, where
n=x_{0}>x_{1}>\cdots>x_{\ell-1}\geq b+2>x_{\ell}
. Moreover, by the last part of Theorem
5.1
, in each iteration of the process above, the base colour chosen must be used to colour at least
b+1
edges. Hence colours
\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil+1,\cdots,k
will never be picked as base colours in this process.

Step 2: Lower bound on the number of edges coloured with base colours

We claim that
t_{i+1}\leq\frac{4(a+1)}{x_{i}}
for all
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
. Indeed, every edge between the complete graphs
V(K_{x_{i+1}})
and
V(K_{y_{m}})
must be coloured with a base colour. However, the at most 2 base colours together can be used to colour at most
2(a+1)
edges. This implies that

\frac{1}{2}x_{i}t_{i+1}\leq(x_{i}-t_{i+1})t_{i+1}=x_{i+1}y_{m}\leq 2(a+1),

where we used
t_{i+1}=y_{m}\leq\frac{1}{2}x_{i}
. Thus,
t_{i+1}\leq\frac{4(a+1)}{x_{i}}
, as claimed.

We can now give a lower bound on the number of edges coloured with base colours in this splitting process. This number is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}t_{i}(x_{i-1}-t_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{x=x_{i}}^{x_{i-1}}(x_{i-1}-t_{i})\text{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{x=x_{i}}^{x_{i-1}}\left(x_{i-1}-\frac{4(a+1)}{x_{i-1}}\right)\text{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{x=x_{i}}^{x_{i-1}}\left(x-\frac{4(a+1)}{x}\right)\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{x=x_{\ell}}^{x_{0}}\left(x-\frac{4(a+1)}{x}\right)\text{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{x=b+1}^{n}\left(x-\frac{4(a+1)}{x}\right)\text{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}-(b+1)^{2}}{2}-4(a+1)\log\frac{n}{b+1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{2b^{2}}{3}-4(a+1)\log\frac{n}{b}.

Recall that only the first
\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
colours can be used as base colours in this process.

Step 3: Not enough edges can be coloured with the first
\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
colours

The total number of edges that can be coloured with the first
\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil
colours is

\sum_{i=1}^{\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil}e_{i}=\binom{n}{2}-b\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}-b^{2}.

So we will have a contradiction if

\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{2b^{2}}{3}-4(a+1)\log\frac{n}{b}>\frac{n^{2}}{2}-b^{2}.

Note that for sufficiently large
k
we have

b^{2}=\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor^{2}\geq\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{k^{2}}{5},

4(a+1)\leq 5a\leq 5\frac{\binom{n}{2}}{\left\lceil{\frac{k}{2}}\right\rceil}\leq\frac{5n^{2}}{k}\leq\frac{5\alpha^{2}k^{2}}{\log k},

\log\frac{n}{b}\leq\log\left(\frac{\alpha k^{1.5}}{(\log k)^{0.5}}\frac{3}{k}\right)=\log\left(\frac{3\alpha k^{0.5}}{(\log k)^{0.5}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}(\log k-\log\log k)+\log(3\alpha)\leq\log k.

Putting these together and using
\alpha=\frac{1}{10}
, we get

\left(\frac{n^{2}}{2}-\frac{2b^{2}}{3}-4(a+1)\log\frac{n}{b}\right)-\left(\frac{n^{2}}{2}-b^{2}\right)=\frac{b^{2}}{3}-4(a+1)\log\frac{n}{b}\geq\frac{k^{2}}{15}-\frac{k^{2}}{20}>0,

as required. This proves that if
k
is sufficiently large, then there is no Gallai colouring with colour distribution sequence
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
. Therefore,
g(k)\geq\frac{\alpha k^{1.5}}{(\log k)^{0.5}}
.
∎

Interplay between the factorization of the Jarlskog Invariant and location of the Solar and Atmospheric Resonances for Neutrino Oscillations in Matter Preprint: FERMILAB-PUB-20-609-T
Stephen J. Parke

Abstract
The Jarlskog invariant which controls the size of intrinsic CP violation in neutrino oscillation appearance experiments is modified by Wolfenstein matter effects for neutrinos propagating in matter. In this paper we give the exact factorization of Jarlskog invariant in matter into the vacuum Jarlskog invariant times two, two-flavor matter resonance factors that control the matter effects for the solar and atmospheric resonances independently. We compare the location of the minima of the factorizing resonance factors with the location of the solar and atmospheric resonances, precisely defined. They are not identical but the fractional differences are both found to be less than 0.1%. In addition, we explain why symmetry polynomials of the square of the mass of the neutrino eigenvalues in matter, such as inverse of the square of the Jarlskog invariant in matter, can be given as polynomials in the matter potential.



I
Introduction

In the current and future long baseline experiments, T2K
Itow et al. 2001
, NOvA
Ayres et al. 2004
, DUNE
Acciarri et al. 2016

;

Neutrino oscillations in matter are significantly different than in vacuum because of the coherent forward scattering of the neutrinos off the electrons in matter. This effect is known as the Wolfenstein matter effect
Wolfenstein 1978
.

The neutrino mass eigenvalues and the mixing angles of the neutrinos in matter are strongly impacted by the matter effect which depends on density of the matter and the energy of the neutrino through the Wolfenstein matter potential

,

(1)

These changes in the masses and the mixing angles have a significant effect on the oscillation probabilities as the neutrinos transverse through matter.

One of the primary goal of the above long baseline accelerator experiments is to measure intrinsic CP violation in the neutrinos sector. In vacuum, the CP-violating part of the neutrino oscillation probability in the appearance channels, e.g.
\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e}
, is given by
Bilenky and Petcov 1987

(2)

L

E_{\nu}
.

Maki et al. 1962
;

J\equiv s_{23}c_{23}s_{13}c^{2}_{13}s_{12}c_{12}\sin\delta\,,

(3)

where
s_{ij}=\sin\theta_{ij}

c_{ij}=\cos\theta_{ij}
.

For neutrinos propagating in matter,
the part of the appearance oscillation probability that depends on the intrinsic CP violation is given by

8\widehat{J}\sin\widehat{\Delta}_{31}\sin\widehat{\Delta}_{32}\sin\widehat{\Delta}_{21}\,,

(4)

where
\widehat{x}
is the matter value for the vacuum variable
x
.
The Jarlskog invariant in matter,
\widehat{J}

Zaglauer and Schwarzer 1988
;
Krastev and Petcov 1988
;
Parke and Weiler 2001

The variables
\widehat{\theta}_{12}

In
Denton and Parke 2019

\widehat{J}\approx\frac{J}{\mathcal{R}_{\odot}^{\text{appx}}\,\mathcal{R}_{\rm atm}^{\text{appx}}}\,,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\odot}^{\text{appx}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-2c_{13}^{2}\cos 2\theta_{12}(a/\Delta m^{2}_{21})+c_{13}^{4}(a/\Delta m^{2}_{21})^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm atm}^{\text{appx}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-2\cos 2\theta_{13}(a/\Delta m^{2}_{ee})+(a/\Delta m^{2}_{ee})^{2}}\,.

(6)

The fractional precision of this factorization is better than 0.07%, for all

Wang and Zhou 2019
with a follow up paper,
Wang and Zhou 2020
, that reproduced the precise factorization of DP.
In this paper we give an extension of the analysis of DP to derive an exact factorization of this Jarlskog invariant in matter as well as a perturbative expansion in the two small quantities
s^{2}_{13}
and
\Delta m^{2}_{21}/\Delta m^{2}_{ee}
.

Then, after defining precisely and calculating the location of the solar and atmospheric resonances to the relevant accuracy in the matter potential for the first time, we compare the location of the resonance with the location of the minima of the factorizing two flavor resonance factors.
Although they are identical at the order given by DP, what is show here, is they are not identical at higher orders but the fractional differences are found to be small, less than 0.1%. A qualitative understanding of why they differ is also given.

As part of our analysis, we show the simplicity of variables that can be expressed as a symmetric polynomial of the neutrino eigenvalues in matter,
\widehat{m^{2}}_{j}
, such as the Jarlskog invariant in matter as well as other examples, Section II. We give the exact factorization of the Jarlskog invariant in matter as a product of two, two flavor resonance factors, Section III as well as a perturbative expansion. In Section IV, we compare the minima of these exact factorizing factors to the actual location of the solar and atmospheric resonance for the matter potential, followed by a summary and conclusion section. There are five Appendices that contain computational details. Thus this paper provides additional understanding of the Wolfenstein matter effect for three neutrinos propagating through matter.

V
Summary and Conclusions

In this paper we have derived three new results for three flavor neutrino oscillations in matter.
First, we have shown that any quantity that can be represented by a symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian,

\widehat{m^{2}}_{j}
, can be written as a polynomial in the matter potential,
a
. Examples of such quantities are

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j>k}\Delta\widehat{m^{2}}_{jk}\right)^{2},\quad\left(\frac{J}{\widehat{J}}\right)^{2}\,

and

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j>k}\Delta\widehat{m^{2}}_{jk}\right)^{2}\times\left(\prod_{i}|\widehat{U}_{\alpha i}|^{2}\right)\,.

The first two are fourth order polynomials in the matter potential whereas the last one is a constant for
\alpha=e
and a quadratic polynomial in the matter potential for
\alpha=\mu,~\tau.
Clearly there are many other such quantities that are symmetric in the eigenvalues.

Second, for the Jarlskog invariant in matter we give the exact factorization of the fourth order polynomial, such that

\widehat{J}=\frac{J}{\mathcal{R}_{\odot}^{\text{ex}}\,\mathcal{R}_{\rm atm}^{\text{ex}}}\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\odot}^{ex}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-2S^{ex}_{sol}\left(\frac{a}{\Delta m^{2}_{21}}\right)+T^{ex}_{sol}\left(\frac{a}{\Delta m^{2}_{21}}\right)^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm atm}^{ex}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-2S^{ex}_{atm}\left(\frac{a}{\Delta m^{2}_{ee}}\right)+T^{ex}_{atm}\left(\frac{a}{\Delta m^{2}_{ee}}\right)^{2}}\,.

The exact coefficients
S^{ex}_{sol}
to
T^{ex}_{atm}
are given in eqs.
28
-
31
. Since the exact coefficients involve the solution to a cubic polynomial which are notoriously challenging to penetrate analytically,
a Taylor series expansion of these coefficients to

{\cal O}(s^{4}_{13}\epsilon),~{\cal O}(s^{2}_{13}\epsilon^{2}),~{\cal O}(\epsilon^{3}),

is also given in eqs.
36
–
39
.
The fractional accuracy of these coefficients is better than
10^{6}
, as shown in Table 1.

Third, we define the solar resonance to be when

\displaystyle\frac{d(\widehat{m^{2}}_{2}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{1})}{da}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sin^{2}\widehat{\theta}_{12}=0.5\,,

and calculate that this occurs when

\displaystyle\frac{a}{\Delta m^{2}_{21}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos 2\theta_{12}}{c^{2}_{13}}+\cos^{2}2\theta_{12}(s^{2}_{13}\epsilon)+\cdots\,.

where the “
\cdots
” are higher order terms in
s^{2}_{13}
and
\epsilon\equiv\Delta m^{2}_{21}/\Delta m^{2}_{ee}
.
Independent of the mass ordering, we define the atmospheric resonance to be when

\displaystyle\frac{d(\widehat{m^{2}}_{3}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{2}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{1})}{da}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sin^{2}\widehat{\theta}_{13}=0.5\,,

and calculate that this occurs when

\displaystyle\frac{a}{\Delta m^{2}_{ee}}

\displaystyle=\cos 2\theta_{13}-s^{2}_{12}c^{2}_{12}(1-2s_{13})\,(\epsilon^{2})+\cdots\,.

For three flavors of neutrinos, these are the most accurate values for the locations of the solar and atmospheric resonances available in the literature.
The value of the matter potential typically quoted for the atmospheric resonance,
a/\Delta m^{2}_{32}=\cos 2\theta_{13}
, differs from above value by 2%, a phenomenological significant difference.

We have also made a comparison between the location of the minima in the matter potential for the two factorizing factors,
\mathcal{R}_{\odot}^{ex}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\rm atm}^{ex}
and the location in matter potential of the solar and atmospheric resonances.
The difference in the value of the matter potential between the location of the minima and the resonance values are 0.06% for solar and 0.005% for atmospheric, therefore they do not coincide. However, these difference are negligible for phenomenological purposes.

To conclude, the understanding of the physics of neutrino propagation in matter is of essential importance to the current NOvA and T2K, and upcoming DUNE and T2HK(K) long baseline neutrino oscillation experiments. The Wolfenstein matter effect significantly complicates three flavor neutrino oscillations in matter compared to vacuum. The exact analytic expressions, even for constant matter density, are analytically impenetrable due to the presence of the
\cos\{\frac{1}{3}\cos^{-1}[\cdots]\}
terms. In this paper we show that variables that depend on the symmetric polynomials of the mass squared of the neutrino eigenstates in matter are just simple polynomials of the matter potential, a significant simplification. We give a number of examples and in particular the Jarlskog invariant in matter which controls the size of intrinsic CP violation in the above neutrino appearance experiments. We give for the first time the exact factorization of the Jarlskog invariant in matter into two quadratic resonance factors. The location of the minima of these resonance factors is compared to the location of the solar and atmospheric resonances to the appropriate order in the small quantities
s^{2}_{13}
and
\Delta m^{2}_{21}/\Delta m^{2}_{ee}
. This required, for the first time, the calculation of the location of the solar and atmospheric resonances beyond leading order. All of this further enhances our understanding of neutrino propagation in matter relevant for the long baseline neutrino oscillation experiments being currently performed and for the next generation of experiments.

Appendix E
Perturbative Expansions about the Resonances

A perturbative expansion is used to obtain the values of the matter potential for the atmospheric and solar resonances.

E.1
Atmospheric Resonance

In this Appendix we perform an perturbative expansion in the matter potential about the approximate value of the matter potential that minimizes

(\widehat{m^{2}}_{3}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{2}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{1})
, that is

a=\Delta m^{2}_{ee}\cos 2\theta_{13}+\delta a

(80)

Starting with the Hamiltonian from eq.
7
, a (2-3) rotation using
U_{23}(\theta_{23},\delta)
is performed to remove the
\theta_{23}
and
\delta
dependence. Then a
\pi/4
rotation is performed in the (1-3) sector using the value of
a
given in eq.
80
. The resulting Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}_{13}(\pi/4)U^{\dagger}_{23}(\theta_{23},\delta)~(2E)H~U_{23}(\theta_{23},\delta)U_{13}(\pi/4)=H_{a}+h

(81)

with

\displaystyle H_{a}=\text{\bf diag}(~

\displaystyle\Delta m^{2}_{ee}(c^{2}_{13}-s_{13}c_{13})+\Delta m^{2}_{12}s^{2}_{12},~~\Delta m^{2}_{21}c^{2}_{12},~~\Delta m^{2}_{ee}(c^{2}_{13}+s_{13}c_{13})+\Delta m^{2}_{12}s^{2}_{12}~)\,,

(82)

\displaystyle\quad h=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\delta a&\delta m^{2}_{+}&\delta a\\
\cdot&0&\delta m^{2}_{-}\\
\cdot&\cdot&\delta a\end{array}\right)

where
\delta m^{2}_{\pm}=\sqrt{2}\Delta m^{2}_{21}s_{12}c_{12}(c_{13}\pm s_{13})
. Note, because the solar crossing has not been resolved the mass eigenstates
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are interchanged. Since
h
is symmetric, the
\cdots
’s are given by the appropriate term above the diagonal.

Now perturbation theory can be used to calculate
(\widehat{m^{2}}_{3}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{2}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{1})
, which to third order in
h
, is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{m^{2}}_{3}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{2}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{1}\approx~d_{31}+2(h^{2}_{13}/d_{31}+h^{2}_{23}/d_{32})+2h_{11}h_{23}(2h_{12}d_{32}-h_{23}d_{31})/(d_{32}^{2}d_{31})\,,

(86)

where
h_{jk}
are the elements of matrix
h
, eq.
E.1
, and
d_{jk}=H_{a}(j,j)-H_{a}(k,k)
, e.g.
d_{31}=\Delta m^{2}_{ee}(2s_{13}c_{13})
. Eq.
86
is a quadratic in the variable
\delta a
as both
h_{11}
and
h_{13}
are linear in
\delta a
.
To obtain an estimate of the shift one needs to go to third order, as first order vanishes, second order is quadratic and third order gives the first odd term in
\delta a
. The value of
\delta a
that minimizes
(\widehat{m^{2}}_{3}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{2}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{1})
is given by

\displaystyle\delta a

\displaystyle=-h_{23}(2h_{12}d_{32}-h_{23}d_{31})/d_{32}^{2}\,\approx-s^{2}_{12}c^{2}_{12}(1-2s_{13})(\Delta m^{2}_{21})^{2}/\Delta m^{2}_{ee}\,.

(87)

This gives eq.
60
. The minimum of eq.
86
reproduces the exact minimum to better than one part in
10^{8}
.

Using the above perturbation theory, one can also show that the value of
\delta a
that minimizes
(\widehat{m^{2}}_{3}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{2})
for NO is given by

\displaystyle\delta a

\displaystyle\approx-s^{2}_{12}c^{2}_{12}(\Delta m^{2}_{21})^{2}/\Delta m^{2}_{ee}\,.

(88)

Thus the alternative definition of the atmospheric resonance, eq.
52
, changes the corrections at
{\cal O}(s_{13}\epsilon^{2})
and similarly for IO.

E.2
Solar Resonance

In this Appendix we perform an perturbative expansion in the matter potential about the approximate value of the matter potential that minimizes

(\widehat{m^{2}}_{2}-\widehat{m^{2}}_{1})

a=a_{0}+\delta a,\quad\quad a_{0}=\Delta m^{2}_{21}\cos 2\theta_{12}/\cos^{2}\theta_{13}

7

\theta_{23}

\delta

\cdot&\delta a\,c^{2}_{13}&\sqrt{2}\,a\,s_{13}c_{13}\\

\displaystyle~d_{21}+2h^{2}_{11}/d_{21}-h^{2}_{13}\,d_{21}/(d_{32}d_{31})

\displaystyle-h^{2}_{13}(d_{31}+d_{32})\,[\,2h_{11}/(d_{32}d_{31}d_{21})+(h_{11}-h_{33})d_{21}/(d^{2}_{31}d^{2}_{32})\,]

57

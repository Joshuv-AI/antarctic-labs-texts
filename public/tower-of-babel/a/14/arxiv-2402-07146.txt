Sums of three cubes over a function field
Tim Browning, Jakob Glas, Victor Y. Wang

Abstract
. We use a function field version of the circle method to prove that a positive proportion of elements in 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t] are representable as a sum of three cubes of minimal degree from 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t] , assuming a suitable form of the Ratios Conjecture and that char ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) > 3 \Char(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3 . The analogue of this conjecture for quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions is known for large fixed q q , via recent developments in homological stability.

1.
Introduction

A folklore conjecture in number theory states that any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, not congruent to
\pm 4
modulo
9
,
admits a representation
as a sum of three cubes of integers. The state of the art around this difficult problem is summarised
in recent work by Wang
[
50
]
, where a modern form of the circle method is developed to prove the conjecture for a positive proportion of integers
k
. Wang’s work is highly conditional, depending on an automorphy conjecture and GRH for certain Hasse–Weil
L
-functions, as well as the Ratios Conjecture from random matrix theory and a Square-Free Sieve Conjecture.

In this paper we investigate the analogous problem over the function field
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
,
with the aim of removing as many of the hypotheses as possible from
[
50
]
.
Let

\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
denote the ring of integers of
K
and let
k\in\mathcal{O}
.
In stark contrast to the case of
\mathbb{Z}
,
when
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq 3
and
q\notin\{2,4,7,13,16\}
,
Serre and Vaserstein
[
46
]
*Lemma 1
have explicitly constructed
linear functions

a_{i}k+b_{i}
, with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
b_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, such that

\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant 3}(a_{i}k+b_{i})^{3}=k.

Vaserstein also gives more complicated polynomial constructions if
q\in\{7,13\}
.
In each case, the degrees of
x,y,z\in\mathcal{O}
solving
x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=k

are
\geqslant\deg{k}
, whereas one might hope for solutions of degree

\sim\tfrac{1}{3}\deg{k}
.
Also,
[
46
]
only constructs a bounded finite number of solutions for each
k
,
and thus it remains open to produce an infinitude of solutions, or even a number of solutions tending to infinity with
\deg{k}
.

According to
[
21
]
,
when
q=16
there is no known polynomial construction,
but a direct adaptation of
[
13
]
*Lemme 4.5 shows that there are no local obstructions
to the representation of
k
as a sum of three cubes from
\mathcal{O}
and so it is natural to conjecture that most, or perhaps all,
k\in\mathcal{O}
admit such a representation.
When
q\in\{2,4\}
, there are local obstructions at places of residue cardinality
4
, because all cubes in
\mathbb{F}_{4}
lie in the proper subfield
\mathbb{F}_{2}
;
but most, or perhaps all,
k\in\mathcal{O}
may still satisfy the Hasse principle,
with a positive proportion then being representable.
If
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})=3
, then
\{x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}:x,y,z\in\mathcal{O}\}
has density
0
in
\mathcal{O}
, however, because
x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=(x+y+z)^{3}
.

To each smooth hyperplane section
c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{6}x_{6}=0
of the hypersurface

x_{1}^{3}+\dots+x_{6}^{3}=0

in
\mathbb{P}^{5}_{K}
, for
\bm{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{6})\in\mathcal{O}^{6}
,
we shall see in §
3
that we can associate a Hasse–Weil
L
-function
L(s,\bm{c})
over
K
.
Thanks to Grothendieck and Deligne
[
17
,
23
]
,
we know that these
L
-functions are actually rational functions of
q^{-s}

that satisfy the Grand Riemann hypothesis (GRH).
Our main result relies on mean-value statistics of the ratio
1/L(s,\bm{c})
over boxes of vectors
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{6}
;
we need an asymptotic formula for

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{6}\\
\deg(c_{1}),\dots,\deg(c_{6})\leqslant Z\end{subarray}}\,\frac{1}{L(s_{1},\bm{c})L(s_{2},\bm{c})},

as
Z\to\infty
.
This is given by Conjecture
3.6
(R2),
a special case of the Ratios Conjecture.
The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3
and
assume the Ratios Conjecture
3.6

for
L(s,\bm{c})
, as
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{6}
varies.
Then each of the following sets has positive lower density in
\mathcal{O}
.

(1)

\{x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}:x,y,z\in\mathcal{O}\textnormal{ monic}\}
.

(2)

\{k:x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=k\textnormal{ is soluble in $\mathcal{O}$ with $\max\{\deg{x},\deg{y},\deg{z}\}\leqslant\lceil\frac{\deg{k}}{3}\rceil$}\}
.

The set in (2) is known in Waring’s problem
as the set of
strict sums of three cubes
,
in the sense of Carlitz
[
20
]
*p. 2964.
We have already seen that the conclusions of the theorem are false if

\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})=3
, where the sets in (1) and (2) have density
0
.
If
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})=2
, then most of the proof of Theorem
1.1
still goes through,
but some of the ingredients would need to be modified. We shall comment further on this in
§
11
.

The Moments and Ratios Conjectures were originally stated for automorphic
L
-functions over
\mathbb{Q}
by Conrey, Farmer, et al.
[
14
,
15
]
.
Their natural extension to
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
has been explained by Andrade and Keating
[
2
]
. The precise form of the Ratios Conjecture we need will be presented in §
3
.
It is plausible that the homological stability framework of
Bergström, Diaconu, Petersen and Westerland

[
4
]
, together with Miller, Patzt, Petersen and Randal-Williams

[
38
]
,
could eventually resolve
the particular form of the Ratios Conjecture needed in the present paper, for
sufficiently large values of
q
.
We defer the details of this to §
11.2
,
where we shall define a
q
-restricted
form of the Ratios Conjecture,
together with a strengthening of
Theorem
1.1
, in
the shape of Theorem
11.1
.
Still working under the Ratios Conjecture, it is likely that one can show that the set

\{k:x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=k\textnormal{ is soluble in $\mathcal{O}$ with $\max\{\deg{x},\deg{y},\deg{z}\}\leqslant\tfrac{\deg{k}}{3}+A$}\}

has lower density approaching
1
as
A\to\infty
, by adapting the proof of
[
50
]
*Theorem 1.6.
Moreover, one can likely show that this set has upper density
<1
for any fixed
A\in\mathbb{R}
,
by adapting local density arguments of Diaconu from
[
18
]
*§ 1.
We defer both tasks to future work, in order to keep the present paper as
clean as possible.

The Ratios Conjecture has already found application in the theory of
rational points
on elliptic curves over
K
. Thus, through the agency of Katz–Sarnak
[
32
]
, and Conrey et al.
[
15
,
16
]
, it has been shown that
Goldfeld’s Conjecture
follows from the Ratios Conjecture for a suitable family of elliptic curve
L
-functions over
K
.
This conjecture states that
there is a density
\frac{1}{2}

of curves with prescribed analytic rank
r\in\{0,1\}
, and
therefore implies the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture (BSD) for a density
1
of curves.
Whereas
L
-function ratios are directly related to analytic ranks and BSD,
their connection to
integral points
and sums of three cubes in Theorem
1.1
is much less direct.

Our proof of
Theorem
1.1
uses the second moment method and requires a detailed analysis of the counting function

(1.1)

N(P)=\#\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{O}^{6}:x_{1}^{3}+\cdots+x_{6}^{3}=0,~|x_{1}|=\cdots=|x_{6}|=|P|\right\},

for given
P\in\mathcal{O}
,
where
|\cdot|
denote the usual absolute value on
K
.
We shall be interested in the size of
N(P)
as
|P|\to\infty
.
The Batyrev–Manin conjecture suggests that an asymptotic formula of the shape

N(P)\sim c|P|^{3}
should hold, for a suitable constant
c>0
. The best progress towards this is to be found in
recent work of Glas and Hochfilzer

[
22
]
, who use
a function field version of the circle method to prove that

(1.2)

N(P)=O_{\varepsilon}(|P|^{3+\varepsilon}),

for any
\varepsilon>0
, assuming only that
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq 3
.
It is worth emphasising that this bound is completely unconditional, unlike the parallel picture over
\mathbb{Z}
, where the pioneering work of Hooley
[
25
,
29
]
and Heath-Brown
[
24
]
is conditional on GRH.
Assuming that
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3
, our proof of Theorem
1.1
entirely rests on our ability to remove the
\varepsilon
from the upper bound (
1.2
). Ultimately we shall only manage to do so at the cost of assuming the Ratios Conjecture for the Hasse–Weil
L
-functions
L(s,\bm{c})
, as
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{6}
varies.

Our argument will broadly follow the path laid down by Wang
[
50
]
over
\mathbb{Z}
, but with a variety of differences and refinements, some of which we have chosen to highlight here.

(1)

The
L
-functions
L(s,\bm{c})
carry less information over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
than over
\mathbb{Q}
, since
q
-adic sums of local coefficients are collapsed into single global coefficients. Nonetheless, these are still the only
L
-functions that we need in our analysis,
provided we choose our weight functions carefully, as suggested in the next item of this list.

(2)

In order to avoid introducing character twists as in
[
10
]
,
which would enlarge and complicate the form of the Ratios Conjecture needed, we restrict the class of weight functions that we use. This allows us to cleanly factor out the relevant oscillatory integral using symmetry ideas of Glas–Hochfilzer
[
22
]
.

(3)

The precise automorphy hypotheses and Square-Free Sieve Conjecture
required in
[
50
]

become unconditional over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
.
For the former, we use Poincaré duality to carefully produce poles of exterior square
L
-functions.
For the latter, we use an argument of Poonen

[
40
]
on square-free values of multivariable polynomials.

(4)

In
Theorem
7.2
we prove a simple version of the
Ekedahl sieve in positive characteristic for square-free moduli, which is sufficient for the applications in this paper.
(A general prime moduli version for arbitrary global fields has been worked out in forthcoming work of
Bhargava–Shankar–Wang
[
6
]
*Theorem 16.)

(5)

The bias in exponential sums that was discovered in

[
49
]
*Lemma 7.7
was
only worked out for diagonal cubic forms in six variables; in Proposition

10.6
we extend the argument to handle arbitrary non-singular senary cubic forms.
This requires new geometric insight on quadric bundles,
via work of Beauville
[
3
]
.

2.
Background

In this section we collect some basic facts about function fields.
Let
K_{\infty}=\mathbb{F}_{q}((t^{-1}))
be the field of Laurent series in
t^{-1}
.
Let
\mathcal{O}^{+}
be the set of monic polynomials
r\in\mathcal{O}
; this is analogous to the set of positive integers in
\mathbb{Z}
.
For
M\in\mathbb{R}
, we shall write
\widehat{M}\coloneqq q^{M}
. Any
\alpha\in K_{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
can be written uniquely as

(2.1)

\alpha=\sum_{i\leqslant M}a_{i}t^{i},\quad a_{M}\neq 0,

for some
M\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Define
|\alpha|\coloneqq\widehat{M}
;
then
|\cdot|
naturally extends to
K_{\infty}
the absolute value on
K
induced by
t^{-1}
.
Moreover,
K_{\infty}
is the completion of
K
with respect to this absolute value.
The analogue of the unit interval in
K_{\infty}
is given by

\mathbb{T}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in K_{\infty}\colon|\alpha|<1\}.

Since
K_{\infty}
is a local field, it can be endowed with a unique Haar measure
\differential\alpha
such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\differential\alpha=1
.
We shall extend the absolute value to
K^{n}_{\infty}
by
|\bm{\alpha}|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}|\alpha_{i}|
and the Haar measure by
\differential\bm{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\differential\alpha_{1}\cdots\differential\alpha_{n}
, for
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in K^{n}_{\infty}
.
Finally, for each prime
\varpi\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
, we have an associated absolute value
|\cdot|_{\varpi}
on
K
, given by
|a|_{\varpi}=q^{-v_{\varpi}(a)\deg\varpi}
. We denote by
K_{\varpi}
the completion of
K
with respect to this absolute value and we write
\mathcal{O}_{\varpi}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in K_{\varpi}\colon|\alpha|_{\varpi}\leqslant 1\}.

Farey dissection

Dirichlet’s approximation theorem holds over
K
.
That is, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
and
Q\in\mathbb{N}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
,
there exist
a\in\mathcal{O}
and
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}

with
\gcd(a,r)=1
and
|a|<|r|\leqslant\widehat{Q}

such that
|r\alpha-a|<\widehat{Q}^{-1}
.
By the ultrametric property, this is enough to obtain an analogue of a
Farey dissection
of the unit interval

(2.2)

\mathbb{T}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
|r|\leqslant\widehat{Q}\end{subarray}}\,\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|<|r|\\
\gcd(a,r)=1\end{subarray}}\,\{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}\colon|r\alpha-a|<\widehat{Q}^{-1}\},

for any
Q\geqslant 1
.

Characters

For
\alpha\in K_{\infty}
given by (
2.1
), we define

\psi\colon K_{\infty}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\quad\psi(\alpha)=e\left(\frac{\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a_{-1})}{p}\right)\quad\textnormal{(with $p=\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})$)}

and set
\psi(0)=1
, where as usual
e(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\exp(2\pi i x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
It is easy to see that
\psi
is a non-trivial additive character of
K_{\infty}
,
and that for
x\in K_{\infty}
and
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, we have

(2.3)

\int_{|\alpha|<\widehat{N}^{-1}}\psi(\alpha x)\differential\alpha=\begin{cases}\widehat{N}^{-1}&\text{if }|x|<\widehat{N},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that if
x\in\mathcal{O}
, then (
2.3
) implies

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(\alpha x)\differential\alpha=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x=0,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In addition, we will make frequent use of the following formula for exponential sums. If
r,a\in\mathcal{O}
are such that
r\neq 0
, then

\frac{1}{\lvert r\rvert}\sum_{\lvert x\rvert<\lvert r\rvert}\psi\left(\frac{ax}{r}\right)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if $r\mid a$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Poisson summation

We call a function
w\colon K_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

smooth
if it is locally constant. Denote by
S(K_{\infty}^{n})
the space of all smooth functions
w\colon K_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with compact support. For such functions the Poisson summation formula
[
10
]
*Lemma 2.1 holds.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in K_{\infty}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and let
w\in S(K_{\infty}^{n})
. Then we have

\sum_{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}}w(\bm{z})\psi(f(\bm{z}))=\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{n}}w(\bm{u})\psi(f(\bm{u})+\bm{c}\cdot\bm{u})\differential\bm{u}.

Delta method

Given
F\in\mathcal{O}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and a weight function
w\in S(K^{n}_{\infty})
, we are interested in the counting function

(2.4)

N_{F}(w,P)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}\\
F(\bm{x})=0\end{subarray}}w\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{P}\right).

For a parameter
Q\geqslant 1
to be specified later, we deduce from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

N_{F}(w,P)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
|r|\leqslant\widehat{Q}\end{subarray}}\,\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|a|<|r|}\int_{|\theta|<|r|^{-1}\widehat{Q}^{-1}}S(a/r+\theta)\differential\theta,

where
\sum^{\prime}_{|a|<|r|}
means that we sum over
a\in\mathcal{O}
with
\gcd(a,r)=1
only,
and where

S(\alpha)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}\end{subarray}}\psi(\alpha F(\bm{x}))w(\bm{x}/P)

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
. As explained in
[
10
]
*§ 4, we can use Poisson summation in Lemma
2.1

to evaluate
S(\theta+a/r)
, giving

(2.5)

N_{F}(w,P)=|P|^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
|r|\leqslant\widehat{Q}\end{subarray}}|r|^{-n}\int_{|\theta|<|r|^{-1}\widehat{Q}^{-1}}\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}}S_{r}(\bm{c})I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c})\differential\theta,

where

(2.6)

S_{r}(\bm{c})\coloneqq\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{|a|<|r|}\sum_{|\bm{x}|<|r|}\psi\left(\frac{aF(\bm{x})-\bm{c}\cdot\bm{x}}{r}\right)

and

(2.7)

I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c})\coloneqq\int_{K_{\infty}^{n}}w(\bm{x})\psi\left(\theta P^{3}F(\bm{x})+\frac{P\bm{c}\cdot\bm{x}}{r}\right)\differential\bm{x}.

Moreover, we will also consider averages of
I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c})
of the form

(2.8)

I_{r}(\bm{c})\coloneqq\int_{|\theta|<|r|^{-1}\widehat{Q}^{-1}}I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c})\differential\theta,

in which case we may write

(2.9)

N_{F}(w,P)=|P|^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
|r|\leqslant\widehat{Q}\end{subarray}}|r|^{-n}\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}}S_{r}(\bm{c})I_{r}(\bm{c}).

Ultimately
Q
will be chosen so that
|P|^{3/2}\asymp\widehat{Q}
.

The expression (
2.5
) is the starting point for our work and from now on we will mostly be concerned with estimating the integrals
I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c})
and the sums
S_{r}(\bm{c})
. To keep notation simple, it is convenient to introduce the normalised sum

S_{r}^{\natural}(\bm{c})\coloneqq|r|^{-(n+1)/2}S_{r}(\bm{c}).

If
r=r_{1}r_{2}
, where both
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathcal{O}
are monic with
\gcd(r_{1},r_{2})=1
, the Chinese remainder theorem readily implies that

(2.10)

S_{r}(\bm{c})=S_{r_{1}}(\bm{c})S_{r_{2}}(\bm{c}).

Therefore the prime factorisation of
r
will play an important role in our analysis.
The letter
\varpi
will generally denote a prime in
\mathcal{O}^{+}
.

Notation

Whenever the letter
F
appears in some context from now on, we take

F=x_{1}^{3}+\dots+x_{n}^{3},

typically with
n=6
. However, we will often work more generally to clarify the nature of arguments.
Let

(2.11)

F^{\ast}(\bm{c})=\prod(c_{1}^{3/2}\pm c_{2}^{3/2}\pm\dots\pm c_{n}^{3/2})\in\mathcal{O}[\bm{c}]

be the (primitive) dual form associated to
F
; it has degree
3\cdot 2^{n-2}
.
Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
be the hypersurface defined by
F=0
. The dual form
F^{*}
defines the dual variety
V^{*}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
of
V
, which parameterises hyperplanes that intersect
V
tangentially. Alternatively,
V^{*}
may be described as the closure of the image under the Gauss map
V\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
given by
\bm{x}\mapsto\nabla F(\bm{x})
. From this description it is clear that

(2.12)

F(\bm{x})\mid F^{*}(\nabla F(\bm{x})),

as an identity in
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Let

(2.13)

\mathcal{S}_{0}=\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}:F^{\ast}(\bm{c})=0\},\quad\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{n}:F^{\ast}(\bm{c})\neq 0\}.

For each
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, let

(2.14)

\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{G}}=\{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\gcd(r,F^{\ast}(\bm{c}))=1\},\quad\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}=\{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\varpi\mid r\Rightarrow\varpi\mid F^{\ast}(\bm{c})\}.

Given
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
and non-zero
B\in\mathcal{O}
,
we will sometimes write
r\mid B^{\infty}
to mean that

\varpi\mid r\Rightarrow\varpi\mid B
.
This is equivalent to
\operatorname{rad}(r)\mid B
, where
\operatorname{rad}(r)
is the radical of
r
.

To connect
S_{r}(\bm{c})
to Hasse–Weil
L
-functions, we define further quantities.
Given a finite field
k
,
let
\mathcal{V}(k)
be the set of
k
-points on the variety
F(\bm{x})=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
,
let
\mathcal{V}_{\bm{c}}(k)
be the set of
k
-points on the variety
F(\bm{x})=\bm{c}\cdot\bm{x}=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{5}_{k}
,
and let

E_{F}(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\#{\mathcal{V}(k)}-\#{\mathbb{P}^{4}(k)},\quad E_{\bm{c}}(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\#{\mathcal{V}_{\bm{c}}(k)}-\#{\mathbb{P}^{3}(k)},

where
\#{\mathbb{P}^{d}(k)}=((\#k)^{d+1}-1)/((\#k)-1)
.
Next, let

(2.15)

E^{\natural}_{F}(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\#k)^{-2}E_{F}(k),\quad E^{\natural}_{\bm{c}}(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\#k)^{-3/2}E_{\bm{c}}(k).

For primes
\varpi\nmid\bm{c}
, it is known that

(2.16)

S^{\natural}_{\varpi}(\bm{c})=E^{\natural}_{\bm{c}}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi\mathcal{O})-\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-1/2}E^{\natural}_{F}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi\mathcal{O});

see
[
30
]
*Lemma 7 for a general reference over prime fields, which carries over directly to prime-power fields.
Moreover, if
\varpi\nmid F^{\ast}(\bm{c})
then
[
10
]
*Lemma 5.2 gives

(2.17)

S_{\varpi^{l}}(\bm{c})=0\quad(l\geqslant 2).

Technical results

We proceed to record some useful results that will often be appealed to during the course of our argument.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R\geqslant 0
and
B\in\mathcal{O}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then

\#{\{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:r\mid B^{\infty},\;\lvert r\rvert=\widehat{R}\}}\ll_{\varepsilon}(\widehat{R}\lvert B\rvert)^{\varepsilon}.

In particular, if
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, then

\#{\{r\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}:\lvert r\rvert=\widehat{R}\}}\ll_{\varepsilon}\lvert\bm{c}\rvert^{\varepsilon}\widehat{R}^{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

\sum_{r\mid B^{\infty}}\bm{1}_{\lvert r\rvert=\widehat{R}}\leqslant\sum_{r\mid B^{\infty}}(\widehat{R}/\lvert r\rvert)^{\varepsilon}=\widehat{R}^{\varepsilon}\prod_{\varpi\mid B}(1-\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-\varepsilon})^{-1}\ll_{\varepsilon}\widehat{R}^{\varepsilon}\lvert B\rvert^{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If

and
A,\varepsilon>0
, then we have

(2.18)

\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}:\,\lvert\bm{c}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{Z}}\#{\{r\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}:\lvert r\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}\}}^{A}\ll_{A,\varepsilon}\widehat{Z}^{n}\widehat{R}^{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

If
\widehat{Z}<\widehat{R}^{A}
, use Lemma
2.2
.
Now suppose
\widehat{Z}\geqslant\widehat{R}^{A}
.
By Hölder’s inequality, we may assume

.
Then
\Sigma
, the left-hand side of (
2.18
), equals

\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}:\,\lvert\bm{c}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1},\dots,u_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}\\
\lvert u_{i}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1},\dots,u_{A}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
\lvert u_{i}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}:\,\lvert\bm{c}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{Z}}\bm{1}_{\operatorname{rad}(u_{1}\cdots u_{A})\mid F^{\ast}(\bm{c})}.

Here
\lvert u_{1}\cdots u_{A}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}^{A}\leqslant\widehat{Z}

\begin{split}\Sigma&\ll_{\varepsilon}\sum_{u_{1},\dots,u_{A}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\,\lvert u_{i}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}}\frac{\widehat{Z}^{n}}{\lvert\operatorname{rad}(u_{1}\cdots u_{A})\rvert^{1-\varepsilon}}\\
&\leqslant\sum_{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\,\lvert r\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}^{A}}\frac{\widehat{Z}^{n}}{\lvert r\rvert^{1-\varepsilon}}\sum_{u_{1},\dots,u_{A}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\,\lvert u_{i}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R},\;u_{i}\mid r^{\infty}}1.\end{split}

Lemma
2.2
now yields
\Sigma\ll_{A,\varepsilon}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\,\lvert r\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}^{A}}\widehat{Z}^{n}\lvert r\rvert^{\varepsilon-1}(\widehat{R}\lvert r\rvert)^{\varepsilon}\ll_{\varepsilon}\widehat{Z}^{n}\widehat{R}^{(2A+1)\varepsilon}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8

\bm{a}^{\ast}{g}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}g(y_{1},y_{2}+a_{2}y_{1},\dots,y_{n}+a_{n}y_{1})\in\mathcal{O}[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]

\sum_{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\,\lvert r\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}}\lvert a_{\bm{c},3}(r)\rvert\leqslant\sum_{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}:\,\lvert d\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}}\lvert a_{\bm{c},3}(d)\rvert\sum_{e\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{G}}:\,\lvert e\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}}\lvert a_{\bm{c},3}(e)\rvert.

But by

and

\sum_{e\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{G}}:\,\lvert e\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}}\frac{\lvert a_{\bm{c},3}(e)\rvert}{\widehat{R}^{1/3+\varepsilon}}\leqslant\sum_{e\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{G}}}\frac{\lvert a_{\bm{c},3}(e)\rvert}{\lvert e\rvert^{1/3+\varepsilon}}\leqslant\prod_{\varpi}\left(1+\frac{O(\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-1/2})}{\lvert\varpi\rvert^{2/3+2\varepsilon}}+\frac{O_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\varpi\rvert^{\varepsilon})}{\lvert\varpi\rvert^{1+3\varepsilon}}\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}1,

So by (

\ll_{A,\varepsilon}\widehat{R}^{(1/3+2\varepsilon)A}\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}:\,\lvert\bm{c}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{Z}}\#{\{d\in\mathcal{R}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}:\lvert d\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}\}}^{A}.

∎

Conjecture 4.5

5.
Integral estimates

Let
\gamma\in K_{\infty}
,
\bm{w}\in K_{\infty}^{n}
and
G\in K_{\infty}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. For a fixed smooth weight function
w\colon K_{\infty}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support, we are interested in integrals of the form

J_{G,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})\coloneqq\int_{K_{\infty}}w(\bm{x})\psi(\gamma G(\bm{x})+\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x})\differential\bm{x},

together with their averages

J^{\Gamma}_{G,w}(\bm{w})=\int_{|\gamma|<\widehat{\Gamma}}J_{G,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})\differential\gamma,

for
\Gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
. (In fact, the dependence of the latter integral on
\Gamma
is mild, as we will soon see.)
With this notation we have
I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c})=J_{F,w}(\theta P^{3},P\bm{c}/r)
in (
2.7
) and

I_{r}(\bm{c})=J_{F,w}^{\Gamma}(P\bm{c}/r)
in
(
2.8
),
where
\Gamma=-\deg(r)-Q
.

When
w
is the indicator function of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we shall write
J_{G}(\gamma,\bm{w})
and
J^{\Gamma}_{G}(\bm{w})
for the sake of brevity. Moreover, we let
H_{G}
be the maximum of the absolute values of the coefficients of
G
and refer to it as the height of
G
.

This level of generality, where
G
is allowed to vary, will be useful when changing variables to analyse
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})
.
Let us recall Lemmas 2.4 and 2.7 of
[
10
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G\in K_{\infty}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
\bm{w}\in K_{\infty}^{n}
. Suppose
|\bm{w}|\geqslant 1
and
|\bm{w}|>H_{G}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi(G(\bm{x})+\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x})\differential\bm{x}=0.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

\Omega=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\colon|\gamma\nabla G(\bm{x})+\bm{w}|\leqslant H_{G}\max\{1,|\gamma|^{1/2}\}\}.

Then we have

J_{G}(\gamma,\bm{w})=\int_{\Omega}\psi(\gamma G(\bm{x})+\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x})\differential\bm{x}.

We now fix a non-singular cubic form
F\in\mathcal{O}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, which we will eventually take to be our initial cubic form
x_{1}^{3}+\cdots+x_{6}^{3}
, but is allowed to be arbitrary in this section. Before we start our investigation, we impose the following conditions on the weight function
w
.

Hypothesis 5.3
.

Let
H(\bm{x})=(\frac{\partial^{i+j}F(\bm{x})}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}})
be the Hessian associated to
F
.

(1)

If
\bm{x}\in\supp(w)
, then
\det H(\bm{x})\neq 0
.

(2)

There exist
\bm{x_{0}}\in K_{\infty}^{n}
with
\lvert\bm{x}_{0}\rvert\leqslant 1
, and an integer
L\geqslant 0
, such that

w(\bm{x})=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{if }|\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0}|<\widehat{L}^{-1},\\
0&\textnormal{else.}\end{cases}

If
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})\in\{2,3\}
, then
\det H(\bm{x})
vanishes identically and so (i) is impossible in this case. Therefore, all of our results in this section have the implicit assumption that
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3
.
Moreover, condition (ii) is non-restrictive, since any compactly supported smooth function
w^{\prime}\colon K_{\infty}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
can be written as

w^{\prime}=s_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}w_{m}
,
where
s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
w_{i}
are indicator functions of some compact ball in
K_{\infty}^{n}
.
Part (ii) of Hypothesis
5.3
implies the crucial symmetry property

(5.1)

w(\bm{x})=w((1+t^{-L}\gamma)\bm{x})
for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{T}
.

In other words,
w
is invariant under scaling by
1+t^{-L}\mathbb{T}
. Throughout this section, all implied constants are allowed to depend on the choice of
w
and in particular implicitly also on the parameter
L
and the vector
\bm{x}_{0}
in the definition of
w
.

For the purposes of Theorem
1.1
, we will be focusing on diagonal cubic forms.
In this setting we shall work with the following explicit weight function.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
F(\bm{x})=a_{1}x_{1}^{3}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}^{3}
, for
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and let
\bm{x}_{0}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{n}
be such that
F(\bm{x}_{0})=0
. Then we define

w(\bm{x})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }|\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0}|<1,\\
0&\text{else.}\end{cases}

We check that such weight functions satisfy
Hypothesis
5.3
when
F
is a diagonal cubic form with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3
.
Condition (i) can then be rephrased as
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\neq 0
for all
\bm{x}\in\supp(w)
, which is obvious. Condition (ii) clearly holds with
L=0
.

Returning to the setting of general weight functions
w
satisfying
Hypothesis
5.3
, we begin with the following estimate.

Lemma 5.5
.

For
\Gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\bm{w}\in K_{\infty}^{n}
, we have

J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})\ll_{w,F}(1+|\bm{w}|)^{1-n/2}.

Moreover, if
|\bm{w}|\gg 1
, then
J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})=0
unless
|\gamma|\asymp|\bm{w}|
.

Proof.

In this proof all implied constants are allowed to depend on
F
.
Upon making the change of variables
\bm{y}=t^{L}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0})
, it follows that

J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})=\widehat{L}^{-n}\psi(\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x_{0}})J_{G}(\gamma,t^{-L}\bm{w}),

where
G(\bm{y})=F(t^{-L}\bm{y}+\bm{x}_{0})
. It is clear that
H_{G}\ll H_{F}\ll 1
. At this point we can use Lemma
5.1
to conclude that
J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})=0
unless
|\bm{w}|\ll\max\{1,|\gamma|\}
. Furthermore, by Lemma
5.2

\displaystyle|J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\widehat{L}^{-n}\text{meas}\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\colon|\gamma\nabla G(\bm{y})+t^{-L}\bm{w}|\leqslant H_{G}\max\{1,|\gamma|^{1/2}\}\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\text{meas}\{\bm{x}\in\supp(w)\colon|\gamma\nabla F(\bm{x})+t^{-L}\bm{w}|\ll\max\{1,|\gamma|^{1/2}\}\}.

Let us denote the set whose measure we want to estimate by
\Omega
. Since
\bm{0}\not\in\supp(w)
, we must have
|\nabla F(\bm{x})|\asymp 1
for all
\bm{x}\in\supp(w)
. If
|\bm{w}|\ll 1
, then
\Omega
is empty unless
|\gamma|\ll 1
, so that we can use the trivial estimate
|J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})|\leqslant 1
to conclude that
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})\ll 1
if
|\bm{w}|\ll 1
. In particular, we may assume
|\bm{w}|\gg 1
for the rest of the proof. In this case the contribution from
|\gamma|\ll 1
vanishes, since then
\Omega
is empty, while for
|\gamma|\gg 1
the defining inequality for
\Omega
becomes

|\gamma\nabla F(\bm{x})+t^{-L}\bm{w}|\ll|\gamma|^{1/2}.

This can only hold if
|\gamma|\asymp|\bm{w}|
. From this we deduce the statement that
J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})=0
unless
|\gamma|\asymp|\bm{w}|
.

It remains to give an upper bound.
This will be similar to
[
10
]
*proof of Lemma 7.3.
Let us now assume that
\bm{x},\bm{x}+\bm{x}^{\prime}
are both in
\Omega
. Note that
\nabla F(\bm{x}+\bm{x}^{\prime})-\nabla F(\bm{x})=H(\bm{x}+\bm{x}^{\prime}/2)\bm{x}^{\prime}
, so that we must then have

(5.2)

|H(\bm{x}+\bm{x}^{\prime}/2)\bm{x}^{\prime}|\ll|\gamma|^{-1/2}.

As
\det H(\bm{y})\neq 0
for all
\bm{y}\in\supp(w)
, compactness of
\supp(w)
implies that we have
|\det H(\bm{y})|\gg 1
for all
\bm{y}\in\supp(w)
. In addition, a straightforward computation shows that
\bm{x}+\bm{x}^{\prime}/2\in\supp(w)
, so that we can multiply (
5.2
) from the left with the inverse of
H(\bm{x}+\bm{x}^{\prime}/2)
, whose entries have absolute value
O(1)
, to conclude that
|\bm{x}^{\prime}|\ll|\gamma|^{-1/2}\ll|\bm{w}|^{-1/2}
. Finally, from this we obtain

\displaystyle J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})\ll\int_{|\gamma|\asymp|\bm{w}|}\text{meas}(\Omega)\differential\gamma\ll|\bm{w}|^{1-n/2}

when
|\bm{w}|\gg 1
, as desired.
∎

The following symmetry property of
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})

generalises
[
22
]
*Lemma 3.6.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K_{\infty}^{\times}
are such that

\lambda_{1}/\lambda_{2}\in 1+t^{-L}\mathbb{T}
.
Then

J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\lambda_{1}\bm{w})=J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\lambda_{2}\bm{w})\quad\textnormal{for any $\bm{w}\in K_{\infty}^{n}$}.

Proof.

Let us write
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
and show that for
\bm{w}
fixed, the value of
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\lambda\bm{w})
only depends on the image of
\lambda
in the quotient group
K_{\infty}^{\times}/(1+t^{-L}\mathbb{T})
. We have

\displaystyle J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\lambda\bm{w})

\displaystyle=\int_{|\gamma|<\widehat{\Gamma}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{n}}w(\bm{x})\psi(\gamma F(\bm{x})+\lambda\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x})\differential\bm{x}\differential\gamma

\displaystyle=|\lambda|^{n}\int_{K_{\infty}^{n}}w(\lambda^{-1}\bm{y})\psi(\bm{w}\cdot\bm{y})\int_{|\gamma|<\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi(\gamma\lambda^{-3}F(\bm{y}))\differential\gamma\differential\bm{y},

where we applied the change of variables
\bm{y}=\lambda\bm{x}
. It follows from (
2.3
) that

\int_{|\gamma|<\widehat{\Gamma}}\psi(\gamma\lambda^{-3}F(\bm{y}))\differential\gamma=\begin{cases}\widehat{\Gamma}&\text{if }|F(\bm{x})|<\widehat{\Gamma}^{-1}|\lambda|^{3},\\
0&\text{else.}\end{cases}

This formula clearly only depends on
|\lambda|
.
Moreover,

w(\lambda^{-1}\bm{y})
depends only on
\lambda\bmod{1+t^{-L}\mathbb{T}}
by (
5.1
). Since we assume
L\geqslant 0
, any two elements of
K_{\infty}^{\times}
whose image in
K_{\infty}^{\times}/(1+t^{-L}\mathbb{T})
coincides must have the same absolute value, from which we deduce that
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\lambda\bm{w})
only depends on
\lambda\bmod{1+t^{-L}\mathbb{T}}
.
∎

The above establishes most of the analogue over
K
of
[
50
]
*Proposition 8.1.
It still remains to show that
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})
vanishes unless
|F^{*}(\bm{w})|
is small.
Working over function fields significantly simplifies the execution of the key ideas,
because fewer dyadic ranges intervene.
We start with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
R
be a compact set such that

R\subset\{\bm{x}\in K_{\infty}^{n}\colon\det H(\bm{x})\neq 0\}
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(R)>0
such that if
\bm{w}\in K_{\infty}^{n}
and
\bm{x}\in R
satisfy

|\nabla F(\bm{x})-\bm{w}|<\widehat{C}^{-1},

it follows that there exists
\bm{s}\in K_{\infty}^{n}

with
\nabla F(\bm{s})=\bm{w}
and

|\bm{s}-\bm{x}|<\widehat{C}|\nabla F(\bm{x})-\bm{w}|.

Proof.

Let
\bm{x}\in R
. Since
\det H(\bm{x})\neq 0
, it follows from the inverse function theorem for local fields

[
43
]
*Part II, § III.9, Theorem 2
that there exist open neighbourhoods
U,U^{\prime}\subset K_{\infty}^{n}
, with
\det H(\bm{y})\neq 0
for any
\bm{y}
in the closure of
U
, such that
\bm{x}\in U
,
\nabla F(\bm{x})\in U^{\prime}
and
\nabla F\colon U\to U^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.
In addition, after shrinking
U^{\prime}
if necessary, we may assume that it is an open ball of radius
\widehat{C}_{0}^{-1}
around
\nabla F(\bm{x})
, for some real
C_{0}=C_{0}(\bm{x})\geqslant 0
.
It follows that if
\bm{w}\in U^{\prime}
, there exists
\bm{s}\in U
such that
\nabla F(\bm{s})=\bm{w}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\bm{w}=\nabla F(\bm{s})=\nabla F(\bm{s}-\bm{x})+H(\bm{x})(\bm{s}-\bm{x})+\nabla F(\bm{x}).

As
\bm{x}\in R
, we have
\det H(\bm{x})\neq 0
, and hence

\bm{s}-\bm{x}=H(\bm{x})^{-1}(\bm{w}-\nabla F(\bm{x})+\nabla F(\bm{s}-\bm{x})).

Moreover, as
R
is compact, the entries of
H(\bm{x})^{-1}
are
O_{R}(1)
. It follows that there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}\geqslant 0
depending only on
R
and
F
such that

|\bm{s}-\bm{x}|\leqslant\widehat{C}_{1}|\bm{w}-\nabla F(\bm{x})|+\widehat{C}_{2}|\bm{s}-\bm{x}|^{2}.

Upon shrinking
U
if necessary, we may assume that
|\bm{s}-\bm{x}|<(2\widehat{C}_{2})^{-1}
, so that we obtain

|\bm{s}-\bm{x}|\leqslant 2\widehat{C}_{1}|\bm{w}-\nabla F(\bm{x})|.

Since
R
is compact, we can cover it with finitely many open sets
U
and then take
C
to be the maximum of the constants that arise in this process.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Assume
L=0
in condition (ii) on the weight function
w
.
Let
\Gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\bm{w}\in K_{\infty}^{n}
.
Then
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})=0
unless
|F^{*}({\bm{w}})|\ll 1+|\bm{w}|^{\deg F^{*}-1}
.

Proof.

Note that if
|\bm{w}|\ll 1
, then
|F^{*}({\bm{w}})|\ll 1+|\bm{w}|^{\deg F^{*}-1}
holds trivially, so that we may assume
|\bm{w}|\gg 1
from now on. Similarly we can assume
1\ll|\bm{w}|\ll\widehat{\Gamma}
, since else
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})=0
by Lemma
5.5
. Using Lemma
5.5
again, it follows that

J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})=\int_{|\gamma|\asymp|\bm{w}|}J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})\differential\gamma.

Let
Z\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be such that
\widehat{Z}\asymp|\bm{w}|
.
We will now consider the contribution to
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})
from those
\gamma
with
|\gamma|=\widehat{Z}
.
To do so, write
\gamma=at^{Z}(1+\gamma^{\prime})
, where
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}
.
Let
\bm{y}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0}
and
G(\bm{y})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F(\bm{y}+\bm{x}_{0})
, as in the proof of Lemma

.
Then let

\bm{x}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\bm{x}=(1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}(\bm{y}+\bm{x}_{0}).

Note that since
(1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}-1\in\mathbb{T}
and

, property (

) of the weight function

implies
w(\bm{x})=w(\bm{x}^{\prime})
.
In particular, after the change of variables
\bm{x}\mapsto(1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\bm{x}
by condition (ii) on
w
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}w(\bm{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\psi(\gamma F(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x}^{\prime})\differential\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\psi((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\bm{x}_{0}\cdot\bm{w})\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\psi((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}(at^{Z}G(\bm{y})+\bm{y}\cdot\bm{w}))\differential\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\psi((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\bm{x}_{0}\cdot\bm{w})\int_{\Omega}\psi((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}(at^{Z}G(\bm{y})+\bm{y}\cdot\bm{w}))\differential\bm{y},

by Lemma
5.2
, where

\Omega\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\colon|at^{Z}\nabla G(\bm{y})+\bm{w}|\ll\widehat{Z}^{1/2}\}

and we used that
\gamma F(\bm{x}^{\prime})=at^{Z}(1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}F(\bm{y}+\bm{x}_{0})
since
F
is homogeneous of degree
3
.
In summary, we have proven the identity

J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})=J_{F,w}((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-3/2}\gamma,(1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\bm{w}),

and applied stationary phase to the latter integral to get an integral over
\bm{y}\in\Omega
.

Upon expressing the integral over
\bm{y}\in\Omega
in terms of
\bm{x}=\bm{y}+\bm{x}_{0}
, we find that

J_{F,w}(\gamma,\bm{w})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}w(\bm{x}^{\prime})\psi(\gamma F(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x}^{\prime})\differential\bm{x}^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\psi((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\Phi(\bm{x}))\differential\bm{x},

where
\Phi(\bm{x})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}at^{Z}F(\bm{x})+\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x}

and

\Omega^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\bm{x}\in\supp(w)\colon|a\nabla F(\bm{x})+t^{-Z}\bm{w}|\ll\widehat{Z}^{-1/2}\}.

The crucial observation here is that
\Omega^{\prime}
is independent of
\gamma^{\prime}
.
In particular, when considering the total contribution to
J^{\Gamma}_{F,w}(\bm{w})
from the set
\{\gamma=at^{Z}(1+\gamma^{\prime}):\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}\}
, we may exchange the order of integration and see that it is given by

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\Phi(\bm{x}))\differential\gamma^{\prime}\differential{\bm{x}}.

Here
\Omega^{\prime}
,
\Phi
depend on
Z
and
a
, but all estimates below will be uniform over
Z
and
a
. Let us now take the Taylor expansion

(1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}a_{k}\gamma^{\prime k},

where
a_{k}=\binom{-1/2}{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
This is well defined, because
|\gamma^{\prime}|<1
and
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq 2
.
Since
|\gamma^{\prime}|<1
, the value of
\psi((1+\gamma^{\prime})^{-1/2}\Phi(\bm{x}))
only depends on
f(\gamma^{\prime})=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}a_{k}\gamma^{\prime k}
if we choose
k_{0}
sufficiently large. We may now apply Lemma
5.2
again with
n=1
to infer that the inner integral is given by

\int_{\Theta}\psi(f(\gamma^{\prime})\Phi(\bm{x}))\differential\gamma^{\prime},

where

\Theta=\{\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}\colon|f^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime})\Phi(\bm{x})|\leqslant\max\{1,|\Phi(\bm{x})|^{1/2}\}\}.

It is easy to see that
|f^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime})|=1
, so that
\Theta
is non-empty only if
|\Phi(\bm{x})|\leqslant 1
, which we shall assume for the rest of the proof.

Since
\bm{x}\in\Omega^{\prime}
, we have
\bm{x}\in\supp(w)
and
|a\nabla F(\bm{x})+t^{-Z}\bm{w}|\ll\widehat{Z}^{-1/2}\ll|\bm{w}|^{-1/2}
. Since we assume
|\bm{w}|\gg 1
for a sufficiently large implied constant, we may invoke Lemma
5.7
to deduce the existence of
\bm{s}\in K_{\infty}^{n}
such that
|\bm{s}-\bm{x}|\ll|a\nabla F(\bm{x})+t^{-Z}\bm{w}|\ll|\bm{w}|^{-1/2}
and
\nabla F(\bm{s})=-a^{-1}t^{-Z}\bm{w}
. Using Taylor expansion at
\bm{s}
it follows that

\displaystyle|aF(\bm{x})+t^{-Z}\bm{w}\cdot\bm{x}|

\displaystyle=|aF(\bm{s})+t^{-Z}\bm{w}\cdot\bm{s}|+O(|\bm{x}-\bm{s}|^{2})

\displaystyle=|aF(\bm{s})+t^{-Z}\bm{w}\cdot\bm{s}|+O(|\bm{w}|^{-1}).

Moreover, as
a\nabla F(\bm{s})=-t^{-Z}\bm{w}
, we have
3aF(\bm{s})=-t^{-Z}\bm{w}\cdot\bm{s}
and hence

{|aF(\bm{s})+t^{-Z}\bm{w}\cdot\bm{s}|=|F(\bm{s})|}.

By (
2.12
), the ratio
F^{*}(\nabla F(\bm{x}))/F(\bm{x})
is a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathcal{O}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
of degree
2\deg F^{*}-3
,
which immediately implies

|F^{*}(\nabla F(\bm{s}))|\ll|F(\bm{s})||\bm{s}|^{2\deg F^{*}-3}.

In addition, as
|\bm{s}-\bm{x}|\ll|\bm{w}|^{-1/2}
, we have
|\bm{s}|\asymp 1
.
Together with
-a^{-1}t^{-Z}\bm{w}=\nabla F(\bm{s})
, this implies

|F^{*}(t^{-Z}\bm{w})|=|F^{*}(\nabla F(\bm{s}))|\ll|F(\bm{s})|

and hence

|F^{*}(\bm{w})|\ll\widehat{Z}^{\deg F^{*}}(\widehat{Z}^{-1}|\Phi(\bm{x})|+|\bm{w}|^{-1})\ll|\bm{w}|^{\deg F^{*}-1},

as desired.
∎

As usual we assume that

\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]

\Char(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3

Throughout this section we

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

be a prime power.

(1)

If

then

(2)

If

S_{\varpi^{2}}^{\natural}(\bm{c})\ll|\varpi|

(3)

If

S_{\varpi^{e}}^{\natural}(\bm{c})\ll|\varpi^{e}|^{1/2}\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}|\gcd(\varpi^{e},\sq(c_{i}))|^{1/4}.

Part (1) is due to

*Lemma 60.
Part (2) is straightforward and is recorded

*Eq. (4.7).
To see part (3) we invoke

]
*Eq. (4.7) to deduce that

S_{\varpi^{e}}^{\natural}(\bm{c})\ll\frac{1}{|\varpi^{e}|^{(n+1)/2}}\cdot|\varpi^{e}|\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}|\varpi^{e}|^{1/2}\{\varpi^{e},c_{i}\}^{1/4},

By multiplicativity, it suffices to show that

A version of the geometric sieve for arbitrary global fields has been worked out in forthcoming work of

Their result sieves out prime moduli,

A similar idea is used in

Theorem 7.2
.

Let

Fix two polynomials

\text{$r\mid\gcd(H_{0}(\bm{c}),H_{1}(\bm{c}))$ and $|r|=\widehat{Y}$}\end{array}\right\}\ll_{\varepsilon}\widehat{Y}^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\widehat{T}^{n}}{\widehat{Y}}+\widehat{T}^{n-1}\right).

Proof.

Case 1:

Applying the Lang–Weil estimate for the quasi-projective variety

\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\

as desired.

Case 2:

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{Y_{1},Y_{2},C}|P|^{n-3}\widehat{C}^{n/p_{2}}\widehat{Y}^{1-n/2}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{|P|\widehat{C}}{\widehat{Y}}\right)^{1-n/2}\left(\widehat{C}^{n/p_{1}}+\widehat{Y}^{n/3p_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{Y_{1},Y_{2}}|P|^{n/2-2}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}}\sum_{C}\widehat{C}^{1+n/p_{2}-n/2}\left(\widehat{C}^{n/p_{1}}+\widehat{Y}^{n/3p_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{Y_{1},Y_{2}}|P|^{n/2-2}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}}|P|^{(1+n/p_{2}-n/2)/2}\left(|P|^{n/2p_{1}}+\widehat{Y}^{n/3p_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{Y_{1},Y_{2}}|P|^{3n/4-3/2}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}},

where we used
1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}=1
and that
\widehat{C}\ll|P|^{1/2}
, as well as the fact that
\widehat{Y}\ll|P|^{3/2}
. Since

for

, to complete our treatment of

it will suffice to show that
\sum_{Y_{1},Y_{2}}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}}\ll 1
.
To do so, recall that
\widehat{Y}_{2}^{n+\varepsilon}>\widehat{W}^{\alpha/2}
.
If we set
\beta=\eta^{\prime}\alpha/(4(n+\varepsilon))
, then recalling the definition of
\widehat{W}
in (
8.3
) this implies

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}/2}\widehat{W}^{-\beta}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}/2}\left(\left(\frac{\widehat{Y}}{|P|^{3/2}}\right)^{\beta}+|P|^{\beta(\varepsilon-1)/2}\right).

Therefore, as
\sum_{Y_{1},Y_{2}}|P|^{\beta(\varepsilon-1)/2}\leqslant Q^{2}|P|^{\beta(\varepsilon-1)/2}\ll 1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leqslant Y_{2}\leqslant Q}\sum_{0\leqslant Y_{1}\leqslant Q-Y_{2}}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\ll|P|^{-3\beta/2}\sum_{0\leqslant Y_{2}\leqslant Q}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}/2+\beta}\sum_{0\leqslant Y_{1}\leqslant Q-Y_{2}}\widehat{Y}_{1}^{\beta}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{0\leqslant Y_{2}\leqslant Q}\widehat{Y}_{2}^{-\eta^{\prime}/2}

\displaystyle\ll 1,

as

. This completes our
proof of Proposition
8.1
.

10.1.
Geometric preliminaries

Throughout this subsection,

k
denotes an arbitrary field with
\cha(k)\neq 2
and
we shall adopt the notation
\mathbb{P}(E)
for the projectivization of a
k
-vector space
E
. (In particular,
\mathbb{P}^{n}
denotes the projective space
\mathbb{P}(k^{n+1})
.)
Suppose that
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a smooth cubic fourfold containing a
k
-plane
M\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
, corresponding to a three-dimensional vector space
L\subset k^{6}
.
Projection away from
M
, defined by the natural map
\mathbb{P}(k^{6})\to\mathbb{P}(k^{6}/L)
, gives a morphism
V\setminus M\to\mathbb{P}(k^{6}/L)
. Blowing up
V
along
M
resolves the indeterminacy locus and gives a fibration
\widetilde{V}\to\mathbb{P}(k^{6}/L)
whose fibres are projective quadric surfaces. Being the blow-up of a smooth variety along a smooth subvariety,
\widetilde{V}
is also smooth. If we denote by
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{P}(k^{6}/L)
the zero scheme
of the determinant of the matrices that define the quadratic forms in the fibers, it follows from Proposition 1.2 and Exemple 1.4.2 of Beauville
[
3
]
that
\mathcal{C}
is a curve of degree 6 which is either smooth or has ordinary double points.
Let
L^{\perp}
be the orthogonal complement of
L
inside
k^{6}
and note that
\mathbb{P}(L^{\perp})
parameterises hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}(k^{6})
containing
M
.
If
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{P}(L^{\perp})
,
we shall write
H_{\bm{c}}
for the corresponding hyperplane containing
M
in
\mathbb{P}(k^{6})
and
H_{\bm{c}}^{\prime}
for the image of
H_{\bm{c}}
in
\mathbb{P}(k^{6}/L)
, which defines a projective line.

If we denote by
V_{\bm{c}}=H_{\bm{c}}\cap V
, then we get again a morphism
V_{\bm{c}}\setminus M\to H_{\bm{c}}^{\prime}
by projecting away from
M
. Blowing up
V_{\bm{c}}
gives a fibration
\widetilde{V}_{\bm{c}}\to H_{\bm{c}}^{\prime}
into quadric surfaces and the locus of degenerate quadric surfaces is given by
\mathcal{C}_{\bm{c}}=H_{\bm{c}}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}
.

Note that after a suitable change of variables we may assume that
L
is given by
x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}=0
, so that
V
is defined by a cubic form of the shape

x_{1}Q_{1}(\bm{x},\bm{y})+x_{2}Q_{2}(\bm{x},\bm{y})+x_{3}Q_{3}(\bm{x},\bm{y}),

for some quadratic forms
Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3}\in k[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]
. Upon defining

W\coloneqq\{(\bm{s},\bm{y})\in\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\colon s_{1}Q_{1}(\bm{0},\bm{y})+s_{2}Q_{2}(\bm{0},\bm{y})+s_{3}Q_{3}(\bm{0},\bm{y})=0\},

we obtain a conic bundle
W\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
by projecting to the first factor. A straightforward computation with the Jacobian criterion shows that smoothness of
V
along
L
implies smoothness of
W
. Hence, on appealing once again to Proposition 1.2 of Beauville
[
3
]
, it follows that
f(\bm{s})=\det(s_1M_1+s_2M_2+s_3M_3)
is non-zero as a polynomial in
\bm{s}
, where
M_{i}
is the
3\times 3
matrix underlying
Q_{i}
. The equation
f=0
thus defines a curve
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Definition 10.2
.

Let
\bm{c}\in L^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
. We say that
\bm{c}
is
good
if

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{\bm{c}}
is smooth of dimension
0
,

(2)

H_{\bm{c}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
contains no plane contained in
V
other than
M
,

(3)

H^{\prime}_{\bm{c}}
is not an irreducible component of
\mathcal{D}
,

and we say that
\bm{c}
is
bad
otherwise.

Note that the definition depends on the choice of
L
. The significance of this definition is that hyperplane sections coming from good
\bm{c}
’s enjoy good geometric properties and bad vectors
\bm{c}
only occur rarely, as we will see in the next two results.

Lemma 10.3
.

Suppose
\bm{c}
is bad. Then
\bm{c}
lies in a subvariety of
\mathbb{P}(L^{\perp})
, all of whose irreducible components have dimension at most one.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is reduced and has at worst ordinary double points, a generic hyperplane section is smooth of dimension
0
. Indeed, as long as
H^{\prime}_{\bm{c}}
does not lie on the dual variety of
\mathcal{C}
or contain one of the singular points of
\mathcal{C}
, the intersection
H^{\prime}_{\bm{c}}\cap\mathcal{C}
will be smooth. Moreover, it will be
0
-dimensional as long as
H^{\prime}_{\bm{c}}
is not a component of
\mathcal{C}
. Each case forces
\bm{c}
to lie on a subvariety of dimension at most one.
If
H_{\bm{c}}
contains two distinct planes contained in
V
, then it must also contain the linear space they span, which has dimension at least 3. Hyperplanes in
\mathbb{P}(k^{6})
containing a fixed three dimensional linear space are parameterised by a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and since there are only finitely many planes contained in
V
, this shows that
\bm{c}
lies on a union of finitely many lines. Finally, the curve
\mathcal{D}
has at most 3 irreducible components and so there are at most 3 possibilities for
\bm{c}
such that
H_{\bm{c}}^{\prime}
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{D}
.
∎

Lemma 10.4
.

Suppose that
\bm{c}
is good. Then every fibre of the quadric surface bundle
\widetilde{V}_{\bm{c}}\to H_{\bm{c}}^{\prime}
has at worst isolated singularities.

Proof.

For
\bm{s}\in H_{\bm{c}}^{\prime}
, let
Q_{\bm{s}}
be the fibre above
\bm{s}
in
\widetilde{V}_{\bm{c}}
, which is a projective quadric surface and hence corresponds to some matrix
M_{\bm{s}}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{4\times 4}(k)
. The claim of the lemma is now equivalent to
M_{\bm{s}}
having rank at least 3. Note that if
\rk M_{\bm{s}}=1
, then
Q_{\bm{s}}
contains a double plane. However,
Q_{\bm{s}}
is also a fibre of the fibration
\widetilde{V}\to\mathbb{P}(k^{6}/L)
and is thus the residual intersection of a linear
3
-space containing
M
with the cubic hypersurface
V
. Therefore, a result of Zak
[
27
]
*Katz’s Appendix, Theorem 2 shows that the singular locus of
Q_{\bm{s}}
has dimension at most 1 and so
Q_{\bm{s}}
cannot be a double plane.
Now assume that
\rk M_{\bm{s}}=2
. This implies that
Q_{\bm{s}}
is a union of two distinct planes. However, this is impossible as at least one such plane would be distinct from
M
and contained in
H_{\bm{c}}
. This would contradict the assumption that
\bm{c}
is good.
∎

Example 10.5
.

Suppose that
L=\{x_{1}=x_{2}=x_{3}=0\}

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},0,0,0)

Fix coordinates

on

\bm{c}
is good. Moreover, as Lemma

is independent of the base field, it follows that if

, then the reduction of
\bm{c}
modulo
\varpi
is also good with respect to

and

.
Recall from part (1) of

that

Proposition 10.6

Suppose that
\bm{c}
is good and

Proof.

It follows from (

S_{\varpi}(\bm{c})=|\varpi|(|\varpi|\#\mathcal{V}_{\bm{c},\varpi}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})-\#\mathcal{V}_{\varpi}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})).

After a suitable

is given by

, since
g_{\bm{c}}(\alpha,\beta)
is just the determinant of the matrix underlying the quadratic form defining
S_{(\alpha:\beta)}
.
Let
\chi\colon\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the unique character of order 2.
It follows from Schmidt
[
42
]
*§ IV.2 that

\#S_{(\alpha:\beta)}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})=|\varpi|^{2}+|\varpi|(1+\chi(g_{\bm{c}}(\alpha,\beta)))+1,

provided
g_{\bm{c}}(\alpha,\beta)\neq 0
.
Let
0\leqslant N_{0}\leqslant 6
be the number of
(\alpha:\beta)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})
such that
g_{\bm{c}}(\alpha,\beta)=0
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\alpha:\beta)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})\\
g_{\bm{c}}(\alpha,\beta)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\hskip-14.22636pt\#S_{(\alpha:\beta)}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})

\displaystyle=(|\varpi|^{2}+|\varpi|)(|\varpi|+1-N_{0})+|\varpi|\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}\\
g_{\bm{c}}(t,1)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\hskip-5.69046pt\chi(g_{\bm{c}}(t,1))+O(1)

\displaystyle=(|\varpi|^{2}+|\varpi|)(|\varpi|+1-N_{0})+O(|\varpi|^{3/2}),

by the classical Weil bound for character sums, which is applicable since
g_{\bm{c}}(t,1)
is square-free.

As the reduction of
\bm{c}
modulo
\varpi
is good, we know from Lemma
10.4
that the matrix underlying the quadratic form defining
S_{(\alpha:\beta)}
has rank at least 3. In particular,
S_{(\alpha:\beta)}
is irreducible and by Lang-Weil we get
\#S_{(\alpha:\beta)}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})=|\varpi|^{2}+O(|\varpi|^{3/2})
. Thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\alpha:\beta)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})\\
g_{\bm{c}}(\alpha,\beta)=0\end{subarray}}\#S_{(\alpha:\beta)}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})=N_{0}|\varpi|^{2}+O(|\varpi|^{3/2}).

Finally, observe that since
\varpi\nmid B(\bm{c})
, property (iii) in Definition
10.2
implies that there are at most 3 possible
(\alpha:\beta)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})
for which
C_{(\alpha:\beta)}
is singular. If
C_{(\alpha:\beta)}
is smooth, then it contains
|\varpi|+1

\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}
-points, while if it is singular it is either a double line or a union of two lines, so that in either case contains
O(|\varpi|)

\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}
-points. Therefore,

\sum_{(\alpha:\beta)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})}\#C_{(\alpha:\beta)}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})=|\varpi|^{2}+O(|\varpi|).

Combining these estimates with the fact that
\#L_{\varpi}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})=|\varpi|^{2}+|\varpi|+1
, it follows from (
10.5
) that

\#\mathcal{V}_{\bm{c},\varpi}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})=|\varpi|^{3}+2|\varpi|^{2}+O(|\varpi|^{3/2}).

Moreover, by Deligne’s bounds (as recorded in Equation (3.12) and the paragraph thereafter of
[
10
]
), we have
\#\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})=|\varpi|^{4}+|\varpi|^{3}+O(|\varpi|^{2})
. Thus
(
10.3
) yields

\displaystyle S_{\varpi}(\bm{c})

\displaystyle=|\varpi|(|\varpi|\#\mathcal{V}_{\bm{c},\varpi}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi})-\#\mathcal{V}_{\varpi}(\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}))=|\varpi|^{4}+O(|\varpi|^{7/2}).

On dividing both sides by
|\varpi|^{7/2}
, we are led to the statement of the proposition.
∎

Motivated by the previous result, for good
\bm{c}\in L^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
we define

\mathcal{N}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{G}}\coloneqq\{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\colon\gcd(r,B(\bm{c}))=1\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{N}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}\coloneqq\{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\colon\operatorname{rad}(r)\mid B(\bm{c})\}

and observe that these sets depend implicitly on
L
. Moreover, it will be convenient to introduce the notation

\mathcal{N}_{\geqslant}(l)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}:\varpi\mid r\Rightarrow v_{\varpi}(r)\geqslant l\},

for any integer
l\geqslant 1
.

10.3.
A general upper bound

Having completed the treatment of individual exponential sums, we will now focus on our case of interest and assume that

F(\bm{x})=x_{1}^{3}+\dots+x_{6}^{3}

for the rest of this section.
Recall the definition (
2.13
) of
\mathcal{S}_{0}
.
For each set
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{0}
, let

(10.6)

f(\mathcal{T})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lvert P\rvert^{6}\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{T}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
\lvert r\rvert\leqslant\widehat{Q}\end{subarray}}\lvert I_{r}(\bm{c})\rvert\cdot\lvert r\rvert^{-6}\cdot\sum_{s\mid r}\lvert r/s\rvert^{4}\lvert S_{s}(\bm{c})\rvert.

At several points later, it will be convenient to
discard
f(\mathcal{T})
for various choices of
\mathcal{T}
. This is achieved through the following result (which is analogous to
[
49
]
*Lemma 5.2).

Lemma 10.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{0}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose
\{\lambda\bm{c}:(\lambda,\bm{c})\in K^{\times}\times\mathcal{T}\}\cap\mathcal{O}^{n}=\mathcal{T}
and
\#{\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{T}:\lvert\bm{c}\rvert\leqslant\widehat{C}\}}=O(\widehat{C}^{n-3-\delta})
for all
C\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
.
Assume
\delta\leqslant\frac{5}{2}
.
Then

f(\mathcal{T})\ll_{\varepsilon}\lvert P\rvert^{3-\delta/2+\varepsilon},

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

The result
[
22
]
*Lemma 6.1 essentially gives a version of this result with
\delta<0
,
and with
s=r
instead of
\sum_{s\mid r}
.
The same method extends to the present setting, with minor modifications that we now describe.

First, the integral estimate
[
22
]
*Eq. (6.9) still holds.
(In fact, we have seen that the stronger estimate (
8.2
) is available.)
Second, taking
n=6
, we have

S_{s}(\bm{c})\ll\lvert s\rvert^{\varepsilon}\lvert s_{1}\rvert^{4}\lvert s_{2}\rvert^{14/3}\lvert s_{3}\rvert^{5}\lvert\gcd(s_{2},\bm{c})\rvert^{1/2},

in the notation of
[
22
]
.
Therefore,

\sum_{s\mid r}\lvert r/s\rvert^{4}\lvert S_{s}(\bm{c})\rvert\ll\lvert r\rvert^{\varepsilon}\lvert r_{1}\rvert^{4}\lvert r_{2}\rvert^{14/3}\lvert r_{3}\rvert^{5}\lvert\gcd(r_{2},\bm{c})\rvert^{1/2},

since
s_{2}\mid r_{2}
and
s_{3}\mid r_{3}
.
This is of the exact same strength as the bound for the

term given in

[
22
]
*Lemma 6.1.
Since
-\frac{5}{2}+\delta\leqslant 0
, the rest of the argument of
[
22
]
goes through with

to give the desired upper bound for

.
∎

10.4.
Factorising exponential sums

Given the bias established in Proposition
10.6
, we typically expect
S^{\natural}_{r}(\bm{c})
to be of size
|r|^{1/2}
for
\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}
, at least when
r
is square-free. To keep track of the error, we will decompose
S_{r}^{\natural}(\bm{c})
into smaller pieces.

For
r\in\mathcal{O}
, let
\phi_{K}(r)=\#(\mathcal{O}/r\mathcal{O})^{\times}
be the Euler totient function. Consider the Dirichlet series

\Psi(s)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}}\frac{\phi_{K}(r)\lvert r\rvert^{-1/2}}{\lvert r\rvert^{s}}=\prod_{\varpi}\frac{1-\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-s-1/2}}{1-\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-s+1/2}}=\frac{\zeta_{K}(s-\frac{1}{2})}{\zeta_{K}(s+\frac{1}{2})}.

In the notation of (
4.1
), it follows from Proposition
10.6
that the local factor
\Phi_{\varpi}(\bm{c},s)
should resemble the local factor
\Psi_{\varpi}(s)
of
\Psi(s)
.
Let
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})
be the
r
th coefficient of the Euler product
\Phi(\bm{c},s)/\Psi(s)
; then

(10.7)

\sum_{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}}\lvert r\rvert^{-s}S_{r,0}(\bm{c})=\Phi(\bm{c},s)/\Psi(s)=\zeta_{K}(s-\tfrac{1}{2})^{-1}\cdot\zeta_{K}(s+\tfrac{1}{2})\cdot\Phi(\bm{c},s).

Since
\mu_{K}(r)
is the
r
th coefficient of
\zeta_{K}(s)^{-1}=\prod_{\varpi}(1-\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-s})
, it follows upon expanding products that

(10.8)

S_{r,0}(\bm{c})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{0},d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
d_{0}d_{1}d_{2}=r\end{subarray}}\mu_{K}(d_{0})\lvert d_{0}\rvert^{1/2}\cdot\lvert d_{1}\rvert^{-1/2}\cdot S^{\natural}_{d_{2}}(\bm{c}).

Moreover,
the Euler product factorisation
\Phi=(\Phi/\Psi)\cdot\Psi
implies

(10.9)

S^{\natural}_{r}(\bm{c})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{0},r_{1}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
r_{0}r_{1}=r\end{subarray}}S_{r_{0},0}(\bm{c})\cdot\phi_{K}(r_{1})\lvert r_{1}\rvert^{-1/2}.

We will need some basic properties of
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})
as a function of
\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}
and
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
.

Lemma 10.8
.

The quantity
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})
is multiplicative in
r
and only depends on
\bm{c}\bmod{r}
.
Also,

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}/r\Lambda^{\perp}}[S_{r,0}(\bm{c})]=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $r=1$,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Being the convolution of multiplicative functions, the multiplicativity is clear. Moreover, from (
10.8
) it follows that
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})
depends only on the list of values
\bm{c}\bmod d
for divisors
d
of
r
, which in turn implies that
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})
only depends on
\bm{c}\bmod r
. Finally, by orthogonality of characters we have

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}/r\Lambda^{\perp}}[S^{\natural}_{r}(\bm{c})]=\phi_{K}(r)|r|^{-1/2},

so that the last assertion follows from the formal identity

\sum_{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}}|r|^{-s}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}/r\Lambda^{\perp}}[S^{\natural}_{r}(\bm{c})]=\Psi(s)\sum_{r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}}|r|^{-s}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}/r\Lambda^{\perp}}[S_{r,0}(\bm{c})],

which follows from
(
10.7
).
∎

We now provide some bounds for
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})
for
\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
.

Lemma 10.9
.

Let
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
,
\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then

S_{r,0}(\bm{c})\ll_{\varepsilon}\lvert r\rvert^{1/2+\varepsilon}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{N}_{\geqslant}(3)\\
d\mid r\end{subarray}}\frac{\lvert{S^{\natural}_{d}(\bm{c})}\rvert}{\lvert d\rvert^{1/2}}.

Moreover, if
B(\bm{c})\neq 0
and
r
is a square-free element of
\mathcal{N}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{G}}
, then

S_{r,0}(\bm{c})\ll_{\varepsilon}|r|^{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Applying the triangle inequality in (
10.8
), followed by Lemma
6.1
to cube-free divisors of
r
, we obtain

\lvert S_{r,0}(\bm{c})\rvert\ll_{\varepsilon}\lvert r\rvert^{1/2+\varepsilon}\cdot\max_{d\mid r}\frac{\lvert{S^{\natural}_{d}(\bm{c})}\rvert}{\lvert d\rvert^{1/2}}\ll_{\varepsilon}\lvert r\rvert^{1/2+2\varepsilon}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{N}_{\geqslant}(3)\\
d\mid r\end{subarray}}\frac{\lvert{S^{\natural}_{d}(\bm{c})}\rvert}{\lvert d\rvert^{1/2}},

which verifies the first part.
If
\varpi\nmid B(\bm{c})
, then (
10.8
),
Proposition
10.6

and Lemma
10.8
together
imply

S_{\varpi,0}(\bm{c})=-\lvert\varpi\rvert^{1/2}+\lvert\varpi\rvert^{-1/2}+S^{\natural}_{\varpi}(\bm{c})\ll 1
.
The second assertion now follows from multiplicativity.
∎

We shall also require the following estimate for averages of
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})
.

Lemma 10.10
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
be such that
B(\bm{c})\neq 0
and
R\geqslant 0
.
Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
\lvert r\rvert=\widehat{R}\end{subarray}}\lvert S_{r,0}(\bm{c})\rvert\ll_{\varepsilon}(\widehat{R}\lvert\bm{c}\rvert)^{\varepsilon}\cdot\widehat{R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{N}_{\geqslant}(2)\\
\lvert d\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lvert{S^{\natural}_{d}(\bm{c})}\rvert}{\lvert d\rvert}.

Proof.

Any
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
can be written uniquely as
r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}
, where
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
are pairwise coprime with

r_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{G}}
square-free,

r_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}
square-free and

r_{3}
square-full.
Upon writing
S_{r,0}(\bm{c})=\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant 3}S_{r_{i},0}(\bm{c})
, and applying Lemma
10.9
to each factor, we get after decomposing into
q
-adic intervals

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
\lvert r\rvert=\widehat{R}\end{subarray}}\lvert S_{r,0}(\bm{c})\rvert\ll_{\varepsilon}\sum_{R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}=R}\widehat{R}^{\varepsilon}\widehat{R}_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}\\
\lvert r_{2}\rvert=\widehat{R}_{2}\end{subarray}}\widehat{R}_{2}^{1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{3},d\in\mathcal{N}_{\geqslant}(2)\\
\lvert r_{3}\rvert=\widehat{R}_{3},\;d\mid r_{3}\end{subarray}}\lvert r_{3}/d\rvert^{1/2}\lvert S^{\natural}_{d}(\bm{c})\rvert.

Since
B(\bm{c})\neq 0
and
|B(\bm{c})|\ll|\bm{c}|^{O(1)}
,
it follows from Lemma
2.2
that the number of
r_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\bm{c}}^{\operatorname{B}}
with
|r_{2}|=\widehat{R}_{2}
is
O_{\varepsilon}((|\bm{c}|\widehat{R}_{2})^{\varepsilon})
. In particular, we get after summing over the
r_{i}
for each fixed
d
, that the right-hand side is

\ll_{\varepsilon}\sum_{R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}=R}(\widehat{R}\lvert\bm{c}\rvert)^{\varepsilon}\widehat{R}_{1}\widehat{R}_{2}^{1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{N}_{\geqslant}(2)\\
\lvert d\rvert\leqslant\widehat{R}_{3}\end{subarray}}(\widehat{R}_{3}/\lvert d\rvert)^{1/2}\lvert d\rvert^{\varepsilon}(\widehat{R}_{3}/\lvert d\rvert)^{1/2}\lvert S^{\natural}_{d}(\bm{c})\rvert.

Lemma
10.10
follows, since
\widehat{R}_{1}\widehat{R}_{2}^{1/2}\widehat{R}_{3}^{1/2}\widehat{R}_{3}^{1/2}\leqslant\widehat{R}_{1}\widehat{R}_{2}\widehat{R}_{3}=\widehat{R}
.
∎

10.5.
Linear space extraction

By a
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear change of variables, when studying linear spaces
L\in\Upsilon
we may concentrate on
L=L_{0}
, as given by (
10.2
).
Elements of
L_{0}^{\perp}
are of the shape
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
for
\bm{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathcal{O}^{3}
.
Motivated by this, we define
\bm{c}^{*}=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
for
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}
.

Our next result will allow us to extract the main contribution from solutions on linear subspaces to our counting function.
Before stating it, we need some more notation. Given
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{O}^{6}
, set

y_{i}=x_{i}+x_{i+3}\quad\text{and}\quad z_{i}=x_{i}-x_{i+3}

for
i=1,2,3
. Moreover, we let
M\in\GL_{6}(\mathcal{O})
be such that
\bm{x}=M(\bm{y},\bm{z})
. We then define the cubic form

\widetilde{F}(\bm{y},\bm{z})=F(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left((y_{i}+z_{i})^{3}+(y_{i}-z_{i})^{3}\right)

and the density

\sigma_{L_{0},w}\coloneqq\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}w(M(\bm{0},\bm{z}))\differential\bm{z}.

Note that
M(\bm{0},\bm{z})=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},-z_{1},-z_{2},-z_{3})
, so that
\sigma_{L_{0}}
measures the density of
K_{\infty}
-points on the lattice
\Lambda_{0}
with respect to
w
.

Lemma 10.11
.

Let
\bm{c}_{0}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}
and assume that
r=r_{0}r_{1}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
. There exists a constant
C\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
|r_{1}|\geqslant\widehat{C}|P|
, then

\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}}I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c}^{*}_{0}+r_{0}\bm{c}^{*})=\sigma_{L_{0},w}\left(\frac{|r_{1}|}{|P|}\right)^{3}.

Proof.

Upon making the unimodular change of variables
\bm{x}\mapsto(\bm{y},\bm{z})
described above, we get that

I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c}_{0}^{*}+r_{0}\bm{c}^{*})=\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}w(M(\bm{y},\bm{z}))\psi\left(\theta P^{3}\widetilde{F}(\bm{y},\bm{z})+\frac{P\bm{c}\cdot\bm{y}}{r_{1}}+\frac{P\bm{c}_{0}\cdot\bm{y}}{r}\right)\differential\bm{y}\differential\bm{z}.

Applying another change of variables given by
\bm{y}^{\prime}=P\bm{y}/r_{1}
, we obtain

\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}}I_{r}(\theta,\bm{c}_{0}^{*}+r_{0}\bm{c}^{*})=\left(\frac{|r_{1}|}{|P|}\right)^{3}\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}}\widehat{g}_{\bm{z}}(\bm{c})\differential\bm{z},

where
\widehat{g}_{\bm{z}}
is the Fourier transform of the function

g_{\bm{z}}\colon K_{\infty}^{3}\to\mathbb{C},\quad\bm{b}\mapsto w(M(r_{1}\bm{b}/P,\bm{z}))\psi\left(\theta P^{3}\widetilde{F}(r_{1}\bm{b}/P,\bm{z})+\frac{\bm{c}_{0}\cdot\bm{b}}{r_{0}}\right).

In particular, using Poisson summation, in the form Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that

\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}}\hskip-2.84544ptI_{r}(\theta,\bm{c}_{0}^{*}+r_{0}\bm{c}^{*})=\frac{|r_{1}|^{3}}{|P|^{3}}\sum_{\bm{b}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}}\psi\left(\frac{\bm{c}_{0}\cdot\bm{b}}{r_{0}}\right)\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}\hskip-5.69046ptw(M(r_{1}\bm{b}/P,\bm{z}))\psi(\theta P^{3}\widetilde{F}(r_{1}\bm{b}/P,\bm{z}))\differential\bm{z}.

Suppose that
\bm{b}\neq\bm{0}
. Then, since we may assume
|r_{1}|\geqslant\widehat{C}|P|
for a sufficiently large constant
C
, we must have
w(M(r_{1}\bm{b}/P,\bm{z}))=0
. It follows that only the term
\bm{b}=\bm{0}
contributes. However, we have
\widetilde{F}(\bm{0},\bm{z})=0
identically in
\bm{z}
, so that we arrive at the statement of the lemma.
∎

10.6.
Proof of Proposition
10.1

We will now combine all the results we have obtained so far to complete the proof of Proposition
10.1
.
For
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{O}^{6}
, we set

S(\mathcal{E})=|P|^{6}\sum_{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\\
|r|\leqslant\widehat{Q}\end{subarray}}|r|^{-5/2}S^{\natural}_{r}(\bm{c})I_{r}(\bm{c}).

Then
S(\mathcal{S}_{0})=E_{2}(P)
, so that

S(\mathcal{S}_{0})=\sum_{L\in\Upsilon}\sum_{\bm{x}\in L\cap\mathcal{O}^{6}}w(\bm{x}/P)+O_{\varepsilon}(|P|^{3-1/4+\varepsilon}).

\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{0}\setminus\bigcup_{L\in\Upsilon}\Lambda^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{2}(L)=\{\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}\colon B(\bm{c})=0\text{ or }c_{1}\cdots c_{6}=0\},

Lemma 10.12
.

For any
C\geqslant 1
and

\#\{\bm{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}\cup\mathcal{E}_{2}(L)\colon|\bm{c}|\leqslant\widehat{C}\}\ll_{\varepsilon}\widehat{C}^{2+\varepsilon}.

\bm{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{2}(L)

Moreover, any
\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}
takes the shape
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathcal{O}^{3}
.
From this description it immediately follows that the contribution from
\bm{c}\in\Lambda^{\perp}
with
c_{1}\cdots c_{6}=0

.

For
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{E}_{1}
, this is already implicit in the proof of Lemma 5.1 of
[
22
]
and so we shall be brief.
Let
m_{1},\dots,m_{k}\in\mathcal{O}

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}

Suppose that

We then partition

.

[
22
]

and

]
gives

∎

Note that for any distinct

\{\bm{c}\in\Lambda_{1}^{\perp}\cap\Lambda_{2}^{\perp}\colon|\bm{c}|\leqslant\widehat{C}\}\ll\widehat{C}^{2}.

In particular, applying Lemma
10.7

twice,
with

.

Investigations on Cmc2 1 ​ – Si 2 ​ P 2 ​ X \text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X} structures and physical properties by first-principles calculations
R. Yang ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, X. Gao ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, F. Wu ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, Q. Wei ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}, M. Xue ? {}^{\textsf{\footnotesize{\mbox{?}}}}

Abstract
The new structures, Cmc2 1 ​ – Si 2 ​ P 2 ​ X \text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X} (X=S, Se, Te, and Po), are predicted, and their mechanical, electronic and optical properties are investigated with the density functional theory, by first principles calculations. The elastic constants of the four compounds are calculated by the stress-strain method. The calculations of the elastic stability criteria and phonon dispersion spectra imply that they are mechanically and dynamically stable at zero pressure. The mechanical parameters, such as shear moduli G G , bulk moduli B B , Young’s moduli E E and Poisson’s ratios v v are evaluated by the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approach. The Cmc2 1 ​ – Si 2 ​ P 2 ​ S \text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S} has the largest hardness due to the largest Young’s modulus in the four compounds, and it is a covalent crystal. The anisotropies of their mechanical properties are also analyzed. The band structures and densities of states, which are calculated by using HSE06, show that Cmc2 1 ​ – Si 2 ​ P 2 ​ X \text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X} compounds are indirect bandgap semiconductors, and the values of the band gaps decrease with increasing atomic number from S, Se, Te, to Po. In addition, the longitudinal sound velocity and transverse sound velocity for Cmc2 1 ​ – Si 2 ​ P 2 ​ X \text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X} have been investigated. The dielectric constant, electron energy loss, refractive index, reflectivity, absorption and conductivity are analyzed to gain the optical properties of Si 2 ​ P 2 ​ X \text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X} . Key words: Cmc2 1 ​ – Si 2 ​ P 2 ​ X \text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X} (X=S, Se, Te, and Po), first principles, mechanical properties, electronic properties, optical properties

Introduction

Silicon compounds, such as silicon dioxide, silicon nitride and silicon oxynitride, have aroused considerable interest in ceramic materials and electronic industry for their excellent physical and chemical properties.
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
is an engineering material that at temperatures up to
1600^{\circ}

1400^{\circ}
C

1

(
\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}
) structure, i.e., electronic structure, elasticity, stability and optical properties
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Liu et al. researched the ground state, dielectric constants, and elastic stiffness of orthorhombic-
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
with various intrinsic point defects
[
8
]
. Zhen Zhu et al. proposed IV-VI compounds as isoelectronic counterparts to layered group V semiconductors, which have significantly broadened the scope of group IV semiconductors. The electronic band structure shows that
\text{SiSP}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is an indirect bandgap semiconductor
[
9
]
. Lv et al. researched dielectric properties, vibrational and ideal strength of
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
ceramic using the first principles
[
10
]
.

In our previous works
[
11
]
, based on the similar properties of nitrogen and phosphorus, the structures of
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
and
\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
were derived, and their mechanical and electronic properties were studied under high pressure. The physical and chemical properties of group-VA (O, S, Se, Te, Po) are similar, according to the structure of
\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}\,\text{(}\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{)}

\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X}
compounds are predicted. By consulting the literature, theoretical and experimental investigations on them have not been carried out up to now.

In this paper, the structure parameters, mechanical, electronic and optical properties of
\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X}
are calculated by using the density functional theory (DFT) method based on plane waves and pseudo-potentials. The stabilities of these structures have been proved through independent elastic constants and phonon dispersion spectra.

2
Calculation methods

For
\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X}
(X=S, Se, Te, and Po), the structures are optimized and the properties are predicted using the DFT method with the generalized gradient approximations of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE), in the Cambridge Serial Total Energy Package (CASTEP) code
[
12
,
13
]
. CASTEP adopts the DFT plane-wave pseudo-potential method, which is used for first principles quantum mechanics calculations that can simulate a wide range of material properties including a structure at the atomic level, vibrational properties, electronic response properties, spectroscopic features etc.

For
\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X}
, the energy cut-off values are set to 700 eV, which determines the number of plane waves, the
k
-point samplings with
9\times 9\times 8
in the first irreducible Brillouin zone
[
14
]
. For the geometry optimization, the value of self-consistent field tolerance threshold is taken as
5.0\times 10^{-7}
eV/atom. The convergence accuracy is set as ultrafine. The convergent value of total energy difference is less than
5.0\times 10^{-6}
eV/atom. The maximum force on the atom and the maximum stress are set to be 0.01 eV/Å and
0.02
GPa. The phonon calculations are carried out within the harmonic approximation, using the linear response approach based on the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) calculation, to determine its phonon dispersion spectra
[
15
,
16
]
. In addition, the hybrid Heyd-Scuseria-Ernzerhof (HSE06) functional is used in order to get more accurate electronic properties since GGA-PBE functional underestimates the bandgap energy of most semiconductors
[
17
,
18
]
.

The elastic constants are calculated using the stress-strain methods. The number of steps for each strain is set as 6 during the strain calculations, and the maximum strain amplitude is 0.003. In order to analyze the mechanical stabilities and calculate the mechanical moduli of
\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X}
, the elastic constants and other mechanical properties are calculated with GGA-PBE. For an orthorhombic crystal system, the elastic stability criteria are as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}C_{ij}>0~(i=0,1,2,3,4,5,6),~C_{11}+C_{12}+C_{33}+2\left(C_{12}+C_{13}+C_{23}\right)>0,\\
C_{11}+C_{22}-2C_{12}>0,~C_{11}+C_{33}-2C_{13}>0,~C_{11}+C_{33}-2C_{23}>0.\end{array}

(2.1)

Based on the nine independent elastic constants, for
\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{X}
structures, the shear modulus
(G)
, bulk modulus
(B)
, Poisson’s ratio
(v)
and Young’s modulus
(E)
are obtained with the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximation
[
19
]
. For orthorhombic lattices, the moduli of Reuss shear
(G_{\rm R})
and the Voigt shear
(G_{\rm V})
are expressed as follows
[
20
]

G_{\rm R}=\frac{15}{4\left(S_{11}+S_{22}+S_{33}\right)-4\left(S_{12}+S_{13}+S_{23}\right)+3\left(S_{44}+S_{55}+S_{66}\right)},

(2.2)

G_{\rm V}=\frac{1}{15}\left(C_{11}+C_{22}+C_{33}-C_{12}-C_{13}-C_{23}\right)+\frac{1}{5}\left(C_{44}+C_{55}+C_{66}\right).

(2.3)

The Voigt bulk modulus (
B_{\rm V}
) and the Reuss bulk modulus (
B_{\rm R}
) can be given by

B_{\rm V}=\frac{1}{9}\left(C_{11}+C_{22}+C_{33}\right)+\frac{2}{9}\left(C_{12}+C_{13}+C_{23}\right),

(2.4)

B_{\rm R}=\frac{1}{\left(S_{11}+S_{22}+S_{33}\right)+2\left(S_{12}+S_{13}+S_{23}\right)}.

(2.5)

The relationship between
C_{ij}
and
S_{ij}
is given by

\left[S_{ij}\right]=[C]^{-1}_{ij}.

(2.6)

The actual polycrystalline moduli can be calculated by the Voigt and Reuss moduli, they are shown as
[
21
]

B=\frac{1}{2}\left(B_{\rm V}+B_{\rm R}\right),

(2.7)

G=\frac{1}{2}\left(G_{\rm V}+G_{\rm R}\right).

(2.8)

Young’s modulus,
E
, is the ratio of the stress and strain, which represents the stiffness property of a material. Poisson’s ratio,
v
, can offer further information about the bonding force feature. The larger Poisson’s ratio reflects a better plasticity. Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio can be expressed by
[
22
,
23
]

E=\frac{9BG}{3B+G},

(2.9)

v=\frac{3B-2G}{2(3B+G)}.

(2.10)

The anisotropy of a crystal material reflects the different arrangement of atoms and different bonding properties in different directions, which affect the physical and chemical properties of materials in different directions. The percent anisotropy (
A_{B}
and
A_{G}
for bulk and shear moduli) and the anisotropic index
A^{U}
describe the elastic anisotropy of a crystal material
[
24
]
. The equations are as follows
[
25
,
26
]

A^{U}=5\frac{G_{\rm V}}{G_{\rm R}}+\frac{B_{\rm V}}{B_{\rm R}}-6\geqslant 0,

(2.11)

A_{B}=\frac{B_{\rm V}-B_{\rm R}}{B_{\rm V}+B_{\rm R}},

(2.12)

A_{G}=\frac{G_{\rm V}-G_{\rm R}}{G_{\rm V}+G_{\rm R}}.

(2.13)

The shear anisotropic factors
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
A_{3}
are given by:

\text{ for the }(100)\text{ plane }A_{1}=\frac{4C_{44}}{C_{11}+C_{33}-2C_{13}},

(2.14)

\text{ for the }(010)\text{ plane }A_{2}=\frac{4C_{55}}{C_{22}+C_{33}-2C_{23}},

(2.15)

\text{ for the }(010)\text{ plane }A_{3}=\frac{4C_{66}}{C_{11}+C_{22}-2C_{12}}.

(2.16)

The three-dimensional (3D) graph of Young’s modulus can more effectively describe the elastic anisotropy. The Young’s modulus equation for orthorhombic crystal is expressed by

\frac{1}{E}=l_{1}^{4}s_{11}+l_{2}^{4}s_{22}+l_{3}^{4}s_{33}+l_{1}^{2}l_{2}^{2}\left(2s_{12}+s_{66}\right)+l_{2}^{2}l_{3}^{2}\left(2s_{23}+s_{44}\right)+l_{1}^{2}l_{3}^{2}\left(2s_{13}+s_{55}\right).

(2.17)

Once we have calculated the Young’s modulus
E
, the bulk modulus
B
and shear modulus
G
, the longitudinal, transverse and average sound velocities are obtained in the materials. The averaged sound velocity
v_{m}
is approximately predicted by the following equation
[
27
]
:

v_{m}=\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{v_{t}^{3}}+\frac{1}{v_{l}^{3}}\right)\right]^{-1/3},

(2.18)

where
v_{l}
and
v_{t}
are longitudinal and transverse sound velocity, which can be obtained from the bulk modulus (
B
) and shear modulus (
G
), by the use of the Navier’s equation
[
28
,
29
]
:

v_{1}=\left(\frac{B+\frac{4}{3}G}{\rho}\right)^{{1}/{2}},

(2.19)

v_{t}=\left(\frac{G}{\rho}\right)^{{1}/{2}}.

(2.20)

In the calculations of the optical properties, the norm-conserving pseudo-potentials were applied to treat the ion-electron interactions
[
30
]
. The optical properties of systems are evaluated by the complex dielectric function, which is dependent on frequency and is as follows

\varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{2}(\omega).

(2.21)

The imaginary part
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)
of the dielectric function could be calculated from the momentum matrix elements between the occupied and unoccupied wave functions, it is shown as
[
31
]

\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{Ve^{2}}{2\piup\hbar m^{2}\omega^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\sum\left|\left\langle kn|p|kn^{\prime}\right\rangle\right|^{2}f(kn)\left[1-f\left(kn^{\prime}\right)\right]\delta\left(E_{kn}-E_{kr^{\prime}}-\hbar\omega\right),

(2.22)

where
V
is the volume of unit cell,
p
expresses the momentum operator,
e
is the charge of a free electron,
f(kn)
stands for the Fermi distribution function,
|kn\rangle
represents a crystal wave function, and
\hbar\omega
is the incident photon energy.

The complex dielectric function describes the optical properties of a medium for different photon energies. The real part of dielectric function is in relation to the electric polarization characteristics of the materials. The peak value of the real part is related to the electron excitation. The real part
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)
of the dielectric function can be derived from the imaginary part
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)
by Kramers-Kroning relationship
[
32
]
:

\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)=1+\frac{2}{\piup}M\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{2}\left(\omega^{\prime}\right)\omega^{\prime}}{\omega^{\prime 2}-\omega^{2}}\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime},

(2.23)

where
M
is the principal value of the integral. CASTEP provides two main approaches to optical properties calculations; one is based on standard DFT Kohn-Sham orbitals and the other one uses time-dependent DFT (TD-DFT) theory. In this paper, the Kohn-Sham DFT is used. The DFT method uses the independent particle approximation (IPA) for the calculation of
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)
based on the ground-state quantities
[
33
]
. The optical properties are analyzed using PBE-GGA.

Furthermore, other properties of the materials can be calculated by the dielectric function, including the optical conductivity, absorption coefficient, energy-loss spectrum and optical reflectivity. They are given as follows
[
34
,
35
,
36
]

\sigma(\omega)=\frac{\mathrm{i}\omega}{4\piup}[1-\varepsilon(\omega)],

(2.24)

I(\omega)=\frac{2\omega k(\omega)}{c},

(2.25)

R(\omega)=\frac{(n-1)^{2}+k^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}+k^{2}},

(2.26)

L(\omega)=\operatorname{Im}\left[\frac{-1}{\varepsilon(\omega)}\right]=\frac{\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)}{\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)}.

(2.27)

Where
k(\omega)
means the extinction coefficient. It can be obtained from
\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)
and
\varepsilon_{2}(\omega)
, which can be expressed as follows
[
37
]

k(\omega)=\left[\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}(\omega)}-\varepsilon_{1}(\omega)}{2}\right]^{{1}/{2}}.

(2.28)

is the largest among

\text{\text{Cmc2}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1}}}\text{\hskip 1.29167pt--\hskip 1.29167pt}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{P}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{S}
has a better capability of resisting to the shape change. Poisson’s ratio shows that

Bilinear Hardy inequalities on metric measure spaces Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 26D10, 22E30, 45J05
Michael Ruzhansky, Anjali Shriwastawa, Daulti Verma

Abstract
. In this paper, we discuss the Hardy inequality with bilinear operators on general metric measure spaces. We give the characterization of weights for the bilinear Hardy inequality to hold on general metric measure spaces having polar decompositions. We also provide several examples of the results, finding conditions on the weights for integral Hardy inequalities on homogeneous Lie groups, as well as on hyperbolic spaces and more generally on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

Now, we recall a concise summary of the bilinear Hardy inequality in various contexts. The bilinear Hardy inequality has important applications in the study of partial differential equations and harmonic analysis. It allows for the analysis of bilinear operators and their properties, and it provides crucial estimates for the behaviour of solutions to certain PDEs involving bilinear terms. It is worth noting that there are various generalisations and refinements of the bilinear Hardy inequality for different function spaces, domains and dimensions. The specific form and conditions of the inequality may vary depending on the particular setting in which it is applied.

In this paper, we obtain the bilinear Hardy inequality on general metric measure spaces with polar decompositions, with some examples and applications in the setting of homogeneous Lie groups, hyperbolic spaces, and Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. Also, we give some characterizations of weights for bilinear Hardy inequality. Indeed, we are not assuming any doubling condition on the measure. The main result of our paper is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

and let
0<q<\infty

\mathbb{X}
be a metric measure space with a polar decomposition (
2.1
) at a.
Let

be measurable functions positive a.e in
\mathbb{X}
such that

and

where
i=1,2.
Let

\displaystyle H^{B}_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})(x):=H^{B}f_{1}(x)\cdot H^{B}f_{2}(x)=\int_{B(a,|x|)}f_{1}(y)dy\int_{B(a,|x|)}f_{2}(y)\,dy.

(1.4)

Then the inequality

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\bigg(H_{2}^{B}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\bigg)^{q}u(x)dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{X}}f_{1}(x)^{p_{1}}v_{1}(x)dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{X}}f_{2}(x)^{p_{2}}v_{2}(x)dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}},

(1.5)

holds for all
f_{1},f_{2}\geq 0,
if and only if the inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(H_{2}(F_{1},F_{2})(\tau)\right)^{q}\widetilde{U}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{1}^{p_{1}}(\tau)\widetilde{V}_{1}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{1/p_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{2}^{p_{2}}(\tau)\widetilde{V}_{2}(\tau)d\tau\right)^{1/p_{2}},

(1.6)

holds for all

where

are the weight functions given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}(\tau):=\int_{\sum_{\tau}}u(\tau,\omega)\,\lambda(\tau,\omega)\,d\omega,

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{i}(\tau):=\left(\int_{\sum_{\tau}}v_{i}^{1-p_{i}^{\prime}}(\tau,\omega)\,\lambda(\tau,\omega)\,d\omega\right)^{1-p_{i}},

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give a brief overview on the basics of metric measure spaces. For the applications and examples purposes, we also recall homogeneous Lie groups, hyperbolic spaces and Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. For more details on metric measure spaces as
well of several functional inequalities on metric measure spaces, we refer to papers

Let us now recall some basics of metric measure spaces.

A metric measure space with
polar decomposition
is a metric space
(\mathbb{X},d)
with a Borel measure
dx
, having the
polar decomposition
at a fixed point
a\in{\mathbb{X}}
:

\int_{\mathbb{X}}g(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}g(r,\omega)\,\lambda(r,\omega)\,d{\omega_{r}}\,dr,

(2.1)

where
\lambda\in L^{1}_{loc},

g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X}),

(r,\omega)\rightarrow a
as
r\rightarrow 0
.
Here the set
\Sigma_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:d(x,a)=r\}
is equipped with measure, which we denote by
d\omega_{r}
.
The function
\lambda
may be dependent on the complete variable
x=(r,\omega)
.

The motivation behind condition (
2.1
) in the context described above is to provide a decomposition formula for the metric measure space
(\mathbb{X},d)
that does not rely on a differential structure. In cases where a metric measure space has a differential structure, such as a Riemannian manifold, the function
\lambda(r,\omega)
can be obtained as the Jacobian of the polar change of coordinates or through the polar decomposition formula. However, the situation being dealt with in this particular context is more general, and there is no assumption of a differential structure on the metric measure space
(\mathbb{X},d)
. Therefore, it becomes useful to introduce a decomposition formula (
2.1
) that holds in this more general setting. Importantly, no doubling condition is imposed on the metric measure space.
Thus, we are considering a wider class of metric measure spaces. Moreover, we provide examples of metric measure spaces that satisfy condition (
2.1
) with different expressions for
\lambda(r,\omega)
. Some of these examples include hyperbolic spaces, which are known for their non-doubling volume growth. This demonstrates that the condition (
2.1
) can be satisfied in diverse settings, including spaces with non-trivial geometric properties. For a detailed analysis and discussion of condition (
2.1
) in the context of general metric measure spaces, we refer to
[
2
]
.

We give some examples of metric spaces having polar decomposition.

(i)

Homogeneous Lie groups
\mathbb{G}
:
For this case, we have
\lambda(r,\omega)={r}^{Q-1},
where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of the homogeneous Lie groups. Simply, one can notice that for the Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n},
we have
\lambda(r,\omega)={r}^{n-1}.
For more details on such groups, we refer to
[
10
,
11
,
30
]
.

(ii)

Hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:
The
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the unique simply connected,
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with a constant negative sectional curvature equal to
-1
. The unicity means that any two Riemannian manifolds which satisfy these properties are isometric to each other. For the case of hyperbolic spaces, we have
\lambda(r,\omega)=(\sinh{r})^{n-1}.
For more details on hyperbolics spaces, we refer to
[
4
,
12
,
16
]
.

(iii)

Cartan-Hadamard manifolds:
A simply connected complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of nonpositive curvature is called a Cartan-Hadamard manifold. Let
K_{M}\leq 0
be the sectional curvature of the Cartan-Hadamard manifold. Let
\exp_{p}:T_{p}M\rightarrow M
be the exponential map at a fixed point
p
, which is also a diffeomorphism. If
J(r,w)
is the density function on
M,
then we have the following polar decomposition:

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(y)\,dy=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}f(\exp_{p}(r,\omega))J(r,\omega)r^{n-1}\text{d}\omega\,dr,

(2.2)

with

\lambda(r,\omega)=J(r,\omega)\,r^{n-1}.

We refer to
[
4
,
16
]
for more explanation about Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q>0,

\alpha+Q<0,

\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q>0
and
\frac{(\alpha+Q)r_{2}}{q}+\frac{(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)r_{2}}{q^{\prime}}+\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q<0.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}=\sigma^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}}\sup_{r>0}\frac{r^{\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q}{p^{\prime}_{1}}}}{(\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{\frac{(\alpha+Q)r_{2}}{q}}}{|\alpha+Q|^{\frac{r_{2}}{q}}}\,\,\frac{\rho^{\frac{(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)r_{2}}{q^{\prime}}}}{(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)^{\frac{r_{2}}{q^{\prime}}}}\,\,\rho^{\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q-1}\,d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{r_{2}}},

which gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}=\sigma^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}}\sup_{r>0}\frac{r^{\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q}{p^{\prime}_{1}}}}{(\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}}}\times

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\alpha+Q|^{\frac{1}{q}}\,(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}\,\frac{r^{\frac{(\alpha+Q)}{q}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q}{r_{2}}}}{\left[\frac{(\alpha+Q)r_{2}}{q}+\frac{(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)r_{2}}{q^{\prime}}+(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)\right]^{\frac{1}{r_{2}}}}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}}\frac{1}{|\alpha+Q|^{\frac{1}{q}}\,(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}\sup_{r>0}\frac{r^{\frac{(\alpha+Q)}{q}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q}{r_{2}}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q}{p^{\prime}_{1}}}}{\left[\frac{(\alpha+Q)r_{2}}{q}+\frac{(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)r_{2}}{q^{\prime}}+(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)\right]^{\frac{1}{r_{2}}}}

which is finite if and only if the power of
r
is zero. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{(\alpha+Q)}{q}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q}{r_{2}}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q}{p^{\prime}_{1}}=0,

(5.5)

with the condition
\mathcal{D}_{1}
from Corollary
5.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha+Q}{q}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+Q}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+Q}{p_{2}^{\prime}}=0.

(5.6)

Solving (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{2}}\implies\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{2}},

(5.7)

which is our given condition of
(ii).

Therefore, we obtain the following result:

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Lie group with a quasi-norm
|\cdot|
and homogeneous dimension
Q
. Let
q\geq p_{1}
and
q<p_{2}
with
p_{1},p_{2},q>1
and
\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p_{2},}
and let
\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the inequality

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{G}}\bigg(H_{2}^{B}(f_{1},f_{2})\bigg)^{q}{|x|}^{\alpha}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{G}}{|f_{1}(x)|}^{p_{1}}{|x|}^{\beta_{1}}dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{G}}{|f_{2}(x)|}^{p_{2}}{|x|}^{\beta_{2}}dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}

(5.8)

holds for all measurable functions
f:\mathbb{G}\to{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if
\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime}_{1})+Q>0,

\alpha+Q<0,

\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q>0,

\frac{(\alpha+Q)r_{2}}{q}+\frac{(\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q)r_{2}}{q^{\prime}}+\beta_{2}(1-p^{\prime}_{2})+Q<0
and
\frac{\alpha+Q}{q}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+Q}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+Q}{p_{2}^{\prime}}=0.

Remark 5.3
.

Similarly, the conditions for the remaining cases of Theorem
4.2
can be determined.

5.2.
Hyperbolic spaces

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the hyperbolic space of dimension
n
. Let
a\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
Let us consider the weights

u(x)=(\sinh{|x|_{a}})^{\alpha},\,v_{1}(x)=(\sinh{|x|_{a}})^{\beta_{1}}\textrm{ and }v_{2}(x)=(\sinh{|x|_{a}})^{\beta_{2}}.

Then,
\mathcal{D}_{1}
in terms of polar coordinates is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{1}}\simeq\sup_{|x|_{a}>0}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh{\rho})^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh{\rho})^{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh{\rho})^{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}.

(5.9)

To evaluate the first, second and the third integral, we need

\alpha+n-1<0
,
\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n>0,
and
{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n>0,
respectively.

Let us further examine the conditions for this supremum to be finite.
For
|x|_{a}\gg 1
, we have
\sinh{\rho}\approx\exp{\rho}.
Then (
5.9
) can be stated as

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\exp{\rho})^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\exp{\rho})^{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\exp{\rho})^{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\simeq\sup_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}(\exp{|x|_{a}})^{\bigg(\frac{\alpha+n-1}{q}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}\bigg)},

which is finite if and only if

\frac{\alpha+n-1}{q}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}\leq 0.

For
|x|_{a}\ll 1
, we have
\sinh{\rho}\approx\rho.
Now, it can be represented as

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh{\rho})^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}{\rho}^{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}{\rho}^{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}(\sinh{\rho})^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho+\displaystyle\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh{\rho})^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}{\rho}^{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}{\rho}^{\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}.

We have
{\sinh{\rho}}_{|x|_{a}\leq\rho<R}\approx\rho
for some small
R,
and in that case the above supremum is

\displaystyle\approx\sup_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\bigg(|x|_{a}^{\alpha+n}+C_{R}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}{|x|_{a}}^{\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}_{1}}}{|x|_{a}}^{\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}.

Now, for
\alpha+n\geq 0
, this is

\approx\sup_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}{|x|_{a}}^{\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n}{p_{2}^{\prime}}},

which is finite if and only if

{\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{{\beta_{2}}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}\geq 0.

At the same time, for
\alpha+n<0
it is

\approx\sup_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}{|x|_{a}}^{\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+{\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}},

which is finite if and only if

\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+{\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}\geq 0.

Summarising, we obtain the following result:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},
where
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the hyperbolic space of dimension
n.
The hyperbolic distance from
x
to
a
is denoted by
|x|_{a}
. Let
1<\max(p_{1},p_{2})\leq q<\infty
and let
\alpha,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the inequality

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}_{n}}\bigg(H_{2}^{B}(f_{1},f_{2})\bigg)^{q}(\sinh{|x|_{a}})^{\alpha}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{|f_{1}(x)|}^{p_{1}}(\sinh{|x|_{a}})^{\beta_{1}}dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}{|f_{2}(x)|}^{p_{2}}(\sinh{|x|_{a}})^{\beta_{2}}dx\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}

(5.10)

holds for all measurable functions
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
if
the parameters satisfy either of the following conditions:

(A)

for
\alpha+n\geq 0,
if
\alpha+n<1
,
\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n>0
and
\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n>0
and
\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n}{p_{2}^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{2}};

(B)

for
\alpha+n<0
, if
\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n>0
and
\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n>0
and
0\leq\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+\frac{\beta_{1}(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n}{p_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta_{2}(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n}{p_{2}^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{1}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{2}}
.

Remark 5.5
.

The other cases of Theorem
4.2
can be treated in a similar way.

5.3.
Cartan-Hadamard manifolds

Let
(M,g)
be a Cartan-Hadamard manifold and let
K_{M}
be constant sectional curvature. Under this assumption, it is well known that
J(t,\omega)
is a function of
t
only. To elaborate, if
K_{M}=-b
for
b\geq 0
, then from (
2.2
), we have

J(t,\omega)=1
for
b=0
, and
J(t,\omega)=\left(\frac{\sinh\sqrt{b}t}{\sqrt{b}t}\right)^{n-1}
for
b>0
,
see e.g.
[
4
,
12
]
.

When
b=0
, let us take
u(x)=|x|_{a}^{\alpha},

v_{1}(x)=|x|_{a}^{\beta}
and
v_{2}(x)=|x|_{a}^{\gamma}
, then the inequality (
1.5
) holds for
1<\max(p_{1},p_{2})\leq q<\infty
if and only if

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|_{a}>0}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{M\backslash B(a,|x|_{a})}|y|_{a}^{\alpha}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}|y|_{a}^{\beta(1-p_{1}^{\prime})}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}|y|_{a}^{\gamma(1-p_{2}^{\prime})}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}<\infty.

(5.11)

After changing to the polar coordinates (see (
2.2
)), this is equivalent to

\sup_{|x|_{a}>0}\bigg(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}{\rho}^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}{\rho}^{\beta(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}{\rho}^{\gamma(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}},

which is finite if and only if conditions of Corollary
5.1
hold with
Q=n
(which is natural since the curvature is zero).

When
b>0
, let us take
u(x)=(\sinh\sqrt{b}{|x|_{a}})^{\alpha},

v_{1}(x)=(\sinh\sqrt{b}{|x|_{a}})^{\beta}
and
v_{2}(x)=(\sinh\sqrt{b}{|x|_{a}})^{\gamma}
. Then the inequality (
1.5
) holds for
1<\max(p_{1},p_{2})\leq q<\infty
if and only if

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|_{a}>0}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{M\backslash B(a,|x|_{a})}(\sinh\sqrt{b}{|y|_{a}})^{\alpha}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}(\sinh\sqrt{b}{|y|_{a}})^{\beta(1-p_{1}^{\prime})}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}(\sinh\sqrt{b}{|y|_{a}})^{\gamma(1-p_{2}^{\prime})}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}<\infty.

After changing to the polar coordinates, this supremum is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|_{a}>0}\bigg(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\sqrt{b}{t})^{\alpha}(\frac{\sinh\sqrt{b}t}{\sqrt{b}t})^{n-1}t^{n-1}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\sqrt{b}{t})^{\beta(1-p_{1}^{\prime})}(\frac{\sinh\sqrt{b}t}{\sqrt{b}t})^{n-1}t^{n-1}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\sqrt{b}{t})^{\gamma(1-p_{2}^{\prime})}(\frac{\sinh\sqrt{b}t}{\sqrt{b}t})^{n-1}t^{n-1}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup_{|x|_{a}>0}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\sqrt{b}{t})^{\alpha+n-1}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\sqrt{b}t)^{\beta(1-p_{1}^{\prime})+n-1}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\sqrt{b}t)^{\gamma(1-p_{2}^{\prime})+n-1}dt\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}^{\prime}}},

which has the same conditions for finiteness as the case of the hyperbolic space in Corollary
5.4
(which is also natural since it is the negative constant curvature case).

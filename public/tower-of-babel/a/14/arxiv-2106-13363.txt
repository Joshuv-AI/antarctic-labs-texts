Maria Gualdani and Nestor Guillen

Abstract
. In this manuscript we establish an L ∞ L^{\infty} estimate for the isotropic analogue of the homogeneous Landau equation. This is done for values of the interaction exponent γ \gamma in (a part of) the range of very soft potentials. The main observation in our proof is that the classical weighted Hardy inequality leads to a weighted Poincaré inequality, which in turn implies the propagation of some L p L^{p} norms of solutions. From here, the L ∞ L^{\infty} estimate follows from certain weighted Sobolev inequalities and De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory.



1.
Introduction and main result

We consider the equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f

\displaystyle=c_{d,\gamma}\textnormal{div}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}{{}^{d}}}\frac{1}{|v-v_{*}|^{-2-\gamma}}\left[f(v_{*},t)\nabla f(v,t)-f(v,t)\nabla_{*}f(v_{*},t)\right]\;dv_{*}\right),

where
f(v,t)
is a non-negative function and the constant
c_{d,\gamma}
is positive and only depends on the dimension and
\gamma
.
The constant
\gamma
belongs to the range of very soft potential,
\gamma\in[-d,-2]
,
d\geq 3
.
Our chief interest in (
1.1
) comes from its apparent similarity to the homogeneous Landau equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=c_{d,\gamma}\textnormal{div}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}{{}^{d}}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(v-v_{*})}{|v-v_{*}|^{-2-\gamma}}\left[f(v_{*},t)\nabla f(v,t)-f(v,t)\nabla_{*}f(v_{*},t)\right]\;dv_{*}\right),

where
\mathbb{P}(z)
is the matrix kernel given by the projection into the orthogonal complement to
z

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(z)=\mathbb{Id}-\frac{z\otimes z}{|z|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

The term
\mathbb{P}(z)
in (
1.2
) is an anisotropy, and it is not present in (
1.1
). Therefore one could consider (
1.1
) as an isotropic version of the Landau equation (
1.2
).

Equation (
1.1
) was first introduced by Krieger and Strain in
[
14
]
, motivated precisely by this relationship, with the hope that an understanding of (
1.1
) might lead to new insights on (
1.2
). In
[
14
]
the authors considered a modification of (
1.1
), for
\gamma=-d=-3
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=a[f]\Delta f+\alpha f^{2},\;\alpha\in[0,1],

where
a[f]:=f\ast|v|^{-1}
. Here, (
1.3
) with
\alpha=1
corresponds to (
1.1
). In
[
14
]
, the authors manage to show that, for
\alpha<3/4
, spherically symmetric and radially decreasing solutions to (
1.3
) do not form singularities in finite time, and in fact become smooth. Shortly after, in
[
11
]
, Gressman, Krieger, and Strain improved this result to the range
\alpha<74/75
, thanks to a new nonlocal inequality for
\gamma=-d
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p+1}\;dv\leq\frac{(p+1)^{2}}{p^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla f^{\frac{p}{2}}|\;dv.

The authors of the present manuscript showed later in
[
12
]
that for
\alpha=1
spherically symmetric and radially decreasing solutions to (
1.3
) regularize instantaneously and remain smooth for all times. Moreover, the approach in
[
12
]
covers (
1.3
) for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
and also provides an
L^{\infty}
result for spherically symmetric solutions of (
1.2
), conditioned on a certain spectral bound holding (for more on this, see the discussion about (
2.6
) below). Later, in
[
13
]
, we make use of the fact that
a[f]
is an
A_{1}
weight and prove an
L^{\infty}
-estimate for weak solutions of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for
\gamma\geq-2
and a conditional
L^{\infty}
-estimate for solutions of the equation for
\gamma\in[-d,-2)
.

The aim of this paper is to show a new
L^{\infty}
-estimate for solutions to (
1.1
) when
\gamma\in(\gamma_{*},-2]
,
\gamma_{*}
being specified bellow. Here is the main theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative classical solution of (
1.1
) with
\gamma\in\left[-1-\frac{d}{2},-2\right]
and initial data
f_{\textnormal{in}}
that belongs to
L_{2}^{1}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(1)

For any
p
with
1\leq p\leq\frac{d+\gamma}{-2-\gamma}
, the norm
\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is non-increasing in time.

(2)

Let
\gamma_{*}\in\left(-1-\frac{d}{2},-2\right)
be the unique solution to

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{d+\gamma+2}=\tfrac{d+\gamma}{-\gamma-2}.

If
f_{\textnormal{in}}
also belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p>\tfrac{d}{d+\gamma+2}
, any solution to (
1.1
) for
\gamma\in(\gamma_{*},-2]
is uniformly bounded for times away from zero, and

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{B_{R}\times[\tau,T]}f(v,t)\leq C(d,\gamma,f_{\textnormal{in}},R,\tau,T).

In particular, for
\gamma\in(\gamma_{*},-2]
, classical solutions of (
1.1
) remain smooth for every finite time.

To illustrate what portion of the “very soft potential” range covered in the above theorem, observe that for
d=3
we have
\gamma_{*}\approx-2.458
while for
d=4
we have
\gamma_{*}\approx-2.87
.
The value
\gamma_{*}
is uniquely defined in the range
(-d,-2)
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is relatively straightforward. For that, we set up first some notation. Let
h[f]
be the Riesz potential of
f
,

\displaystyle h[f]:=(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d+\gamma}{2}}f=C(d,d+\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(v_{*})}{|v-v_{*}|^{-\gamma}}\;dv_{*},

where
C(d,d+\gamma)
is the normalization constant arising in the operator
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d+\gamma}{2}}
, namely

\displaystyle C(d,\alpha)=\pi^{-\tfrac{d}{2}}2^{-\alpha}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-\alpha}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)},\quad 0<\alpha<d.

In particular,
h[f]\to f
as
\gamma\to-d
. Next, we shall denote by
a[f]
the convolution

\displaystyle a[f]:=c_{d,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(v_{*})}{|v-v_{*}|^{-2-\gamma}}\;dv_{*}.

With this notation, we rewrite (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=\textrm{div}\left(a[f]\nabla f-f\nabla a[f]\right),

or, in a non-divergence form,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=a[f]\Delta f-(2+\gamma)fh[f],

provided
c_{d,\gamma}
is chosen as

\displaystyle c_{d,\gamma}:=\frac{C(d,d+\gamma)}{d+\gamma},\quad\textrm{or equivalently}\quad c_{d,\gamma}:=(2+\gamma)C(d,d+2+\gamma),

so that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Delta a[f]=c_{d,\gamma}(d+\gamma)(\gamma+2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{f(v_{*})}{|v-v_{*}|^{-\gamma}}\;dv_{*}=(2+\gamma)h[f].

One can also define
a[f]
to be the operator
(2+\gamma)(-\Delta)^{-\frac{d+2+\gamma}{2}}f
. Note that our choice of
c_{d,\gamma}
(which is well defined for any
\gamma\in[-d,-2]
), does not interfere with the structure of
(
1.1
)
: one can eliminate such constant with a simple time rescaling.

For
\gamma=-2
, equation (
1.1
) reduces substantially: for sufficiently smooth functions
f
, it simplifies to a heat equation,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=c_{d,-2}\textnormal{div}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}{{}^{d}}}f(v_{*},t)\nabla f(v,t)\;dv_{*}\right)=c_{d,-2}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}\Delta f,

thanks to the conservation of the
L^{1}
norm. This is different from the classical Landau equation (
1.2
) when
\gamma=-2
, which has an additional reaction term arising from the derivatives of
\mathbb{P}(v)
.

Now we explain the basic idea of the proof. For a classical solution of (
1.1
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p}\;dv=-\frac{4(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla f^{p/2}|^{2}\;dv+(p-1)(2+\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]f^{p}\;dv.

To bound the right hand side, we estimate the second integral by the first one. This is the key point for Theorem
1.1
, and it is achieved through the weighted Hardy inequality
[
9
]

\displaystyle(d+\gamma)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{\gamma}\phi^{2}\;dv\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{2+\gamma}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv,\quad\gamma>-d.

This inequality implies, via convolution, the following weighted Poincare’s inequality

(1.5)

\displaystyle(d+\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]\phi^{2}\;dv\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv.

For
\gamma
belonging to the interval
(\gamma_{*},-2]
, thanks to (
1.5
), we can show that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p}\;dv\leq 0,

for some
p>\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}
. This is all we need to conclude that
f
belongs to
L^{\infty}
for positive times.

The proof of this last statement exists in the literature. See for example Theorem 3.8 in
[
16
]
or Theorem 2.9 in
[
13
]
. For completeness, we report the proof here, following the method in
[
13
]
. We will show that a bound for
f
in
L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for some
p>\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}
yields an
\varepsilon
-Poincare’s inequality of the form

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]\;\phi^{2}\;dv\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},R)\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{\eta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\phi^{2}\;dv.

This last inequality, combined with a Moser’s iteration, yields the desired
L^{\infty}
-bound for
f
.

1.1.
Short background on the homogeneous Landau equation

The main result in this note states that for the isotropic equation (
1.1
) there is a nontrivial portion of the “very soft potentials range”,
\gamma\in[-d,-2]
, for which one can rule out the formation of singularities in finite time. This is of interest given the similarity of (
1.1
) with the homogeneous Landau equation (
1.2
), and the unresolved question of
L^{\infty}
estimates for the latter equation when
\gamma\in[-d,-2]
. We briefly discuss some of the literature on the Landau equation in order to illustrate what is presently understood about (
1.2
) and what makes the equation in the very soft potentials range much harder to analyze.

The problem of
L^{\infty}
-estimates for solutions to the Landau equation (
1.2
) stands as a difficult unresolved question when
\gamma<-2
. Indeed, as
\gamma
decreases, the kernel in
h[f]
becomes more and more singular, and one requires more integrability for
f
to control
h[f]
.
On the contrary, in the range
\gamma\geq-2
, the regularity theory is well developed. For the hard potentials case (
\gamma\geq 0
) the works of Desvillettes and Villani
[
5
,
6
]
do not only answer the question of regularity of solutions, but also cover existence and long time convergence to equilibrium. For soft potentials (
\gamma\in(-2,0)
) and
d=3
, Alexandre, Liao, and Lin
[
1
]
obtain the propagation of
L^{2}
estimates for solutions (potentially growing with time), from where higher
L^{\infty}
estimates and higher regularity can be obtained. For
\gamma=-2
we refer to
[
17
]
. Silvestre
[
16
]
obtains a priori
L^{\infty}
estimates for solutions when
\gamma\in(-2,0)
that are fully determined from the mass, energy, and entropy of the solution (and accordingly are not growing with time). The results in
[
16
]
also say that for
\gamma\in[-d,-2)
the
L^{\infty}
norm is controlled once the
L^{p}
-norm of
f
, with
p>\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}
, is bounded uniformly in time. Similar results, proven with different method, appear in
[
13
]
. The estimates in
[
13
]
are proved for weak solutions and use the divergence structure of the equation, but yield an
L^{\infty}
estimate for
f(v,t)
that deteriorates as
v
grows. In contrast, the work
[
16
]
uses non-divergence techniques and yields bounds that are global in space.

For very soft potentials there have been several recent works about the nature of potential singularities, which narrow down the ways in which blow up may occur. In
[
10
]
, Golse, Gualdani, Imbert and Vasseur proved that the set of singular times for weak solutions to (
1.2
) (with
\gamma=-3
) has Hausdorff dimension at most 1/2. Very recently, Desvillettes, He, and Jiang provided in
[
4
]
new insides about the behavior of solutions to (
1.2
) (with
\gamma=-3
) in
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm near the blow-up time. Most importantly, they show that even if there is blow-up, after that solutions become smooth again and remain that way for all later times. In
[
2
]
, Bedrossian, Gualdani and Snelson rule out type I self similar blow-up for solutions to (
1.2
) for any
\gamma<-2
.

There is an important connection between
L^{\infty}
bounds and uniqueness. Fournier and Guérin proved a uniqueness result for bounded weak solutions in
[
7
]
, this being for
\gamma\in(-d,-2)
. In fact, the work
[
7
]
guarantees uniqueness of solutions with
f\in L^{1}L^{\infty}
and in particular to bounded solutions. The work
[
7
]
was followed by Fournier’s work in
[
8
]
with a corresponding uniqueness result for
\gamma=-d
. Later in
[
3
]
, Chern and Gualdani proved a uniqueness result for sufficiently integrable solutions for the Landau equation with Coulomb interactions.

1.2.
Outline

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall Hardy’s inequality and make the main new observation of the paper, the inequality in Lemma
2.1
. The new inequality is immediately put to use in Section
3
to prove the first part of Theorem
1.1
. Then, Sections
5
and
6
deal with weighted normed inequalities and a Moser iteration leading to the
L^{\infty}

.

2.
Hardy’s inequality

The main ingredient in this note is the classical Hardy inequality,

(2.1)

\displaystyle(d+\gamma)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{\gamma}\phi^{2}\;dv\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|^{2+\gamma}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv,\quad\gamma>-d.

Let us review one elementary way of proving (
2.1
), a deeper and broader discussion on Hardy’s inequality can be found in the book by Ghoussoub and Moradifam
[
9
]
. First notice that

\displaystyle-\Delta|v|^{2+\gamma}=(d+\gamma)(-\gamma-2)|v|^{\gamma}.

Multiply both sides of this equation by
\phi
. Integration by parts and Cauchy-Schwarz yield

\displaystyle(d+\gamma)(-\gamma-2)\int\phi^{2}|v|^{\gamma}\;dv

\displaystyle=2\int\nabla|v|^{2+\gamma}\phi\nabla\phi\;dv

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int\alpha^{2}(v)|\nabla|v|^{2+\gamma}|^{2}\phi^{2}\;dv\right)^{1/2}\left(\int\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{\alpha^{2}(v)}\;dv\right)^{1/2}.

We pick now the best weight
\alpha(v)
such that

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(v)|\nabla|v|^{2+\gamma}|^{2}\leq|v|^{\gamma}.

or equivalently

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}(v)=\frac{1}{|v|^{2+\gamma}(2+\gamma)^{2}}.

With this choice of
\alpha^{2}(v)
, we obtain (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma\in(-d,-2)
. Fix a non-negative
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
a[f]
and
h[f]
be as in Section
1
, then the following inequality holds for all
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(and limits of such functions)

(2.2)

\displaystyle(d+\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]\phi^{2}\;dv\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv.

Proof.

Fix
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By a change of variables, we see that (
2.1
) is equivalent to the inequalities (with
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
)

(2.3)

\displaystyle(d+\gamma)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v-w|^{\gamma}\phi^{2}\;dv\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v-w|^{2+\gamma}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv.

Let us multiply (
2.3
) by
f(w)\geq 0
and integrate the resulting expression in
w
, we obtain

(2.4)

\displaystyle(d+\gamma)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f*|v|^{\gamma})\phi^{2}\;dv\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f*|v|^{2+\gamma})|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv.

Substituting in (
2.4
) the expression for
\Delta a[f]
and making use of (
1.4
), the lemma is proved.

∎

Although elementary, Lemma
2.1
is key, as it leads to the propagation of
L^{p}
bounds for solutions to (
1.1
), proven in the next section. The range of
p
’s is limited by the constants appearing in (
2.2
), and this is the sole limitation on the range of
\gamma
’s covered by Theorem
1.1
. This motivates the following (admittedly open ended) eigenvalue problem.

Problem
.

Fix
d
and
\gamma\in[-d,-2]
. Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-negative, and let

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\textnormal{iso}}(f):=\inf\limits_{\phi}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]\phi^{2}\;dv}.

Determine under what circumstances can we say that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\textnormal{iso}}(f)>\frac{d+\gamma}{4}.

If
f
is just a generic function in
L^{1}
, then one cannot do better than inequality (
2.2
). To see this, take a sequence of functions
f_{n}
which are converging as
n\to\infty
to a Dirac delta at
0
. For this sequence, (
2.2
) converges to (
2.1
), which is known to be sharp
[
9
, Section 4.3]
.

The corresponding problem for the Landau equation would be,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\textnormal{Landau}}(f):=\inf\limits_{\phi}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(A[f]\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)\;dv}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]\phi^{2}\;dv},

where
A[f]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}{{}^{d}}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(v-v_{*})}{|v-v_{*}|^{-2-\gamma}}f(v_{*})\;dv_{*}
.
The significance of this eigenvalue problem is well known in the Landau and Boltzmann literature. We do not know whether an elementary argument as in Lemma
2.1
yields a similar bound for (
2.6
). If one argues by direct analogy with Lemma
2.1
, one would have to contend with the projection term
\mathbb{P}(v)
appearing in
A[f]
, and it is not immediately clear how this can be done.

The theory of weighted normed inequalities can yield certain estimates for
\Lambda_{\textnormal{iso}}
, or
\Lambda_{\textnormal{Landau}}
. For instance, the value in (
2.5
) is directly related to the quantity

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{B}|B|^{\frac{2}{d}}\frac{\int_{B}h[f]\;dv}{\int_{B}a[f]\;dv},

which happens to be bounded from above for all non-negative
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by a universal constant (see
[
13
]
for further discussion). Finally, it is worth mentioning that for any spherically symmetric and radially decreasing
f
solving (
1.2
) (
\gamma=-d
), the
L^{\infty}
norm of
f
cannot blow up at a finite time
T
if, for this
f
, the quantity (
2.7
) remains bounded by
1/96
(this is likely a non-sharp estimate). This result was shown in
[
12
]
.

3.
Propagation of
L^{p}
bounds

In this section we shall make use of Lemma
2.1
to show that various
L^{p}
norms propagate forward in time, at least for some range of
\gamma
’s.

Lemma 3.1
.

(Propagation of integrability.)
Let
f
be a nonnegative solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
f(v,0)=f_{\textnormal{in}}
. For every
p
such that

\displaystyle 1\leq p\leq\frac{d+\gamma}{-2-\gamma}

the norm
\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}
is non-increasing in
t
. In particular, for every
t\in[0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{p}}.

Proof.

Multiply (
1.1
) by
f^{p-1}
for some
p\geq 1
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}{{}^{d}}
. We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p}\;dv=-\frac{4(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla f^{p/2}|^{2}\;dv+(p-1)(-2-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]f^{p}\;dv.

To estimate the last term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]f^{p}\;dv
we use Lemma
2.1
with
\phi=f^{p/2}
. One gets

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq-4(p-1)\left[\frac{1}{p}-\frac{(-2-\gamma)}{(d+\gamma)}\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla f^{p/2}|^{2}\;dv.

It follows that
\|f(t)\|_{p}^{p}
is non-increasing whenever the expression in the brackets is non-positive, which is the case given the assumption on
p
. This concludes the proof of the lemma, and of the first part of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

For there to be any
p
such that
1\leq p\leq(d+\gamma)/(-2-\gamma)
it must be that

\displaystyle\gamma\geq-1-\frac{d}{2}.

It follows that Lemma
3.1
is of no use for values of
\gamma
close to
-d
.

4.
Controlling the second moment

Solutions to (
1.1
) conserve mass and first moment, but not second moment. In the next lemma we show that second moments grow linearly in time, provided
a[f]
is uniformly bounded.

Lemma 4.1
.

The second moment of
f
, solution to (
1.1
), evolves according to the formula

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(v,t)|v|^{2}\;dv=2(d+2+\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(v,t)a[f](v,t)\;dv.

In particular, for all
t\in[0,T]
and
p>\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(T,v)|v|^{2}\;dv\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\textnormal{in}}(v)|v|^{2}\;dv+TC_{d,\gamma,p}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{p})}^{1-\theta}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}^{1+\theta},

where
\theta:=\frac{|2+\gamma|p^{\prime}}{d}
.

Proof.

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,v)|v|^{2}\;dv=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(v,a[f]\nabla f-f\nabla a[f])\;dv.

Using the integral form for
a[f]
we rewrite the expression on the right, leading to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,v)|v|^{2}\;dv=

\displaystyle-c_{d,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{v}\cdot\frac{f(w)\nabla f(v)-f(v)\nabla f(w)}{|v-w|^{-2-\gamma}}\;dwdv

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{d,\gamma}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{v-w}\cdot\frac{f(w)\nabla f(v)-f(v)\nabla f(w)}{|v-w|^{-2-\gamma}}\;dwdv.

Integration by parts in both
v
and
w
yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,v)|v|^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle=2(d+2+\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fa[f]\;dv,

since

\textrm{div}\left(\frac{z}{|z|^{-2-\gamma}}\right)=\frac{d+2+\gamma}{|z|^{-2-\gamma}}.

This proves the first part of the lemma. For the second part, it is clear that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,v)|v|^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq(d+2+\gamma)\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}}\|a(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Then, integrating the resulting inequality in time, the estimate follows thanks to an elementary interpolation argument (see Remark
4.2
)

\displaystyle\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C_{d,\gamma,p}\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{\theta}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}^{1-\theta}.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

The following estimate is well known and we recall it here for completeness: let
p>\frac{d}{d+\gamma+2}
, for every
s>0
we have that

\displaystyle a[f]

\displaystyle=c_{d,\gamma}\int_{B_{s}(v)}f(w)|v-w|^{2+\gamma}\;dw+c_{d,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{s}(v)}f(w)|v-w|^{2+\gamma}\;dw

\displaystyle\leq c_{d,\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}}\left(\int_{B_{s}}|w|^{(2+\gamma)p^{\prime}}\;dw\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}+c_{d,\gamma}s^{2+\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

Optimizing the right hand side with respect to
s
, the following estimate follows

\displaystyle\|a[f]\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{d,\gamma,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{\theta}\|f\|_{L^{1}}^{1-\theta},\;\textnormal{ where }\theta=\frac{|2+\gamma|p^{\prime}}{d}.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
p>\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}
. For all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in[0,T]
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle a[f](v,t)\geq\ell\langle v\rangle^{2+\gamma},

where

\displaystyle\ell:=c_{d,\gamma}\frac{\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1-\gamma}}{\left(c_{1}+c_{2}T\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{1-\theta}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}^{1+\theta}\right)^{-2-\gamma}}.

Proof.

For any
r>0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}f(v,t)\;dv\leq r^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}f(v,t)|v|^{2}\;dv\leq r^{-2}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{2}}.

From here, taking
r=\frac{2\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{2}}}{\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}}}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}f(v,t)\;dv\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}.

Then, since
|v-w|\leq|v|+r
whenever
w\in B_{r}
,

\displaystyle a(v,t)

\displaystyle\geq c_{d,\gamma}\int_{B_{r}}f(w,t)(|v|+r)^{2+\gamma}\;dw\geq\tfrac{1}{2}c_{d,\gamma}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}(|v|+r)^{2+\gamma}.

In particular

\displaystyle a(v,t)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}c_{d,\gamma}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}r^{2+\gamma}\langle v\rangle^{2+\gamma}

\displaystyle\geq c_{d,\gamma}\frac{\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}^{-1-\gamma}}{\left(c_{1}+c_{2}T\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{1-\theta}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{1}}^{1+\theta}\right)^{-2-\gamma}}\langle v\rangle^{2+\gamma},

using Lemma
4.1
to bound
r^{2+\gamma}
from below.
∎

5.
Some weighted inequalities

In this section we invoke a few results from the theory of integral inequalities with
A_{p}
weights. The result will be integral inequalities of the form

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{1}|\phi|^{r}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for various choices of the exponent
r
, weights
\omega_{i}
, and constant
C
which are pertinent to obtaining estimates a la De Giorgi-Nash-Moser for solutions of (
1.1
). For a more complete discussion we refer the reader to
[
13
, Section 3.2]
.

A central object in these inequalities is the following product of averages of the weights, taken over an arbitrary cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, (here, “
\fint
” denotes average over the set of integration)

\displaystyle\sigma_{r,s}(Q,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=|Q|^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{r}}\left(\fint_{Q}\omega_{1}^{s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{rs}}\left(\fint_{Q}\omega_{2}^{-s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}.

The significance of
\sigma_{r,s}(Q,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is captured by the following theorem (see
[
15
, Theorem 1]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
r\geq 2
, and let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be two weights. Define, for some
C(d,s,r)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{r,s}(Q,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=C(d,s,r)\sup\limits_{Q^{\prime}\subset 8Q}\sigma_{r,s}(Q^{\prime},\omega_{1},\omega_{2}).

Then, for any
\phi
supported in
Q
or any
\phi
such that
\fint_{Q}\phi\;dv=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Q}\omega_{1}|\phi|^{r}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{r,s}(Q,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\left(\int_{Q}\omega_{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The next two propositions give estimates on
\sigma_{r,s}(Q,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
for two combination of weights, namely
\omega_{1}=a[f]^{m},\;\omega_{2}=a[f]
and
\omega_{1}=h[f],\;\omega_{2}=a[f]
.

There are two exponents that will be appearing repeatedly in what follows:

(5.1)

\displaystyle m:=\frac{d}{d-2},\;\;q:=2\left(1+\frac{2}{d}\right).

Proposition 5.2
.

There exists
s>1
and
C>0
depending only on
d
and
\gamma
such that for non-negative
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{2m,s}(Q,a[f]^{m},a[f])\leq C.

Proof.

For
\gamma>-d
, Lemma 3.5 from
[
13
, Section 3]
says there is some
s>1
such that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}a[f]^{ms}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{ms}}\leq C\fint_{Q}a[f]\;dv,\quad C=C(d,\gamma,ms).

As it was also noted in
[
13
, Section 3]
, there is a universal constant such that

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}a[f]\;dv\leq C\inf\limits_{Q}a[f],

which means also that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{Q}a[f]^{-1}\leq C\left(\fint_{Q}a[f]\;dv\right)^{-1}.

Putting these two observations together it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}a[f]^{ms}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2ms}}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}(a[f])^{-s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}\leq C.

Lastly,
m
solves
\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2m}=0
(it is its determining property), and the proposition is proved.
∎

The next one if the key proposition for the proof of (
1.6
):

Proposition 5.3
.

There is
s>\tfrac{d}{2}
such that given a cube
Q\subset B_{R}
with
|Q|\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle\left[\sigma_{2,s}(Q,h[f],a[f])\right]^{2}\leq\theta(|Q|^{\tfrac{1}{d}}):=C(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{p(\gamma,d,s)})},\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{1})})\langle R\rangle^{|2+\gamma|}|Q|^{\tfrac{\eta}{d}}.

Here
\eta:=2-\tfrac{d}{s}>0
and
p(\gamma,d,s):=\tfrac{ds}{d+s(d+\gamma)}
.

In particular, one can chose
s
infinitesimally close to
\tfrac{d}{2}
, resulting in
p(\gamma,d,s)
to be greater, but as close as one wishes to
\tfrac{d}{d+\gamma+2}
.

Proof.

Classical fractional integral estimates say that

\displaystyle\|h[f]\|_{L^{\frac{dp}{d-p(d+\gamma)}}}\leq C_{d,d+\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}},\;\textnormal{ provided }p<\frac{d}{d+\gamma}.

We want to choose
p
so that
s=\frac{dp}{d-p(d+\gamma)}
, which results in
p(\gamma,d,s)=\frac{ds}{d+s(d+\gamma)}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|Q^{\prime}|^{\frac{2}{d}}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}h[f]^{s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq C|Q^{\prime}|^{\frac{2}{d}-\frac{1}{s}}\|f\|_{L^{p(\gamma,d,s)}}.

We can take
s
larger but arbitrarily close to
\tfrac{d}{2}
(to have
\frac{2}{d}-\tfrac{1}{s}
positive) which results in
p(\gamma,d,s)
be strictly greater, but arbitrarily close to,
d/(d+2+\gamma)
. Hence,

\displaystyle|Q|^{\frac{2}{d}}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}h[f]^{s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq C|Q^{\prime}|^{\frac{\eta}{d}}\|f\|_{L^{p(\gamma,d,s)}}.

Thanks to the bound from below for
a[f]
from Corollary
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}a[f]^{-s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq C\langle R\rangle^{|2+\gamma|},\;\forall\;Q^{\prime}\subset 8Q.

We work towards estimating the other factor.

It follows that

\displaystyle|Q^{\prime}|^{\frac{2}{d}}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}h[f]^{s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\fint_{Q^{\prime}}a[f]^{-s}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq C\langle R\rangle^{|2+\gamma|}|Q^{\prime}|^{\frac{\eta}{d}}\|f\|_{L^{p(\gamma,s)}}.

This estimate is for all cubes
Q^{\prime}
such that
Q^{\prime}\subset 8Q
, which proves the proposition.

∎

An immediate consequence of Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
is the following inequality.

Corollary 5.4
.

There is a universal constant
C
such that for all
\phi
we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\;a^{m}[f]\phi^{2m}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\;|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv.

Corollary
5.4
implies, via an elementary interpolation argument, a space-time integral inequality for functions
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 5.5
.

There is a universal constant
C
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\;a[f]\phi^{q}\;dvdt\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\;|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dvdt+\sup\limits_{(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\;dv\right)^{q/2}.

Proof.

We follow the standard proof of this space-time inequality (see proof of Theorem 2.12 and 2.13 in
[
13
]
). First, we estimate the integral of
|\phi|^{q}
with weight
a[f]
by interpolation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\phi|^{q}\;dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\phi^{q\theta+q(1-\theta)}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]^{\frac{2m}{(1-\theta)q}}|\phi|^{2m}\;dv\right)^{\frac{(1-\theta)q}{2m}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\;dv\right)^{\frac{q\theta}{2}}.

The exponent
\theta\in(0,1)
is determined from the relation
\tfrac{1}{q}=\tfrac{1-\theta}{2m}+\tfrac{\theta}{2}
. Simplifying, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\phi|^{q}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]^{m}|\phi|^{2m}\;dv\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\;dv\right)^{\frac{m-1}{m}}.

Now, by Corollary
5.4

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\phi|^{q}\;dv\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\;dv\right)^{\frac{m-1}{m}}.

Integrating this over time we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\phi|^{q}\;dvdt\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dvdt\right)\left(\sup\limits_{(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\;dv\right)^{\frac{m-1}{m}}.

From this last inequality it follows trivially that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\phi|^{q}\;dvdt\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dvdt+\sup\limits_{(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi^{2}\;dv\right)^{2-\frac{1}{m}}.

Noting that
2-\frac{1}{m}=2-\frac{d-2}{d}=1+\frac{2}{d}=\frac{q}{2}
, the corollary is proved.
∎

The other important use of Theorem
5.1
is in proving a
\varepsilon
-Poincaré inequality, which also relies crucially on Proposition
5.3
and the
L^{p}
bound on
f
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
R>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. For any
\phi
supported in
B_{R}(0)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h[f]\;\phi^{2}\;dv\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},R)\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{\eta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\phi^{2}\;dv.

Here,
\varepsilon_{0}:=\theta(1)/C(d,\gamma)
,
\theta(r)
and
\eta
are as in Proposition
5.3
, and

\displaystyle C(f_{\textnormal{in}},R)=(4C)^{\frac{2}{\eta}}\langle R\rangle^{\frac{2|2+\gamma|}{\eta}}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{p(\gamma,d,s)}}^{\frac{2}{\eta}}.

Proof.

Let
Q
be any cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|Q|\leq 1
. Since
\phi=\phi-(\phi)_{Q}+(\phi)_{Q}
it is elementary that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{Q}h[f]\phi^{2}\;dv\leq 4\int_{Q}h[f](\phi-(\phi)_{Q})^{2}\;dv+4(\phi)_{Q}^{2}\int_{Q}h[f]\;dv,

where
(\phi)_{Q}
denotes the average over
Q
,

\displaystyle(\phi)_{Q}=\fint_{Q}\phi\;dv.

Applying Hölder’s inequality to
\int_{Q}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}(a^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|)\;dv
, it follows that

\displaystyle(\phi)_{Q}^{2}\leq\left(\fint_{Q}|\phi|\;dv\right)^{2}\leq\left(\fint_{Q}a\phi^{2}\;dv\right)\left(\fint_{Q}a^{-1}\;dv\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle 4(\phi)_{Q}^{2}\int_{Q}h\;dv

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\fint_{Q}h\;dv\right)\left(\fint_{Q}a^{-1}\;dv\right)\left(\int_{Q}a\phi^{2}\;dv\right)\leq 4C|Q|^{-\frac{2}{d}}\int_{Q}a\phi^{2}\;dv.

Now, we bound the first term on the right of (
5.3
) by means of Theorem
5.1
, so

\displaystyle\int_{Q}h\phi^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathcal{C}_{2,2}(Q,h,a)\int_{Q}a|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv+4C|Q|^{-\frac{2}{d}}\int_{Q}a\phi^{2}\;dv.

Then, by Proposition
5.3
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{Q}h\phi^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq 4C\theta(r)\int_{Q}a|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv+4C|Q|^{-\frac{2}{d}}\int_{Q}a\phi^{2}\;dv,\;r:=|Q|^{\tfrac{1}{d}}.

where
C=C(d,\gamma)
and
\theta
is as in Proposition
5.3
. Adding up these inequalities for each
Q
of the form
r[0,1]^{d}+rz
,
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h\phi^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq 4\theta(r)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv+4Cr^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\phi^{2}\;dv.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\eta(1)/(4C))
, then there is some
r\in(0,1)
such that
4\theta(r)=\varepsilon
, namely

\displaystyle\varepsilon=4C\langle R\rangle^{|2+\gamma|}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{p(\gamma,d,s)}}r^{\eta}.

Indeed, this
r=r(\varepsilon)
is such that

\displaystyle r^{-2}=(4C)^{\frac{2}{\eta}}\langle R\rangle^{\frac{2|2+\gamma|}{\eta}}\|f_{\textnormal{in}}\|_{L^{p(\gamma,d,s)}}^{\frac{2}{\eta}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{\eta}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h\phi^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|\nabla\phi|^{2}\;dv+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},R)\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{\eta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\phi^{2}\;dv,

and the corollary is proved.

∎

6.
Moser’s iteration

A
\varepsilon
-Poincare, inequality like the one obtained in Corollary
5.6
, when valid, yields an
L^{\infty}
estimate for the respective solution
f
of (
1.1
). This is the case regardless of the value of
\gamma\in[-d,-2]
, as was observed for the Landau equation in
[
13
]
.

In Proposition
5.3
and Corollary
5.6
we have proved that the
\varepsilon
-Poincaré inequality holds if
f\in L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for
p>\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}
. In view of Lemma
3.1
, solutions to (
1.1
) belong to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p>\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}
if the initial data belong to the same
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
space and, most importantly, if
\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}\leq\frac{d+\gamma}{-\gamma-2}
. This last inequality holds true for
\gamma\in(\gamma_{*},-2]
, with
\gamma_{*}
the unique solution to

\frac{d}{d+2+\gamma}=\frac{d+\gamma}{-\gamma-2}.

Observe that for
\gamma\in(-d,-2)
the function
\gamma\mapsto d/(d+\gamma+2)
is strictly decreasing, while
\gamma\mapsto(d+\gamma)/(-\gamma-2)
is strictly increasing. At
\gamma=-d
they are equal to
\frac{d}{2}
and
0
, respectively and at
\gamma=-2
they are equal to
1
and
+\infty
, respectively. It follows there is exactly one
\gamma_{*}\in(-d,-2)
where they agree. Alternatively, after solving the respective quadratic equation one can see that
\gamma_{*}
is given by the formula

\displaystyle\gamma_{*}=-1-\tfrac{3}{2}d+\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{5d^{2}-4d+4}.

So, if
\gamma\in(\gamma_{*},-2]
, it should be no surprise that the
L^{\infty}
estimate for
f
follows. As mentioned earlier, there are several ways how to show that. We will follow the Moser’s approach introduced in
[
13
]
, with consists on estimating the norms

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dvdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

for ever increasing powers of
p
and varying cut off functions
\rho
. Although the arguments are very similar to those in
[
13
]
, we go in detail over their derivation here. This will complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We start by deriving an energy identity, then we will use the
\varepsilon
-Poincaré inequality to control the most problematic term (the integral involving a
h[f]f^{p}
term), and arrive at an energy inequality. This energy inequality, together with the space-time weighted inequality (
5.2
), will be repeatedly used to bound the
L^{p}
norms as
p\to\infty
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
p>1
and let
\rho\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle=-\frac{4(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(c_{1}(p)|\nabla\rho|^{2}-\Delta\rho^{2})a[f]f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta a[f])\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv-c_{2}(p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]f^{p/2}(\nabla(\rho f^{p/2}),\nabla\rho)\;dv,

where
c_{1}(p)=4(p-\frac{(p-1)}{p})
,
c_{2}(p)=4(p-2\frac{p-1}{p})
.

Proof.

For simplicity we shall write
a
instead of
a[f]
. From the equation and integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p-1}\partial_{t}f\;dv=-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla(\rho^{2}f^{p-1}),a\nabla f-f\nabla a)\;dv.

The integral on the right is equal to the sum of four terms, which we denote
(\textnormal{I}),(\textnormal{II}),(\textnormal{III})
, and
(\textnormal{IV})
, and which we now analyze one by one.

First, note that
(\nabla f^{p-1},\nabla f)=(p-1)f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}=(p-1)(4/p^{2})|\nabla f^{\frac{p}{2}}|
, therefore

\displaystyle(\textnormal{I})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}(\nabla f^{p-1},a\nabla f)\;dv=\frac{4(p-1)}{p^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}a|\nabla f^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}\;dv.

Next, we rewrite each of the other three terms using integration by parts, as follows

\displaystyle(\textnormal{II})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p-1}(\nabla\rho^{2},a\nabla f)\;dv

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla\rho^{2},a\nabla f^{p})\;dv

\displaystyle=-\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p}(\nabla\rho^{2},\nabla a)+f^{p}a\Delta\rho^{2}\;dv,

\displaystyle(\textnormal{III})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}(\nabla f^{p-1},-f\nabla a)\;dv

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}(\nabla f^{p},-\nabla a)\;dv

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\Delta a+f^{p}(\nabla\rho^{2},\nabla a)\;dv,

\displaystyle(\textnormal{IV})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p-1}(\nabla\rho^{2},-f\nabla a)\;dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{p}(\nabla\rho^{2},\nabla a)\;dv

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p/2}(\nabla f^{p/2},\nabla\rho^{2})\;dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p}\Delta\rho^{2}\;dv.

Adding these identities up, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle=-\frac{4(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}a|\nabla f^{p/2}|^{2}\;dv-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\Delta\rho^{2})af^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta a)\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv-2p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p/2}(\nabla f^{p/2},\nabla\rho^{2})\;dv.

We use the elementary identity
\rho\nabla f^{p/2}=\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})-f^{p/2}\nabla\rho
and rewrite further,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}a|\nabla f^{p/2}|^{2}\;dv=

\displaystyle\int a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dv+\int f^{p}a|\nabla\rho|^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;-2\int f^{p/2}a(\nabla(\rho f^{p/2}),\nabla\rho)\;dv,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p/2}(\nabla f^{p/2},\nabla\rho^{2})\;dv

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p/2}(\nabla(\rho f^{p/2}),\nabla\rho)\;dv-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\;dv.

In conclusion,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle=-\frac{4(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(4(p-\frac{(p-1)}{p})|\nabla\rho|^{2}-\Delta\rho^{2})af^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta a)\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv-4(p-2\frac{p-1}{p})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p/2}(\nabla(\rho f^{p/2}),\nabla\rho)\;dv.

∎

Since for Theorem
1.1
we only consider
p
’s with
p>\frac{d}{d+\gamma+2}
, for the rest of this section we will always assume that
p>\frac{d}{d+\gamma+2}
. This has the added benefit of simplifying some of the constants since we are now bounded away from
1
(note that for
\gamma<-2
, we have
\frac{d}{d+\gamma+2}>1
).

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\rho\in C^{2}_{c}(B_{R})
. Given any three times
T_{1}<T_{2}<T_{3}
in
[0,T]
the quantity

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{(T_{2},T_{3})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv\;

\displaystyle+\;\frac{(p-1)}{p}\int_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dvdt

is not greater than

(6.1)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{T_{2}-T_{1}}+C_{1}\right)\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{3}}\int\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dvdt+C(d,\gamma)p^{2}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]f^{p}(|\nabla\rho|^{2}+|\Delta\rho^{2}|)\;dvdt,

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(f_{\textnormal{in}},R,p)
.

Proof.

Take the identity in Proposition
6.1
. Per Young’s inequality, for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle 2af^{p/2}|(\nabla(\rho f^{p/2}),\nabla\rho)|\leq\varepsilon a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}af^{p}|\nabla\rho|^{2}.

For
\varepsilon=\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\frac{1}{2(p-2\frac{p-1}{p})}
in particular, it follows that

\displaystyle-4(p-2\frac{p-1}{p})af^{p/2}(\nabla(\rho f^{p/2}),\nabla\rho)\leq\frac{2(p-1)}{p}a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}+2(p-2\frac{p-1}{p})^{2}\frac{p-1}{p}af^{p}|\nabla\rho|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(4(p-\frac{(p-1)}{p})|\nabla\rho|^{2}-\Delta\rho^{2})af^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta a)\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv+2(p-2\frac{p-1}{p})^{2}\frac{p}{2(p-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\;dv.

Combining various terms, this inequality can be written more succinctly as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv+\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(C(p)|\nabla\rho|^{2}-\Delta\rho^{2})af^{p}\;dv+(p-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta a)\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv,

where
C(p)=2(p-2\frac{p-1}{p})^{2}\frac{p}{2(p-1)}+4(p-\frac{p-1}{p})
. Since
p\geq\frac{d}{d+\gamma+2}>2
, it is elementary that

\displaystyle C(p)\leq C(d,\gamma)p^{2}.

Now we apply Corollary
5.6
with
\varepsilon=\min\{\frac{1}{p},\eta(1)\}
.
this yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv

\displaystyle+\frac{p-1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(C(d,\gamma)p^{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}-\Delta\rho^{2})af^{p}\;dv+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},\gamma,p_{0},R)p^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv.

Integrate now in
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
and take, on both sides, the sup with respect to
t_{2}
in
(T_{2},T_{3})
and average with respect to
t_{1}
in
(T_{1},T_{2})
. From here it results that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{(T_{2},T_{3})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dv\;+\;\frac{(p-1)}{p}\int_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a|\nabla(\rho f^{p/2})|^{2}\;dvdt

is no larger than

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{T_{2}-T_{1}}+{C(f_{\textnormal{in}},\gamma,p_{0},R)p^{m}}\right)\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}f^{p}\;dvdt+C(d,\gamma)p^{2}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}af^{p}(|\nabla\rho|^{2}+|\Delta\rho^{2}|)\;dvdt.

∎

All that remains of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is covered by the following lemma. This lemma amounts to an
L^{p}\to L^{\infty}
estimate in the spirit of De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory, following Moser’s approach. In what follows, recall that the exponent
q=2(1+\tfrac{2}{d})
was defined in (
5.1
) and that
p>\frac{d}{d+\gamma+2}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
R\geq 1
and
T>0
, then for any
v\in B_{R}
and
t\in(T/2,T)
we have

\displaystyle f(v,t)\leq C(f_{\textnormal{in}},p,\gamma,R,T)\left(1+\frac{1}{T}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\int_{T/4}^{T}\int_{B_{2R}}a[f]f^{p}\;dvdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

We introduce the sequences

\displaystyle T_{n}:=\frac{1}{4}\left(2-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)T,\;\;R_{n}:=\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)R,\;\;p_{n}:=p\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{n},\;\;\forall\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

We also pick a sequence of functions
\rho_{n}\in C^{2}_{c}(B_{R_{n}}(0))
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&0\leq\rho_{n}\leq 1\textnormal{ in }B_{R_{n}}(0)\setminus B_{R_{n+1}}(0),\;\;\rho_{n}\equiv 1\textnormal{ in }B_{R_{n+1}}(0),\\
&|\nabla\rho_{{n+1}}|\leq C(d)R^{-1}2^{n},\\
&|\Delta\rho^{2}_{n+1}|\leq C(d)R^{-2}4^{n}.\end{array}\right.

Now, for each
n\geq 0
, let
E_{n}
denote the quantity,

\displaystyle E_{n}:=\left(\int_{T_{n}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n}^{q}a[f]f^{p_{n}}\;dvdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}.

As it is standard for divergence elliptic equations, we are going to derive a recursive relation for
E_{n}
. To do this, first note
E_{n+1}
may be written as,

\displaystyle E_{n+1}^{p_{n}}=\left(\int_{T_{n+1}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\left(\rho_{{n+1}}f^{\frac{p_{n}}{2}}\right)^{q}\;dvdt\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}.

Thanks to the space-time inequality (
5.2
) we have

\displaystyle C(d,\gamma)^{-1}E_{n+1}^{p_{n}}\leq\sup\limits_{(T_{n+1},T)}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n+1}^{2}f^{p_{n}}(t)\;dv\right\}+\frac{(p_{n}-1)}{p_{n}}\int_{T_{n+1}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]|\nabla(\rho_{n+1}f^{\frac{p_{n}}{2}})|^{2}\;dvdt.

Then, the energy inequality from Proposition
6.2
says that
C(d,\gamma)^{-1}E_{n+1}^{p_{n}}
is no larger than

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2^{n+2}}{T}+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},\gamma,R)p_{n}^{m}\right)\int_{T_{n}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n+1}^{2}f^{p_{n}}\;dvdt

\displaystyle+C(d,\gamma)p_{n}^{2}\int_{T_{n}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]f^{p_{n}}(|\nabla\rho_{n+1}|^{2}+|\Delta\rho_{n+1}^{2}|)\;dvdt.

Note that the first integral is no larger than

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2^{n+2}}{T}+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},\gamma,p_{0},R)p_{n}^{m}\right)\frac{1}{\min\limits_{B_{R}\times(0,T)}a[f]}\int_{T_{n}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{n}^{q}af^{p_{n}}\;dv.

Next, again thanks to
\rho_{n}\equiv 1
in the support of
\rho_{n+1}
, and in particular

\displaystyle|\nabla\rho_{n+1}|^{2}\leq CR^{-2}4^{n}\rho_{n}^{q},\;|\Delta\rho_{n+1}^{2}|\leq CR^{-2}4^{n}\rho_{n}^{q},

so the second integral above is no larger than

\displaystyle C(d,\gamma)p_{n}^{2}R^{-2}4^{n}\int_{T_{n}}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a[f]\rho_{n}^{q}f^{p_{n}}\;dvdt.

In conclusion,

\displaystyle E_{n+1}\leq C\left[\left(\frac{2^{n+2}}{T}+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},\gamma,R)p_{n}^{m}\right)C(f_{\textnormal{in}},T)R^{-2-\gamma}+C(d,\gamma)p_{n}^{2}R^{-2}4^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}E_{n}.

Set

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\max\{4q,(q/2)^{m}\},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=C\left[(T^{-1}+C(f_{\textnormal{in}},\gamma,R)p^{m})C(f_{\textnormal{in}},R^{|2+\gamma|})+C(d,\gamma)p^{2}R^{-2}\right].

Then

\displaystyle E_{n+1}\leq b^{\frac{n}{p_{n}}}m^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}E_{n}.

This recursive relation, and a straightforward induction argument, yield that

\displaystyle E_{n}\leq b^{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{k}{p_{k}}}m^{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{p_{k}}}E_{0}.

Since

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{k}{p_{k}}=\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}k\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}k\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{p}\frac{2q}{(q-2)^{2}},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{p_{k}}=\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{1}{p}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{p}\frac{q}{q-2},

we conclude that

(6.5)

\displaystyle E_{n}\leq b^{\frac{1}{p}\frac{2q}{(q-2)^{2}}}m^{\frac{1}{p}\frac{q}{q-2}}E_{0}.

Observe that

\displaystyle E_{0}\leq\left(\int_{T/4}^{T}\int_{B_{2R}(0)}a[f]f^{p}\;dvdt\right)^{1/p}.

Now, since
\eta_{n}\geq 1
in
B_{R}
and
T_{n}\leq T/2
for all
n
, it follows that

\displaystyle E_{n}\geq\left(\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{B_{2R}}a[f]f^{p_{n}}\;dvdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}.

Considering that
a>0
everywhere, it follows that

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}E_{n}\geq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(T/2,T))}.

This, together with (
6.5
) concludes the proof of the lemma, and of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

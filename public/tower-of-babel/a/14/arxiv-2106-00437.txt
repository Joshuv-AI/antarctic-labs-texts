Homological duality for covering groups of reductive p p -adic groups
Dragoş Frăţilă and Dipendra Prasad

Abstract
. In this largely expository paper we extend properties of the homological duality functor RHom ℋ ⁡ ( − , ℋ ) \RHom_{\mathcal{H}}(-,\mathcal{H}) where ℋ \mathcal{H} is the Hecke algebra of a reductive p p -adic group, to the case where it is the Hecke algebra of a finite central extension of a reductive p p -adic group. The most important properties being that RHom ℋ ⁡ ( − , ℋ ) \RHom_{\mathcal{H}}(-,\mathcal{H}) is concentrated in a single degree for irreducible representations and that it gives rise to Schneider–Stuhler duality for Ext groups (a Serre functor like property). Our simple proof is self-contained and bypasses the localization techniques of [ SS97 , Bez04 ] improving slightly on [ NP20 ] . Along the way we also study Grothendieck–Serre duality with respect to the Bernstein center and provide a proof of the folklore result that on admissible modules this functor is nothing else but the contragredient duality. We single out a necessary and sufficient condition for when these three dualities agree on finite length modules in a given block. In particular, we show this is the case for all cuspidal blocks as well as, due to a result of Roche [ Roc02 ] , on all blocks with trivial stabilizer in the relative Weyl group.

1.1.

Let

The homological duality for the abelian category

An important property established by Bernstein in his Harvard notes

We will denote this representation by

On the other hand, in

\displaystyle\Ext^{i}_{G}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})^{*}\simeq\Ext_{G}^{d(\pi)-i}(\pi^{\prime},\mathbb{D}_{h}(\pi)^{\vee}),

The more general version was proved in

One of the aims of this work is to present a direct proof of (

The strategy is to first show that the full homological duality

\RHom_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi,\pi^{\prime})^{*}\simeq\RHom_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi^{\prime},D_{h}(\pi)^{\vee})

for any smooth representations
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
with
\pi
finitely generated.
This is easy and follows from basic adjunctions naturally extended to idempotented algebras.
For convenience of the reader, and for lack of a precise reference, we provide all the details in

The second step, namely showing that
D_{h}(\pi)
is concentrated in a single degree for an irreducible representation, is non-formal and is based on several ingredients: Bernstein decomposition, second adjointness and a special property of the algebra governing a cuspidal block (Frobenius-symmetric algebra over its center).
Although the proof is already in
[
Ber92
]
we hope that the argument that we present is more streamlined.

Another property of the homological functor

is that on finite length cuspidal representations it agrees (up to a shift) with the contragredient functor.
A sketch of this result for irreducible representations appears already in
[
Ber92
]

To this effect we were led to study the Grothendieck–Serre duality over the Bernstein center

12

In particular, we provide a proof of the folklore result that

In order to state the condition, we need to introduce some notation.

If

We are able to check this condition on a cuspidal block

Let us say a few words about the Grothendieck–Serre duality which we will briefly
review in
Sections

2.4
and
12

Any representation
\pi\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
, besides having an action of
{\widetilde{G}}
, has also an action of the Bernstein center
\mathcal{Z}
, and by definition these two actions commute.
Therefore, any operation that one does to a
\mathcal{Z}
-module will produce again a
{\widetilde{G}}
-module.
The most trivial operation one can think of is taking the contragredient.
This works well, in the sense that it is an involution, on admissible representations.
One would like to extend the contragredient in a functorial way to all finitely generated modules such that it stays an involution.
Working with a fixed Bernstein block at a time, the question now is sent in the commutative algebra court: we have a nice ring (invariants under a finite group of a Laurent polynomial algebra, in particular Cohen–Macaulay) and finitely generated modules over it.
We would like to extend the contragredient from finite length modules to all finitely generated modules in such a way that it stays an involution.
One way to do this, coming from algebraic geometry, is to use the Grothendieck–Serre duality.
Although all the properties are nice and formal, one must now work in the derived category since the abelian category of modules is not preserved anymore under this duality.

For a commutative regular ring
A
of Krull dimension
d
(always containing a field

\omega_{A}^{\circ}:=\omega_{A}[d]\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(A\mathchar 45\relax\mathbf{mod})

is the module of top differentials on

If

\omega_{A}^{\circ}
is the normalized dualizing complex of

\omega_{B}^{\circ}:=\RHom_{A}(B,\omega_{A}^{\circ})

In our context, the (component of the) Bernstein center

is Cohen–Macaulay hence it is finite free over a regular subalgebra.
The above paragraph allows us to see that it has a normalized dualizing complex which moreover lives in a single degree (this is a characterization of Cohen–Macaulay rings).

2.1.
Preliminaries on decomposing categories and centers

Given
I
a set (possibly infinite) and
\mathcal{C}_{i},i\in I
, a family of abelian categories, we can define the product category
\mathcal{C}:=\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
in the 2-category of abelian categories through the usual universal property.
Concretely, one constructs it as follows:

•

the objects are tuples
(X_{i})_{i\in I}
with
X_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
for every
i\in I
;

•

the morphisms are defined by

\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}((X_{i})_{i},(Y_{j})_{j}):=\prod_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}(X_{i},Y_{i});

•

the projection functors
\pi_{i}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}
send a tuple
(X_{s})_{s}
to
X_{i}
.

The universal property of a product is immediately verified.

In addition to the projection functors
\pi_{i}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}
, there are also natural inclusion functors

\iota_{i}\colon\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}

sending an object
X\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
to the tuple that has
X
in position
i
and
0
everywhere else.
By construction, the functor
\iota_{i}
is fully faithful so we can think of the category
\mathcal{C}_{i}
as a full subcategory of the product category
\mathcal{C}
.
We will drop the functor
\iota_{i}
from the notation most of the time.
Two such subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}
for
i\neq j
are (derived) orthogonal, namely

\Ext^{k}_{\mathcal{C}}(X_{i},X_{j})=0,\text{ for all }k\geq 0.

Moreover we notice that the object
(X_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
is the direct sum of the objects
\iota_{i}(X_{i})
in
\mathcal{C}
.
Indeed, let us check the universal property for direct sums: for an arbitrary object
Y=(Y_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
we have

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}((X_{i})_{i},(Y_{i})_{i})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}(X_{i},Y_{i})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(\iota_{i}(X_{i}),(Y_{j})_{j})

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(\oplus_{i}\iota_{i}(X_{i}),(Y_{j})_{j}).

Actually, the same argument shows that
(X_{i})_{i}
is also the direct product of the objects
\iota_{i}(X_{i})
,
i\in I
, in
\mathcal{C}
.

Caution: the above does not mean that in
\mathcal{C}
we always have
\oplus_{i}A_{i}\simeq\prod_{i}A_{i}
for any
A_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
.
See also
Example

2.2
.

As a summary, we have a category
\mathcal{C}
with full subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
i\in I
that are two by two derived orthogonal and moreover every object of
\mathcal{C}
is a direct sum of objects from
\mathcal{C}_{i}
(the subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
split the category
\mathcal{C}
).
Conversely, a category
\mathcal{C}
with full subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
i\in I
, with the above properties is a direct product of

\mathcal{C}_{i}

provided we assume a small technical condition on the categories that we work with which in practice is always verified.

2.2.
Serre functors

The notion of a Serre functor for an additive category was introduced in
[
BK89
]
in order to capture duality phenomena similar to Serre’s duality theorem on smooth projective varieties.
It is intimately related to questions of representability of certain functors.
Moreover this turns out to be a very rigid notion.
In particular, if a Serre functor exists then it is unique (up to natural isomorphism).

Definition 2.9
.

[
Huy06
, Definition 1.28]
[
BK89
, Definition 3.1]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
k
-linear category.
A Serre functor
\mathbb{S}
on
\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence of categories
\mathbb{S}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
together with natural isomorphisms

\eta_{A,B}\colon\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(A,B)^{*}\to\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(B,\mathbb{S}(A)),\,\text{ for all }A,B\in\mathcal{C}.

Remark 2.10
.

It is observed in
[
Huy06
, Lemma 1.30]
that if one assumes that the categories
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}

\mathbb{S}_{1},\mathbb{S}_{2}
,
have finite dimensional Hom spaces, then any functorial isomorphism between
the categories
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
commutes with the Serre functors.

An example of a Serre functor, actually one that motivated the notion, is the classical Serre duality.
Namely, let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
of dimension
d
and denote by
\omega_{X}
its canonical sheaf.
Then Serre duality stipulates a natural isomorphism

\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})^{*}\simeq\Ext^{d-i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F}\otimes\omega_{X}),\text{ for all }\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\Coh(X)).

In other words, the functor

-\otimes\omega_{X}[d]\colon\mathcal{D}^{b}(\Coh(X))\to\mathcal{D}^{b}(\Coh(X))

is a Serre functor.

A simpler example comes from finite dimensional algebras over a field

and is known as the Nakayama functor, see
[
BK89
, §3.2, Example 3]

Let

We consider the derived category

There are two duality functors

The following proposition is very easy to prove from classical adjunctions and our results in

Proposition 2.11

\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(V):=\{f\colon G\to V\mid f(hg)=hf(g),\text{ for all }h\in H\text{ and }g\in G\}^{\mathrm{sm}}.

The pair of functors

\operatorname{ind}_{H}^{G}(V):=\{f\in\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(V)\mid H\backslash\supp(f)\text{ is compact}\}.

In case

Definition 3.1

4.
Splitting the category of representations

4.1.
Compact representations

In this section
G
denotes an arbitrary locally compact td-group which is
countable at infinity.
The most important result we need is that compact representations split the category of smooth representations of
G

Definition 4.1
.

A smooth representation
(\pi,V)
of
G
is said to be compact if all its matrix coefficients have compact support.

Remark 4.2
.

The existence of a compact irreducible representation
implies that
G
has compact center.

The first important theorem about compact representations is
that any compact representation which appears as a subquotient in any smooth representation appears as a direct summand by
[
BZ76
, Theorem 2.44]
. The following theorem is an easy consequence of it:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\rho
be a compact irreducible representation of
G
.
Then matrix coefficients of
\rho
provide us with an injective map of
G\times G
-modules

\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\vee}\subset\mathcal{H}(G),

and this is the only subquotient of
\mathcal{H}(G)
isomorphic to
\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\vee}
.

Moreover, if
\rho^{\prime}
is another irreducible compact representation of
G
, non-isomorphic to
\rho
, then the
G\times G
representation
\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\prime\vee}
does not appear as a subquotient of
\mathcal{H}(G)
.

Proof.

There is a natural map from
\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\vee}
to functions on
G
given by

v\otimes\ell\in\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\vee}\mapsto F_{v\otimes\ell}(g)=\ell(gv),

which if
\rho
is a compact smooth representation of
G
lands inside
compactly supported smooth functions on
G
, i.e., in
\mathcal{H}(G)
. If
\rho
is furthermore irreducible, the resulting map

\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\vee}\subset\mathcal{H}(G)
is injective.

As any compact representation which appears as a subquotient in any smooth representation appears as a direct summand by
[
BZ76
, Theorem 2.44]
, the rest of the theorem is a direct consequence of
the Frobenius reciprocity:

\Hom_{G\times G}[\operatorname{ind}_{\Delta G}^{G\times G}(\mathbb{C}),\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\prime\vee}]=\Hom_{\Delta G}[\rho\boxtimes\rho^{\prime\vee},\mathbb{C}].\qed

We denote by
\mathcal{M}(G)_{c}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}(G)
consisting of those smooth
representations of
G
all whose irreducible subquotients are compact representations.
It is clearly a subcategory closed under subquotients, direct sums and extensions.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}(G)_{nc}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}(G)
formed of representations that have no compact irreducible subquotient.

For
\mathcal{S}
, a collection of irreducible representations of
G
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}(G)_{[\mathcal{S}]}
the subcategory of
\mathcal{M}(G)
formed of representations such that all their irreducible subquotients are isomorphic to an object in

.
Denote by
\mathcal{M}(G)_{[\text{out }\mathcal{S}]}
the subcategory of representations such that none of their irreducible subquotients are isomorphic to an object in
\mathcal{S}
.

The following theorem summarizes the main properties of compact representations, cf.

[
BZ76
, Theorem 2.44]
:

Theorem 4.4
.

For
\mathcal{S}
a finite collection of compact irreducible representations of

(1)

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(G)\simeq\mathcal{M}(G)_{[\mathcal{S}]}\times\mathcal{M}(G)_{[\text{out }\mathcal{S}]}.

This is not completely automatic from

Consider the following condition (see

If the group

The (normalized) functors of parabolic induction and parabolic restriction are defined as follows (for details see
[
Ren10
, VI.1]
whose convention for the modulus character is opposite that of
[
BZ76
]
and
[
BZ77
]
, which explains
interchange of the factors
\delta_{\widetilde{P}}^{\pm 1/2}
of

[
BZ76
]
and
[
BZ77
]

with their inverses here):

\displaystyle\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})\to\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})

\displaystyle\pi\mapsto\operatorname{ind}_{{\widetilde{P}}}^{{\widetilde{G}}}(\pi\otimes\delta_{\widetilde{P}}^{-1/2})

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{{\widetilde{G}}}\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})\to\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})

\displaystyle\tau\mapsto(\operatorname{Res}_{{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\tau)\otimes\delta_{{\widetilde{P}}}^{1/2})_{N}

where

{\widetilde{P}}

Both functors are exact.

Frobenius reciprocity states that
\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{{\widetilde{G}}}
is right adjoint to
\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}
.
As a consequence, parabolic induction preserves limits and parabolic restriction preserves colimits.

It is easy to establish that parabolic induction preserves admissibility (see
[

, III.2.3]
): this is because the double-coset
{\widetilde{K}}\backslash{\widetilde{G}}/{\widetilde{P}}
is finite for any open compact
{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{G}}
.
The analogous result for parabolic restriction is not so immediate (see
Corollary

6.11
).

It is also rather straightforward to show that parabolic restriction preserves finite type representations.
However, the analogous result for parabolic induction is not so trivial and the proof is based on Bernstein’s decomposition
Theorem

7.14
.

We define cuspidal representations of
\widetilde{G}
just as in the linear case: those smooth representations of

\widetilde{G}
whose all nontrivial parabolic restrictions vanish (i.e., all nontrivial Jacquet modules vanish).

By a similar argument as in the linear case (see
[
Ren10
, VI.5.1]
), the geometric lemma holds for
covering groups
{\widetilde{G}}
too
and allows one to calculate the Jordan–Hölder series of the Jacquet modules of a parabolically induced representation of
\widetilde{G}
.
Then one can conclude as in the linear case that every irreducible representation of
\widetilde{G}

is a subquotient of a parabolically induced representation of
\widetilde{G}
induced from a cuspidal representation of a Levi subgroup of
\widetilde{G}
.

5.3.
Iwahori decomposition

Definition 5.1
.

A compact open subgroup
K
of
G
(or of
{\widetilde{G}}
) is said to have an Iwahori factorization with respect to a parabolic

P=LN
with opposite parabolic

if the natural map given by multiplication:

is a bijection, where

\displaystyle K^{+}

\displaystyle=K\cap N,

\displaystyle K^{0}

\displaystyle=K\cap L,

\displaystyle K^{-}

\displaystyle=K\cap N^{-}.

For central extensions
{\widetilde{G}}
, we replace

{\widetilde{P}}={\widetilde{L}}N

{\widetilde{P}}^{-}={\widetilde{L}}N^{-}

.

5.4.
Cartan decomposition

The Cartan decomposition for the group
G
plays an important role in the proof of the uniform admissibility theorem.
The analogous theorem for
{\widetilde{G}}
is proved by taking the inverse image from
G
.
Let us recall some notions before we state the theorem.

Recall the subgroup
{G^{\circ}}\leq G
from
Section

4.2
.
Let

{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
be the subgroup of
{\widetilde{G}}
which is the preimage of
{G^{\circ}}\leq G
.
We put
\Lambda({\widetilde{G}}):={\widetilde{G}}/{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}=G/{G^{\circ}}
; it is a finite rank free
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Definition 5.2
.

The set of unramified characters of
{\widetilde{G}}
is defined to be

\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}}):=\Hom({\widetilde{G}}/{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}},\mathbb{C}^{\times})=\Hom(G/{G^{\circ}},\mathbb{C}^{\times}).

It is clear that the image of
Z({\widetilde{G}})
has finite index in
{\widetilde{G}}/{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
and hence the rank of

Z({\widetilde{G}})
is the same as the rank of
Z(G)
.
In other words, we have
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{d_{G}}
where
d_{G}
is the split rank of
Z(G)
.
Notice that by definition, the unramified characters of
{\widetilde{G}}
are the same as those of
G
(identified through the map
{\widetilde{G}}\to G
).

Construction.

For
\mathbb{L}
, a reductive algebraic group over
F
, we denote by
A_{\mathbb{L}}
, the maximal split torus contained in the center of
\mathbb{L}
, and denote

d(\mathbb{L}):=\dim(A_{\mathbb{L}})
, and call it the split rank of the center of
\mathbb{L}
.
We extend this definition to any finite central extension
{\widetilde{L}}
of
L=\mathbb{L}(F)
, defining

d({\widetilde{L}})=d(\mathbb{L})=\dim(A_{\mathbb{L}})
.

Put
A:=A_{\mathbb{L}}(F)
and notice that the image of
A/A^{\circ}\to L/L^{\circ}
has finite index.
Moreover, the surjection
A\to A/A^{\circ}
admits a section that is a group homomorphism (use a uniformizer in
F
) and so we get an injection
A/A^{\circ}\hookrightarrow L
whose image in
L/L^{\circ}
has finite index.

If
{\widetilde{L}}
is a finite central extension of
L
, then the natural map
Z({\widetilde{L}})\rightarrow Z(L)
induces a map of lattices
\Lambda(Z({\widetilde{L}}))\to\Lambda(Z(L))
whose image has finite index.
We can therefore find a lift
\Lambda(Z({\widetilde{L}}))\to{\widetilde{L}}
that is a group homomorphism and moreover its image in
\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})=\Lambda(L)
has finite index.
Summarizing we have a diagram

\textstyle{{\widetilde{L}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{=}
\textstyle{\Lambda(L)}
\textstyle{Z({\widetilde{L}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lambda(Z({\widetilde{L}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lambda(Z(L))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

and from the discussion above we have a section
\Lambda(Z({\widetilde{L}}))\to Z({\widetilde{L}})
which is a group homomorphism.
Denote its image by
C_{A}
; it is a central subgroup of
{\widetilde{L}}
.

Fix also finitely many elements
F_{A}:=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in{\widetilde{L}}\}

that lift some system of representatives for
\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})/\Lambda(Z({\widetilde{L}}))
.

All in all, we have that
C_{A}F_{A}
provides a set-theoretic lift of
\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})
to
{\widetilde{L}}
that is moreover a group homomorphism when restricted to
\Lambda(Z({\widetilde{L}}))
.

Apply the above discussion to a parabolic subgroup
{\widetilde{P}}={\widetilde{L}}N
and recall the notion of dominant cocharacters of
L
with respect to
P
.
Let
L^{+}
denote the preimage of dominant cocharacters of
L
through the map
L\to\Hom_{\mathsf{gr}}(L,\mathbb{G}_{m})^{*}
given by
m\mapsto(\chi\mapsto\chi(m))
.
We let
{\widetilde{L}}^{+}
denote its preimage in
{\widetilde{L}}
under
{\widetilde{L}}\to L

We can choose the above
F_{A}
to lie in
{\widetilde{L}}^{+}
and similarly we can consider
C_{A}^{+}\subset C_{A}
the subset of dominant elements in
C_{A}
.

Let
P_{0}
be a fixed minimal parabolic subgroup of
G
with Levi decomposition
P_{0}=L_{0}N_{0}
and denote by
{\widetilde{P}}_{0}={\widetilde{L}}_{0}N_{0}
the corresponding subgroups in
{\widetilde{G}}
.

Let
A=A_{0}
be the split component of
P_{0}
and
C_{A}F_{A}\subset{\widetilde{L}}_{0}
as above.

Theorem 5.3

(see
[
BT
]
)
.

(Cartan decomposition)
There exists a maximal compact subgroup

K_{0}
of
G
such that for its inverse image

{\widetilde{K}}_{0}
inside
{\widetilde{G}}
, we have:

(1)

{\widetilde{G}}={\widetilde{P}}_{0}{\widetilde{K}}_{0}={\widetilde{K}}_{0}{\widetilde{P}}_{0}

(2)

{\widetilde{G}}=\bigsqcup_{af\in C_{A}^{+}F_{A}}{\widetilde{K}}_{0}af{\widetilde{K}}_{0}
.

Put
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{K}}_{0},{\widetilde{K}})
to be the Hecke algebra of
{\widetilde{K}}
-biinvariant functions on
{\widetilde{K}}_{0}

where
{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{K}}_{0}
is any compact open subgroup of
{\widetilde{K}}_{0}

It is a finite dimensional algebra over

.

A rather easy consequence of this theorem, which is essential in the proof of the uniform admissibility
theorem (

below), is the following decomposition of the Hecke algebra:

Theorem 5.4
.

With the notation as above, let
K\leq K_{0}\leq G
be compact open subgroups of

{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{K}}_{0}\leq{\widetilde{G}}
be the compact open subgroup of
{\widetilde{G}}

\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}},{\widetilde{K}})=\mathcal{H}_{0}D\mathcal{C}\mathcal{H}_{0}

.

6.
Basic theorems

As before,
{\widetilde{G}}
is a covering group (see
Section

3.1
) of a reductive
p
-adic group
G
, where
G=\mathbb{G}(F)
for
\mathbb{G}
a reductive group over
F
, a non-archimedean local field.
All the basic theorems concerning representations of reductive
p
-adic groups hold also for
{\widetilde{G}}
with the same proofs.
We will give precise references to
[
Ren10
]
where the analogous results for
G
are proved.

The groups
G
and
{\widetilde{G}}
do not have any compact representations in general because their centers may be non-compact.
However, we can ask for the next best thing (see
Section

4.2
):

Definition 6.1
.

An irreducible smooth representation
(V,\pi)\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
is called compact modulo center if all its matrix coefficients have compact support in
{\widetilde{G}}/Z({\widetilde{G}})
.

The classical theorem of Harish-Chandra still holds with the same proof:

Theorem 6.2

(Harish-Chandra, see
[
Ren10
, VI.2.1]
for linear groups)
.

An irreducible smooth
representation
(\pi,V)
of
{\widetilde{G}}
is cuspidal if and only if it is compact modulo center.

Using the easy fact that parabolic induction preserves admissibility, the fact that all compact representations are admissible and the above theorem we can prove the admissibility of irreducible representations (for a proof in the linear case see for example
[
Ren10
, VI.2.2]
or
[
Ber92
, Theorem 12]
):

Theorem 6.3
.

Any irreducible representation of
{\widetilde{G}}
is admissible.

Not only irreducible representations are admissible but in fact they are so in a uniform way.
Below is the precise formulation of the Uniform Admissibility Theorem due to Bernstein whose proof is based on the decomposition of the bi-invariant Hecke algebra given in
Theorem

5.4
and some tricky linear algebra:

Theorem 6.4

(uniform admissibility,
[
Ber74
]
)
.

Let
{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{G}}
be an open compact subgroup.
There exists a constant
c=c({\widetilde{K}})
such that for all irreducible representations
(\pi,V)
of
{\widetilde{G}}
we have

\dim(V^{\widetilde{K}})\leq c
.

One can also look at
[
Ren10
, VI.2.3]
for a proof.

For establishing the Bernstein decomposition, and prior to that, verifying the condition (
KF
) which leads to
Theorem

4.5
, one makes use of the following

Corollary 6.5

(see
[
Ren10
, VI.2.4]
for the linear case)
.

Given an open compact subgroup
{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{G}}
there is a compact modulo center subset
\Omega\subset{\widetilde{G}}
such that for any irreducible cuspidal representation
(V,\pi)
of
{\widetilde{G}}
and any vector
v\in V^{\widetilde{K}}
the function

g\mapsto e_{{\widetilde{K}}}\cdot\pi(g)(v)

has support contained in
\Omega
.

The following theorem, which goes under the name of <<
the geometric lemma
>>,
is extensively used.
It is due to Bernstein and Zelevinsky
[
BZ77
, 2.12]
.

Theorem 6.6

(see
[
BZ77
, 2.12]
)
.

Let
{\widetilde{P}}={\widetilde{M}}N
and
Q={\widetilde{L}}U
be two parabolic subgroups of
{\widetilde{G}}
with Levi decompositions.
Let
\rho
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
{\widetilde{M}}
and put
\tau:=\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{Q}}}^{\widetilde{G}}\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{M}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\rho)
.
Then we have

(1)

If
{\widetilde{L}}
has no Levi subgroup conjugate to
{\widetilde{M}}
, then
\tau=0
.

(2)

If
{\widetilde{M}}
is not conjugate to
{\widetilde{L}}
, then
\tau
has no cuspidal subquotient.

(3)

If
{\widetilde{M}}
and
{\widetilde{L}}
are standard and conjugate,
then
\tau
has a filtration with subquotients isomorphic to
{}^{w}\rho
for
w\in W({\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{M}})/W_{\widetilde{L}}
.
In particular,
\tau
is cuspidal.

In the above statement
W_{\widetilde{L}}
is the Weyl group of
{\widetilde{L}}
and
W({\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{M}})
is the subset of the Weyl group of
{\widetilde{G}}
conjugating
{\widetilde{L}}
into
{\widetilde{M}}
.

The next result, proved using the above theorem, shows that the Jordan–Hölder factors of a parabolically induced representation of an irreducible cuspidal are independent of the chosen parabolic and depend only on the conjugacy
class of the cuspidal datum.

Theorem 6.7

(see
[
BZ77
, 2.9]
)
.

Let
{\widetilde{P}}={\widetilde{L}}N
and
{\widetilde{P}}^{\prime}={\widetilde{L}}^{\prime}N^{\prime}
be two parabolic subgroups in
{\widetilde{G}}
with Levi decompositions.
Let
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\rm cs}({\widetilde{L}})
and
\rho^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\rm cs}({\widetilde{L}}^{\prime})_{\rm cs}
and
\pi=\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\rho)
and
\pi^{\prime}=\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\prime},{\widetilde{P}}^{\prime}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\rho^{\prime})
be the induced representations.
Then
\pi
has finite length and the following are equivalent.

(1)

The cuspidal data
({\widetilde{L}},\rho)
and
({\widetilde{L}}^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
are conjugate,

(2)

The Jordan–Hölder series of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are equivalent,

(3)

The Jordan–Hölder series of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
have a common element.

In particular, if
{\widetilde{L}}={\widetilde{L}}^{\prime}
and
\rho=\rho^{\prime}
, this shows that
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
have the same Jordan–Hölder series, independent of the chosen parabolic.

We can now deduce

Corollary 6.8
.

Parabolic induction preserves finite length representations.

Proof.

If the parabolic induction involves a representation which is irreducible cuspidal then the
corollary follows from the above theorem. In general, any irreducible representation is a subrepresentation of a parabolically induced representation
of an irreducible cuspidal representation. We conclude the above corollary
by the transitivity of parabolic induction.
∎

In order to show the analogous result for parabolic restriction, we need to use the following theorem due to
Howe whose proof for covering groups is the same as in the linear case:

Theorem 6.9

(see
[
BZ77
, Thm 4.1]
or
[
Ren10
, VI.6.3]
)
.

A smooth representation of
{\widetilde{G}}
is of finite length if and only if it is admissible and finitely generated.

A crucial tool that goes into the proof of Howe’s theorem is Jacquet’s lemma. Later, in
Theorem

8.13
, we will state a generalized version of it since it is necessary for the second adjointness.
Until then, let us give the classical statement

Theorem 6.10

(Jacquet’s lemma)
.

Let
V
be an admissible representation of
{\widetilde{G}}
,
{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{G}}
an open compact subgroup admitting an Iwahori decomposition with respect to
{\widetilde{P}}={\widetilde{L}}N
.
Then the following canonical map is surjective

V^{{\widetilde{K}}}\to(V_{N})^{{\widetilde{K}}\cap{\widetilde{L}}}.

Immediately from Jacquet’s lemma we obtain

Corollary 6.11
.

Parabolic restriction preserves admissibility.

Use Howe’s theorem, the above corollary and the easy fact that parabolic restriction preserves finite type to deduce

Corollary 6.12
.

Parabolic restriction preserves finite length representations.

7.
The Bernstein decomposition

We give an exposition of a small part of a theory due to Bernstein which
allows one to decompose the category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
of smooth complex
representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
as a direct product of certain indecomposable full subcategories,
now called the Bernstein components of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
.
The results are due to
[
Ber84
]
where it is also stated that they hold for finite central extensions of reductive
p
-adic groups.
Indeed, no essential modifications are needed to adapt the proof from
[
Ber84
]
to the case of finite central extensions.
However, in the following we follow mostly the exposition of the linear case from
[
Ren10
, VI.7]
giving
precise references in appropriate places.

The idea is that whereas
{\widetilde{G}}
does not have compact representations, the group
{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
does, and moreover, from Harish-Chandra’s
Theorem

6.2
, all irreducible cuspidal representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
restrict to compact representations of
{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
.
So using the results of
Section

4.2
, we can decompose
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
into a cuspidal part and an induced part.
Using induction we express the induced part in a similar way.

7.1.
Cuspidals split

In this section we sketch the decomposition of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
into a product of cuspidal and induced.

We define
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\rm cs}
(resp.,
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathrm{ind}}
) to be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
formed of representations all of whose irreducible subquotients are cuspidal (resp., that have no cuspidal irreducible subquotients).
The set
\operatorname{Irr}_{\rm cs}({\widetilde{G}})
denotes the set of irreducible cuspidal representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
.

Remark 7.1
.

The category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\rm cs}
(resp.,
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathrm{ind}}
) is the pullback of the subcategory of compact representations (resp., non-compact representations) from
\mathcal{M}({{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}})
through the functor
\operatorname{Res}_{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}^{\widetilde{G}}
.

Let
(V,\pi)\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\rm cs}({\widetilde{G}})
and denote by
[\pi]
its inertia class, i.e., its orbit under
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})
.
From
Proposition

4.10
we have that the restriction of
\pi
to
{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
depends only on the inertia class and we know moreover that this restriction has finite length and is semisimple.

Definition 7.2
.

We define
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]}
to be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
formed of those representations
all of whose subquotients belong to
[\pi]
.
Similarly define
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\text{\rm out }\pi]}
.

Remark 7.3
.

Here is a different way of thinking about
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]}
.
If
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{l}
are the irreducible summands of
\pi
restricted to
{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
, then the category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]}
consists precisely in the representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
whose restriction to
{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
are direct sums of
\tau_{i},i=1,\dots,l
,
i.e., those
representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
whose restriction to
{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}({{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}})_{[\mathcal{A}]}
, where
\mathcal{A}=\{\tau_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,l\}
. (See
Section

4.1
.)

Harish-Chandra’s
Theorem

6.2
together with the results from
Section

4.2
give

Theorem 7.4
.

For an irreducible cuspidal representation
\pi
of
{\widetilde{G}}
,
we have a decomposition of the category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
as

\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})\simeq\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]}\times\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\text{out }\pi]}.

One uses the uniform admissibility
Theorem

6.4
to check the condition (
KF
) in
Section

4.1
and then applies
Theorem

4.13
and
Theorem

4.14
to get the following decomposition

Theorem 7.5

(see
[
Ren10
, VI.3.5]
)
.

The subcategories
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\rm cs}
and
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathrm{ind}}
split the category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})=\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\rm cs}\times\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathrm{ind}}

and moreover the cuspidal part decomposes as

(7.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\rm cs}=\prod_{[\pi]\in[\operatorname{Irr}({\widetilde{G}})_{\rm cs}]}\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]}.

Since the center of a product of categories is the product of their centers (see
Proposition

2.6
), we have

Corollary 7.6
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}}))=\prod_{[\pi]\in[\operatorname{Irr}_{\rm cs}({\widetilde{G}})]}\mathcal{Z}_{[\pi]}\times\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathrm{ind}}).

It turns out that for an irreducible cuspidal representation
\pi
the center
\mathcal{Z}_{[\pi]}
of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]}
is not very hard to determine.
Recall the notation
\Lambda({\widetilde{G}})={\widetilde{G}}/{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}
and
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})=\Hom_{\mathsf{gr}}(\Lambda({\widetilde{G}}),\mathbb{C}^{\times})
and notice that the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda({\widetilde{G}})]
is the algebra of regular functions on the algebraic variety (a torus)
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})
.

We first need a lemma whose easy proof follows by considerations of the central character.

Lemma 7.7
.

[
Ren10
, V.2.7]

Given
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\rm cs}({\widetilde{G}})
,
its stabilizer in
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})
,

\mathcal{G}_{\pi}:=\Stab_{\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})}(\pi)=\left\{\chi\in\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})\,\,\left|\pi\otimes\chi\cong\pi\right.\right\},

is finite.

Theorem 7.8

(see
[
Ren10
, VI.10]
or
[
Ber84
, 1.12-1.14]
for a slick proof)
.

Given
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\rm cs}({\widetilde{G}})
, we have a canonical isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]})\simeq\mathbb{C}[\Lambda({\widetilde{G}})]^{\mathcal{G}_{\pi}}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{G}})/\mathcal{G}_{\pi}).

In particular,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]})
is isomorphic to a ring of Laurent polynomials and hence is smooth of Krull dimension equal to the the rank of
\Lambda({\widetilde{G}})
.

Actually, one can do a bit better and find an equivalence of categories
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\pi]}\simeq\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{[\pi]}
with
\mathcal{R}_{[\pi]}:=\End_{{\widetilde{G}}}(\Pi_{[\pi]})
and
\Pi_{[\pi]}:=\operatorname{ind}_{{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\operatorname{Res}_{{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\pi))
.
This will be discussed in
Section

9
.

7.2.
Induced representations

In this section we look at the category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathrm{ind}}
and decompose it into blocks.
The main input is the geometric lemma, see (
[
BZ77
, 2.12]
),
Theorem

6.6
and
Theorem

above,
as well as

[

, VI.5.1]
for a proof that works also for
{\widetilde{G}}
.

Definition 7.9
.

8.
The second adjointness theorem

Recall that the Frobenius reciprocity theorem asserts that for
{\widetilde{P}}
a parabolic subgroup of
{\widetilde{G}}
with Levi subgroup

{\widetilde{L}}
, the functor
\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}
is left adjoint to
\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}

It is natural to look for an adjoint in the other direction.

Although showing that

admits a right adjoint is not difficult (see below), identifying

\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}
is left adjoint to
\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}^{-}}^{\widetilde{G}}
, where
{\widetilde{P}}^{-}
is the opposite parabolic of
{\widetilde{P}}
.

In this section we will sketch the main steps in the proof following the exposition in
[
Ren10
, VI.9]
where all the details are spelled out for linear groups.
In loc.cit., the argument is streamlined using the notion of
completion
of a representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
which we recall below.
Other references for the second adjointness theorem for the linear case include
[
Ber92
, Theorem 19]
,

[
Bus01
]
; a geometric proof is given in
[
BK15
]
.

8.1.
Completion

We recall the notion of completion for smooth
G
-modules where
G
is a locally compact, totally disconnected group admitting a countable basis of neighborhoods of 1 given by compact open subgroups.
For

a smooth
G
-module its completion

is a
G
-module (not smooth anymore)

V

(V^{\vee})^{*}
.

This notion will play a simplifying role in the proof of the second adjointness theorem and it also helps clarify the relationship between
N

N
-coinvariants.

Notice that for a representation
V
of
G
and compact open subgroups
L\subset K
with corresponding idempotents

, we have a natural map
e_{L}V\to e_{K}V

e_{K}w
.

Definition 8.1
.

For
V
, a smooth representation of

, we define the functor

\overline{(\,\,)}\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)\text{ by }

\overline{V}:=\varprojlim(e_{K}V)=\varprojlim(V^{K})

where the inverse limit is taken over all compact open subgroups of
G
.

A few remarks are in order.

Remark 8.2
.

(1)

Since the set of compact open subgroups of
G
is invariant under conjugation by
G
, it follows that
\overline{V}
is a
G
-module and that
\overline{V}^{K}=V^{K}
.
Clearly
V\subset\overline{V}
and the smooth part of
\overline{V}
is
V
.

(2)

The completion functor is exact because the Mittag-Leffler condition is automatically satisfied.

(3)

Another way of writing the completion functor is to note that

\Hom_{G}(\mathcal{H}(G),V)

acquires a
G
-module structure since
\mathcal{H}(G)
is a
G\times G
-module,
and since
\mathcal{H}(G)=\varinjlim\mathcal{H}(G)e_{K}
, it follows that

\overline{V}=\Hom_{G}(\mathcal{H}(G),V).

Note that the exactness of the functor
V\rightarrow\overline{V}

is equivalent to
\mathcal{H}(G)
being a projective
G
-module.

Proposition 8.3
.

For
V
a smooth representation of
G
, we have

\overline{V^{\vee}}=V^{*}.

Proof.

Suppose given
B\colon V\times W\to\mathbb{C}
a pairing of smooth
G
-representations inducing an injective map of representation
V\hookrightarrow W^{*}
.
Since
(W^{*})^{K}=(W^{K})^{*}
for any smooth representation
W
of
G
and any compact open subgroup
K\subset G
, we obtain

\overline{V}=\varprojlim(V^{K})\subset\varprojlim(W^{K})^{*}=(\varinjlim W^{K})^{*}=W^{*}.

In particular, taking
B\colon V^{\vee}\times V\to\mathbb{C}
to be
the natural pairing and using that
(V^{\vee})^{K}=(V^{*})^{K}
for all open compact subgroups
K
, we obtain the claimed equality.
∎

Applying it to the standard bilinear form
B\colon\mathcal{H}(G)\times\mathcal{H}(G)\to\mathbb{C},
we find

Corollary 8.4
.

The completion
\overline{\mathcal{H}}(G)
of
\mathcal{H}(G)
is the space of distributions
D
on
G
such that
e_{K}*D
is a compactly supported distribution on
G
for all idempotents
e_{K}
.

In particular, coupled with
Proposition

2.7
, we deduce:

Corollary 8.5
.

The center of
\mathcal{M}(G)
can be described as
\overline{\mathcal{H}}(G)^{G}
, i.e., the space of invariant distributions
D
on
G
such that
e_{K}*D
is a compactly supported distribution on
G
for all idempotents
e_{K}
.

Remark 8.6
.

It is known that for smooth representations taking invariants with respect to a unipotent subgroup
N
is not a well behaved functor (for example, it is not exact) and as such it is almost never used.
On the other hand, taking coinvariants under
N
leads to nice exact functors (Jacquet functors) which are extremely important in the theory.
The completion allows us to reconcile invariants and coinvariants for

as shown below.

Proposition 8.7
.

The functor
(-)^{N}\circ\overline{(-)}\circ(-)^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\operatorname{{Vect}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is exact.

Proof.

Notice that we have a natural isomorphism
(V^{*})^{N}=(V_{N})^{*}
for any representation
V
of
G
.
In particular, for a smooth representation
V
of
G
, using
Proposition

8.3
we obtain

\overline{V^{\vee}}^{N}=(V^{*})^{N}=(V_{N})^{*}.

Taking
N
-coinvariants is an exact functor because

is an increasing union of compact open subgroups.
So the functor
(-)^{N}\circ\overline{(-)}\circ(-)^{\vee}
identifies with the composition of two exact functors

which concludes the proof.
∎

In other words, taking
N
-invariants on completions of contragredient

In particular, we deduce

Corollary 8.8

The functor

is exact on admissible modules.

The usual tensor–Hom adjunction has the following analogue here:

Proposition 9.1

First we show the projectivity.

Let us now show that

We must show that there’s a non-zero morphism
\Pi_{[\rho]}\to\pi
.
Recall (paragraph before
Theorem

7.4
) that the category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})_{[\rho]}
is defined as consisting of those smooth representations of
{\widetilde{L}}
all whose irreducible subquotients belong to
[\rho]
.
Since
\Pi_{[\rho]}
is a projective representation of
{\widetilde{L}}
, it is enough to prove that
an irreducible representation belonging to
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})_{[\rho]}
, thus isomorphic to
\chi\rho
for some unramified character
\chi\in\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})
, appears as a quotient of
\Pi_{[\rho]}
.
By the adjunction
Lemma

3.2
, we have

\Hom_{{\widetilde{L}}}(\Pi_{[\rho]},\chi\rho)=\Hom_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}(\rho\mid_{{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}},\chi\rho\mid_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}),

and the latter is non-zero as it contains the identity.

In order to show that

\Pi_{[\rho]}

is of finite type, one notices that
{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}\backslash{\widetilde{L}}
is a discrete group and leads to a basis of
\operatorname{ind}_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}^{\widetilde{L}}(\rho\mid_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}})
indexed by these cosets and a basis of
\rho
.
The action of
{\widetilde{L}}
permutes this basis and hence, by choosing a finite generating set of
\rho\mid_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}
, we obtain a finite generating set of
\Pi_{[\rho]}
as an
{\widetilde{L}}
-representation, proving that
\Pi_{[\rho]}
is of finite type.
∎

We put
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}:=\End_{\widetilde{L}}(\Pi_{[\rho]})
. Generalities from
category theory (see
Proposition

2.8
), give us the following corollary:

Corollary 9.2
.

We have an equivalence of categories

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})_{[\rho]}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle\mapsto\Hom_{\widetilde{L}}(\Pi_{[\rho]},\pi).

Below we give a description of the algebra
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
by generators and relations.

Recall that
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}\subset\Hom_{\mathsf{gr}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C}^{\times})=\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})
denotes the stabilizer of
\rho
, where
\Lambda={\widetilde{L}}/{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}=\{\chi\in\Hom_{\mathsf{gr}}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C}^{\times})|\rho\otimes\chi\simeq\rho\}
.
The group
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
naturally acts on the complex torus
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})

by translations, and hence also on the
algebra of regular functions on
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}}))=\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]:=\mathcal{A}
which we denote by
\chi(f)={}^{\chi}f
for

\chi\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
. By Schur’s lemma, the isomorphism
\rho\otimes\chi\simeq\rho
defines a unique map – up to scalars –
from the space
underlying
\rho
to itself, hence provides us with a 2-cocycle
c\in H^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\rho},\mathbb{C})
, which in turn defines a
twisted group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho},c]
, the algebra generated by the intertwining operators from
\rho
to itself
through the isomorphisms
\rho\otimes\chi\simeq\rho
.

Proposition 9.3

(
[
Ber92
, Proposition 28]
)
.

The algebra
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
has the following presentation:

(1)

As a vector space
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}=\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho},c]
,

(2)

\mathcal{A}
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho},c]
are subalgebras,

(3)

fb_{\chi}=b_{\chi}{}^{\chi}f
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
\chi\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
, with

b_{\chi}=1\otimes\chi\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho},c]=\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
.

Proof.

The proof is fairly elementary but we will give a sketch.

First notice that by adjunction, we have a natural isomorphism of vector spaces

(9.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}=\End_{\widetilde{L}}(\operatorname{ind}_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}^{\widetilde{L}}(\rho|_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}))\simeq\End_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}(\rho|_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}})\otimes\mathcal{A}.

Since
\End_{\widetilde{L}}(\operatorname{ind}_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}^{\widetilde{L}}(\mathbb{C}))=\mathcal{A}
, and since
\operatorname{ind}_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}^{\widetilde{L}}(\rho|_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}})\simeq\rho\otimes\operatorname{ind}_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}^{\widetilde{L}}(\mathbb{C})
, this exhibits
\mathcal{A}
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
and moreover it shows that (
9.1
) is an isomorphism of right
\mathcal{A}
-modules.

Second, notice that
\Lambda={\widetilde{L}}/{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}
acts on
\End_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}(\rho|_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}})
and the action factors through the finite quotient
{\widetilde{L}}/Z({\widetilde{L}}){{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}
.
Moreover the action is diagonalizable and the eigenvalues (i.e., characters of
\Lambda
) that appear are, by definition, exactly the elements of
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
.
We deduce a canonical isomorphism of vector spaces

\End_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}(\rho|_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}})\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho}].

If
b_{\chi},b_{\mu}\in\End_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}(\rho|_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}})
correspond to
\chi,\mu\in\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
through the previous isomorphism, then their product belongs to the eigenspace corresponding to
\chi\mu
, i.e., we have
b_{\chi}b_{\mu}=c(\chi,\mu)b_{\chi\mu}
for some non-zero
c(\chi,\mu)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Associativity plus unity implies that
c(-,-)
is a two cocycle of
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
with values in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
This allows us to construct a twisted group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho},c]
and in turn provides us with an isomorphism of algebras

\End_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}(\rho|_{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}})\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho},c].

The last step consists in showing how the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}_{\rho},c]
interact inside
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
.
This is done by writing down explicitly the isomorphism in (
9.1
).
∎

Recall (see
[
Art99
, Proposition III.12.1]
) that an Azumaya algebra
R
over a commutative ring
Z
with identity
is a
Z
-algebra such that for some faithfully flat extension of commutative rings
Z\hookrightarrow Z^{\prime}
we have
R\otimes_{Z}Z^{\prime}\simeq M_{n}(Z^{\prime})
.
Equivalently,
R
is a projective module of constant rank over
Z
and the multiplication map
R\otimes_{Z}R^{op}\to\End_{Z}(R)
is an isomorphism of rings.

In particular, one deduces in a straightforward way the following proposition
from the above presentation of
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
.

Proposition 9.4
.

The natural inclusion
\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{G}_{\rho}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
identifies
\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{G}_{\rho}}
with the center
Z(\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]})
.
In particular
Z(\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]})
is isomorphic to a Laurent polynomial algebra.
Moreover
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
is an Azumaya algebra over its center.

For a different proof, see
[
BH03
, Proposition 8.1]
.
Note that our algebra
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
is a matrix algebra over the algebra
E_{G}
of
[
BH03
]
.

The next lemma is easy and is valid for any Azumaya algebra (see
[
Art99
, Proposition IV.2.1]
).

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
R
be an Azumaya algebra over its center
Z
which is a noetherian ring.
Then the trace map
\mathsf{tr}\colon R\to Z
provides an isomorphism of
R
-bimodules

R\to\Hom_{Z}(R,Z).

Proof.

First, this is obvious for
R=M_{n}(Z)
.
Using faithfully-flat descent, one first defines the trace map unambiguously for any Azumaya algebra

R
and then sees that it provides a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
R\otimes_{Z}R\to Z
which leads to the isomorphism asserted in the statement of the lemma.
∎

Applying this to our algebra
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
with center
\mathcal{Z}_{[\rho]}:=Z(\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]})
which is a Laurent polynomial ring, we get the following corollary (see (
2.8
)):

Corollary 9.6
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
-bimodules

\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{Z}_{[\rho]}}(\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]},\mathcal{Z}_{[\rho]}).

In other words,
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
satisfies condition (FsG) in Definition
2.43
.

Let us now apply what we have learned about
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
to prove the following proposition which plays a crucial role in the study of the homological duality functor (see
Theorem

10.2
).

Recall that for
\rho
a cuspidal representation of
{\widetilde{L}}
, we defined
d(\rho)
to be the split rank of the center of
L
(which is a Levi subgroup of
G
).

Proposition 9.7
.

For
V
, a
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
-module which is finite dimensional over
\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}}(V,\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]})=0\text{ for all }i\neq d(\rho).

Proof.

For simplicity we will put
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
and
\mathcal{Z}:=Z(\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]})
in this proof.
The forgetful functor

\displaystyle F\colon\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}\to\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{Z}

can also be written as
F=-\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}
where
{}_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{Z}}
is viewed as an
\mathcal{R}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{Z}
-bimodule.
As such,
F
has a right adjoint
F^{\prime}:=\Hom_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{Z}},-)
.

Since
\mathcal{R}
is an Azumaya algebra (
Proposition

9.4
), it is a projective
\mathcal{Z}
-module, hence both
F
and
F^{\prime}
are exact functors.
It follows that the adjunction extends to Ext groups.
Applying
Corollary

9.6
we therefore get

\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{R}}(V,\mathcal{R})=\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{R}}(V,F^{\prime}(\mathcal{Z}))=\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{Z}}(V,\mathcal{Z}),

and now we conclude the proof of the proposition by a well-known result in commutative algebra
since
\mathcal{Z}
is a Laurent polynomial algebra (by
Proposition

9.4
)
of Krull dimension
d(\rho)
and
V
is a

\mathcal{Z}
-module which is a
finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Remark 9.8
.

The statement holds with the same proof if we only assume that
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{Z}_{[\rho]}}(\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]},\mathcal{Z}_{[\rho]})
as right
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
-modules.

9.2.
Non-cuspidal blocks: a projective generator

Moving on to the block in
{\widetilde{G}}
corresponding to
\mathfrak{s}=[{\widetilde{L}},\rho]\in\mathcal{B}({\widetilde{G}})
, let us define the representation

\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}}:=\oplus_{{\widetilde{P}}}\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\Pi_{[\rho]})=\oplus_{{\widetilde{P}}}\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{{\widetilde{G}}}(\operatorname{ind}_{{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}}^{{\widetilde{L}}}(\rho|_{{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}}))

where the sum ranges over all parabolics
{\widetilde{P}}
with Levi
{\widetilde{L}}
(it is a finite sum).

Remark 9.9
.

Actually it would be enough to consider
\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\Pi_{[\rho]})
for a single parabolic as this module turns out to be (non-canonically) independent of the parabolic containing
{\widetilde{L}}
.
See
[
Ber92
, p. 96]
) for a proof as well as

[
Ren10
, VI.10.1]
for some more details.
However, we will not need it.
One advantage of taking the direct sum instead of a single parabolic induction is that it makes the isomorphism from
Proposition

10.5
canonical.

Lemma 9.10
.

The module
\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}}
is a finite type projective-generator of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
.

Proof.

The projectivity follows at once as a consequence of the second-adjointness
Corollary

8.16
together with
Proposition

9.1
: the functor
\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{{\widetilde{G}}}
has an exact right adjoint, hence it preserves projectives and
\Pi_{[\rho]}
is projective.
Since parabolic induction preserves finite type (see
Theorem

7.16
) we have that
\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}}
is also finitely generated.

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
be a non-zero representation of
{\widetilde{G}}
.
We need to show that
\Hom_{{\widetilde{G}}}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}},\pi)
is non zero and for that,
since
\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}}
is projective, it is enough to consider the case
\pi
irreducible.
By definition of the block
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
,
\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}^{-}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\pi)
is non-zero in the block

\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})_{[\rho]}
.
The second-adjointness
Theorem

8.15
and the fact that
\Pi_{[\rho]}
is a generator of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})_{[\rho]}
(see
Proposition

9.1
) imply that

\Hom_{\widetilde{G}}(\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\Pi_{[\rho]}),\pi)=\Hom_{\widetilde{L}}(\Pi_{[\rho]},\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}^{-}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\pi))\neq 0

and hence
\Hom_{\widetilde{G}}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}},\pi)\neq 0
.
∎

An abelian category admitting all coproducts and having a finitely generated (compact) progenerator is equivalent to the category of right modules over its endomorphism ring (see
Proposition

2.8
).
Putting
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}:=\End_{{\widetilde{G}}}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}})
, we thus deduce the following:

Proposition 9.11
.

The functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\mapsto\Hom_{{\widetilde{G}}}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}},V)

is an equivalence of categories sending finite length representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
in

\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}

to
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
-modules which are finite dimensional over
\mathbb{C}
.

9.3.
Center of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}

This section plays no role in this work but we record it for completeness.
We give the description of the center of the algebra
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
for
\mathfrak{s}=[{\widetilde{L}},\rho]\in\mathcal{B}({\widetilde{G}})
.
In turn this determines the center of the block
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
(see
Proposition

9.11
,
Remark

2.4
and
Example

2.5
).

Recall that
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})
acts on the irreducible cuspidals
\operatorname{Irr}({\widetilde{L}})_{\rm cs}
and we denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
the stabilizer of
\rho
.
Denote by
W_{[\rho]}:=\Stab_{W_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{G}}}}([\rho])
, the stabilizer of the inertia class
[\rho]
in the relative Weyl group
W_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{G}}}
.

Recall also the
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice
\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})={\widetilde{L}}/{{\widetilde{L}}^{\circ}}=\Lambda(L)=L/{L^{\circ}}
.
The characters of
\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})
form the torus
\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})
whose ring of regular functions is identified with the group algebra of
\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})
:

\mathbb{C}[\Lambda({\widetilde{L}})]=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})).

The linear case of the following theorem is due to
[
Ber84
, 2.12]
but the proof works also for finite central extensions.
Another reference is
[
Ren10
, VI.10.4]
.

Theorem 9.12
.

The algebra
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
contains the Laurent polynomial algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})/\mathcal{G}_{\rho})
as a subalgebra and it is finite as a left (or right)-module over it.
Moreover, the center of
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
is nothing but

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}})=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{X}({\widetilde{L}})/\mathcal{G}_{\rho})^{W_{[\rho]}}.

Since
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}})
has no non-trivial idempotents, we immediately get the following corollary.

Corollary 9.13
.

The category
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
is indecomposable.

9.4.
Revisiting Howe’s theorem

Since Bernstein’s center plays a prominent role in the theory, it seems worthwhile mentioning the following applications whose proofs are parallel to the linear case. This section is only for completeness, it is not used in the sequel.

We say that a smooth representation
V
of
{\widetilde{G}}
is
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
-admissible (where
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
is the Bernstein
center of the category of smooth representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
)
if for any open compact
{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{G}}
, the
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
-module
V^{{\widetilde{K}}}
is finitely generated.
We have

Theorem 9.14

(
[
Ren10
, VI.10.8]
)
.

A smooth representation
V
of
{\widetilde{G}}
is of finite type if and only if it is
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
-admissible and it has only finitely many non-zero Bernstein components.

We say that a representation is
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
-finite if the action of
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
on it factors through an ideal of finite codimension in
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
.

The following is a generalization of Howe’s theorem.

Theorem 9.15
.

Let
V
be a smooth representation of
{\widetilde{G}}
.
Any two of the following properties implies the third, and then
V
is of finite length:

(1)

V
is of finite type,

(2)

V
is admissible,

(3)

V
is
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
-finite.

Proof.

Howe’s theorem says that (1)+(2) imply finite length and hence by Schur’s lemma we have (3).

Assume (1)+(3).
Theorem

9.14
implies that
V
is
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
-admissible. Hence for any open compact
{\widetilde{K}}\leq{\widetilde{G}}
the invariants
V^{\widetilde{K}}
are finitely generated over
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
and now (3) implies that
V^{\widetilde{K}}
is finite dimensional.

Assume (2)+(3). Clearly
V
is
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
admissible and since the action of
\mathcal{Z}({\widetilde{G}})
, a unitary ring, factorizes through a finite codimension ideal we get that
V
has only finitely many non-zero Bernstein components. We conclude by
Theorem

9.14
.
∎

10.
Homological duality

10.1.
Vanishing

Here we prove the vanishing part of our main result, namely
Theorem

1.3
(
1
),
following the strategy in
[
Ber92
]
.

The main ingredients are
Section

7
on Bernstein’s decomposition, Bernstein’s second adjoint
Theorem

8.15
,
and the vanishing result from
Proposition

9.7
.

Consider

\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{fg}
the full subcategory of smooth representations of
{\widetilde{G}}
which are finitely generated.
It is still an abelian category and the parabolic induction and restriction functors preserve it.
The homological duality is defined as the following functor between derived categories

(10.1)

\displaystyle D_{h}\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{b}_{fg}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}}))\to\mathcal{D}^{b}_{fg}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}}))^{op}

\displaystyle\pi\mapsto\RHom_{\widetilde{G}}(\pi,\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}}))

where
\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})
denotes the Hecke algebra of
{\widetilde{G}}
.
The structure of left
{\widetilde{G}}
representation is through the right action of
\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})
on itself and through the involution
f\mapsto\breve{f}\colon\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})\to\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})
defined by
\breve{f}(g):=f(g^{-1})
.

Remark 10.1
.

(1)

Notice that
D_{h}
lands indeed in the bounded derived category because the category of smooth representations
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
has finite global dimension (
Section

3.4
).

(2)

One could also define
D_{h}
without the assumption on finitely generated but then
D_{h}
would land outside smooth modules, indeed in the category of all
\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})
-modules.
We could come back to smooth modules simply by taking the smooth part (see the paragraph after
Proposition

8.9
).

The following theorem is the most important property of the homological duality functor and is due to Bernstein.
Fix
\mathfrak{s}=[{\widetilde{L}},\rho]\in\mathcal{B}({\widetilde{G}})
, a cuspidal datum.

Theorem 10.2
.

If
\pi\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
is of finite length, then
D_{h}(\pi)
has cohomology only in degree
d(\mathfrak{s})
.

Proof.

(following
[
Ber92
, Theorem 31]
)
We need to show that
\Ext^{i}_{\widetilde{G}}(\pi,\mathcal{H})=0
for
i\neq d(\mathfrak{s})
, where we put for short
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})
.

Decompose the representation
\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})
according to Bernstein’s decomposition,

Theorem

7.14
.
Clearly only the component
\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
is important to us.
Moreover, since
\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})
is a projective representation (
Remark

3.3
),
and
\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}}
is a projective generator of
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}
(see
Lemma

9.10
), it is enough to show:

(10.2)

\displaystyle\Ext^{i}_{\widetilde{G}}(\pi,\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}})=0,\text{ for all }i\neq d(\mathfrak{s}).

Recall from §
9.2
that
\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}}=\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{{\widetilde{G}}}(\Pi_{[\rho]})
.
Since parabolic induction and restriction are exact functors, the Frobenius adjunction gives

(10.3)

\displaystyle\Ext^{i}_{\widetilde{G}}(\pi,\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}})=\Ext^{i}_{\widetilde{L}}(\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\pi),\Pi_{[\rho]}).

The representation
\pi
is of finite length, hence by
Corollary

6.12
the same is true of
\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}(\pi)
.
Through the categorical equivalence
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})_{[\rho]}\simeq\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
(see
Proposition

9.11
), the vanishing of (
10.3
) follows from

\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}}(V,\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]})=0,\text{ for all }i\neq d(\rho)=d(\mathfrak{s}),

for all finite dimensional
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
-modules
V
.
This was proved in
Proposition

9.7
.
∎

Given
\pi\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathsf{fl}}
a finite length representation in a given block, we will denote by
\mathbb{D}_{h}(\pi)
the representation
H^{d(\mathfrak{s})}(D_{h}(\pi))
.

10.2.
Interaction with induction and restriction

The objective of this section is to investigate how does the (full, derived) homological duality commute with parabolic induction and restriction.
We follow the proof of the linear case from
[
Ber92
, Theorem 31(4,5)]
.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
{\widetilde{P}}={\widetilde{L}}N
be a parabolic with Levi decomposition in
{\widetilde{G}}
.
We have the following natural isomorphisms of functors when restricted to the bounded derived category of finitely generated smooth
{\widetilde{G}}
-modules
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{fg}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}}))
:

(1)

D_{h}\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}\simeq\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}^{-}}^{\widetilde{G}}D_{h}
,

(2)

D_{h}\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}\simeq\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{P}}}^{\widetilde{G}}D_{h}
.

Proof.

To shorten the notation, we write
\mathbf{i}_{\widetilde{P}}
and
\mathbf{r}_{\widetilde{P}}
for the parabolic induction and restriction functors.

Since all objects in
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{fg}
admit a finite resolution by finitely generated
projective objects, it is enough to define and prove the required isomorphisms for finitely
generated projective objects.
Therefore, using the second adjointness and the Frobenius reciprocity,
we see that the isomorphisms in part (1) and in part (2) of the proposition are equivalent to proving natural isomorphisms

(10.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{i}_{{\widetilde{P}}^{-}}\Hom_{\widetilde{L}}(V,\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{L}}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\widetilde{L}}(V,\mathbf{r}_{{\widetilde{P}}^{-}}(\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}}))),

(10.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{\widetilde{P}}\Hom_{\widetilde{G}}(W,\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\widetilde{G}}(W,\mathbf{i}_{\widetilde{P}}(\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{L}}))),

for
V\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{L}})
and
W\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
, both projective, and finitely generated representations, and where
we have considered
\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{L}})

be a cuspidal datum.
Then, under the equivalence
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}}\simeq\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
, we have the following commutative square of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}})^{op}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}})^{op}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{GS}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{GS}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Proof.

Under an equivalence of categories, the centers are isomorphic.
In this proof we put
Z:=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
So the two
D_{GS}
functors in the diagram are both
\RHom_{Z}(-,\omega_{Z}^{\circ})
.

Corollary

12.2
ensures that the target of the left vertical arrow is correct.
Similarly, use
Corollary

2.41
together with
10.8
for the right vertical arrow.

We have to show that, for
V\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
, there are natural isomorphisms

(12.1)

\displaystyle\RHom_{Z}(\Hom_{\widetilde{G}}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}},V),\omega_{Z}^{\circ})\simeq\Hom_{\widetilde{G}}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}},\RHom_{Z}(V,\omega_{Z}^{\circ})).

Notice that
\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}}
is projective, so we don’t need to derive the Hom space from it.

Using
Lemma

2.16
and
Proposition

10.5
we have
\Hom_{\widetilde{G}}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}},V)\simeq\Pi_{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}({\widetilde{G}})}V
.
We can rewrite the left hand-side of (
12.1
) and use tensor-hom adjunction to conclude.
∎

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
be an irreducible cuspidal representation and put as usual
d=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}({\widetilde{G}}/{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}})
.
It has already been proved that homological duality restricts to a contravariant functor

\mathbb{D}_{h}:=[d]\circ D_{h}\colon\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\rho]}^{\mathsf{fl}}\to(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\rho^{\vee}]}^{\mathsf{fl}})^{op},

see
Corollary

10.6
and
Theorem

10.2
.
We can see that it is not new:

Corollary 12.4
.

For
\rho\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
an irreducible cuspidal representation of
{\widetilde{G}}
, and
d=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}({\widetilde{G}}/{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}})
,
the above functor
\mathbb{D}_{h}
is isomorphic to the contragredient.

Proof.

The block
\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\rho]}
is equivalent to the category
\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
and this equivalence commutes with homological duality (see
Proposition

10.10
) as well as with contragredient (see
Proposition

10.9
).
Moreover, it sends finite length
{\widetilde{G}}
-modules to finite length
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
-modules.
In order to prove the claim it is therefore enough to do it for
\mathbf{mod}\mathchar 45\relax\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
.

The center of
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
is a Laurent polynomial algebra of dimension
d
(see
Proposition

9.4
) and moreover
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
satisfies the condition (
2.8
) (a particular case of
the condition (FsG) in Definition
2.43
) by
Corollary

9.6
.
Applying
Corollary

2.49
we conclude.
∎

The same argument gives the following corollary.

Corollary 12.5
.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})
be an irreducible cuspidal representation and
d=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}}({\widetilde{G}}/{{\widetilde{G}}^{\circ}})
.
Then on the cuspidal block
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{fg}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{[\rho]})
, we have a natural isomorphism of functors

[d]\circ D_{h}\simeq D_{GS/\mathcal{Z}}.

Remark 12.6
.

If
G
is a torus then all smooth representations of a finite central extension
{\widetilde{G}}
are cuspidal and therefore the above corollaries apply to
all
finite length smooth
{\widetilde{G}}
-modules.
Moreover, in this situation the Grothendieck–Serre duality and homological duality agree (up to shift) on all smooth modules.

More generally, the following gives a necessary and sufficient condition for homological duality to be isomorphic to Grothendieck–Serre duality:

Corollary 12.7
.

On a block
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{M}({\widetilde{G}})_{\mathfrak{s}})
, we have that
[d(\mathfrak{s})]\circ D_{h}\simeq D_{GS/\mathcal{Z}}
if and only if
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
satisfies condition (FsG) in Definition
2.43
.

Proof.

Given
Proposition

12.3
, this is simply a restatement of
Corollary

2.48
.
∎

Example 12.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}=[{\widetilde{L}},\rho]
be a cuspidal datum such that the stabilizer of the inertia class
[\rho]
in the relative Weyl group is trivial, i.e., there is no non-trivial
w\in W({\widetilde{L}},{\widetilde{G}})
such that
{}^{w}\rho\simeq\rho\chi
for some unramified character
\chi
of
L
.
Then by a result of Roche
[
Roc02
, Theorem 3.1]
, parabolic induction induces an equivalence of blocks
\mathcal{M}(L)_{[\rho]}\to\mathcal{M}(G)_{\mathfrak{s}}
and therefore a Morita equivalence of
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
As
\mathcal{R}_{[\rho]}
is Azumaya by
Proposition

9.4
, the same is true of
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
By the general property of Azumaya algebras recalled in
Lemma

9.5
, we find that the algebra
\mathcal{R}_{\mathfrak{s}}
satisfies condition
(FsG).

Example 12.9
.

By
Corollary

12.2
,
the Grothendieck–Serre duality on irreducible representations of
{\widetilde{G}}

is simply the contragredient. On the other hand, as it is well known, the homological duality
(same as Aubert-Zelevinsky duality for irreducible representations) interchanges the trivial representation and the Steinberg representation
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
. Therefore the Corollary above is not true for all representations of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
which means that
the condition (FsG) in Definition
2.43
does not hold for the Iwahori block for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)
.

Let us also note the particular case of a free abelian group
\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. (The
abelian category of representations of
\Gamma
is the Iwahori block of
a split torus over any
p
-adic field, so in considering this example, we
are not going outside the realm of
p
-adic groups!)
The category of representations of
\Gamma

is the same as the category of representations of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\simeq\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{d}^{\pm}]
.
From
Corollary

2.49
we get:

Corollary 12.10
.

The Grothendieck–Serre duality, (shifted by
d
) homological duality and contragredient duality all agree on finite length
\Gamma
-modules:

D_{GS}=[d]\circ D_{h}=(-)^{*}\text{ as functors on }\mathcal{M}(\Gamma)^{\mathsf{fl}}.

Equivalently, for a finite length module
M
over
A=\mathbb{C}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dots,X_{d}^{\pm}]
,

\Ext_{A}^{d}(M,A)\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(M,\mathbb{C}),

an isomorphism of
A
-modules, an assertion which can also be made for a finite length module
M
over
A=\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\dots,X_{d}]
.

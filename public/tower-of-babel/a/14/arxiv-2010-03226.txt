BPS Skyrme Submodels of The Five-dimensional Skyrme Model
Emir Syahreza Fadhilla 1, Bobby Eka Gunara 1, Ardian Nata Atmaja 2

Abstract
In this paper, we search for the BPS skyrmions in some BPS submodels of the generalized Skyrme model in five-dimensional spacetime using the BPS Lagrangian method. We focus on the static solutions of the Bogomolny’s equations and their corresponding energies with topological charge B > 0 B>0 is an integer. We consider two main cases based on the symmetry of the effective Lagrangian of the BPS submodels, i.e. the spherically symmetric and non-spherically symmetric cases. For the spherically symmetric case, we find two BPS submodels. The first BPS submodels consist of a potential term and a term proportional to the square of the topological current. The second BPS submodels consist of only the Skyrme term. The second BPS submodel has BPS skyrmions with the same topological charge B > 1 B>1 , but with different energies, that we shall call “topological degenerate” BPS skyrmions. It also has the usual BPS skyrmions with equal energies, if the topological charge is a prime number. Another interesting feature of the BPS skyrmions, with B > 1 B>1 , in this BPS submodel, is that these BPS skyrmions have non-zero pressures in the angular direction. For the non-spherically symmetric case, there is only one BPS submodel, which is similar to the first BPS submodel in the spherically symmetric case. We find that the BPS skyrmions depend on a constant k k and for a particular value of k k we obtain the BPS skyrmions of the first BPS submodel in the spherically symmetric case. The total static energy and the topological charge of these BPS skyrmions also depend on this constant. We also show that all the results found in this paper satisfy the full field equations of motions of the corresponding BPS submodels.



I
Introduction

The Skyrme model was initially designed to be a model of the nucleon
Skyrme 1961
;
Skyrme 1962
. It has topological soliton solutions, known as skyrmions, which can be interpreted as baryons. As topological solitons, the skyrmions are characterized by a conserved topological charge
B
which is identified as the baryon number
Witten 1983
;
Dothan and Biedenharn 1987
. The energies of these skyrmions satisfy the Faddeev-Bogomolny lower energy bound
E\geq|B|

Faddeev 1976
. Unfortunately, this bound can not be saturated by skyrmions with
B\neq 0

Manton 1987
. Under a particular spherically symmetric ansatz, it has been shown that a unit skyrmion (the nucleon), with
B=1
, has an energy about 23% higher than its Faddeev-Bogomolny lower bound
Adkins et al. 1983
. For other skyrmions with
B>1
, their binding energies are even higher than the multiple unit skyrmions and hence mostly unstable
Manton and Sutcliffe 2007
. Nevertheless, skyrmions are not only important in high energy and nuclear physics but also play an important role in other areas of physics from solid-state physics to nonlinear optics
Donati et al. 2016
;
Khawaja and Stoof 2001
;
Baskaran 2011
;
Kiselev et al. 2011
;
Fukuda and Žumer 2011
.

Several attempts have been made to find (more) stable skyrmions, in particular, with
B>1
. These may include imposing non-spherically symmetric ansatzs
Braaten et al. 1990
;
Hitchin et al. 1995
;
Houghton and Sutcliffe 1996
. Other approaches are by considering additional higher-order terms other than the Skyrme term. A good mathematical description of skyrmion, found in
Manton 1987
, describes the skyrmions as a topologically nontrivial map from a Riemannian manifold to another. This mathematical description becomes the key to generalize the Skyrme model by adding the higher-order term, first done in
Marleau 1990
, and it has been turned recently into a combinatoric problem in
Gudnason and Nitta 2017
. A simple extension to the Skyrme model is by adding a term proportional to the square of the topological current density
Adkins and Nappi 1985
. This additional term turns out to be responsible for stabilizing the skyrmions
Jackson et al. 1985
. Furthermore, BPS skyrmions, which saturate the Faddeev-Bogomolny bound, are found in a model consist only of this term and a potential term
Adam et al. 2010a
. Although initially the construction of higher-order term is given for four-dimensional spacetime, a possible generalization in higher dimensional spacetime is demonstrated in
Brihaye et al. 2017
. In that paper, they only studied unit skyrmions (static and stationary), with
B=1
, since the main goal was to find and study rotating black holes with Skyrme hair in five-dimensional spacetime.

The main purpose of this work is to find other possible BPS skyrmions in the generalized five-dimensional Skyrme model considered in
Brihaye et al. 2017
. In particular, we will try to find BPS skyrmions, for any integer
B
, in some (genuine) submodels of the generalized five-dimensional Skyrme model via the BPS Lagrangian method, which was initially used to find BPS vortices in three-dimensional spacetime models
Atmaja 2017
. The method not only succeeded in re-deriving the known BPS vortices, but it was also able to find new Bogomolny equations for other BPS vortices
Atmaja 2020a
. It has also been used to find the BPS monopoles and dyons in various
SU(2)
Yang-Mills-Higgs models in the four-dimensional spacetime
Atmaja and Prasetyo 2018
;
Atmaja 2020b
. The reason for using this method is that it has been successfully used to find the BPS skyrmions in some (genuine and nongenuine) submodels of the generalized Skyrme model in the four-dimensional spacetime
Atmaja et al. 2020

Static
O(5)
Skyrme Model

As we know, the standard static Skyrme Model in

\mathcal{S}_{4}=-\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\gamma_{1}g^{ij}~Tr(R_{i}R_{j})+\gamma_{2}g^{ij}g^{kl}~Tr([R_{i},R_{k}][R_{j},R_{l}])\right]~,

\displaystyle\phi^{a}_{i}\phi^{a}_{j}g^{ij}~,

(2)

\displaystyle\phi^{a}_{[i}\phi^{b}_{j]}\phi^{a}_{[k}\phi^{b}_{l]}g^{ij}g^{kl}~,

Gudnason and Nitta 2017

Brihaye et al. 2017
.

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}+\dots+\lambda_{d}^{2}~,

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{3}^{2}+\dots+\lambda_{d-1}^{2}\lambda_{d}^{2}~,

\displaystyle L_{d}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}\dots\lambda_{d}^{2}~.

(7)

In four-dimensional case (

Adam et al. 2010b
. By following the prescription given in the four-dimensional model, we can construct

for arbitrary spacetime dimensions with

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F^{2n}}{\left(n!\right)^{2}}~,

(8)

\displaystyle F^{2n}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\phi^{a_{1}}_{[i_{1}}\dots\phi^{a_{n}}_{i_{n}]}\phi^{a_{1}}_{[j_{1}}\dots\phi^{a_{n}}_{j_{n}]}g^{i_{1}j_{1}}\dots g^{i_{n}j_{n}}~,

(9)

where

This is just an

(11)

\displaystyle\left(\left(1+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}\right)\left(n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}\right)+n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}\right)\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle+n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}V\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{X}^{2}}{4\sin^{6}\xi\left(r\right)}~.

(41)

There are two non-trivial solutions that simultaneously solve these equations. The first one is

{Q^{2}_{X}\over 4}=\left(1+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}\right)\left(n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}\right)\lambda^{2}_{2}\sin^{6}\xi,

(42)

with
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0
, that, after substituting them into equation (
34
), gives us the Bogomolny equation

\xi^{\prime}=\pm\gamma(n_{1},n_{2})\frac{\sin\xi}{r}~,

(43)

where
\gamma(n_{1},n_{2})
is defined as

\gamma(n_{1},n_{2})\equiv\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}}{1+n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}}}~.

(44)

The second one is

{Q^{2}_{X}\over 4}=n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2}V\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}\sin^{6}\xi~,

(45)

with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=0
, whose corresponding Bogomolny equation is given by

\xi^{\prime}=\pm~\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{4}}}\frac{r^{3}}{n_{1}n_{2}\sin^{3}\xi}\sqrt{V(\xi)}~.

(46)

Both cases show the dependence of the resulting Bogomolny’s equation on the topological charge
B=n_{1}n_{2}
. Let us discuss the features and solutions of those Bogomolny’s equations in the next subsections.

III.1
BPS Skyrme submodel with
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0

Solutions to equation (
43
) are

\xi=2\tan^{-1}\left[\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{\pm\gamma}\right]~,

(47)

with
r_{0}
is a real integration constant. Here we only pick the
(-)
signed solutions that satisfy the boundary conditions (
30
), while the
(+)
signed solutions are related to the anti-BPS skyrmions, with
B<0
. These solutions, with
n_{1}=n_{2}=1
or a unit topological charge
B=1
, coincide with the solutions studied in
Brihaye et al. 2017
. The solutions are smooth functions up to the second-order derivative in the whole domain, thus everywhere regular. The total static energy density of a BPS skyrmion, with topological charge
B
, in this model is given by

\varepsilon_{B}=-T^{0}_{0}=32\lambda_{2}\left(B^{2}+n^{2}+2B\right)\frac{r^{4(\gamma-1)}r_{0}^{4\gamma}}{\left(r^{2\gamma}+r_{0}^{2\gamma}\right)^{4}}~.

(48)

with
n\equiv n_{2}-n_{1}
. One may ask if these BPS Skyrmions saturate the total energy bound. We can show that they do saturate the energy bound at the effective level of total energy. For that purpose we write explicitly the Bogomolny equation (
43
) as follows

\sqrt{1+n^{2}+2B}\xi^{\prime}\mp\sqrt{B^{2}+n^{2}+2B}\frac{\sin\xi}{r}=0.

(49)

In the static case, the total energy is proportional to the action,
E=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{4}\int\sqrt{-g}~d^{4}x~F^{4}
. Substituting the ansatz (
14
) into (
25
), the total energy becomes

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\lambda_{2}\over 4}\int\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\left[(1+n^{2}+2B)\xi^{\prime 2}+(B^{2}+n^{2}+2B)\frac{\sin^{2}\xi}{r^{2}}\right]\frac{\sin^{2}\xi}{r^{2}}

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\lambda_{2}\over 4}\int\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\left[\left(\sqrt{1+n^{2}+2B}\xi^{\prime}\mp\sqrt{B^{2}+n^{2}+2B}\frac{\sin\xi}{r}\right)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\pm 2\sqrt{(1+n^{2}+2B)(B^{2}+n^{2}+2B)}\frac{\sin\xi}{r}\xi^{\prime}\right]\frac{\sin^{2}\xi}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\pm{\lambda_{2}\over 2}\int\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\sqrt{(1+n^{2}+2B)(B^{2}+n^{2}+2B)}\frac{\sin^{3}\xi}{r^{3}}\xi^{\prime},

where the last line is the boundary term.
Therefore, the total energy of the BPS Skyrmions is

E_{B}=\pm\lambda_{2}\pi^{2}\int_{\xi(\infty)}^{\xi(0)}\sqrt{(1+n^{2}+2B)(B^{2}+n^{2}+2B)}\sin^{3}\xi~d\xi.

(51)

The
T^{1}_{1}
component of the stress-energy-momentum tensor vanishes while the remaining diagonal components have the form

\displaystyle T^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{\sin^{4}\xi}{r^{4}}\frac{(n^{2}+2B)(1-B^{2})}{1+n^{2}+2B}~,

(52)

\displaystyle T^{3}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{4}\xi}{r^{4}}\left[n\sqrt{n^{2}+4B}(1+\gamma^{2})+\frac{(n^{2}+2B)(B^{2}-1)}{1+n^{2}+2B}\right]~,

(53)

\displaystyle T^{4}_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{4}\xi}{r^{4}}\left[-n\sqrt{n^{2}+4B}(1+\gamma^{2})+\frac{(n^{2}+2B)(B^{2}-1)}{1+n^{2}+2B}\right]~.

(54)

For
B=1
, which also implies
n=0
, all the remaining diagonal components of the energy-momentum tensor vanish, while they are non-zero for
B>1
in general. The latter cases indicate that we might have BPS skyrmions with non-zero pressures. Using (
48
), the total static energy of the BPS skyrmion is

E_{B}=\frac{16}{3}\lambda_{2}\pi^{2}\sqrt{\left(1+n^{2}+2B\right)\left(B^{2}+n^{2}+2B\right)}~.

(55)

This is in agreement with the result found in
Brihaye et al. 2017
, that the bound energy is linear in
B
, because
\frac{\sqrt{(1+n^{2}+2B)(B^{2}+n^{2}+2B)}}{3}\geq B
and the equality occurs only for
B=1
.
We can see a possible repulsive interaction in this BPS submodel between
B
unit-solitons in the multi-soliton solution, with
B>1
, because the difference between the energy of the multi-soliton solution (
55
) and
B
times the unit-soliton energy, which is
BE_{1}=16\lambda_{2}\pi^{2}B
, are positive and monotonically increasing. For example the energy of
B=2
multi-soliton is
E_{2}=16\sqrt{6}~\lambda_{2}\pi^{2}
which is higher than the total energy of the two unit-solitons,
2E_{1}=32\lambda_{2}\pi^{2}
. This repulsive effect implies the possibility of a soliton creation in the usual collision set-up, similar to the one demonstrated in
Vachaspati 2011
.

Furthermore, for some fixed values of
B>1
, there are more than one BPS skyrmions in which their energies, determined by different values of
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, are not equal. As an example for
B=4
equation (
43
) has two different solutions and thus yields two unequal energies:
E_{4}=32\sqrt{6}\lambda_{2}\pi^{2}
, with
n=2-2=0
, and
E_{4}=16\sqrt{66}\lambda_{2}\pi^{2}
, with
n=1-4=-3
and
n=4-1=3
. The graphical representation for this statement is given in figure
1
. We shall call these types of skyrmions “topological degenerate” BPS skyrmions which are defined as BPS skyrmions with the same topological charges but different energies. This feature arises from how many different ways we can assign integer values on
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
resulting in the same value of
B
. Interestingly, there are also BPS skyrmions without these degenerate topological numbers in which their topological charges are prime numbers.

.

III.2
BPS Skyrme submodel with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=0

In this submodel, solutions of the Bogomolny’s equation (
46
) depend on the ratio of the coupling constants,
\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{4}}
, and also on the non-zero potential. In order to find the solutions of the Bogomolny’s equation (
46
), we choose the potential to take the following form
V(\xi)=\left(1-\cos\xi\right)^{q}
, with
q\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, such that the Bogomolny’s equation (
46
) can be rewritten as
2
2

2

Here again we pick the negative sign solutions that satisfy the boundary conditions (
30
).

\sin^{3}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)\cos^{3}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)\xi^{\prime}=-\sqrt{\frac{2^{2(q-3)}\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{4}}}\frac{r^{3}\sin^{q}\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)}{B}.

(56)

The total static energy for this submodel, related to this form of potential, is given by

E_{B}=128\pi^{2}\sqrt{2\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}}\frac{B}{(q+4)(q+6)}.

(57)

Now, let us introduce a dimensionless variable
z=\left(\frac{2^{2(q-6)}\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{4}
and a function
u=\frac{\xi}{2}
. Substituting them into equation (
56
) yields

\frac{du}{dz}=-\frac{\sin^{q-3}u}{B\cos^{3}u}.

(58)

A solution to this equation, that satisfies the boundary condition (
30
) at the origin,
u={\pi\over 2}
, is given by

\frac{\sin^{4-q}u}{4-q}-\frac{\sin^{6-q}u}{6-q}=-\frac{z}{B}+\frac{2}{\left(4-q\right)\left(6-q\right)}.

(59)

Unfortunately, this solution does not satisfy the boundary condition (
30
) at infinity,
u=0
, for
0<q<4
. For these values of
q
, the function
u
is equal to zero at some finite point
z=z_{cut-off}
and beyond this point
u
becomes complex numbers. Therefore, for each value of
q
, we may define a cut-off point at
z=z_{cut-off}
such that
\xi(z_{cut-off})=0
, which is given by

z_{cut-off}=\begin{cases}\frac{2B}{(4-q)(6-q)}&0<q<4\\
\infty&q\geq 4\end{cases},

(60)

Since the constant
\xi=0
is also a solution of the differential equation (
56
), we may then construct the compacton type solutions for
0<q<4
, while for
q\geq 4
we have regular solutions. Unfortunately, it is difficult to write explicitly the function
u
, or
\xi
, from the solution (
59
) in general and so we have to solve it numerically. Notice that equation (
59
) is singular for
q=4,6
and so we can not use this equation as their solution. Below we will find their solutions starting by writing the explicit form of their Bogomolny’s equations (
58
).

Let us now consider the case of
q=4
in which the potential is
V(\xi)=\left(1-\cos\xi\right)^{4}
. The Bogomolny’s equation (
58
) is explicitly written as

\frac{du}{dz}=-\frac{\sin u}{B\cos^{3}u}

(61)

The right hand side of equation (
61
) is zero near infinity,
z\rightarrow\infty
. This allows
\xi(r)
to converge at this limit, then satisfies the boundary condition near the infinity.
Solving equation (
61
) and imposing the boundary condition at the origin,
u\left(0\right)=\pi/2
, gives

\ln(\sin u)-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}u+\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{z}{B}

(62)

Unfortunately, equation (
62
) is a transcendental equation for
u(z)
, and so we have to use a numerical calculation to find its solution.

Another case is
q=6
that has the potential
V(\xi)=\left(1-\cos\xi\right)^{6}
. In this case the Bogomolny’s equation (
58
) becomes

\frac{du}{dz}=-\frac{\sin^{3}u}{B\cos^{3}u}

(63)

This equation has the same convergence feature as equation (
61
) with a higher convergence rate at
z\rightarrow\infty
.
Integration on (
63
) gives the solution we need. Again by imposing boundary condition at the origin,
u\left(0\right)=\pi/2
, we get

-\ln(\sin u)-\frac{1}{2\sin^{2}u}+\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{z}{B}

(64)

It is easy to observe that equation (
64
) is also transcendental in
u(z)
so we need to use numerical calculation to find its solution. All numerical calculations are given in Figure
3
. We can see that an everywhere regular solution starts to emerge from
q=4
.

) for

IV
BPS Skyrme Submodel in the Non-Spherically Symmetric Lagrangian Density

In this section, we consider the ansatz (
14
) without fixing all the effective fields. It is easy to see that the effective Lagrangian (
15
) also depends explicitly on the polar coordinate
\theta
besides the radial coordinate
r
. In this case, we use the prescription in
Atmaja et al. 2020
as such the general BPS Lagrangian density has the form

L_{BPS}=\frac{1}{r^{3}\sin\theta\cos\theta}\left(Q_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}Q_{i}X_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j=i+1}^{4}Q_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}\right)~,

(65)

where
X_{i}
are functions of
\xi^{\prime}(r)
,
f^{\prime}(\theta)
,
g_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{1})
and
g_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{2})
to obtain a set of Bogomolny’s equations.

The submodel discussed here is obtained by taking
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=0
such that the Lagrangian (
15
) simply becomes

L_{eff}=L_{0}+L_{4}=\lambda_{0}V\left(\phi\right)+\lambda_{4}\left[\frac{1}{r^{6}\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta}\frac{f^{2}}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{4}}\sin^{6}\xi\left(\xi^{\prime}~f^{\prime}~g_{1}^{\prime}~g_{2}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right]~.

(66)

Taking the
L_{BPS}
to be

L_{BPS}=\frac{Q_{X}}{r^{3}\sin\theta\cos\theta}X~,

(67)

with
X=\xi^{\prime}f^{\prime}g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime}
, the
L_{eff}-L_{BPS}=0
becomes a quadratic equation of
X
,

\left(\frac{\lambda_{4}}{r^{6}\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta}\frac{f^{2}}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{4}}\sin^{6}\xi\left(r\right)\right)X^{2}-\left(\frac{Q_{X}}{r^{3}\sin\theta\cos\theta}\right)X+\lambda_{0}V\left(\phi\right)=0~,

(68)

which has solutions

X_{\pm}=\left(\frac{Q_{X}\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{4}r^{3}\sin\theta\cos\theta}{2\lambda_{4}f^{2}\sin^{6}\xi\left(r\right)}\right)\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-4\left(\frac{\lambda_{4}}{Q_{X}^{2}}\frac{f^{2}}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{4}}\sin^{6}\xi\left(r\right)\right)\lambda_{0}V}\right)~.

(69)

The two solutions will coincide if

4\left(\frac{\lambda_{4}}{Q_{X}^{2}}\frac{f^{2}}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{4}}\sin^{6}\xi\left(r\right)\right)\lambda_{0}V=1~

(70)

which implies

Q_{X}=\pm 2\left(\frac{f}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{2}}\sin^{3}\xi\left(r\right)\right)\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}V}~

(71)

and the corresponding Bogomolny’s equation is

\xi^{{}^{\prime}}f^{{}^{\prime}}g_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}g_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=\pm~\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{4}}}\frac{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{2}r^{3}\sin\theta\cos\theta}{f\sin^{3}\xi}\sqrt{V}~.

(72)

The explicit form of
L_{BPS}
is given by

L_{BPS}=\pm\frac{2f\sin^{3}\xi\left(r\right)\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}V}}{r^{3}\sin\theta\cos\theta\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{2}}\xi^{{}^{\prime}}\left(r\right)f^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\theta\right)g_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\varphi_{1}\right)g_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(\varphi_{2}\right).

(73)

It is easy to show that no constraint equations are coming from the Euler-Lagrange equations of the above BPS Lagrangian density.

From the Bogomolny’s equation (
72
), we know that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
have to be the linear functions
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
respectively. Therefore, similar to the spherically symmetric case, we take

g_{1}\left(\varphi_{1}\right)=n_{1}~\varphi_{1},~g_{2}\left(\varphi_{2}\right)=n_{2}~\varphi_{2},

(74)

with
n_{1},n_{2}>0
are integer constants due to the uniqueness in coordinates
\varphi_{1,2}\to\varphi_{1,2}+2\pi
. Using the Bogomolny’s equation (
72
), the total static energy density
\varepsilon
of the BPS skyrmion is given by

\varepsilon=-T_{0}^{0}=\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}V}\frac{2f\sin^{3}\xi}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{2}r^{3}\sin\theta\cos\theta}\xi^{\prime}f^{\prime}g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime},

(75)

and so the total static energy of the BPS skyrmion is

\displaystyle E_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{domain-space}d^{4}x\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}V}\frac{2f\sin^{3}\xi}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{2}}\xi^{\prime}f^{\prime}g_{1}^{\prime}g_{2}^{\prime},

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}}\int_{target-space}\sqrt{V}\frac{f\sin^{3}\xi}{\left(1+f^{2}\right)^{2}}d\xi dfdg_{1}dg_{2},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}B\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{4}}\left<\sqrt{V}\right>_{S^{4}}.

V
Conclusions

We have considered some possible (generic) BPS submodels of the generalized five-dimensional Skyrme Model via the BPS Lagrangian method. The analysis is mainly focused on finding Bogomolny’s Equations and their solutions, with topological charge
B
is a positive integer, and also the total static energy of these BPS skyrmions under a particular ansatz (
14
). The two main cases that have been considered, based on the effective Lagrangian density of the BPS submodels, are the spherically symmetric and the non-spherically symmetric cases. Employing the BPS Lagrangian method, we followed the prescription in
Atmaja et al. 2020
for the BPS Lagrangian density.
In the spherically symmetric case, where only the effective field
\xi
in the ansatz (
14
) is undetermined, we found there are only two possible BPS submodels which are the submodel with
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0
, or namely the Skyrme term, and the submodel with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=0
, the potential term and a term that is proportional to the square of the topological current.

The first BPS submodel with only the Skyrme term has been studied in
Brihaye et al. 2017
for a unit BPS skyrmion solution in which
B=1
. Here we extended the study to the BPS skyrmion solutions with
B>1
. We found two distinct features compared to the corresponding four-dimensional BPS Skyrme model discussed in
Harland 2014
. The first one is that the BPS skyrmions exist only if there is no potential which is in contradiction with the four-dimensional case in which the BPS skyrmions may exist if the potential takes some specific forms
Harland 2014
;
Atmaja et al. 2020
. The second one is the existence of “topological degenerate” BPS skyrmions defined as BPS skyrmions with the same topological charge, but different total static energies; except for the unit BPS skyrmion,
B=1
. Interestingly there are also BPS skyrmions with the same topological charge and energies only if the topological charge is the prime number. Furthermore, the BPS skyrmions, with
B>1
, have non-zero pressures at least in two angular directions while the unit BPS skyrmion has no pressures. The BPS skyrmions, with
B>1
, have total static energy that is higher than the sum of
B
times unit BPS skyrmion which indicates that these BPS skyrmions are repulsive and unstable. It is interesting to investigate the dynamics of soliton scattering in this submodel because the repulsive effect implies the possibility of soliton creation in the usual collision setup, similar to the one demonstrated in
Vachaspati 2011
. We may need to add gravitational force, by incorporating gravity into the BPS submodel, to overcome the pressures so that these BPS skyrmions, with
B>1
, could be stable. This will be studied further in the future article.

The second BPS submodel depends on the specific form of the potential term. In this article, we considered the potential term of the form
V=(1-\cos\xi)^{q}
, with
q\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. We found the BPS skyrmions are compacton solutions for
0<q<4
and are regular solutions for
q\geq 4
. The compacton solutions have a finite cut-off radius proportional to
B^{1/4}
. The total static energy of BPS skyrmions in this submodel is proportional to the topological charge
B
similar to the BPS-Skyrme model in four dimensions
Adam et al. 2010b
. This may be because both models consist of the potential term and the term that is proportional to the square of the topological current,
B^{\mu}B_{\mu}
.

In the non-spherically symmetric case, in which all the effective fields in the ansatz (
14
) are undetermined, we found the only possible BPS submodel is similar to the second BPS submodel in the spherically symmetric case. In this case, the potential term could depend on the polar coordinate
\theta
, and hence the effective field
f
could have general forms which depend on the symmetry of the potential term. This BPS submodel gives a generalization of the corresponding spherically symmetric one in which the general form of the total static energy (
76
) is similar to the one found in
Adam et al. 2010b
. The results found in this more general scheme cover the preceding spherically symmetric case.

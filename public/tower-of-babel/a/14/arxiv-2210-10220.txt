Abstract
We measure the photon-energy spectrum in radiative bottom-meson ( B B ) decays into inclusive final states involving a strange hadron and a photon. We use SuperKEKB electron-positron collisions corresponding to 189 ​ fb − 1 189~\mbox{\,fb}^{-1} of integrated luminosity collected at the Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) \mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)} resonance by the Belle II experiment. The partner B B candidates are fully reconstructed using a large number of hadronic channels. The B → X s ​ γ B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma partial branching fractions are measured as a function of photon energy in the signal B B meson rest frame in eight bins above 1.8 GeV. The background-subtracted signal yield for this photon energy region is 343 ± 122 343\pm 122 events. Integrated branching fractions for three photon energy thresholds of 1.8 ​ Ge ​ V 1.8~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV} , 2.0 ​ Ge ​ V 2.0~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV} , and 2.1 ​ Ge ​ V 2.1~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV} are also reported, and found to be in agreement with world averages.

Introduction

Flavour changing neutral currents (FCNCs) are only allowed in the Standard Model (SM) via loop processes and are therefore highly suppressed
Misiak:2020vlo
. The
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
FCNC decays occur via radiative
b\rightarrow s
transitions, where
B
denotes charged and neutral bottom-mesons, and
X_{s}
denotes all available final states containing net strangeness. These processes are particularly sensitive to non-SM effects
Misiak:2017bgg
.
In addition, their photon-energy spectrum offers access to various interesting parameters, such as the mass of the
b
quark and the function describing its motion inside the
B
meson
RevModPhys.88.035008
;
simba
.

We present an inclusive measurement using
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
decays identified in
\mathchar 28935\relax(4S)\rightarrow B\overline{B}
events in which the partner
B
meson is reconstructed in its hadronic decays (hadronic tagging). This approach is complementary to the untagged or lepton-tagged (see e.g.,
BaBar:2012fqh
) and sum-of-exclusive (e.g.,
Belle:2014nmp
) methods because it has different sources of systematic uncertainty. In addition, tagging provides a purer sample and the kinematic information from the partner-
B
meson gives direct access to observables in the signal-
B
meson rest frame. We denote the photon energy in the signal-
B
meson rest frame as
E^{B}_{\gamma}
. In this paper, the minimum
E^{B}_{\gamma}
photon energy threshold is 1.8 GeV. The inclusive analysis does not distinguish between contributions from
b\rightarrow d\gamma
and
b\rightarrow s\gamma
processes, therefore the much smaller
b\rightarrow d\gamma
contribution is subtracted from the final results with a shape determined from simulation.

Belle II detector

The Belle II
Belle-II:2010dht
detector is designed to reconstruct the final states of electron-positron collisions at center-of-mass energies at or near the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
meson mass. The colliding
e^{+}e^{-}
beams are provided by the SuperKEKB collider
AKAI2018188
at KEK in Tsukuba, Japan. The detector has collected physics data since 2019. Belle II consists of several detector subsystems arranged cylindrically around the beam pipe. In the Belle II coordinate system, the
x
axis is defined to be horizontal and points to the outside of the tunnel for the accelerator’s
main rings, the
y
axis is vertically upward, and the
z
axis is defined in the direction of the electron beam. The azimuthal angle,
\phi
, and the polar angle,
\theta
, are defined with respect to the
z
axis. Three regions in the detector are defined based on
\theta
: forward endcap (
12^{\circ}<\theta<31^{\circ}
), barrel (
32^{\circ}<\theta<129^{\circ}
) and backward endcap (
131^{\circ}<\theta<155^{\circ}
).

The Belle II vertex detector is designed to precisely determine particle decay vertices. It is the innermost subsystem, and consists of a silicon pixel detector and a silicon strip detector. Surrounding the vertexing subsystems is the central drift chamber,
which is used to measure charged-particle trajectories (tracks) to determine their charge and momentum. It also provides important particle-identification information by measuring the specific ionisation of charged tracks. Further particle identification is provided by the time-of-propagation detector and the aerogel ring-imaging Cherenkov detector, which cover, respectively, the barrel and the forward endcap regions of Belle II. Photons and electrons are stopped and their energy deposits (clusters) are read out by the CsI(Tl)-crystal electromagnetic calorimeter. The photon-energy resolution of the ECL is better than 20
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
for photons above 1
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
All the inner components are surrounded by a superconducting solenoid, which provides a uniform axial 1.5 T magnetic field. The
K^{0}_{L}
and muon detector, composed of plastic scintillators and resistive-plate chambers, is the outermost subsystem of Belle II.

\rightarrow

1.6

Event reconstruction and selection

Tag side reconstruction

In each event, one
B
meson candidate is fully reconstructed and used as a tag for the recoiling signal
B
meson candidate. The tag-side
B
meson is reconstructed using the full-event-interpretation algorithm (FEI)
FEI
, which reconstructs hadronic
B
decays from thousands of subdecay chains. The algorithm starts by combining track and ECL cluster information to reconstruct final-state candidate particles, such as electrons, muons, photons, charged pions, and kaons. In the next step, those are combined to form intermediate particles
such as
\pi^{0}
,
K_{S}^{0}
,
D^{(*)}
, and
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
candidates. The intermediate or final-state particles are combined to form
B
candidates in 36
B^{+}
and 32
B^{0}
hadronic modes. For each reconstructed
B
-meson candidate, the algorithm outputs a probability-like score,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{FEI}}
. Correctly reconstructed
B
-meson candidates have a score close to one, whereas non-
B
and misreconstructed candidates tend to have a score close to zero.

For tag-candidate reconstruction, track-quality requirements are imposed
BERTACCHI2021107610
. The longitudinal distance of closest approach of each track from the detector center is required to be
|z_{0}|<2.0~{\rm\,cm}
. A similar criterion for the distance in the transverse plane,
|d_{0}|<0.5~{\rm\,cm}
is also applied. Only charged particles with transverse momenta,
p_{T}
, higher than 0.1
{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
are selected. Furthermore, an event must have at least three tracks passing these selections. Similarly, three or more isolated clusters with
17<\theta<150^{\circ}
and
E>0.1~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
are required in the ECL for each event. The total energy deposited in the ECL should be within
2
and
7~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. Only events with at least
4~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
of measured energy in the Belle II detector are retained. The tag-side
B
candidates are required to have
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{FEI}}>0.001
and beam-constrained mass,
{M_{\mathrm{bc}}}>5.245~{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
, defined as

{M_{\mathrm{bc}}}=\sqrt{(\sqrt{s}/2)^{2}-p_{\mathrm{tag}}^{2}},

(1)

where
\sqrt{s}
is the collision energy and
p_{\mathrm{tag}}
is the reconstructed momentum of the tag-candidate in the CM frame. Furthermore, a
|\Delta E|<0.2~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
requirement is imposed on the energy difference, defined as

\Delta E=E_{\mathrm{tag}}-\sqrt{s}/2,

(2)

where
E_{\mathrm{tag}}
is the energy of the tag-candidate reconstructed in the CM frame.

Signal-side selection

Using the kinematic properties of the reconstructed tag-side meson and the beam-energy constraint, the photon-candidate energy is inferred in the signal-
B
meson rest-frame. All candidates in the range of
{E^{B}_{\gamma}}>1.4~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
are considered. The highest-energy photon in each event is taken as the signal-photon candidate. Studies on simulated signal events show that this selects the correct
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
candidate in more than 99% of cases. Cluster-timing information, derived from a waveform fit to the signal collected in the most energetic crystal of the cluster, is associated to each photon. In order to suppress the large background from out-of-time beam-background clusters or photons associated with low-quality waveform fits, the cluster timing, measured with respect to the collision time, is used. It is required neither to exceed
200~{\rm\,ns}
nor twice the cluster-time uncertainty. This selection introduces about a 2% signal efficiency loss while reducing beam background to negligible levels.

The resulting sample is dominated by photon candidates originating from asymmetric
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
and
\eta\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
decays. This background is suppressed by vetoing
\pi^{0}
and
\eta
decays. Signal-photon candidates are kinematically combined with lower-energy photons in the event and a quantitative measure of compatibility with
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
or
\eta\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
decays is associated to the combinations. The compatibility is determined using a multivariate statistical-learning algorithm that uses the diphoton invariant mass, helicity, and properties of the less-energetic photon, such as energy, polar angle, and smallest ECL cluster-to-track distance. Another statistical-learning algoritm uses Zernike moments to quantify the ECL-photon cluster shape to disentangle misidentified photons from signal clusters. The background suppression of these selections is investigated using the off-resonance data. Furthermore, samples with inverted
\pi^{0}
and
\eta

Photon candidates from background

\rightarrow

and

B

KSFW_moments
,
B

CLEO_cones

BaBar:2014omp

and

in order not to bias the inclusive spectrum. Furthermore, each variable distribution in off-resonance data is compared to
e^{+}e^{-}

\rightarrow

e^{+}e^{-}

\rightarrow

or

Best tag-side candidate selection

After all the selections described in

The probability to have more than one tag-side candidate decreases rapidly and is lower than 1% for 5 or more candidates. If more than one tag-side candidate is present in an event, only the one with the highest

for charged and neutral modes, respectively, a tagging efficiency of
(0.44\pm 0.02)\%
is found. The calibration factors account for differences in the tag-side reconstruction efficiency between data and simulation and are derived in an independent study of hadronic-tagged
B\rightarrow X\ell\nu
decays
Belle-II:2020fst
. The signal-selection efficiency is calculated as the fraction of tagged-signal yield that meet the requirements of
Section

5.2
. It increases approximately linearly from 45% to 63% with
{E^{B}_{\gamma}}~\in~[1.8,2.6]~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
, with a total uncertainty of about 10% in each bin. Background events from
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\overline{q}
are suppressed by 99.5%, while the generic
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background is reduced by 93%.

Fit of sample composition

The tag-side
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
distribution is fit to determine the yields of
B
mesons that provide good kinematic constraints on the signal side, the remaining
q\overline{q}
events, and combinatorial
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events. The sample is divided in 11 bins of
E^{B}_{\gamma}
: three 200-
\mathrm{MeV}
-wide bins for the 1.4–2.0
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
range; seven 100-
\mathrm{MeV}
-wide bins for the 2.0–2.7
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
region; and a single
{E^{B}_{\gamma}}>2.7~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
bin. The first two bins and the last one are chosen as control regions for the fit due to expected large background or low signal yield. The signal region is therefore defined as
1.8<{E^{B}_{\gamma}}<2.7~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
.

The model for the fit of sample composition is determined using simulation. The simulated sample is split into three components: correctly reconstructed (peaking)
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events,
q\overline{q}
decays, and combinatorial background from
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
decays. ‘Peaking’ henceforth is used generically to denote the resonant behaviour in
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
of correctly reconstructed tag-side
B
decays. These components have distinct shapes in
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
, which are parameterised using probability distribution functions (PDFs). To extract the yield of peaking
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
tags, a Crystal Ball function is used
CrystalBall:1986xj
. This function is the sum of a peaking Gaussian part and a polynomial tail. The
q\overline{q}
decays are described by an ARGUS function
ALBRECHT1990278
. Combinatorial
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background is described by a fifth-order Chebyshev polynomial. Studies on simulation show that a lower-order polynomial is insufficient to accurately describe the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
shape of this component.

Likelihood fits to the unbinned
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
distributions are performed simultaneously in the
E^{B}_{\gamma}
bins
zfit
. A modeling fit is performed on separate components in simulated data to determine the shape parameters and fix them. The fit is then applied to the experimental data. The yields of the three components in each
E^{B}_{\gamma}
interval, and the ARGUS shape parameters – which are shared across bins – are determined by the fit (
Figure

1
). The peaking-
B
yields in each
E^{B}_{\gamma}
bin are extracted from the Crystal-Ball normalizations. The peaking-
B
yield estimator is unbiased and has Gaussian uncertainties as shown by checks on simplified simulated experiments.

Residual
B
background subtraction

The resulting peaking
B
yields include contributions from
B\rightarrow X_{s+d}\gamma
events and other correctly-tagged
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
processes, which are considered background. Due to the high-purity of the tagged sample, background contamination is low at high-
E^{B}_{\gamma}
, but grows sharply with decreasing
E^{B}_{\gamma}
.

To remove this background, the PDFs defined in
Section

6
are used to fit simulated
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
samples in which
B\rightarrow X_{s+d}\gamma
events are removed. This procedure extracts yields of peaking nonsignal events in every
E^{B}_{\gamma}
bin. The background predictions are scaled for luminosity, and corrected based on FEI calibration factors
Belle-II:2020fst
,
\gamma
detection-efficiency
gamma_eff
, and
\pi^{0}
efficiency. Branching fractions used in simulation for the background modes are matched to the most recent known values. The
1.4<{E^{B}_{\gamma}}<1.8\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
region, where signal purity is low, is used for validating the background subtraction. Event yields observed in data after subtraction are compared with expectations from background-only simulation. An 8.7% difference is observed, which is assigned as a uniform correction factor for background normalisation across bins. Unfolding

The measured
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
spectrum needs to be corrected (unfolded) for smearing effects. The unfolding uses bin-by-bin multiplicative factors based on the hybrid model. These factors are defined as the ratios between the expected number of events of the generated spectrum and the expected number of corresponding events of the reconstructed spectrum within an
E^{B}_{\gamma}
interval. The measured
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
yields are multiplied by the unfolding factors (see
Section

10
). The bulk of the unfolding factors do not exceed 10-20%, and only the edge bins have 30-60% corrections.

Uncertainties

Multiple sources of systematic uncertainty are considered and are grouped as follows: uncertainties due to assumptions in the fit; uncertainties affecting the signal efficiency estimation; data-MC normalisation in the background estimation; and other sources, such as unfolding procedure, branching fraction normalization and the subtraction of
B\rightarrow X_{d}\gamma
component. The statistical uncertainties of the yields extracted from the fit on data are dominant.

Uncertainties due to assumptions in the fit

To account for assumptions on the values of model parameters, we repeat the fits by varying the Chebyshev polynomial coefficients by their one-standard-deviation uncertainties, and take the maximum shift in signal yield as the uncertainty. We account for a known data-simulation mismodelling of the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
endpoint due to non-simulated run-dependant variations of the collision energy. The signal yields observed in data using alternative models of background shapes with various
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
endpoints are compared. The maximum variation with respect to the central result is taken as uncertainty.

Signal efficiency uncertainties

The signal efficiency is calculated using the simulated hybrid-model signal sample. The values are corrected using FEI simulation-to-data calibration factors,
\mathcal{C}_{B^{0}/B^{+}}

Belle-II:2020fst
, as well as acceptance corrections from the
\pi^{0}
veto and
\gamma
efficiency. The corresponding uncertainties related to these factors are propagated as systematic uncertainties. The signal efficiency is validated in the high-purity
{E^{B}_{\gamma}}\in[2.5,2.6]~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
region, and the observed difference assigned as an uncertainty.

Background uncertainties

The uncertainties associated with the limited size of the simulated samples used in the
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
fits are propagated to the final results. Similarly to the signal efficiency, the background yields extracted from the fits on simulated samples are corrected using FEI calibration,
\pi^{0}
veto efficiency, and
\gamma
detection-efficiency correction factors. Uncertainties on the branching fractions of background decay modes are also included. The observed background-normalisation difference (see
Section

7
) is assigned as a 100% systematic uncertainty.

Other uncertainties

To unfold the measured
E^{B}_{\gamma}
spectrum, we evaluate the hybrid-model shape uncertainties by taking into account the uncertainty on the ratio of the known branching fractions of
B\rightarrow K^{*}\gamma
to that of
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
. The
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
model-parameter uncertainties, based on Ref.
simba
, are also included. The analysis does not distinguish between
X_{s}
and
X_{d}
final-states. The contribution from the
B\rightarrow X_{d}\gamma
component is subtracted assuming the same shape and selection efficiency as
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
. Under this assumption, the
\mathcal{B}({B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma})
and
\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow X_{d}\gamma)
ratio equals
\left|{V_{td}}/{V_{ts}}\right|^{2}
. The full size of the
B\rightarrow X_{d}\gamma
component is assigned as an uncertainty. The uncertainty on the number of
B
meson pairs, used as the branching-fraction normalization, is also taken into account. It is estimated by an independent study with a data-driven method in which off-resonance data are used to subtract the non-
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
contribution from the on-resonance data.

Conclusion

We present a measurement of the photon-energy spectrum in the
B
meson rest frame from
B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma
decays using hadronic-tagging of the partner
B
meson. We also report the inclusive branching ratio
\mathcal{B}({B\rightarrow X_{s}\gamma})
for various thresholds, starting at
{E^{B}_{\gamma}}>1.8~\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
. The results are consistent with the Standard Model and world averages
HFLAV:2019otj
.

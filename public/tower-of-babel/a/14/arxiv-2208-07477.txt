Low Rank Tensor Decompositions and Approximations
Jiawang Nie, Li Wang, and Zequn Zheng

Abstract
. There exist linear relations among tensor entries of low rank tensors. These linear relations can be expressed by multi-linear polynomials, which are called generating polynomials. We use generating polynomials to compute tensor rank decompositions and low rank tensor approximations. We prove that this gives a quasi-optimal low rank tensor approximation if the given tensor is sufficiently close to a low rank one.

Introduction

Let
m
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}
be positive integers.
A tensor
\mathcal{F}
of order
m
and dimension
n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}

can be labelled such that

\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}})_{1\leq i_{1}\leq n_{1},\ldots,1\leq i_{m}\leq n_{m}}.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field (either the real field
\mathbb{R}
or the complex field
\mathbb{C}
).
The space of all tensors of order

m
and dimension
n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}

with entries in
\mathbb{F}
, is denoted as

\mathbb{F}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}
.
For vectors
v_{i}\in\mathbb{F}^{n_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
,
their outer product
v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{m}

is the tensor in
\mathbb{F}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}
such that

(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{m})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\,=\,(v_{1})_{i_{1}}\cdots(v_{m})_{i_{m}}

for all labels in the corresponding range. A tensor like

v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{m}
is called a rank-
1
tensor.
For every tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{F}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}
,
there exist vector tuples
(v^{s,1},\ldots,v^{s,m})
,

s=1,\ldots,r
,
v^{s,j}\in\mathbb{F}^{n_{j}}
, such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}v^{s,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{s,m}.

The smallest such
r
is called the
\mathbb{F}
-rank of
\mathcal{F}
,
for which we denote
\text{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{F})
.
When
r
is minimum, the equation (
1.1
)
is called a rank-
r
decomposition over the field
\mathbb{F}
.
In the literature, this rank is sometimes referenced as
the candecomp-parafac (CP) rank.
We refer to
[
22
,
24
]
for various notions of tensor ranks.
Recent work for tensor decompositions can be found in

[
1
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
26
,
36
,
38
]
.
Tensors are closely related to polynomial optimization

[

,

,
29
,
31
,
32
]
.
Various applications of tensors can be found in

[
18
,
20
,
30
]
.
Throughout the paper, we use the Hilbert-Schmidt norm for tensors:

\|\mathcal{F}\|=\sqrt{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{j}\leq n_{j},1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{F}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}|^{2}}.

The low rank tensor approximation (LRTA) problem is to find a low rank tensor
that is close to a given one.
This is equivalent to a nonlinear least square optimization problem.
For a given tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{F}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

and a given rank
r
, we look for
r

v^{(s)}:=(v^{s,1},\ldots,v^{s,m})
,
s=1,\ldots,r
, such that

\mathcal{F}\,\approx\,\sum\limits_{s=1}^{r}v^{s,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{s,m},\quad v^{s,j}\in\mathbb{F}^{n_{j}}.

This requires to solve the following nonlinear least squares optimization

(1.2)

\displaystyle\min\limits_{v^{(s,j)}\in\mathbb{F}^{n_{j}},\,j=1,\ldots,m}\big\|\mathcal{F}-\sum\limits_{s=1}^{r}v^{s,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{s,m}\big\|^{2}.

When
r=1
, the best rank-
1
approximating tensor always exists
and it is equivalent to computing the spectral norm

[
16
,
19
]
.
When
r>1
, the best rank-
r
tensor approximation may not exist
[
11
]
.
Classically used methods for solving low rank tensor approximations are
the alternating least squares (ALS) method
[
5
,
25
,
40
]
,
higher order power iterations
[
8
]
,
semidefinite relaxations
[
7
,
29
]
,
SVD based methods
[
17
]
,
optimization based methods
[
15
,
36
]
.
We refer to
[
1
,
27
]

for recent work on low rank tensor approximations.

Contributions

In this paper, we extend the generating polynomial method in

[
26
,
27
]

to compute tensor rank decompositions and low rank tensor approximations
for nonsymmetric tensors.
First, we estimate generating polynomials by solving linear least squares.
Second, we find their approximately common zeros,
which can be done by computing eigenvalue decompositions.
Third, we get a tensor decomposition from their common zeros,
by solving linear least squares. To find a low rank tensor approximation,
we first apply the decomposition method to obtain a low rank approximating tensor
and then use nonlinear optimization methods to improve the approximation.
Our major conclusion is that if the tensor to be approximated is sufficiently close
to a low rank one, then the obtained low rank tensor is a quasi-optimal low rank approximation.
The proof is based on perturbation analysis of linear least squares and eigenvalue decompositions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we review some basic results about tensors.
In Section
3
, we introduce the concept of generating polynomials
and study their relations to tensor decompositions.
In Section
4
, we give an algorithm for computing
tensor rank decompositions for low rank tensors.
In Section
5
, we give an algorithm for computing low rank approximations.
The approximation error analysis is also given.
In Section
6
, we present numerical experiments.
Some conclusions are made in Section
7
.

2.1.
Flattening matrices

We partition the dimensions
n_{1},n_{2},...,n_{m}
into two disjoint groups

I_{1}
and
I_{2}
such that the difference

\Big|\prod_{i\in I_{1}}n_{i}-\prod_{j\in I_{2}}n_{j}\Big|

is minimum. Up to a permutation, we write that

I_{1}=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\}
,

I_{2}=\{n_{k+1},\ldots,n_{m}\}.

For convenience, denote that

\begin{array}[]{l}I=\left\{\left(\imath_{1},\ldots,\imath_{k}\right):1\leq\imath_{j}\leq n_{j},j=1,\ldots,k\right\},\\
J=\left\{\left(\imath_{k+1},\ldots,\imath_{m}\right):1\leq\imath_{j}\leq n_{j},j=k+1,\ldots,m\right\}.\end{array}

For a tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\ldots\times n_{m}}
,
the above partition gives the flattening matrix

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mbox{Flat}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\left(\mathcal{F}_{\imath,\jmath}\right)_{\imath\in I,\jmath\in J}.

This gives the most square flattening matrix for
\mathcal{F}
.
Let
\sigma_{r}
denote the closure of all rank-
r
tensors in

\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\ldots\times n_{m}}
,
under the Zariski topology (see
[
6
]
).
The set
\sigma_{r}
is an irreducible variety of
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}
.
For a given tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\sigma_{r}
, it is possible that
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F})>r.

This fact motivates the notion of border rank:

(2.3)

\operatorname{rank}_{B}(\mathcal{F})=\min\left\{r:\mathcal{F}\in\sigma_{r}\right\},

For every tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\ldots\times n_{m}}
,
one can show that

(2.4)

\operatorname{rank}\mbox{Flat}(\mathcal{F})\leq\operatorname{rank}_{B}(\mathcal{F})\leq\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F}).

A property
\mathtt{P}
is said to hold
generically
on

\sigma_{r}
if
\mathtt{P}
holds on a Zariski open subset
T
of
\sigma_{r}
.
For such a property
\mathtt{P}
, each
u\in T
is called a generic point.
Interestingly, the above three ranks are equal for generic points of

\sigma_{r}
for a range of values of
r
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s
be the smaller dimension of the matrix
\mbox{Flat}(\mathcal{F})
.
For every
r\leq s
, the equalities

(2.5)

\operatorname{rank}\mbox{Flat}(\mathcal{F})\,=\,\operatorname{rank}_{B}(\mathcal{F})\,=\,\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F})

hold for tensors
\mathcal{F}
in a Zariski open subset of
\sigma_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\ell}
be the
r\times r
minors of the matrix

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mbox{Flat}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}x^{i,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x^{i,m}).

They are homogeneous polynomials in
x^{i,j}(i=1,\ldots,r,j=1,\ldots,m).

Let
x
denote the tuple
\left(x^{1,1},x^{1,2},\ldots,x^{r,m}\right).

Define the projective variety in
\mathbb{P}^{r\left(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\right)-1}

(2.7)

\displaystyle Z=\left\{x:\phi_{1}(x)=\cdots=\phi_{\ell}(x)=0\right\}.

Then
Y:=\mathbb{P}^{r\left(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\right)-1}\backslash Z

is a Zariski open subset of full dimension.
Consider the polynomial mapping
\pi:Y\rightarrow\sigma_{r}
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left(x^{1,1},x^{1,2},\ldots,x^{r,m}\right)\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(x^{i,1}\right)\otimes\cdots\otimes\left(x^{i,m}\right).

The image
\pi(Y)
is dense in the irreducible variety
\sigma_{r}.

So,
\pi(Y)
contains a Zariski open subset
\mathscr{Y}

of
\sigma_{r}
(see
[
35
]
).
For each
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{Y}
, there exists
u\in Y
such that
\mathcal{F}=\pi(u)
.
Because
u\notin Z
, at least one of
\phi_{1}(u),\ldots,\phi_{\ell}(u)
is nonzero,
and hence
\operatorname{rank}\mbox{Flat}(\mathcal{F})\geq r.
By (
2.4
), we know
(
2.5
) holds for all
\mathcal{F}\in\mathscr{Y}

since
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{F})\leq r.
Since
\mathscr{Y}
is a Zariski open subset of
\sigma_{r}
,
the lemma holds.
∎

By Lemma
2.1

r\leq s
and
\mathcal{F}
is a generic tensor
in
\sigma_{r}

However, for a generic

2.2.
Reshaping of tensor decompositions

A tensor
\mathcal{F}
of order greater than
3
can be reshaped to
another tensor
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
of order
3
. A tensor decomposition of

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
can be converted to a decomposition for
\mathcal{F}

under certain conditions.
In the following, we assume a given tensor
\mathcal{F}
has the decomposition (
2.1
).
Suppose the set
\{1,\ldots,m\}
is partitioned into
3

\{1,\ldots,m\}\,=\,I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup I_{3}.

Let
p_{i}=|I_{i}|\mathrm{~for~}i=1,2,3

For the reshaped vectors

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}w^{s,1}&=&u^{s,i_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u^{s,i_{p_{1}}}&\mathrm{~for~}I_{1}\,=\,\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p_{1}}\},\\
w^{s,2}&=&u^{s,j_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u^{s,j_{p_{2}}}&\mathrm{~for~}I_{2}\,=\,\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{p_{2}}\},\\
w^{s,3}&=&u^{s,k_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes u^{s,k_{p_{3}}}&\mathrm{~for~}I_{3}\,=\,\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{p_{3}}\},\end{array}\right.

we get the following tensor decomposition

(2.10)

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\,=\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}w^{s,1}\otimes w^{s,2}\otimes w^{s,3}.

Conversely, for a decomposition like (
2.10
) for
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}

if all

can be expressed
as rank-

),
then the equation (
2.10
) can be reshaped to
a tensor decomposition for
\mathcal{F}
as in (
2.1
).
When the flattened tensor
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies some conditions,
the tensor decomposition of
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is unique.
For such a case, we can obtain a tensor decomposition for
\mathcal{F}

through the decomposition (

).
A classical result about the uniqueness is

]

Theorem 2.2
.

(Kruskal’s Criterion,
[

]
)
Let
\mathcal{F}=U^{(1)}\circ U^{(2)}\circ U^{(3)}
be a tensor

Let
\kappa_{i}
be the Kruskal rank of

, for
i=1,2,3
.
If

2r+2\leq\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{3},

then

has a unique rank-

The Kruskal’s Criterion
can be generalized for more range of

as in
[
4

Assume the dimension
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq n_{3}\geq 2

and the rank

is such that

2r+2\leq\mathrm{min}(n_{1},r)+\mathrm{min}(n_{2},r)+\mathrm{min}(n_{3},r),

or equivalently, for
\delta=n_{2}+n_{3}-n_{1}-2
,

is such that

r\leq n_{1}+\mathrm{min}\{\frac{1}{2}\delta,\delta\}.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a generic tensor of rank
r
as above
in the space
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
,
then
\mathcal{F}
has a unique rank-
r
decomposition.
There following is a uniqueness result for reshaped tensor decompositions.

Theorem 2.3
.

(Reshaped Kruskal Criterion,
[

For the tensor space

let

be a union of disjoint sets

Suppose

and let

Assume

If

is a generic tensor of rank

in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

3.
Generating Polynomials

Generating polynomials can be used to compute tensor rank decompositions.
We consider tensors whose ranks
r
are not bigger than the highest dimension,
say,
r\leq n_{1}
where
n_{1}
is the biggest of
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}
.
Denote the indeterminate vector variables

\mathbf{x_{1}}=(x_{1,1},...x_{1,n_{1}}),\,\ldots,\,\mathbf{x_{m}}=(x_{m,1},...x_{m,n_{m}}).

A tensor in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

can be labelled by monomials like
x_{1,i_{1}}x_{2,i_{2}}...x_{m,i_{m}}
. Let

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{M}&\coloneqq&\big\{x_{1,i_{1}}...x_{m,i_{m}}\;|\;1\leq i_{j}\leq n_{j}\big\}.\end{array}

For a subset
J\subseteq\{1,2,...,m\}
, denote that

(3.2)

\boxed{\begin{array}[]{rcl}J^{c}&\coloneqq&\{1,2,...,m\}\backslash J,\\
\mathbb{M}_{J}&\coloneqq&\big\{x_{1,i_{1}}...x_{m,i_{m}}\;|\;x_{j,i_{j}}=1\,~\forall\,j\in J^{c}\big\},\\
\mathcal{M}_{J}&\coloneqq&{\rm span}\{\mathbb{M}_{J}\}.\end{array}}

A label tuple
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
is uniquely determined by the monomial

x_{1,i_{1}}\cdots x_{m,i_{m}}
.
So a tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\ldots\times n_{m}}

can be equivalently labelled by monomials such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x_{1,i_{1}}\ldots x_{m,i_{m}}}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}.

With the new labelling by monomials, define the bi-linear product

(3.4)

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{M}}c_{\mu}\mu,\mathcal{F}\rangle\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{M}}c_{\mu}\mathcal{F}_{\mu}.

In the above, each
c_{\mu}
is a scalar
and
\mathcal{F}
is labelled by monomials as in (
3.3
).

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
28
,
33
]
)
For a subset
J\subseteq\{1,2,...,m\}
and a tensor

\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}
,
a polynomial
p\in\mathcal{M}_{J}

is called a generating polynomial for
\mathcal{F}
if

(3.5)

\langle pq,\mathcal{F}\rangle=0\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,q\in\mathbb{M}_{J^{c}}.

The following is an example of generating polynomials.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the cubic order tensor

\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3\times 3}
given as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{F}_{:,:,1}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\mathcal{F}_{:,:,2}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\mathcal{F}_{:,:,3}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}[r]11&20&10\\
7&10&5\\
12&18&9\\
\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}[r]15&24&12\\
15&18&9\\
24&30&15\\
\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}[r]7&10&5\\
9&10&5\\
14&16&8\\
\end{matrix}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

The following is a generating polynomial for
\mathcal{F}
:

p\coloneqq(2x_{1,1}-x_{1,2})(2x_{2,1}-x_{2,2}).

Note that
p\in\mathcal{M}_{\{1,2\}}

and for each
i_{3}=1,2,3

\displaystyle p\cdot x_{3,i_{3}}=(2x_{1,1}-x_{1,2})(2x_{2,1}-x_{2,2})x_{3,i_{3}}.

One can check that for each
i_{3}=1,2,3
,

4\mathcal{F}_{1,1,i_{3}}-2\mathcal{F}_{1,2,i_{3}}-2\mathcal{F}_{2,1,i_{3}}+\mathcal{F}_{2,2,i_{3}}=0.

This is because

\begin{bmatrix}4&-2&-2&1\end{bmatrix}
is orthogonal to

\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{F}_{1,1,i_{3}}&\mathcal{F}_{1,2,i_{3}}&\mathcal{F}_{2,1,i_{3}}&\mathcal{F}_{2,2,i_{3}}\end{bmatrix}

for each
i_{3}=1,2,3
.

Suppose the rank
r\leq n_{1}
is given.
For convenience of notation, denote the label set

(3.6)

\displaystyle J\coloneqq\{(i,j,k):1\leq i\leq r,~2\leq j\leq m,~2\leq k\leq n_{j}\}.

For a matrix
G\in\mathbb{C}^{[r]\times J}

and a triple
\tau=(i,j,k)\in J
, define the bi-linear polynomial

(3.7)

\displaystyle\phi[G,\tau](x)\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}G(\ell,\tau)x_{1,\ell}x_{j,1}-x_{1,i}x_{j,k}\,\,\in\,\mathcal{M}_{\{1,j\}}.

The rows of
G
are labelled by
\ell=1,2,...,r

and the columns of
G
are labelled by
\tau\in J
.
We are interested in
G
such that

\phi[G,\tau]
is a generating polynomial for a tensor

\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\ldots\times n_{m}}
.
This requires that

\displaystyle\langle\phi[G,\tau]\cdot\mu,\mathcal{F}\rangle=0\,\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\mu\in\mathbb{M}_{\{1,j\}^{c}}.

The above is equivalent to the equation
(
\mathcal{F}
is labelled as in (
3.3
))

(3.8)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}G(\ell,\tau)\mathcal{F}_{x_{1,\ell}\cdot\mu}=\mathcal{F}_{x_{1,i}x_{j,k}\cdot\mu}.

Definition 3.3
.

(
[
28
,
33
]
)
When (
3.8
) holds for all
\tau\in J
,
the
G
is called a generating matrix for
\mathcal{F}
.

For given
G
,
j\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
and
k\in\{2,\ldots,n_{j}\}
,
we denote the matrix

(3.9)

M^{j,k}[G]\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}G(1,(1,j,k))&G(2,(1,j,k))&\dots&G(r,(1,j,k))\\
G(1,(2,j,k))&G(2,(2,j,k))&\dots&G(r,(2,j,k))\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
G(1,(r,j,k))&G(2,(r,j,k))&\dots&G(r,(r,j,k))\\
\end{bmatrix}.

For each
(j,k)
, define the matrix/vector

(3.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}A[\mathcal{F},j]&\coloneqq&\Big(\mathcal{F}_{x_{1,\ell}\cdot\mu}\Big)_{\mu\in\mathbb{M}_{\{1,j\}^{c}},1\leq\ell\leq r},\\
b[\mathcal{F},j,k]&\coloneqq&\Big(\mathcal{F}_{x_{1,\ell}\cdot x_{j,k}\cdot\mu}\Big)_{\mu\in\mathbb{M}_{\{1,j\}^{c}},1\leq\ell\leq r}.\end{array}\right.

The equation (
3.8
) is then equivalent to

(3.11)

A[\mathcal{F},j](M^{j,k}[G])^{T}\,=\,b[\mathcal{F},j,k].

The following is a useful property
for the matrices
M^{j,k}[G]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

(
[
28
,
33
]
)
Suppose
\mathcal{F}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}u^{s,1}\otimes...\otimes u^{s,m}

for vectors
u^{s,j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{j}}
.
If
r\leq n_{1}
,
(u^{s,2})_{1}...(u^{s,m})_{1}\neq 0
,
and the first
r
rows of the first decomposing matrix

U^{(1)}\coloneqq[u^{1,1}\,\,\cdots\,\,u^{r,1}]

are linearly independent,
then there exists a
G
satisfying (
3.11
)
and satisfying (for all
j\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
,

k\in\{2,\ldots,n_{j}\}
and
s=1,\ldots,r
)

(3.12)

\displaystyle M^{j,k}[G]\cdot(u^{s,1})_{1:r}=(u^{s,j})_{k}\cdot(u^{s,1})_{1:r}.

4.
Low rank tensor decompositions

Without loss of generality, assume
the dimensions are decreasing as

n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{m}.

We discuss how to compute tensor decomposition
for a tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

when the rank
r
is not bigger than the highest dimension,
i.e.,
r\leq n_{1}
.
As in Theorem
3.4
, the decomposing vectors
(u^{s,1})_{1:r}

are common eigenvectors of the matrices
M^{j,k}[G]
,
with
(u^{s,j})_{k}
being the eigenvalues respectively.
This implies that the matrices
M^{j,k}[G]

are simultaneously diagonalizable.
This property can be used to compute tensor decompositions.

Suppose
G
is a matrix such that (
3.11
) holds and

M^{j,k}[G]
are simultaneously diagonalizable. That is,
there is an invertible matrix
P\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}

such that all the products
P^{-1}M^{j,k}[G]P

are diagonal for all
j=2,\ldots,m
and for all
k=2,\ldots,n_{j}
.
Suppose
M^{j,k}[G]
are diagonalized such that

(4.1)

P^{-1}M^{j,k}[G]P\,=\,\mbox{diag}[\lambda_{j,k,1},\lambda_{j,k,2},\ldots,\lambda_{j,k,r}]

with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j,k,s}
.
For each
s=1,\ldots,r
and
j=2,\ldots,m
,
denote the vectors

(4.2)

u^{s,j}\,\coloneqq\,(1,\lambda_{j,2,s},\ldots,\lambda_{j,n_{j},s}).

When
\mathcal{F}
is rank-
r
, there exist vectors

u^{1,1},\ldots,u^{r,1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}
such that

(4.3)

\mathcal{F}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}u^{s,1}\otimes u^{s,2}\otimes\cdots\otimes u^{s,m}.

The vectors
u^{s,1}
can be found by solving linear equations
after
u^{s,j}
are obtained for
j=2,\ldots,m
and
s=1,\ldots,r
.
The existence of vectors
u^{s,1}
satisfying the tensor decomposition
(
4.3
) is shown in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=V^{(1)}\circ...\circ V^{(m)}
be a rank-r tensor,
for matrices
V^{(i)}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}\times r}
,
such that the first
r
rows of
V^{(1)}
are linearly independent.
Suppose
G
is a matrix satisfying (
3.11
) and

P\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
is an invertible matrix such that all matrix products

P^{-1}\cdot M^{j,k}[G]\cdot P

are simultaneously diagonalized as in (
4.1
).
For
j=2,\ldots,m
and
s=1,\ldots,r
,
let
u^{s,j}
be vectors given as in (
4.2
).
Then, there must exist vectors
u^{1,1},\ldots,u^{r,1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}

such that the tensor decomposition (
4.3
) holds.

Proof.

Since the matrix
P=\begin{bmatrix}p_{1}&\cdots&p_{r}\end{bmatrix}

is invertible, there exist scalars
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(4.4)

\mathcal{F}_{1:r,1,...,1}=c_{1}p_{1}+c_{2}p_{2}+\cdots+c_{r}p_{r}.

Consider the new tensor

\mathcal{H}\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}c_{s}p_{s}\otimes u^{s,2}\otimes\cdots\otimes u^{s,m}.

In the following, we show that
\mathcal{F}_{1:r,:,...,:}=\mathcal{H}

and there exist vectors
u^{1,1},\ldots,u^{r,1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}}

satisfying the equation (
4.3
).

By Theorem
3.4
, one can see that
the generating matrix
G
for
\mathcal{F}

is also a generating matrix for
\mathcal{H}
, so it holds that

(4.5)

\langle\phi[G,\tau]p,\mathcal{F}\rangle=\langle\phi[G,\tau]p,\mathcal{H}\rangle=0,\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,p\in\mathbb{M}_{\{1,j\}^{c}}.

Therefore, we have

(4.6)

\langle\phi[G,\tau]p,\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F}\rangle=0,\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,p\in\mathbb{M}_{\{1,j\}^{c}}.

By (
4.4
), one can see that

(4.7)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F})_{1:r,1,\ldots,1}=0.

In (
4.6
), for each
\tau=(i,2,k)\in J

and
p=1
, we can get

(\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F})_{1:r,:,1,\ldots,1}=0.

Similarly, for
\tau=(i,2,k)\in J
and
p=x_{3,j_{3}}
, we can get

(\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{F})_{1:r,:,:,1,\ldots,1}=0.

Continuing this, we can eventually get

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{F}_{1:r,:,:,\ldots,:}
.
Since the matrix
(V^{(1)})_{1:r,:}
is invertible, there exists a matrix

W\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times r}
such that

V^{(1)}=W(V^{(1)})_{1:r,:}.

Observe that

\mathcal{F}=W\times_{1}\mathcal{F}_{1:r,:,:,\ldots,:}=W\times_{1}\mathcal{H}.

Let
u^{s,1}=W\cdot(c_{s}p_{s})
for
s=1,\ldots,r
.
Then the tensor decomposition (
4.3
) holds.
∎

4.1.
An algorithm for computing tensor decompositions

Consider a tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

with a given rank
r
. Recall that the dimensions are ordered such that

n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{m}
.
We discuss how to compute the rank-
r
tensor decomposition for
\mathcal{F}
.
Recall
A[\mathcal{F},j]
,
b[\mathcal{F},j,k]
as in (
3.10
), for
j>1
.
Note that
A[\mathcal{F},j]
has the dimension
N_{j}\times r
, where

(4.8)

N_{j}\,\coloneqq\,\frac{n_{2}\cdots n_{m}}{n_{j}}.

If
r\leq N_{j}
, then the matrices
A[\mathcal{F},j]

have full column rank for generic cases.
For instance,
r\leq N_{3}
if
m=3
and
r\leq n_{2}
.
Since
N_{2}
is the smallest,
we often use the matrices
A[\mathcal{F},j]
for
j\geq 3
.
For convenience, denote the label set

(4.9)

\Upsilon\,\coloneqq\,\{(j,k):3\leq j\leq m,2\leq k\leq n_{j}\}.

In the following, we consider the case that
r\leq N_{3}
.
For each pair
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
, the linear system (
3.11
)
has a unique solution, for which we denote

Y^{j,k}\,=\,M^{j,k}[G].

For
j=2
, the equation (
3.11
) may not
have a unique solution if
r>N_{2}
.
In the following, we show how to get the tensor decomposition
without using the matrices
M^{2,k}[G]
.
By Theorem
3.4
, the matrices
Y^{j,k}

are simultaneously diagonalizable, that is,
there is an invertible matrix
P\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}

such that all products
P^{-1}Y^{j,k}P

are diagonal for every
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
.
Suppose they are diagonalized as

(4.10)

P^{-1}Y^{j,k}P\,=\,\mbox{diag}[\lambda_{j,k,1},\lambda_{j,k,2},\ldots,\lambda_{j,k,r}]

with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j,k,s}
. Write
P
in the column form

P\,=\,\begin{bmatrix}p_{1}&\cdots&p_{r}\end{bmatrix}.

For each
s=1,\ldots,r
and
j=3,\ldots,m
, let

(4.11)

v^{s,j}\,\coloneqq\,(1,\lambda_{j,2,s},\ldots,\lambda_{j,n_{j},s}).

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
has the rank-
r
decomposition

\mathcal{F}\,=\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}u^{s,1}\otimes...\otimes u^{s,m}.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
,
the linear system (
3.11
)
has a unique solution for each pair
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
.
For every
j\in\{3,...,m\}
, there exist scalars
c_{s,j},c_{s,1}
such that

u^{s,j}\,=\,c_{s,j}v^{s,j},\quad u^{s,1}\,=\,c_{s,1}p_{s}.

Then, we consider the sub-tensor equation
in the vector variables
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}}

(4.12)

\mathcal{F}_{1:r,:,\ldots,:}\,=\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}p_{s}\otimes y_{s}\otimes v^{s,3}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{s,m}.

There are
rn_{2}\cdots n_{m}
equations and
rn_{2}
unknowns.
This overdetermined linear system has solutions such that

y_{s}=c_{s,2}u^{s,2},~\mathrm{for~some}~c_{s,2}\in\mathbb{C}.

After all
y_{s}
are obtained, we solve the linear equation in

z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}-r}

(4.13)

\mathcal{F}_{r+1:n_{1},:,\ldots,:}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}z_{s}\otimes y_{s}\otimes v^{s,3}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{s,m}.

After all
y_{s},z_{s}
are obtained,
we choose the vectors (
s=1,\ldots,r
)

v^{s,1}=\begin{bmatrix}p_{s}\\
z_{s}\end{bmatrix},\quad v^{s,2}=y_{s}.

Then we get the tensor decomposition

(4.14)

\mathcal{F}\,=\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}v^{s,1}\otimes v^{s,2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v^{s,m}.

Summarizing the above, we get the following algorithm
for computing tensor decompositions when
r\leq n_{1}
and
r\leq N_{3}
.
Suppose the dimensions are ordered such that

n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{m}
.

Algorithm 4.2
.

(Rank-
r
tensor decomposition.)

Input:

A tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times...\times n_{m}}

with rank
r\leq\min(n_{1},N_{3})
.

Step 1

For each pair
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
,
solve the matrix equation for the solution
Y^{j,k}
:

(4.15)

A[\mathcal{F},j]Y^{j,k}\,=\,b[\mathcal{F},j,k].

Step 2

Choose generic scalars
\xi_{j,k}
.
Then compute the eigenvalue decomposition
P^{-1}YP=D
for the matrix

Y\,\coloneqq\,\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}}\sum\limits_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}Y^{j,k}.

Step 3

For
s=1,\ldots,r
and
j\geq 3
,
let
v^{s,j}
be the vectors as in (
4.11
).

Step 4

Solve the linear system (
4.12
)
for vectors
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
.

Step 5

Solve the linear system (
4.13
)
for vectors
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
.

Step 6

For each
s=1,\ldots,r
,
let
v^{s,1}=\begin{bmatrix}p_{s}\\
z_{s}\end{bmatrix}
and
v^{s,2}=y_{s}
.

Output:

The tensor rank-
r
decomposition as in (
4.14
).

The correctness of Algorithm
4.2
is justified as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{m}
and

r\leq\min(n_{1},N_{3})
as in (
4.8
).
For a generic tensor
\mathcal{F}
of rank-
r
,
Algorithm
4.2
produces a rank-
r
tensor decomposition for
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

This can be implied by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

4.2.
Tensor decompositions via reshaping

A tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

can be reshaped as a cubic order tensor
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
as in (
2.10
).
One can apply Algorithm
4.2
to compute the tensor decomposition
(
2.10
) for
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
.
If the decomposing vectors
w^{s,1},w^{s,2},w^{s,3}

can be reshaped to rank-
1
tensors, then we can convert
(
2.10
) to a tensor decomposition for
\mathcal{F}
.
This is justified by Theorem
2.3
, under some assumptions.
A benefit for doing this is that we may be able to compute tensor decompositions
for the case that

N_{3}<r\leq p_{2},

with the dimension
p_{2}
as in Theorem
2.3
.
This leads to the following algorithm for computing tensor decompositions.

Algorithm 4.4
.

(Tensor decompositions via reshaping.)
Let
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
be dimensions as in Theorem
2.3
.

Input:

A tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

with rank
r\leq p_{2}
.

Step 1

Reshape the tensor
\mathcal{F}
to
a cubic tensor
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{1}\times p_{2}\times p_{3}}

as in (
2.10
).

Step 2

Use Algorithm
4.2
to compute the
tensor decomposition

(4.16)

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\,=\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}w^{s,1}\otimes w^{s,2}\otimes w^{s,3}.

Step 3

If all
w^{s,1},w^{s,2},w^{s,3}
can be expressed as
outer products of rank-
1
tensors as in (
2.9
),
then output the tensor decomposition as in (
2.1
).
If one of
w^{s,1},w^{s,2},w^{s,3}
cannot be expressed
as in (
2.9
), then the reshaping does not produce
a tensor decomposition for
\mathcal{F}
.

Output:

A tensor decomposition for
\mathcal{F}
as in (
2.1
).

For Algorithm
4.4
,
we have a similar conclusion like Theorem
4.3
.
For cleanness of the paper, we do not repeat it here.

5.
low rank Tensor Approximations

When a tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times...\times n_{m}}

has the rank bigger than
r
, the linear systems in
Algorithm
4.2
may not be consistent.
However, we can find linear least squares solutions for them.
This gives an algorithm for computing low rank tensor approximations.
Recall the label set
\Upsilon
as in (
4.9
).
The following is the algorithm.

Algorithm 5.1
.

(Rank-
r
tensor approximation.)

Input:

A tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times...\times n_{m}}

and a rank
r\leq\min(n_{1},N_{3})
.

Step 1

For each pair
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
,
solve the linear least squares problem

(5.1)

\min\limits_{Y^{j,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}}\quad\bigg\lVert A[\mathcal{F},j](Y^{j,k})^{T}-b[\mathcal{F},j,k]\bigg\rVert^{2}.

Let
\hat{Y}^{j,k}
be an optimizer.

Step 2

Choose generic scalars
\xi_{j,k}

and let

\hat{Y}[\xi]\,=\,\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}}\sum\limits_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}\hat{Y}^{j,k}.

Compute the eigenvalue decomposition

\hat{P}^{-1}\hat{Y}[\xi]\hat{P}=\Lambda
such that

\hat{P}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{p}_{1}&\cdots&\hat{p}_{r}\end{bmatrix}

is invertible and
\Lambda
is diagonal.

Step 3

For each pair
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
,
select the diagonal entries

\mbox{diag}[\hat{\lambda}_{j,k,1}\,\,\hat{\lambda}_{j,k,2}\,\,\ldots\,\,\hat{\lambda}_{j,k,r}]\,=\,\mbox{diag}(\hat{P}^{-1}\hat{Y}^{j,k}\hat{P}).

For each
s=1,\ldots,r
and
j=3,\ldots,m
, let

\hat{v}^{s,j}=(1,\hat{\lambda}_{j,2,2},\ldots,\hat{\lambda}_{j,n_{j},s}).

Step 4

Let
(\hat{y}_{1},\ldots,\hat{y}_{r})

be an optimizer for the following least squares:

(5.2)

\min\limits_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r})}\quad\bigg\lVert\mathcal{F}_{1:r,:,\ldots,:}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}\hat{p}_{s}\otimes y_{s}\otimes\hat{v}^{s,3}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{v}^{s,m}\bigg\rVert^{2}.

Step 5

Let
(\hat{z}_{1},\ldots,\hat{z}_{r})

be an optimizer for the following least squares:

(5.3)

\min\limits_{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})}\quad\bigg\lVert\mathcal{F}_{r+1:n_{1},:,\ldots,:}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}z_{s}\otimes\hat{y}_{s}\otimes\hat{v}^{s,3}\otimes\cdots\otimes\hat{v}^{s,m}\bigg\rVert^{2}.

Step 6

Let
\hat{v}^{s,1}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{p}_{s}\\
\hat{z}_{s}\end{bmatrix}

and
\hat{v}^{s,2}=\hat{y}_{s}
for each

s=1,\ldots,r
.

Output:

The rank-
r
approximation tensor

(5.4)

\mathcal{X}^{gp}\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}\hat{v}^{s,1}\otimes\hat{v}^{s,2}\otimes...\otimes\hat{v}^{s,m}.

If
\mathcal{F}
is sufficiently close to a rank-
r
tensor,
then
\mathcal{X}^{gp}
is expected to be a good rank-
r
approximation.
Mathematically, the tensor
\mathcal{X}^{gp}
produced by Algorithm
5.1

may not be the best rank-
r
approximation. However, in computational practice,
we can use (
5.4
) as a starting point
to solve the nonlinear least squares optimization

(5.5)

\min\limits_{(u^{s,1},\ldots,u^{s,m})}\bigg\lVert\mathcal{F}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}u^{s,1}\otimes u^{s,2}\otimes...\otimes u^{s,m}\bigg\rVert^{2}.

to improve the approximation quality.
Let
\mathcal{X}^{opt}
be the rank-
r
approximation tensor

(5.6)

\mathcal{X}^{opt}\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}u^{s,1}\otimes u^{s,2}\otimes...\otimes u^{s,m}

which is an optimizer to (
5.5
) obtained by
nonlinear optimization methods with

\mathcal{X}^{opt}
as the initial point.

5.1.
Approximation error analysis

Suppose the tensor
\mathcal{F}
has the best (or nearly best) rank-
r
approximation

(5.7)

\mathcal{X}^{bs}\,\coloneqq\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}(x^{s,1})\otimes(x^{s,2})\otimes...\otimes(x^{s,m}).

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the tensor such that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{X}^{bs}+\mathcal{E}.

We analyze the approximation performance of
\mathcal{X}^{gp}
when the distance

\epsilon=\|\mathcal{E}\|
is small.
For a generating matrix
G
and a generic
\xi=(\xi_{j,k})_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}
,
denote that

(5.9)

M[\xi,G]\,\coloneqq\,\frac{1}{\sum\limits_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}}\sum\limits_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}M^{j,k}[G].

Recall the
A[\mathcal{F},j]
,
b[\mathcal{F},j,k]
as in (
3.10
).
Note that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A[\mathcal{F},j]=&~A[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j]+A[\mathcal{\mathcal{E}},j],\\
b[\mathcal{F},j,k]=&~b[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j,k]+b[\mathcal{\mathcal{E}},j,k].\end{split}

Suppose
\left(x^{s,j}\right)_{1}\neq 0
for
j=2,\ldots,m
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}^{gp}
be produced by Algorithm
5.1
.
Let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{X}^{bs},\mathcal{X}^{opt},\mathcal{E},x^{s,j},\xi_{j,k}
be as above.
Assume the following conditions hold:

(i)

The subvectors
(x^{1,1})_{1:r},\ldots,(x^{r,1})_{1:r}
are linearly independent.

(ii)

All matrices
A[\mathcal{F},j]

and
A[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j]
(
3\leq j\leq m
) have full column rank.

(iii)

The first entry

\left(x^{s,j}\right)_{1}\neq 0
for all
j=2,\ldots,m
.

(iv)

The following scalars are pairwisely distinct

(5.11)

\sum_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}(x^{1,j})_{k},...,\sum_{(j,k)\in\Upsilon}\xi_{j,k}(x^{r,j})_{k}.

If the distance
\epsilon=\lVert\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{X}^{bs}\rVert

is sufficiently small, then

(5.12)

\lVert\mathcal{X}^{bs}-\mathcal{X}^{gp}\rVert=O(\epsilon)\quad\quad\quad\lVert\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{X}^{gp}\rVert=O(\epsilon).

where the constants in the above
O(\cdot)
only depend on
\mathcal{F}
and
\xi
.

Proof.

By conditions (i) and (iii) and by Theorem
3.4
,
there exists a generating matrix
G^{bs}
for
\mathcal{X}^{bs}
such that

(5.13)

A[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j](M^{j,k}[G^{bs}])^{T}\,=\,b[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j,k]

for all
j\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
and
k\in\{2,\ldots,n_{j}\}
.
Note that
Y^{j,k}
is the least squares solution to (
5.1
),
so for each
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
,

Y^{j,k}=A[\mathcal{F},j]^{\dagger}\cdot b[\mathcal{F},j,k],\quad M^{j,k}[G^{bs}_{0}]=A[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j]^{\dagger}\cdot b[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j,k].

(The super script
†
denotes the Pseudo-inverse of a matrix.)
By (
5.8
), for
j=2,\ldots,m
, we have

(5.14)

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|A[\mathcal{F},j]-A[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j]\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{X}^{bs}\right\|\leq\epsilon,\\
\left\|b[\mathcal{F},j,k]-b[\mathcal{X}^{bs},j,k]\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{X}^{bs}\right\|\leq\epsilon.\end{array}

Hence, by the condition (ii), if
\epsilon>0
is small enough, we have

(5.15)

\left\|Y^{j,k}-M^{j,k}[G^{bs}]\right\|=O(\epsilon).

for all
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
.
This follows from perturbation analysis for linear least squares
(see
[
12
, Theorem 3.4]
).

By (
5.7
) and Theorem
3.4
,
for
s=1,\ldots,r
and
(j,k)\in\Upsilon
, it holds that

M^{j,k}[G^{bs}]\left(x^{s,1}\right)_{1:r}\quad=\quad\left(x^{s,j}\right)_{k}\left(x^{s,1}\right)_{1:r}.

This means that each
\left(x^{s,1}\right)_{1:r}

is an eigenvector of
M^{j,k}[G^{bs}]
,
associated to the eigenvalue
\left(x^{s,j}\right)_{k}
,
for each
s=

1,\ldots,r
.
The matrices
M^{j,k}[G^{bs}]
are simultaneously diagonalizable,
by the condition (i). So
M[\xi,G^{bs}]
is also diagonalizable.
Note the eigenvalues of
M[\xi,G^{bs}]
are the sums in (
5.11
).
They are distinct from each other, by the condition (iv).
When
\epsilon>0
is small enough,
M[\xi,G^{bs}]
also has distinct eigenvalues.
Write that

Q=\begin{bmatrix}(x^{1,1})_{1:r}&\cdots&(x^{r,1})_{1:r}\end{bmatrix}.

Note that
Q^{-1}M[\xi,G^{bs}]Q=D

is an eigenvalue decomposition.
Up to a scaling on
\hat{P}
in algorithm
5.1
, it holds that

(5.16)

\|\hat{p}_{s}-x^{s,1}\|_{2}=O(\epsilon),\quad\|D-\Lambda\|_{F}=O(\epsilon).

We refer to
[
2
]

for the perturbation bounds in (
5.16
).
The constants in the above
O(\cdot)
eventually only depend on
\mathcal{F},\xi
.

Note that
(\hat{y}_{s},\ldots,\hat{y}_{r})
is the least squares
solution to (
5.2
) and

(5.17)

\mathcal{X}^{bs}_{1:r,:,\ldots,:}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}x^{s,1}\otimes x^{s,2}\otimes x^{s,3}\otimes\cdots\otimes x^{s,m}.

Due to perturbation analysis of linear least squares, we also have

(5.18)

\|\hat{y}_{s}-x^{s,2}\|_{2}=O(\epsilon).

Note that the subvectors
(x^{s,1})_{r+1:n_{1}}

satisfy the equation

(5.19)

\mathcal{X}^{bs}_{r+1:n_{1},:,\ldots,:}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}(x^{s,1})_{r+1:n_{1}}\otimes x^{s,2}\otimes\cdots\otimes x^{s,m}.

Recall that
(\hat{z}_{1},\ldots,\hat{z}_{r})

is the least squares solution to (
5.3
).
Due to perturbation analysis of linear least squares,
we further have the error bound

(5.20)

\|(x^{s,1})_{r+1:n_{1}}-\hat{z}_{s}\|_{2}=O(\epsilon).

Summarizing the above, we eventually get

\|\mathcal{X}^{gp}-\mathcal{X}^{bs}\|=O(\epsilon)
, so

\left\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{X}^{gp}\right\|\leq\left\|\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{X}^{bs}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{X}^{bs}-\mathcal{X}^{gp}\right\|=O(\epsilon).

The constant for the above
O(\cdot)
eventually only
depends on
\mathcal{F}
,
\xi
.
∎

5.2.
Reshaping for low rank approximations

Similar to tensor decompositions, the reshaping trick as in
Section
4.2
can also be
used for computing low rank tensor approximations.
For
m>3
, a tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\cdots\times n_{m}}

can be reshaped as a cubic tensor
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{1}\times p_{2}\times p_{3}}

as in (
2.10
).
Similarly, Algorithm
5.1
can be used to compute
low rank tensor approximations.
Suppose the computed rank-
r
approximating tensor
for
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is

(5.21)

\widehat{\mathcal{X}}^{gp}:=\sum_{s=1}^{r}\hat{w}^{s,1}\otimes\hat{w}^{s,2}\otimes\hat{w}^{s,3}.

Typically, the decomposing vectors

\hat{w}^{s,1},\hat{w}^{s,2},\hat{w}^{s,3}

may not be reshaped to rank-
1
tensors.
Suppose the reshaping is such that

I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup I_{3}=\{1,2,...,m\}

is a union of disjoint label sets and the reshaped dimensions are

p_{1}=\prod_{i\in I_{1}}n_{i},\quad p_{2}=\prod_{i\in I_{2}}n_{i},\quad p_{3}=\prod_{i\in I_{3}}n_{i}.

Let
m_{i}=|I_{i}|
for
i=1,2,3
. By the reshaping, the vectors

\hat{w}^{s,i}
can be reshaped back to a tensor

\hat{W}^{s,i}
of order
m_{i}
, for each
i=1,2,3
.
If
m_{i}=1
,
\hat{W}^{s,i}
is a vector.
If
m_{i}=2
, we can find a best rank-
1

matrix approximation for
\hat{W}^{s,i}
.
If
m_{i}\geq 3
, we can apply Algorithm
5.1
with
r=1

to get a rank-
1
approximation for
\hat{W}^{s,i}
.
In application, we are mostly interested in reshaping such that
all
m_{i}\leq 2
. Finally, this produces
a rank-
r
approximation for
\mathcal{F}
.

The following is a low rank tensor approximation
algorithm via reshaping tensors.

Algorithm 5.3
.

(low rank tensor approximations via reshaping.)

Input

A tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times...\times n_{m}}
and a rank
r
.

Step 1

Reshape
\mathcal{F}
to a cubic order tensor

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{1}\times p_{2}\times p_{3}}
.

Step 2

Use Algorithm
5.1
to compute
a rank-
r
approximating tensor

\widehat{\mathcal{X}}^{gp}
as in (
5.21
) for
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
.

Step 3

For each
i=1,2,3
, reshape each vector
\hat{w}^{s,i}

back to a tensor
\widehat{W}^{s,i}
of order
m_{i}
as above.

Step 4

For each
i=1,2,3
, compute a rank-
1
approximating
tensor
\widehat{X}^{s,i}
for
\widehat{W}^{s,i}
of order
m_{i}
as above.

Output

Reshape the sum

\sum\limits_{s=1}^{r}\widehat{X}^{s,1}\otimes\widehat{X}^{s,2}\otimes\widehat{X}^{s,3}

to a tensor in
\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times...\times n_{m}}
,
which is a rank-
r
approximation for
\mathcal{F}
.

We can do a similar approximation analysis for Algorithm
5.3

as for Theorem
5.2
.
For cleanness of the paper, we do not repeat that.

In the following, we give a comparison with
the generalized eigenvalue decomposition (GEVD) method,
which is a classical one for computing tensor decompositions
when the rank
r\leq n_{2}
. We refer to

[
23
,
34
]

for the work about the GEVD method.
Consider a cubic order tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

with
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq n_{3}
.
Suppose
\mathcal{F}=U^{(1)}\circ U^{(2)}\circ U^{(3)}

is a rank-
r
decomposition and
r\leq n_{2}
.
Assume its first and second decomposing matrices
U^{(1)},U^{(2)}

have full column ranks and the third decomposing matrix

U^{(3)}
does not have colinear columns.
Denote the slice matrices

(6.1)

F_{1}\,\coloneqq\,\mathcal{F}_{1:r,1:r,1},\quad F_{2}\,\coloneqq\,\mathcal{F}_{1:r,1:r,2}.

One can show that

(6.2)

\displaystyle F_{1}=U^{(1)}_{1:r,:}\cdot\mbox{diag}(U^{(3)}_{1,:})\cdot(U^{(2)}_{1:r,:})^{T},\quad F_{2}=U^{(1)}_{1:r,:}\cdot\mbox{diag}(U^{(3)}_{2,:})\cdot(U^{(2)}_{1:r,:})^{T}.

This implies that the columns of
(U^{(1)}_{1:r,r})^{-T}

are generalized eigenvectors of the matrix pair
(F_{1}^{T},F_{2}^{T})
.
Consider the transformed tensor

(6.3)

\hat{\mathcal{F}}\,=\,(U^{(1)}_{1:r,r})^{-1}\times_{1}\mathcal{F}_{1:r,:,:},

For each
s=1,\ldots,r
, the slice

\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{s,:,:}=U^{(2)}_{:,s}\cdot(U^{(3)}_{:,s})^{T}

is a rank-
1
matrix.
The matrices
U^{(2)}
,
U^{(3)}
can be obtained
by computing rank-
1
decompositions
for the slices
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{s,:,:}
.
After this is done, we can solve the linear system

(6.4)

U^{(1)}\circ U^{(2)}\circ U^{(3)}=\mathcal{F}

to get the matrix
U^{(1)}
.
The following is the GEVD method for
computing cubic order tensor decompositions
when the rank
r\leq n_{2}
.

Algorithm 6.6
.

(The GEVD method.)

Input:

A tensor
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}

with the rank
r\leq n_{2}
.

1.

Formulate the tensor
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
as in (
6.3
).

2.

For
s=1,\ldots,r,
compute
U^{(2)}_{:,s}
,
U^{(3)}_{:,s}
from
the rank-
1
decomposition of the matrix
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{s,:,:}
.

3.

Solve the linear system (
6.4
) to get
U^{(1)}
.

Output:

The decomposing matrices
U^{(1)},U^{(2)},U^{(3)}.

Conclusions

.

This paper gives computational methods for
computing low rank tensor decompositions and approximations.
The proposed methods are based on generating polynomials.
For a generic tensor of rank
r\leq\mathrm{min}(n_{1},N_{3})
,
its tensor decomposition can be obtained by Algorithm
4.2
.
Under some general assumptions, we show that if
a tensor is sufficiently close to a low rank one,
then the low rank approximating tensor produced by Algorithm
5.1

is quasi-optimal. Numerical experiments are
presented to show the efficiency of the proposed methods.

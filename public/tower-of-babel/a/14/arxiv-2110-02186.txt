Steady State in Ultrastrong Coupling Regime: Expansion and First Orders
Camille Lombard Latune

Abstract




U
nderstanding better the dynamics and steady states of systems strongly coupled to thermal baths is a great theoretical challenge with promising applications in several fields of quantum technologies. Among several strategies to gain access to the steady state, one consists in obtaining approximate expressions of the mean force Gibbs state, the reduced state of the global system-bath thermal state, largely credited to be the steady state. Here, we present analytical expressions of corrective terms to the ultrastrong coupling limit of the mean force Gibbs state, which has been recently derived. We find that the first order term precisely coincides with the first order correction obtained from a dynamical approach—master equation in the strong-decoherence regime. This strengthens the identification of the reduced steady state with the mean force Gibbs state. Additionally, we also compare our expressions with another recent result obtained from a high temperature expansion of the mean force Gibbs state. We observe numerically a good agreement for ultra strong coupling as well as for high temperatures. This confirms the validity of all these results. In particular, we show that, in term of coherences, all three results allow one to sketch the transition from ultrastrong coupling to weak coupling.

2

Mean force Gibbs state

We consider the following total Hamiltonian

{\cal H}_{SB}=H_{S}+H_{B}+\lambda H_{I},

(1)

where
H_{B}:=\sum_{k}\omega_{k}a_{k}^{{\dagger}}a_{k}
is the bath Hamiltonian composed of the bosonic creation and annihilation operators
a_{k}^{{\dagger}}
and
a_{k}
,
\lambda
is a dimensionless parameter representing the strength of the system-bath coupling,
H_{I}:=AB
is the system-bath coupling term, and
B:=\sum_{k}g_{k}(a_{k}+a_{k}^{{\dagger}})
is the usual coupling bath operator.
As in the quantum Brownian motion
[
28
]
, there is an extra term
\lambda^{2}QA^{2}
appearing during the derivation of the solution which corresponds to the renormalization of the system’s energies due to the interaction with the bath. This is also taken into account explicitly in
[
25
]
. The quantity
Q
, the “re-organization energy”
[
55
,
25
]
, is defined as
Q:=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega J(\omega)/\omega
, where
J(\omega)
is the bath spectral density,
J(\omega)=\sum_{k}g_{k}^{2}\delta(\omega-\omega_{k})
. Such extra term is often added initially, “by hand”, so that it cancels out later on during the calculation when the renormalization takes place. Following this procedure, we add the extra term
\lambda^{2}QA^{2}
so that the total Hamiltonian is now

H_{SB}:=H_{S}+\lambda^{2}QA^{2}+\lambda AB+H_{B}.

(2)

Note the difference of notation between the “natural” Hamiltonian
{\cal H}_{SB}
(
1
) and the renormalized Hamiltonian
H_{SB}
(
2
).
As a matter of completeness, we also mention in Section
7.5
the derivation starting from the “natural” Hamiltonian (
1
), which leads to renormalized final energies (or pseudo-energies). This shows in particular that both derivations are equivalent, as expected, but that the renormalization has to take place at some stage, either initially or finally.

As mentioned in the introduction, the global steady state of the system and bath is expected to be the global thermal state (assuming
[H_{S},A]\neq 0
)
[
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
,
43
]

\rho_{SB}^{\textrm{th}}:=Z_{SB}^{-1}e^{-\beta H_{SB}},

(3)

where
Z_{SB}:={\textrm{Tr}}_{SB}\left(e^{-\beta H_{SB}}\right)
is the partition function. Then, the corresponding reduced steady state is

\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}:=Z_{SB}^{-1}{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}[e^{-\beta H_{SB}}],

(4)

which is called mean force Gibbs state
[
25
,
44
,
43
]
.
We are going to approximate this state in the regime of ultrastrong coupling, when
\lambda
becomes larger than the energy scale of
H_{S}
.

3

General derivation

We first split
H_{SB}
in two,
H_{S}
on one hand, and
\lambda^{2}QA^{2}+\lambda AB+H_{B}
on the other hand, and take it out of the exponential using the usual identities
[
56
]
. It leads to

\displaystyle e^{-\beta H_{SB}}=e^{-\beta(H_{B}+\lambda AB+\lambda^{2}QA^{2})}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\tilde{H}_{S}(u)},

(5)

with
{\cal T}
representing the “
\beta
-ordering operator”, acting on inverse temperatures in the same way as the usual time ordering operator acts on exponential time integrals, and

\tilde{H}_{S}(u):=e^{u(H_{B}+\lambda AB+\lambda^{2}QA^{2})}H_{S}e^{-u(H_{B}+\lambda AB+\lambda^{2}QA^{2})}.

(6)

Now, by noticing that

H_{B}+\lambda AB+\lambda^{2}QA^{2}=\sum_{k}\omega_{k}{\cal D}_{k}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}{\cal D}_{k}^{{\dagger}}
,
where
{\cal D}_{k}:=e^{-\frac{\lambda g_{k}}{\omega_{k}}(b_{k}^{{\dagger}}-b_{k})A}
is a displacement operator by an “amount”
\lambda g_{k}A/\omega_{k}
acting on the mode
k
, we can re-write
H_{B}+\lambda AB+\lambda^{2}QA^{2}
as a “mixture of displaced baths”. To see that, we denote by
a_{l}
and
|a_{l}\rangle
the eigenvalues (assumed to be non-degenerate for simplicity) and corresponding eigenvectors of the observable
A
, and by
{\cal D}_{k,l}:=e^{-\frac{\lambda g_{k}}{\omega_{k}}(b_{k}^{{\dagger}}-b_{k})a_{l}}
the displacement operator by the quantity
\lambda g_{k}a_{l}/\omega_{k}
acting only on the mode
k
. Then, we have

\displaystyle H_{B}+\lambda AB+\lambda^{2}QA^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\omega_{k}{\cal D}_{k}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}{\cal D}_{k}^{{\dagger}}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\omega_{k}\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|{\cal D}_{k}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}{\cal D}_{k}^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\omega_{k}\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|{\cal D}_{k,l}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}{\cal D}_{k,l}^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|H_{B,l},

where we defined the “displaced bath”
H_{B,l}:=\sum_{k}\omega_{k}{\cal D}_{k,l}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}{\cal D}_{k,l}^{{\dagger}}=H_{B}+\lambda a_{l}B+\lambda^{2}a_{l}^{2}Q
, reminiscent of the displaced oscillator picture
[
49
,
50
]
. From there we obtain,

\displaystyle e^{u(H_{B}+\lambda AB+\lambda^{2}QA^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{u\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|H_{B,l}}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|e^{uH_{B,l}},

so that

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{S}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,l^{\prime}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|e^{uH_{B,l}}H_{S}|a_{l^{\prime}}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}+\tilde{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u),

where
H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}:=\sum_{l}h_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|
and
\tilde{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u):=\sum_{l\neq l^{\prime}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}
,
defining
h_{l}:=\langle a_{l}|H_{S}|a_{l}\rangle
, and
h_{l,l^{\prime}}:=\langle a_{l}|H_{S}|a_{l^{\prime}}\rangle
.

Taking out
H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}
from the exponential
e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\tilde{H}_{S}(u)}
, we obtain,

\displaystyle e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\tilde{H}_{S}(u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\beta H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)},

(10)

with

\displaystyle\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle e^{uH_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}}\tilde{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)e^{-uH_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\neq l^{\prime}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}e^{u\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}},

defining
\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}:=h_{l}-h_{l^{\prime}}
. Combining the identities (
5
), (
8
) and (
10
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\rho_{SB}^{\textrm{th}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{SB}^{-1}\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{-\beta H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{SB}^{-1}\sum_{l}e^{-\beta h_{l}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)}.

This leads to the following expression for the mean force Gibbs state,

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{SB}^{-1}\sum_{l}e^{-\beta h_{l}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)}\right].

The toughest part is, as expected,

{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)}\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\int_{0}^{\beta}du_{1}\int_{0}^{u_{1}}du_{2}...\int_{0}^{u_{n-1}}du_{n}{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{1})\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{2})...\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{n})\right].

(14)

In the following, when we mention “first order term” or “higher order terms”, we refer to the terms appearing in the above expansion (
3
).
So far, we made no approximation, making (
3
) an exact expression. Since this problem is not exactly solvable, we have to make some approximations in order to reach an explicit form. Before that, let us see how one can recover the infinite coupling limit.

3.1

Recovering the infinite coupling limit

From expression (
3
), it might not be obvious how one recovers the infinite coupling limit. It actually comes from the terms
\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)
which contain overlaps between displaced baths,
e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}
. The coupling strength between
S
and each mode of the bath is given by
\lambda g_{k}
. Thus, when
\lambda
goes to infinity, the displaced baths
H_{B,l}
and
H_{B,l^{\prime}}
tend to be displaced infinitely far apart from each other (for
l\neq l^{\prime}
). Consequently, the overlap and the expectation value
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime\prime}}}]
tends to zero for increasing coupling strength.

Extending this reasoning to higher order terms, we see that all terms
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{1})\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{2})...\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{n})\right]
contain multiple overlaps of different displaced baths, and therefore tends to zero as the coupling strength increases. In Section
7.3
, we show numerically (for a two-level systems) that the higher order terms tend to zero as the coupling strength increases.
Thus, in the infinite coupling limit, only the first term of the sum in (
3
) is different from zero. It leads to

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss},\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{SB}^{-1}\sum_{l}e^{-\beta h_{l}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\right]

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Z_{S}^{\textrm{ss}})^{-1}\sum_{l}e^{-\beta h_{l}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|,

where
Z_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}:=\frac{Z_{SB}}{Z_{B}}
and
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\right]={\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B}}\right]:=Z_{B}
is the partition function of the uncoupled bath. Note that since
\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss},\infty}
is a normalized state, we also have the identity
Z_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}=\sum_{l}e^{-\beta h_{l}}
.
The above expression (
15
) is exactly equal to the one derived in
[
25
]
, and recently shown to coincide with the steady state in the ultrastrong coupling limit
[
44
]
.

3.2

First approximation

As seen in the previous section, the term
\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)
contains overlaps of displaced baths, so that terms
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{1})\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{2})...\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u_{n})\right]
of increasing order contain overlaps of increasing order and are therefore significantly smaller than terms of lower orders (for large coupling strength). With this assumption, we are going to retain only the first and second term,

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle Z_{SB}^{-1}\sum_{l}e^{-\beta h_{l}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|\Bigg\{{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\right]

(16)

\displaystyle-{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)\right]\Bigg\}.

The first term gives
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\right]=Z_{B}
, as already seen in the previous section. The second term gives,

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)\right]=\sum_{l^{\prime}\neq l^{\prime\prime}}h_{l^{\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}|a_{l^{\prime}}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime\prime}}|

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{u\omega_{l^{\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}}{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime\prime}}}\right].

(17)

Since, when injected in (
16
), this expression will be multiplied by
|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|
on the left-hand side, one only needs to compute

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime}\neq l}h_{l,l^{\prime}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{u\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right],

where the index
l
and
l^{\prime}
were made equal. After some manipulations, we can obtain the following expression for
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]
(details provided in Section
7.1
),

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]

\displaystyle=Z_{B}e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}(e^{u\omega}-1)\left(1-\frac{1-e^{-u\omega}}{1-e^{-\omega\beta}}\right)},

(19)

where
a_{l^{\prime},l}:=a_{l^{\prime}}-a_{l}
. We finally obtain, up to second order,

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}p_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|-\sum_{l,l^{\prime};l\neq l^{\prime}}p_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|,

with
p_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}:=e^{-\beta h_{l}}/Z_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}
and

\displaystyle f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{u\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}(e^{u\omega}-1)\left(1-\frac{1-e^{-\omega u}}{1-e^{-\omega\beta}}\right)}.

Note that we have the following identity (see Section
7.2
)
p_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)=p_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}f_{l^{\prime},l}(\beta)
, implying
\langle a_{l}|\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}|a_{l^{\prime}}\rangle^{*}=\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}|a_{l}\rangle
, as it should be.

Additionally,
f_{+,-}(\beta)
tends to zero when
\lambda
goes to infinity, so that we recover the infinite coupling limit (
15
). One can also see that the first order corrections affect only the coherences (in the eigenbasis of
A
). These observations coincide with the ones in
[
44
]
.
Finally, the expression obtained starting from the natural Hamiltonian (
1
) instead of the renormalized one (
2
) are the same as (
3.2
) and (
3.2
) but substituting the pseudo-energies
h_{l}
by the renormalized ones
h_{l}-\lambda^{2}a_{l}^{2}Q
(see Section
7.5
).

3.3

Approximate expression of
f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

Depending on the bath spectral density, it might not be possible to obtain an exact analytical expression of
f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)
, so that some approximations would have to be made. However, assuming the bath temperature is high, or equivalently that the dominant frequencies in the bath are low, we can obtain an approximate expression of
f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)
witout even specifying the form of the spectral density.
More precisely, we assume that
J(\omega)
vanishes for
\omega\geq\omega_{c}
, where
\omega_{c}\leq\beta^{-1}
. Then, for
\omega\in[0;\beta^{-1}]
, the factor
(e^{u\omega}-1)\left(1-\frac{1-e^{-u\omega}}{1-e^{-\omega\beta}}\right)
can be approximated by

\frac{e^{u\omega}-1}{\omega^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1-e^{-u\omega}}{1-e^{-\omega\beta}}\right)=\frac{u}{\omega}\left(1-\frac{u}{\beta}\right)+{\cal O}(\omega\beta),

(22)

which is actually a very good approximation as soon as
\omega\beta\leq 1
.
With that we obtain

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}(e^{u\omega}-1)\left(1-\frac{1-e^{-\omega u}}{1-e^{-\omega\beta}}\right)}\simeq e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}u\left(1-\frac{u}{\beta}\right)Q},

and

\displaystyle f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{u\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}u\left(1-\frac{u}{\beta}\right)Q}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\frac{1}{\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}\Bigg\{{\textrm{DF}}\left[\frac{1}{2\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}(\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q-\omega_{l,l^{\prime}})\right]

\displaystyle+e^{\beta\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}{\textrm{DF}}\left[\frac{1}{2\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}(\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q+\omega_{l,l^{\prime}})\right]\Bigg\},

where
{\textrm{DF}}(x):=e^{-x^{2}}\int_{0}^{x}{\textrm{d}}u\penalty\ e^{u^{2}}
is sometimes referred to as the Dawson function.

The above expression can be further simplified as follows.
The strong coupling regime can be characterized by a re-organization energy
\lambda^{2}Q
comparable to, or larger than, the energy scale of the system
[
25
,
26
]
. Thus, in the strong coupling regime one can expect to have

\lambda^{2}Q\gg{\textrm{max}}_{l}\penalty\ |h_{l}|
, implying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}(\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q\pm\omega_{l,l^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q

(25)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sqrt{\beta\lambda^{2}Q}.

Finally, since
DF(x)=\frac{1}{2x}+{\cal O}(x^{-3})
for
x\gg 1
(the approximation is actually very good for
x\geq 3
), we can find the following simple approximate expression for
f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)
asuming
\lambda^{2}Q\gg\beta^{-1}
,

f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)=\frac{1+e^{\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}\beta}}{\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q}+\frac{\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}(1-e^{\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}\beta})}{\lambda^{4}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{4}Q^{2}}+{\cal O}[(\lambda^{2}Q\beta)^{-3}].

(26)

4

Example: spin-boson model

As illustration of our results, we consider the versatile and famous spin-boson model
[
57
]
characterised by the following total Hamiltonian

{\cal H}_{SB}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{\Delta}{2}\sigma_{x}+\lambda\sigma_{z}B+H_{B},

(27)

where
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{z}
are the Pauli matrices. The spin Hamiltonian
H_{S}
can be re-written as

H_{S}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{\Delta}{2}\sigma_{x}=\frac{\omega_{S}}{2}\big(|e\rangle\langle e|-|g\rangle\langle g|\big),

(28)

with
\omega_{S}:=\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+\Delta^{2}}
,

\displaystyle|e\rangle:=\frac{(\omega_{S}+\epsilon)|+\rangle+\Delta|-\rangle}{\sqrt{2\omega_{S}(\omega_{S}+\epsilon)}},

\displaystyle|g\rangle:=\frac{-\Delta|+\rangle+(\omega_{S}+\epsilon)|-\rangle}{\sqrt{2\omega_{S}(\omega_{S}+\epsilon)}},

(29)

and
|\pm\rangle
denotes the eigenstates of
\sigma_{z}

\sigma_{z}

a_{l=\pm}=\pm 1

a_{l,l^{\prime}}=a_{+,-}=-a_{-,+}=2

h_{l,l^{\prime}}=h_{+,-}=h_{-,+}=\Delta/2
, and
\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}=h_{l}-h_{l^{\prime}}=\omega_{+-}=-\omega_{-+}=\epsilon
. Additionally, since
\sigma_{z}^{2}={\mathbb{I}}

H_{SB}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{\Delta}{2}\sigma_{x}+\lambda\sigma_{z}B+H_{B}+\lambda^{2}Q,

which simply corresponds to redefining the origin of the spin energy. This can also be verified from the renormalized pseudo-energies defined in Section

{\mathbf{h}}_{l=\pm}:=h_{l=\pm}-a_{l}^{2}\lambda^{2}Q=\pm\epsilon/2-\lambda^{2}Q
. Thus, for the spin-boson model, the energy renormalization induced by the interaction with the bath has no impact on the reduced steady state.

\displaystyle f_{-,+}(\beta)

Comparison with previous results

In a recent paper
[
44
]
, Trushechkin obtains corrections to the ultrastrong coupling limit by deriving and solving a strong-decoherence regime master equation (generalization of the ultra-strong coupling regime). Additionally, a high temperature expansion of the mean force Gibbs state was recently derived in
[
53
]
.
In this section, we compare our result with the aforementioned ones. We start by briefly introducing them.

Trushechkin.
In
[
44
]
, the author derives the first order correction to the steady state in the ultra strong coupling limit, which actually coincides with the general expressions (
3.2
) and (
3.2
) (see details in Section
7.6
). As a technical note, the results in
[
44
]
are actually valid when the bath spectral density satisfies
\lim_{\omega\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}>0
, and the equivalence of the first order correction in
[
44
]
with (
3.2
) and (
3.2
) is guaranteed only within this condition on the bath spectral density (satisfied by usual spectral densities).

As a sanity check for the numerical simulations below, we compare, for the spin-boson model, our approximate expression (
33
) of
f_{+,-}(\beta)
with the high temperature approximation in
[
44
]
based on an over-damped spectral density, also sometimes called Lorentz–Drude spectral density,

J(\omega)=\frac{2Q}{\pi}\frac{\omega_{c}\omega}{\omega_{c}^{2}+\omega^{2}},

(35)

where
Q
is precisely the re-organization energy associated with the over-damped spectral density, and
\omega_{c}
represents the cutoff frequency.
Note that the dimensionless factor
\lambda
is not explicitly present in
[
44
]
, but one can make it appear by multiplying the bath spectral density by
\lambda^{2}
. Then, the reorganisation energy becomes
\lambda^{2}Q
.
Additionally, the steady state populations obtained in
[
44
]
correspond to the one obtained for infinite coupling limit
[
25
]
, as we also derived in (
15
) and (
3.2
).

Timofeev & Trushechkin.
The derivation in
[
53
]
, a generalization to arbitrary system-bath coupling of
[
54
]
, consists in expressing the mean force Gibbs state through approximating the Hamiltonian of mean force. In other words,
\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}=Z_{SB}^{-1}{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}[e^{-\beta H_{SB}}]
is expressed in the form
Z_{MF}^{-1}e^{-\beta H_{MF}}
, and an approximated expression of
H_{MF}
, the Hamiltonian of mean force, is provided to second order in
\beta
in the following form
[
53
]

\displaystyle H_{MF}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{S}^{\textrm{diag}}-\Lambda A^{2}+\sum_{n\neq m}J_{nm}e^{-\beta\Lambda(a_{n}-a_{m})^{2}/6}|n\rangle\langle m|

(36)

\displaystyle+{\cal O}(\beta^{4}),

where
\Lambda
is the re-organization energy,
A=\sum_{n}a_{n}|n\rangle\langle n|
is the system operator coupling with the bath, as previously,
H_{S}^{\textrm{diag}}=\sum_{n}\langle n|H_{S}|n\rangle|n\rangle\langle n|
, and
J_{nm}:=\langle n|H_{S}|m\rangle

\displaystyle H_{MF}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}h_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|-\lambda^{2}QA^{2}

(37)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l\neq l^{\prime}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}e^{-\beta\lambda^{2}Q(a_{l}-a_{l}^{\prime})^{2}/6}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|.

The term
-\lambda^{2}QA^{2}

7

Z_{MF}={\textrm{Tr}}_{B}[e^{-\beta H_{MF}}]
.

\displaystyle H_{MF}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sigma_{z}-\lambda^{2}Q+\frac{\Delta}{2}e^{-2\beta\lambda^{2}Q/3}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{\Delta}{2}e^{-2\beta\lambda^{2}Q/3}\sigma_{x},

[

and applied to the present spin-boson model is

\displaystyle\rho_{MF}=Z_{MF}^{-1}\left[e^{-\omega_{S}^{\prime}\beta/2}|e^{\prime}\rangle\langle e^{\prime}|+e^{\omega_{S}^{\prime}\beta/2}|g^{\prime}\rangle\langle g^{\prime}|\right],

(39)

with
\omega_{S}^{\prime}:=\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}+\Delta^{\prime 2}}
,
\Delta^{\prime}:=e^{-2\beta\lambda^{2}Q/3}\Delta

4

\sigma_{z}

6

Conclusion

We have obtained a perturbative expansion (
3
) and (
14
) of the mean force Gibbs state in the ultra strong coupling regime, using a technique inspired from the displaced oscillator picture
[
49
,
50
]
and polaron transformation. The zero order term confirms the result of
[
25
,
44
]
.
Analytical expression of the first order term is derived, (
3.2
) and (
3.2
), and is found to coincide with the first order correction derived in
[
44
]
(within the mild validity condition of
[
44
]
, see Section
7.6
for more detail). This increases the range of the known situations in which the steady state of a system interacting with a thermal bath does converge to the mean force Gibbs state. This convergence was recently shown for weak coupling up to the first order
[
40
,
45
,
24
]
, for the ultra strong coupling regime up to the zeroth order
[
25
]
, and is now confirmed in the ultra-strong coupling regime up to the first order, thanks to the complementary results in
[
44
]
.

In the high temperature regime, a simple and explicit general expression is derived (
3.3
) for the first order corrections. This expression is numerically compared for the spin-boson model with the result of
[
53
]
obtained by high temperature expansion of the mean force Gibbs state. For sanity check, a comparison of (
3.3
) with the high temperature approximation of
[
44
]
is also made, and the very good agreement in all regimes of parameters is confirmed.
Very good agreement is also observed at ultra strong coupling as well as high temperatures between our result and the one from
[
53
]
. This strengthens the validity of all three approaches. Thanks to them, we are able to draft the transition from the ultra-strong coupling regime to the weak coupling regime, Fig.
1
b.

However, going further, significant discrepancies emerge between our result and
[
53
]
at moderate coupling strength and moderate temperatures.
For those values of parameters, further analysis with higher orders are needed in order to confirm which result is the more accurate, as well as to reconstruct accurately the full transition weak-to-ultra-strong coupling and provide a good approximation of the steady state in all regimes. This does not seem out-of-reach according to the preliminary results on higher order terms obtained in Section
7.3
. Additionally, benchmarking the results with other techniques like reaction coordinate or pseudo-modes would be interesting and instructive.

7.1

Details of the computation of
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]

In order to compute
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]
, one can first rearrange it in the following way,

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]={\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-(\beta-u)H_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt={\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[\underset{k}{\otimes}\left({\cal D}_{k,l}e^{-(\beta-u)b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}}{\cal D}^{{\dagger}}_{k,l}\right)e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt={\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-(\beta-u)H_{B}}e^{-u\sum_{k}\omega_{k}{\cal D}_{k,l}^{{\dagger}}{\cal D}_{k,l^{\prime}}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}{\cal D}_{k,l^{\prime}}^{{\dagger}}{\cal D}_{k,l}}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt={\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-(\beta-u)H_{B}}e^{-u(H_{B}+\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}B+\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q)}\right],

(41)

where
a_{l^{\prime},l}:=a_{l^{\prime}}-a_{l}
. “Taking out” of the second exponential the Hamiltonian
H_{B}
, we obtain,

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-(\beta-u)H_{B}}e^{-u(H_{B}+\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}B+\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q)}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-u(\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q)}{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B}}e^{-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal T}\int_{0}^{u}ds\tilde{B}(s)}\right],

(42)

with
\tilde{B}(s):=e^{sH_{B}}Be^{-sH_{B}}=\sum_{k}g_{k}(e^{s\omega_{k}}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}+e^{-s\omega_{k}}b_{k})
. The time ordered operator
e^{-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal T}\int_{0}^{u}ds\tilde{B}(s)}
can be split in two as follows,

e^{-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal T}\int_{0}^{u}ds\tilde{B}(s)}=e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}f(u)}e^{\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal B}(-u)}e^{-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u)},

(43)

with
f(u):=-uQ+\sum_{k}\frac{g_{k}^{2}}{\omega_{k}^{2}}(e^{u\omega_{k}}-1)
,
{\cal B}(-u):=\sum_{k}\frac{g_{k}}{\omega_{k}}(e^{-u\omega_{k}}-1)b_{k}
, and
{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u):=\sum_{k}\frac{g_{k}}{\omega_{k}}(e^{u\omega_{k}}-1)b_{k}^{{\dagger}}
. The above decomposition can be demonstrated as follows. We denote the left-hand side of (
43
) as
L(u):=e^{-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal T}\int_{0}^{u}ds\tilde{B}(s)}
and the right-hand side by
R(u):=e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}f(u)}e^{\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal B}(-u)}e^{-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u)}
.
By definition of the time ordering operator, or alternatively by taking the time derivative with respect to
u
, the left-hand side of (
43
) satisfies the differential equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial u}L(u)=-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}\tilde{B}(u)L(u).

(44)

Now, by taking the time derivative with respect to
u
of the right-hand side of (
43
), one obtains the following differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}R(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}f(u)+\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\cal B}(-u)\right]R(u)

(45)

\displaystyle-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}R(u)\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u).

Using the expressions of
f(u)
,
{\cal B}(u)
, and
{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u)
, we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}f(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Q+\sum_{k}\frac{g_{k}^{2}}{\omega_{k}}e^{u\omega_{k}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\cal B}(-u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}g_{k}e^{-u\omega_{k}}b_{k},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}g_{k}e^{u\omega_{k}}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}.

(46)

Then, with the help of the identity

R(u)\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u)=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\cal B}^{{\dagger}}(u)+\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}\sum_{k}\frac{g_{k}^{2}}{\omega_{k}}(1-e^{u\omega_{k}})\right]R(u),

(47)

and the discrete version of the re-organization energy
Q=\sum_{k}\frac{g_{k}^{2}}{\omega_{k}}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}R(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}\left[\sum_{k}g_{k}\left(e^{u\omega_{k}}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}+e^{-u\omega_{k}}b_{k}\right)\right]R(u),

which is exactly the same differential equation as (
44
). Since the initial conditions are the same, namely
R(0)=L(0)=\mathbb{I}
, we conclude that
R(u)=L(u)
for all times
u
.

A simple way to conclude the computation of the trace (
7.1
) is using the normal order representation of the operators. Using the following formula
[
58
, p. 116]

e^{\alpha a^{{\dagger}}a}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(e^{\alpha}-1)^{n}}{n!}(a^{{\dagger}})^{n}a^{n},

(49)

where
\alpha
is a c-number and
a^{{\dagger}}
,

7.2

Important property of
f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

By applying the change of variable
v=\beta-u
, one can re-write
f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)
in the form

\displaystyle f_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{u\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}\frac{e^{\omega\beta/2}+e^{-\omega\beta/2}-e^{\omega(u-\beta/2)}-e^{-\omega(u-\beta/2)}}{e^{\omega\beta/2}-e^{-\omega\beta/2}}}

(53)

\displaystyle e^{\beta\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{\beta}dve^{-v\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}\frac{e^{\omega\beta/2}+e^{-\omega\beta/2}-e^{\omega(v-\beta/2)}-e^{-\omega(v-\beta/2)}}{e^{\omega\beta/2}-e^{-\omega\beta/2}}}.

Then, one can easily verify that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{-\epsilon\beta/2}+e^{\epsilon\beta/2}}\left(e^{-\epsilon\beta/2}|+\rangle\langle+|+e^{\epsilon\beta/2}|-\rangle\langle-|\right)

(82)

\displaystyle-\frac{\Delta\beta}{4}\left(1+\frac{\epsilon^{2}\beta}{8\lambda^{2}Q}\right)\sigma_{x}.

Both expressions are equivalent when applying
\beta\lambda^{2}\beta\ll 1
to (
81
). However, in the opposite limit, when
\beta\lambda^{2}Q\gg 1
, the expression (
33
) becomes

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{-\epsilon\beta/2}+e^{\epsilon\beta/2}}\left(e^{-\epsilon\beta/2}|+\rangle\langle+|+e^{\epsilon\beta/2}|-\rangle\langle-|\right)

(83)

\displaystyle-\frac{\Delta}{8\lambda^{2}Q}\sigma_{x}.

This is significantly different from (
40
). While in the above expression the coherences vanish as
1/\lambda^{2}Q
, they vanish exponentially in (
40
) and (
81
).

7.5

Without initial renormalization

As commented above, one can choose to perform the same derivation starting from the “natural” Hamiltonian

{\cal H}_{SB}=H_{S}+H_{B}+\lambda AB,

(84)

instead of the renormalized one
H_{SB}
defined in the main text. Proceeding as previously, we have

\displaystyle e^{-\beta{\cal H}_{SB}}=e^{-\beta(H_{B}+\lambda AB)}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\tilde{\cal H}_{S}(u)},

(85)

with

\tilde{\cal H}_{S}(u):=e^{u(H_{B}+\lambda AB)}H_{S}e^{-u(H_{B}+\lambda AB)}.

(86)

We now have

\displaystyle H_{B}+\lambda AB

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\omega_{k}{\cal D}_{k}b_{k}^{{\dagger}}b_{k}{\cal D}_{k}^{{\dagger}}-\lambda^{2}QA^{2}

(87)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|{\cal H}_{B,l},

with
{\cal H}_{B,l}:=H_{B,l}-\lambda^{2}a_{l}^{2}Q=H_{B}+\lambda a_{l}B
, leading to

\displaystyle e^{u(H_{B}+\lambda AB)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{u\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|{\cal H}_{B,l}}

(88)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|e^{u{\cal H}_{B,l}}.

Then,

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{S}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l,l^{\prime}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|e^{uH_{B,l}}H_{S}|a_{l^{\prime}}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}

(89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}+\tilde{H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u),

where
{\cal H}_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}=H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}=\sum_{l}h_{l}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|
and
\tilde{\cal H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u):=\sum_{l\neq l^{\prime}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|e^{u(H_{B,l}-\lambda^{2}a_{l}^{2}Q)}e^{-u(H_{B,l^{\prime}}-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime}}^{2}Q)}=\\
\sum_{l\neq l^{\prime}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|e^{u{\cal H}_{B,l}}e^{-u{\cal H}_{B,l^{\prime}}}
.
Again, similarly as previously, we obtain,

\displaystyle e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\tilde{\cal H}_{S}(u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\beta H_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{\cal H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)},

(90)

with

\displaystyle\accentset{\approx}{\cal H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle e^{uH_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}}\tilde{\cal H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)e^{-uH_{S}^{\textrm{pop}}}

(91)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\neq l^{\prime}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}e^{u\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|e^{u{\cal H}_{B,l}}e^{-u{\cal H}_{B,l^{\prime}}},

arriving at

\displaystyle\rho_{SB}^{\textrm{th}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{SB}^{-1}\sum_{l}e^{-\beta h_{l}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|e^{-\beta{\cal H}_{B,l}}e^{-{\cal T}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\accentset{\approx}{\cal H}_{S}^{\textrm{coh}}(u)}.

Finally, the main change is that we are led to compute
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta{\cal H}_{B,l}}\right]
for the zeroth order, and
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta{\cal H}_{B,l}}e^{u{\cal H}_{B,l}}e^{-u{\cal H}_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]
for the second order, instead of
{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta H_{B,l}}e^{uH_{B,l}}e^{-uH_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]
. We have,

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta{\cal H}_{B,l}}e^{u{\cal H}_{B,l}}e^{-u{\cal H}_{B,l^{\prime}}}\right]

(93)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-\beta(H_{B,l}-\lambda^{2}a_{l}^{2}Q)}e^{u(H_{B,l}-\lambda^{2}a_{l}^{2}Q)}e^{-u(H_{B,l^{\prime}}-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime}}^{2}Q)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textrm{Tr}}_{B}\left[e^{-(\beta-u)H_{B}}e^{-u(H_{B}+\lambda a_{l^{\prime},l}B+\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q)}\right]

\displaystyle\times e^{\lambda^{2}Q[\beta a_{l}^{2}+u(a_{l^{\prime}}^{2}-a_{l}^{2})]}.

With that, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l}|-\sum_{l,l^{\prime};l\neq l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}h_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{f}}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)|a_{l}\rangle\langle a_{l^{\prime}}|,

where
{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}:=e^{-\beta(h_{l}-a_{l}^{2}\lambda^{2}Q)}/{\cal Z}_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}=e^{-\beta{\mathbf{h}}_{l}}/{\cal Z}_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}
is the renormalized population, with
{\mathbf{h}}_{l}:=h_{l}-a_{l}^{2}\lambda^{2}Q
the renormalized “pseudo-energies” (diagonal elements of
H_{S}
in the eigenbasis of
A
),
{\cal Z}^{\textrm{ss}}:=\sum_{l}e^{-\beta{\mathbf{h}}_{l}}
, and

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta):=\int_{0}^{\beta}du

\displaystyle\times e^{u\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}\frac{e^{\omega\beta/2}+e^{-\omega\beta/2}-e^{\omega(u-\beta/2)}-e^{-\omega(u-\beta/2)}}{e^{\omega\beta/2}-e^{-\omega\beta/2}}},

with
\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}:={\mathbf{h}}_{l}-{\mathbf{h}}_{l^{\prime}}
. Additionally, one can also verify the identity
{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}{\mathbf{f}}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)={\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}{\mathbf{f}}_{l^{\prime},l}(\beta)
.

Conclusion.
If we do not renormalize the Hamiltonian initially, the expressions are un-changed up to the substitution of
h_{l}
by
{\mathbf{h}}_{l}
. The renormalization has to happen, either initially, either finally. Note however that
h_{l,l^{\prime}}
is not changed. One must be aware of these differences of choice especially when defining the strong coupling regime.

7.5.1

Approximation of
{\mathbf{f}}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

Similarly as for
{f}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)
, when the bath spectral density is such that
J(\omega)
vanishes for
\omega\geq\omega_{c}
, where
\omega_{c}\leq\beta^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{u\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}u\left(1-\frac{u}{\beta}\right)Q}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=\frac{1}{\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}\Bigg\{{\textrm{DF}}\left[\frac{1}{2\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}(\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q-\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}})\right]

\displaystyle+e^{\beta\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}}{\textrm{DF}}\left[\frac{1}{2\lambda|a_{l^{\prime},l}|}\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{Q}}(\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q+\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}})\right]\Bigg\}.

Note that
\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q-\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}=2\lambda^{2}Qa_{l}(a_{l}-a_{l^{\prime}})-\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}
and
\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}Q+\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}=2\lambda^{2}Qa_{l^{\prime}}(a_{l^{\prime}}-a_{l})+\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}
. Thus, in the strong coupling regime when
\lambda^{2}Q\gg{\textrm{max}}_{l}\penalty\ |h_{l}|
and
\lambda^{2}Q\beta\gg 1
, it is still legitimate to approximate the function
DF(x)
by
1/2x
, which gives,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{f}}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}Qa_{l,l^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{1}{a_{l}}-\frac{e^{\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}\beta}}{a_{l^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}}{4\lambda^{4}Q^{2}a_{l,l^{\prime}}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{a_{l}^{2}}-\frac{e^{\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}\beta}}{a_{l^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)+{\cal O}[(\lambda^{2}Q/\omega_{l,l^{\prime}})^{-3}].

7.6

Identity of first orders

Here, we compare our general first order result, Eqs. (
3.2
) and (
3.2
), with the general first order expression for the steady state coherences obtained in Eq. (56) of
[
44
]
. This expression was obtained through projection operator techniques
[
28
,
59
,
60
]
, by choosing the operator
{\cal P}
projecting the system’s state onto the diagonal subspace (in the eigenbasis of the unperturbed Hamiltonian). Thus, the non-diagonal elements can be obtained by establishing and solving the dynamics associated with the complementary operator
1-{\cal P}
(section IV.A. of
[
44
]
). The steady state coherences are then deduced by taking the time to infinity. One should keep in mind that the master equation derived in the strong-decoherence limit in
[
44
]
is valid when the bath spectral density is such that
\lim_{\omega\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega}
is non-zero (and possibly infinite). This condition, satisfied by usual spectral densities, guarantees that the unperturbed dynamics in
[
44
]
leads to full decoherence, which is the starting point of the projection technique used therein.

To make the comparison easier, we re-write in the following the results of
[
44
]
using the notations we have been using here. Additionally, in order to simplify the presentation, we consider a simple coupling of the form
V_{I}=\lambda AB
instead of the general one
V_{I}=\sum_{\alpha}A_{\alpha}B_{\alpha}
considered in
[
44
]
. With that, in the eigenbasis
\{|a_{l}\rangle\}_{l}
of
A
, the result of
[
44
]
takes the form

\displaystyle\rho_{l,l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss,ME}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\langle a_{l}|\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss,ME}}|a_{l^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{l,l^{\prime}}\Big[i{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G^{*}(\tau)-i\tau Q]}e^{-i\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}\tau}

\displaystyle-i{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l,l^{\prime}}^{2}[G(\tau)+i\tau Q]}e^{-i\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}\tau}\Big]

with
{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}:=e^{-\beta(h_{l}-a_{l}^{2}\lambda^{2}Q)}/{\cal Z}_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}=e^{-\beta{\mathbf{h}}_{l}}/{\cal Z}_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}
is the renormalized population introduced in Section
7.5
, with
{\mathbf{h}}_{l}:=h_{l}-a_{l}^{2}\lambda^{2}Q
the renormalized “pseudo-energies”, and
{\cal Z}^{\textrm{ss}}:=\sum_{l}e^{-\beta{\mathbf{h}}_{l}}
. Additionally, we defined
G(\tau):=\int_{0}^{\tau}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}ds_{2}c_{B}(s_{2})
, and

\displaystyle c_{B}(s)={\textrm{Tr}}_{B}[e^{iH_{B}s}Be^{-iH_{B}s}B\rho_{B}^{\textrm{th}}].

(99)

The superscript “ME” refers to the master equation nature of the derivation
[
44
]
.

Our result can be expressed as (using the results of the derivation with no initial renormalization of the Hamiltonian, detailed in Section
7.5
, since it is the choice made in
[
44
]
),

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\rho_{l,l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\!\langle a_{l}|\rho_{S}^{\textrm{ss}}|a_{l^{\prime}}\rangle

(100)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-h_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l,l^{\prime}}^{2}[G(-iu)+uQ]}e^{u\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!-h_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{\beta}due^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(-iu)+uQ]}e^{u\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}},

where we used in the second line the identity
{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}{\mathbf{f}}_{l,l^{\prime}}(\beta)={\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}{\mathbf{f}}_{l^{\prime},l}(\beta)
, shown in Section
7.2
.
Now, let us consider the integral of the function
e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(-iu)+uQ]}e^{u\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}}
extended to the complex plan along the contour
{\cal C}
defined as
u:0\rightarrow\beta\rightarrow\beta+ix\rightarrow ix\rightarrow 0
, where
x
is a positive number that later will be taken to infinity. Since it is a closed contour, the integral is equal to zero (as long as
G(u)
is an analytic function).
Additionally, when the bath spectral density satisfies
\lim_{\omega\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}>0
, the real part of the function
G(x)
tends to
+\infty
as
x
increases. The condition
\lim_{\omega\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}>0
is precisely the condition of validity of the results in
[
44
]
. This is not a coincidence. The validity of the master equation in
[
44
]
relies on the full decoherence of the unperturbed dynamics, which is guaranteed as long as
G(x)
tends to
+\infty
as
x
increases, which itself results in the condition
\lim_{\omega\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}>0
on the bath spectral density.
Then, the integral of
e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(-iu)+uQ]}e^{u\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}}
tends to zero on the segment
\beta+ix\rightarrow ix
for
x\rightarrow+\infty
.
As a result, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{l,l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\beta}^{\beta+ix}due^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(-iu)+uQ]}e^{u\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}}

\displaystyle+h_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{+ix}^{0}due^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(-iu)+uQ]}e^{u\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ih_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\tau e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(\tau-i\beta)+(i\tau+\beta)Q]}e^{(i\tau+\beta)\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}}

\displaystyle-ih_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\tau e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(\tau)+i\tau Q]}e^{i\tau\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ih_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\tau e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G^{*}(\tau)-i\tau Q]}e^{-i\tau\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle-ih_{l,l^{\prime}}{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\tau e^{-\lambda^{2}a_{l^{\prime},l}^{2}[G(\tau)+i\tau Q]}e^{-i\tau\bar{\omega}_{l,l^{\prime}}}

which is precisely equal to the expression (
7.6
) of
\rho_{l,l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss,ME}}
. Note that in the last line we used the identities
{\mathbf{p}}_{l^{\prime}}^{\textrm{ss}}e^{\beta\bar{\omega}_{l^{\prime},l}}={\mathbf{p}}_{l}^{\textrm{ss}}
and
G(\tau-i\beta)+(i\tau+\beta)Q=G^{*}(\tau)-i\tau Q
(as well as
\omega_{l^{\prime},l}=-\omega_{l,l^{\prime}}
). This concludes the proof that the expression derived in this paper coincides with the expression obtained in
[
44
]
, although they have been obtained from very different methods and starting point. The equivalence of the expressions is guaranteed as soon as the results in
[
44
]
are valid (namely, as soon as
\lim_{\omega\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{J(\omega)}{\omega^{2}}>0
).
As reminded in the main text, this is an important step forward since it proves that the mean force Gibbs state is indeed the actual steady state (at least up the first order) even when the system interacts
strongly
with the thermal bath.

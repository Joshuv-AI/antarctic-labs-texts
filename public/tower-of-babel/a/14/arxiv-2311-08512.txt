Abelian gauge-like groups of L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras
Bashar Saleh

Abstract
. Given a finite type degree-wise nilpotent L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra, we construct an abelian group that acts on the set of Maurer-Cartan elements of the given L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra so that the quotient by this action becomes the moduli space of equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan elements. Specializing this to degree-wise nilpotent dg Lie algebras, we find that the associated ordinary gauge group of the dg Lie algebra with the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff multiplication might be substituted by the underlying additive group. This additive group acts on the Maurer-Cartan elements, and the quotient by this action yields the moduli space of gauge-equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan elements.



1.
Introduction

Given a dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
where the degree zero part,
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, is nilpotent, there is an associated gauge group
\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{0})=(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathrm{BCH})
, where the multiplication is governed by the well-known but intricate Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula. One of the important features of this group is that it acts on the set/variety of Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted by
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
, and the quotient
\MC(\mathfrak{g})/\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
, usually called the moduli space of gauge equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan elements, denoted by
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
in this paper, is an important object in deformation theory and algebraic topology (see e.g.
[
GM88
,
Laz13
,
Ber15
,
Man22
,
DSV24
]
).

In this paper, we construct an action of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=(\mathfrak{g}_{0},+)
, i.e. the underlying additive group of the vector space
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, on
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
, so that the quotient
\MC(\mathfrak{g})/\mathfrak{g}_{0}
again becomes the moduli space
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
. This might be surprising, as it may indicate that the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula might be replaced with simple addition for certain purposes.

While the gauge group construction has not been generalized for
L_{\infty}
-algebras, the group
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=(\mathfrak{g}_{0},+)
makes sense for any
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, the notions of Maurer-Cartan elements and moduli of gauge equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan elements can be generalized to (degree-wise) nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebras of finite type (
[
Get09
]
) and in those cases the quotient
\MC(\mathfrak{g})/(\mathfrak{g}_{0},+)
will again be
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite type degree-wise nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Then the underlying additive group of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
acts on the set of Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
, and the quotient
\MC(\mathfrak{g})/\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is isomorphic to the moduli space
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
of gauge-equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The conditions on
\mathfrak{g}
can actually be weakened. For a version with the weaker conditions, see Theorem
5.12
.

Theorem
1.1
is proved through the theory of homotopies of algebra maps.
The category of commutative dg algebras (henceforth abbreviated dg algebras) over a field
\mathds{k}
of characteristic zero has a model structure, defined in
[
Hin97
]
, which enables us of giving the notion of homotopy an exact meaning. We will start by considering additive group constructions that act on the set of dg algebra morphisms in a way so that the quotient by this group action becomes the set of homotopy classes of these dg algebra morphisms when the domain is a Sullivan algebra.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be two dg algebras where
A
is a Sullivan algebra. Then there is an additive group
G=G(A,B)
that acts on the set of dg algebra homorphisms from
A
to
B
, denoted by
\Hom(A,B)
, such that

\Hom(A,B)/G=[A,B],

where
[A,B]
denotes the set of homotopy classes of dg algebra morphisms.

Any such group construction would give rise to a reasonable replacement of the gauge group through the following steps: Associated to a nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, there is an associated commutative dg algebra,
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})
, that we will call the
decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra construction
on
\mathfrak{g}
(in a strict sense, we require that
\mathfrak{g}
be bounded either from above or below. However, for our purposes, we will show that we might simply replace
\mathfrak{g}
by its brutal truncation at zero.). Section
5.1
is devoted for explaining what we mean by the adjective “decompleted”. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite type and nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra,
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
is identified with the set
\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})
of dg algebra morphisms from the decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra construction on
\mathfrak{g}
, to the underlying field.

Two morphisms in this set are homotopic if and only if the corresponding Maurer-Cartan elements are gauge equivalent. We have that
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Sullivan algebra, which is a result we will discuss in detail in Section
5
. Hence, it follows that, for any group construction
G
that satisfy the properties of Theorem
1.2
, the group
G(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})
might be regarded as a replacement of the gauge group, in the sense that

\MC(\mathfrak{g})=\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})

and

\MC(\mathfrak{g})/\text{gauge equivalence}=\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})/G(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k}).

We will mainly consider two group constructions that satisfy the properties of Theorem
1.2
,
G_{L}
and
G_{S}
, where
G_{L}
is a large group in general, and where
G_{S}
is a much smaller subgroup. Given that
A
is a Sullivan algebra, the underlying algebra structure of
A
is freely generated by some graded vector space
V
, and consequently
A
is of the form
(\Lambda(V),d)
. We set

G_{L}=G_{L}(A,B)=\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1},

where
\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1}
is the space of linear maps from
V
to
B
of cohomological degree
-1
with the standard additive structure.
We set
G_{S}=\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1}
and the inclusion
G_{S}\subset G_{L}
is given by

x\mapsto(x,0,\dots)
.

1.1.
The group
G_{L}

Given that the dg algebra
A
is Sullivan, we have that two morphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\Hom(A,B)
are homotopic if and only if there is a map
h\colon A\to B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt)
such that when it is precomposed with evaluations at
t=0
and
t=1
gives
\varphi
and
\psi
respectively (
\mathrm{ev}_{0}\circ h=\varphi
and
\mathrm{ev}_{1}\circ h=\psi
) (for connected algebras this result is a classical result in rational homotopy theory, and for unbounded algebras this is proved in
[
CR19
, Proposition 3.10]
). Therefore, it makes sense to declare
\Hom(A,B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt))
as the set of homotopies between maps whose domain is
A
and codomain is
B
.
One way of proving Theorem
1.2
, is through the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be two dg algebras where
A
is a Sullivan algebra. Then there is an additive group
G_{L}
acting on
\Hom(A,B)

so that there is an isomorphism of sets

G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Hom(A,B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt))

where the following diagram commutes

\textstyle{\Hom(A,B)}
\textstyle{G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
projection
\scriptstyle{\cong}
group action
\textstyle{\Hom(A,B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(\mathrm{ev}_{1})_{*}}
\scriptstyle{(\mathrm{ev}_{0})_{*}}
\textstyle{\Hom(A,B).}

Some immediate consequences of this theorem are the following:

•

G_{L}
satisfies the properties of Theorem
1.2
since for every pair of homotopic maps
\varphi\simeq\psi
, there is a group element
g\in G_{L}
that sends
\varphi
to
\psi
, and conversely, for any
g\in G_{L}
and
\varphi\in\Hom(A,B)
,
g\varphi
is homotopic to
\varphi
.

•

The proofs of the transitivity of the homotopy relation in the literature, known to the author, are not constructive, but rather depending on existence of certain lifts (see e.g.
[
GM13
, Corollary 11.4]
,
[
FHT01
, Proposition 12.7]
or
[
CR19
, Proposition 3.10]
). With our approach, there is an interpretation “composition of homotopies” as addition of elements in
G_{L}
, and thus we have a constructive proof of the homotopy relation (namely, if
(g,\varphi)
corresponds to a homotopy from
\varphi
to
\varphi^{\prime}
and
(g^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
corresponds to a homotopy from
\varphi^{\prime}
to
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
, then
(g+g^{\prime},\varphi)
correspond to a homotopy from
\varphi
to
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
).

•

Given a dg algebra morphism
\varphi\colon A\to B
, let

H_{\varphi}=\{h\colon A\to B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt)\ |\ \mathrm{ev}_{0}\circ h=\varphi\}.

It follows by our theorem that
H_{\varphi}\cong G_{L}
. Since this holds for any morphism, it follows that there is a canonical isomorphism of sets
H_{\varphi}\cong H_{\psi}
for any
\varphi,\psi\in\Hom(A,B)
.

1.2.
The group
G_{S}

If the group
G_{L}
is non-trivial, it is infinite dimensional (viewed as a vector space). We will show that it contains a subgroup
G_{S}\subset G_{L}
that is in many cases finite dimensional (viewed as a vector space), that also satisfies the properties of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be two dg algebras where
A=(\Lambda(V),d)
is a Sullivan algebra.
The underlying additive group of the vector space
G_{S}=\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1}
satisfies the properties of Theorem
1.2
.

Some immediate consequences of the theorem are the following:

•

The decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra construction on
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})
on a finite type
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a Sullivan algebra of the form
\Lambda(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}
, where
((s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee})^{n}=\mathfrak{g}_{n-1}^{\vee}
. In particular, we have that
G_{S}(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),k)=\Hom((s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee},\mathds{k})^{-1}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. This yields a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

•

We have that
\Hom(V,B)^{-1}
is finite dimensional in many cases, e.g. when
V
and
B
are of finite type and one of them is finite dimensional. In these cases
G_{S}
can be regarded as the
\mathds{k}
-points of an additive algebraic group
\mathcal{G}_{S}
. Moreover, in these cases,
\Hom(A,B)
could be regarded as the
\mathds{k}
-points of an affine variety
\mathcal{V}
. Associated to the action of
\mathcal{G}_{S}
on
\mathcal{V}
there is an algebraic quotient stack
[\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{G}_{S}]
that might be of interest.

Another question that might be of interest is whether the quotient
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{G}_{S}
exists in the category of algebraic spaces or schemes. Significant works have been devoted to the investigation of when such quotients exist (see e.g.
[
MFK94
,
KM97
,
Kol97
]
). However, the author does not know any immediate way of determining the existence of
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{G}_{S}
through the established theory on this subject.

•

Theorem
1.4
could also be regarded as a way of correcting a minor mistake in
[
Mar96
, Lemma 1.5]
. In loc. cit., it is claimed that for finitely generated minimal Sullivan algebras
\mathcal{M}_{Z}
and
\mathcal{M}_{Y}
, the set
\Hom(\mathcal{M}_{Z},\mathcal{M}_{Y}\otimes\Lambda(t,dt))
is a variety. Since
\Lambda(t,dt)
is infinite dimensional in both degree 0 and 1, this is clearly not true in most cases. However, one might consider
G_{S}\times\Hom(\mathcal{M}_{Z},\mathcal{M}_{Y})
(which embeds into
G_{L}\times\Hom(\mathcal{M}_{Z},\mathcal{M}_{Y})\cong\Hom(\mathcal{M}_{Z},\mathcal{M}_{Y}\otimes\Lambda(t,dt))
) as replacement of
\Hom(\mathcal{M}_{Z},\mathcal{M}_{Y}\otimes\Lambda(t,dt))
that has the structure of a variety.

Remark 1.5
.

The statements of Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
holds for general operad algebras in which the notion of being Sullivan is in accordance with the theory established in
[
CR19
]
.

1.3.
Conventions

•

We use a cohomological convention (the differential raises the degree) for dg algebras, and we use a homological convention (the differential lowers the degree) for dg Lie algebras and
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

•

Given two dg algebras
A
and
B
, the set of dg algebra morphisms from
A
to
B
is denoted by
\Hom(A,B)
.

•

Given two (graded) vector spaces
V
and
W
, the set of linear maps from
V
to
W
is denoted by
\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,W)
and has a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,W)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,W)^{i}
, where
\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,W)^{i}
is the set of linear maps that increase the degree by
i
.

2.
Preliminaries: Sullivan algebras and homotopy

We will recall the definition of Sullivan algebras, originally due to Sullivan
[
Sul77
]
, and introduce a notion of Sullivan stages.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Sullivan algebra

A
is a colimit
\varinjlim A_{i}
of extensions

0=A_{0}\to A_{1}\to A_{2}\to\dots

where
A_{i}
is a quasi-free dg algebra of the form
A_{i}=(\Lambda(V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{i}),d)
where
d(V_{i})\subseteq A_{i-1}=\Lambda(V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{i-1})
and where
A_{i-1}\to A_{i}
is the canonical inclusion (such extensions are usually called KS-extensions or Hirsch-extensions).

We will call
A_{i}
for the
i
’th Sullivan stage
of
A
associated to the sequence of KS-extensions
0=A_{0}\to A_{1}\to A_{2}\to\dots
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be two dg algebras where
A
is a Sullivan algebra. Given two morphisms
\varphi,\psi\in\Hom(A,B)
we say that there exists a
homotopy
from
\varphi
to
\psi
if there exists a morphism

h\colon A\to B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt)

such that
\mathrm{ev}_{0}\circ h=\varphi
and
\mathrm{ev}_{1}\circ h=\psi
,
where
\mathrm{ev}_{a}\colon B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt)\to B
sends
t
to
a
and restricts to the identity on
B
.

It follows from the definition above that a homotopy
h
from a morphism
\varphi
is of the form

h=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}t^{i}dt,

where
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{i}
are linear maps of degrees
0
and
-1
, respectively.

Remark 2.3
.

For every element
x\in A
, we have that the sums
\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}(x)t^{j}
and
\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}(x)t^{i}dt
are finite. However, it is not necessarily true that there is some
N\gg 0
for which
\alpha_{i}=0
and
\beta_{i}=0
whenever
i>N
. For instance, it is very possible that
\beta_{i}
is trivial on indecomposable elements but non-trivial on some decomposable elements.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
Sal23
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

Let
h=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}t^{i}dt
be a homotopy
A\to B\otimes\Lambda(t,dt)
. Then there is an equality

-\alpha_{j}=\frac{d\beta_{j-1}+\beta_{j-1}d}{j},\ j\geq 1.

In particular, the homotopy
h
is completely determined by
\varphi
and
\{\beta_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
.

Proof.

This is obtained by solving the equation
dh=hd
. Detailed calculations are found in the proof of
[
Sal23
, Lemma 2.5]
.
∎

3.
Construction of the
G_{L}
-action

We are now ready to prove the first part of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A=(\Lambda(V),d)
be Sullivan algebra and
B
a dg algebra and let
\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1}
be the space of linear maps from
V
to
B
of degree
-1
and let
G_{L}=G_{L}(A,B)=\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1}
. There is a bijection of sets

\Theta\colon G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)\to\Hom(A,B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt)),

where an element
(\{b_{i}\}_{i\geq 0},\varphi)\in G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)
is sent to a homotopy

h=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}t^{i}dt

where
\beta_{i}|_{V}=b_{i}
.

Proof.

Given an element
(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)\in G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)
we will show that there is a unique homotopy
h=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}t^{i}dt
where
\beta_{i}|_{V}=b_{i}
. We prove this assertion by induction on the Sullivan stages of
A
.

For the first Sullivan stage
A_{1}=(\Lambda(V_{1}),d)
, we have by definition that
d|_{V_{1}}=0
. We will first define a morphism of graded algebras (not of dg algebras)

h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}\colon A_{1}=\Lambda(V_{1})\to B\otimes\Lambda(t,dt)
associated to an element
(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)\in G_{L}(A_{1},B)\times\Hom(A_{1},B)
. A morphism of graded algebras
\Lambda(V_{1})\to B\otimes\Lambda(t,dt)
is completely determined by its restriction to
V_{1}
. For any
v\in V_{1}
we set

h_{1}^{\mathrm{gr}}(v)=\varphi(v)-\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{db_{j-1}(v)}{j}t^{j}+(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{i\geq 0}b_{i}(v)t^{i}dt.

We have that
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}
defines a morphism of dg algebras if and only if
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}(dv)=dh^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}(v)
for all
v\in V_{1}
. We have that
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}(dv)=h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}(0)=0
, and that

dh^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}(v)=d\varphi(v)-(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{i\geq 0}db_{i}(v)t^{i}dt+(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{i\geq 0}db_{i}(v)t^{i}dt=d\varphi(v)=\varphi(dv)=\varphi(0)=0.

Hence
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{1}
defines a morphism of dg algebras which we now instead call
h_{1}
and we set
\Theta(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)=h_{1}
. By Proposition
2.4
and the fact that
d|_{V_{1}}=0
, it follows that if there is a homotopy
\tilde{h}_{1}=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 1}\tilde{\alpha}_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}t^{i}dt
where
\beta_{i}|_{V_{1}}=b_{i}
, then
\tilde{\alpha}_{j}|_{V_{1}}
has to be given by
-db_{j-1}/j=\alpha_{j}|_{V_{1}}
, and thus,
\tilde{h}_{1}=h_{1}
. In particular, for any homotopy
h^{\prime}_{1}=\varphi^{\prime}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha^{\prime}_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta^{\prime}_{i}t^{i}dt
in
\Hom(A_{1},B\otimes\Lambda(t,dt))
we have that
h^{\prime}=\Theta(\{\beta^{\prime}_{i}|_{V}\},\varphi^{\prime})
. This proves that
\Theta
is an isomorphism when
A=A_{1}
is a first stage Sullivan algebra.

Assume now that the assertion is true for Sullivan
n
-stages. We want to show that it is also true for Sullivan
(n+1)
-stages.

Fix an arbitrary element
(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)\in G_{L}(A_{n+1},B)\times\Hom(A_{n+1},B)
. By the inductive hypothesis there is a homotopy

h_{n}=\Theta(\{b_{i}|_{V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{n}}\},\varphi|_{A_{n}})\in\Hom(A_{n},B\otimes\Lambda(t,dt)).

where
h_{n}
is of the form

h_{n}=\varphi|_{A_{n}}+\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}^{n}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta^{n}_{i}t^{i}dt,

where
\beta^{n}_{i}|_{V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{n}}=b_{i}|_{V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{n}}
.

We define a morphism of graded algebras

h_{n+1}^{\mathrm{gr}}\colon A_{n+1}\to B\otimes\Lambda(t,dt)

where
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}|_{A_{n}}=h_{n}
and where
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}|_{V_{n+1}}
is given by
the following: For any
v\in V_{n+1}
we set

h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}(v)=\varphi(v)-\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{db_{j-1}(v)+\beta^{n}_{j-1}(dv)}{j}t^{j}+(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{i\geq 0}b_{i}(v)t^{i}dt.

This completely determines
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}
as a graded algebra morphism. We have that its restriction to
A_{n}
is given by
h_{n}
and thus commuting with the differential. Hence
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}
commutes with the differential if and only if

h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}(dv)=dh_{n+1}^{\mathrm{gr}}(v)
for every
v\in V_{n+1}
.
We have that

h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}(dv)=h_{n}(dv)=\varphi(dv)-\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{d\beta^{n}_{j-1}(dv)}{j}t^{j}-(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}^{n}(dv)t^{i}dt

and

dh^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}(v)=d\varphi(v)-\sum_{j\geq 1}\left(\frac{d\beta^{n}_{j-1}(dv)}{j}t^{j}+(-1)^{|v|}(db_{j-1}(v)+\beta^{n}_{j-1}(dv))t^{j-1}dt\right)

+(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{i\geq 0}db_{i}(v)t^{i}dt

=\varphi(dv)-\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{d\beta^{n}_{j-1}(dv)}{j}t^{j}-(-1)^{|v|}\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}^{n}(dv)t^{i}dt,

giving that
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}(dv)=dh^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}(v)
. Hence
h^{\mathrm{gr}}_{n+1}
defines a morphism of dg algebras which we will call
h_{n+1}
. We set
\Theta(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)=h_{n+1}
. As before, by Proposition
2.4
, the restrictions of
\alpha_{i}
to
V_{n+1}
are uniquely determined by
\varphi
and
\{\beta_{i}|_{V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{n+1}}\}
, which gives as before that
\Theta
is an isomorphism. This completes the induction.
∎

By the theorem above, we might identify
G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)
with
\Hom(A,B\otimes\Lambda(t,dt))
, and thus we will refer to an element
(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)
as a homotopy
h
from
\varphi
to
\mathrm{ev}_{1}(\Theta(\{b_{i}\},\varphi))
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The map
G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)\to\Hom(A,B)
given by

(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)\mapsto\mathrm{ev}_{1}(\Theta(\{b_{i}\},\varphi))

defines a group action.

Proof.

It is clear that
(\{0\},\varphi)\mapsto\varphi
. We need to show that if

h=(\{b_{i}\},\varphi)
defines a homotopy from
\varphi
to
\varphi^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime}=(\{b_{i}^{\prime}\},\varphi^{\prime})
defines a homotopy from
\varphi^{\prime}
to
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
, then
(\{b_{i}+b_{i}^{\prime}\},\varphi)
defines a homotopy from
\varphi
to
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
.

We define a homotopy of homotopies

H=(\{b_{i}^{\prime}\},h)\colon A\to B\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(t,dt)\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\Lambda(s,ds).

In particular
H
is of the form

H=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}t^{i}dt+\sum_{j\geq 1}\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{j}s^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{i}s^{i}ds

where
\beta_{i}|_{V}=b_{i}
and
\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{i}|_{V}=b_{i}^{\prime}

4.
Definition and properties of the
G_{S}
-action

Since
G_{L}(\Lambda(V),B)
is an infinite direct sum of copies of
\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1}
it is either trivial (if
\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(V,B)^{-1}
is) or infinite dimensional viewed as a vector space. A very natural question is if it is possible to realize the homotopy relation by a smaller group. We give an affirmative answer to this question in this section.

By previous section, there is a group action
G_{L}(A,B)\times\Hom(A,B)\to\Hom(A,B)
whenever
A
is Sullivan. Therefore we will refer to the image of
(g,\varphi)
under this group action map by
g\varphi
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the elements
g=(0,\dots,0,b_{n},0,\dots)
and
\widehat{g}=(b_{n}/(n+1),0\dots)
of
G_{L}(A,B)
. For any
\varphi\in\Hom(A,B)
we have that
g\varphi=\widehat{g}\varphi
.

Proof.

We start by defining an endomorphism
\rho_{n}\colon\Lambda(t,dt)\to\Lambda(t,dt)
given by
\rho_{n}(t)=t^{n+1}
and
\rho(dt)=(n+1)t^{n}dt

We have that
(\widehat{g},\varphi)\in G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)
correspond to a homotopy

\widehat{h}=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 1}\widehat{\alpha}_{j}t^{j}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\widehat{\beta}_{i}t^{i}dt

where
\widehat{\beta}_{0}|_{V}=b_{n}/(n+1)
and
\widehat{\beta}_{i}|_{V}=0
for
i\geq 1
.

We consider the composition
h=(\id_{B}\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\rho_{n})\circ\widehat{h}
and get that

h=\varphi+\sum_{j\geq 1}\widehat{\alpha}_{j}t^{j(n+1)}+\sum_{i\geq 0}(n+1)\widehat{\beta}_{i}t^{i(n+1)+n}dt

which clearly correspond to
(g,\varphi)
. From the explicit formulas for
h
and
\widehat{h}
, it is clear that
\mathrm{ev}_{1}\circ h=\mathrm{ev}_{1}\circ\widehat{h}
, i.e. that
g\varphi=\widehat{g}\varphi
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A=(\Lambda(V),d)
be Sullivan algebra and
B
a dg algebra and let
G_{S}=\Hom(V,B)^{-1}
be given the obvious additive group structure. Then
G_{S}
acts on
\Hom(A,B)
through the embedding
G_{S}\to G_{L}
,
b\mapsto(b,0,\dots)
.
The quotient
\Hom(A,B)/G_{S}
is isomorphic to
[A,B]
.

Proof.

If
\varphi
is homotopic to
\psi
, then there is, by Propostion
3.1
, some homotopy
(g,\varphi)=((b_{0},b_{1},\dots),\varphi)\in G_{L}\times\Hom(A,B)
from
\varphi
to
\psi
.
Set
g_{i}=(0,\dots,0,b_{i},0,\dots)
and
\widehat{g}_{i}=(b_{i}/(i+1),0,\dots)
for
i\geq 0
.
Since only finitely many of the
b_{i}
’s are non-trivial, there is some
N
for which
b_{i}=0
for
i>N
.

It follows by Theorem
3.2
that
g\varphi=g_{N}\cdots g_{0}\varphi
. By Proposition
4.1
it follows that
g_{N}\cdots g_{0}\varphi=\widehat{g}_{N}\cdots\widehat{g}_{0}\varphi
. Since
\widehat{g}_{i}\in G_{S}
, the assertion follows.
∎

5.
Gauge-like groups for degree-wise nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebras

We start by recalling the definition of being degree-wise nilpotent and the definition of Maurer-Cartan elements.

Definition 5.1

(
[
Ber15
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. The
lower central series
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a filtration

\mathfrak{g}=\Gamma^{1}\mathfrak{g}\supseteq\Gamma^{2}\mathfrak{g}\supseteq\cdots,

where
\Gamma^{k}\mathfrak{g}
is spanned by all possible expressions one can form using at least
k
elements from
\mathfrak{g}
and the structure maps
\{\ell_{i}\}
. One says that
\mathfrak{g}
is
degree-wise nilpotent
if for every homological degree
n
there is some
k
so that
(\Gamma^{k}\mathfrak{g})_{n}=0
.

Definition 5.2
.

A
Maurer-Cartan element
of a degree-wise nilpotent
L_{\infty}
algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})
is an element
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
that satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}\ell_{n}(x\wedge\cdots\wedge x)=0.

Note that the sum above is finite due to degree-wise nilpotency. The set of Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 5.3
.

The moduli space of gauge-equivalence classes of a degree-wise nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})
can be understood in terms of the nerve construction,
\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
, introduced by Getzler
[
Get09
]
.
\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is a simplicial set where the
n
-simplices are given by
\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{n})
where
\Omega_{n}
is Sullivan-de Rham algebra of polynomial differential forms on the
n
-simplex. In particular
\MC_{0}(\mathfrak{g})=\MC(\mathfrak{g})
. Two Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
are gauge-equivalent if they belong to the same connected component. In particular, the
set of gauge equivalence classes
of
\mathfrak{g}
, denoted by
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
, is given by
\pi_{0}(\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}))
.

5.1.
(Decompleted) Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra constructions

We fix an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})
, with
\ell_{i}\colon\Lambda^{i}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
being the structure maps defining the
L_{\infty}
-structure.
The Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra construction comes in two inequivalent versions that we will discuss in this section. The first version, which we call the
ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg construction
, and will be denoted by
C^{*}_{CE}
, is defined for all
L_{\infty}
-algebras as follows: There is a bar construction
B_{*}
from the category of
L_{\infty}
-algebras with
\infty
-morphisms to the category of conilpotent cocommutative dg coalgebras (see e.g.
[
LV12
, § 11.4]
),

B_{*}(\mathfrak{g})=(\Lambda_{c}(s\mathfrak{g}),\partial)

where
\Lambda_{c}(s\mathfrak{g})
is the graded cofree cocommutative coalgebra on the suspension of
\mathfrak{g}
(where
(s\mathfrak{g})_{n}=\mathfrak{g}_{n-1}
). The differential
\partial
has a decomposition

\partial=\partial_{0}+\partial_{1}+\dots

where
\partial_{i}
decreases the wordlength by
i
, and is determined by
\partial_{i}(sx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge sx_{i+1})\penalty=\pm s\ell_{i+1}(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i+1})
.

The ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra construction on
\mathfrak{g}
is given by the dual algebra of the bar construction on
\mathfrak{g}
; i.e.
C^{*}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g})=B_{*}(\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}
.

If we assume that
s\mathfrak{g}
is of finite type and bounded from above or from below, it follows that
C^{*}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g})
is a direct product of the form

C^{*}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g})=\left(\prod_{i\geq 0}\Lambda^{i}(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee},d\right)

If we furthermore assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is degree-wise nilpotent, it follows that the differential on
C^{*}_{CE}(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}
restricts to a differential on the direct sum

\Lambda(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\Lambda^{i}(s\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}).

In this case, we call the dg algebra
(\Lambda(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee},d)
for the
decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg
dg algebra construction on
\mathfrak{g}
, which we will denote by
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Remark 5.4
.

We would like to emphasize that the ordinary and the decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg constructions are inequivalent and give rise to different cohomology theories. For instance the cohomology of
C^{*}_{CE}
is invariant under quasi-isomorphisms, while the cohomology of
C^{*}
is not. See the next example for a concrete case when two different cohomologies are obtained.

Example 5.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the dg Lie algebra generated by a single element
y
of degree
-1
and where
d(y)=[y,y]
. Applying the ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg construction to it, we get a dg algebra of the form

\left(\prod_{i\geq 0}\Lambda^{i}(a,b),d\right)

where
|a|=0
and
|b|=-1
and where
db=a\wedge a-a
. In particular, we have that

H^{*}(C^{*}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong\mathds{k}\llbracket a\rrbracket/(a^{2}-a)\cong\mathds{k}\cong H^{*}(C^{*}_{CE}(0)),

as expected.

However, when we apply decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg construction to it, we get a dg algebra of the form
(\Lambda(a,b),d)
and the cohomology becomes

H^{*}(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong\mathds{k}[a]/(a^{2}-a)

which is 2-dimensional.

Remark 5.6
.

The ordinary and the decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg constructions coincide whenever
\mathfrak{g}
is concentrated in non-negative degrees.

5.2.
Brutal truncations

The decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra is only defined for finite type nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebras that are either bounded from above or from below. However, the boundedness conditions are not cruicial as we can take ”brutal truncations” (terminology from
[
Wei94
, §1.2]
) to truncate away the positive degree parts, which does not affect
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 5.7
.

If
C=(C,d)
is a chain complex (the differential decreases the degree), the
brutal truncation
of
C
at some integer
n
, denoted by
\sigma_{\leq n}C
, is given by

(\sigma_{\leq n}C)_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\quad\text{if }k>n\\
C_{k}&\quad\text{if }k\leq n\end{array}\right.

and the differential is just given by restriction.

Lemma 5.8
.

Given an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})
, there is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure
(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\})

where

\ell^{\prime}_{n}(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell_{n}(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n})&\quad\text{if }|\ell_{n}(x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n})|\leq 0\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

The brutal truncation
\sigma_{\leq 0}
defines a lax closed and lax symmetric monoidal endofunctor of the category of chain complexes. An
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is equivalent to a morphism
of operads from the
L_{\infty}
operad
\mathcal{L}ie_{\infty}
to the endomorphism operad
\mathcal{E}nd_{(\mathfrak{g},d)}=\{(\hom(\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes n},\mathfrak{g}),\partial_{n}\}
(where
\partial_{n}\theta=d_{\mathfrak{g}}\theta-(-1)^{|\theta|}\theta d_{\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes n}}
for
\theta\in\hom(\mathfrak{g}^{\otimes n},\mathfrak{g})
of homogeneous degree).

Since
\sigma_{\leq 0}
is both lax closed and lax symmetric monoidal, we get a morphism of operads

\mathcal{E}nd_{(\mathfrak{g},d)}\to\mathcal{E}nd_{(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},d)}
which postcomposed with
\mathcal{L}ie_{\infty}\to\mathcal{E}nd_{(\mathfrak{g},d)}
gives the
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure described above.
∎

Corollary 5.9
.

There are isomorphisms of sets

\MC(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})\cong\MC(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})\cong\mathscr{MC}(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},\{\ell^{\prime}_{i}\}).

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
we have that
\ell_{n}(x^{\wedge n})\in\mathfrak{g}_{-2}
giving that
\ell_{n}(x^{\wedge n})=\ell_{n}^{\prime}(x^{\wedge n})
, yielding the first isomorphism. In particular, we have that the 0-simplicies in
\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})
and
\MC_{\bullet}(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\})
are canonically isomorphic. If we can show that their 1-simplicies are also canonically isomorphic, it will follow that
\pi_{0}(\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\}))\cong\pi_{0}(\MC_{\bullet}(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},\{\ell^{\prime}_{i}\}))
, i.e.
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})\cong\mathscr{MC}(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},\{\ell^{\prime}_{i}\})
. We have that
\MC_{1}(\mathfrak{g})=\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{1})
. Since
\Omega_{1}
is concentrated in homological degrees 0 and -1, we have that

(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{1})_{-1}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\otimes(\Omega_{1})_{0}+\mathfrak{g}_{0}\otimes(\Omega_{1})_{-1}
and

(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{1})_{-2}=\mathfrak{g}_{-2}\otimes(\Omega_{1})_{0}+\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\otimes(\Omega_{1})_{-1}
. Thus, it follows as before that
\ell_{n}(y^{\wedge n})=\ell_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\wedge n})
for every
y\in(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{1})_{-1}
. In particular
\MC_{1}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\MC_{1}(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g})
, and the second isomorphism follows.
∎

Notation 5.10
.

We will henceforth denote to the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}^{\prime}\})
simply by
\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}
.

5.3.
Decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebras and Sullivan algebras

The next proposition states that the decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg dg algebra of a nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra is a Sullivan algebra. This is proved in
[
Ber15
, Theorem 2.3]
(even though Berglund restricts to non-negatively graded
L_{\infty}
-algebras, the proof works in greater generality), but we have chosen to write a similar proof but with a slightly different filtration that might simplify the exposition.

Proposition 5.11

(
cf.
[
Ber15
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

The decompleted Chevalley-Eilenberg construction
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})
on a finite type nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},\{\ell_{i}\})
that is bounded from above or from below is a Sullivan algebra.

Proof.

We will define sets
S_{1},S_{2},\dots
of linear maps of the form
f\colon\Lambda^{i}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
i=1,2,\dots
. We set
S_{1}=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots\}
and define
S_{k}
,
k\geq 2
inductively, by declaring
S_{k}
as the set of maps of the form

\ell_{i}\circ(f_{k-1}\wedge\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}),

where
f_{k-1}\in S_{k-1}
. We define
\tau(S_{k})
to be the smallest integer
n
for which there is a map
f\colon\Lambda^{n}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
in
S_{k}
that is not trivial. If no such
n
exists, we set
\tau(S_{k})=\infty
.
For instance, if
\ell_{1}\neq 0
, then
\tau(S_{1})=1
, while
\tau(S_{2})\neq 1
since
\ell_{1}\circ\ell_{1}
is trivial. We have that
\tau(S_{2})=2
if at least one of the following maps
\ell_{1}\circ\ell_{2}
,
\ell_{2}\circ(\ell_{1}\wedge\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}})
is non-trivial.

Since
\ell_{1}
is a differential, and thus trivial when composed with itself,
\tau(S_{k})
has a lower bound depending on
k
, namely
\tau(S_{k})\geq\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\rceil
.

We define now a filtration

\mathfrak{g}=F^{0}\mathfrak{g}\supseteq F^{1}\mathfrak{g}\supseteq\cdots

where
F^{k}\mathfrak{g}
is spanned by all images of the maps in
S_{k}
(for instance,
both
\ell_{1}(x)
and
\ell_{i}(x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{i})
belong to
F^{1}\mathfrak{g}
, while
\ell_{2}(\ell_{1}(x)\wedge y)
belongs to
F^{2}\mathfrak{g}
). Since
\tau(S_{k})\geq\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\rceil
we have that
F^{k}\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\Gamma^{\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\rceil}\mathfrak{g}
and thus, if
\mathfrak{g}
is nilpotent, we have that there is some
n
for which
F^{n}\mathfrak{g}=0
.
Let

V(i)=\Ann(sF^{i}\mathfrak{g})=\{\varphi\in(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}\ |\ \varphi(sF^{i}\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}\}

We have that
0=V(0)\subseteq V(1)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V(n)=(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}
is a filtration of
(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}
. The condition

dV(i)\subseteq\Lambda V(i-1)
is equivalent to the condition

\ell_{k}(F^{i-1}\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathfrak{g})\subseteq F^{i}(\mathfrak{g}),

which holds by the definition of the filtration
F
. This gives that
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})=(\Lambda V,d)
is a Sullivan algebra.∎

5.4.
Proof of main theorem

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
L_{\infty}
-algebra where
\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}
is a finite type degree-wise nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Then the underlying additive group of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
acts on the set of Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\MC(\mathfrak{g})
, and the quotient
\MC(\mathfrak{g})/\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is isomorphic to the moduli space
\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
of gauge-equivalence classes of Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.9
we might just assume that
\mathfrak{g}=\sigma_{\leq 0}\mathfrak{g}
.
The nerve construction of Getzler on a nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, is given by
\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})=\{\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
, where
\Omega_{n}
is the Sullivan-de Rham algebra of polynomial differential forms on the
n
-simplex, and is a Kan complex
[
Get09
, § 4]
. In particular, for any two of its vertices

v_{0},v_{1}\in\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{0})\cong\MC(\mathfrak{g})
that belong to the same connected component, there is a 1-simplex
\theta\in\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{1})
such that
\{d_{0}\theta,d_{1}\theta\}=\{v_{0},v_{1}\}
.

As it is proved in
[
Get09
, Proposition 1.1]
, there are isomorphisms

\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})\cong\MC(\mathfrak{g})
and
\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\Lambda(t,dt))\cong\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{1})
. The face maps
d_{0},d_{1}\colon\MC(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Omega_{1})\to\MC(\mathfrak{g})
correspond to the maps

(ev_{0})_{*},(ev_{1})_{*}\colon\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\Lambda(t,dt))\to\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})

induced by evaluation at
t=0
and
t=1
. Hence two Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
are gauge equivalent if and only if their corresponding dg algebra morphisms
\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})
are homotopic. In particular, we have that

\mathscr{MC}(\mathfrak{g})=\pi_{0}(\MC_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong[C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k}].

By Proposition
5.11

C^{*}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Sullivan algebra, so we might apply Theorem
4.2
, yielding that the right-hand side above is given by the quotient

\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})/G_{S}(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})
.

We have that

G_{S}(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})=\Hom_{\mathds{k}}(\Lambda(s\mathfrak{g})^{\vee},\mathds{k})^{-1}=(s\mathfrak{g})_{1}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{0},

and since
\Hom(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathds{k})\cong\MC(\mathfrak{g})
, the assertion follows.

Genus two curves with everywhere good reduction over quadratic fields
Andrzej Dąbrowski, Mohammad Sadek

Abstract
. We address the question of existence of absolutely simple abelian varieties of dimension 2 with everywhere good reduction over quadratic fields. The emphasis will be given to the construction of pairs ( K , C ) (K,C) , where K K is a quadratic number field and C C is a genus 2 2 curve with everywhere good reduction over K K . We provide the first infinite sequence of pairs ( K , C ) (K,C) where K K is a real (respectively, complex) quadratic field and C C has everywhere good reduction over K K . Moreover, we show that the Jacobian of the curves C C in these families is an absolutely simple abelian variety.

1.
Introduction

It is due to Fontaine,
[
11
]
(see also Abrashkin
[
1
]
), that there are no abelian varieties with everywhere
good reduction over any of the number fields
{\mathbb{Q}}
,
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-1})
,
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})
, or
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{5})
.
The first example of an elliptic curve with everywhere good reduction over a quadratic field
was given by Tate over the field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{29})
, namely, the elliptic curve described by the Weierstrass equation
y^{2}+xy+\left({5+\sqrt{29}\over 2}\right)^{2}y=x^{3}
. Much work has been directed since then to giving examples of elliptic curves with everywhere good reduction over number fields with due attention to quadratic number fields, see

[
13
,
26
,
30
,
33
]
and references therein.
In
[
7
]
, an algorithm was given
to find all elliptic curves over a number field with good reduction outside a given
finite set of primes.

Shafarevich conjectured
[
27
]
that for each number field
K
, finite set of places
S
, and an integer
g\geq 2
, there are only finitely many
K
-isomorphism classes of curves of genus
g
over
K
with good reduction outside
S
. The proof was sketched by him in the hyperelliptic case; for details see
[
23
,
24
]
. According to Fontaine’s result, if
K={\mathbb{Q}}
then
S
must contain at least one prime so that such curves exist.
The papers
[
8
,
20
,
25
]
treated genus two curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with bad reduction
at exactly one prime.

Fixing integers
g,k\geq 2
, one may pose the following question:
does there exist a genus
g
curve with everywhere good reduction over
some number field
K
of degree
k
? Note that if a curve
C
has
everywhere good reduction over some number field, then the jacobian of

C
also has such a property, but the converse is not true in general.
In
[
2
]
, examples of genus 2 curves, hence abelian varieties of dimension 2,
with everywhere good reduction over quartic fields were given. However, the Jacobian varieties
of these curves are not absolutely simple. The first examples of genus two curves with everywhere good reduction
over real quadratic fields were constructed in

[
9
,
10
]
.
This was approached by combining the computation of Hecke eigenvalues of certain Hilbert modular forms, using new rational models of Hilbert modular surfaces, together with assuming the Eichler-Shimura conjecture for Hilbert modular forms. Up to the knowledge of the authors, the only example of a genus 2 curve with everywhere good reduction over a complex quadratic field can be found in
[
3
, Theorem 5.2]
.

In this article we produce genus two curves with everywhere good reduction over real quadratic fields without the current restrictions on the discriminant or the class number of the field. We also display a
1
-parameter family of pairs of genus 2 curves and real (respectively, complex) quadratic fields such that the curve has everywhere good reduction over the quadratic field. Finally we prove the existence of genus two curves with everywhere good reduction over a quadratic field such that its Jacobian has trivial endomorphisms, hence the Jacobian is an absolutely simple abelian variety.

Our approach makes use of Mestre’s algorithm to construct genus 2 curves from their Igusa invariants. Starting from rational invariants that satisfy certain local properties, we obtain genus two curves over the rational field. The only nontrivial automorphism of these curves is the hyperelliptic involution. Moreover, the minimal discriminants of these curves are of prescribed valuations at every rational bad prime of the curve. After a quadratic base change we prove that these curves attain everywhere good reduction. Mestre’s algorithm can be adjusted to produce genus 2 curves with non-generic automorphism groups,
[
6
]
. This enables us to produce infinitely many such curves with everywhere good reduction over corresponding quadratic fields.

2.
Preliminaries on genus two curves

Let
K
be a perfect field.
Let
C
be a (projective smooth) curve of genus two defined by a

Weierstrass equation
over
K
of the form

(1)

\displaystyle E:\;y^{2}+Q(x)y=P(x),\qquad P(x),Q(x)\in K[x],\;\deg Q(x)\leq 3,\;\deg P(x)\leq 6.

Given a Weierstrass equation of the form

(2)

y^{2}=f(x)=a_{0}x^{6}+a_{1}x^{5}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{3}x^{3}+a_{4}x^{2}+a_{5}x+a_{6},

Igusa associated to
f(x)
the invariants
J_{2}
,
J_{4}
,
J_{6}
,
J_{10}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[1/2,a_{0},a_{2},\cdots,a_{6}].
In fact,
J_{10}=2^{-12}\cdot\operatorname{Discriminant}(f)
if
a_{0}\neq 0
, and
J_{10}=2^{-12}a_{1}^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Discriminant}(f)
otherwise. For the full description of these invariants, see
[
12
]
when
a_{0}=0
, and
[
16
, §2]
for the general case. We define
Igusa invariants
,
J_{2i}(E)
,
i=1,2,3,5
, of the Weierstrass equation
E
to be the ones associated to
P(x)+Q(x)^{2}/4
.

Another set of invariants,
Igusa-Clebsch invariants
,
I_{2},I_{4},I_{6},I_{10}
may be associated to the Weierstrass equation
E
. The Igusa invariants are linked to the Igusa-Clebsch invariants as follows:

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{2}/8,

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4J_{2}^{2}-I_{4})/96,

\displaystyle J_{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(8J_{2}^{3}-160J_{2}J_{4}-I_{6})/576,

\displaystyle J_{8}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(J_{2}J_{6}-J_{4}^{2})/4,

(3)

\displaystyle J_{10}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{10}/4096.

If
E^{\prime}:y^{\prime 2}+S(x^{\prime})y^{\prime}=T(x^{\prime})
is another Weierstrass equation describing
C
, then there exist
a,b,c,d\in K
,
e\in K^{\times}
,
H[x]\in K[x]
such that

(4)

\displaystyle x^{\prime}=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d},\qquad y^{\prime}=\frac{ey+H(x)}{(cx+d)^{3}},\qquad\textrm{ where }ad-bc\neq 0.

In this case, we say that
E
and
E^{\prime}
are
K
-equivalent
. Two
K
-equivalent Weierstrass equations describe isomorphic genus 2 curves over
K
.

If
\operatorname{char}K\neq 2
, then one may assume that
Q(x)=0
. Further, one can see that

(5)

\displaystyle J_{10}(E^{\prime})=J_{10}(E)e^{-20}(ad-bc)^{30}.

In fact, two Weierstrass equations
E
and
E^{\prime}
describe isomorphic genus two curves over
\overline{K}
if and only if there is a
\lambda\in\overline{K}^{\times}
such that
J_{2i}(E^{\prime})=\lambda^{2i}J_{2i}(E)
.

We now assume that
K
is a discrete valuation field with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
, and normalized discrete valuation
\nu
. If
C
is a genus two curve defined over
K
by a Weierstrass equation
E:y^{2}+Q(x)y=P(x)
, then
E
is said to be
integral
if
P(x),Q(x)\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}[x]
. An integral Weierstrass equation
E
is
minimal
over
K
if
\nu(J_{10}(E))\leq\nu(J_{10}(E^{\prime}))
for any other integral Weierstrass equation
E^{\prime}
describing
C
over
K
. More precisely,
\nu(J_{10}(E^{\prime}))=\nu(J_{10}(E))+10m
, where
m\geq 0
is given by the formula in (
5
).

The curve
C
is said to have
good reduction
over
K
if there exists an integral Weierstrass equation
E
describing
C
over
K
such that
\nu(J_{10}(E))=0
. It is said to be of
potential good reduction
if there exists a finite extension
K\subset K^{\prime}
such that
C
attains good reduction over
K^{\prime}
. The following criterion for potential good reduction is due to Igusa,
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
C
be a genus two curve defined over
K
by the Weierstrass equation
E:y^{2}+Q(x)y=P(x)
. The curve
C
has potential good reduction if and only if

J_{2i}(E)^{5}/J_{10}(E)^{i}\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
for every
i\leq 5
.

Assuming that
K
is a number field with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
. A Weierstrass equation
E:y^{2}+Q(x)y=P(x)
describing a genus two curve
C
defined over
K
is said to be
integral
if
P(x),Q(x)\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}[x]
; whereas an integral Weierstrass equation
E
is said to be
minimal
at a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
if it is minimal over
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the completion of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
. The equation
E
is said to be
globally minimal
if it is minimal at every prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
. If
K
has class number 1, then one can always describe
C
by a globally minimal Weierstrass equation, see
[
17
, Proposition 2]
.

Similarly,
C
has good reduction at a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
if it has good reduction over
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and
C
is said to have
everywhere good reduction
if
C
has good reduction at every prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
.

Finally, one knows that if a genus two curve has good reduction at a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
, then its Jacobian has good reduction at
\mathfrak{p}
.

3.
Mestre’s algorithm

Let
K
be a number field. If
E
is a Weierstrass equation describing a genus 2 curve such that the Igusa invariants
J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E)
and
J_{10}(E)
are
K
-rational, then this does not mean necessarily that
C
can be described by a Weierstrass equation over
K
. In
[
21
]
, Mestre was able to give a necessary and sufficient condition for
C
to be defined by a Weierstrass equation over
K
when the only nontrivial element in the automorphism group of
C
is the hyperelliptic involution. For genus 2 curves with different automorphism groups, it was shown in
[
6
]
that if the Igusa invariants of
E
are
K
-rational, then
E
is
\overline{K}
-equivalent to a Weierstrass equation defined over
K
.

In what follows, we explain the construction of Mestre and Cardona-Quer. The description of the construction is algorithmic and it is implemented in
MAGMA
[
5
]
and
Sage
[
29
]
. Given a quadruple
(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10})\in K^{4}
,
J_{10}\neq 0
, we want to decide whether there is a Weierstrass equation
defined over
K
whose Igusa invariants are given by this quadruple. Equivalently, one may consider the following problem. If
F
is a field with characteristic
\neq 2,3,5
, we set

\mathcal{M}_{2}(\overline{F})=\{(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10})\in\overline{F}^{4}:J_{10}\neq 0\}/F^{\times}

where the action of
F^{\times}
is defined by a weighted scaling of the form

a\cdot(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10})=(a^{2}J_{2},a^{4}J_{4},a^{6}J_{6},a^{10}J_{10}).

We write
(J_{2}^{\prime},J_{4}^{\prime},J_{6}^{\prime},J_{10}^{\prime})\sim_{F}(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10})
if
(J_{2}^{\prime},J_{4}^{\prime},J_{6}^{\prime},J_{10}^{\prime})=a\cdot(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10})
for some
a\in F^{\times}
. A
moduli point

J\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(\overline{F})
is
defined over
F
if it is the equivalence class of a quadruple
(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10})
with all
J_{2i}\in F
,
i=1,2,3,5
.

Let
C
be a curve of genus 2 defined over
\overline{K}
by a Weierstrass equation
E
. We say that
F\subset\overline{K}
is a
field of moduli
for
C
if the equivalence class of
(J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E),J_{10}(E))
in
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\overline{K})
is defined over
F
. We say that
F\subset\overline{K}
is a
field of definition
for
C
if there exists a Weierstrass equation
E^{\prime}
defined over
F
which is
\overline{K}
-equivalent to
E
. The problem now boils down to: given that
K
is a field of moduli for
C
, is
K
a field of definition for
C
?

Let
C
be a genus 2 curve such that
K
is a field of moduli for
C
. One may construct homogeneous
ternary forms
Q_{J},T_{J}\in K[u,v,w]
of degrees 2 and 3 respectively, see
[
21
, §1.5]
for the explicit description and properties of
Q_{J}
and
T_{J}
. The conic
M_{J}
defined by
Q_{J}=0
has a
K
-rational point if
C
has a nontrivial automorphism other than the hyperelliptic involution, see
[
6
]
. On the other hand, the conic defined by
M_{J}:Q_{J}=0
may not have a
K
-rational point if the only nontrivial automorphism of
C
is the hyperelliptic involution. In the latter case, if
M_{J}(K)=\emptyset
, then there exists a quadratic extension
K\subset K^{\prime}
such that
M_{J}(K^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, see
[
21
]
. The existence of a rational point over
L\in\{K,K^{\prime}\}
gives rise to a parametrization
(u,v,w)=(u(x),v(x),w(x))
where
u,v,w\in L[x]
are polynomials of degree 2. Now we get the Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=T_{J}(u(x),v(x),w(x))
describing
C
where
T_{J}(u(x),v(x),w(x))\in L[x]
is a degree 6 polynomial. The following theorem summarizes the above discussion.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a Weierstrass equation describing a genus 2 curve
C
. Assume that
J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E),J_{10}(E)\in K
. Let
J
be the class of the quadruple of Igusa invariants
(J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),\\
J_{6}(E),J_{10}(E))
of
E
in
\mathcal{M}_{2}(\overline{K})
.

(1)

(Mestre,
[
21
]
) If
{\operatorname{Aut}}(C)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, then

K
is a field of definition for
C
if and only if
M_{J}(K)\neq\emptyset
.

(2)

(Cardona-Quer,
[
6
]
) If
{\operatorname{Aut}}(C)\not\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, then
K
is always a field of definition for
C
.

One remarks that when
{\operatorname{Aut}}(C)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, if
M_{J}(K)=\emptyset
, there are many quadratic extensions
K\subset K^{\prime}
over which
M_{J}(K^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. We set two of the coordinates
u,v,w
to be fixed and solve for the third coordinate. This gives a rational point on the conic over a quadratic extension
K\subset K^{\prime}
.

One disadvantage about Mestre’s algorithm is that the coefficients of the sextic
T_{J}(u(x),v(x),w(x))
are large numbers. This is why we will spend the next section discussing minimization of genus two curves.

4.
Minimization of genus two curves

In this section,
K
is a complete discrete valuation field with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
, maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, residue field
k={\mathcal{O}_{K}}/\mathfrak{m}
, a uniformiser
\pi
, and normalized discrete valuation
\nu
. We assume that
\operatorname{char}k>5
throughout this section. Moreover, we will be mainly concerned with genus two curves described by Weierstrass equations
E
defined over
K
with potential good reduction. In other words,
J_{2i}^{5}(E)/J_{10}^{i}(E)\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
for every
i\leq 5
, see Theorem
2.1
.

Let
(j_{2},j_{4},j_{6},j_{10})\in K^{4}
where
j_{10}\neq 0
. Let
C
be a genus 2 curve defined by a Weierstrass equation
E
over
K
with
(J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E),J_{10}(E))=\lambda\cdot(j_{2},j_{4},j_{6},j_{10})
for some
\lambda\in K^{\times}.
Assume, moreover, that
C
has potential good reduction over
K
. In what follows, we discuss the possibilities for
\nu(J_{10}(E))
if
E
is a minimal Weierstrass equation over
K
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two minimal Weierstrass equations over
K
describing two genus 2 curves with potential good reduction over
K
. Assume moreover that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are
\overline{K}
-equivalent.
Then

|\nu(J_{10}(E_{1}))-\nu(J_{10}(E_{2}))|\in\{0,5,10,15,20\}.

Proof:

We set
\overline{\nu}
to be an extension of
\nu
to
\overline{K}
.
One knows that
(J_{2}(E_{2}),J_{4}(E_{2}),J_{6}(E_{2}),J_{10}(E_{2}))=(\lambda^{2}J_{2}(E_{1}),\lambda^{4}J_{4}(E_{1}),\lambda^{6}J_{6}(E_{1}),\lambda^{10}J_{10}(E_{1}))
for some
\lambda\in\overline{K}^{\times}
. The fact that both
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
are minimal, hence integral, and
J_{2}(E_{2})=\lambda^{2}J_{2}(E_{1})\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
, imply that
\lambda^{2}\in K^{\times}
. Since both curves have potential good reduction over
K
and
\operatorname{char}k>5
, this implies that
\nu(J_{10}(E_{1})),\nu(J_{10}(E_{2}))\leq 20
, see
[
17
, Remarque 13]
. Now, since
\nu(J_{10}(E_{2}))=10\overline{\nu}(\lambda)+\nu(J_{10}(E_{1}))\leq 20
, it follows that

10\overline{\nu}(\lambda)\in\{0,5,10,15,20\}
.

\Box

An integral Weierstrass equation
E:y^{2}+Q(x)y=P(x)
over
K
will be called
reduced
over
K
if
4P(x)+Q(x)^{2}\not\equiv 0
in
k[x]
, otherwise, it is called
non-reduced
over
K
.

Remark 4.2
.

It is easy to see that if
E
is an integral Weierstrass equation describing a genus 2 curve
C
over
K
, and
E
is non-reduced over
K
, then the transformation
x\mapsto x
,
y\mapsto\pi^{1/2}y
yields an integral Weierstrass equation
E^{\prime}
over
K
that describes a genus 2 curve isomorphic to
C
over
K
, and
\nu(J_{10}(E^{\prime}))=\nu(J_{10}(E))-10
.

We also remark that if
E
is a minimal Weierstrass equation over
K
with
\nu(J_{10}(E))\leq 9
, then
E
is reduced over
K
, see
[
17
, Proposition 7]
.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
E:y^{2}+Q(x)y=P(x)
be integral over
K
and
4P(x)+Q(x)^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{6}a_{i}x^{i}
. If

\nu(a_{0})\geq 7,\;\nu(a_{1})\geq 5,\;\nu(a_{2})\geq 3,\;\nu(a_{3})\geq 1,

then
E
is
K
-equivalent to a non-reduced Weierstrass equation
E^{\prime}
via the transformation

x\mapsto\pi^{2}x
,
y\mapsto\pi^{3}y
. Moreover,
\nu(J_{10}(E^{\prime}))=\nu(J_{10}(E))
.

We recall that if
E
is a minimal Weierstrass equation over
K
describing a genus 2 curve
C
with
J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E)\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
and
J_{10}(E)\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, then the curve
C
has potential good reduction since
J_{2i}^{5}(E)/J_{10}^{i}(E)\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
for every
i
, see Theorem
2.1
. The fact that
C
has potential good reduction implies the existence of a finite extension
F
of
K
over which
C
attains good reduction. Moreover, one may assume that
F
is minimal, i.e.,
F
is contained in any other such field, see
[
16
, §3]
. Now we state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10}
be such that
J_{i}\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
,
i=2,4,6
, and
J_{10}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. Let
E
be a minimal Weierstrass equation over
K
describing a genus 2 curve
C
with
(J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E),J_{10}(E))\sim_{\overline{K}}(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},J_{10})
. Let
F
be the minimal field extension over which
C
attains good reduction and suppose that
[F:K]>1
.

If
E
is not
F
-equivalent to a non-reduced Weierstrass equation
E^{\prime}
over
F
with
\nu_{F}(J_{10}(E^{\prime}))=\nu_{F}(J_{10}(E))
, then
\nu(J_{10}(E))\in\{5,10,15\}.
Moreover, one has

a)

If
\nu(J_{10}(E))=5
, then
[F:K]=6
.

b)

If
\nu(J_{10}(E))=10
, then
[F:K]=3
.

c)

If
\nu(J_{10}(E))=15
, then
[F:K]=2
.

Proof:

One sees that
(J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E),J_{10}(E))=(\lambda^{2}J_{2},\lambda^{4}J_{4},\lambda^{6}J_{6},\lambda^{10}J_{10})
for some
\lambda\in\overline{K}^{\times}
. Now since
E
is minimal hence integral, it follows that
\lambda^{2}J_{2}\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
. Therefore,
\lambda^{2}\in K
. Since
\nu(J_{10}(E))=5\nu(\lambda^{2})+\nu(J_{10})=5\nu(\lambda^{2})\leq 20
,
[
17
, Remarque 13]
, it follows that
\nu(J_{10}(E))\in\{0,5,10,15,20\}
. We notice that
\nu(J_{10}(E))\neq 0
as
[F:K]>1
.

Since
C
has potential good reduction over
K
, and
\operatorname{char}k>5
, it follows that the minimal field
F
over which
C
attains good reduction is a totally and tamely ramified extension of
K
, hence cyclic, with
n_{F}:=[F:K]\in\{2,3,4,5,6,8,10\}
, see
[
15
, Corollaire 4.1]
. We set
\pi_{F}
to be a uniformiser in the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
of
F
, and we assume without loss of generality that
\pi_{F}^{n_{F}}
is either
\pi
or
-\pi
. We set
\nu_{F}
to be the corresponding discrete valuation on
F
.

One knows that since
E:y^{2}+Q(x)=P(x)
is not minimal over
F
, this yields that either
E
is non-reduced over
K
or
4P(x)+Q(x)^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{6}a_{i}x^{i}\in{\mathcal{O}_{K}}[x]
has a root
x_{0}
of multiplicity at least
4
in
k
, see for example
[
22
, Lemma 5.3]
.

Since
E
is reduced by assumption, then
4P(x)+Q(x)^{2}
has a root
x_{0}
of multiplicity at least 4. We use the translation map
x-t
where
t
is a lift of
x_{0}
in
{\mathcal{O}_{K}}
. This map does not change
\nu(J_{10}(E))
, and it allows us to assume that
\nu(a_{i})\geq 1
when
i=0,1,2,3
, as now
0
is the root of
4P(x)+Q(x)^{2}
in

5.
Globally minimal Weierstrass equations

In this section we show that the existence of a minimal Weierstrass equation describing a genus 2 curve over a number field

We assume throughout this section that

.

. Let

. Then we list some of the Weierstrass equations

Let

. If, moreover, for every prime

, one either has
\nu_{p}(J_{10}(E))=10

Algorithm 6.1
.

Input:
A quadruple

that satisfies condition (*).

Output:

A quadratic field extension

of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

with everywhere good reduction over

E
over
K
and
(J_{2}(E),J_{4}(E),J_{6}(E),J_{10}(E))\sim_{\overline{K}}(J_{2},J_{4},J_{6},\pm 1)
. Moreover,

is globally minimal except possibly at the primes of

.

The fact that

The steps of the algorithm are as follows:

1)

For every prime

2)

For every prime

of multiplicity

3)

If

is the list of primes in 2), then use the transformation

x\mapsto(\pm p_{1}\ldots p_{n})^{1/2}x,\qquad y\mapsto(\pm p_{1}\ldots p_{n})^{3/2}y.

, where

are the prime divisors of

with

. Moreover, the latter Weierstrass equation has everywhere good reduction over
K
, see Theorem
5.1
.

One remarks that the fact that the input of Algorithm

satisfies condition (*) implies that the associated Weierstrass equation

satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem

. In particular, the algorithm must terminate with the aforementioned output as explained in the proof of Theorem

.

In this section we consider integral Weierstrass equations

. In

In
[

, or

{\mathbb{Q}}

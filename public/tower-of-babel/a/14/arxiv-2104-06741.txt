Diophantine problems over ℤ a ​ b \mathbb{Z}^{ab} modulo prime numbers
Konstantinos Kartas

Abstract
. Let ℤ a ​ b \mathbb{Z}^{ab} be the ring of integers of ℚ a ​ b \mathbb{Q}^{ab} , the maximal abelian extension of ℚ \mathbb{Q} . We show that there exists an algorithm to decide whether a system of equations and inequations, with integer coefficients, has a solution in ℤ a ​ b \mathbb{Z}^{ab} modulo every rational prime.



Introduction

In
[
Ax67
]
, Ax has exhibited an algorithm for deciding whether a given system of polynomial equations (and inequations) over
\mathbb{Z}
has a solution modulo
p
, for all prime numbers
p\in\mathbb{P}
. Phrased differently, the existential theory of all prime fields
\{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}:p\in\mathbb{P}\}
in the language of rings
L_{r}
is decidable. One year later, Ax
[
Ax68
]
generalized this by showing that the full first-order theory of
\{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}:p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is decidable. This was perhaps in sharp contrast with the fact that no algorithm exists for determining whether a system of polynomial equations has a solution in
\mathbb{Z}
and that the corresponding problem for
\mathbb{Q}
, i.e. Hilbert’s tenth problem over
\mathbb{Q}
, is widely open.

In this paper, we study an analogous problem for
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
, where
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
is the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}^{ab}
, the maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. It is not known whether the existential theory of
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
is decidable in
L_{r}
. For some comments on the
conjectural
undecidability of
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
, see the last bullet point on Section 6.3, pg.191 in
[
Koe12
]
.

Our main goal is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

The existential theory of
\{\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}:p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is decidable in
L_{r}
.

An important difference with the existential theory of
\{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}:p\in\mathbb{P}\}
, is that
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
is not a field; in fact it is not even a local ring but merely a direct limit of semi-local rings. In particular, one cannot simply eliminate inequations
\exists x(g(x)\neq 0)
by replacing them with
\exists x,y(yg(x)-1=0)
. Similarly, one cannot replace
\exists x(g_{1}(x)\neq 0\land...\land g_{n}(x)\neq 0)
with
\exists x(g_{1}(x)\cdot...\cdot g_{n}(x)\neq 0)
, since
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
is not an integral domain. Moreover, the ring
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
has lots of nilpotent elements, which arise due to high ramification and make its model theory difficult to understand. Nonetheless, the ring
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
resembles "enough" a valuation ring, so that we can eventually encode our problem inside
ACVF

. We have that

\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}\models\exists x(\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{i}(x)=0\land g_{i}(x)\neq 0)

\iff\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}\models\exists x_{1},...,x_{n}(\bigwedge_{1\leq i,j\leq n}f_{i}(x_{j})=0\land g_{i}(x_{i})\neq 0)

where each
x_{j}=(x_{j1},...,x_{jm})
an
m
-tuple.

Proof.

Immediate from Proposition
1.3.3
.
∎

2.
From
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
to ACVF

In the previous section we saw how
Th_{\exists}(\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab})
reduces to
Th_{\exists}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1})
(see Corollary
1.3.4
). We shall now encode the existential theory of
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}
in
L_{r}
inside the existential theory of
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}
in
L_{val}
. The latter is well understood by a result of Ansombe-Fehm (see Theorem
2.2.1
) and is precisely
ACVF_{(p,p),\exists}
.

2.1.
Positive characteristic valuation rings

Fix a prime
p\in\mathbb{P}
. We convert existential statements about
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}
in
L_{r}
to existential statements about
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}
in
L_{val}
and also
uniformly
in
p
. The main idea originates in Section 4.1
[
Kar20
]
and the following Proposition is an easy adaptation of Proposition 4.1.1
[
Kar20
]
:

Proposition 2.1.1
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{P}
and let
f_{i}(x),g_{i}(x)\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
be multi-variable polynomials in
x=(x_{1},...,x_{m})
for
i=1,...,n
. Then

\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}\models\exists x\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}(f_{i}(x)=0\land g_{i}(x)\neq 0)\iff

\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}\models\exists x\in\mathcal{O}_{v}\bigwedge_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(v(f_{i}(x))>v(g_{j}(x)))

Proof.

First observe that

\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[[t]]^{1/p^{\infty}}/t^{p-1}\cong\varinjlim\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[[t^{1/p^{n}}]]/t^{p-1}\cong\varinjlim\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{n}}]/t^{p-1}\cong\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}\ \ (\dagger)

"
\Rightarrow
": Let
a\in(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1})^{m}
be such that
f_{i}(a)=0\land g_{i}(a)\neq 0
, for
i=1,...,n
and let
\tilde{a}
be any lift of
a
in
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[[t]]^{1/p^{\infty}}
via the isomorphism
(\dagger)
. We see that
v(f_{i}(\tilde{a}))\geq p-1>v(g_{j}(\tilde{a}))
, for all
i,j=1,...,n
.

"
\Leftarrow
": Let
b\in(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[[t]]^{1/p^{\infty}})^{m}
be such that
v(g_{j}(b))<v(f_{i}(b))
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Set
\gamma_{1}=max\{v(g_{j}(b)):j=1,...,n\}
and
\gamma_{2}=min\{v(f_{i}(b)):i=1,...,n\}
and consider the open interval
I=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\subseteq\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
. Since
\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}\mathbb{Z}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}
, we can find
q\in\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}\mathbb{Z}
such that
p-1\in qI
. We now make use of the fact that for each
q\in\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}\mathbb{Z}^{>0}
, there is an
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-embedding

\rho:\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}\to\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}

which maps
t\mapsto t^{q}
. Indeed, if
q\in\frac{1}{p^{N}}\mathbb{Z}^{>0}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, then there exists an embedding
\rho:\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{N}}\to\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{N}}
mapping
t\to t^{q}
, exactly as in Remark 7.9
[
AF16
]
. Such a map can also be extended uniquely to the perfect hull
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}
.

Now let
\rho:\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}\to\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}}
be as above. Then, since
f_{i}(x),g_{j}(x)\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
and
\rho
fixes
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, we get

v(g_{j}(\rho(b)))=v(\rho(g_{j}(b)))=qv(g_{j}(b))<qv(f_{i}(b))=v(\rho(f_{i}(b)))=v(f_{i}(\rho(b)))

for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
. We may thus replace our witness
b
with
a=\rho(b)
.

Since
p-1\in qI
, we get
f_{i}(a)=0\mod t^{p-1}\land g_{i}(a)\neq 0\mod t^{p-1}
, for all
i=1,...,n
. The reduction of
a
modulo
t^{p-1}
, seen as a tuple in
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}
via
\dagger
, is the desired witness.
∎

Remark 2.1.2
.

If we have a positive existential sentence, then one sees that

\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}\models\exists x\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{i}(x)=0\iff ACF_{p}\models\exists x\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{i}(x)=0

and no reference to valuations is needed. A similar observation holds true for an existential sentence defined purely by inequations. On the other hand, we see that

\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}\models\exists x\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}(f_{i}(x)=0\land g_{i}(x)\neq 0)

\not\Rightarrow ACF_{p}\models\exists x\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}(f_{i}(x)=0\land g_{i}(x)\neq 0)

as the example
\exists x(x^{p}=1\land x\neq 1)
shows.

2.2.
Diophantine problems modulo a fixed prime

We will need a special case of the following Theorem due to Ansombe-Fehm:

Theorem 2.2.1

(Corollary 7.2
[
AF16
]
)
.

Let
(K,v)
and
(L,w)
be equicharacteristic henselian non-trivially
valued fields. Then
Th_{\exists}(K,v)=Th_{\exists}(L,w)
in
L_{val}
if and only if
Th_{\exists}(Kv)=Th_{\exists}(Lw)
in
L_{r}
.

In that special case, there is a direct argument which was communicated to me by E. Hrushovski. It bears many similarities with the proof of Corollary 7.7
[
AF16
]
but ultimately relies on A. Robinson’s results on ACVF and not Kuhlmann’s theory of tame fields. We refer to Sections 3.4 and 3.5
[
vdD12
]
for background material on
ACVF
.

Observation 2.2.2
.

We have that
Th_{\exists}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}},v_{t})=ACVF_{(p,p),\exists}
in the language of valued fields
L_{val}
.

Proof.

Every finite extension of
(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t)),v_{t})
will be a complete discrete valued field with residue field
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and thus (abstractly) isomorphic to
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))
(see Theorem 2, pg.33
[
Ser80
]
). Therefore, the valued field
(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))},v_{t})
is a directed union of structures isomorphic to
(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t)),v_{t})
. It follows that

Th_{\exists}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t)),v_{t})=Th_{\exists}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))})=ACVF_{(p,p),\exists}

Moreover, if
(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t)),v_{t})\subset(F,v)\subset(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))},v_{t})
is any intermediate field, then we will also have that
Th_{\exists}(F,v)=ACVF_{(p,p),\exists}
, since by default
Th_{\exists}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t)),v_{t})\subset Th_{\exists}(F,v)\subset Th_{\exists}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))},v_{t})
and as we argued above
Th_{\exists}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t)),v_{t})=Th_{\exists}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}((t))},v_{t})
. In particular, we get that
Th_{\exists}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}((t))^{1/p^{\infty}},v_{t})=ACVF_{(p,p),\exists}
, which is what we wanted to show.

∎

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{P}
and let
f_{i}(x),g_{i}(x)\in\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[x]
be multi-variable polynomials in
x=(x_{0},...,x_{m})
for
i=1,...,n
. Then

\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}\models\exists x\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}(f_{i}(x)=0\land g_{i}(x)\neq 0)\iff

ACVF_{(p,p)}\models\exists x\in\mathcal{O}_{v}\bigwedge_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(v(f_{i}(x))>v(g_{j}(x)))

Proof.

Immediate from Proposition
2.1.1
and Observation
2.2.2
.
∎

Recall that the theory
ACVF_{(q,p)}
is complete in
L_{val}
by A. Robinson for each characteristic pair
(q,p)
(see Section 3.5, Corollary 3.34
[
vdD12
]
) and thus also decidable, since it is recursively axiomatized.

Corollary 2.2.4
.

Fix a prime number
p\in\mathbb{P}
. The existential theory of
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
is decidable in
L_{r}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
1.3.4
, it suffices to show that
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]/t^{p-1}
is existentially decidable in
L_{r}
. Once again, using Lemma
1.1.2
, we may focus on the special case where
\phi
is a conjunction of literals. The conclusion now follows either by using Proposition
2.1.1
and Theorem
2.2.1
or by Proposition
2.2.3
and the fact that
ACVF_{(p,p)}
is (existentially) decidable in
L_{val}
due to A. Robinson.
∎

In Question
3.2
we formulate two unsolved variants.

2.3.
Diophantine problems modulo all primes

It is not difficult now to put all the pieces together:

Theorem A
.

The existential theory of
\{\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}:p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is decidable in
L_{r}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.1.2
, we may focus our attention on sentences
\phi\in L_{r}
of the form

\phi=\exists x(\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{i}(x)=0\land g_{i}(x)\neq 0)

where
x=(x_{1},...,x_{m})
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we have by Corollary
1.3.4
and Proposition
2.2.3
that
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}\models\phi\iff ACVF_{(p,p)}\models\phi^{*}
, where

\phi^{*}=\exists x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}[\bigwedge_{1\leq i,j,k\leq n}(v(f_{i}(x_{j}))>v(g_{k}(x_{k})))]

Note that
\phi^{*}
depends only on
\phi
and
not
on
p
. Now a standard argument may be used to check if
ACVF_{(p,p)}\models\phi^{*}
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}
:

First check if
ACVF_{(0,0)}\models\phi^{*}
, which can be done recursively since
ACVF_{(0,0)}
is decidable in
L_{val}
. If we get a positive answer, an application of Gödel’s completeness theorem gives
ACVF_{(p,p)}\models\phi^{*}
for
p\geq p_{0}
, where
p_{0}\in\mathbb{P}
is
computable
. It suffices to check that
ACVF_{(p,p)}\models\phi^{*}
for finitely many
p\leq p_{0}
, which is again possible since for each individual
p\in\mathbb{P}
the theory
ACVF_{(p,p)}
is decidable. If we get a negative answer, then by completeness of
ACVF_{(0,0)}
, we will have that
ACVF_{(0,0)}\models\lnot\phi^{*}
, whence
ACVF_{(p,p)}\models\lnot\phi^{*}
, for all but finitely many
p\in\mathbb{P}
.
∎

Remark 2.3.1
.

Using Remark
2.1.2
, we see that
T=Th_{\exists^{+}}(\{\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}:p\in\mathbb{P}\})
simply reduces to
ACF
, i.e.

T\models\exists x(\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{i}(x)=0)\iff ACF\models\exists x(\bigwedge_{1\leq i\leq n}f_{i}(x)=0)

and no reference to valuations is needed.

3.
Open problems

The following two questions are of central importance:

Question 3.1
.

(a)
Is
Th(\mathbb{Z}^{ab})
undecidable in
L_{r}
?

(b)
Is
Th_{\exists}(\mathbb{Z}^{ab})
undecidable in
L_{r}
?

For some comments on the
conjectural
undecidability of
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
, see the last bullet point on Section 6.3, pg.191 in
[
Koe12
]
. If one wants to explore aspects of
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
which are more likely to be decidable, one may ask the following natural extensions of the results presented in this paper:

Question 3.2
.

(a)
Is
Th(\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab})
decidable in
L_{r}
? How about
Th(\{\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p\mathbb{Z}^{ab}:p\in\mathbb{P}\})
in
L_{r}
?

(b)
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Is
Th_{\exists}(\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/n\mathbb{Z}^{ab})
decidable in
L_{r}
?

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem
1.1.4
, we see that Question
3.2
(b)
is reduced to the case of
\mathbb{Z}^{ab}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}^{ab}
for
p\in\mathbb{P}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

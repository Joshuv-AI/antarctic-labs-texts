Abstract
. In this work we study existence, asymptotic behavior and stability properties of O ⁡ ( m ) × O ⁡ ( n ) − O(m)\times O(n)- invariant solutions of the Allen-Cahn equation Δ ​ u + u ⁡ ( 1 − u 2 ) = 0 \Delta u+u(1-u^{2})=0 in ℝ m × ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n} with m , n ≥ 2 m,n\geq 2 and m + n ≥ 8 m+n\geq 8 . We exhibit four families of solutions whose nodal sets are smooth logarithmic corrections of the Lawson cone and with infinite Morse index. This work complements the study started in [ 18 ] by Pacard and Wei and [ 1 ] by Agudelo, Kowalckzyk and Rizzi.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Allen-Cahn equation

(1.1)

-\Delta u=u-u^{3}\quad\hbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

Equation (
1.1
) arises in the context of the gradient theory of phase transitions
[
5
]
describing the behaviour of two liquids which are mixed in a container, or two different materials in a binary alloy.

In the seventy’s, the developments in the
\Gamma
-convergence theory, (see
[
16
,
17
]
) showed the strong and still fruitful relation between equation (
1.1
) and the theory of
minimal surfaces.
A famous conjecture raised by E. De Giorgi in 1978
[
10
]
asserts that, at least when
N\leq 7
, if
u:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded solution of (
1.1
) which is monotone in one direction, then
u
is one dimensional, i.e.,
u
depends only on one euclidean variable. In other words,

u(\xi)=v_{\star}(\nu\cdotp\xi+c)\quad\hbox{for }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1},

where
\nu\in S^{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
are fixed and
v_{\star}(t):=\tanh(t/\sqrt{2})
is the unique solution to the corresponding ODE problem

(1.2)

-v^{\prime\prime}_{\star}=v_{\star}-v_{\star}^{3},\qquad v_{\star}(0)=0,\qquad v_{\star}(\pm\infty)=\pm 1.

The De Giorgi conjecture is also motivated by the Bernstein conjecture, which states that any entire
minimal graph
over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is affine if
N\leq 7
( see
[
7
]
). The Bernstein conjecture is known to be true and sharp about the dimension, in fact in
[
8
]
the authors construct an entire minimal graph
\Gamma
over
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
which is not affine, for any
n\geq 4
.

The De Giorgi conjecture is known to be true in dimension
N+1=2
(see
[
13
]
) and
N+1=3
(see
[
6
,
12
]
). Under the additional assumption that

\lim_{\xi_{1}\to\pm\infty}u(\xi_{1},\xi^{\prime})=\pm 1\qquad\forall\,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

one-dimensional symmetry was proved in dimension
4\leq N+1\leq 8
in
[
19
]
. In this case too, the conjecture is sharp about the dimension, since in dimension
N+1=9
there exists a family of solutions to (
1.1
) which are monotone in one direction but not one-dimensional (see
[
11
]
). In fact, the zero level set is close to a dilated version of the minimal graph
\Gamma
constructed in
[
8
]
.

The monotonicity assumption in De Giorgi’s conjecture is related to the stability of the solution and it is strongly connected to the stability properties of the nodal set of the solutions. Stability is a natural tool to classify bounded solutions of equation (
1.1
). In dimension
N+1=2
, stable entire solutions are one-dimensional (see
[
12
]
).

Stable entire solutions to the Allen-Cahn equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
are expected to be one-dimensional if
N\leq 6
, this is related to the fact that the Lawson cones

(1.3)

C_{m,n}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,(n-1)|x|^{2}=(m-1)|y|^{2}\right\},\qquad m,\,n\geq 2

are stable provided
N+1=m+n\geq 8
(see
[
1
,
9
]
), while there are no minimising cones embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
if
N\leq 6
.

In the case
N\geq 7
stable solutions which are not one-dimensional are known to exist (see
[
18
]
). The nodal sets of these solutions are close to stable (actually minimising) minimal hypersurfaces, which are asymptotic to the Lawson cone
C_{m,n}
in the case
m+n\geq 9
or
m+n=8
and
m,\,n\geq 3
.

As for unstable solutions, the authors in
[
2
]
exhibit two families of axially symmetric bounded solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. One of them having Morse Index one and the other having large Morse Index.

In
[
1
]
, the authors consider the case
N+1=m+n\geq 8
with
m,\,n\geq 3
and generalise the existence result in
[
18
]
. More precisely, in
[
1
]
the authors show solutions satisfying that

(1.4)

u(\sigma x,\gamma y)=u(x,y),\qquad\forall\,(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\,(\sigma,\gamma)\in O(m)\times O(n)

and whose zero level set is the disjoint union of two connected components that are smooth logarithmic corrections from the Lawson cone
C_{m,n}
.

Our first main result in this work is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m,n\geq 2
,
N+1=n+m\geq 8
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, there exist a bounded solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to the Allen-Cahn equation (
1.1
) such that

i.

u_{\varepsilon}
is smooth and
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant;

ii.

the zero level set of
u_{\varepsilon}
is the disjoint union of
k
connected components;

iii.

there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
R>2\varepsilon^{-1}
,

(1.5)

\int_{B_{R}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(1-u_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}\right)\leq CR^{N}

iv.

the Morse index of
u_{\varepsilon}
is infinite.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
generalizes the results in
[
1
]
, by exhibiting solutions with nodal sets having
k\geq 2
components diverging from the Lawson cone with logarithmic growth (see Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
below) and where either
n=2
or
m=2
are allowed.

Moreover, the Morse index of these solutions, as well as the solutions in
[
1
]
, is infinite.

Another consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that in higher dimensions, the energy estimate (
1.5
) does not imply that the Morse index is finite.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
u_{eps}
be solution predicted in Theorem
1.1
. Then,
-u_{\varepsilon}
also satisfies properties
{\rm i.-iv.}
in Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand, switching the roles of
m
and
n
, it is possible to construct (in the same manner as for
u_{\varepsilon}
and
-u_{\varepsilon}
) two more solutions
v_{\varepsilon}
and
-v_{\varepsilon}
of
O(n)\times O(m)
invariant solutions to (
1.1
) satisfies properties
{\rm i.-iv.}
in Theorem
1.1
.

Composing with the reflection
\sigma(x,y)=(y,x)
, defined for
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we get two more families of solutions
\pm w_{\varepsilon}:=\pm v_{\varepsilon}\circ\sigma
to (
1.1
) fulfilling properties (
i.
), (
ii.
) and (
iii.
) of Theorem
1.1
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we first find an approximation of the candidate of nodal set. This candidate is in turn, based on suitable
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant hypersurfaces, which are asymptotic to the Lawson cone
C_{m,n}
at infinity, with
m,n\geq 2
,
m+n\geq 8
. Our next result deals with existence and stability of such surfaces.

Denote

E^{+}_{m,n}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,(n-1)|x|^{2}<(m-1)|y|^{2}\right\}

E^{-}_{m,n}:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,(n-1)|x|^{2}>(m-1)|y|^{2}\right\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
m,n\geq 2
,
m+n\geq 8
. Then there exist two unique minimal hypersurfaces
\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}\subset E^{\pm}_{m,n}
asymptotic to the cone
C_{m,n}
at infinity and satisfying that

(i)

\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
are smooth;

(ii)

\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
are
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant;

(iii)

{\rm dist}(\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n},\{0\})=1
;

(iv)

\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,m}
are stable.

Theorem
1.2
is a generalisation of Theorem
1.1
in
[
1
]
, which also deals with the cases
n=2
and
m=6
or
n=6
and
m=2
. If
m+n\geq 9
and
m,n\geq 2
or
m+n=8
and
m,n\geq 3
, stability follows from Theorem
2.1
of
[
14
]
, which also provides the construction of such surfaces under the hypothesis that the tangent cone is minimising. The main contribution of Theorem
1.2
is the proof of existence and stability in the cases
m=2
and
n=6
or
m=6
and
n=2
, which is a non trivial new result.

The next result provides the sets that will serve as main approximation of the nodal set of the solutions predicted in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
m,\,n\geq 2
,
m+n\geq 8
and let
\gamma>0
. Let
\Sigma
denote one of the two minimal hypersurfaces constructed in Theorem
1.2
. Then there exists
\delta_{*}>0
small such that if
0<\delta\leq\delta_{*}
, the system of equations

(1.6)

\delta\big(\Delta_{\Sigma}{\tt h}_{j}+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}{\tt h}_{j}\big)=\gamma(e^{-\sqrt{2}({\tt h}_{j}-{\tt h}_{j-1})}-e^{-\sqrt{2}({\tt h}_{j+1}-{\tt h}_{j})}),\qquad 1\leq j\leq k

has a smooth solution
{\tt h}:=({\tt h}_{1},\dots,{\tt h}_{k})
which is
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant and such that

(1.7)

-\infty\equiv{\tt h}_{0}<{\tt h}_{1}<{\tt h}_{2}<\cdots<{\tt h}_{k}<{\tt h}_{k+1}\equiv+\infty.

Observe that property (
1.7
) in Theorem
1.3
guarantees that the graphs of the
{\tt h}_{j}
are mutually disjoint.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is that the connected components of the zero level set of the solutions to (
1.1
) are close to the graphs of the
{\tt h}_{j}
’s. The proof relies on an infinite dimensional Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we prove Theorem
1.2
and study the Jacobi fields of the predicted surfaces. In Section 3 we prove Theorem

1.1
and leave the calculations of the Morse Index of the solutions to Section 5.

2.
The surfaces
\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
: construction and Jacobi fields

The aim of this Section is to prove Theorem
1.2
, that is to construct the surfaces
\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
, and to study the asymptotic behaviour at infinity of their Jacobi fields. We will actually prove more than Theorem
1.2
, that is we will prove the following Theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
m,n\geq 2
,
m+n\geq 8
. Then there exist two unique minimal hypersurfaces
\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}\subset E^{\pm}_{m,n}
asymptotic to the cone
C_{m,n}
at infinity and satisfying properties (i), (ii) and (iii) of Theorem
1.2
. Moreover

(2.1)

\langle{\tt p},\nu_{\Sigma^{+}_{m,n}}({\tt p})\rangle>0\qquad\forall\,{\tt p}\in\Sigma^{+}_{m,n}

and

(2.2)

\langle{\tt p},\nu_{\Sigma^{-}_{m,n}}({\tt p})\rangle<0\qquad\forall\,{\tt p}\in\Sigma^{-}_{m,n},

being
\nu_{\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}}({\tt p})
the unit normal to
\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
at
{\tt p}
pointing towards
E^{+}_{m,n}
.

In the sequel, we will set
\Sigma:=\Sigma^{-}_{m,n}
and
E^{+}:=E^{+}_{m,n}
. We set

\gamma^{\pm}=-\frac{N-2}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}-(N-1)}.

Remark 2.1
.

(1)

The results in
[
4

and
[

imply that, outside a ball,
\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}

where

(2)

If a minimal surface
\Sigma
has a positive Jacobi field
u>0
, then it is stable. In fact, setting
w:=\log u
, we have

\Delta_{\Sigma}w=\frac{\Delta_{\Sigma}u}{u}-|\nabla_{\Sigma}w|^{2}=-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}-|\nabla_{\Sigma}w|^{2}.

Taking a test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
, multiplying by
\varphi^{2}
and integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}w|^{2}\varphi^{2}+2\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\nabla w\cdotp\nabla\varphi

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}w|^{2}\varphi^{2}+2\int_{\Sigma}|\varphi||\varphi\nabla w|\,|\nabla\varphi|

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}w|^{2}\varphi^{2}+\int_{\Sigma}\varphi^{2}|\varphi\nabla w|^{2}+\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}.

(3)

Point (iv) of Theorem
1.2
follows from Theorem
2.1

\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
have a positive Jacobi field given by the function
{\tt p}\in\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}\mapsto\pm\langle{\tt p},\nu_{\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}}({\tt p})\rangle
, so in particular
\Sigma^{\pm}_{m,n}
are stable.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

We look for
O(m)\times O(n)
-invariant smooth surfaces, that is surfaces generated by a smooth, regular curve
\Upsilon:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

\Upsilon(s):=(a(s),b(s))
in the half-plane

Q:=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,a>0\}

In other words, we want to construct surfaces
\Sigma
such that, for any
{\tt p}\in\Sigma

(s,{\tt x},{\tt y})=(s({\tt p}),{\tt x}({\tt p}),{\tt y}({\tt p}))\in\mathbb{R}\times S^{m-1}\times S^{n-1}

(2.3)

{\tt p}:=\left(a(s){\tt x},b(s){\tt y}\right).

If we parametrise
\Upsilon
by arc length, that is we impose the condition
(a^{\prime})^{2}+(b^{\prime})^{2}=1
, then the zero mean curvature equation translates into the ODE

(2.4)

-a^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime}+a^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}+(m-1)\frac{b^{\prime}}{a}+(n-1)\frac{a^{\prime}}{b}=0.

Now we introduce the change of variables

(2.5)

\tan u=\frac{a}{b}\qquad\tan v=\frac{a^{\prime}}{b^{\prime}}

also used in
[
3
]
, so that equation (
2.4

(2.6)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=X_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=X_{2}(u,v)

where the vector field
X(u,v)=(X_{1}(u,v),X_{2}(u,v))
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} X_{1}(u,v)&:=\cos u\sin u\sin(u-v)\\
X_{2}(u,v)&:=(m-1)\sin u\sin v-(n-1)\cos u\cos v\end{aligned}

The aim now is to study the phase plane associated to vector field
X
, in order to determine all the associated orbits. By periodicity, we can reduce ourselves to consider this vector field in the rectangle
D:=(0,\frac{\pi}{2})\times(-\pi,\pi)

\bar{D}
is compact and

is continuous, it follows that every orbit is complete, that is defined in all

\displaystyle\{(u,v)\in\bar{D}:\,X_{1}(u,v)=0\}

\displaystyle=\{(u,v)\in\bar{D}:\,v=u-k\pi,\,k\in\{0,1\}\}\cup(\{0\}\times[-\pi,\pi])\cup(\{\frac{\pi}{2}\}\times[-\pi,\pi])

\displaystyle\{(u,v)\in\bar{D}:\,X_{1}(u,v)>0\}

\displaystyle=\{(u,v)\in\bar{D}:\,u-\pi<v<u\}

\displaystyle\{(u,v)\in\bar{D}:\,X_{1}(u,v)<0\}

\displaystyle=\{(u,v)\in\bar{D}:\,v<u-\pi\}\cup\{(u,v)\in\bar{D}:\,v>u\}

Similarly, setting

Remark 3.2

Let

By recursion, the solution

5.
The Morse index

Recall that
\sigma:=|\ln(\varepsilon^{2})|
, for
\varepsilon>0
small. In order to prove that the Morse index is infinite for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, it is enough to prove the existence of an infinite dimensional space
X
of compactly supported functions such that the quadratic form

(5.1)

\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}(1+\sigma)\phi^{2}

satisfies

(5.2)

\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}(\phi)<-\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi^{2}\qquad\forall\,\phi\in X.

In order to do so, we first consider the eigenvalue problem for the cone, which reads

(5.3)

-\phi_{rr}-\frac{N-1}{r}\phi_{r}-\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}\phi=\lambda\frac{N-1}{r^{2}}\phi

Introducing the change of variables
r=e^{t}
and writing
\phi(r)=v(t)
, equation (
5.3
) reads

(5.4)

v_{tt}+(N-2)v_{t}+(N-1)(1+\lambda)v=0.

We look for solutions of the form
v(t)=e^{\gamma t}
. A direct calculation shows that
v(t)
is a solution if and only if

\gamma=\gamma_{\pm}(\lambda):=-\frac{N-2}{2}\pm\sqrt{\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}-(N-1)(1+\lambda)}=-\frac{N-2}{2}\pm i\sqrt{\alpha(\lambda)}

where

\alpha(\lambda):=-\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}+(N-1)(1+\lambda)>0

provided
\lambda>\lambda_{0}(N)>0
. As a consequence, for
\lambda=\sigma-2
large enough, the functions

v_{\sigma,+}(t)=e^{-\frac{N-2}{2}t}\cos(\sqrt{\alpha(\sigma-2)}t),\qquad v_{\sigma,-}(t)=e^{-\frac{N-2}{2}t}\sin(\sqrt{\alpha(\sigma-2)}t)

are
formal
point-wise eigenfunctions of the
Jacobi operator
of the cone
C_{m,n}
with eigenvalue
\lambda>\lambda_{0}(N)
, but they have infinite energy space, i.e.,

\int_{1}^{\infty}(v_{\sigma,\pm}(\log s))^{2}\beta(s)a^{m-1}(s)b^{n-1}(s)ds\geq C\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-(N-2)}s^{-2}s^{N-1}ds=\int_{1}^{\infty}s^{-1}ds=\infty.

Set

v_{\sigma}(t):=v_{\sigma,+}(t)=e^{-\frac{N-2}{2}t}\cos(\sqrt{\alpha(\sigma-2)}t)

and observe that
v_{\lambda}
vanishes exactly at the points
t_{k}:=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\alpha(\sigma-2)}}(1+2k)
.

Now, we set

{\tt w}_{\sigma,k}({\tt p}):=w_{\sigma,k}(s):=v_{\sigma}(\log s)\chi_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(\log s),\qquad s=s({\tt p}),\,\forall\,{\tt p}\in\Sigma.

It is possible to see that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\sigma}({\tt w}_{\sigma,k})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\tt w}_{\sigma,k}|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}(1+\sigma){\tt w}_{\sigma,k}^{2}=

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}(-\Delta_{\Sigma}{\tt w}_{\sigma,k}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}{\tt w}_{\sigma,k}){\tt w}_{\sigma,k}-\sigma\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}{\tt w}_{\sigma,k}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(-\partial^{2}_{s}w_{\sigma,k}-\frac{N-1}{s}\partial_{s}w_{\sigma,k}-\frac{N-1}{s^{2}}w_{\sigma,k}\right)w_{\sigma,k}s^{N-1}ds-\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{N-1}{s^{2}}w_{\sigma,k}^{2}s^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle+o(1)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{N-1}{s^{2}}w_{\lambda,k}^{2}s^{N-1}ds+o(1)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}{\tt w}_{\sigma,k}^{2}+o(1),

where the terms
o(1)
tend to zero as
k\to\infty
, uniformly in
\sigma
. Therefore, (
5.2
) is satisfied for any
\phi
in the infinite dimensional space

X:={\rm span}\{{\tt w}_{\sigma,k}:\,k\geq k_{0}\}.

Setting

(5.6)

\tilde{\sigma}:=W\big(\frac{2\sqrt{2}a_{\star}}{\varepsilon^{2}\overline{\beta}}\big)>0,

where
\overline{\beta}:=\max\limits_{{\tt p}\in\Sigma}\beta(s({\tt p}))>0
and where
\beta(s)
is defined in (
2.15
). Due to the monotonicity of
W
we have

\sqrt{2}w=W\big(\frac{2\sqrt{2}a_{\star}}{\varepsilon^{2}\beta}\big)\geq W\big(\frac{2\sqrt{2}a_{\star}}{\varepsilon^{2}\overline{\beta}}\big)=\tilde{\sigma}.

Using the function
\chi_{3}
defined in (
4.21
), we consider the
C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
functions given by

v_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\chi_{3}{\tt w}_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}(\varepsilon\cdotp)v^{\prime}_{\star})^{\natural}_{j}.

The idea is that
v_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}
is zero far from the graphs of
{\tt h}_{j}(\varepsilon\cdotp)
over
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
, for
1\leq j\leq k
, and near those graphs it is given by
{\tt w}_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}
in the direction of the graph, by
v^{\prime}_{\star}
in the orthogonal direction.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists
\bar{k}>0
and
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
and
k>\bar{k}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|\nabla v_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}|^{2}+(3u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1)v_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}^{2}<-c\varepsilon^{2-N}<0.

Remark 5.1
.

Lemma
5.1
yields that, for
\varepsilon(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
, the Morse index of
u_{\varepsilon}
is infinite. In fact the functions
{\tt w}_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}
can be approximated by functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
.

Proof.

Setting
\phi:={\tt w}_{k,\sigma}
and using the expansion of the Laplacian in Fermi coordinates, in
X_{\varepsilon,{\tt h}_{j}}^{-1}(\mathcal{N}_{j,\varepsilon})
we compute

(5.7)

\displaystyle\Delta(\phi v^{\prime}_{\star})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{\Sigma}\phi v^{\prime}_{\star}+\phi v^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\star}-\varepsilon^{2}J_{\Sigma}\phi{\tt h}_{j}v^{\prime}_{\star}-\varepsilon^{2}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi tv^{\prime\prime}_{\star}-2\varepsilon\nabla_{\Sigma}{\tt h}_{j}\cdotp\nabla_{\Sigma}\phi v^{\prime\prime}_{\star}+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\tt h}_{1}|^{2}\phi v^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\star}

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}\phi v^{\prime\prime}_{\star}+\varepsilon^{2}({\tt a}^{ik}\phi_{ik}+{\tt b}^{i}\phi_{i})v^{\prime}_{\star}-\varepsilon^{2}({\tt a}^{ik}{{\tt h}_{1}}_{ik}+{\tt b}^{i}{{\tt h}_{1}}_{i})\phi v^{\prime\prime}_{\star}-2\varepsilon{\tt a}^{ik}\phi_{i}{{\tt h}_{1}}_{k}v^{\prime\prime}_{\star}+\varepsilon^{2}{\tt a}^{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\tt h}_{1}|^{2}\phi v^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\star}

and

(5.8)

\displaystyle(1-3U_{1}^{2})w^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle=(1-3w_{j}^{2})w^{\prime}_{j}-6v_{\star}v^{\prime}_{j}\bigg((2e^{-\sqrt{2}t}-\psi_{0}(-t))e^{-\sqrt{2}({\tt h}_{j}-{\tt h}_{j-1})}

\displaystyle+(-2e^{-\sqrt{2}t}+\psi_{0}(t))e^{-\sqrt{2}({\tt h}_{j+1}-{\tt h}_{j})}+\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\tt h}_{j}|^{2}\psi_{1}(t)+\varepsilon^{2}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\psi_{2}(t)\bigg)

\displaystyle+O(\varepsilon^{2+\gamma}e^{-\varrho|t|}(1+s)^{-(2+\gamma)})\chi_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}.

Moreover, setting
v_{j}(t):=w_{j}(t+{\tt h}_{j+1}-{\tt h}_{j})
and
u_{j}(t):=w_{j}(t+{\tt h}_{j}-{\tt h}_{j-1})
, we have

(1-3U_{1}^{2})v_{j}=(1-3v_{j}^{2})v^{\prime}_{j}+6\sqrt{2}(1-v_{\star}^{2})e^{\sqrt{2}t}e^{-\sqrt{2}({\tt h}_{j+1}-{\tt h}_{j})}+O(\varepsilon^{2+\gamma}e^{-\varrho|t|}(1+s)^{-(2+\gamma)})\chi_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}

for some
\varrho>0
, and

(1-3U_{1}^{2})u_{j}=(1-3u_{j}^{2})u^{\prime}_{j}+6\sqrt{2}(1-v_{\star}^{2})e^{-\sqrt{2}t}e^{-\sqrt{2}({\tt h}_{j}-{\tt h}_{j-1})}+O(\varepsilon^{2+\gamma}e^{-\varrho|t|}(1+s)^{-(2+\gamma)})\chi_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}.

Differentiating the equations satisfied by
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{2}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
we can see that

\displaystyle 6\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{2}v_{\star}v^{\prime}_{\star}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v^{\prime}_{\star})^{2}=-\frac{c_{\star}}{2}

\displaystyle 6\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\psi_{0}-2e^{\sqrt{2}t})v^{\prime}_{\star}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}6(1-v_{\star}^{2})e^{\sqrt{2}t}v^{\prime}_{\star}=\sqrt{2}a_{\star}c_{\star}

so that, multiplying by
v^{\prime}_{j}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(-\Delta(\phi v^{\prime}_{\star})+(3U_{1}^{2}-1)\phi v^{\prime}_{\star}\right)v^{\prime}_{\star}dt

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon^{2}c_{\star}(\Delta_{\Sigma}\phi+|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi)(\varepsilon\cdotp)

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{2}a_{0}c_{\star}e^{-\sqrt{2}({\tt h}_{j-1}(\varepsilon\cdotp)-{\tt h}_{j}(\varepsilon\cdotp))}\phi(\varepsilon\cdotp)+O(\varepsilon^{2+\gamma}(1+s)^{-(2+\gamma)})\chi_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}.

In conclusion, multiplying by
v:=v_{k,\tilde{\sigma}}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\left(-\Delta v+(3u_{\varepsilon}^{2}-1)v\right)vd\xi

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon^{2}c_{\star}\left(m\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}J_{\Sigma}\phi(\varepsilon\cdotp)\phi(\varepsilon\cdotp)+4\sqrt{2}a_{\star}(m-1)\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}{\tt w}(\varepsilon\cdotp)\phi^{2}(\varepsilon\cdotp)\right)(1+O(\varepsilon^{\gamma}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2-N}c_{\star}\left(m\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla_{\Sigma}\phi|^{2}-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi^{2}\right)-4\sqrt{2}a_{\star}(m-1)\int_{\Sigma}{\tt w}\phi^{2}\right)(1+O(\varepsilon^{\gamma}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2-N}\mathcal{Q}_{\tilde{\sigma}}(\phi)(1+O(\varepsilon^{\gamma}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-C\varepsilon^{2-N}\left(\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\phi^{2}\right)(1+O(\varepsilon^{\gamma}))<0

where

c_{\star}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v^{\prime}_{\star})^{2}.

This completes the proof of
{\rm iv.}
in Theorem
1.1
and thus its proof.

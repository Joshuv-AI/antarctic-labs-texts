Abstract
. In this paper various analytic techniques are combined in order to study the average of a product of a Hecke L L -function and a symmetric square L L -function at the central point in the weight aspect. The evaluation of the second main term relies on the theory of Maaß forms of half-integral weight and the Rankin-Selberg method. The error terms are bounded using the Liouville-Green approximation.

1.
Introduction

The asymptotic evaluation of moments of
L
-functions is not only
an important tool to solve problems in number theory and arithmetic
geometry, but is also a subject of independent interest. Various
conjectures predict the shape of the main terms for all moments of

L
-functions within a family of certain type of symmetry (see e.g.
[
6
,
8
]
). Even though exact results for moments are till
now known only for small values this is already sufficient for many
applications.
See, for example,
[
13
]
.
In this regard, the quality of the asymptotic error estimates plays
a crucial role.

In general, there are three main techniques used for determining the
moments: we can apply the approximate functional equation, the
Rankin-Selberg method or the method of analytic continuation, each of
which has certain advantages. The method of the approximate functional
equation which allows the bypassing of convergence problems
is the most common approach. The method of analytic continuation and
the Rankin-Selberg method however reveal the structure of the mean
values and yield exact formulas for the moments. Consequently, the
theory of special functions can be used in order to prove sharp error
estimates.

The problem we consider here requires the combination of all three
methods. We study the asymptotic
behaviour of the first moment of the product of the Hecke

L
-function
L(f,1/2)
and the symmetric square
L
-function

L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2)
in the weight aspect on average, where
f

runs over the space
H_{4k}
of primitive forms of weight
4k
,

k\in\mathbf{N}
.

The most interesting phenomenon of this moment is the presence of the
non-diagonal main term, which is smaller in size and depends on the
special value of the double Dirichlet series

(1.1)

L^{-}_{g}(s)=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{n<0}\frac{\mathscr{L}_{4n}(1/2)}{|n|^{s+1/2}},

(1.2)

\mathscr{L}_{n}(s)=\frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{s}}\left(\sum_{1\leq t\leq 2q;\,t^{2}\equiv n\ (\textup{mod}\ 4q)}1\right).

Isolating this non-diagonal main term and applying the Liouville-Green
method for error estimates, we prove an asymptotic formula with an
arbitrary power saving error term.

Before stating the main theorem
we introduce some notation. Let
\langle f,f\rangle_{1}
be the
Petersson inner product on the space of level
1
holomorphic
modular forms and the standard harmonic weight we denote by

(1.3)

\omega(f):=\frac{\Gamma(2k-1)}{(4\pi)^{2k-1}\langle f,f\rangle_{1}}.

Then we have the following asymptotic formula.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{+})
be a non-negative, compactly supported
function on the interval
[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]
,
\theta_{2}>\theta_{1}>0
, and

(1.4)

\|h^{(n)}\|_{1}\ll 1\text{ for all }n\geq 0.

Then, for any fixed
A>0
, we have

(1.5)

\begin{split}\sum_{k}&h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in H_{4k}}\omega(f)L(f,1/2)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2)=\\
&\frac{HK}{4}\zeta(3/2)\Biggl(2\log{K}-3\log{2\pi}-2\log{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}+3\gamma+\frac{2\zeta^{\prime}(3/2)}{\zeta(3/2)}+\frac{2H_{1}}{H}\Biggr)\\
&+P_{h}(1/K)+L^{-}_{g}(1/4)\sqrt{K}Q_{h}(1/K)+O\left(K^{-A}\right),\end{split}

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function and
\gamma
denotes
the Euler constant. Furthermore,

H:=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(y)dy,\quad H_{1}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(y)\log{y}dy

and
P_{h}(x)
,
Q_{h}(x)
are polynomials in
x
of degree
A-1
and
A
,
respectively, with coefficients depending on the function
h
.

This is an example of mixed moments that were previously investigated, for example, in

[
16
,
19
,
20
,
24
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
consists of several steps. We start
by combining the exact formula for the twisted first moment of symmetric
square
L
-functions and the approximate functional equation for the Hecke

L
-function.
Consequently, the average

\sum_{f\in H_{4k}}\omega(f)L(f,1/2)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2)

splits into a diagonal main term, a non-diagonal main term plus a smaller
contribution expressed in terms of the special functions

(1.6)

\Psi_{k}(x):=x^{k}\frac{\Gamma(k-1/4)\Gamma(k+1/4)}{\Gamma(2k)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(k-\frac{1}{4},k+\frac{1}{4};2k;x\right),

(1.7)

\Phi_{k}(x):=\frac{\Gamma(k-1/4)\Gamma(3/4-k)}{\Gamma(1/2)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(k-\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}-k;1/2;x\right),

where
\Gamma(s)
is the Gamma function and
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;x)
is
the Gauss hypergeometric function.

The diagonal main term is evaluated in Corollary
4.3
. The
Rankin-Selberg method serves to isolate the non-diagonal main term, as
shown in Corollary
5.2
.
Lemma
6.1
provides an estimate for the error term of the
first type.
Using the Liouville-Green method, we approximate the error term of the
second type by the series in
Y_{0}
and
J_{0}
Bessel functions, average
the result over
k
and, as a consequence, prove estimate (
6.8
).

To sum up, we obtain the full asymptotic expansion with an arbitrary power
saving error term. This improves the results of
[
3
]
, where an asymptotic
formula for

(1.8)

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in H_{4k}}\omega(f)L(f,1/2)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2)

was proved with a saving in the logarithmic term of the error using
standard methods based on the approximate functional equation and

[
11
, Lemma 5.8]
.

We note that a similar approach allowed Khan
[
15
]
to evaluate
the second moment

(1.9)

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{2k}{K}\right)\sum_{f\in H_{2k}}\omega(f)L^{2}(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2)

with a good power saving error term, namely
O(K^{\epsilon})
for any

\epsilon>0
. This fact can be easily explained by the exact formula
(see
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
) for the twisted first moment of symmetric
square
L
-functions associated to
H_{2k}
. The off-diagonal term in this
formula is

(1.10)

(-1)^{k}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2l^{1/2}}\frac{\Gamma(k-1/4)}{\Gamma(k+1/4)}\mathscr{L}_{-4l^{2}}(1/2).

To obtain asymptotics for (
1.9
), it is required to average
(
1.10
) over all values of
k
. Consequently, the contribution of
this term is rather small due to the oscillating multiple
(-1)^{k}
. In the
case of (
1.8
), the summation is taken only over even values of

k
because
L(f,1/2)
is identically zero for
f\in H_{2k}
with odd
k
.
As a result, the oscillating multiple
(-1)^{k}
disappears and the summand
corresponding to (
1.10
) becomes the second main term.

Note that the absolute value estimate for the non-diagonal main term in
(
1.8
) is
K^{3/4+\theta}
, where
\theta
denotes the subconvexity
exponent for the Dirichlet series (
1.2
).
Taking the convexity bound
\theta=1/4
, we recover the asymptotic result
of
[
3
]
.
Using the best subconvexity result due to Conrey and Iwaniec
[
7
]
gives
a power saving bound
K^{11/12+\epsilon}
. The Rankin-Selberg method allows
removing the dependence on
\theta
and improving the estimate to
K^{3/4}
.
We finally observe that there are some cancellations between the diagonal
and the non-diagonal main terms. This can be shown by direct computations
but the most convenient way is to choose a suitable form of the approximate
functional equation for
L(f,s)
. Accordingly, we obtain the second main
term of size
K^{1/2}
.

In the level aspect similar results for the Hecke congruence subgroup of
prime level
q
were obtained by Munshi and Sengupta
[
19
]
with the
error estimate
O(q^{-1/8})
. The authors of
[
19
]
isolate only the
diagonal main term. Our expectation is that there is a second main term
of size
q^{-1/2}
.

We remark that the additional average over
k
in
(
1.5
) is only required to estimate the error term
of the second type, which involves the highly oscillatory special function

\Phi_{k}(x)
. It might be possible to smooth out the oscillations of

\Phi_{k}(x)
by using instead the average over
l
in (
6.2
),
and, hence, prove an asymptotic formula for

(1.11)

\sum_{f\in H_{4k}}\omega(f)L(f,1/2)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2).

A consequence of this result is the simultaneous non-vanishing of the
corresponding
L
-functions.

Finally, the methods of the present work shed some light on the structure of the second moment of symmetric square
L
-functions. In the level aspect the upper bound

(1.12)

\sum_{f\in H_{2k}(N)}L^{2}(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2)\ll N^{1+\epsilon}\text{ for any }\epsilon>0\text{ and fixed }k,

was proved by Iwaniec and Michel
[
12
]
in 2001. In the weight aspect this is one of the most challenging unsolved problems in the theory of
L
-functions. It is believed that finding asymptotic formula or even an upper bound for

(1.13)

\sum_{f\in H_{2k}}\omega(f)L^{2}(sym^{2}f,1/2)\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty,

is out of reach by standard tools.
Our results suggest the following approach. Combining exact formula (
3.15
) and the approximate functional equation for
L(sym^{2}f,1/2)
, one can isolate the diagonal and the off-diagonal main terms in (
1.13
). The main difference with
(
1.11
) is the presence of the off-off-diagonal main terms in sums (
6.1
) and (
6.2
). Technically, this can be explained as follows.
The approximate functional equation for symmetric square
L
-functions is longer and, therefore, the required range includes a transition point at which special functions
\Phi_{k}(x)
and
\Psi_{k}(x)
change their behaviour. This produces the third off-off-diagonal main term and creates additional technical difficulties in estimating error terms.

2.
Main tools

2.1.
Theorem of Müller

In this section we keep the notations of
[
18
]
. If
\Gamma
is
a Fuchsian group of the first kind, we denote by
\mathscr{F}(\Gamma,\chi,\kappa,\lambda)
the space of all non-holomorphic automorphic
forms of real weight
\kappa
, multiplier system
\chi
and
Laplace eigenvalue
\lambda=1/2-\rho^{2}
,
\Re{\rho}\geq 0
.
For our application later we will only need the case that
\kappa=1/2
and
\rho=0
. If
\infty
is a cusp of
\Gamma
,
the Fourier-Whittaker expansion of the function
f\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma,\chi,\kappa,\lambda)
at
\infty
is given by

\begin{split}f(W_{\infty}z)&=A_{\infty,0}(y)+\\
&\quad\sum_{n\neq 0}a_{\infty,n}W_{(\operatorname{sgn}{n})\frac{\kappa}{2},\rho}(4\pi|n+\mu_{\infty}|y)\exp(2\pi i(n+\mu_{\infty})x).\end{split}

Here
W_{\infty}
is the width of the cusp
\infty
,
\mu_{\infty}

is the cusp parameter and
W_{(\operatorname{sgn}{n})\frac{\kappa}{2},\rho}
denotes
the Whittaker function. Furthermore, the constant term of the Fourier
expression has the following form:

(2.1)

A_{\infty,0}(y)=\begin{cases}0,&\mu_{\infty}\neq 0\\
a_{\infty,0}y^{1/2+\rho}+b_{\infty,0}y^{1/2+\rho},&\mu_{\infty}=0,\ \rho\neq 0\\
a_{\infty,0}y^{1/2}+b_{\infty,0}y^{1/2}\log{y},&\mu_{\infty}=0,\ \rho=0.\end{cases}

Let
\Gamma
and
\widehat{\Gamma}
be two Fuchsian groups of the
first kind such that
\infty
is a cusp of both groups. If the
Fourier-Whittaker expansions of the functions
f\in\mathscr{F}(\Gamma,\chi,\kappa,\lambda)
and
g\in\mathscr{F}(\widehat{\Gamma},\widehat{\chi},\kappa,\lambda)
at
\infty

are given by

\begin{split}f(W_{\infty}z)&=A_{\infty,0}(y)+\\
&\quad\sum_{n\neq 0}a_{\infty,n}W_{(\operatorname{sgn}{n})\frac{\kappa}{2},\rho}(4\pi|n+\mu_{\infty}|y)\exp(2\pi i(n+\mu_{\infty})x)\\
\intertext{and}\\
g(\widehat{W}_{\infty}z)&=\widehat{A}_{\infty,0}(y)+\\
&\quad\sum_{n\neq 0}\widehat{a}_{\infty,n}W_{(\operatorname{sgn}{n})\frac{\kappa}{2},\rho}(4\pi|n+\widehat{\mu}_{\infty}|y)\exp(2\pi i(n+\widehat{\mu}_{\infty})x),\end{split}

the associated Dirichlet series

(2.2)

L^{+}(f,s)=\sum_{n>0}\frac{a_{\infty,n}}{(n+\mu_{\infty})^{s}},\quad L^{-}(f,s)=\sum_{n<0}\frac{a_{\infty,n}}{|n+\mu_{\infty}|^{s}}

are absolutely convergent for
\Re{s}>\Re{\rho}+1/2
.
For a function
f
on the upper half-plane
\mathbf{H}
and
\kappa\in\mathbf{R}
the slash operator is defined by

(2.3)

(f\big|_{\kappa}M)(z)=\left(\frac{cz+d}{|cz+d|}\right)^{-\kappa}f(Mz),\quad M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{R}).

We prove the following modification of
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
, which is
required for our application.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\rho=0
. Suppose that there are constants
C\in\mathbf{C}

and
\gamma\in\mathbf{R}^{+}
such that for all
z\in\mathbf{H}
we have

(2.4)

\exp(\pi i\kappa/2)(f\big|_{\kappa}J)(z)=Cg(\gamma z),\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then the Dirichlet series
L^{\pm}(f,s)
and
L^{\pm}(g,s)
have a
meromorphic continuation to the entire complex plane. Furthermore,
they have only one pole at
s=1/2
. If
\kappa=-(1+2n)
,

n\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
, then the pole is simple. In all other cases

L^{\pm}(f,s)
and
L^{\pm}(g,s)
have a pole of order
2

at
s=1/2
.

Moreover, the Dirichlet series
L^{\pm}(f,s)
and
L^{\pm}(g,s)

satisfy the functional equations

(2.5)

L^{\pm}(f,s)=C(2\pi\delta)^{2s}\Gamma^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-s\right)\\
\times\Biggl(\frac{L^{\pm}(g,-s)}{\pi}\sin{\pi\left(s\pm\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)}+\frac{L^{\mp}(g,-s)}{\Gamma^{2}(\pm\kappa/2+1/2)}\Biggr),

where

\delta=\sqrt{\gamma/(W_{\infty}\widehat{W}_{\infty})}
.

Remark
.

In the original theorem of Müller (see
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
) it
was stated that for
\rho=0
the functions
L^{\pm}(f,s)
have
poles of order one at
s=1/2
,
which turns out to be true only for certain values of
\kappa
.

Proof.

Equation (
2.5
) is a direct result of
[
18
, Eq. 44]
if
we set
\rho=0
.
We now prove the meromorphic continuation of
L^{+}(f,s)
. Let

(2.6)

\Gamma_{\alpha,\rho}(s):=2^{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(s+\rho+1/2)\Gamma(s-\rho+1/2)}{\Gamma(s+1-\alpha)}\\
\times{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1/2+\rho-\alpha,1/2-\rho-\alpha;s+1-\alpha;1/2\right).

First we note that by
[
18
, Eq. 30, 48]

(2.7)

L^{+}(f,s)=\frac{(2\pi)^{s}}{2\Gamma^{2}(s+1/2)}\left(\Gamma_{1-\kappa/2,0}(s)M(f,s)-\Gamma_{-\kappa/2,0}(s)M(E_{\kappa}f,s)\right).

Here we used the Maaß lowering operator

E_{\kappa}:\mathscr{F}(\Gamma,\chi,\kappa,\lambda)\rightarrow\mathscr{F}(\Gamma,\chi,

\kappa-2,\lambda)

which is defined by

(2.8)

E_{\kappa}=y\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)+\frac{\kappa}{2}.

According to
[
18
, Eq. 52]
we have

(2.9)

\begin{split}M(f,s)&=C\delta\frac{\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}}{(s-1/2)^{2}}+C\delta\frac{\widehat{a}_{\infty,0}+2\log{\delta}\,\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}}{s-1/2}\\
&\quad+\frac{b_{\infty,0}}{(s+1/2)^{2}}-\frac{a_{\infty,0}}{s+1/2}+\text{entire function}.\end{split}

Furthermore, using
[
18
, Eq. 53]
we obtain

\begin{split}M(E_{\kappa}f,s)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(E_{\kappa}f(W_{\infty}yi)-E_{\kappa}A_{\infty,0}(y)\right)y^{s-1}dy\\
&=-C\delta^{2s}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(E_{\kappa}g(\widehat{W}_{\infty}yi)-E_{\kappa}\widehat{A}_{\infty,0}(y)\right)y^{-s-1}dy\\
&\quad+\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\left(E_{\kappa}f(W_{\infty}yi)-E_{\kappa}A_{\infty,0}(y)\right)y^{s-1}dy\\
&\quad-C\delta^{2s}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}E_{\kappa}\widehat{A}_{\infty,0}(y)y^{-s-1}dy-\int_{0}^{\delta}E_{\kappa}A_{\infty,0}(y)y^{s-1}dy.\end{split}

The first two integrals are entire functions.
Applying the Maaß lowering operator defined by (
2.8
), we get

E_{\kappa}A_{\infty,0}(y)=a_{\infty,0}\frac{\kappa-1}{2}y^{1/2}+b_{\infty,0}\frac{\kappa-1}{2}y^{1/2}\log{y}-b_{\infty,0}y^{1/2}.

This implies that

(2.10)

\begin{split}M(E_{\kappa}f,s)&=-\frac{C\delta}{(s-1/2)^{2}}\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}\frac{\kappa-1}{2}+\frac{1}{(s+1/2)^{2}}b_{\infty,0}\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{C\delta}{s-1/2}\left(\widehat{a}_{\infty,0}\frac{\kappa-1}{2}+\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}(\kappa-1)\log{\delta}-\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}\right)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{s+1/2}\left(a_{\infty,0}\frac{\kappa-1}{2}-b_{\infty,0}\right)+\text{entire function}.\end{split}

In order to determine the analytic behaviour of
L^{+}(f,s)
we
analyze the function

F(s,\kappa):=\frac{1}{\Gamma^{2}(s+\frac{1}{2})}\left(\Gamma_{1-\kappa/2,0}(s)+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\Gamma_{-\kappa/2,0}(s)\right).

Using
[
18
, Eq. 22]
and
[
10
, Eq. 9.137.11]
this expression can
be simplified to

(2.11)

\begin{split}F(s,\kappa)&=\frac{2^{1-\kappa/2}}{\Gamma(s+\frac{\kappa}{2})}\Bigg({}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2},\frac{\kappa-1}{2};s+\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\frac{1}{2(s+\frac{\kappa}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2}+1,\frac{\kappa-1}{2}+1;s+\frac{\kappa}{2}+1;\frac{1}{2}\right)\Bigg)\\
&=\frac{2^{1-\kappa/2}}{\Gamma(s+\frac{\kappa}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2},\frac{\kappa+1}{2};s+\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\end{split}

Thus equations (
2.7
), (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and

[
18
, Eq. 22]
imply

\begin{split}\lim_{s\rightarrow 1/2}\Big(s-\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}L^{+}(f,s)&=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1/2}\frac{(2\pi)^{s}C\delta\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}}{2}F(s,\kappa)\\
&=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1/2}\frac{(2\pi)^{s}C\delta\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}}{2^{\kappa/2}\Gamma(s+\frac{\kappa}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2},\frac{\kappa+1}{2};s+\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{(2\pi)^{1/2}C\delta\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}}{2^{\kappa/2}\Gamma(\frac{1+\kappa}{2})}{}_{1}F_{0}\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{(2\pi)^{1/2}C\delta\widehat{b}_{\infty,0}}{\sqrt{2}\Gamma(\frac{1+\kappa}{2})}.\end{split}

Therefore, the double pole at
s=1/2
gets cancelled if and only if

1+\kappa=-2n
,

n\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
,
and consequently, the function
L^{+}(f,s)
has a double pole at

for
\kappa\neq-1,-3,-5,-7,\ldots

For understanding the behaviour of
L^{+}(f,s)
at
-1/2

Using
[
10

(2.12)

\begin{split}G(s,\kappa)&=\frac{1}{2^{\kappa/2}\Gamma(s+1+\frac{\kappa}{2})}\Bigg(2\left(s+\frac{\kappa}{2}\right){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2},\frac{\kappa-1}{2};s+\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\

This implies

\begin{split}\lim_{s\rightarrow-1/2}&\Big(s+\frac{1}{2}\Big)L^{+}(f,s)\\

&=\frac{(2\pi)^{-1/2}b_{\infty,0}}{2^{1+\kappa/2}\Gamma(\frac{1+\kappa}{2})}\Bigg(2^{\frac{\kappa+1}{2}}-2^{\frac{\kappa+1}{2}}\Bigg)\\
&=0\end{split}

and therefore the function

can be analytically continued at

In order to prove the meromorphic continuation of
L^{-}(f,s)
we remark

\begin{split}\frac{2\Gamma^{2}(s+1/2)}{(2\pi)^{s}}&L^{-}(f,s)=\\
&\left(\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\right)^{2}\Gamma_{\kappa/2-1,0}(s)M(f,s)+\Gamma_{\kappa/2,0}(s)M(E_{\kappa}f,s).\end{split}

Using the same approach as before we see that the limit

\begin{split}\lim_{s\rightarrow 1/2}\frac{\kappa-1}{\Gamma\left(s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)}&\Bigg(\frac{1-\kappa}{2}\frac{1}{2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3-\kappa}{2},\frac{3-\kappa}{2};s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&+\left(s+1-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2},\frac{1-\kappa}{2};s+1-\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\Bigg)\end{split}

determines the behaviour of
L^{-}(s,f)
at
s=1/2
. By

[
10
, Eq. 9.137.11]
this limit is equal to

\begin{split}(1-\kappa)\lim_{s\rightarrow 1/2}\frac{s+1-\frac{\kappa}{2}}{\Gamma(s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2},\frac{3-\kappa}{2};s+1-\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{2^{\frac{k+1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2}\right)}.\end{split}

Thus the double pole at
s=1/2
is cancelled if and only if

1-\kappa=-2n,\quad n\in\mathbf{N}_{0},

which is true in case of odd positive integral weight, namely for

\kappa=1,3,5,7,\ldots.

Applying
[
10
, Eq. 9.137.7]
we see infer that

\begin{split}\lim_{s\rightarrow-1/2}&(s+1/2)L^{-}(f,s)=\\
\lim_{s\rightarrow-1/2}&\frac{(2\pi)^{s}2^{\kappa/2-3}(\kappa-1)}{\Gamma(s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2})}\Bigg(\frac{\kappa-1}{2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3-\kappa}{2},\frac{3-\kappa}{2};s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&+2(s+1-\kappa/2){}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2},\frac{1-\kappa}{2};s+1-\frac{\kappa}{2};1/2\right)\Bigg)\frac{b_{\infty,0}}{s+1/2}\\
&+\frac{2^{\kappa/2-1}b_{\infty,0}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}\Gamma(\frac{1-\kappa}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2},\frac{1-\kappa}{2};\frac{1-\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&=\lim_{s\rightarrow-1/2}\frac{(2\pi)^{s}2^{\kappa/2-2}(\kappa-1)b_{\infty,0}}{\Gamma(s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2},\frac{3-\kappa}{2};s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2};\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&\qquad+\frac{2^{\kappa-3/2}b_{\infty,0}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}\Gamma(\frac{1-\kappa}{2})}.\end{split}

Since

\lim_{s\rightarrow-1/2}\frac{(\kappa-1)}{\Gamma(s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2})}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2},\frac{3-\kappa}{2};s+2-\frac{\kappa}{2};1/2\right)=-\frac{2^{\frac{\kappa+1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\kappa}{2}\right)}

this implies that
L^{-}(f,s)
can be analytically continued in
s=-1/2
.

According to
[
18
, Eq. 54]
we have

(2.13)

M(g,s)=M(f,-s)\frac{\delta^{2s}}{C},\quad M(E_{\kappa}g,s)=M(E_{\kappa}f,-s)\frac{\delta^{2s}}{C}.

Then applying
[
18
, Eq. 30, 48, 49, 54]
we get

\frac{2\Gamma^{2}(s+1/2)}{(2\pi)^{s}}L^{+}(g,s)=\frac{\delta^{2s}}{C}\biggl(\Gamma_{1-\kappa/2,0}(s)M(f,-s)\\
+\Gamma_{-\kappa/2,0}(s)M(E_{\kappa}f,-s)\biggr)

and

\frac{2\Gamma^{2}(s+1/2)}{(2\pi)^{s}}L^{-}(g,s)=\frac{\delta^{2s}}{C}\biggl(\frac{(\kappa-1)^{2}}{4}\Gamma_{\kappa/2-1,0}(s)M(f,-s)\\
-\Gamma_{\kappa/2,0}(s)M(E_{\kappa}f,-s)\biggr).

Similarly to the case of
L^{\pm}(f,s)
it follows that the functions

L^{\pm}(g,s)
have a double pole at

unless
\kappa
is
an odd negative integer.
∎

2.3.
Eisenstein series of weight
1/2
and level
4

The connection between quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions and Fourier
coefficients of half-integral weight forms was first discovered by

17

. Similar results were obtained by Shimura

[
25
]
, Shintani
[
26
]
, Cohen
[
5

[

We study the more general Dirichlet series (see
[
4

(2.22)

\mathscr{L}_{n}(s)=\frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{s}}\left(\sum_{1\leq t\leq 2q;t^{2}\equiv n\ (\textup{mod}\ 4q)}1\right).

Only if
n\equiv 0,1\ (\textup{mod}\ 4)
the function
\mathscr{L}_{n}(s)

considered as a function of

does not vanish.
We can think of
\mathscr{L}_{n}(s)

L

If

For any

\mathscr{L}_{n}(1/2)\ll n^{\theta+\epsilon},

The best known result

[
7

\theta=0
.
The completed
L

\mathscr{L}_{n}^{*}(s)=(\pi/|n|)^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2+1/4-\operatorname{sgn}{n}/4)\mathscr{L}_{n}(s)

satisfies the functional equation

(2.24)

\mathscr{L}_{n}^{*}(s)=\mathscr{L}_{n}^{*}(1-s).

The function
\mathscr{L}_{n}^{*}(s)
appears in the Fourier-Whittaker

and level
4
at the cusps
\infty
and

(2.25)

where for a cusp
\alpha

is defined
in
[
22

.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\begin{split}&E^{(1/2)}_{(0;\infty)}(z;s)=\zeta(4s-1)y^{s}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2s-1)\zeta(4s-2)}{4^{2s-1}\Gamma(2s-1/2)}y^{1-s}+\frac{\pi^{2s-3/4}}{4^{s}\Gamma(2s-1/2)}\\
&\quad\phantom{E^{(1/2)}_{(0;\infty)}(z;s)=}\times\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n}^{*}(2s-1/2)}{|n|^{3/4}}W_{\operatorname{sgn}{n}/4,s-1/2}\left(4\pi|n|y\right)\exp{(2\pi inx)}.\end{split}

Proof.

The Fourier-Whittaker expansion for
E_{\infty}(z;s;1/2)
is given in
Section
3.1
of
[
22
]
. Computations for

∎

Computing the limit as

E^{(1/2)}_{(0;\infty)}(z;1/2)=\frac{1}{2}y^{1/2}\log{y}+(\gamma-\log{4\pi})y^{1/2}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\times\\
\times\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n}(1/2)}{|n|^{1/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\operatorname{sgn}{n}}{4}\right)W_{\operatorname{sgn}{n}/4,0}\left(4\pi|n|y\right)\exp{(2\pi inx)}.

and

3.
Preliminary evaluation

Let
\mathbf{H}
be the Poincare upper half-plane and denote by
H_{2k}

the normalized Hecke basis for the space of holomorphic cusp forms
of even weight
2k\geq 2
with respect to the full modular group.
If the function
f\in H_{2k}
has the Fourier expansion

(3.1)

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{k-1/2}\exp(2\pi inz),\quad\lambda_{f}(1)=1,

the associated Hecke
L
-function is defined by

(3.2)

L(f,s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},\quad\Re{s}>1.

Let
\Gamma(s)
be the Gamma function. Then the completed

L
-function

(3.3)

\Lambda(f,s)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Gamma\left(s+\frac{2k-1}{2}\right)L(f,s)

satisfies the functional equation

(3.4)

\Lambda(f,s)=\epsilon_{f}\Lambda(f,1-s),\quad\epsilon_{f}=i^{2k},

and can be analytically continued to the entire complex plane. Note
that by equation (
3.4
) we have
L_{f}(1/2)=0

for odd
k
.

For
\Re{s}>1
the symmetric square
L
-function is defined by

(3.5)

L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,s)=\zeta(2s)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n^{2})}{n^{s}}.

Let

(3.6)

L_{\infty}(s):=\pi^{-3s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-1}{2}+k\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}+k\right).

The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,s):=L_{\infty}(s)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,s)

is entire and satisfies the functional equation

(3.7)

\Lambda(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,s)=\Lambda(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1-s).

In this section we combine the techniques of analytic continuation
and the approximate functional equation in order to express the
average

(3.8)

\sum_{f\in H_{4k}}\omega(f)L(f,1/2)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2)

as a sum of three parts.

First, we use the exact formula for the twisted moment of symmetric
square
L
-functions.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have

\sum_{f\in H_{4k}}\omega(f)\lambda_{f}(l)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2+u)=M^{D}(u,l)\delta_{l=\Box}+M^{ND}(u,l)+ET(u,l),

where

(3.9)

\delta_{l=\Box}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }l\text{ is a full square,}\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3.10)

M^{D}(u,l^{2})=\frac{\zeta(1+2u)}{l^{1/2+u}}+\sqrt{2}(2\pi)^{3u}\cos{\pi(1/4+u/2)}\times\\
\frac{\zeta(1-2u)}{l^{1/2-u}}\frac{\Gamma(2k-1/4-u/2)\Gamma(2k+1/4-u/2)\Gamma(1-2u)}{\Gamma(2k+1/4+u/2)\Gamma(2k-1/4+u/2)\Gamma(1-u)},

(3.11)

M^{ND}(0,l)=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2l^{1/4}}\frac{\Gamma(2k-1/4)}{\Gamma(2k+1/4)}\mathscr{L}_{-4l}(1/2),

(3.12)

ET(0,l)=\frac{1}{l^{1/4}}\sum_{1\leq n<2\sqrt{l}}\mathscr{L}_{n^{2}-4l}(1/2)\Phi_{2k}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4l}\right)+\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}l^{1/2}}\sum_{n>2\sqrt{l}}\mathscr{L}_{n^{2}-4l}(1/2)\sqrt{n}\Psi_{2k}\left(\frac{4l}{n^{2}}\right).

Proof.

See
[
2
, Eq. 2.9, 5.6]
.
∎

Remark
.

The role of the shift
u
is to simplify the evaluation of the diagonal
main term in Section
4
.

Second, we obtain an approximate functional equation for the Hecke

L
-function at the central point.
Let

g(s,u):=\frac{\left(s^{2}-(-1/4-u/2)^{2}\right)\left(s^{2}-(-1/4+u/2)^{2}\right)}{(1/4+u/2)^{2}(1/4-u/2)^{2}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

(3.13)

L(f,1/2)=2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(l)}{\sqrt{l}}V_{k}(l,u)

where

(3.14)

V_{k}(l,u)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}g(s,u)\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}\frac{ds}{(2\pi l)^{s}s},\ \sigma>0.

Proof.

Consider

I(s)=\int_{(\sigma)}g(s,u)\Lambda(f,1/2+s)\frac{ds}{s},\quad\sigma>0.

Moving the contour of integration from
\sigma
to
-\sigma
we pick
up a simple pole at
s=0
, obtaining

2I(s)=\Lambda(f,1/2).

The assertion follows.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma

we

(3.15)

\sum_{f\in H_{4k}}\omega(f)L(f,1/2)L(\operatorname{sym}^{2}f,1/2+u)=2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,u)}{\sqrt{l}}M^{D}(u,l)\delta_{l=\Box}\\

4.
Diagonal main term

In this section we evaluate asymptotically the diagonal term in
(
3.15
), namely

M^{D}:=2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{\sqrt{l}}M^{D}(0,l)\delta_{l=\Box}.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
and any real number
0<a<k
we have

(4.1)

2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,u)}{\sqrt{l}}M^{D}(u,l)\delta_{l=\Box}=2\Biggl(\zeta(1+2u)\zeta(3/2+u)+\\
\sqrt{2}(2\pi)^{3u}\cos{\pi(1/4+u/2)}\zeta(1-2u)\zeta(3/2-u)\times\\
\frac{\Gamma(2k-1/4-u/2)}{\Gamma(2k+1/4+u/2)}\frac{\Gamma(2k+1/4-u/2)\Gamma(1-2u)}{\Gamma(2k-1/4+u/2)\Gamma(1-u)}\Biggr)+O(k^{-a+\epsilon}).

Proof.

Consider

2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,u)}{\sqrt{l}}M^{D}(u,l)\delta_{l=\Box}=\frac{2}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}\frac{g(s,u)}{(2\pi)^{s}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{M^{D}(u,l^{2})}{l^{2s+1}}\frac{ds}{s}.

According to (
3.10
) this is equal to

\frac{2}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}g(s,u)\Biggl[\frac{\zeta(1+2u)\zeta(3/2+u+2s)}{2}+\\
\sqrt{2}(2\pi)^{3u}\cos{\pi(1/4+u/2)}\zeta(1-2u)\zeta(3/2-u+2s)\times\\
\frac{\Gamma(2k-1/4-u/2)\Gamma(2k+1/4-u/2)\Gamma(1-2u)}{\Gamma(2k+1/4+u/2)\Gamma(2k-1/4+u/2)\Gamma(1-u)}\Biggr]\frac{ds}{(2\pi)^{s}s}.

The integrand has poles at

s=0,\quad s=-k-j,\quad j=0,1,\ldots

Note that the poles at
s=-1/4\pm u/2
are compensated by the zeros of

g(s,u)
. Crossing the pole at
s=0
, we can move the line of integration
to any real number
-a
such that
0<a<k
.
Consequently, the resulting integral is bounded by
O(k^{-a+\epsilon})
.
The assertion follows by calculating the residue at
s=0
.
∎

Computing the limit as
u\rightarrow 0
, we evaluate the main term at
the central point.

Corollary 4.2
.

For any
\epsilon>0
and any real number
0<a<k
we have

(4.2)

\begin{split}M^{D}=\zeta(3/2)&\Biggl(-3\log{2\pi}+\frac{\pi}{2}+3\gamma+2\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(3/2)}{\zeta(3/2)}\\
&\quad+\psi(2k-1/4)+\psi(2k+1/4)\Biggr)+O(k^{-a+\epsilon}),\end{split}

where
\psi(x)
is the logarithmic derivative of the Gamma function.

Finally, the diagonal main term can be averaged over the weight
k
with
a suitable test function.

Corollary 4.3
.

For any fixed
A>0
we have

(4.3)

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)M^{D}=\frac{HK}{4}\zeta(3/2)\times\\
\Biggl(2\log{K}-3\log{2\pi}-2\log{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}+3\gamma+2\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(3/2)}{\zeta(3/2)}+2\frac{H_{1}}{H}\Biggr)\\
+P_{h}(1/K)+O(K^{-A}),

where

(4.4)

H=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(y)dy,\quad H_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(y)\log{y}dy

and
P_{h}(x)
is a polynomial in
x
of degree
A-1
and with coefficients
depending on the function
h
.

Proof.

This is derived from Corollary
4.2
by using the asymptotic formula

\psi(2k-1/4)+\psi(2k+1/4)=2\log{k}+2\log{2}+P(1/k)+O\left(\frac{1}{k^{A+1}}\right),

where
P(x)
is a polynomial of degree
A
such that
P(0)=0
,
and results of
[
1
, Section 7]
, namely

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)=\frac{HK}{4}+O\left(\frac{1}{K^{b}}\right)

and

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\log{k}=\frac{HK}{4}(\log{K}-\log{4})+\frac{H_{1}K}{4}+O\left(\frac{1}{K^{b}}\right)

for any
b>0
.
∎

5.
Non-diagonal main term

Consider the non-diagonal term

\begin{split}M^{ND}&:=2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{\sqrt{l}}M^{ND}(0,l)\\
&=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma(2k-1/4)}{\Gamma(2k+1/4)}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{l^{3/4}}\mathscr{L}_{-4l}(1/2).\end{split}

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
A>0
we have

(5.1)

M^{ND}=8\sqrt{2\pi}L^{-}_{g}(1/4)\frac{\Gamma(2k-1/4)}{\Gamma(2k+1/4)}+O(k^{-A}).

Proof.

Consider

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{l^{3/4}}\mathscr{L}_{-4l}(1/2)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma_{1})}\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathscr{L}_{-4l}(1/2)}{l^{s+3/4}}\frac{g(s,0)ds}{s(2\pi)^{s}}.

We assume that
\sigma_{1}>1
to justify the change of order of summation
and integration.
Equation (
2.29
) implies that

\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathscr{L}_{-4l}(1/2)}{l^{s+3/4}}=L^{-}_{g}(s+1/4).

According to Theorem
2.4
the function
L^{-}_{g}(s+1/4)

is holomorphic in
\mathbf{C}
except for a double pole at
s=1/4
, which is
compensated by the zeros of
g(s,0)
. Therefore, moving the contour
of integration from
\sigma_{1}
to
\sigma_{2}=-A
for any
A>0
, we cross
only a simple pole at
s=0
. Consequently,

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{l^{3/4}}\mathscr{L}_{-4l}(1/2)=\res_{s=0}F(s)+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-A}F(s)ds,

where

F(s):=8g(s,0)\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}\frac{L^{-}_{g}(s+1/4)}{(2\pi)^{s}s}.

The functional equation (see (
2.31
))

\begin{split}L^{-}_{g}(s+1/4)&=\frac{\pi^{2s+5/2}}{\sqrt{2}\Gamma^{2}(s+3/4)\sin^{2}\pi(s+1/4)}\times\\
&\quad\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma^{2}(1/4)}L^{+}_{f}(-s-1/4)+\frac{\sin\pi s}{\pi}L^{-}_{f}(-s-1/4)\right)\end{split}

and the estimate

\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}\ll\frac{\exp(-\pi|s|/2)}{k^{A}}

imply that

\int_{-A}\frac{\Gamma(k+s)}{\Gamma(k)}L^{-}_{g}(s+1/4)\frac{g(s,0)ds}{(2\pi)^{s}s}\ll k^{-A}.

The residue at the origin is equal to

\res_{s=0}F(s)=8L^{-}_{g}(1/4).

Therefore, for any
A>0
we have

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{l^{3/4}}\mathscr{L}_{-4l}(1/2)=8L^{-}_{g}(1/4)+O(k^{-A}).

The assertion follows.

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For any fixed
A>0
we have

(5.2)

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)M^{ND}=L^{-}_{g}(1/4)\sqrt{K}Q_{h}(1/K)+O(K^{-A}),

where
Q_{h}(x)
is a polynomial in
x
of degree
A
with coefficients depending
on the function
h
.

Proof.

The statement follows from Lemma
5.1
and
[
21
, Eq 5.11.13]
.
∎

6.
Error terms

Finally, we estimate the last term appearing in (
3.15
)
which we split into two terms

ET:=2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{\sqrt{l}}ET(0,l)=ET_{1}+ET_{2}.

By equation (
3.12
) we have

(6.1)

ET_{1}=\sqrt{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{l}\sum_{n>2\sqrt{l}}\mathscr{L}_{n^{2}-4l}(1/2)\sqrt{n}\Psi_{2k}\left(\frac{4l}{n^{2}}\right),

(6.2)

ET_{2}=2\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{l^{3/4}}\sum_{n<2\sqrt{l}}\mathscr{L}_{n^{2}-4l}(1/2)\Phi_{2k}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4l}\right).

Note that if
l>k^{1+\epsilon}
, then

(6.3)

V_{k}(l,0)\ll\frac{1}{k^{A}}\text{ for any }A>0.

Using inequality (
6.3
) we can assume that
l<k^{1+\epsilon}

in (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) at the cost of a negligible error
term.

6.1.
Error term of the first type

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
A>0
we have

(6.4)

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)ET_{1}\ll K^{-A}.

Proof.

It follows from
[
2
, Lemma 7.3]
that

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)ET_{1}\ll\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\sum_{l<k^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}\frac{l^{-1/24}}{\sqrt{k}}\exp\left(-c\frac{k}{l^{1/4}}\right)\ll K^{-A}.

∎

6.2.
Error term of the second type

First, we prove a simple case corresponding to
N=1
in the Liouville-Green
approximation and then we explain how to modify the arguments in order to
sharpen the estimate to
K^{-A}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

We have

(6.5)

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)ET_{2}\ll K^{1/4+\theta}.

Proof.

We decompose the sum over
l
in (
6.2
) into two parts:

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}=\sum_{l\neq\Box}+\sum_{l=\Box}.

Suppose that
l
is not a full square. To approximate the function
\Phi_{2k}

we apply
[
2
, Theorem 6.10]
with

(6.6)

\cos^{2}{\sqrt{\xi}}:=n^{2}/(4l),

namely

\Phi_{2k}(\cos^{2}\sqrt{\xi})=\frac{-\pi}{\xi^{1/4}(\sin{\sqrt{\xi}})^{1/2}}\left[(1+C_{J})Z_{J}(\xi)+C_{Y}Z_{Y}(\xi)\right].

Using
[
2
, Theorem 6.5]
to evaluate
Z_{J}(\xi)
,
Z_{Y}(\xi)
and

[
2
, Corollary 6.9]
to approximate the constants
C_{J}
,
C_{Y}
,
we have

(6.7)

\Phi_{2k}(\cos^{2}\sqrt{\xi})=\frac{-\pi}{\xi^{1/4}(\sin{\sqrt{\xi}})^{1/2}}\Biggl[\sqrt{\xi}Y_{0}((4k-1)\sqrt{\xi})+\\
\sqrt{\xi}J_{0}((4k-1)\sqrt{\xi})+O\left(\frac{1}{k}\left|\sqrt{\xi}Y_{0}((4k-1)\sqrt{\xi})\right|\right)\Biggr].

In order to apply
[
21
, Eq. 10.7.8]
to approximate the Bessel
functions above we show that the argument is large, namely

((4k-1)\sqrt{\xi})\gg k^{1/2-\epsilon}.

Indeed, since
l
is not a full square there exist
u
and
m
such
that
4l=m^{2}+u
,
1\leq u\leq 2m
. Therefore,

2\sqrt{l}-m=\frac{u}{\sqrt{m^{2}+u}+m}<1

and

[2\sqrt{l}]=m,\quad\{2\sqrt{l}\}=\frac{u}{\sqrt{m^{2}+u}+m}.

Consequently,

\frac{\{2\sqrt{l}\}}{4\sqrt{l}}=\frac{u}{m+\sqrt{m^{2}+u}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m^{2}+u}}\geq\frac{1}{4m^{2}}.

This inequality can be applied to estimate

(4k-1)\sqrt{\xi}=(4k-1)\arccos{\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}}},\quad 1\leq n\leq[2\sqrt{l}].

Changing the variable
n
to
[2\sqrt{l}]-n
we have

(4k-1)\sqrt{\xi}=(4k-1)\arccos{\frac{[2\sqrt{l}]-n}{2\sqrt{l}}},\quad 0\leq n\leq[2\sqrt{l}]-1.

Further, trigonometric identities give

\begin{split}(4k-1)\sqrt{\xi}&=2(4k-1)\arcsin{\sqrt{\frac{n+\{2\sqrt{l}\}}{4\sqrt{l}}}}\\
&\geq 2(4k-1)\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{\{2\sqrt{l}\}}{4\sqrt{l}}}\gg\frac{k}{m}\gg\frac{k}{\sqrt{l}}\gg k^{1/2-\epsilon},\end{split}

as required.

Next, we insert the asymptotic expansion (
6.7
) into
ET_{2}
.
The contribution of the error term in (
6.7
) is majorized by

ET_{2,1}:=\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\leq k^{1+\epsilon}\\
l\neq\Box\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{l^{3/4}}\sum_{n<2\sqrt{l}}(4l-n^{2})^{\theta}\frac{\xi^{1/4}}{(\sin\sqrt{\xi})^{1/2}}\times\\
\left|Y_{0}((4k-1)\sqrt{\xi})\right|\ll\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\frac{1}{k}\sum_{l\leq k^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{1}{l^{3/4}}\sum_{n<2\sqrt{l}}\frac{(4l-n^{2})^{\theta}}{k^{1/2}(\sin\sqrt{\xi})^{1/2}}.

According to (
6.6
) we have

\sin\sqrt{\xi}=\frac{\sqrt{4l-n^{2}}}{2\sqrt{l}},

and therefore,

ET_{2,1}\ll\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\frac{1}{k^{3/2}}\sum_{l\leq k^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{1}{l^{3/4}}\sum_{n<2\sqrt{l}}(4l-n^{2})^{\theta-1/4}l^{1/4}\ll K^{\theta+1/4}.

It remains to estimate the contribution of the main term in (
6.7
)
given by

ET_{2,2}:=\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\sum_{l}\frac{V_{k}(l,0)}{l^{3/4}}\sum_{n<2\sqrt{l}}\mathscr{L}_{n^{2}-4l}(1/2)\times\\
\frac{\sin((4k-1)\sqrt{\xi}-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{4k-1}(\sin\sqrt{\xi})^{1/2}}=\sum_{l\ll K^{1+\epsilon}}\frac{1}{l^{1/2}}\sum_{n<2\sqrt{l}}\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n^{2}-4l}(1/2)}{(4l-n^{2})^{1/4}}\times\\
\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)V_{k}(l,0)\frac{\sin((4k-1)\arccos\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}}-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{4k-1}}.

The inner sum over
k
can be evaluated similarly to
[
1
, Lemma 7.3]
,
as we show now.
Using the Poisson summation formula

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)V_{k}(l,0)\frac{\sin((4k-1)\arccos\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}}-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{4k-1}}=\sum_{m}I(m),

where

I(m)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h\left(\frac{4y}{K}\right)V_{y}(l,0)\frac{\sin((4y-1)\arccos\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}}-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{4y-1}}\times\\
\exp(-my)dy\ll K\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(y)\exp(ig(y))V_{yK/4}(l,0)\frac{dy}{\sqrt{yK-1}}\right|

with

g(y)=\frac{K}{4}y\left(-2\pi m\pm 4\arccos\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}}\right).

Integrating
a
times by parts we obtain

I(m)\ll\begin{cases}\sqrt{K}/(Km)^{a}&m\neq 0\\
\sqrt{K}/(K\arccos\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}})^{a}&m=0.\end{cases}

Finally, since

\arccos\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}}\gg\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}

we have

\sum_{m}I(m)\ll\frac{\sqrt{K}}{(K\arccos\frac{n}{2\sqrt{l}})^{a}}\ll\frac{\sqrt{K}}{(K/\sqrt{l})^{a}}

and

ET_{2,2}\ll K^{-a}.

The last step is to evaluate the sum over
l=\Box
.
Making the change of variables from
l
to
l^{2}
we have to estimate

E_{2,3}:=\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)\sum_{l}\frac{V_{k}(l^{2},0)}{l^{3/2}}\sum_{n<2l}\mathscr{L}_{n^{2}-4l^{2}}(1/2)\Phi_{2k}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4l^{2}}\right).

Using the subconvexity estimate (
2.23
) we have

ET_{2,3}\ll\sum_{l\ll K^{1/2+\epsilon}}\frac{1}{l^{3/2}}\sum_{n<2l}(4l^{2}-n^{2})^{\theta}\left|\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)V_{k}(l^{2},0)\Phi_{2k}\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4l^{2}}\right)\right|.

Changing the variable
n
to
2l-n
gives

ET_{2,3}\ll\sum_{l\ll K^{1/2+\epsilon}}\sum_{n<2l}\frac{(nl)^{\theta}}{l^{3/2}}\left|\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)V_{k}(l^{2},0)\Phi_{2k}\left(\left(1-\frac{n^{2}}{4l^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\right|.

Let

\xi:=4(\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{n}{4l}})^{2}.

Consequently,

\left(1-\frac{n}{2l}\right)^{2}=\cos^{2}\sqrt{\xi}.

Applying
[
2
, Theorem 6.10]
, we have

\Phi_{2k}(\cos^{2}\sqrt{\xi})=\frac{-\pi}{\xi^{1/4}(\sin{\sqrt{\xi}})^{1/2}}\left[(1+C_{J})Z_{J}(\xi)+C_{Y}Z_{Y}(\xi)\right].

Note that now

(4k-1)\sqrt{\xi}=2(4k-1)\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{n}{4l}}\gg\frac{k}{\sqrt{l}}\gg k^{3/4}.

In the same way as in the case
l\neq\Box
we prove

ET_{2,3}\ll K^{-1/4+\theta}.

Finally,

ET_{2}\ll ET_{2,1}+ET_{2,2}+ET_{2,3}\ll K^{1/4+\theta}.

∎

Remark
.

Using
[
2
, Thm. 6.5]
with sufficiently large
N
depending on
A
and taking more terms in the asymptotics for
C_{Y},C_{J}
we obtain equation (
6.7
) with additional series of main terms plus the error term

O\left(\frac{1}{k^{A+2}}\left|\sqrt{\xi}Y_{0}((4k-1)\sqrt{\xi})\right|\right).

As a result, following the proof of Lemma
6.2
for any fixed
A>0
we have

(6.8)

\sum_{k}h\left(\frac{4k}{K}\right)ET_{2}\ll K^{-A}.

Berwald m m -Kropina Spaces of Arbitrary Signature: Metrizability and Ricci-Flatness
Sjors Heefer

Abstract
The (pseudo-)Riemann-metrizability and Ricci-flatness of Finsler spaces with m m -Kropina metric F = α 1 + m ​ β − m F=\alpha^{1+m}\beta^{-m} of Berwald type are investigated. We prove that the affine connection of F F can locally be understood as the Levi-Civita connection of some (pseudo-)Riemannian metric if and only if the Ricci tensor of the canonical affine connection is symmetric. We also obtain a third equivalent characterization in terms of the covariant derivative of the 1-form β \beta . We use these results to classify all locally metrizable m m -Kropina spaces whose 1-forms have a constant causal character. In the special case where the first de Rham cohomology group of the underlying manifold is trivial (which is true of simply connected manifolds, for instance), we show that global metrizability is equivalent to local metrizability and hence, in that case, our necessary and sufficient conditions also characterize global metrizability. In addition, we further obtain explicitly all Ricci-flat, locally metrizable m m -Kropina metrics in ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) D whose 1-forms have a constant causal character. In fact, the only possibilities are essentially the following two: either α \alpha is flat and β \beta is α \alpha -parallel, or α \alpha is a pp-wave and β \beta is α \alpha -parallel.



I
Introduction

A Berwald space is a Finsler space for which the canonical nonlinear connection reduces to a linear connection on the tangent bundle
TM
. This affine connection is necessarily torsion-free. The natural question thus arises whether this affine connection arises as the Levi-Civita connection of some pseudo-Riemannian metric. Simply put, is (the affine connection on) every Berwald space metrizable? In the classical positive definite setting, this is indeed the case, as was first shown in (Szabó, 1981)
[
1
]
.

The proofs of this result
[
1
,
2
]
rely on procedures such as averaging
[
3
]
over the indicatrix, for which it is essential that the Finsler metric
F
is sufficiently smooth and defined everywhere on the slit tangent bundle
TM_{0}
. In the case of Finsler metrics of indefinite signature, however, the domain
\mathcal{A}\subset TM
where
F
is defined is typically only a proper subset of
TM_{0}
and hence the proofs do not extend to this case. And even in the special case that
\mathcal{A}=TM_{0}
, the fact that the indicatrix is not compact in indefinite signatures poses problems.

It was shown in (Fuster et al., 2020)
[
4
]
that Szabó’s metrization theorem indeed does not generalize to the more general setting. In (Heefer et al., 2023)
[
5
]
, the situation was further investigated and necessary and sufficient conditions for local metrizability were obtained in the specific case of
m
-Kropina metrics with closed null 1-form. More recently, the metrizability of (spatially) spherically symmetric Berwald spacetimes was investigated in (Voicu et al., 2024)
[
6
]
.

Here we extend the analysis of (Heefer et al., 2023)
[
5
]
to arbitrary
m
-Kropina metrics, i.e. Finsler metrics of the form
F=\alpha^{1+m}\beta^{-m}
, with no restriction on the 1-form. The culprit behind all counterexamples to Szabó’s theorem obtained in (Heefer et al., 2023)
[
5
]
was the fact that the Ricci tensor constructed from the affine connection—the ‘affine Ricci tensor’—is in general not symmetric. This property—symmetry of the affine Ricci tensor—is in general a necessary condition for local metrizability, and for
m
-Kropina metrics with closed null 1-form it was shown to be a sufficient condition as well
[
5
]
.

Here we show that this results extends to all
m
-Kropina metrics, i.e. we prove that any
m
-Kropina metric is locally metrizable if and only if its affine Ricci tensor is symmetric. Additionally, we further obtain a third necessary and sufficient condition for local metrizability in terms of the covariant derivative of the 1-form
\beta
. We use these results to classify all locally metrizable
m
-Kropina spaces whose 1-forms have a constant causal character.

We also briefly touch upon the issue of global metrizability. We do not obtain a conclusive characterization of global metrizability in general, but in the special case where the first de Rham cohomology group of the underlying manifold is trivial (which is true of simply connected manifolds, for instance), we show that global metrizability is equivalent to local metrizability. Hence, in this case, our necessary and sufficient conditions for local metrizability also characterize global metrizability.

Finally, as an application of our results established thus far, we obtain explicitly all Ricci-flat, locally metrizable
m
-Kropina metrics in
(3+1)
D whose 1-forms have a constant causal character. Here we note that an
m
-Kropina space is locally metrizable and Ricci-flat (in the standard sense) if and only if it is
affinely
Ricci-flat—meaning that its affine Ricci tensor (rather than the standard Finsler-Ricci tensor, its symmetrization) vanishes. Affine Ricci-flatness turns out to be a rather restrictive property. In fact, the only possibilities are essentially the following two: either
\alpha
is flat and
\beta
is
\alpha
-parallel, or
\alpha
is a pp-wave and
\beta
is
\alpha
-parallel.

In the literature,
m
-Kropina metrics have been used for modeling the geometry of spacetime in Finsler extensions of Einstein’s general theory of relativity.
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. Indeed
m
-Kropina metrics have been found to yield exact solutions
[
13
,
8
,
9
]
to the Pfeifer and Wohlfarth’s Finslerian extension of Einstein’s field equation
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
; see (Heefer, 2024)
[
7
]
for a recent review. Our results put strict bounds on the remaining possibilities for solutions of Berwald
m
-Kropina type to this field equation.

Many of the results obtained in this article were first described in the author’s PhD thesis (Heefer, 2024)
[

II.1
Some notational conventions

In what follows, we will often work in local coordinates. Given a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
n
we will typically assume that some chart
\varphi:U\subset M\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is provided, and we will effectively identify any
p\in U
with its image
x=(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\coloneqq\varphi(p)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
under
\varphi
. If
p\in U
then each
X_{p}\in T_{p}M
can be written as
X_{p}=y^{i}\partial_{i}\big|_{p}\eqqcolon y^{i}\partial_{i}
(we will often be sloppy and suppress the dependence on the point
p
), where the tangent vectors
\partial_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
make up the coordinate basis of
T_{p}M
. This decomposition provides natural local coordinates on the tangent bundle
TM
via the chart

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}:TU\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\tilde{\varphi}(p,Y)=(\varphi(p),y^{1},\dots,y^{n})\eqqcolon(x,y),

(1)

where
TU
is given by

\displaystyle TU=\bigcup_{p\in U}\left\{p\right\}\times T_{p}M\subset TM.

(2)

These local coordinates on
TM
in turn provide a natural basis
\{\partial_{i},\bar{\partial}_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
of its tangent space
T_{(x,y)}TM
at
(x,y)
, where we define

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},\qquad\quad\bar{\partial}_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{i}}.

(3)

Finally, we will use the notation
\text{d}x^{i}\text{d}x^{j}
for the symmetrized tensor product of 1-forms, i.e.
\text{d}x^{i}\text{d}x^{j}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}(\text{d}x^{j}\otimes\text{d}x^{j}+\text{d}x^{j}\otimes\text{d}x^{j})
, and whenever we work specifically in Lorentzian signature we will adhere to the sign convention
(-,+,\dots,+)
.

II.2
Basic notions in Finsler geometry

For our purposes, a Finsler space will be a triple
(M,\mathcal{A},F
), where
M
is a smooth manifold,
\mathcal{A}
a conic subbundle of
TM
, and
F
is a Finsler metric on
\mathcal{A}
, defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

A conic subbundle of
TM
is an open subset
\mathcal{A}\subset TM
which is conic in the sense that
(x,\lambda y)\in\mathcal{A}
for any
(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}
and
\lambda>0
, and which satisfies
\pi(\mathcal{A})=M
, where
\pi:TM\to M
is the canonical projection of the tangent bundle. The latter property says that the fibers
\mathcal{A}_{x}
of
\mathcal{A}
are nonempty.

Definition 2
.

A Finsler metric on a conic subbundle
\mathcal{A}
is a smooth map
F:\mathcal{A}\to[0,\infty)
such that

•

F
is positively homogeneous of degree one:

\displaystyle F(x,\lambda y)=\lambda F(x,y)\,,\quad\forall\lambda>0\,;

(4)

•

The fundamental tensor, with components
g_{ij}=\bar{\partial}_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}\left(\frac{1}{2}F^{2}\right)
, is nondegenerate.

The length of a curve
\gamma:(a,b)\to M
(whose lift stays within
\mathcal{A}
) is then defined as

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma)=\int_{a}^{b}F(\gamma(\lambda),\dot{\gamma}(\lambda))\,\text{d}\lambda,\qquad\qquad\dot{\gamma}=\frac{\text{d}\gamma}{\text{d}\lambda}.

(5)

We will sometimes omit the specification of
\mathcal{A}
and say, loosely speaking, that
F
is a Finsler metric on
M
. We say that
F
is positive definite if
g_{ij}
is positive definite, and more generally that
F
has a certain signature if
g_{ij}
has that signature.

The connection coefficients of the canonical torsion-free, metric-compatible homogeneous nonlinear connection are given by

\displaystyle N_{i}^{j}=\tfrac{1}{4}\bar{\partial}_{i}\left[g^{jk}\left(y^{m}\partial_{m}\bar{\partial}_{k}L-\partial_{k}L\right)\right].

(6)

This nonlinear connection yields a smooth decomposition of the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{A}
into a horizontal and a vertical subbundle
1
1

1

For alternative equivalent definitions, see (Javaloyes et al., 2022)
[
22
]
and references therein.
,

\displaystyle T\mathcal{A}=H\mathcal{A}\oplus V\mathcal{A},

(7)

where
\oplus
denotes the Whitney sum of vector bundles. The vertical subbundle
V\mathcal{A}
is canonically defined as

\displaystyle V\mathcal{A}=\ker(\text{d}\pi)=\text{span}\left\{\bar{\partial}_{i}\right\}.

(8)

The horizontal subbundle, on the other hand, is defined by the nonlinear connection as

\displaystyle H\mathcal{A}=\text{span}\left\{\delta_{i}\right\},\qquad\delta_{i}\equiv\partial_{i}-N^{j}{}_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j},

(9)

where we have also introduced the horizontal derivatives
\delta_{i}
.

The curvature tensor of the nonlinear connection has components

\displaystyle R^{k}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i}N^{k}_{j}-\delta_{j}N^{k}_{i},

(10)

and from this we can further define the following two quantities:

\displaystyle\text{Ric}=R^{i}{}_{ij}y^{j},\qquad\quad R_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{\partial}_{i}\bar{\partial}_{j}\text{Ric}.

(11)

In the literature,
R_{ij}
is often just referred to as the Ricci tensor, but we will give it the name
Finsler-Ricci tensor
to distinguish it from the
affine Ricci tensor
that will be introduced momentarily for Berwald spaces. Similarly, we will refer to
\mathrm{Ric}
as the
Finsler-Ricci scalar
. From homogeneity and Euler’s theorem, it follows that the second relation in (
11
) can be inverted as

\displaystyle\text{Ric}=R_{ij}y^{i}y^{j}.

(12)

Ric and
R_{ij}
thus contain the same information, and in particular, we have
\text{Ric}=0
if and only if
R_{ij}=0
, in which the Finsler space is said to be
Ricci-flat
.

II.3
Berwald spaces

A Finsler space is said to be of
Berwald
type, or simply Berwald, if the canonical nonlinear connection reduces to a (necessarily smooth) linear connection on
TM
, or in other words, an affine connection on the base manifold
M
, meaning that the connection coefficients are of the form
N^{k}_{i}=\Gamma^{k}_{ij}(x)y^{j}
for certain coefficients
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}(x)
. In this case, the functions
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
are precisely the Christoffel symbols of said affine connection on
M
. We will refer to this affine connection as the associated affine connection, or simply
the affine connection
on the Berwald space. It follows from the torsion-freeness of the nonlinear connection that the affine connection is torsion-free as well.

The curvature tensors (
10
)-(
11
) of a Berwald space can be written as

\displaystyle R^{k}_{ij}=\bar{R}^{k}{}_{\ell ij}(x)y^{\ell},\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{Ric}=\bar{R}_{ij}(x)y^{i}y^{j},\,\,\,\,\,\,R_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bar{R}_{ij}(x)+\bar{R}_{ji}(x)\right)

(13)

in terms of the curvature tensor and Ricci tensor of the affine connection, given by

\displaystyle\bar{R}^{k}{}_{\ell ij}=\partial_{i}\Gamma^{k}_{j\ell}-\partial_{j}\Gamma^{k}_{i\ell}+\Gamma^{k}_{im}\Gamma^{m}_{j\ell}-\Gamma^{k}_{jm}\Gamma^{m}_{i\ell},\qquad\bar{R}_{lk}=\bar{R}^{i}{}_{l}{}_{ik},

(14)

respectively. We will refer to
\bar{R}^{k}{}_{\ell ij}
and
\bar{R}_{lk}
as (the components of) the
affine curvature tensor
and
affine Ricci tensor
, respectively. Since the last identity in (
13
) will prove to be of particular interest later, we restate it as a lemma.

Lemma 3
.

The Finsler-Ricci tensor of a Berwald space is the symmetrization of the affine Ricci tensor.

We say that a Berwald space is
affinely Ricci-flat
if
\bar{R}_{ij}=0
. Note that this is a stronger condition than (standard) Ricci-flatness,
R_{ij}=0
.

II.4
Metrizability

Given a Finsler space of Berwald type, the canonical nonlinear connection reduces to a linear connection on
TM
that is torsion-free. This leads to the natural question of whether this linear connection is ‘metrizable’.

Definition 4
.

Let
F
be a Berwald metric with underlying manifold
M
. We say that (the canonical affine connection of)
F
is metrizable if it arises as the Levi-Civita connection of some pseudo-Riemannian metric on
M
.

For readability, we usually omit the phrase ‘the canonical affine connection of’ in between brackets and we simply say that
F
is metrizable. A slightly weaker property is that of local metrizability.

Definition 5
.

A Berwald space with underlying manifold
M
is said to be locally metrizable if each point in
M
has a neighborhood that admits a pseudo-Riemannian metric whose Levi-Civita connection coincides with the affine connection restricted to that neighborhood.

\displaystyle F=\alpha^{1+m}\beta^{-m},

\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt{\left|a_{ij}y^{i}y^{j}\right|},\qquad\beta=b_{i}y^{i}

In the physics literature, spacetimes with a metric of

(VSR)

We further introduce the notation

\displaystyle|b|^{2}\equiv a_{ij}b^{i}b^{j}

for the squared norm of

III.1
The Berwald condition

As shown recently
[
5
]
(see also (Heefer, 2024)
[
7
]
for a more in-depth treatment and an alternative proof), an m-Kropina metric
F=\alpha^{1+m}\beta^{-m}
with
n=\dim M>2
is of Berwald type if and only if there exists a smooth vector field with components
f^{i}
on
M
such that

\displaystyle\mathring{\nabla}_{j}b_{i}=m(f_{k}b^{k})a_{ij}+b_{i}f_{j}-mf_{i}b_{j}.

(18)

Here and throughout the remainder of the article,
\mathring{\nabla}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
a_{ij}
. This Berwald condition agrees with the one obtained by Matsumoto
[
19
]
, which was proven under slightly different assumptions.

Whenever the condition (
18
) is satisfied, the affine connection coefficients of the Berwald
m
-Kropina space can be expressed in terms of the Christoffel symbols
\mathring{\Gamma}^{k}_{ij}
of the Levi-Civita connection corresponding to
\alpha
as follows
[
7
,
5
,
19
]

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\ell}_{ij}=\mathring{\Gamma}^{\ell}_{ij}+\Delta\Gamma^{\ell}_{ij},\qquad\Delta\Gamma^{\ell}_{ij}=ma^{\ell k}\left(a_{ij}f_{k}-a_{jk}f_{i}-a_{ki}f_{j}\right)

(19)

This implies in particular that for any given data (
\alpha,\beta
) there can be at most one vector field
f^{k}
satisfying (
18
). (This can be seen for instance by considering
\Delta\Gamma^{\ell}_{ij}
with
i=j\neq\ell
.)

IV
Characterization of metrizability

From here onwards we will focus on
m
-Kropina metrics of Berwald type with
n=\dim M>2
. The main aim in this section is answer the following question:

Q:
Under what conditions can the affine connection on a Berwald
m
-Kropina space be understood, locally, as the Levi-Civita connection of a pseudo-Riemannian metric?

In §
IV.1
, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for local metrizability. Then we discuss several special cases in §
IV.2
. And finally, in §
IV.3
, we have a brief discussion on global metrizability.

IV.1
The main theorem for local metrizability

It is a standard result that the Ricci tensor of any Levi-Civita connection is symmetric. This immediately implies that symmetry of the Ricci tensor constructed from the affine connection of a Berwald space—that is, symmetry of the
affine Ricci tensor
(see Section
II.3
)—is a necessary condition for metrizablilty. To characterize this condition, we will first derive a simple formula for the skew-symmetric part of the affine Ricci tensor, denoted by
\mathcal{A}(\bar{R})
and having components
\mathcal{A}(\bar{R})_{ij}=\bar{R}_{[ij]}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bar{R}_{ij}-\bar{R}_{ji}\right)
. Moreover, to prevent confusion, we remark that the expression
\text{d}f
below denotes the exterior derivative of the 1-form
f
with components
f_{i}=a_{ij}f^{j}
appearing in the Berwald condition (
18
). We also recall that
n=\dim M
.

Lemma 6
.

The skew-symmetric part of the affine Ricci tensor is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\bar{R})=-\frac{mn}{2}\text{d}f

(20)

Proof.

From the definition (
14
) of the affine Ricci tensor of a Berwald space and the fact that
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\Gamma^{k}_{ji}
it follows that the skew-symmetric part of the affine Ricci tensor can be written as

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{[ij]}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\bar{R}_{ij}-\bar{R}_{ji}\right)=\partial_{[i}\Gamma^{k}_{j]k}.

(21)

Since the Levi-Civita connection
\mathring{\Gamma}^{k}_{ij}
has a symmetric Ricci tensor, it follows that
\partial_{[i}\mathring{\Gamma}^{k}_{j]k}=0
and hence we have

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{[ij]}

\displaystyle=\partial_{[i}\Gamma^{k}_{j]k}=\partial_{[i}\Delta\Gamma^{k}_{j]k}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{i}\Delta\Gamma^{k}_{kj}-\partial_{j}\Delta\Gamma^{k}_{ki}\right).

(22)

Employing the expression (
19
) for
\Delta\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
, we find that

\displaystyle\Delta\Gamma^{k}_{kj}=-mnf_{j}

(23)

and this yields

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{[ij]}=\frac{mn}{2}(\partial_{j}f_{i}-\partial_{i}f_{j}),

(24)

which is precisely the coordinate expression of (
20
), as desired.
∎

We then have the following characterization of local metrizability.

Theorem 7
.

For an
m
-Kropina metric
F=\alpha^{1+m}\beta^{-m}
of Berwald type with
\dim M>2
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

F
is locally metrizable;

(ii)

The affine Ricci tensor is symmetric,
\bar{R}_{ij}=\bar{R}_{ji}
;

(iii)

The 1-form
f_{i}
appearing in (
18
) closed.

In this case we can locally write

for some
\psi\in C^{\infty}(M)
and we may define
\tilde{a}_{ij}=e^{-2m\psi}a_{ij}
and
\tilde{b}_{i}=e^{-(1+m)\psi}b_{i}

\tilde{\alpha}

•

F

;

•

\tilde{\beta}

\tilde{\alpha}

•

Proof.

We will prove that (i)

\Rightarrow

\Rightarrow

(i)
\Rightarrow

(ii)

The remaining nontrivial part of the proof amounts to showing that (iii) implies (i).

(iii)

IV.2.1

IV.3
Global metrizability

Note that in the proof of Theorem
7
we made use of the fact that
\text{d}f=0
implies that

is a locally exact 1-form. Here

is the 1-form appearing in the Berwald condition (

is (globally) exact, the proof guarantees that the metric

providing the metrization is in fact globally defined, implying that the Finsler space under consideration is globally metrizable. Hence we have the following.

Proposition 13
.

If
f
is an exact 1-form then

is (globally) metrizable.

In general, not every closed 1-form is (globally) exact, and hence the proof of the theorem does not tell us whether local metrizability is equivalent to global metrizability. In certain specific cases we can say more, however. First, in the case that the 1-form
\beta
is nowhere null, we can use Prop.

to strengthen Cor.
9
and Cor.

Corollary 14
.

An
m
-Kropina space with
\beta
nowhere null is (globally) metrizable.

Proof.

Since
||b|^{2}|
is a globally defined function, it follows from (
28

f
is globally exact. Hence the result follows from Prop.
13
.
∎

Corollary 15
.

An
m
-Kropina space with positive definite
\alpha
is (globally) metrizable.

These results are independent of the topology of the underlying manifold but do require a nontrivial assumption about
\beta
. The following theorem shows that if, alternatively, we know more about the topology, we can also obtain some interesting results even when
\beta
is not necessarily nowhere vanishing.

Theorem 16
.

Suppose the first de Rham cohomology group of
M
is trivial. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

F
is metrizable;

(ii)

F
is locally metrizable;

(iii)

The affine Ricci tensor is symmetric;

(iv)

f_{i}
is a closed 1-form.

In that case, writing
f=\text{d}\psi
, a global metrization of
F
is given by
\tilde{a}=e^{-2m\psi}a
and moreover,
\tilde{b}=e^{-(1+m)\psi}b
is parallel with respect to
\tilde{a}
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the equivalence of global metrizability with the other three (already equivalent) statements. Since global metrizibility trivially implies local metrizability, it suffices to show that
f
being closed implies that
F
is metrizable. So suppose that
f
is closed. Then since the first de Rham cohomology group is trivial,
f
is exact,
f=\text{d}\psi
, with
\psi
defined globally. The proof of Theorem
7
shows that
\tilde{a}=e^{-2m\psi}a
provides a global metrization. Hence
F
is globally metrizable.
∎

Since any simply connected manifold has a trivial first de Rham cohomology group, we have, in particular, the following result.

Corollary 17
.

If
M
is simply connected then (i)-(iv) are equivalent.

The remaining question is the following one:

Q:

Do locally but not globally metrizable
m
-Kropina spaces exist at all?

This is still an open question and we defer its analysis to future work, even though it seems likely that the answer should be affirmative. It is clear, at least, that such a space would need to have a 1-form
f
appearing in the Berwald condition that is
closed but not exact
. For if it were not closed, the space would not be locally metrizable and hence not metrizable; and if it were exact, then it would be globally metrizable. A straightforward method for trying to produce a counterexample is thus given as follows.

1.

Choose your favorite manifold
M
with nontrivial first de Rham cohomology group.

2.

Pick a 1-form
f
that is closed but not exact.

3.

Pick a pseudo-Riemannian metric
\alpha
.

4.

Try to solve the Berwald condition for
\beta
.

An obvious first guess in dimension 3 would be to let
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
with the metric
\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}x^{2}+\text{d}y^{2}+\text{d}\theta^{2}
and
f=\text{d}\theta
, where
(x,y)
are the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\theta
is the standard angle coordinate on the circle
S^{1}
. The 1-form
f=\text{d}\theta
is the prime example of a closed but not exact 1-form, of course. In this case, which is relatively computable, the system defined by the Berwald condition turns out not to be solvable, however. That is, there is no 1-form
\beta
that leads to the prescribed
f=\text{d}\theta
. The same is true if one instead uses the metric
\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}x^{2}+\text{d}y^{2}+y^{2}\text{d}\theta^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
, which corresponds to the standard flat metric
\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}x^{2}+\text{d}\tilde{y}^{2}+\text{d}z^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
if we view
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
as an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the natural way, where

is basically the radial coordinate in the
\tilde{y}z
-plane. Hence, in order to produce counterexamples—if these exist (which we do expect)—one needs to consider more elaborate examples of manifolds with closed but not exact 1-forms. We leave this to future study.

Lemma 19

Any Ricci-flat Lorentzian metric admitting a non-null parallel vector field or 1-form is flat.

Corollary 20

.

\displaystyle\text{F is affinely Ricci-flat}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\text{F is locally metrizable and Ricci-flat}.

is locally metrizable then, by Theorem

VI
Discussion

In this paper, we have studied the local and global metrizability as well as Ricci-flatness of
m
-Kropina spaces of Berwald type. First of all, we obtained necessary and sufficient conditions for the metrizability of Berwald
m
-Kropina metrics
F=\alpha^{1+m}\beta^{-m}
of arbitrary signature. More precisely, Theorem
7
shows that the canonical affine connection of
F
can locally be obtained as the Levi-Civita connection of some pseudo-Riemannian metric if and only if the Ricci tensor constructed from the affine connection is symmetric. In addition, the theorem gives a third equivalent characterization of local metrizability in terms of the covariant derivative of the defining 1-form
\beta
with respect to the defining pseudo-Riemannian metric
\alpha
. Using these characterizations, we have classified all locally metrizable
m
-Kropina metrics whose 1-forms have a constant causal character (Prop.
12
and Cor.
14
). These results are consistent with, and extend, the findings obtained in (Heefer et al., 2023)
[
5
]
, where only closed null 1-forms
\beta
were considered.

In general, global metrizability is a stronger notion than local metrizability. Nevertheless, in the special case where the first de Rham cohomology group of the underlying manifold is trivial (which is true of simply connected manifolds, for instance), we have shown that global metrizability is equivalent to local metrizability. Hence, in this case, our necessary and sufficient conditions for local metrizability also characterize global metrizability.

Finally, we have classified all Ricci-flat, locally metrizable
m
-Kropina metrics in
(3+1)

Abstract
. This paper presents a numerical method for div-curl systems with normal boundary conditions by using a finite element technique known as primal-dual weak Galerkin (PDWG). The PDWG finite element scheme for the div-curl system has two prominent features in that it offers not only an accurate and reliable numerical solution to the div-curl system under the low H α H^{\alpha} -regularity ( α > 0 \alpha>0 ) assumption for the true solution, but also an effective approximation of normal harmonic vector fields regardless the topology of the domain. Results of seven numerical experiments are presented to demonstrate the performance of the PDWG algorithm, including one example on the computation of discrete normal harmonic vector fields.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with the development of new numerical methods for div-curl systems equipped with normal boundary conditions. For simplicity, consider the model problem that seeks a vector field
\bm{u}=\bm{u}({\bm{x}})
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\bm{u})

\displaystyle=f,\qquad{\rm in}\ \Omega,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\times\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{g},\qquad{\rm in}\ \Omega,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}

\displaystyle=\phi_{1},\qquad{\rm on}\ \Gamma,

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a polyhedral domain that is bounded, open, and connected.
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
is the boundary of
\Omega
and is assumed to be the union of a finite number of disjoint surfaces
\Gamma=\bigcup_{i=0}^{L}\Gamma_{i}
, where
\Gamma_{0}
is the exterior boundary of
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{i}\ (i=1,\cdots,L)
are the other connected components with finite surface areas, and
L
denotes the number of holes in the domain
\Omega
geometrically and is known as the second Betti number of
\Omega
or the dimension of the second de Rham cohomology group of
\Omega
. The Lebesgue-integrable real-valued function
f=f({\bm{x}})
and the vector field
\bm{g}=\bm{g}({\bm{x}})
are given in the domain
\Omega
, the coefficient matrix
\varepsilon=\{\varepsilon_{ij}({\bm{x}})\}_{3\times 3}
is symmetric and uniformly positive definite in
\Omega
, and the entries
\varepsilon_{ij}({\bm{x}})
(
i,j=1,\cdots,3
) are in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. The normal boundary data
\phi_{1}
is a given distribution in
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
.

The solution uniqueness for the normal boundary value problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) depends on the topology of the domain
\Omega
. It is well-known that the solution uniqueness holds true for simply connected
\Omega
, while the solution is unique up to a normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic function in
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
defined in (
2.2
) when the domain
\Omega
is not simply connected. The dimension of
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
is identical to the first Betti number of
\Omega
, which is
the rank of the first homology group of
\Omega
or the dimension of the first de Rham cohomology group of
\Omega
.

The div-curl system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) arises in many applications such as electromagnetic fields and fluid mechanics. Computational electro-magnetics plays an important role in many areas of science and engineering such as radar, satellite, antenna design, waveguides, optical fibers, medical imaging and design of invisible cloaking devices
[
14
]
. In linear magnetic fields, the function
f({\bm{x}})
vanishes,
{\bm{u}}
represents the magnetic field intensity and
\varepsilon({\bm{x}})
is the inverse of the magnetic permeability tensor. In fluid mechanics fields, the coefficient matrix
\varepsilon({\bm{x}})
is diagonal with diagonal entries being the local mass density. In electrostatics fields,
\varepsilon({\bm{x}})
is the permittivity matrix.

Several numerical methods based on finite element approaches have been proposed and analyzed for the div-curl system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). For example, a covolume method was developed in three dimensional space by using the Voronoi-Delaunay mesh pairs
[
18
]
. In
[
5
]
, a finite element method based on different test and trial spaces was analyzed for a div-curl system. In
[
16
]
, the authors introduced a mimetic finite difference scheme for the three dimensional magneto-static problems on general polyhedral partitions. In
[
10
]
, the authors developed a mixed finite element method for three dimensional axisymmetric div-curl systems through a dimension reduction technique based on the cylindrical coordinates in simply connected and axisymmetric domains. In
[
4
]
, the authors developed a least-squares finite element method for two types boundary value problems. Another least-squares method was proposed for the div-curl problem based on discontinuous elements on nonconvex polyhedral domains in
[
3
]
. In
[
21
]
, the authors proposed a weak Galerkin finite element method for the div-curl system with either normal or tangential boundary conditions. Another weak Galerkin scheme was introduced in
[
15
]
by using a least-squares approach for the div-curl problem. In
[
17
]
, the authors developed a primal-dual weak Galerkin finite element method for the div-curl system with tangential boundary conditions and proved that the scheme works well with low-regularity assumptions on the exact solution.

Two major challenges for the div-curl model problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) are the low-regularity of the true solution
{\bm{u}}
and the difficulties in approximating the normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector space
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
due to the topological complexity of the domain
\Omega
. The goal of this paper is to address both challenges through a new numerical method based on the primal-dual weak Galerkin (PDWG) finite element aproach originated in
[
23
]
and further developed in
[
20
,
25
,
26
,
7
,
22
,
13
]
for various model problems. Our PDWG numerical method for (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) has two prominent features over the existing numerical methods: (1) it offers an effective approximation of the normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector space
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
regardless of the topology of the domain
\Omega
; and (2) it provides an accurate and reliable numerical solution for the div-curl system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with low
H^{\alpha}
-regularity (
\alpha>0
) assumption for the true solution
{\bm{u}}
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to notations and the derivation of a weak formulation for the div-curl system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) that involves no partial derivatives over the vector field
{\bm{u}}
. Section
3
offers a brief review on the discrete weak gradient and discrete weak curl operators. Section
4
is dedicated to the presentation of the PDWG algorithm for the div-curl problem, together with an algorithm for computing discrete normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector fields.
Section
5
is devoted to a discussion of the solution existence and uniqueness for the PDWG scheme. Section
6
contains a convergence theory for the PDWG approximation, and Section
7
demonstrates the performance of the PDWG algorithm through seven test examples.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

We follow the usual notation for Sobolev spaces and norms, see for example
[
9
,
12
]
. For an open bounded domain
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with Lipschitz continuous boundary and any given real number
s\geq 0
, we use
\|\cdot\|_{s,D}
and
|\cdot|_{s,D}
to denote the norm and seminorm in the Sobolev space
H^{s}(D)
, respectively. The space
H^{0}(D)
coincides with
L^{2}(D)
, for which the norm and the inner product are denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{D}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{D}
, respectively. We use
H(div_{\varepsilon};D)
to denote the closed subspace of
[L^{2}(D)]^{2}
so that
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{v}})\in L^{2}(D)
. The space
H(div;D)
corresponds to the case of
\varepsilon=I
. Analogously, we use
H(curl;D)
to denote the closed subspace of
[L^{2}(D)]^{2}
so that
\nabla\times{\bm{v}}\in[L^{2}(D)]^{3}
. Denote by

H_{0}(curl;D):=\{{\bm{v}}\in H(curl;D),\ {\bm{v}}\times{\bm{n}}=0\mbox{ on }\partial D\}

the closed subspace with vanishing tangential boundary values. When
D=\Omega
, we shall drop the script
D
in the notations. For simplicity, we shall use “
\lesssim
” to denote “less than or equal to up to a general constant independent of the mesh size or functions appearing in the inequality”.

Introduce the following Sobolev space

(2.1)

{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}=\{{\bm{v}}\in H_{0}(curl)\cap H(div_{\varepsilon}),\ \nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{v}})=0,\ \langle\varepsilon{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{i},1\rangle_{\Gamma_{i}}=0,\ i=1,\cdots,L\}.

A vector field
{\bm{v}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}
is said to be
\varepsilon
-harmonic on
\Omega
if it is
\varepsilon
-solenoidal and irrotational on
\Omega
. The space of normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector fields, denoted by
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
, consists of all
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector fields satisfying the zero normal boundary condition; i.e.,

(2.2)

\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)=\{{\bm{v}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}:\ {\nabla\times}{\bm{v}}=0,\ \nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{v}})=0,\ \varepsilon{\bm{v}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma\}.

When
\varepsilon=I
is the identity matrix, the space
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
shall be denoted as
\mathbb{H}_{n,0}(\Omega)
. Analogously, the space of tangential
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector fields, denoted by
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon\tau,0}(\Omega)
, consists of all
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector fields satisfying the zero tangential boundary condition; i.e.,

\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon\tau,0}(\Omega)=\{{\bm{v}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}:\ {\nabla\times}{\bm{v}}=0,\ \nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{v}})=0,\ {\bm{v}}\times{\bm{n}}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma\}.

2.1.
A weak formulation

By testing the equation (
1.1
) against any
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and then using the normal boundary condition (
1.3
) we obtain

(2.3)

(\bm{u},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi)=\langle\phi_{1},\varphi\rangle-(f,\varphi),\qquad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Next, we test the equation (
1.2
) against any
\bm{w}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
to obtain

(2.4)

\displaystyle(\bm{u},\nabla\times\bm{w})=(\bm{g},\bm{w}),\qquad\forall\bm{w}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega).

Summing the equations (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) gives the following

\displaystyle(\bm{u},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi+\nabla\times\bm{w})=(\bm{g},\bm{w})-(f,\varphi)+\langle\phi_{1},\varphi\rangle

for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\bm{w}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A vector-valued function
\bm{u}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}
is said to be a weak solution of the normal boundary value problem for the div-curl system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) if it satisfies the following equation

(2.5)

\displaystyle(\bm{u},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi+\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}})=(\bm{g},{\bm{\psi}})-(f,\varphi)+\langle\phi_{1},\varphi\rangle

for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
{\bm{\psi}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
.

The solution to the variational problem (
2.5
) is generally non-unique. In fact, the homogeneous version of (
2.5
) seeks
{\bm{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}
satisfying

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\bm{u},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi+\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}})=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega),\ {\bm{\psi}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega).

The equation (
2.6
) is easily satisfied by any
\varepsilon
-harmonic function
{\bm{u}}=\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
, and hence the solution non-uniqueness when the
\varepsilon
-harmonic space
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
has a positive dimension. The solution to the div-curl system with normal boundary condition is unique when the solution is further required to be
\varepsilon
-weighted
L^{2}
orthogonal to
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
.

2.2.
An extended weak formulation

Denote by
H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)
the space of functions in
H^{1}(\Omega)
with vanishing value on
\Gamma_{0}
and constant values on other connected components of the boundary; i.e.,

H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)=\{\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega):\ \phi|_{\Gamma_{0}}=0,\ \phi|_{\Gamma_{i}}=\alpha_{i},\ i=1,\dotsc,L\}.

Introduce the following bilinear form:

(2.7)

B({\bm{u}},s;\varphi,{\bm{\psi}}):=({\bm{u}},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi+\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}})+({\bm{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla s).

The extended weak formulation for the normal boundary value problem of the div-curl system seeks
({\bm{u}},s)\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}\times H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(2.8)

B({\bm{u}},s;\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})=F(\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})\qquad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega),\forall{\bm{\psi}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega),

where

F(\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})=(\bm{g},{\bm{\psi}})-(f,\varphi)+\langle\phi_{1},\varphi\rangle.

Note that by testing the curl equation in the div-curl system against any
\rho\in H^{1}_{0c}(\Omega)
we have

(2.9)

(\bm{g},\nabla\rho)=0,\qquad\forall\rho\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega),

which gives rise to the following compatibility condition:

(2.10)

\nabla\cdot\bm{g}=0,\quad\langle\bm{g}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{i},1\rangle_{\Gamma_{i}}=0,\ \text{for}\ i=1,\cdots,L.

Theorem 2.1
.

Under the compatibility condition (
2.10
) for
\bm{g}
, the solution
({\bm{u}};s)
of (
2.8
) satisfies the following equations:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{u}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f,\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{g},\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(2.13)

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\varepsilon{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{1},\qquad\mbox{on }\Gamma.

Proof.

By letting
\bm{\psi}=0
in (
2.8
) we have

(\bm{u},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi)=-(f,\varphi)+\langle\phi_{1},\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}

for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. It follows that
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{u}})=f
and

\varepsilon{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=\phi_{1}\mbox{ on }\Gamma
,
which leads to (
2.11
) and (
2.14
). Next, by letting
\varphi=0
in (
2.8
) we arrive at

\displaystyle(\bm{u},\nabla\times\bm{\psi})+(\bm{\psi},\varepsilon\nabla s)=(\bm{g},\bm{\psi}),\qquad\forall\bm{\psi}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega),

which leads to

\displaystyle(\nabla\times\bm{u}+\varepsilon\nabla s,\bm{\psi})=(\bm{g},\bm{\psi}),

and thus

(2.15)

\displaystyle\nabla\times\bm{u}+\varepsilon\nabla s=\bm{g},\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega.

Now from (
2.15
) we have

(\nabla\times\bm{u}+\varepsilon\nabla s,\nabla s)=(\bm{g},\nabla s),

which, by the usual integration by parts, gives

\langle{\bm{n}}\times\bm{u},\nabla s\rangle_{\Gamma}+(\varepsilon\nabla s,\nabla s)=(\bm{g},\nabla s),

and by the boundary condition of
s=const
on each
\Gamma_{i}
and the compatibility condition (
2.10
)

(\varepsilon\nabla s,\nabla s)=\langle\bm{u},{\bm{n}}\times\nabla s\rangle_{\Gamma}+(\bm{g},\nabla s)=0.

It follows that
\nabla s=0
so that
s\equiv 0
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The homogeneous dual problem of (
2.8
) seeks
\lambda\in H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
and
{\bm{q}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
satisfying

(2.16)

B({\bm{v}},r;\lambda,{\bm{q}})=0\qquad\forall{\bm{v}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3},\ r\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega).

Theorem 2.2
.

The solution to the homogeneous dual problem (
2.16
) is unique.

Proof.

The problem (
2.16
) can be rewritten as

(2.17)

(\bm{v},\varepsilon\nabla\lambda+\nabla\times{\bm{q}})+({\bm{q}},\varepsilon\nabla r)=0

for all
{\bm{v}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}
and
r\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)
. Note that the test against
r\in H^{1}_{0c}(\Omega)
and
\bm{v}=0
ensures
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{q}})=0
and
\langle\varepsilon{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{n}},1\rangle_{\Gamma_{i}}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,L\}
. In addition, by letting
r=0
and varying
\bm{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}
we arrive at

\varepsilon\nabla\lambda+\nabla\times{\bm{q}}=0,

which, by testing against
\nabla\lambda
, leads to

(\varepsilon\nabla\lambda,\nabla\lambda)=0,

so that
\lambda\equiv 0
and hence

\nabla\times\bm{q}=0.

Thus, we have

\bm{q}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon\tau,0}(\Omega),\quad\langle\varepsilon\bm{q}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{i},1\rangle_{\Gamma_{i}}=0\ \text{for}\ i=1,\cdots,L,

which yields
\bm{q}\equiv 0
.
∎

3.
Discrete Weak Gradient and Weak Curl

The extended weak formulation (
2.8
) is based on the gradient and curl differential operators. In this section, we shall review the notion of discrete weak gradient and weak curl which forms a corner stone of the weak Galerkin finite element method. To this end, let
T
be a polyhedral domain with boundary
\partial T
and unit outward normal direction
\bm{n}
on
\partial T
. Define the space of weak functions in
T
by

W(T)=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:v_{0}\in L_{2}(T),v_{b}\in L_{2}(\partial T)\},

where
v_{0}
represents the value of
v
in the interior of
T
, and
v_{b}
represents some specific boundary information of
v
. Analogously, define the space of vector-valued weak functions on
T
by

V(T)=\{\bm{v}=\{\bm{v}_{0},\bm{v}_{b}\}:\bm{v}_{0}\in[L_{2}(T)]^{3},\bm{v}_{b}\in[L_{2}(\partial T)]^{3}\}.

Let
P_{j}(T)
the space of polynomials on
T
with total degree
j
and less. For any
v\in W(T)
, the discrete weak gradient, denoted by
\nabla_{w,j,T}v
, is defined as the unique vector-valued polynomial in
[P_{j}(T)]^{3}
satisfying

(3.1)

(\nabla_{w,j,T}v,\bm{\varphi})_{T}=-(v_{0},\nabla\cdot\bm{\varphi})_{T}+\langle v_{b},\bm{\varphi}\cdot{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall\;\bm{\varphi}\in[P_{j}(T)]^{3}.

Analogously, the discrete weak curl of
{\bm{v}}\in V(T)
, denoted by
\nabla_{w,j,T}\times{\bm{v}}
, is defined as the unique vector-valued polynomial in
[P_{j}(T)]^{3}
, satisfying

(3.2)

(\nabla_{w,j,T}\times{\bm{v}},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=({\bm{v}}_{0},\nabla\times{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}-\langle{\bm{v}}_{b}\times{\bm{n}},{\bm{\varphi}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall\;\bm{\varphi}\in[P_{j}(T)]^{3}.

4.
PDWG Numerical Algorithm

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a finite element partition of the domain
\Omega
consisting of
polyhedra that are shape-regular
[
27
]
. Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the
set of faces in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{h}\setminus\partial\Omega
the set of interior faces. Denote by
h_{T}
the diameter of the element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
h=\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}
the meshsize of the partition
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.

For a given integer
k\geq 0
, we introduce the following finite element spaces subordinated to
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
:

\begin{split}{\bm{V}}_{h}=&\{\bm{v}:\ \bm{v}|_{T}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{3},\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},\\
S_{h}=&\{\{s_{0},s_{b}\}:\ s_{0}|_{T}\in P_{k}(T),s_{b}|_{\partial T}\in P_{k}({\partial T}),\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h},s_{b}|_{\Gamma_{0}}=0,s_{b}|_{\Gamma_{i}}=c_{i}\},\\
M_{h}=&\{\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{b}\}:\ \varphi_{0}|_{T}\in P_{k}(T),\varphi_{b}|_{\partial T}\in P_{k}({\partial T}),\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h},\ \int_{\Omega}\varphi_{0}=0\},\\
{\bm{W}}_{h}=&\{\bm{\psi}=\{\bm{\psi}_{0},\bm{\psi}_{b}\}:\ \bm{\psi}_{0}|_{T}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{3},\bm{\psi}_{b}|_{\partial T}\in G_{k}({\partial T}),\forall T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{b}|_{\Gamma}=0\},\end{split}

where
G_{k}({\partial T}):=[P_{k}(\sigma)]^{3}\times{\bm{n}}_{\sigma}
is the space of polynomials of degree
k
in the tangent space of
{\partial T}
.

For simplicity of notation, for
\sigma\in S_{h}
or
\sigma\in M_{h}
, denote by
\nabla_{w}\sigma
the discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w,k,T}\sigma
computed by using (
3.1
) on each element
T
; i.e.,

(\nabla_{w}\sigma)|_{T}=\nabla_{w,k,T}(\sigma|_{T}),\qquad\sigma\in S_{h}\ \text{or}\ \sigma\in M_{h}.

Analogously, for
{\bm{q}}\in{\bm{W}}_{h}
, denote by
\nabla_{w}\times{\bm{q}}
the discrete weak
curl
\nabla_{w,k,T}\times{\bm{q}}
computed by using (
3.2
) on each element
T
; i.e.,

(\nabla_{w}\times{\bm{q}})|_{T}=\nabla_{w,k,T}\times({\bm{q}}|_{T}),\qquad{\bm{q}}\in{\bm{W}}_{h}.

An approximation of the bilinear form
B(\cdot;\cdot)
is given as follows:

(4.1)

B_{h}({\bm{u}}_{h},s_{h};\varphi,{\bm{\psi}}):=({\bm{u}}_{h},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}\varphi+\nabla_{w}\times{\bm{\psi}})+({\bm{\psi}}_{0},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}s_{h})

for
{\bm{u}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},\ s_{h}\in S_{h},\ \varphi\in M_{h},\ {\bm{\psi}}\in{\bm{W}}_{h}
.

The following is the PDWG finite element method for the div-curl model system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
).

Algorithm 1

(PDWG Algorithm)
.

For an approximate solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
), one may compute

{\bm{u}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
, together with three auxiliary variables
s_{h}\in S_{h}
,
\lambda_{h}\in M_{h}
, and
{\bm{q}}_{h}\in{\bm{W}}_{h}
satisfying

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}s_{1}(\lambda_{h},{\bm{q}}_{h};\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})+B_{h}({\bm{u}}_{h},s_{h};\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})&=F(\varphi,{\bm{\psi}}),\quad\forall\varphi\in M_{h},\ {\bm{\psi}}\in{\bm{W}}_{h},\\
-s_{2}(s_{h},r)+B_{h}(\bm{v},r;\lambda_{h},{\bm{q}}_{h})&=0,\quad\forall\bm{v}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},\ r\in S_{h}.\end{array}\right.

Here the stabilizer
s_{1}
is given by

(4.3)

\displaystyle s_{1}(\lambda_{h},{\bm{q}}_{h};\varphi,\bm{\psi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\langle\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{b},\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle+\rho_{2}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\langle\bm{q}_{0}\times\bm{n}-\bm{q}_{b}\times\bm{n},\bm{\psi}_{0}\times\bm{n}-\bm{\psi}_{b}\times\bm{n}\rangle_{\partial T},

and
s_{2}
is defined accordingly in the space
M_{h}
as follows

\displaystyle s_{2}(s_{h};r)=\rho_{3}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-\gamma}\langle s_{0}-s_{b},r_{0}-r_{b}\rangle_{\partial T},

where
\gamma\geq-1
and
\rho_{i}>0
are parameters with values at user’s discretion.

The PDWG scheme (
4.2
) further provides an approximation of the space of normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector fields
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
, as revealed by Theorem
6.2
in that the difference
\bm{\eta}_{h}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}
is sufficiently close to a true normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic vector field
\bm{\eta}
. For this purpose, we introduce the following notation of discrete normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic functions.

Definition 4.1

(discrete normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic functions)
.

A vector field
\bm{\eta}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}
is said to be a discrete normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic function if there exists a vector field
{\bm{u}}\in H(div_{\varepsilon};\Omega)\cap H(curl;\Omega)
such that

(4.4)

\bm{\eta}_{h}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h},

where
{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}
is the
L^{2}
projection operator onto the finite element space
{\bm{V}}_{h}
and
{\bm{u}}_{h}
is the solution of (
4.2
) for a div-curl system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with load functions
f
,
\bm{g}
, and
\phi_{1}
determined by
{\bm{u}}
.

In practical computation, a discrete normal
\varepsilon
-harmonic function can be readily obtained from (
4.4
) by choosing a smooth vector field
{\bm{u}}
and one solving of the PDWG system (
4.2
).

5.
Solution Existence and Uniqueness

Introduce two semi-norms as follows:

(5.1)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(\lambda_{h},{\bm{q}}_{h}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=\Big(s_{1}(\lambda_{h},{\bm{q}}_{h};\lambda_{h},{\bm{q}}_{h})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\lambda_{h}\in M_{h},\ {\bm{q}}_{h}\in{\bm{W}}_{h},

(5.2)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}s_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=\Big(s_{2}(s_{h};s_{h})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad s_{h}\in S_{h}.

For simplicity, assume that
\varepsilon
is piecewise constant with respect to the partition
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
. Note that all the results can be generalized to piecewise smooth
\varepsilon
without any difficulty.

Denote by
Q_{0}
the
L^{2}
projection operator onto
P_{k}(T)
and
Q_{b}
the
L^{2}
projection operator onto
P_{k}(\sigma)
on each face
\sigma\in\partial T
. Denote by
Q_{h}
the projection operator onto the weak finite element space
S_{h}
or
M_{h}
such that

(Q_{h}w)|_{T}=\{Q_{0}w|_{T},Q_{b}w|_{{\partial T}}\}.

Analogously, we use
\mathbb{Q}_{0}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{b}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{h}
to denote the
L^{2}
projection operators onto
[P_{k}(T)]^{3}
,
G_{k}(\sigma)=[P_{k}(\sigma)]^{3}\times{\bm{n}}_{\sigma}
, and
{\bm{W}}_{h}
, respectively. The
L^{2}
projection operator onto the finite element space
{\bm{V}}_{h}
is denoted as
{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
27
]
The
L^{2}
projections
Q_{h}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}
satisfy the commutative property

(5.3)

\nabla_{w}(Q_{h}w)={{\mathcal{Q}}}_{h}(\nabla w),\qquad\forall w\in H^{1}(T),

(5.4)

\nabla_{w}\times(\mathbb{Q}_{h}{\bm{\psi}})={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}(\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}}),\qquad\forall{\bm{\psi}}\in H(curl;T).

Theorem 5.2
.

The kernel of the matrix of the PDWG finite element scheme (
4.2
) is given by

K_{h}=\{({\bm{v}}_{h},s_{h}=0,\lambda_{h}=0,{\bm{q}}_{h}=0):\ {\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{h}\cap\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)\}.

In other words, the kernel of the matrix of the PDWG scheme (
4.2
) is isomorphic to the subspace of
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
consisting of harmonic functions that are piecewise polynomial of degree
k
.

Proof.

Let
({\bm{u}}_{h},s_{h},\lambda_{h},\bm{q}_{h})
be a solution of (
4.2
) with homogeneous data. It follows that

(5.5)

\displaystyle s_{1}(\lambda_{h},\bm{q}_{h};\lambda_{h},\bm{q}_{h})=0,\quad s_{2}(s_{h},s_{h})=0,

(5.6)

\displaystyle(\bm{u}_{h},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}\varphi+\nabla_{w}\times\bm{\psi})+(\bm{\psi}_{0},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}s_{h})=0,\qquad\forall\varphi\in M_{h},\bm{\psi}\in{\bm{W}}_{h},

(5.7)

\displaystyle(\bm{q}_{0},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}r)+(\bm{v},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}\lambda_{h}+\nabla_{w}\times\bm{q}_{h})=0,\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in{\bm{V}}_{h},r\in S_{h}.

From (
5.5
) we have

(5.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{b},\;\bm{q}_{0}\times\bm{n}=\bm{q}_{b}\times\bm{n},\ s_{0}=s_{b},\ \text{ on }\partial T,

so that
\lambda_{0}\in C(\Omega)
,
s_{0}\in C(\Omega)
and
\bm{q}_{0}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
. Hence,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\nabla\lambda_{0}=\nabla_{w}\lambda_{h},\;\nabla\times\bm{q}_{0}=\nabla_{w}\times\bm{q}_{h}.

Next, by letting
r=0
and varying
\bm{v}
in (
5.7
) we have

\varepsilon\nabla_{w}\lambda_{h}+\nabla_{w}\times\bm{q}_{h}=0,

which, together with (
5.9
), implies

(5.10)

\varepsilon\nabla\lambda_{0}+\nabla\times\bm{q}_{0}=0.

From
\bm{q}_{0}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla\lambda_{0}+\nabla\times\bm{q}_{0},\nabla\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla\lambda_{0},\nabla\lambda_{0})+(\nabla\times\bm{q}_{0},\nabla\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla\lambda_{0},\nabla\lambda_{0})+\langle\bm{n}\times\bm{q}_{0},\lambda_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla\lambda_{0},\nabla\lambda_{0}).

Thus,

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla\lambda_{0},\nabla\lambda_{0})=0,

which gives
\nabla\lambda_{0}=\bm{0}
, and hence
\lambda_{0}\equiv 0
as a function with mean value 0. This further leads to
\lambda_{b}\equiv 0
. Thus, from (
5.10
) we have

\nabla\times\bm{q}_{0}=0,\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega.

Observe that
\bm{q}_{0}
satisfies

(\bm{q}_{0},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}r)=0\qquad\forall r\in S_{h},

which leads to
\bm{q}_{0}\in H(div_{\varepsilon};\Omega)
and

\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\bm{q_{0}})=0,\quad\langle\bm{q}_{0}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{i},1\rangle_{\Gamma_{i}}=0,

for
i=1,2,\cdots L
. This, together with
\nabla\times\bm{q}_{0}=0
and
\bm{q_{0}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
shows that
\bm{q}_{0}\equiv 0
, and hence
\bm{q}_{b}={\bm{n}}\times(\bm{q}_{b}\times{\bm{n}})={\bm{n}}\times 0=0
.

Next, from the Helmholtz decomposition (
A.1
), we have

\bm{u}_{h}=\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times\bm{\tilde{\psi}}+\nabla\tilde{\phi}+\bm{\tilde{\eta}},

with some
\bm{\tilde{\eta}}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
and
\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega)
satisfying
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\bm{\tilde{\psi}})=0
and
\langle\varepsilon\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{i},1\rangle_{\Gamma_{i}}=0
for
i=1,\cdots,L
.
From
s_{2}(s_{h},s_{h})=0
we have
s_{0}=s_{b}
on
{\partial T}
for each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
so that
s_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. It follows that
\nabla_{w}s_{h}=\nabla s_{0}
. If
\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
has dimension 0, then
\bm{\tilde{\eta}}=0
. In (
5.6
), by choosing the test function
\phi
and
\bm{\psi}
to be the
L^{2}
projection of the corresponding function in the Helmholtz decomposition we arrive at

(5.11)

\begin{split}0=&(\bm{u}_{h},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\tilde{\varphi}+\nabla_{w}\times\mathbb{Q}_{h}\bm{\tilde{\psi}})+(\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}s_{h})\\
=&(\bm{u}_{h},{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\varepsilon\nabla\tilde{\varphi}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla\times\bm{\tilde{\psi}})+(\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla s_{0})\\
=&(\bm{u}_{h},\varepsilon\nabla\tilde{\varphi}+\nabla\times\bm{\tilde{\psi}})+(\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla s_{0})\\
=&(\varepsilon\bm{u}_{h},\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{\tilde{\eta}})+(\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla s_{0})\\
=&(\varepsilon(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{\tilde{\eta}}),\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{\tilde{\eta}}),\end{split}

which leads to
\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{\tilde{\eta}}=0
; i.e.,
\bm{u}_{h}
is a harmonic function. As a harmonic function and piecewise polynomial of degree
k
, the first term on the left-hand side of (
5.6
) becomes to be zero for all test functions
\varphi\in M_{h}
and
\bm{\psi}\in{\bm{W}}_{h}
. It follows that
\nabla_{w}s_{h}=0
so that
\nabla s_{0}=\nabla_{w}s_{h}=0
and hence
s_{0}\equiv 0
, so is
s_{b}\equiv 0
.
∎

The following is our main result concerning the solution existence and uniqueness of the numerical scheme (
4.2
).

Theorem 5.3
.

The PDWG finite element scheme (
4.2
) has one and only one solution for all the components except
\bm{u}_{h}
. The solution
\bm{u}_{h}
is unique up to a discrete harmonic function
\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
that is a piecewise polynomial of degree
k
.

For the PDWG element of lowest order (i.e.,
k=0
), a discrete harmonic function
\bm{\eta}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
would be a piecewise constant vector field that is continuous across each interior element interface and has vanishing value on the domain boundary along the normal direction. It follows that one must have
\bm{\eta}_{h}\equiv 0
, or equivalently, the PDWG finite element scheme (
4.2
) has one and only one solution for all the components.

6.
Error Analysis

For the exact solution
\{{\bm{u}};s=0\}
of the div-curl system, we have from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that

(6.1)

\begin{split}B_{h}({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},Q_{h}s;\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})=&({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}\varphi+\nabla_{w}\times{\bm{\psi}})+({\bm{\psi}}_{0},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}Q_{h}s)\\
=&({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi_{0}+\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\\
&+\langle{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{b}-\varphi_{0})+({\bm{\psi}}_{0}-{\bm{\psi}}_{b})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
=&({\bm{u}},\varepsilon\nabla\varphi_{0}+\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\\
&+\langle{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{b}-\varphi_{0})+({\bm{\psi}}_{0}-{\bm{\psi}}_{b})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
=&-(\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{u}}),\varphi_{0})+(\nabla\times{\bm{u}},{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\\
&+\langle{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{b})+({\bm{\psi}}_{b}-{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
&+\langle{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{b}-\varphi_{0})+({\bm{\psi}}_{0}-{\bm{\psi}}_{b})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
&+\langle{\phi}_{1},\varphi_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}\\
=&\langle{\phi}_{1},{\varphi}_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}-(f,\varphi_{0})+(\bm{g},\bm{\psi}_{0})\\
&+\langle{\bm{u}}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{b})+({\bm{\psi}}_{b}-{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.\end{split}

Combining the above equation with the fact that
\lambda=0,\ \bm{q}=0
we obtain

(6.2)

\begin{split}&s_{1}(Q_{h}\lambda-\lambda_{h},\mathbb{Q}_{h}\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{h};\varphi,\bm{\psi})+B({\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h},Q_{h}s-s_{h};\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})\\
=&\langle{\bm{u}}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{b})+({\bm{\psi}}_{b}-{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.\end{split}

The second error equation can be easily obtained as follows:

(6.3)

-s_{2}(Q_{h}s-s_{h},r)+B(\bm{v},r;Q_{h}\lambda-\lambda_{h},\mathbb{Q}_{h}\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{h})=0,

where we have used the fact that
s=0,\ \bm{q}=0
, and
\lambda=0
.

Denote the error functions by

e_{{\bm{u}}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h},\ e_{s}=Q_{h}s-s_{h},\ e_{\lambda}=Q_{h}\lambda-\lambda_{h},\ e_{\bm{q}}=\mathbb{Q}_{h}\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{h}.

Theorem 6.1
.

For the numerical solution
{\bm{u}}_{h},\ s_{h},\ \lambda_{h},\ \bm{q}_{h}
arising from the PDWG scheme (
4.2
), the following estimate holds true:

(6.4)

\begin{split}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\lesssim h^{k+\theta}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{k+\theta},\end{split}

provided that
{\bm{u}}\in[H^{k+\theta}(\Omega)]^{3}
for
\theta\in(1/2,1]
.

Proof.

From (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) we have

\begin{split}s_{1}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}};e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}})+s_{2}(e_{s},e_{s})=&\langle{\bm{u}}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(e_{\lambda,0}-e_{\lambda,b})+(e_{\bm{q},b}-e_{\bm{q},0})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},\end{split}

which leads to

(6.5)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}\lesssim h^{k+\theta}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{k+\theta}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},

where
\theta\in(1/2,1]
and
k
is the order of polynomials for the finite element space
{\bm{V}}_{h}
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

To derive an estimate for the error function
e_{\bm{u}}
, we use the Helmholtz decomposition (
A.1
) to obtain a
\tilde{\phi}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\in{H}_{0}(curl;\Omega)
, and
\tilde{\bm{\eta}}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
such that

(6.6)

e_{\bm{u}}=\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times\bm{\tilde{\psi}}+\nabla\tilde{\phi}+\tilde{\bm{\eta}}.

Assume the following
H^{\alpha}
-regularity holds true for some fixed

:

(6.7)

\|\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\|_{\alpha}+\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{\alpha}\lesssim\|e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}}\|_{0}.

The following is the main convergence result of this paper.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
{\bm{u}}
be a solution of the div-curl system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Assume that the Helmholtz decomposition (
6.6
) has the
H^{\alpha}
-regularity estimate (
6.7
). For a numerical solution
{\bm{u}}_{h},\ s_{h},\ \lambda_{h},\ \bm{q}_{h}
arising from (
4.2
), there exists a harmonic function
\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
such that the following estimate holds true:

(6.8)

\begin{split}\|\varepsilon^{1/2}({\bm{u}}_{h}+\bm{\eta}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}})\|\lesssim h^{k+\theta+\alpha-1}\|{\bm{u}}\|_{k+\theta},\end{split}

provided that
{\bm{u}}\in[H^{k+\theta}(\Omega)]^{3}
for
\theta\in(1/2,1]
.

Proof.

From the first error equation (
6.2
), we have

(6.9)

\begin{split}s_{1}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}};\varphi,\bm{\psi})+B(e_{\bm{u}},e_{s};\varphi,{\bm{\psi}})=&\langle{\bm{u}}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{b})+({\bm{\psi}}_{b}-{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.\end{split}

By letting
\varphi=Q_{h}\tilde{\phi}
and
\bm{\psi}=Q_{h}\bm{\tilde{\psi}}
, we obtain from Lemma
5.1

(6.10)

\begin{split}B(e_{\bm{u}},e_{s};\varphi,\bm{\psi})=&(e_{\bm{u}},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\tilde{\phi}+\nabla_{w}\times\mathbb{Q}_{h}\bm{\tilde{\psi}})+(\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}e_{s})\\
=&(e_{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla_{w}\tilde{\phi}+{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}\nabla_{w}\times\bm{\tilde{\psi}})+(\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}e_{s})\\
=&(e_{\bm{u}},\varepsilon\nabla\tilde{\phi}+\nabla\times\bm{\tilde{\psi}})+(\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla_{w}e_{s})\\
=&(\varepsilon e_{\bm{u}},e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})+(\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\nabla_{w}e_{s})\\
=&(\varepsilon(e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}}),e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})+(\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\nabla_{w}e_{s}).\end{split}

From the definition of the weak gradient we have

\begin{split}(\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\nabla_{w}e_{s})=&(\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\nabla e_{s,0})+\langle\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,b}-e_{s,0}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
=&(\varepsilon\bm{\tilde{\psi}},\nabla e_{s,0})+\langle\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,b}-e_{s,0}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
=&-(\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\bm{\tilde{\psi}}),\nabla e_{s,0})+\langle\varepsilon\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,0}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+\langle\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,b}-e_{s,0}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
=&\langle\varepsilon\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,0}-e_{s,b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+\langle\varepsilon\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,b}-e_{s,0}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
=&\langle\varepsilon(\bm{\tilde{\psi}}-\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}})\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,0}-e_{s,b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.\end{split}

Substituting the above into (
6.10
) then (
6.9
) yields

\begin{split}\|\varepsilon^{1/2}(e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\|^{2}=&B(e_{\bm{u}},e_{s};\varphi,\bm{\psi})-\langle\varepsilon(\bm{\tilde{\psi}}-\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}})\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,0}-e_{s,b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\\
=&\langle{\bm{u}}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}},\varepsilon{\bm{n}}(\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{b})+({\bm{\psi}}_{b}-{\bm{\psi}}_{0})\times{\bm{n}}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}-s_{1}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}};\varphi,\bm{\psi})\\
&-\langle\varepsilon(\bm{\tilde{\psi}}-\mathbb{Q}_{0}\bm{\tilde{\psi}})\cdot{\bm{n}},e_{s,0}-e_{s,b}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},\end{split}

which leads to

\begin{split}\|\varepsilon^{1/2}(e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\|^{2}\lesssim&h^{\theta}\|{\bm{u}}-{\mathcal{Q}}_{h}{\bm{u}}\|_{\theta}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(\varphi,{\bm{\psi}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(\varphi,\bm{\psi}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\
&+h^{\alpha+(\gamma-1)/2}\|\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\|_{\alpha}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

It can be proved that

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(\varphi,{\bm{\psi}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\lesssim h^{\alpha-1}\|(\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\bm{\psi}})\|_{\alpha}.

It follows that

\begin{split}\|\varepsilon^{1/2}(e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\|^{2}\lesssim&h^{\theta}\|{\bm{u}}-Q_{h}{\bm{u}}\|_{\theta}h^{\alpha-1}\|(\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\bm{\psi}})\|_{\alpha}+{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}h^{\alpha-1}\|(\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\bm{\psi}})\|_{\alpha}\\
&+h^{\alpha+(\gamma-1)/2}\|\bm{\tilde{\psi}}\|_{\alpha}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}\|\varepsilon^{1/2}(e_{\bm{u}}-\tilde{\bm{\eta}})\|\lesssim&h^{\alpha+\theta-1}\|{\bm{u}}-Q_{h}{\bm{u}}\|_{\theta}+h^{\alpha-1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}(e_{\lambda},e_{\bm{q}}){|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}+h^{\alpha+(\gamma-1)/2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\\

which gives rise to the error estimate (

).
∎

7.
Numerical Experiments

In this section, we present some numerical results for the PDWG finite
element method proposed and analyzed in previous sections. For simplicity, we choose the lowest order PDWG element; i.e.,
k=0
so that the solution
\bm{u}
is approximated by discontinuous piecewise constant vector fields. The exact solution
\bm{u}
has various regularities ranging from smooth to corner singular. The computational domain
includes convex and non-convex polyhedral regions; some have cavities or multiple toroidal topology. The implementation uses an open-source and publicly available MATLAB package iFEM
[
8
]
. The computational domain is first partitioned into cubes, and each cube is further divided into 6 tetrahedra of equi-volume to form a shape-regular finite element partition. On each tetrahedral element
T
with boundary
\partial T=\cup_{i=1}^{4}F_{i}
, the local finite element space consists of functions given as follows:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{h}|_{T}\in\left[P_{0}(T)\right]^{3},

(7.2)

\displaystyle s_{h}|_{T}=\left\{s_{0},s_{b}\right\}\in\left\{P_{0}(T),\Pi_{i=1}^{4}P_{0}(F_{i})\right\},

(7.3)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}|_{T}=\left\{\varphi_{0},\varphi_{b}\right\}\in\left\{P_{0}(T),\Pi_{i=1}^{4}P_{0}(F_{i})\right\},

(7.4)

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{h}|_{T}=\left\{\bm{\psi}_{0},\bm{\psi}_{b}\right\}\in\left\{\left[P_{0}(T)\right]^{3},T_{0}(\partial T)\right\}.

Among the spaces above,
T_{0}(\partial T)
is tangetial to the boundary and is given by

T_{0}(\partial T):=\{\bm{\psi}:\bm{\psi}_{F_{i,j}}\in\left[P_{0}(F_{i})\right]^{3}\times\bm{n}_{F_{i}},\;j=1,2\text{ and }i=1,2,3,4\},

where
\bm{n}_{F_{i}}
is the outer unit normal vector to face
F_{i}
. The
basis functions for the first three spaces are straightforward. For

T_{0}(\partial T)
, on each face
F_{i}
we choose the normalized vectors representing
the directional vector of any 2 edges (
j=1,2
) among 3 on
\partial F_{i}
, such that
its weak curl is the co-normal vector of this edge with respect to
F_{i}
(
i=1,2,3,4
),

\nabla_{w}\times\left\{\bm{0},\bm{\psi}_{F_{i,j}}\right\}=3\bm{\psi}_{F_{i,j}}\times(\nabla\zeta_{i}),

where
\zeta_{i}

Appendix A
Helmholtz Decomposition

Theorem A.1
.

For any vector-valued function
{\bm{u}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}
, there exists a unique
{\bm{\psi}}\in H_{0}(curl;\Omega),\ \phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
, and
\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}=\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}}+\nabla\phi+{\bm{\eta}},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon{\bm{\psi}})=0,\ \langle\varepsilon{\bm{\psi}}\cdot{\bm{n}}_{i},1\rangle_{\Gamma_{i}}=0,\ i=1,\dotsc,L.

Moreover, the following estimate holds true

(A.3)

\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{H({\rm curl};\Omega)}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{0}\lesssim(\varepsilon{\bm{u}},{\bm{u}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

The following is a sketch of the proof.
Consider the problem of seeking
{\bm{\psi}}\in{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}
such that

(A.4)

(\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}},\nabla\times{\bm{\varphi}})=({\bm{u}},\nabla\times{\bm{\varphi}}),\quad\forall\ {\bm{\varphi}}\in{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}.

Denote by

a({\bm{\psi}},{\bm{\varphi}}):=(\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}},\nabla\times{\bm{\varphi}})

the bilinear form defined on
{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}
.
We claim that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive with respect to the

H({\rm curl};\Omega)
-norm. To this end, it suffices to derive the
following estimate

(A.5)

\|{\bm{v}}\|_{0}\lesssim\|\nabla\times{\bm{v}}\|_{0},\qquad{\bm{v}}\in{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}.

In fact, for
{\bm{v}}\in{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}
, from
Theorem 3.4 (Chapter 1) of
[
12
]
, there exists a vector
potential function
{\bm{\omega}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{3}
such that

(A.6)

\varepsilon{\bm{v}}=\nabla\times{\bm{\omega}},\ \nabla\cdot{\bm{\omega}}=0,\ \|{\bm{\omega}}\|_{1}\lesssim(\varepsilon{\bm{v}},{\bm{v}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the integration by parts and the condition
{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{n}}=0
on

\Gamma
, we have

(\varepsilon{\bm{v}},{\bm{v}})=(\nabla\times{\bm{\omega}},{\bm{v}})=({\bm{\omega}},\nabla\times{\bm{v}}).

It follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
A.6
) that

(\varepsilon{\bm{v}},{\bm{v}})\leq\|{\bm{\omega}}\|_{0}\ \|\nabla\times{\bm{v}}\|_{0}\lesssim(\varepsilon{\bm{v}},{\bm{v}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \|\nabla\times{\bm{v}}\|_{0},

which implies (
A.5
).

Now from the Lax-Milgram Theorem, there exists a unique
{\bm{\psi}}\in{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}
satisfying the equation (
A.4
) such that

\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{H({\rm curl};\Omega)}\lesssim(\varepsilon{\bm{u}},{\bm{u}}).

It is easy to see that
{\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}
is
equivalent to the following quotient space:

H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega)/(\nabla H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega))=\{{\bm{v}}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega):\ (\varepsilon{\bm{v}},\nabla\phi)=0,\ \forall\phi\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)\}.

Thus, by using a Lagrangian multiplier
p\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)
, the
problem (
A.4
) can be re-formulated as follows: Find

{\bm{\psi}}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega)
and
p\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)
such
that

(A.7)

\begin{split}(\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}},\nabla\times{\bm{\varphi}})+(\varepsilon\nabla p,{\bm{\varphi}})&=({\bm{u}},\nabla\times{\bm{\varphi}}),\quad\forall\ {\bm{\varphi}}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega),\\
({\bm{\psi}},\varepsilon\nabla s)&=0,\quad\qquad\qquad\forall\ s\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega).\end{split}

It follows from the first equation of (
A.7
) that

\nabla\times({\bm{u}}-\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}})-\varepsilon\nabla p=0.

Since
p\in H_{0c}^{1}(\Omega)
, the two terms on the left-hand
side of the above equation are orthogonal in the
\varepsilon^{-1}
-weighted

L^{2}(\Omega)
norm. Thus, we have

\nabla\times({\bm{u}}-\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}})=0,

which gives

{\bm{u}}-\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}}\in H^{0}({\rm curl};\Omega).

Thus, there exist unique
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
and

\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{H}_{\varepsilon n,0}(\Omega)
such that

{\bm{u}}-\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times{\bm{\psi}}=\nabla\phi+\bm{\eta},

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

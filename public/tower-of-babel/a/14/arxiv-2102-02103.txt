Hypergraphs with many extremal configurations Thanks: The first and second author’s research is partially supported by NSF awards DMS-1763317 and DMS-1952767.
Xizhi Liu, Dhruv Mubayi, Christian Reiher

Abstract
. For every positive integer t t we construct a finite family of triple systems ℳ t \mathcal{M}_{t} , determine its Turán number, and show that there are t t extremal ℳ t \mathcal{M}_{t} -free configurations that are far from each other in edit-distance. We also prove a strong stability theorem: every ℳ t \mathcal{M}_{t} -free triple system whose size is close to the maximum size is a subgraph of one of these t t extremal configurations after removing a small proportion of vertices. This is the first stability theorem for a hypergraph problem with an arbitrary (finite) number of extremal configurations. Moreover, the extremal hypergraphs have very different shadow sizes (unlike the case of the famous Turán tetrahedron conjecture). Hence a corollary of our main result is that the boundary of the feasible region of ℳ t \mathcal{M}_{t} has exactly t t global maxima.

Let

Recall that the

Given a family

-free

, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}d(\mathcal{H}_{k})=y
.
The feasible region unifies and generalizes many classical problems
such as the Kruskal-Katona theorem
[

9
]
and the Turán problem. It
was introduced recently in

to understand the extremal properties of

was analyzed

Moreover, there is a left-continuous almost everywhere differentiable function

g(\mathcal{F})\colon{\rm proj}\Omega(\mathcal{F})\to[0,1]

\Omega(\mathcal{F})=\bigl\{(x,y)\in[0,c(\mathcal{F})]\times[0,1]\colon 0\leq y\leq g(\mathcal{F})(x)\bigr\}\,.

Let us call

the

There are examples showing that

The stability number of

2.

In this section we present some definitions related to the Lagrangian of a hypergraph,
introduced by Frankl and Rödl in
[

,
and prove a result (Proposition

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{G}}(u,v)=\left\{w\in V(\mathcal{G})\smallsetminus\{u,v\}\colon\exists A\in\mathcal{G}\text{ such that }\{u,v,w\}\subseteq A\right\},

Denote by

For an

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{G}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{H}}\prod_{i\in E}x_{i}\quad\text{ for all }(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathds{R}}^{n}.

\displaystyle\Delta_{n-1}=\left\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}\colon x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}=1\right\}.

is compact,

to

attains a maximum value, called the

and denoted by

For a hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
the maximum number of edges in a blow-up of

Fact 2.3

Proof of Fact

Define

\displaystyle Q=\left(\frac{n}{9}-\frac{n-k}{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{2}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}d_{\mathcal{S}}(i,j)\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\quad{\rm and}\quad K=L_{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}),

.
In other words, there is some

as desired.

Second case: There exists some

Now

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha^{2}_{i}\geq 1.

s

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}d_{\mathcal{S}}(i,j)\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\leq\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}d_{\mathcal{S}}(i,j)\frac{\alpha_{i}^{2}+\alpha_{j}^{2}}{2}=s\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha^{2}_{i}.

Due to

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=M\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}\left(D_{j}+\frac{2}{9}\left(\xi_{j}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)=3L_{\mathcal{G}}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})+\frac{2}{9}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\xi_{j}-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{2}

(

∎

4.2.
Transversals

Roughly speaking, the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}-v
appearing in Lemma
4.3
arises
from an almost balanced blow-up of
\mathcal{G}_{i}
by deleting a small number of edges. When we randomly
select one vertex from each partition class of
\mathcal{H}-v
it is thus very likely that the resulting
transversal induces a copy of
\mathcal{G}_{i}
. In the proof of Lemma
4.3

there are several places where we argue similarly in situations where some vertices from
the transversals have been selected in advance. The precise statement we shall use in these cases
is Lemma
4.5
below.

Consider a 3-graph with
V(\mathcal{G})=[m]
and pairwise disjoint sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
.
The blow-up
\mathcal{G}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}]
of
\mathcal{G}
is obtained from
\mathcal{G}
by replacing each vertex
j\in[m]
with the set
V_{j}
and
each edge
\{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\}\in\mathcal{G}
with the complete
3
-partite
3
-graph
with vertex classes
V_{j_{1}}
,
V_{j_{2}}
, and
V_{j_{3}}
.
For a
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
we say that
a partition
V(H)=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{j\in[m]}V_{j}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-coloring
of

if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Fix a real
\eta\in(0,1)
and integers
m,n\geq 1
.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be a
3
-graph with vertex set
[m]
and let
\mathcal{H}
be a further
3
-graph
with
v(\mathcal{H})=n
.
Consider a vertex partition
V(\mathcal{H})=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in[m]}V_{i}
and the associated
blow-up
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}=\mathcal{G}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}]
of
\mathcal{G}
.
If two sets
T\subseteq[m]
and
S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\not\in T}V_{j}

have the properties

(

)

|V_{j}|\geq(|S|+1)|T|\eta^{1/3}n
for all
j\in T
,

(

)

|\mathcal{H}[V_{j_{1}},V_{j_{2}},V_{j_{3}}]|\geq|\widehat{\mathcal{G}}[V_{j_{1}},V_{j_{2}},V_{j_{3}}]|-\eta n^{3}
for all
\{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\}\in\binom{T}{3}
,

(

)

and
|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)[V_{j_{1}},V_{j_{2}}]|\geq|L_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}(v)[V_{j_{1}},V_{j_{2}}]|-\eta n^{2}
for all
v\in S
and
\{j_{1},j_{2}\}\in\binom{T}{2}
,

then there exists a selection of vertices
u_{j}\in V_{j}
for all
j\in[T]

such that
U=\{u_{j}\colon j\in T\}
satisfies

\widehat{\mathcal{G}}[U]\subseteq\mathcal{H}[U]
and

L_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}(v)[U]\subseteq L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)[U]
for all
v\in S
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
,
then
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}[U]=\mathcal{H}[U]
and

L_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}(v)[U]=L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)[U]
for all
v\in S
.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Choose for
j\in T
the vertices
u_{j}\in V_{j}
independently and uniformly at random
and let
U=\{u_{j}\colon j\in T\}
be the random transversal consisting of these vertices.
By
( )
‣
4.5
and
( )
‣
4.5
we have

\displaystyle{\mathds{P}}\left(\{u_{j_{1}},u_{j_{2}},u_{j_{3}}\}\not\in\mathcal{H}\right)=1-\frac{|\mathcal{H}[V_{j_{1}},V_{j_{2}},V_{j_{3}}]|}{|V_{j_{1}}||V_{j_{2}}||V_{j_{3}}|}\leq\frac{\eta n^{3}}{|V_{j_{1}}||V_{j_{2}}||V_{j_{3}}|}\leq\frac{1}{(|S|+1)^{3}|T|^{3}}

for all edges
\{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\}\in\mathcal{G}
. Similarly
( )
‣
4.5
and
( )
‣
4.5
lead to

\displaystyle{\mathds{P}}\left(\{u_{j_{1}},u_{j_{2}}\}\not\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\mid\{u_{j_{1}},u_{j_{2}}\}\in L_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}(v)\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{|L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)[V_{j_{1}},V_{j_{2}}]|}{|V_{j_{1}}||V_{j_{2}}|}\leq\frac{\eta n^{2}}{|V_{j_{1}}||V_{j_{2}}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta^{1/3}}{(|S|+1)^{2}|T|^{2}}

for all
v\in S
and all distinct
j_{1},j_{2}\in[m]
.
Therefore, the union bound reveals

\displaystyle{\mathds{P}}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{G}}[U]\not\subseteq\mathcal{H}[U]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{|T|}{3}\frac{1}{(|S|+1)^{3}|T|^{3}}<\frac{1}{6}

\displaystyle{\rm and}\qquad{\mathds{P}}\left(L_{\widehat{\mathcal{G}}}(v)\not\subseteq L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{|T|}{2}\frac{\eta^{1/3}}{(|S|+1)^{2}|T|^{2}}<\frac{1}{2(|S|+1)}\quad\text{ for every $v\in S$}.

Altogether, the probability that
U
fails to have the desired properties
is at most

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}+\frac{|S|}{2(|S|+1)}<\frac{2}{3}.

So the probability that
U
has these properties is positive.
∎

In practice the sets
U
obtained by means of Lemma

will be
2
-covered and thus they will be cores of some
subgraphs
F\in\widehat{\mathcal{K}}^{3}_{|U|}
of
\mathcal{H}
. In such situations

\mathcal{M}_{t}
-free
and in order to exploit this fact we need to know that for

the triple system

is in some sense far from being

-colorable
(see Lemma

below). The verification of this statement
requires that we take a closer look into Construction

and the observation that follows

Observation 4.6
.

The triple systems
\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{t}
have the following properties.

(

)

For
i\in[t]
and

the clique

\omega\left(L_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}(v)\right)

of the link graph

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}-\frac{k_{i}}{2}\leq\omega\left(L_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}(v)\right)\leq\frac{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}.

(

)

We have

\frac{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}-\frac{n_{i+1}-1}{k_{i+1}-1}>\frac{Q}{k_{i}^{2}}

for every
i\in[t-1]
, where

(

)

For
i\in[t]
the
3
-graph
\mathcal{G}_{i}
is regular with degree

3\lambda_{t}n_{i}^{2}

and

7n_{i}/8\leq n_{i}-3k_{i}/2\leq\delta_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{i})\leq\Delta_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{i})\leq n_{i}-k_{i}.

Proof.

Part
( )
‣
4.6
follows from the fact that due
to
\mathcal{G}_{i}=(K^{3}_{n_{i}}\smallsetminus H(\mathcal{D}_{i}))\smallsetminus\mathcal{S}_{i}

-partite Turán graph by the deletion of

edges. The proof of part

‣

is similar. For part

‣

it suffices to calculate

\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}-\frac{n_{i+1}-1}{k_{i+1}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q(k_{i}+1)-1}{k_{i}-1}-\frac{Q(k_{i+1}+1)-1}{k_{i+1}-1}

\displaystyle=(2Q-1)\left(\frac{1}{k_{i}-1}-\frac{1}{k_{i+1}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq Q\left(\frac{1}{k_{i}-1}-\frac{1}{k_{i}}\right)>\frac{Q}{k_{i}^{2}}.\qed

As indicated earlier, this has the following consequence.

If

and the triple system

Suppose first that

we know that

If

On the other hand, by Observation

If

, the

Part I. Analysis of

Claim 4.9

Set

6.

For every positive integer

we constructed a family of

that have the same Lagrangian

,
and we showed that there is a family

of

-graphs whose extremal configurations
are balanced blow-ups of

Notice that our choice of

is very close to

, which is the supremum of the

-graphs.
It would be interesting to find for every integer

the minimum value (if it exists)
of

so that

-stable family
\mathcal{F}_{t}
with

.
A result of Erdős
[

]
implies that there are no Turán densities in the

(0,2/9)
. This motivates the following question.

Problem 6.1
.

Does there exist a family
\mathcal{F}
of triple systems with
\pi(\mathcal{F})=2/9

but
\xi(\mathcal{F})\neq 1

For a family

of
r
-graphs let

be the set of abscissae of the global maxima of its feasible

can be every finite cardinal except zero.
In would be interesting to know whether

can be infinite and, in case the answer is
affirmative, there immediately arise further questions.

Problem 6.2
.

For

does there exist a non-degenerate family
\mathcal{F}
of
r
-graphs so that

g(\mathcal{F})
has infinitely many global maxima? If so, can the set

be uncountable?
Can it even contain a non-trivial interval?

Notice that if the last question on intervals has a negative answer,
then in Proposition

the assumption that

should be finite can be omitted. In fact, it is somewhat bizarre that we do not know the following.

Problem 6.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a non-degenerate family of
r
-graphs such that

is infinite.
Can it nevertheless happen that
\mathcal{F}
has finite stability number?

In a forthcoming work
[

we will show an extension of our results
about triples systems to

-global maxima.

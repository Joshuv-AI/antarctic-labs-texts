Conciseness on normal subgroups and new concise words from outer commutator words
Gustavo A. Fernández-Alcober, Matteo Pintonello

Abstract
. Let w = w ⁡ ( x 1 , … , x r ) w=w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}) be an outer commutator word. We show that the word w ⁡ ( u 1 , … , u r ) w(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}) is concise whenever u 1 , … , u r u_{1},\ldots,u_{r} are non-commutator words in disjoint sets of variables. This applies in particular to words of the form w ⁡ ( x 1 n 1 , … , x r n r ) w(x_{1}^{n_{1}},\ldots,x_{r}^{n_{r}}) , where the n i n_{i} are non-zero integers. Our approach is via the study of values of w w on normal subgroups, and in this setting we obtain the following result: if N 1 , … , N r N_{1},\ldots,N_{r} are normal subgroups of a group G G and the set of all values w ⁡ ( g 1 , … , g r ) w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}) with g i ∈ N i g_{i}\in N_{i} is finite then also the subgroup generated by these values, i.e. w ⁡ ( N 1 , … , N r ) w(N_{1},\ldots,N_{r}) , is finite.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this article is to extend the main results of the paper
[
2
]
from the case of lower central and derived words to the more general setting of outer commutator words.
More precisely, as was conjectured in
[
2
]
, we have the following two results.

Theorem A
.

Let
w=w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
be an outer commutator word.
If
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
are non-commutator words in disjoint sets of variables, then the word
w(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
is concise.
In particular, the word
w(x_{1}^{n_{1}},\ldots,x_{r}^{n_{r}})
is concise whenever
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
w=w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
be an outer commutator word.
Assume that
\mathbf{N}=(N_{1},\ldots,N_{r})
is a tuple of normal subgroups of a group
G
such that

w\{\mathbf{N}\}
is finite.
Then the subgroup
w(\mathbf{N})
is also finite.

Here we keep the notation and terminology introduced in

[
2
]
.
Recall in particular that outer commutator words are defined recursively, starting from single variables, in such a way that if
\alpha
and
\beta
are outer commutators in

different variables
, then
w=[\alpha,\beta]
is also an outer commutator.
Also, non-commutator words are the words lying outside the derived subgroup of the free group of infinite rank, i.e. whose exponent sum in at least one of the variables is not zero.

Let
w
be a word.
If we write
w=w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
, we tacitly assume that
w
uses
all
variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
.
Given a tuple
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{r})
of subsets of a group
G
, we let

w\{\mathbf{S}\}
be the set of all values
w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{r})
with

g_{i}\in S_{i}
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
, and
w(\mathbf{S})
is the subgroup generated by
w\{\mathbf{S}\}
.
If all subsets
S_{i}
are equal to
G
, we simply write
w\{G\}
and
w(G)
, respectively.
The word
w
is
concise
if whenever the set
w\{G\}
is finite in a group
G
, then also the subgroup
w(G)
is finite.

For the proofs of Theorems A and B, we rely on the same ideas that we used for the corresponding results in
[
2
]
.
The key is the following theorem on the existence of a linear series for subgroups of the form
w(\mathbf{N})
, where
w
is an outer commutator word.
Following
[
1
]
, we define the
height

\height(w)
of an outer commutator word
w
inductively, with a single variable having height 0, and with the height of
w=[\alpha,\beta]
being

(1.1)

\height(w)=1+\max\{\height(\alpha),\height(\beta)\}.

Notice that the height of an outer commutator in
r
variables will always be at most
r-1
.

For the concepts of extended word, outer commutator extension of a tuple of normal subgroups, and extension by outer commutators, which appear in the statement of the following theorem, we refer the reader to Definitions 3.1, 3.3, and 3.4 of
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
w
be an outer commutator word in
r
variables, of height
h
, and let
\mathbf{N}=(N_{1},\ldots,N_{r})
be a tuple of normal subgroups of a group
G
.
Then there exists a series

(1.2)

[w(\mathbf{N}),w(\mathbf{N})]=V_{0}\leq V_{1}\leq\cdots\leq V_{t}=w(\mathbf{N})

of normal subgroups of
G
such that, for every fixed value
i=1,\ldots,t
, the following hold:

(i)

The section
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
is the image of an extension
v_{i}(\mathbf{M}_{i})
of
w(\mathbf{N})
by outer commutators.

(ii)

The extended word
v_{i}
depends only on
w
, and not on the group
G
or on the tuple
\mathbf{N}
. Furthermore,
v_{i}
has degree either
0
, if
h=0
, or at most
h-1
otherwise.

(iii)

The procedure of construction of the outer commutator extension
\mathbf{M}_{i}
from the tuple
\mathbf{N}
depends only on
w
, and not on the group
G
or on the tuple
\mathbf{N}
.

(iv)

In the section
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
, the word
v_{i}
is linear in one component of the tuple
\mathbf{M}_{i}
.

Furthermore, the length
t
of the series is 1 if
h=0
and at most
2^{h}+2^{h-1}-1
if
h\geq 1
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by induction on
h
.
If
h=0
then
w
is a single variable, and the result is obvious.
On the other hand, if
h=1
then we may assume that
w=[x_{1},x_{2}]
, and the theorem holds by Theorem 2.9 of
[
2
]
.
More precisely, we take the series

V_{0}=\big[[N_{1},N_{2}],[N_{1},N_{2}]\big]\leq V_{1}=\big[N_{1},[N_{1},N_{2}]\big]\leq V_{2}=[N_{1},N_{2}],

and
v_{1}=v_{2}=w
,
\mathbf{M}_{1}=(N_{1},[N_{1},N_{2}])
and
\mathbf{M}_{2}=(N_{1},N_{2})
.

Now let
h\geq 2
and let us write
w=[\alpha,\beta]
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are outer commutator words.
After renaming the variables involved, we may assume that

\alpha=\alpha(x_{1},\ldots,x_{q})
and
\beta=\beta(x_{q+1},\ldots,x_{r})
.
By (
1.1
), we have
\height(\alpha),\height(\beta)\leq h-1
and the induction hypothesis applies to both

\alpha
and
\beta
.
Thus if we set
\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}=(N_{1},\ldots,N_{q})
and

\mathbf{N}^{\beta}=(N_{q+1},\ldots,N_{r})
then there exist two series

(1.3)

A_{0}=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha})]\leq\cdots\leq A_{i}\leq\cdots\leq A_{t^{\alpha}}=\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha})

and

(1.4)

B_{0}=[\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\leq\cdots\leq B_{i}\leq\cdots\leq B_{t^{\beta}}=\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})

such that the corresponding factors
A_{i}/A_{i-1}
and
B_{i}/B_{i-1}
are the images of

v_{i}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{M}^{\alpha}_{i})
and
v_{i}^{\beta}(\mathbf{M}^{\beta}_{i})
, respectively, where:

(a)

v_{i}^{\alpha}
and
v_{i}^{\beta}
are extended words of
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively, each of degree at most
h-2
.

(b)

\mathbf{M}^{\alpha}_{i}
and
\mathbf{M}^{\beta}_{i}
are outer commutator extensions of
\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{N}^{\beta}
, respectively.

(c)

The extended words
v_{i}^{\alpha}
and
v_{i}^{\beta}
, and the procedure of construction of the outer commutator extensions
\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\alpha}

and
\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\beta}
, depend only on
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively, and not on the group
G
or on the tuples
\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{N}^{\beta}
.

(d)

In the sections
A_{i}/A_{i-1}
and
B_{i}/B_{i-1}
, the words
v_{i}^{\alpha}
and
v_{i}^{\beta}
are linear in one component
of the tuples
\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\beta}
, respectively.

Also, since
h\geq 2
, we have
t^{\alpha},t^{\beta}\leq 2^{h-1}+2^{h-2}-1
.

Let us now see how to construct the series for
w
and for the tuple
\mathbf{N}
from the two series (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).
As we will see, the length
t
of this new series will be

t=t^{\alpha}+t^{\beta}+1\leq 2^{h}+2^{h-1}-1,

as desired.

We start by taking the commutator of all terms of the series (
1.3
) with
\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})
.
This way we obtain the series

(1.5)

[A_{0},\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\leq\cdots\leq[A_{i},\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\leq\cdots\leq[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]=w(\mathbf{N}).

By P. Hall’s Three Subgroup Lemma, we have

\begin{split}[A_{0},\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]&=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\\
&\leq[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta}),\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha})]=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),w(\mathbf{N})].\end{split}

Now we multiply all terms of the series (
1.5
) by

[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),w(\mathbf{N})]
, and this is the rightmost part of the series we are seeking (where
t=t^{\alpha}+t^{\beta}+1
, as above):

(1.6)

V_{t^{\beta}+1}=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),w(\mathbf{N})]\leq\cdots\leq V_{t^{\beta}+i+1}=[A_{i},\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\,[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),w(\mathbf{N})]\leq\cdots\leq V_{t}=w(\mathbf{N}).

For every
i=1,\ldots,t^{\alpha}
, the factor
V_{t^{\beta}+i+1}/V_{t^{\beta}+i}
in this series is the image of the subgroup

[v_{i}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{M}^{\alpha}_{i}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})],

which can be represented in the form

v_{t^{\beta}+i+1}(\mathbf{M}_{t^{\beta}+i+1})
by taking

v_{t^{\beta}+i+1}=[v_{i}^{\alpha},\beta(x_{q+1},\ldots,x_{r})]

and defining
\mathbf{M}_{t^{\beta}+i+1}
to be the concatenation of
\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{N}^{\beta}
, where the elements of
\mathbf{N}^{\beta}
occupy the positions
corresponding to the variables
x_{q+1},\ldots,x_{r}
.
Notice that
v_{t^{\beta}+i+1}
is an extended word of
w
of the same degree as
v_{i}^{\alpha}
, so at most
h-2
, since

\beta(x_{q+1},\ldots,x_{r})
does not involve any variables from the set
Y
.
Furthermore,
v_{t^{\beta}+i+1}
only depends on
w
, and

\mathbf{M}_{t^{\beta}+i+1}
is an outer commutator extension of
\mathbf{N}
whose construction only depends on
w
.
Of course,
v_{t^{\beta}+i+1}
and
\mathbf{M}_{t^{\beta}+i+1}
will also depend on
i
, but we are working with a fixed value of
i
.

In a symmetric way, by first taking the commutator of
\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha})
with all terms of the series (
1.4
) and then multiplying by

[w(\mathbf{N}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]
, we get the series

(1.7)

U_{t^{\alpha}+1}=[w(\mathbf{N}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\leq\cdots\leq U_{t^{\alpha}+i+1}=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),B_{i}]\,[w(\mathbf{N}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\leq\cdots\leq U_{t}=w(\mathbf{N}).

Following the notation in Definition 3.1 of
[
2
]
, suppose that the variables from the set
Y
that have been used to construct
the extended words
v_{i}^{\beta}
are contained in the set
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}\}
.
Then the factor
U_{t^{\alpha}+i+1}/U_{t^{\alpha}+i}
of this last series can be generated by the image of the subgroup

u_{t^{\alpha}+i+1}(\mathbf{L}_{t^{\alpha}+i+1})
, where

u_{t^{\alpha}+i+1}=[\alpha(y_{s+1},\ldots,y_{s+q}),v_{i}^{\beta}]

and
\mathbf{L}_{t^{\alpha}+i+1}
is the concatenation of
\mathbf{M}_{i}^{\beta}
and

\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}
, where the elements of
\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}
occupy the positions
corresponding to the
q
variables
y_{s+1},\ldots,y_{s+q}
.
Note that
u_{t^{\alpha}+i+1}
is an extended word of
\beta(x_{q+1},\ldots,x_{r})
of degree at most
h-1
that only depends on
w
.

Now we take the commutator of
\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha})
with the terms of the last series, and subtract
t^{\alpha}
to all indices, getting

(1.8)

Z_{1}=\big[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),[w(\mathbf{N}),\beta(\mathbf{N}^{\beta})]\big]\leq\cdots\leq Z_{i}=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),U_{t^{\alpha}+i}]\leq\cdots\leq Z_{t^{\beta}+1}=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),w(\mathbf{N})].

Finally, we define
V_{0}=[w(\mathbf{N}),w(\mathbf{N})]
and multiply all terms of (
1.8
) by this subgroup, setting

V_{i}=Z_{i}V_{0}
for
i=1,\ldots,t^{\beta}+1
.

Since
V_{0}\leq[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),w(\mathbf{N})]
, we get the series

(1.9)

V_{0}=[w(\mathbf{N}),w(\mathbf{N})]\leq\cdots\leq V_{i}=Z_{i}[w(\mathbf{N}),w(\mathbf{N})]\leq\cdots\leq V_{t^{\beta}+1}=[\alpha(\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}),w(\mathbf{N})].

In this series, the factors
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
for
i=2,\ldots,t^{\beta}+1
are given by the images of the subgroups
v_{i}(\mathbf{M}_{i})
, where

v_{i}=[\alpha(x_{1},\ldots,x_{q}),u_{t^{\alpha}+i}]

is an extended word of
w
of degree at most
h-1
, and
\mathbf{M}_{i}
is the concatenation of
\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{L}_{t^{\alpha}+i}
, where
the elements of
\mathbf{N}^{\alpha}
occupy the positions corresponding to the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{q}
.
On the other hand, the quotient
V_{1}/V_{0}
is given by the image of
v_{1}(\mathbf{M}_{1})
, where

v_{1}=[\alpha(x_{1},\ldots,x_{q}),[y_{1},\beta(x_{q+1},\ldots,x_{r})]]

is an extended word of
w
of degree
1
, and
\mathbf{M}_{1}=(\mathbf{N},w(\mathbf{N}))
, with
w(\mathbf{N})
corresponding to
y_{1}
.

Now the concatenation of (
1.6
) and (
1.9
) is the desired series for
w
and
\mathbf{N}
.
The discussion of the previous paragraphs shows that
v_{i}(\mathbf{M}_{i})
is an extension of
w(\mathbf{N})
for every
i=1,\ldots,t
.
Thus we only need to check linearity of every word
v_{i}
in one component of the vector
\mathbf{M}_{i}
.
For
i=t^{\beta}+1,\ldots,t
, if
v_{i}^{\alpha}
is linear in a component of
\mathbf{M}^{\alpha}_{i}
in the section
V_{i}^{\alpha}/V_{i-1}^{\alpha}
,
then
v_{i}
is linear in the same component of
\mathbf{M}_{i}
in the section
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
, by Lemma 2.8 of
[
2
]
.
For
i=1,\ldots,t^{\beta}
, we can use similarly the linearity in the section
V_{i}^{\beta}/V_{i-1}^{\beta}
.
Finally for
i=1
, since
V_{0}=[w(\mathbf{N}),w(\mathbf{N})]
we have linearity in the component corresponding to
y_{1}
, which takes values in
w(\mathbf{N})
.
∎

Note that Theorem 1.1 generalizes Theorem 3.5 of
[
2
]
, where we provided a series like (
1.2
) for every derived word

\delta_{k}
, of length exactly
2^{k}+2^{k-1}-1
.
Since
\delta_{k}
is of height
k
, the bound for the length of the series given in Theorem 1.1 is attained for derived words.

We can now prove the corresponding version of Theorems 2.11 and 3.6 of
[
2
]
for an arbitrary outer commutator word
w
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
w
be an outer commutator word in
r
variables and let

\mathbf{N}=(N_{1},\ldots,N_{r})
be a tuple of normal subgroups of a group
G
.
Assume that
N_{i}=\langle S_{i}\rangle
for every
i=1,\ldots,r
, where:

(i)

S_{i}
is a normal subset of
G
.

(ii)

There exists
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that all
n_{i}
th powers of elements of
N_{i}
are contained in
S_{i}
.

If for the tuple
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{r})
the set of values
w\{\mathbf{S}\}
is finite of order
m
, then the subgroup
w(\mathbf{N})
is also finite and
of
(m,r,n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
-bounded order.

Proof.

Let us consider the series

[w(\mathbf{N}),w(\mathbf{N})]=V_{0}\leq V_{1}\leq\cdots\leq V_{t}=w(\mathbf{N})

of
Theorem

1.1
.
We have proved that the length
t
of this series is either
1
or less than equal to
2^{h}+2^{h-1}-1
, where
h
is the height of the outer commutator word
w
.
So in any case we have
t\leq 2^{r}-1
, since
h\leq r-1
.
We prove that every
V_{i}
is finite of bounded order by induction on
i
.
The result for
i=0
follows from Lemma 2.10 of
[
2
]
.
Assume now that
i\geq 1
and that the result is true for
V_{i-1}
.
By
Theorem

1.1
, the section
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
coincides with the image of a subgroup
v_{i}(\mathbf{M}_{i})
that is an extension of
w(\mathbf{N})
of degree at most
h
in any case.

Let
\mathbf{M}_{i}=(M_{1},\ldots,M_{s})
, which is an outer commutator extension of
\mathbf{N}
.
Hence
s\geq r
and for every
j=1,\ldots,s
we have
M_{j}=w_{j}(\mathbf{N}_{j})
, where
w_{j}
is an outer commutator word, all components in
\mathbf{N}_{j}
belong to
\mathbf{N}
, and one of these components must be
N_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r
.

Let
T_{j}=w_{j}\{\mathbf{S}_{j}\}
, where
\mathbf{S}_{j}
is obtained from
\mathbf{N}_{j}
by replacing each subgroup
N_{\ell}
with its given generating set
S_{\ell}
.
Hence
T_{j}
is a normal subset of
G
that generates
M_{j}
.
Also, for a given
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, the subset
S_{j}
appears as a component of
\mathbf{S}_{j}
and, since
\mathbf{S}_{j}
consists of normal subsets of
G

by condition (i), it follows from Lemma 2.5 of
[
2
]
that
T_{j}\subseteq S_{j}^{*q}
, where
q
is the number
of variables used by
w_{j}
.
Now recall from
Theorem

1.1
that the word
v_{i}
(and hence also the number
s
of variables of
v_{i}
) and the words
w_{1},\ldots,w_{s}
only depend on
w
, and not on
G
or on
\mathbf{N}
.
Since there are only finitely many outer commutator words in
r
variables, there is a function

f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
T_{j}\subseteq S_{j}^{\ast f(r)}
for every
j=1,\ldots,r
.
If we set
\mathbf{T}=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{s})
then, by Lemma 3.2 of
[
2
]
, we get
v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}\}\subseteq w\{\mathbf{S}\}^{\ast n}
, where

n=f(r)^{r}2^{h}\leq f(r)^{r}2^{r-1}.

Consequently

(1.10)

|v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}\}|\leq(2m+1)^{n},

and
v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}\}
is finite of
(m,r)
-bounded cardinality.
On the other hand, it follows from Lemma 2.1 of
[
2
]
that
v_{i}(\mathbf{M}_{i})
can be generated by the set of values
v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}\}
.

From
Theorem

1.1
, we know that the word
v_{i}
is linear in some position
j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
of the tuple
\mathbf{M}_{i}
modulo
V_{i-1}
.
Since
M_{j}=w_{j}(\mathbf{N}_{j})
is as above, we have
M_{j}\leq N_{\ell}
for some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, and actually
\ell=j

if
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
Now, from linearity, for every tuple
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{s})\in\mathbf{T}
and every
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(1.11)

v_{i}(\mathbf{t})^{\lambda n_{\ell}}=v_{i}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{j},\ldots,t_{s})^{\lambda n_{\ell}}\equiv v_{i}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{j}^{\lambda n_{\ell}},\ldots,t_{s})\pmod{V_{i-1}}.

We have
t_{j}^{\lambda}\in M_{j}\leq N_{\ell}
and then, by (ii) in the statement of the theorem,
t_{j}^{\lambda n_{\ell}}\in S_{\ell}
.
So we get

v_{i}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{j}^{\lambda n_{\ell}},\ldots,t_{s})\in v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}_{j}\},

where
\mathbf{T}_{j}
is the tuple obtained from
\mathbf{T}
after replacing
T_{j}
with
S_{\ell}
.
Similarly to (
1.10
) and taking into account that
\ell=j
if
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, it follows that the set
v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}_{j}\}
is finite of
(m,r)
-bounded cardinality.
By (
1.11
) there exist
(m,r)
-bounded integers
\lambda
and
\mu
, with
\lambda\neq\mu
, such that

v_{i}(\mathbf{t})^{\lambda n_{\ell}}\equiv v_{i}(\mathbf{t})^{\mu n_{\ell}}\pmod{V_{i-1}}.

This implies that
v_{i}(\mathbf{t})
has finite order modulo
V_{i-1}
, bounded in terms of
m
,
r
and
n_{\ell}
.

Summarizing, the abelian quotient
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
is the image of the verbal subgroup
v_{i}(\mathbf{M}_{i})
, which is generated by the set
v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}\}

of
(m,r)
-bounded cardinality, and each element of
v_{i}\{\mathbf{T}\}
has
(m,r,n_{\ell})
-bounded order.
We conclude that the order of
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
is
(m,r,n_{\ell})
-bounded, which completes the proof.
∎

We can now obtain Theorems A and B as special cases of this last result.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
w
be an outer commutator word in
r
variables and let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
be non-commutator words.
Then the word
w(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
is boundedly concise.
In particular,
w(x_{1}^{n_{1}},\ldots,x_{r}^{n_{r}})
is boundedly concise for all
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
w
be an outer commutator word in
r
variables and let
\mathbf{N}=(N_{1},\ldots,N_{r})
be a tuple of normal subgroups of a group
G
.
If
w\{\mathbf{N}\}
is finite of order
m
, then the subgroup

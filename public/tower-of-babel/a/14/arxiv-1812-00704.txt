Non-standard limits of graphs and some orbit equivalence invariants
Alessandro Carderi, Damien Gaboriau, Mikael de la Salle

Abstract
We consider probability measure preserving discrete groupoids, group actions and equivalence relations in the context of general probability spaces. We study for these objects the notions of cost, β \beta -invariant and some higher-dimensional variants. We also propose various convergence results about ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -Betti numbers and rank gradient for sequences of actions, groupoids or equivalence relations under weak finiteness assumptions. In particular we connect the combinatorial cost with the cost of the ultralimit equivalence relations. Finally a relative version of Stuck-Zimmer property is also considered.

Introduction

Limits of discrete structures have attracted a lot of interest in the last decade.
We are interested in sequences of probability measure preserving (p.m.p.) group actions, of graphs or of graphings and in the asymptotic behavior of such invariants as the cost or the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers.
In the course of our investigations we were led to consider graphed p.m.p. discrete groupoids (in short p.m.p. groupoids, see Definition
2.4
) and to introduce the ultraproducts
(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}},\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}})
of sequences of such objects. The use of a non principal ultrafilter
\mathfrak{u}
has the advantage of ensuring convergence
and the existence of limit objects,
strongly related to more standard objects when some extra assumption provides forms of convergence.
Some other authors have considered a study of sequences of group actions via ultraproducts (see for instance

[
Ele10b
,
CKTD13
,
Car15
,
AF16
,
AP18
]
).
The counterpart in our treatment is that we have been led to develop some orbit equivalence theory in the framework of groupoids on non-standard probability spaces.

The cost of p.m.p. countable equivalence relations with countable classes is defined in terms of graphings (see
[
Lev95
,
Gab00
]
) and is an analogue of the rank of a group.
The extension to p.m.p.
s
-countable groupoids
is quite straightforward and has been considered by several authors (on standard Borel spaces) for instance by
[
Ued06
,
Car11
,
AN12
,
Tak15
]
.
The extension to the non-standard setting is also straightforward.
A graphing
\Phi=(A_{i})_{i\in I}
(Definition
2.15
) is a collection of bisections indexed by
I=\mathbb{N}
or some
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
and the
(groupoid) cost
of the p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is the infimum of the costs of its generating graphings:

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G})=\inf\left\{\mathcal{C}(\Phi)\colon\Phi\text{ generating graphings of }\mathcal{G}\right\}.

(1)

It is the infimum of the “Haar” measure of the measurable generating subsets of
\mathcal{G}
.
When
\mathcal{G}
is produced by some p.m.p. action
\Gamma\curvearrowright^{a}(X,\mu)
of a countable group, one denotes the cost of
\mathcal{G}
by
\mathcal{C}(a)
, not to be confused with the cost of the underlying equivalence relation when the action is not free!
Another possible source of confusion is the following. A countable group
\Gamma
is also a p.m.p. groupoid. As such, its cost is the infimum of the cardinal of a set of generators. In
[
Gab00
]
and several further references, the cost of a group was defined as the infimum of the costs of all its (standard) free p.m.p. actions. This latter invariant will subsequently be denoted by
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)
with a subscript
*
for the infimum. We observe in Corollary
4.1
(3) that
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)
is indeed an infimum over all p.m.p. actions, free or not, standard or not. The supremum of the costs of all free (standard) p.m.p.
\Gamma
-actions will be denoted by
\mathscr{C}^{*}(\Gamma)
.
The group
\Gamma
has fixed price when
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)=\mathscr{C}^{*}(\Gamma)
. We introduce the analogous “fixed price problem” (Question
3.25
) for p.m.p. groupoids.

A key technical result in
[
Gab98
, Étape 1]
and
[
Gab00
, Proposition IV.35]
claims that the cost of a p.m.p. equivalence relation can be computed inside a given Lipschitz class of (finite cost) graphings. The same holds for p.m.p. groupoids:
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G})=\inf_{L}\mathcal{C}_{L}(\mathcal{G},\Phi)
where the
L
-
Lipschitz cost

\mathcal{C}_{L}(\mathcal{G},\Phi)

of the graphed groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\Phi)
is the infimum of the costs of all graphings of
\mathcal{G}
which are
L
-Lipschitz equivalent to
\Phi

(see Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.9
).

Of course, the speed of convergence of
\mathcal{C}_{L}(\mathcal{G},\Phi)
to
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G})
as a function of
L
depends on the particular graphed groupoid we are considering. This dependance is what makes the spice of the notion of combinatorial cost for a sequence of graphings (initially introduced by G. Elek
[
Ele07
]
in the combinatorial context of sequences of finite graphs): one works with a uniform Lipschitz scale
L
along the sequence (thus obtaining limits of
\mathcal{C}_{L}(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi)
) and then one let the Lipschitz constant
L
vary.
If
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of graphed p.m.p. groupoids of bounded size, we define the
\mathfrak{u}
-
combinatorial cost
(Definition
3.7
) of the sequence as

c\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{u}}((\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n}):=\inf_{L}\lim_{n\in\mathfrak{u}}\mathcal{C}_{L}(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n}).

The Lipschitz framework is appropriate for uniformly bounded sequences of graphings (Section
3.1
).
For the general unbounded setting, we introduce the more appropriate notions of coarse structure (Section
2.4
) and coarse equivalence (Section
3.2
) for p.m.p. groupoids, inspired by Roe’s topological analogues
[
Roe03
]
and we define the
\mathfrak{u}
-combinatorial cost in that context (Definition
3.11
).

For group actions, our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. For every sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
of p.m.p.
\Gamma
-actions we have:

\mathcal{C}(a_{\mathfrak{u}})=c\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{u}}((a_{n})_{n})\geq\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mathcal{C}(a_{n})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}(a_{n})\geq\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma).

(2)

Moreover if the action
a_{\mathfrak{u}}
is essentially free we have
\mathscr{C}^{*}(\Gamma)\geq\mathcal{C}(a_{\mathfrak{u}})
and hence if
\Gamma
has fixed price we obtain

\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mathcal{C}(a_{n})=c\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{u}}((a_{n})_{n})=\mathcal{C}(a_{\mathfrak{u}})=\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)
.

We observe that this continuity statement fails when one removes the finite generation assumption (see Remark
3.15
).

This Theorem is the specialization to group actions of the following more general statement for graphed groupoids:

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.13
)
.

If a sequence of p.m.p. graphed groupoids
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})
and its ultraproduct groupoid
(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}},\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}})
satisfy
\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mathcal{C}(\Phi_{n})=\mathcal{C}(\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}})<\infty
(i.e. finite cost and “no loss of mass at infinity”), then

c\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{u}}((\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n})=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}})\geq\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{n}).

This allows us to interpret the original Elek’s combinatorial cost
[
Ele07
]
(defined as
c\mathcal{C}((\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n}):=\inf_{L}\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}_{L}(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})
see Remark
3.16
, Formula (
21
)) in terms of cost for ultraproducts of graphed groupoids.

Corollary 1.3

(Combinatorial cost vs cost (Corollary
3.17
))
.

If
(G_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of finite graphs with uniformly bounded degree, then the combinatorial cost is the infimum over all non principal ultrafilters of the costs of the ultraproduct groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}
of the associated
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})
:

c\mathcal{C}((G_{n})_{n})=\inf\{\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}):\mathfrak{u}\textrm{ non principal ultrafilter}\}.

We now concentrate on sofic groups, a class of groups
defined in terms of ultraproducts of finite structures (see Section
3.6
).

Corollary 1.4

(Corollary
4.4
)
.

If
\Gamma
is a sofic group finitely generated by
S
and
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n}

is a sofic approximation to
\Gamma
(with
S

\mathscr{C}^{*}(\Gamma)\geq\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}})=c\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{u}}((\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n})\geq\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{n})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{n})\geq 1.

(3)

If
\Gamma
has moreover fixed price
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)
, then

\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)=c\mathcal{C}((\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n})
and hence if
\Gamma
has fixed price
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)=1
, we obtain
c\mathcal{C}((\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{n})=1
.

Farber sequences
are sequences of finite index subgroups
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
of
\Gamma
giving particular instances of sofic approximations via the natural actions
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}
(see Section
4.1
).
If
\Gamma
is finitely generated by
S
, we denote by
\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{n},S)
the associated Schreier graph.

Corollary 1.5

(Corollary
4.9
)
.

If
\Gamma
is finitely generated by
S
and
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
is a nested Farber sequence, then

\mathcal{C}(a_{\mathfrak{u}})=c\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{u}}(a_{n})=c\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{n},S)_{n})=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}(a_{n})
.

Corollary 1.6

(Corollary
4.11
)
.

If
\Gamma
is finitely generated by
S
, has fixed price
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)
, and
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
is any
(non necessarily nested) Farber sequence, then
we have:

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{rank}(\Gamma_{n})-1}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}+1=c\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{n},S))=\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma).

This corollary is a far reaching extension of a theorem of Abért and Nikolov
[
AN12
, Theorem 1]
(nested and Farber sequences) for which we provide an alternative proof for (nested and) non-necessarily Farber sequences of finite index subgroups – see Corollary

The convergence of the sequence in Corollary

[

This also gives a converse to
[

where an inequality

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{rank}(\Gamma_{n})-1}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}+1\leq c\mathcal{C}(\mathrm{Sch}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{n},S))
was obtained.
The non necessarily fixed price case is considered in Theorem

These corollaries apply potentially to plenty of groups.
Examples of groups with fixed price

include infinite amenable groups, infinite-conjugacy-class (icc) inner amenable groups, direct products

\Gamma

(this includes Mapping Class Groups

We introduce some terminology. A countable group
\Gamma
is called a
Stuck-Zimmer group

if any p.m.p. aperiodic
\Gamma
-action on a standard Borel space has a.s. finite stabilizers.
The key point about Stuck-Zimmer groups
\Gamma
in our context, as observed in
[
ABB+17
]
, is
that under mild divergence assumptions, all sequences
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
of finite index subgroups give indeed Farber sequences (see Theorem

).
If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated Stuck-Zimmer group and

[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]\to\infty
, then
for every torsion free subgroup
\Lambda<\Gamma
, the induced action
\Lambda\curvearrowright\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}
defines a sofic approximation of
\Lambda
.
Thus when
\Gamma
is torsion free and has fixed price
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)
, Corollary

\lim_{n}\frac{\mathrm{rank}(\Gamma_{n})-1}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=\mathscr{C}_{*}(\Gamma)-1

We introduce a relative version of Stuck-Zimmer groups for which the mild divergence assumption concerns some subgroup
N<\Gamma
. We obtain a general result (Theorem
4.15
) which specializes to the following:

Theorem 1.7

(See Theorem
4.18
)
.

Let
\Lambda<\mathrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
be a finite index subgroup and
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
be any sequence of finite index subgroups of

such that
[\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\Gamma_{n}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty

Then
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}
defines a sofic approximation of
\Gamma
and

\lim_{n}\frac{\left(\mathrm{rank}(\Gamma_{n})-1\right)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=0.

(4)

This applies more generally for
\Lambda<\mathrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{Z})
that admits no
\Lambda
-invariant finite union of infinite index subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(see Theorem
4.18

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\Lambda
be any subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})

Any sequence
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
of finite index subgroups of
\Gamma:=\Lambda\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\Gamma

\lim_{n}\frac{\left(\mathrm{rank}(\Gamma_{n})-1\right)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=0.

(5)

This holds for instance for the amenable group

\Gamma=\Lambda\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&2\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}

Lück approximation theorem
[
Lüc94
]
considers a descending sequence
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
of finite index normal subgroups with trivial intersection of a countable group
\Gamma
which acts freely cocompactly on a simplicial complex
L
and it relates the Betti numbers of
\Gamma_{n}\backslash L
to the
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers of the action:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\Gamma\curvearrowright L).

(6)

Bergeron-Gaboriau
[
BG04
]
have interpreted this convergence as a continuity result for
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers of projective systems of fields of simplicial complexes for equivalence relations.
This allowed them to extend the framework so as to encompass non-normal subgroups and non trivial intersection descending sequences of subgroups. They established the convergence of the sequence and identified the limit as the
\ell^{2}
-Betti number of a laminated space
[
BG04
, Théorème 0.2, Théorème 3.1]
.

Our third main result generalizes this continuity statement in several ways: we extend it from equivalence relations to groupoids, from finite quotients to sofic and we consider arbitrary limits instead of projective ones. The use of ultrafilters forces a kind of convergence of the objects as fields of simplicial complexes with groupoid actions and we identify the limits.
We state our result under some boundedness assumption (see Section
5
, see Theorem
5.15
for the version without the boundedness assumption):

Theorem 1.9

(Theorem
5.9
)
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})
be a
sofic
graphed p.m.p. groupoid over
X_{n}
, let
\Sigma_{n}
be a
(\Phi_{n},L)
-uniformly locally bounded
\mathcal{G}_{n}
-simplicial complex for some fixed
L\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\Sigma_{n},\mathcal{G}_{n})=\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}).

In particular if the sequence of
\mathcal{G}_{n}
-simplicial complexes
\Sigma_{n}
is asymptotically uniformly
k
-connected (see Proposition
5.8
), then for every
i\leq k

\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\Sigma_{n},\mathcal{G}_{n})=\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}).

Our approach with ultraproducts allows us to relate the Laplace operators for the finite objects to that of our limit object (compare
[
ES05
, Proposition 6.1]
or
[
Tho08
]
where the finite induced operators do not clearly appear as Laplace operators). Observe however that we make a crucial use of a Lemma of Thom
[
Tho08
]
(see Lemma
5.12
).
The above theorem is also related to a (recent and independent) work of Schrödl
[
Sch18
]
on
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers and Benjamini-Schramm convergence (see also
[
Ele10a
]
). Indeed,
similarly to the case of local-global convergence of graphs (Remark

5.7
and Remark

As a corollary of the above theorem, we obtain the following geometric generalization of Lück approximation Theorem for (non-nested, non normal) sequences of subgroups:

Corollary 1.10

(Corollary
5.10
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group acting freely cocompactly on a simplicial complex
L
. Let
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
be a (non necessarily nested) Farber sequence of finite index subgroups. Then for every

we have:

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\Gamma\curvearrowright L).

In particular, if
L
is
k
-connected, then for every
i\leq k
we have:

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n})}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\Gamma).

More generally, we obtain the following generalization of
[
BG04
, Theorem 3.1]
for non nested, non Farber sequences:

Corollary 1.11

(Corollary
5.11
)
.

Let
(\Gamma_{n})_{n}
be any sequence of finite index subgroups of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
.
Assume
\Gamma
acts freely cocompactly on the simplicial complex
\Sigma
.
Let
a_{\mathfrak{u}}:\Gamma\curvearrowright X_{\mathfrak{u}}
be the ultraproduct of the actions
a_{n}:\Gamma\curvearrowright\Gamma/\Gamma_{n}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{u}}:=\{(x,\gamma)\in X_{\mathfrak{u}}\times\Gamma:\gamma x=x\}

\lim\limits_{n\in\mathfrak{u}}\frac{b_{i}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash\Sigma)}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=\beta^{(2)}_{i}(\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{u}}\backslash\left(X_{\mathfrak{u}}\times\Sigma\right),{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathfrak{u}}),

where
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathfrak{u}}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{u}}

We indicate now how these approximation results must be modified when the boundedness assumptions are not satisfied.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually finite group and let
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nested sequence of finite index normal subgroups with trivial intersection.
Lück approximation theorem
\frac{b_{1}(\Gamma_{n})}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}\to\beta_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma)
does not hold in general when
\Gamma
is not finitely presented, as examplified by torsion groups (thus
b_{1}(\Gamma_{n})=0
) with positive first
L^{2}
-Betti number
[
LO11
]
.
This defect can be fixed by taking a double limit as exposed below and this idea will then be pushed to the framework of ultraproduct (Theorem
5.15
).

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
\Gamma=\langle\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\cdots,\gamma_{d}|r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{k},\cdots\rangle
be a presentation of
\Gamma
with finitely many generators. Let
L
be the associated (
2
-dimensional) Cayley
\Gamma
-complex and let
L_{j}
be the
\Gamma
-invariant co-compact sub-complex associated with the first
j
relations
r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{j}
.
Then

\beta_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{1}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{j})}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}.

The substance of the counter-examples of
[
LO11
]
relies on the fact that these limits cannot be exchanged. Indeed on the one hand,
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{1}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{j})}{[\Gamma:\Gamma_{n}]}=\beta_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma\curvearrowright L_{j})
by Lück approximation theorem
[
Lüc94
]
, and
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma\curvearrowright L_{j})
decreases with
j
to
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(\Gamma)
. On the other hand, for every
n
, we have
b_{1}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{j})=0
for large enough
j
since the canonical surjective maps
\pi_{1}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{j})\to\pi_{1}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{k})
(for
j\leq k
) produce as direct limit
the torsion group
\Gamma_{n}
, so that
\pi_{1}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{j})
is generated by torsion elements for large enough
j
and its abelianization is thus finite.

More generally, in higher dimension, we obtain the following.

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
\Gamma
be a residually finite group and let
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nested sequence of finite index normal subgroups with trivial intersection.
If
\Gamma
acts freely on the
d
-connected simplicial complex
L
, we write
L
as an increasing union
L=\cup\nearrow L_{j}
of
\Gamma
-invariant co-compact sub-complexes

.
The inclusions
L_{j}\subset L_{k}
for

induce linear maps

H_{d}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{j},\mathbb{C})\to H_{d}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{k},\mathbb{C})
between the

Then

\beta_{d}^{(2)}(\Gamma)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lim\limits_{j\leq k,\,k\to\infty}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{Im}\Bigl(H_{d}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{j},\mathbb{C})\to H_{d}(\Gamma_{n}\backslash L_{k},\mathbb{C})\Bigr).

This proposition follows from Theorem
5.15
and the fact that, for free p.m.p. actions, the
L^{2}
-Betti numbers of the equivalence relation generated by the action agree with the ones of the group itself. The above result holds more generally for Farber sequence of subgroups.

In
[
Ele07
]
, Elek introduced another quantity for a graph sequence
(G_{n})_{n}
: the

\beta
-invariant over the field
\mathbb{K}
:

\beta_{\mathbb{K}}((G_{n})_{n}):=\inf_{q}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{|E(G_{n})|-\dim_{\mathbb{K}}V_{q}(G_{n})}{|V(G_{n})|}-1,

where
|E(G_{n})|
and
|V(G_{n})|
are the number of edges and vertices and where
V_{q}(G_{n})
is the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space spanned by the cycles of length
\leq q
in the space of
1
-chains.
Equivalently (when
|V(G_{n})|\to\infty
), this is also

\beta_{\mathbb{K}}((G_{n})_{n}):=\inf_{q}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{1}(G_{n}^{q})}{|V(G_{n})|},

where
G_{n}^{q}
is obtained from the graph
G_{n}
by gluing
2
-cells along every cycle of length
\leq q
.

Inspired by that, we introduce higher dimensional invariants for uniformly bounded vertex degrees sequences of graphs
(G_{n})_{n}
.
We consider
R^{q}(G_{n})
, the
q
-Rips complex of
G_{n}
for all integers
q

and we introduce the
d
-dimensional
\beta
-invariant
:

\beta_{d,\mathbb{K}}((G_{n})_{n}):=\lim_{q\to\infty}\lim_{p\to\infty}\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{Im}\left(H_{d}(R^{q}(G_{n}),\mathbb{K})\to H_{d}(R^{q+p}(G_{n}),\mathbb{K})\right)}{|V_{n}|}.

If
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
and
(G_{n})_{n}
has uniformly bounded degree,
Theorem
5.15
implies that

\beta_{d,\mathbb{\mathbb{Q}}}((G_{n})_{n})=\beta_{d}^{(2)}({\mathcal{R}}_{\mathfrak{u}})

where

{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathfrak{u}}
is the ultraproduct p.m.p. equivalence relation of the sequence of graphs
(G_{n})_{n}
(with well defined coarse structure, see Remark
2.33
).
In particular, if
(G_{n})_{n}
corresponds to a sofic approximation of a countable group
\Gamma
, one gets

\beta_{d,\mathbb{\mathbb{Q}}}((G_{n})_{n})=\beta_{d}^{(2)}(\Gamma)
.

We briefly mention an extension we do not consider in this version in order to limit the length of the present article.
There exists also a notion of coarse equivalence between pairs of sequences
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})_{n}
and
(\mathcal{H}_{n},\Psi_{n})_{n}
of graphed groupoids (compare
[
AF16
]
and
[
Kai17
]
which considers finite structures).
The groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}
are in particular stably orbit equivalent, so that they are related by a compression constant
\iota(\mathcal{G}_{n},\mathcal{H}_{n})
.
When the sequence of compression constants is bounded, the ultraproduct groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{u}}
are orbit equivalent (with compression constant
\iota(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{u}})=\lim\limits_{\mathfrak{u}}\iota(\mathcal{G}_{n},\mathcal{H}_{n})
) and their costs and
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers are related as the standard ones:

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{u}})-1=\iota(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{u}})(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}})-1)\ \text{ and }\beta_{j}^{(2)}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{u}})=\iota(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{u}})\beta_{j}^{(2)}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}).

2
Preliminaries

In this text we will work with probability spaces, often denoted by
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
or simply by
(X,\mu)
whenever the
\sigma
-algebra can be safely omitted from the notation. We will make no further assumption on the probability spaces and in particular we will not assume the
\sigma
-algebra to be countably generated. While working in this context one has to be more careful with some standard definitions since the classical Lusin-Novikov’s uniformization theorem and the von Neumann-Jankow selection theorem do not hold. Therefore we will carefully define all the objects we will need.

A
partial isomorphism
from the probability space
(X,\mu)
to
(Y,\nu)
is a bi-measurable bijection
\varphi:\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)\subset X\to\operatorname{tar}(\varphi)\subset Y
between measurable subsets of
X
and
Y
which preserves the measure.

Definition 2.1
.

A countable
fibred space
over
X
consists in a measure space
(\mathcal{F},\mathscr{A},\widetilde{\mu})
and a measurable map
\pi\colon\mathcal{F}\rightarrow X
such that there is a measurable subset
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\subset X\times\mathbb{N}
(for the product
\sigma
-algebra and product measure) and a bi-measurable measure preserving bijection
u\colon\mathcal{F}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
such that
u(f)\in\{\pi(f)\}\times\mathbb{N}
for every
f
.

Remark 2.2
.

By definition, countable fibred spaces are the measurable subspaces of the trivial fibred space
X\times\mathbb{N}
.
Partitioning each
A\in\mathscr{A}
into the measurable subsets
A_{i}:=A\cap X\times\{i\}
shows that:

•

the map
x\in X\mapsto|\pi^{-1}(x)\cap A|
is measurable and

\tilde{\mu}(A)=\int_{X}\left|\pi^{-1}(x)\cap A\right|d\mu(x);

(7)

•

the subset
\pi(A)=\cup_{i}\pi(A_{i})
is measurable;

•

if
|\pi^{-1}(x)\cap A|\leq 1
for almost every
x\in X
, then
\pi\bigr|_{A}:A\to\pi(A)
is a measure preserving isomorphism;

•

any measurable section
\varphi:B\subset X\to\mathcal{F}
of
\pi
is a measure preserving isomorphism between
B
and
\varphi(B)

(consider for
B_{i}:=\varphi^{-1}(X\times\{i\})
).

When
X
and
\mathcal{F}
are standard Borel spaces and
\pi\colon\mathcal{F}\to X
is a measurable map with countable fibers, the standard selection theorem of Lusin-Novikov
[
LN35
]

produce measurable sections allowing to realize
\mathcal{F}
as a Borel subset of
X\times\mathbb{N}
.
The
height

H(\mathcal{F})
of a fibred space
\mathcal{F}
is the essential supremum of the function
x\mapsto|\pi^{-1}(x)|
for
x\in X
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
has
finite height
if
H(\mathcal{F})
is finite.
The
fibred product

(\mathcal{F}_{1}*\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathscr{A},\pi)
of two countable fibred spaces

(\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathscr{A}_{1},\pi_{1})
and
(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathscr{A}_{2},\pi_{2})
over
X
is defined as usual by

(\mathcal{F}_{1},\pi_{1})*(\mathcal{F}_{2},\pi_{2}):=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\times\mathcal{F}_{2}:\ \pi_{1}(x)=\pi_{2}(y)\right\},

(in short
\mathcal{F}_{1}*\mathcal{F}_{2}
) equipped with the projection
\pi(x,y)=\pi_{1}(x)=\pi_{2}(y)
and the
\sigma
-algebra coming from the injection into
X\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
if
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are realized inside
X\times\mathbb{N}
. This is easily seen to be independent of the realizations.

2.1
Measured groupoids

The main object of study of this text will be
measured groupoids
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
groupoid

\mathcal{G}
over the set
X
is the data of

•

a set
\mathcal{G}
containing
X
; the elements of
X
are called the
units
of
\mathcal{G}
,

•

a
source map

s:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow X
and a
target map

t:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow X
which are the identity when restricted to
X
,

•

for every
g
and
h\in\mathcal{G}
such that
s(g)=t(h)
there is a
product

gh\in\mathcal{G}
satisfying
s(gh)=s(h)
and
t(gh)=t(g)
.

•

for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
there exists a unique
inverse

g^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
s(g^{-1})=t(g)
,
t(g^{-1})=s(g)
,
gg^{-1}=t(g)
and
g^{-1}g=s(g)
.

for which we require that the following identities hold

•

the product is associative, that is
g(hk)=(gh)k
whenever this is defined,

•

for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
we have that
t(g)g=gs(g)=g
.

In this text we will always assume that every groupoid is
s
-
countable
, i.e.,

•

for every
x\in X
the set
s^{-1}(x)
is countable.

Observe that the multiplication induces a map
(\mathcal{G},s)*(\mathcal{G},t)\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
p.m.p. groupoid
over
(X,\mu)
is a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
over
X
equipped with a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
and a measure
\widetilde{\mu}
such that

•

(\mathcal{G},\mathscr{A},\widetilde{\mu},s)
and
(\mathcal{G},\mathscr{A},\widetilde{\mu},t)
are both countable fibred spaces over
(X,\mu)
,

•

the maps
g\in\mathcal{G}\mapsto g^{-1}\in\mathcal{G}
and
(g,h)\in(\mathcal{G},s)\ast(\mathcal{G},t)\mapsto gh
are measurable.

In particular, the measure
\widetilde{\mu}
coincides with both fibred measures for
s
and
t
(see (
7
)).
Observe that we restrict ourselves to measured groupoids with countable fibers, a.k.a. discrete, or countable, or
r
-discrete (see
[
ADR00
]
); but we do not assume that the base space
X
is standard.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid over
X
. The
isotropy group
at
x\in X
is the group

\mathcal{G}_{x}:=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:s(g)=t(g)=x\}
. When the isotropy groups are all trivial, the groupoid is called a
principal groupoid
or an
equivalence relation
. For p.m.p. groupoids, the triviality is required only almost surely.

Example 2.5

(Group action groupoid)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group acting on a probability space
(X,\mu)
preserving the measure. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright X}:=\Gamma\times X
(with the product measured structure) is a p.m.p. groupoid over the probability space
X
(identified with the subset
\{1\}\times X
of
\Gamma\times X
) where
s(\gamma,x)=x
,
t(\gamma,x)=\gamma x
and
(\gamma,x)(\gamma^{\prime},x^{\prime})=(\gamma\gamma^{\prime},x^{\prime})
if
\gamma^{\prime}x^{\prime}=x
.

Example 2.6
.

Lusin-Novikov’s uniformization theorem
[
LN35
]
(see also
[
Kec95
, Theorem 18.10]
), implies that if
(X,\mu)
is a standard Borel space and
{\mathcal{R}}\subset X\times X
is an Borel p.m.p. equivalence relation with countable classes (see
[
FM77
]
), then
{\mathcal{R}}
is a p.m.p. principal groupoid as in Definition
2.4
where
s
and
t
are respectively the second and first coordinate projections
X\times X\to X
.

Example 2.7

(Equivalence relation associated to p.m.p. actions)
.

We warn the reader that, given a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
over
X
, the underlying equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{G}}:=\{(s(x),t(x)):x\in\mathcal{G}\}
is in general
not a p.m.p. groupoid
in the sense of Definition
2.4
, even for the equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright X}=\{(x,\gamma x):x\in X,\gamma\in\Gamma\}

coming from a p.m.p. action of a countable group
\Gamma
.

For example, consider the space
(X=[-1,1],\mathscr{B},\mu)
when
\mathscr{B}
is the
\sigma
-algebra of all Borel-measurable subsets
B
of
[-1,1]
satisfying
B=-B
, and
\mu
the normalized Lebesgue measure. Let
\Omega\subset(0,1]
be a subset which is not Lebesgue-measurable. The map
\varphi:x\mapsto-x
on
\Omega\cup-\Omega
and
x\mapsto x
on its complement is a measurable p.m.p. involution of
([-1,1],\mathscr{B},\mu)
. It defines a p.m.p. action
\alpha
of
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on
[-1,1]
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
is a p.m.p. groupoid (Example
2.5
), but

{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}=[-1,1]\cup\{(x,-x):x\in\Omega\cup-\Omega\}
is not a p.m.p. groupoid, since
x\mapsto|{\mathcal{R}}(x)|
is not measurable (see Remark
2.2
(1)).
Indeed the field of isotropy groups
x\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{x}
is not measurable in any reasonable sense.

Anticipating Definition
2.11
, the map
\varphi
is measurably isotropic but not totally isotropic. If instead,
\Omega=X
, then the corresponding action
\beta:\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is totally free but not measurably free.

However, if the fixed-point set
\mathrm{Fix}(\gamma):=\{x\in X:\gamma x=x\}
is measurable for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright X}
is a p.m.p. principal groupoid (define the
\sigma
-algebra on
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright X}
as the set of all countable unions of sets of the form
\{(x,\gamma x):x\in A\}
for
A\subset X
measurable and
\gamma\in\Gamma
; the fixed-point set condition takes care of the intersections). We warn the reader that the condition that
\mathrm{Fix}(\gamma)
is measurable is not necessary for
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright X}
to carry a p.m.p. principal groupoid structure. An example is given by
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright X=[-1,1]
is defined by
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\cdot x=(-1)^{\varepsilon_{2}}\alpha(\varepsilon_{1})x
for the action
\alpha
of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
considered above. Indeed,
\mathrm{Fix}(0,1)=\Omega\cup-\Omega
is not measurable, but
{\mathcal{R}}_{\Gamma\curvearrowright X}
is just
\{(x,\pm x):x\in X\}
and clearly carries a p.m.p. groupoid structure.

More generally given an at most countable collection of partial isomorphisms
\Phi=(\varphi_{i})_{i}
of
(X,\mu)
, we can define an equivalence relation
{\mathcal{R}}
by declaring that two points
(x,y)
in
X
are equivalent if there exists
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{l})\in\mathbb{N}^{l}
and
(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{l})\in\{-1,1\}^{l}
such that
\varphi_{i_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\ldots\varphi_{i_{l}}^{\varepsilon_{l}}(x)=y
. If moreover for every
\Phi
-word
\varphi=\varphi_{i_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\ldots\varphi_{i_{l}}^{\varepsilon_{l}}
, the fixed-point set
\{x:\varphi(x)=x\}
is measurable, that is if the associated groupoid is
realizable
, then
{\mathcal{R}}
is a p.m.p. groupoid for the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{(x,\varphi(x)):x\in A\}
where
\varphi
is a
\Phi
-word and
A\subset\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)
measurable (see Section
2.6
and
2.8
).

2.2
Full pseudogroup

From now on, let us fix a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
over
(X,\mu)
.

Definition 2.8
.

A
measurable bisection
of a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a measurable subset
\varphi\subset\mathcal{G}
such that the restriction of
s
and
t
to
\varphi
are injective.
These restrictions are partial isomorphisms (Remark
2.2
). Thus every bisection
\varphi
delivers an associated partial isomorphism
\hat{\varphi}:=t\circ(s\bigr|_{\varphi})^{-1}
of
(X,\mu)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
\varphi
and
\psi
are bisections of the p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, then the product
\varphi\psi:=\{gh:g\in\varphi,h\in\psi,s(g)=t(h)\}
is a bisection.

Proof.

We have to prove that
\varphi\psi
is a measurable subset of
\mathcal{G}
. By Remark
2.2
it is enough to show that
(s\bigr|_{\varphi\psi})^{-1}
is measurable. First observe that a point
x\in X
belongs to
s(\varphi\psi)
if and only if there is
g\in\varphi
,
h\in\psi
such that
s(h)=x
and
s(g)=t(h)=\hat{\psi}(x)
. So
s(\varphi\psi)=s(\psi)\cap\hat{\psi}^{-1}(s(\varphi))
is measurable. Moreover
(s\bigr|_{\varphi\psi})^{-1}(x)=(s\bigr|_{\varphi})^{-1}(\hat{\psi}(x))(s\bigr|_{\psi})^{-1}(x)
is measurable, as the composition of the map
x\mapsto((s\bigr|_{\varphi})^{-1}(\hat{\psi}(x)),(s\bigr|_{\psi})^{-1}(x))\in(\mathcal{G},s)\ast(\mathcal{G},t)
, which is measurable by definition of fibred products, and of the product map
(\mathcal{G},s)\ast(\mathcal{G},t)\to\mathcal{G}
which is measurable by assumption.
∎

We observe that there is a countable family
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i}
of measurable bisections which covers
\mathcal{G}
in the sense
\cup_{i}\varphi_{i}=\mathcal{G}
. Indeed, the assumption that
(\mathcal{G},s)
(respectively
(\mathcal{G},t)
) is a fibred space over
X
provides countable families
\{\varphi_{i}^{s}\}_{i}
and
\{\varphi_{j}^{t}\}_{j}
of measurable subsets which both cover
\mathcal{G}
and on which
s
(resp.
t
) is injective. The family
\{\varphi_{i}^{s}\cap\varphi_{j}^{t}\}_{i,j}
does the job. The next remark thus follows:

Remark 2.10
.

The space of units of
X\subset\mathcal{G}
is a measurable subset of
\mathcal{G}
(since
X=\cup_{i}\varphi_{i}^{-1}\varphi_{i}
), and the measures
\mu
and
\widetilde{\mu}
coincide on
X
.
For every bisection
\varphi
, its unit set
\varphi\cap X
is thus measurable (contrarily to the isotropy set
\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi)=\{g\in\varphi:s(g)=t(g)\}
, see Example
2.7
).
Similarly the product
(g,h)\in(\mathcal{G},s)\ast(\mathcal{G},t)\mapsto gh\in\mathcal{G}
maps measurable subsets to measurable subsets.

The
full pseudogroup
of
\mathcal{G}
, denoted
[[\mathcal{G}]]
, is the set of measurable bisections of
\mathcal{G}
modulo null-sets. The full pseudogroup admits a natural metric, the
L_{1}
distance, defined by
d(\varphi,\psi):=\widetilde{\mu}(\varphi\Delta\psi)
and a
trace
, the measure of the fixed points, which can be defined by the formula
\tau(\varphi):=\widetilde{\mu}(X\cap\varphi)
. It is easy to observe that this trace is the restriction of the trace of the associated
II_{1}
von Neumann algebra, see Section
2.3
.

It can be endowed with the following operations:

•

for
\varphi,\psi\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
, their
product
is
\varphi\psi=\{gh:g\in\psi,h\in\psi,s(g)=t(h)\}
;

•

for
\varphi\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
, its inverse is
\varphi^{-1}=\{g^{-1}:g\in\varphi\}
;

•

for
\varphi,\psi\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
their
intersection
is
\varphi\cap\psi
;

•

if
\varphi,\psi\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
are such that
s(\varphi)\cap s(\psi)=\emptyset
and
t(\varphi)\cap t(\psi)=\emptyset
, then the
union
or
join

\varphi\vee\psi
is the bisection
\varphi\cup\psi
.

Moreover the following relations are satisfied

•

the product is associative;

•

all the idempotents (
\varphi\varphi=\varphi
) commute;

•

for every
\varphi\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
we have
\varphi\varphi^{-1}\varphi=\varphi
.

Definition 2.11
.

A measurable bisection
\varphi
of a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is

1.

measurably isotropic
if for every measurable subset
U\subset s(\varphi)
we have
\mu(\hat{\varphi}(U)\Delta U)=0
;

2.

totally isotropic
: if
s(g)=t(g)
for
\tilde{\mu}
-almost every
g\in{\varphi}
;

3.

measurably free
if for every measurable subset
A\subset s(\varphi)
of positive measure there exists a measurable subset
B\subset A
such that
\mu(\hat{\varphi}(B)\Delta B)\not=0
;

4.

totally free
if
s(g)\neq t(g)
for
\tilde{\mu}
-almost every
g\in{\varphi}
.

In a standard Borel probability space, there is no difference between totally and measurably isotropic (resp. free).
Example
2.7
illustrates these notions.

Proposition 2.12
.

Every bisection
\varphi
of a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
admits a measurable decomposition

\varphi=\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}}(\varphi)\sqcup M_{F}(\varphi)
(unique up to a null set) such that

•

\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}}(\varphi)
is measurably isotropic;

•

M_{F}(\varphi)
is measurably free.

Moreover, the measurably free part
M_{F}(\varphi)
admits a countable measurable partition
M_{F}(\varphi)=\sqcup_{i}A_{i}
(up to a null set) such that
\mu(s(A_{i})\cap t(A_{i}))=0
and
\widetilde{\mu}(\cup_{i=1}^{n}A_{i})\geq(1-(2/3)^{n})\widetilde{\mu}(M_{F}(\varphi))
.

Observe that the isotropy set (or fixed-point set)
\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi):=\{g\in\varphi:s(g)=t(g)\}
is a possibly non-measurable subset of
\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}}(\varphi)
(see Example
2.7
).

Proof.

Define
a\leq 1
as the supremum of
\widetilde{\mu}(\cup_{i}B_{i})
over all countable union of measurable subsets of
\varphi
satisfying
\widetilde{\mu}(s(B_{i})\cap t(B_{i}))=0
. Observe that
a
is a maximum: if for every
n
we have a collection
\{C_{j}^{n}\}_{j}
satisfying
\widetilde{\mu}(\cup_{j}C_{j}^{n})\geq a-1/n
, then the collection
\{B_{i}\}_{i}=\{C_{j}^{n}\}_{j,n}
satisfies
\widetilde{\mu}(\cup_{i}B_{i})=a
. Pick such a family
\{B_{i}\}_{i}
attaining the maximum and set
F:=\varphi\setminus\cup_{i}B_{i}
.

F
is measurably isotropic for otherwise
F
would contain a measurable subset
V
such that
\widetilde{\mu}(s(V)\Delta t(V))\not=0
, and the subset
W=V\cap s^{-1}(s(V)\setminus t(V))
is non null and satisfies
\mu(s(W)\cap t(W))=0
, thus contradicting the maximality of
\widetilde{\mu}(\cup_{i}B_{i})
. Set
\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}}(\varphi):=F
.

By taking restrictions of each
B_{i}
one can assume that the family
\{B_{i}\}_{i}
is a partition of
M_{F}(\varphi):=\varphi\setminus\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}}(\varphi)
.

The next step is to modify the family so that
\widetilde{\mu}(B_{1})\geq\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{\mu}(M_{F}(\varphi))
. This is done, inductively on
i
. Set
B_{1}^{1}:=B_{1}
and inductively set
B_{i}^{\prime}=\{g\in B_{i}:s(g)\in t(B_{1}^{i-1})\textrm{ or }t(g)\in s(B_{1}^{i-1})\}
and
B_{1}^{i}:=B_{1}^{i-1}\cup(B_{i}\setminus B_{i}^{\prime})
for
i>1
. Set
B_{1}^{\prime}=\cup_{i}B_{1}^{i}
and observe that
\{B_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i}
is again a partition of
M_{F}(\varphi)
satisfying
\mu(s(B_{i}^{\prime})\cap t(B_{i}^{\prime}))=0
. Moreover,
M_{F}(\varphi)\setminus B_{1}^{\prime}
is contained in the union
(s\bigr|_{\varphi})^{-1}\circ t(B^{\prime}_{i})\cup(t\bigr|_{\varphi})^{-1}\circ s(B^{\prime}_{i})
so that
\widetilde{\mu}(B_{1}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{\mu}(M_{F}(\varphi))
.

By repeating the preceding argument inductively for all
B_{i}
, we obtain in the end that
\widetilde{\mu}(B_{i})\geq\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{\mu}(M_{F}(\varphi)\setminus\cup_{j<i}B_{j})
, which finishes the proof.

The uniqueness up to a null set is clear, as a bisection which is
simultaneously measurably isotropic and measurably free has measure

0
.
∎

2.3
Regular representations and von Neumann algebras of a groupoid

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a p.m.p. groupoid over
(X,\mu)
.

If
\varphi\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
, define a partial isometry
\lambda(\varphi)
(the left-regular representation of
[[\mathcal{G}]]
) on
L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\widetilde{\mu})
as follows. If
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\widetilde{\mu})
one defines
\lambda(\varphi)f(g):=0
if
t(g)\notin t(\varphi)
and
\lambda(\varphi)f(g):=f(h^{-1}g)
otherwise, where
h
is the unique element of
\varphi
such that
t(h)=t(g)
. For example, for
\psi\in[[\mathcal{G}]]
,
\lambda(\varphi)\chi_{\psi}=\chi_{\varphi\psi}
. The left von Neumann algebra of
\mathcal{G}
is
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{G})=\lambda([[\mathcal{G}]])^{\prime\prime}
. It carries a normal faithful trace
\tau=\langle\cdot\chi_{X},\chi_{X}\rangle
. The von Neumann subalgebra generated by the idempotents of
[[\mathcal{G}]]
(the indicator functions of measurable subsets of
X
) is isomorphic to
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
. Its commutant is the right von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})
, generated by the right-regular representation
\rho
which is characterized by
\rho(\varphi)\chi_{\psi}=\chi_{\psi\varphi^{-1}}
.

2.4
Coarse structures and graphings

We now define a notion of coarse structure on a p.m.p. groupoid, inspired by
[
Roe03
]
.

Definition 2.13
.

A
coarse structure
on a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a collection
\mathcal{E}
of measurable subsets of
\mathcal{G}
, called the
controlled sets
, which contains the units and is closed under the formation of measurable subsets, inverses, products and finite unions. A coarse structure is
generating
if
\mathcal{G}
is the union of all controlled sets.
A coarse structure on
\mathcal{G}
has
bounded geometry
if for every controlled set
E
, the measurable function
x\in X\mapsto|E\cap s^{-1}(x)|
is essentially bounded.

A
coarse structure
on a sequence
(\mathcal{G}_{n})_{n}
of p.m.p. groupoids over
X_{n}
is a collection of sequences
(E_{n})_{n}
of measurable subsets
E_{n}\subset\mathcal{G}_{n}
, called the
controlled sequences
, which is closed under the same operations. This means that if the sequences
(E_{n})_{n}
and
(F_{n})_{n}
are controlled, then
(E_{n}^{-1})_{n}
,
(E_{n}F_{n})_{n}
and
(E_{n}\cup F_{n})_{n}
are also controlled.
A coarse structure
\mathcal{E}
on
(\mathcal{G}_{n})_{n}
has
bounded geometry
if for every
(E_{n})\in\mathcal{E}
, the measurable function
n\in\mathbb{N},x\in X_{n}\mapsto|E_{n}\cap s^{-1}(x)|
is essentially bounded.

Note that, if
(\lambda_{n})_{n}
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{N}
with full support, a coarse structure on a sequence
(\mathcal{G}_{n})
is the same notion as a coarse structure on the p.m.p. groupoid
\cup_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}
over the disjoint union
\cup_{n}X_{n}
equipped with the probability measure
\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\mu_{n}
. Moreover the notions of bounded geometry agree.

We will almost always assume that the coarse structures are
countably generated
, i.e., that there is a sequence of controlled sets
E_{k}
such that every controlled set is contained in one of the
E_{k}
(respectively there is a sequence
(E_{k,n})_{n}\in\mathcal{E}
such that every controlled sequence
(E_{n})
is contained in one of the
(E_{k,n})_{n}
).

Example 2.14
.

A sequence
(G_{n})_{n}
of finite graphs with vertex set
X_{n}
defines a coarse structure on the sequence of groupoids
X_{n}\times X_{n}
(the transitive equivalence relation on the set
X_{n}
equipped with the uniform probability measure), by saying that
(E_{n})_{n}
is controlled if
\sup_{n}\sup_{(x,y)\in E_{n}}d(x,y)<\infty
. This coarse structure has bounded geometry if and only if the sequence
(G_{n})_{n}
has (uniformly) bounded degree.

The main source of coarse structures on a p.m.p. groupoid are graphings, that we now define.

Definition 2.15

(Graphings)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a p.m.p. groupoid over the probability space
(X,\mu)
. A
graphing
of
\mathcal{G}
is an at most countable ordered collection of bisections
\Phi=(\varphi_{i})_{i}
indexed by
i\in\mathbb{N}
or
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
for an integer
N
. A graphing is
generating
if the smallest groupoid which contains all the bisections
\varphi_{i}
is
\mathcal{G}
itself.
By a
p.m.p. graphed groupoid

(\mathcal{G},\Phi)
we mean a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
for which a generating graphing
\Phi
has been prescribed.

The
size
of
\Phi
, denoted by
|\Phi|
, is the number of bisections which compose
\Phi
.
A
\Phi
-word of length
k
is a product of
k
elements of
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
.
The set of
\Phi
-words of length at most
k
is denoted by
\Phi^{k}
.
We will also denote by
\overline{\Phi}
the subset of
\mathcal{G}
composed of all elements of elements in
\Phi
, that is
\overline{\Phi}=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:\ g\in\varphi\in\Phi\}
. Observe that
\overline{\Phi^{k}}=\overline{\Phi}^{k}
.
A generating graphing
\Phi=(\varphi_{i})_{i}
of a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
induces also a
length
as follows:

\ell_{\Phi}:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\ g\mapsto\min\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}:g\in\overline{\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k}\}}^{k}\right\}.

(8)

The empty word (the
\Phi
-word of length
0
) is the bisection
X
(the space of units).
The length of every unit is
0
. We will denote by
B_{\ell_{\Phi}}(L)
the ball of length
L
:
B_{\ell_{\Phi}}(L):=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:\ell_{\Phi}(g)\leq L\}
.

Remark 2.16

(Coarse structures vs graphings)
.

If
(\mathcal{G},\Phi)
is a graphed p.m.p. groupoid, then the collection of measurable subsets on which
\ell_{\Phi}
is bounded is a countably generated coarse structure with bounded geometry: the
coarse structure associated to
\Phi
. Similarly, if
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})
is a sequence of graphed groupoids, then the collection of all the sequences
(E_{n}\subset\mathcal{G}_{n})_{n}
satisfying
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N},g_{n}\in E_{n}}\ell_{\Phi_{n}}(g_{n})<\infty
is a countably generated coarse structure with bounded geometry on
(\mathcal{G}_{n})
.

The precise choice of the length function (
8
) is not important, we could as well have chosen a sequence
(a_{i})_{i}
of positive real numbers tending to
+\infty
, and define instead the shortest-path length obtained by assigning to every
h\in\varphi_{i}
the length
a_{i}
:

g\mapsto\min\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{i_{j}}:k\in\mathbb{N},i\in\mathbb{N}^{k},\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{k},g\in\varphi_{i_{1}}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\varphi_{i_{2}}^{\varepsilon_{2}}\dots\varphi_{i_{k}}^{\varepsilon_{k}}\right\}.

Different choices for
a_{i}
give Lipschitz equivalent lengths and they all give the same coarse structure as (
8
) on (sequences of) graphed groupoids.

Conversely, the reader can check that any countably generated coarse structure with bounded geometry on a p.m.p. groupoid is the coarse structure associated to a graphing. Such a graphing is generating if and only if the coarse structure is.

Example 2.17
.

A group is naturally a groupoid, a (generating) graphing is a (generating) subset, and when
G
is finitely generated by
S
,
\Phi=S
and
a_{i}=1
for the elements of
S
, then the above length is the usual word length.
On the contrary, when the generating set
S
is infinite, then
\ell_{\Phi}
is not bounded on
S
and thus
S

it is not controlled for the coarse structure considered in Remark
2.16
.

2.5
Actions

Definition 2.18
.

A (measurable)

action

\theta
of a p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on the fibred space
(\mathcal{F},\pi)
(both over the probability space
(X,\mu)
) is a measurable map
\theta:(\mathcal{G},s)\ast(\mathcal{F},\pi)\to\mathcal{F},(g,f)\mapsto\theta_{g}f
such that:

•

\theta_{g}
is bijection from
\pi^{-1}(s(g))
to
\pi^{-1}(t(g))
,

•

\theta_{gh}=\theta_{g}\theta_{h}
whenever

.

Besides its standard fibred structure
\pi_{s}:(g,f)\mapsto s(g)=\pi(f)
,
the fibred product
(\mathcal{G},s)\ast(\mathcal{F},\pi)
admits also the target fibred structure
\pi_{t}:(g,f)\mapsto t(g)
,
and
\theta
is a morphism of fibred spaces
\theta:((\mathcal{G},s)\ast(\mathcal{F},\pi),\pi_{t})\to(\mathcal{F},\pi)

(i.e.
\pi_{t}(g,f)=\pi(\theta_{g}f)
).
As for actions of groups, we will often forget
\theta
and we will write
gf
for
\theta_{g}f
.
If
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{G}
and

\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{F}
are measurable subsets, we denote by
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{D}
the image of the restriction of the action to
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
, i.e., the image of
\theta:(\mathcal{E},s)*(\mathcal{D},\pi)\rightarrow\mathcal{F},(g,f)\mapsto\theta_{g}f
. Observe that
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{D}
is measurable (same proof as Remark
2.10
).
The
\mathcal{G}
-orbit
of
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{F}
is
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{D}
.
A
fundamental domain
for the action of
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{F}
is a measurable subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{F}
such that the action
\theta

induces an isomorphism of fibred spaces

\left(\left(\mathcal{G},s\right)*\left(\mathcal{D},\pi\right),\pi_{t}\right)\simeq(\mathcal{F},\pi)

(for the target fibration
\pi_{t}
).

By a
\mathcal{G}
-
fibred space
, we mean a fibred space
\mathcal{F}
equipped with a prescribed
\mathcal{G}
-action.

Remark 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a p.m.p. groupoid and let
\mathcal{F}
be a
\mathcal{G}
-fibred space. Then we can define a
quotient

\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{G}
to be the quotient of

by the equivalence relation induced by the action of

. If there exists a fundamental domain

, then this quotient is naturally isomorphic to

. In particular it has a measurable structure and it is itself a fibred space. Observe also that the induced quotient map from

to

.

A p.m.p. subgroupoid

.

2.6
Presentation of p.m.p. groupoids

The notion of presentation of groupoids has been introduced in
[
Alv08
]
.

Definition 2.23
.

A
homomorphism
of p.m.p. groupoids
T:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is a measurable map commuting with source, target, products and inversion. In particular, it sends the units of
\mathcal{G}
to the units of
\mathcal{H}
, since
X=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:s(g)=t(g),g^{2}=g\}
. We say that a homomorphism is
p.m.p.
if the map between the unit spaces is measure preserving.

A homomorphism of groupoids is
units-preserving
if the induced map at the level of units is an isomorphism of probability spaces. In particular a units-preserving homomorphism is always a p.m.p. homomorphism.

A groupoid
\mathcal{N}
is
totally isotropic
if every element
n\in\mathcal{N}
is isotropic:
s(n)=t(n)
. As an example every groupoid
\mathcal{G}
admits the totally isotropic subgroupoid consisting of all its isotropic elements.
Observe however that such a subgroupoid is not p.m.p. in general, see Example
2.7
. However it is the case whenever
\mathcal{G}
is
realizable
, see Definition
2.41
.

A totally isotropic subgroupoid
\mathcal{N}<\mathcal{G}
is
normal
if for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
n\in\mathcal{N}_{s(g)}
, we have
gng^{-1}\in\mathcal{N}_{t(g)}
. As for groups, normal subgroupoids are designed for forming quotients.

Proposition 2.24
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a p.m.p. groupoid and let
\mathcal{N}<\mathcal{G}
be a totally isotropic normal p.m.p. subgroupoid. Then the quotient
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}
is a p.m.p. groupoid and the quotient map
Q:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}
is a p.m.p. and units-preserving homomorphism of p.m.p. groupoids.

Proof.

We have already observed in Example
2.20
that if
\mathcal{N}<\mathcal{G}
is a p.m.p. subgroupoid, then the left action of
\mathcal{N}
on
\mathcal{G}
admits a fundamental domain
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{G}
. If
\mathcal{N}
is a normal totally isotropic groupoid, then this fundamental domain is also a fundamental domain for the action on the right. Indeed, since
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{N}\mathcal{D}
, every element
g\in\mathcal{G}
can be written as
g=nd=d(d^{-1}nd)
so
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N}
. Moreover
if
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
and
n,n^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
are such that
gn=g^{\prime}n^{\prime}
, then
gng^{-1}g=g^{\prime}n^{\prime}g^{\prime-1}g^{\prime}
and hence
g=g^{\prime}
and
gng^{-1}=g^{\prime}n^{\prime}g^{\prime-1}
which implies that
n=n^{\prime}
.
This implies that the quotient
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}
is naturally a fibred space with respect to
s(g\mathcal{N})=s(g)
and
t(g\mathcal{N})=t(g)
. We can define the multiplication as usual, if
g\mathcal{N},h\mathcal{N}\in\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}
are such that
s(g)=t(h)
, then
g\mathcal{N}h\mathcal{N}=gh\mathcal{N}
and this is well defined. The inverse can be defined analogously and it is a routine check that these operations turn
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}
into a p.m.p. groupoid and
Q
is a measurable map between p.m.p. groupoids.
∎

The argument of the proof above gives also the following.

Definition 2.28

Let

Corollary 2.35
.

If two sequences of generating graphings
\Phi_{n}
and
\Psi_{n}
of p.m.p. groupoids
\mathcal{G}_{n}
are
\mathfrak{u}
-coarsely equivalent
(i.e., for every sequence

\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\ell_{\Phi_{n}}(g_{n})<\infty
if and only if
\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}\ell_{\Psi_{n}}(g_{n})<\infty
), then the ultraproduct graphings
\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\Psi_{\mathfrak{u}}
define the same groupoid with coarse structure.

Proof.

The assumption of coarse equivalence means that for every
M
, there is
M^{\prime}
such that, for
\mathfrak{u}\textrm{-a.e. }n
,
\ell_{\Phi_{n}}(g)\leq M\implies\ell_{\Psi_{n}}(g)\leq M^{\prime}
, and conversely
\ell_{\Psi_{n}}(g)\leq M\implies\ell_{\Phi_{n}}(g)\leq M^{\prime}
. Indeed, if for every
n
, we define
M_{n}=\sup\{\ell_{\Psi_{n}}(g):g\in\mathcal{G}_{n},\ell_{\Phi_{n}}(g)\leq M\}
, and pick
g_{n}\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
with
\ell_{\Phi_{n}}(g_{n})\leq M
and
\ell_{\Psi_{n}}(g)=M_{n}
if

and
\ell_{\Psi_{n}}(g)\geq n
otherwise, we obtain that
M^{\prime}:=\lim_{\mathfrak{u}}M_{n}<\infty
. This allows us to identify the groupoids

\{(g_{n})_{n}:\sup_{n}\ell_{\Phi_{n}}(g_{n})<\infty\}/\sim_{\mathfrak{u}}

and

\{(g_{n})_{n}:\sup_{n}\ell_{\Psi_{n}}(g_{n})<\infty\}/\sim_{\mathfrak{u}},

and Proposition
2.31
shows that this identification is measurable and measure preserving.
∎

Remark 2.36
.

In Corollary
2.35
, it is crucial that the two graphings are
\mathfrak{u}
-coarsely equivalent: sequences of different graphings can give rise to rather unrelated limits. For instance, the transitive equivalence relation on a finite set has many different generating graphings (all connected graphs with this finite set as vertex set). However their ultraproducts can be completely different according, for instance, to whether one takes a sequence of expanders, in which case the ultraproduct equivalence relation is strongly ergodic, or a sequence of Hamiltonian cycles in which case the ultraproduct is not ergodic.

2.8
Realizability and ultraproducts

Definition 2.41
.

A p.m.p. groupoid
\mathcal{G}
over the probability space
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
is said to be
realizable
if every measurably isotropic bisection
\varphi
is totally isotropic (Definition
2.11
).

In other words
\mathcal{G}
is realizable if and only if every totally free bisection is measurably free or equivalently if and only if
\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}}({\varphi})=\mathrm{Fix}({\varphi}):=\{g\in\varphi:s(g)=t(g)\}
. The following is classic.

Lemma 2.42
.

Every p.m.p. groupoid on a standard Borel probability space is realizable.

Realizable groupoids are the groupoids that can be studied point-wise and those groupoids that have a
free factor
onto a standard Borel space, see Theorem
3.28
. Therefore, up to some extent, realizable groupoids behave like standard one.

Lemma 2.43

(Realizability)
.

The ultraproduct
(\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}},\Phi_{\mathfrak{u}})
of a sequence of realizable graphed p.m.p. groupoids
(\mathcal{G}_{n},\Phi_{n})
is itself realizable.
Moreover every bisection
\varphi_{\mathfrak{u}}=[\varphi_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
satisfies up to null sets

[\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}_{n}}({\varphi_{n}})]_{\mathfrak{u}}=[\mathrm{Fix}({\varphi_{n}})]_{\mathfrak{u}}=\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi_{\mathfrak{u}})=\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\varphi_{\mathfrak{u}})

and
[M_{F}(\varphi_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}=M_{F}[(\varphi_{n})]_{\mathfrak{u}}
.

Proof.

First observe that since the ultraproduct groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{u}}
is generated by the bisections of the form
[\varphi_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
it is enough to prove that for every
\varphi_{\mathfrak{u}}=[\varphi_{n}]_{\mathfrak{u}}
we have that
[\mathrm{Fix}({\varphi_{n}})]_{\mathfrak{u}}=\mathrm{Fix}_{\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{u}}}(\varphi_{\mathfrak{u}})
. This follows easily from Proposition
2.12
. Indeed consider a decomposition given by Proposition
2.12
, that is
M_{F}(\varphi_{n})=\sqcup_{i}A_{n}^{i}
such that
\mu(\cup_{i=1}^{k}A_{n}^{i})\geq(1-(2/3)^{k})\mu(M_{F}(\varphi_{n}))
and
\mu_{n}(A_{n}^{i}\cap\varphi(A_{n}^{i}))=0

.

Let

be a sequence of realizable graphed groupoids such that

The proof of Theorem

relies on the following fundamental lemma, due to Thom

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a finite von Neumann algebra and
x\in\mathcal{M}
, let
x_{n}\in M_{k_{n}}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
x=x^{*}
and
x_{n}=x_{n}^{*}
for all
n
and assume that
\tau(P(x))=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{k_{n}}Tr(P(x_{n}))
for every polynomial
P
. Then

\tau(\chi_{\{0\}}(x))=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{k_{n}}Tr\left(\chi_{\{0\}}(x_{n})\right).

Proof.

Denote by
\mu_{n}
the spectral measure of
x_{n}
and
\mu
the spectral measure of
x
. These are the compactly supported probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\tau(f(x))=\int fd\mu
and
\frac{1}{k_{n}}Tr(f(x_{n}))=\int fd\mu_{n}
for every Borel function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
. By classical approximation considerations
[
Bil86
, Theorem 30.2]
, the assumption that
\tau(P(x))=\lim_{n}\frac{1}{k_{n}}Tr(P(x_{n}))
implies in particular that
\int fd\mu=\lim_{n}\int fd\mu_{n}
for every bounded continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
. We want to replace
f
by the indicator function of
\{0\}
, and for this it’s enough to prove that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{n}\mu_{n}([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\setminus\{0\})=0
.

Consider the Borel function
\varphi(t)=\log|t|
if
t\neq 0
and
\varphi(t)=0
if
t=0
. Then

\int\varphi d\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\sum_{\lambda\neq 0}\log|\lambda|=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\log\left(\prod_{\lambda\neq 0}|\lambda|\right),

where the sum and the product range over all the non zero eigenvalues of
x_{n}
, counted with multiplicity. The value
\prod_{\lambda\neq 0}|\lambda|
is one of the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
x_{n}
and hence it is an integer number. Therefore
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)d\mu_{n}(t)\geq 0
.

Using the inequality
\varphi(t)\leq(\log\varepsilon)\chi_{[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\setminus\{0\}}(t)+t^{2}
, we obtain the inequality

0\leq(\log\varepsilon)\mu_{n}([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\setminus\{0\})+\frac{1}{k_{n}}Tr(x_{n}^{2}).

This implies that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{n}\mu_{n}([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\setminus\{0\})=0
and concludes the proof.
∎

Note that, like in
[

]
, the same proof works to show that for every algebraic integer

One just replaces the function
\varphi(t)
by
\log|P(t)|
if
P
is a monic polynomial with integer coefficients having
\theta
as a root.

Let us say that a self-adjoint element

of a finite von Neumann algebra is

if there is a sequence

as in the statement of Lemma

Let

, and for every integer

And therefore, this holds with both

Let us now prove Theorem

.

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a finite faithful normal trace
\tau
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert
\mathcal{M}
-module with finite von Neumann dimension and
V,W
(not necessarily closed) sub
\mathcal{M}
-modules of
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\overline{V\cap W}=\overline{V}\cap\overline{W}.

Remark 5.19
.

It is easy to see that the equality
\overline{V\cap W}=\overline{V}\cap\overline{W}
is not true for arbitrary subspaces of
\ell^{2}
. Therefore, already for
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}
, the lemma is not true for Hilbert modules
\mathcal{H}
of infinite dimension.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
the commutant of
\mathcal{M}
in
B(\mathcal{H})
. Since the module
\mathcal{H}
has finite dimension also
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
carries a finite normal faithful trace, denoted
\tau^{\prime}
. Write
V
as the union of an increasing net of Hilbert modules
V_{n}
. Let
p_{n}\in M_{N}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
be the orthogonal projection on
V_{n}
and set
p:=\lim_{n}p_{n}
, the projection on
\overline{V}
. We can do the same for
W
to obtain
W_{n},q_{n}
and
q
. The orthogonal projection on
\overline{V}\cap\overline{W}
is
p\wedge q
. Similarly, since
\overline{V\cap W}
is the closure of the increasing net of closed modules
V_{n}\cap W_{n}
, the orthogonal projection on it is
\lim_{n}p_{n}\wedge q_{n}
. So we have to prove the equality
p\wedge q=\lim_{n}p_{n}\wedge q_{n}
.

By
[
Tak02
, Proposition V.1.6]

\tau^{\prime}(p\wedge q)=\tau^{\prime}(p+q-p\vee q)
and same for
p_{n}
,
q_{n}
, so we have

\tau^{\prime}(p\wedge q-p_{n}\wedge q_{n})=\tau^{\prime}(p-p_{n})+\tau^{\prime}(q-q_{n})-\tau^{\prime}(p\vee q-p_{n}\vee q_{n}).

Both terms
\tau^{\prime}(p-p_{n})
and
\tau^{\prime}(q-q_{n})
go to zero, whereas
\tau^{\prime}(p\vee q-p_{n}\vee q_{n})\geq 0
, so we get
\limsup_{n}\tau^{\prime}(p\wedge q-p_{n}\wedge q_{n})\leq 0
. On the other hand
p_{n}\wedge q_{n}\leq p\wedge q
and therefore we obtain that
p\wedge q=\lim_{n}p_{n}\wedge q_{n}
as required.
∎

It is important for us that we have formulas in terms of “dimensions of kernels” for applying again Lemma
5.12
.

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
be two uniformly locally bounded
\mathcal{G}
-simplicial complexes, with associated boundary maps
\partial^{\prime}_{i}\colon C^{(2)}_{i}(\Sigma^{\prime})\to C^{(2)}_{i-1}(\Sigma^{\prime})
and
\partial_{i}\colon C^{(2)}_{i}(\Sigma)\to C^{(2)}_{i-1}(\Sigma)
, and denote by
p_{i}
the orthogonal projection onto
C^{(2)}_{i}(\Sigma)\subset C^{(2)}_{i}(\Sigma^{\prime})
. Then

\nabla_{i}(\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime})=\mathrm{dim}(\ker\partial_{i})+\mathrm{dim}(\ker\partial^{\prime}_{i+1})-\mathrm{dim}(\ker((1-p_{i})\partial^{\prime}_{i+1})),

where
\dim
stands for the von Neumann dimension over
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

Let
V
denote the Hilbert
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G})
-module
\ker((1-p_{i})\partial^{\prime}_{i+1})
and observe that if
f\in V
, then
\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}f=p_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}f
and hence

\partial_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}f=\partial_{i}p_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}f=\partial^{\prime}_{i}p_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}f=\partial^{\prime}_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}f=0,

since
\partial_{i}
and
\partial_{i}^{\prime}
coincide on the subspace generated by the image of
p_{i}
. Let
\kappa:\ker\partial_{i}\rightarrow\overline{H}_{i}^{(2)}(\Sigma^{\prime})
be the quotient map by
\overline{\operatorname{Im}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}}\cap\ker\partial_{i}
. Consider the sequence of Hilbert
\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{R}})
-modules

0\to\ker\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\iota}V\xrightarrow{\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}}\ker\partial_{i}\xrightarrow{\kappa}W\to 0,

where
\iota
is just the inclusion. We claim that it is weakly exact. The only point which deserves a justification is that the closure of the image of
\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}
, namely
\overline{\operatorname{Im}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}\cap\ker\partial_{i}}
agrees with the kernel of
\kappa
, that is
\overline{\operatorname{Im}\partial^{\prime}_{i+1}}\cap\ker\partial_{i}
which we already established in the previous lemma.

The lemma follows by the additivity of the von Neumann dimension.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.15
.

Let us denote by
\partial_{n,L}^{i}
the boundary map from
C_{i}^{(2)}(\Sigma_{n,L})
to
C_{i-1}^{(2)}(\Sigma_{n,L})
. Since
\partial_{n,L}^{i}
restricted to every orbit

is a classical boundary operator it can be expressed as a matrix with integer coefficients (with coefficients in
[[\mathcal{G}_{n}]]
if
\mathcal{G}_{n}
is only assumed to be sofic). By Lemma
2.39
and Lemma
2.40
we have that

Using a similar argument to all the operators involved in the expression of

∎

Remark 5.21

(
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers for random unimodular ULB rooted simplicial complex)

If
\Theta

\nu

\Delta_{d,\Theta}
on the space
C^{(2)}_{d}(\Theta)

L^{2}
-chains for each dimension

where the sum is extended to all
d
-cells
\sigma
that are adjacent to the root
\rho_{\Theta}
and integrate this quantity with respect to

\beta_{d}^{(2)}(\nu):=\int\frac{1}{d+1}\sum_{\sigma\sim\rho_{\Theta}}\langle p_{d,\Theta}(\mathbf{1}_{\sigma}),\mathbf{1}_{\sigma}\rangle d\nu(\Theta).

(33)

Compare
[
Sch18
]

[
Ele10a
]
.

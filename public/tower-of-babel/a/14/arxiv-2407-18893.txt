The flux norm, Bohr-Sommerfeld Quantization Rules and the scattering problem for h h - Ψ \Psi DO’s on the real line
A. Ifa, M. Rouleux

Abstract
We revisit the well known Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization rule (BS) of order 2 for a self-adjoint 1-D h h -Pseudo-differential operator within the algebraic and microlocal framework of Helffer and Sjöstrand; BS holds precisely when Gram matrix consisting of scalar products of some WKB solutions with respect to the “flux norm” (or microlocal Wonskian) is not invertible. We simplify somewhat our previous proof [ 19 ] by working in spatial representation only, as in complex WKB theory for Schrödinger operator. We consider also the scattering problem.

4
Scattering over a barrier: case of a compactly supported smooth potential

The scattering problem for Schrödinger equation
-h^{2}u^{\prime\prime}(x)+(V(x)-E)u(x)=0
(
2.1
)
at a non-trapping energy
E
has received considerable attention,
depending on the regularity of the potential and its behavior at infinity.

The simplest case of a compactly supported,
piecewise continuous potential
V
for which standard ODE technics apply (like Wronskians) has been considered in
[
3
]
.

Define the
monodromy operator
of (
2.1
)
as the linear operator
M(h)
mapping the state space of a particle with energy
E=k^{2}>0

into itself as follows. To a solution of Schrödinger operator for a free particle

-h^{2}u^{\prime\prime}(x)-k^{2}u(x)=0
we assign a solution of (
2.1
) coinciding with it to the left of the support,
and to this solution, in turn, we assign its value to the right of the support.
It is shown in
[
3
]
that (
2.1
) has a unique solution
u
(which we call Arnold solution) such that

u(x)=e^{ikx}+Be^{-ikx}
to the left of the barrier,
and
u(x)=Ae^{ikx}
to the right of the barrier.

Then the matrix of the monodromy operator in the basis
(f_{d},f_{g})

such that
f_{d}(x)=e^{ikx}
(wave travelling to the right) and
f_{g}(x)=e^{-ikx}
(wave travelling to the left) belongs to the group
\mathop{\rm SU}\nolimits(1,1)
.
It is of the form

M(E,h)=\begin{pmatrix}1/\overline{A}&-\overline{B}/\overline{A}\\
-B/A&1/A\end{pmatrix}

(4.1)

where the complex numbers
A,B
such that
|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}=1

are called the
transmission

and the
reflexion
coefficients.

When
V
is a step function (“hard wall potential”) it is easy to compute the probability amplitudes
A

and
B
using that the solution is
C^{1}
, see
[
3
]
.
For large energies (above the maximum
V_{0}
of
V
, i.e. the height of the
barrier), the reflection coefficient
|B|^{2}
tends to 0, while for energies less than
V_{0}
, the transmission coefficient
|A|^{2}
is exponentially small
(because of tunneling).

When
V
instead is a smooth potential tending sufficiently fast to some limits
V_{-}
as
x\to-\infty
, and
V_{+}
as
x\to+\infty

see
[
24
]
, then (
2.1
) has 2 families of fundamental solutions
(u_{d}^{+},u_{g}^{+})
and
(u_{d}^{-},u_{g}^{-})
with the following WKB asymptotics

\displaystyle u_{d}^{+}(x,h)=b^{+}_{d}(x;h)\exp[i\int_{x_{1}}^{x}\varphi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}/h],\quad x\to+\infty

(4.2)

\displaystyle u_{d}^{-}(x,h)=b_{d}^{-}(x,h)\exp[i\int_{x_{2}}^{x}\varphi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}/h],\quad x\to-\infty

and

\displaystyle u_{g}^{+}(x,h)=b_{g}^{+}(x,h)\exp[-i\int_{x_{2}}^{x}\varphi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}/h],\quad x\to+\infty

(4.3)

\displaystyle u_{g}^{-}(x,h)=b_{g}^{-}(x,h)\exp[-i\int_{x_{1}}^{x}\varphi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}/h],\quad x\to-\infty

where
x_{1}<<0
and
x_{2}>>0
are some “base points” to be defined below.
The scattering matrix
S(h)=\begin{pmatrix}s_{11}&s_{12}\\
s_{21}&s_{22}\end{pmatrix}
is defined as follows.
Any solution of (
2.1
) takes the form

u=c_{d}^{-}u_{d}^{-}+c_{g}^{-}u_{g}^{-}=c_{d}^{+}u_{d}^{+}+c_{g}^{+}u_{g}^{+}

\begin{pmatrix}c_{d}^{+}\\

c_{g}^{+}\end{pmatrix}
,
see
[
12
]
.
When

(u_{d}^{+},u_{g}^{+})=(u_{d}^{-},u_{g}^{-})=(e^{ikx/h},e^{-ikx/h})
, so that
S(h)=I
.
More generally, for
(c_{d}^{-},c_{g}^{+})=(1,0)
(Arnold solution),
the monodromy operator
M(E,h)

S(h)

In case
V_{\pm}=0
and a short range analytic potential, the construction of exact solutions can be carried out as in

using analytic extensions in the complex plane, so computing

[

is not asymptotic to the Wronskian of their asymptotics.
Consider for instance WKB solution in the

[
23
]

P^{\pm}(x;h)\sim\sum_{m\geq-1}h^{m}P_{m}^{\pm}(x)

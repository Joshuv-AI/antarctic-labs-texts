1.
Introduction and main results

Let
n,N\in\mathbb{N}
be integers and let also
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. In this paper we study the following variational problem: find a minimising map
u_{\infty}:\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the space
W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
that solves

(1.1)

\big\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\,\inf\Big\{\big\|f(\mathrm{D}v)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}:\ v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \|g(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\Big\}.

We are also interested in studying the structure of such constrained minimisers, and also in deriving appropriate PDEs that they satisfy as necessary conditions. The functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
will be assumed to satisfy certain natural hypotheses. We note that our general notation in (
1.1
) and subsequently will be either self-explanatory, or a convex combination of otherwise standard symbolisations (as e.g. in
[
17
,
22
,
24
,
37
]
).

The
L^{p}
counterpart of (
1.1
), and especially the
L^{2}
case, is textbook material in the Calculus of Variations (see e.g.
[
20
]
). Especially, the case of
f=|\cdot|
and
g=|\cdot|
, the corresponding Euclidean norms on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N}
respectively, is known as the
p
-
eigenvalue problem
, or the eigenvalue problem for the
p
-Laplacian. The
L^{\infty}
case we study herein, particularly in the vectorial case of
N\geq 2
, is completely new and, despite its importance, has not been considered before. When
N=1
with
f
the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g
the absolute value on
\mathbb{R}
, (
1.1
) reduces to the (scalar)
\infty
-
eigenvalue problem
, whose study goes back to the seminal work of Juutinen-Lindqvist-Manfredi in
[
29
]
. Since then, there has been a considerable interest on this problem and also on relevant ones (see for instance Juutinen-Lindqvist
[
28
]
and Bhattacharya-Marazzi
[
7
]
), as well as on related problems with constraints (see e.g. Aronsson-Barron
[
2
]
and Barron-Jensen
[
4
]
). However, all these works are restricted to either the
1
-dimensional case of
n=1
, or to the scalar case of
N=1
. Crucially, these approaches rely essentially on the (scalar) Aronsson-Euler operator and on the theory of Viscosity Solutions for nonlinear PDE, both of which are not available when
N\geq 2
(for a general introduction to the field we refer to the lecture notes
[
15
,
30
]
). Indeed, virtually all works on scalar-valued Calculus of Variations in
L^{\infty}
rely in some way on viscosity solutions and on the comparison principle. For various interesting works, some of which are relevant to applications and some to the
\infty
-eigenvalue problem, we refer to
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
23
,
27
,
36
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
.

Vectorial and higher order variational problems involving constraints have only very recently started being explored (see
[
33
,
34
]
and also
[
14
]
). In either case, the vectorial nature of the problem requires novel methods which are not based neither on viscosity solutions, nor on the Aronsson-Euler equation. Let us also note that the mere existence of a minimiser
u_{\infty}
to (
1.1
) is a relatively simple matter by applying the Direct Method of the Calculus of Variations and weak* lower-semicontinuity arguments for supremal functionals (under the appropriate quasi-convexity assumptions for
f
, see e.g.
[
6
]
). However, if one wishes to derive additional information on these (generally non-unique) minimisers, for example derive a necessary PDE system they satisfy, then this is a far less trivial matter. Among other complications arising, the
L^{\infty}
norm is non-differentiable, non-strictly convex and lacks the property of being
\sigma
-additive with respect to the domain argument.

The customary starting point for
L^{\infty}
variational problems, which we also employ herein as well, is to obtain minimisers as limits of respective
L^{p}
approximating variational problems as
p\to\infty
. Although this is perhaps not an intrinsic
L^{\infty}
approach, it typically bears significant fruit as these
special
minimisers which are obtained as limits of
L^{p}
minimisers always carry a finer structure. The central novelty of this work though regards the necessary PDE conditions that such minimisers to (
1.1
) satisfy. Our approach in studying (
1.1
) is inspired by a recent development in higher order
L^{\infty}
problems by the author and Moser in
[
35
]
(related earlier observations were made in a geometric higher order context in
[
39
]
). The main idea is that, if one rescales the
L^{p}
Euler-Lagrange equations in a different way from the customary one used to derive the Aronsson-Euler equations as
p\to\infty
, then it is possible to derive a divergence structure PDE for the
L^{\infty}
minimiser. This should be understood as a divergence form counterpart of the non-divergence Aronsson-Euler equations. However, this forcing of divergence structure to the essentially non-divergence
L^{\infty}
equations has a price to be paid: the equations involve measure coefficients arising as auxiliary variables. This is somewhat reminiscent of the way that eigenvalues appear as parameters in an eigenvalue problem.

The observation that a divergence PDE with measure coefficients can be derived in
L^{\infty}
has also been made earlier in a work by Evans and Yu
[
21
]
, and before that had also been conjectured by Aronsson himself, the founder of Calculus of Variations in
L^{\infty}
, in the unpublished note
[
1
]
. However, it was not pursued further, possibly because in the case studied in
[
21
]
as a divergence form counterpart of the
\infty
-Laplacian, these measures could be highly degenerate and supported only on the boundary. This renders the PDE trivial in the interior of the domain. Nevertheless, in the higher order case employed in
[
35
]
involving the
\infty
-Bilaplacian, as well as in the case of constrained problems studied herein, these PDE systems with measure coefficients provide non-trivial information. In particular, in
[
35
]
these measure were absolutely continuous and in fact given by harmonic functions and, for (
1.1
), the boundary is a nullset and these measures are supported on “large” contact sets in the interior.

Now we present our main results. To this end, we will utilise the following hypotheses regarding the functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(a)&f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}),\\
(b)&\text{$f$ is (Morrey) quasiconvex on $\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$},\\
(c)&\text{exist $C_{1},C_{2}>0$ with $C_{1}\leq C_{2}$ such that}\\
&\ 0\,<\,C_{1}f(X)\,\leq\,\partial f(X):X\,\leq\,C_{2}f(X),\\
&\text{for all }X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\setminus\{0\},\\
(d)&\text{exist $C_{3},...,C_{6}>0$, $\alpha>1$ and $\beta\leq(\alpha-1)/\alpha$ such that}\\
&-C_{3}+C_{4}|X|^{\alpha}\,\leq\,f(X)\,\leq\,C_{5}|X|^{\alpha}+\,C_{6},\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ |\partial f(X)|\,\leq\,C_{5}f(X)^{\beta}+\,C_{6},\\
&\text{for all }X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n},\end{array}\right.

and

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(a)&g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
(b)&\text{$g$ is coercive, i.e\ for any $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}$ we have}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \underset{t\to\infty}{\lim}\,g(t\eta)\,=\,\infty,\\
(c)&\text{exist $C_{7},C_{8}>0$ with $C_{7}\leq C_{8}$ such that}\\
&\ \ \ \ 0\,<\,C_{7}g(\eta)\,\leq\,\partial g(\eta)\cdot\eta\,\leq\,C_{8}g(\eta),\\
&\text{for all }\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.\end{array}\right.

In the above,
\partial f
and
\partial g
denote the corresponding derivatives of
f
and
g
respectively, whilst “
:
” and “
\cdot
” symbolise the Euclidean inner products on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N}
respectively. By “(Morrey) quasiconvexity” we mean the usual concept of quasiconvexity for integral functionals as e.g. presented in
[
17
]
, not the “
L^{\infty}
quasiconvexity notions” of Barron-Jensen-Wang
[
6
]
. Under these hypotheses, Theorem
1
that follows is our first main result.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) hold. Then, the following are true:

(A)
The problem (
1.1
) has a solution
u_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(B)
There exist Radon measures

M_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}),\ \ \ \nu_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega})

and a number
\Lambda_{\infty}\geq 0
such that

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{div}(M_{\infty})\,+\,{\Lambda_{\infty}}\partial g(u_{\infty})\nu_{\infty}\,=\,0,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{\infty}\,=\,0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

weakly in
(C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
, with

(1.5)

\Lambda_{\infty}\,=\,\big\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}>\,0.

Further, we have the uniform bounds

(1.6)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\Lambda_{\infty}\,\geq\,\left(\!\max\left\{\frac{C_{4}^{1/\alpha}}{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\|\partial g\|_{L^{\infty}(\{0\leq g\leq 1\})}}-C_{3}^{1/\alpha}\,,\,0\right\}\right)^{\!\!\alpha},\\
&\Lambda_{\infty}\,\leq\,C_{5}\left(\!\frac{\big(g(\cdot\,\eta)\big)^{-1}(1)}{R_{\Omega}}\right)^{\!\!\alpha}+\,C_{6},\end{split}\right.

where
R_{\Omega}
is the radius of the largest open ball in
\Omega
, and
\big(g(\cdot\,\eta)\big)^{-1}
is the inverse of
s\mapsto g(s\eta)
on
[0,\infty)
for any fixed
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\eta|=1
(well-defined by (
1.3
)).

(C)
The quadruple
\big(u_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty},M_{\infty},\nu_{\infty}\big)
satisfies the next approximation properties: there exists a sequence
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq(n/\alpha,\infty)
with
p_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
and for any such
p
, a quadruple

\big(u_{p},\Lambda_{p},M_{p},\nu_{p}\big)\ \in\ W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\times(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})\times\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega})

such that

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{p}\longrightarrow u_{\infty}&\text{ in }C^{\gamma}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}),\text{ for all }\gamma\in(0,1),\\
\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}&\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}),\text{ for all }q\in(1,\infty),\\
\Lambda_{p}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\infty}&\text{ in }[0,\infty),\\
M_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}M_{\infty}&\text{ in }\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}),\\
\nu_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\nu_{\infty}&\text{ in }\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega}),\end{array}\right.

as
p\to\infty
along
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
. Further,
u_{p}
solves the minimisation problem

(1.8)

\big\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\,\inf\Big\{\big\|f(\mathrm{D}v)\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}:\ v\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \|g(v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\Big\}

and
(u_{p},\Lambda_{p})
solves weakly the divergence PDE system

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{div}\Big(f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})^{p-1}\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\Big)+\,(\Lambda_{p})^{p}\,g(u_{p})^{p-1}\partial g(u_{p})\,=\,0,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{p}\,=\,0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Finally, the measures
M_{p},\nu_{p}
are given by

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{split}M_{p}:=&\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\left(\!\frac{f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})}{\Lambda_{p}}\right)^{\!p-1}\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega},\\
\nu_{p}:=&\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}g(u_{p})^{p-1}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega}.\end{split}\right.

We note that the meaning of the satisfaction of (
1.4
) weakly in
(C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
is as follows: for any test map
\phi\in C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

(1.11)

\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\mathrm{D}\phi:\mathrm{d}M_{\infty}\,={\Lambda_{\infty}}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\partial g(u_{\infty})\cdot\phi\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}.

In general, the matrix-valued measure
M_{\infty}
in Theorem
1
implicitly depends on
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}
, but it is not clear how exactly, especially in the case that the derivative
\partial f
is nonlinear. Notwithstanding, if we strengthen our assumptions to include that

is quadratic, together with a more stringent condition on

, we obtain the stronger result of Theorem
2
that follows. Theorem

allows to characterise

as a linear function of a (special) Borel measurable representative

of the gradient of the minimiser. Additionally, we obtain much more detailed information on the structure of the measure coefficients arising. Our additional hypothesis required is the following.

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\text{Exists $\textbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$ such that, for all $X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$}\\
\hskip 80.0ptf(X)=\textbf{A}:X\otimes X,\\
\text{and the assumption \eqref{1.3}(c) is satisfied with $C_{7}=C_{8}$.}\end{array}\right.

Under (
1.12
), we may establish our second principal result, which is given right below.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that (
1.2
), (

) and (

(A)

Hence,

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{div}\big(\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star})\,\mu_{\infty}\big)\,+\,{\Lambda_{\infty}}\partial g(u_{\infty})\nu_{\infty}\,=\,0,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\

.

(B)
The map

(1.15)

\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star}(x)\,=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{k\to\infty}{\lim}\,\mathrm{D}v_{j_{k}}(x),&\text{if the limit exists},\\

where

(1.16)

\mu_{p}:=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\left(\!\frac{f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})}{\Lambda_{p}}\right)^{\!p-1}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega},

\mu_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\mu_{\infty}
in

.

The measures
\mu_{\infty},\nu_{\infty}
concentrate whereon

and

. Additionally,

(1.17)

\left\{\ \ \ \begin{split}\nu_{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\,&=\,\nu_{\infty}\big(\big\{g(u_{\infty})=1\big\}\big)\,=\,1,\phantom{\Big]}\\

Let us note that our results are new even in the special scalar case of the
\infty
-eigenvalue problem, as we provide a new divergence PDE describing
\infty
-eigenvectors, which is an alternative to the fully nonlinear PDE involving the
\infty
-Laplacian derived in
[
29
]
.

It is worth noting that our approach herein allows to bypass the need for theories of generalised solutions for the Aronsson-Euler systems arising in
L^{\infty}
, which are non-divergence, highly degenerate and with discontinuous coefficients. Such theories require different new ideas and some heavy machinery which depart from standard methods involving Viscosity Solutions (see e.g.
[
16
,
31
]
for work in this direction). Even though it is not expected that the Aronsson-Euler systems can become redundant in general, in this particular case we can indeed bypass them.

Now we describe the content and the organisation of this paper. The table of contents gives an idea regarding the order of presentation. The proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
do not appear explicitly in the main text, but instead are a consequence of numerous lemmas and propositions in Sections

-

, which gradually establish all the claims made in the statements of Theorems

-

, plus some additional auxiliary information for the

-problems.

In Section

we discuss our hypotheses showing that, albeit restrictive, they do nonetheless allow for large classes of functions

. In particular, (

)(c) is compatible with the possible lack of convexity. Evidently, (

) is considerably more restrictive, but still allows for a large class of functions

.

In Section
3
we prove a large part of the assertions made in Theorem
1
, those which include existence for (

) and approximation via corresponding
L^{p}
constrained minimisation problems. A new ingredient here is the necessity to construct a strongly precompact class of admissible Lipschitz maps in the respective
L^{p}
minimisation classes, due to the lack of homogeneity of the functionals. Let us also note that for this part of the work, we could have relaxed our (Morrey) quasiconvexity assumption to include only “Barron-Jensen-Wang

quasiconvexity” as in
[
5
,
6
]
to prove existence of minimisers, by utilising Young measures or quasi-minimisers for the approximating

In Section

we establish the satisfaction of the PDE system (
1.4
) under the weaker assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.3
), by utilising tools developed in the previous sections.

In Section

we introduce the appropriate mollification operators required to prove Theorem

. This regularisation scheme utilises results on the geometry of (strongly) Lipschitz domain proved in Hofmann-Mitrea-Taylor
[

, and is closely related to the regularisation schemes used in Ern-Guermond
[

]
. The main idea is to use the existence of smooth vector fields which are transversal to the

to “shrink” the function to a compactly supported one in

, before regularising by convolution.

In Section

we complete the proofs of Theorems

and

by establishing the satisfaction of (

Finally, in Section

we consider the vectorial counterpart of the
\infty
-eigenvalue problem on the ball (i.e. for

being the Euclidean norms) and in this case we are able to compute explicit measures for which the divergence PDE is satisfied.

2.
Preliminaries and discussion of hypotheses

We begin by noting that in this paper we are using the notation

C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})\,:=\,C^{1}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

to symbolise the space of continuous mappings
\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
which vanish on
\partial\Omega
, are
C^{1}
on
\Omega
and whose derivatives extend continuously as mappings
\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
. Further, everywhere in this paper, for
p\in[1,\infty)
we are tacitly using the rescaled
L^{p}
-norms defined as

\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}|h|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\!\!1/p}=\,\left(\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|h|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\!\!1/p},

which, by virtue of Hölder’s inequality, are a family of seminorms which is monotone increasing in
p\in[1,\infty)
. Regarding the notation used in (
1.12
), we note that
A
is a fourth order tensor, seen as a matrix over the matrix space
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
and “
:
” therein is the corresponding Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
:

\textbf{A}:\textbf{B}\,=\sum_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\,\textbf{A}_{\alpha i\beta j}\textbf{B}_{\alpha i\beta j}.

Evidently, “
\otimes
” symbolises the tensor product of matrices, namely
X\otimes Y
is the fourth order tensor with components
X_{\alpha i}Y_{\beta j}
, when
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
. We continue by noting that assumption (
1.2
)(c) readily implies that

(2.1)

f>0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\setminus\{0\},\ \ \ f(0)=0\ \text{ and $f$ is radially increasing on }\mathbb{R}^{N\times n},

in the sense that
t\mapsto f(tX)
is increasing on
(0,\infty)
, for any fixed
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\setminus\{0\}
. Similarly, assumption (
1.3
)(c) implies that

(2.2)

g>0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},\ \ \ g(0)=0\ \text{ and $g$ is radially increasing on }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Albeit restrictive, there are nonetheless numerous (non-convex) functions which satisfy
(
1.2
)-(
1.3
). As an illustration, in the example below we construct a large class of functions which satisfy the inequality (c) even though they are not radially symmetric. This class of course includes all quadratic functions. We argue on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
only, but the construction trivially applies to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as well. Further, we remark that (
1.2
)(c) is compatible with (
1.2
)(b).

Example 3
.

Let
\bf S
be a compact
C^{1}
hypersurface on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
satisfying

{\bf S}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{3/2}(0)\setminus\mathbb{B}_{1/2}(0)

and that any half-ray
\mathrm{span}^{+}[E]:=\{tE:t\geq 0\}
along a unit direction
E\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
intersects
{\bf S}
at exactly one point:

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathrm{span}^{+}[E]\cap{\bf S})=1.

We further suppose that the (outwards orientated) unit normal vector field
n_{\bf S}:{\bf S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfies
n_{\bf S}(X):X>0
, namely the angle between the normal vector at
X
and the direction
X
itself is acute. Let
\alpha>1
and define
f
to be the unique
\alpha
-homogeneous function on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
satisfying

{\bf S}\,=\,\{f=1\}.

Explicitly,
f
can be represented as

f(X):=\inf\Big\{t>0\ :\ [0,X)\cap t^{1/\alpha}\bf S=\emptyset\Big\},

where
[0,X)
is the straight line segment
\{tX:0\leq t<1\}
and
t^{1/\alpha}\bf S
is the dilation of
\bf S
by
t^{1/\alpha}
. Then, by our assumptions on
\bf S
exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{1-\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\,\leq\,(1-\varepsilon_{0})|X|\,\leq\,n_{\bf S}(X):X\,\leq\,|X|\,\leq\,\frac{3}{2},

for all
X\in\bf S
. Since
\partial f(X)=n_{\bf S}(X)
and also
f(X)=1
for
X\in\bf S
, the above inequalities yield

\frac{1-\varepsilon_{0}}{2}f(X)\,\leq\,\partial f(X):X\,\leq\,\frac{3}{2}f(X),

for all
X\in\bf S
. Finally, since

\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}=\bigcup_{t\geq 0}t^{1/\alpha}\bf S,

by noting that
f
is
\alpha
-homogeneous (which implies that
\partial f
is
(\alpha-1)
-homogeneous), the obtained inequality in fact holds on the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

Now we derive some consequences of the satisfaction of assumptions (
1.2
) and (
1.12
). First note that if
C_{7}=C_{8}\equiv C
in (
1.3
), then we have
Cg(\eta)=\partial g(\eta)\cdot\eta
as equality. Even though this is restrictive, there are still several functions satisfying this condition. For instance, for any (possibly non-symmetric) matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\gamma>0
, the function given by

g(\eta):=(A:\eta\otimes\eta)^{2\gamma}

satisfies the identity with
C=4\gamma
, as

\partial g(\eta)\cdot\eta=\Big\{2\gamma(A:\eta\otimes\eta)^{2\gamma-1}A:\big((.)\otimes\eta+\eta\otimes(.)\big)\Big\}\cdot\eta=4\gamma\,g(\eta),

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
f
satisfies (
1.2
) and (
1.12
). Then:

(1)

By setting

\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:=\frac{\textbf{A}+\textbf{A}^{\top_{(1,2)\leftrightarrow(3,4)}}}{2},

where
\textbf{A}^{\top_{(1,2)\leftrightarrow(3,4)}}
is the adjoint of the linear map
\textbf{A}:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
, we have the representations

f(X)=\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:X\otimes X=\big|\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}X\big|^{2},

for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
. In particular, the symmetric part
\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}
of the tensor
A
is positive definite (i.e. non-negative) and hence the square root
\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
is well defined.

(2)

We have the identity

\partial f(X)=\textbf{A}:\big((\cdot)\otimes X+X\otimes(\cdot)\big),

for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

(3)

f
is non-negative and convex on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
with
\{f=0\}=\{0\}
.

(4)

Let
\sigma
denote the spectrum of a linear operator. Then, we have the bounds

\begin{split}\big(\!\min\sigma(\mathbb{S}\textbf{A})\big)|X|^{2}\,\leq\,f(X)\,\leq\,|\textbf{A}||X|^{2},\end{split}

for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

Note that the adjoint (transpose) operator
\textbf{A}^{\top_{(1,2)\leftrightarrow(3,4)}}
is the operator which in index form satisfies

\big({\textbf{A}^{\top_{(1,2)\leftrightarrow(3,4)}}}\big)_{\alpha i\beta j}=\,\textbf{A}_{\beta j\alpha i},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\{1,...,N\}
and all
i,j\in\{1,...,n\}
. This renders
\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
a real self-adjoint (symmetric) fourth order tensor (seen as a matrix over matrices).

Proof of Lemma

4
. (1) The identity
f(X)=\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:X\otimes X
is immediate as the rank-one tensor
X\otimes X
is symmetric, hence
\textbf{A}=\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}
on the symmetric subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}\times n}
. Further, by (
1.12
), (
1.2
) holds for
\alpha=2
and by (
2.1
) we have

\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:X\otimes X\geq 0\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:X\otimes X\geq C_{4}|X|^{2}-C_{3},

for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
. By the spectral theorem it follows that the symmetric tensor
\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}
is positive definite (i.e. non-negative) and
\sigma(\mathbb{S}\textbf{A})\subseteq[0,|\textbf{A}|]
. Further, the estimate above implies that
\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}
is actually strictly positive and
\min\sigma(\mathbb{S}\textbf{A})>0
: indeed, if hypothetically there existed
X_{0}\neq 0
with
\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:X_{0}\otimes X_{0}=0
, then for any
t>0

0=\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:(tX_{0})\otimes(tX_{0})\geq t^{2}C_{4}|X_{0}|^{2}-C_{3},

which leads to a contradiction as
t\to\infty
. Finally, since
A
is positive and symmetric,
\smash{\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}}
exists and is also a positive symmetric operator satisfying
\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}=\smash{\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}f(X)&=\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:X\otimes X\\
&=\Big(\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}^{\top_{(1,2)\leftrightarrow(3,4)}}\Big):X\otimes X\\
&=\big(\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}X\big):\big(\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}X\big)\\
&=\big|\sqrt{\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}}X\big|^{2},\end{split}

for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

(2) Follows by a direct differentiation.

(3) Follows by part (1) by noting that
f
is the restriction of the convex quadratic form
(X,Y)\mapsto\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}:X\otimes Y
on the diagonal of
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

(4) Follows directly from part (1), by recalling the variational definition of the minimum eigenvalue of a symmetric operator.
∎

3.
Existence in
L^{p}
and compactness as
p\to\infty

In order to solve the approximating
L^{p}
constrained problems, we first need the following result which establishes the existence of strongly converging (energy comparison) maps as
p\to\infty
in the respective constrained admissible classes.

Lemma 5
.

For any
v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
, there exists
(t_{p})_{p\in(n/\alpha,\infty]}\subseteq(0,\infty)
with
t_{p}\longrightarrow t_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
, such that

\|g(t_{p}v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\,1

for all
p\in(n/\alpha,\infty]
. Further, if
\|g(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
, then
t_{\infty}=1
.

Proof of Lemma

5
. Fix
v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
and set

\rho_{\infty}(t):=\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}g(t\,v(x)),\ \ \ t\geq 0.

Then, by (
2.2
) and (
1.3
) we have that
\rho(0)=0
and also
\rho_{\infty}\in C([0,\infty))
. Now we show that
\rho_{\infty}
is strictly increasing. Let us begin by showing first it is non-decreasing. For any
s>0
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, (
1.3
) implies

0<C_{7}\frac{g(s\eta)}{s}\,\leq\,\partial g(s\eta)\cdot\eta\,=\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(g(s\eta)\big),

which yields that
s\mapsto g(s\eta)
is strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
. Then, for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
t>s\geq 0
we have that
g(s\,v(x))\leq g(t\,v(x))
(with strict inequality if
v(x)\neq 0
), and therefore

\rho_{\infty}(s)\,=\,\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}g(s\,v(x))\,\leq\,\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}g(t\,v(x))\,=\,\rho_{\infty}(t).

Hence,
\rho_{\infty}
is non-decreasing. We now show that
t\mapsto\rho_{\infty}(t)
is actually strictly increasing on
(0,\infty)
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that this is not the case. Then, by the continuity of
\rho_{\infty}
, there exists
t_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\rho_{\infty}\equiv\rho_{\infty}(t_{0})
on the interval
(t_{0}-\varepsilon_{0},t_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})
. However, by Danskin’s theorem
[
18
]
, the derivative
\rho^{\prime}(t_{0}^{+})
from the right exists, and is given by the formula

\rho_{\infty}^{\prime}(t_{0}^{+})\,=\,\max_{x\in\Omega_{t_{0}}}\left\{\partial g\big(t_{0}v(x)\big)\cdot v(x)\right\}

where

\Omega_{t_{0}}\,=\,\Big\{\bar{x}\in\overline{\Omega}\ :\ \rho_{\infty}(t_{0})=g\big(t_{0}v(\bar{x})\big)\Big\}.

By utilising (
1.3
), the expression for
\rho_{\infty}^{\prime}(t_{0}^{+})
yields

\begin{split}\rho_{\infty}^{\prime}(t_{0}^{+})\,&=\,\frac{1}{t_{0}}\max_{x\in\Omega_{t_{0}}}\left\{\partial g\big(t_{0}v(x)\big)\cdot t_{0}v(x)\right\}\\
&\geq\,\frac{C_{7}}{t_{0}}\max_{x\in\Omega_{t_{0}}}g\big(t_{0}v(x)\big)\\
&=\,\frac{C_{7}}{t_{0}}\rho_{\infty}(t_{0})\\
&>\,0.\end{split}

This is a contradiction to
\rho_{\infty}
being constant on
(t_{0},t_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})
, hence establishing that
\rho_{\infty}
is indeed strictly increasing. Recall now that by (
1.3
)(b) we also have
g(s\eta)\to\infty
as
s\to\infty
. Thus, for any fixed
\bar{x}\in\Omega
for which
v(\bar{x})\neq 0
, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_{\infty}(t)\,\geq\,\lim_{t\to\infty}g\big(t\,v(\bar{x})\big)\,=\,\infty.

The mean value theorem then implies that there exists a number
t_{\infty}>0
such that
\rho_{\infty}(t_{\infty})=1
, namely

\|g(t_{\infty}v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\,1.

Additionally, if
\|g(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
to begin with, then
t_{\infty}=1
. Fix now
p\in(n/\alpha,\infty)
and set

\rho_{p}(t)\,:=\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g\big(t\,v(x)\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,\ \ \ t\geq 0.

Then, by (
2.2
) we have
\rho_{p}(0)=0
and also the strict monotonicity of
s\mapsto g(se)
on
(0,\infty)
. Then, the monotone convergence theorem yields that
\rho_{p}(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
. Therefore, there exists
t_{p}>0
such that
\rho_{p}(t_{p})=1
, namely

\|g(t_{p}v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\,1.

We now claim that
t_{p}\longrightarrow t_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
. Assuming for the sake of contradiction that
t_{p}\not\!\!\longrightarrow t_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
, this means there exists a sequence
(t_{p_{j}})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq(n/\alpha,\infty)
and a
t_{0}\in[0,t_{\infty})\cup(t_{\infty},\infty]
such that
t_{p_{j}}\longrightarrow t_{0}
as
j\to\infty
. Further, the sequence
(t_{p_{j}})_{1}^{\infty}
can be assumed to be monotone (either increasing or decreasing). We first show that
t_{0}<\infty
. Indeed, if we had
t_{0}=\infty
, then the sequence
(t_{p_{j}})_{1}^{\infty}
must be monotone increasing. Hence, by (
1.3
)(b) we have
g(t_{p_{j}}v(x))\nearrow\infty
as
j\to\infty
, for any
x\in\Omega\cap\{v\neq 0\}
. The monotone convergence theorem then yields the contradiction

1\,=\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(t_{p_{j}}v)^{p_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,\nearrow\,\infty.

Therefore, we must have
t_{0}<\infty
and hence
t_{0}\in[0,t_{\infty})\cup(t_{\infty},\infty)
. If now
t_{p_{j}}\longrightarrow t_{0}
, we have
t_{p_{j}}v\longrightarrow t_{0}v
in
C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, as
j\to\infty
. By the continuity of
g
, this implies

g(t_{p_{j}}v)\longrightarrow g(t_{0}v)
in
C(\overline{\Omega})
, as
j\to\infty
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
as
p\to\infty
in the pointwise sense on
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we infer that

\begin{split}1\,&=\,\|g(t_{p_{j}}v)\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)}\\
&=\,\|g(t_{0}v)\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)}\,+\,O\big(\big\|g(t_{p_{j}}v)-g(t_{0}v)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)\\
&=\,\|g(t_{0}v)\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)}+O\big(|t_{p_{j}}-t_{0}|\big)\\
&\!\!\!\overset{j\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ \|g(t_{0}v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&=\,\rho_{\infty}(t_{0}),\end{split}

which is a contradiction if
t_{\infty}\neq t_{0}
due to the strict monotonicity of
\rho_{\infty}
. ∎

Now we proceed to show existence of solution to the approximate constrained minimisation problem.

Lemma 6
.

For any
p>n/\alpha
, the minimisation problem (
1.8
) has a solution
u_{p}\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof of Lemma

6
. Let
p\in(n/\alpha,\infty)
be fixed. We begin by noting that, by virtue of Lemma
5
, the admissible class is non-empty: for any fixed
v_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
v_{0}\not\equiv 0
, there exists
t_{p}>0
such that
\|g(t_{p}v_{0})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, yielding that
t_{p}v_{0}
is in the admissible class of (
1.8
). Next, since by (
1.2
)(b)
f
is (Morrey) quasiconvex in
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
, so is
f^{p}
, as a result of Jensen’s inequality: indeed, for any fixed
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
and
\phi\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

f^{p}(X)\,\leq\Bigg(\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f(X+\mathrm{D}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\!\Bigg)^{\!\!p}\,\leq\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f^{p}(X+\mathrm{D}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.

Further, by (
1.2
)(d)
f^{p}
satisfies the estimate

-C_{3}(p)+C_{4}(p)|X|^{\alpha p}\,\leq\,f^{p}(X)\,\leq\,C_{5}(p)|X|^{\alpha p}+C_{6}(p),

for some
p
-dependent constants
C_{3}(p),...,C_{6}(p)>0
. By standard results on quasiconvex integral functionals (see e.g.
[
17
]
), it follows that
\|f(\mathrm{D}(\cdot))\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
is weakly lower semi-continuous and coercive in the space
W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Further, by the Morrey embedding
W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\subseteq C^{1-\frac{n}{\alpha p}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows that the admissible class is weakly closed as the functional
\|g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
is weakly continuous on
W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence, there exists a minimiser
u_{p}
which solves (
1.8
), as claimed. ∎

Now we consider the Euler-Lagrange equations that the approximate minimiser
u_{p}
satisfies. Unsurprisingly, they involve a Lagrange multiplier, which arises by the integral constraint
\|g(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
.

Lemma 7
.

For any
p>n/\alpha
, let
u_{p}
be the minimiser of (
1.8
) given by Lemma
6
. Then, there exists
\lambda_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
such that the pair
(u_{p},\lambda_{p})\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\times\mathbb{R}
satisfies weakly the PDE system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{div}\Big(f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})^{p-1}\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\Big)+\,\lambda_{p}\,g(u_{p})^{p-1}\partial g(u_{p})\,=\,0,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{p}\,=\,0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Proof of Lemma

7
. Follows by standard results on Lagrange multipliers in Banach spaces (see e.g. Zeidler
[
43
, Th. 43.D, p. 290]
). ∎

Now we obtain additional information on the family of eigenvalues
(\lambda_{p})_{p>n/\alpha}
.

Lemma 8
.

For any
p>n/\alpha
, we set

L_{p}\,:=\,\big\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Then, there exists
\Lambda_{p}>0
such that

\lambda_{p}\,=\,(\Lambda_{p})^{p}

and also

0\,<\,\bigg(\frac{C_{1}}{C_{8}}\bigg)^{\!\!1/p}L_{p}\,\leq\,\Lambda_{p}\,\leq\,\bigg(\frac{C_{2}}{C_{7}}\bigg)^{\!\!1/p}L_{p}.

Proof of Lemma

8
. We begin by noting that
L_{p}>0
, namely the infimum over the admissible class in (
1.8
) is strictly positive, as a consequence of the constraint and our assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) (via (
2.1
)-(
2.2
)): the only map
u\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for which
\|f(\mathrm{D}u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0
is
u_{0}\equiv 0
, but
u_{0}
is not in the admissible class because
\|g(u_{0})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=0
. Next, by testing against
u_{p}
in the weak formulation of the Euler-Lagrange equations (Lemma
7
), we have

\int_{\Omega}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})^{p-1}\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p}):\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,=\,\lambda_{p}\int_{\Omega}g(u_{p})^{p-1}\partial g(u_{p})\cdot u_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.

By (
1.2
)(c) and (
1.3
)(c) we have

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}C_{1}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\leq\,\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p}):\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,\leq\,C_{2}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p}),\\
C_{7}g(u_{p})\,\leq\,\partial g(u_{p})\cdot u_{p}\,\leq\,C_{8}g(u_{p}),\ \ \ \ \end{split}\right.

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e. on
\Omega
. Hence, since
f,g\geq 0
, integration gives

\left\{\ \,\begin{split}C_{1}\ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f^{p}(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,\leq\ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f^{p-1}(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p}):\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,\leq\ C_{2}\ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f^{p}(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n},\\
C_{7}\ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g^{p}(u_{p})\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq\ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g^{p-1}(u_{p})\partial g(u_{p})\cdot u_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,\leq\ C_{8}\ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g^{p}(u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.\ \ \ \ \end{split}\right.

By recalling that

{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}f^{p}(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,=\,(L_{p})^{p}\,>\,0\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ {-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g^{p}(u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,=\,1,

it follows that
\lambda_{p}>0
. By virtue of the above, by defining
\Lambda_{p}:=(\lambda_{p})^{1/p}>0
, we have the estimates

C_{1}(L_{p})^{p}\,\leq\,\lambda_{p}C_{8},\ \ \ \ C_{2}(L_{p})^{p}\,\geq\,\lambda_{p}C_{7},

which lead to the desired inequality.
∎

We now show the existence of solution to the problem (
1.1
) and the compactness of the class of

-pairs of eigenvectors-eigenvalues
(u_{p},\Lambda_{p})_{p>n/\alpha}
.

Proposition 9
.

There exists
(u_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty})\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\times(0,\infty)
such that, along a sequence
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{p}\longrightarrow u_{\infty}&\text{ in }C^{\gamma}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N}),\text{ for all }\gamma\in(0,1),\\
\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}&\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}),\text{ for all }q\in(1,\infty),\\
\Lambda_{p}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\infty}&\text{ in }(0,\infty).\end{array}\right.

Further,
u_{\infty}
solves the minimisation problem (
1.1
) and also
\Lambda_{\infty}
is given by (
1.5
). Finally,
\Lambda_{\infty}
satisfies the uniform bounds (

).

Proof of Proposition

9
. Fix
p>n/\alpha
,
q\leq p
and a map
v_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
v_{0}\not\equiv 0
. Then, by Lemma
5
there exists
(t_{p})_{p\in(n/\alpha,\infty]}\subseteq(0,\infty)
with

as

, such that
\|g(t_{p}v_{0})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
for all
p\in(n/\alpha,\infty]
. By Hölder’s inequality and minimality, this allows to estimate

\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|f(\mathrm{D}(t_{p}v_{0}))\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|f(t_{p}\mathrm{D}v_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Hence,

\sup_{q\geq p}\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\sup_{q\geq p}\|f(t_{p}\mathrm{D}v_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty,

and the finiteness of the last term is a consequence of the compactness of

. Further, by (

) we have the lower bound
f^{q}(X)\geq C_{4}(q)|X|^{\alpha q}-C_{3}(q)
for some

-dependent constants

and all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
. When combined with the previous estimate, this lower bound implies

\sup_{q\geq p}\|\mathrm{D}u_{p}\|_{L^{\alpha q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,C(q)<\infty,

for some
q
-dependent
C(q)>0
. Further, application of Poincaré’s inequality improves the above estimate to

\sup_{q\geq p}\|u_{p}\|_{W^{1,\alpha q}(\Omega)}\,\leq C(q)\,<\infty,

for a new constant
C(q)>0
. Hence, standard compactness and diagonal arguments in Sobolev spaces imply the existence of a map

u_{\infty}\,\in\bigcap_{n/\alpha<p<\infty}W_{0}^{1,\alpha p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

and (for any sequence) a subsequence such that the claimed modes of convergence hold true as
p\to\infty
along this subsequence. Fix now any
v\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
\|g(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
. Note that, necessarily,
v\not\equiv 0
by virtue of (
2.2
). By Lemma
5
, there exists
(t_{p})_{p\in(n/\alpha,\infty)}\subseteq(0,\infty)
with
t_{p}\longrightarrow 1
as
p\to\infty
, such that
\|g(t_{p}v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
for all
p>n/\alpha
. By Lemma
6
, the definition of
L_{p}
in Lemma
8
, Hölder inequality and minimality, we estimate

\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,L_{p}\,\leq\,\|f(t_{p}\mathrm{D}v)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for any such
v
. By the weak lower semi-continuity of the functional
\|f(\mathrm{D}(\cdot))\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
on
W_{0}^{1,\alpha q}(\Omega)
, by letting
p\to\infty
along a subsequence, we obtain for the given fixed
v\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
that

\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\liminf_{p_{j}\to\infty}L_{p}\,\,\leq\,\limsup_{p_{j}\to\infty}L_{p}\,\leq\,\|f(\mathrm{D}v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

In conclusion, by letting also
q\to\infty
, we deduce the energy inequality

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\liminf_{p_{j}\to\infty}L_{p}\,\,\leq\,\limsup_{p_{j}\to\infty}L_{p}\,\leq\,\|f(\mathrm{D}v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for any $v\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})$ with $\|g(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1$.}\end{split}\right.

Next, we note that
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
(and is not merely in the intersection of
L^{\alpha p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
for
p\in(n/\alpha,\infty)
). This follows by (
1.2
), which yields

\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\geq\,C_{4}\|\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\,C_{3}.

As a result, by Morrey’s estimate we have that
u_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Further,
u_{\infty}
is in the admissible class of (
1.1
), since by the uniform convergence
u_{p}\longrightarrow u_{\infty}
on
\overline{\Omega}
as
p\to\infty
along a subsequence, the continuity of
g
, we have

\begin{split}1\,&=\,\|g(u_{p})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\\
&=\,\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,+\,\big(\|g(u_{p})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\,\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\big)\\
&=\,\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,+\,O\big(\|g(u_{p})-g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\big)\\
&=\,\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,+\,O\big(\|g(u_{p})-g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)\\
&\!\!\longrightarrow\,\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\end{split}

as
p\to\infty
subsequentially. In conclusion, by the arbitrariness of the map
v
in our energy inequality, it follows that
u_{\infty}
indeed solves the minimisation problem (
1.1
).

Let now
\Lambda_{\infty}
be
defined
by

\Lambda_{\infty}\,:=\,\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Note that the above definition of
\Lambda_{\infty}
as the infimum in (
1.1
) readily implies that it must be strictly positive. Indeed, by (
2.1
)-(
2.2
), for any map
u_{0}
in the admissible class of (
1.1
) for which we have
\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0
, it follows that
u_{0}\equiv 0
, which is a contradiction since we must also have
\|g(u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0<1
. Further, the choice
v:=u_{\infty}
in our energy inequality implies that
L_{p}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
along a subsequence. Additionally, by Lemma
8
we have that
|L_{p}-\Lambda_{p}|\longrightarrow 0
as
p\to\infty
, hence
\Lambda_{p}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
.

To complete the proof, it remains to establish the claimed uniform bounds on
\Lambda_{\infty}
. By Poincaré’s inequality, since
g(0)=0
, we have

\begin{split}1\,=\,\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,&\leq\,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\|\mathrm{D}(g(u_{\infty}))\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\|\partial g\|_{L^{\infty}(u_{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}))}\|\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Since
g\geq 0
and
\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
, this yields that
0\leq g(u_{\infty})\leq 1
everywhere on
\overline{\Omega}
. Hence, we have
u(\overline{\Omega})\subseteq\{0\leq g\leq 1\}
, which in turn implies

\begin{split}1\,\leq\,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\|\partial g\|_{L^{\infty}(\{0\leq g\leq 1\})}\|\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Since by (
1.2
) we have
|X|\leq C_{4}^{-1/\alpha}\big(f(X)+C_{3}\big)^{1/\alpha}
for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
, we obtain

\begin{split}1\,\leq\,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\|\partial g\|_{L^{\infty}(\{0\leq g\leq 1\})}\frac{\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\|^{1/\alpha}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\,C_{3}^{1/\alpha}}{C_{4}^{1/\alpha}}.\end{split}

The above inequality readily leads to the claimed lower bound for
\Lambda_{\infty}
in (
1.6
).

For the upper bound, consider the Lipschitz function
\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)
of distance from the boundary
\partial\Omega
, extended by zero on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. Since
\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, for any fixed
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\eta|=1
we may invoke Lemma
5
to find
t_{\infty}>0
such that
\xi_{\infty}:=t_{\infty}\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)\eta
is in the admissible class for (
1.1
), namely
\|g(\xi_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
. Since
|\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega))|\leq 1

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e. on
\Omega
, by minimality and (
1.2
), we estimate

\begin{split}\Lambda_{\infty}\,&=\,\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\,\|f(\mathrm{D}\xi_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\,\big\|f\big(t_{\infty}\eta\otimes\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega))\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\,C_{5}\Big(t_{\infty}\big\|\eta\otimes\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega))\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big)^{\!\alpha}+\,C_{6}\\
&\leq\,C_{5}(t_{\infty})^{\alpha}+\,C_{6}.\end{split}

Now we need an estimate for
t_{\infty}
. To this end, let

R_{\Omega}\,:=\,\sup\{r>0\ |\ \exists\ x\in\Omega\,:\ \mathbb{B}_{r}(x)\subseteq\Omega\}.

Then, there exists an
\bar{x}\in\Omega
such that
\mathbb{B}_{R_{\Omega}}(\bar{x})\subseteq\Omega
, and this is the largest such ball. Hence,
\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\partial\Omega)=R_{\Omega}
. Since by construction,
\xi_{\infty}
satisfies
\|g(\xi_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
, it follows that

1\,=\,\sup_{\Omega}\,g\big(t_{\infty}\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)\eta\big)\,\geq\,g\big(t_{\infty}\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},\partial\Omega)\eta\big)\,=\,g(t_{\infty}R_{\Omega}\eta).

By (
2.2
), we have that the function
[0,\infty)\ni s\mapsto g(s\eta)\in[0,\infty)
is strictly increasing and onto because
g(0)=0
and
g(s\eta)\to\infty
as
s\to\infty
. Hence, its inverse function
(g(\cdot\,\eta))^{-1}:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
is well defined. Therefore, since
g(t_{\infty}R_{\Omega}\eta)\leq 1
, we have

t_{\infty}R_{\Omega}\,=\,(g(\cdot\,\eta))^{-1}\big(g(R_{\Omega}\eta)\big)\,\leq\,(g(\cdot\,\eta))^{-1}(1).

Combining the previous estimates, we infer that

\Lambda_{\infty}\,\leq\,C_{5}\bigg(\!\frac{1}{R_{\Omega}}\big(g(\,\cdot\,\eta)\big)^{-1}(1)\bigg)^{\!\!\alpha}+\,C_{6}.

The above estimate completes the claimed uniform upper bound on
\Lambda_{\infty}
in (
1.6
), and therefore Proposition
9
ensues. ∎

4.
The divergence PDE system in
L^{\infty}
, part I

Using the tools already developed, in this section we establish the satisfaction of the divergence PDE system (

Lemma 10

For any

Proof of Lemma

10
. By (
1.16

8

\begin{split}\mu_{p}(\Omega)\,&=\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}\bigg(\frac{f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})}{\Lambda_{p}}\bigg)^{\!p-1}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\

Further, for

1.10
) and assumption (
1.2
)(d), we estimate

\begin{split}\|M_{p}\|(\overline{\Omega})\,&=\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}\bigg(\frac{f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})}{\Lambda_{p}}\bigg)^{\!p-1}\big|\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\big|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\

In view of Lemma
8
and Proposition
9

\begin{split}\|M_{p}\|(\overline{\Omega})\,&\leq\,\frac{C_{5}}{\Lambda_{p}^{p-1}}\!\left(\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\!\frac{p-1+\beta}{p}}+\,\frac{C_{6}}{\Lambda_{p}^{p-1}}\!\left(\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\!\frac{p-1}{p}}\\

&=\,\bigg(\frac{L_{p}}{\Lambda_{p}}\bigg)^{p-1}\big(C_{5}L_{p}^{\beta}+C_{6}\big)\\
&\leq\,\bigg(\frac{C_{8}}{C_{1}}\bigg)^{\!1-\frac{1}{p}}\Big(C_{5}(\Lambda_{\infty}+1)^{\beta}+C_{6}\Big),\end{split}

as claimed. Similarly, by (
1.10

\|g(u_{p})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, we may estimate

\begin{split}\nu_{p}(\overline{\Omega})\,&=\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int}_{\!\!\!\Omega}g(u_{p})^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,\leq\left(\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(u_{p})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\!\!\frac{p-1}{p}}\,=\,1.\end{split}

Next, by setting
\omega(p):=\|g(u_{p})-g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and noting that
\omega(p)\to 0
as
p\to\infty
(along a subsequence) due to the uniform convergence

on
\overline{\Omega}
, we estimate

\begin{split}1\,&=\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(u_{p})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&=\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(u_{p})^{p-1}g(u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\leq\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(u_{p})^{p-1}\Big(g(u_{\infty})+\|g(u_{p})-g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\leq\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(u_{p})^{p-1}\big(1+\omega(p)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n},\\
\end{split}

where in the last step we also used that
g(u_{\infty})\leq 1
on
\Omega
, as a consequence of the continuity and the non-negativity of
g(u_{\infty})
and the constraint
\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
. Hence, the above inequality shows that

1\,\leq\,\nu_{p}(\overline{\Omega})\big(1+\omega(p)\big).

Finally, let us fix

and define the open set

A_{q}\,:=\,\Big\{g(u_{\infty})<\|g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}-2\omega(q)\Big\}.

We note that the desired remaining estimate for

\begin{split}\nu_{p}(A_{q})&=\,\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\int_{A_{q}}g(u_{p})^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\leq\,\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\int_{A_{q}}\Big(g(u_{\infty})+\|g(u_{p})-g(u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big)^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\

\begin{split}\nu_{p}(A_{q})&\leq\,\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\int_{A_{q}}\Big(\big(1-2\omega(q)\big)+\omega(q)\Big)^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\leq\,\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(A_{q})}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}\big(1-\omega(q)\big)^{p-1}\\
&\leq\,\big(1-\omega(q)\big)^{p-1}.\end{split}

This establishes all the claimed estimates.

(\mathrm{D}u_{p})_{p>n/\alpha}
in

\mu_{\infty}
,

and
\nu_{\infty}

\mu_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\mu_{\infty}
,
M_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}M_{\infty}
and
\nu_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\nu_{\infty}

\overline{\Omega}

. We now also show the additional properties of the measure
\nu_{\infty}
. By the weak* lower-semicontinuity of measures on open sets, we have

\nu_{\infty}(A_{q})\,\leq\,\liminf_{p\to\infty}\nu_{p}(A_{q})\,=\,0,

for any
q

Let

By the measure identity

M_{p}\,=\,\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\mu_{p},

The weak formulation of the PDE means that for any

5.
Regularisations up to the boundary

In this section we introduce the appropriate mollifications that will be utilised in the next section to show the satisfaction of the equation (
1.14
). This regularisation scheme utilises results on the geometry of (strongly) Lipschitz domain from Hofmann-Mitrea-Taylor
[
25
]
, and is closely related to the regularisation schemes used in Ern-Guermond
[
19
]
.

To begin with, let
n\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{n-1};\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the outer unit normal vector field on
\partial\Omega
. Then, (see Hofmann-Mitrea-Taylor
[
25
, Sec. 2, 4]
for the proofs of the claims in the paragraph) there exists a vector field
\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
that is globally transversal to
n
on
\partial\Omega
, namely exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\xi\cdot n\,\geq\,\delta_{0},\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\text{-a.e.\ on }\partial\Omega.

Further,
\xi
can be chosen to have length
|\xi|\equiv 1
in an open collar
\{\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)<r_{0}\}
around
\partial\Omega
for some
r_{0}>0
and to vanish on
\{\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)>2r_{0}\}
. If
\partial\Omega
is a compact
C^{\infty}
manifold, then one can choose
\xi:=n
and the transversality condition is satisfied for
\delta_{0}=1
. Further, there exists
\ell,\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
we have

\mathrm{dist}\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x),\,\partial\Omega\big)\geq\,2\varepsilon,\quad\text{ for all }x\in\partial\Omega.

Using the above observations from
[
25
]
, we may now define our mollifiers. Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and some
\varrho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}_{1}(0))
satisfying
\varrho\geq 0
and
\|\varrho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. For any
v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, extended by zero on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, we define
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
by setting

(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)(x)\,:=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\,\varrho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

What this regularisation does is to “compress”
v
to a map which is compactly supported inside
\Omega
before mollifying. However, for technical convenience it is
not
exactly equal to the standard mollifier of the “compressed” function
v\big(\cdot+\varepsilon\ell\xi(\cdot)\big)
, which instead equals the convolution

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\varrho\Big(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\Big)*v\big(\cdot+\varepsilon\ell\xi(\cdot)\big)

(this would require to put “
\xi(x-\varepsilon y)
” instead of “
\xi(x)
” in the formula defining
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}
). The advantage of this slight variance, as we will see right next, is a simpler formula for the derivatives.

The next result lists the main properties of this mollification scheme.

Proposition 12
.

The family of regularisation operators
(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}
satisfy the next properties:

(1)
For any
v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and also
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v\longrightarrow v
as
\varepsilon\to 0
a.e. on
\Omega
and in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for any
q\in[1,\infty)
.

(2)
For any
v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and any convex function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
0\in\mathrm{argmin}\{\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\}
(namely such that
\Phi\geq\Phi(0)
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
), we have

\Phi\big(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v(x)\big)\,\leq\,\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(v),

for any
x\in\Omega
. In particular,
\Phi(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)\leq\|\Phi(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
on
\Omega
.

(3)
For any
v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)\,&=\,\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}v)\big[I+\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{D}\xi\big]^{\top},\\
\big|\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)-\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}v)\big|\,&\leq\,\varepsilon\ell\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\end{split}\right.

on
\Omega
. Also, as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v\longrightarrow v
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
and in
C^{\gamma}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Further,
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}v
in
W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(4)
For any
v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and any convex function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
0\in\mathrm{argmin}\{\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\}
, there exists
C>0
such that

\Phi\big(\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)(x)\big)\,\leq\,\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(\mathrm{D}v)\,+\,\varepsilon C^{*},

for any
x\in\Omega
, where the constant
C^{*}
depends only on
\Omega
,
\ell
,
\mathrm{D}\xi
,
\mathrm{D}\Phi
and
\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. In particular,

\Phi\big(\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)\big)\,\leq\,\|\Phi(\mathrm{D}v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,+\,\varepsilon C^{*},

everywhere on
\Omega
.

Proof of Proposition

12
. (1) A change of variables yields the identity

(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)(x)\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\varrho\bigg(\frac{x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-z}{\varepsilon}\bigg)\,v(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

which, combined with the fact that
\mathrm{dist}\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x),\,\partial\Omega\big)\geq 2\varepsilon
when
x\in\partial\Omega
, imply that
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v\equiv 0
on an open neighbourhood of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
because
v\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and also
\smash{\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}
when
x\in\partial\Omega
(and also for
x
in open neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
). Further, since
\mathrm{supp}(\varrho)\subseteq\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{1}(0)
, the integral above is in fact equal to the same integral taken over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, hence we easily deduce by recursive differentiation that
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(2) Since
\varrho\mathcal{L}^{n}
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by Jensen’s inequality, we estimate

\begin{split}\Phi\big(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v(x)\big)\,&=\,\Phi\left(\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\,\varrho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\\
&\leq\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi\Big(v\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\Big)\varrho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\,\underset{y\in\mathbb{B}_{1}(0)}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi\Big(v\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\Big)\\
&=\,\underset{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(v),\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
. By using that
v\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and our assumption on
\Phi
, we further have

\begin{split}\Phi\big(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v(x)\big)\,&\leq\,\max\bigg\{\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(v),\ \underset{\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))\setminus\Omega}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(v)\bigg\}\\
&\leq\,\max\bigg\{\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(v),\ \Phi(0)\bigg\}\\
&\leq\,\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(v),\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
.

(3) We readily compute

\mathrm{D}\big(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v\big)(x)\,=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{D}v\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\big[I+\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{D}\xi(x)\big]^{\top}\varrho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

which yields the claimed identity. The desired inequality is a simple consequence of the above identity together with the estimate of Part (2). The asserted modes of convergence follow by standard arguments on mollifiers (see e.g.
[
20
]
).

(4) Since
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is convex, it is in
W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
. Fix
R>0
such that

R\,>\,\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Then, by Parts (2)-(3) we estimate

\begin{split}\Phi\big(\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)(x)\big)\,&=\,\Phi\Big(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}v)(x)\,+\,\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}v)\mathrm{D}\xi(x)^{\top}\Big)\\
&\leq\,\Phi\big(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}v)(x)\big)\,+\,\|\mathrm{D}\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R}(0))}\big\|\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}v)\mathrm{D}\xi^{\top}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\,\Phi\big(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{D}v)(x)\big)\,+\,\varepsilon\ell\|\mathrm{D}\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R}(0))}\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq\,\underset{\Omega\cap\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x))}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\,\Phi(\mathrm{D}v)\,+\,C^{*}\varepsilon,\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
, where we have set

C^{*}\,:=\,\ell\|\mathrm{D}\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R}(0))}\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

The proof of the proposition is now complete. ∎

The divergence PDE system in

, part II

In this section we establish the satisfaction of (

(u_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},\nu_{\infty})
, under the hypotheses (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and also (

). We begin with some notation.

Remark 13
.

Under (
1.12
), since
f
is assumed quadratic, (
1.2
) is in fact satisfied for

(see the observations following Example

). Hence, by introducing the positive constant

Next, we derive some differential identities and energy inequalities, which will be utilised to obtain the necessary estimates.

Lemma 14
.

For any
p\in(n/\alpha+2,\infty)
, consider the quadruple
(u_{p},\Lambda_{p},\mu_{p},\nu_{p})
as in Section
4
. Then, for any
v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v-\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,=\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,-\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,+\,\Lambda_{p}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\partial g(u_{p})\cdot(u_{p}-v)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{p}.

Note that in view of (

\nu_{p}(\partial\Omega)=\mu_{p}(\partial\Omega)=0
for

. Thus, all the integrals above are non-trivial only over

Proof of Lemma

14
. Since
v-u_{p}\in W^{1,\alpha p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by using (
1.12
) we have

\begin{split}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v-\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,&=\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\textbf{A}:(\mathrm{D}v-\mathrm{D}u_{p})\otimes(\mathrm{D}v-\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p},\end{split}

which, by virtue of Lemma
4
(2), can be expanded as

\begin{split}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v-\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,&=\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\textbf{A}:\mathrm{D}v\otimes\mathrm{D}v\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,+\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\textbf{A}:\mathrm{D}u_{p}\otimes\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\\
&\ \ \ +\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\textbf{A}:\Big(\mathrm{D}u_{p}\otimes(-\mathrm{D}v)\,+\,(-\mathrm{D}v)\otimes\mathrm{D}u_{p}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\\
&=\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,-\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\\
&\ \ \ +\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\textbf{A}:\Big(\mathrm{D}u_{p}\otimes(\mathrm{D}u_{p}-\mathrm{D}v)\,+\,(\mathrm{D}u_{p}-\mathrm{D}v)\otimes\mathrm{D}u_{p}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\\
=\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f&(\mathrm{D}v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,-\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,+\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{p}):\mathrm{D}(u_{p}-v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}.\end{split}

By arguing as in the proof of Lemma
11
and testing against
\phi:=u_{p}-v
in the weak formulation, we readily deduce the claimed identity. ∎

Lemma 15
.

In the setting of Lemma
14
, for any
p\in(n/\alpha+2,\infty)
we have

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,&=\,\kappa\Lambda_{p},\\
\int_{\overline{\Omega}}|\mathrm{D}u_{p}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,&\leq\,\frac{\kappa}{C_{4}}\big(\Lambda_{p}\,+\,C_{3}\kappa^{-1/p}\big).\end{split}\right.

Proof of Lemma

15
. From (
1.16
), Lemma
8
and Remark
13
(noting also that
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\partial\Omega)=0
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,=\,-\hskip-10.5pt\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\frac{f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})^{p-1}}{\Lambda_{p}^{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,=\,\frac{1}{\Lambda_{p}^{p-1}}L_{p}^{p}=\,\kappa\Lambda_{p}.\end{split}

Further, by (
1.2
) and (
1.12
) (noting also that
\alpha=2
in (
1.2
) under (
1.12
)), we have

\kappa\Lambda_{p}\,=\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,\geq\,C_{4}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}|\mathrm{D}u_{p}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,-\,C_{3}\mu_{p}(\overline{\Omega}).

The claimed inequality is now a consequence of the above together with the bound
\mu_{p}(\overline{\Omega})\leq\kappa^{1-1/p}
, which follows from Lemma
10
and Remark
13
. ∎

The following result is an immediate consequence of assumption (
1.12
), Lemmas
4
,
14
,
15
and Proposition
9
.

Corollary 16
.

In the setting of Lemma
15
, for any
p\in(n/\alpha+2,\infty)
and any map
v\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have the estimate

c_{0}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\big|\mathrm{D}v-\mathrm{D}u_{p}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,\leq\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,-\kappa\Lambda_{p}\,+\,C_{0}\|u_{p}-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

where

c_{0}\,:=\,\min\sigma(\mathbb{S}\textbf{A}),\ \ \ \ \ C_{0}:=\sup_{n/\alpha+2<p<\infty}\!\big\{\Lambda_{p}\|\partial g(u_{p})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big\}.

Now we expound on the methodology utilised in the remainder of this section, in order to complete the proof of Theorem
2
.

Remark 17

(The method)
.

The estimate of Corollary
16
is the main energy estimate we will need to pass to the limit as
p\to\infty
along a sequence in the PDE system (
1.9
) to obtain (
1.14
). The main difficulty in trying that is that both
\mathrm{D}u_{p}
and
\mu_{p}
converge in a weak (weak*) sense only, and in fact in different spaces. Therefore, a priori it is not at all clear that

\mathrm{D}u_{p}\mu_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\mu_{\infty}

and the product
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\mu_{\infty}
may not be a well defined measure, as in general
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}
is in
L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
and only Lebesgue measurable, hence it may not be defined on lower-dimensional subsets of
\overline{\Omega}
on which the Borel measure
\mu_{\infty}
may concentrate (e.g. on hypersurfaces in
\Omega
or on the boundary
\partial\Omega
).

To circumvent these problems, we argue as follows. The idea is to show first that

\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,\mu_{p}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}V_{\infty}\,\mu_{\infty},\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{M}\big(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\big),

as
p\to\infty
, for some Borel measurable
V_{\infty}:\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
in
L^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mu_{\infty};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
. (In particular, the measure
V_{\infty}\mu_{\infty}
is then well defined.) Then, using weak* lower-semicontinuity we let
p\to\infty
in Corollary
16
to obtain for any map
v\in C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
(not just in
W^{1,\infty}_{0}({\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
) that

c_{0}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\big|\mathrm{D}v-V_{\infty}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}\,\leq\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}\,-\kappa\Lambda_{\infty}\,+\,C_{0}\|u_{\infty}-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Next, one would like to set “
v:=u_{\infty}
” to obtain “
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}=V_{\infty}
”. However, this is not directly possible, at the very least because directly
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\mu_{\infty}
is not well defined, and also the limiting process of the previous step prevents us for setting
v=u_{\infty}
due to the lack of regularity. To this end, we utilise the regularisation operators
(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}
introduced in Section
5
to set
v:=\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u_{\infty}
and deduce as
\varepsilon\to 0
that a
special Borel measurable representative
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star}
does exist
, which is a version of the gradient of
u_{\infty}
in the equivalence classes of a.e.-equality for both
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
and for
V_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mu_{\infty};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
. Hence, the PDE system (
1.14
) is satisfied for this representative of
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}
which in particular makes
\partial f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star})\mu_{\infty}
a well-defined measure (recall that
\partial f
is a just linear mapping under (
1.12
)).

Now we deploy the method set out in Remark
17
.

Lemma 18
.

There exists a mapping
V_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mu_{\infty};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
such that, along a subsequence
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
we have

\mathrm{D}u_{p}\,\mu_{p}\,\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\,V_{\infty}\,\mu_{\infty},\quad\text{ in }\mathcal{M}\big(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\big),

as
p\to\infty
. Further, for any non-negative continuous function
\Phi\in C\big(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\big)
such that
X\mapsto\Phi(x,X)
is convex and of quadratic growth at infinity, we have

\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\Phi(\cdot,V_{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}\,\leq\,\liminf_{p\to\infty}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\Phi(\cdot,\mathrm{D}u_{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}.

Proof of Lemma

18
. By Lemma
15
and Proposition
9
, we have that

\sup_{p\in(n/\alpha+2,\infty)}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}|\mathrm{D}u_{p}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}\,<\,\infty.

In view of this estimate, the conclusion follows by the theory of measure-function pairs of Hutchinson in
[
26
, Sec. 4, Def. 4.1.1, 4.1.2, 4.2.1 and Th. 4.4.2]
. ∎

In virtue of the above considerations, we obtain the following rather immediate consequence.

Lemma 19
.

In the setting of Lemma
18
, for any
p\in(n/\alpha+2,\infty)
and any fixed
v\in C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have the estimate

c_{0}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\big|\mathrm{D}v-V_{\infty}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}\,\leq\,\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(\mathrm{D}v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}\,-\kappa\Lambda_{\infty}\,+\,C_{0}\|u_{\infty}-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Proof of Lemma

19
. Since by assumption
\mathrm{D}v\in C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
, we may apply Lemma
18
to
\Phi(x,X):=|\mathrm{D}v(x)-X|^{2}
, which satisfies the required convexity and continuity requirements. Then, we use the estimate of Corollary
16
, together with the facts that
(u_{p},\Lambda_{p})\longrightarrow(u_{\infty},\Lambda_{\infty})
in
C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})\times\mathbb{R}
as
p\to\infty
along a sequence, and that
f(\mathrm{D}v)\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, which is the predual space of
\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega})=(C(\overline{\Omega}))^{*}
. ∎

The following result is the most remarkable consequence of the energy estimate of Lemma
19
.

Lemma 20
.

(i)
For
any
sequence
(v_{j})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq C^{1}_{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
which satisfies the conditions

\left\{\ \ \ \ \begin{split}\lim_{j\to\infty}\|v_{j}-u_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,&=\,0,\\
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\|f(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,&\leq\,\Lambda_{\infty},\end{split}\right.

we have that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\big|V_{\infty}-\mathrm{D}v_{j}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}\,=\,0.

(ii)
If additionally to the above, the sequence
(v_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
satisfies

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|\mathrm{D}v_{j}-\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\,=\,0,

then we obtain the additional conclusion

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{{\Omega}}\big|\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}-\mathrm{D}v_{j}\big|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\,=\,0,

for any
q\in[1,\infty)
. Hence, for any such sequence
(v_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
, we have

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\text{$\mathrm{D}v_{j}\longrightarrow V_{\infty}$, \ \ \ in $L^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mu_{\infty};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})$},\\
&\text{$\mathrm{D}v_{j}\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}$, \ in $L^{q}({\Omega},\mathcal{L}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})$},\end{split}\right.

as
j\to\infty
, along perhaps a subsequence.

We note that, since
L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
is a uniformly convex space when
q\in(1,\infty)
, the assumption of
\mathrm{D}v_{j}\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
can actually be replaced by the weaker condition
\|\mathrm{D}v_{j}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow\|\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
for some
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof of Lemma

20
. (i) It suffices to apply the assumed modes of convergence to the estimate of Lemma
19
, and recall that
\mu_{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\leq\kappa^{1-1/p}
, as a result of Lemma
10
and Remark
13
.

(ii) By Lemma
4
and (
1.5
) (shown in Proposition
9
), the hypothesis

\|f(\mathrm{D}v_{j})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\|f(\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,+\,o(1)_{j\to\infty}

implies that
(\mathrm{D}v_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
, for all
q\in[1,\infty]
. Hence, by passing perhaps to a subsequence, we have that
\mathrm{D}v_{j}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}

in
L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
and also
\mathrm{D}v_{j}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
. Since
\mathrm{D}v_{j}\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
, by the Vitaly convergence theorem and the
L^{\infty}
gradient bound, it follows that in fact the convergence is strong in
L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
. The result ensues. ∎

In the next result we use Lemma
20
identify the limit
V_{\infty}
as a version of
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}
after perhaps modification on a Lebesgue nullset (recall also that
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\partial\Omega)=0
).

Corollary 21
.

There exists a Borel measurable mapping
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star}:\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
, which is a version of both
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
and of
V_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\overline{\Omega},\mu_{\infty};\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
, namely

\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star}\,=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{D}u_{\infty},\ \ \mathcal{L}^{n}\text{-a.e.\ on }\Omega,\\
V_{\infty},\ \ \ \ \mu_{\infty}\text{-a.e.\ on }\overline{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

Further,
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star}
can be represented as

Proof of Lemma

24
. By Lemma

, we have

\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}u_{\infty})\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}^{\star}
in

7.
Computations for an explicit example

In this section we provide some explicit computations in the case of the
\infty
-eigenvalue problem on the ball. Let
n,N\geq 1
and choose
f:=\frac{1}{2}|\cdot|^{2}

and
g:=\frac{1}{2}|\cdot|^{2}
the corresponding Euclidean norms on
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{N}
respectively. We also fix
R>0
and choose
\Omega:=\mathbb{B}_{R}
, the ball of radius
R
centred at the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, for any direction
e\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|e|=1
, the directed cone function

C_{\infty}(x):=\Big(1-\frac{|x|}{R}\Big)e

is a solution to

\|\mathrm{D}C_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}=\,\inf\Big\{\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}:\ v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}_{R};\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}=1\Big\}.

Indeed, we have
C_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}_{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\|C_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}=1
, because
0\leq|C_{\infty}|\leq 1
and also
|C_{\infty}(0)|=1
. Further, for any
x\in\mathbb{B}_{R}\setminus\{0\}
we have
|\mathrm{D}C_{\infty}(x)|=\frac{1}{R}
, which yields

\big\|\mathrm{D}C_{\infty}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}=\frac{1}{R}.

This value in fact is the infimum over all maps in the admissible class. To see this, fix any
v\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{B}_{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}=1
. By continuity, this means that
0\leq|v|\leq 1
and that exists
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{R}
such that
|u(\bar{x})|=1
. Let
(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}
be the family of regularising operators introduced earlier in Section
5
. Then,
\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}v\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{B}_{R};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and by standard arguments on viscosity solutions (see e.g.
[
30
, Section 3]
) there exists
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{B}_{R}
close to
\bar{x}
such that
|\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v|
attains its maximum at
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}
and
|\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon})|\longrightarrow 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Then, for any fixed
z\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{\mathbb{R}}
we have
\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v(z)=0
and hence

\begin{split}1+o(1)_{\varepsilon\to 0}\,&=\,\big|\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon})-\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v(z)\big|\\
&=\,\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{K}^{\varepsilon}v)\big(\lambda\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}+(1-\lambda)z\big)\cdot(\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}-z)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\right|\\
&\leq\,\|\mathrm{D}v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}|\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}-z|.\end{split}

By choosing
z:=R\frac{\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}}{|\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}|}
if
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}\neq 0
and any
z\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{R}
if
\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}=0
, we obtain by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
that

\big\|\mathrm{D}v\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}\,\geq\,\frac{1}{R}.

The above arguments show that the directed cone
C_{\infty}
is indeed a vectorial
\infty
-eigenfunction.

We now consider the necessary PDEs that
C_{\infty}
solves. By invoking Theorems
1
-
2
and by noting that
\Lambda_{\infty}=\big\|\mathrm{D}C_{\infty}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}=1/R
, we see that there exist measures
\mu_{\infty},\nu_{\infty}\in\smash{\mathcal{M}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{R})}
such that

-\mathrm{div}\big(\mathrm{D}C_{\infty}\mu_{\infty}\big)\,=\,\frac{1}{R}C_{\infty}\nu_{\infty}\ \text{ \ in }\Omega.

Since
\nu_{\infty}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{R})=1
and in this case
\{C_{\infty}=\sup_{\mathbb{B}_{R}}C_{\infty}\}=\{0\}
, we deduce that

\nu_{\infty}\,=\,\delta_{0}.

Therefore, since
C_{\infty}\delta_{0}=C_{\infty}(0)\delta_{0}
, the PDE system reduces to

-\mathrm{div}\big(\mathrm{D}C_{\infty}\mu_{\infty}\big)\,=\,\frac{1}{R}e\delta_{0}\ \text{ \ in }\Omega.

Further, since
\mathrm{D}C_{\infty}=-\frac{1}{R}e\otimes\sgn
, where
\sgn
is the sign function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we may compute an explicit measure
\mu_{\infty}
, which in fact is absolutely continuous on
\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{R}
. By using the fundamental solutions of the Laplacian
\Delta

\Phi(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|\ \text{ for }n=2,\ \ \ \Phi(x)=\frac{1}{n(n-2)\alpha(n)|x|^{n-2}}\ \text{ for }n\geq 3

(where
\alpha(n)
symbolises the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
), which in both cases give

\mathrm{D}\Phi(x)\,=\,-\frac{1}{n\alpha(n)|x|^{n-1}}\sgn(x)

and that the system reduces to the single PDE

-\mathrm{div}(-\sgn\mu_{\infty})\,=\,\delta_{0}\ \text{ \ in }\Omega,

we obtain that the PDE is satisfied for the absolutely continuous measure

\mu_{\infty}\,=\,\frac{1}{n\alpha(n)|\cdot|^{n-1}}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\mathbb{B}_{R}}.

Remark 25
.

It is worth noting that, as shown in
[
11
]
, due to the full rotational symmetry and the homogeneity of the vectorial
p
-eigenvalue problem, all vectorial
p
-eigenfunctions are essentially scalar. Even though this is not automatically true for the
\infty
-eigenvalue problem, it does carry over to
p=\infty
at least for those
\infty
-eigenfunctions which are constructed as
L^{p}
-limits. Nevertheless, this reduction to essentially scalar minimisers is not deducible for the problem (
1.1
) for general
f,g
.

1.
Introduction

We consider the initial value problem for the system

u_{t}+\left({u^{2}}/{2}\right)_{x}=0,\ \rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,\ \left(x,t\right)\in\textbf{R}^{1}\times\left(0,\infty\right),

(1)

under the assumption that the initial data consists of a linear combination of
\delta
-distributions, more precisely,

u|_{t=0}=u_{a}\ \delta_{x=a}+u_{b}\ \delta_{x=b},\ \rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{c}\ \delta_{x=c}+\rho_{d}\ \delta_{x=d},

(2)

where
a,b,c,d
are points on the real line positioned according to the inequalities
a<c<b<d
and
u_{a}
,
u_{b}
,
\rho_{c}
,
\rho_{d}\in\textbf{R}^{1}
are real constants.

The system (
1
) is an important system of partial differential equations called the zero pressure gas dynamics system. It finds applications in cosmology and is closely related to the Zeldovich approximation (
[
8
]
). This system describes the evolution of matter
in the expansion of universe as cold dust moving under the effect of gravity alone. Here
u
and
\rho
respectively denote the velocity and the density of the particles,
x\in{\textbf{R}}^{1}
denotes the space variable and
t>0
denotes time.

The first equation in (
1
) is the Burgers equation. In general, even for smooth initial data, we cannot expect to find solutions of this problem in the class of smooth functions. Therefore, we have to look for weak solutions satisfying the given system (
1
) in the sense of distributions, namely

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(u\ \phi_{t}+f(u)\ \phi_{x}\right)\ dxdt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x,0)\ \phi(x,0)\ dx=0,\ \phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\textbf{R}^{1}\times[0,\infty)\right).

The initial value problem for the Burgers equation was studied by Hopf
[
4
]
using the method of vanishing viscosity. He considered the problem

\displaystyle u_{t}+\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=\mu u_{xx},

\displaystyle u|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=u_{0}

for
\mu>0
with locally integrable initial data
u_{0}
under the additional assumption that

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{x}u_{0}\left(\xi\right)\ d\xi}{x^{2}}=0.

This resulted in an explicit formula

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ \frac{x-y}{t}\ e^{-\frac{F(x,y,t)}{2\mu}}}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ e^{-\frac{F(x,y,t)}{2\mu}}},

where

\displaystyle F(x,y,t)=\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2t}+\int_{0}^{y}\ u_{0}\left(\xi\right)\ d\xi,

and the subsequent passage to the limit as
\mu\rightarrow 0
was discussed in detail.

Lax
[
6
]
extended the work of Hopf
[
4
]
to general conservation laws of the form

u_{t}+\left(f(u)\right)_{x}=0,

under bounded measurable initial data
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}
, where the flux function
f\in C^{2}
was assumed to be strictly convex and have superlinear growth at infinity:

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}>0,\ \lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(y)}{|y|}=\infty.

A weak solution for this initial value problem was obtained through the following minimization problem:

For each fixed
t>0
, except possibly for countably many
x\in\textbf{R}^{1}
, there exists a unique minimizer
y(x,t)
for

\displaystyle\theta\left(x,y,t\right):=f^{*}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)+\int_{0}^{y}\ u_{0}\left(\xi\right)d\xi,

where
f^{*}\left(z\right)=\max_{p\in\textbf{R}^{1}}\{pz-f(p)\}
denotes the convex conjugate of
f
.

A weak solution to the above initial value problem was then given by

\displaystyle\overline{u}(x,t)=\left(f^{*}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y(x,t)}{t}\right),

which was defined for a.e.
x\in\textbf{R}^{1}
.

For scalar conservation laws with bounded nonnegative measures as initial data, there are many published works (see Bertsch et. al.
[
1
]
, Demengel and Serre
[
3
]
, Liu and Pierre
[
7
]
and the references given there). These works are concerned with the study of scalar conservation laws of the form

\displaystyle u_{t}+(\phi(u))_{x}=0,

with the initial data at
t=0
, namely

\displaystyle u|_{t=0}=u_{0},

being a bounded measure. The existence and uniqueness of solutions depend on this initial data, on whether the solution is nonnegative or not and on whether the flux
\phi
is odd or convex. It is natural to expect a smoothing effect on the solution because of the strong nonlinearity of the flux
\phi
.

The vanishing viscosity method has been previously applied by Joseph
[
5
]
for the system (
1
) under Riemann-type initial data, namely

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}_{t}+\left({(u^{\epsilon})^{2}}/{2}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2}u^{\epsilon}_{xx},\ \rho^{\epsilon}_{t}+(\rho^{\epsilon}u^{\epsilon})_{x}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\rho^{\epsilon}_{xx},

\displaystyle\left(u^{\epsilon}(x,0),\rho^{\epsilon}(x,0)\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left(u_{{}_{L}},\rho_{{}_{L}}\right),\ &x<0,\\
\left(u_{{}_{R}},\rho_{{}_{R}}\right),\ &x>0.\end{cases}

The given problem was subsequently reduced to the new system

\displaystyle U_{t}^{\epsilon}+\frac{\left(U_{x}^{\epsilon}\right)^{2}}{2}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}U_{xx}^{\epsilon},\ R_{t}^{\epsilon}+R_{x}^{\epsilon}U_{x}^{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}R_{xx}^{\epsilon},

and the new initial data was

\left(U^{\epsilon}(x,0),R^{\epsilon}(x,0)\right)=\begin{cases}\left(u_{{}_{L}}x,\rho_{{}_{L}}x\right),\ &x<0,\\
\left(u_{{}_{R}}x,\rho_{{}_{R}}x\right),\ &x>0.\end{cases}

At this point, it was observed that

\left(\overline{u}^{\epsilon},\overline{R}^{\epsilon}\right)=\left(U_{x}^{\epsilon},R_{x}^{\epsilon}\right)

solved (
1
) under the initially prescribed Riemann-type data. The first step after this reduction was to linearise the first equation in (
1
) by the Hopf-Cole transformation

V^{\epsilon}=e^{-\frac{U^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}}.

This resulted in the consideration of the linear problem

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=V_{xx}^{\epsilon},

\displaystyle V^{\epsilon}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{u_{{}_{L}}x}{\epsilon}},\ &x<0,\\
e^{-\frac{u_{{}_{R}}x}{\epsilon}},\ &x>0.\end{cases}

The second equation in (
1
) involving
\rho
was linearised by a modified Hopf-Cole transformation

S^{\epsilon}=R^{\epsilon}\ e^{-\frac{U^{\epsilon}}{\epsilon}}.

The corresponding linear problem would be

\displaystyle S_{t}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=S_{xx}^{\epsilon},

\displaystyle S^{\epsilon}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\rho_{{}_{L}}x\ e^{-\frac{u_{{}_{L}}x}{\epsilon}},\ &x<0,\\
\rho_{{}_{R}}x\ e^{-\frac{u_{{}_{R}}x}{\epsilon}},\ &x>0.\end{cases}

These two linear problems could then be explicitly solved using the heat kernel and the
\left(u^{\epsilon},\rho^{\epsilon}\right)
were recovered using the relations

\left(u^{\epsilon},\rho^{\epsilon}\right)=\left(-\epsilon\cdot\frac{V_{x}^{\epsilon}}{V^{\epsilon}},\left(\frac{S^{\epsilon}}{V^{\epsilon}}\right)_{x}\right).

The above strategy has be used in
[
2
]
for the problem

\displaystyle u_{t}+\left({u^{2}}/{2}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,\ \rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,

\displaystyle u|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=u_{a}\ \delta_{x=a}+u_{b}\ \delta_{x=b},\ \rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{a}\ \delta_{x=a}+\rho_{b}\ \delta_{x=b},

where
-\infty<a<b<\infty
and
u_{a}
,
u_{b}
,
\rho_{a}
,
\rho_{b}
are real constants.

2.
Computation of the vanishing viscosity limits

In this section, we generalize the ideas developed in
[
2
]
to study the structure of the vanishing viscosity limits for the system

u_{t}+\left({u^{2}}/{2}\right)_{x}=0,\ \rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,\ \left(x,t\right)\in\textbf{R}^{1}\times\left(0,\infty\right)

(3)

subject to the initial data

u|_{t=0}=u_{a}\ \delta_{x=a}+u_{b}\ \delta_{x=b},\ \rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{c}\ \delta_{x=c}+\rho_{d}\ \delta_{x=d},

(4)

u_{a}
,
u_{b}
,
\rho_{c}
,
\rho_{d}

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}_{t}+\left({(u^{\epsilon})^{2}}/{2}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2}u^{\epsilon}_{xx},\ \rho^{\epsilon}_{t}+(\rho^{\epsilon}u^{\epsilon})_{x}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\rho^{\epsilon}_{xx},

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=u_{a}\ \delta_{x=a}+u_{b}\ \delta_{x=b},\ \rho^{\epsilon}|_{t=0}=\rho_{c}\ \delta_{x=c}+\rho_{d}\ \delta_{x=d}.

.

(c)

\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{4z^{3}}+o\left(\frac{1}{z^{3}}\right)\right)\ e^{-z^{2}}\textnormal{ as }z\rightarrow\infty
.

(d)

\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\ z\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)\ e^{z^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
.

The first property can be verified as follows:

\displaystyle\textnormal{erfc}\left(-z\right)=\int_{-z}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\ dy

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\ dy-\int_{-\infty}^{-z}e^{-y^{2}}\ dy

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}-\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-\eta^{2}}\ d\eta

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}-\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right),

whereas the second property follows from the definition of erfc:

\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)=\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-s^{2}}\ ds=0.

We now verify the third and fourth properties. For any
z>1
, we can integrate by parts to get

\displaystyle\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{z}^{\infty}\ \left(-\frac{1}{2t}\right)\ \frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-t^{2}}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2z}\ e^{-z^{2}}+\int_{z}^{\infty}\ \frac{1}{4t^{3}}\ \frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-t^{2}}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{4z^{3}}\right)\ e^{-z^{2}}+\int_{z}^{\infty}\ \frac{3}{4t^{4}}\ e^{-t^{2}}\ dt,

so that

\displaystyle\left|z^{3}\ \left[e^{z^{2}}\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{4z^{3}}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3}{8z^{2}}\ \int_{z}^{\infty}\ \frac{d}{dt}\ \left(e^{z^{2}-t^{2}}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{8z^{2}}.

Since
\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\frac{3}{8z^{2}}=0
, this proves our claim. This property also implies that

z^{2}\ \left|z\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)\ e^{z^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\rightarrow\frac{1}{4}\textnormal{ as }z\rightarrow\infty,

and hence, we can conclude that
\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\ z\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)\ e^{z^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
.

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{a-x}{c-x}\cdot f(B_{\epsilon})\cdot e^{\frac{(a-x)^{2}-(c-x)^{2}}{2t\epsilon}}

Reconstructing a Shellable Sphere from its Facet-Ridge Graph
Yirong Yang

Abstract
We show that the facet-ridge graph of a shellable simplicial sphere Δ \Delta uniquely determines the entire combinatorial structure of Δ \Delta . This generalizes the celebrated result due to Blind and Mani (1987), and Kalai (1988) on reconstructing simple polytopes from their graphs. Our proof utilizes the notions of good acyclic orientations from Kalai’s proof as well as k k -systems introduced by Joswig, Kaibel, and Körner.



1
Introduction

In the 1980s, Blind and Mani
[
3
]
proved an unpublished conjecture of Perles asserting that the graph of a simple polytope determines the entire combinatorial structure of the polytope. Only a year later, Kalai
[
12
]
gave a much simpler proof of this result by presenting an elegant reconstruction algorithm. Kalai’s algorithm requires exponential computation time in the size of the graph. In 2009, Friedman
[
6
]
provided a polynomial time algorithm using the polynomial certificates of Joswig, Kaibel, and Körner
[
9
]
.

There has been great interest in various forms of generalizations of Blind and Mani’s result. For the polytope side of the story, see a recent survey paper by Bayer
[
2
]
, which focuses on reconstructing polytopes from their lower-dimensional skeleta. This paper focuses on a generalization in another direction. Stated in the dual form, Blind and Mani’s result asserts that the combinatorial structure of a simplicial polytope is determined by its facet-ridge graph. This naturally leads to the following question (asked in
[
3
]
and later formulated as a conjecture by Kalai
[
10
]
):

Problem 1.1
.

Is every simplicial sphere completely determined by its facet-ridge graph?

Recently, Ceballos and Doolittle
[
5
]
gave a positive answer to this question for the family of spherical subword complexes; these complexes are strongly shellable. The goal of this paper is to establish the same result for a much larger class of spheres:

Theorem 1.2
.

The combinatorial structure of a shellable simplicial sphere is determined by its facet-ridge graph.

Recall that by a theorem of Bruggesser and Mani
[
4
]
, the boundary complex of any simplicial polytope is shellable. Therefore, our result implies that any simplicial polytope
P
can be reconstructed from the facet-ridge graph of
P
.

It follows from Steinitz’s theorem (see
[
16
, Chapter 4]
) that every simplicial
2
-sphere is realizable as the boundary complex of a
3
-polytope. However, for
d\geq 4
, there are many more shellable simplicial
(d-1)
-spheres than those that are boundaries of
d
-polytopes. Goodman and Pollack
[
7
]
(see also Alon
[
1
]
) showed that there are
2^{\Theta(n\log n)}
combinatorially distinct
d
-polytopes with
n
vertices for
d\geq 4
. On the other hand, the current best lower bound on the number of combinatorially distinct shellable simplicial
(d-1)
-spheres with
n
vertices is
2^{\Omega(n^{\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor})}
for
d\geq 5
and
2^{\Omega(n^{\lceil(d-1)/2\rceil})}
for
d=4
. The former bound (for
d\geq 5)
is due to a construction by Kalai
[
11
]
together with Lee’s proof
[
13
]
of shellability of Kalai’s spheres. The latter bound (for
d=4
) follows from a special case of a construction by Nevo, Santos, and Wilson
[
14
]
and the fact, established by the author in
[
15
]
, that these spheres are shellable. (In fact,
[
15
]
implies that asymptotically there are exactly
2^{\Theta(n^{\lceil(d-1)/2\rceil})}
shellable simplicial
3
-spheres.)

In Section
2
, we review several definitions related to simplicial complexes. In Sections
3
and
4
, we review good acyclic orientations,
k
-frames, and
k
-systems and establish new results about these objects that are crucial for the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Of particular notice are Proposition
3.2
, which provides the starting point of our construction, as well as Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
4.7
, which translate the reconstruction problem into the language of
k
-frames and
k
-systems. In Section
5
, we prove a few lemmas that eventually lead to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. An outline of the main proof can be found at the beginning of Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries

A good reference to definitions and results outlined in this section is
[
16
, Chapter 8]
.

Let
V
be a finite set. A
simplicial complex

\Delta
on
V
is a finite collection of subsets of
V
such that
\{v\}\in\Delta
for every
v\in V
and if
\sigma\in\Delta
and
\tau\subseteq\sigma
, then
\tau\in\Delta
. The elements of
\Delta
are called
faces
. The
dimension
of a face
\sigma\in\Delta
is
\dim\sigma=|\sigma|-1
.
The dimension of
\Delta
is
\dim\Delta=\max\{\dim\sigma:\sigma\in\Delta\}
. We say that
\Delta
is
pure
if all of its maximal faces with respect to inclusion have dimension
\dim\Delta
. In this case, the maximal faces are called
facets
, and faces of dimension
\dim\Delta-1
are called
ridges
.

Let
\Delta
be a pure
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex. Its
facet-ridge graph
is the graph whose vertices represent the facets of
\Delta
and where two vertices form an edge if and only if the corresponding facets share a ridge.

For a face
\sigma\in\Delta
, let
\overline{\sigma}
denote the collection of all subsets of
\sigma
. A
shelling
of
\Delta
is a total ordering
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
of the facets of
\Delta
such that
\overline{T}_{i}\cap(\overline{T}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\overline{T}_{i-1})
is a pure
(d-2)
-dimensional simplicial complex for every
2\leq i\leq n
. If such an ordering exists, then we say that
\Delta
is
shellable
. In this case, for every
1\leq i\leq n
, the family of sets
\{\sigma\subseteq T_{i}:\sigma\nsubseteq T_{j}\text{ for all }j<i\}
ordered by inclusion has a unique minimal element
\mathcal{R}(T_{i})
. We call
\mathcal{R}(T_{i})
the
restriction face
of
T_{i}
, and
\mathcal{R}
the
restriction map
of this shelling.

We say that
\Delta
is
partitionable
if there exists a
partitioning

\Delta=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}[\sigma_{i},T_{i}]
, where the
T_{i}
’s are the facets of
\Delta
and the intervals
[\sigma_{i},T_{i}]
for
1\leq i\leq n
are pairwise disjoint. Here

[\sigma_{i},T_{i}]=\{\sigma\in\Delta:\sigma_{i}\subseteq\sigma\subseteq T_{i}\}

is a Boolean interval. Partitionable complexes are not necessarily shellable. On the other hand, if
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
is a shelling of
\Delta
, then
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}[\mathcal{R}(T_{i}),T_{i}]
is a partitioning of
\Delta
.

Let
\sigma\in\Delta
. The
star
of
\sigma
in
\Delta
, denoted
\st_{\Delta}\sigma
, is the pure
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex whose facets are the facets of
\Delta
containing
\sigma
. The
link
of
\sigma
in
\Delta
is defined to be
\lk_{\Delta}\sigma=\{\tau\in\Delta:\tau\cap\sigma=\varnothing,\tau\cup\sigma\in\Delta\}
. In particular,
\lk_{\Delta}\sigma
is a pure
(d-\dim\sigma-2)
-dimensional simplicial complex whose set of facets is
\{T\setminus\sigma:T\text{ is a facet of }\st_{\Delta}\sigma\}
. Every shelling of
\Delta
induces a shelling of
\st_{\Delta}\sigma
and
\lk_{\Delta}\sigma
.

We call
\Delta
a
simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere
if the
geometric realization

\lVert\Delta\rVert
of
\Delta
is homeomorphic to a topological
(d-1)
-sphere. A simplicial sphere is pure and every ridge is contained in exactly two facets. This means that the facet-ridge graph of a simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere is connected and
d
-regular. Every link of a shellable simplicial sphere is again a shellable simplicial sphere.

Example 2.1
.

(Simplicial
1
-spheres)
Consider the case of
d=2
. A simplicial
1
-sphere
\Delta
with
n
vertices is an
n
-cycle. Its facets are the edges of the cycle, and an ordering
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of these edges is a shelling of
\Delta
if and only if for every
1\leq i\leq n
, the union
e_{1}\cup\cdots\cup e_{i}
is a connected graph. Moreover, the facet-ridge graph
G(\Delta)
of
\Delta
is isomorphic to
\Delta
. It follows immediately that Theorem
1.2
holds for (shellable) simplicial
1
-spheres. Figure
1
depicts
\Delta
(in black) and
G(\Delta)
(in blue) when
n=5
. We use
v_{i}v_{j}
to denote the edge whose endpoints are the vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
.

A simplicial
1
-sphere with
5
vertices and its facet-ridge graph.

3
Good acyclic orientations

Throughout this section, let
d\geq 3
and let
G
be the facet-ridge graph of a simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere
\Delta
. The goal of this section is to define and utilize good acyclic orientations of
G
to obtain a shelling and a corresponding partitioning of
\Delta
. This is the first step of our reconstruction of
\Delta
.

For any subset
W
of the vertex set
V(G)
of
G
, let
G[W]
denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
W
. For a
(d-k-1)
-face
\sigma\in\Delta
, let
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)
denote the set of vertices of
G
corresponding to the facets of
\st_{\Delta}\sigma
. Observe that
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)]
is the facet-ridge graph of
\lk_{\Delta}\sigma
as well as of
\st_{\Delta}\sigma
. In particular, if
\Delta
is shellable, then
\lk_{\Delta}\sigma
is a simplicial
(k-1)
-sphere, and thus
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)]
is
k
-regular.

An orientation
\mathcal{O}
of
G
is
good
if for every
0\leq k\leq d
and
\sigma\in\Delta
of dimension
d-k-1
,
\mathcal{O}
induces exactly one sink on
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)]
. In this paper, we focus on the good orientations that are acyclic. We invoke two simple but important facts about acyclic graphs: first, any acyclic orientation of a graph induces at least one sink on any of its induced subgraphs; second, in an acyclic graph there exists a directed path from any vertex to one of its sinks.

Notation
.

For the rest of the paper, we use uppercase
T
to represent a facet of
\Delta
, and lowercase
t
to represent the corresponding vertex in
V(G)
.

We start by relating the existence of good acyclic orientations to shellability of the simplicial complex.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\Delta
is shellable, then
G
has a good acyclic orientation.

Proof.

Suppose
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
is a shelling of
\Delta
. For every pair of
T_{i},T_{j}
such that
i<j
and
T_{i}\cap T_{j}
is a ridge, orient the edge
t_{i}t_{j}
from
t_{j}
to
t_{i}
. The resulting orientation of
G
must be acyclic with the unique sink
t_{1}
. Furthermore, since the shelling of
\Delta
induces a shelling on every star of
\Delta
, this orientation is good.
∎

The converse of Proposition
3.1
also holds. The crucial beginning step of our construction is the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a good acyclic orientation of
G
. Assume
|V(G)|=n
. Let
t_{1}
be the unique sink of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{O}
. Now define
t_{2},\dots,t_{n}
recursively by taking
t_{i}
to be a sink of
G[V(G)\setminus\{t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1}\})]
for each
2\leq i\leq n
. Then
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
is a shelling of
\Delta
.

This proposition follows easily from
[
8
, Theorem 3]
. For completeness, we outline the proof using the terminology of our paper.

Proof.

For every
1\leq i\leq n
, define

\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{i}):=\bigcap_{t_{j}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}t_{i}}(T_{i}\cap T_{j})=T_{i}\cap\left(\bigcap_{t_{j}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}t_{i}}T_{j}\right).

Since
\mathcal{O}
is a good orientation,
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}[\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{i}),T_{i}]
is a partitioning of
\Delta
. Associate with this partitioning a (different) digraph
G_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}}
with the vertex set
V(G)
as follows: draw an edge directed from
t_{i}
to
t_{j}
if and only if
\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{j})\subseteq T_{i}
. It can be shown that
\mathcal{O}
being an acyclic orientation of
G
implies that
G_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}}
is acyclic. The linear extensions of
G_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}}
are shellings of
\Delta
with the restriction map
\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}
. For more details on this part of the proof, the reader is encouraged to check
[
8
]
.

It remains to show that the ordering
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
defined in the statement of the proposition is a linear extension of
G_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}}
. That is, if
i<j
, then there is no edge directed from
t_{i}
to
t_{j}
in
G_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}}
, or equivalently,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{j})\nsubseteq T_{i}
. Let
1\leq j\leq n
and assume
\dim\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{j})=d-k-1
. The face
\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{j})
is the intersection of
k
distinct ridges
T_{j}\cap T_{\ell}
in
T_{j}
where
t_{\ell}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}t_{j}
is a directed edge in
G
, so
t_{j}
has indegree
k
in
G
. Therefore,
t_{j}
is the unique sink of the
k
-regular graph
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{j}))]
. This means that
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{j}))\subseteq V(G)\setminus\{t_{1},\dots,t_{j-1}\}
. In particular, if
i<j
, then
t_{i}\notin\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{j}))
.
∎

3.2
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be the boundary of the octahedron and let
H
be its facet-ridge graph, labeled and equipped with a good acyclic orientation as indicated in Figure
2
. The eight graphs on the right are the induced subgraphs
H[V(H)\setminus\{t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1}\})]
for
1\leq i\leq 8
; the chosen sink
t_{i}
at each step is highlighted in red. Note that except for the first step, the choice of sink at each step is not necessarily unique. The particular choices made here give rise to the ordering
\{1,2,5,6,3,7,8,4\}
. By Proposition
3.2
, this ordering of
V(H)
corresponds to a shelling of
\Gamma
.

Assume
\Delta
is shellable. Proposition
3.1
then implies that
G
has a good acyclic orientation. To be able to use Proposition
3.2
with only the information of the facet-ridge graph
G
, it is essential to characterize the good acyclic orientations of
G
intrinsically. For the case of simple
d
-polytopes, Kalai
[
12
]
provided an ingenious method to find these good acyclic orientations. Here we extend Kalai’s method to the case of shellable simplicial
(d-1)
-spheres. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an acyclic orientation of
G
. Let
h_{k}^{\mathcal{O}}
be the number of vertices of
G
with indegree
k
with respect to
\mathcal{O}
. Define

f^{\mathcal{O}}:=h_{0}^{\mathcal{O}}+2h_{1}^{\mathcal{O}}+\cdots+2^{k}h_{k}^{\mathcal{O}}+\cdots+2^{d}h_{d}^{\mathcal{O}}.

Recall our hypothesis that
\Delta
is a shellable simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere with the facet-ridge graph
G
. Let
f(\Delta)
denote the total number of faces of
\Delta
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M=\min\{f^{\mathcal{O}}:\mathcal{O}\text{ is an acyclic orientation of }G\}
. Then
M=f(\Delta)
, and
\mathcal{O}
is good if and only if
f^{\mathcal{O}}=M
.

Proof.

Let
t
be a vertex of
G
of indegree
k
with respect to
\mathcal{O}
. For any
i
edges directed into
t
, denote the other endpoints of these edges by
t^{1},\dots,t^{i}
. Then
t
is a sink of
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{i}(T\cap T^{1}\cap\cdots\cap T^{i})]
. This means
t
is a sink in
2^{k}
subgraphs induced by the stars of
\Delta
. Since
\mathcal{O}
is acyclic, each of these subgraphs has at least one sink. Therefore,
f^{\mathcal{O}}\geq f(\Delta)
and
\mathcal{O}
is good if and only if
f^{\mathcal{O}}=f(\Delta)=M
.
∎

Consequently, to distinguish the good acyclic orientations from all other orientations of
G
, it suffices to compute
f^{\mathcal{O}}
for each acyclic orientation
\mathcal{O}
of
G
, and then pick out those orientations that attain the minimum.

4
k
-frames and
k
-systems

Throughout this section, let
d\geq 3
and let
G
be the facet-ridge graph of a shellable simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere
\Delta
. Recall that we use
T
to represent a facet of
\Delta
, and
t
to represent the corresponding vertex in
V(G)
. The definitions of
k
-frames and
k
-systems were introduced in
[
9
]
.

Let
0\leq k\leq d
. A
k
-frame
of
G
is a (not necessarily induced) subgraph of
G
isomorphic
to the complete bipartite graph
K_{1,k}
; the vertex of degree
k
in this subgraph is called the
root
of the
k
-frame. A
0
-frame is just a single vertex, and a
1
-frame is an edge. In Example
3.3
, the union of the two (undirected) edges
37,67
is a
2
-frame of
H
rooted at
7
. We express a
k
-frame with the set of its vertices,
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
, with
t
being the root and
t^{1},\dots,t^{k}
the
k
distinct neighbors of
t
. Therefore, the
2
-frame mentioned above can be written as
\{7,3,6\}
.

Every
k
-frame
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
of
G
determines a
(d-k-1)
-face
T\cap T^{1}\cap\cdots\cap T^{k}
of
\Delta
. On the other hand, if
\sigma
is a
(d-k-1)
-face of
\Delta
and
T
is a facet containing
\sigma
, then
\sigma
is the intersection of
k
distinct ridges
T\cap T^{1},\dots,T\cap T^{k}
and can be represented by the
k
-frame
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
. Therefore, for
1\leq k\leq d
, the
(d-k-1)
-faces of
\Delta
are
not
in one-to-one correspondence with the
k
-frames of
G
. To achieve a one-to-one correspondence, we narrow down the class of
k
-frames to consider by fixing a good acyclic orientation of
G
. Recall from Section
3
that

\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)=\{t\in V(G):T\in\st_{\Delta}\sigma\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a good acyclic orientation of
G
and let
0\leq k\leq d
. Then the set of
(d-k-1)
-faces of
\Delta
are in bijection with the set of
k
-frames of
G
whose roots have indegree
k
with respect to
\mathcal{O}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be a
(d-k-1)
-face of
\Delta
. By Proposition
3.2
,
\mathcal{O}
induces a partitioning
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}[\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{i}),T_{i}]
of
\Delta
where
\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{i})=T_{i}\cap\left(\bigcap_{t_{j}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}t_{i}}T_{j}\right)
for every
i
. Therefore,
\sigma\in[\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T),T]
for a unique facet
T
and
\sigma=T\cap T^{1}\cap\cdots\cap T^{k}
where
t^{i}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}t
for every
1\leq i\leq k
. Then
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
is a
k
-frame representing
\sigma
with a root of indegree
k
. Since
\mathcal{O}
is a good acyclic orientation,
t
must be the unique sink of the
k
-regular graph
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)]
. This proves the uniqueness of such a
k
-frame.
∎

Proposition
4.1
shows that knowing the facet-ridge graph
G
of
\Delta
(and nothing else) allows us to uniquely express every face
\sigma\in\Delta
with a certain
(d-\dim\sigma-1)
-frame of
G
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a good acyclic orientation of
G
. Let
\sigma
be a
(d-k-1)
-face of
\Delta
and let
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
be the unique
k
-frame with a root of indegree
k
that represents
\sigma
. Then we say that
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
is the
principal
k
-frame
representing
\sigma
(with respect to
\mathcal{O}
).

Combining Propositions
3.2
and
4.1
, we can list all faces of
\Delta
in a neat way: given a good acyclic orientation
\mathcal{O}
(which can be found using Proposition
3.4
), we use Hasse diagrams to represent the Boolean intervals
[\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{i}),T_{i}]
and represent the faces in each interval by the principal

-frames.

.

.

Lemma 4.7
.

For every

,

is a

. Moreover,

As previously discussed,

5
Main Result

We assume that
d\geq 3
throughout this section. Let
G
be the facet-ridge graph of a shellable simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere
\Delta
. Our goal is to recover the combinatorial structure of
\Delta
.

We summarize the key observations from Sections
3
and
4
and outline the main steps of the proof:

–

Proposition
3.4
allows us to identify all good acyclic orientations of
G
.

–

Given a good acyclic orientation of
G
, Proposition
3.2
provides an algorithm to recover a shelling of
\Delta
and the corresponding partitioning. This gives rise to a bijection

\{(d-k-1)\text{-faces of }\Delta\}\leftrightarrow\{k\text{-frames of }G\text{ with a root of indegree }k\text{ with respect to }\mathcal{O}\}

for every
0\leq k\leq d
; see Proposition
4.1
.

–

This bijection turns the problem of reconstructing
\Delta
into determining
\{\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
.

–

By Lemma
4.7
,
\{\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
is a compatible family of star-like
k
-systems of
G
.

–

This section is devoted to proving that every compatible family
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
of star-like
k
-systems of
G
is identical to
\{\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
.

–

We can identify all star-like
k
-systems by inspecting all
k
-systems of
G
. Next, considering all such
k
-systems for all
2\leq k\leq d-1
, we can identify the
unique
compatible family of star-like
k
-systems, namely
\{\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
, hence the reconstruction is complete.

We now begin to prove that every compatible family
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}_{2\leq d\leq k-1}
of
k
-systems of
G
is equal to
\{\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}\}_{2\leq d\leq k-1}
. By Lemma
4.5
, when we have fixed a good acyclic orientation
\mathcal{O}
of
G
, this is equivalent to showing that

\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)

(1)

for every
2\leq d\leq k-1
and every
(d-k-1)
-face
\sigma\in\Delta
. The plan to do so is as follows. Let
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
be a shelling of
\Delta
induced by
\mathcal{O}
(see Proposition
3.2
). We first prove that (
1
) holds for every
\sigma
that is a restriction face with respect to this shelling; see Lemma
5.1
. Next, assuming (
1
) holds for all
\sigma\nsubseteq T_{1}
, we prove that (
1
) also holds for all
\sigma\subseteq T_{1}
; see Lemma
5.2
. We then combine both lemmas and complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
using induction on

.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a good acyclic orientation of
G
and let
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
be a shelling induced by
\mathcal{O}
. Let
2\leq k\leq d-1
and let
\mathcal{S}_{k}
be a star-like
k
-system. If
\sigma
is a
(d-k-1)
-dimensional restriction face with respect to this shelling, then
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{i_{1}}:=\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{i_{1}}),\dots,\sigma_{i_{m}}:=\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{i_{m}})
be all of the
(d-k-1)
-dimensional restriction faces with respect to this shelling; here
\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\}
is a subset of
\{1,\dots,n\}
. (In Example
4.3
, the restriction faces corresponding to
k=2
are boxed in orange.)

Let
1\leq j\leq m
. We first show that no edge in
G
is directed into the set

U_{j}:=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})\cup\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{{i_{j+1}}})\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}}).

That is,
U_{j}
is initial in
G
. Suppose
t\in V(G)\setminus U_{j}
and
\ell\geq j
. If
T_{i_{\ell}}
precedes
T
in the shelling, then
t
is not a neighbor of
t_{i_{\ell}}
. Otherwise
t\in\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{\ell}})
, contradicting
t\notin U_{j}
. If
T_{i_{\ell}}
appears after
T
in the shelling and
t
is a neighbor of
t_{i_{\ell}}
, then
t_{i_{\ell}}t
must be directed from
t_{i_{\ell}}
to
t
by the construction in Proposition
3.2
.

We now prove that for every
1\leq j\leq m
,
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})
; we do so by backward induction on
j
. For the base case, which is to show that
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})
, we borrow the idea of the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 1]
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{k}
is star-like, the sink
t_{i_{m}}
of
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})]
is unique. Thus there is a directed path from every vertex in
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})
to
t_{i_{m}}
. This implies
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})
because
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})=U_{m}
is initial. But
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})]
is a
k
-regular subgraph of the connected
k
-regular graph
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})]
, which means
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}})
.

Now let
1\leq j\leq m-1
and assume inductively that
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{\ell}})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{\ell}})
for all
j<\ell\leq m
. Since
U_{j}
is initial, by an argument analogous to the last paragraph,

\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})\subseteq U_{j}=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})\cup\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j+1}})\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{m}}).

Let
t\in\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})
. Let
\Phi_{k}(t)
be the
k
-frame rooted at
t
in
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})]
. Then the vertex set of
\Phi_{k}(t)
is not a subset of any
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{\ell}})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{\ell}})
for

. But this vertex set is a subset of

, so it must be contained in
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})
. Therefore,
t\in\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})
. It follows that
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})
and, by
k
-regularity,
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i_{j}})
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a good acyclic orientation of
G
and let
t_{1}
be the unique sink of
G
. Let
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
be a compatible family of star-like
k
-systems such that

\mathcal{S}_{k}(\tau)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\tau)\text{ for every }2\leq k\leq d-1\text{ and every }(d-k-1)\text{-face }\tau\nsubseteq T_{1}.

Then
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma)
for every
2\leq k\leq d-1
and every
(d-k-1)
-face
\sigma\subseteq T_{1}
.

Recall our assumption that
d\geq 3
. Let
2\leq k\leq d-1
and let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{{d\choose k}}
be the
(d-k-1)
-faces contained in
T_{1}
. The plan is to prove the lemma by induction on

. We divide the proof into three parts, starting with Claims
5.2.1
and
5.2.2
.

Claim 5.2.1
.

The following equality between two graphs holds:

G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{1})]\cup\cdots\cup G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{{d\choose k}})]=G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{1})]\cup\cdots\cup G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{{d\choose k}})].

(2)

Proof of Claim
5.2.1
.

This follows from Lemma
4.5
. Let
\Phi_{k}
be a
k
-frame contained in
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i})]
for some
1\leq i\leq{d\choose k}
. Then the vertex set of
\Phi_{k}
is not contained in any
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\tau)
such that
\tau\nsubseteq T_{1}
, and therefore not contained in
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\tau)
by hypothesis. Therefore,
\Phi_{k}\subseteq G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{j})]
for some
1\leq j\leq{d\choose k}
. The argument works in reverse by swapping “
\mathcal{S}
” with “
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}
.” This proves the equality in (

).
∎

Let
U
denote the union
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{1})\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{{d\choose k}})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{1})\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{{d\choose k}})
and let

denote the graph in (
2
). The next claim will serve as the base case for induction.

Claim 5.2.2
.

Suppose
k=2
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\sigma_{i})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq{d\choose 2}
.

Proof of Claim
5.2.2
.

In this case,
\{G[\mathcal{S}_{2}(\sigma_{i})]\}_{1\leq i\leq{d\choose 2}}
and
\{G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma_{i})]\}_{1\leq i\leq{d\choose 2}}
are induced cycles of
G
, and

is the union of each set of these cycles.

We show that the cycles in
\{G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma_{i})]\}_{1\leq i\leq{d\choose 2}}
only intersect at

and its neighbors. Suppose
t\in G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma_{i})]\cap G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma_{j})]
for some
1\leq i\neq j\leq{d\choose 2}
(note that
{d\choose 2}\geq 3
because

). Then
T
is in the star of two
(d-3)
-faces contained in
T_{1}
. This implies

, so

is either
t_{1}
or a neighbor of
t_{1}
. Therefore, a vertex

has degree

in

if and only if

is not a neighbor of

.

To prove that
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\sigma_{i})=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq{d\choose 2}
, it suffices to show that if

has degree
2
in

, then
t
is in the cycles
G[\mathcal{S}_{2}(\sigma_{i})]
and
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma_{i})]
for the same

. Let
t
be a vertex of degree

in

and let

and

denote the two neighbors of

. Then there is a unique (undirected) path
p_{1}=t\,t^{1}\,v_{1}\,\cdots\,v_{m}\,t_{1}^{j}

and a unique path

in

such that all inner vertices of

and

have degree

in

and

is highlighted in blue.
Then the respective cycles in
\{G[\mathcal{S}_{2}(\sigma_{i})]\}_{1\leq i\leq{d\choose 2}}
and

and

. In other words,

.

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Assume inductively that
\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(\sigma)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k-1}(\sigma)
for every
(d-k)
-face
\sigma\subseteq T_{1}
. This, combined with the hypothesis in the statement of the lemma and Proposition
4.1
, implies that
\mathcal{S}_{k-1}=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k-1}
.

Let
1\leq i\leq{d\choose k}
and let
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
be a
k
-frame of
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i})]
. Consider a neighbor
t^{j}
of
t
. Let
\Phi_{k}(t^{j})
denote the
k
-frame rooted at
t^{j}
in
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i})]
. Furthermore, for
1\leq\ell\leq k
such that
\ell\neq j
, let
\Phi^{\ell}_{k-1}(t^{j})
denote the
(k-1)
-frame rooted at
t^{j}
in
G[\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{\ell-1},t^{\ell+1},\dots,t^{k}\})]
. The
(k-1)
-frames
\Phi^{\ell}_{k-1}(t^{j})
must all be distinct, so their union
\bigcup_{1\leq\ell\leq k,\,\ell\neq j}\Phi^{\ell}_{k-1}(t^{j})
is a
k
-frame. Since
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}
is compatible, this union is contained in
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i})]
. Therefore,

\Phi_{k}(t^{j})=\bigcup_{1\leq\ell\leq k,\ \ell\neq j}\Phi^{\ell}_{k-1}(t^{j}).

In other words, when
\{t,t^{1},\dots,t^{k}\}
is known to be contained in

, for every

, the neighbors of

in

are completely determined by
\mathcal{S}_{k-1}
. Therefore, starting from the principal
k
-frame representing
\sigma_{i}
, we can determine the neighbors of all its vertices in

. Repeating this process allows us to determine the entire

using
\mathcal{S}_{k-1}
.

By applying the same argument to
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}
, we can conclude that
G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i})]
is determined by
\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k-1}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{k-1}=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k-1}
, it follows that
G[\mathcal{S}_{k}(\sigma_{i})]=G[\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\sigma_{i})]
, completing the induction.
∎

.

Example 5.3
.

(Simplicial

, so that
\Delta
is a shellable simplicial
2
-sphere. We will reconstruct

from

with the tools we developed throughout the paper. First, apply Proposition

to find a good acyclic orientation
\mathcal{O}
of

. Then Proposition
3.2
recovers a shelling of
\Delta
induced by
\mathcal{O}
and the corresponding partitioning. We would like to show that every compatible family

of

-systems is identical to
\{\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
. Since

, this is reduced to showing that
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\sigma)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{2}(\sigma)
for all

-faces
\sigma\in\Delta
.

Let
\sigma
be a

-face of
\Delta
. Let

or

by either Lemma

or Lemma

.

The proof of the main theorem requires only one extra step than that for the case of
d=3
in Example
5.3
. Recall that Theorem
1.2
asserts that any shellable
(d-1)
-sphere
\Delta
can be reconstructed from its facet-ridge graph. We assume that

as the case of

has been addressed by Example

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

To prove the statement, we show by induction on

that if
\Delta
is a shellable simplicial
(d-1)
-sphere and
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
is a compatible family of star-like

-systems of the facet-ridge graph of

, then
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
coincides with
\{\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
. The base case of

has been proved in Example
5.3
. Suppose now that the assertion holds for all shellable simplicial spheres of dimension less than

.

We carry the usual notations and assumptions from Example
5.3
. If
\tau
is a
(d-k-1)
-face that is neither contained in

nor a restriction face of any of the other facets, then there is a facet

such that

is a proper subset of

. By Lemma

,
\mathcal{S}_{d-\dim\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T)-1}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T))=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{d-\dim\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T)-1}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T))
. Since
\lk_{\Delta}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T)
is a shellable simplicial sphere of dimension less than
d-1
, it follows from the inductive hypothesis that
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\tau)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\tau)
. Therefore,
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\}_{2\leq k\leq d-1}
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
5.2
. Finally, if
\sigma
is a
(d-k-1)
-face in
[\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{O}}(T_{1}),T_{1}]
, then
\mathcal{S}_{k}(\tau)=\mathcal{V}^{\Delta}_{k}(\tau)
by Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Remark
.

It remains unknown whether Theorem
1.2
holds for more general classes of simplicial spheres, such as constructible spheres, partitionable spheres, or all simplicial spheres (though, at the moment, we do not know if there exist constructible spheres that are not shellable or if there exist simplicial spheres that are not partitionable). The method we employ in this paper cannot be applied to simplicial spheres that are partitionable but not shellable. While partitionability of
\Delta
implies the existence of a good orientation of
G
, only shellability of
\Delta
guarantees that there is an acyclic one. Therefore, without the shellability assumption, Proposition
3.4
will not work as an intrinsic way to identify the good orientations of
G
.

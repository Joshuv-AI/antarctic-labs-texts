Abstract
. We show that the space of first-order deformations of an orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaf over a smooth projective scheme is the first hypercohomology space of a complex which is naturally constructed out of the orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaf. We also provide an obstruction theory of these objects whose target is the second hypercohomology space of this complex.



1.
Introduction

Moduli spaces of principal bundles usually carry interesting geometric structures, being a powerful, and often unique, source of examples of varieties with prescribed properties and characteristics. Nevertheless, these spaces might be non-compact whenever the base (smooth) scheme has dimension higher than
1
. Principal sheaves or singular principal
bundles
[
GS1
,
GS2
,
Sch1
,
Sch2
,
GLSS1
,
GLSS2
]
provide a natural compactification of the moduli space of principal bundles for a connected complex reductive structure group. Therefore, moduli spaces of principal sheaves are projective varieties equipped with an interesting geometry, at least, on a dense subset. In order to check whether or not these properties extend to the compactification, we need a local description of the moduli spaces, precisely over the locus where the principal sheaves fail to be principal bundles. Such description would naturally derive from deformation theory of principal sheaves, which is still missing at present date.

Moduli spaces of sheaves have been very useful in defining invariants. For
instance, Donaldson polynomials or, more recently, Donaldson-Thomas
invariants. It is natural to consider also
G
-principal objects
for a reductive group
G
but, again, we first need to study the deformation
theory of these objects.

In this article we consider orthogonal and symplectic sheaves (see Section
2.3
for the definition).
We show that the deformation and obstruction theory of these objects is controlled by a deformation complex naturally built out of our starting orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaf. A close version of this deformation complex appears in
[
S1
]
where a preliminary study of the deformation theory of quadratic sheaves is presented, along with a beautiful study of framed symplectic sheaves and their moduli spaces (see also
[
S2
]
).

Let us briefly sketch the structure of our paper. After recalling the basic definitions of deformation and obstruction theory in
2.1
, we review in Section
2.2
the classical case of coherent sheaves achieved by Grothendieck. We finish the preliminaries by presenting orthogonal and symplectic sheaves in Section
2.3
. In Section
3
we introduce the deformation complex, providing a description of its zero, first and second hypercohomology spaces. We prove in Section
4
that the space of first order deformations of orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaves coincide with the first hypercohomology space and we construct, in Section
5
, an obstruction theory for these objects with the second hypercohomology space as a target.

2.2.
Coherent sheaves

Let
X
be a projective scheme over
k
. Denote by
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
the category of coherent sheaves on
X
, and write
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
for the bounded derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves with coherent cohomology. Given
E\in\mathrm{Coh}(E)
, by abuse of notation, we denote by
E\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
the complex supported on
0
-degree given by
E
.

For any coherent sheaf
E
one defines its
dual sheaf
by setting
E^{\vee}:=\Hom_{\mathrm{Coh}(X)}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})
and, for any complex
F^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
, its associated
dual complex
is
\mathbf{D}F^{\bullet}:=\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)}(F^{\bullet},\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Every torsion free coherent sheaf injects naturally into its double dual,
E\hookrightarrow E^{\vee\vee}
, but unless
E
is reflexive,
E^{\vee\vee}
is not isomorphic to
E
. On the other hand
\mathbf{D}\circ\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{1}
, so
\mathbf{D}
is an autoequivalence of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
. If
E
is locally free,
\mathbf{D}E\cong E^{\vee}
, but this does not hold for a general coherent sheaf.

For any coherent sheaf
E
over a projective scheme
X
, we define its deformation functor

\mathrm{Def}_{E}:\mathbf{(Art)}\longrightarrow\mathbf{(Sets)}

by associating to any
A\in\mathbf{(Art)}
the set of isomorphism classes of pairs
(\mathcal{E},\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{E}
is a coherent sheaf on
X_{A}:=X\times\mathrm{Spec}(A)
, flat over
\mathrm{Spec}(A)
, and
\gamma:\mathcal{E}|_{X}\to E
is an isomorphism. We say that two pairs
(\mathcal{E},\gamma)
and
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
such that
\gamma^{\prime}\circ(f|_{X})=\gamma
. Every morphism of Artin algebras
a:A\to A^{\prime}
, induces naturally a morphism
p_{a}:X_{A^{\prime}}\to X_{A}
. Functoriality of
\mathrm{Def}_{E}
follows from applying pull-backs under
p_{a}
.

Grothendieck showed that the cohomology of the complex
\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}E
rules the deformation and obstruction theory of
E
. In particular
\mathrm{Def}_{E}
admits a miniversal pro-family and its space of first-order deformations is

\mathrm{Def}_{E}(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2}))\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{X}(E,E)\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}E).

If further,
E
is simple, then
\mathrm{Def}_{E}
is pro-representable. Also,
\mathrm{Def}_{E}
admits a deformation theory with vector space

\mathrm{Obs}(\mathrm{Def}_{E})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X}(E,E)\cong\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}E).

In particular, when
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X}(E,E)=0
, the deformation functor
\mathrm{Def}_{E}
is formally smooth.

2.3.
Orthogonal and symplectic sheaves

An
orthogonal sheaf
(
resp.
a
symplectic sheaf
) on the projective scheme
X
is a pair
(E,\phi)
, where
E
is a torsion-free coherent sheaf on
X
and
\phi:E\otimes E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a homomorphism which is symmetric,
\phi\circ\theta_{E}=\phi
(resp. anti-symmetric,
\phi\circ\theta_{E}=\phi
), under the permutation
\theta_{E}:E\otimes E\to E\otimes E
, and such that its restriction
\phi|_{U_{E}}
to the open subset
U_{E}
where
E
is locally free is non-degenerate. Recalling that the centre of
\mathrm{O}(n,k)
(
resp.

\mathrm{Sp}(2m,k)
) is
\{\mathbf{1},-\mathbf{1}\}
, we say that an orthogonal (
resp.
symplectic) sheaf
(E,\phi)
is
simple
if its automorphism group is
\Aut(E,\phi)=\{\mathbf{1}_{E},-\mathbf{1}_{E}\}
.

A
family of orthogonal
(resp.
symplectic
)
sheaves
parametrized by
S
is a pair
(\mathcal{E},\Phi)
such that
\mathcal{E}\to X\times S
is a torsion-free coherent sheaf, flat over
S
and such that
\mathcal{E}_{s}:=\mathcal{E}|_{X\times\{s\}}
is torsion-free for each closed point
s\in S
, and
\Phi:\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{X\times S}
is a symmetric (resp. anti-symmetric) homomorphism whose restriction
\Phi|_{U_{\mathcal{E}}}
to the open set where
\mathcal{E}
is locally free is non-degenerate. Two families
(\mathcal{E},\Phi)
and
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
of orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaves are
isomorphic
if there exists an isomorphism
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
such that
\Phi|_{U_{\mathcal{E}}}=\Phi^{\prime}|_{U_{\mathcal{E}}}\circ(f|_{U_{\mathcal{E}}}\otimes f|_{U_{\mathcal{E}}})
.

For any coherent sheaf
E
, adjunction gives the identification

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Hom_{\mathrm{Coh}(X)}(E\otimes E,\mathcal{O}_{X}))&\cong&\Hom_{\mathrm{Coh}(X)}(E,E^{\vee}))\\
\phi&\longmapsto&\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}.\end{array}

Permutation composed with the adjunction is the same as restricting to
E\hookrightarrow E^{\vee\vee}
the dual to the adjoint morphism,

(2.3)

(\phi\circ\theta_{E})_{\mathrm{ad}}=\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}^{\vee}|_{E}:E\hookrightarrow E^{\vee\vee}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}^{\vee}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E^{\vee}.

Remark 2.1
.

It follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that there exists a 1:1 correspondence between orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaves and pairs
(E,\phi_{\mathrm{ad}})
, where
E
is a torsion-free sheaf, and
\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}:E\to E^{\vee}
is such that
\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}=\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}^{\vee}|_{E}
(resp.
\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}=-\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}^{\vee}|_{E}
), and
\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}|_{U_{E}}
is an isomorphism.

As in the case of coherent sheaves, the deformation theory of an orthogonal (
resp.
symplectic) sheaf is related to a complex in the derived category naturally build out of it. In the remaining of the section we will see how orthogonal and symplectic sheaves provide a well defined geometrical object living in the derived category.

Given any complex
F^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
, denote by
\theta_{F^{\bullet}}
the derived permutation of
F^{\bullet}\otimes^{L}F^{\bullet}
. Adjunction gives

(2.4)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)}(F^{\bullet}\otimes F^{\bullet},\mathcal{O}_{X}))&\cong&\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)}(F^{\bullet},\mathbf{D}F^{\bullet}))\\
\phi^{\bullet}&\longmapsto&\phi^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{ad}}.\end{array}

As in the case of sheaves, permutation composed with adjunction dualizes morphisms of the form
\psi^{\bullet}:F^{\bullet}\to\mathbf{D}F^{\bullet}
,

(2.5)

(\psi^{\bullet}\circ\theta_{F^{\bullet}})_{\mathrm{ad}}=\mathbf{D}\psi^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{ad}}.

One can give a description of orthogonal and symplectic sheaves in terms of the derived category.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists a 1:1 correspondence between orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaves and pairs
(E,\phi_{\mathrm{ad}})
, where
E
is a complex supported on
0
determined by a torsion-free sheaf, and
\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)}(E,\mathbf{D}E)
such that
\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}=\mathbf{D}\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}
(resp.
\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}=-\mathbf{D}\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}
) and
\mathbb{H}^{0}(\phi_{\mathrm{ad}})|_{U_{E}}:E|_{U_{E}}\to E^{\vee}|_{U_{E}}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

This follows immediately after (
2.2
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and the identification

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)}(E,\mathbf{D}E)\cong\Hom_{\mathrm{Coh}(X)}(E,E^{\vee}),

which can be proved by applying the standard truncations on
\mathbf{D}E
.
∎

3.
The deformation complex

Consider a coherent sheaf
E
over
X
projective and a morphism
\phi:E\otimes E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
such that
\phi\circ\theta_{E}=\phi
. Inspired by J. Scalise
[
S1
]
, we define

\Delta_{(E,\phi)}^{+}:=\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}\mathbf{D}E}+\theta_{\,\mathbf{D}E}\right)\circ(\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{D}E}\otimes\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}):\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}E\longrightarrow\left(\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}\mathbf{D}E\right)^{+}.

Analogously, when
\phi\circ\theta_{E}=-\phi
, set

\Delta_{(E,\phi)}^{-}:=\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}\mathbf{D}E}-\theta_{\,\mathbf{D}E}\right)\circ(\mathbf{1}_{\mathbf{D}E}\otimes\phi_{\mathrm{ad}}):\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}E\longrightarrow\left(\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}\mathbf{D}E\right)^{-}.

Let us also consider their associated mapping cone shifted by
-1
,

\Delta^{+,\bullet}_{(E,\phi)}:=C^{\bullet}(\Delta^{+}_{(E,\phi)})[-1],

and

\Delta^{-,\bullet}_{(E,\phi)}:=C^{\bullet}(\Delta^{-}_{(E,\phi)})[-1].

These complexes fit in the distinguished triangle

\Delta^{\pm,\bullet}_{(E,\phi)}\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta^{\pm}_{(E,\phi)}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\left(\mathbf{D}E\otimes^{L}\mathbf{D}E\right)^{\pm}\longrightarrow\Delta^{\pm,\bullet}_{(E,\phi)}[1],

giving a long-exact sequence in hypercohomology.

.

Let
E
be a coherent sheaf over
X

such that
\phi\circ\theta_{E}=\pm\phi

Proof.

, so we focus on the description of the complex

. Picking the locally free resolution

, one can see that this complex is
0
for

, and in degrees

and

Given an orthogonal sheaf

over the projective scheme

The following result provides a characterization of simple orthogonal and symplectic sheaves on
X_{A}
in terms of their restriction to the the closed subset

.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
A\in\mathbf{(Art)}
and any orthogonal (resp. symplectic) sheaf
(\mathcal{E}_{A},\Phi_{A})
over

, we have that
(\mathcal{E}_{A},\Phi_{A})
is simple if and only if
(E,\phi):=(\mathcal{E}_{A}|_{X},\Phi_{A}|_{X})
is simple.

Proof.

The
A
-module

is finitely generated. Note that
H^{0}(X_{A},\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E}_{A}))\otimes_{A}k=H^{0}(X,\mathrm{End}(E))
and
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{A}}\otimes_{A}1=\mathbf{1}_{E}
.

By Nakayama’s lemma, if
\{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{A}},b_{2},\dots,b_{n}\}\subset H^{0}(X_{A},\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E}_{A}))
are such that

is a basis of

, then

such that

with restriction

. Otherwise, as

Then, the only element in

that restrict to

is

itself. If

is

Left Regular Bands of Groups and the Mantaci–Reutenauer algebra
Jose Bastidas, Sarah Brauner, and Franco Saliola

Abstract
. We develop the idempotent theory for algebras over a class of semigroups called left regular bands of groups (LRBGs), which simultaneously generalize group algebras of finite groups and left regular band (LRB) algebras. Our techniques weave together the representation theory of finite groups and LRBs, opening the door for a systematic study of LRBGs in an analogous way to LRBs. We apply our results to construct complete systems of primitive orthogonal idempotents in the Mantaci–Reutenauer algebra 𝖬𝖱 n ​ [ G ] {\sf{MR}}_{n}[G] associated to any finite group G G . When G G is abelian, we give closed form expressions for these idempotents, and when G G is the cyclic group of order two, we prove that they recover idempotents introduced by Vazirani.

This paper studies a class of semigroup algebras called

A LRB is a semigroup

such that for any

x^{2}=x
and
xyx=xy

To obtain a LRBG, one relaxes these conditions as we now explain.
Following the standard semigroup theory conventions, for any

This element necessarily exists because

A semigroup

Note that if

for all

while if

We obtain from a LRBG

(1)

(2)

Together, the

Both LRBs and group algebras are important in their own right; the latter are fundamental to the representation theory of finite groups, while the former algebras play a key role in the theory of hyperplane arrangements, Coxeter theory, and certain Markov chains such as card shuffling. One way of understanding the role of LRBGs in this context is that they are to the representation theory of wreath products with symmetric groups what LRBs are to the representation theory of Coxeter groups.

Example 2.3

(The Braid arrangement)

Consider the special case where the hyperplane arrangement is the

2.2.
Support lattice and support map

Given a LRB
{\mathcal{B}}
, we consider two relations on
{\mathcal{B}}
.

First, define the relation

x\leq y\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad xy=y.

This is a partial order on
{\mathcal{B}}

Second, define the relation

x\sim y\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad xy=x\text{~and~}yx=y.

This is an equivalence relation and also a semigroup congruence, meaning that
the quotient
{\mathcal{B}}/\mathord{\sim}
inherits a semigroup structure. More
precisely, we have the following.

Proposition 2.5

(Support lattice and support map)

Let

2.3.
Semigroup algebras

We will be interested in the algebras defined by taking linear combinations of the elements in a semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
. Our notation will follow
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.9

(
\mathtt{H}
-basis of a semigroup algebra)
.

For any semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
, denote by
\C{\mathcal{S}}
the

\C
-algebra with linear basis
\{\mathtt{H}_{x}\}_{x\in{\mathcal{S}}}
and multiplication
given by

\mathtt{H}_{x}\mathtt{H}_{y}=\mathtt{H}_{xy}\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in{\mathcal{S}},

and extended
\C
-linearly to all of
\C{\mathcal{S}}
.

For a LRB
{\mathcal{B}}
with support lattice
{\mathcal{L}}
,
we have
two
semigroup algebras
\C{\mathcal{B}}
and
\C{\mathcal{L}}
,
with

•

\{\mathtt{H}_{x}:x\in{\mathcal{B}}\}
denoting a basis of
\C{\mathcal{B}}

for which
\mathtt{H}_{x}\mathtt{H}_{y}=\mathtt{H}_{xy}
for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{B}}
; and

•

\{\mathtt{H}_{X}:X\in{\mathcal{L}}\}
denoting a basis of
\C{\mathcal{L}}

for which
\mathtt{H}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{Y}=\mathtt{H}_{X\vee Y}
for all
X,Y\in{\mathcal{L}}
.

These bases are related by the support map
\mathsf{supp}:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{L}}
, which extends
to a surjective morphism of
\C
-algebras via

\displaystyle\mathsf{supp}:\C{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\C{\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle\mathtt{H}_{x}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\mathtt{H}_{\mathsf{supp}(x)}.

It turns out that the algebra
\C{\mathcal{L}}
is split semisimple (i.e.,
isomorphic to a direct product of copies of
\C
), and that
the kernel of this morphism is the Jacobson radical of
\C{\mathcal{B}}
:
that is,
\ker(\mathsf{supp})=\rad(\C{\mathcal{B}})
.

2.4.
Orthogonal idempotents in LRB algebras

Recall that an element
{\mathfrak{e}}
of an algebra
A
is
idempotent
if

{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}={\mathfrak{e}}
, and it is
primitive
if it cannot be written as

{\mathfrak{e}}={\mathfrak{e}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{e}}_{2}
with
{\mathfrak{e}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{2}
nonzero
idempotents satisfying
{\mathfrak{e}}_{1}{\mathfrak{e}}_{2}=0={\mathfrak{e}}_{2}{\mathfrak{e}}_{1}
.
A family of idempotents
\{{\mathfrak{e}}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq A

is
mutually orthogonal
if
{\mathfrak{e}}_{i}{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}=\delta_{ij}{\mathfrak{e}}_{i}

for all
i,j\in I
, and it is a
complete system
if
\sum_{i\in I}{\mathfrak{e}}_{i}=1.

We are interested in
complete systems of primitive orthogonal
idempotents
(CSoPOI) of semigroup algebras.

There is a well-developed idempotent theory for left regular bands, initiated
by Saliola in
[
27
,
28
,
25
]
and
further developed by Aguiar and Mahajan in
[
4
]
.
Complete systems of primitive orthogonal idempotents of a LRB algebra
\C{\mathcal{B}}

are parameterized by
homogeneous sections
of the support map,
by which we mean linear maps

{\mathfrak{u}}:\C{\mathcal{L}}\to\C{\mathcal{B}}

satisfying

(1)

the composition

\C{\mathcal{L}}\xrightarrow{{\mathfrak{u}}}\C{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{supp}}\C{\mathcal{L}}

is the identity map on
\C{\mathcal{L}}
(i.e.,
{\mathfrak{u}}
is a section
of
\mathsf{supp}
),

(2)

for each
X\in{\mathcal{L}}
, if we write

{\mathfrak{u}}(\mathtt{H}_{X})=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{B}}}c_{x}\mathtt{H}_{x}
with
c_{x}\in\C
,
then
c_{y}=0
if
\mathsf{supp}(y)\neq X
.

To simplify notation, we write
{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}:={\mathfrak{u}}(\mathtt{H}_{X})
for any
X\in{\mathcal{L}}
.
Note that
\sum c_{x}=1
and
\mathsf{supp}({\mathfrak{u}}_{X})=\mathtt{H}_{X}
:

\mathtt{H}_{X}=\mathsf{supp}\left({\mathfrak{u}}(\mathtt{H}_{X})\right)=\mathsf{supp}\Big(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{B}}}c_{x}\mathtt{H}_{x}\Big)=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{B}}}c_{x}\mathsf{supp}(\mathtt{H}_{x})=\Big(\sum_{x}c_{x}\Big)\mathtt{H}_{X}.

Example 2.10

(Uniform section)
.

Given any LRB, one can define the
uniform section
, which is the
homogeneous section
{\mathfrak{u}}
satisfying
c_{x}=c_{y}
whenever
\mathsf{supp}(x)=\mathsf{supp}(y)
. For the free LRB
\mathscr{F}_{2}
, the uniform section is
characterized by

{\mathfrak{u}}_{\emptyset}=\mathtt{H}_{\epsilon},\qquad{\mathfrak{u}}_{\{1\}}=\mathtt{H}_{1},\qquad{\mathfrak{u}}_{\{2\}}=\mathtt{H}_{2},\qquad{\mathfrak{u}}_{\{1,2\}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathtt{H}_{12}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathtt{H}_{21},

where, as in
Example

2.4
, we denote the identity element of
\mathscr{F}_{2}
by
\epsilon
.

Saliola showed in
[
26
]
that any homogeneous section
{\mathfrak{u}}

can be used to define a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents

\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}
for a LRB algebra, as follows. Aguiar–Mahajan
proved in
[
4
, Prop. 11.9]
that all such families
\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}
for a LRB algebra arise in this way.
Thus, the data of a homogeneous section
\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}
is equivalent to that of the
family
\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}
. More precisely:

Theorem 2.11

(
[
26
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
4
, Prop. 11.9]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a finite LRB,
{\mathcal{L}}
its support lattice, and
\C
a field.
Let
{\mathfrak{u}}:\C{\mathcal{L}}\xrightarrow{}\C{\mathcal{B}}
be a homogeneous section of the support map

\mathsf{supp}:\C{\mathcal{B}}\to\C{\mathcal{L}}
.
Define elements
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}
, one for each
X\in{\mathcal{L}}
, by

(2.1)

{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}:={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in{\mathcal{L}}\\
Y>X\end{subarray}}{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\cdot{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}.

Then
\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}
is a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents for
\C{\mathcal{B}}
.
Conversely, every complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents for
\C{\mathcal{B}}
arises in this way.

Moreover, these idempotents satisfy the following very useful property.

Lemma 2.12

(
[
28
, Lemma 5.1]
)
.

For all
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{L}}
, if
\mathsf{supp}(x)\not\leq Y
, then
\mathtt{H}_{x}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}=0
.

Example 2.13
.

We again consider the free LRB
\mathscr{F}_{2}

As illustrated in

Thus the maximum element is

Next, we have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{\{1\}}={\mathfrak{u}}_{\{1\}}-{\mathfrak{u}}_{\{1\}}\cdot{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{\{1,2\}}=\mathtt{H}_{1}-\mathtt{H}_{1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\mathtt{H}_{12}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathtt{H}_{21}\right)=\mathtt{H}_{1}-\mathtt{H}_{12},

\displaystyle=\mathtt{H}_{\epsilon}-{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{\{1,2\}}-{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{\{1\}}-{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{\{2\}}

2.5.
\mathtt{Q}
-basis and semisimplicity of
\C{\mathcal{L}}

There is a second basis for
\C{\mathcal{B}}
called the
\mathtt{Q}
-basis
, defined as follows.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a LRB and
\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}

a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents for

\C{\mathcal{B}}
.
For each
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
, define elements in
\C{\mathcal{B}}
by

(2.2)

\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{x}=\mathtt{H}_{x}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{\mathsf{supp}(x)}.

By
[
28
, Proposition 5.4]
,
the set

\{\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{x}:x\in{\mathcal{B}}\}
is a basis of
\C{\mathcal{B}}

consisting of primitive idempotents.

Since
{\mathcal{L}}
is also a LRB,
Definition

2.14
defines a
\mathtt{Q}
-basis of
\C{\mathcal{L}}
,
which is denoted by
\{\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}
.
Tracing through the construction, one finds that

\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}=\sum_{Y\geq X}\mu(X,Y)\,\mathtt{H}_{Y}\text{ for all }X\in{\mathcal{L}},

where
\mu
is the Möbius function of the support lattice
{\mathcal{L}}
,
and that

\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{Y}=\begin{cases}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X},&\text{if }X=Y,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The
\mathtt{Q}
-bases of
\C{\mathcal{B}}
and
\C{\mathcal{L}}
are related by the support map in
the following way:

(2.3)

\mathsf{supp}(\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{x})=\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{\mathsf{supp}(x)}.

Thus,
\{\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}
is both a basis for
\C{\mathcal{L}}

and
a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents for
\C{\mathcal{L}}
.
Consequently,
\C{\mathcal{L}}
admits the following decomposition as a direct sum of
algebras:

\C{\mathcal{L}}=\bigoplus_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}\mathrm{span}(\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X})\cong\bigoplus_{X\in{\mathcal{L}}}\C.

It thus follows that
\C{\mathcal{L}}
is semisimple and is in fact the semisimple quotient
of
\C{\mathcal{B}}
.

3.
Left Regular Bands of Groups

Next, we will formally introduce left regular bands of groups. Recall that informally, these semigroups are obtained from a LRB
{\mathcal{B}}
by attaching a finite group
G_{x}
to each element
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
according to certain compatibility conditions.
We will then extend several of constructions from LRBs to this context.

3.1.
Main definition

We start by recalling some standard notions of the theory of finite semigroups.
We refer the reader to
[
14
]
or
[
23
, Appendix A]
for more information.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a semigroup.

•

The collection of idempotents of
{\mathcal{S}}
is denoted by
E({\mathcal{S}})
.
That is,
E({\mathcal{S}}):=\{x\in{\mathcal{S}}:x^{2}=x\}
.

•

For each idempotent
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
, the set
x{\mathcal{S}}x
forms a submonoid of
{\mathcal{S}}
with identity element
x
.
The group of units of this subsemigroup
G_{x}:=(x{\mathcal{S}}x)^{\times}
is called the
maximal semigroup of
{\mathcal{S}}
at
x
.
It is maximal with respect to inclusion among the groups in
{\mathcal{S}}
that contain
x
.

•

If
{\mathcal{S}}
is a finite semigroup, for each element
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
there exists
a unique idempotent element of
{\mathcal{S}}
that is a positive power of
s
; see for instance
[
30
, Corollary 1.2]
.
We let
s^{\omega}\in E({\mathcal{S}})
denote this idempotent.

Definition 3.1
.

A finite semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
left regular band of groups (LRBG)
if for all
s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}
:

(LRBG1)

\displaystyle s^{\omega}s=s,

(LRBG2)

\displaystyle sts^{\omega}=st.

Remark 3.2
.

We follow Margolis and Steinberg
[
19
]
in defining
a LRBG as a semigroup satisfying
Equations

LRBG1
and
LRBG2
.
However, there is also a notion of a
band of groups
in the semigroup
literature
[
14
,
15
]
,
and it should be noted that
left regular bands of groups
are not

bands of groups
.
More details can be found in
Appendix

A
, which characterizes the
LRBGs that are bands of groups.

We begin by proving that the set of idempotents
E({\mathcal{S}})
of a LRBG
{\mathcal{S}}
is a LRB.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG and let
E({\mathcal{S}})
denote the set of idempotents of
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Then
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a subsemigroup of
{\mathcal{S}}
. Moreover,
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a LRB.

Proof.

We first prove
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a subsemigroup.
Let
x,y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
.
Then
x^{\omega}=x
and
y^{\omega}=y
,
and so

(xy)^{2}=xyxy=xyx^{\omega}y^{\omega}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}xyy^{\omega}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg1}}{=}xy.

It follows that
xy\in E({\mathcal{S}})
.
To see that
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a LRB, note that (LRB1) holds because every element
of
E({\mathcal{S}})
is idempotent, and (LRB2) follows by setting
s=x
and
t=y
in
Equation

LRBG2
.
∎

The next result identifies the maximal subgroups
G_{x}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
at
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with the set of elements in
{\mathcal{S}}
that are mapped to
x
by the
function
s\mapsto s^{\omega}
. This implies that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a disjoint union of groups.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG and define
\varphi:{\mathcal{S}}\xrightarrow{}E({\mathcal{S}})
by

\varphi(s)=s^{\omega}
.
Then for each
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
, the preimage

\varphi^{-1}(\{x\})=\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}:s^{\omega}=x\}
is the maximal
subgroup
G_{x}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
at
x
.
Consequently,

(3.1)

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigsqcup_{x\in E({\mathcal{S}})}G_{x},\qquad\text{where~}G_{x}=\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}:s^{\omega}=x\}.

Proof.

We first prove
G_{x}\subseteq\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}:s^{\omega}=x\}
.
If
s
is an element of the finite group
G_{x}
,
then some positive power of
s
coincides with the
identity element of
G_{x}
, which is
x
.
Hence,
s^{\omega}=x
.

Conversely, suppose
s^{\omega}=x
.
By
Equation

LRBG1
,
xs=s^{\omega}s=s
and

sx=ss^{\omega}=s
, which implies
s

belongs to the submonoid
x{\mathcal{S}}x
with identity element
x
.
Moreover,
s
is invertible in
x{\mathcal{S}}x
since if
n
is a positive integer
such that
s^{n}=s^{\omega}=x
, then the inverse of
s
is
s^{n-1}
.
Hence,
s\in G_{x}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

There are LRBGs for which the map
s\xmapsto{}s^{\omega}

is not necessarily a semigroup morphism.
We call LRBGs for which
s\xmapsto{}s^{\omega}

is a semigroup morphism
strict LRBGs
; see
Appendix

A
for
a detailed discussion and examples.

The following LRBGs were introduced by Hsiao

.
Note that we use the name

for what Hsiao calls

3.2.1.

-compositions

Fix a finite group

and a positive integer

A
G

s=\big((S_{1},g_{1}),\dots,(S_{k},g_{k})\big),

where

and

We say that

Let

The product of two

where the superscript

Similar to the case of compositions of

For example,

With this product,

In particular, the map

Proposition 3.8
.

The relations
\leq
and
\sim
in
Definition

3.7
are a partial order and a semigroup congruence, respectively.

Proof.

We first prove that
\leq
is a partial order.
Reflexivity follows from
Equation

LRBG1
.
Suppose
s\leq t
and
t\leq s
, then an application of
Equation

LRBG2
shows that
\leq
is antisymmetric:

s=ts^{\omega}=(st^{\omega})s^{\omega}=st^{\omega}=t.

Now suppose
s\leq t
and
t\leq u
, then

su^{\omega}=s(tu^{\omega})^{\omega}=st^{\omega}(tu^{\omega})^{\omega}=t(tu^{\omega})^{\omega}=tu^{\omega}=u,

and so
s\leq u
. Thus,
\leq
is transitive and therefore a partial order.

We now show that
\sim
is an equivalence relation.
Symmetry is clear from the definition, and reflexivity follows again from
Equation

LRBG1
.
Suppose
s\sim t
and
t\sim u
, then

s^{\omega}u=(s^{\omega}t)^{\omega}u=s^{\omega}t^{\omega}u=s^{\omega}t=s,

and similarly
u^{\omega}s=u
. Thus,
\sim
is transitive and therefore an equivalence relation.

Finally, we show that
\sim
is a semigroup congruence:
i.e., that
su\sim tu
and
us\sim ut
for all
s,t,u\in{\mathcal{S}}
with
s\sim t
.
Let
s,t,u\in{\mathcal{S}}
and suppose
s\sim t
.
Then,

(su)^{\omega}tu\overset{(s\sim t)}{=}((s^{\omega}t)u)^{\omega}(t^{\omega}s)u\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}((s^{\omega}t)u)^{\omega}(s)u\overset{(s\sim t)}{=}(su)^{\omega}su\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg1}}{=}su.

Exchanging
s
and
t
above, we obtain
(tu)^{\omega}su=tu
and deduce that
su\sim tu
.
The same trick shows that
us\sim ut
:

(us)^{\omega}ut=(us^{\omega}t)^{\omega}u(t^{\omega}s)=(us^{\omega}t)^{\omega}u(s)=(us)^{\omega}us=us.

Therefore,
\sim
is a semigroup congruence.
∎

,

(

) Follows immediately from the fact that

is a semigroup quotient of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

(

)

s\sim s^{2}
since

is an idempotent.

because
s^{\omega}s=s
.

(
1
)
By
[
15
]
,
{\mathcal{T}}
is a semilattice of groups if and only if

and

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG,
{\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{S}}/\mathord{\sim}
,
and let
\mathsf{supp}
denote the quotient map
\mathsf{supp}:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
.
Then for all

\mathsf{supp}\left((st)^{\omega}\right)=\mathsf{supp}(s^{\omega})\mathsf{supp}(t^{\omega})=\mathsf{supp}(s^{\omega}t^{\omega}).

In particular,
{\mathcal{T}}\to E({\mathcal{T}}):T\mapsto T^{\omega}
is a semigroup
morphism; i.e.,
(st)^{\omega}\sim s^{\omega}t^{\omega}
for all
s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{supp}
is a morphism of semigroups,
it suffices to prove

\mathsf{supp}\left((st)^{\omega}\right)=\mathsf{supp}(s^{\omega}t^{\omega})
,
which is equivalent to

(st)^{\omega}\sim s^{\omega}t^{\omega}
.
First, note that

(st)^{\omega}(s^{\omega}t^{\omega})=(st)^{\omega-1}sts^{\omega}t^{\omega}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}(st)^{\omega-1}sts^{\omega}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}(st)^{\omega-1}st=(st)^{\omega}.

,
{\mathcal{T}}
has central idempotents, which implies

sx\sim xs
for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
.
Applying this several times we have,

\begin{aligned} (st)^{\omega}&=(st)^{\omega}s^{\omega}t^{\omega}\sim t^{\omega}(st)^{\omega}s^{\omega}&(t^{\omega}\in E({\mathcal{S}}))\\
&=t^{\omega-1}t(st)^{\omega}s^{\omega}\sim t^{\omega-1}s^{\omega}t(st)^{\omega}&(s^{\omega}\in E({\mathcal{S}}))\\
&=t^{\omega-1}s^{\omega-1}(st)(st)^{\omega}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg1}}{=}t^{\omega-1}s^{\omega-1}(st)=t^{\omega-1}s^{\omega}t\sim s^{\omega}t^{\omega}&(s^{\omega}\in E({\mathcal{S}}))\end{aligned}\qed

3.4.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}

\mathsf{supp}:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
x,y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
. Left multiplication by

\displaystyle\lambda_{y,x}:G_{x}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle G_{yx}

\displaystyle\lambda_{y,x}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ys,

have the same support, then

\lambda_{x,x^{\prime}}:G_{x^{\prime}}\to G_{x}
is an isomorphism with inverse
\lambda_{x,x^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
.
The fact that
\lambda_{y,x}
is a group morphism follows from property
Equation

LRBG2
:
indeed, for all

(ys)^{\omega}=ysys\cdots ys\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}ys^{\omega}=yx

so that

; and for all

If

then

and

and so

Thus,

\mathsf{supp}(x)=\mathsf{supp}(x^{\prime})
.

3.9
):

\mathtt{H}_{S}=\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{H}_{S^{\omega}}=\mathtt{H}_{S}\sum_{Y\in E({\mathcal{T}})\,:\,Y\geq S^{\omega}}\mathtt{R}_{Y}=\sum_{Y\in E({\mathcal{T}})\colon Y\geq S^{\omega}}\mathtt{R}_{YS},

where in the last step we used
(YS)^{\omega}=YS^{\omega}=Y
, which implies

\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{R}_{Y}=\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{H}_{Y}\mathtt{R}_{Y}=\mathtt{H}_{YS}\mathtt{R}_{Y}=\mathtt{R}_{YS}
.

For the first product formula, note that
\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}
, for
X\in E({\mathcal{T}})
, are
central since
{\mathcal{T}}
has central idempotents and
\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}
is a linear
combination of idempotents (because
\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}\in\C E({\mathcal{T}})
). Thus,

\mathtt{R}_{S}\mathtt{R}_{T}=(\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{S^{\omega}})(\mathtt{H}_{T}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{T^{\omega}})=\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{H}_{T}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{S^{\omega}}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{T^{\omega}}=\begin{cases}\mathtt{H}_{ST}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{S^{\omega}}&\text{if }S^{\omega}=T^{\omega},\\
0&\text{if }S^{\omega}\neq T^{\omega}.\end{cases}

If
S^{\omega}=T^{\omega}
, then
(ST)^{\omega}=S^{\omega}T^{\omega}=T^{\omega}

by
3.11
, from which it follows that

\mathtt{H}_{ST}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{S^{\omega}}=\mathtt{H}_{ST}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{(ST)^{\omega}}=\mathtt{R}_{ST}.

The second product formula follows directly from
Equation

3.10
.
∎

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a LRBG with central idempotents
and let
\{\mathtt{R}_{S}\}_{S\in{\mathcal{T}}}
be the
\mathtt{R}
-basis of
\C{\mathcal{T}}
.
For each
X\in E({\mathcal{T}})
, the map

\C G_{X}\xrightarrow{}\C{\mathcal{T}}\mathtt{R}_{X}:\mathtt{H}_{S}\mapsto\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{R}_{X}

is an isomorphism of algebras, and

(3.12)

\C{\mathcal{T}}=\prod_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}\C{\mathcal{T}}\mathtt{R}_{X}\cong\prod_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}\C G_{X}.

Proof.

By
Equation

3.11
, we have that

\mathtt{R}_{X}=\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}
for all
X\in E({\mathcal{T}})
.
Thus,
\{\mathtt{R}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}
is a complete system of primitive
orthogonal idempotents for
\C E({\mathcal{T}})
which are central in
\C{\mathcal{T}}

since
{\mathcal{T}}
has central idempotents. Thus, as algebras, we have

\C{\mathcal{T}}=\prod_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}\C{\mathcal{T}}\mathtt{R}_{X}
.

Let
\varphi:\C G_{X}\xrightarrow{}\C{\mathcal{T}}\mathtt{R}_{X}

be the map defined on the
\mathtt{H}
-basis by
\varphi(\mathtt{H}_{S})=\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{R}_{X}
.
By
3.17
,
if
S\in G_{X}
, then
\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{R}_{X}=\mathtt{R}_{SX}=\mathtt{R}_{S}

so that
\varphi(\mathtt{H}_{S})=\mathtt{R}_{S}
.
It follows that
\varphi
is an isomorphism of vector spaces.
To see that
\varphi
is a map of algebras, note that
for
S,T\in G_{X}
we have, again by
3.17
,

\varphi(\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{H}_{T})=\varphi(\mathtt{H}_{ST})=\mathtt{R}_{ST}=\mathtt{R}_{S}\mathtt{R}_{T}=\varphi(\mathtt{H}_{S})\varphi(\mathtt{H}_{T}).\qed

Note that
3.18
implies that
\mathsf{supp}:\C{\mathcal{S}}\to\C{\mathcal{T}}
is the semisimple quotient of
\C{\mathcal{S}}
described by Margolis and Steinberg in
[

, § 5.2.1]
.

4.
Idempotents for LRBG algebras

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG.
In this section we construct complete systems of primitive orthogonal idempotents for the semigroup algebra
\C{\mathcal{S}}
.
Recall that
\C{\mathcal{S}}
is the
\C
-algebra with linear basis
\{\mathtt{H}_{s}\}_{s\in{\mathcal{S}}}
and multiplication defined on this basis by

\mathtt{H}_{s}\mathtt{H}_{t}=\mathtt{H}_{st}\qquad\text{for all }s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}.

Producing a CSoPOI for
\C{\mathcal{S}}
is already difficult even when
E({\mathcal{S}})
consists of a single element, i.e. when
{\mathcal{S}}
is a group.
We review this case first.

4.1.
Idempotents for group algebras

We restrict to group algebras
\C G
where
G
is finite. The idempotent theory of
\C G
is integral to understanding
\C G
as an algebra, and thus the representation theory of
G
itself.

There are no closed formulas describing a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents for an arbitrary group algebra
\C G

although some case-by-case constructions can be found in the literature.
For example, the idempotent elements inside the Young seminormal basis for the symmetric group algebra
\C\mathfrak{S}_{n}
form a CSoPOI.
See
[
20
, Theorem 1.5]
.

The general theory of characters allows us to construct a complete family of orthogonal idempotents that lie in the center
Z(\C G)
of the group algebra.
This collection of idempotents is a CSoPOI if and only if
G
is abelian.
Let
\chi
be a character of an irreducible complex representation of
G
.
The
isotypic projector

\mathtt{E}_{\chi}\in\C G
is the element

\mathtt{E}_{\chi}:=\frac{\chi(1_{G})}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\overline{\chi(g)}\mathtt{H}_{g},

where
\overline{\chi(g)}
denotes the complex conjugate of
\chi(g)\in\C^{\times}
.
The name isotypic projector comes from the fact that for any
\C G
-module
V
, the action of
\mathtt{E}_{\chi}
is the projection onto the isotypic component of the irreducible representation with character
\chi
.
In particular,

\mathtt{E}_{\chi}\mathtt{E}_{\phi}=\begin{cases}\mathtt{E}_{\chi}&\text{if }\chi=\phi,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

When the group
G
is abelian, there are as many irreducible representations as group elements.
It follows that in this case, the collection of isotypic projectors is the unique complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents of
\C G
.

Example 4.1
.

Suppose
G=C_{n}=\{1,a,a^{2},\cdots,a^{n-1}\}
is the cyclic group of order
n
, and let
\omega=\omega_{n}\in\C
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity.
Then, the characters of the different irreducible representations of
G
are
\{\chi_{0},\dots,\chi_{n-1}\}
, where
\chi_{j}
is completely determined by
\chi_{j}(a)=\omega^{j}
for all
0\leq j\leq n-1
.
Thus,

\mathtt{E}_{\chi_{j}}=\frac{1}{n}\Big(\mathtt{H}_{1}+\omega^{-j}\mathtt{H}_{a}+\omega^{-2j}\mathtt{H}_{a^{2}}+\dots++\omega^{-(n-1)j}\mathtt{H}_{a^{n-1}}\Big)\in\C G.

4.2.
Idempotents for semilattice of groups algebras

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a LRBG with central idempotents.
Recall from
3.18
that

\C E({\mathcal{T}})
admits a basis
\{\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}

such that the map

\displaystyle\prod_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}\C G_{X}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{}

\displaystyle\C{\mathcal{T}}

defined on each
\C G_{X}
by
\mathtt{H}_{S}\mapsto\mathtt{H}_{S}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}
is an
isomorphism of algebras.
By applying this map to a CSoPOI for each of the algebras
\C G_{X}
,
we obtain a CSoPOI for
\C{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a LRBG with central idempotents.
For each
X\in E({\mathcal{T}})
, fix a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents

\{\mathtt{E}_{X}^{(i)}\}_{i\in I_{X}}
of the group algebra
\C G_{X}
for some index set
I_{X}
.
Then,
\{\mathtt{E}_{X}^{(i)}\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}}),i\in I_{X}}
is a CSoPOI of
\C{\mathcal{T}}
.
Moreover, all CSoPOIs arise in this manner.

4.3.
Idempotents for LRBG algebras

In this section we intertwine the construction of CSoPOI for LRB algebras in

Section

2.4
and the ideas of
Section

4.2
. Our main result is
Theorem

4.3
below.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG with support map
\mathsf{supp}:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}

and let
\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}
be a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents
of the LRB algebra
\C E({\mathcal{S}})
.
For each
X\in E({\mathcal{T}})
,
fix an element
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with
\mathsf{supp}(x)=X
and
a CSoPOI
\{\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}\}_{i}
of
\C G_{x}
.
Define elements
{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}\in\C{\mathcal{S}}
by

{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}={\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}.

Then the collection
\{{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}\}_{X,i}
forms a complete system of primitive orthogonal idempotents
of
\C{\mathcal{S}}
.

Observe that these elements are independent of the choice of
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with
\mathsf{supp}(x)=X
,
since if
x^{\prime}\in E({\mathcal{S}})
is any other element with the same support, then

\{\mathtt{H}_{x^{\prime}}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}\}
is a CSoPOI of
\C G_{x^{\prime}}
by

3.12
,
and
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}(\mathtt{H}_{x^{\prime}}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}){\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}=({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{x})\mathtt{H}_{x^{\prime}}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}={\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}={\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}
for all
i
.

To prove
Theorem

4.3
, we fix the following notation
to be used throughout this section:

•

{\mathcal{S}}
denotes a LRBG with support map
\mathsf{supp}:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{T}}
;

•

\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}
denotes a CSoPOI of the LRB algebra
\C E({\mathcal{S}})
;

•

\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}
is the homogeneous section associated with

\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}
(see
Theorem

2.11
).

Proposition 4.4
.

With the above notation,
for all
X,Y\in E({\mathcal{T}})
,
y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with
\mathsf{supp}(y)=Y
, and
v\in\C G_{y}
,

{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}v{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}=\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}v{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}&\text{if }X=Y,\\
0&\text{if }X\neq Y.\end{cases}

In particular, for all
X
and
i
,

{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}.

Proof.

Note that by linearity, we can assume that
v=\mathtt{H}_{g}
for some
g\in G_{y}
.
The proof is by induction on
X
.

Base case.

Let
X=\top
, the top element of the lattice
E({\mathcal{T}})
.
If
Y=\top
, then
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}={\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}={\mathfrak{u}}_{\top}
and the result follows because
{\mathfrak{u}}_{\top}
is idempotent.
If
Y\neq\top
, then for any
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with
\mathsf{supp}(x)=X
we have

(4.1)

{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}={\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{x}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{x}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}\mathtt{H}_{x}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}\overset{\rm(\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:Saliola's})}{=}0.

Observe that this computation holds as long as the hypotheses of
Lemma

2.12
are satisfied; that is, whenever
X\not\leq Y
. We will use this fact below.

Inductive step.

Assume the result is true for all
Z>X
.
If
X=Y
, we use the recursive definition of the
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}
in
Equation

2.1
:

{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}=\Big({\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}-\sum_{Z>Y}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Z}\Big){\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}={\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}-\sum_{Z>Y}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Z}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y})={\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}.

The elements
Z
in the sum satisfy
Z>X
, so the last equality follows from the induction hypothesis.
If
X\not\leq Y
, then the computation in
Equation

4.1
holds.
Finally, if
X<Y
, we use the induction hypothesis again to deduce:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\Big({\mathfrak{u}}_{X}-\sum_{Z>X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Z}\Big){\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}-\sum_{Z>X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Z}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y})

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}-{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y})={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}-{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{Y}\mathtt{H}_{g}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}=0.

This completes the proof of the first identity.
Finally, for all
X
and
i
we have

{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}={\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}={\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}.\qed

We can prove
Theorem

4.3
using
4.4
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.3
.

Idempotent and orthogonal.

For
X\neq Y
, the orthogonality of
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}
and
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{Y}
implies the orthogonality of
{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}

and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{(Y,j)}
.
For
X=Y
, we have

{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,i)}{\mathfrak{e}}_{(X,j)}=({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X})({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(j)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X})={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(j)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}={\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(j)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}=\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

In the second step we used
\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}=\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{x}{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}=\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{x}=\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}
.

Complete.

This follows from completeness of
\{{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\}_{X}
and each of the
\{\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}\}_{i}
:

\sum_{X,i}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}=\sum_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\Big(\sum_{i}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}\Big){\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}=\sum_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{x}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}=\sum_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}=1.

Above, we use that
\mathtt{H}_{x}
is the unit of
\C G_{x}
and that
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{H}_{x}={\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}
, since
\mathsf{supp}(x)=X
.

Primitive.

Recall that if
e
is an idempotent in a finite dimensional algebra
A
and

e+\rad(A)
is a primitive idempotent in
A/\rad(A)
,
then
e
is also primitive
(see, for instance,
[
4
, Lemma D.28]
or
[
7
, Corollary 1.7.4]
).
Since
\rad(\C{\mathcal{S}})=\ker(\mathsf{supp})
,
it suffices to prove
\mathsf{supp}({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X})

is primitive in
\C{\mathcal{T}}
.

To this end, observe that

\mathsf{supp}({\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X}\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X}{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}_{X})=\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}\mathsf{supp}(\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X})\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}=\mathsf{supp}(\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X})\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X},

and so it
is one of the primitive idempotents of
\C{\mathcal{T}}
in
4.2
.
Indeed, recall that the restriction of the support map to
G_{x}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}
is an isomorphism to
G_{X}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
, and therefore
\mathsf{supp}(\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X})
is a primitive idempotent of
\C G_{X}
and
\mathsf{supp}(\mathtt{E}^{(i)}_{X})\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}_{X}
is a primitive idempotent of
\C{\mathcal{T}}
.
∎

This construction requires having a CSoPOI for each of the group algebras
\C G_{x}
which, as discussed in
Section

4.1
, is already a very difficult problem.
In the following section we restrict to the case in which each of the maximal subgroups
G_{x}
is abelian.

4.4.
Left regular bands of abelian groups

Let us assume that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
left regular band of abelian groups (LRBaG)
: a LRBG in which each maximal subgroup
G_{x}
is abelian. We will give a more explicit construction for a CSoPOI in this case in
Theorem

4.10
. To do so, we will use the dual group
\widehat{G}
of an abelian group
G
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBaG. Define

\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}:=\bigsqcup_{x\in E({\mathcal{S}})}\widehat{G_{x}},

where
\widehat{G}={\rm Hom}(G,\C^{\times})
denotes the dual group of
G

consisting of group morphisms
\phi:G\to\C^{\times}
with multiplication given by

(\phi\phi^{\prime})(g)=\phi(g)\phi^{\prime}(g)
for all
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:G\to\C^{\times}
and
g\in G
.

Given
\phi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
, we let
|{\phi}|
denote the idempotent
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
such that
\phi\in\widehat{G_{x}}
.
Thus,
\phi\in\widehat{G_{|{\phi}|}}
.

Although each
\phi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
is a function
defined on the subgroup
G_{|\phi|}
, we extend it to a function on all of
{\mathcal{S}}
as follows.

Definition 4.6
.

Given
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
\phi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
the
evaluation
of
\phi
on
s
is

\phi(s):=\begin{cases}\phi(s)&\text{if }s\in G_{|{\phi}|},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that
s\in G_{|{\phi}|}
if and only if
s^{\omega}=|{\phi}|
.

We use the partial order
\leq
(resp. equivalence relation
\sim
) on
E({\mathcal{S}})
to define a partial order
\chleq
(resp. equivalence relation
\sim
) on
\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
.
Recall that
yx=y
whenever
x\leq y
or
x\sim y
.
In that case, we have group morphisms
\lambda_{y,x}:G_{x}\to G_{y}
with dual maps
\lambda_{y,x}^{*}:\widehat{G_{y}}\to\widehat{G_{x}}

(see
Section

3.4
).
Explicitly, if
\psi\in\widehat{G_{y}}
, then
\lambda_{y,x}^{*}(\psi)\in\widehat{G_{x}}
is defined by

\lambda_{y,x}^{*}(\psi)(s)=\psi(\lambda_{y,x}(s))=\psi(ys)\quad\text{for all }s\in G_{x}.

Definition 4.7
.

For
\phi,\psi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
, with
x=|{\phi}|
and
y=|{\psi}|
, define

(4.2)

\displaystyle\phi\chleq\psi\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad

\displaystyle x\leq y\text{ and }\phi=\lambda_{y,x}^{*}(\psi);

(4.3)

\displaystyle\phi\sim\psi\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad

\displaystyle x\sim y\text{ and }\phi=\lambda_{y,x}^{*}(\psi).

Since
\lambda_{x,x}^{*}={\rm id}_{\widehat{G_{x}}}
for all
x
, and

\lambda_{z,y}^{*}\circ\lambda_{y,x}^{*}=\lambda_{z,x}^{*}
whenever
x\leq y\leq z
or
x\sim y\sim z
(because
zyx=zx=z
), these relations are reflexive and transitive. Moreover, the antisymmetry of
\leq
implies that of
\chleq
.

We let
\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}:=\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}/\mathord{\sim}
denote the collection of equivalence classes and let
\mathsf{supp}:\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}\to\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}
denote the map that sends an element
\phi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
to its equivalence class
\mathsf{supp}(\phi)\in\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}
.

Example 4.8
.

Fix a finite abelian group
G
and let
{\mathcal{S}}={{\Sigma_{n}[G]}}
be the semigroup of
G
-compositions of
[n]
in
Section

3.2
.
We naturally identify
\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
with the collection
\Sigma_{n}[\widehat{G}]
of
\widehat{G}
-compositions of
[n]
:
tuples
\phi=\big((S_{1},\phi_{1}),\dots,(S_{k},\phi_{k})\big)
with
(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})\in\Sigma_{n}
and
\phi_{i}\in\widehat{G}
for all
i
.
Under the identification
E({{\Sigma_{n}[G]}})\cong\Sigma_{n}
, we have that
|{\phi}|
is the underlying set composition
(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})
.
With
\phi
as above, the evaluation of
\phi
on

is simply

\phi(s)=\phi_{1}(g_{1})\phi_{2}(g_{2})\dots\phi_{k}(g_{k}).

Observe that
\phi(s)
is only defined if
|{s}|=|{\phi}|
, i.e. if
s
and
\phi
have the same underlying set composition.

The partial order
\chleq
on
\widehat{G}
-compositions is determined by

\big((S_{1},\phi_{1}),\dots,(S_{k},\phi_{k})\big)\chleq\big((T_{1},\psi_{1}),\dots,(T_{\ell},\psi_{\ell})\big)

if and only if

(1)

(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})
is refined by
(T_{1},\dots,T_{\ell})
, and

(2)

whenever
S_{m}=T_{i_{m}}\sqcup\dots\sqcup T_{j_{m}}
, we have
\phi_{m}=\psi_{i_{m}}\dots\psi_{j_{m}}
.

In other words, the cover relations of the partial order
\chleq
on
\widehat{G}
-compositions are of the form

\displaystyle\big((S_{1},\phi_{1}),\dots,(S_{i}\sqcup S_{i+1},\phi_{i}\phi_{i+1}),\dots,(S_{k},\phi_{k})\big)\chleq\big((S_{1},\phi_{1}),\dots,(S_{i},\phi_{i}),(S_{i+1},\phi_{i+1}),\dots,(S_{k},\phi_{k})\big).

On the other hand, the relation
\sim
on
\widehat{G}
-compositions satisfies

\big((S_{1},\phi_{1}),\dots,(S_{k},\phi_{k})\big)\sim\big((T_{1},\psi_{1}),\dots,(T_{\ell},\psi_{\ell})\big)

if and only if
k=\ell
and there is a permutation

such that
(S_{i},\phi_{i})=(T_{\sigma(i)},\psi_{\sigma(i)})
for all

.
Thus,

5.
LRBGs with symmetry

We will now consider the case of LRBGs where there is a natural group action.

5.1.
Motivation

Studying LRBGs with a natural group action is motivated by a beautiful result of Bidigare
[
9
]
relating the face algebra of a hyperplane arrangement to Solomon’s Descent algebra. Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{W}
be the
reflection arrangement
associated to a finite Coxeter group
W
, meaning that the hyperplanes
H\in{\mathcal{A}}_{W}
are the fixed space of the reflections in
W
. Then
W
acts on
{\mathcal{A}}_{W}
, and this action extends to the faces
\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}_{W}}
.
Moreover, this action satisfies

w\cdot({\sf F\cdot G})=(w\cdot{\sf F})\cdot(w\cdot{\sf G}).

Thus the algebra
\C\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}_{W}}
is a
W
-module, where

w\cdot\mathtt{H}_{x}=\mathtt{H}_{w\cdot x}.

On the other hand, Solomon proved in
[
29
]
that any finite Coxeter group defines a subalgebra
\sol(W)
of the group algebra
\C W
spanned by sums of elements in
W
with the same
Coxeter descent set
.

In his thesis, Bidigare studied the subalgebra of
W
-invariants
\left(\C\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}_{W}}\right)^{W}
, proving the following:

Theorem 5.1

(Bidigare)
.

For any finite Coxeter group
W
, the algebra
\left(\C\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}_{W}}\right)^{W}
is anti-isomorphic to Solomon’s Descent algebra
\sol(W)
.

In fact, we may take Bidigare’s theorem as definition; through this lens,
\sol(W)
is the precisely the opposite algebra of
\left(\C\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}_{W}}\right)^{W}
.

Hsiao showed that studying the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariants of
the algebra
\C{{\Sigma_{n}[G]}}
yields a parallel relationship to an algebra called the
Mantaci–Reutenauer algebra

{\sf{MR}}_{n}[G]
, to be defined and discussed in
Section

7
. Before doing so, we will define notation and conventions for studying the invariant algebras of any LRBG algebra
\C{\mathcal{S}}
with symmetry.

5.3.
The invariant subalgebra of a LRBaG

Let
W
be a finite group acting on a LRBaG
{\mathcal{S}}
.
We extend this action to the semigroup algebra
\C{\mathcal{S}}
by permuting the
\mathtt{H}
-basis:

w\cdot\mathtt{H}_{s}:=\mathtt{H}_{w\cdot s}\qquad\text{ for all }s\in{\mathcal{S}}.

The basis of local idempotents
\{\mathtt{E}_{\phi}\}_{\phi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}}
is also permuted by the
W
-action:

w\cdot\mathtt{E}_{\phi}=w\cdot\Big(\dfrac{1}{|G_{|{\phi}|}|}\sum_{s\in G_{|{\phi}|}}\overline{\phi(s)}\mathtt{H}_{s}\Big)=\dfrac{1}{|G_{|{\phi}|}|}\sum_{s\in G_{|{\phi}|}}\overline{\phi(s)}\mathtt{H}_{w\cdot s}=\mathtt{E}_{w\cdot\phi},

where
w\cdot\phi
corresponds to the
W
action on
\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
,
and we used that
\phi(s)=(w\cdot\phi)(w\cdot s)
for all
\phi\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}
and
s\in G_{|{\phi}|}
.
Therefore, summing over
W
-orbits of the
\mathtt{H}
-basis and the
\mathtt{E}
-basis,
we obtain two different bases for the invariant subalgebra
(\C{\mathcal{S}})^{W}
, one indexed by
{\mathcal{S}}^{W}
and one indexed by
\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}^{W}
.
Explicitly, define
\{\mathcal{H}_{p}\}_{p\in{\mathcal{S}}^{W}}
and
\{\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}^{W}}
by:

(5.3)

\mathcal{H}_{p}:=\sum_{\mathsf{type}(s)=p}\mathtt{H}_{s}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}:=\sum_{\mathsf{type}(\phi)=\alpha}\mathtt{E}_{\phi}.

Example 5.4
.

In
\Sigma_{3}[G]
, we have

\mathcal{H}_{(2^{g},1^{h})}=\mathtt{H}_{(12^{g}\mid 3^{h})}+\mathtt{H}_{(13^{g}\mid 2^{h})}+\mathtt{H}_{(23^{g}\mid 1^{h})},

\mathcal{E}_{(2^{\phi},1^{\psi})}=\mathtt{E}_{(12^{\phi}\mid 3^{\psi})}+\mathtt{E}_{(13^{\phi}\mid 2^{\psi})}+\mathtt{E}_{(23^{\phi}\mid 1^{\psi})}.

4.3
compatible with the

-action, we need to carefully choose the homogeneous section
{\mathfrak{u}}
.
We say that

is a

-invariant homogeneous section of the support map
if it is a homogeneous section

\{{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}\}_{X\in E({\mathcal{T}})}\subseteq\C E({\mathcal{S}})
such that

w\cdot{\mathfrak{u}}_{X}={\mathfrak{u}}_{w\cdot X}\qquad\text{for all }w\in W\text{ and }X\in E({\mathcal{T}}).

This is equivalent to the map
{\mathfrak{u}}:\C E({\mathcal{T}})\to\C E({\mathcal{S}})
being

-equivariant.

Let
{\mathfrak{u}}
be a

-invariant homogeneous section.
It then follows from the definitions that the corresponding elements
{\mathfrak{e}}^{\circ}
,
\mathtt{Q}^{\circ}
,
\mathtt{R}
, and

The action of

6.2.
External product on Hsiao’s algebra

For any finite group
G
, we define an external product on the space
\C\Sigma[G]
of all
G
-compositions:

\C\Sigma[G]:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\C\Sigma_{n}[G].

Given a set composition
x
of a nonempty subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
of size
n
, its
standardization

{\rm std}(x)\in\Sigma_{n}
is the composition of
[n]
induced by the only order preserving bijection
S\to[n]
. For example,

{\rm std}\Big(\big(368\mid 15\mid 9\big)\Big)=\big(245\mid 13\mid 6\big).

We extend this definition to
G
-compositions (resp.
\widehat{G}
-compositions) by leaving the group elements (resp. characters) unchanged.
For example,

{\rm std}\Big(\big(368^{g_{1}}\mid 15^{g_{2}}\mid 9^{g_{3}}\big)\Big)=\big(245^{g_{1}}\mid 13^{g_{2}}\mid 6^{g_{3}}\big).

Given two
G
-compositions
s,t
(resp.
\widehat{G}
-compositions
\phi,\psi
) of disjoint subsets
S,T\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, we let
(s,t)
(resp.
(\phi,\psi)
) denote their concatenation.
For example, if

\phi=\big(368^{\phi_{1}}\mid 15^{\phi_{2}}\big)\text{~and~}\psi=\big(29^{\psi_{1}}\mid 7^{\psi_{2}}\big),\text{~then~}(\phi,\psi)=\big(368^{\phi_{1}}\mid 15^{\phi_{2}}\mid 29^{\psi_{1}}\mid 7^{\psi_{2}}\big).

Definition 6.11
.

Given
s\in\Sigma_{n}[G]
and
t\in\Sigma_{m}[G]
, define

\mathtt{H}_{s}\star\mathtt{H}_{t}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\in\Gamma_{n+m}[G]\\
{\rm std}(u)=s,\,{\rm std}(v)=t\end{subarray}}\mathtt{H}_{(u,v)}.

Example 6.12
.

With
G={C_{2}}
, we have

\mathtt{H}_{({13}^{-},2^{+})}\star\mathtt{H}_{(1^{-})}=\mathtt{H}_{(13^{-},2^{+},4^{-})}+\mathtt{H}_{(14^{-},2^{+},3^{-})}+\mathtt{H}_{(14^{-},3^{+},2^{-})}+\mathtt{H}_{(24^{-},3^{+},1^{-})}.

Proposition 6.13
.

Extending the
\star
product linearly endows
\C\Sigma[G]
with the structure of a graded associative unital algebra.

Proof.

The unit of
\C\Sigma[G]
under the
\star
product is
\mathtt{H}_{()}
, where
()\in\Sigma_{0}[G]
denotes the only
G
-composition of the empty set.
The associativity is easily verified in the
\mathtt{H}
-basis since (i.) concatenation of
G
-compositions is associative and (ii.) if
(s,t)={\rm std}(u,v)
, where
\ell(s)=\ell(u)
and
\ell(t)=\ell(v)
, and
r={\rm std}(s)
, then
r={\rm std}(u)
.
∎

Remark 6.14
.

When
G
is the trivial group,
\C\Sigma[G]
is the dual of the coalgebra of set compositions introduced by Chapoton
[
13
]
and which has been further studied in relation with Coxeter groups and Species in
[

.

7.1.
The Mantaci–Reutenauer algebra

In
[
17
]
, Hsiao established a connection between his algebra
\C{{\Sigma_{n}[G]}}
and a combinatorial algebra called the
Mantaci–Reutenauer algebra

{\sf{MR}}_{n}[G]
, which was introduced by Mantaci and Reutenauer in
[
18
]
and is a subalgebra of
\C\mathfrak{S}_{n}[G]
.
Intuitively,
{\sf{MR}}_{n}[G]
can be thought of as a “colored” analogue of Solomon’s Descent algebra
\sol(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
, consisting of elements of
\sol(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
“colored” by the elements of
G
.
Though originally defined only when
G
is abelian, more recently, Baumann–Hohlweg
[
6
]
and Hsiao
[
17
]
gave generalizations for any finite group.

Recall that the wreath product
\mathfrak{S}_{n}[G]
is a group with underlying set
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times G^{n}
. Write a generic element in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}[G]
as
(\sigma,g)=\big((\sigma_{1},g_{1}),(\sigma_{2},g_{2}),\cdots,(\sigma_{n},g_{n})\big)
, so
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is in one-line notation. Multiplication is then given by

\big((\sigma_{1},g_{1}),\cdots,(\sigma_{n},g_{n})\big)\cdot\big((\tau_{1},h_{1}),\cdots,(\tau_{n},h_{n})\big)=((\sigma_{\tau_{1}},g_{\tau_{1}}h_{1}),\cdots,(\sigma_{\tau_{n}},g_{\tau_{n}}h_{n})).

For
(\sigma,g)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}[G]
, let
\co(\sigma,g)=\big((a_{1},h_{1}),\cdots,(a_{k},h_{k})\big)\in{\Gamma_{n}[G]}
be the coarsest
G
-composition of
n
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}<\cdots<\sigma_{a_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003ptg_{1}=g_{2}=\cdots g_{a_{1}}=h_{1},

\displaystyle\sigma_{a_{1}+1}<\cdots<\sigma_{a_{1}+a_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003ptg_{a_{1}+1}=\cdots=g_{a_{1}+a_{2}}=h_{2},

\displaystyle\sigma_{a_{1}+\cdots a_{k-1}+1}<\cdots<\sigma_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003ptg_{a_{1}+\cdots a_{k-1}+1}=\cdots=g_{n}=h_{k}.

That
\co(\sigma,g)
is the coarsest composition with this property means that:
h_{1}\neq h_{2}
or
\sigma_{a_{1}}>\sigma_{a_{1}+1}
;
h_{2}\neq h_{3}
or
\sigma_{a_{2}}>\sigma_{a_{2}+1}
; and so on.
Thus if
G
is trivial,
\co(\sigma,g)
encodes the descents of
\sigma
, which are the positions
i\in[n-1]
such that
\sigma_{i}>\sigma_{i+1}
.

Example 7.1
.

Take
G={C_{2}}
.

Appendix A
LRBGs and BGs

In the semigroup theory literature, one finds the notion of a
band of
groups

[
14
,
15
]
,
but it should be noted that
left regular bands of groups
,
as defined in
[
19
]
and in this paper,
are not bands of groups as one might expect from the name.
This appendix serves to outline the differences and to characterize the left
regular bands of groups that are bands of groups
(which we call
strict left regular bands of groups
).

A.1.
Bands of semigroups

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a finite semigroup.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}

be a family of disjoint subsemigroups of
{\mathcal{S}}

such that for every pair
{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}\in{\mathcal{B}}
,
there exists
{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{B}}

such that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}
.
Then
{\mathcal{B}}
is a semigroup for the operation:

{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\ast{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}={\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{\gamma}.

Since
{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}
, it follows that
{\mathcal{B}}

is a
band
(i.e., a semigroup all of whose elements are idempotents),
and we call
{\mathcal{B}}
a
band of semigroups
.
If in addition
{\mathcal{B}}
is commutative, then we call
{\mathcal{B}}

a
semilattice of semigroups
, since a
semilattice
is a semigroup
that is a commutative band.

A semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
union of a band of semigroups
if there exists
a band of semigroups
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
in
{\mathcal{S}}
such that

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigsqcup_{{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\in I}{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}.

Similarly, one defines
semilattices of semigroups
.
By an abuse of terminology, one sometimes encounters
“
{\mathcal{S}}
is a band of
semigroups”
in place of
“
{\mathcal{S}}
is a union of a band of semigroups”
.

If
{\mathcal{S}}
is the union of the band of semigroups
{\mathcal{B}}=\{{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
, then the function

\varphi:{\mathcal{S}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathcal{B}}
defined by

\varphi(s)={\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}
for
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}
is a semigroup
morphism as
s\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}
and
t\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}
imply that

st\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha}\ast{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}
.
Conversely, every union of a band of semigroups arises in this way: if

\varphi:{\mathcal{S}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathcal{B}}
is a surjective semigroup morphism
onto a band
{\mathcal{B}}
, then
{\mathcal{S}}
is the union of the band of semigroups

\{\varphi^{-1}(b)\}_{b\in{\mathcal{B}}}
.

A.2.
Bands of groups

We are mostly interested in the case where each of the semigroups in a band of
semigroups is a group, so we state this definition explicitly.

Definition A.1
.

A semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
union of a band of groups
if there is a decomposition

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in I}G_{\alpha}

into disjoint subsemigroups of
{\mathcal{S}}
such that

(i)

each
G_{\alpha}
is a group, and

(ii)

for each pair
\alpha,\beta\in I
, there exists
a unique
\gamma\in I
such that
G_{\alpha}G_{\beta}\subseteq G_{\gamma}
.

Theorem A.2

(
[
14
, Theorem 1]
)
.

A semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
is a union of a band of groups if and only if there exists a band
{\mathcal{B}}
and a
semigroup morphism
{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
such that the preimage of each
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
is a group.

Example A.3
.

Every band
{\mathcal{B}}
is the union of a band of groups: consider the identity
morphism
{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathcal{B}}
. In this case, the preimages are trivial
groups.

Example A.4
.

Fix a group
G
and a band
{\mathcal{B}}
. Endow
{\mathcal{S}}:={\mathcal{B}}\times G
with the
coordinate-wise product. Then,
{\mathcal{S}}
is a the union of the band of groups

\{{\mathcal{S}}_{b}\}_{b\in{\mathcal{B}}}
, where
{\mathcal{S}}_{b}=\{b\}\times G
.
Note that
{\mathcal{S}}_{b}\cong G
.

Remark A.5
.

Note that if
{\mathcal{S}}
is a union of a band of groups

{\mathcal{B}}=\{G_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
,
then
E({\mathcal{S}})
is in bijection with
{\mathcal{B}}

since each of the subgroups
G_{\beta}
contains exactly one idempotent of

{\mathcal{S}}
. However,
E({\mathcal{S}})
is not necessarily isomorphic to
{\mathcal{B}}
;
in fact,
E({\mathcal{S}})
is not necessarily a subsemigroup of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

A.3.
Left Regular Bands of Groups

Recall that for each element
s
in a finite semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}
, there exists a unique idempotent that is a positive power of
s
and it is denoted by
s^{\omega}
.
Moreover, we can pick
\omega\in\Z_{>0}
uniformly for all
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
, for instance by setting
\omega=|{\mathcal{S}}|!
(see
[
30
, Remark 1.3]
). To ease exposition, we will henceforth assume that
\omega=|{\mathcal{S}}|!
, so that it does not depend on a particular
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
. This assumption does not change any of the claims made.
Following
[
19
]
, we define a

left regular band of groups (LRBG)
to be a semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}

such that for all
s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}
:

(LRBG1)

\displaystyle s^{\omega}s

\displaystyle=s,

(LRBG2)

\displaystyle sts^{\omega}

\displaystyle=st.

If every element of a LRBG is idempotent, then we obtain a semigroup called
a
left regular band
: more precisely,
a semigroup
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
left regular band (LRB)
if for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{B}}
:

(LRB1)

\displaystyle x^{2}

\displaystyle=x,

(LRB2)

\displaystyle xyx

\displaystyle=xy.

LRBs form an important family of examples of LRBGs, and we will see that every
LRBG is obtained by attaching a group to each element of a LRB (subject to some
compatibility conditions).

Lemma A.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG and let
E({\mathcal{S}})
denote the set of idempotents of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Then
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a subsemigroup of
{\mathcal{S}}
. Moreover,
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a LRB.

Proof.

We first prove
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a subsemigroup.
Let
x,y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
.
Then
x^{\omega}=x
and
y^{\omega}=y
,
and so

(xy)^{2}=xyxy=xyx^{\omega}y^{\omega}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}xyy^{\omega}\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg1}}{=}xy.

It follows that
xy\in E({\mathcal{S}})
.
To see that
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a LRB, note that (LRB1) holds because every element
of
E({\mathcal{S}})
is idempotent, and (LRB2) follows by setting
s=x
and
t=y
in
Equation

LRBG2
.
∎

The next result identifies the maximal subgroups of
{\mathcal{S}}
at an idempotent
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with the set of elements in
{\mathcal{S}}
that are mapped to
x
by the
function
s\mapsto s^{\omega}
.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG and define
\varphi:{\mathcal{S}}\xrightarrow{}E({\mathcal{S}})
by

\varphi(s)=s^{\omega}
.
Then for each
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
, the preimage

\varphi^{-1}(\{x\})=\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}:s^{\omega}=x\}
is the maximal
subgroup
G_{x}
of
{\mathcal{S}}
at
x
.

Proof.

We first prove
G_{x}\subseteq\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}:s^{\omega}=x\}
.
If
s
is an element of the finite group
G_{x}
,
then some positive power of
s
coincides with the
identity element of
G_{x}
, which is
x
.
Hence,
s^{\omega}=x
.

Conversely, suppose
s^{\omega}=x
.
By
Equation

LRBG1
,
xs=s^{\omega}s=s
and

sx=ss^{\omega}=s
, which implies
s

belongs to the submonoid
x{\mathcal{S}}x
with identity element
x
.
Moreover,
s
is invertible in
x{\mathcal{S}}x
since if
n
is a positive integer
such that
s^{n}=s^{\omega}=x
, then the inverse of
s
is
s^{n-1}
.
Hence,
s\in G_{x}
.
∎

It follows from
Lemma

A.7
that

{\mathcal{S}}
decomposes into a disjoint union of subgroups as follows:

(A.1)

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigsqcup_{x\in E({\mathcal{S}})}G_{x},\qquad\text{where~}G_{x}=\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}:s^{\omega}=x\}.

Therefore, every LRBG is a
union of groups
. However, it is not
necessarily a
union of a band of groups
because
G_{x}G_{y}
might not be
contained in a single
G_{z}
, as illustrated by the following example.

Example A.8
.

Consider the semigroup
{\mathcal{S}}=\{x,s,y,z\}
with the following multiplication table:

\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}{\mathcal{S}}&x&s&y&z\\
\hline\cr x&x&s&y&z\\
s&s&x&z&y\\
y&y&y&y&y\\
z&z&z&z&z\end{array}

Observe that:

(i)

{\mathcal{S}}
is a monoid with identity element
x
;

(ii)

E({\mathcal{S}})=\{x,y,z\}
is a LRB;

(iii)

the maximal subgroups of
{\mathcal{S}}
are

G_{x}=\{x,s\}
,
G_{y}=\{y\}
, and
G_{z}=\{z\}
; and

(iv)

left multiplication by
s
swaps
y
and
z
.

It is straightforward to check that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a LRBG, but it is not a union
of a band of groups because the
G_{x}G_{y}=\{y,z\}
is not contained in
any single maximal subgroup.

Lemma A.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG. Then for all
s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}
,

(A.2)

(s^{\omega}t)^{\omega}=s^{\omega}t^{\omega}.

Proof.

Start with
s^{\omega}ts^{\omega}t\cdots s^{\omega}t

and iteratively apply (LRBG2) to delete all occurrences of
s^{\omega}

except the first: thus,
(s^{\omega}t)^{m}=s^{\omega}t^{m}
for all positive
m\in\NN
.
Taking
m
such that
t^{m}=t^{\omega}
, one obtains

(s^{\omega}t)^{m}=s^{\omega}t^{\omega}\in E({\mathcal{S}})

since
E({\mathcal{S}})
is subsemigroup of
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Thus,
(s^{\omega}t)^{m}
is idempotent.
∎

A.4.
Strict Left Regular Bands of Groups

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a LRBG and consider the function
\varphi:{\mathcal{S}}\to E({\mathcal{S}})
defined
by
\varphi(s)=s^{\omega}
.
This function maps
{\mathcal{S}}
onto a band and the preimage of every element is a group.
By
Theorem

A.2
,
{\mathcal{S}}
is the union of a band
of groups if
\varphi
is a semigroup morphism.
We define a strict LRBG to be a LRBG for which
s\mapsto s^{\omega}
is a semigroup morphism.

Definition A.10
.

A
strict left regular band of groups (sLRBG)
is a LRBG
{\mathcal{S}}
such that

(sLRBG)

\displaystyle(st)^{\omega}=s^{\omega}t^{\omega}\text{~for all $s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}$.}

Observe that the LRBG of
Example

A.8
is not a sLRBG since

(sy)^{\omega}=z\neq y=s^{\omega}y^{\omega}
.

Proposition A.11
.

A LRBG is a union of a band of groups if and only if it is a strict LRBG.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a sLRBG. By
Lemma

3.3
and
Equation

sLRBG
, the map

\varphi:{\mathcal{S}}\to E({\mathcal{S}})
is a semigroup morphism from
{\mathcal{S}}
onto the band

E({\mathcal{S}})
. By
Lemma

A.7
,
the preimage
\varphi^{-1}(x)
of each
x\in E({\mathcal{S}})
is
a group. Therefore,
{\mathcal{S}}
is the union of the band of groups

\{\varphi^{-1}(x):x\in E({\mathcal{S}})\}
.

Conversely, suppose
{\mathcal{S}}
is a LRBG that is a union of a band of groups.
Then there exists a band
{\mathcal{B}}
and a surjective semigroup morphism
\psi:{\mathcal{S}}\xrightarrow{}{\mathcal{B}}
such that the preimage of each
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
is
a group.
Since
E({\mathcal{S}})
is a subsemigroup of
{\mathcal{S}}
, the restriction

\psi|_{E({\mathcal{S}})}:E({\mathcal{S}})\to{\mathcal{B}}
is a semigroup morphism.
Moreover, it is a bijection because
\psi^{-1}(b)
for
b\in{\mathcal{B}}
is
a group and thus contains a unique idempotent (namely, the identity element
of the group).
Therefore, the composition

\psi|_{E({\mathcal{S}})}^{-1}\circ\psi

is a semigroup morphism that maps each
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
to the identity element
of the group containing
s
; i.e.,
s\mapsto s^{\omega}
.
∎

The following is an important consequence of axiom
Equation

sLRBG
.

Lemma A.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a strict LRBG.
Let
s\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with
s^{\omega}\leq y
. Then,

sy=ys.

Proof.

The claim follows from the following computation, where we use that
x^{\omega}f=f
:

sy\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg1}}{=}(sy)^{\omega}sy\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:strict}}{=}s^{\omega}ysy\overset{(s^{\omega}\leq y)}{=}ysy\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}ys.\qed

In particular, we obtain a new characterization of the partial order
\leq
on a strict LRBG.

Lemma A.13
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a strict LRBG,
s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
x=s^{\omega}\in E({\mathcal{S}})
.
Then,
s\leq t
if and only if
t=ys=sy
for some
y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with
x\leq y
.

Proof.

If
s\leq t
, take
y=t^{\omega}
. Then,
t=sy
by definition of
\leq
, and

xy\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:strict}}{=}(sy)^{\omega}\overset{(s\leq t)}{=}(t)^{\omega}=y,

so
x\leq y
. Finally, by
Lemma

A.12
,
t=ys=sy
.

Conversely, if
t=ys=sy
for some
y\in E({\mathcal{S}})
with
x\leq y
, then
t^{\omega}=(ys)^{\omega}=yx=y
, so
st^{\omega}=st=t
and
s\leq t
.
∎

A.5.
Presheaf construction

In this section we present a construction to obtain a strict LRBG from a
presheaf of groups
over a LRB.
As it turns out, all strict LRBG arise in this manner.
When the underlying LRB is a semilattice, this construction was first
considered by Clifford; see for instance
[
15
, Theorem 4.11]
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a left regular band. We endow
{\mathcal{B}}
with the preorder
\preceq

defined for
x,y\in{\mathcal{B}}
by

x\preceq y\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad yx=y.

This relation is reflexive and transitive, but not necessarily antisymmetric.
In particular, it is possible that
x\neq x^{\prime}
even though
x\preceq x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}\preceq x
.
For example, when
{\mathcal{B}}=\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is the semigroup of faces of a hyperplane
arrangement
{\mathcal{A}}
, we have
{\sf E}\preceq{\sf F}
precisely when
\mathsf{supp}({\sf E})\subseteq\mathsf{supp}({\sf F})
, and any two faces
{\sf F}
and
{\sf F^{\prime}}

with
\mathsf{supp}({\sf F})=\mathsf{supp}({\sf F^{\prime}})
satisfy both

{\sf F}\preceq{\sf F^{\prime}}
and
{\sf F^{\prime}}\preceq{\sf F}
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be the thin category corresponding to this relation.
That is,
\mathfrak{B}
is the category with objects the elements of
{\mathcal{B}}
, and
there is exactly one arrow
x\to y
whenever
x\preceq y
.
A
presheaf of finite groups
on
{\mathcal{B}}
is a functor
{\mathcal{G}}:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathbf{gps}
, where
\mathbf{gps}
is the category of finite groups.
More concretely,
{\mathcal{G}}
consists of the data of

(1)

a finite group
{\mathcal{G}}[x]
for each
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and

(2)

a group morphism
\Delta_{x}^{y}:{\mathcal{G}}[x]\to{\mathcal{G}}[y]
for each pair
x\preceq y
in
{\mathcal{B}}
.
These morphisms satisfy:

(i)

for all
x\in{\mathcal{B}}
, the map
\Delta_{x}^{x}
is the identity morphism of
{\mathcal{G}}[x]
; and

(ii)

whenever
x\preceq y
and
y\preceq z
, one has
\Delta_{y}^{z}\circ\Delta_{x}^{y}=\Delta_{x}^{z}
.

Observe that if
x\preceq x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}\preceq x
,
then
\Delta_{x}^{x^{\prime}}
is an isomorphism with inverse
\Delta_{x^{\prime}}^{x}
.

Remark A.14
.

When
{\mathcal{B}}=\Sigma_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is the semigroup of faces of a hyperplane
arrangement
{\mathcal{A}}
, the data of a presheaf of finite groups on
{\mathcal{B}}

is equivalent to that of a cocommutative
{\mathcal{A}}
-comonoid with values
in the category of finite groups, in the sense of Aguiar and Mahajan’s
Bimonoids for hyperplane arrangements

[
5
]
.

Given a presheaf of finite groups
{\mathcal{G}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
, we construct a semigroup

{\mathcal{S}}
as follows.
As a set,
{\mathcal{S}}
is the disjoint union of all the groups in the image of
{\mathcal{G}}
:

(A.3)

{\mathcal{S}}=\bigsqcup_{x\in{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{G}}[x].

We endow
{\mathcal{S}}
with the following product: for
s\in{\mathcal{G}}[x]
and
t\in{\mathcal{G}}[y]
,

(A.4)

s\cdot t=\Delta_{x}^{xy}(s)\Delta_{y}^{xy}(t).

The product on the right occurs inside the group
{\mathcal{G}}[xy]
.
If two elements
s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}
belong to the same group
{\mathcal{G}}[x]
, then the product
st
in
{\mathcal{S}}
agrees with the product inside the group
{\mathcal{G}}[x]
.

Example A.15
.

Let
\Sigma_{n}
be the LRB of set compositions
[n]
from
Example

2.3

and fix a finite group
G
.
For a set composition
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{k})\in\Sigma_{n}
, let

{\mathcal{G}}[(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})]=(G^{op})^{k},

where
G^{op}
denotes the opposite group of
G
.
If
{\sf E}\preceq{\sf F}
, then

\Delta_{\sf E}^{\sf F}\big((g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\big)=(h_{1},\dots,h_{\ell})

where
h_{i}=g_{j}
if the
i^{th}
block of
{\sf F}
is contained in the
j^{th}
block of
{\sf E}
.
For example, if
{\sf E}=({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}146}\mid{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}23578})
and
{\sf F}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}25}\mid{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}16}\mid{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}37}\mid{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}8}\mid{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}4})
, then

\Delta_{\sf E}^{\sf F}\big(({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}g_{1}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}g_{2}})\big)=({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}g_{2}},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}g_{1}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}g_{2}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}g_{2}},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}g_{1}}).

If
{\sf E}=(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})
and
{\sf F}=(S_{\sigma(1)},\dots,S_{\sigma(k)})
for some permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, then

\Delta_{\sf E}^{\sf F}\big((g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\big)=(g_{\sigma(1)},\dots,g_{\sigma(k)}).

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be the semigroup associated to
{\mathcal{G}}
. The map
{\mathcal{S}}\to{{\Sigma_{n}[G]}}
sending
(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\in{\mathcal{G}}[(S_{1},\dots,S_{k})]
to
\big((S_{1},g_{1}),\dots,(S_{k},g_{k})\big)
is an isomorphism.

Theorem A.16
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be the semigroup associated with a presheaf of finite groups

{\mathcal{G}}
on a LRB
{\mathcal{B}}
. Then
{\mathcal{S}}
is a strict LRBG. Furthermore, every
strict LRBG arises in this manner.

Proof.

We separate the proof in three parts.

(1)

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a strict LRBG with
E({\mathcal{S}})={\mathcal{B}}
. Define a presheaf
{\mathcal{G}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
by

{\mathcal{G}}[x]=G_{x}=\{s\in{\mathcal{S}}:s^{\omega}=x\}\quad\text{ for all }x\in{\mathcal{B}}

and

\Delta_{x}^{y}(s)=ys\quad\text{for all }x\preceq y\in{\mathcal{B}}\text{ and }s\in G_{x}.

Equation

LRBG2
guarantees that the maps
\Delta_{x}^{y}
are group morphisms: for all
g,h\in G[x]
,

\Delta_{x}^{y}(gh)=ygh=ygyh=\Delta_{x}^{y}(g)\Delta_{x}^{y}(h).

Moreover,
\Delta_{x}^{x}
is the identity map, since
x
is the unit of
G_{x}
; and whenever
w\preceq x\preceq y
, we have

\Delta_{x}^{y}(\Delta_{w}^{x}(g))=y(xg)=(yx)g=yg=\Delta^{y}_{w}(g)

for all
g\in G_{w}
. It follows that
{\mathcal{G}}
is a well-defined presheaf of finite groups.

(2)

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a presheaf of finite groups on
{\mathcal{B}}

and let
{\mathcal{S}}
be the semigroup associated with
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Note that the LRB property guarantees that
x\preceq xy
and
y\preceq xy
for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
We verify that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a semigroup (i.e. the product is associative), and that it satisfies the strict LRBG axioms.

Given
s\in{\mathcal{G}}[x]
,
t\in{\mathcal{G}}[y]
, and
u\in{\mathcal{G}}[z]
, functoriality implies

(s\cdot t)\cdot u=\Delta_{xy}^{xyz}(\Delta_{x}^{xy}(s)\Delta_{y}^{xy}(t))\Delta_{z}^{xyz}(u)=\Delta_{x}^{xyz}(s)\Delta_{y}^{xyz}(t)\Delta_{z}^{xyz}(u).

One similarly deduces that
s\cdot(t\cdot u)=\Delta_{x}^{xyz}(s)\Delta_{y}^{xyz}(t)\Delta_{z}^{xyz}(u)
,
which proves associativity.

Observe that for
s,t\in{\mathcal{G}}[x]
, the product
s\cdot t
in
{\mathcal{S}}
agrees with
the product
st
in
{\mathcal{G}}[x]
, so
s^{\omega}=1_{{\mathcal{G}}[x]}
and

s^{\omega}\cdot s=s
.
Now, for
s\in{\mathcal{G}}[x]
and
t\in{\mathcal{G}}[y]
, we have, because
xyx=xy
in
{\mathcal{B}}
,

s\cdot t\cdot s^{\omega}=\Delta_{x}^{xyx}(s)\Delta_{y}^{xyx}(t)\Delta_{x}^{xyx}(1_{{\mathcal{G}}[x]})=\Delta_{x}^{xy}(s)\Delta_{y}^{xy}(t)1_{{\mathcal{G}}[xy]}=s\cdot t.

Thus,
{\mathcal{S}}
is a LRBG.

It follows from the definition of the product in
{\mathcal{S}}
that
{\mathcal{G}}[x]\cdot{\mathcal{G}}[y]\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}[xy]
, therefore
{\mathcal{S}}
is LRBG that is a union
of a band of groups, and hence is a strict LRBG by
A.11
.

(3)

Finally, we show that these constructions are inverses of one another.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a strict LRBG and let
{\mathcal{G}}
the presheaf of groups defined in
the first part of this proof.
We show that the product in
{\mathcal{S}}
agrees with the product defined in
Equation

A.4
.
Take any
s,t\in{\mathcal{S}}
, and let
x=s^{\omega},y=t^{\omega}
. Then,

s\cdot t=\Delta_{x}^{xy}(s)\Delta_{y}^{xy}(t)=xysxyt\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg2}}{=}xyst=s^{\omega}t^{\omega}st\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:strict}}{=}(st)^{\omega}st\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:lrbg1}}{=}st.\qed

Remark A.17
.

The last chain of equalities in the preceding proof cannot be deduced without assuming strictness.
If
{\mathcal{S}}
is the LRBG in
Example

A.8
, the
modified
product on
{\mathcal{S}}

defined in
Equation

A.4
satisfies

s\cdot y=\Delta_{x}^{y}(s)\Delta_{y}^{y}(y)=ysy=y\neq sy.

For a LRBG
{\mathcal{S}}
, we can think of this modified product as a way of “
strictifying
” its semigroup structure.
Following the definitions, this product can directly be defined by:

s\cdot t=s^{\omega}t^{\omega}st.

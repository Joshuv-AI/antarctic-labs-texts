Complete Characterization of Entanglement Embezzlement
Elia Zanoni, Thomas Theurer, Gilad Gour

Abstract
Using local operations and classical communication (LOCC), entanglement can be manipulated but not created. However, entanglement can be embezzled . In this work, we completely characterize universal embezzling families and demonstrate how this singles out the original family introduced by van Dam and Hayden. To achieve this, we first give a full characterization of pure to mixed state LOCC-conversions. Then, we introduce a new conversion distance and derive a closed-form expression for it. These results might be of independent interest.

Quantum entanglement

describes correlations between different particles with no classical counterpart and has both deep foundational implications

If two or more parties are far apart, in practice, they are restricted to local operations and classical communication (LOCC) because during transmission over long distances, physical systems will unavoidably interact with an environment and eventually lose the quantum information they carry. In contrast, it is much simpler to exchange classical information that can easily be amplified and protected. The states that can be prepared with
\locc
, i.e., the separable ones, are therefore considered free and the ones that cannot, which are exactly the entangled ones, are considered costly or resourceful.

According to this reasoning, entanglement is a resource and thus studied within the framework of quantum resource theories (QRTs)
[
19
,
20
]
. In a QRT, a physically motivated restriction such as the one discussed above divides both states and operations into free or resourceful in a consistent manner: Free operations map free states into free states. Once the free operations and states have been fixed, a QRT studies which quantum advantages depend on the resource under consideration and, closely related, how the consumption of resourceful states can help to overcome the restriction. An example is quantum teleportation
[
11
]
: By consuming entangled states,
\locc
allows to teleport quantum systems and thereby simulate arbitrary operations outside
\locc
.

In this sense, consuming entangled states or more generally resourceful states can lead to operational advantages, e.g., in communication scenarios
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. Importantly, if a state can be converted with a free operation into another one, then the former is at least as valuable as the latter in any application that only allows for free operations. Answering the question which states can be converted into each other is thus a central question in any QRT. In entanglement theory, Nielsen’s Theorem
[
3
]
provides a characterization of the deterministic conversion between pure states that was later extended to probabilistic conversions
[
21
]
. Here, we generalize these results and characterize the conversion of a pure state into a mixed state.

The results on state conversions mentioned so far require that an initial state is exactly converted into a target state. However, every physical setup is affected by noise and finitely many measurements will only lead to finite accuracy. It is therefore practically impossible to distinguish the desired target state from a close approximation. This is the physical motivation to investigate whether a state can be
approximately
converted to the target using free operations
[
22
]
. There are several equivalent distances that formalize a notion of closeness between quantum states
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
, which can be used to define conversion distances
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
. These conversion distances measure the error in approximate conversions, i.e., given two states, they are the smallest distance between the second state and the result of any free operation on the first one. In this article, we introduce a new conversion distance under
\locc
defined on pure states. Using our result on exact conversions, we prove that it is topologically equivalent to the other conversion distances and derive a closed formula for it.

This in turn will allow us to completely characterize entanglement embezzlement

,
33

[
35
]
: Whilst in catalysis, the catalyst must be preserved exactly, embezzlement allows to change it by an arbitrarily small amount. Very recently, we have witnessed a renewed interest in the catalysis of various quantum resources
[

,

,

,

,

, with applications far beyond state conversion. In Ref.
[

,

,
17
]
in terms of teleportation fidelity. For further details and examples, see the recent review articles Refs.
[

.

Universal embezzling families are valuable resources in various applications. They are, for example, necessary resources for the efficient simulation of noisy quantum channels with noiseless channels
[

,

[
57

,
59
,
60
,
61

. Lastly, due to the close relation to catalysis, we expect that they will prove useful in applications such as teleportation, where catalysis provides advantages.

The first universal embezzling family was introduced in Ref.
[
32
]
and we call it van Dam and Hayden embezzling family. More recently, additional families have been proposed in Ref.
[
33

. In these works, embezzlement is considered using only local operations. Here, we extend the framework and additionally allow for classical communication, provide a complete characterization of universal embezzling families under LOCC, and discuss in what sense the van Dam and Hayden family is unique.

Notation and Preliminaries

In this article, we restrict ourselves to finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and denote them with capital Latin letters such as
A
,
B
. The dimension of a Hilbert space
C
is denoted by
\left\lvert C\right\rvert
, and the set of density matrices acting on it by
\mathfrak{D}{(C)}
. For the set of pure states in
\mathfrak{D}(C)
, we write
\pure{(C)}
.
Small Greek letters such as
\rho
and
\sigma
denote density matrices, with
\psi
,
\varphi
, and
\chi
reserved for pure states. For
\rho,\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(C)
, the trace distance between
\rho
and
\sigma
is defined as
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho-\sigma\right\rVert_{1}
, where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1}
is the trace norm.

Probability vectors are represented by bold small Latin letters, e.g.,
\mathbf{p}
, with
p_{x}
the
x
-th component of
\mathbf{p}
. The set of probability vectors of length
d
is denoted by
\prob(d)
, which contains the subset
\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
consisting of all
d
-dimensional probability vectors with non-increasing entries. The
k
-th Ky Fan norm of
\mathbf{p}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
is defined as

\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}=\sum_{x=1}^{k}p_{x}

(1)

for
k\in[d]
, where
[d]
is a shorthand notation for
\{1,\dots,d\}
. We write
\mathbf{p}\succ\mathbf{q}
if
\mathbf{p}
majorizes
\mathbf{q}

[
62
,
63
,
64
]
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{1}
for
\sum_{x}|p_{x}-q_{x}|
.

Since this article is concerned with two spatially separated parties, call them Alice and Bob, it is important to make clear which system is under the control of whom: Systems belonging to Alice will always be denoted by
A
or
A^{\prime}
, and systems belonging to Bob by
B
or
B^{\prime}
.
Quantum channels are represented by calligraphic large Latin letters such as
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
, and the set of quantum channels from a bipartite system
AB
to
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}
that Alice and Bob can implement if they are restricted to local operations and classical communication is denoted by
\locc(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
.
For bipartite systems
AB
, we assume w.l.o.g. that
\left\lvert A\right\rvert=\left\lvert B\right\rvert=d
(since with
\locc
, Alice and Bob can always attach and remove local auxiliary systems). Fixing an orthonormal basis
\Set{\Ket{x}_{A}}_{x\in[d]}
for
A
and
\Set{\Ket{x}_{B}}_{x\in[d]}
for
B
, under
\locc
, every
\psi\in\pure(AB)
is then equivalent to its standard form
\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}\Ket{xx}_{AB}
, where
\mathbf{p}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
are the Schmidt coefficients of
\psi
(which, w.l.o.g., we will always assume to be ordered non-increasingly from here on). We denote with
\SR(\psi)
the Schmidt rank of a pure bipartite state
\psi\in\pure(AB)
, i.e., the number of non-zero Schmidt coefficients of
\psi
, and use the symbol
\Phi_{m}=\sum_{x=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\Ket{xx}_{AB}
for the maximally entangled state on
AB
, where
\left\lvert A\right\rvert=\left\lvert B\right\rvert=m
. Finally, for
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
, we write
\rho\xrightarrow{\locc}\sigma
whenever there exists an
\mathcal{N}\in\locc(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
such that
\sigma=\mathcal{N}(\rho)
and
\rho\xrightarrow{\locc}\Set{t_{z},\tau_{z}}
whenever there exists a probabilistic
\locc
protocol that converts
\rho
to
\tau_{z}
with probability
t_{z}
.

3
State conversions with LOCC

As motivated in the introduction, the question of how entangled states can be interconverted is at the core of the resource theory of entanglement. The exact deterministic
\locc
-conversion problem between pure states is solved by Nielsen’s Theorem
[
3
]
: For
\psi
,
\varphi\in\pure(AB)
and
\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{q}\in\prob^{\downarrow}{(|A|)}
their associated Schmidt vectors,
\psi\xrightarrow{\locc}\varphi
if and only if
[
65
]

E_{k}(\psi)\geq E_{k}(\varphi)\quad\,\forall k\in[|A|]\;,

(2)

where
E_{k}(\psi):=1-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}
.
In Ref.
[
21
]
, this result was generalized to the case where the target state
\varphi
is replaced with an ensemble of states: For
\psi
,
\varphi_{1}
,
\dots
,
\varphi_{n}\in\pure(AB)
,
\psi\xrightarrow{\locc}\Set{t_{z},\varphi_{z}}_{z\in[n]}
if and only if

E_{k}(\psi)\geq\sum_{z=1}^{n}t_{z}E_{k}(\varphi_{z})\quad\forall k\in[|A|],

(3)

which can be rewritten as

\min_{k\in[|A|]}\Bigl\{E_{k}(\psi)-\sum_{z}t_{z}E_{k}(\varphi_{z})\Bigr\}\geq 0.

(4)

We extend this result considering a generic mixed state as target.

Proposition 1
.

For
\psi\in\pure(AB)
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
,
\psi\xrightarrow{\locc}\sigma
if and only if there exists a pure state decomposition
\Set{p_{z},\chi_{z}}
of
\sigma
(i.e.,
\sigma=\sum_{z}p_{z}\chi_{z}
) such that

\min_{k\in[|A|]}\Bigl\{E_{k}(\psi)-\sum_{z}p_{z}E_{k}(\chi_{z})\Bigr\}\geq 0.

(5)

That this condition is sufficient follows directly from Eq. (
4
). That it is also necessary follows from the Lo-Popescu Theorem
[
66
]
which implies that if
\rho\xrightarrow{\locc}\sigma
, then there exists a pure state decomposition
\Set{p_{z},\chi_{z}}
of
\sigma
that satisfies Eq. (
4
).
The details of this proof can be found in Appendix
A
.

For certain choices of
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
,
\rho
cannot be converted to
\sigma
by
\locc
. It is then interesting to determine how well we can
approximate

\sigma
with
\rho
and
\locc
. To this end, one can associate with every distance
D
defined on quantum states the conversion distance

D(\rho\to\sigma)=\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})}D(\mathcal{N}(\rho),\sigma).

(6)

This conversion distance determines how close to
\sigma
, with respect to the distance
D
, one can convert
\rho
using only LOCC. A commonly used distance in quantum information is the trace distance
T(\sigma,\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\sigma-\tau\right\rVert_{1}
. The conversion distance associated with it is

T(\rho\to\sigma)=\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathcal{N}(\rho)-\sigma\right\rVert_{1}.

(7)

This conversion distance has an operational interpretation in terms of a result in state discrimination known as Holevo-Helstrom Theorem
[
67
,
68
]
(see Ref.
[
69
, Theorem 3.4]
for a review).
Indeed, if a single copy of either
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
or
\mathcal{N}(\rho)\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
, with
\mathcal{N}\in\locc
, is given with equal probability, then the maximum probability
p_{\max}
of correctly identifying the given state is bounded by

p_{\max}\geq\frac{1}{2}(1+T(\rho\to\sigma)).

(8)

Moreover, for every fixed
\rho
and
\sigma
, there always exists an
\mathcal{N}\in\locc
such that
p_{\max}
is arbitrarily close to this lower bound. In this sense,
T(\rho\to\sigma)
describes how well we can approximate
\sigma
given access to
\rho
and
\locc
.

Another distance used often in quantum information is the purified distance
P(\sigma,\tau)=\inf_{\psi_{\sigma},\psi_{\tau}}T(\psi_{\sigma},\psi_{\tau})

[
26
,
27
]
, where the infimum runs over all purifications
\psi_{\sigma}
and
\psi_{\tau}
of
\sigma
and
\tau
, respectively. In this case, the conversion distance is analogously defined as

P(\rho\to\sigma)=\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc(AB\to A^{\prime}B^{\prime})}P(\mathcal{N}(\rho),\sigma).

(9)

On pure states
\psi
,
\varphi\in\pure(AB)
, we define what we call star conversion distances via

D_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}D(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}),

(10)

where
\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{q}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(|A|)
are the Schmidt coefficients of
\psi
and
\varphi
, respectively, and
D(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})=D(\diag(\mathbf{r}),\diag(\mathbf{q}))
. In general
D_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)\neq D(\psi\to\varphi)
. However, we show in Appendix
B
that in the case of the purified distance, the two conversion distances coincide on pure states.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\psi
,
\varphi\in\pure(AB)
. Then
P(\psi\to\varphi)=P_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)
.

This, in turn, is useful to show that
T(\psi\to\varphi)
and
T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)
are topologically equivalent on pure states, which means that if one can approximate
\varphi
arbitrarily well with a sequence of states
\Set{\psi_{n}}
in the sense that
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(\psi_{n}\to\varphi)=0
, then the same is true for
T_{\star}
, and vice-versa. This is guaranteed by the following result (Appendix
C
).

Lemma 3
.

Let
\psi
,
\varphi\in\pure(AB)
. Then

\frac{1}{2}\left[T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)\right]^{2}\leq T(\psi\to\varphi)\leq\sqrt{2T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)}.

(11)

We are particularly interested in
T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)
because using tools from approximate majorization
[
62
,
63
,
64
,
70
,
71
,
72
,
73
]
, one can derive a closed-form expression for it (Appendix
C
).

Theorem 4
.

Let
\psi
,
\varphi\in\pure(AB)
and let
\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{q}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(|A|)
be their corresponding Schmidt coefficients. Then,

T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=\max_{k\in[\SR(\psi)]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}}.

(12)

If
\xi\in\pure(AB)
is separable and
\psi
,
\varphi
are not,

T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)<T_{\star}(\xi\to\varphi).

(13)

This theorem provides, to the best of our knowledge, the first algorithm to compute a conversion distance in LOCC with a finite number of steps. Indeed, if
\psi
,
\varphi
, and their Schmidt coefficients are known, then one can compute
T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)
using a finite memory, and a finite (perhaps very large) number of operations (additions or subtractions). This is not the case for the other conversion distances because they require a minimization over LOCC, which is in general unfeasible. The second part of the theorem shows that any entangled state is more useful in the approximation of all other entangled states than any separable state.

Whilst Theorem
4
is of independent interest, for example in the characterization of entanglement distillation and dilution
[
74
]
, we discuss in the following how it yields new results concerning the embezzlement of entanglement. When referring to the conversion distance and star conversion distance, we will thus refer to the versions based on the trace distance from here on.

4
Entanglement embezzlement

As mentioned in the introduction, it is impossible to create additional entanglement with
\locc
alone
[
2
,
5
,
19
,
34
]
. If
\rho\in\mathfrak{D}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
is an entangled state, this implies that there cannot exist a
\psi\in\pure(AB)
and a channel
\mathcal{N}\in\locc(AB\to ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime})
such that
\mathcal{N}(\psi)=\psi\otimes\rho
, because this would increase the total amount of entanglement between systems
AA^{\prime}
and
BB^{\prime}
with respect to any additive entanglement measure. It might however be possible to
approximate

\psi\otimes\rho
in the sense that
T(\psi\to\psi\otimes\rho)\leq\varepsilon
for a small
\varepsilon
. In this case, it is hard to distinguish
\psi\otimes\rho
from the approximation. By keeping the systems
A^{\prime}B^{\prime}
, one would thus embezzle entanglement from the owner of
\psi
- and if one would be able to make
\varepsilon
arbitrarily small, it would be impossible to detect.

In fact, in Ref.
[
32
]
, van Dam and Hayden showed that it is possible to embezzle
any
bipartite state
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
arbitrarily well from a family of pure states
\Set{\chi_{n}^{AB}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the sense that
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\sigma)=0
. This implies that an arbitrarily good approximation of any
\sigma
can be embezzled from
\chi_{n}
whilst changing
\chi_{n}
arbitrarily little, as long as
n
is large enough. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 5
.

A family of pure bipartite states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a
universal embezzling family
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\sigma)=0
for every bipartite finite dimensional state
\sigma
.

This definition is very similar to the one provided in Refs.
[
32
,
33
]
. The main difference is that these works only consider protocols using local operations (LO), while in this work, we allow for classical communication too. The set of operations that we consider is therefore larger, and as a result, if a family of states is not an embezzling family according to our definition, then it is not an embezzling family in the sense of Refs.
[
32
,
33
]
. Surprisingly, the original embezzling family proposed in Ref.
[
32
]
is rather unique in a sense that we will specify later, even when we allow for classical communication. Another difference with Refs.
[
32
,
33
]
is that in those works the fidelity was used to quantify the conversion error. Since the fidelity and trace distance are topologically equivalent, this is irrelevant.

The term ‘universal’ in Definition
5
underlines the property that any bipartite state can be embezzled. Since for every bipartite state
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
, where
A=B=m
, it is possible to LOOC-convert the maximally entangled state
\Phi_{m}
into
\sigma
(see, e.g., Proposition
1
), it is enough to check whether
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
to determine if
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family: This follows for example by combining Lemma
3
and the triangular inequality for the star conversion distance proven in Appendix
C
. Even simpler, it is in fact equivalent to only require
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2})=0

[
33
, Lemma 2 for the case of embezzlement with LO]
, because if one can embezzle enough copies of
\Phi_{2}
, then one can convert them into
\Phi_{m}
with LOCC (see Appendix
D
for more details). It is also important to note that technically, one could have required that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}T(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\sigma)=0
, since this would also allow to embezzle any state arbitrarily well. However, since one can always choose a subfamily, we decided to keep the definition in line with Ref.
[
33
]
. Lastly, due to Eq. (
11
), we can replace
T(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\sigma)
in Definition
5
with
T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2})
. Thanks to the closed formula in Theorem
4
, we obtain the following complete characterization of universal embezzling families (Appendix
D
).

Theorem 6
.

A family of pure bipartite states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with corresponding Schmidt coefficients
\Set{\mathbf{p}^{(n)}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{l\in[A_{n}]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{{(2l-1)}}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{{(l-1)}}}=0,

(14)

where
A_{n}=\lceil\SR(\chi_{n})/2\rceil
is the ceiling of half the Schmidt rank of
\chi_{n}
.

The problem of determining if a family of states is a universal embezzling family has therefore been restated as a rather simple optimization problem, which in many cases can be solved numerically or even analytically. By choosing
l=1
in Eq. (
14
) one obtains the following necessary condition for a universal embezzling family.

Corollary 7

(cf. Ref.
[
33
, Lemma 3 (LO)]
)
.

If a family of pure bipartite states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{1}^{(n)}=0
, where
\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d_{n})
are the Schmidt coefficients of
\chi_{n}
.

This implies that if a family of pure bipartite states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\SR(\chi_{n})=+\infty
, where
\SR(\chi_{n})
is the Schmidt rank of
\chi_{n}
. Indeed, considering that the entries of any
\mathbf{p}^{(n)}
sum to one, if the largest entry converges to zero, then the number of non-zero entries must diverge.

When looking for candidates for universally embezzling families, it is common
[
32
,
33
]
to consider families of bipartite states

\Ket{\chi_{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{n}}}\sum_{x=1}^{n}\sqrt{f(x)}\Ket{xx}_{AB},

(15)

defined by a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, where
F_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}f(x)
is a normalization constant. With this choice, one ensures that the families of states have a common structure, i.e.,
\chi_{m>n}
is obtained from
\chi_{n}
by appending additional coefficients and renormalizing. By choosing
f(x)=x^{-1}
, one recovers the universal embezzling family introduced by van Dam and Hayden
[
32
]
. This family is rather unique: If we assume that
f
has a reasonable asymptotic behavior, specifically that it is asymptotically non-increasing and
f(x)/x^{\alpha}
is asymptotically monotonic for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then the family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}
is an embezzling family if and only if
f
is asymptotically close to
x^{-1}
in the sense that for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x^{-1-\varepsilon}}=\infty\,,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x^{-1+\varepsilon}}=0.

(16)

As we will discuss now, the assumptions on the asymptotic behavior of
f
, which we require for technical reasons, are not particularly restrictive. In entanglement theory, it is possible to consider, w.l.o.g., only states with non-increasing Schmidt coefficients. With the first assumption, we require that the family of states, at least asymptotically, has this behavior. The second assumption rules out functions that are asymptotically monotonic but have oscillating components, for an example of such a function see Appendix
G
, Eq. (
G23
).

In Ref.
[
33
]
, it was shown that certain functions that differ from
x^{-1}
by logarithmic factors lead to universal embezzling families too. Since these functions satisfy the constraints in Eq. (
16
), this is in accordance with our findings. In addition, in Appendix
G
, we also show that many non-decreasing functions do not lead to universal embezzling families (see Propositions
G.3
and
G.4
for details).

A special case of the functions discussed so far are the functions
f_{\alpha}(x)=x^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. For the families of states
\Set{\chi_{n}^{\alpha}}
generated by such functions, we analytically compute the exact value of
\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}^{\alpha}\to\chi_{n}^{\alpha}\otimes\Phi_{m})
for
\alpha\geq-1
and lower and upper bound it for
\alpha<-1
. The details of the computations can be found in Appendix
F
and the results for
m=2
are shown in Figure
1
. Clearly, the limit of the star conversion distance is zero only for
\alpha=-1
, showing again the uniqueness of the choice made by van Dam and Hayden amongst the functions
f(x)=x^{\alpha}
.

\Set{\chi^{\alpha}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

. This can be seen in the above plot showing the analytically derived exact value of

.

5
Conclusions

In Proposition
1
we provided necessary and sufficient conditions for a deterministic
\locc
-conversion from a pure bipartite state to a mixed bipartite state. This extends the results already known for pure to pure state
\locc
-conversions, whether deterministic or probabilistic
[
3
,
21
]
. We then exploited this result to prove the topological equivalence of the newly defined star conversion distance between pure states and the trace conversion distance commonly used in literature. The star conversion distance exhibits a closed formula (Theorem
4
). This is remarkable, since the mixed state
\locc
-conversion problem is NP-hard
[
75
]
.

The closed formula in turn allowed us to completely characterize universal embezzling families (see Definition
5
and Refs.
[
32
,
33
]
for an analogous definition for LO) in terms of a simple optimization problem stated in Theorem
6
.
With this characterization at hand, we discussed the uniqueness of the van Dam and Hayden family
[
32
]
. For specific families of states generalizing the van Dam and Hayden family, we explicitly evaluated their star conversion distance to maximally entangled states and showed that they are only universally embezzling if they are exactly the van Dam and Hayden family, see Figure
1
. Therefore, the van Dam and Hayden embezzling family shows unique properties even for protocols that involve classical communication, as already noticed in Ref.
[
32
]
. This suggests a direction for future work, namely, to determine whether LOCC embezzlement implies LO embezzlement (the other direction is trivial), and therefore to investigate if classical communication is relevant in entanglement embezzlement or not. It is worth noting that so far, the research on entanglement embezzlement focuses solely on families composed of pure states. A more comprehensive theory of embezzlement that includes families of mixed states is yet to be developed.

Originally introduced in the resource theory of entanglement as a generalization of catalysis
[
32
]
, embezzlement has recently also been studied in other resource theories including non-uniformity
[
76
,
77
]
, coherence
[
78
,
79
,
80
,
81
]
, and athermality
[
82
,
83
,
84
,
76
,
85
]
. Moreover, fundamental limits for embezzlement have been proved in Ref.
[
86
]
and applied to the resource theories mentioned above. The aforementioned resource theories are related to the resource theory of entanglement via majorization, which is the tool that we used to derive the closed formula for the star conversion distance. A natural next step is to investigate whether it is possible to derive a similar formula in these other majorization-based resource theories. However, this task is not trivial: In the resource theory of non-uniformity, the majorization relation is inverted, and the free state is the maximally mixed state, which is fundamentally different from the free states in the resource theory of entanglement; in the resource theory of athermality, state conversion is described by relative majorization, which is a generalization of majorization. It is worth mentioning that in Ref.
[
84
]
it has been shown that embezzlement of athermality allows to violate the second law of thermodynamics. However, by using the work distance
[
84
]
as conversion distance, or by imposing physical constraints on the catalyst
[
76
]
, e.g., finite dimension or finite energy expectation value, athermality embezzlement is no longer possible, thus restoring the validity of the second law of thermodynamics.

As described in the introduction, universal embezzling families have been useful in many applications
[
15
,
18
,
55
,
56
,
57
,
58
,
59
,
60
,
61
]
. Our results provide an easy way to check whether a family is universally embezzling. Moreover, we ruled out large classes of potential candidates that are fundamentally different from the van Dam and Hayden family. The complete characterization of universally embezzling families therefore contributes to a more efficient usage of entanglement in technology.

Appendix A
Pure to Mixed State Conversions with LOCC

In this section, we prove Proposition
1
of the main text, which we restate for readability.

Proposition 1
.

For
\psi\in\pure(AB)
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
,
\psi\xrightarrow{\locc}\sigma
if and only if there exists a pure state decomposition
\Set{p_{z},\chi_{z}}
of
\sigma
(i.e.,
\sigma=\sum_{z}p_{z}\chi_{z}
) such that

\min_{k\in[|A|]}\Bigl\{E_{k}(\psi)-\sum_{z}p_{z}E_{k}(\chi_{z})\Bigr\}\geq 0.

(A1)

Proof.

We first assume that
\psi
can be converted into
\sigma
with
\locc
operations. Then, according to Refs.
[
66
,
87
]
, there exists a protocol in which Alice performs a generalized measurement
\Set{M_{z}}
and Bob performs a unitary transformation
U_{z}
conditioned on the measurement’s outcome such that
\sigma=\sum_{z}(M_{z}\otimes U_{z})\psi(M_{z}\otimes U_{z})^{\dagger}
. If Alice and Bob record the outcome of the measurement in a classical system
X
, the output of the protocol is
\sum_{z}p_{z}{|z\rangle\!\langle z|}^{X}\otimes\chi_{z}^{AB}\coloneqq\Set{p_{z},\chi_{z}}
, where

\Ket{\chi_{z}}=\frac{(M_{z}\otimes U_{z})\Ket{\psi}}{\left\lVert(M_{z}\otimes U_{z})\Ket{\psi}\right\rVert}\,,\quad p_{z}=\left\lVert(M_{z}\otimes U_{z})\Ket{\psi}\right\rVert^{2}.

(A2)

This shows that Alice and Bob can convert
\psi
into the ensemble
\Set{p_{z},\chi_{z}}
with
\locc
operations, which is equivalent to the condition
[
65
,
21
]

\min_{k\in[d]}\Bigl(E_{k}(\psi)-\sum_{z}p_{z}E_{k}(\chi_{z})\Bigr)\geq 0,

(A3)

where
E_{k}
was introduced in the main text and
d=|A|=|B|
as per our convention. This proves the necessary condition.

For the reverse, we assume that
\Set{p_{z},\chi_{z}}
is a pure state decomposition of
\sigma
that satisfies

\min_{k\in[d]}\Bigl(E_{k}(\psi)-\sum_{z}p_{z}E_{k}(\chi_{z})\Bigr)\geq 0.

(A4)

This is equivalent to
\psi\xrightarrow{\locc}\Set{p_{z},\chi_{z}}=\sum_{z}p_{z}{|z\rangle\!\langle z|}^{X}\otimes\chi_{z}^{AB}

[
65
,
21
]
. Alice and Bob can trace out the classical system and they obtain
\sum_{z}p_{z}\chi_{z}=\sigma
, thus
\psi
can be converted into
\sigma
with
\locc
operations.
∎

Appendix B
Purified Conversion Distance

In this section, we present some results concerning the purified conversion distance
[
26
,
27
]
, which are useful to prove the theorems about the star conversion distance presented in the main text. The purified distance between two states
\rho
,
\sigma\in\mathfrak{D}\left(AB\right)
is defined as

P\left(\rho,\sigma\right)=\sqrt{1-F^{2}\left(\rho,\sigma\right)},

(B1)

where
F\left(\rho,\sigma\right)=\left\lVert\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\right\rVert_{1}
is the fidelity.
The purified distance is a metric and according to Uhlmann’s Theorem
[
23
]
,

P\left(\rho,\sigma\right)=\min_{\psi,\varphi}T\left(\psi,\varphi\right),

(B2)

where
T\left(\rho,\sigma\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\rho-\sigma\right\rVert_{1}
is the trace distance and the minimization runs over all purifications
\psi
and
\varphi
of
\rho
and
\sigma
, respectively. Importantly, the purified distance is topologically equivalent to the trace distance
[
26
]
,

T\left(\rho,\sigma\right)\leq P\left(\rho,\sigma\right)\leq\sqrt{2T\left(\rho,\sigma\right)}.

(B3)

We now recall the definition of the purified conversion distance and purified star conversion distance introduced in the main text,

P\left(\rho\to\sigma\right)=\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc}P\left(\mathcal{N}\left(\rho\right),\sigma\right)\,,\quad P_{\star}\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)=\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}P\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}\right),

(B4)

where
\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{q}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
are the Schmidt coefficients of
\psi
and
\varphi
, respectively, and
P\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}\right)=\sqrt{1-F^{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})}
is the classical version of the purified distance with

F(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})\coloneqq F(\diag(\mathbf{r}),\diag(\mathbf{q}))=\sum_{x}\sqrt{r_{x}q_{x}}.

(B5)

We next prove that the two purified conversion distances recalled above are equal on pure states. To this end, we need the following Lemma.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\mathbf{q}\in\prob(d)
. The function
f\colon\prob(d)\to[0,1]
defined as
f(\mathbf{v})=\left(\sum_{x=1}^{d}\sqrt{q_{x}v_{x}}\right)^{2}
is concave.

Proof.

For details about concave functions, see Ref.
[
88
]
. Here, we are going to use that a twice differentiable function is concave if and only if its Hessian matrix is negative semi-definite. The functions
f_{x,y}(v_{x},v_{y}):=\sqrt{q_{x}v_{x}}\sqrt{q_{y}v_{y}}
are twice differentiable in
v_{x},v_{y}
for
v_{x},v_{y}\in(0,1]
and their Hessian matrices are given by

H\left(v_{x},v_{y}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix}-\sqrt{\frac{q_{x}q_{y}v_{y}}{v_{x}^{3}}}&\sqrt{\frac{q_{x}q_{y}}{v_{x}v_{y}}}\\
\sqrt{\frac{q_{x}q_{y}}{v_{x}v_{y}}}&-\sqrt{\frac{q_{x}q_{y}v_{x}}{v_{y}^{3}}}\end{bmatrix}.

(B6)

Since
\det H(v_{x},v_{y})=0
, one of the eigenvalues of
H(v_{x},v_{y})
is
0
and the other is equal to the trace of
H(v_{x},v_{y})
, which is smaller than or equal to zero. This implies that the functions
f_{x,y}(v_{x},v_{y})
are concave for
v_{x},v_{y}\in(0,1]
. Moreover, since
0=f_{x,y}(0,v_{y})=f_{x,y}(v_{x},0)=f_{x,y}(0,0)
, it is easy to see that
f_{x,y}(v_{x},v_{y})
is in fact concave for
v_{x},v_{y}\in[0,1]
. From this follows that
f
is concave, since

f\left(\mathbf{v}\right)=\sum_{x}v_{x}q_{x}+\sum_{y}\sum_{x\neq y}\sqrt{q_{x}v_{x}}\sqrt{q_{y}v_{y}}

(B7)

is the sum of concave functions.
∎

We are now ready to prove the promised theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\pure(AB)
, then
P\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)=P_{\star}\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)
.

Proof.

We assume w.l.o.g. that all pure states are in standard form, that is,
\Ket{\psi}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}\Ket{xx}_{AB}
, where
\mathbf{p}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
and
\Set{\Ket{x}_{A}}
and
\Set{\Ket{x}_{B}}
are fixed bases for
A
and
B
, respectively (see main text for more details). Let
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
be the Schmidt coefficients or
\psi
and
\varphi
, respectively. With
\mathbf{r}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
, as a consequence of Nielsen’s Theorem
[
3
]
,
\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}
if and only if there exists an
\mathcal{N}\in\locc
such that
\mathcal{N}(\psi)\in\pure(AB)
has Schmidt coefficients
\mathbf{r}
. We notice that

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{N}(\psi),\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-F^{2}(\mathcal{N}(\psi),\varphi)}=\sqrt{1-\left\lvert\Braket{\mathcal{N}(\psi)|\varphi}\right\rvert^{2}}

(B8)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\left(\sum_{x}\sqrt{r_{x}q_{x}}\right)^{2}}=\sqrt{1-F^{2}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})}

\displaystyle=P(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}).

This implies that

\displaystyle P_{\star}\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}P\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}\right)

(B9)

\displaystyle=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N}\in\locc\\
\mathcal{N}(\psi)\in\pure(AB)\end{subarray}}P\left(\mathcal{N}\left(\psi\right),\varphi\right)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc}P\left(\mathcal{N}\left(\psi\right),\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\psi\to\varphi\right).

This inequality follows from the definition of the star purified conversion distance because the minimization in the star conversion distance is done over a smaller set. The non-trivial part is to show that the opposite inequality holds as well. To this end, we want to show that for every mixed state
\sigma
such that
\psi\xrightarrow{\locc}\sigma
, there exists a pure state
\chi
such that
\psi\xrightarrow{\locc}\chi
and
P(\sigma,\varphi)\geq P(\chi,\varphi)
. It is then sufficient to consider only pure output states for the computation of the purified conversion distance, which implies the reverse inequality.

Let
\sigma=\mathcal{M}(\psi)\in\mathfrak{D}(AB)
, where
\mathcal{M}\in\locc
. Also, let
\Set{t_{z},\chi_{z}}
be a pure state decomposition of
\sigma
that satisfies Eq. (
A1
) (where the
\chi_{z}
are not necessarily in standard form), and
\mathbf{s}^{(z)}
be the Schmidt coefficient of
\chi_{z}
for every
z
.
Furthermore, for every
\chi_{z}
, let
\tilde{\chi}_{z}
be the pure state in standard form that is equal to
\chi_{z}
up to local unitaries, let
\tilde{\sigma}=\sum_{z}t_{z}\tilde{\chi}_{z}
,
and define
\chi\in\pure(AB)
as the pure bipartite state (in standard form) with Schmidt coefficients
\mathbf{s}=\sum_{z}t_{z}\mathbf{s}^{(z)}
. We notice that for all
k\in[d]

\displaystyle E_{k}(\chi)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{x=1}^{k}s_{x}=1-\sum_{x=1}^{k}\sum_{z}t_{z}s_{x}^{(z)}

(B10)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{z}t_{z}(1-E_{k}(\chi_{z}))=\sum_{z}t_{z}E_{k}(\chi_{z})

\displaystyle\leq E_{k}(\psi),

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\Set{t_{z},\chi_{z}}
satisfies Eq. (
A1
). This implies that
\mathbf{s}\succ\mathbf{p}
.

The next step is to show that
P(\sigma,\varphi)\geq P(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{q})
. First, we note that due to the von Neumann trace inequality
[
89
,
90
]
,

F^{2}\left(\tilde{\sigma},\varphi\right)=\sum_{z}t_{z}\left(\sum_{x}\sqrt{q_{x}s_{x}^{(z)}}\right)^{2}\geq F^{2}\left(\sigma,\varphi\right)

(B11)

and

F^{2}(\chi,\varphi)=F^{2}\left(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{q}\right)=\left(\sum_{x}\sqrt{q_{x}\sum_{z}t_{z}s_{x}^{(z)}}\right)^{2}.

(B12)

Second, we introduce the concave function
f\left(\mathbf{v}\right)=\left(\sum_{x}\sqrt{q_{x}v_{x}}\right)^{2}
, for
\mathbf{v}\in\prob\left(d\right)
(see Lemma
B.1
), and rewrite Eq. (
B11
) and Eq. (
B12
) as

F^{2}\left(\sigma,\varphi\right)\leq F^{2}\left(\tilde{\sigma},\varphi\right)=\sum_{z}t_{z}f\left(\mathbf{s}^{(z)}\right)\,,\quad F^{2}\left(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{q}\right)=f\left(\sum_{z}t_{z}\mathbf{s}^{(z)}\right).

(B13)

Finally, the concavity of
f
and the multidimensional Jensen’s inequality
[
91
]
imply

F^{2}\left(\sigma,\varphi\right)\leq F^{2}\left(\tilde{\sigma},\varphi\right)=\sum_{z}t_{z}f\left(\mathbf{s}^{(z)}\right)\leq f\left(\sum_{z}t_{z}\mathbf{s}^{(z)}\right)=F^{2}\left(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{q}\right),

(B14)

which is equivalent to
P(\sigma,\varphi)\geq P(\mathbf{s},\mathbf{q})
. So far, we have shown that for every
\sigma=\mathcal{M}(\psi)
, with
\mathcal{M}\in\locc
, there exists an
\mathbf{s}^{(\mathcal{M}(\psi))}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
such that
\mathbf{s}^{(\mathcal{M}(\psi))}\succ\mathbf{p}
and
P(\sigma,\varphi)\geq P(\mathbf{s}^{(\mathcal{M}(\psi))},\mathbf{q})
, thus

\displaystyle P(\psi\to\varphi)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathcal{M}\in\locc}P\left(\mathcal{M}\left(\psi\right),\varphi\right)

(B15)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\mathcal{M}\in\locc}P\left(\mathbf{s}^{(\mathcal{M}(\psi))},\mathbf{q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}P\left(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{\star}\left(\psi\to\varphi\right).

Eq. (
B9
) and Eq. (
B15
) imply that
P(\psi\to\varphi)=P_{\star}\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
The Star Conversion Distance

In this section, we discuss properties of the star conversion distance based on the trace distance and provide proofs omitted in the main text. We begin by proving that the trace star conversion distance is topologically equivalent to the standard conversion distance defined via the trace distance.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\psi
,
\varphi\in\pure(AB)
. Then

\frac{1}{2}\left[T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)\right]^{2}\leq T(\psi\to\varphi)\leq\sqrt{2T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)}.

(C1)

Proof.

Using Eq. (
B3
) and Theorem
2
, we obtain

\displaystyle T(\psi\to\varphi)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc}T(\mathcal{N}(\psi),\varphi)

(C2)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc}P(\mathcal{N}(\psi),\varphi)=P(\psi\to\varphi)=P_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}P(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}\sqrt{2T(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})}=\sqrt{2T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)},

where
T(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\coloneqq T(\diag(\mathbf{p}),\diag(\mathbf{q}))
. Analogously,

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}T(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})

(C3)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}P(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{q})=P_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=P(\psi\to\varphi)=\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc}P(\mathcal{N}(\psi),\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\mathcal{N}\in\locc}\sqrt{2T(\mathcal{N}(\psi),\varphi)}=\sqrt{2T(\psi\to\varphi)}.

∎

This proves that
T_{\star}\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)
is topologically equivalent to
T\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)
.

In the following, we provide the proof of Theorem
4
of the main text, which we restate below for readability. In other words, we derive a closed formula for
T_{\star}\left(\psi\to\varphi\right)
based on the Schmidt coefficients
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
of
\psi
and
\varphi
, respectively.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\psi
,
\varphi\in\pure(AB)
and let
\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{q}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(|A|)
be their corresponding Schmidt coefficients. Then,

T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=\max_{k\in[\SR(\psi)]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}}.

(C4)

If
\xi\in\pure(AB)
is separable and
\psi
,
\varphi
are not, then

T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)<T_{\star}(\xi\to\varphi).

(C5)

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and

\mathfrak{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{q}}=\Set{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}:\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon}
. As shown in Ref.
[
73
]
, there exist probability vectors

\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{q}}
called steepest
\varepsilon
-approximations of
\mathbf{q}
such that
\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\succ\mathbf{q}^{\prime}
for all
\mathbf{q}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathbf{q}}
. Moreover, these steepest
\varepsilon
-approximations can be constructed explicitly: If
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{e}_{1}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon
, then
\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
, otherwise let
k_{\varepsilon}\in[d]
be the index satisfying

\sum_{x=1}^{k_{\varepsilon}}q_{x}\leq 1-\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{x=1}^{k_{\varepsilon}+1}q_{x}>1-\varepsilon.

(C6)

The components of
\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}
are then given by

\bar{q}^{(\varepsilon)}_{x}=\begin{cases}q_{1}+\varepsilon&\text{if }x=1,\\
q_{x}&\text{if }x\in\Set{2,\dots,k_{\varepsilon}},\\
1-\varepsilon-\sum_{x=1}^{k_{\varepsilon}}q_{x}&\text{if }x=k_{\varepsilon}+1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(C7)

We now show that

\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}}=\min\Set{\varepsilon\in[0,1]:\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\succ\mathbf{p}}.

(C8)

First, we notice that if
\mathbf{r}_{\star}
is an optimizer of
\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}_{\star}\right\rVert_{1}
, then
\mathbf{r}_{\star}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{\mathbf{q}}
and therefore
\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon})}\succ\mathbf{r}_{\star}
. By transitivity, we also have
\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\tilde{\varepsilon})}\succ\mathbf{p}
, which implies that

\min\Set{\varepsilon\in[0,1]:\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\succ\mathbf{p}}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}=\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}}.

(C9)

For the reverse inequality, let
\varepsilon_{\star}
be the optimizer of
\min\Set{\varepsilon\in[0,1]:\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\succ\mathbf{p}}
. By definition,
\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon_{\star})}\succ\mathbf{p}
and
\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon_{\star})}\right\rVert_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{\star}
, thus

\min_{\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}}\leq\varepsilon_{\star}=\min\Set{\varepsilon\in[0,1]:\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\succ\mathbf{p}}.

(C10)

This shows that

T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=\min_{\mathbf{r}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}:\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}=\min\Set{\varepsilon\in[0,1]:\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\succ\mathbf{p}}.

(C11)

We observe that
\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{(\varepsilon)}\right\rVert_{(k)}=\min\Set{1,\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}+\varepsilon}
, thus the condition
\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{\varepsilon}\succ\mathbf{p}
is equivalent to

\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}\leq\min\Set{1,\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}+\varepsilon}\,,\quad\forall k\in[d].

(C12)

This expression is further simplified by noticing that
\mathbf{p}
is a probability vector, and therefore
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}\leq 1
for all
k\in[d]
. Consequently,

\bar{\mathbf{q}}^{\varepsilon}\succ\mathbf{p}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}+\varepsilon,\,\forall k\in[d]\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\max_{k\in[d]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}}\leq\varepsilon.

(C13)

In combination with the minimization in Eq. (
C11
) follows that

T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=\max_{k\in[d]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}}.

(C14)

To conclude the proof of the first part of the theorem, we observe that for
k\geq\SR(\psi^{AB})
,
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}=1
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}
is non-decreasing with
k
, thus we can restrict the maximization to
k\leq\SR(\psi^{AB})
.

For the second part, we notice that according to Eq. (
C11
),

T_{\star}(\xi\to\varphi)=\min_{\mathbf{r}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}:\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{e}_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{e}_{1}\right\rVert_{1}=1-q_{1}.

(C15)

Furthermore, from
\mathbf{e}_{1}\succ\mathbf{p}
and the transitivity of the majorization-relation, it follows that

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{r}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}:\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{p}}

(C16)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathbf{r}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)}\Set{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{1}:\mathbf{r}\succ\mathbf{e}_{1}}

\displaystyle=T_{\star}(\xi\to\varphi).

To rule out equality, suppose that
T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)=\max_{k\in[d]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k)}}=1-q_{1}
, and denote with
k_{\star}
an index that achieves this maximum. Then

\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k_{\star})}=1+\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(k_{\star})}-q_{1}.

(C17)

From this expression follows that
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k_{\star})}\leq 1
only if either
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
or if
k_{\star}=1
, and therefore
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
. These conditions are in contrast with the assumption that
\psi
and
\varphi
are not separable. As a consequence, equality in Eq. (
C16
) is unachievable, which proves the second part of the theorem.
∎

Next, we show that the star conversion distance satisfies a triangle inequality. Let
\psi
,
\varphi
, and
\chi\in\pure(AB)
and let
\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{q}
, and
\mathbf{r}
be their Schmidt coefficients. With the help of Eq. (
C14
), this implies that

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\psi\to\chi)

\displaystyle=\max_{n\in[d]}(\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(n)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{(n)})

(C18)

\displaystyle=\max_{n\in[d]}(\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(n)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(n)}+\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(n)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{(n)})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{n\in[d]}(\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(n)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(n)})+\max_{n\in[d]}(\left\lVert\mathbf{q}\right\rVert_{(n)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{r}\right\rVert_{(n)})

\displaystyle=T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)+T_{\star}(\varphi\to\chi).

Remark.
The star conversion distance
T_{\star}(\psi\to\varphi)
was so far only defined for pure bipartite states belonging to the same Hilbert space. This restriction is easily lifted by noting that one can always add separable auxiliary states such that the Hilbert spaces (or dimensions) match. This can be done in multiple ways: Let
\psi\in\pure(AB)
,
\varphi\in\pure(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
, and let
d=\left\lvert A\right\rvert=\left\lvert B\right\rvert
,
d^{\prime}=\left\lvert A^{\prime}\right\rvert=\left\lvert B^{\prime}\right\rvert
. Then

T_{\star}(\psi_{AB}\to\varphi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\equiv T_{\star}(\psi_{AB}\otimes|11\rangle\!\langle 11|_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\to|11\rangle\!\langle 11|_{AB}\otimes\varphi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}).

(C19)

Since in fact we are only interested in the dimension of systems and their spatial separation, a more compact equivalent notation that we will use later can be defined as follows:
Denote with
m
,
m^{\prime}>0
the smallest integers such that
md=m^{\prime}d^{\prime}
, and with

A_{m}
,
B_{m}
,
A_{m^{\prime}}
,
B_{m^{\prime}}
Hilbert spaces of corresponding dimensions. The conversion distance
T_{\star}(\psi_{AB}\to\varphi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})
is then

T_{\star}(\psi_{AB}\to\varphi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\equiv T_{\star}(\psi_{AB}\otimes|11\rangle\!\langle 11|_{A_{m}B_{m}}\to\varphi_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\otimes|11\rangle\!\langle 11|_{A_{m^{\prime}}B_{m^{\prime}}}).

(C20)

Appendix D
Universal Embezzling Families

In the main text, we provided the following definition of universal embezzling families, which we repeat here for readability.

Definition 5
.

A family of pure bipartite states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is called a
universal embezzling family
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}T(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\sigma)=0
for every bipartite finite dimensional state
\sigma
.

Thanks to the triangle inequality for the star conversion distance proven in Eq. (
C18
), a family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an embezzling family if and only if it can embezzle the state
\Phi_{2}
, as already shown in Ref
[
33
, Lemma 2]
for the case of embezzling with LO.

Lemma D.1

(cf. Ref.
[
33
, Lemma 2 (LO)]
)
.

Let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of pure bipartite states. The following three statements are equivalent

1.

\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family.

2.

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2})=0
.

3.

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=0
for every
m\geq 2
.

Proof.

Clearly 2. and 3. follow from 1. due to the definition of universal embezzling families and the topological equivalence of
T
and
T_{\star}
.
Moreover, 3. follows from 2. because

\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil}\xrightarrow{\locc}\Phi_{m}
,
and therefore

\displaystyle T_{\star}

\displaystyle(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

(D1)

\displaystyle\leq T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil})

\displaystyle\leq T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil-1})+T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil-1}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil})

\displaystyle\leq T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2})+T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes 2})+\dots

\displaystyle+T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil-1}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}^{\otimes\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil})

\displaystyle\leq\lceil\log_{2}m\rceil T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2}).

By taking the limit
n\to\infty
on both sides we obtain the desired result. To conclude, we note that 3. implies 1., since for all
\sigma^{AB}
,
\Phi_{|A|}\xrightarrow{\locc}\sigma^{AB}
.
∎

Next, we derive a formula for the conversion distance
T_{\star}(\chi\to\chi\otimes\Phi_{m})
using Eq. (
C14
) and Eq. (
C20
).

Lemma D.2
.

Let
\mathbf{p}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d)
be the Schmidt coefficients of
\chi
. Then

T_{\star}(\chi\to\chi\otimes\Phi_{m})=\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi)]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p_{a_{k}+1}\right\},

(D2)

where
a_{k}=\lfloor k/m\rfloor
,
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor, and
b_{k}=k-a_{k}m
.

Proof.

The Schmidt coefficients of the input and target state are

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{1}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{d\cdot(m-1)\,\text{times}}),

(D3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{(m)}=\frac{1}{m}(\underbrace{p_{1},\dots,p_{1}}_{m\,\text{times}},\dots,\underbrace{p_{d},\dots,p_{d}}_{m\,\text{times}}),

where
\mathbf{u}^{(m)}=(1/m,\dots,1/m)
.
It is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{1}\right\rVert_{(k)}=\begin{cases}\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}&\text{if $k\in[d]$},\\
1&\text{if $d<k\leq d\cdot m$},\end{cases}

(D4)

and by writing
k=a_{k}m+b_{k}
, where
a_{k}=\lfloor k/m\rfloor
,

\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{(k)}=\sum_{x=1}^{a_{k}}p_{x}+b_{k}\frac{p_{a_{k}+1}}{m}=\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}+b_{k}\frac{p_{a_{k}+1}}{m}.

(D5)

Using the closed formula for the star conversion distance given in Theorem
4
, we obtain

T_{\star}(\chi\to\chi\otimes\Phi_{m})=\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi)]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}.

(D6)

∎

A simplified version of this expression can be used to characterize embezzling families.

Theorem 6
.

A family of pure bipartite states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with corresponding Schmidt coefficients
\Set{\mathbf{p}^{(n)}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{l\in[A_{n}]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{{(2l-1)}}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{{(l-1)}}}=0,

(D7)

where
A_{n}=\lceil\SR(\chi_{n})/2\rceil
.

Proof.

Due to Lemma
D.1
and Eq. (
D2
),
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{2}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\},

(D8)

where
a_{k}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor
and
k=2a_{k}+b_{k}
.
First, we prove the necessary condition. Let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a universal embezzling family. We observe that

\displaystyle\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{2}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(1)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{1})}-\frac{b_{1}}{2}p^{(n)}_{a_{1}+1}

(D9)

\displaystyle=p_{1}^{(n)}-0-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{(n)}>0.

Taking the limit for
n\to\infty
in the expression above, we obtain
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{1}^{(n)}=0
. Since
p_{1}^{(n)}
is the largest Schmidt coefficient,
p_{a_{k}+1}^{(n)}
converges to zero too. Since
0\leq\frac{b_{k}}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{2}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}=0

(D10)

and thus

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{2}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

(D11)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{l\in[\lceil\SR(\chi_{n})/2\rceil]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(2l-1)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(l-1)}}\geq 0.

For the sufficient condition, we observe that

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{l\in[A_{n}]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{{(2l-1)}}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{{(l-1)}}}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{1}^{(n)},

(D12)

which implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{1}^{(n)}=0
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{2}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

(D13)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}\right\}

At this point, we have a closer look at

\displaystyle\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}\right\}.

(D14)

If
k
is even, then
a_{k}=k/2=a_{k+1}
, and thus

\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k+1)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k+1})}

(D15)

If
\SR(\chi_{n})
is odd, we can thus without loss of generality restrict the maximization to run over odd
k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]
. If
\SR(\chi_{n})
is even, we must additionally consider
k=\SR(\chi_{n})
. Assume that this is the case: It then holds that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(\SR(\chi_{n}))}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{\left(\SR(\chi_{n})/2\right)}-\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(\SR(\chi_{n})-1)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{\left(\frac{\SR(\chi_{n})}{2}-1\right)}\right)\right\rvert

(D16)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert p^{(n)}_{\SR(\chi_{n})}-p^{(n)}_{\SR(\chi_{n})/2}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq p^{(n)}_{1},

which vanishes in the limit
n\to\infty
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}\right\}

(D17)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[\SR(\chi_{n})],k\text{ odd}}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{l\in\lceil\SR(\chi_{n})/2\rceil}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(2l-1)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(l-1)}}=0.

Eq. (
D17
) shows that
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an embezzling family and concludes the proof.
∎

An important and easy to check necessary condition for a universal embezzling family is given in the following Corollary.

Corollary 7

(cf. Ref.
[
33
, Lemma 3 (LO)]
)
.

If a family of pure bipartite states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{1}^{(n)}=0
, where
\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\in\prob^{\downarrow}(d_{n})
are the Schmidt coefficients of
\chi_{n}
.

Appendix E
Regular Embezzling Families of States

Often, families of states are defined in terms of a positive, monotonic, and continuous function. This motivates the following definition (compare to Ref.
[
33
]
).

Definition E.1
.

A family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a regular family if there exists a monotonic function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
such that

\ket{\chi_{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{n}}}\sum_{x=1}^{n}\sqrt{f(x)}\Ket{xx}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(E1)

where
F_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}f(x)
.

Remark.
If
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a regular family, then there exists a sequence
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the family
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if
\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=0
. However, according to our definition, the family
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is then no longer regular, unless
n_{j}=j
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

For the following proofs, it is beneficial to extend
f
to a monotonic continuous function on
[1,\infty)
. This can often be done trivially by simply extending the domain of
f
. An example is the van Dam and Hayden family where
f(x)=x^{-1}
. Otherwise, we can extend
f
by connecting two consecutive points with straight lines.
If the function
f
is multiplied by a constant factor, this does not change the corresponding family of states. Therefore, from now on we assume for simplicity that
f(1)=1
.

Let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a regular family of states and let
f
be the function associated to it. We define the non-increasing functions
f_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by

f_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if $f$ is non-increasing,}\\
\frac{f(n+1-x)}{f(n)}&\text{if $f$ is non-decreasing.}\end{cases}

(E2)

By our extension of
f
,
f_{n}
is also naturally extended to a continuous function
g(x,y)
such that
g(x,n)=f_{n}(x)
via the definition

g(x,y)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if $f$ is non-increasing,}\\
\frac{f(y+1-x)}{f(y)}&\text{if $f$ is non-decreasing.}\end{cases}

(E3)

The (by definition non-increasing) Schmidt coefficients of
\chi_{n}
are therefore given by

p^{(n)}_{x}=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{F_{n}}.

(E4)

Since
p_{1}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{F_{n}}
, we can restate Corollary
7
for regular families.

Corollary E.2

(cf. Ref.
[
33
, Lemma 3 (LO)]
)
.

If
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a regular universal embezzling family, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}=+\infty
, where
F_{n}
is defined in Definition
E.1
.

In the following proposition, we present bounds on the limit of the conversion distance
T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})
in terms of the function
g(x,y)
introduced in Eq. (
E3
), which will be of use later.

Proposition E.3
.

Let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a regular family of states,
f
be the function associated to it, and
g
be defined as in Eq. (
E3
). If
\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}=\infty
, then

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x},

(E5)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\leq\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

(E6)

If
\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}
exists, this implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

(E7)

Proof.

For better readability, we divide the proof into steps and use the notation
G(x,y)=\int_{1}^{x}g(t,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!t
.

Step 1:

Starting from Eq. (
D2
), we find

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

(E8)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k}m)}+\sum_{x=a_{k}m+1}^{k}p_{x}^{(n)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k}m)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}+\frac{\sum_{x=a_{k}m+1}^{a_{k}m+b_{k}}f_{n}(x)-\frac{b_{k}}{m}f_{n}(a_{k}+1)}{F_{n}}\right\}

Now we observe that the last contribution in the expression above vanishes because by construction
f_{n}(x)\leq 1
and

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{\sum_{x=a_{k}m+1}^{a_{k}m+b_{k}}f_{n}(x)}{F_{n}}\leq\frac{m}{F_{n}},

(E9)

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{\frac{b_{k}}{m}f_{n}(a_{k}+1)}{F_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{F_{n}}.

Moreover, by assumption, the right-hand sides converge to zero in the limit
n\to\infty
, and this ensures that the limit inferior is additive. As a result,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{a\in\Set{0,\dots,\lfloor n/m\rfloor}}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(am)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a)}}

(E10)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(am)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a)}},

where we used that
\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(0)}=0
.

Step 2:

We start with finding bounds for
\sum_{x=1}^{n}f_{n}(x)-\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
. To this end we observe that since
f_{n}(x)
is by construction non-increasing,

\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\leq\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x+f_{n}(n)\leq\sum_{x=1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\leq f_{n}(1)+\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x.

(E11)

By subtracting
\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
from all terms, the desired bounds follow:

0\leq\sum_{x=1}^{n}f_{n}(x)-\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\leq f_{n}(1)=1.

(E12)

Dividing by
F_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}f_{n}(x)
and taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we obtain
\lim_{n\to\infty}1-\frac{\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\sum_{x=1}^{n}f_{n}(x)}=0
. Since
f_{n}(x)=g(x,n)
, the limit can be rewritten as
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{G(n,n)}{F_{n}}=1
.

Step 3:

In this step we want to show that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(am)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a)}}

(E13)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\sum_{x=a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)}}{F_{n}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{n}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

Analogously to the previous step, one can obtain the following bounds for
\frac{\sum_{x=a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)}{F_{n}}
:

\frac{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{F_{n}}\leq\frac{\sum_{x=a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)}{F_{n}}\leq\frac{1+\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{F_{n}},

(E14)

and after taking the maximum over
a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]
and the limit inferior the bounds become

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{F_{n}}

(E15)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\sum_{x=a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)}}{F_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1+\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{F_{n}}.

We can rewrite the last expression as

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{F_{n}}+\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{F_{n}}\right)

(E16)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{F_{n}}\right)+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{F_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{F_{n}}.

By combining Eq. (
E15
) with Eq. (
E16
), one obtains

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\sum_{x=a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)}}{F_{n}}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}f_{n}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{F_{n}}.

(E17)

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(am)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a)}}

(E18)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{G(am,n)-G(a+1,n)}}{G(n,n)},

where we replaced
F_{n}
with
G(n,n)
, which we can do according to step 2.

Step 4:

Let
c_{n}
be the value that maximizes
\max_{1\leq a\leq n/m}\Set{G(am,n)-G(a+1,n)}
and let
c_{n}^{\prime}
be the natural number satisfying
c_{n}^{\prime}\leq c_{n}<c_{n}^{\prime}+1
. This implies

\displaystyle G(c_{n}m,n)-G(c_{n}+1,n)

\displaystyle=\int_{c_{n}+1}^{c_{n}m}g(t,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!t

(E19)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{c_{n}^{\prime}+1}^{c_{n}^{\prime}m}g(t,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!t+\int_{c_{n}^{\prime}m}^{(c_{n}^{\prime}+1)m}g(t,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{c_{n}^{\prime}+1}^{c_{n}^{\prime}m}g(t,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!t+m.

Dividing by
G(n,n)
, taking the limit inferior, and arguing as in the previous step, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq n/m}\Set{G(am,n)-G(a+1,n)}}{G(n,n)}

(E20)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{G(c_{n}m,n)-G(c_{n}+1,n)}{G(n,n)}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{G(c_{n}^{\prime}m,n)-G(c_{n}^{\prime}+1,n)+m}{G(n,n)}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{G(c_{n}^{\prime}m,n)-G(c_{n}^{\prime}+1,n)}{G(n,n)}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{G(am,n)-G(a+1,n)}}{G(n,n)}.

The reverse inequality follows from the fact that we maximize over a larger set. This proves that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{\max_{a\in[\lfloor n/m\rfloor]}\Set{G(am,n)-G(a+1,n)}}{G(n,n)}

(E21)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq n/m}\Set{G(am,n)-G(a+1,n)}}{G(n,n)}.

Step 5:
Combining the results of the previous steps, and in particular Eq. (
E10
), Eq. (
E18
), and Eq. (
E21
), we have proven that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq n/m}\Set{\int_{a+1}^{am}g(x,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{n}g(x,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

(E22)

In the above equation, we can replace
\int_{a+1}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
with
\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
because the difference of the two integrals is finite and divided by a diverging term. Thus

\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq n/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{n}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

(E23)

The expression inside the
\liminf
on the right-hand side of Eq. (
E23
) is a function of
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\Set{1}
and we call it
M(n)
,

M(n)=\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq n/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{n}g(x,n)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

(E24)

We extend this function to real numbers
y>1
as follows:

M(y)=\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

(E25)

Since the natural numbers are a subset of the real numbers,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}M(n)\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}M(y),

(E26)

which proves Eq. (
E5
).
Furthermore, Steps 1-4 can be repeated with exactly the same arguments for the
\limsup
, and they imply

\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}M(n)\leq\limsup_{y\to\infty}M(y).

(E27)

This proves Eq. (
E6
).

In the following, we assume that
\lim_{y\to\infty}M(y)
exists. This implies

\lim_{y\to\infty}M(y)=\liminf_{y\to\infty}M(y)=\limsup_{y\to\infty}M(y).

(E28)

From Eq. (
E26
), Eq. (
E27
), and Eq. (
E28
) we obtain

\lim_{y\to\infty}M(y)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}M(n)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}M(n)\leq\lim_{y\to\infty}M(y).

(E29)

Therefore,
\lim_{n\to\infty}M(n)
exists and is equal to
\lim_{y\to\infty}M(y)
. From Eq. (
E23
) and Eq. (
E27
) we derive

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\lim_{n\to\infty}M(n)=\lim_{y\to\infty}M(y).

(E30)

This proves Eq. (
E7
) and concludes the proof.

∎

Corollary
E.2
combined with Proposition
E.3
leads to the following characterization of embezzling families.

Corollary E.4
.

Let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a regular family of states,
f
be the function associated to it, and
g
be defined as in Eq. (
E3
). If

\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

(E31)

exists, then the family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an universal embezzling family if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}=\infty
and

\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}=0.

(E32)

In addition, thanks to Proposition
E.3
, the task of determining if a family of states is a universal embezzling family is converted into an optimization problem. It is enough to find the maximum of the differentiable function
G_{y}(a)=\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
on
1\leq a\leq y/m
, which is easily done with the help of its derivative

mg(am,y)-g(a,y),\quad 1\leq a\leq y/m.

(E33)

Appendix F
Generalization of the van Dam and Hayden Family

The universal embezzling family
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
introduced by van Dam and Hayden consists of the states

\Ket{\chi_{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{H_{n}}}\sum_{x=1}^{n}\sqrt{x^{-1}}\Ket{xx},

(F1)

where
H_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}x^{-1}
is the
n
-th harmonic number. We generalize this family of states as follows: For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
we introduce the family
\Set{\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where

\Ket{\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}}\sum_{x=1}^{n}\sqrt{x^{\alpha}}\Ket{xx}

(F2)

and
H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}x^{\alpha}
is the
n
-th generalized harmonic number. Note that the families of states
\Set{\chi_{n}^{(\alpha)}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are regular families of states, and the van Dam and Hayden family is recovered for
\alpha=-1
. The corresponding Schmidt coefficients, as per our convention arranged in non-increasing order, are

\mathbf{p}^{(n|\alpha)}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}(1,2^{\alpha},\dots,n^{\alpha})&\text{if $\alpha\leq 0$},\\
\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}(n^{\alpha},\dots,2^{\alpha},1)&\text{if $\alpha>0$}.\end{cases}

(F3)

In the remaining part of this section, we show that the family of state
\Set{\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if
\alpha=-1
and derive bounds on the star conversion distance.

Case
\alpha<-1
.

For
\alpha<-1
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}
is finite, thus the regular family of states
\Set{\chi^{\alpha}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not a universal embezzling family (see Corollary
E.2
). The largest Schmidt coefficient provides a lower bound on the star conversion distance: Using Eq. (
C14
), we obtain

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\to\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle=\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n|\alpha)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n|\alpha)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n|\alpha)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

(F4)

\displaystyle\geq p_{1}^{(n|\alpha)}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right),

where the inequality followed from choosing
k=1
. By taking the limit, we obtain

\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\to\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)=\frac{1}{\zeta(-\alpha)}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right),

(F5)

where
\zeta(-\alpha)=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}x^{\alpha}
is the Riemann Zeta function. To obtain an upper bound, we observe that, since
\alpha<-1
,

\sum_{x=a+1}^{(a+1)m}x^{\alpha}\leq(a+1)^{\alpha}+\int_{a+1}^{(a+1)m}x^{\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x=(a+1)^{\alpha}+\frac{(a+1)^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\left(m^{\alpha+1}-1\right).

(F6)

The right-hand side is decreasing in
a
, and thus

\displaystyle\max_{k\in[n]}

\displaystyle\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n|\alpha)}\right\rVert_{{(k)}}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n|\alpha)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p_{a_{k}+1}^{(n|\alpha)}\right\}

(F7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}\max_{k\in[n]}\Set{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}x^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}\max_{a\in\{0,...,\lfloor n/m\rfloor\}}\Set{\sum_{x=a+1}^{(a+1)m}x^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}\max_{a\in\{0,...,\lfloor n/m\rfloor\}}\Set{(a+1)^{\alpha}+\frac{(a+1)^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}\left(m^{\alpha+1}-1\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}}\left(1+\frac{m^{\alpha+1}-1}{\alpha+1}\right).

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
, and considering that the conversion distance is by definition smaller than one, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\to\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\leq\min\Set{1,\frac{1}{\zeta(-\alpha)}\left(1+\frac{m^{\alpha+1}-1}{\alpha+1}\right)}.

(F8)

Case
\alpha=-1
.

This is the van Dam and Hayden family. Analogously to the previous case, we observe that

\sum_{x=a+1}^{(a+1)m}x^{-1}\leq(a+1)^{-1}+\int_{a+1}^{(a+1)m}x^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x=(a+1)^{-1}+\ln m\leq 1+\ln m.

(F9)

Since
H_{n}^{(1)}=H_{n}
diverges, by following the same steps, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi^{(-1)}_{n}\to\chi^{(-1)}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1+\ln m}{H_{n}}=0.

(F10)

Thus, the limit for the conversion distance is zero as expected for the van Dam and Hayden family.

Case
-1<\alpha<0
.

In this scenario
H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}
diverges when
n\to\infty
, and we can use the results of Section
E
. Here
g(x,y)=x^{\alpha}
, and the derivative with respect to
a
of the function
\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
is

mg(am,y)-g(a,y)=a^{\alpha}(m^{\alpha+1}-1),

(F11)

which is positive in the domain
1\leq a\leq y/m
. This implies that the function
\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
is non-decreasing and the maximum

\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

(F12)

is obtained for
a=y/m
. Due to Proposition
E.3
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\to\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{y/m}^{y}x^{\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}x^{\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}=1-\frac{1}{m^{\alpha+1}}.

(F13)

Case
\alpha=0
.

Here we have

p_{x}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{n}\,,\quad(\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{(m)})_{x}=\frac{1}{nm}.

(F14)

We can compute the star conversion distance directly using Eq. (
C14
) and obtain

T_{\star}(\chi^{(0)}_{n}\to\chi^{(0)}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\max_{k\in[n]}\Set{\frac{k}{n}-\frac{k}{nm}}=1-\frac{1}{m}.

(F15)

Case
\alpha>0
.

Also here
H^{(-\alpha)}_{n}
diverges for
n\to\infty
and we can use the results of Section
E
. The function
g(x,y)
is defined as
g(x,y)=(y+1-x)^{\alpha}/y^{\alpha}
, because
x^{\alpha}
is increasing. From this follows

\displaystyle mg(am,y)-g(a,y)\geq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow m(y+1-am)^{\alpha}\geq(y+1-a)^{\alpha}

(F16)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow a\leq\frac{(y+1)\left(m^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-1\right)}{m^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}-1}\coloneqq a_{\max}.

Since
a_{\max}
increases linearly with
y
and
a_{\max}\leq\frac{y}{m}
is equivalent to
y\geq\frac{m\left(m^{1/\alpha}-1\right)}{m-1}
,
for large enough
y
, the value
a_{\max}
belongs to the interval
[1,y/m]
and is a global maximum.
Plugging
a_{\max}
into Proposition
E.3
, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\to\chi^{(\alpha)}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{a_{\max}}^{a_{\max}m}\frac{(y+1-x)^{\alpha}}{y^{\alpha}}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}\frac{(y+1-x)^{\alpha}}{y^{\alpha}}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}=(m-1)\left(\frac{m-1}{m^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}-1}\right)^{\alpha}.

(F17)

This completes the study of regular families of states defined by
f(x)=x^{\alpha}
. The star conversion distance vanishes only for
\alpha=-1
, thus the only universal embezzling family of this form is the one introduced by van Dam and Hayden. Furthermore, exact values for the limit of the star conversion distance for
\alpha\geq-1
and lower and upper bounds for
\alpha<-1
were provided.

Appendix G
Uniqueness of the van Dam and Hayden Embezzling Family

In this section, we provide further results on the uniqueness of the van Dam and Hayden embezzling family. Given a regular family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, the asymptotic behavior of the function
f
associated to it is relevant to determine whether
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family or not (e.g., see Corollary
E.2
). We thus use the notations little-
\omega
and little-
o
(see, e.g., Ref.
[
92
]
) to describe asymptotic relations between two functions
g,h\colon[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
,

h\in\omega(g)\Leftrightarrow\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{h(x)}{g(x)}=+\infty\,,\quad h\in o(g)\Leftrightarrow\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{h(x)}{g(x)}=0.

(G1)

Before we state the results about the uniqueness of the van Dam and Hayden embezzling family, we prove the following Lemma, which is a direct consequence of Theorem 2 in Ref.
[
93
]
, which we restate here to improve readability: Let
\mu
be a measure on the real line
\mathbb{R}
, and let
f_{i}
,
g_{i}
(
i=1,2
) be four Borel-measurable functions:
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{2}\geq 0
and
g_{2}\geq 0
, and
\int\left\lvert f_{i}g_{j}\right\rvert\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!\mu<\infty
(
i,j=1,2
). If
f_{1}/f_{2}
and
g_{1}/g_{2}
are monotonic in the same direction, then

\int f_{1}g_{1}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!\mu\int f_{2}g_{2}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!\mu\geq\int f_{1}g_{2}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!\mu\int f_{2}g_{1}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!\mu.

(G2)

Lemma G.1
.

Let
f
,
g
be continuous, positive functions on
[a,b]
such that
f(x)/g(x)
is a non-decreasing function on
[a,b]
. Then, for every
x_{1}
such that
a<x_{1}<b
,

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{a}^{x_{1}}g(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\geq\int_{a}^{x_{1}}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{a}^{b}g(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x.

(G3)

Proof.

Let
\tilde{h}(x)=1
and
h(x)=\chi_{[a,x_{1}]}(x)
, where
\chi_{S}(x)
is the characteristic function of the set
S
. Furthermore, let

h_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $a\leq x\leq x_{1}$},\\
e^{-k(x-x_{1})}&\text{if $x_{1}<x\leq b$}.\end{cases}

(G4)

The sequence of functions
\Set{h_{k}}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
h(x)
and satisfies
0\leq h_{k}(x)\leq 1
. Due to the dominated convergence theorem (see, e.g., Ref.
[
94
]
), we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{a}^{b}f(x)h_{k}(x)=\int_{a}^{x_{1}}f(x).

(G5)

The same result holds for
g
.

Since both
f(x)/g(x)
and
\tilde{h}(x)/h_{k}(x)
are non-decreasing, we can apply Theorem 2 of Ref.
[
93
]
to find

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\tilde{h}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{a}^{b}g(x)h_{k}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\geq\int_{a}^{b}f(x)h_{k}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{a}^{b}g(x)\tilde{h}(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x.

(G6)

After taking the limit
k\to\infty
on both sides, we obtain

\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{a}^{x_{1}}g(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\geq\int_{a}^{x_{1}}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{a}^{b}g(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x,

(G7)

which finishes the proof.
∎

With this lemma at hand, we are ready to present the promised results concerning the uniqueness of the van Dam and Hayden embezzling family.

Theorem G.2
.

Let
f
be a positive non-increasing function such that
f(x)/x^{\alpha}
is asymptotically monotonic for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the regular family of states associated to it (see Definition
E.1
). Then
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if
f\in\omega(x^{-1-\varepsilon})\cap o(x^{-1+\varepsilon})\ \forall\varepsilon>0
and
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)=\infty
. Furthermore, if
f\notin\omega(x^{-1-\varepsilon})\cap o(x^{-1+\varepsilon})
for at least one
\varepsilon>0
, then
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is any sequence of natural numbers, is not a universal embezzling family.

Proof.

Before we start, we notice that if
\lim_{j\to\infty}n_{j}=J<\infty
, then
\lim_{j\to\infty}F_{n_{j}}=\sum_{x=1}^{J}f(x)<\infty
, which implies that the family of states
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is not an embezzling family (see Corollary
E.2
). In this proof we will thus assume, w.l.o.g., that
\lim_{j\to\infty}n_{j}=\infty
.

Necessary condition —

Let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a universal embezzling family with corresponding function
f
satisfying the assumptions above. We already proved in Corollary
E.2
that if
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family, then
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)=+\infty
. To show the remainder, let

R=\Set{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}{\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x^{\alpha}}=0}\,,\quad L=\Set{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}{\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x^{\alpha}}=+\infty}.

(G8)

Since
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{\alpha}=0
for
\alpha>0
, we have that
R\neq\emptyset
. Now we prove by contradiction that also
L\neq\emptyset
. Let us assume that
L
is empty. This implies that
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{\alpha}=l_{\alpha}<\infty
for all
\alpha
. If there exists an
\alpha
such that
l_{\alpha}\neq 0
, then
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{\alpha-1}=l_{\alpha}(\lim_{x\to\infty}x)=+\infty
. Thus,
\alpha-1\in L
, and
L\neq\emptyset
, leading to the desired contradiction. If instead
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{\alpha}=0
for all
\alpha
, then
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{-2}=0
. This implies that
f(x)/x^{-2}
converges monotonically to zero for large
x
, i.e., there exists an
N
such that
f(x)<x^{-2}
for
x>N
. Thus,
\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}f(x)<\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}x^{-2}<\infty
. According to Corollary
E.2
, this contradicts the hypothesis that the family under consideration is universally embezzling. We have therefore shown that
L\neq\emptyset\neq R
.

The next step is to prove that
\inf R=\sup L
. From the definition of
R
and
L
follows that
\inf R\geq\sup L
. Let us assume that
\inf R>\sup L
, i.e., that there exists an
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\sup L<\alpha<\inf R
. Since
\alpha\notin L\cup R
, there exists a positive real number
l
such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{\alpha}=l
.
Now pick
\alpha_{1}
such that
\sup L<\alpha_{1}<\alpha<\inf R
. We observe that
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{\alpha_{1}}=l(\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{\alpha}/x^{\alpha_{1}})=+\infty
, thus
\alpha_{1}\in L
. This is in contradiction to the choice
\alpha_{1}>\sup L
. We therefore showed that
\sup L=\inf R
.

So far, we have shown that if
\Set{\chi_{n}}
is a universal embezzling family satisfying our assumptions, then there exists a unique
\alpha
such that
f(x)\in o(x^{\alpha+\varepsilon})\cap\omega(x^{\alpha-\varepsilon})
for every
\varepsilon>0
. What is left to show is that if
\alpha\neq-1
, then the family of states corresponding to
f
cannot be universally embezzling. From the above discussion, we know that
\Set{\alpha>0}\subseteq R
, thus we can focus on
\alpha\leq 0
and split our discussion into two scenarios,
\alpha<-1
and
-1<\alpha\leq 0
.

If
\alpha<-1
, then there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
\alpha+\varepsilon<-1
. Since
f\in o(x^{\alpha+\varepsilon})
and
f(x)/x^{\alpha+\varepsilon}
converges monotonically to zero for large
x
, there exists an
N
such that
f(x)<x^{\alpha+\varepsilon}
for
x>N
. This implies that
\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}f(x)<\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+\varepsilon}<\infty
and therefore
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)<\infty
, which, according to Corollary
E.2
, contradicts the hypothesis that
\Set{\chi_{n}}
is a universal embezzling family. Let
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be any sequence of natural number such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}n_{j}=\infty
. Then
\lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{x=1}^{n_{j}}f(x)=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)<\infty
. This implies, again due to Corollary
E.2
, that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is not a universal embezzling family.

If
-1<\alpha\leq 0
, then there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
\alpha-\varepsilon>-1
. We notice that
f(x)/x^{\alpha-\varepsilon}
diverges to infinity for large
x
, because
f\in\omega(x^{\alpha-\varepsilon})
. This implies that there exists an
N
such that
f(x)\geq x^{\alpha-\varepsilon}
for
x>N
and therefore
\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}f(x)\geq\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-\varepsilon}=\infty
,
which allows us to use the results of Section
E
. Since
f(x)/x^{\alpha-\varepsilon}
is non-decreasing for
x>N
, for all
y
such that
y/m>N
, we can use Lemma
G.1
to obtain

\int_{N}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{N}^{y/m}x^{\alpha-\varepsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\geq\int_{N}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{N}^{y}x^{\alpha-\varepsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x.

(G9)

This implies that

\frac{(y/m)^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}-N^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}}{(y)^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}-N^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}}\geq\frac{\int_{N}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{N}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

(G10)

Next we introduce
\delta:=\alpha-\varepsilon+1>0
and take on both sides the limit inferior
y\to\infty
, leading to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{\delta}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{(y/m)^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}-N^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}}{(y)^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}-N^{\alpha-\varepsilon+1}}

(G11)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{N}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{N}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{1}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x},

where in the last equality, we used that
\int_{1}^{N}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
is finite, whilst
\int_{N}^{\infty}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
diverges.
As last step, we observe that according to Proposition
E.3
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

(G12)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{y/m}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{1}{m^{\delta}}

\displaystyle>0.

Using the remark after Definition
E.1
, we obtain that for
-1<\alpha\leq 0
, there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family, contradicting the hypothesis.
Once we combine the results for
\alpha<-1
and
-1<\alpha\leq 0
, we have that if
\Set{\chi_{n}}
is a universal embezzling family satisfying our assumptions,
f(x)\in o(x^{-1+\varepsilon})\cap\omega(x^{-1-\varepsilon})
for all
\varepsilon>0
. We have also shown that if
f\notin o(x^{-1+\varepsilon})\cap\omega(x^{-1-\varepsilon})
for at least one
\varepsilon>0
, then there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the family
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family. This concludes the first part of the proof.

Sufficient condition —
Let
f
be a function satysfying our assumptions. Since
f(x)/x^{-1}
is asymptotically monotonic by assumption,
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{-1}
exists in
[0,+\infty]
. A priori, it can be either
0
,
0<l\in\mathbb{R}
, or
+\infty
. If
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{-1}=l>0
, then by definition of the limit, for any
l>\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
there exists an
N
such that
\left\lvert\frac{f(x)}{x^{-1}}-l\right\rvert<\tilde{\varepsilon}
for all
x>N
. This is equivalent to
(l-\tilde{\varepsilon})x^{-1}<f(x)<(l+\tilde{\varepsilon})x^{-1}
for all
x>N
. Since
\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}f(x)\geq(l-\tilde{\varepsilon})\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}x^{-1}=+\infty
, we can compute the star conversion distance using Proposition
E.3
, and find

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

(G13)

\displaystyle=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a<N}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}+\max_{N\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{N\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{N\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{N}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{l+\tilde{\varepsilon}}{l-\tilde{\varepsilon}}\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{N\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}x^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{N}^{y}x^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle=\frac{l+\tilde{\varepsilon}}{l-\tilde{\varepsilon}}\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\log m}{\log y-\log N}

\displaystyle=0.

Thus if
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{-1}=l\neq 0
, then
\Set{\chi_{n}}
is a universal embezzling family.

Suppose now
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{-1}=0
. Since
f(x)/x^{-1}
is non-increasing for large
x
, there exists an
\tilde{N}
such that
f(x)<x^{-1}
for all
x>\tilde{N}
. For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, by hypothesis,
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{-1-\varepsilon}=+\infty
. Furthermore, since by assumption
\int_{1}^{\infty}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x=\infty
, while
\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-\varepsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x<\infty
, there exists an
\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
, such that
\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\geq\int_{1}^{y}x^{-1-\varepsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
for
y>\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
. Let
N_{\varepsilon}=\max\Set{\tilde{N},\tilde{N}_{\varepsilon}}
. Using again Proposition
E.3
and performing the same steps as in Eq. (
G13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{N_{\varepsilon}\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

(G14)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{N_{\varepsilon}\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}x^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}x^{-1-\varepsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle=\log m\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{1-y^{-\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\log m.

Since this is true for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log m=0.

(G15)

This implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=0
, i.e.,
\{\chi_{n}\}
is a universal embezzling family.

Let us now consider the case
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x^{-1}=\infty
. From this immediately follows that
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)=+\infty
, so we can use the results of Section
E
. Furthermore, there exists an
N
such that
f(x)/x^{-1}
is non-decreasing for
x\geq N
. We write
f(x)=x^{-1}h(x)
and thus
h(x)
is non-decreasing for
x\geq N
. Computing the derivative of
\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
for
a\geq N
, we find

\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!a}=mf(am)-f(a)=\frac{h(am)-h(a)}{a}\geq 0.

(G16)

Thus,
\max_{N\leq a\leq y/m}\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x=\int_{y/m}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
and, by following the same steps as in Eq. (
G13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{N\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

(G17)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{y/m}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
f(x)/x^{-1+\varepsilon}
is non-increasing for
x>N_{\varepsilon}
by hypothesis,
x^{-1+\varepsilon}/f(x)
is non-decreasing for
x>N_{\varepsilon}
. By applying Lemma
G.1
(for
y
large enough), we obtain

\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y}x^{-1+\varepsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\geq\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y/m}x^{-1+\varepsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x.

(G18)

From this follows that

\frac{\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}\geq\frac{(y/m)^{\varepsilon}-N_{\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}}{y^{\varepsilon}-N_{\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}}.

(G19)

Taking the
\limsup
on both sides, and adding the finite contributions
\int_{1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
to the diverging integrals
\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
and
\int_{N_{\varepsilon}}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x
, we obtain

\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{1}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}\geq\frac{1}{m^{\varepsilon}}.

(G20)

Combining Eq. (
G17
) and Eq. (
G20
) we get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\leq 1-\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{1}^{y/m}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}\leq 1-\frac{1}{m^{\varepsilon}}.

(G21)

This is true for all
\varepsilon>0
, thus taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain the desired result

\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}1-\frac{1}{m^{\varepsilon}}=0.

(G22)

This concludes the proof of the sufficient condition.
∎

The above result on the uniqueness of the van Dam and Hayden family can be expressed as follows: Any regular family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
G.2
is a universal embezzling family if and only if
f
, the function associated to
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, is asymptotically close to
x^{-1}
, the function associated to the van Dam and Hayden family, where asymptotically close means
f\in\omega(x^{-1-\varepsilon})\cap o(x^{-1+\varepsilon})
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

The assumption that
f(x)/x^{\alpha}
is asymptotically monotonic for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is crucial for our proof and does not follow from the monotonicity of
f
. There are functions
that are non-increasing, but oscillate asymptotically when multiplied by powers of
x
. An example is the function

f(x)=\frac{1+(1+\sin\ln\ln x)\ln x}{x},

(G23)

which is non-increasing, but
xf(x)
oscillates between
1
and
\infty
. Theorem
G.2
does not provide any information about families of states associated to such functions, and it cannot be used to determine whether such families are universally embezzling or not.

Next, we prove two related propositions concerning regular families of states associated to non-decreasing functions.

Proposition G.3
.

Let
f
be a positive non-decreasing function such that
f(x)/x^{\alpha}
is asymptotically non-increasing for at least one
\alpha>0
. Then the regular family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
associated to
f
(see Definition
E.1
) is not a universal embezzling family. Furthermore, there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family.

Proof.

Since
f(x)
is non-decreasing and
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)
diverges, we can use Proposition
E.3
and Lemma
G.1
, to obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int_{a}^{am}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}g(x,y)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

(G24)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\max_{1\leq a\leq y/m}\Set{\int^{y-a+1}_{y-am+1}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\Set{\int^{y(1-1/m)+1}_{1}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\Set{\int^{y(1-1/m)+1}_{1}x^{\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}}{\int_{1}^{y}x^{\alpha}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\alpha+1}.

The last inequality is based on Lemma
G.1
and is derived as in the previous cases. Using the remark after Definition
E.1
, we obtain that
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not a universal embezzling family. Furthermore, there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family.
∎

Proposition G.4
.

Let
f
be a positive non-decreasing function such that
f(x)/e^{kx}
is asymptotically non-decreasing for at least one
k>0
. Then the regular family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
associated to
f
is not a universal embezzling family. Furthermore, there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family.

Proof.

From Eq. (
D2
) follows that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\liminf_{n\to\infty}p_{1}^{(n)}

(G25)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n)}{\sum_{x=1}^{n}f(x)}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{f(y)}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{y-1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}f(x)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\liminf_{y\to\infty}\frac{\int_{y-1}^{y}e^{kx}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}{\int_{1}^{y}e^{kx}\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!x}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)(1-e^{-k}).

Also here the last inequality is due to Lemma
G.1
and the family of states is not a universal embezzling family. Furthermore, there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family.
∎

Appendix H
Asymptotically Regular Families

In the definition of universal embezzling families, Definition
5
, and in all the results about embezzlement, only the asymptotic behaviour of a family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is relevant. This motivates the following definition, which is a generalization of regular families.

Definition H.1
.

A family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is called asymptotically regular if there exists an asymptotically monotonic function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
such that

\ket{\chi_{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{n}}}\sum_{x=1}^{n}\sqrt{f(x)}\Ket{xx}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(H1)

where
F_{n}=\sum_{x=1}^{n}f(x)
.

We next show that our results hold for asymptotically regular families too. To this end, we start with the following theorem.

Theorem H.2
.

Let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an asymptotically regular family and let
f
be the function associated to it (see Definition
H.1
). Then one can construct a function
\tilde{f}
that satisfies

1.

\tilde{f}
corresponds to a regular family
\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(see Definition
E.1
),

2.

\lim_{x\to\infty}\tilde{f}(x)/f(x)=1
,

3.

\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family,

4.

\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
contains a universal embezzling subfamily if and only if
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
contains a universal embezzling subfamily.

Proof.

Since
f
is by assumption asymptotically monotonic,
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)
exists in the extended domain
[0,\infty]
. We first study the case
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=l
, with
0<l\in\mathbb{R}
. In this case, we choose
\tilde{f}(x)=l
, which corresponds to a regular family. Moreover, from the definition of
\tilde{f}
follows that
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/\tilde{f}(x)=1
. We also notice that for every
0<\varepsilon<l
, there exists an
N
such that
l-\varepsilon<f(x)<l+\varepsilon
for all
x\geq N
. This implies that
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)\geq\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}f(x)\geq\sum_{x=N}^{\infty}(l-\varepsilon)=\infty
. Similarly,
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}\tilde{f}(x)=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}l=\infty
. Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}^{-1}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{F}_{n}^{-1}=0
, we obtain that
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{1}^{(n)}=0
(where
\mathbf{p}^{(n)}
are the Schmidt coefficients of
\chi_{n}
), and therefore, according to Eq. (
D2
),

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

(H2)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}p^{(n)}_{x}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}p^{(n)}_{x}\right\},

where
a_{k}=\lfloor k/m\rfloor
and
b_{k}=k-ma_{k}
. The Schmidt coefficients are by definition non-increasing, i.e., obtained by reordering
\Set{f(x)/F_{n}}_{x\in\mathbb{N}}
. Since there are at most
N
natural numbers
x
that do not satisfy the condition
l-\varepsilon<f(x)<l+\varepsilon
, there are at most
N
Schmidt coefficients that do not satisfy
\frac{l-\varepsilon}{F_{n}}<p^{(n)}_{x}<\frac{l+\varepsilon}{F_{n}}
. We call the set of indices corresponding to these Schmidt coefficients
A
(thus
\left\lvert A\right\rvert\leq N
) and observe that for any
a,b\in\Set{N,\dots,n}
such that
a\leq b
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x=a}^{b}p^{(n)}_{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Set{a,\dots,b}\setminus A}p^{(n)}_{x}+\sum_{x\in\Set{a,\dots,b}\cap A}p^{(n)}_{x}

(H3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\Set{a,\dots,b}\setminus A}p^{(n)}_{x}+\sum_{x\in\Set{a,\dots,b}\cap A}\frac{l}{F_{n}}+\sum_{x\in\Set{a,\dots,b}\cap A}\left(p^{(n)}_{x}-\frac{l}{F_{n}}\right).

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{x=a}^{b}\frac{(l-\varepsilon)}{F_{n}}+\sum_{x\in\Set{a,\dots,b}\cap A}\left(p^{(n)}_{x}-\frac{l}{F_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle<\sum_{x=a}^{b}p^{(n)}_{x}

(H4)

\displaystyle<\sum_{x=a}^{b}\frac{(l+\varepsilon)}{F_{n}}+\sum_{x\in\Set{a,\dots,b}\cap A}\left(p^{(n)}_{x}-\frac{l}{F_{n}}\right),

and therefore

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}\frac{l-\varepsilon}{F_{n}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}p^{(n)}_{x}\right\}

(H5)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}\frac{l+\varepsilon}{F_{n}}\right\},

which implies

\displaystyle(l-\varepsilon)\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n-\lfloor n/m\rfloor}{F_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}p^{(n)}_{x}\right\}

(H6)

\displaystyle\leq(l+\varepsilon)\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n-\lfloor n/m\rfloor}{F_{n}}.

Observing that
\sum_{x=1}^{N}f(x)+(l-\varepsilon)(n-N)<F_{n}<\sum_{x=1}^{N}f(x)+(l+\varepsilon)(n-N)
, we obtain

\frac{l-\varepsilon}{l+\varepsilon}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}p^{(n)}_{x}\right\}\leq\frac{l+\varepsilon}{l-\varepsilon}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right).

(H7)

Since Eq. (
H7
) holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, we conclude that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{N,\dots,n}}\left\{\sum_{x=a_{k}+1}^{k}p^{(n)}_{x}\right\}=1-\frac{1}{m}.

(H8)

Inserting this result into Eq. (
H2
), we have

\liminf_{x\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})=1-\frac{1}{m}.

(H9)

The function
\tilde{f}
is a rescaling of
f(x)=x^{0}
, which we already studied in Section
F
. This implies that the family
\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equal to the family
\Set{\chi^{(0)}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}T_{\star}(\tilde{\chi}_{n}\to\tilde{\chi}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{x\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}^{(0)}\to\chi_{n}^{(0)}\otimes\Phi_{m})

(H10)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{x\to\infty}T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m}).

This proves that neither
f
nor
\tilde{f}
corresponds to families with universally embezzling subfamilies (and are therefore also not universally embezzling themselves).

We consider now the case
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=0
, thus
f
is asymptotically non-increasing. Let
N
be such that
f
is non-increasing on
(N,\infty)
and let
a=\min_{x\in\Set{1,\dots,N}}f(x)
. Since
f
is positive,
a>0
. Let
M>N
be such that
f(x)<a
for every
x\geq M
(such
M
exists because
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=0
). In this case, we define
\tilde{f}
as

\tilde{f}(x)=\begin{cases}f(M)&\text{if }x\leq M,\\
f(x)&\text{if }x>M.\end{cases}

(H11)

Clearly, the family
\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
associated to it is regular and
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/\tilde{f}(x)=1
. Furthermore, the ordered Schmidt coefficients satisfy for all
x\geq M

p^{(n)}_{x}=\frac{f(x)}{F_{n}}=\frac{\tilde{f}(x)}{F_{n}}=\tilde{p}^{(n)}_{x}\frac{\tilde{F}_{n}}{F_{n}}.

(H12)

If
F_{n}
converges,
\tilde{F}_{n}
converges too and by Corollary
7
neither
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
nor
\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are universal embezzling families (and do not contain universal embezzling subfamilies). If instead
F_{n}
diverges, so does
\tilde{F}_{n}
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tilde{F}_{n}}{F_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{x=1}^{n}\tilde{f}(x)}{\sum_{x=1}^{n}f(x)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{x=M}^{n}\tilde{f}(x)}{\sum_{x=M}^{n}f(x)}=1.

(H13)

Using Eq. (
D2
) and Eq. (
H12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

(H14)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in\Set{mM,\dots,n}}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tilde{F}_{n}}{F_{n}}\max_{k\in\Set{mM,\dots,n}}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}\tilde{p}^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}\tilde{p}^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\tilde{\chi}_{n}\to\tilde{\chi}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m}),

where again
a_{k}=\lfloor k/m\rfloor
and
b_{k}=k-ma_{k}
. Also in this case, we therefore proved that
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if
\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family (and the same holds for subfamilies).

Lastly, we consider the case when
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)=\infty
, and
f
is asymptotically non-decreasing. Analogously to the previous case, let
N
be such that
f
is non-decreasing for
x\in(N,\infty)
. Let
a
be the maximum of
f(x)
for
x\in\Set{1,\dots,N}
, and let
M>N
be such that
f(x)>a
for
x\geq M
. Also here, we define
\tilde{f}
via

\tilde{f}=\begin{cases}f(M)&\text{if }x\leq M,\\
f(x)&\text{if }x>M.\end{cases}

(H15)

The family of states
\Set{\tilde{\chi}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is regular,
\lim_{x\to\infty}\tilde{f}(x)/f(x)=1
, and
\lim_{x\to\infty}\tilde{F}_{n}/F_{n}=1
. The Schmidt coefficients associated to
\chi_{n}
and
\tilde{\chi}_{n}
are related by

p^{(n)}_{x}=\frac{f(n+1-x)}{F_{n}}=\frac{\tilde{f}(n+1-x)}{F_{n}}=\tilde{p}^{(n)}_{x}\frac{\tilde{F}_{n}}{F_{n}}\quad\forall x\leq n-M.

(H16)

Using Eq. (
D2
) again and the relation between Schmidt coefficients derived in Eq. (
H16
) we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle T_{\star}(\chi_{n}\to\chi_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m})

(H17)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n-M]}\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}p^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tilde{F}_{n}}{F_{n}}\max_{k\in[n-M]}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}\tilde{p}^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{k\in[n]}\left\{\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(k)}-\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(n)}\right\rVert_{(a_{k})}-\frac{b_{k}}{m}\tilde{p}^{(n)}_{a_{k}+1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{\star}(\tilde{\chi}_{n}\to\tilde{\chi}_{n}\otimes\Phi_{m}).

This proves the theorem.
∎

Thanks to Theorem
H.2
, Theorem
G.2
also holds for asymptotically regular families.

Corollary H.3
.

Let
f
be a positive asymptotically non-increasing function such that
f(x)/x^{\alpha}
is asymptotically monotonic for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the asymptotically regular family of states associated to
f
(see Definition
H.1
). Then
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family if and only if
f\in\omega(x^{-1-\varepsilon})\cap o(x^{-1+\varepsilon})
for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}f(x)=\infty
. Furthermore, if
f\notin\omega(x^{-1-\varepsilon})\cap o(x^{-1+\varepsilon})
for at least one
\varepsilon>0
, then
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is any sequence of natural numbers, is not a universal embezzling family.

Proof.

Combine Theorem
H.2
and Theorem
G.2
.
∎

For the same reasons, Proposition
G.3
and Proposition
G.4
also hold for asymptotically regular families.

Corollary H.4
.

Let
f
be a positive asymptotically non-decreasing function such that
f(x)/x^{\alpha}
is asymptotically non-increasing for at least one
\alpha>0
. Then the asymptotically regular family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
associated to
f
is not a universal embezzling family. Furthermore, there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family.

Corollary H.5
.

Let
f
be a positive asymptotically non-decreasing function such that
f(x)/e^{kx}
is asymptotically non-decreasing for at least one
k>0
. Then the asymptotically regular family of states
\Set{\chi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
associated with
f
is not a universal embezzling family. Furthermore, there are no sequences
\Set{n_{j}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Set{\chi_{n_{j}}}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a universal embezzling family.

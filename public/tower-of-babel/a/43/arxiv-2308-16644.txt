Paired kernels and their applications
M. Cristina Câmara, Jonathan R. Partington

Abstract
This paper considers paired operators in the context of the Lebesgue Hilbert space on the unit circle and its subspace, the Hardy space H 2 H^{2} . The kernels of such operators, together with their analytic projections, which are generalizations of Toeplitz kernels, are studied. Results on near-invariance properties, representations, and inclusion relations for these kernels are obtained. The existence of a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing any projected paired kernel and, more generally, any nearly S ∗ S^{*} -invariant subspace of H 2 H^{2} , is derived. The results are applied to describing the kernels of finite-rank asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators.



Keywords:

paired operator; paired kernel; Toeplitz operator; invariant subspace; nearly-invariant subspace; truncated Toeplitz operator;
kernel.

Introduction

Let
X
be a Banach space,
P\in\mathcal{L}(X)
a projection, and
Q=I-P
the complementary projection. An operator of the form
AP+BQ
or
PA+QB
with
A,B\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is called
a
paired operator

[
16
,
23
,
24
]
. In this paper we consider the case when
A=M_{a}
and
B=M_{b}
are
multiplication operators on
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, where
\mathbb{T}
denotes the unit circle, with
a,b\in L^{\infty}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, and we denote by
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{a,b}
the operators defined on
L^{2}
by

S_{a,b}f=aP^{+}f+bP^{-}f\,\,,\qquad\Sigma_{a,b}f=P^{+}(af)+P^{-}(bf).

(1.1)

Paired operators first appeared in the context of the theory of
singular integral equations

[
26
,
25
]
. Consider the canonical example of a singular integral operator on
L^{2}
,

(Lf)(t)=A(t)f(t)+B(t)(S_{\mathbb{T}}f)(t),

(1.2)

with
A,B\in L^{\infty}
and

(S_{\mathbb{T}}f)(t)=\frac{1}{\pi i}\hbox{PV}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{f(y)}{y-t}\,dy,\qquad t\in\mathbb{T}.

(1.3)

It is well known that, denoting by
P^{\pm}
the orthogonal
projections from
L^{2}
onto the Hardy spaces
H^{2}_{+}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and

H^{2}_{-}=\overline{H^{2}_{0}}=(H^{2}_{+})^{\perp}
, respectively,
identified with closed subspaces of
L^{2}
,
we have
I=P^{+}+P^{-}
and
S_{\mathbb{T}}=P^{+}-P^{-}
, so that
the operator
L
can be expressed as

L=(A+B)P^{+}+(A-B)P^{-}.

(1.4)

We may write this as
S_{a,b}

with
a=A+B\,,\,b=A-B
,
while its adjoint is a paired operator of the second type in (
1.1
),

S^{*}_{a,b}f=\Sigma_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}.

(1.5)

Paired operators are also closely related to
Toeplitz operators of the form

T_{a}=P^{+}aP^{+}_{|H^{2}},

(1.6)

where
a\in L^{\infty}
is called the
symbol
of the operator. If we represent
S_{a,b}
in the form

\begin{pmatrix}P^{+}aP^{+}&P^{+}bP^{-}\\
P^{-}aP^{+}&P^{-}bP^{-}\end{pmatrix}:H^{2}_{+}\oplus H^{2}_{-}\to H^{2}_{+}\oplus H^{2}_{-},

(1.7)

we see that paired operators are dilations of Toeplitz operators. If
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}

(that is, invertible in
L^{\infty}
)
we can write

S_{a,b}=aP^{+}+bP^{-}=b\left(\frac{a}{b}P^{+}+P^{-}\right),

(1.8)

which is equivalent after extension
[
2
]
to the Toeplitz operator
T_{a/b}

[
4
]
.

However, paired operators and spaces that turn out to be kernels of paired operators, called
paired kernels
, also
appear in different guises, for instance in the
study of dual truncated Toeplitz operators
[
7
]
, in the description of scalar-type block Toeplitz
kernels
[
14
]
, in the characterization of the ranges of
finite-rank truncated Toeplitz operators
[
5
]
, and in the study of nearly invariant subspaces for shift semigroups
[
22
]
.

We shall consider mainly paired operators of the form
S_{a,b}
, where the
pair
(a,b)
is called a
symbol pair
. These operators
have been considered mostly under particular conditions, such as invertibility in
L^{\infty}
, for
a,b
or
a/b

[
26
,
25
]
; see also
[
23
]
and references therein.
The operator
S_{a,b}
is said to be of
normal type
if
a,b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
.
This is by far the most studied case, but some particular types of
non-normal paired operators have also been considered
[
23
,
24
,
9
,
15
]
.
We shall assume throughout the paper, more generally, that
a,b\in L^{\infty}
and
the pair
(a,b)
is
nondegenerate
; that is,
a,b
and
a-b
are nonzero a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.
We also use the notation
P^{\pm}\phi=\phi_{\pm}
for
\phi\in L^{2}
.

Here we shall study in particular the properties of kernels of
paired operators, called
paired kernels
, and their projections into

H^{2}_{+}
and
H^{2}_{-}
, called
projected paired kernels
. Denoting

{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b},\qquad{\rm ker}^{\pm}_{a,b}=P^{\pm}{\rm ker}_{a,b},

(1.9)

we have that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}=P^{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=:{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
for the possibly unbounded Toeplitz operator
T_{a/b}
. If
a/b\in L^{\infty}
then
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
is a
Toeplitz kernel
, i.e., the kernel of a bounded Toeplitz operator; otherwise it may not be a closed subspace of
H^{2}_{+}:=P^{+}L^{2}
. However, we can define a one-to-one correspondence (see Section 2) between
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
and
{\rm ker}_{a,b}
, where the latter is closed because it is the kernel of a bounded operator. So one can also see paired kernels as being the natural closed space generalizations of Toeplitz kernels, allowing us to study the
kernels of unbounded Toeplitz operators
of the form
T_{a/b}
with
a/b\not\in L^{\infty}
in terms of the bounded operators

S_{a,b}
and
P^{+}
on
L^{2}
.
It is thus natural to ask whether some known properties
of Toeplitz kernels with bounded symbols
can be extended or related with corresponding properties of paired kernels
or their projections on
H^{2}_{+}
. Alternatively one can look at this as studying the question of how certain properties of a Toeplitz operator extend to a dilation of the form (
1.7
).

In the following sections we study several properties of paired kernels which extend or are in contrast with various known properties of Toeplitz kernels. To compare the case where
a/b\in L^{\infty}
(and
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
is a Toeplitz kernel) with that where
a/b\notin L^{\infty}
and to illustrate some natural questions arising in the latter case, we start by considering in Section
3
an example which appears in the study of nearly invariant subspaces for shift semigroups
[
22
]
. This leads to the study of near invariance properties of paired kernels (Section
4
); to the question of existence of a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing any given
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}

and, more generally, any space of the form
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
, including the
closed nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}_{+}

(Section
5
); to investigating the relations between paired kernels for operators with connected symbol pairs (Section
6
). In Section
7
the results are applied to study and describe the kernels of finite rank asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators
[
10
]
, showing in particular that, surprisingly, they do not depend on the range space if the latter is “large” enough.

2
Projected paired kernels

Recall that we denote the kernel of a paired operator, which we call a
paired kernel
, by

{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}

(2.1)

and we call

{\rm ker}^{\pm}_{a,b}=P^{\pm}{\rm ker}_{a,b}=P^{\pm}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}

(2.2)

a
projected paired kernel
.

Each function
f\in L^{2}
belongs to one and only one
paired kernel
[
6
, Thm. 4.6]
. On the other hand,
for any
\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
,

\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\iff\phi_{+}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b},

where
T_{a/b}
is the possibly unbounded Toeplitz operator with symbol
a/b
defined on the domain

D_{a/b}=\{\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}:\frac{a}{b}\phi_{+}\in L^{2}\}.

Analogously, for the dual Toeplitz operator
[
7
]

\check{T}_{b/a}:\check{D}_{b/a}=\left\{\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}:\frac{b}{a}\phi_{-}\in L^{2}\right\}\to H^{2}_{-},

defined by

\check{T}_{b/a}\phi_{-}=P^{-}\frac{b}{a}\phi_{-},

we have that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\check{T}_{b/a}
.

If
a/b\in L^{\infty}
then
D_{a/b}=H^{2}_{+}
and we say that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is a
Toeplitz kernel
, i.e., the kernel of a bounded Toeplitz operator.

Paired kernels and their projections into
H^{2}_{\pm}
can be related as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

The operators

\mathcal{P}^{+}:{\rm ker}_{a,b}\to{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+},\qquad\mathcal{P}^{+}\phi=\frac{b}{b-a}\phi

(2.3)

and

\mathcal{M}_{a/b}:{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\to{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-},\qquad\mathcal{M}_{a/b}\phi_{+}=\frac{a}{b}\phi_{+}

(2.4)

are well-defined and bijective with inverses

({\mathcal{P}^{+}})^{-1}:{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\to{\rm ker}_{a,b}\,\,,\qquad({\mathcal{P}^{+}})^{-1}\phi_{+}=(1-\frac{a}{b})\phi_{+}

(2.5)

and

\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{a/b}:{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}\to{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{a/b}\phi_{-}=\frac{b}{a}\phi_{-},

(2.6)

and we have
\mathcal{P}^{+}\phi=P^{+}\phi
, and
\mathcal{M}_{a/b}(\mathcal{P}^{+}\phi)=P^{-}\phi

for
\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

\dim{\rm ker}_{a,b}<\infty\iff\dim{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}<\infty\iff\dim{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}<\infty

and, if these dimensions are finite, then they are equal.

The following is also an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.1
,
noting that if
\phi_{+}=0
(or similarly for

\phi_{-}
) on a set of positive measure then, by the Luzin–Privalov theorem, it is
0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
then
\phi=0\iff\phi_{+}=0\iff\phi_{-}=0
.

Note that, although
\mathcal{P}^{+}
is bounded and bijective, its inverse is not necessarily bounded when
b\not\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
, since
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
may not be closed in
H^{2}_{+}
, as shown in
an example in the next section.

Just as we can relate the kernels of Toeplitz operators with those of dual Toeplitz operators, we can also reduce the study of
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
to that of
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}
, as the next proposition shows.

Proposition 2.4
.

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}=\bar{z}\overline{{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}}
.

Proof.

For
\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
we have

\displaystyle\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\hbox{ for some }\phi_{+}\in H^{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bar{a}(\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{+}})+\bar{b}(\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{-}})=0\hbox{ for some }\phi_{+}\in H^{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bar{b}(\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{-}})+\bar{a}\psi_{-}=0\hbox{ for some }\psi_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{-}}\in{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}\iff\phi_{-}\in\bar{z}\overline{{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}}.

∎

From now on we shall mainly focus on the properties of the projected
paired kernels
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
.
To compare the case where
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
with that where
b\not\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
, we start by considering a particular example
of the latter, which is used in
[
22
]
.

3
An example and questions it raises

Let
\theta
be the singular inner function
\theta(z)=\exp\dfrac{z-1}{z+1}
for
z\in\mathbb{T}

and let
a=\bar{\theta}
,
b(z)=z+1
. The kernel of
S_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
is described by

\bar{\theta}\phi_{+}+(z+1)\phi_{-}=0,\qquad\hbox{i.e.,}\quad\quad\frac{\phi_{+}}{z+1}=-\theta\phi_{-}.

(3.1)

Since the left-hand side of this last equation represents a function in the
Smirnov class
\mathcal{N}_{+}
and the right-hand side represents
a function in
L^{2}
, we have that both belong to
H^{2}_{+}
and thus, from

\bar{\theta}\frac{\phi_{+}}{z+1}=-\phi_{-},

(3.2)

we conclude that
\phi_{+}/(z+1)\in K_{\theta}
, where
K_{\theta}

denotes the model space
H^{2}_{+}\ominus\theta H^{2}_{+}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}}
.
It follows that
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}\subseteq(z+1)K_{\theta}
and the converse inclusion is easily seen to be true, so

{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}=(z+1)K_{\theta}.

(3.3)

Clearly
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits_{\bar{\theta},z+1}=\left(1-\frac{\bar{\theta}}{z+1}\right){\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}

is a closed subspace of
L^{2}
, since
S_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
is bounded, but

\mathcal{P}^{+}{\rm ker}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}={\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}=(z+1)K_{\theta}

(3.4)

is not closed
[
22
, Prop 3.2]
, so
\mathcal{P}^{+}
does not have a bounded inverse.

Note that, while
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
is not closed, it is nevertheless contained
in a (closed) minimal Toeplitz kernel, by which we mean a Toeplitz kernel that contains

{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
and it itself contained in any other Toeplitz kernel containing

{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
. Indeed, on the one hand,

(z+1)K_{\theta}={\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}\subsetneq K_{z\theta}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{z}\bar{\theta}}.

(3.5)

On the other hand it was shown in
[
11
]
that for any nonzero
\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel to which
\phi_{+}
belongs. Now,
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{z}\bar{\theta}}
is the minimal kernel
containing the function

f=(z+1)\frac{\theta-\theta(0)}{z}\in{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1},

since we have

\bar{z}\bar{\theta}f=\bar{z}\frac{z+1}{z}(1-\theta(0)\bar{\theta})=\bar{z}\overline{(z+1)(1-\overline{\theta(0)}\theta)},

where
(z+1)(1-\overline{\theta(0)}\theta)
is an outer function in
H^{2}_{+}

(see
[
12
, Thm, 2.2]
). Thus
K_{z\theta}
is the minimal Toeplitz kernel containing

{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
.

These results naturally raise several questions,
especially when compared with some known results for Toeplitz kernels.

Question
3
.1.

Having shown that
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
is not a Toeplitz kernel, one may ask
whether
there is any Toeplitz kernel contained in that space.
The answer is negative, due to the
near invariance properties of Toeplitz kernels

[
11
]
; these imply
in particular that no Toeplitz kernel can be contained in
(z+1)H^{2}_{+}
, since Toeplitz kernels are
nearly
\dfrac{1}{z+1}
invariant
[
11
]

and
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}=(z+1)K_{\theta}
.
This equality also shows that, in contrast with Toeplitz kernels, projected paired kernels may not be
nearly
\dfrac{1}{z+1}
invariant.
But do other near invariance properties of Toeplitz kernels extend to
projected paired kernels? This is studied in Section
4
, comparing the two cases
where
a/b\in L^{\infty}
and
a/b\not\in L^{\infty}
.

Question
3
.2.

On the other hand, there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
,
which is
K_{\theta z}
. Is there a
minimal Toeplitz kernel containing any given nontrivial

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
?
We answer this question in the affirmative, and in a more general setting, in Section
5
, by showing that
the closure of any projected paired kernel admits a representation of the form

f\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
with
f\in H^{2}_{+}
and
g\in L^{\infty}
, and we discuss the
existene of such a representation for
projected paired kernels. Note that not only can every Toeplitz kernel be
writen as a product of the form
f\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}

[
20
]
, but we also
have the same property for other projected paired kernels: for instance,

{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}=(z+1)K_{\theta}
.

Question
3
.3.

The relation (
3.5
) can be rewritten as
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta}\bar{z},1}
, raising the question of what may be
the inclusion relations between the two projected paired
kernels when we multiply the two elements of the symbol pair
(a,b)
by certain functions.
For Toeplitz operators we have, for instance, that

h_{-}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}\implies\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h_{-}g},

where the inclusion is strict if the inner factor of
\overline{h_{-}}
is non-constant

[
8
]
and an equality if
\overline{h_{-}}
is outer in
H^{\infty}
; also,

h_{+}\in H^{\infty}\implies h_{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h_{+}g}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},

where the inclusion is strict if
h_{+}
has a non-constant inner factor
[
8
]

and an equality if
h_{+}
is invertible in
H^{\infty}
.
We study how analogous inclusion relations can be established
for general projected paired kernels, and whether the inclusion is strict or an equality,
in Section
6
.

The results are applied to study kernels of (asymmetric) truncated Toeplitz operators in Section
7
.

4
Near invariance properties

A subspace
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H^{2}_{+}
is said to be

nearly
S^{*}
-invariant
if and only if

f_{+}\in\mathcal{S},f_{+}(0)=0\implies S^{*}f_{+}\in\mathcal{S}.

(4.1)

Here
S^{*}
denotes the backward shift on
H^{2}_{+}
, i.e.,

S^{*}=T_{\bar{z}}
.

Noting that
f_{+}(0)=0
means that
\bar{z}f_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
and, in that case,
S^{*}f=\bar{z}f
, it is clear that
(
4.1
) is equivalent to

f_{+}\in\mathcal{S},\bar{z}f_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}\implies\bar{z}f_{+}\in\mathcal{S}

(4.2)

and we say, equivalently, that
\mathcal{S}
is nearly
\bar{z}
-invariant
[
11
]
. More generally, if
\eta
is a complex-valued
function defined a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
we say that
\mathcal{S}\subseteq H^{2}_{+}
is
nearly
\eta
-invariant
if and only if

f_{+}\in\mathcal{S},\eta f_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}\implies\eta f_{+}\in\mathcal{S}.

(4.3)

In this case if
\eta\in L^{\infty}
we can also say that
\mathcal{S}
is nearly
T_{\eta}
-invariant.

Toeplitz kernels are closed nearly
S^{*}
-invariant spaces. Furthermore, in
[
11
]
a large class of
functions
\eta
was described for which all Toeplitz kernels are nearly

\eta
-invariant, and which includes all functions
in
\overline{H^{\infty}}
and all rational functions without poles in
\mathbb{D}^{e}\cup\{\infty\}
,
where
\mathbb{D}^{e}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|>1\}
.

In particular, Toeplitz kernels are nearly
\bar{\theta}
-invariant and nearly
\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}
-invariant, where
\theta
is any inner function and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\cup\mathbb{D}
.
As a consequence of this, we conclude that no Toeplitz kernel can be contained in
\theta H^{2}_{+}
or in

(z-z_{0})H^{2}_{+}
for
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\cup\mathbb{D}
.

It is clear from the examples of Section
3
that the latter property cannot be extended to
projected paired kernels in general. Other near invaiance properties of Toeplitz kernels, however, are
shared with
projected paired kernels.

The following results are simple consequences of the definitions at the beginning
of this section. We assume that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\neq\{0\}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is nearly
\eta
-invariant for every
\eta\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
.

Corollary 4.2
.

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is nearly
\bar{\theta}
-invariant for every inner function
\theta
,
and therefore there exists a function
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
such that

\phi_{+}\not\in\theta H^{2}_{+}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
is nearly
R
-invariant for every rational function
R
bounded at
\infty
whose poles lie in
\mathbb{D}
.

Naturally, if
a/b\in L^{\infty}
, this property can be extended to every rational
R
without
poles in
\mathbb{D}^{e}\cup\{\infty\}
, since in that case
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
is a Toeplitz kernel.

It follows from Proposition
4.1
, in particular, that projected paired kernels
are nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}_{+}
, and so are their closures, by the following
result.

Proposition 4.4
.

If
\mathcal{S}\subset H^{2}_{+}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant,
then its closure is also nearly
S^{*}
-invariant.

Proof.

Clearly we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{S}\neq\{0\}
.
In that case, since
\mathcal{S}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant, there must exist
h\in\mathcal{S}
with
h(0)=1
.
Now if
f\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}
with
f(0)=0
, then
there is a sequence
(f_{n})
in
\mathcal{S}
with
f_{n}\to f
in norm and
hence
f_{n}(0)\to f(0)=0
.
Thus for each
n
we have that
f_{n}-f_{n}(0)h
is a function in
\mathcal{S}
vanishing at
0
,
so
f_{n}-f_{n}(0)h=zg_{n}
for some

g_{n}\in\mathcal{S}
, and
\lim f_{n}=f=\lim zg_{n}
.
Hence
(g_{n})
converges to a function
g\in\bar{\mathcal{S}}
such that
f=zg
.
∎

The closed nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}_{+}
admit
a representation as a product of the form
uK_{\theta}
, where
u\in H^{2}_{+}
and

K_{\theta}
is a model space, which will be considered in the
next section. In the case of
the closure of
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
,
u
must be outer by Corollary
4.2
.

The notion of near
\eta
-invariance can naturally be extended to
{\rm ker}^{-}_{a,b}
, replacing

H^{2}_{+}
by
H^{2}_{-}
. The following
proposition
shows that it is enough to consider the problem of near invariance for
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
is nearly
\bar{\eta}
-invariant in
H^{2}_{-}
if and only if

{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}
is nearly
\eta
-invariant in
H^{2}_{+}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of Proposition
2.4
.
Suppose that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
is nearly
\bar{\eta}
-invariant in
H^{2}_{-}
, i.e.,

\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}_{a,b},\quad\bar{\eta}\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}\implies\bar{\eta}\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}_{a,b},

and let

\phi_{+}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}},\qquad\eta\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}.

Then
\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{+}}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
,
\bar{\eta}\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{+}}\in H^{2}_{-}
,
so
\bar{\eta}\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{+}}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
by near invariance which,
by Proposition
2.4
, implies that
\eta\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}
. So
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}
is nearly
\eta
-invariant in
H^{2}_{+}
.
The converse is proved analogously.
∎

Since
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is nearly
\bar{z}
-invariant in
H^{2}_{+}
(equivalently,
nearly
S^{*}
-invariant), it follows that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
is nearly
z
-invariant in
H^{2}_{-}
. Therefore,
if
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{\pm}\neq\{0\}
, there exists
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
with
\phi_{+}(0)\neq 0

and there exists
\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
such that
(z\phi_{-})(\infty)\neq 0

(i.e.,
\overline{\psi_{+}(0)}\neq 0
,
where
\psi_{+}=\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{-}}
).
Analogously, since
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is nearly
\frac{1}{z-z_{0}}
-invariant for any
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
, there exists, for any
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
,
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
with
\phi_{+}(z_{0})\neq 0

and
\psi_{-}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}
such that
\psi_{-}(1/z_{0})\neq 0
.

5
Minimal Toeplitz kernels and representations of projected paired kernels

If
a/b\in L^{\infty}
, then
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
is a Toeplitz kernel; but, in general,
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
may not even be a closed subspace
of
H^{2}_{+}
, as in the case studied in Section
3
. There it was also shown that,
although
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},z+1}
is not a Toeplitz
kernel, one can nevertheless determine a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing it. It is thus natural
to ask if such a property holds for every projected paired kernel. The answer is in the
affirmative, as one of the consequences of the following theorem.

Recall that a
maximal function

\phi_{m}
for a Toeplitz kernel
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}

is one such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
is the minimal kernel to which
\phi_{m}
belongs (see
[
11
]
).
Every Toeplitz kernel possesses a maximal function.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
a\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
and
b\in H^{2}_{+}
such that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\neq\{0\}

and
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\subset H^{2}_{+}
.
Then there exists a minimal Toeplitz kernel containing
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
, which is

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a\bar{b}/b_{o}}
, where

b_{o}
is the outer factor of
b
.
Moreover, if
\phi_{m}
is a maximal function for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
, then
b\phi_{m}

is a maximal function for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a\bar{b}/b_{o}}
.

Proof.

If
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\neq\{0\}
, let
\phi_{m}
be a maximal function of

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
, and write

\phi_{m}=I_{+}O_{+}
with

I_{+}
inner and
O_{+}
outer in
H^{2}_{+}
.
Then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{z}\overline{I_{+}}\overline{O_{+}}/O_{+}}

(5.1)

[
11
, Thm. 5.1]
.
On the other hand, there exists a minimal
kernel for
b\phi_{m}\in b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\subset H^{2}_{+}
which,
if
b=b_{i}b_{o}
is an inner–outer factorization of
b
with
b_{i}
inner and
b_{o}
outer, is given by

K_{\rm min}(b\phi_{m})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\dfrac{\bar{z}\overline{I_{+}}\overline{b_{i}}\overline{b_{o}}\overline{O_{+}}}{b_{o}O_{+}}}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\dfrac{\bar{z}\overline{b\phi_{m}}}{b_{o}O_{+}}}.

(5.2)

Let us show that

K_{\rm min}(b\phi_{m})\supset b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}.

(5.3)

Let
\psi_{+}
be any non-zero element of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
;
then (see (
5.1
))
\dfrac{\bar{z}\overline{I_{+}}\overline{O_{+}}}{O_{+}}\psi_{+}\in H^{2}_{-}
and

\underbrace{\frac{\bar{z}\overline{I_{+}}\overline{O_{+}}\overline{b_{i}}\overline{b_{o}}}{O_{+}b_{o}}}_{\in L^{\infty}}\underbrace{(b\psi_{+})}_{\in H^{2}_{+}}=\underbrace{\overline{b_{o}}}_{\in\overline{H^{2}_{+}}}\underbrace{\frac{\bar{z}\overline{I_{+}}\overline{O_{+}}}{O_{+}}\psi_{+}}_{\in H^{2}_{-}=\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}_{+}}}\in\bar{z}\overline{H^{1}}\cap L^{2}\subset\bar{z}H^{2}_{+}=H^{2}_{-}.

(5.4)

Thus
b\psi_{+}\in K_{\rm min}(b\phi_{m})
as defined in (
5.2
), and (
5.3
) holds.
On the other hand, since
b\phi_{m}\in b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
, any Toeplitz kernel
containing
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
must also contain
K_{\rm min}(b\phi_{m})
,
so the latter is the minimal kernel containing
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
.
Finally, from
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{z}\overline{I_{+}}\overline{O_{+}}/O_{+}}

we conclude that

a=\dfrac{\bar{z}\overline{I_{+}}\overline{O_{+}}}{O_{+}}h_{-}=\dfrac{\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{m}}}{O_{+}}h_{-}

for some
h_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}
(by
[
13
, Cor. 7.8]
. Therefore,

a\frac{\bar{b}}{b_{o}}=\frac{\bar{z}\overline{b\phi_{m}}}{b_{o}O_{+}}h_{-}

and it follows from (
5.2
) that
K_{\rm min}(b\phi_{+})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a\bar{b}/b_{o}}
.

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Every subspace of
H^{2}_{+}
of the form
uK_{\theta}
,
where
u\in H^{2}_{+}
and
\theta
is an inner function, is contained
in a minimal Toeplitz kernel.

As an example, take the example of Section
3
, namely
(z+1)K_{\theta}
. By Theorem

5.1
we have that the minimal kernel containing

(z+1)K_{\theta}
is

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}\frac{\overline{z+1}}{z+1}}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}\bar{z}}=K_{\theta z},

as shown before.

A well-known theorem by Hitt
[
21
]
describes the closed nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}_{+}
as having
the form
M=uK
, where
u\in H^{2}_{+}
has unit norm,
u(0)>0
,

u
is orthogonal to all elements of
M
vanishing at the origin,
K
is
an
S^{*}
-invariant subspace, and the operator of multiplication by
u

is isometric from
K
into
M
. Naturally, one can have

K=\{0\}
or
K=H^{2}_{+}
, but the most interesting cases are those in which
K
is a model space
K_{\theta}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

For every nondegenerate
(a,b)
there exists a minimal kernel containing
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
,
which coincides with
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
if
a/b\in L^{\infty}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
, the closure of
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant, so we have that
the closure of
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is
uK
, where
u\in H^{2}_{+}
is outer, by Corollary
4.2
,
and
K=H^{2}_{+}
or
K=K_{\theta}
with
\theta
inner. In the
latter case, the
result follows from
Proposition
4.1
, Proposition
4.4
, Hitt’s theorem, and
Corollary
5.2
.
If
K=H^{2}_{+}
, then, since
uH^{2}_{+}\subseteq H^{2}_{+}
is closed and
u
is outer, we must have

u\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
and
uK=H^{2}_{+}
.
∎

Hayashi showed in
[
20
]
that the kernel of every Toeplitz operator
T_{g}
can be
written as
uK_{\theta}
where
u
is outer,
u^{2}
is rigid (an exposed point of the unit ball of
H^{1}
),
\theta
is inner with

\theta(0)=0
, and
u
multiplies
K_{\theta}
isometrically onto the Toepliz kernel.
It may happen that
u\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
and, in that case, we can write

uK_{\theta}=u\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}u^{-1}}={\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},u}.

(5.5)

Other representations of a similar form can be found for Toeplitz
kernels. For instance, if

g\in L^{\infty}
admits a Wiener–Hopf factorization of the form
g=g_{-}\bar{\theta}g_{+}
with

g_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}
,
g_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
and
\theta
inner, then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}=g_{+}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}}=g^{-1}_{+}K_{\theta}={\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta},g^{-1}_{+}}.

(5.6)

Another example where

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}=b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}

(5.7)

is the case studied in Section
3
, which is not a Toeplitz kernel. It is thus natural to ask for conditions under which
(
5.7
) holds and, in general, what is the relation between
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
and
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
for
a,b\in L^{\infty}

nondegenerate.
We have the following.

Proposition 5.4
.

For
a,b\in L^{\infty}
we have that

b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap H^{2}_{+}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
and

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}=b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap H^{2}_{+}\,\,\,{\rm if\,and\,only\,if}\,\,\,{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq bH^{2}_{+}.

(5.8)

Proof.

We have
a\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\subseteq H^{2}_{-}
, so, for any
\phi_{+}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
such that

b\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
,

a\underbrace{(b\phi_{+})}_{\in H^{2}_{+}}+b\underbrace{(-a\phi_{+})}_{\in H^{2}_{-}}=0,

and it follows that
b\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
. So
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap H^{2}_{+}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
.

Now, it is clear that
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap H^{2}_{+}={\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
implies that

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq bH^{2}_{+}
.
Conversely, suppose that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq bH^{2}_{+}
.
Then, for any
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
we have
\phi_{+}=b\psi_{+}

with
\psi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
, and therefore, for some
\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
,

\displaystyle a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle ab\psi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle b(a\psi_{+}+\phi_{-})=0.

We have the standing assumption that
b\neq 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, so
a\psi_{+}=-\phi_{-}
.
We conclude that
\psi_{+}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
, so
\phi_{+}\in b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap H^{2}_{+}
, and it
follows that

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap H^{2}_{+}.

∎

An immediate consequence of Proposition
5.4
is the following.

Corollary 5.5
.

If
b\in H^{\infty}
then
b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
.

It is clear from the invariance results of Section
4
that we can have
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}\subseteq bH^{2}_{+}
, with
b\in H^{\infty}
, only if
b
is outer.
Moreover, we have the following.

Corollary 5.6
.

If
b\in H^{\infty}
is outer and either (i)
a\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
or
(ii)
b\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, then
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}=b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
.

Proof.

If
b\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
then
bH^{2}_{+}=H^{2}_{+}
and the equality follows from (
5.8
).
If
a\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
then

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\iff\underbrace{\frac{\phi_{+}}{b}}_{\in\mathcal{N}^{+}}=\underbrace{-\frac{\phi_{-}}{a}}_{\in L^{2}}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}\cap L^{2}=H^{2}_{+},

so
\phi_{+}\in bH^{2}_{+}
and again the equality in Corollary
5.6

follows from (
5.8
).
∎

6
Inclusion relations

As in the case of Toeplitz kernels
[
11
]
, the near invariance properties of projected paired kernels imply certain lower bounds for the dimension of a paired kernel
containing a given function. For instance, if

\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
and
\phi_{+}(0)=0
, then
{\rm ker}_{a,b}
must also
contain the function
\psi\in L^{2}
with
\psi_{+}=\bar{z}\phi_{+}
(note that this
defines
\psi
by Proposition
2.1
).
As another example, using a similar reasoning, if there exists
\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
such that

\phi_{+}\in\theta H^{2}_{+}
or
\phi_{-}\in\bar{\theta}H^{2}_{-}
where
\theta

is inner but not a finite Blaschke product, then
{\rm ker}_{a,b}
is infinite-dimensional.

On the other hand, it is easy to see that, if
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are
inner functions, then

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta_{1},b\overline{\theta_{2}}}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+},

(6.1)

We may then ask if the inclusion is strict and,
in that case, how much “smaller”

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta_{1},b\overline{\theta_{2}}}
is with respect to
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
and,
in particular, when it is
\{0\}
.
More generally, one may ask what are the relations between two paired kernels, or two projected paired kernels,
whose symbol pairs are related by multiplication operators.

Note that, since a nontrivial paired kernel cannot be contained in a different one, and indeed their intersection is
\{0\}
(see
[
6
]
), obtaining, for example,
a paired operator analogue of the property
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\theta g}\subsetneq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
(valid for

g\in L^{\infty}
and
\theta
inner, nonconstant) is possible only
by saying that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits_{\theta a,b}
is isomorphic to a proper subspace of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits_{a,b}
.
Alternatively, taking Proposition
2.1
into account, we can say it in an equivalent and simpler way as
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\theta a,b}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
(cf. Proposition

6.1
).

In the following propositions we present several inclusion relations between projected paired kernels which generalise similar properties valid for Toeplitz kernels,
using in particular the near invariance properties of
Section
4
to establish strict inclusions.

We recall that, for any
\eta\in L^{\infty}
,

{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{a\eta,b\eta}
, so
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}={\rm ker}^{+}_{a\eta,b\eta}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

(i) If
h_{-}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
then
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{-}}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}
.

(ii) If
\overline{h_{-}}
is outer, then

\displaystyle(a)\quad{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{-}}={\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}

\displaystyle\hbox{if }\frac{a}{bh_{-}}\in L^{\infty},

\displaystyle(b)\quad{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}={\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}

\displaystyle\hbox{if }\frac{a}{b}\in L^{\infty}.

(iii) If
\overline{h_{-}}
has a non-constant inner factor, then

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{-}}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}.

Proof.

(i)
a\phi_{+}+bh_{-}\phi_{-}=0\implies a\phi_{+}+b(h_{-}\phi_{-})=0
and

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\implies(ah_{-})\phi_{+}+b(h_{-}\phi_{-})=0
.

(ii) Let now
\overline{h_{-}}
be outer. We start by proving the second equality
(ii)(b). We have

ah_{-}\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\iff\dfrac{\phi_{-}}{h_{-}}=-\dfrac{a}{b}\phi_{+}\iff\dfrac{\overline{\phi_{-}}}{\overline{h_{-}}}=-\overline{\dfrac{a}{b}\phi_{+}}.

Since the left-hand side of the last equation is in the Smirnov class
\mathcal{N}_{+}

when
\overline{h_{-}}
is outer and the right-hand side is in
L^{2}
if

a/b\in L^{\infty}
, we have under these assumptions that
\phi_{-}/h_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
,
so
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
since
a\phi_{+}+b\dfrac{\phi_{-}}{h_{-}}=0
, and thus

{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
. The equality follows from (i).

Next, we have that

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\implies a\phi_{+}+bh_{-}\dfrac{\phi_{-}}{h_{-}}=0\implies\dfrac{\phi_{-}}{h_{-}}=-\dfrac{a}{bh_{-}}\phi_{+},

and we conclude analogously that
\dfrac{\phi_{-}}{h_{-}}\in H^{2}_{-}
so

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{-}}
. Again the equality (ii)(a) follows from (i).

(iii) Suppose that
\overline{h_{-}}
has a non-constant inner factor.
If
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{-}}
then, for any
\phi\in L^{2}
such that

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
we must also have
\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
such that

a\phi_{+}+bh_{-}\psi_{-}=0
.
Thus
\phi_{-}=h_{-}\psi_{-}
. This implies that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{-}\subseteq h_{-}H^{2}_{-}
, which is impossible by Corollary
4.2
because
\overline{h_{-}}
has a nonconstant inner factor. So

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{-}}\subsetneq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
.

A similar argument shows that
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
a/b\in L^{\infty}
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
. Then

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a(z-z_{0}),b}={\rm ker}^{+}_{az,b}
.

Proof.

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a(z-z_{0}),b}={\rm ker}^{+}_{az(z-z_{0})/z,b}={\rm ker}^{+}_{az,b}

by Proposition
6.1
(ii), since
(z-z_{0})/z\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
its conjugate is
1-\overline{z_{0}}z
, an outer function in
H^{\infty}
.
∎

As an immediate corollary we have:

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
. Then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g(z-z_{0})^{n}}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{gz^{n}}
.

Remark 6.4
.

This corollary allows us to generalize several results from
[
8
, Sec. 6]
,
in particular Theorems 6.2 and 6.7 in
[
8
]
, which deal
with the relations between
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\theta g}
, for a
finite Blaschke product
\theta
, to the case where the symbol has
zeros of integer order on
\mathbb{T}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

(i) If
h_{+}\in H^{\infty}
then

h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{+},b}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\quad\hbox{and}\quad h_{+}{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{+}}.

(ii) If
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, then

h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{+},b}={\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}=h_{+}^{-1}{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{+}}.

(iii) If
h_{+}
has a non-constant inner factor, then

h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{+},b}\subsetneq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\quad\hbox{and}\quad h_{+}{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{+}}.

Proof.

(i) We can write
(ah_{+})\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
as
a(h_{+}\phi_{+})+b\phi_{-}=0
,
from which
h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{+},b}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
; then if
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
we have

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
for some
\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
, so,
since
a(h_{+}\phi_{+})+(bh_{+})\phi_{-}=0
, we have
h_{+}\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{+}}
.

(ii) If
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, then from
a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
we obtain

ah_{+}(\phi_{+}h_{+}^{-1})+b\phi_{-}=0
, so

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{+},b}
and we have equality from (i).
The second equality follows from
h_{+}^{-1}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{+}^{-1},b}=h_{+}^{-1}{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{+}}
.

(iii) If
h_{+}
has a non-constant inner factor, we cannot have

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq h_{+}H^{2}_{+}
nor
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{+}}\subseteq h_{+}H^{2}_{+}
by the near-invariance result of Corollary
4.2
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
6.1
(ii) and Proposition
6.5
(ii) we have
the following.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
B=B_{-}gB_{+}\in L^{\infty}
, where

B_{-}^{\pm 1}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}

and
B_{+}^{\pm 1}\in H^{\infty}
. Then

{\rm ker}^{+}_{aB,b}=B_{+}^{-1}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ag,b}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,Bb}=B_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,gb}.

Proof.

\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}_{aB,b}\iff\exists\phi_{-}:aB\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0,

that is,

aB_{-}gB_{+}\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
.

We write this as
\exists\phi_{-}:ag(B_{+}\phi_{+})+b(\phi_{-}B^{-1}_{-})=0
.
Equivalently,

\exists\psi_{-}:ag(B_{+}\phi_{+})+b\psi_{-}=0
.

Finally, this holds if and only if
B_{+}\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}_{ag,b}
, i.e.,

\phi_{+}\in B^{-1}_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{ag,b}
.

The other identity is proved similarly.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

If
h_{-}\in\mathcal{G}\overline{H^{\infty}}
then

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}={\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}={\rm ker}^{+}_{a,h^{-1}_{-}b}={\rm ker}^{+}_{a,h_{-},b}
.

Proof.

We have
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}
; conversely,

\displaystyle ah_{-}\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle a\phi_{+}+b(h^{-1}_{-}\phi_{-})=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+},

so
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}={\rm ker}^{+}_{ah_{-},b}={\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh^{-1}_{-}}
and,
replacing
h_{-}
by
h^{-1}_{-}
we conclude that

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}={\rm ker}^{+}_{a,bh_{-}}
.

∎

Since the inclusion in (
6.1
) is strict when
\theta_{1}
or
\theta_{2}
is
not constant or, equivalently,

{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta,b}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,b}
when
\theta
is a non-constant inner function, one
may ask whether the dimensions of
those two spaces can be compared, as in the case of Toeplitz kernels.
The following
theorem generalises analogous results obtained in
[
3
, Sec. 2]

and
[
8
, Sec. 6]
for Toeplitz kernels, and can be proved
in a similar way.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
\theta
be a non-constant finite Blaschke product. Then

(i)
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta,b}
is finite-dimensional if and only if
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is finite-dimensional.
Similarly for
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a,\theta b}
.

(ii) If
\dim{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}=d<\infty
and
\dim K_{\theta}\geq d
then
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta,b}=\{0\}
.

(iii) If
\dim{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}=d<\infty
and
\dim K_{\theta}=k<d
, then

\dim{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta,b}=d-k
.

Note that by Proposition
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
the same result holds if we replace

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
by
{\rm ker}_{a,b}
etc.
When
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is not finite-dimensional, one
can still compare it with
{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta,b}
by means of
the following decomposition.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
\theta
be a non-constant finite Blaschke product.
If
\theta
has
k
zeros in
\mathbb{D}
, counting multiplicities,
in which case we can write
\theta=B_{-}z^{k}B_{+}
with
B_{-}^{\pm 1}\in\overline{H_{\infty}}
and

B_{+}^{\pm 1}\in H^{\infty}
, then
there exist
\psi_{j+}\in H^{2}_{+}
(
j=0,\ldots,k-1
) with

\psi_{j+}(0)=1
, for each
j
such that the following direct sum decomposition holds:

\displaystyle{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{k}B_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{a\theta,b}+\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits\{\psi_{0+},z\psi_{1+},\ldots,z^{k-1}\psi_{(k-1)+}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{k}{\rm ker}^{+}_{az^{k},b}+\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits\{\psi_{0+},z\psi_{1+},\ldots,z^{k-1}\psi_{(k-1)+}\}.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.6
it is enough to consider

{\rm ker}^{+}_{az^{k},b}
.
Since
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant,
there exists
\psi_{0+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
with

\psi_{0+}(0)=1
;
let
\psi_{0-}
be given by
a\psi_{0+}+b\psi_{0-}=0
. Then for any
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}
,

\displaystyle a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle az\frac{\phi_{+}-\phi_{+}(0)\psi_{0+}}{z}+a\phi_{+}(0)\psi_{0+}+b\phi_{-}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle az\frac{\phi_{+}-\phi_{+}(0)\psi_{0+}}{z}+b(\phi_{-}-\phi_{+}(0)\psi_{0-})=0,

so

\tilde{\phi}_{+}:=\frac{\phi_{+}-\phi_{+}(0)\psi_{0+}}{z}\in{\rm ker}^{+}_{az,b}

and

\phi_{+}=z\tilde{\phi}_{+}+\phi_{+}(0)\psi_{0+}
.

Therefore
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}=z{\rm ker}^{+}_{az,b}\oplus\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits\{\psi_{0}\}
.

Proceeding analogously with
{\rm ker}^{+}_{az,b},{\rm ker}^{+}_{az^{2},b},\ldots,{\rm ker}^{+}_{az^{k-1},b}
,
we get

{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{+}=z^{k}{\rm ker}^{+}_{az^{k},b}\oplus\mathop{\rm span}\nolimits\{\psi_{0+},z\psi_{1+},\ldots,z^{k-1}\psi_{(k-1)+}\},

where
\psi_{j+}\in H^{2}
and
\psi_{j+}(0)=1
for all
j=0,1,\ldots,k-1
.
∎

We now obtain a description of some related
projected paired kernels that will be used in the next section
to study truncated Toeplitz operators:
{\rm ker}^{+}_{p_{1},p_{2}}
,
{\rm ker}^{+}_{\alpha p_{1},p_{2}}
and

{\rm ker}^{+}_{p_{1},\alpha p_{2}}={\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\alpha}p_{1},p_{2}}
, where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}

are polynomials of degrees
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
respectively
without common zeros, and
\alpha
is an inner function.
Note that
{\rm ker}^{+}_{p_{1},p_{2}}
can be related to a new class of Toeplitz-like
operators introduced in

[
17
,
18
,
19
]
: see
[
6
]
.

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
satisfy the assumptions above,
and let moreover
p_{i}=p_{i\mathbb{D}}p_{i\mathbb{T}}p_{i\mathbb{E}}
, for
i=1,2
, where
the zeros of
p_{i\mathbb{D}}
,
p_{i\mathbb{T}}
and
p_{i\mathbb{E}}

are in
\mathbb{D},\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{D}^{e}
, respectively, and we denote
by
n_{i\mathbb{D}},n_{i\mathbb{T}}
and
n_{i\mathbb{E}}
the corresponding degrees. Then

(i)
{\rm ker}^{+}_{p_{1},p_{2}}=\{0\}
if
n_{2}\leq m:=n_{2\mathbb{T}}+n_{1\mathbb{T}}+n_{2\mathbb{E}}+n_{1\mathbb{D}}
;

(ii)
{\rm ker}^{+}_{p_{1},p_{2}}=p_{1\mathbb{T}}p_{2\mathbb{T}}p_{1\mathbb{D}}p_{2\mathbb{E}}\mathcal{P}_{n_{2}-m-1}
,
where
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}
, for
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, denotes the space of all
polynomials of degree less than or equal to
\ell
, and

\dim{\rm ker}^{+}_{p_{1},p_{2}}=n_{2}-m=n_{2\mathbb{D}}-n_{1\mathbb{D}}-n_{1\mathbb{T}}

if
n_{2}>m
.

Proof.

p_{1}\phi_{+}+p_{2}\phi_{-}=0\iff p_{1}\phi_{+}=-p_{2}\phi_{-}=-\underbrace{p_{2\mathbb{T}}p_{2\mathbb{E}}p_{2\mathbb{D}}}_{\text{degree }n_{2}}\phi_{-}=q_{n_{2}-1},

where
q_{n_{2}-1}
is a polynomial of degree less than or equal to
n_{2}-1
.
Since

\dfrac{q_{n_{2}-1}}{p_{2}}\in H^{2}_{-}
and
\dfrac{q_{n_{2}-1}}{p_{1}}\in H^{2}_{+}
,

q_{n_{2}-1}
must be of the form

q_{n_{2}-1}=p_{2\mathbb{T}}p_{2\mathbb{E}}p_{1\mathbb{T}}p_{1\mathbb{D}}q
, where
q
is a polynomial.
Thus if
n_{2}-1<m
then
q_{n_{2}-1}=0
and (i) holds.
Also, if
n_{2}>m
then (ii) holds.
∎

Proposition 6.11
.

With the same assumptions and notation as in Proposition

, let

where the projected paired kernel on the right-hand side is described in Proposition

6.10

Proof.

(i) If

.

(ii) If

by Proposition

.
∎

Analogously, we have:

.

With the same assumptions and notation as in Proposition

,

.
∎

7
Kernels of finite-rank asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators

Paired operators can also be defined in the matricial setting. They appear in the
literature
[
23
,
16
,
7
]

as operators on
(L^{2})^{n}
with
n\times n
matricial coefficients
A,B\in\mathcal{L}((L^{2})^{n})
.

In most cases
A
and
B
are multiplication operators and in that case the paired
operator takes the form

T_{A,B}=AP^{+}+BP^{-}\qquad\hbox{with}\quad A,B\in(L^{\infty})^{n\times n}.

(7.1)

One can, however, consider other possible generalizations of scalar paired operators, for instance

T_{A,B}:(L^{2})^{n}\to L^{2},\qquad T_{A,B}=AP^{+}+BP^{-}\quad\hbox{with}\quad A,B\in\mathcal{L}((L^{2})^{n},L^{2})

(7.2)

or, considering in particular multiplication operators,

T_{A,B}=AP^{+}+BP^{-}:(L^{2})^{n}\to L^{2}\qquad\hbox{with}\quad A,B\in(L^{\infty})^{1\times n},

(7.3)

A=[a_{i}]^{T}_{i=1,\ldots,n},\qquad B=[b_{i}]^{T}_{i=1,\ldots,n}.

The kernels of operators of this form are defined by the Riemann–Hilbert problem

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\phi_{i+}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\phi_{i-},\qquad\phi_{i\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}.

(7.4)

The latter have appeared in recent works: see, for instance,

[
1
]
and
[
14
]
.

We apply here the results of the latter, together with those of previous sections,
to study the kernels of finite-rank asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators (ATTO for short) of the form

A_{\phi}^{\theta,\alpha}:K_{\theta}\to K_{\alpha},\qquad A_{\phi}^{\theta,\alpha}=P_{\alpha}\phi{P_{\theta}}_{|K_{\theta}},

(7.5)

where
\theta,\alpha
are inner functions,
P_{\theta}
and
P_{\alpha}

denote the orthogonal projections from
L^{2}
onto
K_{\theta}
and
K_{\alpha}
respectively,
and
\phi\in L^{\infty}
is called the
symbol
of the ATTO.

Indeed, if we define
P_{1}
to be the projection
P_{1}(x,y)=x
,
then we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A_{\phi}^{\theta,\alpha}=P_{1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G},

(7.6)

where
G
has the matrix symbol

G=\left[\begin{matrix}\bar{\theta}&0\\
\phi&\alpha\end{matrix}\right].

(7.7)

Now it was shown in
[
14
, Thm. 3.1 and Cor. 3.4]
that the kernels of block
Toeplitz operators with symbols of the form (
7.7
)
are of so-called
scalar type
, i.e., can be described as
scalar multiples of a fixed vector function, and can be
obtained from a pair of functions
f,g\in\mathcal{F}^{2}
,
where
\mathcal{F}
consists of all complex-valued functions defined a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
,
provided that
f
and
g
satisfy the relation

Gf=g
.

To present the results from
[
14
]
that will be used here, it is useful
to settle some notation, as follows:

(i) if
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{F}^{2}
, then we say that
f
is
left-invertible in
\mathcal{F}
if and only if there exists
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{F}^{2}
such that

\tilde{f}^{T}f=1
, and, in that case,
\tilde{f}^{T}
is called the left
inverse of
f
in
\mathcal{F}
;

(ii) we define
\mathcal{H}_{X}=\{h\in\mathcal{H}:Xh\in\mathcal{H}\}

for any subspace
\mathcal{H}\subseteq(L^{2})^{n}
and any
X\in\mathcal{F}^{n\times n}
;

(iii) for any matricial coefficients
A,B
with elements in
\mathcal{F}
let
us define
T_{A,B}f=AP^{+}f+BP^{-}f
for

f\in(L^{2})^{n}
and
write
{\mathfrak{s}}_{A,B}=A(H^{2}_{+})^{n}\cap B(H^{2}_{-})^{n}
.

With this notation we can formulate Corollary 3.4 in

[
14
]
as follows.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
and
g=(g_{1},g_{2})
belong to
\mathcal{F}^{2}

and such that
Gf=g
with
G\in(L^{\infty})^{2\times 2}
.
If
\mathcal{S}:={\mathfrak{s}}_{(\det G)f^{T},-g^{T}}=\{0\}
,
then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A^{\theta,\alpha}_{\phi}=\mathcal{K}f_{1}
, where

\mathcal{K}=\tilde{f}^{T}[(H^{2}_{+})^{2}]_{f\tilde{f}^{T}}\cap\tilde{g}^{T}[(H^{2}_{-})^{2}]_{g\tilde{g}^{T}}.

(7.8)

We now apply Theorem
7.1
and the results of the previous sections
to study the behaviour of an ATTO of the form (
7.5
) with finite rank.
As explained in
[
10
, Sec. 3]
we are led to take
the symbol to be

\phi=\bar{\theta}R_{+}-\alpha R_{-}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{\theta}P^{\alpha}_{n_{j}-1}(t_{j})-\alpha P^{\bar{\theta}}_{n_{j}-1}(t_{j})}{(z-t_{j})^{n_{j}}},

(7.9)

where

(i)
R_{\pm}
are rational functions vanishing at
\infty
, such that
R_{-}

has no poles in
\mathbb{D}^{e}\cup\mathbb{T}
and
R_{+}
has no poles in
\mathbb{D}\cup\mathbb{T}
;

(ii)
t_{j}\in\mathbb{T}

(j=1,\ldots,N)
are regular points for
\theta
and
\alpha
, i.e.,

\theta
and
\alpha
are analytic in a neighbourhood of each
t_{j}
(in which case

\bar{\theta}
, which can be extended to a definition outside
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
by
\bar{\theta}(z)=\overline{\theta(1/\bar{z})}
for

|z|>1
, is also analytic in a neighbourhood of
t_{j}
);

(iii)
P^{\alpha}_{n_{j}-1}
and
P^{\bar{\theta}}_{n_{j}-1}
are the Taylor polynomials of order

n_{j}-1
, relative to the point
t_{j}
, for
\alpha
and for
\bar{\theta}
,
respectively.

Defining

\displaystyle R_{2}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{+}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{P^{\alpha}_{n_{j}-1}(t_{j})}{(z-t_{j})^{n_{j}}}=\frac{Q_{2}}{\mathcal{E}D_{2+}},

(7.10)

\displaystyle R_{1}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{-}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{P^{\bar{\theta}}_{n_{j}-1}(t_{j})}{(z-t_{j})^{n_{j}}}=\frac{Q_{1}}{\mathcal{E}D_{1-}},

(7.11)

where

\mathcal{E}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(z-t_{j})^{n_{j}},\quad\hbox{with}\quad\deg\mathcal{E}=n_{\mathbb{T}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j},

(7.12)

and
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are polynomials,
D_{2+}
is the denominator of
R_{+}
, with
n_{-}

zeros (including multiplicities) in
\mathbb{D}^{e}
, and
D_{1-}
is the denominator of
R_{-}
,
with
n_{+}
zeros in
\mathbb{D}
, we can write, from (
7.9
),

\phi=\bar{\theta}R_{2+}-\alpha R_{1-}.

(7.13)

Then
G
in (
7.7
) takes the form

G=\left[\begin{matrix}\bar{\theta}&0\\
\bar{\theta}R_{2+}-\alpha R_{1-}&\alpha\end{matrix}\right]

(7.14)

and one can verify that
f,g\in\mathcal{F}^{2}
, defined by

\displaystyle f=\left[\begin{matrix}f_{1}\\
f_{2}\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}\theta\\
\theta R_{1-}\end{matrix}\right]=\theta\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
\frac{Q_{1}}{\mathcal{E}D_{1-}}\end{matrix}\right],

\displaystyle g=\left[\begin{matrix}g_{1}\\
g_{2}\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
R_{2+}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
\frac{Q_{2}}{\mathcal{E}D_{2+}}\end{matrix}\right],

(7.15)

satisfy
Gf=g
and have
left inverses, respectively,

\tilde{f}^{T}=[\bar{\theta}\quad 0],\qquad\tilde{g}^{T}=[1\quad 0].

(7.16)

To apply Theorem
7.1
,
we start by showing that the assumption
\mathcal{S}=\{0\}
in Theorem
7.1

is satisfied when
K_{\alpha}
is “large enough”.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G
be given by (
7.14
) with
\alpha
such that

\dim K_{\alpha}\geq m:=n_{+}+n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}
,
and let
f,g
be defined as in (
7.15
).
Then
\mathcal{S}={\mathfrak{s}}_{(\det G)f^{T},-g^{T}}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

We have
\det G=\alpha\bar{\theta}
and
\mathcal{S}
is defined by

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\phi_{1+}+\frac{Q_{1}}{\mathcal{E}D_{1-}}\phi_{2+}\right)=\phi_{1-}+\frac{Q_{2}}{\mathcal{E}D_{2+}}\phi_{2-}

(7.17)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{D_{1-}}\underbrace{(\mathcal{E}D_{1-}\phi_{1+}+Q_{1}\phi_{2+})}_{\psi_{+}}=\frac{z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}}{D_{2+}}\underbrace{\frac{\mathcal{E}D_{2+}\phi_{1-}+Q_{2}\phi_{2-}}{z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}}}_{\psi_{-}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\alpha D_{2+}\psi_{+}=z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}D_{1-}\psi_{-},

where
\psi_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}
. So
\psi=\psi_{+}+\psi_{-}\in{\mathfrak{s}}_{\alpha D_{2+},-z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}D_{1-}}=\{0\}
,
by Corollary
2.3
and Proposition
6.11
(i)
(with
p_{1}=D_{2+}

p_{2}=-z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}D_{1-}

\alpha\left(\phi_{1+}+\frac{Q_{1}}{\mathcal{E}D_{1-}}\phi_{2+}\right)=\frac{\alpha}{\mathcal{E}D_{1-}}\psi_{+}=0

and, of course, analogously for the right-hand side of (

), so
\mathcal{S}=\{0\}
.
∎

Next we characterise the spaces
[(H^{2}_{+})^{2}]_{f\tilde{f}^{T}}
and

[(H^{2}_{+})^{2}]_{g\tilde{g}^{T}}
in (
7.8
). Note that

f\tilde{f}^{T}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&0\\

so

(\phi_{1+},\phi_{2+})\in[(H^{2}_{+})^{2}]_{f\tilde{f}^{T}}\iff R_{1-}\phi_{1+}\in H^{2}_{+}\iff\phi_{1+}\in\mathcal{E}D_{1-}H^{2}_{+}

and

(\phi_{1-},\phi_{2-})\in[(H^{2}_{-})^{2}]_{g\tilde{g}^{T}}\iff R_{2+}\phi_{1-}\in H^{2}_{-}\iff\phi_{1-}\in\frac{\mathcal{E}D_{2+}}{z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}}H^{2}_{-}.

(7.20)

We can now formulate the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\phi
be given by (
7.13
) and (
7.10
)–(
7.12
),
and
let
\dim K_{\alpha}\geq m:=n_{+}+n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}

Then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A^{\theta,\alpha}_{\phi}
do not depend on
\alpha
and we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}=\mathcal{E}D_{1-}D_{2+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}z^{m}}\left[\begin{matrix}1\\
R_{1-}\end{matrix}\right]\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A^{\theta,\alpha}_{\phi}=\mathcal{E}D_{1-}D_{2+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}z^{m}}.

K_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

By (
7.8
), (
7.19
) and (
7.20
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\bar{\theta}\quad 0]\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{E}D_{1-}H^{2}_{+}\\
H^{2}_{+}\end{matrix}\right]\cap[1\quad 0]\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{\mathcal{E}D_{2+}}{z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}}H^{2}_{-}\\
H^{2}_{-}\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}\mathcal{E}D_{1-}H^{2}_{+}\cap\frac{\mathcal{E}D_{2+}}{z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}}H^{2}_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}\mathcal{E}D_{1-}{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta}D_{1-},-\frac{D_{2+}}{z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}\mathcal{E}D_{1-}{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta}D_{1-}z^{n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}},-D_{2+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}\mathcal{E}D_{1-}D_{2+}{\rm ker}^{+}_{\bar{\theta}z^{n_{+}+n_{-}+n_{\mathbb{T}}},-1}

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}\mathcal{E}D_{1-}D_{2+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}z^{m}},

where we used Proposition
6.12
, and the result follows from Theorem

7.1
.
∎

Note that, if
z^{m}
divides
\theta
then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\bar{\theta}z^{m}}
is the

, so
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A_{\phi}^{\theta,\alpha}
is isomorphic to

K_{\theta/z^{m}}
.

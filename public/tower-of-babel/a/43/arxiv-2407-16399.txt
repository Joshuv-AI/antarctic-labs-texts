A new numerical scheme for Itô stochastic differential equations based on Wick-type Wong-Zakai arguments
Alberto Lanconelli, Berk Tan Perçin

Abstract
The aim of this note is to propose a novel numerical scheme for drift-less one dimensional stochastic differential equations of Itô’s type driven by standard Brownian motion. Our approximation method is equivalent to the well known Milstein scheme as long as the rate of convergence is concerned, i.e. it is strongly convergent with order one, but has the additional desirable property of being exact for linear diffusion coefficients. Our approach is inspired by Wick-type Wong-Zakai arguments in the sense that we only smooth the white noise through polygonal approximation of the Brownian motion while keep the equation in differential form. A first order Taylor expansion of the diffusion coefficient allows us to solve the resulting equation explicitly and hence to provide an implementable approximation scheme.



Key words and phrases: Brownian motion, stochastic differential equation, Wick product, Milstein scheme.

AMS 2000 classification: 60H10, 65C30, 60H25.

1
Statement of the main result

Let
\{X(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be the unique strong solution of the Itô stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX(t)=\sigma(X(t))dB(t),&t\in]0,1];\\
X(0)=a,&\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\{B(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is a standard one dimensional Brownian motion,
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function while
a\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. Assume that:

•

\sigma
and
\sigma\sigma^{\prime}
are continuously differentiable with bounded derivatives.

We have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
let
\{x_{N}(k)\}_{k\in\{0,...,N\}}
be defined recursively as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{N}(k+1)=x_{N}(k)+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\left(\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\right\}-1\right),&k\in\{0,...,N-1\};\\
x_{N}(0)=a.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Then, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X(1)-x_{N}(N)|]\leq\frac{\mathtt{c}}{N},

(1.3)

where
\{X(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is the unique strong solution of the SDE (
1.1
) while
\mathtt{c}
is a positive constant independent of
N
. Moreover, when
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is linear, the sequence
\{x_{N}(k)\}_{k\in\{0,...,N\}}
coincides with
\{X\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\}_{k\in\{0,...,N\}}
.

In words: scheme (
1.2
) provides a strong approximation of order one for the unique strong solution of the SDE (
1.1
); in addition, the scheme is exact when the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is linear.

Remark 1.2

(Implementability of the scheme)
.

Scheme (
1.2
) is fully explicit since the term

\displaystyle\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\right\},

(1.4)

which denotes a
Wick exponential
(see e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
7
]
), is simply a shorthand notation for

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{2N}\right\}

which makes the overall numerical scheme as

\displaystyle x_{N}(k+1)=x_{N}(k)+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\Bigg(\exp\Bigg\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))\Bigg(B\Big(\frac{k+1}{N}\Big)-B\Big(\frac{k}{N}\Big)\Bigg)-\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{2N}\Bigg\}-1\Bigg).

This expression is fully explicit and hence implementable for approximation purposes.

Remark 1.3

(Vanishing derivative of
\sigma
)
.

Possibly vanishing values of
\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))
in the ratio
\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}
from (
1.2
) do not cause any problem of ill-posedness. In fact, exploiting the Wick-series expansion of (
1.4
) we can write conditionally on
\mathcal{F}^{B}_{k/N}
(here we denote by
\{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
the augmented Brownian filtration) that

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\left(\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\right\}-1\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{j}}{j!}\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)^{\diamond j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{j-1}}{j!}\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)^{\diamond j},

thus making perfectly sense for possibly vanishing values of
\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))
. Here, the symbol
\cdot^{\diamond j}
denotes the
j
-th Wick power.

Remark 1.4

(Comparison with Milstein scheme)
.

If we Taylor expand the Wick exponential in (
1.2
) up to the second order, we recover the famous Milstein scheme
[
16
,
8
]
. In fact, recalling the Wick-series expansion of (
1.4
) utilized in Remark
1.3
we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\left(\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\right\}-1\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{2}\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)^{\diamond 2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}{2}\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)^{\diamond 2}

\displaystyle=\sigma(x_{N}(k))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}{2}\left(\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{N}\right),

where in the last equality we exploit the identity between Wick powers and Hermite polynomials
[
7
]
. Substituting the last member above into the right hand side of (
1.2
) we obtain the Mistein scheme for the SDE (
1.1
).

Remark 1.5

(Equation with drift)
.

Following the proof of Theorem (
1.1
) presented in Section
2
below, one can see how the introduction of a drift term in equation (
1.1
) causes a non trivial issue in the derivation of our scheme. In fact, while the Wick exponential possesses a Wick-free representation as mentioned in Remark
1.2
, the corresponding term for an equation with drift will fail in that direction. And since the scheme must be fully implementable, the presence of general terms involving Wick products may prevent to reach that goal. We devote to a future study the investigation of such an extension.

Remark 1.6
.

Our main theorem is a further example of the beneficial effect of treating some aspects of the theory of Itô SDEs from a Wick calculus’ perspective. We mention in passing that the role of Wick product as
natural
Gaussian convolution, shown in
[
2
]
and
[
15
]
, has been identified for Poisson
[
14
]
and Gamma
[
13
]
distributions as well. The approach presented here (which essentially relies on the properties of chaos decompositions) together with those contributions may lead to analogous approximation results for stochastic equations driven by non-Gaussian noises.

1.1
Heuristic derivation of the scheme

In this section we describe some basic ideas that lead to the derivation of scheme (
1.2
). This is inspired by Wong-Zakai approximation results
[
17
,
18
]
for Itô’s type SDEs firstly obtained in
[
6
]
(see also
[
5
]
and the references quoted there) and then extended to different contexts in
[
1
,
3
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. The key observation guiding the approach has to be found in the correspondence between Itô-Skorohod integration and Gaussian Wick product; for a detailed discussion on this issue we refer the reader to
[
4
]
.
Exploiting this fact we can indeed rewrite the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{X}(t)=\sigma(X(t))\diamond\dot{B}(t),&t\in]0,1];\\
X(0)=a,&\end{cases}

(1.5)

where
\{\dot{B}(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
denotes Gaussian White Noise. Now, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the partition of the interval
[0,1]
given by
\{0,1/N,2/N,...,1\}
and denote by
\{B_{N}(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
the associated polygonal approximation of
\{B(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
; this means that

\displaystyle B_{N}(t):=B(k/N)+(t-k/N)\frac{B((k+1)/N)-B(k/N)}{1/N},\quad t\in\left[k/N,(k+1)/N\right[,k\in\{0,...,N-1\},

and replacing
\{B(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
with
\{B_{N}(t)\}_{t\in[0,1]}
we get

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{X}_{N}(t)=\sigma(X_{N}(t))\diamond\dot{B}_{N}(t),&t\in]0,1];\\
X_{0}=a.&\end{cases}

(1.6)

Notice that the function
t\mapsto\dot{B}_{N}(t)
is piece-wise constant; therefore, we can rewrite equation (
1.6
) more accurately as the collection of the following nested Cauchy problems

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{N,k+1}(t)=\sigma(x_{N,k+1}(t))\diamond\frac{B((k+1)/N)-B(k/N)}{1/N},&t\in]k/N,(k+1)/N];\\
x_{N,k+1}(k/N)=x_{N,k}(k/N),&\end{cases}

(1.7)

for
k\in\{0,...,N-1\}
and with the initial condition:
x_{N,1}(0)=x_{N,0}(0)=a
. We now Taylor-expand up to the first order the function
\sigma
in (
1.7
) around the initial condition
x_{N,k}(k/N)
to get

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{N,k+1}(t)\approx

\displaystyle\left(\sigma(x_{N,k}(k/N))+\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N,k}(k/N))(x_{N,k+1}(t)-x_{N,k}(k/N))\right)\diamond\frac{B((k+1)/N)-B(k/N)}{1/N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N,k}(k/N))x_{N,k+1}(t)\diamond\frac{B((k+1)/N)-B(k/N)}{1/N}

\displaystyle+\left(\sigma(x_{N,k}(k/N))-\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N,k}(k/N))x_{N,k}(k/N))\right)\diamond\frac{B((k+1)/N)-B(k/N)}{1/N}

(1.8)

Equation (
1.1
) is linear in
\dot{x}_{N,k+1}(t)
and can be easily solved either using the reduction method proposed in
[
6
]
or by means of (conditional on
\mathcal{F}^{B}_{k/N}
)
\mathcal{S}
-transform
[
9
]
which will convert the previous stochastic equation into a linear deterministic one. In particular, one will find that the value of the solution to (
1.1
) at the node
(k+1)/N
is given by

\displaystyle x_{N,k+1}((k+1)/N)=

\displaystyle x_{N,k}(k/N)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(x_{N,k}(k/N))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N,k}(k/N))}\left(\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N,k}(k/N))\left(B\left(\frac{k+1}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\right\}-1\right).

If we now set
x_{N}(k):=x_{N,k}(k/N)
for
k\in\{0,...,N\}
we see that the previous identity coincides with scheme (
1.2
).

2
Proof of the main result

To ease the notation, for
k\in\{1,...,N\}
we set

\displaystyle Z_{k}:=\sqrt{N}\left(B\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)-B\left(\frac{k-1}{N}\right)\right),

so that
\{Z_{k}\}_{k\in\{1,...,N\}}
is a collection of independent standard Gaussian random variables; therefore, scheme (
1.2
) now reads

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{N}(k+1)=x_{N}(k)+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\left(\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))Z_{k+1}/\sqrt{N}\right\}-1\right),&k\in\{0,...,N-1\};\\
x_{N}(0)=a.\end{cases}

(2.1)

We start proving the following upper bound for the second moment of
x_{N}(k+1)
. In the sequel we will write
\mathcal{F}_{k}
for the sigma algebra generated by
\{Z_{1},...,Z_{k}\}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\{0,...,N\}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)^{k}(a^{2}+1)-1,

(2.2)

where
L_{1}
stands for the Lipschitz constant of the function
\sigma
while
M_{1}
is a positive constant with which the inequality

\displaystyle\sigma(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq M_{1}(1+x^{2}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}

(2.3)

holds true (the existence of such
M_{1}
follows from the global Lipschitz continuity of
\sigma
).

Proof.

Notice that for all
k\in\{1,...,N\}
the random variable
x_{N}(k)
is
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable and hence taking the square of both sides in (
2.1
) and computing conditional expectations with respect to the sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{k}
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k+1)^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=x_{N}(k)^{2}+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))Z_{k+1}/\sqrt{N}\right\}-1\right)^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right].

Here, the mixed product of
\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}
with
\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))Z_{k+1}/\sqrt{N}\right\}-1
vanishes since the first term is
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable while the second one has null conditional expectation. Moreover, a simple computation gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\exp^{\diamond}\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))Z_{k+1}/\sqrt{N}\right\}-1\right)^{2}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]=\exp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}/N\right\}-1;

hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k+1)^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=x_{N}(k)^{2}+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}\left(\exp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}/N\right\}-1\right),

and taking expectation of both sides we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k+1)^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}\frac{\exp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}/N\right\}-1}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}\right].

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}/N\right\}-1}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2(j-1)}}{j!N^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2(j-1)}}{j!N^{j-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2(j-1)}}{(j-1)!N^{j-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}/N\}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k+1)^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}\frac{\exp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}/N\right\}-1}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]+\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]+\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{1}(1+x_{N}(k)^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N};

here, we utilized the inequality

\displaystyle\sigma(x)^{2}\leq M_{1}(1+x^{2}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

The previous computation shows that the sequence of second moments
k\mapsto\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]
satisfies the recursive inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k+1)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N},

with
\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(0)^{2}]=a^{2}
, which gives via a simple algebraic manipulation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{N}(k)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq a^{2}\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)^{k}+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\frac{\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)^{k}-1}{\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)-1}

\displaystyle=a^{2}\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)^{k}+\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)^{k}-1

\displaystyle=\left(1+M_{1}\frac{\exp\{L_{1}^{2}/N\}}{N}\right)^{k}(a^{2}+1)-1.

The proof is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
; we will compare our scheme with the Milstein’s one and show that they are of the same order. In the sequel we will denote the Milstein scheme with
\{y_{N}(k)\}_{k\in\{0,...,N\}}
, namely

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{N}(k+1)=y_{N}(k)+\sigma(y_{N}(k))Z_{k+1}/\sqrt{N}+\sigma(y_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(y_{N}(k))\left(Z_{k+1}^{2}-1\right)/2N\\
y_{N}(0)=a.\end{cases}

(2.4)

We start with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X(1)-x_{N}(N)|]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|X(1)-y_{N}(N)|]+\mathbb{E}[|y_{N}(N)-x_{N}(N)|]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|X(1)-y_{N}(N)|]+\mathbb{E}[|y_{N}(N)-x_{N}(N)|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is well known (see
[
8
]
) that there exists a constant
C_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|X(1)-y_{N}(N)|]\leq\frac{C_{1}}{N};

(2.5)

our aim is to prove that there exists a constant
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|y_{N}(N)-x_{N}(N)|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{N},

which in combination with (
2.5
) is equivalent to (
1.3
).

For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\{0,...,N-1\}
we have (recall Remark
1.4
)

\displaystyle x_{N}(k+1)-y_{N}(k+1)=

\displaystyle x_{N}(k)-y_{N}(k)+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k)))Z_{k+1}/\sqrt{N}

\displaystyle+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(y_{N}(k)))\left(Z_{k+1}^{2}-1\right)/2N

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))}\sum_{j\geq 3}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{j}}{j!}Z_{k+1}^{\diamond j}/N^{\frac{j}{2}}.

We now take the square of both sides in the equality above and compute conditional expectations with respect to the sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{k}
; recall that the Wick powers
Z_{k+1}^{\diamond j}
are orthogonal in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
for different
j
’s (see
[
7
]
) and hence all the mixed products generated with the square of the right hand side above will vanish in conditional expectation. Moreover, the squared
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)
-norm of
Z_{k+1}^{\diamond j}
is
j!
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(x_{N}(k+1)-y_{N}(k+1))^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=

\displaystyle(x_{N}(k)-y_{N}(k))^{2}+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k)))^{2}/N

\displaystyle+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(y_{N}(k)))^{2}/2N^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}\sum_{j\geq 3}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2j}}{j!^{2}}j!/N^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{N}(k)-y_{N}(k))^{2}+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k)))^{2}/N

\displaystyle+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(y_{N}(k)))^{2}/2N^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}\sum_{j\geq 3}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2j}}{j!N^{j}}.

Let us focus on the last term above:

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2}}\sum_{j\geq 3}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2j}}{j!N^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{N}\sum_{j\geq 3}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2(j-1)}}{j!N^{j-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{N}\sum_{j\geq 3}\frac{\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))^{2(j-1)}}{(j-1)!N^{j-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{N}\sum_{j\geq 3}\frac{L_{1}^{2(j-1)}}{(j-1)!N^{j-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{N}\left(e^{L_{1}^{2}/N}-1-L_{1}^{2}/N\right).

This gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(x_{N}(k+1)-y_{N}(k+1))^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq

\displaystyle(x_{N}(k)-y_{N}(k))^{2}+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k)))^{2}/N

\displaystyle+(\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(y_{N}(k)))^{2}/2N^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}}{N}\left(e^{L_{1}^{2}/N}-1-L_{1}^{2}/N\right)

and taking the expectation of both sides yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(x_{N}(k+1)-y_{N}(k+1))^{2}]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(x_{N}(k)-y_{N}(k))^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[(\sigma(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k)))^{2}]/N

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[(\sigma(x_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(x_{N}(k))-\sigma(y_{N}(k))\sigma^{\prime}(y_{N}(k)))^{2}]/2N^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}]}{N}\left(e^{L_{1}^{2}/N}-1-L_{1}^{2}/N\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{L_{1}^{2}}{N}+\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{2N^{2}}\right)\mathbb{E}[(x_{N}(k)-y_{N}(k))^{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathbb{E}[\sigma(x_{N}(k))^{2}]}{N}\left(e^{L_{1}^{2}/N}-1-L_{1}^{2}/N\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{L_{1}^{2}}{N}+\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{2N^{2}}\right)\mathbb{E}[(x_{N}(k)-y_{N}(k))^{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{M_{1}}{N}\left(e^{L_{1}^{2}/N}-1-L_{1}^{2}/N\right)

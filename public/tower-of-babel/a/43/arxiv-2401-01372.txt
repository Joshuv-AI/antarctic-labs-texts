Hopf algebras for the shuffle algebra and fractions from multiple zeta values
Li Guo, Wenchuan Hu, Hongyu Xiang, Bin Zhang

Abstract
. The algebra of multiple zeta values (MZVs) is encoded as a stuffle (quasi-shuffle) algebra and a shuffle algebra. The MZV stuffle algebra has a natural Hopf algebra structure which has important applications to MZVs. This paper equips a Hopf algebra structure to the MZV shuffle algebra. The needed coproduct is defined by a recursion through a family of weight-increasing linear operators. To verify the Hopf algebra axioms, we make use of a family of fractions, called Chen fractions, that have been used to study MZVs and also serve as the function model for the MZV shuffle algebra. Applying natural derivations on functions and working in the context of locality, a locality Hopf algebra structure is established on the linear span of Chen fractions. This locality Hopf algebra is then shown to descend to a Hopf algebra on the MZV shuffle algebra, whose coproduct satisfies the same recursion as the first-defined coproduct. Thus the two coproducts coincide, establishing the needed Hopf algebra axioms on the MZV shuffle algebra.

Introduction

This paper constructs a Hopf algebra structure on the shuffle algebra from multiple zeta values, where the coproduct satisfies a differential type recursion and is the descendant of a natural coproduct on the space of fractions defining MZVs.

1.2.
Hopf algebra structures on the MZV quasi-shuffle algebra and MZV shuffle algebra

Further understanding of MZVs depends on revealing deeper structures of the MZV quasi-shuffle algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},*)
and MZV shuffle algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}})
, including their possible Hopf algebra structures.

By the general construction, the MZV quasi-shuffle algebra has a natural enrichment to a Hopf algebra with the deconcatenation coproduct
[
26
]
.
Its action on the MZV shuffle algebra has been used to obtain large classes of algebraic relations of MZVs
[
28

34

Hopf algebras have played a critical role in the study of motivic MZVs

14
,
35
]

The situation is quite different for a Hopf algebra structure on the MZV shuffle algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}})

First, the space
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
has the natural deconcatenation of the vectors

[s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}]\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{k}[s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}]\otimes[s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{k}].

But it is not compatible with the product
\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}}
on
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
.

Next note that in the MZV shuffle algebra
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}})
, the notion of
[\vec{s}]\in{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
is a contracted form of elements in
\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1}
via the algebra isomorphism
\rho
extending Eq. (

(12)

\rho:({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}})\to(\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1},{\mbox{\cyrs X}}),\ [s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}]\to x_{0}^{s_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{s_{k}-1}x_{1}.

The shuffle algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle x_{0},x_{1}\rangle x_{1},{\mbox{\cyrs X}})
has a natural Hopf algebra structure with the deconcatenation coproduct of words. However, this coproduct does not pull back to
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}})
. As a simple example, for the word

corresponding to

, its deconcatenation coproduct is

The purpose of this paper is to provide a Hopf algebra structure on
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}})

In fact, the definition of the coproduct is motivated by a natural coproduct of Chen fractions which define MZVs. Further applications will be given in later studies
[
20

.

The rest of the introduction will convey some idea of our approach and give an outline of the construction.

1.3.
New Hopf algebra structure on the MZV shuffle algebra

The goal of Section
2
is to introduce just enough notions to state the main theorem on the Hopf algebra structure on
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}})
. The main ingredient of the construction is the coproduct
{\Delta_{\geq 1}}
which can be most easily defined by a recursion derived from a coderivation condition on a family of linear operators on
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
(Definition
2.3
).
Examples are provided to illustrate the recursion. The main result on the corresponding Hopf algebra structure is stated in Theorem
2.5
, followed by a sketch of the proof, and the verification that the coproduct is well defined (Proposition
2.6
).

It is might be possible to prove Theorem
2.5
by directly verifying the axioms of a bialgebra. However, checking that the coproduct is an algebra homomorphism is made challenging due to the complexity of the shuffle product in
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
. For example, the shuffle product of two one-dimensional vectors

and
[t]
is the Euler decomposition formula in disguise

[s]\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}}[t]=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\big(\!\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{s+i-1}\\
\scriptstyle{i}\end{array}\!\big)[s+i,t-i]+\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\big(\!\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{t+j-1}\\
\scriptstyle{j}\end{array}\!\big)[t+j,s-j].

In general, a product formula is provided in
[
22
]
, but its complexity makes it impractical for further computations.

We will instead take an indirect yet quite natural approach, using tools from Chen fractions and locality. This is carried out in Section
3
. The first tool is the representation of MZVs by functions of the form

\frac{1}{(x_{i_{1}}+x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+x_{i_{k}})^{s_{1}}(x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+x_{i_{k}})^{s_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{s_{k}}},

where
s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. They are called Chen fractions due their relation to Chen cones
[
17
]
.
They are also called MZV fractions
[
21
]
since they define MZVs
[
3
,
25
,
32
]

\zeta(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})=\sum_{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\frac{1}{(x_{i_{1}}+x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+x_{i_{k}})^{s_{1}}(x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+x_{i_{k}})^{s_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{s_{k}}}.

The product of two Chen fractions with disjoint variables satisfies the shuffle relation, giving rise to the shuffle relation of MZVs
[
17
,
21
]
, thus serving the same purpose as the integral representations of MZVs in Eq. (
4
).
Therefore, a Hopf algebraic structure on Chen fractions is of independent interest and is the motivation for our choice of the Hopf algebra structure on
{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
.

Working with the function subspace spanned by Chen fractions has the advantage they are naturally equipped with a family of partial derivatives. Since these derivations increase the degree of a Chen fraction, they can be used to give a recursive definition of a coproduct, by requiring that the derivations are also coderivations with respect to the tensor product (Definition
3.5
).
Furthermore, as the product of two Chen fractions is just the function multiplication, the derivations can be used give an inductive argument to verify the bialgebra axioms.

However, in order for these advantages of Chen fractions to take effect, their space first needs to be closed under the function multiplication, which is not the case. So apply our second tool of locality algebraic structures, motivated by the locality principle in physics contexts and abstracted into an algebraic framework in
[

17
,
18

19

. A locality set is simply a set equipped with a symmetric binary relation. Imposing compatibility conditions of the relation with various algebraic axioms leads to the corresponding locality algebraic structures, in particular locality Hopf algebras. Applying the locality framework to the space Chen fractions equips the space with a locality Hopf algebra structure (Theorem
3.8

[
2

6

as well as providing a locality setting for the recent study of multi-differential algebra and multi-Novikov algebra arising from regularity structures in stochastic PDEs

]

In Section

\frac{1}{(x_{i_{1}}+x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+x_{i_{k}})^{s_{1}}(x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+x_{i_{k}})^{s_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{s_{k}}}\mapsto[s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}]

To begin with, a

(X,\top)
where
X

\top:=X\times_{\top}X\subseteq X\times X

locality relation

For a variable set

[

The fraction is also called a MZV fraction
[
21
]
because its free summation for the variable
x_{i}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
gives the MZV:

(28)

\zeta(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})=\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k})^{s_{1}}(n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k})^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{k}^{s_{k}}}.

See

Let
{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}

3.2.
Locality multi-differential Hopf algebras

Here we give the notion of locality multi-differential Hopf algebras, in order to be applied to the locality multi-differential algebra of Chen fractions.

The coalgebra structure can be generalized to the locality setting
[

]

For a locality vector space
(C,\top)
over
\mathbb{Q}
, let

C\otimes_{\top}C

denote the image of the composition map

\mathbb{Q}(C\times_{\top}C)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(C\times C)\rightarrow C\otimes C,

where the first map is the inclusion and the second map is the quotient map modulo bilinearity.

(i)

Let
\Delta:C\to C\otimes C
be a linear map. The triple
(C,\top,\Delta)
is called a
(counitary) locality coalgebra
if it satisfies the following conditions.

(a)

For
U\subset C
,

(32)

\Delta(U^{\top})\subseteq U^{\top}\otimes_{\top}U^{\top},

which is equivalent to the condition that, for
c\in C
,

\Delta(c^{\top})\subseteq c^{\top}\otimes_{\top}c^{\top}.

(b)

The coassociativity holds:

({\rm Id}_{C}\otimes\Delta)\,\Delta=(\Delta\otimes{\rm Id}_{C})\,\Delta.

(c)

There is a
counit
, namely a linear map
\varepsilon:C\to K
such that

({{\rm Id}}_{C}\otimes\varepsilon)\,\Delta=(\varepsilon\otimes{{\rm Id}}_{C})\,\Delta={{\rm Id}}_{C}
.

(ii)

A
graded locality coalgebra
is a locality coalgebra

(C,\top,\Delta)
with a grading
C=\oplus_{n\geq 0}C_{n}
such that, for
U\subseteq C
,

(33)

\Delta(C_{n}\cap U^{\top})\subseteq\bigoplus_{p+q=n}(C_{p}\cap U^{\top})\otimes_{\top}(C_{q}\cap U^{\top}).

Moreover, the graded locality coalgebra is called
connected
if

\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}C_{n}=\ker\varepsilon,

and so
C_{0}
is one-dimensional.

(iii)

A
locality bialgebra
is a sextuple
(B,\top,m,u,\Delta,\varepsilon)
consisting of a locality algebra
(B,\top,m,u)
and a locality coalgebra
\left(B,\top,\Delta,\varepsilon\right)

such that
\Delta
and
\varepsilon
are locality algebra homomorphisms as stipulated in Eq. (
31
).

(iv)

A locality bialgebra
B
is called
graded
if there is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-grading
B=\oplus_{n\geq 0}B_{n}
with respect to which
B
is both a graded locality algebra and a graded locality coalgebra. In this case,
B
is called
connected
if it is connected as a coalgebra.

(v)

A
locality Hopf algebra
is a septuple
(B,\top,m,\Delta,u,\varepsilon,S)
such that
(B,\top,m,\Delta,u,\varepsilon)
is a locality bialgebra,
S:B\to B
is called an
antipode
which is a linear map such that
a\top b
implies
a\top S(b)
for
a,b\in B
,
and

S\star{\rm Id}_{B}={\rm Id}_{B}\star S=u\,\varepsilon.

Generalizing the notion of differential (associative) Hopf algebra

[

,

Definition 3.4

as defined in Eq. (

\Delta d_{i}(a^{\top})=(\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{i}+d_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta(a^{\top})\subseteq a^{\top}\otimes_{\top}a^{\top}.

3.3.
Locality multi-differential Hopf algebra of Chen fractions

We now apply the general notion of locality multi-differential Hopf algebras to the locality multi-differential algebra
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\partial_{i})
in Proposition
3.3
.

With the derivations
\partial_{i}:=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
, we define

(35)

d_{i,j}:={\partial}_{i}-{\partial}_{j},

which are again derivation. Then we have
[
17
]

\displaystyle{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}=\frac{d_{i_{1},i_{0}}^{s_{1}-1}d_{i_{2},i_{1}}^{s_{2}-1}\cdots d_{i_{k},i_{k-1}}^{s_{k}-1}}{(s_{1}-1)!(s_{2}-1)!\cdots(s_{k}-1)!}{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}.

Definition 3.5
.

Define a linear map

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}:\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}

by the following recursion.

(i)

For the constant function
1
, define

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}(1):=1\otimes 1.

(ii)

For
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{k}=1
, define

(40)

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\big({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\big):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{j}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{j+1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}.

(iii)

Recursively, define

(41)

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{j}+1,\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{j}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}):=\frac{\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{i_{j},i_{j-1}}+d_{i_{j},i_{j-1}}\otimes\mathrm{id}}{s_{j}}\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{j},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{j}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}).

Since the partial derivatives
\partial_{i}
and hence all
d_{i,j}
commute among themselves, iterations of the recursion does not depend on the order of applications. Therefore, the linear map
\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}
is well-defined. Complete details of the proof mirrors that of Proposition
2.6
.

Note that Eq. (
41
) means

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}d_{i_{j},i_{j-1}}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{i_{j},i_{j-1}}+d_{i_{j},i_{j-1}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}.

This is the defining identity that the derivations
d_{i,j}
are also locality coderivations as in Eq. (
34
).

Define a linear map
\varepsilon:\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}
on the basis
{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}
by

\varepsilon(f)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}1,&{\rm if}\,f=1,\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 3.6
.

The quadruple
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}},\varepsilon,\{\partial_{i}\}_{i})
is a locality multi-differential coalgebra.

Proof.

Since

{\partial}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}d_{j,j-1},,\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},

the recursion in Eq. (
41
) is equivalent to the coderivation condition

(42)

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}{\partial}_{i}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{i}+{\partial}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}},\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

It then follows that

(43)

\begin{split}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}})(\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{i}+{\partial}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{i}+\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}+{\partial}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}),\\
(\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{i}+{\partial}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{i}+\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}+{\partial}_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\otimes\mathrm{id}).\end{split}

Now we prove the coassociativity

(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})=(\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

of
\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}
by induction on

. When
|\vec{s}|=k
, we have
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{k}=1
, and the coproduct is just the deconcatenation and hence is coassociative:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\big({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq\ell\leq k}{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{j}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{j+1}},\cdots,x_{i_{\ell}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{\ell+1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\big({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,1,\cdots,1\\
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\big)

Assume that
\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}
is coassociative for Chen fractions with
|\vec{s}|=\ell\geq k
. Then for a Chen fraction
{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}
with
|\vec{s}|=\ell+1
, there is

. So we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\cdots s_{j}\cdots s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}})\Big(\frac{\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}+d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}}{s_{j}-1}\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\cdots s_{j}-1\cdots s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\textcircled{1}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}+\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}+d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}}{s_{j}-1}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\cdots s_{j}-1\cdots s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle\overset{\textcircled{2}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}+\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}+d_{{i_{j}},{i_{j-1}}}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}}{s_{j}-1}(\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\cdots s_{j}-1\cdots s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle\overset{\textcircled{3}}{=}

\displaystyle(\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1}\cdots s_{j}\cdots s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}),

where
\textcircled{1}

43
),
\textcircled{3}
follows from Eqs. (

) and (

), and
\textcircled{2}
follows from the induction hypothesis. This completes the inductive proof that
\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}
is coassociative.

We further show that
\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}
is a locality coproduct, namely,
\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}(\{f\}^{\top})\subseteq\{f\}^{\top}\otimes_{\top}\{f\}^{\top}
for arbitrary

To begin with, it is known that
1\in\{f\}^{\top}
, and

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}(1)=1\otimes 1\in\{f\}^{\top}\otimes_{\top}\{f\}^{\top}.

Next let

x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}

Finally, we show that

∎

We apply the induction on the sum of weights

Now let

44

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\Big({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\

\displaystyle\overset{\textcircled{1}}{=}

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\Big(\frac{d_{i_{p},i_{p-1}}}{s_{p}-1}\big({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{p}-1,\cdots,s_{k}\\

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{id}\otimes d_{i_{p},i_{p-1}}+d_{i_{p},i_{p-1}}\otimes\mathrm{id}}{s_{p}-1}\Big(\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}\big({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{p}-1,\cdots,s_{k}\\

Theorem 3.8
.

The sextuple
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\cdot,\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}},1,\varepsilon,\{\partial_{i}\}_{i})
is a locality connected multi-differential bialgebra and hence a locality multi-differential Hopf algebra as defined in Definition
3.4
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
,
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\cdot,1,\{\partial_{i}\}_{i})
is a locality multi-differential algebra. By Proposition
3.6
,
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},

\top,\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}},\varepsilon,\{\partial_{i}\}_{i})
is a locality multi-differential coalgebra. Also, by Lemma
3.7
,
\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}
is a locality algebra homomorphism with respect to the locality multiplication
``\cdot"
.

We now check that
\varepsilon
is a locality algebra homomorphism as follows.

(i)

\varepsilon(1)=1=\varepsilon(1)\cdot\varepsilon(1)
,

(ii)

For basis elements
f,g\in{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}
such that
f\top\ g
and
f\neq 1
or
g\neq 1
,

\varepsilon(f\cdot g)=0=\varepsilon(f)\cdot\varepsilon(g).

So
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\cdot,\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}},1,\varepsilon)
is a locality bialgebra.

We next show that the locality bialgebra
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\cdot,\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}},1,\varepsilon)
is a connected graded locality bialgebra.
Let

F_{0}:=\{1\},\quad F_{m}:=\big\{{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\,\big|s_{1}+\cdots+s_{k}=m\,\big\}.

Then we have the disjoint union

{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}}=\bigsqcup_{m=0}^{\infty}F_{m},

and

is a graded vector space:

Since the grading is given by the degrees of fractions,
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\cdot,1)
is a graded locality algebra.

To show that
(\mathbb{Q}{F^{\mathrm{Ch}}},\top,\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}},\varepsilon)
is a graded locality coalgebra under this grading,
fix some

. Then

and we have

Next we verify

(45)

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}(\mathbb{Q}F_{m}\cap U^{\top})\subseteq\bigoplus_{p+q=m}(\mathbb{Q}F_{p}\cap U^{\top})\otimes_{\top}(\mathbb{Q}F_{q}\cap U^{\top})

by induction on

.

\sum_{i}a_{i}h_{i}\in U^{\top}
with

, we have
h_{i}\in U^{\top}
. So we only need to prove Eq. (
45
) for basis elements, which take the form
h={\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\

Now the induction on
m\geq k
proceeds as follows.

For the initial step
m=k
, we have
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{k}=1
and
h={\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\

\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}1,\cdots,1\\

Assume that Eq. (
45

and consider
{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{i_{1}},\cdots,x_{i_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\in F_{m+1}\cap U^{\top}

s_{j}\geq 2
for some

∎

4.
The MZV shuffle Hopf algebra

Now we use a locality algebra homomorphism

4.1.
The passage from Chen fractions to the MZV shuffle algebra

The locality multiplication of Chen fractions as functions is closely related to the shuffle product of MZVs.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}=\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
be an alphabet. As we have already seen, the linear space
\mathbb{Q}W
with a basis
W=W_{\mathcal{A}}
of words in
{\mathcal{A}}
carries the shuffle product
X
. Let
W_{1}
be the subset of words of the form

with
s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}
distinct. Let
{\bf 1}
be the empty word. The set

has a natural locality relation
\top_{W_{1}}

(47)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(x_{0}^{s_{1}-1}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{0}^{s_{k}-1}x_{i_{k}})\top_{W_{1}}(x_{0}^{t_{1}-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{0}^{t_{\ell}-1}x_{j_{\ell}}),&\text{if }\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\}\cap\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{\ell}\}=\emptyset,\\
{\bf 1}\top_{W_{1}}w,&w\in W_{1}.\end{array}\right.

This locality relation extends to a locality relation
\top_{\mathbb{Q}W_{1}}
on
\mathbb{Q}W_{1}
. It is easy to see that the restriction of the shuffle product on
\mathbb{Q}W
equips
\mathbb{Q}W_{1}
with a locality commutative algebra structure. We further have

Proposition 4.1
.

x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}).

So

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\pi)(B\otimes{\partial}_{\ell_{i}})({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}})=

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\pi)\big(B({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})\otimes{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])\otimes\pi{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}}).

Let
p_{1}=\ell_{k+1}
,
\ldots
,
p_{m}=\ell_{m+k}
, that is
\ell_{i}=p_{i-k}
. Then

(\pi\otimes\pi)(B\otimes{\partial}_{\ell_{i}})({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}})\\
=A([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])\otimes\pi{\partial}_{p_{i-k}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{p_{1}},\cdots,x_{p_{m}}\end{array}\Big]}}).

By Lemma
4.6
and a change of variables,

\displaystyle(A\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i})(\pi\otimes\pi)({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}})=

\displaystyle A([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])\otimes\delta_{i-k}\pi({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])\otimes\delta_{i-k}\pi({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{p_{1}},\cdots,x_{p_{m}}\end{array}\Big]}}).

By Lemma
4.9
, we have

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\pi)(B\otimes{\partial}_{\ell_{i}})({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}})=(A\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i})(\pi\otimes\pi)({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}}\otimes{\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}\\
x_{\ell_{k+1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k+m}}\end{array}\Big]}}),

as needed.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

The coproduct
\Delta^{\prime}:{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\longrightarrow{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}\otimes{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}}
is a coderivation:

\Delta^{\prime}\delta_{i}=(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i}+\delta_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\prime}.

Proof.

It is obvious that
(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i}+\delta_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\prime}({\bf 1})=0=\Delta^{\prime}\delta_{i}({\bf 1})
. Next let
(s_{1},\cdots,s_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{k}
.

Case 1:
If
1\leq i\leq k
, then for distinct
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, by Lemma
4.10
and Eq.(
42
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}\delta_{i}([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}\Delta^{\prime}([s_{1},\cdots,s_{j}+1,\cdots,s_{k}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{i}s_{j}(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{j}+1,\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{j}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{i},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{i}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\pi)(\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}+{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{i},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{i}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i}+\delta_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{i},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{i}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i}+\delta_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\prime}([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}]).

Case 2:
If
i>k
, then
\Delta^{\prime}\delta_{i}([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])=0
. By Lemma
4.10
and Eq.(
42
), we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i}+\delta_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\prime}([s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}])=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\text{\textcircled{s}}\delta_{i}+\delta_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\pi)(\mathrm{id}\otimes{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}+{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta^{\mathrm{chen}}{\partial}_{\ell_{i}}({\scriptsize{\Big[\begin{array}[]{c}s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\\
x_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,x_{\ell_{k}}\end{array}\Big]}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we can complete the proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Proposition 4.12
.

The quintuple
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}},{\bf 1},{\Delta_{\geq 1}},\varepsilon_{\geq 1})
in Theorem
2.5
coincides with the quintuple
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}},{\bf 1},\Delta^{\prime},\varepsilon_{\geq 1})
in Theorem
4.8
and hence is a Hopf algebra.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.11
,
\Delta^{\prime}
satisfies the boundary conditions and the recursion that define
{\Delta_{\geq 1}}
in Definition
2.3
. Thus the two coproducts are the same. Thus statement of the proposition holds and
({{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}},\overline{{\mbox{\cyrs X}}},{\bf 1},{\Delta_{\geq 1}},\varepsilon_{\geq 1})
is a Hopf algebra.
∎

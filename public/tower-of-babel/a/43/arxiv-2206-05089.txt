Policy Gradient Reinforcement Learning for Uncertain Polytopic LPV Systems based on MHE-MPC
Hossein Nejatbakhsh Esfahani, Sébastien Gros

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a learning-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach for the polytopic Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) systems with inexact scheduling parameters (as exogenous signals with inexact bounds), where the Linear Time Invariant (LTI) models (vertices) captured by combinations of the scheduling parameters becomes wrong. We first propose to adopt a Moving Horizon Estimation (MHE) scheme to simultaneously estimate the convex combination vector and unmeasured states based on the observations and model matching error. To tackle the wrong LTI models used in both the MPC and MHE schemes, we then adopt a Policy Gradient (PG) Reinforcement Learning (RL) to learn both the estimator (MHE) and controller (MPC) so that the best closed-loop performance is achieved. The effectiveness of the proposed RL-based MHE/MPC design is demonstrated using an illustrative example.



keywords

Model Predictive Control, Moving Horizon Estimation, Reinforcement Learning, Polytopic LPV, Multi-Model Linear System

1
Introduction

Model predictive control (MPC) is an advanced control approach and widely used to many control problems due to its capability to handle both input and state constraints by making explicit use of the process model to predict the future evolution of the system
(
Rawlings et al. 2017
)
. However, in most applications some of the model parameters are uncertain at design time so that the prediction model may not be accurate enough to capture the true dynamics of a real system that this issue can affect the closed-loop performance
(
Cairano 2012
)
.

In many real applications of MPC, an accurate model of the (possibly nonlinear) real system may not be available so that the simplified linear models are commonly adopted as the prediction models, which are captured at a specific operation point of the real system. However, these identified models may not work for some uncertain systems where some parameters of the dynamics vary or not be known perfectly at different operation points. Therefore, the Linear Parameter-Varying (LPV) framework is usually adopted to address the issue above in which the linear models with parametric uncertainties (i.e, exogenous scheduling parameters) are represented by the LPV models
(
Leith and Leithead 2000
)
.

The use of polytopic LPV prediction models has been proven to be an effective approach to develop the MPC schemes for both the linear and nonlinear systems, e.g., see
(
Pour et al. 2017
;
Ding and Pan 2016
;
Pipino and Adam 2019
)
. In most of the MPC-based polytopic LPVs, a multi-model approach is used to represent a convex combination of the vertices as Linear Time Invariant (LTI) models in which the uncertainty (exogenous/endogenous scheduling parameter with known bounds) is associated to the convex combination vector. However, if the scheduling parameters are not known and constantly change, the robust MPC schemes, i.e, a tube MPC can be used
(
Langson et al. 2004
)
. As an alternative approach, as we consider in this paper, this combination vector is assumed to be constant or changing slowly, which can be estimated by a parameter estimation scheme
(
Di Cairano and Danielson 2021
;
Zhou et al. 2017
)
. As an interesting method to concurrently estimate this vector and some unmeasured states, a Moving Horizon Estimation (MHE) was adopted in
(
Pipino et al. 2020
)
. However, the LTI models used as vertices of the polytopic LPV may not be exact due to the inexact selection of the scheduling parameter bounds and then, these wrong models used in both the MHE and MPC schemes can decrease the closed-loop performance. As a solution to address this problem, a learning mechanism, i.e, a Reinforcement Learning (RL) can be leveraged to learn these schemes with the aim of increasing the closed-loop performance.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a powerful tool for solving Markov Decision Processes (MDP) problems
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
, which has been recently combined with the MPC and MHE schemes in order to improve their performance where the adopted prediction/estimation models may not capture the real system dynamics perfectly, e.g., see
(
Esfahani et al. 2021b
;
Kordabad et al. 2021
;
Cai et al. 2021
)
.

Motivated by this observation in the context of the MPC-based RL, in this paper, we propose to use an MHE scheme to deliver the convex combination vector needed in the MPC scheme and deal with the polytopic LPV systems with possibly inexact vertices (wrong LTI models) by adopting a Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) RL to adjust a certain number of the parameters in the parameterized MHE and MPC schemes. It is worth highlighting that the proposed
compatible deterministic policy gradient reinforcement learning based on a combined MHE-MPC scheme
is a central contribution in this paper. Although this learning-based (state-parameter) estimator/controller scheme is developed and employed in the context of the LPV systems in this paper, the proposed algorithm can be readily used to control many other applications, i.e, partially observable systems in which a combined estimator/observer-MPC is needed.

In what follows, the polytopic LPV model is introduced in Section
2
, and the parameterized MHE and MPC schemes are detailed in Section
3
. The MHE/MPC-based policy gradient reinforcement learning is then developed in Section
4
. Simulations on a mass-spring-damper case study are reported in Section
5
, and finally, conclusions and future work are given in Section
6
.

2
Uncertain Polytopic LPV

In this paper, we focus on the classic (pure) LPV systems, in which the uncertain dynamical model of the system can be described as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+1}=A\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}+B\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{k}

(1a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{y}}_{k}=C(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}

(1b)

where the scheduling parameters
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are considered as some exogenous signals applied to the real system. The vectors
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are states and control inputs, respectively. The system measurements is labeled by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{y}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{e}
. Hence, the system (
1
) can be represented by a polytopic approximation
\left[A\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right),B\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right),C\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right)\right]\in\Omega
, where a polytope
\Omega
is defined as a convex hull upon the Linear Time Invariant (LTI) models as follows:

\displaystyle\Omega=Co\left\{[A_{1},B_{1},C_{1}],[A_{2},B_{2},C_{2}],\cdots,[A_{\ell},B_{\ell},C_{\ell}]\right\}

(2)

where
\ell=2^{p}
is the number of vertices and
Co\{\cdot\}
reads a convex hull. Indeed, the LTI matrices are delivered by combinations of the scheduling parameters at their bounds
\underline{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}},\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}}
. We then evaluate the variable matrices in (
1
) as a convex combination of the LTI models (a.k.a vertices) as follows:

\displaystyle A\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}A_{i},\quad B\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}B_{i},

(3a)

\displaystyle C\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{k}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}C_{i}

(3b)

where
A_{i},B_{i},C_{i}
are known matrices of appropriate size and
\beta_{i},i=1\cdots\ell
is the unknown weight of each vertex (a.k.a convex combination vector) such that:

\displaystyle\chi=\left\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}:\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}=1,\quad 0\leq\beta_{i}\leq 1\right\}

(4)

Assumption 1

By
(
Di Cairano 2016
)
, the convex combination vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{k}
is assumed to be constant or changing slowly with respect to the dynamics and there exists an estimator to compute the estimate of this vector such that
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}\in\chi
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{0+}
.

Note that the stability and constraint satisfaction of the MPC schemes for the polytopic LPV systems under Assumption 1 was detailed and established in
(
Di Cairano and Danielson 2021
)
and by Lemma 2 and Theorem 1 in
(
Di Cairano 2016
)
. We then focus on this kind of the LPV system where its vertices may not be known and exact. As discussed, the vertices in a polytopic LPV may have a wrong model because of the inexact bounds of the scheduling parameters, some parametric uncertainties and the linearization approximation effects. Then, the wrong LTI models in the polytope
\Omega
can be introduced by the inexact vertices as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}=Co\left\{[\hat{A}_{1},\hat{B}_{1},\hat{C}_{1}],[\hat{A}_{2},\hat{B}_{2},\hat{C}_{2}],\cdots,[\hat{A}_{\ell},\hat{B}_{\ell},\hat{C}_{\ell}]\right\}

(5)

In the context of MPC schemes for the polytopic LPVs, under Assumption 1, one needs to evaluate an estimation of
\beta_{i},i=1\cdots\ell

(
Di Cairano and Danielson 2021
;
Pipino et al. 2020
)
. In this paper we propose to adopt an MHE scheme to deliver these unknown weights while some unmeasured states are estimated. However, the inexact vertices in
\hat{\Omega}
can affect parameter/state estimations, the policy captured from the MPC and the closed-loop performance, subsequently. In addition to this inexact polytope issue, the partially measurable states can be challenging since the MHE scheme as an estimator of
\beta_{i,k}
needs to capture all states as measurements and minimize a distance as a model matching error between these measurements and those states delivered by the uncertain LPV model.

To cope with these problems, we propose to parameterize both the MHE and MPC schemes and adopt a reinforcement learning to adjust them in order to achieve the best closed-loop performance even when using some wrong LTI models in a polytopic LPV system. More precisely, it is possible to generate the optimal policy based on a wrong model by learning the MHE and MPC schemes.

3
Adjustable MHE-MPC in a LPV Framework

Under Assumption 1, an obvious choice for the combination vectors used in the MPC scheme would be
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j|k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{k}
, for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{k,\ldots,k+N}
. As discussed in
(
Di Cairano 2016
)
, this choice could not be proper because if the entire
\beta
-parameter prediction vector suddenly changes, the MPC value function may not be decreasing. Hence, under Assumption 1, one can select a
N
-step delay in this parameter prediction as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j|k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k-N+j},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0,\ldots,N}

(6)

We then adopt an MHE-based estimator to deliver these weights needed in an MPC scheme. Let us formulate a parameterized MHE scheme as
\beta
-parameter and state estimator, where the vertices of the polytopic LPV are inexact and the real system states are not fully measurable.

\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k-N\ldots k},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k-N\ldots k}\right\}=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}\gamma^{N}\begin{Vmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k-N}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k-N}\end{Vmatrix}_{A_{\theta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=k-N+1}^{k}\gamma^{k-j}\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mu}}_{j}\right\|_{R_{\theta}}^{2}+\left\|\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j|k}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j+1}=\hat{A}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j}+\hat{B}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}_{j},

(7a)

\displaystyle\quad\hat{A}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}\hat{A}_{i},\quad\hat{B}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}\hat{B}_{i},

(7b)

\displaystyle\quad\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{y}}}_{j}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}\hat{C}_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mu}}_{j},

(7c)

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}=1,\quad 0\leq\beta_{j,i}\leq 1

(7d)

\displaystyle\quad\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j|k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j|k}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j|k-1},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j}=Col\left\{\beta_{j,1},\ldots,\beta_{j,\ell}\right\}

(7e)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{(0,1)}
is a discount factor and
\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{y}}}_{j},\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}_{j}
are the measurements available at the physical time
k
. We label
R_{\theta}
as an adjustable weight matrix penalizing the deviation between some estimated states captured from the LPV model and their corresponding measurements. The first term in the above least squares problem is an arrival cost weighted with matrix
A_{\theta}
, which aims at approximating the information prior to
k-N_{\text{MHE}}
, where
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}
is the available estimation
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k-N}
at time
k-1
. We then detail a policy gradient RL in order to adjust the combination vectors
\beta_{i}
, where the LTI models (vertices of the polytope) are wrong.

This paper proposes a learning-based estimation and control for a polytopic LPV system with possibly inexact LTI models at the vertices, where the control policy is delivered by a parameterized MPC scheme as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}\quad\gamma^{k+N}\left(T_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+N},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{k+N})+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}_{f}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{k+N}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=k}^{k+N-1}\mathcal{G}_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{j})+\sum_{j=k}^{k+N-1}\gamma^{j}\left(l_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{j})+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{j}\right)

(8a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j+1}=\hat{A}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j}+\hat{B}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{j},

(8b)

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}_{j|k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{j-N}

(8c)

\displaystyle\quad\hat{A}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}\hat{A}_{i},

(8d)

\displaystyle\quad\hat{B}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\rho}}_{j}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j,i}\hat{B}_{i},

(8e)

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{g}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{j})\leq 0,

(8f)

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{j})\leq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{j},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{h}}_{\theta}^{f}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+N})\leq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{k+N}

(8g)

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{k,\ldots,k+N}\geq 0

(8h)

where
T_{\theta}
and
l_{\theta}
are the parameterized terminal and stage costs, respectively. The function
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
is labeled the cost modification term, which can be, i.e, in a form of gradient
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}^{\top}[\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{j}]^{\top}
. However, the polytopic LPV model is uncertain with wrong vertices that could cause some violations in its constraints and bring the MPC in an infeasible region. To avoid such an infeasibility, we introduce some slack variables
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}
penalized by enough large weights
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}_{f}
. We also propose to have some adjustable parameters upon inequality constraints
h_{\theta},h^{f}_{\theta}
. Then, we let RL to tune the MPC parameters (a.k.a policy parameters). Note that, the proposed parameterization of the MHE and MPC is a general form of a rich parameterization and one may take into account some of these parameters to be adjusted by RL.

Note that the parameterized terminal cost in the MPC scheme (
8
) is defined as a function of the terminal states and the convex combination vector as follows:

\displaystyle T_{\theta}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+N}^{\top}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{k+N,i}P_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k+N}

(9)

where the matrices
P_{i}>0,i\in\mathbb{Z}_{1,\ldots,\ell}
are adjusted by RL. However, the initial values for these matrices can be computed offline via a Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI) based on a parameter-dependent Lyapunov function and a parameter-dependent linear control law detailed in
(
Di Cairano and Danielson 2021
)
.

4
MHE/MPC-based Policy Gradient RL

Let us define the closed-loop performance of a parameterized policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}
for a given stage cost
L\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}\right)
as the following total expected cost:

\displaystyle J\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{s}}\Bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}L\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}\right)\Bigg|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{k})\Bigg]

(10)

where the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\varrho_{s}}[\cdot]
is taken over the distribution of the Markov chain
\varrho_{s}
in the closed-loop under policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}
. We then seek the optimal policy parameters as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{\star}=\mathrm{arg}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}J\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}\right)

(11)

As a learning-based control approach in this paper, we propose to use a parameterized MPC scheme as a policy approximation in order to deliver
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}
. As an advantage to use a MPC-based policy approximation instead of DNNs, it can offer a learning-based controller so that all the state-input constraints are satisfied and the closed-loop performance is improved by adjusting the policy parameters.

For a given estimated state
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}
and parameter
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}
delivered by the MHE scheme, the policy captured by a parameterized MPC scheme is

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}^{\star}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}\right)

(12)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}^{\star}
is the first element of the control input sequence
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}^{\star}
delivered by (
8
).

In this section, we propose a policy gradient RL framework based on MPC and MHE to deal with the polytopic LPV systems with wrong LTI models.

4.1
Compatible Deterministic Actor-Critic

In the context of DPG-based RL algorithms, the policy parameters
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
can be directly optimized by the gradient descent steps such that the best expected closed-loop cost (a.k.a policy performance index
J
) can be captured by applying the policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
.

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}\leftarrow\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}-\alpha\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})

(13)

for some
\alpha>0
small enough as the step size. In the context of hybrid controller/observer scheme MHE-MPC, the input signal can be interpreted as a sequence of measurements
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{y}}=\left[y^{1}_{0,\cdots,k},\cdots,y^{n_{y}}_{0,\cdots,k}\right]^{\top}
at the physical time
k
, where
n_{y}
is the number of system states selected as measurable outputs. Then, the intermediate variables
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}
are delivered by the MHE scheme based on the history of the measurements and fed to the MPC scheme to deliver the control policy. Assuming that this history constitutes a Markov state, one can define a policy performance index by the following expected value:

\displaystyle J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}):=\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left[Q_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}\right)\right)\right]

(14)

where the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}
is taken over the distribution of the Markov chain resulting from the real system in closed-loop with
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
. The action-value function
Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
is then defined as follows:

\displaystyle{Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left({\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=L\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{y}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}}\right)+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left[{{V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left({\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k+1}}\right)|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}}\right]

(15)

where,
L\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{y}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}}\right)
reads as baseline cost (RL stage cost), which is a function of measurable states and actions at the current time
k
. Based on the proposed DPG theorem by
(
Silver et al. 2014
)
and the fact that both the
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
and
Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
are functions of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}
, the policy gradient equation is described as follows:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})=\mathbb{E}\left[{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}){\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}}{Q_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k})|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right]

(16)

To represent the effect of the parameterized MHE upon the policy gradient, the sensitivity of the policy w.r.t
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
can be updated such that the new policy gradient is described by the following expectation:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Xi{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}}{Q_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k})|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\Big]

(17)

where the Jacobian matrix
\Xi
is obtained by the following chain rule:

\displaystyle\Xi=\left(\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}\nabla_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}+\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\right)

(18)

In this paper, we adopt a
compatible deterministic actor-critic
algorithm
(
Silver et al. 2014
)
in which the action-value function
Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k})
can be replaced by a class of compatible function approximator
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k})
such that the policy gradient is preserved. Therefore, the compatible function for a deterministic policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
delivered by the parameterized MHE-MPC scheme can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}={{\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\right)}^{\top}}\Xi^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}+{V^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}\right)

(19)

The first term in the above compatible function as critic part is an estimation for the advantage function and the second term estimates a value function for the history of the measurements delivered as a summarized variable
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}
by the MHE scheme. Both functions can be computed by the linear function approximators as follows:

\displaystyle{V^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Upsilon}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}\right)^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}},

(20a)

\displaystyle{A^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Psi}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}\right)^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}

(20b)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Upsilon}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}\right)
is the summarized measurement feature vector in order to constitute all monomials of the history of the measurements with degrees less than or equal to
2
. The vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Psi}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}\right):=\Xi\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\theta}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k})}\right)
includes the state-action features. Considering (
19
), the policy gradient (
17
) is then rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})=\mathbb{E}\left[\Xi\Xi^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{w}}}\right]

(21)

In this paper, the parameterized policy in the context of policy gradient RL is proposed to be captured by the MPC scheme (
8
). To evaluate the policy gradient (
21
), one need to calculate some sensitivities upon the MPC and MHE schemes in order to compute the Jacobian matrix
\Xi
. Hence, the Jacobian matrices
{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
,
\nabla_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
and
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
can be computed by the sensitivity analysis for the parameterized MPC scheme while the gradients
{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}}_{k}
and
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\hat{\beta}}}_{k}
are obtained as a sensitivity term for the parameterized MHE scheme.

4.2
Sensitivity Analysis and LSTD-based DPG

4.2.1
Sensitivity Computation

We describe next how to compute the sensitivities (gradients) needed in the proposed policy gradient RL framework based on MHE-MPC. To that end, let us define the Lagrange functions
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta},\mathcal{L}_{\theta}
associated to the MHE and MPC schemes (
7
), (
8
) as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}\left(\hat{z}\right)=\hat{\Lambda}_{\theta}+\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\lambda}}}^{\top}\hat{G}_{\theta}

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\theta}\left(z\right)=\Lambda_{\theta}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\lambda}}^{\top}G_{\theta}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mu}}^{\top}H_{\theta}

(23)

where
\Lambda_{\theta}
and
\hat{\Lambda}_{\theta}
are the total parameterized costs of the MPC and MHE schemes, respectively. The inequality constraints of (
8
) are collected by
H_{\theta}
while
G_{\theta}
and
\hat{G}_{\theta}
gather, respectively, the equality constraints in the MPC and MHE schemes. We then label
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\lambda}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\lambda}}}
the Lagrange multipliers associated to the equality constraints
G_{\theta},\hat{G}_{\theta}
of the MPC and MHE, respectively. Variables
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\mu}}
are the Lagrange multipliers associated to the inequality constraints of the MPC scheme. Let us label
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Gamma}}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}\right\}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Gamma}}}=\left\{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\beta}}}\right\}

6
Conclusion

In this paper, an MHE-MPC scheme was proposed to deal with the linear systems with polytopic uncertainty in the context of the LPV systems with exogenous scheduling parameters. However, the performance of the adopted multi-model approach can be affected where the bounds of the scheduling parameters are different from their real values applied to the real system. To address a data-driven MHE-MPC approach for the polytopic LPV systems with inexact LTI models, we proposed to adopt a policy gradient reinforcement learning to capture the optimal policy and achieve the best closed-loop performance by adjusting the MHE and MPC schemes.

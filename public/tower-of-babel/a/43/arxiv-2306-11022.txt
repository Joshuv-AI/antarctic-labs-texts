1.
Introduction

Let
Q
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
f\in L^{\infty}(Q)
. This paper concerns the functional

(1.1)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}+f\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x

defined on
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
n\geq 2
. By a Hadamard-in-the-mean inequality, henceforth (HIM), we mean inequality of the form

(HIM)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi)\geq 0\quad\quad\forall\ \varphi\in W_{0}^{1,n}(Q,\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The fact that (HIM) holds for more general

is already indicated by the observation that
\int_{Q}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x=0
whenever
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,n}(Q,\mathbb{R}^{n})
because the map
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
:
F\mapsto\det F
is a so-called null Lagrangian; cf. e.g.
[
4
]
. Hence, if
f
is a constant function then (HIM) always holds.

The classical pointwise Hadamard inequality, which is easily proved through the use of the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, is

(1.2)

\displaystyle c_{n}|\det A|\leq|A|^{n},

where
c_{n}:=n^{\frac{n}{2}}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
|A|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}A^{2}_{ij}

c_{n}
is the largest number for which (

) holds.
Using this and the fact that
\int_{Q}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x=0
, we find that (HIM) holds for those essentially bounded and measurable functions

which obey

(1.3)

\displaystyle||f-\bar{f}_{Q}||_{\infty}\leq n^{\frac{n}{2}},

where
\bar{f}_{Q}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}f\,{\rm d}x
.
This condition is sharp and, moreover, it is necessary and sufficient for the sequential weak lower semicontinuity of the functional

when

is a two-state function, i.e. a function of the form

and

; see

then

is weakly lower semicontinuous on

We note that a “quartic version” of (HIM) for

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\gamma\det\nabla\varphi)\,{\rm d}x\geq 0\quad\quad\forall\ \varphi\in W^{1,4}_{0}(Q,\mathbb{R}^{2})\ ,

The authors of

2.
Preliminary remarks

At this point, we collect together some basic features of functionals of the form

(2.1)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi):=\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}+f\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x,\quad\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
f\in L^{\infty}(Q)
and
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
I_{n}
be given by (
2.1
). Then the following are true:

(i)

I_{n}(\lambda\varphi)=\lambda^{n}I_{n}(\varphi)
for any
\lambda>0
, and
I_{n}(\varphi)
is finite for every
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

If
\varphi^{*}
is a stationary point then
I_{n}(\varphi^{*})=0
.

(iii)

In general, the functional
I_{n}
is not invariant with respect to changes of domain
Q
. If
Q^{\prime}=F(Q)
, where
F
is conformal, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi;Q):=\int_{Q^{\prime}}|\nabla\Phi(y)|^{n}+f^{\prime}(y)\det\nabla\Phi(y)\,{\rm d}y,

where
\Phi:=\varphi\circ F^{-1}
and
f^{\prime}:=f\circ F^{-1}
.

(iv)

If
f
is constant then
I_{n}(\varphi)\geq 0
for every
\varphi\in W^{1,n}_{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Part (i) is clear.

To see (ii), suppose
\varphi^{*}\in W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a stationary point of the functional, so that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{Q}n|\nabla\varphi^{*}|^{n-2}\nabla\varphi^{*}\cdot\nabla\zeta+f\,{\rm cof}\,\nabla\varphi^{*}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,{\rm d}x=0,\quad\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(Q)
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, a straightforward approximation argument shows that the weak form (
2.3
) holds for
\zeta
in
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now take
\zeta=\varphi^{*}
and apply the identity
{\rm cof}\,\nabla\varphi^{*}\cdot\nabla\varphi^{*}=n\det\nabla\varphi^{*}
, so that (
2.3
) becomes
nI_{n}(\varphi^{*})=0
. Hence (ii).

For (iii), (
2.2
) follows by making the prescribed change of variables and bearing in mind that
\nabla F^{T}\nabla F=(\det\nabla F){\bf 1}
when
F
is a conformal map. It should now be clear that changing the domain changes
f
.

Finally, (iv) follows from the fact that
\int_{Q}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x=0
, cf.
[
4
]
, for instance.
∎

We remark that (i) or (ii) imply in particular that if the functional
I_{n}
attains a minimum in
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
then the value of the minimum is zero and (HIM) is automatic. Note, however, that the functional is not necessarily ‘coercive enough’ to guarantee that a minimum is attained. This is one of the reasons why (HIM) is interesting: we obtain results that are consistent with but do not depend upon the coercivity just mentioned.

We now derive a necessary condition on
f
for the functional
I_{n}
to be nonnegative. Let
O(n)
be the set of rotations and reflections in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Let
\varphi\in W^{1,n}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy
\nabla\varphi\in O(n)
almost everywhere in
\Omega\subset Q
where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Lipschitz domain. The existence of such
\varphi
follows from
[
5
, p. 199 and Rem.2.4]
.
Let
\Omega^{\pm}=\{x\in\Omega:\det\nabla\varphi(x)=\pm 1\}
and observe that, since
\det\nabla\varphi
is a null Lagrangian, it must hold that
\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega^{-})=\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega^{+})=\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)/2
.
Hence

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=n^{n/2}\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)+\int_{\Omega^{+}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega^{-}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x

rearranges to

(2.4)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi)=\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}{2}\left(2n^{n/2}+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{+}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{-}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x\right),

which thereby forms a necessary condition for
I_{n}\geq 0
. Here,
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int
denotes the mean value of the integrand on the respective set. Adapting this idea leads to the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
I_{n}
be given by (
2.1
) and assume that
I_{n}\geq 0
on
W^{1,n}_{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
\Omega\subset Q
be a Lipschitz domain and let
\varphi\in W^{1,n}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy
\nabla\varphi\in O(n)
almost everywhere in

.
Then
it is necessary that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{+}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{-}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2n^{n/2}.

Proof.

The argument above shows that

(2.6)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi)=\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}{2}\left(2n^{n/2}-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{+}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{-}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x\right).

Replacing

by
\phi:=(-\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots)
, we find that the roles of

and
\Omega^{-}
are exchanged, i.e.

which leads directly to

\displaystyle I_{n}(\phi)=\frac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega)}{2}\left(2n^{n/2}-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{-}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Omega^{+}}f(x)\,{\rm d}x\right).

∎

3.
Two-state
f

In this section, we assume that
f
takes only two values in
Q:=(-1,1)^{n}
, i.e., that
f=M\chi_{\Omega}
, where
M\in\mathbb{R}
,
\Omega
is a strict subset of
Q
that is open, and
\chi_{\Omega}
is the characteristic function of
\Omega
in
Q
. Our aim is to show that, under a relatively mild regularity assumption on
\partial\Omega
, the functional

(3.1)

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi):=\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}+M\chi_{\Omega}\det\nabla\varphi\,dx

is nonnegative on
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q,\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
|M|\leq 2c_{n}
, where
c_{n}
is the constant appearing in the pointwise Hadamard inequality (
1.2
).
In fact, to show that
I_{n}\geq 0
on
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q,\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
|M|\leq 2c_{n}
and
\Omega
is measurable is very easy because

\displaystyle I_{n}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=I_{n}(\varphi)-\underbrace{\frac{M}{2}\int_{Q}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x}_{=0}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}+\frac{M}{2}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x+\int_{Q\setminus\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}-\frac{M}{2}\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x

and both terms on the right-hand side are nonnegative in view of the pointwise Hadamard inequality.
The regularity assumption enables us, via a blow-up argument, to prove that the sequential weak lower semicontinuity of
I_{n}
is equivalent to that of the special functional

(3.3)

\displaystyle I_{n}^{+}(\varphi):=\int_{Q}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}+M\chi_{Q^{+}}\det\nabla\varphi\,dx,

which, in turn, holds if and only if
I_{n}^{+}\geq 0
on
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e. if and only if a particular form of (HIM) holds. Here,
Q^{+}:=\{x\in Q:x_{n}\geq 0\},
and it is worth emphasising that the prefactor
M
is exactly the same in both (
3.1
) and (
3.3
). Thus, by these steps, the nonnegativity of
I_{n}
on
W_{0}^{1,n}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is equivalent to the nonnegativity of
I_{n}^{+}
on the same space. We shall see in Proposition
3.2
below that in fact

I_{n}^{+}(\varphi)\geq 0\quad\forall\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\iff|M|\leq 2c_{n},

a result whose proof serves to connect our work with the literature on quasiconvexity at the boundary
[
2
]
.

It is convenient to divide the present section into two: Subsection
3.1
deals exclusively with the nonnegativity of the special functional
I_{n}^{+}
, while Subsection
3.2
focuses on
I_{n}
.

3.1.
The nonnegativity of

The following result was proved by Mielke and Sprenger in

extends this result to any

. If
\Gamma

\displaystyle W^{1,n}_{\Gamma}(Q^{+},\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{\phi\in W^{1,n}(Q^{+};\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\phi=0\,\text{ on }\partial Q^{+}\setminus\Gamma\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

and let the functional

be given by

\displaystyle I_{n}^{++}(\varphi):=\int_{Q^{+}}|\nabla\varphi|^{n}+c\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x.

Then

Proof.

That the stated condition is sufficient for the nonnegativity of

. Then we suppose that

, and find

such that

Let

. Let

be the

canonical basis vector, let

and set

. Define for

the function

\varphi^{0}(x)=A\frac{x-a}{|x-a|^{2}},

where

is a fixed

matrix to be chosen shortly. Then

\displaystyle\nabla\varphi^{0}(x)=\frac{A}{|x-a|^{2}}({\bf 1}-2\nu(x)\otimes\nu(x))

provided

. The matrix

\displaystyle-c_{n}\det\nabla\varphi^{0}=|\nabla\varphi^{0}|^{n},

and we see that the integrand of

\displaystyle F(\nabla\varphi^{0})

\displaystyle=|\nabla\varphi^{0}|^{n}+c\det\nabla\varphi^{0}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{c}{c_{n}}-1\right)|\nabla\varphi^{0}|^{n},

which, other than the fact that

The purpose of this section is to demonstrate concretely that, in the planar case

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(A_{k}^{2}+B_{k}^{2})\left(\frac{1-\cos(kz)}{z^{2}}\right).

\displaystyle\int_{L}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+c\det\nabla\varphi\,\,{\rm d}x<-\frac{(c-c^{\prime})}{c^{\prime}}\int_{L}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x.

We now extend
\varphi
to a map
\Phi
in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
by setting

\Phi(x_{1},x_{2}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})&\textrm{if}\ x\in L\\[5.69054pt]
\varphi(0,x_{2})&\textrm{if}\ x\in[0,\delta]\times[0,1]\\[5.69054pt]
\varphi(\delta-x_{1},x_{2})&\textrm{if}\ x\in R.\end{array}\right.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}+f(x)\det\nabla\Phi\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=2\int_{L}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+c\det\nabla\varphi\,{\rm d}x+\delta\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{2}\varphi(0,x_{2})|^{2}\,dx_{2}

\displaystyle<-\frac{2(c-c^{\prime})}{c^{\prime}}\int_{L}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+\delta\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{2}\varphi(0,x_{2})|^{2}\,dx_{2},

the right-hand side of which can be made negative by taking
\delta
, which in this setting is the width of the insulation region
\{f=0\}
, sufficiently small. We conclude that for any

of the form (

) with

, the insulation region cannot be made arbitrarily small.
∎

Let

(a)

Proof.

(a)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle I\left(\varphi,\scalebox{0.7}{{\begin{tabular}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr$0$&$-4$\\
\hline\cr$4$&$0$\\
\hline\cr\end{tabular}}}\,\right)

\displaystyle=2\int_{Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}}|\nabla f|^{2}+|\nabla g|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-4\int_{Q_{1}}\det\nabla f+\det\nabla g+{\rm cof}\,\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+4\int_{Q_{3}}\det\nabla f+\det\nabla g+{\rm cof}\,\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\,{\rm d}x

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
Q_{1}
be the unit square in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
N
be a large natural number, and let
A
,
B
be harmonic functions on
Q_{1}
obeying the following boundary conditions

\begin{array}[]{l l l}A(1,x_{2})=0&\quad B(1,x_{2})=0&\quad 0\leq x_{2}\leq 1\\
A(x_{1},1)=0&\quad B(x_{1},1)=0&\quad 0\leq x_{1}\leq 1\\
A(0,x_{2})=0&\quad B(0,x_{2})=g_{0}(0,x_{2})&\quad 0\leq x_{2}\leq 1\\
A(x_{1},0)=g_{0}(x_{1},0)&\quad B(x_{1},0)=0&\quad 0\leq x_{1}\leq 1,\end{array}

where the function
g_{0}
is such that

\displaystyle g_{0}(x_{1},0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}-\frac{1}{n}\sin(n\pi x_{1})\quad\quad 0\leq x_{1}\leq 1

\displaystyle g_{0}(0,x_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\sin(n\pi x_{2})\quad\quad 0\leq x_{2}\leq 1.

Let

\displaystyle g(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}(B(x)+A(x))e_{1}&\textrm{if}\ x\in Q_{1}\\
(B(Rx)-A(Rx))e_{1}&\textrm{if}\ x\in Q_{2},\end{array}\right.

where
R(x_{1},x_{2})=(-x_{1},x_{2})
.
Then, as
N\to\infty
,

(4.46)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(g;Q_{1})

\displaystyle=2\pi\ln N+O(1)

(4.47)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(g;Q_{2})

\displaystyle=O(1).

Proof.

By a direct calculation, one finds that

(4.48)

\displaystyle A(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n\pi,n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{k}\sin(kx_{1})\sinh(k(x_{2}-1)),

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\int_{0}^{1}g_{0}(s,0)\sin(ks)\,ds}{\sinh(-k)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}\frac{1}{n\sinh(n\pi)}&\textrm{if}\ 1\leq n\leq N\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Hence,

\displaystyle A(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\sin(n\pi x_{1})\sinh(n\pi(x_{2}-1))}{n\sinh(n\pi)}.

Similarly,

(4.49)

\displaystyle B(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{-\sin(n\pi x_{2})\sinh(n\pi(x_{1}-1))}{n\sinh(n\pi)}.

(Note that
B(x_{1},x_{2})=-A(x_{2},x_{1})
.) Using these expressions together with the fact that
A
and
B
are harmonic, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1}}\nabla A\cdot\nabla B\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}\frac{-nm}{n^{2}+m^{2}}\alpha_{n\pi}\beta_{m\pi}\sinh(m\pi)\sinh(n\pi),

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{2}+m^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\pi n\coth(\pi n)-1}{2n^{2}}+O(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\pi\coth(\pi n)}{2n}+O(1)

(4.50)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\ln N}{2}+O(1)\quad\textrm{as}\ N\to\infty.

In the course of the above, the fact that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{m^{2}+n^{2}}=\frac{\pi n\coth(\pi n)-1}{2n^{2}}+O(1/N),

which can be deduced by integrating the function
h(z):=\frac{\cot(\pi z)}{z^{2}+n^{2}}
around a rectangular contour with corners at
\pm(N+\frac{1}{2})\pm 2Ni
, has been used.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(B;Q_{1})=\mathbb{D}(A;Q_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{n\pi\,\alpha_{n\pi}^{2}\sinh(2n\pi)}{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\pi\frac{n\sinh(2n\pi)}{4n^{2}\sinh^{2}(n\pi)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\pi\coth(\pi n)}{2n}

(4.51)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\ln N}{2}+O(1)\quad\textrm{as}\ N\to\infty.

To conclude the proof, we see from the fact that
g=A+B
in
Q_{1}
, (
4.50
) and (
4.51
) that, as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(g;Q_{1})=2\pi\ln N+O(1)

On
Q_{2}
,
g(x)=B(Rx)-A(Rx)
, and hence

(4.52)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(g;Q_{2})=2\left(\mathbb{D}(A;Q_{1})-\int_{Q_{2}}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\zeta\,{\rm d}x\right),

where
\eta(x):=B(Rx)
and
\zeta(x):=A(Rx)
. We calculate that

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{2}}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla\zeta\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{2}+m^{2}},

which, by the argument leading to (
4.50
), goes as
\frac{\pi\ln N}{2}+O(1)
as

Inserting this and (
4.51
) into (
4.52
) leads to the conclusion that
\mathbb{D}(g;Q_{2})=O(1)
as

.
∎

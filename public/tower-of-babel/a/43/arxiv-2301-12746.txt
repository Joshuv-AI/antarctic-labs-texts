Jakub Koncki, Andrzej Weber

Abstract
. Let G G be a linear semisimple algebraic group and B B its Borel subgroup. Let 𝕋 ⊂ B \mathbb{T}\subset B be the maximal torus. We study the inductive construction of Bott-Samelson varieties to obtain recursive formulas for the twisted motivic Chern classes of Schubert cells in G / B G/B . To this end we introduce two families of operators acting on the equivariant K-theory K 𝕋 ​ ( G / B ) ​ [ y ] K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y] , the right and left Demazure-Lusztig operators depending on a parameter. The twisted motivic Chern classes coincide (up to normalization) with the K-theoretic stable envelopes. Our results imply wall-crossing formulas for a change of weight chamber and slope parameters. The right and left operators generate a twisted double Hecke algebra. We show that in the type A A this algebra acts on the Laurent polynomials. This action is a natural lift of the action on K 𝕋 ​ ( G / B ) ​ [ y ] K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y] with respect to the Kirwan map. We show that the left and right twisted Demazure-Lusztig operators provide a recursion for twisted motivic Chern classes of matrix Schubert varieties.



1.
Introduction

Schubert varieties and their cohomological invariants are important objects of enumerative geometry.
These varieties are usually singular, yet they admit a well-studied resolution of singularities called Bott-Samelson resolution.
In many cases the inductive construction of Bott-Samelson varieties gives rise to recursive formulas for various cohomological invariants. These formulas allow to compute the class of a Schubert cell from classes of smaller cells.

The study of inductive properties of various characteristic classes of Schubert varieties is widely presented in the literature. Starting from formulas for fundamental classes in cohomology
[

]
or in K-theory
[

,

]

The motivic Chern class

In this paper we consider only the K-theoretic stable envelopes. They depend on a fractional line bundle called slope.

Best known

One of the important notions in the theory of K-theoretic stable envelopes are R-matrices (also called wall-crossing formulas).
They describe the behavior of the stable envelope for elementary modifications of parameters (weight chamber and slope).
They were studied in e.g.
[

In

Our recursive formulas allow for an alternative, geometric approach to the wall-crossing problem based on the study of Bott-Samelson resolution.
The right recursion leads to the wall-crossing formula related to the change of slope (theorem

), which is equivalent to the results of
[

]

Recursive formulas in the K-theory surprisingly rely on simple algebraic operators involving characters and the classes of line bundles. It turns out that one can build an algebra generated by natural lifts of operators
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,\lambda}}
and
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,\lambda}}
, denoted by
{\mathscr{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,\lambda}
and
{\mathscr{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,\lambda}
, or their variants depending on a scalar
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{s,a}}
and
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,a}
. That algebra acts on the representation ring
R(\mathbb{T}^{2})
extended by the formal variable
y
. Practically this means, that to check identities in this algebra it is enough to perform calculus involving Laurent polynomials in two sets of variables and
y
. This algebra formally resembles the construction of Hecke algebra of Ginzburg-Kapranov-Vasserot, but there is a difference: the algebra of
[
GKV
]
acts on
R(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{\vee})
, where
\mathbb{T}^{\vee}
is the dual torus. On the other hand we have a dependence on
\lambda
, which means that our operators can be treated as functions on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
.
It is remarkable that the braid relations hold for the lifted operators. Braid relations can be deduced from the properties of the motivic Chern classes or simply checked by hand performing elementary transformation of rational functions. Our twisted Hecke algebra is presented in full generality in the last section. Before, starting from section
10
we analyze the
A_{n}
case. The braid relations have the form (satisfied by left and right operators)

{\mathscr{T}}_{i,s_{i+1}s_{i}\lambda}\circ{\mathscr{T}}_{i+1,s_{i}\lambda}\circ{\mathscr{T}}_{i,\lambda}={\mathscr{T}}_{i+1,s_{i}s_{i+1}\lambda}\circ{\mathscr{T}}_{i,s_{i+1}\lambda}\circ{\mathscr{T}}_{i+1,\lambda}

or

{\mathfrak{T}}_{i,a}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i+1,a+b}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i,b}={\mathfrak{T}}_{i+1,b}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i,a+b}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i+1,a}\,,

see lemma
11.2
and propositions
11.6
,
11.8

The quadratic relations hold:
if
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle\not\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\not\in\mathbb{Z}

{\mathscr{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,s_{i}\lambda}\circ{\mathscr{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,\lambda}=-y\,{\rm id}\,,

{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,-a}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,a}=-y\,{\rm id}\,.

For integer values of the parameter

{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,1-a}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,a}=-y\,{\rm id}\,,

see lemma

Dependence on the parameter is not a surprise. Felder in
[

]
, see also
[

, stated braid relations in their incarnation of Yang-Baxter equation for R-matrices

R^{(12)}(z)R^{(13)}(z+w)R^{(23)}(w)=R^{(23)}(w)R^{(13)}(z+w)R^{(12)}(z)\,.

Notations

All considered varieties are complex and quasi-projective. We consider an algebraic torus

Suppose that

(treated as a subsheaf of meromorphic functions) has the natural linearization such that

•

•

is the normal weight to

Suppose now that

2.3.
Equivariant K-theory

For a quasiprojective
\mathbb{T}
-variety

and the equivariant K-theory of vector bundles

K_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\simeq G^{\mathbb{T}}(X)\,,

The equivariant K-theory of a point is isomorphic to the ring of Laurent polynomials

K_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})]\simeq\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm},\dots,t_{\operatorname{rk}\mathbb{T}}^{\pm}]\,.

[

implies that the restriction map induces an isomorphism

S^{-1}K_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\simeq S^{-1}K_{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}})\,.

Suppose that a
\mathbb{T}
-variety

is smooth and projective. Suppose moreover that the fixed point set

is finite. Then the equivariant K-theory of

is a free

-module
1

1

and

For an equivariant complex vector bundle

\mathbb{T}
-variety

The Lefschetz-Riemann-Roch formula (LRR for short) allows to compute push-forwards using restriction to the fixed point set. Let us remind it here.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
Tho
, theorem 3.5]
and
[

, theorem 5.11.7]
)

Let

be smooth
\mathbb{T}
-varieties. Let

be an equivariant proper morphism. Consider a component of the fixed point set

For

\frac{(p_{*}\alpha)_{|F}}{eu(\nu(F\subset Y))}=\sum_{F^{\prime}\in I}(p_{F^{\prime}})_{*}\frac{\alpha_{|F^{\prime}}}{eu(\nu(F^{\prime}\subset X))}\in S^{-1}K_{\mathbb{T}}(F)\,,

where

is the normal bundle and

We will apply LRR theorem for Bott-Samelson resolution, where the fixed point sets are finite, hence the pushforward maps

for all simple roots
\alpha
.

3.2.
Weyl group actions

The Weyl group acts on the maximal torus
\mathbb{T}
by conjugation. This induces a left Weyl group action on the characters
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})
. The action extends naturally to an action on fractional characters and the equivariant K-theory of a point

K_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})]\,.

For a generalized flag variety
G/B
we have two actions (left and right) of the Weyl group on the equivariant K-theory
K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)
.
The left action of
W=N\mathbb{T}/\mathbb{T}
is well defined on
(G/B)^{\mathbb{T}}
. It does not preserve the torus action. It is invariant when twisted by the torus automorphism given by
W
action. On the other hand the right action is torus equivariant, but it is not holomorphic. It is obtained by the identification

G/B\simeq K/T
, where
K\subset G
is a maximal compact group and
T\subset K
is a maximal compact torus. We can assume that
T\subset\mathbb{T}
. The right action is the natural action of
W=NT/T
.
See
[
MNS
, section 3.1]
or
[
Kn
]
for a definition and detailed discussion of these actions. For an element
w\in W
we denote the left action of
w
by
w^{\textsc{l}}
and the right one by
w^{\textsc{r}}
. On restrictions to the fixed point set the actions are given by the following formulas.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
w,\sigma\in W
and
x\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)
we have

\displaystyle w^{\textsc{r}}(x)_{|\sigma}

\displaystyle=x_{|\sigma w}\,,

\displaystyle w^{\textsc{l}}(x)_{|\sigma}

\displaystyle=w(x_{|w^{-1}\sigma})\,.

Remark 3.2
.

The action of
W
on
K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)
via
w^{\textsc{r}}
satisfies
(uw)^{\textsc{r}}=u^{\textsc{r}}w^{\textsc{r}}
i.e. is an action from the left, despite its name.

3.3.
Reflections

The Weyl group can be identified with the group generated by the reflections in the simple roots of the Lie algebra. We record the following proposition following directly from the properties of root systems (cf.
[
Hump
, proposition in section 1.2]
).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be roots and
s_{\alpha},s_{\beta}
the corresponding reflections. Let
w\in W
be a Weyl group
element. Then

s_{\beta}=ws_{\alpha}w^{-1}\iff\beta=\pm w\alpha\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\,.

We will need further properties of the Weyl group.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
s\in W
be any reflection. There exists
w\in W
such that
wsw^{-1}
is a simple reflection.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be the positive root corresponding to
s
. By proposition
3.3
it is enough to show that there exists
w\in W
such that
w\alpha
is a simple root. This is a standard fact, see e.g.
[
CarLieAlg
, proposition 5.12]
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\in W
are reflections such that
q_{3}=q_{1}q_{2}q_{1}
,
q_{1}
is simple and
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
. Let
w\in W
be any Weyl group element. Then

l(wq_{1})<l(wq_{1}q_{2})\iff l(w)<l(wq_{3})\,.

Proof.

First let us note that
l(w)<l(wq_{i})
if and only if the weight of the tangent space of the one dimensional
\mathbb{T}
-orbit joining
w
with
wq_{i}
at
w
is a negative root.
Let
\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
be positive roots corresponding to reflections
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
, respectively. The reflection
q_{1}
is simple and
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
, therefore
q_{1}\alpha_{2}
is also a positive root (see e.g.
[
CarLieAlg
, lemma 5.9]
). Thus
q_{1}\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}
(cf. proposition
3.3
).
The tangent weight at
w
to the one-dimensional
\mathbb{T}
-orbit connecting
wq_{3}
and
w
is equal to
-w\alpha_{3}
. The tangent weight at
wq_{1}
to the one-dimensional
\mathbb{T}
-orbit connecting
wq_{1}q_{2}
and
wq_{1}
is equal to
-wq_{1}\alpha_{2}
. These two weights are equal which proves the lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
s
be a simple reflection,
\alpha_{s}
the corresponding simple root and
w\in W
a Weyl group element.

•

Suppose that
l(w)<l(ws)
then
w\alpha_{s}
is a positive root.

•

Suppose that
l(w)<l(sw)
then
w^{-1}\alpha_{s}
is a positive root.

Proof.

The first part follows from the fact that
-w\alpha_{s}
is the tangent weight at
w
of the orbit connecting
w
and
ws
. The second part follows from the first since
l(w)<l(sw)
if and only if
l(w^{-1})<l(w^{-1}s)
.
∎

4.
Bott-Samelson resolution

The construction of Demazure-Lusztig operators are based on the geometry of Bott-Samelson resolution. We briefly summarize what we need below.

4.1.
Inductive construction

Let
{\underline{w}}

be a reduced word decomposition of
w\in W
. We denote by

p_{\underline{w}}:Z_{\underline{w}}\to X_{w}\subset G/B

the corresponding Bott–Samelson resolution of singularities (see e.g.
[
BK
, section 2.2]
,

[
AMSS
, section 2]
or
[
RW
, section 3]
).
Let

\partial Z_{\underline{w}}=p_{{\underline{w}}}^{-1}(\partial X_{w}),\qquad Z^{\circ}_{\underline{w}}=Z_{\underline{w}}\setminus\partial Z_{\underline{w}}\,.

It is a standard fact that

p_{{\underline{w}}}:(Z_{\underline{w}},\partial Z_{\underline{w}})\to(X_{w},\partial X_{w})\,.

is a simple normal crossing resolution of singularities.
The boundary
\partial Z_{\underline{w}}
consists of
l(w)
components corresponding to omitting a single letter in
{\underline{w}}
. Denote these components by
\partial_{j}Z_{\underline{w}}
for
j\in\{1,2,\dots,l(w)\}
.

The Bott–Samelson resolution may be constructed inductively. Consider a simple reflection
s
. Suppose that
l(ws)>l(w)
. Then we have a pullback diagram

\textstyle{Z_{\underline{ws}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{\underline{ws}}}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{\underline{ws}}}
\textstyle{X_{ws}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{ws}}
\textstyle{G/B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{s}}
\textstyle{Z_{\underline{w}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{\underline{w}}}
\textstyle{X_{w}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{w}}
\textstyle{G/B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{s}}
\textstyle{G/P_{s}}

The map
\pi_{\underline{ws}}:Z_{\underline{ws}}\to Z_{\underline{w}}
has a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant section
i:Z_{\underline{w}}\to Z_{\underline{ws}}
. We have an equality

\partial Z_{\underline{ws}}=\pi_{\underline{ws}}^{-1}(\partial Z_{\underline{w}})\cup i(Z_{\underline{w}})\,.

One may use the above construction to inductively describe the fixed point set
Z_{\underline{w}}^{\mathbb{T}}
. It can be identified with the set of binary sequences of length
l(w)
. For the empty word identification is obvious, the variety
Z_{\varnothing}
is a single point. Suppose that
l(ws)>l(w)
.
Let
{\varepsilon}
be a binary sequence of length
l(w)
. By the inductive assumption it corresponds to a fixed point in
Z_{\underline{w}}^{\mathbb{T}}
. The preimage
\pi^{-1}_{{\underline{ws}}}({\varepsilon})
is a projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
equipped with a nontrivial action of
\mathbb{T}
. The set
\pi^{-1}_{{\underline{ws}}}({\varepsilon})^{\mathbb{T}}
consists of two points. Identify these fixed points with
({\varepsilon},0)
and
({\varepsilon},1)
. We assume that
({\varepsilon},0)=i({\varepsilon})
is the point that lies in the image of section
i
. For a detailed discussion of the fixed point set and an example see
[
RW
, section 3.2]
.

4.2.
Pull-backs of line bundles

Consider a fractional character
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. We are interested in the pullback
p^{*}_{\underline{w}}\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
of the corresponding line bundle to the Bott–Samelson variety. Let us recall several classical results.

Proposition 4.1

(Kempf lemma
[
Kempf
, section 2, lemma 3]
)
.

Let
{\underline{w}}
be a reduced word decomposition of
w\in W
. Suppose that
s
is the reflection corresponding to a simple root
\alpha_{s}
. Assume that
l(ws)>l(w)
. Then, we have an isomorphism

p^{*}_{\underline{ws}}\mathcal{L}(s\lambda)\simeq\pi_{\underline{ws}}^{*}p_{\underline{w}}^{*}\mathcal{L}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z_{{\underline{ws}}}}(-\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{s}\rangle\cdot i(Z_{\underline{w}}))\in\operatorname{Pic}(Z_{\underline{ws}})\,.

Corollary 4.2
.

Kempf lemma holds equivariantly. Consider the natural
\mathbb{T}
-linearization of bundle
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
(i.e. such that the weight at
{\rm id}\in G/B
is equal to
-\lambda
) and the natural linearization of
\mathcal{O}_{Z_{{\underline{ws}}}}(i(Z_{\underline{w}}))
. We have an isomorphism

p^{*}_{\underline{ws}}\mathcal{L}(s\lambda)\simeq\pi_{\underline{ws}}^{*}p_{\underline{w}}^{*}\mathcal{L}(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Z_{{\underline{ws}}}}(-\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}_{s}\rangle\cdot i(Z_{\underline{w}}))\in\operatorname{Pic}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z_{\underline{ws}})\,.

Proof.

We only need to prove that
\mathbb{T}
-linearizations of both sides agree. Due to
[
Brion
, proposition 2.10]

it is enough to check that the weights at some chosen fixed point agree. On the maximal fixed point
p^{-1}_{\underline{ws}}(ws)
both bundles have weight
-w\lambda.

∎

Proposition 4.3

(Chevalley formula
[
Demazure
, Paragraph 4.3]
, see also
[
KRW
, proposition 4.1]
for this formulation)
.

Let
{\underline{w}}=s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\dots,s_{i_{l(w)}}
be a reduced word decomposition of
w\in W
. Let
\alpha_{j}
be the simple root corresponding to the reflection
s_{i_{j}}
. Consider elements

\gamma_{j}=s_{i_{l(w)}}s_{i_{l(w)-1}}\dots s_{i_{j+1}}\cdot\alpha_{j}\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\,.

There is an isomorphism

p^{*}_{\underline{w}}\mathcal{L}(\lambda)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z_{{\underline{w}}}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{l(w)}\langle\lambda,\gamma_{j}^{\vee}\rangle\cdot\partial_{j}Z_{\underline{w}}\Big)\in\operatorname{Pic}(Z_{\underline{w}})\,.

Proposition 4.4
.

The coefficients
\langle\lambda,\gamma_{j}^{\vee}\rangle
from Chevalley formula belong to the set

\{\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle|\alpha\text{ is a root, }l(w)>l(ws_{\alpha})\}\,,

where
s_{\alpha}
is the reflection corresponding to the root
\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
s_{\gamma_{j}}
be the reflection corresponding to the root
{\gamma_{j}}
. We need to prove that

l(w)>l(ws_{\gamma_{j}})\,.

Let
w_{>j}=s_{i_{j+1}}s_{i_{j+2}}\dots s_{i_{l(w)}}\in W
. We have
\gamma_{j}=w_{>j}^{-1}\cdot\alpha_{j}
. Proposition
3.3
implies that

s_{\gamma_{j}}=w^{-1}_{>j}s_{i_{j}}w_{>j}\in W\,.

It follows that
ws_{\gamma_{j}}
is represented by the word
{\underline{w}}
with
s_{i_{j}}
omitted. Therefore
l(w)>l(ws_{\gamma_{j}})\,.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

In proposition
4.4
we do not assume that roots are positive. Of course the set of coefficients may be much smaller, e.g. all appearing coefficients are nonnegative if
\lambda
is dominant.

5.
Twisted motivic Chern class

The K-theoretic characteristic classes of singular varieties are our main protagonists. Below we recall the twisted version of motivic Chern classes.

5.1.
Motivic Chern class

The motivic Chern class was defined in
[
BSY
]
(see also
[
SYp
]
for a survey). Its equivariant version is due to
[
AMSS
,
FRW
]
. Here we remind only the definition of
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant motivic Chern class. Consult
[
AMSS
,
FRW
]
for a detailed account.

Definition 5.1

(after
[
FRW
, section 2.3]
)
.

The motivic Chern class assigns to every
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant map of quasi-projective
\mathbb{T}
-varieties
f:Z\to Y
an element

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(f)=\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y)\in G^{\mathbb{T}}(Y)[y]

such that the following properties are satisfied

1. Additivity:

Let
Z
be a
\mathbb{T}
-variety and
U\subset Z
an invariant open subvariety. Then

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y)=\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{|U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y)+\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z\setminus U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{|Z\setminus U}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y)\,.

2. Functoriality:

For an equivariant proper map
g:Y\to Y^{\prime}
we have

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g\circ f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y^{\prime})=g_{*}\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y)\,.

3. Normalization:

For a smooth
\mathbb{T}
-variety
X
we have

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id}_{X})=\lambda_{y}(T^{*}X)\,.

The equivariant motivic Chern class is the unique assignment satisfying the above properties. For a smooth
\mathbb{T}
-variety
X
we may consider the class
\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z\to X)
as an element of
K_{\mathbb{T}}(X)[y]
.

The above definition is meaningful also in the non-equivariant setting, i.e. for
\mathbb{T}
equal to the trivial group (cf.
[
BSY
]
). See
[
AMSS
, theorem 4.2]
for an equivalent definition in terms of a natural transformation of functors.

5.2.
Twisted class

The twisted motivic Chern class was defined in
[
KonW
]
. We repeat its definition here.
Let
(Y,\partial Y)
be a pair, consisting of an algebraic quasiprojective
\mathbb{T}
-variety
Y
and an invariant closed subvariety
\partial Y\subset Y
. We assume that the complement
Y^{\circ}=Y\setminus\partial Y
is smooth.
Let
\Delta
be an invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
Y
with support contained in the boundary
|\Delta|\subset\partial Y
.

Definition 5.2

(
[
KonW
, definition 2.2]
)
.

The twisted motivic Chern class of a triple
(Y,\partial Y;\Delta)
as above is defined by the formula

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Y,\partial Y;\Delta)=f_{*}\Big(\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\lceil f^{*}(\Delta)\rceil)\cdot\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z^{\circ}\subset Z)\Big)\,,

where
f:(Z,\partial Z)\to(Y,\partial Y)
is a resolution of singularities such that
\partial Z=f^{-1}(\partial Y)
is a simple normal crossing divisor and
Z^{\circ}=Z\setminus\partial Z
.
We assume that

f_{|Z^{\circ}}:Z^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}

is an isomorphism.

It is proved in
[
KonW
, corollary 4.6]

that this class is well defined, i.e. it does not depend on a choice of a SNC resolution of singularities.

Remark 5.3
.

The twisted motivic Chern class may be obtained as a limit of the equivariant version of Borisov-Libgober elliptic class
[
BoLi1
]
, whenever it is defined, see
[
KonW
, Section 3]
for a detailed discussion.

5.3.
Twisted classes of Schubert cells and stable envelopes

The K-theoretic stable envelopes were defined in
[
O2
,
OS
]
. They depend on a fractional line bundle called slope. It turns out that for homogeneous varieties the stable envelopes for a small anti-ample slope coincide (up to normalization) with the motivic Chern classes of Schubert varieties, see
[
AMSS
, theorem 8.5]
,
[
FRW
]
and
[
Kon2
, theorem 6.2]
. The twisted class is a class depending on a fractional line bundle which generalizes the motivic Chern class and coincides with the stable envelopes for an arbitrary slope, see
[
KonW
, theorem 7.1]
.
Consider a generalized flag variety
G/B
.
Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
be a fractional character and
w\in W
a fixed point.

Proposition 5.4

(
[
KonW
, section 10]
)
.

There exists a unique
\mathbb{T}
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
\Delta_{w,\lambda}
on
X_{w}
such that

(1)

Divisor
\Delta_{w,\lambda}
represents
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)_{|X_{w}}
.

(2)

Support of
\Delta_{w,\lambda}
is contained in the boundary
\partial X_{w}
.

Definition 5.5
.

The twisted motivic Chern class of a Schubert cell is defined as

(5)

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,\lambda):=\iota_{w*}\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{w},\partial X_{w};\Delta_{w,\lambda})\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]\,.

Remark 5.6
.

To compute the class
\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,\lambda)
one may use the Bott-Samelson resolution. The divisor
p_{\underline{w}}^{*}\Delta_{w,\lambda}
is the unique
\mathbb{T}
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor representing
p_{\underline{w}}^{*}\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
with support contained in
\partial Z_{\underline{w}}
. Its coefficients are computed by Chevalley formula (cf. propositions
4.2
and
4.4
).

Remark 5.7

(
[
KonW
, proposition 2.5]
)
.

The twisted motivic Chern class generalizes the motivic Chern class, i.e. for a small anti-ample
\lambda
we have

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,\lambda)=\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,0)=\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{w}^{\circ}\subset G/B)\,.

Theorem 5.8

(
[
KonW
, theorem 7.1 and remark 6.4]
)
.

Suppose that a fractional character
\lambda
is general enough. Then the class

q^{-\frac{1}{2}\dim X^{+}_{w}}\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}_{-q}(w,\lambda)\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[q^{1/2},q^{-1/2}]

is equal to the stable envelope

\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(w)\,.

Remark 5.9
.

We write
\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}_{-q}
for the image of the class
\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}
in a map

\rho:K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]\to K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[q^{-1/2},q^{1/2}]\,,

which sends
y
to
-q
.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
\lambda
be an arbitrary rational character. Let
\lambda^{-}
be a small anti-ample fractional character and

\varepsilon
a small enough positive rational number. Then the class

q^{-\frac{1}{2}\dim X^{+}_{w}}\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}_{-q}(w,\lambda)\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[q^{1/2},q^{-1/2}]

is equal to the stable envelope

\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda+\varepsilon\lambda^{-}}(w)\,.

Remark 5.11
.

We do not include the definition of stable envelope here. For a definition adapted to generalized flag varieties see e.g.
[
AMSS
,
SZZ
,
KonW
]

Remark 5.12
.

We write
\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}(w)
for the stable envelope of the fixed point
w\in W
for weight chamber
\mathfrak{C}
, slope
\lambda
and the tangent polarization.
We consider stable envelopes only for
the tangent bundle
TG/B
as a polarization.
This restriction does not reduce the generality of results.
Stable envelopes corresponding to different
polarizations are related by a shift of slope and renormalization, see
[
O2
, Paragraph 9.1.12]
.
We usually consider stable envelopes for the positive weight chamber
\mathfrak{C^{+}}
.

6.
Twisted Demazure-Lusztig operators

Many cohomological invariants of Schubert varieties can be computed inductively due to the inductive construction of resolution of singularities. By application of Demazure or Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand operators, starting from the class of 0-dimensional Schubert variety, the fundamental class can be computed,
[
BGG
]
. The same is true for the motivic Chern classes in K-theory as proven in
[
AMSS
]
, where Demazure-Lusztig operators are employed. The
twisted version demands a twisted version of operators.

6.1.
The definitions and main properties

Let
\alpha_{s}
be a simple root and
s\in W
the corresponding reflection. Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{s}
the relative tangent bundle of the projection
\pi_{s}:G/B\to G/P_{s}
. Let us recall the definition of Demazure-Lusztig operator.

Definition 6.1
.

The Demazure-Lusztig operator
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s}}
is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{y}(\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*})\cdot\pi_{s}^{*}\pi_{s*}-{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot s^{\textsc{r}}-\frac{(1+y)}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}\,.

They appeared already in Lusztig’s article
[
Lusztig
]
on the K-theory of flag varieties. In fact,
K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[q,q^{-1}]
is a free, rank one module over the Hecke algebra, hence it admits both right and left Hecke action.
The relation with characteristic classes was exposed in
[
AMSS
]
. Because of the relation with Hirzebruch
\chi_{y}
-genus we use the variable
y
which is equal to
-q
from the Lusztig’s work.
The left Demazure-Lusztig
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s}}
operator is defined by (cf.
[
MNS
, Section 5.3]
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s}}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1+y\alpha^{-1}_{s}}{1-\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\cdot s^{\textsc{l}}-\frac{(1+y)}{1-\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\cdot{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}\,.

Note that here
\alpha_{s}^{-1}
denotes the one-dimensional representation, the dual of
\alpha_{s}
. We use here the multiplicative notations for characters.

Remark 6.2
.

For the flag varieties in type
A
the left Demazure-Lusztig operators appeared in form of the R-matrix relations connecting weight functions
[
RTV0
, section 7.2]
,
[
RTV
, theorem 3.2]
. In
[
FRW
]
it was shown, that the trigonometric weight functions coincide with the motivic Chern classes of Schubert cells.

We define a twisted version of the Demazure-Lusztig operators operators.

Definition 6.3
.

(1)

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
we define the twisted Demazure-Lusztig operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot s^{\textsc{r}}-\frac{(1+y)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil a\rceil}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot s^{\textsc{r}}+\frac{(1+y)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil a\rceil-1}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}\cdot{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}

(2)

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be a fractional character. We define the twisted Demazure-Lusztig operator
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,\lambda}}:K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]\to K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]
by

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,\lambda}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-\langle\lambda,\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\rangle}}\,.

(3)

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
we define the left twisted Demazure-Lusztig operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,a}}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1+y\alpha^{-1}_{s}}{1-\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\cdot s^{\textsc{l}}-\frac{(1+y)\cdot\alpha_{s}^{-\lceil a\rceil}}{1-\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\cdot{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+y\alpha^{-1}_{s}}{1-\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\cdot s^{\textsc{l}}+\frac{(1+y)\cdot\alpha_{s}^{1-\lceil a\rceil}}{1-\alpha_{s}}\cdot{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}\,.

(4)

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be a fractional character. We define the twisted left Demazure-Lusztig operator
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,\lambda}}:K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]\to K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]
by

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,\lambda}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,\langle\lambda,\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\rangle}}\,.

Formally the definitions make sense after the inversion of the elements
1-\mathcal{L}
and
1-\alpha
, but it will be clear later, that the operations lead to
K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]
, see remark
6.5
.

6.2.
Right Demazure-Lusztig operators

We note several straightforward consequences of the above definition.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
s
be a simple reflection,
\lambda
a fractional character and
a\in\mathbb{R}
a real number.

(1)

For

the twisted operators are equal to Demazure-Lusztig operators

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}\,,\qquad{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,a}}

(2)

Operators

are morphisms of

-modules.

(3)

For a small enough positive real number

we have

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a-\varepsilon}}\,,\qquad{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,a}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,a-\varepsilon}}

(4)

Let

be an anti-ample fractional character.
For a small enough positive real number
\varepsilon>0
we have

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,\lambda-\varepsilon\lambda^{-}}}\,,\qquad{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,a}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,\lambda+\varepsilon\lambda^{-}}}

(5)

Let
\lambda
be a fractional character. The twisted Demazure-Lusztig operator is equal to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{y}(\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*})\cdot\pi_{s}^{*}\pi_{s*}-\left(1+\frac{(1+y)(1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil-\langle\lambda,\alpha_{s}\rangle\rceil})}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\right)\cdot{\rm id}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s}}-\frac{(1+y)(1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil-\langle\lambda,\alpha_{s}\rangle\rceil})}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot{\rm id}\,.

Remark 6.5
.

For every integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the element
1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{n}
is divisible by
1-\mathcal{L}_{s}
in the equivariant K-theory
K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)
. Therefore, the twisted Demazure-Lusztig operators define maps

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}:K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]\to K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]\,.

Similarly
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,a}}
acts on
K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]
. This action does not preserve the
K_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
-module structure. On the other hand, unlike
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}
, it descenss to an action on
K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/P)[y]
for any parabolic group
P\supset B
.

We prove quadratic relation for the twisted Demazure-Lusztig polynomials.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
s\in W
be a simple reflection and
a\in\mathbb{R}
a real number. Suppose that

. Then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-a}}=-y\,{\rm id}_{K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]}\,.

Proof.

For two real numbers
a,b
and
\xi\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[y]
the composition
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,b}}
evaluated at
\xi
is of the form

\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\left(\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}\xi-\frac{(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-\lceil b\rceil}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}s^{\textsc{r}}\xi\right)-\frac{(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil a\rceil}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\left(\frac{1+{y}{\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}s^{\textsc{r}}\xi-\frac{(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil b\rceil}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\xi\right)\,.

Suppose that
b=-a
and the number

is not an integer, then

-\lceil b\rceil=-\lceil-a\rceil=\lfloor a\rfloor=\lceil a\rceil-1\,.

In the expression above the coefficient of

is equal to

-\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot\frac{(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil a\rceil-1}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}-\frac{(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil a\rceil}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot\frac{1+{y}{\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}=0\,.

The coefficient of
\xi
is equal to

\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot\frac{1+y\mathcal{L}_{s}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{*}}+\frac{(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\lceil a\rceil}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\cdot\frac{(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{1-\lceil a\rceil}}{1-\mathcal{L}_{s}}\,.

Simplifying we obtain
-y
.
∎

Corollary 6.7

Suppose that

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-a}}=-y-(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{a}\cdot{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-a}}\,.

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a}}\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-a}}=({\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a+\varepsilon}}-(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{a})\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-a-\varepsilon}}={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,a+\varepsilon}}\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-a-\varepsilon}}-(1+y)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{a}\cdot{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,-a}}

The result follows from proposition

∎

7.
Twisted Hecke algebra

We describe the algebra of operations

{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{w,a}}

, purely algebraically, we lift this action to the endomorphisms of the representation ring

.

Let

[

[
MNS
, proposition 5.3 (c)]
). Moreover, the line bundle

is

∎

.

Left and right twisted Demazure-Lusztig operators commute. Let

The twisted Demazure-Lusztig operator
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s_{1},a}}
commutes with both the left Weyl group action and multiplication by a character from

, see proposition

.
∎

8.1.
Recursive formula for
G/B

In this section we prove a counterpart of theorem
6.9

[

, proposition 7.3]
, see also
[

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be a fractional character and
w\in(G/B)^{\mathbb{T}}\simeq W
a fixed point.

(1)

Suppose that
l(sw)>l(w)
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(sw,\lambda)={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,w\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,\lambda))\,.

(2)

Suppose that
\lambda
is general enough, then

\displaystyle(-y)^{\frac{1}{2}(l(w)-l(sw)+1)}\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(sw,\lambda)={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,w\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,\lambda))\,.

Proof.

First we prove the theorem for
w={\rm id}
. The Schubert variety of
{\rm id}
is a single point. Thus, the class
\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id},\lambda)
does not depend on the character
\lambda
. It vanishes at all fixed points other than
{\rm id}
and at the fixed point
{\rm id}
we have

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id},\lambda)_{|{\rm id}}=eu(T_{{\rm id}}G/B)\,.

Restrictions of the relative tangent bundle
\mathcal{L}_{s}
are given by
{\mathcal{L}_{s}}_{|{\rm id}}=\alpha_{s}^{-1}
and
{\mathcal{L}_{s}}_{|s}=\alpha_{s}
. (We treat
\alpha_{s}
as a character and we apply the multiplicative notation.) It follows that

(8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id},\lambda))={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,s\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id},\lambda))={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{s,s\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id},s\lambda))=\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(s,\lambda)\,.

This proves the theorem for
w={\rm id}\,.

Let us focus on the case
l(ws)>l(w)
. Then
l(ws)=l(w)+l(s)
. We have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,w\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,\lambda))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,w\lambda}}\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{w^{-1},w\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id},w\lambda))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{w^{-1},w\lambda}}\circ{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,w\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}({\rm id},w\lambda))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{r}}_{w^{-1},w\lambda}}(\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(s,w\lambda))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(sw,\lambda)\,,

where the first and the fourth equality follow from proposition
7.8
(1), the second from lemma
7.2
and the third from equation (
8
).

The proof of the other case is analogous. In the last equality we need to use proposition
7.8
(2) instead of proposition
7.8
(1).
∎

8.2.
Recursion for
G/P

The left induction is also valid for homogeneous varieties
G/P
where
P
is an arbitrary parabolic subgroup. Denote by
\pi
the projection
\pi:G/B\to G/P

W_{P}
be a subgroup of
W
corresponding to
P

(G/P)^{\mathbb{T}}
is in bijection with the set of left cosets
W/W_{P}

W^{P}\subset W
be a set of minimal length representatives. For a coset
wW_{P}
we consider the Schubert variety

X_{wW_{P}}=\overline{B\pi(w)}\subset G/P\,,\qquad X^{\circ}_{wW_{P}}=B\pi(w)\subset G/P\,,\qquad\partial X_{wW_{P}}=X_{wW_{P}}\setminus X_{wW_{P}}^{\circ}\,.

Denote by
i_{wW_{P}}
the inclusion
X_{wW_{P}}\to G/P
. The rational Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G/P)
coincides with
\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G/B)^{W_{P}}.
For a coset
wW_{P}
and a rational character
\lambda\in\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G/P)
let
\Delta^{P}_{wW_{P},\lambda}
be the unique
\mathbb{T}
-invariant divisor with support contained in the boundary
\partial X_{wW_{P}}

\mathcal{L}(\lambda)_{|X_{wW_{P}}}

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(wW_{P},\lambda):=i_{wW_{P}*}\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(X_{wW_{P}},\partial X_{wW_{P}};\Delta^{P}_{wW_{P},\lambda})\in K^{\mathbb{T}}(G/P)[y]\,.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
w\in W^{P}
and
\lambda\in\operatorname{Pic}_{\mathbb{Q}}(G/P)

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(wW_{P},\lambda)=\pi_{*}\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(w,\lambda)\,.

Proof.

Fix a reduced word decomposition
{\underline{w}}
of
w
. The element
w
is a minimal length representative, therefore the map

\pi_{|X_{w}}\colon X_{w}\to X_{wW_{P}}

is birational. It is isomorphic on the Schubert cell and
\pi^{-1}_{|X_{w}}(\partial X_{wW_{P}})=\partial X_{w}.
Thus

(Z_{\underline{w}},\partial Z_{{\underline{w}}}){\xrightarrow{p_{{\underline{w}}}}}(X_{w},\partial X_{w}){\xrightarrow{\pi_{|X_{w}}}}(X_{wW_{P}},\partial X_{wW_{P}})

is a SNC resolution of singularities.

The divisor
\pi_{|X_{w}}^{*}\Delta^{P}_{wW_{P},\lambda}
represents
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)_{|X_{w}}
. It is
\mathbb{T}
-invariant and contained in the boundary of Schubert variety
X_{w}
. Therefore (cf.
[
KonW
, section 10]

\pi_{|X_{w}}^{*}\Delta^{P}_{wW_{P},\lambda}=\Delta_{w,\lambda}\,.

It follows that both considered classes are equal to the push-forward of

\operatorname{mC}^{\mathbb{T}}(Z_{\underline{w}},\partial Z_{\underline{w}};p^{*}_{{\underline{w}}}\Delta_{w,\lambda})\,.

∎

Lemma 8.4

Proposition 8.7

Consider

Lemma 8.8

Let
\tilde{s}

8.3.
Wall crossing for change of the coweight chamber

Due to theorem
5.8
we obtained a recursive formula for twisted motivic Chern classes. After translation to stable envelopes it reads as follows:

Corollary 8.11
.

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be a general enough fractional character and
w\in(G/B)^{\mathbb{T}}\simeq W
a fixed point. Then

(9)

\displaystyle q^{1/2}\cdot\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(sw)={}{\mathcal{T}^{{\textsc{l}},q}_{s,w\lambda}}(\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(w))\,,

where
{\mathcal{T}^{{\textsc{l}},q}_{s,w\lambda}}
denotes the operator
{\mathcal{T}^{\textsc{l}}_{s,w\lambda}}
after substitution
y=-q
.

Stable envelopes depend on a choice of a coweight chamber
\mathfrak{C}
. Up to this point we considered only the positive coweight chamber
\mathfrak{C_{+}}
.
All other chambers are of the form
\sigma\mathfrak{C_{+}}
for some
\sigma\in W
.
It follows from the naturality of stable envelopes (
[
AMSS
, lemma 8.2 (a)]
) that for Weyl group elements
\sigma,w\in W
, arbitrary slope
\lambda
and arbitrary coweight chamber
\mathfrak{C}
we have

(10)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\textsc{l}}(\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}(w))=\operatorname{stab}_{\sigma\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}(\sigma w)\,.

For any choice of coweight chamber
\mathfrak{C}
and slope
\lambda
classes

\{\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{\lambda}(w)\}_{w\in W}
form a base of the localized K-theory

S^{-1}K_{\mathbb{T}}(G/B)[q^{1/2},q^{-1/2}]

over the localized Laurent polynomials ring

S^{-1}K_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)[q^{1/2},q^{-1/2}]\,.

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
be two coweight chambers. Wall-crossing formula (or R-matrix) describes the base change matrix from the basis
\{\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C}_{1}}^{\lambda}(w)\}_{w\in W}
to
\{\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C}_{2}}^{\lambda}(w)\}_{w\in W}
(see
[
O2
, Paragraph 9.2.11]
). Recursion (
9
) may be used to obtain such a formula.

Theorem 8.12
.

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{C}^{*})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be a general enough fractional character and
w,\sigma\in W
Weyl group elements. Let
s\in W
be a simple reflection. Then

\operatorname{stab}_{\sigma s\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(w)=\frac{(1-q)\cdot\sigma\alpha_{s}^{\lfloor\langle w\lambda,\sigma\alpha_{s}\rangle\rfloor}}{1-q\sigma\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\operatorname{stab}_{\sigma\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(\sigma s\sigma^{-1}w)+\frac{q^{1/2}(1-\sigma\alpha^{-1}_{s})}{1-q\sigma\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\operatorname{stab}_{\sigma\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(w)\,.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{w}=s\sigma^{-1}w
. Corollary
8.11
for
\tilde{w}
implies that

q^{1/2}\cdot\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(s\tilde{w})=\frac{1-q\alpha^{-1}_{s}}{1-\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\cdot s^{\textsc{l}}(\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(\tilde{w}))-\frac{(1-q)\cdot\alpha_{s}^{-\lceil\langle\tilde{w}\lambda,\alpha_{s}\rangle\rceil}}{1-\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\cdot\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(\tilde{w})\,,

which may be rewritten as

s^{\textsc{l}}(\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(\tilde{w}))=\frac{(1-q)\cdot\alpha_{s}^{-\lceil\langle\tilde{w}\lambda,\alpha_{s}\rangle\rceil}}{1-q\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(\tilde{w})+\frac{q^{1/2}(1-\alpha^{-1}_{s})}{1-q\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\operatorname{stab}_{\mathfrak{C_{+}}}^{\lambda}(s\tilde{w})\,.

We apply the left Weyl group action of
\sigma^{\textsc{l}}
to this equation and use formula (
10
). Note that
\sigma^{\textsc{l}}
acts also on the coefficients
\alpha_{s}\in K_{\mathbb{T}}(pt)
. We obtain

\operatorname{stab}_{\sigma s\mathfrak{C}_{+}}^{\lambda}(\sigma s\tilde{w})=\frac{(1-q)\cdot\sigma\alpha_{s}^{-\lceil\langle\tilde{w}\lambda,\alpha_{s}\rangle\rceil}}{1-q\sigma\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\operatorname{stab}_{\sigma\mathfrak{C}_{+}}^{\lambda}(\sigma\tilde{w})+\frac{q^{1/2}(1-\sigma\alpha^{-1}_{s})}{1-q\sigma\alpha^{-1}_{s}}\operatorname{stab}_{\sigma\mathfrak{C}_{+}}^{\lambda}(\sigma s\tilde{w})\,.

Substitution
\tilde{w}=s\sigma^{-1}w
completes the proof. The exponent

{-\lceil\langle s\sigma^{-1}w\lambda,\alpha_{s}\rangle\rceil}={-\lceil-\langle\sigma^{-1}w\lambda,\alpha_{s}\rangle\rceil}

is equal to

\lfloor\langle w\lambda,\sigma\alpha_{s}\rangle\rfloor
.
∎

The above theorem describes the wall-crossing formula from the coweight chamber
\sigma\mathfrak{C_{+}}
to
\sigma s\mathfrak{C_{+}}
. All chambers are of the form
\sigma^{\prime}\mathfrak{C_{+}}
for some
\sigma^{\prime}\in W
. Therefore, repetitive use of the above theorem determines the wall-crossing formula between arbitrary coweight chambers.

10.1.
The Kirwan map

In the study of the homogeneous spaces for

it is convenient to consider

instead of
{\rm SL}_{n}

Let

Let
\mathbb{T}\subset{\rm GL}_{n}
be the diagonal torus. Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}

The root

For

denote by

associated with coordinates. The equivariant K-theory of the point

The equivariant K-theory of the flag manifold

is generated over

by the classes of the tautological line bundles
V_{i}/V_{i-1}

be a set of variables. The surjection

\displaystyle K_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\underline{t}^{\pm 1},\underline{x}^{\pm 1}]

\displaystyle\to K_{\mathbb{T}}({\rm GL}_{n}/B)\,,

\displaystyle x_{i}\;

\displaystyle\mapsto\;[V_{i}/V_{i-1}]

has a geometric interpretation. Let
\mathbb{T}^{2}
be the product of tori and let
\underline{t}
and
\underline{x}
be the sets of coordinate characters. The first copy of
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
acts on

by the left multiplication, the second copy acts by the right multiplication. The composition of the restriction map and the natural isomorphism

Let

11.
Universal algebra

We define an algebra acting on the ring of Laurent polynomials. We will show that the generating operations satisfy the same quadratic and braid relations, as those acting on the level of K-theory of
{\rm GL}_{n}/B
.
The described algebra can be compared with the algebra generated by Demazure-Lusztig operators which computes the motivic Chern classes of matrix Schubert varieties.

11.1.
The small algebra

Let us define operations

{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}\colon\mathbb{Z}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots,z_{n}^{\pm 1},y]\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots,z_{n}^{\pm 1},y]

on Laurent polynomials in
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}
. The operations depend on
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
and
a\in\mathbb{Q}
. For
f\in\mathbb{Z}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots,z_{n}^{\pm 1},y]
let

L_{i}=\frac{z_{i+1}}{z_{i}}

and

{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}(f)=-\frac{(1+y)L_{i}^{\lceil a\rceil}\cdot f-\big(1+y\,L_{i}^{-1}\big)\cdot s_{i}f}{1-L_{i}}\,,

where

s_{i}f(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{i},z_{i+1},\dots,z_{n},y)=f(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{i+1},z_{i},\dots,z_{n},y)\,.

If we set
z_{i}=z_{i+1}
then the numerator is equal to 0, therefore, it is divisible by
z_{i}-z_{i+1}
, which is equivalent to divisibility in Laurent polynomials by
1-\frac{z_{i}}{z_{i+1}}
. Hence the operators
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}
transform Laurent polynomials into Laurent polynomials. The operators
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}
can be treated as variants of the isobaric divided differences, see e.g.
[
RimanyiSzenes
, section 2.1]
. Another way of writing the operators
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}
is the following

{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}(f)=\frac{\big(1+y\,L_{i}^{-1}\big)\cdot s_{i}f}{1-L_{i}}+\frac{(1+y)L_{i}^{\lceil a\rceil-1}\cdot f}{1-L_{i}^{-1}}\,.

This form is adapted to the localization formula.
A slight modification of the operator
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}
will be useful

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,a}(f)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1+y)L_{i}^{\lceil a\rceil}\cdot f-\big(1+y\,L_{i}\big)\cdot s_{i}f}{1-L_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(1+y\,L_{i}\big)\cdot s_{i}f}{1-L_{i}}+\frac{(1+y)L_{i}^{\lceil a\rceil-1}\cdot f}{1-L_{i}^{-1}}\,.

It is elementary to note that

{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,a}((1+y\,L_{i})f)=(1+y\,L_{i})~{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}(f)\,,

i.e.
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,a}
differs from
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}
by conjugation with the multiplication by
(1+y\,L_{i})
.

The properties described below hold for both
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{r}}_{{i,a}}
and
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\textsc{l}}_{i,a}
, therefore we will simply use the notation
{\mathfrak{T}}_{i,a}
.

Lemma 11.1

Suppose

If

Proof.

The proof is identical as in proposition

The operators

Lemma 11.2

For

{\mathfrak{T}}_{i,\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2}}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i+1,\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1}}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i,\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}={\mathfrak{T}}_{i+1,\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i,\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1}}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{i+1,\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2}}

Remark 11.3
.

The vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})
can be understood as the weight for the maximal torus of
{\rm GL}_{3}
, then the above formula coincides with the formula from example
7.6

Proof.

The formula can be checked by hand (or rather using a computer algebra software). We can assume that
n=3
,
i=1
. Since in the formulas appears
\lceil\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\rceil
one is led to consider various cases depending on the fractional parts. Setting
a=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
and
b=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}
it is enough to check two cases assuming for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}

{\mathfrak{T}}_{1,a}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{2,a+b}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{1,b}={\mathfrak{T}}_{2,b}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{1,a+b}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{2,a}

and

{\mathfrak{T}}_{1,a}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{2,a+b+1}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{1,b}={\mathfrak{T}}_{2,b}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{1,a+b+1}\circ{\mathfrak{T}}_{2,a}\,.

Verification is an elementary, although lengthy, calculation.

There is an alternative method of the proof, not demanding direct calculation, but relying on the fact that Demazure-Lusztig operators generate an action of the Hecke algebra on the K-theory of
{\rm GL}_{n}/B

It will be presented after theorem

A note on weighted simultaneous Diophantine approximation on manifolds
Demi Allen, Baowei Wang

Abstract
In this note, we present an improvement to a recent result due to Beresnevich, Levesley, and Ward (2021) pertaining to weighted simultaneous Diophantine approximation on manifolds.



2020
Mathematics Subject Classification
: Primary 11J13, 28A78; Secondary 11J83.

Keywords and phrases: Weighted Diophantine approximation, Manifolds, Hausdorff dimension, Mass Transference Principle.

1
Introduction and statement of the result

A fundamental problem in metric Diophantine approximation is to understand the “size”, specifically the Lebesgue measure, Hausdorff measure, and Hausdorff dimension, of the classical set of
simultaneously
\psi
-well approximable points
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For a given approximating function
\psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, that is the set

W_{n}(\psi)=\left\{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\max_{1\leq i\leq n}{|qx_{i}-p_{i}|}<\psi(q)\text{ for infinitely many }(\mathbf{p},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

For monotonic approximating functions, the Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff measure of
W_{n}(\psi)
are characterised, respectively, by classical results due to Khintchine
[
15
,
16
]
and Jarník
[
14
]
from the 1920s and 1930s. In the 1940s, Duffin and Schaeffer constructed a counter-example demonstrating that monotonicity of the approximating function is essential in the one-dimensional version of Khintchine’s Theorem and proferred a conjecture concerning the expected result for non-monotonic approximating functions
[
12
]
. A higher-dimensional version of the Duffin-Schaeffer Conjecture, which encapsulates the setting of simultaneous Diophantine approximation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, was formulated by Sprindžuk in
[
25
, Chapter 1, Section 8]
. In 1990, Pollington and Vaughan
[
20
]
proved this conjecture of Sprindžuk for
n\geq 2
and, in a recent breakthrough, the original conjecture of Duffin and Schaeffer was proved by Koukoulopoulos and Maynard
[
19
]
. Moreover, in
[
8
]
Beresnevich and Velani demonstrated the equivalence of the Duffin-Schaeffer Conjecture and its appropriate Hausdorff measure counterpart. This equivalence is a consequence of the celebrated Mass Transference Principle proved in
[
8
]
. Taking all of the above results together, we have a fairly complete picture regarding the metric theory of the set
W_{n}(\psi)
.

In recent years, it has become increasingly popular to study various generalisations of the set
W_{n}(\psi)
and how they interact with other naturally arising sets such as curves and manifolds. The particular generalisation of
W_{n}(\psi)
which will concern us here will be the
weighted simultaneously
\Psi
-approximable points
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Moreover, we will be interested in studying the Hausdorff dimension of the intersection of this set with manifolds for a particular form of the approximating function
\Psi
. Throughout, given
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
s\geq 0
, we will write
{\cal H}^{s}(X)
to denote the Hausdorff
s
-measure of
X
. We will denote by
\dimh{X}
the Hausdorff dimension of
X
and we will write
\lambda_{n}(X)
to denote the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
X
. For definitions and properties of Hausdorff measures and dimension, we refer the reader to
[
13
]
.

Let
\Psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
be an approximating function such that

\Psi(q)=(\psi_{1}(q),\dots,\psi_{n}(q))

where
\psi_{i}:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. We define the
weighted simultaneously
\Psi
-approximable points
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as

W_{n}(\Psi)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|qx_{i}-p_{i}|<\psi_{i}(q),\quad 1\leq i\leq n,\quad\text{for i.m. }(\mathbf{p},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

Throughout, we will frequently use the shorthand “i.m.” for “infinitely many”. When
\Psi(q)=(q^{-\tau_{1}},\dots,q^{-\tau_{n}})
for some
\mathbf{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
, we write
W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})
in place of
W_{n}(\Psi)
.

When considering manifolds, we will look at them locally on some open subset
{\cal U}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and will use the following Monge parameterisation without loss of generality:

\displaystyle{\cal M}=\{(x,f(x)):x\in{\cal U}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(1)

where
d
is the dimension of the manifold,
m
is the codimension of the manifold, i.e.
d+m=n
, and
f:{\cal U}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

Considering the Hausdorff dimension of the intersection of
W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})
with a manifold
{\cal M}
, we are able to establish the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\cal M}=\{(x,f(x)):x\in{\cal U}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
where
f:{\cal U}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is such that
f\in C^{(2)}
. Here,
d
is the dimension of the manifold
{\cal M}
and
m
is its codimension, so that
n=d+m
. Suppose
\mathbf{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
is such that

\tau_{1}\geq\tau_{2}\geq\dots\geq\tau_{d}\geq\max_{d+1\leq i\leq n}{\tau_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{d+j}}<1.

Then,

\dimh{(W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})\cap{\cal M})}\geq\min_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\{\frac{n+1+\sum_{k=i}^{n}{(\tau_{i}-\tau_{k})}}{\tau_{i}+1}-m\right\}.

Theorem
1.1
improves upon a recent result due to Beresnevich, Levesley, and Ward
[
6
, Theorem 1.9]
, who established the same lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension of
W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})\cap{\cal M}
as in Theorem
1.1
but subject to more stringent conditions on the
weight vector

\mathbf{\tau}
. More precisely, they required that
\mathbf{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
satisfies

\tau_{1}\geq\tau_{2}\geq\dots\geq\tau_{d}\geq\max_{d+1\leq i\leq n}\left\{\tau_{i},\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}}{d}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{d+j}}<1.

In the interest of brevity, we refer the reader to
[
6
]
and references therein for a detailed overview regarding the state of the art of this problem. Nevertheless, it is still worth remarking here that in the classical case of unweighted simultaneous Diophantine approximation, where the approximating functions in each co-ordinate direction are equal, establishing results about
W_{n}(\psi)\cap{\cal M}
is a well-studied problem. In particular, in this case, we note that the problem of establishing upper and lower bounds for the Hausdorff dimension of
W_{n}(\psi)\cap{\cal M}
for various manifolds and various forms of the approximating function
\psi
has been studied in, for example,
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
24
,
26
]
. Indeed, one may think of Theorem
1.1
and
[
6
, Theorem 1.9]
as a generalisation of
[
5
, Theorem 1]
to the setting of weighted simultaneous approximation on manifolds. Results relating to the Lebesgue measure of the set of weighted simultaneously
\Psi
-approximable points,
W_{n}(\Psi)
, not intersected with manifolds, can be traced back to work of Khintchine
[
17
]
. The Hausdorff dimension of sets of weighted simultaneously approximable points was studied by Rynne in
[
22
]
.

Our proof strategy for establishing Theorem
1.1
mirrors that employed by Beresnevich, Levesley, and Ward in proving
[
6
, Theorem 1.9]
. In particular, we begin by establishing a “Dirichlet-type” theorem for weighted approximation on manifolds (Theorem
3.1
) and we use this result to construct an appropriate full measure set contained in our manifold
{\cal M}
. Our proof is completed via an application of a mass transference principle from “rectangles to rectangles” proved recently by Wang and Wu in
[
27
]
which allows us to deduce the desired lower bound on the Hausdorff dimension of
W_{n}(\tau)\cap{\cal M}
. The key differences allowing for the improvement in Theorem
1.1
compared with
[
6
, Theorem 1.9]
are the more general “Dirichlet-type” theorem and the use of this more recent mass transference principle from
[
27
]
in the final step of our proof. At the analogous point in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.9]
, Beresnevich, Levesley and Ward use an earlier mass tranference principle from “balls to rectangles” established by Wang, Wu, and Xu in
[
28
]
.

2
Mass transference principle from rectangles to rectangles

The original Mass Transference Principle due to Beresnevich and Velani
[
8
]
allows us to infer Hausdorff measure statements for
\limsup
sets of balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
from appropriate Lebesgue measure statements. This result is somewhat surprising since Hausdorff measure is in some sense a refinement of Lebesgue measure. As a result, the Mass Transference Principle has a number of profound consequences and has come to be an important tool in the study of metric Diophantine approximation. For example, one such consequence is the aforementioned example that the Mass Transference Principle implies the equivalence of the Duffin-Schaeffer Conjecture and its Hausdorff measure analogue.

Given a real number
s>0
and a ball
B:=B(x,r)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of radius
r
centred at
x
, let
B^{s}:=B(x,r^{\frac{s}{n}})
. In particular, note that
B^{n}=B
. In its most simple form, the Mass Transference Principle reads as follows.

Mass Transference Principle

(Beresnevich – Velani,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\{B_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
radii
r(B_{j})\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. Let
s>0
and let
\Omega
be a ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Suppose that, for any ball
B
in
\Omega
,

{\cal H}^{n}\big(\/B\cap\limsup_{j\to\infty}B^{s}_{j}{}\,\big)={\cal H}^{n}(B)\ .

Then, for any ball
B
in
\Omega
,

{\cal H}^{s}\big(\/B\cap\limsup_{j\to\infty}B^{n}_{j}\,\big)={\cal H}^{s}(B)\ .

Remark
.

The statement above is a simplified form of
[
8
, Theorem 2]
. Also recall that for Borel sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the Hausdorff
n
-measure,
{\cal H}^{n}
, is a constant multiple times
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure,
\lambda_{n}
(see, for example,
[
13
]
). So the above statement really does allow us to pass between Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff measure statements.

Since the publication of
[
8
]
, the original Mass Transference Principle from “balls to balls” stated above has been extended in a multitude of directions. For example, in
[
9
]
, Beresnevich and Velani established a mass transference principle for systems of linear forms which was later improved in
[
2
]
. This result was further extended to a mass transference principle for sets satisfying a “local scaling property” in locally compact metric spaces in
[
1
]
. A mass transference principle from “balls to rectangles” was established in
[
28
]
and has subsequently been upgraded to a mass transference principle from “rectangles to rectangles” in
[
27
]
. Results relating to a mass transference principle from “balls to arbitrary open sets” have been established by Koivusalo and Rams
[
18
]
and Zhong
[
29
]
.

As hinted at earlier, for our current purposes, we are particularly interested in the mass transference principle from “rectangles to rectangles” established in
[
27
]
. To aid readability we will not state the result of
[
27
]
in full generality but rather we will present here a simplified statement which follows as a corollary of the mass transference principle given by
[
27
, Theorem 3.4]
and which is more directly applicable to the problem at hand.

Let
{\cal U}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\cal U}_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
{\cal U}_{i}
being locally compact in
\mathbb{R}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. For
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
and a constant
c>0
, let

W^{c}_{\mathbf{a}}=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{c}{q^{a_{i}}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq n,\quad\text{for i.m. }(\mathbf{p},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

For
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and a constant
c^{\prime}>0
, define

W^{c^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{t})=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{a_{i}+t_{i}}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq n,\quad\text{for i.m. }(\mathbf{p},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

The following statement is a consequence of
[
27
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Wang – Wu,
[
27
]
)
.

Let
W^{c}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
W^{c^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{t})
be as defined above and suppose that

\lambda_{n}(W^{c}_{\mathbf{a}})=\lambda_{n}({\cal U}).

Then,

\displaystyle\dimh{(W^{c^{\prime}}_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{t}))}\geq\min_{A\in{\cal A}}\left\{\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2}}{1}+\frac{\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{3}}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{2}}{t_{k}}}{A}\right\},

where

{\cal A}=\{a_{i},a_{i}+t_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}

and for each
A\in{\cal A}
, the sets
{\cal K}_{1},{\cal K}_{2},{\cal K}_{3}
are defined as

{\cal K}_{1}=\{k:a_{k}\geq A\},\quad{\cal K}_{2}=\{k:a_{k}+t_{k}\leq A\}\setminus{\cal K}_{1},\quad{\cal K}_{3}=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus({\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2})

thus giving a partition of
\{1,\dots,n\}
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we will present our proof of Theorem
1.1
. We follow essentially the same strategy as laid out by Beresnevich, Levesley, and Ward in their proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.9]
. As already alluded to, the main difference is that we use the mass transference principle from “rectangles to rectangles” proved in
[
27
]
, whereas Beresnevich, Levesley and Ward used the earlier mass transference principle from “balls to rectangles” established in
[
28
]
. Our proof is split into three main parts. In Section
3.1
we prove a Dirichlet-type theorem (Theorem
3.1
) for weighted approximation on manifolds. In Section
3.2
we use Theorem
3.1
to construct an appropriate full measure set and make some other preliminary preparations which eventually enable us to apply the mass tranference principle for rectangles (Theorem
2.1
) to complete the proof in Section
3.3
.

3.1
A Dirichlet-type theorem for weighted simultaneous approximation on manifolds

The first step in establishing Theorem
1.1
is to prove a Dirichlet-type theorem for weighted simultaneous approximation on manifolds which will eventually help us to “construct” a suitable full measure set to which we can apply Theorem
2.1
. The following statement provides a suitable Dirichlet-type theorem and is a modification of
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
. Indeed, we follow the same line of proof as the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
with some parts following (almost) verbatim.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\cal M}=\{(x,f(x)):x\in{\cal U}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
where
f:{\cal U}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is such that
f\in C^{(2)}
. Let
\mathbf{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{d}
be such that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{d+j}}<1,\quad{\text{and}}\quad\min_{1\leq i\leq d}{a_{i}}>1.

Suppose also that

\displaystyle(a_{1}-1)+\dots+(a_{d}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{d+j}}=1.

(2)

For any
x\in{\cal U}
, there exists an integer
Q_{0}
such that for any
Q>Q_{0}
there exists
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}
with
1\leq q\leq Q
and
\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\in{\cal U}
such that

\displaystyle\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{4^{m/d}}{qQ^{a_{i}-1}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d,

(3)

and

\displaystyle\left|f_{j}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)-\frac{p_{d+j}}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}+1}}\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m.

(4)

Furthermore, for any
x\in{\cal U}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
, there exist infinitely many tuples
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}
with
\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\in{\cal U}
satisfying (
4
) and

\displaystyle\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{4^{m/d}}{q^{a_{i}}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d.

(5)

Proof.

Since
{\cal M}
is constructed via a twice continuously differentiable function
f:{\cal U}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, we can choose a suitable
{\cal U}
such that, without loss of generality, the following two constants exist:

C=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,k\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}{\sup_{x\in{\cal U}}{\left|\frac{\partial^{2}f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(x)\right|}}<\infty,

and

D=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq d\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}{\sup_{x\in{\cal U}}{\left|\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\right|}}<\infty.

For
1\leq j\leq m
, define

g_{j}=f_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}{x_{i}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}}.

Consider the system of inequalities,

\displaystyle\left|qg_{j}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{p_{i}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)}-p_{d+j}\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{Q^{-\tau_{j+d}}}{4}\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m,

(6)

\displaystyle|qx_{i}-p_{i}|

\displaystyle<\frac{4^{m/d}}{Q^{a_{i}-1}},\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d,

(7)

\displaystyle|q|\leq Q.

(8)

It is a consequence of Minkowski’s Theorem for Systems of Linear Forms (see, for example,
[
23
, Theorem 2C]
) that there exists a non-zero integer solution
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
satisfying the inequalities (
6
)–(
8
). More precisely, consider the matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}&\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\dots&\frac{\partial f_{1}}{{\partial}x_{d}}&-1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
g_{m}&\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial x_{1}}&\dots&\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial x_{d}}&0&\dots&-1\\
x_{1}&-1&\dots&0&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{d}&0&\dots&-1&0&\dots&0\\
1&0&\dots&\dots\ &\dots&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}.

It is straightforward to verify that
|\det(A)|=1
. Next we consider the product of the terms on the right-hand sides of (
6
), (
7
), and (
8
). Recalling condition (
2
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}{\frac{Q^{-\tau_{j+d}}}{4}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}{\frac{4^{m/d}}{Q^{a_{i}-1}}}\right)\times Q

\displaystyle=4^{-m}\cdot\frac{1}{Q^{\tau_{d+1}}}\cdots\frac{1}{Q^{\tau_{d+m}}}\cdot(4^{m/d})^{d}\cdot\frac{1}{Q^{a_{1}-1}}\cdots\frac{1}{Q^{a_{d}-1}}\cdot Q

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{Q^{(a_{1}-1)+\dots+(a_{d}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{d+j}}}}

\displaystyle=1.

Thus, it follows from Minkowski’s Theorem for Systems of Linear Forms that there exists a non-zero integer solution
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
satisfying the inequalities (
6
)–(
8
), as required.

Now, fix some
x\in{\cal U}
. Then, since
{\cal U}
is open, there exists a ball
B(x,r)
for some
r>0
which is contained in
{\cal U}
. Define

{\cal Q}=\left\{Q\in{\mathbb{N}}:\max_{1\leq i\leq d}{\left(\frac{4^{m/d}}{Q^{a_{i}-1}}\right)}<\min\left\{1,r,\left(\frac{1}{2Cd^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\right\}.

Since
a_{i}>1
for all
1\leq i\leq d
, we must have

\max_{1\leq i\leq d}{\left(\frac{4^{m/d}}{Q^{a_{i}-1}}\right)}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad Q\to\infty.

Thus, there exists some integer
Q_{0}
such that
Q\in{\cal Q}
for any integer
Q\geq Q_{0}
. We will show that for any
Q\in{\cal Q}
, a solution
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
satisfying inequalities (
6
)–(
8
) also satisfies inequalities (
3
) and (
4
).

Let us first deal with the case where
q=0
. By the definition of the set
{\cal Q}
, we have

\max_{1\leq i\leq d}{\left(\frac{4^{m/d}}{Q^{a_{i}-1}}\right)}<1.

Thus, if
q=0
, it follows from (
7
) that
|p_{i}|<1
, and hence we must have
p_{i}=0
, for all
1\leq i\leq d
. Subsequently, in this case, (
6
) yields

|p_{d+j}|<\frac{Q^{-\tau_{j+d}}}{4}<1\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m.

Hence, in the case when
q=0
, our solution must be the zero vector, thus contradicting Minkowski’s Theorem for Systems of Linear Forms. Hence we must have
|q|\geq 1
and, without loss of generality, we will suppose from now on that
q\geq 1
.

Dividing (
7
) by
q
, we obtain

\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{4^{m/d}}{qQ^{a_{i}-1}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d,

which coincides precisely with (
3
). Moreover, note that it follows from the definition of
{\cal Q}
that
\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\in B(x,r)\subset{\cal U}
.

Now we need to prove that the solution
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
satisfying inequalities (
6
)–(
8
) also satisfies (
4
). This conclusion follows via Taylor’s Approximation Theorem (see, for example,
[
21
, Chapter 9]
). In detail, since
\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\in B(x,r)\subset{\cal U}
and
f:{\cal U}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is twice differentiable, we can use the second order case of Taylor’s Theorem in
d
dimensions to conclude that

\displaystyle f_{j}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)=f_{j}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\left(\frac{p_{i}}{q}-x_{i}\right)}+R_{j}(x,\hat{x})

(9)

for some
\hat{x}
on the line connecting
x
and
\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)
where

R_{j}(x,\hat{x})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{\frac{\partial^{2}f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(\hat{x})\left(\frac{p_{i}}{q}-x_{i}\right)\left(\frac{p_{k}}{q}-x_{k}\right)}}.

Combining the above with our definition of
g_{j}
, we can rewrite the left-hand side of (
6
) as

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\left|qg_{j}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{p_{i}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)}-p_{d+j}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|q\left(f_{j}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\left(x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right)-R_{j}(x,\hat{x})-\sum_{i=1}^{d}{x_{i}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{p_{i}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)}-p_{d+j}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|qf_{j}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)-p_{d+j}-qR_{j}(x,\hat{x})\right|.

(10)

Suppose for a moment that

\displaystyle|qR_{j}(x,\hat{x})|<\frac{q^{-\tau_{j+d}}}{4}\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m.

(11)

With this assumption, it follows from the reverse triangle inequality combined with (
3.1
), (
6
), and (
8
), that

\displaystyle\left|qf_{j}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)-p_{d+j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|qg_{j}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{p_{i}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)}-p_{d+j}\right|+|qR_{j}(x,\hat{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q^{-\tau_{j+d}}}{4}+\frac{q^{-\tau_{j+d}}}{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{-\tau_{j+d}}}{2}.

Dividing through by
q
, we obtain,

\left|f_{j}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)-\frac{p_{d+j}}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}+1}},

which is precisely the inequality given in (
4
) which we were trying to establish. Thus, to complete this part of the proof, it remains to verify that we can indeed assume (
11
). To this end, write
a:=\min_{1\leq i\leq d}{a_{i}}
. By hypothesis, we have
a>1
. Upon recalling the definition of our constant
C
defined on page
3.1
, it follows from the definition of
R_{j}(x,\hat{x})
and (
7
) that, for each
1\leq j\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle|qR_{j}(x,\hat{x})|

\displaystyle=\left|q\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(\hat{x})\left(\frac{p_{i}}{q}-x_{i}\right)\left(\frac{p_{k}}{q}-x_{k}\right)\right)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{q}{2}\right|\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{\left(C\left|\frac{p_{i}}{q}-x_{i}\right|\left|\frac{p_{k}}{q}-x_{k}\right|\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{qCd^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{4^{m/d}}{qQ^{a-1}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{Cd^{2}4^{2m/d}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{q}\cdot\frac{1}{Q^{2(a-1)}}.

Since
\tau_{j+d}<1
, the inequality (
11
) would follow if we could show

\displaystyle\frac{Cd^{2}4^{2m/d}}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{Q^{2(a-1)}}<\frac{1}{4}.

(12)

Now, since
Q\in{\cal Q}
, the inequality (
12
) follows immediately from the definition of
{\cal Q}
. Hence this part of the proof is complete.

Finally, it remains to prove the second part of the theorem, that for any
x\in{\cal U}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
, there exist infinitely many tuples
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{N}}
with
\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\in{\cal U}
which satisfy (
4
) and (
5
). We will argue by contradiction so let us suppose for a moment that there are only finitely many solutions and denote by
{\cal A}
the set of all solutions
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n+1}
. Since
x\in{\cal U}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
, there must be some
1\leq k\leq d
such that
x_{k}\notin{\mathbb{Q}}
. For such
k
, there exists some
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\delta\leq\min_{(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{{\cal A}}}{|qx_{k}-p_{k}|}.

(13)

Since each
Q\in{\cal Q}
corresponds to a solution in
{\cal A}
, and
{\cal Q}
is infinite while
{\cal A}
is finite, there must exist one solution
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n},q)\in{\cal A}
which corresponds to infinitely many
Q
, say
\{Q_{\ell}\}_{{\ell}\geq 1}
. Thus, for all
\ell\geq 1
, there exists
1\leq q\leq Q_{\ell}
such that

|qx_{k}-p_{k}|<\frac{4^{m/d}}{Q_{\ell}^{a_{k}-1}}\leq\frac{4^{m/d}}{q^{a_{k}-1}}.

Since
a_{k}>1
, the middle term above tends to
0
which yields a contradiction to (
13
). Thus, we conclude that for any
x\in{\cal U}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
there are infinitely many solutions to (
5
) also satisfying (
4
).
∎

3.2
Preparing to use the mass transference principle for rectangles

We now turn our attention to constructing a suitable full measure
\limsup
set to which we will later apply the mass transference principle for rectangles (Theorem
2.1
). Following again the same line of reasoning as in
[
6
]
, we begin by defining

\displaystyle{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})=

\displaystyle\left\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1}:\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\in{\cal U}\text{ and}\ \left\|qf\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\right\|<\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}}},\ \ 1\leq j\leq m\right\}.

Note that from Theorem
3.1
we have that for all
x\in{\cal U}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
, that is for almost all
x\in{\cal U}
, there are infinitely many different vectors
(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})
for which

\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{4^{m/d}}{q^{a_{i}}},\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d,

where

(a_{1}-1)+\dots+(a_{d}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{d+j}}=1,\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{1\leq i\leq d}{a_{i}}>1.

Writing

B_{(\mathbf{p},q)}(c)=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{c}{q^{a_{i}}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d\right\}

with
c=4^{m/d}
, by Theorem
3.1
we have

\lambda_{d}\left(\limsup_{(\mathbf{p},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})}{B_{(\mathbf{p},q)}(c)}\right)=\lambda_{d}({\cal U}).

Eventually we will apply the mass transference principle for rectangles (Theorem
2.1
) to the full measure
\limsup
set

\limsup_{(\mathbf{p},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})}{B_{(\mathbf{p},q)}(c)}.

First, however, we will establish some other necessary preliminaries. For some constant
c^{\prime}>0
, let us write

W^{\prime}:=\left\{(x,f(x))\in{\cal U}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:x\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}{B\left(\frac{p_{i}}{q},\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{\tau_{i}+1}}\right)}\quad\text{for i.m. }(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})\right\}.

For
c^{\prime}
sufficiently small such that

Dc^{\prime}d<\frac{1}{4}

we will show that

\displaystyle W^{\prime}

\displaystyle\subset W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})\cap{\cal M}.

(14)

The consequence of this is that it will be sufficient for us to consider the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\displaystyle W:=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:x\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}{B\left(\frac{p_{i}}{q},\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{\tau_{i}+1}}\right)}\quad\text{for i.m. }(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})\right\}.

(15)

To see this, suppose for a moment that (
14
) is true. Then, a lower bound on the Hausdorff dimension of the set
W^{\prime}
will automatically yield a lower bound for
\dimh{(W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})\cap{\cal M})}
, which is what we are actually interested in. Next, let

\displaystyle\pi_{d}(W^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\{x\in{\cal U}:(x,f(x))\in W^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{\tau_{i}+1}},\quad 1\leq i\leq d,\quad\text{for i.m. }(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\tau)\right\}

be the orthogonal projection of
W^{\prime}
onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and note that
\pi_{d}(W^{\prime})=W
. We recall that Hausdorff dimension is preserved under bi-Lipschitz mappings (see, for example,
[
13
, Corollary 2.4]
). Since
\pi_{d}
, being an orthogonal projection, is such a mapping, we have

\dimh{W}=\dimh{\pi_{d}(W^{\prime})}=\dimh{W^{\prime}}

and so we conclude that it is sufficient for us to find a lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension of the set
W
.

In order to show the inclusion in (
14
), let us suppose that
(x,f(x))\in W^{\prime}
and let
(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})
be such that

x\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}{B\left(\frac{p_{i}}{q},\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{\tau_{i}+1}}\right)}.

To prove the containment, we need to show that for such
x
, we also have

\|qf_{j}(x)\|<q^{-\tau_{j+d}}\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m.

To do this, we will again use Taylor’s Approximation Theorem. Recalling our earlier definitions of the constants
C
and
D
(see page
3.1
),
together with the definition of the set
{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})
, and the assumption that
\tau_{1}\geq\dots\geq\tau_{d}
, it follows from (
9
) that

\displaystyle\|qf_{j}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|qf_{j}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q},\dots,\frac{p_{d}}{q}\right)\right\|+\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)(qx_{i}-p_{i})}\right|+\left|\frac{1}{q}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{\frac{\partial^{2}f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(\hat{x})(p_{i}-qx_{i})(p_{k}-qx_{k})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\left|\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\right||p_{i}-qx_{i}|}+\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{\left|\frac{\partial^{2}f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{k}}(\hat{x})\right||p_{i}-qx_{i}||p_{k}-qx_{k}|}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}}}+Dc^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{q^{-\tau_{i}}}+\frac{C(c^{\prime})^{2}}{2q}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{q^{-\tau_{i}}q^{-\tau_{k}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}}}+Dc^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{q^{-\tau_{i}}}+\frac{C(c^{\prime})^{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{q^{-1-\tau_{i}-\tau_{k}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}}}+Dc^{\prime}dq^{-\tau_{d}}+\frac{C(c^{\prime})^{2}d^{2}}{2}q^{-1-2\tau_{d}}.

By choosing
c^{\prime}
small and letting
q
be sufficiently large we have

Dc^{\prime}d<\frac{1}{4},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{C(c^{\prime})^{2}d^{2}}{2}q^{-1-\tau_{d}}<\frac{1}{4}.

Thus it follows that

\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}}}+Dc^{\prime}dq^{-\tau_{d}}+\frac{C(c^{\prime})^{2}d^{2}}{2}q^{-1-2\tau_{d}}<\frac{1}{2q^{\tau_{j+d}}}+\frac{1}{4q^{\tau_{d}}}+\frac{1}{4q^{\tau_{d}}}\leq\frac{1}{q^{\tau_{j+d}}}

where the last inequality follows from the assumption that
\tau_{d}\geq\max_{1\leq j\leq m}{\tau_{j+d}}
.

3.3
Completing the proof via an application of the mass transference principle for rectangles

Recall the Monge parameterisation of the manifold (
1
). Since
{\cal U}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which can be expressed as a countable union of closed cubes, by the countable stability of Hausdorff dimension we are free to ask
{\cal U}
to be of the form
{\cal U}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\cal U}_{i}
with each
{\cal U}_{i}
being a finite closed interval in
\mathbb{R}
. Then we are in a position to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
by applying the mass transference principle for rectangles
(Theorem
2.1
) to obtain a lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension of the set
W
defined in (
15
). To this end, for some suitably chosen
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{d}
, we will consider the set

W_{\mathbf{a}}=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<{\frac{4^{m/d}}{q^{a_{i}}}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d\quad\text{for i.m. }(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})\right\}.

Provided that
\min_{1\leq i\leq d}{a_{i}}>1
and (
2
) is satisfied, it follows from the preceding arguments in Section
3.2
that
W_{\mathbf{a}}
is of full Lebesgue measure in
{\cal U}
. Thus, given
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{d}

we may apply Theorem
2.1
to
W_{\mathbf{a}}
to obtain a lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension of the set

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{{a_{i}+t_{i}}}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d\quad\text{for i.m. }(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})\right\},

where
c^{\prime}>0
is the suitably small constant we chose earlier in Section
3.2
. In what follows, we will always choose
a_{i}
and
t_{i}
such that
a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
and so

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\cal U}:\left|x_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\right|<\frac{c^{\prime}}{q^{1+\tau_{i}}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d\quad\text{for i.m. }(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},q)\in{\cal N}(f,\mathbf{\tau})\right\},

which coincides precisely with the set
W
from (
15
) which we are interested in. Thus, any lower bound for
\dimh(W_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{t}))
is automatically a lower bound for
\dimh{W}
and hence also for
\dimh{(W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})\cap{\cal M})}
.

We are now ready to apply Theorem
2.1
. We split the remainder of the proof into two cases:

Case 1:

\displaystyle{\tau_{d}\geq\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}}{d}}
.

This case has already been addressed in
[
6
]
. However, for completeness, we briefly sketch the argument here.
In this case, we let

a_{i}=1+\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}}{d},\quad\text{and}\quad t_{i}=(1+\tau_{i})-a_{i},\quad{\text{for all}}\ 1\leq i\leq d.

Note that we have
a_{i}>1
and
a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq d
. Also note that

(a_{1}-1)+\dots+(a_{d}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}=d\times\left(\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}}{d}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}=1.

Hence (
2
) is satisfied.

Next, following the notation of Theorem
2.1
, we consider the set

\mathcal{A}=\{a_{i},a_{i}+t_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}.

Arranging the elements of
{\cal A}
in descending order, since
\tau_{1}\geq\dots\geq\tau_{d}
, we have

a_{1}+t_{1}\geq a_{2}+t_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{d}+t_{d}\geq a_{1}=\cdots=a_{d}.

Subcase (i):
Suppose
A=a_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq d
. In this case, the sets
{\cal K}_{1},{\cal K}_{2},{\cal K}_{3}
appearing in Theorem
2.1
corresponding to this
A
are:

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}\geq A\}=\{1,2,\cdots,d\},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}+t_{k}\leq A\}\setminus{\cal K}_{1}=\emptyset,

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{3}

\displaystyle=\{1,\dots,d\}\setminus({\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2})=\emptyset.

So the corresponding “dimension number” obtained via Theorem
2.1
in this case is

\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2}}{1}+\frac{\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{3}}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{2}}{t_{k}}}{A}=d.

Subcase (ii):
Suppose
A=a_{i}+t_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq d
. We may suppose that
A>a_{1}
, otherwise the conclusion of Subcase (i) holds. Let
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq i
be the least index such that
a_{i^{\prime}}+t_{i^{\prime}}=a_{i}+t_{i}
. Then the sets
{\cal K}_{1},{\cal K}_{2},{\cal K}_{3}
appearing in Theorem
2.1
corresponding to this
A
are:

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}\geq A\}=\emptyset,

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}+t_{k}\leq A\}\setminus{\cal K}_{1}=\{i^{\prime},\dots,d\},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{3}

\displaystyle=\{1,\dots,d\}\setminus({\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2})=\{1,\dots,i^{\prime}-1\}.

The calculation which gives the corresponding “dimension number” obtained via Theorem
2.1
in this case is identical to the calculation in Subcase (i) of Case 2, as given on pages
3.3
–15, since these cases yield the same sets
{\cal K}_{1},{\cal K}_{2},{\cal K}_{3}
.

Case 2:

\displaystyle{\tau_{d}<\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}}{d}}
.

In this case, let
1\leq K\leq d
be the largest integer such that

\displaystyle\tau_{K}>\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}-(\tau_{K+1}+\dots+\tau_{d})}{K}.

(16)

We choose

a_{i}=\tau_{i}+1\quad\text{for }K+1\leq i\leq d,

and

a_{i}=\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}-(\tau_{K+1}+\dots+\tau_{d})}{K}+1\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq K.

We set

t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}-a_{i}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq d.

Note that we have
a_{i}>1
and
a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq d
in this case. We also note that

\displaystyle(a_{1}-1)+\dots+(a_{d}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}

\displaystyle=K\left(\frac{1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}-(\tau_{K+1}+\dots+\tau_{d})}{K}\right)+\sum_{k=K+1}^{d}{\tau_{k}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}-(\tau_{K+1}+\dots+\tau_{d})+\sum_{k=K+1}^{d}{\tau_{k}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}

\displaystyle=1,

so (
2
) holds.

Following again the notation of Theorem
2.1
, we consider the set

{\cal A}=\{a_{i},a_{i}+t_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}.

Arranging the elements of
{\cal A}
in descending order we see that

\displaystyle a_{1}+t_{1}(=\tau_{1}+1)\geq\dots

\displaystyle\geq a_{K}+t_{K}(=\tau_{K}+1)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{K definition}}}{{>}}a_{1}=\dots=a_{K}

\displaystyle>a_{K+1}=a_{K+1}+t_{K+1}

\displaystyle\geq a_{K+2}=a_{K+2}+t_{K+2}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\vdots

\displaystyle\geq a_{d}=a_{d}+t_{d}.

To establish the “dimension number” obtained via Theorem
2.1
, we will consider three possible subcases. Throughout, we will frequently use the very useful observation that

\displaystyle t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}-a_{i}=1+\tau_{i}-(1+\tau_{i})=0\quad\text{when }K+1\leq i\leq d.

(17)

Subcase (i):

A=a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq K
.

Suppose
A\in{\cal A}
is such that
A=a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq K
and let
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq i
be the least index such that
a_{i^{\prime}}+t_{i^{\prime}}=A
. Then the sets
{\cal K}_{1},{\cal K}_{2},{\cal K}_{3}
appearing in Theorem
2.1
corresponding to this
A
are:

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}\geq A\}=\emptyset,

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}+t_{k}\leq A\}\setminus{\cal K}_{1}=\{i^{\prime},\dots,d\},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{3}

\displaystyle=\{1,\dots,d\}\setminus({\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2})=\{1,\dots,i^{\prime}-1\}.

Thus, when
A\in{\cal A}
is such that
A=a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq K
, the corresponding “dimension number” obtained by using Theorem
2.1
is

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2}}{1}+\frac{\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{3}}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{2}}{t_{k}}}{A}

\displaystyle=(d-i^{\prime}+1)+\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{i^{\prime}-1}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k=i^{\prime}}^{d}{t_{k}}}{a_{i}+t_{i}}

\displaystyle=(d-i^{\prime}+1)+\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k=i}^{d}{t_{k}}-\sum_{k=i^{\prime}}^{i-1}a_{k}-\sum_{k=i^{\prime}}^{i-1}t_{k}}{a_{i}+t_{i}}.

By the definition of
i^{\prime}
, it follows that
a_{k}+t_{k}=a_{i}+t_{i}
for all
i^{\prime}\leq k<i
. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2}}{1}+\frac{\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{3}}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{2}}{t_{k}}}{A}

\displaystyle=(d-i^{\prime}+1)+\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k=i}^{d}{t_{k}}}{a_{i}+t_{i}}-(i-i^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(d-i+1)+\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k=i}^{d}{t_{k}}}{a_{i}+t_{i}}

\displaystyle=(d-i+1)+\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{d}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k=i}^{d}{(t_{k}+a_{k})}}{a_{i}+t_{i}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{a condition}}}{{=}}(d-i+1)+\frac{\left(d+1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\tau_{j+d}}\right)-\sum_{k=i}^{d}{(1+\tau_{k})}}{1+\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=(d-i+1)+\frac{i-\sum_{k=i}^{n}{\tau_{k}}}{1+\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-i+1)(1+\tau_{i})+i-\sum_{k=i}^{n}{\tau_{k}}}{1+\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-i+1)(1+\tau_{i})+m(1+\tau_{i})-m(1+\tau_{i})+i-\sum_{k=i}^{n}{\tau_{k}}}{1+\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d+1+m)+(d-i+1+m)\tau_{i}-m(1+\tau_{i})-\sum_{k=i}^{n}{\tau_{k}}}{1+\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)+(n-i+1)\tau_{i}-\sum_{k=i}^{n}{\tau_{k}}}{1+\tau_{i}}-m

\displaystyle=\frac{n+1+\sum_{k=i}^{n}{(\tau_{i}-\tau_{k})}}{1+\tau_{i}}-m.

In the penultimate line above we used the assumption that
n=d+m
.

Subcase (ii):

A=a_{i}=a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
for some
K+1\leq i\leq d
.

Suppose
A\in{\cal A}
is such that
A=a_{i}=a_{i}+t_{i}=1+\tau_{i}
for some
K+1\leq i\leq d
and let
i\leq i^{\prime}\leq d
be the greatest index such that
a_{i^{\prime}}=A
. Then the sets
{\cal K}_{1},{\cal K}_{2},{\cal K}_{3}
appearing in Theorem
2.1
corresponding to this
A
are:

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}\geq A\}=\{1,...,i^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}+t_{k}\leq A\}\setminus{\cal K}_{1}=\{i^{\prime}+1,\dots,d\},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{3}

\displaystyle=\{1,\dots,d\}\setminus({\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2})=\emptyset.

Recall (
17
) that
t_{k}=0
for all
K+1\leq k\leq d
. The corresponding “dimension number” obtained in this case is

\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2}}{1}+\frac{\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{3}}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{2}}{t_{k}}}{A}=d-\frac{\sum_{k=i^{\prime}+1}^{d}{t_{k}}}{a_{i}+t_{i}}=d.

Subcase (iii):

A=a_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq K
.

If
A\in{\cal A}
is such that
A=a_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq K
, then the sets
{\cal K}_{1},{\cal K}_{2},{\cal K}_{3}
appearing in Theorem
2.1
corresponding to this
A
are:

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}\geq A\}=\{1,...,K\},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{k:a_{k}+t_{k}\leq A\}\setminus{\cal K}_{1}=\{K+1,\dots,d\},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{3}

\displaystyle=\{1,\dots,d\}\setminus({\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2})=\emptyset.

The corresponding “dimension number” in this case is

\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{1}\cup{\cal K}_{2}}{1}+\frac{\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{3}}{a_{k}}-\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{2}}{t_{k}}}{A}=d-\frac{\sum_{k=K+1}^{d}{t_{k}}}{a_{i}+t_{i}}=d.

Thus, we conclude from these cases that

\dimh(W_{n}(\mathbf{\tau})\cap{\cal M})\geq\dimh{W}=\dimh{(W_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{t}))}\geq\min_{1\leq i\leq d}\left\{\frac{n+1+\sum_{k=i}^{n}{(\tau_{i}-\tau_{k})}}{\tau_{i}+1}-m\right\},

and hence our proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.

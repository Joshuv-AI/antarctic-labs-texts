Remarks on Generalized Calabi-Gray Manifolds
Teng Fei

Abstract
Generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds are non-Kähler complex manifolds with very explicit geometry yet not being homogeneous. In this note, we demonstrate that how generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds can be used to answer some questions in non-Kähler geometry.



1
Introduction

Generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds are introduced in
[
Fei16
]
as new examples of complex non-Kähler manifolds with very accessible geometry. Their constructions depend on a nonconstant holomorphic map
\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and a hyperkähler manifold
M
. When
\varphi
is the Gauss map of an immersed minimal surface
Y
in a flat 3-torus and that
M
is of real dimension 4, we recover the classical constructions due to Calabi
[
Cal58
]
and Gray
[
Gra69
]
. Generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds have found their applications in solving the Hull-Strominger system
[
Fei17
,
FHP21b
,
Fei20
]
, the Anomaly flow
[
FHP21a
]
, and building new models in theoretical physics
[
CPS19
]
etc.

Noticeably, there has been an uprising interest in complex non-Kähler geometry in recent years. Many new ideas and conjectures have been proposed, and many exciting conferences are held. This particular piece of work is inspired by author’s discussion with his colleagues at one of the
Geometry & TACoS
workshops.

It turns out that generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds (with slight modification in some cases) can be used to answer many newly proposed questions in non-Kähler geometry.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we first recall the construction of the generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds and their relation with twistor spaces. Then we derive some existence and non-existence results about special metrics on generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds. In Section 3 and 4, we demonstrate that generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds (with slight modification if necessary) provide counterexamples to two questions in complex non-Kähler geometry. Along the way, we also prove the existence of balanced metrics on suspension of balanced manifolds with invariant volume forms and present a brief list of interesting questions.

The Generalized Calabi-Gray Manifolds

Let us recall the construction of generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds from
[
Fei16
]
, with some extensions to a broader setting.

Let
M
by a compact hyperkähler manifold, namely that
M
is equipped with a Riemannian metric
g
and three integrable complex structures
I
,
J
, and
K
satisfying the quaternion relation
IJK=-1
, such that all of
I
,
J
, and
K
together with
g
define Kähler metrics on
M
. In fact, one gets an
S^{2}=\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
worth of Kähler structures on
M
as follows. Let
\omega_{I}
,
\omega_{J}
, and
\omega_{K}
be the Kähler forms associated to
I
,
J
, and
K
respectively. For any point
(a,b,c)\in S^{2}=\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1\}
, the complex structure
aI+bJ+cK
defines a Kähler metric on
M
, whose Kähler form is simply
a\omega_{I}+b\omega_{J}+c\omega_{K}
.

It is very natural to consider the twistor space
Z
of a hyperkähler manifold
M
(see for example
[
HKLR87
]
), which encodes the whole
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
-family of Kähler structures on
M
. As a smooth manifold, the twistor space is simply the product
Z=M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. However,
Z
is equipped with a tautological yet twisted integrable complex structure
\mathfrak{I}
, which can be described as follows. For any point
z=(m,(a,b,c))\in M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}=Z
, we have

\mathfrak{I}_{z}=(aI+bJ+cK)_{m}\oplus j_{(a,b,c)},

where
j
is the canonical complex structure on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It is well-known that one can recover all the information about
M
from the complex geometry of
Z
. The twistor space
Z
has the following properties.

Proposition 2.1
.

(See for example
[
HKLR87
]
)

(a).

The natural projection
\pi:Z=M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is holomorphic.

(b).

For any point
m\in M
,
\{m\}\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\subset M\times\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}=Z
is a complex submanifold of
(Z,\mathfrak{I})
.

(c).

The bundle
\bigwedge^{2}F^{*}_{\pi}\otimes\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(2)
over
Z
has a global holomorphic section which defines a holomorphic symplectic form over each fiber of
\pi
. Here
\mathcal{O}(1)
is the hyperplane bundle over
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
F^{*}_{\pi}
is the relative cotangent bundle of the holomorphic fibration
\pi:Z\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let
2m
be the complex dimension of the hyperkähler manifold
M
, then we know that
Z
is a complex manifold of complex dimension
2m+1
. Regarding Hermitian metrics on twistor spaces, we have the following well-known results.

Proposition 2.2
.

(a).

Z
admits balanced metrics, which are Hermitian metrics such that their
2m
-th powers are closed.

(b).

Z
does not admit any Kähler metric. In fact more generally,
Z
does not admit any astheno-Kähler metric.

Proof.

(a).

The natural Hermitian metric
\omega
(the product metric) on
Z
takes the form
\omega=\omega_{FS}+a\omega_{I}+b\omega_{J}+c\omega_{K}
, where
\omega_{FS}
is the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It is straightforward to check that
\mathrm{d}(\omega^{2m})=0
.

(b).

Recall that a Hermitian metric
\omega
on a complex
n
-fold is called astheno-Kähler if
i\partial\bar{\partial}(\omega^{n-2})=0
. Clearly Kähler metrics are special cases of astheno-Kähler metrics. Suppose that
Z
admits an astheno-Kähler metric
\omega_{1}
satisfying
i\partial\bar{\partial}(\omega_{1}^{2m-1})=0
.Then by integration by part, we get

0=\int_{Z}i\partial\bar{\partial}(\omega_{1}^{2m-1})\wedge\omega=\int_{Z}\omega_{1}^{2m-1}\wedge i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega.

(1)

On the other hand, using the computation given in
[
Fei16
, Theorem 5.4]
or
[
DLDM17
, Theorem 3.2]
, we have

i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega=\omega_{FS}\wedge\omega,

hence
\omega_{1}^{2m-1}\wedge i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega
is a positive top form, contradiction!

∎

\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}

to
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}

\pi:Z\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}

\textstyle{X=\varphi^{*}Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}

Let
X
be a greatly generalized Calabi-Gray manifold. Very much like Proposition
2.1
, we have

Proposition 2.3
.

(a).

The natural projection
p:X\to Y
is holomorphic.

(b).

For any point
m\in M
,
\{m\}\times Y\subset M\times Y=X
is a complex submanifold of
X
.

(c).

The canonical bundle
K_{X}
of
X
can be computed as

K_{X}\cong p^{*}(K_{Y}\otimes\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2m)).

(2)

As a generalization of Proposition
2.2
, the following statements hold.

Proposition 2.4
.

(cf.
[
LZ17
]
)

(a).

If
Y
admits a balanced metric, so does
X
.

(b).

X
cannot admit an astheno-Kähler and a plurisubharmonic metric at the same time. In particular,
X
is always non-Kähler.

Proof.

(a).

Let
\omega_{Y}
be any balanced metric on
Y
. It is straightforward to check that the product metric
\omega_{Y}+a\omega_{I}+b\omega_{J}+c\omega_{K}
on
X=M\times Y
is a balanced metric.

(b).

Recall that a Hermitian metric
\omega
is called
plurisubharmonic
if
i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega\geq 0
as a (2,2)-form.

First, we show that
X
admits a plurisubharmonic metric if and only if
Y
does so. If
X
admits a plurisubharmonic metric, so do all complex submanifolds of
X
. In particular, by Proposition
2.3
(b),
Y
also admits a plurisubharmonic metric. On the other hand, let
\omega_{2}
be a plurisubharmonic metric on
Y
, then the metric
\omega=\omega_{2}+a\omega_{I}+b\omega_{J}+c\omega_{K}
is a plurisubharmonic metric on
X
.

Next, let us assume that there is also an astheno-Kähler metric
\omega_{1}
on
X
. Exactly the same calculation as in (
1
) yields the contradiction. Here we need that

i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega=i\partial\bar{\partial}\omega_{2}+\varphi^{*}\omega_{FS}\wedge(a\omega_{I}+b\omega_{J}+c\omega_{K})\geq\varphi^{*}\omega_{FS}\wedge(a\omega_{I}+b\omega_{J}+c\omega_{K}),

and that
\varphi
is not a constant so
\omega_{1}^{2m-1}\wedge\varphi^{*}\omega_{FS}\wedge(a\omega_{I}+b\omega_{J}+c\omega_{K})
is strictly positive on an open subset of
X
.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Complex manifolds admitting both an astheno-Kähler metric and a plurisubharmonic metric form a strictly larger category than Kähler manifolds. In fact, there exists a compact complex non-Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
with a Hermitian metric
\omega
satisfying
i\partial\bar{\partial}(\omega^{k})=0
for

. Such metrics are studied in

.

Definition 2.6
.

A

K_{Y}\cong\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(2m).

(3)

In view of (
2

is holomorphically trivial, in which case
X
is a non-Kähler Calabi-Yau manifold.

3
The First Question

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold. It is well-known that if the first Chern class of
X
vanishes in the real de Rham cohomology group, then its canonical bundle
K_{X}
must be torsion, meaning that a finite power of
K_{X}
is holomorphically trivial. It is natural to ask if a similar statement holds when
X
is non-Kähler, where we may work with a stronger assumption that the first Chern class of
X
vanishes in the Bott-Chern cohomology instead. It is noted in
[
Tos15
]
that Magnússon
[
Mag17
]
gives an example of a compact non-Kähler manifold with vanishing first Chern class in the Bott-Chern cohomology whose canonical bundle has infinite order.

As completely dropping the Kähler condition leads to Magnússon’s counterexample
[
Mag17
]
, one may ask what happens if we replace the Kähler condition by a slightly weaker one. In this spirit, Alexandra Otiman asks during the
Geometry & TACoS
workshop that if there exists a compact complex manifold with balanced metrics such that its first Chern class vanishes in the Bott-Chern cohomology while its canonical bundle is not torsion.

It turns out such an example exists by considering greatly generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds. To be more specific, we show that

Proposition 3.1
.

For a generic compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 5
, there exists a nonconstant holomorphic map
\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, such that the associated greatly generalized Calabi-Gray manifold
X
with
M
being a hyperkähler 4-manifold satisfies all the conditions in Otiman’s question, namely:

(a).

X
admits a balanced metrics.

(b).

c_{1}(X)=0\in H^{1,1}_{BC}(X;\mathbb{R})
.

(c).

K_{X}
has infinite order.

Because
Y
is a Riemann surface, we know that Proposition
3.1
(a) holds automatically due to Proposition
2.4
(a). For Proposition
3.1
(b), we show that it is essentially a topological condition:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a holomorphic map from a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
to
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let
M
be a hyperkähler 4-manifold. Proposition
3.1
(b) holds if and only if the topological degree of
\varphi
is
g-1
.

Proof.

As the Bott-Chern class of
c_{1}(X)
is represented by the curvature of
-K_{X}
, from (
2
) we know that

c_{1}(X)\in p^{*}H^{1,1}_{BC}(Y;\mathbb{R}).

Because
Y
is a compact Riemann surface, we also know that
H^{1,1}_{BC}(Y;\mathbb{R})\cong H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{R}
, therefore
c_{1}(X)=0\in H^{1,1}_{BC}(X;\mathbb{R})
if and only if
K_{Y}\cong\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(2m)
topologically over
Y
. Note that
m=1
since
M
is a hyperkähler 4-manifold, we see immediately that
K_{Y}\cong\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(2)
topologically if and only if
\varphi
is of degree
g-1
.
∎

Consequently, to prove Proposition
3.1
, we only need to show:

Proposition 3.3
.

For a generic compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 5
, there exists a holomorphic map
\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
g-1
such that
K_{Y}\otimes\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)
is of infinite order in
Pic^{0}(Y)
, the Picard variety of
Y
.

Proof.

The proof is based on the following facts from algebraic geometry.

(a).

[
ACGH85
, p.206]
For any compact Riemann surface
Y
of genus
g\geq 5
, every component of
W_{g-1}^{1}(Y)
has dimension at least
g-4\geq 1
. Here
W_{d}^{r}(Y)
is the variety that parameterizes all holomorphic line bundles of degree
d
with
h^{0}\geq r+1
on
Y
.

(b).

[
ACGH85
, pp.372-373]
For a generic compact Riemann surface
Y
of genus
g\geq 5
, a generic line bundle in
W_{g-1}^{1}(Y)
is base-point-free.

Consider a generic compact Riemann surface
Y
of genus
g\geq 5
, we take a generic line bundle
L\in W_{g-1}^{1}(Y)
. Since the linear series associated to
L
is base-point-free, the sections of
L
give rise to a holomorphic map
\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\cong L
. In particular,
\varphi
is of degree
g-1
and
K_{Y}\otimes L^{-2}\in Pic^{0}(Y)
. As torsion elements in
Pic^{0}(Y)
are countable and the dimension of
W_{g-1}^{1}(Y)
is at least 1, we see that a generic choice of
L
would make
K_{Y}\otimes L^{-2}
of infinite order in
Pic^{0}(Y)
.
∎

A remaining question is that does Magnússon’s example
[
Mag17
]
carry a balanced metric? Magnússon’s example falls in the broader construction called “suspension” that we shall describe. Let
M
be a complex manifold and
f
a holomorphic automorphism of
M
. Let
\tau
be a complex number with positive imaginary part. We can define a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action on
M\times\mathbb{C}
by

(1,0)\cdot(x,z)=(f(x),z+1),\quad(0,1)\cdot(x,z)=(x,z+\tau).

It is clear that this
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action is free, so the quotient manifold, denoted by
M_{f}
, is smooth and equipped with a projection map over
C=\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau)
.
Moreover, the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action preserves the natural complex structure on
M\times\mathbb{C}
so the complex structure descends to
X
and the projection map
\pi:M_{f}\to C
is a holomorphic fibration. Such a suspension construction has been recently studied in Qin-Wang
[
QW18
]
and Fino-Grantcharov-Verbitsky
[
FGV22
]
.

Regarding the existence of balanced metrics on
M_{f}
, we have the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
M
admits a balanced metric whose volume form is invariant under
f
, then
M_{f}
admits balanced metrics.

In the case of Magnússon’s example,
M
is a Kähler manifold with holomorphically trivial canonical bundle, therefore any Ricci-flat Kähler metric on
M
satisfies the condition in Proposition
3.4
. As a corollary, we conclude that Magnússon’s example provides an alternative manifold satisfying all conditions asked by Otiman.

It is clear that as a smooth manifold, we have
M_{f}\cong M^{\prime}_{f}\times S^{1}
, where
M^{\prime}_{f}
is the quotient of
M\times\mathbb{R}
by the
\mathbb{Z}
-action

1\cdot(x,z)=(f(x),z+1)

coming together with a projection
\pi^{\prime}:M^{\prime}_{f}\to S^{1}
. It follows that
\pi:M_{f}\to C
decomposes as
\pi=(\pi^{\prime},\mathrm{id})
under the obvious identifications
M_{f}\cong M^{\prime}_{f}\times S^{1}
and
C\cong S^{1}\times S^{1}
. Let
m
be the complex dimension of
M
, we have the following useful lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
3.4
, for any small open interval
I\subset S^{1}
, there exists a closed non-negative
(m,m)
-form on
M_{f}
such that it is strictly positive away from
\pi^{\prime-1}(I)\times S^{1}\subset M^{\prime}_{f}\times S^{1}=M_{f}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may think of
S^{1}
as the closed interval
[0,1]
with two end points glued together and that
0\in I
. In this way, we may think of
M^{\prime}_{f}
as the quotient of
M\times[0,1]
by the relation
(x,0)\sim(f(x),1)
. Let
(s,t)
be the standard coordinates on
S^{1}\times S^{1}=C
, and let
\rho(s)
be a smooth nonnegative function on
S^{1}
vanishing in
I
.

Let
\omega
be a balanced metric on
M
such that
\omega^{m}
is fixed by
f
. Consider the following nonnegative
(m,m)
-form

\Omega=\omega^{m}+\rho(s)\omega^{m-1}\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}t

on
M\times[0,1]\times S^{1}
. Since
\omega^{m}
is invariant under
f
and
\rho(0)=\rho(1)=0
, the above form descends to a non-negative
(m,m)
-form on
M_{f}
, with the desired property in the lemma. We need
\omega
to be a balanced metric on
M
to check that
\mathrm{d}\Omega=0
.
∎

To prove Proposition
3.4
, we simply take two non-intersecting open intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in
S^{1}
and apply the above Lemma to construct
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
respectively. Then
\Omega=\Omega_{1}+\Omega_{2}
is a closed positive
(m,m)
form on
M_{f}
, which is equivalent to a balanced metric. This construction gives a constructive realization of a special case in
[
Mic82
, Theorem 5.5]
without invoking verification of the “topologically essential” condition.

The Second Question and More

In his talk ‘‘Non-Kähler manifolds with vanishing first Bott-Chern class’’
3
3

3

at
Geometry & TACoS
, Tosatti asks the following question:

Question.
Let
X
be a compact complex manifold with vanishing first Chern class in the Bott-Chern cohomology. Is it true that holomorphic tensors on
X
must be parallel with respect to Chern connection of a Chern-Ricci flat metric?

The motivation of this question is that its Kähler version holds by applying the Bochner technique. By considering generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds, we see the answer to this question is negative. In particular, we have:

Proposition 4.1
.

There exist generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds with Chern-Ricci flat balanced metrics such that its nonzero holomorphic 1-forms (which exist in abundance) are not parallel under any connection.

Proof.

In fact, we can consider the simplest kind of generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds introduced in
[
Fei16
,
FHP21b
]
. These are generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds associated to
\varphi:Y\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
Y
is a Riemann surface of genus
g
, and that the hyperkähler factor
M
is either a K3 surface or a flat 4-torus. The canonical bundle
K_{X}
of
X
is holomorphically trivial as
\varphi
satisfies condition (
3
) that

K_{Y}\cong\varphi^{*}\mathcal{O}(2).

It is shown in
[
Fei16
]
that such a construction exists for any
g\geq 3
and that the natural Hermitian metric on
X
is balanced and Chern-Ricci-flat. Moreover, it is also shown that all holomorphic 1-forms on
X
are pullbacks of holomorphic 1-forms on
Y
, which forms a complex vector space of dimension
g
. Since these are abelian differentials on a Riemann surface of high genus, they all have zeroes and cannot be parallel under any connection.
∎

Let me end this paper by proposing and formulating some questions about (greatly) generalized Calabi-Gray manifolds of interest.

(a).

How to compute all the Hodge numbers of a generalized Calabi-Gray manifold? Some of them are discussed in
[
Fei16
]
. This question is highly related to computation of Hodge numbers of a twistor space, which is discussed in
[
ES93
]
.

(b).

This is a question asked my Misha Verbitsky. Consider the greatly generalized Calabi-Gray manifold
X
associated to
\varphi:T^{2}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
M=T^{4}
. In this case,
X
is topologically a 6-torus with a twisted complex structure such that its first Chern class is nonpositive but not zero. Is it true that any complex structure on
T^{6}
with zero first Chern class must be Kähler?

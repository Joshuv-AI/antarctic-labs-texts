Indecomposable characters on direct limit of symmetric groups with diagonal embeddings
N. Nessonov, N. T. S. Ngo

Abstract
In this paper we obtain the complete description of all indecomposable characters (central positive-definite functions) of inductive limits of the symmetric groups under block diagonal embedding. As a corollary we obtain the full classification of the isomorphism classes of these inductive limits. Keywords: Infinite symmetric group; Character; Factor representation.

1.1
The inductive limit of symmetric groups

In this subsection we define the group
\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
as an inductive limit of symmetric groups with the diagonal embedding.

We regard the group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
as the group of all bijections (symmetries) of the set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{i}(\sigma)(x_{1},x_{2})=(\sigma(x_{1}),x_{2}).

(1.1)

In order to define formally the inductive limit of groups

which correspond to the embedding (
1.1

of positive integers, where

for all

Suppose that

,
{}^{j}\!\!N_{k}=\frac{N_{j}}{N_{k}}

\mathbb{X}_{N_{j}}=\mathbb{X}_{N_{k}}\otimes\mathbb{X}_{(\!^{j}\!\!N_{k})}

Define the group

The group

(1.2)

For each

Proposition 1.1

\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
can be extended by continuity with respect to the metric
\rho
to a representation of
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
. Therefore, the following equality holds

\displaystyle A\xi_{0}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}\left(O^{-i}\right)\left(\,{}^{{}^{(O^{i})}}\!\!f_{i}\otimes\delta_{0}\right).

(2.21)

Hence, for any
f\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{X}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}},\nu_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)
we have

\displaystyle A\mathfrak{M}(f)\xi_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{mult_oper}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{M}(f)\,A\xi_{0}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}ff_{i}\otimes\delta_{i}.

(2.22)

We thus get

\displaystyle\|A\|^{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{X}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}}|f(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\,\nu_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}=\|A\|^{2}\;\|\mathfrak{M}(f)\xi_{0}\|^{2}\geq\int\limits_{\mathbb{X}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}}|f(x)|^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{i}(x)|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}\,\nu_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}.

It follows from this that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{i}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{X}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}},\nu_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)}\leq\|A\|^{2}.

In particular,
f_{i}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{X}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}},\nu_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)
and
\mathfrak{M}(f_{i})\in\mathcal{F}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)^{\prime\prime}
for all
i
(see Corollary
2.5
).
Hence, using (
2.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle A\xi_{0}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}\left(O^{-i}\right)\mathfrak{M}\left(\,{}^{{}^{(O^{i})}}\!\!f_{i}\right)\xi_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{covariance}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{M}\left(f_{i}\right)\mathcal{F}\left(O^{-i}\right)\xi_{0}.

(2.23)

The last equality, Lemma
2.3
and Corollary
2.5
imply that

\displaystyle A=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{M}\left(f_{i}\right)\mathcal{F}\left(O^{-i}\right).

(2.24)

Therefore, the equality
\mathcal{F}(O)A\xi_{0}=A\mathcal{F}(O)\xi_{0}
is equivalent to relations
\,{}^{{}^{O}}\!\!f_{i}=f_{i}
, where
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows from Lemma
2.2
that function
f_{i}
should be constant almost everywhere. In other words, there are constants
c_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
f_{i}\equiv c_{i}
almost everywhere and
A\xi_{0}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}\;\mathcal{F}(O^{-i})\xi_{0}
.

Finally, note that equality
\mathfrak{M}(f)\;A\xi_{0}=A\;\mathfrak{M}(f)\xi_{0}
,
f\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{X}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}},\nu_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)
is equivalent to

\displaystyle c_{i}\cdot\;f=c_{i}\cdot\,^{{}^{(O^{-i})}}\!\!f,\;\;i\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
f
is arbitrary, Lemma
2.2
implies that
c_{i}=0
for all
i\neq 0
. Therefore,
A\xi_{0}=c_{0}\xi_{0}
. By lemma
2.3
,
A
is a scalar operator.
∎

2.3
Construction of an irreducible representation of the group
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\times\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}

For the
{\rm II}_{1}
factor representation
\mathcal{F}
there is a corresponding irreducible representation
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}
of the group
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\times\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
acting in the Hilbert space

such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{F}^{(2)}\left(g,\operatorname{id}\right)=\mathcal{F}(g)\text{ where }\operatorname{id}\text{ is the identity of }\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}},\;g\in\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}};\\
&\mathcal{F}^{(2)}(g,g)\xi_{0}=\xi_{0}\text{ for all }g\in\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\text{ and }\mathcal{F}^{(2)}\left(\operatorname{id},\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)\subset\mathcal{F}\left(\overline{\mathfrak{S}}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)^{\prime};\\
&\mathcal{F}^{(2)}\left(\operatorname{id},O^{k}\right)=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(O^{k})\text{ (see \eqref{right_component})}.\end{aligned}

(2.25)

In order to define
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}
let us introduce the antiunitary operator
\mathcal{J}
acting on

as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}\eta\right)(x,m)=\overline{\eta\left(O^{m}x,-m\right)},\;\eta\in\mathcal{H}=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{X}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}},\nu_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)\otimes l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Then direct calculations show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\,\mathcal{F}(g)\xi_{0}=\mathcal{F}\left(g^{-1}\right)\xi_{0}=\left(\mathcal{F}(g)\right)^{*}\xi_{0},\;g\in\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}};

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{J}\eta,\mathcal{J}\zeta\right)=\left(\zeta,\eta\right)\text{ for all }\zeta,\eta\in\mathcal{H}.

Combining (
2.7
), (
2.11
), (
2.18
) and (

) we obtain

These equalities, Lemma
2.3
and Corollary

imply that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{F}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{F}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)^{\prime}.

satisfies conditions (

).

Proposition 2.7
.

The representation
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}
is irreducible.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
2.6
it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\mathcal{F}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{F}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)^{\prime}.

(2.29)

Denote for convenience the factor
\mathcal{F}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}\right)^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
as
M
. Consider arbitrary bounded operators
A^{\prime}
and
B
such that
A^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
and
B\in\mathcal{J}M^{\prime}\mathcal{J}
.
In order to prove (
2.29
) it is enough to check that

\displaystyle A^{\prime}B=BA^{\prime}.

(2.30)

According to Corollary
2.5
vector
\xi_{0}
is a cyclic vector for
M
. Hence, there is a sequence
\left\{B_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset M
such that

\displaystyle\left\|B\xi_{0}-B_{n}\xi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

(2.31)

In particular,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{l,m\to\infty}\left\|B_{l}\xi_{0}-B_{m}\xi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

(2.32)

Since
\left(UV\xi_{0},\xi_{0}\right)=\left(VU\xi_{0},\xi_{0}\right)
for all
U,V\in M
we have

\displaystyle\left\|B_{l}^{*}\xi_{0}-B_{m}^{*}\xi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\|B_{l}\xi_{0}-B_{m}\xi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.33)

Relations (
2.32
), (
2.33
) and equalities
V^{\prime}B_{n}=B_{n}V^{\prime}
imply that the sequence
B_{n}^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}
is a Cauchy sequence for any
V^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
. Namely,

\displaystyle\lim_{l,m\to\infty}\left\|B_{l}^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}-B_{m}^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\lim_{l,m\to\infty}\|V^{\prime}(B_{l}^{*}\xi_{0}-B_{m}^{*}\xi_{0})\|=0.

Therefore, we can define the linear operators
\widehat{B}
and
B^{\sharp}
as follows

\displaystyle\widehat{B}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}V^{\prime}\xi_{0},\;B^{\sharp}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}=\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n}^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0},\;\;V^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}.

Denote by
\mathcal{D}\left(\widehat{B}\right)
and
\mathcal{D}\left(B^{\sharp}\right)
the domains of the operators
\mathcal{D}\left(\widehat{B}\right)
and
\mathcal{D}\left(B^{\sharp}\right)
. It is clear that
M^{\prime}\xi_{0}\subset\mathcal{D}\left(\widehat{B}\right)

and
M^{\prime}\xi_{0}\subset\mathcal{D}\left(B^{\sharp}\right)
. For any
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}M\mathcal{J}\subset M^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, we have
W^{\prime}B=BW^{\prime}
. Now, it follows from (
2.31
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left(BW^{\prime}\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)&=\left(W^{\prime}B\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{B_approximation}}}{{=}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(W^{\prime}B_{n}\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(B_{n}W^{\prime}\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(W^{\prime}\xi_{0},B_{n}^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\\
&=\left(W^{\prime}\xi_{0},B^{\sharp}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right).\end{aligned}

Thus,
\left(W^{\prime}\xi_{0},B^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\left(W^{\prime}\xi_{0},B^{\sharp}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)
for all
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}M\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
(see Lemma
2.3
). By Lemma
2.3
the set
\left\{W^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right\}_{W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}M\mathcal{J}}
is norm dense in
\mathcal{H}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(\eta,B^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\left(\eta,B^{\sharp}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)

(2.34)

for all
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
and
V^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
.
Hence, for arbitrary
U^{\prime},V^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}M\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left(A^{\prime}BU^{\prime}\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)&=\left(A^{\prime}U^{\prime}B\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(A^{\prime}U^{\prime}B_{n}\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(B_{n}A^{\prime}U^{\prime}\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(A^{\prime}U^{\prime}\xi_{0},B_{n}^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)\\
&=\left(A^{\prime}U^{\prime}\xi_{0},B^{\sharp}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equality_conju}}}{{=}}=\left(A^{\prime}U^{\prime}\xi_{0},B^{*}V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right)=\left(BA^{\prime}U^{\prime}\xi_{0},V^{\prime}\xi_{0}\right).\end{aligned}

This proves the equality (
2.30
) and concludes the proof.

3
Preliminaries about the representation theory of the symmetric groups

In this section we remind some notions and facts from the representation theory of the symmetric groups
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
which are used in the proof of the main theorem.

3.1
The minimal element of the conjugacy class

Consider the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{m}}
the conjugacy class of the group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
, which consists of permutations of the cycle type
\mathfrak{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{N})
, where
m_{i}
is the number of independent cycles of length
i
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
support
of a permutation
s\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
is the subset
\operatorname{supp}_{{}_{N}}\,s=\left\{x\in\mathbb{X}_{N}\mid sx\neq x\right\}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Further, we also use the notation
\mathfrak{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{l})
for a cycle type
\mathfrak{m}
instead of
\mathfrak{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{N})
if
m_{l+1}=\ldots=m_{N}=0
(here
l<N
).

Clearly, for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{m}}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{N}
we have
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}i\cdot m_{i}=N
and
\#\operatorname{supp}_{{}_{N}}\,s\,=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{N}i\,\cdot m_{i}
.
For any distinct elements
n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{j-1}\in\mathbb{X}_{N}
denote by
(n_{0}\;n_{1}\;\ldots\;n_{j-1})
the cyclic permutation
c\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
such that
c(n_{i})=n_{i+1({\rm mod}\,j)}
. Denote by
s_{i}
the transposition
(i\;i+1)
. The elements
\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{N-1}\}
are also known as the
Coxeter generators
of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Consider a conjugacy class
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{m}}
of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
. Let
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{p}\}
, where
1<i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{p}
, be the set of those
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,N\}
for which
m_{i}\geq 1
. The
minimal element
of the conjugacy class
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the permutation
\sigma_{\mathfrak{m}}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sigma\!_{\mathfrak{m}}=&\left(1\;2\;\ldots\;i_{1}\right)\cdots\left(\left(m_{i_{1}}-1\right)i_{1}+1\;\;\left(m_{i_{1}}-1\right)i_{1}+2\;\;\ldots\;\;m_{i_{1}}\,i_{1}\right)\cdots\\
&\left(m_{i_{1}}\,i_{1}+1\;\;\;m_{i_{1}}\,i_{1}+2\;\;\;\ldots\;\;\;m_{i_{1}}\,i_{1}+i_{2}\right)\cdots\\
&\left(m_{i_{1}}\,i_{1}+\left(m_{i_{2}}-1\right)i_{2}+1\;\;\;m_{i_{1}}\,i_{1}+\left(m_{i_{2}}-1\right)i_{2}+2\;\;\;\ldots\;\;\;m_{i_{1}}\,i_{1}+m_{i_{2}}\,i_{2}\right)\cdots\end{aligned}

It is clear that
\sum\limits_{q=1}^{p}i_{q}m_{i_{q}}=N-m_{1}
and
\operatorname{supp}_{{}_{N}}\,\sigma\!_{\mathfrak{m}}=\left\{1,2,\ldots,N-m_{1}\right\}\subset\mathbb{X}_{N}
. The crucial property of
\sigma_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the following decomposition into the product of Coxeter generators:

\displaystyle\sigma\!_{\mathfrak{m}}=s_{j_{1}}s_{j_{2}}\cdots s_{j_{r}},

(3.1)

where
j_{1}<j_{2}<\ldots<j_{r}
are elements of
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
and
r=N-m_{1}-\sum\limits_{q=1}^{p}m_{i_{q}}
. The existence of such decomposition follows from the equality

(i\;i+1\;\ldots\;i+j)=s_{i}s_{i+1}\ldots s_{i+j-1}.

For any permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
denote by
\kappa_{{}_{N}}(\sigma)
the minimal number of factors in the decomposition of
\sigma
into the product of transpositions. Define the
sign (or signature)
of permutation
\sigma
as
\operatorname{sgn}_{N}(\sigma)=(-1)^{\kappa_{{}_{N}}(\sigma)}
.

Lemma 3.4

Suppose that permutation

Consider an arbitrary Coxeter generator

Otherwise, according to (

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}(s_{i})v_{T}=\pm\frac{1}{a_{i+1}-a_{i}}v_{T}+\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{(a_{i+1}-a_{i})^{2}}}v_{T^{\prime}},

(3.8)

where

Next, we show that for any

Thus, for any permutation

whose support does not contain

1,\penalty\ &\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Now, let us apply this observation to
(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}(\sigma)v_{T},v_{T})

4
The approximation theorem for the characters on
\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}

In this section we state and prove the approximation theorem for characters on the group
\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
which is a crucial part of the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Firstly, let us recall the definition of a character.

Definition 4.1
.

A function
\chi\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
on a group
G
is called a
character
if the following conditions hold

(a)

\chi
is
central
, i.e.
\chi(gh)=\chi(hg)
for all
g,h\in G
;

(b)

\chi
is a
positive-definite function
, i.e. for any elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in G
the matrix
\left[\chi(g_{i}g_{j}^{-1})\right]
is a Hermitian and positive-semidefinite matrix;

(c)

\chi
is
normalized
, i.e.
\chi(\operatorname{id})=1
, where
\operatorname{id}
is the identity of the group
G
.

If additionally

(d)

\chi
is
indecomposable
, i.e.
\chi
cannot be represented as a sum of two linear independent functions that satisfy (a) and (b),

then
\chi
is called an
indecomposable character
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\pi_{\chi}
be the representation of
G
, obtained from
\chi
via the Gelfand-Naimark-Segal (shortly GNS) construction. Then the property (d) is equivalent to
\pi_{\chi}
being a factor representation.

The following fact is an analogue of the approximation theorem from
[
6
]
for the characters of the standard infinite symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

6 Each indecomposable character
\chi
on
\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
is a weak limit of some sequence of normalized irreducible characters of the groups
\mathfrak{S}_{N_{k}}
. Namely, there is an increasing sequence
\left\{k(l)\right\}_{l=1}^{\infty}
of positive integers and there exist the partitions
\,{}^{k(l)}\!\lambda\vdash N_{k(l)}
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\chi_{{}_{{}^{k(l)}\!{\scriptstyle\lambda}}}(g)=\chi(g)\text{ for all }g\in\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}.

Proof.

Consider the GNS-representation
\left(\pi_{\chi},\mathcal{H}_{\chi},\xi_{\chi}\right)
of the group
\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
acting in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
with the cyclic and separating vector
\xi_{\chi}
such that
\left(\pi_{\chi}(g)\xi_{\chi},\xi_{\chi}\right)=\chi(g)
for all
g\in\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}
. Denote by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi})
the set of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
. For any conjugation-invariant subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi})
define its commutant by

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi}):AB=BA\text{ for all }B\in\mathcal{S}\right\},\penalty\ \left(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}.

We denote by
\left[\mathcal{S}\mathfrak{V}\right]
the smallest closed subspace containing
\mathcal{S}\mathfrak{V}
, where
\mathfrak{V}
is a subset of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
.

Since
\chi
is a character, then according to the GNS-construction we have

\displaystyle\left[\pi_{\chi}(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}})\xi_{\chi}\right]=\left[\pi_{\chi}(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}})^{\prime}\xi_{\chi}\right]=\mathcal{H}_{\chi}.

(4.1)

For convenience we denote the
w^{*}
-algebra
\left(\pi_{\chi}(\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}})\right)^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
, which is a factor of type
{\rm II}_{1}
, as
M
. The faithful normal normalized trace
\operatorname{tr}
on
M
is a vector state defined by
\xi_{\chi}
, i.e.
\operatorname{tr}(a)=\left(a\xi_{\chi},\xi_{\chi}\right)
for

.
Denote by
L^{2}\left(M,\operatorname{tr}\right)
the Hilbert space which is the completion of
M
with respect to the norm which correspond to the inner product
<a,b>_{\operatorname{tr}}=\operatorname{tr}\,(b^{*}a)
, where
a,b\in M
.

From now on we suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}=L^{2}\left(M,\operatorname{tr}\right)
. Then we can assume that the operators
a\in M
acts by
left multiplication
; i. e.

L^{2}\left(M,\operatorname{tr}\right)\ni\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}a\eta\in L^{2}\left(M,\operatorname{tr}\right).

In this case the operators
a^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
are being realized by operators of the
right multiplication
by the elements of
a\in M

L^{2}\left(M,\operatorname{tr}\right)\ni\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}\eta\;\cdot\;a\in L^{2}\left(M,\operatorname{tr}\right).

Each element

defines an inner automorphism

of the factor
M

\displaystyle{\rm Ad}\,g(a)=\pi_{\chi}(g)a\pi_{\chi}(g^{-1}),\;\;a\in M.

The map

\displaystyle M\ni a\stackrel{{\,{}^{{}^{N_{k}}}\!\!E}}{{\mapsto}}\frac{1}{N_{k}!}\sum\limits_{g\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{k}}}{\rm Ad}\,g(a)\in M

(4.2)

is the
conditional expectation

[
10
]
which projects
M
onto the subalgebra

\,{}^{N_{k}}\!\!M=\left\{a\in M:{\rm Ad}\,g(a)=a\;\text{ for all }g\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{k}}\right\}.

If
\lambda\vdash N_{k}
and
P_{\lambda}=\frac{\dim^{2}\lambda}{N_{k}!}\sum\limits_{g\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{k}}}\chi_{\lambda}(g)\pi_{\chi}(g)
, where

is the dimension of the irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{N_{k}}
that corresponds to

belongs to the center of the finite-dimensional algebra

and

\,{}^{{}^{N_{k}}}\!\!E\left(\pi_{\chi}(g)\right)=\sum\limits_{\lambda\vdash N_{k}}\chi_{\lambda}(g)\,P_{\lambda}\;\text{ for all }\;g\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{k}}.

(4.3)

The conditional expectations
\,{}^{{}^{N_{k}}}\!\!E
are orthogonal projections in
L^{2}(M,\operatorname{tr})
. Since
\,{}^{{}^{N_{k}}}\!\!E\geq\,^{{}^{N_{k+1}}}\!\!E
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|\,{}^{{}^{N_{k}}}\!\!E(a)-\,^{\infty}\!E(a)\right\|_{L^{2}(M,\operatorname{tr})}=0\;\text{ for any }a\in M,

(4.4)

where
\,{}^{\infty}\!E=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\,^{{}^{N_{k}}}\!\!E
.
Since

4.2

\,{}^{\infty}\!E(a)=\operatorname{tr}(a)I\;\text{ for any }a\in M.

Thus, combining (

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sum\limits_{\lambda\vdash N_{k}}\left(\chi_{\lambda}(g)-\chi(g)\right)^{2}\operatorname{tr}\left(P_{\lambda}\right)=0\;\text{ for any }g\in\mathfrak{S}_{\widehat{\mathbf{n}}}.

(4.5)

Consider two sequences of positive reals
\left\{\epsilon_{l}\right\}
и
\left\{\delta_{l}\right\}
which satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\max\left\{\epsilon_{l},\delta_{l}\right\}=0\text{ и }\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\frac{\epsilon_{l}\,N_{l}!}{\delta_{l}}=0.

(4.6)

Now, using (

) we find for each

such that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda\vdash N_{m}}\left(\chi_{\lambda}(g)-\chi(g)\right)^{2}\operatorname{tr}\left(P_{\lambda}\right)<\epsilon_{l}\text{ for all }g\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{l}}\text{ and }m\geq k(l).

(4.7)

Put

, where

, and
\,{}^{k}\!\Lambda(m)=\bigcup\limits_{g\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{l}}}\Lambda(m,g)
. Applying (

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda:(\lambda\vdash N_{m})\&(\lambda\notin\,^{l}\!\Lambda(m))}\operatorname{tr}(P_{\lambda})>1-\frac{\epsilon_{l}\,N_{l}!}{\delta_{l}}.

(4.8)

This inequality and (
4.6
) imply that for each

there exists a partition
\,{}^{k(l)}\!\lambda\vdash N_{k(l)}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\chi\!_{{}_{\,{}^{k(l)}\!\lambda}}(g)-\chi(g)\right|\leq\sqrt{\delta_{l}}\;\text{ for all }\;g\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{l}}.

Since
\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\delta_{l}=0
we have
\lim\limits_{l\to\infty}\chi\!_{{}_{\,{}^{k(l)}\!{\scriptstyle\lambda}}}(g)=\chi(g)
for all

. Thus, the sequences

and

satisfy the required conditions. Proposition
4.2
is proved.
∎

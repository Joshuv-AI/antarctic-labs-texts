A monolithic divergence-conforming HDG scheme for a linear fluid-structure interaction model Thanks: G. Fu gratefully acknowledges the partial support of this work from U.S. National Science Foundation through grant DMS-2012031.
Guosheng Fu, Wenzheng Kuang

Abstract
. We present a novel monolithic divergence-conforming HDG scheme for a linear fluid-structure interaction (FSI) problem with a thick structure. A pressure-robust optimal energy-norm estimate is obtained for the semidiscrete scheme. When combined with a Crank-Nicolson time discretization, our fully discrete scheme is energy stable and produces an exactly divergence-free fluid velocity approximation. The resulting linear system, which is symmetric and indefinite, is solved using a preconditioned MinRes method with a robust block algebraic multigrid (AMG) preconditioner.

1.
Introduction

Fluid-structure interaction (FSI) describes a multi-physics phenomenon that involves the highly non-linear coupling between a deformable or moving structure and a surrounding or internal fluid. There has been intensive interest in solving FSI problems due to its wide applications in biomedical, engineering and architecture fields, such as the simulation of blood-cell interactions, the study of wing fluttering in aerodynamics and the design of dams with reservoirs. However, it is generally difficult to achieve analytical solution to FSI problem with its nonlinear and multi-physics nature. Instead, there have been extensive studies in its numerical solutions and an increasing demand for more efficient and accurate numerical schemes

[
8
,
12
,
14
,
26
,
38
]
.

Numerical methodologies for solving FSI problems can be roughly categorized into partitioned and monolithic schemes.
Distinct mechanisms in fluid and structure domains naturally suggest solvers using partitioned schemes
[
17
,
36
]
. This numerical procedure treats each physical phenomenon separately and allows the use of existing software frameworks that are well-established for each subproblem. However, the design of efficient partitioned schemes that produce stable and accurate results remains a challenge, especially when the density of fluid is comparable to that of structure due to numerical instabilities known as added mass effect
[
10
]
. The design and analysis of partitioned schemes to circumvent such problems has been an active research area in the past decade
[
10
,
18
,
32
,
3
,
6
]
.
An alternative to partitioned strategy is the monolithic approach, which solves the fluid flow and structure dynamics simultaneously using one unified fully-coupled formulation
[
27
,
43
,
39
]
. The boundary conditions on the fluid-structure interface will be automatically satisfied in the procedure. Monolithic schemes are usually more robust than partitioned schemes and allow more rigorous analysis of discretization and solution techniques
[
29
,
38
]
. However, monolithic schemes have been criticized for requiring well-designed preconditioners
[
23
,
35
,
2
]
, more memory and computation time since the whole system is solved in one formulation.

In this paper, we present a novel monolithic divergence-conforming HDG scheme for
a linear FSI problem
with a thick structure.
The fluid Stokes problem is discretized using the divergence-free HDG
scheme of Lehrenfeld and Schöberl
[
30
,
31
]
, and the structure linear elasticity problem is discretized using
the divergence-conforming HDG scheme of Fu et al.
[
22
]
.
We approximate the fluid and structure velocities together using a single

H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming finite element space, and we also introduce
a global (hybrid) unknown that approximate the tangential component of the
velocities on the mesh skeleton for the purpose of efficient
implementation.
A pressure-robust optimal energy-norm estimate
is obtained for the resulting semidiscrete scheme.
We then use a Crank-Nicolson time discretization, and the fluid-structure interface conditions are naturally treated
monolithically. Our fully discrete scheme produces an exactly
divergence-free fluid velocity approximation and is energy stable.

When polynomials of degree
k\geq 1
is used in the scheme,
the global linear system, which is symmetric and indefinite,
consists of degrees of freedom (DOFs) for
the normal component of velocity (of polynomial degree
k
) on the mesh
skeleton (facets), the tangential hybrid velocity (of polynomial degree
k-1
)
on the mesh skeleton, and one pressure DOF per element on the
mesh. The linear system problem is then solved via a preconditioned MinRes
method
[
41
]
with a block diagonal preconditioner which is of similar form as the uniform
preconditioner studied in Olshanskii et al.
[
37
]

for a generalized Stokes interface problem. We further use an auxiliary
space preconditioner of Xu
[
44
]

with algebraic multigrid (AMG) for the velocity block and a hypre AMG preconditioner
for the pressure block to arrive at the final block AMG preconditioner.
This preconditioner is numerically verified to be robust with respect to
mesh size, time step size, and material parameters.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce the spatial and temporal
discretization of the divergence-conforming HDG scheme for
a linear FSI problem with a thick structure.
We then present the block AMG preconditioner in
Section
3
.
The a priori error analysis of the semidiscrete scheme is performed in
Section
4
.
Numerical results are presented in Section
5
.
We conclude in Section
6
.

\displaystyle\;-(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}),\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{s}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s})\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{s}\cdot((\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$})\hbox{\boldmath$n$}+\mathsf{tang}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))\,\mathrm{ds},

\displaystyle(p^{f},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{f}=

\displaystyle\;-(\nabla p^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{f}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}p^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{f})\,\mathrm{ds},

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{s}=

\displaystyle\;-(\nabla(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}),\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{s}+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s})(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{s})\,\mathrm{ds}.

Combining these equations, we get

\displaystyle(\rho\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))-(p^{f},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{f}-(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$},q_{h}^{f})_{f}\;\;

\displaystyle+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))+\lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{s}-(\hbox{\boldmath$f$},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})\;

\displaystyle=\;(\rho^{f}\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f}-\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{f}+(\rho^{s}\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}-\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{s}-\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{s}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\int_{\Gamma_{h}}(\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{f}+\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{s})((\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}+\mathsf{tang}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})))\,\mathrm{ds}

\displaystyle=0,

where
we used the PDE (
1
), (
1
), and the
dynamic interface condition in (
2.1
).
This completes the proof of the equation
(
8a
)
.
∎

Lemma 2.2

(Stability for the semi-discrete scheme)
.

Let
(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{h}^{s})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}\times Q_{h}^{k-1,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1,s}
be the numerical solution to the semi-discrete scheme (
5
).
Then, the velocity approximation on the fluid domain is exactly divergence free:

(9)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f}}=0,

and the following energy identity holds:

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}E_{h}=-2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}))+(\hbox{\boldmath$f$},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}),

where

E_{h}:=(\rho\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})+\lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h})_{s}+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{h}))

is the total energy.

Proof.

Let us first prove the divergence-free property (
9
).
By the choice of the velocity finite element space
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k}
and
fluid pressure finite element space
Q_{h}^{k-1,f}
, we have

\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f}}\in Q_{h}^{k-1,f}
. Now taking

q_{h}^{f}=\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f}}
in equation (
5a
), we get

(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})_{f}=0.

Hence the divergence-free property (
9
) holds true.

Next, let us prove the energy identity (
10
).
Taking test function

(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})=(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})

in equation (
5a
), and using the divergence-free property
(
9
), we get

\displaystyle(\rho\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}))+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}))+\lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})_{s}

\displaystyle\;=\;(\hbox{\boldmath$f$},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}).

Since
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}
and

\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{s}}\in\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k-1,s}
,
equations (
5b
)–(
5c
) implies that

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}=\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s},\text{ and }\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{s}}=\partial_{t}{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}}_{h}^{s}.

Hence,

2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}))=2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}_{h}),(\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h},\partial_{t}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{h}^{s}))=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{h}^{s}))),

and

\lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s})_{s}\right).

Combining the above equations, we arrive at the energy identity
(
10
). This completes the proof.

Remark 2.1

(Connection with the coupled momentum method)
.

The idea of using the same finite element space
for displacement and velocity approximations
to
eliminate the displacement unknowns in the global linear system was
originated in the coupled momentum method of
Figueroa et al.
[
19
]
,
where they considered an FSI problem with thin structure.
See also related work in
[
36
]
.

With the help of Lemma
2.4
, we proceed to implement
the fully discrete scheme (
11
) as follows:
Let
(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{0},\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s,0},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{h}^{s,0})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1,s}
be a proper projection of the initial data
in (
1h
).
For
j=1,2,\cdots
, we proceed the following three steps to
advance solution from time level
t_{j-1}
to time level
t_{j}=t_{j-1}+\delta t_{j-1}
:

(1)

Determine the time step size

\delta t_{j-1}
and compute the right hand side

F^{j-1/2}
in (
14b
).

(2)

Solve
for

(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{j-1/2},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{j-1/2},p_{h}^{f,j-1/2})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}\times Q_{h}^{k-1,f}
using equations (
14a
).

(3)

Recover velocity and displacement approximations
at time
t_{j}
using the relations (
14c
).

The major computational cost of the above implementation lies in
the global linear system solver in step (2).
To make the linear system problem easier to solve, we
introduce an equivalent
characterization of the solution to the equations (
14a
)
in Lemma
2.5
below. In the actual implementation,
we solve the equivalent linear system problem (
15
) in step (2)
instead of (
14a
).
In the next section, we will design
an efficient block AMG preconditioner for this system.

Lemma 2.5

(A modified implementation of the scheme (
14a
))
.

For any positive integer

at time
t_{j}=j\,\delta t

such that the following
equations holds:

(16a)

\displaystyle(\rho\mathtt{D}_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{j},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{j},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{j}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))-(p_{h}^{f,j},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{f}

\displaystyle\;\;-(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{j},q_{h}^{f})_{f}\;\;+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s,j}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s,j}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))+\lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s,j},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{s}

\displaystyle\;=\;(\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{j},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}),

(16b)

\displaystyle(\mathtt{D}_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s,j}-\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{j-1/2},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle\;=\;0,

(16c)

\displaystyle\langle\mathtt{D}_{t}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}_{h}^{s,j}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{j-1/2},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s}}_{h}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle\;=\;0,

for all

(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h},q_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h}^{s})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}\times Q_{h}^{k-1,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1,s}
,
where

\mathtt{D}_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}^{j}:=\frac{1}{\delta t}\left(\frac{11}{6}\phi^{j}-3\phi^{j-1}+\frac{3}{2}\phi^{j-2}-\frac{1}{3}\phi^{j-3}\right)

is the third order BDF
discretization of the time derivative
\partial_{t}\phi
.
We can proceed along the same lines as in Subsection

2.4.1
to implement the scheme (
16
) such that
we only need to solve a global linear system
of the form (
15
) in each time step.

3.
Preconditioning

3.1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we concentrate ourselves to the efficient solver for the
linear system problem (
15
).
The same technique can be used to solve the related linear system
for the scheme based on BDF3 time stepping (
16
).
To simplify notation, we remove all temporal indices in this section.
Hence the linear system problem we are interested in have the following specific
form: Find

(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},p_{h})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}\times Q_{h}^{k-1}
such that

(17)

\displaystyle(2\rho\frac{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}}{\delta t},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))+\delta t\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))

\displaystyle-(p_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})-(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},q_{h})-(\frac{2}{\delta t\,\lambda^{s}}p_{h},q_{h})_{s}\;\;=\;F((\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))

for all

(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h},q_{h})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}\times Q_{h}^{k-1}
. Note that all the
finite element spaces are defined on the whole domain
\Omega
.
To further simplify notation, we denote

\mu:=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$\mu^{f}$&on $\Omega^{f}$\\
$0.5\delta t\,\mu^{s}$&on $\Omega^{s}$\end{tabular}\right.,\text{ and }\gamma:=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$0$&on $\Omega^{f}$\\
$2/\delta t/\lambda^{s}$&on $\Omega^{s}$\end{tabular}\right..

We also denote

A_{h}^{\mu}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})):=2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))+\delta t\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})),

which is an HDG discretization of the variable coefficient diffusion
operator
-\nabla\cdot(\mu\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}))
on the whole domain
\Omega
.
Hence, the formulation (
17
) simplifies to

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\delta t}(\rho\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})+A_{h}^{\mu}((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))-(p_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})-(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},q_{h})-(\gamma p_{h},q_{h})=F((\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})).

The problem (
18
) can be rewritten in a matrix-vector formulation:
Find
[\underline{\mathsf{u}}_{h};\mathsf{p}_{h}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{u}+N_{p}}
such that

\displaystyle\left[\begin{tabular}[]{cc}$\mathsf{A}_{h}^{\mu}+\frac{2}{\delta t}\mathsf{M}^{\rho}_{h}$&$\mathsf{B}_{h}$\\[3.44444pt]
$\mathsf{B}_{h}^{T}$&$-\mathsf{M}^{\gamma}_{h}$\end{tabular}\right]\left[\begin{tabular}[]{l}$\underline{\mathsf{u}}_{h}$\\
$\mathsf{p}_{h}$\end{tabular}\right]=\left[\begin{tabular}[]{l}$\mathsf{F}$\\
$0$\end{tabular}\right],

where
\underline{\mathsf{u}}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{u}}
is the coefficient vector for the
compound velocity approximation

\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}:=(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}
with

N_{u}
being the dimension of the compound finite element space

\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}
,

{\mathsf{p}}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{p}}
is the coefficient vector for the
pressure approximation

{p}_{h}\in{Q}_{h}^{k-1}
with

N_{p}
being the dimension of the finite element space

{Q}_{h}^{k-1}
. Moreover,
the matrix
\mathsf{A}_{h}^{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{u}\times N_{u}}

is associated with the bilinear form

A_{h}^{\mu}(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})
,
the matrix
\mathsf{M}_{h}^{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{u}\times N_{u}}

is associated with the bilinear form

(\rho\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})
,
the matrix
\mathsf{B}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{p}\times N_{u}}

is associated with the bilinear form

-(p_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})
,
the matrix
\mathsf{M}_{h}^{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{p}\times N_{p}}

is associated with the bilinear form

(\gamma p_{h},q_{h})
, and
the vector
\mathsf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{u}}
is associated
with the linear form
F(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})
.
The big matrix in the linear system (
3.1
) has a block structure and is
symmetric and indefinite, with the 1-1 block

\mathsf{A}_{h}^{\mu}+\frac{2}{\delta t}\mathsf{M}^{\rho}_{h}

being symmetric positive definite (SPD),
and the 2-2 block
-\mathsf{M}^{\gamma}_{h}
being symmetric and negative semi-definite.

A popular method to solve the symmetric saddle point problem
(
3.1
), which we adopt in this work,
is to use a preconditioned MinRes solver
[
41
]

with the following block diagonal preconditioner

[
34
,
28
]
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}=\left[\begin{tabular}[]{cc}$\widehat{\mathsf{iA}}$&$0$\\[3.44444pt]
$0$&$\widehat{\mathsf{iS}}$\end{tabular}\right]

where
\widehat{\mathsf{iA}}
is an appropriate preconditioner of the SPD matrix

\mathsf{A}:=\mathsf{A}_{h}^{\mu}+\frac{2}{\delta t}\mathsf{M}^{\rho}_{h},

and

\widehat{\mathsf{iS}}
is an appropriate preconditioner of the
(dense) Schur complement SPD matrix

\mathsf{S}:=\mathsf{B}_{h}^{T}(\mathsf{A}_{h}^{\mu}+\frac{2}{\delta t}\mathsf{M}^{\rho}_{h})^{-1}\mathsf{B}_{h}+\mathsf{M}_{h}^{\gamma}.

The detailed construction of the
preconditioner for the Schur complement (pressure) matrix
\mathsf{S}
is discussed in Subsection
3.2
, where
we borrow ideas in the literature on preconditioning
the closely related, generalized Stokes problem
[
9
,
15
,
37
]
.
The detailed construction of the
preconditioner for the SPD velocity matrix
\mathsf{A}

is discussed in Subsection
3.3
, where
we use
an auxiliary space preconditioner
[
44
]

along with algebraic multigrid.
We mention that for polynomial degree
k\geq 2
, the preconditioned
MinRes solver is applied to the static condensed subsystem of
(
3.1
), see the discussion in Remark
3.2
.

Remark 3.1

(Connection with a generalized Stokes interface problem)
.

The discretization (
18
), or the form (
3.1
),
is closely related to
a divergence-conforming HDG discretization of a
generalized Stokes interface problem (with a fixed interface)
with variable density
\rho
and variable viscosity

\mu
, c.f.

[
21
]
. The only difference between the divergence-conforming HDG linear system for
the generalized Stokes interface problem and the
current FSI problem is that the pressure block is zero for the
former, while it is
-\mathcal{M}_{h}^{\gamma}

for the latter, which is a symmetric negative semi-definite
matrix and
represents the compressibility of the structure.
A non-zero pressure block also appears in the
finite element discretization of the Stokes problem
using pressure-stabilized methods, or the linear elasticity problem with
a displacement-pressure formulation.

Remark 3.2

(Static condensation for
k\geq 2
)
.

When polynomial degree
k\geq 2
, we shall
solve the linear system problem
(
3.1
) using static condensation to locally eliminate
interior velocity DOFs and high-order pressure DOFs

[
31
]
.
The resulting global linear system after static condensation
consists of DOFs for the normal component of velocity
approximation (of degree
k
)
in
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}

and the tangential (hybrid) velocity approximation (of degree
k-1
)
in
\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}

on the mesh skeleton (facets), and
cell-average of pressure approximations (of degree 0) on the mesh.
We denote the compound velocity space corresponding to
the DOFs on mesh skeleton by
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k,\mathsf{gl}}
which is a subset of the compound space

\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}
. The global pressure space is
simply the space of piecewise constants
Q_{h}^{0}
.
The linear system after condensation has a similar structure as that in
(
3.1
) with a reduced size. We shall apply the precondioned
MinRes solver for the condensed system in this case.
For this case, the matrices
\mathsf{A}

and
\mathsf{S}
shall be understood to be defined on the
reduced spaces
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k,\mathsf{gl}}
and
Q_{h}^{0}
, respectively.

3.2.
Preconditioning the Schur complement pressure matrix

\mathsf{S}

The preconditioner
\widehat{\mathsf{iS}}
acts on the
piecewise constant global pressure space
Q_{h}^{0}
, and
is given as follows:

(27)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{iS}}=(\mathsf{M}_{h}^{\mu,\gamma})^{-1}+(\mathsf{N}_{h}^{\rho,\gamma})^{-1},

where

\mathsf{M_{h}^{\mu,\gamma}}
is the
weighted mass matrix associated with the bilinear form

((\mu^{-1}+\gamma)p_{h},q_{h})
on the piecewise constant
global pressure space
Q_{h}^{0}
,
and
\mathsf{N}_{h}^{\rho,\gamma}
is the matrix
associated with the bilinear form

(28)

\displaystyle(\gamma p_{h},q_{h})+\frac{\delta t}{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash{\partial\Omega}}\int_{F}\frac{\{\rho^{-1}\}}{h}[\![p_{h}]\!][\![q_{h}]\!]\,\mathrm{ds},\quad\forall p_{h},q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0},

where

\{\rho\}|_{F}:=\frac{\rho^{+}+\rho^{-}}{\rho^{+}\rho^{-}}
is the geometric
average of
\rho
, and

[\![\phi]\!]=\phi^{+}-\phi^{-}
is the jump of
\phi
on an interior facet
F
.
Note that the mass matrix
\mathsf{M}^{\mu,\gamma}
is diagonal and
its inversion is trivial.
Also, note that the bilinear form (
28
)
corresponds to the interior penalty discretization of the operator

\gamma p-\frac{\delta t}{2}\nabla\cdot(\rho^{-1}\nabla p)

with a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition using the piecewise constant space
Q_{h}^{0}
.
The jump term
in (
28
) was shown in
[
40
]
to be
spectrally equivalent to the operator

\frac{\delta t}{2}\mathsf{B}_{h}^{T}(\mathsf{M}_{h}^{\rho})^{-1}\mathsf{B}_{h}
when
the density
\rho
is uniformly bounded from above and below.
Hence,
(\mathsf{N}_{h}^{\rho,\gamma})^{-1}
serves as a robust
preconditioner for the (dense) Schur complement matrix

\frac{\delta t}{2}\mathsf{B}_{h}^{T}(\mathsf{M}_{h}^{\rho})^{-1}\mathsf{B}_{h}+\mathsf{M}_{h}^{\gamma}
.
In the actual numerical realization
of
(\mathsf{N}_{h}^{\rho,\gamma})^{-1}

, we use the hypre’s BoomerAMG preconditioner
[
16
,
25
]

for the matrix
\mathsf{N}_{h}^{\rho,\gamma}
.

We note that the
pressure Schur complement preconditioner (
27
)
was initially introduced for the generalized Stokes problem (constant
density, constant viscosity, and
\gamma=0
) by
Cahouet and Chabard
[
9
]
.
Robustness of this
Cahouet-Chabard preconditioner
for the generalized Stokes problem
with respect to variations in the mesh size

h
and time step size
\delta t
was proven in
[
5
,
33
,
37
]
.
It was then generalized by Olshanskii et al.
[
37
]

to the generalized Stokes interface problem
(variable density, variable viscosity, and
\gamma=0
).
While a theoretical proof of the robustness of the preconditioner in
[
37
]
for the variable density and viscosity
case was lacking due to the lack of regularity results for the stationary
Stokes interface problem, numerical results performed in

[
37
]
seems to indicate that the preconditioner is
robust also with respect to the jumps in viscosity and density
in large parameter ranges.
Hence, our preconditioner (
27
) can be considered as a
generalization of the one in
[
37
]
to take into account the
structure compressibility (
\gamma>0
on
\Omega_{s}
) in the pressure block.

3.3.
Preconditioning the velocity stiffness matrix
\mathsf{A}

The matrix
\mathsf{A}
corresponds to the divergence-conforming
HDG discretization of the elliptic operator
\frac{2}{\delta t}\rho\hbox{\boldmath$u$}-2\nabla\cdot(\mu\mathbf{D}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}))
. Here we propose to use
the auxiliary space precondioner
[
44
]
developed in
[
20
]
.
The auxiliary space is the continuous linear Lagrange
finite elements:

\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{cg}:=\{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}\in\hbox{\boldmath$H$}_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\;\;\hbox{\boldmath$v$}|_{K}\in[\mathcal{P}^{1}(K)]^{d},\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The auxiliary space preconditioner for
\mathsf{A}
is of the following
form:

(29)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{iA}}=\mathsf{R}+\mathsf{P}\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathsf{P}^{T},

where
\mathsf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{u}^{\mathsf{gl}}\times N_{u}^{\mathsf{gl}}}
is the (point) Gauss-Seidel smoother for the matrix

\mathsf{A}
, with
N_{u}^{\mathsf{gl}}

being the dimension of the reduced compound space

\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k,\mathsf{gl}}
,
the matrix
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{c}\times N_{c}}
is the matrix associated with the
following bilinear form on the auxiliary space
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{cg}
:

(\frac{2}{\delta t}\mathtt{u}_{h},\mathtt{v}_{h})+2(\mu\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\mathtt{u}_{h}),\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\mathtt{v}_{h})),\quad\forall\mathtt{u}_{h},\mathtt{v}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{cg},

where
N_{c}
is the dimension of
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{cg}
,
and the matrix

\mathsf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{u}^{\mathsf{gl}}\times N_{c}}

is associated with the projector

\underline{\Pi}:\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{cg}\rightarrow\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k,\mathsf{gl}}
, which is defined as follows:
for any function

\mathtt{u}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{cg}
, find

\underline{\Pi}\mathtt{u}_{h}=(\Pi\mathtt{u}_{h},\widehat{\Pi}\mathtt{u}_{h})\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k,\mathsf{gl}}
such that

(30a)

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{F}(\Pi\mathtt{u}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$})(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$})\mathrm{ds}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{F}(\mathtt{u}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$})(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$})\mathrm{ds},

(30b)

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{F}\mathsf{tang}(\widehat{\Pi}\mathtt{{u}}_{h})\cdot\mathsf{tang}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})\mathrm{ds}=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{F}\mathsf{tang}(\mathtt{u}_{h})\cdot\mathsf{tang}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})\mathrm{ds},

for all
(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k,\mathsf{gl}}.

Note that the projector is locally facet-by-facet defined,
and the transformation matrix
\mathsf{P}
is sparse.
For the numerical realization
\mathcal{A}^{-1}
, we
again use hypre’s BoomerAMG.

4.
Semidiscrete a priori error analysis

In this section, we present an a prior error analysis for the semidiscrete
scheme (
5
). To simplify notation, we write

\displaystyle A\lesssim B

to indicate that there exists a constant
C
, independent of mesh size
h
,
material parameters
\rho^{f/s}
,
\mu^{f/s}
,
\lambda^{s}
, and the numerical solution, such that
A\leq CB
.

We denote the following (semi)norms:

(31a)

\displaystyle\|(\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}})\|_{i,h}

\displaystyle:=\;\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}}\left(\|\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})\|_{K}^{2}+\frac{\alpha k^{2}}{h}\|\Pi_{h}(\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))\|_{\partial K}^{2}\right),

(31b)

\displaystyle\|(\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}})\|_{i,*,h}

\displaystyle:=\;\left(\|(\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}})\|_{i,h}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}}h\|\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$})\|_{\partial K}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(31c)

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s}}\}|\!|\!|_{h}

\displaystyle:=\;\left(\|\rho^{1/2}\hbox{\boldmath$v$}\|^{2}+2\mu^{s}\|(\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s}})\|_{s,h}^{2}+\lambda^{s}\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s}\|^{2}_{s}\right)^{1/2},

for
i\in\{f,s\}

and
(\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}^{s}})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1,s}
, where we denote
\|\cdot\|
as the
L^{2}
-norm on
\Omega
,
\|\cdot\|_{i}
as the
L^{2}
-norm on
\Omega^{i}
.
The inequality (
7
) implies the
coercivity of the bilinear form
A_{h}^{i}
with respect to
the norm
\|\cdot\|_{i,h}
.
We also have the following boundedness of the operator
A_{h}^{i}
:

(32)

\displaystyle A_{h}^{i}((\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}),(\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$w$}}_{h}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}})\|_{i,*,h}\|(\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$w$}}_{h})\|_{i,h},

for all

(\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}})\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}^{i}+\left(\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,i}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1,i}\right)
and

(\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$w$}}_{h})\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,i}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1,i}
,
where

\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}^{i}:=\{(\hbox{\boldmath$v$},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}}):\;\;\hbox{\boldmath$v$}|_{K}\in H^{2}(K),\;\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}\}.

We use the classical Brezzi-Douglas-Marini (BDM) interpolator

\Pi_{BDM}
[
4
, Proposition 2.3.2]
to project
u
and

\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}
onto the finite element spaces
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}^{k}_{h,0}
and

\hbox{\boldmath$V$}^{k,s}_{h,0}
. We denote
\Pi_{Q}
as the
L^{2}
-projection onto the finite
element space
Q_{h}^{k-1}
. Note that due to the commuting projection property, we have:

(33a)

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta^{s}$},q_{h}^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\eta^{s}$},q_{h}^{s})_{s},\quad

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}^{s}\in Q_{h}^{k-1,s},

(33b)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f}|_{\Omega^{f}}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{Q}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f})|_{\Omega^{f}}=0.

The following standard approximation property of the BDM projector

\Pi_{BDM}
and the
L^{2}
-projector
\Pi_{h}
onto

\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}
is well-known; see
[
30
, Proposition 2.3.8]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}\cap[H^{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})]^{d}
.
Then the following estimates hold:

(34)

\displaystyle\|(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}-\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$u$},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}}-\Pi_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$u$})\|_{i,*,h}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{2k}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{i}}\|\hbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{H^{k+1}(K)}^{2},

for
i\in\{f,s\}
.

To further simplify notation, we denote:

\displaystyle\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}})

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}-\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$u$},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}-\Pi_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}),

\displaystyle\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}},\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}})

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}-\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}|_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{s}}-\Pi_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}),

\displaystyle\delta_{p^{f}}

\displaystyle:=p^{f}-\Pi_{Q}p^{f}

,

\displaystyle\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}})

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}-\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$u$},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}),

\displaystyle\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}},\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}})

\displaystyle:=(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h}-\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}),

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{p^{f}}

\displaystyle:=p^{f}_{h}-\Pi_{Q}p^{f}

.

where

(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}},p^{f}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}_{h}})\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}}\times Q_{h,0}^{k-1,f}\times\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}}

is the solution to the semi-discrete scheme (
5
), with
the componound spaces denoted as

\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}}:=\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1},\;\;\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}}:=\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1,s}.

Lemma 4.2

(Error equations of the semi-discrete scheme (
5
))
.

We have
the following error equations for the semi-discrete scheme (
5
) :

(35a)

\displaystyle(\rho\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle-(\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{p^{f}},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{f}+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}}\right)+\lambda^{s}(\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{s}

\displaystyle=\;(\rho\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}}\right)+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}}\right),

(35b)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s})_{s}

\displaystyle=\;(\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s})_{s},

(35c)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h}^{s}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=\;\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{s}},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h}^{s}\rangle_{s}.

for all
(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}},q_{h}^{f},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s}})\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}}\times Q_{h,0}^{k-1,f}\times\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k,s}}
.

Proof.

By subtracting the semi-discrete scheme (
8a
) from the consistency result (
5a
), then adding and subtracting the above projectors, we can get the error equation (
35a
), where the commutative property of BDM interpolation (
33
) is used. Then (
35b
) and (
35c
) can be easily derived since we have
\partial_{t}\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}=\partial_{t}\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}
,
\partial_{t}\Pi_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}=\partial_{t}\Pi_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}
.
∎

Note that due to the same finite elements space of velocity and displacement
approximation in
\Omega^{s}
, the error equations (
35b
) and
(
35c
) actually imply that

\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}}=\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}},\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{s}}=\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}}
.
Now we are ready to present the main result in this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}},p_{h}^{f},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}_{h}^{s}})
be the solution
to semi-discrete scheme (
5
) with initial data such that

\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}(0),\varepsilon_{p^{f}}(0),\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(0)\right)=\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$0$}},0,\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$0$}}\right)
.
Assume the solution
(\hbox{\boldmath$u$},\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s})
to the model problem (
1
)
is smooth.
Then the following estimation holds for all
T>0
:

(36)

|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}(T),\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\}|\!|\!|_{h}^{2}+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}\lesssim\;h^{2k}\left(\text{\Large$\Xi$}_{1}+\text{\Large$\Xi$}_{2}+\text{\Large$\Xi$}_{3}\right),

where

\displaystyle\text{\Large$\Xi$}_{1}

\displaystyle:=T\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\rho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)}^{2}+\mu^{s}\|\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega^{s})}\right)\mathrm{dt},

\displaystyle\text{\Large$\Xi$}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\hbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega^{f})}^{2}\mathrm{dt},

\displaystyle\text{\Large$\Xi$}_{3}

\displaystyle:=\mu^{s}\|\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}\left(H^{k+1}(\Omega^{s})\right)}.

Proof.

Here we use the standard energy argument. Take
(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}},q_{h}^{f})=(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}},\varepsilon_{p^{f}})
in error equation (
35a
) and plug in
\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}}=\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}},\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{s}}=\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{s}}
, we get:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left(\|\rho^{1/2}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|^{2}+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\right)+\lambda^{s}\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}\|_{s}^{2}\right)}_{:=\mathcal{H}(t)}+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\;(\rho\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}})+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\right)+2\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\partial_{t}\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\right),

where we used the exactly divergence-free property of
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f}
and

\Pi_{BDM}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f}
. By plugging in the right-hand side the chain rule for
the time derivative

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\right)=A_{h}^{s}\left(\partial_{t}\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\right)+A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\partial_{t}\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\right),

and then applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and boundedness of
A_{h}^{i}
(
32
), we get:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}\mathcal{H}(t)+2\mu^{f}A_{h}^{f}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\right)\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\rho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|\,\|\rho^{1/2}\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|+2\mu^{f}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,*,h}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}

\displaystyle+2\mu^{s}\|\partial_{t}\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\|_{s,*,h}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\|_{s,h}+2\mu^{s}\frac{d}{dt}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Theta^{1/2}|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\}|\!|\!|_{h}+2\mu^{f}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,*,h}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}+2\mu^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\right),

where
\Theta:=\left(\|\rho^{1/2}\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|^{2}+2\mu^{s}\|\partial_{t}\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\|_{s,*,h}^{2}\right)
. Integrate both sides over time from
t=0
to
t=T
, combined with
\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}(0),\varepsilon_{p^{f}}(0),\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(0)\right)=\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$0$}},0,\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$0$}}\right)
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(T)+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}A_{h}^{f}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\right)\mathrm{dt}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Theta^{1/2}|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\}|\!|\!|_{h}\mathrm{dt}+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,*,h}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}\mathrm{dt}

\displaystyle+\mu^{s}A_{h}^{s}\left(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T),\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\right).

Applying the coercivity and boundedness of
A_{h}^{i}
, and Young’s inequality, we get:

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}(T),\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\}|\!|\!|_{h}^{2}+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Theta^{1/2}|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\}|\!|\!|_{h}\mathrm{dt}

\displaystyle+\mu^{f}\gamma_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,*,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}+\mu^{s}\gamma_{2}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\|_{s,*,h}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu^{f}}{\gamma_{1}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}+\frac{\mu^{s}}{\gamma_{2}}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\|_{s,h}^{2},

for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
. The last two terms would be absorbed by the left-hand side when
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are big enough. Then we have:

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}(T),\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\}|\!|\!|_{h}^{2}+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Theta^{1/2}|\|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\}\||_{h}\mathrm{dt}

\displaystyle+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,*,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}+\mu^{s}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\|_{s,*,h}^{2}.

By applying the Gronwall-type inequality
[
11
, Propostion 3.1]
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get:

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}(T),\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}(T)\}|\!|\!|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\Theta^{1/2}\mathrm{dt}+\left(\max_{0\leq\mathrm{t}\leq T}\left(\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{t}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,*,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}+\mu^{s}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\|_{s,*,h}^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim T\int_{0}^{T}\Theta\mathrm{dt}+\mu^{f}\int_{0}^{T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}}\|_{f,*,h}^{2}\mathrm{dt}+\mu^{s}\max_{0\leq\mathrm{t}\leq T}\|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$\delta$}_{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}}}\|_{s,*,h}^{2}.

Finally, the estimate (
36
) is obtained by the above inequality
and the approximation properties of the projectors in Lemma

4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.1

(Robust velocity/displacement estimates)
.

It is clear that the velocity and displacement error estimate (
36
) is independent of the pressure approximation
p_{h}^{f}
and
the lame parameter
\lambda^{s}
.
Moreover, the error estimate (
36
) is optimal in the energy norm

|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_{h}
, which contains a discrete
H^{1}
-norm on
\Omega^{s}
.
On the other hand, we can only obtain a suboptimal convergence of order
\mathcal{O}(h^{k})
for
the
L^{2}
-norm of the velocity approximation from (
36
).
However, our numerical results in the next section indicate that the velocity
L^{2}
-norm
seems to be optimal. The proof of the optimality of the velocity
L^{2}
-norm is our
future work.

5.2.
Example 2: a linear two-dimensional test case

We consider a simplified linear version of the numerical experiment reported in

[
35
,
7
]
. We use the similar set-up as in
[
7
]
.
We consider a fluid domain,
\Omega^{f}=(0,6)\times(0,0.5)[\mathrm{cm}]^{2}
, and
a structure domain,
\Omega^{s}=(0,6)\times(0.5,0.6)[\mathrm{cm}]^{2}
, connected by an
interface
\Gamma=\{(x,y):\;x\in(0,6),y=0.5\}
.
We consider the FSI problem (
1
)–(
2.1
) with

\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{f}=\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{s}=0
, where we add
a linear
spring
term,
\beta^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}
to the
first equation in (
1
):

\rho^{s}\partial_{t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}+\beta^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}-\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{s}(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s})=0.

The material parameters are given as follows:

\rho^{s}=1.1[\mathrm{g/cm^{3}}],

\mu^{s}=0.575\times 10^{6}[\mathrm{dye/cm^{2}}],

\beta^{s}=4\times 10^{6}[\mathrm{dye/cm^{4}}],

\lambda^{s}=1.7\times 10^{6}[\mathrm{dye/cm^{2}}],

\rho^{f}=1[\mathsf{g/cm^{3}}],

\mu^{f}=0.035[\mathsf{g/(cm\cdot s)}]
,
which are within physiologically realistic
values of blood flow in compliant arteries.
The flow is initially at rest, and we take the following boundary conditions
which model a pressure driven flow:

\displaystyle(\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$n$})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}=

\displaystyle\;-p_{in}(t),\;\;\;

\displaystyle\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f})=\;0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on
}\Gamma^{f}_{in}:=\{(x,y):x=0,y\in(0,0.5)\},

\displaystyle(\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$n$})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}=

\displaystyle\;0,\;

\displaystyle\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f})=\;0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on
}\Gamma^{f}_{out}:=\{(x,y):x=6,y\in(0,0.5)\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$n$})=

\displaystyle\;0,\;

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}=\;0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma^{f}_{bot}:=\{(x,y):x\in(0,6),y=0\},

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}=

\displaystyle\;0,\;\;\;

\displaystyle\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s})=\;0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on
}\Gamma^{s}_{in/out}:=\{(x,y):x\in\{0,6\},y\in(0.5,0.6)\},

\displaystyle(\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$n$})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}=

\displaystyle\;0,\;

\displaystyle\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s})=\;0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma^{s}_{top}:=\{(x,y):x\in(0,6),y=0.6\},

where the time-dependent
pressure boundary source term at the inlet
\Gamma_{in}^{f}
is given as
follows:

\displaystyle p_{in}(t)=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$\frac{p_{\max}}{2}\left(1-\cos(\frac{2\pi t}{t_{\max}})\right)$,&if $t\leq t_{\max}$,\\
$0$,&if $t>t_{\max}$,\end{tabular}\right.

where
t_{\max}=0.03[\mathrm{s}]
and

p_{\max}=1.333\times 10^{4}[\mathrm{dyne/cm^{2}}]

The final time of the simulation is
T=1.2\times 10^{-2}[\mathrm{s}]

In this example, we use the divergence-conforming HDG scheme with

11
).
The additional spring term
\beta^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}^{s}

in the structure equation does not alter the
form of the resulting global linear system.
Hence we still apply the preconditioned
MinRes solver using the preconditioner (
3.1
)

29

27

Due to different boundary conditions, we shall add the boundary

\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{in}^{f}\cup\Gamma_{out}^{f}\cup\Gamma_{top}^{s}}\int_{F}\frac{1}{\rho\,h}p_{h}q_{h}\,\mathrm{ds}

28

\mathsf{N}_{h}^{\rho,\gamma}

27

and take the following continuous linear velocity auxiliary finite element space

\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{cg}:=\{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}\in\hbox{\boldmath$H$}^{1}(\Omega):\;\;\hbox{\boldmath$v$}|_{K}\in[\mathcal{P}^{1}(K)]^{d},\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\;\hbox{\boldmath$v$}|_{\Gamma_{in/out}^{s}}=0,\;\hbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}|_{\Gamma_{bot}^{f}}=0,\;\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$})|_{\Gamma_{in/out}^{f}\cup\Gamma^{s}_{top}}=0\}

in the velocity block (
29
).

For the discretization parameters, we consider
polynomial degree

a uniform unstructured triangular mesh with mesh size

6.
Conclusion

We have present a novel monolithic divergence-conforming HDG scheme for
a linear FSI problem with a thick structure. The fully discrete scheme
produces an exactly divergence-free fluid velocity approximation and
is energy-stable.
Furthermore, we design an efficient block AMG
preconditioner and use it with a preconditioned MinRes solver for the resulting symmetric and
indefinite global linear system. This preconditioner is numerically observed
to be robust with respect to the mesh size, time step size and material
parameters in large parameter ranges. A theoretical analysis of this
preconditioner is our future work.

The extension of our scheme to other FSI models including thin structure
and/or moving interfaces consists of our ongoing work.

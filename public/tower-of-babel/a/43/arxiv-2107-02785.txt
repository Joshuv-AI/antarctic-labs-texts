Abstract
. We study integral curvature conditions for a Riemannian metric g g on S 4 S^{4} that quantify the best bilipschitz constant between ( S 4 , g ) (S^{4},g) and the standard metric on S 4 S^{4} . Our results show that the best bilipschitz constant is controlled by the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the Weyl tensor and the L 1 L^{1} -norm of the Q Q -curvature, under the conditions that those quantities are sufficiently small, g g has a positive Yamabe constant and the Q Q -curvature is mean-positive. The proof of the result is achieved in two steps. Firstly, we construct a quasiconformal map between two conformally related metrics in a positive Yamabe class. Secondly, we apply the Ricci flow to establish the bilipschitz equivalence from such a conformal class to the standard conformal class on S 4 S^{4} .



1.
Introduction

A bilipschitz map between two metric spaces
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
is a map
f\colon X\to Y
for which there exists a constant
L>0
so that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq Ld_{X}(x_{1},x_{2}).

Let
g
be a smooth Riemannian metric on
S^{4}
.
In this paper we give integral curvature conditions which control the best bilipschitz constant between
(S^{4},g)
and
S^{4}
equipped with the standard spherical metric. We show that the dependence of
L
can be controlled by the conformally invariant
L^{2}
-norm of the Weyl tensor and the
L^{1}
-norm of the
Q
-curvature whenever those norms are sufficiently small.
The only non-conformally invariant quantity that controls the bilipschitz constant is the
Q
-curvature of the metric.

The conformally invariant Yamabe constant,
Y([g])
is defined as

\displaystyle Y([g])=\inf_{\hat{g}=e^{2w}g}\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}_{\hat{g}}(S^{4})^{1/2}}\int R_{\hat{g}}dv_{\hat{g}}.

We prove the following theorem for metrics with non-negative Yamabe constant.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g_{1}
be a metric so that
Y([g_{1}])\geq 0
. Let
g_{0}
be a metric in the conformal class of
g_{1}
.
Suppose additionally that

(1)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{4}}Q_{1}dv_{1}\geq 0,

where
Q_{1}
is the
Q
-curvature of
g_{1}
.
Let
w\colon S^{4}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the smooth function such that
g_{1}=e^{2w}g_{0}
.
There exists
\epsilon_{1}>0
such that if

\displaystyle\alpha=\int_{S^{4}}|Q_{1}e^{4w}-Q_{0}|dv_{0}<\epsilon_{1},

then there exists a quasiconformal and bilipschitz map
f\colon(S^{4},g_{1})\to(S^{4},g_{0})
whose bilipschitz constant depends only on
\alpha
and
g_{0}
. More precisely, the bilipschitz constant
L_{1}
of
f
satisfies

\displaystyle L_{1}\leq 1+C\alpha,

where
C
only depends on
g_{0}
.

We also prove a theorem that quantifies the bilipschitz constant of the identity map between
(S^{4},g_{0})
and
S^{4}
equipped with the standard metric.
The solution to the Yamabe problem in this setting guarantees the existence of a metric in the conformal class of
g
that has constant scalar curvature. This metric is often called the Yamabe metric.
The following theorem states that the best bilipschitz constant between the Yamabe metric of a conformal class and the standard spherical metric depends on the
L^{2}
-norm of the Weyl tensor.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g_{0}
be a Yamabe metric of constant and positive scalar curvature on
S^{4}
, let

\displaystyle\beta=\int_{S^{4}}|W|^{2}dv_{0},

and let

\displaystyle\gamma=\int_{S^{4}}|B|^{2}dv_{0},

where
W
is the Weyl tensor and
B
is the Bach tensor.
There exists a constant
\epsilon_{2}>0
such that if
\beta<\epsilon_{2}
, then

(S^{4},g_{0})
is bilipschitz equivalent to
(S^{4},g_{c})
, where
g_{c}
is the standard metric for
S^{4}
. That is, for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
there exists
L_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L_{2}}d_{g_{c}}(x,y)\leq d_{g_{0}}(x,y)\leq L_{2}d_{g_{c}}(x,y),

where
L_{2}\leq 1+C^{\prime}\gamma\beta
.

From these theorems we directly deduce the main theorem of the paper.

Theorem 1.3

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
g
be a smooth Riemannian metric on
S^{4}
with a positive Yamabe constant and let
g_{0}
be the Yamabe metric in the conformal class of
g
with constant scalar curvature. Additionally, suppose that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{4}}Qdv\geq 0,

where
Q
is the
Q
-curvature for
g
. There exists constants
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
so that if

\displaystyle\alpha=\int_{S^{4}}|Q_{1}e^{4w}-Q_{0}|dv_{0}<\epsilon_{1}

and

\displaystyle\beta=\int_{S^{4}}|W_{g}|^{2}dv_{g}<\epsilon_{2},

then there exists a bilipschitz map
f\colon(S^{4},g)\to(S^{4},g_{c})
, where
g_{c}
is the standard metric on
S^{4}
, such that
the bilipschitz constant
L
of
f
depends on
\alpha
and
\beta
in the sense that

\displaystyle L\leq 1+C_{1}\alpha+C_{2}\beta,

where
C_{1}
may depend on the conformal class of
g
and
C_{2}
only depends on the
L^{2}
-norm of the Bach tensor for
g_{0}
.

The result in the main theorem is related to the general question of bilipschitz uniformization of a metric space.
What conditions are needed on a metric space for it to be bilipschitz equivalent to a model space (in our case the four-dimensional sphere)?
In the two-dimensional setting this problem has been studied extensively.
Bonk and Lang in
[
4
]
showed that
({\mathbb{R}}^{2},g)
is bilipschitz equivalent to
({\mathbb{R}}^{2},|dx|^{2})
if the Gaussian curvature is integrable and the integral of the positive part of the Gaussian curvature is less than
2\pi
. In two dimensions this is a sharp result as a counterexample can be constructed by taking an infinite cylinder with one hemispherical cap. This theorem was first conjectured by Fu in
[
14
]
, where he showed a similar result with the added condition that the
L^{1}
-norm of the Gaussian curvature of
g
is small.

Bonk, Heinonen and Saksman in
[
3
]
were able to extend the two dimensional result to all even dimensions. They showed that
({\mathbb{R}}^{4},e^{2w}|dx|^{2})
is bilipschitz to
({\mathbb{R}}^{4},|dx|^{2})
whenever the
L^{1}
-norm of the
Q
-curvature for
e^{2w}
is sufficiently small.
Their methods can be applied to the spherical setting and so a special case of Theorem
1.1
, when
g_{1}
is conformally flat and
g_{0}
is the standard metric on the sphere, follows from their work.

The results in
[
3
]
were improved upon in
[
28
]
where Wang showed that if the
Q
-curvature is integrable and the positive part of the
Q
-curvature integrates to less than
2^{n-2}((n-2)/2)!\pi^{n/2}
, then the space is bilipschitz equivalent to the standard Euclidean space.
In both the two dimensional and higher dimensional cases the inequality is sharp. An infinite cylinder with one hemispherical cap will give the equality case and such a manifold is not bilipschitz equivalent to the standard Euclidean space.
This method was also applied to the Heisenberg group by Austin in
[
2
]
, where the author constructed a quasiconformal map whose Jacobian was comparable to the logarithmic potential of a given measure with small total variation.

We also note that quantifying the best bilipschitz constant between two non-compact manifolds also quantifies the best constant in the isoperimetric inequality of the manifold.
In dimension two, the asymptotic isoperimetric inequality on a non-compact surface depends only on the integral of the Gaussian curvature. This was shown by Finn in
[
13
]
and followed the work of Cohn-Vossen
[
11
]
and Huber
[
22
]
.
Following the two-dimensional result, asymptotic isoperimetric inequalities in four dimensions were shown to depend on the
L^{1}
-norm of the
Q
-curvature by Chang, Qing and Yang in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
. This was also generalized by Wang, in
[
29
]
. Wang showed that the constant in the isoperimetric inequality only depends on the
L^{1}
-norm of the
Q
curvature when the underlying manifold is
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.

We adapt the methods in
[
3
]
to consider the non-conformally flat setting.
We study the dependence of the best bilipschitz constant between
(S^{4},g)
and
(S^{4},g_{c})
, where
g_{c}
is the standard metric on
S^{4}
, on integral curvature quantities. The dependence relies on the conformal invariant
L^{2}
-norm of the Weyl tensor and the
L^{1}
-norm of the
Q
-curvature of
g
. Our main theorem can also be applied to certain settings on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
. For example, if
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
is equipped with a metric that is asymptotically flat, we expect our results to still hold.
For simplicity we work only on
S^{4}
and we do not discuss the non-compact setting.
In the compact setting, the identity map between
(S^{4},g_{1})
and
(S^{4},g_{0})
is always bilipschitz. So, opposed to the previous work on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, the question of the existence of a bilipschitz map is no longer relevant.
Rather, we study the dependence of the best bilipschitz constant on the
Q
-curvature and Weyl tensor.

The bilipschitz map given by the main theorem is achieved as the composition of two maps.
The first map is a bilipschitz map between the original metric and the Yamabe metric within its conformal class. This map is constructed via a vector flow argument that follows the work in
[
3
]
.
The flow yields a quasiconformal map from
(S^{4},g_{1})
to
(S^{4},g_{0})
; it is additionally bilipschitz as a map between
(S^{4},g_{1})
and
(S^{4},g_{0})
.

The use of vector fields to construct quasiconformal mappings on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
was first developed by Reimann in
[
26
]
. He showed that if the Ahlfors conformal strain tensor of a vector field
v
satisfied

\displaystyle\|Sv\|_{\infty}=\|(Dv+Dv^{T})/2-\operatorname{Tr}(Dv)\operatorname{Id}/n\|_{\infty}<c,

then the flow along
v
is quasiconformal with a quasiconformal constant that is less than
e^{nct}
at time
t\geq 0
.
Pierzchalski in
[
25
]
partially generalized the work of Reimann to the Riemannian manifold setting.
Bonk, Heinonen and Saksman, in
[
3
]
, developed this further to show that the Jacobian of the flow maps corresponded to the divergence of
v
. We discuss the quasiconformal theory in Section
2
.
We also generalize the quasiconformal vector flow theory of the above authors to the more general setting of Riemannian metrics on
S^{4}
.

On a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the
Q
-curvature is defined as

\displaystyle Q=\frac{1}{12}\left({-\Delta R+\frac{1}{4}R^{2}-3|E|^{2}}\right),

where
R
is the scalar curvature and
E
is the traceless Ricci tensor. The
Q
-curvature satisfies a conformal transformation law similar to the Gaussian curvature for surfaces. More precisely,

(2)

\displaystyle P_{g_{0}}w+2Q_{g_{0}}=2Q_{g}e^{4w},

whenever
g=e^{2w}g_{0}
. Here,
P_{g_{0}}
is the Paneitz operator and is defined by

\displaystyle P=\Delta^{2}+\operatorname{div}\left({\frac{2}{3}R-2\operatorname{Ric}}\right)d.

If
g_{0}=|dx|^{2}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
, then (
2
) becomes

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}w=2Q_{g}e^{4w}.

The function
w
, under the condition that
e^{2w}|dx|^{2}
is a normal metric, can be expressed as a logarithmic potential

\displaystyle w(x)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\log\frac{|y|}{|x-y|}Q_{g}(y)e^{4w(y)}dy+C,

where
C
is a constant (see
[
9
]
for a discussion of normal metrics).
Using this observation, the authors of
[
3
]
defined a vector field
v
so that

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}v\sim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\log\frac{|y|}{|x-y|}Q_{g}(y)e^{4w(y)}dy.

If a map is given as the solution to the differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{df(t,x)}{dt}=v(f(t,x)),

then the Jacobian of
f
will be comparable to
e^{4w}
. The map
f
was then shown to be quasiconformal as well, which implies that it is bilipschitz between
({\mathbb{R}}^{4},e^{2w}|dx|^{2})
and the standard Euclidean space.

The crucial part of the argument in
[
3
]
is that the vector field arises from a logarithmic potential. In order to adapt these methods to prove Theorem
1.1
, we employ the Green’s function for the Paneitz operator as a substitute for the logarithmic potential.
The Green’s function for the Paneitz have been studied previously (see e.g., Gursky and Malchiodi
[
18
]
and Hang and Yang
[
20
]
).
Whenever the Paneitz operator is invertible, the function
w
can be expressed as

\displaystyle w(x)=\int_{S^{4}}G(x,y)Q(y)e^{4w(y)}dv_{g_{0}}(y)+C,

where
G(x,y)
is the Green’s function and
C
is a constant.
We then show that the Green’s function behaves similarly to a logarithmic potential, which allows us to use methods as in
[
3
]
to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
1.2
is a result of the fact that the Ricci flow on
(S^{4},g_{0})
converges as
t\to\infty
to the standard metric on the sphere whenever the Weyl tensor has a sufficiently small norm in
L^{2}
.
This was originally shown by Gursky in
[
16
]
for Yamabe metrics on
S^{n}
. It was shown by Chen in
[
10
]
for asymptotically flat metrics on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

In the literature there are many results that assume some curvature pinching on
S^{4}
or
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
to imply that the Ricci flow converges to the standard structure (see e.g.,
[
10
,
16
,
19
,
23
,
24
]
).
Recently, Chang and Chen in
[
5
]
proved quantitative estimates on the rate of convergence of the flow that only depend on the
L^{2}
-norm of the Weyl tensor.
They also showed that the
L^{2}
-norm of certain integral curvature quantities are monotonically decreasing in time.
These previous results give sufficient convergence for large time to the standard structure on the sphere. For short time, we introduce the
L^{2}
-norm of the Bach tensor to control the bilipschitz distortion of the flow.

The Weyl tensor arises from the orthogonal decomposition of the Riemannian tensor as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Rm}=W+\frac{1}{2}E\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}g+\frac{1}{24}Rg\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}g,

where
E=\operatorname{Ric}-\frac{1}{4}Rg
is the traceless Ricci tensor. The Weyl tensor is traceless and conformally invariant.
The Bach tensor arises as the gradient of the functional
g\mapsto\int|W|^{2}dv
. Since this functional is also conformally invariant it can be shown that the Bach tensor transforms as

\displaystyle B_{e^{2w}g}=e^{-2w}B_{g}.

In coordinates,
B
is expressed as

\displaystyle B_{ij}=\nabla^{k}\nabla^{l}W_{kijl}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Ric}^{kl}W_{kijl}.

The
L^{1}
-norm of the Bach tensor is a conformal invariant. However, for
p>1
, the
L^{p}
-norm of
B
depends on the conformal factor by the transformation law for
B
.

The argument in
[
5
]
uses the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula to show that if the Weyl tensor has small
L^{2}
-norm, then the
L^{2}
-norm of
|W|+|E|+|R-\overline{R}|
is also controlled, where
\overline{R}
is the average scalar curvature on
S^{4}
. Recall that on
(S^{4},g_{0})
,

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}=\int_{M}|W|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|E|^{2}+\frac{1}{24}R^{2}dv_{g_{0}}.

If
g_{0}
is a positive Yamabe metric, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}\int_{S^{4}}R^{2}dv_{g_{0}}\leq 16\pi^{2}.

Therefore, we have that
\|E\|_{2}\leq 2\|W\|_{2}
.
This, combined with parabolic-type estimates, gives exponential convergence of the metric to the standard structure on
S^{4}
along the Ricci flow.

This estimate provides fast convergence for large time. For short time we use the
L^{2}
-norm of the Bach tensor in order to show added integrability for
W
. We show that the
L^{4}
-norm of
W
and
E
is controlled by the
L^{2}
-norms of both
W
and
B
. Added with the parabolic-type estimates for the Ricci flow, we prove sufficient bounds for small time to show Theorem
1.2
.

In Section
2
, we provide the preliminary lemmas related to quasiconformal maps and the Green’s function for the Paneitz operator needed to prove Theorem
1.1
. This includes a discussion of quasiconformal flows on Riemannian manifolds.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
using quasiconformal flows. In Section
4
, we provide the preliminary estimates of the norm of
W
using the Bach tensor. In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

1.1.
Notation

We attempt to follow the standard notation in this paper. For a Riemannian metric
g
on an orientable manifold
M
, we denote
d_{g}
to be the distance associated with
g
and
dv_{g}
to be the volume form with respect to
g
.
The quantities
R
,
Q
,
\operatorname{Ric}
,
E
,
W
and
B
are the scalar curvature,
Q
-curvature, Ricci tensor, traceless Ricci tensor, Weyl tensor and Bach tensor respectively.
The space
W^{k,p}(M)
denotes the Sobolev space for real-valued functions on
M
and for manifolds
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. The space
W^{k,p}(M_{1},M_{2})
denotes the Sobolev space for maps
f\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
. If
U\subset(M,g)
, then
|U|_{g}
denotes the volume of
U
with respect to
dv_{g}
. The volume may be denoted as
|U|
when the metric is clear from context.
Constants written as
C(A)
denote that the constant
C(A)
depends on
A
. The statement
A\sim B
is used to mean that there exists a constant
C
so that

\frac{1}{C}B\leq A\leq CB
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Quasiconformal Maps and Quasiconformal Flows

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
be Riemannian manifolds. A continuous map
f\colon(M_{1},g_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2})
is
K
-
quasiconformal
if
f
is a homeomorphism,
f\in W^{1,n}_{\text{loc}}(M_{1},M_{2})
and for almost every
x\in M_{1}

\displaystyle\|Df(x)\|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x),

where
Df
is the derivative of
f
and
J_{f}=\det(Df)
. The function
J_{f}
can also be defined as the Hodge star dual of the pullback of the volume form of
(M_{2},g_{2})
.

If
(M_{1},g_{1})=(M_{2},g_{2})=({\mathbb{R}}^{n},|dx|^{2})
, then Reimann in
[
26
]
characterized vector fields whose flow maps are quasiconformal.
The
Ahlfors conformal strain tensor
of a vector field
v\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined as

(3)

\displaystyle Sv:=\frac{1}{2}(Dv^{T}+Dv)-\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Tr}(Dv)\operatorname{Id}.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
26
, Theorem 5]
)
.

Let
v\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a vector field such that for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
v_{t}
is continuous,
v_{t}\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
v_{t}(x)=O(|x|\log|x|)
for
x\to\infty
and
\|Sv_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq c
, where
c
is independent of
t
.
Then the solutions to the differential equation

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{df_{t}}{dt}=v_{t}(f_{t}(x))\quad\text{and}\quad f_{0}(x)=x

exist, are unique and are
e^{nct}
-quasiconformal.

Bonk, Heinonen and Saksman extended this proposition to characterize the behavior of
J_{f_{t}}
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
3
, Proposition 3.6]
)
.

Let
v
be as in Proposition
2.2
and let
f_{t}
be the solution to (
4
).
Then

(5)

\displaystyle\log J_{f_{t}}(x)=\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{div}v_{t^{\prime}}(f_{t^{\prime}}(x))dt^{\prime}

for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

The goal of this section is to prove a suitable version of these theorems for the case of closed and orientable Riemannian manifolds.
Let
(M,g)
be a closed, orientable Riemannian manifold, we define the
Ahlfors conformal strain tensor on
M
as

(6)

\displaystyle S_{g}v:=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{v}g-\frac{1}{n}(\operatorname{div}_{g}v)g,

where
\mathcal{L}_{v}
is the Lie derivative along
v
.
This definition was given by Pierzchalski in
[
25
]
.
If a vector field
v
satisfies
S_{g}v=0
, then
v
is a conformal Killing field. The infinitesimal diffeomorphisms produced by flowing along
v
will be conformal with respect to
g
.
Additionally, the operator
S_{g}
satisfies the conformal invariance property,

(7)

\displaystyle S_{e^{2w}g}v=e^{2w}S_{g}v.

This follows from a quick calculation. We can express
2S_{e^{2w}g}v
in local coordinates as

\displaystyle 2S_{e^{2w}g}v

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{v}(e^{2w})g+e^{2w}\mathcal{L}_{v}g-\frac{2}{n}\left({\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial x_{i}}+v_{i}\frac{\partial(\log(\det(e^{2w}g)^{1/2}))}{\partial x_{i}}}\right)e^{2w}g

\displaystyle=L_{v}(e^{2w})g-\left({2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\frac{\partial w}{\partial x_{i}}}\right)e^{2w}g+e^{2w}\mathcal{L}_{v}g-\frac{2}{n}\operatorname{div}_{g}(v)e^{2w}g.

The first two terms cancel and the second two terms correspond to
2e^{2w}S_{g}v
. The conformal invariance in (
7
) immediately gives that

(8)

\displaystyle|S_{e^{2w}g}v|_{e^{2w}g}=|S_{g}v|_{g}

and so the supremum norm of
S_{g}v
is invariant under any conformal change to the metric
g
.

Pierzchalski showed the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
25
, Theorem 3]
)
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a closed and orientable Riemannian manifold. Let
v\colon M\times[0,\infty)
be a smooth vector field satisfying
\|S_{g}v_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq c
, where
c
is independent of
t
. Then the solutions to the differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{df_{t}}{dt}=v_{t}(f_{t}(x))\quad\text{and}\quad f_{0}(x)=x

exist, are unique and are
e^{nct}
-quasiconformal.

Pierzchalski proved the deformation theorem only for smooth vector fields.
We will now show that the result is still true when the vector fields are only in
W^{1,1}(S^{n})
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
v\colon S^{n}\times[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a vector field such that for every
t\in[0,\infty)
,
v_{t}\in W^{1,1}(S^{n})

and
\|S_{g}v_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq c
, where
c
is independent of
t
and
S
is defined in terms of a smooth Riemannian metric
g
on
S^{n}
. Then the solutions to the differential equation

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{df_{t}}{dt}=v_{t}(f_{t}(x))\quad\text{and}\quad f_{0}(x)=x

exist, are unique and are
e^{nct}
-quasiconformal.

We first show the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
S_{g_{c}}
and
S_{g}
be the Ahlfors conformal strain tensors for the metrics
g_{c}
and
g
, where
g_{c}
is the standard metric on the sphere.
Then for any vector field
v\colon S^{n}\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with measurable derivatives,

\displaystyle\|S_{g_{c}}v\|_{\infty}\leq C(g)(\|S_{g}v\|_{\infty}+\|v\|_{\infty}).

Proof.

Fix
x\in S^{n}
. By (
8
), it suffices to consider
Sv
instead of
S_{g_{c}}v
, where
S
is the tensor taken with respect to the Euclidean metric.
We can express Equation (
6
) in coordinates, where
x=0
and

The Green’s Function

In the proof for Theorem

Gursky in

Proposition 2.12
.

If

is a smooth compact

\displaystyle\int_{M}Q_{g}\geq 0

,

\displaystyle G(x,y)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\log\frac{1}{d_{g}(x,y)}+h(x,y),

and

In order to prove Proposition

, we quote the following lemma.

Lemma 2.13

(
[
18
, Lemma 2.8]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact
4
-dimensional Riemannian manifold such that

\displaystyle\int_{M}Q_{g}\geq 0

and

.
If

is a radial function around a point

and

is the metric for the conformal normal coordinates around

\displaystyle P_{\tilde{g}}u=\Delta_{g}^{2}u+\frac{1}{12}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}R(o)x^{k}x^{l}\mathcal{D}(u)+O(r)u+O(r^{2})|u^{\prime}|+O(r^{3})|u^{\prime\prime}|+O(r^{4})|u^{\prime\prime\prime}|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(u)=\frac{2u^{\prime}}{r}-2u^{\prime\prime}

Proof of Proposition
2.12
.

The existence of

follows from
[

, Theorem A]
.
We apply Lemma

to

. If

\displaystyle P_{\tilde{g}}u=\delta_{x}(y)+\frac{1}{12}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}R(o)x^{k}x^{l}\mathcal{D}\left({\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\log\frac{1}{r}}\right)+O(r\log r).

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\log\frac{1}{r})=O(r^{-2}).

So

\displaystyle P_{\tilde{g}}\log\frac{1}{r}=\delta_{x}(y)+O(1).

Therefore

\displaystyle P_{\tilde{g}}\bigg(G_{\tilde{g}}(x,y)-\log\frac{1}{r})=O(1)

for all

.

, then

\displaystyle\left|{D_{x}\bigg(\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,z)}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}\bigg)}\right|\leq\frac{C(g_{0})}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|{D_{x}\psi\bigg(\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,z)}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}\bigg)}\right|\leq\frac{C(g_{0})}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}.

We can now bound the left hand side of (ii).

\displaystyle\frac{D_{x}\rho(x,y)}{\rho(x,y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left({\int_{S^{4}}D_{x}\psi\biggl(\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,z)}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}\biggr)J_{\Phi}(z)dv_{0}(z)}\right)\bigg/\left({\int_{S^{4}}\psi\biggl(\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,z)}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}\biggr)J_{\Phi}(z)dv_{0}(z)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(g_{0})}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}\frac{\left({\int_{B(x,d_{g_{0}}(x,y)/2)}J_{\Phi}(z)dv_{0}(z)}\right)}{\left({\int_{B(x,d_{g_{0}}(x,y)/4}J_{\Phi}(z)dv_{0}(z)}\right)},

where in the second inequality we used the support of
\psi
and (

).
By Proposition

,

\displaystyle\frac{D_{x}\rho(x,y)}{\rho(x,y)}\leq\frac{C(g_{0})^{K}}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}.

∎

Lemma 3.2

For every

Proof.

Recall that by Proposition

\displaystyle G(x,y)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\log\frac{1}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)}+h(x,y),

\displaystyle|\tilde{G}(x,y)-G(\Phi(x),y)|\leq C(g_{0},K)

By Proposition

\displaystyle D_{x}\tilde{G}(x,y)\psi\left({\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,\Phi^{-1}(y))}{r}}\right)(x-\Phi^{-1}(y))

\displaystyle+\tilde{G}(x,y)D_{x}\psi\left({\frac{d_{g_{0}}(x,\Phi^{-1}(y))}{r}}\right)(x-\Phi^{-1}(y))

\displaystyle S(x-\Phi^{-1}(y))_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}(x^{l})(g_{0})_{lj}+\partial_{j}(x^{l})(g_{0})_{il}+(x^{l}-\Phi^{-1}(y)^{l})(\partial_{l}g_{0})_{ij}-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{div}_{g_{0}}(x-\Phi^{-1}(y))(g_{0})_{ij}

Lemma 3.4

\displaystyle M_{k}(j)

.

∎

]

Proposition 4.1

)
.

Let
g_{0}
be the Yamabe metric on
S^{4}
with a positive Yamabe constant, then

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{4}}|E|^{2}dv_{0}\leq\int_{S^{4}}|W|^{2}dv_{0}

and

(28)

\displaystyle 24\left({16\pi^{2}-\int_{S^{4}}|W|^{2}dv_{0}}\right)\leq Y([g])^{2}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
g_{0}
be a Yamabe metric on
S^{4}
with positive Yamabe constant.
There exists an
\epsilon>0
so that if

\displaystyle M=\int_{S^{4}}(|W|^{2}+|E|^{2})dv_{0}<\epsilon^{2},

then

(29)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{4}}|E|^{4}dv_{0}\leq CM\int_{S^{4}}|B|^{2}dv_{0}

and

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{4}}|W|^{4}dv_{0}\leq CM\int_{S^{4}}|B|^{2}dv_{0},

where
C>0
does not depend on
g_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof of the two inequalities (
29
) and (
30
) depends
on two equivalent expressions of the Bach tensor via the second Bianchi identity which we now explain.

Start with Bach tensor as defined in (
25
).
A well-known relationship between
the Weyl tensor and the Ricci tensor via the Bianchi identity, a proof of which can also be found in
[
7
, p. 124]
, is that

(31)

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}W_{ijlk}:=(\delta W)_{ijl}=\frac{1}{2}(dA)_{ijl}.

where

\displaystyle(dA)_{ijl}=\nabla_{i}A_{jl}-\nabla_{j}A_{il},

and
A_{ij}=\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}-\frac{1}{6}Rg_{ij}
.
Applying (
31
) to the expression of the
Bach tensor (
25
), we find that (
[
6
, p. 717]
)

(32)

\displaystyle B_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta E_{ij}+\frac{1}{6}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}R-\frac{1}{24}\Delta Rg_{ij}-E^{kl}W_{ikjl}+E_{i}^{k}E_{jk}-\frac{1}{4}|E|^{2}g_{ij}+\frac{1}{6}RE_{ij}

(33)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta E_{ij}-E^{kl}W_{ikjl}+E_{i}^{k}E_{jk}-\frac{1}{4}|E|^{2}g_{ij}+\frac{1}{6}RE_{ij},

since
R
is constant.
If we multiply (
32
) by
E
and integrate, then

\displaystyle\int|\nabla E|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int EB+2\int EEW-2\int\operatorname{Tr}E^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\int|E|^{2}\operatorname{Tr}E-\frac{1}{3}\int R|E|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int EB+2\int EEW-2\int\operatorname{Tr}E^{3}-\frac{1}{3}\int R|E|^{2},

since
\operatorname{Tr}(E)=0
. By (
26
),

\displaystyle Y([g])\bigg(\int|E|^{4}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int|\nabla|E||^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\int R|E|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int|\nabla E|^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\int R|E|^{2}.

So

\displaystyle Y([g])\bigg(\int|E|^{4}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int EB+2\int EEW-2\int\operatorname{Tr}E^{3}-\frac{1}{6}\int R|E|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|B\|_{2}\|E\|_{2}+2\|W\|_{2}\|E\|_{4}^{2}+2\|E\|_{4}^{2}\|E\|_{2},

by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and since
R>0
. By our assumption on
\|E\|_{2}
and
\|W\|_{2}
,

\displaystyle Y([g])\|E\|_{4}^{2}\leq 2M^{1/2}\|B\|_{2}+4M^{1/2}\|E\|_{4}^{2}.

So if
\epsilon<Y([g])/4
, then

\displaystyle\|E\|_{4}^{2}\leq\frac{2M^{1/2}}{Y([g])-4M^{1/2}}\|B\|_{2}.

The constant is bounded by
CM^{1/2}
, where
C
is independent of
M
and
Y([g])
, by Equations (
27
) and (
28
).
Hence the inequality in (
29
) is satisfied.

We next prove the inequality in (
30
).
Starting from the definition of Bach tensor (
25
),

\displaystyle\int B_{ij}A_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int A_{ij}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}W_{iklj}-\frac{1}{2}A_{kl}W_{ikjl})

\displaystyle=\int-\nabla_{k}A_{ij}\nabla_{l}W_{iklj}-\frac{1}{2}A_{ij}A_{kl}W_{ikjl}

\displaystyle=\int-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{k}A_{ij}-\nabla_{i}A_{kj})\nabla^{l}W_{iklj}-\frac{1}{2}A_{ij}A_{kl}W_{ikjl},

by the symmetry of
W
. Therefore, from
the (
31
),

\displaystyle\int B_{ij}A_{ij}=\int|\delta W|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int A_{ij}A_{kl}W_{ikjl}.

Also,

\displaystyle\int|\nabla W|^{2}=4\int|\delta W|^{2}+72\int\det W-\frac{1}{2}\int R|W|^{2},

see
[
7
, p. 126]
. So

\displaystyle\int|\nabla W|^{2}=4\int BA+2\int WAA+72\int\det W-\frac{1}{2}\int R|W|^{2}.

Since the Weyl tensor has zero trace we have that
WAA=WEE
and that
BA=BE
. Substituting this we arrive at

(34)

\displaystyle\int|\nabla W|^{2}=4\int BE+2\int WEE+72\int\det W-\frac{1}{2}\int R|W|^{2}.

We now can prove (
30
) in a similar way as the proof for (
29
).
By (
26
) and (
34
)

\displaystyle Y([g])\bigg(\int|W|^{4}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int|\nabla|W||^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\int R|W|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\int BE+2\int WEE+72\int\det W-\frac{1}{3}\int R|W|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int BE+2\int WEE+72\int\det W,

since
R>0
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle Y([g])\bigg(\int|W|^{4}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|W\|_{2}(\|W\|_{4}^{2}+\|E\|_{4}^{1/2})+\|E\|_{2}\|B\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(M^{1/2}\|W\|_{4}^{2}+M^{1/2}(1+M^{1/2})\|B\|_{2}),

by (
29
). So if
\epsilon<Y([g])/C
, then

\displaystyle\|W\|_{4}^{1/2}\leq C\frac{M^{1/2}(1+M^{1/2})}{Y([g])-CM^{1/2}}\|B\|_{2}.

The coefficient of
\|B\|_{2}
is controlled by a constant times
M^{1/2}
, independently of
\epsilon
and
Y([g])
, by Equations (
27
) and (
28
).
So, the inequality in (
30
) is satisfied.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

With these preliminaries, we now proceed with the proof for Theorem
1.2
. For the reader’s convenience we restate the theorem.

Theorem
1.2
.

Let
g_{0}
be a Yamabe metric of constant and positive scalar curvature on
S^{4}
, let

\displaystyle\beta=\int_{S^{4}}|W|^{2}dv_{0},

and let

\displaystyle\gamma=\int_{S^{4}}|B|^{2}dv_{0},

where
W
is the Weyl tensor and
B
is the Bach tensor.
There exists a constant
\epsilon_{2}>0
such that if
\beta<\epsilon_{2}
, then

(S^{4},g_{0})
is bilipschitz equivalent to
(S^{4},g_{c})
, where
g_{c}
is the standard metric for
S^{4}
. That is, for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
there exists
L_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L_{2}}d_{g_{c}}(x,y)\leq d_{g_{0}}(x,y)\leq L_{2}d_{g_{c}}(x,y),

where
L_{2}\leq 1+C^{\prime}\gamma\beta
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we will apply the normalized Ricci flow to
(S^{4},g_{0})
and show that under the flow the metric converges sufficiently rapidly to
g_{c}
.
Results of this type have been shown previously and the proofs will not be provided.
The normalized Ricci flow is defined by

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dt}=-2\operatorname{Ric_{g}}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{R_{g}}g,

where

\displaystyle\overline{R}=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}_{g}(S^{4})}\int_{S^{4}}R_{g}dv_{g}

and
R_{g}
is the scalar curvature of
g
.

We now recall a result of Gursky
[
16
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

On
S^{4}
, if
g_{0}
has a positive Yamabe constant and

\displaystyle\int_{S64}|W|^{2}<\epsilon_{2},

where
W
is the Weyl tensor for
g_{0}
, then
the solution to (
35
) with initial condition
g(0)=g_{0}
exists for all time and converges to
g_{c}
.

We remark that in the proof of Gursky, he first established the convergence of the metric for a short time to a metric with bounded curvature that satisfies a pinching condition.
He then quoted an earlier work of Huisken
[
23
]
to establish the exponential convergence of the metric to
g_{c}
under the flow (see also
[
24
]
).

In a recent work of Chang and Chen
[
5
]
, they have established the monotonicity of the
L^{p}
-norm of a certain curvature quantity under the flow.
They use this to give a quantitative estimate of the result of the Gursky. More precisely they have established the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

If
(S^{4},g(t))
is a normalized Ricci
flow starting from a unit volume
positive Yamabe metric
(S^{4},g_{0})
with

\displaystyle\int_{S^{4}}|W|_{g_{0}}^{2}dv_{g_{0}}<10^{-3}\pi^{2}

then for all
t>0

and
p\in[2,2+\frac{1}{3}],

\displaystyle\||W|+|E|+|R-\bar{R}|\|_{\infty}(t)\leq C_{1}(1+t^{-\frac{2}{p}})e^{-C_{2}t}\||W|+|E|)\|_{p}(0),

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are dimensional constants and independent of
g_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first show that
\|G(0)\|_{4}
is controlled by
\beta
. Recall that
G(0)=|W|+|E|+|R-\overline{R}|
. At time
0
the scalar curvature is constant and so we have that
G(0)=|W|+|E|
.
By Proposition
4.2
,
\|G(0)\|_{4}\leq C\|G(0)\|_{2}\|B(0)\|_{2}
, where
B
is the Bach tensor at time
0
.
So it suffices to show that
\|G(0)\|_{2}
is controlled by
\beta
.
The Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula implies that

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}=\int_{S^{4}}(|W|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|E|^{2}+\frac{1}{24}R^{2})dv_{g_{0}}.

Since
R
is constant, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{4}}R^{2}dv_{g_{0}}=Y([g_{0}])^{2}.

In
[
1
]
, Aubin showed that the Yamabe constant is largest for
g_{c}
and so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}\int_{S^{4}}R^{2}dv_{g_{0}}\leq 16\pi^{2}.

This, together with the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula gives that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{4}}|E|^{2}dv_{g_{0}}\leq\beta

and so

\displaystyle\|G(0)\|_{2}^{2}\leq 3\beta.

The normalized Ricci flow equation gives that

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{dg}{dt}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2|\operatorname{Ric}-\frac{1}{4}Rg|+\frac{1}{2}|(R-\overline{R})g|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|G(t)\|_{\infty}.

By Theorem
5.2
,

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{dg}{dt}}\right|\leq Ct^{-2/(2+\delta)}e^{-C^{\prime}t}\|G(0)\|_{2+\delta}.

By Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
4.2
,

\displaystyle Ct^{-2/(2+\delta)}e^{-C^{\prime}t}\|G(0)\|_{2+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-2/(2+\delta)}e^{-C^{\prime}t}\|G(0)\|_{4}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-2/(2+\delta)}e^{-C^{\prime}t}\|B(0)\|_{2}\|G(0)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-2/(2+\delta)}e^{-C^{\prime}t}\|B(0)\|_{2}\beta.

where
C
is a positive constant that may differ in each line.
Since
2/(2+\delta)<1
, by integrating
dg/dt
,

\displaystyle\|g(t)-g_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq C\gamma\beta,

where the constant does not depend on
g_{0}
.

So
g_{0}
is bilipschitz equivalent to
g(t)
for all
t>0
. By Theorem
5.1
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=g_{c}
and so
g_{0}
is bilipschitz equivalent to
g_{c}
.
∎

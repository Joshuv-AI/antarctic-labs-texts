Abstract
. The Dunford property ( C ) (C) for composition operators on H p H^{p} -spaces ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ), as well as for their adjoints, is completely characterized within the class of those induced by linear fractional transformations of the unit disc. As a consequence, it is shown that the Dunford property is stable in such a class addressing a particular instance of a question posed by Laursen and Neumann.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

In 1959, E. Bishop used a Banach space version of the analytic duality principle established by Silva, Köthe, Grothendieck and others operator theorists to study connections between spectral decomposition properties of a Banach space operator and its adjoint. Of particular interest in this setting are operators satisfying the
Bishop property

(\beta)
since E. Albrecht and J. Eschmeier
[
2
]
developed a complete duality theory for them. Actually, the property
(\beta)
turns out to characterize restrictions of decomposable operators to closed invariant subspaces, and Albrecht and Eschmeier’s analytic functional model shows that every Banach space operator is similar to the quotient of an operator with the Bishop property. Similarly, quotients of decomposable operators are determined by the decomposition property
(\delta)
and in fact, both properties
(\beta)
and
(\delta)
are completely dual: an operator has one if and only if its adjoint has the other.

The class of decomposable operators, introduced by Foiaş
[
16
]
in the sixties, constitutes a generalization of spectral operators (in the sense of Dunford) and many operators in Hilbert spaces as unitary operators, self-adjoint operators or more generally, normal operators are decomposable. Recall that a linear bounded operator
T
acting on a Banach space
X
is
decomposable
if for every open cover
\{U_{1},U_{2}\}
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, there exist closed
T
-invariant subspaces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
of
X
so that
X=X_{1}+X_{2}
and the spectrum of the restriction
T|_{X_{i}}
is contained in
U_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.
It turns out that
T
is decomposable if and only if
T
satisfies both properties
(\beta)
and
(\delta)
.

Although the class of operators which are decomposable is not stable by restricting them to closed invariant subspaces, it turns out that the class of those which satisfy the
Dunford property

(C)
is. This property, pioneered by Dunford in the sixties, has played a major role in the development of the theory of spectral operators. Being the Dunford property
(C)
a weaker property than the Bishop one (see the Millers’ example
[
22
]
), one has that an operator
T
is decomposable if and only if it satisfies both properties
(C)
and
(\delta)
. We refer to the monograph by Laursen and Neumann
[
21
]
for more on this subject.

Studying local spectral properties for classes of
concrete operators
acting on function spaces leads naturally to interesting questions from the function theoretic perspective, and this is one of the driving aim of the present work. At this regard, it is worthy to point out the works of the Millers’ and coauthors
[
4
]
or
[
23
]
or the one by Aleman and Persson
[
3
]
regarding the classical Cesàro operator or Cesàro type operators.

One of the most significant classes of operators having the property
(C)
is the one consisting of
multipliers
acting on
functional Banach spaces
(see
[
11
]
for the definition and
[
21
, Proposition 1.6.9]
for the result). In particular, it turns out that every multiplier of the Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
1\leq p<\infty
has the property
(C)
but fails property
(\delta)
. In this work, we characterize the Dunford property
(C)
for composition operators
C_{\varphi}
, and their adjoints
C_{\varphi}^{*}
, whenever they are induced by linear fractional transformations
\varphi
of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
in
H^{p}
-spaces (
1<p<\infty
). They constitute a first approach to a more comprehensive analysis of the study of composition operators induced by any holomorphic self map of
\mathbb{D}
. At this regard, it is worthy to remark that while the spectrum of composition operators induced by linear fractional transformations of
\mathbb{D}
in
H^{p}
-spaces is known (see
[
11
, Chapter 8]
and the references therein), determining the spectrum of general composition operators in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
remains as an open (and difficult) question (see
[
17
]
for recent results in this setting).

As we will show, the Dunford property
(C)
is strongly dependent on the fixed-point configuration of the inducing symbol
\varphi
, and fails drastically in those cases when the operator does not have it, that is, the operator does not have even the
single-valued extension property
. Moreover, when both
C_{\varphi}
and
C_{\varphi}^{*}
have the Dunford property
(C)
, it turns out that they are decomposable operators. In Table
1
we summarise the characterization, and before proceeding further with comments, we recall some preliminaries.

The setting

For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
Hardy space

H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is defined as the complex Banach space of analytic functions
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
with finite norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})}:=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\bigg)^{1/p}.

For
p=\infty
, the space
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
consists of all bounded analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
equipped with the supremum norm.

Fixed
1<p<\infty
, it is a well-known fact that each element of the dual space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
may be regarded univocally as a continuous functional of the form

\varphi_{h}(f):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})\,h(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta,

for some function
h\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T})/H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
, where
\mathbb{T}
denotes the unit circle,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
and

H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}):=\bigg\{g\in H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}):\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(e^{i\theta})\,d\theta=0\bigg\}=zH^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}).

Equivalently, any functional
\varphi:H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
in the dual space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
can be identified with a function
g\in H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\varphi(f):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})\,\overline{g(e^{i\theta})}\,d\theta.

In fact, this correspondence establishes a sesquilinear dual pairing between
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
and
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
. In particular, the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is reflexive for every
1<p<\infty
.

Composition operators.

If
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic map, the
composition operator

C_{\varphi}
is defined as

\displaystyle C_{\varphi}:H^{p}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle\to H^{p}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto f\circ\varphi.

The boundedness of each composition operator
C_{\varphi}:H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is guaranteed by the
Littlewood Subordination Theorem
. Clearly,
C_{\varphi}
is invertible in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if the function
\varphi
is an
automorphism
of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

In this work, we consider composition operators induced by
linear fractional transformations
of
\mathbb{D}
, namely, holomorphic maps of
\mathbb{D}
given by

\varphi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\quad ad-bc\neq 0

where

|b\overline{d}-a\overline{c}|+|ad-bc|\leq|b|^{2}-|d|^{2}

(see, for instance,
[
9
]
). The linear fractional transformations can be classified according to their fixed points in the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
.
A linear fractional map
\varphi
that takes
\mathbb{D}
into itself is called
parabolic
if it has just one fixed point, which must lie in
\mathbb{T}
. We will distinguish the cases of
parabolic automorphisms
(PA) and
parabolic non-automorphisms
(PNA).

The other possible situation is when
\varphi
has two fixed points, and the classification depends on the location of such fixed points. One of them must lie in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}.
If it lies on
\mathbb{T}
, then
C_{\varphi}
is called
hyperbolic
. If the other fixed point lies in
\mathbb{T}
, then
\varphi
is an automorphism and will be called
hyperbolic automorphism
(HA). If it lies in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,
\varphi
is a
hyperbolic non-automorphism of first kind
(HNA I), and if it lies in
\mathbb{D}
it will be called a
hyperbolic non-automorphism of second kind
(HNA II).

Finally, if
\varphi
has no fixed points in
\mathbb{T}
, one of them must lie in
\mathbb{D}
, and the other fixed point must lie in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.
Here, we distinguish two situations: if
\varphi
is an automorphism it is called
elliptic
(EA), and otherwise,
\varphi
is said to be
loxodromic
(LOX).

After conjugations with appropriate linear fractional transformations, every of these maps can be expressed in a standard form as described in Table
1
. This classification is well-known in the literature and has been used extensively (see, for instance,
[
18
,
20
]
).

Local spectral theory

Local spectral theory arose as an effort to extend some of the most important properties of normal operators to broader classes of operators in the context of Banach spaces. In this regard, the notion of
decomposable operators
emerges: bounded linear operators
T
on a Banach space
X
for which each splitting of the spectrum
\sigma(T)
corresponds to a sum decomposition of the space
X
consisting of
T
-invariant subspaces. The class of decomposable operators is considerably large: for instance, all operators admitting a sufficiently rich functional calculus are decomposable, as well as any operator with totally disconnected spectrum.
Among the local spectral properties, of particular relevance is the
single-valued extension property
.

Definition 1.1
.

A linear bounded operator
T
in
X
has the single-valued extension property (abbreviated as SVEP) if for every open subset
U\subset\mathbb{C}
and every analytic function
f:U\rightarrow X
satisfying

(T-zI)f(z)=0,\qquad\text{for all }z\in U

it follows that
f\equiv 0.

It is clear that every operator whose point spectrum has empty interior automatically possesses the SVEP. This property is closely related to the concept of
local resolvent
of an operator. Given a linear bounded operator
T
in
X
and a vector
x\in X
, the local resolvent of
T
at
x
(denoted by
\rho_{T}(x)
) is the union of all the open sets
U\subset\mathbb{C}
for which there exists an analytic function
f_{x}:U\rightarrow X
satisfying

(1.1)

(T-zI)f_{x}(z)=x,\qquad z\in U.

For those operators
T
enjoying the SVEP, the functional equation (
1.1
) has a unique solution for each
x\in X
. Hence, in that case, there exists a unique local resolvent function
f_{x}:\rho_{T}(x)\to X
satisfying (
1.1
).

The
local spectrum
of
T
at a vector
x\in X
is defined as
\sigma_{T}(x):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho_{T}(x)
. With this notion, one may define the
local spectral subspaces
(or simply the
spectral subspaces
) of
T
associated to a subset
F\subset\mathbb{C}
as

X_{T}(F):=\{x\in X\,:\,\sigma_{T}(x)\subseteq F\}.

For each
F\subset\mathbb{C}
the spectral subspace
X_{T}(F)
is a (non-necessarily closed) linear manifold in
X
which is hyperinvariant by the operator
T
.
With the definition of (local) spectral subspaces at hands, we recall the
Dunford property

(C)
:

Definition 1.2
.

A linear bounded operator
T
in
X
has the Dunford’s property
(C)
if the local spectral subspace
X_{T}(F)
is closed for every closed set
F\subset\mathbb{C}.

It turns out that every decomposable operator has property
(C)
, and every operator with property
(C)
enjoys the SVEP (see
[
21
, Theorem 1.2.7 and Proposition 1.2.19]
).

(C)
for composition operators

induced by linear fractional maps in

, as well as for their adjoints

\varphi

H^{p}(\mathbb{D})

\varphi

In order to deal with the remaining cases, in Section

(C)
. In Section

, we consider invertible composition operators and show that if
\varphi

,

.

2.
A sufficient condition

In this section, we present a general result for linear bounded operators acting on Banach spaces which provides a sufficient condition in order to have the Dunford property
(C)
, along with some more (local) spectral features. This result, which can be understood as an extension of
[
21
, Proposition 1.6.12]
, will be applied in the context of composition operators.

We start by recalling the
glocal spectral subspaces
associated to an operator
T
in order to deal with a variant of (local) spectral subspaces which are better suited for operators which do not have the SVEP. Given a closed set
F\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, the glocal spectral subspace
\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)
consists of all
x\in X
for which there exists an analytic function
f:\mathbb{C}\setminus F\rightarrow X
such that

(T-zI)f(z)=x,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus F.

It is clear that the equality
\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)=X_{T}(F)
holds for every closed set
F\subset\mathbb{C}
whenever the operator
T
enjoys the SVEP. But in general, we have the inclusion
\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)\subseteq X_{T}(F)
.

Since we are dealing with Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, for every operator
T
and closed set
F\subset\mathbb{C}
, we will denote by
H^{p}_{T}(F)
and
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{T}(F)
the associated local spectral subspaces and glocal spectral subspaces, respectively.

Glocal spectral subspaces behave well with respect to the adjoint operation. Indeed, by
[
21
, Proposition 2.5.1]
, if
T:X\to X
is a linear bounded operator and
F
and
G
are two disjoint closed subsets of
\mathbb{C}
, then

(2.1)

\mathcal{X}_{T}(F)\subseteq{{}^{\perp}\mathcal{X}^{*}_{T^{*}}(G)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{X}^{*}_{T^{*}}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{T}(G)^{\perp},

where
M^{\perp}
denotes the annihilator of a linear manifold
M\subseteq X
, while
{{}^{\perp}N}
denotes the preannihilator of a linear manifold
N\subseteq X^{*}
. Actually, since
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is reflexive for
1<p<\infty
, having in mind the dual pairing between
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
, (
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
), the property (
2.1
) reads as

\mathcal{H}^{p}_{T}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}_{T^{*}}(G^{*})^{\perp},

where
A^{*}:=\{\overline{z}:z\in A\}
for every subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{C}
.

Before stating the main result of the section, let us recall that for each
x\in X
, the
local spectral radius
of
T
at
x
is the quantity

r_{T}(x):=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T^{n}x\right\|^{1/n}.

If
T
has the SVEP, then
r_{T}(x)=\max\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma_{T}(x)\}
for every non-zero
x\in X.
Besides, we denote by
r(T)
the spectral radius of
T
and by
\eta(\sigma(T))
the full spectrum of
T
, namely, the union of
\sigma(T)
and every bounded connected component of
\rho(T)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T:X\to X
be a bounded linear operator in a complex Banach space
X
. Assume further that for each non-empty relatively open subset
U\subseteq\sigma(T)
, the glocal spectral subspace
\mathcal{X}_{T}(\overline{U})
is dense in
X
. Then,
\mathcal{X}^{*}_{T^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
for every closed subset
F\subsetneq\sigma(T^{*})
. As a consequence, the following properties hold:

(i)

If
\sigma(T^{*})
is not a singleton, then
\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(T^{*})=\emptyset
.

(ii)

T^{*}
has the Dunford’s property
(C)
.

(iii)

\sigma_{T^{*}}(x)=\sigma(T^{*})
for every
x\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(iv)

r_{T^{*}}(x)=r(T^{*})
for every
x\in X^{*}\setminus\{0\}.

(v)

If
M
is any non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
T^{*}
, then

\sigma(T^{*})\subseteq\sigma(T^{*}|_{M})\subseteq\eta(\sigma(T^{*})).

Proof.

Let
F\subsetneq\sigma(T^{*})
be a closed subset and consider
U
a non-empty relatively open set in
\sigma(T^{*})
such that
F\cap\overline{U}=\emptyset.
Now, by (
2.1
), it follows that

\mathcal{X}^{*}_{T^{*}}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{T}(\overline{U})^{\perp}=\{0\},

where the last equality holds by the density of
\mathcal{X}_{T}(\overline{U})
.

First, let us prove property (i). Assume that
\sigma(T^{*})
has at least two points and take
\lambda\in\sigma(T^{*})
. As a consequence,

\ker(T^{*}-\lambda I)\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{*}_{T^{*}}(\{\lambda\})=\{0\},

so
\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(T^{*})=\emptyset.
In particular,
T^{*}
has the SVEP and
\mathcal{X}^{*}_{T^{*}}(F)=X^{*}_{T^{*}}(F)
for every closed set
F\subseteq\sigma(T^{*})
.

In order to prove properties (ii)-(v), first observe that
T^{*}
also enjoys the SVEP when
\sigma(T^{*})
is reduced to a singleton, because in that case, its point spectrum has empty interior. To show (ii), let
F\subseteq\sigma(T^{*})
be an arbitrary closed set. Then,

X^{*}_{T^{*}}(F)=\begin{cases}\{0\},&\text{ if }F\subsetneq\sigma(T^{*}),\\
X^{*},&\text{ if }F=\sigma(T^{*}),\end{cases}

and so
T^{*}
has the Dunford property
(C)
.

Now, both properties (iii) and (iv) follow immediately from property (ii). Finally, (v) is a direct application of
[
21
, Proposition 1.2.16 (e)]
and
[
25
, Theorem 0.8]
.

3.
Dunford property for invertible composition operators

In this section, we consider the Dunford property
(C)
for invertible composition operators
C_{\varphi}
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and their adjoints
C_{\varphi}^{*}
. As it was pointed out previously, the Dunford property
(C)
will be independent of
1<p<\infty
and rely upon exclusively on the nature of the automorphism
\varphi
.

In 1996, Smith proved that if
\varphi
is an elliptic or parabolic automorphism,
C_{\varphi}
is generalized scalar (see
[
29
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
), and therefore a decomposable operator. Accordingly, we are left with composition operators induced by hyperbolic automorphisms.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a hyperbolic automorphism of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. Then,
C_{\varphi}
does not have the SVEP in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for any
1\leq p<\infty
and
C_{\varphi}^{*}
has the Dunford’s property
(C)
for every
1<p<\infty
.

In order to prove such a result, our first step is to understand the set of eigenvectors of
C_{\varphi}
when
\varphi
is a hyperbolic automorphism. In this case, the linear fractional model establishes similarity between
C_{\varphi}
and a composition operator
C_{\varphi_{r}}
with hyperbolic symbol
\varphi_{r}
of the canonical form:

\varphi_{r}(z):=\frac{z+r}{1+rz},\quad 0<r<1,

where
\varphi_{r}
fixes the points
\pm 1
, being
1
the Denjoy-Wolff point. In this light, the spectrum of
C_{\varphi_{r}}
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, was characterized by Nordgren
[
24
]
:

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})})=\bigg\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\bigg(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\bigg)^{-1/p}\leq|\lambda|\leq\bigg(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}

and its point spectrum is
\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(C_{\varphi_{r}}|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})})=\mathrm{int}\big(\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})})\big)
. In fact, for each eigenvalue
\left(\frac{1+r}{1-r}\right)^{\lambda}
(
-1/p<\PR(\lambda)<1/p
) of
C_{\varphi_{r}}
, the associated eigenspace is infinite-dimensional, and an example of associated eigenvector is given by

w_{\lambda}(z):=\bigg(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\bigg)^{\lambda},\quad\text{ where }\;-1/p<\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)<1/p.

It is clear that the adjoint
C_{\varphi}^{*}
of any composition operator with hyperbolic automorphism symbol
\varphi
is similar to
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
for some
0<r<1
and
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}})=\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})})
.

In order to apply Theorem
2.1
in this context, we prove that, in general, some subsets of eigenfunctions of
C_{\varphi}
feature strong spanning properties in each Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
with
1<p<\infty
(see
[
12
, Lemma 1]
in the Hilbert space setting).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a hyperbolic automorphism and consider the composition operator
C_{\varphi}:H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\to H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for some
1<p<\infty
. Then, for every set
F\subseteq\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(C_{\varphi})
with a cluster point inside
\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(C_{\varphi})
, the linear manifold

\mathrm{span}\big\{\!\ker(C_{\varphi}-\lambda I):\lambda\in F\big\}

is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
\varphi=\varphi_{r}
for some
0<r<1
. Denote by
B_{1/p}
the open vertical strip
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,-1/p<\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)<1/p\}
and consider the following
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
-valued function
\varrho:B_{1/p}\to H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by
\varrho(\lambda):=w_{\lambda}
where, as above,

w_{\lambda}(z):=\bigg(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\bigg)^{\lambda},\quad\text{ for each }\;-1/p<\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)<1/p.

Since
C_{\varphi_{r}}w_{\lambda}=\big(\tfrac{1+r}{1-r}\big)^{\lambda}w_{\lambda}
, observe that the mapping
\lambda\mapsto\big(\tfrac{1+r}{1-r}\big)^{\lambda}
sends
B_{1/p}
onto the point spectrum
\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(C_{\varphi_{r}})
. Hence, there is a correspondence between
F
and a set
\Lambda\subseteq B_{1/p}
which, by continuity, has a cluster point inside
B_{1/p}
.

Now, we claim that
\varrho:B_{1/p}\to H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is an analytic function. To do so, consider
p^{\prime}>1
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. It is enough to show that, for each
g\in H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}),
the map

\lambda\in B_{1/p}\mapsto\langle\varrho(\lambda),g\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\frac{1+e^{i\theta}}{1-e^{i\theta}}\right)^{\lambda}\overline{g(e^{i\theta})}d\theta

is holomorphic, which follows immediately by means of standard methods involving Morera’s Theorem.

At this point, consider a bounded linear functional
\eta\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
such that
\langle w_{\lambda},\eta\rangle=0
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
. The map
\lambda\mapsto\langle w_{\lambda},\eta\rangle
is a holomorphic function on
B_{1/p}
which annihilates on the set
\Lambda
. Taking into account that
\Lambda
has a cluster point, we conclude that
\langle w_{\lambda},\eta\rangle=0
for every
\lambda\in B_{1/p}
. Accordingly, our quest reduces to prove that
\mathrm{span}\{w_{\lambda}:\lambda\in B_{1/p}\}
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
. To do so, we will show that

(3.1)

\overline{\mathrm{span}\{w_{it}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}}^{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})}=H^{p}(\mathbb{D})

for every
1\leq p<\infty
. In
[
12
, Lemma 1]
, the authors proved the case
p=2
by means of the Paley-Wiener Theorem, which clearly yields
1\leq p\leq 2
.

Fix the principal branch of the logarithm, and consider the band

\Pi:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\;-\pi/2<\mathrm{Im}(z)<\pi/2\}.

Note that proving (
3.1
) is equivalent to prove that

(3.2)

\overline{\mathrm{span}\big\{e^{\lambda z}:\,z\in\Pi,\;\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)=0\}}^{H^{p}(\Pi)}=H^{p}(\Pi),

where the space
H^{p}(\Pi)
consists of all holomorphic functions
f:\Pi\to\mathbb{C}
for which there exists a harmonic function
u
such that
|f(z)|^{p}\leq u(z)
for all
z\in\Pi
(we refer to Duren’s book
[
15
, Ch. 10]
for more on these spaces).

The assertion in (
3.2
) is, indeed, a particular instance of a more general situation studied in
[
6
]
by Bracci, Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Yakubovich, where the authors give a complete characterization of those non-elliptic semigroups of holomorphic self-maps of the unit disc for which the linear span of the eigenfunctions of the generator of the corresponding semigroup of composition operators is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}
of the Koenigs domain
\Omega
associated to the semigroup. Likewise, the authors give necessary and sufficient conditions for completeness in
H^{p}(\Omega)
.

We borrow the proof of the instance (
3.2
) from
[
6
]
and include it for the sake of completeness, referring to the manuscript for more details and further results.

Write
\lambda=it
with
t\in\mathbb{R}
and observe that
e^{itz}\in H^{\infty}(\Pi)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence,
\|e^{itz}\|_{H^{p}(\Pi)}\leq C
for some absolute constant
C>0
. Accordingly, the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|e^{itz}\|_{H^{p}(\Pi)}e^{-t\beta}dt

converges uniformly on
\Pi
for every
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\beta)>0
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, take
M>0
such that

\int_{M}^{\infty}\|e^{itz}\|_{H^{p}(\Pi)}e^{-t\beta}\,dt<\varepsilon.

Note that for those
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\beta)>-\pi/2
the integral

(3.3)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}\,dt=\frac{1}{-iz+\beta},

for
z\in\Pi
. Analogously, for those
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(\beta)<\pi/2
the integral

(3.4)

\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}\,dt=\frac{1}{iz-\beta},

for
z\in\Pi
.

Denote by
F_{\beta}
the function in (
3.3
), namely
F_{\beta}(z)=\frac{1}{-iz+\beta}
for
z\in\Pi
. Clearly,
F_{\beta}\in H^{\infty}(\Pi)
and it is not difficult to prove that each
I_{\beta}^{M}(z)=\int_{0}^{M}e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}\,dt
converges to
F_{\beta}
in
H^{\infty}(\Pi)
as
M\to\infty
. Note that
I_{\beta}^{M}
can be seen as the Laplace transform of the complex Borel measure compactly supported in
[0,+\infty)
defined by
\mu=\chi_{[0,M]}(t)\,dt
. Approximating
\mu
(in the sense of measures) by finite linear combinations of measures
\mu_{n}
given by

(3.5)

\mu_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu\bigg(\bigg[\frac{(j-1)M}{n},\frac{jM}{n}\bigg)\bigg)\delta_{\frac{jM}{n}},

it follows that the functions

P_{n}(z)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{itz}d\mu_{n}(t),\qquad(z\in\Pi)

belong to
\hbox{span}\{e^{itz}:t\geq 0\}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and moreover, there exists
C>0
such that
\sup_{z\in\Pi}|P_{n}(z)|\leq C
. Hence, for every
z\in\Pi
fixed,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|P_{n}(z)-I_{\beta}^{M}(z)\big|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{M}e^{itz}\,d\mu_{n}(t)-\int_{0}^{M}e^{itz}e^{-t\beta}\,dt\right|=0,

where the last limit is
0
because the latter integral converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
for
z
fixed since
\{\mu_{n}\}
converges to
\mu
in the sense of measures. Accordingly,
\{P_{n}\}
converges weak-star in
H^{\infty}(\Pi)
to
I_{\beta}^{M}
.

Arguing similarly with (
3.4
), it follows that the rational functions with simple poles outside
\overline{\Pi}
are contained in the closure of
\hbox{span}\{e^{itz}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
in the weak-star topology of
H^{\infty}(\Pi)
. Now, each rational function in
H^{\infty}(\Pi)
can be uniformly approximated in
\Pi
by rational functions with simple poles. In addition, it is well-known that rational functions with poles outside
\overline{\Pi}
are weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Pi)
(see, for instance,
[
14
, Corollary 1]
). Accordingly,
\hbox{span}\{e^{itz}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Pi)
and therefore, weak-star dense in
H^{p}(\Pi)
. Since
1<p<\infty
, it is dense in
H^{p}(\Pi)
in the weak topology and Mazur’s Lemma (see
[
13
, Corollary 3, Chapter 2]
) ensures the density in
H^{p}(\Pi)
.
∎

As previously mentioned, in
[
12
]
the authors established that the linear span of the eigenfunctions of any invertible composition operator of hyperbolic symbol is dense in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Using that fact, and reminding that for each
1<p\leq 2
the inclusion
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\hookrightarrow H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is continuous and that
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
lies densely within
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, Proposition
3.2
follows directly for
1<p<2
(and, by the same token, for
p=1
).

As a consequence of Proposition
3.2
, we obtain the density of certain glocal spectral subspaces associated to
C_{\varphi}
, which will allow us to apply Theorem
2.1
in order to obtain the desired result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
and
C_{\varphi}
the induced composition operator in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for some
1<p<\infty
. Then, the glocal spectral subspace
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi}}(\overline{U})
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for every non-empty relatively open subset
U
of
\sigma(C_{\varphi})
.

Proof.

Let
U
be a non-empty relatively open subset of
\sigma(C_{\varphi})
. Then,

\ker(C_{\varphi}-\lambda I)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi}}(\{\lambda\})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi}}(\overline{U}),\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in\overline{U}.

Taking into account that
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi}}(\overline{U})
is a linear manifold, we conclude that

\mathrm{span}\{\ker(C_{\varphi}-\lambda I):\lambda\in\overline{U}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi}}(\overline{U}).

Finally, since the closure of a non-empty open set always has cluster points, our result follows directly from Proposition
3.2
.
∎

With both propositions at hands, the proof of Theorem
3.1
is almost straightforward.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

As mentioned above, for each
1<p<\infty
, the point spectrum of
C_{\varphi}
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is an annulus and an argument involving the eigenfunctions yields that
C_{\varphi}
has not the SVEP (see
[
29
, Theorem 1.4]
). On the other hand, Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.3
imply that
C_{\varphi}^{*}
has the Dunford’s property
(C)
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
for every
1<p<\infty
.
∎

As a consequence, we are able to describe all the local spectral manifolds and characterize some of the (local) spectral properties of
C_{\varphi}:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
and
C_{\varphi}
the induced composition operator in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for some
1<p<\infty
. Let
C_{\varphi}^{*}
be its adjoint operator in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
and
p^{\prime}>1
such that
\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Then:

(i)

The point spectrum of
C_{\varphi}^{*}
is empty.

(ii)

H^{p^{\prime}}_{C_{\varphi}^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
for every closed set
F\subsetneq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*})
.

(iii)

\sigma_{C_{\varphi}^{*}}(f)=\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*})
for every non-zero
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
.

(iv)

r_{C_{\varphi}^{*}}(f)=r(C_{\varphi}^{*})
for every non-zero
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
.

(v)

If
M
is any non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
C_{\varphi}^{*}
, then

\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*}|_{M})=\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*})\quad\text{ or }\quad\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*}|_{M})=\big\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|\leq r(C_{\varphi}^{*})\big\}.

Proof.

Properties (i)-(iv) follow as a byproduct of Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.3
. A little extra argument is needed to state (v). Observe that

\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*})\subseteq\sigma({C_{\varphi}^{*}}|_{M})\subseteq\eta(\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*})).

Now, a result of Scroggs
[
26
]
states that if a point
\lambda
of a hole
K
of
\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*})
belongs to
\sigma({C_{\varphi}^{*}}|_{M})
, then
K\subset\sigma({C_{\varphi}}|_{M}),
which yields (v).
∎

4.
Dunford property for non-invertible composition operators

In this section, we characterize in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
the Dunford property
(C)
for non-invertible composition operators
C_{\varphi}
induced by linear fractional maps as well as their adjoints. Since loxodromic maps induce composition operators with disconnected spectrum in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
(and hence, they are decomposable) and the parabolic maps induce decomposable composition operators
[
27
]
, we are left with the hyperbolic non-automorphisms cases (of first and second kind).

4.1.
Hyperbolic non-automorphisms I

Let
\varphi
be a hyperbolic non-automorphism of first kind of
\mathbb{D}
, namely, a linear fractional map with a fixed point in
\mathbb{T}
and the other one in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a hyperbolic non-automorphism of first kind of
\mathbb{D}
. Then,
C_{\varphi}
does not have the SVEP in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for any
1\leq p<\infty
and
C_{\varphi}^{*}
has the Dunford’s property
(C)
.

If
\varphi
is a hyperbolic non-automorphism of first kind of
\mathbb{D}
, then
\varphi
is conjugated to one of its canonical form:

(4.1)

\varphi_{r}(z)=rz+(1-r),\qquad 0<r<1.

Accordingly, in order to prove Theorem
4.2
, it suffices to consider the transformations described in (
4.1
). It is straightforward that the functions

e_{s}(z)=(1-z)^{s},\qquad\PR(s)>-1/p

belong to
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and satisfy

C_{\varphi_{r}}e_{s}=r^{s}e_{s}.

Thus,
\{r^{s}:\PR(s)>-1/p\}=D(0,r^{-1/p})\subseteq\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(C_{\varphi_{r}}).
Indeed, Kamowitz
[
19
]
showed that

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}})=\overline{D(0,r^{-1/p})}.

Now, we proceed with the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\varphi=\varphi_{r}
for some
0<r<1
given by (
4.1
). Note that the map

s\in\big\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\PR(s)>-1/p\big\}\longmapsto e_{s}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})

is a vector-valued analytic map and consider
V=\{z\in D(0,r^{-1/p}):\PR(z)>0\}
. The map
f:V\rightarrow H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
given by

f(\lambda)=e_{\frac{\Log(\lambda)}{\Log(r)}},

where
\Log:\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the principal branch of the logarithm, is also a vector-valued analytic map that satisfies

(C_{\varphi_{r}}-\lambda)f(\lambda)=0,\qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in V,

which proves that
C_{\varphi_{r}}
does not have the SVEP, as claimed.

Now, we will apply Theorem
2.1
to derive the Dunford’s property
(C)
for the adjoint
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
. First, let us show that
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(\overline{U})
is dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for every non-empty relatively open subset
U
of
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}})
.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(\overline{U})
contains
\ker(C_{\varphi_{r}}-\lambda)
for every
\lambda\in\overline{U}
, it follows that

\textnormal{span}\{e_{s}:r^{s}\in\overline{U}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(\overline{U}).

Now, consider
\eta\in\textnormal{span}\{e_{s}:r^{s}\in\overline{U}\}^{\perp}\subset H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}.
Then, the holomorphic map

s\in\big\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\PR(s)>-1/p\big\}\longmapsto\langle e_{s},\eta\rangle\in\mathbb{C}

vanishes in every
s
such that
r^{s}\in\overline{U}.
Since
\overline{U}
has accumulation points, it follows that
\langle e_{s},\eta\rangle=0
for every
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\PR(s)>-1/p
. Now, observe that

\textnormal{span}\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}\subseteq\textnormal{span}\big\{e_{s}:\PR(s)>-1/p\big\}.

But
\textnormal{span}\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}
contains all the polynomials, hence it follows that
f\equiv 0
and so
\mathcal{H}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}^{p}(\overline{U})
is dense as claimed. In conclusion,
Theorem
2.1
yields that
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
has the Dunford property in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
.
∎

As a consequence, we deduce the following:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a hyperbolic non-automorphism map of first kind and
C_{\varphi}
the induced composition operator in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for some
1<p<\infty
. Let
C_{\varphi}^{*}
be its adjoint operator in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
and
p^{\prime}>1
such that
\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Then:

(i)

The point spectrum of
C_{\varphi}^{*}
is empty.

(ii)

H^{p^{\prime}}_{C_{\varphi}^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
for every closed set
F\subsetneq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*})
.

(iii)

\sigma_{C_{\varphi}^{*}}(f)=\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*})
for every non-zero
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
.

(iv)

r_{C_{\varphi}^{*}}(f)=r(C_{\varphi}^{*})
for every non-zero
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
.

(v)

If
M
is a non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
C_{\varphi}^{*}
, then
\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*}|_{M})=\sigma(C_{\varphi}^{*}).

4.4.
Hyperbolic non-automorphisms II

Now, we consider
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
hyperbolic non-automorphisms of second kind of
\mathbb{D}
, namely, linear fractional maps with a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
and the other one lying in
\mathbb{T}
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a hyperbolic non-automorphism of second kind. Then,
C_{\varphi}
has the Dunford’s property
(C)
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for every
1<p<\infty
. Moreover,
C_{\varphi}^{*}:H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}\to H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
does not have the SVEP for any
1<p<\infty
.

In order to prove this result, we consider the following maps, which have
0
and
1
as fixed points:

(4.2)

\varphi_{r}(z)=\frac{rz}{1-(1-r)z},\qquad 0<r<1.

It turns out that every hyperbolic non-automorphism of second kind is conjugated to one of the maps of the form (
4.2
). The spectrum of these composition operators
C_{\varphi_{r}}
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, for each
1\leq p<\infty
, was characterized by Kamowitz. Specifically,

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}})=\overline{D(0,r^{1/p})}\cup\{1\}.

Our approach is inspired by a construction of Shapiro that appears in
[
28
, p. 864]
and traces back to a joint paper with Bourdon
[
7
]
. The author shows that the operator
C_{\varphi_{r}}
acting on the Hilbert space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is unitarily equivalent to
I_{1}\oplus(rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*},
where
I_{1}
is the identity acting on the one-dimensional space of constants,
\psi_{r}(z)=rz+(1-r)
is a hyperbolic non-automorphism of first kind and
(rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*}
acts in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\varphi_{r}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a hyperbolic non-automorphism of second kind of the form (
4.2
). Then, for every
1<p<\infty,
the operator
C_{\varphi_{r}}
in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is similar to the operator
I_{1}\oplus(rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*}
, where
\psi_{r}(z)=rz+(1-r)
and
(rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*}
acts in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

Let
1<{p^{\prime}}<\infty
be such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{{p^{\prime}}}=1
, and consider
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
in
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
. Let
H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}):=zH^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}),
that is, the closed subspace of
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
-functions vanishing at
0
. It is easy to check that each functional in
H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
can be identified with a function of
H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, with the same dual pairing that relates
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
and
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Since
1
is the reproducing kernel at
0
and
\varphi_{r}(0)=0
, it follows that the rank-one operator
1\otimes 1
in
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
commutes with
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}.
Observe that
1\otimes 1
is a projection onto the one-dimensional space consisting of constants
\langle 1\rangle
, and so
I-1\otimes 1
is a projection onto
zH^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}).
Hence,
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
also commutes with
I-1\otimes 1
and therefore
zH^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
is invariant under
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
.

Now, denote by
M_{z}:H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
and
M_{1/z}:H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
the bounded operators of multiplication by
z
and
1/z
, respectively. Observe that these operators are inverses of each other. Similar arguments as in
[
7
, p. 35-36]
yield that

\langle C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}f,z^{n}\rangle=\langle M_{z}(rC_{\psi_{r}})M_{1/z}f,z^{n}\rangle

for every
f\in H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since polynomials are dense in
H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that

C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}|_{H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})}=M_{z}\big(rC_{\psi_{r}}|_{H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})}\big)M_{1/z}.

Finally, since
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})=\langle 1\rangle\oplus H_{0}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D}),
and
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}|_{\langle 1\rangle}=I_{1}
, we deduce that
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
is similar to
I_{1}\oplus(rC_{\psi_{r}})
, and the result follows by taking adjoints.
∎

With this result at hands, we are in position to prove Theorem
4.5
.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\varphi=\varphi_{r}
for some
0<r<1
of the canonical form in (
4.2
). By Lemma
4.6
, using the decomposition
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})=\langle 1\rangle\oplus H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, the operator
C_{\varphi_{r}}
is similar to
T:=I_{1}\oplus(rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*}
. Thus, by Corollary
4.3
, it follows that
\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(C_{\varphi_{r}})=\{1\}.
As a consequence, both
C_{\varphi_{r}}
and
T
have the SVEP.

Now, let
F\subsetneq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}})
be a closed set. Consider
f=f_{1}\oplus f_{2}\in H^{p}_{T}(F)
and
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\oplus\Gamma_{2}:\mathbb{C}\setminus F\rightarrow\langle 1\rangle\oplus H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
satisfying

(T-zI)\Gamma(z)=f\qquad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus F.

Observe that
(I_{1}-zI)\Gamma_{1}(z)=f_{1}
and
((rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*}-zI)\Gamma_{2}(z)=f_{2}
for every
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus F
, so
H^{p}_{T}(F)=H^{p}_{I_{1}}(F)\oplus H^{p}_{rC_{\psi_{r}}^{*}}(F).
Finally, by Theorem
4.2
,
H^{p}_{rC_{\psi_{r}}^{*}}(F)
is closed, so
H^{p}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(F)
is closed as well and
C_{\varphi_{r}}
has the Dunford property
(C)
, as claimed.

It remains to show that
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
does not have the SVEP in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
. Just recall that
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
is similar to
I_{1}\oplus(rC_{\psi_{r}})
acting in
H^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{D})
, and it is straightforward to check that the fact that
C_{\psi_{r}}
does not enjoy the SVEP (see Theorem
4.2
) implies that
C_{\varphi_{r}}^{*}
does not either, which yields the statement.
∎

Indeed, the identity
H^{p}_{T}(F)=H^{p}_{I_{1}}(F)\oplus H^{p}_{rC_{\psi_{r}}^{*}}(F)
obtained previously gives us plenty of information about local spectral features of
C_{\varphi_{r}}
, which we recollect in our next result:

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\varphi_{r}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a hyperbolic non-automorphism of second kind of the form (
4.2
) and
1<p<\infty.
Consider
C_{\varphi_{r}}
acting on
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
F\subsetneq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}})
a closed set. Then:

(i)

\sigma_{\text{{p}}}(C_{\varphi_{r}})=\{1\}
and its associated eigenspace is
\langle 1\rangle
.

(ii)

\displaystyle H^{p}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(F)=\begin{cases}\{0\},&\text{if }F\subsetneq\overline{D(0,r^{1/p})},\\
H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D}),&\text{if }F=\overline{D(0,r^{1/p})},\\
\langle 1\rangle,&\text{if }F=\{1\}.\end{cases}

(iii)

If
f
is a non-zero function in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, then

\sigma_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(f)=\begin{cases}\{1\},&\text{if }f\in\langle 1\rangle,\\
\overline{D(0,r^{1/p})},&\text{if }f\in H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D}),\\
\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(iv)

If
f
is a non-zero function in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, then

r_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(f)=\begin{cases}r^{1/p},&\text{if }f\in H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D}),\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(v)

Let
M\subset H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
be a non-trivial closed invariant subspace for
C_{\varphi_{r}}
. Then,

\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}|_{M})\in\big\{\{1\},\overline{D(0,r^{1/p})},\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}})\big\}.

Proof.

Note that (i) has been actually proved in the proof of Theorem
4.5
. To prove (ii), recall that
C_{\varphi_{r}}
is similar to
T:=I_{1}\oplus(rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*}
(where
(rC_{\psi_{r}})^{*}
is acting on
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
) and
H^{p}_{T}(F)=H^{p}_{I_{1}}(F)\oplus H^{p}_{rC_{\psi_{r}}^{*}}(F)
for every closed set
F\subsetneq\sigma(C_{\varphi_{r}}).
By Corollary
4.3
, if
F\subsetneq\overline{D(0,r^{1/p})}
, then
H^{p}_{rC_{\psi_{r}}^{*}}(F)=\{0\}
. Since
H^{p}_{I_{1}}(F)=\{0\}
as well, we deduce that
H^{p}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(F)=\{0\}
in that case.

On the other hand, if
F=\overline{D(0,r^{1/p})}
, then
H^{p}_{rC_{\psi_{r}}^{*}}(F)=H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, and bringing it back via the isomorphism
M_{z}:H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
we deduce that
H^{p}_{C_{\varphi_{r}}}(F)=H_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{D}),
as stated. Finally, it is straightforward to check that
H^{p}_{T}({{1}})=\langle 1\rangle,
which completes the proof of (ii). The properties (iii)-(v) follow easily from (ii) and the basic properties of local spectra and spectral subspaces.
∎

A final remark: stability of the Dunford property

Finally, in the class of linear fractional composition operators, we address the question whether the product of two commuting operators with the Dunford property
(C)
inherits this property (see
[
1
]
for recent results). It is well-known that if two linear fractional composition operators
C_{\varphi}
and
C_{\psi}
commute,
\varphi
and
\psi
has the same fixed points (see
[
9
]
, for instance). In addition,
C_{\varphi}C_{\psi}
is the composition operator
C_{\psi\circ\varphi}
and each of the classes in the classification of the linear transformations of
\mathbb{D}
according to their fixed points remains invariant under composition. Hence, keeping in mind the Table
1
, we are left with two cases:
if
\varphi
is a parabolic automorphism and
\psi
a parabolic non-automorphism (or viceversa) and if
\varphi
is an elliptic automorphism an
\psi
a loxodromic map with fix points 0 and
\infty
(or viceversa). In the first case,
\psi\circ\varphi
is a parabolic non-automorphism and in the second one is a loxodromic map. Accordingly, in both cases
C_{\psi\circ\varphi}
is decomposable, and therefore satisfies the Dunford property
(C)
.

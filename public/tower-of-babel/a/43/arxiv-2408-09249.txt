Polarization effects in the elastic e ​ p → → e → ​ p e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p and e → ​ p → → e ​ p \vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep processes in the case of parallel spins
M.V. Galynskii, V.V. Bytev, V.M. Galynsky

Abstract
In the one-photon exchange approximation, we analyze polarization effects in the elastic e → ​ p → → e ​ p \vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep and e ​ p → → e → ​ p e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p processes in the case when the spin quantization axes of a target proton at rest and an incident or scattered electron are parallel. To do this, in the kinematics of the SANE Collaboration experiment [A. Liyanage et al. , Phys. Rev. C 101 , 035206 (2020) ] using the J. Kelly [ Phys. Rev. C 70 , 068202 (2004) ] and [I. Qattan et al. Phys. Rev. C 91 , 065203 (2015) ] parametrizations for the Sachs form factor ratio R ≡ μ p ​ G E / G M R\equiv\mu_{p}G_{E}/G_{M} , a numerical analysis was carried out of the dependence of the longitudinal polarization degree transferred to the scattered electron in the e ​ p → → e → ​ p e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p process and double spin asymmetry in the e → ​ p → → e ​ p \vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep process on the square of the momentum transferred to the proton as well as on the scattering angle of the electron. It is established that the difference in the longitudinal polarization degree of the scattered electron in the e ​ p → → e → ​ p e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p process in the case of conservation and violation of the scaling of the Sachs form factors can reach 70 %. This fact can be used to set up polarization experiments of a new type to measure the ratio R R . For double spin asymmetry in the e → ​ p → → e ​ p \vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep process, the corresponding difference does not exceed 2.32 %. This fact means that it’s not sensitive to the effects of the Sachs form factor scaling violation and could be used as test for the R ≈ 1 R\approx 1 equality.

II
Helicity and diagonal spin bases

The spin 4-vector
s=(s_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$s$})
of the fermion with 4-momentum
p
(
p^{2}=m^{2})
satisfying
the conditions of orthogonality and normalization is given by

\displaystyle s=(s_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$s$}),\;s_{0}=\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$c$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$p$}}{m},\;\mbox{\boldmath$s$}=\mbox{\boldmath$c$}+\frac{\,(\mbox{\boldmath$c$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$p$})\,\mbox{\boldmath$p$}}{m(p_{0}+m)\,},

(2)

where
c
is the spin quantization axis (
\mbox{\boldmath$c$}^{2}=1
).

Expressions (
2
) allow us to determine the spin 4-vector
s=(s_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$s$})

by a given 4-momentum
p=(p_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$p$})
and 3-vector
c
. On the contrary, if the 4-vector

s
is known, then the spin quantization axis
c
is given by

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$c$}=\mbox{\boldmath$s$}-\frac{s_{0}}{p_{0}+m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$p$},

(3)

i.e. the vectors
c
and
s
at a given
p
uniquely define each other.

For calculation of polarization effects in high-energy physics process one usually utilize helicity basis
introduced by Jacob and Wick
[
46
]
, in which the spin quantization axis
c

is directed along the momentum of the particle

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$c$}=\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=\mbox{\boldmath$p$}/|\mbox{\boldmath$p$}|,

while the spin 4-vector
s
(
2
) reads

\displaystyle s=(s_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$s$})=(|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}|,v_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}),

where
v_{0}
and
v
are the time and space components of the 4-velocity vector
v=p/m
(
v^{2}=1
).

The popularity of the helicity basis is primarily due to the simplicity of the physical
interpretation of the helicity definition (projection of the spin in the direction of the particle momentum),
and its emphasis on the center of mass system. At the same time, studying
of helicities of moving particles is analogous to the study of the spins of particles at rest
[
48
,
47
]
.
However, there are several important factors which prevent helicity from playing the dominant
role in describing the spin projection of particles. One is that helicity is not a particle
characteristic that is invariant under the Lorentz transformation
[
48
,
47
,
49
,
50
]
.
In interpreting the dynamics of spin interaction, the amplitudes of scattering processes
with and without changing the sign of the particle helicity are often referred to as
amplitudes with and without a spin flip. However, since the particle momentum
is changed by the interaction, it is clear that such a classification is
very arbitrary. Both types of amplitudes actually describe a process with
a change in the particle spin state.

In general, for a system of two particles with different 4-momenta

q_{1}=(q_{10},\mbox{\boldmath$q_{1}$})
(before interaction) and
q_{2}=(q_{20},\mbox{\boldmath$q_{2}$})

(after interaction) the possibility of quantization of spins in one common direction,
including the case when particles have different masses, is determined
by the three-dimensional vector given by
[
47
]

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$a$}=\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{2}/q_{20}-\mbox{\boldmath$q$}_{1}/q_{10}.

(4)

Since the common spin quantization axis (
4
) defines the spin basis
other than the helicity one and is the difference of two three-dimensional vectors,
the geometric image of which is the diagonal of a parallelogram, it is natural to call
it the diagonal spin basis (DSB). For the first time, in a four-dimensional covariant form,
the DSB was constructed in the work
[
51
]
in the process

\displaystyle e(p_{1})+p\,(q_{1},s_{p_{1}})\to e(p_{2})+p\,(q_{2},s_{p_{2}}).

(5)

In it, the spin 4-vectors of the initial and final protons

and
s_{p_{2}}

and

51

\displaystyle s_{p_{1}}=\frac{m^{2}q_{2}-(q_{1}q_{2})\,q_{1}}{m\sqrt{(q_{1}q_{2})^{2}-m^{4}}}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle s_{p_{2}}=\frac{(q_{1}q_{2})\,q_{2}-m^{2}q_{1}}{m\sqrt{(q_{1}q_{2})^{2}-m^{4}}}\,.

In the laboratory frame (LF), where the initial proton rests,

\displaystyle s_{p_{1}}=(0,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}_{2})\,,\;s_{p_{2}}=(|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{2}|,v_{20}\,\mbox{\boldmath$n_{2}$}),

where

In the ultrarelativistic limit, when the masses of protons can be neglected,

The fundamental fact that the Lorentz little group common to a system of two
particles with different momenta is realized in the DSB leads to a number
of remarkable consequences. First, in this basis, particles before and after interaction in the scattering channel
have common spin operators
[

interactions with and without changing of the spin states of the particles involved in the reaction,
making it possible to trace the dynamics of the spin interaction.
Second, in the DSB, the mathematical structure of the amplitudes is maximally
simplified owing to the coincidence of the particle spin operators, the separation of Wigner
rotations from the amplitudes
[
51
,
52

scalar products of 4-vectors that characterize the reaction. Third, in the DSB,
the spin states of massless particles coincide up to the sign with the helicity states,
see Eqs. (

\displaystyle{b_{0}}_{\mu}\,{b_{0}}_{\nu}-{b_{1}}_{\mu}\,{b_{1}}_{\nu}-{b_{2}}_{\mu}\,{b_{2}}_{\nu}-{b_{3}}_{\mu}\,{b_{3}}_{\nu}=g_{\mu\nu},

(24)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the metric tensor in the Minkowski space,
which is naturally divided into the sum of the longitudinal and transverse parts:

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}^{\|}+g_{\mu\nu}^{\bot},

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{\|}={b_{0}}_{\mu}\,{b_{0}}_{\nu}-{b_{3}}_{\mu}\,{b_{3}}_{\nu},

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{\bot}=-{b_{1}}_{\mu}\,{b_{1}}_{\nu}-{b_{2}}_{\mu}\,{b_{2}}_{\nu}.

For the transverse part of the metric tensor we have

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{\bot}=g_{\mu\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}^{\|}.

In terms of
(q_{\pm})_{\mu}
,
(q_{\pm})_{\nu}
, the tensor
g^{\bot}_{\mu\nu}
has the form

\displaystyle g^{\bot}_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{(q_{+})_{\mu}(q_{+})_{\nu}}{q_{+}^{2}}+\frac{(q_{-})_{\mu}(q_{-})_{\nu}\,}{-q_{-}^{2}}.

(25)

For calculations we also use the Mandelstam variables

\displaystyle s=(p_{1}+q_{1})^{2},\,t=(q_{2}-q_{1})^{2},\,u=(q_{2}-p_{1})^{2},

(26)

with the standard connection equation

\displaystyle s+t+u=2m_{0}^{2}+2m^{2}.

By reversing the relation in Eqs. (
26
), for scalar products in terms of
s,t,u
we have:

\displaystyle 2p_{1}q_{1}=2p_{2}q_{2}=s-m_{0}^{2}-m^{2},\;

\displaystyle 2p_{1}q_{2}=2p_{2}q_{1}=m_{0}^{2}+m^{2}-u,\,

(27)

\displaystyle 2p_{1}p_{2}=2m_{0}^{2}-t,\;p_{+}q_{+}=s-u,

\displaystyle 2q_{1}q_{2}=2m^{2}-t,\,q_{+}^{2}=4m^{2}-t.

III.1
Ultrarelativistic limit

In the ultrarelativistic limit, when the mass of an electron can be neglected,
for the Mandelstam variables in the LF we have:

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=m^{2}+2mE_{1},

\displaystyle-t

\displaystyle=Q^{2}=4E_{1}E_{2}\sin^{2}(\theta_{e}/2),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=m^{2}-2mE_{2},

where the

The energies of the final electron

\displaystyle q_{-}^{2}=-2mE_{-},\;E_{2p}=m+E_{-},\,E_{\pm}=E_{1}\pm E_{2}.

The dependences of

V.1
Differential cross section of the
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
process

In the OPE approximation, the matrix elements of the process
ep\to ep
are the product of the electron
(J_{e})
and proton currents
(J_{p})

\displaystyle M_{ep\to ep}=4\pi\alpha\,T_{ep}/q^{2},

(39)

\displaystyle T_{ep}=(J_{e})^{\mu}(J_{p})_{\mu}\,.

(40)

The lepton
(J_{e})^{\mu}
and proton
(J_{p})_{\mu}
currents read:

\displaystyle(J_{e})^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{u}(p_{2})\gamma^{\mu}u(p_{1}),

\displaystyle(J_{p})_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{u}(q_{2})\Gamma_{\mu}(q^{2})u(q_{1}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{1}\gamma_{\mu}+\frac{F_{2}}{4m}(\;\hat{q}\gamma_{\mu}-\gamma_{\mu}\hat{q}\;).

Here
u(p_{i})
and
u(q_{i})
are the bispinors of electrons and protons
with the 4-momenta
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
, respectively, where

p_{i}^{2}=m_{0}^{2}
and
q_{i}^{2}=m^{2}
, having the properties

\overline{u}(p_{i})u(p_{i})=2m_{0}
and
\overline{u}(q_{i})u(q_{i})=2m

(i=1,2)
;

F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are the Dirac and Pauli proton form factors, respectively;

q=q_{-}=q_{2}-q_{1}
is the 4-momentum transferred to the proton;

\hat{q}=(q)_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}
, where
\gamma^{\mu}
are the Dirac matrices.

It is well known that the SFFs
G_{E}
and
G_{M}
could be expressed in terms of
the Dirac and Pauli proton form factors:

\displaystyle G_{E}=F_{1}-\tau_{p}F_{2}\,,\;G_{M}=F_{1}+F_{2}.

The differential cross section of the process
ep\to ep
reads

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{ep\to ep}}{dt}=\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{\lambda_{s}}\,\frac{|T_{ep}|^{2}}{t^{2}}\,,

(41)

where
\lambda_{s}=(s-(m+m_{0})^{2})(s-(m-m_{0})^{2})
is Källén’s function.

In the standard approach
[
49
,
50
]
, the calculation of the squares of the amplitude
modules
|T_{ep}|^{2}
is reduced to the convolution of the lepton (
L^{\mu\nu}
)
and hadron (
H_{\mu\nu}
) tensors

\displaystyle|T_{ep}|^{2}=H_{\mu\nu}L^{\mu\nu},

where

\displaystyle L^{\mu\nu}=\textmd{Tr\,}(\tau_{e_{2}}\gamma^{\mu}\tau_{e_{1}}\gamma^{\nu}),

(42)

\displaystyle H_{\mu\nu}=2\,\textmd{Tr\,}(\tau_{p_{2}}\,\Gamma_{\mu}\tau_{p_{1}}\,\overline{\Gamma}_{\nu})\,.~~~

(43)

Here symbol “Tr” denotes the operation to calculate the trace from Dirac’s operators,

\tau_{e_{i}}
and
\tau_{p_{i}}

(i=1,2)
are the polarization density matrices
of the initial and final states of electrons and protons (
\lambda_{p_{1}}
and

\lambda_{e_{2}}
are the degrees of polarization of the initial proton
and the final electron, and
\gamma_{5}
is the Dirac’s matrix):

\displaystyle\tau_{e_{1}}=(\hat{p_{1}}+m_{0})/2,\,

\displaystyle\tau_{e_{2}}=(\hat{p_{2}}+m_{0})(1-\lambda_{e_{2}}\gamma_{5}\hat{s}_{e_{2}})/2\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\tau_{p_{1}}=(\hat{q_{1}}+m)(1-\lambda_{p_{1}}\gamma_{5}\hat{s}_{p_{1}})/2\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{p_{2}}=(\hat{q_{2}}+m)/2\,.

The lepton tensor
L^{\mu\nu}
(
42
) in terms of the 4-vectors
p_{\pm}
has the form:

\displaystyle 2L^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{+}^{\mu}p_{+}^{\nu}-p_{-}^{\mu}p_{-}^{\nu}+p_{-}^{2}g^{\mu\nu}+2im_{0}\lambda_{e_{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(p_{-})_{\rho}(s_{e_{2}})_{\sigma}.

In the ultrarelativistic limit when
s_{e_{2}}=p_{2}/m_{0}
, it takes the form

\displaystyle 2L^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{+}^{\mu}p_{+}^{\nu}-p_{-}^{\mu}p_{-}^{\nu}+p_{-}^{2}g^{\mu\nu}+i\lambda_{e_{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(p_{-})_{\rho}(p_{+})_{\sigma}.

The explicit form of the tensor
H_{\mu\nu}
(
43
) is rather cumbersome;
for this reason we omit it.

The expression for
|T_{ep}|^{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle 2\,|T_{ep}|^{2}=\frac{4m^{2}}{q_{+}^{2}}|T|^{2}.

Since
q_{+}^{2}=4m^{2}(1+\tau_{p}
), then the differential cross section of the process (
10
) calculated in an arbitrary reference frame in the DSB (
12
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{2\lambda_{s}(1+\tau_{p})}\,\frac{|{T}|^{2}}{t^{2}},

(45)

\displaystyle|T|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{0}+\lambda_{p_{1}}\lambda_{e_{2}}I_{1},

(46)

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{E}^{\,2}Y_{1}+\tau_{p}\,G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{2},

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{p}(G_{E}G_{M}Y_{3}+G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{4}),

where
\lambda_{p_{1}}
and
\lambda_{e_{2}}
are the degrees of polarization of the initial proton
and the final electron; the functions
Y_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots 4
) are given by

\displaystyle Y_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{+}q_{+})^{2}+q_{+}^{2}q_{-}^{2},

\displaystyle Y_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{+}q_{+})^{2}-q_{+}^{2}(q_{-}^{2}+4m_{0}^{2}),

(47)

\displaystyle-Y_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\kappa_{1}\,m^{2}\,((p_{+}q_{+})^{2}+q_{+}^{2}(q_{-}^{2}-4\,m_{0}^{2}\,))\,z_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle Y_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(m^{2}p_{+}q_{+}-\kappa_{1}q_{+}^{2})(\kappa_{1}p_{+}q_{+}-m_{0}^{2}q_{+}^{2})z_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle z_{1}=(\kappa_{1}^{2}-m^{2}m_{0}^{2})^{-1/2}\,,\;\kappa_{1}=q_{1}p_{2}.

In the case of arbitrary spin 4-vectors
s_{p_{1}}
and
s_{e_{2}}
,
the expressions
for
Y_{3}
and
Y_{4}
in the cross section (
45
) are given by

\displaystyle Y_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\,m_{0}m\,(s_{p_{1}})^{\mu}(g^{\bot})_{\mu\nu}(s_{e_{2}})^{\nu}q_{+}^{2}\,,

(48)

\displaystyle Y_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\,m_{0}m\,(q_{+}s_{p_{1}})(q_{+}s_{e_{2}}).

Note, first, that the polarized part of the cross section (
45
)
includes the term with
Y_{3}
containing the product of the SFFs,
G_{E}G_{M}
,
and according to (
48
), is determined by the transverse part of the metric
tensor
(g^{\bot})_{\mu\nu}
(
25
);
second, in the cross section of the process
e\vec{p}\to e\vec{p}

in DSB (
6
) there is no similar structure, see
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
.

In the ultrarelativistic limit, when the mass of an electron can be neglected,
for the functions
Y_{i}
(
47
) in the LF we obtain expressions, that depend only on the energy of the initial and final electrons:

\displaystyle Y_{1}=8m^{2}(2E_{1}E_{2}-mE_{-}),

\displaystyle Y_{2}=8m^{2}(E_{1}^{\,2}+E_{2}^{\,2}+mE_{-}),

(49)

\displaystyle Y_{3}=-(2m/E_{2})\,Y_{1},

\displaystyle Y_{4}=8m^{2}E_{+}E_{-}(m-E_{2})/E_{2}.

V.2
Polarization of the final electron in the
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
process

The expression for the square of the amplitude modulus
|T|^{2}
(
46
) can be written as

\displaystyle|T|^{2}=I_{0}+\lambda_{p_{1}}\lambda_{e_{2}}I_{1}=I_{0}\,(1+\lambda_{e_{2}}\lambda_{e_{2}}^{f}),

(50)

where
\lambda_{e_{2}}^{f}
is the degree of longitudinal polarization
transferred from the initial proton to the final electron in the
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
process:

\displaystyle\lambda_{e_{2}}^{f}=\lambda_{p_{1}}\frac{I_{1}}{I_{0}}=\lambda_{p_{1}}\frac{\tau_{p}\,(G_{E}G_{M}Y_{3}+G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{4})}{G_{E}^{\,2}Y_{1}+\tau_{p}G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{2}}.

Dividing the numerator and denominator in the last expression by
Y_{1}G_{M}^{\,2}
and introducing
the experimentally measured ratio
R\equiv\mu_{p}G_{E}/G_{M}
, we get:

\displaystyle\lambda_{e_{2}}^{f}=\lambda_{p_{1}}\,\frac{\mu_{p}\tau_{p}\,((Y_{3}/Y_{1})R+\mu_{p}(Y_{4}/Y_{1}))}{R^{\,2}+\mu_{p}^{2}\,\tau_{p}\,(Y_{2}/Y_{1})\,}.

(51)

Note that for the ratio
Y_{2}/Y_{1}
in the denominator of expression (
51
)
in the LF, the equality
Y_{2}/Y_{1}=1/\varepsilon
is valid, where
\varepsilon
is the degree
of linear polarization of the virtual photon (
38
).

Inverting relation (
51
), we obtain a quadratic equation with respect to
R

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}R^{2}-\alpha_{1}R+\alpha_{0}\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2}=0

(52)

with the coefficients

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=\lambda_{e_{2}}^{f}/\lambda_{p_{1}},\;\alpha_{1}=\tau_{p}\,\mu_{p}\,Y_{3}/Y_{1},

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}=\tau_{p}\,\mu_{p}^{2}\,Y_{4}/Y_{1},\;\alpha_{3}=\tau_{p}\,\mu_{p}^{2}\,Y_{2}/Y_{1}.

Solutions to Eq. (
52
) read

\displaystyle R=\frac{\alpha_{1}\pm\sqrt{\alpha_{1}^{2}-4\alpha_{0}(\alpha_{0}\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2})}}{2\alpha_{0}}.

They allow us to extract the ratio
R
from the results of an experiment to measure the polarization
transferred to the electron
\lambda_{e_{2}}^{f}
in the
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
process.

VI
Polarization effects in the
\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep
process

VI.1
Cross section of the
\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep
process

In the OPE approximation, the differential cross section of the process (
16
), calculated in an arbitrary reference
frame in DSB (
18
), read

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{\lambda_{s}(1+\tau_{p})}\,\frac{|{T}|^{2}}{t^{2}},

(55)

\displaystyle|T|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{0}+\lambda_{e_{1}}\lambda_{p_{1}}I_{1},

(56)

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{E}^{\,2}Y_{1}+\tau_{p}\,G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{2},

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{p}(G_{E}G_{M}Y_{3}+G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{4}),

where
\lambda_{e_{1}}
and
\lambda_{p_{1}}
are the degrees of polarization of the initial
electron and proton, and the functions
Y_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots 4
) read

\displaystyle Y_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{+}q_{+})^{2}+q_{+}^{2}q_{-}^{2},

\displaystyle Y_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p_{+}q_{+})^{2}-q_{+}^{2}(q_{-}^{2}+4m_{0}^{2}),

(57)

\displaystyle-Y_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\kappa_{2}\,m^{2}((p_{+}q_{+})^{2}+q_{+}^{2}(q_{-}^{2}-4\,m_{0}^{2}\,))\,z_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle Y_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(m^{2}p_{+}q_{+}-\kappa_{2}q_{+}^{2})(\kappa_{2}p_{+}q_{+}-m_{0}^{2}q_{+}^{2})z_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle z_{2}=(\kappa_{2}^{2}-m^{2}m_{0}^{2})^{-1/2}\,,\;\kappa_{2}=q_{1}p_{1}.

In the case of arbitrary spin 4-vectors
s_{e_{1}}
and
s_{p_{1}}
, the expressions
for
Y_{3}
and
Y_{4}
in the cross section (
55
) have the form:

\displaystyle Y_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\,m_{0}m\,(s_{p_{1}})^{\mu}(g^{\bot})_{\mu\nu}(s_{e_{1}})^{\nu}q_{+}^{2}\,,

(58)

\displaystyle Y_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\,m_{0}m\,(q_{+}s_{p_{1}})(q_{+}s_{e_{1}}).

Note, first, that the polarized part of the cross section (
55
) includes the term
with
Y_{3}
containing the product of the SFFs,
G_{E}G_{M}
, and according to (
58
), is determined by the transverse part of the metric tensor

(g^{\bot})_{\mu\nu}
(
25
).
Second, there is no similar structure in the cross section of the

e\vec{p}\to e\vec{p}
process in the DSB (
6
), see
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
.

In the ultrarelativistic limit, when the mass of an electron can be neglected,
for the functions
Y_{i}
(
57
) in the LF we obtain expressions, that depend only on the energy of the initial and final electrons:

\displaystyle Y_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8m^{2}(2E_{1}E_{2}-mE_{-}),

\displaystyle Y_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8m^{2}(E_{1}^{\,2}+E_{2}^{\,2}+mE_{-}),

(59)

\displaystyle-Y_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2m/E_{1})\,Y_{1},

\displaystyle-Y_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8m^{2}E_{+}E_{-}(m+E_{1})/E_{1}.

The polarization asymmetry in the process (
16
) is determined by the square
of the amplitude modulus (
56
) as follows
[
55
,
56
]
:

\displaystyle A=\frac{|{T}|^{2}(\lambda_{e_{1}}=-1)-|{T}|^{2}(\lambda_{e_{1}}=+1)}{|{T}|^{2}(\lambda_{e_{1}}=-1)+|{T}|^{2}(\lambda_{e_{1}}=+1)}.

As a result we have

\displaystyle A=-\lambda_{p_{1}}\frac{\tau_{p}\,(G_{E}G_{M}Y_{3}+G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{4})}{G_{E}^{\,2}Y_{1}+\tau_{p}G_{M}^{\,2}Y_{2}}.

By dividing the numerator and denominator in the last expression into
Y_{1}G_{M}^{\,2}
and introducing
the experimentally measured ratio
R\equiv\mu_{p}G_{E}/G_{M}
, we get:

\displaystyle A=-\lambda_{p_{1}}\,\frac{\mu_{p}\tau_{p}\,((Y_{3}/Y_{1})R+\mu_{p}(Y_{4}/Y_{1}))}{R^{\,2}+\mu_{p}^{2}\,\tau_{p}\,(Y_{2}/Y_{1})\,}.

(60)

Note that expressions (
60
) for polarization asymmetry in the
\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep

process and the expressions (
51
) for electron transferred polarization
\lambda_{e_{2}}^{f}

in the
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
process coincide up to the sign.
For this reason, the quadratic equation for extracting the ratio

R
coincides with the explicit form of equation (
52
) and has
coefficients of the same shape, except for one:
\alpha_{0}=-A_{exp}/\lambda_{p_{1}}
,
where
A_{exp}
is experimentally measured polarization asymmetry.

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, in the one-photon exchange approximation we analyze polarization effects
in the elastic
\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep
and
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
processes in the case
when the spin quantization axes of the target proton at rest and the incident or scattered
electron are parallel.
To do this, in the kinematics of the SANE Collaboration experiment
[
17
]

using the Kelly
[
37
]
and Qattan
[
38
]
parametrizations for the Sachs form factor
ratio
R\equiv\mu_{p}G_{E}/G_{M}
, a numerical analysis was carried out of the dependence of
the longitudinal polarization degree transferred to the scattered electron in the
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p

process and the double spin asymmetry in the
\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep
process on the square
of the momentum transferred to the proton as well as on the scattering angle of the electron.
As it turned out, the Kelly
[
37
]
and Qattan
[
38
]
parametrizations
give almost identical results.

As a result of calculations, it was established that the relative difference
in the longitudinal polarization degree of the final electron in the

e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
process for the case of conservation and violation
of the SFF scaling can reach 70 %, which can be used to conduct a polarization experiment of
a new type of measurement of the SFF ratio
R
.

For the double spin asymmetry in the
\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep
process this difference
is rather small and does not exceed 1.79 % for the electron beam energy
E_{1}=4.725
GeV
and 2.32 % for
E_{1}=5.895
GeV. For this reason, the measurement of polarization asymmetry
in the
\vec{e}\vec{p}\to ep
process can be used as a test to verify the conservation of the SFF scaling.

At present, the experiment on measuring the degree of longitudinal polarization transferred
to the final electron in the process
e\vec{p}\to\vec{e}p
seems to be quite realistic,
since a proton target with a high degree of polarization
P_{t}=70\pm 5
% has been already created
and is used in the experiment
[
17
]
. For this
reason, it would be most appropriate to conduct the proposed experiment at the setup
used in
[
17
]
at the same proton polarization degree
P_{t}=0.70
, electron beam
energies
E_{1}=4.725
GeV and 5.895 GeV.

The difference between the proposed experiment and the one in
[
17
]

consists in the fact that an incident electron beam must be unpolarized, and the detected
scattered electron must move strictly along the direction of the spin quantization axis
of a resting proton target. In the proposed experiment, it is necessary to measure only
the longitudinal polarization degree of the scattered electron,which is an advantage
compared to the AR method
[
4
]
used in JLab polarization experiments.

A note on d d -maximal p p -groups I
Messab Aiech, Hanifa Zekraoui, Yassine Guerboussa

Abstract
. A finite p p -group G G is said to be d d -maximal if d ⁡ ( H ) < d ⁡ ( G ) d(H)<d(G) for every subgroup H < G H<G , where d ⁡ ( G ) d(G) denotes the minimal number of generators of G G . A similar definition can be formulated when G G is acted on by some group A A . We generalize results of B. Kahn and T. Laffey to the latter case, and give them in particular alternative short proofs. We answer moreover a question of Y. Berkovich about the minimal non-metacyclic p p -groups.

Introduction

Let

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group acted on by a group
A
, and assume that
G
is
d
-maximal for
A
-subgroups.

(1)

If
G
is abelian, then it is elementary abelian.

(2)

If
N
is a normal
A
-subgroup of
G
such that
N\leq\Phi(G)
, then
G/N
is a
d
-maximal for
A
-subgroups.

(3)

\Phi(G)=[G,G]
.

(4)

The factors
\gamma_{n}/\gamma_{n+1}
of the lower central series
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
G
are elementary abelian.

(5)

If
M
is an
A
-subgroup of
G
of index
p
, then
d(M)=r-1
, where
r=d(G)
.

Proof.

For (1), observe that
G_{1}=\{x\in G\mid x^{p}=1\}
is an
A
-subgroup, and
d(G)=d(G_{1})
; hence
G=G_{1}
. For (2), observe that
d(H/N)\leq d(H)
for every
A
-subgroup
H\leq G
containing
N
, and that
d(G/N)=d(G)
since
\Phi(G/N)=\Phi(G)/N
. As
[G,G]
is
A
-invariant, (3) follows at once from (2) and (1). Next, note that for every integer
n\geq 1
, we have a canonical epimorphism
x\gamma_{2}\otimes y\gamma_{n}\mapsto[x,y]\gamma_{n+1}
, from
\gamma_{1}/\gamma_{2}\otimes\gamma_{n}/\gamma_{n+1}
to
\gamma_{n+1}/\gamma_{n+2}
. Now, (4) follows at once from (3) and induction on
n
. For (5), observe that
|\Phi(G):\Phi(M)|<|G:M|=p
, so
\Phi(M)=\Phi(G)
; the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Assume
G
is a counterexample of minimal order; in particular
\gamma_{3}(G)\neq 1
. By viewing
\gamma_{3}(G)
as a normal subgroup of
\Gamma=G\rtimes A
, we obtain
[\gamma_{3}(G),\Gamma]<\gamma_{3}(G)
; choose therefore a maximal subgroup
N\leq\gamma_{3}(G)
which contains
[\gamma_{3}(G),\Gamma]
. Hence,
N
is a normal
A
-subgroup of
G
,
G/N
is
d
-maximal by Lemma
2.1
(2), and plainly
\gamma_{3}(G/N)\neq 1
. The minimality of
G
implies
N=1
, so
|\gamma_{3}(G)|=p
.

Let
Z=\gamma_{2}(G)\cap Z(G)
, and consider the map
g\mapsto g^{*}
, from
G
to
\mathrm{Hom}(\gamma_{2}(G)/Z,\gamma_{3}(G))
, defined by
g^{*}(tZ)=[g,t]
for all
t\in\gamma_{2}(G)
. The latter is readily seen to be a group homomorphism whose kernel is equal to
C=C_{G}(\gamma_{2}(G))
. Now,
G/C
embeds in
\mathrm{Hom}(\gamma_{2}(G)/Z,\gamma_{3}(G))
, and the latter is isomorphic to
\gamma_{2}(G)/Z
(since
\gamma_{2}(G)/Z
is elementary abelian); thus
|G:C|\leq|\gamma_{2}(G):Z|
; equivalently
p^{d(G)}\leq|C:Z|
. We claim that
\Phi(C)\leq Z
. Once this is proved, it follows that
d(G)\leq d(C)
, which is absurd since
C
is
A
-invariant and
C<G
(as
G
has class
3
).

For the last claim, observe that
[C,G,C]=[G,C,C]=1
, so by the three subgroups lemma,
[C,C,G]=1
, that is
[C,C]\leq Z
. Hence, we have only to show that
C^{p}\leq Z
. Let
c\in C
and
g\in G
. As
G
has class
3
, one easily sees by induction on
n
that

[c,g^{n}]=[c,g]^{n}[c,g,g]^{\binom{n}{2}}.

For
n=p
, we have
[c,g^{p}]=1
as
G^{p}\leq\gamma_{2}(G)
, and
[c,g,g]^{\binom{p}{2}}=1
as
|\gamma_{3}(G)|=p
; hence
[c,g]^{p}=1
. But
[c^{p},g]=[c,g]^{p}
because
c
commutes with
\gamma_{2}(G)
. Thus
C^{p}\leq Z
, as desired.

We have

3.
The result of being powerful

Suppose
X
and
Y
are two normal subgroups of
G
; it is well-known that

X\leq YX^{p}[X,G]\implies X\leq Y.

(Otherwise, one can find a maximal
G
-invariant subgroup
W\leq X
that contains
X\cap Y
, but such a
W
also contains
X^{p}
and
[X,G]
, a contradiction.) The point here is that in order to establish
X\leq Y
, one can suppose that
X^{p}=[X,G]=1
(in other words, we prove our claim in
G/X^{p}[X,G]
, and then deduce it for
G
by the above implication). This trick has been used in proving Theorem
1.6
; we shall use it to prove the next result as well.

Lemma 3.1
.

[cf.
[
7
, Corollary 1 ]
]
Let
G
be a
2
-group, and
N,M\leq G
.

(i)

If
N
is almost powerfully embedded in
G
, then so are
N^{2}
,
[N,G]
.

(ii)

If
N,M\leq G^{2}
, and both of them are almost powerfully embedded in
G
, then so are
NM
and
[N,M]
.

Proof.

(i) Let
X=[N,G]
or
N^{2}
. We have
X\leq G^{2}
, so by Theorem
1.6
, we have only to show that
[X,G]\leq X^{2}
.

For
X=[N,G]
, we may assume
[N,G,G,G]=1
. It follows that
[N^{2},G]
lies in
[N,G]^{2}
. Indeed, for
a\in N
and
g\in G
, we have

[a^{2},g]=[a,g][a,g,a][a,g]=[a,g]^{2}[a,g,a].

Thus,
[N^{2},G]\leq[N,G]^{2}[N^{2},N]
. Assuming
g\in N
, it follows from the above identity that
[N^{2},N]\leq\gamma_{2}(N)^{2}\gamma_{3}(N)
. Next, observe that
\gamma_{3}(N)\leq[N^{4},N]
; so replacing
a
by
a^{2}
yields
[a^{4},g]=[a^{2},g]^{2}[a^{2},g,a^{2}]
, but
[a^{2},g,a^{2}]=[a^{2},g,a]^{2}
. Now, we have
\gamma_{3}(N)\leq\gamma_{2}(N)^{2}\leq[N,G]^{2}
. Thus,
[N,G,G]\leq[N^{2},G]\leq[N,G]^{2}
, as claimed.

For
X=N^{2}
, we may assume
[N^{2},G,G]=1
, and in particular
[N,G,G,G]=1
; therefore
[N^{2},G]\leq[N,G]^{2}\leq(N^{2})^{2}
.

(ii) Similarly, by Theorem
1.6
, we need only to show that
[NM,G]\leq(NM)^{2}
, and
[N,M,G]\leq[N,M]^{2}
. The first case is trivial. For the second, we may assume

. Once this is proved, it follows by symmetry that

; so both of

and

lie in

, and by the three-subgroups lemma, the same is true for
[N,M,G]
. To prove that claim, let

,

, and

. We have

, and
[a,b,g^{2}]=[a,b,g]^{2}
; so

[N^{2},M]\leq[N,M]^{2}[N,M,N]\leq[N,M]^{2}[N,M,G^{2}]\leq[N,M]^{2}.

∎

Proof of Corollary

.

We proceed by induction on

. For

, the result is immediate by Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
, and Lemma

(3). Next, assume that

is almost powerfully embedded in

, for some

. It follows that

, and we have

by lemma

(4); so
P_{n+1}(G)\leq\gamma_{n+1}(G)
. Conversely,

\gamma_{n+1}(G)=[P_{n}(G),G]\leq P_{n}(G)^{2}=P_{n+1}(G),

so,

. Moreover,
\gamma_{n+1}(G)
is almost powerfully embedded in

by Lemma

(i). This shows that
P_{n}(G)=\gamma_{n}(G)
is almost powerfully embedded in

, for all

.

Next, assume that

holds for some

. Clearly, we have

. Conversely, since

Thus,

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
2
-group, and
H,K\leq G
such that
[K,H]\leq K^{2}
. Then

d(HK)\geq d(H)+d(K)-d(H\cap K);

and the equality holds if and only if
(H\cap K)^{2}=H^{2}\cap K^{2}
.

Indeed, the assumption
[K,H]\leq K^{2}
implies that
HK\leq G
and
(HK)^{2}=H^{2}K^{2}
. Hence,

|HK:(HK)^{2}|=\frac{|H|\cdot|K|}{|H\cap K|}\frac{|H^{2}\cap K^{2}|}{|H^{2}|\cdot|K^{2}|},

therefore,

2^{d(HK)}=2^{d(H)+d(K)}\frac{|H^{2}\cap K^{2}|}{|H\cap K|}.

As
(H\cap K)^{2}\leq H^{2}\cap K^{2}
, we have
\frac{|H^{2}\cap K^{2}|}{|H\cap K|}\geq 2^{-d(H\cap K)}
; the proposition is immediate now.

4.
Further results

We start by two general facts that we need in the sequel.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
G
is a
p
-group in which every maximal subgroup
M
satisfies
d(M)=d(G)-1
, then all the subgroups of index
p^{2}
in
G
are normal.

Indeed, let
H\leq G
be of index
p^{2}
, and
M
be a maximal subgroup containing
H
. Then
H
is maximal in
M
, so
\Phi(M)\leq H
. On the other hand, we have

|G:\Phi(G)|=p|M:\Phi(M)|
, so
\Phi(M)=\Phi(G)
. Thus

\gamma_{2}(G)\leq H
; the result follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
G
is a non-abelian
d
-maximal
p
-group, with
p>2
, then

(i)

\Omega_{1}(G)
is not abelian.

(ii)

d(\gamma_{2}(G))\leq d(G)-2
.

If
\Omega_{1}(G)
is abelian, then clearly
|\Omega_{1}(G)|=p^{d(\Omega_{1}(G))}
. Also,
G
has class
2
and
\gamma_{2}(G)
is elementary abelian (by Theorem
1.3
, or Lemma
2.1
(4)), so the map
x\mapsto x^{p}
is an endomorphism of
G
. It follows that

p^{d(G)}=\left|G:\Phi(G)\right|\leq\left|G:G^{p}\right|=\left|\Omega_{1}(G)\right|=p^{d(\Omega_{1}(G))}.

Hence,
d(G)\leq d(\Omega_{1}(G))
, a contradiction; this proves (i). Next, we have
\gamma_{2}(G)<\Omega_{1}(G)
by (i), so one can find

x\in G\setminus\gamma_{2}(G)
of order
p
. It follows that

H=\gamma_{2}(G)\left\langle x\right\rangle
is elementary abelian of rank
d(\gamma_{2}(G))+1
. If (ii) is false, then we would have
G=H
, and so
G=\left\langle x\right\rangle
, a contradiction.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
G
is a
d
-maximal non-abelian
p
-group, with
d(G)=3
and

, then

is isomorphic to the central product
C_{p^{2}}*S
, where

is the extraspecial
p
-group of order
p^{3}
and exponent
p
; in other words

G=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{p^{2}}=b^{p}=c^{p}=1,[a,b]=[a,c]=1,[b,c]=a^{p}\rangle.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
and Lemma
4.2
(ii), we have
|\gamma_{2}(G)|=p
, so
|G|=p^{4}
. Moreover,
\Omega_{1}(G)
has order
p^{3}
as
|G:\Omega_{1}(G)|=|G^{p}|=p
, and
\Omega_{1}(G)
is not abelian by Lemma
4.2
(i). Let
A\lhd G
be elementary abelian of rank
2
; as
A\leq\Omega_{1}(G)
, we have
A\nleq Z(G)
, hence
M=C_{G}(A)<G
. Also,
G/M
embeds naturally in
\mathrm{Aut}(A)\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, so
|G:M|=p
, that is
|M|=p^{3}
; and
M
is abelian because
A\leq Z(M)
. Choose
v\in A
and

such that
\Omega_{1}(G)=\langle v,w\rangle
. As
[v,w]\neq 1
, one can find
u\in M
such that
[v,w]=u^{p}
. Now,
u
has order
p^{2}
, so
\langle u\rangle\lhd G
by Lemma
4.1
; hence
[u,w]=u^{ip}
for some

, set

,

and

and

, and

, and

. (It should be clear that the latter group is

-maximal.)

∎

Lemma 4.4

Let

IsDNMax:= function (G)
local nor, max;
nor := NormalSubgroups(G);
nor := Filtered(nor, x -> Size(x)<Size(G) and IsSubgroup(x, DerivedSubgroup(G)));

2.1
Central Path Method

Our algorithm relies on two new ingredients: stochastic central path and projection maintenance. The central path method considers the linear programs

\displaystyle\min_{Ax=b,x\geq 0}c^{\top}x\quad\text{(primal)}\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{A^{\top}y\leq c}b^{\top}y\quad\text{(dual)}

with
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
. Any solution of the linear program satisfies
the following optimality conditions:

\displaystyle x_{i}s_{i}

\displaystyle=0\text{ for all }i,

(1)

\displaystyle Ax

\displaystyle=b,

\displaystyle A^{\top}y+s

\displaystyle=c,

\displaystyle x_{i},s_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 0\text{ for all }i.

We call
(x,s,y)
feasible if it satisfies the last three equations above.
For any feasible
(x,s,y)
, the duality gap of
(x,s,y)
is
\sum_{i}x_{i}s_{i}
.
The central path method finds a solution of the linear
program by following the central path which uniformly decrease the duality gap. The central path
(x_{t},s_{t},y_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+n+d}
is a path parameterized by
t
and defined by

\displaystyle x_{t,i}s_{t,i}

\displaystyle=t\text{ for all }i,

(2)

\displaystyle Ax_{t}

\displaystyle=b,

\displaystyle A^{\top}y_{t}+s_{t}

\displaystyle=c,

\displaystyle x_{t,i},s_{t,i}

\displaystyle\geq 0\text{ for all }i.

where
x_{t,i}
is the
i
-th coordinate of
x_{t}
and
s_{t,i}
is the
i
-th coordinate of
s_{t}
. It is known
[
YTM94
]
how to transform linear programs by adding
O(n)
many variables
and constraints so that:

•

The optimal solution remains the same.

•

The central path at
t=1
is near
(1_{n},1_{n},0_{d})
where
1_{n}
and
0_{d}

are all
1
and all
0
vectors with lengths
n
and
d
.

•

It is easy to convert an approximate solution of the transformed program to the original one.

For completeness, a theoretical version of such result is included in Lemma
A.6
.
This result shows that it suffices to move gradually
(x_{1},s_{1},y_{1})
to
(x_{t},s_{t},y_{t})
for small enough

.

2.1.1
Short Step Central Path Method

The short step central path method maintains
x_{i}s_{i}=\mu_{i}
for some vector
\mu
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(\mu_{i}-t)^{2}=O(t^{2})\quad\text{ for some scalar }t>0.

(3)

Since the duality gap is
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}
, it suffices to find
x
and
s
satisfying the above equation with small enough
t
. There are many variants of central path methods. We will focus on the version that decreases
t
and takes a step of
\mu
at the same time. The purpose of moving
\mu
is to maintain the invariant (
3
) and the purpose of decreasing
t
is decrease the duality gap, which is roughly
nt
.
One natural way to maintain the invariant (
3
) is to do a gradient descent step on the energy
\sum_{i}(\mu_{i}-t)^{2}
defined in (
3
), namely, moving
\mu
to
\mu-h(\mu-t)
with step size
h
6
6

6

The classical view of central path method is to take a Newton step on the system (
2
), which turns out to be same as taking a gradient step on the energy defined in (
3
). However, our main algorithm will choose a different energy and this gradient descent view is crucial for designing our algorithm.
.

More generally, say we want to move from
\mu
to

\mu+\delta_{\mu}
, we approximate
the term
(x+\delta_{x})_{i}(s+\delta_{s})_{i}
by
x_{i}s_{i}+x_{i}\delta_{s,i}+s_{i}\delta_{x,i}

and obtain the following system:

\displaystyle X\delta_{s}+S\delta_{x}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu},

\displaystyle A\delta_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(4)

\displaystyle A^{\top}\delta_{y}+\delta_{s}

\displaystyle=0,

where
X=\textrm{diag}(x)
and
S=\textrm{diag}(s)
. This equation is the linear approximation of the
original goal (moving from
\mu
to
\mu+\delta_{\mu}
), and
that the step is explicitly given by the formula

\displaystyle\delta_{x}=\frac{X}{\sqrt{XS}}(I-P)\frac{1}{\sqrt{XS}}\delta_{\mu}\text{ and }\delta_{s}=\frac{S}{\sqrt{XS}}P\frac{1}{\sqrt{XS}}\delta_{\mu},

(5)

where
P=\sqrt{\frac{X}{S}}A^{\top}\left(A\frac{X}{S}A^{\top}\right)^{-1}A\sqrt{\frac{X}{S}}

is an orthogonal projection and the formulas
\frac{X}{\sqrt{XS}},\frac{X}{S},\cdots
are the diagonal matrices of the corresponding vectors.

In turns out that one can decrease
t
by
1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
a multiplicative factor every iteration while maintaining the invariant (
3
). This requires
\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{n})
iterations to converge. Combining this with the inverse maintenance technique
[
Vai87
]
, this gives a total runtime of
n^{2.5}
.
More precisely, the algorithm maintains the invariant
\sum_{i}(\mu_{i}-t)^{2}=O(t^{2})
by making steps bring
\mu_{i}
closer to
t
while taking steps to decrease
\mu_{i}
uniformly. The progress of the whole algorithm is measured by
t
because the duality gap of
(x_{t},s_{t},y_{t})
is bounded by
nt
.

2.1.2
Stochastic Central Path Method

This part discusses how to modify the short step central path to decrease the cost per iteration to roughly
n^{\omega-\frac{1}{2}}

Since our goal is to implement a central path method in sub-quadratic time per iteration, we do not even have the budget to compute

due to the formula of
\delta_{x}

\delta_{s}
(

However, even if the projection matrix

To avoid moving along a dense

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu,i}=\begin{cases}\delta_{\mu,i}/p_{i},&\text{with probability }p_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{{\scriptscriptstyle def}}}}{{=}}k\cdot\left(\frac{\delta_{\mu,i}^{2}}{\sum_{l}\delta_{\mu,l}^{2}}+\frac{1}{n}\right);\\
0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

(6)

The sparse direction is defined so that we are moving
in the same direction in expectation (
\E[\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu,i}]=\delta_{\mu,i}
)
and that the direction has as small variance as possible (
\E[\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu,i}^{2}]\leq\frac{\sum_{i}\delta_{\mu,i}^{2}}{k}
).
If the projection matrix is given explicitly, we can apply the projection matrix on

.
This paper picks

and the sum of the cost of

During the whole algorithm, we maintain a projection matrix

and
\overline{s}
such that

\overline{s}_{i}
are multiplicative approximations of

and

respectively for all

. Since we maintain the projection at a nearby point

, our stochastic step

,

and

are defined by

\displaystyle\overline{X}\widetilde{\delta}_{s}+\overline{S}\widetilde{\delta}_{x}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu},

\displaystyle A\widetilde{\delta}_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(7)

\displaystyle A^{\top}\widetilde{\delta}_{y}+\widetilde{\delta}_{s}

\displaystyle=0,

which is different from (
4

Note that this system uses
\overline{X}
and
\overline{S}

Similar to (

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{x}=\frac{\overline{X}}{\sqrt{\overline{X}\overline{S}}}(I-\overline{P})\frac{1}{\sqrt{\overline{X}\overline{S}}}\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu}\text{ and }\widetilde{\delta}_{s}=\frac{\overline{S}}{\sqrt{\overline{X}\overline{S}}}\overline{P}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\overline{X}\overline{S}}}\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu}.

The projection matrix we maintain is of the form

for the short step central path step here.
In this case, we have

If the changes

for all

Since it takes

For the other extreme case when

iterations. In

The short step central path method is defined using the approximation
(x+\delta_{x})_{i}(s+\delta_{s})_{i}\sim x_{i}s_{i}+x_{i}\delta_{s,i}+s_{i}\delta_{x,i}
.
This approximate is accurate if

and

\displaystyle X^{-1}\delta_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{XS}}(I-P)\frac{1}{\sqrt{XS}}\delta_{\mu}\sim\frac{1}{t}(I-P)\delta_{\mu},

(10)

where we used
x_{i}s_{i}\sim t
for all

If we know that

\displaystyle\|X^{-1}\delta_{x}\|_{\infty}\leq\|X^{-1}\delta_{x}\|_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{t}\|(I-P)\delta_{\mu}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{t}\|\delta_{\mu}\|_{2}\leq 1/2,

where we used that

is an orthogonal projection matrix. This

Electric Fields and Chiral Magnetic Effect in Cu + Au Collisions
Wei-Tian Deng, Xu-Guang Huang

Abstract
The non-central Cu + Au collisions can create strong out-of-plane magnetic fields and in-plane electric fields. By using the HIJING model, we study the general properties of the electromagnetic fields in Cu + Au collisions at 200 200 GeV and their impacts on the charge-dependent two-particle correlator γ q 1 ​ q 2 = ⟨ cos ⁡ ( ϕ 1 + ϕ 2 − 2 ​ ψ RP ) ⟩ \gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}=\langle\cos(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}-2\psi_{\rm RP})\rangle (see main text for definition) which was used for the detection of the chiral magnetic effect (CME). Compared with Au + Au collisions, we find that the in-plane electric fields in Cu + Au collisions can strongly suppress the two-particle correlator or even reverse its sign if the lifetime of the electric fields is long. Combining with the expectation that if γ q 1 ​ q 2 \gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}} is induced by elliptic-flow driven effects we would not see such strong suppression or reversion, our results suggest to use Cu + Au collisions to test CME and understand the mechanisms that underlie γ q 1 ​ q 2 \gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}} .

I
Introduction

Over the past few years, there has been increasing interest in the quantum-anomaly-related transport phenomena and their experimental signals in heavy-ion collisions. Such anomalous transports include chiral magnetic effect (CME)
Kharzeev:2007jp
;
Fukushima:2008xe
;
Vilenkin:1980fu
, chiral vortical effect (CVE)
Vilenkin:1979ui
;
Kharzeev:2007tn
;
Son:2009tf
, chiral separation effect (CSE)
Son:2004tq
;
Metlitski:2005pr
, chiral electric separation effect (CESE)
Huang:2013iia
;
Jiang:2014ura
;
Pu:2014cwa
;
Pu:2014fva
, etc. All these effects involve a net chiral imbalance in the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) and their occurrence, if confirmed, provides us hitherto unique evidence for the local P and CP violation in QGP. For review on the anomalous transports in heavy-ion collisions, see for example Refs
Bzdak:2012ia
;
Kharzeev:2013ffa
;
Liao:2014ava
.

The CME in QGP can be neatly expressed as

\displaystyle{\bf J}=\sigma_{5}{\bf B}\;\;{\rm with}\;\;\sigma_{5}=N_{c}\sum_{f}\frac{q_{f}^{2}\mu_{5}}{2\pi^{2}},

(1)

is a magnetic field. Equation (

). In real heavy-ion collisions, the magnetic fields are generated mostly in the direction perpendicular to the reaction plane (For discussions about the event-by-event fluctuation of the magnetic field orientation, see Refs.
Bloczynski:2012en
), therefore the CME is expected to induce a charge separation with respect to the reaction plane which may be detected, as proposed by Voloshin
Voloshin:2004vk
, via the two-particle correlator

\displaystyle\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}=\langle\cos(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}-2\psi_{\rm RP})\rangle,

(2)

2

v_{2}

\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}

Wang:2012qs

The purpose of this paper is to propose to use another colliding system, that is, the Cu + Au collisions at RHIC to test CME-induced and the
v_{2}
-driven contributions to
\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}
. The idea is the following. If
\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}
is dominated by
v_{2}
-driven effects, we expect that
\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}
(more precisely,
\Delta\gamma\equiv\gamma_{\rm OS}-\gamma_{\rm SS}
, because the directed flow in Cu + Au collisions may contribute to both
\gamma_{\rm OS}
and
\gamma_{\rm SS}
. This contribution does not exist in Au + Au collisions.) would not change too much from Au + Au collisions to Cu + Au collisions. (A plausible guess would be that
\Delta\gamma
as a function of centrality in Cu + Au collisions lie between that in Cu + Cu and Au + Au collisions). However, on the other hand, if
\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}

is driven by CME, then the correlator in Cu + Au collisions will be very different from that in Au + Au collisions. This is because that, in noncentral Cu + Au collisions, due to the asymmetric collision geometry, there can generate electric fields pointing from Au to Cu nuclei which can induce an in-plane charge separation in addition to the out-of-plane charge separation due to CME. Thus the resulting charge dipolar distribution will deviate from the out-of-plane direction, and as a consequence, both the SS and OS correlators will descend, see Fig.
1
for an illustration. If the in-plane electric fields are so strong (or somehow equivalently, if their lifetime is long. We will not distinguish these two situations.) that the resulting charge dipole is mainly along the in-plane direction, one should observe a positive
\gamma_{\rm SS}
and a negative
\gamma_{\rm OS}
as opposite to that in Au + Au collisions, see the most right panel in Fig.
1
. Thus if the measurement in Cu + Au collisions shows a strong reduction or even sign-reversion for
\gamma_{\rm OS}
and
\gamma_{\rm SS}
comparing to that in Au + Au collisions, it will be a hint for the EM-field (i.e., CME plus electric field) interpretation of
\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}
. Otherwise, it will indicate a
v_{2}
-driven interpretation of
\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}
.

\gamma_{q_{1}q_{2}}

IV
Summary and discussions

In summary, by using HIJING model, we simulated the generation of the EM fields in Cu + Au collisions at
200
GeV and studied the general properties of the EM fields, see Sec.
II
. We found that a strong electric field along the impact parameter direction exists in Cu + Au collisions. This in-plane electric field gives a negative contribution to the two-particle correlator
\Delta\gamma
(defined in Eq. (
III.1
)). By using a one-component model (i.e., by assuming
\Delta\gamma
is driven by EM-field induced effects) we studied how the in-plane electric field influences
\Delta\gamma
in Cu + Au collisions and found that it may strongly suppress or even change the sign of
\Delta\gamma
compared with that in Au + Au collisions.

Some discussions are in order:

(1) The correlator
\Delta\gamma
may receive other non-EM-field induced contributions (contained in the irreducible component,
C(q_{1},\phi_{1};q_{2},\phi_{2})
, of the two-particle distribution), potentially the LCC, which are not taken into account in the analysis in Sec.
III
. However, inclusion of the non-EM-field induced contributions will not affect the role played by the in-plane electric field in Cu + Au collisions. The in-plane electric field always tends to drive an in-plane component of the charge dipole and thus tends to suppress
\Delta\gamma
.

(2) However, there is the possibility that
\Delta\gamma
is dominated by non-EM-field induced effects and the CME and electric field play minor roles. In this case, one does not expect a strong suppression or reversion of
\Delta\gamma
in Cu + Au collisions compared with that in Au + Au collisions. This provides us a feasible way to clarify how important the EM fields can be: If the experimental data in Cu + Au collisions really show a strong suppression or reversion of
\Delta\gamma
, it will strongly indicate that the EM fields play dominant roles in
\Delta\gamma
(it will also indicate a large electric conductivity of the quark-gluon matter); otherwise it will be a hint for the
v_{2}
-driven interpretation of
\Delta\gamma
.

(3) The modeling of
\Delta\gamma
in Sec.
III
is illustrating, it can be improved by adopting a more realistic model, e.g., the two-component model
Bzdak:2012ia
;
Bloczynski:2013mca
for
\Delta\gamma
(i.e., to include the contributions driven by
v_{2}
), by taking into account more realistic spacetime dependence of the EM fields, and by including the final state interactions
Ma:2011uma
;
Shou:2014zsa
. These will be our future tasks and the results will be presented in future publications.

(4) From Eq. (
III.1
) we observe that the magnetic and the electric contributions to
\Delta\gamma
are additive, thus it is interesting to construct suitable observables to separately characterize the in-plane and out-of-plane charge separations caused by the electric fields and magnetic fields, respectively. One possible observable is the charge dipole or multiple vector proposed in Ref.
Liao:2010nv
;
Bloczynski:2012en
, more detailed analysis will be future task.

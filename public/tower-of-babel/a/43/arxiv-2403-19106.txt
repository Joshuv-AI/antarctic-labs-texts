Branching problem of tensoring two Verma modules and its application to differential symmetry breaking operators
Reiji Murakami

Abstract
Kobayashi-Pevzner discovered in [Selecta Math., 2016] that the failure of the multiplicity-one property in the fusion rule of Verma modules of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} occurs exactly when the Rankin-Cohen bracket vanishes, and classified all the corresponding parameters. In this paper we provide yet another characterization for these parameters, and give a precise description of indecomposable components of the tensor product. Furthermore, we discuss when the tensor products of two Verma modules are isomorphic to each other for semisimple Lie algebras 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} .



Keywords—
branching laws, symmetry breaking operator, tensor product, Verma module, BGG category, Lie algebra

1
Introduction.

The object of this article is the tensor product of two Verma modules. Recently, a new phenomenon has been discovered in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-case, including the failure of the multiplicity-freeness in the fusion rule for
singular
integral parameters, see
[
6
, Sect. 9]
for details. This article provides a precise description of indecomposable components of the tensor product in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-case (Theorem
1.3
), and proves that the failure of the self-duality of indecomposable components corresponds exactly to the multiplicity-two phenomenon (Theorem
1.2
).

As an introduction, we begin with a general result of the tensor product of two Verma modules. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{h}
a Cartan subalgebra, and
\mathfrak{b}
a Borel subalgebra containing
\mathfrak{h}
. Let
M(\mu):=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}\mathbb{C}_{\mu}
be the Verma module for
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, see Section
2.1
for further notation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime},\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
satisfy
\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}=\nu^{\prime}+\nu^{\prime\prime}
. If each of the two sets
\{\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}\}
and
\{\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime}\}

contains at least one anti-dominant element, then one has an isomorphism as
\mathfrak{g}
-modules:

\displaystyle M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})\cong M(\nu^{\prime})\otimes M(\nu^{\prime\prime}).

The anti-dominant assumption cannot be dropped, as we shall see in Example
1.7
.
In general, it is a hard problem to give a precise description of the decomposition of
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
, in particular, because the tensor product is not necessarily completely reducible even when both
M(\mu^{\prime})
and
M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
are irreducible.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. We identify
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with
\mathbb{C}
such that the unique positive root is given by
2
as usual.
Then, the Verma module
M(\mu)
has a
(\mu+1)
-dimensional simple quotient
L(\mu)
if
\mu\in\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
. Following the notation as in
[
6
, p. 894]
, we define the
multiplicity space
of
M(-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime})
in
M(-\lambda^{\prime})\otimes M(-\lambda^{\prime\prime})
by:

\displaystyle H(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}):=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{g}}(M(-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}),M(-\lambda^{\prime})\otimes M(-\lambda^{\prime\prime})).

Kobayashi-Pevzner
[
6
, Thm. 9.1]
discovered that the Rankin-Cohen brackets
\mathcal{RC}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}
(see (
1
) below) vanishes if and only if
H(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime})
is of dimension
2
, and found all the triples
\{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}\}
of parameters when this happens. In Theorem
1.2
, we give yet another characterization of this condition
by means of the
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure of the
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-primary component
(M(-\lambda^{\prime})\otimes M(-\lambda^{\prime\prime}))^{\chi_{-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}+1}}
corresponding to the generalized infinitesimal character
-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}+1
, see Section
2.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}\in 2\mathbb{N}
, then the following four conditions on the triple
(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime})
are equivalent:

(i)
\dim H(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime})=2
.

(ii)
\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{Z},\ 2\geq\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime},\ and\ \lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}\geq|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|+2
.

(iii)
\mathcal{RC}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}=0
.

(iv)
2\leq\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}
and
(M(-\lambda^{\prime})\otimes M(-\lambda^{\prime\prime}))^{\chi_{-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}+1}}=M(-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime})\oplus M(\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}-2)
.

The equivalences
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
were established earlier in
[
6
, Sect. 9]
. The operator
\mathcal{RC}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}
in
(iii)
is a bidifferential operator, referred to as the
Rankin-Cohen bracket
of degree
\ell:=\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{N}
, defined by

\mathcal{RC}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{j}\frac{(\lambda^{\prime}+\ell-1)_{j}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\ell-1)_{\ell-j}}{j!(\ell-j)!}\frac{\partial^{\ell}}{\partial{z_{1}}^{\ell-j}\partial{z_{2}}^{j}}\Bigg\rvert_{z_{1}=z_{2}},

(1)

where
(x)_{n}
stands for the descending factorial
x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1)
. The operator was introduced originally in number theory to produce holomorphic modular forms of higher weight
\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+2\ell\ (\ell\in\mathbb{N})
out of those of lower weights
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
.
From the viewpoint of representation theory,
\mathcal{RC}_{\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}}^{\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}}
is a
symmetry breaking operator
from a
\tilde{G}\times\tilde{G}
-equivalent holomorphic line bundle to a
\tilde{G}
-equivalent one over the Poincaré disk where
\tilde{G}
denotes the universal covering group of
SU(1,1)
. Such an operator shows how
\tilde{G}\times\tilde{G}
-symmetry is “broken” in keeping the diagonal symmetry of
\tilde{G}
. See
[
6
, Sect. 1.1 and Sect. 9]
.

The novelty of Theorem
1.2
is the equivalence of
(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
, which is derived from Theorem
1.3
about a description of the
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure of the tensor product of two Verma modules.
Let
P(a)
denote the projective covering of
M(a)
, see Definition
2.1
and Example
2.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
, one has an isomorphism as
\mathfrak{g}
-modules:

M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})\cong\bigoplus_{a\in A}P(a)\oplus\bigoplus_{b\in B}M(b).

We set
X:=\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2\mathbb{N}
. The finite (possibly empty) set
A\equiv A(\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime})
and a countable set
B\equiv B(\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime})
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\emptyset&if\ \mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}\notin\mathbb{N},\\
X\ \cap\ [-|\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime}|,-2]&if\ \mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N},\\
X\ \cap\ [-\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime}-2,-2]&otherwise,\end{cases}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=X\setminus(A\cup A^{*}),

where
A^{*}:=\{-a-2\in\mathbb{Z}:\ a\in A\}
.

See
[
5
, Thm. 4.10]
for analogous results on the restriction of parabolic Verma modules with respect to
\mathfrak{so}_{n+1}\supset\mathfrak{so}_{n}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For the tensor product
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
, the following four conditions on
(\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
are equivalent.

(i)
\dim\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{g}}(N,M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime}))\leq 1
for any simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module
N\in\mathcal{O}
;

(ii)
\dim\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{g}}(M(\nu),M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime}))\leq 1
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
;

(iii) the tensor product
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
is self-dual;

(iv) it does not contain any reducible Verma module as a direct summand.

We shall use Remark
1.6
in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.5
.

The finite set
A
is contained in
\{-2,-3,-4,\cdots\}
, see Example
2.2
. It is non-empty if and only if
(\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
satisfies
\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime}\notin\{0,\pm 1\}
.

Remark 1.6
.

For
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, the Verma module
M(\nu)
occurs in
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
as a direct summand if and only if

\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and

|\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime}|\leq\nu\leq\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}
. See
[
6
, Thm. 9.1]
. This condition is an analogue of the Clebsch-Gordan formula.

Example 1.7
.

If
(\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime},\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime})=(0,0,i,-i)
, then the tensor product
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
is not isomorphic to
M(\nu^{\prime})\otimes M(\nu^{\prime\prime})
because Theorem
1.3
shows

\displaystyle M(0)\otimes M(0)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}M(-2k),

\displaystyle M(i)\otimes M(-i)

\displaystyle\cong P(-2)\oplus\bigoplus_{k=2}^{\infty}M(-2k),

but
P(-2)
is not isomorphic to the direct sum
M(0)\oplus M(-2)
.
In this case, the assumption in Theorem
1.1
is not satisfied because
0
is not anti-dominant.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Our proof relies on the earlier work
[
4
]
and
[
2
]
. Kobayashi
[
4
]
established a general framework of the restriction of parabolic Verma modules to reductive subalgebras, and proved the branching rule in the Grothendieck group for the discretely decomposable case, whereas Kåhrström
[
2
]
showed that a tensoring with Verma modules preserves Verma flags.

2.1
Preliminaries of parabolic Verma modules.

We collect some basic notions of parabolic Verma modules, see
[
1
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathfrak{h}
a Cartan subalgebra, and W the Weyl group of the root system
\Delta:=\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. We fix a positive system
\Delta^{+}
, take the corresponding Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{n}
with nilpotent radical
\mathfrak{n}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, and let
\rho
be half the sum of positive roots. A standard parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
is a parabolic subalgebra containing the Borel subalgebra

\mathfrak{b}
. We take a Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{u}
with
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{l}
, and set
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l}):=\Delta^{+}\cap\Delta(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{h})
and
\mathfrak{n}_{-}(\mathfrak{l}):=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
. Let
U(\mathfrak{g})
be the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
Z(\mathfrak{g})
its center.

A
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
is called
locally
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-finite
if for every
x\in M
the space
Z(\mathfrak{g})x
is finite dimensional. Let
\mathcal{Z}_{fin}
be the category of locally
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-finite
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Category
\mathcal{O}
is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{Z}_{fin}
such that the objects are finitely generated and
\mathfrak{h}
-semisimple, and locally
\mathfrak{n}
-finite. Category
\mathcal{O}^{\mathfrak{p}}
is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}
such that the objects are locally
\mathfrak{n}_{-}(\mathfrak{l})
-finite.

We write the set of dominant integral for
\Lambda^{+}(\mathfrak{l}):=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}:\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in\mathbb{N}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all}\ \alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})\}
where
\alpha^{\vee}
denotes the coroot of
\alpha
. For
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}(\mathfrak{l})
, let
F_{\lambda}
denote the finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-module with highest weight
\lambda
. We also write
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
for
F_{\lambda}
if it is one-dimensional. We regard
F_{\lambda}
(and
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
) as a
\mathfrak{p}
-module by letting
\mathfrak{u}
act trivially. The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{p})}F_{\lambda}
is referred to as a parabolic Verma module. For
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{b}
, it reduces to the usual Verma module
M(\lambda)\equiv M^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{b}}(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. An element
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is said to be
anti-dominant
if
\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\notin\mathbb{N}_{+}=\{1,2,\cdots\}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
, equivalently, it
M(\lambda)
is simple.

The Harish-Chandra isomorphism parametrizes the set
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}\text{-alg}}(Z(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbb{C})
of
\mathbb{C}
-algebraic homomorphisms as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W\cong\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}\text{-alg}}(Z(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbb{C}),\ \nu\mapsto\chi_{\nu}.

In our parametrization, the infinitesimal character of the trivial module is
\rho\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W
and that of the Verma module
M(\lambda)
is
\lambda+\rho
. Any locally
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-finite
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
admits a primary decomposition

\displaystyle M=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathfrak{h}*/W}M^{\chi_{\nu}},

where
M^{\chi_{\nu}}
is the
\nu
-primary component of
\nu
, namely,
M^{\chi_{\nu}}
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of elements annihilated by a power of
\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(\chi_{\nu}:Z(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathbb{C})
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
1
, Ch. 3.9]
)
.

For
M\in\mathcal{O}
, a surjective
\mathfrak{g}
-map
\phi:P\to M
is called a
projective covering
of
M
if
P\in\mathcal{O}
is a projective module without any proper submodule such that its image of
\phi
equals
M
.

We omit
\phi
when it is obvious. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, let
P(\lambda)
denote the projective cover of the Verma module
M(\lambda)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. For
\lambda\notin\{-2,-3,\cdots\}
, one has
P(\lambda)=M(\lambda)
. If
\lambda\in\{-2,-3,\cdots\}
, there is a non-split short exact sequence of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules:

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow M(-\lambda-2)\rightarrow P(\lambda)\rightarrow M(\lambda)\rightarrow 0.

2.2
Branching problem for parabolic Verma modules.

The tensor product
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
may be viewed as the restriction of the Verma module
M(\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime})=U(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{b})}\mathbb{C}_{(\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime})}

\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
. We then utilize the general framework in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
. In particular, the concept of discrete decomposability of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, which was introduced in
[
3
]
as an algebraic counterpart of the unitary representation without continuous spectrum, is useful in our study of the tensor product.

Definition 2.3

(
[
4
, Def. 3.1]
)
.

A
\mathfrak{g}
-module
X
is called
discretely decomposable
if there is an ascending sequence

\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of submodules such that
\bigcup X_{i}=X
and that each
X_{i}
has finite length.

Moreover for a full subcategory
\mathcal{A}
of the category of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, we say

is
discretely decomposable
in
\mathcal{A}
if every
X_{i}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}
.

with respect to a reductive subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. In general, the restriction may not contain any simple
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-module. A geometric criterion on the pair
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathfrak{p})
was proved in
[
4
]
about when the restriction

is descretely deconposable. In what follows, we recall an

which is slightly stronger than this

A semisimple element

is said to be hyperbolic if
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits(H)
is diagonalizable with all eigenvalues being real.
We write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{u}_{-}+\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{u}
for the decomposition into the sum of eigenspaces of
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits(H)

0

Then
\mathfrak{p}(H):=\mathfrak{l}+\mathfrak{u}

\mathfrak{g}

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.14
.

If
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
N\in\mathcal{O}
, then one has the following isomorphism in the Grothendieck group:

\displaystyle[M(\mu)\otimes N]=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\Pi(N)}\dim N_{\nu}\ [M(\mu+\nu)].

Proof.

Since
N
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, one has the following isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

\displaystyle M(\mu)\otimes N=(U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}\mathbb{C}_{\mathfrak{\mu}})\otimes N\cong U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}(\mathbb{C}_{\mu}\otimes N).

We take a filtration
0=B_{0}\subset B_{1}\subset B_{2}\subset\cdots
of
\mathbb{C}_{\mu}\otimes N
as a
\mathfrak{b}
-module such that
B_{i+1}/B_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}_{\mu_{i}}
for some
\mu_{i}\in\mu+\Pi(N)
. In turn,
\{U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}B_{i}\}
is a Verma flag of
M(\mu)\otimes N
.
Then the proposition follows because
(U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}B_{i+1})/(U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b})}B_{i})\cong M(\mu_{i})
.
∎

Remark 2.15

If

Suppose that

3
Indecomposable components of
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
in the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

Throughout this section, we suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

1.3

1.4

Since each weight space of Verma modules is one-dimensional for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, the following equation holds in the Grothendieck group from Proposition
2.14
.

\displaystyle[M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})]=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}[M(\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k)].

(3)

Let
M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}
denote the
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-primary component of the tensor product
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
for the generalized eigenvalue
\nu+1\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}/W\cong\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}\text{-alg}}(Z(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})\cong\bigoplus_{\nu+1\in W\cdot(\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}+1-2\mathbb{N})}M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}.

We know that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}
is of finite length, and want to determine the structure of each
M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}
for the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\nu\in X
,
M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}
has a Verma flag. Moreover, in the Grothendieck group, one has

\displaystyle[M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}]=\begin{cases}[M(\nu)+M(-\nu-2)]&\nu\in A^{\prime}\cup A^{\prime*},\\
[M(\nu)]&\nu\notin A^{\prime}\cup A^{\prime*}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})
has a Verma flag, so does every component
M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}
, whence the first assertion. The second assertion is derived from
(
3
)
because

\displaystyle X\cap X^{*}\setminus\{-1\}=A^{\prime}\cup A^{\prime*}

is the set of
\nu\in X
such that
\nu\neq\nu^{*}
and that
[M(\nu^{*})]
occurs in
(
3
)
.
∎

We also use the following lemma
[
6

:

Lemma 3.2

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{g}}(M(\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k),M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime}))\neq\{0\}.

For the sake of completeness, we give a direct proof. The dimension of weight spaces is given as

\displaystyle\dim(M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime}))_{\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k}=k+1.

Since
\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\nolimits(\mathfrak{n})
maps
(M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime}))_{\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k}
to
(M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime}))_{\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k+\nu}
, it has a non-trivial kernel, namely, there exists a non-zero singular vector in
(M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime}))_{\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k}
.

By Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
, one has an isomorphism
M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}\cong M(\nu)
for
\nu\in X\setminus(A^{\prime}\cup A^{\prime*})

\chi_{\nu+1}=\chi_{\nu^{*}+1}
where
\nu^{*}=-\nu-2
. What remains is to determine the structure of
M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}

\nu\in A^{\prime}
. We compute

3.1
Generic case.

We begin with the case
\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}\notin\mathbb{N}
. Every Verma module
M(\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k)
is irreducible, and the set of (generalized) eigenvalues
\{\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k+1:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
are distinct from each other. Hence the equation of the Grothendieck group (
3

3.2
Singular case 1.

We assume
\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
but at least one of
\mu^{\prime}
or
\mu^{\prime\prime}
does not belong to
\mathbb{N}

A^{\prime}

Suppose
\nu\in A^{\prime}

[M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}]=[M(\nu)]+[M(-\nu-2)]=[P(\nu)]

M^{\chi_{\nu+1}}

3.3
Singular case 2.

Finally, we assume
\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\mu^{\prime}\geq\mu^{\prime\prime}

One has the following short exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(-\mu^{\prime\prime}-2)\rightarrow M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes M(\mu^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes L(\mu^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow 0.

We have already determined the structure of

\displaystyle M(\mu^{\prime})\otimes L(\mu^{\prime\prime})\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\mu^{\prime\prime}}M(\mu^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime\prime}-2k)

Proof.

The implication (i)

\displaystyle B\cap\mathbb{N}

Motivic six-functor formalism for log schemes
Doosung Park

Abstract
. We establish the motivic six-functor formalism for fs log schemes. In particular, we prove the exact base change property, projection formula, and Poincaré duality. We also define Borel-Moore motivic homology, G-theory, and Chow homology of fs log schemes and the category of Chow motives over fs log schemes.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14F42; Secondary 14A21

1.
Introduction

Log geometry, developed by Deligne, Faltings, Fontaine, Illusie, and Kato, gives boundaries to schemes in an algebraic way.
This provides an effective tool to understand the limit behavior of families of schemes.
For example,
let
f\colon X\to S
be a proper smooth morphism of schemes,
and let
S\to T
be an open immersion of schemes.
Suppose that we want to study the limit behavior of
f
in
T-S
.
For this,
we may consider an extension
g\colon Y\to T
of
f
such that
g
is proper flat,
but imposing the smoothness condition on
g
is often too strong.
What log geometry can help in this situation is that in good cases we can impose log structures on
Y
and
T
such that
g
becomes
log smooth
.
Smooth morphisms are more convenient to deal with than general morphisms,
and log smooth morphisms have a similar privilege.

An early application of log geometry is Tsuji’s proof
[
29
]
of the
C_{st}
conjecture in
p
-adic Hodge theory.
To take advantage of log geometry not just in
p
-adic cohomology theories but in broader cohomology theories,
logarithmic motivic homotopy theory,
extending
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory of schemes initiated by Morel-Voevodsky
[
18
]
,
is desirable.
The reason for this is that the
\infty
-category of motivic spectra is a natural place where cohomology theories can live.
Author’s joint papers with Binda and Østvær
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
aim for this with a specialized interest in non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant cohomology theories.

On the other hand,
we focus on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory of log schemes here.
This paper is the last chapter of the series initiated in
[
23
]
and further continued in
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
, and
[
26
]
.
This series covers the author’s thesis
[
22
]
with a more flexible setting.

1.1.
Cohomology and Borel-Moore homology of log schemes

Before digging into the details of the paper,
let us shortly overview recent developments in the area of motivic or Chow theory of log schemes:

•

In
[
1
, Definition 2.7]
,
Barrott defines the log Chow group
A_{*}^{\dagger}(X)
for fs log scheme
X
,
which can be formulated as

A_{*}^{\dagger}(X):=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{Y\in X_{div}}\mathrm{CH}_{*}(Y).

Here, the colimit runs over the category of dividing covers of
X
.
Molcho, Pandharipande, and Schmitt
[
17
]
employ this definition for the moduli spaces of stable curves.

•

Böhning, Graf von Bothmer, and van Garrel
[
4
]
define the prelog Chow ring for simple normal crossing scheme
X
.
They employ this to study semistable degeneration.

•

Gregory and Langer
[
10
]
define log motivic cohomology of semistable varieties.
They relate this to the log Hyodo-Kato Hodge-Witt sheaves.

•

Ito, Kato, Nakayama, and Usui
[
11
]
define log motives modulo homological or numerical equivalences over an fs log scheme.
Using this,
Kato, Nakayama, and Usui
[
14
]
suggest a simple construction of the category of mixed motives over a field.

•

Shuklin
[
27
]
constructs a motive
M(X)
in Voevodsky’s category of geometric motives
\mathrm{DM}_{gm}(k,\mathbb{Q})
for every fs log scheme
X
over
k
.
As a consequence,
he defines log motivic cohomology of
X
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
.

In §
4.2
,
we extend Borel-Moore homology theories of schemes to fs log schemes based on the Grothendieck six-functor formalism for fs log schemes.
An advantage of this approach is that the Grothendieck six-functor formalism provides a recipe for computing cohomology and Borel-Moore homology theories.

More precisely,
if
B
is a base scheme and
\mathbb{E}
is a commutative algebra object of
\mathcal{SH}(B)
,
then we define
Borel-Moore
\mathbb{E}
-homology
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{BM}}(X/S)\in\mathrm{Sp}
of
X
relative to
S
for any morphism
f\colon X\to S
of fs log schemes.
See Definition
4.2.1
for the details.
On the other hand,
we can associated a cohomology theory
\mathbb{E}(X)\in\mathrm{Sp}
for fs log schemes
X
over
B
according to
[
23
]
.
We have the formal properties of
\mathbb{E}(X)
as follows:

•

(
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance)
If
X
is an fs log scheme,
then the morphism

p^{*}\colon\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1})

is an isomorphism,
where
p\colon X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}\to X
is the projection.

•

(Invariance under verticalization)
Let
f\colon X\to S
be an exact log smooth morphism of fs log schemes.
Then the morphism

j^{*}\colon\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X-\partial_{S}X)

is an isomorphism,
where
j\colon X-\partial_{S}X\to X
is the open immersion removing the relative boundary
\partial_{S}X
.

•

(Invariance under dividing covers) If
f\colon Y\to X
is a dividing cover of fs log schemes,
then the morphism

f^{*}\colon\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(Y)

is an isomorphism.

•

(Strict Nisnevich descent)

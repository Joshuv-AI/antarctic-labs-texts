On Boundary Exact Controllability of One-Dimensional Wave Equations with Weak and Strong Interior Degeneration
Peter I. Kogut, Olha P. Kupenko ( kupenko.olga@gmail.com ), Günter Leugering

Abstract
In this paper we study exact boundary controllability for a linear wave equation with strong and weak interior degeneration of the coefficient in the principle part of the elliptic operator. The objective is to provide a well-posedness analysis of the corresponding system and derive conditions for its controllability through boundary actions. Passing to a relaxed version of the original problem, we discuss existence and uniqueness of solutions, and using the HUM method we derive conditions on the rate of degeneracy for both exact boundary controllability and the lack thereof.



keywords

Degenerate wave equation, boundary control, existence result, weighted Sobolev spaces, exact controllability.

Introduction

In this paper we discuss exact boundary controllability for one-dimensional degenerate wave equations with a weak and strong interior degeneration in the principle part of the elliptic operator. Let
[0,T]
be a given time interval. For simplicity, let
c
and
d
be a given pair of real numbers such that
0\leq c<1<d\leq 2
. We set

\Omega_{1}=[c,1),\quad\Omega_{2}=(1,d],\quad\Omega=(c,d),\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{0}=\Omega\setminus\{1\}.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given weight function with properties

(i)

a(1)=0
,
a(x)>0
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{1\}=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}
, and there exist subintervals
(x^{\ast}_{1},1)\subset\Omega_{1}
and
(1,x^{\ast}_{2})\subset\Omega_{2}
such that
a(\cdot)
is monotonically decreasing on
(x^{\ast}_{1},1)
, monotonically increasing on
(1,x^{\ast}_{2})
, and

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},1)}\frac{(1-x)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\frac{(1-x)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}>0,

(1)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in(1,x^{\ast}_{2}]}\frac{(x-1)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=\lim_{x\searrow 1}\frac{(x-1)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}>0;

(2)

(ii)

a\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{1\})
;

(iii)

\left(\sqrt{a}\right)_{x}\not\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
whereas
\left(\sqrt{a}\right)^{-1}_{x}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

As an example of function
a:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with the above indicated properties (i)–(iii), we may consider the following one:

a(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-x)^{2p_{1}},&\text{ if }\ x\in[c,1]\\
(x-1)^{2p_{2}},&\text{ if }\ x\in(1,d],\end{array}\right.\quad\text{ with $p_{1},p_{2}>0$}.

(3)

It is easy to check that, in this case, properties (i)–(iii) hold with
x_{1}^{\ast}=c
,
x_{2}^{\ast}=d
, and

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},1)}\frac{(1-x)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\frac{(1-x)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=2p_{1},

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in(1,x^{\ast}_{2}]}\frac{(x-1)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=\lim_{x\searrow 1}\frac{(x-1)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=2p_{2},

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{a}\right)_{x}\not\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\ \text{ and }\ \left(\sqrt{a}\right)^{-1}_{x}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\ \text{ if $p_{1}$ and $p_{2}$ are less than $1$}.

We are concerned with the following controlled system

\displaystyle y_{tt}-\left(a(x)y_{x}\right)_{x}=0\quad\text{in }\ (0,T)\times\Omega,

(4)

\displaystyle y(t,c)=f_{c}(t),\quad y(t,d)=f_{d}(t)\quad\text{on }\ (0,T),

(5)

\displaystyle y(0,\cdot)=y_{0},\quad y_{t}(0,\cdot)=y_{1}\quad\text{ in }\ \Omega,

(6)

\displaystyle f_{c},f_{d}\in\mathcal{F}_{ad}=L^{2}(0,T).

(7)

Here,
y_{0}
, and
y_{1}
are given functions, and
\mathcal{F}_{ad}
stands for the class of admissible controls.

The system (
4
)-(
7
) describes the dynamics of a linear elastic string with out-of-the-plane displacement under the actions of boundary sources
f_{c},f_{d}
acting on the system as controls through the Dirichlet boundary conditions at
x=c
and
x=d
. The coefficient
a(x)
can be interpreted as the spatially varying stiffness (modulus of elasticity) of the elastic string. In contrast to the standard case that is widely studied in the literature (see, for instance,
[
25
]
), where the stiffness is assumed to be positive and bounded away from zero, we assume that the string
[c,d]
has a defect at the internal point
x_{0}=1
. In case the defect occurs at the endpoint
x_{0}=c
, the problem has been investigated in
[
1
]
. In the latter case, the spatial operator is related to a classical singular Sturm-Liouville-problem that has been treated already by Weyl in
[
30
]
. Degeneration in that context is related to the notions of limit-point and limit-cycle. In
[
1
]
the authors define

\mu_{a}:=\sup\limits_{0<x\leq\ell}\frac{x|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}

(8)

for the problem on the interval
[0,\ell]
. The problem is called weakly damaged if
0\leq\mu_{a}<1
in which case
\frac{1}{a}\in L^{1}
, and strongly damaged in case
1\leq\mu_{a}<2
. The authors show, among other things, one-sided boundary observability and consequently one-sided boundary exact controllability if
\mu_{a}<2
with an observability/controllability time approaching
+\infty
as
\mu_{a}\rightarrow 2
. Thus, for
\mu_{a}\geq 2
, these properties are lost.

Using the Liouville transform (see
[
11
]
1954), it is possible to transform the system above into a homogeneous wave equation with singular potential on an interval that tends to infinity if
\mu_{a}\geq 2
. See e.g.
[
12
]
, where controllability properties are investigated based on this transformation. Working in the
L^{\infty}
-framework, the author obtains similar results as in
[
1
]
.

The authors of this article are not aware of any publication where in-span degeneration of the wave equation is treated, in particular in the context of controllability or observability. For the parabolic case see
[
5
]
.
The main question that we are going to discuss in this paper, therefore, is how the defect at the internal point
x_{0}=1
affects the transmission conditions at the singular (damage) point and the corresponding solution of the system (
4
)–(
7
) as well as its observability or controllability properties.

Such analysis could be important for many applications. In particular, for
the cloaking problem (building of devices that lead to lack of obeservability)
[
15
]
,
the evolution of damage in materials
[
16
]
, optimization problems for elastic bodies arising in contact mechanics, coupled systems, composite materials
[
17
]
, where ’life-cycle-optimization’ appears as a challenge.

The indicated type of degeneracy raises a number of new questions related to the well-posedness of the hyperbolic equations in suitable functional spaces as well as new estimates for their solutions. Hence, new tools are necessary for the analysis of the corresponding optimal control problems. It should be emphasized here that boundary value problems for degenerate elliptic and parabolic equations have received a lot of attention in the last years (see, for instance,
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
23
,
24
,
26
]
).
As for the control issue for degenerate wave equations, we already mentioned
[
1
,
14
]
(see also
[
20
]
for the sensitivity analysis of optimal control problems for wave equations in domain with defects).

The purpose of this paper is to provide a qualitative analysis of system (
4
)–(
7
), prove an exact controllability result, and investigate how the degree of degeneracy in the principle coefficient
a(x)
affects the system (
4
)–(
7
) and its solution.
In contrast to the recent results
[
1
]
, where the authors study controllability and observability for degenerate equation of the form (
4
) with the degeneracy of (
4
) at the boundary
x=c=0
,
we focus on the case where the ’damaged’ point is internal. So, our core idea is to to pass from the original initial-boundary value problem (
4
)–(
7
) to a relaxed version, namely, to some transmission problem with appropriate compatibility conditions at the ’damaged’ point. We show that these conditions play a crucial role and essentially depend on the ’degree of degeneracy’ (for some generalization we refer to
[
18
]
). In multi-dimensional situation, when the ’damage zone’ does not split the domain
\Omega
onto several disconnected subdomains, there is an expectation that the boundary controllability of the corresponding hyperbolic equation with interior degeneration could be true under some suitable ’degree of degeneracy’ in the diffusion coefficients. Although, in general, the problem of boundary controllability in multi-dimensional case appears as a challenge. This issue will be considered in details in the forthcoming paper.

In Section
2
we introduce a special class of weighted Sobolev spaces that are associated with the original initial-boundary value problem. It allows us not only to to study in detail some properties of their elements in the regions which are in close vicinity to the ’damaged’ point, but also propose an appropriate relaxation for to the initial-boundary value problems (
4
)–(
6
). In Section
3
, we mainly focus on the well-posedness of the proposed relaxation for the original controlled system. It allows us to consider in Section
4
the issues related to the boundary observability of degenerate wave equations. In Section
5
we discuss the questions of exact and null boundary controllability of the original degenerate system and the lack of these properties for strong degeneration.

2
Preliminaries

To specify the original controlled system (
4
)–(
7
) and fix the main ideas, we begin with some preliminaries and assumptions. Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given weight function with properties (i)–(iii). For
u
smooth, define the functional
\|\cdot\|_{a}
as follows

\|u\|_{a}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left[u^{2}+au_{x}^{2}\right]\,dx\right)^{1/2}.

Let
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
be the standard Sobolev space. We denote by
H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
,
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
, and
W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
the spaces which are defined as follows

•

H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
is the closure of the set
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
with respect to the
\|\cdot\|_{a}
-norm;

•

H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
is the closure of the set
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with respect to the
\|\cdot\|_{a}
-norm;

•

W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
is the space of maps
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with distributional derivatives
u_{x}
that satisfy
u_{x}\in L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
, where

L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)=\left\{v:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\ :\ v\ \text{ is measurable and }\ \int_{\Omega}av^{2}\,dx<+\infty\right\}.

First note that since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, we have that
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
. Moreover, due to compactness of the embedding

H^{1}_{a}(\left(c,1-\varepsilon\right)\cup\left(1+\varepsilon,d\right))\hookrightarrow C^{0,1}([c,1-\varepsilon]\cup[1+\varepsilon,d]),\quad\text{ for all $\varepsilon>0$ small enough},

we see that, if
y\in H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
, then
y(\cdot)
is an absolutely continuous function in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{1\}
. So, the conditions
y(c)=0
and
y(d)=0
are consistent for all
y\in H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. Therefore,
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
can be equivalently defined as the closed subspace of
H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
such that

H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega):=\left\{y\in H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\ :\ y(c)=y(d)=0\right\}.

It is worth to notice that, unlike classical Sobolev space, the subspace of smooth functions are not necessarily dense in
W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
. So, for ’typical’ weight functions
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with properties (i)–(iii) it is unknown whether the identity
H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)=W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
is valid (for the corresponding examples we refer to
[
8
,
27
]
). Therefore, it would be plausible to suppose that
H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
, in general.

We also have
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
, where

W^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)=\left\{y\in W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\ :\ y(c)=y(d)=0\right\}
.

Setting

\displaystyle\mu_{1,a}:=\sup_{x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},1)}\frac{(1-x)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)},\quad\mu_{2,a}:=\sup_{x\in(1,x^{\ast}_{2}]}\frac{(x-1)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)},

(9)

we deduce from properties (i)–(ii) that there exist constants
\kappa_{1,a}\geq 1
and
\kappa_{2,a}\geq 1
such that

\sup_{x\in\Omega_{1}}\frac{(1-x)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=\kappa_{1,a}\mu_{1,a},\quad\sup_{x\in\Omega_{2}}\frac{(x-1)|a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=\kappa_{2,a}\mu_{2,a}.

(10)

Then the common characteristic of the weight functions
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with properties (i)–(iii) can be summarizing as follows (see see Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 in
[
19
]
for comparison).

Proposition 1
.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a weight function satisfying properties (i)–(iii). Then

\displaystyle a(x)\geq a(c)\frac{(1-x)^{\kappa_{1,a}\mu_{1,a}}}{(1-c)^{\kappa_{1,a}\mu_{1,a}}},\quad\forall\,x\in[c,1]\subset[0,1],

(11)

\displaystyle a(x)\geq a(d)\frac{(x-1)^{\kappa_{2,a}\mu_{2,a}}}{(d-1)^{\kappa_{2,a}\mu_{2,a}}},\quad\forall\,x\in[1,d]\subset[1,2].

(12)

Proof.

Making use of representation (
10
), we get

(1-x)a^{\prime}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.1b}}}}{{\geq}}-\kappa_{1,a}\mu_{1,a}a(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega_{1}.

(13)

Integrating this inequality over
[c,x]\subset[c,1)
, where
c>0
by the initial assumptions,

\int_{c}^{x}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\,ds\geq-\kappa_{1,a}\mu_{1,a}\int_{c}^{x}\frac{1}{1-s}\,ds,\quad\forall\,x\in[c,1),

we arrive at the inequality (
11
). Arguing in a similar manner, we have

(x-1)a^{\prime}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.1b}}}}{{\leq}}\kappa_{2,a}\mu_{2,a}a(x),\quad\forall\,x\in(1,d].

(14)

Therefore,

\int_{x}^{d}\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\,ds\leq\kappa_{2,a}\mu_{2,a}\int_{x}^{d}\frac{1}{s-1}\,ds,\quad\forall\,x\in(1,d].

From this and the fact that
d<2
we deduce (
12
).
∎

Arguing in a similar manner, it is easy to establish the following inequalities.

\displaystyle a(x)\geq a(x_{1}^{\ast})\frac{(1-x)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{(1-x_{1}^{\ast})^{\mu_{1,a}}},\quad\forall\,x\in[x_{1}^{\ast},1]\subset[c,1],

(15)

\displaystyle a(x)\geq a(x_{2}^{\ast})\frac{(x-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{(x_{2}^{\ast}-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}},\quad\forall\,x\in[1,x_{2}^{\ast}]\subset[1,d].

(16)

The next result is crucial for our further consideration and it explores some remarkable properties of the weight functions
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying conditions (i)–(iii).

Proposition 2
.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a weight function satisfying properties (i)–(iii). Then the following assertions hold true:

\displaystyle 0<\mu_{1,a}<2\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\mu_{2,a}<2.

(17)

Proof.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given function with properties (i)–(iii). Setting

k:=\left\|\left(\sqrt{a}\right)_{x}^{-1}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widehat{a}(x):=ka(x)\ \text{ for all $x\in\Omega$},

we see that the function
\widehat{a}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
possesses all properties (i)–(iii) and
the direct calculations show that

\mu_{i,a}=\mu_{i,\widehat{a}}
for
i=1,2
.
Moreover, in this case, we have

\left\|\left(\sqrt{\widehat{a}}\right)_{x}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\left\|\left(\sqrt{a}\right)_{x}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1.

Hence, without loss of generality, we can suppose that the function
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is such that

\left\|\left(\sqrt{a}\right)_{x}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1.

(18)

As a consequence of this condition, we have

\sup_{x\in[c,1)}\frac{2\sqrt{a(x)}}{|a^{\prime}(x)|}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{x\in(1,d]}\frac{2\sqrt{a(x)}}{|a^{\prime}(x)|}\leq 1.

(19)

So, we can suppose that

2\sqrt{a(x)}\leq|a^{\prime}(x)|,\quad\forall\,x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},x^{\ast}_{2}].

(20)

Since
a(\cdot)
is a monotonically decreasing function on
(x^{\ast}_{1},1)
and
a(\cdot)
is a monotonically increasing function on
(1,x^{\ast}_{2})
, it follows from (
20
) that

a^{\prime}(x)\leq-2\sqrt{a(x)},\quad\forall\,x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},1)\quad\text{and}\quad a^{\prime}(x)\geq 2\sqrt{a(x)},\quad\forall\,x\in(1,x^{\ast}_{2}].

Then, after integration, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\frac{a^{\prime}(s)}{\sqrt{a(s)}}\,ds\leq-2(1-x),\quad\forall\,x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},1),

(21)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}\frac{a^{\prime}(s)}{\sqrt{a(s)}}\,ds\geq 2(x-1),\quad\forall\,x\in(1,x^{\ast}_{2}].

(22)

Taking into account that
a(1)=0
, we deduce from (
21
)–(
22
) that

\sqrt{a(x)}\geq 1-x,\quad\forall\,x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},1)\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{a(x)}\geq x-1,\quad\forall\,x\in(1,x^{\ast}_{2}],

and, as a consequence of these inequalities, we have

a(x)\geq(x-1)^{2},\quad\forall\,x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},x^{\ast}_{2}].

(23)

Utilizing the monotonicity property of
a(\cdot)
around the point
1
and the fact that
\left(\sqrt{a}\right)_{x}\not\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we deduce from (
23
) that there exists a positive value
\gamma\in(0,2)
such that

a(x)=\mathcal{O}\left(|x-1|^{2-\gamma}\right)\quad\text{ in $[x^{\ast}_{1},x^{\ast}_{2}]$},

(24)

that is,
a(x)\thicksim|x-1|^{2-\gamma}
near the degeneration point
1
.
Therefore, in view of representation (
9
), we finally have

\displaystyle\mu_{1,a}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in[x^{\ast}_{1},1)}\frac{|x-1||a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=2-\gamma<2,

(25)

\displaystyle\mu_{2,a}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in(1,x^{\ast}_{2}]}\frac{|x-1||a^{\prime}(x)|}{a(x)}=2-\gamma<2.

(26)

∎

Let
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
be some intermediate space with
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
.
Our next intention is to show that due to the properties (i)–(iii) of the weight function
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with respect to the scalar product

\left<u,v\right>_{H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}a(x)u^{\prime}(x)v^{\prime}(x)\,dx,\quad\forall\,u,v\in V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega).

(27)

To do so, it is enough to establish some version of Friedrichs’s inequality. Following in many aspects
[
1
,
19
]
, we will do it in two different manners (see Lemmas
3
and
4

Lemma 3
.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given function with properties (i)–(iii). Let
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
be some intermediate space with
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. Let
u
be an arbitrary element of
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
.
Then the following inequality

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq D_{a}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)}=D_{a}\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}

(28)

holds true for all
u\in V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
, where

\displaystyle D^{2}_{a}

\displaystyle=\max\{D_{1,a},D_{2,a}\},

(29)

\displaystyle D_{1,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(x_{1}^{\ast}-c\right)\left(2-x_{1}^{\ast}-c\right)}{2\min\limits_{x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}]}a(x)}+\frac{\left(1-x_{1}^{\ast}\right)^{2}}{a(x_{1}^{\ast})\left(2-\mu_{1,a}\right)},

(30)

\displaystyle D_{2,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(d-x_{2}^{\ast}\right)\left(d+x_{2}^{\ast}-2\right)}{2\min\limits_{x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d]}a(x)}+\frac{\left(x_{2}^{\ast}-1\right)^{2}}{a(x_{2}^{\ast})\left(2-\mu_{2,a}\right)}.

(31)

Proof.

Let
u
be a given element of
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. Then, for any
x\in[c,1)
, we have

\displaystyle|u(x)|=\left|\int_{c}^{x}u^{\prime}(s)\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{c}^{x}\sqrt{a(s)}u^{\prime}(s)\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(s)}}\,ds\right|\leq\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)}\left(\int_{c}^{x}\frac{ds}{a(s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)
stands for the weighted Lebesgue space endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{2}a\,dx\right)^{1/2}
.

From this and estimate (
15
), by Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1},a\,dx)}\int_{c}^{1}\int_{c}^{x}\frac{ds}{a(s)}\,dx=\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1},a\,dx)}\int_{c}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}\,dx\frac{ds}{a(s)}

\displaystyle=\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1},a\,dx)}\left[\int_{c}^{x_{1}^{\ast}}\frac{1-s}{a(s)}\,ds+\int_{x_{1}^{\ast}}^{1}\frac{1-s}{a(s)}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.1d}}}}{{\leq}}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1},a\,dx)}\left[\frac{1}{\min\limits_{x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}]}a(x)}\int_{c}^{x_{1}^{\ast}}(1-s)\,ds+\frac{(1-x_{1}^{\ast})^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(x_{1}^{\ast})}\int_{x_{1}^{\ast}}^{1}(1-s)^{1-\mu_{1,a}}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1},a\,dx)}\left[\frac{\left(x_{1}^{\ast}-c\right)\left(2-x_{1}^{\ast}-c\right)}{2\min\limits_{x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}]}a(x)}+\frac{\left(1-x_{1}^{\ast}\right)^{2}}{a(x_{1}^{\ast})\left(2-\mu_{1,a}\right)}\right].

(32)

Arguing in a similar manner, for any
x\in(1,d]
, we have

|u(x)|=\left|\int_{x}^{d}u^{\prime}(s)\,ds\right|\leq\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)}\left(\int_{x}^{d}\frac{ds}{a(s)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, estimate (
16
) and Fubini’s theorem lead us to the following chain of estimates

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2})}\leq\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2},a\,dx)}\int_{1}^{d}\int_{x}^{d}\frac{ds}{a(s)}\,dx=\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2},a\,dx)}\int_{1}^{d}\int_{1}^{s}\,dx\frac{ds}{a(s)}

\displaystyle=\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2},a\,dx)}\left[\int_{1}^{x_{2}^{\ast}}\frac{s-1}{a(s)}\,ds+\int_{x_{2}^{\ast}}^{d}\frac{s-1}{a(s)}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.1e}}}}{{\leq}}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2},a\,dx)}\left[\frac{1}{\min\limits_{x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d]}a(x)}\int_{x_{2}^{\ast}}^{d}(s-1)\,ds+\frac{(x_{2}^{\ast}-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(x_{2}^{\ast})}\int_{1}^{x_{2}^{\ast}}(s-1)^{1-\mu_{2,a}}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2},a\,dx)}\left[\frac{\left(d-x_{2}^{\ast}\right)\left(d+x_{2}^{\ast}-2\right)}{2\min\limits_{x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d]}a(x)}+\frac{\left(x_{2}^{\ast}-1\right)^{2}}{a(x_{2}^{\ast})\left(2-\mu_{2,a}\right)}\right].

(33)

It remains to notice that due to Proposition
2
, the obtained estimates (
32
) and (
33
) are consistent.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given function with properties (i)–(iii). Let
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
be some intermediate space with
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. Then the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{a}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)}=C_{a}\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}

(34)

holds true for all
u\in V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
with

C_{a}^{2}=4\max\left\{\frac{(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}]}a(x)},\frac{(1-x_{1}^{\ast})^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(x_{1}^{\ast})},\frac{(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d]}a(x)},\frac{(x_{2}^{\ast}-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(x_{2}^{\ast})}\right\}

(35)

In particular, if
x_{1}^{\ast}=c
and
x_{2}^{\ast}=d
, then (
34
) can be specified as follows

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 4\max\left\{\frac{(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(c)},\frac{(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(d)}\right\}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,a\,dx)}

(36)

Proof.

Let
u
be a given element of
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
.
Taking into account the following transformation

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{x}(1-s)u^{\prime}(s)u(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{c}^{x}(1-s)\frac{d}{ds}u^{2}(s)\,dx=\frac{1}{2}(1-x)u^{2}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}u^{2}(s)\,dx,

(37)

which is valid for all
x\in[c,1)
, we observe that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{c}^{x}\left[(1-s)u^{\prime}(s)-\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{c}^{x}\left[(1-s)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u^{2}(s)-(1-s)u^{\prime}(s)u(s)\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{2.7}}}}{{=}}\int_{c}^{x}\left[(1-s)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}-\frac{1}{4}u^{2}(s)\right]\,ds-\frac{1}{2}(1-x)u^{2}(x),\quad\forall\,x\in[c,1).

From this, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{x}u^{2}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{c}^{x}(1-s)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=4\int_{c}^{x_{1}^{\ast}}(1-s)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds+4\int_{x_{1}^{\ast}}^{x}(1-s)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.4a}}}}{{\leq}}4(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}\int_{c}^{x_{1}^{\ast}}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds+4\int_{x_{1}^{\ast}}^{x}(1-s)^{\mu_{1,a}}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.1d}}}}{{\leq}}\frac{4(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}]}a(x)}\int_{c}^{x_{1}^{\ast}}a(s)\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds+\frac{4(1-x_{1}^{\ast})^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(x_{1}^{\ast})}\int_{x_{1}^{\ast}}^{x}a(s)\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{4(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}]}a(x)},\frac{4(1-x_{1}^{\ast})^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(x_{1}^{\ast})}\right\}\int_{c}^{x}a(s)\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds,\quad\forall\,x\in[x_{1}^{\ast},1).

Taking the limit as
x\nearrow 1
in the last relation, we arrive at the estimate

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq\max\left\{\frac{4(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}]}a(x)},\frac{4(1-x_{1}^{\ast})^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(x_{1}^{\ast})}\right\}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1},a\,dx)}.

(38)

By analogy with the previous case, we make use of the following transformation which is valid for each
x\in[1,d]
.

\int_{x}^{d}(s-1)u(s)u^{\prime}(s)\,ds=\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}^{d}(s-1)\frac{d}{ds}u^{2}(s)\,ds=\frac{1}{2}\left[-(x-1)u^{2}(x)-\int_{x}^{d}u^{2}(s)\,ds\right].

(39)

Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{x}^{d}\left[(s-1)u^{\prime}(s)+\frac{1}{2}u(s)\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{d}\left[(s-1)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u^{2}(s)+(s-1)u(s)u^{\prime}(s)\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{2.9}}}}{{=}}\int_{x}^{d}\left[(s-1)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}-\frac{1}{4}u^{2}(s)\right]\,ds-\frac{1}{2}(x-1)u^{2}(x).

Since
(x-1)u(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[1,d]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{d}

\displaystyle u^{2}(s)\,ds\leq 4\int_{x}^{x_{2}^{\ast}}(s-1)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds+4\int_{x_{2}^{\ast}}^{d}(s-1)^{2}\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.1e}, \eqref{1.0.4a}}}}{{\leq}}\left[\frac{4(x_{2}^{\ast}-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(x_{2}^{\ast})}\int_{x}^{x_{2}^{\ast}}a(s)\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds+\frac{4(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d]}a(x)}\int_{x_{2}^{\ast}}^{d}a(s)\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{4(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d]}a(x)},\frac{4(x_{2}^{\ast}-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(x_{2}^{\ast})}\right\}\int_{x}^{d}a(s)\left[u^{\prime}(s)\right]^{2}\,ds,\quad\forall\,x\in(1,x_{2}^{\ast}].

As a result, passing to the limit in the last relation as
x\searrow 1
, we arrive at the inequality

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2})}\leq\max\left\{\frac{4(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{\min\limits_{x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d]}a(x)},\frac{4(x_{2}^{\ast}-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(x_{2}^{\ast})}\right\}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2},a\,dx)}.

(40)

Thus, the announced estimate (
34
) is a direct consequence of (
38
) and (
40
).
∎

As a direct consequence of Lemmas
3
and
4
, we have the following result.

Theorem 5

(Friedrichs’s inequality)
.

Let

a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given function with properties (i)–(iii). Let
u
be an arbitrary element of some intermediate space
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. Then

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\min\{D_{a},C_{a}\}\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\
=\min\left\{\max\left\{\sqrt{D_{1,a}},\sqrt{D_{2,a}}\right\},C_{a}\right\}\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/2},\quad\forall\,u\in V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega),

(41)

where the constants
D_{1,a}
,
D_{2,a}
and
C_{a}
are defined in (
30
), (
31
), and (
35
), respectively.

Now we can give the following conclusion.

Corollary 6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5
,
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with respect to the scalar product

\left<u,v\right>_{V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}a(x)u^{\prime}(x)v^{\prime}(x)\,dx,\quad\forall\,u,v\in V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega).

(42)

In what follows, we will distinguish two possible cases for the weight function
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Namely, we say that we deal with

•

a weak degeneration in (
4
) if
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies properties (i)–(iii) and
1/a\in L^{1}(\Omega)
;

•

a strong degeneration in (
4
) if
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies properties (i)–(iii) and
1/a\not\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Starting with the weak degenerate case, we note that due to the continuous embedding
W^{1,1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
and the estimates

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|y|\,dx\leq|\Omega|^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|y|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{|\Omega|}\,\|y\|_{a},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|y_{x}|\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|y_{x}|^{2}a\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Omega}a^{-1}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\|y\|_{a},

we have the following result (we refer to
[
1
, Proposition 2.5]
for the details).

Theorem 7
.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a weight function satisfying properties (i)–(iii) and
1/a\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)=W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
,

W^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
,
W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,1}(\Omega)
,
W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega)
compactly, and
W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into the class of absolutely continuous functions on
\overline{\Omega}
, so

\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}y(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}y(x),\quad|y(1)|<+\infty,\quad\forall\,y\in H^{1}_{a}(\Omega),

(43)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\sqrt{a(x)}y(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}\sqrt{a(x)}y(x)=0,\quad\forall\,y\in H^{1}_{a}(\Omega).

(44)

In addition, if
y
is an arbitrary element of the space

W^{2}_{a}(\Omega):=\left\{y\in W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\ :\ ay_{x}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\right\},

(45)

then the following transmission condition

\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=L,\quad\text{with }\ |L|<+\infty,

(46)

holds true.

However, the situation changes drastically if we deal with strong degeneration in (
4
). Indeed, let us consider the following example. Let
c=0
,
d=2
, and

y(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|x-1|^{-\frac{1}{4}}-1,&\text{ if }\ x\in(0,1),\\
|x-1|^{\frac{1}{2}},&\text{ if }\ x\in[1,2).\end{array}\right.

Setting
a(x)=|x-1|^{7/4}
, we see that properties (i)–(iii) hold true. Moreover, in this case we have
1/a\not\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then, in spite of the fact that the function
y:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
has a discontinuity of the second kind at
x^{0}=1
, a direct calculations show that

\|y\|^{2}_{a}=\int_{0}^{2}\left[y^{2}(x)+a(x)y_{x}^{2}(x)\right]\,dx<+\infty

whereas

a(x)y_{x}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\frac{1}{4}|x-1|^{\frac{1}{2}},&\text{ if }\ x\in(0,1),\\
+\frac{1}{2}|x-1|^{\frac{5}{4}},&\text{ if }\ x\in[1,2).\end{array}\right.

So, transmission conditions (
46
) for the given function
y
with finite
H^{1}_{a}
-norm can be specified as follows

\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=0.

(47)

In fact, we have the following result (see
[
1
, Proposition 2.5]
for comparison).

Theorem 8
.

Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a weight function satisfying properties (i)–(iii) and
1/a\not\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
V^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
be some intermediate space with
H^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\subseteq V^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
. Then the following assertions hold true:

\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}|x-1|y^{2}(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}|x-1|y^{2}(x)=0,\quad\forall\,y\in V^{1}_{a}(\Omega),

(48)

\displaystyle\exists\,x_{i}\in\Omega_{i},\ i=1,2,\text{ such that }\ y(x)=o\left(|x-1|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\ \text{for a.a. }\ x\in(x_{1},x_{2}),

(49)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=0,\quad\forall\,y\in V^{2}_{a}(\Omega),

(50)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}|x-1|a(x)y_{x}(x)^{2}=\lim_{x\searrow 1}|x-1|a(x)y_{x}(x)^{2}=0,\quad\forall\,y\in V^{2}_{a}(\Omega),

(51)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)\varphi_{x}(x)y(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)\varphi_{x}(x)y(x)=0,\quad\forall\,y\in V^{1}_{a}(\Omega),\ \forall\,\varphi\in V^{2}_{a}(\Omega),

(52)

where the small symbol
o
stands for the Bachmann-Landau asymptotic notation, and

V^{2}_{a}(\Omega):=\left\{y\in V^{1}_{a}(\Omega)\ :\ ay_{x}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\right\}.

Proof.

Let
y\in V^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
. Without loss of generality, we assume (for the simplicity) that
x_{1}^{\ast}=c
and
x_{2}^{\ast}=d
. Let us show that the function

v(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-x)y^{2}(x),&c\leq x<1,\\
0,&x=1,\\
(x-1)y^{2}(x),&1<x\leq d\end{array}\right.

is continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
. Indeed,
v
is locally absolutely continuous in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{1\}
and

v_{x}=\mathrm{sign}\,(x-1)\,y^{2}(x)+2|x-1|y(x)y_{x}(x),\quad\text{a.e. in }\ \overline{\Omega}.

Since
y\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|x-1|^{2}

\displaystyle y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.4a}}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}|x-1|^{\mu_{1,a}}y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{2}}|x-1|^{\mu_{2,a}}y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.1d}--\eqref{1.0.1e}}}}{{\leq}}\frac{(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(c)}\int_{\Omega_{1}}a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx+\frac{(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(d)}\int_{\Omega_{2}}a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\frac{(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(c)},\frac{(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(d)}\right\}\|y\|^{2}_{a},

(53)

it follows that
v_{x}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence,
v
is an absolutely continuous functions and, as a consequence, the limits
\lim_{x\nearrow 1}|x-1|y^{2}(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}|x-1|y^{2}(x)=L
do exist and must vanish, for otherwise
y^{2}(x)\sim L/|x-1|
(near the point
x_{0}=1
) would be not integrable. So, we come into conflict with the initial condition:
y\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. From this and the fact that
a(x)=O(|x-1|)
in some neighborhood of
x=1
, we immediately deduce properties (
48
)–(
49
).

To prove the equality (
50
), it is enough to observe that the function
a(x)y_{x}(x)
with
y\in V^{2}_{a}(\Omega)
is absolutely continuous. Hence, the limits
\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)y_{x}(x)=L
do exist and must vanish, for otherwise
a(x)y_{x}(x)^{2}\sim L^{2}/a(x)
(near the point
x_{0}=1
) would be not integrable. So, we come into conflict with the initial condition:
y\in V^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
.

To establish property (
51
), we set

v(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-x)a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x),&c\leq x<1,\\
0,&x=1,\\
(x-1)a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x),&1<x\leq d,\end{array}\right.

where
y
is an arbitrary element of
V^{2}_{a}(\Omega)
. Then
v(x)
is continuous on
\Omega
. Indeed,
v
is locally absolutely continuous in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{1\}
and

\displaystyle v_{x}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sign}\,(x-1)\,a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x)+2|x-1|y_{x}(x)\left(a(x)y_{x}(x)\right)_{x}

\displaystyle\quad-|x-1|a_{x}(x)y^{2}_{x}(x)=I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x)+I_{3}(x),\quad\text{a.e. in }\ \overline{\Omega}.

Since
y\in V^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
, it follows that
I_{1}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. The same conclusion is true for the second term
I_{2}
. Indeed, in view of estimate (
53
), we have

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int_{\Omega}|x-1|^{2}y_{x}(x)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(a(x)y_{x}(x)\right)_{x}^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.1.2}}}}{{\leq}}2\sqrt{\max\left\{\frac{(1-c)^{\mu_{1,a}}}{a(c)},\frac{(d-1)^{\mu_{2,a}}}{a(d)}\right\}}\|y\|_{a}\|(ay_{x})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<+\infty.

As for the third term, we see that

\displaystyle\|I_{3}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.1d}--\eqref{1.0.1e}}}}{{\leq}}{\mu_{1,a}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx+{\mu_{2,a}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{1.0.4a}}}}{{\leq}}2\int_{\Omega}a(x)y^{2}_{x}(x)\,dx\leq 2\|y\|^{2}_{a}<+\infty.

So,
v(x)
is absolutely continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
. As a consequence, we see that
the limits
\lim_{x\nearrow 1}|x-1|a(x)y_{x}(x)^{2}=\lim_{x\searrow 1}|x-1|a(x)y_{x}(x)^{2}=L
do exist and must vanish, for otherwise
a(x)y_{x}(x)^{2}\sim L/|x-1|
(near the point
x_{0}=1
) would be not integrable.

It remains to prove relation (
52
). We do it by proving that the function

v(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a(x)\varphi_{x}(x)y(x),&c\leq x<1,\\
0,&x=1,\\
a(x)\varphi_{x}(x)y(x),&1<x\leq d\end{array}\right.

is continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
. This follows by the arguments as above, because

\displaystyle\|v_{x}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{a}y_{x}||\sqrt{a}\varphi_{x}|\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|y||(a\varphi_{x})_{x}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|y\|_{a}\|\varphi\|_{a}+\|y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|(a\varphi_{x})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<+\infty,

and, therefore,
v
is absolutely continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
. Thus, we see that
the limits

\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)\varphi_{x}(x)y(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)\varphi_{x}(x)y(x)=L

(54)

do exist. To conclude the proof, we show that
L=0
. Indeed, in view of the property (
50
), we have

a(x)|\varphi_{x}(x)|=\left|\int_{1}^{x}\left(a\varphi_{x}\right)_{x}\,dx\right|\leq\sqrt{|x-1|}\|(a\varphi_{x})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\ \forall\,x\in\Omega_{0},\ \forall\,\varphi\in V^{2}_{a}(\Omega).

(55)

Hence, if we assume that
L\neq 0
, then, in a small neighborhood
\mathcal{U}(1)
of
x=1
, for any functions
y\in V^{1}_{a}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in V^{2}_{a}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{A.1}}}}{{\leq}}a(x)|\varphi_{x}(x)||y(x)|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{A.2}}}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{|x-1|}|y(x)|\|(a\varphi_{x})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Const}}{2}\,\sqrt{|x-1|}|y(x)|,\ \forall\,x\in\mathcal{U}(1).

From this we deduce that

\frac{L}{\sqrt{|x-1|}}\leq\mathrm{Const}\,|y(x)|,\ \forall\,x\in\mathcal{U}(1).

However, since
y
is an
L^{2}(\Omega)
-function, this relations becomes inconsistent. Thus,
L=0
.
∎

The main technical difficulty related to the problem (
4
)–(
6
) comes from the degeneration effect at the point
x_{0}=1
. Therefore, taking now into account Theorems
7
and

y_{tt}-\left(a(x)y_{x}\right)_{x}=0\quad\text{in }\ (0,T)\times(c,1)\ \text{and }\ (0,T)\times(1,d),

with the initial conditions

(62)

Since transmission conditions (

) were substantiated in Theorems
7

8

On well-posedness of the degenerate transmission problems

In this section we recall the main results of semi-group theory concerning weak and classical notions of solutions for differential operator equation.
Let
a:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a given function with properties (i)–(iii). Let
V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
be some intermediate space with
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)

-closure of a linear span of
H^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)
with any element

We associate with it the Hilbert space

and endow it with the scalar product (see
[
1
]
for comparison)

\left<\left[u\atop v\right],\left[\widetilde{u}\atop\widetilde{v}\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}=\int_{\Omega}v(x)\widetilde{v}(x)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}a(x)u_{x}(x)\widetilde{u}_{x}(x)\,dx.

We define the unbounded operator
\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}_{a}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{a}
, associated with the problem (
56
)–(
62
) provided
f_{c}(t),f_{d}(t)\equiv 0
, as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left[u\atop v\right]=\left[v\atop\left(au_{x}\right)_{x}\right].

(63)

and either

D(\mathcal{A})=\left\{\left[u\atop v\right]\in W^{2}_{a}(\Omega)\times V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\ :\ \begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}u(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}u(x),\\
\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x)=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x),\\

(64)

if
1/a\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, or

D(\mathcal{A})=\left\{\left[u\atop v\right]\in W^{2}_{a}(\Omega)\times V^{1}_{a,0}(\Omega)\ :\ \begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a\varphi_{x}u=0=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a\varphi_{x}u,\ \forall\,\varphi\in H^{2}_{a}(\Omega),\\
\displaystyle\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x)=0=\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x),\\
u(d)=0\end{array}\right\}

(65)

provided

Arguing as in
[

, Section II.2]

is a dense subset of
\mathcal{H}_{a}
.

Lemma 9
.

\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}_{a}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{a}
is the generator of a contraction semi-group in
\mathcal{H}_{a}
.

Proof.

It is well-known that if
H
is a Hilbert space and
B:D(B)\subset H\rightarrow H
is a densely defined linear operator such that both
B
and
B^{\ast}
are dissipative, i.e.,

\left<Bu,u\right>_{H}\leq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\left<u,B^{\ast}u\right>_{H}\leq 0\quad\forall\,u\in D(B),

then
B
generates a strongly continuous semi-group of contraction operators
[
22
, p. 686]
. Let us show that the operator
\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}_{a}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{a}
satisfies the above mentioned properties.

Indeed, since the inclusion
\mathcal{A}\left[u\atop v\right]\in\mathcal{H}_{a}
is obvious for all
\left[u\atop v\right]\in D(\mathcal{A})
, it remains to check whether the properties

\left<\mathcal{A}\left[u\atop v\right],\left[u\atop v\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}\leq 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\left<\left[u\atop v\right],\left(\mathcal{A}\right)^{\ast}\left[u\atop v\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}\leq 0\quad\forall\,\left[u\atop v\right]\in D(\mathcal{A})

(66)

hold true.
We do it for the case (II),
1/a\not\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, because the case (I) can be considered in a similar manner. The first inequality in (
66
) immediately follows from the definition of the set
D(\mathcal{A})
and the following relations

\left<\mathcal{A}\left[u\atop v\right],\left[u\atop v\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}=\left<\left[v\atop\left(au_{x}\right)_{x}\right],\left[u\atop v\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\left(au_{x}\right)_{x}v\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}}av_{x}u_{x}\,dx\\
=\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\left[\int_{c}^{x}\left(au_{s}\right)_{s}v\,ds+\int_{c}^{x}av_{s}u_{s}\,ds\right]+\lim_{x\searrow 1}\left[\int_{x}^{d}\left(au_{s}\right)_{s}v\,ds+\int_{x}^{d}av_{s}u_{s}\,ds\right]\\
=\left[\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x)v(x)\right]-\left[\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x)v(x)\right]=0,\quad\text{for all }\ \left[u\atop v\right]\in D(\mathcal{A})

(67)

by the transmission conditions.

Taking into account the equality

\left<\mathcal{A}\left[u\atop v\right],\left[\widetilde{u}\atop\widetilde{v}\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}=\left<\left[u\atop v\right],\mathcal{A}^{\ast}\left[\widetilde{u}\atop\widetilde{v}\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}},\quad\left[u\atop v\right],\left[\widetilde{u}\atop\widetilde{v}\right]\in D(\mathcal{A}),

we see that

\left<\mathcal{A}\left[u\atop v\right],\left[\widetilde{u}\atop\widetilde{v}\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}=\left<\left[v\atop\left(au_{x}\right)_{x}\right],\left[\widetilde{u}\atop\widetilde{v}\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\left(au_{x}\right)_{x}\widetilde{v}\,dx+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\Omega_{i}}av_{x}\widetilde{u}_{x}\,dx\\
=\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\left[\int_{c}^{x}\left(au_{s}\right)_{s}\widetilde{v}\,ds+\int_{c}^{x}av_{s}\widetilde{u}_{s}\,ds\right]+\lim_{x\searrow 1}\left[\int_{x}^{d}\left(au_{s}\right)_{s}\widetilde{v}\,ds+\int_{x}^{d}av_{s}\widetilde{u}_{s}\,ds\right]\\
=\lim_{x\nearrow 1}\left[-\int_{c}^{x}au_{s}\widetilde{v}_{s}\,ds-\int_{c}^{x}v\left(a\widetilde{u}_{s}\right)_{s}\,ds\right]+\lim_{x\searrow 1}\left[-\int_{x}^{d}au_{s}\widetilde{v}_{s}\,ds-\int_{x}^{d}v\left(a\widetilde{u}_{s}\right)_{s}\,ds\right]\\
+\left[\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x)\widetilde{v}(x)-\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)u_{x}(x)\widetilde{v}(x)\right]\\
+\left[\lim_{x\nearrow 1}a(x)\widetilde{u}_{x}(x)v(x)-\lim_{x\searrow 1}a(x)\widetilde{u}_{x}(x)v(x)\right]\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by t.c.}}}{{=}}-\int_{\Omega}\left(a\widetilde{u}_{x}\right)_{x}v\,dx-\int_{\Omega}a\widetilde{v}_{x}u_{x}\,dx=\left<\left[u\atop v\right],\left[-\widetilde{v}\atop-\left(a\widetilde{u}_{x}\right)_{x}\right]\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{a}}.

Hence,

,
and arguing as in (

is a dissipative operator. Thus,

+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega_{0}}\left(1-B_{a}\right)y_{t}^{2}(t,x)\,dx\,dt\\

Since

\displaystyle-\frac{(x-1)a_{x}(x)}{a(x)}\geq-\frac{|x-1||a_{x}(x)|}{a(x)}\geq-\max\{\mu_{1,a},\mu_{2,a}\}\quad\text{in }\ [x_{1}^{\ast},x_{2}^{\ast}],

\displaystyle-\frac{(x-1)a_{x}(x)}{a(x)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{4.0.1}}}}{{\geq}}-\mu_{1,a}\quad\forall\,x\in[c,x_{1}^{\ast}],

\displaystyle-\frac{(x-1)a_{x}(x)}{a(x)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{4.0.2}}}}{{\geq}}-\mu_{2,a}\quad\forall\,x\in[x_{2}^{\ast},d],

it follows that

I_{3}+I_{4}\geq\left(2-\max\{\mu_{1,a},\mu_{2,a}\}\right)\int_{0}^{T}E_{y}(0)\,dt=\left(2-\max\{\mu_{1,a},\mu_{2,a}\}\right)TE_{y}(0).

Taking into account that

I_{1}=2\int_{\Omega_{0}}\left[(x-1)y_{x}(t,x)y_{t}(t,x)\right]_{t=0}^{t=T}\,dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{by \eqref{4.1.5.1}}}}{{\geq}}-\max\{4,C_{a}^{2}\}E_{y}(0),

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{0}}y(t,x)y_{t}(t,x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\min\{D_{a},C_{a}\}}y^{2}(t,x)+\min\{D_{a},C_{a}\}y_{t}^{2}(t,x)\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\min\{D_{a},C_{a}\}E_{y}(0),

where
\min\{D_{a},C_{a}\}

I_{2}\geq-2B_{a}\min\{D_{a},C_{a}\}E_{y}(0)=-\min\{D_{a},C_{a}\}\max\left\{\mu_{1,a},\mu_{2,a}\right\}E_{y}(0).

Thus, the announced estimate (

Sivers function of sea quarks in the Light-Cone Model
Xiaoyan Luan, Zhun Lu

Abstract
We calculate the Sivers function of u ¯ \bar{u} and d ¯ \bar{d} quarks using the overlap representation within the light-cone formalism. The light-cone wave functions of the proton is obtained in terms of the | q ¯ ​ q ​ B ⟩ |\bar{q}qB\rangle Fock states motivated by the meson-baryon fluctuation model. We consider the final-state interaction at the level of one gluon exchange. In a simplified scenario, the Sivers function of u ¯ \bar{u} and d ¯ \bar{d} can be expressed as the convolution of the Sivers function of the pion inside the proton and the unpolarized distribution of q ¯ \bar{q} inside the pion. The model parameters are fixed by fitting the unpolarized sea quark distributions to the known parameterizations. We present the numerical results for f 1 ⟂ u ¯ / p ​ ( x , 𝒌 T 2 ) f_{1}^{\perp\bar{u}/p}(x,\bm{k}_{T}^{2}) and f 1 ⟂ d ¯ / p ​ ( x , 𝒌 T 2 ) f_{1}^{\perp\bar{d}/p}(x,\bm{k}_{T}^{2}) . The first transverse moment of the sea quark Sivers functions in our model are find to be negative and the magnitude is about 0.004 at most.



I
Introduction

The transverse momentum dependent (TMD) parton distribution functions (PDFs), also called as unintegrated PDFs, describe the probability of finding a quark with certain light-cone momentum fraction
x
and transverse momentum
k_{T}
in a hadron
Collins and Soper 1982
.
Among them the Sivers function
Sivers 1990
is of particular interests.
Due to the time-reversal-odd (T-odd) spin-orbit correlation of partons inside the nucleon,
the Sivers function describes the distortion in the distribution of unpolarized partons inside a transversely polarized nucleon.
Thereby it can give rise to the single-spin asymmetries (SSAs) in leading-twist in various semi-inclusive high energy processes.
During the last two decades, the SSAs related to Sivers function in semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering (SIDIS) have been measured by the HERMES
et al. HERMES Collaboration
;
et al. HERMES Collaboration
, COMPASS
Alekseev et al. 2009
;
Alekseev et al. 2010
;
Adolph et al. 2012
;
Adolph et al. 2017
, and JLab Hall A
Qian et al. 2011
;
Zhao et al. 2014
Collaborations.
Recently, the measurement on the Sivers effects in Drell-Yan and W/Z production have also been performed by COMPASS
Aghasyan et al. 2017
and STAR
Adamczyk et al. 2016
.
The data from these experiments were utilized by
different groups
Anselmino et al. 2005
;
Efremov et al. 2005
;
Collins et al. 2006
;
Vogelsang and Yuan 2005
;
Anselmino et al. 2009a
;
Anselmino et al. 2012
;
Bacchetta and Radici 2011
;
Echevarria et al. 2014
;
Anselmino et al. 2017a
;
Martin et al. 2017
;
Boglione et al. 2018
;
Bury et al. 2021

to extract the quark Sivers functions of the proton without or with TMD evolution.
On the other hand, various model calculations have been applied to calculate the quark Sivers functions, such as the the spectator model
Brodsky et al. 2002a
;
Boer et al. 2003
;
Bacchetta et al. 2004
, the light-cone quark model
Lu and Ma 2004
;
Pasquini and Yuan 2010
, the non-relativistic constituent quark model
Courtoy et al. 2008
, and the MIT bag model
Yuan 2003
;
Courtoy et al. 2009
.

Although there are plenty of measurements on the Sivers asymmetry combined with phenomenological extractions, our knowledge on the Sivers function is still limited.
Currently, most of the information from theoretical study and data focuses on the Sivers functions of the valence quarks.
The Sivers functions of the sea quarks and gluon are less constrained compared to those of the
u
and
d
quarks.
In the Drell-Yan process, the anti-quarks play a more significant role since the dominant sub-process is
q\bar{q}\rightarrow\gamma^{\star}\rightarrow
.
The time-reversal property of the Sivers function predicts that it present generalized universality, i.e., the sign of the Sivers function measured in Drell-Yan process should be opposite to its sign measured in SIDIS
Collins 2002
;
Brodsky et al. 2002b
;
Brodsky et al. 2002c
.
The verification of this sign change
Anselmino et al. 2009b
;
Kang and Qiu 2009
;
Peng and Qiu 2014
;
Echevarria et al. 2014
;
Anselmino et al. 2017b
is one of the most fundamental tests of our understanding of the QCD dynamics, and it is also the main pursue of the existing and future Drell-Yan facilities
Aghasyan et al. 2017
;
Adamczyk et al. 2016
;
Arbuzov et al. 2021
.
As pointed out in Ref.
Bury et al. 2021
, the sea quark Sivers function could play an important role in the test of the sign-change between SIDIS and Drell-Yan, since the sign for anti-quark Sivers function almost exclusively defines the sign of the asymmetry of Drell-Yan and
W/Z
production in polarized
pp
collision.
Furthermore, sea quark Sivers function is also of importance to explain the azimuthal asymmetry of kaon production in in SIDIS.

In this work, we apply an intuitive model to calculate the Sivers functions of the
\bar{u}
and
\bar{d}
quarks using the overlap representation.
In the model the sea quark degree freedom is generated by the assumption that the proton can fluctuate to a composite state containing a meson
M
and a baryon
B
.
It is similar to the meson cloud effect which was proposed by Sullivan through analyzing the deep inelastic scattering (DIS) process
Sullivan 1972
,
and has been applied to study the sea quark distributions of the nucleon
Koepf et al. 1996
;
Huang et al. 2004
;
Brodsky and Ma 1996
.
We consider the
\bar{q}q
component of the pion meson and derive the light-cone wave functions (LCWFs) of the proton in terms of the Fock-state
|\bar{q}qB\rangle
.
As proposed in Ref.
Lu and Schmidt 2007
, the overlap representation
Brodsky et al. 2001a
can be also applied to calculate the Sivers function with LCWFs.
In this approach, the Sivers function is proportional to the overlap of the LCWFs of the initial-state and final-state nucleons differ by
\Delta L_{z}=\pm 1
, where
L_{z}
is the orbital angular momentum of the active quark/antiquark.
The values of the parameters in the model are determined by fitting the unpolarized sea quark distributions in the same model to the available parameterizations.
We note that the Sivers function of the light sea has been calculated with a chiral Lagrangian
He and Wang 2019
;
the sea quark Sivers functions in the small
x
region has also been studied within color glass condensate framework
Dong et al. 2019
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Sec. II, we derive the LCWFs of the proton in terms of the Fock state
|\bar{q}qB\rangle
.
In Sec. III, we apply the LCWFs to calculate the unpolarized sea quark distribution
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/p}(x,\bm{k}_{T}^{2})
and the sea quark Sivers function
f_{1T}^{\bot\bar{q}/p}(x,\bm{k}_{T}^{2})
.
In Sec. IV, we present the numerical result for the sea quark Sivers function
f_{1T}^{\bot\bar{q}/p}(x,\bm{k}_{T}^{2})
and the first transverse momentum
f_{1T}^{\bot(1)\bar{q}/p}(x)
.
We summarize the paper in Sec. V.

II
LCWFs of the proton in terms of
|\bar{q}qB\rangle
.

The light-cone formalism has been widely recognized as a convenient way to calculate the parton distribution functions of nucleon and meson
Lepage and Brodsky 1980
.
Within the light-cone approach, the wave functions for a hadronic composite state can be expressed as LCWFs in Fock-state basis.
On the other hand, the overlap representation has also been used to study various form factors of the hadrons
Brodsky et al. 2001a
and the pion
Xiao and Ma 2003
, anomalous magnetic moment of the nucleon
Brodsky et al. 2001a
as well as generalized parton distribution functions
Brodsky et al. 2001b
. recently, the has also applied to calculate the T-odd TMDs
Lu and Schmidt 2007
;
Bacchetta et al. 2008a

In this section, we will extend the light-cone formalism to calculate the Sivers function of sea quarks.

Another important ingredient needed in the calculation is the generation of the sea quark degree of freedom.
For this purpose we will apply the baryon-meson fluctuation model, in which the the proton can fluctuate to a composite system formed by a meson
M
and a baryon
B
, where the meson is composed in terms of
q\bar{q}
:

\displaystyle|p\rangle\to|MB\rangle\to|q\bar{q}B\rangle.

(1)

In this work we consider the fluctuation
|p\rangle\to|\pi^{+}n\rangle
and

|p\rangle\to|\pi^{-}\Delta^{++}\rangle
.
For the above proton composite state, the LCWFs can be calculated from the following matrix elements
Brodsky et al. 2001a
;
Xiao and Ma 2003
;
Bacchetta et al. 2008a

\displaystyle\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(r,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{r^{+}}{(P-r)^{+}}}\frac{1}{r^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}}\overline{U}_{B}(P-r,\lambda_{B})\gamma_{5}U(P,\lambda_{P})

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\frac{k^{+}}{(r-k)^{+}}}\frac{1}{k^{2}-m^{2}}\overline{u}(r-k,\lambda_{q})\gamma_{5}\nu(k,\lambda_{\bar{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,\bm{r}_{T})\psi_{{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T}),

(2)

where
U
,
U_{B}
,
u
and
\nu
are the Dirac spinors of the proton, and the baryon in the fluctuated state, the quark and the antiquark inside the meson, respectively.
k
and
r
denote the momenta of the antiquark and the meson, with
x
and
y
representing their light-cone momentum fractions.
The indices
\lambda_{N}
,
\lambda_{B}
,
\lambda_{q}
,
\lambda_{\bar{q}}
denote the helicity of the proton, the baryon, the quark and sea quark, respectively.
To evaluate Eq. (
II
), we apply the conventions in Refs.
Lepage and Brodsky 1980
;
Meissner et al. 2007
for the spinors (in the case of quark and antiquark):

\displaystyle u(k,+)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3/2}k^{+}}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}k^{+}+m\\
k_{1}+ik_{2}\\
\sqrt{2}k^{+}-m\\
k_{1}+ik_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(3)

\displaystyle u(k,-)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3/2}k^{+}}}\begin{pmatrix}k_{1}+ik_{2}\\
\sqrt{2}k^{+}+m\\
k_{1}-ik_{2}\\
\sqrt{2}k^{+}-m\\
\end{pmatrix},

(4)

\displaystyle\nu(k,+)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3/2}xr^{+}}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2}xr^{+}-m\\
k_{1}+ik_{2}\\
\sqrt{2}k^{+}+m\\
k_{1}+ik_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(5)

\displaystyle\nu(k,-)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{3/2}k^{+}}}\begin{pmatrix}-k_{1}+ik_{2}\\
\sqrt{2}k^{+}-m\\
k_{1}-ik_{2}\\
\sqrt{2}k^{+}-m\\
\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where
m
is the quark/antiquark mass,
k_{i}
is the i-th component of the transverse momenta.
Similarly, replacing
k
to
P
(or
P-r
), and
m
to
M
(or
M_{B}
), one can obtain the spinors of the proton (or baryon
M
).
In principle there could be higher-spin components for the
\Delta
baryon, in this work we only consider the spin-
1/2
components for simplicity.

After some algebra, we find that the wave function
\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}
can be organized to the following form

\displaystyle\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})=

\displaystyle\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,\bm{r}_{T})\psi_{{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T}),

where
\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,r_{T})
can be viewed as the wave function of the nucleon in terms
\pi B
components, and
\psi_{{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})
is the pion wave function in terms of
q\bar{q}
components.
For the former ones, they have the expression:

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{+}(y,\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{B}-(1-y)M}{\sqrt{1-y}}\phi_{1},

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{-}(y,\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{1}+ir_{2}}{\sqrt{1-y}}\phi_{1},

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{+}(y,\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{1}-ir_{2}}{\sqrt{1-y}}\phi_{1},

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{-}(y,\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-y)M-M_{B}}{\sqrt{1-y}}\phi_{1},

(7)

and
\phi_{1}\equiv\phi_{1}(y,\bm{r}_{T})
is the wave function in the momentum space

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(y,\bm{r}_{T})=-\frac{g_{1}(r^{2})\sqrt{y(1-y)}}{\bm{r}_{T}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(m_{\pi}^{2})},

(8)

where
m_{\pi}
is the mass of
\pi
meson,
g_{1}(r^{2})
is for the coupling of the nucleon-pion-baryon vertex, and

\displaystyle L_{1}^{2}({m_{\pi}^{2}})=yM_{B}^{2}+(1-y){m_{\pi}^{2}}-y(1-y)M^{2}.

(9)

As for the wave functions of the meson fluctuated from the nucleon in terms of
q\bar{q}
,
\psi_{{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{p}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})
, they have the following expressions:

\displaystyle\psi{{}_{+}}{{}_{+}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{my}{\sqrt{x(y-x)}}\phi_{2},

\displaystyle\psi{{}_{+}}{{}_{-}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{y(k_{1}-ik_{2})-x(r_{1}-ir_{2})}{\sqrt{x(y-x)}}\phi_{2},

\displaystyle\psi{{}_{-}}{{}_{+}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{y(k_{1}+ik_{2})-x(r_{1}+ir_{2})}{\sqrt{x(y-x)}}\phi_{2},

\displaystyle\psi{{}_{-}}{{}_{-}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{-my}{\sqrt{x(y-x)}}\phi_{2}.

(10)

Again
\phi_{2}=\phi_{2}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})
is the wave function in the momentum space

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})=\frac{g_{2}\sqrt{\frac{x}{y}(1-\frac{x}{y})}}{(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})^{2}+L_{2}^{2}(m^{2})},

(11)

g_{2}(k^{2})
is the coupling of the pion-quark-antiquark vertex, and

\displaystyle L_{2}^{2}(m^{2})=\frac{x}{y}m^{2}+\left(1-\frac{x}{y}\right)m^{2}-\frac{x}{y}\left(1-\frac{x}{y}\right){m_{\pi}}^{2}.

(12)

For the coupling
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, we adopt the dipolar form factor

\displaystyle g(p^{2})=g\frac{p^{2}-m^{2}}{|p^{2}-\Lambda^{2}_{X}|^{2}},

(13)

therefore,

\displaystyle g_{1}(r^{2})

\displaystyle=-g_{1}(1-y)\frac{\bm{r}_{T}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(m_{\pi}^{2})}{[\bm{r}_{T}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})]^{2}},

(14)

\displaystyle g_{2}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=-g_{2}(1-\frac{x}{y})\frac{(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})^{2}+L_{2}^{2}(m^{2})}{[(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})^{2}+L_{2}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\bar{q}})]^{2}}.

(15)

Here,
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are the free parameters which represent the strength of the nucleon-pion-baryon vertex and pion-quark-antiquark vertex, respectively; and
\Lambda_{\pi}
and
\Lambda_{\bar{q}}
are the cutoff parameters.

III
Sea quark Sivers function in the overlap representation

The unpolarized distribution of sea quarks can be calculated from the overlap representation of the proton wave function derived in the previous section
Brodsky et al. 2001a
:

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(16\pi^{3}\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\{\lambda\}}|\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(16\pi^{3}\right)^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda_{N},\lambda_{B}}|\psi^{\lambda_{N}}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,\bm{r}_{T})|^{2}\sum_{{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}|\psi_{{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})|^{2}

\displaystyle=f_{1}^{\pi/p}(y,\bm{r}_{T})f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}\left(\frac{x}{y},\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T}\right),

(16)

where
\{\lambda\}={\lambda_{N},\lambda_{B},\lambda_{q},\lambda_{\bar{q}}}
. In the above equation,
f_{1}^{\pi/p}
denotes the distribution of the pion inside the proton:

\displaystyle f_{1}^{\pi/P}(y,\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{3}}(|\psi^{+}_{+}|^{2}+|\psi^{+}_{-}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{1}^{2}}{16\pi^{3}}\frac{y(1-y)^{2}[\bm{r}^{2}_{T}+(M_{B}-M(1-y))^{2}]}{[\bm{r}_{T}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})]^{4}}

(17)

while
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}(\frac{x}{y},\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})
is the sea quark unpolarized TMD distribution in a pion with the momentum fraction
x/y
and the intrinsic transverse momentum
\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T}
:

\displaystyle f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}(\frac{x}{y},\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{3}}(|\psi{{}_{+}}{{}_{+}}|^{2}+|\psi{{}_{+}}{{}_{-}}|^{2}+|\psi{{}_{-}}{{}_{+}}|^{2}+|\psi{{}_{-}}{{}_{-}}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}_{2}}{8\pi^{3}}\frac{(1-\frac{x}{y})^{2}[m^{2}+(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})^{2}]}{[(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})^{2}+L^{2}_{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\bar{q}})]^{4}}.

(18)

After integrating out the longitudinal momentum fraction
y
and the transverse momentum
\bm{r}_{T}
, we can obtain the unpolarized distribution of the sea quarks inside the proton

\displaystyle f_{1}^{\bar{q}/p}(x,\bm{k}_{T})

\displaystyle=\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}\int{d^{2}\bm{r}_{T}}f_{1}^{\pi/p}(y,\bm{r}_{T})f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}(\frac{x}{y},\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T}).

(19)

Thus, having the LCWFs of the proton in terms of
|\bar{q}qB\rangle
, the unpolarized sea quark distribution in the proton can be expressed as the convolution of the
\pi
distribution in the proton
f_{1}^{\pi/p}(y,\bm{r}_{T})
and the antiquark distribution in the pion
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}(\frac{x}{y},\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})
.

Using the overlap representation of the LCWFs, the sea quarks Sivers function can be calculated from the following expression
Lu and Schmidt 2007
;
Bacchetta et al. 2008a

\displaystyle\frac{2(\widehat{\bm{s}}_{T}\times\bm{k}_{T})\cdot\widehat{\bm{P}}}{M}f_{1T}^{\bot}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T}^{2},\bm{r}_{T}^{2})=\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{r}^{\prime}_{T}}{16\pi^{3}}G(x,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{k}^{\prime}_{T})

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\lambda\}}[\psi^{\uparrow*}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})\psi^{\uparrow}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}^{\prime}_{T})

\displaystyle-\psi^{\downarrow*}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}_{T})\psi^{\downarrow}_{{\lambda_{B}}{\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{\bar{q}}}}(x,y,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{r}^{\prime}_{T})]

(20)

Here,
\uparrow
and
\downarrow
denote the transverse polarization states of proton. The function
G(x,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{k}^{\prime}_{T})
is the factor simulating the final state interaction (in DIS) between the active antiquark and the spectator
Lu and Schmidt 2007
;
Bacchetta et al. 2008a
,
In this work we adopt the following form

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\,G(x,\bm{k}_{T},\bm{k}^{\prime}_{T})=-\frac{C_{F}\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(\bm{k}_{T}-\bm{k}^{\prime}_{T})^{2}},

(21)

corresponding to the one-gluon exchange approximation of the gauge-link.

Substituting the light-cone wave functions of the proton into Eq.(
20
), we find that the sea quark sivers function in our model has the form:

\displaystyle f_{1T}^{\bot,\bar{q}/p}(x,\bm{k}^{2}_{T})

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}\int{d^{2}\bm{r}_{T}}\int{d^{2}\bm{l}_{T}}\frac{g_{1}^{2}C_{F}\alpha_{s}}{16\pi^{4}}\frac{\bm{l}_{T}\cdot\bm{k}_{T}}{\bm{k}_{T}^{2}}\frac{My(1-y)^{2}[M_{B}-M(1-y)]}{{L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})}[\bm{r}_{T}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})]^{2}[(\bm{r}_{T}-\bm{l}_{T})^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})]^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{g^{2}_{2}}{8\pi^{3}}\frac{(1-\frac{x}{y})^{2}[m^{2}+(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})^{2}]}{[(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T})^{2}+L^{2}_{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\bar{q}})]^{2}[(\bm{k}_{T}-\frac{x}{y}\bm{r}_{T}-(1-\frac{x}{y})\bm{l}_{T})^{2}+L^{2}_{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\bar{q}})]^{2}}.

(22)

with
\bm{l}_{T}=\bm{k}_{T}-\bm{k}_{T}^{\prime}=\bm{r}_{T}-\bm{r}_{T}^{\prime}
.
The integration over
y
,
\bm{r}_{T}
and
\bm{l}_{T}

In the above calculations we have adopted the dipolar form factor for the pion-quark-antiquark coupling in

\displaystyle g_{2}(k^{\prime^{2}})

\displaystyle\frac{2(\widehat{\bm{s}}_{T}\times\bm{r}_{T})\cdot\widehat{\bm{P}}}{M}f_{1T}^{\bot,\pi/P}(y,\bm{r}^{2}_{T})=\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{r}^{\prime}_{T}}{16\pi^{3}}G(y,\bm{r}_{T},\bm{r}^{\prime}_{T})

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{B}}[\psi^{\uparrow*}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,\bm{r}_{T})\psi^{\uparrow}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,\bm{r}^{\prime}_{T})-\psi^{\downarrow*}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,\bm{r}_{T})\psi^{\downarrow}_{\lambda_{B}}(y,\bm{r}^{\prime}_{T})].

\displaystyle f_{1T}^{\bot,\pi/p}(y,\bm{r}^{2}_{T})=-\frac{g_{1}^{2}C_{F}\alpha_{s}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{My(1-y)^{2}[M_{B}-M(1-y)]}{[\bm{r}_{T}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})]^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{g_{1}^{2}C_{F}\alpha_{s}}{16\pi^{3}}\frac{My(1-y)^{2}[M_{B}-M(1-y)]}{{L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})}[\bm{r}_{T}^{2}+L_{1}^{2}(\Lambda^{2}_{\pi})]^{3}}.

V
CONCLUSION

In this work, we studied the sea quark (
\bar{u}
and
\bar{d}
) Sivers function of the proton using a light-cone model.
In the model the proton can fluctuate to the Fock state formed by a pion meson and a baryon
B
, where the pion meson is composed by the
q\bar{q}
pair.
We derive the light-cone wavefunction of this composite system in terms of
q\bar{q}B
.
Using the overlap representation of LCWFs, we calculated the unpolarized sea quark distribution function
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/p}(x,\bm{k}_{T})
and the sea quark Sivers function
f_{1T}^{\bot,\bar{q}/p}(x,\bm{k}^{2}_{T})
.
In the calculation, we adopt the dipole form factors for both the nucleon-meson-baryon vertex and the meson-quark-antiquark vertex.
The values of the parameters in the model are fixed by fitting the model result
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}(x)
and
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/p}(x)
to GRV LO parametrization and MSTW2008 parametrization, respectively.
The calculation of the sea quark Sivers functions can be simplified by assuming the transverse momentum
\bm{l}_{T}
does not go through the pion-quark-antiquark vertex, which means that the final-state interaction occurs between the pion meson as a whole and the spectator.
In this scenario the sea quark Sivers function can be expressed as the convolution of
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}

and
f_{1}^{\bar{q}/\pi}
.
The numerical results show that the sign of the Sivers function of the
\bar{u}
and
\bar{d}
are negative in the entire
x
and
k_{T}
region.
For the first transverse moment of the sea quark Sivers function, the The size is 0.004 at most.
We also compared our model results with the recent extraction of
xf_{1T}^{\bot(1),\bar{q}/p}(x)
and find some similarities and differences between them which need to be further classified by future theoretical studies and experimental measurement.
As an extension, our model can be used to estimate the other sea quark TMD distributions in leading-twist.
In conclusion, our study provide useful information of the sea quark Sivers function in a proton. Further study is needed in order to provide more stringent constraints on the sea quark Sivers functions.

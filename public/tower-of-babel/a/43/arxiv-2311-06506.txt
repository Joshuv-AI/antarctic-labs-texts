From Toda hierarchy to KP hierarchy
Di Yang, Jian Zhou

Abstract
. Using the matrix-resolvent method and a formula of the second-named author on the n n -point function for a KP tau-function, we show that the tau-function of an arbitrary solution to the Toda lattice hierarchy is a KP tau-function. We then generalize this result to tau-functions for the extended Toda hierarchy (ETH) by developing the matrix-resolvent method for the ETH. As an example the partition function of Gromov–Witten invariants of the complex projective line is a KP tau-function, and an application on irreducible representations of the symmetric group is obtained.



1.
Introduction

The Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy and
the Toda lattice hierarchy are two important integrable hierarchies. In this
paper, we will show that the tau-function of an arbitrary solution to the Toda lattice hierarchy
gives an infinite family of tau-functions of the KP hierarchy.
This is achieved by combining two different results:
a formula
[
55
]
of the second-named author on the
n
-point function for a KP tau-function in the big cell,
and a work
[
50
]
of the first-named author on the
n
-point function for a Toda tau-function.

The KP hierarchy is an infinite family of equations with infinitely many unknown functions,
which can be written using the Lax pair formalism as follows:

(1)

\frac{\partial L_{\rm KP}}{\partial p_{k}}=\left[\left(L^{k}\right)_{+},L\right],\quad k\geq 1,

where

(2)

L_{\rm KP}=\partial+\sum_{j\geq 1}u_{j}\partial^{-j},\quad\partial=\partial/\partial X,

is a pseudodifferential operator, called the
Lax operator
.
For details about pseudodifferential operators and their operations see for example
[
15
]
.
The independent variables
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},\dots
are called
times
.
Since
\partial L_{\rm KP}/\partial p_{1}=\partial L/\partial X
, we identify
p_{1}
with
X
.

Let
{\bf p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)
denote the infinite vector of KP times.
It is well known (see e.g.
[
15
]
) that an arbitrary solution
(u_{1}({\bf p}),u_{2}({\bf p}),\dots)

to the KP hierarchy (
1
) can be compactly represented by a single function
\tau=\tau({\bf p})

called the
tau-function
as

(3)

u_{1}({\bf p})=\frac{\partial^{2}\log\tau}{\partial p_{1}^{2}},\quad u_{2}({\bf p})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\log\tau}{\partial p_{1}\partial p_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{3}\log\tau}{\partial p_{1}^{3}},\quad\dots,

and the tau-function
\tau
satisfies the
Hirota bilinear identities
given by

(4)

\res_{\lambda=\infty}\tau({\bf p}-[\lambda^{-1}])\tau({\bf p^{\prime}}+[\lambda^{-1}])e^{\sum_{k\geq 1}(p_{k}-p_{k}^{\prime})\lambda^{k}}d\lambda=0.

Here
[\lambda^{-1}]:=(\frac{1}{\lambda},\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}},\frac{1}{3\lambda^{3}},\dots)
.
Equivalence between (
4
) and (
1
) is a standard result in the theory of integrable systems, see for example
[
15
]
.
Equation (
4
) itself gives the defining equations for a KP tau-function (namely, we do not
need to start with a solution
(u_{1}({\bf p}),u_{2}({\bf p}),\dots)
to the KP hierarchy (
1
)).

Let
\Lambda:f(x)\mapsto f(x+1)
be the shift operator and

(5)

L:=\Lambda+v(x)+w(x)\Lambda^{-1}

the
Lax operator
.
The
Toda lattice hierarchy
(also known as the 1d Toda chain or 1-Toda hierarchy)
can be defined using the Lax pair formalism as

(6)

\frac{\partial L}{\partial m_{i}}=\frac{1}{(i+1)!}\left[(L^{i+1})_{+},L\right],\quad i\geq 0.

Here, for a difference operator
P
written in the form
P=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{k}\Lambda^{k}
,

P_{+}
is defined as
\sum_{k\geq 0}P_{k}\Lambda^{k}
. We also denote
{\bf m}=(m_{0},m_{1},m_{2},\dots)
.

Let
(v(x,{\bf m}),w(x,{\bf m}))
be an arbitrary power-series-in-
{\bf m}
solution
to the Toda lattice hierarchy, and
\tau(x,{\bf m})
the tau-function
[
21
]
of this solution.
Our first result of this paper is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

The tau-function
\tau(x,{\bf m})
for the Toda lattice hierarchy
is a tau-function of the KP hierarchy for any
x
,
where
{\bf m}
and the KP times
{\bf p}
are related by

(7)

m_{i}=i!\,p_{i+1},\quad i\geq 0.

Let us now recall some earlier results in the literature that motivate us to obtain this result.
It is well known in matrix model theory
[
1
,
31
]

that using the theory of orthogonal polynomials,
the GUE partition function
\{Z_{N}({\bf s};\epsilon)\}_{N\geq 1}
is a tau-function for the truncated Toda lattice hierarchy,
in the sense that the variable
x
in (
5
) is restricted to positive integers
N
.
Here
N
is the size of the matrices of the Hermitian matrices used to defined the partition function.
A less well-known result is that for each
N
,
Z_{N}({\bf s};\epsilon)
is also a tau-function of the KP hierarchy
[
47
]
.
Both of these results have been revisited recently and some new perspectives naturally arise.

First of all,
in
[
21
, Deﬁnition 1.2.2]
,
a notion of matrix resolvent for the Toda lattice hierarchy was introduced.
Based on this,
a definition of the tau function of Dubrovin-Zhang type of the Toda lattice tau-function was given in
[
21
, Deﬁnition 1.2.4]
.
Usually a normalization constant is chosen so that the partition function of Gaussian unitary ensemble (GUE) is equal to
1

when all the coupling constant is set to be equal to zero.
In
[
21
, Appendix A]
it was shown that after multiplying by a suitable correction factor,
the GUE partition functions give us a tau-function of Dubrovin-Zhang type.
In
[
21
]
it was also shown that the correction factor can be obtained by using the theory of
Toda tau-functions, so this method is applicable to other examples.

Note the major goal of
[
21
]
is to develop a method
for computing
n
-point function associated with the tau-function
of the Toda lattice hierarchy based on the matrix-resolvent method,
by generalizing the earlier results developed by Bertola, Dubrovin and the first-named
author in the cases of KdV hierarchy
[
7
]
and Drinfeld-Sokolov hierarchies associated
with simple Lie algebras
[
8
]
.
Earlier the second-named author proved an explicit formula
[
54
]
for the Schur expansion
of the Witten-Kontsevich tau-function of the KdV hierarchy.
Balogh and the first-named author interpreted this formula in terms of the
affine coordinates of the Witten-Kontsevich tau-function in
[
5
]
.
Inspired by
[
7
]
,
the second-named author proved a formula
[
55
]

of
n
-point function associated with
an arbitrary tau-function of the KP hierarchy,
based on the affine coordinates of the element in the Sato Grassmannian
corresponding to the tau-function.
The formula in
[
21
]
have a difference from the formula in
[
55
]
:
In the former matrices of power series are used whereas ordinary power series are used in the latter.
To remedy the difference, the first-named author proved a formula for
n
-point function
using only power series in
[
50
]
for tau-functions of Toda lattice hierarchy (see also
[
23
]
).

Secondly,
it was shown in
[
56
]
that the normalized GUE partition function

\frac{Z_{N}(s;\epsilon)}{Z_{N}({\bf 0};\epsilon)}

gives rise to a family of KP tau-functions in the big cell parameterized by the
t’Hooft coupling constant
x=N\epsilon
(see also
[
35
,
43
]
).
Furthermore,
an explicit formula for the affine coordinates for this family of KP tau-function was derived in
[
56
]
.
As an application,
the formulas for the corresponding
n
-point functions were obtained by applying the formula in
[
55
]
.

Now the GUE partition functions can be studied from two different perspectives: either as a tau-function of Dubrovin-Zhang type of the Toda lattice hierarchy,
or as a family of tau-functions of the KP hierarchy.
The belief that this is just a special case of general phenomenon leads us to Theorem
1.1
.

There are earlier results that also lead to Theorem
1.1
.
It is well known that by introducing the t’Hooft coupling constant
x=N\epsilon
,

\log\frac{Z_{N}({\bf s};\epsilon)}{Z_{N}({\bf 0};\epsilon)}
has an expansion as weighted sum
of ribbon graphs
[
21
,
34
,
56
]
.
In other words, the enumeration of ribbon graphs gives rise to a special family of tau-functions
of the KP hierarchy which become a special tau-function for the Toda lattice hierarchy
by multiplying by a suitable correction factor; furthermore,
GUE is a matrix model for enumerating the ribbon graphs.
Recall that ribbon graphs can be regarded as
clean
dessins.
As a generalization,
the weighted sum of Grothendieck’s dessins d’enfants has
been shown by Zograf
[
59
]
(see also Kazarian and Zograf
[
36
]
)
to be a tau-function of the KP hierarchy, that is
referred to as the dessin partition function.
Based on this fact, the second-named author had found the affine coordinates
of the tau-function of the KP hierarchy associated with the dessin counting
[
57
]
,
and so explicit formula for the
n
-point functions of the dessin partition function
can be written down using the general formula in
[
55
]
.
The dessin partition function is a family of KP tau-functions parameterized by two parameters.
It reduces to some other well-known tau-function by suitably specifying these parameters
[
57
]
.
A proposal to study the dualities among different models based on the theory of KP hierarchy
was then proposed in
[
57
]
and was further elaborated in
[
58
]
.

The study of KP tau-functions and the study of Toda tau-functions were merged
in our earlier work
[
52
]
in our study of the dessin tau-function and its role in unifying various theories.
Since counting dessins are similar to counting ribbon graphs and
GUE provides a matrix model for enumerating the ribbon graphs,
one naturally expects a matrix model for enumerating dessins.
The problem was addressed by Ambjørn and Chekhov
[
4
]
, where
they proposed a matrix model in the class of generalized Kontsevich models
and an equivalent Hermitian one matrix model.
In
[
52
]
, we found that the Laguerre unitary ensemble (LUE)
gives a simpler and rigorous matrix model for the dessins
counting in the sense that the dessin partition function is equal to the
normalized

LUE partition function.
Furthermore, by multiplying by a suitable correction factor,
the dessin partition function gives rise to a Dubrovin–Zhang type tau-function of the Toda lattice hierarchy.
It was proposed in
[
52
]
to study the
duality between dessin partition function with partition functions of other theories from the
viewpoint of Toda lattice hierarchy.
In this new approach, one can apply the theory of normal forms of integrable hierarchies
and the extended Toda hierarchy
[
11
]
as developed by Dubrovin and Zhang
[
25
,
26
]
.
In fact,
the relevant Frobenius manifold is the Frobenius manifold associated with the Gromov–Witten invariants
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
It has already been used in
[
19
]
(cf.
[
21
,
51
]
) to calculate the GUE correlators.

Based on some earlier results of Dijkgraaf and Witten
[
16
]
,
Eguchi and Yang
[
27
]
proposed a matrix model for the Gromov-Witten invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(see also
[
28
]
).
Besides the Toda lattice hierarchy,
the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-partition function satisfies an extra family of flows introduced in
[
27
,
32
,
53
]
(see also
[
10
,
11
,
17
,
27
,
42
]
).
These flows are called
extended flows
by Getzler
[
32
]
and Zhang
[
53
]
.
They can be defined using the Lax pair formalism
[
11
]
as follows:

(8)

\frac{\partial L}{\partial b_{i}}=\frac{2}{i!}\left[\left(L^{i}(\log L-c_{i})\right)_{+},L\right],\quad i\geq 0,

where
c_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\frac{1}{j}
are harmonic numbers and for the definition of
\log L
see
[
11
,
42
]
.
The flows (
8

6
)
of the Toda lattice hierarchy, and they also pairwise commute.
All-together, (
6
), (
8
) form the
extended Toda hierarchy
(ETH)
[
11
,
32
,
53
]
.

Let
(v({\bf b}+x{\bf 1},{\bf m}),w({\bf b}+x{\bf 1},{\bf m}))
be an arbitrary solution
to the ETH, and
\tau({\bf b}+x{\bf 1},{\bf m})
the tau-function
[
11
]
of this solution. Here
{\bf b}=(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\dots)
and
{\bf 1}=(1,0,0,\dots)
.
The following theorem
generalizes Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The tau-function
\tau({\bf b}+x{\bf 1},{\bf m})
for the ETH
is a KP tau-function for any
x
and
{\bf b}
, where
{\bf m}
and
{\bf p}
are related by (
7
).

The proof is in Section

.

It was conjectured in
[

,
18
,
27
,
32
]
, and was proved
by Dubrovin and Zhang
[

]
that
the partition function

of
Gromov–Witten (GW) invariants of

(see Section

of a particular solution to the ETH. (Independently, Okounkov and Pandharipande

proved that

Based on the result of Dubrovin and Zhang and on Theorem

Corollary 1.3
.

The partition function

,

.

The stationary sector of the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-partition function is

We compute in Section

its affine coordinates by two different methods: One by the results of

[

The former gives us an explicit closed formula,

We then get a closed formula for some Plancherel averages in Corallary

2.
The formula for the
n
-point function for a KP tau-function

In this section we first review the explicit formula (see Theorem A below) obtained by the second-named author
for the
n
-point function for an arbitrary KP tau-function in the big cell, and then consider the converse statement that
gives a criterion for a KP tau-function.

Before entering into the details let us introduce some
notations
.
Denote by
\mathcal{R}
a suitable ground ring.
For a formal power series
F({\bf p})\in\mathcal{R}[[{\bf p}]]
,
define the
n
-point function associated with
F({\bf p})
by

(9)

G_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{k_{i}}{\xi_{i}^{k_{i}+1}}\cdot\frac{\partial^{n}F({\bf p})}{\partial p_{k_{1}}\dots\partial p_{k_{n}}}\biggr|_{{\bf p}={\bf 0}},\quad n\geq 0.

By a
partition

\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots)
, we mean a sequence of
weakly decreasing non-negative integers with
\mu_{k}=0

for sufficiently large
k
.
The length
\ell(\mu)
is the number of the non-zero parts of
\mu
and the weight
|\mu|:=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\dots
.
The set of all partitions will be denoted by
\mathcal{P}
, and the set of partitions of weight
d
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{d}
.
The
Schur polynomial
s_{\mu}({\bf p})
associated to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}
is a polynomial in the variables

{\bf p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\dots)
,
defined by

(10)

s_{\mu}({\bf p}):=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell(\lambda)}(h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}({\bf p})),

where
h_{j}({\bf p})
are polynomials defined by the generating function

(11)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h_{j}({\bf p})z^{j}:=e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{k}z^{k}}.

One constructive way to describe tau-functions of the KP hierarchy is
to use Sato’s Grassmannian. Let

z^{1/2}\mathcal{R}[[z,z^{-1}]]
be the space of formal series in
z
of half integral powers.
An element
V
in the big cell
Gr_{0}
of Sato’s Grassmannian is specified by a sequence of series

(12)

\Psi_{j}(z)=z^{j+1/2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}A_{i,j}z^{-i-1/2},\quad j\geq 0,

where the coefficients
A_{i,j}
are called the
affine coordinates
of
V
. To any element
V\in Gr_{0}
, there corresponds
a particular KP tau-function
Z_{V}
defined by

(13)

Z_{V}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}c_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}({\bf p}),

where, for a partition
\lambda
written in terms of the Frobenius notation
[
38
]

\lambda=(m_{1},\dots,m_{k}|n_{1},\dots,n_{k})
,

(14)

c_{\lambda}:=(-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{k}}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}(A_{m_{i},n_{j}}).

The following theorem was obtained in
[
55
]
.

Theorem A
(
[
55
]
).
The
n
-point function associated with
\log Z_{V}
is given by the following formula:
For
n=1
,

(15)

G_{1}(\xi)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}A_{i,j}\xi^{-i-j-2},

and for
n\geq 2
,

(16)

G_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\text{$n$-cycles}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}B(\xi_{\sigma(i)},\xi_{\sigma(i+1)})-\frac{\delta_{n,2}}{(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{2}},

where

(17)

B(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})=\begin{cases}i_{\xi_{i},\xi_{j}}\frac{1}{\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}}+A(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}),&i<j,\\
A(\xi_{i},\xi_{i}),&i=j,\\
i_{\xi_{j},\xi_{i}}\frac{1}{\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}}+A(\xi_{i},\xi_{j}),&i>j,\end{cases}

and

(18)

\begin{split}A(\xi,\eta)=&\sum_{i,j\geq 0}A_{i,j}\xi^{-j-1}\eta^{-i-1}.\end{split}

Here we have used the following notation:

(19)

\iota_{x,y}\frac{1}{(x-y)^{n}}=-\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{-n}{k}x^{-n-k}y^{k}.

Theorem A enables one to easily compute the
n
-point function once
we have found the affine coordinates
A_{i,j}
.
For example, there is only one
2
-cycle,
so the two-point function is given by

\displaystyle G_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})-A(\xi_{2},\xi_{1})}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}-A(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\cdot A(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}).

Let us consider the converse of the above Theorem A. Recalling the simple fact that
multiplying a KP tau-function
\tau({\bf p})

by the exponential of an arbitrary affine linear function of
{\bf p}

(20)

\tau({\bf p})\cdot e^{C_{0}+\sum_{k\geq 1}C_{k}p_{k}},\quad a_{k}\in\mathcal{R}~(k\geq 0),

produces again a KP-tau function (which can be verified directly using (
4
)), we immediately obtain from Theorem A the following

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
F
be a formal power series in
{\bf p}
and
G_{n}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})
its
n
-point function.
If there are
(A_{i,j})_{i,j\geq 0}
such that for all
n\geq 2

G_{n}
are given by (
16
),
then
Z=e^{F}
is a tau-function of the KP hierarchy.

We note that Corollary
2.1
is also given in
[
3
]
.

The next proposition describes how the affine coordinates change under (
20
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
V
,
V^{\prime}
be two elements in the big cell having affine coordinates
A_{i,j}
,
A^{\prime}_{i,j}
, respectively,
and let
Z_{V}
,
Z_{V^{\prime}}
be the KP tau-functions associated to
V
,
V^{\prime}
. If there exist
C_{1},C_{2},\dots
such that

(21)

Z_{V^{\prime}}=e^{\sum_{k\geq 1}C_{k}p_{k}}Z_{V},

then we have the following identity:

(22)

\sum_{i,j\geq 0}A^{\prime}_{i,j}\xi^{-j-1}\eta^{-i-1}+\frac{1}{\xi-\eta}=\left(\sum_{i,j\geq 0}A_{i,j}\xi^{-j-1}\eta^{-i-1}+\frac{1}{\xi-\eta}\right)\frac{e^{-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}C_{\ell}\xi^{-\ell}}}{e^{-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}C_{\ell}\eta^{-\ell}}}.

Proof.

By direct verifications using (
15
).
∎

3.
From the Toda lattice hierarchy to the KP hierarchy

In this section, we show that an arbitrary tau-function for the Toda lattice hierarchy is a KP tau-function.

Let
(v(x,{\bf m}),w(x,{\bf m}))
be an arbitrary power-series-in-
{\bf m}
solution
to the Toda lattice hierarchy (
6
), and
\tau(x,{\bf m})
the tau-function
[
11
,
26
,
21
]
of this solution.
The solution
(v(x,{\bf m}),w(x,{\bf m}))
is in one-to-one correspondence
with its initial value

(23)

v(x,{\bf 0})=:f(x),\quad w(x,{\bf 0})=:g(x).

Denote by
L_{\rm ini}
the initial Lax operator:

(24)

L_{\rm ini}=\Lambda+f(x)+g(x)\Lambda^{-1},

and denote

(25)

s(x)=-(1-\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}(\log g(x)).

Recall from
[
50
]
that two elements

(26)

\psi_{1}(\lambda,x)=(1+{\rm O}(\lambda^{-1}))\lambda^{x},\quad\psi_{2}(\lambda,x)=(1+{\rm O}(\lambda^{-1}))e^{-s(x)}\lambda^{-x}

are called forming
a pair of wave functions
of
L_{\rm ini}
if

(27)

\displaystyle L_{\rm ini}(\psi_{1})=\lambda\psi_{1},\quad L_{\rm ini}(\psi_{2})=\lambda\psi_{2},

(28)

\displaystyle d(\lambda,x):=\psi_{1}\,\Lambda^{-1}(\psi_{2})-\psi_{2}\,\Lambda^{-1}(\psi_{1})=\lambda e^{-s(x-1)}.

The following formula is proved in
[
50
]
:

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\geq 0}\frac{(i_{1}+1)!\cdots(i_{n}+1)!}{\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}+2}\dots\lambda_{n}^{i_{n}+2}}\,\frac{\partial^{n}\log\tau(x,{\bf m})}{\partial m_{i_{1}}\dots\partial m_{i_{n}}}\bigg|_{{\bf m}={\bf 0}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}\frac{e^{ns(x-1)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}}\sum_{\text{$n$-cycles}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}D(\lambda_{\sigma(j)},\lambda_{\sigma(j+1)};x)-\frac{\delta_{n,2}}{(\lambda-\mu)^{2}},

where
n\geq 2
, and

(30)

D(\lambda,\mu;x):=\frac{\psi_{1}(\lambda,x)\psi_{2}(\mu,x-1)-\psi_{1}(\lambda,x-1)\psi_{2}(\mu,x)}{\lambda-\mu}.

Remark 3.1
.

The function
D(\lambda,\mu;x)
looks very similar to the
Christoffel–Darboux kernel in matrix models
[
14
,
41
]
.
We hope to study their relations in a future work.

In
[
50
]
the following expansion is also observed:

(31)

\frac{e^{s(x-1)}}{\mu}D(\lambda,\mu;x)\frac{\lambda^{x}}{\mu^{x}}=\frac{1}{\lambda-\mu}+\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{A_{i,j}(x)}{\lambda^{j+1}\mu^{i+1}}=:B(\lambda,\mu;x).

We note that the expansion given in
[
50

5.2.
Affine coordinates from the Okounkov–Pandharipande formula

We proceed with recalling the Okounkov–Pandharipande formula

To consider the GW invariants of

one has to consider all the moduli spaces

The expected dimension of

When

and
(g,d)=(1,0)

The first case corresponds to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},1)

and the second case is impossible.

With the contribution of zero point correlators,
the free energy of the stationary GW invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
then

(113)

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}({\bf m})=q+\mathcal{F}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}({\bf m}).

Let

\tilde{Z}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}({\bf m})=e^{q}\cdot Z^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}({\bf m}).

As a corollary to Corollary
1.3

The corrected partition function
\tilde{Z}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}({\bf m})
is a KP tau-function.

The corrected partition function

has the following

(115)

\tilde{Z}^{\mathbb{P}^{1}}({\bf m})=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\geq 0}\frac{m_{i_{1}}\cdots m_{i_{n}}}{n!}\biggl\langle{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{i_{j}}(2)}\biggr\rangle^{\bullet\mathbb{P}^{1}}_{d},

\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree

By using the GW/H correspondence

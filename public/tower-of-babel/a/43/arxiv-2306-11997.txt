Cyclic relative difference families with block size four and their applications
Chenya Zhao, Binwei Zhao, Yanxun Chang, Tao Feng, Xiaomiao Wang, Menglong Zhang

Abstract
Given a subgroup H H of a group ( G , + ) (G,+) , a ( G , H , k , 1 ) (G,H,k,1) difference family (DF) is a set ℱ \mathcal{F} of k k -subsets of G G such that { f − f ′ : f , f ′ ∈ F , f ≠ f ′ , F ∈ ℱ } = G ∖ H \{f-f^{\prime}:f,f^{\prime}\in F,f\neq f^{\prime},F\in\mathcal{F}\}=G\setminus H . Let g ​ ℤ g ​ h g\mathbb{Z}_{gh} is the subgroup of order h h in ℤ g ​ h \mathbb{Z}_{gh} generated by g g . A ( ℤ g ​ h , g ​ ℤ g ​ h , k , 1 ) (\mathbb{Z}_{gh},g\mathbb{Z}_{gh},k,1) -DF is called cyclic and written as a ( g ​ h , h , k , 1 ) (gh,h,k,1) -CDF. This paper shows that for h ∈ { 2 , 3 , 6 } h\in\{2,3,6\} , there exists a ( g ​ h , h , 4 , 1 ) (gh,h,4,1) -CDF if and only if g ​ h ≡ h ( mod 12 ) gh\equiv h\pmod{12} , g ⩾ 4 g\geqslant 4 and ( g , h ) ∉ { ( 9 , 3 ) , ( 5 , 6 ) } (g,h)\not\in\{(9,3),(5,6)\} . As a corollary, it is shown that a 1-rotational S ( 2 , 4 , v ) (2,4,v) exists if and only if v ≡ 4 ( mod 12 ) v\equiv 4\pmod{12} and v ≠ 28 v\neq 28 . This solves the long-standing open problem on the existence of a 1-rotational S ( 2 , 4 , v ) (2,4,v) . As another corollary, we establish the existence of an optimal ( v , 4 , 1 ) (v,4,1) -optical orthogonal code with ⌊ ( v − 1 ) / 12 ⌋ \lfloor(v-1)/12\rfloor codewords for any positive integer v ≡ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 6 ( mod 12 ) v\equiv 1,2,3,4,6\pmod{12} and v ≠ 25 v\neq 25 . We also give applications of our results to cyclic group divisible designs with block size four and optimal cyclic 3 3 -ary constant-weight codes with weight four and minimum distance six.



Keywords
: difference family; cyclic group divisible design; 1-rotational Steiner system; cyclic constant-weight code; optical orthogonal code

Introduction

Throughout this paper, every union of sets will be understood as multiset union with multiplicities of elements preserved. Denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
the cyclic group of order
v
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of a finite group
(G,+)
and let
k
be an integer satisfying
2\leqslant k\leqslant|G|
. A
(G,H,k,1)
-
relative difference family
(briefly DF) is a set
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-subsets of
G
(called
base blocks
) such that the list

\Delta\mathcal{F}:=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\Delta F:=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\{f-f^{\prime}:f,f^{\prime}\in F,f\neq f^{\prime}\}

covers each element of
G\setminus H
exactly once and each element of
H
zero time. There are
(|G|-|H|)/(k(k-1))
base blocks in a
(G,H,k,1)
-DF and hence, a necessary condition for the existence of such a family is
|G|\equiv|H|\pmod{k(k-1)}
. Another obvious necessary condition is
|G|\geqslant k|H|
; otherwise, given any base block
F\in\mathcal{F}
, there exist two elements of
F
that lie in a same right coset of
H
in
G
, a contradiction.

Lemma 1.1
.

If there exists a
(G,H,k,1)
-DF, then
|G|\equiv|H|\pmod{k(k-1)}
and
|G|\geqslant k|H|
.

When
H
consists only of the identity
1_{G}
of
G
, a
(G,1_{G},k,1)
-DF is often written as a
(G,k,1)
-DF. When
G
is a cyclic group, a
(G,H,k,1)
-DF is said to be
cyclic
and written as a
(|G|,|H|,k,1)
-CDF.

Difference families arose from the study of designs with a given group of automorphisms. We
here only briefly recall what has been done for cyclic difference families with base block size three or four. The reader is referred to
[
2
,
3
]
for more topics on difference families.

As far back as 1939, Peltesohn
[
32
]
showed that for
h\in\{1,3\}
, a
(gh,h,3,1)
-CDF exists if and only if

and
(g,h)\neq(3,3)

[

[

[

When

]

[

]

-CDFs such that

[
38

recently developed an efficient direct construction method to establish the existence of

-CDFs for

.

[

For

-CDF if and only if

3.1
Cyclic group divisible designs

A
group divisible design
(GDD) is a triple
(V,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{B})
, where
V
is a set of
points
,
\mathcal{G}
is a partition of
V
into
groups
, and
\mathcal{B}
is a family of subsets of
V
(called
blocks
) such that every block intersects any given group in at most one point, and every two points belonging to distinct groups are contained in exactly one block. A
k
-GDD of
type

h^{g}
is a GDD in which the size of each block is
k
and there are
g
groups of size
h
. For more details on group divisible designs, the reader is referred to
[
4
,
22
]
.

An
automorphism
of a GDD (
V,{\cal G},{\cal B}
) is a permutation on
V
leaving
{\cal G}
and
{\cal B}
invariant, respectively. A
k
-GDD of type
h^{g}
is said to be

if it admits an automorphism consisting of a cycle of length
gh
. Without loss of generality, we regard
\mathbb{Z}_{gh}
as an automorphism group of a cyclic
k
-GDD of type
h^{g}
. If the stabilizer of any block of a cyclic GDD of type
h^{g}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{gh}
is trivial, then the GDD is called

. The blocks of a strictly cyclic

-GDD of type

can be partitioned into orbits under

. Choose any fixed block from each orbit and then call them base blocks. These base blocks form a
(gh,h,k,1)
-CDF. Therefore, a strictly cyclic
k
-GDD of type
h^{g}
yields a
(gh,h,k,1)
-CDF, and vice versa. Combining Theorem
2.1
, we have the following theorem.

3.3
Optimal cyclic constant-weight codes

A
q
-ary code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
is a set of vectors in
\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}^{n}
. An element in
\mathcal{C}
is called a
codeword
. For two codewords
u=(u_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}
and
v=(v_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}
, the
Hamming distance
between
u
and
v
is
d_{H}(u,v)=|\{0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1:u_{i}-v_{i}\neq 0\}|
. The
Hamming weight
of
u
is the
Hamming distance between
u
and the zero vector 0. A code is said to be of
constant-weight

w
if the Hamming weight of each codeword is a constant
w
. A code
\mathcal{C}
is said to have
minimum distance

d
if
d_{H}(u,v)\geqslant d
for all distinct
u,v\in\mathcal{C}
. A
q
-ary code of length
n
, minimum distance
d
and constant-weight
w
is denoted by an
(n,d,w)_{q}
code.

An
(n,d,w)_{q}
code
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
cyclic
if
(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
implies
(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n-1},u_{0})\in\mathcal{C}
. We use the notation
CA_{q}(n,d,w)
to denote the largest possible number of codewords among all cyclic
(n,d,w)_{q}
codes with given parameters
n,d,w
and
q
. A cyclic
(n,d,w)_{q}
code is called
optimal
if it has
CA_{q}(n,d,w)
codewords.

Lan, Chang and Wang
[
29
]
gave an equivalent definition of a cyclic
(n,d,w)_{q}
code from a set-theoretic perspective.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cyclic
(n,d,w)_{q}
code. For each
u=(u_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}\in\mathcal{C}
, construct a
w
-subset
B_{u}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\{1,2,\ldots,q-1\}
such that
(i,u_{i})\in B_{u}
if and only if
u_{i}
is nonzero. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\{B_{u}:u\in\mathcal{C}\}
. For any codeword
B=\{(i_{1},a_{1}),(i_{2},a_{2}),\ldots,(i_{w},a_{w})\}\in\mathcal{C^{{}^{\prime}}}
, define the
projection

p(B)
of
B
to be the set
p(B)=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{w}\}
. For any
B_{u},B_{v}\in\mathcal{C^{{}^{\prime}}}
, define the distance
d_{H}(B_{u},B_{v})
to be
d_{H}(u,v)
. It is readily checked that
d_{H}(B_{u},B_{v})=2w-|p(B_{u})\cap p(B_{v})|-|B_{u}\cap B_{v}|
. Then a cyclic
(n,d,w)_{q}
code
\mathcal{C}
is a set of
w
-subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\times\{1,2,\ldots,q-1\}
satisfying that (1) for any
B\in{\cal C}
, the cardinality of
p(B)
is
w
; (2)
d_{H}(A,B)\geq d
for any distinct codewords
A,B\in{\cal C}
; (3) if
B\in{\cal C}
, then
B+1\in{\cal C}
, where
B+1=\{(x+1{\>({\rm mod}\ n)},a):(x,a)\in B\}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

3.4
Optimal optical orthogonal codes

The study of optical orthogonal codes was first motivated by an application in a fiber optic code-division multiple access channel which requires binary sequences with good correlation properties (cf.
[
18
]
).

Let
v
and
k
be positive integers. A
(v,k,1)

optical orthogonal code
(briefly
(v,k,1)
-OOC),
{\cal C}
, is a family of
(0,1)
-sequences (called
codewords
) of
length

v
and
weight

k
satisfying the following two properties (all subscripts are reduced modulo
v
):

(1)

{\sum}_{0\leq t\leq v-1}x_{t}x_{t+i}\leq 1
for any
{\bf x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{v-1})\in{\cal C}
and any integer
i\not\equiv 0\pmod{v}
(
the auto-correlation property
);

(2)

{\sum}_{0\leq t\leq v-1}x_{t}y_{t+i}\leq 1
for any
{\bf x}=(x_{0},x_{1},

\ldots,

x_{v-1})\in{\cal C}
,
{\bf y}=(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{v-1})\in{\cal C}
with
{\bf x}\neq{\bf y}
, and any integer
i
(
the cross-correlation property
).

A convenient way of viewing OOCs is from a set-theoretic perspective. A
(v,k,1)
-OOC can be defined as a set of
k
-subsets (corresponding to codewords) of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
whose list of differences does not contain repeated elements (cf.
[
37
, Theorem 2.1]
). A
(v,k,1)
-OOC is said to be
J-optimal
if the size of the set of missing differences (in
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
) of this OOC is less than or equal to
k(k-1)
, and hence a J-optimal
(v,k,1)
-OOC contains
\lfloor\frac{v-1}{k\left(k-1\right)}\rfloor
codewords.
\lfloor\frac{v-1}{k\left(k-1\right)}\rfloor
is an upper bound of the number of codewords of a
(v,k,1)
-OOC, and is often referred to as
the Johnson bound
.

Theorem 3.4
.

There exists a J-optimal
(v,4,1)
-OOC for any positive integer
v\equiv 1,2,3,4,6\pmod{12}
or
v\equiv 0\pmod{24}
with the only definite exception of
v=25
.

Proof.

Since a
(gh,h,4,1)
-CDF with
h\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
contains
\frac{gh-h}{12}=\lfloor\frac{gh-1}{12}\rfloor
base blocks, every CDF from Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
implies the existence of a J-optimal
(gh,4,1)
-OOC. Therefore, there exists a J-optimal
(v,4,1)
-OOC for any
v\equiv 1,2,3,4,6\pmod{12}
and
v\not\in\{16,18,25,27,28,30\}
. By
[
1
, Theorem 1.6]
, there exists a J-optimal
(v,4,1)
-OOC for
v\in\{16,18,27,28,30\}
and there is no J-optimal
(25,4,1)
-OOC. By
[
13
, Theorem 6.12]
, there exists a J-optimal
(v,4,1)
-OOC for any
v\equiv 0\pmod{24}
.
∎

We remark that Ge and Yin

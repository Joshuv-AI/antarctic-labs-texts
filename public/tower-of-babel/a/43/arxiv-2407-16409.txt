Cascade ( Ξ 0 \Xi^{0} ) Baryon Spectroscopy in the Relativistic Framework of Independent Quark Model Preprint: APS/123-QED
Rameshri V. Patel, Manan N. Shah

Abstract
The spectroscopy of Ξ 0 \Xi^{0} is performed within the relativistic framework of independent quark model. The equal mixture of scalar and vector components in the potential having Martin-like form is considered for the confinement. With the suitable potential parameters for Ξ 0 \Xi^{0} , mass spectra for high radial and orbital excitation is calculated. The experimentally observed values of ground state magnetic moment, branching ratios and asymmetry parameters for radiative weak decays, Ξ 0 → Λ 0 + γ 0 \Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}+\gamma^{0} & Ξ 0 → Σ 0 + γ 0 \Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Sigma^{0}+\gamma^{0} are obtained to validate the model. The spin parity of experimentally known resonances like Ξ ⁡ ( 1530 ) \Xi(1530) , Ξ ⁡ ( 1820 ) \Xi(1820) , & Ξ ⁡ ( 2030 ) \Xi(2030) are confirmed through the Regge trajectories in ( J , M 2 ) (J,M^{2}) plane. The spin pa+rity of Ξ ⁡ ( 1950 ) \Xi(1950) , Ξ ⁡ ( 2130 ) \Xi(2130) , & Ξ ⁡ ( 2250 ) \Xi(2250) are predicted using those Regge trajectories. The radiative decay width and magnetic moment of first resonance is also predicted.

We explore constituent quarks within a hadron using the relativistic framework of the independent quark model. In this model, the properties of individual quarks are governed by a Dirac equation formulated in the hadron’s rest frame. The potential in this equation showcases a Lorentz structure, featuring an even blend of scalar and vector components.

We propose that quarks within a hadron system experience independent motion within a flavor-independent central potential, characterized by a Martin-like form

V(r)=\frac{(1+\gamma_{0})}{2}(\Lambda r^{0.1}+V_{0}),

(1)

where,
\Lambda
is the potential strength and
V_{0}
is the depth of the potential. The Dirac equation for a quasi-independent quark in the center of mass frame has the form of

\left[E^{D}_{q}-\bm{\hat{\alpha}}.\bm{\hat{p}}-\hat{\beta}m_{q}-V(r)\right]\psi_{q}(\vec{r})=0,

(2)

where
E^{D}_{q}
represents the Dirac energy of a quark,
m_{q}
is the current quark mass and
\psi_{q}(\vec{r})
is the four-component quark wave-function which is a spinor. As discussed in
Greiner:2000cwh
, the solution of Eqn.(
2
) can be expressed as

\psi_{q}(\vec{r})=\begin{pmatrix}ig(r)\Omega_{jlm}\left(\frac{\bm{r}}{r}\right)\\
-f(r)\Omega_{jl^{\prime}m}\left(\frac{\bm{r}}{r}\right)\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

Here, the spinor spherical harmonics
\Omega_{jlm}
defined as given in Ref.
Greiner:2000cwh
,

\Omega_{jlm}=\sum_{m^{\prime},m_{s}}\big(l\frac{1}{2}j\rvert m^{\prime}m_{s}m\big)Y_{lm^{\prime}}\chi_{\frac{1}{2}m_{s}},

(4)

with parity
\hat{P}_{0}\Omega_{jlm}=(-1)^{l}\Omega_{jlm}
,
\chi_{\frac{1}{2}m_{s}}
being eigenfunctions of
\bm{\hat{S}}^{2}

\&

\hat{S}_{3}
and
Y_{lm}
being the spherical harmonics. The radial parts of Dirac spinors follow
second-order ordinary differential equations.

\frac{d^{2}g(r)}{dr^{2}}+\Bigg[(E^{D}_{q}+m_{q})[E^{D}_{q}-m_{q}-V(r)]\\
-\frac{k(k+1)}{r^{2}}\Bigg]g(r)=0,

(5)

\frac{d^{2}f(r)}{dr^{2}}+\bigg[(E^{D}_{q}+m_{q})[E^{D}_{q}-m_{q}-V(r)]\\
-\frac{k(k-1)}{r^{2}}\bigg]f(r)=0,

(6)

where
k
is the eigenvalue of the operator
\hat{k}=(1+\bm{\hat{L}}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}})
having the value,

k=\begin{cases}-(l+1)=-\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip 19.91684ptfor\hskip 2.84544ptj=l+\frac{1}{2}\\
\hskip 32.72049ptl=+\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hskip 19.91684ptfor\hskip 2.84544ptj=l-\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}

(7)

These ODEs can be made equivalent to ODE obeyed by the reduced radial part of the Schrödinger wave function
Barik:1982nr

\frac{d^{2}R^{Sch}(r)}{dr^{2}}+\left[m_{q}(E^{Sch}_{q}-V(r))-\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}\right]R^{Sch}(r)=0.

(8)

For the potential of Martin-like for Eqn.(
1
), we can define a dimensionless variable
\rho=\frac{r}{r_{0}}
that will reduce these Eqns. (
5
),(
6
) & (
8
) to the equivalent form,

\frac{d^{2}g(\rho)}{d\rho^{2}}+\left[\epsilon^{D}-\rho^{0.1}-\frac{k(k+1)}{\rho^{2}}\right]g(\rho)=0,

(9)

\frac{d^{2}f(\rho)}{d\rho^{2}}+\left[\epsilon^{D}-\rho^{0.1}-\frac{k(k-1)}{\rho^{2}}\right]f(\rho)=0,

(10)

\frac{d^{2}R^{Sch}(\rho)}{d\rho^{2}}+\left[\epsilon^{Sch}-\rho^{0.1}-\frac{l(l+1)}{\rho^{2}}\right]R^{Sch}(\rho)=0.

(11)

Here,

\epsilon^{D}=(E^{D}_{q}-m_{q}-V_{0})(m_{q}+E^{D}_{q})^{\frac{0.1}{2.1}}\left(\frac{2}{\Lambda}\right)^{\frac{2}{2.1}}

(12)

and

\epsilon^{Sch}=m_{q}\left(E^{Sch}_{q}-V_{0}\right)\left(m_{q}\right)^{\frac{-2}{2.1}}\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda}\right)^{\frac{2}{2.1}}.

(13)

In the
Sch.
case,
r_{0}=(m_{q}\Lambda)^{\frac{-1}{2.1}}
and
r_{0}=\left[(m_{q}+E^{D}_{q})\frac{\Lambda}{2}\right]^{\frac{-1}{2.1}}
in the Dirac case
Barik:1982nr
. The Schrödinger equation can be solved numerically using the code given in Ref.
Lucha:1998
and the Dirac energies for the individual quarks can be found by equating
\epsilon^{D}
to
\epsilon^{Sch}
. So this formalism can give the spin average masses of the three-body hadron system like a baryon (having constituent quark
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
, and
q_{3}
) as

M_{SA}^{q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}}=E^{D}_{q_{1}}+E^{D}_{q_{2}}+E^{D}_{q_{3}}-E_{CM}.

(14)

Where
E_{CM}
is the parametric center of mass correction considered to remove the effects which come from the inability of the center of mass to remain invariant. We fit the potential parameters by equating the theoretical spin average mass with the experimental spin average mass of the
S
wave, where the experimental spin average mass can be calculated as,

M_{SA}=\frac{\sum_{J}(2J+1)M_{nJ}}{\sum_{J}(2J+1)}.

(15)

which take the form of
(M_{1/2}+2M_{3/2})/3
for the
S

\displaystyle\mu_{B}=\sum_{q}<\phi_{sf}|\vec{\mu}_{qz}|\phi_{sf}>,

(22)

where

\displaystyle\mu_{q}=\frac{e_{q}}{2m_{q}}\sigma_{q}.

(23)

tHere,

and

m_{q}^{eff}

III.1
Radiative decay

Radiative decays of baryons offer enhanced insights into the intrinsic structure of baryons and their correlation with the mass of constituent quarks. The radiative decay width of light baryons, such as
\Xi^{0}
, is relatively modest when compared to heavy baryons. However, it is not insignificantly small. Given the distinctive status of the cascade as a light baryon, it is worthwhile to delve into the calculation of its radiative decay width. The expression for the electromagnetic radiative decay width can be formulated based on the radiative transition magnetic moment (in
\mu_{N}
) and photon energy (
q

=

M_{3/2}-M_{1/2}
) as
Dey:1994qi
;
Thakkar:2010ij
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{R}=\frac{q^{3}}{4\pi}\frac{2}{2J+1}\frac{e^{2}}{m_{p}^{2}}|\mu_{\frac{3}{2}^{+}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}^{+}}|^{2},

(25)

where the transition magnetic moment takes the form,

\mu_{\frac{3}{2}^{+}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}^{+}}=\sum_{i}\big<\phi_{sf}^{\frac{3}{2}^{+}}|\mu_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma_{i}}|\phi_{sf}^{\frac{1}{2}^{+}}\big>\\
=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}(\mu_{u}-\mu_{s}).

(26)

The key distinction in this transition magnetic moment lies in how we determine the magnetic moment of the quarks participating in this process. We obtain this by taking the geometric mean of their effective masses
Thakkar:2010ij
;
Dhir:2009ax
, as illustrated in the equation below.

\displaystyle m_{i}^{eff}=\sqrt{m_{i(\frac{3}{2}^{+})}^{eff}m_{i(\frac{1}{2}^{+})}^{eff}}.

(27)

Our obtained results are discussed in the last section.

III.2
Radiative Weak decay

The most dominant decay of
\Xi^{0}
is
\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Lambda\pi^{0}
, but, the radiative weak decay is the second and it is observed experimentally. The calculation of decay widths of the transitions
\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Lambda\gamma
&
\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Sigma^{0}\gamma
will be helpful in verifying our model and its parameters.
We calculate the decay width for the radiative weak decays of
\Xi^{0}
using a joint description of weak radiative (WR) and nonleptonic (NL) hyperon decays (HD) in broken
SU(3)
. The two groups of decays are linked via
SU(2)_{W}
spin symmetry and vector-meson dominance (VMD)
Zenczykowski:2005cs
. The effective Lagrangian for weak radiative hyperon decay
B_{i}\rightarrow B_{f}\gamma
is

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{f}i\sigma_{\mu\nu}(p_{f}-p_{i})^{\nu}(C+D\gamma_{5})u_{i}A^{\mu},

(28)

where,
C
&
D
are parity-conserving and parity-violating amplitudes. For this case, the decay is given by
Zenczykowski:2005cs
,

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{m_{i}^{2}-m_{f}^{2}}{2m_{i}}\right)^{3}(|C|^{2}+|D|^{2}),

(29)

and the asymmetry by
Zenczykowski:2005cs

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{2Re(C^{*}D)}{|C|^{2}+|D|^{2}}.

(30)

Here, parity-conserving amplitudes obtained in the ground-state baryon pole
model from the NLHD amplitudes via the
SU(2)_{W}
+VMD route, are given by
Zenczykowski:2005cs
,

C(B_{i}\rightarrow B_{f}\gamma)=\left(\frac{e}{g}\right)\frac{1}{(m_{i}+m_{f})\sqrt{2}}\\
\times B(B_{i}\rightarrow B_{f}U^{0})

(31)

B
describe amplitudes for the emission of a linear superposition
U^{0}
of virtual vector mesons
\rho^{0},\omega,\phi,
corresponding to a
photon and obtained by the
SU(6)_{W}
symmetry from the
NLHD amplitudes and given by,

B(\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}U^{0})=\bigg[\frac{-1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(3\frac{fp}{dp}-1\right)(\mu_{\Xi^{0}}-\mu_{\Lambda^{0}})\\
-\left(\frac{fp}{dp}+1\right)\mu_{\Sigma\Lambda}\bigg]\frac{N}{\mu_{p}D},

(32)

and

B(\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Sigma^{0}U^{0})=\bigg[\bigg(\frac{fp}{dp}+1\bigg)(\mu_{\Xi^{0}}-\mu_{\Sigma^{0}})\\
+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(3\frac{fp}{dp}-1\right)\mu_{\Sigma\Lambda}\bigg]\frac{N}{\mu_{p}D}.

(33)

In this approach, the parity-violating WRHD amplitudes has the form,

D(B_{i}\rightarrow B_{f}\gamma^{0})=\bigg(\frac{e}{g}\bigg)\frac{1}{(m_{i}-m_{f})\sqrt{2}}\\
\times A(B_{i}\rightarrow B_{f}U^{0})

(34)

where amplitudes A are related by
SU(2)_{W}
to the (vanishing in the soft-meson limit) correction terms in NLHD as,

\displaystyle A(\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\lambda^{0}U^{0})=\frac{-2+\epsilon}{9\sqrt{3}}\frac{1-x}{1-x^{2}}b_{R}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}S_{R}

(35)

\displaystyle A(\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Sigma^{0}U^{0})=\frac{-1}{3}\frac{1+x}{1-x^{2}}b_{R}-\frac{5}{6}S_{R}

(36)

The challenge of Weak Radiative Hyperon Decays (WRHD) has persisted for around six decades. It can be seen as a weak-interaction-related counterpart to the matter of baryon magnetic moments. However, unlike the baryon magnetic moments issue, which was well understood early in the development of the quark model, WRHD continues to generate significant controversy. Recent observation of negative asymmetry of
\Lambda\rightarrow n\gamma

BESIII:2022rgl
confirms the Hara’s-theorem-satisfying nature of WRHD
Zenczykowski:2020hmg

The good observational data for weak radiative decay contains their branching ratios and the asymmetry parameters of both the possible decays of
\Xi^{0}

\Xi^{0}\rightarrow\Lambda^{0}+\gamma^{0}

Zenczykowski:2005cs
. The calculated values of branching ratios and asymmetries
\alpha_{\Xi\Lambda^{0}\gamma}
&
\alpha_{\Xi\Sigma^{0}\gamma}
are in close proximity of experimentally observed values.

Having seen the potential of our approach to predict the experimental data in the natural way with the fixation of model parameters

, &
\sigma

Having the parameters for the baryons observed experimentally, we can correlate the parameters with the mass of the baryon which then be extrapolated to the baryons with less or no experimental data. This will enable us to predict the masses and other spectroscopic properties of those unobserved baryons which will be useful in resolving the future experimental uncertainties.

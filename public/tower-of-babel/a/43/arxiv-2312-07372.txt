Incoherent processes in dileptons production in proton-nucleus scattering at high energies
Sergei P. Maydanyuk 1,2, Gyorgy Wolf (1)

Abstract
Purpose: Incoherent processes in production of lepton pairs in the scattering of protons off nuclei are investigated. Methods: New quantum mechanical model is constructed which uses generalization of the nuclear model of emission of photons in the proton-nucleus reactions in region from low up to high energies with inclusion of formalism of production of lepton pairs. Results: (1) Model with the coherent matrix elements is tested for the scattering of protons on the Be {}^{\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}_{\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Be} nuclei at energy of proton beam E p E_{\rm p} of 2.1 GeV. The calculated cross section of production of lepton pairs is in good agreement with experimental data obtained by DLS Collaboration. (2) We analyzed dilepton production for nuclei-targets Be 9 {}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 9$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Be} , C 12 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 12$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{C} , O 16 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 16$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{O} , Mg 24 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 24$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Mg} , Ca 44 {}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 44$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ca} , Au 197 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 197$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Au} at E p = 2.1 E_{\rm p}=2.1 GeV. Coherent cross sections of dilepton production are monotonously decreased with increasing of nucleus-target mass. This new effect is explained by suppressing of production of dileptons (by nuclear matter of nucleus-target). (3) Production of lepton pairs is more intensive at larger E p E_{\rm p} . (4) For p + Be 9 p+{{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 9$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Be}} at E p = 2.1 E_{\rm p}=2.1 GeV we analyzed role of incoherent processes in production of pairs of leptons. Inclusion of incoherent proton-nucleon processes to the model improves agreement with experimental data a little. A new phenomenon of suppression of production of lepton pairs at low energies due to incoherent processes is observed. This is explained by dominant coherent contribution at very low energies. (5) Adding of the longitudinal amplitude of virtual photon suppresses the cross section of dilepton production a little (effect is observed for p + Be 9 p+{{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 9$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Be}} at E p = 2.1 E_{\rm p}=2.1 GeV). (6) The incoherent contribution has a leading role in dilepton production (ratio between incoherent and coherent contributions is 10–100). Also our model provides the tendencies of the full spectrum for p + Nb 93 p+{{}^{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 93$}}_{\makebox[7.97226pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Nb}} at E p = 3.5 E_{\rm p}=3.5 GeV in good agreement with experimental data obtained by HADES collaboration, and shows large role of incoherent processes. Conclusion: Results above confirms importance of incoherent processes in study of dilepton production in this reaction.

As the simplest mechanism, process of dilepton production can go through exchange of virtual photon, which is emitted by one nucleon of
the nuclei participated in collision.
The full picture of emission of photons is obtained due to evolution of moving
electric charges and magnetic momentums of nucleons (or nucleon resonances), which belong to the nuclei in reaction.
This leads to dependence of dilepton production on the different mechanisms and aspects of the studied nuclear reaction
(which depends on the chosen way of description of nuclear interactions),
and even properties of nuclei.
From this point of view, the question appears: how important is
the role of such mechanisms in nuclear reaction in the study of production of dileptons?
It could be interesting to clarify, how many-nucleon dynamics influences on production of dileptons in the nuclear collision.

From the previous study it has been known that
physics of many-nucleon dynamics in the nuclear reactions (in study of emission of photons) is related with
incoherent processes which are significantly larger than coherent ones

The second group (incoherent bremsstrahlung) includes rest of terms without momentum from relative distance between the nucleus-target and the scattered proton.
It includes momenta of relative distances between individual nucleons of the nucleus-target and the scattering proton

\alpha

Liu_Maydanyuk_Zhang_Liu.2019.PRC.hypernuclei

For production of leptons pair we should take into account only connection between two wave functions of virtual photon

V
Definition of cross-section of production of leptons pair

Cross-sections of the production of leptons pair can be defined using logic of determination of emission of bremsstrahlung photons in nucleon-nucleus and nucleus-nucleus scattering
(see Refs.
Maydanyuk.2023.PRC.delta
;
Maydanyuk_Zhang_Zou.2016.PRC
;
Maydanyuk.2012.PRC
;
Maydanyuk_Zhang.2015.PRC
, reference therein).
We define cross-section of production of leptons pair on the basis of the full matrix element
p_{fi}
in frameworks of formalism in Ref.
Maydanyuk_Zhang_Zou.2016.PRC

(see Eq. (22) in that paper, reference therein).
Finally, the cross-sections of production of dileptons is obtained
on the basis of matrix element of nuclear part (
100
)–(
105
) as
[see Eqs. (
116
), App.
B
for details]

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dw_{\rm ph}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\,c^{5}}\>\displaystyle\frac{w_{\rm ph}\,E_{i}}{k_{i}}\>\bigl|M_{\rm full}\bigr|^{2}.\end{array}

(66)

In this paper the matrix elements are calculated on the basis of wave functions with quantum numbers
l_{i}=0
,
l_{f}=1
and
l_{\rm ph}=1

[here,
l_{i}
and
l_{f}
are orbital quantum numbers of wave function
\Phi_{\rm p-nucl}(\mathbf{r})
for states before emission of photon and after this emission,

l_{\rm ph}
is orbital quantum number of photon in the multipole approach].

We will formulate the following rule for such a transformation of formula for cross section.

Number of all final states for process of emission of bremsstrahlung photon
should be changed into number of all final states for process of production of lepton pairs.

In particular, for current problem we have:

•

For the previous formalism with emission of bremsstrahlung photons,
the final states is state of the emitted bremsstrahlung real photon,

•

For the new formalism with production of lepton pair,
the final states are states of the produced leptons.

So, we find the following change:

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{d^{3}k}_{\rm ph,f}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,(2\,E_{\rm ph,f})}\to\prod\limits_{f}\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{d^{3}k}_{e,f}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,(2\,E_{e,f})}.\end{array}

(67)

Applying such a rule, we will rewrite Eq. (
66
) and obtain

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\Bigl(d^{2}\sigma^{\rm(br)}=\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{c^{5}}\,\displaystyle\frac{2E_{i}}{k_{i}}\,\bigl|M_{\rm full}\bigr|^{2}\>\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{d^{3}k}_{\rm ph}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,(2E_{\rm ph})}\Bigr)&\to&\Bigl(d^{2}\sigma^{\rm(lep)}=\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{c^{5}}\,\displaystyle\frac{2E_{i}}{k_{i}}\,\bigl|M_{\rm full}^{\rm(lep)}\bigr|^{2}\>\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{d^{3}k}_{e,f1}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,(2E_{\rm e,f1})}\,\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{d^{3}k}_{e,f2}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,(2E_{\rm e,f2})}\Bigr).\end{array}

(68)

Taking into account square of the coherent matrix element after reduction of all
\delta
-functions in form (
65
),
we write down the final solution as

\begin{array}[]{rllll}d^{2}\sigma^{\rm(lep)}=\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{8}e^{2}}{8\,c^{5}}\cdot\par|f|^{2}\,\displaystyle\frac{E_{i}}{k_{i}\,E_{\rm e,f1}^{2}}\;|\mathbf{k}_{{\rm e},f}|\,\Bigl[2\,\sin^{2}\theta+(1-\cos{2\,\theta})\Bigr]\;\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos{2\, \theta}}{1-\cos{2\, \theta}}\;do_{e,2},\end{array}

(69)

\begin{array}[]{rllll}\vskip 4.2679ptf&=&-\,\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{3\hbar w_{\rm ph}}}\,(2\pi)^{3}\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{N}\,m_{\rm p}}{\mu}\>Z_{\rm eff}^{\rm(mon,\,0)}\,\Bigl\{J_{1}(0,1,0)-\displaystyle\frac{47}{40}\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}}\cdot J_{1}(0,1,2)\Bigr\},\\
f^{vir}&=&-\,\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{3\hbar w_{\rm ph}}}\,(2\pi)^{3}\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{N}\,m_{\rm p}}{\mu}\>Z_{\rm eff}^{\rm(mon,\,0)}\,\Bigl\{J_{1}^{vir}(0,1,0;0)-\displaystyle\frac{47}{40}\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}}\cdot J_{1}^{vir}(0,1,2;0)\Bigr\}.\end{array}

(70)

Here,

E_{i}
is energy of relative motion between proton in beam and nucleus as target in the center-of-mass frame of these objects
(before emission of virtual photon),

E_{{\rm e},\,f1}
is energy of one produced lepton,

\mu=m_{A}\,m_{\rm p}/(m_{A}+m_{\rm p})
is reduced mass,

\theta
is angle between direction of virtual photon emission (defined by
\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}/|\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}|
) and
direction of one lepton emission (with index
i
; defined by
\mathbf{k}_{e,i}/|\mathbf{k}_{e,i}|
).

Radial integrals are
[see Eqs. (
129
)]

\begin{array}[]{llllll}J_{1}^{real}(l_{i},l_{f},n)&=&\displaystyle\int\limits^{+\infty}_{0}\displaystyle\frac{dR_{i}(r,l_{i})}{dr}\>R^{*}_{f}(l_{f},r)\,j_{n}(k_{\rm ph}r)\;r^{2}dr,\\
\tilde{J}^{real}\,(c,l_{i},l_{f},n)&=&\displaystyle\int\limits^{+\infty}_{0}R_{i}(l_{i},r)\,R^{*}_{f}(l_{f},r)\,j_{n}(c\,k_{\rm ph}r)\;r^{2}dr,\end{array}

(71)

\begin{array}[]{llllll}J_{1}^{vir}(l_{i},l_{f},n,\;l^{\parallel})&=&\displaystyle\int\limits^{+\infty}_{0}\displaystyle\frac{dR_{i}(r,l_{i})}{dr}\>R^{*}_{f}(l_{f},r)\,j_{n}(k_{\rm ph}^{\bot}r)\;j_{l^{\parallel}}(k^{\parallel}r)\;r^{2}dr,\\
\tilde{J}^{vir}\,(c,l_{i},l_{f},n;\;l^{\parallel})&=&\displaystyle\int\limits^{+\infty}_{0}R_{i}(l_{i},r)\,R^{*}_{f}(l_{f},r)\,j_{n}(c\,k_{\rm ph}^{\bot}r)\;j_{l^{\parallel}}(k^{\parallel}r)\;r^{2}dr.\par\end{array}

(72)

Here,

k_{\rm ph}^{\bot}=|\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}^{\bot}}|
and

k_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}=|\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}}|
are
perpendicular and longitudinal parts of wave vector
\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}}
of photon
(we have
\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}}\parallel\mathbf{e^{(3)}}
,
for real photon
\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}}=0
),
integrals in Eqs. (
71
) are without inclusion of the longitudinal part of virtual photon,
integrals in Eqs. (
72
) are with inclusion of the longitudinal part of virtual photon
(see App.

),

is radial part of wave function
\Phi_{\rm p-nucl}(\mathbf{r})
in
i
-state or
f
-state,

is spherical Bessel function of order

.
The effective electric charge is

\begin{array}[]{llll}Z_{\rm eff}^{\rm(mon)}(\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph})=\displaystyle\frac{m_{\rm p}\,Z_{A}(\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph})-m_{A}\,z_{\rm p}}{m_{A}+m_{\rm p}}.\par\end{array}

One can redefine the cross section of production of pair of leptons, where dependence on energy of these leptons or their invariant mass is included:

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\vskip 1.42262ptd^{2}\sigma^{\rm(lep)}=\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{8}e^{2}}{8\,c^{5}}\cdot\par|f\,(M,\chi)|^{2}\,\displaystyle\frac{E_{i}}{k_{i}\,E_{\rm e,f1}^{2}}\;|\mathbf{k}_{{\rm e},f}|\,\Bigl[2\,\sin^{2}\theta+(1-\cos{2\,\theta})\Bigr]\;\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos{2\, \theta}}{1-\cos{2\, \theta}}\;do_{e,2},\\
|\mathbf{k}_{\rm e,1}|=\displaystyle\frac{M}{2},\quad\par E_{\rm e}^{2}=|\mathbf{k}_{{\rm e},f}|^{2}+m_{\rm e}^{2},\quad\chi=\displaystyle\frac{k_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}}{k_{\rm ph}^{\bot}}.\end{array}

VIII
Conclusions and perspective

In this paper we investigate production of lepton pairs in the scattering of protons off nuclei at intermediate energy region.
For that research we construct a new model.
In description of emission of virtual photons we generalize the formalism of emission of photons in the proton nucleus scattering
(see Refs.
Maydanyuk_Zhang.2015.PRC
;
Maydanyuk.2012.PRC
;
Maydanyuk_Zhang_Zou.2016.PRC
;
Liu_Maydanyuk_Zhang_Liu.2019.PRC.hypernuclei
;
Maydanyuk_Zhang_Zou.2019.PRC.microscopy
and reference therein).
We do not include here important contribution from Dalitz-decays and direct decays of vector mesons.
On the basis of such a model we obtain the following.

•

We calculate the cross section of production of lepton pairs in the scattering of protons on the
{}^{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 9$}}_{\makebox[3.98613pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{B}

Appendix C
Inclusion of longitudinal polarization of virtual photon

In description of emission of virtual photon
we should take into account its non-zero longitudinal polarization.
To understand that, we follow to the formalism developed in Sect.
II
.
We need to calculate the following matrix element

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mathbf{I}_{1,\,\mu}=\biggl\langle\>\Phi_{f}(\mathbf{r})\;\biggl|\,e^{-i\,\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}\mathbf{r}}\;\mathbf{\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr^{\mu}}}\biggr|\>\Phi_{i}(\mathbf{r})\biggr\rangle,\end{array}

(117)

where we have to take into account the longitudinal part of virtual photon.

In QED, two independent gauges are imposed on wave function of the emitted real photon.
These are Lorentz gauge like
\partial^{\mu}A_{\mu}\,(\mathbf{r},t)=0
and Coulomb gauge like
\mathbf{\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\,A}=0
(or alternative one),
where
A_{\mu}^{\lambda}(x)
can be defined in Eq. (
9
).
In result, we obtain two independent relations between vectors of polarization
\mathbf{e}_{\mu}^{\lambda}
and wave vector of photon
\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}}
.
In particular, Coulomb gauge gives requirement that space vectors of polarization are perpendicular to wave vector of photon,
i.e.
\mathbf{e}^{(1,2)}\cdot\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}}=0
.

In the case of virtual photon,
Coulomb gauge
is not imposed, and only one relation still applied on vectors of polarization and wave vector of photon.
So, in a general case, we do not have requirement that space vectors of polarization are perpendicular to wave vector of photon,
i.e. we obtain
\mathbf{e}^{(1,2)}\cdot\mathbf{k_{\rm ph}}\neq 0
.
By such a reason, we write down the full wave vector of virtual photon as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}=\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}+\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\perp},\end{array}

(118)

where
\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\perp}
is transverse part of wave vector which is perpendicular to the space vectors of polarization
\mathbf{e}^{(1,2)}
(like for real photon),

\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}
is rest, i.e longitudinal part of wave vector of the virtual photon.
So, we write down

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\vskip 4.2679pt\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\perp}\mathbf{r},&e^{\pm i\,\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}\mathbf{r}}=e^{\pm i\,\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\parallel}\mathbf{r}}\cdot e^{\pm i\,\mathbf{k}_{\rm ph}^{\perp}\mathbf{r}}.\end{array}

(119)

117

The parameters of the proton–nucleus potential

We calculate the radial wave functions
R_{l}(r)
for proton nucleus scattering numerically concerning the chosen potential
of interaction between the proton and the spherically symmetric core.
For description of proton-nucleus interaction we use the potential as

,
where
v_{c}(r)
,
v_{N}(r)
,
v_{\rm so}(r)
and
v_{l}(r)
are Coulomb,
nuclear, spin-orbital and centrifugal components

Becchetti.1969.PR
:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\vskip 2.84526ptv_{N}(r)=-\displaystyle\frac{V_{R}}{1+\exp{\displaystyle\frac{r-R_{R}} {a_{R}}}},\hskip 5.69054ptv_{l}(r)=\displaystyle\frac{l\,(l+1)}{2mr^{2}},\hskip 5.69054ptv_{\rm so}(r)=V_{\rm so}\,{\mathbf{q\cdot l}}\,\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\pi}^{2}}{r}\,\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\,\Bigl[1+\exp\Bigl(\displaystyle\frac{r-R_{\rm so}}{a_{\rm so}}\Bigr)\Bigr]^{-1},\\
v_{c}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{Ze^{2}}{r},&\mbox{at }r\geq R_{c},\\
\displaystyle\frac{Ze^{2}}{2R_{c}}\;\biggl\{3-\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{R_{c}^{2}}\biggr\},&\mbox{at }r<R_{c}.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(130)

We use the parametrization proposed by Becchetti and Greenlees in
Becchetti.1969.PR

which has been tested in numerous research papers:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{array}[]{llll}V_{R}=54.0-0.32\,E+0.4\,Z/A^{1/3}+24.0\,I,&V_{\rm so}=6.2,\\
\end{array}\\
\begin{array}[]{llll}R_{R}=r_{R}\,A^{1/3},&R_{c}=r_{c}\,A^{1/3},&R_{\rm so}=r_{\rm so}\,A^{1/3},\\

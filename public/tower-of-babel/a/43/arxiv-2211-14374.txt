On inclusion relations between weighted spaces of entire functions Thanks: G. Schindl is supported by FWF-Project 10.55776/P33417
Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We characterize the inclusions of weighted classes of entire functions in terms of the defining weights resp. weight systems. First we treat weights defined in terms of a so-called associated weight function where the weight(system) is based on a given sequence. The abstract weight function case is then reduced to the weight sequence setting by using the so-called associated weight sequence. As an application of the main statements we characterize closedness under point-wise multiplication of these classes.

1.
Introduction

Weighted spaces of entire functions are defined as follows:

(1.1)

H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{C}):=\{f\in H(\mathbb{C}):\|f\|_{v}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|v(|z|)<+\infty\}.

Here
H(\mathbb{C})
denotes the class of entire functions and
v:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
is the (radial and strictly positive)
weight function.
From now on, except stated explicitly otherwise, we assume that
v
is

(*)

continuous,

(*)

non-increasing and

(*)

rapidly decreasing, i.e.
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}t^{k}v(t)=0
for all
k\geq 0
.

This is the same notion to be a weight (function) as it has been considered in
[
BonetTaskinen18
]
,
[
BonetLuskyTaskinen19
]
,
[
solidassociatedweight
]
and in
[
Bonet2022survey
]
. In
[
Bonet2022survey
, Sect. 2]
weighted classes are even introduced for weights which are not necessarily non-increasing and rapidly decreasing; such
v
’s are called
general weights
. Also in
[
BierstedtBonetTaskinen98
]
,
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
]
more general notions have been considered. It is known that
H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{C})
is a Banach space w.r.t. the norm
\|\cdot\|_{v}
and similarly such weighted classes can be defined when
\mathbb{C}
is replaced by any other (non-empty) open subset
G\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and
v:G\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
. Another very prominent example in the literature is the unit disc
G=\mathbb{D}
. In this article we will focus on
G=\mathbb{C}
except in Section
3.1
and so, in order to lighten notation, we frequently write
H^{\infty}_{v}
instead of
H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{C})
when the case is clear (except e.g. in Section
3.1
). In Section
2
all relevant information on weights, sequences, weight systems and the corresponding weighted classes is gathered.

Such weighted spaces (and operators on them) have been studied during the last years from different aspects. Very recently in
[
Bonet2022survey
]
a detailed survey of available results on this topic is given; see also the numerous literature citations therein.

The aim of this paper is to study and to compare growth relations between weights and weight systems and inclusion relations of the corresponding weighted spaces. To be more precise, let
v
and
w
be two weights and write
v\hypertarget{ompreceq}{\preceq}w
if

(1.2)

w(t)=O(v(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty.

Let us call
v
and
w

equivalent,
written
v\hypertarget{sim}{\sim}w
, if

v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w\;\text{and}\;w\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}v.

Note that relation
\sim
is denoted by the symbol
\approx
in
[
Bonet2022survey
]
and by
\asymp
in
[
AbakumovDoubtsov15
]
and
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
]
. Obviously, by definition one has

(1.3)

v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w\Longrightarrow H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq H^{\infty}_{w}(\mathbb{C}),

with continuous inclusion (and similarly if
\mathbb{C}
is replaced by any other subset
G\subseteq\mathbb{C}
). So equivalent weights yield equivalent weighted classes (as locally convex vector spaces) and the main question is: What can be said about the implication
\Longleftarrow
in (
1.3
), i.e., can the inclusion (resp. equality) of weighted spaces be characterized in terms of a growth relation between two given weights?

In Section
3.1
we are concerned with this problem. As the anonymous referee has commented in his report, in fact the desired characterization follows from a more general result in
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
]
dealing with the composition operator
C_{\varphi}(f):=f\circ\varphi
for
G=\mathbb{D}
and it also holds for
G=\mathbb{C}
; see Theorem
3.2
. However, the obtained characterization does not involve the weights
v
resp.
w
directly but a technical modification; see (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). In order to get now the desired equivalence (
1.3
) (for
G=\mathbb{D}
and
G=\mathbb{C}
) one has to assume that
v
is
essential.

We also give a second independent proof of this statement: If
v
is
essential,
then alternatively by using a consequence of the recent works
[
AbakumovDoubtsov15
]
and
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
]
in (
1.3
) equivalence holds true (again for
G=\mathbb{D}
and
G=\mathbb{C}
); see Theorem
3.3
.

In this work we are basically interested in the case when
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
is a given (weight) sequence which satisfies standard growth assumptions and

v=v_{M}:t\mapsto\exp(-\omega_{M}(t)).

Here,
\omega_{M}
denotes the so-called
associated weight function
; see Section
2.4
. Such particular weights have been considered in
[
solidassociatedweight
]
where solid hulls and cores of the corresponding weighted classes have been studied. The advantage in this setting is that
v_{M}
is based on a given sequence and so desired properties for
v_{M}
can be expressed and characterized in terms of growth and regularity assumptions for
M
(known and used in the ultradifferentiable and ultraholomorphic setting). Therefore, more structure and information is involved and available. For example, by combining another recent result from
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
]
and the crucial Lemma

that any

is essential (for the weighted entire case) and hence in Theorem

the equivalence
v_{M}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}v_{N}\Longleftrightarrow H^{\infty}_{v_{M}}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq H^{\infty}_{v_{N}}(\mathbb{C})
is characterized by the following growth relation between
M
and
N
:

(1.4)

\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;N_{j}\leq AM_{j}.

When an abstract weight
v
is given, the idea is to reduce this case to the weight sequence setting. This can be achieved by considering the so-called

M^{v}
; see Section

our standard assumptions on

to be a weight are convenient. Moreover, it turns out that in order to use

(
1.4
) is much more restrictive than the (known) comparison relation

(1.5)

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{j}}{N_{j}}\right)^{1/j}<+\infty,

which is crucial in the ultradifferentiable setting. In (

) instead resp. in addition of an uniform constant

the geometrically growing factor

is appearing with a parameter

. This difference is connected to a dilatation in the function argument in the weight

; see (

) and (

[

. On the other hand, when treating such spaces we have to replace

Moreover, in view of this observation two kinds of weight systems can be considered ”naturally” also for abstractly given weights. We can either consider

, the
dilatation-type,
motivated by the aforementioned explanations, or
t\mapsto v(t)^{c}
, called the

In both cases

is the parameter and means, for

, that

\omega_{M}
as a dilatation/stretching parameter resp. ”outside the function” as a multiplicative parameter. The latter case requires another generalization of
\preceq
; see (
2.7
). All precise definitions can be found in Section

.

In Section
3
we are able to prove characterizations for the inclusion (resp. equality) of weighted spaces given by dilatation- and exponential-type systems. Again, this can be purely expressed in terms of given

and

Finally, based on these results in Section

\mathfrak{m}:(f,g)\mapsto f\cdot g
acting on such weighted classes. Again, the weight function case is reduced to the weight sequence case and in the weight sequence setting point-wise multiplication yields naturally the appearance of the so-called
convolved sequence;
see Section

\mathfrak{m}
; see Proposition

M
is precisely the classical

i.e.
(M.2)
in
[

, resp. the corresponding condition (

However, our characterization for weight systems is in contrast to the fact that a weighted class defined by a single weight function

2.
Weights, weighted spaces and growth conditions

2.1.
Basic convention and comments

We use the notation
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\dots,\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{>0}=\{1,2,\dots\}
.

We call a weight
v

normalized
when
v(t)=1
for all
t\in[0,1]
. A normalized
v
satisfies
v(t)\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
.

When both
v
,
w
are (normalized) weight functions, then the product
v\cdot w
is so, too.

For defining the class in (
1.1
) normalization can be assumed w.l.o.g.: Otherwise replace
v
by a normalized weight, say
v^{n}
, and such that
v(t)=v^{n}(t)
for all large
t>1
. This gives
H^{\infty}_{v}=H^{\infty}_{v^{n}}
as l.c.v.s.

Since
w(t),v(t)\neq 0
for all
t
, (
1.2
) precisely means that

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;w(t)\leq Cv(t).

2.2.
Weight systems and corresponding weighted classes

Let
\underline{\mathcal{V}}=(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
be a non-increasing sequence of weights, i.e.
v_{n}\geq v_{n+1}
for all
n
. Then we define the (LB)-space
H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}}
of the Banach spaces
H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}
, so

(2.1)

H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}}:=\varinjlim_{n\rightarrow\infty}H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}.

Analogously, if
\overline{\mathcal{V}}=(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
is a non-decreasing sequence of weights, i.e.
v_{n}\leq v_{n+1}
for all
n
, then we define the Fréchet-space
H^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}}
to be the projective limit of the Banach spaces
H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}
, so

(2.2)

H^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}}:=\varprojlim_{n\rightarrow\infty}H^{\infty}_{v_{n}}.

Let
v
be a weight function and
c>0
, then set

(2.3)

v_{c}:t\mapsto v(ct),\hskip 20.0ptv^{c}:t\mapsto v(t)^{c},

and write again
v
instead of
v_{1}
resp.
v^{1}
. We summarize:

(i)

v
is a weight function if and only if some/each weight
v_{c}
resp. if and only if some/each
v^{c}
is so.

(ii)

For any
d\geq c>0
we have the continuous inclusions

H^{\infty}_{v_{c}}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq H^{\infty}_{v_{d}},\hskip 15.0ptH^{\infty}_{v^{c}}\subseteq H^{\infty}_{v^{d}}.

Note that
v^{d}(t)\leq v^{c}(t)
for all large
t
since eventually
v(t)\leq 1
(and for all
t\geq 0
if
v
is normalized).

(iii)

v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w
if and only if
v_{c}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w_{c}
if and only if
v^{c}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w^{c}
for some/each
c>0
.

Consider the corresponding weight systems

(2.4)

\underline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v_{c})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}},\hskip 20.0pt\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v_{\frac{1}{c}})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}.

Thus
\underline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-increasing sequence of weights, whereas
\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-decreasing sequence. Similarly, if
v\leq 1
, then consider

(2.5)

\underline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v^{c})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}},\hskip 20.0pt\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}:=(v^{\frac{1}{c}})_{c\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}.

Since
v\leq 1
the set
\underline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-increasing sequence of weights, whereas
\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}
is a non-decreasing sequence. The assumption
v\leq 1
holds if
v
is normalized.

Definition 2.1
.

The weight systems in (
2.4
) are called to be of dilatation-type and the systems in (
2.5
) of exponential-type.

Let us write
v\hypertarget{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
if

(2.6)

\exists\;c\geq 1:\;\;\;v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w_{c},

and
v\hypertarget{simc}{\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
, if

v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0ptw\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}v.

Similarly, write
v\hypertarget{ompreceqpowc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
if

(2.7)

\exists\;c\geq 1:\;\;\;v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w^{c},

and
v\hypertarget{simpowc}{\sim^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
, if

v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0ptw\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}v.

Note:

(*)

Both
v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
and
v\hyperlink{ompreceqpowc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
are weaker than
v\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w
.

(*)

v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
resp.
v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
if and only if

\exists\;c\geq 1\;\forall\;a>0:\;\;\;v_{a}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w_{ac}\;\;\;\text{resp.}\;\;\;v^{a}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}w^{ac}.

These observations imply the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
v
and
w
be (normalized) weight functions. If
v\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}w
, then

H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}}\subseteq H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{c}}},\hskip 20.0ptH^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{\mathfrak{c}}}\subseteq H^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\mathfrak{c}}},

and if
v\hyperlink{ompreceqpowc}{\preceq^{\mathfrak{c}}}w
, then

H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}}\subseteq H^{\infty}_{\underline{\mathcal{W}}^{\mathfrak{c}}},\hskip 20.0ptH^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathfrak{c}}}\subseteq H^{\infty}_{\overline{\mathcal{W}}^{\mathfrak{c}}},

with continuous inclusions.

We close with the following observation:

Remark 2.3
.

Let
v
be a (normalized) weight function, then

(2.8)

\forall\;d>c>0:\;\;\;\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{v^{c}(t)}{v^{d}(t)}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}v^{c-d}(t)=+\infty.

However, in general
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{v_{c}(t)}{v_{d}(t)}=+\infty
is not clear.

2.3.
Weight sequences and conditions

Let
M=(M_{j})_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. The corresponding sequence of quotients is denoted by
\mu_{j}:=\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}
,
j\geq 1
, and set
\mu_{0}:=1
.
M
is called
normalized
if
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}
and
M
is called
log-convex
if

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;M_{j}^{2}\leq M_{j-1}M_{j+1},

equivalently if the sequence
(\mu_{j})_{j}
is non-decreasing. If
M_{0}=1
and
M
is log-convex, then
(M_{j})^{1/j}\leq\mu_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.

Definition 2.4
.

M
is called a weight sequence if

1=M_{0}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty.

Moreover consider the set

\hypertarget{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}:=\{M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\;M\;\text{is normalized, log-convex},\;\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty\},

i.e.
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
if and only if
M
is a log-convex weight sequence with
M_{1}\geq 1
. Note that each
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
is non-decreasing since
\mu_{j}\geq 1
for all
j
and
j\mapsto\mu_{j}
is non-decreasing.

M
(with
M_{0}=1
) has condition
moderate growth
, denoted by
(\text{mg})
, if

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;j,k\in\mathbb{N}:\;M_{j+k}\leq C^{j+k}M_{j}M_{k}.

In
[
Komatsu73
]
this is denoted by
(M.2)
and also known under the name
stability under ultradifferential operators.

Let
M,N\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we write
M\leq N
if
M_{j}\leq N_{j}
for all
j
and
M\hypertarget{preceq}{\preceq}N
if (
1.5
), i.e.

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{j}}{N_{j}}\right)^{1/j}<+\infty.

M
and
N
are called
equivalent
, written
M\hypertarget{approx}{\approx}N
, if

M\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}N\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0ptN\hyperlink{preceq}{\preceq}M.

Property
(\operatorname{mg})
is clearly preserved under
\approx
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
M
be a log-convex weight sequence then there exists an equivalent
N\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
such that
\mu_{j}=\nu_{j}
for all
j
sufficiently large: Since
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{j}=+\infty
and
(\mu_{j})_{j}
is non-decreasing, there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\mu_{j}\geq\mu_{j_{0}}>1
for all
j\geq j_{0}
. Then set

\nu_{j}:=1,\;\;\;j<j_{0},\hskip 15.0pt\text{and}\hskip 15.0pt\nu_{j}:=\mu_{j},\;\;\;j\geq j_{0},

and consequently

(2.9)

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\frac{1}{C}N_{j}\leq M_{j}\leq CN_{j}.

2.4.
Associated weight function

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
(with
M_{0}=1
), then the
associated weight function

\omega_{M}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by

\omega_{M}(t):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{t^{j}}{M_{j}}\right)\;\;\;\text{for}\;t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\hskip 30.0pt\omega_{M}(0):=0.

For an abstract introduction of the associated function we refer to
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I]
, see also
[
Komatsu73
, Definition 3.1]
and the more recent work
[
regularnew
]
.

If
\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}>0
, then
\omega_{M}(t)=0
for sufficiently small
t
, since
\log\left(\frac{t^{j}}{M_{j}}\right)<0\Leftrightarrow t<(M_{j})^{1/j}
holds for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. (In particular, if
M_{j}\geq 1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\omega_{M}
is vanishing on
[0,1]
.) Moreover, under this assumption
t\mapsto\omega_{M}(t)
is a continuous non-decreasing function, which is convex in the variable
\log(t)
and tends faster to infinity than any
\log(t^{j})
,
j\geq 1
, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty
implies that
\omega_{M}(t)<+\infty
for each finite
t
which shall be considered as a basic assumption for defining
\omega_{M}
. In particular, this holds true provided
M
is a weight sequence.

If
M
is a log-convex weight sequence, then let us introduce the
counting function

(2.10)

\Sigma_{M}(t):=|\{j\geq 1:\;\;\;\mu_{j}\leq t\}|.

In this case we can compute
M
by involving
\omega_{M}
as follows, see
[
mandelbrojtbook
, Chapitre I, 1.4, 1.8]
and also
[
Komatsu73
, Prop. 3.2]
:

(2.11)

M_{j}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{t^{j}}{\exp(\omega_{M}(t))},\;\;\;j\in\mathbb{N}.

If
M
is a weight sequence which is not log-convex, then the right-hand side of (
2.11
) yields
M^{\operatorname{lc}}_{j}
with
M^{\operatorname{lc}}
denoting the
log-convex minorant
of
M
and
M^{\operatorname{lc}}
is a log-convex weight sequence.

Moreover, one has

\forall\;t\in[0,\mu_{1}]:\;\;\;\omega_{M}(t)=0,

which follows by the known integral representation formula (see
[
mandelbrojtbook
, 1.8. III]
and also
[
Komatsu73
,
(3.11)
]
)

(2.12)

\omega_{M}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{M}(u)}{u}du=\int_{\mu_{1}}^{t}\frac{\Sigma_{M}(u)}{u}du.

In particular, if
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
, then
\omega_{M}
vanishes on
[0,1]
.

Finally, for any weight sequence
M
we have

(2.13)

\omega_{M^{\operatorname{lc}}}=\omega_{M}.

2.5.
Weighted spaces of entire functions defined in terms of
\omega_{M}

For any weight sequence
M
(in the sense of Definition
2.4
) and for any parameter
c>0
we set

(2.14)

v_{M,c}(t):=\exp(-\omega_{M}(ct)),\;\;\;v^{c}_{M}(t):=\exp(-c\omega_{M}(t)),\;\;\;t\geq 0.

Note that each
v_{M,c}
and
v^{c}_{M}
is a weight by the properties of the associated weight function. Then define

\displaystyle H^{\infty}_{v_{M,c}}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H(\mathbb{C}):\|f\|_{v_{M,c}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|v_{M,c}(|z|)<+\infty\}

Remark 2.14

3.
Characterization of inclusions and equalities of weighted classes

In this section we give a complete characterization of the inclusion (and equality) of weighted classes of entire functions in terms of the growth of the corresponding weights. We treat both dilatation- and exponential-type weight systems. But before we focus on single (essential) weights and show that in this case one has an equivalence in (
1.3
).

3.1.
Characterization for essential weights

We recall the notions from
[
BierstedtBonetTaskinen98
, Def. 1.1, Sect. 1.B]
; see also
[
Bonet2022survey
, Sect. 3]
. Let a weight
v
be given, then consider the function (radial growth condition)
w_{v}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
given by

(3.1)

w_{v}:=\frac{1}{v}

and set

B_{w_{v}}(\mathbb{C}):=\{f\in H(\mathbb{C}):|f(z)|\leq w_{v}(|z|),\;\forall\;z\in\mathbb{C}\}.

The function
\widetilde{w_{v}}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)

associated with

w_{v}
is defined by

(3.2)

\widetilde{w_{v}}(z):=\sup\{|f(z)|:f\in B_{w_{v}}(\mathbb{C})\},\;\;\;z\in\mathbb{C},

and
[
BierstedtBonetTaskinen98
, Observation 1.5]
gives that
\widetilde{w_{v}}
is again radial. Finally, set

(3.3)

\widetilde{v}:=\frac{1}{\widetilde{w_{v}}}.

If
v=v_{M}
, then we write
\widetilde{v_{M}}
. Similarly these notions can be introduced for arbitrary (open and connected) sets
G
and even when using a more general notion for
v
being a weight; e.g. see
[
BierstedtBonetTaskinen98
]
,
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
]
(
v
is only assumed to be continuous and strictly positive). In
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
, Prop. 1.3]
it has been shown that isometrically
H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{D})=H^{\infty}_{\widetilde{v}}(\mathbb{D})
but the proof there gives
H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{C})=H^{\infty}_{\widetilde{v}}(\mathbb{C})
too; see also
[
BierstedtBonetTaskinen98
, Observ. 1.12]
.

Finally, we have the following definition; see
[
Bonet2022survey
, Sect. 3]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A weight
v
is called essential if
v\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\widetilde{v}
.

We can now formulate the main theorem in this context. It directly follows from the more general results
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
, Prop. 2.1, Cor. 2.2]
dealing with the composition operator
C_{\varphi}(f):=f\circ\varphi
acting on weighted classes of analytic functions defined on
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u
and
v
be weights. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The weights are related by
\widetilde{u}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}v
.

(ii)

The weights are related by
\widetilde{u}\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\widetilde{v}
.

(iii)

We have
H^{\infty}_{u}(G)\subseteq H^{\infty}_{v}(G)
(with continuous inclusion) for
G=\mathbb{D}
and/or
G=\mathbb{C}
.

Thus, if
u
is essential, then the inclusion in
(iii)
holds if and only if
u\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}v
.

When
G=\mathbb{D}
then the relation
u\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}v
means
v(t)\leq Cu(t)
for all
0\leq t<1
and some
C\geq 1
.

Proof.

We apply
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
, Prop. 2.1]
to
\varphi=\operatorname{id}
and hence
C_{\operatorname{id}}=\operatorname{id}
; see also
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
, Cor. 2.2]
. Note that in
[
BonetDomanskiLindstroemTaskinen98
, Prop. 2.1]
the authors deal with
G=\mathbb{D}
but the proof there is also valid for
G=\mathbb{C}
.
∎

However, we give a second independent proof of the previous result by involving different techniques (optimal functions).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u
and
v
be weights and assume that
u
is essential. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The weights are related by
u\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}v
.

(ii)

We have
H^{\infty}_{u}(G)\subseteq H^{\infty}_{v}(G)
(with continuous inclusion) for
G=\mathbb{D}
and/or
G=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

It remains to prove
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. For
G=\mathbb{C}
we use
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
, Thm. 2]
(see also
[
Bonet2022survey
, Thm. 3]
) and get that
u
is essential if and only if
u
is
approximable by the maximum of an analytic function modulus,
i.e.

(3.4)

\exists\;f_{u}\in H(\mathbb{C}):\;\;\;\frac{1}{u}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}t\mapsto M(f_{u},t),

with
M(f,t):=\sup\{|f(z)|:|z|=t\}
; see
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
, Def. 3]
and
[
Bonet2022survey
, p. 3, p. 5]
. Recall that
w=\frac{1}{u}
denotes the weight used in the notation in
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
]
. (
3.4
) gives that
f_{u}\in H^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{C})
and more precisely this relation ensures the existence of
optimal functions

f_{u}\in H^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{C})
admitting sharp estimates from below.

Then (
3.4
) and the inclusion
H^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{C})\subseteq H^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{C})
(as sets) give

\exists\;C,D\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\frac{1}{u(t)}\leq CM(f_{u},t)\leq CD\frac{1}{v(t)},

i.e.
v(t)=O(u(t))
and so
u\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}v
is verified.

When
G=\mathbb{D}
, then by
[
AbakumovDoubtsov15
, Thm. 1.2]
, see also
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
, Thm. 1, Rem. 1, Def. 1, Thm. 5]
and
[
Bonet2022survey
, Thm. 2]
, again
u
is essential if and only if (
3.4
) is valid. The rest follows as before.

Note that for this case in order to apply the cited results we do not require necessarily that
u
is rapidly decreasing.
∎

The next result illustrates that the notion of essentiality and the weight sequence setting are very closely connected via involving the associated weight sequence.

Theorem 3.4
.

We get the following:

(i)

Let
M
be a log-convex weight sequence. Then
v_{M}
is essential (for the weighted entire case) and in fact we even get
v_{M}=\widetilde{v_{M}}
.

(ii)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

u
is an essential weight.

(b)

u\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}v_{M^{u}}
is valid.

By recalling (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) we thus have that
v_{M,c}
is essential and
\widetilde{v_{M,c}}=v_{M,c}
for each
c>0
. The difference between
u\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}v_{M^{u}}
and the weaker relation (
2.23
) (due to the appearing quadratic power) illustrates that (log)-convexity is in general a weaker requirement than essentiality; see also Remark
3.6
for more explanations.

Proof.

(i)
For any weight
u
in
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
, Lemma 1]
, by using a statement from
[
ErdoesKoevari1956
]
, it has been shown that (see also
[
Bonet2022survey
, p.5]
):

(3.5)

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;P_{u}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\widetilde{u}(t)}\leq 6P_{u}(t).

For this recall the notation
w=\frac{1}{u}
,
\widetilde{w}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{u}}
used in
[
AbakumovDoubtsov18
]
. Therefore, by combining (
3.5
) with Lemma
2.16
applied to
u=v_{M}
we get

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\frac{1}{v_{M}(t)}\leq\frac{1}{\widetilde{v_{M}}(t)}\leq\frac{6}{v_{M}(t)},

i.e.
v_{M}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\widetilde{v_{M}}
is verified and so
v_{M}
is essential.

Moreover, for any growth condition
w
we get
0\leq\widetilde{w}\leq w
; see
[
BierstedtBonetTaskinen98
, Prop. 1.2]
. Thus by definitions (
3.1
), (
3.3
) and the first part in (
3.5
)

v_{M}=\frac{1}{w_{v_{M}}}\leq\frac{1}{\widetilde{w_{v_{M}}}}=\widetilde{v_{M}}\leq v_{M}

holds and we are done.

(ii)(a)\Rightarrow(b)
Since
u
is essential the non-trivial estimate
\widetilde{u}(t)\leq Cu(t)
holds true for some
C\geq 1
and all

. Then the second part in (

) and (

) yield

\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\frac{1}{v_{M^{u}}(t)}=P_{u}(t)\leq\frac{1}{u(t)}\leq\frac{C}{\widetilde{u}(t)}\leq 6CP_{u}(t)=\frac{6C}{v_{M^{u}}(t)},

and so

is verified.

(ii)(b)\Rightarrow(a)
The assumption and the first part in (
3.5
) give

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\frac{1}{Cu(t)}\leq\frac{1}{v_{M^{u}}(t)}=P_{u}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\widetilde{u}(t)},

hence
\widetilde{u}(t)\leq Cu(t)
. Since always
u(t)\leq\widetilde{u}(t)
holds true (see
[
BierstedtBonetTaskinen98
, Prop. 1.2]
) the relation
u\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\widetilde{u}
is verified and we are done.
∎

Fuglede’s conjecture holds in ℤ p × ℤ p n \mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
Tao Zhang

Abstract
Fuglede’s conjecture states that for a subset Ω \Omega of a locally compact abelian group G G with positive and finite Haar measure, there exists a subset of the dual group of G G which is an orthogonal basis of L 2 ​ ( Ω ) L^{2}(\Omega) if and only if it tiles the group by translation. In this paper, we prove a divisibility property for a set in ℤ p × ℤ p n \mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}} . Then using the divisibility property and equi-distributed property, we prove that Fuglede’s conjecture holds in the group ℤ p × ℤ p n \mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}} . Key words and phrases : Fuglede’s conjecture, tile, spectral set. AMS subject classifications : 05B25, 52C20, 11P99, 42B05, 43A40.

Introduction

Fourier analysis can be viewed as a tool to study an arbitrary function
f
on (say) the reals
\mathbb{R}
, by looking at how such a function decomposes into a series of orthogonal basis. In this paper, we consider a generalization of this concept.

Definition 1.1
.

A bounded measurable subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mu(\Omega)>0
is called spectral, if there is a subset
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the set of exponential functions
\{e_{\lambda}(x)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a complete orthogonal basis, where
e_{\lambda}(x)=e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda,x\rangle}
, that is

\langle e_{\lambda},e_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}e_{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}(x)dx=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\mu(\Omega),

where
\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}=1
if
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
0
otherwise,
and every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
can be expressed as

f(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}(x),

for some
a_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}
.
\Lambda
is called the spectrum of
\Omega
, and
(\Omega,\Lambda)
is called a spectral pair in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The other class of sets under study is that of tiling sets. Tiling is one of the most diverse and ubiquitous concepts of modern mathematics.

Definition 1.2
.

A subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
tiles
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by translation, if there is a translation set
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that almost all elements of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
have a unique representation as a sum
a+t
, where
a\in A
,
t\in T
. We will denote this by
A\oplus T=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
T
is called the tiling complement of
A
, and
(A,T)
is called a tiling pair in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In
[
9
]
, Fuglede proposed the following conjecture, which connected these two notions.

Conjecture 1.3
.

A subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive and finite Lebesgue measure is a spectral set if and only if it tiles
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by translation.

Fuglede
[

]
proved this conjecture under the additional assumption that the tiling set or the spectrum is a lattice subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. After some positive results, Tao
[
28
]
disproved this conjecture by constructing a spectral set in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
which is not a tile. Later, tiles with no spectrum in
\mathbb{R}^{5}

[

[

It is natural to formulate the following conjecture, which is in a more general setting, the locally compact abelian group.

Conjecture 1.4

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group. A subset
\Omega\subseteq G
of positive and finite Haar measure is a spectral set if and only if it is a tile.

In this general setting, the conjecture is far from being solved and is false for some specific groups as Tao

Let

2
Properties of spectral sets and tiles

In this section, we give some properties of spectral sets and tiles in finite abelian groups. The following notations are fixed throughout this paper.

•

Let
a,b
be integers such that
a\leq b
, denote
[a,b]=\{a,a+1,\dots,b\}
.

•

For any positive integer
m
,
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
is the ring of integers modulo
m
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{*}=\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}:\ \gcd(a,m)=1\}
.

•

Let
p
be a prime, for any
t\in[0,p^{m}-1]
, let
t_{p}[i]
be the
i
-th coefficient of
p
-adic representation of
t
, i.e.,
t=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}t_{p}[i]p^{i}
, where
t_{p}[i]\in[0,p-1]
. We will simply use
t[i]
instead of
t_{p}[i]
when there is no misunderstanding. Let
M(t)
be the minimal
i
such that
t[i]\neq 0
, i.e.,

M(t):=\min\{i\in[0,m-1]:t[i]\neq 0\}.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, then
G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{t}}
, for some
m_{i}
with
m_{1}\mid m_{2}\mid\dots\mid m_{t}
and
m_{1}>1
. For any
g\in G
, we will represent
g
as
(g_{1},\dots,g_{t})
, where
g_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{i}}
.
For any
g,h\in G
, we define their inner product by

\langle g,h\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}g_{i}h_{i}\frac{m_{t}}{m_{i}}.

Let

\chi_{g}(h)=e^{\frac{2\pi i\cdot\langle g,h\rangle}{m_{t}}},

and
\chi_{g}\circ\chi_{h}=\chi_{g+h}
.
Then the set
\widehat{G}=\{\chi_{g}:\ g\in G\}
with
\circ
forms a group which is isomorphic to
G
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}[G]
denote the group ring of
G
over
\mathbb{Z}
. For any
A\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
,

A
can be written as
A=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g
, where
a_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}
. For any
\chi\in\widehat{G}
, define

\chi(A)=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}\chi(g).

The following inversion formula shows that
A
is completely determined
by its character value
\chi(A)
, where
\chi
ranges over
\widehat{G}
.

Lemma 2.1

((Fourier inversion formula))
.

Let
G
be an abelian group. If
A=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
, then

a_{g}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{G}}\chi(A)\chi(g^{-1}),

for all
g\in G
.

Since we mainly work on the finite abelian group, we restate the definition of spectral set.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
A subset
A\subseteq G
is said to be spectral if there is a subset
B\subseteq G
such that

\{\chi_{b}:b\in B\}

forms an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}(A)
, the vector space of complex valued functions on
A
with Hermitian inner product
\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{a\in A}f(a)\overline{g}(a)
. In such case, the set
B
is called a spectrum of
A
, and
(A,B)
is called a spectral pair.

Since the dimension of
L^{2}(A)
is
|A|
, the pair
(A,B)
being a spectral pair is equivalent to

|A|=|B|\textup{ and }\chi_{b-b^{\prime}}(A)=0\textup{ for all }b\neq b^{\prime}\in B.

The set of zeros of
A
is defined by

\mathcal{Z}_{A}=\{g\in G:\chi_{g}(A)=0\}.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
g\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, then
rg\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{t}}^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
g\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, then we have

\chi_{g}(A)=\sum_{h\in A}e^{\frac{2\pi i\cdot\langle g,h\rangle}{m_{t}}}=0.

By Galois theory,

\chi_{rg}(A)=\sum_{h\in A}e^{\frac{2\pi i\cdot\langle rg,h\rangle}{m_{t}}}=\sum_{h\in A}e^{\frac{2\pi i\cdot r\langle g,h\rangle}{m_{t}}}=0

for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{t}}^{*}
, which means that
rg\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
.
∎

Now we give some equivalent conditions of spectral pair.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A,B\subseteq G
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

(A,B)
is a spectral pair.

(b)

(B,A)
is a spectral pair.

(c)

|A|=|B|
and
(B-B)\backslash\{0\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
.

(d)

The pair
(aA+g,bB+h)
is a spectral pair for all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{m_{t}}^{*}
and
g,h\in G
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (a), (b) and (c) can be found in
[
25
]
.

For (a)
\Rightarrow
(d), note that
|B|=|bB+h|
and
(bB+h)-(bB+h)=b(B-B)
, by (c) and Lemma
2.3
,
(A,bB+h)
is a spectral pair. By (b),
(bB+h,A)
is a spectral pair, then a similar discussion as above,
(aA+g,bB+h)
is a spectral pair. For (d)
\Rightarrow
(a), we only need to take
a=b=1
and
g=h=0
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.
A subset
A\subseteq G
is said to be a tile if there is a subset
T\subseteq G
such that each element
g\in G
can be expressed uniquely in the form

g=a+t,\ a\in A,\ t\in T.

We will denote this by
G=A\oplus T
.
The set
T
is called a tiling complement of
A
, and
(A,T)
is called a tiling pair.

The following result can be found in
[
25
]
,
[
27
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
A,T
be subsets in
G
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

(A,T)
is a tiling pair.

(b)

(T,A)
is a tiling pair.

(c)

(A+g,T+h)
is a tiling pair.

(d)

|A|\cdot|T|=|G|
and
(A-A)\cap(T-T)=\{0\}
.

(e)

|A|\cdot|T|=|G|
and
\mathcal{Z}_{A}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{T}=G\backslash\{0\}
.

If
|A|=1
or
A=G
, then the set
A
is called trivial. It is easy to see that a trivial set is a spectral set and also a tiling set. In the following of this paper, we will only consider nontrivial set.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
(A,T)
is a nontrivial tiling pair of
G
, then
\mathcal{Z}_{A}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{Z}_{T}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{Z}_{A}=\emptyset
, then by Lemma
2.6
,
\mathcal{Z}_{T}=G\backslash\{0\}
. By the Fourier inversion formula (Lemma
2.1
), we have
T=G
, then
|A|=1
, which is a contradiction. Hence
\mathcal{Z}_{A}\neq\emptyset
. Similarly, we can get
\mathcal{Z}_{T}\neq\emptyset
.
∎

3
Sets in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}

Recall that for
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, their inner product is

\langle u,v\rangle=p^{n-1}u_{1}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}.

Define

H(u,t):=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}:\langle x,u\rangle=t\},

and

H_{A}(u,t):=H(u,t)\cap A,

for
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. Then the following lemma, which is called equi-distributed property, can be found in
[
25
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
and
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. Then
u\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
if and only if
|H_{A}(u,t)|=|H_{A}(u,t^{\prime})|
for all
t^{\prime}\equiv t\pmod{p^{n-1}}
.

Now we prove the divisibility property for a set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, which is useful in the following sections.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. If
(a,p^{i_{1}}),(0,p^{i_{2}}),\dots,(0,p^{i_{s}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{s}\leq n-1
, then
p^{s}\mid|A|
.

Proof.

For any
2\leq r\leq s
,
v\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{r}}-1]
and
k\in[0,p-1]
, define

S(r,v,k)=\{u\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{r-1}}-1]:u[i]=v[i]\textup{ for }i\in[0,n-2-i_{r}]\textup{ and }u[n-1-i_{r}]=k\}.

Claim:
For any
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
v\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{r}}-1]
and
k\in[0,p-1]
, we have

\cup_{u\in S(r,v,k)}\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}H_{A}((a,p^{i_{r-1}}),up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1})=H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1}).

If
(x,y)\in\cup_{u\in S(r,v,k)}\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}H_{A}((a,p^{i_{r-1}}),up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1})
, then we have

axp^{n-1}+yp^{i_{r-1}}\equiv up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1}\pmod{p^{n}},

which leads to
y\equiv u\pmod{p^{n-1-i_{r-1}}}.
By the definition of
S(r,v,k)
,

yp^{i_{r}}\equiv vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1}\pmod{p^{n}},

then
(x,y)\in H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\cup_{u\in S(r,v,k)}\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}H_{A}((a,p^{i_{r-1}}),up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1})\subseteq H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1}).

(1)

If
(x,y)\in H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1})
, then we have

yp^{i_{r}}\equiv vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1}\pmod{p^{n}},

which leads to
y\equiv v+kp^{n-1-i_{r}}\pmod{p^{n-i_{r}}}
. Then

axp^{n-1}+yp^{i_{r-1}}\equiv up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1}\pmod{p^{n}}

for some
u\in S(r,v,k)
and
j\in[0,p-1]
. Hence
(x,y)\in\cup_{u\in S(r,v,k)}\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}H_{A}((a,p^{i_{r-1}}),up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\cup_{u\in S(r,v,k)}\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}H_{A}((a,p^{i_{r-1}}),up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1})\supseteq H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1}).

(2)

Combining (
1
) and (
2
), we have

\cup_{u\in S(r,v,k)}\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}H_{A}((a,p^{i_{r-1}}),up^{i_{r-1}}+jp^{n-1})=H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+kp^{n-1}).

This ends the proof of the claim.

Since
(a,p^{i_{1}}),(0,p^{i_{2}}),\dots,(0,p^{i_{s}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle|H_{A}((a,p^{i_{1}}),up^{i_{1}}+jp^{n-1})|=|H_{A}((a,p^{i_{1}}),up^{i_{1}}+j^{\prime}p^{n-1})|,

\displaystyle|H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+jp^{n-1})|=|H_{A}((0,p^{i_{r}}),vp^{i_{r}}+j^{\prime}p^{n-1})|

for all
u\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{1}}-1]
,
2\leq r\leq s
,
v\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{r}}-1]
and
0\leq j\neq j^{\prime}\leq p-1
. For any
(x,y)\in A
, we have
yp^{i_{s}}\equiv v_{s}p^{i_{s}}+jp^{n-1}
for some
v_{s}\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{s}}-1],

j\in[0,p-1]
. Hence

A=\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}\cup_{v_{s}\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{s}}-1]}H_{A}((0,p^{i_{s}}),v_{s}p^{i_{s}}+jp^{n-1}).

Then we have

\displaystyle|A|=

\displaystyle|\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}\cup_{v_{s}\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{s}}-1]}H_{A}((0,p^{i_{s}}),v_{s}p^{i_{s}}+jp^{n-1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p|\cup_{v_{s}\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{s}}-1]}H_{A}((0,p^{i_{s}}),v_{s}p^{i_{s}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p|\cup_{j=0}^{p-1}\cup_{v_{s-1}\in S(s,v_{s},0)}\cup_{v_{s}\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{s}}-1]}H_{A}((0,p^{i_{s-1}}),v_{s-1}p^{i_{s-1}}+jp^{n-1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{2}|\cup_{v_{s-1}\in S(s,v_{s},0)}\cup_{v_{s}\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{s}}-1]}H_{A}((0,p^{i_{s-1}}),v_{s-1}p^{i_{s-1}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{s}|\cup_{v_{1}\in S(2,v_{2},0)}\dots\cup_{v_{s-1}\in S(s,v_{s},0)}\cup_{v_{s}\in[0,p^{n-1-i_{s}}-1]}H_{A}((a,p^{i_{1}}),v_{1}p^{i_{1}})|.

Hence
p^{s}\mid|A|.

∎

For
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
,
we define the relation
u\sim v
if there exists
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}^{*}
such that
u=rv
. Then the equivalent classes in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
by
\sim
are

(1,0),(c,p^{i})\text{ for all $c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}$ and $i\in[0,n-1]$.}

By Lemma
2.3
, when we study the zero set
\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, we only need to consider the above equivalent classes.

4
Tile
\Rightarrow
Spectral

In this section, we will prove that if
A
tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, then
A
is a spectral set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. By Lemma
2.6
, if
A
is a nontrivial tiling set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, then
|A|\mid p^{n+1}
, hence
|A|=p^{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
be a tiling set. If
|A|=p
, then
A
is a spectral set.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
,
\mathcal{Z}_{A}\neq\emptyset
. Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, define the set

B=\{r(x,y):\ r\in[0,p-1]\}.

Note that
(B-B)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
and
|A|=|B|=p
, by Lemma
2.4
,
(A,B)
is a spectral pair.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
be a tiling set. If
|A|=p^{t}
,
2\leq t\leq n
, then
A
is a spectral set.

Proof.

Suppose
(A,T)
forms a tiling pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, then
|T|=p^{n-t+1}
.
Let
I=\{i\in[0,n-1]:\ (0,p^{i})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}\}
and
J=\{i\in[0,n-1]:\ (0,p^{i})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}\}
.
By Lemma
3.2
, we have
|I|\leq t
and
|J|\leq n-t+1
.
On the other hand,
\mathcal{Z}_{A}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{T}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
, then
|I|+|J|\geq n
. Hence
t-1\leq|I|\leq t
.

If
|I|=t
, define

B=\{\sum_{i\in I}s_{i}(0,p^{i}):s_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
b\neq b^{\prime}\in B
,
b-b^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}(0,p^{i})
, where
r_{i}\in[-p+1,p-1]
.
Note that
\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}(0,p^{i})\sim(0,p^{j})
, where
j=\min\{i\in I:r_{i}\neq 0\}
, then
(B-B)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
. Since
|B|=p^{t}=|A|
, then we have
(A,B)
is a spectral pair.

If
|I|=t-1
, then
|J|=n-t+1
. Suppose
I=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{t-1}\}
and
J=\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n-t+1}\}
, where
0\leq a_{1}<\dots<a_{t-1}\leq n-1
,
0\leq b_{1}<\dots<b_{n-t+1}\leq n-1
and
a_{i}\neq b_{j}
. Now we divide our discussion into three cases.

Case 1: There exist
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
a_{k}
such that
(d,p^{a_{k}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}
and
(c,p^{b_{j}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}
for all
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
b_{j}<a_{k}
.

Define

B=\{s_{0}(d,p^{a_{k}})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-t+1}s_{i}(0,p^{b_{i}}):s_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
b\neq b^{\prime}\in B
,
b-b^{\prime}=r_{0}(d,p^{a_{k}})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-t+1}r_{i}(0,p^{b_{i}})
, where
r_{i}\in[-p+1,p-1]
.
Note that

r_{0}(d,p^{a_{k}})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-t+1}r_{i}(0,p^{b_{i}})\sim\begin{cases}(d,p^{a_{k}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }b_{i}<a_{k};\\
(r_{l}^{-1}r_{0}d,p^{b_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0,\ b_{l}<a_{k},\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i<l;\\
(0,p^{b_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}=0,\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i<l.\end{cases}

Then
(B-B)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{T}
,
hence
B
is a spectrum for
T
, but
|B|=p^{n-t+2}>|T|
, which is a contradiction.

Case 2: There exist
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
b_{k}
such that
(d,p^{b_{k}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
and
(c,p^{a_{j}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
for all
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
a_{j}<b_{k}
.

Define

B=\{s_{0}(d,p^{b_{k}})+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}s_{i}(0,p^{a_{i}}):s_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
b\neq b^{\prime}\in B
,
b-b^{\prime}=r_{0}(d,p^{b_{k}})+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}r_{i}(0,p^{a_{i}})
, where
r_{i}\in[-p+1,p-1]
. Note that

r_{0}(d,p^{b_{k}})+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}r_{i}(0,p^{a_{i}})\sim\begin{cases}(d,p^{b_{k}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }a_{i}<b_{k};\\
(r_{l}^{-1}r_{0}d,p^{a_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0,\ a_{l}<b_{k},\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i<l;\\
(0,p^{a_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}=0,\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }i<l.\end{cases}

Then
(B-B)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
. Since
|B|=p^{t}=|A|
, then we have
(A,B)
is a spectral pair.

Case 3:
\{(c,p^{a_{i}}):c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},i\in[1,t]\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, and
\{(c,p^{b_{i}}):c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},i\in[1,n-t+1]\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{T}
.

If
(1,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}
, define

B=\{s_{0}(1,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-t+1}s_{i}(0,p^{b_{i}}):s_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
b\neq b^{\prime}\in B
,
b-b^{\prime}=r_{0}(1,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-t+1}r_{i}(0,p^{b_{i}})
, where
r_{i}\in[-p+1,p-1]
. Note that

r_{0}(1,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-t+1}r_{i}(0,p^{b_{i}})\sim\begin{cases}(1,0),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\geq 1;\\
(r_{l}^{-1}r_{0},p^{b_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0,\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i<l;\\
(0,p^{b_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}=0,\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i<l.\end{cases}

Then
(B-B)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{T}
,

B
is a spectrum for
T
, but
|B|=p^{n-t+2}>|T|
, which is a contradiction. Hence
(1,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, define

C=\{s_{0}(1,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}s_{i}(0,p^{a_{i}}):s_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
c\neq c^{\prime}\in C
,
c-c^{\prime}=r_{0}(1,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}r_{i}(0,p^{a_{i}})
, where
r_{i}\in[-p+1,p-1]
. Note that

r_{0}(1,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}r_{i}(0,p^{a_{i}})\sim\begin{cases}(1,0),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }i\geq 1;\\
(r_{l}^{-1}r_{0},p^{a_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}\neq 0,\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i<l;\\
(0,p^{a_{l}}),&\textup{ if }r_{0}=0,\ r_{l}\neq 0\text{ and }r_{i}=0\text{ for all }1\leq i<l.\end{cases}

Then
(C-C)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
. Since
|C|=p^{t}=|A|
, we have
(A,C)
is a spectral pair.

We claim that the tiling pair
(A,T)
satisfies at least one of Case 1, Case 2 and Case 3.
Assume that Case 1 and Case 2 fail. By Lemmas
2.6
and
3.2
,
\{(c,p^{a_{i}}):c\in[0,p-1],a_{i}<b_{1}\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
. Since Case 2 fails, then
\{(c,p^{b_{1}}):c\in[0,p-1]\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{T}
. Repeating above argument, we get that Case 3 holds. This completes the proof.
∎

5
Spectral
\Rightarrow
Tile

In this section, we will prove that if
A
is a spectral set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, then
A
tiles
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
.
Note that
\mathcal{Z}_{A}\neq\emptyset
, then
p\mid|A|
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
be a spectral set. If
|A|>p^{n}
, then
A=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
.

Proof.

Suppose
(A,B)
is a spectral pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, then
|A|=|B|>p^{n}
. By pigeonhole principle, there exist
(b,x),(b^{\prime},x)\in B
with
b\neq b^{\prime}
. Then

(b,x)-(b^{\prime},x)\sim(1,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}.

For any
i_{0}\in[0,n-1]
and
c_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
,
x
can be represented as

x=\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{i_{0}\}}x_{i}(0,p^{i})+x_{n}(1,0)+x_{i_{0}}(c_{0},p^{i_{0}}),

where
x_{i}\in[0,p-1]
.
Since
|B|>p^{n}
, by pigeonhole principle, there exist
u,v\in B
such that

\displaystyle u=\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{i_{0}\}}u_{i}(0,p^{i})+u_{n}(1,0)+u_{i_{0}}(c_{0},p^{i_{0}}),

\displaystyle v=\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{i_{0}\}}u_{i}(0,p^{i})+u_{n}(1,0)+v_{i_{0}}(c_{0},p^{i_{0}}),

with
u_{i_{0}}\neq v_{i_{0}}
, then we obtain

u-v\sim(c_{0},p^{i_{0}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}.

Thus,
\mathcal{Z}_{A}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
. By the Fourier inversion formula,
A=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
.
∎

It is easy to see that the set
A=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
is a spectral set. Now we consider the spectral set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
with
|A|\leq p^{n}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
be a spectral set. If
|A|=p
, then
A
is a tiling set.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{Z}_{A}\neq\emptyset
, we divide our discussion into three cases.

Case 1:
(1,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
.

Define

T=\{(0,x):\ x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\}.

For any
i\in[0,n-1]
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-i}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
, if
(0,x)\in H_{T}((c,p^{i}),tp^{i}+jp^{n-1})
, then
xp^{i}\equiv tp^{i}+jp^{n-1}
, which leads to
x\equiv t+jp^{n-1-i}\pmod{p^{n-i}}
. Hence

|H_{T}((c,p^{i}),tp^{i}+jp^{n-1})|=p^{i}

for any
i\in[0,n-1]
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-i}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1].

By Lemma
3.1
,

(c,p^{i})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}\text{ for all }i\in[0,n-1],\ c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Then we have

\mathcal{Z}_{A}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{T}=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}})\backslash\{(0,0)\}.

Note that
|A||T|=p^{n+1}
, by Lemma
2.6
,
(A,T)
is a tiling pair.

Case 2: there exists
s\in[0,n-1]
such that
(0,p^{s})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
.

Define

T=\{(x,\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{n-s-1\}}y_{i}p^{i}:\ x,y_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
j\in[0,p-1]
and
t\in[0,p^{n-1}-1]
, if
(x,\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{n-s-1\}}y_{i}p^{i})\in H_{T}((1,0),t+jp^{n-1})

\displaystyle x\equiv j\pmod{p},

\displaystyle t=0.

Hence

|H_{T}((1,0),t+jp^{n-1})|=\begin{cases}p^{n-1},&\text{ if }t=0;\\
0,&\text{ if }t\neq 0,\end{cases}

for any
j\in[0,p-1]
and
t\in[0,p^{n-1}-1]
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle(1,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}.

(3)

For any
k\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{s\}
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-k}-1]
and

which leads to

\displaystyle cx+y_{n-1-k}\equiv j\pmod{p},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{n-s-1\}}y_{i}p^{i}\equiv t\pmod{p^{n-1-k}}.

Then we have

|H_{T}((c,p^{k}),tp^{k}+jp^{n-1})|=\begin{cases}p^{k+1},&\text{ if }k<s\text{ and }t[n-s-1]=0;\\
0,&\text{ if }k<s\text{ and }t[n-s-1]\neq 0;\\
p^{k},&\text{ if }k>s,\end{cases}

for
k\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{s\}
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-k}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle(c,p^{k})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}\text{ for all }k\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{s\},c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

(4)

For any
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[0,n-s-2]}y_{i}p^{i}\equiv t\pmod{p^{n-s-1}},

\displaystyle cx\equiv j\pmod{p}.

Then we have

|H_{T}((c,p^{s}),tp^{s}+jp^{n-1})|=p^{s}

for all
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-s}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle(c,p^{s})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}\text{ for all }c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}.

(5)

Combining (
3
), (
4
) and (
5
), we have

\mathcal{Z}_{A}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{T}=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}})\backslash\{(0,0)\}.

Note that
|A||T|=p^{n+1}
, by Lemma
2.6
,
(A,T)
is a tiling pair.

Case 3: there exist
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
and
s\in[0,n-1]
such that
(c,p^{s})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
.

Define

T=\{(-c^{-1}y_{n-s-1},\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}y_{i}p^{i}):\ y_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
t\in[0,p^{n-1}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
, if
(-c^{-1}y_{n-s-1},\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}y_{i}p^{i})\in H_{T}((1,0),t+jp^{n-1})
, then

\displaystyle-c^{-1}y_{n-s-1}\equiv j\pmod{p},

\displaystyle t=0.

Hence

|H_{T}((1,0),t+jp^{n-1})|=\begin{cases}p^{n-1},&\text{ if }t=0;\\
0,&\text{ if }t\neq 0,\end{cases}

for any
t\in[0,p^{n-1}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle(1,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}.

(6)

,

,

,

,

and

.

3.1
,

\displaystyle(d,p^{k})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}\text{ for all }k\in[0,n-1]\backslash\{s\},d\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

(7)

For any
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

,

\displaystyle-dc^{-1}y_{n-s-1}+y_{n-s-1}\equiv(1-dc^{-1})y_{n-s-1}\equiv j\pmod{p}.

|H_{T}((d,p^{s}),tp^{s}+jp^{n-1})|=p^{s},

with

,

and

.

3.1
,

\displaystyle(d,p^{s})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}\text{ for all }d\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},d\neq c.

(8)

Combining (

), (
7
) and (

), we have

\mathcal{Z}_{A}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{T}=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}})\backslash\{(0,0)\}.

Note that

, by Lemma
2.6
,

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
be a spectral set. If
|A|=p^{s}
with
2\leq s\leq n
, then
A
is a tiling set.

Proof.

Suppose
(A,B)
is a spectral pair, then
|A|=|B|=p^{s}
. Let
I=\{i\in[0,n-1]:(0,p^{i})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}\}
and
J=\{i\in[0,n-1]:(0,p^{i})\in\mathcal{Z}_{B}\}
.
Since
(B-B)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
and
(A-A)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{B}
, then
|I|\geq s-1
and
|J|\geq s-1.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have
s-1\leq|I|\leq s
and
s-1\leq|J|\leq s.

Case 1:
|I|=s
.

Define

T=\{(x,\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash I}y_{i}p^{n-1-i}):x,y_{i}\in[0,p-1]\}.

For any
t\in[0,p^{n-1}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
, if
(x,\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash I}y_{i}p^{n-1-i})\in H_{T}((1,0),t+jp^{n-1})
, then

\displaystyle x\equiv j\pmod{p},

\displaystyle t=0.

Hence

|H_{T}((1,0),t+jp^{n-1})|=\begin{cases}p^{n-s},&\text{ if }t=0;\\
0,&\text{ if }t\neq 0,\end{cases}

for any
j\in[0,p-1]
and
t\in[0,p^{n-1}-1]
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle(1,0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}.

(9)

For any
k\in I
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-k}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
, if
(x,\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash I}y_{i}p^{n-1-i})\in H_{T}((c,p^{k}),tp^{k}+jp^{n-1})
, then

xcp^{n-1}+(\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash I}y_{i}p^{n-1-i})p^{k}\equiv tp^{k}+jp^{n-1}\pmod{p^{n}},

which leads to

\displaystyle xc\equiv j\pmod{p},

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash I}y_{i}p^{n-1-i}\equiv t\pmod{p^{n-1-k}}.

Then we have

|H_{T}((c,p^{k}),tp^{k}+jp^{n-1})|=\begin{cases}p^{t_{0}},&\text{ if }t[i]=0\text{ for }i<n-1-k,n-1-i\in I;\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

for
k\in I
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-k}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
, where
t_{0}=|\{i\in[0,n-1]\backslash I:i<k\}|
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle(c,p^{k})\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}\text{ for all }k\in I,\ c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}.

(10)

For any
k\in[0,n-1]\backslash I
,
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
t\in[0,p^{n-1-k}-1]
and
j\in[0,p-1]
, if

Note that

\sum_{i\in I}d_{i}(0,p^{i})+d_{i_{0}}(c_{0},p^{i_{0}})\sim\begin{cases}(c_{0},p^{i_{0}}),&\text{ if }d_{i_{0}}\neq 0\text{ and }d_{i}=0\text{ for all }i<i_{0};\\
(d_{k}^{-1}d_{i_{0}}c_{0},p^{k}),&\text{ if }d_{i_{0}}\neq 0,\ d_{k}\neq 0,\ k<i_{0}\text{ and }d_{i}=0\text{ for all }i<k;\\
(0,p^{k}),&\textup{ if }d_{i_{0}}=0,\ d_{k}\neq 0\text{ and }d_{i}=0\text{ for all }i<k.\end{cases}

By (
21
) and (
22
), we have
(D-D)\backslash\{(0,0)\}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, then
D
is a spectrum for
A
, but
|D|=p^{n}>|A|
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

6
Conclusion

From the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we can see that the divisibility property and equi-distributed property in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
play an important role. In
[
25
]
, the author had proved that the equi-distributed property holds in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. Actually, the divisibility property can also be generalized to the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
m,n
be integers with
m\leq n
. Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. If
(a,p^{i_{1}}),\ (0,p^{i_{2}}),\\
\dots,(0,p^{i_{s}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
for some
a\in p^{m-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
and
0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{s}\leq n-1
, then
p^{s}\mid|A|
.

The proof of Lemma
6.1
is similar with the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we omit the details. In order to study the zero set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, we define an equivalence relation. For
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
,
we define the relation
u\sim v
if there exists
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}^{*}
such that
u=rv
. Then the equivalent classes in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
by
\sim
are

(p^{i},0),(c_{i}p^{i},p^{j})\text{ for all $i\in[0,m-1]$, $j\in[0,n-1]$ and $c_{i}\in[0,p^{m-i}-1]$ with $\gcd(c_{i},p)=1$.}

Note that the first zero in Lemma
6.1
is
(a,p^{i_{1}})
, where
a\in p^{m-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
, and there are much more equivalent classes in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, it seems that our method does not work for the general case
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. It would be interesting to consider the Fuglede’s conjecture in
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
.

Laser-assisted charged Higgs boson decay in Two Higgs Doublet Model - type II
S. Mouslih, M. Jakha, S. El Asri, S. Taj, B. Manaut, R. Benbrik, E Siher

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the charged Higgs boson decay in the context of the type-II two-Higgs-doublet model in the presence of a circularly polarized electromagnetic field of laser radiation. The calculations are performed by adopting the Furry picture approach of non-perturbative interactions with the external electromagnetic field. Using the method of exact solutions for charged particles states in the presence of a circularly polarized electromagnetic wave field and evaluating the S S -matrix elements, an exact analytic expression is derived for the decay width of leptonic, hadronic and bosonic decay modes. The branching ratios of different decay modes with multiple photon emission and absorption from the laser beam are analyzed and found to be dramatically modified in the region of superstrong fields. The dependencies of the decay width on the laser field strength and frequency are also examined. The results obtained may be interesting for future experimental and theoretical investigations. Keywords: Laser-assisted processes, Charged Higgs decay, 2HDM-type II, Branching ratio, Decay width

2.
Outline of the theory

We consider the decay of the charged Higgs boson
H^{+}
in the presence of a laser field. Since we have three decay modes, this section is divided into three subsections. Each one is dedicated to the calculation of the decay width for each decay mode. The calculation is detailed for the first decay mode, and elsewhere we limit ourselves to the necessary and give the final expressions. We will start with the leptonic decay, then the hadronic and finally the bosonic decay mode.

2.1.
Leptonic decay width of the charged Higgs in the presence of a laser field

We begin by considering the leptonic decay process of charged Higgs boson
H^{+}
, with 4-momentum
k_{H^{+}}
and mass
M_{H^{+}}
, into an antilepton (
\ell^{+}
) and a corresponding neutrino (
\nu_{\ell}
) in a circularly polarized electromagnetic (EM) field. It can be schematized as follows:

H^{+}(k_{H^{+}})\longrightarrow\ell^{+}(p_{1})+\nu_{\ell}(p_{2}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \ell\equiv(e,\mu,\tau),

(1)

where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are the free 4-momenta of antilepton and neutrino. The corresponding Feynman diagram is presented in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Feynman diagram for the leptonic decay of the charged Higgs boson (
H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
).

We take the laser field as a circularly polarized monochromatic EM field, which is classically described by the following 4-potential

\displaystyle A^{\mu}(\phi)=a^{\mu}_{1}\cos(\phi)+a^{\mu}_{2}\sin(\phi),

(2)

where
\phi=(k.x)
is the phase of the laser field.
k=(\omega,\textbf{k})
is the wave 4-vector and
\omega
is the laser frequency. The 4-amplitudes
a^{\mu}_{1}=|\textbf{a}|(0,1,0,0)
and
a^{\mu}_{2}=|\textbf{a}|(0,0,1,0)
are orthogonal and equal in magnitude, which implies
(a_{1}.a_{2})=0
and
a_{1}^{2}=a_{2}^{2}=a^{2}=-|\textbf{a}|^{2}=-(\xi_{0}/\omega)^{2}
where
\xi_{0}
is the electric field strength. We assume that this 4-potential satisfies the Lorentz gauge condition,
k_{\mu}A^{\mu}=0
, which means
(k.a_{1})=(k.a_{2})=0
, indicating that the wave vector
k
is chosen to be along the
z
-axis.

The equivalent low-order
S
-matrix element for the process (
1
) in 2HDM type II can be written as follows
[
31
]
:

S_{fi}=\frac{igm_{\ell}}{2\sqrt{2}M_{W}}\tan(\beta)\int d^{4}x\overline{\psi}_{\nu_{\ell}}(x,p_{2})(1+\gamma^{5})\psi_{\ell^{+}}(x,p_{1})\overline{\phi}_{H}(x,k_{H^{+}}),

(3)

where
g
is the electroweak coupling constant.
m_{\ell}
and
M_{W}
are the mass of the outgoing lepton and
W
-boson. In order to take into account the interaction of the charged Higgs (0-spin particle) with the laser field, its wave function obeys the Klein-Gordon equation for bosons of spin zero. Hence, the corresponding Volkov solutions are given, normalized to the volume
V
, as follows
[
32
]
:

\phi_{H}(x,k_{H^{+}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2QV}}\times e^{iS(q,x)},

(4)

where
Q
is the total energy of
H^{+}
in the presence of the laser field, and

S(q,x)=q.x-\frac{e(a_{1}.k_{H^{+}})}{(k.k_{H^{+}})}\sin(\phi)+\frac{e(a_{2}.k_{H^{+}})}{(k.k_{H^{+}})}\cos(\phi),

(5)

where
e=-|e|<0
is the charge of electron, and the 4-momentum
q
of the dressed
H^{+}
is given by

q_{=}k_{H^{+}}-\frac{e^{2}a^{2}}{2(k.k_{H^{+}})}k.

(6)

The square of
q
reads

q^{2}=M^{*2}_{H^{+}}=M_{H^{+}}^{2}-e^{2}a^{2},

(7)

where
M^{*}_{H^{+}}
is the effective mass of
H^{+}
acquired inside the laser field.

The outgoing antilepton is described by the relativistic Dirac-Volkov functions
[
30
]
normalized to the volume
V
as follows:

\psi_{\ell^{+}}(x,p_{1})=\bigg[1-\frac{e\not{k}\not{A}}{2(k.p_{1})}\bigg]\frac{v(p_{1},s_{1})}{\sqrt{2Q_{1}V}}\times e^{iS(q_{1},x)},

(8)

where

S(q_{1},x)=q_{1}.x-\frac{e(a_{1}.p_{1})}{(k.p_{1})}\sin(\phi)+\frac{e(a_{2}.p_{1})}{(k.p_{1})}\cos(\phi).

(9)

v(p_{1},s_{1})
is the free Dirac spinor for the antilepton with 4-momentum
p_{1}
and spin
s_{1}
satisfying the following relation:
\sum_{s_{1}}v(p_{1},s_{1})\overline{v}(p_{1},s_{1})=\not{p}_{1}+m_{\ell}
. The 4-vector
q_{1}=(Q_{1},\textbf{q}_{1})
is the quasi-momentum, which is related to
p_{1}
by the following relation:

q_{1}=p_{1}-\frac{e^{2}a^{2}}{2(k.p_{1})}k,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ q_{1}^{2}=m^{*2}_{\ell}=m_{\ell}^{2}-e^{2}a^{2},

(10)

where
m^{*}_{\ell}
is the effective mass of the anti-leptons inside the laser field.

The outgoing neutrino, which is electrically neutral, does not interact with the EM field. Its wave function is given by
[
31
]
:

\displaystyle\psi_{\nu_{\ell}}(x,p_{2})=\dfrac{u(p_{2},s_{2})}{\sqrt{2E_{2}V}}\times e^{-ip_{2}.x},

(11)

where
E_{2}=p_{2}^{0}=|\textbf{p}_{2}|
, and
u(p_{2},s_{2})
is the free Dirac spinor satisfying
\sum_{s_{2}}u(p_{2},s_{2})\overline{u}(p_{2},s_{2})=\not{p}_{2}
.

After some algebraic manipulations, we find that
S_{fi}
becomes

\begin{split}S_{fi}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=&\frac{igm_{\ell}}{2\sqrt{2}M_{W}}\frac{\tan(\beta)}{\sqrt{8QQ_{1}E_{2}V^{3}}}\int d^{4}x\overline{u}(p_{2},s_{2})\Big[\Big(1+\gamma^{5}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{e\not{k}\not{A}}{2(k.p_{1})}\Big)\Big]v(p_{1},s_{1}),\\
&\times e^{i(q_{1}+p_{2}-q).x}\times e^{-iz_{\ell}\sin(\phi-\phi_{0})},\end{split}

(12)

where we have used the following transformation:

e^{i(S(q_{1},x)-S(q,x))}=e^{i(q_{1}-q).x}\times e^{-iz_{\ell}\sin(\phi-\phi_{0})},

(13)

with

z_{\ell}=e\sqrt{\bigg(\frac{a_{1}.p_{1}}{k.p_{1}}-\frac{a_{1}.k_{H^{+}}}{k.k_{H^{+}}}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg(\frac{a_{2}.p_{1}}{k.p_{1}}-\frac{a_{2}.k_{H^{+}}}{k.k_{H^{+}}}\bigg)^{2}},

(14)

and

\phi_{0}=\arctan\bigg[\frac{(a_{2}.p_{1})(k.k_{H^{+}})-(a_{2}.k_{H^{+}})(k.p_{1})}{(a_{1}.p_{1})(k.k_{H^{+}})-(a_{1}.k_{H^{+}})(k.p_{1})}\bigg].

(15)

After integration over the space-time and using the transformation that introduces Bessel functions

e^{iz\sin(\phi)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}J_{n}(z)e^{in\phi},

(16)

the
S
-matrix element can be written as a sum of ordinary Bessel functions, such that

S_{fi}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{igm_{\ell}}{2\sqrt{2}M_{W}}\frac{\tan(\beta)}{\sqrt{8QQ_{1}E_{2}V^{3}}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(q_{1}+p_{2}-q-nk)\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}.

(17)

The quantity
\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}=\overline{u}(p_{2},s_{2})\Big[\Big(1+\gamma^{5}\Big)\Big(b_{n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{k}\not{a}_{1}b_{1n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{k}\not{a}_{2}b_{2n}(z_{\ell})\Big)\Big]v(p_{1},s_{1}),

(18)

where
C(p_{1})=e/[2(k.p_{1})]
and the three coefficients
b_{n}(z_{\ell})
,
b_{1n}(z_{\ell})
and
b_{2n}(z_{\ell})
are expressed as a function of ordinary Bessel functions by
[
33
]

\begin{split}&b_{n}(z_{\ell})=J_{n}(z_{\ell})e^{in\phi_{0}},\\
&b_{1n}(z_{\ell})=\frac{1}{2}\big[J_{n+1}(z_{\ell})e^{i(n+1)\phi_{0}}+J_{n-1}(z_{\ell})e^{i(n-1)\phi_{0}}\big],\\
&b_{2n}(z_{\ell})=\frac{1}{2i}\big[J_{n+1}(z_{\ell})e^{i(n+1)\phi_{0}}-J_{n-1}(z_{\ell})e^{i(n-1)\phi_{0}}\big].\\
\end{split}

(19)

z_{\ell}
(
\ell
stands for leptonic) is the argument of Bessel functions already defined in Eq. (
14
) and
n
, called the order, is interpreted as the number of photons exchanged between the laser field and the decay process.

To calculate the partial decay width,
\Gamma(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})
, we multiply the square
S
-matrix element,
|S_{fi}|^{2}
, by the density of final states, and divide it by the time
T
and finally, we have to make the average on the initial spins and the sum on the final ones. Thus, the laser-assisted leptonic decay width is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\Gamma(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}),

(20)

where the individual partial decay width for each
n
,
\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})
, is defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=\frac{g^{2}m^{2}_{\ell}\tan^{2}(\beta)}{64M^{2}_{W}Q}\int\dfrac{d^{3}q_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}Q_{1}}\int\dfrac{d^{3}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}E_{2}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(q_{1}+p_{2}-q-nk)|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2},

(21)

where

\displaystyle|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2}=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\Big|\overline{u}(p_{2},s_{2})\Big[\Big(1+\gamma^{5}\Big)\Big(b_{n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{k}\not{a}_{1}b_{1n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{k}\not{a}_{2}b_{2n}(z_{\ell})\Big)\Big]v(p_{1},s_{1})\Big|^{2}.

(22)

Performing the integration over
d^{3}p_{2}
and using
\delta^{4}(q_{1}+p_{2}-q-nk)=\delta^{0}(Q_{1}+E_{2}-Q-n\omega)\delta^{3}(\textbf{q}_{1}+\textbf{p}_{2}-\textbf{q}-n\textbf{k})
, we find

\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=\frac{g^{2}m^{2}_{\ell}\tan^{2}(\beta)}{64(2\pi)^{2}M^{2}_{W}Q}\int\dfrac{d^{3}q_{1}}{Q_{1}E_{2}}\delta^{0}(Q_{1}+E_{2}-Q-n\omega)|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2},

(23)

with
\textbf{q}_{1}+\textbf{p}_{2}-\textbf{q}-n\textbf{k}=0
. Choosing the rest frame of
H^{+}
and using
d^{3}q_{1}=|\textbf{q}_{1}|Q_{1}dQ_{1}d\Omega_{\ell}
, we get

\begin{split}\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=&\frac{g^{2}m^{2}_{\ell}\tan^{2}(\beta)}{64(2\pi)^{2}M^{2}_{W}Q}\int\frac{|\textbf{q}_{1}|}{E_{2}}|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2}dQ_{1}d\Omega_{\ell}\delta^{0}\bigg(Q_{1}-Q-n\omega\\
&+\sqrt{\Big(n-\frac{e^{2}a^{2}}{2(k.k_{H^{+}})}\Big)^{2}\omega^{2}+Q^{2}_{1}-m^{*2}_{\ell}+2\omega\sqrt{Q^{2}_{1}-m^{*2}_{\ell}}\Big(\frac{e^{2}a^{2}}{2(k.k_{H^{+}})}-n\Big)\cos(\theta)}\bigg),\end{split}

(24)

where we have replaced
E_{2}
by its expression (the square root) to show the
Q_{1}
dependence inside the delta function. The remaining integral over
dQ_{1}
can be solved by using the familiar formula
[
31
]
:

\displaystyle\int dyf(y)\delta(g(y))=\dfrac{f(y)}{|g^{\prime}(y)|}\bigg|_{g(y)=0}.

(25)

Thus, we get

\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell})=\frac{g^{2}m^{2}_{\ell}\tan^{2}(\beta)}{64(2\pi)^{2}M^{2}_{W}Q}\int\frac{|\textbf{q}_{1}||\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2}d\Omega_{\ell}}{Q+n\omega+\frac{Q_{1}\omega\cos(\theta)}{\sqrt{Q_{1}^{2}-m^{*2}_{\ell}}}\Big(n-\frac{e^{2}a^{2}}{2(k.k_{H^{+}})}\Big)},

(26)

where
g^{2}=8G_{F}M_{W}^{2}/\sqrt{2}
, with
G_{F}=(1.166\penalty\ 37\pm 0.000\penalty\ 02)\times 10^{-11}\penalty\ \text{MeV}^{-2}
is the Fermi coupling constant.

The term
|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2}
in (
22
) can be calculated by converting the sums over the spins into traces as follows:

|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2}=\text{Tr}\big[\not{p}_{2}\Lambda^{n}(\not{p}_{1}-m_{\ell})\overline{\Lambda}^{n}\big],

(27)

where

\begin{split}&\Lambda^{n}=\big(1+\gamma^{5}\big)\big(b_{n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{k}\not{a}_{1}b_{1n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{k}\not{a}_{2}b_{2n}(z_{\ell})\big),\\
&\overline{\Lambda}^{n}=\big(1-\gamma^{5}\big)\big(b^{*}_{n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{a}_{1}\not{k}b^{*}_{1n}(z_{\ell})-C(p_{1})\not{a}_{2}\not{k}b^{*}_{2n}(z_{\ell})\big).\end{split}

(28)

The trace calculation is performed with the help of FeynCalc
[
34
]
. Refer to appendix
A
for the detailed and explicit expression of
|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n}_{fi}}|^{2}
.

2.2.
Hadronic decay width of the charged Higgs in the presence of a laser field

In the framework of beyond standard model, we consider the hadronic decay of
H^{+}
into a pair of quarks

H^{+}(k_{H^{+}})\longrightarrow q(p_{1})+\overline{q}^{\prime}(p_{2}),

(29)

where
q\equiv(u,c,t)
and
q^{\prime}\equiv(d,s,b)
. The overall coupling between
H^{+}
and the quarks is defined as follows
[
35
]
:

\text{vertex}-H^{+}\text{-}q\text{-}\overline{q}^{\prime}=\frac{igV_{qq^{\prime}}}{2\sqrt{2}M_{W}}(A+B\gamma^{5}),

(30)

where
A=m_{q^{\prime}}\tan(\beta)+m_{q}\cot(\beta)
and
B=m_{q^{\prime}}\tan(\beta)-m_{q}\cot(\beta)
.
V_{qq^{\prime}}
is the element of the CKM matrix. The lowest-order
S
-matrix element of the hadronic decay of
H^{+}
is described as follows:

S_{fi}(H^{+}\rightarrow q\overline{q}^{\prime})=\frac{igV_{qq^{\prime}}}{2\sqrt{2}M_{W}}\int d^{4}x\overline{\psi}_{q}(x,p_{1})(A+B\gamma^{5})\psi_{\overline{q}^{\prime}}(x,p_{2})\overline{\phi}_{H}(x,k_{H^{+}}),

(31)

where
\psi_{q}
and
\psi_{\overline{q}^{\prime}}
are, respectively, the relativistic Dirac-Volkov functions that describe the quark and antiquark inside the laser field.

To compute the hadronic decay width of
H^{+}
in the presence of an EM field, we follow the same procedure as for the leptonic decay width. We get

\Gamma(H^{+}\rightarrow q\overline{q}^{\prime})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow q\overline{q}^{\prime}),

(32)

where
\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow q\overline{q}^{\prime})
is defined by

\Gamma^{n}(H^{+}\rightarrow q\overline{q}^{\prime})=\frac{G_{F}\sqrt{2}|V_{qq^{\prime}}|^{2}}{16(2\pi)^{2}Q}\int\frac{|\textbf{q}_{1}||\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n,h}_{fi}}|^{2}d\Omega_{q}}{Q+n\omega+\frac{\omega Q_{1}\cos(\theta)}{\sqrt{Q_{1}^{2}-m^{*2}_{q}}}\Big(n-\frac{e^{2}a^{2}}{2(k.k_{H^{+}})}\Big)}.

(33)

The term
|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n,h}_{fi}}|^{2}
(the superscript
h
stands for hadronic and
n
is the number of photons) is reduced to trace calculation as follows:

\begin{split}|\overline{\mathcal{M}^{n,h}_{fi}}|^{2}=&\text{Tr}\Big[(\not{p}_{1}+m_{q})\Big\{\Big(A+B\gamma^{5}\Big)b_{n}(z_{h})+\Big(C(p_{1})\not{a}_{1}\not{k}(A+B\gamma^{5})-C(p_{2})(A+B\gamma^{5})\not{k}\not{a}_{1}\Big)\\
&\times b_{1n}(z_{h})+\Big(C(p_{1})\not{a}_{2}\not{k}(A+B\gamma^{5})-C(p_{2})(A+B\gamma^{5})\not{k}\not{a}_{2}\Big)b_{2n}(z_{h})\Big\}(\not{p}_{2}-m_{q^{\prime}})\\
&\times\Big\{\Big(A-B\gamma^{5}\Big)b^{*}_{n}(z_{h})+\Big(C(p_{1})(A-B\gamma^{5})\not{k}\not{a}_{1}-C(p_{2})\not{a}_{1}\not{k}(A-B\gamma^{5})\Big)b^{*}_{1n}(z_{h})\\
&+\Big(C(p_{1})(A-B\gamma^{5})\not{k}\not{a}_{2}-C(p_{2})\not{a}_{2}\not{k}(A-B\gamma^{5})\Big)b^{*}_{2n}(z_{h})\Big\}\Big],\end{split}

(34)

where
z_{h}
is the argument of Bessel functions. Note here that
C(p_{1})=\eta e/[2(k.p_{1})]
and
C(p_{2})=\eta^{\prime}e/[2(k.p_{2})]
, where the two factors
\eta=-2/3
and
\eta^{\prime}=1/3
are, respectively, due to the fractional charge of up and down quarks. Regarding the sign, it should be noted that we considered
e<0
as the charge of electron. The result of trace (
34
) is included in appendix
A
.

2.3.
Bosonic decay width of the charged Higgs in the presence of a laser field

The bosonic decay mode for the
H^{+}
is depicted as follows:

H^{+}(k_{H^{+}})\longrightarrow W^{+}(p_{W})+\Phi(p_{\Phi}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ (\Phi\equiv h,H,A),

(35)

with
h
,
H
and
A
being the light CP even neutral scalar, the heavy CP even neutral scalar and CP odd neutral pseudoscalar, respectively. The overall couplings between one gauge boson and two Higgs bosons in 2HDM of type II are defined as follows
[
36
]
:

\begin{split}&\text{vertex}\penalty\ H^{+}\text{-}W^{+}\text{-}h=\frac{-ig}{2}\cos(\beta-\alpha)(p_{W}^{\mu}+p_{h}^{\mu}),\\
&\text{vertex}\penalty\ H^{+}\text{-}W^{+}\text{-}H=\frac{-ig}{2}\sin(\beta-\alpha)(p_{W}^{\mu}+p_{H}^{\mu}),\\
&\text{vertex}\penalty\ H^{+}\text{-}W^{+}\text{-}A=\frac{-ig}{2}(p_{W}^{\mu}+p_{A}^{\mu}).\end{split}

(36)

The lowest-order
S
-matrix element for the laser-assisted bosonic decay of the
H^{+}
is as follows:

S_{fi}(H^{+}\rightarrow W^{+}\Phi)=\frac{-ig}{2}g_{{}_{HW\Phi}}\int d^{4}x\overline{\psi}_{\Phi}(x,p_{\Phi})(p_{W}^{\mu}+p_{\Phi}^{\mu})\psi_{W}(x,p_{W})\overline{\phi}_{H}(x,k_{H^{+}}),

(37)

where
g_{{}_{HW\Phi}}=\cos(\beta-\alpha)
,
g_{{}_{HW\Phi}}=\sin(\beta-\alpha)
and
g_{{}_{HW\Phi}}=1
correspond respectively to the decays
H^{+}\rightarrow W^{+}h
,
H^{+}\rightarrow W^{+}H
and
H^{+}\rightarrow W^{+}A
. In order to take into account the interaction of the outgoing electrically charged
W^{+}
-boson (1-spin particle) with the EM field, we will describe it by the following wave function
[
37
,
38
]
:

\psi_{W}(x,p_{W})=\bigg[\textsl{g}_{\mu\nu}-\dfrac{e}{(k.p_{W})}\big(k_{\mu}A_{\nu}-k_{\nu}A_{\mu}\big)-\frac{e^{2}}{2(k.p_{W})^{2}}A^{2}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}\bigg]\frac{\varepsilon^{\nu}(p_{W},\lambda)}{\sqrt{2p^{0}_{W}V}}\times e^{iS(q_{W},x)},

(38)

where
\textsl{g}_{\mu\nu}=\text{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
is the metric tensor of Minkowski space,
\varepsilon^{\nu}(p,\lambda)
is the
W^{+}
-boson polarization 4-vector such that
\sum_{\lambda=1}^{3}\varepsilon_{\mu}(p_{W},\lambda)\varepsilon_{\nu}^{*}(p_{W},\lambda)=-\textsl{g}_{\mu\nu}+p^{W}_{\mu}p^{W}_{\nu}/M_{W}^{2}
, and

S(q_{W},x)=-q_{W}.x+\dfrac{e(a_{1}.p_{W})}{k.p_{W}}\sin(\phi)-\dfrac{e(a_{2}.p_{W})}{k.p_{W}}\cos(\phi),

(39)

with the dressed 4-momentum
q_{W}=(Q_{W},\textbf{q}_{W})
and the effective mass
M_{W}^{*}
that the boson
W^{+}
acquires inside the EM field. For the final neutral scalar bosons, they are described by the following wave function:

4.
Conclusion

In summary, we provided theoretical evidence that the branching ratios of charged Higgs decay can be modified in a large range by applying an appropriate laser field. Using
S
-matrix method, we have performed analytical calculations for charged Higgs decay in the presence of a circularly polarized radiation field. Our main result is shown in Fig.
6
, where the chosen free parameters allow only two channels,
H^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
and
H^{+}\rightarrow t\bar{b}
. It is revealed that
\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
channel, which is the lowest in the absence of a laser, becomes totally dominant in the region of superstrong fields
[10^{12}-10^{13}\penalty\ \text{V/cm}]
. The fact that an appropriate laser field can modify branching ratios is extremely important, especially when dealing with a yet undiscovered particle. Recently, we found that the laser has an unprecedented effect on the branching ratios of vector bosons
W^{-}
and
Z

[
24
,
25
]
. Without forgetting that these results remain purely theoretical and therefore require experimental verification. It is time to redouble our efforts to take advantage of laser technology and implement it into large colliders in parallel with its continuous progress since the 1960s. The intense electromagnetic environment, combined with conventional acceleration, could be a prospective way to increase the collision energy required for detection of the charged Higgs boson.

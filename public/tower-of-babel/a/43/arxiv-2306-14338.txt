Abstract

The case of compactly supported measures ν \nu is also considered. All of this is then applied to (bounded) composition operators C T , ρ : f ↦ f ∘ T C_{T,\rho}\colon f\mapsto f\circ T on L 2 ​ ( ℝ κ , μ ρ ) L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},\mu_{\rho}) with affine symbols T = A + a T=A+a , where d ​ μ ρ ​ ( x ) = ρ ⁡ ( x ) ​ d ​ x \mathrm{d}\mu_{\rho}(x)=\rho(x)\mathrm{d}x , ρ ⁡ ( x ) = ψ ​ ( ‖ x ‖ ) − 1 \rho(x)=\psi(\|x\|)^{-1} , ψ \psi is a continuous positive real valued function and ∥ ⋅ ∥ \|\cdot\| is the Euclidean norm on ℝ κ {\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa} . 2.1.
Connections with the Stieltjes moment problem

The fields of real and complex numbers are denoted by

{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathbb{C}
, respectively. Set
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{0,1,2,3,\ldots\}
. If no
ambiguity arises, the characteristic function of a
subset
Y
of a set
X
is denoted by
\chi_{Y}
. We
write
\mathfrak{B}(X)
for the
\sigma
-algebra of all
Borel subsets of a topological Hausdorff space
X
. A
Borel measure on
X
is said be
compactly
supported
if it vanishes off a compact subset of
X
.
By a
Radon
measure on
X
, we mean a Borel
measure on
X
which is finite on compact sets and
inner regular. The closed support of a Borel measure

\mu
on
X
is denoted by
\supp\mu
(it always
exists for Radon measures). In this paper we consider
mostly finite Borel measures on locally compact
Hausdorff spaces whose all open sets are

\sigma
-compact. As a consequence, such measures are
Radon measures, meaning in particular that they are
regular (see e.g.,
[
29
, Theorem 2.18]
). Given
a real-valued function
\psi
on
{\mathbb{R}}
(or on a
subinterval of
{\mathbb{R}}
) and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},
we denote by

\psi^{(k)}
the
k
th derivative of
\psi
provided
it exists. If
k=1,2
, we also use the notation

\psi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}
.

A sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is said to
be a
Stieltjes moment sequence
if there exists a Borel
measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle a_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}x^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};

(2.1.1)

such
\mu
is called a
representing measure
of

\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. If
\mu
in (
2.1.1
) is
unique, the Stieltjes moment sequence

\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is called
determinate
.
Otherwise, we call it
indeterminate
. It is worth
recalling that each Stieltjes moment sequence which
has a compactly supported representing measure is
determinate (cf.
[
16
]
). We refer the reader to

[
30
,
1
,
16
,
5
,
31
]
for the
foundations of theory of moment problems.

The following lemma shows that the hyperbolic cosine
transform is analytic and that its Taylor’s
coefficients at zero are related to the Stieltjes
moment problem.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Let
\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be the hyperbolic cosine
transform of a Borel measure
\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Then

(i)

\psi
has the power series expansion

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{(2n)!}\,x^{2n},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

(2.1.2)

where
\gamma_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u^{2n}\mathrm{d}\nu(u)<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

(ii)

the derivative
\psi^{\prime}(x)
of
\psi

at
x
is given by

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u\sinh(xu)\mathrm{d}\nu(u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.1.3)

Moreover, the following hold:

(a)

there is only one
Borel measure
\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
satisfying
(
1.1
),

(b)

the sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
(
2.1.2
) is unique and is a determinate
Stieltjes moment sequence with a representing measure

\nu\circ\omega
, where
\omega\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is given by
\omega(u)=\sqrt{u}
for
u\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
(\nu\circ\omega)(\varDelta)=\nu(\omega(\varDelta))
for

\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
.

Proof.

(i) & (ii) Applying
[
29
, Theorem 1.27]
, we
obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\cosh(xu)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}u^{2n}\mathrm{d}\nu(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u^{2n}\mathrm{d}\nu(u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.1.4)

This implies (
2.1.2
). Using similar reasoning
and the oddness of
\sinh,
we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u\sinh(xu)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{(2n+1)!}\,x^{2n+1},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.1.5)

Term-by-term differentiation of the power series in
(
2.1.2
) yields

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{(2n+1)!}x^{2n+1}\overset{\eqref{gru-gru}}{=}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u\sinh(xu)\mathrm{d}\nu(u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

We now prove the “moreover” part. Clearly, by the
uniqueness of Taylor’s coefficients for power series,
the sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
(
2.1.2
) is unique. Applying the measure
transport theorem
[
2
, Theorem 1.6.12]
yields

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u^{2n}\mathrm{d}\nu(u)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}t^{n}\mathrm{d}\nu\circ\omega(t),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Therefore,
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a
Stieltjes moment sequence with a representing measure

\nu\circ\omega
. In view of (
2.1.2
), the
radius of convergence of the power series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{(2n)!}\,x^{n}

is equal to infinity, so by the Cauchy radius formula
(see
[
9
, Theorem III.1.3]
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\frac{\gamma_{n}}{(2n)!}}=0.

This implies that there exists
M\in(0,\infty)
such
that (see Footnote
2
)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}\leqslant M(2n)!,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Hence, by
[
31
, Proposition 1.5]
(or by
Carleman’s criterion
[
31
, Corollary 4.5]
and
Stirling’s approximation theorem

[
32
, Theorem 9.7.1]
), the Stieltjes moment
sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is determinate
(it is also determinate as a Hamburger moment
sequence). This proves (b). It remains to justify (a).
Suppose that
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are Borel measures on

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
satisfying (
1.1
). Then, in view of
the above discussion, the measures
\nu_{1}\circ\omega

and
\nu_{2}\circ\omega
are representing measures of
the determinate Stieltjes moment sequence

\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Thus
\nu_{1}\circ\omega=\nu_{2}\circ\omega
, which implies that

\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Positive definite functions on

*
-semigroups

Let
(S,+,*)
be an abelian

*
-semigroup with a commutative addition
+
,
involution
*
and the zero element denoted by
0
. We
call a function
\psi\colon S\to\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\psi(s_{j}^{*}+s_{k})\lambda_{j}\bar{\lambda}_{k}\geqslant 0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\,\{s_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq S,\,\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{C}.

(2.2.1)

If
\psi(s^{*})=\overline{\psi(s)}
for all

and the inequality in (

) holds for all

,
\{s_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq S
and

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}=0
, then
\psi
is called
conditionally positive definite
.

In Bernstein’s terminology (see

[
6

), positive definite functions
on the abelian

-semigroup
({\mathbb{R}},+,x^{*}=x)
are
called

. Note that if

\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
is exponentially convex,
then either
\psi=0
or
\psi({\mathbb{R}})\subseteq(0,\infty)
. Indeed, substituting

,

and
\lambda_{1}=1
into (

) gives

for all

, so

. If
\psi(x_{0})=0

, then

, p. 69,

]
yields

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\psi(x)=\psi\Big(\Big(\frac{x_{0}}{2}\Big)^{*}+\Big(x-\frac{x_{0}}{2}\Big)\Big)\leqslant\sqrt{\psi(x_{0})}\sqrt{\psi(2x-x_{0})}=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

which shows that
\psi=0
.

We need the following characterizations of

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Let
\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be a function and

the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\psi
is exponentially convex,

(ii)

the function

is

-semigroup

({\mathbb{R}}_{+},+,x^{*}=x)
for every

.

Moreover, if
\psi
is continuous and

\{s_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}

(iii)

the function

(iv)

-semigroup

That the conditions (i) and (ii) are equivalent can

\Rightarrow

\psi
is continuous. Denote by

the

and a sequence

2.4.
The logarithmic derivative

It follows from Theorem
2.3.3
that the
hyperbolic cosine transform
\psi
of a Borel measure

\nu
has at most exponential growth if and only if
the closed support of the measure
\nu
is compact. In
this section we will provide yet another criterion for
the compactness of the closed support of the measure

\nu
formulated in terms of the logarithmic
derivative of the transform
\psi
(see
Theorem
2.4.1
below).

Before stating the aforementioned result, we
define the class
\mathscr{H}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we denote by
\mathscr{H}_{k}
the set of all
entire functions
\varPhi
on
\mathbb{C}
of the form

\displaystyle\varPhi(z)=\alpha_{0}+\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

(2.4.1)

where
\alpha_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
\alpha_{k}>0
and

\alpha_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
for all integers

. Set

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{H}_{k},

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\{\varPhi\in\mathscr{H}\colon\varPhi(0)>0\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\bullet}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\{\psi\in\mathscr{H}\colon\psi^{(k)}(0)>0\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{2\bullet}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big\{\psi\in\mathscr{H}\colon\psi^{(2k)}(0)>0\text{ and }\psi^{(2k+1)}(0)=0\Big\}.

Note that
\varPhi({\mathbb{R}}_{+})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and

\lim_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\ni t\to\infty}\varPhi(t)=\infty
for
every
\varPhi\in\mathscr{H}
. Since
\varPhi\in\mathscr{H}
is uniquely determined by its values on

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
(resp.,
{\mathbb{R}}
), we will also write
\psi\in\mathscr{H}
when
\psi
is a function on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

(resp.,
{\mathbb{R}}
) that has an extension to the entire
function
\widehat{\psi}\in\mathscr{H}
. Clearly

\exp\in\mathscr{H}_{\bullet}
.

Theorem 2.4.1
.

Suppose that
\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is the
hyperbolic cosine transform of a nonzero Borel measure

\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Then

(i)

if

, then
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{2\bullet}
,

(ii)

the limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}
exists in

[0,\infty]
and

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}=\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}=\sup\supp\nu,

(2.4.2)

(iii)

the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\nu
is compactly supported,

(b)

\inf_{b\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(x)\mathrm{e}^{-b|x|}<\infty,

(c)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(\psi^{(2n)}(0)\big)^{1/n}<\infty,

(d)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}<\infty.

Proof.

(i) By Lemma
2.1.1
, the function
\psi

extends uniquely to the entire function
\widehat{\psi}
on
\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

(2.4.3)

where
\gamma_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u^{2n}\mathrm{d}\nu(u)<\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Since
\supp\nu\neq\{0\},

\gamma_{n}>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Indeed, otherwise

\gamma_{k}=0
for some
k\geqslant 1
(the case

k=0
is excluded by the assumption that

\nu\neq 0
), which implies that
\supp\nu=\{0\}
, a contradiction. This shows that

\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{2\bullet}
.

(ii) Set
r=\sup\supp\nu
. We show that

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}\leqslant r.

(2.4.4)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

r<\infty
. Clearly,
r=\max\supp\nu
and
\supp\nu\subseteq[0,r]
. By Lemma
2.1.1
, we
have

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,r]}u\sinh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,r]}u\tanh(x\,u)\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)

\displaystyle\leqslant r\int_{[0,r]}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)

\displaystyle=r\psi(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

which yields (
2.4.4
).

Set
R=\liminf_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}
. By
(
2.1.3
),
R\geqslant 0
. We claim that

\displaystyle\supp\nu\subseteq[0,R].

(2.4.5)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
R<\infty
. Suppose to the contrary that there exits
M>R
such that
\nu((M,\infty))>0
. Take

. We show that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}{\int_{(L,\infty)}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}=1.

(2.4.6)

For this, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{[0,L]}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}{\int_{(M,\infty)}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}=0.

(2.4.7)

That (

following estimates:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,L]}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)\leqslant\nu([0,L])\cosh(Lx),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

\displaystyle\int_{(M,\infty)}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)\geqslant\nu((M,\infty))\cosh(Mx),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\cosh(Lx)}{\cosh(Mx)}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\exp(Lx)}{\exp(Mx)}=0\quad\text{(because $M>L$).}

A similar reasoning may be used to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u\sinh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}{\int_{(L,\infty)}u\sinh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}

\displaystyle=1.

(2.4.8)

Combining (
2.1.3
) with (
2.4.6
) and
(
2.4.8
) yields

\displaystyle R=\liminf_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u\sinh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{(L,\infty)}u\sinh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}{\int_{(L,\infty)}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}

\displaystyle\geqslant L\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{(L,\infty)}\sinh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}{\int_{(L,\infty)}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}

\displaystyle=L\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{(L,\infty)}\tanh(x\,u)\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}{\int_{(L,\infty)}\cosh(x\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)}

\displaystyle\geqslant L\lim_{x\to\infty}\tanh(x\,L)

\displaystyle=L>R,

a contradiction. This proves our claim.

It follows that

\displaystyle r\overset{\eqref{brzusio}}{\leqslant}R=\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}\leqslant\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}\leqslant\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}\overset{\eqref{heliou3}}{\leqslant}r,

which implies that the limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}
exists in

[0,\infty]
and (
2.4.2
) holds.

(iii) That the conditions (a) and (d) are equivalent
follows from (ii). By Theorem
2.3.3
, (a)
implies (b). That (b) implies (a) follows from
Theorems
2.3.1
and
2.3.3
.
Finally, by Lemma
2.1.1
and
Theorem
2.3.3
, (a) and (c) are equivalent
(cf. Remark
2.1.3
). This completes the
proof.
∎

Below, we will formulate a version of
Theorem
2.4.1
suitable for composition
operators considered in this paper.

Theorem 2.4.2
.

Suppose that
\nu
is a nonzero Borel
measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that
\supp\nu\neq\{0\}
and

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\cosh(\sqrt{x}\,u)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)<\infty,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Then
\varphi\in\mathscr{H}_{\bullet}
, the
limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}\sqrt{x}\,\displaystyle{\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi(x)}}

exists in
[0,\infty]
and

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sqrt{x}\,\displaystyle{\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi(x)}}=\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)}\sqrt{x}\,\displaystyle{\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi(x)}}=\frac{1}{2}\sup\supp\nu.

Proof.

Consider the function
\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

defined by
\psi(x)=\varphi(x^{2})
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since the hyperbolic cosine
\cosh
is even, we get

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\cosh(xu)\mathrm{d}\nu(u)<\infty,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

In view of the proof of Theorem
2.4.1
,
the entire function
\widehat{\psi}
on
\mathbb{C}

defined by (
2.4.3
) extends
\psi
and

\widehat{\psi}\in\mathscr{H}_{2\bullet}.

Define the entire function
\widehat{\varphi}
on

\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{(2n)!}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Clearly
\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathscr{H}_{\bullet}.
Moreover

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\widehat{\psi}(\sqrt{x})=\psi(\sqrt{x})=\varphi(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

which means that
\widehat{\varphi}
is a (necessarily
unique) entire function on
\mathbb{C}
extending
\varphi.

Applying Theorem
2.4.1
(ii), we obtain the
remaining conclusions. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Applications to composition
operators

3.1.
Composition operators with affine symbols

In the subsequent sections we will show that the
cosubnormality of a class of bounded composition
operators on
L^{2}
spaces with affine symbols on
the
\kappa
-dimensional Euclidean space is
closely related to the hyperbolic cosine
transform. Let us first establish the necessary
terminology and notation. Given a complex
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we denote by
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear
operators on
\mathcal{H}
. If
S\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
, then

{\mathscr{N}}(S)
and
{\mathscr{R}}(S)
stand for the kernel and
the range of
S
respectively. We say that an
operator
S\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is

\bullet

hyponormal
if
SS^{*}\leqslant S^{*}S
,

\bullet

subnormal
if there exist
a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
and a normal operator

N\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{K}

(isometric embedding) and
Sh=Nh
for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

\bullet

cohyponormal
(resp.
cosubnormal
)
if the adjoint
S^{*}
of
S
is hyponormal (resp. subnormal).

It is well known that subnormal operators are
hyponormal, but not conversely. We refer the reader to

[
10
]
for the foundations of the theory of
subnormal and hyponormal operators.

To give the reader a better insight
into the topic of this section, we
start with a more general context.
First, note that the only hyponormal
composition operator on an
L^{2}
space
over a finite measure space is an
isometric operator (see

[
18
, Lemma 7]
). Since the
measure spaces considered in the
forthcoming paragraphs are finite, we
focus our attention on the issue of
cosubnormality. Denote by
V_{\kappa}

the
\kappa
-dimensional Lebesgue
measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
(
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
). Take a Borel function

\rho\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\to(0,\infty)
and define the Borel
measure
\mu_{\rho}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}

by

\displaystyle\mu_{\rho}(\varDelta)=\int_{\varDelta}\rho(x)\mathrm{d}V_{\kappa}(x),\quad\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}),

(3.1.1)

If
\rho(x)=1
for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, we
write
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa})
in place of

L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
. An affine transformation
T
of

{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
is of the form
T=A+a
, i.e.,

\displaystyle Tx=Ax+a,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},

where
A
is a linear transformation of

{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Note that

\displaystyle T^{n}x=A^{n}x+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A^{j}a,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.1.2)

The identity transformation of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
is
denoted by
I
. The composition operator

C_{T,\rho}
on
L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
is formally
defined by

\displaystyle C_{T,\rho}(f)=f\circ T,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}).

The operator
C_{T,\rho}
is well defined if and only if
A

is invertible (see, e.g., the proof of

[
38
, Theorem 13.1]
), and if this is the case, then

C_{T,\rho}
is densely defined and closed.

Henceforth, we assume that
A
is invertible.

This implies that
T
itself is invertible. The inverse of

T
, denoted by
S
, is of the form
S=A^{-1}-A^{-1}a
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle Sx=A^{-1}x-A^{-1}a,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}.

Now we turn to the class of composition
operators, which we intend to examine in this
section. Let us make the following standing
assumption:

\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to(0,\infty)
is a continuous
even function and
\rho\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\to(0,\infty)
is the density function defined by

\rho(x)=\psi(\|x\|)^{-1},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},

where
\|x\|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{\kappa}^{2}}
for
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\kappa})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.

(3.1.3)

(For simplicity, we do not express explicitly the
dependence of the density
\rho
on the function

\psi
in notation.) Since the measures
V_{\kappa}
and

\mu_{\rho}
are mutually absolutely continuous, it
follows from
[
12
, (1.1)]
that if
(
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.3
) hold, then

C_{T,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
if and only if

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}\frac{\psi(\|T(x)\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}<\infty
, and if
this is the case, then

\displaystyle\|C_{T,\rho}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{|\det A|}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}\frac{\psi(\|T(x)\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}.

(3.1.4)

Recall that if
C_{T,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
,
then
C_{S,1/\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{1/\rho}))
and
(see
[
35
, (UE)]
; see also

[
12
, Remark
4^{\mathrm{o}}
, p. 403]
):

\displaystyle|\det A|C_{T,\rho}^{*}=U_{\rho}^{-1}C_{S,1/\rho}U_{\rho},

(3.1.5)

where
U_{\rho}\colon L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})\to L^{2}(\mu_{1/\rho})
is the unitary isomorphism
defined by
U_{\rho}f=\rho f
for
f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
. It follows from (
3.1.5
)
that
C_{T,\rho}
is cosubnormal if and only if

C_{S,1/\rho}
is subnormal. Using the
change-of-variables theorem (see

[
29
, Theorem 7.26]
) yields

\displaystyle(\mu_{1/\rho}\circ S^{-1})(\Delta)

\displaystyle:=\mu_{1/\rho}(S^{-1}(\Delta))

\displaystyle\hskip 2.58334pt=|\det A|\int_{\varDelta}\frac{\psi(\|T(x)\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1/\rho}(x),\quad\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}),

which implies that the Radon-Nikodym derivative

h_{S,1/\rho}:=\mathrm{d}\mu_{1/\rho}\circ S^{-1}/\mathrm{d}\mu_{1/\rho}
of
\mu_{1/\rho}\circ S^{-1}
with
respect to
\mu_{1/\rho}
is of the form

\displaystyle h_{S,1/\rho}(x)=|\det A|\frac{\psi(\|T(x)\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}.

(3.1.6)

Using the identity (
3.1.2
) and
iterating (
3.1.6
), we see that

\displaystyle h_{S^{n},1/\rho}(x)=|\det A|^{n}\frac{\psi(\|T^{n}(x)\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

where
h_{S^{n},1/\rho}:=\mathrm{d}\mu_{1/\rho}\circ(S^{n})^{-1}/\mathrm{d}\mu_{1/\rho}
. Hence, applying
Lambert’s criterion for subnormality (see

[
22
, Corollary 4]
) to the composition
operator
C_{S,1/\rho}
and using the fact that
the measures
\mu_{1/\rho}
and
V_{\kappa}
are
mutually absolutely continuous, we get:

C_{T,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
is
cosubnormal if and only if

\{\psi(\|T^{n}(x)\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a
Stieltjes moment sequence for
V_{\kappa}
-almost
every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.

(3.1.7)

We need the following stronger version of
(
3.1.7
) (cf.
[
35
, Proposition 2.4]
).

Lemma 3.1.1
.

Suppose that (
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.3
) hold.
A composition operator
C_{T,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
is cosubnormal if and only if

\{\psi(\|T^{n}(x)\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Stieltjes
moment sequence for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.

Proof.

In view of (
3.1.7
), it suffices to prove the
“only if” part. Assume that
C_{T,\rho}
is
cosubnormal. First, note that if
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, then
the set

\displaystyle\varOmega_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}:=\Big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\colon\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\psi(\|T^{i+j}(x)\|)\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{j}\geqslant 0\Big\}

is closed. By the Stieltjes theorem (see

[
5
, Theorem 6.2.5]
) and (
3.1.7
),
we see that
V_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\varOmega_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})=0
. Since the set

{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\varOmega_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is
open, it must be empty, so

\varOmega_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}={\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Hence

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\colon T(x)\in\varOmega_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\}={\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.
Summarizing, we have proved that

\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\psi(\|T^{i+j}(x)\|)\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{j}\geqslant 0
and
\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\psi(\|T^{i+j+1}(x)\|)\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{j}\geqslant 0
,

for all finite sequences
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Combined with the nontrivial part
of the Stieltjes theorem, this shows that for
every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, the sequence

\{\psi(\|T^{n}(x)\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a
Stieltjes moment sequence.
∎

The question of the existence of cohyponormal
composition operators
C_{A+a,\rho}
with a nontrivial
translation part was studied in detail in

[
12
, Sections 4 and 5]
(see also

[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
). Roughly speaking, the
answer is negative in all but one case, when
\|A\|=1
,
a\notin{\mathscr{R}}(I-AA^{*})
and
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\psi^{\prime}(x)/\psi(x)<\infty
. The last inequality is
automatically satisfied if
\psi
is the hyperbolic
cosine transform of a compactly supported Borel
measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
(see Theorem
2.4.1
). If

A=I
, then trivially
{\mathscr{R}}(I-AA^{*})=\{0\}
. This
case is analyzed in Theorems
3.2.2
and

3.3.2
, and Proposition
3.3.3
. If

\|A\|=1
and
a\in{\mathscr{N}}(I-A)\setminus\{0\}
, then

a\notin{\mathscr{R}}(I-AA^{*})
(see (
3.2.9
)). This
issue, in turn, is examined in
Propositions
3.2.4
and
3.2.6
. Finally,
the case when
\|A\|=1
and
a\notin{\mathscr{R}}(I-AA^{*})

is discussed in Proposition
3.2.7

for
\kappa=1
.

3.2.
Cosubnormality
versus
hyperbolic cosine
transform

We begin this section by showing that if a

C_{0}
-semigroup
\{C_{I+ta_{0},\rho}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
of
composition operators induced by translations consists
of cosubnormal operators, where
a_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\{0\}
, then the function
\psi
must be
the hyperbolic cosine transform of a compactly
supported Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, and
vice
versa
. Before doing so, we will prove the following
general fact. It is worth emphasizing here that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}
may
not exist for
\psi\in\mathscr{H}
(see

[
11
]
).

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Suppose that (
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.3
) hold
and either
\psi=\mathrm{const}
or
\psi
is a member
of
\mathscr{H}
such that
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}<\infty
. Then
C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))

for every
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, and the map

{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\ni a\longmapsto C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
is continuous in the strong
operator topology.

Proof.

Set
\varDelta_{\theta}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\colon\|x\|\leqslant\theta\}
for
\theta>0
. Since
\psi
is
monotonically increasing on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\psi^{\prime}(x)}{\psi(x)}}<\infty
, we infer from
[
12
, Lemma 1.1(ii)]

with
\varphi=\psi
and
\tau=0
that for every

\theta>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\varDelta_{\theta}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}\frac{\psi(\|x+a\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}\leqslant\sup_{a\in\varDelta_{\theta}}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}\frac{\psi(\|x\|+\|a\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}\leqslant\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}\frac{\psi(\|x\|+\theta)}{\psi(\|x\|)}<\infty.

This together with (
3.1.4
) implies that

C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
for every

a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, and

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\varDelta_{\theta}}\|C_{I+a,\rho}\|<\infty,\quad\theta>0.

(3.2.1)

Next, we show that
\lim_{h\to 0}C_{I+b+h,\rho}f=C_{I+b,\rho}f
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
and

b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Fix
b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Let

f\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous
function with compact support. Let
r>0
be such that

\varDelta_{r}
contains the support of
f
. Set
R=r+\|b\|+1
. Observe that
f
is uniformly
continuous. Therefore, for
\varepsilon>0
, there
exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\colon\|x-y\|\leqslant\delta\implies|f(x)-f(y)|\leqslant\varepsilon.

(3.2.2)

Then

\displaystyle\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\varDelta_{R}\;\;\forall h\in\varDelta_{\delta}\colon f(x+b+h)=0.

(3.2.3)

It follows that

\displaystyle\|C_{I+b+h,\rho}f-C_{I+b,\rho}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}|f(x+b+h)-f(x+b)|^{2}\frac{1}{\psi(\|x\|)}\mathrm{d}V_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip 4.30554pt\hskip-7.3194pt\overset{\eqref{jak-num}}{=}\int_{\varDelta_{R}}|f(x+b+h)-f(x+b)|^{2}\frac{1}{\psi(\|x\|)}\mathrm{d}V_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle\hskip-3.87495pt\overset{\eqref{jak-num-0}}{\leqslant}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{\varDelta_{R}}\frac{1}{\psi(\|x\|)}\mathrm{d}V_{\kappa}(x),\quad h\in\varDelta_{\delta},

so
\lim_{h\to 0}C_{I+b+h,\rho}f=C_{I+b,\rho}f

for every continuous function
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support. Using (
3.2.1
) and
the fact that continuous complex functions on

{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
with compact support are dense in

L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
(see
[
29
, Theorem 3.14]
), we
conclude that the map
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\ni a\longmapsto C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
is continuous
in the strong operator topology. This completes the
proof.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.2.2
.

Suppose that (
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.3
) hold
and
a_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\{0\}
. Then the
following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

C_{I+ta_{0},\rho}
is a bounded cosubnormal
operator on
L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and

\lim_{t\to 0+}C_{I+ta_{0},\rho}=C_{I,\rho}
in the strong
operator topology,

(ii)

\psi
is the hyperbolic cosine transform of a
compactly supported Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Moreover, if (ii) holds, then
C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
and
C_{I+a,\rho}
is
cosubnormal for every
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, and the
map
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\ni a\longmapsto C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
is continuous in the strong
operator topology.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Set
\varLambda_{t}=C_{I+ta_{0},\rho}
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. It is easily seen
that
\varLambda_{0}
is the identity operator on

L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
and
\varLambda_{s+t}=\varLambda_{s}\varLambda_{t}
for all
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Hence, by the continuity hypothesis,

\{\varLambda_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup.
It follows from
[
25
, Theorem 1.2.2]
that there
exist
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
M\in[1,\infty)
such
that

\displaystyle\|\varLambda_{t}\|^{2}\leqslant M\mathrm{e}^{\omega t},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

(3.2.4)

By (
3.1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\varLambda_{t}\|^{2}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}\frac{\psi(\|x+ta_{0}\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

This together with (
3.2.4
) implies that

\displaystyle\psi(\|x+ta_{0}\|)\leqslant M\psi(\|x\|)\mathrm{e}^{\omega t},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

(3.2.5)

Substituting
x=0
and
t=\frac{s}{\|a_{0}\|}

into (
3.2.5
), we see that

\displaystyle\psi(s)\leqslant M_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{1}s},\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

where
M_{1}=M\psi(0)
and

\omega_{1}=\frac{\omega}{\|a_{0}\|}
. Since
\psi

is even, we have

\displaystyle\psi(s)\leqslant M_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\omega_{1}|s|},\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(3.2.6)

It follows from (
3.1.2
) and
Lemma
3.1.1
applied to
T=I+ta_{0}
that the
sequence
\{\psi(\|x+nta_{0}\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
a Stieltjes moment sequence for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. In particular,
substituting
x=\frac{\xi}{\|a_{0}\|}a_{0}
and
t=\frac{s}{\|a_{0}\|}
and using the assumption that

\psi
is even, we deduce that for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}

and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the sequence
\{\psi(\xi+ns)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Stieltjes moment sequence,
which as such is positive definite on the

*
-semigroup
(\mathbb{Z}_{+},+,n^{*}=n)
. Applying
Lemma
2.2.1
, w conclude that
\psi

is exponentially convex. Hence, by (
3.2.6
) and
Theorem
2.3.3
,
\psi
is the hyperbolic
cosine transform of a compactly supported Borel
measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Suppose that
\psi
is the hyperbolic
cosine transform of a compactly supported Borel measure
\nu

on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Since
\psi\neq 0
,
\nu\neq 0
. If
\supp{\nu}=\{0\}
, then
\psi=\mathrm{const}
. In turn, if
\supp{\nu}\neq\{0\}
, then, by Theorem
2.4.1
(i),
\psi\in\mathscr{H}
. It follows from Theorem
2.4.1
(iii)
and Lemma
3.2.1
that
C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
for every
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, and
the map
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\ni a\longmapsto C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
is continuous in the strong operator
topology. In particular,
\{C_{I+ta_{0},\rho}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup.

Next, we will show that
C_{I+a,\rho}
is cosubnormal
for every
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Fix
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\{0\}
(the case
a=0
is
trivial). Let
\varphi_{\xi\eta}
be as in
Theorem
2.2.3
. Denote by
P
the orthogonal
projection of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
onto
\langle a\rangle
,
the one-dimensional span of
\{a\}
. Let
P^{\perp}

stand for the orthogonal projection of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}

onto
\langle a\rangle^{\perp}
. Clearly,

P^{\perp}=I-P
and
Px=s_{x}a
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, where
s_{x}=\frac{\langle x,a\rangle}{\|a\|^{2}}
. Fix
u\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
and
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
. Then

\displaystyle\cosh(u\|x+na\|)

\displaystyle=\cosh\Big(\sqrt{\|ua\|^{2}(n+s_{x})^{2}+\|uP^{\perp}x\|^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\xi\eta}(n+s_{x}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

where
\xi=\|ua\|^{2}
and
\eta=\|uP^{\perp}x\|^{2}
.
Hence, by Theorem
2.2.3
, we have

\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\cosh(u\|x+(n_{i}+n_{j}+\varepsilon)a\|)\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{j}\\
=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\varphi_{\xi\eta}\Big(\Big(n_{i}+\frac{s_{x}+\varepsilon}{2}\Big)+\Big(n_{j}+\frac{s_{x}+\varepsilon}{2}\Big)\Big)\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{j}\geqslant 0,

for all finite sequences

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and

\{n_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Thus, the
sequence
\{\cosh(u\|x+(n+\varepsilon)a\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is positive definite on the

*
-semigroup
(\mathbb{Z}_{+},+,n^{*}=n)
for
\varepsilon=0,1

and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. In view of (
1.1
), the
sequence
\{\psi(\|x+(n+\varepsilon)a\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is positive definite on the

*
-semigroup
(\mathbb{Z}_{+},+,n^{*}=n)
for

\varepsilon=0,1
. By the Stieltjes theorem (see

[
5
, Theorem 6.2.5]
), the sequence

\{\psi(\|x+na\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Stieltjes
moment sequence for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.
Combined with Lemma
3.1.1
, this implies that

C_{I+a,\rho}
is cosubnormal. This completes the
proof.
∎

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) of

Proposition 3.2.4
.

Suppose that (
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.3
) hold. Assume
also that
a_{0}\in{\mathscr{N}}(I-A)\backslash\{0\}
,

C_{A+ta_{0},\rho}
is a bounded cosubnormal operator on

L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and there exists

\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\varepsilon}\|C_{A+ta_{0},\rho}\|<\infty.

(3.2.7)

Then
\psi
is the hyperbolic cosine transform
of a compactly supported Borel measure on

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Proof.

Since the space
{\mathscr{N}}(I-A)
is unitarily
equivalent to
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa_{1}}
for some
1\leqslant\kappa_{1}\leqslant\kappa
, we can consider the
composition operator
C_{I_{1}+ta_{0},\rho_{1}}
on

L^{2}(\mu_{\rho_{1}})
, where
I_{1}
is the identity
transformation of
{\mathscr{N}}(I-A)
,

\rho_{1}=\rho|_{{\mathscr{N}}(I-A)}
and
\mu_{\rho_{1}}
is
the Borel measure defined by (
3.1.1
) with

{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa_{1}}
in place of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1.

C_{I_{1}+ta_{0},\rho_{1}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho_{1}}))
for every

.

Indeed, by (

\displaystyle\|C_{A+ta_{0},\rho}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{|\det A|}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}}\frac{\psi(\|Ax+ta_{0}\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{\mathscr{N}}(I-A)}\frac{\psi(\|(I_{1}+ta_{0})(x)\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}\leqslant|\det A|\|C_{A+ta_{0},\rho}\|^{2}<\infty,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

(3.2.8)

Hence, by (
3.1.4
),
C_{I_{1}+ta_{0},\rho_{1}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho_{1}}))
for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Step 2.

C_{I_{1}+ta_{0},\rho_{1}}
is
cosubnormal for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Indeed, by Lemma
3.1.1
applied to

,

\{\psi(\|(A+ta_{0})^{n}(x)\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
a Stieltjes moment sequence for every

\{\psi(\|(I_{1}+ta_{0})^{n}(x)\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

. Applying Step 1 and

, we conclude that

is cosubnormal for every

.

Step 3.

\lim_{t\to 0+}C_{I_{1}+ta_{0},\rho_{1}}=C_{I_{1},\rho_{1}}
in the strong operator topology.

Indeed, arguing as in the proof of Lemma
3.2.1
,
we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0+}C_{I_{1}+ha_{0},\rho_{1}}f=C_{I_{1},\rho_{1}}f

for every continuous function

with compact support. It follows from (

),
(
3.2.7
) and (
3.2.8
) that

Finally, using Steps 1, 2 and 3, we can apply
implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) of
Theorem
3.2.2
to
\{C_{I_{1}+ta_{0},\rho_{1}}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Lack of cosubnormality

It follows from Corollary
3.2.3
that if

\psi\in\mathscr{H}\cup\{\mathrm{const}\}
satisfies
the assumptions of this corollary, then
\psi
is not
the hyperbolic cosine transform of a compactly
supported Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
if and only if
there exists
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\{0\}
such
that
C_{I+a,\rho}
is not cosubnormal. In fact, as
shown below, the set of all
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
for
which
C_{I+a,\rho}
is not cosubnormal is open.

Lemma 3.3.1
.

Suppose that (
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.3
) hold
and for every
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
,
C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
. Then the set

\displaystyle\varXi_{\rho}:=\Big\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\colon C_{I+a,\rho}\text{ is not cosubnormal}\Big\}

is open.

Proof.

It is enough to show that the complement of

\varXi_{\rho}
is closed. Let
a_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.
Take a sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
such that
C_{I+a_{k},\rho}
is
cosubnormal for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}=a_{0}
. By Lemma
3.1.1
, the sequence

\{\psi(\|x+na_{k}\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Stieltjes
moment sequence for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
and
every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since the class of Stieltjes
moment sequences is closed in the topology of
pointwise convergence (see

[
5
, Theorem 6.2.5]
) and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\psi(\|x+na_{k}\|)=\psi(\|x+na_{0}\|),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

we see that the sequence

\{\psi(\|x+na_{0}\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Stieltjes
moment sequence for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Using
Lemma
3.1.1
again, we conclude that

C_{I+a_{0},\rho}
is cosubnormal.
∎

For certain functions
\psi
, the set
\varXi_{\rho}
may
contain a punctured disk centered at 0. The following theorem
does not seem to follow from Lemma
3.3.1
.

Theorem 3.3.2

It follows from Lemma
3.2.1
that
C_{I+a,\rho}\in\boldsymbol{B}(L^{2}(\mu_{\rho}))
for every

. It is easy to verify that the function

5

and

. Substituting

is convex. It
suffices to show that
(\log\psi)^{\prime\prime}\geqslant 0
, or equivalently that

\displaystyle\varTheta:=\psi^{\prime\prime}\psi-\psi^{\prime 2}\geqslant 0.

Straightforward computations yield

\displaystyle\varTheta(x)=\cosh(x)\Big(2\sin(x)\tanh(x)+\delta\Big(1-\frac{\cos(x)}{\cosh(x)}\Big)\Big),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since the function
\varTheta
is even, we
deduce that
\log\psi
is convex if and only if

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varTheta}(x):=2\sin(x)\tanh(x)+\delta\Big(1-\frac{\cos(x)}{\cosh(x)}\Big)\geqslant 0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

(3.3.2)

Note that

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\cos(x)}{\cosh(x)}\geqslant 0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varTheta}(x)\geqslant 0,\quad x\in[0,\pi].

(3.3.3)

Since
0\leqslant\tanh(x)\leqslant 1
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, we see that
2\sin(x)\tanh(x)\geqslant-2

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, and hence

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varTheta}(x)\overset{\eqref{fdse}}{\geqslant}-2+\delta\Big(1-\frac{\cos(x)}{\cosh(x)}\Big),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

(3.3.4)

It follows from
\delta\geqslant\frac{2\cosh\pi}{\cosh\pi-1}
that
\delta>2
and

\displaystyle\cosh(x)\geqslant\cosh\pi\geqslant\frac{\delta}{\delta-2}\geqslant\frac{\delta}{\delta-2}\cos(x),\quad x\in[\pi,\infty).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\cosh(x)-\frac{\delta}{\delta-2}\cos(x)\geqslant 0,\quad x\in[\pi,\infty),

which together with (
3.3.4
) yields

\widetilde{\varTheta}(x)\geqslant 0
for all
x\in[\pi,\infty)
. Combined with (
3.3.3
), this
implies (
3.3.2
), which is equivalent to the
convexity of
\log\psi
.

Now, using
[
12
, Corollary 5.3]
(with

\varphi
as in the proof of Theorem
3.3.2
),
we conclude that the operator
C_{I+a,\rho}
is
cohyponormal for every
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. This
together with Theorem
3.3.2
applied to
k=4

completes the proof.
∎

3.4.
Final remarks

To relate our main result Theorem
3.2.2
to the
Embry-Lambert theorem (see
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
),
let us observe that under the assumptions (
3.1.1
) and
(
3.1.3
), the composition operator
C_{T,\rho}
is
unitarily equivalent to the operator
\widetilde{C}_{T,\psi}

on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa})
defined by

\displaystyle(\widetilde{C}_{T,\psi}f)(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\psi(\|Tx\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}}\,f(Tx),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}).

Indeed, this follows from the identity

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{T,\psi}=W_{\psi}^{-1}C_{T,\rho}W_{\psi},

where
W_{\psi}\colon L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa})\to L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})

is the unitary isomorphism defined by

(W_{\psi}f)(x)=\sqrt{\psi(\|x\|)}\,f(x)
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
and
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa})
. The adjoint

\widetilde{C}_{T,\psi}^{*}
of
\widetilde{C}_{T,\psi}
takes
the following form

\displaystyle(\widetilde{C}_{T,\psi}^{*}f)(x)=\frac{1}{|\det A|}\sqrt{\frac{\psi(\|x\|)}{\psi(\|T^{-1}x\|)}}\,f(T^{-1}x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}).

In particular, if
A=I
, then

\displaystyle(\widetilde{C}_{I+a,\psi}f)(x)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{\psi(x+a)}{\psi(x)}}\,f(x+a)&\text{if
$\kappa=1$,}\\[8.61108pt]
\sqrt{\frac{\psi(\|x+a\|)}{\psi(\|x\|)}}\,f(x+a)&\text{if
$\kappa>1$,}\end{cases}\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}),a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},

and

\displaystyle(\widetilde{S}_{a}f)(x)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{\psi(x)}{\psi(x-a)}}\,f(x-a)&\text{if $\kappa=1$,}\\[8.61108pt]
\sqrt{\frac{\psi(\|x\|)}{\psi(\|x-a\|)}}\,f(x-a)&\text{if
$\kappa>1$,}\end{cases}\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}),a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa},

where
\widetilde{S}_{a}:=\widetilde{C}_{I+a,\psi}^{*}
for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. In case
\kappa=1
, this resembles the weighted
translation semigroups on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
studied by Embry and
Lambert in
[
13
,
14
]
(see also

[
28
]
for the multivariable case). More precisely,
for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
is an invariant
subspace for
\widetilde{S}_{t}
, and the family
\{S_{t}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
given by
S_{t}:=\widetilde{S}_{t}|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})}
is the
weighted translation semigroup with symbol

\phi:=\sqrt{\psi}
(notation and terminology as in

[
14
]
).

Since the adjoint of a
C_{0}
-semigroup is a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(see
[
25
, Corollary 3.8 and Theorem 10.4]
),
Theorem
3.2.2
takes the following form.

Theorem 3.4.1
.

Suppose that
\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to(0,\infty)
is a continuous
even function and
a_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\{0\}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\{\widetilde{S}_{ta_{0}}\}_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup
of bounded subnormal operators on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa})
,

(ii)

\psi
is the hyperbolic cosine transform of a
compactly supported Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Moreover, if (ii) holds, then
\widetilde{S}_{a}
is a
bounded subnormal operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa})
for every

a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
.

In the following example, we will show that considering
the hyperbolic cosine transform of measures with unbounded
supports naturally leads to unbounded composition operators
which are cosubnormal. Since in this article we deal only
with bounded operators, we circumvent the obstacles of
unboundedness by using some approach that turns out to be
equivalent to cosubnormality in the case of bounded
composition operators. For more information on unbound
subnormal composition operators, we refer the reader to

[
8
, Section 7.4]
.

Example 3.4.2
.

In view of Theorem
2.3.3
and Lemma

3.1.1
, to show that the operators

\{C_{I+a,\rho}\colon a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\}
are
bounded and cosubnormal, it suffices to prove that if
the conditions (
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.3
)
hold,
\psi
is exponentially convex and

\displaystyle\inf\nolimits_{b\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sup\nolimits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(x)\mathrm{e}^{-b|x|}<\infty,

(3.4.1)

then
\{\psi(\|x+na\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a
Stieltjes moment sequence for all
x,a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. We will show that this can be the case
if we drop the assumption (
3.4.1
), but at the
cost of losing the boundedness of the composition
operators under consideration.

Indeed, let us consider the function
\psi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to(0,\infty)
defined by
\psi(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x^{2}}
for

x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Clearly,
\psi
is continuous and even.
Also,
\psi
is exponentially convex because

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\psi(x_{j}^{*}+x_{k})\lambda_{j}\bar{\lambda}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{(x_{j}+x_{k})^{2}}\lambda_{j}\bar{\lambda}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{2x_{j}x_{k}}(\mathrm{e}^{x_{j}^{2}}\lambda_{j})\overline{(\mathrm{e}^{x_{k}^{2}}\lambda_{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{l}}{l!}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}(x_{j}x_{k})^{l}(\mathrm{e}^{x_{j}^{2}}\lambda_{j})\overline{(\mathrm{e}^{x_{k}^{2}}\lambda_{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{l}}{l!}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{l}\mathrm{e}^{x_{j}^{2}}\lambda_{j}\Big|^{2}\geqslant 0,

for all finite sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Clearly,
\psi
does not satisfy the condition
(
3.4.1
).

Now we show that
\{\psi(\|x+na\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

is a Stieltjes moment sequence for all
x,a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. Fix
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\setminus\{0\}
. Denote by
P
the orthogonal projection of

{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
onto the one-dimensional span
\langle a\rangle
of
\{a\}
. Let
P^{\perp}
stand for the
orthogonal projection of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
onto
\langle a\rangle^{\perp}
. Then
P^{\perp}=I-P
and
Px=s_{x}a
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
, where
s_{x}=\frac{\langle x,a\rangle}{\|a\|^{2}}
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\psi(\|x+na\|)=\mathrm{e}^{\|P^{\perp}x\|^{2}+\|Px+na\|^{2}}=\alpha(\mathrm{e}^{2s_{x}\|a\|^{2}})^{n}q^{-\frac{1}{2}n^{2}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(3.4.2)

where
\alpha:=\mathrm{e}^{\|P^{\perp}x\|^{2}+s_{x}^{2}\|a\|^{2}}
and

q:=\mathrm{e}^{-2\|a\|^{2}}
. It is well known that if
r\in(0,1)
,
then
\{r^{-\frac{1}{2}n^{2}}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is an
indeterminate Stieltjes moment sequence (see
[
3
, Sect. 2]
; see also
[
34
, pp. J.106 and J.107]
).
This together with (
3.4.2
) implies that the sequence

\{\psi(\|x+na\|)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Stieltjes moment
sequence for all
x,a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}
. However, in view of

[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
, the operators

\{C_{I+a,\rho}\colon a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa}\backslash\{0\}\}

are not bounded on
L^{2}(\mu_{\rho})
.

\diamondsuit

Proof II of implication

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
of
Theorem

.

By (ii-d), we have

\displaystyle\psi(x-n)\mathrm{e}^{-b(x-n)}\leqslant a\mathrm{e}^{2nb},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Therefore, according to (ii-a), (ii-b) and
Lemma
2.2.1
, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the
function
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\ni x\longmapsto\psi(x-n)\mathrm{e}^{-b(x-n)}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is bounded, continuous and positive definite on the

*

({\mathbb{R}}_{+},+,x^{*}=x)
. In view of
[
5
, Corollary
4.4.5]
, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, there exists a
finite Borel measure
\mu_{n}
on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

\displaystyle\psi(x-n)\mathrm{e}^{-b(x-n)}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathrm{e}^{-xu}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(A.1)

As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathrm{e}^{-xu}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(u)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{psi-e}}{=}\psi(x-n)\mathrm{e}^{-b(x-n)}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.38193pt=\psi((x+1)-(n+1))\mathrm{e}^{-b((x+1)-(n+1))}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{psi-e}}{=}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathrm{e}^{-xu}\mathrm{e}^{-u}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n+1}(u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Since the Borel measure
\mathrm{e}^{-u}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n+1}(u)
is
finite on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the injectivity of the Laplace
transform (see
[
5
, Proposition 4.4.2]
)
implies that

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(\varDelta)=\int_{\varDelta}\mathrm{e}^{-u}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n+1}(u),\quad\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

or equivalently that

\displaystyle\mu_{n+1}(\varDelta)=\int_{\varDelta}\mathrm{e}^{u}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(u),\quad\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

By induction on
n
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(\varDelta)=\int_{\varDelta}\mathrm{e}^{nu}\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(u),\quad\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(A.2)

It follows from (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) that

\displaystyle\psi(x-n)\mathrm{e}^{-b(x-n)}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathrm{e}^{-(x-n)u}\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(u),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Since for any
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
there exist
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
y=x-n
, we get

\displaystyle\psi(y)\mathrm{e}^{-by}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathrm{e}^{-yu}\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(u),\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(A.3)

Fix any
\tilde{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\tilde{y}<0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\colon

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\big(\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\colon\mathrm{e}^{-\tilde{y}u}>\alpha\}\big)=0\Big\}\overset{(*)}{=}\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(\mathrm{e}^{-\tilde{y}u})^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(u)\bigg)^{1/n}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{psy2}}{=}\lim_{n\to\infty}\psi(n\tilde{y})^{1/n}\mathrm{e}^{-b\tilde{y}}\overset{\eqref{coshtr2}}{\leqslant}\lim_{n\to\infty}a^{1/n}\mathrm{e}^{2b|\tilde{y}|}\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{2b|\tilde{y}|},

where
(*)

[
29

4]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0=\mu_{0}\big(\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\colon\mathrm{e}^{-\tilde{y}u}>\mathrm{e}^{2b|\tilde{y}|}\}\big)=\mu_{0}\big((2b,\infty)\big),

or equivalently
\supp\mu_{0}\subseteq[0,2b]
. Hence,
by the measure transport theorem, we have

\displaystyle\psi(y)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(ii-c)}}{=}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\psi(y)+\psi(-y)\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{psy2}}{=}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{[0,2b]}\mathrm{e}^{-y(u-b)}\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(u)+\int_{[0,2b]}\mathrm{e}^{y(u-b)}\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(u)\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.16663pt=\int_{[0,2b]}\cosh(y(u-b))\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(u)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.16663pt=\int_{[-b,b]}\cosh(yu)\mathrm{d}\mu_{*}(u)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.16663pt=\int_{[-b,0)}\cosh(y(-u))\mathrm{d}\mu_{*}(u)+\mu_{*}(\{0\})+\int_{(0,b]}\cosh(yu)\mathrm{d}\mu_{*}(u)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.16663pt=\int_{(0,b]}\cosh(yu)\mathrm{d}\tilde{\mu}_{*}(u)+\mu_{*}(\{0\})+\int_{(0,b]}\cosh(yu)\mathrm{d}\mu_{*}(u)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.16663pt=\int_{[0,b]}\cosh(yu)\mathrm{d}\nu(u),\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\mu_{*}\colon\mathfrak{B}([-b,b])\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,

\tilde{\mu}_{*}\colon\mathfrak{B}((0,b])\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and

\displaystyle\mu_{*}(\varDelta)

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}(b+\varDelta),\quad\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}([-b,b]),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{*}(\varDelta)

\displaystyle=\mu_{*}(-\varDelta),\quad\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}((0,b]),

Exponent-Critical Groups Geetha Venkataraman, Dr. B. R. Ambedkar University Delhi, geetha@aud.ac.in
Simon R. Blackburn, William Cocke, Andrew Misseldine

Abstract
We define and investigate the property of being ‘exponent-critical’ for a finite group. A finite group is said to be exponent-critical if its exponent is not the least common multiple of the exponents of its proper non-abelian subgroups. We explore properties of exponent-critical groups and give a characterization of such groups. This characterization generalises a classical result of Miller and Moreno on minimal non-abelian groups; interesting families of p p -groups appear.

Introduction

Often times in group theory questions about a group can be reduced to questions about its proper subgroups. This is especially true of various first order properties of groups. For example, the question “does a group contain an element of a certain order?” can be answered by examining all cyclic subgroups of the group. The question “is a given finite group solvable?” can be answered by examining all
2
-generated subgroups of the group
[
7
]
. A more complicated and celebrated result is Thompson’s classification of
N
-groups, finite groups all of whose subgroups are either solvable or Fitting-free
[
15
]
. A famous and classical line of research involved the question of what can be said about a non-abelian group all of whose proper subgroups are abelian. These groups are known as
minimal non-abelian
and were studied by Miller and Moreno in 1903
[
13
]
. In this article, we introduce the following question: “What does the exponent of the non-abelian proper subgroups of
G
imply about the exponent of
G
?” More precisely, we make the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

A finite group
G
is
exponent-critical
if the exponent of
G
is not the least common multiple of the exponents of its proper non-abelian subgroups.

To give an example, we observe that the dihedral group
D_{16}
of order
16
is exponent-critical: it has exponent
8
, and any proper non-abelian subgroup of
D_{16}
is isomorphic to the group
D_{8}
of exponent
4
. As a non-example, we note that
D_{24}
is not exponent-critical: it has exponent
12
and contains proper non-abelian subgroups isomorphic to
D_{12}
(of exponent
6
) and
D_{8}
(of exponent
4
).

We remark that if we drop the non-abelian condition in our definition above, the problem becomes trivial. Indeed, the family of finite groups whose exponent is not the least common multiple of the exponents of its proper subgroups is exactly the family of non-trivial cyclic groups of prime power order.

In this paper, we investigate (finite) exponent-critical groups. An abelian group
G
is exponent-critical if and only if it is non-trivial, so it is the non-abelian case which is interesting. The exponent
\exp(G)
of a non-cyclic group
G
is the least common multiple of the exponents of its maximal subgroups, as every cyclic subgroup is contained in a maximal subgroup. So at least one maximal subgroup of a non-abelian exponent-critical group must be abelian. But a finite group with an abelian maximal subgroup is solvable. (This result originally appeared in a paper by Herstein
[
9
]
, is a weakening of a result in Scott
[
14
, 13.4.6]
, and is a homework problem in Dixon and Mortimer
[
6
, 3.4.7]
.) So, in particular, we may make use of the theory of Hall subgroups when investigating exponent-critical groups.

Every minimal non-abelian group is exponent-critical, so our characterization of exponent-critical groups is as an extension of Miller and Moreno’s characterization of minimal non-abelian groups. Since minimal non-abelian groups are well-studied, it is especially interesting to find exponent-critical groups that are not minimal non-abelian. We will construct many such examples.

In this paper we show that exponent-critical groups must lie in several explicitly defined families, and all groups in these families are exponent-critical. Before discussing our results, we define the following (standard) notation. For a group
G
, we write
Z(G)
for the center of
G
and
\Phi(G)
for the Frattini subgroup of
G
. If
H
is a subgroup
G
(written
H\leq G
), we write
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
for the normalizer of
H
in
G
and
\mathbf{C}_{G}(H)
for the centralizer of
H
in
G
. We will write
\exp(G)
for the exponent of
G
. For an element
g\in G
, we write
o(g)
for the order of
g
.

We first show that exponent-critical groups cannot be divisible by a large number of distinct primes:

Theorem A
.

The order of a non-abelian exponent-critical group is divisible by at most three distinct primes.

The exponent-critical groups divisible by exactly three primes are classified as follows:

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian finite group whose order is divisible by exactly three distinct primes. Then
G
is exponent-critical if and only if
G
is a direct product of a cyclic Sylow subgroup of
G
and its complement, which is minimal non-abelian.

The complement in Theorem
B
is a minimal non-abelian group
H
whose order is divisible by exactly two primes. Such groups

are well understood: see Mastnak and Radjavi
[
12
, Section 2.2]
, for example, for a description of Miller and Moreno’s classification of such groups using modern notation.

We now turn to the case when an exponent-critical group has order divisible by exactly two primes
p
and
q
. We need the following definition in order to state our theorem. Recall that the
p
-part
of a natural number
n=p^{a}m
with
\gcd(p,m)=1
is
p^{a}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
p
be a prime number. A subgroup

of a group

is a
p
-witness for

if it is non-abelian, proper, and the

-parts of

and

are equal.

It is not hard to see that a finite group

is exponent-critical if and only if there exists a prime

dividing the order of

such that no

-witness for
G
exists.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian exponent critical group whose order is divisible by exactly two distinct primes
p
and

. Without loss of generality, swapping

and
q
if necessary, we may suppose that
G
does not possess a
p
-witness for
G
. Then

is isomorphic to one of the following families of exponent-critical groups.

(i)

G
is a direct product of a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup of

and its complement which is minimal non-abelian.

(ii)

G
is a semi-direct product of a normal abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of

by a cyclic Sylow
q
-subgroup

. The subgroup

is a direct product of cyclic groups of order
p^{m}
for some positive integer

. We have
|G:\mathbf{C}_{G}(P)|=q
, and

acts non-trivially and irreducibly on

.

(iii)

is a semi-direct product of a normal abelian Sylow

-subgroup

of

by a cyclic Sylow
q
-subgroup

. The subgroup

is a direct product of a cyclic group of order

and an elementary abelian group. We have

. The action of the subgroup

on

preserves this direct product, acting non-trivially and irreducibly on the elementary abelian group, and trivially on the cyclic group of order

. We have

.

(iv)

is a semi-direct product of a (unique) normal Sylow

-subgroup

, so

and

and trivially on

.

Again, we comment that the structure of the complement in part (i) of this theorem, namely the structure of a minimal non-abelian

for example.

It remains to consider exponent-critical

-groups

. The problem naturally splits into two cases, depending on the number of abelian maximal subgroups of

. We say that an exponent-critical
p
-group

is of

\cal A
if

has exactly one maximal subgroup which is abelian, and is of

\cal B
if

has more than one abelian maximal subgroup. (If

is an element of maximal order in a non-abelian exponent-critical group

, then every maximal subgroup of

containing

is abelian. So

possesses at least one non-abelian maximal subgroup. Thus every exponent-critical

-group has type
\cal A

.) The following theorem provides a characterisation of

-groups of type
\cal B
:

Theorem D
.

A non-abelian finite
p
-group has type
\cal B
if and only if it is
2
-generated with derived subgroup of order
p
.

Finally, we consider exponent-critical
p
-groups of type
{\cal A}
. We construct a ‘universal’ group as a quotient
U/{D}
of a certain semidirect product

. In order to state our result we now define this semidirect product.

Definition 1.3
.

•

Let
W
be the abelian group defined by

W=\langle a_{0}\rangle\times\langle a_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\times\langle a_{p-1}\rangle

where
a_{0}
has order
p^{m}
and
a_{i}
has order
p^{m-1}
for
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

•

Let

be the automorphism (see Lemma
4.5
) of

such that

\phi(a_{i})=\begin{cases}a_{i}a_{i+1}&\text{ for }0\leq i\leq p-2\\
a_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}a_{j}^{-\binom{p}{j}}&\text{ for }i=p-1.\end{cases}

•

Let
\langle b_{0}\rangle
be the cyclic group generated by an element
b_{0}
of order

. Define

to be the semi-direct product
U=W\rtimes\langle b_{0}\rangle
, where

acts on

via the automorphism

. So
b_{0}^{-1}wb_{0}=\phi(w)
for all

.

•

Define

to be the normal subgroup of order
p
generated by the element
a_{0}^{p^{m-1}}a_{p-1}^{p^{m-2}}
.

•

Let
\cal N
be the set of normal subgroups

of

such that:

{D}\leq N\leq\Phi(U),\text{ }N\cap\langle a_{0}\rangle=\{1\},\text{ and }U^{\prime}\not\leq N.

Theorem E
.

(i)

Let

be an exponent-critical
p
-group of exponent

and type
\cal A
. Then

is isomorphic to

, where

.

(ii)

Any group of the form

with

is a non-abelian exponent-critical

-group of exponent

.

(iii)

There are no exponent-critical

-groups of exponent

and type

. When

is odd or

, the converse to

holds: Any group of the form

with

is an exponent-critical

-group of exponent

and type

.

In particular, this theorem shows that when

is odd or

there exists a unique maximal exponent-critical

-group

of exponent

and type

We should mention that the characterization of exponent-critical groups has applications to explicit computations involving varieties of groups

]
; this was how we originally came across this problem.

Exponent-critical groups of order divisible by three primes.

We will prove Theorem

and

in this section.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian exponent-critical finite group. We mentioned in the introduction that
G
is solvable. Suppose that the order of
G
is divisible by four or more primes.
Since
G
is non-abelian, it must be the case that some Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
is not central. Let
q
be a prime dividing the order of
G
such that
\mathbf{C}_{G}(P)
does not contain a Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G
. Let
H_{pq}
be a
pq
-Hall subgroup of
G
, which is necessarily non-abelian. For any other prime
r
dividing
|G|
, a Hall
pqr
-subgroup
H_{pqr}
of
G
is therefore non-abelian. Moreover,
H_{pqr}
is proper since
|G|
is divisible by four or more primes. So
H_{pqr}
is a
p
-witness, a
q
-witness and an
r
-witness for
G
. So there exists an
\ell
-witness for
G
for all primes

dividing the order of

, which contradicts

being exponent-critical.
∎

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian, exponent-critical group. If the order of
G
is divisible by three distinct primes, then
G
has a non-trivial central cyclic Sylow subgroup and all Sylow subgroups of
G
are abelian.

Proof.

By the same reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
A
, the group
G
has a non-abelian
pq
-Hall subgroup for two primes
p
and
q
dividing
|G|
. This subgroup is proper as three primes divide the order of
G
, and so it is a
p
-witness and
q
-witness for
G
. Suppose
r
is the third prime dividing
|G|
. If either the
pr
- or
qr
-Hall subgroups are non-abelian, then we have an
r
-witness for
G
and so
G
is not exponent-critical. Therefore both these Hall subgroups are abelian. Hence all Sylow subgroups of
G
are abelian, and the Sylow
r
-subgroup centralizes a Sylow
p
- and Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G
. If the Sylow

-subgroup is not cyclic, then

would contain a proper subgroup which is an

-witness for

, which would imply that

is not exponent-critical.
∎

We now prove Theorem
B
, which classifies exponent-critical groups whose orders are divisible by only
3
distinct primes.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Suppose
G
is exponent-critical. Then
G
has a central cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup

by Theorem
2.1
. Let
H
be a Hall

-subgroup of

. Since

is central,

is a direct product of

and

. If

were abelian, then

would be abelian. Hence

is non-abelian. Suppose by way of contradiction that

has a non-abelian proper subgroup

. Then

and

is the least common multiple of
\exp(PK)
and

, which implies that

is not exponent-critical. Hence

is minimal non-abelian.

Conversely, suppose that

is a direct product of a cyclic

-group

and a minimal non-abelian

-group

. Any proper subgroup of

containing

is abelian, since

-witness for

, and hence

is exponent-critical.
∎

3
Exponent-critical groups of order divisible by exactly two primes

Before proving Theorem
C
, we show that each of the four families of groups given in Theorem

consists of exponent-critical groups, by showing that there is no

-witness for any group

in the family.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
and
q
be distinct primes. Let

be a direct product of a normal cyclic Sylow

-subgroup

and a minimal non-abelian
q
-subgroup

. Then

is exponent-critical.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction,
H
is a
p
-witness for
G
. Since
P
is cyclic,
P\leq H
. Since
H
is proper,
H=P\times K
for some proper subgroup

of

. Since

is minimal non-abelian,
K
is abelian. But then
H
is abelian, so
H
is not a

-witness for

. This contradiction shows that

is exponent-critical.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p
and
q
be distinct primes. Suppose
G
is a non-abelian semi-direct product of a normal abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
by a cyclic
q
-complement
Q
. Suppose that
P
is the direct product of cyclic groups of order
p^{m}
, where
m
is a positive integer. Furthermore, suppose that
|G:\mathbf{C}_{G}(P)|=q
and
Q
acts non-trivially and irreducibly on

. Then

is exponent-critical.

Proof.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that
H
is a
p
-witness for
G
. We see that
H=P_{1}Q_{1}
where
P_{1}=P\cap H
and where
Q_{1}
is a
q
-group. By replacing

by a conjugate if necessary, we may assume that

. Since

is non-abelian,

is cyclic, and

, we see that

. Since

is normal and

, we see that

is normalised by

. Because

has exponent divisible by the

of the exponent of

, the quotient

is non-trivial. The action of

is irreducible and

is

. So

is generated by

and

, and hence

generates

(as

). Hence

. Thus

, and so

is not proper. This contradiction establishes the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p
and
q
be distinct primes. Moreover, suppose
G
be a non-abelian semi-direct product of a normal abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
by a cyclic
q
-complement
Q
. For

, suppose that
P
is the direct product of a cyclic group of order
p^{m}
, and an elementary abelian
p
-group. Suppose

centralises the cyclic group of order

, and acts irreducibly on the elementary abelian group. Furthermore, suppose that
|G:\mathbf{C}_{G}(P)|=q
. Then

is exponent-critical.

Proof.

We may write

where

is a cyclic group of order

and where

is elementary abelian;

centralises

and acts irreducibly by conjugation on

. We choose a generator

for

, so

.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that

is a

-witness for

. As in Lemma

, we may assume that

where

is normalised by

.

Since

is non-abelian, there exists an element

where

. Now

acts non-trivially on

, since

acts irreducibly and non-trivially on

, we deduce that

. Since

is normalised by

and so

. But

. Since

divides the exponent of

, and since

. Since

, we find that

). But

. Thus

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p
and
q
be distinct primes. Let
G=Q\rtimes P
be a (non-abelian) semi-direct product of a Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
by a non-normal cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
. Suppose that
Q
is special, so either

is elementary abelian or
Q^{\prime}=Z(Q)=\Phi(Q)
. Moreover, suppose that

acts (by conjugation) trivially on

and irreducibly on

, Then

is exponent-critical.

Proof.

Let

satisfy the conditions of the lemma. We show that there is no

-witness for

.

Assume for a contradiction that

is a
p
-witness for
\exp(G)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

for some

and

is

-invariant. Clearly

contains an element

which is of maximal order in

, and so (since

.

Suppose that

. Since

. So

is abelian and, since

. Since

.

Proof of Theorem

.

Lemmas

3.4
show that the four families described in the theorem consist entirely of exponent-critical groups. It suffices to show that any non-abelian exponent-critical

-group lies in one of these four families.

Let
G
be a non-abelian exponent-critical group with
|G|=p^{\alpha}q^{\beta}
where

and

are distinct primes. Suppose that there is no

-witness for

. So all proper subgroups containing a Sylow

-subgroup

of

Part I:

Suppose that a complement

. Since no non-abelian proper subgroup of

is minimal non-abelian. Now suppose that

is not cyclic. Let

. Then

is a

-witness for

, which is a contradiction. So

.

Now let us consider the case when

is normal but its complement, say

, is not. So we can find an element

which is not in

. Thus

is a non-abelian subgroup of

. So

and therefore

. Furthermore

is a

-witness for

. So
|G:\mathbf{C}_{G}(P)|=q
.

Let the exponent of

be

. Write

for the subgroup of elements of

, and note that

. Set

. Indeed,

acts non-trivially on

by conjugation, and the only automorphisms of

have

, we see that

acts non-trivially on

Let

is an

. Indeed

has exponent

. Since the order of

(3.1)

of irreducible submodules, where

and, since

. Since all the elements in

have order

, we see that

has a subgroup of

of exponent

. We divide our argument into two sub-cases:

Sub-case 1: Suppose that

acts non-trivially on a submodule

.

We see that

is a non-abelian subgroup of

, and (since

. Hence

.

Sub-case 2:

acts trivially on all submodules

.

Since

acts non-trivially on

acts non-trivially on one of modules

. (In particular, this implies that

, and so

, and

. If

acts non-trivially by conjugation on

-witness, and so we deduce that

centralises

.

Taking

-powers in

induces a surjective

-module homomorphism

from

to the

-module

, this homomorphism is non-trivial. Since

and

. Since

is trivial and irreducible, it has dimension

and so

is a direct product of a cyclic group of order

with an elementary abelian group.

Let

. The element

has order

and (since

is coprime to

)

.
The action of

by conjugation on

, where the action of

.

Part II:
P
is not normal and so

is abelian

Clearly in this case,

and by Burnside’s Normal

-complement Theorem,

has a normal

-complement

. Therefore

is a semi-direct product of its Sylow

-subgroup

by a non-normal abelian Sylow

-subgroup

.

Suppose, for a contradiction, that

is not cyclic. Let

have maximal order. Then
\langle x\rangle Q
is a proper subgroup, so must be abelian (otherwise we have a witness for the

The subgroup

∎

4
Exponent-critical
p
-groups

We now examine exponent-critical
p
-groups. We will prove Theorems
D
and
E
in this section.

Let
P
be a non-abelian
p
-group of order
p^{n}
. We denote by
{\cal M}_{P}
the set of elements of
P
of maximal order, or equivalently the set of elements that have order equal to
\exp(P)
. Note that
P
is exponent-critical if and only if
{\cal M}_{P}\cap M=\emptyset
for all non-abelian maximal subgroups
M
of
P
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
P
be a finite non-abelian exponent critical
p
-group. Then
P=\langle a,b\rangle
where
a\in P
has maximal order and
b\in P
. Moreover,
P
is solvable of derived length
2
.

Proof.

The Frattini subgroup
\Phi(P)
is contained in all maximal subgroups. Since at least one maximal subgroup is abelian,
\Phi(P)
is abelian. Since
P^{\prime}\leq P^{\prime}P^{p}=\Phi(P)
, and since
P
is non-abelian,
P
is solvable of derived length
2
.

We now show that
P
is
2
-generated. Suppose, for a contradiction, that all elements in
{\cal M}_{P}
are central. Let
a\in{\cal M}_{P}
, and let
x,y\in P
be such that
[x,y]\not=1
. As
x
is not central, it does not have maximal order. Hence, using the fact that
[a,x]=1
, we see that
xa
has maximal order. Since
a
is central,
[xa,y]=[x,y]^{a}[a,y]=[x,y]\not=1
. Thus we have a contradiction as required, and we may deduce that there exists an element
a\in{\cal M}_{P}
of maximal order that is not central. Let
b\in P
be such that
[a,b]\not=1
. Thus
\langle a,b\rangle
is a non-abelian subgroup of exponent
\exp(P)
and so
P=\langle a,b\rangle
. Hence the theorem follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
D
.

Suppose
P
is a non-abelian finite group which is
2
-generated and
|P^{\prime}|=p
. We show that
P
is exponent critical of type
\cal B
. Let
P=\langle a,b\rangle
. Since
P
is nilpotent,
[P^{\prime},P]
is a proper subgroup of
P^{\prime}
. Thus
P
has nilpotency class 2. In particular,
P^{\prime}\leq Z(P)
. Since
P
has nilpotency class 2 we get that
1=[a,b]^{p}=[a^{p},b]=[a,b^{p}]
. So
a^{p}
and
b^{p}
are central. Hence
\Phi(P)\leq Z(P)
as in a
p
-group
\Phi(P)
is the subgroup generated by
P^{\prime}
and the
p
-th powers of a generating set. Let
M
be a maximal subgroup. Then
\Phi(P)\leq M
. Since
P
is
2
-generated,
\Phi(P)
has index
p^{2}
in
P
, and so has index
p
in
M
. Since
\Phi(P)
is central in
P
, we see that
Z(M)\geq\Phi(P)
and so the centre of
M
has index at most
p
in
M
. But then
M
must be abelian (and
Z(M)=M
). Thus
P
is a minimal non-abelian group and hence exponent-critical of type
\cal B
, as required.

Conversely, suppose that
P
is exponent-critical of type
\cal B
. By Theorem
4.1
,
P=\langle a,b\rangle
for elements
a,b\in P
with
a\in{\cal M}_{P}
. Since
P
has type
\cal B
, it contains distinct abelian maximal subgroups
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. For
x,y\in P
we see that
[x,y]\in P^{\prime}\leq\Phi(P)\leq M_{1}\cap M_{2}
and so
[x,y]
commutes with all elements of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
. Since
P=M_{1}M_{2}
, we deduce that
P^{\prime}
is central in
P
and so
P
has nilpotency class 2. Hence
[x,yz]=[x,y][x,z]
and
[xy,z]=[x,z][y,z]
for all
x,y,z
in
P
. Using this we see that
P^{\prime}=\langle[a,b]\rangle
. Let
M_{a}
be a maximal subgroup of
P
such that
a\in M_{a}
. Since
a
has maximal order and
P
is exponent-critical we see that
M_{a}
is abelian. Clearly
b^{p}\in M_{a}
. Since
P
has nilpotency class
2
, we see that
1=[a,b^{p}]=[a,b]^{p}=[a^{p},b]
. So
o([a,b])=p=|P^{\prime}|
, and part (iii) of the theorem follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be a non-abelian exponent-critical

(i)

(ii)

, where

has exponent dividing

.

(iii)

is abelian, of exponent dividing

A
is normal, since all maximal subgroups of a
p

A
were non-abelian,

would have a proper non-abelian subgroup of exponent

and hence

would not exponent-critical. This contradiction shows that

is abelian. Since

is of type
{\cal A}
, we have that

is the unique maximal subgroup of

which is abelian. Clearly,

To prove part (ii), first note that

, and so

, and

is elementary abelian of order

. Let

. Since

. Consider the subgroup

has index

in

, we get that

. Since

, the order of

and so

. Hence

has index

and so is maximal. If

. But

To prove (iii), we note that

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
P
be a
p
-group. Suppose
P
possesses an abelian maximal subgroup
A
. Let
b\in P\setminus A
.

(i)

The map
\kappa:A\rightarrow A
defined by
\kappa(x)=[x,b]
is a homomorphism.

(ii)

For
x\in A
, and
b\in P\setminus A
,

(bx)^{i}=b^{i}x^{i}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j+1}}.

(4.1)

(iii)

For
x\in A
, and
b\in P\setminus A
,

[x,_{p}b]=\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}[x,_{i}b]^{-\binom{p}{i}}.

(4.2)

Proof.

To prove (i), we note that for all
x\in A
,
[x,b]\in A
as
A
is normal in
P
. So
[x,b]^{y}=[x,b]
for all
y\in A
, since
A
is abelian. Hence

[xy,b]=[x,b]^{y}[y,b]=[x,b][y,b],

and
\kappa
is a homomorphism, as required.

We prove the equality (
4.1
) by induction on
i
. The equality (
4.1
) clearly holds when
i=1
. Assume the equality holds for a fixed value of
i
. Since
P/A
is abelian, all commutators lie in the abelian subgroup
A
. Hence

\displaystyle(bx)^{i}bx

\displaystyle=b^{i}x^{i}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j+1}}\right)bx\text{, by our inductive hypothesis}

\displaystyle=b^{i+1}x^{i}[x,b]^{i}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j+1}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}[x,_{j+1}b]^{\binom{i}{j+1}}\right)x

by part (i)

\displaystyle=b^{i+1}x^{i+1}[x,b]^{\binom{i}{1}+\binom{i}{2}}\left(\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j+1}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=2}^{i}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ as }x\in A,

\displaystyle=b^{i+1}x^{i+1}\prod_{j=1}^{i}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j+1}+\binom{i}{j}},\text{ since }\binom{i}{i+1}=0,

\displaystyle=b^{i+1}x^{i+1}\prod_{j=1}^{i}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i+1}{j+1}},

and so part (ii) follows by induction.

Finally, we prove the part (iii) of the lemma. We first prove that, for any positive integer
i
,

b^{-i}xb^{i}=x\prod_{j=1}^{i}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j}}.

(4.3)

To show this, we note that
b^{-1}xb=x[x,b]
and so (
4.3
) holds when
i=1
. If (
4.3
) holds for a given value of
i
, we see that

\displaystyle b^{-(i+1)}xb^{i+1}

\displaystyle=b^{-1}x\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j}}\right)b

\displaystyle=x[x,b]\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{i}[x,_{j+1}b]^{\binom{i}{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=x\prod_{j=1}^{i+1}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i}{j}+\binom{i}{j-1}}

\displaystyle=x\prod_{j=1}^{i+1}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{i+1}{j}},

and so, by induction, the equation (
4.3
) holds for all
i
. Now
b^{p}\in A
since
A
is maximal. As
A
is abelian,
b^{-p}xb^{p}=x
, and so

x=x\prod_{j=1}^{p}[x,_{j}b]^{\binom{p}{j}}.

Hence part (iii) of the lemma follows.
∎

\kappa^{i-1}(K)=\langle a_{p-1}^{p^{m-2}}\rangle
. Since

is normal in

, we have
\kappa^{i-1}(K)\leq K
and so part (vii) of the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

.

By Theorem

. Consider the free group

to

[[a,_{i}b],[a,_{j}b]]=1

. Lemma

. Finally, Lemma

(iii) shows that
[a_{0},_{p}b_{0}]=\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}[a_{0},_{j}b_{0}]^{-\binom{p}{j}}

for some normal subgroup

. Moreover, under this isomorphism the element

We now check that the normal subgroup

lies in
\cal N

and

both have order

. Since

2

Consider the element

, since

, we see that

4.3
(i). Now, by Lemma

\displaystyle(ba)^{p}

\displaystyle=b^{p}a^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}[a,_{j}b]^{\binom{p}{j+1}}

\displaystyle=a^{p}[a,_{p-1}b]\,b^{p}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{p-2}[a,_{j}b]^{\binom{p}{j+1}/p}\right)^{p},

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=(ba)^{p^{m-1}}=\left((ba)^{p}\right)^{p^{m-2}}

\displaystyle=a^{p^{m-1}}[a,_{p-1}b]^{p^{m-2}},

To prove part (ii), we first investigate the orders of elements in the quotient

M/{D}
of

(4.4)

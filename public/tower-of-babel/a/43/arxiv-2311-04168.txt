Introduction

Let
q\in(0,1).
In
[
13
]
, L. Vaksman and S. Sinel’shchikov put forward a construction of a
q
-analogue of Hermitian symmetric spaces of non-compact type via a
q
-analogue of the Harish-Chandra embedding. This construction yields a
q
-analogue of the
*
-algebra of polynomial functions on the bounded symmetric domain that is the image of the Harish-Chandra embedding. In the simplest case (see
[
13
]
, section
9
), this yields the algebra
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbb{C})_{q},
the quantum disc. This is the
*
-algebra generated by a single generator
z
subject to the relation

z^{*}z=q^{2}zz^{*}+(1-q^{2})I.

Another particular case, derived explicitly in
[
12
]
, gives the algebra
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{n})_{q},
a
q
-analogue of polynomial functions on the open matrix ball
\mathbb{D}_{n}:=\{Z\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}:Z^{*}Z<I\}.

Let
C(\overline{\mathbb{D}})_{q}
be the universal enveloping
C^{*}
-algebra of
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbb{C}).
It follows from the classification of its irreducible representations [
[
15
]
, Corollary 1.13.], that for all
q,
the
C(\overline{\mathbb{D}})_{q}
is isomorphic to the Toeplitz algebra
C^{*}(S)
, i.e. the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the unilateral shift
S\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}))
acting as
e_{k}\mapsto e_{k+1}
on the natural orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
. In particular, it follows that the algebras
C(\overline{\mathbb{D}})_{q},

0<q<1,
are all isomorphic.

The aim of this paper is to prove a similar result for
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{2})_{q}:
The universal enveloping algebras
C(\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{2})_{q}
of
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{2})_{q}
are isomorphic for all values of
q.

This ties in with the larger project of trying to understand how, in
q
-analogues, the
C^{*}
-algebraic structure depends on the deformation parameter. The development up to now seems to indicate that they usually are independent of it, at least for
0<q<1.
This was observed by S.L. Woronowicz
[
16
]
for his compact quantum group
SU_{q}(2)
. Same results has been proven the Vaksman-Soibelman spheres, as well as similar objects by relating them to graph
C^{*}
-algebras. In the 90’s G. Nagy proved it for
SU_{q}(3)
(
[
9
]
), whose proof was then extended by the author to work for all compact semi-simple Lie groups
[
3
]
.
It also interesting to look at the limit
q\to 0,
as for some of the examples listed above (e.g.
SU_{q}(2)
, Vaksman-Soibelman spheres), the
q
-independence can be proven by showing ismorphism with
q=0
, and that this is a graph-
C^{*}
-algebra
[
4
]
. A resent paper by M. Matassa and R. Yuncken
[
8
]
generalized this and showed that in the case of the usual
q
-deformed compact Lie groups, one can use crystal bases to show that the limit
q\to 0

C^{*}
-algebra is coming from a higher rank graph. However, in this case (as of now), there is still the question of ismorphisms with non-zero values of
q.

1.1

The Quantum group
\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q}
and its irreducible representations

Recall the definition of the Hopf algebra

\mathbb{C}[SL_{n}]_{q}
. It is defined by the generators
\{t_{i,j}\}_{i,j=1,\ldots,n}
and the relations

\displaystyle t_{\alpha,a}t_{\beta,b}-qt_{\beta,b}t_{\alpha,a}=0,

\displaystyle a=b\quad\&\quad\alpha<\beta,

\displaystyle\quad\text{or}\quad a<b\quad\&\quad\alpha=\beta,

\displaystyle t_{\alpha,a}t_{\beta,b}-t_{\beta,b}t_{\alpha,a}=0,

\displaystyle\alpha<\beta\quad\&\quad a>b,

\displaystyle t_{\alpha,a}t_{\beta,b}-t_{\beta,b}t_{\alpha,a}-(q-q^{-1})t_{\beta,a}t_{\alpha,b}=0,

\displaystyle\alpha<\beta\quad\&\quad a<b,

\displaystyle\det\nolimits_{q}\mathbf{t}=1.

Here
\det_{q}\mathbf{t}
is the q-determinant of the matrix

\mathbf{t}=(t_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{n}
.

It is well known (see
[
6
]
or other standard book on quantum groups) that

\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(\mathbb{C}[SL_{n}]_{q},\star)
is
a Hopf
*
-algebra. The co-multiplication
\Delta
, the co-unit
\varepsilon
, the antipode
S
and the involution
\star
are defined as follows

\Delta(t_{i,j})=\sum_{k}t_{i,k}\otimes t_{k,j},\quad\varepsilon(t_{i,j})=\delta_{ij},\quad S(t_{i,j})=(-q)^{i-j}\det\nolimits_{q}\mathbf{t}_{ji},

and

t_{i,j}^{\star}=(-q)^{j-i}\det\nolimits_{q}\mathbf{t}_{ij},

where
{\mathbf{t}}_{ij}
is the matrix derived from
{\mathbf{t}}
by discarding its
i
-th row and
j
-th column.

Let
\{e_{k}|k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
and let
S
be the isometric shift
e_{k}\mapsto e_{k+1}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}).
For
q\in(0,1),
we now introduce the operators
d_{q},C_{q}\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}))
given by the formulas

\begin{array}[]{ccc}C_{q}e_{m}=\sqrt{1-q^{2m}}e_{m},&d_{q}e_{m}=q^{m}e_{m}&\text{ for all $k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.$}\\
\end{array}

(1)

Now let

\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{11}=S^{*}C_{q},&T_{12}=-qd_{q},\\
T_{21}=d_{q},&T_{22}=C_{q}S\\
.\end{array}

(2)

It is not hard to verify the formulas

\begin{array}[]{cc}d_{q}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j}S^{j}(I-SS^{*})S^{*j},&C_{q}^{2}=I-d_{q}^{2}\end{array}.

It follows from this that
d_{q},C_{q}\in C^{*}(S),
the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
S,
and therefore
T_{ij}\in C^{*}(S),
for
i,j=1,2.

Notice that the following relations hold

\begin{array}[]{ccc}T_{11}=T_{22}^{*}&T_{11}T_{22}-qT_{12}T_{21}=0&T_{12}T_{21}=T_{21}T_{12}\\
T_{11}T_{12}=qT_{12}T_{11}&T_{11}T_{21}=qT_{21}T_{11}&T_{21}=\sqrt{I-T_{22}T_{11}}\\
&T_{11}T_{22}=q^{2}T_{22}T_{11}+(1-q^{2})I&\\
\end{array}

(3)

from which we conclude that the operators
T_{ij}
determine an irreducible
*
-representation
\pi:\mathbb{C}[SU_{2}]_{q}\to C^{*}(S)\subset\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}))

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\pi(t_{ij})=T_{ij},&\text{(for $1\leq i,j\leq 2$)}.\end{array}

(4)

For
1\leq i\leq n-1,
let
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[SU_{2}]_{q}
be the
*
-homomorphism determined by

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\phi_{i}(t_{i,i})=t_{1,1},&&\phi_{i}(t_{i+1,i+1})=t_{2,2},\\
\phi_{i}(t_{i,i+1})=t_{1,2},&&\phi_{i}(t_{i+1,i})=t_{2,1}\\
&\text{and }\phi_{i}(t_{k,j})=\delta_{k,j}I\text{ otherwise.}&\end{array}

(5)

Then
\pi_{i}=\pi\circ\phi_{i}
is a
*
-representation of
\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}).
Moreover
\pi_{i}(\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q})\subseteq C^{*}(S).

Let
s_{i}
denote the adjacent transposition
(i,i+1)
in the symmetric group
S_{n}.

Definition 1
.

For an element
s\in S_{n}
consider a reduced decomposition of
s=s_{j_{1}}s_{j_{2}}\dots s_{j_{m}}
into a product of adjacent transposition and put

\pi_{s}=\pi_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\pi_{j_{m}}.

It is known that
\pi_{s}
is independent of the specific reduced expression of
s,
in the sense that another reduced decomposition gives a unitary equivalent
*
-representation.

Recall that the
length
of
s\in S_{n},
denoted by
\ell(s),
is the number of adjacent transpositions in a reduced decomposition of
s=s_{j_{1}}s_{j_{2}}\dots s_{j_{\ell(s)}}.
For the identity element
e\in S_{n}
, we let
\ell(e)=0.
For
s,t\in S_{n},
it is easy to see that the inequality

\ell(st)\leq\ell(s)+\ell(t).

holds, and that
\ell(s^{-1})=\ell(s).

Let
\varphi=\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}\}\in[0,2\pi)^{n}
be a
n
-tuple such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}.

Then we can define a one-dimensional
*
-representation
\chi_{\varphi}:\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q}\to\mathbb{C}
by the formula

\chi_{\varphi}(t_{ij})=e^{i\varphi_{j}}\delta_{ij}.

(6)

From the work of Soibelman, the following is known (with
S_{n}
the Weyl group of
SU_{n}
)

Proposition
2
.

[
[
7
]
, Theorem
6.2.7
]
Every irreducible
*
-representation
\Pi
of
\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q}
is equivalent to one of the form

\pi_{s}\otimes\chi_{\varphi}

(7)

for
s\in S_{n}
and
\varphi=[\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}]\in[0,2\pi)^{n},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}.
Conversly, such pairs give rise to non-equivalent irreducible
*
-representations of
\mathbb{C}[SU_{n}]_{q}.

1.2

The
*
-algebra
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{n})_{q}

In what follows
\mathbb{C}
is a ground field and
q\in(0,1)
. We assume
that all the algebras under consideration has a unit
I.
Consider the well-known algebra

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Mat}_{n}]_{q}
defined by its generators
z_{a}^{\alpha}
,

\alpha,a=1,\dots,n
, and the commutation relations

\displaystyle z_{a}^{\alpha}z_{b}^{\beta}-qz_{b}^{\beta}z_{a}^{\alpha}=0,

\displaystyle a=b\quad\&\quad\alpha<\beta,

\displaystyle\quad\text{or}\quad a<b\quad\&\quad\alpha=\beta,

(8)

\displaystyle z_{a}^{\alpha}z_{b}^{\beta}-z_{b}^{\beta}z_{a}^{\alpha}=0,

\displaystyle\alpha<\beta\quad\&\quad a>b,

(9)

\displaystyle z_{a}^{\alpha}z_{b}^{\beta}-z_{b}^{\beta}z_{a}^{\alpha}-(q-q^{-1})z_{a}^{\beta}z_{b}^{\alpha}=0,

\displaystyle\alpha<\beta\quad\&\quad a<b.

(10)

This algebra is a quantum analogue of the polynomial algebra

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Mat}_{n}]
on the space of
n\times n
-matrices.
Let
A=(a_{j,k})_{j,k}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
be a
n\times n
matrix of positive integers
a_{j,k}
, and denote by
z(A)
the monomial

\begin{array}[]{cc}z(A):=\\
(z_{n}^{n})^{a_{n,n}}(z_{n}^{n-1})^{a_{n,n-1}}\ldots(z_{n}^{1})^{a_{n,1}}(z_{n-1}^{n})^{a_{n-1,n}}\ldots(z_{n-1}^{1})^{a_{n-1,1}}\ldots(z_{1}^{n})^{a_{1,n}}\ldots(z_{1}^{1})^{a_{1,1}}.\end{array}

(11)

It follows from the Bergman diamond lemma that monomials
z(A)
,
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}),
form a basis for the vector space
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathrm{Mat}_{n}]_{q}.
Hence
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathrm{Mat}_{n}]_{q}
admits a natural gradation given by
\text{deg}(z_{k}^{j})=1
, and in general

\text{deg}(z(A))=|A|,
where

|A|=\sum_{k,j=1}^{n}a_{k,j}.

(12)

In a similar way, we write
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathrm{Mat}}_{n}]_{q}
for the algebra defined by generators

(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}
,
\alpha,a=1,\ldots,n
, and the relations

\displaystyle(z_{b}^{\beta})^{*}(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}-q(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}(z_{b}^{\beta})^{*}=0,\quad a=b\quad\&\quad\alpha<\beta,\qquad\text{or}

\displaystyle a<b\quad\&\quad\alpha=\beta,

(13)

\displaystyle(z_{b}^{\beta})^{*}(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}-(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}(z_{b}^{\beta})^{*}=0,

\displaystyle\alpha<\beta\quad\&\quad a>b,

(14)

\displaystyle(z_{b}^{\beta})^{*}(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}-(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}(z_{b}^{\beta})^{*}-(q-q^{-1})(z_{b}^{\alpha})^{*}(z_{a}^{\beta})^{*}=0,

\displaystyle\alpha<\beta\quad\&\quad a<b.

(15)

A gradation in
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathrm{Mat}}_{n}]_{q}
is given by
\text{deg}(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}=-1
.

Consider now the algebra
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{n})_{q},
whose generators are
z_{a}^{\alpha}
,
(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}
,
\alpha,a=1,\dots,n
, and the list of relations is formed by (
8
) –
(
15
) and

\displaystyle(z_{b}^{\beta})^{*}z_{a}^{\alpha}=q^{2}\cdot\sum_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}=1}^{n}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}=1}^{n}R_{ba}^{b^{\prime}a^{\prime}}R_{\beta\alpha}^{\beta^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot z_{a^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(z_{b^{\prime}}^{\beta^{\prime}})^{*}+(1-q^{2})\delta_{ab}\delta^{\alpha\beta},

(16)

with
\delta_{ab}
,
\delta^{\alpha\beta}
being the Kronecker symbols, and

R_{ij}^{kl}=\begin{cases}q^{-1},&i\neq j\quad\&\quad i=k\quad\&\quad j=l\\
1,&i=j=k=l\\
-(q^{-2}-1),&i=j\quad\&\quad k=l\quad\&\quad l>j\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The involution in
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{n})_{q}
is introduced in an
obvious way:
*:z_{a}^{\alpha}\mapsto(z_{a}^{\alpha})^{*}
.

It is known from
[
11
]
(Corollary 10.4) that we have an isomorphism of vector spaces

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Mat}_{n}]_{q}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathrm{Mat}}_{n}]_{q}\rightarrow\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{n})_{q}

(17)

induced by the linear map
a\otimes b\mapsto a\cdot b.

Thus, we can consider the algebras
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Mat}_{n}]_{q}
and
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathrm{Mat}}_{n}]_{q}
as sub-algebras of
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{n})_{q},
if we identify them with the images of
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Mat}_{n}]_{q}\otimes I
and
I\otimes\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathrm{Mat}}_{n}]_{q}
respectively.

The Case

In the case of

, the relations defining
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathrm{Mat}_{2})_{q}
specialized to the following:

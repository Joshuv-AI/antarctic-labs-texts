Compressible fluid inside a linear oscillator
Václav Mácha

Abstract
We use Feireisl-Lions theory to deduce the existence of weak solutions to a system describing the dynamics of a linear oscillator containing a Newtonian compressible fluid. The appropriate Navier-Stokes equation is considered on a domain whose movement has one degree of freedom. The equation is paired with the Newton law and we assume a no-slip boundary condition.

Introduction

It is known that the presence of a fluid inside a freely moving body has a tremendous effect on the stability of the system. This has been investigated several times, most recently by Disser, Galdi, Mazzone and Zunino

The same effect also appears in the case of compressible fluid inside a body – we refer to
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. In
[
8
]
authors tackle a system consisting of a pendulum containing a compressible fluid. The numerical simulations provided there showed that the compressible fluids provide better damping than the incompressible ones. This may be explained by a simple idea – there appears a flow even if the oscillator is linear (there is no swing). Consequently, the mechanical energy of the whole system dissipates because of the viscosity term. For more see the next subsection.

The main aim of the paper is to prove the existence of solution to such system, i.e., a linear oscillator consisting of a spring and a container filled by a compressible fluid. A container
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, whose position is expressed by an unknown function
{\bf b}:[0,T)\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
{\bf b}(t)\times{\bf e}_{1}=0
(i.e.
\Omega_{t}=\Omega_{0}+{\bf b}(t)
), is joined by a spring to a point whose position is at
{\bf f}(t)
, where
{\bf f}:[0,T)\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
{\bf f}(t)\times{\bf e}_{1}=0
is given – this means that the oscillations are forced as one end of the spring possesses a motion prescribed by
{\bf f}
.

The movement of the fluid inside a container is given by the Navier-Stokes equations, i.e.

\begin{split}\partial_{t}(\varrho{\bf w})+\diver(\varrho{\bf w}\otimes{\bf w})-\diver T&=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{t}\\
\partial_{t}\varrho+\diver\varrho{\bf w}&=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{t}.\end{split}

(1.1)

Here
{\bf w}:[0,T)\times\Omega_{t}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a velocity of the fluid and
\varrho:[0,T)\times\Omega_{t}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is its density. We assume the no-slip boundary condition, i.e.

{\bf w}{\restriction_{\partial\Omega_{t}}}=\dot{\bf b}.

The stress tensor
T
is given as

T=T({\bf w},\varrho)=S(\nabla{\bf w})-Ip(\varrho)

(1.2)

where

S(\nabla{\bf w})=\mu D{\bf w}+(\lambda+\mu)I\diver{\bf w},

(1.3)

I
is an identity
d\times d
matrix and
Dw=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla w+(\nabla w)^{T}\right)
,
\mu>0
,
\lambda+\frac{2}{3}\mu>0
,
and

p(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma}

(1.4)

for some

specified later.

The force caused by the spring, whose given stiffness is
k>0

{\bf F}(t)=-({\bf b}(t)-{\bf f}(t))k

By Newton law, this cause a change in linear momentum and by the transport theorem we write

{\bf F}=\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\varrho{\bf w}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\partial_{t}\varrho{\bf w}+\diver(\varrho{\bf w}\otimes{\bf w})\,\mathrm{d}x

and we use (
1.1
) with the integration by parts in order to deduce the following coupling between the fluid and the movement of the container:

-({\bf b}(t)-{\bf f}(t))k=\left(\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}T{\bf n}{\rm d}S_{x}\cdot{\bf e}_{1}\right){\bf e}_{1}.

Note that the movement is allowed only in
{\bf e}_{1}
direction.

1.1
Rigid body case, incompressible case

In the case of the rigid body (it is sufficient to take

{\bf w}\equiv\dot{\bf b}
and forget about (

)
1
) one end up with an equation

\ddot{\bf b}+\frac{k}{M}{\bf b}=\frac{k}{M}{\bf f}(t)

Let assume that

. As this equation is a second-order linear differential equation with constant coefficients, we may use a standard theory (Duhamel formula) to deduce that for
\omega^{2}\neq\frac{k}{M}
the solutions have form

{\bf b}\cdot{\bf e}_{1}=c_{1}\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}t\right)+c_{2}\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}t\right)+\frac{k}{k-M\omega^{2}}\sin(\omega t)

where

are arbitrary constants.
However, there appears a resonance for
\omega^{2}=\frac{k}{M}
– the solution in such case is

{\bf b}\cdot{\bf e}_{1}=-\frac{\omega}{2}t\cos(\omega t)+c_{1}\sin(\omega t)+c_{2}\cos(\omega t)

and one can see that such solution is unbounded.

Consider now an incompressible fluid. Let
\varrho\equiv 1
and
\diver{\bf w}=0
in (
1.1
). Also assume that

is an independent unknown. In that case one solution is

, pressure is such that

\nabla p=\dot{\bf b}

and we end up with the same system as in the rigid body case. Roughly speaking, the force acting on the fluid is potential and it does not cause the fluid to move. Therefore, there appears no damping.

However, this phenomena does not happen once the fluid is compressible. Indeed, the assumptions
{\bf w}=\dot{\bf b}
and
\nabla p(\varrho)=\dot{\bf b}
yield a contradiction – the second equation gives
\varrho(t,x)=\varrho(t)
and thus
\partial_{t}\varrho
is nonzero function of time which cannot be compensated by
\diver\varrho{\bf w}=\nabla\varrho{\bf w}+\varrho\diver{\bf w}
and thus (
1.1
)
2
is not fulfill. Consequently, there must appear a non-trivial flux.

\lim_{\delta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(p(\varrho_{\delta})-(\lambda+2\mu)\diver{\bf v}_{\delta})T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\overline{p(\varrho)}-(\lambda+2\mu\diver{\bf v})\right)T_{k}(\varrho)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t

(4.19)

since integrands on both sides are equi-integrable provided
k
is fixed. Next, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}^{\gamma}T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}}\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t\geq\ \int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\varrho_{\delta}^{\gamma}-\varrho^{\gamma})(T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho))\geq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t

and

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\diver{\bf v}_{\delta}T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-\diver{\bf v}\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)+T_{k}(\varrho)-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\diver{\bf v}_{\delta}\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t\\
\leq 2\|\diver{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}.

With help of (
4.19
) we get

0=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\varrho_{\delta}^{\gamma}T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}}\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-(\lambda+2\mu)\left(\diver{\bf v}_{\delta}T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-\diver{\bf v}\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t\\
\geq\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{\gamma+1}-2\|\diver{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}.

This yields

\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq c

Using a smoothening kernel we may deduce the validity of equation

\partial_{t}b(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)})-\diver(b(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}){\bf v})+(b^{\prime}(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)})\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-b(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}))\diver{\bf v}=b(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)})\left(\overline{(T_{k}(\varrho)-T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho)\varrho)\diver{\bf v}}\right)

(4.20)

in
D^{\prime}((0,T)\times\Omega)
. We plan to tend with
k
to
\infty
. First, we have

\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\to\varrho\ \mbox{ strongly in }L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega),\ 1\leq p<\gamma.

This follows as

\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-\varrho\|^{p}_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq\mbox{liminf}_{\delta\to 0}\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{p}\\
\leq\mbox{liminf}_{\delta\to 0}2^{p}k^{p-\gamma}\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\gamma}((0,T)\times\Omega}^{\gamma}\to 0.

for
k\to\infty
.

Since
b^{\prime}(z)\equiv 0
for
z\geq M
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left|b(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)})\left(\overline{(T_{k}(\varrho)-T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho)\varrho)\diver{\bf v}}\right)\right|\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}t\\
\leq\sup_{0\leq z\leq M}|b^{\prime}(z)|\|{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}W^{1,2}}\mbox{liminf}_{\delta\to 0}\|T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\varrho_{\delta}-T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\|_{L^{2}(Q_{k,m})}.

(4.21)

where
Q_{k,m}=\{(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega,\ \overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\leq M\}
. The Hölder inequality yields

\|T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\varrho_{\delta}-T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\|_{L^{2}(Q_{k,m})}^{2}\leq\|T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\varrho_{\delta}-T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}\|T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\varrho_{\delta}-T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\|_{L^{\gamma+1}(Q_{k,m})}^{(\gamma+1)/\gamma}.

(4.22)

Further, we have

\|T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\varrho_{\delta}-T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq 2^{\gamma}k^{1-\gamma}\sup_{\delta}\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\gamma}((0,T)\times\Omega)}^{\gamma}

and, since
T^{\prime}_{k}(z)z\leq T_{k}(z)
,

\|T^{\prime}_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\varrho_{\delta}-T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\|_{L^{\gamma+1}(Q_{k,m})}\leq 2\left(\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{\gamma+1}(Q_{k,m})}\right)\\
\leq 2\left(\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})-T_{k}(\varrho)\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|T_{k}(\varrho)-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{\gamma+1}(Q_{k,m})}+\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{\gamma+1}(Q_{k,M})}\right)\leq c

(4.23)

The renormalize continuity equation (
4.18
) follows from (
4.20
), (
4.21
), (
4.22
) and (
4.23
).

To conclude the proof we introduce

L_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}z\log z\mbox{ for }0\leq z\leq k,\\
z\log k+z\int_{k}^{z}\frac{1}{s^{2}}T_{k}(s)\ {\rm d}s\mbox{ for }z\geq k.\end{array}\right.

We use this as
b
in the renormalized equation (
4.18
) to conclude

\partial_{t}L_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})+\diver(L_{k}(\varrho_{\delta}){\bf v}_{\delta})+T_{k}(\varrho_{\delta})\diver{\bf v}_{\delta}=0

and

\partial_{t}L_{k}(\varrho)+\diver(L_{k}(\varrho){\bf v})+T_{k}(\varrho)\diver{\bf v}=0.

Similarly to the previous section we deduce (compare with (
4.13
))

\int_{\Omega}L_{k}(\varrho(\tau,\cdot))-\overline{L_{k}(\varrho(\tau,\cdot))}\ {\rm d}x\geq 0

for almost all
\tau\in[0,T]
. We send
k\to\infty
to deduce that
\varrho\log\varrho=\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}
which yields
\varrho_{\delta}\to\varrho
almost everywhere. The proof of Theorem
3.1
is finished.

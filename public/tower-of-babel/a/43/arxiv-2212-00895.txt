Local to global principle for higher moments over function fields
Andy Hsiao, Junhong Ma Blackmer, Severin Schraven, Ying Qi Wen

Abstract
We establish a local to global principle for higher moments over holomorphy rings of global function fields and use it to compute the higher moments of rectangular unimodular matrices and Eisenstein polynomials with coefficients in such rings.



1
Introduction

A classical problem in number theory is to compute the natural density of subsets of the integers. The natural density of a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is given by considering the number of points in
A\cap[-H;H)^{d}
, normalizing it by
(2H)^{d}
- the number of points in the whole “box”
[-H;H)^{d}
- and then taking
H\rightarrow\infty
. A convenient tool to compute such densities in special situations was developed in
[
12
, Lemma 20]
. If the set
A
can be characterised locally in the sense that
A=\bigcap_{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap U_{p})
for some defining sets
U_{p}\subseteq Z_{p}^{d}
(which is the case if
A
is defined by equations modulo prime powers), then under certain
conditions the natural density of
A
can be expressed in terms of the Haar measures of the
U_{p}
. One would naturally expect that such a local to global principle for natural density should hold for any global field. Indeed, a similar result was established for number fields in
[
1
, Proposition 3.2]
. Finally, the case of global function fields was covered in
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
.

One should think of the natural density as a substitute for a finite Haar measure on the integers. It then becomes natural to ask whether one can make sense of the notion of expected value (or any higher moment). If
U_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
are again the defining sets of our set in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, then expected value is defined as

\displaystyle\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{a\in[-H;H)^{d}}\frac{|\{p\ :\ p\text{ prime},\ a\in U_{p}\}|}{(2H)^{d}}.

This means, for the elements in the box
[-H;H)^{d}
we count in how many defining sets it is contained, average over the total number of points in the box and let the side length of the box go to infinity. This notion was considered in
[
13
]
for Eisenstein polynomials.

One can make a similar definition of expected value over the ring of algebraic integers of number fields or for higher moments. In
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
a local to global principle for higher moments over number fields has been established based on a local to global principle for natural densities
[
12
,
1
]
. In
[
6
]
the notion of natural density for holomorphy rings of global function fields has been introduced (slightly different from the one in
[
10
]
), where the boxes are replaced by Riemann-Roch spaces (see Section
2
for precise definitions). For further generalization and overview we directed the interested reader to
[
2
]
. In this paper we will prove a local to global principle for higher moments over global function fields and show how to use this tool for some interesting examples.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we recall the local to global principle for the natural density in global function fields as introduced in
[
4
]
. In Section
3
we will prove our main theorem, the local to global principle for higher moments over function fields and in Section
4
we will apply it to some examples (coprime pairs, affine Eisenstein polynomials and rectangular unimodular matrices, all with coefficients in the holomorphy ring of some global function field).

2
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the basic definitions and results from
[
4
]
. We follow the terminology of
[
14
]
for function fields and related concepts.

Let
F
be a global function field, that is a finite extension
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes a finite field with
q
elements.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{P}
a valuation ring of
F
, having maximal ideal
P
. The set of all such places of
F
will be denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{F}
.
If
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq\mathbb{P}_{F}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

S_{t}=\{P\in S\ :\ \text{deg}(P)\geq t\}.

(2.1)

Moreover, we write
\mathcal{O}_{S}
to denote the holomorphy ring of
S

\mathcal{O}_{S}=\bigcap_{P\in S}\mathcal{O}_{P}.

The easiest example of holomorphy ring is
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
as this consists of the intersection of all the valuation rings of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
different from the infinite place (see
[
14
, Section 1.2]
).
We denote by
\text{Div}(F)
the set of divisors of
F
, i.e. the free abelian group on the set
\mathbb{P}_{F}
. Furthermore,
for
D=\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}n_{P}P\in\text{Div}(F)
we define
v_{P}(D)=n_{P}
. The support
\text{supp}(D)
of
D
is defined as the finite subset of
\mathbb{P}_{F}
for which
v_{P}(D)
is non-zero. For
D,\widetilde{D}\in\text{Div}(F)
we write
D\leq\widetilde{D}
if and only if
v_{P}(D)\leq v_{P}(\widetilde{D})
for all
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
. Note that this defines a partial order on
\text{Div}(F)
.
Moreover, we will write
D\geq 0
whenever
v_{P}(D)\geq 0
for all
P
in
\mathbb{P}_{F}
. Let

\text{Div}^{+}(F)=\{D\in\text{Div}(F)\;|\;D\geq 0\}.

For
S\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{F}
let
\mathcal{D}_{S}
be the subset of divisors of
\text{Div}^{+}(F)
having support contained in the complement of
S
.

Let
(a_{D})_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, then we write for
a\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\lim_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}a_{D}=a

if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
D_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
such that for all
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
with
D\geq D_{\varepsilon}
one has
|a-a_{D}|<\varepsilon
. Similarly one defines
\limsup_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}a_{D}
and
\liminf_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}a_{D}
.
For further information on Moore–Smith convergence see
[
3
, Chapter 2]
.

We define the upper density for
A\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}
as follows:

\overline{\rho}_{S}(A):=\limsup_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}\frac{|A\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}|}{q^{\ell(D)d}},

where
\mathcal{L}(D)
is the Riemann-Roch space attached to the divisor
D
and
\ell(D)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{L}(D))
.
Analogously, one can give a notion of lower density
\underline{\rho}_{S}
by replacing the limes superior by the limit inferior. Whenever these two quantities coincide, we define the density of
A
as
\overline{\rho}_{S}(A)=\underline{\rho}_{S}(A)=\rho_{S}(A)
.

This definition of density coincides with the classical definition,

\displaystyle\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A\cap\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\ :\ \deg(f)\leq d\}|}{|\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\ :\ \deg(f)\leq d\}|},

when
\mathcal{O}_{S}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
(that is
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
and
S
is all the places except the infinite place). This was used in
[
10
]
to compute the density of square-free, multivariate polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
The reader should notice that in the case of the coordinate ring of the
\mathbb{P}^{d}
a similar notion of density was provided in
[
11
]
.

For a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{P}
let us denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
its completion. As
F
is a global function field,
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
admits a normalized Haar measure, which we denote by
\mu_{P}
. By abuse of notation we will denote the product measure on
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
also by
\mu_{P}
.
For
U\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}^{n}
we denote by
\partial U
the boundary of
U
with respect to the
P
-adic metric.

In
[
4
]
the following local to global principle for densities over global function fields was shown (this is an extension of the work of
[
10
]
).

Theorem 2.1

(
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
d
be a positive integer,
S
be a proper, nonempty subset of places of

,

as defined in (

) and

(2.2)

Let

be defined by

\displaystyle{\sum_{P\in S}\mu_{P}(U_{P})}

(ii)

Theorem 2.2

(

)

.

Note that in

The next Corollary follows from Theorem

. It relates the density of the defining sets to their Haar measures and will play a crucial role in the proof of our main theorem.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
F
be a global function field with full field of constant equal to

. Let

be a positive integer,

be a proper, nonempty subset of the places of

(2.5)

Proof.

Define
V_{P}=U_{P}
for

and
V_{P}=\emptyset
otherwise. Then
(V_{P})_{P\in S}
satisfies the assumption of Theorem

and

corresponds to

with

.

3
Higher moments

For a family
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
, we would like to define the expected value of the number of sets
U_{P}
such that a random element
a\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}
lies in and also the corresponding higher moments.

Definition 1
.

Let
F
be a global function field with full field of constant equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
n
and
d
be positive integers,
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq\mathbb{P}_{F}
. Suppose
U_{P}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
.
Then we define
the
n
-th moment of the system
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
to be

\mu_{n}=\lim\limits_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}\displaystyle{\frac{\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}}\lvert\{P\in S\mid a\in U_{P}\}\rvert^{n}}{q^{\ell(D)d}}},

(3.1)

if it exists.
We call
\mu_{1}
the expected value of the system
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
.

Our main theorem gives a way to compute higher moments for certain systems.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d
and
n
be positive integers. Let
F
be a global function field with full field of constant equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
S
be a proper, nonempty subset of
\mathbb{P}_{F}

\mathcal{O}_{S}
be the holomorphy ring of

be a measurable set such that
\mu_{P}(\partial(U_{P}))=0
. Let
S_{t}\coloneqq\{P\in S\mid\deg{(P)}\geq t\}
.
If

\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{\rho}_{S}\left(\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}\mid a\in U_{P}\text{ for some }P\in S_{t}\}\right)=0

(3.2)

is satisfied, and for some
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
there exist absolute constants

, such that for all

\left|\left\{P\in S\mid\deg(P)>c^{\prime}\deg(D)^{\alpha},a\in U_{P}\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}\right\}\right|<c

(3.3)

and that there exists a sequence
(v_{P})_{P\in S}
, such that for all

and all

\displaystyle\left|\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}U_{P_{j}}\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}\right|

\displaystyle\leq q^{\ell(D)d}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}v_{P_{j}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in S}v_{P}

\displaystyle\text{converges},

(3.5)

then it follows that

\displaystyle\mu_{n}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}\displaystyle{\frac{\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}}\lvert\{P\in S\mid a\in U_{P}\}\rvert^{n}}{q^{\ell(D)d}}}

exists and
\mu_{n}<\infty
.
For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
we denote by

\mu_{n}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\begin{Bmatrix}n\\

\forall i<j\in\{1,\dots,l\},\ P_{i}\neq P_{j}\end{subarray}}\prod\limits_{m=1}^{l}\mu_{P_{m}}(U_{P_{m}}).

(3.6)

.

2.

\displaystyle\begin{Bmatrix}n\\

4.

Proof.

We fix

For

\displaystyle\tau(a,P)=\begin{cases}1,&a\in U_{P},\\

For

\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}}\frac{\left(\sum\limits_{P\in S}\tau(a,P)\right)^{n}}{q^{\ell(D)d}}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}R_{j}(M,D),

where for all

, we define

\displaystyle R_{j}(M,D)\coloneqq\sum_{a\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}}\frac{\left(\sum\limits_{P\in S_{M}}\tau(a,P)\right)^{n-j}\left(\sum\limits_{P\in S,\ \deg(P)<M}\tau(a,P)\right)^{j}}{q^{\ell(D)d}}.

First we show that for all

the terms

are negligible for

going to infinity. We define

\displaystyle l_{a,D}\coloneqq\left|\left\{P\in S\mid\deg(P)>c^{\prime}\deg(D)^{\alpha},a\in U_{P}\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}\right\}\right|.

Then by (

and all

. Thus, we get for

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{-\ell(D)d}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\hskip-21.33955pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}:\\

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\begin{Bmatrix}n\\
l\end{Bmatrix}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1},\dots,P_{\ell(\tau)}\in S\\
\deg(P_{1}),\dots,\deg(P_{l(\tau)})<M\\
\forall i<j\in\{1,\dots,l(\tau)\},\ P_{i}\neq P_{j}\end{subarray}}\rho\left(\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{l(\tau)}U_{P_{j}}\cap\mathcal{O}^{d}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\begin{Bmatrix}n\\
l\end{Bmatrix}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1},\dots,P_{l}\in S\\
\deg(P_{1}),\dots,\deg(P_{l})<M\\
\forall i<j\in\{1,\dots,l(\tau)\},\ P_{i}\neq P_{j}\end{subarray}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{l}\mu_{P_{j}}(U_{P_{j}}).

Taking
M\rightarrow\infty
yields (
3.6
).
Using (
3.5
) and the crude estimate
\begin{Bmatrix}n\\
l\end{Bmatrix}\leq n^{n}
, one gets

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\leq n^{n+1}\left(1+\sum\limits_{P\in S}\mu_{P}(U_{P})\right)^{n}<\infty.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

We briefly compare this with the results in
[
8
,
7
]
. There an alternative definition of expected value (respectively higher moment) was used. Namely, under the same assumptions for
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
as in Definition
1
they define

I=\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}\mid a\in U_{P}\text{ for infinitely many }P\in S\}

(3.8)

and
\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}_{I}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}\setminus I
.
Then they define
the
n
-th moment of the system
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
to be

\lim\limits_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}}\displaystyle{\frac{\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{d}_{I}}\lvert\{P\in S\mid a\in U_{P}\}\rvert^{n}}{q^{\ell(D)d}}},

(3.9)

if it exists. Let us call this the renormalized
n
th moment.

This means that they consider, in our language, the moments of the sets
(U_{P}\setminus I)_{P\in S}
. The restriction to the complement of
I
prevents the moment from being infinity in a trivial fashion. The rationale in
[
8
,
7
]
is that the moment of a random variable with respect to a probability measure does not change when altered on a null set. Furthermore, for system
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
satisfying (
2.2
), one can show that the set of elements which lie in infinitely many sets
U_{P}
has density zero (see the lemma below). Hence, the renormalized moment should be seen as a renormalized version of the more natural notion in Definition
1
. If
(U_{P}))_{P\in S}
satisfies (
3.2
),
(U_{P}\setminus I)_{P\in S}
satisfies all the conditions of Theorem
2.1
and
I
is closed in all
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
for
P\in S
, then the
n
th moment of
(U_{P}\setminus I)_{P\in S}
coincides with the regularized
n
th moment.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F
be a global function field with full field of constant equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
d
a positive integer,
S
be a proper, nonempty subset of places of
F
,
S_{t}
as defined in (

) and
\mathcal{O}_{S}
the holomorphy ring of
S
.
For any

, let

be a Borel-measurable and

defined as in (

).

1.

For all
P\in S
holds

\displaystyle\mu_{P}(U_{P}\setminus I)=\mu_{P}(U_{P}).

2.

If
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
satisfies (

), then

\rho_{S}(I)=\rho_{S}(\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}\mid a\in U_{P}\text{ for infinitely many }P\in S\})=0.

Proof.

For the first part we note that
F
is a finite extension of

and therefore countable. Thus,

is countable too. Recall that

is a Haar measure and hence,

If

4
Applications

In this section, we will verify the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, i.e. compute all higher moments, for various examples that were considered in the existing literature.

4.1
Coprime
n
-tuples

In this subsection we will compute all higher moments of coprime
n
-tuples. Here a coprime
n
-tuple denotes an
n
-tuple such that all entries are coprime over a specified ring. The computation of the natural density of coprime pairs over the integers is classical and goes back to Mertens and Césaro in the
1870
’s.

The density of coprime
n
-tuples over holomorphy rings have been calculated in
[
6
]
. We now compute the higher moments over holomorphy rings.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F
be a global function field with full field of constant equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer. Let
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq\mathbb{P}_{F}
and let
\mathcal{O}_{S}
be the holomorphy ring of
S
. Define the system
U_{P}=(P\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P})^{n}\setminus\{0\}
for each
P\in S
. Then all moments exist and are given by (
3.6
), where

\mu_{P}(U_{P})=q^{-n\deg(P)}.

(4.1)

Proof.

We show that the system satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
. We first check that condition (
3.2
) is satisfied using Theorem
2.2
. Consider the polynomials
f(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=x_{1}
and
g(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=x_{2}
. Then for positive integers
t
, define

\displaystyle S_{t}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n}\mid f(a)\in P\text{ and }g(a)\in P\text{ for some }P\in S_{t}\}

\displaystyle=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n}\mid a_{1}\in P\text{ and }a_{2}\in P\text{ for some }P\in S_{t}\}.

Note that
A_{t}=\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}\mid a\in U_{P}\text{ for some }P\in S_{t}\}
is a subset
A_{t}\subset S_{t}(f,g)
. By Theorem
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{\rho}_{S}(A_{t})\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{\rho}_{S}(S_{t}(f,g))=0.

So
\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{\rho}_{S}(A_{t})=0
.

Next we check that condition (
3.3
) is satisfied. Let
\alpha=1
. Fix
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{n}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
. As
a\neq(0,\dots,0)
, we can without loss of generality assume that
a_{1}\neq 0
. Now by
[
14
, Theorem 1.4.11]
, we have

\sum_{P\in S}\deg(P)v_{P}(a_{1})=-\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}\setminus S}\deg(P)v_{P}(a_{1}).

Recall that
x\in\mathcal{L}(D)
implies that
v_{P}(x)\geq-v_{P}(D)
. So we have for
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in S}\deg(P)v_{P}(a_{1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}\setminus S}\deg(P)v_{P}(a_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}\setminus S}\deg(P)v_{P}(D)

\displaystyle\leq\deg(D).

For a lower bound, we have for any constant
c^{\prime}>0

\sum_{P\in S,\deg(P)\geq c^{\prime}\deg(D)}\deg(P)v_{P}(a_{1})\leq\sum_{P\in S}\deg(P)v_{P}(a_{1})\leq\deg(D).

Now since
P\setminus=\{x\in F\mid v_{P}(x)\geq 1\}
and
a_{1}\neq 0
, we have

c^{\prime}\deg(D)\cdot|\{P\in S\ :\ \deg(P)\geq\deg(D),a\in U_{P}\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{n}\}|\leq\deg(D),

hence condition (
3.3
) is satisfied for any choice of
c^{\prime}>0
.

For condition (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), let
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\in S
be pairwise distinct places. Then we get for
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}

\mathcal{L}(D)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}(P_{j}\mathcal{O}_{P_{j}})=\mathcal{L}(D_{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}}),

where
D_{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}}
is defined by

v_{\widetilde{P}}(D_{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }\widetilde{P}\in\{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\},\\
v_{\widetilde{P}}(D)&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
\deg(P_{1}),\dots,\deg(P_{r})\leq c^{\prime}\deg(D)
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\deg(D_{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}})=\deg(D)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(P_{j})\geq\deg(D)-rc^{\prime}\deg(D)=(1-rc^{\prime})\deg(D).

Hence, if we pick
0<c^{\prime}<\frac{1}{r}
, then we can use the Riemann-Roch theorem
[
14
, Theorem 1.4.17 (b.)]
and obtain that there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
F
and
c^{\prime}
such that for all
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
with
\deg(D)\geq C
holds

\displaystyle\ell(D_{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}})

\displaystyle=\deg(D_{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}})+1-g=\deg(D)+1-g-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(P_{j})

\displaystyle=\ell(D)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\deg(P_{j}),

where
g
is the genus of
F
. Thus, we obtain for
\deg(D)\geq C

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}(D)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}P_{j}|=|\mathcal{L}(D_{P_{1},\dots,P_{r}})|=q^{\ell(D)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}q^{-\deg(P_{j})}.

Hence, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
with
\deg(D)\geq C
and all pairwise distinct places
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\in S
with
\deg(P_{1}),\dots,\deg(P_{r})\leq\frac{1}{2r}\deg(D)
holds

|\mathcal{L}(D)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}P_{j}|=q^{\ell(D)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}q^{-\deg(P_{j})}.

(4.2)

Thus, we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}(D)^{n}\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}U_{P_{j}}|=|\mathcal{L}(D)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}P_{j}|^{n}=q^{n\ell(D)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}q^{-n\deg(P_{j})}.

So for each
P\in S
, we choose
v_{P}=q^{-n\deg(P)}
.
This sequence satisfies (
3.4
). The series
\sum_{P\in S}q^{-n\deg(P)}
is dominated by the Zeta function
Z(q^{-n})=\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}q^{-n\deg(P)}
. For
n\geq 2
, the Zeta function converges by
[
14
, Prop. 5.1.6]
, hence (
3.5
).
Therefore, we can invoke Theorem
3.1
.
Note that
(P\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P})^{n}
is a subgroup of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}^{n}
and singletons are null sets, so (
4.1
) holds true as

\mu_{P}((P\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P})^{n}\setminus\{0\})=\mu_{P}((P\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P})^{n})=|\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}^{n}/(P\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P})^{n}|^{-1}=q^{-n\deg(P)}.

∎

4.2
Affine Eisenstein polynomials

In this subsection, we will compute all higher moments of affine Eisenstein polynomials. The affine Eisenstein polynomials over holomorphy rings can be used to study totally ramified extension, see
[
4
]
and the references therein for more details. In said paper, also the density of affine Eisenstein polynomials is computed (see
[
4
, Theorem 3.6]
). The density of the shifted/affine Eisenstein polynomials over number fields have been computed in
[
5
]
and the higher moments over number fields have been considered in
[

For this section we will by abuse of notation identify polynomials of degree

with the corresponding

-tuple of coefficients.

Let
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq\mathbb{P}_{F}
and

\displaystyle a_{d}\notin P,a_{0}\notin P^{2}\text{ and }a_{i}\in P\ \forall i\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}.

is said to be Eisenstein if there exists
P\in S

is

This will be the system for Eisenstein polynomials in
\mathcal{O}_{S}[x]
as
U_{P}\cap\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d+1}
represents exactly the
P
-Eisenstein polynomials.

Next we introduce shifted Eisenstein polynomials. For
P\in S

-Eisenstein polynomial if there exists

-Eisenstein polynomial.

-Eisenstein.

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\ I+II.

Note that the coefficients of

and hence,

Let

Now suppose

Next we are going to verify conditions (

.

As for coprime pairs we choose

and

for

Let

for each

-Eisenstein for some

\displaystyle=a_{d-2}+\binom{d-1}{1}t_{i}a_{d-1}+\binom{d}{2}t_{i}^{2}a_{d}

\displaystyle a_{d-3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle a_{d-3}+\binom{d-2}{1}t_{i}a_{d-2}+\binom{d-1}{2}t_{i}^{2}a_{d-1}+\binom{d}{3}t_{i}^{3}a_{d}

We partition

\displaystyle=\overline{a}_{d-1}(a_{d},t_{i})

\displaystyle a_{d-2}

\displaystyle a_{d-1}

\displaystyle\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Q_{m})

\displaystyle a_{d-2}

4.3
Rectangular unimodular matrices

In this subsection we compute all higher moments of rectangular unimodular matrices over function fields. Rectangular unimodular matrices have already been considered in the literature in similar situations. Namely, their density over number fields has been calculated in
[
9
]
; their density over function fields has been done in
[
4
]
, and their expected value over rationals has been established in
[
8
]
.

Let us recall the definition of rectangular unimodular matrices over a Dedekind domain.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Dedekind domain and
n<m\in\mathbb{N}
. A matrix
M\in\text{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathcal{D})
is called rectangular unimodular, if and only if
M\mod P
has full rank for all non-zero prime ideal
P
of
\mathcal{D}

[
9
, Proposition 3]
. This is equivalent to saying that the matrix has a basic minor which is not contained in
P\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
, where a basic minor of a matrix is the determinant of a square submatrix that is of maximal size.
Note that rectangular unimodular matrices in the case
n=1
correspond to coprime pairs.
We will use (
3.1
) and ideas of
[
8
]
to compute all higher moments of rectangular unimodular matrices.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
n,m
be positive integers such that
n<m
and
F
be a global function field with full field of constants equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq\mathbb{P}_{F}
. For any
P\in S
, let
V_{P}
be the set of matrices in
\text{Mat}_{n\times m}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P})
for which the ideal generated by the basic minors is contained in
P\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
and let
I
be the set of matrices contained in infinitely many
V_{P}
. We define
U_{P}=V_{P}\setminus I
.

Then the system
(U_{P})_{P\in S}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
3.1
, and the higher moments are given by (
3.6
), where

\mu_{P}(U_{P})=1-\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-q^{-deg(P)(m-i)}\right).

(4.12)

Proof.

We have (
4.12
) due to the computation in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
and the fact that
\mu_{P}(U_{P}\cap I)=0
(by Lemma
3.4
). We are left to check that the assumptions of Theorem (
3.1
) are satisfied.

We start by noting that
V_{P}
is clopen and
I
is closed, hence
\mu_{P}(\partial U_{P})\leq\mu_{P}(I)=0
by Lemma
3.4
. Condition (
3.2
) is satisfied as shown in
[
4
, Theorem 13]
. We want to show Condition (
3.3
) is satisfied for
\alpha=1
.
Let
M\in\text{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\setminus I
. Fix a submatrix of
M
corresponding to some basic minor of
M
, and denote it by
A
. When
M\in U_{P}
, by definition, there exist some
A
such that
\det(A)\neq 0
. We choose such an
A
. One can see that when
M\in U_{P}\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{n\times m}
, then
A\in\mathcal{L}(D)^{n\times n}
. Thus,

\displaystyle v_{P}(\det(A))

\displaystyle=v_{P}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\text{sgn}(\sigma)\prod^{n}_{i=1}A_{i,\sigma(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{n}\\
\prod^{n}_{i=1}A_{i,\sigma(i)}\neq 0\end{subarray}}v_{P}\left(\prod^{n}_{i=1}A_{i,\sigma(i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-nv_{P}(D).

This implies, as for coprime pairs, that for
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in S}\deg(P)v_{P}(\det(A))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}\setminus S}\deg(P)v_{P}(\det(A))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}\setminus S}\deg(P)nv_{P}(D)

\displaystyle\leq n\deg(D).

Hence, we can obtain a lower bound, for any constant
c^{\prime}>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in S\\
\deg(P)\geq c^{\prime}\deg(D)\end{subarray}}\deg(P)v_{P}(\det(A))\leq\sum_{P\in S}\deg(P)v_{P}(\det(A))\leq n\deg(D).

Since
0\neq\det(A)\in P\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
,
and
P=\{x\in F\mid v_{P}(x)\geq 1\}
, we have

c^{\prime}\deg(D)|\{P\in S:\deg(P)>\deg(D),M\in U_{P}\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{n\times m}_{I}\}|\leq n\deg(D).

Hence condition (
3.3
) is satisfied for any
c^{\prime}>0
.

Now we check condition (
3.4
) and (
3.5
). For each
A\in{\mathcal{L}(D)}^{n\times n}
such that
0\neq\det(A)\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}
, by definition, there exists
M\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}U_{P_{i}}\cap{\mathcal{L}(D)_{I}}^{n\times m}
containing
A
as a submatrix. There are less or equal than
\binom{m}{n}|\mathcal{L}(D)|^{nm-n^{2}}
such choices per matrix
M
. So we have

\lvert\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}U_{P_{i}}\cap{\mathcal{L}(D)_{I}}^{n\times m}\rvert\leq\binom{m}{n}|\mathcal{L}(D)|^{nm-n^{2}}\lvert\{A\in{\mathcal{L}(D)}^{n\times n}\mid 0\neq\det(A)\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}\}\rvert.

Fix an arbitrary
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
. Define
\phi_{1}
to be the inclusion map
\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathcal{L}(D))\rightarrow\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
, and
\phi_{2}
to be the natural quotient map
\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\rightarrow\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}}})
. Let
\phi=\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{1}
. As
\{A\in{\mathcal{L}(D)}^{n\times n}\mid 0\neq\det(A)\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}\}
is a subset of
\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathcal{L}(D))
, we get

\displaystyle\lvert\{A\in{\mathcal{L}(D)}^{n\times n}\mid 0\neq\det(A)\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}\}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\{B\in\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}}})\mid\det(B)=0\}\rvert\cdot\lvert\ker(\phi)\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\{B\in\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}}})\mid\det(B)=0\}\rvert\cdot\lvert\mathcal{L}(D)\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}\rvert^{n^{2}}.

Recall that
P_{i}\cap\mathcal{O}_{S}
and
P_{j}\cap\mathcal{O}_{S}
are distinct maximal ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{S}
for
P_{i}\neq P_{j}
(see
[
14
, Prop. 3.2.9.]
) and therefore, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the following is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-modules

\displaystyle\pi:\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{P_{i}}}),

\displaystyle\left(a_{jk}+\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}\right)_{1\leq j,k\leq n}

\displaystyle\mapsto\left((a_{jk}+P_{1})_{1\leq j,k\leq n},\dots,(a_{jk}+P_{r})_{1\leq j,k\leq n}\right).

Clearly we have that
\det(a_{jk}+\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i})=0\mod\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}
if and only if
\det(a_{jk}+P_{i})=0\mod P_{i}
for all
P_{i}
. Therefore,

\lvert\{B\in\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}}})\mid\det(B)=0\}\rvert=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\lvert\{B_{i}\in\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{P_{i}}})\mid\det(B_{i})=0\}\rvert.

We know that the quotient ring
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{P_{i}}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg(P_{i})}}
(see
[
14
, Prop. 3.2.9.]
). So we have

\displaystyle\lvert\{B_{i}\in\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\nicefrac{{\mathcal{O}_{S}}}{{P_{i}}})\mid\det(B_{i})=0\}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\text{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg(P_{i})}})\setminus\text{GL}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg(P_{i})}})\rvert

\displaystyle=q^{deg(P_{i})n^{2}}-\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(q^{\deg(P_{i})n}-q^{\deg(P_{i})k})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}q^{\deg(P_{i})n(n-1)}.

By (
4.2
), there exist a constant
C>0
such that for all
D\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
with
\deg(D)\geq C
, it holds that

\lvert\mathcal{L}(D)\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}P_{i}\rvert=q^{\ell(D)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}q^{-\deg(P_{j})}.

Now, combine everything, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}U_{P_{i}}\cap\mathcal{L}(D)^{n\times m}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\binom{m}{n}q^{(nm-n^{2})\deg(D)}q^{\ell(D)n^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}q^{-n^{2}\deg(P_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\big(2^{n}q^{\deg(P_{i})n(n-1)}\big)

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}q^{nm\ell(D)}\prod_{j=1}^{r}q^{-n\deg(P)}

for a constant
C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
n,m
. Observe that
\sum\limits_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}q^{-n\deg(P)}
is the Zeta function
Z(q^{-n})
. By
[
14
, Prop. 5.1.6.]
, it converges when
n>1
. The case

corresponds to coprime
m
-tuples and is covered by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

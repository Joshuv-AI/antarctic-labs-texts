Mixed Isogeometric Methods for Linear Elasticity with Weakly Imposed Symmetry
Jeremias Arf

Abstract
We consider and discretize a mixed formulation for linear elasticity with weakly imposed symmetry in two and three dimensions. Whereas existing methods mainly deal with simplicial or polygonal meshes, we take advantage of isogeometric analysis (IGA) and consequently allow for shapes with curved boundaries. To introduce the discrete spaces we use isogeometric discrete differential forms defined by proper B-spline spaces. For the proposed schemes a proof of well-posedness and an error estimate are given. Further we discuss our ansatz by means of different numerical examples.



Keywords—
elasticity, de Rham complex, mixed method, isogeometric analysis

Introduction

In general it is hard to define numerical methods for partial differential equations (PDEs) which guarantee convergence and stability such that the approximate solution depends continuously on (initial-)boundary conditions as well as on source functions. Thus, it is remarkable that Arnold, Falk and Winther established with the Finite Element Exterior Calculus (FEEC)
[
2
,
3
]
an abstract guideline for the design of several mixed formulations in order to have both, stability and convergence. The underlying structure is related to the so-called Hodge-Laplacians associated to some Hilbert complex and the structure-preserving discretization of such complexes. In
[
3
]
the needed key properties for finite-dimensional ansatz and test function spaces are derived and moreover the polynomial differential forms on simplicial meshes are introduced. Latter spaces are fundamental objects in the development of mixed methods. However, as already indicated the underlying approach of FEEC is quite abstract and the results allow for other discrete spaces. For instance, Buffa et al.
[
16
]
utilized isogeometric analysis (IGA) to define structure-preserving discrete de Rham chains. With such discrete isogeometric differential forms one obtains stable approximations e.g. within the field of Maxwell’s equations, where curved geometries can be handled due to the IGA ansatz. Although we find in literature different Hilbert complexes corresponding to interesting PDEs, like the
Hessian complex
in linearized General Relativity (see
[
26
]
), it is not always easy to apply directly the steps from
[
3
]
. Especially the definition of discrete subcomplexes equipped with bounded cochain projections might be complicated.
This point becomes evident if complexes with higher derivatives are studied. One well-known example is the
elasticity complex
in which second derivatives appear; cf.
[
23
]
. Admittedly, suitable Finite Elements for this chain in
2D
were introduced, e.g. in
[
4
,
7
]
, but one needs a lot of degrees of freedom per element and the basis definition is not straightforward. Another way to use FEEC is the ansatz
complexes from complexes
, where two or more Hilbert complexes are used to discretize PDEs. This approach can be seen in
[
26
]
, but also in
[
6
]
in the context of linear elasticity. Due to the increased number of complexes the authors in the last two publications are able to reduce the discretization problem to classical de Rham sequences, which are well-understood and easy to approximate.

We want to follow this idea of coupled complexes in this article. To be more precise, we use the connection between de Rham chains and linear elasticity as described in
[
6
,
19
]
and combine the underlying formulation with weakly imposed symmetry with the concept of isogeometric differential forms from
[
16
]
. Whereas already existing methods like in
[

27
,
19
]
mainly use Finite Elements on simplicial or polygonal meshes, we want to exploit IGA to incorporate computational domains with curved boundaries in
2D
and
3D
.

2
Abstract framework

In this section we study a special class of variational formulations derived from coupled Hilbert complexes. Although we are interested in the case of linear elasticity later, we choose here an abstract notion for reasons of clearness.

For the theoretical background we use and recommend the publications
[
3
,
19

Definition 1

(Closed Hilbert complexes)
.

A closed Hilbert complex is a sequence of Hilbert spaces
(W^{k})_{k}
together with closed densely defined linear operators
d^{k}\colon V^{k}\subset W^{k}\rightarrow W^{k+1}
, where
V^{k}
is the domain of
d^{k}
and further we have closed ranges
\mathfrak{R}(d^{k})\subset\mathfrak{Ker}(d^{k+1})\subset W^{k+1}
. This means
d^{k+1}\circ d^{k}=0,\ \forall k
.

The domain spaces
(V^{k},d^{k})
define the corresponding domain complex which determines a bounded Hilbert complex, where the
V^{k}
are equipped with the graph inner product
\langle v,w\rangle_{V}\coloneqq\langle v,w\rangle+\langle d^{k}v,d^{k}w\rangle
. We write for the associated norms
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{V}^{2}\coloneqq\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert d^{k}v\right\rVert^{2}
. Here and in the following
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
stands for the norm induced by the underyling Hilbert spaces
W^{k}
and we write just
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{W^{k}}
. Such complexes are key objects within the theory of FEEC, where mixed formulations of Hodge Laplacians are considered. Especially the stable discretization of various mixed problems are closely related to Hilbert complexes. That is why we will exploit this notion, too, in order to define an abstract mixed weak formulation below.

2.1
Continuous problem

Now we restrict ourselves to a special case of Hilbert complexes as explained in the assumption below.

Assumption 1

(Coupled Hilbert complexes )
.

Let

(W^{k},d^{k})

The mapping

Lemma 1
.

Let
R,Q
be two Hilbert spaces.
Let
T\colon R\rightarrow Q
be a bounded linear mapping w.r.t the norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{R},\ \left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{Q}
induced by the inner products. Further assume

\displaystyle\underset{{q}\in Q}{\inf}\ \underset{{w}\in R}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle T{w},{q}\rangle_{Q}}{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert_{R}\ \left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{Q}}\geq C_{{\dagger}}>0.

Then there is for all
{q}\in Q
an element
{w}\in R
s.t.
T{w}={q}
and
\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert_{R}\leq\frac{1}{C_{{\dagger}}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{Q}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof steps of
[
5
, Lemma 2]
.

For reasons of clarification we write for the
Q
and
R
inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q}
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{R}
respectively. The Hilbert space adjoint
T^{*}
exists and satisfies

C_{{\dagger}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{Q}\leq\underset{{w}\in R}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle T{w},{q}\rangle_{Q}}{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert_{R}}=\underset{{w}\in R}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle{w},T^{*}{q}\rangle_{R}}{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert_{R}}=\left\lVert T^{*}{q}\right\rVert_{R},\ \ q\in Q.

Thus we have injectivity of
T^{*}
and the left-inverse of
T^{*}
is bounded with operator norm
\leq 1/C_{{\dagger}}
. This implies the surjectivity of
T
and the right-inverse
T^{{\dagger}}
is bounded by
1/C_{{\dagger}}
.
∎

Definition 3

There are positive constants

\displaystyle\textup{(S1)}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathfrak{A}({\tau},{\tau})\geq C_{S1}\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{V}^{2},\ \forall{\tau}\in kB,

we see

d^{n-1}\tau_{h}=d^{n-1}\Pi_{h}^{n-1}\tau=\Pi_{h}^{n}d^{n-1}\tau=v_{h}.

Furthermore we find
\tau^{\mathfrak{Ker}}_{h}\in\mathfrak{Ker}(d^{n-1})\cap V_{h}^{n-1},\ \tau^{\perp}_{h}\in\big(\mathfrak{Ker}(d^{n-1})\cap V_{h}^{n-1}\big)^{\perp}
s.t.
\tau_{h}=\tau^{\mathfrak{Ker}}_{h}+\tau^{\perp}_{h}
, where the orthogonal complement is taken within
V_{h}^{n-1}
. Since
(V_{h}^{k},d^{k})
define a structure-preserving complex discretization we can apply the discrete Poincaré inequality
[
2
, Theorem 3.6]
, i.e.

C_{P}\left\lVert d^{n-1}\tau_{h}\right\rVert=C_{P}\left\lVert d^{n-1}\tau^{\perp}_{h}\right\rVert\geq\left\lVert\tau^{\perp}_{h}\right\rVert_{V}.

Above,
C_{P}
does not dependent on
h
, but on the operator norm
\left\lVert\Pi_{h}^{n-1}\right\rVert
.

Then, obviously it is

q_{h}-\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp}\in\bar{V}_{h}^{n},

where
\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}\colon\bar{W}^{n}\rightarrow\bar{V}_{h}^{n}
is the
\bar{W}^{n}
-orthogonal projection onto
\bar{V}_{h}^{n}
. Due to the assumed inf-sup stability, we can apply Lemma
1
, where we choose

T\colon V_{h}^{n-2}\rightarrow\bar{V}_{h}^{n}\ ,\ w\mapsto\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}\bar{d}^{n-1}S_{n-2}w.

One notes the fact
\langle\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}\bar{d}^{n-1}S_{n-2}{w},{q}\rangle=\langle\bar{d}^{n-1}S_{n-2}{w},{q}\rangle,\ \forall w\in V_{h}^{n-2},\ \forall q\in\bar{V}_{h}^{n}
.
Hence, there is a
w_{h}\in V_{h}^{n-2}
with
\left\lVert w_{h}\right\rVert_{V}\leq\frac{1}{C_{IS}}\left\lVert q_{h}-\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp}\right\rVert
and

\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}\bar{d}^{n-1}S_{n-2}w_{h}=Tw_{h}=q_{h}-\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp}.

Next we define

\sigma_{h}\coloneqq d^{n-2}w_{h}+\tau_{h}^{\perp}\in V_{h}^{n-1}.

We have
d^{n-1}\sigma_{h}=d^{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp}=d^{n-1}\tau_{h}=v_{h}
. Besides, it is

\displaystyle\langle S_{n-1}\sigma_{h},q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\sigma_{h},q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}d^{n-2}w_{h},q_{h}\rangle+\langle\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp},q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}\bar{d}^{n-1}S_{n-2}w_{h},q_{h}\rangle+\langle\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp},q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle q_{h}-\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp},q_{h}\rangle+\langle\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp},q_{h}\rangle=\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert^{2}.

Thus, we obtain
\langle S_{n-1}{\sigma}_{h},{q}_{h}\rangle+\langle d^{n-1}{\sigma}_{h},{v}_{h}\rangle=\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert^{2}
. In view of the next inequality chain

\displaystyle\left\lVert d^{n-1}\sigma_{h}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\sigma_{h}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert+\left\lVert w_{h}\right\rVert_{V}+\left\lVert\tau_{h}^{\perp}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert+\ \frac{1}{C_{IS}}\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{C_{IS}}\left\lVert\bar{\Pi}_{h}^{n}S_{n-1}\tau_{h}^{\perp}\right\rVert+C_{P}\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert+\ \frac{1}{C_{IS}}\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert+\frac{1}{C_{IS}}C_{S_{n-1}}\left\lVert\tau_{h}^{\perp}\right\rVert+C_{P}\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\Big(\left\lVert v_{h}\right\rVert+\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert\Big),

we see the validity of (S2) in the discrete case. We remark that in the last line we can choose
C_{2}=1+C_{P}+\frac{1}{C_{IS}}+\frac{C_{P}C_{S_{n-1}}}{C_{IS}}
, with
C_{S_{n-1}}
denoting the continuity constant of
S_{n-1}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

3.1
Linear elasticity with weakly imposed symmetry

In linear elasticity a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\ n\in\{2,3\}
is identified with some elastic body that is loaded with some force distribution
{f}
. In our case
\Omega
will be a bounded Lipschitz domain. The state of the body can be expressed through the displacement field
{u}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
that measures the difference between current configuration
\Omega+{u}
and initial configuration, namely
\Omega
. Governing equations of the underlying model guaranteeing a physically reasonable description of the deformation process are

\displaystyle{A}{\sigma}

\displaystyle={\varepsilon}({u})\hskip 8.5359pt

\displaystyle\textup{in}\ \ \Omega,

(5)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\sigma

\displaystyle={f}\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\textup{in}\ \ \Omega.

(6)

Above
{\varepsilon}({u})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla{u}+(\nabla{u})^{T})
denotes the symmetric gradient and corresponds to the mechanical strain in the linear theory. Further,
{A}\colon\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
is the compliance tensor, which comprises the material properties and
{\sigma}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
is the symmetric stress tensor.
\mathbb{S}
stands for the space of symmetric matrices. A classical conservation of momentum argument relates the stress tensor and the force
{f}
and as a result one obtains equation (
6
). An important, often considered special case are homogeneous and isotropic materials. Then the compliance tensor simplifies to

{A}\sigma=\frac{1}{2\mu}\Big({\sigma}-\frac{\lambda}{n\lambda+2\mu}\textup{tr}({\sigma}){I}\Big),

and the material properties are condensed in form of Lamé coefficients
\lambda\geq 0,\ \mu>0
. Within this document we assume that
A
defines a bounded and coercive bilinear form
\langle A\ \cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})
. Actually, we have to specify some boundary conditions to complete the model, where we first consider the easy case of pure Dirichlet conditions, i.e. we assume
{u}={u}_{D}
on
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
for some given
{u}_{D}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{n})
*
*

*

H^{1/2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the image of the trace mapping
\gamma\colon H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For more information about trace spaces we refer to
[
29
]
.
. Supposing the invertibility of
{A}
one gets a formulation only dependent on the displacement variable as combination of (
5
) and (
6
), namely

\nabla\cdot({C}{\varepsilon}({u}))={f},\ \ \ {C}\coloneqq{{A}}^{-1},

(7)

where
{C}
is the so-called elasticity tensor.
One standard approach to solve the latter equation in case of homogeneous boundary conditions weakly, is the primal formulation:

\textup{Find}\ \ {u}\in{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\ \ \textup{s.t.}\ \ \langle{C}{\varepsilon}(u),\varepsilon({v})\rangle=\langle{f},{v}\rangle,\ \ \forall{v}\in{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

In general the latter formulation is unstable in the (nearly) incompressible regime, i.e. if
\lambda
is large. Another point is the non-locality of stress-strain relations in more general material situations as viscoelasticity. Consequently, the primal weak formulation is not optimal also in view of generalizability. Therefore one can find in literature the Hellinger-Reissner mixed formulation that reads: Find
{\sigma}\in{H}(\Omega,\textup{div},\mathbb{S}),

{v}\in{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
s.t.

\displaystyle\langle{A}{\sigma},{\tau}\rangle\ +\

\displaystyle\langle u,\nabla\cdot{\tau}\rangle\ \color[rgb]{0,0,0}

\displaystyle=\langle{u}_{D},{\tau}\cdot\nu\rangle_{\Gamma},\ \hskip 11.38092pt

\displaystyle\forall{\tau}\in{H}(\Omega,\textup{div},\mathbb{S}),

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot{\sigma},{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{f},{v}\rangle,\ \hskip 11.38092pt

\displaystyle\forall{v}\in{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(8)

where
\nu
in
\langle{u}_{D},{\tau}\cdot\nu\rangle_{\Gamma}
stands for the outer unit normal to the boundary
\Gamma
.

To obtain a mixed weak formulation for the elasticity system that does not require test spaces with strong symmetry, i.e. subspaces of
H(\Omega,\textup{div},\mathbb{S})
, Arnold et al. introduced in
[
6
]
a weak form with weakly imposed symmetry. It can be rewritten equivalently as:
Find

{\sigma}\in{H}(\Omega,\textup{div},\mathbb{M}),\ {u}\in{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
{p}\in{{{L}}}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{s(n)}),\ s(n)=2n-3
s.t.

\displaystyle\langle{A}{\sigma},{\tau}\rangle\ +\

\displaystyle\langle u,\nabla\cdot{\tau}\rangle\ +\

\displaystyle\langle{p},\textup{Skew}\,{\tau}\rangle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

\displaystyle=\langle{u}_{D},{\tau}\cdot\nu\rangle_{\Gamma},\ \hskip 11.38092pt

\displaystyle\forall{\tau}\in{H}(\Omega,\textup{div},\mathbb{M}),

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot{\sigma},{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{f},{v}\rangle,\ \hskip 11.38092pt

\displaystyle\forall{v}\in{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(9)

\displaystyle\langle\textup{Skew}\,{\sigma},{q}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\ \hskip 11.38092pt

\displaystyle\forall{q}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{s(n)}).

We get the last form if one drops the symmetry condition and replaces it by a variational statement, namely we assume

\langle\textup{Skew}\,{\mathcal{\sigma}},q\rangle=0,\ \forall q\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{s(n)}),

which is equivalent to

\int_{\Omega}q_{i}\ (\sigma_{ij}-\sigma_{ji})dx=0,\ \ \forall q_{i}\in L^{2}(\Omega),\ i\neq j\ .

The mentioned constraint is incorporated by means of a Lagrange multiplier
and this explains the additional variable
{p}
and the last line in (

({\sigma},{u})

. Main advantage of the latter problem statement is the obtained flexibility for discretization approaches compared to (
3.1

3.2.1
B-splines and isogeometric de Rham spaces

Here, we state a short overview of B-spline functions, spaces respective, and some basic results in the univariate as well as in the multivariate case.

Following
[
17
,
9
]
for a brief
exposition, we call an increasing sequence of real numbers
\Xi\coloneqq\{\xi_{1}\leq\xi_{2}\leq\dots\leq\xi_{k+\textsf{p}+1}\}
for some
\textsf{p}\in\mathbb{N}

knot vector
, where we assume
0=\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=\dots=\xi_{\textsf{p}+1},\ \xi_{k+1}=\xi_{k+2}=\dots=\xi_{k+\textsf{p}+1}=1
, and call such knot vectors
p
-open.
Further the multiplicity of the
j
-th knot is denoted by
m(\xi_{j})
.
Then the univariate B-spline functions
\widehat{B}_{j,\textsf{p}}(\cdot)
of degree
p
corresponding to a given knot vector
\Xi
is defined recursively by the
Cox-DeBoor formula
:

\displaystyle\widehat{B}_{j,0}(\zeta)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1,\ \ \textup{if}\ \zeta\in[\xi_{j},\xi_{j+1})\\
0,\ \ \textup{else},\end{cases}

and if
\textsf{p}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\ \textup{we set}

\displaystyle\widehat{B}_{j,\textsf{p}}(\zeta)\coloneqq\frac{\zeta-\xi_{j}}{\xi_{j+\textsf{p}}-\xi_{j}}\widehat{B}_{j,\textsf{p}-1}(\zeta)+\frac{\xi_{j+\textsf{p}+1}-\zeta}{\xi_{j+\textsf{p}+1}-\xi_{j+1}}\widehat{B}_{j+1,\textsf{p}-1}(\zeta),

where one puts
0/0=0
to obtain well-definedness. The knot vector
\Xi
without knot repetitions is denoted by
\{\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{N}\}
.

The multivariate extension of the last spline definition is achieved by a tensor product construction. In other words, we set for a given knot vector
\boldsymbol{\Xi}\coloneqq\Xi_{1}\times\dots\times\Xi_{d}
, where the
\Xi_{l}=\{\xi_{1}^{l},\dots,\xi_{k_{l}+\textsf{p}_{l}+1}^{l}\},\ l=1,\dots,d
are
\textsf{p}_{l}
-open, and a given
degree vector

\textbf{{p}}\coloneqq(\textsf{p}_{1},\dots,\textsf{p}_{d})
for the multivariate case

\displaystyle\widehat{B}_{\textbf{i},\textbf{{p}}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})\coloneqq\prod_{l=1}^{d}\widehat{B}_{i_{l},\textsf{p}_{l}}(\zeta_{l}),\ \ \ \ \forall\,\textbf{i}\in\mathit{\mathbf{I}},\ \ \boldsymbol{\zeta}\coloneqq(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{d}),

(10)

with
d
as the underlying dimension of the parametric domain
\widehat{\Omega}\coloneqq(0,1)^{d}
and
I
the multi-index set
\textup{{I}}\coloneqq\{(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})\ |\ 1\leq i_{l}\leq k_{l},\ l=1,\dots,d\}
.

B-splines fulfill several properties and for our purposes the most important ones are:

•

If for all internal knots the multiplicity satisfies
1\leq m(\xi_{j})\leq m\leq\textsf{p},
then the B-spline basis functions
\widehat{B}_{i,\textsf{p}}({\cdot})
are globally
C^{\textsf{p}-m}
-continuous. Therefore we define in this case the regularity integer
r\coloneqq\textsf{p}-m
. Obviously, by the product structure, we get splines
\widehat{B}_{\textbf{i},\textbf{{p}}}(\cdot)
which are
C^{r_{l}}
-smooth w.r.t. the
l
-th coordinate direction if the internal multiplicities fulfill
1\leq m(\xi_{j}^{l})\leq m_{l}\leq\textsf{p}_{l},\ \forall j,\ r_{l}\coloneqq\textsf{p}_{l}-m_{l},
in the multivariate case. In case of
r_{i}<0
we have discontinuous splines w.r.t. the
i
-th coordinate direction.

•

The B-splines
\{\widehat{B}_{\textbf{i},\textbf{{p}}}\ |\ \ \textbf{i}\in\textbf{I}\}
are linearly independent.

•

For univariate splines
\widehat{B}_{i,\textsf{p}},\ \textsf{p}\geq 1,r\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\hat{\partial}_{\zeta}\widehat{B}_{i,\textsf{p}}(\zeta)=\frac{\textsf{p}}{\xi_{i+\textsf{p}}-\xi_{i}}\widehat{B}_{i,\textsf{p}-1}(\zeta)-\frac{\textsf{p}}{\xi_{i+\textsf{p}+1}-\xi_{i+1}}\widehat{B}_{i+1,\textsf{p}-1}(\zeta),

(11)

with
\widehat{B}_{1,\textsf{p}-1}(\zeta)\coloneqq\widehat{B}_{k+1,\textsf{p}-1}(\zeta)\coloneqq 0
.

•

The support of the spline
\widehat{B}_{i,\textsf{p}}
is in the interval
[\xi_{i},\xi_{i+\textsf{p}+1}]
. Moreover, the knots
\psi_{j}
define a subdivision of the interval
(0,1)
and for each element
I=(\psi_{j},\psi_{j+1})
we find a
i
with
(\psi_{j},\psi_{j+1})=(\xi_{i},\xi_{i+1})
.

The space spanned by all univariate splines
\widehat{B}_{i,\textsf{p}}
corresponding to a given knot vector with degree
p
and global regularity
r
is denoted by

S_{\textsf{p}}^{r}\coloneqq\textup{span}\{\widehat{B}_{i,\textsf{p}}\ |\ i=1,\dots,k\}.

For the multivariate case we just define the spline space as the product space

S_{\textsf{p}_{1},\dots,\textsf{p}_{d}}^{r_{1},\dots,r_{d}}\coloneqq S_{\textsf{p}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes S_{\textsf{p}_{d}}^{r_{d}}=\textup{span}\{\widehat{B}_{\textbf{i},\textbf{{p}}}\ |\ \textbf{i}\in\mathit{\mathbf{I}}\}

of proper univariate spline spaces.

To define discrete spaces based on splines we require a parametrization mapping

\textbf{F}\colon\widehat{\Omega}\coloneqq(0,1)^{n}\rightarrow\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

which parametrizes the computational domain.
The knots stored in the knot vector
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
, corresponding to the underlying splines, determine a mesh in the parametric domain
\widehat{\Omega}
, namely
\widehat{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\{K_{\textbf{j}}\coloneqq(\psi_{j_{1}}^{1},\psi_{j_{1}+1}^{1})\times\dots\times(\psi_{j_{n}}^{n},\psi_{j_{n}+1}^{n})\ |\ \textbf{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n}),\ \textup{with}\ 1\leq j_{i}<N_{i}\},
and
with

a unique solution. Then, the corresponding Brezzi stability conditions are fulfilled; cf. Def.
3
.

Proof for
2D
:

As already mentioned, we look only at the inf-sup condition (
4
) which reads for Fig.
7
: There is a constant
C_{IS}>0
with

\underset{{q}_{h}\in P_{h}}{\inf}\ \underset{{w}_{h}\in R_{h}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot{w}_{h},{q}_{h}\rangle}{\left\lVert{w}_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\ \left\lVert{q}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}>C_{IS}.

One notes that
(R_{h},P_{h})
is an isogeometric Taylor-Hood space pair. From
[

, Theorem 4.1]
we get the validity of the above inequality for a suitable

, which is independent of

, as long as
h\leq h_{\max}
for a proper

. This finishes the first proof.

∎

Proof for
3D
:

Again it is enough to look at the following inf-sup condition

\underset{{v}_{h}\in P_{h}}{\inf}\ \underset{{w}_{h}\in R_{h}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{w}_{h}),{v}_{h}\rangle}{\left\lVert{w}_{h}\right\rVert_{\textup{curl}}\ \left\lVert{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}\geq C>0,

which implies the property (S2) in the Brezzi conditions, to prove inf-sup stability. Unfortunately, latter condition is not trivial to check and we follow the proof of the Taylor-Hood stability in

that is based on the so-called macroelement technique. Before we specify this technique, we need to adapt the preliminary steps made in the mentioned paper to our setting. Basic idea for preparations is to first reduce the original inf-sup condition to a different one, that is derived utilizing Verfürth’s trick. Applying this trick, we will consider an even stronger inf-sup condition involving the
H^{1}
-norm in the denominator. The detailed steps for the proof of the inf-sup conditions are outlined in the subsequent sections which result in Lemma

. This lemma finally yields a
h_{\max}>0
and a

s.t. (
15
) is satisfied
\forall h\leq h_{\max}
.
∎

3.2.4
Verfürth’s trick

Here and until Theorem
2
we are in the
3D
setting, i.e.
n=3
.

We want to emphasize that the proof structure and steps are inspired by the stability proof of the Taylor-Hood space pair in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
. Nevertheless, since the spaces we use differ and since in our setting matrix fields appear, we write down the important steps in a detailed fashion. We begin with a new auxiliary inf-sup condition which involves boundary conditions.

First, one notes the fact that

H^{1}_{0}(\widehat{\Omega},\mathbb{M})\cap(S_{\textsf{p},\textsf{p},\textsf{p}}^{r,r,r})^{3\times 3}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{1}({R}_{h,0}),

with row-wise acting
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
. Furthermore, it is
P_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Since
\Xi
defines a
H^{1}
-bounded bijection
{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})\rightarrow{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})
one has

\displaystyle\underset{{q}\in{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\inf}\ \ \underset{{\tau}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert\Xi{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\ \left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}=\underset{{q}\in{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}{\inf}\ \ \underset{{\tau}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot{\tau},{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\ \left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}\geq C_{IS1}>0,

where the last inequality sign is clear due to the classical result of Girault and Raviart
[
20
]
. Clearly, we obtain then

\underset{{q}\in{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\inf}\ \ \underset{{\tau}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\ \left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}}\geq C_{IS2}>0,

(16)

where
C_{IS2}=\frac{C_{IS1}}{C_{c}}
is determined by the inequality
\left\lVert\Xi{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C_{c}\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}
.

Now let
{q}\in P_{h,0}\backslash\{0\}
arbitrary, but fixed. Then we find a
\overline{{\tau}}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})
s.t.

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{\tau}}),{q}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq C_{IS2}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}^{2},

(17)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Integration by parts and (
17
) lead to

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}),{q}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{\tau}}),{q}\rangle+\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\Xi\overline{{\tau}}),{q}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq C_{IS2}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}^{2}+\langle(\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\Xi\overline{{\tau}}),\nabla{q}\rangle.

(18)

And then we see for the last term in (
3.2.4
):

\displaystyle\langle(\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\Xi\overline{{\tau}}),\nabla{q}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|\langle\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\Xi\overline{{\tau}},\nabla{q}\rangle_{L^{2}(K)}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\lVert\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\Xi\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\left\lVert\nabla{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Xi}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{-1}\left\lVert\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}h_{K}\left\lVert\nabla{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Xi}\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{-2}\left\lVert\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla{q}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\Big)^{1/2}.

In view of Assumption
3
and Lemma
8
there is a constant
C_{V1}<\infty
with

\displaystyle\langle(\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}-\Xi\overline{{\tau}}),\nabla{q}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C_{V1}\left\lVert\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{V1}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h},

where
\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h}\coloneqq\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Remark 3
.

Indeed
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{P,h}
defines a norm on
P_{h,0}\subset{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

The above estimate and (
3.2.4
) yield directly the inequality

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\Pi_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}),{q}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq C_{IS2}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}^{2}-C_{V1}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h}.

(19)

Consequently, setting
C_{c}\coloneqq 1+C_{P1}
it is
\left\lVert{\Pi}_{h,0}^{R}\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C_{c}\left\lVert\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}
(see Lemma
8
) and

\displaystyle\underset{{\tau}\in R_{h,0}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C_{c}\left\lVert\overline{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}}\big(C_{IS2}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}^{2}-C_{V1}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{IS2}}{C_{c}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}-\frac{C_{V1}}{C_{c}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h}.

(20)

The last relation holds for all
{q}\in P_{h,0}
.
Now, consider the inf-sup condition

\displaystyle\exists C_{IS}>0\ \textup{s.t.}\ \forall h>0:\underset{{q}\in P_{h,0}}{\inf}\ \ \underset{{\tau}\in R_{h,0}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}}\geq C_{IS}.

(21)

The Verfürth trick (
[
31
]
) can now be used to reduce latter inf-sup condition (
21
) to the new condition

\displaystyle\exists C_{V}>0\ \textup{s.t.}\ \forall h>0:\underset{{\tau}\in R_{h,0}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}}\geq C_{V}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h}.

(22)

Namely, using (
22
) and (
3.2.4
) we have

\displaystyle\underset{{\tau}\in R_{h,0}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{IS2}}{C_{c}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}-\frac{C_{V1}}{C_{c}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{IS2}}{C_{c}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}-\frac{C_{V1}}{C_{c}C_{V}}\underset{{\tau}\in R_{h,0}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\underset{{\tau}\in R_{h,0}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}}\geq C_{IS}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}},

with
C_{IS}=\frac{C_{IS2}}{C_{c}}\Big(1+\frac{C_{V1}}{C_{c}C_{V}}\Big)^{-1}=\frac{C_{IS2}}{C_{c}}\Big(\frac{C_{c}C_{V}+C_{V1}}{C_{c}C_{V}}\Big)^{-1}=\frac{C_{IS2}C_{V}}{C_{c}C_{V}+C_{V1}}
.

Applying the Poincaré inequality
\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq(1+C_{Po})|{\tau}|_{H^{1}}
we can simplify (
22
) to

\displaystyle\exists C_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}>0,\ \textup{s.t.}\ \forall h>0:\underset{{\tau}\in R_{h,0}}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{|{\tau}|_{H^{1}}}\geq C_{V}^{{}^{\prime}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{P,h}.

(23)

Above
|\cdot|_{H^{1}}
stands for the standard Sobolev seminorm.

Thus it is justified to consider (
23
) instead of (
21
).

Lemma 9
.

There is a constant

and a mesh size

and

(27)

where

\displaystyle{V}_{M}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{{v}_{|M}\ |\ {v}\in\big(V_{h,3}^{0}(\textsf{p},r)\big)^{3},\ \textup{supp}({v})\subset M\},

(28)

\displaystyle P_{M}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{{q}_{|M}\ |\ q\in\bar{{W}}_{h}^{3}=V_{h,3}^{0}(\textsf{p}-1,r),\ \int_{M}qdx=0\}

(29)

Proof.

Since we only consider spline basis functions this follows by Section 4.3 and Theorem 4.1 in

]
with NURBS weight function
\omega=1
. The mentioned reference shows that the macroelements with

Remark 4
.

Our definition of macroelements depends on

. However, the results in

A row-wise application of the last lemma yields the existence of a constant
C_{\textup{macro}}^{{}^{\prime}}>0
s.t. for all macroelements

and
{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
we find a

\displaystyle\langle{\tau}_{M},\nabla{q}\rangle\geq C_{\textup{macro}}^{{}^{\prime}}|{\tau}_{M}|_{H^{1}(M)}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{M}},

(30)

if

. One notes that

is independent from

and the macroelement. Next we choose for each macroelement one
{x}_{M}\in M
, e.g. the push-forward of the center in the corresponding parametric macroelement

\displaystyle\tilde{{\tau}}_{M}\coloneqq\big[\Xi^{-1}({\tau}_{M})\big]{J}_{{x}_{M}}\textbf{J}^{-1}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{M}.

\displaystyle=\langle{{\tau}}_{M},\nabla{q}\rangle+\langle\Xi\big[\overline{{\tau}}_{M}\big(\textbf{J}^{-1}-\textbf{J}_{{x}_{M}}^{-1}\big)\big],\nabla{q}\rangle

(31)

The second term in line (
3.2.6
) can be estimated as follows.
Introduce the auxiliary matrix
\tilde{\textbf{J}}\coloneqq\textbf{J}_{{x}_{M}}\big(\textbf{J}^{-1}-\textbf{J}_{{x}_{M}}^{-1}\big).

We set
{\sigma}_{M}\coloneqq\Xi\big[[\Xi^{-1}({\tau}_{M})]\tilde{\textbf{J}}\big]
and have

\displaystyle\langle{\sigma}_{M},\nabla{q}\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{{\sigma}}_{M}|\textup{det}(\textbf{J})|,\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}\textbf{J}^{-1}\rangle_{L^{2}(\widehat{M})},

where

. With

we can find bounded and continuous coefficient functions
c_{ij}^{kl}=c_{ij}^{kl}(\tilde{\textbf{J}},\textup{det}(\textbf{J}),\textbf{J}^{-1},\Xi,\Xi^{-1})
s.t.

\displaystyle\langle{\sigma}_{M},\nabla{q}\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{M}}(\widehat{{\tau}}_{M})_{kl}\ c_{ij}^{kl}\ \hat{\partial}_{j}\widehat{{q}}_{i}d\widehat{{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert c_{ij}^{kl}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{M})}\left\lVert\widehat{{\tau}}_{M}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{M})}\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{M})}

\displaystyle\leq C\tilde{C}\left\lVert c_{ij}^{kl}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{M})}h|\widehat{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}(\widehat{M})}\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\widehat{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}\left\lVert c_{ij}^{kl}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{M})}|\widehat{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}(\widehat{M})}\Big(\sum_{K\subset\widehat{M}}h\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}\sqrt{C_{\textup{elem}}}\left\lVert c_{ij}^{kl}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{M})}|{\widehat{{\tau}}}_{{M}}|_{H^{1}({\widehat{M}})}\Big(\sum_{K\subset\widehat{M}}h^{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(32)

To obtain the second inequality sign above we use the estimate for the Poincare constant

of rectangular domains

C_{Po}(D)\leq{\textup{diam}(D)}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ C_{Po}(\widehat{M})\leq\tilde{C}h,\ \textup{for a proper }\tilde{C},\ \ \ \textup{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Braess}{}{}]}}.

Analogously to
[
11
]
, one introduces the auxiliary mapping
\widehat{{q}}_{a}\coloneqq\widehat{{q}}-\frac{1}{|\widehat{M}|}\int_{\widehat{M}}\widehat{{q}}d\widehat{{x}}
. Obviously, we have
\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}=\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}_{a}
. Besides, with the norm equivalence statement in line (3.37) of
[
11
]
we obtain for some constant
C_{H^{1}}
independent of the mesh size

|\widehat{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}(\widehat{M})}\leq C_{H^{1}}|{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}({M})}.

(33)

Further, by the regularity assumption on
F
, there is for each
\varepsilon>0
a mesh size

s.t. for all

we get

\displaystyle|\tilde{\textbf{J}}|=\big|\textbf{J}_{{x}_{M}}\big(\textbf{J}^{-1}(\widehat{{x}})-\textbf{J}^{-1}_{{x}_{M}}\big)\big|

\displaystyle<\varepsilon,\ \ \ \forall M\in\mathcal{M}_{h},\forall\widehat{{x}}\in\widehat{M}.

Hence, since
{\sigma}_{M}=\Xi\big[[\Xi^{-1}({\tau}_{M})]\tilde{\textbf{J}}\big]
there is a constant
C_{\textup{para}}<\infty
which is independent of
h
and the macroelement such that

|c_{ij}^{kl}|<C_{\textup{para}}\varepsilon\ \ \textup{on}\ \widehat{M},

(34)

for
h
small enough.
Combining (
33
) and (

) we find a constant
C_{\textup{para}}^{{}^{\prime}}<\infty
with

\displaystyle|\langle{\sigma}_{M},\nabla{q}\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{para}}^{{}^{\prime}}\varepsilon|{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}({M})}\Big(\sum_{K\subset\widehat{M}}h^{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}_{a}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(35)

Applying the inequality chain (3.47) in
[
11
]
we obtain a constant
C_{\textup{est}}
s.t.

\Big(\sum_{K\subset\widehat{M}}h^{2}\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{{q}}_{a}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq C_{\textup{est}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}.

(36)

In view of (
35
) and (
36
) it is

\displaystyle\langle\Xi\tilde{{\tau}}_{M},\nabla{q}\rangle\geq C_{\textup{macro}}^{{}^{\prime}}|{\tau}_{M}|_{H^{1}(M)}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}-C_{\textup{para}}^{{}^{\prime}}\varepsilon C_{\textup{est}}|{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}({M})}\left\lVert{{q}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}.

Hence, if we choose

for some suitable

we get

.
And since

smooth by assumption, we obtain for some

and with the Poincaré inequality:

\displaystyle|\tilde{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}(M)}

\displaystyle=\bigg|\big[\Xi^{-1}({\tau}_{M})\big]\textbf{J}_{{x}_{M}}\textbf{J}^{-1}\bigg|_{H^{1}(M)}\leq C_{\textup{est},2}\left\lVert{{\tau}}_{M}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\textup{est},2}(1+C_{Po}(\Omega))|{\tau}_{M}|_{H^{1}(M)}

One notes

With the last estimate and (

\displaystyle\langle\Xi\tilde{{\tau}}_{M},\nabla{q}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq C_{\textup{macro}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}|{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}({M})}\left\lVert{{q}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}\geq\frac{C_{\textup{macro}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}{C_{\textup{est},2}(1+C_{Po}(\Omega))}|\tilde{{\tau}}_{M}|_{H^{1}(M)}\left\lVert{{q}}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}.

In other words with

3.2.7
Inf-sup condition for spaces without boundary conditions

From the last section together with the Verfürth trick we get the validity of (

) if
h\leq h_{\max}
. However, there we assumed special boundary conditions and the property of vanishing mean value for the variable

. Thus, we want to prove the next lemma.

Lemma 10
.

There is a mesh size
h_{\max}>0
and a constant
C_{IS}^{{}^{\prime}}>0
s.t.

\displaystyle\ \forall 0<h\leq h_{\max}:\underset{{q}\in{P}_{h}}{\inf}\ \underset{{\tau}\in R_{h}\cap H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})}{\sup}\ \ \frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{q}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{{L}^{2}}}\geq C_{IS}^{{}^{\prime}}.

(39)

Before proving this lemma we first look at a useful auxiliary result.

Lemma 11
.

It exists a
\tilde{h}>0
such that
\forall h\leq\tilde{h}
, we find
{M}_{h}^{i}\in R_{h}\cap H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M}),\ i=1,2,3
with

\displaystyle\left\lVert{M}_{h}^{i}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}<\infty,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\cdot(\Xi{M}_{h}^{i})-{e}_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{\frac{C_{IS}}{2\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}},\frac{1}{5\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}}\}\eqqcolon C_{L,1},\ \ \forall i,\ \ C_{\Omega}\coloneqq 1/|\Omega|.

Above the
C_{i}
are independent of
h
and
{e}_{i}
denote the canonical basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proof.

In this proof we write
\Pi_{h}=(\Pi_{h}^{1},\Pi_{h}^{1},\Pi_{h}^{1})^{T}
, i.e. we have a row-wise application of
\Pi_{h}^{1}
, where
\Pi_{h}^{1}\coloneqq\Pi_{h,3}^{1}(\textsf{p}+1,r+1)
is the spline projection from Sec.
3.2.1
onto
V_{h,3}^{1}(\textsf{p}+1,r+1)
with degree
\textsf{p}+1
and regularity
r+1
. In particular it holds

\Pi_{h}\tau\in R_{h}\cap H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M}),\ \ \forall\tau\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M}).

Now introduce the matrix
{M}_{h}^{1}\coloneqq\Pi_{h}\bigg[\Xi^{-1}\bigg(

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}

\bigg)\bigg]\in R_{h}
and
{X}_{1}\coloneqq

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Exploiting the approximation properties of the splines, cf.
[
16
, Theorem 5.3 and Remark 5.1]
, one obtains

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\Xi{M}_{h}^{1}-{e}_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\Xi{M}_{h}^{1}-\Xi\Xi^{-1}{X}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C_{\Xi}\left\lVert{M}_{h}^{1}-\Xi^{-1}{X}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Xi}\left\lVert\Pi_{h}\Xi^{-1}{X}_{1}-\Xi^{-1}{X}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Xi}C_{R}h\left\lVert\Xi^{-1}{X}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{2}}

fo some constant
C_{R}
.
And by the continuity of the projection operators it is

\displaystyle\left\lVert{M}_{h}^{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=\left\lVert\Pi_{h}\Xi^{-1}{X}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq(1+C_{R})\left\lVert\Xi^{-1}{X}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\eqqcolon C_{1}.

Since
{X}_{1}
is fixed we get directly the assertion for
i=1
. The cases
i=2,\ i=3
can be treated similarly.
∎

Proof of Lemma
10
.

Let
{q}\in{P}_{h}
arbitrary, but fixed. Further we write
\tilde{{q}}\coloneqq{q}-\bar{{q}},
where

\bar{{q}}_{i}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}{q}_{i}d{x}=\textup{const.}

Then we define

\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}\coloneqq\bar{{q}}_{i}\ {M}_{h}^{i},\ \ \ \

with the
{M}_{h}^{i}
from Lemma
11
.
By the previous section we know that there is a
\tilde{{\tau}}\in R_{h}\cap{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})
with

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\tilde{{\tau}}),\tilde{{q}}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq C_{IS}\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Set
{\tau}\coloneqq\tilde{{\tau}}+\sum_{i}\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}
. This means

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{{\tau}}),{{q}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\tilde{{\tau}}),\tilde{{q}}\rangle+\underbrace{\sum_{i}\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}),\tilde{{q}}\rangle}_{\eqqcolon(\star,1)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\tilde{{\tau}}),\bar{{q}}\rangle}_{\eqqcolon(\star,2)}+\underbrace{\sum_{i}\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}),\bar{{q}}\rangle}_{\eqqcolon(\star,3)}.

Using Lemma
11
the three last terms can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle|(\star,1)|\leq\sum_{i}|\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}),\tilde{{q}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}|\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i})-\bar{{q}}_{i}{e}_{i},\tilde{{q}}\rangle|+|\langle\bar{{q}}_{i}{e}_{i},\tilde{{q}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}|\bar{{q}}_{i}||\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{{M}}_{h}^{i})-{e}_{i},\tilde{{q}}\rangle|+0

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}C_{L,1}|\bar{{q}}_{i}|\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}C_{L,1}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}\left\lVert\bar{{q}}_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{L,1}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{L,1}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}\big(\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

\displaystyle(\star,2)=\langle\Xi\tilde{{\tau}},\nabla\bar{{q}}\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle(\star,3)=\sum_{i}\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}),\bar{{q}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}),\bar{{q}}_{j}{e}_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i})-\bar{{q}}_{i}{e}_{i},\bar{{q}}_{j}{e}_{j}\rangle+\sum_{i,j}\langle\bar{{q}}_{i}{e}_{i},\bar{{q}}_{j}{e}_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}\left\lVert\bar{{q}}_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}C_{L,1}\ \left\lVert\bar{{q}}_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\bar{{q}}_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}\left\lVert\bar{{q}}_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-3\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}C_{L,1}\ \left\lVert\bar{{q}}_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-3\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}C_{L,1})\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{\tau}),{{q}}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq C_{IS}\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1-3\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}C_{L,1})\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-C_{L,1}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}\big(\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(C_{IS}-C_{L,1}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}\big)\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(1-4C_{L,1}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}})\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C_{IS,2}(\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})^{2},

(40)

for a suitable
C_{IS,2}>0
if
C_{L,1}
is small enough, see Lemma
11
.

And on the other hand we can estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\tilde{{\tau}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}+\sum_{i}\left\lVert\overline{{M}}_{h}^{i}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sum_{i}|\bar{{q}}_{i}|\left\lVert{{M}}_{h}^{i}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sum_{i}\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}C_{i}\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{IS,3}(\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}),

(41)

where
C_{IS,3}\coloneqq\max\{1,3\sqrt{C_{\Omega}}C_{i}\}
.
Finally, we can combine (
3.2.7
) and (
3.2.7
) to see

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\nabla\cdot(\Xi{{\tau}}),{{q}}\rangle}{\left\lVert{\tau}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{IS,2}}{C_{IS,3}}(\left\lVert\tilde{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\bar{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}})\geq\frac{C_{IS,2}}{C_{IS,3}}(\left\lVert{{q}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}).

The arbitrariness of
{q}
finishes the proof.

∎

With Lemma
10
it follows the well-posedness of the discrete method also for three dimensions. Thereby, we obtain the next error estimate which corresponds to Corollary

in the abstract setting.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\textsf{p}\geq 2,\ \textsf{p}-1>r\geq 0,\ \textsf{p}\geq s\geq 0
.
And let
(\sigma,u,p)
be the exact solution of the continuous problem (
3.1
) and
(\sigma_{h},u_{h},p_{h})
the approximate solutions obtained utilizing the discrete spaces from Def.

.
Then, assuming enough regularity for
(\sigma,u,p)

independent of

:

\displaystyle\left\lVert[{\sigma}-{\sigma}_{h},{u}-{u}_{h},{p}-{p}_{h}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{conv}h^{s}\ \big(\left\lVert{\sigma}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\textup{div})}+\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+\left\lVert{p}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\big),

where
\left\lVert[\tau,{v},{q}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}=\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{\textup{div}}^{2}+\left\lVert{v}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\lVert{q}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For the spaces in the
3D
setting we further have the improved estimate:

\left\lVert\nabla\cdot{\sigma}-\nabla\cdot{\sigma}_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C_{conv}h^{\textsf{p}+1}\left\lVert\nabla\cdot{\sigma}\right\rVert_{H^{\textsf{p}+1}}.

Proof.

We look at the proof in
3D
. The estimate for
2D
follows by similar steps.

By the well-posedness, cf. Theorem
1
, we have a quasi-optimal estimate like in Corollary
2
. Thus due to projection properties in Corollary
3
one obtains easily the first estimate. More precisely, there is a constant
C_{QO}
with

\displaystyle\left\lVert[{\sigma}-{\sigma}_{h},{u}-{u}_{h},{p}-{p}_{h}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{QO}\left\lVert[{\sigma}-\Pi_{h}^{\Sigma}{\sigma},{u}-\Pi_{h}^{U}{u},{p}-\Pi_{h}^{P}{p}]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{QO}\big(\left\lVert{\sigma}-\Pi_{h}^{\Sigma}{\sigma}\right\rVert_{\textup{div}}+\left\lVert{u}-\Pi_{h}^{U}{u}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert{p}-\Pi_{h}^{P}{p}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s}\ \big(\left\lVert{\sigma}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\textup{div})}+\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+\left\lVert{p}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}\big),

where

in the last line depends on
C_{QO}
and the projections.
Th second estimate of the assertion can be obtained by a standard approach. We observe the fact
\langle\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h},w_{h}\rangle=0,\ \forall w_{h}\in U_{h}
and that
\nabla\cdot\tau_{h}\in U_{h},\forall\tau_{h}\in\Sigma_{h}
. This means we have the equality chain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h},\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla\cdot\sigma-\Pi_{h}^{U}\nabla\cdot\sigma,\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\rangle+\langle\Pi_{h}^{U}\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h},\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma-\Pi_{h}^{U}\nabla\cdot\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\ \left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+0

Thus, again in view of Corollary
3
, we get

\hskip 28.45274pt\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma-\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma-\Pi_{h}^{U}\nabla\cdot\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{\textsf{p}+1}\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma\right\rVert_{H^{\textsf{p}+1}}
. Since we use

to define

The statement for

can be proven similarly and we omit this part here.

With the last theorem and with Theorem
1
we can summarize that the discretization of the mixed formulation of linear elasticity with weakly imposed symmetry utilizing the proposed method is possible and from a theoretical point of view we should obtain arbitrarily high convergence orders, provided regular enough exact solutions. Indeed, we showed the well-posedness only for
h\leq h_{\max}
for some proper
h_{\max}>0
. But in all the numerical experiments we carried out, we do not see problems for large

. Maybe a closer study of the proofs in order to specify

would be advisable. Another point one should mention are the assumed boundary conditions. In the sections above we considered the easy case of pure displacement boundary conditions. Clearly, in applications the case of mixed boundary conditions involving traction forces is more interesting. This is why we give some remarks concerning generalizations of our approach including such mixed boundary conditions in the next section.

3.3
Brief remarks on generalizations and stability

3.3.1
Mixed boundary conditions

Up to now we studied the simple case of pure displacement boundary conditions specified by
u_{D}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However another important type of boundary conditions are the so-called traction forces. More precisely, one has a disjoint partition of the boundary
\overline{\Gamma_{t}}\cup\overline{\Gamma_{D}}=\partial\Omega,\ \Gamma_{t}\cap\Gamma_{D}=\emptyset
together with a suitable mapping
t_{n}
and the requirement

\sigma\cdot\nu=t_{n}\ \textup{on}\ \Gamma_{t}\ \ \ \textup{and}\ \ \ u=u_{D}\ \textup{on}\ \Gamma_{D}.

If the traction forces are prescribed by a given auxiliary stress mapping
\tilde{\sigma}
, i.e.
\tilde{\sigma}\cdot\nu=t_{n}
, we can reduce the mixed boundary conditions to the case
t_{n}=0
by considering
\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}
. Latter condition can be implemented in the discrete setting very easily by adapting
\Sigma_{h}
to

\Sigma_{h,\Gamma_{t}}\coloneqq\{\tau\in\Sigma_{h}\ |\ \tau\cdot\nu=0\ \textup{on}\ \Gamma_{t}\}.

Of course, the mixed boundary conditions lead also to a small change on the right-hand side of (
3.1
). For smooth enough
u_{D}
one considers the integral only over
\Gamma_{D}
instead of
\langle u_{D},\tau\cdot\nu\rangle_{\Gamma}
. Finally, we remark that in the discrete setting we assume for reasons of simplification that
\Gamma_{t}
and
\Gamma_{D}
are the union of full mesh boundary elements.

3.3.2
Multi-patch geometries

If one considers more complicated geometries, it might be reasonable to describe the overall domain
\Omega
by means of a collection of patches
\Omega_{k},\ k=1,\dots,N,\ N\in\mathbb{N}
. To be more precise, if we are in the field of multi-patch IGA, we assume

\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\overline{\Omega_{k}}\ \ \ \ ,\ \textbf{F}_{k}\colon[0,1]^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\Omega_{k}}\ \ ,

for a suitable number
N
. On the
k
-th patch we can define the discrete spaces
\Sigma_{h}^{(k)},U_{h}^{(k)},\
etc. as described in Sec.
3.2.2
. The global spaces are then straightforwardly defined as

\Sigma_{h}\coloneqq\{\sigma\in H(\Omega,\textup{div},\mathbb{M})\ |\ \sigma_{|\Omega_{k}}\in\Sigma_{h}^{(k)}\},\ U_{h}=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\ |u_{|\Omega_{k}}\in U_{h}^{(k)}\},\ \ \textup{etc.}\ \ \ \ \ .

The implementation of the coupling conditions for the stress spaces can then be achieved rather easily by exploiting the divergence preserving property of the transformation
\mathcal{Y}_{2}
. One further notes the fact that also in the multi-patch setting
\nabla\cdot\Sigma_{h}\subset U_{h}
. Numerical tests suggest the well-posedness of the mixed formulation for multi-patch parametrizations, although we can not give a proof. Besides, a combination of mixed boundary conditions and multi-patch IGA is possible.

3.3.3
Stability in the (nearly) incompressible regime

Within the scope of an isotropic and homogeneous elastic material we already mentioned the two Lamé coefficients
\lambda,\mu
determining the compliance tensor
A
. A special case are (nearly) incompressible materials that correspond to large
\lambda
values,
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
, respectively. An important motivation to consider mixed formulations is to obtain reasonable approximate solutions also for this regime. Hence, we want to show here that we can achieve stability for
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
with our discretization ansatz, at least in case of B-spline parametrizations.
To show the latter, we follow the argumentation of Arnold et al. in
[
5
]
and we use
[
10
,
28
]
. Furthermore, we restrict ourselves mainly to the three-dimensional case, since the argumentation for
2D
is in principle the same.

In Theorem
2
, we see an error estimate that depends on some constant
C_{conv}
. This constant in turn depends on various items, namely on
\Omega,\textbf{F},\textbf{J}
, etc. But, to obtain a stable convergence behavior w.r.t.
\lambda
we have to check if we can define
C_{conv}
independent of
\lambda
. Since in the Brezzi stability requirements in Def.
3
we only get a
\lambda
-dependence for (S1) it is enough to consider the coercivity estimate constant
C_{S1}
and the continuity constant
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
of the bilinear form
\langle A\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to see the stability in the incompressible limit. Due to

\displaystyle\langle A\sigma,\tau\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\ \ \forall\sigma,\forall\tau\in H(\Omega,\textup{div},\mathbb{M}),

it remains to look at
C_{S1}
from (S1). As explained in
[
5
]
we get stability for the case of homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition if
I\in\Sigma_{h}
, where
I
stands for the identity tensor. First we have a look at the three-dimensional situation. In view of the definition of
\Sigma_{h}
it is easy to see that

I\in\Sigma_{h}\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ \textup{adj}(\textbf{J})^{k}\in\big(S_{\textsf{p}+1,\textsf{p},\textsf{p}}^{r,r-1,r-1}\times S_{\textsf{p},\textsf{p}+1,\textsf{p}}^{r-1,r,r-1}\times S_{\textsf{p},\textsf{p},\textsf{p}+1}^{r-1,r-1,r}\big)^{T},\forall k,\

where
\textup{adj}(\textbf{J})^{k}
is the
k
-th column of the adjugate matrix
\textup{adj}(\textbf{J})=\textup{det}(\textbf{J})\textbf{J}^{-1}
of
J
. By the definition of the adjugate matrix as the transpose of the cofactor matrix, it is easy to see that for
\textbf{F}\in(S_{q,q,q}^{s,s,s})^{3},\textbf{F}=(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})^{T}
with
q>s\geq 1
we obtain

\textup{adj}(\textbf{J})^{k}\in\big(S_{2q,2q-1,2q-1}^{s,s-1,s-1}\times S_{2q-1,2q,2q-1}^{s-1,s,s-1}\times S_{2q-1,2q-1,2q}^{s-1,s-1,s}\big).

For example it is

\textup{adj}(\textbf{J})_{11}=J_{22}J_{33}-J_{23}J_{32}=\underbrace{\hat{\partial}_{2}F_{2}}_{\in S_{q,q-1,q}^{s,s-1,s}}\ \underbrace{\hat{\partial}_{3}F_{3}}_{\in S_{q,q,q-1}^{s,s,s-1}}-\underbrace{\hat{\partial}_{3}F_{2}}_{\in S_{q,q,q-1}^{s,s,s-1}}\ \underbrace{\hat{\partial}_{2}F_{3}}_{\in S_{q,q-1,q}^{s,s-1,s}}\in S_{2q,2q-1,2q-1}^{s,s-1,s-1}.

Similar thoughts lead to the other entries. Thus
\mathcal{Y}^{-1}_{2}(\textup{adj}(\textbf{J})^{k})\in V_{h,3}^{2}(2q,s)
for
k=1,2,3
. Consequently, if we choose the discrete spaces such that
2q\leq\textsf{p}+1
and
r\leq s
, we have indeed
I\in\Sigma_{h}
. In other words, if one wants stability for large
\lambda
it might be advisable to apply a
p
-refinement step.

In the
2D
case the condition
I\in\Sigma_{h}
is easier to see since then

\textup{adj}(\textbf{J})=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\partial}_{2}F_{2}&-\hat{\partial}_{2}F_{1}\\
-\hat{\partial}_{1}F_{2}&\hat{\partial}_{1}F_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

For
\textbf{F}\in(S_{q,q}^{s,s})^{2}
one has
I\in\Sigma_{h}
if
\textsf{p}\geq q,\ s\geq r
. In particular, the isogeometric paradigm fits to the stability condition.

The situation for non-zero
u_{D}
can be handled by first solving the original problem on the space
\Sigma_{h}^{tr}\coloneqq\Sigma_{h}\cap\{\tau\ |\ \int_{\Omega}\textup{tr}(\tau)dx=0\}
and then adding
cI
to the obtained solution
\sigma_{h}
, where the constant
c
is determined by the first line of the mixed formulation, by the equation

\langle A(\sigma_{h}+cI),I\rangle=\langle u_{D},I\cdot\nu\rangle_{\Gamma},

respectively.

It is natural to ask, whether a generalization to NURBS parametrizations with non-trivial weight functions is possible. Although numerical experiments indicate a generalization possibility, we do not have a proof available.

Another point would be the incompressibility stability for mixed boundary conditions which we address next. Below we follow the steps in
[
10
]
and
[
28
]
. Let
\sigma_{h}\in\Sigma_{h,\Gamma_{t}}
arbitrary but fixed with
\emptyset\neq\Gamma_{t}\subsetneq\partial\Omega
. Let
\Gamma_{D}\coloneqq\partial\Omega\backslash\Gamma_{t}
. Then, e.g. by the results in
[
24
]
, there exists a
w\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
s.t.

\nabla\cdot w=\textup{tr}(\sigma_{h}),\ \ \textup{and}\ \ \left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C\left\lVert\textup{tr}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

where
C
is a constant depending on
\Omega,\Gamma_{t}
and
\Gamma_{D}
.
With
\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h})
denoting the deviatoric part
†
†

†

We have the relation
\sigma=\frac{1}{n}\textup{tr}(\sigma)I+\textup{dev}(\sigma)
.
of
\sigma_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left\lVert\textup{tr}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\langle\textup{tr}(\sigma_{h}),\nabla\cdot w\rangle=\langle\sigma_{h}-\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h}),\nabla w\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h},w\rangle-\langle\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h}),\nabla w\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\left\lVert\nabla\cdot\sigma_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\textup{tr}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\big).

In view of the condition (S1) we can assume now that
\sigma_{h}\in kB_{h}\coloneqq\{{\tau}\in\Sigma_{h}\ |\ \langle\nabla\cdot{\tau}_{h},{v}_{h}\rangle+\langle\textup{Skew}\,{\tau}_{h},{q}_{h}\rangle=0,\ \forall({v}_{h},{q}_{h})\in{U}_{h}\times P_{h}\}

\lambda

With Remark 5.3 (Chapter VI.) in

\displaystyle\langle A\sigma_{h},\sigma_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\mu}\left\lVert\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4\mu}\left\lVert\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{4\mu}\left\lVert\textup{dev}(\sigma_{h})\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

3.4.4
Problems and outlook

The examples from above can be seen as a proof of concept and that the mixed weak form of linear elasticity with weakly imposed symmetry can be discretized exploiting isogeometric spaces. Although different numerical experiments show promising results, there certainly arise aspects which deserve a closer elaboration. Respectively, for reasons of completeness, we should mention issues we observed when computing approximations. The first one is quite obvious and already mentioned, namely the large number of degrees of freedom that appear. Especially for three-dimensional domains, one readily obtains large linear systems which require special solvers, like iterative schemes. But then we solve such systems only by approximation and an additional error contribution has to be taken into account.

Another issue we noted is the presence of local stress oscillations in case of mixed boundary conditions. To be more precise, at points where displacement boundary and traction boundary parts meet, meaning at points in
\overline{\Gamma_{t}}\cap\overline{\Gamma_{D}}
, we get in general beside the natural large stress magnitudes also local stress overshoots which seem problematic. As an example we display in Fig.
21
(a) the first stress component for the Cook’s membrane problem and a coarse mesh
h=1/7
. In the top left corner of the membrane we can recognize the overshoots. Although this problem appears to be crucial, we see approaches to alleviate it. First of all, regions with oscillatory stresses can be reduced by standard mesh refinements; see
21
(c). Second, we further observed more stable stresses if we combine high polynomial degrees with high regularity, see Fig.
21
(b). Furthermore, in all the tests we made, one obtains stable displacement approximations even if the stresses show overshoots. Indeed, a more rigorous explanation of the stress behavior has to be established and more numerical experiments have to be carried out in order to understand this phenomenon. This could be the starting point of another article.

4
Conclusion

In this article we studied approximation methods for the equations of linear elasticity based on the Hellinger-Reissner formulation with weakly imposed symmetry. Doing so, we made use of isogeometric analysis and isogeometric discrete differential forms. We showed the existence of a unique discrete solution in two and three dimensions and observed a stable behavior in the incompressible regime. Furthermore, a convergence estimate was derived that guarantees the possibility of arbitrary high convergence orders, provided smooth enough exact solutions. A main advantage of the approach with IGA is the exact geometry representation for various shapes. But there are also disadvantages regarding the proposed method and there were also points mentioned which should be considered in more detail. First of all, the mixed ansatz leads to a relatively large number of degrees of freedom. Further, numerical experiments indicate that the error estimation in
3D
might be too pessimistic, since we saw for the displacement a faster convergence than theoretically predicted. Besides, a study of the local stress overshoots appearing for mixed boundary conditions is advisable. The mentioned issues could be the starting point of future work.

v=(v_{1},v_{2})^{T}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then we get

\displaystyle\textup{Skew}(\textup{curl}\,v)=\textup{Skew}(\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{2}v_{1}&-\partial_{1}v_{1}\\
\partial_{2}v_{2}&-\partial_{1}v_{2}\end{bmatrix})=\partial_{1}v_{1}+\partial_{2}v_{2}.

Further, choose
w\in H(\Omega,\textup{curl},\mathbb{M})
. On the one hand, it is

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\Xi w)

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(\begin{bmatrix}-w_{22}-w_{33}&w_{21}&w_{31}\\
w_{12}&-w_{11}-w_{33}&w_{32}\\
w_{13}&w_{23}&-w_{11}-w_{22}\end{bmatrix})=\begin{bmatrix}-\partial_{1}w_{22}-\partial_{1}w_{33}+\partial_{2}w_{21}+\partial_{3}w_{31}\\
\partial_{1}w_{12}-\partial_{2}w_{11}-\partial_{2}w_{33}+\partial_{3}w_{32}\\
\partial_{1}w_{13}+\partial_{2}w_{23}-\partial_{3}w_{11}-\partial_{3}w_{22}\end{bmatrix}

On the other hand we obtain

\displaystyle\textup{Skew}(\nabla\times w)

\displaystyle=\textup{Skew}(\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{2}w_{13}-\partial_{3}w_{12}&\partial_{3}w_{11}-\partial_{1}w_{13}&\partial_{1}w_{12}-\partial_{2}w_{11}\\
\partial_{2}w_{23}-\partial_{3}w_{22}&\partial_{3}w_{21}-\partial_{1}w_{23}&\partial_{1}w_{22}-\partial_{2}w_{21}\\
\partial_{2}w_{33}-\partial_{3}w_{32}&\partial_{3}w_{31}-\partial_{1}w_{33}&\partial_{1}w_{32}-\partial_{2}w_{31}\end{bmatrix})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{3}w_{31}-\partial_{1}w_{33}-\big(\partial_{1}w_{22}-\partial_{2}w_{21}\big)\\
\partial_{1}w_{12}-\partial_{2}w_{11}-\big(\partial_{2}w_{33}-\partial_{3}w_{32}\big)\\
\partial_{2}w_{23}-\partial_{3}w_{22}-\big(\partial_{3}w_{11}-\partial_{1}w_{13}\big)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\partial_{1}w_{22}-\partial_{1}w_{33}+\partial_{2}w_{21}+\partial_{3}w_{31}\\
\partial_{1}w_{12}-\partial_{2}w_{11}-\partial_{2}w_{33}+\partial_{3}w_{32}\\
\partial_{1}w_{13}+\partial_{2}w_{23}-\partial_{3}w_{11}-\partial_{3}w_{22}\end{bmatrix}

The comparison of the the right-hand sides of the last two equality chains finishes the proof.

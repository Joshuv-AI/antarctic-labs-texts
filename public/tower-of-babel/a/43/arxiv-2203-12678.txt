Piecewise scalable frames Thanks: The first author was supported by NSF DMS 1609760
Peter G. Casazza, Laura De Carli, Tin T. Tran

Abstract
. In this paper we define “piecewise scalable frames”. This new scaling process allows us to alter many frames to Parseval frames which is impossible by the previous standard scaling. We give necessary and sufficient conditions for a frame to be piecewise scalable. We show that piecewise scalability is preserved under unitary transformations. Unlike standard scaling, we show that all frames in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} are piecewise scalable. We also show that if the frame vectors are close to each other, then they might not be piecewise scalable. Several properties of scaling constants are also presented. Keywords: finite frames, scalable frames, orthogonal projections

Introduction

Hilbert space frame theory has become one of the most broadly applied subjects in Applied Mathematics today
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
15
]
. This subject
originated in the work of Duffin and
Schaeffer
[
12
]
when they were studying non-harmonic Fourier series.

Definition 1.1
.

A family of vectors
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
in an
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a
frame
if there are constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
satisfying:

A\|x\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,x_{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|x\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

If
A=B
this is an
A-tight frame
and if
A=B=1
this is a
Parseval frame
. The largest
A
and smallest
B
satisfying
this inequality are called the
lower
(respectively,
upper
)
frame bound
. The frame is said to be
unit-norm
if all elements have norm one. The
analysis operator
of the frame is the
operator
T:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\ell_{2}(m)
given by
Tx=\{\langle x,x_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{m}
. The
synthesis operator
of the frame
is
T^{*}:\ell_{2}(m)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}
and satisfies
T^{*}(\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}x_{i}
. The
frame operator
is
S=T^{*}T
and is the
positive, self-adjoint, invertible operator
S:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}
given by:

S(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,x_{i}\rangle x_{i}.

B
is the largest eigenvalue of
S
and
A
is the smallest eigenvalue of
S
.
The quotient
B/A
is called the
condition number
.

We recover vectors by the formula:

x=S^{-1}S(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,x_{i}\rangle S^{-1}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,S^{-1/2}x_{i}\rangle S^{-1/2}x_{i}.

It follows that
\{S^{-1/2}x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame.
This requires inverting the frame operator which might be difficult. Since a frame is
A
-tight if and only if

Sx=Ax
for all
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, Parseval frames are the most desirable since
S=I
.
So we want
to alter a frame in a simple manner to make it Parseval.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a frame
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
scalable
if there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
(the scaling constants of the frame) for which

\{c_{i}x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

That is, a frame
\mathcal{X}
is scalable with constants
\{c_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
if, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,c_{i}x_{i}\rangle|^{2}=\|x\|^{2},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
denote the standard scalar product and norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We can let
X=[x_{i}]_{i=1}^{m}
be the matrix whose columns are the vectors
x_{i}
, and observe that a frame is scalable if and only if there exists a diagonal matrix
D
for which the columns of the matrix
XD
form a Parseval frame.

There is a lot of literature on the subject of scalable frames
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

.
Unfortunately the frames for which this standard scaling process is possible are very few.
Also, these papers scale the frame by using a very large number of
c_{i}=0
, which make this process unusable in practice where all the frame vectors
are needed for the application.
In this paper, we give a more general definition of scaling
and we study the properties of frames that can be transformed into Parseval frames using this definition.

Definition 1.3
.

A frame
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
piecewise
scalable
if there exist an orthogonal projection
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and constants
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}

so that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Here,
I=I_{n}
denotes the identity operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When there is no ambiguity, we also denote with
I
the
n\times n
identity matrix. We recall that an orthogonal projection is a linear operator
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
P\circ P=P
and
P^{*}=P
. If a frame is piecewise scalable with an orthogonal projection
P
, we sometimes say that it is
P
-piecewise scalable.
Thus, a frame
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable if there exist an orthogonal projection
P
and constants
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}.

Equivalently, a frame is piecewise scalable if there exist an orthogonal projection
P
and diagonal matrices
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
for which the columns of
PXD_{1}+(I-P)XD_{2}
form a Parseval frame.
Clearly, if
D_{1}=D_{2}
we are back to the definition of scalable frames. So scalable frames are piecewise scalable.
Note that the piecewise scalability of a frame
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is preserved under the replacement
x_{i}
with
-x_{i}
for any
i
.

It is not difficult to find piecewise scalable frames which are not scalable. For example, in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a frame
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
is scalable if and only if the
vectors are orthogonal. But we have a strong result for piecewise scalable.

Theorem 1.4
.

A frame
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is piecewise scalable by every non-trivial orthogonal projection
P
.

Proof.

Let
P
be a non-trivial orthogonal projection on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is not possible that
Px_{1}=Px_{2}=0
or
P
is trivial. Also, it is not possible that

Px_{i}=0=(I-P)x_{i}
or
x_{i}=0
. It follows that there is
i\not=j\in\{1,2\}
so that
Px_{i}\not=0\not=(I-P)x_{j}
. So letting
a_{i}=\frac{1}{\|Px_{i}\|},\ a_{j}=0
and

b_{i}=0,\ b_{j}=\frac{1}{\|(I-P)x_{j}\|}
, then

\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is a Parseval frame.
∎

Another reason why piecewise scalable frames are interesting is that they are both a generalization and a special case of scalable frames. Indeed, if frame
\mathcal{X}
is scalable, then it is also piecewise scalable (with constants
a_{i}=b_{i}
) with respect to any orthogonal projections. Also, if
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable with an orthogonal projection
P
,
then the sets
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are scalable frames in the Hilbert spaces
P(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(I-P)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively, (see Theorem
2.1
).

Throughout, we will denote vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x=(x(1),\ldots,x(n))
. We denote with
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0),\ldots,e_{n}=(0,\ldots,0,1)
the vectors of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we use
[m]
to denote the set
\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we give several necessary and sufficient conditions for a frame to be piecewise scalable. In Section 3, we show that piecewise scalability is preserved under unitary transformations and give some related results. In Section 4 we show that frames in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are always piecewise scalable. We also show that if the frame vectors are close to each other, then it cannot be piecewise scalable for almost all orthogonal projections. Finally, in Section 5, we present some properties of scaling constants.

2.
Some results and examples

In this section we prove necessary and sufficient conditions for piecewise scalabilty of frames in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable.

(2)

There is an orthogonal projection
P
and scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
so that:

(a)

Both
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are scalable frames with scaling constants
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and

\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
in the range of
P
and
I-P
, respectively.

(b)

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\langle x,Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,(I-P)x_{i}\rangle=0.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
By (1), there are scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and an orthogonal projection
P
so that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval
frame. Since the projection of a Parseval frame is a Parseval frame, and

P\left(a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\right)=a_{i}Px_{i},

it follows that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame. Similarly,
\{b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame and (2)(a) is proved.

We now prove (2)(b). Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
; in view of (2)(a) and the fact that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame, we can write the following chain of identities.

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle+\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|Px\|^{2}+\|(I-P)x\|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle.

Thus,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle=0
and (2)(b) is proved.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: We need to show that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
; using (2)(a) and (2)(b), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|Px\|^{2}+\|(I-P)x\|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\langle x,Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,(I-P)x_{i}\rangle=\|x\|^{2}.

So (1) is proved.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is piecewise scalable with projection
P
, then the set
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\cup\{(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a scalable frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
(2)(a), there exist constants
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
so that both

\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are Parseval frames for
P(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(I-P)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively. Thus, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle Px,a_{i}Px_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle(I-P)x,b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|Px\|^{2}+\|(I-P)x\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}.

as required
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If there exist an orthogonal projection
P
and a subset
I\subset[m]
such that
\{Px_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i\in I^{c}}
are scalable in the range of
P
and
I-P
respectively, then
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable.

Proof.

Let
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{b_{i}\}_{i\in I^{c}}
so that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i\in I^{c}}
are Parseval frames for
P(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(I-P)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively.
Let
a_{i}=0
if
i\in I^{c}
and
b_{i}=0
if
i\in I
. Then, the sets
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are scalable frames in the range of
P
and
I-P
with constants
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, and
a_{i}b_{i}=0
for all
i\in[m]
. The conclusion follows from Theorem
2.1
.
∎

The following is a special case of Corollary
2.3
which we will use later on.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If there exist a set
I\subset[m]
of size
n-1
and an orthogonal projection
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\{Px_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an orthogonal set of nonzero vectors, and
(I-P)x_{j}
is nonzero for some
j\notin I
, then
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable.

Example 2.5
.

There exist bases for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which are non-scalable but are piecewise scalable.

Indeed, let
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
be an orthogonal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.
Let
\epsilon>0
and let

x_{i}=(y_{i},\epsilon)\mbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,n-1,\mbox{ and }x_{n}=(0,\,...,\,0,1).

These vectors are linearly independent since
\det[x_{i}]_{i=1}^{n}=\det[y_{i}]_{i=1}^{n-1}\not=0
.

The set
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is not scalable because the
x_{i}
are not orthogonal,
but it is a piecewise scalable frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To see this, let
P
be the orthogonal projection on the first
n-1
coordinates. Then
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}=\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
, which is an orthonormal set, and
(I-P)x_{n}=x_{n}
. By Corollary
2.4
,
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is piecewise scalable.

With the same idea as in Example
2.5
, we can construct many other examples of piecewise scalable frames with more vectors than dimension.

Example 2.6
.

Let
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a scalable frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m+1}
be the frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

x_{i}=(y_{i},\epsilon_{i}),i\in[m]\mbox{ and }x_{m+1}=e_{n}.

Then
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m+1}
is a piecewise scalable frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We mentioned in the introduction that one problem with scaling is that most of the time, scaling is done by setting most of the scaling coefficients
equal to zero. This problem does not always occur in piecewise scaling, as the following example shows. In this example in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, we have an arbitrarily
large number of vectors which have all coefficients but four equal to zero for scaling while all coefficients are non-zero for piecewise scaling.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{4}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and let
\{y_{2i},y_{2i+1}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a family of orthonormal bases for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
For
n=1,2,\ldots,m
, we define a set of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
as in the following:

x_{4n+1}=(y_{2n},2y_{2n}),\ x_{4n+2}=(y_{2n},-2y_{2n}),

x_{4n+3}=(y_{2n+1},2y_{2n+1}),\ x_{4n+4}=(y_{2n+1},-2y_{2n+1}).

Consider the frame for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
given by:
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{4(m+1)}
.
This frame is easily scalable with
a_{i}=1
for
i=1,2,3,4
and
a_{i}=0
for
i\geq 5
.

Now we look at piecewise scaling. If we let
P
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
, then
\{Px_{n}\}_{n=5}^{4(m+1)}
is a set of orthonormal bases for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. And

\{\frac{1}{2}(I-P)x_{n}\}_{n=5}^{4(m+1)}
is also a set of orthonormal bases for
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{e_{3},e_{4}\}
. So we let

a_{i}=b_{i}=1
for
i=1,2,3,4
and
a_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and
b_{i}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}
for for
i\geq 5
. Then
\{a_{n}Px_{n}+b_{n}(I-P)x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{4(m+1)}=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{4(m+1)}
where
\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and

z_{4n+1}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(y_{2n},y_{2n}),\ z_{4n+1}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(y_{2n},-y_{2n}),

\ z_{4n+3}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(y_{2n+1},y_{2n+1}),\ z_{4n+4}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(y_{2n+1},-y_{2n+1}).

Note that for each
n\in[m]
, these four vectors form an
orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. So
\{a_{n}Px_{n}+b_{n}(I-P)x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{4(m+1)}
is a set of
(m+1)
-orthonormal bases for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and so multiplying all the vectors
by
\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}
produces a Parseval frame for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
P
be an orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
Y=P(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Z=(I-P)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
T_{1},T_{2}
be
the analysis operators of
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, respectively.
We extend these operators to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by defining them to be zero on the orthogonal complement of their domains. Also, if
V
is a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
I_{V}
is the operator which is the identity on
V
and zero on
V^{\perp}
.

The following theorem can be viewed as an “operator form” of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.8
.

With the notation and the definitions stated above, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable with the projection
P
and scaling constants
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
.

(2)

We have

T_{1}^{*}D_{1}^{2}T_{1}=I_{Y},\quad T_{2}^{*}D_{2}^{2}T_{2}=I_{Z}\mbox{ and }T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}=T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}=0,

where
D_{1},D_{2}
are diagonal operators on
\ell_{2}(m)
with diagonal elements
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
respectively.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that the
analysis operator of the frame
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is
T=D_{1}T_{1}+D_{2}T_{2}
. So the synthesis operator is

T^{*}=T_{1}^{*}D_{1}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}
and the frame operator is
S=(T_{1}^{*}D_{1}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2})(D_{1}T_{1}+D_{2}T_{2})
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
Since
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is Parseval, we have that
S=I
and
\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are Parseval frames for
Y
and
Z
, respectively. Therefore,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=(T_{1}^{*}D_{1}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2})(D_{1}T_{1}+D_{2}T_{2})

\displaystyle=T_{1}^{*}D_{1}^{2}T_{1}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}^{2}T_{2}+T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}

\displaystyle=I_{Y}+I_{Z}+T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}

\displaystyle=I+T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}.

So
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}=0
. Since
T_{1}=0
on
Z
and
T_{2}=0
on
Y
, we have that
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}
=0 if and only if
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}=T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}
=0.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: Given (2) it is immediate that
S=I
. ∎

Corollary 2.9
.

A frame
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is piecewise scalable with an orthogonal projection
P
and scaling constants
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
if and only if
\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are Parseval frames in the range of
P
and
I-P
, respectively, and
the rows of the matrix
[a_{i}Px_{i}]_{i=1}^{m}
are orthogonal to the rows of
[b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}]_{i=1}^{m}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, it is enough to show that the conditions (1) and (2) below are equivalent:

(1)

The rows of the matrix
[a_{i}Px_{i}]_{i=1}^{m}
are orthogonal to the rows of
[b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}]_{i=1}^{m}
.

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\langle x,Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,(I-P)x_{i}\rangle=0.

Let
T_{1},T_{2},D_{1},D_{2}
be operators defined as in Theorem
2.8
. Then (1) is equivalent to
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}=T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}=0
. We now show that the latter is equivalent to (2).

Indeed, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\langle x,Px_{i}\rangle\langle x,(I-P)x_{i}\rangle=\langle T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}x,x\rangle=\langle T^{*}_{2}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}x,x\rangle.

Hence, if
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}=T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}=0
then obviously, we have (1).

Conversely, suppose we have (1); then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\langle T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}x,x\rangle=\langle T^{*}_{2}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}x,x\rangle=0.

This implies

\langle(T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1})x,x\rangle=0,

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}
is symmetric, it follows that it is zero,
and so
T_{1}^{*}D_{1}D_{2}T_{2}=T_{2}^{*}D_{2}D_{1}T_{1}=0
. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Standard piecewise scalable frames

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the canonical orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any subset
J\subset[n]
, we call the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{e_{i}\}_{i\in J}
, which is denoted by
\Pi_{J}
, the canonical projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For example, for any
1\leq k\leq n
,

\Pi_{[k]}x=(x(1),\ldots,x(k),0,\ldots,0)
is the canonical projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
.

A special case of Definition
1.3
is the following.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a frame
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we say that
\mathcal{X}
is a standard piecewise scalable frame if there exists a canonical projection
\Pi_{J}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable with

\Pi_{J}
.

Let
\Pi_{J}
be a canonical projection of rank
k\geq 1
. After perhaps a change of coordinates, we can assume that
J=[k]
. For
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we let

y_{i}=\Pi_{[k]}x_{i}=(x_{i}(1),x_{i}(2),\ldots,x_{i}(k),0,\ldots,0)

and

z_{i}=(I-\Pi_{[k]})x=(0,\ldots,0,x_{i}(k+1),\ldots,x_{i}(n)).

Thus, by our definition, a frame
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is piecewise scalable with the projection
\Pi_{[k]}
if there exist scalars
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
so that
\{a_{i}y_{i}+b_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We refomulate Theorem
2.1
for this case.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a fixed
0<k<n
, let
y_{i}=\Pi_{[k]}(x_{i})
and
z_{i}=(I-\Pi_{[k]})x_{i},i\in[m]
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exist scalars
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
for which

\{a_{i}y_{i}+b_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)

There are scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(a)

\{a_{i}y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are both Parseval frames for their span.

(b)

For every
1\leq j\leq k
and every
k+1\leq\ell\leq m
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}y_{i}(j)b_{i}z_{i}(\ell)=0.

Equivalently, the
j^{th}
-row of the matrix
[a_{i}y_{i}+b_{i}z_{i}]_{i=1}^{m}
is orthogonal to the
\ell^{th}
-row for all

1\leq j\leq k
and
k+1\leq\ell\leq n
.

(3)

There are scalars
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(a)

\{a_{i}y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are both Parseval frames for their span.

(b)

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\langle x,y_{i}\rangle\langle x,z_{i}\rangle=0\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 3.3
.

Condition (2)(b) in Theorem
3.2
is necessary. The following example shows this.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
be a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, where

x_{1}=(1,0,1,1),\ x_{2}=(0,1,1,-1),\ x_{3}=e_{3},\ x_{4}=e_{1}.

Let
k=2
. Then

y_{1}=(1,0,0,0),\ y_{2}=(0,1,0,0),\ y_{3}=(0,0,0,0),\ y_{4}=(1,0,0,0),

and

z_{1}=(0,0,1,1),\ z_{2}=(0,0,1,-1),\ z_{3}=(0,0,1,0),\ z_{4}=(0,0,0,0).

So if we choose
a_{1}=a_{2}=1
,
a_{3}=a_{4}=0
, and
b_{1}=b_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
,
b_{3}=b_{4}=0
then
\{a_{i}x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}=\{y_{1},y_{2}\}
is a Parseval frame for
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and
\{b_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}=\{{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}}z_{1},{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}}z_{2}\}
is a Parseval frame for

\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{e_{3},e_{4}\}
. But we can see that the set of vectors,
\{a_{i}y_{i}+b_{i}z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
, i.e., the set of the columns of the matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0&0&a_{4}\\
0&a_{2}&0&0\\
b_{1}&b_{2}&b_{3}&0\\
b_{1}&-b_{2}&0&0\end{pmatrix}

is not a Parseval frame for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
for any choices of
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
. Note that if the
a_{i}
,
b_{i}
are chosen as above, the columns of the matrix
A
do not even form a frame.

We note that a frame can be piecewise scalable with one but not all canonical orthogonal projections. Also, a frame may not be piecewise scalable with any canonical orthogonal projections.

In the following example, we will use the fact
[
13
]
that in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a spanning set of vectors which lie in an open quadrant is not scalable.

Example 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, satisfying

x_{i}(j)>0,\mbox{ for all }i\in[m]\mbox{ and }j\in[n].

Then any canonical projection
\Pi_{J}
,
\{\Pi_{J}x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{(I-\Pi_{J})x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
both lie in a quadrant so at least one is not scalable.

If a frame
\mathcal{X}
is scalable, then every frame obtained from
\mathcal{X}
through a unitary transformation is scalable as well
[
13
]
.
The next theorem shows that also the property of being piecewise scalable is preserved by unitary transformations. We prove first the following.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a piecewise scalable frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with an orthogonal projection
P
. Let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an orthogonal projection and let
U
be a unitary transformation such that
UP=QU
. Then
U(\mathcal{X})
is piecewise scalable with projection
Q
and with the same scaling constants.

Proof.

By assumption, there exist constants
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
so that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame. We show that

\{a_{i}QUx_{i}+b_{i}(I-Q)Ux_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame as well. For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,\ a_{i}QUx_{i}+b_{i}(I-Q)Ux_{i})\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,\ a_{i}UPx_{i}+b_{i}U(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle U^{*}x,\ a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|U^{*}x\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}.

Thus,
\{a_{i}QUx_{i}+b_{i}(I-Q)Ux_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame, as required.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a piecewise scalable frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with an orthogonal projection
P
of rank
k
. Then the following hold:

(1)

For every unitary transformation
U:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the frame
U(\mathcal{X})
is piecewise scalable.

(2)

For every orthogonal projection
Q
of rank
k
, there exists a unitary transformation
U
such that
U(\mathcal{X})
is
Q
-piecewise scalable.

Proof.

(1): Let
Q=UPU^{*}
. Since
QU=UP
, Lemma
3.6
yields that
U(\mathcal{X})
is piecewise scalable with projection
Q
.

(2): Let
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
and
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be orthonormal bases of
P(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Q(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively. We complete these sets to orthonormal bases of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that we denote with
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. We define a linear map
U:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
Uu_{i}=v_{i},i\in[n]

and we extend it by linearity to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The operator
U
is unitary because it maps an orthonormal basis to an orthonormal basis.
We show that
UP=QU
and hence (2) follows by Lemma
3.6
.

It is enough to show that
(UP)u_{i}=(QU)u_{i}
for every
i\in[n]
.
Indeed, when
i\leq k
,
(UP)u_{i}=Uu_{i}=v_{i}=Qv_{i}=(QU)u_{i}
and
(UP)u_{i}=0=Qv_{i}=(QU)u_{i}
when
i>k
. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

A frame
\mathcal{X}
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is piecewise scalable if and only if there exists a unitary transformation
U:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
U(\mathcal{X})
is
\Pi_{[k]}
-piecewise scalable for some
k\leq n
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable with a projection
P
of rank
k
, by Part 2 of Theorem
3.7
we can choose a unitary transformation
U
so that
U(\mathcal{X})
is piecewise scalable with projection
\Pi_{[k]}
.

Now suppose that there exists a unitary transformation
U
such that
U(\mathcal{X})
is
\Pi_{[k]}
-piecewise scalable. By Part 1 of Theorem
3.7
,
U^{-1}U(\mathcal{X})=\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable.
∎

4.
Piecewise scalability of frames in low dimensional spaces

In this section we prove that all frames in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are piecewise scalable. In
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, we show that frames whose vectors are close to each other (in the sense of Corollary
4.4
) are not piecewise scalable with any projection of rank 2. We then generalize this result in higher dimensional spaces.

Theorem 4.1
.

Every frame for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is piecewise scalable.

Proof.

It is enough to show that any frame with three vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is piecewise scalable. Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
a:=\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle\geq 0
. Note that
a<1
. Let
u=\lambda(x_{1}+x_{2})+z,\mbox{ where }z\perp\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\|z\|=1
, and
\lambda=\pm\sqrt{\frac{a}{1-a^{2}}}
. Let
P
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{u\}
. So

Px=\frac{1}{\|u\|^{2}}\langle x,u\rangle u,\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

We first show that
\langle(I-P)x_{1},(I-P)x_{2}\rangle=0
. To see this, we compute:

\|u\|^{2}=2\lambda^{2}(1+a)+1

and so

\displaystyle\langle(I-P)x_{1},(I-P)x_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle-\langle Px_{1},Px_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=a-\frac{1}{\|u\|^{2}}\langle x_{1},u\rangle\langle x_{2},u\rangle

\displaystyle=a-\frac{1}{\|u\|^{2}}\langle x_{1},\lambda(x_{1}+x_{2})\rangle\langle x_{2},\lambda(x_{1}+x_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=a-\frac{1}{\|u\|^{2}}\lambda^{2}(1+\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle)^{2}

\displaystyle=a-\dfrac{\frac{a}{1-a^{2}}(1+a)^{2}}{2\frac{a}{1-a^{2}}(1+a)+1}

\displaystyle=a-\dfrac{a(1+a)}{1+a}=0.

Now we check that
(I-P)x_{1},(I-P)x_{2}
are nonzero and
Px_{3}\not=0
.

Indeed, if
(I-P)x_{1}=0
then

x_{1}=\frac{\langle x_{1},u\rangle}{\|u\|^{2}}u=\frac{\langle x_{1},u\rangle}{\|u\|^{2}}[\lambda(x_{1}+x_{2})+z].

This implies that
z\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
, which is imposible by the choice of
z
. Similarly, we have that
(I-P)x_{2}\not=0
.

To see
Px_{3}\not=0
, note that we have two choices of
\lambda
to get the vector
u
. One of the choices must satisfy
Px_{3}\not=0
; otherwise,
x_{3}
is orthogonal to
\pm\sqrt{\frac{a}{1-a^{2}}}(x_{1}+x_{2})+z
and so
x_{3}\perp z
, a contradiction since
\mathcal{X}
spans
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Thus,
\{(I-P)x_{1},(I-P)x_{2},Px_{3}\}
is an orthogonal set of nonzero vectors. This implies that
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable with the projection
P
.
∎

We have proved that every frame in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is piecewise scalable by choosing a linearly independent subset of the frame, say
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
, and then constructing an orthogonal projection
P
so that
Px_{1},Px_{2}
are nonzero and orthogonal, and
(I-P)x_{3}\not=0
.

The same strategy would work in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
if, for any given linearly independent set
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, one of the following holds.

(1)

There is an orthogonal projection
P
of rank 2 so that
Px_{1},Px_{2}
are nonzero and orthogonal, and
(I-P)x_{3},(I-P)x_{4}
are nonzero and orthogonal.

(2)

There is an orthogonal projection
P
of rank 3 so that
Px_{1},Px_{2}
and
Px_{3}
are nonzero and orthogonal, and
(I-P)x_{4}\not=0
.

Let us analyze the first case.
To construct the projection
P
, we need to find orthonormal vectors
u
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
such that the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{u,v\}
, i.e.,

Px=\langle x,u\rangle u+\langle x,v\rangle v,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{4},

satisfies
Px_{1}
,
Px_{2}\neq 0
and
(I-P)x_{3}
,
(I-P)x_{4}\neq 0
, and also

Px_{1}\perp Px_{2}\mbox{ and }(I-P)x_{3}\perp(I-P)x_{4}.

The last 2 conditions are equivalent to

\langle x_{1},u\rangle\langle x_{2},u\rangle+\langle x_{1},v\rangle\langle x_{2},v\rangle=0,

and

\langle x_{3},u\rangle\langle x_{4},u\rangle+\langle x_{3},v\rangle\langle x_{4},v\rangle=\langle x_{3},x_{4}\rangle.

Thus, in order to find a desirable
P
, we need to find vectors
u
,
v
by solving a system of 5 equations with 8 variables.
In principle such a system has a great chance of having solutions, but unfortunately, this is not always the case.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|x\|=\|y\|=1
and
\epsilon>0
. If
\|x-y\|<\epsilon
, then for any orthogonal projection
P:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

\max\{\langle Px,Py\rangle,\langle(I-P)x,(I-P)y\rangle\}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon.

Proof.

Compute:

\langle Px,Py\rangle=\langle Px,Px\rangle+\langle Px,P(y-x)\rangle\geq\|Px\|^{2}-\epsilon.

Similarly,

\langle(I-P)x,(I-P)y\rangle\geq\|(I-P)x\|^{2}-\epsilon.

But

1=\|Px\|^{2}+\|(I-P)x\|^{2}\mbox{ so }\max\{\|Px\|^{2},\|(I-P)x\|^{2}\}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

These three inequalities prove the lemma.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
0<\epsilon<1
and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|<\epsilon
for all
i\not=j
. Let
P
be an
orthogonal projection on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One of the following must hold:

(1)

\langle Px_{i},Px_{j}\rangle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}-4\epsilon,\mbox{ for all }i\not=j.

(2)

\langle(I-P)x_{i},(I-P)x_{j}\rangle\geq\dfrac{1}{2}-\epsilon,\mbox{ for all }i\not=j.

Proof.

We assume that (2) fails and show that (1) holds. After re-indexing, we may assume

\langle(I-P)x_{1},(I-P)x_{2}\rangle<\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon.

By Lemma
4.2
,

\langle Px_{1},Px_{2}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon.

Fix
1\leq i\not=j\leq m
and let
x=x_{i}-x_{1},\ y=x_{j}-x_{2}
. By assumption,

|\langle Px_{1},Py\rangle|\leq\epsilon,\quad|\langle Px_{2},Px\rangle|\leq\epsilon,\ \mbox{ and }|\langle Px,Py\rangle|\leq\epsilon^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\langle Px_{i},Px_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Px_{1}+Px,Px_{2}+Py\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Px_{1},Px_{2}\rangle+\langle Px_{1},Py\rangle+\langle Px,Px_{2}\rangle+\langle Px,Py\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon-2\epsilon-\epsilon^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}-4\epsilon.

The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. If, for all
i\not=j
,
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|<\epsilon<\frac{1}{8}
, then
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}

is not piecewise scalable with any orthogonal projection of rank 2.

Proof.

It is known that a frame for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is not scalable if and only if all the frame vectors lie in the same open quadrant
[
6
,
13
]
. Let
P
be any rank 2 orthogonal projection on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. By Theorem
4.3
,
either

\left\langle Px_{i},Px_{j}\right\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}-4\epsilon>0
,
or
\langle(I-P)x_{i},\ (I-P)x_{j}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon>0
for every
i,j\in[m],i\not=j
.
In either case, the projections of the frame vectors lie in an open quadrant of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and cannot be scalable.
By Theorem
2.1
, the frame is not piecewise scalable.
∎

We now generalize Corollary
4.4
in higher dimensional spaces. First, we need a lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\frac{1}{4}\leq\|x\|,\|y\|\leq 1
and
\|x-y\|<\epsilon<\frac{1}{16}
, then

\left\|\frac{x}{\|x\|}-\frac{y}{\|y\|}\right\|\leq 32\epsilon.

Proof.

We compute:

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{x}{\|x\|}-\frac{y}{\|y\|}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\dfrac{\|y\|x-\|x\|y}{\|x\|\|y\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 16(\|\|y\|x-\|y\|y+\|y\|y-y\|x\|\|)

\displaystyle\leq 16(\|y\|\|x-y\|+\|y\|\|x-y\|)

\displaystyle\leq 32||y||\,\|x-y\|<32\epsilon,

which is the claim.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|<\epsilon<\frac{1}{64}
, for all
i\not=j
. Then
\mathcal{X}
is not piecewise scalable with any projection of rank
k
, where
2\leq k\leq n-2
.

Proof.

Re-indexing and switching
P
and
(I-P)
if necessary, by Theorem
4.3
, we may assume

\langle Px_{1},Px_{i}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}-4\epsilon
for all
2\leq i\leq m
.
This implies
\|Px_{i}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}-4\epsilon>\frac{1}{4}
for all
i
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle(32\epsilon)^{2}>\left\|\dfrac{Px_{i}}{\|Px_{i}\|}-\dfrac{Px_{j}}{\|Px_{j}\|}\right\|^{2}=2-2\left\langle\dfrac{Px_{i}}{\|Px_{i}\|},\dfrac{Px_{j}}{\|Px_{j}\|}\right\rangle,

and hence,

\langle Px_{i},Px_{j}\rangle\geq\left(1-\dfrac{(32\epsilon)^{2}}{2}\right)\|Px_{i}\|\|Px_{j}\|,\mbox{ for all }i\not=j.

We now show that
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is not scalable. We proceed by way of contradiction by assuming that there exist constants
\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
such that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame for
P(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{2}\|Px_{i}\|^{2}=k\geq 2
.

Moveover, we have

\displaystyle\|Px_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=a_{1}^{2}\|Px_{1}\|^{4}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}a_{i}^{2}|\langle Px_{1},Px_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq a_{1}^{2}\|Px_{1}\|^{4}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}a_{i}^{2}\left(1-\dfrac{(32\epsilon)^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\|Px_{1}\|^{2}\|Px_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\dfrac{(32\epsilon)^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}a_{1}^{2}\|Px_{1}\|^{4}+\sum_{i=2}^{m}a_{i}^{2}\left(1-\dfrac{(32\epsilon)^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\|Px_{1}\|^{2}\|Px_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\dfrac{(32\epsilon)^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\|Px_{1}\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{2}\|Px_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\dfrac{(32\epsilon)^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}\|Px_{1}\|^{2}\,k.

It follows that
k=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{rank}P\leq\dfrac{1}{\left(1-\frac{(32\epsilon)^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}}<2
, which contradicts our assumptions. Thus,
\{Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is not scalable and by Theorem
2.1
,
\mathcal{X}
is not piecewise scalable with projection
P
.
∎

The following theorem gives a sufficient conditions for a unit-norm frame in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
to be piecewise scalable with a projection
P
of rank
2
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Suppose that there exist linearly independent vectors
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathcal{X}
such that
\langle x_{2},x_{4}\rangle=\langle x_{3},x_{4}\rangle=0
, and
\langle x_{1},x_{4}\rangle\not=0
. Then
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable.

Proof.

We construct an orthogonal projection
P
of rank
2
such that

Px_{1}\perp Px_{2}\mbox{ and }(I-P)x_{3}\perp(I-P)x_{4},

and all these vectors are nonzero. By Corollary
2.3
,
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable with projection
P
.

Let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{2},x_{4}\}
and let
w=(I-Q)x_{1}
.
Then,
w
is orthogonal to
x_{2}
and
x_{4}
, and it is nonzero because the
x_{i}
’s are independent. Furthermore,

\langle x_{1},w\rangle=\langle x_{1},(I-Q)x_{1}\rangle=\|(I-Q)x_{1}\|^{2}=\|w\|^{2}\neq 0.

Thus, with
u=\frac{w}{\|w\|}
we have that

\langle x_{2},u\rangle=\langle x_{4},u\rangle=0,\mbox{ and }\langle x_{1},u\rangle\neq 0.

Note also that
u\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}\}
.

We now let
R:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{1},x_{3},u\}
and let
z=(I-R)x_{2}
. So
z
is orthogonal to
x_{1}
,
x_{3}
and
u
. If it was
z=0
then
x_{2}\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{1},x_{3},u\}
, and so
x_{2}=ax_{1}+bx_{3}+cu
for some scalars
a,b,c
. Since
u\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}\}
, we have that
u=\alpha x_{1}+\beta x_{2}+\gamma x_{4}
for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
.
It follows that

(a+c\alpha)x_{1}+(c\beta-1)x_{2}+bx_{3}+c\gamma x_{4}=0.

Since
x_{i}
’s are linearly independent, we must have

a+c\alpha=0,\ c\beta=1,\ b=0,\ c\gamma=0,

and so
b=\gamma=0
. Thus,
u=\alpha x_{1}+\beta x_{2}
. Note that
\alpha\not=0
, since
u\perp x_{2}.
But
x_{4}
is orthogonal to
x_{2}
and
u
, and so also
\langle x_{4},x_{1}\rangle=0
, a contradiction. Thus,
z\not=0
, and

\langle x_{2},z\rangle=\langle x_{2},(I-R)x_{2}\rangle=\|(I-R)x_{2}\|^{2}=\|z\|^{2}\neq 0.

With
v=\frac{z}{\|z\|}
, we have that

\langle x_{1},v\rangle=\langle x_{3},v\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle=0,\mbox{ and }\langle x_{2},v\rangle\not=0.

Now let
P
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{u,v\}
. Then

Px=\langle x,u\rangle u+\langle x,v\rangle v\ \mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.

We can see at once that

\displaystyle\langle Px_{1},Px_{2}\rangle=\langle x_{1},u\rangle\langle x_{2},u\rangle+\langle x_{1},v\rangle\langle x_{2},v\rangle=0,

\langle(I-P)x_{3},(I-P)x_{4}\rangle=\langle x_{3},x_{4}\rangle-\langle x_{3},u\rangle\langle x_{4},u\rangle-\langle x_{3},v\rangle\langle x_{4},v\rangle=0.

To complete the proof, we need to show that
Px_{1},Px_{2},(I-P)x_{3}
, and
(I-P)x_{4}
are nonzero. Clearly,
Px_{1},Px_{2}\not=0
since
\langle x_{1},u\rangle\not=0
and
\langle x_{2},v\rangle\not=0
. Let us show that
(I-P)x_{3}
and
(I-P)x_{4}
are nonzero.

Indeed, if
x_{3}=Px_{3}=\langle x_{3},u\rangle u
, then
x_{3}\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}\}
, which is impossible. Similary, if
x_{4}=Px_{4}
, then
x_{4}=\langle x_{4},v\rangle v.
Since
\langle x_{1},v\rangle=0
, it follows that
\langle x_{1},x_{4}\rangle=0
. This contradicts our assumptions.
∎

5.
Properties of scaling constants of piecewise scalable frames

In this section, we give some basic properties of scaling constants of piecewise scalabe frames. Let us start by showing that we cannot scale vectors of unit-norm frames by large numbers to get Parseval frames.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\{c_{i}x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame for some nonzero scalars
\{c_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, then

\max_{i}c_{i}^{2}\leq 1=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{2}}{n}.

Moreover,
|c_{i}|=1
if and only if
x_{i}\perp x_{j}

for all
1\leq j\not=i\leq m
.

Proof.

For any
i\in[m]
, we have that

\|c_{i}x_{i}\|^{4}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\langle c_{i}x_{i},c_{j}x_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\|c_{i}x_{i}\|^{2}.

It follows that
c_{i}^{2}\leq 1
for all
i
. Since this is a Parseval frame, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|c_{i}x_{i}\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{2}=n.

The second equality follows.

To prove the second part of the proposition, we assume that
|c_{i}|=1
for some
i
. Then

\|x_{i}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\langle x_{i},c_{j}x_{j}\rangle|^{2}=|\langle x_{i},x_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i\not=j=1}^{m}|\langle x_{i},c_{j}x_{j}\rangle|^{2}.

So
|c_{j}||\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle|=|\langle x_{i},c_{j}x_{j}\rangle|=0
for all
1\leq j\not=i\leq 1
.

The converse is obvious.
∎

The following propositions give a bound for the scaling constants of a piecewise scalable frame.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise scalable with constants
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
, then

n\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max(a^{2}_{i},b^{2}_{i}).

Proof.

Let
P
be an orthogonal projection for which
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For any
i\in[m]
, we have

\displaystyle\|a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|a_{i}Px_{i}\|^{2}+\|b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max(a_{i}^{2},b_{i}^{2})(\|Px_{i}\|^{2}+(I-P)x_{i})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\max(a_{i}^{2},b_{i}^{2}).

From
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2}=n
, we get the claim.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
P
be an orthogonal projection. If
\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
and
\{b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are Parseval frames for
P(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(I-P)(\mathbb{R}^{n})
respectively, then

|a_{i}b_{i}|\leq\sqrt{a_{i}^{2}+b_{i}^{2}}\text{ and }\min(|a_{i}|,|b_{i}|)\leq\sqrt{2}\text{ for all }i\in[m].

Proof.

Since
\{a_{i}Px_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Parseval frame, for any
i\in[m]
, we have

\|a_{i}Px_{i}\|^{4}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\langle a_{i}Px_{i},a_{j}Px_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\|a_{i}Px_{i}\|^{2}.

It follows that

a^{2}_{i}b^{2}_{i}\|Px_{i}\|^{2}\leq b_{i}^{2}\text{ for all }i.

Similarly,

a_{i}^{2}b_{i}^{2}\|(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2}\leq a_{i}^{2}\ \text{ for al }i.

Since
\|Px_{i}\|^{2}+\|(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2}=\|x_{i}\|^{2}=1
, the first conclusion follows.

To see the second conclusion, note that

a_{i}^{2}\|Px_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1\text{ and }b_{i}^{2}\|(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2}\leq 1\text{ for all }i.

If
|a_{i}|\leq|b_{i}|
then

a_{i}^{2}=a_{i}^{2}(\|Px_{i}\|^{2}+(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2})\leq a_{i}^{2}\|Px_{i}\|^{2}+b_{i}^{2}\|(I-P)x_{i}\|^{2}\leq 2.

Similarly, if
|b_{i}|\leq|a_{i}|
, then
b_{i}^{2}\leq 2
. Thus,
\min(|a_{i}|,|b_{i}|)\leq\sqrt{2}
for
i\in[m]
.
∎

Unlike the properties of scaling constants of scalable frames as shown in Proposition
5.1
, for piecewise scalable frames, the constants can be arbitrarily large. The reason is that the vectors
Px_{i}
or
(I-P)x_{i}
might have tiny norms.

Example 5.4
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
be a unit-norm frame for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where

x_{1}=(\epsilon,\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}),\quad x_{2}=(\sqrt{2}/2,\sqrt{2}/2).

Let
P
be the orthogonal projection onto
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{span}\{e_{1}\}
. Then

Px_{1}=(\epsilon,0),\quad(I-P)x_{1}=(0,\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}),

Px_{2}=(\sqrt{2}/2,0),\quad(I-P)x_{2}=(0,\sqrt{2}/2).

We can see that
\{a_{i}Px_{i}+b_{i}(I-P)x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
is a Parseval frame with
a_{1}=1/\epsilon,b_{1}=0,a_{2}=0,b_{2}=\sqrt{2}.

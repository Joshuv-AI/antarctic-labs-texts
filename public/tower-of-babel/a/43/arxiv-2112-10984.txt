Analytic Bootstrap in 2D Boundary Conformal Field Theory:
Towards Braneworld Holography

Abstract
Recently, boundary conformal field theories (BCFTs) have attracted much attention in the context of quantum gravity. This is because a BCFT can be dual to gravity coupled to a heat bath CFT, known as the island model. On this background, it would be interesting to explore the duality between the boundary and the braneworld. However, this seems to be a challenging problem. The reason is because although there has been much study of rational BCFTs, there has been comparatively little study of irrational BCFTs, and irrational BCFTs are expected to be the boundary duals of the braneworlds. For this reason, we explore properties of boundary ingredients: the boundary primary spectrum, the boundary-boundary-boundary OPE coefficients and the bulk-boundary OPE coefficients. For this purpose, the conformal bootstrap is extremely useful. This is the first step in providing an understanding of BCFTs in the context of braneworld holography by using the conformal bootstrap. The techniques developed in this paper may be useful for further investigation of irrational BCFTs.

One goal of this paper is to formulate the bootstrap equation in irrational BCFTs
and to clarify what we can learn from the bootstrap equation beyond rational BCFTs.
The techniques developed in this paper are very useful to provide a new understanding of the braneworld holography from the CFT side.

At the end of this paper, we propose some interesting questions that we can address by using our techniques.

For a zero-point function on a cylinder, we have the following two choices of how to cut,

\parbox{70.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{1-1.pdf}}}=\parbox{70.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{1-2.pdf}}}.

(4.1)

The corresponding bootstrap equation is given by

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{P}\ \rho({\alpha}_{P})\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus1.pdf}}}=g^{2}\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p})\overline{C_{p\mathbb{I}}C_{p\mathbb{I}}}\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus2.pdf}}},

(4.2)

where the diagram represents the Virasoro character
\chi_{p}(\tau)
(which depends only on the conformal dimension
h_{p}
and independent of whether the field lives on the boundary or the bulk),
and
\rho({\alpha}_{P})
represents the density of the “boundary” primary states, which is different from the density of the “bulk” primary states
\rho({\alpha}_{p})\equiv\rho({\alpha}_{p},{\alpha}_{p})
with lowercase letters.
The right hand side can be approximated by the vacuum block if we take the
\tau\to i0
limit.

5
5

5

Here we assume that the boundary satisfies the condition
g\neq 0
.
In general, this condition would be satisfied since D-branes usually have non-zero tension.

As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{P}\ \rho({\alpha}_{P})\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus1.pdf}}}

\displaystyle\simeq g^{2}\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus3.pdf}}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=g^{2}\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{P}\ S_{0P}\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus1.pdf}}}.

In the second equation, we use the modular-S transformation (
2.12
).
Thus, the universal formula for the density of the boundary primary states is given by

\rho({\alpha}_{P})\simeq g^{2}S_{0P},\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P}\to\infty.

(4.4)

This is similar to the Cardy formula for the spectrum of bulk primaries.
Indeed, the same result was derived in
[
46
]
by the traditional approach using the inversion Laplace transformation.
Note that although we retain the non-universal factor
g
,
this factor is subleading in the large
h_{p}
asymptotics.
This is just a constant in the conformal dimension
h_{p}
.

One can take another limit
\tau\to i\infty
, where the left hand side can be approximated by the vacuum block.
In a similar way as (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle g^{2}\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p})\overline{C_{p\mathbb{I}}C_{p\mathbb{I}}}\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus2.pdf}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ S_{0p}\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus2.pdf}}}.

(4.5)

This equation implies

g^{2}\rho({\alpha}_{p})\overline{C_{p\mathbb{I}}C_{p\mathbb{I}}}=S_{0p},\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{p}\to\infty.

(4.6)

As show in (
2.13
),
the density of the bulk primary states is given by

\rho({\alpha}_{p})\simeq S_{0p}S_{0p}\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{p}\to\infty,

(4.7)

therefore, we can simplify this result as

\overline{C_{p\mathbb{I}}C_{p\mathbb{I}}}\simeq g^{-2}S_{0p}^{-1},\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{p}\to\infty.

(4.8)

We have a mathematical subtlety involving positivity.
On the left-hand side of the bootstrap equation, the coefficients can be negative.
It implies that the asymptotic formula is more sensitive to the size of the averaging window than other cases with positive coefficients.
Nevertheless, it would be expected that the result is true for a suitable size of the averaging window.

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p})\overline{C_{ijp}C_{p\mathbb{I}}}\parbox{100.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{two-a.pdf}}}=\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{P}\ \rho({\alpha}_{P})\overline{C_{iP}C_{jP}}\parbox{100.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{two-b.pdf}}}.

(4.10)

Let us take the limit
z\to 0
, where the left-hand side can be approximated by a single block with the conformal dimension
h_{p_{min}}
, which is the lowest dimension in the OPE between
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
.
By making use of the fusion transformation , we obtain the following equation,

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{P}\ \rho({\alpha}_{P})\overline{C_{iP}C_{jP}}\parbox{100.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{two-b.pdf}}}\simeq\rho({\alpha}_{p_{min}})\overline{C_{ijp_{min}}C_{p_{min}\mathbb{I}}}\parbox{100.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{two-c.pdf}}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle=\rho({\alpha}_{p_{min}})\overline{C_{ijp_{min}}C_{p_{min}\mathbb{I}}}\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{P}\ {\mathbb{F}}_{{\alpha}_{p_{min}},{\alpha}_{P}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{j}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
{\alpha}_{j}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
\end{array}\right]\parbox{100.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{two-b.pdf}}}.

From this equation, we obtain the universal formula for the bulk-boundary OPE coefficients as

\rho({\alpha}_{P})\overline{C_{iP}C_{jP}}\simeq\rho({\alpha}_{p_{min}})\overline{C_{ijp_{min}}C_{p_{min}\mathbb{I}}}{\mathbb{F}}_{{\alpha}_{p_{min}},{\alpha}_{P}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{j}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
{\alpha}_{j}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P}\to\infty.

(4.12)

In particular, in the case
i=j
, we have a simple formula,

\rho({\alpha}_{P})\overline{C_{iP}C_{iP}}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}_{0,{\alpha}_{P}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{i}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
{\alpha}_{i}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P}\to\infty.

(4.13)

By using (
4.4
), we have

\overline{C_{iP}C_{iP}}\simeq g^{-2}S_{0P}^{-1}{\mathbb{F}}_{0,{\alpha}_{P}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{i}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
{\alpha}_{i}&{\alpha}_{i}\\
\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P}\to\infty.

(4.14)

Note that the Virasoro block expansion of the bulk two-point function on a disk often appears in the evaluation of the entanglement entropy in BCFTs.
For example, one can provide constraints on the bulk-boundary OPE coefficients by considering the holographic entanglement entropy in BCFTs
[
80
]
.
It would be interesting to consider a combination of the constraints from the bootstrap equation and the holographic entanglement entropy.

This asymptotic formula is similar to that of the bulk-bulk-bulk OPE coefficients (

\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}\to\infty.

5
Light-Cone Bootstrap in BCFT

Besides the high-low temperature limit, we can analytically solve the bootstrap equation in the light-cone limit.
As shown in
[
60
,
31
,
61
]
, we can derive the universal feature for large-spin states in this limit.
Here we have a natural question: how we can derive the large-spin universality in a BCFT?
In this section, we will provide simple examples of the light-cone bootstrap in BCFTs.

5.1
Torus partition function with a hole: bulk-boundary OPE coefficients

For a torus partition function with a hole, we can consider the following two choices of how to cut,

\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{7-1.pdf}}}=\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{7-2.pdf}}}.

(5.1)

The corresponding bootstrap equation can be expressed by

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{Q}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})\rho({\alpha}_{Q})\overline{C_{pQ}C_{pQ}}\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{hole-b.pdf}}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{q}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{q}\ \ \rho({\alpha}_{p})\rho({\alpha}_{q},\bar{{\alpha}}_{q})\overline{C_{pqq}C_{p\mathbb{I}}}\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{hole-a.pdf}}}.

Here, the OPE coefficients
\overline{C_{pQ}C_{pQ}}
are functions of three parameters
({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p},{\alpha}_{Q})
,
unlike the examples in Section
4
. For example, in the zero-point cylinder partition function (
4.2
),
the OPE coefficients
\overline{C_{p\mathbb{I}}C_{p\mathbb{I}}}
have only only one parameter because of
{\alpha}_{p}=\bar{a}_{p}
.
The OPE coefficients
C_{pqq}
can also have more parameters because the bulk primaries
q
with the Liouville momentum
{\alpha}_{q}\neq\bar{a}_{q}
can exist in this case.
Let us consider the limit where the hole of the right torus shrinks whereas the left torus remains.
If we assume our theory to be a
c>1
compact CFT with no extended current,
the density of the bulk-primaries satisfies

\displaystyle\rho(0,\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{if \ }\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise }.\\
\end{array}\right.

(5.3)

Therefore, if the right torus is dominated by the vacuum primary, then the left torus is also dominated by the vacuum.
This is completely the same strategy as the light-cone bootstrap.
Let us take the limit where the hole of the right torus shrinks and the bridge between the two tori has a large distance.
By using the fusion transformation, we obtain

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{Q}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})\rho({\alpha}_{Q})\overline{C_{pQ}C_{pQ}}\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{hole-b.pdf}}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\simeq\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{hole-c.pdf}}}

\displaystyle=\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ S_{0p}S_{0\bar{p}}\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{hole-d.pdf}}}

\displaystyle=\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{Q}\ S_{0p}S_{0\bar{p}}{\mathbb{F}}_{0,{\alpha}_{Q}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{p}&{\alpha}_{p}\\
\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}&\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\\
\end{array}\right]\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{hole-b.pdf}}}.

Thus, the universal formula for the bulk-boundary OPE coefficients is given by

\rho({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})\rho({\alpha}_{Q})\overline{C_{pQ}C_{pQ}}\simeq S_{0p}S_{0\bar{p}}{\mathbb{F}}_{0,{\alpha}_{Q}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{p}&{\alpha}_{p}\\
\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}&\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\\
\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{p},h_{Q}\to\infty.

(5.5)

Here the anti-chiral conformal dimension
\bar{h}_{p}
can be free chosen, therefore, this formula tells us the bulk-boundary OPE in the large-spin limit.
By using the Cardy formula for the bulk primary operators and boundary primary operators, we obtain

\overline{C_{pQ}C_{pQ}}\simeq g^{-2}S_{0Q}^{-1}{\mathbb{F}}_{0,{\alpha}_{Q}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{p}&{\alpha}_{p}\\
\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}&\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\\
\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{p},h_{Q}\to\infty.

(5.6)

or equivalently,

\overline{C_{pI}C_{pI}}\simeq g^{-2}\left(\overline{S_{0p}}\right)^{-1}{\mathbb{F}}_{0,\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\alpha}_{I}&{\alpha}_{I}\\
{\alpha}_{p}&{\alpha}_{p}\\
\end{array}\right],\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{p}\to\infty.

(5.11)

Note that if we take the
\tau\to i\infty
limit, the first line of (

) is dominated by the vacuum block with
h_{p}=\bar{h}_{p}=0
.
At first glance, this seems strange because the vacuum torus block with two external operators should vanish unless the external operators are the vacuum.
Indeed, this is consistent with
C_{\mathbb{I}I}=0
if
I\neq\mathbb{I}
.

5.3
Re-derivation of CFT universality from BCFT

In the last of this section, we will comment on the relation between the light-cone bootstrap in CFTs and BCFTs.
In fact, we can always obtain a bootstrap equation in CFTs as a special case of a bootstrap equation in BCFTs.
Let us show the simplest case by using a torus partition function with a hole.
For this partition function, we can consider the following two choices of how to cut,

\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{9-1.pdf}}}=\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{9-2.pdf}}}.

(5.12)

The corresponding bootstrap equation can be expressed as

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{q}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})\rho({\alpha}_{q})\overline{C_{q\mathbb{I}}C_{q\mathbb{I}}}\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{mod-b.pdf}}}

(5.13)

\displaystyle=\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{q}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})\rho({\alpha}_{q})\overline{C_{q\mathbb{I}}C_{q\mathbb{I}}}\parbox{120.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{mod-a.pdf}}}.

In the large distance limit between two tori, this bootstrap equation reduces to

\displaystyle\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})\chi_{h_{p}}(\tau)\chi_{\bar{h}_{p}}(\bar{\tau})

(5.14)

\displaystyle=\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{p}\ \int{\mathrm{d}}\bar{{\alpha}}_{p}\ \rho({\alpha}_{p},\bar{{\alpha}}_{p})\chi_{h_{p}}\left(-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)\chi_{\bar{h}_{p}}\left(-\frac{1}{\bar{\tau}}\right).

This is just the modular bootstrap equation without boundary.
In this sense, the limit considered in Section
5.1
is definitely the light-cone limit,
and the result is a slight generalization of the light-cone modular bootstrap.

6
Implication for Braneworld Holography

The CFT
2
with boundaries has new ingredients, the boundary primary operators, which live only on the boundary.
We may think of the boundary primaries as primaries in a CFT
1
.
If the duality between the boundary and the braneworld is true,
the boundary primaries should satisfy some properties expected from the gravity side as explained in the introduction.
In this section, we will present some thoughts on them.

6.1
Cardy formula

We have shown that the density of the boundary primary states at high energy is given by

\rho(h_{P})\simeq\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\sqrt{\frac{c}{6}\left(h_{P}-\frac{c}{24}\right)}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P}\to\infty.

(6.1)

In other words, the entropy is given by

S(E)\simeq 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{c}{6}E},

(6.2)

where the energy for the boundary primaries is defined as
E=h-\frac{c}{24}
.
This is just a chiral part of the Cardy formula in 2D CFTs.
We should mention that this is not the spectrum of the CFT
1
(i.e, the braneworld) itself but the CFT
1
coupled to the heat bath CFT
2
, like the island model.
Note that this result matches with the entropy of a 2D gravity
[
82
]
.

So far, the Cardy formula for the open strings is universal in any CFT.
Let us consider a particular class of CFTs, which have sparse spectrum and large central charge.
These properties are expected if a given CFT is dual to classical gravity.
If we restrict ourselves to such a CFT, we may extend the validity regime of our universal formula to
E=O(c)
.
The reason is because the vacuum dominance is crucial in our derivation and naively the sparseness and the large
c
may support this vacuum dominance beyond the limit
E\to\infty
.
In fact, this extension of the validity regime has been shown for the Cardy formula in CFTs without boundaries in
[
83
]
,

S(E)\simeq 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{c}{3}E},\ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ E>\frac{c}{12},

(6.3)

where the energy
E
is defined as
E=h+\bar{h}-\frac{c}{12}
.
One can expect that something similar happens in BCFTs.
In that, if we assume that the boundary primary spectrum satisfies an analog of the sparseness condition (see (2.14) in
[
83
]
),
then the Cardy formula for the boundary primaries can hold in the regime
h_{P}=O(c)
.
In fact, this is true in some sense.
The main difference between the modular bootstrap equations for CFTs with no boundaries and BCFTs is that
the former is self-dual under the modular-S transformation but the latter is not.
Nevertheless, one can consider a symmetrized version of the cylinder partition function,

\displaystyle Z(\tau)\equiv\int{\mathrm{d}}{\alpha}_{P}\ \left(\rho^{bdy}({\alpha}_{P})+g^{2}\rho^{bulk}({\alpha}_{P})\overline{C_{P\mathbb{I}}C_{P\mathbb{I}}}\right)\parbox{60.0pt}{\hbox{\includegraphics[width]{torus1.pdf}}},

where we abuse the capital letters, which describe the boundary primaries, to label the bulk primaries in the second term.
That is,

are the “bulk”-boundary OPE coefficients.
This partition function is self-dual under the modular-S transformation, therefore, we can show the validity regime extension for the holographic BCFT in the same way as

We have to mention that

\rho^{bulk}(\Delta_{P})
with
\Delta_{P}=h_{P}+\bar{h}_{P}
.
Consequently, we obtain the following statement,

If we assume our theory to have large central charge and satisfy the following sparseness condition,

(6.5)

then the spectrum satisfies the Cardy formula in the extended regime,

S(E)\simeq 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{c}{6}E},\ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ E\geq\frac{c}{24}.

(6.6)

Here we define
E=h-\frac{c}{24}
, following the notation in
[
83

.

Note that the sparseness condition (the large gap condition) on the boundary primary spectrum is conjectured in
[

from a different viewpoint.

6.2
Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis

The eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) is one criterion of quantum chaos
[
85
,
86
,
87
]
.
The statement is as follows:

If a given system exhibits thermalization, the matrix elements of (few-body) observables
O
in the energy eigenstate basis should follow the equation,

\braket{n|O|m}=f_{O}(E)\delta_{nm}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{S(R)}{2}}g_{O}(E_{n},E_{m})R_{nm},

(6.7)

where S(E) is the entropy at the every
E=\frac{E_{n}+E_{m}}{2}
and the functions
f_{O}(E)
and
g_{O}(E_{n},E_{m})
are smooth and
\mathcal{O}(1)
functions.
The matrix
R_{nm}
is a random matrix variable following a Gaussian distribution with zero mean and unit variance.

One can easily check a system satisfying the ETH thermalizes

\overline{\braket{\phi(t)|O|\phi(t)}}=\braket{O}_{\text{micro}}

(6.8)

and

\overline{\left(\braket{\phi(t)|O|\phi(t)}-\overline{\braket{\phi(t)|O|\phi(t)}}\right)^{2}}\simeq\mathrm{e}^{-S(E)},

(6.9)

where the overline denotes time averaging.

In fact, our results are consistent with this ETH in the following way.
We regard low-body operators as light operators and take the states
\ket{n}
to be at high energy, which are responsible for thermalization.

9
9

9

The ETH in 2D CFTs without boundaries was investigated by the bootstrap in
[
46
,
47
,
48
,
66
]
.

Then, what we are interested in are the heavy-heavy-light OPE coefficients.
For convenience, we again exhibit our results for the boundary-boundary-boundary OPE coefficients in terms of the boundary primary entropy:

•

boundary-boundary-boundary OPE coefficients for heavy-heavy-heavy weights,

\sqrt{\overline{C_{PQR}^{2}}}\simeq\left(\frac{27}{16}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}h_{P}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{3}{4}S(h_{P})},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P},h_{Q},h_{R}\gg c,\left|h_{P}-h_{Q}\right|,\left|h_{Q}-h_{R}\right|,

(6.10)

•

boundary-boundary-boundary OPE coefficients for heavy-heavy-light weights,

\sqrt{\overline{C_{IPQ}^{2}}}\simeq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}S(h_{P})},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P},h_{Q}\gg c,\left|h_{P}-h_{Q}\right|,

\overline{C_{IPP}}\simeq\mathrm{e}^{-\left(1-\frac{12}{c}\sqrt{\frac{c}{6}\left(\frac{c}{24}-h_{p_{min}}\right)}\right)S(h_{P})},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ h_{P}\gg c,

boundary-boundary-boundary OPE coefficients for heavy-light-light weights,

(6.13)

One can find that the result (

Note that this is not a proof of the ETH.
If our theory is really chaotic,
the exponential suppression of the OPE coefficients should be found in the average over an exponentially small window, not over an order one window.
The dependence on the size of the window can be investigated by using the Tauberian theorem as in
[
88
,
43
]
.
It would be interesting to consider an analog of this issue in BCFTs.

In general, the dimension
h_{p_{min}}
is of order
O(1)
, therefore, in the large
c
limit, we have

\overline{C_{IPP}}\simeq O(1).

(6.14)

That is, although the ETH is not satisfied in general CFTs, it is satisfied in holographic CFTs.
The point is that even though the off-diagonal part (
6.11
) is universal, the diagonal part (
6.12
) is theory-dependent.

It is hard to directly check the randomness in the off-diagonal part.
Nevertheless, one can indirectly check it by considering a
n
-point function on a cylinder.

If the OPE coefficients reflect the randomness in the ETH, the following property should be satisfied

\overline{C_{IP_{1}P_{2}}C_{IP_{2}P_{3}}C_{IP_{3}P_{4}}\cdots C_{IP_{n-1}P_{n}}}\simeq\mathrm{e}^{\left(n-1\right)S(h_{P})},

(6.15)

where we just substitute the ETH and use the Wick theorem for the random matrices.
Indeed, the conformal bootstrap for the
n
-point function on a cylinder gives the same result.
This is one consistency check for the randomness.

Let us move on to the holographic CFT case.
Like the Cardy formula, the key point in the derivation of the OPE asymptotics is the vacuum block approximation.
Therefore, one can naturally expect that sparseness and large central charge support the extension of the vacuum block dominance
, and then the OPE asymptotics can hold beyond the regime
h_{P},h_{Q},h_{R}\gg c
.
We do not prove it here in an explicit way, but it would be interesting to give a rigorous proof of this statement.

Discussion

We propose some remaining questions and interesting future works at the end of this paper:

•

Hellerman bound

One interesting topic is to investigate the largest gap between the vacuum and the first primary state.
The value of the largest gap is important because if there exists a CFT dual to pure gravity on AdS
3
,
it should have the gap
\Delta_{gap}=\frac{c}{12}+O(1)
, which corresponds to the BTZ threshold.
This problem was investigated in
[
32
]
,
which showed that the largest gap obeys
\Delta_{gap}\leq\frac{c}{6}+O(1)
by utilizing an approximated modular bootstrap equation (improved versions can be found in
[
33
,
31
,
34
,
35
]
).
It would be interesting to consider an analog of this result in BCFTs.
We would like to mention that the weakest version of the gap can be straightforwardly obtained by applying the tricks in
[
32
,
31
,
34
,
35
]
to the symmetrized partition function (
6.4
),

\min\left(h_{gap}^{bdy},h_{gap}^{bulk}\right)\leq\frac{c}{12},

(7.1)

where
h_{gap}
is the chiral spectral gap between the vacuum and the first primary state, and
\frac{c}{12}
comes from the Hellerman bound (we can take
\frac{c}{18.2}
instead, following
[
35
]
).
It would also be interesting to consider the gap in the OPE
[
36
]
, which is the generalization of the Hellerman bound into four-point functions.

Another progress in this direction can be found in
[
63
]
,
which shows that we need primary fields with twist less than
\frac{c}{12}
to be consistent with positivity of the density of states.
The key tool to show it is the fusion matrix bootstrap in the light-cone limit.
As shown in Section
5
,
we can take a generalization of the light-cone limit in BCFTs.
Therefore, it may be possible to show a similar no-go theorem from the partition function on a manifold with boundaries.

We may be able to consider an analog of the Maloney-Witten partition function
[
90
]
in BCFTs (see
[
91
]
),
in which one can find a negative density of states.
In a similar way as
[
63
]
, we may find a resolution of this problem by looking at the fusion matrix approach as shown in this paper.

•

Boundary condition

An important remaining task is to fix the boundary entropy in some way.
In this paper, we do not focus on the details of the boundary condition.
However, it is worth investigating the boundary condition for reproducing the braneworld.
We can find some progress in this direction in
[
92
]

In particular, one can find interesting results in
[
23
]
, one of which provides the upper and lower bound on the boundary entropy by making use of semindefinite programming using the program SDPB
[
30
]
.
This is complementary to our results.

11
11

11

The information at low energy (including the boundary entropy) can be studied by the bootstrap equation at the fixed point as in
[
23
]
. On the other hand, the information at high energy can be studied by the bootstrap equation in the high-low temperature limit as in our results.

It would be interesting to consider an analytic (Virasoro) bootstrap for the boundary entropy as a next step and for this purpose,
the fusion matrix could be useful.

12

12

However, there is a possibility that while the

It implies that the analytic Virasoro bootstrap has the capacity to give more strict constraints. Indeed, the numerical Virasoro bootstrap provides more strict constraints on the maximal gap

.

Wedge Holography

Recently, a new holographic duality has been proposed in

This is a duality between AdS

The basic idea comes from the AdS/BCFT correspondence.

Since this new holography is in an early phase of investigation, many parts remain unexplained yet.
For example, it is very interesting but still unclear what difference can be found in the Virasoro spectrum for the AdS

/CFT

1

Our fusion matrix bootstrap in BCFTs could be useful in such a situation where we have little knowledge about the spectrum.
We might be able to obtain characteristic properties of the CFT dual to the wedge,
which can be compared to the AdS
3

This type of challenge would provide new support on the wedge holography.

•

Impossible brane configuration

A brane configuration where a brane overlaps itself should be prohibited.
We can translate this constraint into the CFT language by the AdS/BCFT correspondence.
With this idea, some constraints have been given (for example,
[

,
95
]
, which investigate properties of the braneworld by the AdS/BCFT).
It would be interesting to reproduce these types of constrains from the conformal bootstrap,
because the conformal bootstrap is particularly useful to exclude impossible spectra.
We would like to comment that the value
\frac{c}{32}
discovered in
[
95
]
sometimes appear in the light-cone conformal bootstrap
[

and the light-cone modular bootstrap
[

.
Now that we have formulated the light-cone bootstrap in BCFTs,
it would be interesting to investigate the relation between them.

•

Some assumptions from braneworld

In order for gravity to be localized on the brane,
we need a large brane tension. Moreover, the more we decrease the brane tension, the larger the mass the graviton gets.
Therefore, we have to consider the brane with the large tension if we would like to use the braneworld holography
[
17
]
.
We also have some constraints from the existence of the bulk causality
[
97
,
84
]
.
This type of assumption appears as some constraints on the BCFT data on the BCFT side (for example, see
[
98
,
84
]
).
It would be interesting to make use of these assumptions to solve the bootstrap equation.
In particular, it would be interesting to study whether such a theory can exist in a consistent way with the bootstrap equation.

We can also consider a similar approach in the AdS/BCFT setup.

13
13

13

Note that although the AdS/BCFT is similar to the braneworld holography, they are different from each other in a precise sense.
For example, while in the braneworld holography, all degrees of freedom of the conformal boundary on the BCFT side are localized on the brane on the bulk side,
this is not true in the AdS/BCFT.

It is naturally expected that not all boundary states can be dual to classical gravity with an EOW, only some do.
Thus, we have a question, which boundary condition can have a good classical gravity description.
To answer this question, it is natural to use the bootstrap equation with few known constraints from the consistency between BCFT calculations and gravity calculations (e.g.,
[
99
]
). It would be interesting to further explore this direction.

•

Generalization to BCFTs with other symmetries

It would be interesting to consider a generalization of our results into a BCFT with a rich structure.
For example, recently, the asymptotic behavior of the density of states transforming under a given irreducible representation of a global symmetry has been studied in

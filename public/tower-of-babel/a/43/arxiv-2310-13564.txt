h ​ p hp -optimal convergence of the original DG method for linear hyperbolic problems on special simplicial meshes
Z. Dong, L. Mascotto

Abstract
We prove h ​ p hp -optimal error estimates for the original discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method when approximating solutions to first-order hyperbolic problems with constant convection fields in the L 2 L^{2} and DG norms. The main theoretical tools used in the analysis are novel h ​ p hp -optimal approximation properties of the special projector introduced in [Cockburn, Dong, Guzmán, SINUM, 2008]. We assess the theoretical findings on some test cases. AMS subject classification : 65N12; 65N30; 65N50. Keywords : discontinuous Galerkin; optimal convergence; linear hyperbolic problems; a priori error estimation; p p -version.

Introduction

The discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method was
first introduced in
[
21
]

for the approximation of solutions to the neutron transport equation,
i.e., a first-order linear hyperbolic problem.
This method,
which we shall refer to as the original DG method,
was later analysed in
[
18
]
;
advances on the convergence of the scheme were given in
[
15
]
.
The convergence rates in the
L^{2}
norm of the
h
-version of the method
proven therein are suboptimal by half an order;
this was also apparent from the numerical experiments in
[
20
]
.
On special classes of meshes,
optimal convergence for the
h
-version was established in
[
8
]
.

Fewer results are available for the
p
- and
hp
-versions of the method.
In
[
13
]
,

hp
error estimates were derived
for a streamline diffusion version of the discontinuous Galerkin method
on fairly general quadrilateral meshes.
Shortly after, in
[
14
]
,
for the original method
and linear polynomial convection fields,

p
convergence was shown suboptimal by half a order for the convection-diffusion case,
but optimal for the linear hyperbolic case;
for more general vector fields, suboptimality by one order and a half was discussed as well.
The main theoretical tools in this reference
are
hp
optimal approximation properties of the
L^{2}
projector
on the boundary of tensor product elements.
More recently
[
11
]
and again for tensor product elements,
such a suboptimality was reduced to half an order only for a special class of convective fields.

This paper aims at extending even more the knowledge on the convergence analysis
of the
hp
-discontinuous Galerkin (
hp
-DG)
for first order hyperbolic problems in the following aspects:
for constant convection fields,

•

we prove
hp
-optimal convergence of the method in the
L^{2}
norm,
thus generalizing the results in
[
8
]

to the
p
-version of the method;

•

we prove
hp
-optimal convergence of the method in the DG norm
also on special simplicial meshes as in
[
8
]
,
thus generalizing the results in
[
13
]

to the case of simplicial meshes.

To this aim, we analyse the
hp
approximation properties of the Cockburn-Dong-Guzmán
(henceforth denoted by CDG) projector
introduced in
[
9
]
.
In particular, we generalise the one dimensional results in
[
22
, Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
]
to simplices in two and three dimensions.

A possible reason why the simplicial mesh case was not contemplated in
[
13
,
14
]

is that
hp
-optimal convergence estimates
for the trace of the polynomial
L^{2}
-projection operator on simplices,
crucial in the analysis of the CDG projector properties
were derived later
[
7
,
19
]
.

Notation.

Let
D
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1
,
2
, and
3

\partial D
.
The space of Lebesgue measurable and square integrable functions over

.
The Sobolev space of positive integer order

.
We also write

We endow

(\cdot,\cdot)_{s,D},\qquad\qquad\qquad{\left|{\cdot}\right|}_{s,D},\qquad\qquad\qquad{\left\|{\cdot}\right\|}_{s,D}.

Interpolation theory is used to construct Sobolev spaces of positive noninteger order;
duality is used to define negative order Sobolev spaces.

The trace theorem is valid for
H^{s}(D)
,
1/2<s<3/2
.
In particular, given
s
in the above range and
g
in
H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{g})
,
being
\Gamma_{g}
any subset of
\partial D
with nonzero measure in

we are allowed to define the space

H^{s}_{g}(D,\Gamma_{g}):=\{v\in H^{s}(D,\Gamma_{D})\mid v_{|\Gamma_{D}}=g\}.

The space of polynomials of nonnegative degree

\mathbb{P}_{p}(D)
.

The continuous problem.

Given
\Omega
a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
, with boundary
\Gamma
,
consider
\boldsymbol{\beta}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
c
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
We introduce the inflow part
\Gamma_{-}
of the boundary of
\Omega
as follows:
given
\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})
the outward normal to
\Gamma
at
\mathbf{x}
,

\Gamma_{-}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega\mid\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})<0\}.

The characteristic part
\Gamma_{0}
is analogously defined as

\Gamma_{0}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega\mid\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x})=0\}.

The outflow part
\Gamma_{+}
of
\Gamma
is given by
\Gamma\setminus(\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{0})
.
We are interested in the approximation of solutions to convection-reaction problems:
given
g
in
L^{2}(\Gamma_{-})
,

\begin{cases}\text{find }u\text{ such that }\\
\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla u+c\ u=f&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=g&\text{on }\Gamma_{-}.\end{cases}

(1)

Introduce the graph space

V:=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\},

which we endow with the graph norm

{\left\|{v}\right\|}_{V}^{2}:={\left\|{v}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}+{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla v}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}.

(2)

We can define a trace operator from the graph space onto the space

L^{2}(|\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}|,\Gamma):=\{v\text{ measurable on }\Gamma\mid(|\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}|v,v)_{0,\Gamma}<\infty\}.

Let

V_{g}:=\{v\in V\mid\text{$g$ is the trace of $v$ and belongs to }L^{2}(|\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}|,\Gamma)\}.

(3)

We introduce the bilinear form on
V_{g}\times L^{2}(\Omega)
as

b(u,v)=(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla u,v)_{0,\Omega}+(c\ u,v)_{0,\Omega}.

The weak formulation of problem (
1
) reads

\begin{cases}\text{find }u\in V_{g}\text{ such that}\\
b(u,v)=(f,v)_{0,\Omega}\qquad\forall v\in L^{2}(\Omega).\end{cases}

(4)

Henceforth, we assume that there exists a positive constant
\overline{c}_{0}
such that

\overline{c}:=c-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\geq\overline{c}_{0}.

(5)

Problem (
4
) is well posed with respect to the graph norm in (
2
);
see, e.g.,
[
3
]
.

Admissible simplicial meshes

We follow
[

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a simplicial mesh of the domain
\Omega

\mathcal{F}_{h}
be its set of (

)-dimensional facets.
We distinguish the facets in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
into internal and boundary facets;
the former are those
F
not contained in
\partial\Omega
.
The union of the interior facets of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is
\Gamma_{h}^{\interior}
.

With each element
T
of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
we associate its diameter
h_{T}

and its outward unit vector
\mathbf{n}_{T}
,
which is defined almost everywhere on
\partial T
.
The set of (
d-1
)-dimensional facets of
T
is
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}}
.
With each facet
F
in
\mathcal{F}_{h}
we associate its diameter
h_{F}

and a unit normal vector
\mathbf{n}_{F}
,
which is pointing outward
\Omega
if
F
is a boundary facet.
We only consider meshes
\mathcal{T}_{h}
that are
\sigma
shape-regular
i.e., any element
T
of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is star-shaped
with respect to a ball of radius
larger than or equal to
h_{T}\sigma
.
We associate with each mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}

piecewise differential operators by adding a subscript
h
,
for instance
\nabla_{h}
denotes the piecewise gradient.

We distinguish the facets in
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}}
into outflow, inflow, and characteristics (with respect to
\boldsymbol{\beta}
) facets,
depending on whether they satisfy either of the two following properties:

\mathbf{n}_{T}{}_{|F}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}>0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathbf{n}_{T}{}_{|F}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}<0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathbf{n}_{T}{}_{|F}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}=0.

The union of the outflow and inflow facets is
\Gamma^{T}_{\out}
and
\Gamma^{T}_{\interior}
, respectively.

We require that each element
T
of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
satisfy the two following properties:

(
A1
)

T
has only one outflow facet,
which we denote by
F_{T}^{+}
;

(
A2
)

each interior facet
F
that is an inflow facet for
T

is included in the outflow facet for another simplex in the mesh.

The second assumption above implies that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
can be nonconforming,
i.e., hanging facets are allowed.
Meshes satisfying the above properties can be always constructed;
see, e.g.,
[
8
, Appendix]
.

The focus of the paper is on
p
-optimal estimates;
therefore, we pick quasi-uniform meshes
and denote the mesh size of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
by
h
.

Given
p
in
\mathbb{N}
and
s
in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
we associate with a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
as in Section
2.1

the spaces

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{q_{p}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid q_{p}{}_{|T}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(T)\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}

and

H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid v_{|T}\in H^{s}(T)\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

Differential operators defined piecewise over
\mathcal{T}_{h}
are denoted with the same symbol of the original operator with an extra subcript
h
;
for instance, the broken gradient is
\nabla_{h}
.

Given an interior facet
F
, we denote by
T^{+}
and
T^{-}

the two elements of
\mathcal{T}_{h}

such that
F
is an outflow and inflow facet for
T^{+}
and
T^{-}
, respectively.
Given

in
\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and

in the face

,
we write

v_{h}^{\pm}(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}v_{h}(\mathbf{x}\pm\delta\boldsymbol{\beta}).

We introduce the jump operator
\left[\!\left[\cdot\right]\!\right]:H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\to L^{2}(\Gamma_{h}^{\interior})
given by
\left[\!\left[v_{h}\right]\!\right]{}_{|F}:=v_{h}^{+}-v_{h}^{-}

for all internal edges

.

Given a boundary facet

, we denote by
T
the only
element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
F
belongs to
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}}
.
Since every function
v_{h}
in
\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

is single valued on boundary facets,
for all
\mathbf{x}
in the face
F
,
we write
v_{h}(\mathbf{x})=v_{h}{}_{T}(\mathbf{x})
,
where

is the only element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}

such that

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\mathcal{F}_{T}}}

Henceforth, given two positive quantities

2.2
The original DG method

Consider the space
V_{h}
given by
\mathbb{P}_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
The original
[
21
]
DG method for (
4
) reads

\begin{cases}\text{find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that}\\
\mathcal{B}(u_{h},v_{h})=(f,v_{h})_{0,\Omega}-(g,\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\ v_{h})_{0,\Gamma_{-}}\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.\end{cases}

(6)

where

\mathcal{B}(u_{h},v_{h}):=(\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla_{h}u_{h}+c\ u_{h},v_{h})_{0,\Omega}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\left[\!\left[u_{h}\right]\!\right],\mathbf{n}_{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\ v_{h}^{+})_{0,\Gamma^{T}_{\interior}}-(u_{h},\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\ v_{h})_{0,\Gamma_{-}}.

The solution
u_{h}
can be computed starting at the inflow boundary
\Gamma_{-}
;
solving local problems derived from (
6
)
on the elements abutting
\Gamma_{-}
;
transmitting the solution to the neighbouring elements
in the
\boldsymbol{\beta}
direction through upwind.

An equivalent alternative formulation used, e.g., in
[
8
]
is derived
using an integration by parts and the fact that
\boldsymbol{\beta}
is a constant field.
Notably, an integration by parts implies that
the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}(u_{h},v_{h})&=-(u_{h},\boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot\nabla_{h}v_{h})_{0,\Omega}\!+\!\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(u_{h}^{-},\mathbf{n}_{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\ \left[\!\left[v_{h}\right]\!\right])_{0,\Gamma^{T}_{\interior}\setminus\Gamma_{-}}\!+\!(c\ u_{h},v_{h})_{0,\Omega}+(u_{h},\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}\ v_{h})_{0,\Gamma_{+}}.\end{split}

(7)

Method (
6
) is well posed
and coincides with that introduced in
[
21
]

and analysed in
[
18
]
.
A priori estimates with respect to the data are derived in
[
13
, Lemma 2.4 and Section 5]
.
Previous results requiring stronger assumptions
on
c
are given, e.g., in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\overline{c}
be given in (
5
).
The following bound holds true:

\overline{c}{\left\|{u_{h}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left\|{|\mathbf{n}_{T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\!\left[u_{h}\right]\!\right]}\right\|}_{0,\Gamma^{T}_{\interior}}^{2}+{\left\|{|\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}|^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}}\right\|}_{0,\Gamma}^{2}\leq\overline{c}^{-1}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}+2{\left\|{g}\right\|}_{0,\Gamma_{-}}^{2}.

All constants appearing in the stability estimates of Proposition
2.1
are explicit,
and independent of
h
and
p
.
This is essential to derive the optimal error estimates
in Section
4
below.
Former stability results display stability estimates
that are suboptimal in the polynomial degree;
see, e.g.,
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Method (
6
) is consistent.
The following Galerkin orthogonality property follows:
given
u
and
u_{h}
the solutions to (
4
) and (
6
),

\mathcal{B}(u-u_{h},v_{h})=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(8)

In particular, the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot)

is also well defined on
V_{g}\times V_{h}
,
where
V_{g}
is defined in (
3
).

Remark 1
.

The case of nonconstant
\boldsymbol{\beta}

is considered in
[
10
]
.
Further assumptions on the advection field are needed.
The presentation of this manuscript might be generalised
to a similar setting at the price of extra technicalities.

3.1
Preliminary results

We recall
hp

L^{2}
projector on a simplex
T
in the
L^{2}
norm on the boundary of
T
.
This result traces back to
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]

and
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
\Pi^{0}_{p}:L^{2}(T)\to\mathbb{P}_{p}(T)
denote the

projector defined as

(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v,q_{p})_{0,T}=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall q_{p}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(T).

(9)

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the notation of Section

,
there exists a positive
C
independent of
\sigma

{\left\|{u-\Pi^{0}_{p}u}\right\|}_{0,F_{T}^{+}}\leq C\left(\frac{h_{T}}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left|{u}\right|}_{1,T}\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(T).

As a consequence, for a possibly larger constant

and

{\left\|{u-\Pi^{0}_{p}u}\right\|}_{0,F_{T}^{+}}\leq C\left(\frac{h_{T}}{p}\right)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{k+1,T}\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall u\in H^{k+1}(T).

In the one dimensional case, the norm on the right-hand side of
the last inequality is in fact a seminorm.

As we are interested in deriving estimates explicit in the polynomial degree
and the estimates explicit in the element size are already known from
[
9
]

we shall prove approximation properties on reference elements.

Set
\widehat{I}:=[-1,1]

We recall some properties of orthogonal polynomials;

[

]

Let

(10)

Given
\Gamma(\cdot)
the Gamma function,
further let

orthogonal set of Jacobi polynomials over

satisfying

[

((1-x)^{\ell}J_{i}^{\ell},J_{j}^{\ell})_{0,\widehat{I}}=\frac{2^{\ell+1}}{2j+\ell+1}\frac{\Gamma(j+\ell+1)\Gamma(\ell+1)}{\Gamma(\ell+1)\Gamma(j+\ell+1)}\delta_{j,\ell}=\frac{2^{\ell+1}}{2j+\ell+1}\delta_{j,\ell}\qquad\qquad\forall i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

3.2
The CDG projector and the main result

The CDG operator was introduced
for the analysis of superconvergent DG methods for second-order elliptic problems
in
[
9
]
.
It can be defined for simplices in any dimensions
satisfying assumptions (
A1
)–(
A2
).
In particular, recall that
F_{T}^{+}
is the only outflow facet of
T
.

The CDG operator

\mathcal{P}_{p}:H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(T)\to\mathbb{P}_{p}(T)

is defined as

\displaystyle(v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v,q_{p-1}^{T})_{0,T}=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall q_{p-1}^{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{p-1}(T),

(12a)

\displaystyle(v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v,q_{p}^{F_{T}^{+}})_{0,F_{T}^{+}}=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall q_{p}^{F_{T}^{+}}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(F_{T}^{+}).

(12b)

In
[
9
]
, the following error estimate was proven
for sufficiently smooth functions:

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,T}\leq C\ h^{p+1}{\left|{v}\right|}_{p+1,\Omega}.

The constant
C
above depends on
\sigma
and the polynomial degree
p
.
The remainder of the section is devoted to carefully detail the dependence on
p
.
Namely, we shall prove the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let the assumptions (
A1
) and (
A2
) be valid,

T
be in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
and
\Pi^{0}_{p}
be the operator in (
9
).
Then, there exist a positive constant
C

independent of
h
and
p
, such that,
for any
v
in
H^{k+1}(T)
,
k
positive,
we have

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,T}{\leq C\frac{h^{\min(p,k)+1}}{p^{k+1}}{\left\|{v}\right\|}_{k+1,T}.}

3.3
The 1D case

We prove Theorem
3.2
in the case of one dimensional simplices,
i.e., on intervals.
This was already done in
[
22
, Lemmas 3.5 and 3.6]
.
However, we deem that reviewing the main steps from
[
22
]

is beneficial for the understanding of the extension to the two and three dimensional cases
in Sections
3.4
and
3.5
below, respectively.

Without loss of generality, we pick
T=\widehat{I}
and assume
-1
to be the outflow facet.
In 1D, the operator
\mathcal{P}_{p}
is defined imposing orthogonality up to order
p-1
over
\widehat{I}

and imposing that
\mathcal{P}_{p}v(-1)=v(-1)
;
this last condition replaces the orthogonality on the outflow facet.

The proof of optimal
p
-convergence can be split in the following steps:

1.

we write
\mathcal{P}_{p}v
as a combination of Legendre polynomials
and compute the corresponding coefficients;
up to order
p-1
, the coefficients are the same as those of the expansion of
v
;
the coefficient of order
p
is given by a tail of the other Legendre coefficients;

2.

we relate such a tail to the coefficients of
v
minus its
L^{2}
projection
on the triangle restricted to the outflow facet;

3.

we deduce the assertion using trace error estimates as in Lemma
3.1
.

Step 1: writing
\mathcal{P}_{p}v
with respect to the Legendre basis and with explicit coefficients.

Given
v
in
L^{2}(\widehat{I})
, we consider its expansion with respect to Legendre polynomials

v(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j}\ L_{j}(x).

Consider also the truncated expansion up to order
p
given by

\mathcal{P}_{p}v(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{j}\ L_{j}(x).

The orthogonality condition (
12a
) with respect to polynomials of degree
p-1
gives

\mathcal{P}_{p}v(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\mathbf{v}_{j}\ L_{j}(x)+\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{p}\ L_{p}(x).

The last coefficient is given imposing the condition
v(-1)=\mathcal{P}_{p}v(-1)
.
More precisely, since
L_{j}(-1)=(-1)^{j}
for all
j
in
\mathbb{N}
,
we write

v(-1)=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j}\mathbf{v}_{j},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{P}_{p}v(-1)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{j}\mathbf{v}_{j}+(-1)^{p}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{p}.

We deduce

(-1)^{p}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{p}=\sum_{j=p}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j}(-1)^{j},

whence we get

\mathcal{P}_{p}v(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\mathbf{v}_{j}\ L_{j}(x){+}\big(\sum_{j=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j-p}\mathbf{v}_{j}\big)\ L_{p}(x).

(13)

Given
\Pi^{0}_{p}
the
L^{2}
projection as in (
9
),
we arrive at

\begin{split}(v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v)(x)&=\sum_{j=p}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j}\ L_{j}(x)-\big(\sum_{j=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j-p}\mathbf{v}_{j}\big)\ L_{p}(x)\\
&=\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j}\ L_{j}(x)+\mathbf{v}_{p}L_{p}(x)-\mathbf{v}_{p}L_{p}(x)-\big(\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j-p}\mathbf{v}_{j}\big)\ L_{p}(x)\\
&=\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j}\ L_{j}(x)-\big(\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j-p}\mathbf{v}_{j}\big)\ L_{p}(x)\\
&=(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(x)-(-1)^{-p}\big(\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j}\mathbf{v}_{j}\big)\ L_{p}(x).\end{split}

(14)

Step 2: relate the tail of the Legendre coefficients to the trace of
v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v

on the outflow facet.

Observe that

(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(x)=\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j}L_{j}(x),\qquad(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(-1)=\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j}L_{j}(-1)=\sum_{j=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{j}\mathbf{v}_{j}.

(15)

Combining (
14
) and (
15
) gives

(v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v)(x)=(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(x)-(-1)^{p}[(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(-1)]L_{p}(x)

Recall from (
10
) that

{\left\|{L_{p}}\right\|}^{2}_{0,\widehat{I}}=\frac{2}{2p+1}.

(16)

We deduce

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}\leq{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}+|(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(-1)|^{2}\frac{2}{2p+1}.

Step 3: deduce the desired bound using Lemma
3.1
.

The 1D version of Lemma
3.1
reads

|(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(-1)|^{2}\lesssim(p+1)^{-1}{\left|{v}\right|}_{1,\widehat{I}}^{2}.

Combining the two equations above yields

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}\lesssim{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}+(p+1)^{-1}{\left|{v}\right|}_{1,\widehat{I}}.

The assertion follows
noting that
\mathcal{P}_{p}
and
\Pi^{0}_{p}
preserve polynomials of maximum degree
p

and standard polynomial approximation results.

3.4
The 2D case

We prove Theorem
3.2
in two dimensions.
Compared to the 1D case in Section
3.3
,
the role of orthogonal basis functions is now played by tensor product Jacobi polynomials (with different weights)
collapsed on the triangle via the Duffy transformation;
in other words, we use Koornwinder polynomials
following the construction in
[
4
,
7
]
;
see also
[
12
,
17
]
.

First, we introduce a reference 2D simplex
T
:

\widehat{T}:=\left\{\left(z\frac{1-y}{2},y\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ \middle|\ (z,y)\in[-1,1]^{2}\right\}.

(17)

The reference triangle
\widehat{T}
corresponds to the reference square
\widehat{Q}:=[-1,1]^{2}

collapsed under the Duffy transformation

(z,y)\in\widehat{Q}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\left(z\frac{1-y}{2},y\right).

This particular choice of
\widehat{T}
is convenient for our purposes,
as we shall analyse the approximation properties of
\mathcal{P}_{p}

assuming that
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
is the segment
[-1,1]\times\{-1\}
.

We introduce the orthogonal polynomial basis (known as Koornwinder polynomial basis
[
17
]
) over
\widehat{T}
given by

\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y):=L_{j}\left(\frac{2x}{1-y}\right)J_{\ell}^{2j+1}(y)\left(\frac{1-y}{2}\right)^{j}\qquad\qquad\forall j,\ \ell\in\mathbb{N}.

(18)

This is an orthogonal basis over
\widehat{T}
.
In fact, using the Duffy transformation, we have

\int_{\widehat{T}}\Phi_{i,j}(x,y)\Phi_{k,\ell}(x,y)dx\ dy=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}L_{i}(z)L_{k}(z)\ dz\right)\left(\int_{-1}^{1}J_{j}^{2i+1}(y)J_{\ell}^{2k+1}(y)\left(\frac{1-y}{2}\right)^{i+k+1}dy\right).

If
i\neq k
, the above inner product is zero.
Thus, without loss of generality, we can assume
i=k
.
Using (
11
),
we write

\begin{split}&\int_{\widehat{T}}\Phi_{i,j}(x,y)\Phi_{i,\ell}(x,y)dx\ dy=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}L_{i}(z)L_{i}(z)\ dz\right)\left(\int_{-1}^{1}J_{j}^{2i+1}(y)J_{\ell}^{2i+1}(y)\left(\frac{1-y}{2}\right)^{2i+1}dy\right)\\
&={\frac{2}{2i+1}}2^{-2j-1}\frac{2^{2j+2}}{2\ell+2j+2}\delta_{j,\ell}={\frac{2}{2i+1}}2^{-2j-1}\frac{2^{2j+1}}{j+\ell+1}\delta_{j,\ell}={\frac{2}{2i+1}}\frac{1}{j+\ell+1}\delta_{j,\ell}.\end{split}

(19)

With this at hand, we extend the results in Section
3.3
to 2D simplices.
Henceforth, let
v
denote a sufficiently regular function over
\widehat{T}
.

Step 1: writing
\mathcal{P}_{p}v
with respect to the Koornwinder basis and with explicit coefficients.

We expand
v
with respect to the Koornwinder basis in (
18
):

v(x,y)=\sum_{j+\ell=0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y).

(20)

In what follows, the indices appearing
under the summation symbol are always taken nonnegative;
moreover, we set
\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}
equal to
0

if
j
or
\ell
are negative.

Since
\mathcal{P}_{p}v
belongs to
\mathbb{P}_{p}(\widehat{T})
, we expand it with respect to the Koornwinder basis:

\mathcal{P}_{p}v(x,y)=\sum_{j+\ell=0}^{p}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{j,\ell}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y).

By the orthogonality property (
12a
), we readily get

\mathcal{P}_{p}v(x,y)=\sum_{j+\ell=0}^{p-1}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)+\sum_{j+\ell=p}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{j,\ell}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y).

The coefficients corresponding to the Koornwinder’s polynomials of order
p

are found using the facet orthogonality condition (
12b
)
on the facet
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
.
Notably, we impose the identity

\int_{F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}\Big[{\sum_{j+\ell=p+1}^{\infty}}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,-1)+\sum_{j+\ell=p}(\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{j,\ell})\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,-1)\Big]L_{k}(x)\ dx=0\qquad\forall k=0,\dots,p.

Using (
18
)
and the fact that
J_{\ell}^{2i+1}(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
,
this identity can be rewritten as

\begin{split}&\sum_{j+\ell=p}\int_{F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}(\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{j,\ell})(-1)^{p-j}L_{j}(x)L_{k}(x)dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}\sum_{j+\ell=\widetilde{p}}\int_{F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-j}L_{j}(x)L_{k}(x)dx=0\quad\forall k=0,\dots,p.\end{split}

Further using (
16
) and the orthogonality property (
10
) of the Legendre polynomials,
we end up with

(-1)^{p-k}\frac{2}{2k+1}[\mathbf{v}_{k,p-k}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{k,p-k}]+\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-k}\frac{2}{2k+1}\mathbf{v}_{k,\widetilde{p}-k}=0\qquad\qquad\forall k=0,\dots,p.

In other words, the order
p
coefficients of
\mathcal{P}_{p}v
are given by

(-1)^{p-k}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{k,p-k}=\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-k}\mathbf{v}_{k,\widetilde{p}-k}\qquad\qquad\forall k=0,\dots,p.

This is the 2D counterpart of (
13
).
For completeness, we collect the above identities and arrive at

\mathcal{P}_{p}v(x,y)=\sum_{j+\ell=0}^{p-1}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)+\sum_{j+\ell=p}\Big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}\Big)\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y).

As in the 1D case, we deduce

\begin{split}(v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v)(x,y)&=\sum_{j+\ell=p}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j,p-j}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)-\sum_{j+\ell=p}(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j})\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)\\
&=\sum_{j+\ell=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j,p-j}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)-\sum_{j+\ell=p}(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j})\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)\\
&=(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(x,y)-\sum_{j+\ell=p}(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j})\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y).\end{split}

(21)

Step 2: relate the tail of the Koornwinder coefficients to the trace of
v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v
on the outflow facet.

Using that
J_{\ell}^{2j+1}(-1)=(-1)^{\ell}
for all
\ell
and
j
in
\mathbb{N}
,
we write explicitly the trace of
v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v
on
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
:

(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(x,y)=\sum_{j+\ell=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y),\qquad(v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)(x,-1)=\sum_{j+\ell=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}L_{j}(x).

The orthogonality property (
10
) of the Legendre polynomials over
\widehat{I}:=[-1,1]
and (
16
),
we write

\begin{split}{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}&=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}{\left\|{L_{j}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}\Big(\sum_{\ell=\max(p+1-j,0)}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}\Big)^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{2}{2j+1}\Big(\sum_{\ell=\max(p+1-j,0)}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\mathbf{v}_{j,\ell}\Big)^{2}.\end{split}

Next, we focus on the second term on the right-hand side of (
21
).
Using (
19
),
we can write

\begin{split}&{\left\|{\sum_{j+\ell=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}\big)\Phi_{j,\ell}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}^{2}=\sum_{j+\ell=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}\big)^{2}{\left\|{\Phi_{j,\ell}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j+\ell=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}\big)^{2}\frac{2}{2j+1}\frac{1}{j+\ell+1}=\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{j=0}^{p}\frac{2}{2j+1}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}\big)^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{2}{2j+1}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{p+1}{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}.\end{split}

(22)

The last identity follows from the fact that the quantity
in the parenthesis is squared
and the only relevant fact is the alternating sign of the

(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}
term.

Step 3: deduce the desired bound using Lemma
3.1
.

Combining the above inequality with (
21
)
and using the 2D version of Lemma
3.1
,
we deduce

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}\leq{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}+(p+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}\lesssim{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}+(p+1)^{-1}{\left|{v}\right|}_{1,\widehat{T}}.

The assertion follows
noting that
\mathcal{P}_{p}
and
\Pi^{0}_{p}
preserve polynomials of maximum degree
p

and standard polynomial approximation results.

3.5
The 3D case

We prove Theorem
3.2
in three dimensions.
Compared to the 1D and 2D cases of Sections
3.3
and
3.4
,
the role of orthogonal basis functions is now played by tensor product Jacobi polynomials (of different orders)
collapsed on the tetrahedron using the 3D Duffy transformation;
in other words, we use 3D Koornwinder polynomials
following the construction in
[
7
,
24
,
16
]
.

Introduce the reference cube
\widehat{Q}=[-1,1]^{3}

and the reference tetrahedron
\widehat{T}
of vertices

(-1,-1,-1);\qquad\qquad(1,-1,-1);\qquad\qquad(0,1,-1);\qquad\qquad(0,0,1).

We denote the lower face of
\widehat{T}
by
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
;
it coincides with the reference triangle in Section
3.4

at
z=1
.

We consider the 3D Duffy transformation
\mathcal{D}:\widehat{Q}\to\widehat{T}

that maps each
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=\mathbf{z}
in
\widehat{Q}

into
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\mathbf{x}
in
\widehat{T}
as follows:

x_{1}=z_{1}\frac{1-z_{2}}{2}\frac{1-z_{3}}{2},\qquad\qquad x_{2}=z_{2}\frac{1-z_{3}}{2},\qquad\qquad x_{3}=z_{3}.

The Jacobian of the transformation is

\det\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1-z_{2}}{2}\frac{1-z_{3}}{2}&0&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}z_{1}\frac{1-z_{3}}{2}&\frac{1-z_{3}}{2}&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}z_{1}\frac{1-z_{2}}{2}&-\frac{z_{2}}{2}&1\\
\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1-z_{2}}{2}\left(\frac{1-z_{3}}{2}\right)^{2}.

We also compute the formal inverse
\mathcal{D}^{-1}
of the transformation
\mathcal{D}
:

z_{3}=x_{3},\qquad\qquad z_{2}=x_{2}\frac{2}{1-z_{3}}=\frac{2x_{2}}{1-x_{3}},\qquad\qquad z_{1}=x_{1}\frac{4}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}=\frac{4x_{1}}{1-2x_{2}-x_{3}}.

We introduce the following
L^{2}
-orthogonal basis over
\widehat{T}
:
given a multi-index
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})
in
\mathbb{N}^{3}
,

\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})=L_{j_{1}}\left(\frac{4x_{1}}{1-2x_{2}-x_{3}}\right)J_{j_{2}}^{2j_{1}+1}\left(\frac{2x_{2}}{1-x_{3}}\right)\left(\frac{1-2x_{2}-x_{3}}{2(1-x_{3})}\right)^{j_{1}}J_{j_{3}}^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2}(x_{3})\left(\frac{1-x_{3}}{2}\right)^{j_{1}+j_{2}}.

We only check the norm of the above Koornwinder polynomials.
Transforming
\widehat{T}
into
\widehat{Q}
by
\mathcal{D}^{-1}

and recalling (
11
),
we deduce

\begin{split}&\int_{\widehat{T}}\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}\\
&=\int_{\widehat{Q}}L_{j_{1}}(z_{1})^{2}J_{j_{2}}^{2j_{1}+1}(z_{2})^{2}\left(\frac{1-z_{2}}{2}\right)^{2j_{2}+1}J_{j_{3}}^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2}(z_{3})^{2}\left(\frac{1-z_{3}}{2}\right)^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2}\!\!\!d\mathbf{z}\\
&=\int_{\widehat{I}}L_{j_{1}}(z_{1})^{2}\ dz_{1}\int_{\widehat{I}}J_{j_{2}}^{2j_{1}+1}(z_{2})^{2}\left(\frac{1-z_{2}}{2}\right)^{2j_{2}+1}\!\!\!dz_{2}\int_{\widehat{I}}J_{j_{3}}^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2}(z_{3})^{2}\left(\frac{1-z_{3}}{2}\right)^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2}\!\!\!dz_{3}\\
&=\frac{2}{2j_{1}+1}\frac{2}{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2}\frac{2}{{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2j_{3}+3}}\qquad\forall\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})\in\mathbb{N}^{3}.\end{split}

(23)

Next, we prove
p
-optimal approximation properties in the
L^{2}
norm of the operator
\mathcal{P}_{p}
given as in (
12
)
with the role of outflow face played by the facet
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
.

Step 1: writing
\mathcal{P}_{p}v
with respect to the 3D version Koornwinder basis and with explicit coefficients.

Consider the following expansion of
v
:

v(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x}).

In what follows, the indices appearing
under the summation symbol are always taken nonnegative;

|\mathbf{j}|
stands for
j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}
;

\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}
is set to
0
if any of the indices
j_{1}
,

j_{2}
, and
j_{3}
is negative.

Using (
12a
), we realise that

v(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=0}^{p-1}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{j}}\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x}),

where the coefficients
\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{j}}
,

|\mathbf{j}|=p
are to be determined using (
12b
).

First, we write the trace of any polynomial basis function on
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
,
i.e., we impose the passage through
x_{3}=-1
:

\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x}){}_{|F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}=L_{j_{1}}\left(\frac{2x_{1}}{1-x_{2}}\right)J_{j_{2}}^{2j_{1}+1}(x_{2})\left(\frac{1-x_{2}}{2}\right)^{j_{1}}(-1)^{j_{3}}.

Let
\Psi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
be the 2D Koornwinder basis in (
18
)
on the reference triangle
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
.
We impose (
12b
):

\begin{split}&\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\int_{F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}[\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{j}}](-1)^{j_{3}}L_{j_{1}}\left(\frac{2x_{1}}{1-x_{2}}\right)L_{j_{2}}(x_{2})\left(\frac{1-x_{2}}{2}\right)^{j_{1}}\Psi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}\\
&+\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=\widetilde{p}}\int_{F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}(-1)^{j_{3}}L_{j_{1}}\left(\frac{2x_{1}}{1-x_{2}}\right)L_{j_{2}}(x_{2})\left(\frac{1-x_{2}}{2}\right)^{j_{1}}\Psi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}=0\\
&\qquad\qquad{\forall\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\text{ such that }\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq p.}\end{split}

We rewrite the above relation as

\begin{split}&\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\int_{F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}[\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathbf{j}}](-1)^{j_{3}}\Psi_{j_{1},j_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})\Psi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}\\
&+\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=\widetilde{p}}\int_{F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}(-1)^{j_{3}}\Psi_{j_{1},j_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})\Psi_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}=0\quad{\forall\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\text{ such that }\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq p.}\end{split}

Using the orthogonality property (
19
)
of the Koornwinder polynomials in 2D,
we deduce

\begin{split}&(-1)^{p-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}\frac{2}{2\ell_{1}+1}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+1}(\mathbf{v}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},p-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},p-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}})\\
&+\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})}\frac{2}{2\ell_{1}+1}\frac{1}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+1}\mathbf{v}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\widetilde{p}-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}=0\qquad\forall\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\text{ such that }\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq p.\end{split}

In other words, we have

\widehat{\mathbf{v}}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},p-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}=\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\widetilde{p}-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}\qquad\qquad{\forall\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\text{ such that }\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq p.}

This provides us with the representation

\mathcal{P}_{p}v(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=0}^{p-1}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}{\mathbf{v}_{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x}).

We get

\begin{split}&(\mathbf{v}-\mathcal{P}_{p}\mathbf{v})(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j_{1},j_{2},p-j_{1}-j_{2}}\Phi_{j}(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})\\
&=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{j_{1},j_{2},p-j_{1}-j_{2}}\Phi_{j}(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})\\
&=(\mathbf{v}-\Pi^{0}_{p}\mathbf{v})(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x}).\end{split}

(24)

Step 2: relate the tail of the 3D version Koornwinder coefficients to the trace of
v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v
on the outflow facet.

The restriction of
\mathbf{v}-\Pi^{0}_{p}\mathbf{v}
on the outflow facet
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}

reads

\begin{split}(\mathbf{v}-\mathcal{P}_{p}\mathbf{v})(\mathbf{x})_{|F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}&=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}L_{j_{1}}\left(\frac{2x_{1}}{1-x_{2}}\right)J_{j_{2}}^{2j_{1}+1}(x_{2})\left(\frac{1-x_{2}}{2}\right)^{j_{1}}(-1)^{j_{3}}\\
&=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p+1}^{+\infty}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\Psi_{j_{1},j_{2}}(\mathbf{x})(-1)^{j_{3}}.\end{split}

We take the
L^{2}
norm on both sides over
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
,
use the orthogonality property (
19
) of Koornwinder polynomials over
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
,
and get,
for
\widetilde{j}
equal to
\max(p+1-(j_{1}+j_{2}),0)
,

{\left\|{\mathbf{v}-\mathcal{P}_{p}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}=\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=0}^{+\infty}{\left\|{\Psi_{j_{1},j_{2}}}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}\big(\sum_{j_{3}=\widetilde{j}}(-1)^{j_{3}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\big)^{2}=\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=0}^{+\infty}\frac{2}{2j_{2}+1}\frac{1}{j_{1}+j_{2}+1}\big(\sum_{j_{3}=\widetilde{j}}(-1)^{j_{3}}\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{j}}\big)^{2}.

We show a bound on the
L^{2}
norm over
\widehat{T}
of the second term on the right-hand side of (
24
)
in terms of the
L^{2}
norm of
\mathbf{v}-\mathcal{P}_{p}\mathbf{v}
on
F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}
.
To this aim, we use (

):

\begin{split}&{\left\|{\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}(\mathbf{x})}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}^{2}=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)^{2}{\left\|{\Phi_{\mathbf{j}}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{|\mathbf{j}|=p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)^{2}\frac{2}{2j_{1}+1}\frac{1}{j_{1}+j_{2}+1}\frac{1}{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+1}\\
&=\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=0}^{p}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)^{2}\frac{2}{2j_{1}+1}\frac{1}{j_{1}+j_{2}+1}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=0}^{+\infty}\big(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{{j_{1},j_{2},\widetilde{p}-j_{1}-j_{2}}}\big)^{2}\frac{2}{2j_{1}+1}\frac{1}{j_{1}+j_{2}+1}=\frac{1}{p+1}{\left\|{\mathbf{v}-\Pi^{0}_{p}\mathbf{v}}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}.\end{split}

Step 3: deduce the desired bound using optimal trace error estimates.

Combining the above inequality with (
21
)
and using the 3D version of Lemma
3.1
,
we deduce

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}\leq{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}+(p+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}\lesssim{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{T}}+(p+1)^{-1}{\left|{v}\right|}_{1,\widehat{T}}.

The assertion follows
noting that
\mathcal{P}_{p}
and
\Pi^{0}_{p}
preserve polynomials of maximum degree
p

and standard polynomial approximation results.

3.6

The CDG operator satisfies

To this aim, we show separate bounds on the facets.

By (

Approximation properties on the inflow facets are more elaborated
and are discussed in the next result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let the assumptions (

) and (

Proof.

The proof of the 1D case is fairly simple.

Proof of (

Here

Proof of (

) in 2D.

Here,
\widehat{T}
is as in (

).
Recall expansion (

)
with the caveat that coefficients
related to negative indices are set to zero.

Introduce

\REM:=\sum_{j+\ell=p}(\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j})\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)

and

{\widehat{C}_{p}}:=\max_{j+\ell=p}{\left\|{\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}.

We have

{\left\|{\REM}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j+\ell=p}|\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}|^{2}{\left\|{\Phi_{j,\ell}(x,y)}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}\leq{\widehat{C}_{p}}^{2}\sum_{j+\ell=p}|\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}|^{2},

\begin{split}{\left\|{\REM}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}&\lesssim{\widehat{C}_{p}}^{2}\sum_{j+\ell=p}|\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}|^{2}\frac{2}{2j+1}\frac{2j+1}{2}\\
&\leq{\widehat{C}_{p}}^{2}\frac{2p+1}{2}\sum_{j+\ell=p}|\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}|^{2}\frac{2}{2j+1}.\end{split}

We proceed as in (

{\left\|{\REM}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}\lesssim{\widehat{C}_{p}}^{2}\frac{2p+1}{2}\sum_{j+\ell=p}|\sum_{\widetilde{p}=p+1}^{+\infty}(-1)^{\widetilde{p}-p}\mathbf{v}_{j,\widetilde{p}-j}|^{2}\frac{2}{2j+1}\leq{\widehat{C}_{p}}^{2}\frac{2p+1}{2}{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}.

Combining (

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}\leq{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}+{\left\|{\REM}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}\lesssim{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}+{\widehat{C}_{p}}p^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}.

(26)

If we were able to prove that

and (

In fact, we would write

{\left\|{v-\mathcal{P}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}\leq{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}+{\left\|{\REM}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}\lesssim{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}+{\left\|{v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v}\right\|}_{0,F^{+}_{\widehat{T}}}.

For the sake of presentation, we fix

to be the inflow facet of
\widehat{T}
with negative

If this is the case, then a direct application of definition (

\Phi_{j,\ell}{}_{|F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}=(-1)^{j}J_{\ell}^{2j+1}(y)\left(\frac{1-y}{2}\right)^{j}.

Taking into account the Jacobian of the transformation mapping the facet

into the “vertical” facet

and recalling (

),
we deduce

{\left\|{\Phi_{j,\ell}}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}=\int_{-1}^{1}\left[J_{\ell}^{2j+1}(y)\right]^{2}\left(\frac{1-y}{2}\right)^{2j+1}=\frac{2^{2j+2}}{2j+1+2\ell+1}\frac{1}{2^{2j+1}}=\frac{1}{1+j+\ell}.

Since we are considering couples

{\left\|{\Phi_{j,\ell}}\right\|}_{0,F^{-}_{\widehat{T}}}^{2}=\frac{1}{p+1},

i.e.,

.

Proof.

Introduce

The rewriting of the bilinear form in (

6
Conclusions

We analyzed
p
-optimal approximation properties of the CDG operator
in the
L^{2}
norm and the
L^{2}
norm of the trace
on simplices with only one outflow facet in 1D, 2D, and 3D.
These results are instrumental in deriving

p
-optimal error estimates for the original DG method on special simplicial meshes.
Numerical experiments validate the predicted convergence rates measured in the
L^{2}
norm
also for convection fields and meshes not satisfying the assumptions used in our proofs;
convergence rates are observed for the error measured
in the full DG norm that are
better than those proven in theory.

Regularly limited, Grothendieck, and Dunford–Pettis operators


Abstract
We introduce and study the enveloping norms of regularly 𝒫 \cal{P} -operators, where 𝒫 \cal{P} is an ”almost” version of limited, Grothendieck, and of Dunford–Pettis operators in Banach lattices. Several further topics related to these operators are investigated.

Various types of compactness in Banach spaces were in streamline
of functional analysis in the second half of the 20th century.
At some point, in order to diversify the classes of compact operators,
the Banach lattice structure had come in play by Meyer-Nieberg,
Dodds, Fremlin, Aliprantis, Wickstead, and others.
In the present paper, we continue this line of study with main
emphasis on regularity of operators under the investigation.
We introduce enveloping norms on spaces of regularly
{\cal P}
-operators
and give conditions under which these spaces are complete under their
enveloping norms.

It is natural to replace

(3)

Lemma 2.2.1
.

For a vector subspace
{\cal P}
of

(\ref{enveloping norm})
defines a norm on

\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}=\inf\{\|S\|:S\in{\cal P}\ \&\ (\forall x\in E)\ |Tx|\leq S|x|\}\ \ \ (T\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)).

(4)

If

is a vector subspace of
{\cal P}
then

\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}_{1}}\geq\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\geq\|T\|_{r}\geq\|T\|\ \ \ \ \ (\forall\ T\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}_{1}(E,F)).

(5)

Proof.

Only the triangle inequality for
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}
and the
formula (
4
) require some explanations.

(A) Let
T_{1},T_{2}\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Pick

S_{1},S_{2}\in{\cal P}
with
\pm T_{1}\leq S_{1}
,
\pm T_{2}\leq S_{2}
,

\|S_{1}\|\leq\|T_{1}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}+\varepsilon
, and

\|S_{2}\|\leq\|T_{2}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}+\varepsilon
.
Then
\pm(T_{1}+T_{2})\leq S_{1}+S_{2}\in{\cal P}
, and

\|T_{1}+T_{2}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\leq\|S_{1}+S_{2}\|\leq\|S_{1}\|+\|S_{2}\|\leq\|T_{1}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}+\|T_{2}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}+2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0

\|T_{1}+T_{2}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\leq\|T_{1}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}+\|T_{2}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}
.

(B) Denote the right side of (
4

R(T)
.
If
\pm T\leq S\in{\cal P}
then

\pm Tx=\pm(T(x_{+})-T(x_{-}))=\pm T(x_{+})\mp T(x_{-})\leq S(x_{+})+S(x_{-})=S|x|

for all
x\in E

|Tx|\leq S|x|
for all
x\in E
, and hence

\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\geq R(T)
.

If
S\in{\cal P}
satisfies
|Tx|\leq S|x|
for all
x\in E
then,
for all
y\in E_{+}
,
|Ty|\leq Sy
and consequently
\pm Ty\leq Sy
.
Therefore
\pm T\leq S
, and hence
R(T)\geq\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}
.
∎

2.3

The following theorem can be considered as an extension of

Theorem 2.3.1
.

Let
{\cal P}
be a subspace of
\text{\rm L}(E,F)

closed in the operator norm.
Then
\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)
is a Banach space under the
enveloping norm.

Proof.

Let
(T_{n})
be

\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}
-Cauchy in
\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)
,
say
T_{n}=P_{n}-R_{n}
for
P_{n},R_{n}\in{\cal P}\cap{\cal L}_{+}(E,F)

WLOG, we can assume that
\|T_{n+1}-T_{n}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}<2^{-n}
for all

n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\geq\|\cdot\|
, there exists some

T\in\text{\rm L}(E,F)
with
\|T-T_{n}\|\to 0
.
We obtain
T\in{\cal P}
because
T_{n}\in{\cal P}
and
{\cal P}
is closed
in the operator norm. Pick
S_{n}\in{\cal P}
with
\|S_{n}\|<2^{-n}
and

\pm(T_{n+1}-T_{n})\leq S_{n}
. By (
4
),
|(T_{n+1}-T_{n})x|\leq S_{n}|x|
for
all
x\in E
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
{\cal P}
is closed in the operator norm,

Q_{n}:=\|\cdot\|\text{\rm-}\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}S_{k}\in{\cal P}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since

|(T-T_{n})x|=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}|(T_{k}-T_{n})x|\leq\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}|(T_{k+1}-T_{n})x|\leq Q_{n}|x|\ \ \ \ \ (\forall x\in E),

then
\pm(T-T_{n})\leq Q_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus
-Q_{n}\leq(T-T_{n})\leq Q_{n}
and hence
0\leq(T-T_{n})+Q_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

T=[(T-T_{n})+Q_{n}]+[T_{n}-Q_{n}]=[(T-T_{n})+Q_{n}+P_{n}]-[R_{n}+Q_{n}]\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F),

and hence
(T-T_{n})\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now,
\|T-T_{n}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\leq\|Q_{n}\|<2^{1-n}

(T_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}}}{{\to}}T
.

F\in\text{\rm(d)}
. Then

\text{\rm r-a-G}(E,F)={\cal L}_{r}(E,F).

Proof.

It suffices to prove that each positive operator
T:E\to F
is
a-G
.
Let
0\leq T\in{\cal L}(E,F)
,
and let
(f_{n})
be disjoint
\text{\rm w}^{\ast}
-null in
F^{\prime}
.
Since
F\in\text{\rm(d)}
then
(|f_{n}|)
is also
\text{\rm w}^{\ast}
-null.
As
T^{\prime}:F^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
is positive,
T^{\prime}
is continuous and hence
T^{\prime}
is

\text{\rm w}^{\ast}
-continuous.
Therefore
(T^{\prime}|f_{n}|)
is
\text{\rm w}^{\ast}
-null in
E^{\prime}
.
It follows from
|T^{\prime}f_{n}|\leq T^{\prime}|f_{n}|
that

(|T^{\prime}f_{n}|)
is
\text{\rm w}^{\ast}
-null. Since

E\in\text{\rm(a-DP}^{\ast}\text{\rm P)}
,

|T^{\prime}f_{n}|(x_{n})\to 0
for each disjoint bounded
(x_{n})
in
E
.
By Assertion
1.3.2
(ii),
\|T^{\prime}f_{n}\|\to 0
and hence

(T^{\prime}f_{n})
is
w
-null. Therefore
T
is
a-G
.
∎

Proposition 5.5.3
.

\text{\rm DP}(E,F)\subseteq\text{\rm Lm}(E,F)

-continuous norm,

It is enough to show that if the norm in

, the order interval

(y_{n})

\text{\rm w}^{\ast}

(g_{n})
in

. Let the norm in

-continuous and

There is a positive order bounded

in

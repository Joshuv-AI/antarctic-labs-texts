Palm measures for Dirac operators and the Sine β \operatorname{Sine}_{\beta} process
Benedek Valkó, Bálint Virág

Abstract
We characterize the Palm measure of the Sine β \operatorname{Sine}_{\beta} process as the eigenvalues of an associated operator with a specific boundary condition.



1
Introduction

The Palm measure of a point process is its distribution as seen from a typical point. We study the Palm measure of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process, which is the bulk scaling limit of the eigenvalues for a large class of random matrices. (See
Valkó and Virág 2009
,
Valkó and Virág 2017
,
Bourgade et al. 2014
.)
There are several reasons to do this:

•

the secular function for (the operator associated to) the Palm measure has as simpler description than that of the original
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process,

•

the intensity of the Palm measure is given by the two-point correlation functions of the original process, a source of many open problems and conjectures,

•

the operator description of the Palm measure can be given explicitly: this is the main goal of the current paper,

•

in the case of the circular beta ensembles, the finite version of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process, the distribution of the Verblunsky coefficients can be given explicitly,

•

the Palm measures are closely related to the well-studied circular Jacobi ensembles and Hua-Pickrell measures of random matrix theory. We will describe the connection explicitly.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}
be independent two-sided standard Brownian motion, and for
t\in(0,1]
set

\displaystyle y_{t}=e^{b_{2}(u)-u/2},\quad x_{t}=-\int_{u}^{0}e^{b_{2}(s)-\tfrac{s}{2}}db_{1},\quad u=u(t)=\tfrac{4}{\beta}\log t.

(1)

Let
q
be Cauchy distributed random variable independent of
b_{1},b_{2}
.
Define the function
R(t):(0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times 2}
via

\displaystyle R=\frac{X^{t}X}{2\det X},\qquad X=\begin{pmatrix}1&-x\\
0&y\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

In this paper, we consider the
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\beta}
operator

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\beta}u=R^{-1}Ju^{\prime},\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

(3)

acting on absolutely continuous of functions of the form
u:(0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, with the boundary conditions
[1,0]^{t}
at
0
and
[-q,-1]^{t}
at
1
. The spectrum of the
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\beta}
operator is given by the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process, see
Valkó and Virág 2017
. Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

The Palm measure of the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
process has the same law as the spectrum of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\beta}
with modified boundary condition
[1,0]^{t}
at
1
.

The proof of this theorem relies on two key facts: one is the shift invariance of the process and the other is the special relationship between the spectral measure and the eigenvalue distribution for the
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\beta}
operator. Note that in this paper the spectral measure is always a real valued measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
.

For an
n\times n
unitary matrix
U
and a nonzero vector
v
the spectral measure at
v
is the probability measure

\sum_{\lambda}\langle v,\varphi_{\lambda}\rangle^{2}\delta_{\lambda}.

Here the sum is over all eigenvalues
\lambda
, and the
\varphi_{\lambda}
are the unit length eigenvectors of
U
forming an orthonormal basis.

For the Dirac operators of the type (
3
), we replace
\langle v,\varphi_{\lambda}\rangle
by the value of
\varphi_{\lambda}
at
0
or
1
to get the
left
and
right spectral measures
. More precisely,

\displaystyle\mu_{\textup{left}}=\sum_{\lambda}|\varphi_{\lambda}(0)|^{2}\delta_{\lambda},\qquad\mu_{\textup{right}}=\sum_{\lambda}|\varphi_{\lambda}(1)|^{2}\delta_{\lambda},

(4)

where
\varphi_{\lambda}
is the eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda
with normalization
\int_{0}^{1}\varphi^{t}_{\lambda}R\varphi_{\lambda}dt=1
. For the Dirac operators we consider the spectral measure has infinite total mass.

An important ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1
is the description of how the right spectral measure of a random Dirac operator changes when it is biased by the weight of zero. This is the content of the following proposition, see Proposition
28
for a more precise formulation.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mu_{q}
denote the right spectral measure of the random Dirac operator of the form (
3
) with boundary conditions
[1,0]^{t}
and
[-q,-1]^{t}
, with
\mu_{\infty}
corresponding to boundary conditions
[1,0]^{t}
at both ends.
Assume that
q
is chosen independently of the function
R
with a sufficiently regular distribution. Then the law of
\mu_{q}
biased by
\mu_{q}((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
has a distributional limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
given by
\mu_{\infty}
.

Another important ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1
is the following.

Proposition 3
.

The weights of the right spectral measure of the
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{\beta}
operator are independent of each other and from the sequence of eigenvalues. The weights have gamma distribution with shape parameter
\beta/2
and mean 2.

This is closely related to Theorem 9 in
Najnudel and Virág 2021
, where the spectral measure is implicitly used to define the bead process, a Markov process with stationary distribution given by the
\operatorname{Sine}_{\beta}
point process. The connection is through the relationship between spectral measures and rank-one perturbations. Rather than exploring this connection, we deduce Proposition
3
from its finite-
n
analogue.

For
n\geq 1
and
\beta>0
the size
n
circular beta-ensemble is the distribution of
n
points on the unit circle with probability density

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{n,\beta}}\prod_{j<k\leq n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{\beta}.

(5)

The
Killip-Nenciu measure

\mu_{n,\beta}^{\textup{KN}}
for given
n\geq 1
and
\beta>0
is the random probability measure where the support is given by the size
n
circular beta-ensemble, and the weights have Dirichlet
(\beta/2,\dots,\beta/2)
joint distribution and are independent of the support.

In general, a finitely supported probability measure on the unit circle can be characterized by its modified Verblunski coefficients from the theory of orthogonal polynomials. When a probability measure is supported on
n
points, all but the first
n
modified Verblunski coefficients vanish. The first
n-1
modified Verblunski coefficients are all in the unit disk
{\mathbb{U}}=\{z:|z|<1\}
, with the last one being on the unit circle
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
(see
Killip and Nenciu 2004
,
Bourgade et al. 2009
).

The joint distribution of the modified Verblunski coefficients
\gamma_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
of the Killip-Nenciu measure was described in
Killip and Nenciu 2004
and
Bourgade et al. 2009
. It was shown that
\gamma_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
are independent,
\gamma_{k}
have density on
\mathbb{U}
proportional to

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}(n-k-1)-1},\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-2,

(6)

and
\gamma_{n-1}
is uniform on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
.
Note that the distribution of
|\gamma_{k}|^{2}
is Beta
(1,\frac{\beta}{2}(n-k-1))
, and
\gamma_{k}
has rotationally invariant distribution.

In
Valkó and Virág 2017
it was shown that the spectral information of a probability measure on the unit circle can also be described via a Dirac operator of the form (
3
), with
R
defined from piece-wise constant paths
x,y
via (
2
), that are built using the modified Verblunski coefficients. See Section
4
and Proposition
31
below. We extend the connection further by the following lemma, see Proposition
33
for the precise formulation.

Lemma 4
.

Consider a probability measure
\mu
supported on
e^{i\lambda_{j}},1\leq j\leq n
, and the corresponding Dirac operator
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
. Then the left spectral measure of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is the same as the lifting of
\mu
to
\mathbb{R}
stretched by
n
and multiplied by
2n
:

\mu_{\textup{left},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(n\lambda_{j}+2nk\pi)=2n\mu(e^{i\lambda_{j}}),\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},1\leq j\leq n.

The factor
2
is related to our convention in (
2
). This convention comes from the carousel representation, see
Valkó and Virág 2017
, in which the rotation speed is then given by the eigenvalue
\lambda
. Equivalently, this is related to the intensity
1/(2\pi)
of the Sine
β
process.

The Palm measure corresponding to a measure with random Verblunski coefficients can be explicitly characterized in certain cases. The following is a special case of Proposition
50
below.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mu
be a random probability measure supported on
n
points on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
.
Assume that its modified Verblunsky coefficients
\gamma_{i},0\leq i\leq n-1
are independent and have rotationally invariant law. Then the Palm measure of
\mu
agrees with the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit of
\mu
biased by the weight of the arc
\{e^{i\theta},\theta\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\}
. The modified Verblunski coefficients of the Palm measure are given by

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}_{i}=-\gamma_{i}\frac{1-\bar{\gamma}_{i}}{1-\gamma_{i}},\qquad n=0,\ldots,n-2,\qquad\gamma^{\prime}_{n-1}=1.

(7)

In the setting of probability measures on the unit circle the analogue of boundary condition for Dirac operators is given by the notion of Aleksandrov measures. The modified Verblunski coefficients of Aleksandrov measures have a more complex description, leading to formulas such as (
7
), see Section
6
.

Proposition
5
provides an explicit description of Verblunski coefficients of the Palm measure of the Killip-Nenciu measure as follows.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mu
be chosen from the Killip-Nenciu law with parameters
\beta,n
, let
\tilde{\mu}
be the uniform probability measure on the support of
\mu
, and
let
\nu
be
\mu
biased by the weight of 1.
Let
X
be picked from
\mu
, and
Y
be picked from the
\tilde{\mu}
.
Then
\operatorname{supp}\mu(X\cdot)
,
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}(Y\cdot)
, and
\operatorname{supp}\nu(\cdot)
all have the same law.
The Verblunski coefficients
\gamma_{k}^{\prime}
of
\nu
are independent and have density proportional to

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}(n-k-1)}|1-z|^{-2},\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-2,

(8)

and
\gamma_{n-1}^{\prime}=1
.

The distribution of
\nu
can be computed directly by noting that its support is the size
n
circular beta-ensemble conditioned to have a point at 1, and the weights are just a size-biased version of the Dirichlet
(\beta/2,\dots,\beta/2)
distribution.

The support of the measure
\nu
has a point at 1, and if we remove that point then the joint density of the remaining points
is proportional to

\displaystyle\prod_{i<j\leq n-1}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}|1-z_{j}|^{\beta}.

(9)

This
is a circular Jacobi beta-ensemble of size
n-1
, with parameter
\delta=\beta/2
.

The weights of
\nu
are independent of the support, and they are Dirichlet distributed with parameters
\beta/2
(
n-1
times) and
\beta/2+1
(once). The last weight belongs to the point at
1
.

If we remove the point at 1 from the
\nu
and renormalize the weights to sum up to 1, then the support and the weights are still independent, with the
n-1
remaining weights distributed as Dirichlet
(\beta/2,\dots,\beta/2)
.
The modified Verblunski coefficients
\gamma_{k},0\leq k\leq n-2
of this measure were described in
Bourgade et al. 2009
. These are independent of each other, and the density of
\gamma_{k}
on
{\mathbb{U}}
is proportional to

(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}(n-k-2)-1}|1-z|^{\beta},\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-1,

and the density of
\gamma_{n-2}
on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
is proportional to

|1-z|^{\beta}
.
It would be interesting to find a direct derivation of this fact.

Problem 7
.

Find a simple derivation of the joint distribution of the modified Verblunsky coefficients for the random probability measure obtained by removing the point 1 from
\nu
(and renormalizing), directly from Proposition
6
.

2
Dirac operators and their spectral measures

2.1
Dirac operator basics

Let
R:{\mathcal{I}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a function taking values in nonnegative definite matrices. Here
{\mathcal{I}}
is an interval with
\overline{{\mathcal{I}}}=[0,\sigma]
, that may be open at one of its endpoints. Most often we will deal with the case
[0,1)
or
(0,1]
.

In this paper, a
Dirac operator
is defined as

{\boldsymbol{\tau}}u=R^{-1}Ju^{\prime},\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

(10)

acting on some subset of functions of the form
u:{\mathcal{I}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Assumption 1
.

R(t)
is positive definite for all
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
,
\|R\|,\|R^{-1}\|
are locally bounded on
{\mathcal{I}}
. Moreover,
\det R(t)=1/4
for all
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

If
R
satisfies Assumption 1 then it can be parametrized as

\displaystyle R=\frac{X^{t}X}{2\det X},\qquad X=\begin{pmatrix}1&-x\\
0&y\end{pmatrix},\qquad y>0,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(11)

The assumption
\det R=1/4
can be replaced by the more general condition
\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}\det R(s)\,ds<\infty
. This setting is equivalent to ours up to a time change.

Assumption 2
.

•

If
{\mathcal{I}}
is open on the left then there is a nonzero
\mathfrak{u}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
so that we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}\left\|\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{t}R\right\|ds<\infty,\qquad\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<t\\
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{I}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{t}R(s)\mathfrak{u}_{0}\,(J\mathfrak{u}_{0})^{t}R(t)J\mathfrak{u}_{0}dsdt<\infty.

(12)

•

If
{\mathcal{I}}
is open on the right then there is a nonzero
\mathfrak{u}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
so that we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}\left\|\mathfrak{u}_{1}^{t}R\right\|ds<\infty,\qquad\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s>t\\
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{I}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{u}_{1}^{t}R(s)\mathfrak{u}_{1}\,(J\mathfrak{u}_{1})^{t}R(t)J\mathfrak{u}_{1}dsdt<\infty.

(13)

Note that if
{\mathcal{I}}
is closed and
R
satisfies Assumption 1 then (
12
) and (
13
) hold for any
\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Moreover, if
\bar{I}=[0,\sigma]
,
0<t<\sigma
and
R
satisfies Assumptions 1-2 with an appropriate
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
or
\mathfrak{u}_{1}
(if applicable), then it also satisfies the assumptions on
{\mathcal{I}}\cap[0,t]
.

The Dirac operator
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
with boundary conditions
\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}
on
{\mathcal{I}}
is self-adjoint on an appropriate domain. Let
L^{2}_{R}=L^{2}_{R}({\mathcal{I}})

denote the
L^{2}
sub-space of
{\mathcal{I}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
functions with the squared norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{R}^{2}=\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}f^{t}(s)R(s)f(s)ds.

(14)

Let
ac
denote the subset of
{\mathcal{I}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
functions that are absolutely continuous. The following proposition follows from standard theory of Dirac operators (see e.g.
Weidmann 1987
,
Valkó and Virág 2017
).

Proposition 8
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
be a Dirac operator satisfying Assumption 1 on
{\mathcal{I}}
. Let
\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}
be non-zero vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with which the appropriate case of Assumption 2 is satisfied. Then
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is self-adjoint on the following domain:

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\{v\in L^{2}_{R}\cap{\text{\sc ac}}\,:\,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}v\in L^{2}_{R},\,\lim_{s\downarrow 0}v(s)^{t}\,J\,\mathfrak{u}_{0}=0,\,\,\lim_{s\uparrow\sigma}v(s)^{t}\,J\,\mathfrak{u}_{1}=0\}.

(15)

We denote the self-adjoint operator
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
with boundary conditions

\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}
by
\mathtt{Dir}(R_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
or
\mathtt{Dir}(x_{\cdot}+iy_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
.

When
\mathfrak{u}_{0}\not\,\parallel\mathfrak{u}_{1}
then we also make the following assumption, which is just a normalization.

Assumption 3
.

\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1}=1
.

Note that if
\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}
satisfy this assumption then we have
u_{1}=\frac{1}{\|u_{0}\|^{2}}J^{t}\mathfrak{u}_{0}+c\mathfrak{u}_{0}
with
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

If
\mathfrak{u}_{0}\not\,\parallel\mathfrak{u}_{1}
then
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(R_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
has an inverse that is a Hilbert-Schmidt integral operator (see
Weidmann 1987
or
Valkó and Virág 2017
).

Proposition 9
.

Suppose that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(R_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
with Assumptions 1-3 are satisfied. Then
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt integral operator on
L^{2}_{R}
given by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1}f(s)=\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}K_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1}}(s,t)R(t)f(t)dt,\qquad K_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1}}(s,t)=\mathfrak{u}_{0}\mathfrak{u}_{1}^{t}\mathbf{1}(s<t)+\mathfrak{u}_{1}\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}(s\geq t),

(16)

and we have

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1}\|_{\textup{HS}}^{2}=2\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<t\\
(s,t)\in{\mathcal{I}}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{u}_{0}^{t}R(s)\mathfrak{u}_{0}\,\mathfrak{u}_{1}^{t}R(t)\mathfrak{u}_{1}dsdt.

(17)

Let
Y=\tfrac{X}{\sqrt{\det X}}
with
X
defined in (
11
). Then the conjugated integral operator

\displaystyle{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}:=Y{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1}Y^{-1}

(18)

is self-adjoint on
L^{2}
with integral kernel

\displaystyle K_{{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(s,t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\,a_{0}(s)a_{1}(t)^{t}\mathbf{1}(s<t)\;+\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,a_{1}(s)a_{0}(t)^{t}\mathbf{1}(s\geq t),

(19)

where

a_{0}=\frac{X\mathfrak{u}_{0}}{\sqrt{\det X}},\qquad a_{1}=\frac{X\mathfrak{u}_{1}}{\sqrt{\det X}}.

(20)

We have
\operatorname{spec}({\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\operatorname{spec}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1})
, in particular
\|{\mathtt{r}\,}\tau\|^{2}_{\textup{HS}}=\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{-1}\|^{2}_{\textup{HS}}
.

2.2
Secular function of a Dirac operator

Valkó and Virág 2020
introduced the
secular function
of a Dirac operator as a generalization of the (normalized) characteristic polynomial of a matrix.

Definition 10
.

Suppose that the Dirac operator
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(R_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
satisfies Assumptions 1-3 on
{\mathcal{I}}
. We define its integral trace as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}\mathfrak{u}_{0}R(s)\mathfrak{u}_{1}ds,

(21)

and its secular function as

\displaystyle\zeta_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(z)=e^{-z\cdot{\mathfrak{t}}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}{\det}_{2}(I-z\,{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}).

(22)

Here
{\det}_{2}
is the (second) regularized determinant, see
Simon 2005c
, Chapter 9.

The secular function of a Dirac operator
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is an entire function with zero set given by
\operatorname{spec}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, and it is real on
{\mathbb{R}}
. As the next proposition shows, it can also be represented in terms of a canonical system. (See Proposition 13 in
Valkó and Virág 2020
.)

Proposition 11
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{I}}
is closed on the right, and
R
,
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
satisfy Assumptions 1 and 2.
There is a unique vector-valued function
H:{\mathcal{I}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
so that for every
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
the function
H(\cdot,z)
is the solution of the ordinary differential equation

\displaystyle J\frac{d}{dt}H(t,z)

\displaystyle=zR(t)H(t,z),\qquad t\in{\mathcal{I}},\qquad\lim_{t\to 0}H(t,z)=\mathfrak{u}_{0}.

(23)

For any
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
the vector function
H(t,z)
satisfies
\|H(t,z)\|>0
, and its two entries are entire functions of
z
mapping the reals to the reals.

In addition, if
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(R_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
with

\mathfrak{u}_{1}
satisfying Assumption 3, then

\displaystyle\zeta_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(z)=H(1,z)^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1}.

(24)

The proposition immediately implies that under the listed conditions for any
0<t<\sigma
we have

\zeta_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}(z)=H(t,z)^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1},

where
\zeta_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}
is the secular function of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
defined on
{\mathcal{I}}\cap[0,t]
with boundary conditions
\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}
.

As the next proposition shows, the function
H(t,\cdot)
is continuous on compacts in
t
at
t=0
, and it depends continuously on
R
as well. We use the notation
\mathfrak{t}_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}
and
{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{t}
for the integral trace and resolvent of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
restricted to
{\mathcal{I}}\cap[0,t]
.

Proposition 12
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{I}}
is closed on the right, and
R
,
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
satisfy Assumptions 1 and 2, and let
H
be the solution of (
23
). Suppose that
\mathfrak{u}_{1}
satisfies Assumption 3, and let
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
be the Dirac operator defined by
R,\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}
. Then there is an absolute constant
c>1
(depending only on
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
) so that for all
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
we have

\displaystyle|H(t,z)-\mathfrak{u}_{0}|\leq\left(c^{|z|(|\mathfrak{t}_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}|+\|{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{t}\|+\int_{0}^{t}|a_{0}(s)|^{2}ds)}-1\right)c^{\left(|z|(|\mathfrak{t}_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}|+\|{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{t}\|+\int_{0}^{t}|a_{0}(s)|^{2}ds)+1\right)^{2}}.

(25)

Suppose that
\tilde{R}
,
\mathfrak{u}_{0},\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}_{1}
also satisfy Assumptions 1-3 on the same
{\mathcal{I}}
, let
\widetilde{H}
be the solution of (
23
), and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
the corresponding Dirac operator.
Then there is an absolute constant
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
(depending only on
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
) so that for all
t\in{\mathcal{I}}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
we have

\displaystyle|H(t,z)-\tilde{H}(t,z)|\leq\left(c^{|z|\left(|\mathfrak{t}_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}-\tilde{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}|+\|{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{t}-{\mathtt{r}\,}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{t}\|+\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}|a_{0}(s)-\tilde{a}_{0}(s)|^{2}ds\int_{0}^{t}(|a_{0}(s)|^{2}+|\tilde{a}_{0}(s)|^{2})ds}\right)}-1\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 150.0pt\times c^{\left(|z|(|\mathfrak{t}_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}|+|\mathfrak{t}_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\tau}},t}|+\|{\mathtt{r}\,}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{t}\|+\|{\mathtt{r}\,}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{t}\|+\int_{0}^{t}\left(|a_{0}(s)|^{2}+|\tilde{a}_{0}(s)|^{2}\right)ds)+1\right)^{2}}.

(26)

Proof.

Theorem 9.2(c) of
Simon 2005c
shows that if
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
are Hilbert-Schmidt operators on the same domain then

\displaystyle|{\det}_{2}(I-z\kappa_{1})-{\det}_{2}(I-z\kappa_{2})|\leq|z|\cdot\|\kappa_{1}-\kappa_{2}\|_{2}c^{|z|^{2}(\|\kappa_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\kappa_{2}\|_{2}^{2})+1}

(27)

with an absolute constant
c
. Using (
24
) one readily obtains a bound of the forms (
25
), (
26
) for
|H(t,z)^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1}-1|
and
|H(t,z)^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1}-\tilde{H}(t,z)^{t}J\tilde{\mathfrak{u}}_{1}|
, without the
a_{0},\tilde{a}_{0}
integral terms.

Note that Assumptions 1-3 also hold for
R,\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}+\mathfrak{u}_{0}
(and similarly for
\tilde{R},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1}+\mathfrak{u}_{0}
). These yield bounds for
|H(t,z)^{t}J(\mathfrak{u}_{0}+\mathfrak{u}_{1})-1|

Proposition 13
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{I}}
is closed from the right, and

\mathfrak{u}_{0}
satisfy Assumptions 1 and 2.

. In particular, if

are given by the zero set of

Proof.

By differentiating (

J\partial_{t}\partial_{z}H=RH+zR\partial_{z}H,\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}(H^{t}J\partial_{z}H)=H^{t}RH.

For

H^{t}J\partial_{\lambda}H(\sigma)-H^{t}J\partial_{\lambda}H(\varepsilon)=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}H^{t}RHds>0.

We let

at

\displaystyle 0<\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}H^{t}RHds=H^{t}J\partial_{z}H(\sigma)\in{\mathbb{R}}.

This proves that

.

If

In this section we will work with the case

Consider a Dirac operator
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(R_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})

satisfying Assumptions 1 and 2.
Denote the spectrum of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}

\operatorname{spec}{\boldsymbol{\tau}}

Definition 15

The

\displaystyle\mu_{\textup{left}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}\tau}\frac{f_{\lambda}(0)^{t}f_{\lambda}(0)}{\|f_{\lambda}\|_{R}^{2}}\delta_{\lambda},\qquad\mu_{\textup{right}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}\tau}\frac{f_{\lambda}(1)^{t}f_{\lambda}(1)}{\|f_{\lambda}\|_{R}^{2}}\delta_{\lambda}.

By Corollary
14
these measures are well-defined. If

is closed from the right then

Definition 16
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be closed on the right, and
\mathfrak{u}_{0}=[1,0]^{t}
. Suppose that
that
R
,
\mathfrak{u}_{0}
satisfy Assumptions 1 and 2, and consider

from (

Here we take the continuous branch of logarithm so that

for

The next result shows how we can identify the spectral measure of a Dirac operator using the phase function.

Lemma 17
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be closed on the right,
\mathfrak{u}_{0}=[1,0]^{t}
, and
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[-q,-1]
or
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[1,0]
. Suppose that
R,\mathfrak{u}_{0}
satisfy Assumptions 1 and 2, let
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(R_{\cdot},\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
, and consider the phase function

given in Definition

\mu_{\textup{right}}=2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(\alpha^{-1})^{\prime}(2\pi k+u)\delta_{\alpha^{-1}(2\pi k+u)}

where
\cot(u/2)=-q
if
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[-q,-1]
and

if
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[1,0]

\mu_{\textup{right}}(\lambda)=\frac{2}{\partial_{\lambda}\alpha(1,\lambda)}=\frac{A^{2}(\lambda)+B^{2}(\lambda)}{A^{\prime}(\lambda)B(\lambda)-A(\lambda)B^{\prime}(\lambda)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
13
the spectrum of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is given by the zero set of the function
H(1,z)^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1}
. We have
H(1,z)^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1}=A(z)-qB(z)
if
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[-q,-1]
, and
H(1,z)^{t}J\mathfrak{u}_{1}=B(z)
if
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[1,0]

is exactly the set

If

is an eigenvalue then by (

\int_{0}^{1}H^{t}RHds=H^{t}J\partial_{z}H(1)=A^{\prime}(z)B(z)-A(z)B^{\prime}(z).

This gives the expression

\mu_{\textup{right}}(\lambda)=\frac{H(\lambda,1)^{t}H(\lambda,1)}{H^{t}(1,\lambda)J\partial_{\lambda}H(1,\lambda)}=\frac{A^{2}(\lambda)+B^{2}(\lambda)}{A^{\prime}(\lambda)B(\lambda)-A(\lambda)B^{\prime}(\lambda)}.

We also have

\partial_{\lambda}\alpha(1,\lambda)=2\Im\frac{A^{\prime}-iB^{\prime}}{A-iB}=2\frac{A^{\prime}(\lambda)B(\lambda)-A(\lambda)B^{\prime}(\lambda)}{A^{2}(\lambda)+B^{2}(\lambda)},

which proves

Lemma 18
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{I}}
is closed on the right. Let
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
,
\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{n},n\geq 1\}
be Dirac operators on
{\mathcal{I}}
with common left boundary condition
\mathfrak{u}_{0}

satisfying Assumptions 1 and 2. Assume that

\|{\mathtt{r}\,}\tau_{n}-{\mathtt{r}\,}\tau\|_{\textup{HS}}\to 0,\qquad\mathfrak{t}_{\tau_{n}}\to\mathfrak{t}_{\tau},\qquad\int_{{\mathcal{I}}}|a_{0}(s)-a_{0,n}(s)|^{2}ds\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
.
Then in the vague topology of measures,

\displaystyle\mu_{\textup{right},n}\rightarrow\mu_{\textup{right}},\qquad\mu_{\textup{left},n}\rightarrow\mu_{\textup{left}}.

(31)

Proof.

From
{\mathtt{r}\,}\tau_{n}\to{\mathtt{r}\,}\tau
we get
\lambda_{k,n}\to\lambda_{k}
. From the continuity bound of Proposition
12
we get that
H_{n}\to H
uniformly on compacts. This implies that we must have
\mathfrak{u}_{1,n}\to\mathfrak{u}_{1}
. Indeed, assume that there is a subsequence with a different limit
\mathfrak{v}
. Then the zero set of
\mathfrak{u}_{1,n}JH_{n}
(the spectrum of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{n}
) would converge locally on compacts to the

by Hurwitz’s theorem, which would contradict

.

To prove that the right spectral weights converge, we use the formula given by Lemma
17
.
Since

has no real zeros, we can extend the definition of
\alpha
from (

{\mathbb{R}}
. We also get that
\alpha_{n}\to\alpha
uniformly on compacts in some neighborhood of

we get

, which in turn shows

.
∎

We introduce the projection operator

A

\displaystyle T_{r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+r^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}r&1\\
-1&r\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{T}_{r}(z)=\mathcal{P}T_{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}z\\

Biasing by the weight at zero and the Palm measure

In this section we discuss the notion of conditioning a random measure to charge location
x
: “biasing by the weight of
x
”. For translation-invariant random measures, this coincides with the notion of the Palm measure.

Definition 21
.

Given a random measure
\mu
on a metric space
S
, let
\mu_{\varepsilon}
have Radon-Nikodym derivative

\frac{\mu(B_{\varepsilon}(x))}{E\mu(B_{\varepsilon}(x))}

with respect to
\mu
. That is,
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is
\mu
biased by the weight of
B_{\varepsilon}(x)
.
We define
\mu
biased by the weight of
x\in S
as the
\varepsilon\to 0
distributional limit of
\mu_{\varepsilon}
if it exists.

Remark 22
.

If
\mu
is a random probability measure, and
X
is a random pick from
\mu
, then
\mu
biased by the weight of
x
can be interpreted as the posterior distribution of
\mu
given
X=x
in the Bayes sense.

Often a slightly more general version of Definition
21
is needed, where the metric space is
decorated
with a random function
\xi:S\to R
, and arbitrary measure space. The
decoration

\xi
seems unnecessary at first, but is a standard tool in Palm measure theory. It will be useful in defining Palm measures for operators.

Definition 23
.

Let
\mu
be a random measure on a metric space
S
, and let
\xi
be a random measurable function
S\to R
, where
R
is a measure space.
Let
(\mu_{\varepsilon},\xi_{\varepsilon})
have Radon-Nikodym derivative

\frac{\mu(B_{\varepsilon}(x))}{E\mu(B_{\varepsilon}(x))}

with respect to
(\mu,\xi)
. That is,
(\mu_{\varepsilon},\xi_{\varepsilon})
is
(\mu,\xi)
biased by the weight of
B_{\varepsilon}(x)
.
We define
(\mu,\xi)
biased by the weight of
x\in S
as the
\varepsilon\to 0
distributional limit of
(\mu_{\varepsilon},\xi_{\varepsilon})
if it exists.

When
\xi
is constant, it can be ignored and Definition
23
is equivalent to Definition
21
.

Next, we define the Palm measure of a decorated, translation invariant random measure with finite intensity. One could ignore the decoration
\xi
for the first reading, and think of it as almost surely constant. Even in the general case, it is good to think of
\xi
as “coming along for the ride”.

Let
\mu
be a random measure on
\mathbb{R}
, and let
\xi
be a random function from
\mathbb{R}
to some measure space
R
. We assume that
(\mu,\xi)
is translation invariant:

(\mu(\cdot+t),\xi(\cdot+t))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(\mu,\xi),\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

and that for some positive
\rho

E\mu(A)=|A|\rho\qquad\text{for any Borel set $A\subset\mathbb{R}$.}

(34)

The number
\rho
is called the
intensity
of
\mu
.

Definition 24
.

Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}
with positive and finite Lebesgue measure. Let
(\mu_{A},\xi_{A})
be distributed as the biasing of
(\mu,\xi)
by
\mu(A)
, namely for any measurable set
M
we have

P((\mu_{A},\xi_{A})\in M)=\frac{E[\mu(A);\,(\mu,\xi)\in M]}{E\mu(A)}

Given
\mu_{A}
, let
X
be a random point picked from the probability measure
\mu_{A}(A\cap\cdot)/\mu_{A}(A)
, the normalized version of
\mu_{A}
restricted to
A
. Then the
Palm measure

(\mu_{*},\xi_{*})
of
(\mu,\xi)
is defined as the translation
(\mu_{A},\xi_{A})(\cdot-X)
.

When
\xi
is constant, in words, the Palm measure is the re-centering of the
\mu(A)
-biased version
\mu_{A}
of
\mu
at a random point picked from the restriction of
\mu_{A}
to
A
.

Definition
24
can be summarized in terms of bounded test functions
f
as follows:

Ef(\mu_{*},\xi_{*})=\frac{1}{E\mu(A)}E\int_{A}f(\mu(\cdot-x),\xi(\cdot-x))\mu(dx).

which agrees with the classical definition, see
Kallenberg 2002
, Chapter 11, equation (1). There, in Lemma 11.2 it is shown that the definition does not depend on the set
A
.

Proposition 25
.

Let
(\mu,\xi)
be translation-invariant decorated random measure on
\mathbb{R}
, and assume that
E\mu\{[0,1]\}=\rho<\infty
. Then
(\mu,\xi)
biased by the weight of
0
exists and equals the Palm measure of
(\mu,\xi)
.

Proof of Proposition
25
.

By Definition
23
, for each
\varepsilon>0
the Palm measure is given by

\nu=\mu_{\varepsilon}(\cdot-X_{\varepsilon})
where
X_{\varepsilon}
is picked from a probability measure supported on
B_{\varepsilon}(0)
. Equivalently,

\mu_{\varepsilon}=\nu(\cdot+X_{\varepsilon}).

Since
\nu
does not depend on
\varepsilon
, and
X_{\varepsilon}
converges in law to
0
, the pair
(\nu,X_{\varepsilon})
converges in law to
(\nu,0)
. By the continuity theorem,
\mu_{\varepsilon}=\nu(\cdot+X_{\varepsilon})
converges to
\nu(\cdot+0)=\nu
.
∎

Remark 26
.

In Definition
24
we defined the Palm measure for decorated translation invariant random measures on
{\mathbb{R}}
with a finite intensity. The same setup (with the appropriate modifications) works for rotation invariant random measures on the unit circle with a finite expected total weight. In particular, Proposition
25
holds for such random measures.

Let
\mathbb{S}=[0,2\pi)
with the topology of the circle, and let
\{\cdot\}_{2\pi}
be the standard covering map
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{S}
. In other words,
\{x\}_{2\pi}=x-2\pi\lfloor x/(2\pi)\rfloor
, the single element of
[0,2\pi)\cap(x+2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Lemma 27
.

Let
\kappa>0
and
\lambda
be a differentiable random bijection
{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and set
V=\{\lambda^{-1}(0)\}_{2\pi}
. Let
\gamma
be a random variable taking values in a Polish space
\mathcal{S}
.
For
u\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the measure

\displaystyle\Xi_{u}=\kappa\sum_{w\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}+u}\lambda^{\prime}(w)\delta_{\lambda(w)}.

(35)

Let
U
be independent of
(\lambda,\gamma)
, and assume that the law of
\{U\}_{2\pi}
is absolutely continuous with bounded density
f
on
\mathbb{S}
. Moreover, assume that on the support of the law of the random variable
V
the density
f
is positive and continuous on
\mathbb{S}
.

Then
(\Xi_{U},(U,\gamma))
biased by the weight of zero exists and has the law of
(\Xi_{V},(V,\gamma))
biased by
f(V)
. In particular, if
U
is uniform on
\mathbb{S}
or
\lambda(0)=0
a.s., then
(\Xi_{U},(U,\gamma))
biased by the weight of zero has the law of
(\Xi_{V},(V,\gamma))
.

The variable
\gamma
is just coming along for the ride, it is not essential in the proof of the lemma. However, we will use this more general statement in Proposition
28
below.

Proof.

By definition, multiplication by a constant
\kappa
commutes with biasing, so it suffices to show the claim for
\kappa=1
.

Let
A_{\varepsilon}=[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
and
\varepsilon\to 0
. It suffices to show that the joint law of
(\lambda,\gamma,\{U\}_{2\pi})
biased by
\Xi_{U}(A_{\varepsilon})
converges to the law of
(\lambda,\gamma,V)
biased by
f(V)
.
Now let

\varphi
be a bounded continuous test function. We will show that as
\varepsilon\to 0

\frac{1}{E[\Xi_{U}[A_{\varepsilon}]]}{E[\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,\{U\}_{2\pi})\Xi_{U}[A_{\varepsilon}]]}\to\frac{1}{E[f(V)]}E[\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,V)f(V)].

(36)

We have

\displaystyle{E[\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,\{U\}_{2\pi})\Xi_{U}[A_{\varepsilon}]]}

\displaystyle=E[\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,\{U\}_{2\pi})\sum_{w\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}+U}\lambda^{\prime}(w)1_{A_{\varepsilon}}(\lambda(w))]

\displaystyle=E\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,\{u\}_{2\pi})\lambda^{\prime}(u)1_{A_{\varepsilon}}(\lambda(u))f(\{u\}_{2\pi})du.

By a change of variables, this equals

\displaystyle E\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,\{\lambda^{-1}(x)\}_{2\pi})f(\{\lambda^{-1}(x)\}_{2\pi})dx

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon E\left[\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,\{\lambda^{-1}(X_{\varepsilon})\}_{2\pi})f(\{\lambda^{-1}(X_{\varepsilon})\}_{2\pi})\right]

where
X_{\varepsilon}
is uniform on
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
and independent of
\lambda
.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
we have
\{\lambda^{-1}(X_{\varepsilon})\}_{2\pi}\to V
in probability. By the bounded convergence theorem and the assumptions on

\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}E[\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,\{U\}_{2\pi})\Xi_{U}[A_{\varepsilon}]]\to E\left[\varphi(\lambda,\gamma,V)f(V)\right].

This convergence (together with the case

) now
implies (
36
), and the statement of the lemma follows.

4
Finite support measures on the unit circle

In
Valkó and Virág 2017
it was shown that one can associate a Dirac operator to a finitely supported probability measure on the unit circle. The construction relies on the theory of orthogonal polynomials
Simon 2005a
, see
Simon 2005b
for a shorter summary.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure whose support is exactly
n
points
e^{i\lambda_{j}},1\leq j\leq n
on the unit circle
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
. Denote the Gram-Schmidt ortogonalization of the polynomials
1,z,\cdots,z^{n}
with respect to
\nu
by
\Phi_{k},k=0,\dots,n
. These are the orthogonal polynomials with respect to
\nu
, with
\Phi_{n}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-e^{i\lambda_{j}})
. Together with the reversed polynomials
\Phi_{k}^{*}(z)=z^{k}\overline{\Phi_{k}(1/\bar{z})}
, they satisfy the famous Szegő recursion (see e.g. Section 1.5, volume 1 of
Simon 2005a
):

\displaystyle\binom{\Phi_{k+1}}{\Phi_{k+1}^{*}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\\
-\alpha_{k}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}z&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\binom{\Phi_{k}}{\Phi_{k}^{*}},\qquad\binom{\Phi_{0}}{\Phi_{0}^{*}}=\binom{1}{1},\qquad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

(37)

Here
\alpha_{k}
,
0\leq k\leq n-1
are the Verblunsky coefficients, they satisfy
|\alpha_{k}|<1
for
0\leq k\leq n-2
and
|\alpha_{n-1}|=1
. The map between the probability measures supported on
n
points on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
and the Verblunsky coefficients
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
is invertible, and both the map and its inverse are continuous.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}(z):=\frac{z-i}{z+i}
the Cayley transform mapping
\overline{{\mathbb{H}}}
to
\overline{{\mathbb{U}}}
, and introduce its matrix version

U=\begin{pmatrix}1&-i\\
1&i\end{pmatrix}.

(38)

The following definition constructs a path and a Dirac operator from a probability measure supported on finitely many points on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
.

Definition 30
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure supported on
n
distinct points on the unit circle, and let
\alpha_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
be its Verblunsky coefficients. Define
b_{k},
,
0\leq k\leq n
as

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=0,\quad b_{k}=\mathcal{P}\begin{pmatrix}1&\bar{\alpha}_{0}\\
\alpha_{0}&1\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}1&\bar{\alpha}_{k-1}\\
\alpha_{k-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\binom{0}{1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq n,

(39)

and
z_{k}=\mathcal{U}^{-1}(b_{k})
,
0\leq k\leq n
.

Let
z(t)=z_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}
,
\mathfrak{u}_{0}=[1,0]^{t}
and
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[-z_{n},-1]
if
z_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[1,0]
if
z_{n}=\infty
. We call
z_{0},\dots,z_{n}
the
path parameter
of
\nu
in
{\mathbb{H}}
, and
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(z(\cdot),\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
the
Dirac operator corresponding to
\nu
. We call
b_{0},\dots,b_{n}
the path parameter of
\nu
in
{\mathbb{U}}
.

The following result is proved in Proposition 16 of
Valkó and Virág 2017
.

Proposition 31
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure supported on
n
distinct points
e^{i\lambda_{j}},1\leq j\leq n
on the unit circle
\{|z|=1\}
. Let
z_{k},0\leq k\leq n
be the path parameter of
\nu
in
{\mathbb{H}}
introduced in Definition
30
, and let
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=\mathtt{Dir}(z(\cdot),\mathfrak{u}_{0},\mathfrak{u}_{1})
be the Dirac operator corresponding to
\nu

Then
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
satisfies Assumptions 1 and 2 on
{\mathcal{I}}=[0,1]
, and also Assumption 3 if
z_{n}\neq\infty
. Moreover, the spectrum of
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is the set
\{n\lambda_{j}+2\pi nk:1\leq j\leq n,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. The spectrum contains 0 if and only if
z_{n}=\infty
.

The path parameter of a probability measure supported on
n
distinct points can be expressed in a more convenient way using the
modified
Verblunsky coefficients
\gamma_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
. These are defined
recursively in terms of the Verblunsky coefficients as follows:

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=\bar{\alpha}_{0},\qquad\gamma_{k}=\bar{\alpha}_{k}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{1-\bar{\gamma}_{j}}{1-\gamma_{j}},\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(40)

By definition we have
|\gamma_{k}|=|\alpha_{k}|
for all
0\leq k\leq n-1
, in particular
|\gamma_{n-1}|=1
. The recursion (
40
) shows that the map between the regular and modified Verblunsky coefficients is invertible.

The modified Verblunsky coefficients are connected to the affine group of isometries of the hyperbolic plane fixing a particular boundary point. In the half plane representation
{\mathbb{H}}
we choose the boundary point to be
\infty
: in that case the transformations are of the form
z\to\frac{1}{y}(z-x)
with
x+iy\in{\mathbb{H}}
. The matrix representation and the transformation corresponding to a
x+iy\in{\mathbb{H}}
is given by

A_{x+iy,{\mathbb{H}}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-x\\
0&y\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{x+iy,{\mathbb{H}}}(z)=\mathcal{P}A_{x+iy,{\mathbb{H}}}\binom{z}{1}.

(41)

Note that
x+iy
is the pre-image of
i
under
\mathcal{A}_{x+iy,{\mathbb{H}}}
.

In the unit disk
{\mathbb{U}}
representation of the hyperbolic plane we choose the fixed point to be
1
, in this case the corresponding linear fractional transformations can be parameterized by the pre-image
\gamma\in{\mathbb{U}}
of
0
.
The matrix representation and the linear fractional transformation corresponding to
\gamma\in{\mathbb{U}}
are given by

A_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{1-\gamma}&\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\\
\frac{\bar{\gamma}}{\bar{\gamma}-1}&\frac{1}{1-\bar{\gamma}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}(z)=\mathcal{P}A_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}\binom{z}{1}.

(42)

The half-plane and disk representations can be connected via the Cayley transform
\mathcal{U}
and the corresponding matrix
U
:

\displaystyle A_{U^{-1}(\gamma),{\mathbb{H}}}=U^{-1}A_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}\,U,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{U^{-1}(\gamma),{\mathbb{U}}}=\mathcal{U}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{A}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}\circ\mathcal{U}.

For a particular sequence of modified Verblunsky coefficients

we introduce the variables

,

as

\displaystyle v_{k}+iw_{k}=\mathcal{U}^{-1}(\gamma_{k})-i.

(43)

Note that
v_{k},w_{k}
are finite for

with
w_{k}>-1
. We have
w_{n-1}=-1
, with
v_{n-1}=\infty
for
\gamma_{n-1}=1
, and
v_{n-1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
otherwise. A direct computation gives

\displaystyle w_{k}=2\Re\frac{\gamma_{k}}{1-\gamma_{k}},\quad v_{k}=-2\Im\frac{\gamma_{k}}{1-\gamma_{k}},\quad\text{if $\gamma_{k}\neq 1$.}

(44)

The following lemma shows that the path parameter can be expressed in a simple way in terms of the modified Verblunsky coefficients.

with

Using this identity with (

If

\displaystyle b_{k}=\mathcal{P}\begin{pmatrix}1&-\bar{\alpha}_{0}\\

Since

and

equation (

\displaystyle A_{z_{k},{\mathbb{H}}}=A_{v_{k-1}+i(w_{k-1}+1),{\mathbb{H}}}\,A_{z_{k-1},{\mathbb{H}}}.

From (

The following statement extends the results of Proposition

Proposition 33
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure supported on
n
distinct points
e^{i\lambda_{j}},1\leq j\leq n
on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
, and let
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
be the Dirac operator corresponding to

as defined in Definition

Then

\displaystyle\mu_{\textup{left},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(n\lambda_{j}+2nk\pi)=2n\,\nu(e^{i\lambda_{j}}),\qquad k\in{\mathbb{Z}},1\leq j\leq n.

(51)

Proof.

For
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
let
H(t,\lambda)
be the solution of the eigenfunction equation

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}H=\lambda H,\qquad H(0,\lambda)=\mathfrak{u}_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\

Denote by

the path parameter of

in
{\mathbb{H}}

From Definition

it follows that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=R^{-1}J\frac{d}{dt}
where

\displaystyle R(t)=R_{k}=\frac{X_{k}^{t}X_{k}}{2\det X_{k}},\qquad\text{for $t\in[k/n,(k+1)/n)$}.

(54)

Introduce the normalized orthogonal polynomials

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(z)=\Phi_{k}(z)/\Phi_{k}(1),\quad\Psi_{k}^{*}(z)=\Phi_{k}^{*}(z)/\Phi_{k}^{*}(1).

(55)

From Proposition 32 of
Valkó and Virág 2020
it follows that
the function
H(t,\lambda)
satisfies the identities

\displaystyle H(\tfrac{k}{n},\lambda)

\displaystyle=H_{k}(\lambda)=e^{-\frac{i\lambda k}{2n}}X_{k}^{-1}U^{-1}\binom{\Psi_{k}(e^{i\lambda/n})}{\Psi_{k}^{*}(e^{i\lambda/n})},\qquad 0\leq k\leq n-1,

(56)

\displaystyle H(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=X_{k}^{-1}U^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{i\lambda}{2}(t-k/n))}&0\\
0&e^{\frac{-i\lambda}{2}(t-k/n)}\end{pmatrix}UX_{k}\,H_{k}(\lambda),\qquad t\in(k/n,(k+1)/n].

From (
54
), (
56
) and (
57
) we obtain

\|H(\cdot,\lambda)\|_{R}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{k}(\lambda)^{t}R_{k}H_{k}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2\Im z_{k}}|\Psi_{k}(e^{i\lambda/n})|^{2},

where in the last step we also used that
|\Phi_{k}(e^{i\lambda/n})|=|\Phi_{k}^{*}(e^{i\lambda/n})|
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Setting
\tilde{\lambda}=n\lambda_{j}+2nk\pi
for a

,

we get

\displaystyle\|H(\cdot,\tilde{\lambda})\|_{R}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2\Im z_{k}}|\Psi_{k}(e^{i\lambda_{j}})|^{2},

(58)

and since
|H(0,\tilde{\lambda})|=|\binom{1}{0}|=1
, this leads to

\displaystyle\mu_{\textup{left},{\boldsymbol{\tau}}}(n\lambda_{j}+2nk\pi)=\frac{2n}{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\Im z_{k}}|\Psi_{k}(e^{i\lambda_{j}})|^{2}}.

(59)

Hence to prove (
51

\displaystyle\nu(e^{i\lambda_{j}})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\Im z_{k}}|\Psi_{k}(e^{i\lambda_{j}})|^{2}.

(60)

Fix
1\leq j\leq n
, and let
g
be the degree

g(e^{i\lambda_{j}})=1
and
g(e^{i\lambda_{\ell}})=0
for

. The polynomials
\Psi_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
are orthogonal with respect to

\int\bar{\Psi}_{k}(z)g(z)d\nu(z)=\bar{\Psi}_{k}(e^{i\lambda_{j}})\nu(e^{i\lambda_{j}})

g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Psi_{k}(z)\frac{\bar{\Psi}_{k}(e^{i\lambda_{j}})\nu(e^{i\lambda_{j}})}{\|\Psi_{k}\|_{\nu}^{2}},

and using

\displaystyle 1=\nu(e^{i\lambda_{j}})\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{|\Psi_{k}(e^{i\lambda_{j}})|^{2}}{\|\Psi_{k}\|^{2}_{\nu}}.

(61)

The needed identity (

\Im z_{k}=\|\Psi_{k}\|_{\nu}^{2}
.

By Section 1.5 of volume 1,
Simon 2005a
we have

\|\Phi_{k}\|_{\nu}^{2}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(1-|\gamma_{\ell}|^{2}),

which together with (

\|\Psi_{k}\|^{2}_{\nu}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac{1-|\gamma_{\ell}|^{2}}{|1-\gamma_{\ell}|^{2}}

∎

6
Aleksandrov measures

Biasing a measure on the unit circle by the weight of one is closely related to the classical notion of an Aleksandrov measure. We review some basic facts about such measures, and refer the reader to
Simon 2005a
volume 1, Section 3.2 and volume 2, Section 10.2 for more background.

Definition 42
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure supported on
n
distinct points on the unit circle. Denote its Verblunsky coefficients by
\alpha_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
. For
|\eta|=1
the probability measure corresponding to the Verblunsky coefficients
\eta\alpha_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
is denoted by
\nu_{\eta}
, and it is called the
Aleksandrov measure
corresponding to
\nu
with Aleksandrov parameter
\eta
.

Lemma 43
.

The following statements hold for the Aleksandrov measure
\nu_{\eta}
.

1.

The path parameter
b_{\eta,0},\dots,b_{\eta,n}
of
\nu_{\eta}
in
{\mathbb{U}}
is given by
\eta^{-1}b_{0},\dots,\eta^{-1}b_{n}
.

2.

\nu_{\eta}(1)>0
if and only if
\eta=b_{n}
.

3.

The measure
\nu_{\eta}
is continuous as a function of
\eta
.

Proof.

We have

\begin{pmatrix}\eta&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&\bar{\alpha}\\
\alpha&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\eta&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\bar{\eta}\bar{\alpha}\\
\eta\alpha&1\end{pmatrix},

hence the first statement follows by (
39
).

By part (b) of Proposition
33
it follows that
\nu_{\eta}(1)>0
exactly if
b_{\eta,n}=1
. This shows that the first statement of our lemma implies the second one.

The third statement follows from the fact that a probability measure supported on
n
distinct points depends continuously on its Verblunsky coefficients (see Theorem 1.5.6 in Volume 1 of
Simon 2005a
).
∎

If one averages the Aleksandrov measure in its parameter then the result is the uniform measure on the unit circle.

Theorem 44

(Theorem 10.2.2, volume 2,
Simon 2005a
)
.

For a finitely supported discrete probability measure
\nu
on the unit circle the averaged measure
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\nu_{e^{i\varphi}}d\varphi
is the uniform measure.

The next theorem ties Aleksandrov measures to conditioning. See Section
3

for the definition of conditioning by the weight of a point, and recall the path parameter
b_{0},\ldots,b_{n}
of a measure
\mu
from Section
4
.

Proposition 45
.

Let
a_{k}
be a sequence of points on the unit circle, let
\mu
be a measure supported on
n
points with
b_{n}
defined in Definition
30
, and let
\mu_{a_{k}}
denote the Aleksandrov measure of
\mu
with parameter
a_{k}
. Then the following are equivalent

1.

a_{k}\to b_{n}

2.

\mu_{a_{k}}\to\mu_{b_{n}}

3.

For every neighborhood
A
of 1,
\mu_{a_{k}}(A)>0
for all large enough
k
.

Proof.

The measure
\mu
is continuous as a function of its Verblunsky parameters, see Theorem 1.5.6 in volume 1,
Simon 2005a
, hence
\mu_{a}
is continuous in
a
. This immediately shows that 1 implies 2. Since
\mu_{b_{n}}(1)>0
by Lemma
43
, 2 implies 3.

Now assume 3, and take a converging subsequence of
a_{k_{m}}\to\eta
. The continuity of
\mu_{x}
in
x
implies that
\mu_{a_{k}}\to\mu_{\eta}
, in particular the support of
\mu_{a_{k}}
converges to the support of
\mu_{\eta}
. If
\eta\neq b_{n}
then the support of
\mu_{\eta}
does not contain 1, and we could find a neighborhood
A
of 1 so that for large enough
k
we have
\mu_{a_{k}}(A)=0
, contradicting 3. This shows that any subsequential limit of
a_{k}
is equal to
b_{n}
, proving 1.
∎

Corollary 46
.

Fix
\mu
supported on
n
points on the unit circle, and let
U
be any random variable so that
b_{n}\in\operatorname{supp}\operatorname{law}U
. Then
\mu_{U}
conditioned on the weight of 1 is a deterministic measure equal to
\mu_{b_{n}}
.

Proof.

For
0<\varepsilon<1
let
A_{\varepsilon}
be the arc of the unit circle corresponding to the angles in
(-\pi\varepsilon,\pi\varepsilon)
.

First we claim that
E\mu_{U}(A_{\varepsilon})>0
for all
\varepsilon
. Let
\varphi
be a continuous function with values in
[0,1]
with
\varphi(1)=1
supported on
A_{\varepsilon}
. If
u\to b_{n}
then
\mu_{u}\to\mu_{b_{n}}
, so the functional
u\mapsto\int\varphi d\mu_{u}
is continuous. Let
B
the set of

so that
\int\varphi d\mu_{u}>\int\varphi d\mu_{b_{n}}/2=:a/2
. Note that
a\geq\mu_{b_{n}}(1)>0
. By continuity,
B
is an open neighborhood of
b_{n}
, so

, and on this event,

\mu_{U}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}})>\int\varphi d\mu_{U}>a/2

so
E\mu_{U}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}})>aP(U\in B)/2>0
.

Let
\mu_{U_{k}}
be distributed as
\mu_{U}
biased by
\mu_{U}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}})
; this exists since
E\mu_{U}(A_{\varepsilon_{k}})>0

The sequence
\mu_{U_{k}}
satisfies Proposition

U_{k}\to b_{n}
and
\mu_{U_{k}}\to\mu_{b_{n}}
in law.
∎

Corollary 47

(Conditioning on a point at 1.)
.

Let
\mu
be a random measure supported on
n
points on the unit circle so that for every
|u|=1
the Aleksandrov measure
\mu_{u}
has the same distribution as
\mu

\mu

\mu_{b_{n}}
.
Moreover, if

Proof.

Let
U
be uniform. Consider the joint distribution

where

be the event that

(-\pi\varepsilon,\pi\varepsilon)

Take a bounded continuous functional

Note that

Thus we see that the law of

7
Verblunski coefficients after biasing by the weight of one

In this section we describe how random Verblunski coefficients change after conditioning on the weight of one. We have already seen that this is related to Aleksandrov measures, but the connection uses the path parameter
b_{k},0\leq k\leq n
, and the dependence can be complicated. In many cases, we still have simple formulas. For this, we again use the connection between the modified Verblunski coefficients and the affine group of isometries of the hyperbolic plane introduced in Section
4
.

Recall that the affine group of linear fractional transformations of the unit disk fixing the point
1
can be parametrized with the pre-image
\gamma
of 0. The linear fractional transformation
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}
can be represented as a
2\times 2
matrix
A_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}
via (
42
).

The inverse of
A_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}
corresponds to a linear fractional transformation whose parameter is denoted by
\gamma^{\iota}
:

\displaystyle\gamma^{\iota}=-\gamma\frac{1-\bar{\gamma}}{1-\gamma},\qquad A_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}^{-1}=A_{\gamma^{\iota},{\mathbb{U}}}.

(70)

Note that
\gamma\to\gamma^{\iota}
is not an analytic function: it does not change the modulus,

|\gamma|=|\gamma^{\iota}|,

but it is not a rotation around 0.
From the definition (
70
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}(\gamma)=0,\quad\mbox{ i.e. },\quad\mathcal{A}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}^{-1}(0)=\mathcal{A}_{\gamma^{\iota}}(0)=\gamma.

(71)

Using the
\gamma\to\gamma^{\iota}
map the connection between the regular and modified Verblunski coefficients (
40
) simplifies to

\gamma_{0}=\bar{\alpha}_{0},\qquad-\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\gamma_{k-1}^{\iota}}=\frac{\bar{\alpha}_{k}}{\bar{\alpha}_{k-1}},\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

For
u\in\partial{\mathbb{U}},\gamma\in{\mathbb{U}}
define the Poisson kernel as

\operatorname{Poi}(\gamma,u)=\Re\frac{u+\gamma}{u-\gamma}=\frac{1-|\gamma|^{2}}{|u-\gamma|^{2}}.

The normalization is so that if
\Theta
is uniform random on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
, then the expected value of
\operatorname{Poi}(\gamma,\Theta)
is 1. We have

\operatorname{Poi}(\gamma,1)=\frac{1-|\gamma|^{2}}{|1-\gamma|^{2}}=\det A_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}=\mathcal{A}_{\gamma,{\mathbb{U}}}^{\prime}(1).

(72)

Our next result shows how the
\gamma\to\gamma^{\iota}
operation shows up naturally when considering the reversed (and rescaled) version of a path corresponding to a discrete probability measure on
\partial{\mathbb{U}}
.

Lemma 48
.

Let
\nu
be a probability measure supported on
n
distinct points on the unit circle. Denote the modified Verblunski coefficients of
\nu
by
\gamma_{k},0\leq k\leq n-1
, and consider the path
b_{k},0\leq k\leq n
defined by (
39
) in Definition
30
.
For
0\leq k\leq n-1
define the reversed path parameter as

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{k}={\mathcal{A}}_{b_{n-1},{\mathbb{U}}}(b_{n-k-1})\qquad 0\leq k\leq n-1.

(73)

Then
b_{0}^{\prime},\dots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}
are the first
n
elements of the path produced by a sequence of modified Verblunsky coefficients starting with
\gamma^{\iota}_{n-2},\ldots,\gamma^{\iota}_{0}
.

Proof.

From (
49
) we have

{\mathcal{A}}_{b_{k},{\mathbb{U}}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{k-1},{\mathbb{U}}}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{0},{\mathbb{U}}}.

Hence, by (
47
) we get

\displaystyle b_{k}^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{n-2}}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{0}}({\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{0}}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{n-k-2}}^{-1}(0))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{n-2}}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{n-k-1}}(0)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{n-2}^{\iota}}^{-1}\circ\cdots\circ{\mathcal{A}}_{\gamma_{n-k-1}^{\iota}}^{-1}(0).

Equation (
47
) now shows that
b_{0}^{\prime},\dots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}
are the first
n
elements of the path produced by a sequence of modified Verblunsky coefficients starting with
\gamma^{\iota}_{n-2},\ldots,\gamma^{\iota}_{0}
.
∎

The following lemma describes the distribution of
\alpha^{\iota}
if
\alpha
has rotationally invariant distribution on
{\mathbb{U}}
.

Lemma 49
.

Assume that
\alpha\in{\mathbb{U}}
is random with a rotationally invariant distribution. Then the law of
\alpha^{\iota}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the law of
\alpha
with density
\operatorname{Poi}(\alpha,1)
.

Proof.

By first conditioning on
|\alpha|=r
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\alpha=r\eta
where

is deterministic, and
\eta
is uniform on the unit circle. (Note that
\alpha=0
exactly if
\alpha^{\iota}=0
, and
\operatorname{Poi}(0,1)=1

Then

\alpha^{\iota}=-\alpha\frac{1-\bar{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}=-r\eta\frac{1-r\eta^{-1}}{1-r\eta}=rg_{r}(\eta),\qquad g_{r}(u)=\frac{u-r}{ru-1}.

Since
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\iota}
is an involution, we have
g_{r}^{-1}=g_{r}
. By the change of variables formula, the density of

at

is given by

|(g_{r}^{-1})^{\prime}(u)|=|g_{r}^{\prime}(u)|=\left|\frac{r^{2}-1}{(1-ru)^{2}}\right|=\operatorname{Poi}(ru,1).\qed

If the modified Verblunsky coefficients of a finite discrete probability measure on

\displaystyle\operatorname{Poi}(b_{n-1},1)=\det A_{b_{n-1},{\mathbb{U}}}=\det(A_{\gamma_{n-2},{\mathbb{U}}}\cdots A_{\gamma_{0},{\mathbb{U}}})=\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}\det A_{\gamma_{i},{\mathbb{U}}}=\prod_{i=0}^{m-2}\operatorname{Poi}(\gamma_{i},1),

which shows the equivalence of the distributions in (2) and (3).

The last claim follows from Proposition

Enhanced Schmidt number criteria based on correlation trace norms
Armin Tavakoli, Simon Morelli

Abstract
The Schmidt number represents the genuine entanglement dimension of a bipartite quantum state. We derive simple criteria for the Schmidt number of a density matrix in arbitrary local dimensions, given that certain symmetric measurements exist. They are based on the trace norm of correlations obtained from seminal families of quantum measurements, specifically symmetric informationally complete measurements and mutually unbiased bases. Our criteria are strictly stronger than both the well-known fidelity witness criterion and the computable cross-norms or realignment criterion.



I
Introduction

Entanglement is a paradigmatic resource for quantum information science and technology as well as a fundamental feature of quantum theory. To understand the quantum relation between a pair of particles, it is a natural endeavour to not only ask whether they are entangled but also consider how many of their degrees of freedom genuinely exhibit entanglement. Indeed, if two particles, with one hundred local levels each, are shown to be entangled, we still have no information on how many of these levels are involved in generating the entanglement. This leads to the notion of a genuine entanglement dimension, which is captured by the Schmidt number of a bipartite density matrix. In this more general picture, standard separability corresponds to the special case in which the Schmidt number is just one.

However, deciding whether a given density matrix is entangled is an NP-hard problem
Gurvits 2004
;
Gharibian 2010
, and characterising the Schmidt number appears even harder. Therefore, it is important to derive sufficient conditions for bounding the Schmidt number. Ideally, such a condition should detect many interesting types of entanglement, be valid for any quantum state in arbitrary and unequal local dimensions, and be easy to compute. Examples of Schmidt number criteria in this spirit are known, for instance by examining the fidelity between the density matrix and the maximally entangled
d
-dimensional state
Terhal and Horodecki 2000
. A different approach is based on generalising both the well-known positive partial-transpose entanglement criterion
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
and the computable cross-norm or realignment criterion (CCNR)
Chen and Wu 2003
;
Rudolph 2005
to apply also for Schmidt numbers
Hulpke et al. 2004
;
Johnston 2014
. A similar criteria based on the Bloch decomposition is known
Klöckl and Huber 2015
. Recently, also criteria based on covariance matrices have been developed
Liu et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2024
.

In this work, we derive two simple and general criteria for the Schmidt number. They are each based on analysing the correlations obtained from locally performing certain quantum measurements. These measurements correspond to well-known families, namely symmetric informationally complete (SIC) measurements and mutually unbiased bases (MUBs). The former corresponds to a maximal set of projections that have identical modulus overlaps
Zauner 1999
;
Renes et al. 2004
and the latter corresponds to a maximal set of bases such that any element of any basis when measured in any other basis has uniformly distributed outcomes
Wootters and Fields 1989
. Both SICs and MUBs have a broad role in quantum information theory. In the specific context of entanglement, they have been used for entanglement witnessing
Spengler et al. 2012
;
Shang et al. 2018
;
Bae et al. 2019
;
Li and Chen 2021
as well as semi-device-independent
Bakhshinezhad et al. 2024
and fully device independent entanglement certification
Tavakoli et al. 2021
. They can also be used to witness Schmidt numbers via fidelity estimation
Fickler et al. 2014
;
Morelli et al. 2023
.

Our Schmidt number criteria are different; they are based on analysing the trace norm of the correlations obtained when both particles are exposed to either a SIC measurement or MUB measurements. A norm criterion for entanglement detection with SICs was introduced in
Shang et al. 2018
and with MUBs in
Siudzińska 2022
, our results can be seen as a generalisation of those to Schmidt number criteria. Importantly, we prove that both our criteria are strictly stronger than both the fidelity criterion and the CCNR criterion for Schmidt numbers. We further show that in dimensions where both SICs and complete sets of MUBs exists, our two criteria may be closely related. Finally, we show evidence in support of our criteria admitting resource-efficient generalisations, in which fewer measurements are used to detect the Schmidt number. Deciding these conjectures is left as an open problem.

II
Preliminaries

We now introduce the necessary preliminaries for the later presentation of our results.

II.1
Schmidt number

Consider a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
with subsystem dimensions
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
respectively. The state is said to be entangled if it does not admit a separable decomposition

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}\otimes\varphi_{\lambda},

(1)

where
\{p_{\lambda}\}
is a probability distribution, and
\phi_{\lambda}
and
\varphi_{\lambda}
are arbitrary pure states on system
A
and
B
respectively. A generalisation of separability is obtained by considering the set of states that have a specific Schmidt number. Specifically, the Schmidt number of
\rho_{AB}
is the smallest positive integer
r
for which it is possible to generate the state by stochastically preparing bipartite pure states that are, up to local unitary transformations, confined to an at most
r
-dimensional local Hilbert space. The latter is equivalent to the Schmidt rank of the pure state, written
\text{SR}(\psi)
, being no more than
r
. The Schmidt rank of a pure state denotes the number of terms appearing in the Schmidt decomposition
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{i=1}^{\text{SR}(\psi)}\lambda_{i}\ket{\alpha_i,\beta_i}
, where
\{\ket{\alpha_i}\}_{i}
and
\{\ket{\beta_i}\}_{i}
are, respectively, orthonormal states and
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i}
satisfy
\lambda_{i}>0
and
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}=1
.
Thus, we may write the Schmidt number as

\displaystyle r(\rho_{AB})\equiv\min_{\{p_{\lambda}\},\{\psi_{\lambda}\}}\Big\{

\displaystyle r_{\text{max}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\quad\rho_{AB}=\sum_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}\outerproduct{\psi_\lambda}{\psi_\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad r_{\text{max}}=\max_{\lambda}\text{SR}(\psi_{\lambda})\Big\}.

(2)

Notice that the set of states with Schmidt number
r
is convex and that in the case of
r=1
Eq. (
2
) reduces to Eq. (
1
).

Let us now discuss two well-known Schmidt number criteria. Consider that we want to determine the Schmidt number of
\rho_{AB}
when
d_{A}=d_{B}\equiv d
. A sufficient criterion is that the fidelity between
\rho_{AB}
and the maximally entangled state violates the following inequality
Terhal and Horodecki 2000

F(\rho)=\max_{\psi_{\text{max}}}\hskip 5.69054pt\langle\psi_{\text{max}}|\rho_{AB}|\psi_{\text{max}}\rangle\leq\frac{r}{d},

(3)

where
\ket{\psi_\text{max}}
is any maximally entangled state, i.e. any pure state with maximally mixed reductions.

A second criterion is the CCNR criterion for Schmidt numbers
Johnston 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2023
.
Let
\{\gamma^{A}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d_{A}^{2}}
and
\{\gamma^{B}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d_{B}^{2}}
be two orthonormal bases of Hermitian matrices in dimensions
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
respectively, i.e.
\tr\left(\gamma_{j}^{A}\gamma_{k}^{A}\right)=\tr\left(\gamma_{j}^{B}\gamma_{k}^{B}\right)=\delta_{j,k}
. Then every state
\rho_{AB}
can be expanded as

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i,j}\tr(\rho_{AB} \gamma_i^A \otimes\gamma_j^B)\gamma_{i}^{A}\otimes\gamma_{j}^{B}

(4)

and we denote the matrix encoding the coefficients as
[\mathbf{C}]_{ij}=\tr(\rho_{AB} \gamma_i^A \otimes\gamma_j^B)
.
The CCNR criterion stipulates that

\|\mathbf{C}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq r,

(5)

for all states with Schmidt number at most
r
, where the trace-norm, also known as the one-norm, is defined as
\|A\|_{\text{tr}}=\tr\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}
and corresponds to the sum of all singular values of
A
.

II.2
SICs and MUBs

In a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space, a SIC-POVM (or SIC measurement) is defined as a set of positive operators
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d^{2}}
such that
E_{i}=\frac{1}{d}\outerproduct{\psi_i}{\psi_i}
and

\left|\innerproduct{\psi_j}{\psi_k}\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{d+1}\quad\forall j\neq k.

(6)

The overlap constant is determined indirectly by the completeness condition of the measurement. SIC-POVMs are known to exist in every dimension up to well-above one hundred
Scott and Grassl 2010
;
Scott 2017
and are conjectured to exist in every dimension.

A pair of bases of
d
-dimensional Hilbert space are called unbiased if the modulus overlap between any pair of their respective elements is identical. By extension, a set of
m
bases is called mutually unbiased if every pair of bases are unbiased with respect to each other. Thus, if for each
z=1,\ldots,m
we have a basis
\{\ket{g_a^z}\}_{a=1}^{d}
, they are mutually unbiased if and only if

\left|\innerproduct{g_a^z}{g_{a'}^{z'}}\right|^{2}=\begin{cases}\delta_{a,a^{\prime}}&\text{if }z=z^{\prime}\\
\frac{1}{d}&\text{if }z\neq z^{\prime}\end{cases}.

(7)

For any
d
there exists at least
m=3
MUBs and at most
m=d+1
MUBs. If the upper bound is saturated, the set of MUBs is called a complete set and it is also tomographically complete. Complete sets of MUBs are known to exist in all prime power dimensions
Wootters and Fields 1989
.

III
Results

III.1
SIC criterion

Consider that each subsystem of
\rho_{AB}
is measured with some SIC-POVM,
\{E_{a}^{A}\}_{a=1}^{d_{A}^{2}}
and
\{E_{b}^{B}\}_{b=1}^{d_{B}^{2}}
respectively.
The resulting outcome statistics is given by Born’s rule,

P^{\text{SIC}}_{ab}=\tr\left(\rho_{AB}E^{A}_{a}\otimes E^{B}_{b}\right).

(8)

We write
\mathbf{P}
for the
d_{A}^{2}\times d_{B}^{2}
matrix whose entries are
P^{\text{SIC}}_{ab}
. We will now consider the trace norm of
\mathbf{P}
. Our first result shows that this norm acts as a Schmidt number criterion.

Result 1

(SIC criterion)
.

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
of local dimensions
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
and of Schmidt number at most
r
, it holds that

\|\mathbf{P}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq\frac{1+r}{K},

(9)

where we define a constant depending on the local dimensions
K=\sqrt{d_{A}(d_{A}+1)}\sqrt{d_{B}(d_{B}+1)}.

Proof.

The index of coincidence for a POVM
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and state
\sigma
is defined as
I(\sigma)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\tr(E_i \sigma)|^{2}
. In the case of
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
being a SIC-POVM, Rastegin derived a bound
Rastegin 2023
on
I
in terms of the purity of
\sigma
. In Appendix
A
we extend the result to the case where
\sigma
can be any linear operator. The resulting bound is

I(\sigma)\leq\frac{\tr\left(\sigma\right)^{2}+\tr\left(\sigma^{\dagger}\sigma\right)}{d(d+1)}.

(10)

We will use this as a lemma. Since the trace norm is convex we can w.l.o.g. restrict our analysis of states with Schmidt number
r
to pure states with Schmidt rank
r
. Also, due to the freedom of choosing local bases, we can w.l.o.g. write the state as
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}\ket{ss}
, where
\{\lambda_{s}\}
is the set of Schmidt coefficients. The probabilities are then given by

\displaystyle P_{ab}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}\langle ss|E^{A}_{a}\otimes E^{B}_{b}|tt\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}^{2}D_{s}^{a,b}+\sum_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}O_{s,t}^{a,b},

(11)

where we have defined
D_{s}^{a,b}=\langle ss|E^{A}_{a}\otimes E^{B}_{b}|ss\rangle
and
O_{s,t}^{a,b}=\langle ss|E^{A}_{a}\otimes E^{B}_{b}|tt\rangle
. Consider now the trace norm of the matrix
D_{s}=\sum_{a,b}D_{s}^{a,b}\outerproduct{a}{b}
and use that
D_{s}=\outerproduct{\alpha_s}{\beta_s}
where
\ket{\alpha_s}=\sum_{a}\langle s|E^{A}_{a}|s\rangle\ket{a}
and
\ket{\beta_s}=\sum_{b}\langle s|E^{B}_{b}|s\rangle\ket{b}
. A direct calculation gives

\|D_{s}\|_{\text{tr}}=\sqrt{\innerproduct{\alpha_s}{\alpha_s}}\sqrt{\innerproduct{\beta_s}{\beta_s}}=\\
\sqrt{I_{A}(\outerproduct{s}{s})}\sqrt{I_{B}(\outerproduct{s}{s})}\leq\frac{2}{K},

(12)

where we have first used that
\innerproduct{\alpha_r}{\alpha_r}
and
\innerproduct{\beta_r}{\beta_r}
each yield the index of coincidence for
\{E_{a}^{A}\}
and
\{E^{B}_{b}\}
, and then used the bound (
10
) for each factor separately. Following the same steps, we can also bound the trace norm of the matrix
O_{s,t}=\sum_{a,b}O_{s,t}^{a,b}\outerproduct{a}{b}
as

\|O_{s,t}\|_{\text{tr}}=\sqrt{I_{A}(\outerproduct{s}{t})}\sqrt{I_{B}(\outerproduct{s}{t})}\leq\frac{1}{K},

(13)

where we have again used Eq. (
10
) but now for the linear operator
\sigma=\outerproduct{s}{t}
.

Working from Eq. (
11
), we now obtain our final result

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}^{2}\|D_{s}\|_{\text{tr}}+\sum_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}\|O_{s,t}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{K}\left(2\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}^{2}+\sum_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\left(1+\bigg(\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}\bigg)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+r}{K}.

In the first line we have used the triangle inequality. In the second line we have used the norm bounds (
12
) and (
13
). In the third line have we used normalisation
\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}^{2}=1
and that
\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}^{2}+\sum_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}=\sum_{s,t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}=\left(\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}\right)^{2}
. In the fourth line we have used that Schmidt rank
r
implies
\left(\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}\right)^{2}\leq r

Terhal and Horodecki 2000
.
∎

Note that in the special case of standard separability, namely when
r=1
, the criterion (
9
) reduces to that introduced in Ref.
Shang et al. 2018
. It was shown in
Shang et al. 2018
that
\|\mathbf{P}\|_{\text{tr}}
is invariant under the specific choice of local SIC-POVMs.
To see this, let
\{E_{i}^{A/B}\}_{i}
and
\{\tilde{E}_{i}^{A/B}\}_{i}
be two arbitrary normalized SIC POVMs, such that

\displaystyle[\mathbf{P}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\rho_{AB}E^{A}_{i}\otimes E^{B}_{j}\right)

(15)

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\tilde{P}}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\rho_{AB}\tilde{E}^{A}_{i}\otimes\tilde{E}^{B}_{j}\right).

(16)

We can now write
\tilde{E}_{i}^{A/B}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d^{2}}O^{A/B}_{ij}E^{A/B}_{j}
where
O^{A/B}
are orthogonal matrices, as the angles between SICs remain unchanged.
More precisely, consider the space of Hermitian
d_{A/B}\times d_{A/B}
matrices spanned by the set
\{E_{i}^{A/B}\}_{i}
with the usual inner product given by the trace of the product of two matrices. Both
\{E_{i}^{A/B}\}_{i}
and
\{\tilde{E}_{i}^{A/B}\}_{i}
span the same space and are bases thereof. By definition, the inner product
\tr(E_i^{A/B}E_j^{A/B})=\tr(\Tilde{E}_i^{A/B}\Tilde{E}_j^{A/B})
remains invariant under the transformation
O^{A/B}
and therefore the transformation is described by an orthogonal matrix.
It then holds that

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\tilde{P}}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{d^{2}}O^{A}_{ik}O^{B}_{jl}[\mathbf{P}]_{kl},

(17)

or, alternatively,
\mathbf{\tilde{P}}=O^{A}\mathbf{P}(O^{B})^{T}
, which implies
\|\mathbf{\tilde{P}}\|_{\text{tr}}=\|\mathbf{P}\|_{\text{tr}}
.

III.2
MUB criterion

Consider that subsystem
A
of
\rho_{AB}
is measured in the
l
’th basis selected from a complete set of MUBs,
\{\ket{g_a^l}\}_{a}
. Similarly, subsystem
B
is measured in the
k
’th basis of another complete set of MUBs
\{\ket{h_b^k}\}_{b}
. The statistics is given by Born’s rule

Q^{\text{MUB}}_{al,bk}=\langle g_{a}^{l},h_{b}^{k}|\rho_{AB}|g_{a}^{l},h_{b}^{k}\rangle.

(18)

We associate this
d_{A}(d_{A}+1)\times d_{B}(d_{B}+1)
table of probabilities to the matrix
\mathbf{Q}=\sum_{a,l,b,k}Q^{\text{MUB}}_{al,bk}\outerproduct{a,l}{b,k}
. Our second main results is a Schmidt number criterion based on the trace norm of this data table.

Result 2

(MUB criterion)
.

For any bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
of local dimensions
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
and of Schmidt number at most
r
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{Q}\right\|_{tr}\leq 1+r.

(19)

Proof.

For an arbitrary positive semidefinite operator
\sigma
, it follows from the uncertainty relations derived in
Larsen 1990
;
Ivanovic 1992
;
Wu et al. 2009
that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{d+1}\sum\limits_{a=0}^{d-1}|\tr\left(G_{a}^{l}\sigma\right)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tr(\sigma^\dagger\sigma)+\tr(\sigma)^{2},

(20)

for
G_{a}^{l}=\outerproduct{g_a^l}{g_a^l}
(and similarly
H_{b}^{k}=\outerproduct{h_b^k}{h_b^k}
).
We will use this as a lemma.

By convexity of the trace norm, we need only to consider pure states of Schmidt rank
r
. Without loss of generality, we can represent the state in the computational basis,
|\psi\rangle=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}|s,s\rangle
. We can then write

\left[\mathbf{Q}\right]_{al,bk}=\sum\limits_{s,t=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}\tr\left(\outerproduct{t}{s}G_{a}^{l}\right)\tr\left(\outerproduct{t}{s}H_{b}^{k}\right).

(21)

Define
[D_{s}]_{al,bk}=\tr\left(\outerproduct{s}{s}G_{a}^{l}\right)\tr\left(\outerproduct{s}{s}H_{b}^{k}\right)
and
[O_{s,t}]_{al,bk}=\tr\left(\outerproduct{t}{s}G_{a}^{l}\right)\tr\left(\outerproduct{t}{s}H_{b}^{k}\right)
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\|D_{s}\|_{\text{tr}}=\sqrt{\sum_{l=1}^{d+1}\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}\langle s|G_{a}^{l}|s\rangle^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}\langle s|H_{b}^{k}|s\rangle^{2}}

\displaystyle\|O_{s,t}\|_{\text{tr}}=\sqrt{\sum_{l=1}^{d+1}\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}|\langle s|G_{a}^{l}|t\rangle|^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}|\langle s|H_{b}^{k}|t\rangle|^{2}}.

Applying Eq. (
20
) to each factor above, we obtain that
\|D_{s}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq 2
and
\|O_{s,t}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq 1
. Using these bounds and basic properties of Schmidt coefficients, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{Q}\right\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}^{2}\|D_{s}\|_{\text{tr}}+\sum\limits_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}\|O_{s,t}\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum\limits_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}^{2}+\sum\limits_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}

\displaystyle=1+\left(\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}\right)^{2}\leq 1+r.

(22)

∎

Note that in the special case of standard separability, namely when
r=1
, the criterion (
19
) reduces to that introduced in Ref.
Siudzińska 2022
.
Similar to the SIC case, also the quantity
\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{\text{tr}}
is invariant under the specific choice of local MUBs. The reasoning follows the same lines, with the only difference that the projectors of the MUBs form a spanning set instead of a basis of the space of Hermitian matrices.
Assume there exist two inequivalent complete sets of MUBs in system
A
and
B
respectively. Let
\{G_{a}^{l}\}_{a,l}
and
\{\tilde{G}_{a}^{l}\}_{a,l}
be the respective basis elements of these two sets in
A
and
\{H_{b}^{k}\}_{b,k}
and
\{\tilde{H}_{b}^{k}\}_{b,k}
the respective basis elements of the two complete sets in
B
. It now holds that

\displaystyle[\mathbf{Q}]_{al,bk}

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\rho_{AB}G^{l}_{a}\otimes H^{k}_{b}\right)

(23)

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\tilde{Q}}]_{al,bk}

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\rho_{AB}\tilde{G}^{l}_{a}\otimes\tilde{H}^{k}_{b}\right).

(24)

Since the set
\{G_{a}^{l}\}_{a,l}
spans the space of Hermitian
d_{A}\times d_{A}
matrices, we can write
\tilde{G}_{a}^{l}=\sum\limits_{a^{\prime},l}O^{A}_{al,a^{\prime}l^{\prime}}G^{l^{\prime}}_{a^{\prime}}
. The same holds for
\{H_{b}^{k}\}_{b,k}
in system
B
,
\tilde{H}_{b}^{k}=\sum\limits_{b^{\prime},k^{\prime}}O^{B}_{bk,b^{\prime}k^{\prime}}H^{k^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}
.
Since the operator
O^{A}
preserves the angles between the spanning sets
\{G_{a}^{l}\}_{a,l}
and
\{\tilde{G}_{a}^{l}\}_{a,l}
,
O^{A}
is an orthogonal matrix. The same holds for
O^{B}
.
It then holds that

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\tilde{Q}}]_{al,bk}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a^{\prime},l^{\prime},b^{\prime},k^{\prime}}O^{A}_{al,a^{\prime}l^{\prime}}O^{B}_{bk,b^{\prime}k^{\prime}}[\mathbf{Q}]_{a^{\prime}l^{\prime},b^{\prime}k^{\prime}},

(25)

or, alternatively,
\mathbf{\tilde{Q}}=O^{A}\mathbf{Q}(O^{B})^{T}
, which implies
\|\mathbf{\tilde{Q}}\|_{\text{tr}}=\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{\text{tr}}
.

III.3
Relation to fidelity criterion and CCNR criterion

In the special case in which the local dimensions are equal, we can compare the criteria in Results
1
and
2
to the fidelity criterion (
3
). We now show that the formers are strictly stronger than the latter.

To this end, let us begin with rederiving the fidelity criterion (
3
) from Result
1
. Note that the maximally entangled state
\ket{\phi^+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{ii}
can be written as
Morelli et al. 2023

\outerproduct{\phi^+}{\phi^+}=(d+1)\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}E_{i}\otimes E_{i}^{*}-\frac{1}{d}\openone,

(26)

where
\{E_{i}\}
is any SIC-POVM. Since any maximally entangled state takes the form
\ket{\psi_\text{max}}=\openone\otimes U\ket{\phi^+}
for some unitary, we can define the pair of SIC-POVMs
E^{A}_{i}=E_{i}
and
E^{B}_{i}=U(E_{i})^{*}U^{\dagger}
and write

\outerproduct{\psi_\text{max}}{\psi_\text{max}}=(d+1)\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}E_{i}^{A}\otimes E_{i}^{B}-\frac{1}{d}\openone.

(27)

For the optimal choice of
\psi_{\text{max}}
, the fidelity becomes

F(\rho)=(d+1)\sum_{i}\tr(\rho E_i^A\otimes E_i^B)-\frac{1}{d}=(d+1)\sum_{i}P_{ii}^{\text{SIC}}-\frac{1}{d}.

(28)

Note that
\sum_{i}P_{ii}^{\text{SIC}}=\tr(\mathbf{P})
. It then holds that

\tr(\mathbf{P})=\sum\limits_{i}\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{P})\leq\sum\limits_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{P})|\leq\sum\limits_{i}\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{P})=\left\|\mathbf{P}\right\|_{\text{tr}},

(29)

where we have used that the sum of the modulus eigenvalues of any square matrix is bounded by the sum of its singular values
\sigma_{i}
. In conclusion,

F(\rho)\leq(d+1)\|\mathbf{P}\|_{\text{tr}}-\frac{1}{d}\leq\frac{r}{d},

(30)

where in the last step we used the Schmidt number bound from Result
1
. Thus we have recovered the fidelity criterion (
3
).

In a similar way, the fidelity criterion can also be recovered from Result
2
. One notes that for any complete set of MUBs
Morelli et al. 2023

\outerproduct{\phi^+}{\phi^+}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{z=1}^{d+1}\sum_{a=1}^{d}G_{a}^{z}\otimes(G_{a}^{z})^{*}-\frac{1}{d}\openone,

(31)

where
G_{a}^{z}=\outerproduct{g_a^z}{g_a^z}
.
Defining
H^{z}_{a}=U(G_{a}^{z})^{*}U^{\dagger}
we write

\outerproduct{\psi_\text{max}}{\psi_\text{max}}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{z=1}^{d+1}\sum_{a=1}^{d}G_{a}^{z}\otimes H_{a}^{z}-\frac{1}{d}\openone.

(32)

For the optimal choice of
\psi_{\text{max}}
, the fidelity becomes

F(\rho)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{a,z}\tr(\rho G_a^z\otimes H_a^z)-\frac{1}{d}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{a,z}Q_{az,az}^{\text{MUB}}-\frac{1}{d}.

(33)

Noting that
\sum_{a,z}Q_{az,az}^{\text{MUB}}=\tr(\mathbf{Q})\leq\left\|\mathbf{Q}\right\|_{\text{tr}}
we
finally find

F(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{d}\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{\text{tr}}-\frac{1}{d}\leq\frac{r}{d},

(34)

where we used the Schmidt number bound from Result
2
and again recover the fidelity criterion (
3
).

When the SIC criterion for entanglement detection was introduced in Ref.
Shang et al. 2018
, it was conjectured to be strictly stronger than the CCNR criterion (
5
).
This conjecture was stated in the form that
\left\|\mathbf{C}\right\|_{\text{tr}}>1
implies
\left\|\mathbf{P}\right\|_{\text{tr}}>2/K
.
This conjecture was later proven correct in Ref.
Jivulescu et al. 2022
, by showing that
\left\|\mathbf{P}\right\|_{\text{tr}}\geq(\left\|\mathbf{C}\right\|_{\text{tr}}+1)/K
.
This result immediately implies that also the SIC criterion for Schmidt number detection is strictly stronger than the CCNR criterion for Schmidt numbers.
To witness Schmidt number
r+1
with the CCNR criterion, one needs to observe
\left\|\mathbf{C}\right\|_{\text{tr}}>r
. This in turn implies
\left\|\mathbf{P}\right\|_{\text{tr}}>(r+1)/K
, meaning that the SIC criterion witnesses the same Schmidt number
r+1
.

In Ref.
Shang et al. 2018
examples of states are given, where entanglement is detected by the SIC criterion, but not by the CCNR or PPT criterion. We now present an example where our criteria outperform both the fidelity criterion and the CCNR criterion for the detection of Schmidt number 3. Define the state
\rho(q)=q\outerproduct{\phi^+}{\phi^+}+(1-q)\ket{0}\bra{1}
in dimension 3. It clearly hods that
F(\rho)=q
and therefore Schmidt number 3 is detected only for
q>2/3
by the fidelity criterion. The CCNR criterion detects Schmidt numer 3 for
q>0.6162
, whereas both our criteria do so already for
q>0.6089
. This is striking, as the improvement in the detection is already visible for a simple family of states in dimension 3. We expect this improvement to be more visible for specifically designed families of states and also increase with the dimension.

III.4
Towards equivalence of the criteria

It is natural to ask whether there is a relation between Result
1
and Result
2
. To study this, we use that Ref.
Beneduci et al. 2013
introduces a way to constructively obtain a SIC-POVM from a complete set of MUBs. This method gives a set of operators that form a SIC-POVM if and only if the operators are positive, which cannot be a priori guaranteed. We will use this result to show the following.

Result 3
.

Assume that a complete set of MUBs exists in both dimensions
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
, and apply to these the construction of Theorem 3 of Ref.
Beneduci et al. 2013
. If this construction results in a valid SIC-POVM, the criteria of Result
1
and
2
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d(d+1)}
be a complete set of MUB projectors and
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d^{2}}
a set of SIC projectors as defined above. Since both of these are spanning sets for the Hermitian
d\times d
operators, there must exist a
d^{2}\times d(d+1)
operator mapping one set to the other.
If it succeeds, the construction of Ref.
Beneduci et al. 2013
explicitely gives such an operator
\mathbf{\Theta}
.
It holds

\displaystyle E_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d(d+1)}\mathbf{\Theta}_{ij}G_{j}=\frac{1}{d\sqrt{d+1}}\sum\limits_{j\in I_{i}}^{d(d+1)}G_{j}+\frac{1-\sqrt{d+1}}{d^{2}}\openone,

(35)

where the
d^{2}
sets
I_{i}
are chosen by taking one element of each MUB in such a way that any two sets share exactly one element. The identity matrix can be written as the sum of the elements of any basis. Splitting the identity evenly as a sum of the
(d+1)
bases, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Theta}=\frac{1}{d\sqrt{d+1}}M+\frac{1-\sqrt{d+1}}{d^{2}(d+1)}C,

(36)

where all entries in
M
are either 0 or 1, such that each row has exactly
d+1
entries 1 and any two rows have exactly one shared entry 1. All entries in
C
are 1.
Using that
MM^{T}=d\openone+C
,
MC^{T}=CM^{T}=(d+1)C
and
CC^{T}=d(d+1)C
, it follows

\mathbf{\Theta}\mathbf{\Theta}^{T}=\openone/(d(d+1))
. Therefore the singular value decomposition is
\mathbf{\Theta}=O_{1}\Delta O_{2}^{T}
with

\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d(d+1)}}&&&0&\ldots&0\\
&\ddots&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d(d+1)}}&0&\ldots&0\end{pmatrix}.

(37)

It now holds that

\displaystyle[\mathbf{P}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[\rho E_{i}^{A}\otimes E_{j}^{B}\right]

(38)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[\rho\ (\sum\limits_{k}\mathbf{\Theta}_{ik}^{A}G_{k}^{A})\otimes(\sum\limits_{l}\mathbf{\Theta}_{jl}^{B}G_{l}^{B})\right]

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,l}\mathbf{\Theta}_{ik}^{A}\mathbf{\Theta}_{jl}^{B}\mathrm{tr}\left[\rho\ G_{k}^{A}\otimes G_{l}^{B}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,l}\mathbf{\Theta}_{ik}^{A}\mathbf{\Theta}_{jl}^{B}[\mathbf{Q}]_{kl}

(41)

and therefore
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{\Theta}^{A}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{\Theta}^{B})^{T}
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{P}\right\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathbf{\Theta}^{A}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{\Theta}^{B})^{T}\right\|_{\text{tr}}=\left\|\Delta^{A}\right\|_{2}\left\|\Delta^{B}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{Q}\right\|_{\text{tr}}

(42)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\left\|\mathbf{Q}\right\|_{\text{tr}},

where
K
is defined as in Eq. (
1
). Equality in the second step comes from the fact that all singular values of
\Delta^{A/B}
are equal and that
\|\mathbf{Q}\|
can have at most
\min(d_{A}^{2},d_{B}^{2})
singular values.
The claim follows immediately.

∎

Based on this result, we put forward the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

Provided that both a SIC and a complete set of MUBs exist in dimension
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
, the criteria in Result
1
and
2
are equivalent. It then holds that
\left\|\mathbf{Q}\right\|_{\text{tr}}=K\left\|\mathbf{P}\right\|_{\text{tr}}
.

Explicit calculations show that this is indeed the case for prime dimensions smaller than 20.
These findings bear the question if the quantity
\left\|\mathbf{Q}\right\|_{\text{tr}}=K\left\|\mathbf{P}\right\|_{\text{tr}}
can be used to define an entanglement monotone. In Appendix
B
we show that this is not the case by explicitely constructing a counterexample.

IV
Examples: depolarising and dephasing noise

We consider two commonly used noise models that naturally occur in many quantum information scenarios, namely when a maximally entangled state is subject to noise of uniform spectral density (depolarisation) and noise causing decoherence in a given basis (dephasing). A depolarisation of the maximally entangled state leads to an isotropic state, which takes the form

\rho_{v}^{\text{iso}}=v\outerproduct{\phi^+_d}{\phi^+_d}+\frac{1-v}{d^{2}}\openone,

(43)

where
\ket{\phi^+_d}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\ket{i,i}
is the maximally entangled state and
v\in[0,1]
is the visibility of the noisy state. It is known that for the isotropic state to have Schmidt number
r+1
the visibility
v
must be greater than the critical value
v_{\text{opt}}=\frac{rd-1}{d^{2}-1}

Terhal and Horodecki 2000
.

In a similar vein, a dephasing in the computational basis of the maximally entangled state leads to the noisy state

\rho_{u}^{\text{deph}}=u\outerproduct{\phi^+_d}{\phi^+_d}+\frac{1-u}{d}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\outerproduct{i,i}{i,i},

(44)

for some visibility
u\in[0,1]
. The critical visibility for the dephased state having Schmidt number
r+1
is known to be
u_{\text{opt}}=\frac{r-1}{d-1}

Bavaresco et al. 2018
.

We shall now see that both the SIC-criterion and the MUB-criterion for high-dimensional entanglement detection can exactly recover these optimal visibility thresholds.
For any value of
v>v_{\text{opt}}
and
u>u_{\text{opt}}
it holds that

\displaystyle F(\rho_{v}^{\text{iso}})=\frac{1+(d^{2}-1)v}{d^{2}}>\frac{r}{d}

(45)

\displaystyle F(\rho_{u}^{\text{deph}})=\frac{1+(d-1)u}{d}>\frac{r}{d}

(46)

and so the fidelity witness is optimal in both cases.
We have already shown that our criteria are strictly stronger than the fidelity witness. In fact it follows immediately from Eqs. (
30
) and (
34
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{dF(\rho)+1}{d(d+1)}>\frac{r+1}{d(d+1)},

(47)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Q}\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle\geq dF(\rho)+1>r+1

(48)

and therefore also the SIC and MUB criterion are optimal.

V
Conjectures

In this section we investigate the case of more general sets of measurements and put forward two conjectures concerning their potential use for Schmidt number detection.

V.1
Equiangular measurements

We now extend Result
1
to more general sets of symmetric measurements that are not necessarily tomographically complete.
Consider a set of
n>d
pure states
\{\ket{\psi_a}\}_{a=1}^{n}
in
d
-dimensional Hilbert space. We call the set an equiangular measurement (EAM) if the following two conditions are satisfied. Firstly, by defining the subnormalised projector
E_{a}=d/n\outerproduct{\psi_a}{\psi_a}
, we can interpret
\{E_{a}\}_{a}
as a quantum measurement. This is equivalent to requiring that the quantum states resolve the identity.

\sum_{a=1}^{n}\outerproduct{\psi_a}{\psi_a}=\frac{n}{d}\openone_{d}.

(49)

Secondly, the quantum states are equiangular, i.e. the modulus overlap of all distinct states (
a\neq a^{\prime}
) is constant;

\left|\innerproduct{\psi_a}{\psi_{a'}}\right|^{2}=\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}.

(50)

In the case of
n=d^{2}
we recover the definition of a SIC-POVM.

In analogy with the approach leading up to Result
1
, we can define the
n\times n
matrix
\|\mathbf{P}_{n}\|_{\text{tr}}
encoding the outcome statistics when measuring with two EAMs
\{E_{a}^{A}\}_{a}^{n}
and
\{E_{b}^{B}\}_{b}^{n}
. Then for any state of Schmidt number
r
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{n}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq\frac{n-d+d(d-1)r}{n(n-1)}.

(51)

In contrast to the full SIC case, the quantity
\|\mathbf{P}_{n}\|_{\text{tr}}
depends on the specific choice of EAM.
The proof of this statement is given in Appendix
C
. Note that we assume here both the dimension and the number of elements in the EAMs to be equal for both subsystems. The proof for the general case of different local dimensions and number of EAM elements works analogously.

The bound in Eq. (
51
) is not tight, except when
n=d^{2}
or
r=1
. For the latter we recover the result proven in
Shi 2024
. We conjecture the following refinement of the statement.

Conjecture 2
.

For any state of Schmidt number
r
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{n}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq\frac{d(d-1)+r(n-d)}{n(n-1)}.

(52)

For
n=d^{2}
or
r=1
this coicides with Eq. (
51
).

V.2
Incomplete sets of MUBs

If only a subset of
m<d+1
MUBs is used, we can define the
md\times md
matrix
\|\mathbf{Q}_{m}\|_{\text{tr}}
encoding the outcome statistics when measuring with
md
local MUB projectors
\{E_{a}^{l}\}_{a,l}
and
\{E_{b}^{k}\}_{b,k}
. Result
2
can immediately be extended to

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Q}_{m}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq\frac{m-1}{d}+r,

(53)

where
\mathbf{Q}_{m}
encodes the probabilities of
m
MUBs. In contrast to the complete MUB case, the quantity
\|\mathbf{Q}_{m}\|_{\text{tr}}
depends on the set of chosen MUBs.
Take for example the state
\ket{\phi}=(\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt{2}
in dimension 3. When measuring in the standard basis and any other unbiased basis of the Wootters-Fields construction
Wootters and Fields 1989
, we obtain
\|\mathbf{Q}_{2}\|_{\text{tr}}=5/3
and thus detect entanglement. Measuring any two unbiased bases of the Wootters-Fields construction except the standard basis results in
\|\mathbf{Q}_{2}\|_{\text{tr}}=4/3
and no entanglement is detected.
As for EAMs, we assume the local dimension and number of MUBs to be equal for system A and B, but also the general case follows straightforewardly from the proof of Result
2
.
The bound given in Eq. (
53
) is generally not tight for
m<d+1
and we conjecture the following refinement.

Conjecture 3
.

For any state of Schmidt number
r
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{Q}_{m}\|_{\text{tr}}\leq 1+\frac{(m-1)r}{d}.

(54)

For
m=d+1
or
r=1
this coincides with Eq. (
53
).

VI
Discussion

We have presented two criteria for Schmidt number detection in arbitrary-dimensional systems. These criteria make use of the table of measurement statistic obtained when measuring in the set of projectors forming a symmetric informationally complete POVM or a complete set of mutually unbiased bases. Both criteria are strictly stronger than the fidelity criterion and the CCNR criterion. In fact, it is not to hard to construct examples of states which are detected by our criteria but not by the two others. Under certain circumstances, we have found that our two criteria can be shown to be equivalent and we conjecture this to always be the case, insofar both constructions exist in a given dimension. This connection between the SIC-based and the MUB-based criterion does not leave the other redundant, but contrarily gives two options to test the same criterion. Indeed, both the existence and construction of SIC-POVMs and complete sets of MUBs are not known in arbitrary-dimensions. Here the two criteria complement each other. While the SICs up to some low three digit dimensions can efficiently be obtained by the results of Ref.
Scott and Grassl 2010
;
Scott 2017
, MUBs can be constructed in arbitrary prime power dimension
Wootters and Fields 1989
.

The motivation for the presented criteria is not experimental applicability but primarily a theoretical understanding of high-dimensional entanglement. Nonetheless, they can still be relevant for determining Schmidt numbers in experiments. Measuring a complete set of MUBs or SIC projectors is experimentally as complex as full state tomography and the state can be reconstructed by the obtained statistics. However, it is not necessary to measure all projectors forming a SIC or a complete set of MUBs to apply our criteria. The singular values of any diagonal submatrix are smaller than the singular values of the full correlation matrix. It is hence possible to identify subsets of projectors that already lead to a violation of the presented bounds, without the need to fully reconstruct the correlation matrix
\mathbf{Q}
or
\mathbf{P}
. Additionally, our results can be adopted in designing experiments, as they bound the Schmidt number that can be experimentally witnessed with a fidelity witness.

Our work leaves several conjecture that are interesting to decide. Another relevant question is whether also other norms can be used to obtain (perhaps even stronger) Schmidt number criteria. Finally, it may also be interesting to consider whether norm-based entanglement criteria can be extended from our bipartite consideration to also detect the dimension of genuine multipartite entanglement
Cobucci and Tavakoli 2024
.

Extending Rastgin’s proof to general operators

In this appendix we give a proof that the index of coincidence
I(\sigma)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}|\tr(E_i \sigma)|^{2}
for a SIC-POVM
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d^{2}}
and any linear operator
\sigma
satisfies the relation

\displaystyle I(\sigma)=

\displaystyle\frac{\tr(\sigma)^{2}+\tr(\sigma^\dagger\sigma)}{d(d+1)}.

(55)

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d^{2}}
be a SIC-POVM and define

\displaystyle F_{i}=\sqrt{d(d+1)}E_{i}-\frac{\sqrt{d+1}-1}{d\sqrt{d}}\openone.

(56)

A quick calculation shows that
\tr(F_iF_j)=\delta_{ij}
and so
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d^{2}}
forms an orthonormal basis for the linear operators. Therefore for any operator
\sigma
it holds that

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d^{2}}\tr(F_i\sigma)F_{i}

(57)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d^{2}}d(d+1)\tr(E_i\sigma)E_{i}-\tr(\sigma)\openone.

(58)

We subtract
\tr(\sigma)\openone
, multiply each side with
\sigma^{\dagger}+\tr(\sigma)\openone
and take the trace, resulting in

\displaystyle\tr(\sigma^\dagger\sigma)+(d+2)\tr(\sigma)^{2}

(59)

\displaystyle=\tr(\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d^2}d^2(d-1)^2\tr(E_i\sigma)^*\tr(E_j\sigma)E_iE_j).

(60)

Using
\tr(E_iE_j)=\delta_{ij}/(d(d+1))+1/(d^{2}(d+1))
and
\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d^{2}}\tr(E_i\sigma)^{*}\tr(E_j\sigma)=\tr(\sigma)^{2}

\displaystyle I(\sigma)=\frac{\tr(\sigma)^{2}+\tr(\sigma^\dagger\sigma)}{d(d+1)}.

(61)

Appendix B
Entanglement monotones

We have seen that both Result
1
and
2
are invariant under local unitaries, more efficient than the CCNR criterion and conjectured to be ultimately equivalent. These facts bear the obvious question if these quantities can be used to define entanglement monotones of the form
f(\|\mathbf{P}(\rho)\|_{\text{tr}})
, where
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a monotonically increasing and convex function.

It clearly holds that the matrix
\mathbf{P}
is additive

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\lambda\rho+(1-\lambda)\sigma)=\lambda\mathbf{P}(\rho)+(1-\lambda)\mathbf{P}(\sigma)

(62)

and from the convexity of
f
and the triangle-inequality of the trace norm it holds that

\displaystyle f(\|\mathbf{P}(\lambda\rho+(1-\lambda)\sigma)\|_{\text{tr}})

(63)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda f(\|\mathbf{P}(\rho)\|_{\text{tr}})+(1-\lambda)f(\|\mathbf{P}(\sigma)\|_{\text{tr}}).

(64)

According to
Vidal 2000
, in order to show that
f(\|\mathbf{P}(\rho)\|_{\text{tr}})
is an entanglement monotone, one has to prove that

\displaystyle f(\|\mathbf{P}(\rho)\|_{\text{tr}})\geq\sum\limits_{k}p_{k}f(\|\mathbf{P}(\rho_{k})\|_{\text{tr}}),

(65)

where
p_{k}=\tr{\mathcal{E}_k(\rho)}
and
\rho_{k}=\mathcal{E}_{k}(\rho)/p_{k}
for any unilocal quantum operation
\mathcal{E}_{k}
performed by any party (local instrument).

However, it is possible to increase the correlation norms by local operations.
Take for example the state
\rho_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\outerproduct{\phi^+}{\phi^+}\otimes\openone/2\otimes\openone/2
. The CCNR criterion and the SIC-criterion then become

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{C}(\rho_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right\|_{\text{tr}}=1

(66)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{P}(\rho_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right\|_{\text{tr}}=2.

(67)

Two projective measurements in
\sigma_{Z}
in system
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
result either in
\rho^{\prime}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\outerproduct{\phi^+}{\phi^+}\otimes\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\outerproduct{i}{i}
or in
\rho^{\prime\prime}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\outerproduct{\phi^+}{\phi^+}\otimes\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\outerproduct{j}{j}
.
It holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{C}(\rho^{\prime}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right\|_{\text{tr}}=\left\|\mathbf{C}(\rho^{\prime\prime}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right\|_{\text{tr}}=2

(68)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{P}(\rho^{\prime}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right\|_{\text{tr}}=\left\|\mathbf{P}(\rho^{\prime\prime}_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}})\right\|_{\text{tr}}=3.

(69)

The norm criteria can therefore be increased by a local operation, even without classical communication. This stems from the fact that these matrix norm methods measure correlations, not entanglement. This can be used as entanglement criteria, as classical correlations cannot surpass a given threshold. But at the same time the norms can be increased by creating additional classical correlations.
Note that this example also holds for less strict criteria than the ones requested by Ref.
Vidal 2000
.

Appendix C
Proof of the EAM bound

We first show for any operator
\sigma
and EAM
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
the index of coincidence
I(\sigma)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\tr(E_i \sigma)|^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle I(\sigma)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(n-d)\tr(\sigma)^{2}+d(d-1)\tr(\sigma^\dagger\sigma)}{n(n-1)}.

(70)

This extends the result of Rastegin to linear operators and the bound of Eq. (
10
) to EAMs.
The general proof follows the same steps as Rastegin
Rastegin 2023
with the following adaptations

\displaystyle a_{0}=\tr(\sigma)/d

(71)

\displaystyle a_{k}=\frac{1}{d}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{d-1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\omega^{-k(i-1)}\tr(E_i \sigma)

(72)

\displaystyle\tr(\sigma^\dagger\sigma)\geq\tr(\sigma)^{2}/d^{2}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}^{*}a_{k}

(73)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}^{*}a_{k}=\frac{n-1}{d^{2}(d-1)}(nI(\sigma)-\tr(\sigma)^{2}).

(74)

Eq. (
70
) follows immediately.

In the same way as for SIC-POVMs, we can restrict our analysis to pure states with Schmidt decomposition
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}\ket{ss}
. The probabilities are then given by

\displaystyle P_{ab}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}\langle ss|E^{A}_{a}\otimes E^{B}_{b}|tt\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}\lambda_{s}^{2}D_{s}^{a,b}+\sum_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}O_{s,t}^{a,b},

(75)

with
D_{s}^{a,b}=\langle ss|E^{A}_{a}\otimes E^{B}_{b}|ss\rangle
and
O_{s,t}^{a,b}=\langle ss|E^{A}_{a}\otimes E^{B}_{b}|tt\rangle
. A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\|D_{s}\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{I_{A}(\outerproduct{s}{s})}\sqrt{I_{B}(\outerproduct{s}{s})}\leq\frac{(n-d)+d(d-1)}{n(n-1)}

(76)

\displaystyle\|O_{s,t}\|_{\text{tr}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{I_{A}(\outerproduct{s}{t})}\sqrt{I_{B}(\outerproduct{s}{t})}\leq\frac{d(d-1)}{n(n-1)}

(77)

Now we can write

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{P}_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{s=1}^{r}\lambda_{s}^{2}\|D_{s}\|+\sum\limits_{s\neq s}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}\|O_{s,t}\|

(78)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(n-d)+d(d-1)}{n(n-1)}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{r}\lambda_{s}^{2}+\frac{d(d-1)}{n(n-1)}\sum\limits_{s\neq t}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}

(79)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-d}{n(n-1)}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{r}\lambda_{s}^{2}+\frac{d(d-1)}{n(n-1)}\sum\limits_{s,t=1}^{r}\lambda_{s}\lambda_{t}

(80)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n-d+d(d-1)r}{n(n-1)}.

(81)

By convexity the same result holds for all mixed states of Schmidt number
r
.

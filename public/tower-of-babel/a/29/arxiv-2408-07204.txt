\displaystyle a\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\phi(x)|Jh_{\epsilon}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(u(x,t))\phi(x)|Jh_{\epsilon}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}g(u(x,t))\phi(x)|J_{\partial\Omega_{\epsilon}}h_{\epsilon}(x)|\,d\sigma(x)

Remark 3.1
.

A simple computation shows that the metric
\mu=\mu(\epsilon)=|Jh_{\epsilon}|\,d\,x
is exactly the metric
h^{*}\,d\,x
obtained by pull-backing the canonical metric in
\Omega_{\epsilon}
. Also, the first term in the operator

A_{\epsilon}:H^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\rightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega,\mu)
defined by

\displaystyle<A_{\epsilon}u,\phi>_{-1,1,\mu}=\int_{\Omega}(h_{\epsilon}^{*}\nabla_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}h_{\epsilon}^{*-1}u)(x,t)\cdot(h_{\epsilon}^{*}\nabla_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}h_{\epsilon}^{*-1}\phi)(x)|Jh_{\epsilon}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle+a\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\phi(x)|Jh_{\epsilon}(x)|\,dx,\,u,\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mu)

(27)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{-1,1,\mu}
is the dual pairing between
H^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
and its dual (which we denote here by
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mu))
,
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator in
\Omega
, with respect to the metric
\mu(\epsilon)
.

The equation (
26
) can be written as an abstract equation in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mu)
:

{u}_{t}=-A_{\epsilon}u+H_{\epsilon}{u}.

where

H({u})=F(u)+G(u),

\displaystyle F(u)(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(u(x,t))\psi(x)\,|Jh_{\epsilon}(x)|\,d\,x

\displaystyle G(u)(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}g(u(x,t))\psi(x)|J_{\partial\Omega}h_{\epsilon}(x)|\,d\sigma(x)

with
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mu).

We finally observe that the metrics
\mu(\epsilon)
generated by the new inner product are uniformly equivalent to the usual euclidean metric, so the spaces
H^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
can be identified as metric spaces with the (fixed) usual Sobolev space

4.1
Sectoriality in

Lemma 4.1
.

The operators
-A_{\epsilon}

defined by (

\left\langle-A_{\epsilon}u,u\right\rangle_{-1,1,\mu}\geq C\parallel u\parallel_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}
where

is a positive constant independent of
\epsilon

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
,
a_{\epsilon}:H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the bilinear form given by

a_{\epsilon}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(h_{\epsilon}^{*}\nabla_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}h_{\epsilon}^{*-1}u)(x,t)\cdot(h_{\epsilon}^{*}\nabla_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}h_{\epsilon}^{*-1}v)(x)|Jh_{\epsilon}(x)|\,dx+a\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)v(x)|Jh_{\epsilon}(x)|\,dx.

The bilinear form
a_{\epsilon}
is symmetric, continuous and

<-A_{\epsilon}u,v>_{-1,1}=a_{\epsilon}(u,v),\,\,\forall v\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Thus
A_{\epsilon}
is also symmetric.
We claim that
a_{\epsilon}
is uniformly coercive, that is, there exists
a constant
C>0
such that
a_{\epsilon}(u,u)\geq C\parallel u\parallel^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
for all

In fact, we have

\displaystyle a_{\epsilon}(u,u)

\displaystyle=\,\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}b_{ij}b_{ik}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}|Jh_{\epsilon}|\,dx+\,a\int_{\Omega}u^{2}|Jh_{\epsilon}|\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\epsilon)\sum_{i}{\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}}^{2}\,|Jh_{\epsilon}|\,dx+au^{2}\,|Jh_{\epsilon}|\,d\,x,

where the

are the entries of the inverse transposed of the Jacobian matrix of

that is,

b_{11}^{\epsilon}=1,\,b_{12}^{\epsilon}=\frac{-\varphi(x_{2},\epsilon)cos(x_{1}/\epsilon)\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{1+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{2}}(x_{2},\epsilon)sin(x_{1}/\epsilon)},\,b_{21}^{\epsilon}=0,\,b_{22}^{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{2}}(x_{2},\epsilon)sin(x_{1}/\epsilon)},

and

\begin{pmatrix}1&b_{12}\\

It is easy to show, using the hypotheses in (

\displaystyle\geq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2},

(31)

as claimed.

It follows from Lax-Milgram Theorem, that
A_{\epsilon}
is surjective and, therefore, self-adjoint. The claimed estimate for
A_{\epsilon}
follows immediately from (

{\square}

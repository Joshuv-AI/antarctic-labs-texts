Symplectic Representation and Turbulent Global Solutions of Incompressible Navier-Stokes Equations in ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3}
Yongqian Han

Abstract
. The incompressible Navier-Stokes equations are considered. We find that there exist infinite non-trivial solutions of static Euler equations. Moreover there exist random solutions of static Euler equations. Provided Reynolds number is large enough and time variable t t goes to infinity, these random solutions of static Euler equations are the path limits of corresponding Navier-Stokes flows. But the double limit of these Navier-Stokes flows do not exist. Therefore these solutions are called turbulent solutions.

1.
Introduction

The Navier-Stokes equations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are given by

(1.1)

u_{t}-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla P=0,

(1.2)

\nabla\cdot u=0,

where

,

and
\partial_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}

(

Taking curl with equation (
1.1

\omega_{t}-\nu\Delta\omega+(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-(\omega\cdot\nabla)u=0.

Here vorticity

and

(1.4)

\begin{split}\omega(t,x)=&\nabla\times u(t,x).\\
\end{split}

The velocity vector

1.1
)
(

The Fourier transformation of

1.2

(1.5)

\xi_{j}\hat{u}^{j}=\xi_{1}\hat{u}^{1}+\xi_{2}\hat{u}^{2}+\xi_{3}\hat{u}^{3}=0.

It is that
\hat{u}(t,\xi)
is perpendicular to
\xi

\xi\bot\hat{u}

\hat{u}(t,\xi)\in T_{\xi}{\mathbb{S}}^{2}

for any
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}

For any vector
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}-\{0\}

where

and

1.2
). We call that the formulation

Now

(1.7)

\begin{split}\omega(t,x)=&\nabla\times u(t,x)\\
=&-\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\phi(t,x)+(A\times\nabla)\Delta\psi(t,x).\\
\end{split}

Thanks the following observations

(1.8)

\begin{split}(A\times\nabla)\cdot u(t,x)&=(A\times\nabla)\cdot(A\times\nabla)\phi(t,x)\\
&=\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\phi(t,x),\\
\end{split}

(1.9)

\begin{split}(A\times\nabla)\cdot\omega(t,x)&=(A\times\nabla)\cdot(A\times\nabla)\Delta\psi(t,x)\\
&=\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\Delta\psi(t,x),\\
\end{split}

taking scalar product of equation (
1.1
) with
A\times\nabla
,
we have

(1.10)

\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\{\phi_{t}-\nu\Delta\phi\}+(A\times\nabla)\cdot\{(u\cdot\nabla)u\}=0.

And taking scalar product of (
1.3
) with
A\times\nabla
,
we derive

(1.11)

\big\{(A\cdot A)\Delta-(A\cdot\nabla)^{2}\big\}\Delta\{\psi_{t}-\nu\Delta\psi\}+(A\times\nabla)\cdot\{(u\cdot\nabla)\omega-(\omega\cdot\nabla)u\}=0.

Therefore
(\phi,\psi)
satisfies equations (
1.10
)
(
1.11
) provided

satisfies equations (
1.1
)
(
1.6

There is a large literature (see

2

(

\begin{split}&Y(r)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}V(r)\\
r\partial_{r}V(r)\end{array}\right),\\
&Y(R_{1})=KY(R_{2}),\\
\end{split}

where
2\times 2
matrix
K\in SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\det K
=1.

By Sturm-Liouville theory (see
[
3
]
, Page 72, Theorem 4.3.1),
there exist countable infinite eigenvalues

0<\zeta_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\zeta_{n}\leq\cdots

and eigenfunction
V_{j}(r)
corresponding to eigenvalue
\zeta_{j}
of
equation (
3.12
) with separated boundary condition
(
3.13
) or coupled boundary condition (
3.14
).
Each eigenvalue is real, and may be geometrically simple or double.

For the eigenfunctions
V_{j}
of problem (
3.12
) (
3.13
),
by the regular theory of elliptic equations, we have
V_{j}\in H^{1}_{0}({\cal R})\cap H^{\infty}({\cal R})
(
j=1,2,\cdots
) provided

(m_{11},m_{12})=(1,0)
and
(m_{21},m_{22})=(1,0)
.

Therefore there exist solutions

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\lambda_{j}^{2}=\zeta_{j}^{2}+\eta^{2},\;\;j=1,2,\cdots\\
&\Psi_{j}(r,x_{3})=\alpha V_{j}(r)\sin(\eta x_{3})+\beta V_{j}(r)\cos(\eta x_{3})\\
\end{split}

of problem (
3.5
). Here
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\eta

are any real constants.

Thus there exist solution class
{\mathbb{Z}}_{E}
of static Euler
equations and solution class
{\mathbb{Z}}_{NS}
of Navier-Stokes
equations. Here

(3.16)

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{E}=\cup^{\infty}_{j=1}\cup_{\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}}Z^{e}_{j\eta},\\
&Z^{e}_{j\eta}=\mbox{span}\big\{\lambda_{j}(A\times\nabla)\big(V_{j}(r)\sin(\eta x_{3})\big)+\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(V_{j}(r)\sin(\eta x_{3})\big),\\
&\hskip 62.59605pt\lambda_{j}(A\times\nabla)\big(V_{j}(r)\cos(\eta x_{3})\big)+\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(V_{j}(r)\cos(\eta x_{3})\big)\big\},\\
\end{split}

(3.17)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{Z}}_{NS}=&\cup^{\infty}_{j=1}\cup_{\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}}Z^{ns}_{j\eta},\\
Z^{ns}_{j\eta}=&\mbox{span}\big\{u^{s}_{j\eta},u^{c}_{j\eta}\big\},\\
u^{s}_{j\eta}=&\lambda_{j}e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}_{j}t}(A\times\nabla)\big(V_{j}(r)\sin(\eta x_{3})\big)\\
&+e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}_{j}t}\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(V_{j}(r)\sin(\eta x_{3})\big),\\
u^{c}_{j\eta}=&\lambda_{j}e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}_{j}t}(A\times\nabla)\big(V_{j}(r)\cos(\eta x_{3})\big)\\
&+e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}_{j}t}\{(A\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(V_{j}(r)\cos(\eta x_{3})\big),\\
\end{split}

where
Z^{e}_{j\eta}
and
Z^{ns}_{j\eta}
are linear spaces for any

j=1,2,\cdots
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

4.
Conclusion

Some interesting and significant phenomena are founded by observing
the solutions in Section 2 and Section 3.

There exist some special solution classes of Navier-Stokes equations,
for instance
{\mathbb{X}}_{NS}^{2R}
,
{\mathbb{X}}_{NS}
,

{\mathbb{X}}^{s}_{NS}
,
{\mathbb{Y}}_{NS}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{NS}
, which
will be called turbulent solution classes.

Let
\mu
be random number and
\Gamma_{\mu}
be a path. In the path

\Gamma_{\mu}
, we have

(4.1)

\begin{split}t\nu=\mu\\
\end{split}

for any
(\nu,t)\in\Gamma_{\mu}
.

Assume that
u_{ns}(t,x^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{X}}_{NS}^{2R}
is the
solution of Navier-Stokes equation,
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and

r^{\prime}=|x^{\prime}|
. Then there exist
\Phi(x^{\prime})=\Phi(r^{\prime})
and
\Psi(x^{\prime})=\Psi(r^{\prime})
such that

\begin{split}u_{ns}(t,x^{\prime})=&(e_{3}\times\nabla)\Big\{\frac{1}{4\pi t\nu}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\exp\{-\frac{|y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4t\nu}\}\Phi(|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|)dy^{\prime}\Big\}\\
&+\{(e_{3}\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\Big\{\frac{1}{4\pi t\nu}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\exp\{-\frac{|y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4t\nu}\}\Psi(|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|)dy^{\prime}\Big\}.\\
\end{split}

Letting
(\nu,t)\in\Gamma_{\mu}
and
(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)
, we
have path limit

(4.2)

\begin{split}u_{e}(x^{\prime})=&\lim_{(\nu,t)\in\Gamma_{\mu},(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)}u_{ns}(t,x^{\prime})\\
=&(e_{3}\times\nabla)\Phi_{l}(r^{\prime})+\{(e_{3}\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\Psi_{l}(r^{\prime}),\\
\Phi_{l}(r^{\prime})=&\Phi_{l}(x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4\pi\mu}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\exp\{-\frac{|y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4\mu}\}\Phi(|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|)dy^{\prime},\\
\Psi_{l}(r^{\prime})=&\Psi_{l}(x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4\pi\mu}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\exp\{-\frac{|y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4\mu}\}\Psi(|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|)dy^{\prime}.\\
\end{split}

It is obvious that random
u_{e}
is the solution of static Euler
equations and
u_{e}\in{\mathbb{X}}_{E}^{2R}
. Moreover double limit

\lim_{(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)}u_{ns}(t,x^{\prime})
does not exist.

Assume that
u_{ns}(t,x)\in{\mathbb{X}}_{NS}
is the solution of
Navier-Stokes equation. Then there exist linear space
X_{ns\lambda}
and
real constants
\alpha
,
\beta
such that
u_{ns}(t,x)\in X_{ns\lambda}
and

\begin{split}u_{ns}(t,x)=&\alpha\big\{\lambda e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}t}(e_{3}\times\nabla)\big(\frac{1}{r}\sin(\lambda r)\big)+e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}t}\{(e_{3}\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(\frac{1}{r}\sin(\lambda r)\big)\big\}\\
&+\beta\big\{\lambda e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}t}(e_{3}\times\nabla)\big(\frac{1}{r}\cos(\lambda r)\big)+e^{-\nu\lambda^{2}t}\{(e_{3}\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(\frac{1}{r}\cos(\lambda r)\big)\big\},\\
\end{split}

where
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
r=|x|
. Letting
(\nu,t)\in\Gamma_{\mu}
and

(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)
, we have path limit

(4.3)

\begin{split}u_{e}(x)=&\lim_{(\nu,t)\in\Gamma_{\mu},(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)}u_{ns}(t,x)\\
=&\alpha\big\{\lambda e^{-\mu\lambda^{2}}(e_{3}\times\nabla)\big(\frac{1}{r}\sin(\lambda r)\big)+e^{-\mu\lambda^{2}}\{(e_{3}\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(\frac{1}{r}\sin(\lambda r)\big)\big\}\\
&+\beta\big\{\lambda e^{-\mu\lambda^{2}}(e_{3}\times\nabla)\big(\frac{1}{r}\cos(\lambda r)\big)+e^{-\mu\lambda^{2}}\{(e_{3}\times\nabla)\times\nabla\}\big(\frac{1}{r}\cos(\lambda r)\big)\big\}.\\
\end{split}

It is obvious that random
u_{e}
is the solution of static Euler
equations and
u_{e}\in X_{e\lambda}
since
X_{e\lambda}
is linear space.
Moreover double limit
\lim_{(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)}u_{ns}(t,x)

does not exist.

Similarly assume that
u_{ns}(t,x)\in{\mathbb{X}}^{s}_{NS}
(

{\mathbb{Y}}_{NS}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}_{NS}
respectively) is the solution
of Navier-Stokes equation. Then there exists linear space
X^{s}_{ns\lambda}

(
Y^{ns}_{j\lambda}
or
Z^{ns}_{j\lambda}
resp.) such that
u_{ns}(t,x)\in X^{s}_{ns\lambda}
(
Y^{ns}_{j\lambda}
or
Z^{ns}_{j\lambda}
resp.). Since

X^{s}_{e\lambda}
(
Y^{e}_{j\lambda}
or
Z^{e}_{j\lambda}
resp.) is linear space, we
can prove that there exists the path limit

u_{e}(x)=\lim_{(\nu,t)\in\Gamma_{\mu},(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)}u_{ns}(t,x)

such that
u_{e}(x)\in X^{s}_{e\lambda}
(
Y^{e}_{j\lambda}
or
Z^{e}_{j\lambda}

resp.). But the double limit
\lim_{(\nu,1/t)\rightarrow(0,0)}u_{ns}(t,x)
does not exist.

In experiment or numerical simulation of fluid motion ruled by
Navier-Stokes equations, large Reynolds number
\frac{1}{\nu}\gg 1
is
firstly given. Then after a long long time, we observe the fluid
motion at some time
1\ll t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}<\cdots
.
(\nu,t_{n})
is
located in the path
\Gamma_{\mu_{n}}
where
\mu_{n}=t_{n}\nu
(
n=1,2,\cdots
). We can see random of fluid based on the existence of path
limit of velocity along
\Gamma_{\mu_{n}}
provided the initial
velocity of fluid is in one class of
{\mathbb{X}}_{E}^{2R}
,

{\mathbb{X}}_{E}
,
{\mathbb{X}}^{s}_{E}
,
{\mathbb{Y}}_{E}
and

{\mathbb{Z}}_{E}
.

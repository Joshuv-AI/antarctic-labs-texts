Well-covered Unitary Cayley Graphs of Matrix Rings over Finite Fields and Applications
Shahin Rahimi, Ashkan Nikseresht Department of Mathematics, College of Sciences, Shiraz University,71457-44776, Shiraz, Iran

Abstract
Suppose that F F is a finite field and R = M n ​ ( F ) R=M_{n}(F) is the ring of n n -square matrices over F F . Here we characterize when the Cayley graph of the additive group of R R with respect to the set of invertible elements of R R , called the unitary Cayley graph of R R , is well-covered. Then we apply this to characterize all finite rings with identity whose unitary Cayley graph is well-covered or Cohen-Macaulay.



Keywords: unitary Cayley graph, well-covered graph, matrix rings, edge ideal.

2
Well-covered unitary Cayley graphs of matrix rings over finite fields

In this section, we characterize
n
-square matrix rings over finite fields for which the unitary Cayley graph is
well-covered. To begin with, note that
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\}
is an independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
, exactly whenever

\{0,x_{2}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}-x_{1}\}
is so. Therefore, without loss of generality, we consider independent sets that contain
zero. In addition, it is not hard to see that the unitary Cayley graph of a finite field is well-covered. Our first
result shows that
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
is well-covered for
n\leq 2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any finite field
F
, if
n\leq 2
, then the graph
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
is well-covered.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{1}(F)\big)=\mathcal{G}(F)
is a complete graph and well-covered. Let
A
be an independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{2}(F)\big)

containing zero. Also, let
|A|=m+1
for some
0\leq m<|F|^{2}-1
. Note that any non-unit element of
M_{2}(F)
has either
the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\right)
or
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r\\
\alpha r\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, for some row vector
r\in F^{2}
and
\alpha\in F
. We show that
A
cannot be a maximal independent set. To that aim,
there are three cases to investigate:

Case (i):
A=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
r_{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}
. Since
m<|F|^{2}-1
, we can find a nonzero row vector
r_{m+1}
distinct from all
r_{i}
’s. Therefore,
A\cup\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
r_{m+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}
is an independent set, as claimed.

Case (ii):
A=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{1}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{m}\\
\alpha_{m}r_{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}
where
\alpha_{i}\in F
and
0\neq r_{i}\in F^{2}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Since
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{1}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)-\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{i}\\
\alpha_{i}r_{i}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is not invertible, we can see that
\alpha_{1}r_{1}-\alpha_{i}r_{i}=\alpha(r_{1}-r_{i})
for some
\alpha\in F
or

r_{1}=r_{i}
. Thus it can be concluded that
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{1}
or
r_{i}=\beta_{i}r_{1}
for some
\beta_{i}\in F
.

Assume that
r_{i}
is not a multiple of
r_{1}
and
r_{j}=\beta_{j}r_{1}
for some
1\leq i,j\leq m
. Then since

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{i}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{i}\end{smallmatrix}\right)-\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\beta_{j}r_{1}\\
\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is not invertible, we have
\alpha_{1}r_{i}-\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}r_{1}=\gamma(r_{i}-\beta_{j}r_{1})
for some
\gamma\in F
. This yields that
\alpha_{1}=\gamma=\alpha_{j}
. Therefore, either there exists an
i
with
r_{i}
not a multiple of
r_{1}

and
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{1}
for all
j
, that is,

A=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{1}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{2}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{m}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right\},

or
r_{i}
is a multiple of
r_{1}
for all
i
, that is,

A=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{1}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\beta_{2}r_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\beta_{m}r_{1}\\
\alpha_{m}\beta_{m}r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}.

In the former case, since
m<|F|^{2}-1
, choosing
r_{m+1}\in F^{2}
distinct from all
r_{i}
’s yields that
A\cup\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{m+1}\\
\alpha_{1}r_{m+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}

is an independent set, as desired. In the latter case,
A
is strictly contained in the independent set
\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha r_{1}\\
\beta r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mid\alpha,\beta\in F\}
, as required.

Case (iii):
A=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
r_{s}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{s+1}\\
\alpha_{s+1}r_{s+1}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\ldots,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{m}\\
\alpha_{m}r_{m}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}
where
r_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
\alpha_{i}\in F
for
s+1\leq i\leq m
. Since

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}r_{j}\\
\alpha_{j}r_{j}\end{smallmatrix}\right)-\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
r_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is not invertible for
1\leq k\leq s
and
s+1\leq j\leq m
, we infer that
r_{k}

is a multiple of
r_{j}
for all
s+1\leq j\leq m
and
1\leq k\leq s
. Hence all
r_{i}
are multiples of
r_{1}
. Again,

A
is strictly contained in the independent set
\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha r_{1}\\
\beta r_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mid\alpha,\beta\in F\}
, as promised.

Therefore, we have shown that any maximal independent set in
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{2}(F)\big)
possesses at least
|F|^{2}
elements. Now, the
result follows from Lemma
1.1
(
i
).
∎

Next we prove the converse of Proposition
2.1
. To this end, we introduce some new tools which we call

k
-diagonal matrices.

Definition 2.2
.

Let

be a positive integer and
1\leq k,l\leq n
. By

, we mean an

in which, the entry
(i,j)
is
a_{i-l}
whenever
j-i=k
and

, else it is zero (with modulo
n

where
0
is replaced with
n
). We say that a matrix
D
is reduced
k
-diagonal if
D=D_{k,l}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})

for some suitable
n
,
k
and

. Also, for convenience, we let

D_{k,k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})=D_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
.

Example 2.3
.

Let

and

. Then we have:

D_{1,1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\

a_{2}&0&0\end{pmatrix},D_{1,2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{2}&0\\
0&0&0\\
a_{1}&0&0\end{pmatrix},D_{1,3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}&0\\

D_{2,1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\

Note that the

-th row in

is zero. Also, it can be seen that

where

0&0&a_{1}&0\\

0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

In the next lemma, we construct an independent set in
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
by using the reduced
k
-diagonals.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F
be a finite field and
n
be a positive integer. Then
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\mid 1\leq k\leq n,a_{i}\in F\}
is an independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
.

Proof.

Let
D_{k}=D_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
and
D_{l}=D_{l}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1})
be two elements of
\mathcal{D}
. If
k=l
, it can be seen
that
D_{k}-D_{l}
contains a zero row. Hence, assume that
k\neq l
. Observe that there are exactly two rows in
D_{k}-D_{l}
,
in which only one entry is possibly nonzero, the
k
-th and the
l
-th rows. These two entries are in the same column,
that is, the
(k+l)
-th column. Therefore, the
k
-th and the
l
-th rows are linearly dependent and
D_{k}-D_{l}
is not
an invertible matrix in
M_{n}(F)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Note that the independent set
\mathcal{D}
of the previous lemma contains the zero matrix as
D_{k}(0,\ldots,0)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
m<n
are positive integers,
F
is a finite field,
D_{k}=D_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
is an element of
\mathcal{D}
as
in Lemma
2.4
, and
A
is an element of any maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
containing
\mathcal{D}
. Then, the
matrix composed of any
m
rows of
D_{k}
and the other rows of
A
is not invertible.

Proof.

Denote the rows of
A
and
D_{k}
by
r_{i}
and
d_{i}
, respectively. We use induction on
m
. First, letting
m=1
, we may
assume that the first row of the mentioned matrix is
d_{1}
and
k\neq 1
. Note that
\det(A)
and
\det\big(A-D_{l}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1})\big)
are zero for every

and

, by assumption. So:

\begin{vmatrix}d_{1}\\
r_{2}\\
\vdots\\
r_{n}\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}r_{1}\\
r_{2}\\
\vdots\\
r_{n}\end{vmatrix}-\begin{vmatrix}r_{1}-d_{1}\\
r_{2}\\
\vdots\\
r_{n}\end{vmatrix}=\det(A)-\det\big(A-D_{k}(0,\ldots,a_{1-k},\ldots 0)\big)=0.

Therefore, in this case we get the desired

and without loss of generality, let the
m
rows from
D_{k}
be

. Using row

\begin{vmatrix}d_{1}\\

\vdots\\

\vdots\\

d_{2}\\
\vdots\\

d_{2}\\
\vdots\\
d_{m}\\
r_{m+1}\\
\vdots\\
r_{n}\end{vmatrix}.

By the induction hypothesis the first term is zero, so:

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}d_{1}\\

\displaystyle=-\begin{vmatrix}r_{1}-d_{1}\\

\displaystyle=-\begin{vmatrix}r_{1}\\

Finally, we can present the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
F
be a finite field. Then, the graph
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
is well-covered if and only if

.

Proof.

Taking Proposition
2.1
into account, we only have to show the implication.
Suppose
n>2
and let
M
be any maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
containing
\mathcal{D}
as in Lemma
2.4
and
A\in M
. Let
1\leq k\leq n
. The
determinant of the matrix which is obtained by replacing the
k
-th row of
D_{k}(1,\ldots,1)
with the
k
-th row of

is
\pm a_{k,2k}
which must be zero by Lemma
2.5
(note that here

is calculated modulo
n
). Therefore,
M
has the property that each of its elements has
n
zeros in

(k,2k)
for each

. Note that there are

such matrices. On the other hand, consider the invertible matrix

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_{n-2}&0\\

where

is the identity matrix of order

and

. This matrix has the aforementioned property (since

) and is not in

(

) allows us to

3
Well-covered and Cohen-Macaulay unitary Cayley graphs of finite rings

In this section, we utilize the results of the previous section to characterize finite rings for which the unitary Cayley graph is well-covered or
Cohen-Macaulay.

Initially, we state a crucial tool that allows us to focus on semisimple rings instead of arbitrary rings for studying
well-coveredness. In addition, this proposition generalizes
[
11
, Corollary 2.4]
to all finite rings using
different methods. Here
J(R)
denotes the Jacobson radical of
R
(see
[
2
, p. 120]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
R
is a finite ring,
A
is a subset of
R
, and
\bar{A}
denotes the image of
A
in
R/J(R)
. The
following statements hold.

(i)

A
is a maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
if and only if
\bar{A}
is a maximal independent
set of
\mathcal{G}\big(R/J(R)\big)
and
A=A+J(R)
.

(ii)

\bar{A}
is a maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(R/J(R)\big)
if and only if
A+J(R)
is a maximal
independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
.

In particular,
\mathcal{G}(R)
is a well-covered graph if and only if so is
\mathcal{G}\big(R/J(R)\big)
.

Proof.

Note that if
x\in R
and
j\in J(R)
, then
x\in\mathrm{U}(R)
if and only if
x+j\in\mathrm{U}(R)
if and only if
x+J(R)\in\mathrm{U}\big(R/J(R)\big)
.
Therefore,
A
is an independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
if and only if
A+J(R)
is an independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
if and only if

\bar{A}
is an independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(R/J(R)\big)
. From this, (
i
) and (
ii
) follow.

For the last part, observe that if
A
is a maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
, then by (
i
),
|A|=|\bar{A}||J(R)|
and if

\bar{A}
is a maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(R/J(R)\big)
, then
|A+J(R)|=|\bar{A}||J(R)|
. Hence, the assertion
follows immediately.
∎

Well-coveredness of unitary Cayley graphs is settled for finite commutative rings in
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
and for

n
-square matrix rings over finite fields in Theorem
2.6
. In what follows, we also settle it for certain
decomposable finite rings from which the main result can be achieved. First, a lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
F
is a finite field and
n>1
. If
A
is a nonzero element of
M_{n}(F)
, then some non-unit
B\in M_{n}(F)

exists such that
A-B
is invertible.

Proof.

Since
A\neq 0
, a nonzero entry exists in
A
, say
a_{ij}
. Replace the
i
-th row of
A
with zero row to obtain

A^{\prime}
and let
k=j-i
. Letting
B=A^{\prime}-D_{k,i}(1,\ldots,1)
, we see that
A-B
can be obtained from
D_{k,i}(1,\ldots,1)
by replacing the

i
-th row of
D_{k,i}
with the
i
-th row of
A
. So
\det(A-B)=\pm a_{ij}\neq 0
. Moreover,
B
contains a zero row,
the

i
-th row, which completes the proof.
∎

Recall that if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are graphs on vertex sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, then their
conjunction product
is the graph
on vertex set
V_{1}\times V_{2}
, in which, two vertices
(v_{1},v_{2})
and
(u_{1},u_{2})
are adjacent when
v_{1}
is adjacent
to
u_{1}
and
v_{2}
is adjacent to
u_{2}
in
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively. It is routine to check that
\mathcal{G}(R_{1}\times R_{2})
is the conjunction product of
\mathcal{G}(R_{1})
and
\mathcal{G}(R_{2})
. In
[
22
]
, well-coveredness of several products of
graphs including the conjunction product is studied.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
22
, Theorem 4 & Proposition 11]
)
.

Suppose that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are graphs without isolated vertices and
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are maximal independent sets in

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively. Then
I_{1}\times V(G_{2})
and
V(G_{1})\times I_{2}
are maximal independent sets in the
conjunction product of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Also if this conjuction product is well-covered, then

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are well-covered and
\alpha(G_{1})|G_{2}|=\alpha(G_{2})|G_{2}|.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
F
be a finite field and
n>1
. Then, for any finite ring
R
, the graph
\mathcal{G}\big(R\times M_{n}(F)\big)
is not
well-covered.

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
and
X
be the set of all non-unit elements of
M_{n}(F)
. We show that

N=(R\times\{0\})\cup(M\times X)
is a maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}\big(R\times M_{n}(F)\big)
. Noting that units of

R\times M_{n}(F)
are of the form
(a,b)
, such that
a\in\mathrm{U}(R)
and
b\in\mathrm{U}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
, it is not difficult to see that

N
is an independent set. If
N\cup\{(r,A)\}
is an independent set for some
(r,A)\in\big(R\times M_{n}(F)\big)\setminus N
,
then
(r,A)-(r-1,0)=(1,A)
implies that
A
is not a unit element of
M_{n}(F)
and hence
r\notin M
. Since
A\neq 0
,
utilizing Lemma
3.2
we can find some non-unit
B\in M_{n}(F)
such that
A-B
is invertible. Since
M
is
maximal and
r\notin M
, there is an
m\in M
such that
r-m\in\mathrm{U}(R)
. But then
(m,B)\in N
and

(r,A)-(m,B)=(r-m,A-B)
is a unit element of
R\times M_{n}(F)
, that is,
(r,A)
is adjacent to
(m,B)
, which is in
contradiction with the independence of
N\cup\{(r,A)\}
. Consequently,
N
is a maximal independent set.

Now, if
\mathcal{G}\big(R\times M_{n}(F)\big)
is
well-covered, according to Lemma
3.3
, we observe that

|R||F|^{n^{2}-n}=|M||F|^{n^{2}}
. Therefore, letting
|F|=q
, we have
|R|=q^{n}|M|
. Noting that

|\mathrm{U}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)|=(q^{n}-1)(q^{n}-q)\cdots(q^{n}-q^{n-1}),

and that
(R\times\{0\})\cap(M\times X)=M\times\{0\}
, it can be seen that:

\displaystyle|N|

\displaystyle=|R|+|M|\big(q^{n^{2}}-(q^{n}-1)\cdots(q^{n}-q^{n-1})-1\big)

\displaystyle=|M|\Big(q^{n^{2}}-(q^{n}-1)\big((q^{n}-q)\cdots(q^{n}-q^{n-1})-1\big)\Big)<|M|q^{n^{2}},

which is a contradiction, since by Lemma
3.3
,
M\times M_{n}(F)
is a maximal independent set of

\mathcal{G}\big(R\times M_{n}(F)\big)
. Hence the result follows.
∎

Now, we are ready to present the main results of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
R
be a finite ring. Then the unitary Cayley graph of
R
is well-covered if and only if one of the following holds:

(i)

R/J(R)
is isomorphic to
F
,
F\times F
, or
M_{2}(F)
for some finite field
F
;

(ii)

R/J(R)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

First by utilizing Proposition
3.1
, we see that
\mathcal{G}(R)
is well-covered if and only if so is

\mathcal{G}\big(R/J(R)\big)
. Since
R
is an Artinian ring and
J\big(R/J(R)\big)=0
, thus
R/J(R)
is a semisimple ring. Hence by Lemma
1.1
(
ii
) we have
R/J(R)\cong M_{n_{1}}(D_{1})\times\cdots\times M_{n_{r}}(D_{r})
for some division rings
D_{i}
and positive integers
n_{i}
. Also,
Wedderburn’s Little Theorem states that every finite division ring is a field and therefore so are the
D_{i}
’s. Now, if
all the
n_{i}
’s are equal to
1
, then
R/J(R)
is a commutative ring and the result follows from
[
11
, Theorem
2.3]
. Hence we assume that there is some
n_{i}>1
. Using Proposition
3.4
, one can observe that if
R/J(R)

is well-covered, then
R/J(R)\cong M_{n}(F)
for some finite field
F

and positive integer
n
. Now, by applying Theorem
2.6
the assertion follows.
∎

The following example shows that there are several non-commutative rings with a well-covered unitary Cayley graph.

Example 3.6
.

Let
R=T_{n}(F)
be the ring of upper triangular matrices over the finite field
F
. Since
J(R)
is the set of upper
triangular matrices which have zero on the main diagonal, we have
R/J(R)\cong F^{n}
. Thus
\mathcal{G}\big(T_{n}(F)\big)
is well-covered
if and only if either
n\leq 2
or
F=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. As another example note that
\mathbb{Z}_{m}/J(\mathbb{Z}_{m})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
, where
m^{\prime}
is the
squarefree part of
m
. Thus if we set
R=M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{m})
for some
n\geq 2
, then
R/J(R)\cong M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}})
and is
indecomposable if and only if
\mathbb{Z}_{m^{\prime}}
is so if and only if
m^{\prime}
is prime if and only if
m
is a prime power. Therefore,
\mathcal{G}(R)
is well-covered
if and only if
n=2
and
m
is a prime power. More generally, if
n\geq 2
, the ring
M_{n}(R)
for an arbitrary commutative ring

R
is well-covered if and only if
n=2
and
R
is a local ring, because

M_{n}(R)/J\big(M_{n}(R)\big)\cong M_{n}\big(R/J(R)\big)
is indecomposable if and only if
R/J(R)
is indecomposable if and only if
R/J(R)
is a field.

Next we characterize rings
R
with a CM unitary Cayley graph. For this, we need a necessary
combinatorial condition for Cohen-Macaulayness of graphs which is stated in terms of simplicial
complexes. Recall that a
simplicial complex
on vertex set
[n]
means a family
\Delta
of
subsets of
[n]
with the property that if
A\in\Delta
and
B\subseteq A
, then
B\in\Delta
. Each
element of
\Delta
is called a
face
of
\Delta
and maximal elements of
\Delta
are called

facets
of
\Delta
. Dimension of a face
A
of
\Delta
is
|A|-1
and if all facets of

\Delta
have the same dimension, then
\Delta
is called
pure
. Also
\dim\Delta
is
defined as the maximum dimension of all faces of
\Delta
. It is easy to see that if
G
is a graph
on vertex set
[n]
, then the family of all independent sets of
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{Ind}(G)
is a
simplicial complex called the
independence complex
of
G
. It is clear that
G
is
well-covered if and only if
\mathrm{Ind}(G)
is pure. Now assume that
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and for each
F\subseteq[n]
, let
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
and
I_{\Delta}=\langle x_{F}|F\subseteq[n],F\notin\Delta\rangle
be the

Stanley-Reisner ideal
of
\Delta
in
S
. It is said that
\Delta
is CM (over
K
), when

S/I_{\Delta}
is a CM ring. Thus it follows that a graph
G
is CM if and only if
\mathrm{Ind}(G)
is CM. A pure
simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
connected in codimension 1
, when for each two facets

F,G
of
\Delta
, there is a sequence
F=F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}=G
of facets of
\Delta
, such that

|F_{i-1}\cap F_{i}|=|F_{i}|-1
for each
1\leq i\leq t
. It is well-known that if
\Delta
is CM
over some field, then it is pure and connected in codimension 1 (see
[
8
, Lemma 9.1.12]
). Finally,
recall that the
pure
d
-skeleton

\Delta^{[d]}
of
\Delta
is the pure simplicial complex whose
facets are all
d
-dimensional faces of
\Delta
. Now we study when independence complex of

\mathcal{G}(R)
is connected in codimension 1.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
n\geq 2
,
\Delta
is the independence complex of
\mathcal{G}\big(M_{n}(F)\big)
and
d=\dim\Delta
, then

\Delta^{[d]}
is not connected in codimension 1.

Proof.

Let
R=M_{n}(F)
. Note that by Lemma
1.1
(
i
),
d=|F|^{n^{2}-n}-1
. Since the family of
matrices with a fixed zero row is a maximal independent set of
\mathcal{G}(R)
with size
d+1=|F|^{n^{2}-n}
,
we see that
\Delta
has more than one facet. Thus if
\Delta^{[d]}
is connected in codimension 1,
for each facet
A
of
\Delta
, there must exist a facet
B
of
\Delta
with
|A\cap B|=d
. Below we
show that this does not hold and hence the claim follows.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the family of all matrices with first row equal to zero and
M
be an arbitrary
element of
\mathcal{F}
. We prove that there is no independent set containing
\mathcal{F}\setminus\{M\}
other than

\mathcal{F}
. On the contrary, if
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is such an independent set, then there is a matrix
N\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with
nonzero first row
r_{1}\in F^{n}
. Extend
\{r_{1}\}
to two different bases
\{r_{1},r^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{n}\}
and
\{r_{1},r^{\prime\prime}_{2},\ldots,r^{\prime\prime}_{n}\}
of
F^{n}
. This is possible since
n\geq 2
. Suppose that the
i
-th row of
N
is
r_{i}
. Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be matrices with zero first rows
and
i
-th rows equal to
r_{i}-r^{\prime}_{i}
and
r_{i}-r^{\prime\prime}_{i}
, respectively. At least one of
X
and
X^{\prime}
, say

X
, is in
\mathcal{F}\setminus\{M\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. But then the rows of
N-X
are linearly independent and
N
is
adjacent to
X
in
\mathcal{G}(R)
, contradicting independence of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. From this the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
\Delta
is the independence complex of
\mathcal{G}(R)
and
d=\dim\Delta
. If
J(R)\neq 0
, then

\Delta^{[d]}
is not connected in codimension 1.

Proof.

According to Proposition
3.1
, facets of
\Delta^{[d]}
are of unions of cosets of
J(R)
in
R
. Thus at
least two such facets exist and if
A
and
B
are two such facets,
|A\cap B|
is a multiple of
|J(R)|
. But if

\Delta^{[d]}
is connected in codimension 1, for each facet
A
of
\Delta
, there must
exist a facet
B
of
\Delta
with
|A\cap B|=d=|A|-1
. This is not a multiple of
|J(R)|
, unless
|J(R)|=1
, as required.
∎

Now we can state when
\mathcal{G}(R)
is a CM graph. We should mention that for commutative rings, this result is proved in

[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
. Also recall that a simplicial complex
\Delta
is said to be
shellable
when its
facets can be sorted as
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
, such that for each
i>1
the simplicial complex
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}\rangle\cap\langle F_{i}\rangle
is pure and has dimension

\dim F_{i}-1
, where
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}\rangle
means the simplicial complex whose facets are exactly
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
. We say a graph is shellable whenever
its independence complex is so.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that
R
is a finite ring. The following are equivalent.

(i)

The unitary Cayley graph of
R
is CM.

(ii)

The unitary Cayley graph of
R
is well-covered and shellable.

(iii)

Either
R
is a field or
R\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(
iii
)
\Rightarrow
(
ii
) is part of
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
(note that there, by
shellable, it is meant pure shellable). (
ii
)
\Rightarrow
(
i
) is well-known (see, for example,

[
8
, Theorem 8.2.6]
). Now assume (
i
) holds. To prove (
iii
),
by Lemma
3.8
and
[
8
, Lemma 9.1.12]
, we may assume that
J(R)=0
.
If
R
is commutative, then (
iii
) holds by
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
. If
R
is a
non-commutative indecomposable ring, then
R\cong M_{n}(F)
for some
n\geq 2
and it follows from
Lemmas
3.7
and
2.6
that
\mathrm{Ind}\big(\mathcal{G}(R)\big)
is not a pure complex connected in
codimension 1 and hence it is not CM. Now if
R
is non-commutative but it is decomposable, then it
follows from Proposition
3.4
that
\mathcal{G}(R)
is not well-covered and hence it is not CM.
∎

We say that a graph
G
is
Gorenstein
(over
K
) when the ring
S/I(G)
is Gorenstein.

Corollary 3.10
.

For a finite ring
R
, the graph
\mathcal{G}(R)
is Gorenstein if and only if
R\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
3.9
, since every Gorenstein ring is CM and because of the well-known facts that the only complete Gorenstein graphs are
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
and
disjoint union of Gorenstein graphs is Gorenstein.
∎

It should be mentioned that it looks there is a small inaccuracy in

R
is a field, then
\mathcal{G}(R)
is Gorenstien. But
when

is a field,

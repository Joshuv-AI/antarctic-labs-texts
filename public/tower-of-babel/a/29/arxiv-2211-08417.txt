1.
Introduction

Given a graph

and an integer

-colouring

-colouring (a partition of

-acyclic colouring of

Grunbaum
[

introduced the concept of acyclic colourings, and A. V. Kostochka
[

proved that deciding whether G has

[
2

or to find upper bounds for its value.

Fertin

[
6

established a simple lower bound on the acyclic chromatic number: if

Alon
et al.

[

showed that there are graphs

for which
\chi_{a}(G)=\Omega\!\left(\frac{\Delta(G)^{4/3}}{(\log\Delta(G))^{1/3}}\right)
as

.
This follows from an analysis of random graphs in the Erdős–Rényi model

, where

of existing.

Upper bounds over

in terms of the maximum degree

have been studied for a few decades.
It has been established that, for any graph

of maximum degree
\Delta
, one has
\chi_{a}(G)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Delta^{4/3}\right)

[
1
,
14
,
7

. To our knowledge, the best known upper bound is given by Gonçalves, Montassier, and Pinlou: for every graph

of maximum degree

, one has
\chi_{a}(G)\leq\frac{3}{2}\Delta^{4/3}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Delta\right)

[
10
]
. This bound is tight up to a polylogarithmic factor, as certified by the lower bound described above
[
1
]

If moreover

[
5
]

K_{2,t}
-free graph

[
1
,
7
]
.
The bound in
[
7
]
is moreover constructive, and in particular there is a random algorithm that returns in expected polynomial time an acyclic

-colouring of any

-regular

-free graph

is within a factor

Motivated by the above observation, we have seeked for obstructions that let us have a constant approximation algorithm for the acyclic colouring problem over
d
-regular graphs. We show that even cycles of length at most

are such obstructions.
More generally, our work studies the impact of forbidding one or several fixed subgraphs

in a graph

on the value of
\chi_{a}(G)
. Given a fixed set of graphs
\mathscr{F}
, a graph G is called
\mathscr{F}
-free
if no graph

appears as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph of
G
. When

, we say that

-free.
We let

The general framework

2.1.
Structural properties of graphs with obstructions

We focus on the average degree of
F
-free graphs.

Lemma 1

(Folklore)
.

Every multigraph of average degree
d
contains a bipartite sub-multigraph of average degree at least
d/2
.

Lemma 2

(Folklore)
.

Every multigraph of average degree
d
contains a sub-multigraph of minimum degree at least
\left\lfloor d/2\right\rfloor+1
.

Theorem 1

(Erdős, Gallai, 1959
[
4
]
)
.

For every integer
t\geq 2
, if a given graph
G
is
P_{t}
-free, then its average degree is at most
t-2
.

Theorem
1
has been conjectured to hold more generally when
P_{t}
is replaced with any tree on
t
vertices.

Conjecture 1

(Erdős, Sós, 1963)
.

For every fixed integer
t\geq 2
, and every tree
T
on
t
vertices, if a graph
G
is
T
-free, then its average degree is at most
t-2
.

Ajtai, Komlós, Simonovits and Szemerédi have reportedly proved the Erdős-Sós conjecture when
t
is large enough. Their work is still in preparation for publication.
In order to avoid having to rely on a private result, we can use the following easy result instead.

Lemma 3
.

Let
T
be any fixed rooted tree on
t
vertices. If a graph
G
has minimum degree
t-1
, then there is a copy of
T
rooted in each vertex
v\in V(G)
in
G
.

Proof.

The proof is standard; we include it for completeness.
We construct a copy of
T
rooted in
v
greedily, by following a DFS ordering
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}

of
V(T)
.
We first fix
T_{1}\coloneqq\{v\}
, then for every
2\leq i\leq t
we construct
T_{i}
by adding
v_{i}
to the tree
T_{i-1}
already constructed, so that
T_{i}
is isomorphic to
T[\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\}]
. To do so, we need to find a neighbour of the parent node
v_{j}
of
v_{i}
in
T
(by assumption we have
j<i
) that is distinct from
V(T_{j})
. The existence is due to the fact that
v_{j}
has at most
i-2<t-1
neighbours in
T_{i-1}
and at least
t-1
neighbours in
G
. At the end of that process, we obtain a tree
T_{t}
rooted in
v
and isomorphic to
T
.
∎

By combining Lemma
2
and Lemma
3
, we obtain a
2
-approximation of the Erdős-Sós conjecture as a corollary, which will be sufficient for our applications in this paper.

Corollary 1
.

For every fixed forest
T
on
t
vertices, if a given graph
G
is
T
-free, then its average degree is at most
2(t-2)
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
F^{\prime}
be any properly
2
-coloured forest on
t-1
vertices, and let
F
be the bipartite graph obtained from
F^{\prime}
by adding a new vertex adjacent to one colour class. Then every
F
-free graph
G
on
n
vertices has average degree
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\sqrt{tn}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
F_{0}
be the colour class of
F^{\prime}
that is linked to the new vertex in
F
.

Let
G
be an
F
-free bipartite graph, and let
d
be its average degree.
By combining Lemmas
1
and
2
, there exists a bipartite subgraph
G_{0}
of
G
of minimum degree at least
\left\lfloor d/4\right\rfloor+1
. Let
v_{0}\in V(G_{0})
be any fixed vertex, and let
G_{1}=G[N(v_{0}),N^{2}(v_{0})]
be the bipartite subgraph induced by the first and second neighbourhoods of
v_{0}
.

On the one hand, we have
|E(G_{1})|\geq|N(v_{0})|(d/4-1)\geq d/4(d/4-1)
, hence the average degree of
G_{1}
is at least
\frac{2|E(G_{1})|}{n-1}\geq\frac{d(d/4-1)}{2n-2}
. We infer that there exists a sub graph
G_{2}
of
G_{1}
of minimum degree at least
\frac{d(d/4-1)}{4n-4}
.

On the other hand, if
G_{2}
had minimum degree at least
t-3
, then by Lemma
3
one could find a copy of
F^{\prime}
in
G_{2}
such that
F_{0}\subseteq N(v_{0})
, which together with
v_{0}
would yield a copy of
F
in
G
. This is a contradiction, so the minimum degree of

is at most

. We conclude that

, as desired.
∎

2.2.
Main technical theorem

The proof of our main theorem is an abstract generalisation of that of
[
7
, Theorem 2]
, where we have replaced the compression entropy machinery with an inductive counting approach. We have made that choice because the resulting proof is shorter, less technical, and self-contained. Moreover, as a byproduct we obtain exponential lower bounds for the number of colourings satisfying the hypothesis of the theorem.
We note however that it would be possible to do the same using entropy compression, and obtain a random algorithm that returns such a colouring in expected polynomial time.

We begin with the introduction of a few notions that will be needed in order to formulate the theorem, which needs a high level of generality so that we can apply it in the variety of applications appearing in the forthcoming sections.

Given a graph
G
, a set of
constraints

\Gamma
consists in a set of pairs of vertices of
G
. For a given vertex
v\in V(G)
, we let
N_{\Gamma}(v)
consist of all vertices that form a pair in
\Gamma
together with
v
. The
constraint-degree of
v
is
\deg_{\Gamma}(v)\coloneqq|N_{\Gamma}(v)|
, and the
maximum degree of
\Gamma
is
\Delta(\Gamma)\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(G)}\deg_{\Gamma}(v)
.
Given an integer
k\geq 1
, a
\Gamma
-proper
k
-colouring of
G
is a mapping
\phi\colon V(G)\to[k]
such that
\phi(u)\neq\phi(v)
for every
\{u,v\}\in\Gamma
. For instance, an
E(G)
-proper
k
-colouring of
G
coincides with the usual notion of proper
k
-colouring. More generally, letting
H
be the graph on vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
\Gamma
, a
\Gamma
-proper
k
-colouring of
G
is a proper
k
-colouring of
H
.

Given an even cycle
C=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-1})
in
G
and a
k
-colouring
\phi
of
G
(which may be improper), we say that
C
is
bicoloured
in
\phi
if
\phi(v_{i})=\phi(v_{i+2\bmod 2\ell})
for every
i\in[2\ell]
.
We say that
C
is
\Gamma
-free
if
\{v_{i},v_{i+2\bmod 2\ell}\}\notin\Gamma
for every
i\in[2\ell]
.
Given a set
\Pi
of cycles in
G
, we say that
\phi
is
\Pi
-acyclic if no cycle in
\Pi
is bicoloured in
\phi
.
Note that, if
E(G)\subseteq\Gamma
, and if
\Pi
is the set of
\Gamma
-free cycles of
G
, then any
\Gamma
-proper
\Pi
-acyclic colouring of
G
is in particular a proper acyclic colouring of
G
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a graph,
\Gamma
a set of constraints, and
\Pi
a set of cycles of
G
.
Fix
\tau>1
, and

K:=\Delta(\Gamma)+\tau+\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\limits\frac{\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi)}{\tau^{2\ell-3}}.

Then there exist at least
\tau^{|V(G)|}

\Gamma
-proper
\Pi
-acyclic
K
-colourings of
G
.

Proof.

For any subgraph
H\subseteq G
, we denote
X[H]\coloneqq X\cap 2^{V(H)}
for any family of subsets of vertices
X
of
G
. We let
\mathcal{A}(H)
be the set of
\Gamma[H]
-proper
\Pi[H]
-acyclic
K
-colourings of
H
. By convention, if
H
is the empty graph,
\mathcal{A}(H)
contains a single trivial colouring. We show the following property for any subgraph
H\subseteq G
by strong induction:

(IH
2
)

\forall v_{0}\in V(H),\quad\left|\mathcal{A}(H)\right|\geq\tau\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|.

If
V(H)
is empty, (
IH
) is trivially true. Suppose
V(H)\neq\emptyset
and let
v_{0}\in V(H)
. By induction, assume (
IH
) is true for all strict subgraph
H^{\prime}\subset H
. We define
\mathcal{F}
, the set of
flawed extensions
, as the subset of
K
-colourings of
H
such that every colouring
\phi\in\mathcal{F}
is
\Gamma[H-v_{0}]
-proper
\Pi[H-v_{0}]
-acyclic, but contains a conflict due to the colour given to
v_{0}
. In symbols,
\phi\in\mathcal{F}
if and only if
{\phi}_{|H-v_{0}}\in\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})
and
\phi\notin\mathcal{A}(H)
.
We distinguish two types of conflict.

(a)

There is a vertex
u\in V(H)
such that
\{u,v_{0}\}\in\Gamma
and
\phi(u)=\phi(v_{0})
.

(b)

v_{0}
is contained in a bicoloured cycle of
\Pi
.

We let
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and
\mathcal{F}_{b}
be the subsets of colourings that respectively contain a conflict of type (a) or of type (b). These two subsets are not necessarily disjoint.

By definition of
\mathcal{F}
, we have have
\left|\mathcal{A}(H)\right|=K\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|-\left|\mathcal{F}\right|
, and
\left|\mathcal{F}\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{F}_{a}\right|+\left|\mathcal{F}_{b}\right|
.

To complete the proof of the induction, we show these two following points:

(i)

\displaystyle\;\left|\mathcal{F}_{a}\right|\leq\Delta(\Gamma)\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\;\left|\mathcal{F}_{b}\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi)}{\tau^{2\ell-3}}
.

Proof of point (i).

Let
c\in\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})
. By definition,
v_{0}
belongs to at most
\Delta(\Gamma)
constraints in
\Gamma
, hence there at most
\Delta(\Gamma)
flawed extensions of
c
that induce a conflict of type
(a)
. Therefore, we have
\left|\mathcal{F}_{a}\right|\leq\Delta(\Gamma)\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|
.
∎

Proof of point (ii).

For
\ell\geq 2
, let
\Omega_{\ell}
be the set of cycles in
\Pi
of length
2\ell
which contain
v_{0}
. By asumption,
|\Omega_{\ell}|\leq\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi)
.
For a cycle
C\in\Omega_{\ell}
, we define
\mathcal{F}_{C}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{b}
the subset of colourings for which
C
is effectively bicoloured. Clearly, we have
\mathcal{F}_{b}=\bigcup_{\ell\geq 2}\bigcup_{C\in\Omega_{\ell}}\mathcal{F}_{C}
.

Let
C=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-1})\in\Omega_{\ell}
. We construct an injection from
\mathcal{F}_{C}
to
\mathcal{A}(H\setminus\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-3}\})
. Let
\phi\colon\mathcal{F}_{C}\to\mathcal{A}(H\setminus\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-3}\})
be the application that simply uncolours
v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-3}
(uncolouring
v_{0}
ensures that all conflicts are resolved). The inverse application
\phi^{-1}
colours
v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-3}
by alternating the colours of
v_{2\ell-1}
and
v_{2\ell-2}
, which by definition is the only way to ensure that
C
is bicoloured. Therefore,
\left|\mathcal{F}_{C}\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{A}(H\setminus\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-3}\})\right|
.

We apply the induction hypothesis (
IH
) iteratively on the vertices
v_{2\ell-3},\ldots,v_{1}
in that order, and obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{C}\right|\leq\left|\mathcal{A}(H\setminus\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-3}\})\right|\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\left|\mathcal{A}(H\setminus\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-4}\})\right|\leq\cdots\leq\frac{1}{\tau^{2\ell-3}}\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|.

Finally, since
\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi)
corresponds to the number of cycles
C\in\Pi
of length
2\ell
that contain a fixed vertex
v_{0}\in V(G)
, we conclude that

\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{F}_{b}\right|&\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\sum_{C\in\Omega_{\ell}}\left|\mathcal{F}_{C}\right|\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\sum_{C\in\Omega_{\ell}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2\ell-3}}\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi)}{\tau^{2\ell-3}}\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|.\end{split}

∎

A straightforward application of
(i)
and
(ii)
completes the proof of the induction:

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}(H)\right|

\displaystyle\geq K\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|-\left|\mathcal{F}_{a}\right|-\left|\mathcal{F}_{b}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left(K-\Delta(\Gamma)-\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi)}{\tau^{2\ell-3}}\right)\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\geq\tau\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v_{0})\right|.

An iterative application of (
IH
) to every
v\in V(G)
implies that
\left|\mathcal{A}(G)\right|\geq\tau^{\left|V(G)\right|}>1
.
∎

3.
Trimming the space of obstructions

The following theorem states that if a graph
F
in the family of obstructions
\mathscr{F}
has several connected components, then only one of them sensibly affects the value of
a(d,\mathscr{F})
. Therefore, it suffices to study the asymptotic behaviour of this function in the case where
F
is connected.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
F
has connected components
(F_{i})_{i\in[r]}
. There exists a constant
c_{F}
such that for any
d\geq 0
,

\max_{i\in[r]}a(d,F_{i})\;\leq\;a(d,F)\;\leq\;\max_{i\in[r]}a(d,F_{i})+c_{F}.

Proof.

First, we prove the lower bound.
Let
d\geq 0
. Let
j\in[r]
be such that
a(d,F_{j})=\max\limits_{i\in[r]}a(d,F_{i})
. Let
G
be an
F_{j}
-free graph with
\Delta(G)\leq d
such that
\chi_{a}(G)=a(d,F_{j})
. since
G
must also be
F
-free, we have
\chi_{a}(G)\leq a(d,F)
.

Now, we focus on the upper bound.
We explicitly show that for any
d\geq 0
, we have

(1)

a(d,F)\leq\max_{i\in[r]}a(d,F_{i})+\left|V(F)\right|.

We proceed by induction on
r
, the number of connected components of
F
.

The base case
r=1
is trivial (applying the same argument for the lower bound). Suppose now that
r\geq 2
. Let
d\geq 0
. Let
G
be an
F
-free graph with
\Delta(G)\leq d
such that
\chi_{a}(G)=a(d,F)
. Consider whether
G
is
F_{r-1}
-free or not:

•

If
G
is
F_{r-1}
-free, then
\chi_{a}(G)\leq a(d,F_{r-1})
, which clearly proves inequality (
1
).

•

Otherwise,
G
contains a subgraph
G_{0}
isomorphic to
F_{r-1}
, thus
G\setminus G_{0}
must be
(F\setminus F_{r-1})
-free, therefore,
\chi_{a}(G\setminus G_{0})\leq a(d,F\setminus F_{r-1})
. Since

has

connected components, namely
F_{0},\dots,F_{r-2}
, we apply the induction hypothesis and obtain

a(d,F\setminus F_{r-1})\leq\max_{i\in[r-1]}a(d,F_{i})+\left|V(F\setminus F_{r-1})\right|.

Given a
k
-acyclic colouring of

, we can construct a
(k+\left|V(G_{0})\right|)
-acyclic colouring of

by assigning a new unique colour to each vertex of

. Since
\left|V(G_{0})\right|=\left|V(F_{r-1})\right|
, and
\left|V(F\setminus F_{r-1})\right|+\left|V(F_{r-1})\right|=\left|V(F)\right|
, we have

\chi_{a}(G)\leq a(d,F\setminus F_{r-1})+\left|V(F_{r-1})\right|\leq\max_{i\in[r-1]}a(d,F_{i})+\left|V(F)\right|.

Clearly,

∎

The next theorem indicates that we only need to consider the case where is

Theorem 4
.

If
F
is not bipartite, or if
\left|E(F)\right|>4\left|V(F)\right|
, then

a(d,F)=\Omega\!\left(\frac{d^{4/3}}{(\ln d)^{1/3}}\right).

Proof.

We adapt the proof in
[
1
]
to work on the bipartite random graph model
G=G(n,n,p)
. Let
A
and
B
be the parts of
V(G)
(each of size
n
). Let

. If
F
is not bipartite, then

is obviously
F
-free. The probability that

contains a subgraph isomorphic to

is bounded by
(2n)^{\left|V(F)\right|}p^{\left|E(F)\right|}=\mathcal{O}\!\left(n^{\left|V(F)\right|-\left|E(F)\right|/4}(\log n)^{\left|E(F)\right|/4}\right)

\left|E(F)\right|>4\left|V(F)\right|

as

Let

, and let

be any partition of

into

. Let us write
A_{i}:=V_{i}\cap A
and
B_{i}:=V_{i}\cap B

of odd cardinality, we obtain sub-parts

of even cardinality, which cover at least

. We split every non-empty set

into subsets of size

. We obtain

A^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ldots,A^{\prime\prime}_{k_{A}}

k_{B}\geq\frac{n}{4}

of vertices in
B

Observe that, for every
(i,j)\in[k_{A}]\times[k_{B}]

in

be that event, which occurs with probability

There are at most

Therefore,

almost surely.
The degree of each vertex follows the binomial distribution

∎

In particular

4.
Constant upper bound

In this section, we want to characterise obstructions that force to have a bounded acyclic chromatic number.
The
1
-subdivision
of a graph

, denoted by
G^{(1)}
, is obtained by replacing each edge in
G
by a path of length
2
. Any graph

with
\Delta(G)\geq 2
has
\Delta(G)=\Delta(G^{(1)})
.
In 2008,
Dvořák
[

]
gave a complete characterisation of the graphs of bounded acyclic chromatic number through the analysis of
1
-subdivisions.

Theorem 5

(Dvořák)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of graphs of bounded chromatic number.

\mathcal{G}
has bounded acyclic chromatic number if and only if there exists a constant
c
such that for any graph
H
, if
H^{(1)}
is a subgraph of a graph in
\mathcal{G}
then
\chi(H)\leq c
.

As a consequence of this theorem, we obtain a constant upper bound on the acyclic chromatic number of
F
-free graphs when
F
is a subgraph of a
1
-subdivided tree (said otherwise,
F
is a forest in which no pair of vertices of degree at least
3
are linked by a path of odd length).

Theorem 6
.

Let
F\subseteq T^{(1)}
where
T
is a tree. There exists a constant
c_{F}
such that for all

,

a(d,F)\leq c_{F}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the non-empty class of
F
-free graphs, which have chromatic number at most
\left|V(F)\right|-1
. Let
H
be a graph and suppose there exists
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that
H^{(1)}\subseteq G
. So, by assumption,
G
is
F
-free graph, and
H^{(1)}
is
F
-free, which in turn implies that
H^{(1)}
is
T^{(1)}
-free.
H
must be
T
-free, otherwise

, thus

, a contradiction. Therefore,
\chi(H)\leq\left|V(T)\right|-1
. We conclude the proof using Theorem
5
.
∎

Theorem

completely characterises the graphs

for which

is bounded by a constant. Wood
[

]
determined bounds on the acyclic chromatic number of graphs subdivisions, and in particular showed that

Observe that

is

-free for every graph

that is not a subgraph of a

-subdivided tree. So by definition, we have

, and we can derive the following.

Proposition 1
.

If

is not a subgraph of any 1-subdivided tree, then

a(d,F)>\sqrt{\frac{d+1}{2}}

5.
Sublinear upper bound

In this section, we show that for any fixed forest

,

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be a
t
-degenerate graph of maximum degree
\Delta
. Then

\chi_{a}(G)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(t^{8/3}\Delta^{2/3}\right).

Proof.

Since

is

of

where each vertex has out-degree at most
t

is a path/cycle of

which contains no directed subpath of length
2
in
\vec{G}
. Note that an antidirected cycle is necessarily even.
Let
\Gamma_{0}
be the set of pairs of vertices linked by a directed path of length at most
2
in
\vec{G}
, and let
\Pi
be the set of antidirected cycles.

The graph
(V(G),\Gamma_{0})
is
(t^{2}+t)

\Gamma_{0}
-proper
(t^{2}+t+1)

.
Now, we will construct an improper
\Pi
-acyclic
\mathcal{O}\!\left((t\Delta)^{2/3}\right)

\phi_{1}
of
G
.
Observe that if there exists such a colouring, the Cartesian product of
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
yields a proper acyclic

\mathcal{O}\!\left(t^{8/3}\Delta^{2/3}\right)

, and the theorem holds.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
consist of all pairs of vertices
\{u,v\}
such that
\deg_{\vec{G}}^{-}(u,v)\geq(t\Delta)^{1/3}
.

Claim 1
.

The maximum constraint-degree is
\Delta(\Gamma_{1})\leq(t\Delta)^{2/3}
.

Proof.

Let
v_{0}\in V(G)
, and let
A_{0}
be the set of out-going arcs from
N^{-}(v_{0})
.

On the one hand, each vertex
w\in N^{-}(v_{0})
is incident to at most
t
arcs from
A_{0}
by assumption on the maximum out-degree of
\vec{G}
. Thus
t\left|N^{-}(v_{0})\right|\geq|A_{0}|
. On the other hand, each vertex
u\in N_{\Gamma_{1}}(v)
is incident to at least

(t\Delta)^{1/3}
arcs from
A_{0}
by definition of
\Gamma_{1}
. Hence we have
t\left|N^{-}(v_{0})\right|\geq|A_{0}|\geq(t\Delta)^{1/3}\left|N_{\Gamma_{1}}(v_{0})\right|
.

Since
\Delta\geq\left|N^{-}(v_{0})\right|
and
\deg_{\Gamma_{1}}(v_{0})=\left|N_{\Gamma_{1}}(v_{0})\right|

\deg_{\Gamma_{1}}(v_{0})\leq(d\Delta)^{2/3}
.
∎

We now let
\Pi_{1}\subseteq\Pi
be the set of
\Gamma_{1}
-free antidirected cycles in
\vec{G}
.

Claim 2
.

\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi_{1})\leq\frac{1}{2}(t\Delta)^{\ell-2/3}
, for every integer
\ell\geq 2

Proof.

Let
v_{0}\in V(G)
.

The number of antidirected paths of length
2\ell-2
that begin with an in-going arc from
v_{0}
is at most
\deg^{-}(v_{0})t^{\ell-1}\Delta^{\ell-2}
, since there are at most
t

\vec{G}
. Given such a path
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-2})
, if we assume that
\{v_{0},v_{2\ell-2}\}\notin\Gamma_{1}
, there are at most
(t\Delta)^{1/3}

2\ell
. So the number of
\Gamma_{1}
-free antidirected

is at most
\frac{1}{2}\deg^{-}(v_{0})t^{\ell-2/3}\Delta^{\ell-5/3}
, where we have divided the bound by

because each cycle is counted twice in the previous process, depending on the direction in which we construct it.
On the other hand, the number of antidirected paths of length
2\ell-3
that begin with an out-going arc of

is at most
\deg^{+}(v_{0})(t\Delta)^{\ell-2}

(v_{0},\ldots,v_{2\ell-4})

\deg^{+}(v_{0})(t\Delta)^{1/3}
if we assume that
\{v_{2\ell-3},v_{2\ell-1}\}\notin\Gamma_{1}
. The number of
\Gamma_{1}
-free antidirected
2\ell
-cycles that contain an out-going edge from

is therefore at most
\frac{1}{2}\deg^{+}(v_{0})t^{\ell-2/3}\Delta^{\ell-5/3}
.
Overall, the number of
\Gamma_{1}
-free antidirected

is at most
\frac{1}{2}(\deg^{+}(v_{0})+\deg^{-}(v_{0}))t^{\ell-2/3}\Delta^{\ell-5/3}\leq\frac{1}{2}(t\Delta)^{\ell-2/3}
.
∎

We may now apply Theorem
2
on the graph

, with the set of constraints
\Gamma_{1}
and the set of cycles
\Pi_{1}
, after fixing
\tau\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(t\Delta)^{2/3}
. We obtain that there are at least
\tau^{|V(G)|}

\Gamma_{1}
-proper
\Pi_{1}
-acyclic

-colourings of

, where

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\Delta(\Gamma_{1})+\tau+\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{\Delta_{2\ell}(\Pi_{1}))}{\tau^{2\ell-3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}(t\Delta)^{2/3}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\sum_{\ell\geq 2}(t\Delta)^{4/3-\ell/3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{2}(t\Delta)^{2/3}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{(t\Delta)^{2/3}}{1-(t\Delta)^{-1/3}}=\left(1+\sqrt{2}\right)(t\Delta)^{2/3}+\mathcal{O}\!\left((t\Delta)^{1/3}\right),\hskip-100.0pt

.

We finish the proof by observing that a

-acyclic

-acyclic
K
-colouring of

.
∎

Let
F
be a forest on
t
vertices, and let

be a tree obtained by adding edges between the connected components of

.
If a graph
G
is not
(t-2)
-degenerate, then it contains a subgraph

. By Lemma

,

, and therefore also

We conclude that any

-free graph

is

-degenerate, hence we have the following result as a corollary.

Corollary 2
.

For every forest

on

6.
Obstructions for the linear upper bound

Having established that
a(d,F)=\mathcal{O}\!\left(d^{2/3}\right)
whenever

is a forest, we now consider obstructions that contain a cycle.

Proposition 2
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and let
F
be a graph that contains a cycle. Then

a(d,F)>\frac{d}{2}+1.

Proof.

For
d\geq 2
and
g\geq 3
, the existence of
d
-regular graphs of girth

is well known (see
[
13
]
for example). By taking
g
large enough, we obtain a graph
G
that is
F
-free and

-regular, and therefore satisfies
\chi_{a}(G)>\frac{d}{2}+1
.
∎

It has already been established in
[
1
]
that
K_{2,t}
-free graphs of maximum degree
\Delta
have acyclic chromatic number at most
\mathcal{O}\!\left(\sqrt{t}\Delta\right)
. When

is fixed, this bound is tight up to a multiplicative constant as a result of the previous theorem.

We recall that a graph
F
is

-acyclic if there exists

is a forest.

Lemma 5
.

Let
F
be a fixed

-acyclic bipartite graph with

for any integer

.

Let

. There are at most
\Delta^{2\ell-3}

(2\ell-3)

. Let

be the other endpoint of such a path; we claim that there are at most
4(t-3)\Delta

3

and

in

. We now prove the claim.

We assume that

and

to

-paths from

to

. Let

be such that

is

. We now prove a similar result for

,

and

We say that a neighbour of

is trivial if it is isolated in

. Let
(F_{i})_{i\in[r]}

. Define

by adding an edge between

The special case of cycle obstructions

The linear bound defined of Theorem

7.1.
C_{4}
-free graphs

We begin with a bound of the number of cycles that contain a fixed vertex in

-free graphs.

Lemma 6
.

Given a
C_{4}
-free graph
G
, let
\Omega_{G}
denote its set of cycles. Then

\Delta_{2\ell}(\Omega_{G})\leq\frac{\Delta}{2}(\Delta-1)^{2\ell-3},

for any integer

Proof.

Let
v_{0}\in V(G)
be a fixed vertex, and
\ell\geq 3
a fixed integer. There are at most
\Delta(\Delta-1)^{2\ell-3}
paths of length
2\ell-2
starting from

, which can be computed by performing a BFS of depth
2\ell-2
from
v_{0}
. For each of these paths, there is a unique way to close it into a
2\ell
-cycle with a common neighbour of its extremities, since
G
is
C_{4}
-free (if two vertices have two common neighbours, then they form a
C_{4}
). With this enumeration, each cycle is counted twice (clockwise and anticlockwise), hence we divide the total by 2.
∎

We note that the bound provided by Lemma
6
is asymptotically tight, since in the incidence graph
G
of a projective plane, of maximum degree

Lemma 7

Given a bipartite graph

7.3.
Going below the
2\Delta
threshold

When
\mathscr{F}
is a collection of cycles, it seems that, given an
\mathscr{F}
-free graph
G
, there is no better general upper bound on
\Delta_{2\ell}(\Omega_{G})
than
\Delta(G)^{2\ell-C_{\mathscr{F}}}
for some constant
C_{\mathscr{F}}
. In particular, if we wish to apply Theorem
2
in order to obtain an upper bound on
a(d,\mathscr{F})
, then we need to have
\tau>d
. Since we have
\Delta(\Gamma)=d
in that setting, there is no hope to obtain an upper bound below
2d
.
We show, with a more involved technique that uses properties of proper colouring in sparse graphs, that it is possible to obtain an upper bound below that threshold for a small family of cycles
\mathscr{F}
.

Theorem 11
.

For every graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
and of girth (at least)
7

\chi_{a}(G)\leq\frac{\Delta}{W(1)}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\sqrt{\Delta}\right)<1.7633\,\Delta+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\sqrt{\Delta}\right),

Proof of Theorem
11
.

Let
G
be a graph of maximum degree
\Delta\geq 3
and girth at least 7.

We fix
\alpha:=1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}
,
\tau:=\alpha\Delta
, and
\sigma:=\frac{1}{2(\alpha^{5}-\alpha^{3})}+\frac{2}{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}-\alpha}\right)^{2}

K:=\left\lceil\frac{\tau+\sigma}{W(1)}+\sqrt{\Delta}\right\rceil
.

Using the fact that
\alpha^{p+2}-\alpha^{p}\geq 2(\alpha-1)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\Delta}}
for all

For any subgraph

The more cycles we consider, the more constraints we impose to the colouring of

Fact 1
.

Let

For a given vertex

•

the cycles of
\Pi
which do not contain
v
.

•

the cycles of
\Pi
which contain at most one neighbour of

Since any cycle containing

necessarily contains at least two neighbours of

Fact 2

\Pi\boxminus v\subseteq\Pi\minuso v

We show the following property for any subgraph

by strong induction:

(IH
11

\displaystyle\forall v\in V(H),\forall\Pi\subseteq\Omega_{H},\quad\left|\mathcal{A}(H,\Pi)\right|\geq\tau\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v,\Pi\boxminus v)\right|.

If

and let

,

By induction, assume (

) is true for all strict subgraph

.

Consider the set
\mathcal{A}(H,\Pi\boxminus v)
, which contains the proper colourings of

such that any cycle of

which happens to be bicoloured must contain at least two neighbours of

, i.e. belongs to

To complete the proof of the induction, we show the following inequalities.

(i)

\left|\mathcal{A}(H,\Pi\boxminus v)\right|\geq\;(\tau+\sigma)\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v,\Pi\boxminus v)\right|
.

(ii)

\left|\mathcal{F}\right|\leq\;\sigma\left|\mathcal{A}(H-v,\Pi\boxminus v)\right|

Proof of Inequality (i).

The proof is inspired by

Let
\mathbf{c}
be a random variable with uniform distribution on

For a colouring

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}(H,\Pi\boxminus v)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{A}(H-v,\Pi\boxminus v)}\ell_{c}

Under the condition of event

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{\mathbf{c}}\right]

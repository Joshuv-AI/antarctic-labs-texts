The Modified Toda Hierarchy
Wenjuan RUI ab, Wenchuang GUAN a, Yi YANG c and Jipeng CHENG ab

Abstract
In this paper, modified Toda (mToda) equation is generalized to form an integrable hierarchy in the framework of Sato theory, which is therefore called mToda hierarchy. Inspired by the fact that Toda hierarchy is 2-component generalization of usual KP hierarchy, mToda hierarchy is constructed from bilinear equations of 2-component first modified KP hierarchy, where we provide the corresponding equivalence with Lax formulations. Then it is demonstrated that there are Miura links between Toda and mToda hierarchies, which means the definition of mToda hierarchy here is reasonable. Finally, Darboux transformations of the Toda and mToda hierarchies are also constructed by using the aforementioned Miura links.

\displaystyle{}L_{2}=\bar{u}_{-1}(s,\mathbf{x})\Lambda^{-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{u}_{i}(s,\mathbf{x})\Lambda^{i},\qquad\bar{u}_{-1}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}^{(1)}}L_{j}=\bigl[\bigl(L_{1}^{n}\bigr)_{\Delta,\geq 1},L_{j}\bigr],\qquad\partial_{x_{n}^{(2)}}L_{j}=\bigl[\bigl(L_{2}^{n}\bigr)_{\Delta^{*},\geq 1},L_{j}\bigr],\qquad j=1,2,

, while for Toda hierarchy, it is just

Formal difference operators

In this section, let us review some important properties about formal difference operators, which will be used in the discussion of mToda Lax equations. Symbols involving
\Delta
and
\Delta^{*}
used here can be found in
[
14

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the algebra of smooth complex functions in the indeterminate
s
and variables
\mathbf{x}
. The space
\mathcal{A}\bigl[\bigl[\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}\bigr]\bigr]
of formal difference operators consists of all expressions of the form

\displaystyle A=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}(s)\Lambda^{m}.

Define the following symbols for above
A

\displaystyle A_{P}=\sum_{\text{$m$ satisfies $P$}}a_{m}(s)\Lambda^{m},\qquad A_{[k]}=a_{k}(s)\Lambda^{k},\qquad A^{*}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda^{-m}a_{m}(s),

\mathcal{A}((\Lambda))
and
\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Lambda^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)
denote the subspace of

\mathcal{A}\bigl[\bigl[\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}\bigr]\bigr]
, whose elements take
the following forms, respectively:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=m_{0}}^{\infty}a_{m}(s)\Lambda^{m}\in\mathcal{A}((\Lambda)),\qquad\sum_{m=-\infty}^{n_{0}}a_{m}(s)\Lambda^{m}\in\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Lambda^{-1}\bigr)\bigr),

for some fixed integers
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
.
Both
\mathcal{A}((\Lambda))
and
\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Lambda^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)
are the associative
rings, where the multiplication is defined by

\displaystyle\bigl(f(s)\Lambda^{i}\bigr)\bigl(g(s)\Lambda^{j}\bigr)=f(s)g(s+i)\Lambda^{i+j}.

Given
A\in\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Lambda^{\pm 1}\bigr)\bigr)
and
f\in\mathcal{A}
, we use
Af
or
A\cdot f
to denote the multiplication of
A
with
f
, while
A(f)
means the action of
A
on
f
.

Introduce the difference operator
\Delta=\Lambda-1
and its adjoint operator
\Delta^{*}=\Lambda^{-1}-1
, and define the following multiplication operations as follows
[
14
]
, for

:

\displaystyle{}\Delta^{j}\cdot f(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{j}{i}(\Delta^{i}(f))(s+j-i)\Delta^{j-i},

\displaystyle{}\Delta^{*j}\cdot f(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{j}{i}(\Delta^{*i}(f))(s+j-i)\Delta^{*j-i}.

Then two associative rings
\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)
and
\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{*-1}\bigr)\bigr)
are of the following forms:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\infty}^{k_{0}}b_{m}(s)\Delta^{m}\in\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{-1}\bigr)\bigr),\qquad\sum_{m=-\infty}^{l_{0}}b_{m}(s)\Delta^{*m}\in\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{*}\bigr)^{-1}\bigr),

for some fixed integers
k_{0}
and
l_{0}
.
If define the following expansions
for
Q\in\big\{\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}\big\}
and
R\in\{\Delta,\Delta^{*}\}
by

\displaystyle(Q-1)^{-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}Q^{-j},\qquad(R+1)^{-k}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{-k}{j}R^{-k-j},\qquad k>0,

(2.1)

then for arbitrary formal difference operator
A\in\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Lambda^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)
(resp.
\mathcal{A}((\Lambda))
), it can be transformed into
A\in\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)

(
resp.
\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{*}\bigr)^{-1}\bigr)
)
by (
2.1
) and
\Lambda=\Delta+1

(
resp.
\Lambda^{-1}=\Delta^{*}+1
)
, and vice versa.
Then we have

\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Lambda^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)=\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{-1}\bigr)\bigr),\qquad\mathcal{A}((\Lambda))=\mathcal{A}\bigl(\bigl(\Delta^{*}\bigr)^{-1}\bigr).

Similar to
A_{P}
with
P\in\{\geq k,\leq k,>k,<k\}
and

, we can also define

(or

(

Lemma 2.1
.

For any formal difference operator
A\in\mathcal{A}\bigl[\bigl[\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}\bigr]\bigr]
,

\displaystyle A_{\geq 0}=A_{\Delta,\geq 0},\qquad

\displaystyle A_{\leq 0}=A_{\Delta^{*},\geq 0},

\displaystyle A_{\Delta,[0]}=A_{\geq 0}(1),\qquad

\displaystyle A_{\Delta^{*},[0]}=A_{\leq 0}(1).

Proof.

Firstly, by (
2.1
), we can find that
\Lambda^{-k}
,
k>0
can not produce non-negative
\Delta
-powers, therefore
if assume

\displaystyle A_{\geq 0}=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\Lambda^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}(\Delta+1)^{i}=A_{\Delta,\geq 0}.

If further apply

to

A_{\Delta,[0]}=A_{\geq 0}(1)
. Others can be similarly proved.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any formal difference operator
A\in\mathcal{A}\bigl[\bigl[\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}\bigr]\bigr]
,

\displaystyle{}\bigl(A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{*-1}\bigr)_{\geq 1}\cdot\Delta^{*}=A_{\geq 1}-A_{\geq 1}|_{\Lambda=1}=A_{\Delta,\geq 1},

\displaystyle{}\bigl(A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{*-1}\bigr)_{\leq 0}\cdot\Delta^{*}=A_{<0}+A_{\geq 0}|_{\Lambda=1}=A_{\Delta,\leq 0},

\displaystyle{}\bigl(A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{*-1}\bigr)_{\geq 1}\cdot\Delta^{*}=A_{>0}+A_{\leq 0}|_{\Lambda=1}=A_{\Delta^{*},\leq 0},

\displaystyle{}\bigl(A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{*-1}\bigr)_{\leq 0}\cdot\Delta^{*}=A_{<0}-A_{<0}|_{\Lambda=1}=A_{\Delta^{*},\geq 1}.

Proof.

Firstly, by Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\bigl(A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{*-1}\bigr)_{\geq 1}\cdot\Delta^{*}

\displaystyle{}=\bigl(A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{-1}\bigr)_{\geq 0}\cdot\Delta=\bigl(A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{-1}\bigr)_{\Delta,\geq 0}\cdot\Delta

\displaystyle{}=\bigl(A_{\Delta,\geq 1}\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{-1}\bigr)\cdot\Delta=A_{\Delta,\geq 1}.

If subtract
A
from the first relation, we can obtain the second one.
The third and fourth formulas can be similarly obtained.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any formal difference operator

and any

\displaystyle{}\Delta^{k}\cdot A\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-k}=\Delta^{*k}\cdot\Lambda^{k}(A)\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{*-k},

\displaystyle{}\bigl(\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{-k}\cdot A^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k}\bigr)_{\Delta^{*},\geq k}=\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{-k}\cdot(A_{\Delta,\geq k})^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k}=\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{-k}\cdot(A_{\Delta,\geq k})^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k},

\displaystyle{}\bigl(\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{-k}\cdot A^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k}\bigr)_{\Delta,\geq k}=\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{-k}\cdot(A_{\Delta^{*},\geq k})^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k}=\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{-k}\cdot(A_{\Delta^{*},\geq k})^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k}.

Proof.

The first relation is derived by
\Delta^{*}=-\Lambda^{-1}\Delta
and
\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{*-1}=-\Lambda\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-1}
.
If assume
A=\sum_{m}a_{m}(s)\Delta^{m}
, then by
\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{*-k}\cdot\Delta^{*k}=1
,

\displaystyle\bigl(\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{-k}\cdot A^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k}\bigr)_{\Delta^{*},\geq k}=\biggl(\bigl(\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{*-k}\Lambda^{-k}\bigr)\sum_{m}\Delta^{*m}a_{m}(s)\bigl(\Lambda^{k}\Delta^{*k}\bigr)\biggr)_{\Delta^{*},\geq k}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\bigl(\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{-k}\cdot A^{*}\cdot\Delta^k\bigr)_{\Delta^*,\geq k}}{}=\sum_{m\geq k}\Delta^{*m-k}a_{m}(s-k)\Delta^{*k},

\displaystyle\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-k}\cdot\bigl(A_{\Delta,\geq k}\bigr)^{*}\cdot\Delta^{k}=\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\bigl(\Delta^{*-k}\Lambda^{-k}\bigr)\sum_{m\geq k}^{\infty}\Delta^{*m}a_{m}(s)\bigl(\Lambda^{k}\Delta^{*k}\bigr)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-k}\cdot\bigl(A_{\Delta,\geq k}\bigr)^{*}\cdot\Delta^k}{}=\sum_{m\geq k}^{\infty}\Delta^{*m-k}a_{m}(s-k)\Delta^{*k},

which imply the second relation. Similarly, we can prove the third formula.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For any formal difference operator
A\in\mathcal{A}\bigl[\bigl[\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1}\bigr]\bigr]
and any function
f\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\bigl(f^{-1}\cdot A\cdot f\bigr)_{\Delta,\geq 1}=f^{-1}\cdot A_{\geq 0}\cdot f-f^{-1}\cdot A_{\geq 0}(f)=f^{-1}\cdot A_{>0}\cdot f-f^{-1}\cdot A_{>0}(f),

\displaystyle\bigl(f^{-1}\cdot A\cdot f\bigr)_{\Delta^{*},\geq 1}=f^{-1}\cdot A_{\leq 0}\cdot f-f^{-1}\cdot A_{\leq 0}(f)=f^{-1}\cdot A_{<0}\cdot f-f^{-1}\cdot A_{<0}(f),

\displaystyle\bigl(\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Delta^{-1}\cdot f\cdot A\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta\bigr)_{\Delta,\geq 1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-1}\cdot f\cdot A_{\geq 0}\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta-\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-1}\cdot(A_{\geq 0})^{*}(f)\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-1}\cdot f\cdot A_{>0}\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta-\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{-1}\cdot(A_{>0})^{*}(f)\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta,

\displaystyle\bigl(\iota_{\Lambda}\Delta^{*-1}\cdot f\cdot A\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta^{*}\bigr)_{\Delta^{*},\geq 1}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{*-1}\cdot f\cdot A_{\leq 0}\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta^{*}-\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{*-1}\cdot(A_{\leq 0})^{*}(f)\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{*-1}\cdot f\cdot A_{<0}\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta^{*}-\iota_{\Lambda^{\pm 1}}\Delta^{*-1}\cdot(A_{<0})^{*}(f)\cdot f^{-1}\cdot\Delta^{*}.

Proof.

By using Lemma
2.1
, we can get

\displaystyle\bigl(f^{-1}\cdot A\cdot f\bigr)_{\Delta,\geq 1}

\displaystyle{}=\bigl(f^{-1}\cdot A\cdot f\bigr)_{\Delta,\geq 0}-\bigl(f^{-1}\cdot A\cdot f\bigr)_{\Delta,[0]}

\displaystyle{}=\bigl(f^{-1}\cdot A\cdot f\bigr)_{\geq 0}-\bigl(f^{-1}\cdot A\cdot f\bigr)_{\geq 0}|_{\Lambda=1}

\displaystyle{}=f^{-1}\cdot A_{\geq 0}\cdot f-f^{-1}\cdot A_{\geq 0}(f)

\displaystyle{}=f^{-1}\cdot A_{>0}\cdot f-f^{-1}\cdot A_{>0}(f).

Similarly, we can get the second formula. As for the third formula, it can be derived by the third relation in Lemma
2.3
with

and formula for

.
As for the fourth formula, it can be proved by similar to the third one.
∎

3
Lax formulations of mToda hierarchy

In this section, we first derive the Lax formulations of the mToda hierarchy from the bilinear equation (
1.1
). Then, we also provide the proof of bilinear equation obtained from the Lax formulations.

For 2-component first mKP hierarchy (
1.1
), let us introduce wave functions
\Psi_{j}(s,\mathbf{x},z)
,
{j\!=\!1,2}
, and their adjoint
\Psi_{j}^{*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)
in terms of the tau function as follows:

\displaystyle{}\Psi_{1}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{0,s}\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\bigl[z^{-1}\bigr]_{1}\bigr)}{\tau_{1,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{s}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},z)}=w^{(1)}(s,\mathbf{x},z)z^{s}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},z)},

(3.1)

\displaystyle{}\Psi_{1}^{*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{1,s}\bigl(\mathbf{x}+\bigl[z^{-1}\bigr]_{1}\bigr)}{\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{-s}{\rm e}^{-\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},z)}=w^{(1)*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)z^{-s}{\rm e}^{-\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},z)},

(3.2)

\displaystyle{}\Psi_{2}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{0,s+1}(\mathbf{x}-[z]_{2})}{\tau_{1,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{s}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(2)},z^{-1})}=w^{(2)}(s,\mathbf{x},z)z^{s}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(2)},z^{-1})},

(3.3)

\displaystyle{}\Psi_{2}^{*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{1,s-1}(\mathbf{x}+[z]_{2})}{\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{-s+1}{\rm e}^{-\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(2)},z^{-1})}=w^{(2)*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)z^{-s}{\rm e}^{-\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(2)},z^{-1})},

where
w^{(j)}(s,\mathbf{x},z)
and
w^{(j)*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)
are formal power series
with respect to

(for
j=1
) and

(for
j=2
)

\displaystyle w^{(1)}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{i}(s,\mathbf{x})z^{-i},\qquad

\displaystyle w^{(2)}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{c}_{i}(s,\mathbf{x})z^{i},

\displaystyle w^{(1)*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c^{\prime}_{i}(s,\mathbf{x})z^{-i},\qquad

\displaystyle w^{(2)*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{c}^{\prime}_{i}(s,\mathbf{x})z^{i+1},

with

and
\bar{c}_{0}(s,\mathbf{x})\neq 0
.
Hence, the bilinear equation (
1.1
) can be rewritten into

\displaystyle\oint_{C_{\infty}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}z}\Psi_{1}(s,\mathbf{x},z)\Psi_{1}^{*}\bigl(s^{\prime},\mathbf{x}^{\prime},z\bigr)+\oint_{C_{0}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}z}\Psi_{2}(s,\mathbf{x},z)\Psi_{2}^{*}(s^{\prime},\mathbf{x}^{\prime},z)=1,

where

denotes the circle around

, while

denotes the circle around
z=0
. Both
C_{\infty}
and
C_{0}
are anticlockwise.

Next, introduce the wave operators
W_{j}
and
\widetilde{W}_{j}
,
j=1,2
, as

4
Anti-Miura and Miura transformations between Toda

and mToda hierarchies

In this section, we first briefly introduce some basic facts about the Toda hierarchy and the automorphism of Toda and mToda hierarchies. Based on these, we will discuss the anti-Miura and Miura transformations between the Toda and mToda hierarchies. In this paper, we adopt the following convention, the Miura transformation refer to the transformation from mToda to Toda, whereas the anti-Miura transformation is the one from Toda to mToda.

5
mToda tau functions

In this section, we will discuss mToda tau functions and their relations with Toda tau functions.
Note that from mToda Lax formulation, we have proved the mToda bilinear equation in terms of wave functions. Firstly, let us prove the existence of tau functions for mToda hierarchy from mToda bilinear equation.

Proposition 5.1
.

Given mToda wave functions
\Psi_{i}
and mToda adjoint wave functions
\Psi_{i}^{*}
,
i=1,2
, satisfying

\displaystyle\oint_{C_{\infty}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}z}\Psi_{1}(s,\mathbf{x},z)\Psi_{1}^{*}\bigl(s^{\prime},\mathbf{x}^{\prime},z\bigr)+\oint_{C_{0}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi{\rm i}z}\Psi_{2}(s,\mathbf{x},z)\Psi_{2}^{*}\bigl(s^{\prime},\mathbf{x}^{\prime},z\bigr)=1,

(5.1)

then there exist tau functions
\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})
and
\tau_{1,s}(\mathbf{x})
such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{0,s}\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\bigl[z^{-1}\bigr]_{1}\bigr)}{\tau_{1,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{s}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},z)},

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}^{*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{1,s}\bigl(\mathbf{x}+\bigl[z^{-1}\bigr]_{1}\bigr)}{\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{-s}{\rm e}^{-\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},z)},

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{0,s+1}(\mathbf{x}-[z]_{2})}{\tau_{1,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{s}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(2)},z^{-1})},

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}^{*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau_{1,s-1}(\mathbf{x}+[z]_{2})}{\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})}z^{-s+1}{\rm e}^{-\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(2)},z^{-1})},

where
(\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x}),\tau_{1,s}(\mathbf{x}))
is called mToda tau pair.

Proof.

Firstly, by Corollary
4.7
, we can find that
\Phi_{j}(s,\mathbf{x},z)
,
j=1,2
, defined by (
4.8
) are Toda wave functions, thus there exists
tau functions
\tau_{s}^{\rm Toda}
such that

\displaystyle c_{0}^{-1}(s)\Psi_{1}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau^{{\rm Toda}}_{s}\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\bigl[z^{-1}\bigr]_{1}\bigr)}{\tau^{{\rm Toda}}_{s}(\mathbf{x})}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},z)}z^{s},

\displaystyle c_{0}^{-1}(s)\Psi_{2}(s,\mathbf{x},z)=\frac{\tau^{{\rm Toda}}_{s+1}(\mathbf{x}-[z]_{2})}{\tau^{{\rm Toda}}_{s}(\mathbf{x})}{\rm e}^{\xi(\mathbf{x}^{(2)},z^{-1})}z^{s}.

So, if denote
\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})=\tau^{{\rm Toda}}_{s}(x)
,
\tau_{1,s}(\mathbf{x})=c_{0}^{-1}(s)\tau^{{\rm Toda}}_{s}(x)
, then we can get relations for
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
.

Next, if let

\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=\mathbf{x}+\bigl[\lambda^{-1}\bigr]_{1}
and
s^{\prime}=s
in (
5.1
), we can get

\displaystyle w^{(1)}\bigl(s,\mathbf{x}+\bigl[\lambda^{-1}\bigr]_{1},\lambda\bigr)w^{(1)*}(s,\mathbf{x},\lambda)=1,

then we can derive the expression for
\Psi_{1}^{*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)
.
Similarly, letting
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=\mathbf{x}+[\lambda]_{2}
, we can get

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}w^{(2)}(s,\mathbf{x}+[\lambda]_{2},\lambda)w^{(2)*}(s+1,\mathbf{x},\lambda)=1,

from which we can get the expression for
\Psi_{2}^{*}(s,\mathbf{x},z)
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For the function
f(s,\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{A}
, if it satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{\pm\xi(\tilde{\partial}_{\mathbf{x}^{(1)}},z^{-1})}f(s,\mathbf{x})=\Lambda^{\mp}{\rm e}^{\pm\xi(\tilde{\partial}_{\mathbf{x}^{(2)}},z)}f(s,\mathbf{x}),

(5.2)

then

is a constant independent of
s
and
\mathbf{x}
.

Compare the coefficients of
z^{j}
,

, in (
5.2
), we can get

\displaystyle f(s,\mathbf{x})=f(s\mp 1,\mathbf{x}),\qquad\partial_{x_{n}^{(1)}}f(s,x)=\partial_{x_{n}^{(2)}}f(s\mp 1,\mathbf{x})=0,

which implies that
f
is a constant independent of
s
and
\mathbf{x}
.
∎

Proposition 5.3

,

, then
by using (

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{s}^{\rm Toda}\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\bigl[z^{-1}\bigr]_{1}\bigr)}{\tau_{s}^{\rm Toda}(\mathbf{x})}=\frac{\tau_{0,s}\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\bigl[z^{-1}\bigr]_{1}\bigr)}{\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})},\qquad\frac{\tau_{s+1}^{\rm Toda}(\mathbf{x}-[z]_{2})}{\tau_{s}^{\rm Toda}(\mathbf{x})}=\frac{\tau_{0,s+1}(\mathbf{x}-[z]_{2})}{\tau_{0,s}(\mathbf{x})},

∎

6

Now we can use Miura and anti-Miura transformations shown in above section to construct the corresponding Darboux transformations of Toda and mToda hierarchies by the following way:

\displaystyle{\rm Toda}\xrightarrow{\text{anti-Miura}}{\rm mToda}\xrightarrow{{\rm Miura}}{\rm Toda},\qquad{\rm mToda}\xrightarrow{{\rm Miura}}{\rm Toda}\xrightarrow{\text{anti-Miura}}{\rm mToda}.

.

Given Toda eigenfunction
q
and Toda adjoint eigenfunction

, Toda Lax operators
(\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2})

in the following way:

•

Case

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}^{[1]}=\mathcal{T}_{1,2}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\iota_{\Lambda^{-1}}\mathcal{T}_{1,2}^{-1},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}^{[1]}=\mathcal{T}_{1,2}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2}\cdot\iota_{\Lambda}\mathcal{T}_{1,2}^{-1},

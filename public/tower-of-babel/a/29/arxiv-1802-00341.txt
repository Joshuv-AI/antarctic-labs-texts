Abstract
. In this paper we investigate some strong convergence theorems for partial sums with respect to Vilenkin system.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification.
42C10.

Key words and phrases:
Vilenkin system, partial sums, Fejér means, Hardy space, strong convergence.

1.
Introduction

It is well-known (for details see e.g.
[
8
]
and
[
14
]
) that the
Vilenkin system does not form a basis in the space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).

Moreover, there is a function in the Hardy space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right),
(for details see
[
12
,
13
,
21
,
22
]
) such that the partial sums of
f
are not bounded in
L_{1}
-norm.
However, (see e.g.
[
2
,
23
]
) the subsequence
S_{M_{n}}
of partial sums are bounded from the Hardy
space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the Lebesgue space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right):

(1)

\left\|S_{M_{k}}f\right\|_{H_{1}}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}\text{ \ \ \ }(k\in\mathbb{N}).

Moreover, in Gát
[
7
]
(see also Simon
[
18
,
19
]
) it was proved the following strong convergence result for
all
f\in H_{1}:

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f-f\right\|_{1}}{k}=0,

where
S_{k}f
denotes the
k
-th partial sum of the Vilenkin-Fourier series
of
f.

It follows that there exists an absolute constant
c,
such that

(2)

\frac{1}{\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}}{k}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}\text{ \ }\left(n=2,3...\right)

and

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}}{k}=\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}},

for all
f\in H_{1}.

Analogical result for the trigonometric system was proved by Smith
[
20
]
, and for the Walsh-Paley system by Simon
[
17
]
.

If the partial sums of Vilenkin-Fourier series was bounded from
H_{1}
to
L_{1}
we also would have:

(3)

\underset{n\in\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}_{+}}{\sup}\frac{1}{n}\underset{m=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left\|S_{m}f\right\|_{1}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}},

but as it was presented above the boundednes of the partial sums does not hold
from
H_{1}
to
L_{1}
, However, we have inequality (
2
).

On the other hand, in the one-dimensional case, Fujji
[
6
]
and Simon
[
16
]

proved that maximal operator Fejér means are bounded from
H_{1}
to
L_{1}
,
that is

(4)

\underset{n\in\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}_{+}}{\sup}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\underset{m=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}S_{m}f\right\|_{1}<c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}.

So, natural question has arised that if inequality (
3
) holds true,
which would be generalization of inequality (
4
) or is we have negative
answer on this problem.

In this paper we prove that there exists a function
f\in H_{1}
such that

\underset{n\in\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}_{+}}{\sup}\frac{1}{n}\underset{m=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left\|S_{m}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

This paper is organized as follows: in order not to disturb our discussions
later on some definitions and notations are presented in Section 2. For the
proofs of the main results we need some auxiliary Lemmas. These results are
presented in Section 3. The formulation and detailed proof of main results
can be found in Section 4.

2.
Definitions and Notations

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.

Let
m:=(m_{0,}m_{1},\dots)
denote a sequence of positive integers not
less than 2.

Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,\dots,m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k}.

Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{m_{j}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{j}}

,
s.

The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k}\text{ \qquad}(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is the Haar measure on
G_{m_{\text{ }}}
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

If
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}m_{n}<\infty
, then we call
G_{m}
a bounded
Vilenkin group. If the generating sequence
m
is not bounded, then
G_{m}

is said to be an unbounded Vilenkin group.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots)\qquad\left(\text{ }x_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighbourhood of
G_{m}
namely

\displaystyle I_{0}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle G_{m},

\displaystyle I_{n}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},\dots,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\}\text{ }(x\in G_{m},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N})

Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\overline{I_{n}}:=G_{m}

\backslash

I_{n}

.

Let

e_{n}:=\left(0,\dots,0,x_{n}=1,0,\dots\right)\in G_{m}\qquad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

If we define the so-called generalized number system based on
m
in the
following way:

M_{0}:=1,\text{\qquad}M_{k+1}:=m_{k}M_{k\text{ }},\ \qquad(k\in\mathbb{N})

then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as
n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j}
where
n_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}

~(j\in\mathbb{N})
and only a finite number of
n_{j}`
s differ from zero. Let
\left|n\right|:=\max

\{j\in\mathbb{N};

n_{j}\neq 0\}.

Next, we introduce on
G_{m}
an orthonormal system, which is called the
Vilenkin system.

At first define the complex valued function
r_{k}\left(x\right):G_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
the generalized Rademacher functions as

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\exp\left(2\pi\imath x_{k}/m_{k}\right)\text{
\qquad}\left(\imath^{2}=-1,\text{ }x\in G_{m},\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now define the Vilenkin system
\psi:=(\psi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G_{m}
as:

\psi_{n}\left(x\right):=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Specially, we call this system the Walsh-Paley one if
m\equiv 2.

The norm (or quasi norm) of the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
is defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{G_{m}}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}d\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}\qquad\left(0<p<\infty\right).

The Vilenkin system is orthonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G_{m}\right)\,
(for details see e.g.
[
1
,
25
]
).

If
\ f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
we can define Fourier coefficients,
partial sums of the Fourier series, Fejér means, Dirichlet kernels with
respect to the Vilenkin system in the usual manner:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(k)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\psi}_{k}d\mu\text{\thinspace\qquad\ \ \ \ }\left(\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ }\right)

\displaystyle S_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\psi_{k}\ \text{\qquad\ \ }\left(\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\text{ }S_{0}f:=0\right)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S_{k}f\text{ \qquad\ \ \ \ \ }\left(\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\text{ }\right)

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k\text{ }}\text{ \qquad\ \ \qquad}\left(\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\text{ }\right).

Recall that

(5)

\quad\hskip 0.0ptD_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\text{ }M_{n},\text{ \ \ \ }x\in I_{n}\\
\text{ }0,\text{ \qquad}x\notin I_{n}\end{array}\right.

and

(6)

D_{s_{n}M_{n}}=D_{M_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{s_{n}-1}\psi_{kM_{n}}=D_{M_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{s_{n}-1}r_{n}^{k},\text{ \qquad}1\leq s_{n}\leq m_{n}-1.

The
n
-th Lebesgue constant is defined in the following way

L_{n}=\left\|D_{n}\right\|_{1}.

It is well-known
[
25
]
that

(7)

L_{n}=O(\log n),\ \ n\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover, (for unbounded Vilenkin systems it can be found in
[
5
]
,
for bounded Vilenkin systems see e.g.
[
9
]
and
[
11
,
24
]
) there exist absolute constant

c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

(8)

c_{1}\log n\leq\frac{1}{n}\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}L\left(k\right)\leq c_{2}\log n,\ \ (n=2,3,...).

The concept of the Hardy space
[
4
]
can be defined in various manners, e.g.
by a maximal function

f^{\ast}:=\sup_{n}\left|S_{M_{n}}f\right|\text{ \ \ }\left(f\in G_{m}\right),

saying that
f
belongs to the Hardy space if
f^{\ast}\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right).
This definition is suitable if the sequence
m
is bounded.
In this case a good property of the space
\left\{f\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right):\text{ }f^{\ast}\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right)\right\}
is
the atomic structure
[
4
]
. To the definition of space of Hardy type for
an arbitrary
m
, first we give the concept of the atoms
[
16
]
. A set
I\subset G_{m}
is called an interval if for some
x\in G_{m}
and
n\in N
,

I
is of the form
I=\bigcup\limits_{k\in U}I_{n}\left(x,k\right)
, where
U
is obtained from
Z_{m_{n}}
by dyadic partition.

The sets
U_{1},U_{2},...\subset Z_{m_{n}}
, are obtained by means of such
a partition if

U_{1}=\left\{0,...,\left[\frac{m_{n}}{2}\right]-1\right\},\text{ \ }U_{2}=\left\{\left[\frac{m_{n}}{2}\right],...,m_{n}-1\right\},

U_{3}=\left\{0,...,\left[\frac{\left[m_{n}/2\right]-1}{2}\right]-1\right\},\text{ \ }U_{4}=\left\{\left[\frac{[m_{n}/2]-1}{2}\right],...,\left[\frac{m_{n}}{2}\right]-1\right\},...

etc.;
[\ \ ]
denotes the entire part. We define the atoms as follows: the
function
a\in L^{\infty}\left(G_{m}\right)
is called an atom if
eather
a\equiv 1
or there exists an interval
I
for which
\sup a\subset I,

\left|a\right|\leq\left|I\right|^{-1}
and
\int_{I}a=0

hold.
\left(\left|I\right|\text{ denotes the Haar measure of }I\text{ }\right).

Now we can define the space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
(for details see e.g
[
26
,
27
]
) as the set of all functions
f=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}
,where
a_{i}
’s are atoms and for the coefficients
\lambda_{i}
we have
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|<\infty.

H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
is a Banach space with respect to the norm

(9)

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}:=\inf\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|<\infty.

The infimum is taken over all decompositions

f=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}.

It is known [7]
that
\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}
is equivalent to
\left\|f^{\ast\ast}\right\|_{1}\left(f\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right)\right)
, where
f
**
\left(x\right):=\sup_{I}\left|I\right|^{-1}\left|\int_{I}f\right|
,
\left(x\in G_{m}\text{, }x\in I\text{ and }I\text{
is interval}\right)
. Since by (
5
)

f^{*}\left(x\right)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left|I_{n}\left(x\right)\right|}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)\mu\left(u\right)\right|

we have
f^{\ast}\leq f^{\ast\ast}
and, thus,
H\left(G_{m}\right)\subset\left\{f\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right):\text{ }f^{\ast}\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right)\right\}.
Moreover these spaces coincide if the sequence
m
is bounded.

3.
Main Result

Our main result reads:

Theorem 1
.

a) Let
f\in H_{1}.
Then there exists an absolute constant
c,
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}.

b) Let
\varphi:N_{+}\rightarrow[1,

\infty)
be a nondecreasing
function satisfying the condition

(10)

\overline{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{\log n}{\varphi_{n}}=+\infty.

Then there exists a function
f\in H_{1},
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n\varphi_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

Corollary 1
.

(see e.g.
[
10
,
16
,
18
]
)There exists a function
f\in H_{1},
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

By using (
7
) we can conclude that

\frac{1}{n\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}}{n\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}{\log k}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}.

The proof of part a) is complete.

Under the condition (
10
) there exists an increasing sequence of the positive integers
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
such that

\overline{\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{\log M_{{\alpha_{k}}}}{\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}}=+\infty

and

(11)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}^{1/2}}{\log^{1/2}M_{{\alpha_{k}}}}<c<\infty.

Let

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\lambda_{k}}{a_{k}},

where

a_{k}=r_{\alpha_{k}}D_{M_{{}_{\alpha_{k}}}}=D_{2M_{{}_{\alpha_{k}}}}-D_{M_{{}_{\alpha_{k}}}}

and

\lambda_{k}=\frac{\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}^{1/2}}{\log^{1/2}M_{{\alpha_{k}}}}.

By the definition of
H_{1}
and (
9
), if we apply (
11
) we can conclude that
f\in H_{1}.
Moreover,

(12)

\widehat{f}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\lambda_{k}},\,\,\text{\ \ \ \ \thinspace\thinspace}j\in\left\{M_{\alpha_{k}},...,2M_{\alpha_{k}}-1\right\},\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N}\\
0\text{ },\text{ \thinspace\qquad\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}j\notin\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\{M_{\alpha_{k}},...,2M_{\alpha_{k}}-1\right\}.\text{ }\end{array}\right.

Since

D_{j+M_{\alpha_{k}}}=D_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}+\psi_{{}_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}}D_{j},\text{ \qquad when \thinspace\thinspace}j\leq M_{\alpha_{k}},

if we apply (
12
) we obtain that

(13)

\displaystyle S_{j}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}f+\sum_{v=M_{\alpha_{k}}}^{j-1}\widehat{f}(v)\psi_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}f+{\lambda_{k}}\sum_{v=M_{\alpha_{k}}}^{j-1}\psi_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}f+{\lambda_{k}}\left(D_{j}-D_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}f+{\lambda_{k}}\psi_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}D_{j-M_{\alpha_{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

In view of (
1
) we can write that

(14)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{1}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|S_{M_{\alpha_{k}}}f\right\|_{1}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}.

By combining (
8
) and (
14
) we get that

\displaystyle\left\|S_{n}f\right\|_{1}\geq\left\|I_{2}\right\|_{1}-\left\|I_{1}\right\|_{1}\geq{\lambda_{k}}{L\left({n-M_{\alpha_{k}}}\right)}-c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\underset{n\in\mathbf{\mathbb{N}}_{+}}{\sup}\frac{1}{n\varphi_{n}}\underset{k=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left\|S_{k}f\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2M_{\alpha_{k}}\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}}\underset{\left\{M_{\alpha_{k}}\leq l\leq 2M_{\alpha_{k}}\right\}}{\sum}\left\|S_{l}f\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2M_{\alpha_{k}}\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}}\underset{\left\{M_{\alpha_{k}}\leq l\leq 2M_{\alpha_{k}}\right\}}{\sum}\left(\frac{L\left(l-M_{\alpha_{k}}\right)\varphi_{2M{\alpha_{k}}}^{1/2}}{\log^{1/2}M_{\alpha_{k}}}-c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c\varphi_{2M{\alpha_{k}}}^{1/2}}{2M_{\alpha_{k}}\log^{1/2}M_{\alpha_{k}}\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}}\underset{l=1}{\overset{M_{\alpha_{k}}-1}{\sum}}L\left(l\right)-c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c\varphi_{2M{\alpha_{k}}}^{1/2}\log M_{\alpha_{k}}}{\log^{1/2}M_{\alpha_{k}}\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}}\geq\frac{c\log^{1/2}M_{\alpha_{k}}}{\varphi_{2M_{\alpha_{k}}}^{1/2}}\rightarrow\infty,\text{ as \ }k\rightarrow\infty.

The proof is complete.

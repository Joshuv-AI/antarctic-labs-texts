Pressure-robust staggered DG methods for the Navier-Stokes equations on general meshes Thanks: Submitted to the editors Month day, year.
Dohyun Kim, Lina Zhao, Eric Chung, Eun-Jae Park

Abstract
In this paper, we design and analyze staggered discontinuous Galerkin methods of arbitrary polynomial orders for the stationary Navier-Stokes equations on polygonal meshes. The exact divergence-free condition for the velocity is satisfied without any postprocessing. The resulting method is pressure-robust so that the pressure approximation does not influence the velocity approximation. A new nonlinear convective term that earning non-negativity is proposed. The optimal convergence estimates for all the variables in L 2 L^{2} norm are proved. Also, assuming that the rotational part of the forcing term is small enough, we are able to prove that the velocity error is independent of the Reynolds number and of the pressure. Furthermore, superconvergence can be achieved for velocity under a suitable projection. Numerical experiments are provided to validate the theoretical findings and demonstrate the performances of the proposed method.



Keywords:
Staggered grid, Discontinuous Galerkin method, Divergence free, Pressure robustness, Navier-Stokes equations, General meshes, Superconvergence

The Navier-Stokes equations play an important role in fluid dynamics.
A number of finite element methods were proposed to solve the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations and accomplished many advances in the past half-century

Traditional inf-sup stable finite element methods do not give robust velocity approximation when large irrotational force is considered in general.
Such methods yield velocity approximations which depend on a pressure-dependent error contribution
\nu^{-1}\inf_{q\in Q_{h}}\|p-q_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
where
\nu
is the viscosity and
Q_{h}
is the discrete pressure space.
The influence of the pressure-dependent error contribution is most pronouncing in the no-flow example which was first considered in
[
25
]
.
One of the main reason for the pressure-dependent error contribution is that the velocity approximation does not satisfy the incompressibility condition.
To satisfy the incompressibility condition, divergence-free finite element methods were proposed, see, for example
[
20
,
45
,
32
,
33
,
29
,
44
]
.
It is by no means trivial to construct finite element spaces that satisfy inf-sup condition and at the same time yield divergence free velocity. One of the approach exploited is to
enrich the velocity space without violating inf-sup condition. However, the construction of a suitable space to enrich velocity space is tricky. As an alternative, one can use divergence free velocity reconstruction operator to modify the right hand side
[
4
,
38
,
27
,
8
]
.
When the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations are considered, the reconstruction operator should be applied to the velocity for each nonlinear iteration and this leads to additional computational cost.
For the case of evolutionary incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, several pressure-robust numerical approaches were considered such as
H(\mathrm{div}\,)
-conforming discontinuous Galerkin (DG)
[
42
,
34
]
,

-conforming mixed finite element method with grad-div stabilization
[
28
,
1
]
, and continuous interior penalty methods
[
5
]
.
While some of numerical simulations show optimal convergence behavior, error estimates provided in the aforementioned works are suboptimal.

. Since then it has been successfully applied for

Recently, Zhao and Park

For a pressure-robust method, Zhao et al.
[

]

proposed a lowest-order pressure-robust staggered DG method for
the Stokes equations. They used a reconstruction operator based on
an
H
(div) conforming function space on polygonal meshes.
However, its extension to high-order methods is not trivial.

The objective of this paper is to design and analyze a pressure
robust staggered DG method of arbitrary polynomial orders on
polygonal meshes for the Navier-Stokes problem. The discrete
formulation involves velocity gradient, velocity and pressure, and
the continuities for all the variables are staggered on the
inter-element boundaries in line with
[
47
]
. The
staggered continuity naturally gives an inter-element flux term
which is free of stabilization parameters and meanwhile ensures
the inf-sup stability of the resulting bilinear forms. The
proposed method is pressure-robust without enriching the velocity
space nor introducing the divergence-preserving reconstruction
operator. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first
pressure-robust method on polygonal meshes without enriching the
discrete spaces and without establishing reconstruction operators.
As such, the construction of the method is relatively simple by
using standard polynomial spaces and the extension to arbitrary
polynomial orders is straightforward. Another novel contribution
lies in the design of a new nonlinear convective term that
guarantees non-negativity. We prove that the resulting method
yields a divergence-free velocity approximation without any
postprocessing, which is particularly important for Navier-Stokes
equations. In addition, the unique solvability of the discrete
solution is proved under a smallness condition involving only the
solenoidal part of the body force. Importantly, the convergence
estimate for velocity is proved to be independent of the pressure
and of the viscosity. The staggered DG method designed herein
offers some unique features which makes it advantageous over other
polygonal methods: It is locally conservative over each
dual-element; superconvergence can be obtained;
it is stable without numerical flux nor penalty term; exact
divergence free condition is satisfied. It should be also
emphasized that developing a divergence free element on 3D is much
more difficult and our approach can be extended to 3D
straightforwardly. The current paper focuses on 2D to present the
core of the method and simplify the presentation.

The organization of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce the pressure-robust staggered DG method on polygonal meshes and give some useful lemmas including the divergence-free property of the velocity approximation.
In Section
3
, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the nonlinear discrete problem using fixed-point argument.
Then
a priori
error estimates for all variables are proved in Section
4
.
In Section
5
, several numerical experiments are conducted to verify the pressure robustness and the optimality of the method.
We end in Section
6
with some concluding remarks.

Staggered discontinuous Galerkin method

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded simply connected polygonal domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
.
For given data
\bm{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
and
\bm{g}\in[H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)]^{2}
, the incompressible Navier-Stokes problem in the conservative form seeks the unknown velocity
\bm{u}
and the pressure
p
satisfying

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta\bm{u}+\mathrm{div}\,(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u})+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\bm{f}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{g}

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\left\langle p\right\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=0.

Here,
\left\langle p\right\rangle_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}p\,\textup{d}\mathbf{x}
and
\nu>0
is a real number representing the kinematic viscosity of the fluid.
We introduce an auxiliary variable
\bm{G}=\nu\nabla\bm{u}
, then (
1
) can be recast into the following first order system

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}\bm{G}-\nabla\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\,\bm{G}+\mathrm{div}\,(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u})+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\bm{f},

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{g},

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\left\langle p\right\rangle_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=0.

In the remainder of this paper, we assume
\bm{g}=\bm{0}
for simplicity.

We denote the Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
and we write
H^{k}(\Omega)
when
p=2
.
Those spaces are equipped with norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)}
.

W^{k,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
denote the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with respective norms.
Here,
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
is the infinitely differentiable function spaces with compact support.
We also define

H(\mathrm{div}\,;\Omega)=\{\bm{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}:\nabla\cdot\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\},\quad L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega):\left\langle q\right\rangle_{\Omega}=0\}.

In the sequel we use
C
to denote a generic positive constant which may have different values at different occurrences.
The weak solution to (
2
) is defined by

\displaystyle(\nu^{-1}\bm{G}-\nabla\bm{u},\bm{H})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\bm{H}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2\times 2},

(3)

\displaystyle(\bm{G}-\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u}-p\bm{I},\nabla\bm{v})

\displaystyle=(\bm{f},\bm{v})

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2},

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\bm{u},q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall q\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

We can prove the following stability estimate proceeding similarly to
[
8
]
and the proof is omitted for simplicity.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\bm{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
, let
\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{0}+\nabla\chi
be the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition with
(\bm{f}_{0},\chi)\in H(\mathrm{div}\,;\Omega)\times(H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega))
. Further,
\bm{f}_{0}
satisfies
\nabla\cdot\bm{f}_{0}=0
.
If
(\bm{G},\bm{u},p)
is the solution to (
2
),
then, there holds

\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In other words,
\bm{u}
is independent of the irrotational force
\nabla\chi
.

We now introduce some basic notations regarding staggered meshes that will be exploited in the construction of the staggered DG method.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a star-shaped polygonal partition of
\Omega
, and the set of all the edges is called primal-edges and is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{pr}
.
In addition, we use
\mathcal{F}_{pr}^{i}
and
\mathcal{F}_{pr}^{b}
to stand for the interior edges and boundary edges, respectively.
For each primal-element

and connect it to all the vertices of

The resulting triangulation is denoted as

and the edges generated during the subdivision process are called dual-edges and are denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{dl}

We rename the union of sub-triangles sharing the common vertex
\bm{x}
as
S(\bm{x})
.
Here, we choose
\bm{x}
to be an interior point of the kernel of
S(\bm{x})\in\mathcal{S}
and we use
\mathcal{N}
to represent the union of all interior points
\bm{x}
.
For each interior edge

, we use
\mathcal{D}(e)
to stand for the dual-mesh, which is the union of two triangles in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
sharing the common edge

.
For each boundary edge

, we use

to denote a triangle in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
having the edge

, see Figure
1
for an illustration.
For each edge

, we define a unique normal vector

and tangential vector

by the outward normal vector and counter-clockwise tangential vector of one of its neighboring element, respectively.

For every element

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(q,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})_{\tau}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{pr}}\left\langle q,\left\llbracket\bm{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{e},

(11)

\displaystyle N_{h}(\bm{w};\bm{\psi},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\bm{\psi}\otimes\bm{w},\nabla\bm{v})_{\tau}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}}\left\langle\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\},\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{\psi}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left\langle|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\}|,\left\llbracket\bm{\psi}\right\rrbracket\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{e},

(12)

for any
(H,\bm{v},q)\in H_{h}\times V_{h}\times Q_{h}^{0}
and
(\bm{\psi},\bm{w})\in V_{h}\times V_{h}
.

In the remainder of this paper, we take
\bm{g}=\bm{0}
for simplicity.
Note that the formulation is based on the conservative form of the Navier-Stokes equation and the upwind term is added to ensure the non-negativity, see Lemma
2.9
. Also, integration by parts and the definitions of the discrete spaces lead to the following discrete adjoint properties

\displaystyle B_{h}(\bm{H},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=-B_{h}^{*}(\bm{v},\bm{H})

\displaystyle\forall(\bm{H},\bm{v})\in H_{h}\times V_{h},

(13)

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{v},q)

\displaystyle=-b_{h}^{*}(q,\bm{v})

\displaystyle\forall(\bm{v},q)\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}.

By the definition of the discrete bilinear forms and the interpolation operators, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that
(\bm{G},\bm{u},p)\in([H(\mathrm{div}\,;\Omega)]^{2}\cap[H^{1/2+\epsilon}(\Omega)]^{2\times 2})\times[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{2}\times(L^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1/2+\epsilon}(\Omega))

Then, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle B_{h}(\Pi_{h}\bm{G}-\bm{G},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle b_{h}(J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u},q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall q\in Q_{h},

\displaystyle B_{h}^{*}(J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u},\bm{H})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\bm{H}\in H_{h},

\displaystyle b_{h}^{*}(I_{h}p-p,\bm{v})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in V_{h}.

We have the discrete inf-sup condition for
B_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and

(cf.
[

,
47
]
).

Lemma 2.7
.

There exists
C_{B}
and
C_{b}
independent of

0<C_{B}\leq\adjustlimits{\inf}_{\bm{v}\in V_{h}}{\sup}_{\bm{H}\in H_{h}}\frac{B_{h}(\bm{H},\bm{v})}{\left\|\bm{H}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{h}},\quad 0<C_{b}\leq\adjustlimits{\inf}_{q\in Q_{h}}{\sup}_{\bm{v}\in V_{h}}\frac{b_{h}^{*}(q,\bm{v})}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|q\right\|_{1,h}}.

Integration by parts reveals the consistency of the nonlinear trilinear form
N_{h}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
.

Lemma 2.8

(Consistency)
.

Let
\bm{u}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2}
be the solution to (
1
). Then the following holds:

N_{h}(\bm{u};\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})=(\mathrm{div}\,(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u}),\bm{v}_{h})\quad\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}.

In the next lemma, we state the non-negative property for
N_{h}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
, which is crucial for the subsequent analysis.

Lemma 2.9

(Non-negativity)
.

Let
\bm{w}\in H(\mathrm{div}\,;\Omega)
with
\nabla\cdot\bm{w}=0
. Then we have

N_{h}(\bm{w};\bm{v},\bm{v})\geq 0\quad\forall\bm{v}\in V_{h}+[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
be given. From integration by parts, we have

(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{w},\nabla\bm{v})_{\tau}=-(\mathrm{div}\,(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{w}),\bm{v})_{\tau}+\left\langle(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{w})\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\right\rangle_{\partial\tau}.

(14)

Since
\nabla\cdot\bm{w}=0
, integration by parts and straightforward computation yield

(\mathrm{div}\,(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{w}),\bm{v})_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\cdot\bm{v}\right\rangle_{\partial\tau}

and

\left\langle(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{w})\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\right\rangle_{\partial\tau}=\left\langle\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\cdot\bm{v}\right\rangle_{\partial\tau}.

Then (
14
) can be rewritten as

(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{w},\nabla\bm{v})_{\tau}=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\cdot\bm{v}\right\rangle_{\partial\tau}.

Summing over all the elements
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\bm{v}\otimes\bm{w},\nabla\bm{v})_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\langle\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\cdot\bm{v}\right\rangle_{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}}\left\langle\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n},\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{v}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{b}}\left\langle\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\cdot\bm{v}\right\rangle_{e}.

This and the definition of
N_{h}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
yield

\displaystyle N_{h}(\bm{w};\bm{v},\bm{v})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{b}}\left\langle\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n},\bm{v}\cdot\bm{v}\right\rangle_{e}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left\langle|\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}|,\left\llbracket\bm{v}\right\rrbracket\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left\langle|\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}|,\left\llbracket\bm{v}\right\rrbracket\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

∎

By using Hölder’s inequality and the discrete Sobolev embedding theorem, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10

(Boundedness)
.

For any
\bm{z}_{h},\bm{v}_{h},\bm{w}_{h}\in V_{h}
, it holds

N_{h}(\bm{z}_{h};\bm{v}_{h},\bm{w}_{h})\leq C_{N}\left\|\bm{z}_{h}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{h}.

To ease later analysis, we define the divergence-free subspace of
V_{h}
by

V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}\,}=\{\bm{v}\in V_{h}:\bm{v}\in H(\mathrm{div}\,;\Omega),\;\nabla\cdot\bm{v}=0\}.

This subspace plays an important role in subsequent sections.
We close this section by observing the connection between
b_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
.

Lemma 2.11
.

If
\bm{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
satisfies

b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},q)=0\quad\forall q\in Q_{h}^{0},

(15)

then
\bm{v}_{h}\in H(\mathrm{div}\,;\Omega)
and it is divergence-free.

Proof.

Note that

b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},c)=0

for any constant
c
by the definition.
Therefore, the condition (
15

b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},q)=0\quad\forall q\in Q_{h}.

By (
13

0=b_{h}(\bm{v}_{h},q)=b_{h}^{*}(q,\bm{v}_{h})\quad\forall q\in Q_{h}.

Take
q
so that (cf. (SD1)-(SD2))

\displaystyle(q+\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h},p_{k-1})_{\tau}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall p_{k-1}\in\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(\tau),\;\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

\displaystyle\left\langle q-\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rrbracket,p_{k}\right\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\forall p_{k}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(e),\;e\in\mathcal{F}_{pr}.

Then we obtain

0=b_{h}^{*}(q,\bm{v}_{h})=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\tau)}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{pr}}\left\|\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}.

Therefore,
\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rrbracket=0
for all

.
Since
\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\rrbracket_{e}=0

.
Furthermore, since

∎

Remark 2.12
.

(divergence free velocity).
Our proposed scheme can yield a divergence-free velocity by following Lemma
2.11
, which is a desirable feature. Thanks to the divergence-free property and the specially designed term for the nonlinear convective term, we are able to prove that the convergence estimates are independent of the pressure variable and the coefficient
\nu

. Unlike the existing works on polygonal meshes
[

,
27
,
39
,
53
]
, we do not require velocity reconstruction, which can greatly reduce the computational complexity and ease the construction of the method.

3
Existence and uniqueness

In this section, we discuss the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
7
).
A solution operator
T_{h}:V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,\rightarrow V_{h}
is defined as follows: For given
\bm{z}_{h}\in V_{h}
, find
\bm{w}_{h}=T_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})\in V_{h}
so that

A_{h}((\bm{S}_{h},\bm{w}_{h},r_{h}),(\bm{H},\bm{v},q))+N_{h}(\bm{z}_{h};\bm{w}_{h},\bm{v})=(\bm{f},\bm{v})\quad\forall(\bm{H},\bm{v},q)\in H_{h}\times V_{h}\times Q_{h}

(16)

for some
\bm{G}_{h}\in H_{h}
and
p_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0}
.
Here,

A_{h}((\bm{S},\bm{w},r),(\bm{H},\bm{v},q))=\nu^{-1}(\bm{S},\bm{H})+B_{h}^{*}(\bm{w},\bm{H})+B_{h}(\bm{S},\bm{v})+b_{h}^{*}(r_{h},\bm{v})+b_{h}(\bm{w},q).

Observe that finding the solution to (
7
) is equivalent to finding a fixed-point
\bm{u}_{h}
of
T_{h}
so that

T_{h}(\bm{u}_{h})=\bm{u}_{h}

with its corresponding
\bm{G}_{h}
and
p_{h}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

T_{h}
is well-defined on
V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
and
T_{h}(\bm{z}_{h})\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
for all
\bm{z}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{z}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
be given.
From Lemma
2.11
, it is clear that
T_{h}(\bm{z}_{h})\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
.
By Lemma
2.9
and the definition of
A_{h}
, we obtain

A_{h}(\varphi,\varphi)+N_{h}(\bm{z}_{h};\bm{v},\bm{v})\geq\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{H}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\forall\varphi=(\bm{H},\bm{v},q)\in H_{h}\times V_{h}\times Q_{h}^{0}.

Let
\varsigma_{i}=(\bm{G}_{i},\bm{w}_{i},p_{i})
be the solution to (
16
) with
\bm{z}_{h}
for
i=1,2
.
Then

\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}_{1}-\bm{G}_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq A_{h}(\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2},\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2})+N_{h}(\bm{z}_{h};\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2},\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2})=0.

Therefore, we have
\bm{G}_{1}=\bm{G}_{2}
.
Furthermore, Lemma
2.7
and taking
(\bm{v},q)=(\bm{0},0)
in (
16
) lead to

\left\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\right\|_{h}\leq C\sup_{\bm{H}\in H_{h}}\frac{B_{h}(\bm{H},\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2})}{\left\|\bm{H}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}=C\sup_{\bm{H}\in H_{h}}\frac{B_{h}^{*}(\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2},\bm{H})}{\left\|\bm{H}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}=0.

Finally, the discrete adjoint property (
13
) and (
16
) with
(\bm{H},q)=(\bm{0},0)
read

0=b_{h}^{*}(p_{1}-p_{2},\bm{v})=b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{1}-p_{2})\quad\forall\bm{v}\in V_{h}.

By the inf-sup condition (
2.7
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\left\|p_{1}-p_{2}\right\|_{1,h}\leq C\sup_{\bm{v}\in V_{h}}\frac{b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{1}-p_{2})}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}=0.

Therefore, we have
\varsigma_{1}=\varsigma_{2}
.
Since
V_{h}
is finite dimensional space and
T_{h}
is linear,
T_{h}
is well-defined.

∎

Then, we have the following stability estimate.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\bm{z}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
, we have

\left\|T_{h}(\bm{z}_{h})\right\|_{h}=\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{h}\leq C_{B}^{-2}\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
\bm{f}_{0}
is from the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition
\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{0}+\nabla\chi
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{z}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
and
\bm{w}_{h}=T_{h}(\bm{z}_{h})
. Then there exists
\varsigma=(\bm{G}_{h},\bm{w}_{h},p_{h})
such that

A_{h}(\varsigma,\varphi)+N_{h}(\bm{z}_{h};\bm{w}_{h},\bm{v})=(\bm{f},\bm{v})\quad\forall\varphi=(\bm{H},\bm{v},q)\in H_{h}\times V_{h}\times Q_{h}^{0}.

Taking
\varphi=\varsigma
, we obtain

\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq A_{h}(\varsigma,\varsigma)+N_{h}(\bm{z}_{h};\bm{w}_{h},\bm{w}_{h})=(\bm{f},\bm{w}_{h}).

From Lemma
2.7
and (
16
), we have

C_{B}^{2}\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq\nu^{-2}\left\|\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\nu^{-1}(\bm{f},\bm{w}_{h}).

Finally, recall that
\bm{w}_{h}=T_{h}(\bm{z}_{h})\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
by Lemma
3.1
.
Then the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition
\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{0}+\nabla\chi
yields

C_{B}^{2}\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{h}\leq\nu^{-1}\frac{(\bm{f}_{0}+\nabla\chi,\bm{w}_{h})}{\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{h}}=\nu^{-1}\frac{(\bm{f}_{0},\bm{w}_{h})}{\left\|\bm{w}_{h}\right\|_{h}}\leq\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Lemma
3.2
implies that
T_{h}(\bm{z}_{h})
is independent of the irrotational part of
\bm{f}
.

By Lemma
3.2
and the Brouwer fixed point theorem, the existence of the fixed-point
\bm{u}_{h}=T_{h}(\bm{u}_{h})
is guaranteed.
To show that the fixed-point is unique, it suffices to show that
T_{h}
is a contraction mapping.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{0}+\nabla\chi
satisfies

\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<C_{B}^{4}C_{N}^{-1}\nu^{2}.

Then
T_{h}
has a unique fixed-point in

W_{h}^{\rho}=\{\bm{v}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,:\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{h}\leq\rho<C_{B}^{2}C_{N}^{-1}\nu\}.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\varsigma}_{i}=(\bm{G}_{i},\bm{w}_{i},p_{i})
be the solution to (
16
) with
\bm{z}_{i}\in V_{h}^{\mathrm{div}}\,
for
i=1,2
.
Then we have

A_{h}(\bm{\varsigma}_{i},\bm{\varphi})+N_{h}(\bm{z}_{i};\bm{w}_{i},\bm{v})=(\bm{f},\bm{v})\quad\forall\varphi=(\bm{H},\bm{v},q)\in H_{h}\times V_{h}\times Q_{h}^{0}.

Taking
\bm{\varphi}=\bm{\varsigma}_{1}-\bm{\varsigma}_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}_{1}-\bm{G}_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq A_{h}(\bm{\varsigma}_{1}-\bm{\varsigma}_{2},\bm{\varsigma}_{1}-\bm{\varsigma}_{2})+N_{h}(\bm{z}_{1};\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2},\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2})

\displaystyle=N_{h}(\bm{z}_{2}-\bm{z}_{1};\bm{w}_{2},\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\left\|\bm{z}_{1}-\bm{z}_{2}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{w}_{2}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\right\|_{h}.

From Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\right\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}^{-2}\nu^{-2}\left\|\bm{G}_{1}-\bm{G}_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}^{-2}C_{N}\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{z}_{2}-\bm{z}_{1}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{w}_{2}\right\|\left\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\right\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}^{-4}C_{N}\nu^{-2}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{z}_{2}-\bm{z}_{1}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\right\|_{h}.

Recalling that
\bm{w}_{i}=T_{h}(\bm{z}_{i})
, there holds

\displaystyle\left\|T_{h}(\bm{z}_{1})-T_{h}(\bm{z}_{2})\right\|_{h}\leq C_{B}^{-4}\nu^{-2}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{z}_{2}-\bm{z}_{1}\right\|_{h}.

By assuming
\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<C_{B}^{4}C_{N}^{-1}\nu^{2}
,
T_{h}
is a contraction mapping.
Therefore, an application of the Brouwer fixed-point theorem implies that there exists a unique fixed-point in
W_{h}^{\rho}
.
∎

4
A priori

In this section, we derive

In particular, the

\|\cdot\|_{h}

Firstly, we have the following

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\bm{u},\bm{G})
be the solution of (
1

(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{G}_{h})\in V_{h}\times H_{h}
be the discrete solution of (

Then the following estimate holds:

\left\|J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{h}\leq C_{B}\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The Galerkin orthogonality reads

\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h},\bm{H})+B_{h}^{*}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{H})=0\quad\forall\bm{H}\in H_{h}.

By Lemma

B_{h}(\bm{H},J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})=-B_{h}^{*}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},\bm{H})=\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h},\bm{H}).

This and Lemma

imply that

\displaystyle\left\|J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}\sup_{\bm{H}\in H_{h}}\frac{B_{h}(\bm{H},J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})}{\left\|\bm{H}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle=C_{B}\sup_{\bm{H}\in H_{h}}\frac{\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h},\bm{H})}{\left\|\bm{H}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

We decompose the velocity error
\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}
into two parts,

\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}=(\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u})+(J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})=:\bm{e}_{*}+\bm{e}_{h}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{0}+\nabla\chi
be the Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition of
\bm{f}
.
Then we have

|N_{h}(\bm{u};\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})-N_{h}(\bm{u}_{h};\bm{v}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})|\leq\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(C_{-1}\left\|\bm{e}_{*}\right\|_{0,4,h}+C_{*}\left\|\bm{e}_{h}\right\|_{h})\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{h}

With the aid of the aforementioned lemmas, we can prove the convergence of

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that

Then we have

\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\nu^{2}\left\|\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{0,4,h}+\left\|\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Proof.

Let
(\bm{G}_{h},\bm{u}_{h},p_{h})\in H_{h}\times V_{h}\times Q_{h}^{0}
be the solution to (
7

Then we have

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}_{h}-\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})

\displaystyle=B_{h}^{*}(\bm{u}_{h}-\bm{u},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}).

By Lemma
2.6
and the adjoint properties of
B_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
B_{h}^{*}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=B_{h}^{*}(\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})

\displaystyle=B_{h}(\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})

\displaystyle=B_{h}(\bm{G}_{h}-\bm{G},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}).

The consistency of discrete operators and (
7b
) yield

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=N_{h}(\bm{u};\bm{u},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})-N_{h}(\bm{u}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})

\displaystyle+b_{h}^{*}(p-p_{h},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}).

Lemmas
2.6
,
4.2
, (
13
) and (
7c
) lead to

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=N_{h}(\bm{u};\bm{u},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})-N_{h}(\bm{u}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})

\displaystyle+b_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u},I_{h}p-p_{h})+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})

\displaystyle=N_{h}(\bm{u};\bm{u},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})-N_{h}(\bm{u}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u})

\displaystyle+\nu^{-1}(\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G},\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G})

\displaystyle\leq C\nu^{-1}(\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{0,4,h}+\left\|\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+C_{*}\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{h}^{2}.

Using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\nu^{-1}(1-C_{*}C_{B}^{-2}\nu^{-2}\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\nu^{-1}(\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{0,4,h}+\left\|\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

This coupled with the assumption
\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{*}^{-1}C_{B}^{2}\nu^{2}
yields

\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\left\|\bm{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{0,4,h}+\left\|\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Therefore, the proof is completed.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
\bm{f}_{0}
satisfies the assumption in Theorem
4.3
and the weak solution
(\bm{u},p)
to (
1
) belongs to
[H^{k+2}(\Omega)]^{2}\times H^{k+1}(\Omega)
.
Then it holds

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\nu h^{k+1}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\left\|p-p_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}(\nu\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}+\left\|p\right\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}).

In addition, the following superconvergence holds

\displaystyle\left\|J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.4
and Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\bm{G}_{h}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\nu^{2}\left\|\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{0,4,h}+\left\|\bm{G}-\Pi_{h}\bm{G}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}(\nu^{2}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{W^{k+1,4}(\Omega)}+\left\|\bm{G}\right\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}).

Note that
\bm{G}=\nu\nabla\bm{u}
, the Sobolev embedding theorem yields

\left\|\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{k+1}(\nu^{2}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{W^{k+1,4}(\Omega)}+\nu\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}).

Then the assumption on
\bm{f}_{0}
implies

\left\|\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\nu h^{k+1}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}.

This and Lemma
4.1
yield the superconvergence

\left\|J_{h}\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{h}\leq C\nu^{-1}\left\|\bm{G}-\bm{G}_{h}\right\|_{h}\leq Ch^{k+1}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}.

The second assertion can be derived by using the triangle inequality and Lemma
2.3
.

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{u}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\left\|\bm{u}_{h}-J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{k+2}(\Omega)}.

Finally, we prove the error estimates for the pressure variable.
By the inf-sup condition
[
3
]
and integration by parts, the following holds

\displaystyle C\left\|p_{h}-p\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{(\nabla\cdot\bm{v},p_{h}-p)}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{(\nabla\cdot\bm{v},p_{h}-I_{h}p)}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}+\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{(\nabla\cdot\bm{v},I_{h}p-p)}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{-b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{h}-I_{h}p)}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}+Ch^{k+1}\left\|p\right\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, it is enough to bound the first term on the right-hand side.
Using the definition and the stability estimate of
J_{h}
, we obtain

\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{-b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{h}-I_{h}p)}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}\leq C\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{-b_{h}(J_{h}\bm{v},p_{h}-I_{h}p)}{\left\|J_{h}\bm{v}\right\|_{h}}.

The error equation reads

\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{-b_{h}(\bm{v},p_{h}-I_{h}p)}{\left\|\bm{v}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}}\leq C\sup_{\bm{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{B_{h}(\bm{G}_{h}-\bm{G},J_{h}\bm{v})+N_{h}(\bm{u}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},J_{h}\bm{v})-N_{h}(\bm{u};\bm{u},J_{h}\bm{v})}{\left\|J_{h}\bm{v}\right\|_{h}}.

Combining Lemma
4.2
and the above estimates completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The usual finite element methods for the (Navier-)Stokes problem yields velocity error estimates that depend on
\nu^{-1}\left\|p-I_{h}p\right\|_{0}
.
Thanks to the special finite element pairs designed, our method yields divergence free velocity, thereby the velocity error estimate is independent of
\nu
.
The error estimates for velocity gradient and pressure also depend on the positive power of
\nu
, which is desirable when
\nu\ll 1
.

6
Conclusion

In this study, we developed a high-order polygonal staggered DG method for the Navier-Stokes equations.
A new finite element pair is introduced in the spirit of
[
47
]
, which enables us to achieve pressure robustness.
It is worth mentioning that the proposed scheme yields a divergence free velocity approximation without any postprocessing which is a desirable feature in the applications of (Navier-)Stokes equations.
Another novel contribution lies in the design a new nonlinear convective term that earns non-negativity.
A full convergence analysis is carried out, showing that the error estimate for velocity is independent of pressure and of the viscosity.
Finally, several numerical experiments are carried out, and we can observe that our scheme is indeed pressure robust. The numerical experiments indicate that our method is a good candidate for practical applications in particular for problems with high Reynolds number.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
4.2

In this appendix, we prove Lemma
4.2
.

Proof.

By adding and subtracting
N_{h}(J_{h}\bm{u};J_{h}\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})
, we obtain

\displaystyle N_{h}(\bm{u};\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})-N_{h}(\bm{u}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=N_{h}(\bm{e}_{*};\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})+N_{h}(J_{h}\bm{u};\bm{e}_{*},\bm{v}_{h})

(17)

\displaystyle N_{h}(\bm{e}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+N_{h}(J_{h}\bm{u};\bm{e}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}).

We will bound each term separately.
First we have

\displaystyle N_{h}(\bm{e}_{*};\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=-(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{e}_{*},\nabla\bm{v}_{h})+\left\langle\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{*}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\},\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{u}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}.

The first term on the right-hand side can be bounded using Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev inequality

-(\bm{u}\otimes\bm{e}_{*},\nabla\bm{v}_{h})\leq C\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{e}_{*}\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\left\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}.

Let us focus on the second term.
For
e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
, the trace inequality (
5
) implies that

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{*}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\},\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{u}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{e}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{*}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right\|_{L^{4}(e)}\left\|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{u}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right\|_{L^{4}(e)}\left\|\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{L^{4}(T)}+h_{T}^{1/2}\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{1}(T)}\right)h_{T}^{1/4}\left\|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{*}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right\|_{L^{4}(e)}h_{T}^{-1/2}\left\|\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{2}(e)},

where
T
is a neighboring triangle of
e
.
By using shape regularity
h_{T}\leq\rho h_{e}
, summing over edges yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{*}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\},\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{u}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}\leq C\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{e}\left\|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{*}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right\|_{L^{4}(e)}^{4}\right)^{1/4}\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{e}^{-1}\left\|\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By combining these, we obtain

N_{h}(\bm{e}_{*};\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})\leq C\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{e}_{*}\right\|_{0,4,h}\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{h},

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{0,4,h}
is defined in (
4
).
Similarly, we obtain

N_{h}(J_{h}\bm{u};\bm{e}_{*},\bm{v}_{h})\leq C\left\|J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{e}_{*}\right\|_{0,4,h}\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{h}\leq C\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{e}_{*}\right\|_{0,4,h}\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|.

It remains to estimate the last two terms on the right hand side of (
17
) . Observe that

\displaystyle N_{h}(\bm{e}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=-(\bm{u}_{h}\otimes\bm{e}_{h},\nabla\bm{v}_{h})+\left\langle\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\},\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{u}_{h}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}+\left\langle|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\}|,\left\llbracket\bm{u}_{h}\right\rrbracket\cdot\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\rangle_{\mathcal{F}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{e}_{h}\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}\left(\left\|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{u}_{h}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right\|_{L^{4}(e)}+\left\|\left\llbracket\bm{u}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{4}(e)}\right)\left\|\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{e}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\}\!\!\right\}\right\|_{L^{4}(e)}\left\|\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}.

The discrete trace inequality (
2.2
) yields

\displaystyle N_{h}(\bm{e}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{e}_{h}\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle+C\left\|\bm{e}_{h}\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}_{h}}h_{e}^{-1}\left\|\left\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By (
6
), we obtain

N_{h}(\bm{e}_{h};\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\leq C\left\|\bm{u}_{h}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{e}_{h}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{h}.

Similarly, we obtain

N_{h}(J_{h}\bm{u};\bm{e}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})\leq C\left\|J_{h}\bm{u}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{e}_{h}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{h}\leq C\left\|\bm{u}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|\bm{e}_{h}\right\|_{h}\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{h}.

Combining the preceding estimates with Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
3.4
concludes the proof.

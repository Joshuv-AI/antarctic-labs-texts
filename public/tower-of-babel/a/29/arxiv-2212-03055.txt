Homomorphic images of algebraic groups
Uri Bader and Elyasheev Leibtag

Abstract
. We study topological group theoretic properties of algebraic groups over local fields. In particular, we find conditions under which such groups have closed images under arbitrary continuous homomorphisms into arbitrary topological groups.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a local field, and let
{\mathbf{G}}
denote a linear algebraic group over
k
.
The group
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
has a natural locally compact topology, called
the
k
-point analytic topology
, making it a topological group.
In this paper, we are interested in properties of
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
viewed as a topological group.

Convention
.

Throughout this paper, unless explicitly specified otherwise, all topological groups are assumed to be Hausdorff, hence Tychonoff.

A topological group that admits no weaker (Hausdorff) group topology is called
minimal
.
It is straightforward that every compact group is minimal.
Moreover, the center of a Polish minimal group must be compact, see Lemma
4.14
.
In
[
Omo66
]
and
[
Got73
]
, H. Omori and M. Goto showed that for connected real algebraic groups this necessary condition is in fact sufficient - a connected real algebraic group with a compact center is minimal.
The following is an extension of the above result which applies to an arbitrary local field of characteristic zero, see Theorem
7.1
.

Theorem A
.

Let
k
be a local field of characteristic zero, and let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected
k
-algebraic group.
Then the group
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
is minimal if and only if the center of
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
is compact.

We note that the work of Omori and Goto uses the connectivity of the real group
G
in an essential way.
Connectivity is a feature lacking over non-Archimedian local fields which are totally disconnected. As such, our proof of Theorem
A
is entirely different from theirs.

It is a standard fact that
a Polish topological group is minimal if and only if
its image is closed under any
injective
continuous group homomorphism into any topological group, see Corollary
4.13
below.
A topological group is said to be
sealed
if its image is closed under any (not necessarily injective) continuous group homomorphism into any topological group.
Clearly, a Polish sealed group must have a compact center for all of its quotients.
Relying on the work of Omori and Goto,
Mayer showed in
[
May97
]
that
for connected locally compact groups this necessary condition is in fact sufficient - a connected locally compact group such that all of its quotients have a compact center is sealed.
The following is a generalization that applies to connected algebraic groups over an arbitrary local field of characteristic zero, see Theorem
8.1
.

Theorem B
.

Let
k
be a local field of characteristic zero and let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected
k
group with a
k
-Levi Decomposition,
{\mathbf{L}}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
. The following are equivalent

(1)

The group
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
is sealed.

(2)

For any normal unipotent
k
-algebraic subgroup
{\mathbf{N}}\lhd{\mathbf{G}}
the center
Z(({\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{N}})(k))
is compact.

(3)

The center of the reductive group
{\mathbf{L}}
is anisotropic (equivalently, the center of
{\mathbf{L}}(k)
is compact) and the action of
{\mathbf{L}}
on
\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
is with no non-trivial fixed points,

(4)

The center of the reductive group
{\mathbf{L}}
is anisotropic (equivalently, the center of
{\mathbf{L}}(k)
is compact) and the action of
{\mathbf{L}}
on
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}}))
is with no invariant vectors.

1.1.
Previous literature

As already mentioned, minimality questions for connected groups were considered by
Omori, Goto and Mayer, see
[
Got73
]
,
[
Omo66
]
and
[
May97
]
.
In the special case of semisimple groups, Theorem
A
and
B

were established by Bader-Gelander in
[
BG17
]
over an arbitrary local filed.
In another direction, an interesting recent work by Megrelishvili and Shlossberg gives minimality criteria for groups of the form
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(F)
, where
F\leq k
is an arbitrary subfield, see
[
MS22
]
, as well as
[
Shl22
]
concerning number theoretic applications.

For an extensive survey on minimality and closed image properties we refer the reader to
[
DM14
]
,
[
Meg98
]
,
[
DU98
]
and
[
Ban17
]
.

Lately, Carter and Willis investigated the sealed property in the context of locally compact totally disconnected groups arising as automorphism groups of buildings, see
[
CW22
]
.

Regarding infinite-dimensional isometry groups, Duchesne showed that the Polish topology of the isometry group of the infinite-dimensional hyperbolic space is minimal

[
Duc20
]
.

In a recent work by Ghadernezhad and de la Nuez González, the authors investigate minimality of the automorphism group of homogeneous structures, see
[
GG19
]
. Moreover, in
[
dlNG22
]
, de la Nuez González points out that the compact-open topology on the
homeomorphism group of a surface without
boundary is minimal.

1.2.
A note on positive characteristic

In proving Theorems
A
and
B
, we use the existence of a Levi Decomposition over
k
, and that the connected
k
-unipotent radical is
k
-split. Moreover, in proving Theorem
B
, we use the fact that the Levi factor is completely reducible. These properties are always satisfied in zero characteristic, but do not generally hold over fields of positive characteristics, hence the zero characteristic assumption we take in Theorems
A
and
B
.
However, it seems that even over a field of positive characteristic, these assumptions could be removed in many cases, maybe in all cases.
This direction of study will be carried elsewhere.
Yet, as a “proof of concept”, we show in Section
9
how the tools developed in this paper may be used to reprove the aforementioned result of Bader-Gelander, namely proving Theorems
A
and
B

in the special case of semisimple groups over an arbitrary local field.

1.3.
Description of the paper

In section
2

we introduce a baby case presenting the main techniques and ideas that will be used throughout the paper. In particular, the impotent role played by admitting a continuous linear action on a finite-dimensional topological vector space.
In section
3
we recall a few topological properties of
k
-points of algebraic groups. We also provide auxiliary lemmas concerning groups acting by automorphisms on locally compact abelian groups.
Section
4
is devoted to a brief survey of the minimal and sealed group properties, as well as the relative properties for subgroups. The importance of these relative properties is that in order to show minimality, we show relative minimality and co-minimality of a subgroup, usually the normal subgroup under a semi-direct product structure.

We point out that the standard topology on a finite-dimensional vector space over a local field
is not
minimal.
In section
5
we show that the standard topology on a finite-dimensional vector space over a local field
is
minimal amongst all group topologies admitting a continuous action by an adequate multiplicative subgroup.
Section
6
shows that if a vector space is a representation space of an algebraic torus then the torus acts on the vector space by an adequate multiplicative subgroup of the field, thus making the vector space minimal with respect to a continuous torus action.

In section
7
we prove Theorem
A
.
We first prove it for solvable groups, using the linear action of the torus on the unipotent radical, and then treat the general case by finding a co-compact solvable subgroup (the solvable radical of a minimal parabolic) and showing that it is minimal.
Section
8
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
B
. As in the baby case of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)
, we first show how minimality of simple groups implies the sealed property for these groups. For general algebraic groups, we show by induction on the nilpotency degree in the unipotent radical that the radical is relatively sealed.
In section
9
we show how the tools developed in this paper can be used to prove previously known results for semisimple groups over local fields of any characteristic.

Baby case

In this section, we clarify some of the ideas involved in our proofs by applying them to the special
cases (already well-know)

and

2.9

2.10

.

Here
k

2.1.
Some general facts

Theorem 2.1

(Banach,
[
Ban31
]
)
.

Every continuous surjective morphism between Polish groups is open.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a Polish group with topology
\sigma
. If
G
admits a weaker group topology
\omega
, and a bijective
\omega
-continuous homomorphism
f\colon G\to H
onto a Polish group
H
, then
\omega=\sigma
.

Proof.

Since
\omega
is weaker than
\sigma
, the map
f
is
\sigma
-continuous as well. By the Open Mapping Theorem
2.1
, the map
f
is a homeomorphism onto
H
. For any open set
W\in\sigma
,
f(W)
is open in
H
, since
f
is
\omega
-continuous,
f^{-1}(f(W))=W
is open in
\omega
, thus
\omega=\sigma
.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Merson, Lemma 7.2.3
[
DPS90
]
)
.

Let
G
be a topological group with topology
\sigma
, and let
\omega\leq\sigma
a weaker group topology. If there exists a subgroup
H\leq G
, such that both the subspace topologies coincide on
H
, and both quotient topologies coincide on
G/H
, then
\omega=\sigma
.

Proof.

Let
S\in\sigma
be a
\sigma
-open identity neighborhood. We will show that there exist
W\in\omega
, a
\omega
-open identity neighborhood, with
W\subset S
.
Let
S^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\sigma
be a symmetric identity neighborhood such that
S^{\frac{1}{2}}S^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset S
.
Since both subspace topologies coincide on
H
, there exists
U\in\omega
an open identity neighborhood, with
U\cap H=S^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap H
.
Let
U^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\omega
be a symmetric open identity neighborhood such that
U^{\frac{1}{2}}U^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset U
.
Since both quotient topologies coincide, we have that the
\sigma
-open subset
(S^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap U^{\frac{1}{2}})H
is
\omega
-open as well.
We set
W=(S^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap U^{\frac{1}{2}})H\cap U^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\omega
.
Fixing
y\in W
we find
x\in S^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap U^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
h\in H
such that
y=xh
.
Then
h=x^{-1}y\in U^{\frac{1}{2}}U^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset U
. So
h\in U\cap H\subset S^{\frac{1}{2}}
, making
y=xh\in S^{\frac{1}{2}}S^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset S
, as needed.
∎

A general strategy for proving minimality of a group
G
, using the Merson Lemma, is by showing that there exists a subgroup
H\leq G
, such that for all weaker group topologies, the subspace topology on
H
and the quotient topology on
G/H
coincide with the a-piori stronger subspace and quotient topologies.

2.2.
Weaker additive group topologies on vector spaces

Let
k
be a local field with absolute value
|\cdot|
, and let
V
be a
n
-dimensional
k
-vector space with norm
\|\cdot\|
.
We consider the topology on
k
to be the locally compact field topology, and the topology on
V
to be the locally compact norm topology.

Lemma 2.4
.

Any additive group topology on
V
which is strictly weaker than the norm topology does not contain bounded open sets.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a weaker topology on
V

and assume that there exists a bounded open
0_{V}
-neighborhood
W\in\omega
with
\|W\|<\lambda
.
We will show that every norm-open ball contains an
\omega
-neighborhood, and hence that the norm topology is equivalent to
\omega
.
Let
B_{\varepsilon}\subset V
be the norm-open ball of radius
\varepsilon>0
.
Denote by
\overline{B_{\lambda}}
the norm-closed ball of radius
\lambda
, and

\operatorname{ann}(\varepsilon,\lambda)\coloneqq\overline{B}_{\lambda}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
.
The annulus
\operatorname{ann}(\varepsilon,\lambda)
is compact in the norm topology, so it is compact with respect to a weaker topology
\omega
as well.
As any group topology is Tychonoff, there exists a
\omega
-open
0_{V}
-neighborhood
W^{\prime}
, such that
W^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{ann}(\varepsilon,\lambda)=\emptyset
.
Finally, we get that the
\omega
-open set
W^{\prime}\cap W
is contained in the open ball
B_{\varepsilon}
, as needed.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
B_{\lambda}
be the open ball of radius
\lambda
in
k
, and let
m\colon B_{\lambda}\times V\to V
be the scalar multiplication map. Then there does not exist a strictly weaker additive group topology on
V
such that the multiplication map
m
, is a continuous map.

Proof.

As all norms on finite dimensional vector spaces induce the same topology, we may assume without loss of generality that
\|\cdot\|
is an
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
norm.
Assume by contradiction that
\omega
is a strictly weaker group topology on
V
such that the scalar multiplication map

m\colon B_{\lambda}\times V\to V
is continuous with respect to
\omega
.
The unit sphere
S_{1}
is norm-compact and therefore
\omega
-compact.
Let
W\in\omega
be a
0_{V}
-neighborhood not intersecting the norm sphere
S_{1}
.
By continuity of
m
there exist
W^{\prime}\in\omega
a
0_{V}
-neighborhood, and
\varepsilon<\lambda
such that
m(B_{\varepsilon}\times W^{\prime})\subset W
.
By assumption,
\omega
is strictly weaker than the norm topology, hence by Lemma
2.4

W^{\prime}
is unbounded.
Let
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
be a vector such that
\varepsilon^{-1}<\|w^{\prime}\|
. Since the norm is
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
there exists a scalar
x\in k
such that
|x|=\|w^{\prime}\|^{-1}
. We get that
(x,w^{\prime})\in B_{\varepsilon}\times W^{\prime}
, making
m(x,w^{\prime})=xw^{\prime}\in S_{1}
as
\|xw^{\prime}\|=|x|\cdot\|w^{\prime}\|=1
, in contradiction to
W
not intersecting the unit sphere.
∎

In particular, we get as a corollary a fundamental theorem in functional analysis.

Corollary 2.6

(
[
Bou87
, Chapter I.§2.3 Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
k
be a local field. Any
n
-dimensional topological
k
-vector space
V
is homeomorphic to the topological norm space
k^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional topological vector space over a local field
k
. This means that
V
admits an additive group topology
\tau
, such that the scalar multiplication map

m\colon k\times V\to V
is continuous.
Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a basis of
V
.
For any
i=1,...,n
the map

m_{i}\colon k\to V\ \ m_{i}\colon k\mapsto k\cdot e_{i}

is continuous. And since
\tau
is an additive group topology we get a continuous additive group homomorphism from the product
k^{n}
onto
V
.

\Psi\colon k^{n}\to V\ \ \Psi\colon(k_{1},...,k_{n})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\cdot e_{i}

The continuity of
\Psi
implies that the
\tau
topology on
V
is weaker than the norm topology.
By Lemma
2.5
above,
\tau
is equal to the norm topology, and therefore the topology is unique.
∎

2.3.
Minimality of the homothety group

Equipped with the product topology, the continuous action of
k^{*}
on
V
makes
A=k^{*}\ltimes V
a topological group. We call the group
A
the homothety group of
V
, and we call the standard product topology on
A
the analytic topology.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\omega
be a group topology on
A
weaker than the analytic topology. Then the restriction of
\omega
to the subspace
V
is equal to the analytic norm topology.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be the action map of
k^{*}
on
V
, that is,

\alpha\colon k^{*}\times V\to V\times V,\ \ \alpha\colon(r,v)\mapsto(rv,v)

Since
\omega
is a group topology on
A
, the action by conjugation is continuous with respect to the
\omega
-subspace topologies, thus the map
\alpha
is continuous with respect to the
\omega
-subspace topologies on
k^{*}
and on
V
.
By strengthening the topology on the domain, the map
\alpha
is still continuous.
So by letting
V
admit the
\omega
-subspace topology
\omega_{V}
, and embellishing
k^{*}
with analytic topology,
the map
\alpha
remains continuous.
Since
\omega_{V}
is an additive group topology on
V
, the subtraction operation

\mathrm{-}\colon V\times V\to V,\ \ \mathrm{-}\colon(v,w)\mapsto v-w

is
\omega_{V}
-continuous.
Observe that on the local field
k
, the addition by
1
map,
\mathrm{+_{1}}\colon B_{1}\to k^{*}
mapping
a
to
a+1
is continuous.
We get that the map
m\coloneqq\mathrm{-}\circ\alpha\circ(\mathrm{+_{1}}\times Id)

m\colon B_{1}\times V\to V\ \ m\colon(x,v)\mapsto(x+1)v-v=xv

is a continuous multiplication by scalars in the unit ball.
By Lemma
2.5
above, the restricted topology
\omega_{V}
on
V
is equal to the norm topology.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\omega
be a group topology on
A
, which is weaker than the analytic topology. Then the
\omega
-quotient topology on
A/V
,
\omega_{A/V}
, is equal to the analytic quotient topology on
A/V
, which is isomorphic to
k^{*}
.

Proof.

The subgroup
V
is normal in
A
,
so the action by conjugation
A\times V\to V
is continuous with respect to the
\omega
-subspace topologies.
Since
V
is in the kernel of the action, we get continuous action of the quotient group
A/V
on
V
.
As abstract groups
A/V
is isomorphic to
k^{*}
and the action on
V
is by scalar multiplication on the vector space
V
.
By Lemma
2.7
, the
\omega
-subspace topology
\omega_{V}
, is equal to the norm topology on
V
.
We, therefore, get a continuous representation
\pi\colon A/V\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
onto the closed analytic subgroup of scalars,
k^{*}\leq\mathrm{GL}(V)
.
Thus
\pi
is a continuous bijection onto a Polish group, so by Corollary
2.2
, the
\omega_{A/V}
-quotient topology is equal to the Polish analytic topology on
A/V
.
∎

Proposition 2.9

(see
[
Meg98
, Theorem 4.7(b)]
)
.

The homothety group
k^{*}\ltimes V
, endowed with the analytic topology, is minimal.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a group topology on
A=k^{*}\ltimes V
, weaker than the analytic topology.
By Lemma
2.7
, the subspace topology
\omega_{V}
is equal to the analytic subspace topology, and by Lemma
2.8
the
\omega
-quotient topology on
A/V
is equal to the quotient of the analytic topology. Hence by Lemma
2.3
,
\omega
is equal to the analytic topology.
∎

2.4.
Minimality and sealedness of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)

Proposition 2.10

(
[
BG17
, Theorem 5.4]
)
.

The group
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)
, endowed with the analytic topology, is minimal.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a group topology on
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)
, weaker than the analytic topology.
The Borel subgroup
P
, is isomorphic to the homothety group
k^{*}\ltimes k
, hence by Proposition
2.9
,
P
is minimal. Thus for any weaker group topology
\omega
, the subspace topology on
P
coincides with the analytic topology.
Moreover, since the analytic topology is Polish,
P
is
\omega
-closed. Hence the
\omega
-quotient topology on
G/P
is Hausdorff and since the analytic quotient topology on
G/P
is compact it does not admit any strictly weaker typology.
By Merson’s Lemma
2.3

\omega
is equal to the analytic topology on
G
.
∎

Proposition 2.11

(
[
BG17
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

The group
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)
, endowed with the analytic topology, is sealed.

Proof.

Let
f\colon G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)\to H
be a continuous homomorphism into a topological group
H
.
By
[
Mar91
, Chapter I, Theorem (1.5.6)(i) and Theorem (2.3.1)(b)]
, any non-trivial normal subgroup of
G
is co-compact, thus if
f
is not injective
f(G)
is compact and obviously closed.
We thus assume as we may that
f
is injective.
By Proposition
2.10
, the
f
-initial topology
\tau^{f}
must coincide with the analytic topology on
G
.
Therefore, we get that
f
is a homeomorphism onto its image.
Therefore the subspace topology on
f(G)
is Polish, hence closed, see
[
Mel16
, Theorem 2.16]
.
∎

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Algebraic groups over local fields

We assume that the reader is familiar with the basic theory of algebraic groups.

Throughout this section we let
k
be a local field (of any characteristic), with absolute value
|\cdot|
, and denote by
\bar{k}
its algebraic closure.
Algebraic groups over
k
will be identified with their
\bar{k}
points and will be denoted by boldface letters. Their
k
-points will be denoted by corresponding Roman letters.
We regard
G={\mathbf{G}}(k)
as a topological group equipped with a
k
-analytic structure.

As seen in the next two propositions, for a local field
k
, the geometric properties of the group
{\mathbf{G}}
interact nicely with the topological
k
-point structure on
G
. Namely, under a
k
-morphism of algebraic groups the image of the
k
-points is topologically closed, and geometrically (Zariski) dense.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
Mar91
, Chapter I, Proposition (2.5.3)(ii)]
)
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected
k
group, then
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
is Zariski dense in
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
,
{\mathbf{H}}
be
k
-algebraic groups, and
f\colon{\mathbf{G}}\to{\mathbf{H}}
be a
k
-morphism. Then the image of the
k
-points,
f(G)
, is a closed subgroup of
H
.
Moreover, the kernel of
f
,
\ker(f)
is a
k
-algebraic group such that as
k
-analytic topological spaces
{\mathbf{G}}(k)/{\ker(f)}(k)\cong f(G)
.

Proof.

This follows by
[
BDL17
, Proposition 2.2]
, identifying
{\mathbf{H}}
as a
k
-variety with a
{\mathbf{G}}
action given by left multiplication via
f
, i.e.
g.h=f(g)h
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
,
{\mathbf{H}}
and
f
be as above.
Note that
f(G)
is Zariski dense in the
k
-algebraic group
f({\mathbf{G}})
. But generally,
f(G)
is not onto the
k
-points
f({\mathbf{G}})(k)
. For example let
k={\mathbb{R}}
, take
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{SL}_{2}
and let
f
be the central quotient map. Observe that

f(G)=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})\subsetneq\mathrm{PGL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})=f({\mathbf{G}})(k)

As seen in the remark above, one should be careful when dealing with
k
-points of quotient varieties, as these may not be
k
-points of a
k
-algebraic quotient.
We recall that in the case of a parabolic subgroup, or in the case of split solvable groups, the quotient of the
k
-points is equal to the
k
-points of the quotient group.

Definition 3.4

(
[
Bor12
, Definition 15.1]
)
.

A connected solvable
k
-group is called
split
if it admits a composition series by
k
-subgroups with successive quotients
k
-isomorphic to
{\mathbf{G}}_{m}
or
{\mathbf{G}}_{a}
.

Remark 3.5

(
[
Bor12
, Theorem 15.4]
)
.

If the field
k
is perfect, then any connected unipotent group is
k
-split.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
Bor12
, Corollary 15.7]
)
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected
k
-algebraic group and
{\mathbf{A}}
a normal connected
k
-split solvable subgroup. Then the
k
-point algebraic map
\pi\colon G\to{\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{A}}(k)
is surjective, i.e.
G/A={\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{A}}(k)
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected
k
-algebraic group. A subgroup
P\leq G
is called parabolic if

P={\mathbf{P}}(k)
for some
k
-parabolic subgroup
{\mathbf{P}}<{\mathbf{G}}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected
k
-algebraic group, and
P\leq G
a parabolic subgroup. Then the center of the parabolic subgroup,
Z(P)
, equals the center of the group,
Z(G)
.

Proof.

Denote by
{\mathbf{P}}
the Zariski closure of
P
. This is a parabolic subgroup.
Since
G
is Zariski dense in
{\mathbf{G}}
,
Z(G)
is central in
{\mathbf{G}}
, hence contained in
{\mathbf{P}}
.
We conclude that
Z(G)<Z(P)
.
We will argue to show the other inclusion,
Z(P)<Z(G)
.

For any element in the center of
P
,
z\in Z(P)
, we may define a
k
-morphism of algebraic varieties

\mathbf{c}_{z}\colon{\mathbf{G}}\to{\mathbf{G}}\ \ g\mapsto[g,z]

The map
\mathbf{c}_{z}
is a
k
-morphism of algebraic varieties, as it is defined by group operations in
{\mathbf{G}}
.
Since
P
is Zariski dense in
{\mathbf{P}}
, and
P\subset\mathbf{c}_{z}^{-1}(\{e\})
, we get that
{\mathbf{P}}\subset\mathbf{c}_{z}^{-1}(\{e\})
. Implying that for any
g\in{\mathbf{G}}
,
p\in{\mathbf{P}}
,
\mathbf{c}_{z}(gp)=\mathbf{c}_{z}(g)
.
So the map
\mathbf{c}_{z}
factors to a
k
-morphism
\hat{\mathbf{c}}_{z}\colon{\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbf{G}}
.
Since
{\mathbf{P}}
is parabolic, the quotient variety
{\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{P}}
is a connected complete
k
-variety. Therefore the map
\hat{\mathbf{c}}_{z}
must be constant.
Thus
z\in Z({\mathbf{G}})
, and in particular in
Z(G)
, as needed.
∎

3.2.
Faithful actions on vector spaces

This subsection is devoted to proving proposition

bellow.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
L
be a group acting faithfully by automorphisms on an abelian group
A
, and denote
G=L\ltimes A
. Then any non-trivial normal subgroup
M\lhd G
intersects
A
non-trivially.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
M\cap A=\{e\}
.
Observe that
[M,A]\subset M\cap A=\{e\}
, let
m=l_{m}a_{m}
be an element in
M
. For any
a\in A
,
[a,m]=e
, so
a=mam^{-1}=l_{m}al_{m}^{-1}
. By the faithfulness of the
L
-action, we get that
l=e
. Therefore
M\subset M\cap A=\{e\}
is trivial in contradiction to
M
being non-trivial.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
k
be a local field, and
V
be a finite-dimensional
k
-representation of a group
L
.
Let
N\leq V
be an
L
invariant additive subgroup.
If the
L
representation
V
is faithful and irreducible, then the
L
action on the quotient

is faithful.

Proof.

By self-duality of local fields, the Pontryagin dual of the vector space
V
,
\widehat{V}
, is a vector space isomorphic to
V
.
The
L
action on the Pontryagin dual
\widehat{V}
is defined by pre-composition and is linear irreducible and faithful as well.
Let
N^{0}=\left\{\chi\in\widehat{V}:\ \forall n\in N\ \chi(n)=1\right\}
be the annihilator of the subgroup
N
. The annihilator
N^{0}
is
L
invariant, so by irreducibility
N^{0}
contains a linear basis of the dual space
\widehat{V}
.
Therefore the
L
action on
N^{0}
is faithful making the
L
action on the dual
\widehat{N^{0}}\cong V/N
faithful as well.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
k
be a local field, and
V
be a finite-dimensional
k
-representation of a group

and set
G=L\ltimes V
.
If the
L

is faithful and irreducible, then any normal subgroup

is either contained in

or contains

.

Proof.

Assume

, and denote

.
The group

acts by automorphisms on

and we may identify the quotient
G/N
with

. By lemma

the action of

on

is faithful. The normal subgroup

intersects

trivially, hence by lemma

is trivial implying that

, thus

as needed.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
k={\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for some prime
p
, and let

be an
n
-dimensional
k
-vector space.
Any additive subgroup

which does not contain a vector subspace is compact.

Proof.

We will show that

is compact by showing its Pontryagin dual is discrete.
Observe that if

is contained in a vector subspace

, then

contains the linear subspace

.
Since

Hence,

4.
Minimal and Sealed groups

The purpose of this section is to survey two topological properties of groups, called minimality and sealdness, as well as a relative version of these properties.
The objects studied in this article are
k
-points of algebraic groups over a local field
k
.
Therefore we will pay attention to the case where
G
is a locally compact Polish topological group.
In order to keep this article self-contained, we include proofs for the special case of locally compact Polish groups. However, many of the lemmas are true for a more general class of topological groups.
For a full and comprehensive account of minimal group topologies (as well as the relative notions for subgroups) and closed image properties we refer the ready to
[
DM10
]
,
[
DM14
]
,
[
Meg98
]
,
[
DU98
]
and
[
Ban17
]
.

4.1.
Minimal topologies on groups

Let
G
be a group, with a group topology
\tau
. That is
\tau
is a (Hausdorff) topology on
G
such that the multiplication and inverse operations are continuous. Any subgroup of a topological group is a topological group with the subspace topology. We will denote the subspace topology on
H\leq G
by
\tau_{H}
, and denote the quotient topology on
G/H
by
\tau_{G/H}
.
Given two group topologies
\omega
and
\tau
on
G
, we say that a group topology
\omega
is weaker than
\tau
if
\omega\leq\tau
, that is, any
\omega
-open set is
\sigma
-open. We say
\omega
is strictly weaker than
\sigma
, if
\omega\leq\sigma
and
\omega\neq\sigma
.

Definition 4.1
.

A topological group
G
is called
minimal
if it does not admit a strictly weaker (Hausdorff) group topology.

Example 4.2
.

Compact groups are minimal. Observe that the identity map between the strong and weak topologies is a continuous and closed bijection.

Example 4.3
.

The homothety group
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
is minimal (See proposition
2.9
).

Example
4.3
, is a well-known result (see
[
DS79
]
), the minimality of the homothety group follows particularly from the work of Omori
[
Omo66
, Theorem 1.1]
, which classifies all minimal real algebraic groups.

Example 4.4

(Omori)
.

Any real algebraic group with a compact center is minimal.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
G
and
H
be topological groups, and
f\colon G\to H
be an injective continuous homomorphism. The topology on
G
generated by inverse images of open subsets in
H
is called the
f
-initial topology
and is denoted
\tau^{f}
.

Remark 4.6
.

The initial topology
\tau^{f}
is a group topology weaker than the given topology on
G
, and the map
f
is open with respect to the initial topology.
Therefore, if
G
is minimal then any injective continuous homomorphism is a topological embedding onto the image.

Theorem 4.7

(Stoyanov, Prodanov
[
SP84
]
)
.

Let
G
be a Polish abelian group. Then
G
is minimal if and only if it is compact.

Proof.

By the Stoyanov and Prodanov Theorem
[
DPS90
, Theorem 2.7.7]
, any abelian minimal group is pre-compact. Hence Polish abelian minimal groups are compact.
In this paper, we are interested in locally compact Polish groups, for sake of completeness, we provide a short argument in the case
G
is locally compact abelian.
Let
\beta\colon G\to\beta G
be the Bohr compactification of the locally compact abelian group
G
, see
[
Kat04
, VII.5]
.
Since minimal groups are homeomorphic to their images, we get that
G
is homeomorphic to
\beta(G)
, and since
G
is locally compact,
\beta(G)
is locally compact and hence closed, implying that
G
is compact.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G
be a second countable topological group.
For any
\omega
, a weaker group topology on
G
there exists a group topology
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
, such that
\omega^{\prime}
is second countable.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
denote the given second countable topology on
\sigma
, and let
\omega\leq\sigma
be a weaker group topology.
We will show that there exists a second countable weaker group topology
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
.

Let
\{W_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}\in\omega
be a system of open identity neighborhoods.
Since the group topology
\omega
is Tychonoff,
\cap_{\alpha\in A}\overline{W_{\alpha}}=\cap_{\alpha\in A}W_{\alpha}=\{e\}
, where
\overline{W_{\alpha}}
is the
\omega
-closure.
Observe that
\overline{W_{\alpha}}
is
\sigma
-closed as well. Thus we obtain an open cover
\bigcup_{\alpha\in A}(G\setminus W_{\alpha})=G\setminus\{e\}
.
The
\sigma
-subspace topology on
G\setminus\{e\}
is second countable and hence Lindelof, implying that there exists a countable subcover
\{W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}(G\setminus W_{i})=G\setminus\{e\}
, particularly
\cap_{i=1}^{\infty}{W_{i}}=\{e\}
.
By
[
BG17
, Proposition 5.6]
there exist a weaker, a priori not necessarily Hausdorff, group topology
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
, such that
\omega^{\prime}
is second countable and contains the collection
\{W_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
.
But since
\cap_{i=1}^{\infty}{W_{i}}=\{e\}
, the topology
\omega^{\prime}
is indeed Hausdorff and therefore
\omega^{\prime}
is a second countable weaker group topology.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
G
be a second countable group. If
G
does not admit any weaker second-countable group topologies then
G
is minimal.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
denote the given second countable topology on
G
, and let
\omega\leq\sigma
be a weaker group topology. By Lemma
4.8
there exist a weaker group topology
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
, such that
\omega^{\prime}
is second countable, therefore
\sigma=\omega^{\prime}
implying that
\sigma=\omega=\omega^{\prime}
, as needed.
∎

4.2.
Relative minimality

The reader may benefit from keeping in mind the following example.

Example 4.10
.

Consider the group
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
with the standard topology, and the subgroup
{\mathbb{R}}\leq{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
. Although the group
{\mathbb{R}}
is not minimal, it does have the following property (see Lemma
2.7
): any weaker topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
agrees with the standard topology on the subgroup
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
G
be a topological group, and let
U\leq G
be a closed subgroup.
We say that
U
is minimal in
G
if all weaker group topologies on
G
coincide on the subgroup
U
.

In the context of Polish topological groups, minimality can be interpreted in terms of a closed image property.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
G
be a Polish topological group, and let
U\leq G
be a closed subgroup. Then
U
is minimal in
G
if and only if for any injective continuous homomorphism
f\colon G\to H
into a topological group
H
, the image
f(U)
is closed in
H
.

Proof.

Assume that
U
is minimal in
G
.
Let
H
be a topological group and
f\colon G\to H
be an injective continuous homomorphism with a dense image.
The
f
-initial topology
\tau^{f}
is a weaker group topology on
G
, which must coincide on
U
.
Therefore, we get that the restriction
f_{U}\colon U\to H
is an open bijection onto
f(U)
.
Therefore the subspace topology on
f(U)
is Polish hence
f(U)
is closed, see
[
Mel16
, Theorem 2.16]
.

Now assume that the image of
U
is closed under any injective map into a topological group.
Denote by
\sigma
the given topology on
G
, and let
\omega\leq\sigma
be a weaker topology on
G
.
Following Corollary
4.9
we assume without loss of generality that
\omega
is second countable.

Let
\iota\colon G\to\overline{G}
be the completion of
G
with respect to the two-sided uniformity defined by the group topology
\omega
, see
[
Bou13
, Chapter III, §3, no. 4 ]
and
[
Rai46
]
, this completion is also known as the Raikov-completion of the group.

Since
\omega
is second countable, it follows by
[
Bou13
, Chapter III, §3, no. 4, Proposition 7]
that the group completion
\overline{G}
is Polish.
By assumption,
\iota(U)
is closed in
\overline{G}
, making
\iota(U)
a Polish group.
The completion map
\iota
is a homeomorphism onto the image, making
\iota|_{U}\colon U\to\iota(U)
a homeomorphism from
U
to
\iota(U)
.
Hence, with respect to the
\omega
-subspace topology,
U
is a Polish group.
Following the Banach Open Mapping Theorem
2.1
, restricted to
U
, the identity map onto the weaker topology is open, hence the Polish topology
\omega_{U}
is equal to the original group topology on
U
.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
G
be a Polish group. Then
G
is minimal if and only if the image of
G
under any injective continuous homomorphism is closed.

Lemma 4.14

(
[
DPS90
, Theorem 7.2.5]
)
.

Let
G
be a Polish group. If
G
is minimal, then its center,
Z(G)
, is compact.

Proof.

Denote by
Z=Z(G)
the center of
G
. Let
H
be a topological group and
f\colon Z\to H
a continuous injective homomorphism with a dense image.
We will show that
f(Z)
is closed in

Since

\Delta_{Z}=\{(z,f(z))\in G\times H:\ z\in Z\}

.
Let
\hat{f}\colon G\to(G\times H)/\Delta_{Z}
, be the quotient map restricted to

,
\hat{f}(g)=(g,e_{H})\Delta_{Z}
, and let
\delta\colon H\to(G\times H)/\Delta_{Z}
be the quotient map restricted to
\{e\}\times H
,
\delta(h)=(e_{G},h)\Delta_{Z}
.
Observe that
\hat{f}
is injective, so by minimality of

,
\hat{f}(G)
is closed. From the following commuting diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(G\times H)/\Delta_{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{f}}

we get that

, and as
\hat{f}
and

is a pre-image of the closed subset

, hence

We get that the center

is minimal therefore by Theorem

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
G
be a Polish group, and let
A_{1},A_{2}\lhd G

Then their product

Let

For any

Letting

be the quotient map, and
\iota\colon H/N_{1}\to H/N_{1}\times H/N_{2}

.
The maps

are continuous, making

For

4.3.
Co-minimality

Similarly to relative minimality, the reader may benefit from keeping in mind the following example.

Example 4.17
.

Consider the group
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
with the standard topology, and the subgroup
{\mathbb{R}}\leq{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
. Although the group
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
is not minimal, it does have the following property (see Lemma
2.8
): any weaker topology on
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}
induces the same quotient topology on the quotient
{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
G
be a topological group.
A closed subgroup
U\leq G
is said to be
co-minimal in
G
if any weaker topology on
G
induces the same quotient topology on the quotient space
G/U
.

Example 4.19
.

Any minimal co-compact subgroup is co-minimal.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
G
be a topological group, and
U\leq G
a subgroup. If
U
is minimal in
G
and co-minimal in
G
, then
G
is minimal.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
denote the given group topology on
G
. For any
\omega\leq\sigma
weaker group topology,
\omega_{U}=\sigma_{U}
and
\omega_{G/U}=\sigma_{G/U}
, hence by Merson’s Lemma
2.3
,
\omega=\sigma
.
∎

Corollary 4.21
.

Let
G
be a topological group, and
U\leq G
be a compact open subgroup. There does not exist a strictly weaker group topology
\omega
on
G
, such that
U
is open in
\omega
.

Proof.

Denote by
\sigma
the given group topology on
G
.
For any
\omega\leq\sigma
weaker group topology, by the compactness of
U
,
\omega_{U}=\sigma_{U}
. And since
U
is open with respect to both
\omega
and
\sigma
, the quotient topologies are both discrete, hence
\omega_{G/U}=\sigma_{G/U}
, hence by Lemma
4.20
,
\omega=\sigma
.
∎

4.4.
Minimal properties of semi-direct products of groups

A group topology on a semi-direct product

induces subspace topologies on the subgroups
L
and
U
, and
L
acts continuously on
U
by conjugation.
Conversely, for
L
and
U
topological groups with
L
acting by continuous automorphisms on
U
, the product topology is a topological group structure on
L\ltimes U
.
Here we will emphasize the relation between topological conditions on the action of
L
on
U
, and the minimality of
U
as a subgroup of
L\ltimes U
.

Remark 4.22
.

Assume
L
acts continuously by automorphisms on
U

C\leq L
is a subgroup in the kernel of the
L

(L\ltimes U)/C
with the quotient of the product topology is isomorphic to
L/C\ltimes U
with the product of the quotient topology.
If
N\lhd U
is an
L
-invariant closed normal subgroup then
(L\ltimes U)/N
with the quotient of the product topology is isomorphic to
L\ltimes U/N
with the product of the quotient topology.

Remark 4.23
.

Let
G
be a topological group acting continuously on a topological space
X
.
Then
G
acts continuously on
X
with respect to any stronger topology on
G
.

, acts continuously by conjugation on

with respect to the
\omega
-subspace topology,

.

, embellished with the stronger

\omega_{U}
on

.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L\times U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L/C\times U}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

4.5.
Sealed groups

We saw that Polish minimal groups have the property that any continuous injective homomorphism mapping them to another topological group has a closed image.
In this subsection, we are interested in groups having a closed image under any continuous homomorphism.

Definition 4.26
.

Let
G
be a topological group. We say that
G
is
sealed
if for any continuous homomorphism
f\colon G\to H
into a topological group
H
, the image
f(G)
is closed.

Example 4.27
.

Compact groups are sealed.

Example 4.28
.

Simple Polish minimal groups are sealed, as any homomorphism from a simple group is either trivial or injective.

Also here, the relative property is very useful.

Definition 4.29
.

For a subgroup
U\leq G
, we say that
U
is sealed in
G
, if for any continuous homomorphism
f\colon G\to H
into a topological group
H
, the image
f(U)\leq H
is closed.

Lemma 4.30

(Cf.
[
DU98
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

Let
N\lhd G
be a closed normal subgroup, with the quotient map
\pi\colon G\to G/N
.
If
N
is sealed in
G
and
U\leq G
is a subgroup such that
\pi(U)
is sealed in
\pi(G)
, then
UN
is sealed in
G
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon G\to H
be a continuous homomorphism with a dense image. Since the image of
N
is closed, we get that
f(N)
is a closed normal subgroup of
H
. Therefore, the induced map
\hat{f}\colon G/N\to H/f(N)
is a morphism of topological groups. Since
\pi(U)
is sealed in
\pi(G)
, we get that
\hat{f}(\pi(U))
is a closed subgroup in
H/f(N)
. Hence Its pre-image in
H
, which is equal to
f(UN)
, is closed.
∎

Corollary 4.31
.

Let
N\leq G
be a co-compact subgroup. If
N
is sealed, then
G
is sealed.

Proof.

Since compact groups are sealed, the proof follows from Lemma
4.30
above, by taking

.
∎

Corollary 4.32
.

If
U_{1},U_{2}
are normal subgroups that are sealed in

then
U_{1}U_{2}
is sealed in
G
.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon G\to G/U_{2}
be the quotient map. Given a topological group

, and continuous homomorphism

, the map

is a continuous homomorphism. Since

is sealed in

,

is closed in

. Hence
\pi(U_{1})
is sealed in

, so by Lemma

is sealed in

.
∎

Corollary 4.33

(
[
DU98
, Theorem 2.13]
)
.

If

are sealed groups, then so is

.

5.
Rigidity of scalar multiplication

Let

be a local field with absolute value
|\cdot|
, and let

.
We consider the topology on

Although the norm topology on

is not minimal (see Theorem

), we saw in Lemma

is minimal.

The main goal of this section is to prove Lemma

below. The lemma asserts that if the additive group

admits a continuous scalar multiplication restricted to an analytic multiplicative subgroup then the group topology on

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be a local field such that

is a finite filed extension of

, and let

be an infinite

-analytic subgroup.
Then

does not admit an additive group topology, which is strictly weaker than the norm topology, and such that the

action on

, given by scalar multiplication, is continuous.

Moreover, given an

-invariant compact subgroup

. The topological group

does not admit group topology that is strictly weaker than the quotient topology induced from

5.1.
Moderated subsets

In order to prove Lemma
5.1
we will need the notion of a moderated set. We will see in Lemma
5.8
that admitting a continuous multiplication over a moderated subset guarantees that the analytic topology on the vector space is minimal.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
A\subset k
be a subset containing
0
. We call the set

moderated
if

contains a null sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, i.e.
\lim_{n}\|a_{n}\|=0
, such that

for some

.

Example 5.3
.

Any 0-neighborhood in

is moderated.

Example 5.4
.

Let

and
A=S^{1}-1
where
S^{1}
is the unit circle in
{\mathbb{C}}
. For any value
\delta<1
, the set
A
contains an element
a
with absolute value
\delta

is moderated.

Example
5.4
can be viewed as a special case of Lemma

below, observing that the set

is an
{\mathbb{R}}
analytic subset of
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Assume
k
is a degree
m
extension of a local field
k_{0}
. We may view
k\simeq k_{0}^{m}
as an
m
-dimensional
k_{0}
-analytic manifold. Lemma

asserts us that

-analytic subsets are moderated.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
k
is a finite extension of a local field

, and let

be a positive dimensional

-analytic sub-manifold containing

is a moderated subset.

Proof.

Since
A
is a positive dimensional analytic sub-manifold, there exists an analytic immersion
f\colon B\to A
satisfying
f(0)=0
, where

is the open unit ball in
k_{0}
.
We let
L
be the derivative of
f
at
0
and denote
a=\|L(1)\|
.
We fix a non zero

and observe that for every
n

\|L(x^{n})\|=|x^{n}|\cdot a
.
We fix
\epsilon<a
small enough so
(a-\varepsilon)(a+\varepsilon)^{-1}>|x|
and let
\lambda=(a-\varepsilon)^{-1}(a+\varepsilon)|x|^{-1}
.
By definition of the differential, there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for

,

-\varepsilon<\frac{\|f(x^{n})\|}{|x^{n}|}-\frac{\|L(x^{n})\|}{|x^{n}|}<\varepsilon,

thus

0<a-\varepsilon<\frac{\|f(x^{n})\|}{|x^{n}|}<a+\varepsilon.

We set
a_{n}=f(x^{n+N})\in A
and note that for every

\frac{|a_{n}|}{|a_{n+1}|}=\frac{\|f(x^{n+N})\|}{|x^{n+N}|}\cdot\frac{|x^{n+N+1}|}{\|f(x^{n+N+1})\|}\cdot|x|^{-1},

thus

1<(a-\varepsilon)(a+\varepsilon)^{-1}|x|^{-1}<\frac{|a_{n}|}{|a_{n+1}|}<(a-\varepsilon)^{-1}(a+\varepsilon)|x|^{-1}=\lambda.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let

be a subset such that the scalar multiplication map

is continuous with respect to an additive group topology on

. Then the scalar multiplication

m_{1}\colon(S-1)\times V\to V,\ \ (s-1,v)\mapsto(s-1)\cdot v

is continuous.

Proof.

The map

sending

to

is continuous, maps the subset
S-1
to the subset
S
.
By continuity of
m_{0}
the map

\alpha\colon S\times V\to V\times V,\ \ (s,v)\mapsto(s\cdot v,v)

is continuous.
The subtraction of vectors is a continuous operation on
V
.
Denoting the subtraction map by
\mathrm{-}\colon V\times V\to V
, we get continuity of the the multiplication map,
m_{1}\coloneqq\mathrm{-}\circ\alpha\circ(\mathrm{+_{1}}\times Id)
,

m_{1}\colon(S-1)\times V\to V,\ \ (s-1,v)\mapsto s\cdot v-v=(s-1)\cdot v

as needed.
∎

5.2.
Weaker topologies on quotients of vector spaces

Similar to the assertion of Lemma

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

be a compact additive subgroup. Then any strictly weaker topology on

Proof.

If

We will see that, given a moderated subset in

Proof.

6.
Linear algebraic action of Tori

We will be using the following consequence of Schur’s Lemma.

Lemma 6.1

(Schur’s lemma)
.

Let
(\pi,V)
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
k
-representation of a commutative group
T
. Then the division ring
\text{End}_{T}(V)
is commutative and it is a finite field extension of
k
. Moreover, as
\text{End}_{T}(V)
-modules,
\text{End}_{T}(V)\cong V
.

Proof.

Since
T
is commutative, for any

the linear operator
\pi(t)
is intertwining. We therefore get a map
\pi:T\to\text{End}_{T}(V)
.
Irreducibility of the
T
representation implies that
V
is irreducible as an
\text{End}_{T}(V)
-module.
Therefore
V
must be a quotient of
\text{End}_{T}(V)
, but since
\text{End}_{T}(V)
is a division ring we get that

as
\text{End}_{T}(V)
-modules.
By irreducibly,
\pi(T)
spans
\text{End}_{T}(V)
, so the finite-dimensional division ring
\text{End}_{T}(V)
is commutative, and therefore a finite field extension of
k
.
∎

In this section we let
k
be a local field of characteristic zero and

be a finite-dimensional

-vector space.
We let

be a

-algebraic torus, and denote by

-points. We fix a

-rational representation of algebraic groups

. I.e.
\pi
is the
k
-point map of a rational

-representation. Note that by Zariski density of the

-points, if the algebraic representation of
{\mathbf{T}}
is irreducible then so is the representation of its

-point

.

The topology assumed on the

-points of a

-algebraic group is the

-analytic topology, which we will refer to as the analytic topology.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that the representation
\pi
is irreducible. Let

Proof.

By Schur’s Lemma above

,

is a representation into

The representation map

By the Banach Open Mapping Theorem

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T\times V}

The primary objective of this section is to prove the following.

Henceforth in this section, we let

7.1.
Relative minimality of the unipotent radical

We will show relative minimality of the unipotent radical

.
As a first step, we show that the center of the unipotent radical is relatively minimal.

Lemma 7.2

has a compact center, then the center of the unipotent radical,

, is minimal in
G
.

Proof.

We let
{\mathbf{L}}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
be a Levi decomposition of
{\mathbf{G}}
, thus
{\mathbf{L}}
is a reductive
k
-group.
Denoting by
{\mathbf{V}}
the center of the unipotent radical
\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
, we get that
V={\mathbf{V}}(k)=Z(\mathcal{R}_{u}(G))
, which is isomorphic to a finite dimensional
k
-vector space, and the action of
L
on
V
is
k
- linear, see the discussion in §
3.1.1
.

Let
\omega
be a weaker group topology on
G
.
We will construct a bijective
\omega_{V}
-continuous homomorphism
from
V
onto a Polish group,
and conclude by Corollary
2.2
that
V
is minimal in
G
.

Fix a maximal
k
-torus
{\mathbf{T}}\leq{\mathbf{L}}
,
and consider the corresponding
k
-rational representation
\pi\colon T\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
.
Denote by
V^{T}=\{v\in V:\pi(t)v=v\}
the subspace of
T
-invariant vectors in
V
.
Since the action of
T
on
V
is algebraic,
the quotient space
V_{T}=V/V_{T}
is the
k
-points of the algebraic quotient
{\mathbf{V}}_{T}={\mathbf{V}}/{\mathbf{V}}^{\mathbf{T}}
.
We get a
k
-rational representation
\pi_{T}\colon T\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{T})
such that the
T
action on
V_{T}
is without invariant vectors.

The
\omega
-subspace topology on
T\ltimes V
is a group topology, and with respect to the
\omega
-subspace topologies, the torus
T
acts continuously on
V
.
Since the action of
T
is continuous, the invariant subspace
V^{T}
is
\omega_{V}
-closed, making the
\omega
-quotient topology
\omega_{V/V^{T}}
on
V_{T}
a group topology.

Remark
4.23
implies that endowing
T
with the analytic topology, it still acts continuously on
V
equipped with the
\omega
-subspace topology,
\omega_{V}
.
Following Corollary
6.3
, the additive group
V_{T}
does not admit a weaker topology such that the action of
T
is continuous, hence the
\omega
-quotient topology
\omega_{V/V^{T}}
is equal to the analytic topology.

Let
\{{\mathbf{T}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a collection of maximal
k
-tori that generate
{\mathbf{L}}
. Let
\Psi
be the product of the quotient maps from
V
onto
V_{T_{i}}
. That is,
\Psi
is a
k
-morphism of algebraic groups

\Psi\colon V\to\prod_{i=1}^{m}V_{T_{i}}.

By Proposition
3.2
, the image
\Psi(V)
is a Polish subgroup.

For any
i=1,...,m
, the quotient map from

onto
V_{T_{i}}
is continuous with respect to the
\omega
induced topology.
Since the
\omega
-quotient typology,
\omega_{V/V^{T_{i}}}
, is equal to the analytic topology, the map
\Psi
is a
\omega_{V}
-continuous homomorphism onto the Polish group
\Psi(V)
.

We are left to show that
\Psi
is injective.
The kernel
\ker(\Psi)=\cap_{i=1}^{m}V^{T_{i}}
is fixed by all
{\mathbf{T}}_{i}
,
hence is fixed by
{\mathbf{L}}
, and in particular by
L
.
Since
\ker(\Psi)\leq V
is central in
\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)
, it is a central subgroup of

.
As

is assumed to have a compact center,
\ker(\Psi)
must be compact, thus
\ker(\Psi)
must be trivial, as it is a vector subspace of

.
We got that the map
\Psi
is indeed a
\omega_{V}
-continuous bijection from

onto a Polish space, as needed.
∎

Next, we observe that for any
k
-group, the relative minimality of the center of its unipotent radical implies the relative minimality of the unipotent radical itself.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
{\mathbf{U}}
be a connected unipotent
k
-group.
Let
\omega
be a group topology on
U
, weaker than the analytic topology.
If the
\omega
-subspace topology is equal to the analytic topology on
Z(U)
, then
\omega
is equal to the analytic topology on
U
.

Proof.

Let
\{{\mathbf{Z}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
denote the upper central series of the group
{\mathbf{U}}
. By Zariski-density, the collection
Z_{i}={\mathbf{Z}}_{i}(k)
is the upper central series for
U
.
We show that if the
\omega
-subspace topology on
Z_{i}
is equal to the analytic topology then the
\omega
-subspace topology on
Z_{i+1}
is equal to the analytic topology.

Indeed, assume
\omega_{Z_{i}}
is equal to the analytic topology.
Let
\rho\colon{\mathbf{Z}}_{i}\to{\mathbf{Z}}_{i}/{\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1}
be the algebraic quotient map, for any
u\in{\mathbf{U}}
define the
k
-morphism of algebraic varieties

\mathbf{c}_{u}\colon{\mathbf{Z}}_{i+1}\to{\mathbf{Z}}_{i},\ \mathbf{c}_{u}(h)=[h,u].

Observe that the composition
\varphi_{u}=\rho\circ\mathbf{c}_{u}
is a homomorphism, as for any
x,y\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{i+1}

\displaystyle\varphi_{u}(xy)

\displaystyle=[xy,u]\ \ {\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1}

\displaystyle=xyuy^{-1}(u^{-1}u)x^{-1}u^{-1}\ \ {\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1}

\displaystyle=x[y,u]ux^{-1}u^{-1}\ \ {\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1}

\displaystyle=xux^{-1}u^{-1}[y,u]\ \ {\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{u}(x)\varphi_{u}(y)

So
\varphi_{u}
is a
k
-morphism of algebraic groups, we will denote by
\varphi^{k}_{u}\colon Z_{i+1}\to({\mathbf{Z}}_{i}/{\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1})(k)
the map between the
k
-points.

We claim that there exists a finite collection
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\in U
such that
\cap_{j=1}^{m}\ker(\varphi^{k}_{u_{j}})=Z_{i}
.

First, we claim that it is enough to intersect over the Zariski-dense set
U
i.e. that

\bigcap_{u\in{\mathbf{U}}}\ker(\varphi_{u})=\bigcap_{u\in U}\ker(\varphi_{u})

Indeed, let
x\in\cap_{u\in U}\ker(\varphi_{u})
then

[x,U]\subset{\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1}
, by Zarsiki-density we get that
[x,{\mathbf{U}}]\subset{\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1}
, implying that
x\in\cap_{u\in{\mathbf{U}}}\ker(\varphi_{u})
.
So
\cap_{u\in{\mathbf{U}}}\ker(\varphi_{u})=\cap_{u\in U}\ker(\varphi_{u})={\mathbf{Z}}_{i}
as claimed.

Since any descending chain of Zariski closed subsets must eventually be constant, there exists a finite collection
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\in U
such that
\cap_{j=1}^{m}\ker(\varphi_{u_{j}})={\mathbf{Z}}_{i}
. Since
\ker(\varphi_{u_{j}})(k)=\ker(\varphi^{k}_{u_{j}})
, see Proposition
3.2
, we get that
\cap_{j=1}^{m}\ker(\varphi^{k}_{u_{j}})=Z_{i}
.

The product map

\Psi=\prod_{i=j}^{m}\varphi^{k}_{u_{j}}\colon Z_{i+1}\to({\mathbf{Z}}_{i}/{\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1})(k)^{m}

is
k
-algebraic homomorphism, hence its image
\Psi(Z_{i+1})
is a closed subgroup with respect to the analytic topology on
({\mathbf{Z}}_{i}/{\mathbf{Z}}_{i-1})(k)^{m}
.
By construction,
\ker(\Psi)=\cap_{j=1}^{m}\ker(\varphi_{u_{j}})=Z_{i}
, and so the map
\Psi
factors through a
\omega
-quotient continuous bijection map from
Z_{i+1}/Z_{i}
onto the Polish group
\Psi(Z_{i+1})
. We get by Corollary
2.2
, that the
\omega
-quotient topology on
Z_{i+1}/Z_{i}
is equal to the analytic topology, therefore, by applying Merson’s Lemma
2.3
to the subgroup
Z_{i}\leq Z_{i+1}
we get that the
\omega
-topology on
Z_{i+1}
is equal to the analytic topology, as needed.

By assumption, the
\omega
-subspace topology on
Z_{1}
is equal to the analytic topology, by applying the claim above inductively for
i=1,..,n
we get that the
\omega
topology in
Z_{n}=U
is equal to the analytic topology.
∎

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected solvable
k
-algebraic group, and let
{\mathbf{T}}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})

k
-Levi decomposition of

Denote by

\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)

Then there exist a finite dimensional

, and an algebraic

-rational representation

, with kernel

(a)

For any group topology
\omega

\omega
-subspace topology on
\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)
equals the analytic topology, then

\omega

(b)

For any closed subgroup

\omega^{\prime}
-subspace topology on
\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)

\omega^{\prime}
-continuous.

Proof.

Let
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be the upper central series of the nilpotent group
\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)
.
For any
i=1,...,m
, the subgroup
Z_{i}
is normal in
G

G
on the finite dimensional
k
-vector space
V_{i}=Z_{i}/Z_{i-1}

We define the representation space

by taking the direct sum of the central quotient spaces,

, and thus
attain a
k
-rational representation
\pi\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
.

By construction, for any

[\mathcal{R}_{u}(G),Z_{i}]\subset Z_{i-1}
hence
\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)\in\ker(\pi)
.
By definition

, so

as well, implying that
C\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)\subset\ker(\pi)
.
We will show that

Let

,
x=tu
for some

and

, since

we get that

.
We prove by induction on

For

Assume that

Fix

. Since

is contained in

.
By induction assumption, the semisimple element

, commutes with

.
Observe that

We get that

, and hence

, and

.
Thus

.

Since (a) is a special case of (b), obtained by choosing

, we are left to prove (b).
Let
C_{0}
be a closed subgroup of

, and set

.
Let
\omega^{\prime}
be a weaker group on

, such that the
\omega^{\prime}
-subspace topology on
\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)
equals the analytic topology.
The conjugation action of
G_{0}
on

is continuous with respect to the
\omega^{\prime}
-subspace topologies.
In turn, the induced action of
G_{0}
on
Z_{i}/Z_{i-1}
is continuous with respect to the
\omega^{\prime}
-induced topologies.
Since

the
\omega^{\prime}
-induced topology in
Z_{i}
is analytic and hence the
\omega^{\prime}
-induced topology on
Z_{i}/Z_{i-1}
is equal to the standard analytic norm topology on the vector space.
By the construction of the map
\pi
, the action of
G
on

is via the conjugation action of

on
Z_{i}
, which is
\omega^{\prime}
continuous. Therefore the action of
G/C_{0}
on
V
via the conjugation action of
G_{0}
on
Z_{i}
is
\omega^{\prime}
continuous.
∎

7.3.
Minimal algebraic groups

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected reductive
k
-algebraic group, and let
P\leq G
be a minimal parabolic in
G
. If
G
has a compact center, then
P
is a minimal topological group.

Moreover, for any central

-algebraic subgroup
{\mathbf{Z}}_{0}\leq Z({\mathbf{P}})
, the group
P/Z_{0}
is minimal.

Proof.

By the structure of parabolic subgroups
[
Bor12
, Proposition 20.6]
, the minimal parabolic subgroup
P\leq G
is equal to the semi-direct product
\mathrm{C}_{G}(T)\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)
, where
{\mathbf{T}}
is a maximal
k
-split torus, and
\mathrm{C}_{G}(T)
is the centralizer of
T
in
G
.

Denote by
{\mathbf{A}}
the solvable
k
-algebraic group
{\mathbf{T}}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
.
We claim that
Z(A)<Z(P)
.
Indeed, for
a\in Z(A)
, since
T\subset A
, clearly
a\in\mathrm{C}_{G}(T)
hence
a\in T
, so
[a,\mathrm{C}_{G}(T)]=\{e\}
and thus
a\in Z(P)
.
Following Lemma
3.8
,
Z(P)=Z(G)
, and
Z(G)
is compact, therefore
Z(A)
is compact.
Hence, by Proposition
7.9
the group
A
is minimal.

Moreover, let
Z_{0}\leq Z(P)
be a
k
-algebraic subgroup. As
Z(P)=Z(G)
, the group
Z_{0}
is central in
G
and is therefore a central algebraic torus.
The group
A^{\prime}=TZ_{0}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)
is equal to the
k
-points of the
k
-algebraic group
{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Z}}_{0}
.
Since
T\subset A^{\prime}
for any

,

hence
a\in TZ_{0}
and therefore commutes with

as well, making

. Hence
Z(A)
is compact, and by Proposition
7.9
the group
A^{\prime}/Z_{0}
is minimal.

Following
[
Mar91
, Chapter I, Proposition (2.3.6)]
, the anisotropic group
\mathrm{C}_{G}(T)/T
is compact. Therefore the solvable subgroup
A\leq P
is co-compact, and in particular,

is co-compact in

.
Hence
A
, (

), is a minimal co-compact subgroup of
P
, (
P/Z_{0}
) thus by Lemma
4.20
,
P
(

), is minimal.
∎

Proof of theorem

.

If

For the other direction,

[

Set

.
We will show that

.

Let
\omega
be a group topology on

, weaker than the analytic topology.
Let

, since

and hence compact.
Moreover, by Proposition

, the

-subspace topology on

is equal to the analytic topology. Hence by Corollary

, the

-subspace topology on

In order to show co-minimality of

is minimal. Observe that since
{\mathbf{A}}
is a

-split solvable group then

as

Let

be the

-algebraic quotient map.
We claim that
\pi(Z({\mathbf{L}}))=Z({\mathbf{L}}/{\mathbf{T}})
, clearly
\pi(Z({\mathbf{L}}))\subset Z({\mathbf{L}}/{\mathbf{T}})
.
For the other direction, let

be an element that is mapped into

\mathbf{c}_{s}\colon{\mathbf{L}}\to{\mathbf{L}},\ \ \mathbf{c}_{s}\colon l\mapsto[s,l]

8.
Sealed algebraic groups

We saw that for algebraic groups over local fields of characteristic zero, having a compact center is a sufficient condition for minimality.
Mayer showed in
[
May97
]
that
for connected locally compact groups this necessary condition is in fact sufficient - a connected locally compact group such that all of its quotients have compact centers is sealed.
The following is a generalization that applies to connected algebraic groups over an arbitrary local field of characteristic zero.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
k
be a local field of characteristic
0
and let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected
k
-group with a
k
-Levi decomposition
{\mathbf{L}}\ltimes\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
. The following are equivalent

(1)

The group
{\mathbf{G}}(k)
is sealed.

(2)

For any normal unipotent
k
-algebraic subgroup
{\mathbf{N}}\lhd{\mathbf{G}}
the center
Z(({\mathbf{G}}/{\mathbf{N}})(k))
is compact.

(3)

The center of the reductive group
{\mathbf{L}}
is anisotropic (equivalently, the center of
{\mathbf{L}}(k)
is compact) and the action of
{\mathbf{L}}
on
\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
is with no non-trivial fixed points,

(4)

The center of the reductive group
{\mathbf{L}}
is anisotropic (equivalently, the center of
{\mathbf{L}}(k)
is compact) and the action of
{\mathbf{L}}
on
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}}))
is with no invariant vectors.

Remark 8.2
.

One can define condition (2’) to be the condition that the center of any quotient group is compact, note that clearly (2’) implies (2).
For connected real algebraic groups, the equivalence of (2’) and (1) is shown in
[
May97
, Proposition 2.14]

and the equivalence of (4) and (1) is shown in
[
May97
, Theorem 2.5]
.
In both cases the methods of proof rely on the result of Omori, and they differ from the methods used here.

In this section we assume that
k
is a local field of characteristic zero.
As usual, algebraic groups over
k
will be identified with their
\bar{k}
points and will be denoted by boldface letters. Their
k
-points will be denoted by corresponding Roman letters.

We let
{\mathbf{G}}
a connected
k
-algebraic group and denote its unipotent radical by
\mathcal{R}_{u}({\mathbf{G}})
, the
k
-points of the unipotent radical will be denoted by
\mathcal{R}_{u}(G)
.
We recall that the group

is the subgroup of

generated by the unipotent element in

, see
[
Mar91
, Chapter I, (1.5.2)]
.

8.1.
Sealed reductive groups

In this subsection, we will consider the special case where

-group.

Since the sealed property is closed under taking products and quotients and has the three spaces property (see Lemma

-simple groups.
The following Lemma is a version of Proposition

Lemma 8.3

Let

-simple group. Then

Proof.

Let

, be a continuous homomorphism into a topological group

By

If the kernel of

Thus we may assume that

is a finite central subgroup. Let

By Proposition

.
∎

We saw in Corollary

Corollary 8.4

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected semisimple

-group, then
G
is sealed.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected semisimple

-group, then

Assume without loss of generality that

Let

Following

Corollary 8.6
.

Let

be a connected semisimple

Let

Let

9.
A note on positive characteristic

As a proof of concept, we will show how the tools developed in the previous sections can be used to show the minimality of semisimple groups over local fields of positive characteristics.

Similar to the proof of Proposition

, we show that minimal-parabolic subgroups are minimal as topological groups and conclude minimality by co-compactness of parabolic subgroups.
Throughout this section we let

Lemma 9.1

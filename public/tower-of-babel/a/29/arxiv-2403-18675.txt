Abstract
. We introduce and study the notion of C ℍ 1 C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}} -regular submanifold with boundary in sub-Riemannian Heisenberg groups. As an application, we prove a version of Stokes’ Theorem for C ℍ 1 C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}} -regular submanifolds with boundary that takes into account Rumin’s complex of differential forms in Heisenberg groups.

1.
Introduction

The classical Stokes’ Theorem states that, if
M
is a smooth
m
-dimensional manifold with boundary, then the equality

\int_{M}d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega

holds for every smooth
(m-1)
-form on
M
. The aim of the present paper is to provide a generalization of Stokes’ Theorem to sub-Riemannian Heisenberg groups, as we now explain.

Recall that sub-Riemannian Geometry deals with the study of manifolds endowed with a distinguished sub-bundle, called horizontal bundle, of the tangent bundle. A great deal of effort was devoted in the last decades to the study of
intrinsic
analytic and geometric properties of sub-Riemannian manifolds: this is even more true for
Heisenberg groups
which, are the simplest examples of (non-Riemannian) sub-Riemannian manifolds and show such rich features and raise so many interesting open questions that they naturally became subject of extensive investigations. Heisenberg groups are introduced later in Section
2.1
; here we only recall that the
n
-th Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a step 2 nilpotent Lie group diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
that is endowed with a left-invariant, bracket-generating horizontal sub-bundle
H\mathbb{H}^{n}
of dimension
2n
, with a one-parameter family
(\delta_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
of group isomorphisms (called dilations), and with a left-invariant and homogeneous distance
d
. The Hausdorff dimension of the metric space
(\mathbb{H}^{n},d)
is
Q\coloneqq 2n+2
.

In this paper we will prove that Stokes’ Theorem holds for submanifolds of Heisenberg groups: before stating it, we however need to discuss the two keywords,
submanifolds
and
forms
in Heisenberg groups, that are needed for the very statement of Stokes’ Theorem.

The first ingredient is provided by
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular
(or
\mathbb{H}
-regular
) submanifolds, i.e., submanifolds that are
C^{1}
regular from the viewpoint of intrinsic geometry.
These submanifolds are of a completely different nature depending on whether they have
low dimension

1\leq k\leq n
, in which case they are
k
-dimensional submanifolds of class
C^{1}
tangent to
H\mathbb{H}^{n}
, or
low codimension

1\leq k\leq n
, then they are (locally) defined as non-critical level sets of maps from
\mathbb{H}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
that are continuously differentiable along horizontal directions.
In particular,
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds of low dimension
k
are (contained in) Legendrian

The first contribution of the present paper is the study of

-regular submanifolds with boundary in Heisenberg groups, see also

, is also a
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold; however, this definition would not be appropriate. To explain why, we use the following example in Euclidean geometry (similar examples could be produced in Heisenberg groups): the surface
S=\{(x,y,x\sin\frac{1}{x}):x>0,y\in\mathbb{R}\}
is

\mathbb{R}^{3}
and its boundary
\partial S=\{(0,y,0):y\in\mathbb{R}\}
is also smooth; however,

is not of class

with boundary
. The classical definition of

requires that, for every

is the center of the ball; using a little bit of Whitney Extension Theorem, one realizes that this is equivalent to requiring that, for every

whose interior contains

This discussion motivates the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
1\leq m\leq 2n+1
; we say that
S\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
is an
m
-dimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold with boundary
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

S
is a non empty
m
-dimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold;

(2)

\partial S
is a non empty
(m-1)
-dimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold;

(3)

for every
p\in\partial S
there exist a neighbourhood
U
of
p
and an
m
-dimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold
S^{\prime}\subseteq U
such that
U\cap\overline{S}\subseteq S^{\prime}
and, for every
r>0
,
U(p,r)\cap(S^{\prime}\setminus\overline{S})\neq\emptyset
.

In Definition
3.1
we also introduce the (local) notion of submanifolds with boundary that are
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular in an open set. Definition
1.1
is stated to work for any dimension and codimension; however, it is quite clear that in the low-dimensional case
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
regular submanifolds with boundary are exactly Euclidean
C^{1}
submanifolds with boundary that are tangent to the horizontal distribution.

This implies that also the boundary, whose tangent space is contained in the tangent space to the submanifold, is tangent to the horizontal distribution.
In the case of low codimension
k=2n+1-m
one needs to distinguish between the non-critical case
1\leq k\leq n-1
and the critical case
k=n
: the first is somewhat easier and one can provide a useful equivalent definition in terms of level/superlevel sets, see Theorem

. On the contrary, the case of critical codimension

is much more delicate, as it corresponds to the case in which the submanifold is of low codimension but its boundary is low dimensional. This is why in the critical case it is much more difficult to prove our main result, which we now state.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
1\leq m\leq 2n+1
,
S\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is an
m
-dimensional orientable
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
regular submanifold with boundary and
S,\partial S
have locally finite measures in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then

(3)

\int_{S}d_{c}\omega=\int_{\partial S}\omega\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{H}}^{m-1},

where
\mathcal{D}^{m-1}_{\mathbb{H}}
denotes the space of Heisenberg differential
(m-1)
-forms with compact support.

As customary, the symbol
d_{c}
stands for the exterior Rumin differential:
d_{c}=d
if
m\neq n+1
, while in the critical case
m=n+1
one writes
d_{c}=D
.
The measures on
S,\partial S
referred to in Theorem
1.2
are the Hausdorff spherical measures on
S,\partial S
of the appropriate dimensions that are (respectively)
m,m-1
(in the low dimensional case
m\leq n
),
m+1,m
(in the low-codimensional and non-critical case
m\geq n+2
) or
n+2,n
(in the critical case
m=n+1
).
A local version of Theorem
1.2
is also proved in Theorem
5.1
, from which Theorem
1.2
follows. Observe that Stokes’ Theorem can be written in the language of currents as
\partial_{c}\llbracket{S}\rrbracket=\llbracket{\partial S}\rrbracket
, and Theorem
5.1
is indeed stated in these terms.

In the low-dimensional case, Theorems
1.2
and
5.1
follows directly from the classical Stokes’ Theorem. For
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds of low codimension
1\leq k\leq n
they can be proved via an approximation scheme, performed in Section
4
, in which the submanifold
S
and its boundary
\partial S
are
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-approximated by, respectively, smooth submanifolds
S_{h}
and their boundaries
\partial S_{h}
: the classical Stokes Theorem holds for each
S_{h}
and the equality
\int_{S_{h}}d_{c}\omega=\int_{\partial S_{h}}\omega
passes to the limit, as
h\to\infty
, to get (
3
). The approximation scheme is particularly delicate in the case of critical codimension
k=n
: the major difficulty one encounters is that, while it is fairly easy to produce smooth approximations
S_{h}
of
S
, Frobenius Theorem makes it quite challenging to “cut” into
S_{h}
an
n
-dimensional boundary
\partial S_{h}
that is tangent to the horizontal distribution, as the latter is totally non-integrable. Note that this difficulty does not arise if
k=n=1
in which case it is enough to find some approximation
S_{h}
and then “cut” it (thus creating the boundary
\partial S_{h}
) along some horizontal curve. This idea was exploited in
[
21
, Theorem 5.3]
, where Stokes’ Theorem in
\mathbb{H}^{1}
was proved for the critical dimension, although with a different formulation. However, following a technically quite elaborate argument, in Lemma
4.2
we were able to produce an approximating sequence
S_{h}
with the extra property that
\partial S_{h}=\partial S
for every
h
, which is even more than requested. We believe that the approximation results of Lemmata
4.1
and
4.2
might be of use for future applications.

Notation and preliminary results

2.1.
Heisenberg groups and Heisenberg algebras

Definition 2.1
.

For
n\geq 1
we denote by
\mathbb{H}^{n}
the
n
-th
Heisenberg group
, identified with
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
through exponential coordinates. We denote a point
\mathbb{H}^{n}\ni p=(x,y,t)
by
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. If
p=(x,y,t),q=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the group operation is defined as

p\cdot q\coloneqq(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle x^{\prime},y\rangle).

If
\mathbb{H}^{n}\ni p=(x,y,t)
, its inverse is
p^{-1}=(-x,-y,-t)
and
0=(0,0,0)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the identity of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, we denote by
\tau_{p}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
the left translation, i.e.,
\tau_{p}(q)\coloneqq p\cdot q
. For
\lambda>0
, we denote by
\delta_{\lambda}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
the dilations of the Heisenberg groups defined, for
p=(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, by
\delta_{\lambda}(x,y,t)\coloneqq(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda^{2}t)
. Observe that dilations form a one-parameter family of group isomorphisms.

We denote by
Q\coloneqq 2n+2
the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{2n+1}
is the Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
and it is
Q
-homogeneous with respect to dilations.

Definition 2.2
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
(or by
\mathfrak{h}
when the dimension
n
is clear) the
(2n+1)
-dimensional Lie algebra of left invariant vector fields in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The algebra
\mathfrak{h}
is generated by the vector fields
X_{1},...,X_{n},Y_{1},...,Y_{n},T
where (for
1\leq j\leq n
)

X_{j}\coloneqq\partial_{x_{j}}-\frac{y_{j}}{2}\partial_{t},\qquad Y_{j}\coloneqq\partial_{y_{j}}+\frac{x_{j}}{2}\partial_{t},\qquad T\coloneqq\partial_{t}.

We denote by
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
the horizontal subspace of
\mathfrak{h}
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{h}_{1}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}(X_{1},...,X_{n},Y_{1},...,Y_{n}),

and by
\mathfrak{h}_{2}
the linear span of
T
; the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
admits the 2-step stratification
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}
.

If
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
we also denote by
H_{p}\mathbb{H}^{n}\coloneqq\operatorname{span}(X_{1}(p),...,X_{n}(p),Y_{1}(p),...,Y_{n}(p))
and by
H\mathbb{H}^{n}\coloneqq\bigcup_{p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}H_{p}\mathbb{H}^{n}
the horizontal fiber bundle. We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the inner product on
\mathfrak{h}
that makes the basis
X_{1},..,X_{n},Y_{1},...,Y_{n},T
orthonormal and if
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{p}
the corresponding inner product on
H_{p}\mathbb{H}^{n}
that makes the basis
X_{1}(p),...,X_{n}(p),Y_{1}(p),...,Y_{n}(p)
orthonormal. We will use
|\cdot|
and
|\cdot|_{p}
to denote the corresponding norms.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a function
d:\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{H}^{n}\to[0,+\infty)
is a
left invariant and homogeneous distance
if

(i)

d(p,q)=d(r\cdot p,r\cdot q)
for all
p,q,r\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

(ii)

d(\delta_{\lambda}(p),\delta_{\lambda}(q))=\lambda d(p,q)
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\lambda>0
.

We define the associated norm
\|\cdot\|
to
d
as
\|p\|\coloneqq d(0,p)
for every
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Moreover, if for every
(x,y,t),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
|(x,y)|_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}=|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}
we have
\|(x,y,t)\|=\|(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t)\|
we say that
d
is a
rotationally invariant
distance.

For
r>0
and
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
we define the
open ball

U(p,r)
as

U(p,r)\coloneqq\{q\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(p,q)<r\}.

In the following we denote with
d
a fixed left invariant, homogeneous and rotationally invariant distance on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and with
U(\cdot,\cdot)
the associated open balls.

Example 2.5
.

There are many examples of left invariant, homogeneous and rotationally invariant distances on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the most notable being the following.

(i)

The
Carnot-Carathéodory

d_{cc}
defined for
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
as

d_{cc}(0,p):=\inf\left\{\|h\|_{L^{1}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{2n})}:\begin{array}[]{l}\text{the curve $\gamma_{h}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{H}^{n}$ defined by}\\

(ii)

The

(iii)

The

Proposition 2.6

(
[
14
, Proposition 1.3.15]
)
.

Let
d_{1}
and

be left invariant and homogeneous distances on

Definition 2.7
.

Let
m\geq 0
. Given any left invariant, homogeneous and rotationally invariant distance on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{m}
the spherical Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
defined for

\mathcal{S}^{m}(E)\coloneqq\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\inf\left\{\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(2r_{i})^{m}:\exists\;(p_{i})_{i}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n},\exists\;(r_{i})_{i}\text{ with }0<r_{i}<r\text{ and }E\subset\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}U(p_{i},r_{i})\right\}.

It is well known that

coincides with

\mathbb{H}^{n}

Definition 2.8

(Projection on the “horizontal” hyperplanes)
.

Let
q=(x_{1},...,x_{n},y_{1},...,y_{n},t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n},p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
be given. We set

\pi_{p}(q)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}X_{j}(p)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}Y_{j}(p).

For fixed
p

\pi_{p}
is a projection, while for fixed
q

is a smooth section of the horizontal bundle

(it is in fact a left-invariant vector field).

Proposition 2.9
.

For any
a,b,p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
the following relations hold:

(1)

\pi_{p}(a\cdot b)=\pi_{p}(a)+\pi_{p}(b)
;

(2)

d(a,b)\geq C|\pi_{p}(a)-\pi_{p}(b)|_{p}
.

where
C
is a positive constant only depending on the distance
d
.

We omit the boring proof.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an open set and

at

By Proposition

(4)

\displaystyle d(q,q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=d(f(b),f(b^{\prime}))=d((f(b))^{-1}\cdot f(b^{\prime}),0)

0\\

f_{j}
is

is biLipschitz continuous, i.e., there exist

From the latter and (

Lemma 2.18

Let
1\leq k\leq n
and

(i)

(ii)

(2)

The implication

and an open ball

where

will be chosen later. Then there exist a function

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}g
of maximal rank on

and

S\cap U(p,3r)=\{q\in U(p,3r):g(q)=0\}.

Using the Whitney Extension Theorem (see
[
19

14
, Theorem 2.3.8]

g\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{k})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus[\{g=0\}\cap U(p,3r)],\mathbb{R}^{k})

By
[
20
, Proposition 3.25]
, up to an isometry of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
we can assume that

. Possibly reducing

Fix

\chi_{r}(q)\coloneqq\chi_{1}(\delta_{1/r}(q))

. Then the functions

(7)

\begin{cases}0\leq\chi_{r}\leq 1\\
\chi_{r}\equiv 1\mbox{ on }U(p,r)\\
\chi_{r}\equiv 0\mbox{ on }\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus U(p,2r)\\

We now consider the differential

\text{ $\widehat{\nabla}_{\mathbb{H}}h\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}_{k\times k}$\qquad on $\mathbb{H}^{n}$};

\displaystyle=*\left(\lim_{h\to+\infty}\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\left(f_{1}-\frac{1}{h}\right)\wedge\cdots\wedge\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{k}}{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\left(f_{1}-\frac{1}{h}\right)\wedge\cdots\wedge\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{k}|}(\Phi_{h}(\xi))\right)

\displaystyle=*\left(\lim_{h\to+\infty}\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{k}}{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{k}|}(\Phi_{h}(\xi))\right)

\displaystyle=*(n^{\mathbb{H}}_{S}(\Phi(\xi)))

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbb{H}}_{S}(\Phi(\xi)).

(d)

Since

uniformly on

(e)

With these observations in mind we get

3.
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds with boundary

After providing the definition of
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifolds with boundary we will give equivalent characterizations first in the case of low and non-critical codimension
k<n
, then in the (easy) case of low dimension (i.e., codimension
k\geq n+1
), and eventually in the case of critical codimension
k=n
. Then we will introduce and discuss the natural way to induce an orientation on the boundary.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq k\leq 2n+1
and
O\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an open set. We say that
S\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a
k
-codimensional (or
(2n+1-k)
-dimensional)
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold with boundary in
O
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

O\cap S
is a non empty
k
-codimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold;

(2)

O\cap\partial S
is a non empty
k+1
-codimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold;

(3)

for all
p\in O\cap\partial S
there exist a neighbourhood
U
of
p
and a
k
-codimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold
S^{\prime}\subseteq U
such that
U\cap\overline{S}\subseteq S^{\prime}
and, for every
r>0
,
U(p,r)\cap(S^{\prime}\setminus\overline{S})\neq\emptyset
.

We will omit reference to the open set
O
in case the latter is clear from the context.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
O\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an open set and
S\subseteq O
such that
O\cap\partial S\neq\emptyset
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

S
is a
k
-codimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold with boundary in
O
;

(ii)

the statement
(1)
from Definition
3.1
holds and for all
p\in O\cap\partial S
there exist a neighbourhood
U
of
p
and functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{k+1}\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(U)
such that

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{1},\dots,\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{k+1}
are linearly independent in
U
,

\displaystyle U\cap S=\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\cdots=f_{k}(q)=0,f_{k+1}(q)>0\},

\displaystyle f_{k+1}(p)=0.

Remark 3.3
.

If
(ii)
of Theorem
3.2
holds, then

U\cap\overline{S}=\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\cdots=f_{k}(q)=0,f_{k+1}(q)\geq 0\}.

In fact, the left hand side in the previous equality is clearly contained in the right hand one; the opposite inclusion can be easily proved taking into account that the map
(f_{1},\dots,f_{k+1}):U\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is open (see for instance
[
22
, Lemma 2.10]
). In particular,

U\cap\partial S=\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\cdots=f_{k}(q)=0,f_{k+1}(q)=0\}

and

is a

-regular submanifold of codimension
k+1
.

Remark 3.4
.

By a standard mollification procedure (see for instance in
[
29
, Proposition 2.10]
), the functions

in

of Theorem

can be chosen to be of class

on the open set

.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let us prove
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: since properties (1) and (2) in Definition
3.1
hold, respectively, by assumption and by Remark
3.3
, we have only to prove property (3). Fix
p\in O\cap\partial S
. Let
U,f_{1},\dots,f_{k+1}
be as in
(ii)
. We define

S^{\prime}\coloneqq\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\cdots=f_{k}(q)=0\}.

It is clear that
S^{\prime}
is a
k
-codimensional
C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}
-regular submanifold and
U\cap\overline{S}\subseteq S^{\prime}
. We must prove that, for every
r>0
,

. The map
(f_{1},\dots,f_{k+1}):U\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is open, so for every

there exists a
\varepsilon>0

B(0,\varepsilon)=\{a\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}:|a|_{\mathbb{R}^{k+1}}<\varepsilon\}\subseteq(f_{1},\dots,f_{k+1})(U(p,r)),

since, by Remark

.
Therefore, there exists

.

We now prove
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Fix

and let

be as in
(3)
of Definition

; then, there exist a neighbourhood
U
of

and functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(U)
such that, setting

we have

•

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{1},\dots,\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{k}
are linearly independent on
U
,

•

U\cap\overline{S}\subseteq S^{\prime}
,

•

for every

,
U(p,r)\cap(S^{\prime}\setminus\overline{S})\neq\emptyset
.

By property
(2)
, possibly reducing
U
, there exist
g_{1},\dots,g_{k+1}\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{H}}(U)
such that

•

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}g_{1},\dots,\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}g_{k+1}
are linearly independent on

,

•

U\cap\partial S=\{q\in U:g_{1}(q)=\dots=g_{k+1}(q)=0\}
.

Let

be such that

are linearly independent. Setting

and, possibly, reducing

,
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{1},\dots,\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}f_{k+1}
are linearly independent on

(15)

\displaystyle U\cap\partial S

\displaystyle=\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\cdots=f_{k+1}(q)=0\}.

The inclusion

\displaystyle U\cap\partial S

\displaystyle\subseteq\{q\in U:f_{1}(q)=\cdots=f_{k+1}(q)=0\}

\displaystyle=S^{\prime}\cap\{q\in U:f_{k+1}(q)=0\}

holds because

and, if

For the opposite inclusion

The set

. Notice that

Possibly restricting

The inclusion

and the claim (

and

, there exist a real number

and a continuous map

such that

•

is an homeomorphism between

and

•

\zeta_{i}^{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U_{i}^{j}),i\in\mathbb{N},j\in\{1,2\},
such that

(29)

0\leq\zeta_{i}^{j}\leq 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\
j=1,2\end{subarray}}\zeta_{i}^{j}=1\text{ on $\overline{S}$.}

It is not restrictive to assume that the covering
(U_{i}^{1})_{i}\cup(U_{i}^{2})
of
O\cap\overline{S}
is locally finite, so that the sum in (
29
) is well defined.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{2n-k}_{\mathbb{H}}(O)
be fixed; for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
j=1,2
denote by
(S_{h})_{h}
the
C^{1}
submanifolds approximating
S
(in the sense of currents) in
U^{j}_{i}
. Using Lemma
2.35
we get

(30)

\begin{split}\llbracket{S}\rrbracket(d_{c}(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega))&=\lim_{h\to\infty}\llbracket{S_{h}}\rrbracket(d_{c}(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega))=\lim_{h\to\infty}\int_{S_{h}}d_{c}(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{=}}\lim_{h\to\infty}\int_{\partial S_{h}}\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega=\lim_{h\to\infty}\llbracket{\partial S_{h}}\rrbracket(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega)\ =\ \llbracket{\partial S}\rrbracket(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega),\end{split}

where the equality marked by
\ast
follows from the classical Stokes’ Theorem if
k\leq n-1
(when
d_{c}=d
is the classical exterior differentiation) while, if
k=n
, it follows from Stokes’ Theorem and Remark
2.29
upon observing that

\displaystyle\int_{S_{h}}D(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{h}}d(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega-\theta\wedge L^{-1}((d(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega))_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial S_{h}}\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega-\theta\wedge L^{-1}((d(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega))_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}})=\int_{\partial S}\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega,

the last equality following from the fact that
\partial S_{h}
equals
\partial S
and it is tangent to the horizontal distribution. Finally,

\displaystyle\partial_{c}\llbracket{S}\rrbracket(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\
j=1,2\end{subarray}}}\partial_{c}\llbracket{S}\rrbracket(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega)=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\
j=1,2\end{subarray}}}\llbracket{S}\rrbracket(d_{c}(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega))=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathbb{N}\\
j=1,2\end{subarray}}}\llbracket{\partial S}\rrbracket(\zeta^{j}_{i}\omega)=\llbracket{\partial S}\rrbracket(\omega)

and the proof is accomplished.
∎

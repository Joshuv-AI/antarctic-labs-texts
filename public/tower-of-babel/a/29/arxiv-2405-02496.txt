Galois Correspondence for Partial Groupoid Actions
Wesley G. Lautenschlaeger and Thaísa Tamusiunas

Abstract
. We prove a Galois correspondence theorem for groupoids acting orthogonally and partially on commutative rings. We also consider partial actions that are not orthogonal, presenting two correspondences in this case: one for strongly Galois partial groupoid actions and one for global groupoid actions (without restriction). Some examples are presented.



2020 AMS Subject Classification:
Primary. 13B05. Secondary. 20L05, 18B40.

Keywords:
groupoid, Galois theory, partial actions, Galois correspondence, orthogonal action

1.
Introduction

Chase, Harrison and Rosenberg developed, in 1965, a Galois Theory for finite groups acting on commutative rings, in where they exhibit a generalization of the Fundamental Theorem of the Galois theory
[
5
]
.
Right after, Villamayor and Zelinsky
[
18
]
generalized one of the constructions of
[
5
]
to extensions of commutative rings with finitely many idempotents. Although they did not explicitly defined groupoid actions, this was the intermediate object that they worked to prove their correspondence theorem. This was the first time that groupoids appeared in a Galois context.

A Galois correspondence theorem for groupoids acting on commutative rings was showed in
[
15
]
. Given
B\subseteq A
a commutative Galois extension with finite Galois groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, it was proved a one-to-one correspondence between the wide subgroupoids of
\mathcal{G}
and the separable,
\beta
-strong
B
-subalgebras of
A
, where
\beta
is a (global) action of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
. Other Galois theories concerning global action of groupoids were also developed in
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
.

The study of partial group actions is recent in mathematical terms and its first objective was to classify
C^{*}
-algebras
[
8
]
. In a groupoid context, many questions concerning partial actions were investigated. For instance, in
[
1
]
it was introduced the concept of partial action of ordered groupoids on rings; in
[
12
]
, it was showed that there is a biunivocal correspondence between partial groupoid actions and global inverse semigroupoid actions; in
[
2
]
, a globalization theorem was proved and a characterization theorem for Galois extensions was showed. We highlight the special case in
[
3
]
, where the authors related a class of partial groupoid actions to partial group actions, calling them as
group-type
partial groupoid actions. The relation groupoid-group presented in
[
3
]
was used in
[
4
]
to construct a Galois correspondence for partial actions of groupoids, but only restricted to group-type partial actions, and the mechanisms used there cannot be applied to any partial action. Therefore, a different approach needs to be thought of.

So, the first objective of this work is to extend the Galois correspondence presented in
[
4
]
to a partial groupoid action without requiring the group-type property. For this, we will relate a partial Galois extension to the Galois extension regarding its globalization. The partial-to-global relation will give a more general partial Galois correspondence, as we will present in Section 3.

The second objective is to improve the assumption of Galois correspondence theorems for groupoid actions in general. In fact, all the Galois correspondences found in the literature require the action to be orthogonal, that is,
A=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}
, where
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is a partial action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
and
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is the set of identities of
\mathcal{G}
. This hypothesis covers a broad class of actions, but is still restrictive because it does not cover them all. Every action can be orthogonalized, but the problem is that if we have a Galois extension
A|_{A^{\varepsilon}}
regarding an orthogonalization
\varepsilon
, it does not imply that the extension
A|_{A^{\alpha}}
is Galois regarding the original action
\alpha
. In Section 4 we present some examples, as well as we prove two Galois correspondences without the orthogonal hypothesis.

We give now a brief outline of the paper. In the second section we present basic definitions and results about partial groupoid actions and Galois theory. In the third section we prove our first main result, which states that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the wide subgroupoids
\mathcal{H}
of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
and determined separable,
\alpha
-strong
A^{\alpha}
-subalgebras
C
of the algebra
A
, where
\alpha
is a partial action of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
(Theorem
3.6
). In Section 4 we define orthogonalization of a given partial action and we study which properties of Galois theory remain valid under orthogonalization. We also define strongly
\alpha
-Galois extensions and we present in Theorem
4.11
a Galois correspondence in this case, which is our second main result. Some examples are presented. We end the paper with our third main result, which is a Galois correspondence for global actions without restrictions on the action (Theorem
4.16
).

By rings, we mean unital rings. We also use the notation
A\triangleleft B
to indicate that
A
is a two-sided ideal of the ring
B
.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall that a
groupoid

\mathcal{G}
is a small category in which every morphism is invertible. Given a morphism
g\in\mathcal{G}
, we denote its
domain
and
range
by
d(g)
and
r(g)
, respectively. Hence,
d(g)=g^{-1}g
and
r(g)=gg^{-1}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{G}_{0}
denotes the set of objects of
\mathcal{G}
. Instead of using
Mor(\mathcal{G})
to denote the set of morphisms of
\mathcal{G}
, we identify
\mathcal{G}
with
Mor(\mathcal{G})
. We denote

\mathcal{G}_{2}=\{(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}:\text{the product }gh\text{ is defined}\}.

For each
x\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, we identify
x
with the identity morphism
id_{x}
, thus considering
\mathcal{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.

Given
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, consider
\mathcal{G}(e)=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:r(g)=d(g)=e\}
. It is easy to verify that
\mathcal{G}(e)
is a group for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, called
the isotropy group associated to

e
. A groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
connected
if given any
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
there exists
g\in\mathcal{G}
with
d(g)=e_{1}
and
r(g)=e_{2}
.

Given
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
we set
\mathcal{G}(e_{1},e_{2}):=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:d(g)=e_{1}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r(g)=e_{2}\}
. It is well-known that any groupoid is a disjoint union of connected subgroupoids. Indeed, we define the following equivalence relation in
\mathcal{G}_{0}
: for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
,

\displaystyle e_{1}\sim e_{2}

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }\mathcal{G}(e_{1},e_{2})\neq\emptyset.

Every equivalence class
\bar{e}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}/\!\!\sim
determines a connected subgroupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\bar{e}}
of
\mathcal{G}
, whose set of identities is
\bar{e}
. The subgroupoid
\mathcal{G}_{\bar{e}}
is called the
connected component of
\mathcal{G}
associated to
\bar{e}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{G}=\dot{\cup}_{\bar{e}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}/\!\sim}\mathcal{G}_{\bar{e}}
.

Given a nonempty set
S
, we recall that the
coarse groupoid
associated to
S
is the groupoid
S^{2}=S\times S
, where the product
(s,t)(u,v)
is defined if and only if
s=v
and in this case
(s,t)(u,v)=(u,t)
. If
\mathcal{G}
is a connected groupoid, we have
\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{G}_{0}^{2}\times\mathcal{G}(e)
(c.f.
[
13
, Proposition 3.3.6]
), and this isomorphism does not depend on the choice of
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Notice that every two finite coarse groupoids with the same amount of identities are isomorphic. Hence we will just denote by
\mathcal{A}_{n}
the coarse groupoid with
n
identities. Thus, if
\mathcal{G}
is a finite connected groupoid with cardinality
|\mathcal{G}_{0}|=n
and
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, we have
\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{A}_{n}\times\mathcal{G}(e)
.

2.1.
Partial Groupoid Actions

We begin with the definition of partial groupoid action.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid and
A
be a ring. We say that
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is a
partial action
of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
if, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}

A_{g}\triangleleft A_{r(g)}\triangleleft A
,
\alpha_{g}:A_{g^{-1}}\to A_{g}
is an isomorphism of rings, and

(P1)

\alpha_{e}=\text{Id}_{A_{e}}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, and
A=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}
;

(P2)

\alpha_{h}^{-1}(A_{g^{-1}}\cap A_{h})\subset A_{(gh)^{-1}}
, for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
;

(P3)

\alpha_{g}\circ\alpha_{h}(x)=\alpha_{gh}(x)
, for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{2},x\in\alpha_{h}^{-1}(A_{g^{-1}}\cap A_{h})
.

The first thing to note is that condition (P1) is not the same as reference
[
2
]
, because we require that
A=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}
, and in
[
2
]
this condition was not requested. When
\mathcal{G}
is a group with identity element
1_{\mathcal{G}}
, this condition translates into
A=A_{1_{\mathcal{G}}}
. If we do not require for this, it may happen that a partial groupoid action is not a partial group action, even when the groupoid is a group.

Naturally, a partial action is said to be
global
if
A_{g}=A_{r(g)}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
2
, Lemma 1.1]

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a partial action of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on the ring
A
. Then it hold:

(i)

\alpha_{g}^{-1}=\alpha_{g^{-1}}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

(ii)

\alpha_{g}(A_{g^{-1}}\cap A_{h})=A_{g}\cap A_{gh}
, for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

We say that the partial action
\alpha
is
preunital
if each
A_{e}
is generated by a central idempotent of
A
, denoted by
1_{e}
, for
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. We say that a partial action
\alpha
is
unital
if each
A_{g}
is generated by a central idempotent
1_{g}
, for
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

We say that a partial action
\alpha
is
orthogonal
if

(PO)

A=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}

holds.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a partial action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. A global action
\beta=(B_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
B
is said to be a
globalization
of
\alpha
if, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, there is a monomorphism of rings
\varphi_{e}:A_{e}\to B_{e}
such that

(G1)

\varphi_{e}(A_{e})\triangleleft B_{e}
;

(G2)

\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{g})=\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{r(g)})\cap\beta_{g}(\varphi_{d(g)}(A_{d(g)}))
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
;

(G3)

\beta_{g}\circ\varphi_{d(g)}(a)=\varphi_{r(g)}\circ\alpha_{g}(a)
, for all
a\in A_{g^{-1}}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
;

(G4)

B_{g}=\sum_{r(h)=r(g)}\beta_{h}\left(\varphi_{d(h)}(A_{d(h)})\right)
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

We have a characterization for partial actions that have globalizations.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
2
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a preunital partial action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. Then
\alpha
has a globalization
\beta
if and only if
\alpha
is unital. Moreover, such
\beta
is unique up to isomorphism.

2.2.
Galois Theory

In this subsection we present some basic concepts regarding Galois theory. From now on, assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a groupoid, and
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is a unital partial action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. We define

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}=\{a\in A:\alpha_{g}(a1_{g^{-1}})=a1_{g},\text{ for all }g\in\mathcal{G}\},

the
subring of invariants of

A

under

\alpha
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that
A
is a
partial
\alpha
-Galois extension of

A^{\alpha}
if there is
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq A
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\alpha_{g}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}})=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}\delta_{e,g}1_{e},

for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. In this case, we say that the set
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a set of
partial
\alpha
-Galois coordinates of

A

over

A^{\alpha}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
. We define the
restriction of
\alpha
to

\mathcal{H}
as

\displaystyle\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}=(A_{h},\alpha_{h})_{h\in\mathcal{H}}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
C
be a
A^{\alpha}
-subalgebra of
A
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{C}=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:\alpha_{g}(a1_{g^{-1}})=a1_{g},\text{ for all }a\in C\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{C}
, for all
C
. However,
\mathcal{G}_{C}
is not always a subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
. An example of that can be found at
[
7
, Example 6.3]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
C
be a
A^{\alpha}
-subalgebra of a ring
A
. We say that
C
is:

(i)

A^{\alpha}
-
separable
if
C
is a projective (
C\otimes_{A^{\alpha}}C^{op}
)-module
[
10
, Ch. III
\lx@sectionsign
1]
;

(ii)

\alpha
-
strong
if for all
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
with
r(g)=r(h)
and
g^{-1}h\notin\mathcal{G}_{C}
, and for all nonzero idempotent
e\in A_{g}\cup A_{h}
there is an element
a\in C
such that
\alpha_{g}(a1_{g^{-1}})e\neq\alpha_{h}(a1_{h^{-1}})e

[
4
, Proposition 5.4]
.

Remark 2.9
.

Concerning
C
being
A^{\alpha}
-separable, we have the following equivalent definition (c.f.
[
10
, Théorème 1.4]
):
C
is
A^{\alpha}
-separable if there is an idempotent
e=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in C\otimes_{A^{\alpha}}C
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}=1_{C}
and

e(1_{C}\otimes x-x\otimes 1_{C})=0
, for all
x\in S
. We say that
e
is an
idempotent of separability
of the extension
C|_{A^{\alpha}}
.

3.
Correspondence Theorem

This section is intended to prove a Galois correspondence for partial groupoid actions. From now on, assume that the groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is finite.

Take
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Consider
\mathcal{G}(-,e)=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:r(g)=e\}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}(-,e)=\{e=g_{1,e},g_{2,e}\ldots,g_{n_{e},e}\}
, where
n_{e}
is the cardinality
|\mathcal{G}(-,e)|
of
\mathcal{G}(-,e)
. Consider the following assertion:

(1)

\displaystyle\text{For each }g\in\mathcal{G},g_{r(g),i}^{-1}gg_{d(g),i}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}\text{ implies that }g\in\mathcal{G}_{0},\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n_{r(g)}=n_{d(g)}.

In
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
, it was proved that if (
1
) holds, then
A
being a partial
\alpha
-Galois extension of
A^{\alpha}
implies that
B
is a
\beta
-Galois extension of
B^{\beta}
, where
\beta
is the globalization of
\alpha
, a global action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
B
. We shall prove that this hypothesis is superfluous. So we start this section by improving
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
\alpha
is orthogonal. Consider a global action
\beta
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
B
, and assume that
\beta
is a globalization of
\alpha
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

A
is a partial
\alpha
-Galois extension of
A^{\alpha}
.

(ii)

B
is a
\beta
-Galois extension of
B^{\beta}
.

Proof.

The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is connected. For the general case, it is enough to obtain a convenient enumeration for each connected component of
\mathcal{G}
.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is finite and connected,
\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{A}_{n}\times G
, where
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is the coarse groupoid with
n
identities and
G
is a finite group with
m
elements
[
13
, Proposition 3.3.6]
. Observe that
n_{e}=|\mathcal{G}(-,e)|=nm
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Now enumerate
G
as the following:
G=\{1_{G}=g_{0},\ldots,g_{m-1}\}
. Let
\mathbb{S}_{n}
be the group of permutation of
n
elements. Consider
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
given by
\sigma(i)=i-1
, for all
1<i\leq n
, and
\sigma(1)=n
. Hence, given
1\leq j\leq mn
, there are unique
0\leq q\leq m-1
and
0\leq r\leq n-1
such that
j=qn+r+1
. We define, then,
g_{e_{i},j}=(e_{\sigma^{r}(i)},e_{i},g_{q})
.

In this enumeration,
g_{e_{i},1}=(e_{\sigma^{0}(i)},e_{i},g_{0})=(e_{i},e_{i},1_{G})=e_{i}
. Given
\ell=(e_{i},e_{j},g_{k})\in\mathcal{G}
and given
1\leq u=qn+r\leq mn
, we have:

\displaystyle g_{r(\ell),u}^{-1}\ell g_{d(\ell),u}

\displaystyle=g_{e_{j},u}^{-1}\ell g_{e_{i},u}=(e_{\sigma^{r}(j)},e_{j},g_{q})^{-1}(e_{i},e_{j},g_{k})(e_{\sigma^{r}(i)},e_{i},g_{q})

\displaystyle=(e_{j},e_{\sigma^{r}(j)},g_{q}^{-1})(e_{i},e_{j},g_{k})(e_{\sigma^{r}(i)},e_{i},g_{q})=(e_{\sigma^{r}(i)},e_{\sigma^{r}(j)},g_{q}^{-1}g_{k}g_{q}).

Therefore

\displaystyle g_{r(\ell),u}^{-1}\ell g_{d(\ell),u}\in\mathcal{G}_{0}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\sigma^{r}(i)=\sigma^{r}(j)\text{ and }g_{q}^{-1}g_{k}g_{q}=1_{G}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow i=j\text{ and }g_{k}=g_{q}g_{q}^{-1}=1_{G}\Leftrightarrow\ell\in\mathcal{G}_{0},

which ends the proof.
∎

Throughout this section, assume that
\alpha
is orthogonal and that the ring
A
is commutative. We fix the notation
\beta=(B_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
to denote a global action of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
B
that is a globalization of
\alpha
. According to
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
, it follows that
B=\prod_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}B_{e}
, so that
\beta
is an orthogonal action. To shorten the notation, we will assume
A_{e}\subseteq B_{e}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, instead of
\varphi_{e}(A_{e})\subseteq B_{e}
. We will also denote by
1_{g}
the identity of
A_{g}
and by
1_{g}^{\prime}
the identity of
B_{g}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

Now we shall prove the first part of the Galois correspondence.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A
be a partial
\alpha
-Galois extension of
A^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{H}
a wide subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
. Then
\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}
is a partial action of
\mathcal{H}
on
A
and
A
is a partial
\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}
-Galois extension of
C=A^{\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}}
. Furthermore,
C
is
A^{\alpha}
-separable,
\alpha
-strong and
\mathcal{G}_{C}=\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}
is an orthogonal partial action of
\mathcal{H}
on
A
and that
A
is a partial
\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}
-Galois extension of
C
with the same partial Galois coordinates of
A
over
A^{\alpha}
.

Consider the globalization
\beta
of
\alpha
. Define
B_{g}^{\prime}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in\mathcal{H}\\
r(h)=r(g)\end{subarray}}\beta_{h}(A_{d(h)})
and
\beta_{g}^{\prime}=\beta_{g}|_{B_{g^{-1}}^{\prime}}
. Then
\beta^{\prime}=(B_{g}^{\prime},\beta_{g}^{\prime})_{g\in\mathcal{H}}
is a global action of
\mathcal{H}
on
B^{\prime}=\prod_{e\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}B_{e}^{\prime}
. In fact,

\displaystyle\beta_{h}^{\prime}(B_{h^{-1}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\beta_{h}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{H}\\
r(k)=d(h)\end{subarray}}\beta_{k}(A_{d(k)})\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{H}\\
r(k)=d(h)\end{subarray}}\beta_{h}(\beta_{k}(A_{d(k)}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{H}\\
r(k)=d(h)\end{subarray}}\beta_{hk}(A_{d(k)})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{H}\\
r(\ell)=r(h)\end{subarray}}\beta_{\ell}(A_{d(\ell)})=B_{h}^{\prime}.

Since
\beta_{h}
is an isomorphism for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, it follows that
\beta_{h}^{\prime}
is also an isomorphism. Clearly
\beta_{e}^{\prime}=\beta_{e}|_{B_{e}^{\prime}}=\text{Id}_{B_{e}}|_{B_{e}^{\prime}}=\text{Id}_{B_{e}^{\prime}}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and given
(h,k)\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
,
\beta_{h}^{\prime}\circ\beta_{k}^{\prime}=\beta_{h}|_{B_{d(h)}^{\prime}}\circ\beta_{k}|_{B_{d(k)}^{\prime}}=\beta_{h}|_{B_{r(k)}^{\prime}}\circ\beta_{k}|_{B_{d(k)}^{\prime}}=\beta_{h}\circ\beta_{k}|_{B_{d(k)}^{\prime}}=\beta_{hk}|_{B_{d(hk)}^{\prime}}=\beta_{hk}^{\prime}
.

Moreover,
\beta^{\prime}
is the globalization of
\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}
. Indeed,
A_{e}\triangleleft B_{e}^{\prime}
by definition, for all
e\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Furthermore,
A_{h}=A_{r(h)}\cap\beta_{h}(A_{d(h)})
, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. In particular, since
A_{d(h)}\subseteq B_{d(h)}^{\prime}
, it follows that
\beta_{h}(A_{d(h)})=\beta_{h}^{\prime}(A_{d(h)})
, from where
A_{h}=A_{r(h)}\cap\beta_{h}^{\prime}(A_{d(h)})
, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. By the same reason it follows that
\beta_{h}^{\prime}(a)=\alpha_{h}(a)
, for all
a\in A_{h^{-1}},h\in\mathcal{H}
. Finally, the condition (G4) is precisely the definition of
B_{h}
, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
.

The action
\beta
of
\mathcal{G}
on
B
induces a partial action
\gamma
of
\mathcal{G}
on
B^{\prime}
via restriction, and
\beta

\beta_{g}((x+y)1_{g^{-1}}^{\prime})e_{2}=(x+y)e_{2}=ye_{2}
, for all
x\in C^{\prime}
and
y\in B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}(1_{B}-1_{B^{\prime}})
. Considering

, we obtain
\beta_{g}(y1_{g^{-1}}^{\prime})e_{2}=ye_{2}
, for all
y\in B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}(1_{B}-1_{B^{\prime}})
. Since
B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}
is
\beta
-strong and separable over
B^{\beta}
, it follows that there are
u_{j},v_{j}\in B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}
,
1\leq j\leq m
, such that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}v_{j}=1_{B}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\beta_{g}(v_{j}1_{g^{-1}}^{\prime})=0
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{C}
. Thus
0=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}\beta_{g}(v_{j}(1_{B}-1_{B^{\prime}})1_{g^{-1}}^{\prime})e_{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}v_{j}\beta_{g}((1_{B}-1_{B^{\prime}})1_{g^{-1}}^{\prime})e_{2}=1_{B}(1_{g}^{\prime}-1_{g}^{\prime\prime})e_{2}=(1_{B}-1_{B^{\prime}})e_{2}=e_{2}
, from where it follows that
e=0
, that is, Claim 2 holds.

Now, by Claim 2 and
[
15
, Theorem 4.5]
,
\overline{C}=B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{\overline{C}}^{\prime}}}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{\overline{C}}^{\prime}=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:\beta_{h}(x1_{h^{-1}}^{\prime})=x1_{h}^{\prime},\text{ for all }x\in\overline{C}\}
. Besides that, by definition of
\overline{C}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{\overline{C}}=\mathcal{G}_{\overline{C}}^{\prime}
if and only if
g\in\mathcal{G}_{C^{\prime}}=\mathcal{G}_{C}
, from where
\mathcal{G}_{C}=\mathcal{G}_{\overline{C}}
. It follows that
\overline{C}=B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}
and
A^{\alpha|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}=(B^{\prime})^{\beta^{\prime}}1_{A}=B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}1_{B^{\prime}}1_{A}=B^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}_{C}}}1_{A}=\overline{C}1_{A}=C^{\prime}1_{A}=C
.
∎

Now we can state the main result of this section. This theorem generalizes the Galois Correspondence for orthogonal groupoid actions
[

, Theorem 4.6]
, for orthogonal group-type partial groupoid actions
[

and for partial group actions

(Galois Correspondence for Orthogonal Partial Groupoid Actions)
.

Let

-Galois extension of

It follows by Theorems

4.
General Actions

Until now, Galois theory for groupoid actions has required the orthogonality of the action and the reason is to assure the invariance of the trace map. This hypothesis, even though it covers a large class of actions, is somewhat restrictive. So the goal of this section is to develop a Galois theory for nonorthogonal partial actions of groupoids.

4.1.
Orthogonalization

Definition 4.1
.

Two partial actions
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
and
\varepsilon=(E_{g},\varepsilon_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
E
are
equivalent
(we denote by
\alpha\simeq\varepsilon
) if there is a collection of ring isomorphisms
\{\varphi_{e}:A_{e}\to E_{e}:e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}\}
such that

(i)

\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{g})=E_{g}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
,

(ii)

\varphi_{r(g)}\circ\alpha_{g}(a)=\varepsilon_{g}\circ\varphi_{d(g)}(a)
, for all
a\in A_{g^{-1}}
.

Let
\alpha=(A_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
be a preunital partial action (not necessarily orthogonal) of a finite groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
A
. We wish to construct a partial action
\varepsilon
of
\mathcal{G}
on a ring
E
which is orthogonal and equivalent to
\alpha
. Indeed, consider
E=\prod_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}
and let
\varphi_{e}:A_{e}\to E
be the natural inclusion given by
\varphi(a)=(a\delta_{e,f})_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}
. Define
E_{g}=\varphi_{r(g)}(A_{g})
and
\varepsilon_{g}=\varphi_{r(g)}\circ\alpha_{g}\circ\varphi_{d(g)}^{-1}|_{E_{g^{-1}}}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Then it is easy to see that
\varepsilon=(E_{g},\varepsilon_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
is an orthogonal partial action of
\mathcal{G}
on
E
and
\varepsilon\simeq\alpha
.

Definition 4.2
.

If an orthogonal partial action
\varepsilon
is equivalent to a partial action
\alpha
, we say that
\varepsilon
is the
orthogonalization
of
\alpha
.

Remark 4.3
.

Observe that all the orthogonalizations are equivalent, that is, orthogonalization is unique up to equivalence, and that is why we use the term “the” orthogonalization instead of “an” orthogonalization.

As well as in the previous section, given a unital partial action
\alpha
, we fix the notation
\beta=(B_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
to represent its globalization, which is an orthogonal global action on a ring
B=\prod_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}B_{e}
such that
A_{e}
is an ideal of
B_{e}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. We also fix the notation
\varepsilon=(E_{g},\varepsilon_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
to denote the orthogonalization of
\alpha
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\alpha
be a unital partial action and
\beta
its globalization. Then
\beta
is also the globalization of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

It only takes to observe that we can realize
E=\prod_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}A_{e}
as an ideal of
B
and that
\varepsilon
coincides with the standard restriction of
\beta
to
E
as defined in
[
2
, Section 1]
, that is,
E_{e}=B_{e}\cap E
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
,
E_{g}=E_{r(g)}\cap\beta_{g}(E_{d(g)})
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
, and
\varepsilon_{g}=\beta_{g}|_{E_{g^{-1}}}
.
∎

Partial Galois extensions also have some properties preserved by orthogonalizations.

Proposition 4.5
.

On the above notations, assume that
A|_{A^{\alpha}}
is a partial
\alpha
-Galois extension. Then
E|_{E^{\varepsilon}}
is a partial
\varepsilon
-Galois extension.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an
\alpha
-Galois partial coordinate system of
A
over
A^{\alpha}
, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\alpha_{g}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}})=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}1_{e}\delta_{e,g},

for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Write
x_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}\varphi_{e}(x_{i}1_{e})\in E
and
y_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}\varphi_{e}(y_{i}1_{e})\in E
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\prime}\varepsilon_{g}(y_{i}^{\prime}\varphi_{d(g)}(1_{g^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}\varphi_{e}(x_{i}1_{e})\varepsilon_{g}(\varphi_{f}(y_{i}1_{f})\varphi_{d(g)}(1_{g^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{r(g)}(x_{i}1_{r(g)})\varepsilon_{g}(\varphi_{d(g)}(y_{i}1_{d(g)})\varphi_{d(g)}(1_{g^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{r(g)}(x_{i}1_{r(g)})\varphi_{r(g)}\circ\alpha_{g}\circ\varphi_{d(g)}^{-1}(\varphi_{d(g)}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{r(g)}(x_{i}\alpha_{g}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}})1_{r(g)})=\varphi_{r(g)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\alpha_{g}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}})1_{r(g)}\right).

There are two cases to consider: (1)

; or (2)

. Suppose (1). Then
\varphi_{r(g)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\alpha_{g}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}})1_{r(g)}\right)=\varphi_{r(g)}(1_{r(g)})=1_{C_{r(g)}}
. Now suppose (2). Thus

\varphi_{r(g)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\alpha_{g}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}})1_{r(g)}\right)=\varphi_{r(g)}(0)=0.

Hence,
\{x_{i}^{\prime},y_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a
\varepsilon
-Galois partial coordinate system of
E
over
E^{\varepsilon}
.
∎

The converse of the above proposition is not true even for the global case, as we can see in the next example.

Example 4.6
.

Consider
\mathcal{G}=\{g,g^{-1},r(g),d(g)\}
and

is a commutative ring and the

Define

\displaystyle A_{g^{-1}}=A_{d(g)}=Re_{1}\oplus Re_{2},

\displaystyle\quad A_{g}=A_{r(g)}=Re_{2}\oplus Re_{3},

\displaystyle\beta_{g}(ae_{1}+be_{2})=be_{2}+ae_{3},

\displaystyle\quad\beta_{e}=\text{Id}_{A_{e}},\text{ for }e\in\mathcal{G}_{0},\,\,\text{ and }\beta_{g^{-1}}=\beta_{g}^{-1}.

Hence

is not a
\beta
-Galois extension of

\beta

In particular, for

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i1}e_{1}+x_{i2}e_{2}+x_{i3}e_{3})\beta_{g}(y_{i1}e_{1}+y_{i2}e_{2})=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i1}e_{1}+x_{i2}e_{2}+x_{i3}e_{3})(y_{i1}e_{3}+y_{i2}e_{2})=0

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i2}y_{i2}e_{2}+x_{i3}y_{i1}e_{3}=0

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i2}y_{i2}\right)e_{2}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i3}y_{i1}\right)e_{3}=0.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i1}e_{1}+x_{i2}e_{2}+x_{i3}e_{3})\beta_{r(g)}(y_{i2}e_{2}+y_{i3}e_{3})=e_{2}+e_{3}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i2}y_{i2}e_{2}+x_{i3}y_{i3}e_{3}=e_{2}+e_{3}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i2}y_{i2}\right)e_{2}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i3}y_{i3}\right)e_{3}=e_{2}+e_{3}.

. But then

, a contradiction.

Now consider the orthogonalization

4.2.
Strongly
\alpha
-Galois Extension

Keeping the notations of the previous subsection, define

\displaystyle\varphi:A

\displaystyle\to E

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto(\varphi_{e}(a1_{e}))_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}.

We have that
\varphi
is a monomorphism. Indeed,
\varphi(a)=0
if and only if
\varphi_{e}(a1_{e})=0
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. But
\varphi_{e}(a1_{e})=0
if and only if
a1_{e}=0
, since
\varphi_{e}:A_{e}\to E_{e}
is a monomorphism, and from (P1) it follows that
a=0
if and only if
a1_{e}=0
, for all

.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a wide subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
. The following assertion holds:
\varphi(A^{\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}})=E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}}}\cap\varphi(A)
.

Proof.

Take
a\in A^{\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}}}
. Given
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi(a)\varphi_{d(h)}(1_{h^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi_{d(h)}(a1_{d(h)})\varphi_{d(h)}(1_{h^{-1}}))=\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi_{d(h)}(a1_{d(h)}1_{h^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi_{d(h)}(a1_{h^{-1}}))=\varphi_{r(h)}\circ\alpha_{h}\circ\varphi_{d(h)}^{-1}(\varphi_{d(h)}(a1_{h^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\varphi_{r(h)}\circ\alpha_{h}(a1_{h^{-1}})=\varphi_{r(h)}(a1_{h})

\displaystyle=\varphi_{r(h)}(a)\varphi_{r(h)}(1_{h})=\varphi(a)\varphi_{r(h)}(1_{h}).

Since we chose
h
arbitrarily, we have that
\varphi(a)\in E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}}}
, proving the first inclusion.

For the reverse, consider
a\in A
such that
\varphi(a)\in E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}}}
. Then, given
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we have that
\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi(a)\varphi_{d(h)}(1_{h^{-1}}))=\varphi(a)\varphi_{r(h)}(1_{h})
. However

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi(a)\varphi_{d(h)}(1_{h^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi_{d(h)}(a1_{d(h)})\varphi_{d(h)}(1_{h^{-1}}))=\varepsilon_{h}(\varphi_{d(h)}(a1_{h^{-1}}))=\varphi_{r(h)}\circ\alpha_{h}(a1_{h^{-1}}),

and
\varphi(a)\varphi_{r(h)}(1_{h})=\varphi_{r(h)}(a1_{r(h)})\varphi_{r(h)}(1_{h})=\varphi_{r}(h)(a1_{h})
.

Therefore,
\varphi_{r(h)}(\alpha_{h}(a1_{h^{-1}}))=\varphi_{r(h)}(a1_{h})
. Since
\varphi_{r(h)}
is a monomorphism, it follows that
\alpha_{h}(a1_{h^{-1}})=a1_{h}
, for all

, that is,

.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

Until now, we can conclude the following:

(1)

There are properties in Galois theory that are valid for orthogonal actions, but not for nonorthogonal ones, so the theory cannot be reduced to the orthogonal case.

(2)

Theorem
4.11
gives a generalization of the Galois correspondences that already exist in the literature, but it is still restrictive, once it requires that the partial action is strongly Galois, and not all partial actions over Galois extensions belongs to this class, as we can see in the next example.

Example 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{g,f_{1}\}\cup\{h,f_{2}\}
be the groupoid given by the disjoint union of two groups isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, that is,
g^{2}=f_{1}=f_{1}^{2}
and
h^{2}=f_{2}=f_{2}^{2}
. Consider a commutative ring
R
and
A=Re_{1}\oplus Re_{2}
, with
e_{1},e_{2}
orthogonal idempotents whose sum is
1_{A}
. Define
A_{k}=A
, for all
k\in\mathcal{G}
,

\displaystyle\beta_{g}(ae_{1}+be_{2})=be_{1}+ae_{2}=\beta_{h}(ae_{1}+be_{2}),

and
\beta_{f_{1}}=\beta_{f_{2}}=\text{id}_{A}
. We have that
\beta
is a (nonorthogonal) global action of
\mathcal{G}
on
A
. Moreover,
A
is
\beta
-Galois over
A^{\beta}=R
with coordinate system
x_{i}=y_{i}=e_{i}
,
i=1,2
. However, we have that
A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}(f_{1})}}=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{G}(f_{2})}}=A^{\beta}
, from where it follows that
A
is not strongly
\beta
-Galois over
A^{\beta}
.

4.3.
Weakening the hypothesis

To finish the paper, we extend, in the global case, the Galois correspondence for Galois extensions that are not strongly Galois. The main argument is the following proposition.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
\beta=(A_{g},\beta_{g})
be a global action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a commutative ring
A
. If
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are wide groupoids of
\mathcal{G}
such that
A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}
, then the wide subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
generated by
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, named
\mathcal{H}
, is such that
A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}}}=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}
.

Proof.

It is true that
A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}}}\subseteq A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}
, since
\mathcal{H}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. For the reverse inclusion, consider
a\in A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}
. Take
h\in\mathcal{H}
. We have that
h=h_{1}\cdots h_{n}
, for some positive integer
n
and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\cup\mathcal{H}_{2}
with
d(h_{i})=r(h_{i+1})
, for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then

\displaystyle\beta_{h}(a1_{h^{-1}})

\displaystyle=\beta_{h_{1}\cdots h_{n}}(a1_{d(h_{n})})=\beta_{h_{1}\cdots h_{n-1}}(\beta_{h_{n}}(a1_{d(h_{n})}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{h_{1}\cdots h_{n-1}}(a1_{r(h_{n})})=\beta_{h_{1}\cdots h_{n-1}}(a1_{d(h_{n-1})})

\displaystyle=\beta_{h_{1}\cdots h_{n-2}}(\beta_{h_{n-1}}(a1_{d(h_{n-1})}))=\cdots

\displaystyle=\beta_{h_{1}}(a1_{d(h_{1})})=a1_{r(h_{1})}=a1_{h}.

∎

Keeping the notations of the previous subsection, we define an equivalence relation
\sim
in
\mathsf{W}
by:
\mathcal{H}_{1}\sim\mathcal{H}_{2}
if and only if
A^{\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}=A^{\alpha|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}
. If
\alpha
is global, then by Proposition
4.15
each
\sim
-class has a unique maximal element with respect to inclusion. Namely, given
\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{W}
, we write
\mathcal{H}^{max}
to represent the maximal element of the
\sim
-class of
\mathcal{H}
. Therefore we can define the set

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}^{max}=\{\mathcal{H}^{max}:\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{W}\}.

Now we state the correspondence that finish our paper. We keep the same notation
\mathsf{W}
and
\mathsf{W}^{max}
for a global action
\beta
on a ring
A
. When the extension
A|_{A^{\beta}}
is strongly Galois in the theorem below, we have exactly Theorem
4.11
for global actions, since in this case every
\sim
-class consists of only one wide subgroupoid. If
\beta
is orthogonal, we have the Galois correspondence for orthogonal groupoid actions on commutative rings
[
15
, Theorem 4.6]
, since
\beta
being orthogonal implies that the extension
A|_{A^{\beta}}
is strongly Galois and that
\beta
is equal to its own orthogonalization.

Theorem 4.16

(Galois Correspondence for Global Groupoid Actions)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite groupoid acting on a commutative ring
A
via a unital global action
\beta
. Consider the orthogonalization
\varepsilon
of
\beta
on a ring
E
. Assume that
A_{g}\neq 0
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, and that
A
is a
\beta
-Galois extension of
A^{\beta}
. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the sets
\mathsf{W}^{max}
and
\mathsf{A}
given by
\mathsf{W}^{max}\ni\mathcal{H}\mapsto A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}}}
with inverse
\mathsf{A}\ni C\mapsto(\mathcal{G}_{\varphi(C)})^{max}
.

Proof.

Firstly we apply
[
15
, Theorem 4.6]
on the orthogonalization
\varepsilon
of
\beta
to obtain a Galois correspondence between all the wide subgroupoids of
\mathcal{G}
and all the
E^{\varepsilon}
-separable,
\varepsilon
-strong subalgebras
C
of
E
, that is,
\mathcal{H}\leftrightarrow E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}}}
. Restricting this correspondence to
\mathsf{W}^{max}
, we have a one-to-one correspondence
\mathcal{H}^{max}\leftrightarrow E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}
.

Claim 1.
There is a one-to-one correspondence
E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}\leftrightarrow A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}=\varphi^{-1}(E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}\cap\varphi(A))
.

Indeed, define
\theta:\{E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}:\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{W}\}\to\{A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}^{\max}}}:\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{W}\}
by
\theta(E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}})=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}^{\max}}}
. We have that
\theta
is injective, since if
\theta(E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}})=\theta(E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{K}^{max}}})
, for
\mathcal{H}^{max},\mathcal{K}^{max}\in\mathsf{W}^{max}
, then
A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{K}^{max}}}
, from where it follows by Proposition
4.15
that
\mathcal{H}\sim\mathcal{K}
, that is,
\mathcal{H}^{max}=\mathcal{K}^{max}
. Hence
E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}=E^{\varepsilon|_{\mathcal{K}^{max}}}
. Furthermore, Proposition
4.8
implies that
\theta
is surjective, proving the claim.

Claim 2.

\mathsf{A}=\{A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}:\mathcal{H}^{max}\in\mathsf{W}^{max}\}
.

Indeed, for each
C\in\mathsf{A}
, there is a wide subgroupoid
\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{W}
such that
C=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}}}
. But
\mathcal{H}\sim\mathcal{H}^{max}
, that is,
A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}}}=A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}
, by Proposition
4.15
. So
\mathsf{A}\subseteq\{A^{\beta|_{\mathcal{H}^{max}}}:\mathcal{H}^{max}\in\mathsf{W}^{max}\}
. The other inclusion is immediate.

So, by Claim 1 and Claim 2, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the sets
\mathsf{W}^{max}
and
\mathsf{A}
.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

The same argument used in Proposition
4.15
cannot be applied in the partial case to obtain an analogous of Theorem
4.16
, since given
a\in A
,
h,k\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\cup\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that
(h,k)\in\mathcal{G}_{2}
, we do not have, in general, that
a1_{k^{-1}h^{-1}}\in A_{k^{-1}}\cap A_{k^{-1}h^{-1}}
. Therefore we cannot guarantee that
\alpha_{hk}(a1_{k^{-1}h^{-1}})=\alpha_{h}\circ\alpha_{k}(a1_{k^{-1}h^{-1}})
. The existence of a more general Galois correspondence theorem for partial groupoid actions will require other technique and is still an open question.

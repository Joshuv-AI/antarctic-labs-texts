Introduction

A hypergraph
G=(V,E)
consists of vertex set

and

-uniform hypergraph is just a (simple) graph.
The concept of a hypergraph may be viewed as a variant of the concept of a block design. A block design is a
k
-uniform hypergraph for some
k
, with objects as vertices and blocks as edges, so that any realization of a hypergraph associated with a block design is simultaneously a realization of that design
[
5
,
15
]

For

, if they are contained in some edge of

. For

, let

be the set of neighbors of

in

and

be the set of edges containing

in
G
. The degree of a vertex

in

, denoted by

or

which contain

A path in a hypergraph

is a vertex-edge alternating sequence
v_{0}e_{1}v_{1}\dots e_{p}v_{p}
such that
v_{0},\dots,v_{p}
are distinct vertices,
e_{1},\dots,e_{p}
are distinct edges, and

for

; such a path is also called a path from

to

A cycle in a hypergraph

is a vertex-edge alternating sequence

such that
v_{0},\dots,v_{p-1}
are distinct vertices,
e_{1},\dots,e_{p}
are distinct edges, and

interpreted as

). The number of edges in a path or a cycle is known as its length.
If there is a path from

to
v
for any
u,v\in V(G)
, then we say that
G
is connected. A hypergraph is said to be linear if any two edges have at most one vertex in common. A hypertree is a connected hypergraph with no cycles. Obviously, hypertrees are linear. A unicyclic hypergraph is a connected hypergraph with exactly one cycle and the length of the unique cycle is known as its girth.

A hyperstar is a hypertree in which all edges contain a vertex in common, and this vertex is called its center. If a hyperstar has exactly one edge, then we may choose any vertex as its center. The
k
-uniform hyperstar on
n
vertices is denoted by

. Let
S_{n}=S_{n,2}
, which is the ordinary star (graph) on
n
vertices.

For a
k
-uniform hypertree

with
m=\frac{n-1}{k-1}

, then we call

a
k
-uniform loose path, denoted by

. Let

, which is the ordinary path (graph) on
n

For a hypergraph
G

In the rest of this paper we assume that

In this section, we propose some local operations (grafting operations) that increase or decrease the
\alpha
-spectral radius of a hypergraph that is not necessarily uniform.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected hypergraph with
u,v\in V(G)
and
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}\in E(G)
such that
u\notin e_{i}
,
v\in e_{i}
, and
(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})\cup\{u\}\notin E(G)
for
i=1,\dots,r
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the hypergraph obtained from
G
by moving edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
from
v
to
u
. Let
x=x(G)
. If
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
, then
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})>\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

Proof.

For
w\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})
, let
s_{wv}
be the number of edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
containing both
w
and
v
. For
\{w,z\}\subseteq V(G)
, it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\quad a_{wz}(G^{\prime})-a_{wz}(G)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}s_{wv}&\mbox{if }w\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\}),z=u,\mbox{ or }w=u,z\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})\\
-s_{wv}&\mbox{if }w\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\}),z=v,\mbox{ or }w=v,z\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-\rho_{\alpha}(G)

\displaystyle\geq x^{\top}(A_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-A_{\alpha}(G))x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{w,z\}\subseteq V(G)}(a_{wz}(G^{\prime})-a_{wz}(G))\left(\alpha(x_{w}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}x_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})}s_{wv}\left(\alpha(x_{w}^{2}+x_{u}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}x_{u}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{w\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})}s_{wv}\left(\alpha(x_{w}^{2}+x_{v}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}x_{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})}s_{wv}\left(\alpha(x_{u}^{2}-x_{v}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}(x_{u}-x_{v})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})}s_{wv}(x_{u}-x_{v})(\alpha(x_{u}+x_{v})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}).

As
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-\rho_{\alpha}(G)\geq 0
, i.e.,

\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})\geq\rho_{\alpha}(G)
. Suppose that
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})=\rho_{\alpha}(G)
. Then
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})=x^{\top}A_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})x
, and thus
x(G^{\prime})=x
.
Let
N_{1,2}=N_{G}(u)\cap\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})
,
N_{1}=N_{G}(u)\setminus N_{1,2}
and
N_{2}=\cup_{i=1}^{r}(e_{i}\setminus\{v\})\setminus N_{1,2}
.
Note that
N_{G^{\prime}}(u)=N_{1}\cup N_{1,2}\cup N_{2}
and
a_{uw}(G^{\prime})=a_{uw}(G)
for
w\in N_{1}
.
If
N_{1,2}\neq\emptyset
, then
\sum_{w\in N_{1,2}}(a_{uw}(G^{\prime})-a_{uw}(G))(\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)x_{w})>0
. If
N_{1,2}=\emptyset
, then
\sum_{w\in N_{2}}a_{uw}(G^{\prime})(\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)x_{w})>0
.
From the eigenequations of
G
and
G^{\prime}
at
u
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})x_{u}-\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in N_{G^{\prime}}(u)}a_{uw}(G^{\prime})(\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)x_{w})-\sum_{w\in N_{G}(u)}a_{uw}(G)(\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)x_{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in N_{1,2}}(a_{uw}(G^{\prime})-a_{uw}(G))(\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)x_{w})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{w\in N_{2}}a_{uw}(G^{\prime})(\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)x_{w})

\displaystyle>0,

a contradiction.
It follows that
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})>\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.
∎

We note that Theorem
3.1
holds for both uniform hypergraphs and hypergraphs that are not necessarily uniform.
The cases
\alpha=0,\frac{1}{2}
have been given in
[

]
.

The following theorem generalizes a result in
[
10
]
for
\alpha=0
, see also

]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{u,v\}
be an edge of a connected graph
G
, and
G_{uv}
the graph obtained from
G
by deleting edge
\{u,v\}
and adding edges
\{u,w\}
and
\{w,v\}
, where
w
is a new vertex not in
G
. If
\{u,v\}
lies in some internal path of
G
, then
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})\leq\rho_{\alpha}(G)
with equality if and only if
\alpha=0
and
G\cong W_{n}
with
n\geq 6
, where
n=|V(G)|
.

Proof.

A label of the vertices of
G_{uv}
is obtained from a label of the vertices of
G
by adding the new vertex
w
.
We consider cases corresponding to the two types of the internal path.

Case 1.

uv
lies on an internal path
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{k+1}
of type
(i)
, where
v_{k+1}=v_{0}
and
d_{G}(v_{0})\geq 3
.

In this case,
C_{k+1}
is a proper induced subgraph of
G
, and thus by Lemma
2.2
,
\rho_{\alpha}(G)>\rho_{\alpha}(C_{k+1})=2
.

Relabel vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{k}
as
0,1,\dots,k
.
Let
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
be the corresponding entries of the
\alpha
-Perron vector
x
of
G
at vertices
0,1,\dots,k
. By Lemma
2.1
,
x_{j}=x_{k+1-j}
for
j=1,\dots,k
.

Case 1.1.

k
is even. We may take
u=\frac{k}{2}
and
v=\frac{k}{2}+1
. Then
x_{u}=x_{v}
. Let
y
be the vector with
y_{z}=x_{z}
for
z\in V(G)
and
y_{w}=x_{u}
.
By the choice of
y
,
A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y
and
\rho_{\alpha}(G)y
differ only in the entries corresponding to
w
, and

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{w}

\displaystyle=2\alpha y_{w}+(1-\alpha)(y_{u}+y_{v})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{u}+2(1-\alpha)x_{u}

\displaystyle=2x_{u}

\displaystyle<\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{u}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{w}.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

Case 1.2.

k
is odd. We may take
u=\frac{k-1}{2}
and
v=\frac{k+1}{2}
. Let
y
be the vector with
y_{z}=x_{z}
for
z\in V(G)
and
y_{w}=x_{v}
. Since
2\alpha x_{v}+2(1-\alpha)x_{u}=A_{\alpha}(G)x_{v}=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{v}>2x_{v}
, we have
x_{u}>x_{v}
. By the choice of
y
,
A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y
and
\rho_{\alpha}(G)y
differ only in the entries corresponding to
w
and
v
, and we have

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{w}

\displaystyle=2\alpha y_{w}+(1-\alpha)(y_{u}+y_{v})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{v}+(1-\alpha)(x_{u}+x_{v})

\displaystyle<2\alpha x_{v}+(1-\alpha)(x_{u}+x_{u})

\displaystyle=\left(A_{\alpha}(G)x\right)_{v}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{v}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{w},

and

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{v}

\displaystyle=2\alpha y_{v}+(1-\alpha)(y_{w}+y_{u})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{v}+(1-\alpha)(x_{v}+x_{u})

\displaystyle<2\alpha x_{v}+(1-\alpha)(x_{u}+x_{u})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{v}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{v}.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

Case 2.

uv
lies on an internal path
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{k+1}
of type
(ii)
, where
d_{G}(v_{0}),d_{G}(v_{k+1})\geq 3
.

Relabel
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k+1}
as
0,1,\dots,k+1
.
Let
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k+1}
be the corresponding entries of the
\alpha
-Perron vector
x
of
G
at
0,\dots,k+1
. Assume that
x_{0}\leq x_{k+1}
. Let
t
be the smallest index such that
x_{t}=\min\{x_{i}:0\leq i\leq k+1\}
. Then
t\leq k
. Assume that
u=t
and
v=t+1
.

Suppose first that
t>0
. Let
y
be a vector with
y_{z}=x_{z}
for
z\in V(G)
and
y_{w}=x_{u}=x_{t}
. Then
A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y
and
\rho_{\alpha}(G)y
differ only in the entries corresponding to
w
and
u
, and we have

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{w}

\displaystyle=2\alpha y_{w}+(1-\alpha)(y_{u}+y_{v})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)(x_{u}+x_{v})

\displaystyle<2\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)(x_{t-1}+x_{v})

\displaystyle=\left(A_{\alpha}(G)x\right)_{u}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{u}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{w},

and

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{u}

\displaystyle=2\alpha y_{u}+(1-\alpha)(y_{t-1}+y_{w})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)(x_{t-1}+x_{u})

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha x_{u}+(1-\alpha)(x_{t-1}+x_{v})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{u}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{u}.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

Suppose in the following that
t=0
. Let
S
be the set of neighbors of
0
other than
1
and
s=\sum_{j\in S}x_{j}
. Let
d_{0}=d_{G}(0)
.

Case 2.1.

(1+\alpha)x_{0}\leq\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)s
.

Note that
d_{G}(0),d_{G}(k+1)\geq 3
. From the result in
[
23
]
mentioned above and by Lemma
2.2
, we have
\rho_{0}(G)\geq 2
with equality if and only if
G\cong W_{n}
with
n\geq 6
.
As
\rho_{\alpha}(G)
is a strictly increasing function for
\alpha\in[0,1)
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G)\geq 2
with equality if and only if
\alpha=0
and
G\cong W_{n}
.

Let
y
be the vector with
y_{z}=x_{z}
for
z\in V(G)
and
y_{w}=x_{u}=x_{0}
. Then
A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y
and
\rho_{\alpha}(G)y
differ only in the entries corresponding to
w
and
u
, and we have

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{w}

\displaystyle=2\alpha y_{w}+(1-\alpha)(y_{u}+y_{v})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{0}+(1-\alpha)(x_{0}+x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)(s+x_{1})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{0}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{w},

and

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{u}

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{0}y_{u}+(1-\alpha)(s+y_{w})

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)(s+x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)(s+x_{1})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{0}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{u}.

If one of these inequalities is strict, then
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
by Lemma
2.3
.
If these inequalities are equalities, then
(1+\alpha)x_{0}=\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)s
and
x_{0}=x_{1}
, implying
\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{0}=\left(A_{\alpha}(G)x\right)_{0}=\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)(s+x_{1})=(1+\alpha)x_{0}+(1-\alpha)x_{1}=2x_{0}
, i.e.,
\rho_{\alpha}(G)=2
,
i.e.,
\alpha=0
and
G\cong W_{n}
.

Case 2.2.

(1+\alpha)x_{0}>\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)s
.

In this case,
(1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0})x_{0}>(1-\alpha)s>0
, and then
x_{0}>s
, implying
s+x_{0}<2x_{0}\leq(d_{0}-1)x_{0}
, i.e.,

(1+\alpha)s+(1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0})x_{0}<\alpha s+(1-\alpha)(d_{0}-1)x_{0}
.
For
j\in S
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{j}\geq\alpha x_{j}+(1-\alpha)x_{0}
, implying

\rho_{\alpha}(G)s\geq\alpha s+(1-\alpha)(d_{0}-1)x_{0}>(1+\alpha)s+(1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0})x_{0}.

Thus

\alpha d_{0}\frac{(1-\alpha)s}{1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0}}+(1-\alpha)(s+x_{0})\\
<\rho_{\alpha}(G)\frac{(1-\alpha)s}{1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0}}.

Let
y
be the vector with
y_{0}=\frac{(1-\alpha)s}{1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0}}
,
y_{z}=x_{z}
for
z\in V(G)\setminus\{0\}
, and
y_{w}=x_{u}=x_{0}
. Then
A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y
and
\rho_{\alpha}(G)y
differ only in the entries corresponding to
j\in S
,
w
and
u
, and for
j\in S
we have

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{j}

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{G}(j)y_{j}+(1-\alpha)\sum_{i\in N_{G}(j)}y_{i}

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{G}(j)x_{j}+(1-\alpha)\left(\sum_{i\in N_{G}(j)\setminus\{0\}}x_{i}+\frac{(1-\alpha)s}{1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle<\alpha d_{G}(j)x_{j}+(1-\alpha)\left(\sum_{i\in N_{G}(j)\setminus\{0\}}x_{i}+x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{j}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{j},

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{w}

\displaystyle=2\alpha y_{w}+(1-\alpha)(y_{0}+y_{1})

\displaystyle=2\alpha x_{0}+(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)s}{1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0}}+x_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha d_{0}x_{0}+(1-\alpha)(s+x_{1})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{0}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{w},

and

\displaystyle\left(A_{\alpha}(G_{uv})y\right)_{u}

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{0}y_{u}+(1-\alpha)(s+y_{w})

\displaystyle=\alpha d_{0}\frac{(1-\alpha)s}{1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0}}+(1-\alpha)(s+x_{0})

\displaystyle<\rho_{\alpha}(G)\frac{(1-\alpha)s}{1+\alpha-\alpha d_{0}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha}(G)y_{u}.

Now by Lemma
2.3
,
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

By combining the above cases, we have either
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
or
\alpha=0
and
G\cong W_{n}
with
n\geq 6
for which we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G_{uv})=\rho_{\alpha}(G)=2
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For
k-2\geq r\geq 2
, let
G
be a hypergraph with two pendant edges
e_{1}=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k-1},u\}
and
e_{2}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1},u\}
at
u
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the hypergraph obtained from
G
by moving vertex
w_{1}
from
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
. Then
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

Proof.

For
\{w,z\}\subseteq V(G)
,
it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\quad a_{wz}(G^{\prime})-a_{wz}(G)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }w=w_{1},z\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\},\mbox{ or }w\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\},z=w_{1},\\
-1&\mbox{if }w=w_{1},z\in\{w_{2},\dots,w_{k-1}\},\mbox{ or }w\in\{w_{2},\dots,w_{k-1}\},z=w_{1},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
x=x(G^{\prime})
. By Lemma
2.1
,
x_{w_{1}}=x_{v_{1}}=\dots=x_{v_{r-1}}
and
x_{w_{2}}=\dots=x_{w_{k-1}}
. Since
\{w_{2},\dots,w_{k-1},u\}\in E(G^{\prime})
,
A_{\alpha}(G^{\prime}[\{w_{2},\dots,w_{k-1},u\}])=A_{\alpha}(K_{k-1})
is a principal submatrix of
A_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})>\rho_{\alpha}(K_{k-1})=k-2\geq r
. From the eigenequation of
G^{\prime}
at
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, we have

\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})x_{w_{1}}=\alpha rx_{w_{1}}+(1-\alpha)((r-1)x_{w_{1}}+x_{u}),

\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})x_{w_{2}}=\alpha(k-2)x_{w_{2}}+(1-\alpha)((k-3)x_{w_{2}}+x_{u}),

and thus
(\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-r-\alpha+1)x_{w_{1}}=(1-\alpha)x_{u}=(\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-k-\alpha+3)x_{w_{2}}
, implying

x_{w_{1}}\leq x_{w_{2}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-\rho_{\alpha}(G)

\displaystyle\leq x^{\top}(A_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-A_{\alpha}(G))x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{w,z\}\subseteq V(G)}(a_{wz}(G^{\prime})-a_{wz}(G))(\alpha(x_{w}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}x_{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\}}(\alpha(x_{w_{1}}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w_{1}}x_{z})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{z\in\{w_{2},\dots,w_{k-1}\}}(\alpha(x_{w_{1}}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w_{1}}x_{z})

\displaystyle=\alpha(2r-k)x_{w_{1}}^{2}-\alpha(k-2)x_{w_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2(1-\alpha)x_{w_{1}}\left(\sum_{z\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\}}x_{z}-\sum_{z\in\{w_{2},\dots,w_{k-1}\}}x_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha(2r-k)x_{w_{1}}^{2}-\alpha(k-2)x_{w_{2}}^{2}+2(1-\alpha)x_{w_{1}}\left((r-1)x_{w_{1}}-(k-2)x_{w_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\alpha(2r-k)+2(1-\alpha)(r-1))x_{w_{1}}^{2}-\alpha(k-2)x_{w_{2}}^{2}-2(1-\alpha)(k-2)x_{w_{1}}x_{w_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha(2r-k)+2(1-\alpha)(r-1)-\alpha(k-2)-2(1-\alpha)(k-2))x_{w_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2(r-k+1)x_{w_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle<0,

implying
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G,G_{0},G_{1},G_{2}
be connected hypergraphs with
G_{0}
being a cycle of length two, where
E(G)=E(G_{0})\cup E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
,
E(G_{0})=\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
with
e_{1}\cap e_{2}=\{u,v\}
,
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{0})=\{u\}
,
V(G_{2})\cap V(G_{0})=\{v\}
, and
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})=\emptyset
.
Let
|e_{i}|=n_{i}
for
i=1,2
. If
n_{1}-2\geq n_{2}\geq 2
, let
u_{1}\in e_{1}\backslash\{u,v\}
and
G^{\prime}
be the hypergraph obtained from

by moving vertex

from
e_{1}
to

. Then
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

Let

,
e_{2}=\{u,v_{1},\dots,v_{n_{2}-2},v\}
. For
\{w,z\}\subseteq V(G)
,
it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\quad a_{wz}(G^{\prime})-a_{wz}(G)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }w=u_{1},z\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n_{2}-2}\},\mbox{ or }w\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n_{2}-2}\},z=u_{1},\\
-1&\mbox{if }w=u_{1},z\in\{u_{2},\dots,u_{n_{1}-2}\},\mbox{ or }w\in\{u_{2},\dots,u_{n_{1}-2}\},z=u_{1},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
x=x(G^{\prime})
. By Lemma
2.1
,
x_{u_{1}}=x_{v_{1}}=\dots=x_{v_{n_{2}-2}}
and
x_{u_{2}}=\dots=x_{u_{n_{1}-2}}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we also have
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})>\rho_{\alpha}(K_{n_{1}-1})=n_{1}-2\geq n_{2}
. From the eigenequation of

at

and

, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})x_{u_{1}}

\displaystyle=\alpha n_{2}x_{u_{1}}+(1-\alpha)\left(x_{u}+x_{v}+(n_{2}-2)x_{u_{1}}\right),

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})x_{u_{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha(n_{1}-2)x_{u_{2}}+(1-\alpha)\left(x_{u}+x_{v}+(n_{1}-4)x_{u_{2}}\right),

and thus
(\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-n_{2}+2-2\alpha)x_{u_{1}}=(1-\alpha)(x_{u}+x_{v})=(\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-n_{1}+4-2\alpha)x_{u_{2}}

Therefore

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-\rho_{\alpha}(G)

\displaystyle\leq x^{\top}(A_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})-A_{\alpha}(G))x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{w,z\}\subseteq V(G)}(a_{wz}(G^{\prime})-a_{wz}(G))(\alpha(x_{w}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}x_{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n_{2}-2}\}}(\alpha(x_{u_{1}}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{u_{1}}x_{z})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{z\in\{u_{2},\dots,u_{n_{1}-2}\}}(\alpha(x_{u_{1}}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{u_{1}}x_{z})

\displaystyle=\alpha(2n_{2}-n_{1}-1)x_{u_{1}}^{2}-\alpha(n_{1}-3)x_{u_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2(1-\alpha)x_{u_{1}}\left(\sum_{z\in\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n_{2}-2}\}}x_{z}-\sum_{z\in\{u_{2},\dots,u_{n_{1}-2}\}}x_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha(2n_{2}-n_{1}-1)x_{u_{1}}^{2}-\alpha(n_{1}-3)x_{u_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2(1-\alpha)x_{u_{1}}\left((n_{2}-2)x_{u_{1}}-(n_{1}-3)x_{u_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\alpha(2n_{2}-n_{1}-1)+2(1-\alpha)(n_{2}-2))x_{u_{1}}^{2}-\alpha(n_{1}-3)x_{u_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2(1-\alpha)(n_{1}-3)x_{u_{1}}x_{u_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\alpha(2n_{2}-n_{1}-1)+2(1-\alpha)(n_{2}-2)-\alpha(n_{1}-3)-2(1-\alpha)(n_{1}-3))x_{u_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2(n_{2}-n_{1}+1)x_{u_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle<0,

implying
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

For
k\geq 3
, let
e=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}
be an edge of a connected hypergraph
G
, and
e\setminus\{w_{2}\}
,
\{w_{1},w_{2}\}

\notin E(G)
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the hypergraph obtained from
G
by removing
w_{2}
from
e
and attaching an edge
\{w_{1},w_{2}\}
to
w_{1}
. Then
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})<\rho_{\alpha}(G)
.

Proof.

Note that
a_{wz}(G^{\prime})-a_{wz}(G)\leq 0
for

, and

. Then

, and thus

. By Lemma

,

.
∎

4
Extremal
\alpha
-spectral radius of hypertrees

In this section, we determine the unique hypertrees with maximum and/or minimum
\alpha
-spectral radius among some classes of hypertrees.

4.1
The
\alpha
-spectral radius of uniform hypertrees

Let
D_{n,k,c}
be the
k
-uniform hypertree obtained from
S_{k,k}
by attaching
c
pendant edges at one vertex and
\frac{n-1}{k-1}-1-c
pendant edges at another vertex, where
\frac{n-1}{k-1}\geq 3
and
1\leq c\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2(k-1)}\right\rfloor
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T
be a
k
-uniform hypertree of order
n
, where
2\leq k\leq n
.
Then
\rho_{\alpha}(T)\leq\frac{n\alpha+k-2+\sqrt{(n\alpha+k-2)^{2}-4(n-1)(k\alpha-1)}}{2}
with equality if and only if

Thus, among hypertrees on

-spectral radius;

(b)

-spectral radius.

5

-spectral radius of unicyclic hypergraphs

In this section, we study the

We consider

For integers

Theorem 5.1

Proof.

It is trivial if

be the unique cycle of

and

is an edge

outside

Theorem 5.2

Let

Suppose that

Now we move to consider unicyclic hypergraphs that are not necessarily uniform.

Theorem 5.3
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

be a unicyclic hypergraph on

6
Maximum
\alpha
-spectral radius of hypergraphs with fixed number of pendant edges

For
2\leq k\leq n
, the complete
k
-uniform hypergraph, denoted by

, is a hypergraph

of order

such that

consists of all

-subsets of

.

For

and

, let

be the set of connected

-uniform hypergraphs of order

with

, or

.

For

and

, let

is a hypergraph on
n
vertices with

, it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\quad a_{wz}(G^{*})-a_{wz}(G)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2^{n-p-2}-1&\mbox{if }w=v_{1},z\in\{v_{3},\dots,v_{r}\},\mbox{ or }w\in\{v_{3},\dots,v_{r}\},z=v_{1},\\
-1&\mbox{if }w=v_{2},z\in\{v_{3},\dots,v_{r}\},\mbox{ or }w\in\{v_{3},\dots,v_{r}\},z=v_{2},\end{cases}

and
a_{wz}(G^{*})-a_{wz}(G)\geq 0
otherwise. Let
x=x(G)
. By Lemma
2.1
,
x_{v_{2}}=x_{v_{3}}=\dots=x_{v_{r}}
and
x_{u}=x_{v}
for
u,v\in V_{0}\setminus\{v_{1}\}
. Note that
A_{\alpha}(G[e_{1}])=A_{\alpha}(K_{r})
is a principal submatrix of
A_{\alpha}(G)
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G)>\rho_{\alpha}(K_{r})=r-1
. From the eigenequation of
G
at
v_{2}
, we have

\rho_{\alpha}(G)x_{v_{2}}=\alpha(r-1)x_{v_{2}}+(1-\alpha)\left(x_{v_{1}}+(r-2)x_{v_{2}}\right),

which implies
(1-\alpha)x_{v_{1}}=(\rho_{\alpha}(G)-r+2-\alpha)x_{v_{2}}
, and thus
x_{v_{1}}>x_{v_{2}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\quad\rho_{\alpha}(G^{*})-\rho_{\alpha}(G)

\displaystyle\geq x^{\top}(A_{\alpha}(G^{*})-A_{\alpha}(G))x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{w,z\}\subseteq V(G)}(a_{wz}(G^{*})-a_{wz}(G))\left(\alpha(x_{w}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{w}x_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\{v_{3},\dots,v_{r}\}}(2^{n-p-2}-1)(\alpha(x_{v_{1}}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{v_{1}}x_{z})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{z\in\{v_{3},\dots,v_{r}\}}(\alpha(x_{v_{2}}^{2}+x_{z}^{2})+2(1-\alpha)x_{v_{2}}x_{z})

\displaystyle=\alpha(r-2)(2^{n-p-2}-1)x_{v_{1}}^{2}+\alpha(r-2)(2^{n-p-2}-1)x_{v_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2\alpha(r-2)x_{v_{2}}^{2}+2(1-\alpha)\sum_{z\in\{v_{3},\dots,v_{r}\}}x_{z}(x_{v_{1}}-x_{v_{2}})

\displaystyle=\alpha(r-2)(2^{n-p-2}-1)x_{v_{1}}^{2}+(\alpha(r-2)(2^{n-p-2}-3)-2(1-\alpha)(r-2))x_{v_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2(1-\alpha)(r-2)x_{v_{1}}x_{v_{2}}

\displaystyle>\left(\alpha(r-2)(2^{n-p-2}-1)+\alpha(r-2)(2^{n-p-2}-3)-2(1-\alpha)(r-2)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+2(1-\alpha)(r-2)\right)x_{v_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\alpha(2^{n-p-2}-2)(r-2)x_{v_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

implying
\rho_{\alpha}(G^{*})>\rho_{\alpha}(G)
, a contradiction. Therefore
|e_{1}|=\dots=|e_{p}|=2
, and
G\cong H^{p}_{n}
.
∎

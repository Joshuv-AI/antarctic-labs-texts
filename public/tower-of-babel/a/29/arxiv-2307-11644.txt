Explicit Constraints on the Geometric Rate of Convergence of Random Walk Metropolis-Hastings
Riddhiman Bhattacharya and Galin L. Jones

Abstract
Convergence rate analyses of random walk Metropolis-Hastings Markov chains on general state spaces have largely focused on establishing sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity or on analysis of mixing times. Geometric ergodicity is a key sufficient condition for the Markov chain Central Limit Theorem and allows rigorous approaches to assessing Monte Carlo error. The sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity of the random walk Metropolis-Hastings Markov chain are refined and extended, which allows the analysis of previously inaccessible settings such as Bayesian Poisson regression. The key technical innovation is the development of explicit drift and minorization conditions for random walk Metropolis-Hastings, which allows explicit upper and lower bounds on the geometric rate of convergence. Further, lower bounds on the geometric rate of convergence are also developed using spectral theory. The existing sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity, to date, have not provided explicit constraints on the rate of geometric rate of convergence because the method used only implies the existence of drift and minorization conditions. The theoretical results are applied to random walk Metropolis-Hastings algorithms for a class of exponential families and generalized linear models that address Bayesian Regression problems.

Introduction

The complicated distributions that arise in modern statistics often require Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods in order to use them for statistical inference
[
6
]
. Metropolis-Hastings (MH) algorithms
[
15
,
27
]
are fundamental to MCMC and are often used as building blocks for component-wise MCMC algorithms
[
18
,
24
]
when full-dimensional updates are infeasible. Under weak regularity conditions MH will eventually produce a representative sample from the desired target distribution, but a fast rate convergence is crucial to the efficacy and reliability of the algorithm in practice.

Assessing the reliability of a MH simulation is possible when a Markov chain CLT holds
[
12
,
13
,
21
,
31
,
37
]
. A key sufficient condition for a Markov chain CLT is geometric ergodicity
[
8
,
14
,
20
]
, which will also ensure that the Markov chain will converge quickly, at least in a qualitative sense
[
22
]
. As such, there has been significant work on MH Markov chains on general state spaces focused on establishing sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity
[
5
,
10
,
19
,
34
,
36
]
.

In particular, there are well-known sufficient conditions for geometric convergence of random walk MH (RWMH)
[
16
,
26
,
29
]
, which are now described informally but will be stated more carefully later. Suppose the target distribution
F
has a Lebesgue density
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, then the conditions for geometric ergodicity come in two parts
[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
. The first is that
f
is superexponentially light in the sense that the tails of
f
decay at an exponential rate. If
\|\cdot\|
is the usual Euclidean norm, the second is the so-called curvature condition

\displaystyle\limsup_{\|x\|\to\infty}\left<\frac{x}{\|x\|},\frac{\nabla f(x)}{\|\nabla f(x)\|}\right><0.

(1)

The curvature condition has been verified in some applications
[
9
,
38
]
, but it does not hold for some important settings such as Bayesian Poisson regression models
[
19
]
. This is addressed in Section
2
where a more general geometric structure of

is exploited to refine and generalize the sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity of RWMH. The new conditions are verified for an important class of exponential family Bayesian regression models, including some Bayesian Poisson regression models. The details are given in Theorems
1
and
2
.

A limitation of the sufficient conditions, including those developed here, is that they only imply the

property of geometric ergodicity and previously have not provided any constraints in the geometric rate of convergence
[
16
, p. 342]
. Some additional notation is required to describe this more precisely. If

denotes total variation distance and

the marginal distribution of the

if there exists a real-valued function

on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and

such that

d_{TV}(\mathcal{L}(X_{n}),F)\leq M(x)\rho^{n}.

(2)

The sufficient conditions only imply the existence of an appropriate

and
\rho
because the technique used in deriving them only implies the existence of appropriate drift and minorization conditions. This is addressed in Section
3
by deriving
explicit
drift and minorization conditions for RWMH, which appears to be a first for MH on general state spaces and requires some novel technical arguments. This will then allow an appeal to existing upper and lower bounds
[

,
32
, see, e.g.,]
for

While establishing drift and minorization conditions is a standard approach to establishing geometric ergodicity
[
28
]
another approach entails establishing a spectral gap for the Markov kernel operator of the Markov Chain
[
3
]
. Both approaches often provide similar bounds on the convergence of the Markov chain
[
30
]
. Lower bounds on

Due to the importance of RWMH, there is a substantial amount of related work

assumes a Gaussian proposal, strong log-concavity, and Lipschitz gradient. By contrast, our work does not require distributional assumptions on the proposal and the target log-likelihood need not be strongly concave. Others have focused on bounding the conductance, restricted to a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{p}

[

11

. There is also work on RWMH mixing time bounds which under the assumption that a drift condition exists

2
Geometric Ergodocity of RWMH

Suppose the distribution
F
with support
\mathbb{R}^{p}
has Lebesgue density

f
and for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the proposal distribution

Q(x,\cdot)
has Lebesgue density
q(x,\cdot)
such that

q(x,y)=q(y,x)=q(\|x-y\|)>0
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and

f(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. The Hastings ratio is

h(x,y)=\frac{f(y)}{f(x)}

and set
\alpha(x,y)=h(x,y)\wedge 1
. Let
\delta_{x}(\cdot)
denote the Dirac measure at
x
. The one-step Markov transition kernel for the RWMH is given by

\displaystyle P(x,dy)=\alpha(x,y)q(x,y)dy+\delta_{x}(dy)\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left(1-\alpha(x,y)\right)q(x,y)dy\right]

(3)

and for
n\geq 2

P^{n}(x,dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}P(x,du)P^{n-1}(u,dy).

The assumptions imply that the Markov kernel has
F
as its invariant distribution, is aperiodic,
F
-irreducible, and Harris recurrent. Hence if
\|\cdot\|_{TV}
denotes the
total variation norm, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, as
n\to\infty
,

\|P^{n}(x,\cdot)-F(\cdot)\|_{TV}\to 0.

The remainder of this section focuses on developing sufficient
conditions for the geometric ergodicity of RWMH chains. However,
these conditions will not provide any constraints on
\rho
, which is
addressed in Section
3
.

Assumption 1
.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denote the usual Euclidean inner
product. The target density
f(x)
is continuously differentiable and
satisfies the superexponential condition

\displaystyle\limsup_{\|x\|\to\infty}\left\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|},\nabla\log f(x)\right\rangle=-\infty.

(4)

Note that the region of certain acceptance for RWMH is given by

A(x)=\left\{y:f(y)\geq f(x)\right\}.

It is crucial that

A(x)
have a convenient geometric structure. Suppose

0<\alpha<\cos^{-1}(7/8)
and let

\tilde{\epsilon}_{\alpha}=(8-8\cos\alpha)^{1/2}
. Denote the unit
hollow sphere centered at the origin by
\mathbb{S}^{p-1}
. For

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\alpha}<\tilde{\epsilon}_{\alpha},

(5)

define the cone

W^{K}_{\epsilon_{\alpha}}(x)=\left\{x-a\xi:0<a<K,\ \xi\in\mathbb{S}^{p-1},\ \left\|\xi-\frac{x}{\|x\|}\right\|\leq\frac{\epsilon_{\alpha}}{2}\right\}.

(6)

Let
\overline{A(x)}
denote the closure of
A(x)
and let

\partial\overline{A(x)}
denote its boundary.

Assumption 2
.

There exists an
M_{p}>0
such that for any
\|x\|>M_{p}
, there is
y\in\partial\overline{A(x)}
, so that
W^{K}_{\epsilon_{\alpha}}(y)\subset A(x)
.

The following result shows that these assumptions are sufficient for
ensuring geometric ergodicity of the Random Walk Metropolis-Hastings
algorithm.

Theorem 1
.

If Assumptions
1
and
2
hold, then the
RWMH algorithm is geometrically ergodic.

Proof.

A proof is provided in Appendix
A.1
, but the
argument is essentially a refinement of previous work
[
16
, Theorem
4.3]
.
∎

1
extends prior results

16
]

2
can be challenging. There are sufficient conditions which may be easier to verify in some applications.

Proposition 1

1

.

(20)

Proof.

Example 1
.

Suppose, for
i=1,\ldots,n
,

Y_{i}\mid X_{i}=x_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle ind}}{{\sim}}Poi(e^{x^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\theta})

and if
g(\theta)
is strongly convex, the prior satisfies
\nu(\theta)\propto e^{-g(\theta)}
.
The posterior is characterized by

\displaystyle f(\theta\mid y,x)\propto\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_{i}x_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}\theta-e^{x_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}\theta}\right)-g(\theta)\right\}.

(21)

Denote by
c(\theta,x_{i})=\exp\left(x^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\theta\right)
. Notice that

\nabla^{2}c(\theta,x_{i})=e^{x_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}\theta}\,x_{i}x_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}\geq 0,

which implies
c(\theta,x_{i})
is convex. Also, for some for some
m>0

-\nabla^{2}\log f(\theta\mid y,x)=\nabla^{2}g(\theta)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{x^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\theta}x_{i}x^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}>m\,I

and hence

is strongly convex. Thus there exists a unique global minimum, say
\theta^{*}(y,x)
, which may be computed numerically.

The same argument as the in the proof of Theorem
2
will establish that the RWMH chain is geometrically ergodic and Proposition
3
will yield lower bounds on the geometric rate of convergence. Specifically, for a bounded proposal density
q(x,y)\leq B
and

1-\frac{B}{f(\theta^{*}(y,x)\mid y,x)}\leq\rho.

As a concrete example suppose the proposal is a Gaussian centered at the current state
x
with covariance matrix

. Then

and hence

1-\frac{1}{f(\theta^{*}(y,x)\mid y,x)(2\pi h)^{p/2}}\leq\rho.

This suggests choosing the scale

since otherwise it could easily be the case that

and, even though it is geometrically ergodic, the convergence would be prohibitively slow.

Convergence Lower Bounds Using Spectral Theory

Let

be the Hilbert space of all functions that are square integrable with respect to

\left\langle h,g\right\rangle_{2}=\int h(x)g(x)\,dF(x)

and associated norm denoted

is the set of all functions in
\mathbb{L}_{2}(F)
with
\int h\,dF=0
. Recall that
P
, defined in (
3
), is the Markov kernel for the RWMH, which is reversible with respect to
F
by construction. For any

,

Pf(x):=\int f(y)P(x,dy)\in\mathbb{L}_{2,0}(F)

and hence
P
may be regarded as a positive linear operator on
\mathbb{L}_{2,0}(F)

[
35
]
. The spectrum of
P
is the set

S(P)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:P-\lambda Id\ \text{is not invertible}\right\}

and
S_{0}(P)
is the spectrum under the restriction of the function class being
\mathbb{L}_{2,0}(F)
. The spectral gap of
P
is

\gamma_{P}=1-\sup\left|S_{0}(P)\right|

and define

.

If

and the Dirichlet form for

\mathcal{E}(P,h)=\left\langle h,h\right\rangle_{2}-\left\langle Ph,h\right\rangle_{2},

then define

Moreover, it can be shown that

.

There are well-known bounds for

in terms of the conductance of

,
\Phi^{*}_{P}=\Phi_{P}(1/2)

[
25

with

\displaystyle\Phi_{P}(v)=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{A}P(x,A^{c})F(dx)}{F(A)}:\,F(A)\leq v\right\},\quad v\in(0,1/2].

Specifically, a fundamental result
[
25
, Theorem 3.5]
yields

\frac{1}{2}\left[\Phi^{*}_{P}\right]^{2}\leq Gap_{R}(P)\leq\Phi^{*}_{P}.

The following assumptions are required for the results in this section.

6
Final Remarks

In addition to improving the sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity, the first explicit constraints on the geometric rate of convergence for RWMH on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
with a general proposal were developed. These constraints allow one to begin to study how various features might impact the geometric convergence rate of RWMH. However, the computable upper bounds were obtained by appealing to well known results
[
32
]
, which even though they are often conservative can produce practical, reasonable bounds
[
22
,
23
,
33
]
.
Moreover, the quality of the upper bounds depends on the quality of the drift and minorization conditions. The approach taken here used the same drift function as previous work. Alternative drift functions for specific target distributions or improved inequalities could result in sharper upper bounds.

The approach to establishing explicit drift and minorization conditions for RWMH appears to have potential for wider application to other MH algorithms. For example, recent work established sufficient conditions for geometric ergodicity of Metropolis Adjusted Langevin Algorithms
[
34
]
and the approach taken here would seem to apply in developing explicit constraints on its geometric rate of convergence.

Proof of Theorem

The following calculation will establish that the density in question is both superexponentially light and log-concave and hence the result will follow from Proposition

Note that

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\theta}{\left\|\theta\right\|},\nabla\log f(\theta\mid X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\theta}{\left\|\theta\right\|},\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-n\,\nabla c(\theta)-\nabla g(\theta)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\|\theta\right\|}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta^{\mathsf{T}}x_{i}-n\,\theta^{\mathsf{T}}\nabla c(\theta)-\theta^{\mathsf{T}}\nabla g(\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left\|\theta\right\|}\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta^{\mathsf{T}}x_{i}-n\,\theta^{\mathsf{T}}\nabla c(0)-n\,\theta^{\mathsf{T}}\nabla^{2}c(\xi_{1,\theta})\theta

where

are values in the line joining

\theta

Lemma 15
.

Under assumptions
12
and
13
,

\displaystyle Gap_{R}(P)\leq\frac{1}{2}\,L\,\frac{p\,e^{-V_{{\dagger}}(0)}}{\tilde{J}^{p/2+1}}

where
\tilde{J}=m_{1}
in the first case and
\tilde{J}=V_{{\dagger}}^{\prime\prime}(0)
in the second case.

Proof of Lemma
15
.

Note that by Cramer-Rao lower bound, for any vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

Var_{F}\left(\left\langle v,X\right\rangle\right)\geq v^{\mathsf{T}}\,\mathbb{E}_{F}\left[\nabla^{2}U(X)\right]^{-1}\,v\geq\left\|v\right\|^{2}\,L^{-1}.

Also, for the function
g_{v}(x)=\langle v,x-\mathbb{E}_{F}(X)\rangle
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(P,g_{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int F(dx)\,P(x,dy)\left(g_{v}(y)-g_{v}(x)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int F(dx)\,P(x,dy)\left\langle v,y-x\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int F(dx)\,q\left(\left\|y-x\right\|\right)\,\left\langle v,y-x\right\rangle^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int F(dx)\left\|v\right\|^{2}\left\|z\right\|^{2}Q(0,dz)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|v\right\|^{2}}{2}\int\left\|z\right\|^{2}q(\|z\|)dz.

Now, note that

\displaystyle\int\left\|z\right\|^{2}q(\|z\|)dz

\displaystyle=\int\left\|z\right\|^{2}\exp\left(-V_{{\dagger}}(\|z\|)\right)dz

\displaystyle=\int\|z\|^{2}\exp\left(-V_{{\dagger}}(0)-\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}V_{{\dagger}}^{\prime\prime}(s\|z\|)ds\right).

where the second line follows from the fact that
0
is the mode of
q(\cdot)
. Now, in the case there exists an
m_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\int\left\|z\right\|^{2}q(\|z\|)dz

\displaystyle\leq\int\|z\|^{2}\exp\left(-V_{{\dagger}}(0)-\frac{m_{1}\,\|z\|^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p\,e^{-V_{{\dagger}}(0)}}{m^{p/2+1}_{1}}.

In the case where
V_{{\dagger}}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\cdot)\geq 0
, we know that

\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}V_{{\dagger}}^{\prime\prime}(s\|z\|)ds\geq\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{2}V_{{\dagger}}^{\prime\prime}(0).

Hence we obtain the same bound as done previously with
V_{{\dagger}}^{\prime\prime}(0)
instead of
m_{1}
. Therefore

\displaystyle Gap_{R}(P)

\displaystyle=\inf_{h\in\mathbb{L}_{2,0}(F)}\frac{\mathcal{E}\left(Ph,h\right)}{\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathcal{E}\left(Pg_{v},g_{v}\right)}{\left\|g_{v}\right\|^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\,L\,\frac{p\,e^{-V_{{\dagger}}(0)}}{\tilde{J}^{p/2+1}}

where
\tilde{J}=m_{1}
in the first case and
\tilde{J}=V_{{\dagger}}^{\prime\prime}(0)
in the second case.
∎

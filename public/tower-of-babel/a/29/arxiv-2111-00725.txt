1.
Introduction

Let
\displaystyle\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{j}}
be the Laplace operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Its heat semigroup is defined by

\displaystyle e^{t\Delta}\varphi(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{t\Delta}(x-y)\varphi(y)dy,~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ \ t>0,

where
e^{t\Delta}(x)
denotes the Gauss-Weierstrass kernel

e^{t\Delta}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{n/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

And the fractional Laplacian can be defined as a pseudo-differential operator via the Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}((-\Delta)^{\alpha}f)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2\alpha}\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi),

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform. The corresponding fractional heat semigroup is defined as

\mathcal{F}\left(e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f\right)(\xi):=e^{-t|\xi|^{2\alpha}}\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi),\quad\alpha\in(0,1).

When
\alpha=1/2
, it is the Poisson semigroup.
In the literature, the fractional heat semigroup
\{e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}\}_{t>0}
has widely used in the study of partial differential equations, harmonic analysis, potential theory and modern probability theory. For example,
the semigroup
\{e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}\}_{t>0}
is usually applied to construct the linear part of solutions to fluid equations in the mathematic physics, e.g. the generalized Naiver-Stokes equation, the quasi-geostrophic equation, the
MHD equations. In fact,
e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}f(x)
is the solution of the heat equation related to the fractional Laplacian:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrl}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u(x,t)&=&0,\ \ \ \quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},\\
u(x,0)&=&f(x),\ \ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

And, in the field of probability theory, the researchers use
\{e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}\}_{t>0}
to describe some kind
of Markov processes with jumps. For further information and the related applications of fractional heat
semigroups
\{e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}\}_{t>0}
, we refer the reader to
[
4
,
9
]
. In
[
12
]
, by an invariant derivative technique and the Fourier analysis method, the authors concluded that the kernel,
e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}(x)
satisfy the following pointwise estimate

0<{e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}(x)}\leq\frac{t}{(t^{1/2\alpha}+|x|)^{n+2\alpha}},\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}.

In this article, we will introduce the heat semigroup
\{e^{-{t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}\}_{t>0}
into the analysis of martingale transforms in probability. Martingale transforms was considered firstly by D. L. Burkholder in 1966; see
[
3
]
. In
[
3
]
, the author proved the almost everywhere convergence of the martingale transforms. In martingale theory, we always treat the martingale transforms as a corresponding tool of the singular integral operators in harmonic analysis. In fact, we want to analyze the behavior of the following type sum

(1.1)

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{j}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x))

where
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a bounded sequence of real numbers and
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is an increasing sequence of positive numbers. Observe that in the case
v_{j}\equiv 1
, the above series is telescopy, and their behavior coincide with
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)
. This way of analyzing convergence of sequences was considered by Jones and Rosemblatt for ergodic averages(see
[
10
]
), and latter by Bernardis et al. for differential transforms(see
[
2
]
). The authors considered the differential transforms related to the one-sided fractional Poisson type operator sequence and the heat semigroup generated by Laplacian(see
[
5
,
6
]
).

To better understand the behavior of the sum in (
1.1
), we shall analyze its “partial sums” defined as follows.
For each
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},~N=(N_{1},N_{2})
with
N_{1}<N_{2},
we define the partial sum operators

(1.2)

T_{N}f(x)=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We shall also consider the maximal operator

(1.3)

T^{*}f(x)=\sup_{N}\left|T_{N}f(x)\right|,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where the supremum are taken over all
N=(N_{1},N_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
N_{1}<N_{2}
.
In
[
5
]
, the authors proved the boundedness of the above operators related with the one-sided fractional Poisson type operator sequence. And the same results was gotten for the above operators related with the heat semigroup generated by Laplacian in
[
6
]
.

Some of our results will be valid only when the sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is lacunary. That means that there exists a
\lambda>1
such that
\displaystyle\frac{a_{j+1}}{a_{j}}\geq\lambda,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, we shall prove the boundedness of the operator
T^{*}
in the weighted spaces

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega),
where
\omega
is the usual Muckenhoupt weight on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We refer the reader to the book by J. Duoandikoetxea
[
7
, Chapter 7]
for definitions and properties of the
A_{p}
classes.
We shall also analyze the boundedness behavior of the operators in
L^{\infty}
and
BMO
spaces. The space
BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is defined as the set of functions
f
such that
f^{\sharp}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

f^{\sharp}(x)=\sup_{x\in B}\Big\{\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\Big|f(z)-\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f\Big|dz\Big\}.

We define
\|f\|_{BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|f^{\sharp}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. In fact, we have the following results:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
\lambda
-lacunary sequence with
\lambda>1
. Let
T^{*}
be the operator defined in (
1.3
). Then

(a)

for any
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{p}
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,p,\omega,\lambda
,
\left\|v\right\|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

\left\|T^{*}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)},

for all functions
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\omega).

(b)

for any
\omega\in A_{1}
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,\omega,\lambda
,
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

\omega\left({\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\left|T^{*}f(x)\right|>\sigma\}}\right)\leq C\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)},\quad\sigma>0,

for all functions
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\omega).

(c)

given
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
then either
T^{*}f(x)=\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or
T^{*}f(x)<\infty
for
a.e.

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. And in this latter case, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,\lambda
,
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

\left\|T^{*}f\right\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(d)

given
f\in BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
then either
T^{*}f(x)=\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or
T^{*}f(x)<\infty
for
a.e.

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. And in this latter case, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,\lambda
,
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

(1.4)

\left\|T^{*}f\right\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The constants
C
appeared above all are independent of
N.

Remark 1.2
.

From the conclusions in Theorem
1.1
, for
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\omega)
with
\omega\in A_{p}
, we can define
Tf
by the limit of
T_{N}f
in
L^{p}
norm

Tf(x)=\lim_{(N_{1},N_{2})\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty)}T_{N}f(x),\quad\quad~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

For more results related with the convergence of
T_{N}f
, see Theorem
3.4
.

In classical harmonic analysis, if
f=\chi_{(0,1)}
and
\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transform, it is known that
\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\int_{-r}^{0}\mathcal{H}(f)(x)dx\ \sim\log\frac{e}{r}
as
r\to 0^{+}
. In general, this is the growth of a singular integral applied to a bounded function at the origin. The following theorem shows that if
f
is a bounded function, the growth of
T^{*}f
at the origin is of the same order of a singular integral operator.
Some related results about the local behavior of variation operators can be found in
[
1
]
. One dimensional results about the variation of convolution operators can be found in
[
11
]
. And one dimensional results about differential transforms of one-sided fractional Poisson type operator sequence is proved in
[
5
]
.

The following theorem analyzes the local growth behavior of
T^{*}
in
L^{\infty}
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for some
1\leq p\leq\infty.

Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be any increasing sequence and
T^{*}
defined in (
1.3
). Then for every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with support in the unit ball
B=B(0,1)
, for any ball
B_{r}\subset B
with
2r<1
, we have

\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left|T^{*}f(x)\right|dx\leq C\left(\log\frac{2}{r}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In the statement above,
\displaystyle p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1},
and if
p=1
,
\displaystyle p^{\prime}=\infty.

2.
Uniform
L_{p}
boundedness of the operators
T_{N}

In this section, we will make some preparations to prove Theorem
1.1
. In fact, we will prove the uniform boundedness of the operators
T_{N}.
The standard Calderón-Zygmund theory will be a fundamental tool in proving the
L^{p}
boundedness of the operators
T_{N}
. For this theory, the reader can see some classical textbooks about harmonic analysis, for example, see
[
7
,
8
]
. Nowadays it is well known that the fundamental ingredients in the theory are the
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness for some
1<p_{0}\leq\infty
and the smoothness of the kernel of the operator. Even more, the constants that appear in the results only depend on the boundedness constant in
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the constants related with the size and smoothness of the kernel.

In the following proposition, we present and prove the
L^{2}
boundedness of the operators
T_{N}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There is a constant
C>0
depending on
n
and
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
(not on
N
) such that

\|T_{N}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Using the Plancherel theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{N}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)\right)\Big\}^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\mathcal{F}{\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(\cdot)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(\cdot)\right)\Big\}}(\xi)\right)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}\left(e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f\right)(\xi)dt\Big\}^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\Big|\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}\left(e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f\right)(\xi)dt\Big|\Big\}^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=C_{v}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\left|\xi\right|^{2\alpha}e^{-t\left|\xi\right|^{2\alpha}}\big|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\big|dt\Big\}^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\left|\xi\right|^{2\alpha}e^{-t\left|\xi\right|^{2\alpha}}dt|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)|\Big\}^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\xi\right|^{2\alpha}e^{-t\left|\xi\right|^{2\alpha}}dt\Big||\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)|\Big\}^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,n}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}.

Then the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
n
and
\alpha
such that

(i)

0<{e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x)}\leq C\frac{t}{(t^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|x|)^{n+2\alpha}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},~t>0,

(ii)

\left|\partial_{t}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x)\right|\leq C\frac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|x|)^{n+2\alpha}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},~t>0,

(iii)

\left|\nabla_{x}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x)\right|\leq C\frac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|x|)^{n+1}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},~t>0,

and

(iv)

{\left|\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x)\right|\leq C\frac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|x|)^{n+2\alpha+1}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},~t>0.}

Proof.

For (i), it was proved in
[
9
, Lemma 5.4]
. For the other estimations, we can get the proof easily by using the results in
[
12
, Lemmas 2.1–2.2, Remark 2.1]
.
∎

The following proposition contains the size description of the kernel and the smoothness estimates that are required in the Calderón-Zygmund theory.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then

T_{N}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{N}(y)f(x-y)dy

with

K_{N}(y)=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right).

Moreover, there exists constant
C>0
depending on
n,\alpha
and
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
(not on
N
) such that, for any
y\neq 0,

i)

\displaystyle|K_{N}(y)|\leq\frac{C}{|y|^{n}}
,

ii)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}K_{N}(y)|\leq\frac{C}{|y|^{n+1}}
.

Proof.

i)
Regarding the size condition for the kernel, by Lemma
2.2
we have

\displaystyle|K_{N}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\left|v_{j}\right|\left|e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,v}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\partial_{t}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,v,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|y|)^{n+2\alpha}}dt=C_{n,v,\alpha}{1\over{|y|^{n}}}.

ii)

With a similar argument as above in
i)
, by Lemma
2.2
we get

\displaystyle|\nabla_{y}K_{N}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\left|v_{j}\right|\left|\nabla_{y}e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-\nabla_{y}e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,v}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\left|\partial_{t}\nabla_{y}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,v,\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|y|)^{n+2\alpha+1}}dt=C_{n,v,\alpha}{1\over{|y|^{n+1}}}.

The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Then, we have the following theorem about the uniform boundedness of
T_{N}
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a positive increasing sequence and
\{T_{N}\}_{N=(N_{1},N_{2})}
be the operator
T_{N}
defined in (
1.2
). We have the following statements:

(a)

for any
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in A_{p}
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,p,\omega
,
\left\|v\right\|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

\left\|T_{N}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)},

for all functions
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\omega).

(b)

for any
\omega\in A_{1}
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,\omega
,
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

\omega\left({\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\left|T_{N}f(x)\right|>\sigma\}}\right)\leq C\frac{1}{\sigma}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)},\quad\sigma>0,

for all functions
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\omega).

(c)

there exists a constant
C
depending on
n
,
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

\left\|T_{N}f\right\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for all functions
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

(d)

there exists a constant
C
depending on
n
,
\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\alpha
such that

\left\|T_{N}f\right\|_{BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

The constants
C
appeared above all are independent of
N.

Proof.

Previously, we have remarked that the constants in the
L^{p}
boundedness only depend on the initial constant in
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(in our case
p_{0}=2
), the size constant and smoothness constant of the kernel. Hence the uniform boundedness of the operators
T_{N}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
spaces is a direct consequence of the Calderón-Zygmund theory. The finiteness of
T_{N}
for functions in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is obvious, since for each
N
,
K_{N}
is an integrable function.
On the other hand, if
f\in BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we can proceed as follows. Let
B=B(x_{0},r_{0})
and
B^{*}=B(x_{0},2r_{0})
with some
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r_{0}>0
. We decompose
f
to be

f=(f-f_{B})\chi_{B^{*}}+(f-f_{B})\chi_{(B^{*})^{c}}+f_{B}=:f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}.

The function
f_{1}
is integrable, hence
T_{N}f_{1}(x)<\infty,

a.e.\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.
For
T_{N}f_{2}
, we note that, for any
x\in B
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f_{2}(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y)f_{2}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k}r_{0}<|x_{0}-y|\leq 2^{k+1}r_{0}}\frac{t}{(t^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|x-y|)^{n+2\alpha}}\left|f(y)-f_{B}\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq Ct\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{(2^{k}r_{0})}^{-2\alpha}{1\over{(2^{k}r_{0})}^{n}}\int_{|x_{0}-y|\leq 2^{k+1}r_{0}}\left|f(y)-f_{B}\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq Ct\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{(2^{k}r_{0})}^{-2\alpha}(1+2k)\left\|f\right\|_{BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty.

So,
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f_{2}(x)
is finite for any
x\in B
and
t>0.
Since
T_{N}f_{2}(x)
is a finite summation and
x_{0},r_{0}
is arbitrary,
T_{N}f_{2}(x)<\infty

a.e.

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Finally we note that
T_{N}f_{3}(x)\equiv 0
, since
e^{{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}}f_{3}\equiv f_{B}
for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence,
T_{N}f(x)<\infty

a.e.

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.
Then, by Propositions
2.1
and
2.3
we get the proof of part
(c)
of Theorem
2.4
. To get
(d)
, since
T_{N}1=0
, the known arguments give the conclusion, see
[
11
]
.
∎

), shows that, without lost of generality, we may assume that

(3.1)

1<\lambda\leq{a_{j+1}\over a_{j}}\leq\lambda^{2},\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a
\lambda
-lacunary sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and a multiplying sequence
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, we can define a
\lambda
-lacunary sequence
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
verifying the following properties:

(i)

1<\lambda\leq\eta_{j+1}/\eta_{j}\leq\lambda^{2},\quad\left\|\{\omega_{j}\}\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}=\left\|\{v_{j}\}\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
.

(ii)

For all
N=(N_{1},N_{2})
, there exists
N^{\prime}=(N_{1}^{\prime},N_{2}^{\prime})
with
T_{N}=\tilde{T}_{N^{\prime}},

where
\tilde{T}_{N^{\prime}}
is the operator defined in (
1.2
) for the new sequences
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}.

Proof.

We follow closely the ideas in the proof
of Proposition 3.2 in
[
2
]
. We include them here for completeness.

Let
\eta_{0}=a_{0}
, and let us construct
\eta_{j}
for positive
j
as follows(the argument for
negative
j
is analogous). If
\lambda^{2}\geq{a_{1}/a_{0}}\geq\lambda,
define
\eta_{1}=a_{1}
. In the opposite case where

a_{1}/a_{0}>\lambda^{2},
let
\eta_{1}=\lambda a_{0}.
It verifies
\lambda^{2}\geq\eta_{1}/\eta_{0}=\lambda\geq\lambda.
Further,
a_{1}/\eta_{1}\geq\lambda^{2}a_{0}/\lambda a_{0}=\lambda.
Again, if
a_{1}/\eta_{1}\leq\lambda^{2},
then
\eta_{2}=a_{1}.
If this is not the case, define
\eta_{2}=\lambda^{2}a_{0}\leq a_{1}
. By
the same calculations as before,
\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
are part of a lacunary sequence satisfying (
3.1
).
To continue the sequence, either
\eta_{3}=a_{1}
(if
a_{1}/\eta_{2}\leq\lambda^{2}
) or
\eta_{2}=\lambda^{3}\eta_{0}
(if
a_{1}/\eta_{2}>\lambda^{2}
). Since
\lambda>1,
this process ends at some
j_{0}
such that
\eta_{j_{0}}=a_{1}.
The rest of the elements
\eta_{j}

are built in the same way, as the original
a_{k}
plus the necessary terms put in between two
consecutive
a_{j}
to get (
3.1
).
Let
J(j)=\{k:a_{j-1}<\eta_{j}\leq a_{j}\}
, and
\omega_{k}=v_{j}
if
k\in J(j)
. Then

v_{j}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x))=\sum_{k\in J(j)}\omega_{k}(e^{-a_{k+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)-e^{-a_{k}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)).

If
M=(M_{1},M_{2})
is the number such that
\eta_{M_{2}}=a_{N_{2}}
and
\eta_{M_{1}-1}=a_{N_{1}-1}
, then we get

T_{N}f(x)=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x))\\
=\sum_{k=M_{1}}^{M_{2}}\omega_{k}(e^{-a_{k+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x)-e^{-a_{k}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f(x))=\tilde{T}_{M}f(x),

where
\tilde{T}_{M}
is the operator defined in (
1.2
) related with sequences
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
\{\omega_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
M=(M_{1},M_{2})
.
∎

This proposition allows us to assume in the rest of the article that the lacunary sequences

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
for the case of the fractional laplacian we shall need a Cotlar’s type inequality to control the operator
T^{*}
.
Namely, we shall prove the following theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

For each
q\in(1,+\infty),
there exists a constant
C
depending on
n,\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\lambda
such that, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and every
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

T_{M}^{*}f(x)\leq C\left\{{\mathcal{M}}(T_{(-M,M)}f)(x)+{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x)\right\},

where

T_{M}^{*}f(x)=\sup_{-M\leq N_{1}<N_{2}\leq M}\left|T_{N}f(x)\right|

and

{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\left(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}\left|f(y)\right|^{q}dy\right)^{1\over q},\quad 1<q<\infty.

For the proof of this theorem we shall need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
a
\lambda
-lacunary sequence and
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=m}^{M}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y)\right)\right|\leq{{C_{v,\lambda,\alpha,n}}\over a_{m}^{n/2\alpha}},

(ii)

if
k\geq m
and
z,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|z-y|\geq a_{k}^{1/2\alpha}
, then

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(z-y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(z-y)\right)\Big|\,\leq C_{\lambda,v,\alpha,n}\frac{1}{a_{k}^{n/2\alpha}}\lambda^{-(k-m+1)}.

Proof.

By the mean value theorem and Lemma
2.2
, there exists
a_{j}\leq\xi_{j}\leq a_{j+1}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=m}^{M}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x,y)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\sum_{j=m}^{M}(a_{j+1}-a_{j})\left|\partial_{t}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x,y)\big|_{t=\xi_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\sum_{j=m}^{M}(a_{j+1}-a_{j})\left|\frac{1}{(\xi_{j}^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+|x-y|)^{n+2\alpha}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\sum_{j=m}^{M}(\lambda^{2}-1)a_{j}^{-{n\over 2\alpha}}\leq C_{v,\lambda}\frac{1}{a_{m}^{n/2\alpha}}\sum_{j=m}^{M}\frac{1}{\lambda^{n(j-m)/2\alpha}}\leq C_{v,\lambda,\alpha,n}{1\over a_{m}^{n/2\alpha}},

where we have used
\displaystyle\lambda\leq{a_{j+1}\over a_{j}}\leq\lambda^{2}.

Now we shall prove (ii). By the mean value theorem, there exist
a_{j}\leq\xi_{j}\leq a_{j+1}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(z-y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(z-y)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\sum_{j=m}^{M}(a_{j+1}-a_{j})\left|\partial_{t}e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x,y)\big|_{t=\xi_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,\alpha,n}\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}(\lambda^{2}-1)a_{j}\frac{1}{(\xi_{j}^{1/{2\alpha}}+|x-y|)^{n+2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,v,\alpha,n}\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}{a_{j}\over(\xi_{j}^{1/{2\alpha}}+|x-y|)^{n+2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,v,\alpha,n}\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}{\frac{a_{j}}{a_{k}}}\left({1\over a_{k}^{n/2\alpha}}\right)\leq C_{\lambda,v,\alpha,n}{1\over a_{k}^{n/2\alpha}}\lambda^{-(k-m+1)},

where we have used that
k\geq m.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Observe that, for any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
N=(N_{1},N_{2}),

T_{N}f(x_{0})=T_{(N_{1},M)}f(x_{0})-T_{(N_{2}+1,M)}f(x_{0}),

with
-M\leq N_{1}<N_{2}\leq M.

Then, it suffices to estimate
\left|T_{(m,M)}f(x_{0})\right|
for
\left|m\right|\leq M
with constants independent of
m
and
M.
Denote
B_{k}=B(x_{0},a_{k}^{1/2\alpha})
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let us split
f
as

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=f\chi_{B_{m}}+f\chi_{B_{m}^{c}}=:f_{1}+f_{2}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left|T_{(m,M)}f(x_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|T_{(m,M)}f_{1}(x_{0})\right|+\left|T_{(m,M)}f_{2}(x_{0})\right|

\displaystyle=:I+II.

For
I
, by Lemma
3.3
(i), we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\left|T_{(m,M)}f_{1}(x_{0})\right|=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{j=m}^{M}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x_{0},y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x_{0},y)\right)f_{1}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,v,\lambda,\alpha}{1\over\left|a^{n/2}_{m}\right|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|f_{1}(y)\right|dy\leq C_{v,\lambda,n,\alpha}{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0}).

For part
II
,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=\left|T_{(m,M)}f_{2}(x_{0})\right|=\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})}\left|T_{(m,M)}f_{2}(x_{0})\right|dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})}\left|T_{(-M,M)}f(z)\right|dz+\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})}\left|T_{(-M,M)}f_{1}(z)\right|dz

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})}\left|T_{(m,M)}f_{2}(z)-T_{(m,M)}f_{2}(x_{0})\right|dz

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})}\left|T_{(-M,m-1)}f_{2}(z)\right|dz

\displaystyle=:A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4}.

(If
m+1=-M
, we understand that
A_{4}=0
.) It is clear that

A_{1}\leq{\mathcal{M}}(T_{(-M,M)}f)(x_{0}).

For
A_{2},
by the uniform boundedness of
T_{N}
, we get

A_{2}\leq\left(\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B_{m-1}}\left|T_{(-M,M)}f_{1}(z)\right|^{q}dz\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f_{1}(z)\right|^{q}dz\right)^{1/q}\\
=C\left(\frac{1}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B_{m}}\left|f(z)\right|^{q}dz\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left(\frac{\lambda^{n/2\alpha}}{a_{m}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B_{m}}\left|f(z)\right|^{q}dz\right)^{1/q}\leq C{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x_{0}).

For the third term
A_{3}
, with
z\in B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|T_{(m,M)}f_{2}(z)-T_{(m,M)}f_{2}(x_{0})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{m}^{c}}K_{(m,M)}(z-y)f(y)dy-\int_{B_{m}^{c}}K_{(m,M)}(x_{0}-y)f(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{m}^{c}}\left|K_{(m,M)}(z-y)-K_{(m,M)}(x_{0}-y)\right|\left|f(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\int_{B_{2^{j}m}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}m}}\left|K_{(m,M)}(z-y)-K_{(m,M)}(x_{0}-y)\right|\left|f(y)\right|dy,

where
B_{2^{j}m}=B(x_{0},2^{j}a_{m}^{1/2\alpha})
for any
j\geq 1.

By the mean value theorem, we know that there exists
\xi
on the segment
\overline{x_{0}z}
such that

\displaystyle\left|K_{m,M}(z-y)-K_{m,M}(x_{0}-y)\right|\leq\left|\bigtriangledown_{\xi}K_{m,M}(\xi-y)\right|\left|z-x_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\left|z-x_{0}\right|}{\left|\xi-y\right|^{n+1}}\leq C\frac{1}{2^{j}}\cdot\frac{a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha}}{a_{m}^{1/2\alpha}}\cdot{1\over|2^{j}a_{m}|^{n/2\alpha}},

where we have used that in each summand,
y\in B_{2^{j}m}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}m}
. Hence

\left|T_{m,M}f_{2}(z)-T_{m,M}f_{2}(x_{0})\right|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha}}{a_{m}^{1/2\alpha}}{1\over|2^{j}a_{m}|^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B_{2^{j}m}}\left|f(y)\right|dy\\
\leq C{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0})\frac{a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha}}{a_{m}^{1/2\alpha}}\sum_{j=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\leq C{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0}).

Then,

\displaystyle A_{3}=\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B_{m-1}}\left|T_{m,M}f_{2}(z)-T_{m,M}f_{2}(x_{0})\right|dz\leq C{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0}).

For the latest one,
A_{4},
we have

A_{4}=\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})}\left|T_{(-M,m-1)}f_{2}(z)\right|dz\\
\leq\frac{2^{n/2}}{a_{m-1}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})}\int_{B_{m}^{c}}\left|K_{(-M,m-1)}(z-y)f(y)\right|dydz.

Then, we consider the inner integral appeared in the above inequalities first. Since
z\in B(x_{0},{1\over 2}a_{m-1}^{1/2\alpha})
,

and the sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
\lambda
-lacunary sequence, we have
\left|z-y\right|\sim\left|y-x_{0}\right|.

From this and by Lemma
3.3
(ii), we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{m}^{c}}\left|K_{(-M,m-1)}(z-y)f(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m}^{+\infty}\int_{B_{k+1}\setminus B_{k}}\left|\sum_{j=-M}^{m-1}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(z-y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(z-y)\right)f(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,v,\alpha,n}\sum_{k=m}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-(k-m+1)}\left({1\over a_{k}^{n/2\alpha}}\int_{B_{k+1}\setminus B_{k}}\left|f(y)\right|dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,v,\alpha,n}{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0})\sum_{k=m}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-(k-m+1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,v,\alpha,n}{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0}).

Hence,

A_{4}\leq C{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0}).

Combining the estimates above for
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
and
A_{4}
, we get

II\leq{\mathcal{M}}(T_{(-M,M)}f)(x_{0})+C{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x_{0}).

And then we have

\left|T_{(m,M)}f(x_{0})\right|\leq C\left({\mathcal{M}}(T_{(-M,M)}f)(x_{0})+{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x_{0})\right).

As the constants
C
appeared above all only depend on
\left\|v\right\|_{l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
,
\lambda
,
\alpha
and
n
, we have proved that

T^{*}_{M}f(x_{0})\leq C\left\{{\mathcal{M}}(T_{(-M,M)}f)(x_{0})+{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x_{0})\right\}.

This complete the proof of the theorem.

.

, we have

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=L}^{M}v_{j}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y))\varphi(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Big(\sum_{j=L}^{M}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j}+1}\frac{1}{(t^{1/2\alpha}+|x-y|)^{n+2\alpha}}dt\Big)|\varphi(x-y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq C\big(a_{M+1}^{-{n\over 2\alpha}}-a_{L}^{-{n\over 2\alpha}}\big)\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\ {L,M\to+\infty}.

On the other hand, since
\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(x-y))dy=0
for any
j\in\mathbb{Z},
we can write

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}v_{j}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y))\left(\varphi(x-y)-\varphi(x)\right)dy.

And, then proceeding as in the case
A
, and by using the fact that
\varphi
is a test function, we have

\displaystyle|B|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\left|e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|\left|\varphi(x-y)-\varphi(x)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{a_{j+1}|y|}{(a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}+|y|)^{n+2\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}a_{j}^{1\over 2\alpha}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|y|}{a_{j}^{1\over 2\alpha}}\frac{1}{(1+{|y|\over a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}})^{n+2\alpha}}{dy\over a_{j}^{n/2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,\alpha}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}a_{-L}^{1/2\alpha}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}{a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}\over a_{-L}^{1/2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,\alpha}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}{\lambda^{1/2\alpha}\over\lambda^{1/2\alpha}-1}a_{-L}^{1/2\alpha}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\ {L,M\to+\infty},

where we have used the assumption
1/2<\alpha<1
to make the integral convergent.

For the case
0<\alpha<{1\over 2}
, we can write

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,1)}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}v_{j}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y))\left(\varphi(x-y)-\varphi(x)\right)dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B(0,1)^{c}}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}v_{j}(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y))\left(\varphi(x-y)-\varphi(x)\right)dy

\displaystyle:=B_{1}+B_{2}.

For
B_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{a_{j+1}|y|}{(a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}+|y|)^{n+2\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}a_{j}^{1\over 2\alpha}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{|y|}{a_{j}^{1\over 2\alpha}}\frac{1}{(1+{|y|\over a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}})^{n+2\alpha}}{dy\over a_{j}^{n/2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}\int_{0}^{a_{j}^{-{1/2\alpha}}}{r^{n}\over{(1+r)^{n+2\alpha}}}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}a_{-L}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}{a_{j}\over a_{-L}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}{\lambda\over\lambda-1}a_{-L}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\ {L,M\to+\infty}.

For
B_{2},
we have

\displaystyle|B_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{B(0,1)^{c}}\frac{a_{j+1}}{(a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}+|y|)^{n+2\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{B(0,1)^{c}}\frac{1}{(1+{|y|\over a_{j}^{1/2\alpha}})^{n+2\alpha}}{dy\over a_{j}^{n/2\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int^{+\infty}_{a_{j}^{-{1/2\alpha}}}r^{-2\alpha-1}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}a_{-L}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}{a_{j}\over a_{-L}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}{\lambda\over\lambda-1}a_{-L}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\ {L,M\to+\infty}.

So, when
0<\alpha<1/2
, we proved that
|B|\rightarrow 0,
as
L,M\rightarrow+\infty.
And for the case
\alpha={1\over 2},
it has been proved in
[
13
]
. Hence, we proved that
T_{N}\varphi(x)
converges for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\varphi
being a test function.

As the set of test functions is dense in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Theorem
1.1
we get the
a.e.
convergence for any function in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Analogously, since
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)
is dense in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)
, we get the
a.e.
convergence for functions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)
with
1\leq p<\infty
. By using the dominated convergence theorem, we can prove the convergence in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\omega)
norm for
1<p<\infty
, and also in measure.
∎

4.
Proof of the local growth of the maximal operator
T^{*}

In this section, we will give the proof of the local growth of the maximal operator
T^{*}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We will prove it only in the case
1<p<\infty.
For the case
p=1
and
p=\infty
, the proof is similar and easier. Since
2r<1,
we know that
B\backslash B_{2r}\neq\emptyset.
Let
f(x)=f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x)
, where
f_{1}(x)=f(x)\chi_{B_{2r}}(x)
and
f_{2}(x)=f(x)\chi_{B\backslash B_{2r}}(x)
. Then

\left|T^{*}f(x)\right|\leq\left|T^{*}f_{1}(x)\right|+\left|T^{*}f_{2}(x)\right|.

By Theorem
1.1
, we have

\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left|T^{*}f_{1}(x)\right|dx\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left|T^{*}f_{1}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f_{1}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

On the other hand, applying Hölder’s inequality on the integers and on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Fubini’s Theorem and Lemma
2.2
, for
1<p<\infty
and any
N=(N_{1},N_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f_{2}(x)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}f_{2}(x)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\left|v_{j}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right)f_{2}(x-y)~dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|\left|f_{2}(x-y)\right|~dy\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big(\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\Big\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|\left|f_{2}(x-y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dy\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\Big\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|~dy\Big\}^{p^{\prime}/p}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left|e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|\left|f_{2}(x-y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|e^{-a_{j+1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)-e^{-a_{j}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}(y)\right|\left|f_{2}(x-y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{t}{(t^{1/2\alpha}+|y|)^{n+2\alpha}}dt\right)\left|f_{2}(x-y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{|y|^{n}}\left|f_{2}(x-y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left|T^{*}f_{2}(x)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{|y|^{n}}\left|f_{2}(x-y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle=C\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\left|f_{2}(y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{r\leq|x-y|\leq 2}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}~dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\log\frac{2}{r}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

where we have used the fact
y\in B\backslash B_{2r}
and
x\in B_{r}
.
Therefore we arrive to

\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\left|T^{*}f(x)\right|dx\leq C\left(1+\Big(\log\frac{2}{r}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\log\frac{2}{r}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Then we get the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

∎

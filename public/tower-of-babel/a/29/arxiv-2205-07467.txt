q q -Munchausen Reinforcement Learning
Lingwei Zhu, Zheng Chen, Eiji Uchibe, Takamitsu Matsubara

Abstract
The recently successful Munchausen Reinforcement Learning (M-RL) features implicit Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization by augmenting the reward function with logarithm of the current stochastic policy. Though significant improvement has been shown with the Boltzmann softmax policy, when the Tsallis sparsemax policy is considered, the augmentation leads to a flat learning curve for almost every problem considered. We show that it is due to the mismatch between the conventional logarithm and the non-logarithmic (generalized) nature of Tsallis entropy. Drawing inspiration from the Tsallis statistics literature, we propose to correct the mismatch of M-RL with the help of q q -logarithm/exponential functions. The proposed formulation leads to implicit Tsallis KL regularization under the maximum Tsallis entropy framework. We show such formulation of M-RL again achieves superior performance on benchmark problems and sheds light on more general M-RL with various entropic indices q q .



1
Introduction

Temporal difference (TD) learning nowadays encompasses a large number of important reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms that attempt to learn the optimal value function by bootstrapping the current estimate
[
Sutton and Barto 2018
]
.
Recently proposed modifications on the TD loss often introduce additional information metrics of policies, e.g. Shannon entropy of the current policy for encouraging better exploration of the state-action space
[
Ziebart 2010
]
; or Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence for penalizing deviation from some given baseline policy
[
Azar et al. 2012
,
Fox et al. 2016
]
.
Such augmentation trades off optimality bias for benefits such as multimodal optimal policy (Shannon entropy)
[
Haarnoja et al. 2017
]
or average of past values (KL)
[
Vieillard et al. 2020a
]
.

To avoid computing the recursively defined KL regularized optimal policy that could incur large error, Munchausen RL (M-RL)
[
Vieillard et al. 2020b
]
proposes to implicitly perform KL regularization by adding logarithm of the current stochastic policy of any kind to the TD loss.
Empirically, significant improvement upon Deep Q-Network
[
Mnih et al. 2015
]
has been shown by adding the log-softmax-policy, derived as the result of Shannon entropy augmentation.
M-RL is an exemplar method of implicit KL regularization: in this setting a new action value function is defined and iterated upon, and the greedy policy with respect to which resembles the true explicitly regularized one
[
Kozuno et al. 2019
]
.

Motivated by the claim that arbitrary stochastic policy could be used for the Munchausen log-policy augmentation, in many scenarios it is desired to exploit other stochastic but more
compact
policies assigning probabilities only to a subset of actions.
Such policies are more well-suited to problems that require safety or parsimony on action selection: e.g. some actions under certain circumstances must have strictly zero probabilities of being selected.
This formulation naturally invites Tsallis sparse entropy
[
Lee et al. 2018
,
Chow et al. 2018
]
.
However, when Tsallis sparse policy is used in place of the softmax in the log-policy augmentation, confusing flat learning curves emerge for almost every problem considered, contradicting the general claim.
In this paper, we view the problem as a result of the mismatch between the conventional logarithm and the non-logarithmic (generalized) nature of Tsallis entropy
[
Tsallis 1988
]
: the behavior of Tsallis entropy is better described by the deformed
q
-logarithm/exponential functions rather than their standard counterparts
[
Yamano 2002
]
.

To correct the mismatch, we propose to use the
q
-log-policy drawing inspiration from the Tsallis statistics literature.
Maximum Tsallis entropy framework
[
Lee et al. 2020
]
is employed under which we show the Munchausen augmentation leads to implicit Tsallis KL regularization
[
Furuichi et al. 2004
]

by the help of
q
-logarithm and its
2-q
duality
[
Naudts 2002
]
.
The corrected formulation ensures the consistency between the non-logarithmic Tsallis policies and the Munchausen augmentation, again leading to improved performance as the original M-RL did.

The contribution of this paper is mainly theoretical in introducing the Tsallis KL regularization to bridge the gap between the maximum Tsallis entropy framework and implicit KL regularization put forward by M-RL.
Related work and background are provided in Section
2
and
3
, respectively.
Section
4
introduces the proposed method to achieving implicit Tsallis KL regularization.
We validate the proposed method by several proof-of-concept experiments on benchmark problems in Section
5
, and discuss possible future directions in Section
6
.

2
Related Work

Entropy Regularization.

Recent successful RL algorithms often feature the use of entropy regularization, which refers to augmenting the reward function with the Shannon entropy of policies as bonus
[
Ziebart 2010
,
Haarnoja et al. 2017
,
Haarnoja et al. 2018
]
, or penalizing deviating from some baseline policy (which is often the previous policy) via KL divergence
[
Azar et al. 2012
,
Fox et al. 2016
]
.
It is known that the Shannon entropy augmentation renders the optimal policy stochastic and smoothens the optimization landscape
[
Ahmed et al. 2019
]
, and KL regularization groups the error by summation, which could lead to asymptotic cancellation of errors if mild assumptions such as the sequence of errors is martingale difference
[
Azar et al. 2012
,
Vieillard et al. 2020a
]
.
This result testified to prior work that empirically averaged action values and showed to substantially improve upon the vanilla Deep Q-Network (DQN)
[
Anschel et al. 2017
,
Lan et al. 2020
]
.

However, a KL regularized policy recursively depends on the previous policies which poses a challenge for implementation
[
Geist et al. 2019
]
.
Exactly computing such recursively defined policy is intractable in practice since prohibitive memory complexity will be incurred by the need of storing all previous policies.
On other hand, naïvely evaluating only one previous policy as a surrogate for the recursive dependence typically results in large greedy step errors and underperformance
[
Nachum et al. 2017
,
Vieillard et al. 2020b
]
.
Recently, there is a trend on implicitly performing KL regularization so its properties can be enjoyed without needing to care about its recursive nature
[
Vieillard et al. 2020b
,
Vieillard et al. 2020c
]
, in which Munchausen DQN (MDQN) was one of the state-of-the-art method, achieving comparable performance with distributional methods.
However, different with the claim that any stochastic policy could be used, MDQN with Tsallis entropy induced policies showed flat learning curves for even very simple control problems.
In this paper, we show that it is because of the mismatch between the logarithm and the non-logarithmic Tsallis entropy.

Tsallis Statistics.

In the field of statistical mechanics, it is well known that a wide range of phenomena in nature such as chaos or fractals can be successfully described by power-law theories rather than the traditional Gibbs-Boltzmann statistics
[
Suyari and Tsukada 2005
]
.
Tsallis entropy has hence been proposed
[
Tsallis 1988
]
and successfully applied to various problems of which power law was of concern.
Tsallis entropy can be seen as a generalization of Shannon entropy, described by deformed logarithm and exponential functions
[
Naudts 2002
,
Yamano 2002
]
.

In RL, the introduction of Tsallis entropy was quite recent, benefited greatly from the sparsity-inducing Tsallis sparse policy
[
Martins and Astudillo 2016
,
Lee et al. 2018
,
Chow et al. 2018
]
.
It turns out that the Tsallis sparse entropy and Shannon entropy are both special cases of the maximum general Tsallis entropy framework considered in
[
Lee et al. 2020
]
.
However, maximum Tsallis entropy methods (excluding Shannon entropy) often suffer from various errors: the closed-form sparsemax policy is inherently more sensitive to errors than softmax
[
Lee et al. 2020
]
, while other entropies do not possess closed-form policy and entail approximation which could incur significant greedy step error.
Motivated by the error cancellation effect of KL, we introduce Tsallis KL (relative entropy) regularization into the maximum Tsallis entropy framework

[
Furuichi et al. 2004
,
Ohara 2007
]
which has not seen published results to the best of the authors’ knowledge.

3
Background

3.1
Reinforcement Learning

We focus on discrete-time discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) which are expressed by the quintuple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
denote state space and finite action space, respectively.

P(\cdot|s,a)
denotes transition probability over the state space given state-action pair
(s,a)
,
and
r(s,a)\in[0,r_{max}]
defines the reward associated with that transition.
When time step
t
is of concern, we write
r_{t}:=r(s_{t},a_{t})
.

\gamma\in(0,1)
is the discount factor.
A policy
\pi(\cdot|s)
is a mapping from the state space to distributions over actions.
For control purposes, we define the state-action value function following policy
\pi
as
Q_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{A}|}
:

\displaystyle Q_{\pi}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}r_{t}|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a\right],

(1)

where the expectation is with respect to the policy
\pi
and transition probability.

For notational convenience, we define the inner product for any two functions
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{A}|}
as
\left\langle F_{1},F_{2}\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
.

\boldsymbol{1}
denotes an all-one vector whose dimension should be clear from the context.
With the above notations, the Bellman operator acting upon any function
Q\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{A}|}
can be defined as:
T_{\pi}Q:=r+\gamma P_{\pi}Q
, where
P_{\pi}Q\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{A}|}\!:=\!\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a),a^{\prime}\sim\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime})}\!\left[Q(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\right]\!=\!P\!\left\langle\pi,Q\right\rangle
, and the definition should be understood as component-wise.
Repeatedly applying the Bellman operator renders
Q
converges to the unique fixed point
Q_{\pi}
, which also includes the optimal action value function
Q_{*}:=Q_{\pi^{*}}

[
Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis 1996
]
.
In this paper we focus on value iteration methods that perform the following loop to obtain the optimal policy:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\pi_{k+1}=\argmax_{\pi}\!\left\langle\pi,Q_{k}\right\rangle,&\\
Q_{k+1}=r+\gamma P\!\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}\right\rangle.&\end{cases}

(2)

In practice, one often parametrizes
Q
functions by weight vectors
\theta
and optimizes the TD loss
Q_{\theta}-r-\gamma\left\langle\pi,Q_{\bar{\theta}}\right\rangle
to obtain the new action value, where
\bar{\theta}
denotes a fixed target of
\theta

[
Mnih et al. 2015
]
.

3.2
Entropy Regularization

Recent RL algorithms often feature the trade-off between faster convergence and optimality bias introduced by information metrics.
Specifically, Shannon entropy, KL divergence and Tsallis entropy are among the most employed, respectively defined as
\mathcal{H}\left(\pi\right)\!:=\!\left\langle-\pi,\ln\pi\right\rangle
,
D_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi||\bar{\pi}\right)\!:=\!\left\langle\pi,\ln\pi-\ln\bar{\pi}\right\rangle
and
S_{q}(\pi)\!:=\!\frac{k}{q-1}\left(1-\left\langle\boldsymbol{1},\pi^{q}\right\rangle\right)
.
Note that
S_{q}(\pi)
converges to
\mathcal{H}\left(\pi\right)
when
k\!=\!\frac{1}{2},q\rightarrow 1
.
In this paper we consider the case
k\!=\!\frac{1}{2},q\!=\!2
for
S_{q}(\pi)
which corresponds to the Tsallis sparse entropy
S_{2}(\pi)\!:=\!\frac{1}{2}\!\left\langle\pi,(1-\pi)\right\rangle

[
Lee et al. 2018
,
Chow et al. 2018
]
.
Let us associate a coefficient
\tau
to
\mathcal{H}\left(\pi\right)
,
we can then succinctly write the soft value iteration as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\pi_{k+1}=\argmax_{\pi}\!\left\langle\pi,Q_{k}-\tau\ln\pi\right\rangle,&\\
Q_{k+1}=r+\gamma P\!\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}-\tau\ln\pi_{k+1}\right\rangle.&\end{cases}

(3)

It is well known that
\pi_{k+1}=\exp\left(\tau^{-1}(Q_{k}-V_{k})\right)
, where
V_{k}\!=\!\tau\log\left\langle\boldsymbol{1},\tau^{-1}Q_{k}\right\rangle
is the soft value function
[
Haarnoja et al. 2017
]
.
If we replace
\mathcal{H}\left(\pi\right)
with
S_{2}(\pi)
in Eq. (
3
), the greedy policy becomes a
sparsemax
policy:
\pi_{k+1}\!=\!\left[\frac{Q_{k}}{\tau}-\psi\left(\frac{Q_{k}}{\tau}\right)\right]_{+}
, where
[\cdot]_{+}=\max\{\cdot,0\}
, and
\psi
is the normalization ensuring the policy sums to 1:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi\left(\frac{Q(s,\cdot)}{\tau}\right)=\frac{\sum_{a\in S(s)}\frac{Q(s,a)}{\tau}-1}{|S{(s)}|},\end{split}

(4)

where
S(s)
is the set of actions satisfying
1\!+\!i\frac{Q(s,a_{(i)})}{\tau}\!>\!\sum_{j=1}^{i}\frac{Q(s,a_{(j)})}{\tau}
,
a_{(j)}
indicates the action with
j
th largest action value,
|S(s)|
denotes the cardinality of
S(s)
.
It is hence clear that Tsallis entropy augmentation renders policies sparse by truncating actions
a^{\prime}
that have lower values than the normalization
\frac{Q(s,a^{\prime})}{\tau}<\psi\left(\frac{Q(s,a^{\prime})}{\tau}\right)
.
This truncation is shown in the bottom of Figure
2
.
In the rest of the paper, we shall respectively call the Boltzmann softmax and Tsallis sparsemax policy as softmax and sparsemax policy in short.
We also term MDQN with sparsemax augmentation as log-sparsemax MDQN.

3.3
Munchausen RL

The concept of M-RL can be best described by that the authors advocate for
“optimizing for the immediate reward augmented by the scaled log-policy of the agent when using any TD scheme”

[
Vieillard et al. 2020b
, p.1]
.
As a concrete instantiation, M-RL is implemented via Munchausen Deep Q-Netwrok (MDQN):

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}\pi_{k+1}=\argmax_{\pi}\left\langle\pi,Q_{k}\right\rangle+\tau\mathcal{H}\left(\pi\right)\\
Q_{k+1}=r+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\alpha\tau\ln\pi_{k+1}}}+\gamma P\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\tau\ln\pi_{k+1}}\right\rangle,\end{cases}\end{split}

(5)

where the blue term comes from that M-RL requires a stochastic policy and hence Shannon entropy augmentation is employed.
The red term is the Munchausen log-policy term, with
\alpha
an additional coefficient.

The evaluation step of Eq. (
5
) has the following equivalence:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&Q_{k+1}\!-\!{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\alpha\tau\ln\pi_{k+1}}}=\\
&r\!+\!\gamma P\!\left\langle\!\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}\!-\!{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha\tau\ln\frac{\pi_{k+1}}{\pi_{k}}}\!-\!\alpha\tau\ln\pi_{k}\!-\!{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1\!-\!\alpha)\tau\ln\pi_{k+1}}\!\!\right\rangle\\
&=r+\gamma P\big(\!\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}-\alpha\tau\ln\pi_{k}\right\rangle-\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\alpha\tau D_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi_{k+1}||\pi_{k}\right)+(1-\alpha)\tau\mathcal{H}\left(\pi_{k+1}\right)\!\big).\end{split}

(6)

By defining a new action value function
Q^{\prime}_{k+1}:=Q_{k+1}\!-\!\alpha\tau\ln\pi_{k+1}
, we see that the augmentation of
\alpha\tau\ln\pi_{k+1}
led to implicit KL regularization.

4
Tsallis Munchausen RL

4.1
Logarithm Sparsemax Policy

Figure 1
:
MDQN with the standard
\ln\pi
augmentation,
with
\pi
being the Tsallis sparsemax policy induced by Tsallis sparse policy
S_{2}(\pi)
.
Mean and
\pm 1
standard deviation are averaged over 50 independent trials.

While MDQN was shown to perform well with Shannon entropy, it exhibits an almost flat learning curve for even simple benchmark problems such as
CartPole-v1
in the classic control library
[
Brockman et al. 2016
]
, as can be seen from Figure
1
.

The failure of MDQN in solving the task can be interpreted from two viewpoints: advantage and KL regularization.
In the advantage perspective, one may consider the Boltzmann policy
encapsulates
information of the action gap
[
Farahmand 2011
]
since
\pi_{k+1}=\exp\left(\tau^{-1}(Q_{k}-V_{k})\right)
, and the logarithm can hence be regarded as
decapsulating
it.
When the Tsallis policy is adopted, the logarithm fails to decipher the information contained in the power-law Tsallis entropy.

The KL regularization perspective is intuitive in that the equivalence in Eq. (
6
) no longer holds with Tsallis entropy augmentation
\tau S_{2}(\pi_{k+1})\!=\!-\tau\left\langle\pi_{k+1},(1-\pi_{k+1})\right\rangle
(
\tau
absorbs the constant
\frac{1}{2}
).
Since no logarithm term appears, it is clear that implicit KL regularization can no longer be performed.
It is hence desired that the Tsallis entropy be written in logarithms to match the derivation in Eq. (
6
):

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}=r+\alpha\tau\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}+\gamma P\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}-\tau\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}\right\rangle,

(7)

where
\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}
is the assumed logarithm function capable of representing
(1-\pi_{k+1})
.
There indeed exists such logarithm functions by drawing inspiration from the Tsallis statistics literature, by which we can describe the general Tsallis entropy
S_{q}(\pi)
via the deformed
q
-logarithm.
We introduce it in the next sub-section.

4.2
q
-logarithm

Tsallis entropy
S_{q}(\pi)
has been heavily exploited in statistical physics to describe phenomena that exhibit power-law behavior.
To see it is a generalization of Shannon entropy,
S_{q}(\pi)
can be defined in terms of the deformed
q
-logarithm as follows:

\displaystyle S_{q}(\pi)=\left\langle-\pi^{q},\ln_{q}\pi\right\rangle,\quad\ln_{q}\pi:=\begin{cases}\ln\pi&\text{if }q=1\\
\frac{\pi^{1-q}-1}{1-q}&\text{if }q\neq 1.\end{cases}

(8)

S_{q}(\pi)
and
q
-logarithm have the following properties:

•

Convexity.

S_{q}(\pi)
is convex for
q\leq 0
, concave for
q\geq 0
, and is a linear function for
q=0
.

•

Boundedness.

0\leq S_{q}(\pi)\leq\ln_{q}|\mathcal{A}|
for all
q
, where
|\mathcal{A}|
denotes the cardinality of the action set.

•

Monotonicity.

\ln_{q}\pi
is monotonically increasing with respect to
\pi
.

•

Pseudo-additivity.

q
-logarithm satisfies:

\displaystyle\ln_{q}{\pi\mu}=\ln_{q}\pi+\ln_{q}\mu+(1-q)\ln_{q}\pi\ln_{q}\mu.

(9)

Here, convexity ensures the regularization properties analyzed in
[
Geist et al. 2019
,
Li et al. 2019
]
hold.
Boundedness allows for derivation of lower- and upper-bounds on the optimal action values
[
Lee et al. 2018
,
Lee et al. 2020
]
.

Maximizing the general Tsallis entropy
S_{q}(\pi)
in RL has been considered in
[
Lee et al. 2020
]
.
However, taking one step further, employing general Tsallis KL regularization (Tsallis relative entropy) has never seen published results to the best of authors’ knowledge.
Nonetheless, we believe its introduction into RL is necessary for rendering MDQN compatible with Tsallis entropy.
Moreover, (generalized) KL divergence is fundamental in the sense that it produces the entropy and the mutual information as special cases
[
Furuichi et al. 2004
]
.

With the help of
q
-logarithm , one might hope to correct the mismatch in Eq. (
7
) by modifying the Munchausen term to its
\ln_{q}
version, hence recover implicit Tsallis KL regularization.
However, this point is not straightforward as the result of the pseudo-additivity Eq. (
9
):

\displaystyle\ln_{q}\!\frac{\pi_{k+1}}{\pi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}\cdot\frac{1}{\pi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}+\ln_{q}\frac{1}{\pi_{k}}+(1-q)\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}\ln_{q}\frac{1}{\pi_{k}}.

The term
\ln_{q}\!\frac{\pi_{k+1}}{\pi_{k}}
was crucial in forming the implicit KL regularization in Eq. (
6
).
Unfortunately for
q
-logarithm there is a residual
(q-1)\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}\ln_{q}\frac{1}{\pi_{k}}
, which destroys the equivalence required.
Furthermore, the residual involves both the current policy
\pi_{k+1}
and the base one
\pi_{k}
, hence one cannot define a new action value same as
Q^{\prime}_{k+1}
.

Fortunately, by resorting to the
2-q
duality from the Tsallis statistics literature, we can define Tsallis KL divergence in another way, rendering implicit Tsallis KL regularization possible.

4.3
Tsallis KL Regularization

Figure 2
:
(Top) Illustration of KL and Tsallis KL divergence components
\pi_{1}\ln\frac{\pi_{1}}{\pi_{2}}
and
-\pi_{1}\ln_{q}{\frac{\pi_{2}}{\pi_{1}}}
between Boltzmann policies and Gaussian policies when
q=2
.
(Bottom) Illustration of sparsemax acting upon
\pi_{1}
by truncating actions that have values lower than
\psi
defined in Eq. (
4
).

Before introducing the solution, we need a few definitions.
For any two policies
\pi
and
\mu
, we define their Tsallis KL divergence as:

\displaystyle D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi\left|\!\right|\mu\right):=-\left\langle\pi,\ln_{q}\frac{\mu}{\pi}\right\rangle,

(10)

where it reduces to KL divergence when
q\rightarrow 1
.
Examples of KL and Tsallis KL of the frequently used Boltzmann and Gaussian policies are shown in Figure
2
.
Tsallis KL divergence possesses the following properties similar to that of KL
[
Furuichi et al. 2004
]
:

•

Nonnegativity.

D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi\left|\!\right|\mu\right)\geq 0
.

•

Convexity.
For
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
and
q\geq 0
, consider policies
\pi_{i},\mu_{i},i=1,2
and their interpolation
\tilde{\pi}:=\lambda\pi_{1}+(1-\lambda)\pi_{2}
,
\tilde{\mu}:=\lambda\mu_{1}+(1-\lambda)\mu_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\tilde{\pi}\left|\!\right|\tilde{\mu}\right)\leq\lambda D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi_{1}\left|\!\right|\mu_{1}\right)+(1-\lambda)D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi_{2}\left|\!\right|\mu_{2}\right).

(11)

The above properties hold for both KL and Tsallis KL divergences, hence we can expect their regularization have similar characteristics.
For example, it might be possible to replace the theoretical results
[
Kozuno et al. 2019
,
Vieillard et al. 2020a
]
with their
q
-logarithm version.

The first step of deriving implicit Tsallis KL regularization is to rewrite
S_{q}(\pi_{k+1})
into a compatible form with the inner product
\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}-\tau\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}\right\rangle
, where
q^{*}
is some dual entropic index of
q
: since the original definition
S_{q}(\pi)=\left\langle-\pi_{k+1}^{q},\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}\right\rangle
has power expectation which does not easily translate to the regularization scheme in Eq. (
3
).
To this end, we adopt the
q^{*}=2-q
duality
[
Naudts 2002
]
which has the equivalence
[
Suyari and Tsukada 2005
]
:

\displaystyle S_{q}(\pi_{k+1})=\left\langle-\pi_{k+1}^{q},\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}\right\rangle=\left\langle-\pi_{k+1},\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}\right\rangle,

(12)

Eq. (
12
) defined a dual
q^{*}
-logarithm which is consistent with the one used in recent RL literature
[
Lee et al. 2020
]
.
The properties of Eq. (
8
) still hold with by replacing
q=2-q^{*}
.

The second step concerns expressing Tsallis KL divergence by
q^{*}
-logarithm without the residual term.
By the
q
-power expectation formulation of Tsallis KL
[
Ohara 2007
]
we have

\displaystyle D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi_{k+1}\left|\!\right|\pi_{k}\right):=-\left\langle\!\pi_{k+1},\ln_{q}\frac{\pi_{k}}{\pi_{k+1}}\!\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\pi_{k+1}^{q},\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}-\ln_{q}\pi_{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle\pi_{k+1}^{q},\frac{\pi_{k+1}^{1-q}-\pi_{k}^{1-q}}{1-q}\right\rangle.

We now show the equivalence of
\left\langle\pi_{k+1}^{q},\ln_{q}\pi_{k+1}-\ln_{q}\pi_{k}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\pi_{k+1},\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}-\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k}\right\rangle
by exploiting the
2-q
duality for the
S_{q}(\pi)

[
Suyari and Tsukada 2005
, Eq.(12)]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle\pi_{k+1},\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}\!-\!\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k}\right\rangle\!=\!\left\langle\!\!\pi_{k+1},\frac{\pi_{k+1}^{q^{*}-1}-1}{q^{*}-1}\!-\!\frac{\pi_{k}^{q^{*}-1}-1}{q^{*}-1}\!\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle\!\pi_{k+1},\frac{\pi_{k+1}^{q^{*}-1}-\pi_{k}^{q^{*}-1}}{q^{*}-1}\!\right\rangle=\left\langle\!-\pi_{k+1},\frac{\left(\frac{\pi_{k}}{\pi_{k+1}}\right)^{q^{*}-1}\!-1}{\pi_{k+1}^{1-q^{*}}(q^{*}-1)}\!\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle\!-\pi_{k+1}^{2-q^{*}},\frac{\left(\frac{\pi_{k}}{\pi_{k+1}}\right)^{q^{*}-1}\!-1}{q^{*}-1}\!\right\rangle\!=\!\left\langle\!\!-\pi_{k+1}^{q},\frac{\left(\frac{\pi_{k}}{\pi_{k+1}}\right)^{1-q}\!-1}{1-q}\!\right\rangle\\
&=D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi_{k+1}||\pi_{k}\right),\end{split}

(13)

Given the above equation, we can now reformulate the Tsallis version of MDQN as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&Q_{k+1}=r+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\alpha\tau\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}}+\gamma P\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\tau\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}}\right\rangle,\\
&\Leftrightarrow Q_{k+1}-\alpha\tau\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}=\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q_{k}-\alpha\tau\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k}\right\rangle-\\
&\qquad\left\langle\pi_{k+1},\alpha\tau(\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}-\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k})-(1-\alpha)\tau\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}\right\rangle,\\
&\Leftrightarrow Q^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}=\\
&r\!+\!\gamma P\!\left\langle\pi_{k+1},Q^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right\rangle\!-\!\alpha\tau D^{q}_{\!K\!L}\!\left(\pi_{k+1}||\pi_{k}\right)\!+\!(1-\alpha)\tau S_{q}(\pi_{k+1}),\end{split}

(14)

where
Q^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}\!:=\!Q_{k+1}-\alpha\tau\ln_{q^{*}}\!\pi_{k+1}
is the new action value function.
Hence it is clear that Eq. (
14
) generalizes MDQN in Eq. (
6
) by letting
q=1
.

4.4
Tsallis Policies

Similar with the exponential policy form induced by Shannon entropy,
q^{*}
-logarithm induces
q^{*}
-exponential
\exp_{q^{*}}\!
as its inverse function:

\displaystyle\exp_{q^{*}}\!(Q)=\begin{cases}\exp(Q),&\text{if }q=1\\
\left[1+(q^{*}-1)Q\right]^{\frac{1}{q^{*}-1}}_{+},&\text{if }q\neq 1.\end{cases}

(15)

q^{*}
-exponential has been exploited in
[
Lee et al. 2020
]
to yield the maximum Tsallis entropy policies as
\pi^{*}_{q^{*}}\!=\!\exp_{q^{*}}\!(Q/q^{*}-\psi_{q^{*}}(Q)/q^{*})
, where
\psi_{q^{*}}
is the normalization term depending on specific choices of the dual entropic index
q^{*}
.
When
q^{*}\neq 1,2,\infty
, there might be no closed-form expression for
\psi_{q^{*}}
and hence the policy
\pi^{*}_{q^{*}}
.

Motivated by the property of KL regularized policy

\pi_{k+1}\!\propto\!\pi_{k}\exp(Q_{k})\!\propto\!\pi_{k-1}\exp(Q_{k}+Q_{k-1})\!\propto\!\dots\!\propto\!\exp(\sum_{j=1}^{k}Q_{j}),

where we drop the regularization coefficient
\tau
for convenience,
it is natural to expect the Tsallis KL regularized policies possess a similar averaging effect.
This indeeds holds for the
q^{*}
-exponential since we have
[
Yamano 2002
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\exp_{q^{*}}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}Q_{j}\right)^{q^{*}-1}&\!\!\!\!\!\!=\left(\exp_{q^{*}}\!Q_{1}\dots\exp_{q^{*}}\!Q_{k}\right)^{q^{*}-1}\!\!\\
&+\sum_{j=2}^{k}(q^{*}-1)^{j}\!\!\!\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{j}}^{k}\!Q_{i_{1}}\dots Q_{i_{j}},\end{split}

(16)

When
q^{*}\!=\!2
, Eq. (
16
) reduces to:

\displaystyle\exp_{q^{*}}\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}Q_{j}\right)\!\!\!=\exp_{q^{*}}\!Q_{1}\dots\exp_{q^{*}}\!Q_{k}\!+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
i_{1}<\dots<i_{j}\end{subarray}}^{k}\!\!\!\!Q_{i_{1}}\dots Q_{i_{j}},

in which the residual term
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\dots<i_{j}\end{subarray}}^{k}Q_{i_{1}}\dots Q_{i_{j}}
can be regarded as a special case of adaptively weighting the contribution of each
Q_{j}
function rather than the uniform weighting.
One can also define the
q^{*}
-product
\times_{q^{*}}

[
Suyari and Tsukada 2005
, Eq.(29)]
so that
\exp_{q^{*}}\!Q_{1}\times_{q^{*}}\exp_{q^{*}}\!Q_{2}=\exp_{q^{*}}\!(Q_{1}+Q_{2})
.

One of the most important features of KL regularization is the averaging effect when facing errors.

5.3
Distributional Munchausen

We implemented TEMDQN and log-sparsemax MDQN on top of Quantile Regression DQN (QR-DQN)
[
Dabney et al. 2018
]
and compare them on a subset of 10 full-fledged Atari games
[
Bellemare et al. 2013
]
.
Instead of learning the conditional expectation
Q
function, distributional RL attempts to learn the distribution itself:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&Q_{\pi}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\pi}(s,a)\right],\\
&r+\gamma P\!\left\langle\pi,Q_{Z}\right\rangle(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma Z(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}).\end{split}

(17)

Specifically, QR-DQN aims to approximate
Z
by regressing state-action pairs
(s,a)
to a uniform probability distribution supported by
N
fixed quantiles
\varphi_{i}:Z_{\varphi}(s,a):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\varphi_{j}(s,a)}
, where
\delta_{\{\cdot\}}
is a Delta function.
The implementations of distributional TEMDQN and log-sparsemax MDQN replace every appearance of
Q
with
Z
in Eqs. (
5
), (
14
).
Implementation details are provided in Appendix
A.4
.

We allow
3\times 10^{7}
environment steps for training and the results are averaged over three seeds and shown in Figure
6
.
It is visible that log-sparsemax MDQN has the same trend as in prior experiments of yielding nearly flat learning curves, which is outperformed by TEMDQN by a large margin.

6
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper we showed that Munchausen RL with log-sparsemax-policy augmentation exhibited significantly poor performance for almost every problem considered, which contradicted the general claim of M-RL that arbitrary stochastic policy could be used.
We showed that the poor performance was due to the mismatch between the conventional logarithm and the non-logarithmic (power) nature of Tsallis entropy.
Inspired by the Tsallis statistics literature, we reformulated Tsallis entropy and rewrote the M-RL update rule by
q
-logarithm.
The resultant algorithm enjoyed implicit Tsallis KL divergence regularization which has neven been considered in RL literature.
Experiments on various benchmark problems validated the effectiveness of the proposed method.

One interesting future direction is to examine various ways of implicit Tsallis KL regularization by following a similar idea to existing literature such as MoVI, SQL, and compare their empirical performance.

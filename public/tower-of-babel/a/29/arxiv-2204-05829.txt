Abstract
. We consider the restrictions of Shi arrangements to Weyl cones, their relations to antichains in the root poset, and their intersection posets. For any Weyl cone, we provide bijections between regions, flats intersecting the cone, and antichains of a naturally-defined subposet of the root poset. This gives a refinement of the parking function numbers via the Poincaré polynomials of the intersection posets of all Weyl cones. Finally, we interpret these Poincaré polynomials as the Hilbert series of two isomorphic graded rings, one arising from the Varchenko-Gel’fand ring and another, which we call the order ring since it turns out to be naturally associated to the order polytope.

1.
Introduction and main results

Shi arrangements are well-studied hyperplane arrangements associated to an irreducible finite Weyl group.
These were initially defined by Shi to study
Kazhdan-Lusztig cells
of affine Weyl groups
[
18
]
and since then have appeared in many algebraic, geometric, and combinatorial contexts.
We refer to
[
2
, §5.1.4]
and
[
14
]
for their history in the context of finite Weyl groups.
Among many, two classical results state that

•

the number of regions is the
parking function number

(h+1)^{\ell}
where
h
is the Coxeter number and
\ell
is the rank, and

•

the number of dominant regions is the
Catalan number

\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(d_{i}+h)/{d_{i}}
where
h
and
\ell
are as above and
d_{1},\dots,d_{\ell}
are the invariant degrees.

In this paper, we study interpretations and refinements of these two (and of related) formulas in terms of the intersection poset of the Shi arrangement.
In the final section, we discuss two ring constructions, one for arrangements and one for posets, which turn out to be isomorphic in the present context.

1.1.
Background

We start with briefly fixing the necessary notation.
We refer to
[
15
]
for further background on root systems and to
[

]

In

Let
\Delta\subseteq\Phi^{+}\subseteq\Phi\subset V
be an irreducible crystallographic root system with a given choice of
simple roots

\Delta
,

\Phi^{+}
and
negative roots

\Phi^{-}=-\Phi^{+}
inside a Euclidean space
V
of dimension
\ell
with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
Then the
Shi arrangement
is

{\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})={\sf Shi}_{0}(\Phi^{+})\cup{\sf Shi}_{1}(\Phi^{+})\,,

where

{\sf Shi}_{i}(\Phi^{+})=\big\{H_{\beta,i}=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,\beta\rangle=i\}\mid\beta\in\Phi^{+}\big\}\,.

We observe that
{\sf Shi}_{0}(\Phi^{+})=\mathcal{A}(W)
is the
reflection arrangement
of the corresponding Weyl group
W=W(\Phi)
.
It in particular does not depend on the choice of simple and positive roots.
The
dominant cone
in
\mathcal{A}(W)
is the (open) cone of
V
defined by

C=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,\beta\rangle>0\text{ for all }\beta\in\Phi^{+}\}

and for any
w\in W
, the map
w\mapsto wC
is a bijection between elements in
W
and cones of
\mathcal{A}(W)
, that is, the (open) connected components of the complement
V\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}(W)}H
.
These cones have the explicit description

wC=\bigcap_{\beta\,\in\,\operatorname{Inv}(w)}H_{\beta,0}^{-}\cap\bigcap_{\beta\,\in\,\Phi^{+}\setminus\operatorname{Inv}(w)}H_{\beta,0}^{+}\,,

where
\operatorname{Inv}(w)=\Phi^{+}\cap w\Phi^{-}
is the
inversion set
of
w\in W
and

H_{\beta,i}^{+}=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,\beta\rangle>i\}\text{ and }H_{\beta,i}^{-}=\{v\in V\mid\langle v,\beta\rangle<i\}

are the open halfspaces defined by the hyperplane
H_{\beta,i}
.
Each cone
wC
contains (possibly multiple) Shi regions.
This suggests the following refinement

(h+1)^{\ell}=\#\mathbf{R}\big({\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})\big)=\sum_{w\in W}\#\mathbf{R}(wC)\,,

where
\mathbf{R}\big({\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})\big)
is the set of Shi regions and

\mathbf{R}_{w}=\big\{R\in\mathbf{R}({\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+}))\mid R\subset wC\big\}

is the set of Shi regions inside the cone
wC
.
The Shi regions
\mathbf{R}_{e}
for the
identity element

{e}\in W
inside the dominant cone
C
are the
dominant regions
and are counted by the Catalan numbers.
These are in bijection with certain subsets of positive roots, namely antichains in the
root poset
which is the poset on positive roots
\Phi^{+}
given by the cover relations
\beta\prec\gamma
for
\gamma-\beta\in\Delta

[
5
]
.

1.2.
Main results

Our first result generalizes this bijective correspondence to Shi regions inside any cone
wC
.
The
ceiling set

\mathrm{ceil}(R)\subseteq\Phi^{+}
of a Shi region
R
is the set of positive roots
\beta
such that
H_{\beta,1}
is a facet-defining hyperplane of
R
with
R\subset H_{\beta,1}^{-}
.
The following gives a bijection between
\mathbf{R}_{w}
and the set
\mathbf{A}_{w}
of antichains in the subposet
\Phi^{+}\setminus\operatorname{Inv}(w^{-1})
of the root poset.
To an antichain
A\in\mathbf{A}_{w}
, we associate the
order ideal
of
\Phi^{+}\setminus\operatorname{Inv}(w^{-1})
generated by
A
,

{{\mathcal{I}}}(A)=\{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}\setminus\operatorname{Inv}(w^{-1})\mid\gamma\prec\beta\text{ for some }\beta\in A\}\,.

Theorem 1.1
.

The map
\varphi_{w}:\mathbf{R}_{w}\to\mathbf{A}_{w}
given by

R\mapsto w^{-1}\big(\mathrm{ceil}(R)\big)

is a bijection with inverse
\varphi_{w}^{-1}:\mathbf{A}_{w}\to\mathbf{R}_{w}
given by

A\mapsto\left\{v\in V~\middle|~\begin{matrix}0<\langle v,\gamma\rangle<1\text{ for }\gamma\in w\big({{\mathcal{I}}}(A)\big)\\[3.0pt]
1<\langle v,\gamma\rangle\hskip 22.0pt\text{ for }\gamma\notin w\big({{\mathcal{I}}}(A)\big)\end{matrix}\right\}\,.

This theorem solves a problem originally proposed by Athanasiadis-Linusson in
[
6
, Section 4]
, but we will see that it follows directly from previous work of Athanasiadis
[
5
]
and Armstrong-Reiner-Rhoades
[
3
]
.
Indeed, without making it explicit, this correspondence was already implicitly considered in
[
3
]
, see
2.15

The main result of this paper is the following, which gives a bijection between antichains in
\mathbf{A}_{w}
(and thus also of regions in
\mathbf{R}_{w}
) and

\mathcal{L}_{w}=\big\{X\in\mathcal{L}\big({\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})\big)\mid X\cap wC\neq\emptyset\big\},

where
\mathcal{L}\big({\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})\big)
is the set of (non-empty) intersections of hyperplanes of
{\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})
.
While having a similar flavor as
1.1
, this result is—to the best of our knowledge—an interpretation of antichains of the root poset that has not appeared in the literature to the best of our knowledge.

Theorem 1.2
.

The map
\psi_{w}:\mathcal{L}_{w}\to\mathbf{A}_{w}
given by

X\mapsto\big\{w^{-1}(\beta)\mid X\subseteq H_{\beta,1}\big\}

is a bijection with inverse
\psi_{w}^{-1}:\mathbf{A}_{w}\to\mathcal{L}_{w}
given by

A\mapsto\bigcap_{\beta\in wA}H_{\beta,1}\,.

Remark 1.3

(Connection to nonnesting partitions)
.

Antichains in the root poset (that is, elements of
\mathbf{A}_{e}
for the identity element
e\in W
) are called
nonnesting partitions
.

1.2
shows that the map
A\mapsto\bigcap_{\beta\in A}H_{\beta,1}
is injective.
Athanasiadis and Reiner showed injectivity of the map
A\mapsto\bigcap_{\beta\in A}H_{\beta,0}
, see
[
7
, Corollary 6.2]
.
Their result establishes the embedding of the
nonnesting flats
into the intersection lattice
\mathcal{L}\big(\mathcal{A}(W)\big)
.
Thus the above result may be seen as a
lift
of the nonnesting flats into the affine intersection lattice
\mathcal{L}\big({\sf Shi}_{1}(\Phi^{+})\big)
whose image are exactly the flats cutting through the dominant cone
C
(that is, flats in
\mathcal{L}_{e}
).
Our proof of injectivity does
not
rely on their argument, nor can we deduce their result from ours without reproducing their argument.

Remark 1.4

(Similarities to a poset of Biane and Josuat-Vergés)
.

In
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
, Biane and Josuat-Vergés consider the partial order on
noncrossing partitions
, which depends on a choice of a
Coxeter element
, given by the intersection of the
absolute order
and the
Bruhat order
.
The rank sizes of their posets are also given by the Narayana numbers and all lower intervals are also Boolean lattices, compare
1.5
.
In type
A_{4}
, there is no choice of Coxeter element for which that poset is isomorphic to the interesection poset
\mathcal{L}_{e}
.

Our third result is proved as part of the proof of
1.2
and concerns the structure of
\mathcal{L}_{w}
as a poset ordered by reverse inclusion.
This poset is an order ideal of of the intersection poset
\mathcal{L}\big({\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})\big)
of the full arrangement and thus a meet-semilattice ranked by codimension, for which every lower interval
[V,X]\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{w}
is a geometric lattice.
The following theorem strengthens this property and shows that the Möbius function values of lower intervals are all equal to
\pm 1
, implying that
\mathcal{L}_{w}
is Eulerian.

Theorem 1.5
.

All lower intervals of the poset
\mathcal{L}_{w}
are Boolean.
Thus,
\mu(V,X)=(-1)^{{\operatorname{codim}}(X)}
for all
X\in\mathcal{L}_{w}
and in paricular

\#\mathcal{L}_{w}=\#\mathbf{R}_{w}.

The next results follow immediately from the above.
We state them here, as they interpret and generalize previously-studied combinatorial properties of Shi arrangements.
The
Poincaré polynomial
of the cone
wC
is

\mathrm{Poin}(wC,t)=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{L}_{w}}|\mu(V,X)|~t^{{\operatorname{codim}}{X}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\thinspace c_{k}(wC)\thinspace~t^{k},

and the coefficients
\thinspace c_{k}(wC)\thinspace
are its
Whitney numbers
.
Thus
1.5
gives the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

The
k
{}^{\operatorname{th}}
Whitney number of
wC
is

\thinspace c_{k}(wC)\thinspace=\#\{A\in\mathbf{A}_{w}\mid\#A=k\}.

Summing up these Whitney numbers yields the following refinement of the parking function numbers, which was studied by Armstrong-Rhoades in type
A

[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
and then extended to type
C
by Mészáros
[
17
, Theorem 6]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

We have

\#\big\{R\in\mathbf{R}_{w}\mid\#\mathrm{ceil}(R)=k\big\}=\sum_{w\in W}\thinspace c_{k}(wC)\thinspace\,.

Summing over all
k
yields

\sum_{w\in W}\#\mathbf{A}_{w}=\mathbf{R}\big({\sf Shi}(\Phi^{+})\big)=(h+1)^{\ell}\,.

The
Narayana numbers
are a refinement of the Catalan numbers and one way to define these is by counting antichains in the root poset by cardinality, i.e.,

{\sf Nar}^{k}(\Phi^{+})=\#\big\{A\in\mathbf{A}_{e}\mid\#A=k\big\}\,.

1.6
thus gives another interpretation of the Narayana numbers in terms of the Whitney numbers of the dominant cone.

Corollary 1.8
.

We have

{\sf Nar}^{k}(\Phi^{+})=\thinspace c_{k}(C)\thinspace\,.

Remark 1.9

(There is no
m
-eralization)
.

The Shi arrangement has a well-known
m
-eralization
given by

{\sf Shi}^{(m)}(\Phi^{+})=\bigcup_{-m<k\leq m}{\sf Shi}_{k}(\Phi^{+})\,.

Its region count is the

(mh+1)^{\ell}
and its dominant region count is the

\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(d_{i}+mh)/{d_{i}}
.
There is also an

-eralization of antichains by Athanasiadis
[
5
]
.
Unfortunately, the theory presented in this paper does not extend beyond
m=1
.
In type
A_{2}
with
m=2
, the dominant cone
C
of
{\sf Shi}^{(2)}(\Phi^{+})
is

The two shaded regions have the same single-element ceiling set,
H_{\alpha+\beta,2}
(dashed).
Moreover, the three hyperplanes

H_{\alpha,1}\cap H_{\beta,1}\cap H_{\alpha+\beta,2}=\{\bullet\}

meet at a zero-dimensional flat with absolute Möbius function value

.
Thus we can have neither a bijection between regions in the dominant cone and certain antichain generalizations defined via ceiling sets (indeed, the bijection in
[
5
]
uses more information than only ceilings), nor can we have a bijection between flats cutting through

and these dominant regions.
In particular, the above flat is the unique flat with absolute Möbius function value not equal to
1
and the number of flats inside

is

12

Although we do not define them here, the Fuss-Catalan numbers are refined by

and one can check that the Whitney number distribution matches Fuss-Narayana distribution in rank

(for all

).
This pattern fails in higher ranks as, for example, the the Poincaré polynomial of the dominant cone of in type
A_{3}
with

is
1+12t+29t^{2}+13t^{3}
while the corresponding Fuss-Narayana polynomial is
1+12t+28t^{2}+14t^{3}
.

3.2.
The order ring of a finite poset

Although our initial interest in order rings came from studying subposets of the root poset, the definition of the order ring–as well as its description via Heaviside functions–extends verbatim to any (finite) poset

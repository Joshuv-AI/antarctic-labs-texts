Abstract
. We set up a topological framework for degenerations of symplectic manifolds into singular spaces paying a special attention to the behavior of Lagrangian manifolds and their (holomorphic) membranes. We show that degenerations into singular toric varieties provide a source of exotic Lagrangian tori.



1.
Introduction

By their very definition, symplectic manifolds are smooth manifolds with a distinguished closed non-degenerate 2-form. Important class of examples is provided by complex projective varieties. If an algebraic subvariety
X^{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
is non-singular then it is a
(2n)
-dimensional smooth manifold from the point of view of differential topology. The ambient projective space
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
comes with the Fubini-Study 2-form
\omega
. As
\omega
is closed an non-degenerate,
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
is a
(2N)
-dimensional symplectic manifold, while
X
is a
(2n)
-dimensional symplectic manifold inside.

This construction gives an abundant source of examples of symplectic manifolds. But as is, it excludes singular projective manifolds that may be also very useful for constructions in classical symplectic geometry. For instance, suppose that we have a Lagrangian submanifold
L
inside the smooth locus
X^{\circ}
of a singular projective variety
X
. If
X
is smoothable to a non-singular variety
X_{t}
(inside the ambient space
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
) then
X^{\circ}
symplectically embeds to
X_{t}
and thus
L
gets embedded as a Lagrangian variety also to
X_{t}
. Thus a Lagrangian manifold inside a singular projective varieties yields a Lagrangian in a conventional symplectic manifold after smoothing.

In their treatment of mirror symmetry for minuscule varieties
[
11
]
,
Bondal and Galkin suggested an approach via Lagrangian tori obtained from toric degenerations as above.
In 2010 Galkin and Usnich
[
19

have suggested to use this approach for smoothings of weighted projective planes

given by the Markov triples

2.
Degenerations to singular symplectic spaces

2.1.
Symplectic singular spaces

Kähler varieties are complex analytic manifolds with a compatible symplectic structure.
Any smooth complex projective variety
X
or, more generally, complex submanifold of a Kähler variety
Y
is a Kähler manifold itself.
Singular projective varieties are more delicate objects.
At the same time they are very useful as they might come (somewhat paradoxically) as degenerations of rigid (undeformable) Kähler manifolds such as
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

For the purposes of this paper it is convenient to keep the topological set-up as much as possible and the algebraic set-up as little as possible.
With this in mind, we substitute singular projective varieties with the following rather general class of spaces generalizing complex algebraic varieties:
singular symplectic spaces in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
.
Furthermore, we may replace
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
by another Kähler variety of sufficiently large dimension as follows.

Let
Y
be a (smooth, but possibly non-compact) Kähler manifold of dimension
N
with the symplectic form
\omega
.
Let
\Sigma\subset X\subset Y
be closed subspaces such that

X^{\circ}=X\smallsetminus\Sigma

is a smooth connected complex submanifold of
Y
of dimension
n
.
The form
\omega
is a symplectic form on
X^{\circ}
. Its top exterior power
\omega^{n}
is a volume form on
X^{\circ}
and is defined everywhere on
Y
. Its cohomology class
[\omega^{n}]
can be evaluated on any
2n
-cycle in
Y
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that

(X,\Sigma)
is a
singular symplectic
space
of (complex) dimension
n
in the ambient Kähler manifold
Y

if
\Sigma
and
X
are closed subspaces of
Y
with
\Sigma\subset X

such that the following conditions hold.

(1)

The complement
X^{\circ}=X\smallsetminus\Sigma
is a smooth connected
n
-dimensional complex submanifold of
Y
.

(2)

For any
x\in X^{\circ}
the inclusion homomorphism
\phi_{x}:H_{2n}(X;{\mathbb{R}})\to H_{2n}(X,X\smallsetminus\{x\};{\mathbb{R}})=H_{2n}({\mathbb{C}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\};{\mathbb{R}})={\mathbb{R}}

is an isomorphism.

In particular, the fundamental class
[X]\in H_{2n}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
is well-defined as the image of the fundamental class of
({\mathbb{C}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\})
under the composition of
\phi^{-1}
and the homomorphism induced by the inclusion
X\subset Y
.

(3)

We have

\int\limits_{X^{\circ}}\omega^{n}=[\omega^{n}][X],

where the right-hand side is defined by pairing of
[X]\in H_{2n}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
and
[\omega^{n}]\in H^{2n}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
.

In particular,
X^{\circ}
has a finite volume with respect to
\omega
.

We denote the restriction of the symplectic form from
Y
to
X^{\circ}
also with
\omega
.
It turns
X^{\circ}
into a Kähler manifold.

Remark 2.2
.

Below by default we use Čech cohomology
H^{k}
for our topological spaces in consideration. The corresponding homology groups (over fields
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}
) are obtained as dual vector spaces of the corresponding cohomology groups. Note, however, that in most applications our main examples are CW-complexes, so in this case we may also equivalently choose singular or cellular cohomology as the principal homology theory.

Definition 2.3
.

A singular symplectic space
(X,\Sigma)
is called a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space if
the following conditions hold.

(1)

The inclusion homomorphism

(1)

\iota:H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{2}(X^{\circ};{\mathbb{R}})

is injective.

(2)

The canonical class
K\in H^{2}(X^{\circ};{\mathbb{R}})
is in the image of (
1
).

Recall that
\bar{a}\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
is called a cohomological extension of
a\in H^{2}(X^{\circ};{\mathbb{R}})
if
a
is the image of
\bar{a}
under
(
1
).
The following proposition is simply a translation
of Definition
2.3
in the language of cohomological extension.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that
(X,\Sigma)
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space in
Y
.

(1)

A cohomological extension of any class in
H^{2}(X^{\circ};{\mathbb{R}})
is unique.

(2)

A cohomological extension of the canonical class
K\in H^{2}(X^{\circ};{\mathbb{R}})

exists.

For brevity, we denote the unique extension of the canonical class of a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space
X
also by
K\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
.

Remark 2.5
.

The second condition in Definition
2.3
mimics (in the context of symplectic singular spaces) the key property of
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein algebraic varieties.

The dense smooth part
X^{\circ}

is naturally a symplectic
(2n)
-manifold with the symplectic structure
\omega
induced by the
symplectic form
on
Y
.
The cohomological extension in
H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
of
[\omega]\in H^{2}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
is given by the cohomology class of the symplectic form in
Y
.
Where confusion is not possible we denote it still with
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space
X\subset Y
is
monotone
if the classes

K
and
[\omega]
are proportional, i.e.
K=\alpha[\omega]\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
for some constant
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.2.
Lagrangian submanifolds of
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-spaces

Recall that for an orientable Lagrangian (or just only totally real) submanifold
L
of a symplectic manifold
X^{\circ}
we have a canonical trivialization along
L
of the top exterior power
\Lambda^{n}(T^{*}X^{\circ})
of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}X^{\circ}

(computed with respect to any symplectically compatible almost complex structure)
given by a real volume form on
L
. Even if
L
is non-orientable, we get a trivialization of
\Lambda^{n}(T^{*}X^{\circ})
over any orientation preserving loop on
L
.

For a smooth compact surface
B\subset X^{\circ}
with boundary such that
\partial B\subset L
is a collection of orientation-preserving loops in
L
, we define
K(B)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
as the obstruction to extending this trivialization from
\partial B
to
B
.
Since twice
\partial B
is always orientation-preserving, for a general smooth closed surface
B\subset X^{\circ}
with
\partial B\subset L
we may consistently define

K(B)\in\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
as one half of the obstruction to extending the corresponding trivialization on
2\partial B
. Clearly, the value
K(B)
depends only on
[B]\in H_{2}(X^{\circ},L)
.
The homomorphism

(2)

\mu:H_{2}(X^{\circ},L;{\mathbb{Q}})\to{\mathbb{Q}}

defined by
\mu([B])=-2K(B)

is called the
Maslov index
of
[B]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
(X,\Sigma)
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space and
L\subset X^{\circ}
is a Lagrangian then the homomorphism (
2
) factors through the inclusion homomorphism

(3)

H_{2}(X^{\circ},L;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{2}(X,L;{\mathbb{Q}}).

Proof.

The difference of two classes in
H_{2}(X^{\circ},L;{\mathbb{Q}})
with the same image under (
3
) comes from an absolute
{\mathbb{Q}}
-cycle in
X^{\circ}
that is homologous in
X
to a class supported by
L
. By the second condition in Definition
2.3
the canonical class vanishes on this difference.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

If the homomorphism (
3
) is surjective then
there exists a unique homomorphism

(4)

\mu:H_{2}(X,L;{\mathbb{Q}})\to{\mathbb{Q}}

extending (
2
).

This homomorphism is still called the Maslov index.

Since
X\subset Y
and
Y
are Kähler, we may compute the symplectic area of a surface
B\subset X
with boundary on a Lagrangian submanifold
L
. Since
L
is Lagrangian,
the result
\omega(B)
only depends on its homology class, thus producing the
area homomorphism

(5)

\omega:H_{2}(X,L;{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(X,\Sigma)
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space.
A Lagrangian submanifold
L\subset X^{\circ}
is called
monotone in
X
if (
3
) is an epimorphism and the homomorphisms (
5
) and (
4
) are proportional.

Clearly, if
L\subset X
is monotone then the
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space

is also monotone, and the proportionality coefficients in Definitions
2.6
and
2.9
differ by multiplication by
-2
.

2.3.
Symplectic degenerations

Let
0\in T\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be the open unit complex disk (which we view as a symplectic surface of finite area),

Y
be a Kähler manifold (e.g.
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
), and
(\mathcal{X},\Sigma)
be a singular symplectic space in
Y\times T
.
Denote by

(6)

p:\mathcal{X}\to T

the restriction of the projection
Y\times T\to T
to
\mathcal{X}
, and
by
X_{t}=p^{-1}(t)
the fiber over
t\in T
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\Sigma)
be a singular symplectic space in
Y\times T
with
\Sigma\subset Y\times\{0\}
, such that

X=\mathcal{X}\cap(Y\times\{0\})
is compact and
(X,\Sigma)
is a singular space in
Y=Y\times\{0\}
.
We say that
X\subset Y
is a
closed degeneration
of
X_{t}\subset Y=Y\times\{t\}
,
t\neq 0
, if
the following conditions hold.

(1)

The map (
6
) is proper (in particular, each
X_{t}
is compact).

(2)

The restriction

p|_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}
is a submersion.

In this case we say that
X_{t}
,
t\in T\smallsetminus\{0\}
, is a
symplectic smoothing
of
(X,\Sigma)
and that
(\mathcal{X},p)
is a
symplectic deformation
of
(X,\Sigma)
.

We have
X^{\circ}=X\smallsetminus\Sigma=\mathcal{X}^{\circ}\cap X
.
The fiber
X=X_{0}
is called the
central fiber
.
Fibers
X_{t}
,
t\neq 0
, are called
general fibers
.

Proposition 2.11
.

If
(\mathcal{X},p)
is a symplectic deformation then the inclusion of the central fiber
X\subset\mathcal{X}
induces an isomorphism in all cohomology groups
H^{k}(\mathcal{X};{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{k}(X;{\mathbb{R}})

(and thus also in all homology groups
H_{k}(X;{\mathbb{R}})\to H_{k}(\mathcal{X};{\mathbb{R}})
).

Proof.

Recall that our basic homology theory is Čech cohomology and that
Y
is a Kähler manifold. For homology computations, we may use coverings by small open disks in
Y
and by products of small disks in
Y\times T
. Since
X
is compact,
any such open covering of
X
is induced by an open covering of
p^{-1}(U)
with the same nerve, where
0\in U\subset T
is a subdisk of
T
. But
p^{-1}(U)
is a deformational retract of
\mathcal{X}
and thus the corresponding inclusion induces isomorphisms in homology.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

A smooth embedded path
\Gamma\subset T
connecting
0
and
t
defines a closed set
\Sigma_{\gamma}\subset X_{t}
of zero
\omega^{n}
-volume, and a symplectomorphism

\Phi_{\gamma}:X^{\circ}\to X_{t}\smallsetminus\Sigma_{\gamma}.

Proof.

The complement
\tilde{\Gamma}=p^{-1}(\Gamma)\smallsetminus\Sigma
is a smooth
(2n+1)
-submanifold of
Y\times T
with boundary composed of
X^{\circ}
and
X_{t}
. The form
\omega
is symplectic on each
X_{s}
,
s\in T
. The annihilator of the restriction of
\omega
to the interior of
\tilde{\Gamma}
gives the characteristic foliation

defining a connection on

that preserves the symplectic form.
The leaf of
{\mathcal{F}}
passing through a point

is a closed interval whose other end is a point
x_{t}\in X_{t}
.
We define
\Phi_{\gamma}(x_{0})=x_{t}
and
\Sigma_{\gamma}=X_{t}\smallsetminus\Phi_{\gamma}(X^{\circ})
.
The locus
\Sigma_{\gamma}
is clearly closed. It remains to prove that
\int\limits_{X_{t}\smallsetminus\Sigma_{\gamma}}\omega^{n}=\int\limits_{X_{t}}\omega^{n}
.
But the latter integral coincides with the evaluation of cohomological class
[\omega^{n}]
(which can be represented in Čech cohomology with the help of De Rham isomorphism) at
[X_{t}]
. By Proposition
2.11

[X_{t}]\in H_{2n}(\mathcal{X};{\mathbb{R}})
is in the image of the inclusion homomorphism from
H_{2n}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
. With the help of a (
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
-valued) section of

we see that
[X_{t}]
has to be the image of
[X]
.
Conservation of symplectic form by
\Phi_{\gamma}
implies that

\int\limits_{X_{t}\smallsetminus\Sigma_{\gamma}}\omega^{n}=\int\limits_{X\smallsetminus\Sigma}\omega^{n}=[\omega^{n}][X]=[\omega^{n}][X_{t}].

∎

Suppose that

Proof.

B_{t}\subset X_{t}
,
\partial B_{t}\subset L_{t}
,
we get a new membrane
B\subset\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
,
\partial B\subset L
,
by attaching to
B_{t}
a cylinder
\Gamma\times\partial B_{t}
along the symplectic connection defined by the path
\Gamma
. By construction we have
\omega(B)=\omega(B_{t})
.
The trivializations of the canonical classes of
X^{\circ}
and
X_{t}
over (the orienting double cover of)
L
and
L_{t}
define the trivialization of the canonical class of
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
over (the orienting double cover of)
L\cup L_{t}
. Thus we may speak of the values of the relative canonical class
and we get

(9)

\mu_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}(B_{t})=\mu_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}(B).

But these values depend only on the classes of
B_{t}
and
B
in
H_{2}(\mathcal{X},L_{t};{\mathbb{Q}})
and
H_{2}(\mathcal{X},L;{\mathbb{Q}})
since
\mathcal{X}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space.
Furthermore, since
B_{t}\subset X_{t}
and
X_{t}
is a regular fiber of
p
, we have
\mu_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}(B_{t})=\mu_{X_{t}}(B_{t})
.

To conclude the proof we note that the membrane
B
must be homologous in
H_{2}(\mathcal{X},L)
to a membrane
B_{0}
contained in
X
, since the embedding
X\subset\mathcal{X}
is a homotopy equivalence. We get
\mu_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}(B_{0})=\mu_{X}(B_{0})
since
X
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space.
Thus
\mu_{X}(B_{0})=\mu_{X_{t}}(B_{t})
. Since
\omega(B_{0})=\omega(B)=\omega(B_{t})
we conclude that
L_{t}\subset X_{t}
is monotone with the same monotonicity coefficient as
L\subset X
.
∎

Remark 2.19
.

For monotonicity of
L_{t}
the hypothesis that
(\mathcal{X},\Sigma)
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space is crucial.
While
\Phi_{\gamma}(L)\subset X_{t}
is Lagrangian for any symplectic smoothing
X_{t}

of a symplectic singular space
(X,\Sigma)
, it does not have to be monotone even if
(X,\Sigma)
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space and
L\subset X^{\circ}
is a monotone unless we assume that
X_{t}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-smoothing, i.e.
(\mathcal{X},\Sigma)
is also a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space.

3.
Toric degenerations

In this section we apply the framework described in the previous section to the case when the ambient space
Y={\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
is projective,
X=X\subset Y
is a normal toric variety whose embedding to
Y
is defined by a convex lattice polyhedron
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\Sigma\subset X
is the singular locus of
X
. In this case smoothings of
X
can be produced in the framework of the patchworking construction introduced by Viro
[
32
]
(see also its interpretation in
[
20
]
and
[
23
]
). We plan to give a detailed description of applying patchworking in the context of symplectic smoothings in our future paper
[
18
]
.

3.1.
Toric geometry set-up

Here we recall the basic language of toric geometry (cf. e.g.
[
12
]
and the references therein). Let
M\approx{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be an integer lattice of rank
n
and
N=M^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
its dual lattice. Denote by
M_{{\mathbb{R}}}=M\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
the corresponding real vector space and by
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}=N\otimes{\mathbb{R}}=M_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{*}
its dual space. Let

\Delta\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}

be a convex polyhedron with integer vertices and non-empty interior. Denote by
\mathcal{F}\subset M\smallsetminus\{0\}
the set of the primitive outwards supporting vectors for the facets of
\partial\Delta
.

The polyhedron
\Delta
determines a toric Kähler manifold
X=X_{\Delta}
. There are several constructions useful in different frameworks. As a symplectic manifold,
X
may be obtained from the quotient
\Delta\times(M_{{\mathbb{R}}}/M)
of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\Delta=\Delta\times M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
by the lattice
M\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Namely, over a face
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\partial\Delta

we take a quotient of the fiber torus
M_{{\mathbb{R}}}/M
by the subtorus annihilating the vector subspace of
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
parallel to
\Delta^{\prime}
.
The projection to
\Delta
yields the
moment map

\mu_{\Delta}:X_{\Delta}\to\Delta.

For
x\in\Delta
the inverse image
\mu_{\Delta}^{-1}(x)
is a real torus of dimension equal to the dimension of the face of
\Delta
containing
x
.

As a complex manifold
X
can be obtained with the help of
\mathcal{F}
. Namely, each face
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\partial\Delta
gives us a subset
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
consisting of the supporting vectors to the facets whose closure contains
\Delta^{\prime}
.
The elements of
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
generate a cone in
M
, with the dual cone
\sigma_{\Delta^{\prime}}\subset N
.
The spectrum of the semigroup algebra (over
{\mathbb{C}}
) of
\sigma_{\Delta^{\prime}}
is a (possibly singular) complex chart
U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
. The complex variety
X
is obtained by gluing these charts. All the charts contain the complex torus
M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\approx({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
.
If
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
is an integer basis of the subspace
M_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\Delta^{\prime}}\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
it spans,
then the chart
U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
is isomorphic to

\tilde{U}_{\Delta^{\prime}}=({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{\dim\Delta^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n-\dim\Delta^{\prime}}.

If it is only a rational basis then the chart
U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
is isomorphic to the quotient of
\tilde{U}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
by a finite group (with the number of elements equal to the discriminant of
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
). Considered together, symplectic and complex constructions yield the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

The singular locus of
X_{\Delta}
is given by

\Sigma=\bigcup\mu_{\Delta}^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime}),

where the union is taken over faces
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\partial\Delta
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}\subset M
is not an integer basis of
M_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\Delta^{\prime}}
.

If
\Delta
is simple, i.e. each of its vertices is adjacent to
n
facets, then a neighborhood of each singular point of
\Sigma
in
X
can be presented as the quotient singularity of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by a finite group.

In particular,
X_{\Delta}
is smooth outside of a complex codimension 2 locus and
H_{2n}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbb{Q}}
.

There is yet the third toric variety construction exhibiting
X_{\Delta}
as a subvariety of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
with
m=\#(\Delta\cap N)-1
, thus giving its non-singular part a Kähler structure. For this purpose consider a bijection between the
m+1
homogeneous coordinates of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
and the lattice points of
\Delta
. Each lattice point of
\Delta
is a multiplicatively linear functional on
M_{{\mathbb{C}}}=M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\approx({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
. Taken together, they define a map to
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{m+1}
and, after projectivization, to
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
. The closure
X_{\Delta}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
of the image recovers at the same time the symplectic toric variety of the first construction and the complex toric variety from the second construction,
see
[
20
]
. This implies the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

For each lattice polytope
\Delta\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
the pair
(X_{\Delta},\Sigma)
is a singular symplectic space.

The smooth locus
X^{\circ}=X\smallsetminus\Sigma
is a Kähler manifold.
The inverse image

Z=\mu^{-1}_{\Delta}(\partial\Delta)

is a complex (reducible) hypersurface. Smooth points of
Z
are simple poles of the meromorphic volume form

(10)

\Omega=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\bigwedge\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{dz_{j}}{z_{j}}.

Thus the homology class (with closed support) of
Z\cap X^{\circ}
is dual to minus the canonical class on
X^{\circ}
. Here
z_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, are basis coordinates in the complex torus
U_{\Delta}=M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\approx({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
.

Each element
v\in M
can be viewed by duality as a functional
v:N_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
For a facet
\delta\subset\Delta
denote with
v_{\delta}\in\mathcal{F}\subset M
its outward primitive integer supporting vector. Then
v_{\delta}|_{\delta}
is constant. Denote
h_{\delta}=v_{\delta}(\delta)
.
The condition that
0
is contained in the interior of
\Delta
is equivalent to the condition that
h_{\delta}>0
for all facets
\delta\subset\Delta
.
In this case the fiber

L=\mu^{-1}_{\Delta}(0)\subset X

is a Lagrangian torus.
Furthermore, the restriction of
\Omega
to
L
is a real volume form (of total volume 1). Thus
the Maslov index
m(B)
of a membrane
B\subset X
,
\partial B\subset L
, coincides with twice the intersection number
\#(B\cap Z)
,

(11)

m(B)=2\#(B\cap Z).

Lemma 3.3
.

The Lagrangian submanifold
L\subset X^{\circ}
is monotone if and only if
h=h_{\delta}
does not depend on the choice of the facet
\delta\subset\Delta
.

Proof.

Connect
0
and a point inside
\delta
with a simple path
\gamma\subset\Delta
.
Let
D_{\gamma}\subset X
be the disk obtained from
\gamma\times S\subset\Delta\times(M_{{\mathbb{R}}}/M)

after the quotient. Here
S\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}/M
is a simple closed curve parallel to
v_{\delta}
.
By (
11
) we have
m(D_{\gamma})=2
. But
\omega(D_{\gamma})=h_{\delta}
by the construction of the symplectic form
\omega
on the cotangent space
T^{*}\Delta=\Delta\times(M_{{\mathbb{R}}}/M)
. The lemma follows from the observation that the disks
D_{\gamma}
generate the group
H_{2}(X^{\circ},L;{\mathbb{Q}})
.
∎

3.2.
Toric
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-spaces and Maslov indices.

In general,
(X_{\Delta},\Sigma)
does not have to be a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space.

Proposition 3.4
.

A pair
(X_{\Delta},\Sigma)
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space if and only if for each vertex
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Delta
(a face of dimension 0) the set
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}\subset M
is contained in an affine hyperplane.

Proof.

To compute the group
H_{2}(X_{\Delta};{\mathbb{R}})
in terms of
\Delta
we
consider the vector space
V_{\Delta}
whose basis is in 1-1 correspondence with the facets of
\Delta
and the linear map
\lambda:V_{\Delta}\to M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
sending each facet to its outward normal vector. For the dense torus
U_{\Delta}\approx({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
in
X_{\Delta}
we have

H_{2}(X_{\Delta},U_{\Delta};{\mathbb{R}})=V_{\Delta}.

The long exact sequence of
(X_{\Delta},U_{\Delta};{\mathbb{R}})
implies that
H_{2}(X_{\Delta};{\mathbb{R}})=\ker\lambda
.
Let
\operatorname{Sk}^{n-2}(\Delta)
be the union of the faces of
\Delta
of dimension up to
n-2
.
Since the inclusion homomorphism

H_{2}(X\smallsetminus\mu_{\Delta}^{-1}(\operatorname{Sk}^{n-2}(\Delta));{\mathbb{R}})\to H_{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})

is surjective, the inclusion homomorphism
H_{2}(X^{\circ};{\mathbb{R}})\to H_{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
is surjective as well.

Since
\Delta^{\prime}
is a vertex, its inverse image
\mu^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})
is a point. The chart
U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
is contractible and contains a fiber torus
L_{x}=\mu^{-1}(x)
,
x\in\operatorname{Int}\Delta
.
Connecting
x
to a facet
\delta
adjacent to
\Delta^{\prime}
with a simple path we get a disk
D_{\gamma}
of Maslov index 2 as in
the proof of Lemma
3.3
. The class

[D_{\gamma}]\in H_{2}(X_{\Delta},L_{x};{\mathbb{R}})=H_{2}(X_{\Delta},U_{\Delta};{\mathbb{R}})=V_{\Delta}

is the basis vector corresponding to
\delta
.
Thus
K_{X^{\circ}}
has equal values on all basis vectors in
V_{\Delta}
.
But
D_{\gamma}
is contained in
U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
.
Contractibility of
U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
implies

H_{2}(U_{\Delta^{\prime}},L_{x})=H_{1}(L_{x})=M\approx{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

If
K_{X^{\circ}}
is extendable to
X^{\circ}\cup U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
then any linear relation on the elements of
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
has to have zero sum of coefficients. Thus the elements of
\mathcal{F}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
must be contained in a hyperplane of
M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Conversely, if they are contained in such a hyperplane, we may find an extension of
K_{X^{\circ}}
to
X^{\circ}\cup U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
, and thus also to
X
.
∎

The hyperplane containment condition is vacuous in the case when each vertex of
\Delta
is adjacent to
n
facets.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
\Delta
is simple then
(X_{\Delta},\Sigma)
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space.

From now on we suppose that
0\in\Delta\subset N_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is a simple monotone convex lattice polyhedron, so that
L=L_{0}
is a monotone Lagrangian torus in the
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space
(X_{\Delta},\Sigma)
.

Suppose that
B\subset X_{\Delta}
is a membrane, i.e. the image of a smooth surface map in
X
such that
\partial B\subset L
.
Suppose that
x\in B\cap Z
is isolated.
By Proposition
3.1
, a neighbourhood of
x
is locally isomorphic to the quotient of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
by a group of order
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
We define the local intersection number
\#_{x}(B\cap Z)
as
\frac{1}{l}
times the local intersection number of the inverse images of
B
and
Z
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. It is a rational number.

Recall that
U_{\Delta}=\mu^{-1}_{\Delta}(\operatorname{Int}\Delta)
has the canonical complex structure of
M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\approx({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
. Thus it comes with the natural map

\operatorname{Log}:({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}\approx U_{\Delta}\to M_{{\mathbb{R}}}\approx{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

given in coordinates by
\operatorname{Log}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=(\log|z_{1}|,\dots,\log|z_{n}|)
.

A non-zero element
v\in M
generates a subgroup in
M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, and thus also a holomorphic cylinder
C_{v}\subset({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
such that
\operatorname{Log}(C_{v})\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is the line passing through the origin and parallel to
v
.
We refer to any multiplicative translate
aC_{v}
,
a\in M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
as a
holomorphic cylinder parallel to
v
.

By a
proper holomorphic curve in
X
with boundary in
L
we mean the image
B=f(S)\subset X
of a non-constant continuous map
f:S\to X
from a compact Riemann surface
S
with boundary
\partial S
such that
f|_{S\smallsetminus\partial S}
is holomorphic and
f(\partial S)\subset L
. We say that
B
is irreducible if
S
is connected. We refer to an irreducible proper holomorphic curve with boundary in
L
(as well as to its image in
X
) as a
holomorphic membrane
for
L
if its
boundary

\partial B=f(\partial S)
is non-empty.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
B\subset X_{\Delta}
is
a holomorphic membrane for
L_{x}
,
x\in\operatorname{Int}\Delta
.
Then
B\cap Z
is a finite set and
the Maslov index of
B
can be computed as

(12)

m(B)=2\sum\limits_{z\in B\cap Z}\#_{z}(B\cap Z),

and
\#_{z}(B\cap Z)>0
for any
z\in B\cap Z
.

Proof.

Since
Z
is disjoint from a neighborhood of
\partial B\subset L
, the set of isolated points of
Z\cap B
must be finite,
l(\#_{z}(B\cap Z))
is the local intersection number of a curve and a hypersurface in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and thus is a positive integer number.
The formula (
12
) is a corollary of (
11
) and the surjectivity of the homomorphism
H_{2}(X^{\circ},L)\to H_{2}(X,L)
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
B=f(S)\subset X_{\Delta}
is
a holomorphic membrane for
L_{x}=\mu_{\Delta}^{-1}(x)
,
x\in\operatorname{Int}\Delta
,
such that
f^{-1}(Z)\subset S
consists of a single point.

Then
\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}|_{B}
is constant for any multiplicatively linear map

\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}:M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

defined as the pairing with an element

\nu\in N
such that
\nu([\partial B])=0
.

Here we view
[\partial B]
as the element of
H_{1}(L_{x};{\mathbb{R}})=M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
(sitting on the lattice
M\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
).

Proof.

Note that
\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}:M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is a product of integer powers of the holomorphic coordinates in the dense torus
U_{\Delta}\subset X_{\Delta}
.
Let
w\in S\smallsetminus\partial S
be the (only) point of
f^{-1}(Z)
. Its image
f(w)\in Z
is contained in the toric chart
U_{\Delta^{\prime}}
for a face
\Delta^{\prime}\subset\Delta
, and thus the meromorphic function
\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}\circ f
near
w
can be expressed in a local coordinate
z
near
w
as

c(z-w)^{k}+o((z-w)^{k}),

c\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
If
k>0
then a small punctured disk centered at
w
covers a small disk in
{\mathbb{C}}
centered at 0. If
k<0
, we get such covering for
1/(\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}\circ f)
. In both cases we conclude that the linking number of
\nu_{\mathbb{C}}\circ f(\partial S)
and
0
in
{\mathbb{C}}
is
k\neq 0
.
We get a contradiction since this number is given by
\nu[\partial B]=0
.
Thus
k=0
and
the function
\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}\circ f
can be extended to a continuous function on
S
holomorphic on
S\smallsetminus\partial S
and constant on
\partial S
(since
f(\partial S)\subset L_{x}
).
By the maximum principle,
\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}\circ f
is constant.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
B=f(S)\subset X_{\Delta}
is a holomorphic membrane for

L_{x}=\mu_{\Delta}^{-1}(x)
,
x\in\operatorname{Int}\Delta
, such that
f^{-1}(Z)\subset S
consists of a single point.

Then
[\partial B]\neq 0\in H_{1}(L)=M
, and
B\cap(M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
is contained in a holomorphic cylinder
aC_{[\partial B]}
,
a\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
, parallel to
[\partial B]\in M
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
,
\nu_{{\mathbb{C}}}
is constant on the set
B\cap(M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
for any
\nu\in N
with
\nu[\partial B]=0
.
Thus if
[\partial B]=0
then
B
must consist of a single point which leads us to a contradiction.
If
[\partial B]\neq 0
then the multiplicative projection of
B\cap(M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
along
[\partial B]
consists of a single point in
({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n-1}
, and therefore,
B\cap(M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
is contained in
aC_{[\partial B]}
for some
a\in({\mathbb{C}}^{\times})^{n}
.
∎

4.
Holomorphic membranes in toric
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-degenerations

As it was suggested in
[
16
]
, monotone Lagrangian submanifolds may be symplectically distinguished by the enumeration of holomorphic disks of the smallest possible Maslov index. The disks are holomorphic with respect to an almost complex structure compatible with the symplectic structure.
In the case of orientable monotone Lagrangian with the positive proportionality coefficient between the Maslov index and the area, any holomorphic membrane has the Maslov index of at least 2, so no bubbling of disks is possible.
By the Riemann-Roch formula, the expected dimension of the space of Maslov index 2 disks is
n-1
.
Thus by the Gromov compactness
[
21
]
we have finitely many of such disks passing through a fixed point of the Lagrangian in the case if these disks are regular.

Consider a closed symplectic degeneration
p:\mathcal{X}\to T
,
X_{t}=p^{-1}(t)
,
t\in T\ni 0
.
Let
L=L_{0}\subset X_{0}
be an orientable Lagrangian submanifold (disjoint from the singular locus
\Sigma\subset X_{0}\subset\mathcal{X}
).
The image
L_{t}=\Phi_{\gamma}(L_{0})
of
L_{0}
under the symplectomorphism
\Phi_{\gamma}:X^{\circ}\to X_{t}\smallsetminus\Sigma_{\gamma}
provided by Proposition
2.12
is an orientable Lagrangian in the smooth symplectic manifold
X_{t}
. If
\mathcal{X}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-degeneration and
L_{0}\subset X_{0}
is monotone then by Proposition
2.18
the Lagrangian
L_{t}\subset X_{t}
is also monotone.
In other words,
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-smoothings of
(X,L)
where
L
is a monotone Lagrangian submanifold of
X
produce monotone Lagrangian submanifolds
L_{t}
diffeomorphic to
L=L_{0}
in the smooth symplectic manifold
X_{t}
,
t\neq 0
.

For the rest of the paper we assume that
(X_{t},L_{t})
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-smoothing of
(X_{0},L_{0})
where
L_{0}\subset X_{0}
is a monotone orientable Lagrangian.
Thus for any relative homology class
\beta_{t}\in H_{2}(X_{t},L_{t})
with
m(\beta_{t})=2
the number of holomorphic disks
\#_{\operatorname{disks}}(\beta_{t})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
passing through a point
a\in L_{t}
for a generic compatible almost complex structure is well-defined.
It is invariant under symplectomorphisms, and does not depend on the choice of a generic compatible almost complex structure.
Here we reduce the number of disks mod 2 to avoid a need to discuss orientation issues.

Since
L\subset X^{\circ}
, we have a canonical identification
H_{1}(L_{t})=H_{1}(L)
for any
t\in T
by Proposition
2.12
.
For a class
\alpha\in H_{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
we define

(13)

\#_{\operatorname{disks}}(\alpha)=\sum\limits_{\beta_{t}}\#_{\operatorname{disks}}(\beta_{t})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},

where the sum is taken over all
\beta_{t}\in H_{2}(X_{t},L_{t})
of Maslov index 2 and such that
\partial\beta_{t}=\alpha\in H_{1}(L_{t};{\mathbb{R}})
.
By the Gromov compactness and monotonicity, the sum on the right-hand side is finite since the area of all holomorphic disks in the sum is the same (and thus bounded).
By Corollary
2.13
, the sum does not depend on the choice of
t\neq 0
.
We define

(14)

\Delta^{\operatorname{disks}}(L)=\operatorname{ConvexHull}\{\alpha\in H_{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})\ |\ \#_{\operatorname{disks}}(\alpha)\neq 0\}.

This is a convex lattice polygon in the vector space
H_{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
with the lattice
H_{1}(L)/\operatorname{Tor}H_{1}(L)
.
Any symplectomorphism of
(X_{t},L_{t})
induces a lattice-preserving isomorphism of
H_{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
. As a lattice polygon,
\Delta^{\operatorname{disks}}(L)
is a symplectic invariant of
(X_{t},L_{t})
.

Following
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
, we consider the case when
L=\mu_{\Delta}^{-1}(0)
is a monotone Lagrangian torus in the toric variety
X_{\Delta}
which is a symplectic
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-space. Note that by Corollary
3.5
the latter holds for any simple lattice polygon
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. More generally, the
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-condition coincides with the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein condition on the normal toric variety
X_{\Delta}
. Denote

\Delta^{*}=\operatorname{ConvexHull}(\mathcal{F})\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}},

where
\mathcal{F}\in M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is the set of primitive outward supporting vectors for the facets of
\Delta
.

Theorem 1
.

For any
{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma
-smoothing
(X_{t},L_{t})
of
(X_{\Delta},L)
we have

\Delta^{\operatorname{disks}}(L)=\Delta^{*}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\in H_{1}(L;{\mathbb{R}})
is a class such that
\#_{\operatorname{disks}}(\alpha)\neq 0
.
Consider the smooth embedded path

\alpha

All these disks are contained in the symplectic product

By the Gromov compactness we may extract a subsequence of holomorphic disk membranes converging to a possibly reducible (bubbled) curve

\alpha

The components of

m(A_{k})=-2K.[A_{k}]

A_{k}
and
B_{j}
are positive rational numbers adding to

.
Suppose that

Z

10

and twice the local intersection number of

and

at

\Lambda^{n}(T^{*}(X_{\Delta}\smallsetminus\Sigma))
given by (
10

Thus

must be a disk component with

The image

is contained in the relative interior of a face of
\Delta

If

.
Since the vertex chart

The Maslov index of membranes in

If

Let

\Delta^{*},(\Delta^{\prime})^{*}\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}\approx{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Let

\Sigma

\Sigma^{\prime}

Suppose that

Corollary 2
.

If the pairs

(X^{\prime}_{t},L^{\prime}_{t})
are symplectomorphic then the polytopes
\Delta^{*},(\Delta^{\prime})^{*}\subset M_{{\mathbb{R}}}
coincide up to an automorphism of the lattice

Proof.

The automorphism of

is induced by the restriction to
L_{t}
of the symplectomorphism between
X_{t}
and
X^{\prime}_{t}

∎

In other words, if
X_{\Delta}
and
X_{\Delta^{\prime}}
are different toric degenerations of the same symplectic manifold then the tori
L_{t}
and

are symplectically different.

Corollary 3
.

The number of non-symplectomorphic Lagrangian tori in a complex projective variety is bounded from below by the number of its
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein toric degenerations.

Example 4.1
.

In the case
n=2
there is an infinite number of different
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein toric degenerations of Del Pezzo surfaces, see
[
22
]
for
\dim H_{2}(X_{\Delta};{\mathbb{R}})=1
and in
[
14
]
for
\dim H_{2}(X_{\Delta};{\mathbb{R}})>1
.
This implies that the number of distinct monotone Lagrangian tori in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and all other Del Pezzo surfaces is also infinite (cf.
[
31
]
and
[
30
]
).

In particular, the toric degenerations of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
are described by the Markov triples
(a,b,c)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
,
a\leq b\leq c
, satisfying to the Diophantine equation
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=3abc
with the infinite number of solutions. Distinct Markov triples
(a,b,c)
produce distinct monotone Lagrangian tori
L(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})
coming from the monotone Clifford tori in the weighted projective plane
{\mathbb{P}}(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})

For other Del Pezzo surfaces the toric degenerations with Picard number equal to 1 are described in
[
22
]
while those of Picard number greater than 1 are constructed in
[
14
]
. All these degenerations produce exotic monotone Lagrangian tori in the corresponding Del Pezzo surfaces. Furthermore, Theorem
1
implies that symplectic geometry of the resulting torus detects the Picard number of the degeneration.

Example 4.2
.

For
n>2
, we can get examples of exotic Lagrangian tori by taking the product of tori from
4.1

is the monotone fiber circle in
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\approx S^{2}

Theorem

implies that if two toric degeneration tori

By the Gromov compactness, the number of classes

The element

\sum\#_{\operatorname{disks}}(\alpha)\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}[H_{1}(L)]

Remark 4.3
.

If the central fiber of the degeneration has terminal singularities,
then the Floer potential of the Lagrangian torus

is determined by
1
,
because the only non-trivial lattice points of the polytope are vertices and the respective
number of discs for them equals one.

In the non-terminal case, the Theorem reduces the determination of Floer potentials to a finite algebraic question.

The idea to use toric degenerations to study mirror symmetry for Fano manifolds

Example 4.7

Let

As a complex variety, it is a Fano manifold of complex dimension

As a symplectic manifold, it is monotone, and its symplectomorphism class depends only on

For any trivalent connected graph

The combinatorial non-abelian Torelli theorem

Categorifying non-commutative deformations
Agnieszka Bodzenta, Alexey Bondal

Abstract
. We define the functor ncDef ( Z 1 , … , Z n ) \textrm{ncDef}_{(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})} of non-commutative deformations of an n n -tuple of objects in an arbitrary 𝕜 \mathbbm{k} -linear abelian category 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} . In our categorified approach, we view the underlying spaces of infinitesimal flat deformations as Deligne finite categories, i.e. finite length abelian categories admitting projective generators, with n n isomorphism classes of simple objects. More generally, we define the functor ncDef ζ \textrm{ncDef}_{\zeta} of non-commutative deformations of an exact functor ζ : 𝒜 → 𝒵 \zeta\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{Z} of abelian categories. Here the role of an infinitesimal non-commutative thickening of 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} is played by an abelian category ℬ {\mathcal{B}} containing 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} and such that 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} generates ℬ {\mathcal{B}} by extensions. The functor ncDef ζ \mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta} assigns to such ℬ {\mathcal{B}} the set of equivalence classes of exact functors ℬ → 𝒵 {\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{Z}} which extend ζ \zeta . We prove that an exact functor on an infinitesimal extension is fully faithful if and only if it is fully faithful on the first infinitesimal neighbourhood. We show that if ζ \zeta is fully faithful, then the functor ncDef ζ \textrm{ncDef}_{\zeta} is ind-represented by the extension closure of the essential image of ζ \zeta . We prove that for a flopping contraction f : X → Y f\colon X\to Y with the fiber over a closed point C = ⋃ i = 1 n C i C=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}C_{i} , where C i C_{i} ’s are irreducible curves, { 𝒪 C i ​ ( − 1 ) } \{\mathcal{O}_{C_{i}}(-1)\} is the set of simple objects in the null-category for f f . We conclude that the null-category ind-represents the functor ncDef ( 𝒪 C 1 ​ ( − 1 ) , … , 𝒪 C n ​ ( − 1 ) ) \textrm{ncDef}_{(\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}}(-1),\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{C_{n}}(-1))} .

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we develop a categorical point of view on non-commutative deformation theory.
The classical commutative infinitesimal deformation theory of a mathematical object defined over a base field
\mathbbm{k}
is governed by the so-called
deformation functor
on the category of commutative local Artinian algebras with values in the category of sets
[
Sch68
]
. The functor assigns to such an algebra
A
all possible flat deformations of the object over
\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits A
.
O. A. Laudal initiated the study of non-commutative infinitesimal deformations of a module over an algebra by replacing Artinian algebras by non-commutative Artinian algebras
[
Lau02
]
.

When deforming a collection of objects, a new phenomenon is observed: in contrast to the commutative case, a non-commutative deformation space can be made more complicated than just the product of the deformation spaces for the objects deformed separately. In a sense, objects interact in the deformation like entangled particles in Quantum Mechanics. This interaction of objects is governed by their pairwise
\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits
groups. For non-commutative deformations of
n
modules over a
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra, O. A. Laudal suggested to replace Artinian algebras by
n
-pointed algebras
[
Lau02
]
, which are basic algebras with a fixed splitting
\mathbbm{k}^{\oplus n}\to A
of the maximal semi-simple quotient
A/\textrm{rad A}\simeq\mathbbm{k}^{\oplus n}
.

Our proposal is to replace the category of
n
-pointed algebras by the category
\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}
of Deligne finite categories, by which we mean finite length abelian categories with
n
simple objects,
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, satisfying the finiteness condition on dimension of Hom and
\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}
groups and possessing a projective generator (
cf.

[
Del90
]
).
Also we consider a bigger category
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}}
, whose objects are similar categories with the condition on the existence of a projective generator omitted. These categories play the role of ind-representing objects for deformation functors, analogous to complete local algebras that pro-represent deformation functors in the classical deformation theory of Schlessinger
[
Sch68
]
.

Our approach is based on the observation that, for a commutative Artinian algebra
A
and a scheme
X
, a deformation along
\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits A
of a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
, i.e. a sheaf on
X\times\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits A
flat over
\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits A
, is uniquely determined by an
exact
functor
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits A\to\mathop{\textrm{QCoh}}\nolimits(X)
, where
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits A
is the category of finite dimensional right
A
-modules. This supports the idea to define a non-commutative infinitesimal deformation of a finite collection of
n
objects
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
in an abelian category
\mathcal{Z}
as an exact functor
{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{Z}
out of a Deligne finite category
{\mathcal{A}}
, which, for any
i
, takes the simple object
S_{i}
in
{\mathcal{A}}
to
Z_{i}
.

The
non-commutative deformation functor
of an ordered collection
(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
of objects in
\mathcal{Z}

\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})}:\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits

assigns to
{\mathcal{A}}
the set of isomorphism classes of these exact functors. A further extension of the theory where the deformation functor is a 2-functor out of the
2-category

\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}
to the 2-category of groupoids will be discussed in a subsequent paper. In particular, it will be explained that the 2-categorical abbreviation of the
\infty
-category deformation theory for collections of objects in an abelian category does not lose any information of the latter.

In the categorical approach of this paper, there is no need of the choice of the splitting for the semi-simple part of the algebra, as it was for
n
pointed algebras. Instead, we consider the category
{\rm Bas}_{n}
of basic unital finite dimensional
\mathbbm{k}
algebras such that
A/\textrm{rad}A\simeq\mathbbm{k}^{n}
together with a full order on simple modules, i.e. a homomorphism
q_{A}\colon A\to\mathbbm{k}^{n}
which induces an isomorphism
A/\textrm{rad}A\simeq\mathbbm{k}^{n}
.
Morphism in
\textrm{Bas}_{n}
are conjugacy classes of homomorphisms
\varphi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
satisfying
q_{A^{\prime}}\circ\varphi=q_{A}
. Here, we say that
\varphi,\psi\colon A\to A^{\prime}
are conjugate if there exists an invertible
u\in A^{\prime}
with
q_{A^{\prime}}(u)=1
, such that
\varphi=u\psi u^{-1}
. Note that morphisms in this category are not just homomorphisms of algebras.

According to Theorem
4.2
, the category
{\rm Bas}_{n}
is equivalent to the category opposite to
\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}
. The equivalence takes algebra
A
from
{\rm Bas}_{n}
to the category
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits A
.

It turns out that the categorified deformation theory of this paper is good for constructing the ‘universal deformation’, which in categorical terms means the (ind)representability of the non-commutative deformation functor. Namely, we prove the ind-representability under the condition that the collection of objects that we deform is simple. Following Y. Kawamata
[
Kaw18
]
, we say that a collection
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of objects in a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category
{\mathcal{Z}}
is
simple
, if

\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})=\delta_{ij}.

We denote by
{\mathcal{F}}(\{Z_{i}\})
the smallest full subcategory in
{\mathcal{Z}}
closed under extensions and containing
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
. Then we prove

Theorem A.

(see Theorem
6.6
)
Let
(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
be an ordered simple collection of objects in abelian category
{\mathcal{Z}}
. If
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}(\bigoplus Z_{i},\bigoplus Z_{i})
is finite, then
{\mathcal{F}}(\{Z_{i}\})
is the object in
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}}

that ind-represents the non-commutative deformation functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})}
.

Note that the (pro)representability of the deformation functor does not hold for the category of
n
-pointed algebras, mostly because morphisms in the underlying category were homomorphisms of algebras and not the conjugacy classes of those. Instead, there exists the hull, i.e. a surjective morphism from a representable functor to the relevant deformation functor
[
Lau02
]
.

The functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
can be viewed as a deformation of an (exact) functor
T_{(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})}\colon\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
, i.e. of an additive functor which maps the
i
’th simple
\mathbbm{k}^{n}
-module to
Z_{i}
. Now we replace
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}
by an arbitrary abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
. We introduce the category
\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}
of abelian categories which are infinitesimal extensions of
{\mathcal{A}}
, i.e. categories
{\mathcal{B}}
containing
{\mathcal{A}}
as a full subcategory and such that the extension closure of
{\mathcal{A}}
is
{\mathcal{B}}
.

For an exact functor
\zeta\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
, we consider a non-commutative deformation functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
. It assigns to
{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}
the set of equivalences classes of exact functors
F\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
together with an isomorphism
F|_{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\zeta
.

Theorem B.

(see Theorem
6.4
)
Let
\zeta\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
be a fully faithful functor. Then the extension closure
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}(\zeta({\mathcal{A}}))
of the full subcategory
\zeta({\mathcal{A}})\subset{\mathcal{Z}}
ind-represents the non-commutative deformation functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}
.

We give a criterion of the fully faithfulness of an exact functor
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
provided
{\mathcal{A}}
is the extension closure of a full subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
. We show that
\Phi
is fully faithful if and only if its restriction to the first infinitesimal neighbourhood of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
is, see Theorem
6.14
.

The original geometric motivation for this paper was to better understand the relation between flops and non-commutative deformations.
For the case of a flopping contraction of a
(0,-2)
-curve
C
on a threefold
X
, Y. Toda proposed in
[
Tod07
]
an interpretation of the flop-flop functor as the spherical twist with respect to a spherical functor
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\rm mod-}A)\to{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
, where
A=\mathbbm{k}[x]/x^{n}
. Also he gave an interpretation for
{\rm Spec}A
as the moduli of (commutative) deformations of
{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(-1)
in
\mathop{\textrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
.

This idea was further developed by W. Donovan and M. Wemyss in
[
DW16
]
. They interpreted the flop-flop functor for a more general threefold flopping contraction with an irreducible flopping curve
C
in terms of the so-called contraction algebra, which is a hull for the non-commutative deformation functor for
{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(-1)
in the sense of Laudal.
Using M. Van den Bergh’s non-commutative resolution which gives an equivalence
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\Lambda)
, for a suitable non-commutative algebra
\Lambda
, W. Donovan and M. Wemyss transferred their analysis into the pure algebraic framework of
\Lambda
representations. They considered the contraction algebra
\Lambda_{con}
, which is a quotient of
\Lambda
by an ideal
I
, and proved that
I
regarded as a
\Lambda-\Lambda
bimodule defines a self-equivalence of

{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\Lambda)
. When this equivalence is transported into
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(X)
it coincides with the flop-flop functor. In subsequent paper
[
DW19
]
, the authors considered the case of reducible flopping curve
C
on a threefold
X
and described relations between auto-equivalences for flops with centers in different irreducible components of
C
.

Consider a more general flopping contraction
f\colon X\to Y
of Gorenstein varieties with fibers of dimension bounded by 1. Assume that variety
Y
has canonical hypersurface singularities of multiplicity 2 (
cf.

[
VdB04
]
). In
[
BB22
]
, we introduced the
null-category

\mathscr{A}_{f}=\{E\in\mathop{\textrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X)\,|\,Rf_{*}(E)=0\}

and proved that under the above assumptions the ‘flop-flop’ equivalence is the
spherical twist
for the spherical functor
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{A}_{f})\to{\mathcal{D}}^{b}(\mathop{\textrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X))
, the derived functor of the embedding
\mathscr{A}_{f}\to\mathop{\textrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
.

In this paper, we relate the null-category to non-commutative deformations.
Theorem
7.13
states that simple objects in
\mathscr{A}_{f}
are
{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{y,i}}(-1)
, where
C_{y,i}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
run over irreducible components of 1-dimensional fibers
C_{y}
for
f
over all closed points of
y
. Fix one closed point
y\in Y
with 1-dimensional fiber
C_{y}
over it and consider the
null-category with support in the fiber

\mathscr{A}_{f,C_{y}}=\{E\in\mathscr{A}_{f}\,|\,\textrm{Supp }E\subset C_{y}\}

Theorem C.

(see Theorem
7.14
)
The null-category
\mathscr{A}_{f,C_{y}}
is an object of
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}
that ind-represents the non-commutative deformation functor of the collection
\{{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{y,i}}(-1)\}
.

If we assume further that
\dim X=\dim Y=3
and
Y
is the spectrum of a noteherian local ring, then any object of
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is supported on the fiber
C
of
f
over the unique closed point of
Y
, i.e.
\mathscr{A}_{f}\simeq\mathscr{A}_{f,C}
. Moreover, under these assumptions the category
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is Deligne finite, hence we have

Corollary

(see Corollary
7.15
)
Let
R
be a noetherian local ring of dimension 3. If
f\colon X\to\mathop{\textrm{Spec}}\nolimits R
is a flopping contraction, then
the category
\mathscr{A}_{f}

represents the non-commutative deformation functor of the
\{{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{i}}(-1)\}
.

When
Y
is 3-dimensional but not local, category
\mathscr{A}_{f}
is the direct sum of categories
\mathscr{A}_{f,C_{y}}
where
y
runs over all closed points in
Y
with 1-dimensional
f
-fibers
C_{y}=\bigcup C_{y,i}
. Thus, category
\mathscr{A}_{f}

represents
the functor of non-commutative deformations of
\{{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{y,i}}(-1)\}
.

Families of quasi-coherent sheaves over an affine base

4.
An abelian category as the base of a non-commutative deformation

We introduce the category
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}
of Hom and
\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}
-finite
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian categories with
n
isomorphism classes of simple objects and its full subcategory

of Deligne finite categories.
We show that

yields an equivalence
\mathop{\textrm{Bas}}\nolimits_{n}\simeq\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}
.
As the central point of categorification in this paper, we reinterpret the functor of non-commutative deformations as a functor
\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
.

4.1.
An anti-equivalence between
\mathop{\textrm{Bas}}\nolimits_{n}
and
\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}

Recall that an object of an abelian category is

if it has no proper subobjects. An object is

if it is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of simple objects. For an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{A}})
the full subcategory of semi-simple objects.

We say that a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category is

if it is
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits
and
\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}
-finite, contains finitely many non-isomorphic simple objects, and any object admits a finite filtration with semi-simple graded factors. An abelian category of finite length is a
Deligne finite category
if it has a projective generator.
By
[
Del90
, Proposition 2.14]
, a Deligne finite category
{\mathcal{A}}
with projective generator
P
is equivalent to the category of finitely generated modules over the (finite dimensional)
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra
\mathop{\textrm{End}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(P)
.

We define the category
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}
. Its objects are pairs
({\mathcal{A}},Q_{\mathcal{A}})
, where
{\mathcal{A}}
is a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category of finite length and

is a fully faithful functor with image

. For
({\mathcal{A}},Q_{{\mathcal{A}}})
,

,

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}}(({\mathcal{A}},Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}),({\mathcal{B}},Q_{{\mathcal{B}}}))=\{(F,\varphi\colon F\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}Q_{B})\,|\,F\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}\textrm{ is exact }\}/\sim

where
(F,\varphi)\sim(F^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
if there exists
\psi\colon F\to F^{\prime}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(\psi\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}})=\varphi
.
One easily checks that the composition in
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}

We often omit

and write
{\mathcal{A}}\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}
.

By
\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}
we denote the full subcategory of
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}
consisting of Deligne finite categories.

Consider functor
\Upsilon\colon\textrm{Bas}_{n}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}

and

as follows:

(20)

\displaystyle\Upsilon(A,q_{A})=(\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits A,{q_{A}}_{*}),

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\alpha)=(\alpha_{*}\colon\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits B\to\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits A,\textrm{can}_{\alpha}\colon\alpha_{*}{q_{B}}_{*}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{q_{A}}_{*}),

Lemma 4.1
.

Functor
\Upsilon
is well-defined.

Proof.

Since
\textrm{rad}A
annihilates any semi-simple
A
-module,
functor
{q_{A}}_{*}
is an equivalence of
\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{\oplus n}
with
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits A)
, i.e.
(\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits A,{q_{A}}_{*})\in\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}
.
For
\alpha\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\textrm{Bas}}\nolimits_{n}}((A,q_{A}),(B,q_{B}))
, functor
\alpha_{*}
is exact and
\textrm{can}_{\alpha}
is an isomorphism.
For homomorphisms
\alpha,\beta\colon A\to B
conjugated by
u\in B^{\times}
,
q_{B}(u)=1
, the map
b\mapsto bu^{-1}
is an isomorphism of
B-A

\psi_{u}\colon B_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{\simeq}B_{\beta}
. Isomorphism (

is the identity on the
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces underlying

{q_{B}}_{*}
. Since the same is true about

and

\textrm{can}_{\alpha}\circ{\psi_{u}}^{-1}{{q_{B}}_{*}}=\textrm{can}_{\beta}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2

Recall that a

The following theorem can be deduced from Gabriel’s description of abelian categories of finite length

First,

Clearly,

5.
Infinitesimal extensions of subcategories

The Gabriel product of two strictly full subcategories of an exact category allows us to define the

-infinitesimal extension

of a subcategory

) we show that
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{k}^{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{A}})
is abelian if

is a full abelian subcategory.

5.1.

Let

be an exact category

]

]

.

For strictly full subcategories
{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}}
in
{\mathcal{Z}}
, the pair
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{A}}\ast{\mathcal{B}})
is

(1)

a kernel pair if
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{B}})
are kernel pairs,

(2)

a cokernel pair if
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a cokernel pair and
({\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{B}})
is a kernel and a cokernel pair.

Proof.

Consider
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
Z\in{\mathcal{A}}\ast{\mathcal{B}}
which fits into a short exact sequence
0\to A\xrightarrow{i}Z\xrightarrow{p}B\to 0
with
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. A morphism

yields a morphism of short exact sequences

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{B}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathop{\textrm{Id}}\nolimits}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{C.\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{pf}

By the snake lemma we get an exact sequence:

0\to\ker f\to\ker pf\to A\to\mathop{\textrm{coker}\,}\nolimits f\to\mathop{\textrm{coker}\,}\nolimits pf\to 0.

Conditions
(1)
imply that
\ker pf\in{\mathcal{C}}
, hence
\ker f=\ker(\ker pf\to A)\in{\mathcal{C}}

Conditions

imply that
\mathop{\textrm{coker}\,}\nolimits pf\in{\mathcal{B}}

and
\mathop{\textrm{coker}\,}\nolimits(\ker pf\to A)\in{\mathcal{A}}

\mathop{\textrm{coker}\,}\nolimits f\in{\mathcal{A}}\ast{\mathcal{B}}

∎

Proposition 5.4
.

The pair
({\mathcal{A}}\ast{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}}\ast{\mathcal{B}})

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be an abelian category. We say that

if

{\mathcal{A}}\ast{\mathcal{B}}

5.3.
Infinitesimal extensions of full abelian subcategories are abelian

Proposition 5.6
.

The
k
-infinitesimal extension
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{k}^{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{A}})
of a full abelian subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}
is abelian.

Proof.

We check that
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{k}^{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{A}})
is closed under kernels and cokernels.

For a morphism
f\colon Z_{1}\to Z_{2}
in
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{k}^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}})
, let
l,n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
Z_{1}\in\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}})
,
Z_{2}\in\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}})
. We check that
\ker f\in\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}})
,
\mathop{\textrm{coker}\,}\nolimits f\in\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}})
, i.e. that
(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}({\mathcal{A}}),\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}({\mathcal{A}}))
is a kernel and a cokernel pair.

First, we show by induction on
n
that
({\mathcal{A}},\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}({\mathcal{A}}))
is a kernel and a cokernel pair. Since
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}
is a full abelian subcategory,
({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{A}})
is a kernel and a cokernel pair. By Lemma
5.1
,
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}({\mathcal{A}})\simeq\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n-1}({\mathcal{A}})\ast{\mathcal{A}}
. Then, Lemma
5.3
and the inductive hypothesis imply that
({\mathcal{A}},\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}({\mathcal{A}}))
is a kernel and a cokernel pair.

Next, for fixed
n
we show by induction on
l
that
(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}({\mathcal{A}}),\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}({\mathcal{A}}))
is a kernel and a cokernel pair. The case
l=0
, i.e.
({\mathcal{A}},\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{n}({\mathcal{A}}))
is discussed above. By Lemma
5.1
,

\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}({\mathcal{A}})\simeq\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l-1}({\mathcal{A}})\ast{\mathcal{A}}
. The statement follows from Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Since an ascending union of abelian subcategories is abelian, Proposition
5.6
implies

Corollary 5.7
.

The extension closure
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}})
of a full abelian subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}
is abelian.

6.
Non-commutative deformations of an exact functor

We introduce the category
\widetilde{\textrm{Fin}}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
of abelian categories which are extension generated by an abelian subcategory equivalent to
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
. Category
\widetilde{\textrm{Fin}}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
has a full subcategory
\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
whose objects are categories equivalent to
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{k}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We define the functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
of non-commutative deformations of a given exact functor
\zeta\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
. We prove that
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
is a full subcategory of the category of ind-objects over
\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
and that
the category
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
ind-represents
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}
if the functor
\zeta
is fully faithful. We give an example of an exact functor
\xi\colon\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{3}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
for which
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\xi}
is not ind-representable.

6.1.
Definition of the deformation functor

For an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
, we denote by
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
the category of abelian categories extension generated by
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
. Objects in
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
are pairs
({\mathcal{A}},Q_{{\mathcal{A}}})
of an abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
and a fully faithful extension generating exact functor
Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{A}}
. For
({\mathcal{A}},Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}),({\mathcal{B}},Q_{{\mathcal{B}}})
in
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}(({\mathcal{A}},Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}),({\mathcal{B}},Q_{{\mathcal{B}}}))=\{(F,\varphi\colon F\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}Q_{{\mathcal{B}}})|F\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}\emph{isexact}\}/\sim

where
(F,\varphi)\sim(F^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
if there exists
\psi\colon F\xrightarrow{\simeq}F^{\prime}
such that
\varphi^{\prime}\circ\psi_{Q_{\mathcal{A}}}=\varphi
. We often omit
Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}
and write
{\mathcal{A}}\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
.

We denote by
\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
the full subcategory of
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
-finite categories
, i.e. those
{\mathcal{A}}\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
for which there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{k}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathcal{A}}
.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}}\subset\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}}
,
\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}\subset\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}}
are full subcategories whose objects are
\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}
-finite categories (see
Theorem
4.6
).

Given an exact functor
\zeta\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
we define a functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
via:

(29)

\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}({\mathcal{A}},Q_{{\mathcal{A}}})=\{(T,\tau\colon T\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\zeta)\,|\,T\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}\textrm{ is exact}\}/\sim

where
(T,\tau)\sim(T^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
if there exists
\psi\colon T\xrightarrow{\simeq}T^{\prime}
such that
\tau^{\prime}\circ\psi_{Q_{\mathcal{A}}}=\tau
.

For
(F,\varphi)\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
, we
define
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}(F,\varphi)\colon\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}({\mathcal{B}})\to\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}({\mathcal{A}})
via:

(30)

\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}(F,\varphi)(T,\tau)=(T\circ F,T\circ F\circ Q_{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{T\varphi}T\circ Q_{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{\tau}\zeta).

6.2.
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
as a subcategory of ind-objects over
\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a category and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\wedge}
the category of functors
{\mathcal{C}}^{\textrm{op}}\to\textrm{Sets}
.
Consider the Yoneda embedding
h\colon{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\wedge}
,
C\mapsto h_{C}(-)\colon=\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{C}}}(-,C)
. An
ind-object
over
{\mathcal{C}}
is an object of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\wedge}
isomorphic to
\varinjlim_{I}h\circ\alpha
, for some functor
\alpha\colon I\to{\mathcal{C}}
with
I
filtrant and small.
We denote by
\textrm{Ind}({\mathcal{C}})
the full subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\wedge}
with ind-objects as objects.

We say that a functor
\Upsilon\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\wedge}
is
ind-representable
if it is isomorphic to an ind-object.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
,
{\mathcal{B}}\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
and
T\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T({\mathcal{A}})\subset\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
and functor
T
factors via the inclusion
i_{l}\colon\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\to{\mathcal{B}}
. Therefore,

\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})=\varinjlim\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})).

Proof.

Any functor
F\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
restricts to a a functor
F_{l}\colon\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\to\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
. Indeed, the isomorphism
F\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\simeq Q_{{\mathcal{B}}}
implies that functor
F
induces an equivalence of the subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
with the subcategory
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{B}}
. Exactness of
F
implies that
F|_{\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})}
is a functor
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\to\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
.

By definition an
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
-finite category
{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
is equivalent to
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
, for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that, for
{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
, a functor
F\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
decomposes as
F\colon{\mathcal{A}}\simeq\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\xrightarrow{F_{l}}\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{l}^{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\xrightarrow{i_{l}}{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

The functor
h\colon\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}\to{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}^{\wedge}
,
h_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-)=\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}(-,{\mathcal{A}})
, for
{\mathcal{A}}\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
, induces an equivalence of
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
with a full subcategory of
\mathop{\textrm{Ind}}\nolimits(\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}})
.

Proof.

Lemma
6.1
implies that, for any
{\mathcal{A}}\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
, functor
h_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-)
is an ind-object over
\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
.
It remains to check that functor
h
is fully faithful.

By
[
KS06
, formula (2.6.4)]
,
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}^{\wedge}}(h_{{\mathcal{A}}},h_{{\mathcal{B}}})\simeq\varprojlim_{i}\varinjlim_{j}\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{i}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0}),\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{j}^{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0}))
.
Lemma
6.1
implies that
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}^{\wedge}}(h_{{\mathcal{A}}},h_{{\mathcal{B}}})\simeq\varprojlim\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{i}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0}),{\mathcal{B}})
. Under this isomorphism, the morphism of Hom-spaces
h_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})\to\varprojlim\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{i}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0}),{\mathcal{B}})
induced by
h
maps
F\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
to the family
\{F|_{\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{i}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})}\in\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{i}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0}),{\mathcal{B}})\}_{i}
.

As any object and any morphism in
{\mathcal{A}}
belongs to the subcategory
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits_{i}^{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
, for some
i
, morphism
h_{{\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}}}
is an isomorphism, i.e. functor
h
is fully faithful.
∎

Analogous arguments show

Theorem 6.3
.

The functor
h\colon\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}\to{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}}^{\wedge}
,
h_{{\mathcal{A}}}(-)=\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}}(-,{\mathcal{A}})
induces an equivalence of

with a full subcategory of
\mathop{\textrm{Ind}}\nolimits(\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n})
.

6.3.
Ind-representability of nc deformations of fully faithful functors

Let
\zeta\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
be a fully faithful exact functor of abelian categories. By Corollary
5.7
,
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
is an abelian category. Denote by
\iota\colon\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})\to{\mathcal{Z}}
the inclusion functor. Then
\zeta
decomposes as
\iota\circ\zeta_{0}
, for
\zeta_{0}\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\to\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
, and
(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0}),\zeta_{0})
is an object of
\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Consider a fully faithful exact functor
\zeta\colon{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
of abelian categories. Then
(\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{{\mathcal{Z}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{0}),\zeta_{0})
ind-represents the functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\iota
induces a morphism of functors
\Phi\colon h_{\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits({\mathcal{A}}_{0})}\to\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}
which maps
(T,\tau\colon T\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\zeta_{0})
in
\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{{\mathcal{A}}_{0}}}({\mathcal{A}},\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits({\mathcal{A}}_{0}))
to
(\iota\circ T,\iota\circ T\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\iota\tau}\iota\circ\zeta_{0}\simeq\zeta)\in\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}({\mathcal{A}})
.

As any exact functor
F\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
such that
F\circ Q_{{\mathcal{A}}}\simeq\zeta
is uniquely decomposed as
\iota\circ\overline{F}
, for
\overline{F}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits({\mathcal{A}}_{0})
, the natural transformation
\Phi
is an isomorphism.
∎

In the case when
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\simeq\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}
and category
{\mathcal{Z}}
is
\mathbbm{k}
-linear, an (exact) functor
\zeta\colon\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
is determined by a
\mathbbm{k}^{n}
-object in
{\mathcal{Z}}
, see Proposition
2.4
, i.e. by an
n
-tuple
\sigma=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
of objects in
{\mathcal{Z}}
. Functor
\zeta
is fully faithful if and only if
\sigma
is a
simple collection
(
cf.

[
Kaw18
]
), i.e. if and only if

\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathop{\textrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{Z}}(Z_{i},Z_{j})=\delta_{ij}.

Theorem
6.4
implies

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\sigma=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
be a simple collection in a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category
{\mathcal{Z}}
and
\zeta\colon\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
an exact functor determined by it. If
\sigma
is a simple collection then
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits_{\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
is ind-represented by the extension closure
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}(\sigma)\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Fin}}\nolimits}_{\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}}
.

The ind-representability of the deformation functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}\colon\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
(
27
) requires the deformed collection
\sigma
to satisfy a finiteness condition:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\sigma=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})
be a simple collection in a
\mathbbm{k}
-linear abelian category
{\mathcal{Z}}
and
\zeta\colon\mathop{\textrm{mod--}}\nolimits\mathbbm{k}^{n}\to{\mathcal{Z}}
an exact functor determined by it. If
\sigma
is a simple collection and
\dim\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\bigoplus_{i}Z_{i},\bigoplus_{i}Z_{i})
is finite, then the category
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\sigma)\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}
ind-represents the functor
\mathop{\textrm{ncDef}}\nolimits_{\zeta}\colon{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits_{n}}^{\mathop{\textrm{op}}\nolimits}\to\mathop{\textrm{Sets}}\nolimits
.

Proof.

Any object in
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}(\sigma)
admits a finite filtration with graded factors in
\sigma
. Hence, if
\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\bigoplus Z_{i},\bigoplus Z_{i})
is finite dimensional then
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}(\sigma)
is
\mathop{\textrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}
-finite, i.e.
\mathop{\mathpzc{Ex}}\nolimits^{\mathcal{Z}}(\sigma)\in\widetilde{\mathop{\textrm{Df}}\nolimits}_{n}
(see Theorem
4.6
). The statement follows from Corollary
6.5
.
∎

6.4.

We give an example of a not fully faithful functor

Consider category

\textstyle{1}

Let

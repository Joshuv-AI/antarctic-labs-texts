Abstract.
In this paper, a general hybrid fixed point theorem for the contractive mappings in generalized Banach spaces is proved via measure of weak non-compactness and it is further applied to fractional integral equations for proving the existence results for the solutions under mixed Lipschitz and weakly sequentially continuous conditions. Finally, an example is given to illustrate the result.

Keywords:
Banach space, weakly compact, Measure of weak
non-compactness, Fixed point theory, Integral equations.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 46E15, 46E40, 47H10, 37C25, 47N20.

This paper is centered around the following question: under which conditions on its entries, the operator equation

Our main results are applied to investigate the existence of solutions for the following coupled system of quadratic integral

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S
be a nonempty, closed, and convex subset of a generalized Banach algebra
X
satisfying the sequential condition
(\mathcal{GP}).
Assume that
A,C:X\longrightarrow X
and
B:S\longrightarrow X
are three weakly sequentially continuous operators satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

A
and
C
are contractive with Lipschitz matrix
M_{A}
and
M_{C}
respectively,

(ii)

A
is regular on
X,

(
i.e.
A
maps
X
into the set of all invertible elements of
X),

(iii)

B(S)
is bounded,

(iv)

x=Ax\cdot By+Cx,\ \ y\in S\Rightarrow x\in S,
and

(v)

\left(\displaystyle\dfrac{I-C}{A}\right)^{-1}
is weakly compact.

Then, the operator equation
(
1.1
)
has, at least, a solution in
S
as soon as
\|B(S)\|M_{A}+M_{C}\in\mathcal{M}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is a matrix converging to zero.
\diamondsuit

Remark 3.1
.

If
M_{A}=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n},\ \ M_{C}=(\overline{a}_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
and
\|B(S)\|=(b_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
then

\|B(S)\|M_{A}+M_{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}b_{1}a_{11}+\overline{a}_{11}&\ldots&b_{n}a_{1n}+\overline{a}_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b_{1}a_{n1}+\overline{a}_{n1}&\ldots&b_{n}a_{nn}+\overline{a}_{nn},\\
\end{array}\right).

\diamondsuit

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.
It is easy to check that the vector

is a solution for the operator equation

if and only
if
x
is a fixed point for the inverse operator

The use of assumption
(i)
as well as perov’s theorem
[

]
leads to for each

there is a unique

x_{y}=Ty.
Indeed,
let

\varphi_{y}(x)=Ax\cdot By+Cx.

Notice that

Applying Perov’s theorem
[

]
, we obtain that

say

From assumption

it follows that the operator

is well defined on

Let

be a weakly convergent sequence of
S
to a point

Then the relation

holds and, therefore

in view of assumption

Since

Consequently

Now a standard argument shows that

Suppose the contrary, then there exists a weakly neighborhood

in view of Theorem

.

Claim 1:
We start by showing that the operators
A,C:C(J,X)\times C(J,X)\longrightarrow C(J,X)\times C(J,X)
and
B:S\longrightarrow C(J,X)\times C(J,X)

is continuous on
J
for all
(x,y)\in C(J,X)\times C(J,X).
To see this, let
\{t_{n},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be any
sequence in J converging to a point

in
J.
Then,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|A_{i}(x,y)(t_{n})-A_{i}(x,y)(t)\|&=&\|f_{i}(t_{n},x(t_{n}),y(t_{n}))-f_{i}(t,x(t),y(t))\|\\
\\
&\leq&a_{i1}\|x(t_{n})-x(t)\|+a_{i2}\|y(t_{n})-y(t)\|\\
\\
&+&\|f_{i}(t_{n},x(t),y(t)-f_{i}(t,x(t),y(t)\|.\end{array}

The continuity of
x,y
and
t\mapsto f_{i}(t,x,y)
on
[0,1]

implies that the function
A_{i}(x,y)
is continuous.
Let
\{(x_{n},y_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a weakly convergent sequence of
C(J,X)\times C(J,X)
to a point
(x,y).
In this case, the set
\{(x_{n},y_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}\}

is bounded and so, we can apply the Dobrakov’s theorem
[
12
]
in order to get

(x_{n}(t),y_{n}(t))\rightharpoonup(x(t),y(t))\text{ in }X\times X.

Based on assumption
(\mathcal{H}_{0})(d),
it is shown that
A_{i}(x_{n},y_{n})(t)\rightharpoonup A_{i}(x,y)(t)
and then, we can again apply the Dobrakov’s theorem to obtain the weak sequential continuity of the operator
A.
Besides, the use of assumption
(\mathcal{H}_{1})(b)
and assumption
(\mathcal{H}_{2})(b)
as well as the Dobrakov’s theorem
[
12
, page 36]
leads to the two maps
B
and
C
are weakly sequentially continuous.

Claim 2:
The operators
A
and
C
are contractive. The claim regarding the operator
A
is
immediate, from assumption
(\mathcal{H}_{0})(e).
Let us fix arbitrary
(x,y),(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in C(J,X)\times C(J,X).
If we

then we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|C_{i}(x,y)(t)-C_{i}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})(t)\|&=&\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}I^{\beta_{i}^{k}}h_{i}^{k}(t,x(t),y(t)-\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}I^{\beta_{i}^{k}}h_{i}^{k}(t,\tilde{x}(t),\tilde{y}(t))\right\|\\

This implies that

Claim 3:
Let

From the Hahn-Banach theorem there exists a linear function

\left\|B_{i}(x,y)(t^{\prime})-B_{i}(x,y)(t)\right\|=\phi\left(B_{i}(x,y)(t)-B_{i}(x,y)(t)\right),\ \ i=1,2.

Based on the first inequality in (
4.1

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left\|B_{i}(x,y)(t^{\prime})-B_{i}(x,y)(t)\right\|&=&\phi\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{j})}g_{i}(s,x(s),y(s))ds-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}g_{i}(s,x(s),y(s))\right)\\

\\
&+&\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}\phi(g_{i}(s,x(s),y(s)))ds\\

&\leq&P\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}-(t-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}|}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}ds+P\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\frac{(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}ds.\end{array}

This implies that

\left\|B_{i}(x,y)(t^{\prime})-B_{i}(x,y)(t)\right\|\to 0\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,
and consequently

is a weakly equi-continuous subset.
Let now

be any sequence in
S.
From the first inequality in (
4.1
), it follows that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left\|B_{i}(t,x_{n}(t),y_{n}(t))\right\|&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha_{i}-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i})}\left\|g_{i}(s,x_{n}(s),y_{n}(s))\right\|ds\\
\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{PT^{\alpha_{i}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)},\end{array}

for all

This demonstrate that
\{B_{i}(x_{n},y_{n}),\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a uniformly bounded sequence in

and so,

is sequentially relatively weakly compact. Hence,

[

[

Claim 4:

For all

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|x(t)\|&\leq&\left\|f_{1}(t,x(t),y(t))\right\|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\displaystyle\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha_{1}-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1})}\left\|g_{1}(s,u(s),v(s))\right\|ds+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}I^{\beta_{1}^{i}}\|h_{1}^{i}(t,x(t),y(t))\|\\

&\leq&\left[\rho(\|x\|_{\infty}+\|y\|_{\infty})+F_{0}\right]\displaystyle\frac{PT^{\alpha_{1}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+1)}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{T^{\beta_{1}^{k}}}{\Gamma(\beta_{1}^{k}+1)}\left[\rho(\|x\|_{\infty}+\|y\|_{\infty})+H_{0}\right]\end{array}

This implies that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|x(t)\|+\|y(t)\|&\leq&\big[\rho(\|x\|_{\infty}+\|y\|_{\infty})+F_{0}\big]P\left(\frac{T^{\alpha_{1}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+1)}+\frac{T^{\alpha_{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{2}+1)}\right)\\

Consequently,

\|x\|_{\infty}\leq r_{0}\text{ and }\|y\|_{\infty}\leq r_{0}.

To end the proof, we apply Theorem

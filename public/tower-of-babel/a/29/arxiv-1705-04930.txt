The homotopy category of flat functors
Esmaeil Hosseini and Ali Zaghian

Abstract

1.
Introduction

Functor categories are used as a potent tool for solving some
important problems in representation theory. They are introduced by
Maurice Auslander in
[
A66
]
and used in his proof of the first
Brauer-Thrall conjecture (see
[
A78
]
,
[
AR74
]
,
[
AR72
]
).

In this work, we assume that
\mathcal{C}
is a small category,
(\mathcal{G},\textmd{-}\otimes\textmd{-})
is a closed symmetric tensor
Grothendieck category and
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{G})
is the
category of all covariant functors from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{G}
. The homotopy
category of
\mathcal{A}
has some interesting triangulated subcategories.
One of them is the homotopy category
{\mathbb{K}({{\rm{Flat}}}\mathcal{A})}
of flat functors which
is studied by Amnon Neeman in
[
Ne08
]
and
[
Ne10
]
. He
invented that, when
\mathcal{C}
is a category with a single object and

\mathcal{G}
is the category of modules over a ring
R
with
1\neq 0
, the
homotopy category
{\mathbb{K}({{\rm{Proj}}}R)}
of projective modules can be replaced by a
quotient of
{\mathbb{K}({{\rm{Flat}}}\mathcal{A})}
modulo its thick subcategory consisting of all
pure acyclic complexes. This quotient is called the pure derived
category of flat
R
-modules and denoted by
{\mathbb{D}_{\rm{pur}}({{\rm{Flat}}}R)}
(see

[
Mu07
]
,
[
AS12
]
,
[
HS13
]
,
[
MS11
]
for more
details). This is an important future of Neeman’s work, because,
there are some closed symmetric tensor Grothendieck categories with
no non-zero projective object (see
[
Ha97
, III. Ex. 6.2]
).

We know that there are different methods to define flatness in

\mathcal{G}
. One of them is defined by the categorical purity and the
other one is defined by the tensor purity. But, categorical flats
are trivial in some situations. For example, if

X=\mathbb{P}^{n}(R)=\mathrm{Proj}(R[x_{1},...,x_{n}])
is the projective

n
-space over a commutative ring
R
, then the category

\mathfrak{Qco}X
of all quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is a closed
symmetric tensor Grothendieck category. By
[
ES15
, Corollary
4.6]
,
\mathfrak{Qco}X
does not have
non-zero categorical flat objects. This shows that
categorical flats are rare in Fun
(\mathcal{C},\mathfrak{Qco}X)
. Due to
this lack of data the study needed to employ another way to define
flatness. This is motivated us to define another notion of flatness
in
\mathcal{A}
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we define our
concept of flatness in
\mathcal{A}
and show that any object in
\mathcal{A}
has a
flat precover and a cotorsion preenvelope. In Section 3, we show
that the category of all complexes in
\mathcal{A}
admits flat covers and
cotorsion envelopes. In Section 4, we prove that the pure derived
category of flat functors can be replaced by the homotopy category
of dg-cotorsion complexes of flat functors.

Before starting, let us fix some notations and definitions.

Category of complexes.

Let
\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{A})

(complexes

Let

1.2
.

Cotorsion theories.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a Grothendieck category. The
right

of a class
\mathbb{S}
in
\mathbb{A}
is

\mathbb{S}^{\perp}:=\{\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{A}\ |\ {\rm{Ext}}_{\mathbb{A}}^{1}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{B})=0,\ {\rm{for\ all}}\ \mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{S}\}.

The left orthogonal

is defined dually. The pair

of classes in
\mathbb{A}
is called a

if
\mathbb{X}^{\perp}=\mathbb{Y}

and
\mathbb{X}={}^{\perp}\mathbb{Y}
. An object

\mathbf{X}^{\prime}\in{}^{\perp}\mathbb{Y}
and
\mathbf{Y}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Y}
. The

special

\mathbb{X}
-
precover
is defined dually. A
cotorsion theory
(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
in
\mathbb{A}
is
called

if any object in
\mathbb{A}
has a special

\mathbb{X}
-precover and a special
\mathbb{Y}
-preenvelope.

Flatness in functor categories

Let

The category

Remark 2.2
.

It is a well known fact in positive logic that the partial positive type of an element is not maximal in general. Similarly, in the local logic context,
{\mathrm{ltp}}^{M}_{+}(a)
is not maximal. Indeed, it suffices to find a homomorphism

while

{\mathrm{ltp}}^{M}_{+}(a)\subsetneq{\mathrm{ltp}}^{N}_{+}(h(a))

Let

Suppose

. As
Lemma

2.11
below shows, this notion is the right replacement of finite satisfiability in local logic. In particular, boundedness is the necessary condition to apply compactness in local logic.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
x=\{x_{i}\}_{i\in N}
be a variable. Let
I
be the subset of indexes
i\in N
such that there is a constant symbol on the sort of
x_{i}
and
J
the set of pairs of indexes
(i,j)\in N\times N
such that
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are on the same sort. A
bound
of
x
in
{\mathtt{T}}
is a partial atomic type of the form

\mathrm{B}(x)\coloneqq\bigwedge_{i\in I}{\mathrm{d}}_{i}(x_{i},c_{i})\wedge\bigwedge_{(i,j)\in J}{\mathrm{d}}_{i,j}(x_{i},x_{j})

where
c_{i}
is a constant on the sort of
x_{i}
for
i\in I
,
{\mathrm{d}}_{i}
is a locality relation on the sort of
x_{i}
for
i\in I
and
{\mathrm{d}}_{i,j}
is a locality relation on the common sort of
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
for
(i,j)\in J
.

As a relevant example, we point out the following special case. We say that a variable
x
is
duplicate free
if
x
has no duplicated sorts (counting constants), i.e. it contains no single variable with the same sort as a constant or another single variable in
x
. For instance, a variable is duplicate free and pointed if and only if it is
minimal pointed
(pointed without proper pointed subvariables). When
x
is duplicate free, the only bound of
x
is
\mathrel{\top}
.

Definition 2.9
.

A subset of formulas
\Gamma(x)\subseteq{\mathrm{For}}^{x}({\mathtt{L}})
is
boundedly satisfiable
in
{\mathtt{T}}
if there is a bound
\mathrm{B}(x)
such that
\Gamma(x)\wedge\mathrm{B}(x)
is finitely locally satisfiable in
{\mathtt{T}}
. In that case, we say that
\mathrm{B}
is a
feasible bound
for
\Gamma
.

Remark 2.10
.

When
\Gamma(x)\models_{\mathtt{L}}\mathrm{B}(x)
for some bound
\mathrm{B}(x)
, we have that
\Gamma(x)
is boundedly satisfiable if and only if it is finitely locally satisfiable. In particular, when
x
is duplicate free, as bounds on
x
are trivial, we get that
\Gamma(x)
is boundedly satisfiable if and only if it is finitely locally satisfiable.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a subset of
\Pi_{1}
-local formulas of
{\mathtt{L}}
. Then,
\Gamma
is locally satisfiable in
{\mathtt{T}}
if and only if it is boundedly satisfiable in
{\mathtt{T}}
.

Proof.

Obviously, if
\Gamma
is locally satisfiable, then it is boundedly satisfiable as any
a
realising
\Gamma
gives us a feasible bound for
\Gamma
. On the other hand, suppose that
\Gamma(x)
is boundedly satisfiable. Pick a feasible bound
\mathrm{B}(x)
for
\Gamma(x)
. Let
{\mathtt{L}}(\underline{x})
be the expansion of
{\mathtt{L}}
given by adding new constant symbols for each variable in
x
: we take
\mathtt{B}_{\underline{x}_{i},c_{i}}
and
\mathtt{B}_{\,\underline{x}_{i},\underline{x}_{j}}
according to
\mathrm{B}
— for any other constant
c^{\prime}
in
{\mathtt{L}}(\underline{x})
, we define
\mathtt{B}_{\underline{x}_{i},c^{\prime}}
according to the structure of
\mathtt{D}
. Now, consider
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}={\mathtt{T}}\wedge\Gamma(\underline{x})
. By hypothesis,
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}
is finitely locally satisfiable. Thus, by
[
RFS24
, Theorem 1.9]
, there is a local
{\mathtt{L}}(\underline{x})
-structure
M_{\underline{x}}
satisfying
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}
. Hence, the respective
{\mathtt{L}}
-reduct

is a local model of
{\mathtt{T}}
and the interpretation of
\underline{x}
realises
\Gamma
.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq{\mathrm{LFor}}^{x}_{+}({\mathtt{L}})
be a partial lp-typeof
{\mathtt{T}}
. Then, there is a lp-typeextending
\Gamma
.

Proof.

As
\Gamma
is boundedly satisfiable, pick any feasible bound
\mathrm{B}(x)
. Take
\Gamma^{\prime}(x)=\Gamma(x)\wedge\mathrm{B}(x)
. Now, consider
\mathcal{K}=\{\Sigma\subseteq{\mathrm{LFor}}^{x}_{+}({\mathtt{L}}):\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma
finitely locally satisfiable in
{\mathtt{T}}\}
, which is partially ordered by
\subseteq
. For any chain
\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{K}
we have that
\bigcup\Omega
is an upper bound for
\Omega
in
\mathcal{K}
. Indeed, for any
\Lambda\subseteq\bigcup\Omega
finite, there is
\Sigma\in\Omega
such that
\Lambda\subseteq\Sigma
, so
\bigcup\Omega
is finitely locally satisfiable, concluding
\bigcup\Omega\in\mathcal{K}
. By Zorn’s Lemma, there is a maximal element
p\in\mathcal{K}
. We know that
\Gamma\subseteq p
and
p
is boundedly satisfiable. On the other hand, if
p\subseteq\Sigma
with
\Sigma
boundedly satisfiable, then
\Sigma\in\mathcal{K}
and, by maximality of
p
,
\Sigma=p
. Hence,
p
is a lp-typeextending
\Gamma
.
∎

In general, we consider only partial lp-types, as local compactness
[
RFS24
, Theorem 1.9]
applies to this kind of formulas. However, in the case of pointed variables, we can handle positive types by means of the following result.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
M
be a local
{\mathtt{L}}
-structure. Let

be a pointed variable and

. Then,
{\mathrm{ltp}}^{M}_{+}(a)\subseteq{\mathrm{ltp}}^{M}_{+}(b)
if and only if
{\mathrm{tp}}^{M}_{+}(a)\subseteq{\mathrm{tp}}^{M}_{+}(b)
.

Proof.

Obviously,
{\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(a)\subseteq{\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(b)
implies
{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a)\subseteq{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b)
. Conversely, assume
{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a)\subseteq{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b)
. Take

. Without loss of generality,
\varphi(x)=\exists y\mathrel{}\phi(x,y)
with

. Then, there is
c
such that
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}\phi(a,c)
. As

is pointed, we can find a term

on the sort of

. As
M
is a local structure, there is a locality relation
{\mathrm{d}}
with

where
\varphi^{\prime}(x)=\exists y\in{\mathrm{d}}(t(x))\mathrel{}\phi(x,y)\in{\mathrm{LFor}}^{x}_{+}({\mathtt{L}})
. As

, we conclude that

{\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(a)\subseteq{\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(b)
.
∎

It should be noted that, contrary to what happens in usual positive logic, lp-typesmight not be preserved under adding parameters. Explicitly, a partial lp-typeof

over

is not generally a bound over

. For instance, consider
[

{\mathtt{L}}\coloneqq\{P_{n},Q_{n},{\mathrm{d}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and

. Such a counterexample cannot be given when

Let

. Then, every partial lp-typeof

be a partial lp-typeof

of

for

It is natural to wonder if we can relax the pointedness hypothesis in the previous lemma. The following result shows that, in lpcmodels, the LJCPsuffices. Furthermore, in some sense, the LJCPis equivalent to preservation of types.

Lemma 2.18

Let

be a local
{\mathtt{L}}
-structure,

be subsets of

and

is a partial lp-typeof

By adding parameters to the language, we may assume that

is empty.

Suppose first that

has the LJCPover

. Let
\Gamma(x)
be a partial lp-typeof

over

is a positive embedding as

is a lpcstructure. On the other hand, consider the
{\mathtt{L}}(B)

of

given by

for

. As

is a positive embedding,

.

Now, assume that

has the LJCPover

and every partial lp-typeon a minimal pointed variable of

is a partial lp-typeof
M
over

. Let

and pick

minimal pointed in

. Then,

is a lp-typeon a minimal pointed variable of

over

. By hypothesis,

is a partial lp-typeof

over

{\mathtt{L}}(B)

of

is a positive embedding. By maximality, as

is a lp-typeover
\emptyset

.

Consider the language
{\mathtt{L}}
with two sorts
\mathtt{S}=\{1,2\}
, locality relations
\mathtt{D}^{s}=\{{\mathrm{d}}^{s}_{q}:q\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\}

of sort

and

, where

is the zero of sort

has the LJCPover

and

Now, let

be an

is finitely satisfiable, we find

of sort

in

{\mathtt{L}}

Take

Let

of

In positive logic, a key notion generalizing Hausdorffness is semi-Hausdorffness. The definition of semi-Hausdorffness depends on the fact that pull-backs by substitutions of variables are well-defined continuous maps between the spaces of types. Unfortunately, in local positive logic, for non pointed variables, the pull-back of a lp-typeby a substitution of variables is not necessarily maximal, i.e. it may be only a partial lp-type. Therefore, for non pointed variables, pull-backs by substitutions of variables are not necessarily well-defined maps between spaces of types. In particular, the definition of semi-Hausdorffness only makes sense for pointed variables.

Lemma 2.28
.

Let

and

be pointed variables and
\zeta
a substitution of

(1)

\zeta^{*}\coloneqq{\mathrm{Im}}^{-1}\zeta_{*}\colon\mathrm{S}^{y}({\mathtt{T}})\to\mathrm{S}^{x}({\mathtt{T}})

Pick

\mathrm{S}^{x}({\mathtt{T}})

Theorem 3.6

One useful consequence of saturation is homogeneity. In local positive logic, we have the following version of homogeneity for pointed tuples.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
M\models^{\mathrm{pc}}_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}
be
\kappa
-saturated and
a
and
a^{\prime}
pointed tuples in

with

such that
{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a)={\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a^{\prime})

. Then, there is
b^{\prime}
such that
{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(ab)={\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a^{\prime}b^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Say
a,a^{\prime}\in M^{y}
. Consider
p(x)\coloneqq\{\varphi(x,a^{\prime}):\varphi(x,a)\in{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b/a)\}
. We claim that
p(x)
is a lp-typeover
a^{\prime}
. As
{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b/a)
contains a bound over
a
,
p(x)
also contains a bound over
a^{\prime}
. Obviously,
p(x)
is closed under finite conjunctions. For any
\varphi(x,a)\in{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b/a)
, we have that
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x,y)\in{\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(a)
. By
Lemma

2.13
, as
{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a)={\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a^{\prime})
and
a
and
a^{\prime}
are pointed, we conclude that
{\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(a)={\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(a^{\prime})
, so
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x,y)\in{\mathrm{tp}}_{+}(a^{\prime})
. Consequently,
p(x)
is finitely satisfiable. Hence, by
Lemma

2.11
,
p(x)
is a partial lp-typeover
a^{\prime}
. Finally, if
\varphi(x,a^{\prime})\notin p(x)
, then
\varphi(x,a)\notin{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b/a)
, so
M\not\models_{\mathtt{L}}\varphi(b,a)
. By
[
RFS24
, Lemma 2.12]
, there is a lp-formula
\psi(x,y)
with

such that

. Thus,
\psi(x,a)\in{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b/a)
, so

with

. As
\varphi(x,a^{\prime})
is arbitrary, we conclude that

is a lp-typeover

by
Lemma

2.16
.

Since

is
\kappa
-saturated, by
3.4
, there is
b^{\prime}
realising

, so

. Hence, for any lp-formula

, we have that

if and only if
\varphi(x,a^{\prime})\in p(x)
, if and only if
\varphi(x,a)\in{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(b/a)

. In conclusion,
{\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(ab)={\mathrm{ltp}}_{+}(a^{\prime}b^{\prime})
.
∎

In addition to saturation, it is often desirable to ensure the existence of enough automorphisms; that is strong homogeneity. In model theory, it is customary to work with saturated and strongly homogeneous (for a sufficiently big cardinal) structures; these structures are often referred to as
monster
models. By adopting the methodology for constructing monster models in classic first-order logic, we can construct corresponding monster models in local positive logic. We provide here a sketch of the proof.

Definition 3.9
.

Without the LJCP, retractors are not necessarily unique. However, we can still classify them in a natural way, getting also a bound on the number of retractors. In
[

]
, Hrushovski suggested a way of doing so; he suggested considering all the possible types and when they are compatible. We provide here a rephrasing of that idea that seems to work better in the many sorted case.

Definition 3.17
.

We say that two local models

and
B
of
{\mathtt{T}}
are
(locally) compatible in
{\mathtt{T}}
if there are homomorphisms

and

to a common local model

. As usual, we omit

if it is clear from the context.

.

Let
M\models^{\mathrm{pc}}_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}
. Then, for any

,

. In particular,

is an equivalence relation in the class

and

for every

.

Proof.

As
A_{1}\frown M
and
A_{2}\frown M
. There are then homomorphisms
f_{1}\colon A_{1}\to B_{1}
,
f_{2}\colon M\to B_{1}
,
g_{1}\colon M\to B_{2}
and
g_{2}\colon A_{2}\to B_{2}
with

. As

, we have that

and
g_{1}
are positive embeddings, so

. As

is pointed, by
[

, Theorem 3.5]

{\mathtt{L}}(M)
-homomorphisms

and

to a common local model

. Therefore,

.

∎

Unfortunately, this is the only connection that holds — a theory with local retractors does not need to have a non-local universal model.

Example 3.44
.

Consider the one-sorted local language
{\mathtt{L}}=\{0,1,+,\cdot\}
with locality relations
\mathtt{D}=\{{\mathrm{d}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(with the usual ordered monoid structure induced by the natural numbers), where
0,1
are constants and
+,\cdot
are ternary relations.

Consider the structure
\mathbb{R}
with the usual field interpretation (
+,\cdot
are the graphs of addition and multiplication respectively) and metric interpretation of the locality relations. By
Theorem

3.26
,
\mathbb{R}
is local universal for
{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathbb{R}}={\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(\mathbb{R})
. Since every field endomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
is the identity, it is in particular an automorphism thus
\mathbb{R}
is a retractor by
Lemma

3.22
. Since
\mathbb{R}
is local universal, it is the unique retractor (up to isomorphism).

However, as a negative theory,
{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathbb{R}}
is unbounded. Indeed, we have the positive formula
x<y
, which is expressed by
\exists zw\mathrel{}(z\cdot w=1\wedge y=x+z^{2})
. Now,
x<y
denials equality in
{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since
\mathbb{R}
has infinite ordered sequences, by compactness, there is a positively closed model of
{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathbb{R}}
of any cardinality.

More generally, in fields we can use the formula
\exists zw\mathrel{}(z\cdot w=1\wedge y=x+z)
, which positively defines inequality in every field. Hence, by compactness, for any infinite field
K
, the negative theory
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(K)
must have positively closed models of arbitrarily large cardinality. Thus, for instance, we can consider
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Indeed:

The
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
as
{\mathtt{L}}
-structure where the locality predicates are interpreted according to the
p
-adic metric is again local universal in their own theory
{\mathtt{T}}_{p}={\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by
Theorem

3.26
. Since every field endomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is also the identity,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the unique retractor of
{\mathtt{T}}_{p}
by
Lemma

3.22
. However, it has arbitrarily large (non-local) positively closed models as inequality is positively definable.

Similarly, we can consider
\mathbb{C}
as
{\mathtt{L}}
-structure and observe that, again,
{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(\mathbb{C})
has arbitrarily large (non-local) positively closed models, and
\mathbb{C}
is local universal
for
{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
by
Theorem

3.26
. If
h\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is an
{\mathtt{L}}
-endomorphism, it fixes
\mathbb{Q}
(since it is in particular a field endomorphism). For any
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, we have that
\mathbb{C}\models{\mathrm{d}}_{m}(na,nb)
if and only if
\|a-b\|\leq\nicefrac{{m}}{{n}}
. Consequently,
h
is continuous. Since
\mathbb{R}
is the closure of
\mathbb{Q}
,

setwise fixes
\mathbb{R}
, so

is an endomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
, so it pointwise fixes
\mathbb{R}
. Thus, it is either the identity or complex conjugation, and in any case an automorphism. We thus get here as well that
\mathbb{C}
is the unique retractor for
{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathbb{C}}
by

3.22
.

If there is a non-local universal positively closed model

, one might expect that the local retractors are precisely the maximal local substructures of

(i.e. the local components containing the constants). This is the case for instance in

Consider the one-sorted local language
{\mathtt{L}}

Theorem 3.49

The following hold:

(1)

{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathfrak{C})
is a closed subspace of the set of all function on
\mathfrak{C}

(2)

(3)

{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathfrak{C})

\mathrm{T}_{1}

Every ball of

(5)

The inversion map

Proof.

Note that the set of endomorphisms of

(2)

\varphi

.

Say

and

are the parameters of
\varphi
and
\psi

\sigma(\varphi(\mathfrak{C},c))\subseteq\psi(\mathfrak{C},c)
if and only if

\mathfrak{C}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\psi(\sigma(a),c)
. Now, since

\mathfrak{C}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\psi(a,\sigma^{-1}(c))

\displaystyle\{\sigma:\sigma(\varphi(\mathfrak{C},c))\subseteq\psi(\mathfrak{C},c)\}=

\displaystyle\{\sigma:\mathfrak{C}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\phi(bc,\sigma^{-1}(c))\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\sigma:\mathfrak{C}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\phi(\sigma(bc),c)\}=\mathrm{ev}^{-1}_{bc}(\phi(\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{C},c)).

If there is an entourage on

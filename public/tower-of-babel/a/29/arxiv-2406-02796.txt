Øyvind S. Auestad

Abstract
. We obtain rates of convergence of numerical approximations of abstract linear parabolic evolution equations in Banach spaces. Our estimates extend known results from the literature of finite element approximations of parabolic equations to more general equations and numerical approximation methods. As an example, we consider parabolic equations on surfaces and surface finite element approximations.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
35A24, 35A25, 35K15, 35K05, 65L60.

1.
introduction

We derive semidiscrete and fully discrete error estimates of numerical approximations of abstract linear evolution equations,

(1)

\displaystyle\dot{u}=-Au,\quad u(0)=x\in X,

where
X
is a Banach space and
-A
is the generator of an analytic semigroup on
X
. The main result of this paper is Theorem
2.2
, which is an abstract semidiscrete error estimate that generalize established estimates from the literature of finite element approximations of parabolic equations. In particular, when
A
is a second order elliptic operator and
X=L^{2}(\Omega)
for some regular domain
\Omega
, one can show that under appropriate conditions there is
\lambda\geq 0
and
C,\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{h}(t)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\theta/2+\rho/2}h^{\theta}\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/2}x\|_{X},\quad\theta\in[0,2],\ \rho\in[-1,\theta]\cap[-2+\theta,\theta],

where
0<h<1
and
u_{h}
is the ordinary piecewise linear Galerkin approximation of
u
. In the case of
A
selfadjoint, the non-smooth data estimate
\theta=2,\rho=0
and the smooth data estimate
\theta=2,\rho=2
follows by the results of
[
9
]
. In the more general case of
A
not necessarily selfadjoint,
[
7
]
showed the non-smooth data estimate, smooth data estimates with
\theta=1,\rho=1
and
\theta=2,\rho=1
, in addition to similar estimates in the
H^{1}(\Omega)
-norm, while
[
13
]
showed the smooth data estimate
\theta=2,\rho=2
. Finally, non-smooth data estimates with
\rho\leq 0
were shown in the case of
A
selfadjoint in
[
12
]
.

We extend these error estimates to a broader class of equations and numerical methods. First, in Theorem
2.2
, which holds for evolution equations in Banach spaces where
-A
generates an analytic semigroup, and its numerical approximation is only required to be uniformly sectorial (see Assumption
2.1
for precise conditions). Then, in Lemma
2.15
and
2.18
, which under similar conditions covers semidiscrete error estimates in norms related to fractional powers of the (possibly shifted) operator
A
, and finally, in Lemma
2.21
which is an error estimate accommodating less regular data than Theorem
2.2
. As an example, we show in Corollary
3.10.1
that this covers equations involving elliptic operators on surfaces, and the surface finite element approximation proposed in
[
5
]
. A key component of all proofs are the interpolation inequalities of Lemma
2.5
, which includes a Heinz–Kato type inequality for operators on Banach spaces.

Further, we derive similar fully discrete error estimates by combining our semidiscerete approximation with a backward Euler approximation in time, and Theorem
2.14
and
2.24
describes rates of convergence in this case. In particular, the inequalities of Lemma
2.7
,
2.8
and
2.9
extend Theorem 9.1, 9.2 and 9.3 in
[
16
]
to equations involving operators that are not necessarily strictly positive, and contains an additional factor ensuring exponential decay in time whenever
A
is strictly positive. In Corollary
3.10.2
we show that this covers elliptic operators on surfaces, and surface finite element approximations combined with backward Euler.

The motivation for studying parameterized estimates of the form of Theorem
2.2
,
2.14
and
2.24
—where the user can trade off regularity of the initial condition against either a singularity at
t=0
or rate of convergence—is their importance in deriving optimal rates of convergence of numerical approximations of stochastic evolution equations. All results mentioned in this introduction turn out to be essential components of the error analysis in
[
2
]
and
[
1
]
.

2.
Semidiscrete and fully discerete error estimates

Throughout the paper we use the following notation: for a Banach space
X
, we denote by
L(X)
the Banach space of bounded linear operators on
X
with its usual norm, while for a linear operator
A
, we denote its spectrum by
\sigma(A)
. We also denote by
C
a generic constant, which may change from line to line. Parameter dependence of
C
is omitted if not relevant. We will frequently (e.g. in Lemma
2.5
, condition
(A8):
and
(A9):
) and without further explanation consider operators
B\in L(X)
, with the property that
BA\in L(X)
for some densely defined and possibly unbounded linear
A:D(A)\to X
, with a bounded inverse. By this we understand that
B
extends to a bounded linear operator
D(A^{-1})\to X
, where we understand
D(A^{-1})
as the completion of
X
using the
\|A^{-1}\cdot\|_{X}
-norm.

2.1.
Semidiscrete error estimate

In this section we study (
1
) and its semidiscrete numerical approximation,

(2)

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{h}=-A_{h}u_{h},\quad u_{h}(0)=\pi_{h}x\in X_{h}\subseteq X,

under the conditions of Assumption
2.1
.

Assumption 2.1
.

(A1):

X
is a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, and
X_{h}\subseteq X,\ 0<h<1
, are closed linear spaces.

(A2):

\pi_{h}:X\to X_{h}
is bounded and linear,
\|\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}
is independent of
h
, and
\pi_{h}x=x
for any
x\in X_{h}
.

(A3):

A:D(A)\subseteq X\to X
is a closed and densely defined linear operator, and it is sectorial in the sense that: there is
\lambda\geq 0
, such that

\displaystyle\sigma(\lambda+A)\subseteq\Sigma_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},\ |\mathrm{arg}(z)|<\delta\},

for some
\delta\in(0,\pi/2)
, and for some
M>0
,

\displaystyle\|(z-\lambda-A)^{-1}x\|_{X}\leq\frac{M}{|z|}\|x\|_{X},\quad\text{for any }x\in X\text{ and any }z\notin\Sigma_{\delta}.

(A4):

A_{h}:X_{h}\to X_{h}
are linear operators, and are sectorial (uniformly in
h
), in the sense that: for the same
\lambda,\delta,M
, as in
(A3):
, and for any
0<h<1
,

\displaystyle\sigma(\lambda+A_{h})\subseteq\Sigma_{\delta}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|(z-\lambda-A_{h})^{-1}x\|_{X}\leq\frac{M}{|z|}\|x\|_{X},\quad\text{for any }x\in X_{h}\text{ and any }z\notin\Sigma_{\delta}.

(A5):

For some
r>0
, the following estimate holds,

\displaystyle\|((\lambda+A)^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{h})^{-1}\pi_{h})x\|_{X}\leq Ch^{r}\|x\|_{X},\quad x\in X.

-A
and
-A_{h}
defined as in
(A3):
and
(A4):
are the generators of analytic semigroups on
X
and
X_{h}
, respectively. In what follows these will be denoted by
S(\cdot)
and
S_{h}(\cdot)
. For any
t\geq 0
we define,

\displaystyle u(t):=S(t)x,\quad u_{h}(t):=S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x,

as the solutions to (
1
) and (
2
) respectively. It is well known that with
u,u_{h}
above, (
1
) and (
2
) holds in the sense of the limit in the time derivative being taken in the norm topology on
X
. The stronger result holds,

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{S(t+\Delta t)-S(t)}{\Delta t}=-AS(t),

with the limit being taken in the uniform operator topology, since
S(\cdot):\Sigma_{\pi/2-\delta}\to L(X)
, defined in (
3
), is analytic (see e.g. Section 2.5.1 in
[
17
]
).

The condition
(A5):
is interpreted as the error in the numerical approximation of the corresponding stationary problem
(\lambda+A)u=x
, where the numerical approximation is given by
(\lambda+A_{h})u_{h}=\pi_{h}x
. When we can associate to
A
a sesquilinear form,
a:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
, where
V,H
are Hilbert spaces with
V\subseteq H
densely and continuously (see Lemma
3.1
), we think of
\lambda\geq 0
as some constant ensuring that “the shifted sesquilinear form”,
a(\cdot,\cdot)+\lambda(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
, is coercive on
V
. Elliptic operators with Gårding’s inequality is the canonical example. More generally, one can consider elliptic operators on surfaces with a Ladyzhenskaya inequality (as described in Section 3 in
[
6
]
). In Theorem
2.2
we will see that even though
\lambda
is needed for the stationary problem above to have a solution, it only influences the error in approximating the corresponding Cauchy problem (
1
) through a factor that possibly grows or decays exponentially with time.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper, and is an extension of Theorem 3.5 in
[
16
]
to more general equations and numerical approximation techniques.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under Assumption
2.1
, there is
C,\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{h}(t)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}h^{\theta}\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X},\quad x\in D((\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}),

for
\theta\in[0,r]
and
\rho\in[0,\theta]
.

Before we are able to prove Theorem
2.2
, we need to recall some properties of analytic semigroups and their generators. The analytic semigroup generated by
-A
in
(A3):
is defined by the following Dunford integral,

(3)

\displaystyle S(t):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\lambda+\gamma}e^{-zt}(z-A)^{-1}\,dz,

where
\gamma:=\{se^{\pm i\delta},\ s\geq R\}\cup\{Re^{i\phi},\ |\phi|\in[\delta,\pi]\}
, for any
R\geq 0
, oriented counterclockwise, running through
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Sigma_{\delta}
and surrounding
\sigma(A)
(see e.g. Theorem 3.1 in
[
17
]
). Owing to condition
(A3):
the integral (
3
) converges in the uniform operator topology, and is independent of the choice of
R
. With
\lambda,A
as in
(A3):
, we can define fractional powers of
\lambda+A
by,

(4)

\displaystyle(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}:=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1+\alpha}e^{-\lambda t}S(t)\,dt,\qquad(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}:=\frac{\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1+\alpha}(t+\lambda+A)^{-1}(\lambda+A)\,dt,

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, while the expression for
(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}
also holds for any
\alpha\geq 1
(see Equation 6.9 and Theorem 6.9 in
[
15
]
, respectively, using that the operator semigroup generated by
-\lambda-A
, is equal to
e^{-\lambda\,\cdot}S(\cdot)
). The following properties of generators of analytic semigroups are well known.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda,A
be as in
(A3):
. Then,

(a)

there is
C,\epsilon>0
such that for any
\alpha\geq 0
,
t>0
and
x\in X

(5)

\displaystyle S(t)x\in D((\lambda+A)^{\alpha})\quad\text{with}\quad\|(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}S(t)x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\alpha}\|x\|_{X},

with convention
(\lambda+A)^{0}=I
,

(b)

for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in D((\lambda+A)^{\gamma})
with
\gamma=\max(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)
,

\displaystyle(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}(\lambda+A)^{\beta}x=(\lambda+A)^{\beta}(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}x=(\lambda+A)^{\alpha+\beta}x,

(c)

D((\lambda+A)^{\alpha})
is dense in
X
,

(d)

(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}S(t)x=S(t)(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}x
for
x\in D((\lambda+A)^{\alpha})
,

(e)

and finally for
\alpha\in[0,1]
,

(6)

\displaystyle\|(S(t)-I)x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\lambda t}t^{\alpha}\|(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}x\|_{X}.

Proof.

See appendix
A
. It is worth noting some parameter dependence of
C
and
\epsilon
in (
5
). We have
0<\epsilon<\sup\{s>0:\sigma(\lambda-s+A)\subseteq\Sigma_{\delta^{\prime}}\}
for some
\delta^{\prime}\in[\delta,\pi/2)
, while
C
depends on
M,\delta^{\prime}
and
\alpha
.
∎

The next lemma asserts that the semigroup generated by
-A_{h}
on
X_{h}
has a smoothing effect similar to (
5
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A_{h}
be as in condition
(A4):
. Then there is
C,\epsilon>0
, such that for any
x\in X
,
\alpha\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A_{h})^{\alpha}S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\alpha}\|x\|_{X}.

Proof.

See appendix
A
. The parameter dependence of
C
and
\epsilon
are as in (
5
) (but with
A
replaced by
A_{h}
, see e.g. Lemma
A.1
).
∎

For the proof of Theorem
2.2
, the interpolation inequalities of Lemma
2.5
will be key. The first interpolation inequality of that lemma is a special case of the moment inequality for sectorial operators when
\phi\geq 0
(see Section 2.7.4
[
17
]
), and the extension to
\phi\in[-1,0]
is straight forward. The second interpolation inequality is similar to the Heinz–Kato inequality (see
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
and Section 2.8.3 in
[
17
]
), but accommodates operators on Banach spaces, as opposed to only Hilbert spaces.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\lambda,A
be as in condition
(A3):
. Then, for any
\phi\in[-1,1]
,
x\in D((\lambda+A)^{\phi})\cap X
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, the following interpolation inequality holds,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A)^{\phi\alpha}x\|_{X}\leq C\|(\lambda+A)^{\phi}x\|_{X}^{\alpha}\|x\|_{X}^{1-\alpha}.

Further, for any
B\in L(X)
with the property that
B(\lambda+A)^{\phi}\in L(X)
, the following interpolation inequality holds,

\displaystyle\|B(\lambda+A)^{\phi\alpha}\|_{L(X)}\leq C\|B(\lambda+A)^{\phi}\|_{L(X)}^{\alpha}\|B\|_{L(X)}^{1-\alpha}.

Proof.

The proof of the first inequality for
\phi\in[0,1]
is given in Section 2.7.4 in
[
17
]
. The arguments are the same as those in the proof of the second inequality with
\phi\geq 0
. We include the full argument for both inequalities.

For ease of notation, and without loss of generality, assume
\lambda=0
. The case
\phi=0
is clear for any
\alpha
, and the cases
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
for any
\phi
. For the first inequality, the case
\phi=1
is given in Theorem 6.10 in
[
15
]
(see also Proposition 2.2.15 in
[
14
]
). In the case of
\phi\in(0,1)
, we have
\phi\alpha\in(0,1)
, and

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|A^{\phi\alpha}x\|_{X}&=|\frac{\sin(\alpha\phi\pi)}{\pi}|\|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1+\phi\alpha}(t+A)^{-1}Ax\,dt\|_{X}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\int_{0}^{K}t^{-1+\phi\alpha}\|(t+A)^{-1}Ax\|_{X}\,dt+\int_{K}^{\infty}t^{-1+\phi\alpha}\|(t+A)^{-1}A^{1-\phi}\|_{L(X)}\|A^{\phi}x\|_{X}\,dt\bigg)\\
&\leq CK^{\phi\alpha}\|x\|_{X}+CK^{-\phi+\phi\alpha}\|A^{\phi}x\|_{X}.\end{split}

Here we used that since the inequality holds for
\phi=1
, and
\|(t+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct^{-1}
,
\|A(t+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}\leq C
(by the bound on the resolvent in
(A3):
and the identity
A(t+A)^{-1}=I-t(t+A)^{-1}
) we must have

\displaystyle\|(t+A)^{-1}A^{1-\phi}\|_{L(X)}=\|A^{1-\phi}(t+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}\leq C\|(t+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}^{\phi}\|A(t+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}^{1-\phi}\leq Ct^{-\phi}.

Setting
K=(\|A^{\phi}x\|_{X}/\|x\|_{X})^{1/\phi}
in (
7
), we get the estimate.

For
\phi\in(-1,0)
, we have
\phi\alpha\in(-1,0)
,
\alpha\phi-\phi\geq 0
, and by the previous inequality,

\displaystyle\|A^{\phi\alpha}x\|_{X}=\|A^{\phi(\alpha-1)}A^{\phi}x\|_{X}\leq C\|A^{-\phi}A^{\phi}x\|_{X}^{1-\alpha}\|A^{\phi}x\|_{X}^{\alpha}\leq C\|A^{\phi}x\|_{X}^{\alpha}\|x\|_{X}^{1-\alpha}.

The second inequality of the lemma also holds for
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
for any
\phi
, and any
\alpha
when
\phi=0
. For
\phi\in(0,1)
, arguing as in (
7
), and using the boundedness of
B
to pass it under the integral sign, we find that,

\displaystyle\|BA^{\phi\alpha}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle=|\frac{\sin(\phi\alpha\pi)}{\pi}|\|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1+\phi\alpha}B(t+A)^{-1}A\,dt\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\int_{0}^{K}t^{-1+\phi\alpha}\|B\|_{L(X)}\|A(t+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}\,dt+\int_{K}^{\infty}t^{-1+\phi\alpha}\|BA^{\phi}\|_{L(X)}\|A^{1-\phi}(t+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}\,dt\big)

\displaystyle\leq CK^{\phi\alpha}\|B\|_{L(X)}+CK^{-\phi+\phi\alpha}\|BA^{\phi}\|_{L(X)},

Setting
K=(\|BA^{\phi}\|_{L(X)}/\|B\|_{L(X)})^{1/\phi}
gives the inequality.

For
\phi\in(-1,0)
, we have
\phi\alpha\in(-1,0)
, and by using the definition of
A^{\phi}
in (
4
), the boundedness of
B
to pass it under the integral sign, and the inequality
\|A^{-\phi}S(t)\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct^{\phi}
from Lemma
2.3
, we find

\displaystyle\|BA^{\phi\alpha}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\phi\alpha)}\|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\phi\alpha}BS(t)\,dt\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\int_{0}^{K}t^{-1-\phi\alpha}\|B\|_{L(X)}\|S(t)\|_{L(X)}\,dt+\int_{K}^{\infty}t^{-1-\phi\alpha}\|BA^{\phi}\|_{L(X)}\|A^{-\phi}S(t)\|_{L(X)}\,dt\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq CK^{-\phi\alpha}\|B\|_{L(X)}+CK^{\phi-\phi\alpha}\|BA^{\phi}\|_{L(X)}.

Setting
K=(\|BA^{\phi}\|_{L(X)}/\|B\|_{L(X)})^{-1/\phi}
, gives the inequality.
∎

The last lemma that we list before proving Theorem
2.2
uses the previous interpolation inequality to assert that if a collection of operators satisfy a couple of inequalities, we can interpolate these inequalities to arrive at the particular expression in Theorem
2.2
. In the proof of Theorem
2.2
, we will apply this lemma to the operator
e^{-\lambda t+\epsilon t}(S(t)-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h})
, where
\epsilon>0
is the smallest
\epsilon
from Lemma
2.3
and
2.4
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\lambda,A
be as in condition
(A3):
, and let
B_{t,h}\in L(X)
be a collection of bounded linear operators satisfying,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}\leq C,\quad\|B_{t,h}(\lambda+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ch^{r},\quad\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct^{-1}h^{r},

for some
C>0
. Then, the following estimate holds,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}x\|\leq Ct^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}h^{\theta}\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X},\quad x\in D((\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}),

for
\theta\in[0,r]
, and
\rho\in[0,\theta]
.

Proof.

We have,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ch^{r\alpha},\quad\|B_{t,h}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct^{-1+\alpha}h^{r},

for
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
and in view of Lemma
2.5
, we have that this holds for
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Thus, we get,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle=\|B_{t,h}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}^{\beta}\|B_{t,h}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C(t^{-1+\alpha}h^{r})^{\beta}(h^{r\alpha})^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}h^{\theta},

where we have set
\alpha=\rho/r
and
\beta=(\rho-\theta)/(\rho-r)
, for some
\theta\in[0,r]
, and
\rho\in[0,\theta]
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

It suffices to show the inequalities of Lemma
2.6
for the operator
e^{\epsilon t}(e^{-\lambda t}S(t)-e^{-\lambda t}S_{h}(t)\pi_{h})
, with
\epsilon>0
the smallest
\epsilon
from Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
. Recall that the operator semigroup generated by
-\lambda-A
is
e^{-\lambda\,\cdot}S(\cdot)
, and so without loss of generality, assume in the following that
\lambda=0
.

In order to show the inequalities of Lemma
2.6
, take
x\in X
, and decompose the error as follows,

\displaystyle S(t)x-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x

\displaystyle=(I-\pi_{h})S(t)x+\pi_{h}S(t)x-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x

\displaystyle=:(I)+(II),

where we have set,

\displaystyle(I)=(I-\pi_{h})S(t)x,\quad(II)=\pi_{h}S(t)x-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x.

First, note that
\|(I)+(II)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}\|x\|_{X}
, which follows by Lemma
2.3
and
2.4
. To show the remaining inequalities we treat
(I)
and
(II)
separately.

Define
\Delta_{h}:=A^{-1}-A_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}
(as in condition
(A5):
with
\lambda=0
). Next we verify that
\|(I)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}
and
\|(I)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}
. Note that,

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle=(I-\pi_{h})A^{-1}AS(t)x

\displaystyle=(A^{-1}-\pi_{h}A^{-1})S(t)Ax

\displaystyle=(A^{-1}-A_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}+A_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}-\pi_{h}A^{-1})S(t)Ax

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{h}-\pi_{h}\Delta_{h})S(t)Ax.

This in turn gives that
\|(I)\|_{X}\leq C\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|AS(t)\|_{L(X)}\|x\|_{X}
, and
\|(I)\|_{X}\leq C\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|S(t)\|_{L(X)}\|Ax\|_{X}
, and by applying condition
(A5):
, and Lemma
2.3
, we get the desired inequalities.

It remains to show that
\|(II)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}
and
\|(II)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}
. In order to do so, we follow
[
10
]
, and use an integral expression for
(II)
. To that end, define
e_{h}(t):=(II)
, and note that we have,

\displaystyle\dot{e}_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=-\pi_{h}AS(t)x+A_{h}S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x

\displaystyle=-A_{h}(\pi_{h}S(t)x-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x)+A_{h}\pi_{h}S(t)x-\pi_{h}AS(t)x

\displaystyle=-A_{h}e_{h}(t)+(A_{h}\pi_{h}-\pi_{h}A)S(t)x

\displaystyle=-A_{h}e_{h}(t)+A_{h}\pi_{h}(A^{-1}-A_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h})AS(t)x

\displaystyle=-A_{h}e_{h}(t)+A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(t)x,

In the next to last equality, we used that
\pi_{h}=A_{h}A_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}
. Therefore (since
e_{h}(0)=0
), we have,

\displaystyle e_{h}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(s)x\,ds.

We split the integral above in two as in Lemma 3.1 in
[
13
]
,

\displaystyle e_{h}(t)=\int_{0}^{t/2}S_{h}(t-s)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(s)x\,ds+\int_{t/2}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(s)x\,ds=:(i)+(ii),

and estimate each term separately. Integrating by parts, we get,

\displaystyle(ii)=\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(t)x-S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(t/2)x+\int_{t/2}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}A^{2}S(s)x\,ds,

where we used the identity,

\displaystyle\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(t)x-S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(t/2)x

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{t/2}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}\bigg(S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(s)x\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{t/2}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(s)x\,ds-\int_{t/2}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}A^{2}S(s)x\,ds.

We start by showing
\|(II)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}
. For
(i)
, we have by condition
(A5):
, Lemma
2.3
and
2.4
,

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t/2}S_{h}(t-s)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}S(s)Ax\,ds\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t/2}\|A_{h}S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|S(s)\|_{L(X)}\|Ax\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}\int_{0}^{t/2}(t-s)^{-1}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X},

For the first and second term if
(ii)
, we get using the boundedness of the semigroups and of the projection
\pi_{h}
, in addition to condition
(A5):
:

\displaystyle\|\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}S(t)Ax\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|S(t)\|_{L(X)}\|Ax\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X},

\displaystyle\|S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}S(t/2)Ax\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|S(t/2)\|_{L(X)}\|Ax\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}.

For the last term in
(ii)
, we have by Lemma
2.3
and
2.4
, condition
(A5):

\displaystyle\|\int_{t/2}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}A^{2}S(s)x\,ds\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t/2}^{t}\|S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|AS(s)\|_{L(X)}\|Ax\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}\int_{t/2}^{t}s^{-1}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}.

We therefore get that
\|(II)\|\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}h^{r}\|Ax\|_{X}
.

It remains to show
\|(II)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}
. Using integration by parts on
(i)
as above, we get,

\displaystyle(i)=-S_{h}(t/2)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}S(t/2)+S_{h}(t)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}+\int_{0}^{t/2}A_{h}^{2}S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}S(s)x\,ds.

We note that,

\displaystyle\|A_{h}S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}S(t/2)x\|_{X}\leq\|A_{h}S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|S(t/2)x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X},

and,

\displaystyle\|S_{h}(t)A_{h}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}x\|_{X}\leq\|A_{h}S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X},

while for the third term of
(i)
, we have,

\displaystyle\|\int_{0}^{t/2}S_{h}(t-s)A_{h}^{2}\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}S(s)x\,ds\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t/2}\|A_{h}^{2}S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|S(s)\|_{L(X)}\|x\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}\int_{0}^{t/2}(t-s)^{-2}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}.

For the first two terms of
(ii)
(after integration by parts) we have using condition
(A5):
, Lemma
2.3
and
2.4

\displaystyle\|\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(t)x\|_{X}\leq C\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|AS(t)\|_{L(X)}\|x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X},

and

\displaystyle\|S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}AS(t/2)x\|_{X}\leq\|S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|AS(t/2)\|_{L(X)}\|x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}.

By the same arguments, we have for the last term of
(ii)
,

\displaystyle\|\int_{t/2}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\Delta_{h}A^{2}S(s)x\,ds\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t/2}^{t}\|S_{h}(t-s)\pi_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}\|_{L(X)}\|A^{2}S(s)\|_{L(X)}\|x\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}\int_{0}^{t/2}s^{-2}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}.

Therefore, we get that
\|(II)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r}\|x\|_{X}
.

Since all inequalities of Lemma
2.6
have been shown for
e^{\epsilon t}(S(t)-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h})
, the proof is finished.
∎

2.2.
Fully discrete error estimate

By combining
A_{h}
in Theorem
2.2
with a rational approximation of the exponential function, we get a fully discrete approximation of
S(t)
. To that end, we let
r(z):=(1+z)^{-1}
, partition the time interval
[0,T]
uniformly with increments
\Delta t=T/N
for some positive integer
N
, and define,

(8)

\displaystyle S_{h,\Delta t}(t):=\begin{cases}I,\quad&t=0,\\
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r(\Delta tA_{h})^{n+1}\chi_{(t_{n},t_{n+1}]}(t),\quad&t>0.\end{cases}

Here
t_{n}=n\Delta t
,
I
is the identity, and
\chi_{D}(\cdot)
is the characteristic function on the set
D
.

In this section we combine Theorem
2.2
with Lemma
2.7
,
2.8
and
2.9
in order to arrive at an error estimate for our fully discrete approximation, which is stated in Theorem
2.14
. The proofs of Lemma
2.7
,
2.8
and
2.9
share some similarity to those of Theorem 9.1, 9.2 and 9.3 in
[
16
]
, with the difference being that those theorems assume that we can always choose
\lambda=0
—we need to accommodate the cases where
\lambda
has to be chosen greater than
0
. This is e.g. the case for the example we consider in Section 3. To accommodate these cases, we are going to pay with a factor that grows exponentially with time. When we may choose
\lambda=0
we recover the results from Chapter 9 in
[
16
]
, with an additional factor decaying exponentially with time.

Lemma
2.10
and
2.11
are similar to Lemma
2.7
,
2.8
and
2.9
, and are needed to get a fully discrete error estimate for less regular data, stated in Theorem
2.24
. The proofs of the five following lemmas are all based on a Dunford integral representation of the difference,

\displaystyle S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\lambda+\gamma}(e^{-zt_{n}}-r(\Delta tz)^{n})(z-A_{h})^{-1}\,dz,

where
\gamma
is as in (
3
). The details are given in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose condition
(A4):
holds, and let
S_{h,\Delta t}
be as in (
8
). Then there is
C,c,\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}\|x\|_{X}.

Lemma 2.8
.

Under the same conditions as in Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}\Delta t\|(\lambda+A_{h})\pi_{h}x\|_{X}.

Lemma 2.9
.

Under the same conditions as in Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}t_{n}^{-1}\Delta t\|x\|_{X}.

Lemma 2.10
.

Under the same conditions as in Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}t_{n}^{-1/2}\|(\lambda+A_{h})^{-1/2}\pi_{h}x\|_{X}.

Lemma 2.11
.

Under the same conditions as in Lemma
2.7
,

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}t_{n}^{-1}\Delta t^{1/2}\|(\lambda+A_{h})^{-1/2}\pi_{h}x\|_{X}.

The following condition and lemma will be useful for “replacing
A_{h}
by
A
” in the following error estimates.

(A6):

For some
C>0
, the following estimate holds,

(9)

\displaystyle\|A_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ch^{-r}\|x\|_{X},\quad\text{for any }x\in X_{h}.

Lemma 2.12
.

Under the conditions of Assumuption
2.1
and condition
(A6):
, there is some
C>0
, such that,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A_{h})\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq C\|(\lambda+A)x\|_{X},\quad\text{for any }x\in X.

Proof.

Due to
(A6):
, we have
\|(\lambda+A_{h})x\|_{X}\leq Ch^{-r}\|x\|_{X}
as well. It suffices to show that
\|(\lambda+A_{h})\pi_{h}(\lambda+A)^{-1}x\|_{X}\leq C\|x\|_{X}
, for any
x\in X
. To that end,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A_{h})\pi_{h}(\lambda+A)^{-1}x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|(\lambda+A_{h})\pi_{h}((\lambda+A)^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{h})^{-1}\pi_{h}+(\lambda+A_{h})^{-1}\pi_{h})x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi_{h}x\|_{X}+\|(\lambda+A_{h})\pi_{h}\|_{L(X_{h})}\|((\lambda+A)^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{h})^{-1}\pi_{h})x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\|x\|_{X},

where we used condition
(A5):
.
∎

We will need the following lemma to show our first fully discrete error estimate.

Lemma 2.13
.

Suppose the conditions of Assumption
2.1
and
(A6):
holds. Then, there is
C,c,\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))\pi_{h}x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}t_{n}^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}\Delta t^{\theta/r}\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X},

for
\theta\in[0,r]
and
\rho\in[0,\theta]
.

Proof.

Set
B_{t_{n},\Delta t}:=e^{-c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))\pi_{h}
, where
c
is either the biggest or smallest
c
from Lemma
2.7
,
2.8
and
2.9
, depending on the sign of
\lambda-\epsilon
. By combining the inequalities of Lemma
2.7
,
2.8
and
2.9
, with Lemma
2.12
, we have,

\displaystyle\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}\|_{L(X)}\leq C,\quad\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}(\lambda+A)^{-1}\|_{L(X)}\leq C\Delta t,\quad\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct_{n}^{-1}\Delta t,

Therefore, we have,

\displaystyle\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}\leq C\Delta t^{\alpha},\quad\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct_{n}^{-1+\alpha}\Delta t,

for
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
and therefore in
[0,1]
by Lemma
2.5
. Finally, this gives,

\displaystyle\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle=\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}^{\beta}\|B_{t_{n},\Delta t}(\lambda+A)^{-\alpha}\|_{L(X)}^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C(t_{n}^{\alpha-1}\Delta t)^{\beta}(\Delta t^{\alpha})^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle=C\Delta t^{\theta/r}t_{n}^{-\theta/r+\rho/r},

where we have set
\alpha=\rho/r
,
\theta=\rho+(r-\rho)\beta\in[\rho,r]
. Thus
\theta\in[0,r]
and
\rho\in[0,\theta]
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to derive our first fully discrete error estimate.

Theorem 2.14
.

Suppose the conditions of Assumption
2.1
and
(A6):
holds. Then, there is
C,c,\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(S(t)-S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h})x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}(h^{\theta}+\Delta t^{\theta/r})\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/2}x\|_{X},

for
\theta\in[0,r]
and
\rho\in[0,\theta]
.

Proof.

For
t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}]
, we decompose the error as follows,

\displaystyle S(t)-S(t_{n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad+S(t_{n+1})-S_{h}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+S_{h}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}-S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h}.

For the first term, we have for some
\theta\in[0,r],\rho\in[0,\theta]
,

\displaystyle\|(S(t)-S(t_{n+1}))x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))S(t)x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))(\lambda+A)^{-\theta/r}(\lambda+A)^{\theta/r-\rho/r}S(t)(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))(\lambda+A)^{-\theta/r}\|_{L(X)}\|(\lambda+A)^{\theta/r-\rho/r}S(t)\|_{L(X)}\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X}.

Using Lemma
2.3
we have,

\displaystyle\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))(\lambda+A)^{-\theta/r}\|_{L(X)}\leq C\Delta t^{\theta/r}\quad\text{and}\quad\|(\lambda+A)^{\theta/r-\rho/r}S(t)\|_{L(X)}\leq Ce^{(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r},

for
\theta\in[0,r]
and
\rho\in[0,\theta]
. This finishes the first term.

For the two next terms, we use Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
2.13
. For the last term, we note that,

\displaystyle S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}-S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h}=0,\quad t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}].

To replace
t_{n+1}^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}
with
t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}
we use that since
-\theta/r+\rho/r\leq 0
, we have that
t_{n+1}^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}\leq t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}
.
∎

2.3.
Semidiscrete and fully discrete error estimates in different norms, and for less regular data

The rest of this section is devoted to showing semidiscrete error estimates in the
\|(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}\cdot\|_{X}
-norm, for
\alpha\in[-1/2,1/2]
, in addition to semidiscrete and fully discrete error estimates of the form of Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.14
where
\rho
can be chosen negative. In order to do so, we require stronger assumptions than those we managed with in the previous two subsections. The first additional condition that we need is similar to
(A5):
, with the error computed in a different norm.

(A7):

For
\lambda,A
as in
(A3):
, the following estimate also holds,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A)^{1/2}((\lambda+A)^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{h})^{-1}\pi_{h})x\|_{X}\leq Ch^{r/2}\|x\|_{X},\quad x\in X.

With this additional condition, we are able to show another semidiscrete error estimate, which is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.15
.

Suppose the conditions of Assumption
2.1
,
(A6):
and
(A7):
holds. Then there is
C,\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}(u(t)-u_{h}(t))\|_{X}\leq Ce^{(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\theta/r}h^{\theta-\alpha r}\|x\|_{X},

for
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
and
\theta\in[r\alpha,r]
.

For the proof of Lemma
2.15
, we need the following couple of lemmas.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
B_{t,h}\in L(X)
be a collection of bounded linear operators satisfying,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct^{-1/2},\quad\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct^{-1}h^{r/2},

for some
C>0
. Then, the following estimate holds,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}x\|\leq Ct^{-\theta/r}h^{\theta-r/2}\|x\|_{X},\quad x\in X,

for
\theta\in[r/2,r]
.

Proof.

We have for
\beta\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle=\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}^{\beta}\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C(t^{-1/2})^{\beta}(t^{-1}h^{r/2})^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\theta/r}h^{\theta-r/2},

where we have set and
\theta=r-r\beta/2\in[r/2,r]
.
∎

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
B_{t,h}\in L(X)
be a collection of bounded linear operators satisfying the inequalities of Lemma
2.6
, and assume that
(\lambda+A)^{1/2}B_{t,h}
satisfies the inequalities of Lemma
2.16
. Then,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}B_{t,h}x\|_{X}\leq Ct^{-\theta/r}h^{\theta-\alpha r}\|x\|_{X}

where
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
and
\theta\in[r\alpha,r]
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
2.5
on the inequalities from Lemma
2.6
with
\rho=0
and Lemma
2.16
, we get for
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
, and
\beta\in\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\lambda+A)^{1/2}B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}^{2\alpha}\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}^{1-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq C(t^{-\theta_{1}/r}h^{\theta_{1}-r/2})^{2\alpha}(t^{-\theta_{2}/r}h^{\theta_{2}})^{1-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\theta/r}h^{\theta-\alpha r},

where
\theta_{1}\in[r/2,r],\theta_{2}\in[0,r]
,
\theta=2\alpha\theta_{1}+(1-2\alpha)\theta_{2}\in[\alpha r,r]
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.15
.

The proof is similar in idea to the proof of Theorem
2.2
. We need to show the inequalities of Lemma
2.16
for the operator
e^{-\lambda t+\epsilon t}(\lambda+A)^{1/2}(S(t)-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h})
, and then employ Lemma
2.17
to get the desired inequality (since we have the remaining inequalities for the operator
e^{-\lambda t+\epsilon t}(S(t)-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h})
by the proof of Theorem
2.2
). Here,
\epsilon
is the same as that in Theorem
2.2
. We can, as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, assume without loss of generality and for ease of notation that
\lambda=0
, since
e^{-\lambda\,\cdot}S(\cdot)
is the operator semigroup generated by
-\lambda-A
. Therefore, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\|A^{1/2}(S(t)-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h})x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1/2}\|x\|_{X},

\displaystyle\|A^{1/2}(S(t)-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h})x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r/2}\|x\|_{X}.

Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
we let
\Delta_{h}:=A^{-1}-A_{h}^{-1}\pi_{h}
. Let now
e_{h}(t):=S(t)x-S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x
. Then,

\displaystyle\dot{e}_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=-Ae_{h}(t)+(A_{h}-A)S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x

\displaystyle=-Ae_{h}(t)+A\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x.

It may be seen that condition
(A7):
implies
X_{h}\subseteq D(A^{1/2})
, and since we are applying
A
to members of
X_{h}
, the equality above is understood in a
D(A^{-1/2})
-sense, which we understand as the completion of
X
using the norm
\|A^{-1/2}\cdot\|_{X}
. Using that
A^{1/2}
is closed to pass it under the integral sign, we get (note that
e_{h}(0)=(I-\pi_{h})x
),

\displaystyle A^{1/2}e_{h}(t)=A^{1/2}S(t)(I-\pi_{h})x+\int_{0}^{t}AS(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds=(I)+(II).

For
(I)
we have:

\displaystyle\|(I)\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|AS(t)A^{1/2}(A^{-1}-A^{-1}\pi_{h})x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|AS(t)A^{1/2}(\Delta_{h}-\Delta_{h}\pi_{h})x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|AS(t)\|_{L(X)}\|\Delta_{h}(I-\pi_{h})x\|_{X},

so that
\|(I)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r/2}\|x\|_{X}
, by condition
(A7):
, the boundedness of
I-\pi_{h}
and Lemma
2.3
. Finally,
\|(I)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1/2}\|x\|_{X}
by Lemma
2.3
and the boundedness of
I-\pi_{h}
.

For the second term, we have,

\displaystyle(II)=\int_{0}^{t/2}AS(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds+\int_{t/2}^{t}AS(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds=(i)+(ii),

and using integration by parts, we have,

\displaystyle(i)=-AS(t/2)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}x+AS(t)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}\pi_{h}x+\int_{0}^{t/2}A^{2}S(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds,

where we used that,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{d}{ds}\bigg(AS(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t/2}A^{2}S(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t/2}AS(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds

\displaystyle=AS(t/2)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}x-AS(t)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}\pi_{h}x.

Using integrating by parts on
(ii)
as well, gives,

\displaystyle(ii)=A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x-S(t/2)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}x+\int_{t/2}^{t}S(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}^{2}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds,

where we used that,

\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}\bigg(S(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\bigg)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{t/2}^{t}AS(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t/2}^{t}S(t-s)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}^{2}S_{h}(s)\pi_{h}x\,ds

\displaystyle=A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(t)\pi_{h}x-S(t/2)A^{1/2}\Delta_{h}A_{h}S_{h}(t/2)\pi_{h}x.

We start by showing
\|(II)\|_{X}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon t}t^{-1}h^{r/2}\|x\|_{X}

where we used that owing to condition

The next lemma is another useful semidiscrete error estimate.

Lemma 2.18
.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let

be a collection of bounded linear operators satisfying,

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ch^{r/2},\quad\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ct^{-1/2}h^{r},

\displaystyle\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle=\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}^{\beta}\|B_{t,h}\|_{L(X)}^{1-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\theta/r}h^{\theta+r/2},

where we have set
\theta=r(1-\beta)/2\in[0,r/2]
.
∎

we have for
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
,

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}t_{n}^{-\alpha},

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}\|_{L(X)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}t_{n}^{-1}\Delta t^{(1-\alpha)r},

which gives for some
\gamma\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|(S_{h}(t_{n})-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n}))(\lambda+A)^{\alpha}\|_{L(X)}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t_{n}}(t_{n}^{-\alpha})^{1-\gamma}(t_{n}^{-1}h^{(1-\alpha)})^{\gamma}.

Setting
\theta=(1-\alpha)\gamma r
, and
\rho=-\alpha r
gives the inequality.
∎

The next theorem extends Theorem
2.14
to the case of
\rho<0
.

Theorem 2.24
.

Suppose the conditions of Assumption
2.1
,
(A6):
,
(A8):
and
(A9):
holds. Then, there is
C,c,\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(S(t)-S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h})x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{c(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}(h^{\theta}+\Delta t^{\theta/2})\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X},

for
\theta\in[0,r]
and
\rho\in[-r/2,0]\cap[-r+\theta,0]
.

Proof.

The proof is almost the same as that of Theorem
2.14
, but now we have to use Lemma
2.21
and Lemma
2.23
: for
t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}]
, we decompose the error as follows,

\displaystyle S(t)-S(t_{n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad+S(t_{n+1})-S_{h}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+S_{h}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}-S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}-S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h}.

For the first term, we have for some
\theta\in[0,r],\rho\in[-r/2,0]\cap[-r+\theta,0]
,

\displaystyle\|(S(t)-S(t_{n+1}))x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))S(t)x\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))(\lambda+A)^{-\theta/r}(\lambda+A)^{\theta/r-\rho/r}S(t)(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))(\lambda+A)^{-\theta/r}\|_{L(X)}\|(\lambda+A)^{\theta/r-\rho/r}S(t)\|_{L(X)}\|(\lambda+A)^{\rho/r}x\|_{X}.

As in the proof of Theorem
2.14
, we have by Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\|(I-S(t_{n+1}-t))(\lambda+A)^{-\theta/r}\|_{L(X)}\leq C\Delta t^{\theta/r}\quad\text{and}\quad\|(\lambda+A)^{\theta/r-\rho/r}S(t)x\|_{X}\leq Ce^{(\lambda-\epsilon)t}t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}\|x\|_{X}.

Combining these inequalities finishes the first term. For the two next terms, we now use Lemma
2.21
and Lemma
2.23
. For the last term, we also here note that,

\displaystyle S_{h,\Delta t}(t_{n+1})\pi_{h}-S_{h,\Delta t}(t)\pi_{h}=0,\quad t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}],

and arguing as in the proof of Theorem
2.14
we may replace
t_{n+1}^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}
with
t^{-\theta/r+\rho/r}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

3.
Example: surface finite element approximation of a parabolic equation

To relate the abstract conditions of Assumption
2.1
to differentiation operators, Lemma
3.1
is key. To that end, let
V,H
be Hilbert spaces with
V\subseteq H
, densely and continuously with inclusion
j:V\to H
, and let
a:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
be a coercive and continuous sesquilinear form on
V
. Recall that
a
is continuous if for some
C>0
,

\displaystyle|a(u,v)|\leq C\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V},\quad u,v\in V,

and it is coercive if for some
c>0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(a(u,u))\geq c\|u\|_{V}^{2},\quad u\in V.

By Lax–Milgram theorem, the operator,

\displaystyle A:V\to V^{*},\quad Au=a(u,\cdot),

is an isomorphism. Since
(\varphi,j\cdot)_{H}\in V^{*}
for any
\varphi\in H
, we can also define (without changing notation), an operator
A:D(A)\to H
,
D(A)\subseteq V
by identifying
a(u,\cdot)
with
\varphi\in H
, in the case when,

\displaystyle a(u,\cdot)=(\varphi,j\cdot)_{H},\quad u\in D(A).

The following lemma asserts that
A:D(A)\to H
is sectorial.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A:D(A)\to H,\ D(A)\subseteq V
be defined by
(Au,jv)_{H}=a(u,v)
for any
v\in V
, where
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is a coercive and continuous sesquilinear form on
V
. Set
M^{\prime}:=1+C/c
, where
C,c
are the continuity and coercivity constants of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, respectively. Then:

(1)

for any
\delta\in(\pi/2-\sin^{-1}(M^{\prime-1}),\pi/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(A)\subseteq\Sigma_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},\ |\mathrm{arg}(z)|<\delta\},

(2)

and for any
z\notin\Sigma_{\delta}
, we have,

(10)

\displaystyle\|(z-A)^{-1}x\|_{H}\leq\frac{M}{|z|}\|x\|_{H},\quad\text{where}\quad M=\frac{M^{\prime}\cos(\pi/2-\delta)}{1-M^{\prime}\sin(\pi/2-\delta)}\geq M^{\prime}.

Proof.

See Chapter 2 in
[
17
]
, and in particular Theorem 2.1, Equation 2.4 and 2.5.
∎

With Lemma
3.1
at hand, we are ready to connect the results of the previous section to results related to the numerical approximation of parabolic partial differential equations. We consider the model problem,

\displaystyle\dot{u}=-Lu,\quad u(0)=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\Gamma),

where (we refer to Appendix
C
for the appropriate definitions):

(1)

\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a compact (and therefore boundaryless)
2
-dimensional
C^{3}
-surface,

(2)

V
is a closed subspace of
H^{1}(\Gamma)
, and
H
is
L^{2}(\Gamma)
,

(3)

L:=\mathcal{L}-I
, where
\mathcal{L}
is defined as in Lemma
3.1
, using the (coercive and continuous) sesquilinear form,

\displaystyle a(u,v):=\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\overline{v}+u\overline{v}\,d\sigma,

on
V
, where
\nabla_{\Gamma}
are tangential derivatives on
\Gamma
, and
\sigma
is surface measure on
\Gamma
, and
\overline{v}
is the complex conjugate of
v
.

By Lemma
C.2
we deduce that
D(\mathcal{L})=H^{2}(\Gamma)\cap V
, and for
u\in H^{2}(\Gamma)\cap V
,
Lu=\Delta_{\Gamma}u
and
\mathcal{L}u=(I-\Delta_{\Gamma})u
for a.e.
x\in\Gamma
. The latter identity combined with Lemma
C.2
, gives for any
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
,

(11)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}^{-1}f\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

which is an elliptic regularity estimate for solutions,
u
, of

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u=f.

By Lemma
3.1
it is clear that
-L
generates an analytic semigroup on
L^{2}(\Gamma)
—in the setting of Assumption
2.1
,
(A3):
we have
L=A
and
\mathcal{L}=\lambda+A
, with
\lambda=1
.

In order to approximate
\mathcal{L}
numerically, we use the surface finite element approximation proposed in
[
5
]
. To that end, let
\Gamma_{h}:=\bigcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\tau\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a
2
-dimensional surface, consisting of a collection,
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, of planar simplices,
\tau
, whose vertices lie on
\Gamma
, and where
h:=\max_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\text{diam}(\tau)
. The collection
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is regular in the sense that for some
C>0
,

\displaystyle C^{-1}h\leq 2\rho(\tau)\leq\text{diam}(\tau)\leq 2r(\tau)\leq Ch,\quad\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

where
\rho,r
are the radii of the incircle and circumcircle, respectively. Let
V_{h}\subseteq C(\overline{\Gamma}_{h})
, be the finite dimensional linear space of continuous functions that are affine linear when restricted to
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We denote the nodal basis of
V_{h}
associated to the triangle vertices
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N_{h}}\}\in\Gamma_{h}\cap\Gamma
, by
\varphi_{n}
,
n=1,\dots,N_{h}
. Thus
\varphi_{n}
are piece wise affine linear, with
\varphi_{n}(x_{n})=1
and
\varphi_{n}(x_{j})=0
,
j\neq n
.

To relate functions defined on
\Gamma_{h}
to those defined on
\Gamma
, the closest point projection is key. To that end, let
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
be a tubular neighbourhood of
\Gamma
(see Appendix
C
). The closest point projection,
p:\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\to\Gamma
is defined by,

\displaystyle p(x):=x-d(x)\nabla d(x),

where
d
is the signed distance function of
\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma
is a
C^{3}
-surface, we have
p\in C^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
. For
h
small enough, we have
\Gamma_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
, and
p
restricted to
\Gamma_{h}
becomes a bijection
\Gamma_{h}\to\Gamma
. Therefore, for any function
f:\Gamma_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
, we can define the lift,

(12)

\displaystyle\iota_{h}f:=f\circ p|_{\Gamma_{h}}^{-1}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}.

Our surface finite element approximation of
\mathcal{L}
, denoted
\mathcal{L}_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}
, is defined by requiring that,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{h}u,v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}=a_{h}(u,v),\quad u,v\in V_{h},

where
a_{h}:V_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by,

(13)

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,v)=\int_{\Gamma_{h}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}u\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}\overline{v}+u\overline{v}\,d\sigma_{h}.

Here,
\nabla_{\Gamma_{h}}
,
\sigma_{h}
are tangential derivatives and surface measure on
\Gamma_{h}
, respectively—the tangential derivatives on the discrete surface are defined similarly as on the smooth surface, but only in an almost everywhere sense since the unit normal on the discrete surface is only defined almost everywhere. Similarly, we define,

\displaystyle L_{h}:=\mathcal{L}_{h}-I.

Finally we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{h}:L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})\to V_{h}
the
L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})
-orthogonal projection onto
V_{h}
. We are now ready to list some useful properties of
\Gamma_{h}
and
\mathcal{L}_{h}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma_{h}
be as above. Then the quotient of surface area measure,
\delta_{h}:\Gamma_{h}\to[0,\infty)
, defined (
\sigma_{h}
-a.e.) by,

\displaystyle\int_{p(U)}d\sigma=\int_{U}\delta_{h}\,d\sigma_{h},\quad U\subseteq\Gamma_{h}\text{ measurable},

satisfies,

(14)

\displaystyle\|1-\delta_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq Ch^{2},

(15)

\displaystyle\|1-\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\underline{\partial}_{i}(1-\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq Ch,

and

(16)

\displaystyle\|\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\underline{\partial}_{i}\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq C,

for some
C>0
independent of
h
. Here
\underline{\partial}_{i}
denotes the
i

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
x_{3}=0
on a given
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and so surface measure on
\tau
is given by
d\sigma_{h}=dx_{1}dx_{2}
. For
x\in\tau
,
\delta_{h}
is defined by,

(17)

\displaystyle\delta_{h}^{2}:=\begin{vmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}p_{2}&\partial_{x_{1}}p_{3}\\
\partial_{x_{2}}p_{2}&\partial_{x_{2}}p_{3}\end{vmatrix}^{2}+\begin{vmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}p_{1}&\partial_{x_{1}}p_{3}\\
\partial_{x_{2}}p_{1}&\partial_{x_{2}}p_{3}\end{vmatrix}^{2}+\begin{vmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}p_{1}&\partial_{x_{1}}p_{2}\\
\partial_{x_{2}}p_{1}&\partial_{x_{2}}p_{2}\end{vmatrix}^{2}.

The inequality (
14
) holds by Lemma 4.1 in
[
6
]
, and from the proof of that lemma we also gather that,

(18)

\displaystyle\|d\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\leq Ch^{2},\quad\|\nu_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\leq Ch,

with
\nu_{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}d
,
i=1,2
, for some
C>0
that only depends on the
C^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
-norm of
d
. For any
x\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
,

and
i,k=1,2,3

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{j}}p_{i}=\delta_{ij}-\nu_{j}\nu_{i}-d\nu_{ij},\quad\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x_{j}}p_{i}=-\nu_{kj}\nu_{i}-\nu_{j}\nu_{ik}-\nu_{k}\nu_{ij}-d\nu_{ijk},

where
\delta_{ij}=1
if

and
0
otherwise. Using (

, we get

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{j}}p_{i}-\delta_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\leq Ch^{2},\quad\|\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x_{j}}p_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\leq Ch.

Since
p
is defined on all of
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
,
\delta_{h}
has a natural extension to a tubular neighbourhood of any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, given by (
17
), and which we denote by
\tilde{\delta}_{h}
. By (
14
) we have that
\delta_{h}\in[1/2,3/2]
for all
h
small, so by the expression (
17
) for
\delta_{h}^{2}
, we must have that for
i=1,2,3
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{\delta}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}=\|\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\delta}_{h}^{-1}\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{\delta}_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{\delta}_{h}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{\delta}_{h}^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\leq Ch,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on the
C^{3}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
-norm of

. The inequality above implies that
\|\nabla\tilde{\delta}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}\leq Ch
, which in turn implies that
\|\nabla\tilde{\delta}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq Ch
. From the proof of Lemma 3 in
[
5

we gather that for any
\tilde{f}
defined in a neighbourhood of
\tau

such that for

,

\displaystyle|(\nabla_{\Gamma}\iota_{h}f)\circ p|\leq C|\nabla\tilde{f}|,\quad x\in\tau.

This gives for

,

\displaystyle\|\underline{\partial}_{i}(1-\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\nabla\tilde{\delta}_{h}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq C\|\tilde{\delta}_{h}^{-2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\|\nabla\tilde{\delta}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq Ch,

for all

small. Note furthermore that by (

\displaystyle\|1-\iota_{h}\delta_{h}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}=\|\delta_{h}^{-1}(1-\delta_{h})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq\|\delta_{h}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\|1-\delta_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq Ch^{2}.

Combining these observations gives (
15
) and (
16
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
a
and
a_{h}
be as above, and let

. Then the following estimates holds,

\displaystyle|a(u,v)-a_{h}(\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}v)|\leq Ch^{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}.

The first inequality of the lemma follows by Lemma 4.7 in

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{h}
and

be as above. Then the norms,

with

with constants independent of

]
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma_{h}
and
\mathcal{L}_{h}
be as above. Then there is

, such that for any

This inequality holds since the mesh is regular. When the mesh is regular, the following inequality holds (see e.g. Theorem 3.2.6 in

We are now ready to place the surface finite element approximation above into the framework of Assumption
2.1
. To that end, let:

(1)

.

(2)

X_{h}:=\iota_{h}V_{h}
.

(3)

.

(4)

.

(5)

.

Note first that condition
(A3):
holds by Lemma

for the operator

. The next lemma asserts that condition

holds for the discrete operator,

.

Lemma 3.6
.

A_{h}:X_{h}\to X_{h}
defined as above satisfies condition
(A4):
.

Proof.

Condition
(A4):
holds by Lemma
3.1
provided we can show that the continuity and coercivity constants of the sesquilinear form,

\displaystyle(\iota_{h}\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}\cdot,\cdot)_{H}:\iota_{h}V_{h}\times\iota_{h}V_{h}\to\mathbb{R},

on the lifted finite element space
\iota_{h}V_{h}
, equipped with the

-norm, are independent of
h
.

We first verify that the continuity constant can be chosen independently of
h
. For any
u,v\in\iota_{h}V_{h}
, we have,

\displaystyle(\iota_{h}\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}-(\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,(1-\delta_{h})\iota_{h}^{-1}v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle=a_{h}(\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}v)-(\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,(1-\delta_{h})\iota_{h}^{-1}v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}.

For the first term, we have,

\displaystyle|a_{h}(\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}v)|\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)},

by using Cauchy–Schwarz, and Lemma
3.4
. For the second term, note first that owing to (

) and (

\|\mathcal{L}_{h}^{1/2}f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\leq Ch^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}
for

. Then by Cauchy–Schwarz,

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,(1-\delta_{h})\iota_{h}^{-1}v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}_{h}^{1/2}\|_{L(V_{h})}\|\mathcal{L}_{h}^{1/2}\iota_{h}^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}\|(1-\delta_{h})\iota_{h}^{-1}v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\|1-\delta_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{h})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)},

where we used Lemma

. Combining the two terms, we see that,

\displaystyle(\iota_{h}\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}u)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)},

for some
C>0
independent of

. This shows that the continuity constant of

does not depend on

.

It remains to show that the coercivity constant can be chosen independently of

. Also here we have for

,

\displaystyle(\iota_{h}\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,u)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=a_{h}(\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}u)-(\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,(1-\delta_{h})\iota_{h}^{-1}u)_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{h})},

and for the first term, we get,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}(a_{h}(\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}u))=\|\iota_{h}^{-1}u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma_{h})}^{2}\geq c\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2},

by Lemma
3.4
. For the second term, we have by the same arguments as in the case of the continuity constant,

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,(1-\delta_{h})\iota_{h}^{-1}u)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}|

\displaystyle\leq Ch\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2},

so that combining the two terms, yields,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}((\iota_{h}\mathcal{L}_{h}\iota_{h}^{-1}u,\iota_{h}^{-1}u)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)})\geq c\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}-Ch\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

It follows that
((I+A_{h})\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
is coercive with constant that does not depend on
h
for all
h
small.
∎

and

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

and

for any

, and so condition

Lemma B.1

Appendix C
Differential operators on surfaces

By a connected and bounded
d
-dimensional
C^{k}
-surface, we understand:

(1)

a connected and bounded
d
-dimensional hypersurface
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, where
\Gamma
is open if
\Gamma
has boundary, and closed otherwise,

(2)

it has a unit normal
\nu:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, an associated tubular neighbourhood,
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}:=\{x=x_{0}+\alpha\nu(x_{0}),\ x_{0}\in\Gamma,\ |\alpha|<\epsilon\}
, and

(3)

a signed distance function,
d\in C^{k}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
, satisfying:
|d(x)|=\inf_{y\in\Gamma}|x-y|
, and
\nabla d(x_{0}+\alpha\nu(x_{0}))=\nu(x_{0})
, for any
x_{0}\in\Gamma,\ |\alpha|<\epsilon
.

The tangent plane at every
x\in\Gamma
, denoted
T_{x}\Gamma
, is defined as the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, orthogonal to the surface normal
\nu(x)
, and the tangential derivative of a function
f:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
, denoted
\nabla_{\Gamma}f(x)\in T_{x}\Gamma
, is defined by projecting the gradient in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
of a smooth extension,
\tilde{f}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
with
\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}=f
, onto the tangent plane. That is, we set,

(25)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\Gamma}f(x)=(\underline{\partial}_{1}f,\dots,\underline{\partial}_{d+1}f):=(I-\nu(x)\nu(x)^{T})\nabla\tilde{f}(x),\quad x\in\Gamma.

To define higher order derivatives, we proceed as follows: since
\nu
has a
C^{k-1}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
-extension (take
\nabla d
), the
k
’th row of the matrix
I-\nu\nu^{T}
has a
C^{k-1}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
-extension, which we denote by
a_{k}\in C^{k-1}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon};\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. For
\tilde{f}\in C^{m}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
with
m\geq 2
, and
f:=\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}
, we can compute second order derivatives as

\displaystyle\underline{\partial}_{i}\underline{\partial}_{j}f(x):=(a_{i}(x)\cdot\nabla)(a_{j}(x)\cdot\nabla)\tilde{f}(x),\quad x\in\Gamma,\quad i,j=1,\dots,d+1,

and similarly for higher order. The second order tangential derivative above has an extension to
C^{n}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
, where
n=\min(k-2,m-2)
. For multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}
,
|\alpha|\leq\min(k,m)
, and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}
with
\beta_{j}\in\{1,\dots,d+1\}
and unique entries, an arbitrary tangential derivative of order
|\alpha|
has the representation

\displaystyle\underline{\partial}^{\alpha,\beta}f:=\underline{\partial}_{\beta_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\underline{\partial}_{\beta_{d+1}}^{\alpha_{d+1}}f,\quad x\in\Gamma.

Here,
\beta
was included since the components of tangential derivatives does not commute.

We define
L^{p}(\Gamma),p\geq 1
as the linear space of equivalence classes of measurable functions with,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}^{p}:=\int_{\Gamma}|f|^{p}\,d\sigma<\infty,

that are equal if they differ on sets of measure
0
, where
\sigma
is the surface measure of
\Gamma
. For an
f\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
, we say that
g\in L^{p}(\Gamma)
, is a weak derivative if there exists a sequence of functions,
\tilde{f}_{n}\in C^{s}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
, such that
\tilde{f}_{n}|_{\Gamma}\to f
in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
, and
\underline{\partial}_{\beta}^{\alpha}(\tilde{f}_{n}|_{\Gamma})\to g
in
L^{p}(\Gamma)
, where
\sum_{i}|\alpha_{i}|=s
.

Finally, we define
H^{s}(\Gamma)

s=1,2,\dots
as the subspace of
L^{2}(\Gamma)
, (where we identify members and their weak derivatives
\sigma
-almost everywhere), that has all weak derivatives up to order
s
in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
, and give it the norm,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}^{2}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s,\beta}\|\underline{\partial}_{\beta}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

H^{s}(\Gamma)
as defined above is complete, as it is the completion of
C^{s}(\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon})
restricted to
\Gamma
, using the
H^{s}(\Gamma)
-norm. It is a Hilbert space when given the inner product,

\displaystyle(f,g)_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}:=(f,g)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s,\beta}(\underline{\partial}_{\beta}^{\alpha}f,\underline{\partial}_{\beta}^{\alpha}g)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

We define the Laplace–Beltrami operator for
u\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
by
\Delta_{\Gamma}u:=\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}u
. The following lemma is useful for simplifying the expression for the
H^{2}(\Gamma)
-norm above.

Lemma C.1

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma\in C^{2}
be compact, and
u\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
. Then,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}+\|\Delta_{\Gamma}u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2},

for some
C>0
.

By the divergence theorem and a density argument, we have for
\Gamma\in C^{2}
compact (and therefore boundaryless),

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}uv\,d\sigma=-\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v\,d\sigma,

for any
u\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
and
v\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
. This observation combined with Lemma
C.1
yields the following estimate.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
\Gamma\in C^{2}
be compact, and
u\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
. Then,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|(I-\Delta_{\Gamma})u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

for some
C>0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
C.1
it suffices to show that,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\|\Delta_{\Gamma}u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq C\|(I-\Delta_{\Gamma})u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

We have by the divergence theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}(u-\Delta_{\Gamma}u)^{2}\,d\sigma=\int_{\Gamma}u^{2}\,d\sigma+2\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}u\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}u\,d\sigma+\int_{\Gamma}(\Delta_{\Gamma}u)^{2}\,d\sigma,

which gives the inequality.
∎

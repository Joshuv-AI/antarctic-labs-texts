Rogue waves in the massive Thirring model Journal: ******
Junchao Chen, Bo Yang, Bao-Feng Feng

Abstract
In this paper, general rogue wave solutions in the massive Thirring (MT) model are derived by using the Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy reduction method and these rational solutions are presented explicitly in terms of determinants whose matrix elements are elementary Schur polynomials. In the reduction process, three reduction conditions including one index- and two dimension-ones are proved to be consistent by only one constraint relation on parameters of tau-functions of the KP-Toda hierarchy. It is found that the rogue wave solutions in the MT model depend on two background parameters, which influence their orientation and duration. Differing from many other coupled integrable systems, the MT model only admits the rogue waves of bright-type, and the higher-order rogue waves represent the superposition of fundamental ones in which the non-reducible parameters determine the arrangement patterns of fundamental rogue waves. Particularly, the super rogue wave at each order can be achieved simply by setting all internal parameters to be zero, resulting in the amplitude of the sole huge peak of order N N being 2 ​ N + 1 2N+1 times the background. Finally, rogue wave patterns are discussed when one of the internal parameters is large. Similar to other integrable equations, the patterns are shown to be associated with the root structures of the Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomial hierarchy through a linear transformation.

Derivation of rogue wave solutions

In this section, general rogue wave solutions will be derived by using the KP hierarchy reduction method.
Prior to the tedious derivation, we list the main steps as shown in the subsequent subsections.
First, we introduce the dependent variable transformations

u=\rho_{1}\frac{g}{f^{\ast}}e^{\mathrm{i}(1+\sigma\rho_{1}\rho_{2})\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}x+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}t\right)}\,,\quad v=\rho_{2}\frac{{h}}{{f}}e^{\mathrm{i}(1+\sigma\rho_{1}\rho_{2})\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}x+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}t\right)}\,,

(9)

where
f,g,h
are complex functions, and
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are real constants.
The MT model (
1
)–(
2
) is transformed into the following bilinear equations

\displaystyle(\mathrm{i}D_{x}-\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}})g\cdot f=-\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}hf^{\ast}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{i}D_{x}-\sigma\rho^{2}_{2})f\cdot f^{\ast}=-\sigma\rho^{2}_{2}hh^{\ast}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{i}D_{t}-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}})h\cdot f^{\ast}=-\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{2}}gf\,,

(12)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{i}D_{t}-\sigma\rho^{2}_{1})f^{\ast}\cdot f=-\sigma\rho^{2}_{1}gg^{\ast}\,,

(13)

where
D
is the Hirota’s bilinear differential operator defined by

D_{s}^{n}D_{y}^{m}f\cdot g=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}-\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{\prime}}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\prime}}\right)^{m}f(y,s)g(y^{\prime},s^{\prime})|_{y=y^{\prime},s=s^{\prime}}\,.

Next, we consider a set of higher-dimensional bilinear equations in extended KP hierarchy which includes negative flows

\displaystyle(D_{x_{1}}+a)\tau_{n,k+1,l}\cdot\tau_{n+1,k,l}=a\tau_{n+1,k+1,l}\tau_{n,k,l},

(14)

\displaystyle(bD_{x_{-1}}+1)\tau_{n,k,l+1}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=\tau_{n-1,k,l+1}\tau_{n+1,k,l},

(15)

\displaystyle(aD_{t_{a}}-1)\tau_{n+1,k,l}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=-\tau_{n+1,k-1,l}\tau_{n,k+1,l},

(16)

\displaystyle(bD_{t_{b}}-1)\tau_{n+1,k,l}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=-\tau_{n+1,k,l-1}\tau_{n,k,l+1}\,,

(17)

which allow a wide class of algebraic solutions in terms of Gram determinant.
Based on these algebraic solutions, we restrict them to satisfy the dimension- and index-reduction conditions:

\displaystyle[\partial_{x_{1}}-b(a-b)\partial_{t_{b}}]\tau_{n,k,l}=C_{1}\tau_{n,k,l},

(18)

\displaystyle[\partial_{x_{-1}}+\frac{a-b}{b}\partial_{t_{a}}]\tau_{n,k,l}=C_{2}\tau_{n,k,l},

(19)

\displaystyle\tau_{n+1,k+1,l-1}=e^{{\rm i}\kappa_{0}}\tau_{n,k,l},

(20)

where

,
C_{2}
and
\kappa_{0}
are real constants.
Then such algebraic solutions satisfy the (1+1)-dimensional bilinear equations:

\displaystyle(D_{x_{1}}+a)\tau_{n,k+1,l}\cdot\tau_{n+1,k,l}=ae^{{\rm i}\kappa}\tau_{n,k,l+1}\tau_{n,k,l},

(21)

\displaystyle(bD_{x_{-1}}+1)\tau_{n,k,l+1}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=e^{-{\rm i}\kappa}\tau_{n,k+1,l}\tau_{n+1,k,l},

(22)

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{ab}{(a-b)}D_{x_{-1}}-1\right]\tau_{n+1,k,l}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=-\tau_{n+1,k-1,l}\tau_{n,k+1,l},

(23)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{a-b}D_{x_{1}}-1\right]\tau_{n+1,k,l}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=-\tau_{n+1,k,l-1}\tau_{n,k,l+1}\,.

(24)

Finally, by setting the coordinate transformations

\displaystyle(D_{x_{1}}+a)\tau_{n,k+1,l}\cdot\tau_{n+1,k,l}=a\tau_{n+1,k+1,l}\tau_{n,k,l},

(29)

\displaystyle(bD_{x_{-1}}+1)\tau_{n,k,l+1}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=\tau_{n-1,k,l+1}\tau_{n+1,k,l},

(30)

\displaystyle(aD_{t_{a}}-1)\tau_{n+1,k,l}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=-\tau_{n+1,k-1,l}\tau_{n,k+1,l},

(31)

\displaystyle(bD_{t_{b}}-1)\tau_{n+1,k,l}\cdot\tau_{n,k,l}=-\tau_{n+1,k,l-1}\tau_{n,k,l+1}\,.

(32)

Proof.

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{1}}\tau_{n,k+1,l}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}m^{(n,k,l)}_{ij}&\varphi^{(n+1,k+1,l)}_{i}\\

\displaystyle\tau_{n+1,k+1,l}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}m^{(n,k,l)}_{ij}&\varphi^{(n+1,k+1,l)}_{i}&\varphi^{(n+1,k,l)}_{i}\\

\mu_{0}\psi^{(n+1,k+1,l)}_{j}&0\end{array}\right|,

\mu_{0}\psi^{(n,k,l+1)}_{j}&0&0\end{array}\right|-\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}m^{(n,k,l)}_{ij}&\varphi^{(n+1,k,l-1)}_{i}\\

Applying the Jacobi identity of determinants to these bordered determinants, the four bilinear equations (

In order to construct the algebraic solutions, we first introduce

3.2
Dimensional reduction

According to the generalized dimensional reduction technique developed in
BYang2021TW
,
we introduce the following linear differential operators

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\partial_{x_{1}}-b(a-b)\partial_{t_{b}},\ \ \mathcal{L}_{2}=\partial_{x_{-1}}+\frac{a-b}{b}\partial_{t_{a}},

(48)

by which the dimensional reduction conditions (
18
) and (
19
) become

\mathcal{L}_{1}\tau_{n,k,l}=C_{1}\tau_{n,k,l},\ \ \mathcal{L}_{2}\tau_{n,k,l}=C_{2}\tau_{n,k,l}.

(49)

It is straightforward that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\mathcal{B}_{j}\mathcal{L}_{1}m^{(n,k,l)}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\mathcal{B}_{j}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)+\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)\right]m^{(n,k,l)},

(50)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\mathcal{B}_{j}\mathcal{L}_{2}m^{(n,k,l)}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\mathcal{B}_{j}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p)+\mathcal{Q}_{22}(q)\right]m^{(n,k,l)},

(51)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)=p-b+\frac{b(b-a)}{p-b},\ \ \mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)=q+b+\frac{b(b-a)}{q+b},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p)=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{a-b}{b(p-a)},\ \ \mathcal{Q}_{22}(q)=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{a-b}{b(q+a)}.

Using the Leibnitz rule, one has the operator relations
BYang2021TW

\mathcal{A}_{i}\mathcal{Q}_{s,1}(p)=\sum^{i}_{\mu=0}\frac{1}{\mu!}\left[\left(f_{1}\partial_{p}\right)^{\mu}\mathcal{Q}_{s,1}(p)\right]\mathcal{A}_{i-\mu},\ \ \mathcal{B}_{i}\mathcal{Q}_{s,2}(q)=\sum^{i}_{\mu=0}\frac{1}{\mu!}\left[\left(f_{2}\partial_{q}\right)^{\mu}\mathcal{Q}_{s,2}(q)\right]\mathcal{B}_{i-\mu},\ \ (s=1,2),

(52)

which suggest

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}=\sum^{i}_{\mu=0}\frac{1}{\mu!}\left[\left(f_{1}\partial_{p}\right)^{\mu}\mathcal{Q}_{s,1}(p)\right]m^{(n,k,l)}_{i-\mu,j}+\sum^{j}_{\nu=0}\frac{1}{\nu!}\left[\left(f_{2}\partial_{q}\right)^{\nu}\mathcal{Q}_{s,2}(q)\right]m^{(n,k,l)}_{i,j-\nu},\ \ \ \ (s=1,2).

(53)

Next, the specific functions
[f_{1}(p),f_{2}(q)]
and values of
(p,q)
need to be determined to guarantee that
coefficients of certain indices vanish in the above summation.
To this end, we solve the first two algebraic equations

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{11}(p)=0,\ \ \mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{12}(q)=0,

(54)

and get the following simple roots:

p_{0}=\sqrt{b(b-a)}+b,\ \ q_{0}=\sqrt{b(b-a)}-b.

(55)

It is noted that
p_{0}
and
q_{0}
are also simple roots of equations
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{21}(p)=0
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{22}(q)=0
respectively, since
\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{22}(q)
are associated with
\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)
and
\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)
through the simple relations

\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p)=\frac{a}{b}[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)-a+2b]^{-1},\ \ \mathcal{Q}_{22}(q)=\frac{a}{b}[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)+a-2b]^{-1}.

(56)

Hence, the terms with
\mu=\nu=1
on the r.h.s of (
53
) vanish at the point
(p_{0},q_{0})
.

Following the steps in the simple root’s case in Ref.
BYang2021TW
, we need to solve the differential equations

\left(f_{1}\partial_{p}\right)^{2}\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)=\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p),\ \ \left(f_{2}\partial_{q}\right)^{2}\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)=\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q).

(57)

Then the following functions can be derived

\mathcal{W}_{1}(p)=\left(\frac{p-b}{\sqrt{b(b-a)}}\right)^{\pm 1},\ \ \mathcal{W}_{2}(q)=\left(\frac{q+b}{\sqrt{b(b-a)}}\right)^{\pm 1}.

(58)

and

f_{1}(p)=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{1}(p)}{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{1}(p)}=\pm(p-b),\ \ f_{2}(q)=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{2}(p)}{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{2}(p)}=\pm(q+b).

(59)

Because the above two signs yield equivalent rogue wave solutions, we choose positive sign in the following derivation.
From the conditions (
54
) and (
57
), we can find that

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{L}_{1}m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}=\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p_{0})\sum^{i}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu=0,\\
\mu:even\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mu!}\left.m^{(n,k,l)}_{i-\mu,j}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}+\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q_{0})\sum^{j}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=0,\\
\nu:even\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\nu!}\left.m^{(n,k,l)}_{i,j-\nu}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}},

(60)

In order to prove the second dimensional reduction, noticing that
\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p)
can be expressed as a function of
\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)
, we consider a general function

F[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)]\equiv F(\mathcal{Q}_{11})
.
By using the Faà di Bruno formula and the relation
f_{1}\partial_{p}=\partial_{\ln\mathcal{W}_{1}}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\left(f_{1}\partial_{p}\right)^{l}F(\mathcal{Q}_{11})=\partial^{l}_{\ln\mathcal{W}_{1}}F(\mathcal{Q}_{11})=\sum_{m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots+lm_{l}=l}\frac{\frac{d^{\hat{m}}F(\mathcal{Q}_{11})}{d\mathcal{Q}^{\hat{m}}_{11}}\prod^{l}_{j=1}\left[(f_{1}\partial_{p})^{j}\mathcal{Q}_{11}\right]^{m_{j}}}{(l!)^{-1}\prod^{l}_{i}m_{i}!(i!)^{m_{i}}}.

(61)

where
\hat{m}=\sum^{l}_{i=1}m_{i}
.
Furthermore, by using the conditions (
54
) and (
57
), one finds that

\displaystyle\left(f_{1}\partial_{p}\right)^{l}F[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p_{0})]=0,\ \ (l\mbox{ is odd}),

(62)

\displaystyle\left(f_{1}\partial_{p}\right)^{l}F[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p_{0})]=\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2m_{2}+\cdots+lm_{l}=l,\\
m_{1}=m_{3}=\cdots=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\frac{d^{\hat{m}}F(\mathcal{Q}_{11})}{d\mathcal{Q}^{\hat{m}}_{11}}\prod^{l}_{j=1}\left[(f_{1}\partial_{p})^{j}\mathcal{Q}_{11}\right]^{m_{j}}}{(l!)^{-1}\prod^{l}_{i}m_{i}!(i!)^{m_{i}}}\right|_{p=p_{0}}\triangleq\mathcal{C}_{1,l}\left(F[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p_{0})]\right),\ \ (l\mbox{ is even}).

(63)

The similar calculation for a general function
F[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)]\equiv F(\mathcal{Q}_{12})
gives

\displaystyle\left(f_{2}\partial_{q}\right)^{l}F[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q_{0})]=0,\ \ (l\mbox{ is odd}),

(64)

\displaystyle\left(f_{2}\partial_{q}\right)^{l}F[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q_{0})]=\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2m_{2}+\cdots+lm_{l}=l,\\
m_{1}=m_{3}=\cdots=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\frac{d^{\hat{m}}F(\mathcal{Q}_{12})}{d\mathcal{Q}^{\hat{m}}_{12}}\prod^{l}_{j=1}\left[(f_{2}\partial_{q})^{j}\mathcal{Q}_{12}\right]^{m_{j}}}{(l!)^{-1}\prod^{l}_{i}m_{i}!(i!)^{m_{i}}}\right|_{p=p_{0}}\triangleq\mathcal{C}_{2,l}\left(F[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q_{0})]\right),\ \ (l\mbox{ is even}).

(65)

Applying the above formulas (
62
)-(
65
) to the specific functions
F[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)]=\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p)
and
F[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)]=\mathcal{Q}_{22}(q)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{L}_{2}m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}=\sum^{i}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu=0,\\
\mu:even\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1,\mu}[\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p_{0})]}{\mu!}\left.m^{(n,k,l)}_{i-\mu,j}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}+\sum^{j}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=0,\\
\nu:even\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2,\nu}[\mathcal{Q}_{22}(q_{0})]}{\nu!}\left.m^{(n,k,l)}_{i,j-\nu}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}.

(66)

On the r.h.s. of above equation, the coefficients in the first term of two summations are
\mathcal{C}_{1,0}[\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p_{0})]=\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p_{0})
and
\mathcal{C}_{2,0}[\mathcal{Q}_{22}(q_{0})]=\mathcal{Q}_{22}(q_{0})
, respectively.

Next, we restrict the general determinant (
47
) to

\tau_{n,k,l}=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\left(\left.m_{2i-1,2j-1}^{(n,k,l)}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}\right).

(67)

By using the contiguity relations (
60
) and (
66
) as in Ref.
Ohta2012NLS
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}\tau_{n,k,l}=\left[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p_{0})+\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q_{0})\right]N\tau_{n,k,l}=4\sqrt{b(b-a)}N\tau_{n,k,l},

(68)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}\tau_{n,k,l}=\left[\mathcal{Q}_{21}(p_{0})+\mathcal{Q}_{22}(q_{0})\right]N\tau_{n,k,l}=-\frac{4\sqrt{b(b-a)}}{ab}N\tau_{n,k,l},

(69)

which imply that the tau function (
67
) satisfies the dimensional reduction conditions (
49
).

3.3
Index reduction

Similar to the dimensional reduction procedure in the above subsection, we introduce the difference operator

\Delta\tau_{n,k,l}=\tau_{n+1,k+1,l-1},

(70)

so that the index reduction condition (
20
) becomes

\Delta\tau_{n,k,l}=e^{{\rm i}\kappa_{0}}\tau_{n,k,l}.

(71)

From the definition of element
m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}
, we can find

\Delta m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\mathcal{B}_{j}\Delta m^{(n,k,l)}=\mathcal{A}_{i}\mathcal{B}_{j}\left[\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p)\times\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q)\right]m^{(n,k,l)},

(72)

where

\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p)=\frac{p(p-a)}{p-b}=\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)-a+2b,\ \ \mathcal{Q}_{32}(q)=-\frac{q+b}{q(q+a)}=-[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)+a-2b]^{-1},

(73)

which yield

\left.\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{31}(p)\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}=0,\ \ \left.\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}_{32}(q)\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}=0.

(74)

Furthermore, applying above formulas (
62
)-(
65
) to the specific functions
F[\mathcal{Q}_{11}(p)]=\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p)
and
F[\mathcal{Q}_{12}(q)]=\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q)
and utilizing general operator relations (
52
), we arrive at

\left.\Delta m_{ij}^{(n,k,l)}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}=\left[\sum^{i}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu=0,\\
\mu:even\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1,\mu}[\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})]}{\mu!}\right]\times\left[\sum^{j}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=0,\\
\nu:even\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2,\nu}[\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})]}{\nu!}\right]\left.m^{(n,k,l)}_{i-\mu,j-\nu}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}.

(75)

It is easy to see that the coefficients of the first term in above two summations are
\mathcal{C}_{1,0}[\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})]=\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})
and
\mathcal{C}_{2,0}[\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})]=\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})
.
Thus, according to the calculation in Ref.
BYang2020DNLS
, one has the following matrix relation

\left(\left.\Delta m_{2i-1,2j-1}^{(n,k,l)}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}=L\left(\left.m_{2i-1,2j-1}^{(n,k,l)}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}U,

(76)

where
L
and
U
are following lower and upper triangular matrices with diagonal entries as
\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})
and
\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})
, respectively.

\displaystyle L=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})&0&\cdots&0\\
\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1,2}[\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})]}{2!}&\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1,2N-2}[\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})]}{(2N-2)!}&\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1,2N-4}[\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})]}{(2N-4)!}&\cdots&\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})\\
\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle U=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})&\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2,2}[\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})]}{2!}&\cdots&\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2,2N-2}[\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})]}{(2N-2)!}\\
0&\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})&\cdots&\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2,2N-4}[\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})]}{(2N-4)!}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})\\
\end{array}\right).

Taking determinant on both sides of equation (
76
), we have

\Delta\tau_{n,k,l}=\left[\mathcal{Q}_{31}(p_{0})\mathcal{Q}_{32}(q_{0})\right]^{N}\tau_{n,k,l}=\Omega^{N}_{0}\tau_{n,k,l},

(79)

with
\Omega_{0}=-\frac{p_{0}(p_{0}-a)}{q_{0}(q_{0}+a)}=-\frac{[\sqrt{b(b-a)}+b][\sqrt{b(b-a)}+b-a]}{[\sqrt{b(b-a)}-b][\sqrt{b(b-a)}-b+a]}
.
If we further restrict
a
and
b
to be pure imaginary parameters,
\Omega_{0}
becomes a complex number whose modulus is one.
Hence, by setting
e^{{\rm i}\kappa}=\Omega^{N}_{0}
, the index reduction condition (
71
) is proved.

3.4
Complex conjugacy condition

As mentioned previously, by taking
a
and
b
as the following pure imaginary numbers

a={\rm i}\alpha,\ \ b={\rm i}\alpha(1+\sigma\rho_{1}\rho_{2}),

(80)

with the real constant
\alpha
, the roots (
55
) can be rewritten as

p_{0}=q^{*}_{0}=\sqrt{-\sigma\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\alpha^{2}(1+\sigma\rho_{1}\rho_{2})}+{\rm i}\alpha(1+\sigma\rho_{1}\rho_{2}),

(81)

which implies that the condition for the existence of rogue wave solution:
-1<\rho_{1}\rho_{2}<0
for
\sigma=1
or
0<\rho_{1}\rho_{2}<1
for
\sigma=-1
need to be satisfied.
Besides, by imposing the parameter constraint
\eta_{0}=\xi^{*}_{0}
and noticing that the coordinate transformations
x_{1}=\frac{\rho_{2}}{\alpha\rho_{1}}x
and
x_{-1}=-\frac{\rho_{1}\alpha(1+\sigma\rho_{1}\rho_{2})}{\rho_{2}}t
are real,
one can find that

\left.\left[m_{i,j}^{(n,k,l)}\right]^{*}\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}}=\left.\left[m_{j,i}^{(-n-1,-k,-l)}\right]\right|_{p=p_{0},q=q_{0}},

6
Summary and Discussions

In this paper, we have constructed the general rogue wave solutions in the MT model by using the KP hierarchy reduction method.
These solutions are represented in terms of determinants in which elements are given by elementary Schur-polynomials. In the process of constructing these solutions, we proved that two dimension-reduction conditions and one index-reduction condition are satisfied simulteneously under the same constraint of parameters.
The local structure analyses show that two background parameters influence the orientation and duration but they do not affect the heights of peak in rogue waves.
It is also shown that compared with other integrable systems, there exist no additional parameters in rogue wave solutions of the MT model, and hence only bright-type rogue wave appears.
The
N
-the order rogue waves correspond to the superposition of
N(N+1)/2
fundamental ones, and their arrangement patterns are determined by the values of non-reducible parameters.
By setting all internal parameters to be zero, we obtain the super rogue wave of
N
-th order in which the sole huge peak is located at the center and its maximum amplitude is
2N+1
times the background.
Finally, we have discussed rogue wave patterns when one of the internal parameters is large.
It is shown that similar to other integrable systems, these patterns are linked to the root structure of the Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomial hierarchy through a linear transformation.

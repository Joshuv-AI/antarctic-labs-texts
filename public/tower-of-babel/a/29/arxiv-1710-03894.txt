Abstract
. Calculi of string diagrams are increasingly used to present the syntax and algebraic structure of various families of circuits, including signal flow graphs, electrical circuits and quantum processes. In many such approaches, the semantic interpretation for diagrams is given in terms of relations or corelations (generalised equivalence relations) of some kind. In this paper we show how semantic categories of both relations and corelations can be characterised as colimits of simpler categories. This modular perspective is important as it simplifies the task of giving a complete axiomatisation for semantic equivalence of string diagrams. Moreover, our general result unifies various theorems that are independently found in literature and are relevant for program semantics, quantum computation and control theory.

\par\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{(Co)algebras over a (co)monad}
It is of interest to identify under which conditions our universal construction of $\mathsf{Rel}(\mathcal{C})$ lifts to relations over $\mathcal{C}^{T}$, the category of algebras
of some monad $T\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$.
\par\begin{prop}Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a complete and cocomplete regular category that obeys
Assumption~\ref{ass:threlations} with respect to its (regular epi, mono)
factorisation system. Further assume that every regular epi splits. Next, let
$T$ be a monad on $\mathcal{C}$ such that $T$ and $T^{2}$ preserve pushouts of regular
epis. Then the category $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ of algebras over $T$ is a complete and
cocomplete regular category that obeys Assumption~\ref{ass:threlations} with
respect to its (regular epi, mono) factorisation system.
\end{prop}\par Note that if $\mathcal{C}$ is a complete and cocomplete category, then regularity is
just the statement that regular epis are stable under pullback, and
Assumption~\ref{ass:threlations} is just the statement that the canonical map
given by the pullback of the pushout of a span of regular epis is again a
regular epi.
\par\begin{proof}The completeness, cocompleteness, and regularity
of $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ under the above conditions is a standard result, see e.g. \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Bor94}{}{}, Th. 4.3.5]}. In brief, the completeness of $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ is
easily inherited from the completeness of $\mathcal{C}$. The cocompleteness requires
the regular epis to split and the existence of products. This allows explicit
construction of coequalizers in $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ and, using the existence of
coproducts in $\mathcal{C}$, this immediately implies the cocompleteness of
$\mathcal{C}^{T}$ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Bor94}{}{}, Prop. 4.3.4]}). The stability of regular epis
under pullback in $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ follows from the fact that the forgetful functor
$U\colon\mathcal{C}^{T}\to\mathcal{C}$ preserves and reflects both pullbacks and regular
epis. Thus, since regular epis are stable under pullback in $\mathcal{C}$, the same
holds true in $\mathcal{C}^{T}$.
\par It then remains to show that, in $\mathcal{C}^{T}$, the canonical map given by the
pullback of the pushout of a span of regular epis is again a regular epi.
Since $T$ preserves pushouts of regular epis, such a pushout in $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ may
simply be obtained by computing the pushout $P$ of the underlying maps in
$\mathcal{C}$, and using the universal property of the $T$-image of this pushout to
get a map $c\colon TP\to P$. Since $T^{2}$ also preserves pushouts of regular
epis, it can be shown that this map $c$ is in fact an algebra, and the pushout
in $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Bor94}{}{}, Prop. 4.3.2]}). On the other hand, as limits in
a category of algebras, pullbacks in $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ can easily be computed via
pullbacks in $\mathcal{C}$. Thus the canonical map in question is simply the
canonical map in $\mathcal{C}$ lifted to a map of algebras, and hence is again a
regular epi. This proves the claim.
\end{proof}\par We may dualise Proposition \ref{prop:algebrasmonad} to the case of corelations
in the category of coalgebras over a comonad; in this case we assume our
category is coregular, and that it is the regular monos that split in $\mathcal{C}$
and whose pullbacks are preserved by the comonad $T$ and by $T^{2}$.
\par Note also that the assumptions above can be varied or weakened to still obtain
that the category of (regular epi, mono) relations in a category of algebras can
be constructed as a pushout. For example, it is straightforward to relax
cocompleteness to, say, finite cocompleteness, or to put conditions on the
properties of $\mathcal{C}^{T}$ instead of on $\mathcal{C}$ and $T$.\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Examples}
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Equivalence Relations}
\par Our first example concerns the construction of equivalence relations starting
from injective functions. For $n\in\mathbb{N}$, write $\overline{n}$ for the set
$\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}$, and $\uplus$ for the disjoint union of sets. We fix a prop
$\mathsf{ER}$ whose arrows $n\to m$ are the equivalence relations on

\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Partial Equivalence Relations}
\par Partial equivalence relations (PERs) are common structures in program semantics, which date back to the seminal work of Scott~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Scott1975-datatypeslattices}{}{}]} and recently revamped in the study of quantum computations (e.g., \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Jacobs_quantumPER,HasuoH11_GOIQuantumPER}{}{}]}). Our approach yields a characterisation for the prop $\mathsf{PER}$ whose arrows $n\to m$ are PERs on $\overline{n}\uplus\overline{m}$, with composition as in $\mathsf{ER}$. The ingredients of the construction generalise Example~\ref{ex:er} from total to partial maps. Instead of $\mathsf{F}$ one starts with $\mathsf{PF}$, the prop of partial functions, which has a factorisation system involving the sub-prop of partial surjections and the sub-prop of injections. Another ``degenerate'' case is the one of lattices. Recall that a lattice is a
poset with binary joins and meets. Just as for any pre-order, one can see a
lattice $L$ as a category: objects are the lattice elements and there is an
arrow from $a\in L$ to $b\in L$ precisely when $a\leq b$ in the lattice.
\par Let us now check Assumption~\ref{ass:thcorelations} for $L$. First, $L$ has
pushouts and pullbacks: they are just given by joins and meets. For the second
and third requirement, we need to fix a factorisation system. Above we noticed
that the factorisation system $(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{I})$ works for any $\mathcal{C}$; in the case
of a lattice, we may also consider the factorisation $(\mathcal{I},\mathcal{C})$. Indeed,
because there is at most one arrow between any two objects of $L$, every arrow
is both an epi and a mono, and thus every subcategory of $L$ is a subcategory of
the monos in $L$. Also, notice that the only isomorphisms are the identities;
thus the subcategory $\mathcal{I}$ of isomorphisms is just the discrete sub-category $|L|$ formed by the objects of $L$, with their identity arrows. Choosing the factorisation system
$(|L|,L)$, and letting $\mathcal{A}=L$, then gives data that satisfies
Assumption~\ref{ass:thcorelations}.
\par(Co)spans for a lattice turn out to be familiar order-theoretic notions. The set of arrows from $a\in L$ to $b\in L$ in $\mathsf{Cospan}(L)$ is equal to the \emph{upper
set} of $a\vee b$, and composition is given by join. Similarly, the set of arrows from $a\in L$ to $b\in L$ in $\mathsf{Span}(L)$ is equal to the \emph{lower set} of $a\wedge b$, and
composition is given by meet. Then the category $\mathsf{Corel}(L)$ is equivalent to the
terminal category $\mathbf{1}$; any two cospans in $L$ are considered equivalent as
$(|L|,L)$-corelations.

there is a unique morphism between any two objects. That is to say, $\mathsf{Corel}(L)\cong\mathbf{1}$.
\par\par Our example can be generalised from lattices to arbitrary posets with pushouts
and pullbacks. It is straightforward to show that a poset $P$ with all pushouts
and pullbacks need not itself be a lattice, but that elements $a$ and $b$ have a
join if and only if they have a meet, and moreover if and only if they have an
upper bound or lower bound. This means that $P$ must be a coproduct of lattices.

the same (identity, any morphism) factorisation system $(|P|,P)$ and apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:corelations}. Here the homsets of $\mathsf{Cospan}(P)$ and $\mathsf{Span}(P)$
can still be described as upper sets of joins and lower sets of meets, with the
caveat that the homset is empty when the relevant join or meet does not exist.
The category of $(|P|,P)$-corelations is then equivalent to the coproduct of $n$
copies of the terminal category, and Theorem~\ref{thm:corelations} asserts the usual pushout
square. Thus in this case the pushout of the categories of spans and cospans in
$P$ results in counting the number of lattice summands, or `connected
components', of $P$.

\par We now consider an example for the abelian case: the prop $\mathsf{SV}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ whose arrows $n\to m$ are $\mathsf{k}$-linear subspaces of $\mathsf{k}^{n}\times\mathsf{k}^{m}$, for a field $\mathsf{k}$. Composition in $\mathsf{SV}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ is relational: $V\,;\,W=\{(v,w)\mid\exists u.(v,u)\in V,(u,w)\in W\}$. Interest in $\mathsf{SV}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ is motivated by various recent applications. We mention the case where $\mathsf{k}$ is the field of Laurent series, in which $\mathsf{SV}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ constitutes a denotational semantics for signal flow graphs~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Bonchi2014b,BaezErbele-CategoriesInControl,Bonchi2015,BonchiSZ17}{}{}]}, and the case $\mathsf{k}=\mathsf{Z}_{2}$, in which $\mathsf{SV}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ is isomorphic to the phase-free ZX-calculus, an algebra for quantum observables~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CoeckeDuncanZX2011,BialgAreFrob14}{}{}]}.
\par Now, in order to apply our construction, note that $\mathsf{SV}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ is isomorphic to $\mathsf{Rel}(\mathsf{Vect}_{\scriptscriptstyle k})$, where $\mathsf{Vect}_{\scriptscriptstyle k}$ is the abelian prop whose arrows $n\to m$ are the linear maps of type $\mathsf{k}^{n}\to\mathsf{k}^{m}$ (the monoidal product is by direct sum). This follows from the observation that subspaces of $\mathsf{k}^{n}\times\mathsf{k}^{m}$ of dimension $z$ correspond to mono linear maps from $\mathsf{k}^{z}$ to $\mathsf{k}^{n}\times\mathsf{k}^{m}$, whence to jointly mono spans $n\xleftarrow{}z\xrightarrow{}m$ in $\mathsf{Vect}_{\scriptscriptstyle k}$.

\emph{corelations} over $\mathsf{Vect}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$. This representation can be understood
by recalling the 1-1 correspondence between subspaces of $\mathsf{k}^{n}\times\mathsf{k}^{m}$ and (solution sets of) homogeneous systems of equations $Mv=0$,
where $M$ is a $z\times(n+m)$ matrix. Writing the block decomposition $M=(M_{1}\>|\>-M_{2})$, where $M_{1}$ is a $z\times n$ matrix and $M_{2}$ a $z\times m$ matrix, this is the same as solutions to $M_{1}v_{1}=M_{2}v_{2}$. These systems
then yield jointly epi cospans $n\xrightarrow{M_{1}}z\xleftarrow{M_{2}}m$ in $\mathsf{Vect}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$,
that is, corelations.
\par\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Linear Corelations over a Principal Ideal Domain}
\par We now consider the generalisation of the linear case from fields to principal
ideal domains (PIDs). In order to form a prop, we need to restrict our attention
to finitely-dimensional \emph{free} modules over a PID $\mathsf{R}$. The symmetric
monoidal category of such modules and module homomorphisms, with monoidal
product by direct sum, is equivalent to the prop $\mathsf{FMod}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{R}}$ whose arrows $n\to m$ are $\mathsf{R}$-module homomorphisms $\mathsf{R}^{n}\to\mathsf{R}^{m}$ or, equivalently,

$\mathsf{FMod}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{R}}$ is not abelian. However, it is still finitely bicomplete and has a
costable (epi, split mono)-factorisation system. Note that the fact that the ring $\mathsf{R}$ is a PID matters for the existence of pullbacks, as it is necessary for
submodules of free $\mathsf{R}$-modules to be free---pushouts exist by self-duality
of $\mathsf{FMod}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{R}}$.

\par\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Relations of Algebras over a Field}
\par We give a simple example of Proposition~\ref{prop.algebras}. The category
$\mathsf{VECT}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ of vector spaces and linear maps over some field $\mathsf{k}$, is a
complete, cocomplete, regular category. Moreover, every epi splits: every
surjective linear map has a section.
\par Consider the monad $T\colon\mathsf{VECT}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}\to\mathsf{VECT}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ for algebras over
$\mathsf{k}$ (that is, for vector spaces equipped with a bilinear multiplication).
This maps a vector space $V$ to the direct sum $\bigoplus_{\{n\in\mathbb{N}\}}V^{\otimes n}$. Since each map $V\mapsto V\otimes\dots\otimes V$ preserves
both epis and pushouts of epis, so does their coproduct $T$. Note also that all
epis in $\mathsf{VECT}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{k}}$ are regular. This means both $T$ and $T^{2}$ preserve
pushouts of regular epis, and hence Proposition~\ref{prop.algebras} applies.
Thus we can construct the category of relations between algebras over $\mathsf{k}$
as a pushout of spans and cospans.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Corelations of Coalgebras}
\par As mentioned, Proposition \ref{prop:algebrasmonad} dualises to yield a universal
construction for corelations of coalgebras for a comonad $T$. The dualised
assumptions require that $T$ and $T^{2}$ preserve pullbacks of monos. This is true
for instance when $T$ is a polynomial endofunctor, as it is the case for
comonads encountered in the setting of functional programming like the
environment comonad $A\mapsto A\times E$, see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{TarmoComonads}{}{}]}. Another
example is when $T$ is the comonad defined by a geometric morphism to a coregular
topos, such as $\mathsf{Set}$.
\par An interesting class of case studies for this construction stems from the analysis of state-based systems, which are typically formalised as coalgebras $X\to FX$ for an endofunctor $F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{JacobsBookCoalgebra}{}{}]}. When $F$ is accessible and $\mathcal{C}$ is locally finitely presentable, one can construct the cofree comonad $T\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$ over $F$ through a so-called terminal sequence~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Worrell99}{}{}]}. The comonad $T$ yields an operational semantics for $F$-systems: given $X\to FX$, it maps $X$ to the carrier $\Omega$ of the cofree $F$-coalgebra $\Omega\to F\Omega$ on $X$.
\par Now, because $T$ is cofree, the condition of preserving pullbacks of monos required in Proposition~\ref{prop:algebrasmonad} can actually be checked on $F$. Indeed, the image under $T$ of a pullback diagram can be computed pointwise on its terminal sequence, which essentially involves repeated applications of the functor $F$. The condition is met for instance for coalgebras for the finite double-powerset functor $\mathcal{PP}\colon\mathsf{Set}\to\mathsf{Set}$: this functor has been used to model ground logic programs \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{KomendantskayaMP10,KomendantskayaP16,BZ-LogProg2}{}{}]}, with operational semantics given by the comonad mapping a formula to its execution tree in the logic program.
\par Given that bisimulation of coalgebras can be expressed both in terms of spans and cospans \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{StatonBisimulation}{}{}]}, it is an interesting question --- to be explored in future work --- whether corelations also model a sensible notion of behavioural equivalence, for well-chosen examples.
\par\par\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Concluding remarks}
\par In summary, we have shown that categories of (co)relations may, under certain
general conditions, be constructed as pushouts of categories of spans and
cospans. In particular, especially since categories of spans and cospans can
frequently be axiomatised using distributive laws, this offers a method of
constructing axiomatisations of categories of (co)relations. Our results extend
to the setting of props, and more generally symmetric monoidal categories.
Moreover, these results are readily illustrated, unifying a diverse series of
examples drawn from algebraic theories, program semantics, quantum computation,
and control theory.
\par Looking forward, note that in the monoidal case the resulting (co)relation
category is a so-named hypergraph category: each object is equipped with a
special commutative Frobenius structure. Hypergraph categories are of
increasing interest for modelling network-style diagrammatic languages, and
recent work, such as that of decorated corelations \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Fon16}{}{}]} or the
generalized relations of Marsden and Genovese \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{MG17}{}{}]}, gives precise methods for
tailoring constructions of these categories towards chosen applications. Our
example on relations in categories of algebras for a monad
(Subsection~\ref{ssec.monadalg}) hints at general methods for showing the
present universal construction applies to these novel examples. We leave this as
an avenue for future work.

symmetric monoidal category. Moreover, the quotient functor $\Gamma{\colon}\mathsf{Cospan}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathsf{Corel}(\mathcal{C})$ is a strict monoidal functor.
\end{prop}\begin{proof}The first task is to show that $\mathsf{Corel}(\mathcal{C})$ is indeed a symmetric monoidal
category. We show that $\oplus$ induces a monoidal product, which we shall also
write $\oplus$, on $\mathsf{Corel}(\mathcal{C})$. Given two corelations $a$ and $b$, with
representatives $\xrightarrow{f}\xleftarrow{g}$ and $\xrightarrow{h}\xleftarrow{k}$ we define their monoidal
product $a\oplus b$ to be the corelation represented by the cospan $\xrightarrow{f\oplus h}\xleftarrow{g\oplus k}$. This is well defined: given $\xrightarrow{f^{\prime}}\xleftarrow{g^{\prime}}$,
$\xrightarrow{h^{\prime}}\xleftarrow{k^{\prime}}$ and $m_{1},m_{2}$ in $\mathcal{M}$ such that $f^{\prime}=f;m_{1}$, $g^{\prime}=g;m_{1}$, $h^{\prime}=h;m_{2}$, $k^{\prime}=k;m_{2}$, the monoidality of $\oplus$ in $\mathcal{C}$ implies
$f^{\prime}\oplus g^{\prime}=(f\oplus g);(m_{1}\oplus m_{2})$ and $h^{\prime}\oplus k^{\prime}=(h\oplus k);(m_{1}\oplus m_{2})$. Since $\mathcal{M}$ is closed under $\oplus$, $m_{1}\oplus m_{2}$ again lies in
$\oplus$, and the product corelation is independent of choice of
representatives.
\par To show that $\Gamma$ is a strict monoidal functor we just need to
check $\Gamma(a\oplus b)=\Gamma a\oplus\Gamma b$,
where $a$ and $b$ are cospans. This follows immediately from the definition:
the monoidal product of the corelations that two cospans represent is by
definition the corelation represented by the monoidal product of the two
cospans.
\end{proof}\par\begin{prop}$\Pi{\colon}\mathsf{Span}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathsf{Corel}(\mathcal{C})$ is a strict monoidal
functor.
\end{prop}\begin{proof}Again, we just need to check that $\Pi(a\oplus b)=\Pi a\oplus\Pi b$. For this we need that the monoidal product preserves pushouts.

$$X\oplus X^{\prime}\longrightarrow(X+_{N}Y)\oplus(X^{\prime}+_{N^{\prime}}Y^{\prime})\longleftarrow Y\oplus Y^{\prime}.$$These cospans are isomorphic by the fact $\oplus$ preserves pushouts, and
hence represent the same corelation.

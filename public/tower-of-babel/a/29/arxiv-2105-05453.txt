Toric orbifolds associated with partitioned weight polytopes in classical types
Tatsuya Horiguchi, Mikiya Masuda, John Shareshian, Jongbaek Song

Abstract
. Given a root system Φ \Phi of type A n A_{n} , B n B_{n} , C n C_{n} , or D n D_{n} in Euclidean space E E , let W W be the associated Weyl group. For a point p ∈ E p\in E not orthogonal to any of the roots in Φ \Phi , we consider the W W -permutohedron P W P_{W} , which is the convex hull of the W W -orbit of p p . The representation of W W on the rational cohomology ring H ∗ ​ ( X Φ ) H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi}) of the toric variety X Φ X_{\Phi} associated to (the normal fan to) P W P_{W} has been studied by various authors. Let { s 1 , … , s n } \{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\} be a complete set of simple reflections in W W . For K ⊆ [ n ] K\subseteq[n] , let W K W_{K} be the standard parabolic subgroup of W W generated by { s k : k ∈ K } \{s_{k}:k\in K\} . We show that the fixed subring H ∗ ​ ( X Φ ) W K H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi})^{W_{K}} is isomorphic to the cohomology ring of the toric variety X Φ ​ ( K ) X_{\Phi}(K) associated to a polytope obtained by intersecting P W P_{W} with half-spaces bounded by reflecting hyperplanes for the given generators of W K W_{K} . By a result of Balibanu–Crooks, the cohomology rings H ∗ ​ ( X Φ ​ ( K ) ) H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi}(K)) are isomorphic with cohomology rings of certain regular Hessenberg varieties.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Phi
be a crystallographic root system in Euclidean space
E
. The linear hyperplanes orthogonal to the roots in
\Phi
determine a complete fan
\Delta_{\Phi}
in
E
, which determines in turn a toric variety
X_{\Phi}
. The action of the associated finite reflection group
W
on
E
preserves
\Delta_{\Phi}
and so gives rise to an action of
W
on
X_{\Phi}
, which induces a (graded) linear representation of
W
on the rational cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi};{\mathbb{Q}})
. This representation has been studied when
\Phi
is of type
A
by Procesi in
[
Pro90
]
, by Stanley in
[
Sta95
]
and by Stembridge in
[
Ste94
]
; and when
\Phi
is an arbitrary irreducible system by Stembridge in
[
Ste92
]
and by Dolgachev and Lunts in
[
DL94
]
. The ring structure of
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi};{\mathbb{Q}})
is determined by Danilov’s Theorem (see
[
Dan78
,
Jur80
]
), and was studied further by Klyachko in
[
Kly85
]
and by Abe in
[
Abe15
]
. In
[
Blu15
]
, Blume provided a result that involves both the
W
-representation and the ring structure. Namely, he shows that if
\Phi
is of type
A
,
B
, or
C
, then the quotient space
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X_{\Phi}/W
is a toric orbifold. So, the fixed point subring

(1.1)

H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi};{\mathbb{Q}})^{W}\cong H^{\ast}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})

is determined by the combinatorial structure of some complete fan. We observe that, in general, the usual torus action on
X_{\Phi}
does not descend to an action on the quotient
X_{\Phi}/W
, since this action does not commute with that of
W
on
X_{\Phi}
.

Here we generalize (
1.1
). We consider the case where
\Phi
belongs to any of the infinite families of irreducible root systems of types
A,B,C
or
D
. Let
\Sigma=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
be a fixed set of simple roots in
\Phi
. For each
\alpha\in\Phi
, let
s_{\alpha}
be the reflection through the hyperplane orthogonal to
\alpha
. We write
s_{i}
for
s_{\alpha_{i}}
. So,
W=\langle s_{i}\mid i\in[n]\rangle
. Given
K\subseteq[n]
, we define as usual the parabolic subgroup

W_{K}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\langle s_{i}\mid i\in K\rangle.

We study here the fixed point subring
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi};{\mathbb{Q}})^{W_{K}}
. We will see that the ring is isomorphic with the cohomology ring of the toric variety associated to a fan determined by
\Phi
and
K
, and describe this fan explicitly.

More precisely, the fan
\Delta_{\Phi}
is the normal fan to a simple lattice polytope, the
W
-permutohedron

P_{W}
. One obtains
P_{W}
by choosing a point
p
in the interior of one of the maximal cones of
\Delta_{\Phi}
and taking the convex hull of the
W
-orbit
W(p)
. (We will make particular choices for
p
that suit our purposes.) For each
k\in[n]
, we define
H(k)
to be the hyperplane orthogonal to
\alpha_{k}
,
and define the closed half-space

H(k)^{\leq}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in E\mid\alpha_{k}^{\mathsf{v}}(x)\leq 0\}.

(Here
\alpha_{k}^{\mathsf{v}}
is the coroot associated to
\alpha_{k}
.)

Now, given
K\subseteq[n]
, we define the polytope

P_{W}(K)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}P_{W}\cap\bigcap_{k\in K}H(k)^{\leq}.

We will prove that
P_{W}(K)
is a simple rational polytope. We define
X_{\Phi}(K)
to be the projective toric variety associated to (the normal fan of)
P_{W}(K)
. Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\Phi
is an irreducible root system of type
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
, or
D_{n}
and
K\subseteq[n]
, then the rings
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi};{\mathbb{Q}})^{W_{K}}
and
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi}(K);{\mathbb{Q}})
are isomorphic.

We will prove Theorem
1.1
when
\Phi
is of type A in Section
5
, and explain how to adjust the proof in types B and C and in type D, in Appendices
A
and
B
, respectively.

The variety
X_{\Phi}
is a regular semisimple Hessenberg variety, as defined and studied by De Mari, Procesi and Shayman in
[
DMPS92
]
. In
[
Tym08
]
, Tymoczko defines a representation of
W
on
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi};{\mathbb{Q}})
, known as the dot representation.
Moreover, one can see from
[
Tym08
,
Tef11
]

that the dot representation and the representation determined by the action of
W
on
\Delta_{\Phi}
are the same. It was shown by Balibanu–Crooks in
[
BalCro20
]
and later by Vilonen–Xue in
[
VX21
]
that, for an arbitrary regular semisimple Hessenberg variety
X
in a flag variety
{\mathcal{B}}
associated to
\Phi
, and arbitrary
K\subseteq\Sigma
, there is a regular Hessenberg variety
Y_{K}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}
such that the graded
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebras

H^{\ast}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})^{W_{K}}
and
H^{\ast}(Y_{K};{\mathbb{Q}})
are isomorphic. Therefore, in the case at hand, there is some
Y_{K}
such that

(1.2)

H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi}(K);{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H^{\ast}(Y_{K};{\mathbb{Q}}).

However, it is not necessarily the case that we can choose such a
Y_{K}
that is isomorphic with
X_{\Phi}(K)
, as we will see in Section
6
.

Work of Danilov in
[
Dan78
]
and that of Jurkiewicz in
[
Jur80
]
tells us that the face numbers of
P_{W}(K)
determine the betti numbers of
X_{\Phi}(K)
, and vice versa. (See Theorem
2.1
below.) This allows us two approaches to understanding these face numbers. First, Stembridge gives in
[
Ste92
]
essentially combinatorial formulas for the characters of the representations under consideration here. Combining these formulas with Theorem
1.1
, we obtain essentially combinatorial formulas for the face numbers. Second, Precup gives in
[
Pre18
]
combinatorial formulas for the betti numbers of regular Hessenberg varieties. Combining Precup’s formulas with Theorem
2.1
and (
1.2
), we obtain a priori different formulas.

We do not consider the exceptional crystallographic root systems in this paper. We do expect that the analogue of Theorem
1.1
holds for these systems also. We mention this and other open questions in Section
8
.

2.
Toric orbifolds

We recall here the presentation given by Danilov and by Jurkiewicz for the cohomology of a toric variety associated to the normal fan of a rational, simple polytope. (See for example
[
Ewa86
, Chapter VII.3]
for a discussion of the relation between fans and toric varieties.) We discuss in particular the case where the fan in question arises from a root system.
We refer the reader to
[
Hum72
]
and
[
Hum90
]
for relevant Lie-theoretic terminology.

2.1.
Cohomology rings of toric orbifolds

A rational polytope
P
(with respect to a lattice
M
) is
simple
if, for every positive integer
k
, every codimension-
k
face of
P
is the intersection of
k
facets. The normal fan
\Delta(P)
to a simple polytope is called a
simplicial fan
, and the corresponding toric variety
X(P)
is a
toric orbifold
.

Next we record a version that suits our purposes of the results of Danilov and of Jurkiewicz mentioned above. We denote by
N
the dual lattice of
M
and denote by
\left<~,~\right>
the standard pairing between
M
and
N
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
Dan78
, Theorem 10.8 and Remark 10.9]

Let
P
be a simple polytope, rational with respect to the lattice
M
.
Let
X(P)
be the toric orbifold associated to
P
and let
\mathcal{F}(P)
be the set of facets of
P
. For each
F\in\mathcal{F}(P)
, let
\nu(F)\in N
be an (inward) normal vector to
F
.
Then

H^{\ast}(X(P))\cong{\mathbb{Q}}[\tau_{F}\mid F\in\mathcal{F}(P)]/\mathcal{K},

where
\deg\tau_{F}=2
and
\mathcal{K}
is the ideal generated by

(1)

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}\left<u,\nu(F)\right>\tau_{F}
for all
u\in M
; and

(2)

\prod_{k=1}^{r}\tau_{F_{i_{k}}}
for all
F_{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\dots,F_{i_{r}}\in\mathcal{F}(P)
with
F_{i_{1}}\cap F_{i_{2}}\cap\dots\cap F_{i_{r}}=\emptyset
.

2.2.
Toric varieties associated to Weyl chambers

Let
E
be an
n
-dimensional Euclidean space with an inner product
(\ ,\ )
.
Let
\Phi
be a root system in
E
.
Let
L(\Phi)\subset E
be the root lattice of
\Phi
and
\hat{L}(\Phi^{\mathsf{v}})\subset E^{*}
its dual lattice which is called the coweight lattice of
\Phi
.
Fix a set of simple roots
\Sigma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}\subset E
and its dual basis
\Sigma^{\ast}=\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\}\subset E^{*}
. Then
L(\Phi)
and
\hat{L}(\Phi^{\mathsf{v}})
are the lattices generated by
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\ast}
, respectively.

Let
W\leq GL(E)
be the Weyl group, generated by reflections through hyperplanes orthogonal to simple roots. We embed
W
in
GL(E^{\ast})
, by the usual dual representation of
W
on
E^{*}
.
We now consider the fan
\Delta_{\Phi}
in
E^{\ast}
with maximal cones

\sigma_{u}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{cone}(u^{-1}\omega_{1},\ldots,u^{-1}\omega_{n})\ \ \ \textrm{for}\ u\in W.

The set of primitive generators for the
1
-dimensional cones in
\Delta_{\Phi}
is

\Phi^{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\displaystyle\bigcup_{u\in W}\{u^{-1}\omega_{1},u^{-1}\omega_{2},\ldots,u^{-1}\omega_{n}\}.

Since the set
\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\}
forms a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
\hat{L}(\Phi^{\mathsf{v}})
, the fan
\Delta_{\Phi}
is nonsingular. Hence the associated toric variety
X_{\Phi}
is smooth.
For simplicity, we will write
\Delta
and
X
for
\Delta_{\Phi}
and
X_{\Phi}
, respectively, from now on.

2.3.
The
W
-permutohedron

The fan
\Delta
is the normal fan to a simple, lattice polytope
P_{W}
, know as a
weight polytope
or
W-permutohedron
. Fix a point
{\mathbf{a}}\in E
such that
(\alpha,{\mathbf{a}})\neq 0
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
, and let
P_{W}
be the convex hull of the
W
-orbit of
{\mathbf{a}}
,

(2.1)

P_{W}=\text{conv}\{w(\mathbf{a})\mid w\in W\}\subset E.

(A short proof that
\Delta
is the normal fan to
P_{W}
appears in
[
Kam10
, Appendix A]
.)

The permutohedron and permutohedral variety of type

3.1.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
I,J\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
, the facets
F(I)
and
F(J)
of
P_{A_{n-1}}
intersect nontrivially if and only if
I\subset J
or
J\subset I
.

Proof.

Assume that
I\subset J
. Then,

\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in F(J)\mid\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}=\sum_{i\in[|I|]}a_{i}\}\subset F(I)\cap F(J).

Conversely, suppose that
I\not\subset J
and
J\not\subset I
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
|I|\leq|J|
. Now, we choose an element
i\in I\setminus J
. Then, the
i
-th coordinate of any vertex of
F(I)
is an element of
\{a_{k}\mid k\in[|I|]\}
, while the
i
-th coordinate of any vertex of
F(J)
is an element of
\{a_{k}\mid k\in[n]\setminus[|J|]\}
. Since
|I|\leq|J|
, two sets
[|I|]
and
[n]\setminus[|J|]
have empty intersection, which implies that
F(I)
and
F(J)
have no common vertex. Hence, the lemma follows.
∎

\{\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n}\}\subset({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\ast}
be the basis dual to the standard basis
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then
E^{\ast}=({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\ast}/{\mathbb{R}}\left<\epsilon_{1}+\ldots+\epsilon_{n}\right>
. We denote by
e_{i}\in E^{\ast}
the image of
\epsilon_{i}
under the projection
({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}\to E^{*}
. The coweights, namely the duals of simple roots (
3.1
), are

\omega_{i}=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{i}~\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n-1

The coweight lattice
\hat{L}(\Phi_{A_{n-1}}^{\mathsf{v}})
is generated by
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-1}
. Moreover, an (inward) normal vector to
F(I)
, regarded as an element of
\hat{L}(\Phi_{A_{n-1}}^{\mathsf{v}})
, is given by

(3.3)

e_{I}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}.

Since
P_{A_{n-1}}
is a simple polytope, any codimension-
d

F
of
P_{A_{n-1}}
is the intersection of
d

P_{A_{n-1}}
, say
F(I_{1})\cap\cdots\cap F(I_{d})
. Hence, Lemma
3.2
implies that every face
F
of
P_{A_{n-1}}
is determined by a nested sequence

(3.4)

I_{\bullet}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(I_{1}\subsetneq I_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq I_{d})

from
\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
.
Such a face
F
will be denoted by
F(I_{\bullet})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F(I_{1})\cap\dots\cap F(I_{d})
.

We write
X_{A_{n-1}}
for the toric variety associated with the permutohedron
P_{A_{n-1}}
. This variety is often called the
permutohedral variety
. We refer to
[
DMPS92
,
Pro90
,
Huh14
]
. Since
P_{A_{n-1}}
is a simple lattice polytope, we may apply Theorem
2.1
to

, which gives the following presentation.

Proposition 3.3
.

The cohomology ring of
X_{A_{n-1}}
is given by

(3.5)

H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})\cong{\mathbb{Q}}\big[\tau_{I}\mid I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}\big]/\left(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}\right)

with
\deg\tau_{I}=2
, where

(1)

\mathcal{I}=\big(\sum_{I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}}\langle u,e_{I}\rangle\tau_{I}\mid u\in L(\Phi_{A_{n-1}})\big)
and

(2)

\mathcal{J}=\big(\tau_{I}\tau_{J}\mid I,J\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}\text{ with }I\not\subset J\text{ and }J\not\subset I\big)
.

Here,
e_{I}
is the element of the coweight lattice
\hat{L}(\Phi_{A_{n-1}}^{\mathsf{v}})
defined in (
3.3
) and

\left<\ ,\ \right>:L(\Phi_{A_{n-1}})\times\hat{L}(\Phi_{A_{n-1}}^{\mathsf{v}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the natural paring between the root lattice and the coweight lattice.

Remark 3.4
.

As
P_{A_{n-1}}
is a flag polytope, the generators of
\mathcal{J}
can be taken to be quadratic forms as in (2) above.

3.2.
The ring of invariants.

The action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
on the permutohedron
P_{A_{n-1}}
induces an action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
on
H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})
. We now investigate the action of parabolic subgroups of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
on
H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})
.

Let
K
be a subset of
[n-1]
. We will write
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
, rather than
W_{K}
as above, for the subgroup of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
generated by
\{s_{k}:k\in K\}
.
Given
I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
, we write
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}^{I}
for the setwise stabilizer of
I
in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
. For a non-negative integer
m
, we consider the sum of the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-orbit of
\tau_{I}^{m}\in H^{*}(X_{A_{n-1}})
:

[\tau_{I}^{m}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}^{I}}\tau_{w(I)}^{m}\in H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}}.

As
\tau_{u(I)}\tau_{v(I)}\in\mathcal{J}
whenever
u\neq v
in
\mathfrak{S}_{K}/\mathfrak{S}_{K}^{I}
, we see that

(3.6)

[\tau_{I}^{m}]=\sum_{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}^{I}}\tau_{w(I)}^{m}=\left(\sum_{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}^{I}}\tau_{w(I)}\right)^{m}=[\tau_{I}]^{m},

where
[\tau_{I}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\tau_{I}^{1}]
.

The action of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
on
[n]
determines an action on the set of nested sequences of subsets of
[n]
. The correspondence between codimension-
d
faces of
P_{A_{n-1}}
and nested sequences from (
3.4
) is equivariant with respect to the respective
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
actions.
We write
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}^{I_{\bullet}}
for the subgroup of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
which fixes a nested sequence
I_{\bullet}
. For a non-negative integral vector
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})
, we denote the product
\prod_{i=1}^{d}\tau_{I_{i}}^{m_{i}}
by
\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}
and consider the sum of its
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-orbit:

[\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}^{I_{\bullet}}}\tau_{w(I_{\bullet})}^{m}\in H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
I_{\bullet}
be a nested sequence as in (
3.4
) and
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})
a vector of non-negative integers. Then
[\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}]=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[\tau_{I_{i}}]^{m_{i}}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove

[\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}]=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[\tau_{I_{i}}^{m_{i}}]

by (
3.6
). The coefficients of monomials in
\tau_{J}
’s with
J\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
are all one on both sides above and any monomial on the left hand side appears on the right hand side. So, it suffices to show that any monomial on the right hand side appears on the left hand side. A monomial on the right hand side is of the form

\prod_{i=1}^{d}\tau_{w_{i}(I_{i})}^{m_{i}}

with
w_{i}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
for
i=1,\dots,d
. Therefore, it suffices to show the existence of
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
such that
wI_{i}=w_{i}I_{i}
for all
i
.

For simplicity, we consider first the special case where
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}={\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
. We have

\begin{split}&I_{1}\subsetneq I_{2}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq I_{d},\\
&w_{1}I_{1}\subsetneq w_{2}I_{2}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq w_{d}I_{d}.\end{split}

Then it is clear that there exists
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
such that

w(I_{i}\backslash I_{i-1})=w_{i}I_{i}\backslash w_{i-1}I_{i-1}\quad(i=1,\dots,d)

where
I_{0}=\emptyset
, but this means that
wI_{i}=w_{i}I_{i}
for all
i
.

The argument for the general case is essentially same as that for the special case above.
We consider the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-orbit decomposition of
[n]
,

[n]=N_{1}\sqcup N_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup N_{s}.

This induces a decomposition of
I_{i}

I_{i}=I_{i}^{(1)}\sqcup I_{i}^{(2)}\sqcup\dots\sqcup I_{i}^{(s)},

where
I_{i}^{(j)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}I_{i}\cap N_{j}
. Then, we can write
w_{i}=(w_{i}^{(1)},\dots,w_{i}^{(s)})
where
w_{i}^{(j)}\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
fixes
[n]\setminus N_{j}
pointwise.
For
j=1,\dots,s
, we have

w_{i}I_{i}=w_{i}^{(1)}I_{i}^{(1)}\sqcup w_{i}^{(2)}I_{i}^{(2)}\sqcup\dots\sqcup w_{i}^{(s)}I_{i}^{(s)}

and

\displaystyle I_{i}\backslash I_{i-1}

\displaystyle=(I_{i}^{(1)}\backslash I_{i-1}^{(1)})\sqcup\dots\sqcup(I_{i}^{(s)}\backslash I_{i-1}^{(s)}),

\displaystyle w_{i}I_{i}\backslash w_{i-1}I_{i-1}

\displaystyle=(w_{i}^{(1)}I_{i}^{(1)}\backslash w_{i-1}^{(1)}I_{i-1}^{(1)})\sqcup\dots\sqcup(w_{i}^{(s)}I_{i}^{(s)}\backslash w_{i-1}^{(s)}I_{i-1}^{(s)}).

For each
j=1,\dots,s
, there exists a permutation
w^{(j)}
on
N_{j}
such that

w^{(j)}(I_{i}^{(j)}\backslash I_{i-1}^{(j)})=w_{i}^{(j)}I_{i}^{(j)}\backslash w_{i-1}^{(j)}I_{i-1}^{(j)}\quad(i=1,\dots,d).

Therefore,
w=(w^{(1)},\dots,w^{(s)})\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
is the desired element.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

For any subgroup
H
of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, one can consider the sum of the
H
-orbit of
\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}
but Lemma
3.5
does not hold in general.
For instance, if we take
H
to be the alternating subgroup of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
, then the sum of the
H
-orbit of
\tau_{1}\tau_{12}
is

(3.7)

\tau_{1}\tau_{12}+\tau_{2}\tau_{23}+\tau_{3}\tau_{13}.

On the other hand, the sum of the
H
-orbit of
\tau_{1}
is
\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}+\tau_{3}
and that of
\tau_{12}
is
\tau_{12}+\tau_{23}+\tau_{13}
and their product is

\tau_{1}\tau_{12}+\tau_{1}\tau_{13}+\tau_{2}\tau_{12}+\tau_{2}\tau_{23}+\tau_{3}\tau_{23}+\tau_{3}\tau_{13},

which does not coincide with (
3.7
).

As a consequence of Lemma
3.5
, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.7
.

The ring of invariants
H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}}
is generated by
\{[\tau_{I}]\mid I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}\}
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the dense torus in
X_{A_{n-1}}
corresponding to the origin in the normal fan to
P_{A_{n-1}}
. By
[
BDCP90
, Theorem 8]
, the
T
-equivariant cohomology ring of
X_{A_{n-1}}
is presented as

H_{T}^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})={\mathbb{Q}}\big[\tau_{I}\mid I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}\big]/(\tau_{I}\tau_{J}\mid I\not\subset J\text{ and }J\not\subset I).

Here, we regard
\tau_{I}
as an element of
H^{2}_{T}(X_{A_{n-1}})
.
From this explicit presentation, one can see that the monomials
\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}
form an additive basis of
H_{T}^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})
.

Let
x
be an arbitrary element of
H_{T}^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}}
. We represent
x
as a polynomial in
\tau_{I}
’s, say
p
, and let
\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}
be a monomial which appears in the polynomial
p
with a nonzero coefficient, say
c
.
Since
x
is
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-invariant, any monomial in the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-orbit of
\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}
appears in the polynomial
p
. Therefore, the number of monomials which appear in
p-c[\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}]
is strictly smaller than that in
p
. Since
p-c[\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}]
is again
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-invariant, we can apply the same argument to
p-c[\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}]
unless it is zero. Repeating this procedure, we finally reach zero and this shows that
x
is a linear combination of
[\tau_{I_{\bullet}}^{m}]
’s and hence a polynomial in
[\tau_{I}]
’s by Lemma
3.5
. Therefore
H_{T}^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}}
is generated by
[\tau_{I}]
’s for
I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
.
The natural homomorphism
H_{T}^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})\to H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})
is surjective and
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-equivariant. Therefore its restriction
H_{T}^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}}\to H^{\ast}(X_{A_{n-1}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}}
is also surjective. This fact and the above observation prove the proposition.
∎

4.
Toric varieties associated with partitioned permutohedra

4.1.
Partitioned permutohedra

If
\Phi
is of type
A_{n-1}
, then for every
k\in[n-1]
, the half-space
H(k)^{\leq}
satisfies

\displaystyle H(k)^{\leq}=\{x\in E\mid x_{k}\leq x_{k+1}\}

and the partitioned permutohedron
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
associated with a subset
K\subset[n-1]
is defined by

P_{A_{n-1}}(K)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}P_{A_{n-1}}\cap\bigcap_{k\in K}H(k)^{\leq}.

See Figure
2
for several examples of partitioned permutohedra of types
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
.

P_{A_{2}}(\{1\})
P_{A_{2}}(\{2\})
P_{A_{2}}(\{1,2\})
P_{A_{3}}(\{1\})
P_{A_{3}}(\{1,2\})
P_{A_{3}}(\{1,2,3\})

Figure 2.
Examples of partitioned permutohedra in type
A_{2}
and
A_{3}
.

Now we study the face structure of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
. Let
H(k)
be the boundary of
H(k)^{\leq}
, which is the hyperplane in
E
defined by
x_{k}=x_{k+1}
, and let
H(k)^{<}
be the interior of
H(k)^{\leq}
in
E
. We note that facets of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
are of the form

F_{K}(I)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F(I)\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K)~\text{or}~H_{K}(k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}H(k)\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K).

Proposition 4.1
.

For
k\in[n-1]
and
I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
, the following claims hold.

(1)

We have
F(I)\cap H(k)\not=\emptyset
if and only if
I
is
s_{k}
-invariant, i.e.
\{k,k+1\}
is contained in either
I
or
[n]\backslash I
. In this case,
F(I)
is bisected by the hyperplane
H(k)
.

(2)

We have
F(I)\subset H(k)^{<}
if and only if
k\in I
but
k+1\notin I
.

Therefore,
F(I)\cap H(k)^{\leq}\not=\emptyset
if and only if
k\in I
whenever
k+1\in I
.

Proof.

(1) We observe first that the facet
F(I)
is either bisected by
H(k)
or has empty intersection with
H(k)
. Indeed, since the permutohedron
P_{A_{n-1}}
is
s_{k}
-invariant, the reflection through the hyperplane
H(k)
either preserves
F(I)
or sends
F(I)
to another facet
F(s_{k}(I))
. In the latter case,
F(I)\cap F(s_{k}(I))=\emptyset
, since it follows from the definition of
F(I)
in (
3.2
) that the two facets have no common vertex. As
I
is
s_{k}
-invariant if and only if
F(I)
is invariant under the reflection through the hyperplane
H(k)
if and only if
F(I)
is bisected by
H(k)
, statement (1) holds.

(2) If
k\in I
but
k+1\not\in I
, then it follows from the definition of
F(I)
in (
3.2
) that any vertex
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of
F(I)
satisfies
x_{k}\leq a_{|I|}<x_{k+1}
, which implies that
F(I)
is contained in
H(k)^{<}
. Conversely, if
F(I)
is contained in
H(k)^{<}
, then exactly one of
k
and
k+1
is contained in
I
by (1). If
k\notin I
but
k+1\in I
, then the same argument as above shows that
x_{k+1}\leq a_{|I|}<x_{k}
for every vertex
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
of
F(I)
, which implies that
F(I)
is contained in the open half-space opposite to
H(k)^{<}
. Therefore,
k\in I
but
k+1\notin I
.
∎

Motivated by Proposition
4.1
, we make the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
K
be a subset of
[n-1]
and
[n]=N_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup N_{s}
the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{K}
-orbit decomposition of
[n]
. Given
i\in[s]
, we call a subset
M
of
N_{i}
a
lower subset
if
k\in M
whenever
k\in N_{i}
and
k+1\in M
. We define

\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}\mid\text{$I\cap N_{i}$ is a lower subset of $N_{i}$ for all $1\leq i\leq s$}\}.

For instance, consider the root system of type
A_{2}
, take
K=\{1\}
, and consider the corresponding parabolic subgroup
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\{1\}}
of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
. Then,
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\{1\}}
-orbit decomposition of
[3]
is given by
\{1,2\}\sqcup\{3\}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{A_{2}}(K)
consists of
\{1\},\{3\},\{1,2\}
and
\{1,3\}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

For
I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
and
k\in[n-1]
, we have

(1)

H_{K}(k)
is a facet of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
if and only if
k\in K
,

(2)

F_{K}(I)
is a facet of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
if and only if
I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)
.

Therefore, the set of facets of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
corresponds bijectively to
\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)\cup K
.

Proof.

Statement (1) follows quickly from the definition of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)

and statement (2) follows from Proposition
4.1
.
∎

4.2.
Flagness of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)

Recall that a codimension-
d
face of
P_{A_{n-1}}
is represented by a nested sequence

I_{\bullet}=(I_{1}\subsetneq I_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq I_{d})

of elements
I_{1},\dots,I_{d}
in
\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
as in (
3.4
). We denote by
F(I_{\bullet})
and
C(I_{\bullet})
the face corresponding to
I_{\bullet}
and its barycenter respectively.

Lemma 4.4
.

(1)

C(I_{\bullet})\in H(k)
if
I_{\bullet}
is
s_{k}
-invariant.

(2)

C(I_{\bullet})\in P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
if
I_{j}\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)
for each
1\leq j\leq d
.

Proof.

(1) If
I_{\bullet}
is
s_{k}
-invariant, then each facet
F(I_{j})
of
P_{A_{n-1}}
is invariant under the reflection through the hyperplane
H(k)
. This implies statement (1).

(2) Suppose that
I_{j}\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)
for each
1\leq j\leq d
. Then
F_{K}(I_{j})
is a facet of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
by Corollary
4.3
-(2).
Let
k\in K
. If
I_{\bullet}
is
s_{k}
-invariant, then

C(I_{\bullet})\in H(k)

by (1) above. If
I_{\bullet}
is not
s_{k}
-invariant, then some
I_{j}
is not
s_{k}
-invariant. Hence
k\in I_{j}
but
k+1\notin I_{j}
since
I_{j}\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)
, which implies
F(I_{j})\subset H(k)^{<}
by Proposition
4.1
-(2). It follows that

C(I_{\bullet})\in F(I_{\bullet})=\bigcap_{j=1}^{d}F(I_{j})\subset H(k)^{<}.

In any case
C(I_{\bullet})\in H(k)^{\leq}
for
k\in K
. Hence we get

C(I_{\bullet})\in F(I_{\bullet})\cap\bigcap_{k\in K}H(k)^{\leq}\subset P_{A_{n-1}}\cap\bigcap_{k\in K}H(k)^{\leq}=P_{A_{n-1}}(K),

proving the statement (2).
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

For any subset
K\subset[n-1]
, the associated partitioned permutohedron
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
is a flag polytope.

Proof.

We must show that, assuming each pair from a set

\{F_{K}(I_{1}),\dots,F_{K}(I_{p}),H_{K}(k_{1}),\dots,H_{K}(k_{q})\}

of facets of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
has nonempty intersection, we have

(4.1)

F_{K}(I_{1})\cap\cdots\cap F_{K}(I_{p})\cap H_{K}(k_{1})\cap\cdots\cap H_{K}(k_{q})\neq\emptyset.

This is obvious when
p=0
because every
H_{K}(k)
contains the barycenter of
P_{A_{n-1}}
. We will prove (
4.1
) when
p\geq 1
as follows.

Since
F_{K}(I_{i})\cap F_{K}(I_{j})\neq\emptyset
for all
i,j\in[p]
and
F_{K}(I)=F(I)\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
for all
I\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}
, we see that
F(I_{i})\cap F(I_{j})\neq\emptyset
for all
i,j\in[p]
. As
P_{A_{n-1}}
is flag, we may permute indices to obtain a nested sequence

I_{\bullet}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left(I_{1}\subsetneq I_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq I_{p}\right)

by Lemma
3.2
. We show that
C(I_{\bullet})
lies in the intersection (
4.1
).

First, we claim that
C(I_{\bullet})\in F_{K}(I_{1})\cap\cdots\cap F_{K}(I_{p})
. Indeed, since
F_{K}(I_{j})
is a facet of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
for
1\leq j\leq p
, we have
I_{j}\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)
by Corollary
4.3
-(2) and hence
C(I_{\bullet})\in P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
by Lemma
4.4
-(
2
). It follows that

\displaystyle C(I_{\bullet})\in F(I_{\bullet})\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}F(I_{i})\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}F_{K}(I_{i}).

Next, we claim that
C(I_{\bullet})\in H_{K}(k_{1})\cap\cdots\cap H_{K}(k_{q}).
By assumption we have

\emptyset\neq F_{K}(I_{i})\cap H_{K}(k_{j})\subset F(I_{i})\cap H(k_{j})

for any
1\leq i\leq p
and
1\leq j\leq q
.
Therefore, each
I_{i}
is
s_{k_{j}}
-invariant for any
1\leq j\leq q
by Proposition
4.1
-(1) and hence
I_{\bullet}
is
s_{k_{j}}
-invariant whenever
1\leq j\leq q
. Since
C(I_{\bullet})\in P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
as observed above, it follows from Lemma
4.4
-(
1
) that

C(I_{\bullet})\in P_{A_{n-1}}(K)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{q}H(k_{j})=\bigcap_{j=1}^{q}H_{K}(k_{j}).

Two claims above prove the desired (
4.1
).
∎

There are two types of facets,
F_{K}(I)
and
H_{K}(k)
, in
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
. We know that
H_{K}(k)\cap H_{K}(k^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
for arbitrary
k,k^{\prime}\in K
since the barycenter of
P_{A_{n-1}}
is contained in
H_{K}(k)
for all
k\in K
. The next proposition tells us which pairs of facets of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
have empty intersection.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
F_{K}(I),F_{K}(J)
and
H_{K}(k)
be facets of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
, so that
I,J\in\mathfrak{F}_{A_{n-1}}(K)
and
k\in K
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

F_{K}(I)\cap F_{K}(J)=\emptyset
if and only if
I\not\subset J
and
J\not\subset I
.

(2)

F_{K}(I)\cap H_{K}(k)=\emptyset
if and only if
I
is not
s_{k}
-invariant.

Proof.

(1) Since
F_{K}(I)\subset F(I)
and
F_{K}(J)\subset F(J)
, the “if” part is obvious by Lemma
3.2
. In order to prove the “only if” part, we prove

F_{K}(I)\cap F_{K}(J)\not=\emptyset\ \text{ if $I\subset J$ or $J\subset I$}.

If
I=J
, then
(*)
above trivially holds and we may assume
I\subsetneq J
without loss of generality. Then, we have a flag
I_{\bullet}=(I\subsetneq J)
and the barycenter
C(I_{\bullet})
of
F(I_{\bullet})
is in
F(I_{\bullet})\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
by Lemma
4.4
-(2). This proves
(*)
above since
F(I_{\bullet})\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K)=F_{K}(I)\cap F_{K}(J)
, thereby proving the “only if” part.

(2) Statement (2) is equivalent to the following:

F_{K}(I)\cap H_{K}(k)\not=\emptyset
if and only if
I
is
s_{k}
-invariant.

Furthermore,
I
is
s_{k}
-invariant if and only if
F(I)\cap H(k)\not=\emptyset
by Proposition
4.1
-(1). Therefore, it suffices to prove the following:

F_{K}(I)\cap H_{K}(k)\not=\emptyset
if and only if
F(I)\cap H(k)\not=\emptyset
.

The “only if” part in
(**)
above is trivial because

(4.2)

F_{K}(I)\cap H_{K}(k)=F(I)\cap H(k)\cap P_{A_{n-1}}(K).

The “if” part in
(**)
above can be proved as follows. If
F(I)\cap H(k)\not=\emptyset
, then the barycenter
C(I)
of
F(I)
is in
F(I)\cap H(k)
. Since
H_{K}(k)
is a facet of
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
, we have
k\in K
by Corollary
4.3
-(1) and hence
C(I)
is also in
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
by Lemma
4.4
-(1). This together with (
4.2
) shows
C(I)\in F_{K}(I)\cap H_{K}(k)
, proving the “if” part in
(**)
above.
∎

4.3.
Cohomology of the toric variety associated with
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)

The primitive inward normal vector of the facet
F_{K}(I)
agrees with that of
F(I)
. On the other hand, an (inward) normal vector of
H_{K}(k)
is given by

-\alpha^{\mathsf{v}}_{k}=-e_{k}+e_{k+1}

because
H_{K}(k)=P_{A_{n-1}}(K)\cap\{x\in E\mid\alpha^{\mathsf{v}}_{k}(x)=0\}
and
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
is contained in the half-space
H(k)^{\leq}=\{x\in E\mid\alpha_{k}^{\mathsf{v}}(x)\leq 0\}
.

Let
X_{A_{n-1}}(K)
be the toric variety associated with a partitioned permutohedron
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
. Since
P_{A_{n-1}}
is a simple polytope, so is
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)
. Hence,
X_{A_{n-1}}(K)
is a toric orbifold as described in Section
2
. Now, applying Theorem
2.1
together with Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
to
X_{A_{n-1}}(K)
, we obtain the following.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
K
be a subset of
[n-1]
.
The cohomology ring of the toric orbifold
X_{A_{n-1}}(K)
associated with a partitioned permutohedron
P_{A_{n-1}}(K)

6.
Hessenberg varieties

The toric variety
X_{\Phi}
can be realized as a subvariety of a flag variety, as shown by De Mari, Procesi and Shayman in
[
DMPS92
]
. Let
G
be a connected, simply-connected, semisimple algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
B
a Borel subgroup and
T\leq B
a maximal torus. We denote by
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{b}
, and
{\mathfrak{h}}
their respective Lie algebras. There is a Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, with
\Phi
the associated root system. Assume that
G
has rank
n
and, as above, that the set of simple roots in
\Phi
is
\Sigma=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
.

A
\mathfrak{b}
-submodule
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is called a
Hessenberg space
if it contains
\mathfrak{b}
. In fact, a subspace
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a Hessenberg space if and only if
\mathcal{H}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Psi}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}
, where
\Psi
is a set of negative roots such that
\gamma+\alpha\in\Psi
whenever
\gamma\in\Psi
,
\alpha
is a simple root, and
\gamma+\alpha
is a negative root. In particular,

\mathcal{H}_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathfrak{b}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}

is a Hessenberg space.

The
Hessenberg variety

\Hess(x,\mathcal{H})
associated with an element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and a Hessenberg space
\mathcal{H}
is

\Hess(x,\mathcal{H})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{gB\in G/B\mid\mbox{Ad}(g^{-1})(x)\in\mathcal{H}\}.

An element
r\in\mathfrak{g}
is
regular
if its centralizer in
\mathfrak{g}
has smallest possible dimension, and a
regular Hessenberg variety
is any
\Hess(r,\mathcal{H})
such that
r
is regular. Similarly, a
regular semisimple Hessenberg variety
is any
\Hess(s,H)
such that
s
is regular and semisimple.

Regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties (for arbitrary
G
as above) were defined and studied in
[
DMPS92
]
, after work in type A by De Mari and Shayman in
[
DMS88
]
. The type A case has received considerable attention, as it involves connections between combinatorics, geometry and representation theory, see for example
[
Tym08
,
SW16
,
BroCho18
,
AHMMS20
]
.

Given a regular semisimple Hessenberg variety
\Hess(s,\mathcal{H})
, we may assume that
T
is the centralizer of
s
in the adjoint action of
G
. Then
T
acts on
\Hess(s,\mathcal{H})
by left translation. This action is equivariantly formal, as defined by Goresky, Kottwitz and MacPherson in
[
GKM98
]
. It follows that the
T
-equivariant cohomology ring of
\Hess(s,\mathcal{H})
is isomorphic with a certain ring of functions from the vertex set of the associated moment graph to a polynomial ring. As observed by Tymoczko in
[
Tym08
]
, the Weyl group
W
of
G
acts on this moment graph, and this gives rise to representations of
W
on both the
T
-equivariant and ordinary cohomology rings of
\Hess(s,\mathcal{H})
. The representation of
W
on
H^{\ast}(\Hess(s,\mathcal{H}))
is called the
dot representation
. The key points for our purposes are the following ones:

•

It is shown in
[
DMPS92
]
that
X_{\Phi}
is isomorphic with
\Hess(s,\mathcal{H}_{1})
for every regular semisimple
s\in\mathfrak{g}
.

•

The (graded) representation of
W
on
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi})
determined by the action of
W
on the weight polytope
P_{W}
, defined in (
2.1
), is isomorphic to the dot representation of
W
on
H^{\ast}(\Hess(s,\mathcal{H}_{1}))
, see
[
Tym08
,
Tef11
]
.

Moreover, for regular semisimple
s\in\mathfrak{g}
, arbitrary Hessenberg space
\mathcal{H}
, and
K\subseteq[n]
, the fixed point subring
H^{\ast}(\Hess(s,\mathcal{H}))^{W_{K}}
(in the dot representation) is isomorphic to the cohomology ring of a regular Hessenberg variety determined by
K
. For each
\alpha\in\Sigma
, fix
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\setminus\{0\}
. Given
K\subseteq[n]
, set
n_{K}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i\in K}e_{\alpha_{i}}
. There exist infinitely many
s_{K}\in{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
[s_{K},n_{K}]=0
and
s_{K}+n_{K}
is regular. Choose any such
s_{K}
and set
r_{K}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}s_{K}+n_{K}
.

The following result was proved by Balibanu and Crooks in
[
BalCro20
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

[
BalCro20
, Proposition 4.7]

Let
s\in\mathfrak{g}
be regular semisimple and let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a Hessenberg space. For each
K\subseteq[n]
, the graded rings
H^{\ast}(\Hess(s,\mathcal{H}))^{W_{K}}
and
H^{\ast}(r_{K},\mathcal{H}))
are isomorphic.

Remark 6.2
.

Before the work of Balibanu–Crooks, there were various partial results. Brosnan and Chow proved in
[
BroCho18
]
that when

, the graded vector spaces
H^{\ast}(\Hess(s,\mathcal{H}))^{W_{K}}
and

are isomorphic. Under the assumption that

, Theorem

was proved by the first two named authors of this paper along with H. Abe and Harada for

in
[
AHHM17
]
, and later by the same two named authors along with T. Abe, Murai and Sato for arbitrary

[

]
.
Another proof of Theorem

was given by Vilonen and Xue in
[
VX21
]
.

Combining Theorem
6.1
in the case
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}
with Theorem

, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
G
be a connected, simply-connected, simple complex algebraic group of classical type, with rank
n
.
For arbitrary
K\subseteq[n]
, the graded rational cohomology rings

and

\Hess(r_{K},\mathcal{H}_{1})

In fact, Peterson variety

[

), while the corresponding toric variety

7.

and write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{G}
for the usual inner product of characters of a finite group

. It follows from (

) and Theorem
1.1
that

(7.2)

h_{P_{W}(K)}(t)=\langle\chi_{\Phi},1\rangle_{W_{K}}=\frac{1}{|W_{K}|}\sum_{w\in W_{K}}\chi_{\Phi}(w).

Stembridge provides in
[
Ste94
, Corollary 1.6]
a general formula for
\chi_{\Phi}(w)
, and gives specific formulas for types
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}
, and
D_{n}
in
[
Ste94
, Corollary 6.1, Corollary 7.2, Theorem 9.2]
, respectively. These can be combined with (
7.2
) to give formulas for
h_{P_{W}(K)}(t)
. We write a specific formula in the case
\Phi
is of type
A_{n-1}
, where
[
Ste94
, Corollary 6.1]
says that if
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
has cycle type
\lambda=\lambda(w)=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
, then

(7.3)

\chi_{\Phi}(w)=E_{\ell}(t)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}[\lambda_{j}]_{t}.

(Here
E_{m}(t)=\sum_{w\in S_{m}}t^{\sf{des}(w)}
is the Eulerian polynomial, that is, the generating function for descents, and
[m]_{t}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{t^{m}-1}{t-1}
.)

Combining (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), we obtain the following result.

Proposition 7.1
.

Assume that
\Phi
is of type
A_{n-1}
and
K\subseteq[n-1]
. Then

h_{P_{W}(K)}(t)=\frac{1}{|W_{K}|}\sum_{w\in W_{K}}E_{\ell(\lambda(w))}(t)\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(\lambda(w))}[\lambda(w)_{j}]_{t}.

Example 7.2
.

Say
n=5
and
K=\{1,2,4\}
. Then
W_{K}\cong{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}
has one element of cycle type
(1,1,1,1,1)
, four of type
(2,1,1,1)
, three of type
(2,2,1)
, two of type
(3,1,1)
and two of type
(3,2)
. According to Proposition
7.1
,

\displaystyle h_{P_{W}(K)}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}(E_{5}(t)+4E_{4}(t)(1+t)+3E_{3}(t)(1+t)^{2}+2E_{3}(t)(1+t+t^{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2E_{2}(t)(1+t+t^{2})(1+t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+9t+17t^{2}+9t^{3}+t^{4}.

Next we compute
h_{P_{W}(K)}(t)
using (
7.1
) and Corollary
6.3
, which together imply that

(7.4)

h_{P_{W}(K)}(t)=\sum_{i}\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^{2i}(\Hess(r_{K},\mathcal{H}_{1}))t^{i}.

In
[
Pre18
]
, Precup shows that the nonempty intersections of a regular Hessenberg variety
\Hess(r_{K},\mathcal{H})
with Schubert cells in the associated flag variety determine an affine paving. She gives a criterion for determining which such intersections are nonempty and a formula for the dimensions of the nonempty intersections. Applying Precup’s results in the case
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}
, we obtain the following result. We maintain the notation from Section

.

Proposition 7.3

(See Lemma 2 of
[
Pre18
]
)
.

Let
K\subseteq[n]
and fix

\Sigma_{K}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\alpha_{i}:i\in K\}
and define

For

Then

8.
Open questions

We discuss here some questions and conjectures related to our work.

8.1.
Quotient polytopes and quotient varieties

Say
P\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a rational simple polytope and
G\leq GL_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
preserves
P
setwise. The action of
G
on
P
determines an action on the toric variety
X(P)
and we may form the quotient,
X(P)/G
. Let us assume that
G
is generated by reflections through hyperplanes intersecting transversally with the edges of
P
. Then there is a “nice” fundamental region
R
for this action, that is, a set of orbit representatives that is convex and whose closure is a polytope with every facet being supported by either a hyperplane supporting a facet of
P
or a hyperplane associated to a reflection in
G
. We write
P/G
for the polytope
\overline{R}
.

Question 8.1
.

If
G
is generated by reflections in
GL_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
and acts on
P
as in the paragraph directly above, must
X(P)/G
be isomorphic with
X(P/G)
?

As mentioned in the introduction, Blume showed in
[
Blu15
]
that Question
8.1
has positive answers when
P
is a weight polytope of type
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
, and
G
is the associated Weyl group with the natural action on the root space. Below, we introduce a couple of more known examples supporting Question
8.1
.

Consider the symmetric product
\mathsf{SP}^{n}(\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1})=(\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1})^{n}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, where
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
permutes the factors of
(\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1})^{n}
. We regard
(\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1})^{n}
as the toric variety corresponding to the
n
-dimensional cube, say
[0,1]^{n}
.
The fundamental region for the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-action on
[0,1]^{n}
given by permuting factors is

(8.1)

[0,1]^{n}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}\mid 0\leq x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x_{n}\leq 1\},

which is an
n
-simplex. Observe that the toric variety
X([0,1]^{n}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{n})
is
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathsf{SP}^{n}(\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{1})
, see
[
Maa05
, Corollary 2.6]
for instance.

Another example is the weighted projective space
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}_{(1,2,\dots,n)}=S^{2n-1}/S^{1}
, where
S^{1}
-action on
S^{2n-1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is given by

t\cdot(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=(t^{1}z_{1},\dots,t^{n}z_{n}),\quad\text{for }t\in S^{1},~(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in S^{2n-1}.

This can be constructed as the quotient of
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}
by the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-action permuting coordinates as well. Indeed, the quotient morphism
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}_{(1,2,\dots,n)}
defined by

[z_{1};\cdots;z_{n}]\mapsto[e_{1}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n});\cdots;e_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})],

where
e_{i}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
is the elementary symmetric function, induces an isomorphism between
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}_{(1,2\dots,n)}
. We regard
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}
as the toric variety corresponding to the standard simplex

\Delta^{n-1}=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid x_{1}\geq 0,\dots,x_{n}\geq 0\text{ and }x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}=1\right\}.

The canonical
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
-action on
\Delta^{n-1}
given by permuting coordinates defines

\Delta^{n-1}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\Delta^{n-1}\mid x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x_{n}\},

whose associated toric variety is
\mathbb{C}{\rm P}^{n-1}_{(1,2,\dots,n)}
.

Remark 8.2
.

(a)

Question
8.1
has a negative answer if we allow arbitrary finite group actions. Given
n\geq 2
, consider the toric variety
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and let
G\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
act on
X
, with generator sending
(x,y)\in X
to
(y,x)
. The betti numbers of
X/G
satisfy
\beta_{2}(X/G)=1
and
\beta_{4}(X/G)=2
. Thus the rational cohomology ring of
X/G
is not generated in degree two, and
X/G
is not a toric variety by Theorem
2.1
.

(b)

Let
T
be a dense torus in
X(P)
with free action on
T
extending to action on
X(P)
. This action on
X(P)
does not in general descend to an action on
X(P)/G
, since the actions of
T
and
G
on
X(P)
need not commute.

8.2.
Other Coxeter groups

We believe that the following question has a positive answer.

Question 8.3
.

Let
\Phi
be an arbitrary crystallographic root system, with associated Weyl group
W
. Does the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
hold for
X_{\Phi}
?

The crystallographic condition allows the construction of the variety
X_{\Phi}
from the fan determined by
\Phi
. However, we can construct a ring with presentation as given in Theorem

from an arbitrary root system. This ring, which we call

.

Question 8.4
.

Let
\Phi
be an arbitrary root system. Does the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
hold for the action of
W
on
R_{\Phi}
?

8.3.
Regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties and actions on moment graphs

As discussed in Section
6
, the representation of
W
on
H^{\ast}(X_{\Phi})
is the same as the dot representation on

, with
s
regular semisimple. The ring

admits a dot representation for every regular semisimple

\mathcal{H}
. It is reasonable to hope that some analogue of Theorem

Problem 8.5

Find an appropriate definition of a quotient moment graph, so that

(2)

Assuming (1) is solved, find a “nice” variety with equivariantly formal torus action giving rise to a given quotient moment graph.

Unramifiedness of weight 1 1 Hilbert Hecke algebras
Shaunak V. Deo, Mladen Dimitrov, Gabor Wiese

Abstract
. We prove that the Galois pseudo-representation valued in the mod p n p^{n} cuspidal Hecke algebra for GL ⁡ ( 2 ) \mathrm{GL}(2) over a totally real number field F F , of parallel weight 1 1 and level prime to p p , is unramified at any place above p p . The same is true for the non-cuspidal Hecke algebra at places above p p whose ramification index is not divisible by p − 1 p-1 . A novel geometric ingredient, which is also of independent interest, is the construction and study, in the case when p p ramifies in F F , of generalised Θ \Theta -operators using Reduzzi–Xiao’s generalised Hasse invariants, including especially an injectivity criterion in terms of minimal weights.

Introduction

Starting with Wiles
[
24
]
and Taylor–Wiles
[
21
]
,
R=\mathbb{T}
theorems have been developed and taken a role as cornerstones in number theory. They provide both the existence of Galois representations with values in Hecke algebras satisfying prescribed local properties and modularity lifting theorems.
The state of
R=\mathbb{T}
theorems for
2
-dimensional representations in residual characteristic
p
of the absolute Galois group
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
and Hecke algebras acting on elliptic modular forms is quite satisfactory. In particular, the notoriously difficult case of Galois representations that are unramified at an odd prime
p

has been settled by ground-breaking work of Calegari and Geraghty
[
3
]
, in which they show that those correspond to modular forms of weight
1
.
More precisely, given an odd irreducible representation
\bar{\rho}:\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
unramified outside
a finite set of places
S
not containing
p
, they show that

R^{S}_{\mathbb{Q},\bar{\rho}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\bar{\rho}},

where
R^{S}_{\mathbb{Q},\bar{\rho}}
is the universal deformation ring parametrising deformations of
\bar{\rho}

which are unramified outside
S
and
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\bar{\rho}}
is
the local component at
\bar{\rho}
of a weight
1
Hecke algebra of a certain level prime to
p
.

In this article, we address the corresponding question for parallel weight
1
Hilbert modular forms over a totally real field
F
of degree
d=[F:\mathbb{Q}]\geqslant 2
and ring of integers
\mathfrak{o}
.
We focus on the construction of the Galois (pseudo-)representation with values in the parallel weight
1
Hecke algebra with
p
-power torsion coefficients and proving its local ramification properties.
In particular, given a finite set
S
of places in
F
relatively prime to
p
and a totally odd irreducible representation
\bar{\rho}:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
unramified outside
S
we show that there exists a surjective homomorphism

R^{S}_{F,\bar{\rho}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\bar{\rho}},

where
R^{S}_{F,\bar{\rho}}
is the universal deformation ring parametrising deformations of
\bar{\rho}

which are unramified outside
S
and
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\bar{\rho}}
is
the local component at
\bar{\rho}
of a weight
1
Hecke algebra of a certain level prime to
p
(see Corollary
3.10
for a precise statement).

Let
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)
be the
R
-module of Hilbert modular forms of
parallel weight
\kappa\geqslant 1
and prime to
p
level
\mathfrak{n}
over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
R
, as in Definition
2.1
.
This
R
-module is equipped with a commuting family of Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
as well as with diamond operators
\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle
for all primes
\mathfrak{q}
of
F
not dividing
\mathfrak{n}
.
Let
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension containing the images of all embeddings of
F
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, and let
\mathcal{O}
be its
valuation ring,
\varpi
a uniformiser and
\mathbb{F}=\mathcal{O}/\varpi
its residue field.
We put
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O})=\underset{n}{\underrightarrow{\lim}\;}M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
and define the parallel weight
1
Hecke algebra

\mathbb{T}^{(1)}=\Image\big(\mathcal{O}[T_{\mathfrak{q}},{\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle}]_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O}))\big),

as well as its cuspidal quotient
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\mathrm{cusp}}
acting faithfully on the submodule of parallel weight
1
cuspforms.
We can now state the main results of this article. Let
p\mathfrak{o}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}\mathfrak{p}^{e_{\mathfrak{p}}}

with
e_{\mathfrak{p}}\geqslant 1
. We emphasise that there is no restriction on the ramification of
p
in
F
.

Theorem 0.1
.

There exists a
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}
-valued pseudo-representation
P^{(1)}
of
\mathrm{G}_{F}
of degree
2
which is unramified at all primes
\mathfrak{q}
not dividing
\mathfrak{n}p
and satisfies
P^{(1)}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}})=(T_{\mathfrak{q}},{\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle})
.

Moreover, if
p-1
does not divide
e_{\mathfrak{p}}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
, then
P^{(1)}
is also unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
and satisfies

P^{(1)}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})=(T_{\mathfrak{p}},{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle})
, in particular
T_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathbb{T}^{(1)}
.

Finally, the pseudo-representation
P_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{(1)}
obtained after composing
P^{(1)}
with the natural surjection

\mathbb{T}^{(1)}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{(1)}
is unramified at all
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
and satisfies
P_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{(1)}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})=(T_{\mathfrak{p}},{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle})
.

The strategy of the proof is based on the
doubling
method developed in
[
23
]
, further simplified and conceptualised in
[
14
]
and
[
3
]
. The parallel weight
1
Hilbert modular forms over
\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}
can be mapped into some higher weight in two ways, per prime
\mathfrak{p}
dividing
p
, either by multiplication by a suitable power of the total Hasse invariant, or by applying a
V
-operator.
That
doubling
map is used by Calegari–Specter in
[
4
]
to prove an analogue of Theorem
0.1

when
F=\mathbb{Q}
, for which they successfully develop the notion of a
p
-
ordinary
pseudo-representation.
In that case, one knows by a result of Katz that the doubling map is injective.
Furthermore, the existence of the Hecke operator
T_{p}
acting on weight
1
modular forms and the knowledge of its precise effect on the
q
-expansion (both due to Gross) allow one to show that the image of the doubling map is contained in the
p
-ordinary part of the higher weight space.

The existence of an optimally integral Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
acting on parallel weight
1
Hilbert modular forms with arbitrary coefficients having the desired effect on their
q
-expansions (see
[
6
]
improving on and correcting previous works such as
[
15
]
and
[
14
]
) allows us to adapt the overall Calegari–Specter strategy to the Hilbert modular setting, while slightly generalising and clarifying some aspects of their arguments (see §
3
), the main challenge being to prove the injectivity of the doubling map. Note that the
simple calculation in
[
14
]
showing that injectivity after restriction to an eigenspace is insufficient as the Hecke algebra modulo
p
need not be semi-simple.
Instead, we observe that the injectivity of the doubling map would follow from the injectivity of a certain generalised
\Theta
-operator, introduced in the foundational work
[
2
]
of Andreatta–Goren for Hilbert modular forms in characteristic
p
defined over the Deligne–Pappas moduli space. When
p
is unramified in
F
, the theory of partial
\Theta
-operators was also developed by Diamond–Sasaki
[
10
]
in a more general setting using a slightly different approach from that of
[
2
]
.
However, when
p
is ramified in
F
, the results of
[
10
]
do not apply, while those of
[
2
]
are not sufficiently precise for our purposes, as the Hilbert modular forms defined over the Deligne–Pappas model ‘miss’ some weights, and as a consequence the injectivity result of the latter paper is not optimal.
In order to tackle this problem, we go back to the root of the problem and work with the Pappas–Rapoport moduli space, which does not miss any weight.

Capitalising on the theory of generalised Hasse invariants developed by Reduzzi–Xiao
[
17
]

in this context, we carefully revisit
[
2
]
and develop in §
1.5
the needed theory of generalised
\Theta
-operators over the Pappas–Rapoport moduli space and prove a refined injectivity criterion in terms of the minimal weights. In particular, we show that the generalised
\Theta
-operators are indeed injective on parallel weight
1
Hilbert modular forms provided their weight is minimal at
\mathfrak{p}
.
By the recent works
[
8
,
9
]
of Diamond and Kassaei (see §
1.3
) weight
1
Hilbert modular forms having ‘non-minimal’
weight at
\mathfrak{p}
could only possibly exist when
p-1
divides
e_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and are products of forms of partial weight
0
at
\mathfrak{p}
with generalised Hasse invariants.

In order to show the vanishing of the space of Katz cuspforms of partial weight
0
at
\mathfrak{p}
, and thus complete the proof of
the second and third parts of the Theorem, in §
1.4
we construct a partial Frobenius endomorphism
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}^{e}}
of this space and show that it is simultaneously injective and nilpotent.
Our construction is inspired by the one in Diamond–Sasaki
[
10
, §9.8]
in the case when
p
is unramified in
F
.
We also compute its effect on
q
-expansions, which is crucially used in our proof and, in order to avoid having to switch between different cusps, we only study the partial Frobenius operator of an appropriate power of
\mathfrak{p}
, rather than of
\mathfrak{p}
itself.

In the language of linear representations, we prove the following result, which can be seen as a first step towards an
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem.

Corollary

(Corollary
3.10
)
.

For every non-Eisenstein maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}
(see Definition
3.8
) there exists a representation

\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\mathfrak{m}}),

unramified at all primes
\mathfrak{q}
not dividing
\mathfrak{n}
such that
\tr(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}}))=T_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
\det(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}}))={\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle}
.

We believe that our modest contribution to the theory of generalised
\Theta
-operators in the setting of the Pappas–Rapoport splitting model
is worthwhile on its own, beyond the application to our main theorem.
On our way to the injectivity criterion, we also explore some related themes, such as the relation between Hilbert modular forms defined over the Pappas–Rapoport model with those defined over the Deligne–Pappas model, and the
q
-expansion and vanishing loci of the generalised Hasse invariants defined by Reduzzi–Xiao.
We hope that it bridges the gap between many existing references in the literature and also clarifies some important aspects of the theory of mod
p
Hilbert modular forms.
In the meantime, motivated by geometric Serre weight conjectures,
Diamond
[
7
]
extended the techniques of
[
10
]
to also construct partial
\Theta
-operators which have an optimal effect on weights in the case where
p
ramifies in
F
. Moreover, Diamond generalised in
[
7
]
the construction of the partial Frobenius operators (our partial Frobenius operator
\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}^{e}}
is essentially Diamond’s
V_{\mathfrak{p}}^{e}
). Note that Diamond also describes kernels of partial
\Theta
-operators in terms of images of his partial Frobenius maps
V_{\mathfrak{p}}
(see
[
7
, Thm. 9.1.1]
).
However, our construction is less technical because we restrict to the Rapoport locus and we only consider the case of weights
0
at
\mathfrak{p}
.

Next we give, following Katz, a geometric definition of the space of Hilbert modular forms.

Definition 1.1

A Katz Hilbert modular form of weight

Its

Remark 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}|p
,

We now collect some properties of the generalised Hasse invariants that will be used in the sequel.
Let

1.3.
Minimal weights

We recall the notion of
minimal weight
of a mod
p
Hilbert modular form.

Definition 1.8
.

We define the minimal weight of
0\neq f\in M_{k}^{\mathrm{Katz}}(\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{n};\mathbb{F})
to be the unique weight
k^{\prime}
such that

f=g\cdot\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}h_{\sigma}^{n_{\sigma}}
, where
g\in M_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Katz}}(\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{n};\mathbb{F})
and the integers
(n_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
are as large as possible.

Lemma 1.9
.

The notion of minimal weight is well defined.

Proof.

First note that
Z_{\sigma}
is non-empty for every
\sigma\in\Sigma
.
Indeed, this follows from
[
9
, Cor. 5.7]
.
Alternatively, we have shown in Remark
1.6
that
Z_{\tau,i}
is non-empty for every
\tau\in{\overline{\Sigma}}
and
2\leq i\leq e_{\mathfrak{p}(\tau)}
.
Moreover, it is well known that the zero locus of
h_{\tau}=\prod_{i=1}^{e_{\mathfrak{p}(\tau)}}h_{\tau,i}
in
X^{\mathrm{Ra}}
is non-empty for every
\tau\in{\overline{\Sigma}}
(see
[
2
, Cor. 8.18]
).
By Lemma
1.5
, the Hasse invariants
h_{\tau,i}
with
\tau\in{\overline{\Sigma}}
and
2\leq i\leq e_{\mathfrak{p}(\tau)}
are invertible on
X^{\mathrm{Ra}}
.
Therefore, it follows that the divisor
Z_{\tau,1}
is non-empty for every
\tau\in{\overline{\Sigma}}
.

Recall from Lemma
1.5
that the zero loci of two different generalised Hasse invariants do not have a common divisor. Let
j_{\sigma}
be the order of vanishing of a Hilbert modular form
f\neq 0
on
Z_{\sigma}
.
So, if we divide
f
by
\prod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}h_{\sigma}^{j_{\sigma}}
, we get the modular form

needed in Definition

.
Hence, it follows that the notion of minimal weight is indeed well defined (see also the proof of
[
2
, Thm. 8.19]
and
[
9
, §8]
).
∎

Lemma 1.12

Let

There is a finite subset

with

and

of

equals

In particular,
f
and

have the same geometric

Proof.

The result is trivial for

Seeing

as a meromorphic section of the line bundle
\bigotimes_{\tau\in{\overline{\Sigma}}}{\omega_{{\widetilde{\tau}}}}^{\otimes k_{\tau}}
over

be the order of vanishing of

along the divisor

for

and

Dividing

yields a holomorphic section on all of

is a surjective map of locally free sheaves with a locally free kernel over the normal scheme

, the orders of the poles of
\KS_{\tau}(d(r(f))_{\tau})
are less than or equal to the orders of the poles of

,

,

(see the proof of

for more details).
Note that

has no poles on
\widetilde{X}
,

i.e.
,
v_{\delta}(\pi^{*}f)\geqslant 0
.
Combining Lemma
1.25
(
ii
) and Lemma

, we get that
\Theta_{\tau}(f)
has no poles over
\widetilde{X}
.
Hence, the section obtained by descending from

to

extends to all of

∎

The effect of

on the geometric

and can be described as follows. The identification (
1.4

\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{c}_{+}\cup\{0\}}a_{\xi}(f)q^{\xi}
of

where the middle map is given by the idempotent at
\tau
.
By
[

we obtain the following

(1.42)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\tau}(f)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathfrak{c}_{+}}\bar{\tau}_{\mathfrak{c}}(1\otimes\xi)a_{\xi}q^{\xi}.

The proof of our main theorem uses the injectivity of
\Theta_{\tau}
on certain mod

Proposition 1.28
.

Let

and let

.
Suppose

and

does not divide

.

In particular, if the weight of

\Theta_{\tau}(f)\neq 0

Proof.

We follow the proof of

, Prop. 15.10]

be the uniformiser at the generic point of an irreducible component of the Weil divisor of

attached to

(

n:=v_{\delta}(r(f))=-\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}p^{j}k_{\phi^{j}\circ\tau}-(p^{f_{\mathfrak{p}}}-1)\sum_{j=1}^{e_{\mathfrak{p}}-1}k_{\tau,j}\equiv-k_{\tau,e_{\mathfrak{p}}}\pmod{p}.

Hence

and Lemma
1.26
(

2.
Doubling and Hecke algebras

2.1.
Hilbert modular forms of parallel weight
1

It is important to distinguish between Katz Hilbert modular forms defined on the fine moduli space and those on the coarse quotient by the action of the totally positive units of
{\mathfrak{o}}
. The latter enjoy the good Hecke theory for
\mathrm{GL}(2)
and are the natural objects to study
in relation with two dimensional Galois representations (see
[
14
]
).
In this section, we will define Hilbert modular forms of parallel weight building on Definition
1.1
.
Even though we give a definition valid in all levels
\mathfrak{n}
that are prime to
p
, we nevertheless need to consider the following condition (which is stronger than the one we imposed in §
1
) expressing that
\mathfrak{n}
is sufficiently divisible:

(2.43)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{$\mathfrak{n}$ is divisible by a prime above a prime number $q$ splitting completely in $F(\sqrt{\epsilon}\,|\,\epsilon\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{+}^{\times})$},\\
&\text{and such that $q\equiv-1\pmod{4\ell}$ for all prime numbers $\ell$ such that
$[F(\mu_{\ell}):F]=2$. }\end{split}

This condition ensures that
\mathcal{X}^{{\mathrm{DP}}}
is a scheme on which
[\epsilon]\in E={\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{+}/\{\epsilon\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}|\epsilon-1\in\mathfrak{n}\}^{2}
acts properly and discontinuously by sending
(A,\lambda,\mu)
to
(A,\epsilon\lambda,\mu)
(see
[
12
, Lem. 2.1(iii)]
). For any
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}
, any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
R
, and any parallel weight
k
, this induces an action of
E
on
M_{k}^{\mathrm{Katz}}(\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{n};R)
, whose invariants are denoted by
M_{k}^{\mathrm{Katz}}(\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{n};R)^{E}
. The following definition is equivalent to the one used in
[
14
, §2.2]
.

Definition 2.1
.

If
\mathfrak{n}
satisfies (
2.43
), then the space of Hilbert modular forms over a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
R
of (parallel) weight
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
and level
\mathfrak{n}
is given by

M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}}M_{k}^{\mathrm{Katz}}(\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{n};R)^{E},

where
k=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\kappa\sigma
.
For a general level
\mathfrak{n}
, let
\mathfrak{q}_{1}\neq\mathfrak{q}_{2}
be primes such that both
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}_{2}
satisfy (
2.43
) and define

M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)=M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}_{1},R)\cap M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}_{2},R),

where the intersection can be taken in
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}_{1}\mathfrak{q}_{2},R)
.
Note that the primes
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
can be chosen from a set of primes of positive density and that the definition does not depend on this choice.

For
f\in M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)
, we let
\sum_{\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{I}\cup\{(0)\}}a(\mathfrak{b},f)q^{\mathfrak{b}}
be the adelic
q
-expansion of
f
, where

\mathcal{I}
denotes the group of fractional ideals of
F
(see
[
14
, §2.6]
).

We denote by
S_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)
the
R
-submodule of
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)
consisting of Hilbert modular cuspforms.

For
f\in M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)
and
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}
, we will let
f_{\mathfrak{c}}
denote the corresponding
E
-invariant element of

M_{k}^{\mathrm{Katz}}(\mathfrak{c},\mathfrak{n};\mathbb{F})
or, equivalently, its geometric
q
-expansion at the cusp
\infty_{\mathfrak{c}}
(see
[
14
, §2.5]
).
Recall that when
\mathfrak{n}
satisfies (
2.43
),
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},R)
is endowed with Hecke and diamond operators (see
[
14
, §3.1-3.3]
).
When
\mathfrak{n}
is not sufficiently divisible, Hecke and diamond operators exist nonetheless because they stabilise the intersection
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}_{1},R)\cap M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n}\mathfrak{q}_{2},R)
, where the auxiliary primes
\mathfrak{q}_{1},\mathfrak{q}_{2}
may be chosen appropriately.
When it is not clear from the context, a superscript between brackets indicates the weight of the space of Hilbert modular forms on which an operator acts, e.g.
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}
.
Since we are interested in torsion coefficients, we let
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O})=\underset{n}{\underrightarrow{\lim}\;}M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
, where the inductive limit is taken by identifying
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
with the subspace
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\varpi\mathcal{O})

of
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n+1})
.

2.2.
Doubling

We shall rely on the following lifting result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{n}
satisfies (
2.43
).
There exists a
\kappa_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
\kappa\geqslant\kappa_{0}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the natural map

M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}\to M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})

is a Hecke equivariant isomorphism.

Proof.

The proof of
[
14
, Lem. 2.2]
works unchanged after replacing
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by
\mathcal{O}
and
p
by
\varpi^{n}
.
∎

We also need a generalisation of the total Hasse invariant modulo
\varpi^{n}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a
\kappa_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\kappa_{n}-1)
is a multiple of
(p-1)p^{n-1}
,
and a modular form
h_{n}\in M_{\kappa_{n}-1}(\mathfrak{o},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
having
q
-expansion equal to
1
at
\infty_{\mathfrak{c}}
for all
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}
. In particular, it does not vanish at any cusp.

Proof.

Let
h\in M_{p-1}(\mathfrak{o},\mathbb{F})
be the usual Hasse invariant (see
[
14
, §3.4]
). Note that since it exists for every level satisfying (
2.43
), it exists in level
\mathfrak{o}
. For
r
such that
r(p-1)
is big enough to apply Lemma
2.2
, the
modular form
h^{r}\in M_{r(p-1)}(\mathfrak{o},\mathcal{O})
has
q
-expansion congruent to
1
modulo
\varpi
at each cusp
\infty_{\mathfrak{c}}
,
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}
.
A big enough power of it satisfies the required congruence relation and condition on the weight.
∎

The theory of generalised
\Theta
-operators presented in §
1.5
allows us to prove the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{n}
satisfies (
2.43
).
Then there does not exist any
0\neq f\in M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathbb{F})
such that
f_{\mathfrak{c}}
has minimal weight at a fixed prime
\mathfrak{p}
dividing
p
(see Corollary
1.14
) for all
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}
and such that
a(\mathfrak{b},f)=0
for all ideals
\mathfrak{b}\subset{\mathfrak{o}}
not divisible by
\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

The minimality of the weight at
\mathfrak{p}
implies that there exists a
\tau\in{\overline{\Sigma}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
h_{\widetilde{\tau}}
does not divide
f_{\mathfrak{c}}
(the proof of Corollary
1.14
implies that this is true for all
\tau\in{\overline{\Sigma}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
).
Let
\mathfrak{b}=(\xi)\mathfrak{c}^{-1}
. Then, by definition,
a_{\xi}=a(\mathfrak{b},f)

and this is zero unless
\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{b}
, in which case
\mathfrak{p}\mid(\xi)
.
Thus, it follows that
\bar{\tau}_{\mathfrak{c}}(1\otimes\xi)=0
.
By (
1.42
), this shows that the geometric
q
-expansion of
\Theta_{\tau}(f_{\mathfrak{c}})
vanishes at
\infty_{\mathfrak{c}}
for all
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}
,
i.e.
,
\Theta_{\tau}(f_{\mathfrak{c}})=0
, contradicting the injectivity criterion from Proposition
1.28
.
∎

For
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
V_{\mathfrak{p}}
-operator by (see
[
6
]
, improving on and correcting previous works such as
[
15
]
and
[
14
]
, for the definition of
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}
)

V_{\mathfrak{p},n}={\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle}^{-1}(h_{n}T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})}h_{n})

with
h_{n}
and
\kappa_{n}
from Lemma
2.3
.
A simple computation on
q
-expansions (see
[
14
, Prop. 3.6]
) shows that
V_{\mathfrak{p},n}
has the following effect on adelic
q
-expansions:

\displaystyle a((0),V_{\mathfrak{p},n}f)

\displaystyle=a((0),f)[\mathfrak{p}^{-1}],

\displaystyle a(\mathfrak{r},V_{\mathfrak{p},n}f)

\displaystyle=a(\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\mathfrak{r},f)

for non-zero ideals
\mathfrak{r}\subseteq{\mathfrak{o}}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
be a prime and assume that
\mathfrak{n}
satisfies (
2.43
).

(i)

If
f\in S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O})
and
a(\mathfrak{b},f)=0
for all ideals
\mathfrak{b}\subset{\mathfrak{o}}
not divisible by
\mathfrak{p}
, then
f=0
.

(ii)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the ‘doubling map’

(h_{n},V_{\mathfrak{p},n}):S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{(f,g)\mapsto h_{n}f+V_{\mathfrak{p},n}g}M_{\kappa_{n}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})

is injective and compatible with the Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
for
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
.
The Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})}
acts on the image by the formula
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})}\circ(h_{n},V_{\mathfrak{p},n})=(h_{n},V_{\mathfrak{p},n})\circ\left(\begin{smallmatrix}T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}&1\\
-{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
In particular, the image
W_{\mathfrak{p},n}
of
(h_{n},V_{\mathfrak{p},n})
lies in the
\mathfrak{p}
-ordinary part of
M_{\kappa_{n}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
and is stable under all Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
for
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
.

If
(p-1)
does not divide
e_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then the same statements hold after replacing the spaces
S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O})
and
S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
by
M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O})
and
M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
, respectively.

Proof.

(
i
)
For
f\in S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi)
, the claim is precisely the content of Lemma
2.4
, in view of Corollaries
1.14
and
1.16
.
The induction step from
n-1
to
n
follows from the
q
-expansion principle and the exact sequence

0\to S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\varpi^{n-1}\mathcal{O}\to S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})\to S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n-1}).

(
ii
) The injectivity follows from (
i
) applied to the first component of an element in the kernel.
The matrix is obtained from a calculation as in
[
14
, Lem. 3.7]
.
∎

2.3.
Hecke algebras

For
\kappa\geqslant 1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the following complete Artinian (resp. Noetherian) semi-local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras

(2.44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{n}&=\Image\big(\mathcal{O}[T_{\mathfrak{q}},{\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle}]_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}))\big),\\
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}&=\Image\big(\mathcal{O}[T_{\mathfrak{q}},{\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle}]_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(S_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}))\big),\text{ resp.,}\\
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}&=\Image\big(\mathcal{O}[T_{\mathfrak{q}},{\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle}]_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O}))\big)=\varprojlim\limits_{n}\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{n},\\
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{\mathrm{cusp}}&=\Image\big(\mathcal{O}[T_{\mathfrak{q}},{\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle}]_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(S_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O}))\big)=\varprojlim\limits_{n}\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}.\end{split}

Note that they all contain
{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle}
for
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
since
p
is relatively prime to
\mathfrak{n}
.
Moreover, the restriction to the cusp space gives surjective morphisms
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}
and
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{\mathrm{cusp}}
. We also consider the torsion free Hecke
\mathcal{O}
-algebra:

\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{\mathcal{O}}=\Image\big(\mathcal{O}[T_{\mathfrak{q}},{\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle}]_{\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}))\big).

Let
I_{n}
be the annihilator of
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{\mathcal{O}}
acting on
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
. Then we have natural surjective ring homomorphisms

\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{\mathcal{O}}/I_{n}\textnormal{ and }\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{\mathcal{O}},

the latter coming from the fact that the intersection
\bigcap_{n}I_{n}
is zero.
For sufficiently large
\kappa
, both homomorphisms are isomorphisms due to Lemma
2.2
.
However, this need no longer be true in our principal case of interest
\kappa=1
since the inclusions

M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})\hookrightarrow M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})\textnormal{ and }M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(K/\mathcal{O})\hookrightarrow M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},K/\mathcal{O})

need not be isomorphisms, in general. The kernel of
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\mathcal{O}}
is a finitely generated torsion
\mathcal{O}
-module, which is isomorphic to the kernel of
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\mathcal{O}}/I_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.
Recall that multiplication by the Hasse invariant
h_{n}
allows us to see
M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
inside
M_{\kappa_{n}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
equivariantly for all Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle
for
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
, yielding a surjection
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{n}
(see Proposition
2.5
).
For a prime
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
, consider also the Hecke algebra:

(2.45)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n}=\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n}[T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})}]\subset\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{\kappa_{n}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}))\big).

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
. Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
there is a surjection sending
T^{(\kappa_{n})}_{\mathfrak{p}}
to
U
(considered as a polynomial variable):

\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}[T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)},U]/\big(U^{2}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}U+{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle}\big).

The same statement holds after replacing
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}
by
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{n}
, provided
(p-1)\nmid e_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

The injection from Proposition
2.5
gives a morphism
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}}\big(S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})^{\oplus 2}\big)

of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras compatible with
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle
for all
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
and, hence, we get a surjection
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}
.
Moreover,
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})}
acts on the image of
S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})^{\oplus 2}
via the matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}&1\\
-{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, whence
it is annihilated by its characteristic polynomial
U^{2}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}U+{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle}
and does not satisfy any non-trivial linear relation over
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{n}[T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}]
, thus proving the existence of the desired homomorphism.
For the surjectivity, let us observe that the image of
S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})^{\oplus 2}
is contained in the

T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})}
-ordinary subspace of
M_{\kappa_{n}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
, and that the endomorphism

T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})}+{\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle}(T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(\kappa_{n})})^{-1}
of the latter space acts on the former as
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}&0\\
0&T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
Finally, assuming
(p-1)\nmid e_{\mathfrak{p}}
allows us to apply Proposition
2.5
with
M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
instead of
S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
, leading to the validity of the result with
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}
replaced by
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{n}
.
∎

3.1.
Pseudo-representations of degree
2

In this section, we recall some definitions due to Chenevier
[

]
and Calegari–Specter
[

]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a complete Noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field

considered
with its natural

An

P=(T,D)
consisting of continuous maps

T,D:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to R

(i)

D
is a group homomorphism
\mathrm{G}_{F}\to R^{\times}
,

(ii)

T(1)=2
and
T(gh)=T(hg)=T(g)T(h)-D(g)T(g^{-1}h)
for all
g,h\in\mathrm{G}_{F}
.

We extend
T:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to R
to an
R
-linear map
R[\mathrm{G}_{F}]\to R
and we denote this map by
T
as well.

Given
g\in\mathrm{G}_{F}
, we define
D(g-1):=D(g)-T(g)+1
.

The pseudo-representation
P=(T,D)
is said to be unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
if

D(h-1)=T(g(h-1))=0
for all
g\in\mathrm{G}_{F}
and all
h\in\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Any continuous representation
\rho:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(R)
yields a degree
2

pseudo-representation
P_{\rho}=(\tr\circ\rho,\det\circ\rho)
. The converse is true when the semi-simple representation
\bar{\rho}:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
corresponding to the residual pseudo-representation is absolutely irreducible (see
[
5
, Thm. 2.22]
).
Further, if
\rho
is unramified outside a finite set of places
S
, then so is
P_{\rho}
. Again, the converse is true in the residually absolutely irreducible case. This can be seen by applying
loc. cit.
to the Galois group of the maximal extension of
F
unramified outside
S
over
F
.

We introduce a notion of ordinarity inspired from Calegari–Specter
[
4
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\widetilde{P}=(P,\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})
with
P=(T,D)
a degree
2
pseudo-representation of
\mathrm{G}_{F}
over
R
and
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}\in R

a root of
X^{2}-T(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})X+D(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})\in R[X]
.

We say that
\widetilde{P}
is ordinary at
\mathfrak{p}
of weight
\kappa\geqslant 1
, if for all
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and all
g\in\mathrm{G}_{F}
we have

(i)

D(h-1)=0
and
T(h-1)=\chi_{p}^{\kappa-1}(h)-1
, where
\chi_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic cyclotomic character,

(ii)

T\big(g(h-\chi_{p}^{\kappa-1}(h))(h^{\prime}\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}-\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})\big)=0
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that our notion of
\mathfrak{p}
-ordinary pseudo-representations implies the one of Calegari–Specter (
[
4
, Def. 2.5]
).
Let
P=(T,D):\mathrm{G}_{F}\to R^{2}
be a degree
2
pseudo-representation and let
(\bar{T},\bar{D}):\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\mathbb{F}^{2}
be its residual pseudo-representation.
Suppose there exists a lift
\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathrm{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}
of the arithmetic Frobenius at
\mathfrak{p}
such that the polynomial
X^{2}-\bar{T}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})X+\bar{D}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})
has
distinct
roots in
\mathbb{F}
.
Then
(P,\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-ordinary pseudo-representation in the sense of Definition
3.2
if and only if it is
\mathfrak{p}
-ordinary in the sense of Calegari–Specter.
However, if this hypothesis does not hold, then we expect that the two notions are not equivalent.

Let
\overline{P}=(\overline{T},\overline{D}):\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\mathbb{F}^{2}
be a fixed degree
2
pseudo-representation unramified outside
\mathfrak{n}p\infty
.
Denote by
P^{\mathrm{ps}}=(T^{\mathrm{ps}},D^{\mathrm{ps}}):\mathrm{G}_{F}\to(R^{\mathrm{ps}})^{2}
the universal deformation of
\overline{P}
unramified outside
\mathfrak{n}p\infty
in the category of complete Noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras with residue field
\mathbb{F}
and consider the quotient

(3.46)

\displaystyle R^{\mathrm{ps}}[X]/(X^{2}-T^{\mathrm{ps}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})X+D^{\mathrm{ps}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}))\twoheadrightarrow R^{\mathrm{ord}}

which classifies pairs
(P,\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})
such that
P
is a deformation of
\overline{P}
unramified outside
\mathfrak{n}p\infty
and
(P,\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})
is ordinary at
\mathfrak{p}
of weight
\kappa
.
The universal ring
R^{\mathrm{ord}}
, classifying deformations of
\overline{P}
which are unramified outside
\mathfrak{n}p\infty
and are ordinary at
\mathfrak{p}
of weight
\kappa
, is the quotient of the ring
R^{\mathrm{ps}}[X]/(X^{2}-T^{\mathrm{ps}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})X+D^{\mathrm{ps}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}))
by the ideal generated by the set

\left\{D^{\mathrm{ps}}(h-1),T^{\mathrm{ps}}(h-1)-\chi_{p}^{\kappa-1}(h)+1,T^{\mathrm{ps}}\big(g(h-\chi_{p}^{\kappa-1}(h))(h^{\prime}\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}-X)\big)\;|\;h,h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}},g\in\mathrm{G}_{F}\right\}

and a direct computation shows that
R^{\mathrm{ord}}
is independent of the choice of
\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Note that
R^{\mathrm{ord}}
is a finite
R^{\mathrm{ps}}
-algebra.
As
R^{\mathrm{ps}}
is a local ring, it follows that
R^{\mathrm{ord}}
is a semi-local ring and all of its maximal ideals contain the unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{{\mathrm{ps}}}
of
R^{{\mathrm{ps}}}
.
After going modulo
\mathfrak{m}^{{\mathrm{ps}}}
in
R^{\mathrm{ord}}
, it is easy to see, using the description of the ideal from the previous paragraph, that the number of maximal ideals of
R^{\mathrm{ord}}
is the number of distinct
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
such that
(\overline{P},\alpha)
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-ordinary pseudo-representation of weight
\kappa
.

Now suppose
\overline{P}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
and
\kappa\equiv 1\pmod{p-1}
. Then we have

\overline{T}\big(g(h-\chi_{p}^{\kappa-1}(h))(h^{\prime}\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}-X)\big)=\overline{T}\big(g(h-1)h^{\prime}\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)-X\overline{T}(g(h-1))=\overline{T}(h^{\prime}\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}}g(h-1))=0.

Here we are repeatedly using the fact that
\overline{T}(g(h-1))=0
for all
g\in G_{F}
and
h\in\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which is a consequence of the assumption that
\overline{P}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Thus, in this case, we see that
(\overline{P},\alpha)
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-ordinary pseudo-representation of weight
\kappa
if and only if
\alpha
is a root of the polynomial
X^{2}-\overline{T}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})X+\overline{D}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
Hence, in this case,
R^{\mathrm{ord}}
is a semi-local Noetherian ring with two maximal ideals if the polynomial
X^{2}-\overline{T}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})X+\overline{D}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})
has two distinct roots and it is a local Noetherian ring otherwise.

3.2.
Existence of an ordinary Hecke algebra-valued pseudo-representation

We continue to use the notation from §
2
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}
be any maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}
(or equivalently of
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{n}
for some

) and
denote also by
\mathfrak{m}
the maximal ideals of
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}
and
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n}

defined as the pull-back of

.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{n,\mathfrak{m}}
-valued pseudo-representation

of
\mathrm{G}_{F}
of degree
2

and

In particular, after replacing
\mathcal{O}
by a finite unramified extension, there exists a unique semi-simple Galois representation

for all primes
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
.

Proof.

After enlarging
K
, we may assume that it contains all the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}
acting on
M_{\kappa_{n}}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})
.
The
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}
generated by the Hecke operators outside the level and
p
is torsion-free and reduced, hence

\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa_{n})}_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K=\prod_{g\in\mathcal{N}}K

denotes the set of newforms occurring in

.
As is well known, one can attach to each such eigenform

a

-pseudo-representation

of degree

unramified outside

such that

P_{g}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}})=(a(\mathfrak{q},g),\psi_{g}(\mathfrak{q})\Norm(\mathfrak{q})^{\kappa_{n}-1})
for all
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
,
where

.
Since the natural homomorphism

(see
[
5
, Cor. 1.14]
).

Note that
\mathrm{N}(\mathfrak{q})^{\kappa_{n}-1}\equiv 1\pmod{\varpi^{n}}
.
Composing

with the surjection

Finally

along with
[

∎

M_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
throughout. The arguments continue to work if we assume that
p-1
does not divide
e_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which is used in Corollary
2.6
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
. Then
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{(1)}
, i.e., for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the Hecke operator

T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}
acts on
S_{1}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
by an element of
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{{\mathrm{cusp}},n}
.
Moreover, if
(p-1)\nmid e_{\mathfrak{p}}
, then one also has
T_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{T}^{(1)}
.

3.4.
Non-Eisenstein ideals

Definition 3.8
.

A maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}
(or of
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{(\kappa)}
) is called Eisenstein if the corresponding
(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(\kappa)}/\mathfrak{m})
-valued pseudo-representatation of
\mathrm{G}_{F}
is the sum of two
(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(\kappa)}/\mathfrak{m})
-valued characters.

We now prove that in the non-Eisenstein case it suffices to consider the cuspidal Hecke algebra.

Proposition 3.9
.

The localisation of the natural surjection
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{(\kappa)}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(\kappa)}_{n,\mathrm{cusp}}
at any non-Eisenstein maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}_{n}^{(\kappa)}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that the localisation of
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})/S_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
at a non-Eisenstein ideal vanishes.
By multiplication by a suitable power of
h_{n}
which does not vanish at any cusp (see Lemma
2.3
),
we can assume that
\kappa
is sufficiently large so that Lemma
2.2
applies yielding

M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})=M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
. Hence the natural Hecke equivariant morphism

M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})/S_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O})\to M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})/S_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})

is surjective. The former, however, can be Hecke equivariantly embedded into
M_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathbb{C})/S_{\kappa}(\mathfrak{n},\mathbb{C})
which is
well known to be generated by Eisenstein series whose Galois representations are reducible. This proves the proposition.
∎

Henceforth we assume
\mathfrak{m}
to be a non-Eisenstein ideal of
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}
, so that the corresponding
residual Galois representation
{\overline{\rho}}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is absolutely irreducible.
Therefore, by combining Theorem
0.1
with a result of Chenevier (
[
5
, Thm. 2.22]
), we get a representation

\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\mathfrak{m}}),

unramified outside
\mathfrak{n}p\infty
, and uniquely characterised by the property that for all primes
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
one has
\tr(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}}))=T_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
\det(\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{q}}))=\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle
.
By combining Theorem
0.1
with Proposition
3.9
, we deduce that the pseudo-representation
P^{(1)}_{\mathfrak{m}}

is unramified at all primes
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
and, by the discussion after Definition
3.1
, we conclude that

\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}
is unramified at these primes as well.
Let
S
be the set of places of
F
dividing
\mathfrak{n}\infty
and let
R^{S}_{F,{\overline{\rho}}_{\mathfrak{m}}}
be the universal deformation ring of
{\overline{\rho}}_{\mathfrak{m}}
unramified outside
S
in the category of complete Noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras with residue field
\mathbb{F}
.
Hence
\rho_{\mathfrak{m}}
induces an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra homomorphism
R^{S}_{F,{\overline{\rho}}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

As
\mathbb{T}^{(1)}
is generated by
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
\langle\mathfrak{q}\rangle
for
\mathfrak{q}\nmid\mathfrak{n}p
as an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.10
.

There exists a surjective homomorphism
R^{S}_{F,{\overline{\rho}}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{(1)}_{\mathfrak{m}}
of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras.

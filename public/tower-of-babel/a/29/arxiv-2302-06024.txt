The central limit theorem on nilpotent Lie groups
Timothée Bénard, Emmanuel Breuillard

Abstract
We formulate and establish the central limit theorem for products of i.i.d. random variables on arbitrary simply connected nilpotent Lie groups, allowing a possible bias. We find that some interesting new phenomena arise in the presence of a bias: the walk spreads out at a higher rate in the ambient group, while the limiting hypoelliptic diffusion process may not always have full support. We use elementary Fourier analysis to establish our results which include Berry-Esseen bounds under optimal moment assumptions, as well as an analogue of Donsker’s invariance principle. Various examples of nilpotent Lie groups are treated in detail showing the variety of different behaviours. We also obtain a characterization of when the limiting distribution is an ordinary gaussian and answer a question of Tutubalin regarding asymptotically close distributions on nilpotent Lie groups.

Introduction

Given a Lie group
G
and a probability measure
\mu
on
G
, it is a natural problem to investigate the large scale geometry of the convolution powers
\mu^{*N}
for large
N
, or, in other words, the distribution of the product
X_{1}\cdots X_{N}
of
N
independent random variables in
G
with law
\mu
. We refer the reader to the survey
[
SC01
]
for a pleasant introduction to the topic, as well as to the books
[
Gre63
,
VSCC92
,
Lia04
,
BQ16
,
Var21
]
.

In the abelian case
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the answer is provided by the classical central limit theorem: provided
\mathbb{E}(X_{1}^{2})<\infty
, we have the convergence in law:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(X_{1}+\dots+X_{N}-N\chi\right)\underset{N\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma^{2}),

where
\chi
and
\sigma^{2}
denote the expectation and covariance matrix of
X_{1}
respectively, and
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
is the centered Gaussian distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with covariance matrix
\sigma^{2}
.

What is meant by a central limit theorem in a general Lie group
G
is less clear, because there rarely is a canonical way to renormalize the random walk. See
[
Gre63
]
,
[
ST66
, p. 3]
,
[
Vir74
]
,
[
Bre04
, Chapter 1]
for a discussion. However, if the Lie group
G
is nilpotent and simply connected, then
G
is diffeomorphic to its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
via the exponential map
[
CG90
]
and, although there may not exist any dilating automorphisms
[
Dye70
,
DL57
]
, there are natural ways to renormalize the space, leading to meaningful limit theorems.

In this paper we shall be interested in the central limit theorem for i.i.d. random walks on an arbitrary simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G
. These are the simply connected real Lie groups
G
whose central descending series
G^{[i+1]}:=[G,G^{[i]}]
terminates at the trivial group in a finite number of steps. They can alternatively be described as the connected subgroups of unipotent upper triangular matrices
[
CG90
, Theorem 1.1.1]
.

In his early work on the subject, Tutubalin
[
Tut64
]
handled successfully the case where
G
is the Heisenberg group. It is the simplest instance of a non-commutative nilpotent Lie group.

Example 1

(Heisenberg group)
.

Let
G
be the classical
3
-dimensional Heisenberg group, defined as
\mathbb{R}^{3}=\oplus_{i=1}^{3}\mathbb{R}e_{i}
endowed with the group structure

x*y=(x_{1}+y_{1},\,x_{2}+y_{2},\,x_{3}+y_{3}+\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})).

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
satisfying the moment conditions
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(|x_{i}|^{2})<\infty
for
i=1,2
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(|x_{3}|)<\infty
. The mean of
\mu
in the abelianization is identified with
X_{\mu}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2})
. Let
r>0
. If
X_{\mu}=0
, set
D_{r}:x\mapsto(x_{1}r,x_{2}r,x_{3}r^{2})
, while if
X_{\mu}\neq 0
set
D_{r}:x\mapsto(x_{1}r,x_{2}r,x_{3}r^{3})
.

In the centered case
X_{\mu}=0
, Tutubalin
[
Tut64
]
proves that the renormalized distribution
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N})
converges to a probability measure on
G
, which is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue. In fact, it is the law of the value at time
t=1
of a hypoelliptic diffusion
(B^{(1)}_{t},B^{(2)}_{t},L_{t})
on
G
, where
(B^{(1)}_{t},B^{(2)}_{t})
is a planar Brownian motion and
L_{t}
is its Lévy area (see e.g.
[
FV10
]
).

On the other hand, if
X_{\mu}\neq 0
, Tutubalin
[
Tut64
]
shows that the recentered and renormalized distribution
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}})
, i.e. the distribution of
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(x*(-NX_{\mu}))
when
x\sim\mu^{*N}
, converges towards an ordinary gaussian (normal) distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Notice that
\det D_{r}=r^{3}
in the centered case, while
\det D_{r}=r^{5}
in the non-centered case. This means that
the random walk “spreads out” more in space in the presence of a non-trivial bias.

For an arbitrary simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G
, the central limit theorem (CLT) for
centered
random walks, i.e. for measures
\mu
whose projection to the abelianization
G/[G,G]
has zero mean, is by now a classical result and several proofs are known. The case where
G
is stratified follows easily from Wehn’s infinitesimal CLT
[
Weh62
]
or from Stroock-Varadhan
[
SV73
]
, while the general case is handled by Crépel and Raugi in
[
CR78
]
. See also
[
CCLP99
]
for a more recent treatment of the centered CLT for stratified nilpotent Lie groups. Berry-Esseen estimates have also been derived by Pap
[
Pap91
]
and Bentkus-Pap
[
BP96
]
in the stratified centered case. Last but not least, in the case where the driving measure
\mu
has a continuous density, Alexopoulos
[
Ale02a
,
Ale02c
]
obtained very complete results for the centered CLT on an arbitrary nilpotent Lie group including Berry-Esseen bounds and even a full Edgeworth expansion.

Our main goal in this paper will be to formulate and establish the general case of the CLT and the Berry-Esseen estimates, see Theorems
1.1

1.6
, allowing the group to be non-stratified and most importantly the walk to be
non-centered
(we also say “biased”). We shall make no assumption of regularity on the measure
\mu
, only necessary moment assumptions. We will also prove an analogue of Donsker’s invariance principle in our context (
Theorem

1.7
), establishing that the stochastic process obtained by interpolating the random walk does converge in law towards a limiting diffusion process in the topology of Hölder continuous paths (and even in the “rough paths” topology), see
Section

5.3
.

Unlike the case of commutative groups,
the analysis of non-centered walks does not reduce to that of centered walks
.
It is true that certain questions for non-centered walks can be solved by reducing to the centered case, for example the behavior of
\mu^{*n}
in a neighborhood of the origin: as Alexopoulos observes in
[
Ale02a
, 1.11]
,
d\mu^{*n}(x)=e^{-\beta_{\mu}n}\chi_{\mu}(x)d\mu_{0}^{*n}(x)
where
\mu_{0}
is a centered probability measure,
\beta_{\mu}>0
, and
\chi_{\mu}
is a well chosen character of the group. This however yields no information on the kind of questions we are considering in this paper, namely the behavior of
\mu^{*n}
around its average, where most of the mass is located. A delicate issue in establishing a central limit theorem for biased walks is to understand precisely how to renormalize the random walk after recentering, in order to obtain an absolutely continuous probability measure in the limit. The large scale geometry of the distribution
\mu^{*N}
depends in a subtle way on the increment average and attempts to find a suitable renormalization have been made by Virtser
[
Vir74
]
for the full upper-triangular unipotent matrix group, then by Raugi
[
Rau78
]
for general nilpotent Lie groups. However neither formulation is fully satisfactory: Virtser’s renormalization is too strong, leading in some cases to non-absolutely continuous distributions in the limit, whereas Raugi’s renormalization may lead to full escape of mass in the limit (see the end of
Section

5.1
for a detailed discussion). We shall show the right way to renormalize non-centered random walks on nilpotent Lie groups, so that the limiting distribution is always a smooth probability measure.

We also present a detailed analysis of the limiting measure. In particular, we give decay estimates at infinity (see
Proposition

3.8
) proving that the limit density is a rapidly decreasing Schwartz function similarly to what is known in the centered case
[
Heb89
,
Var88
,
Sie84
]
. It came as a surprise to us to discover that a new phenomenon takes place in the biased case, and only in step
3
or more: due to certain inaccessibility constraints
[
Sus87
,
AS04
,
Sac07
]
appearing in the presence of a bias,
the limiting measure may not have full support
in general and the identity can lie on the boundary of the support, even though it is absolutely continuous. This phenomenon occurs in all free nilpotent Lie groups of step at least
3
whenever there is a non-zero bias (see
Theorem

1.2
). On the other hand there are Lie groups, such as the upper triangular unipotent groups, where it never happens and the limit measure always has full support (see
Theorem

1.3
). For such groups we will also establish a lower bound on the density, by exploiting a version of Harnack’s inequality for hypoelliptic operators recently established in
[
KP16
]
. Yet there are also examples, such as filiform Lie algebras of dimension at least
4
, where both phenomena occur depending on the increment average. Typically the limiting measure is far from being an ordinary Gaussian distribution, but we shall also characterize exactly when this is the case (
Theorem

1.4
).

In the context of hypoelliptic heat kernel estimates and diffusion processes on manifolds, the consequences of a non-trivial drift for the support and the small time estimates have been studied in several influential papers, starting with those of Ben Arous and Léandre
[
BeLe91
]
(see also
[
AKS93
]
and more recently
[
CHT21
]
). In these papers the drift refers to a first order term in the infinitesimal generator of the diffusion. In our context, the non-centeredness assumption on the driving measure is not reflected by a first order term in the infinitesimal generator of the limiting diffusion. Rather, it forces the generator to be time dependent. This time dependency plays a crucial role here, especially as the generator obtained for a frozen time t is not hypoelliptic. Below, we will present examples of limiting measures with non-full support and also no drift term at all in the (hypoelliptic) infinitesimal generator.

We note that our central limit theorem also applies to random walks on
finitely generated
(discrete) nilpotent groups. Indeed a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group can always be realized as a co-compact discrete subgroup of its Malcev closure, which is a connected and simply connected nilpotent Lie group
[
CG90
]
(the torsion subgroup is a finite normal subgroup, which does not play a role in the asymptotic behavior of the random walk). We refer the reader to
[
Ale02b
,
Bre10
,
Ish03
,
IKN20
,
IKN21
,
DH21
]
for related articles where limit theorems for random walks on finitely generated nilpotent groups as well as on nilpotent covers of compact manifolds are obtained.

Contrary to previous work on the nilpotent CLT, which relied on martingale techniques
[
SV73
,
Vir74
,
CCLP99
]
or convergence theorems for semigroups of contracting operators
[
Weh62
,
CR78
,
Rau78
]
, our proof follows a rather down-to-earth Fourier analytic approach, which allows for greater flexibility and provides directly information on the rate of convergence. Similar techniques are employed by the second named author
[
Bre05
]
and by Diaconis and Hough
[
DH21
,
Hou19
]
in their work on the centered local limit theorem. Similarly as in Lindeberg’s proof of the classical CLT on
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
Fel68
]
), we shall use a
replacement scheme
, where at each step one inserts a new random variable distributed according to the limit measure. However, we will first need to replace the Lie group product
*
by an associated graded product
*^{\prime}
, for which the renormalization acts by automorphisms. Coordinatewise, we are led to consider random variables of the form:

\sum_{n_{1}<\ldots<n_{t}\leq N}M(X_{n_{1}},\ldots,X_{n_{t}})

(1)

where
M
is a fixed polynomial function on
\mathfrak{g}
, and the
X_{i}
’s represent i.i.d. variables with law
\mu
. Lindeberg’s replacement scheme for polynomials has also been exploited recently in a different context in works on noise stability and influences
[
MOO10
,
Cha06
]
, see also
[
Rot75
]
.

We now pass to the more precise description of our results.

1.1
Main results

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We identify
G
with
\mathfrak{g}
via the exponential map.
Let
s
be the step (or nilpotency class) of
\mathfrak{g}
, that is the smallest integer
s
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{[s+1]}=\{0\}
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}:=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{[i-1]}]
is the central descending series. We denote by
*
the Lie product on
G
, which we view as being defined on
\mathfrak{g}
. By abuse of notation we also denote by
*
the associated convolution product of measures on
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that
*
is given by a polynomial map on
\mathfrak{g}
as follows from the Campbell-Hausdorff formula
[
Dyn47
]
:

x*y=x+y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]+\frac{1}{12}[x,[x,y]]+\frac{1}{12}[y,[y,x]]+\dots

We consider a random walk
S_{n}=X_{1}*\ldots*X_{n}
associated to a probability measure
\mu
on
(\mathfrak{g},*)
. We let
\mu_{ab}
be the projection of
\mu
onto the abelianization
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}:=\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})\in\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
the mean.

It turns out that the large scale behaviour of
S_{n}
on the Lie group, is captured by a certain filtration of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely a certain decreasing sequence of ideals, whose definition depends only on
\overline{X}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
,

\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(t+1)}=\{0\}

such that
[\mathfrak{g}^{(i)},\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i+j)}
for all
i,j
.

In
the centered case
, i.e. when
\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})=0
, the filtration is simply the central descending series, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}
and
t=s
. The projection of the walk to
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
exhibits diffusive behavior and will therefore be of order
\sqrt{n}
as
n
grows. On the other hand the coordinates of
S_{n}
lying deeper in the filtration, i.e. in say
\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}\smallsetminus\mathfrak{g}^{[i+1]}
, grow faster at a rate
n^{i/2}
. Therefore if we renormalize the walk accordingly we may hope that it converges towards a non-degenerate random variable on
\mathfrak{g}
, analogous say to the Gaussian law in the abelian case.
For a precise formulation, let us fix supplementary vector subspaces
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}
. This induces a direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{(s)}
, whose coordinate projections we denote by

\displaystyle\pi^{(i)}:\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi^{(i)}(x)=x^{(i)}.

Let us define a one-parameter family of
dilations

(D_{r})_{r>0}
by the formula

D_{r}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g},\,\,\,D_{r}(x)=\sum_{i\geq 1}r^{i}x^{(i)}.

(2)

Given
r>0
and a Borel measure
\eta
on
\mathfrak{g}
, we write
D_{r}(\eta)
the push-forward measure of
\eta
by
D_{r}
, it is characterized by the relation
D_{r}(\eta)(f)=\eta(f\circ D_{r})
for every measurable function
f:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
The centered CLT for the
\mu
-walk on
(\mathfrak{g},*)
claims the convergence in law

D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N})\to\nu

(3)

where
\nu
is a certain smooth probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
. It also involves a description of
\nu
as the value at time
t=1
of a certain explicit one-parameter semigroup of probability measures
(\sigma_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. However, one must be careful that the semigroup structure for
\sigma_{t}
refers to a new Lie group product
*^{\prime}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, inherited from the choice of subspaces
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
and defined by

a*^{\prime}b=\lim_{t\to+\infty}D_{1/t}(D_{t}(a)*D_{t}(b)).

(4)

Equivalently,
*^{\prime}
is the Lie group structure induced by the new Lie bracket
[x,y]^{\prime}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, defined on
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\times\mathfrak{m}^{(j)}
as the projection of
[x,y]
onto
\mathfrak{m}^{(i+j)}
parallel to the other
\mathfrak{m}^{(k)}
. With this new Lie structure,
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
is again an
s
-step nilpotent Lie group, and the dilations
D_{r}
become automorphisms. The semigroup
(\sigma_{t})_{t>0}
can now be defined (as in
[
Hun56
]
) by way of its infinitesimal generator

L_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\leq\dim\mathfrak{g}_{ab}}E^{2}_{i}+B

(5)

where
E_{i},B
denote left invariant vectors fields on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
, with the
E_{i}
’s forming a basis of
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
in which the covariance matrix of
\mu_{ab}
is the identity, while
B\in\mathfrak{m}^{(2)}
is
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(x^{(2)})
. The measure
\nu=\sigma_{1}
admits a smooth and exponentially decaying density, which may be characterized alternatively as the value at time
t=1
of the fundamental solution (heat kernel)
u:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
to the PDE

\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\leq\dim\mathfrak{g}_{ab}}E^{2}_{i}+B=0.

(6)

Here, we identify a vector field on
\mathfrak{g}
with the corresponding Lie derivative.

A proof of the centered central limit theorem (
3
) can be found in the work of Tutubalin
[
Tut64
]
in the case of the Heisenberg group and Crepel and Raugi
[
CR78
]
in general, with roots in the early work of Wehn
[
Weh62
]
who proved an infinitesimal CLT on general Lie groups, see also
[
Gre63
,
SV73
,
Bre04
]
. Note that the limit law
\nu
is entirely determined by the covariance matrix of
\mu_{ab}=\pi^{(1)}(\mu)
and the mean of
\pi^{(2)}(\mu)
, where
\pi^{(i)}(\mu)
denotes the push-forward of
\mu
by
\pi^{(i)}
. The convergence (
3
) holds assuming the existence of a finite second moment, i.e.
d(0,x)\in L^{2}(\mu)
for some (any) left invariant geodesic distance
d
on
(\mathfrak{g},*)
or
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
. Equivalently,
\|x^{(i)}\|^{1/i}\in L^{2}(\mu)
for each
i
.

Example 2
.

Let
G
be the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg group, so

\mathfrak{g}=\{xe_{1}+ye_{2}+ze_{3}\,|\,[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3},[e_{1},e_{3}]=[e_{2},e_{3}]=0\},

and assume that
\mu_{ab}
is normalized, that is
\mathbb{E}(x^{2})=\mathbb{E}(y^{2})=1
,
\mathbb{E}(xy)=\mathbb{E}(x)=\mathbb{E}(y)=\mathbb{E}(z)=0
. Then
\nu
coincides with the distribution of the random variable
Y=(x(1),y(1),z(1))
, where
(x(t),y(t))
is a standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
z(t)
is the Lévy area (signed area between the cord and the brownian path). The density of
\nu
has been computed by Paul Lévy
[
Lév51
]
(see also
[
Gav77
, Théorème 1]
):

d\nu(x,y,z)=\frac{dxdydz}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cos(2\xi z)\frac{\xi}{\sinh{\xi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})\xi/\tanh(\xi)}d\xi.

For centered walks on general nilpotent Lie groups there is in general no known closed formula for the limit density
u(1,x)
of
\nu=u(1,x)dx
, but so-called
Gaussian estimates
that control its decay at infinity are available
[
Heb89
,
Var88
,
VSCC92
]
. Namely there is
C>0
such that for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
:

\frac{1}{C}\exp(-Cd(0,x)^{2})\leq u(1,x)\leq C\exp(-d(0,x)^{2}/C)

(7)

with a similar upper bound for all partial derivatives of
u(1,.)
. See also
[
AG16
]
where certain explicit formulas for
u(1,x)
are derived in the case of filiform Lie algebras.

Now let us turn to
the non-centered case
, i.e. when
\overline{X}_{\mu}:=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
is possibly non-zero. Then, as in the abelian case, the random walk
S_{n}
needs to be translated by
-nx
for some representative
x
of
\overline{X}_{\mu}
in
\mathfrak{g}
, so that its projection to
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
has zero mean and we can then attempt to renormalize the resulting random variable. It turns out that the right filtration to be considered for the renormalization is finer and that it depends on the way
[x,.]
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
. Indeed, we are looking at the random product:

X_{1}*\ldots*X_{n}*(-nx)=Y_{1}*Y_{2}^{x}*\ldots*Y_{n}^{(n-1)x}

(8)

where
Y_{i}:=X_{i}*x^{-1}
and
Y^{g}:=g*Y*g^{-1}
. This is now a product of independent centered variables, though they are no longer identically distributed, due to the iterated adjoint action of
x
.

The drift of the random walk is linear in the
x
direction, while it only moves diffusively, at rate
n^{1/2}
, in the other directions of
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, so in effect
x
behaves as a bracket of order
2
even though it is not in the commutator subgroup. In order to take this into account, we introduce a new filtration as follows. For
i\leq 2s-1
, we let
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
be the subspace generated by brackets of length
a
in which
x
appears at least
b
times, where
a+b\geq i
. For
i=2
however we set
\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
rather than
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]+\mathbb{R}x
. Equivalently,
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
is defined recursively by setting
\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}]+[x,\mathfrak{g}^{(i-1)}]
for
i\geq 1
, while
\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{g}
.

We thus obtain a nested sequence of ideals:

\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}}_{\mathfrak{g}}\supseteq\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}}_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}\supseteq\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}}_{[\mathfrak{g},x]+[[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g},]\mathfrak{g}]}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(2s)}=0.

We call the filtration
(\mathfrak{g}^{(i)})_{i\leq 2s-1}
the
weight filtration
of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
\overline{X}_{\mu}
. Indeed , note that it does not depend on the choice of representative
x\in\mathfrak{g}
of
\overline{X}_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
. Furthermore
\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
for each
i
with equality if
\overline{X}_{\mu}=0
. We then choose supplementary subspaces
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
as before so that
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}
and denote by
X_{\mu}
the representative in
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
:

\overline{X}_{\mu}=X_{\mu}+[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\,\,\,X_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}.

(9)

We keep the notation
y^{(i)}=\pi^{(i)}(y)
to denote the projection of
y\in\mathfrak{g}
onto
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
. We also define the dilations
D_{r}
as in (
2
) above, and the
*^{\prime}
Lie product as in (
4
). We warn the reader that, unlike in the centered case,
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
may no longer be bracket-generating in
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
as happens already in the Heisenberg group case. Finally we define the nilpotent operator
a_{X_{\mu}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by setting

a_{X_{\mu}}(y)=\pi^{(i+2)}([X_{\mu},y])\textnormal{ when }y\in\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}.

(10)

Given a probability measure
\mu
on
G
, we say that it has a finite moment of order
m\in(0,+\infty)
for the weight filtration if
y\mapsto\|y^{(i)}\|^{m/i}
is in
L^{1}(\mu)
for each
i
(where we take some norm, say Euclidean, on each
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
). Since
\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
and the inclusion can be strict when
\overline{X}_{\mu}\neq 0
, this is a slightly weaker constraint on
\mu
than the ordinary notion of moment on nilpotent Lie groups.

Theorem 1.1

(Non-centered CLT)
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
with finite second moment for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}_{\mu}:=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})\in\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
. Assume that the support of
\mu_{ab}
is not contained in a proper affine subspace of
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
. Then

D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}})\underset{N\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\nu

(11)

where
\nu
is a smooth probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
. More precisely, writing
\nu_{t}:=D_{\sqrt{t}}(\nu)
, we have
\nu_{t}=u(t,x)dx
where the density
u(t,x)
is the (smooth) fundamental solution to the following hypoelliptic time-dependent PDE:

\partial_{t}v=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\leq\dim\mathfrak{g}_{ab}}(\exp(ta_{X_{\mu}})E_{i})^{2}v-(\exp(ta_{X_{\mu}})B_{\mu})v.

(12)

Here the
E_{i}
’s form a basis of
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
in which
\pi^{(1)}(\mu)
has identity covariance matrix. And
B_{\mu}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(x^{(2)})
is the “commutator mean” of
\mu
.

In (
12
), a vector
X\in\mathfrak{g}
is seen as a left invariant vector field on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
, with the corresponding Lie derivative

Xf=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{f(x*^{\prime}\varepsilon X)-f(x)}{\varepsilon}.

Remark
. It follows from the theorem that the limit measure
\nu
depends only on
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
,
\Cov(\mu_{ab})
the covariance matrix of
\mu_{ab}
, the mean of
\mu
in projection to
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}
, and the choice of weight decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i}\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
.

Remark
. The result remains valid verbatim if we replace the translation
\delta_{-NX_{\mu}}
in (
11
) by an arbitrary one
\delta_{-NX_{\mu}+g_{N}}
, provided the sequence
g_{N}\in\mathfrak{g}
is such that
\|g_{N}^{(i)}\|=o(N^{i/2})
for each
i\geq 1
. We may also replace the multiplicative recentering by an additive recentering, up to adjusting the limiting smooth probability measure, see
5.5
.

We shall also establish upper estimates on the density
\nu=u(1,x)dx
, namely the existence of constants
C,c>0
such that

u(1,x)\leq C\exp(-d^{\prime}(0,x)^{c})

(13)

where
d^{\prime}
is a left-invariant geodesic (e.g. Riemannian) metric on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
, and similar estimates hold for all partial derivatives of
u(1,.)
(see
Proposition

3.8
), showing that
u(1,.)
is a Schwartz function on
\mathfrak{g}
.

The question of a lower bound is more subtle. In the centered case, Gaussian lower estimates have been known for a while
[
Var88
,
VSCC92
]
. However, when one authorizes the walk to be non-centered, the picture changes drastically as the support of the limiting distribution
\nu
may not even coincide with
\mathfrak{g}
. In this case, the support of
\nu
is a proper closed subset with non-empty (dense) interior. Describing the support is a question related to
control theory
on Lie groups (
[
Sac07
]
). We compute the support in an explicit example in the case of the filiform Lie algebra of step
3
(
3.23
). This leads to the following:

Theorem 1.2

(Never full support)
.

Assume
G
is a
free
nilpotent Lie group of step at least
3
. Then for any
non-centered
probability measure
\mu
on
G
satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
, the support of the limiting distribution
\nu
does not contain the identity in its interior.

At the extreme opposite, we identify a class of nilpotent Lie groups for which every limiting distribution must have full support (
Section

3.3.1
), and give a satisfactory lower bound (
Corollary

3.32
). This class contains the upper triangular unipotent groups

Upp_{d}(\mathbb{R}):=\{a_{ij}\in\SL_{d}(\mathbb{R});a_{ii}=1,i>j\Rightarrow a_{ij}=0\},

or any nilpotent Lie group of step at most
2
. It is defined by the
double cancellation condition
(
DC
): there exists a bracket-generating family
(v_{i})
of elements in
(\mathfrak{g},[.,.])
, such that any bracket of the form
[v_{i},[v,[\dots[v,v_{i}]\dots]]
vanishes.

Theorem 1.3

(Full support and lower bound)
.

Assume
(\mathfrak{g},[.,.])
satisfies
(
DC
)
. Then for any probability measure
\mu
on
G
as in
Theorem

1.1
, the support of the limiting measure
\nu
is all of
\mathfrak{g}
and its density
u(1,x)
satisfies:
\exists\alpha,A>0
,
\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}
,

u(1,x)\geq\alpha\exp(-A\,d^{\prime}(0,x)^{2}).

In
Section

3.4
we discuss a general lower bound valid in general even when the limiting distribution is not of full support. Using the recent work
[
KP16
]
, we provide an answer in terms
Harnack chains
whose increments are not too close to the boundary of the support, see
Theorem

3.28
. This analysis allows us to establish that
u(1,x)
does not vanish on the interior of the support. As as by-product, our lower bound allows us to characterize the case where
\nu
is an ordinary Gaussian distribution (in the ordinary Euclidean sense). The implication i)
\implies
iii) below is due to Crepel-Raugi
[
CR78
]
.

Theorem 1.4

(Characterization of the Gaussian case)
.

Keep the assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
. The following are equivalent.

i)

For all
a\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, we have

[X_{\mu},\mathfrak{g}^{[a]}]=\mathfrak{g}^{[a+1]}.

ii)

The eigenvalues of
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}
are exactly
\{N^{-(2k-1)/2},\,1\leq k\leq s\}
.

iii)

The limit measure
\nu
is Gaussian (in the classical Euclidean sense) on
\mathfrak{g}
.

In
[
Tut64
]
(see also
[
ST66
, §5.4]
) Tutubalin asks for necessary and sufficient conditions for a pair of probability measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
on a nilpotent Lie group to be
asymptotically close
in the sense that

\sup_{A\in\mathcal{C}}|\mu_{1}^{*n}(A)-\mu_{2}^{*n}(A)|\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

where
\mathcal{C}
is a certain class of Borel subsets of
\mathfrak{g}
, which for definiteness we will take to be the class of all convex subsets. Solutions were provided in the case of the upper triangular unipotent
n\times n
matrix group, first by Tutubalin
[
Tut64
, §6]
for
n=3
, then by Virtser
[
Vir74
, Theorem 5]
for arbitrary
n
and only certain values of
\overline{X}_{\mu}
. Thanks to
Theorem

1.1
we can answer Tutubalin’s question in any simply connected nilpotent Lie group.

Corollary 1.5

(Characterization of asymptotically close measures)
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Consider two probability measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
on
G
with a finite second moment for their respective weight filtrations and non singular covariance matrix in the abelianization.
The measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are asymptotically close if and only if their projections to
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
have the same mean and covariance matrix, and their projections to
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}
have the same mean.

We recall that
\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}=[\mathfrak{g},[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]]+[\overline{X}_{\mu},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
. In particular the equivalence classes of aymptotically close measures are determined by a finite number of parameters. This is in sharp contrast with what happens in semisimple Lie groups, see
[
ST66
]
,
[
Bou81
]
.

While previous approaches for proving the CLT were based either on probabilistic methods (martingale convergence
[
SV73
,
Vir74
]
) or functional analytic methods (perturbation of operator semigroups
[
Weh62
,
CR78
,
Rau78
]
), we use characteristic functions. This approach is motivated by
[
DH21
,
Hou19
]
. This has a pay-off as it allows us to obtain Berry-Esseen bounds, yielding information on the rate of convergence.
It is formulated in terms of the moments of
\mu
. Given any
r>0
and a choice of norm on
\mathfrak{g}
, the
r
-th moment of
\mu
(for the weight filtration) is defined by:

m_{r}(\mu):=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 2s-1}\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\|x^{(i)}\|^{r/i}d\mu(x).

Theorem 1.6

(Berry-Esseen estimate)
.

Keep the assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
. Then for every smooth function of compact support
f

on
\mathfrak{g}
we have for all
N\geq 1

|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}})(f)-\nu(f)|\leq\varepsilon_{N}\max_{|\alpha|\leq\dim\mathfrak{g}+4}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{1}}

where
\varepsilon_{N}=o_{\mu}(1)
.
Furthermore, if
\mu
has a finite third moment for the weight filtration, then we can take

\varepsilon_{N}\leq C(1+m_{2}(\mu)^{c_{s}})(1+m_{3}(\mu))N^{-1/2}

where
s\geq 1
is the nilpotency class of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
c_{s}=6s^{2}
while
C>0
is a constant depending only on
(\mathfrak{g},[.,.])
, a choice of norm and Haar measure, the line
\mathbb{R}\overline{X}_{\mu}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
, and the weight decomposition
(\mathfrak{m}^{(i)})_{i}
.

Here,
\partial^{\alpha}
refers to partial derivatives of
f
(in the classical sense on a vector space), and
|\alpha|
is the order of the derivation.

Finally, we shall prove that the previous CLT can be upgraded to the convergence of the full interpolated process in the spirit of Donsker’s theorem
[
RY99
, Theorem XIII.1.9]
. We consider:

W^{(N)}(t):=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\big(X_{1}*\dots*X_{\lfloor tN\rfloor}*(tN-\lfloor tN\rfloor)X_{\lfloor tN\rfloor+1}*-tNX_{\mu}\big)

where the
X_{i}
’s are i.i.d. with law
\mu
. Note that
W^{(N)}
defines a random variable with values in the space of locally Lipschitz paths on
\mathfrak{g}
.

In view of (
12
), the natural candidate to be the limit of
W^{(N)}
is the continuous left-invariant diffusion process on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
whose infinitesimal generator at time
t\geq 0
(see
Section

3.1
) is

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\left(\exp(ta_{X_{\mu}})E_{i}\right)^{2}+\exp(ta_{X_{\mu}})B_{\mu}.

(14)

Denoting by
\sigma=\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
the law of the diffusion on the space of paths,
Theorem

1.1
establishes the convergence in law of
W^{(N)}(1)
towards
\sigma_{1}
.

The process associated to
(\mathscr{L}_{t})_{t}
will have
\alpha
-Hölder sample paths for any
\alpha\in[0,1/2)
. We endow the space
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
\alpha
-Hölder paths with the topology of
\alpha
-Hölder convergence on compact intervals. It is induced by the (separable complete) metric
d_{\alpha}=\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{-n}d_{\alpha,n}
, where:

d_{\alpha,n}((x(t))_{t},(y(t))_{t}):=\|x(0)-y(0)\|+\sup_{t\neq s\in[0,n]}\frac{\|x(s)^{-1}*x(t)-y(s)^{-1}*y(t)\|}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}.

The
*
operation in this formula could be replaced with
*^{\prime}
or
+
without changing the induced topology on
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathfrak{g})
. Note that convergence in the
\alpha
-Hölder topology is more and more restrictive as
\alpha
grows, and implies uniform convergence on compact intervals (case
\alpha=0
).

Theorem 1.7

(Central limit theorem for processes)
.

Keep the assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
and assume further that
\mu
has finite moments of all orders. Then for each
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, as
N
tends to
+\infty
, the distribution of
W^{(N)}
converges to
\sigma
in the space
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathfrak{g})
endowed with the topology of
\alpha
-Hölder convergence on compact intervals.

Given a fixed
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, the proofs require only a finite moment of order
2mb_{\max}
for the weight filtration, where
m
is any integer
m>1/(1-2\alpha)
, and
b_{\max}\leq 2s-1
is the length of the weight filtration
\mathfrak{g}^{(b)}
defined by
\mathfrak{g}^{(b_{\max})}\supsetneq\mathfrak{g}^{{(b_{\max}+1)}}=\{0\}
.

Such invariance principles have been studied previously in the special case of centered walks. See in particular
[
CCLP99
]
, which treats the
\alpha=0
case, and
[
BFH09
]
, where
Theorem

1.7
is proven for centered walks on the free nilpotent Lie group. We remark in passing that in the centered case the limiting diffusion is not time-dependent and even admits an explicit representation in terms iterated integrals of Brownian motion, see
[
Kun80
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
Ben89
, Théorème 17]
. We shall also briefly discuss convergence in the sense of rough paths.

Remark
. In a sequel to this paper
[
BB23
]
, we pursue our study further and establish the local limit theorem for biased walks on arbitrary simply connected nilpotent Lie groups, thus extending the recent work of Diaconis and Hough
[
DH21
,
Hou19
]
to the biased case.

1.2
Organization of the paper

In
Section

2
, we set up the notations to be used in the rest of the paper. In
Section

3
, we study the class of diffusion processes on simply connected nilpotent Lie groups that arise as limits of rescaled right random walks with i.i.d. increments.
Section

4
is devoted to various techniques of truncation, graded replacement and Lindeberg replacement, that help in estimating the Fourier transform of the random walk. In
Section

5
, we prove the central limit theorem, the Berry-Esseen estimate, and the analogue of Donsker’s invariance principle for nilpotent random walks. We also characterize pairs of asymptotically close measures.

2
Set-up and notation

In this section we set up the terminology and notations used throughout the paper.

2.1
Weight filtration on
\mathfrak{g}

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra and
\overline{X}\in\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, we define recursively the following nested sequence of ideals:
\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{g}
and for
i\geq 1

\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}]+[\overline{X},\mathfrak{g}^{(i-1)}].

We note that it is well-defined (independently of the choice of representative of
\overline{X}
in
\mathfrak{g}
), and satisfies for
i,j\geq 1
,

[\mathfrak{g}^{(i)},\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i+j)}.

(15)

We call
(\mathfrak{g}^{(i)})_{i\geq 1}
the
weight filtration
of
\mathfrak{g}
associated to
\overline{X}
. When
\overline{X}=0\mod[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, this is the familiar descending central series, which we denote by
(\mathfrak{g}^{[i]})_{i\geq 1}
. In general we have
\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
, and
\mathfrak{g}^{(2i)}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{[i+1]}
. In particular, if
\mathfrak{g}
has nilpotency class at most
s
, then
\mathfrak{g}^{[s+1]}=\mathfrak{g}^{(2s)}=0
.
The weight filtration
(\mathfrak{g}^{(i)})_{i\geq 1}
on
\mathfrak{g}
allows to define a
graded Lie algebra
:

gr_{\overline{X}}(\mathfrak{g}):=\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}/\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}

(16)

whose Lie bracket is inherited from that of
\mathfrak{g}
using (
15
).

Example 3
.

In the case of the Heisenberg group

\mathfrak{g}=\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\,|\,[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3}\rangle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\overline{X}=e_{2}\mod[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

we have

\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}=\{0\}
,
\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}=\mathbb{R}e_{3}
,
(\mathfrak{g}^{(i)})_{i\geq 4}=\{0\}
, and
gr_{\overline{X}}(\mathfrak{g})
is abelian.

2.2
The bias extension
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}

We define a new Lie algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
by adding to
\mathfrak{g}
an independent copy
\chi
of the bias
\overline{X}\in\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. This extension is a convenient technical device to dissociate the contributions of the drift incarnated by
\overline{X}
, which ought to have weight
2
, from those of the fluctuations of the random walk in the direction of
\overline{X}
, which ought to have weight
1
. Many intermediate results will therefore be formulated in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, before being translated back to
\mathfrak{g}
.

If
\overline{X}=0
, we just set
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}
,
\chi=0
. Otherwise, we define
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
as the Lie algebra direct sum:

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{R}

and given a lift
X
of
\overline{X}
to
\mathfrak{g}
, we set
\chi:=(X,1)
. In this way,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}+\mathbb{R}\chi
where
\chi
is linearly independent from
\mathfrak{g}
and satisfies
[\chi,x]=[X,x]
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. We call
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
the
bias extension
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We also extend the weight filtration from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
by setting
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
if
i\neq 2
, and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(2)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}+\mathbb{R}\chi
. It is straightforward to check that this is indeed a filtration, i.e.
[\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i)},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(j)}]\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i+j)}
, and is independent of the choice of lift. The filtration
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(i)})_{i\geq 1}
determines as in (
16
) a graded Lie algebra
gr_{\overline{X}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
, which we call the
graded bias extension
.

Observe that
gr_{\overline{X}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})=gr_{\overline{X}}(\mathfrak{g})
if
\overline{X}=0
. If
\overline{X}\neq 0
,
gr_{\overline{X}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
is isomorphic to the Lie algebra
semi-direct
sum

gr_{\overline{X}}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathbb{R},

where the
\mathbb{R}
factor acts on the ideal
gr_{\overline{X}}(\mathfrak{g})
via the nilpotent endomorphisms
a_{X}(t)
defined on
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}/\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}
by the formula

a_{X}(t)(y)=[tX,y]\mod\mathfrak{g}^{(i+3)}.

Different choices of representative of
X
in
\mathfrak{g}
lead to isomorphic extensions of
gr_{\overline{X}}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Remark
. The limit measure
\nu_{t}
appearing in the CLT (
Theorem

1.1
) will arise as the projection of the value at
t
of a one-parameter semigroup of probability measures
\lambda_{t}
defined on
gr_{\overline{X}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
and left-invariant for that Lie product. See
Section

5
.

2.3
Grading

We choose for all
i\leq 2s-1
a vector subspace
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}.

It follows that
\mathfrak{g}
is the (vector space) direct sum

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{(2s-1)}

and we may identify
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
with
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. From now on, the notation
X
will indicate the unique element of
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
lifting
X_{\mu}
. We call
(\mathfrak{m}^{(i)})_{i\leq 2s-1}
a
weight decomposition
of
\mathfrak{g}
. It naturally extends to a weight decomposition of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
:

\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(1)}\oplus\dots\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(2s-1)}

by setting
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
if
i\neq 2
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(2)}=\mathfrak{m}^{(2)}\oplus\mathbb{R}\chi
.
These splittings allow to define new compatible Lie brackets on
\mathfrak{g}
,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
by setting

[x,y]^{\prime}=\pi^{(i+j)}([x,y])

(17)

if
x\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)},y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(j)}
and
\pi^{(k)}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(k)}
denotes the linear projection modulo the other
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(k^{\prime})}
,
k^{\prime}\neq k
. Notice that the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
is an ideal of
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},[.,.]^{\prime})
. The Lie product associated to
[.,.]^{\prime}
will be denoted by
*^{\prime}
. With this new Lie bracket, the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
) becomes a Lie algebra naturally isomorphic to the graded Lie algebra
gr_{\overline{X}}(\mathfrak{g})
(resp.
gr_{\overline{X}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
) and the
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}
) form a
grading
.

We fix a choice of weight grading
(\mathfrak{m}^{(i)})_{i\leq 2s-1}
and write
X\in\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
the lift of
\overline{X}
.

Remark
. Comparing
15
and
17
, we see that
X
lies in the center of
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},*^{\prime})
.

Example 4
.

In the case of the biased Heisenberg group mentioned in
2.1
, we can choose
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}e_{2}
,
\mathfrak{m}^{(2)}=\{0\}
,
\mathfrak{m}^{(3)}=\mathbb{R}e_{3}
. The bracket
[.,.]^{\prime}
is such that
[e_{i},e_{j}]^{\prime}=0
and
[\chi,e_{j}]^{\prime}=[e_{2},e_{j}]
for
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
.

2.4
Dilations

The weight decompositions on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
induce a one-parameter subgroup of dilations
D_{r}
, which are the linear maps that act on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)}
by multiplication by
r^{i}
. Note that with the
*^{\prime}
product,
D_{r}
becomes an automorphism of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
(and
\mathfrak{g}
):
\forall x,y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
,

D_{r}(x*^{\prime}y)=D_{r}x*^{\prime}D_{r}y.

The determinant of the dilations
D_{r}
on
\mathfrak{g}
is
\det D_{r}=r^{d_{\overline{X}}}
, where

d_{\overline{X}}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\dim\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}.

(18)

We call
d_{\overline{X}}
the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}
. When
\overline{X}=0
,
d_{\overline{X}}
coincides with the homogeneous dimension of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely
d_{0}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\dim\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}
, which controls the volume growth of balls in
G
by the Bass-Guivarc’h formula
[
Gui73
,
Bre14
]
. Note that
d_{\overline{X}}\geq d_{0}
in general.

2.5
Bi-grading on the free Lie algebra

For basics on the free Lie algebra we refer the reader to
[
Ser06
, chapter 2]
and
[
Reu93
]
.
We denote by
L^{[r]}
the
r
-bracket
, which is defined recursively as
L^{[1]}(x)=x
and

L^{[r]}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=[L_{r-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1}),x_{r}],

an element of the free Lie algebra on
r
letters.

Throughout we will consider formal expressions involving Lie brackets of
N
indeterminates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
corresponding to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
-valued random variables. In particular the product of

can be expanded via the Campbell-Hausdorff formula (see

]
) into an element of the

\Pi(\underline{x})=\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{\underset{\,1\leq n_{1}<\dots<n_{t}<\infty}{r_{1}+\dots+r_{t}=r},\,r_{i}\neq 0}L_{\underline{r}}(x_{n_{1}}^{\otimes r_{1}},\dots,x_{n_{t}}^{\otimes r_{t}})

where
\underline{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{t})
and

is a linear combination with rational coefficients of terms, each of which is an

of some permutation of the

Example 5
.

The Campbell-Hausdorff formula allows to compute
L_{1}(x_{1})=x_{1}
,
L_{1,1}=\frac{1}{2}L^{[2]}
,
L_{2,1}=L_{1,2}=\frac{1}{12}L^{[3]}

L_{1,1,1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\frac{1}{6}L^{[3]}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})+\frac{1}{6}L^{[3]}(x_{3},x_{2},x_{1}).

Note that the free Lie algebra comes with a natural grading given by bracket length. To account for the weight structure discussed above, we will need to consider a further grading on the
s
-step free Lie algebra. To each indeterminate

we associate

for

and expand the product

as an element of the
s
-step free Lie algebra
\mathcal{F}_{s}
on the set of
(2s-1)N
indeterminates
\{x_{j}^{(i)}\}_{1\leq j\leq N,1\leq i\leq 2s-1}

i
to each

and defining the weight of an

\mathcal{F}_{s}=\bigoplus_{1\leq a\leq s,\,1\leq b\leq 2s-1}\mathcal{F}_{s}^{[a,b)}

(19)

We also set

Coming back to our Lie algebra

\Pi^{[a,b)}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(b)}\cap\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{[a]}.

(20)

In fact, when the evaluation is taken in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
with the

2.7
Polynomial functions and degree

With the above basis, we obtain a well-defined notion of degree of a polynomial function by assigning degree

2.8
The driving measure

Let

In particular, for every polynomial

Two measures

We will estimate the

-average) of an integrable test function

using Fourier analysis. Given

\widehat{\mu}(\xi)=\int_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{\xi}(x)d\mu(x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathfrak{g}}e_{-\xi}(x)f(x)dx.

\displaystyle\mu(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\widehat{\mu}(\xi)d\xi.

2.9

We will consider a measure

2.10

3
Limiting diffusion on a nilpotent Lie group

In this section, we study the diffusion processes on a nilpotent Lie group that arise as limits of rescaled i.i.d. right random walks. We start by recalling background material on the general theory of diffusion processes on Lie groups. Then we focus on the case of limiting diffusions on nilpotent Lie groups, and establish hypoellipticity as well as a general Gaussian upper bound. We continue with a description of the support of the time-
1
distribution, and give several examples. Finally, we give a general lower bound, and several corollaries, notably characterizing the case where the limiting process is Gaussian in the ordinary Euclidean sense.

3.1
Diffusions on a connected Lie group

We start by recalling some standard facts about diffusion processes on a connected real Lie group.
We refer the reader to the books
[
Lia04
]
and
[
HS01
]
as well as Hunt’s original article
[
Hun56
]
and the papers by Stroock and Varadhan
[
SV72
,
SV73
]
for more background material on diffusion processes. The support of invariant diffusions was also studied by Siebert, see
[
Sie82
, Theorem 2]
. Connections with sub-Riemannian geometry are discussed in
[
VSCC92
,
Kup97
]
and references therein.

Let
H
be a connected real Lie group and denote by
1_{H}
the identity element of
H
.
A
continuous left-invariant diffusion process
on
H
is a stochastic process
(W(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
satisfying the following:

•

W(0)=1_{H}
with probability
1
.

•

The random map
W:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow H,t\mapsto W(t)
is continuous with probability
1
.

•

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0=t_{0}<\dots<t_{k}<+\infty
, the increments
\left(W(t_{i})^{-1}W(t_{i+1})\right)_{i=0,\dots,k-1}
are mutually independent.

In addition, if for any
0\leq s<t
, the distribution of the increment
W(s)^{-1}W(t)
only depends on
t-s
, then the process is called
homogeneous
.

Denote by
\Omega(H)
the set of continuous paths
c:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow H
, set
\mathcal{F}_{t}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the projections
s\mapsto c(s)
where
s\leq t
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\cup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\mathcal{F}_{t}
. The law of a diffusion process
W
on
H
is a certain distribution
\sigma
on
(\Omega(H),\mathcal{F}_{\infty})
. We denote by
\sigma_{s}
the image of
\sigma
by
c\mapsto c(s)
, or in other terms the law of
W(s)
. We denote by
\mathcal{Q}
the collection of probability measures on
(\Omega(H),\mathcal{F}_{\infty})
which arise from a diffusion, and call them diffusion laws on
H
.

A left-invariant second order differential operator on
H
defines a diffusion law on
H
as follows. Denote by
\mathfrak{h}
the Lie algebra of
H
, identify any vector
X\in\mathfrak{h}
with its associated left-invariant vector field on
H
and with the corresponding Lie derivative. In particular, for any
f\in C^{1}(H)
,

X.f:x\mapsto\lim_{\varepsilon\underset{\neq}{\to}0}\frac{f(xe^{\varepsilon X})-f(x)}{\varepsilon}.

Consider a time-dependent left-invariant second order differential operator
\mathscr{L}=(\mathscr{L}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
of the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}{E_{i}(t)}^{2}+B(t)

where
E_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
for
i=1,\ldots,q
and
B:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
are smooth maps.

Fact 3.1

(Infinitesimal generator
[
Lia04
]
)
.

There exists a unique diffusion law
\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}
such that for every
s\geq 0
and every
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable variable
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(H)
, the process

\displaystyle f(c(t))-f(c(s))-\int_{s}^{t}(\mathscr{L}_{r}f)(c(r))dr

(
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
)

is a martingale on
(\Omega(H),(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq s},\sigma)
.

Note that
\sigma
characterizes
\mathscr{L}
because for any fixed
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(H)
,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left(f\left[c(s)^{-1}c(s+\varepsilon)\right]-f(1_{H})\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{s}\right)\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{L}_{s}f(1_{H})

where the arrow stands for the convergence in probability.

A diffusion process on
H
with infinitesimal generator
\mathscr{L}
is by definition a stochastic process
W
on
H
with law
\sigma
. Observe then that for any
s>0
, the process
(W(s)^{-1}W(s+t))_{t\geq 0}
is a diffusion with infinitesimal generator
(\mathscr{L}_{s+t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
. It follows that
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
is homogeneous if and only if
\mathscr{L}
is independent of the time parameter, and in this case the distributions
\sigma_{t}
form a semigroup:
\forall s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

\sigma_{s}*\sigma_{t}=\sigma_{s+t}.

We now describe the support of the time-
t
distribution
\sigma_{t}
associated to
\mathscr{L}
. Given an open interval
I
, a
C^{1}
path
\gamma:I\rightarrow H
, and
s\in I
, we define the multiplicative derivative
\partial\gamma(s)\in\mathfrak{h}
as the derivative at
t=0
(in the classical sense) of the path
t\mapsto\gamma(s)^{-1}\gamma(s+t)
. We say that a continuous, piecewise
C^{1}
path
\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow H
is
\mathscr{L}
-
horizontal
if its multiplicative derivative satisfies

\partial\gamma(s)\in\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mathbb{R}E_{i}(s)+B(s)

for every
s
in the complement of a finite subset of
[0,t]
.

Fact 3.2

(Support
[
SV72
,
SV73
]
)
.

The support of
\sigma_{t}
is the closure of the set of arrival points
\gamma(t)
, where
\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow H
is a continuous piecewise
C^{1}

\mathscr{L}
-horizontal path.

This fact is a consequence of Stroock-Varadhan’s support theorem
[
SV72
]
for diffusion processes on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, applied to
W
locally in charts of
H
as in
[
SV73
, page 284]
, where it is shown that invariant diffusions on Lie groups never explode in finite time.

Next we give a smoothness criterion for the distribution
\sigma_{t}
. Denote by
\mathcal{H}
the Lie algebra of vector fields on the product manifold
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times H
generated by
(E_{i})_{i\leq q}
and
\partial_{t}+B
, where
\partial_{t}
stands for the constant vector field
(1,0)
. We see

as functions

, where

is the tangent bundle of

.
Recall Hörmander’s criterion for hypoellipticity:

Fact 3.3

(Hypoellipticity
[
Hör67
]
)
.

Assume the Hörmander condition: for every point
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times H
, every vector
v\in TH_{x}
tangent to

, there exists
Y\in\mathcal{H}
such that
Y(t,x)=v
. Then the differential equation on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times H
given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}E_{i}^{2}+B=0

(21)

is hypoelliptic, i.e. every solution (in the sense of distributions) is smooth.

A direct computation shows that the distribution
u=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\delta_{t}\otimes\sigma_{t}\,dt
is solution of (
21
). Assuming (
21
) is hypoelliptic, one may then write for
t>0
,

\sigma_{t}=u(t,x)dx

where

is a smooth function, and

stands for a left-invariant Haar measure on

.

Hypoellipticity also yields the following domination principle
[
VSCC92
, Corollary III.1.3]
: for any open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times H
, any compact subset
K\subseteq\Omega
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that for every solution

of (

\displaystyle\|v\|_{C^{p}(K)}\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|v|\,dtdx

(22)

where

is the supremum on

of the absolute value of the derivatives of

up to order

. Later, we will use this inequality to convert a moment estimate on

into a bound on the derivatives of the density

.

We also record a Harnack inequality, namely a statement to the effect that non-negative solutions of (
21
) do not decrease too much while going along a small horizontal path. We rely here on the recent work of Kogoj and Polidoro
[
KP16
]
addressing the setting of a time-dependent hypoelliptic equation. Let
1_{H}\in U\subseteq H
be an open neighborhood of the identity, whose closure is compact and included in a chart of

. Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon_{1}
be two parameters and define
\mathscr{P}
as the set of couples

such that there exists a

\gamma:[\varepsilon_{0},\tau]\rightarrow U
such that
\gamma(\varepsilon_{0})=1_{H}
,
\gamma(\tau)=x
.

Fact 3.4

(Harnack inequality
[
KP16
]
)
.

Assume the Hörmander condition from
3.3
. Let
U,\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\mathscr{P}
as above. Let
L
be a compact set in the interior of the closure of
\mathscr{P}
in

.
Then there exists a constant
C>1
such that
for any non negative solution
v:(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},2\varepsilon_{1})\times U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
of (

), one has

v(\varepsilon_{0},1_{H})\leq C\inf_{(t,x)\in L}v(t,x).

This fact is a consequence
of
[
KP16
, Corollary 2]
, applied in the bounded chart

to the differential operator
\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}(E^{\prime}_{i})^{2}+B^{\prime}
where
E^{\prime}_{i}(t)=E_{i}(-t),B^{\prime}(t)=-B(-t)
. The time reversal we use here allows to convert the supremum on the left-hand side in the Harnack inequality from
[
KP16
]
into an infimum on the right-hand side, as displayed above.

Finally, we put aside any assumption of hypoellipticity on
\sigma
and record a moment estimate due to Kisynski
[
Kis79
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
Jør75
, Proposition 3.1]
) dealing with homogeneous diffusions. Denote by

the distance induced by a left-invariant Riemmanian metric on

.

Fact 3.5

(Quadratic exponential moment
[
Kis79
]
)
.

Assume

is independent of the time parameter (but not necessarily hypoelliptic). Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for

, all

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{H}\exp\left(\varepsilon d_{H}(x,y)^{2}\right)\,d\sigma_{t}(y)<+\infty.

3.2
Invariance and regularity of the limiting diffusion

In this subsection we restrict our attention to a certain family of diffusion processes on nilpotent Lie groups. They are those that arise as limiting processes for right random walks with i.i.d. increments. Using
Section

3.1
, we prove that every such diffusion is smooth, and give a Gaussian upper bound on its density and each of its derivatives (
Proposition

3.8
).
In the biased case, the diffusion process we have to deal with can be seen in two ways, either as a time-dependent left-invariant diffusion process on
\mathfrak{g}
with absolutely continuous increments (with generator (

) below), or as the restriction to the affine hyperplane
\mathfrak{g}+t\chi
of a bona fide left-invariant time-homogeneous diffusion process on the bias extension

(

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra,
\overline{X}\in\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
a bias, and
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{b}\mathfrak{m}^{(b)}
an adapted weight decomposition for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}
. We saw in
Section

2
that these data define a bias extension
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, a variable
\chi
for which
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}+\mathbb{R}\chi
, and graded Lie bracket or Lie product
[.,.]^{\prime}
,
*^{\prime}
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
.

For the rest of the section, we fix vectors
E_{1},\dots,E_{q},B,\mathcal{Y}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
such that the linear span of
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{q}\}
is
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
,
B\in\mathfrak{m}^{(2)}
, and
\mathcal{Y}\in\chi+\mathfrak{m}^{(2)}
. Our goal is to study the diffusion law
\sigma
on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
whose infinitesimal generator is given by
\mathscr{L}=(\mathscr{L}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
where

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\left(\Ad(t\mathcal{Y})E_{i}\right)^{2}+\Ad(t\mathcal{Y})B.

(23)

Here the notation
\Ad
refers to the adjoint representation for

, namely for every

in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}

\Ad(Y)X=Y*^{\prime}X*^{\prime}(-Y)

to refer to a diffusion process with law

If the random walk on
(\mathfrak{g},*)
is driven by a measure
\mu

3.3
Support of the limiting diffusion

We keep the notations of

3.2
and describe the support of the distribution

. After a general and explicit description, we turn to two remarkable examples: for the upper triangular nilpotent Lie algebra, the support of

Let us begin with a general description of the support

of

is smooth. We use the

Recall that if

is an open interval and

When

denotes the tangent map of the left

A theorem of Strichartz
[

]
ensures that the derivation

admits an inverse, generalizing the classical Riemann integral in the abelian case, expressed in terms of

(
[

]
) as follows. Given a bounded and measurable path

, set

\displaystyle\int^{*^{\prime}}_{[a,b]}c\,

\displaystyle:=\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\frac{(-1)^{e(\tau)}}{r^{2}\binom{n-1}{e(\tau)}}\int_{\{a<t_{1}<\dots<t_{r}<b\}}L^{[r]^{\prime}}(c(t_{\tau(1)}),\dots,c(t_{\tau(r)}))\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}

(30)

\displaystyle=\int_{[a,b]}c(t)dt\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{a<t_{1}<t_{2}<b\}}[c(t_{1}),c(t_{2})]^{\prime}dt_{1}dt_{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,+\frac{1}{6}\int_{\{a<t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}<b\}}[[c(t_{1}),c(t_{2})]^{\prime},c(t_{3})]^{\prime}\,+\,[[c(t_{3}),c(t_{2})]^{\prime},c(t_{1})]^{\prime}dt_{1}dt_{2}dt_{3}\,+\,\dots

where

refers to

(see

) and

represents the number of consecutive subscripts whose order is reversed under the permutation

To reduce to this situation, we introduce some other weight decomposition

such that

and set

is the isomorphism that factorizes via the identity on the vector space

. Call
\mathring{*}^{\prime}
the induced graded product on
\mathfrak{g}
, set
\mathring{E}_{i}=\phi(E_{i})
,
\mathring{B}=\phi(B)
,
\mathring{\mathcal{Y}}=\phi(\mathcal{Y})
, and
\mathring{\sigma}
the induced diffusion law on
(\mathfrak{g},\mathring{*}^{\prime})
as in (
23
). We know from

In particular,
\text{supp}\,\sigma_{1}=\phi^{-1}(\text{supp}\,\mathring{\sigma}_{1})

\mathring{\sigma}
.

Observe now that the condition

guarantees that

extends to a morphism from

to

by setting

is the diffusion law generated by

By

does not contain

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
0<t_{0}<t_{1}
. The set of arrival points
\gamma(t_{1})
, for
\mathscr{L}
-horizontal paths
\gamma:[t_{0},t_{1}]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
starting at
\gamma(t_{0})=0
and whose derivative satisfies
\|\partial\gamma\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, contains an open set of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Recall from the remark following
3.11
that a path
\gamma
is
\mathscr{L}
-horizontal if and only if

is
\mathscr{L}_{+}
-horizontal, where
\mathscr{L}_{+}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\Ad(t(B+\mathcal{Y}))E_{i}^{2}
. We may thus assume that

. Denote by
\widetilde{X}
the vector field on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{g}
given by
(\frac{d}{dt},\Ad(t\mathcal{Y})X)

that the family

is bracket-generating on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, the associated flows project into horizontal paths. The result now follows from Chow’s theorem
[
Bel96
]
.

Denote by

the support of
\sigma_{1}
, and
\mathring{\mathscr{S}}
the interior of

and starting at

Lemma 3.25

(Accessibility)

Let

be a compact subset. For

large enough, for any

\mathscr{L}
-horizontal, such that

and

,

The idea is to use the characterization of

as the closure of arrival points of horizontal paths on

starting at

(

) to generate a path from

very close to

on the time interval

to
x
, or any

using

Proof.

For

, set

and

Lemma 3.27

(Harnack Inequality on a space-time cone)

Let

(33)

We now deduce a general lower bound for the density

the smallest integer

-regular chain

from

to

Theorem 3.28

(General lower bound)

Let

3.28

.

The density
\sigma_{1}
is positive on the interior of its support:

\forall x\in\mathring{\mathscr{S}},\,\,\,\,u(1,x)>0.

Choose the compact set

such that

. There is a fixed small neighborhood

of

for any
\varepsilon>0
small enough. By

is a

-horizontal paths. More precisely, given

follows from the combination of

Lemma 3.31
.

Let
K
be a large enough compact subset of
\mathfrak{g}
. For
a<b\in\mathbb{R}
,

E_{K}(a,x,b,y)\leq E_{hor}(a,x,b,y)+1.

Proof.

It is sufficient to check that for some
K
compact, every horizontal path
\gamma
on an interval
[a,b]
whose energy is bounded by
1
is
K
-regular, in the sense that

\gamma(b)\in\gamma(a)*^{\prime}\Ad(a\mathcal{Y})D_{\sqrt{b-a}}K.

Case
B=0
.
We may replace
\gamma
by
\Ad(-a\chi)(\gamma(a)^{-1}*^{\prime}\gamma)
which is horizontal on

and has same energy as
\gamma
. This allows to assume

. Then we need check that
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}}\gamma(b)
is bounded uniformly in
\gamma
. We set

. Moreover, using that

, we get

\displaystyle\partial c(t)

\displaystyle=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}}b\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\gamma(bt)^{-1}*^{\prime}\gamma(bt+b\varepsilon)}{b\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}}b\Ad(bt\mathcal{Y})v_{\gamma}(bt)

\displaystyle=\Ad(t\mathcal{Y})\sqrt{b}\,v_{\gamma}(bt).

Hence, the path
c
is horizontal on

is bounded, independently of

General case

is

To conclude, we characterize the case when the limit distribution
\sigma_{1}
is an ordinary Euclidean Gaussian distribution. It is pleasant that the criterion below is expressed only in terms of the weight filtration of
\mathfrak{g}
, and ultimately only in terms of the adjoint action of the mean
\overline{X}
on successive quotients of the descending central series. The forward direction (
4.\implies 6.
below) is due to Crépel and Raugi
[

, §4]

1.4
from the introduction.

Theorem 3.33

(Characterization of the Gaussian case)
.

Let
\sigma
be the diffusion law associated to
\mathscr{L}
as in
eq.

23
. The following are equivalent.

1.

The Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[.,.]^{\prime})
is abelian.

2.

For all
a\in\{1,\dots,s\}

\mathfrak{g}^{[a]}=\mathfrak{g}^{(2a-1)}.

3.

For all
i\in\{1,\dots,2s-1\}
,

\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\neq\{0\}\iff\text{$i$ is odd}.

4.

For all
a\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, we have

\mathfrak{g}^{[a]}=L^{[a]}(X^{\otimes(a-1)}\otimes\mathfrak{g}).

5.

For all
a\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, we have

[X,\mathfrak{g}^{[a]}/\mathfrak{g}^{[a+1]}]=\mathfrak{g}^{[a+1]}/\mathfrak{g}^{[a+2]}.

6.

The distribution
\sigma_{1}
is Gaussian (in the classical Euclidean sense) on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

1.\implies 2.
By definition of
[.,.]^{\prime}
, the assumption
[.,.]^{\prime}=0
means that for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2s-1
,

[\mathfrak{g}^{(i)},\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i+j+1)}.

The result follows by induction on
a
. Indeed, the case
a=1
is tautological. For the induction step, we observe that

\mathfrak{g}^{[a+1]}=[\mathfrak{g}^{[a]},\mathfrak{g}]=[\mathfrak{g}^{(2a-1)},\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(2a+1)}

and the reverse inclusion is clear from the definition of
\mathfrak{g}^{(2a+1)}
.

2.\implies 3.
An element in
\mathfrak{g}^{(2a)}
must be in
\mathfrak{g}^{[a+1]}
, i.e. in
\mathfrak{g}^{(2a+1)}
by assumption. Hence
\mathfrak{g}^{(2a)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(2a+1)}
. The claim on the
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
’s now follows from their definition.

3.\implies 1.
Since
[\mathfrak{m}^{(i)},\mathfrak{m}^{(j)}]^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{(i+j)}
, by assumption the left hand side is zero unless both
i
and
j
are odd, in which case the right hand side is zero as
i+j
is even.

2.\iff 4.
Only
2.\implies 4.
needs a proof. We argue by downwards induction on
a
. The result is clear for a
a=s
. For the induction step, it is enough to notice that

\mathfrak{g}^{[a]}=\mathfrak{g}^{(2a-1)}\subseteq L^{[a]}(X^{\otimes(a-1)}\otimes\mathfrak{g})+\mathfrak{g}^{[a+1]}

with
\mathfrak{g}^{[a+1]}=L^{[a+1]}(X^{\otimes a}\otimes\mathfrak{g})\subseteq L^{[a]}(X^{\otimes(a-1)}\otimes\mathfrak{g})
by the induction hypothesis.

4.\iff 5.
Similar as
2.\iff 4.

1.\implies 6.
One may replace
\mathcal{Y}
by
\chi
in the definition of the generator
\mathscr{L}
which because if
(\mathfrak{g},[.,.]^{\prime})
is abelian, then
\Ad(t\mathcal{Y})_{|\mathfrak{g}}=\Ad(t\chi)_{|\mathfrak{g}}
. One may also assume that
q=q_{1}
, i.e. the vectors
E_{i}
in the second order term for
\mathscr{L}
define a basis of
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
. Now,
\nu=\sigma_{1}
is the limit measure in the central limit theorem for
\mu
. In this specific context where
4
. holds, the result is due to Crepel-Raugi
[
CR78
, Section 4]
, and their theorem also states that
\nu=\sigma_{1}
is Gaussian with expectation
B
.

6.\implies 1.
If
(\mathfrak{g},[.,.]^{\prime})
is not abelian, then there exists a horizontal path
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that the ratio
\|\gamma(1)\|/\ell(\gamma)
is arbitrary large. It follows that the ratio
d_{hor}(1/2,0,1,x)/\|x\|
converges to
0
along a sequence of
x
going to infinity. The lower bound from
Corollary

3.30
then contradicts the assumption that
\sigma_{1}
is Gaussian (it does not decrease fast enough).

Example 6

(Full upper-triangular group)
.

If
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{1\leq i<j\leq q_{1}+1}\mathbb{R}E_{i,j}
is the Lie algebra of the group of upper triangular matrices (see
Section

3.3.1
), then the distribution
\sigma_{1}
is Gaussian if and only if
\overline{X}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq q_{1}}c_{i}E_{i,i+1}\mod\mathfrak{g}^{[2]}
with at most one the
c_{i}
’s equal to zero. Indeed, if
c_{i}
and
c_{i+k-1}
both vanish, then the application
[\overline{X},.]
from
\mathfrak{g}^{[k-1]}/\mathfrak{g}^{[k]}
to
\mathfrak{g}^{[k]}/\mathfrak{g}^{[k+1]}
is not surjective: one checks easily that
E_{i,i+k}
is not in the image. This prevents
\sigma_{1}
to be Gaussian by
Theorem

3.33
. The converse implication is straightforward.

Example 7

(3-step filiform case)
.

If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}A\oplus\mathbb{R}T\oplus\mathbb{R}[A,T]\oplus\mathbb{R}[A,[A,T]]
is the step-
3
filiform Lie algebra (see
Section

3.3.2
), then the distribution
\sigma_{1}
is Gaussian if and only if
\overline{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{*}A\oplus\mathbb{R}T\mod\mathfrak{g}^{[2]}
.

4
The three reductions

To prove the limit theorems announced earlier, we need to perform a series of reductions. This section is devoted to them.
We shall show that the random product can be altered slightly (allowing a small error) in three different ways. First, we shall truncate the random variables in order to deal with the moment assumptions. Then (graded replacement) we shall replace the Lie product by the graded Lie product (for which dilations are automorphisms) and third, we shall show that we can replace the driving measure by any measure with the same moments of order at most 2 (Lindeberg replacement). While performing those reductions, we will need to allow moderate deviations of the random product. This flexibility will be used (in a weak form) to take into account the recentering in the proof of the central limit theorem in
Section

5
. The full extent of moderate deviations is to be used in our follow-up paper
[
BB23
]
where we establish the local central limit theorem.

The notations
(\mathfrak{g},*,\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},\overline{X},(\mathfrak{m}^{(b)})_{b\leq 2s-1},X,\chi,*^{\prime})
as well as
\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})
and
\Pi^{[a,b)}
are those of
Section

2
. We assume
\mu
is a probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
with finite second moment for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}
, and such that
\mu_{ab}
has expectation
\overline{X}
. In particular
X=\mathbb{E}(\mu^{(1)})
. We will mostly work with
\widetilde{\mu}=\mu-X+\chi
the associated probability measure on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
.

4.1
Truncation and moment estimates

We first establish moment estimates. To fix ideas, we start with the case where
\mu
has a finite moment of every order.
Consider a monomial function
M:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of degree
b\geq 1
, see
Section

2.7
. We define its statistics
M^{\infty}
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
as the formal series

M^{\infty}(\underline{x})=\sum_{1\leq n_{1}<\dots<n_{t}<\infty}M(x_{n_{1}},\dots,x_{n_{t}}).

For every
N\geq 1
, we identify
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}
as the subset of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
made of sequences that vanish after the
N
first terms. In particular,
M^{\infty}
is a well-defined function on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and assume
\mu
has a finite moment of order
2mb
. Then for
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}}\left[M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]\ll_{m}N^{bm},

and in particular for
\delta_{0}>0
,
g,h\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})
,
\underline{y}=(g,\underline{x},h)
,
a,b\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}}\left[\|\Pi^{[a,b)}(\underline{y})\|^{2m}\right]\ll_{m}N^{(b+2\delta_{0})m}.

Proof.

Given
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\dim\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}
, denote by
x\mapsto x^{\alpha}
the corresponding monomial on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
(say for some ordering of the basis
e^{(i)}_{j}
fixed in
Section

2
) and write
d(\alpha)
its degree for the weight filtration. Expanding the power
2m
, we can write for
N\geq 1
,
\underline{x}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}
,

\displaystyle M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2mb}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\ell}}\sum_{\underline{n}\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{\ell}}C_{\underline{\alpha}}x_{n_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots x_{n_{\ell}}^{\alpha_{\ell}}

where
\sup_{\underline{\alpha}}|C_{\underline{\alpha}}|=O_{m}(1)
and

\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\ell}=\left\{\underline{\alpha}\in(\mathbb{N}^{\dim\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}})^{\ell}\,:\,\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d(\alpha_{i})=2mb\,\text{ and }\,\inf_{i}d(\alpha_{i})\geq 1\right\}.

Then by linearity and independence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}}\left[M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2mb}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\ell}}\sum_{\underline{n}\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{\ell}}C_{\underline{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(x_{n_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}})\dots\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(x_{n_{\ell}}^{\alpha_{\ell}}).

The terms for which some
x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
has degree
1
satisfy
\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}})=0
, hence the
\underline{\alpha}
that contribute belong to

\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\ell}=\left\{\underline{\alpha}\in(\mathbb{N}^{\dim\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}})^{\ell}\,:\,\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d(\alpha_{i})=2mb\,\text{ and }\,\inf_{i}d(\alpha_{i})\geq 2\right\}

thus forcing
\ell
to belong to
\{1,\dots,mb\}
.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}}\left[M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{mb}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\ell}}\sum_{\underline{n}\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{\ell}}C_{\underline{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(x_{n_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}})\dots\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(x_{n_{\ell}}^{\alpha_{\ell}})

and the bound for
\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}}\left[M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]
follows because the parameters
\ell,\underline{\alpha}
vary in a bounded set independent of
N
, the parameter
\underline{n}
varies in a set of cardinality
N^{\ell}\leq N^{mb}
, and the product of expectations is
O_{m}(1)
by the moment assumption on
\mu
.

To prove the second assertion, we note that
\Pi^{[a,b)}(g,\underline{x},h)
is a finite formal linear combination (depending on
a,b
but not
N
) of statistics in
\underline{x}
associated to monomials of degree at most
b
, and such that each statistics of degree
b^{\prime}\leq b
has its coefficients bounded above by

O(1)\sum_{i_{1}+\dots+j_{\ell}=b-b^{\prime}}\|g^{(i_{1})}\|\dots\|g^{(i_{k})}\|\,\|h^{(j_{1})}\|\dots\|h^{(j_{\ell})}\|

if
b^{\prime}<b
, and
O(1)
otherwise.
Given the condition on
g,h\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})
, this bound is in particular
O(N^{\frac{b-b}{2}^{\prime}+\delta_{0}})
. The result follows by Hölder Inequality and the previous bound established for
\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}}\left[M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]
.
∎

In order to obtain similar moment estimates but with weaker moment assumptions on
\mu
, we introduce the
truncation

T_{N}\widetilde{\mu}
as the image of
\widetilde{\mu}
by

T_{N}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},x=\oplus x^{(b)}\mapsto\oplus T_{N}x^{(b)}
where

T_{N}x^{(b)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{(b)}&\mbox{if }\|x^{(b)}\|\leq N^{b/2}\\
0&\mbox{if }b\geq 2\text{ and }\|x^{(b)}\|>N^{b/2}\\
c_{N}&\mbox{if }b=1\text{ and }\|x^{(1)}\|>N^{1/2}\end{array}\right.

and
c_{N}=-\widetilde{\mu}({\|x^{(1)}\|>N^{1/2}})^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(x^{(1)}\mathbbm{1}_{{\|x^{(1)}\|\leq N^{1/2}}})
is chosen so that
T_{N}\widetilde{\mu}
is centered in the
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
-coordinate.

It is good to notice first that one may replace
\widetilde{\mu}
by
T_{N}\widetilde{\mu}
at a very low cost.

Lemma 4.2

(Cost of truncation)
.

Let
q\in[2,+\infty)
. If
\mu
has finite
q
-th moment then for all
N\geq 1
,

\|\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}-(T_{N}\widetilde{\mu})^{\otimes N}\|\leq\epsilon_{N}N^{-\frac{q}{2}+1}

where
\|.\|
is the norm in total variation, and the sequence
\epsilon_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}_{>0}
goes to zero while being bounded by
\epsilon_{N}\leq C(1+m_{q}(\mu))
where
C>0
is a constant that may only depend on
q
.

Proof.

Note that

\widetilde{\mu}\{T_{N}x\neq x\}\leq\widetilde{\mu}\{\max_{b}\|x^{(b)}\|^{1/b}>N^{1/2}\}\leq\epsilon_{N}N^{-\frac{q}{2}}

with
\epsilon_{N}
as in the statement.
Hence
T_{N}^{\otimes N}
is the identity on a subset of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}
of
\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}
-measure at least
(1-\epsilon_{N}N^{-\frac{q}{2}+1})
, up to multiplying
\epsilon_{N}
by a bounded constant.
∎

We now show that
(T_{N}\widetilde{\mu})^{\otimes N}
satisfies the moment estimates from
4.1
.

Proposition 4.3

(Moment estimate)
.

Assume that
\mu
has a finite second moment. For
N\geq 1
,
(N_{i})_{i\leq N}\in[0,N]^{N}
,
m\geq 1
an integer,

\mathbb{E}_{\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left[{M^{\infty}}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]\ll_{m}N^{bm},

and in particular for
\delta_{0}\geq 0
,
g,h\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})
,
\underline{y}=(g,\underline{x},h)
,
a,b\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left[\|\Pi^{[a,b)}(\underline{y})\|^{2m}\right]\ll_{m}N^{(b+2\delta_{0})m}.

Moreover, the implicit constant in
\ll_{m}
can be taken of the form
C(1+m_{2}(\mu)^{mb})
where
C=C(\mathfrak{g},[.,.],\mathbb{R}\overline{X},(\mathfrak{m}^{(b)})_{b},(e^{(i)}_{j})_{i,j},m)>0
is a constant and
m_{2}(\mu)
is the second moment of
\mu
.

It is quite remarkable that
\mu
is only assumed to have finite second moment.

Proof.

Arguing as for
4.1
(with
\otimes_{i}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}
in place of
\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left[M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{mb}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\ell}}\sum_{\underline{n}\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{\ell}}C_{\underline{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}_{T_{N_{n_{1}}}\widetilde{\mu}}(x_{n_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}})\dots\mathbb{E}_{T_{N_{n_{\ell}}}\widetilde{\mu}}(x_{n_{\ell}}^{\alpha_{\ell}})

but it is no longer true that the product of expectation is
O_{m}(1)
. Nonetheless, note that for
d(\alpha)\geq 2
,
N^{\prime}\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{T_{N^{\prime}}\widetilde{\mu}}(|x^{\alpha}|)=\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}\left(|(T_{N^{\prime}}x)^{\frac{2\alpha}{d(\alpha)}}|^{\frac{d(\alpha)}{2}-1}|(T_{N^{\prime}}x)^{\frac{2\alpha}{d(\alpha)}}|\right)=O(N^{\prime\frac{d(\alpha)}{2}-1})

because
|T_{N^{\prime}}x^{\frac{2\alpha}{d(\alpha)}}|=O(N^{\prime})
and
\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(|(T_{N^{\prime}}x)^{\frac{2\alpha}{d(\alpha)}}|)
is bounded independently of
N^{\prime}
(by a constant of the form
C(1+m_{2}(\mu))
with
C
as in the statement).

This bound implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left[M^{\infty}(\underline{x})^{2m}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{mb}\sum_{\underline{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\ell}}\sum_{\underline{n}\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{\ell}}O_{m}(N^{mb-\ell})

\displaystyle=O_{m}(N^{mb})

as the parameters
\ell,\underline{\alpha}
vary in a bounded set independent of
N
, while the parameter
\underline{n}
varies in a set of cardinality
N^{\ell}
.

The estimate for
\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left[\|\Pi^{[a,b)}(\underline{y})\|^{2m}\right]\ll_{m}N^{(b+2\delta_{0})m}
follows by the same argument as for
4.1
.

The comment on the constant in
\ll_{m}
is just an inspection of the proof.
∎

4.2
Graded replacement

In the remainder of
Section

4
we keep the assumption that
\mu
has a finite second moment for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}
. We now show how to replace the product
*
by the its graded counterpart
*^{\prime}
, for which the dilations
(D_{r})_{r>0}
act as automorphisms.

Proposition 4.4

(Graded replacement)
.

For
N\geq 1
,
(N_{i})_{i\leq N}\in[0,N]^{N}
,
g,h\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})
,
\underline{y}=(g,\underline{x},h)
,
m\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left(\|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\Pi(\underline{y})-D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{y})\|^{2m}\right)\ll_{m}N^{(-1+2\delta_{0})m}.

Moreover, the implicit constant in
\ll_{m}
can be taken of the form
C(1+m_{2}(\mu)^{(2s-1)m})
where
C=C(\mathfrak{g},[.,.],\mathbb{R}\overline{X},(\mathfrak{m}^{(b)})_{b},(e^{(i)}_{j})_{i,j},m)>0
and
m_{2}(\mu)
is the second moment of
\mu
.

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality, it is sufficient to show that the bound for

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left(\|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\Pi^{(b)}(\underline{y})-D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\Pi^{\prime(b)}(\underline{y})\|^{2m}\right).

But
\Pi^{(b)}(\underline{y})-\Pi^{\prime(b)}(\underline{y})
is the projection of
\Pi^{(b)}(\underline{y})
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{(b+1)}
parallel to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(b)}
. Hence,

\|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\Pi^{(b)}(\underline{y})-\Pi^{\prime(b)}(\underline{y}))\|\leq N^{-(b+1)/2}\,\|\Pi^{(b)}(\underline{y})\|

and the result follows because we proved in
Proposition

4.3
that

\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}\left[\|\Pi^{(b)}(\underline{y})\|^{2m}\right]\ll_{m}N^{(b+2\delta_{0})m}

where the implicit constant in
\ll_{m}
has the desired form.
∎

Remark
. The same holds for
\widetilde{\mu}
in place of
T_{N}\widetilde{\mu}
under the assumption of a finite moment of order
2mb_{\max}
, where
b_{\max}\leq 2s-1
is the length of the weight filtration. Just use
4.1
in place of
Proposition

4.3
.

We infer a graded approximation at the level of Fourier transforms. It will be used to prove the central limit theorem with Berry-Esseen estimate (and the local limit theorem in
[
BB23
]
).

Corollary 4.5
.

For
\xi
a linear form on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
,
N\geq 1
,
(N_{i})_{i\leq N}\in[0,N]^{N}
,
g,h\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})
,
\underline{y}=(g,\underline{x},h)
,

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}[e_{\xi}(\Pi(\underline{y}))-e_{\xi}(\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{y}))]\big|\leq C(1+m_{2}(\mu)^{s})\,\|D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi\|\,N^{-1/2+\delta_{0}}

where
C=C(\mathfrak{g},[.,.],\mathbb{R}\overline{X},(\mathfrak{m}^{(b)})_{b},(e^{(i)}_{j})_{i,j})>0
.

Proof.

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}[e_{\xi}(\Pi(\underline{y}))-e_{\xi}(\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{y}))]|

\displaystyle\ll\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}(|\xi\Pi(\underline{y})-\xi\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{y})|)

\displaystyle\leq\|D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi\|\,\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}(\|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\Pi(\underline{y})-\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{y}))\|)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+m_{2}(\mu)^{s})\|D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi\|\,N^{-1/2+\delta_{0}}

where the last line is given by the inequality
\|.\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|.\|_{L^{2}}
followed by

Proposition

4.4
(case
m=1
).
∎

We also deduce a result of graded approximation that is uniform on the time interval
\{0,\dots,N\}
. It will be useful to prove our theorem à la Donsker in
Section

5.3
. Here we do not use truncation.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
. For
N\geq 1
, set

F_{N}=\left\{\underline{x}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}:\sup_{k\leq N}\|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\Pi\left((x_{i})_{i=1}^{k},-k\chi\right)-D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\Pi^{\prime}((x_{i})_{i=1}^{k},-k\chi)\|>N^{-\alpha}\right\}.

If
\mu
has finite moment of order
2mb_{\max}
where
b_{\max}
is the length of the weight filtration, then

\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}(F_{N})=O_{\alpha,m}(N^{(-1+2\alpha+\frac{1}{m})m}).

In particular
\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}(F_{N})
goes to
0
provided
m>1/(1-2\alpha)
.

Proof.

The result follows by replacing the supremum by a sum, then using the Markov inequality and (the remark after the proof of)
Proposition

4.4
with
\delta_{0}=0
.
∎

4.3
Lindeberg replacement

We show how to replace the driving measure by any other measure which coincides up to order
2
for the weight filtration. The substitution takes place at the level of Fourier transforms.

Proposition 4.7

(Lindeberg replacement)
.

Let
\eta
be a probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
with finite second moment for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}
and which coincides with
\mu
up to order
2
. For
\xi
a linear form on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
,
N\geq 1
,
(N_{i})_{i\leq N}\in[0,N]^{N}
,
g,h\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})
,
\underline{y}=(g,\underline{x},h)
,

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}}[e_{\xi}(\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{y}))]-\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta}}[e_{\xi}(\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{y}))]\big|\leq\,(\|D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi\|+\|D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi\|^{3})N^{3s\delta_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}N^{-1}_{i}\,\varepsilon_{N}

where
\varepsilon_{N}=o_{\eta}(1)
. More generally, assuming
\mu
,
\eta
both have finite
m
-th moment for some
m\in[2,3]
, we may take
\varepsilon_{N}=o_{\eta}(N^{-\frac{m}{2}+1})
if
m<3
and
\varepsilon_{N}=O_{\eta}(N^{-1/2})
if
m=3
.

Moreover, in the
m=3
case, the implicit constant in
O_{\eta}
can be taken of the form
C(1+m_{2}(\mu)+m_{2}(\eta))^{6s^{2}}(1+m_{3}(\mu)+m_{3}(\eta))
where
C=C(\mathfrak{g},[.,.],\mathbb{R}\overline{X},(\mathfrak{m}^{(b)})_{b},(e^{(i)}_{j})_{i,j})>0
and
m_{q}(\mu),m_{q}(\eta)
denote the
q
-th moments of
\mu
and
\eta
.

In this paper, we will apply this proposition with
(N_{i})
constant equal to
N
. In
[
BB23
]
, we will need to consider gradually increasing
(N_{i})
. In both cases,
\sum_{i=1}^{N}N^{-1}_{i}=O(1)
, so the right-hand side is
O_{\eta}(1)(\|D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi\|+\|D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi\|^{3})N^{3s\delta_{0}}\,\varepsilon_{N}
.

Proof.

We replace
\widetilde{\mu}
-variables by
\widetilde{\eta}
-variables successively. At the step
i
, we set

\kappa_{i}=g\otimes T_{N_{1}}\widetilde{\mu}\otimes\dots\otimes T_{N_{i-1}}\widetilde{\mu}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\theta_{i}=T_{N_{i+1}}\widetilde{\eta}\otimes\dots\otimes T_{N_{N}}\widetilde{\eta}\otimes h

and we need show that

\Delta_{i}:=|\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{i}\otimes(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}-T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta})\otimes\theta_{i}}(e_{\xi}(\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{u},v,\underline{w})))|\leq(\|{D_{\sqrt{N}}}\xi\|+\|{D_{\sqrt{N}}}\xi\|^{3})\,N_{i}^{-1+3s\delta_{0}}\varepsilon_{N}.

Setting
\xi_{N}=D_{\sqrt{N}}\xi
,
\underline{u}_{N}=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\underline{u}
,
v_{N}=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}v
,
\underline{w}_{N}=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\underline{w}
, we can use that dilations commute with
*^{\prime}
to rewrite

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}=|\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{i}\otimes(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}-T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta})\otimes\theta_{i}}(e_{\xi_{N}}(\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{u}_{N},v_{N},\underline{w}_{N})))|.

We now isolate the part of
\Pi^{\prime}(\underline{u}_{N},v_{N},\underline{w}_{N})
depending on
v
. To do so, we observe that for
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
,

g_{1}*^{\prime}g_{2}*^{\prime}g_{3}=g_{1}*^{\prime}g_{3}+P_{g_{1},g_{3}}(g_{2})

where
P_{g_{1},g_{3}}
is a polynomial of bounded degree without constant term and whose coefficients depend polynomially on
g_{1},g_{3}
. Setting
x_{N}=\Pi^{\prime}\underline{u}_{N}
,
z_{N}=\Pi^{\prime}\underline{w}_{N}
, we then rewrite

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{i}\otimes(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}-T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta})\otimes\theta_{i}}\big(e_{\xi_{N}}(x_{N}*^{\prime}z_{N}+P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N}))\big)|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{i}\otimes\theta_{i}}\big[e_{\xi_{N}}(x_{N}*^{\prime}z_{N})\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}-T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta})}(e_{\xi_{N}}(P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N})))\big]|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{i}\otimes\theta_{i}}|\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}-T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta})}(e_{\xi_{N}}(P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N})))|.

Using Taylor expansion up to order
3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu})}(e_{\xi_{N}}(P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N})))=1+\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu})}(-2i\pi\xi_{N}P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N}))

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu})}(-4\pi^{2}(\xi_{N}P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N}))^{2})

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu})}(O(\xi_{N}P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N}))^{3})

and the analogous formula for
T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta}
.

•

•

•

If

O_{\eta}(N^{-3/2}_{i})&\mbox{if $m\in[3,+\infty)$}\end{array}\right.

Combining the above Taylor expansions and the monomial estimates, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}-T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\eta})}(P_{x_{N},z_{N}}(v_{N})))|\leq(\|\xi_{N}\|+\|\xi_{N}\|^{3})(1+\|x_{N}\|+\|z_{N}\|)^{3s}N^{-1}_{i}\varepsilon_{N}

Lemma 4.8

We conclude this section with a discussion of several papers that preceded us in attempting to prove the central limit theorem for non-centered random walks on a general nilpotent Lie group. The case of the Heisenberg group was handled successfully by Tutubalin
[
Tut64
]
but a substantial difficulty remained to formulate the correct renormalization for an arbitrary group.

In
[
Vir74
]
, Virtser proposes a renormalization for walks on the full upper-triangular unipotent matrix group, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{R}E_{i,j}
with
[E_{i,j},E_{k,\ell}]=\mathbbm{1}_{j=k}E_{i,\ell}-\mathbbm{1}_{i=\ell}E_{k,j}
. While Virtser’s normalization is appropriate (and coincides with ours) for certain values of
\overline{X}
, as in
[
Vir74
, Theorem 2 & 3]
, for certain other values his renormalization is too strong and may lead to non absolutely continuous limiting measures. This occurs for instance if
n=4
, and
\overline{X}=sE_{1,2}+tE_{3,4}\mod[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
with
s,t\neq 0
. Indeed, in this case direct computation shows that our subspace of weight at least
3
is
\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}=\mathbb{R}E_{1,4}+\mathbb{R}(E_{1,3}-t/sE_{2,4})\subsetneq\mathfrak{g}^{[2]}
. However, Virtser renormalizes the whole subspace
\mathfrak{g}^{[2]}
with coefficient
N^{-3/2}
, hence shrinking to a point the coordinate
\mathfrak{g}^{[2]}/\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}
which lives at scale
N
.

In
[
Rau78
]
, Raugi proposes a renormalization for walks on a general nilpotent Lie group. We explain below why his renormalization is too weak to prevent escape of mass in non-centered cases, despite the CLT formulated in his paper.

Raugi’s renormalization is defined with respect to the sequence of ideals
\{\mathfrak{I}^{k,\ell},0\leq\ell<k\leq s\}
, where
\mathfrak{I}^{k,\ell}
is the linear span of
\mathfrak{g}^{[k+1]}
and brackets containing
k
elements of
\mathfrak{g}
, at least
\ell
of which are in
\mathbb{R}\overline{X}
. One also authorizes the case
(k,\ell)=(1,1)
and set
\mathfrak{I}^{(1,1)}=\mathfrak{g}^{[2]}+\mathbb{R}\overline{X}
. The ideals
\mathfrak{I}^{k,\ell}
are then ordered by lexicographical order, i.e.

\mathfrak{I}^{1,0}\supseteq\mathfrak{I}^{1,1}\supseteq\mathfrak{I}^{2,0}\supseteq\mathfrak{I}^{2,1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathfrak{I}^{k,0}\supseteq\mathfrak{I}^{k,1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathfrak{I}^{k,k-1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathfrak{I}^{s+1,0}=\{0\}

and for each each
(k,\ell)
above, of successor
(k,\ell)_{+}
, one may choose a supplementary subspace
\mathfrak{r}^{k,\ell}
such that

\mathfrak{I}^{k,\ell}=\mathfrak{r}^{k,\ell}\oplus\mathfrak{I}^{(k,\ell)_{+}}

in particular
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus\mathfrak{r}^{k,\ell}
. Raugi’s system of dilation is the family
R_{t}
of linear maps defined on
\mathfrak{r}^{k,\ell}
by
R_{t}x=t^{k+\ell}x
except on
\mathfrak{r}^{1,1}
where
R_{t}x=tx
. The
main problem
with Raugi’s definition is that the weight of the dilation may be strictly subadditive when taking brackets:
[\mathfrak{r}^{k_{1},\ell_{1}},\mathfrak{r}^{k_{2},\ell_{2}}]
is not necessarily included in
\oplus\{\mathfrak{r}^{k,\ell}\,:\,k+\ell\geq k_{1}+\ell_{1}+k_{2}+\ell_{2}\}
. This leaves room for escape of mass, contradicting
[
Rau78
]
. We now formulate a precise counterexample where this phenomenon occurs.

Counterexample to
[
Rau78
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{\ell}
be the step-3 filiform algebra defined by
\mathfrak{\ell}=\oplus_{i=1}^{4}\mathbb{R}e_{i}
where
[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{3}
,
[e_{1},e_{3}]=e_{4}
,
[e_{2},e_{3}]=0
,
e_{4}\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{\ell})
. In particular,

\mathfrak{\ell}=\mathbb{R}e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{R}e_{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}[e_{1},e_{2}]\oplus\mathbb{R}[e_{1},[e_{1},e_{2}]].

Let
\mathfrak{\ell}^{\prime}
be a nilpotent Lie algebra of step
4
, and
(e^{\prime}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq d^{\prime}}
a basis adapted to its central descending series, in particular
e_{d^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{\ell}^{\prime[4]}
. Endow the vector space
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{\ell}\oplus\mathfrak{\ell}^{\prime}
with the bracket
[.,.]
defined by
[\mathfrak{\ell},\mathfrak{\ell}^{\prime}]=0
, extending the bracket on
\mathfrak{\ell}^{\prime}
, and whose restriction to
\mathfrak{\ell}
corresponds to the original bracket on
\mathfrak{\ell}
modified by imposing
[e_{2},e_{3}]=e^{\prime}_{d}
. Direct computation shows that
(\mathfrak{g},[.,.])
is indeed a Lie algebra (of step
4
). Now let
\mu
be a compactly supported non-lattice probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
with expectation
\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})=e_{2}\mod[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
in the abelianization. Notice
\mathfrak{I}^{3,2}=\mathfrak{I}^{4,0}=\mathfrak{\ell}^{\prime[4]}
and
\mathfrak{I}^{4,1}=\{0\}
. Given an arbitrary choice of
(\mathfrak{r}^{k,\ell})_{k,\ell}
, Raugi renormalises the projection of
\mu^{*n}*-NX
to
\mathfrak{r}^{4,0}=\mathfrak{\ell}^{\prime[4]}
by
N^{-4/2}
, but
Theorem

5.2
(and even Crepel-Raugi
[
CR78
]
in this particular case) predicts that this projection is spread at scale
N^{5/2}
, whence the escape of mass.

5.2
Asymptotically close measures

We deduce from our central limit theorem a necessary and sufficient condition for two measures on
\mathfrak{g}
to be asymptotically close, namely
Corollary

1.5
. We keep the notations from the beginning of
Section

5
and start with a variant of the CLT whose recentering is not multiplicative, but additive.

Lemma 5.5

(CLT with additive recentering)
.

As
N
goes to
+\infty
, we have

D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}-NX)\longrightarrow(\nu*^{\prime}\chi)-\chi.

We emphasize that
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}-NX)
differs from the measure
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}*-NX)
that we studied until now. Indeed,
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}-NX)
refers to the image of
\mu^{*N}
by the map
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},x\mapsto D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(x-NX)
(see conventions in
Section

2.9
), so here the recentering refers to the addition on
\mathfrak{g}
seen as a vector space, and not the Lie product
*
.
In a similar way, the measure in the limit is the image of
\nu
by the map
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},x\mapsto(x*^{\prime}\chi)-\chi
which induces a diffeomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, it is also a smooth probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

We observe that

\mu^{*N}-NX=\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}-N\chi
, so we just need to check that
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}\rightarrow\nu*^{\prime}\chi
.
By the graded replacement lemma (
Corollary

4.5
), we may replace
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}
by
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\widetilde{\mu}^{*^{\prime}N}
, so it boils down to showing the convergence
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\widetilde{\mu}^{*^{\prime}N}*^{\prime}-N\chi)\rightarrow\nu
. Applying
Corollary

4.5
again, we are left to check that

D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}*-N\chi)\rightarrow\nu
. As
\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}*-N\chi=\mu^{*N}*-NX
, this follows from the central limit theorem
5.2
.

∎

Proof of
Corollary

1.5
.

If two measures are asymptotically close, then so are their projections to the abelianization. Combining the classical CLT and the fact that the class of convex subsets is invariant by dilations and translations, one deduces that those projections must have same mean and covariance matrix. In particular, the weight filtrations for both measures coincide and we may thus choose a common family of dilations and recentering for both measures. Using again the invariance properties of
\mathcal{C}
, the limiting distributions in
5.5
must coincide, hence the corresponding measures
\nu
as well, or more generally the underlying diffusion laws
\sigma
. Examining infinitesimal generators, we conclude that the commutator means, i.e. the
B
term in
\mathscr{L}
, also coincide.

The converse statement is a direct consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{C}
is closed under dilations and translations,
5.5
, and the fact that weak convergence of measures toward an absolutely continuous distribution implies uniform convergence on the class
\mathcal{C}

[
RR62
, Theorem 4.2]
.
∎

5.3
Convergence of processes

In this subsection, we prove
Theorem

1.7
, namely the analog of Donsker’s invariance principle
[
RY99
, Theorem XIII.1.9]
for the
\mu
-walk on
(\mathfrak{g},*)
. For
N\geq 1
,
t\geq 0
, we recall the interpolated random process
W^{(N)}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
defined by

W^{(N)}(t)=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\big(X_{1}*\dots*X_{\lfloor tN\rfloor}*(tN-\lfloor tN\rfloor)X_{\lfloor tN\rfloor+1}*-tNX\big)

where the
X_{i}
’s are i.i.d. with law
\mu
, and
X=\mathbb{E}(\mu^{(1)})
is the lift to
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
of
\overline{X}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
.
We also recall that we have associated to
\mu
a
*^{\prime}
-left-invariant diffusion process on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
, namely the time-dependent diffusion
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
with infinitesimal generator
\mathscr{L}_{t}(\mu,(\mathfrak{m}^{(b)})_{b})
and law
\sigma
defined in (
34
).

Our goal is to show that
W^{(N)}
converges in law to
W
in the topology of
\alpha
-Hölder convergence on bounded intervals.
We begin by rewriting the process as:

\displaystyle W^{(N)}(t)=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\big(x_{1}*\dots*x_{\lfloor tN\rfloor}*(tN-\lfloor tN\rfloor)x_{\lfloor tN\rfloor+1}*-tN\chi\big)

(40)

where
x_{i}=X_{i}-X+\chi
is now a family of i.i.d. variables with law
\widetilde{\mu}
. Then we set

S^{(N)}(t)=D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}\big(x_{1}*^{\prime}\dots*^{\prime}x_{\lfloor tN\rfloor}*^{\prime}(tN-\lfloor tN\rfloor)x_{\lfloor tN\rfloor+1}\big).

From
Corollary

4.6
and the remark following it,
Theorem

1.7
will reduce to the following. We recall that
Z(t)=W(t)*^{\prime}t\chi
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Assume that
\mu
has finite
m
-th moment for any
m>0
. Given any
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, the process
S^{(N)}
converges in law towards the process
Z
in
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
endowed with the topology of
\alpha
-Hölder convergence on bounded intervals.

We shall use a classical approach, via Kolmogorov’s tightness criterion
[
RY99
, Chapter VIII]
. This method reduces the proof of
Proposition

5.6
to the combination of the next two lemmas.

Lemma 5.7

(Convergence of finite-dimensional distributions)
.

For every
k\geq 1
,
0\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{k}<\infty
, one has the convergence in law, as
N\to+\infty
:

(S^{(N)}(t_{i}))_{i\leq k}\longrightarrow(Z(t_{i}))_{i\leq k}.

Proof.

For
N\geq 1
, denote by
t^{(N)}_{i}=\inf\{k/N:k/N\geq t_{i}\}
. Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}(\|S^{(N)}(t_{i})-S^{(N)}(t^{(N)}_{i})\|>\varepsilon)=o(1)\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathbb{P}(\|Z(t_{i})-Z(t^{(N)}_{i})\|>\varepsilon)=o(1)

so it is sufficient to prove that for any
F:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
continuous and bounded,

(S^{(N)}(t^{(N)}_{i}))_{i\leq k}(F)-(Z(t^{(N)}_{i}))_{i\leq k}(F)=o_{F}(1).

But, setting
s^{(N)}_{i}=t^{(N)}_{i}-t^{(N)}_{i-1}
(where
t^{(N)}_{0}=0
), one may rewrite

(S^{(N)}(t^{(N)}_{i}))_{i\leq k}=\Psi(\mathcal{S}^{(N)}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{S}^{(N)}_{k})\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(Z(t^{(N)}_{i}))_{i\leq k}=\Psi(\mathcal{Z}^{(N)}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{Z}^{(N)}_{k})

where
\Psi:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{k}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{k},(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\mapsto(x_{1}*^{\prime}\dots*^{\prime}x_{i})_{i\leq k}
is a continuous map,
the variables
\mathcal{S}^{(N)}_{1},\mathcal{Z}^{(N)}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{S}^{(N)}_{k},\mathcal{Z}^{(N)}_{k}
are mutually independent, with each
\mathcal{S}^{(N)}_{i}
(resp.
\mathcal{Z}^{(N)}_{i}
) distributed as
S^{(N)}(s^{(N)}_{i})
(resp.
Z(s^{(N)}_{i})
). To conclude, it is now sufficient to check that
\mathcal{S}^{(N)}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{(N)}_{i}
for each
i
, but this follows from the central limit theorem
5.2
.
∎

Lemma 5.8

(Tightness)
.

Let
d^{\prime}
be a left-invariant Riemannian metric on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
.
For every segment
[0,R]
, every integer
m\geq 1
there is
C_{m}>0
such that for all
N\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq R}\mathbb{E}\left(d^{\prime}(S^{(N)}_{s},S^{(N)}_{t})^{2m}\right)\leq C_{m}|s-t|^{m}.

(41)

Proof.

Suppose
s=\frac{i}{N}
,
t=\frac{i+j}{N}
for integers
i,j\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle d^{\prime}(S^{(N)}_{s},S^{(N)}_{t})

\displaystyle=d^{\prime}(0,-S^{(N)}_{s}*^{\prime}S^{(N)}_{t})=d^{\prime}(0,D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(x_{i+1}*^{\prime}\dots*^{\prime}x_{i+j}))

\displaystyle\ll_{d^{\prime}}\|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(x_{i+1}*^{\prime}\dots*^{\prime}x_{i+j})\|.

For any
m\geq 1
,
Proposition

4.3
and the remark following it tells us that

\mathbb{E}\left(\|D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(x_{i+1}*^{\prime}\dots*^{\prime}x_{i+j})\|^{2m}\right)\ll_{m}\left(\frac{j}{N}\right)^{m}=|t-s|^{m},

as desired. In general with
i:=\lfloor Ns\rfloor
,
i+j=\lfloor Nt\rfloor
, we may write

d^{\prime}(S^{(N)}_{s},S^{(N)}_{t})^{2m}\ll_{m}d^{\prime}(S^{(N)}_{s},S^{(N)}_{i/N})^{2m}+d^{\prime}(S^{(N)}_{i/N},S^{(N)}_{(i+j)/N})^{2m}+d^{\prime}(S^{(N)}_{(i+j)/N},S^{(N)}_{t})^{2m}.

Taking expectations, this becomes
O_{d^{\prime},m}(1)|t-s|^{m}
, as we may see after distinguishing the cases
|t-s|<1/N
and
|t-s|\geq 1/N
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

5.6
.

According to Kolmogorov’s tightness criterion and the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
RY99
]
(using the Riemannian metric
d^{\prime}
in place of the Euclidean metric),
5.8
implies that the sequence of processes
S^{(N)}
is relatively compact in the topology of
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
for any
\alpha<\frac{m-1}{2m}
. Together with
5.7
this ensures the desired convergence in law to
\lambda
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.7
.

Let
W^{\prime(N)}
be the process obtained from the formula (
40
) for
W^{(N)}
by replacing the product
*
by the product
*^{\prime}
. From
Corollary

4.6
we see that the sequence of processes
W^{(N)}
and
W^{\prime(N)}
have the same limit in the
\alpha
-Hölder topology for each
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
.

On the other hand,
W^{\prime(N)}(t)=S^{(N)}(t)*^{\prime}-t\chi
, and the operator on
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R},\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
defined by
c(t)\mapsto c(t)*^{\prime}t\chi
is continuous. Hence it follows from
Proposition

5.6
that
W^{\prime(N)}
converges in law to the random process
(Z(t)*^{\prime}-t\chi)_{t\geq 0}
, which is just
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
by definition.

∎

Remark
. When
\alpha=0
,
Proposition

5.6
can also be derived from the main result of
[
SV73
]
.

Remark
. (Rough paths) We note in passing that, in fact, a stronger version of Proposition
5.6
holds. Namely, for a suitable choice of dilations
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}
(i.e. of subspaces
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(b)})_{b}
), the convergence also holds in the finer
rough paths
topology, see
[
LQ02
,
FV10
,
FH14
]
, namely in the Polish space of
\alpha
-Hölder paths with respect to the
*^{\prime}
-invariant Carnot-Carathéodory metric
d^{\prime}_{cc}(x,y)
defined on
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},*^{\prime})
by any Euclidean norm on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{R}\chi
. This is defined exactly as the Riemannian metric, except that we only allow paths that are tangent to the distribution of planes formed by the left translates of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{R}\chi
. If
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(b)}
is chosen so that it lies in the span
V_{b}
of all brackets of length at most
b
in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{R}\chi
(which is possible thanks to
3.9
), then for small
x
(see “ball-box theorem”,
[
Bel96
, Thm 7.3.4]
,
[
Gro96
, Thm 0.5.A, p97]
)

d^{\prime}_{cc}(0,x)\simeq\max_{b\geq 0}d_{euc\ell}(x,V_{b})^{1/(b+1)}\ll\max_{b\geq 1}\|x^{(b)}\|^{1/b},

where
V_{0}:=0
, while for large
x
,
d^{\prime}_{cc}(0,x)
is controlled by
d_{euc\ell}(0,x)
, where
d_{euc\ell}
is the Euclidean distance on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. The same proof then shows
5.8
with
d^{\prime}_{cc}
in place of
d^{\prime}
. As a consequence the sample paths of the diffusion
\lambda
are
\alpha
-Hölder for the Carnot-Carathéodory metric
d^{\prime}_{cc}
for each
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
. In the centered case on the free nilpotent Lie group, Donsker’s theorem in the rough paths topology was first obtained in
[
BFH09
]
by a similar method.

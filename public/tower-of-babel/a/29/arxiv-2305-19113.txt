Algebraicity and the p p -adic Interpolation of Special L L -values for certain Classical Groups
Yubo Jin

Abstract
. In this paper, we calculate the ramified local integrals in the doubling method and present an integral representation of standard L L -functions for classical groups. We explicitly construct local sections of Eisenstein series such that the local ramified integrals represent certain ramified L L -factors. As an application, we prove algebraicity of special L L -values and construct p p -adic L L -functions for symplectic, unitary, quaternionic unitary and quaternionic orthogonal groups.

11F67, 11F55

1.
Introduction

One of the central problems in number theory is the study of special values of
L
-functions. The main object studied in this paper is the standard
L
-function for automorphic forms on classical groups. We present an integral representation for these
L
-functions using the doubling method. Utilizing the integral representation, we prove the algebraicity of certain special
L
-values and construct
p
-adic
L
-functions interpolating these values. We introduce our results and compare with works in the literature in the following three subsections.

1.1.
Integral representations

Let
G
be a classical group over a number field
F
defined as in (
2.2
) or (
2.4
). The first main theme of this paper is an integral representation for standard
L
-functions of classical groups. One way to obtain such an integral representation is the doubling method originated in
[
25
,
45
]
. Take a cuspidal representation
\pi
of
G(\mathbb{A})
and a cusp form
\phi\in\pi
. We consider a doubling embedding (
2.7
)
G\times G\to H
into a bigger classical group
H
defined as in (
2.6
). The main strategy of the doubling method is to pullback an Eisenstein series
E(h;f_{s})
on
H(\mathbb{A})
induced from a character
\chi
along such an embedding and to pair it with the cusp form on
G(\mathbb{A})
. That is, we consider the global integral of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi,f_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G(F)\times G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})\times G(\mathbb{A})}E((g_{1},g_{2});f_{s})\overline{\phi_{1}(g_{1})}\phi_{2}(g_{2})\chi(\nu(g_{2}))^{-1}dg_{1}dg_{2},

where

is the image of

in

and
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are certain translate of

It is shown in
[

]
that this global integral has an Euler product expression (

such that
\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi,f_{s})
represents the partial
L
-function of
\phi
, i.e. all the ramified local
L
-factors are set to
1
. For the study of arithmetic problems of special
L
-values and especially the construction of
p
-adic
L
-functions, the information at ramified places is indispensable.

The definition of local
L
-factors is indeed a fundamental problem in the study of automorphic representations. In
[
36
]
, Langlands conjectured that one can associate to any cuspidal representation
\pi=\otimes\pi_{v}
a local
L
-factor
L_{v}(s,\pi_{v})
and an epsilon factor such that the global
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
satisfies a functional equation. In
[
57
]
, Yamana defined these local factors and proved the functional equation using the doubling method. His approach works for all irreducible automorphic representations of classical groups and is used in proving some analytic properties of
L
-functions. However, he did not construct the local sections of Eisenstein series and did not compute the local integrals explicitly so it is not clear how his computations can be used to study the algebraicity of special
L
-values or to construct
p
-adic
L
-functions.

We study the ramified local integrals in a different way which is inspired by
[
49
]
. Assume
\phi
is fixed by some open compact subgroup

(
3.23
) and is an eigenform for a certain Hecke algebra

. For a Hecke character

whose conductor divides

, we define the

-function

to be a Dirichlet series of the Hecke eigenvalues of

. This extends the definition of the
L
-function for symplectic groups in
[
49
]
and is an analogue of the
L
-functions for classical (elliptic) modular forms defined by Dirichlet series of Fourier coefficients. In particular, the
L
-function

has all bad Euler factors outside the conductor of

. In
[
49

, Shimura constructs local sections of Eisenstein series explicitly at all places such that

represents

. Our main theorem on integral representation, which extends his result to all classical groups, is stated as follow.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
2.3
,
6.1
) There is a choice of

such that

(1.2)

\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi,f_{s})=C\cdot L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi\times\chi\right)\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(s;\phi_{\infty},f_{s}^{\infty})\cdot\langle\phi^{\prime},\phi\rangle,

where

is some simple translate of

If

is a unitary group, we assume all places

are nonsplit in the quadratic extension defining the group

]

We do not study the archimedean integral in general in this work. For the special cases we are considering, the archimedean computations follow from
[

]
and
[

]
.

Recently, Cai, Friedberg, Ginzburg and Kaplan

presented an integral representation for

1.2.
The algebraic result

The celebrated Deligne’s conjecture
[
14
]
claims that the critical values of motivic
L
-functions, up to certain periods, are algebraic numbers. In this paper, we study the automorphic counterpart of this conjecture. As the approach here relies heavily on the theory of Shimura varieties, we restrict ourselves to the classical group
G
whose underlying symmetric space is hermitian. Such groups (except some orthogonal groups) are listed at the beginning of Section
5
. In these cases, the notion of the algebraic modular forms is well defined. We refer the reader to the beginning of Section
5.3
for a summary of various characterizations of algebraic modular forms in the literature. All of them rely on the fact that the symmetric space of
G
is hermitian so that one can associate
G
to a Shimura variety.

We fix the following setup. Assume
F
is a totally real number field. Let
\boldsymbol{l}=(l,...,l)
be a parallel weight satisfying

(1.3)

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}m+1&\text{ Case II,}\\
n+1&\text{ Case III, IV, V.}\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\text{ when }F\neq\mathbb{Q},

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}m+1&\text{ Case II,}\\
n+r+1&\text{ Case III, IV, V,}\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\text{ when }F=\mathbb{Q},

Fix a specific prime

(1)

(2)

(3)

(4)

When the group is totally isotropic (i.e.

This kind of result is also obtained in

. The analytic properties are studied there but there are no explicit formulas as for the confluent hypergeometric function in
[
47

so that we do not know the algebraic properties of these functions so far.

2.
The Integral Representation of the Standard
L
-function

We present a global integral representation of the standard
L
-function for classical groups in this section. The definition of the local
L
-factors and computation of the local integrals will be carried out in next two sections.

2.1.
The doubling embedding

We start by fixing some general notations. For an associative ring
R
with identity, denote by
\mathrm{Mat}_{m,n}(R)
the
R
-module of all
m\times n
matrices with entries in
R
. Set
\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(R)=\mathrm{Mat}_{n,n}(R)
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(R)=\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(R)^{\times}
. For

for its transpose. Denote by

and

, or even simply

and

if their sizes are clear from the context, for the identity matrix and zero matrix in

, respectively. We follow the setup for classical groups as in
[

]
.

Let

\rho

. The couple
(D,\rho)
considered in this paper will belong to the following five types:

(a)

(b)

(c)

is a division algebra central over a quadratic extension

(d)

\mathbf{D}

For

Fix a triple

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:W\times W\to D
such that

(2.1)

\langle x,y\rangle^{\rho}=\epsilon\langle y,x\rangle,\quad\langle ax,by\rangle=a\langle x,y\rangle b^{\rho},\quad(a,b\in D;x,y\in W).

We always assume such a form to be non-degenerate, i.e.
\langle x,W\rangle=0
implies
x=0
. Denote the ring of all
D
-linear endomorphisms of
W
by
\mathrm{End}_{D}(W)
and
\mathrm{GL}_{D}(W)=\mathrm{End}_{D}(W)^{\times}
. If we view elements of
W
as row vectors, then
\mathrm{GL}_{D}(W)
acts on
W
from the right. The classical group of
\mathcal{W}
is defined as

(2.2)

G:=G(\mathcal{W}):=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{D}(W):\langle xg,yg\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle\text{ for all }x,y\in W\},

which is a (possibly disconnected) reductive algebraic group over
F
. By fixing a basis of
W
, we can identify
\mathrm{End}_{D}(W)
with
\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(D)
and
\mathrm{GL}_{D}(W)
with
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(D)
. Then
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
can be expressed as a matrix of the form

(2.3)

\Phi=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1_{m}\\
0&\theta&0\\
\epsilon\cdot 1_{m}&0&0\end{array}\right]\text{ with }n=2m+r,\theta^{\ast}=\epsilon\theta\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(D)

and thus the classical group
G
can be realized as

(2.4)

G:=G(W,\Phi)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(D):g\Phi g^{\ast}=\Phi\}.

We assume
m\geq 1
throughout the paper to avoid the discussion of definite classical groups. Doubling the underlying
\epsilon
-hermitian space we consider
\mathcal{V}=(W\oplus W,\langle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\rangle)
where

(2.5)

\langle\langle(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\rangle\rangle:=\langle x_{1},y_{1}\rangle-\langle x_{2},y_{2}\rangle\text{ for }(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\in W\oplus W.

By fixing a basis of
\mathcal{V}
, the classical group
G(\mathcal{V})
is isomorphic to

(2.6)

H=\{h\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(D):gJ_{n}g^{\ast}=J_{n}\},\quad J_{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1_{n}\\
\epsilon\cdot 1_{n}&0\end{array}\right].

Note that

R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi&0\\
0&-\Phi\end{array}\right]R^{\ast}=J_{n},

with

R=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&\frac{\epsilon}{2}\cdot 1_{r}&0&0&\frac{\epsilon}{2}\cdot 1_{r}&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&-\epsilon\cdot 1_{m}\\
1_{m}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&\theta^{-1}&0&0&-\theta^{-1}&0\\
0&0&0&1_{m}&0&0\\
0&0&1_{m}&0&0&0\end{array}\right].

Then we define a doubling map

(2.7)

\displaystyle G\times G

\displaystyle\to H

\displaystyle(g_{1},g_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{1}&0\\
0&g_{2}\end{array}\right]R^{-1}.

We thus view
G\times G
as a subgroup of
H
and identify
(g_{1},g_{2})
with its image in
H
. More explicitly, if we write

(2.8)

g_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{1}&f_{1}&b_{1}\\
h_{1}&e_{1}&j_{1}\\
c_{1}&k_{1}&d_{1}\end{array}\right],\qquad g_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{2}&f_{2}&b_{2}\\
h_{2}&e_{2}&j_{2}\\
c_{2}&k_{2}&d_{2}\end{array}\right],

with
a_{1},a_{2},d_{1},d_{2}
of size
m\times m
,
e_{1},e_{2}
of size
r\times r
, then

(2.9)

(g_{1},g_{2})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{2}&-\frac{j_{2}}{2}&\frac{\epsilon h_{1}}{2}&\frac{\epsilon(e_{1}-e_{2})\theta}{4}&\frac{\epsilon h_{2}}{2}&\frac{\epsilon j_{1}}{2}\\
-k_{2}&d_{2}&0&\frac{\epsilon k_{2}\theta}{2}&-\epsilon c_{2}&0\\
\epsilon f_{1}&0&a_{1}&\frac{f_{1}\theta}{2}&0&b_{1}\\
\epsilon\theta^{-1}(e_{1}-e_{2})&\epsilon\theta^{-1}j_{2}&\theta^{-1}h_{1}&\theta^{-1}\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{2}\theta&-\theta^{-1}h_{2}&\theta^{-1}j_{1}\\
\epsilon f_{2}&-\epsilon b_{2}&0&-\frac{f_{2}\theta}{2}&a_{2}&0\\
\epsilon k_{1}&0&c_{1}&\frac{k_{1}\theta}{2}&0&d_{1}\end{array}\right].

2.2.
The global zeta integral

Let
F
be a number field with adele ring
\mathbb{A}
. We consider tuples
(D,\rho,\epsilon)
of following five cases:

(Case I, Orthogonal)

(D,\rho)
of type (a) with
\epsilon=1
,

(Case II, Symplectic)

(D,\rho)
of type (a) with
\epsilon=-1
,

(Case III, Quaternionic Orthogonal)

(D,\rho)
of type (b) with
\epsilon=1
,

(Case IV, Quaternionic Unitary)

(D,\rho)
of type (b) with
\epsilon=-1
,

(Case V, Unitary)

(D,\rho)
of type (c) with
D=E
and
\epsilon=-1
.

Remark 2.1
.

In this paper, we label our groups as Case I-V for simplicity but we may also call the name of the groups (i.e. orthogonal, symplectic, …) so that one can easily compare the groups here with the one in other papers. The notion of orthogonal, symplectic and unitary groups are well known and appear frequently in the literature while the groups of Case III, IV do not have a standard name. Here we call them quaternionic orthogonal or unitary depending on whether the form defining the group is hermitian (like the orthogonal group) or skew-hermitian (like the unitary). But indeed, both groups have been called ‘quaternionic unitary’ in the literature so the reader should be careful about their meaning. For example, the groups studied in
[
21
]
and
[
51
]
are the quaternionic orthogonal group here.

Denote
\mathbb{A}_{D}=D\otimes_{F}\mathbb{A}
for the adelization and
D_{v}=D\otimes_{F}F_{v}
for the localization at a place
v
of
F
. Fix an
\epsilon
-hermitian matrix
\Phi\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(D)
of the form in (
2.3
) with
\theta
an anisotropic matrix (so the Witt index of
\Phi
is
m
and
n=2m+r
) and define group
G,H
as in (
2.4
),(
2.6
) with a doubling embedding
G\times G\to H
given by (
2.7
). For global groups, we will write
G(\mathbb{A}),H(\mathbb{A}),G(F_{v}),H(F_{v})
for its adelization and localization but simply write
G=G(F),H=H(F)
for the rational points if its meaning is clear from the context.

Remark 2.2
.

In this paper, we shall use the term ‘totally isotropic group’ to indicate the group whose associated
\epsilon
-hermitian form is totally isotropic (i.e.
r=0
in (
2.3
)) instead of using the term split group or quasi-split group. In the sequel, when discussing the local groups
G(F_{v})
, we will distinguish the ‘split’ and ‘nonsplit’ case according to whether
v
is split in
D
or not.

Let
P\subset H
be the Siegel parabolic subgroup whose Levi component is
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(D)
. More explicitly,
P=M\ltimes N
with

(2.10)

M=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&\hat{a}\end{array}\right]:a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(D)\right\},\quad N=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&b\\
0&1\end{array}\right]:b\in S_{n}(F)\right\}.

Here
S_{n}(F)
is an additive algebraic group with

(2.11)

S_{n}(F)=\left\{b\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(D):\epsilon b+b^{\ast}=0\right\}.

Let
\chi:E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a Hecke character and extend it to a character on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{D})
(still denoted by
\chi
) by taking the composite with the reduced norm
\nu:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{D})\to\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
. Consider the induced representation

(2.12)

\mathrm{Ind}_{P(\mathbb{A})}^{H(\mathbb{A})}(\chi|\nu(\cdot)|^{s})

consisting functions
f_{s}:H(\mathbb{A})\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(2.13)

f_{s}(pg)=\chi(\nu(a))|\mathrm{N}_{E/F}(\nu(a))|^{s+\kappa}f_{s}(g),\text{ for }p=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&\hat{a}\end{array}\right]\in P(\mathbb{A}),

where

(2.14)

\kappa=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{n-1}{2}&\text{ Case I, }\\
\frac{n+1}{2}&\text{ Case II, }\\
\frac{2n+1}{2}&\text{ Case III, }\\
\frac{2n-1}{2}&\text{ Case IV, }\\
\frac{n}{2}&\text{ Case V.}\end{array}\right.

We then form the Eisenstein series

(2.15)

E(h;f_{s})=\sum_{\gamma\in P(F)\backslash H(F)}f_{s}(\gamma h),\qquad h\in H(\mathbb{A})

on
H(\mathbb{A})
associated to a standard section
f_{s}
.

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
G(\mathbb{A})
with trivial central character and
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\pi
be two cusp forms. The global integral we consider is

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G(F)\times G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})\times G(\mathbb{A})}E((g_{1},g_{2});f_{s})\overline{\phi_{1}(g_{1})}\phi_{2}(g_{2})\chi(\nu(g_{2}))^{-1}dg_{1}dg_{2}.

Unfolding the Eisenstein series, we get

(2.17)

\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s})=\int_{G(\mathbb{A})}f_{s}(\delta(g,1))\langle\pi(g)\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle dg,

where

(2.18)

\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle=\int_{G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})}\phi_{1}(g)\overline{\phi_{2}(g)}dg,

is the standard inner product on
G(\mathbb{A})
and

(2.19)

\delta=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1_{r}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&1_{m}&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&1_{m}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&1_{r}&0&0\\
0&0&-1_{m}&0&1_{m}&0\\
0&\epsilon\cdot 1_{m}&0&0&0&1_{m}\end{array}\right].

Here
\delta
is chosen such that
\delta(g,g)\delta^{-1}\in P
. Also note that we must take
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\pi
in a same representation space otherwise the integral will be identically zero.

Write
\pi=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\pi_{v}
and assume
\phi_{1}=\otimes_{v}\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2}=\otimes_{v}\phi_{2,v}
with
\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2,v}\in\pi_{v}
. Also choose the section
f_{s}
such that
f_{s}=\prod_{v}f_{s,v}
is factorizable with local sections
f_{s,v}\in\mathrm{Ind}_{P(F_{v})}^{H(F_{v})}(\chi|\nu(\cdot)|^{s})
. Due to the uniqueness of the pairing,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is factorizable in the sense that
\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle=\prod_{v}\langle\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2,v}\rangle
, where

(2.20)

\langle\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2,v}\rangle=\int_{G(F_{v})}\overline{\phi_{1,v}(g)}\phi_{2,v}(g)dg

is the local pairing. Then
\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s})
has an Euler product expression

(2.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s})

\displaystyle=\prod_{v}\mathcal{Z}_{v}(s;\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2,v},f_{s,v}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{v}(s;\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2,v},f_{s,v})

\displaystyle=\int_{G(F_{v})}f_{s,v}(\delta(g,1))\langle\pi(g)\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2,v}\rangle dg.

Hence, the global integral
\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s})
can be studied locally place by place.

In some works of the doubling method (e.g.
[
6
,
25
,
50
,
53
]
), the integral of the following form is considered

(2.22)

\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(g_{2};\phi_{1},f_{s})=\int_{G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})}E((g_{1},g_{2});f_{s})\overline{\phi_{1}(g_{1})}dg_{1}.

The computation of (
2.22
) is same as the one for (
2.16
). In particular, we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(g_{2};\phi_{1},f_{s})

\displaystyle=\chi(\nu(g_{2}))\int_{G(\mathbb{A})}f_{s}(\delta(g_{1},1))\phi_{1}(g_{2}g_{1})dg_{1}

\displaystyle=\chi(\nu(g_{2}))\prod_{v}\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}_{v}(s;\phi_{1,v},f_{s,v}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}_{v}(g_{2};\phi_{1,v},f_{s,v})

\displaystyle=\int_{G(F_{v})}f_{s,v}(\delta(g_{1},1))\phi_{1,v}(g_{2}g_{1})dg_{1}.

2.3.
Main result on integral representations

The first main result of this paper is an integral representation of standard
L
-functions. That is, we make the choice of
f_{s}
such that the global integral
\mathcal{Z}
in (
2.16
) represents the
L
-function defined in Section
3
. We summarize our result here.

Let
\mathfrak{o}
be the ring of integers of
F
and
\mathcal{O}
a maximal order of
D
. Denote
\mathfrak{o}_{v},\mathcal{O}_{v}
for their localizations and assume
D_{v}=\mathcal{O}_{v}\otimes_{\mathfrak{o}_{v}}F_{v}
. For a finite place

corresponds to a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
of
\mathfrak{o}
, denote
\varpi_{v}
for the uniformizer of
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
and set
q_{v}=|\varpi_{v}|_{v}^{-1}
. Fix an
\mathfrak{o}
-ideal

with

. Define the following open compact subgroup of

(2.24)

where
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\prod_{v,\mathfrak{c}_{v}\geq 1}\mathfrak{p}_{v}
is the support of

.

Let
\phi\in\pi
be a cusp form. Set
S_{\infty}
be the set of all archimedean places of
F
. Denote
S_{1}
be the set consisting of places dividing
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
and
S_{2}
the set consisting of places dividing
\mathfrak{n}_{2}
. We make the following assumptions:

(1)
2\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
and

for all

,

(2)
\phi
is fixed by

and is unramified outside

, i.e. fixed by

for

,

(3)
\phi
is an eigenfunction for the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(K(\mathfrak{n}),\mathfrak{X})
as in Section
3.4
,

(4)
\chi
has conductor
\mathfrak{n}_{2}
,

(5) In Case V, all places

are nonsplit in
\mathcal{O}
.

The standard
L
-function
L(s,\phi\times\chi)
of
\phi

\chi
is defined in Section
3.4
. There is an Euler product expression

(2.25)

L(s,\phi\times\chi)=\prod_{v}L_{v}(s,\phi_{v}\times\chi_{v}).

When

is the unramified local

-factors defined with

in Section

. When

L
-factors, and in particular

if

. The integral representation for the partial
L
-function

L^{S_{1}\cup S_{2}}(s,\phi\times\chi):=\prod_{v\notin S_{1}\cup S_{2}}L_{v}(s,\phi\times\chi)

is well known. We make the choice of local sections
f_{s,v}
properly for

such that the global integral
\mathcal{Z}
represent the complete
L
-function. Let

(2.27)

w=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1_{m}\\
0&1_{r}&0\\
\epsilon\cdot 1_{m}&0&0\end{array}\right]

be a Weyl element. Define
\eta_{1}\in G(\mathbb{A})
to be an element such that
(\eta_{1})_{v}=w
for

and
(\eta_{1})_{v}=1
for

. Similarly set

,

.

Keep the assumptions of

\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi,f_{s})=C\cdot L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi\times\chi\right)\cdot\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(s;\phi_{\infty},f_{s}^{\infty})\cdot\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\langle\pi(\eta)\phi_{v}|U^{\prime}(\mathfrak{n_{1}}),\phi_{v}\rangle.

Here:

(a)

), (

3.1.
Unramified local

In this and the next subsection, we do not consider three split cases (i.e Case III’, IV’, V’). Let

(3.1)

Recall the Cartan decomposition

\displaystyle G(F)

\displaystyle=\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{1},...,e_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}\\

\mathfrak{n}_{2}
. Consider the open compact subgroup

(3.23)

K(\mathfrak{n})=G(\mathfrak{o})\cap\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathrm{Mat}_{m}(\mathcal{O})&\mathrm{Mat}_{m,r}(\mathcal{O})&\mathrm{Mat}_{m}(\mathcal{O})\\
\mathrm{Mat}_{r,m}(\mathfrak{n}\mathcal{O})&1+\mathrm{Mat}_{r}(\mathfrak{n^{\prime}}\mathcal{O})&\mathrm{Mat}_{r,m}(\mathcal{O})\\
\mathrm{Mat}_{m}(\mathfrak{n}\mathcal{O})&\mathrm{Mat}_{m,r}(\mathfrak{n}\mathcal{O})&\mathrm{Mat}_{m}(\mathcal{O})\end{array}\right],

with
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
the support of
\mathfrak{n}
. For
v
a finite place corresponds to a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
denote
\mathfrak{M}_{v}=\mathrm{GL}_{m}(D_{v})\cap\mathrm{Mat}_{m}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
and set

(3.24)

\mathfrak{Q}_{v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}G(\mathfrak{o}_{v})&\mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{n},\\
\mathrm{diag}[u,1_{r},\hat{u}]\text{ with }u\in\mathfrak{M}_{v}&\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{n_{1}}\\
1&\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{n_{2}}.\end{array}\right.

Let
\mathfrak{Q}=\prod_{v}\mathfrak{Q}_{v}
and
\mathfrak{X}=K(\mathfrak{n})\mathfrak{Q}K(\mathfrak{n})
. Define the global Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}(K(\mathfrak{n}),\mathfrak{X})
associated to
K(\mathfrak{n})
and
\mathfrak{X}
be the one generated by double cosets
[K(\mathfrak{n})\xi K(\mathfrak{n})]
with
\xi\in\mathfrak{Q}
. Its action on a cusp form
\phi\in\pi
can be similarly defined as in the local case treated above. Assume
\phi\in\pi
is fixed by
K(\mathfrak{n})
and is an eigenfunction for
\mathcal{H}
. That is

(3.25)

\phi|[K(\mathfrak{n})\xi K(\mathfrak{n})]=\lambda_{\xi}(\phi)\phi.

The standard
L
-function of
\phi
twisted by
\chi
is defined as

(3.26)

L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi\times\chi\right)=b(s,\chi)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in K(\mathfrak{n})\backslash\mathfrak{X}/K(\mathfrak{n})\\
\xi=\mathrm{diag}[u,1,\hat{u}]\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\xi}(\phi)\chi(\nu(u))|\mathrm{N}_{E/F}(\nu(u))|^{s+\kappa}.

Clearly, it has an Euler product expression

(3.27)

L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi\times\chi\right)=\prod_{v}L_{v}\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi_{v}\times\chi_{v}\right),

with
L_{v}\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi_{v}\times\chi_{v}\right)
the local
L
-factors defined in the previous two subsections. Note that when
v\nmid\mathfrak{n}
is unramified,
G(\mathfrak{o}_{v})
may have Witt index
m^{\prime}\geq m
and the unramified local
L
-factors is defined by replacing
m
with
m^{\prime}
in Section
3.1
.

Remark 3.5
.

We give several remarks on our
L
-functions.

(1) Here we define the
L
-function by a Dirichlet series associated to certain Hecke eigenvalues which can be viewed as an analogue of the
L
-function for classical (
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
) modular forms. This kind of
L
-function is also studied in
[
50
,
53
]
for symplectic and unitary groups.

(2) As the reader may notice, we are writing
L(s,\phi\times\chi)
to indicate its dependence on the cusp form
\phi
. Unlike the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
case, we do not have a clear correspondence between eigenforms and cuspidal representations. This is because, the subspace of
\pi
fixed by
K(\mathfrak{n})
(take
\mathfrak{n}
to be the conductor of
\pi
) may not be of dimension one. However, we remark that newform theory is recently known for unitary groups in
[
1
,
2
]
.

(3) The unramified local
L
-factors defined here are really the Langlands
L
-function associated to the natural embedding of the
L
-group
{{}^{L}}G
into a general linear group.

(4) We make no claim that our definition of ramified
L
-factors is ‘correct’. Indeed, it is a conjecture of Langlands
[
36
]
that for any cuspidal representation
\pi
one can associate to any place is a local
L
-factor
L_{v}(s,\pi_{v})
and a local root number
\epsilon_{v}(s,\pi_{v})
such that the global
L
-function satisfies a functional equation of the form

L(s,\pi)=\epsilon(s,\pi)L(1-s,\pi).

Using the doubling method, Yamana
[
57
]
gives a definition of local
L
-factors and proves the functional equation for classical groups. However, he does not define these factors explicitly as in our Proposition
3.1
,
3.3
and it is not clear how his approach can be used to study algebraicity or
p
-adic properties which is interested in this paper. We have not compared our
L
-factors with his and we also do not know whether the
L
-function defined here can be proved to satisfy a functional equation.

4.
The Non-archimedean Local Integrals

We carried out the computations of the non-archimedean local integrals in this section. We keep the setting for non-archimedean local fields and tuples
(D,\rho,\epsilon)
as in the beginning of Section
3
. The unramified local integrals are well known but we will also review the computations for completeness. For ramified local integrals, it is also well known that one can choose a local section
f_{s,v}
such that
\mathcal{Z}_{v}(s;\phi_{1,v},\phi_{2,v},f_{s,v})\neq 0
. For our purpose, we explicitly construct certain local sections
f_{s}^{\dagger},f_{s}^{\ddagger},f_{s}^{p}
such that
\mathcal{Z}
represents the local
L
-factors defined in the last section or the
p
-adic modifications. These local sections will also be chosen such that the Eisenstein series has a nice Fourier expansion. To make our notations and computations consistent, we do not consider the split cases (i.e. Case III’, IV’, V’). For Case III’, IV’, the local groups are isomorphic to the groups in Case I, II and our arguments can be directly extended to these two cases. The Case V’ should be treated separately and we omit it for simplicity. We will assume in this paper that Case V’ does not occur in the ramified setting.

4.1.
Setup for non-archimedean local integrals

Let

(4.1)

G:=G(F):=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(D):g\Phi g^{\ast}=\Phi\},\,\Phi=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1_{m}\\
0&\theta&0\\
\epsilon\cdot 1_{m}&0&0\end{array}\right],

with
n=2m+r
and
\theta^{\ast}=\epsilon\theta\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(D)
not necessarily anisotropic. Let

(4.2)

H:=H(F):=\{h\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(D):hJ_{n}h^{\ast}=J_{n}\},\,J_{n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1_{n}\\
\epsilon\cdot 1_{n}&0\end{array}\right],

and define an embedding

(4.3)

\displaystyle G\times G

\displaystyle\to H,

\displaystyle(g_{1},g_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{1}&0\\
0&g_{2}\end{array}\right]R^{-1},

with

R=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&\frac{\epsilon}{2}\cdot 1_{r}&0&0&\frac{\epsilon}{2}\cdot 1_{r}&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&-\epsilon\cdot 1_{m}\\
1_{m}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&\theta^{-1}&0&0&-\theta^{-1}&0\\
0&0&0&1_{m}&0&0\\
0&0&1_{m}&0&0&0\end{array}\right].

We identify
(g_{1},g_{2})
with its image in
H
. Let
P\subset H
be the Siegel parabolic subgroup consisting elements of the form
\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\ast&\ast\\
0_{n}&\ast\end{array}\right]
, with Levi decomposition
P=M\ltimes N
. Consider the induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{P(F)}^{H(F)}(\chi|\nu(\cdot)|^{s})
for a character
\chi:E^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let
\pi
be an admissible representation of
G(F)
and
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\pi
. For a section
f_{s}\in\mathrm{Ind}_{P(F)}^{H(F)}(\chi|\nu(\cdot)|^{s})
we consider the local integral

(4.4)

\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s})=\int_{G(F)}f_{s}(\delta(g,1))\langle\pi(g)\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle dg,

where

\delta=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1_{r}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&1_{m}&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&1_{m}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&1_{r}&0&0\\
0&0&-1_{m}&0&1_{m}&0\\
0&\epsilon\cdot 1_{m}&0&0&0&1_{m}\end{array}\right].

4.2.
The unramified local integrals

Assume
\chi
is an unramified character of
E^{\times}
. Let
\pi
be an unramified admissible representation of
G(F)
and
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=\phi\in\pi
a spherical vector.

Take the local section
f_{s}^{0}\in\mathrm{Ind}_{P(F)}^{H(F)}(\chi|\nu\cdot|^{s})
to be the spherical section normalized such that

(4.5)

f_{s}^{0}(pk)=\chi(\nu(a))|\mathrm{N}_{E/F}(\nu(a))|^{s+\kappa}b(s,\chi)\text{ with }p=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&\hat{a}\end{array}\right],k\in H(\mathfrak{o}).

Here
b(s,\chi)
is the normalizing factor given in Section
3.1
. Note that in (
4.1
),
\theta
is not necessarily anisotropic. Let
m^{\prime}
be the Witt index of
G
. That is
G
is isomorphic to the following
F
-group

(4.6)

G^{\prime}:=G^{\prime}(\Phi^{\prime}):=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(D):g\Phi^{\prime}g^{\ast}=\Phi^{\prime}\}

with

\Phi^{\prime}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1_{m^{\prime}}\\
0&\theta^{\prime}&0\\
\epsilon 1_{m^{\prime}}&0&0\end{array}\right],n=2m^{\prime}+r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime\ast}=\epsilon\theta^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GL}_{r^{\prime}}(D)\text{ anisotropic }.

Then there exists a matrix
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(D)
with
S\Phi^{\prime}S^{\ast}=\Phi
and the isomorphism between
G
and
G^{\prime}
are given by

(4.7)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}G,

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto SgS^{-1}.

Denote by
\pi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}
for the admissible representation and cusp form of
G^{\prime}(F)
obtained from
\pi,\phi
under isomorphism (
4.7
). Then the local
L
-factor
L(s,\phi\times\chi)=L(s,\phi^{\prime}\times\chi)
is defined in Section
3.1
with
m
replaced by
m^{\prime}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
f_{s}^{0}
chosen as above and
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=\phi\in\pi
a spherical vector, we have

(4.8)

\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi,\phi,f_{s}^{0})=L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi\times\chi\right)\langle\phi,\phi\rangle.

Proof.

The computation is similar to the one in
[
42
]
for symplectic groups. We have another doubling embedding

\displaystyle G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}

\displaystyle\to H,

\displaystyle(g_{1},g_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto R^{\prime}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{1}&0\\
0&g_{2}\end{array}\right]R^{\prime-1},\qquad R^{\prime}=R\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}S&0\\
0&S\end{array}\right]

which is compatible with (
4.3
),(
4.7
). Then the integral (
4.4
) is equivalent to the integral

\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},f_{s})=\int_{G^{\prime}(F)}f_{s}(\delta(g,1))\langle\pi^{\prime}(g)\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle dg.

By Cartan decomposition, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{1},...,e_{m^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
0\leq e_{1}\leq...\leq e_{m^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}f^{0}_{s}(\delta\cdot(\mathrm{diag}[\widetilde{\varpi}^{e_{1}},...,\widetilde{\varpi}^{e_{m^{\prime}}},1_{r^{\prime}},\widetilde{\varpi}^{-e_{1}},...,\widetilde{\varpi}^{-e_{m^{\prime}}}],1))\int_{K_{e_{1},...,e_{m^{\prime}}}}\langle\pi^{\prime}(g)\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle dg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{1},...,e_{m^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
0\leq e_{1}\leq...\leq e_{m^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\left(\chi(\nu(\widetilde{\varpi}))|\mathrm{N}_{E/F}(\nu(\widetilde{\varpi}))|^{s+\kappa}\right)^{e_{1}+...+e_{m^{\prime}}}b(s,\chi)\lambda_{e_{1},...,e_{m^{\prime}}}(\phi^{\prime})\langle\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi^{\prime}\times\chi\right)\langle\phi^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle.

∎

4.3.
The local section
f_{s}^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}}

In this and the next two subsections, we consider the ramified local integrals. We will always assume
2\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
and
\theta\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
in the ramified cases. For an integer
\mathfrak{c}\geq 1
let
N^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})
be the subgroup of
N(F)
consisting of elements of the form

(4.9)

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1_{r}&0&x&y\\
0&1_{2m}&\epsilon y^{\ast}&z\\
0&0&1_{r}&0\\
0&0&0&1_{2m}\end{array}\right],\,x\in S_{r}(\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}),y\in\mathrm{Mat}_{r,2m}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O}),z\in S_{2m}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O}).

Define
f_{s}^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}}\in\mathrm{Ind}_{P(F)}^{H(F)}(\chi|\nu(\cdot)|^{s})
to be a local section supported on
P(F)J_{n}N^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})
with

(4.10)

f_{s}^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}}(pJ_{n}n)=\chi(\nu(a))|\mathrm{N}_{E/F}(\nu(a))|^{s+\kappa}\text{ for }p=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
0&\hat{a}\end{array}\right]\in P(F),n\in N^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}).

Note that when pulling back along
G\times G\to H
, we have

(4.11)

f_{s}^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}}((g_{1}k_{1},g_{2}k_{2}))=\chi(\nu(d_{k_{1}}))\chi(\nu(a_{k_{2}}))f_{s}^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}}((g_{1},g_{2}))

for
k_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{k_{1}}&f_{k_{1}}&b_{k_{1}}\\
h_{k_{1}}&e_{k_{1}}&j_{k_{1}}\\
c_{k_{1}}&k_{k_{1}}&d_{k_{1}}\end{array}\right]\in K^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})
and
k_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{k_{2}}&f_{k_{2}}&b_{k_{2}}\\
h_{k_{2}}&e_{k_{2}}&j_{k_{2}}\\
c_{k_{2}}&k_{k_{2}}&d_{k_{2}}\end{array}\right]\in K(\mathfrak{p^{c}})
.

The following proposition is an analogue of the computations in
[
49
]
for symplectic groups. We extend the arguments there to all classical groups. Especially, the main difficulty in the computations is to deal with the group with
r\neq 0
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
\phi\in\pi
is fixed by
K(\mathfrak{p^{c}})
and is an eigenvector of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(K(\mathfrak{p^{c}}),\mathfrak{X})
. Set
\phi_{1}=\pi(w)\phi,\phi_{2}=\phi
. Assume
\chi
is an unramified character and
2\in\mathcal{O}^{\times},\theta\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
, then

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(s;\pi(w)\phi,\phi,f_{s}^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}})=\chi(\varpi)^{\mathfrak{c}m\mathbf{d}_{1}}q^{-\mathfrak{c}m\mathbf{d}_{2}(s+\kappa)}L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi\times\chi\right)\cdot\left\langle\phi|U^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}),\phi\right\rangle.

Here

(4.13)

\mathbf{d}_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ Case I, II, V},\\
2&\text{ Case III, IV},\end{array}\right.\qquad\mathbf{d}_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\text{ Case I, II},\\
2&\text{ Case III, IV, V},\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Denote

\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})=\mathrm{GL}_{m}(D)\cap\mathrm{Mat}_{m}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O}),\,\mathfrak{Q}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})=\{\mathrm{diag}[\hat{u},-1_{r},u],u\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})\}.

We claim that

f_{s}^{\dagger}(\delta(g,1))\neq 0\text{ if and only if }g\in K(\mathfrak{p^{c}})\mathfrak{Q}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})K^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}).

Write
g=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&f&b\\
h&e&j\\
c&k&d\end{array}\right]
with
a,d
of size
m\times m
,
e
of size
r\times r
and compute

\delta(g,1)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{e+1}{2}&0&\frac{\epsilon h}{2}&\frac{\epsilon(e-1)\theta}{4}&0&\frac{\epsilon j}{2}\\
0&1&0&0&0&0\\
\epsilon f&0&a&\frac{f\theta}{2}&0&b\\
\epsilon\theta^{-1}(e-1)&0&\theta^{-1}h&\theta^{-1}\frac{e+1}{2}\theta&0&\theta^{-1}j\\
-\epsilon f&0&-a&-\frac{f\theta}{2}&1&-b\\
\epsilon k&\epsilon&c&\frac{k\theta}{2}&0&d\end{array}\right].

The elements in
P(F)J_{n}N^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})
can be written as

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
0&0&D_{1}&D_{2}\\
0&0&D_{3}&D_{4}\end{array}\right]J_{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1_{r}&0&x&y\\
0&1_{2m}&\epsilon y^{\ast}&z\\
0&0&1_{r}&0\\
0&0&0&1_{2m}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
\epsilon D_{1}&\epsilon D_{2}&\epsilon D_{1}x+D_{2}y^{\ast}&\epsilon D_{1}y+\epsilon D_{2}z\\
\epsilon D_{3}&\epsilon D_{4}&\epsilon D_{3}x+D_{4}y^{\ast}&\epsilon D_{3}y+\epsilon D_{4}z\end{array}\right],

with
x\in S_{r}(\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}),y\in\mathrm{Mat}_{r,2m}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O}),z\in S_{2m}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O})
. Assume
\delta(g,1)
is of above form, then comparing two expressions we need

D_{1}=\theta^{-1}(e-1),\,D_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\epsilon\theta^{-1}h\end{array}\right],\,D_{3}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-f\\
k\end{array}\right],\,D_{4}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\epsilon a\\
1&\epsilon c\end{array}\right],

and

\displaystyle\epsilon D_{1}x+D_{2}y^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\theta^{-1}\frac{e+1}{2}\theta,

\displaystyle D_{1}y+D_{2}z

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\epsilon\theta^{-1}j\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\epsilon D_{3}x+D_{4}y^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\frac{f\theta}{2}\\
\frac{k\theta}{2}\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle D_{3}y+D_{4}z

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon&-\epsilon b\\
0&\epsilon d\end{array}\right].

First of all, write
y=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}y_{1}&y_{2}\end{array}\right]
, then

\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\frac{f\theta}{2}\\
\frac{k\theta}{2}\end{array}\right]=\epsilon D_{3}x+D_{4}y^{\ast}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\epsilon fx-\epsilon ay_{2}^{\ast}\\
\epsilon kx+y_{1}^{\ast}+\epsilon cy_{2}^{\ast}\end{array}\right]

implies

ay_{2}^{\ast}=-\epsilon f(\epsilon x-\frac{\theta}{2}),\qquad y_{1}^{\ast}+\epsilon cy_{2}^{\ast}=-k(\epsilon x-\frac{\theta}{2}).

Since by our assumption
\frac{\theta}{2}\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathcal{O})
and the condition on
x
, the first equation forces
a
to be invertible and thus

y_{2}^{\ast}=-\epsilon a^{-1}f(\epsilon x-\frac{\theta}{2}),\qquad y_{1}^{\ast}=-(k-ca^{-1}f)(\epsilon x-\frac{\theta}{2}).

The condition on
y
then forces
a^{-1}f,k-ca^{-1}f\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m,r}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O})
. Secondly, from

\theta^{-1}\frac{e+1}{2}\theta=\epsilon D_{1}x+D_{2}y^{\ast}=\epsilon\theta^{-1}(e-1)x+\epsilon\theta^{-1}hy_{2}^{\ast},

we obtain

\epsilon(e-1+ha^{-1}f)x=\frac{e+1-ha^{-1}f}{2}\theta.

The condition on
x
then forces
e-ha^{-1}f+1\in\mathrm{Mat}_{r}(\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O})
. Finally, comparing
D_{4}
and
D_{3}y+D_{4}z
, the condition on
z
forces

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\epsilon a\\
1&\epsilon c\end{array}\right]^{-1}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon&-\epsilon b\\
0&\epsilon d\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon ca^{-1}&\epsilon d\\
-a^{-1}&a^{-1}b\end{array}\right]\in\mathrm{Mat}_{2m}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O})

and hence
a^{-1},ca^{-1},a^{-1}b\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}\mathcal{O})
. Since
a
is invertible, we can write

g=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1_{m}&0&0\\
ha^{-1}&1_{r}&0\\
ca^{-1}&-\epsilon\hat{a}h^{\ast}\theta^{-1}&1_{m}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&0&0\\
0&e-ha^{-1}f&0\\
0&0&\hat{a}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1_{m}&a^{-1}f&a^{-1}b\\
0&1_{r}&-\epsilon\theta f^{\ast}\hat{a}\\
0&0&1_{m}\end{array}\right]

and our claim clearly follows.

By straightforward computations and the fact that

K(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathfrak{c}})\mathfrak{Q}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})K^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})=K(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathfrak{c}})\mathfrak{Q}K(\mathfrak{p^{c}})\cdot K(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathfrak{c}})\mathrm{diag}[\varpi^{-\mathfrak{c}}\cdot 1_{m},1_{r},\varpi^{\mathfrak{c}}\cdot 1_{m}]K^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}),

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(s;\pi(w)\phi,\phi,f^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}}_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(\varpi)^{\mathfrak{c}m\mathbf{d}_{1}}q^{-\mathfrak{c}m\mathbf{d}_{2}(s+\kappa)}\sum_{\xi\in K(\mathfrak{p^{c}})\backslash\mathfrak{X}/K(\mathfrak{p^{c}})}\lambda_{\xi}(\phi)(\chi(\nu(\widetilde{\varpi}))|\mathrm{N}_{E/F}(\nu(\widetilde{\varpi}))|^{s+\kappa})^{\mathfrak{d}(\xi)}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int_{K(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathfrak{c}})\mathrm{diag}[\varpi^{-\mathfrak{c}}\cdot 1_{m},-1_{r},\varpi^{\mathfrak{c}}\cdot 1_{m}]K^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}})}\left\langle\pi(gw)\phi,\phi\right\rangle dg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(\varpi)^{\mathfrak{c}m\mathbf{d}_{1}}q^{-\mathfrak{c}m\mathbf{d}_{2}(s+\kappa)}L\left(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi\times\chi\right)\left\langle\phi|U^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p^{c}}),\phi\right\rangle.

∎

4.4.
The local section
f_{s}^{\ddagger,\mathfrak{c}}

If
\chi
is a ramified character then
\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s}^{\dagger,\mathfrak{c}})
will be identically zero. In this subsection, we define a section
f_{s}^{\ddagger,\mathfrak{c}}
as a twist of
f_{s}^{\dagger,0}
such that
\mathcal{Z}(s;\phi_{1},\phi_{2},f_{s}^{\ddagger,\mathfrak{c}})
is a non-zero constant. Assume
\chi
has conductor
\mathfrak{p^{c}}
, we define

(4.14)

\displaystyle f_{s}^{\ddagger,\mathfrak{c}}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathcal{O})/\varpi^{\mathfrak{c}}\mathrm{GL}_{m}(\mathcal{O})}\chi^{-1}(\nu(u))

\displaystyle\times f_{s}^{\dagger,0}\left(h\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1_{r}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&1_{m}&0&0&0&\frac{u}{\varpi^{\mathfrak{c}}}\\
0&0&1_{m}&0&-\frac{\epsilon u^{\ast}}{\varpi^{\mathfrak{c}}}&0\\

5.3.
Algebraic modular forms

For later use, we summarize the definition and properties of algebraic modular forms here. For holomorphic modular forms considered in this paper, there are mainly four characterizations of algebraic modular forms:

(1) In
[
27
,
28
,
41
]
automorphic forms are interpreted as sections of certain automorphic vector bundles. The canonical model of automorphic vector bundles then defines a subspace of algebraic automorphic forms.

(2) In
[
7

21
,
24
,
53
]
algebraic modular forms are defined via CM points.

(3) In
[
22
,
23
,
24
]

\mathrm{GL}_{2}

(4) In
[
24
]
, there is yet another characterization using the pullback to classical modular forms over
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. Moreover, three definitions (2,3,4) are also proved to be equivalent there.

We first review the adelic definition of modular forms. Denote

Remark 8.7
.

(1) In this paper we have only consider one critical point at

(3) We have only considered the parallel weight in this section. Especially, our archimedean computations have only done for scalar weight. For the general weight,

]
and

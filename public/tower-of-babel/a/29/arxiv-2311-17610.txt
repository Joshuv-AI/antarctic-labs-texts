Abstract




Abstract:

By using the global deformation of almost complex structures which are compatible with a symplectic form off a Lebesgue measure zero subset, we construct a (measurable) Lipschitz Kähler metric
such that the one-form type Calabi-Yau equation on an open dense submanifold is reduced to the complex Monge-Ampère equation with respect to the measurable Kähler metric.
We give an existence theorem for solutions to the one-form type Calabi-Yau equation on closed symplectic manifolds.

Keywords:
Calabi-Yau equation; Hodge theory for nonsmooth manifolds; deformation of almost Kähler structures; (measurable) Lipschitz Kähler structures.

Introduction

Yau’s Theorem for closed Kähler manifolds
[
75
]
states that one can prescribe the volume form
of a Kähler metric within a given Kähler class.
This result occupies a central place in the theory of Kähler manifolds (cf. Calabi
[
8
,
9
]
), with wide-ranging applications in geometry
and mathematical physics (cf. Joyce
[
40
]
, Yau
[
74
]
and Fu-Yau
[
28
]
).

More precisely, Yau’s Theorem is as follows.
Let
(M^{2n},\omega,J,g_{J})
be a closed Kähler manifold of real dimension
2n
, where
\omega
denotes the Kähler form,

J
an
\omega
-compatible complex structure and
g_{J}(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
.
Then given a smooth volume form
e^{F}\omega^{n}
on
M^{2n}
there exists a unique smooth function
\varphi
satisfying

\displaystyle(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}\varphi)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{F}\omega^{n},

\displaystyle\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}\varphi

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,\,\,\,\sup_{M^{2n}}\varphi=0

(1.1)

as long as
F
satisfies the necessary normalization condition that

\int_{M^{2n}}e^{F}\omega^{n}=\int_{M^{2n}}\omega^{n}.

(1.2)

Equation (
1
) is known as the complex Monge-Ampère equation for Kähler manifolds.
It is based on the method of continuity and in any given situation requires laborious a priori estimates for the derivatives of
the solution
\varphi
up to third order (cf. S.-T. Yau
[
75
]
or D. D. Joyce
[
40
, Chapter 6]
).
The uniqueness of the solution
\varphi
has an elementary proof found by Calabi
[
9
, p. 86]
.

In a similar vein, L. Caffarelli, J. J. Kohn, L. Nirenberg and J. Spruck
[
7
]
have proved regularity results for the Dirichlet problem
associated the following equation:

\det(\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{k}})=f

(1.3)

in a strictly pseudoconvex domain.
Also there has been interest in extending the Dirichlet problem for Equation (
1.3
) in a strictly pseudoconvex domain with
continuous boundary data.
Again, in general
f
must be replaced by Borel measure (cf. S. Kołodziej
[
41
]
and the references therein).

Recall that a Kähler metric is positive definite real
(1,1)
form
\omega
,
namely a Hermitian metric, which satisfies

d\omega=0
.
One extension of Yau’s Theorem, initiated by Cherrier
[
16
]
in the 1980’s, is to remove this closedness condition.
This was carried out in full generality in Tosatti-Weinkove
[
66
]
and the references therein where it was shown that (
1
)
has a unique solution for
\omega
up to adding a (unique) constant to
F
.
In
[
29
,
30
]
, Fu, Wang and Wu consider the form -type Calabi-Yau equations.

A different type of extension of Yau’s Theorem is to the case when
J
is a non-integrable almost complex structure.
Recall that an almost complex structure
J
is said to be tamed by symplectic from
\omega
when the bilinear form
\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)

is positive definite. The almost complex structure
J
is said to be compatible (or calibrate) with
\omega
when the same bilinear form is also symmetric,
that is,
\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)>0
and
\omega(J\cdot,J\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
(cf. M. Gromov
[
34
]
or McDuff-Salamon
[
46
]
).
In 1990’s, Gromov posed the following problem to P. Delanoë
[
19
]
:
Let
(M^{2n},\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold,
J
an almost complex structure compatible with
\omega

and
F
a smooth function on
M^{2n}
with

\int_{M^{2n}}e^{F}\omega^{n}=\int_{M^{2n}}\omega^{n}.

Can one find a smooth function
\varphi
on
M
such that
\omega+d(Jd\varphi)
is a symplectic form taming
J
and satisfying

(\omega+d(Jd\varphi))^{n}=e^{F}\omega^{n}?

(1.4)

However, P. Delanoë
[
19
]
showed that when
n=2
the answer to this question is negative, this was later extended to all
dimensions by Wang-Zhu
[
71
]
.
The key ingredient of their results in the construction of a smooth function such that
\omega+d(Jd\varphi_{0})
is on the boundary of the
set of taming symplectic forms (so its
(1,1)
part is semipositive definite but not strictly positive),
and yet
(\omega+d(Jd\varphi_{0}))^{n}>0
.
This is possible because in this case the
(2,0)+(0,2)
part of
d(Jd\varphi_{0})
contributes a strictly positive amount.
This indicate that the problem with Gromov’s suggestion is that the 2-form
d(Jd\varphi)
is in general not of type
(1,1)
with respect to
J
,
due to the fact that
J
may not be integrable.
Its
(1,1)
part will be denoted by

\sqrt{-1}\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}\varphi=\frac{1}{2}(d(Jd\varphi))^{(1,1)}

which agrees with the standard notation when
J
is integrable.

Therefore, one can consider the Monge-Ampère equation for non-integrable almost complex structures.
There are many important works when the base manifolds are almost complex manifolds.
We refer to Harvey-Lawson
[

, Tosatti-Wang-Weinkove-Yang
[

]
, Chu-Tosatti-Weinkove
[

]
,
and the references therein.

Suppose that
(M^{2n},\omega,J,g_{J})
is a closed almost Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
.
By using the deformation of
\omega
-compatible almost complex structures,
we can construct a measurable Kähler flat structure
(\omega,J_{0},g_{0})
on
M^{2n}
which is homotopy equivalent to
the given smooth almost Kähler structure
(\omega,J,g_{J})
and satisfies Lipschitz condition (
A.7

(more details see Appendix

It is similar to Kähler case (cf. Yau
[

we have the following generalized Calabi-Yau equation of Donaldson type (that is, the one-form type Calabi-Yau equation) on symplectic manifolds:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(M^{2n},\omega)
is a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.
Let
F\in C^{\infty}(M^{2n},\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\int_{M^{2n}}e^{F}\omega^{n}=\int_{M^{2n}}\omega^{n}.

Then, there exists a smooth
1
-form,
a(F)
, on
M^{2n}
satisfying

(\omega+da(F))^{n}=e^{F}\omega^{n}.

Therefore,
\omega+da(F)
is a new symplectic form on
M^{2n}
which is cohomologous to
\omega
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
can be regarded a generalization of Yau’s Theorem
[
75
]
in symplectic case.
Suppose that
(M^{2n},\omega)
is a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.
If there exists an
\omega
-compatible, integrable complex structure
J_{0}
on
M^{2n}
,
let
g_{J_{0}}(\cdot,\cdot):=\omega(\cdot,J_{0}\cdot)
, then
(\omega,J_{0},g_{J_{0}})
is a Kähler structure on
M^{2n}
.
Thus, in Kähler case, the Calabi-Yau equation is reduced to the complex Monge-Ampère equation.
By Yau’s Theorem
[
75
]
, there exists a unique solution to the complex Monge-Ampère equation for closed Kähler manifolds
(see also Aubin
[
2
, Chapter 7]
).
In general, the

in Theorem

Notice that in
[

, Székelyhidi, Tosatti and Weinkove pointed that for Gauduchon conjecture
[

]

(that is, there is a Gauduchon metric

Donaldson
[

conjectured that the space of Kähler metrics is geodesic convex by smooth geodesic and that it is a metric space.
Following Donaldson’s program, X. X. Chen
[

,
14
]

[

studied the space of Kähler metrics.

Notice that by the solution of the Calabi-Yau equation on a closed symplectic manifold
(M^{2n},\omega)
,
we obtain a new symplectic form on
M^{2n}
which is cohomologous to the given symplectic form
\omega
.
Hence, on closed symplectic manifolds, uniqueness of the solution to the Calabi-Yau equation on given symplectic manifold can be regarded
as the uniqueness of the obtained symplectic structure on the given symplectic manifold in the sense of following definition.

Let
M^{2n}
be a closed manifold of dimension
2n
.
For two symplectic forms
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{1}
on
M^{2n}
,
we say that
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{1}
have equivalent relation if and only if
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{1}

are connected by a path of cohomologous symplectic forms (cf. D. A. Salamon
[
50
]
).
Hence, the equivalent relation of solutions to the Calabi-Yau equation is defined as follows:

Definition 1.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.
Suppose that
a_{0}(F)
and
a_{1}(F)\in\Omega^{1}(M^{2n})
are two solutions of the Calabi-Yau equation on
(M^{2n},\omega)
:

(\omega+da)^{n}=e^{F}\omega^{n},

where

a\in\Omega^{1}(M^{2n}),\,\,\,\int_{M^{2n}}\omega^{n}=\int_{M^{2n}}e^{F}\omega^{n}.

a_{0}(F)
and
a_{1}(F)
have an equivalent relation if and only if
\omega+da_{0}(F)
and
\omega+da_{1}(F)

are connected by a path of cohomologous symplectic forms.

\omega+da_{0}(F)
and
\omega+da_{1}(F)
are called isotopic.

Notice that by Moser isotopy (cf. Moser
[
47
]
or McDuff-Salamon
[
46
, §3.3]
),

\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{1}
have equivalent relation if and only if there exists a diffeomorphism

\psi:M^{2n}\rightarrow M^{2n}
isotopic to the identity such that
\psi^{*}\omega_{1}=\omega_{0}
.
Denote the space of symplectic forms on
M^{2n}
representing the class
[\omega]
by

\Psi(M^{2n},[\omega]):=\{\rho\in\Omega^{2}(M^{2n})\,|\,\rho^{n}/\omega^{n}>0,d\rho=0,[\rho]=[\omega]\}.

This is an open set in the Fréchet space of all closed 2-forms on
M^{2n}
representing the class
[\omega]
.
Hence, the uniqueness of the solution to the Calabi-Yau equation on closed symplectic manifolds is equivalent to the following question
(cf. McDuff-Salamon
[
46
, Chapter 13]
or Salamon
[
50
]
):

Question 1.4
.

Suppose that
(M^{2n},\omega)
is a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.
Is
\Psi(M^{2n},[\omega])
connected?

Remark 1.5
.

(1) McDuff
[
45
]
(see also McDuff-Salamon
[
46
, Theorem 9.7.4]
) considered symplectic manifold
(T^{2}\times S^{2}\times S^{2},\omega)
.
Here the set
\Psi(T^{2}\times S^{2}\times S^{2},[\omega])
is disconnected.
However, by an arbitrarily small pertubation of the cohomology class
[\omega]
, the two known distinct connected components of

\Psi(T^{2}\times S^{2}\times S^{2},[\omega])

merge to a single connected component.

(2) Question
1.4
is completely open for closed symplectic four-manifolds (cf. McDuff-Salamon
[
46
, Chapter 13]
).
The Donaldson geometric flow approach to uniqueness problem for hyperKähler surfaces and general symplectic four manifolds.
The Donaldson geometric flow was introduced by S. K. Donaldson in
[
22
]
.
For a detailed exposition see R. Krom and D. A. Salamon
[
42
]
.

Suppose that
(M^{2n},\omega)
is a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.
We can find an
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
on
M^{2n}
.
Set
g_{J}(\cdot,\cdot):=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
which is a

-invariant Riemannian metric on
M^{2n}
,
then the triple

is an almost Kähler structure on

(cf.
[

]
).
The

-extension of the almost complex structure

on the complexified tangent bundle

is defined by

J(X+\sqrt{-1}Y)=JX+\sqrt{-1}JY

for
X,Y\in TM^{2n}
.
Obviously,
J^{2}=-id
on
TM^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
Therefore,

TM^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}=T^{(1,0)}\oplus T^{(0,1)},

where,

T^{(1,0)}:=\{X\in TM^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}\,|\,JX=\sqrt{-1}X\}.

J
induces an almost complex structure on
\Lambda^{k}T^{*}M^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.
Hence

\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M^{2n}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\Lambda^{(2,0)}\oplus\Lambda^{(1,1)}\oplus\Lambda^{(0,2)}

is the space of
(p,q)
-forms.
Let
P_{2,0}
,
P_{1,1}
and

be the projections to
\Lambda^{(2,0)}
,
\Lambda^{(1,1)}
and

For each symplectic potential
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M^{2n},\mathbb{R})
, set

\displaystyle\omega(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega+d(Jd\phi)

(2.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(\phi)+H(\phi)+\overline{\tau(\phi)}

where,

\displaystyle\tau(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{2,0}(\omega(\phi)),

\displaystyle H(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{1,1}(\omega(\phi)).

(2.3)

.

(cf. Wang-Zhu

.

Definition 2.5

Suppose that

(1) If

3
Global theory of the Calabi-Yau equation

This section is devoted to studying global theory of the Calabi-Yau equation on symplectic manifolds.

Suppose that
(M^{2n},\omega)
is a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
.
Given
F\in C^{\infty}(M^{2n},\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\int_{M^{2n}}e^{F}\omega^{n}=\int_{M^{2n}}\omega^{n},

we want to find a
1
-form
a\in\Omega^{1}(M^{2n})
such that
a
is a solution of the following
Calabi-Yau equation:

(\omega+da)^{n}=e^{F}\omega^{n}.

(3.1)

For
(M^{2n},\omega)
, we can find an almost Kähler structure,
(\omega,J,g_{J})
, on
M^{2n}

[
46
]
.
Where
J
is an
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure on
M^{2n}
,
g_{J}(\cdot,\cdot):=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
.
If
a
is very small, then

a=Jd\phi
, where
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M^{2n},\mathbb{R})
satisfying that

\int_{M^{2n}}\phi\omega^{n}=0,

then Equation (
3.1
) is reduced to the following complex Monge-Ampère equation:

(\omega+dJd\phi)^{n}=e^{F}\omega^{n}.

(3.2)

It is easy to see that if
J
is integrable,
then

dJd\phi=2\sqrt{-1}\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}\phi,\,\,\,dJd(\Omega^{0}(M^{2n}))=d\Omega^{1}(M^{2n}),

otherwise,
dJd(\Omega^{0}(M^{2n}))\subsetneqq d\Omega^{1}(M^{2n})
.
In Section
2
, we point out that the nonexistence of the complex Monge-Ampère equation (that is the Calabi-Yau equation of Gromov type)
and local existence theorem for one-form type Calabi-Yau equation (
3.1
) (Theorem
2.13
).
For studying global theory of the one-form type Calabi-Yau equation on closed symplectic manifold
(M^{2n},\omega)
, by
using Darboux’s coordinate subatlas
[
46
]
,
we can deform an
\omega
-compatible almost Kähler structure to an
\omega
-compatible (measurable) Lipschitz Kähler flat structure off a Lebesgue measure zero subset,
then the Calabi-Yau equation on an open dense submanifold of symplectic manifold can be reduced to the complex
Monge-Ampère equation with the (measurable) Lipschitz Kähler flat structure (cf. Proposition
A.8
in
A
).
Thus, we can use Yau’s method
[
74
]
to solve the Calabi-Yau equation on symplectic manifolds.

Suppose that
(M^{2n},\omega,J,g_{J})
is a closed almost Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
.
If there exists a
1
-form
a\in\Omega^{1}(M^{2n})
(one may assume that
a
is very small)
such that
\omega(a)=\omega+da
is a new symplectic form on
M^{2n}
,
then there exists a new almost Kähler structure
(\omega(a),J(a),g_{J(a)})
on
M^{2n}
,
where
J(a)
is an
\omega(a)
-compatible almost complex structure on
M^{2n}
,

g_{J(a)}(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(a)(\cdot,J(a)\cdot)
.
Notice that in general, if
a
is not very small,
J
is not tamed by the new symplectic form
\omega(a)
.
Let
e^{F}=\omega(a)^{n}/\omega^{n}
, then
a,F
satisfy the Calabi-Yau equation
\omega(a)^{n}=e^{F}\omega^{n}
.
Let
h(1)=g_{J}-\sqrt{-1}\omega
(resp.
h(a)(1)=g_{J(a)}-\sqrt{-1}\omega(a)
) be an almost Hermitian metric with respect to
(\omega,J,g_{J})

(resp.
(\omega(a),J(a),g_{J(a)})
) on
M^{2n}
.
By using the global deformation of almost complex on
M^{2n}
off a Lebesgue measure zero subset,
one can deform
h(1)
(resp.
h(a)(1)
) to a (measurable) Lipschitz Kähler flat
metric
h(0)
(resp.
h(a)(0)
) on
M^{2n}
off a Lebesgue measure zero subset.
More precisely, by Proposition
A.8
, one can define a partition,
P(\omega(a),J(a),J)
, of
M^{2n}
, and
find a finite Darboux’s coordinate subatlas
\mathcal{V}(a)=\{V_{k}(a)\}_{1\leq k\leq N(a)}

On each

On

&\\

where

Clearly, by Darboux’s theorem, it is possible to find complex coordinates

.

(cf. Joyce

h(0)(p)=2(|dz_{1}|^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+|dz_{n}|^{2})

\omega(a)_{p}=\sqrt{-1}(a_{1}dz_{1}\wedge d\bar{z}_{1}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+a_{n}dz_{n}\wedge d\bar{z}_{n})
.

Lemma B.2
.

(cf. Joyce
[
40
, Lemma 6.1.1]
)
Restricted to open dense submanifold,
\mathring{M}^{2n}(a,P)
, of
M^{2n}
,
Calabi-Yau equation

(\omega+da)^{n}=Ae^{f}\omega^{n}

is reduced to the complex Mongue-Ampère equation

(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(a))^{n}=Ae^{f}\omega^{n}.

Suppose that
\varphi_{i}(a)
,
\varphi_{j}(a)
are two Kähler potential on

and

respectively.
If

, and

is a constant on

.
Thus,
\omega+\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(a)=\omega+da
is positive on
M^{2n}
.

Proof.

Choose holomorphic coordinates
z_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,z_{n}
on connected open set
V_{k}(a)
of

.
Then in

, the new metric

is

h_{c\bar{d}}(a)(0)=h_{c\bar{d}}(0)+\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{k}(a)}{\partial z_{c}\partial\bar{z}_{\bar{d}}}.

As usual, we may interpret

as an

invertible Hermitian matrix indexed by

associated to Hermitian metric

in

Recall that if

is connected,
then on
V_{i}(a)\cap V_{j}(a)

\displaystyle da

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dd^{c}\varphi_{i}(a)=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{i}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dd^{c}\varphi_{j}(a)=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{j}(a).

Hence,

Note that

is continuous on

,
and
\omega+da
non degenerate on
M^{2n}
.
Thus,
\omega+\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(a)

.

By Theorem

\displaystyle\Delta^{1}\phi(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2h(0)(p)^{c\bar{d}}\partial_{c}\bar{\partial}_{d}\phi(p)

(B.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}(dJd\phi)^{(1,1)}(p)(v_{j},\bar{v}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}(da)^{(1,1)}(p)(v_{j},\bar{v}_{j}).

\Delta^{1}
is the Laplacian of the second canonical connection with respect to the almost Kähler metric
g_{J}
at

).
Also by Tosatti-Weinkove-Yau

.

to

hence we have the following lemma:

Lemma B.3
.

(
[

, Lemma 6.3.2]
)
In the situation of the previous lemma, at

,

\Pi^{n}_{j=1}a_{j}=Ae^{f(p)},\,\,\,\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z}_{j}}(p)=a_{j}-1

Remark B.4
.

By a result of Wang and Zhu
[

,
in general, for a solution of the Calabi-Yau equation

By the previous lemmas, it is easy to obtain the following lemma:

Lemma B.5

(

(h(a)^{c\bar{d}}(0))

By the first and last equations in Lemma

(\Delta^{L}\phi)(p)\leq n-nA^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{\frac{f}{n}}<n.

As these hold for all

Proposition B.6
.

Let
(M^{2n},\omega,J,g_{J})
be an almost Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
.
Let
f\in C^{0}(M^{2n},\mathbb{R})
,
a\in C^{1}(T^{*}M^{2n})
and
A>0
.
Set
\omega(a)=\omega+da=\omega+dJd\phi
, suppose

\omega(\phi)^{n}=Ae^{f}\omega^{n}
, and let
h(a)(0)=h(\phi)(0)
be the Kähler metric with symplectic form
\omega(a)=\omega(\phi)
on
\mathring{M}^{2n}(a,P)

Then

\Delta^{L}\phi\leq n-nA^{\frac{1}{n}}e^{\frac{f}{n}}<n,

and there are constant
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
depending only on
n
and upper bounds for
\|f\|_{C^{0}_{1}}
and
\|\Delta^{L}\phi\|_{C^{0}_{1}}
,
such that

\|h(\phi)_{c\bar{d}}(0)\|_{C^{0}_{1}}\leq c_{1},\|h(\phi)^{c\bar{d}}(0)\|_{C^{0}_{1}}\leq c_{2}

and

\|da\|_{C^{0}_{1}}=\|\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(a)\|_{C^{0}_{1}}\leq c_{3}.

Here all norms are with respect to the metric
g_{1}
(that is,
g_{J}
).

This shows that a priori bound for
\Delta^{L}\phi

dJd\phi=da=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(a).

Notice that
\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi(a)
is smooth on

Lemma B.7
.

(D.D. Joyce
[
40
, Lemma 6.3.5]
)
In the situation of Proposition
B.6
, we have

d\phi\wedge Jd\phi\wedge\omega^{n-1}=\frac{1}{n!}|\nabla\phi|^{2}_{h(0)}\omega^{n}

and

d\phi\wedge Jd\phi\wedge\omega^{n-j-1}\wedge\omega(\phi)^{j}=G_{j}\omega^{n}

for
j=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,n-1
, where
G_{j}
is a nonnegative real function on
M^{2n}
.

Proof.

Notice that
\omega^{n-1}
is a positive
J-(n-1,n-1)
form on
M^{2n}
,

*_{g_{J}}(\omega^{n-1})=(n-1)!\omega,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\omega^{n}=n!dvol_{g_{J}},

where
dvol_{g_{J}}
is the volume element with respect the metric
g_{J}
.
Since

d\phi=\partial_{J}\phi+\bar{\partial}_{J}\phi,\,\,Jd\phi=\sqrt{-1}(\bar{\partial}_{J}\phi-\partial_{J}\phi),

thus

d\phi\wedge Jd\phi\wedge\omega^{n-1}=(d\phi\wedge Jd\phi,*_{g_{J}}\omega^{n-1})dvol_{g_{J}}=\frac{1}{n!}(d\phi\wedge Jd\phi,\omega)\omega^{n}.

(B.13)

It is easy to see that

(d\phi\wedge Jd\phi,\omega)=|\nabla\phi|^{2}_{g_{J}}.

The reason

is a nonnegative multiple of
\omega^{n}
is that

is a positive

,
which is what we have to prove.
But using equations in Lemma

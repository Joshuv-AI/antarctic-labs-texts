Introduction

Given a prime
p\geq 2
, a nonzero element
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
is called a
quadratic residue
, equivalently, a square modulo
p
whenever the quadratic congruence
x^{2}-u\equiv 0\tmod p
is solvable. Otherwise, it called a
quadratic nonresidue
. A finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
contains
(p+1)/2
squares
\mathcal{R}=\{u^{2}\tmod p:0\leq u<p/2\}
, including zero. The quadratic residues are uniformly distributed over the interval
[1,p-1]
. Likewise, the quadratic nonresidues are uniformly distributed over the same interval.

Let
k\geq 1
be a small integer. This note is concerned with the longest runs of consecutive quadratic residues and consecutive quadratic nonresidues (or any pattern)

u,\quad u+1,\quad u+2,\quad\ldots,\quad u+k-1,

(1)

in the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and large subsets
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
N(k,p)
be a tally of the number of sequences
1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a large prime, and let
k=O\left(\log p\right)
be an integer. Then, the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
contains
k
consecutive quadratic residues
(
or quadratic nonresidues or any pattern
)
. Furthermore, the number of
k
tuples has the asymptotic formulas

(i)

\displaystyle N(k,p)=\frac{p}{2^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right),

if
k\geq 1.

(ii)

\displaystyle N(k,p)=\frac{p}{2^{k}}+O\left(k^{2}\right),

if
k\geq 1.

The first expression is suitable for applications requiring any integer
k
, and the second expression is suitable for applications requiring small integers
k
. The proofs are assembled in Section
8
. The main results are proved using a new counting technique, based on Lemma
5.2
. It provides sharper error terms than the standard technique based on Lemma
5.1
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
p\geq 2
be a large prime, and let

(ii)

\displaystyle N(k,p,\mathcal{A})=\frac{\#\mathcal{A}}{2^{k}}+O\left(k\right),

if

Quadratic Symbol

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a prime, and let
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The
quadratic symbol
modulo
p
of a nonzero element
u
is defined by

\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }u\text{ is a quadratic residue};\\
-1&\text{ if }u\text{ is not a quadratic residue}.\\
\end{cases}

(5)

In term of calculations, this can be determined via the Euler criterion

\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)=u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv\pm 1\tmod p.

(6)

Lemma 2.1
.

(Legendre)
Let
p\geq 2
and
q
be a pair of distinct primes. Then, the quadratic symbol satisfies the following properties.

(i)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)\equiv a^{(p-1)/2}\tmod p,

Euler congruence equation.

(ii)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{ab}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)\left(\frac{b}{p}\right),

completely multiplicative function.

(iii)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{(p-1)/2},

evaluation at
a=-1
.

(iv)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{(p^{2}-1)/8},

evaluation at
a=2
.

(v)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=\left(-1\right)^{(p-1)(q-1)/4},

quadratic reciprocity.

Some Quadratic Exponential Sums

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a prime. The finite Fourier transform of a periodic function
f:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
of period
p
is defined by

\hat{f}(s)=\frac{1}{\eta_{p}\sqrt{p}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}f(t)e^{i2\pi st/p},

(7)

where
\eta_{p}=1
if
p\equiv 1\tmod 4
or
\eta_{p}=i
if
p\equiv 3\tmod 4
.

Except for a normalizing factor, the standard Gauss sum is a finite Fourier transform of the nonprincipal character
\chi:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, namely,

\tau_{s}(\chi)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi(t)e^{i2\pi ts/p}.

(8)

Lemma 3.1
.

The quadratic character mod
p
is the unique fix point of the finite Fourier transform. Specifically

\left(\frac{s}{p}\right)=\frac{1}{\eta_{p}\sqrt{p}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{t}{p}\right)e^{i2\pi st/p},

(9)

where
\eta_{p}=1
if
p\equiv 1\tmod 4
or
\eta_{p}=i
if
p\equiv 3\tmod 4
.

i\sqrt{p}\text{ if }p\equiv 3\tmod p.\\
\end{cases}

(10)

5
Characteristic Functions For Quadratic Residues

The standard characteristic function of quadratic residues and quadratic nonresidues are induced by the quadratic symbol.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a prime, and let
(x\,|\,p)
be the quadratic character mod
p
. If
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a nonzero element, then,

(i)

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}\left(u\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv 1\tmod p,\\
0&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\tmod p.\\
\end{array}\right.

(ii)

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{2}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\tmod p,\\
0&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv 1\tmod p,\\
\end{array}\right.

are the characteristic functions for quadratic residues and quadratic non residues modulo
p
respectively in the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

A new representation of the characteristic function for quadratic residues and quadratic nonresidues are introduced below.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a prime, and let
\tau
be a primitive root mod
p
. If
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a nonzero element, then,

(i)

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(u)=\sum_{0\leq n<(p-1)/2}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{0\leq m\leq p-1}e^{i2\pi\left(\tau^{2n}-u\right)m/p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv 1\tmod p,\\
0&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\tmod p.\\
\end{array}\right.

(ii)

\displaystyle\overline{\Psi}_{2}(u)=\sum_{0\leq n<(p-1)/2}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{0\leq m\leq p-1}e^{i2\pi\left(\tau^{2n+1}-u\right)m/p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\tmod p,\\
0&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv 1\tmod p.\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

(i) For a fixed
u\neq 0
, the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
equation

\tau^{2n}-u=0

(19)

has a unique solution
n=n_{0}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots(p-1)/2-1\}
if and only if
0\neq u=\tau^{2n_{0}}
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
. This, in turns, implies
that the inner exponential sum collapses to
p
. Specifically,

\sum_{0\leq m\leq p-1}e^{i2\pi\left(\tau^{2n}-u\right)m/p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv 1\tmod p,\\
0&\text{ if }u^{(p-1)/2}\equiv-1\tmod p.\\
\end{array}\right.

(20)

Otherwise,
\tau^{2n}-u\neq 0
, which implies that the inner exponential sum vanishes.
∎

6
Estimate For The Sum
T(k,p)

The calculations for the sum

T(k,p)=\sum_{0\neq n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\prod_{0\leq i\leq k-1}\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{0\leq n_{i}<(p-1)/2}1\right)

(21)

assumes the existence of a sequence of
k
consecutive quadratic residues

u,\quad u+1,\quad u+2,\quad\ldots,\quad u+k-1

(22)

in the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. However, it is valid for any pattern of quadratic residues and nonresidues.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a large prime, let
k=O\left(\log p\right)
be an integer, then,

(i)

\displaystyle T(k,p)=\frac{p-1}{2^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k},

if
k\geq 1.

(ii)

\displaystyle T(k,p)=\frac{p}{2^{k}}+O\left(k\right),

if
k\geq 1.

Proof.

(i) Routine calculations return

\displaystyle T(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\prod_{0\leq i\leq k-1}\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{0\leq n_{i}<(p-1)/2}1\right)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{p}\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}.

(ii) For small integer
k=O\left(\log p\right)
, the binomial series leads to

\displaystyle T(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2^{k}}\left(1-\binom{k}{1}\frac{1}{p}+\binom{k}{2}\frac{1}{p^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{p^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2^{k}}\left(1+k\binom{k}{k/2}\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2^{k}}+O\left(k\right),

since the central binomial coefficient
\binom{k}{k/2}\leq 2^{k}
.
∎

\displaystyle U_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n_{1}<(p-1)/2\\
0<m_{1}\leq p-1\end{subarray}}e^{i2\pi\left((\tau^{2n_{1}}-u-a_{1})m_{1}\right)}\right)

(36)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\cdots\times\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n_{1}<(p-1)/2\\
0<m_{1}\leq p-1\end{subarray}}e^{i2\pi\left((\tau^{2n_{k-1}}-u-a_{k-1})m_{k-1}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p+1}{2p}\times\cdots\times\frac{p+1}{2p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p+1}{2p}\right)^{k-1}.

∎

Consecutive Quadratic Residues And Nonresidues

Consecutive quadratic nonresidues is one of the simplest configuration of a subset of two or more consecutive quadratic nonresidues. The earliest attempts are surveyed in
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
, et alii. A more general result was proved by Carlitz
[
10
, Theorem 3]
using a counting technique based on Lemma
5.1
. More precisely, the number of
k
consecutive quadratic residue symbols or any pattern of quadratic residue and quadratic nonresidue symbols

\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)=\varepsilon_{0},\quad\left(\frac{u+1}{p}\right)=\epsilon_{1},\quad\cdots,\quad\left(\frac{u+k-1}{p}\right)=\epsilon_{k-1},

(37)

where
\epsilon_{n}=\pm 1
, in the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
has the asymptotic formula

\displaystyle N(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{0\leq u\leq p-1}\left(1+\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)\epsilon_{0}\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{u+1}{p}\right)\epsilon_{1}\right)\cdots\left(1+\left(\frac{u+k-1}{p}\right)\epsilon_{k-1}\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2^{k}}+E(k,p),

see Lemma
5.1
for details on the the characteristic functions. An explicit error term
E(k,p)=\pm(k+1)(3+\sqrt{p})
is proved in

[
25
, Corollary 5]
. A slightly different proof appears in a new survey
[
20
, Theorem 5.6]
. For
k\leq 2
, the exponential sums involved
have exact evaluations, and there are no error terms. But, in general, for
k\geq 3
, with very few exceptions, the exponential sums are estimated, and have
error terms of the forms
E(k,p)=O(k\sqrt{p})
. A new and sharper proof and counting technique based on Lemma
5.2
is given here.

Let
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k-1}
be a sequence of distinct and increasing integers. Let
p\geq 2
be a large prime, and let
\tau\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a primitive root. A pattern of
k
consecutive quadratic residues and quadratic nonresidues
u+a_{0},u+a_{1},u+a_{2},\ldots,u+a_{k-1}
exists if and only if the system of equations

\tau^{2n_{0}}=u+a_{0},\quad\tau^{2n_{1}}=u+a_{1},\quad\tau^{2n_{2}}=u+a_{2},\quad\ldots,\quad\tau^{2n_{k-1}}=u+a_{k-1},

(39)

has one or more solutions. A solution consists of a
k
-tuple
n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{k-1}
of integers such that
0\leq n_{i}<(p-1)/2
for
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, and some
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let

N(k,p)=\#\left\{u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:\ord_{p}(u+a_{i})=2\right\}

(40)

for
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
, denotes the number of solutions.

Proof.

(Theorem
1.1
): The total number of solutions is written in terms of characteristic function for quadratic residues, see Lemma
5.2
, as

\displaystyle N(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Psi_{2}\left(u+a_{0}\right)\Psi_{2}\left(u+a_{1}\right)\cdots\Psi_{2}\left(u+a_{k-1}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\prod_{0\leq i\leq k-1}\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n_{i}\leq(p-1)/2\\
0\leq m_{i}\leq p-1\end{subarray}}\psi\left((\tau^{2n_{i}}-u-a_{i})m_{i})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(k,p)\quad+\quad U(k,p).

The term
T(k,p)
, which is determined by the indices
m_{0}=m_{1}=\cdots=m_{k-1}=0
, has the form

T(k,p)=\sum_{0\neq u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\prod_{0\leq i\leq k-1}\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{0\leq n_{i}<(p-1)/2}1\right),

(42)

and the term
U(k,p)
, which is determined by the indices
m_{0}\neq 0,m_{1}\neq 0,\ldots,m_{k-1}\neq 0
, has the form

U(k,p)=\sum_{0\neq u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\prod_{0\leq i\leq k-1}\left(\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n_{i}\leq(p-1)/2\\
1\leq m_{i}\leq p-1\end{subarray}}\psi\left((\tau^{2n_{i}}-u-a_{i})m_{i})\right)\right).

(43)

(i) Applying Lemma
6.1
-i to the term
T(k,p)
, and Lemma
7.1
-i to the term
U(k,p)
, yield

\displaystyle N(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(k,p)\quad+\quad U(k,p)

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{2^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}+O\left(\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}\;\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

for all sufficiently large primes
p\geq 2
.

(ii) Applying Lemma
6.1
-ii to the term
T(k,p)
, and Lemma
7.1
to the term
U(k,p)
, yield

\displaystyle N(k,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(k,p)\quad+\quad U(k,p)

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2^{k}}+O\left(k\right)+O\left(\frac{k}{2^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2^{k}}+O\left(k\right)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

for all sufficiently large primes
p\geq 2
.
∎

10
The Least Quadratic Nonresidue

Two slightly different heuristics for the conjectured upper bound of the least quadratic nonresidue are given in
[
24
]
and
[
30
]
. These are summarized below.

Conjecture 10.1
.

For every large prime
p\geq 3
,

n_{p}\ll(\log p)(\log\log p).

(46)

The heuristic is based on the proportion of primes
p
such that the
n
th prime
p_{n}
is the least quadratic nonresidue modulo
p
. The proportion has a geometric distribution, and its proof is based on quadratic reciprocity and Dirichlet theorem for primes in arithmetic progressions. For example, the probability for each
n\geq 1
is given by limit

P(n_{p}=p_{n})=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\#\{p\leq x:n_{p}=p_{n}\}}{\pi(x)}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

(47)

Note that the form of the main term in Theorem
1.1
-ii implies that quadratic residues (or quadratic non residues) in a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are independent or nearly independent random variables
X=X(p)
with probability

P\left(\ord_{p}\left(X\right)=2\right)=\frac{1}{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{\varepsilon}}\right),

(48)

where
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary small number.

On average, the expected value
\overline{n}
of the least quadratic residue
n_{p}
is quite small

\overline{n}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{2<p\leq x}n_{p}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{p_{n}}{2^{n}}=3.67464\ldots,

(49)

where
\pi(x)=\{p\leq x\}
, and
p_{n}
is the
n
th prime, see
[
6
, p. 253]
, and
[
19
]
for the generalized concept.

Proof.

(Theorem
1.3
) To obtain a reductio ad absurdum, suppose that there exists a prime
p\geq 2
for which
n_{p}>(\log p)(\log\log p)
. Let
k=(\log p)(\log\log p)
. This implies that the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
contains a sequence of
k
consecutive quadratic residues

u,\quad u+1,\quad u+2,\quad\ldots,\quad u+k-1.

(50)

This immediately implies that

\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)=1,\quad\left(\frac{u+1}{p}\right)=1,\quad\cdots,\quad\left(\frac{u+k-1}{p}\right)=1.

(51)

By Theorem
1.1
, the total number of such sequences of quadratic residues of length
k
is

N(k,p)=\frac{p}{2^{k}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\gg 1.

(52)

Taking logarithm, and simplifying return

\displaystyle\log p-k\log 2+k\log\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\log\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)\gg 0.

(53)

Rearranging it, and replacing
k=(\log p)(\log\log p)
give

\displaystyle\log p

\displaystyle\gg

\displaystyle k\log 2+O\left(\frac{k}{p}\right)-\log\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)

(54)

\displaystyle\gg

\displaystyle(\log p)(\log\log p)\log 2+O\left(\frac{(\log p)(\log\log p)}{p}\right)+\log C_{0},

where
C_{0}\approx 1
. Clearly, this is false. Hence, a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
contains a quadratic nonresidues
n_{p}\ll(\log p)(\log\log p)
.
∎

The best upper bound for the parameter
H\geq 0
for which a character
\chi(n)
modulo
p
is constant on the interval
[N,N+H]
is
H<7.07p^{1/4}\log p
for large primes, see
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
, and
[
31
]
. The above result in Theorem
1.3
provides an improved and effective upper bound
H\ll(\log p)(\log\log p)
for the parameter
H\geq 0
. While the result
N(k,p)=p/2^{k}+O(k\sqrt{p})

in
[
25
, Corollary 5]
provides an effective lower bound
H\gg\log p
.

Corollary 10.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be a large prime, let
\chi
be a nonprincipal character modulo
p
, and let
x\geq 1
be a large real number. Define the real value function

f(x)=\sum_{0\leq n\leq x}\chi(n).

(55)

Then,
f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

\displaystyle f(x)=f(x+p)
,

is periodic of period
p
.

(ii)

\displaystyle f(x)\ll p^{1/2}\log p
,

is of absolute bounded variation on the real line.

(iii)

\displaystyle f(N)<f(N+1)<\cdots<f(N+H)
,

is monotonically increasing on a short interval
[N,N+H]
if and only if

H\ll(\log p)(\log\log p)
, for any
N\geq 0
.

(iv)

\displaystyle f(N)>f(N+1)>\cdots>f(N+H)
,

is monotonically decreasing on a short interval
[N,N+H]
if and only if

H\ll(\log p)(\log\log p)
, for any
N\geq 0
.

11
Recursive Algorithm

Let
p
be a prime, let
n_{p}
denotes the least quadratic nonresidue modulo
p
. The Burgess upper bound of the least quadratic nonresidue claims that

n_{p}\leq c_{0}p^{\frac{1}{4\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon},

(56)

where
c_{0}>0
, is a constant, and
\varepsilon>0
is a small number, see
[
4
]
for a survey and discussion. A recursive technique based on the Vinogradov trick for generating sharper upper bounds is introduced here. It will be demonstrated that a few iterations of the algorithm leads to the new upper bound

n_{p}\leq c_{3}p^{\frac{1}{4e\sqrt{e}}+6\varepsilon},

(57)

where
c_{3}>0
is a constant. The numerical values of the exponents of the upper bounds (
56
) and (
57
) are

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{e}}=0.151632664928158\ldots,

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4e\sqrt{e}}=0.0557825400371075\ldots.

For sufficiently large prime
p
, and very small
\varepsilon>0
, the last
2
iterations amounts to a power saving by a factor of

p^{\frac{1}{4\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{4e\sqrt{e}}-6\varepsilon}=p^{0.0958501248910509-5\varepsilon}.

(58)

Theorem 11.1
.

(Vinogradov trick)
Let
p
be a prime, let
x<p
, and let
\chi\neq 1
be the quadratic character modulo
p
. If
\varepsilon>0
is a small number, and

\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)=o(x),

(59)

then there exists
n\leq x^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon}
such that
\chi(n)=-1
.

A recent proof appears in
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
, and the earliest proof in
[
33
]
. The generalization to arbitrary characters
\chi\neq 1
modulo
p
, and different approaches to the proofs are also available in the literature. Here, this result is turned into a recursive algorithm.

Theorem 11.2
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
n_{p}
denotes the least quadratic nonresidue modulo
p
. If
\varepsilon>0
is a small number, and let
\chi\neq 1
be the quadratic character modulo
p
, then

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq p}\chi(n)\leq c_{2}p^{1/4e\sqrt{e}+6\varepsilon},

(ii)

\displaystyle n_{p}\leq c_{2}p^{\frac{1}{4e\sqrt{e}}+6\varepsilon},

where
c_{2}>0
is a constant.

Proof.

The upper bound is generated by a few iterations of the ”recursive” Vinogradov trick given below.

Iteration 1.
Let
X_{0}=c_{0}p^{1/4+2\varepsilon}
. The standard Burgess exponential sum inequality is

\sum_{n\leq X_{0}}\chi(n)\leq c_{0}p^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}=o(X_{0}),

(60)

where
c_{0}>0
is a constant, see
[
4
]
,
[
20
]
, et alii, detailed discussions. The first iteration of the Vinogradov trick yields

\displaystyle n_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle X_{0}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon}

(61)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(c_{0}p^{\frac{1}{4}+2\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}p^{\frac{1}{4\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon_{1}},

where
2(1/\sqrt{e}+\varepsilon)\varepsilon+\varepsilon/4=\varepsilon_{1}\leq 3\varepsilon
, and
c_{1}>0
is a constant. This is the standard unconditional upper bound for quadratic nonresidues in (
56
). This iteration is well known in the number theory literature.

Iteration 2.
Let
X_{1}=c_{1}p^{1/4\sqrt{e}+4\varepsilon}
. The corresponding exponential sum inequality is

\sum_{n\leq X_{1}}\chi(n)\leq c_{1}p^{\frac{1}{4\sqrt{e}}+3\varepsilon}=o(X_{1}).

(62)

The second iteration of the Vinogradov trick yields

\displaystyle n_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle X_{1}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon}

(63)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(c_{1}p^{\frac{1}{4\sqrt{e}}+4\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}p^{\frac{1}{4e}+\varepsilon_{2}},

where
4(1/\sqrt{e}+\varepsilon)\varepsilon+\varepsilon/4\sqrt{e}=\varepsilon_{2}\leq 4\varepsilon
, and
c_{2}>0
is a constant.

Iteration 3.
Let
X_{2}=c_{2}p^{1/4e+5\varepsilon}
. The corresponding exponential sum inequality is

\sum_{n\leq X_{2}}\chi(n)\leq c_{2}p^{\frac{1}{4e}+4\varepsilon}=o(X_{2}).

(64)

The third iteration of the Vinogradov trick yields

\displaystyle n_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle X_{2}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon}

(65)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(c_{2}p^{\frac{1}{4e}+5\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{3}p^{\frac{1}{4e\sqrt{e}}+\varepsilon_{3}},

where
5(1/\sqrt{e}+\varepsilon)\varepsilon+\varepsilon/4e=\varepsilon_{3}\leq 6\varepsilon
, and
c_{3}>0
is a constant.
∎

Computational Complexity Of Quadratic Residues And Square Roots

The identification of an element
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
as a quadratic residue (or quadratic nonresidue) has nearly linear deterministic time complexity
O\left(\log p)(\log\log p)^{c}\right)
for some
c>0
. The discovery of an algorithm of linear complexity is an open problem, see
[
6
, p. 3]
for details.

The determination of the roots of congruence
x^{2}-u\equiv 0\tmod p
has slightly higher time complexity depending on the following data.

(1)

The residue class of the prime
p\geq 3
.

(2)

The method used to compute a quadratic nonresidue: probabilistic, deterministic.

(3)

The method used to compute the roots: probabilistic, deterministic.

The worst case has deterministic time complexity
O\left(\log p)^{4}\right)
bit operations. Many algorithms such as Cipolla algorithm, tonelli algorithm, Berlekamp algorithm etc, are explained in
[
6
, p. 157]
,
[
11
, p. 102]
,
[
9
]
. Some polynomials for computing the square roots are provided in
[
8
]
.

Twin Quadratic Residues And Nonresidues

The precise numbers of pairs
QQ
,
QN
,
NQ
or
NN
of quadratic residues and quadratic nonresidues are proved in
[
7
, Theorem 6.3.1]
. The proof based on Lemma
5.2
is given below.

Theorem 14.1
.

Let
p\geq 3
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\gcd(a,p)=1
. The number of pairs
n
and
n+a
such that
(n|p)=\epsilon_{0}
and
(n|p)=\epsilon_{1}
is exactly

N(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},P)=\frac{1}{4}\left(p-2-\epsilon_{0}\left(\frac{a}{p}\right)-\epsilon_{1}\left(\frac{-a}{p}\right)-\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{1}\right),

(66)

where
\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1
.

Proof.

Sum the product of the two characteristic functions over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
:

\displaystyle N(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},p)

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
n\neq 0,n\neq-a\end{subarray}}\left(1+\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)\epsilon_{0}\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{n+a}{p}\right)\epsilon_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(p-2+\epsilon_{0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
n\neq 0,n\neq-a\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)+\epsilon_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
n\neq 0,n\neq-a\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{n+a}{p}\right)+\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
n\neq 0,n\neq-a\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{n^{2}+an}{p}\right)\right).

Use Lemma
3.4
to evaluate

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\\
n\neq 0,n\neq-a\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{n^{2}+an}{p}\right)=-1

(68)

and simplify the expression.
∎

A new proof based on Lemma
5.2
yields the same result up to a small error term. In particular, at
k=2
, Theorem
1.1
reduces to

N(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},P)=N(2,p)=\frac{p}{2^{k}}+O\left(k\right)=\frac{p}{4}+O\left(1\right).

(69)

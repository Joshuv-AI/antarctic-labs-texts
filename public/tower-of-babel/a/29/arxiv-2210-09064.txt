Every diffeomorphism is a total renormalization of a close to identity map
Pierre Berger, Nicolaz Gourmelon, Mathieu Helfter

Abstract
For any 1 ≤ r ≤ ∞ 1\leq r\leq\infty , we show that every diffeomorphism of a manifold of the form ℝ / ℤ × M \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times M is a total renormalization of a C r C^{r} -close to identity map. In other words, for every diffeomorphism f f of ℝ / ℤ × M \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times M , there exists a map g g arbitrarily close to identity such that the first return map of g g to a domain is conjugate to f f and moreover the orbit of this domain is equal to ℝ / ℤ × M \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times M . This enables us to localize near the identity the existence of many properties in dynamical systems, such as being Bernoulli for a smooth volume form.



Introduction

0.1
Statements of the main theorems

Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}
be the unit closed ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 0.1

(Primitive renormalization)
.

A
primitive renormalization

G
of a diffeomorphism
g\in\mathrm{Diff}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is a rescaling of an iteration of
g
. In other words, there exists
N\geq 2
and an embedding
\psi:\mathbb{B}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}^{n}
such that
g^{i}(\psi(\mathbb{B}^{n}))\cap\psi(\mathbb{B}^{n})=\emptyset
for every
0<i<N
and:

G=\psi^{-1}\circ g^{N}\circ\psi.

A long standing open problem of dynamical systems theory is:

Problem 0.2

(1971)
.

Which dynamics can be reached by renormalization of close to identity maps?

This problem was first studied by Ruelle and Takens in
[
RT71
]
. Motivated by the study of turbulence, they proved that for any integer
n\geq 2
, any dynamics on the
n
-dimensional torus is the renormalization of a
C^{n}
-close to identity map. This enabled them to construct perturbations of the identity map of the torus with a strange attractor. Based on this, they conjectured that this appears as well in fluid dynamics and could be used as a mathematical definition of the notion of turbulence
[
Lor63
]
.

The main mathematical issue with this result is that the regularity is limited by the dimension of the torus. However when considering flows, this problem was solved by Newhouse, Ruelle and Takens in
[
NRT78
]
: given any vector filed
X
equal to a rotation on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
and any map
F_{0}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1})
homotopic to the identity,
they perturbed
X
to
\widetilde{X}
so that
its first return map to a global transverse section is
F_{0}
.
Yet
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is “much larger” than
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n-1})
and so the mathematical
0.2
remains unsolved.

A breakthrough was then performed in the seminal work of Turaev who proved that a
C^{r}
-
dense subset
of
C^{r}
-orientation preserving embeddings of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
could be obtained after renormalization of an arbitrarily close to identity map, for every
0\leq r\leq\infty
.

A first main result is a solution to
0.2
, where we improve Turaev’s theorem to obtain, via a self-contained and new proof,
any

C^{r}
-orientation preserving map of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(instead of maps among a dense subset):

Theorem A
.

For any
1\leq r\leq\infty
and any orientation preserving
G\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, in any neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
of the identity, there exists
g\in{\mathcal{N}}
such that a primitive renormalization of
g
is
equal
to
G
. Moreover the rescaling map of this renormalization can be chosen affine.

A natural open problem is whether
g
can be obtained conservative or symplectic when
G
is conservative or symplectic. In this direction let us mention the work of Gonchenko-Shilnikov-Turaev
[
GST07
]
who proved that, for every
0\leq r\leq\infty
, a
C^{r}
-dense subset of volume preserving embeddings of
\mathbb{B}^{2}

could be obtained after renormalization of an arbitrarily close to identity volume preserving map.
Recently Fayad and Saprykina in
[
FS22
]
showed that any conservative map of the
n
-dimensional ball can be realized by renormalized iteration of a conservative
C^{n}
-perturbation of the identity.

If all these theorems indicate the richness of the possible dynamical behaviors near the identity, one can object the following. In the setting of
Definition

0.1
, the orbit of
\bigcup_{k=0}^{N-1}\psi(\mathbb{B}^{n})
of the renormalization domain might be extremely small and so experimentally not observable. This objection is lifted completely when the renormalization domain intersects every orbit. This leads us to generalize the notion of renormalization by the following:

Definition 0.3

(Renormalization)
.

Let
r\in\{1,\dots,\infty\}\cup\{\omega\}
and let
V
be a manifold (with boundary). A map
g\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)
is
renormalizable
if there exists a strict submanifold with corners
\Delta\subsetneq V
such that:

•

there exists a bijective, local
C^{r}
-diffeomorphism
H:\Delta\to V
, called the
rescaling map
of the
renormalization domain

\Delta
,

•

the first return time
\tau:\Delta\to\mathbb{N}^{*}
into
\Delta
by
g
is bounded and the
renormalization

G=H\circ g^{\tau}\circ H^{-1}
belongs to
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)
.

The map
g
is
totally renormalizable
if the forward orbit of
\Delta
covers
V
, i.e.
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}g^{n}(\Delta)=V
. The map
G
is then a
total renormalization
of
g
.

Remark 0.4
.

Note that if
g\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
displays a primitive renormalization with embedding
\psi:\mathbb{B}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}^{n}
and time
N
, then

\Delta:=\psi(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is renormalization domain of
g
with constant return time
\tau\equiv N
and rescaling map
H=\psi^{-1}
. Hence
Definition

0.3
generalizes

Definition

0.1
. Note that the latter renormalization is never total.

Moreover
Definition

0.3
allows to consider a larger class of manifolds
V
as we do not ask
H=\psi^{-1}
to be continuous on the boundary of the renormalization domain. The next example is about a total renormalization on the circle; a renormalization which is not primitive.

Example 0.5
.

When
V
is the circle
\mathbb{T}
, a diffeomorphism
g
is totally renormalizable iff it does not fix a point. Indeed in this case, take any point
0\in\mathbb{T}
and consider the interval
\Delta=[0,g(0))
. Then we glue the two endpoints of
\Delta
using
g
to obtain a circle and we uniformize it to obtain
\mathbb{T}
. This defines a map
H
. For this setting one easily shows that the mapping
g
is renormalizable. This construction was intensively used by Yoccoz
[
Yoc95b
]
.

Let
V
be a compact manifold (possibly with corners) and
1\leq r\leq\infty
. We recall that the
support

\mathrm{supp}\>f
of
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)
is the closure of the set of points such that
f(x)\neq x
.

Definition 0.6
.

Let
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{0}(V)
be the component of the identity in
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)
. Let
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V)
be the subset of
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{0}(V)
formed by maps isotopic to
\mathrm{id}
through isotopies
(f_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
whose support
\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathrm{supp}\>f_{t}
is a compact subset of
V\setminus\partial V
.

Observe that when
V
is boundaryless, it holds
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{0}(V)=\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V)
. A natural question is:

Question 0.7
.

For which manifold
V
, any map
F\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V)
is a
total
renormalization of a close to identity map?

So far no example of such a manifold
V
was known. In this work we
give a full class of examples:

Theorem B
.

Let
0\leq r\leq\infty
, let
M
be a compact manifold of dimension
\geq 1
and put
{V:=\mathbb{T}\times M}
. Let
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)
be a neighborhood of the identity. Then any
G\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V)
is a total renormalization of some
g\in{\mathcal{N}}
.

If
A
implies that every
local
dynamical phenomenon can be found near the identity,
B
implies that every
global
dynamical phenomenon can be found near the identity. A new improvement brought by the latter result is that the renormalization domain is larger than in all of the previous extensions of Ruelle-Takens theorems: its orbit coincides with the whole domain of the dynamics. In
E
, we will give a precise formula defining the renormalization domain and rescaling map involved in
B
. This will enable new applications such as the proof of existence of maps preserving smooth SRB near the identity (see
Corollary

C
) or universal maps whose
renormalization domains decrease as slow as we want (see
Corollary

D
).

In
Proposition

0.10
, we show that
B
is wrong when
\mathbb{T}\times M\approx\mathbb{T}
, hence the set of dimensions of the manifold is optimal. On the other hand, a natural open problem communicated to us by Turaev is:

Problem 0.8
.

Show that a dense subset of
\mathrm{Diff}^{\omega}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is equal to the renormalization of a close to identity map in
\mathrm{Diff}^{\omega}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
?

Another natural question is:

Question 0.9
.

Is
B
correct in the area preserving or symplectic categories?

An extension of
B
regards the
C^{r}
-families
f_{\mathscr{P}}=(f_{p})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}
of maps
f_{p}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)
and indexed by a manifold
{\mathscr{P}}
. A family
(f_{p})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}
is
of class
C^{r}
if the following is in
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})
:

(0.1)

\widehat{f_{\mathscr{P}}}:=(x,p)\mapsto(f_{p}(x),p)\;.

We denote by
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}
the space of such families endowed with the topology induced by

\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})
. Let
\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{r}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}
be the component of the identity in
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}
for homotopies
(f_{p,t})_{(p,t)\in{\mathscr{P}}\times[0,1]}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)_{{\mathscr{P}}\times[0,1]}
whose support
\bigcup_{(t,p)\in[0,1]\times{\mathscr{P}}}\mathrm{supp}\>f_{p,t}\times\{p\}
is a compact subset of the interior of
V\times{\mathscr{P}}
. Observe that
\widehat{f_{\mathscr{P}}}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})
.

Theorem B
′
.

Let
0\leq r\leq\infty
, let
M
and
{\mathscr{P}}
be compact manifolds of dim
\geq 1
and set
{V:=\mathbb{T}\times M}
. Let
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}
be a neighborhood of
(id)_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}
.

Then for any
{(G_{p})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}}
, there exist

(g_{p})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}\in{\mathcal{N}}
and a rescaling map (independent of
p\in{\mathscr{P}}
)
of a total renormalization domain which renormalizes each
g_{p}
to
G_{p}
.

This theorem implies that any bifurcation in
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}_{c}(V)
occurs at small unfolding of the identity.
In
Section

1.1
, we will state the main general
E
which implies
Theorems
A
and
B
and also its parametric counterpart
E′
which implies
B′
. In
Section

0.2
we will give several applications of them.
Now let us discuss the optimality of
B
.

Proposition 0.10
.

When
r\geq 2
,
B
is wrong if
V\approx\mathbb{T}
, i.e. when it is isomorphic to the circle as a smooth manifold (and so
\dim M=0
).

Proof.

Indeed if
F
is a renormalization of a close to identity map
f
for a renormalization domain
\Delta
, then we have necessarily
|\tau(\theta)-\tau(\theta^{\prime})|\leq 1
for any
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Delta
. Let
N:=\min\{\tau(\theta),\tau(\theta^{\prime})\}
. We compute the derivative of the
N-1
first iterates
(\theta_{i})_{i}
and
(\theta^{\prime}_{i})_{i}
of
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
:

\log\left|\frac{D_{\theta}F}{D_{\theta^{\prime}}F}\right|=\log\left|\frac{D_{\theta}f^{N}}{D_{\theta^{\prime}}f^{N}}\right|+o(1)\quad\text{when }f\to id

=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\log\left|\frac{D_{\theta_{i}}f}{D_{\theta^{\prime}_{i}}f}\right|+o(1)\leq\|\log|Df|\|_{C^{1}}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}|\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{\prime}|+o(1)=o(1),

where the latter inequality uses that
\|\log|Df|\|_{C^{1}}
is small while the segments
[\theta_{i},\theta_{i}^{\prime}]
are disjoint and so the union of their length is at most
1
. Hence this proves that the derivative of
F
is constant and so that
F
must be a rotation.
∎

Also we cannot change
\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{r}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
by
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{r}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
in
B
. Indeed the latter proposition applied to the boundary of
[0,1]
implies immediately:

Corollary 0.11
.

There are
G\in\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
which are not total renormalization of
C^{2}
-close to identity map.

Yet in view of
0.7
,
B
seems to be generalizable for a manifold
V
on which
\mathbb{T}
acts properly discontinuously without any fixed point.

Applications and open problems

Smooth SRB near the identity

In
Section

we will state

]
, an answer to an open question of Thouvenot:

Corollary C
.

In any neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}
of
\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})

Universal mappings

A map
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)
is said to be
universal
if there exists a dense subset of
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{r}(V)
such that each of its elements is a renormalization of
f
. Bonatti and Díaz in
[
BD03
]
have shown that universal maps are locally
C^{1}
-generic on
\mathbb{B}^{3}
. Turaev in
[
Tur15
]
has shown that
universal maps are locally
C^{\infty}
-generic on
\mathbb{B}^{2}
. Yet mathematicians wondered whether we can “see” the universality of such mapping. While there are infinitely many renormalization domains, the above proofs lead to a very small volume for the union of their orbits.

Corollary D
.

Let
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
n\geq 2
. For any sequence
(F_{i})_{i\geq 0}
of maps
F_{i}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{r}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
and any sequence of positive numbers
(\ell_{i})_{i\geq 0}
s.t.
\sum\ell_{i}<1
, there exists a
C^{r}
-arbitrarily close to identity map
f\in\mathrm{Diff}_{0}^{r}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])
which displays a family of renormalization domains
(\Delta_{i})_{i\geq 0}
such that:

1.

a renormalization of
f
associated to
\Delta_{i}
is
F_{i}
for every
i\geq 0
,

2.

the orbit
\tilde{\Delta}_{i}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}f^{n}(\Delta_{i})
has volume equal to
\ell_{i}
,

3.

the sets
\tilde{\Delta}_{i}
and
\tilde{\Delta}_{j}
are disjoint for
i\neq j
.

This corollary will be proved in
Section

1.2
.

The proof of the main theorem is constructive and it seems to us that, in the case where
M
is boundaryless, the map
g
of
B
depends smoothly on
G
in a neighborhood of the identity. This leads us to propose:

Conjecture 0.12
.

For every compact boundaryless manifold
M
of dimension
\geq 1
, there exists a neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
of
id\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
such that for every neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}
of
{id\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)}
, there is a smooth (tame) injective map
{\cal I}:G\in{\mathcal{N}}_{0}\mapsto g\in{\mathcal{N}}
such that
G
is a total renormalization of
g={{\mathcal{I}}}(G)
for every
G\in{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
.

Roughly speaking, this conjecture asserts that modulo total renormalization, a fixed neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
of
id\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
can be smoothly embedded into any smaller neighborhood. This defines infinitely many inverse branches of the renormalization operator with image converging to the identity.

0.3
Sketch of proof

Plugins and pluggable dynamics:

The framework of the proof of the main theorem relies on a new object called
plugin
and the notion of
pluggable map
. A plugin is a renormalizable map of a special form, so that it has a canonical renormalization called its
output
. See Def.
1.1
and
1.3
below and
Figs.

1
and
2
. We will say that a map is
pluggable
if it is the output of an arbitrarily close to identity plugin and likewise for its inverse. In particular a pluggable map is a total renormalization of a close to identity map.
Most of this work will be dedicated to show
F
stating that:

any map of
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
is pluggable.

The finite regularity counterpart of
F
is stated as
E
in
Section

1.1
and will be deduced from
F
in
Section

2.3
.
We will deduce Theorems
A
and
B
and
Corollaries

C
and
D
from
E
in
Section

1.2
.

In
Section

1.3
we precise the topologies of the involved spaces. Also we will show that the following group is formed by pluggable maps, see
Proposition

1.16
:

\mathbb{G}_{1}:=\{(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times M\mapsto(\theta+\nu(y),y)\in\mathbb{T}\times M:\nu\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M,\mathbb{T})\}\;.

Topological group structure on
\mathbb{P}
.

It is easier to work with pluggable maps rather than directly the set of total renormalizations of close to identity maps. Indeed, we will show in
Proposition

2.4
that the set
\mathbb{P}
of pluggable maps endowed with the composition rule
\circ
is a group. To prove this, we will define in
Section

2.1
a binary operation
\star
on compatible plugins
g_{1},g_{2}
such that the
output of
g_{1}\star g_{2}
is the composition of the outputs of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
. See
Fig.

5
.
In
Section

2.2
we will show that the following group is formed by pluggable maps, see
Proposition

2.7
:

\mathbb{G}_{2}:=\left\{(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times M\mapsto(\theta,F(y))\in\mathbb{T}\times M:F\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)\right\}\;.

Then in
Section

2.3
we will show that
\mathbb{P}
is closed in
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
, see
Proposition

2.9
.
To prove this, we will construct plugins whose dynamics enlarge an iterate of the renormalization domain and will perform a perturbation therein. Note that the elements of
\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
generated by compositions of elements of
\mathbb{G}_{1}
and
\mathbb{G}_{2}
have constant derivatives w.r.t.
\theta
. Consequently we will need to construct other diffeomorphisms in
\mathbb{P}
to prove
F
.
To do so we will consider the space:

\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)\text{ of compactly supported vector fields on }\mathbb{T}\times M\;,

and study the space
{\mathfrak{p}}
of vector fields whose flow is pluggable:

{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M):\mathrm{Fl}^{t}_{X}\in\mathbb{P},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}\;,

where
\mathrm{Fl}^{t}_{X}
denotes the flow of
X
at time
t
.
Using that
\mathbb{P}
is a closed subgroup, we will deduce in
Section

2.4
that
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a closed sub-Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
, see
Proposition

2.16
.

Note that the following Lie algebras are formed by fields whose flows are in
\mathbb{G}_{1}
or
\mathbb{G}_{2}
:

{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}=\{X_{1}:(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times M\mapsto(v(y),0)\in\mathbb{R}\times TM\ :\ v\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}\;,

{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}=\{X_{2}:(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times M\mapsto(0,f(y))\in\mathbb{R}\times TM\ :\ f\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)\}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}\;.

Construction of pluggable flows.

In
Section

3.1
, using the connectedness of
{\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)}
and that
\mathbb{P}
is a closed group, we will show that, to prove
F
, it suffices to show:

{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)\;.

This equality will be stated in
Proposition

3.3
. Its proof relies on two phenomena.
The first one is stated in
Proposition

3.7
as:

\{Y\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M):\exists X\in{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ such that }Y=[X,Y]\}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}\;,

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the Lie brackets.
This will be proved in
Section

3.2
by noting that for any
Y=[X,Y]
, it holds

\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{t}=\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{t\cdot e^{-s}}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{-s}
. Then by using the same technique as for the proof of the closedness of
\mathbb{P}
,
we will show that arbitrarily close to identity there exists a plugin with output
\mathrm{Fl}_{e^{-s}Y}^{t}
for any
s
large enough.
We will conclude the proof by doing two
\star
products of the latter with plugins with outputs
\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}
and
\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{-s}
.

The second phenomenon, stated in
Proposition

3.10
, is that for any vector field
T\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)
, there exist finite families
(X_{i})_{i},(Y_{i})_{i},(Z_{i})_{i}
of vector fields in
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)
such that:

T=\sum_{i}[Y_{i},Z_{i}]\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{i}=[X_{i},Y_{i}]\;.

From this we will deduce the same statement for vector fields in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
. To show
Proposition

3.10
, we will remark that when
M=\mathbb{R}
, for the vector fields
X:y\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto-y
and

, it holds

and for any
T\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, with
Z=\int_{-\infty}^{y}T(t)dt
, it also holds

. Then we will deduce a compactly supported and parametric version of this property which will enable us to prove

Proposition

3.10
in the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally, we will use a partition of unity to deduce the proposition for any manifold.

These phenomena will enable us to prove
Proposition

3.1
stating that for any
T\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
and
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
depending only on
\theta
, the field
\phi\cdot T
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Indeed, by the second phenomenon, there are
X_{i},Y_{i},Z_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}

\phi\cdot T=\sum_{i}\phi\cdot[Y_{i},Z_{i}]=\sum_{i}[\phi\cdot Y_{i},Z_{i}]\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{i}=[X_{i},Y_{i}]\;.

Also a simple computation shows that
[\phi\cdot Y_{i},X_{i}]=\phi\cdot Y_{i}
. As
X_{i}
is in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}
, we deduce by the first phenomenon that
\phi\cdot Y_{i}
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
. This gives that
\phi\cdot T\in{\mathfrak{p}}
as stated in
Proposition

3.1
.
In
Section

3.1
, we will use Fourier decomposition Theorem and the closedness of

to deduce

Proposition

3.1
stating that any vector field of the form

. Finally using this with a Lie bracket with an element of

) as stated by

Proposition 1.7

Let

\mu_{g}\mapsto\mu_{G}:=H_{\sigma*}\frac{\mu_{g}|\Delta}{\mu_{g}(\Delta)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{G}\mapsto\mu_{g}:=\sum_{N\geq 0}g^{*N}H_{\sigma}^{*}\mu_{G}|\{\tau>N\}\;.

Moreover:

1.

\mu_{g}
is ergodic iff
\mu_{G}
is ergodic,

2.

\mu_{g}
is hyperbolic iff
\mu_{G}
is hyperbolic.

3.

\mu_{g}
is a smooth volume form iff
\mu_{G}
is a smooth volume form,

conjugates

of

\mu_{g^{\tau}}\mapsto\mu_{g}:=\sum_{N\geq 0}g^{*N}\mu_{g^{\tau}}|\{\tau>N\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{g}\mapsto\mu_{g^{\tau}}:=\frac{\mu_{g}|\Delta}{\mu_{g}(\Delta)}\;.

is ergodic iff

is ergodic (see for instance

1.3.1
Topologies on spaces of smooth maps and parameter families

We endow
V
with a Riemannian metric. Let
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}(V)
be the space of smooth vector fields on
V
that are tangent to the boundary (if any).
We endow
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(V)
and
the space
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}(V)
with the following distances:

d_{C^{\infty}}(f,g)=\max_{x\in V}\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{-k}\min(1,\|D_{x}^{k}f-D^{k}_{x}g\|)

and

d_{C^{\infty}}(X,Y)=\max_{x\in V}\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{-k}\min(1,\|D_{x}^{k}X-D^{k}_{x}Y\|)\;.

For these distances
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(V)
and
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}(V)
are complete. Actually
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(V)
is a Lie group with algebra the Fréchet space
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}(V)
.
We endow the connected component
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{0}(V)
of the identity with the topology induced by
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(V)
.

On the other hand, we endow the space
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
and the space of compactly supported smooth vector fields
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
with the finer
Whitney topology
. A basis of open sets of these respective topologies is:

U_{\eta,f,m}:=\{g\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V):\|D_{x}^{k}f-D^{k}_{x}g\|\leq\eta(x),\forall k\leq m\}

and

U_{\eta,X,m}:=\{Y\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V):\|D_{x}^{k}X-D^{k}_{x}Y\|\leq\eta(x),\forall k\leq m\}\;,

among
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
,
X\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
, and continuous functions
{\eta:V\setminus\partial V\to(0,\infty)}
. A well known theorem asserts that
f_{n}\to f
in
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
if and only if there exists a compact subset
K\subset V\setminus\partial V
such that the supports of
f
and

are included in
K
for

and
for every

, we have

in the uniform
C^{r}
-topology when

\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)

We endow

and

with the topologies induced by
\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})
and
\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{r}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})
with the inclusions:

\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V)_{{\mathscr{P}}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}^{r}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{r}(V)_{{\mathscr{P}}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{r}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})

Y_{\mathscr{P}}:=(Y)_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}{\mathfrak{g}}_{1{\mathscr{P}}}
. The latter computation gives:

(3.7)

[W_{\mathscr{P}},Y_{\mathscr{P}}](\theta,y)=((f_{p}(\theta,y),-\partial_{\theta}f_{p}(\theta,y)\cdot y_{1},0,\dots,0))_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}\;.

Thus
X_{\mathscr{P}}-[W_{\mathscr{P}},Y_{\mathscr{P}}]
is in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{3{\mathscr{P}}}
.
Now for the general case we conclude by using a partition of unity as before.
∎

It allows to conclude:

Proof of
F′
.

Consider
G_{\mathscr{P}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)_{\mathscr{P}}
. As a corollary of
F
, for any
r\geq 0
, the map

can be approximated by a composition of flows of vector fields with a zero
{\mathscr{P}}
-coordinate.
Thus the family

can be approximated arbitrarily close by a composition of compactly supported families of flows of vector fields in the

-norm for any

. By

, each of the families of flows belongs to

. Since

is a group by

the above composition belongs to

as well and it comes that

is in

. ∎

3.2
Eigenvectors of the adjoint representation

Observe that the Lie algebra generated by
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
contains the vector fields that do not depend on the
\mathbb{T}
-coordinate. To obtain more elements in
{\mathfrak{p}}
we study the eigenvectors of operators of the form:

\mathrm{ad}_{X}:Y\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)\mapsto[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)\;,

for
X\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
.

In this subsection, we show that if
Y
is an eigenvector of
\mathrm{ad}_{X}
for
X\in{\mathfrak{p}}
then
Y
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
(see
Proposition

3.7
). This will enable us to prove
Proposition

3.1
in
Section

3.3
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
V
be a manifold and let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a subspace of
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
. We denote:

{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}):=\{Y\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V):\exists X\in{\mathfrak{g}}\text{ such that }Y=[X,Y]\}\;.

The following is the key proposition enabling to construct new examples of pluggable flows:

Proposition 3.7
.

If
Y\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
satisfies
\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y=Y
for
X\in{\mathfrak{p}}
then
Y
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
:

{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{p}})\subset{\mathfrak{p}}\;.

Proof.

Given a map
f\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
and a vector field
W\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)
we denote the pushforward of
W
by
f
as:

\mathrm{Ad}_{f}W:=f_{*}W:=Df\circ W\circ f^{-1}\;.

This notation is consistent with the usual composition rules given by the following commuting diagram:

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}&&{W}&&\\
&M&\longrightarrow&TM&\\
f&\downarrow&&\downarrow&Df\\
&M&\longrightarrow&TM&\\
&&{\mathrm{Ad}_{f}W}&&\\
\end{array}\;.

The following contains a key idea for the proof of the main theorem:

Lemma 3.8
.

If
Y
satisfies
[X,Y]=Y
, then for every
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
it holds:

(3.8)

\mathrm{Fl}^{t}_{Y}=\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{-s}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{t\cdot e^{-s}}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}\;.

Proof.

We recall the following well known result on adjunction of vector fields by flows, see e.g.
[
KN63
, Prop 1.9]
:

Fact 3.9
.

Let
U,W\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)
be two vector fields then it holds:

\partial_{t}\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{U}^{t}}W_{|t=0}=-[U,W]\;.

First observe that by the latter fact it holds
X=\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}}(X)
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let then
Y_{s}:=\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}}(Y)=D\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}\circ Y\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{-s}
.
Observe that since
Y=[X,Y]
, it holds:

(3.9)

[X,Y_{s}]=[\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}}(X),\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}}(Y)]=\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}}([X,Y])=\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}}(Y)=Y_{s}\;.

Also for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:

(3.10)

\partial_{s}Y_{s}=\partial_{t}(Y_{s+t})_{|t=0}=\partial_{t}\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{t}}(Y_{s})_{|t=0}\;.

Thus by the latter fact it follows:

(3.11)

\partial_{s}Y_{s}=-[X,Y_{s}]=-Y_{s}\;.

Consequently
\partial_{s}Y_{s}=-Y_{s}
and thus
e^{-s}\cdot Y=Y_{s}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}}(Y)
. After integration between
0
and
t
, we obtain:

(3.12)

\mathrm{Fl}_{e^{-s}\cdot Y}^{t}=\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{t}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{-s}\;.

As
\mathrm{Fl}_{e^{-s}\cdot Y}^{t}=\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{t\cdot e^{-s}}
we obtain the desired result by composing the latter equation on the right by
\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}
and on the left by
\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{-s}
.
∎

We can now prove
Proposition

3.7
.
Fix a neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}
of
\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
and let us show the existence of a plugin in
{\mathcal{N}}
whose output is
\mathrm{Fl}^{t}_{Y}
. Let
{\mathcal{N}}_{c}
be the neighborhood of
\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times M)
given by
Lemma

2.10
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
be sufficiently large such that
\mathrm{Fl}^{e^{-s}\cdot t}_{Y}
belongs to the neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}_{c}
of the identity. Then for any
k\geq 1
large enough
\mathrm{Fl}^{e^{-s}\cdot t}_{Y}
is the output of a plugin
g\in{\mathcal{N}}
of step
2^{-k}
. Since
X\in{\mathfrak{p}}
, it holds
\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}\in\mathbb{P}
so for any
k\geq 1
large enough
\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{s}
and
\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{-s}
are the outputs of plugins
h
and
f
in
{\mathcal{N}}
of respective steps
2^{-k}
and
2^{-k-1}
. So for any large
k
the map
\mathrm{Fl}^{t}_{Y}
is the output of the plugin
(h\star g)\star f
with step
2^{-k-2}
. Since this holds for any neighbourhood
{\mathcal{N}}
of the identity the plugin
(h\star g)\star f
can be taken arbitrarily close to identity. Therefore for every
t
, there exists a plugin with output
\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{t}
arbitrarily close to identity for any small enough step. Hence
Y
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
.
∎

A second main ingredient of the proof of the main theorem is the following of independent interest:

Proposition 3.10
.

For every
T\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
, there exist finite families
(X_{i})_{i},(Y_{i})_{i},(Z_{i})_{i}
of vector fields in
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
such that:

T=\sum_{i}[Y_{i},Z_{i}]\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{i}=[X_{i},Y_{i}]\;.

The proof of this proposition and its parametric counterpart will occupy the full
Section

3.3
.
Now note that the following is an isomorphism of Lie algebras:

{\mathfrak{i}}_{2}:X\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)\mapsto(0,X)\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}\;.

Thus, by applying this isomorphism to the image of sets involved in the statement of
Proposition

3.10
for every
T\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
, there exist finite families
(X_{i})_{i},(Y_{i})_{i},(Z_{i})_{i}
of vector fields in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
such that:

T=\sum_{i}[Y_{i},Z_{i}]\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{i}=[X_{i},Y_{i}]\;.

In other words, we proved:

Corollary 3.11
.

Any element of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
can be written as a sum of Lie brackets of elements of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
with elements in
{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathrm{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}})\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
.

This corollary allows to deduce:

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

Let
p_{\theta}:\mathbb{T}\times M\to\mathbb{T}
be the projection on the
\mathbb{T}
-coordinate.

First note that by Fourier decomposition theorem, the space
{\mathfrak{g}}_{3}
is the closure of the vector space spanned by elements of the form:

\phi\circ p_{\theta}\cdot Y\quad\text{for }\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}\;.

Since
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a closed vector space by
Proposition

2.16
, it suffices to show any such
\phi\circ p_{\theta}\cdot Y
is in
{\mathfrak{p}}
.
To do so, we first start with the case where there exists
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
such that
Y=[X,Y]
, i.e. we assume that
Y\in{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathrm{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}})
.
Since the
\mathbb{T}
-coordinate
p_{\theta}\circ X
of

is zero, it follows:

(3.13)

[X,\phi\circ p_{\theta}\cdot Y]=\phi\circ p_{\theta}\cdot DY(X)-DX(\phi\circ p_{\theta}\cdot Y)=\phi\circ p_{\theta}\cdot[X,Y]=\phi\circ p_{\theta}\cdot Y\;.

Thus by

3.7
we have

since

.
Now in the general case, for every

, by

,

is a Lie-algebra and each

∎

For

3.3
Decomposition of vector fields

To prove the
F
, it remains only to show
Proposition

3.10
. We first prove this proposition in the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which we will deduce from the case
M=\mathbb{R}
and a parametric version thereof.
This whole section is dedicated to this proof.
For a manifold
M
, let us denote:

{\mathfrak{Eig}}(M):=\{Y\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M):\exists X\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)\text{ such that }\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y=[X,Y]=Y\}\;.

We can rephrase
Proposition

3.10
as:

Proposition 3.15
.

Every vector field in
T\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
is a finite sum of vector fields of the form
[Y_{i},Z_{i}]
with

Y_{i}\in{\mathfrak{Eig}}(M)
. In other words:

[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(M),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(M)]=\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(M).

Two key ingredients of the proof of this proposition are the following observations:

Fact 3.16
.

The vector field
Y:y\mapsto 1
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfies
[X,Y]=Y
with
X:y\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto-y
.

Fact 3.17
.

For any diffeomorphism
\psi\colon W\to V
between manifolds and any vector fields
{X,Y\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)}
satisfying

[X,Y]=Y
, it holds
[\psi^{*}X,\psi^{*}Y]=\psi^{*}Y
.

An important consequence of the latter fact is:

Fact 3.18
.

For any diffeomorphism
\psi\colon W\to V
from a manifold
W
into
V
, if
X\in[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(V),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)]
, then
\psi^{*}X\in[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(W),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(W)]
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}
be a manifold. We define:

{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathcal{Q}}=\Bigl\{Y_{\mathcal{Q}}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathcal{Q}}:\text{ there exists a family }X_{\mathcal{Q}}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathcal{Q}}\text{ such that }[X_{\mathcal{Q}},Y_{\mathcal{Q}}]=Y_{\mathcal{Q}}\Bigr\}.

We can prove
Proposition

3.15
in the case
M=\mathbb{R}
:

Lemma 3.19
.

We have:

[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}),\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})]=\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\;.

Moreover for any manifold

{\mathcal{Q}}
, every
T_{\mathcal{Q}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
satisfies
T_{\mathcal{Q}}=[Y_{\mathcal{Q}},Z_{\mathcal{Q}}]
for some
{Y_{\mathcal{Q}}\in{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}
and
Z_{\mathcal{Q}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
.

Proof.

We first give an intuitive idea of the proof using

3.16
. First note that for every
T\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, there exist
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
Y=[X,Y]
and
T=[Y,Z]
. Indeed it suffices to take
X\colon y\mapsto-y
,
Y\colon y\mapsto 1
and
Z\colon y\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{y}T(t)\,dt
. This almost proves the first assertion of the Lemma.
To have exactly the desired result we shall modify
X
,
Y
and
Z
to make them compactly supported.
Let
\widetilde{T}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. Take intervals
[-A,A]\subset(-a,a)
containing its support. Consider the map
\psi\colon y\mapsto y\cdot e^{e^{(a^{2}-y^{2})^{-1}}}
from
(-a,a)
to
\mathbb{R}
.
We compute its derivative at
y\in(-a,a)
by:

(3.19)

D\psi(y)=\phi(y)\cdot e^{e^{(a^{2}-y^{2})^{-1}}}\;,\quad\text{ where }\phi(y):=1+2y^{2}\cdot(a^{2}-y^{2})^{-2}e^{(a^{2}-y^{2})^{-1}}\;.

Thus the map
\psi
is a diffeomorphism. This allows to consider the push-forward:

(3.20)

T=\psi_{*}\widetilde{T}=D\psi\circ\widetilde{T}\circ\psi^{-1}\;.

Now we define as above:

(3.21)

X\colon y\mapsto-y\,,\quad Y\colon y\mapsto 1\quad\text{and}\quad Z\colon y\mapsto\int_{-\infty}^{y}T(t)\,dt\,.

Note that
\tilde{T}=\psi^{*}T
and put
\widetilde{X}=\psi^{*}X
and
\widetilde{Y}=\psi^{*}Y
. Then by
3.17
, it holds:

(3.22)

\widetilde{Y}=[\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}]\quad\text{and}\quad[\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z}]=\widetilde{T}\;.

Let us show that these pulled-back vector fields on
(-a,a)
extend smoothly by
0
to
\mathbb{R}
.
For any vector field
S\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
\psi^{*}S=(D\psi)^{-1}\circ S\circ\psi=\tfrac{S\circ\psi}{D\psi}
.
It follows:

(3.23)

\widetilde{X}(y)=\psi^{*}X(y)=\tfrac{-\psi(y)}{D\psi(y)}=\tfrac{-y}{\phi(y)}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{Y}(y)=\psi^{*}Y(y)=\tfrac{1}{D\psi(y)},

Observe that
\phi(y)
grows exponentially fast to
+\infty
when
|y|\to a
. Thus
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
have all their derivatives tending to
0
as
y
tends to
\pm a
. So they extend smoothly by
0
to vector fields in
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Also note that
Z(y)=0
for
y\leq\psi(-A)
and
Z(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}T(t)\,dt
for
y\geq\psi(A)
. Thus:

(3.24)

\widetilde{Z}(y)=\psi^{*}Z(y)=0\quad\mbox{ for }-a<y\leq-A\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{Z}(y)=\frac{1}{D\psi(y)}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}}T(t)\cdot dt\quad\mbox{ for }A\leq y<a.

Likewise
\widetilde{Z}
extends smoothly by
0
to form a vector field in
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. As
\widetilde{Y}=[\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}]
and
[\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z}]=\widetilde{T}
, this proves the first assertion of the lemma.

For the parametric assertion, observe that
\psi
,
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
depends only on the segment
[-a,a]
. Hence given
\widetilde{T}_{\mathcal{Q}}=(\widetilde{T}_{q})_{q\in{\mathcal{Q}}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
, we set
[-A,A]\subset(-a,a)
containing the supports of all
\widetilde{T}_{q}
and define
\psi
,
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
as above. Then we observe that
\widetilde{Z}_{q}=\psi^{*}\int T_{q}
depends smoothly on
q
and define a family
\widetilde{Z}_{\mathcal{Q}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathcal{Q}}
which satisfies the desired equalities with
\widetilde{T}_{\mathcal{Q}}
and the constant families
(\widetilde{X})_{q\in{\mathcal{Q}}}
and
(\widetilde{Y})_{q\in{\mathcal{Q}}}
.
∎

We are going to use the parametric assertion of the latter lemma to obtain:

Lemma 3.20
.

We have
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]=\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Lemma

3.19
corresponds to the case
n=1
. For
n\geq 2
, given
T\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we write its components as
T=(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})
. By linearity of the condition, it suffices to show that each vector field
(0,\dots,0,T_{i},0,\dots,0)
is in
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
.
Using an adjunction by a permutation of the coordinates and
3.18
, its suffices to show that each
(T_{i},0,\dots,0)
is in
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
. In other words, it suffices to prove that the following subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is included in
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
:

{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto(h(y),0,\dots,0):h\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})\}\;.

To this end, note that
X_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\mapsto\widehat{X_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}}\in{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an isomorphism of Lie algebras. By
Lemma

3.19
with
{\mathcal{Q}}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, we have:

{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=[\widehat{{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}},{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]\;.

Finally we note that
\widehat{{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}
is formed by vector fields of the form
\widehat{Y_{\mathcal{Q}}}
such that

Y_{\mathcal{Q}}=[X_{\mathcal{Q}},Y_{\mathcal{Q}}]
for
X_{\mathcal{Q}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Thus
\widehat{Y_{\mathcal{Q}}}=[\widehat{X_{\mathcal{Q}}},\widehat{Y_{\mathcal{Q}}}]
, this proves that
{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
.

∎

We can now treat the general case:

Proof of
Proposition

3.15
.

Let
T\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(M)
. Then it decomposes in a finite sum
T=\sum_{i}T_{i}
where each
T_{i}
is compactly supported in an open set
U_{i}
which is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via a map
\psi_{i}\colon U_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the push forward
\psi_{i*}T_{i}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By
Lemma

3.20

the field
\psi_{i*}T_{i}|U_{i}
belongs to
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]
. Thus by
Lemma

3.19
, it holds
T_{i}|U_{i}\in[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(U_{i}),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(U_{i})]
. This means that
T_{i}=\sum_{\text{Finite}}[Y_{j},Z_{j}]
with
Y_{j}=[X_{j},Y_{j}]
for some
X_{j},Y_{j},Z_{j}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Extending all these vector fields by
0
, we obtain that
T_{i}
belongs to
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(M),\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(M)]
. So does
T=\sum_{i}T_{i}
.
∎

Parametric counterpart.

We set:

{\mathfrak{Eig}}(M)_{\mathscr{P}}:=\{Y_{\mathscr{P}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)_{\mathscr{P}}:Y_{\mathscr{P}}=[X_{\mathscr{P}},Y_{\mathscr{P}}]\text{ with }X_{\mathscr{P}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(M)_{\mathscr{P}}\}\;.

We shall prove
Proposition

3.13
that we rephrase as:

Proposition 3.21
.

It holds:

[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(M)_{\mathscr{P}},\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(M)_{\mathscr{P}}]=\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(M)_{\mathscr{P}}.

Similarly to the proof of
Proposition

3.15
,
Proposition

3.21
is an easy consequence of the following:

Lemma 3.22
.

We have
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}},\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}}]=\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
Proposition

3.15
, we only need to prove that the following Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}}
of
\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}}
is included in
[{\mathfrak{Eig}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}},\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}}]
:

{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}}:=\{((h_{p},0,\dots,0))_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}:h_{\mathscr{P}}=(h_{p})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})_{\mathscr{P}}\}\;.

To this end we proceed as in
Lemma

3.20
, by using the isomorphism of Lie algebra:

X_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times{\mathscr{P}}}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times{\mathscr{P}}}\mapsto Y_{\mathscr{P}}\in{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\mathscr{P}}\text{ such that }\widehat{X_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times{\mathscr{P}}}}=\widehat{Y_{\mathscr{P}}}\;,

and using
Lemma

3.19
.
∎

Appendix A
Smoothness of outputs

The proof of
Proposition

1.5
stating that the output of a plugin is necessarily smooth is similar to the classical renormalization performed by Douady-Ghys
[
Dou87
,
Ghy84
]
, Yoccoz
[
Yoc95a
]
and Shilnikov-Turaev
[
ST00
]
.

Proof of
Proposition

1.5
.

Let
g
be a plugin with step
\sigma
. Let
\pi:\tilde{\mathbb{T}}\times M:=\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{T}\times M
be the canonical cover. Let
\tilde{g}
be a lift of
g
such that
\tilde{g}(0,y)=(\sigma,y)
for every
y\in M
.

Fact A.1
.

The action
\phi:(k,z)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\tilde{\mathbb{T}}\times M\mapsto\tilde{g}^{k}(z)\in\tilde{\mathbb{T}}\times M
is free, proper and discontinuous.

Proof.

The action is free since no point of
\Delta+\mathbb{Z}
is fixed by
\tilde{g}
nor in its complement (every point must come back to
\Delta+\mathbb{Z}
). It is discontinuous since any
x\in\mathbb{R}\times M
has its orbit which equals the one of a certain
z\in[k,k+\sigma)\times M
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by
Definition

1.1
.(iii), and the orbit of
z
is discrete by
Definition

1.1
.(i). Finally the action is proper since
\tau
is bounded by some
N\geq 1
by
Definition

1.1
.(ii), and so any
\tilde{g}^{N+1}(\theta,y)
has its
\mathbb{R}
-coordinate greater than
\theta+\sigma
. ∎

Thus the quotient
C:=\tilde{\mathbb{T}}\times M/\phi
is a manifold. As
\tilde{g}
sends the left hand side of
\Delta
to its right hand side, the image of
\Delta
by the group action is both open and closed, hence equal to the connected set
C
. Therefore,
\Delta_{\sigma}=[0,\sigma)\times M
is a fundamental domain of this group action. Also the rescaling map
H_{\sigma}:\Delta_{\sigma}\to\mathbb{T}\times M
induces a diffeomorphism between
C
and
\mathbb{T}\times M
.

Now observe that
\tilde{T}:(\theta,y)\mapsto(\theta+1,y)
and

\tilde{g}
commutes:
\tilde{T}\circ\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}\circ\tilde{T}
.
Thus
\tilde{T}
defines a smooth diffeomorphism
T
on
C
. To determine
T
, we (abusively) identify
\Delta_{\sigma}
to a subset of both
\tilde{\mathbb{T}}\times M
and
\mathbb{T}\times M
.
Given
x\in\Delta_{\sigma}
, the point
\tilde{T}(x)\in[1,1+\sigma)\times M
is equivalent to the point
y\in\Delta_{\sigma}
such that there exists
k\geq 0
satisfying
\tilde{g}^{k}(y)=\tilde{T}(x)
. Note that
k=\tau(y)
and so
\tilde{T}(x)=\tilde{g}^{\tau}(y)
.
Composing by
\pi
, we obtain that
x=g^{\tau}(y)
. Hence
T
is equal to the inverse of the action of
g^{\tau}
on
C
. Therefore
G
is a diffeomorphism.
Observe that this construction depends continuously on
g
and so the output depends continuously on the plugin. As the space of plugins is connected, it comes that the space of outputs is connected
to
\mathrm{id}
by
Example

1.4
.

∎

Appendix B
Lie algebras associated to closed subgroups

This section is dedicated to show
Proposition

2.16
which states that
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a closed Lie algebra. We prove this using general arguments on closed subgroups of
\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
where
V
is a manifold.

Proposition B.1
.

For every closed subgroup
\mathbb{G}\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
, the following is
a closed Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)
:

{\mathfrak{g}}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V):\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{t}\in\mathbb{G},\;\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

We immediately deduce the result of
Proposition

2.16
by applying the latter proposition with
V=\mathbb{T}\times M
and
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{P}
.

Proof of
Proposition

B.1
.

{\mathfrak{g}}
is a vector space.
First note that if
X\in\mathbb{G}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds
{\lambda\cdot X\in{\mathfrak{g}}}
. Now for
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, for any large integer
N
and
r\geq 1
, observe that:

\mathrm{Fl}_{X+Y}^{1/N}=\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{1/N}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{1/N}+O(N^{-2})

for the
C^{r}
-norm. Thus we have:

Fl_{X+Y}^{1}=\left(Fl_{X}^{1/N}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{1/N}\right)^{N}+O_{C^{r}}(N^{-1}),

and the supports of
\left(Fl_{X}^{1/N}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{1/N}\right)^{N}
are included in the union of those of
X
and
Y
. Thus

Fl_{X+Y}^{1}
is the limit of
\left(Fl_{X}^{1/N}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{1/N}\right)^{N}
when
N\to\infty
in the topology of
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)
. As
\mathbb{G}
is a group, the map
(Fl_{X}^{1/N}\circ\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{1/N})^{N}
belongs to
\mathbb{G}
, and since
\mathbb{G}
is closed the map
Fl_{X+Y}^{1}
also belongs to
\mathbb{G}
. Also for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, by replacing
X,Y
by
(tX,tY)
, we obtain that
Fl_{X+Y}^{t}=Fl_{tX+tY}^{1}
belongs to
\mathbb{G}
.

{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Lie algebra.
For
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
r\geq 1
, we have for the
C^{r}
-norm:

\mathrm{Fl}_{[X,Y]}^{\tau^{2}}=[\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{\tau},\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{\tau}]+O(\tau^{3})

Thus by taking
\tau^{2}=1/N
small we have:

\mathrm{Fl}_{[X,Y]}^{1}=\left([\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{\sqrt{N}},\mathrm{Fl}_{Y}^{\sqrt{N}}]\right)^{N}+O({\sqrt{N}}^{-1})\;.

So
\mathrm{Fl}_{[X,Y]}^{1}
is in
\mathbb{G}
. Similarly, we have for any
t
that
\mathrm{Fl}_{[tX,Y]}^{1}\in\mathbb{G}
, and so
\mathrm{Fl}_{[X,Y]}^{t}=\mathrm{Fl}_{[tX,Y]}^{1}\in\mathbb{G}
.

{\mathfrak{g}}
is closed.
As for every
t\geq 0
, the map
\mathrm{Fl}^{t}:X\in\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)\mapsto\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{t}
is continuous and
\mathbb{G}
is closed, the set
\{X\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V):\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{t}\in\mathbb{G}\}
is closed. Thus the intersection
{\mathfrak{g}}
of the latter sets for all
t
is closed.
∎

To state the parameteric counterpart of the latter proposition, given a manifold
{\mathscr{P}}
, we define:

\mathrm{Fl}^{t}:X_{\mathscr{P}}=(X_{p})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}\in\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}\mapsto(\mathrm{Fl}^{t}_{X_{p}})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}\in\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}\;.

Note that the following diagram commutes:

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}&&\mathfrak{inc}&&\\
&\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}&\hookrightarrow&\mathfrak{diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})&\\
\mathrm{Fl}^{t}&\downarrow&&\downarrow&\mathrm{Fl}^{t}\\
&\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}&\hookrightarrow&\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})&\\
&&{inc}&&\\
\end{array}

where
\mathfrak{inc}(X_{\mathscr{P}}):=\widehat{X_{\mathscr{P}}}
and
inc(f_{\mathscr{P}}):=\widehat{f_{\mathscr{P}}}
.

Corollary B.2
.

For every closed subgroup
\mathbb{G}_{\mathscr{P}}\subset\mathrm{Diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}
, the following is
a closed Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}
:

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathscr{P}}:=\{X_{\mathscr{P}}\in\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}:\mathrm{Fl}_{X}^{t}\in\mathbb{G},\;\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Proof.

First note that
inc(\mathbb{G}_{\mathscr{P}})
is a closed Lie sub-group of
\mathrm{Diff}^{\infty}_{c}(V\times{\mathscr{P}})
. Hence it define via
Proposition

B.1
a closed Lie algebra
\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathscr{P}}}
. By commutativity of the diagram, we have:

\mathfrak{inc}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathscr{P}}):=(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathscr{P}}})\;.

Hence
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathscr{P}}
is a closed Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{diff}_{c}^{\infty}(V)_{\mathscr{P}}
.
∎

1.
Introduction

A
strongly regular graph
srg
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
is a simple and undirected graph, neither complete nor edgeless, that has the following properties:

(1)

It is a regular graph of order
v
and valency
k
.

(2)

For each pair of adjacent vertices
x,y
, there are exactly
\lambda
vertices adjacent to both
x
and
y
.

(3)

For each pair of nonadjacent vertices
x,y
, there are exactly
\mu
vertices adjacent to both
x
and
y
.

For example, the pentagon is an srg
(5,2,0,1)
and the Petersen graph is an srg
(10,3,0,1)
. The following spectral characterization of srgs is well known. Let
\Gamma
be a simple connected
k
-regular graph that is neither complete nor edgeless. Then
\Gamma
is strongly regular if and only if its adjacency matrix has exactly two distinct eigenvalues different from
k
.

An srg
(u^{2},r(u-\epsilon),\epsilon u+r^{2}-3\epsilon r,r^{2}-\epsilon r)
is said to be of

Latin square type or negative Latin square type
according as
\epsilon=1
or
-1
. Typical examples of strongly regular graphs of Latin square type or negative Latin square type come from nonsingular
quadrics in the projective space
\mathsf{PG}(n,q)
of odd dimension
n
over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of order
q

[
30
]
. It seems that more examples of
strongly regular graphs of Latin square type are known than those of negative Latin square type
[
13
]
.

Let
G
be an additively written abelian group, and let
D
be an inverse-closed subset of
G
such that
0_{G}\not\in D
. The
Cayley graph on
G
with connection set
D
, denoted by
{\rm Cay}(G,D)
, is the graph with the elements of
G
as vertices; two vertices are adjacent if and only if their difference belongs to
D
. It is known that the eigenvalues of
{\mathrm{Cay}}(G,D)
are the character values of
D
. More precisely, for a (complex) character
\psi
of
G
, define

\psi(D)=\sum_{x\in D}\psi(x).

Then all the eigenvalues of
{\mathrm{Cay}}(G,D)
are given by
\psi(D)
,
\psi\in{\hat{G}}
, where
{\hat{G}}
is the (complex) character group of
G
.

During the past two decades, strongly regular Cayley graphs on the additive groups of finite fields have been extensively studied due to their close connections with certain substructures in finite geometries. If the connection set
D
of
{\mathrm{Cay}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}},D)
is invariant under multiplication by elements of the multiplicative group
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
, then the subset
{\mathcal{D}}=\{x{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}:x\in D\}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{\ast}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
can be viewed as a set of points of
\mathsf{PG}(n-1,q)
; in this case,
{\mathrm{Cay}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}},D)
is strongly regular if and only if
{\mathcal{D}}
is a projective two-intersection set, or a two-character set, in
\mathsf{PG}(n-1,q)
. Further links between strongly regular Cayley graphs and substructures in finite classical polar spaces such as
m
-ovoids and
i
-tight sets are known
[
2
,
3
,
22
]
. In this paper we will only consider strongly regular Cayley graphs related to
m
-ovoids. Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a finite classical polar space and
{\mathcal{M}}
be a set of points in
{\mathcal{S}}
. We say that
{\mathcal{M}}
is an
m
-ovoid
if every generator of
{\mathcal{S}}
meets
{\mathcal{M}}
in exactly
m
points. There are many known constructions of
m
-ovoids
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
22
,
25
]
.

An elliptic quadric
{\mathsf{Q}}^{-}(5,q)
of
\mathsf{PG}(5,q)
is trivially a
(q+1)
-ovoid, which gives rise to a negative Latin square type srg
(q^{6},r(q^{3}+1),-q^{3}+r^{2}+3r,r^{2}+r)
with
r=q^{2}-1
.
On the other hand, it is known that nontrivial
m
-ovoids in
{\mathsf{Q}}^{-}(5,q)
exist whenever
q
is an odd prime power and
m=(q+1)/2
. Since the dual of
{\mathsf{Q}}^{-}(5,q)
as a generalized quadrangle is
{\mathsf{H}}(3,q^{2})
, such a
\frac{(q+1)}{2}
-ovoid in
{\mathsf{Q}}^{-}(5,q)
can be interpreted as a hemisystem in
{\mathsf{H}}(3,q^{2})
, which consists of
\frac{(q+1)(q^{3}+1)}{2}
lines containing exactly half of the lines
through every point of
{\mathsf{H}}(3,q^{2})
. Hemisystems have been studied extensively in
[
1
,
4
,
10
,
12
,
27
,
35
]
. They give rise to negative Latin square type srg
(q^{6},r(q^{3}+1),-q^{3}+r^{2}+3r,r^{2}+r)
with
r=(q^{2}-1)/2
. In
[
4
]
, the authors constructed a new
\frac{(q+1)}{2}
-ovoid for
q\equiv 3\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{4})
in
{\mathsf{Q}}^{-}(5,q)
by choosing
2(q+1)
cosets from the
4(q^{2}+q+1)
th cyclotomic classes of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{6}}
.
The first author of this paper could generalize the construction of strongly regular Cayley graphs in
[
4
]
by using three-valued Gauss periods
[
32
]
. In particular, the following theorem was proved.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
32
]
)
Let
q
be an odd prime power.
There exists an srg
(q^{6},r(q^{3}+1),-q^{3}+r^{2}+3r,r^{2}+r)
with
r=(q^{2}-1)M/2
, in the following cases:

(1)

M=3
and
q\equiv 7\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{24})
;

(2)

M=7
and
q\equiv 11,51\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{56})
.

The three-valued Gauss periods used in
[
32
]
all arise from cyclic arcs of Singer type in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
. An
M
-
arc
in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
is a set of
M
points no three of which are collinear. An arc is said to be
cyclic
if it is a point orbit under the action of a cyclic collineation group
H
of
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
on the point set of
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
. If
H
is a subgroup of a Singer group of
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
the arc is said to be of
Singer type
. It turns out that besides the cyclic arcs of Singer type of size
3
and
7
used in
[
32
]
, there are a lot more “small” cyclic arcs of Singer type known
[
31
,
37
]
in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
. A natural question arises: Can these cyclic
M
-arcs of Singer type with
M>7
give rise to strongly regular Cayley graphs? In this paper, we investigate this problem and come up with sufficient conditions for these cyclic
M
-arcs of Singer type to give rise to srg
(q^{6},r(q^{3}+1),-q^{3}+r^{2}+3r,r^{2}+r)
with
r=(q^{2}-1)M/2
and
M>7
. For the detailed statement of our results, see Theorem
2.7
.

We further investigate to what extent our construction in Theorem
2.7
works. Let
M,h
be positive integers such that
M
is odd,
1\leqslant h\leqslant M-1
and
M\,|\,(h^{2}+h+1)
, and let
{\mathcal{P}}_{M,h}
be the set of primes
p\equiv h\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
. We use

\Psi_{M,h}
to denote the set of primes
p\equiv h\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
with

p\equiv 3\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{4})
such that there exists
a strongly regular Cayley graph on
({\mathbb{F}}_{p^{6}},+)

with parameters
(p^{6},r(p^{3}+1),-p^{3}+r^{2}+3r,r^{2}+r)
, where
r=(p^{2}-1)M/2
.
Furthermore, for the quadratic character
\eta
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{3}}
and a fixed primitive element
\omega\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{3}}
, define

\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,\beta}=\{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{M,h}\,:\,\eta(1+\omega^{\frac{(p^{3}-1)i}{M}})=\alpha,1\leqslant i\leqslant M-1,\eta(2)=-\alpha,\eta(-1)=\beta\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{3}}\},

where
\alpha,\beta\in\{1,-1\}
. In this paper, we will prove that
\Psi_{M,h}
contains almost all primes in
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\{1,-1\}}\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
.
In particular, the following theorem is proved.

Theorem 1.2
.

If the order of
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
is odd, then
\Psi_{M,h}
contains infinitely many primes.

The values of
M<200
satisfying the condition in Theorem
1.2

are

7,31,49,73,79,103,127,151,199.

Thus, using the
M
’s listed above and Theorem
1.2
we obtain many new infinite families of strongly regular Cayley graphs of negative Latin square type.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we review a
construction of strongly regular Cayley graphs based on three-valued Gauss periods given in
[
32
]
. Furthermore, we state Chebotarëv’s density theorem, which will be used to prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section

, we give a lower bound on primes

such that the Gauss periods in

of characteristic

are guaranteed to take exactly three values forming an arithmetic progression. This result is needed for applying our construction of strongly regular Cayley graphs in Theorem
2.7
. In Section
4
, we show that

\Psi_{M,h}
contains almost all primes in
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\{1,-1\}}\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
. Furthermore, we study conditions under which
\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,\beta}
is an infinite set. In particular, we determine

\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,\beta}
in the cases where
M=3,7
, or
21
. In Section
5
, we study the structure of the Galois group
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
, where

is obtained by adjoining
\sqrt{1+\zeta_{M}^{i}}
,

, and

to

. Finally we give a proof of Theorem

. In Section

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cyclotomic classes, Gauss periods, and Gauss sums

Let
p
be a prime and let
\zeta_{p}=\exp(2\pi i/p)\in{\mathbb{C}}
be a primitive
p^{\rm th}
root of unity. For positive integers
f
and
n
, let
q=p^{f}
and let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
denote the finite field of order
q^{n}
.
Define

\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast},\;\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(x)=\zeta_{p}^{{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{n}/p}(x)},\;\forall x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}

where
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{n}/p}
is the trace map from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. It can be easily shown that
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
is a nontrivial character of the additive group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
. All characters of
({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}},+)
are given by
\psi_{a}
,
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, where
\psi_{a}(x)=\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(ax)
,
\forall x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.

Let
\omega
be a fixed primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
and
N
be a positive integer dividing
q^{n}-1
. The cosets
C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})}=\omega^{i}\langle\omega^{N}\rangle
,
0\leqslant i\leqslant N-1
, of
\langle\omega^{N}\rangle
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
are called the
N^{\rm th}
cyclotomic classes
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
. The numbers
(i,j)_{N}:=|(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})}+1)\cap C_{j}^{(N,q^{n})}|
,
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant N-1
, are called
cyclotomic numbers
.

The
N^{\rm th}
Gauss periods
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
are defined as the character values of cyclotomic classes:

\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})=\sum_{x\in C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})}}\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(x),\,\,\,0\leqslant i\leqslant N-1.

On the other hand, for a multiplicative character
\chi
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, the
Gauss sum
is defined by

G_{q^{n}}(\chi)={\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{\ast}}}\chi(x)\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(x).

By the orthogonality relations of characters, the
N^{\rm th}
Gauss periods of

{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
can be expressed as a linear combination of Gauss sums:

(2.1)

\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}G_{q^{n}}(\chi_{N}^{j})\chi_{N}^{-j}(\omega^{i}),\;\,0\leqslant i\leqslant N-1,

where
\chi_{N}
is a fixed multiplicative character of order
N
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.

The following is a well-known result on Eisenstein sums.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
5
, Theorem 12.1.1]
)
Let
\chi
be a nontrivial multiplicative character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
whose restriction to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is trivial. Let
L
be a system of coset representatives of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{\ast}
such that
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{n}/q}
maps
L
onto
\{0,1\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Define

L_{0}=\{x\in L:\,{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{n}/q}(x)=0\},\,{\rm and}\;L_{1}=L\setminus L_{0}.

Then

\sum_{x\in L_{0}}\chi(x)=\frac{1}{q}G_{q^{n}}(\chi).

Let
S=\{i\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}):\omega^{i}\in L_{0}\}
. Then
S
is a Singer difference set in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)}
, and all the hyperplanes of
\mathsf{PG}(n-1,q)
can be obtained by cyclically shifting
S
. Let

\chi_{N}
be a multiplicative character of order
N
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
. In the rest of this subsection we always assume that
N\,|\,(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
. Under this assumption, the restriction of
\chi_{N}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is trivial; by (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}G_{q^{n}}(\chi_{N}^{j})\chi_{N}^{-j}(\omega^{i})

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{q^{n}-1}{N(q-1)}+\frac{q}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{\ell\in S}\chi_{N}^{j}(\omega^{\ell-i}).

Let
S_{N}
denote the multiset
\rho(S)
, where
\rho
is the natural epimorphism
\rho:{\mathbb{Z}}_{(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}
. We identify
S_{N}
with the group ring element
S_{N}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}c_{i}[i]\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}]
, where

c_{i}=|S\cap\{jN+i:j=0,1,\ldots,\frac{(q^{n}-1)}{N(q-1)}-1\}|,\;0\leqslant i\leqslant N-1.

These numbers are the intersection sizes of
C_{0}^{(N,q^{n})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
with the hyperplanes of
\mathsf{PG}(n-1,q)
. Define

(2.3)

F_{N}=\{c_{i}:0\leqslant i\leqslant N-1\}.

That is,
F_{N}
consists of distinct intersection sizes of
C_{0}^{(N,q^{n})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
with the hyperplanes of
\mathsf{PG}(n-1,q)
. Also let

(2.4)

I_{\beta}=\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}:c_{i}=\beta\},\,\beta\in F_{N}.

Then, we have

(2.5)

S_{N}=\sum_{\beta\in F_{N}}\beta I_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}].

By (
2.1
), the Gauss period
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})
with
i\in I_{\beta}
is related to hyperplane intersection size by

(2.6)

\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})=-\frac{q^{n}-1}{N(q-1)}+q\beta.

In
[
21
]
, the authors studied the question of when the Gauss periods
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})
,
i=0,1,\ldots,N-1
, with
N\,|\,(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
take exactly three values forming an arithmetic progression in relation to the existence of circulant weighing matrices and
3
-class translation association schemes. One important special case where the Gauss periods
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})
,
i=0,1,\ldots,N-1
, with
N\,|\,(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
take exactly three values forming an arithmetic progression is when
n=3
and
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
is a cyclic arc of Singer type in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
. Both Maruta
[
31
]
and Szönyi
[
37
]
studied
the question of when
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
is a cyclic arc of Singer type in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
.

Let
n=3
,
N|(q^{2}+q+1)
, and
M=(q^{2}+q+1)/N
. Assume that
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
is an
M
-arc in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
. Then each line of
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
meets
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
in
0,1
, or
2
points. So we have
\beta_{1}=2
,
\beta_{2}=1
,
\beta_{3}=0
using the notation introduced above. Hence by (
2.6
), the Gauss periods
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{n})})
take exactly three values
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
, with
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})=(-M+2q,-M+q,-M)
. To simplify notation we write
I_{i}:=I_{\beta_{i}}
.
Then, by Lemma 2.5 in
[
21
]
, we have

(2.7)

|I_{1}|=\frac{M-1}{2},\,|I_{2}|=q-M+2,\,|I_{3}|=\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{M}-q+\frac{M-3}{2}.

Furthermore, by (
2.5
), we have

(2.8)

S_{N}=2I_{1}+I_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}].

2.2.

Let
q\equiv 3\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{4})
be a prime power and
\omega
be a fixed primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
. Let
N
be an odd positive integer dividing
q^{2}+q+1
, and let
C_{i}^{(N,q^{3})}=\omega^{i}\langle\omega^{N}\rangle
,

.
We suppose that the Gauss periods
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{3})})
,

, take exactly three values
\alpha_{1}=-M+2q,\alpha_{2}=-M+q,\alpha_{3}=-M
. Recall that

Let
T_{1},T_{2}
be a partition of

, and
let

T_{i}^{\prime}\equiv 4^{-1}T_{i}\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{N})

for

. Define

X=2T_{1}^{\prime}\cup(2T_{2}^{\prime}+N)\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2N})

and

\displaystyle Y_{X}=

\displaystyle\,\{Ni+4j\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{4N}):(i,j)\in(\{0,3\}\times T_{1}^{\prime})\cup(\{1,2\}\times T_{2}^{\prime})\}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\cup\{Ni+4j\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{4N}):i=0,1,2,3,\,j\in 4^{-1}I_{1}\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{N})\}.

Let
\gamma
be a fixed primitive element of

such that

\gamma^{q^{3}+1}=\omega
.
Define

(2.11)

D_{X}=\bigcup_{i\in Y_{X}}C_{i}^{(4N,q^{6})},

where

,

. It is clear that

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[

)
For

. Let

. If

\displaystyle\,2\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(\omega^{c}\bigcup_{\ell\in X}C_{\ell}^{(2N,q^{3})})-\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(\omega^{c}\bigcup_{\ell\in 2^{-1}I_{2}}C_{\ell}^{(N,q^{3})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm G_{q^{3}}(\eta),&\mbox{ if $c\in 2^{-1}I_{2}\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{N})$,}\\

then

with

.

2.3.
A partition of a conic and its reduction modulo
2N

Viewing
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
as a

-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we will use

as the underlying vector space of

0\leqslant i\leqslant q^{2}+q
.
Define a quadratic form
Q:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
by setting
Q(x)=\text{Tr}_{q^{3}/q}(x^{2})
. It is straightforward to check that

is nonsingular.
Therefore,

defines a conic
{\mathcal{Q}}
in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
, which contains
q+1
points. Consequently each line of
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
meets
{\mathcal{Q}}
in
0
,
1
or
2
points.
Consider the following subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q^{2}+q+1}
:

W_{\mathcal{Q}}=\{i\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1}):Q(\omega^{i})=0\}=\{d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}\},

where the elements are numbered in any unspecified order.
Then, the conic
{\mathcal{Q}}
is equal to
\{\langle\omega^{d_{i}}\rangle:\,0\leqslant i\leqslant q\}
.
Furthermore,
W_{\mathcal{Q}}\equiv 2^{-1}S\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1})
, where

S=\{i\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1}):{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{3}/q}(\omega^{i})=0\}
is a Singer difference set in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q^{2}+q+1}
. Hence,

(2.15)

W_{\mathcal{Q}}W_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(-1)}={\mathbb{Z}}_{q^{2}+q+1}+q[0]\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{q^{2}+q+1}],

where
W_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(-1)}=\{-x\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1}):x\in W_{{\mathcal{Q}}}\}

We consider a partition of

.
For
d_{0}\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}
, we define

(2.16)

{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{Q}}:=\{\omega^{d_{i}}{\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{3}/q}(\omega^{d_{0}+d_{i}}):\,1\leqslant i\leqslant q\}\cup\{2\omega^{d_{0}}\}

It is clear that
X_{\mathcal{Q}}\pmod{q^{2}+q+1}=W_{\mathcal{Q}}
.
The subset
X_{\mathcal{Q}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{2(q^{2}+q+1)}
can be written as

X_{\mathcal{Q}}\equiv 2E_{1}\cup(2E_{2}+(q^{2}+q+1))\pmod{2(q^{2}+q+1)}

for some

with

. That is, we are partitioning

into its
even
and
odd

It follows that

W_{\mathcal{Q}}\equiv 2(E_{1}\cup E_{2})\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1})

and

S\equiv 4(E_{1}\cup E_{2})\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1}),

i.e., the partition of
X_{\mathcal{Q}}
into its even and odd parts induces partitions of
W_{\mathcal{Q}}
and
S
, respectively.
We give the following important properties of

.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
19

)
If we use any other

in place of

in the definition of

has the property that

or
X_{\mathcal{Q}}+(q^{2}+q+1)\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{2(q^{2}+q+1)})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

(
[
19
, Theorem 3.7, Remark 3.8]
,
[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
)

With notation as above,

\displaystyle\,2\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(\omega^{c}\bigcup_{i\in X_{\mathcal{Q}}}C_{i}^{(2(q^{2}+q+1),q^{3})})-\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(\omega^{c}\bigcup_{i\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}}C_{i}^{(q^{2}+q+1,q^{3})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm G_{q^{3}}(\eta),&\mbox{if $c\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1})\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}$, }\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

where
\eta
is the quadratic character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Assume that

(hence

). Then,
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{3})})

2.4.
Chebotarëv’s density theorem

Let
M
be an odd positive integer.
Let
h
be a positive integer such that
M\,|\,(h^{2}+h+1)
,
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{M,h}
be the set of primes such that
p\equiv h\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
. For

\alpha,\beta\in\{1,-1\}
,

define

(2.20)

\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,\beta}=\{p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{M,h}\,:\,\eta(1+\omega^{\frac{(p^{3}-1)i}{M}})=\alpha,1\leqslant i\leqslant M-1,\eta(2)=-\alpha,\eta(-1)=\beta\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{3}}\},

where
\eta
is the quadratic character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{3}}
.
In this paper, we will consider the question of whether there are infinitely many primes in
\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,\beta}
. In particular, we will show that
\Psi_{M,h}
contains almost all primes in
\bigcup_{\alpha\in\{1,-1\}}\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
, where
\Psi_{M,h}
is defined in Section 1.
To study this problem, we will use the Chebotarëv density theorem, which is a generalization of the well-known Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions.

Let
F
be a finite Galois extension of an algebraic number field
E
.
Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
be the rings of integers in
F
and
E
, respectively.
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}
be a prime ideal in
E
unramified in
F
and
{\mathfrak{P}}
be a prime ideal in
F
lying over
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then, there is a unique monomorphism
h

from
{\mathrm{Gal}}(({\mathcal{O}}_{F}/{\mathfrak{P}})/({\mathcal{O}}_{E}/{\mathfrak{p}}))
to
{\mathrm{Gal}}(F/E)
such that
h(\sigma)({\mathfrak{P}})={\mathfrak{P}}
for any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(({\mathcal{O}}_{F}/{\mathfrak{P}})/({\mathcal{O}}_{E}/{\mathfrak{p}}))
and the map from
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}/{\mathfrak{P}}
to itself induced by
h(\sigma):{\mathcal{O}}_{F}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
coincides with
\sigma
. In particular, the image
\sigma_{\mathfrak{P}}
of the Frobenius automorphism
x\mapsto x^{|{\mathcal{O}}_{E}/{\mathfrak{p}}|}
by
h
is called the
Frobenius substitution
with respect to
{\mathfrak{P}}
in
F/E
. The Frobenius substitution
depends on the choice of
{\mathfrak{P}}
lying over
{\mathfrak{p}}
only up to conjugation, i.e.,

\sigma_{\tau{\mathfrak{P}}}=\tau\sigma_{{\mathfrak{P}}}\tau^{-1}
for any
\tau\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(F/E)
.

Let
P(E)
be the set of prime ideals in
E
, and let
P^{\prime}(E)
be the set of prime ideals in
E
unramified in
F
. For any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(F/E)
, we use
C_{\sigma}
to denote the conjugacy class of
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(F/E)
, i.e.,

C_{\sigma}=\{\tau^{-1}\sigma\tau\,:\,\tau\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(F/E)\}
; and let
S_{\sigma}
be the set of prime ideals of
E
defined by

S_{\sigma}=\{{\mathfrak{P}}\cap E\in P^{\prime}(E)\,:\,{\mathfrak{P}}\mbox{ is a prime ideal in $F$ such that $\sigma_{{\mathfrak{P}}}\in C_{\sigma}$}\}.

For any subset
S
of
P(E)
, we define the
natural density
of
S
to be

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{|\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in S:N({\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant x\}|}{|\{{\mathfrak{p}}\in P(E):N({\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant x\}|}

if this limit exists, where
N({\mathfrak{p}})
is the absolute norm of
{\mathfrak{p}}
. If the natural density of
S
exists, then it is actually equal to the
Dirichlet density
of
S
defined by

\lim_{s\to 1^{+}}\Big(\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in S}\frac{1}{N({\mathfrak{p}})^{s}}\Big)\Big/\Big(\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in P(E)}\frac{1}{N({\mathfrak{p}})^{s}}\Big).

The following is known as Chebotarëv’s density theorem
[
36
]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

The density of
S_{\sigma}
is equal to

\frac{|C_{\sigma}|}{|G|}
; in particular, if
|C_{\sigma}|\neq 0
, then there are infinitely many prime ideals in
S_{\sigma}
. Here the claim is valid with either notion of density.

We denote both the Dirichlet density and the natural density of
S_{\sigma}
by
\delta(S_{\sigma})
.
We will use Theorem
2.8
in Section
5
.

3.
Cyclic arcs of Singer type in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)

In this section, we consider the question of when the first assumption in Theorem
2.7
is satisfied. That is, we consider the question of when the Gauss periods
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{3})})
,
i=0,1,\ldots,N-1
, take exactly three
values
-M+2q,-M+q,-M
. Note that the Gauss periods
\psi_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}}(C_{i}^{(N,q^{3})})
,
i=0,1,\ldots,N-1
, take exactly three
values
-M+2q,-M+q,-M
if and only if
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
is an
M
-arc in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
. Furthermore, it is clear that
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
is an
M
-arc in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
if and only if
\omega^{j_{1}N},\omega^{j_{2}N},\omega^{j_{3}N}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for all distinct
0\leqslant j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\leqslant M-1
.
Observe that

a_{1}\omega^{j_{1}N}+a_{2}\omega^{j_{2}N}+a_{3}\omega^{j_{3}N}\not=0
for all

(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}

and for all distinct
0\leqslant j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\leqslant M-1

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 2.84544pt

1+b_{1}\omega^{i_{1}N}\not=b_{2}\omega^{i_{2}N}
for all

(b_{1},b_{2})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}

and for all distinct
1\leqslant i_{1},i_{2}\leqslant M-1

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 2.84544pt

(1+C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})})\cap C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}\setminus\{1\}
, i.e.,
(0,0)_{N}=q-2
.

Thus, there is a close relationship between cyclic arcs of Singer type in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
and cyclotomic numbers. The following is our main theorem in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M,h
be positive integers such that
1\leqslant h\leqslant M-1
and
M\,|\,(h^{2}+h+1)
.
Let
q
be a power of a prime
p
such that
q\equiv h\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
.
Let
N=\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{M}
and
\omega
be a primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
. Suppose that

p>\Big(\frac{{18}M}{\phi(M)}\Big)^{\phi(M)/2{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(p)},

where
\phi
is the Euler totient function.
Then
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
is an
M
-arc in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
.

Remark 3.2
.

The theorem above was essentially proved by Maruta
[
31
]
and Szönyi
[
37
]
, where they did not give an explicit lower bound on
p
. We will give a proof by using a couple of recent results in the study of cyclotomic numbers
[
15
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

(
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
)
Let
q
be a power of a prime
p
and
M,e
be positive integers such that
q=eM+1
. Let
\omega
be a primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
\zeta_{M}=e^{2\pi i/M}
, and let
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}a_{i}x^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
. Suppose that

p>\Big(\frac{M}{\phi(M)}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}a_{i}^{2}\Big)^{\phi(M)/2{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(p)}.

Then
f(\omega^{e})=0
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if and only if

f(\zeta_{M})=0
in
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

(
[
15
, Proposition 5.8]
)
Let
M\geqslant 3
be a positive integer. Let
s_{i},t_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, be integers modulo
M
such that the
s_{i}
’s are pairwise distinct, the
t_{i}
’s are pairwise distinct, and
the
(s_{i}-t_{i})
’s modulo
M
are pairwise distinct.
Then,

\zeta_{M}^{s_{1}+t_{2}}+\zeta_{M}^{s_{2}+t_{3}}+\zeta_{M}^{s_{3}+t_{1}}-\zeta_{M}^{s_{1}+t_{3}}-\zeta_{M}^{s_{2}+t_{1}}-\zeta_{M}^{s_{3}+t_{2}}\not=0.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
:

The set
C_{0}^{(N,q^{3})}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
of points in
\mathsf{PG}(2,q)
is an
M
-arc if and only if

M_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{j_{1}N}&\omega^{j_{2}N}&\omega^{j_{3}N}\\
\omega^{j_{1}Nq}&\omega^{j_{2}Nq}&\omega^{j_{3}Nq}\\
\omega^{j_{1}Nq^{2}}&\omega^{j_{2}Nq^{2}}&\omega^{j_{3}Nq^{2}}\\
\end{pmatrix}

is nonsingular for all distinct
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\in\{0,1,\ldots,M-1\}
.

Define

g_{i,j}^{(h)}(x)=x^{j}+x^{i(h+1)}+x^{i+j(h+1)}-x^{i}-x^{j(h+1)}-x^{j+i(h+1)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
, where
the exponents
j,i(h+1),i+j(h+1),i,j(h+1),j+i(h+1)
are reduced modulo
M
.
Let
A_{1}=\{j,i(h+1),i+j(h+1)\}
and
A_{2}=\{i,j(h+1),j+i(h+1)\}
, where each element is reduced modulo
M
. Assume that
i,j\not\equiv 0\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
and
i\not\equiv j\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
. Then,
A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset
since
\gcd{(M,h)}=\gcd{(M,h+1)}=1
. Furthermore, it is easily checked that if two elements in
A_{1}
(resp.
A_{2}
) are equal,
so are all three elements in
A_{1}
(resp.
A_{2}
).
Hence, by writing
g_{i,j}^{(h)}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}a_{i}x^{i}
,
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\forall i
, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}a_{i}^{2}=6,12
or
18
.

Noting that
\omega^{N(q-1)(q+1)}=\omega^{N(q-1)(h+1)}
, we have

\det(M_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}})=\omega^{j_{1}N(q^{2}+q-1)+Nj_{2}+Nj_{3}}g_{j_{2}-j_{1},j_{3}-j_{1}}^{(h)}(\omega^{N(q-1)}).

Hence
M_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}}
is nonsingular if and only if
g_{j_{2}-j_{1},j_{3}-j_{1}}^{(h)}(\omega^{N(q-1)})\not=0
. Moreover, by applying Proposition
3.3
with
\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}a_{i}^{2}=18
,

g_{j_{2}-j_{1},j_{3}-j_{1}}^{(h)}(\omega^{N(q-1)})\not=0
if and only if

g_{j_{2}-j_{1},j_{3}-j_{1}}^{(h)}(\zeta_{M})\not=0
.

We now set

(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=(0,(j_{2}-j_{1})h,(j_{2}-j_{1})(1+h))

and

(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=(j_{3}(1+h)-j_{1}-j_{2}h,j_{3}-j_{1},j_{2}-j_{1}).

Then the
s_{i}
are pairwise distinct, and the
t_{i}
are pairwise distinct. Furthermore,
s_{1}-t_{1}=-j_{3}(1+h)+j_{1}+j_{2}h
,
s_{2}-t_{2}=(j_{2}-j_{1})h-(j_{3}-j_{1})
and
s_{3}-t_{3}=(j_{2}-j_{1})h
, which
are all distinct. Hence, by Proposition
3.4
, we have

g_{j_{2}-j_{1},j_{3}-j_{1}}^{(h)}(\zeta_{M})\not=0
. This completes the proof of the theorem. ∎

4.

In this section, we consider the question of when the other assumption in Theorem

is purely a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2N}
. We will use the same notation introduced in Section 2.3. (The results in this section are valid for all odd prime power

.)

Recall that

W_{\mathcal{Q}}:=\{i\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{q^{2}+q+1}):Q(\omega^{i})=0\}=\{d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{q}\},

In the definition of

(see (

to be

. In either case,

is purely a subset
of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2N}
if and only if
\eta(2)\not=\eta(g_{M}(\omega^{u}))
for all

with

u\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{N})\in 2^{-1}I_{1}
.
∎

.

(
[
32
, Lemma 5.8]
)
For
u\in W_{\mathcal{Q}}
with

u\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{N})\in 2^{-1}I_{1}
,
it holds that

(4.1)

\eta(g_{M}(\omega^{u}))=\eta(-1)\eta(1-\omega^{\frac{\ell_{u}(q+1)(q^{3}-1)}{M}})\eta(1-\omega^{\frac{2\ell_{u}q(q^{3}-1)}{M}}).

In

, by using Lemmas

and

were determined in the cases where

,
7

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\eta
be the quadratic character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
. Then,

is purely a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2N}

Let

\zeta_{M}=e^{2\pi i/M}

For a positive inetegr

This group and its subgroups, in particular the subgroup of cyclotomic units, have been studied in

In particular, nontrivial relations, called

The following relations in

A relation derived from the polynomial identity

Furthermore, the signs or roots of unity involved in the multiplicative relations were usually ignored in those studies while here we need to be concerned about the signs. Moreover, our problem is weaker than that treated in the previous studies since we are interested in characterizing pairs
(q,M)

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\eta
be the quadratic character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{3}}
and
\ell
be an integer,
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant M-1
such that
\gcd{(\ell,M)}=1
. Then, the following hold:

(1)

\eta(1+\epsilon_{M}^{\ell})=\eta(1+\epsilon_{M}^{-\ell})
.

(2)

Let

and

be odd positive integers such that
t\not|s
.
Then,

\eta(1+\epsilon_{t}^{s\ell})=\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\eta(1+\epsilon_{s}^{i}\epsilon_{t}^{\ell})

if

is a prime power with

\prod_{i=0}^{p^{j}-1}\eta(1+\epsilon_{p^{j}}^{i}\epsilon_{M}^{\ell})=\eta(1+\epsilon_{M}^{p^{j}\ell})

for

(3)

\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)-1}\eta(1+\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i}\ell})=1
.

(4)

\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)/2-1}\eta(1+\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i}\ell})=\eta(-1)

if
-1\in\langle 2\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
.

Proof:
Note that
\eta(\epsilon_{M}^{\ell})=1
.
The claims (1) and (2) follow from the trivial relations.

We now prove the claim in (3).
Since
2^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)}\equiv 1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)-1}(1+\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i}\ell})

\displaystyle\,=\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)-1}\frac{1-\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i+1}\ell}}{1-\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i}\ell}}=\frac{1-\epsilon_{M}^{2^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)}\ell}}{1-\epsilon_{M}^{\ell}}=1.

Next, we prove the claim in (4).
Since
2^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)/2}\equiv-1\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M})
, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)/2-1}(1+\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i}\ell})

\displaystyle\,=\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)/2-1}\frac{1-\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i+1}\ell}}{1-\epsilon_{M}^{2^{i}\ell}}=\frac{1-\epsilon_{M}^{2^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)/2}\ell}}{1-\epsilon_{M}^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\,=\frac{1-\epsilon_{M}^{-\ell}}{1-\epsilon_{M}^{\ell}}=-\epsilon_{M}^{-\ell}

The proof of the proposition is now complete.
∎

Proposition 4.6

Let

1

\displaystyle\prod_{x\in X\cup 2X}\zeta_{M}^{-2x\ell}\prod_{x\in X\cup 2X}(1-\zeta_{M}^{2x\ell})^{2}=\prod_{x\in U_{M}}(1-\zeta_{M}^{2x\ell})=\Phi_{M}(1)=1.

.

By this correspondence and Theorem
2.8
with
F=E_{M}
,
E={\mathbb{Q}}
,
G={\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\sigma_{{\mathfrak{P}}}
above,
\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,\beta}
is
an infinite set if there is
\sigma\in G
satisfying that

\sigma(\zeta_{M})=\zeta_{M}^{h}
,
\sigma^{3}(x_{i,0})=\alpha x_{i,0}
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant M-1
,
\sigma^{3}(\sqrt{2})=-\alpha\sqrt{2}
and

\sigma^{3}(\zeta_{4})=\beta\zeta_{4}
. Thus we need to study the structure of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.

We first list a few basic properties of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
from Galois theory.

Fact 5.1
.

(i)

{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is a normal subgroup of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
since

{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
is a normal extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(ii)

{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is an abelian
2
-group since all the extensions

{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},x_{i,0})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
,
i=0,1,\ldots,M-1
, and

{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
are of degree at most
2
.
In particular,
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is an elementary abelian
2
-group since it is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\prod_{i=0}^{M-1}{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},x_{i,0})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
.

We next use the following fundamental result in Kummer’s theory.
See, e.g.,
[
29
, Chapter XI]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic
0
containing
\zeta_{n}
and
K^{\ast}=K\setminus\{0\}
be the multiplicative group of
K
.
Let
R
be a subgroup of
K^{\ast}
containing
(K^{\ast})^{n}=\{a^{n}\,:\,a\in K^{\ast}\}
, and
K(\sqrt[n]{R})
denote the field extension of
K
obtained by adjoining all elements in
\{\sqrt[n]{a}\,:\,a\in R\}
. Assume that
K(\sqrt[n]{R})/K
is an abelian extension and
R/(K^{\ast})^{n}
is finite. Then
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K(\sqrt[n]{R})/K)
is isomorphic to
R/(K^{\ast})^{n}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
R=\langle 2,-1,1+\zeta_{M}^{i},y\,:\,i=1,2,\ldots,M-1,y\in({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})^{\ast})^{2}\rangle
. Then,
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is isomorphic to
R/({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})^{\ast})^{2}
.

Proof:
Noting that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is abelian, apply Theorem
5.2
with
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
and
n=2
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

It holds that
2\not\in\langle-1,1+\zeta_{M}^{i},y\,:\,i=1,2,\ldots,M-1,y\in({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})^{\ast})^{2}\rangle
.

Proof:
Assume that
2=(-1)^{i_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{M-1}(1+\zeta_{M}^{i})^{c_{i}}\cdot x^{2}
for some
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
. Since
(-1)^{i_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{M-1}(1+\zeta_{M}^{i})^{c_{i}}
is a unit,
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{M}]
and
x
is not a unit in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
.
Thus
(2)
is ramified in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
. On the other hand, it is well known that
(2)
is ramified in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
if and only if
4\,|\,M
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

-1\in\langle 1+\zeta_{M}^{i},y\,:\,i=1,2,\ldots,M-1,y\in({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})^{\ast})^{2}\rangle
if and only if
-1\in\langle 2\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M^{\prime}})
for some divisor
M^{\prime}>1
of
M
.

Proof:
Assume that
-1\not\in\langle 2\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M^{\prime}})
for all divisors
M^{\prime}>1
of
M
, and
-1=\prod_{i=1}^{M-1}(1+\zeta_{M}^{i})^{c_{i}}\cdot x^{2}
for some integers
c_{i}
and
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
.
Let
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})/{\mathbb{Q}})
be defined by
\sigma:\zeta_{M}\mapsto\zeta_{M}^{2}
. Since
{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)
is odd, we have

\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)-1}\sigma^{i}(-1)=-1.

On the other hand, since
\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)-1}(1+\zeta_{M}^{2^{i}\ell})=1
as in the proof of Proposition
4.4
(3),

\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)-1}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\sigma^{i}(1+\zeta_{M}^{j})^{c_{j}}\Big)\sigma^{i}(x)^{2}=\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M}(2)-1}\sigma^{i}(x)^{2}.

Hence,
-1
is a square in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M})
, which is impossible since
M
is odd.

Conversely, if
-1\in\langle 2\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M^{\prime}})
for some divisor
M^{\prime}>1
of
M
, we have
-1=\zeta_{M^{\prime}}\prod_{i=0}^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{M^{\prime}}(2)/2-1}\sigma^{i}(1+\zeta_{M^{\prime}})

as in the proof of Proposition
4.4
(4).
∎

Note that the condition that
-1\not\in\langle 2\rangle\,({\mathrm{mod\,\,}}{M^{\prime}})
for all divisors
M^{\prime}>1
of
M
is equivalent to that the order of
2\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is odd in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
.
Let
D_{M}
be the field obtained by adjoining
all
x_{i,0}
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant M-1
, to
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Furthermore, let
D_{M}^{\prime}
be the field obtained by adjoining
\zeta_{4}
to
D_{M}
.
Summarizing the previous lemmas, we have the following.

Proposition 5.6
.

(1)

{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is isomorphic to

{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
.

(2)

If the order of
2\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is odd in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
,
then
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is isomorphic to

{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
.

Proof:
Note that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is isomorphic to a subgroup of index at most
2
of
{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
. By Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
, we have

|{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))|\not=|{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))|,

which implies that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
.
Similarly,
{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is isomorphic to a subgroup of index at most
2
of
{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt{-1})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
. Then, by Lemmas
5.3
and
5.5
,

{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M},\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
if the order of
2\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is odd in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
. ∎

Let
K
be any subfield of
E_{M}
.
For any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
, let
\sigma_{|K}
denote the restriction of

\sigma
to
K
.

Corollary 5.7
.

(1)

{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
is isomorphic to

{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. In particular, the group embedding
\varphi:{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})\to{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}})
defined by
\varphi(\sigma)=(\sigma_{|{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})},\sigma_{|D_{M}^{\prime}})
gives the isomorphism.

(2)

If the order of
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
is odd, then
{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
is isomorphic to

{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. In particular, the group embedding
\varphi:{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})\to{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
defined by
\varphi(\sigma)=(\sigma_{|{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})},\sigma_{|{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-1})},\sigma_{|D_{M}})
gives the isomorphism.

Proof:
We only give a proof of Part (1) of the corollary. Part (2) can be proved similarly.
As in the proof of Proposition
5.6
, we have

E_{M}\not=D_{M}^{\prime}
, which implies that
{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})\cap D_{M}^{\prime}={\mathbb{Q}}
.
Then, by Galois theory,
\varphi
gives the isomorphism between

{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
and

{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}})
.
∎

The following is our main theorem in this section.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
h
be a positive integer such that
1\leqslant h\leqslant M-1
and
M\,|\,(h^{2}+h+1)
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\Psi_{M,h,1,1}\cup\Psi_{M,h,1,-1}
is an infinite set.

(2)

If the order of
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
is odd, both
\Psi_{M,h,1,1}
and
\Psi_{M,h,1,-1}
are infinite sets.

Proof:
By (
5.1
) and Theorem
2.8
, we will show that there exists a

\sigma\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
\sigma(\zeta_{M})=\zeta_{M}^{h}
,
\sigma^{3}(x_{i,0})=x_{i,0}
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant M-1
,
\sigma^{3}(\sqrt{2})=-\sqrt{2}
(and
\sigma^{3}(\zeta_{4})=\beta\zeta_{4}
for each
\beta\in\{1,-1\}
for the latter statement).

(1) Let
\sigma_{1}\in{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
\sigma_{1}(\sqrt{2})=-\sqrt{2}
.
Since the exponent of
{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{M}))
is
2
,
there is a
\sigma_{2}\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
\sigma_{2}(\zeta_{M})=\zeta_{M}^{h}
and
\sigma_{2}^{3}={\mathrm{id}}
or of order
2
.
If
\sigma_{2}^{3}
is of order
2
, we use
\sigma_{2}^{4}
in the place of
\sigma_{2}
. The newly named
\sigma_{2}
satisfies

\sigma_{2}(\zeta_{M})=\zeta_{M}^{h}
and
\sigma_{2}^{3}={\mathrm{id}}
.
Therefore the element
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\in{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}^{\prime}/{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
has the required property mentioned above.

(2) Let
\sigma_{1}
be the same as in (1) above.
Furthermore, fix
\beta\in\{1,-1\}
and let

\sigma_{2}\in{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}})
be such that
\sigma_{2}(\zeta_{4})=\beta\zeta_{4}
.
Similar to (1),
there is a
\sigma_{3}\in{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
such that
\sigma_{3}(\zeta_{M})=\zeta_{M}^{h}
and
\sigma_{3}^{3}={\mathrm{id}}
.
Therefore the element

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{4})/{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathrm{Gal}}(D_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq{\mathrm{Gal}}(E_{M}/{\mathbb{Q}})
has the required property mentioned above.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:
In order to apply Theorem
2.7
, we require
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
; so we set
\beta=-1
, and consider
\Psi_{M,h,1,-1}
. By Theorem
5.8
(2),

\Psi_{M,h,1,-1}
is an infinite set if the order of
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
is odd. Therefore by applying Theorem
2.7

together with Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
4.3
, the conclusion of the theorem follows. ∎

6.
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we gave a sufficient condition for the existence of strongly regular Cayley graphs with parameters
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)=(p^{6},r(p^{3}+1),-p^{3}+r^{2}+3r,r^{2}+r)
, where
r=(p^{2}-1)M/2
and
M|(p^{2}+p+1)
. In particular, we proved that strongly regular graphs with the above parameters exist for sufficiently large primes
p\in\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
. Further, we considered the question for which
M,h
and
\alpha
,
\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
is an infinite set. In fact, we proved that
\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
is an infinite set if the order of
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
is odd. A few natural questions arise.

Problem 6.1
.

Find other classes of
M
such that
\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
is an infinite set.

Problem 6.2
.

Evaluate the density of
\Psi_{M,h,\alpha,-1}
when it is an infinite set.

We studied these problems in the case where
M
is a prime power with certain extra conditions in the supplementary note
[
33
]
. However, the problems above are fully open in the case where
M
is not a prime power and the order of
2
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/M{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
is even except for the case
M=21
.

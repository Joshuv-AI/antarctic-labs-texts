Correspondence Theory for Modal Fairtlough-Mendler Semantics of Intuitionistic Modal Logic
Zhiguang Zhao

Abstract
We study the correspondence theory of intuitionistic modal logic in modal Fairtlough-Mendler semantics (modal FM semantics) [ 13 ] , which is the intuitionistic modal version of possibility semantics [ 19 ] . We identify the fragment of inductive formulas [ 14 ] in this language and give the algorithm 𝖠𝖫𝖡𝖠 \mathsf{ALBA} [ 7 ] in this semantic setting. There are two major features in the paper: one is that in the expanded modal language, the nominal variables, which are interpreted as atoms in perfect Boolean algebras, complete join-prime elements in perfect distributive lattices and complete join-irreducible elements in perfect lattices, are interpreted as the refined regular open closures of singletons in the present setting, similar to the possibility semantics for classical normal modal logic [ 33 ] ; the other feature is that we do not use conominals or diamond, which restricts the fragment of inductive formulas significantly. We prove the soundness of the 𝖠𝖫𝖡𝖠 \mathsf{ALBA} with respect to modal FM frames and show that the 𝖠𝖫𝖡𝖠 \mathsf{ALBA} succeeds on inductive formulas, similar to existing settings like [ 7 , 33 , 34 ] . Keywords : possibility semantics, Fairtlough-Mendler semantics, correspondence theory, nucleus, complete Heyting algebra, intuitionistic modal logic

Our Methodology.

Our aim is to see if Sahlqvist-type correspondence theory could work for semantics whose algebraic counterpart is based on locales, i.e. complete Heyting algebras, which are not necessarily perfect (where every element is join-generated by complete join-primes).

We study the correspondence theory of intuitionistic modal logic in the modal version of Fairtlough-Mendler semantics, using algorithmic correspondence theory methods, as explained in
[
6
,
7
]
. We define the class of inductive formulas for this semantics as well as the Ackermann Lemma Based Algorithm
\mathsf{ALBA}
which computes the first-order correspondents of inductive formulas.

Following the methodology of
[
33
]
, our semantic analysis of the modal FM frames is base on their dual algebraic structures, which are complete Heyting algebras with complete operators (not necessarily perfect), where complete join-primes are not always available, in contrast to settings like
[
7
]
. We use the representation of complete Heyting algebras as the refined regular open subsets in Fairtlough-Mendler frames, and we identify one Heyting algebra with an operator (HAO) and one Boolean algebra with an operator (BAO) as the dual algebraic structures of a given modal FM frame
\mathbb{F}
: namely, the HAO
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
of refined regular open subsets of
\mathbb{F}
and the BAO
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
of arbitrary subsets of
\mathbb{F}
. Therefore, we can define a natural order-embedding
e:\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}\to\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}

c:\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}\to\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
sending a subset of the domain of
\mathbb{F}
to the smallest refined regular open subset containing it. We will use
c
substantially in the interpretations of the expanded modal language
\mathcal{L}^{+}
of the algorithm
\mathsf{ALBA}
, which will play an important role in the relational semantics and the refined regular open translation of the expanded modal language.

To summarize, the principles for choosing the interpretations of the expanded modal language are the following:

•

The set of possible interpretations of nominals is join-dense in the complete algebra of possible values of propositional variables;

•

The set of possible interpretations of conominals is meet-dense in the complete algebra of possible values of propositional variables (only in settings when this is possible);

•

The interpretation of black connectives should be the adjoints of modalities in the basic propositional language;

•

These interpretations should be expressible in a first-order way.

.

For any modal FM frame

, let the
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}=(\mathsf{RO}_{12}(X),\varnothing,H,\cap,\lor_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}},\to_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}},\Box_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}})

Y\lor_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}Z:=\mathsf{I}_{1}\mathsf{C}_{2}(Y\cup Z)
,
Y\to_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}Z:=\mathsf{I}_{1}((X-Y)\cup Z)
.

.

(cf.
[

For any modal FM frame

,

The essential difference between the correspondence for Kripke semantics for intuitionistic modal logic and the current setting is that the algebra

4.2
The Auxiliary BAO
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}

Consider any modal FM-frame
\mathbb{F}=(X,\leq_{1},\leq_{2},R)
, there is another way of viewing it, namely taking it as a trirelational frame
\mathbb{F}_{3}=(X,\leq_{1},\leq_{2},R)

\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}

The formal definition of the BAO
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
is given as follows:

Definition 4.4
.

For any modal FM-frame
\mathbb{F}=(X,\leq_{1},\leq_{2},R)
, define
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}=(P(X),\varnothing,W,\cap,\cup,-,\Box_{\mathsf{K}},\Box_{\leq_{1}},\Box_{\leq_{2}})

\Box_{\mathsf{K}}(a)=\{w\in W\mid R[w]\subseteq a\}

\Box_{\leq_{i}}(a)=\{w\in W\mid(\forall v\geq_{i}w)(v\in a)\}

Since
\mathsf{RO}_{12}(X)\subseteq P(X)
, we have that

is an order-embedding according to the order of the two algebras. Since in
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
, arbitrary intersections of refined regular open subsets are again refined regular open,

is completely meet-preserving. Notice also that
\Box_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
is the restriction of
\Box_{\mathsf{K}}
to
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
. All these observations can be summarized as follows:

Lemma 4.5
.

e:\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
is a completely meet-preserving order-embedding such that
e\circ\Box_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}=\Box_{\mathsf{K}}\circ e
.

However, since
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
and
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
have different definitions of join,
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
is
not

\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}

We have the following corollary by

e:\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
has a left adjoint

Clearly,

4.3
Interpreting the Additional Symbols

4.3.1
Nominals

Nominals are originally introduced in hybrid logic (see e.g.
[
5
, Chapter 14]
). They are used as special propositional variables and are interpreted as singletons, to refer to a world of the domain. In correspondence theory, nominals are used for computing the minimal valuation of propositional variables so as to eliminate them.

In the literature, nominals are interpreted as atoms (in complete atomic Boolean algebra based settings), completely join-prime elements (in perfect distributive lattice based settings), completely join-irreducible elements (in perfect (non-distributive) lattice based settings), regular open closures of singletons (in the setting of possibility semantics of classical normal modal logic). The common feature is that the selected class of elements join-generates the relevant complete lattices.

Therefore, in our setting, we need to find a subset of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
which join-generates the whole
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
. We take
\mathsf{Nom}(\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}):=\{c(\{x\})\mid x\in X\}
be the set of refined regular open closures of singletons in
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
, which will be shown to be the join-dense set of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
.

atoms

perfect distributive lattices

complete join-primes

perfect general lattices

complete join-irreducibles

For the first equality, it is easy to see that for any

4.3.2
Black Diamond

The black diamond
\vardiamond
comes from tense logic, which is the backward looking diamond “there was a moment where…”. When
\Box
is interpreted on the relation
R
,
\vardiamond
is interpreted on the relation
R^{-1}
. These two connectives has the following property:
\varphi\to\Box\psi
is globally true in a model iff
\vardiamond\varphi\to\psi
is globally true in that model. This property is called the
adjunction property
algebraically. In correspondence theory, we use the black diamond to compute the minimal valuation of a propositional variable, and the adjunction property is exactly what we need. Therefore, our task here is to find the left adjoint of
\Box_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
.

We know from lattice theory that in complete lattices, a map has a left adjoint iff it is completely meet-preserving. Since
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
and
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
are both complete, and
\Box_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}:\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}\to\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
and
\Box_{\mathsf{K}}:\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}\to\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{K}}
are both completely meet-preserving, therefore they both have left adjoints, which are denoted by
\vardiamond_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
and
\vardiamond_{\mathsf{K}}
, respectively.

Lemma 4.8
.

(Folklore.)

\vardiamond_{\mathsf{K}}(X)=R[X]
for any

.

=

\bigwedge_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}\{Z\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}\mid(c\circ\vardiamond_{\mathsf{K}}\circ e)(Y)\leq Z\}

=

In the present section, we will define the

(\forall y\geq_{1}x)(\exists z\geq_{2}y)(\exists z^{\prime}\leq_{1}z)\alpha(z^{\prime})

Definition 5.3

(Refined Regular Open Translation)
.

The regular open translation of a formula in
\mathcal{L}^{+}
into

is given as follows:

ST_{x}(p_{i})

:=

P_{i}x
;

ST_{x}(\bot)

:=

x\neq x
;

ST_{x}(\top)

:=

x=x
;

ST_{x}(\mathbf{i})

:=

\mathsf{RO}^{12}_{x}(i=x)

ST_{x}(\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2})

ST_{x}(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2})

ST(\varphi\leq\psi):=\forall x(ST_{x}(\varphi)\to ST_{x}(\psi))

7
The Algorithm
\mathsf{ALBA}

In the present section, we define the algorithm
\mathsf{ALBA}
which compute the first-order correspondence of the input formula, in the style of
[
7
,
34
]
.

The algorithm
\mathsf{ALBA}
executes in three stages.
\mathsf{ALBA}
receives a formula
\mathsf{Ant}\to\mathsf{Suc}
as input and transforms it into the inequality
\mathsf{Ant}\leq\mathsf{Suc}
.

1.

Preprocessing and First approximation
:

(a)

We apply the following
distribution rules
exhaustively:

•

In
\mathsf{Ant}
, rewrite every subformula of the former form into the latter form:

–

(\beta\lor\gamma)\land\alpha
,
(\beta\land\alpha)\lor(\gamma\land\alpha)

–

\alpha\land(\beta\lor\gamma)
,
(\alpha\land\beta)\lor(\alpha\land\gamma)

•

In
\mathsf{Suc}
, rewrite every subformula of the former form into the latter form:

–

(\beta\land\gamma)\lor\alpha
,
(\beta\lor\alpha)\land(\gamma\lor\alpha)

–

\alpha\lor(\beta\land\gamma)
,
(\alpha\lor\beta)\land(\alpha\lor\gamma)

–

\alpha\to\beta\land\gamma
,
(\alpha\to\beta)\land(\alpha\to\gamma)

–

\Box(\alpha\land\beta)
,
\Box\alpha\land\Box\beta

(b)

Apply the
splitting rules
:

\alpha\leq\beta\ \ \ \alpha\leq\gamma\alpha\leq\beta\land\gamma\qquad\alpha\leq\gamma\ \ \ \beta\leq\gamma\alpha\lor\beta\leq\gamma

Now for each obtained inequality
\varphi_{i}\leq\psi_{i}
, We apply the
first-approximation rule
:

\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\varphi_{i}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\psi_{i}\varphi_{i}\leq\psi_{i}

Now we call each quasi-inequality
\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\varphi_{i}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\psi_{i}
a
system
, and use
\mathsf{S}
to denote a meta-conjunction of inequalities. When
\mathsf{S}
is empty, we denote it as
\varnothing
.

2.

The reduction-elimination cycle
:

In this stage, for each system
\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\varphi_{i}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\psi_{i}
, we apply the following rules to eliminate all the propositional variables:

(a)

Splitting rules
:

(\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\beta)\ \ \ (\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\gamma)\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\beta\land\gamma

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\leq\beta\ \&\ \alpha\leq\gamma\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\leq\beta\land\gamma\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi

(\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\gamma)\ \ \ (\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \beta\leq\gamma)\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\lor\beta\leq\gamma

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\leq\gamma\ \&\ \beta\leq\gamma\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\lor\beta\leq\gamma\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi

(b)

Residuation rules
:

\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \vardiamond\alpha\leq\beta\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\Box\beta

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \vardiamond\alpha\leq\beta\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\leq\Box\beta\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi

\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\land\beta\leq\gamma\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\beta\to\gamma

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\land\beta\leq\gamma\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\leq\beta\to\gamma\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi

(c)

Approximation rule
:

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathbf{i}\leq\varphi\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}\leq\psi\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi

The nominal introduced by the approximation rule must not occur in the system before applying the rule.

(d)

Deleting rules
:

\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \alpha\leq\top\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi

\varnothing\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\top\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \alpha\leq\top

(e)

Right-handed Ackermann rule
. This rule eliminates propositional variables and is the core of the algorithm:

\mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}(\theta/p)\leq\iota_{j}(\theta/p)\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi(\theta/p)\leq\psi(\theta/p)\mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}\leq p\ \&\ \mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}\leq\iota_{j}\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi

where:

i.

p
does not occur in
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
;

ii.

Each
\eta_{i},\psi
is positive, and each
\iota_{i},\varphi
negative in
p
, for
1\leq i\leq m
;

iii.

\theta:=\theta_{1}\lor\ldots\lor\theta_{n}
.

3.

Output
: If in Stage 2, the algorithm gets stuck for some systems, i.e. some propositional variables cannot be eliminated, then the algorithm stops and output “failure”. Otherwise, each initial system after the first approximation rule has been reduced to a set of pure quasi-inequalities Reduce
(\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\varphi_{i}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\psi_{i})
, and then the output is a set of pure quasi-inequalities
\bigcup_{i\in I}
Reduce
(\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\varphi_{i}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\psi_{i})
. Then we can use the conjunction of the refined regular open translations of the quasi-inequalities to obtain the first-order correspondence (notice that in the standard translation of each quasi-inequality, we need to universally quantify over all the individual variables).

Example 7.1
.

Here we give an example. For the sake of clarity we add propositional quantifiers and nominal quantifiers before the quasi-inequality.

\forall p(\Box p\to p)

\forall p(\Box p\leq p)

\forall p\forall\mathbf{i}(\mathbf{i}\leq\Box p\ \Rightarrow\mathbf{i}\leq p)

\forall p\forall\mathbf{i}(\vardiamond\mathbf{i}\leq p\ \Rightarrow\mathbf{i}\leq p)

\forall\mathbf{i}(\mathbf{i}\leq\vardiamond\mathbf{i})

\forall i\forall x(ST_{x}(\mathbf{i})\to ST_{x}(\vardiamond\mathbf{i}))

\forall i\forall x(\mathsf{RO}^{12}_{x}(i=x)\to\mathsf{RO}^{12}_{x}(\exists y(Ryx\land\mathsf{RO}^{12}_{x}(i=y))))

8
Success

In the present section, we show the success of
\mathsf{ALBA}
on any inductive formula
\varphi\to\psi
.

Theorem 8.1
.

\mathsf{ALBA}
succeeds on any inductive formula
\varphi\to\psi
and outputs a set of pure quasi-inequalities and a first-order formula.

Proof.

We check the shape of the inequalitys or systems in each stage, for the input formula
\mathsf{Ant}\to\mathsf{Suc}
:

Stage 1.

After applying the distribution rules, it is easy to see that
\mathsf{Ant}
becomes the form
\bigvee\bigwedge\mathsf{PIA}_{p}
, and
\mathsf{Suc}
becomes of the form
\bigwedge\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}
, where

\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}::=\mathsf{POS}_{\mathsf{Prop}}\mid\mathsf{PIA}_{q}\to\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}\mid\Box\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}.

Then by applying the splitting rules, we get a set of inequalities of the form
\bigwedge\mathsf{PIA}_{p}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}
.

After the first approximation rule, we get systems of the form
\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\bigwedge\mathsf{PIA}_{p}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}
.

Stage 2.

In this stage, we deal with each system
\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\bigwedge\mathsf{PIA}_{p}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}
.

For the inequality
\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\bigwedge\mathsf{PIA}_{p}
, by first applying the splitting rule for
\land
and then exhaustively applying the residuation rules for
\Box
and
\to
, we get inequalities of the form
\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p
or
\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq\top
or
\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq\bot
, where

\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}::=\mathbf{i}_{0}\mid\vardiamond\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\mid\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\land\mathsf{POS}_{A_{p}},

where
A_{p}=\{q\in\mathsf{Prop}\mid q<_{\Omega}p\}
.

Now we deal with the
\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}
part.

•

If the system is of the form
\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\mathsf{PIA}_{q}\to\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}
, then we have the following execution of
\mathsf{ALBA}
:

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\land\mathsf{PIA}_{q}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \&\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}_{0}\land\mathsf{PIA}_{q}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \&\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}_{0}\ \&\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathsf{PIA}_{q}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}

\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \&\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathbf{i}_{0}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}.

•

If the system is of the form
\mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\Box\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}
, then we have the following execution of
\mathsf{ALBA}
:

\mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \Rightarrow\ \vardiamond\mathbf{i}_{0}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}

\mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \&\ \mathbf{j}\leq\vardiamond\mathbf{i}_{0}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathsf{Suc}^{\prime}

Therefore, by the reduction strategies above, we get a quasi-inequality of the form (here
\mathsf{Pure}
is a meta-conjunction of inequalities without propositional variables):

\mathop{\mbox{\Large\&}}(\mathsf{MinVal}_{p}\leq p)\ \&\ \mathsf{Pure}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{j}\leq\mathsf{POS}_{\mathsf{Prop}}.

Now we can apply the right-handed Ackermann rule to an
\Omega
-miminal variable
q
to eliminate it. Since there are only finitely many propositional variables, we can always find another
\Omega
-miminal variable to eliminate. Finally we eliminate all propositional variables and get pure quasi-inequalities and the refined regular open translation.
∎

The equivalences above follow from the fact that the interpretations of
\vardiamond
and
\Box
form an adjunction pair, and the interpretations of
\land
and
\to
form a residuation pair.

For the approximation rule, the soundness proof is similar to the first-approximation rule: for any modal FM frame
\mathbb{F}
, any valuation
V
,

\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\varphi_{i}\leq\psi_{i}

iff

V(\varphi_{i})\subseteq V(\psi_{i})

iff

(\forall Z\in\mathsf{Nom}(\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}})\mbox{ s.t. }Z\subseteq V(\varphi_{i}))(Z\subseteq V(\psi_{i}))

(\forall Z\in\mathsf{Nom}(\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}})(Z\subseteq V(\varphi_{i})\ \Rightarrow\ Z\subseteq V(\psi_{i}))
.

Therefore, if
\mathbb{F}\Vdash\mathsf{S}\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi_{i}\leq\psi_{i}
, then for any
V
, if
\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\mathsf{S}
and
V(\mathbf{i})\subseteq V(\varphi_{i})
, then
V(\varphi_{i})\subseteq V(\psi_{i})

\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\mathbf{i}\leq\psi_{i}

\mathbb{F}\Vdash\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathbf{i}\leq\varphi_{i}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}\leq\psi_{i}
.

If
\mathbb{F}\Vdash\mathsf{S}\ \&\ \mathbf{i}\leq\varphi_{i}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{i}\leq\psi_{i}

V

Proposition 9.4
.

The right-handed Ackermann rule is sound in
\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume that
n=1
and
m=1
. Then it suffices to show the following equivalence:

•

\mathbb{F}\Vdash\theta\leq p\ \&\ \eta\leq\iota\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi
;

•

\mathbb{F}\Vdash\eta(\theta/p)\leq\iota(\theta/p)\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi(\theta/p)\leq\psi(\theta/p)
.

\Downarrow
: Assume that
\mathbb{F}\Vdash\theta\leq p\ \&\ \eta\leq\iota\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi.
Then for any valuation
V
on
\mathbb{F}
, if
\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\eta(\theta/p)\leq\iota(\theta/p),
then take
V^{\prime}=V^{p}_{V(\theta)}
, then since
p
does not occur in
\theta
, we have
V^{\prime}(\theta)=V(\theta)=V^{\prime}(p),
therefore
V(\eta(\theta/p))=V^{\prime}(\eta(\theta/p))=V^{\prime}(\eta),
similarly
V(\iota(\theta/p))=V^{\prime}(\iota),
so from
\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\eta(\theta/p)\leq\iota(\theta/p)
we get
V^{\prime}(\eta)\subseteq V^{\prime}(\iota).
Therefore
\mathbb{F},V^{\prime}\Vdash\varphi\leq\psi.
Therefore
V^{\prime}(\varphi)\subseteq V^{\prime}(\psi)
. Similar to
\eta
and
\iota
we get
V^{\prime}(\varphi)=V(\varphi(\theta/p))
and
V^{\prime}(\psi)=V(\psi(\theta/p)),
so
\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\varphi(\theta/p)\leq\psi(\theta/p).
By the arbitrariness of
V
we get
\mathbb{F}\Vdash\eta(\theta/p)\leq\iota(\theta/p)\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi(\theta/p)\leq\psi(\theta/p).

\Uparrow
: Assume
\mathbb{F}\Vdash\eta(\theta/p)\leq\iota(\theta/p)\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi(\theta/p)\leq\psi(\theta/p).
Then for any valuation
V
on
\mathbb{F}
, if
\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\theta\leq p\ \&\ \eta\leq\iota,
then
V(\theta)\subseteq V(p),V(\eta)\subseteq V(\iota).
Therefore by the polarity of
p
in
\eta(\theta/p)
and
\iota(\theta/p)
we have that
V(\eta(\theta/p))\subseteq V(\eta)\subseteq V(\iota)\subseteq V(\iota(\theta/p)).
So from
\mathbb{F}\Vdash\eta(\theta/p)\leq\iota(\theta/p)\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi(\theta/p)\leq\psi(\theta/p)
we get
V(\varphi)\subseteq V(\varphi(\theta/p))\subseteq V(\psi(\theta/p))\subseteq V(\psi),
so
\mathbb{F},V\Vdash\varphi\leq\psi
. Therefore by the arbitrariness of
V
we get
\mathbb{F}\Vdash\theta\leq p\ \&\ \eta\leq\iota\ \Rightarrow\ \varphi\leq\psi.

10
Conclusions

In this paper, we study the correspondence theory of intuitionistic modal logic in modal Fairtlough-Mendler semantics, which is the intuitionistic modal counterpart of possibility semantics. Our study can be regarded as the study of correspondence theory for complete Heyting algebras with complete operators which are not necessarily perfect.

We proposed the general principles to choose the interpretations of the expanded modal language used in the algorithm
\mathsf{ALBA}
, and apply it in the current setting. Therefore, we interpret the nominals in our setting as the refined regular open closures of singletons, which is join-dense in
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
and can be expressed in a first-order way.

For future directions, we mention the following:

•

In the present paper, we use a join-dense set of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
to interpret the nominals. Whether a meet-dense set of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathsf{RO}_{12}}
which can be expressed in a first-order way can be found is a question related to whether we can expand the fragment of inductive formulas here, which is also related to finding correspondents of the
\mathsf{KC}
formula
\neg p\lor\neg\neg p
and the
\mathsf{LC}
formula
(p\to q)\lor(q\to p)
in the present semantic setting.

•

In the present paper, we only have
\Box
as the modality, since it interacts well with arbitrary intersection. A future direction is to find a way to incorporate
\Diamond
into the picture.

•

In the present paper, we consider complete Heyting algebra expansions, therefore it is natural to also consider the correspondence theory of complete lattice expansions which are not necessarily perfect.

•

In the present paper, we do not consider the canonicity and completeness concepts related to the intuitionistic modal version of possibility semantics, which is a future direction.

Introduction

In this paper we consider a class of nonvariational degenerate elliptic
operators of the kind

Lu=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{ij}\left(x\right)X_{i}X_{j}u

(1.1)

where
\left\{a_{ij}\left(x\right)\right\}_{i,j=1}^{m}
is a symmetric
uniformly positive matrix of bounded measurable functions defined in the whole

\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n>m
), possibly discontinuos but satisfying a
VMO

assumption, and
X_{1},...,X_{m}
are real smooth vector fields satisfying
Hörmander rank condition in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1
-homogeneous
w.r.t. a family of nonisotropic dilations. (Precise definition will be given
later). We
do not
assume that the vector fields are left invariant
w.r.t. an underlying Lie group of translations, so our context is more general
than that of Hörmander vector fields on Carnot groups. Our first main
result consists in proving global
W_{X}^{2,p}
a-priori estimates, for any

p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
, for these operators (see Theorem

1.4
for the exact statement). We also extend our result to higher
order derivatives, proving that if
u\in W_{X}^{2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and, for some positive integer
k
, the coefficients
a_{ij}

belong to
W_{X}^{k,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
Lu\in W_{X}^{k,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, then
u\in W_{X}^{k+2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and the corresponding global a priori estimate holds:

\|u\|_{W_{X}^{k+2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\left\{\|Lu\|_{W_{X}^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right\}.

For Hörmander operators, global results are usually known in the case of
Carnot groups while, in the general case, only local results are usually
known. The context of homogeneous (but not left invariant) Hörmander
vector fields represents an interesting intermediate case in which,
notwithstanding the lacking of a group structure, very interesting global
results can be established. The study of (stationary and evolutive) operators
built with homogeneous Hörmander vector fields has been started in recent
years by Biagi and Bonfiglioli in
[
1
]
and continued e.g. in

[
2
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,

]
.

Regularity results and a-priori estimates in the scale of Sobolev spaces have
been proved:

-

for Hörmander operators on groups, in local form, by Folland in

[
19
]
; for sum of squares of general Hörmander vector fields, in
local form, by Rothschild-Stein in
[
23
]
; for sum of squares of
Hörmander vector fields on Carnot groups, in global form, by
Bramanti-Brandolini in
[
8

; for sum of squares of

;

) with
VMO

[

; without the Carnot group assumption, by Bramanti-Brandolini in

[

(and, with more complete results, in
[
8

).

The study of nonvariational operators with
VMO
coefficients has been
initiated in 1991-93 by Chiarenza-Frasca-Longo
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
for
uniformly elliptic operators. The classical
W^{2,p}
-theory for uniformly
elliptic operators requires the uniform continuity of the coefficients

a_{ij}
, and it is well-known that for nonvariational uniformly elliptic
operators with general bounded measurable coefficients, in dimension
n>2
,
the
W^{2,p}
-theory breaks down. The
VMO
requirement is a kind of
“uniform continuity in integral sense” which actually allows for some soft kind of discontinuities, hence
Chiarenza-Frasca-Longo’s results represent a significant extension of the
classical elliptic theory of the 1950-1960’s. The techique firstly devised by
Chiarenza-Frasca-Longo has been later applied to uniformly parabolic equations
(see
[
10
]
), Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck ultraparabolic equations (see

[
11
]
), and to nonvariational operators built on Hörmander vector
fields, as already explained. Chiarenza-Frasca-Longo’s technique heavily
relies on representation formulas of
u_{x_{i}x_{j}}
in terms of
Lu
, via a
singular kernel “with variable coefficients”, obtained from the fundamental solution of the model operator with constant
coefficients. This singular kernel needs to be expanded in series of spherical
harmonics, getting singular kernels “with constant
coefficients”. To these singular integrals “of convolution type” one needs to apply results of
L^{p}

continuity of both the corresponding singular integral operator and the
commutator of the singular integral operator with a
BMO
function. This
technique exploits both translation invariance and homogeneity (in suitable
senses) of the model operator with constant
a_{ij}
or, in some cases, the
possibility of approximating locally the operator under study with another one
possessing these properties. Moreover, it relies on the knowledge of fine
properties of the fundamental solution of the constant coefficient operator,
with bounds on the derivatives of every order of this fundamental solution,
which have to be uniform as the constant matrix
\left(a_{ij}\right)

ranges in a fixed ellipticity class.

A different technique to prove
W^{2,p}
a-priori estimates for nonvariational
elliptic or parabolic operators with
VMO
coefficients has been devised in
2007 by Krylov in
[
20
]
(and exploited in a series of subsequent papers). A
key step in Krylov’ technique constists in establishing a pointwise estimate
on the sharp maximal function of the second derivatives of
u
,
\left(u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\right)^{\#}
, in terms of
\overline{L}u
where
\overline{L}
is the model operator with constant
a_{ij}
. Once this estimate is
proved, a clever procedure allows to exploit the
VMO
assumption on
a_{ij}

for replacing the constant coefficient operator with
L
. In turn, in order to
establish this estimate on
\left(u_{x_{i}x_{j}}\right)^{\#}
, Krylov
makes use of many deep classical results on elliptic or parabolic operators
with constant coefficients. The extension of this part of Krylov’ technique to
degenerate operators of Hörmander type seems very difficult. A first
positive result in this direction has been obtained by Bramanti-Toschi in

[
14
]
in the case of Carnot groups. More recently, Dong and Yastrzhembskiy

[
18
]
have successfully employed this technique for (degenerate) kinetic
Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck operators.

In this paper we present a new approach which combines some ideas of both the
strategies described above and shows some advantages. We follow Krylov in the
idea of exploiting an estimate on the sharp maximal function of the second
order derivatives (in terms of the constant coefficient operator) to prove

L^{p}
estimates for the operator with
VMO
coefficients (see Theorem

3.9
). But, in order to prove this bound on the sharp
maximal function, we do not follow Krylov’ technique but use representation
formulas for second order derivatives in terms of the
constant
coefficient operator
(so that the singular kernel is not a “variable kernel” in the sense of Calderón-Zygmund
theory) and make use of techniques of singular integrals in spaces of
homogeneous type. The link between the two ingredients of our proof (singular
integral operators
and
sharp maximal function) is contained in our
Theorem
3.10
, an abstract real analysis result in spaces of
homogeneous type, which in our opinion can be of independent interest. This
technique, as usual, allows to prove, at first, a-priori estimates for smooth
functions with
small
support. Passing from this result to global
a-priori estimates for any
W_{X}^{2,p}
-function requires the use of suitable
cutoff functions, a covering theorem, and interpolation inequalities for first
order derivatives. These properties are not trivial but, in our setting, the
presence of dilations, the validity of a global doubling property, and some
known properties of the measure of metric balls, allow to prove them in a
relatively direct way.

Our technique, as a whole, seems more flexible than both the techniques to
which it is inspired, and in some sense it is easier than those. For instance,
one could use the technique in this paper to reobtain the main result in

[
15
]
with a shorter proof.

The results in this paper represent the first instance of a complete
regularity theory in Sobolev spaces for nonvariational operators built on
Hörmander vector fields, with global bounds in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let us now come to precise definitions and assumptions.

Let us consider a family
X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
of
homogeneous
Hörmander vector fields on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This means that the
following conditions (H.1), (H.2), (H.3) hold:

(H.1)

there exists a family of (non-isotropic) dilations

\{\delta_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
of the form

\delta_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\qquad\delta_{\lambda}(x)=\left(\lambda^{\sigma_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\sigma_{n}}x_{n}\right),

(1.2)

where
1=\sigma_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\sigma_{n}
, such that
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}

are
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
1
, i.e.,

X_{j}\left(f\circ\delta_{\lambda}\right)=\lambda\,\left(X_{j}f\right)\circ\delta_{\lambda},\quad\text{for every $\lambda>0$, $f\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)$ and $j=1,\ldots,m$.}

In what follows, we denote by

q=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{j}

(1.3)

the so-called
\delta_{\lambda}
-homogeneous dimension of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\delta_{\lambda})
.

(H.2)

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
are linearly independent (as vector
fields) and satisfy Hörmander’s rank condition at
0
, i.e.,

\dim\left\{Y\left(0\right):Y\in\mathrm{Lie}\left(X\right)\right\}=n.

Here
\mathrm{Lie}(X)
stands for the smallest Lie subalgebra of

\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
containing
X
, where
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Lie algebra of all the smooth vector fields on

\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Finally, we make the following dimensional assumptions:

(H.3)

we require that
q>2
and

N:=\mathrm{dim}\left(\mathrm{Lie}\left\{X\right\}\right)>n\geq 2.

(1.4)

Throughout the paper we let
p:=N-n\geq 1
and we denote the points of

\mathbb{R}^{N}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
by

(x,\xi),\quad\text{with }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{p}$.}

We stress that the vector fields
X_{i}
are
not
assumed to be
translation invariant with respect to any underlying structure of Lie group,
so that our context is different from that of Carnot groups.

Remark 1.1

Assumption (H.3) is not restrictive for the following reason. As noted in

[
5
, Section 2]
, if (H.3) does not hold, that is
N=n
, then our vector
fields
X_{1},...,X_{m}
are the generators of a Carnot group. Under this
assumption, the main results in this paper, although not explicitly present in
the literature, could be plainly obtained combining the techniques in

[
8
, Chap. 8]
and
[
8
, Chap. 12.]
. Instead, under our
assumption (H.3), the vector fields
X_{1},...,X_{m}

cannot
be the
generators of a Carnot group (again, this is noted in
[
5
, Section 2]
).
Hence the proofs written in this paper cannot be directly specialized to the
case of Carnot groups; however it will be apparent that in the case of Carnot
groups the proof of the present results would be easier, following the same line.

In this paper we are going to study
nonvariational second order
operators
(
1.1
) built with a family of vector fields

X_{1},...,X_{m}
satisfying (H.1)-(H.3), where:

(H.4)

\left\{a_{ij}\right\}_{i,j=1}^{m}
is a symmetric
uniformly positive matrix of bounded measurable functions: there exists

\nu>0

\nu\left|w\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{ij}\left(x\right)w_{i}w_{j}\leq\nu^{-1}\left|w\right|^{2}\text{ for every }w\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.5)

Moreover, we will assume that the coefficients
a_{ij}
, although possibly
discontinuous, belong to
VMO\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, the space of
functions with
vanishing mean oscillation
, with respect to the
structure of
space of homogeneous type
induced in

by

For these operators, we are interested in proving global

-estimates (for

in

Lu

Let us first introduce the Sobolev spaces related to our system of
Hörmander vector fields, fixing the related notation (see

Definition 1.2
(Multiindices, Sobolev spaces)

Let
X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
be as above. For any multiindex
I=\left(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{k}\right)
(with
i_{j}\in\left\{1,2,...,m\right\}
), we say
that
I
has
length
k
, we write
\left|I\right|=k
, and set

X_{I}f=X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}...X_{i_{k}}f

for any function
f
such that these derivatives exist in classical or weak sense.

For any
p\in\left[1,\infty\right]
, positive integer
k
, and domain

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the Sobolev space

W_{X}^{k,p}\left(\Omega\right)=\left\{f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\left\|f\right\|_{W_{X}^{k,p}\left(\Omega\right)}<\infty\right\}

where

\left\|f\right\|_{W_{X}^{k,p}\left(\Omega\right)}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)}+\sum_{\left|I\right|\leq k}\left\|X_{I}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\Omega\right)}

and all the derivatives are meant in weak sense.

We also define
W_{0,X}^{k,p}\left(\Omega\right)
as the closure of

C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
in
W_{X}^{k,p}\left(\Omega\right)
.

Let us recall some known approximation result by smooth functions, which holds
for Sobolev spaces defined by any system of Hörmander vector fields:

Theorem 1.3

(See
[
8
, Thm. 2.9, Coroll. 2.10]
). Let

for some open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, some
p\in[1,\infty)
and some positive integer
k
.

(a). If
\Omega^{\prime}
is an open set compactly contained in
\Omega
, then
there exists a sequence
\left\{u_{j}\right\}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
such that
u_{j}\rightarrow u
in
W_{X}^{k,p}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)
.

(b) If
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
then
u\phi\in W_{0,X}^{k,p}\left(\Omega\right)
.

(1.6)

2.1
Geometry of vector fields and real analysis
tools

Let us start with a couple of abstract definitions.

Definition 2.1
(Quasidistance)

Let
X
be a set. We say that a function
d:X\times X\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a
quasidistance
in
X
if there
exists a constant
C_{d}\geq 1
such that, for every
x,y,z\in X
:

(i)
d\left(x,y\right)=0\Longleftrightarrow x=y

(ii)
d\left(x,y\right)=d\left(y,x\right)

(iii)
d\left(x,y\right)\leq C_{d}\left(d\left(x,z\right)+d\left(z,y\right)\right)

In this case we also say that
\left(X,d\right)
is a
quasimetric
space
.

If
\left(X,d\right)
is a quasimetric space, the
d
-balls, defined, for

x\in X
and
r>0
as

B_{r}\left(x\right)=\left\{y\in X:d\left(x,y\right)<r\right\},

induce a topology.

Definition 2.2
(Space of homogeneous type)

Let
\left(X,d\right)
be a quasimetric space, and assume that:

(a) the
d
-balls are open w.r.t. the topology they induce;

(b) there exists a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
X
, satisfying the
doubling property: there exists a constant
C_{\mu}\geq 1
such that

0<\mu\left(B_{2r}\left(x\right)\right)\leq C_{\mu}\cdot\mu\left(B_{r}\left(x\right)\right)<\infty

for every

.

Then we say that
\left(X,d,\mu\right)
is a

type
, in the sense of Coifman-Weiss
[
17
]
. The constants

in (b)
and

in (iii) of the previous Definition are called the constants of

X
.

Let

(2.1)

\gamma(0)=x
,
\gamma(1)=y

(a.e. on

Given any

. Without risk of confusion,

\mathbb{R}^{n}

The construction of the fundamental solution of

performed in

Theorem 2.12

(See

(2.16)

(2.17)

\left|B_{\widetilde{X}}(z,r)\right|=\omega_{Q}\,r^{Q},\quad\text{where $\omega_{Q}=\left|B_{\widetilde{X}}(0,1)\right|$}.

\left(ii\right)

\left(iii\right)

2.3
Cutoff functions and coverings of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

A tool that we will need in the following is a family of cutoff functions
adapted to balls of fixed radius and centered anywhere in the space,
satisfying uniform bounds w.r.t. the center of the ball. Since we have at our
disposal dilations but not translations adapted to the vector fields, building
these functions is not a trivial task. We need to recall the following deep
known result on the volume of metric balls. It is a global version of a famous
result by Nagel-Stein-Weinger

the homogeneity of vector fields.

Theorem 2.17

(See
[
2
, Thm. C]

Proposition 2.19
(bounded overlapping)

Let
\left(X,d,\mu\right)
be
a space of homogeneous type. For every
R>0
there exists a family of balls

\mathcal{B=}\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},R\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A}

such that:

(i)
{\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{{}_{\alpha\in A}}}B\left(x_{\alpha},R\right)=X

(ii) for every
H>1
, the family of dilated balls

\mathcal{B}^{H}=\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},HR\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A}

has the bounded overlapping property, that is: there exists a constant
N

(depending on
H
and the constants of
X
, but independent of
R
) such that
every point of
X
belongs to at most
N
balls
B\left(x_{\alpha},HR\right)
.

Proof.
To simplify the notation, this proof is written assuming that
d
is a
distance. If
d
is a general quasidistance, some inessential changes have to
be made in the constants.

For a fixed number
r>0
, let
\mathcal{F}_{r}
be the collection of all the
families of pairwise disjoint balls of radius
r
in
X
. The set

\mathcal{F}_{r}
is partially ordered by the set inclusion, hence by Zorn’s
lemma it possesses a maximal element. This means that there exists a family

\mathcal{B}_{0}\mathcal{=}\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},r\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A}
of pairwise disjoint balls in
X
, such that for every
x\in X
the ball
B\left(x,r\right)
intersects at least one ball
B\left(x_{\alpha},r\right)
. Therefore every
x\in X
has distance
<2r
from some

x_{\alpha}
, so that, taking
r=R/2
,

\mathcal{B}=\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},R\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A}

is a covering of
X
.

Now, fix a number
H>1
and for any
x\in X
let us compute an upper bound for
the number
N_{x}
of balls
\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},HR\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A_{x}}
containing
x
(with
A_{x}\subset A
). For every ball

B\left(x_{\alpha},HR\right)
of this family we have, by the triangle
inequality:

B\left(x_{\alpha},HR\right)\subset B\left(x,2HR\right)\subset B\left(x_{\alpha},3HR\right).

Hence

B\left(x,2HR\right)\supset{\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in A_{x}}}B\left(x_{\alpha},HR\right)\supset{\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{\alpha\in A_{x}}}B\left(x_{\alpha},R/2\right)

and since the balls
B\left(x_{\alpha},R/2\right)
are pairwise disjoint,

\mu\left(B\left(x,2HR\right)\right)\geq{\textstyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\in A_{x}}}\mu\left(B\left(x_{\alpha},R/2\right)\right)

by the doubling condition

\geq c\left(\mu,H\right){\textstyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\in A_{x}}}\mu\left(B\left(x_{\alpha},3HR\right)\right)\geq c\left(\mu,H\right){\textstyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\in A_{x}}}\mu\left(B\left(x,2HR\right)\right).

We have proved that

c\left(\mu,H\right){\textstyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\in A_{x}}}\mu\left(B\left(x,2HR\right)\right)\leq\mu\left(B\left(x,2HR\right)\right)<\infty

so we conclude that
\operatorname{card}\left(A_{x}\right)=N_{x}
is
finite and

N_{x}\leq\frac{1}{c\left(\mu,H\right)},

independently from
x
and
R.

3
Operators with constant coefficients

Throughout this section we keep considering operators (
1.1
) with

constant
coefficients
a_{ij}
, satisfying assumptions (H.1)-(H.4),
that is:

L_{A}u=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}u.

Our aim is twofold:

(1) to prove a representation formula for
X_{i}X_{j}u
in terms of
L_{A}u
,
holding true for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
involving a suitable singular integral operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
;

(2) to prove
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
continuity of this
singular integral operator, obtaining
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

estimates for
X_{i}X_{j}u
, with constant depending on the coefficients

a_{ij}
only through the number
\nu
appearing in (
1.5
).

We wish to stress that, in view of the overall strategy of this paper, points
(1)-(2) are necessary steps. If we were interested just in the validity of

L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
estimates for
X_{i}X_{j}u
in terms of

L_{A}u
, we could derive them almost immediately from the result in

[
3
]
. But this would not allow to extend the result to variable
coefficient operators.

3.1
Representation formulas for
X_{i}X_{j}u

The next result contains a first form of the representation formula for second
derivatives
X_{i}X_{j}u
.

Theorem 3.1

For

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)\geq\varepsilon}f\left(y\right)\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)d\eta dy+c_{ij}^{A}f\left(x\right)

For the proof we need the following know result:

.

\displaystyle A_{1,2}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}<\left\|v\right\|<1}\left[\omega\left(v\right)\varepsilon^{-Q}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\right)\left(v\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\omega\right)\left(v\right)\varepsilon^{1-Q}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\left(v\right)\right]\varepsilon^{Q}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}<\left\|v\right\|<1}\left[\omega\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}+\widetilde{X}_{i}\omega\cdot\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\right]\left(v\right)dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}<\left\|v\right\|<1}\widetilde{X}_{i}\left(\omega\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\right)\left(v\right)dv=A_{1,2},

that is, the term
A_{1,2}^{\varepsilon}
is actually independent of

\varepsilon.
Hence we have proved (
3.1
) with
c_{ij}=A_{1,2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|c_{ij}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{1}{2}<\left\|v\right\|<1}\left|\widetilde{X}_{i}\omega\right|\left|\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\right|dv+\int_{\frac{1}{2}<\left\|v\right\|<1}\left|\omega\right|\left|\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\right|dv

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\int_{\frac{1}{2}<\left\|u\right\|<1}\frac{1}{\left\|u\right\|^{Q-1}}dv+\int_{\frac{1}{2}<\left\|u\right\|<1}\frac{1}{\left\|u\right\|^{Q}}dv\right\}=c^{\prime},

where
c^{\prime}
depends on the matrix
A
only through the number
\nu.
In
the last inequality, we have exploited the estimates (uniform w.r.t. the
matrix
A
)

\left|\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\left(u\right)\right|\leq\frac{c}{\left\|u\right\|^{Q-1}};\left|\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\left(u\right)\right|\leq\frac{c}{\left\|u\right\|^{Q}}

which are actually contained in the
proof
of Theorem
2.14
, point (ii).

The value of the constant
A_{1,2}
can be rewritten more transparently if we
apply the divergence theorem on the (smooth) ellipsoidal shell
\frac{1}{2}<\left\|u\right\|<1
, and exploit the fact that
\omega\left(v\right)=1
for
\left\|v\right\|=1
and
\omega\left(v\right)=0
for
\left\|v\right\|=1/2
, which shows that the constant is also
independent from the cutoff function
\omega
. Writing

\widetilde{X}_{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}a_{il}\left(w\right)\partial_{w_{l}}

and recalling that, by the structure of homogeneous vector fields (see

[
3
, §1]
),

\widetilde{X}_{i}f\left(w\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\partial_{w_{l}}\left(a_{il}f\right)\left(w\right),

we have

A_{1,2}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\int_{\left\|u\right\|=1}\left(a_{il}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\right)\left(v\right)\nu_{l}d\sigma\left(v\right)=\int_{\left\|u\right\|=1}\left(\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\right)\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\cdot\nu\right)d\sigma\left(v\right)

where
\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\cdot\nu\right)\
is the scalar product
between
\widetilde{X}_{i}
and outer normal to
\left\{v:\left\|v\right\|=1\right\}
.

Next, we need to rewrite the representation formula for second order
derivatives in terms of some singular integral living in the space

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, involving the singular kernel

K_{A}\left(x,y\right)=X_{i}^{x}X_{j}^{x}\Gamma_{A}\left(x;y\right),

(3.4)

whose basic properties have been stated in Proposition

2.15
. To this aim we need to define, in an unusual way, a

smoothed version
of
K_{A}
.

Let
\psi_{\varepsilon,R}
be a family of radial cutoff function in the group

\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\left(u\right)=\phi_{\varepsilon,R}\left(d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(u\right)\right)

(3.5)

where
\phi_{\varepsilon,R}
is a compactly supported piecewise linear
function such that

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon,R}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=1\text{ for }2\varepsilon\leq t\leq R

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon,R}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=0\text{ for }t\leq\varepsilon\text{
and for }t\geq 2R.

(This time it is not necessary to build the cutoff function using a smooth
homogeneous norm, like in the proof of Theorem
3.1
,
because we will not need to differentiate
\psi_{\varepsilon,R}
). Let

K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)d\eta

(3.6)

(note that
K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}
is not just
K_{A}
times the cutoff
function, but is built saturating
\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}
times a cutoff function).

Note that

\displaystyle d\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle>2R\Longrightarrow d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)>2R\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)=0\Longrightarrow K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)=0.

Instead,

d\left(x,y\right)<\varepsilon\text{ does \emph{not} imply }\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)=0,

so the kernel
K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)
does not

vanish
near the diagonal. Nevertheless, it is absolutely integrable
w.r.t.
x
, because:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)\right|dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)\right|d\eta dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left(u\right)\right|du\leq c\int_{\varepsilon<d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(u\right)<2R}\frac{du}{d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(u\right)^{Q}}=c\log\left(\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\right).

(3.7)

We can now prove the desired representation formula:

Theorem 3.3

For every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
the following representation formula holds, for
i,j=1,2,...,m

\displaystyle X_{i}X_{j}u\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)L_{A}u\left(y\right)dy+c_{ij}^{A}L_{A}u\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\equiv-\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(L_{A}u\right)\left(x\right)+c_{ij}^{A}L_{A}u\left(x\right),

where the limit exists locally uniformly. More generally, for every
f\in C_{0}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
the limit

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(f\right)\left(x\right)

exists, locally uniformly. The constants
c_{ij}^{A}
are those appearing in
Theorem
3.1
.

Proof.
We start writing, by (
2.14
)

u\left(x\right)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Gamma_{A}\left(x;y\right)L_{A}u\left(y\right)dy.

Then, by Theorem
3.1
we have:

\displaystyle X_{i}X_{j}u\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)\geq\varepsilon}L_{A}u\left(y\right)\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)d\eta dy

\displaystyle+c_{ij}^{A}L_{A}u\left(x\right).

To simplify notation, from now on, throughout this proof, we will drop the
sub- or super-script
A
, simply writing
L,\widetilde{\Gamma},c_{ij}
instead
of
L_{A},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A},c_{ij}^{A}.

Let us consider the operator:

Tf\left(x\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)\geq\varepsilon}\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)f\left(y\right)d\eta dy

defined, as we have seen from the proof of the representation formula, for

f\in C_{0}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for any
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
We have proved that this limit is uniform in
x
. We have to show that

Tf\left(x\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}\left(f\right)\left(x\right).

Let
\psi_{\varepsilon,R}
be the cutoff function defined as in
(
3.5
), for
R>\varepsilon>0
, and let

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}f\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)\geq\varepsilon}\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)f\left(y\right)d\eta dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)f\left(y\right)d\eta dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)\geq\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)f\left(y\right)d\eta dy

\displaystyle\equiv T_{\varepsilon,R}f\left(x\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{\varepsilon\leq\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)<2\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)f\left(y\right)d\eta dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)>R}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)f\left(y\right)d\eta dy

\displaystyle\equiv T_{\varepsilon,R}f\left(x\right)+S_{\varepsilon}^{1}f\left(x\right)+S_{R}^{2}f\left(x\right).

Now, we exploit the vanishing property in Proposition

2.16
:

\int_{r_{1}\leq d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(u\right)<r_{2}}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\right)\left(u\right)du=0.

Therefore, for every
f\in C_{0}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
we
can write:

\displaystyle\left|S_{\varepsilon}^{1}f\left(x\right)\right|=\left|\int_{\varepsilon\leq\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)<2\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)f\left(y\right)d\eta dy\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\varepsilon\leq\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)<2\varepsilon}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\left(1-\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)\left[f\left(y\right)-f\left(x\right)\right]d\eta dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\varepsilon\leq\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)<2\varepsilon}\frac{c}{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)^{Q}}\left|f\right|_{\alpha}d\left(x,y\right)^{\alpha}dyd\eta

\displaystyle\leq c\left|f\right|_{\alpha}\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)<2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)^{Q-\alpha}}dyd\eta\leq c\left|f\right|_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\rightarrow 0

so that

S_{\varepsilon}^{1}f\left(x\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ uniformly in }x\text{
as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

As to
S_{R}^{2}f
, we can write

\displaystyle\left|S_{R}^{2}f\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)>R}\frac{c}{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)^{Q}}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|d\eta dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\int_{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)>R}\frac{\left|f\left(y\right)\right|}{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)^{Q-1}}d\eta dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{d\eta}{\widetilde{d}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)^{Q-1}}\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|\frac{d\left(x,y\right)}{\left|B\left(x,d\left(x,y\right)\right)\right|}dy.

If
f
is compactly supported in
B\left(0,r_{1}\right)
and
x\in B\left(0,r_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\left|S_{R}^{2}f\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\int_{B\left(x,r_{1}+r_{2}\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|\frac{d\left(x,y\right)}{\left|B\left(x,d\left(x,y\right)\right)\right|}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\int_{d\left(x,y\right)<r_{1}+r_{2}}\frac{d\left(x,y\right)}{\left|B\left(x,d\left(x,y\right)\right)\right|}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R}\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\left(r_{1}+r_{2}\right)

hence
\left|S_{R}^{2}f\left(x\right)\right|\rightarrow 0

locally uniformly as
R\rightarrow\infty
. Hence:

S_{\varepsilon}f\left(x\right)=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}f\left(x\right)+S_{\varepsilon}^{1}f\left(x\right)

and

Tf\left(x\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}S_{\varepsilon}f\left(x\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}f\left(x\right),

where the operator
Tf\
is well defined for every Hölder continuous
compactly supported
f
, and is also equal to

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}\left(f\right)\left(x\right)

for the same class of functions
f
, where the limit exists locally uniformly
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.2
L^{p}
estimates for the constant coefficient operator

The aim of this section is to prove the following

Theorem 3.4
(
L^{p}
estimates for
L_{A}
)

Keeping the assumptions
stated at the beginning of section
3
, for every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
there exists a constant
c>0
, depending on
A
only
through the number
\nu
in (

), such that

\left\|X_{i}X_{j}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq c\left\|L_{A}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Moreover, the operator
T
defined as

Tf\left(x\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}\left(f\right)\left(x\right)

for
f\in C_{0}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, can be extended to a
linear continuous operator on
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, with
norm bounded by the same constant
c
.

To prove the above result, we will show the following:

Theorem 3.5

Under the same assumptions of the previous
theorem, for every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
there exists a constant

c>0
, depending on
A
only through the number
\nu
in (
1.5
),
such that

\left\|T_{\varepsilon,R}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

for every
f\in C_{0}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
(and, by
density, every
f\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
), every

R>\varepsilon>0
(and
c
independent of
R,\varepsilon
).

Let us first show how Theorem
3.4
follows from Theorem

3.5
.

By Fatou’s Lemma we have, for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
1<p<\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left|T_{\varepsilon,R}\left(L_{A}u\right)\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|T_{\varepsilon,R}\left(L_{A}u\right)\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\leq c\left\|L_{A}u\right\|_{p}^{p},

hence by the representation formula in Theorem
3.3
, and
keeping into account the uniform bound on the constants

in

3.1
, we conclude

\left\|X_{i}X_{j}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq c\left\|L_{A}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

for every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
and
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, with constant
c
depending on
A
only through the
number
\nu
in (
1.5
).

We can also conclude that, since
C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is dense in
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, the operator
T
, for
which we know that

Tf\left(x\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}T_{\varepsilon,R}\left(f\right)\left(x\right)

for every

,
can be extended to a linear continuous operator

So Theorem
3.4
follows.

In order to prove Theorem
3.5
, we will apply the
following abstract result in spaces of homogeneous type.

Theorem 3.6

(See
[
9
, Thm. 4.1]
). Let
\left(X,d,\mu\right)
be a space of homogeneous type (see Definition

2.2
),
\mu
a regular measure. Let
K:X\times X\setminus\left\{x=y\right\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a kernel satisfying
the following conditions: there exist constants
\boldsymbol{A},\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{C}>0
such that:

(i)

for every
x,y\in X

(
x\neq y
)
one has

\left|K(x,y)\right|+\left|K(y,x)\right|\leq\frac{\boldsymbol{A}}{\left|B(x,d(x,y))\right|};

(ii)

for every
x,x_{0},y\in X
such that
d(x_{0},y)\geq M\,d(x_{0},x)>0
, it holds

\left|K(x,y)-K(x_{0},y)\right|+\left|K(y,x)-K(y,x_{0})\right|\leq\boldsymbol{B}\,\left(\frac{d(x_{0},x)}{d(x_{0},y)}\right)^{\beta}\cdot\frac{1}{\left|B(x_{0},d(x_{0},y))\right|}

for some
\beta>0
and some constant
M>1
such that if
d(x_{0},y)\geq M\,d(x_{0},x)
, then
d\left(x_{0},y\right)
and
d\left(x,y\right)

are equivalent.

(iii)

for every
z\in X
and
0<r<R<\infty
, one has

\left|\int_{\{r<d(z,y)<R\}}K(z,y)d\mu\left(y\right)\right|+\left|\int_{\{r<d(z,x)<R\}}K(x,z)d\mu\left(x\right)\right|\leq\boldsymbol{C}.

Let
K_{\varepsilon}
(for
\varepsilon>0
) be a “regularized
kernel” defined in such a way that the following properties holds:

(a)

K_{\varepsilon}\left(x,\cdot\right)
and
K_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,x\right)
are locally integrable for every
x\in X

(b)

K_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the standard estimates (i), (ii), with
constant bounded by
c^{\prime}\left(\boldsymbol{A}+\boldsymbol{B}\right)

and
c^{\prime}
absolute constant (independent of
\varepsilon
).

(c)

There exists an absolute constant
c^{\prime}
such that for every

z\in X

\left|\int_{\{r<d(z,y)<R\}}K_{\varepsilon}(z,y)d\mu\left(y\right)\right|+\left|\int_{\{r<d(z,x)<R\}}K_{\varepsilon}(x,z)d\mu\left(x\right)\right|\leq c^{\prime}\boldsymbol{C}.

For every

T_{\varepsilon}f\left(x\right)=\int_{X}K_{\varepsilon}\left(x,y\right)f\left(y\right)d\mu\left(y\right).

Then, for every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
,

can be
extended to a linear continuous operator on
L^{p}\left(X\right)
, and

\left\|T_{\varepsilon}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(X\right)}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(X\right)}

for every

, with
c>0
independent of

\varepsilon.
Moreover, the constant
c
in the last estimate depends on the
quantities involved in the assumptions as follows:

c\leq c^{\prime}\left(\boldsymbol{A}+\boldsymbol{B}+\boldsymbol{C}\right),

with

“absolute” constant.

In the previous statement, “absolute” constant means a constant depending on
\left(X,d,\mu\right)
but not on
the kernel
K\left(x,y\right)
(neither on the parameter
\varepsilon
).

Remark 3.7

The previous theorem has been stated and proved in
[
9
, Thm. 4.1]

defining
K_{\varepsilon}
in a specific way (as a linear truncation of
K

near the pole). Since we need to apply this result to smoothed kernels

K_{\varepsilon}
defined with a different procedure, in the present statement
we have pointed out explicitly which are the only properties of

which are actually used in the proof
[
9
, Thm. 4.1]
. The present statement is therefore slightly different (and more
general) than that in
[

]
. On the other hand, this forces us to
check that our smoothed kernels

actually satisfy properties (a)-(b)-(c).

So, to prove Theorem
3.5

the kernels
K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)
defined in
(

3.6
.

As to property (a), we have already checked in (

integrability of
K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,\cdot\right)
. To show the
analogous property for
K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(\cdot,x\right)
we need
to apply Lemma

. We can write:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(y,x\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left(\left(x,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(y,0\right)\right)\right|d\eta dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left((x,0)^{-1}\ast(y,\zeta)\right)\right|d\zeta dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left(u\right)\right|du

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\varepsilon<d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(u\right)<2R}\frac{du}{d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(u\right)^{Q}}=c\log\left(\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\right)

where in the first identity we have performed the change of variables
(
3.2
).

So we have checked property (a) for the kernels
K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)
(note that this property is
not
uniform w.r.t.

\varepsilon
). The next proposition states that also property (b) is satisfied.

Proposition 3.8

(i).
There exists
c>0
such that for every

and every

\left|K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)\right|+\left|K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(y,x\right)\right|\leq\frac{c}{\left|B\left(x,d\left(x,y\right)\right)\right|}.

and

\displaystyle\left|K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x_{0},y\right)-K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)\right|+\left|K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(y,x_{0}\right)-K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(y,x\right)\right|

(iii).
There exists
c>0
such that for every

\left|\int_{r_{1}<d\left(x,y\right)<r_{2}}K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(x,y\right)dy\right|+\left|\int_{r_{1}<d\left(x,y\right)<r_{2}}K_{\varepsilon,R}^{A}\left(y,x\right)dy\right|\leq c

with

independent of
\varepsilon
. The constants depend on the matrix
A

only through the number
\nu
.

Proof.
The proof of this Proposition is an easy adaptation of the proof of the

given in
[

of the estimates in Theorem

, (ii). To prove (iii) for

, to assert that

\int_{r_{1}<d_{\widetilde{X}}\left(\left(y,\eta\right),\left(x,0\right)\right)<r_{2}}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}\widetilde{\Gamma}\cdot\psi_{\varepsilon,R}\right)\left(\left(y,\eta\right)^{-1}\ast\left(x,0\right)\right)d\eta dy=0

.

Summarizing, since the kernels

3.5

3.3
Estimates on the mean oscillation of
X_{i}X_{j}u
in terms of

L_{A}u

Throughout this section we will show how, combining the representation formula
in Theorem
3.3
, the
L^{p}
-continuity result in Theorem

3.4
, and some real analysis in spaces of homogeneous type,
we can prove an estimate on the mean oscillation of
X_{i}X_{j}u
over balls,
in terms of
L_{A}u
, which will be the key point to extend to operators with
variable coefficients
a_{ij}\left(x\right)
the
L^{p}
estimates proved.
The result is the following:

Theorem 3.9

Under the assumptions (H.1)-(H.4) stated in §1,
for every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
there exists
c>0
such that for
every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
r>0
,

x_{0},\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x_{0}\in B\left(\overline{x},r\right)
, we have, for
i,j=1,...,m
and every
k\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|X_{i}X_{j}u\left(x\right)-\left(X_{i}X_{j}u\right)_{B_{r}}\right|dx

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\frac{1}{k}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\mathcal{M}\left(X_{h}X_{l}u\right)\left(x_{0}\right)+k^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|L_{A}u\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\right\}.

The constant
c
depends on the matrix
A
only through the number
\nu
in
(
1.5
).

Here
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator which has been
defined in (
2.7
).

To get the above theorem we will exploit an abstract result which we think can
be of independent interest, and therefore we state in the general context of
spaces of homogeneous type.

Let
T
be a singular integral operator which we already know to be bounded on

L^{p}\left(X\right)
on some space of homogeneous type
\left(X,d,\mu\right)
and some (or all)
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
. Just to
simplify notation, in the integrals we will always write
dx
instead of

d\mu\left(x\right)
. The operator
T
has kernel
K\left(x,y\right)
,
which means that

Tf\left(x\right)=\int_{X}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(y\right)dy

at least when
f
is compactly supported and
x
does not belong to

\operatorname{sprt}f.
We assume that the kernel
K
satisfies the mean value
inequality

\left|K\left(x_{0},y\right)-K\left(x,y\right)\right|\leq C\frac{d\left(x_{0},x\right)}{d\left(x_{0},y\right)B\left(x;y\right)}\text{ if }d\left(x_{0},y\right)\geq Md\left(x_{0},x\right)

(3.9)

where

B\left(x;y\right)=\mu\left(B\left(x,d\left(x,y\right)\right)\right)

and
M>1
is such that the condition
d\left(x_{0},y\right)\geq Md\left(x_{0},x\right)
implies the equivalence of
d\left(x_{0},y\right)
and

d\left(x,y\right)
(if
d
is a distance, like in our case,
M=2
is a
good choice).

Let
q>1
be an exponent such that

\left|B\left(x,kr\right)\right|\leq ck^{q}\left|B\left(x,r\right)\right|

(3.10)

for every
k\geq 1,r>0,x\in X.
Such exponent exists in every space of
homogeneous type; in our concrete application, by (
2.3
),
this exponent will be exactly the number that we have called
q
, i.e. the
homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Under the above assumptions we prove the following:

Theorem 3.10

There exists
c>0
such that for every
f\in L^{p}\left(X\right)
,
x_{0}\in X
, ball
B_{r}=B\left(\overline{x},r\right)\ni x_{0}
(for some
r>0
,
\overline{x}\in X
),
k\geq M
, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}}\left|Tf\left(x\right)-\left(Tf\right)_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\frac{1}{k}\mathcal{M}f\left(x_{0}\right)+k^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\right\}.

Proof.
To simplify notation, this proof is written assuming
d
a distance (and not
just a quasidistance), in particular
M=2
. The proof for a general
quasidistance has some inessential changes.

For fixed
r>0
,
k\geq 2\left(=M\right)
and
f\in L^{p}\left(X\right)
, let

f=f\chi_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}+f\chi_{\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}}\equiv f_{1}+f_{2},

then for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|Tf\left(x\right)-\left(Tf\right)_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\right|dx\leq 2\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|Tf\left(x\right)-c\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|Tf_{1}\left(x\right)\right|dx+2\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|Tf_{2}\left(x\right)-c\right|dx\equiv I+II.

By Hölder inequality and
L^{p}
continuity of
T
we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|Tf_{1}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leq 2C\left(\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{X}\left|f_{1}\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=2C\left(\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leq ck^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p},

where we have exploited (
3.10
).

Next, since, in the integral
II
,
x\in B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)

and
\operatorname{sprt}f_{2}\subset\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}
,

Tf_{2}\left(x\right)=\int K\left(x,y\right)f_{2}\left(y\right)dy=\int_{\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}}K\left(x,y\right)f\left(y\right)dy\text{.}

Then, in
II
, pick
c=Tf_{2}\left(x^{\ast}\right)=\int_{\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}}K\left(x^{\ast},y\right)f\left(y\right)dy
for some
x^{\ast}\in B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)
such that
Tf_{2}\left(x^{\ast}\right)
is finite (
Tf_{2}
is an

L^{p}
function). Then, by the mean value inequality (
3.9
)
for
K
,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=2\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|\int_{\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}}\left[K\left(x,y\right)-K\left(x^{\ast},y\right)\right]f\left(y\right)dy\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\int_{\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}}\frac{d\left(x,x^{\ast}\right)}{d\left(x,y\right)\left|B\left(x;y\right)\right|}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|dydx

\displaystyle\leq cr\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left(\int_{\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}}\frac{\left|f\left(y\right)\right|}{d\left(x,y\right)\left|B\left(x;y\right)\right|}dy\right)dx.

(3.11)

Note that under our assumptions on
\overline{x},x,y
the distances
d\left(\overline{x},y\right)
and
d\left(x,y\right)
are equivalent. Since

x_{0}\in B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)
, also
d\left(x_{0},y\right)
and
d\left(x,y\right)
are equivalent. Moreover,

\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}\subset\left(B_{\left(k-1\right)r}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{c},

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)^{c}}\frac{\left|f\left(y\right)\right|}{d\left(x,y\right)\left|B\left(x;y\right)\right|}dy\leq c\int_{\left(B_{\left(k-1\right)r}\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{c}}\frac{\left|f\left(y\right)\right|}{d\left(x_{0},y\right)\left|B\left(x_{0};y\right)\right|}dy

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{h}\left(k-1\right)r\leq d\left(x_{0},y\right)<2^{h+1}\left(k-1\right)r}\frac{\left|f\left(y\right)\right|}{d\left(x_{0},y\right)\left|B\left(x_{0};y\right)\right|}dy

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{h}\left(k-1\right)r\left|B_{2^{h}\left(k-1\right)r}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{2^{h+1}\left(k-1\right)r}\left(x_{0}\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{h}\left(k-1\right)r}\frac{1}{\left|B_{2^{h+1}\left(k-1\right)r}\left(x_{0}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{2^{h+1}\left(k-1\right)r}\left(x_{0}\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\left(k-1\right)r}\mathcal{M}f\left(x_{0}\right).

(3.12)

where in the up to last inequality we have exploited the doubling property
.

By (
3.11
)-(
3.12
) we conclude

II\leq cr\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\frac{c_{1}}{\left(k-1\right)r}\mathcal{M}f\left(x_{0}\right)dx=\frac{c}{k-1}\mathcal{M}f\left(x_{0}\right)\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{k}\mathcal{M}f\left(x_{0}\right)

since for,
k\geq 2
,
k
and
k-1
are equivalent. So we are done.

.
By Theorem

\displaystyle X_{i}X_{j}u\left(x\right)

Also, if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|\left(c_{ij}^{A}L_{A}u\right)\left(x\right)-\left(c_{ij}^{A}L_{A}u\right)_{B_{r}}\right|dx

The first step is the proof of a control on the mean oscillation of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}|X_{i}X_{j}u\left(x\right)-(X_{i}X_{j}u)_{B_{r}}|dx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}|X_{i}X_{j}u\left(x\right)-(X_{i}X_{j}u)_{B_{r}}|dx\leqslant\frac{c}{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}\mathcal{M}(X_{i}X_{j}u)\left(x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p}\left\{\left(\mathcal{M}(|Lu|^{p})\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{1/p}+\frac{1}{|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)|^{1/p}}\left\|L_{A}u-Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right)}\right\}.

(4.2)

Next, let us write, denoting by
\left(\overline{a}_{ij}\right)
the

,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}|L_{A}u(x)-Lu(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\cap B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}|\overline{a}_{ij}-a_{ij}(x)|^{p\beta}dx\right)^{1/\beta}\left(\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\cap B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}|X_{i}X_{j}u|^{p\alpha}dx\right)^{1/\alpha}\ .

(4.3)

Since the coefficients
\overline{a}_{ij},a_{ij}
are bounded by
\nu^{-1}
we
have

\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\cap B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}|\overline{a}_{ij}-a_{ij}(x)|^{p\beta}dx\leqslant\left(2\nu^{-1}\right)^{\beta p-1}\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\cap B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}\left|a_{ij}\left(x\right)-\overline{a}_{ij}\right|dx.

(4.4)

Recalling that all our estimates hold for every constant matrix
A
in the
fixed ellipticity class (
1.5
), we now choose a particular
constant matrix
\left\{\overline{a}_{ij}\right\}
, depending on
r,k,R
:

\overline{a}_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}(a_{ij})_{B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}&\text{if }kr\geq R\\
(a_{ij})_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}&\text{if }kr\leqslant R\end{array}\right.

and with this definition we easily get

\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\cap B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}\left|a_{ij}\left(x\right)-\overline{a}_{ij}\right|dx\leqslant\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}c\left|B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right|a_{R}^{\sharp}&\text{if }kr\geq R\\
c\left|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|a_{R}^{\sharp}&\text{if }kr\leqslant R\end{array}\right.

Next, since
B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)
and
B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\
intersect, in the case
kr\geq R
we can write, by the doubling
condition,

\left|B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right|\leq\left|B_{kr}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right|\leq c\left|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|

so that in any case

\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\cap B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}\left|a_{ij}\left(x\right)-\overline{a}_{ij}\right|dx\leqslant c\left|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|a_{R}^{\sharp}

and by (
4.2
), (
4.3
), (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|}\int_{B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)}\left|X_{i}X_{j}u\left(x\right)-\left(X_{i}X_{j}u\right)_{B_{r}}\right|dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c}{k}\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}\mathcal{M}(X_{i}X_{j}u)\left(x_{0}\right)+ck^{q/p}\left(\mathcal{M}(|Lu|^{p})\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p}\frac{1}{|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)|^{1/p}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(\left|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\right|a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p\beta}\left(\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)\cap B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)}|X_{i}X_{j}u|^{p\alpha}dx\right)^{1/p\alpha}.

(4.5)

Note that the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle ck^{q/p}\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p\beta}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)|}\int_{B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)}|X_{i}X_{j}u|^{p\alpha}dx\right)^{1/p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant ck^{q/p}\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p\beta}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left(\mathcal{M}(\left|X_{i}X_{j}u\right|^{p\alpha})\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{1/p\alpha}

with
c
also depending on
\nu.
In the last inequality we have exploited the
fact that
x_{0}\in B_{kr}\left(\overline{x}\right)
. Inserting this bound
into (
4.5
), we finally get the statement of Theorem
4.1
.

4.2
Local
L^{p}
estimates

Theorem 4.2

For every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
there
exist
R,c>0
such that for every ball
B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)
and

u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)

\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}\leq c\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}.

(4.6)

The constant
c
in (
4.6
) depends on the numbers
p,\nu,
the
vector fields
\left\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\right\}
and the function
a_{\cdot}^{\sharp}
, but does does not depend on
R
or
x^{\ast}.

Proof.
Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right))
, with
R
to be
chosen later, then for every
x_{0}
, applying Theorem
4.1
and taking
the supremum over all the balls
B_{r}\left(\overline{x}\right)

containing
x_{0}
we get, for every

,

\displaystyle\left(X_{i}X_{j}u\right)^{\#}\left(x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c}{k}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\mathcal{M}(X_{h}X_{l}u)\left(x_{0}\right)+ck^{q/p}\left(\mathcal{M}\left(|Lu|^{p}\right)\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{1/p}\

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p}\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p\beta}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left(\mathcal{M}(\left|X_{h}X_{l}u\right|^{p\alpha})\left(x_{0}\right)\right)^{1/p\alpha}.

(4.7)

Note that this inequality holds for every
p,\alpha,\beta\in(1,\infty)
with

\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=1
, with the constants
c
in (
4.7
) also
depending on these three numbers. For a fixed
p\in(1,\infty)
, let us choose
three numbers
p_{1},\alpha,\beta\in(1,\infty)
with
\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=1
and
\alpha p_{1}<p
. Let us rewrite (

) with

replaced by

p_{1}\
and then take

norms of both
sides. We get:

\displaystyle\left\|\left(X_{i}X_{j}u\right)^{\#}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq\frac{c}{k}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|\mathcal{M}(X_{h}X_{l}u)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}\left(|Lu|^{p_{1}}\right)\left(y\right)\right|^{p/p_{1}}dy\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p_{1}\beta}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\mathcal{M}(\left|X_{h}X_{l}u\right|^{p_{1}\alpha})\left(y\right)\right|^{p/p_{1}\alpha}dy\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{k}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|\mathcal{M}(X_{h}X_{l}u)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left\|\mathcal{M}\left(|Lu|^{p_{1}}\right)\right\|_{L^{p/p_{1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{1/p_{1}}

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p_{1}\beta}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|\mathcal{M}(\left|X_{h}X_{l}u\right|^{p_{1}\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p/p_{1}\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{1/p_{1}\alpha}.

Since
p/p_{1}
and
p/\left(p_{1}\alpha\right)
belong to
\left(1,\infty\right)
, by the
L^{p}
bound on the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
, Theorem
2.8
, we get, also recalling that
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\left\|\left(X_{i}X_{j}u\right)^{\#}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{k}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left\||Lu|^{p_{1}}\right\|_{L^{p/p_{1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{1/p_{1}}

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p_{1}\beta}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|\left|X_{h}X_{l}u\right|^{p_{1}\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p/p_{1}\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}^{1/p_{1}\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{k}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle+ck^{q/p_{1}}\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p_{1}\beta}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}.

Note that, since

, we are
entitled to apply the Fefferman-Stein inequality (Theorem

2.10
) and write:

\displaystyle\left\|X_{i}X_{j}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}\leq c\left\{\frac{1}{k}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.+k^{q/p_{1}}\left[\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}+\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p_{1}\beta}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}\right]\right\}.

Recall that by now
p,p_{1},\alpha,\beta
, and therefore the constant
c
, are
fixed, and the above inequality holds for every
k
large enough. Choosing now

large enough we can write:

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\left\|X_{i}X_{j}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq ck^{q/p_{1}}\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}+\left(a_{R}^{\sharp}\right)^{1/p_{1}\beta}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}\right\}.

Recall that in Theorem
4.1
the constant

does not depend on
R
,
hence the same is true in (
4.8
). Since by assumptions (H.5) the function

vanishes as

, we can finally choose

and conclude that for that small

\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}\leq c\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x^{\ast}\right)\right)}.

4.3
Global
L^{p}
estimates

The next step consists in deriving the following version of global a priori estimates:

Theorem 4.3

For every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
there
exists
c>0
, depending on numbers
p,\nu,
the vector fields
\left\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\right\}
and the function
R\mapsto a_{R}^{\sharp}
, such
that for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
we have

\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\right\}.

Proof.
Let
H>1
be as in Lemma
2.18
and fix
R>0
small enough so that
Theorem
4.2
is applicable to balls
B_{HR}
. We can then
build a family of cutoff functions
\left\{\phi^{x}\left(\cdot\right)\right\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
as in Lemma
2.18
. Next, by
Proposition
2.19
we can cover
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a family

\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},R\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A}
of balls
such that the family of dilated balls
\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},HR\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A}
has the bounded overlapping property. Let us
consider the family of cutoff functions adapted to these balls; letting let

\phi_{\alpha}=\phi^{x_{\alpha}}
we have:

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}\text{ in }B_{R}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)

\sup_{y}\left|\phi_{\alpha}\left(y\right)\right|+\sum_{h=1}^{m}\sup_{y}\left|X_{h}\phi_{\alpha}\left(y\right)\right|+\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\sup_{y}\left|X_{h}X_{l}\phi_{\alpha}\left(y\right)\right|\leq c_{2}

with
c_{1},c_{2}
independent of
\alpha
(while
R
is by now fixed).

Then we can write, for every
\alpha\in A
,

\displaystyle\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}\leq\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\frac{1}{c_{1}}\left\|\phi_{\alpha}X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\{\left\|X_{h}X_{l}\left(u\phi_{\alpha}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\left\|X_{h}uX_{l}\phi_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\|uX_{h}X_{l}\phi_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}\left(u\phi_{\alpha}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right\}

by Theorem
4.2

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\left\|L\left(u\phi_{\alpha}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right\}

Next, we sum up over
\alpha\in A
, finding, since
\left\{B\left(x_{\alpha},R\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in A}
are a covering of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq\sum_{\alpha\in A}\sum_{h,l=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}X_{l}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{R}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{\alpha\in A}\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{HR}\left(x_{\alpha}\right)\right)}\right\}

applying Proposition
2.19

\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\right\}.

Adding
\sum_{h=1}^{m}\left\|X_{h}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}
to both sides of the last inequality we get the conclusion.

Corollary 4.4

For every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
there
exists
c>0
, depending on numbers
p,\nu
, the vector fields
\left\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\right\}
and the function
R\mapsto a_{R}^{\sharp}
, such
that for every
u\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and for every

\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(B_{R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{1,p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}\right\},

with

independent of

\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{2}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right),\phi=1

\phi_{R}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right),\phi_{R}=1\text{ in }B_{R}^{\ast}\left(0\right),

Let

and for a fixed

us apply Theorem
4.3
to the function

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(B_{R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u\phi_{R}\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq c\left\{\left\|L\left(u\phi_{R}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\left\|u\phi_{R}\right\|_{W_{X}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{1,p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}\right\}.

inequality for intermediate norms which holds for
homogeneous
vector fields:

Theorem 4.5

(See
[
3
, Prop.2.9]
). For every
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
there exists
c_{p}
such that for every

we have

\left\|X_{i}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq\varepsilon\left\|X_{i}^{2}u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\frac{c_{p}}{\varepsilon}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

.

We can now finally conclude the:

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Let
u\in W_{X}^{2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and, for a fixed
R,
take a sequence
\left\{u_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
such

we have

-norm. This is possible in view of

1.3
. By Corollary

we can

\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(B_{R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu_{i}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}+\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{W_{X}^{1,p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}\right\}

and, passing to the limit as

, we conclude

\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(B_{R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{1,p}\left(B_{2R}^{\ast}\left(0\right)\right)}\right\}

for every
u\in W_{X}^{2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, with
c

independent of
R
. Passing to the limit for
R\rightarrow+\infty
, we get

\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\right\}

for every

. Finally, we apply
the interpolation inequaltiy in Theorem

with

small enough, and conclude

\left\|u\right\|_{W_{X}^{2,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\leq c\left\{\left\|Lu\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}\right\},

that is our desired global estimate.

Higher order estimates

6
Regularization of solutions

In this section we want to prove the following result which, together with
Theorem
5.1
, will imply the full statement of Theorem

Theorem 6.1

Under assumptions (H.1)-(H.4) stated in section 1,
assume that for, some
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
and positive integer

,
a_{ij}\in W_{X}^{k,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

To prove the above result we will apply the known
local

result which holds for nonvariational operators built on a general system of
Hörmander vector fields (not necessarily homogeneous):

Theorem 6.2
(See
[
8
, Thm. 12.2 (a)]
)

Let

X_{1},..,X_{m}
be a system of Hörmander vector fields in a bounded
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\left\{a_{ij}\left(x\right)\right\}_{i,j=1}^{m}
satisfy our assumption (H.4) stated in section 1 and
let
L
be as in (
1.1
). For every domain
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
,
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
, nonnegative integer
k
, if

,

, and

, then
u\in W_{X}^{k+2,p}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)
.

The quoted result in
[
8
, Thm. 12.2 (a)]
actually contains also local
a priori estimates in

, but we
will not apply those local estimates, but the

proved in Theorem

.

.

, assume

, let

in

with the derivatives of

\phi_{R}
, up to order

. By Theorem

5.1
) for

let

L_{A}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}X_{i}X_{j}.

(7.1)

Since
A
is symmetric and positive definite, it admits a
unique

symmetric and positive definite square root, say
S
. As a consequence,
writing
S=(s_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{m}
, we have

L_{A}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}Y_{j}^{2},\text{ \ \ where $Y_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i,j}X_{i}$}.

On the other hand, since
S
is non-singular, it is not difficult to recognize
that the family
Y=\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}\}\subseteq\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfies assumptions (H.1)-(H.3). In particular, since the
s_{i,j}
’s are
constant, one has

\mathrm{Lie}(Y)=\mathfrak{a}.

(7.2)

Gathering these facts, we can apply point (1) of Theorem
2.12

also to the family
Y
: there exist a homogeneous Carnot group
\mathbb{F}

and a system

\widetilde{Y}=\{\widetilde{Y}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{Y}_{m}\}

of Lie-generators for the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbb{F})
such that,
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
, the vector field
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
is a lifting of

Y_{i}
in the sense of (
2.11
).

The key observation
is that, in view of
[
4
, Remark 2.3]
, the construction of
\mathbb{F}

does not really depend on
Y
, but only on the Lie algebra
\mathrm{Lie}(Y)

and on the choice of an
adapted basis
. As a consequence, using
(
7.2
) and choosing the
same adapted basis
used for the
construction of
\mathbb{G}
, we obtain

\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{G}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{Y}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i,j}\widetilde{X}_{i}.

Summing up, the couple
\left(\mathbb{G},\widetilde{X}\right)
associated
with
X
provides a ‘lifting pair’ for the family
Y=\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}\}

(in the sense of point (1) of Theorem
2.12
)
which is
independent of the fixed matrix
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
.
This means in
particular that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{Y}_{j}^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}.

Therefore, if
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}
is the homogeneous fundamental solution
vanishing at infinity of the operator
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{Y}_{j}^{2}
on
the group
(\mathbb{G},\widetilde{X})
, the fundamental solution of
L_{A}
is
given, according to
[
1
, Thm. 1]
, by (
2.13
), and points (2) and (3) of Theorem
2.12
hold.

In order to prove the uniform estimates on the derivatives of
\Gamma_{A}

contained in Theorem
2.14
, we will need the following known fact:

Theorem 7.1
(Uniform estimates on
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}
)

(See
[
8
, Thm.6.18]
)
Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with generators

\widetilde{X}_{1},...,\widetilde{X}_{m}
, let
A
be an
m\times m
symmetric
constant matrix satisfying (
1.5
) and let
\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}

be the homogeneous fundamental solution, vanishing at infinity, of

\widetilde{L}_{A}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i,j}\widetilde{X}_{i}\widetilde{X}_{j}
.
Then for every positive integer
r
, there exists a constant
c
, only
depending on the group, the ellipticity constant
\nu
and the integer
r
,
such that:

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\left\|z\right\|=1}\sup_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq r}\left|\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}}{\partial z^{\alpha}}\left(z\right)\right|\leq c\left(r,\mathbb{G},\nu\right).

(Here we use the notation
\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}
as in
(
2.15
)).

By homogeneity, the sup on the unit sphere
\left\{\left\|z\right\|=1\right\}
can be replaced by the sup on any compact set
K
excluding the
origin, with the constant
c
also depending on
K.

This bound implies that, for every fixed homogeneous norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\left|\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{s}}\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}\left(z\right)\right|\leq\frac{c}{\left\|z\right\|^{Q-2+s}}

(7.3)

with
c
depending on the matrix
A
only through the number
\nu
.

Proof of Theorem
2.14
.
Point (I) of Theorem
2.14

is contained in
[
2
, Thm.1.3]
, once we know formula
(
2.13
).

In order to prove point (II) of Theorem
2.14
, assuring the
uniformity of the constant
C_{r}
as the matrix
A
ranges in the ellipticity
class
\mathcal{M}_{\nu}
, we need to revise the proof of point (II) contained
in
[
2
]
, exploiting at a crucial point the uniform bound
(
7.3
). Let us distinguish three cases, according to kind
of
x
- or
y
-derivatives acting on
\Gamma_{A}
.

Case I:

Z_{1}\cdots Z_{r}=X_{i_{1}}^{x}\cdots X_{i_{r}}^{x}
. Using
the integral representation formula in point (I) of Theorem

2.14
for the
X
-derivatives of
\Gamma_{A}(\cdot;y)
, we can
write, for
x\neq y
,

Z_{1}\cdots Z_{r}\Gamma_{A}(x;y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{r}}\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}\right)\left((y,0)^{-1}\ast(x,\eta)\right)d\eta.

(7.4)

Hence, by the uniform bound (
7.3
), we have

\left|Z_{1}\cdots Z_{r}\Gamma_{A}(x;y)\right|\leq c\left(r,\nu,\mathbb{G}\right)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}d_{\widetilde{X}}^{2-Q-r}\left(0,(y,0)^{-1}\ast(x,\eta)\right)d\eta.

Then we exploit a result in
[
2
, Cor. 4.11]
: there exists a
constant
C>0
such that, for every
x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}d_{\widetilde{X}}^{2-Q-r}\left(0,(y,0)^{-1}\ast(x,\eta)\right)d\eta\leq C\,\frac{d_{X}^{2-r}(x,y)}{\left|B_{X}(y,d_{X}(x,y))\right|}.

(7.5)

Combining the last two inequalities we get

\left|Z_{1}\cdots Z_{r}\Gamma_{A}(x;y)\right|\leq C\,\frac{d_{X}^{2-r}(x,y)}{\left|B_{X}(y,d_{X}(x,y))\right|}\leq C^{\prime}\,\frac{d_{X}^{2-r}(x,y)}{\left|B_{X}(x,d_{X}(x,y))\right|}

where in the last inequality we have exploited the equivalence between

\left|B_{X}(y,d_{X}(x,y))\right|
and
\left|B_{X}(x,d_{X}(x,y))\right|
, which follows by the doubling property.

Case II:

Z_{1}\cdots Z_{r}=X_{j_{1}}^{y}\cdots X_{j_{r}}^{y}
. We
start with the integral representation formula in point (I) of Theorem

2.14
for the
X
-derivatives of
\Gamma_{A}(x;\cdot)

X_{i_{1}}^{y}\cdots X_{i_{s}}^{y}\left(\Gamma_{A}(x;\cdot)\right)(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left(\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{s}}\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}\right)\left((x,0)^{-1}\ast(y,\eta)\right)\,d\eta

and then we can argue exactly as in Case I, getting the same bound.

Case III:

Z_{1}\cdots Z_{r}=X_{j_{1}}^{y}\cdots X_{j_{k}}^{y}\,X_{i_{1}}^{x}\cdots X_{i_{h}}^{x}
(with
h+k=r
). This is a bit more
delicate. We start from the integral representation formula in point (I) of
Theorem
2.14
for the mixed
X
-derivatives of

\Gamma_{A}
, writing

\displaystyle X_{j_{1}}^{x}\cdots X_{j_{t}}^{x}X_{i_{1}}^{y}\cdots X_{i_{s}}^{y}\Gamma_{A}(x;y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left(\widetilde{X}_{j_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{j_{t}}\left(\left(\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{s}}\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}\right)\circ\iota\right)\right)\left((y,0)^{-1}\ast(x,\eta)\right)d\eta\,

(7.6)

where
\iota
denotes the inversion map of the Lie group
\mathbb{G}
. First
of all, by repeatedly using
[
4
, Lem. 5.3]
, we can write

\widetilde{X}_{j_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{j_{t}}\left(\left(\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{s}}\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}\right)\circ\iota\right)=\left(Z_{j_{1}}\cdots Z_{j_{t}}\,\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{s}}\,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}\right)\circ\iota,

(7.7)

where
Z_{j_{1}},\ldots,Z_{j_{t}}
are suitable smooth vector fields

D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
1
but not necessarily left-invariant.
Since all the vector fields in (
7.7
) are
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree
1
, we are entitled to apply
[
5
, Lemma
3.4]
, obtaining

Z_{j_{1}}\cdots Z_{j_{t}}\,\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{s}}\,\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}=\!\!\!\sum_{r\,\leqslant\,|\alpha|\,\leqslant\,r\sigma_{n}}\!\!\!\!\gamma_{\alpha}(z)\,\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{m})\widetilde{\Gamma}_{A}.

(7.8)

Here,
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is a homogeneous polynomial
of (Euclidean) degree
|\alpha|
in the non-commuting variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
, and
\gamma_{\alpha}
is a
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous
polynomial of degree
|\alpha|-r
. We now observe that, on account of
(
7.3
), we have

\left|\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(\widetilde{X}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{X}_{m})\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}(z)\right|\leq c_{\nu,r}\,d_{\widetilde{X}}^{2-Q-|\alpha|}(0,z)\qquad(z\neq 0),

where the constant
c_{\nu,r}
can be chosen independently of
\alpha
.
Moreover, since
\gamma_{\alpha}
is (smooth and)
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of
degree
|\alpha|-r
, we get

\left|\gamma_{\alpha}(z)\right|\leq c_{r}\,d_{\widetilde{X}}^{|\alpha|-r}(0,z)\qquad(z\neq 0),

(7.9)

where
c=c_{r}>0
is another constant which can be chosen independently of

\alpha
. Gathering all these facts, we obtain the following estimate

\left|\widetilde{X}_{j_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{j_{t}}\left(\left(\widetilde{X}_{i_{1}}\cdots\widetilde{X}_{i_{s}}\Gamma_{A,\mathbb{G}}\right)\circ\iota\right)\right|\leq c_{\nu,r}\,d_{\widetilde{X}}^{2-Q-r}\left(0,z^{-1}\right)=c_{\nu,r}\,d_{\widetilde{X}}^{2-Q-r}\left(0,z\right).

By (
7.6
) this implies

\left|X_{j_{1}}^{x}\cdots X_{j_{t}}^{x}X_{i_{1}}^{y}\cdots X_{i_{s}}^{y}\Gamma_{A}(x;y)\right|\leq c_{\nu,r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}d_{\widetilde{X}}^{2-Q-r}\left(0,(y,0)^{-1}\ast(x,\eta)\right)d\eta,

which by (
7.5
) implies the desired uniform bound. So we are done.

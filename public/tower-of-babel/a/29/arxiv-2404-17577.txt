On Quasi-Locality and Decay of Correlations for Long-Range Models of Open Quantum Spin Systems
Eric B. Roon, Robert Sims

Abstract
We consider models of open quantum spin systems with irreversible dynamics and show that general quasi-locality results for long-range models, e.g. as proven for the Heisenberg dynamics associated to quantum systems in [ 27 ] , naturally extend to this setting. Given these bounds, we provide two applications. First, we use these results to obtain estimates on a strictly local approximation of these finite-volume, irreversible dynamics. Next, we show how these bounds can be used to estimate correlation decay in various states.



1
Introduction

Since the pioneering work of Lieb and Robinson, see
[
24
]
, quasi-locality bounds for the dynamics of
models of quantum spin systems have become an indispensable tool in this many-body framework.
By now, the well-known list of applications is long, and we refer the interested reader to the books
[
7
,
28
]
as well as the
review-oriented article
[
30
]
for a more complete set of references.

In the present work, we focus on studying the irreversible dynamics associated to open quantum systems which allow for dissipative interactions.
To our knowledge, the first quasi-locality results for such models were obtained in
[
17
]
and further extensions, as well as some applications, can be found in
[
3
,
11
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
26
,
31
,
34
]
.
Our main goal here is to extend the results of
[
27
]
, where the authors consider the Heisenberg dynamics of quantum spin models with
long-range interactions, to this setting of long-range models with an irreversible dynamics, defined as a quantum dynamical semigroup.
In fact, our efforts were inspired by statements in the work of
[
34
]
where the authors indicate that they are unaware of such an extension in the literature.

Let us now briefly distinguish our results from that of some recent works. In the past few years,
much attention has been given to understanding quasi-locality results for the dynamics of models
with long-range interactions. Interest in these bounds started with investigations of the Heisenberg dynamics
associated to quantum spin Hamiltonians, see
[
12
,
14
,
23
,
27
,
36
,
37
]
, and results for models with an irreversible dynamics
soon followed, see
[
16
,
34
]
.

In contrast to the results of
[
34
]
, we derive a power-law Lieb-Robinson bound where, in a certain regime, the upper bound is linear in time. In
[
19
, Theorem 3]
, the authors develop methods to obtain estimates on the spatial correlations of a dynamical fixed point. Our methods diverge from
[
19
]
, in that we do not require a reversibility assumption on the local Lindbladians, and instead we estimate the correlations using ideas which go back to
[
33
]
. The authors of
[
16
]
also require reversibility in their clustering estimates. Moreover,
[
16
]
contains an iterative method to establish a power-law Lieb-Robinson bound whereas we use perturbative methods inspired by
[
27
]
. Lastly, we emphasize that our results focus on models of open quantum systems defined over a discrete metric space. In contrast, we note that two recent papers
[
8
,
9
]
generalize Lieb-Robinson bounds for open quantum systems to models in the continuum.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2.1
, we introduce the
basic set-up for the models we consider. Next, in Section
2.2
,
we discuss a fundamental quasi-locality result for a class of models with finite-range interactions. We state this key estimate as Theorem
2.1
.
Given this bound, we adapt the methods of
[
27
]
to models with an irreversible dynamics.
We present this as two separate results. First, in Theorem
2.4
,
we demonstrate an estimate for a finite-range approximation of the finite-volume dynamics.
Next, Theorem
2.5
establishes an estimate analogous to the result in
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
.
In particular, a quasi-locality bound for the finite-volume dynamics is expressed in terms of
a quasi-locality bound for a finite-range approximation.
Interestingly, our result in Theorem
2.5
differs from that of
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]

in that we are able to recover the well-known (static) Lieb-Robinson bound of
[
31
, Theorem 2]
in the limit as the finite-range parameter
R
tends to infinity. Next, we supplement Theorem
2.5
with Theorem
2.6
which
provides an explicit estimate for models where only power-law decay of the interactions is known.
Then, in Section
2.3
, we consider approximations of the finite-volume dynamics by a strictly local evolution.
Such estimates are crucial in many applications, and we describe our results in
Theorem
2.8
and Theorem
2.9
.

Finally, in Section
3
, we estimate correlation decay in various states. First, in Section
3.1
, we follow
the techniques of
[
29
]
and demonstrate
estimates on dynamic correlations for states with a quantified spatial decay, now in this case of an irreversible dynamics.
Then, in Section
3.2
, we consider correlation decay for a class of rapidly mixing, dynamical fixed points.
Unlike the general methods in
[
19
]
, we here obtain our results
with ideas that go back to earlier work of Poulin, see
[
33
]
. In an appendix, we
relate our assumption of rapid mixing to other well-studied notions.

2
Locality

2.1
Set-Up

All the models we consider will be defined on a countable metric space.
In many applications, the models of interest arise naturally on
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
where
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, but
the precise structure of
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
is unnecessary for most of the rigorous results discussed here. In general, we take

(\Gamma,d)
to be a countable metric space. For any
X\subset\Gamma
, we will denote
the cardinality of
X
by
|X|
and the diameter of
X
by
{\rm diam}(X)=\sup\{d(x,y):x,y\in X\}
.
Given
X\subset\Gamma
and
r\geq 0
, it is convenient to a introduce an
r
-inflation of
X
by setting

X(r)=\{y\in\Gamma:d(y,x)\leq r\mbox{ for some }x\in X\}
. By
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, we will denote
the set of finite subsets of
\Gamma
,
i.e.

\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)=\{X\subset\Gamma:|X|<\infty\}
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a countable metric space. In order to introduce the models investigated here, we first associate to
(\Gamma,d)
the
structure of a quantum spin system. To do so, for each
x\in\Gamma
, we select a finite-dimensional (complex) Hilbert space of
states
\mathcal{H}_{x}
and an algebra of observables
\mathcal{A}_{x}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{x})
, the collection of
bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{H}_{x}
to itself. For any finite set
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
, the Hilbert space of
states associated with
\Lambda
is then defined as the tensor product

\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{x}

(2.1)

and similarly, the algebra of observables in
\Lambda
is given by

\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}_{x}\,.

(2.2)

For finite sets
\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda\subset\Gamma
, we identify
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{0}}

with the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{0}}\otimes{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}_{\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{0}}
of
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
and
simply write
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{0}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
. An algebra of all strictly local observables
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}^{\rm loc}
is then defined by the
inductive limit

\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}^{\rm loc}=\bigcup_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}\,.

(2.3)

Given this, the
C^{*}
-algebra of quasi-local observables
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}
is taken to be the
norm completion of
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}^{\rm loc}
. The interested reader can find more details about this
construction
e.g.
in
[
6
,
7
]
.

For us, interactions will consist of a family
\mathscr{L}=\{L_{Z}:Z\subset\Gamma\text{ finite }\}
of bounded linear maps
L_{Z}:\mathcal{A}_{Z}\to\mathcal{A}_{Z}
in
Lindblad
form. Discovered independently in 1976 by
[
25
]
and
[
15
]
, the Lindblad equation characterizes generators of a norm-continuous, one parameter semigroup of unital completely positive maps. For us, this means there exists
H_{Z}=H_{Z}^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}
, a number
\ell_{Z}\in\mathbb{N}
and operators
K_{1},\dots,K_{\ell_{Z}}\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}
so that for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}
, one has

L_{Z}(A)=i[H_{Z},A]+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{Z}}K_{j}^{*}AK_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\{K_{j}^{*}K_{j},A\}.

(2.4)

The total interaction in a given finite volume,
\Lambda
, is then the operator

\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda}=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}L_{Z}.

(2.5)

The
\Lambda
-local dynamics generated by
\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda}
form a norm-continuous, one-parameter semigroup of operators

T^{\Lambda}_{t}(A)=e^{t\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda}}(A),\quad A\in\mathcal{A},

(2.6)

which is well defined since
\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda}
is bounded. The Lindblad form guaruntees that
T^{\Lambda}_{t}
have the properties of a quantum dynamical semigroup. Namely, for each
t\in[0,\infty)
and
\Lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, the map
T_{t}^{\Lambda}
is unit preserving and completely positive (ucp). We refer the interested reader to
[
1
,
2
]
for more on the theory of quantum dynamical semigroups.

Complete positivity is a general condition some maps between
C^{*}
-algebras possess. We recall that for a finite dimensional Hilbert space
H
, the one may regard
B(H)\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}
as
n\times n
,
B(H)
-valued block matrices. Any linear map
\Phi:B(H)\to B(H)
induces a linear map on
B(H)\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}
by acting block-wise

\Phi\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{n\times n}([[X_{i,j}]]_{i,j=1}^{n}):=[[\Phi(X_{i,j})]]_{i,j=1}^{n}.

(2.7)

The linear map
\Phi
is said to be completely positive if for all
n
, the matrix
\Phi\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{n\times n}(X^{*}X)
is positive semidefinite for all
X\in B(H)\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}
.
Recall, by
[
32
, Proposition 3.6]
, that completely positive maps are also completely bounded in the sense that the quantity

\|\Phi\|_{cb}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\Phi\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{n\times n}\|<\infty.

(2.8)

In fact, every linear map between matrix algebras is completely bounded by
[
32
, Proposition 8.11]
. In particular, given a finite volume
X\subset\Lambda\in P_{0}(\Gamma)
, and a linear map
\Phi\in B(\mathcal{A}_{X})
, one has

\|\Phi\otimes\id_{\Lambda\setminus X}\|_{cb}=\|\Phi\|_{cb}\|\|\id_{\Lambda\setminus X}\|_{cb}=\|\Phi\|_{cb}\,,

(2.9)

see,
e.g.

[
10
, Corollary 3.5.5]
for a detailed proof.

We describe a class of completely bounded maps for the purposes of our model. Given a finite volume
Y
, the set
CB_{0}(Y)
is defined to be the set of completely bounded maps
\Phi
on
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
satisfying
\Phi(\mathbbm{1}_{Y})=0
. An example of such a map is given by fixing
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
and defining
\Phi(A)=[B,A]
for
A\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
.

We distinguish classes of models according to short or long range interactions by introducing decay functions, and note
that this notion is primarily of use in contexts where
\Gamma
is infinite.
A non-increasing function
F:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is said to be an
F
-function on
(\Gamma,d)
if:

i)
F
is uniformly summable, in the sense that

\|F\|=\sup_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{y\in\Gamma}F(d(x,y))<\infty

(2.10)

and

ii)
F
satisfies a convolution condition,
i.e.
there is a positive number
C
for which, given any pair
x,y\in\Gamma
,

\sum_{z\in\Gamma}F(d(x,z))F(d(z,y))\leq CF(d(x,y))

(2.11)

We define the convolution constant
C_{F}
as the infimum over all constants
C
for which (
2.11
) holds.
In general, one readily checks that if
F
is an
F
-function on
(\Gamma,d)
, then for any
a\geq 0
, the function
F_{a}(r)=e^{-ar}F(r)
is also
an
F
-function on
(\Gamma,d)
. Moreover, one has that
\|F_{a}\|\leq\|F\|
and
C_{F_{a}}\leq C_{F}
.

The set of doubly anchored interactions are those for which there exists a constant
M
so that for any
x,y\in\Gamma
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma):\\
x,y\in Z\end{subarray}}\|L_{Z}\|_{cb}\leq MF(d(x,y)).

(2.12)

In particular, the infimum of those
M
defines a norm
\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}
, which we call the
F
-norm.

As indicated above, many interesting quantum spin models are defined with
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. In this case, the existence of an
F
-function
is clear. In fact, the function

F(r)=\frac{1}{(1+r)^{\alpha}}\quad\mbox{for any }r\geq 0

(2.13)

is an
F
-function on
(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu},d)
whenever
\alpha>\nu
. Clearly, the above function
F
is summable (take for example
d(x,y)=|x-y|
with the

\ell^{1}
-distance), and a simple convexity argument shows that
C_{F}\leq 2^{\alpha}\|F\|
.

Under some mild regularity assumptions, one can establish the existence of
F
-functions more generally. We say that a metric space

(\Gamma,d)
is
\nu
-regular if there are positive numbers
\kappa
and
\nu
for which

\sup_{x\in\Gamma}|b_{x}(n+1)|\leq\kappa(n+1)^{\nu}\quad\mbox{for any }n\geq 0.

(2.14)

Here we have used
b_{x}(n)
to denote the closed ball of radius
n
centered at
x\in\Gamma
.
Note that if
(\Gamma,d)
is
\nu
-regular, then the function
F
in (
2.13
) is an
F
-function on
(\Gamma,d)
whenever
\alpha>\nu+1.

In fact, for any
x\in\Gamma
and any non-increasing, non-negative function
F
,

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\Gamma}F(d(x,y))=F(0)+\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle y\in\Gamma:}}{{n<d(x,y)\leq n+1}}}F(d(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(0)+\sum_{n\geq 0}F(n)|b_{x}(n+1)\setminus b_{x}(n)|

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(0)+\kappa\sum_{n\geq 0}F(n)(1+n)^{\nu}

where the last bound uses that
(\Gamma,d)
is
\nu
-regular. Taking
F
as in (
2.13
), it is clear that
F
is uniformly summable whenever

\alpha>\nu+1
. As the previously indicated bound on the convolution constant does not depend on the metric space, the above
F
is an
F
-function on
any
\nu
-regular
(\Gamma,d)
.

We note that, for many arguments, different regularity assumptions suffice. For example, the metric space
(\Gamma,d)
is
said to be
\nu
-surface regular if there are positive numbers
\kappa
and
\nu>1
for which

\sup_{x\in\Gamma}|b_{x}(n+1)\setminus b_{x}(n)|\leq\kappa(n+1)^{\nu-1}\quad\mbox{for any }n\geq 0.

(2.16)

Clearly, if
(\Gamma,d)
is
\nu
-surface regular, then
(\Gamma,d)
is
\nu
-regular (with the same value of
\nu
) as

\displaystyle b_{x}(n+1)=\{x\}\cup\bigcup_{m=0}^{n}\left(b_{x}(m+1)\setminus b_{x}(m)\right)\quad\mbox{implies}\quad|b_{x}(n+1)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\kappa\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1+m)^{\nu-1}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\kappa)(1+n)^{\nu}\,.

There may be situations where these different regularity assumptions are useful (note that if
(\Gamma,d)
is
\nu
-surface regular, then
the function
F
in (
2.13
) is an
F
-function on
(\Gamma,d)
whenever
\alpha>\nu
, as is the case when
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
)
however, we state our results in terms of the notion of
\nu
-regularity, as in (
2.14
), as this is more general among metric spaces.

2.2
Finite Range Approximations

Our first result, Theorem
2.1
below, presents the essential arguments of
[
31
]
from a
slightly different perspective. Here we focus on an estimate corresponding to a finite-range approximation of the finite-volume dynamics.
It is clear that our arguments allow us to recover a simplified version of Theorem 2 from
[
31
]
and we state this as
Theorem
2.2
. Moreover, if a given model is finite-range and uniformly bounded, then we obtain an estimate
with strong spatial decay; this is the content of Corollary
2.3
below.
Next, we use Theorem
2.1
to derive an estimate on the difference between the finite-volume dynamics
and this finite-range approximation; this is the content of Theorem
2.4
. Our argument here is similar to that of

[
27
]
with two main differences. First, we work in the setting of open quantum systems.
Next, unlike the results in
[
27
]
, our estimates, see specifically Theorem
2.5
, allow us to recover the bound from
[
31
]
by letting the finite range approximation parameter
tend to infinity. We end this section with Theorem
2.6
which provides an explicit estimate for
a class of models with polynomially decay.

For the results proven below, we assume
(\Gamma,d)
is a
\nu
-regular metric space equipped with an
F
-function
F
.
As described above, let
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra of quasi-local observables defined over
(\Gamma,d)
and take

\mathscr{L}=\{L_{Z}\}_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}
to be a dissipative interaction. For any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
, we denote
the finite-volume generator and corresponding irreversible dynamics by

\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda}=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}L_{Z}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{t}^{\Lambda}={\rm exp}(t\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda})\quad\mbox{for any }t\geq 0.

(2.18)

Analogously, we consider an approximate, finite-volume dynamics, for any
R>0
, by setting

\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R}=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Z\subset\Lambda:}}{{{\rm diam}(Z)\leq R}}}L_{Z}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{t}^{\Lambda,R}={\rm exp}(t\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R})\quad\mbox{for any }t\geq 0.

(2.19)

Throughout the rest of this section, we will deal extensively with
k
-tails of the exponential power series. As such we introduce the following notation for ease of reading: for all
t\geq 0
and
k>0
, we write

\mathscr{E}_{t}(k):=\sum_{n=\lceil k\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(2.20)

We will often omit the ceiling function and instead write
n\geq k
when no confusion can arise.

We now state and sketch a well-known proof of a locality estimate
in the special case of this finite-range approximation.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a countable metric space equipped with an
F
-function
F
.
Let
\mathscr{L}=\{L_{Z}\}_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}
be a dissipative interaction with
\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}<\infty
.
Fix
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
with
X\cap Y=\varnothing
.
For any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
with
X\cup Y\subset\Lambda
,
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, and

K\in CB_{0}(Y)
one has that

\|K(T^{\Lambda,R}_{t}(A))\|\leq\frac{\|K\|_{cb}\|A\|}{C_{F}}\mathscr{E}_{vt}\left(\frac{d(X,Y)}{R}\right)\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Y}F(d(x,y))

(2.21)

for any
t\geq 0
and
R>0
. Here
v=\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}C_{F}
.

Proof.

Consider the function
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
given by

f(t)=K(T^{\Lambda,R}_{t}(A))\quad\text{for all $t\geq 0$.}

(2.22)

Clearly, one has that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(t)=K(\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R}T^{\Lambda,R}_{t}(A))

\displaystyle=K(\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R}\,T^{\Lambda,R}_{t}(A))

\displaystyle\quad+K((\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R}-\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R})T^{\Lambda,R}_{t}(A))

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R}f(t)+K((\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R}-\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R})T^{\Lambda,R}_{t}(A)).

Regarding the above as a first-order, non-homogeneous linear equation, the unique solution of the corresponding initial value problem is

f(t)=T^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R}_{t}(f(0))+\int_{0}^{t}T^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R}_{t-s}(K((\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R}-\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R})T^{\Lambda,R}_{s}(A)))\,ds.

(2.23)

The norm bound

f(t)\leq\|f(0)\|+\|K\|_{cb}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R}-\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R})T^{\Lambda,R}_{s}(A)\|\,ds.

(2.24)

readily follows.

For the convenience of iteration, introduce

C_{A}(Z,t)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in CB_{0}(Z):\\
G\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|GT^{\Lambda,R}_{t}(A)\|}{\|G\|_{cb}}\quad\text{ for any $Z\subset\Lambda$ and $t\geq 0$.}

(2.25)

One checks that
C_{A}(Z,t)\leq\|A\|
for all
t\geq 0
, and moreover,

C_{A}(Z,0)\leq\|A\|\delta_{X}(Z),

where
\delta_{X}(Z)=1
if
Z\cap X\neq\varnothing
and equals zero otherwise. Notice that for each
s\geq 0
, we have

\|(\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda,R}-\mathscr{L}^{\Lambda\setminus Y,R})T^{\Lambda,R}_{s}(A)\|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda:\\
Z\cap Y\neq\varnothing\\
\diam(Z)\leq R\end{subarray}}\|L_{Z}T^{\Lambda,R}_{s}(A)\|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda:\\
Z\cap Y\neq\varnothing,\\
\diam(Z)\leq R\end{subarray}}\|L\|_{cb}C_{A}(Z,s).

It follows from (
2.24
) and the above that

C_{A}(Y,t)\leq C_{A}(Y,0)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda:\\
Z\cap Y\neq\varnothing,\\
\diam(Z)\leq R\end{subarray}}\|L_{Z}\|_{cb}\int_{0}^{t}C_{A}(Z,s)ds.

Upon iteration, we find that

C_{A}(Y,t)\leq C_{A}(Y,0)+\|A\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

(2.26)

where

a_{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z_{1}\subset\Lambda:\\
Z_{1}\cap Y\neq\varnothing\\
\diam(Z_{1})\leq R\end{subarray}}\|L_{Z_{1}}\|_{cb}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z_{2}\subset\Lambda:\\
Z_{2}\cap Z_{1}\neq\varnothing\\
\diam(Z_{2})\leq R\end{subarray}}\|L_{Z_{2}}\|_{cb}\cdots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z_{n}\subset\Lambda:\\
Z_{n}\cap Z_{n-1}\neq\varnothing\\
\diam(Z_{n})\leq R\end{subarray}}\|L_{Z_{n}}\|_{cb}\,\delta_{X}(Z_{n}).

(2.27)

The convergence of this iteration is easily justified, see
e.g.
the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
30
]
.
Next, one readily checks that
a_{n}=0
if
nR<d(X,Y)
. Lastly, the general sums defining
a_{n}
satisfy the estimate

a_{n}\leq\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}^{n}C_{F}^{n-1}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Y}F(d(x,y))\quad\text{for any $n\geq 1$}

(2.28)

which can be argued as in the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
29
]
.
Since
C_{A}(Y,0)=0
, we have found that

C_{A}(Y,t)\leq\frac{\|A\|}{C_{F}}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Y}F(d(x,y))\sum_{n\geq\frac{d(X,Y)}{R}}\frac{(\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}C_{F}t)^{n}}{n!},

from which (
2.21
) follows.
∎

The argument in the proof above follows closely the arguments for the proof of Theorem 2 in
[
31
]
.
We have included this not only for completeness, but also because our later arguments use heavily
these finite range approximations. In fact, we state the following simplified version of the result in
[
31
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem 2 in
[
31
]
)
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a countable metric space equipped with an
F
-function
F
.
Let
\mathscr{L}=\{L_{Z}\}_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}
be a dissipative interaction with
\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}<\infty
.
Fix
X,Y\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
with

In the event that a particular model is bounded and finite range, then Theorem

provides an estimate with strong spatial decay.
To make this precise, let us first introduce some terminology. Let

be a countable metric space.
A dissipative interaction

.
Furthermore, we say that a dissipative interaction

\|\mathscr{L}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}\|L_{Z}\|_{cb}<\infty\,.

(2.31)

Corollary 2.3

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a

-function

on

Proof.

Let

We now use Theorem

to provide an estimate on the difference between the finite-volume dynamics and this finite-range approximation.
For the estimate which follows, it is convenient to introduce a variant of our decay function.
Recall that we are working on a countable metric space
(\Gamma,d)
equipped with an
F
-function
F

In terms of

Since

In this case, if

Theorem 2.4

Let
(\Gamma,d)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda:\\

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}\sum_{z\in\Lambda\setminus X(r)}F(d(x,z))\sum_{y\in X(r)\setminus X}F(d(z,y))

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{L}\|_{F}\|F\|\sum_{z\in\Lambda\setminus X(r)}F(d(x,z))

Theorem 2.8

In this section, we use the quasi-locality estimates proven in the previous section to obtain

quantified spatial decay, see Definition

bound is the content of Theorem
3.2
, and the methods we use are similar to those found in
[
29
]
.
We supplement Theorem

with more explicit estimates, depending on the decay of the
interaction generating the dynamics, in Lemma
3.3
and Lemma

. Next, we consider a class of dynamical fixed-points, see Definition

.
For these states, we obtain estimates on decay of correlations, see Theorem

.

3.1
Spatially Decaying States

The goal of this section is to estimate the effect of an irreversible dynamics
on the decay of correlations for states with quantified spatial decay.
To make this precise, we introduce the notion of a decay function which governs
the correlations for states on a local algebra. As in the previous section,
we consider quantum spin systems defined over a countable metric space
(\Gamma,d)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
be finite and let
\omega
be a state on the local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
.
We say that
\omega
has spatial correlations governed by
G_{\Lambda}
if there is a function

G_{\Lambda}:[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to[0,2]
for which given any
x,y\in[0,\infty)
the map
d\mapsto G_{\Lambda}(x,y,d)
is
non-increasing and

|\omega(AB)-\omega(A)\omega(B)|\leq\|A\|\|B\|G_{\Lambda}(|X|,|Y|,d(X,Y))

(3.1)

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
where
X
and
Y
are arbitrary subsets of
\Lambda
.

Of course, for any state
\omega
on
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
,
\omega
has correlations governed by
the constant function
G_{\Lambda}(x,y,d)=2
. On the other hand, if
\omega
is a
product state, then one may take
G_{\Lambda}(x,y,d)=0
for any
d>0
. In more interesting situations,
\omega
has
correlations governed by a function of the form
G_{\Lambda}(x,y,d)=x^{n}y^{m}\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(d)

where
n
and
m
are non-negative integers and
\tilde{G}_{\Lambda}(d)
decays swiftly to (or is identically) zero for large
d
. In any case, one has the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a countable metric space,
\mathscr{L}=\{L_{Z}\}_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}
a dissipative interaction,
and
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
finite. If
\omega
is a state on
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
with correlations
governed by
G_{\Lambda}
, then for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}

\left|\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB))-\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(A))\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B))\right|\leq\|A\|\|B\|G_{\Lambda}(|X(r)|,|Y(r)|,d(X(r),Y(r))+C_{A,B}^{\Lambda}(r,t).

(3.2)

for any
t\geq 0
and
2\leq 2r\leq d(X,Y)
. Here we have denoted by

\displaystyle C_{A,B}^{\Lambda}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|A\|\|T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B)-T_{t}^{Y(r)}(B)\|+\|B\|\|T_{t}^{\Lambda}(A)-T_{t}^{X(r)}(A)\|

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\|T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB)-T_{t}^{(X\cup Y)(r)}(AB)\|\,.

Proof.

For any
t\geq 0
, set
B_{t}^{\omega}=B-\omega(T^{\Lambda}_{t}(B))\mathbbm{1}
and observe that

\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB))-\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(A))\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B))=\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))\,.

(3.4)

With
Z=X\cup Y

|\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))|\leq|\omega(T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))|+|\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))-\omega(T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))|

(3.5)

for any

. If
2\leq 2r<d(X,Y)

T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB_{t}^{\omega})=T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB)-\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B))T_{t}^{Z(r)}(A)=T_{t}^{X(r)}(A)T_{t}^{Y(r)}(B)-\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B))T_{t}^{X(r)}(A)

In this case, the first term on the right hand side of (
3.5

\displaystyle|\omega(T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\omega(T_{t}^{X(r)}(A)T_{t}^{Y(r)}(B))-\omega(T_{t}^{X(r)}(A))\omega(T_{t}^{Y(r)}(B))\right|

(3.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\omega(T_{t}^{X(r)}(A))\left(\omega\left(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B)-T_{t}^{Y(r)}(B)\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|A\|\|B\|G_{\Lambda}\left(|X(r)|,|Y(r)|,d(X(r),Y(r))\right)+\|A\|\|T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B)-T_{t}^{Y(r)}(B)\|

For the second term on the right hand side of (
3.5

T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB_{t}^{\omega})-T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB_{t}^{\omega})=T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB)-T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB)-\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(B))\left(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(A)-T_{t}^{X(r)}(A)\right)

and therefore,

|\omega(T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))-\omega(T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB_{t}^{\omega}))|\leq\|T_{t}^{\Lambda}(AB)-T_{t}^{Z(r)}(AB)\|+\|B\|\|T_{t}^{\Lambda}(A)-T_{t}^{X(r)}(A)\|

(3.9)

Combining (
3.5
), (
3.7
), and (
3.9
), we have established (
3.25
).
∎

3.2
Dynamical Fixed-Points

The goal of this section is to quantify decay of correlations for a class of
dynamical fixed points. We discuss our assumptions on convergence to the fixed points
in relations to other applications in the literature in an appendix. As in the previous section, we first prove
a general bound, see Theorem
3.6
below, and then follow-up with more explicit estimates in
Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.8
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a countable metric space and
\mathscr{L}=\{L_{Z}\}_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}
be a dissipative interaction.
Take
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
finite and consider
\pi
a state on the local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
.
We say that
\pi
is a local dynamical fixed-point of
\mathscr{L}
with convergence governed by
g_{\Lambda}
if

1.

\pi\circ T^{\Lambda}_{t}=\pi
for all
t\geq 0
.

2.

There exists a function
g_{\Lambda}:[0,\infty)\to[0,2]
for which one has

|(\pi-\omega)\circ T^{\Lambda}_{t}(A)|\leq g_{\Lambda}(t)\|A\|

for all states
\omega
on
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
,
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
, and
t\geq 0
.

For any local dynamical fixed-point,
i.e.
state
\pi
on
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
satisfying 1 above, the function

g_{\Lambda}(t)=2
governs convergence to
\pi
in a trivial sense. We are more interested
in situations where the convergence is governed by a non-increasing function
g_{\Lambda}
with

g_{\Lambda}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. In the later case, one readily checks that any such local dynamical fixed-point is
unique.

The following result estimates correlations in such local dynamical fixed-points.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a countable metric space,
\mathscr{L}=\{L_{Z}\}_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)}
a dissipative interaction,
and
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
finite. Let
\pi
be a local dynamical fixed-point for
\mathscr{L}
with
convergence governed by
g_{\Lambda}
. For any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
where
X,Y\subset\Lambda

satisfy
d(X,Y)>0
, one has that

Appendix A
Appendix: Some Semigroup Theory

This appendix seeks to show that our definition of a local dynamical fixed-point as in Definiton
3.5
is logically equivalent to a few other conditions that are present in the literature. In particular, we show that
\|(\pi-\psi)(T^{\Lambda}_{t}(A))|\to 0
uniformly exponentially fast (see Theorem
A.1
). We also show that our notion is equivalent to the assumption that the associated Schrödinger picture semigroup satisfies rapid mixing in the sense of
[
19
, Theorem 1]
(see Theorem
A.8
). Because our constants depend on the volume, existence of a local dynamical fixed point implies neither global nor local rapid mixing in the sense of
[
5
,
11
]
.

We prove the following general theorem

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and let
L:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a bounded Lindblad generator with associated quantum dyanmical semigroup
(T_{t}|t\geq 0)
in the Heisenberg picture. By
(T^{\prime}_{t}|t\geq 0)
we mean the semigroup acting on
B(\mathcal{H})^{*}
by pre-composition. The following are equivalent:

1.

There exists a state
\pi\in B(\mathcal{H})^{*}
so that
\pi\circ T_{t}=\pi
for all
t\geq 0
and there exists a function
g_{\pi}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
so that
g_{\pi}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
and for all states
\psi\in B(\mathcal{H})^{*}
, and all
A\in B(\mathcal{H})
, one has

|(\psi-\pi)\circ T_{t}(A)|\leq\|A\|g_{\pi}(t)\,.

(A.1)

We call such a state a dynamical fixed point of
(T_{t}|t\geq 0)
.

2.

There exist constants
c>0
and
\gamma>0
, and a rank-one projection
P:B(\mathcal{H})^{*}\to B(\mathcal{H})^{*}
so that

\|T^{\prime}_{t}-P\|\leq ce^{-\gamma t}\,.

(A.2)

Before beginning the proof, let us mention an immediate application to our models in the following:

Corollary A.2
.

Let
(\Gamma,d)
be a discrete countable metric space, and let
\mathscr{L}
be a local dissipative interaction. Fix
\Lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\Gamma)
and let
(T^{\Lambda}_{t}|t\geq 0)
be the associated dynamical semigroup. Then, the following are equivalent:

1.

\pi
is a local dynamical fixed point (see Definition
3.5
).

2.

The state
\pi
is a fixed point of
(T^{\Lambda}_{t}|t\geq 0)
and there exist constants
c,\gamma>0
which may depend on
\Lambda
so that

|(\pi-\psi)\circ T^{\Lambda}_{t}(A)|\leq c\|A\|e^{-\gamma t}\,,\quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}^{*}\text{ and }A\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}\,.

(A.3)

for all states
\psi
and all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
.

In particular, if one of the above equivalent conditions holds in
\Lambda
, one can take
g_{\Lambda}(t)=ce^{-\gamma t}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
A.1
, there is a rank-one projection
P:\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}^{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}^{*}
so that the pre-composition semigroup
S_{t}:=(T^{\Lambda}_{t})^{\prime}
converges to
P
exponentially fast in the norm of
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}^{*}
. By the proof of Theorem
A.1
below, it follows that
P(\pi)=\pi
and for all
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}^{*}
, one has
P(f)=f(\mathbbm{1})\pi
. Then,

|(\psi-\pi)\circ T^{\Lambda}_{t}(A)|\leq\|A\|\|S_{t}(\psi)-S_{t}(\pi)\|=\|A\|\|(S_{t}-P)(\psi)\|\leq\|A\|\|S_{t}-P\|\leq c\|A\|e^{-\gamma t}\,.

(A.4)

The reverse implication is obvious.
∎

Before we begin the proof of Theorem
A.1
, we will recall several lemmas for the convenience of the reader. These can be found in more generality in
[
13
]
. We shall fix
X
as a finite-dimensional vector space equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|
. Moreover,
(S_{t}:t\geq 0)
will denote a semigroup of linear operators
S_{t}:X\to X
so that
S_{t}
is uniformly continuous in the sense that
\|S_{t}-\id\|\to 0
as
t\downarrow 0
. We will write
A
for the generator of
S_{t}
, which we note is a bounded operator on
X
.

Lemma A.3

(Proposition II.2.3
[
13
]
)
.

Let
X
be a vector space and let
Y\subset X
be a vector subspace of
X
. Let
(S_{t}|t\geq 0)
be a norm continuous semigroup on
X
and suppose that
S_{t}(Y)\subset Y
for all
t\geq 0
. If
A
is the generator of
S_{t}
, then the restriction
S_{t}|_{Y}
is a norm-continuous semigroup with generator
A|_{Y}
.

Proof.

The continuity and semigroup parts of the claim are obvious. To see that
A|_{Y}
generates
S_{t}|_{Y}
, note that for any
y\in Y
, we have

\left\|\frac{S_{t}|_{Y}y-y}{t}-A|_{Y}y\right\|_{Y}=\left\|\frac{S_{t}y-y}{t}-Ay\right\|_{X}\to 0\quad\text{ as }t\downarrow 0.

(A.5)

Since the generator of a semigroup is unique, it must be that
A|_{Y}
generates the restricted semigroup.
∎

Let us recall from
[
13
, Definition I.1.5]
the
growth bound

\omega_{0}
of a semigroup
(S_{t}:t\geq 0)
is the quantity

\omega_{0}=\inf\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\exists M_{r}\geq 1\colon\|S_{t}\|\leq M_{r}e^{rt}\}\,.

(A.6)

Recall that the spectral radius
rad(S_{t})
is the supremum over all the elements of the spectrum of
S_{t}
.

Let us recall an elementary subadditivity result.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable function that is subadditive and uniformly bounded on compact intervals. Then, the following limits exist:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t}=\inf_{t>0}\frac{f(t)}{t}\,.

(A.7)

For a proof, see
[
13
, Lemma V.1.23]
.

Lemma A.5

(Proposition V.1.22
[
13
]
)
.

Let
S_{t}
be a norm-continuous semigroup on a vector space
X
with generator
A
. The following equalities hold:

\omega_{0}=\inf_{t>0}t^{-1}\log\|S_{t}\|=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}\log\|S_{t}\|

(A.8)

In particular, have
rad(S_{t})=e^{t\omega_{0}}
.

Proof.

Note that the function
f(t):=\log\|S_{t}\|
is a measurable subadditive function. Moreover, since
A
is bounded, we have

\log(\|S_{t}\|)\leq\log e^{t\|A\|}=t\|A\|\,.

(A.9)

Hence,
f(t)
is uniformly bounded on compact intervals. Therefore, by the Lemma
A.4
,
v=\inf t^{-1}f(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}f(t)
exists. Note for all
t>0
,
e^{vt}\leq e^{\frac{t}{t}\log\|S_{t}\|}=\|S_{t}\|
by definition. One has
e^{v\cdot 0}=1=\|S_{0}\|
as well since
S_{0}=\id
. Thus
v\leq\omega_{0}
.

On the other hand, if
\epsilon>0
, there is some
t_{0}\geq 0
so that for any
t\geq t_{0}
, we have
t^{-1}f(t)\leq v+\epsilon
. Hence
\|S_{t}\|\leq e^{(v+\epsilon)t}
. Moreover, on
[0,t_{0}]
, we may bound
\|S_{t}\|
above uniformly by
e^{t_{0}\|A\|}\geq 1
. Thus,
\|S_{t}\|\leq M_{v+\epsilon}e^{(v+\epsilon)t}
for all
t\geq 0
by taking
M_{v+\epsilon}=\max\{1,e^{t_{0}\|A\|}\}
. Hence
\omega_{0}\leq v+\epsilon
, and so it must be that
\omega_{0}=v
.

Now, by the Gelfand-Hadamard formula (
cf.

[
4
]
or
[
38
]
) and the semigroup property, we know that for any
t>0
,

rad(S_{t})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{nt}\|^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\exp\left(\frac{t}{nt}\log\|T_{nt}\|\right)=e^{t\omega_{0}}

(A.10)

by continuity. That the relation holds when
t=0
follows readily from the fact that
S_{0}=\id
, completing the proof.
∎

The contents of the following Lemma can be found in Propositions V.3.3.2 and V.3.3.5 of
[
13
]
. See also Lyapunov’s Theorem V.3.3.6 in
[
13
]
.

Lemma A.6

(Lyapunov’s Theorem)
.

Let
(S_{t}|t\geq 0)
be a semigroup on a vector space
X
. The following are equivalent:

1.

The limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|S_{t}\|=0
.

2.

There exists
\epsilon>0
and
M>0
so that

\|S_{t}\|\leq Me^{-\epsilon t}

(A.11)

I.e.,
S_{t}
is uniformly exponentially stable.

Proof.

That 2 implies 1 is obvious here. To see the other direction, recall that
e^{t\omega_{0}}=rad(S_{t})\leq\|S_{t}\|
. Hence
\omega_{0}<0
, and the inequality follows from Lemma
A.5
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
S_{t}
be a norm-continuous semigroup on a finite dimensional vector space
X
with a generator
A
. Assume that the limit

\|\cdot\|-\lim_{t\to\infty}S_{t}=P\neq 0

(A.12)

exists. Then, there are constants
M\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
so that

\|S_{t}-P\|\leq Me^{-\epsilon t}

(A.13)

We follow the general idea of the proof given in Lemma V.4.2 of
[
13
]
.

Proof.

First, we claim that
P
is a projection onto the subspace
F:=\{x\in X\colon S_{t}x=x\,\forall t\geq 0\}
. To see this, note that for any
s\geq 0
, one has

\displaystyle S_{s}P=\lim_{t\to\infty}S_{s}S_{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}S_{t}S_{s}=\lim_{t\to\infty}S_{s+t}=P\,.

Therefore, if
S_{t}x=x
for all
t\geq 0
, then
Px=x
by passing to the limit. Hence,
F\subset PX
. On the other hand,
S_{t}P=P
so for any
y\in PX
, there is
x
so that

and in particular
S_{t}y=S_{t}Px=Px=y
for any

. That is,
y
is a fixed point of
S_{t}
.

Thus
X=PX\oplus(\id-P)X=F\oplus(\id-P)X

S_{t}|_{(\id-P)X}
is given by the composition
S_{t}(\id-P)
. Moreover, by the commutation of
S_{t}
and
P

(S_{t}|_{(\id-P)X}|t\geq 0)
is a norm continuous semigroup such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|S_{t}(\id-P)\|=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|S_{t}-S_{t}P\|=\lim_{t\to\infty}\|S_{t}-P\|=0\,.

In other words,
S_{t}(\id-P)
is an exponentially stable semigroup on
(\id-P)X
. Therefore, by Lyapunov’s theorem, there exists
M\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
so that

\|S_{t}|_{(\id-P)X}\|=\|S_{t}(\id-P)\|=\|S_{t}-P\|\leq Me^{-\epsilon t},

Recall that one may equip
B(\mathcal{H})^{*}

\|\xi_{h}\|=\|\xi_{1}\|+\|\xi_{2}\|

are positive linear functionals and
\|\Re(\xi)\|=\|\xi_{1}\|+\|\xi_{2}\|

We are now ready to complete the proof of Theorem

Proof.

(

): Let
P:B(\mathcal{H})^{*}\to B(\mathcal{H})^{*}

. Observe that
P
is a bounded, idempotent operator with
P(\pi)=\pi
. Observe that for any nonzero positive linear functional

\|(f-f(\mathbbm{1})\pi)\circ T_{t}\|=f(\mathbbm{1})\|(\tilde{f}-\pi)\circ T_{t}\|\leq f(\mathbbm{1})g_{\pi}(t)\,,

Let
\xi\in B(\mathcal{H})^{*}

\xi=\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}+i\xi_{3}-i\xi_{4}
be the Jordan decomposition of

\displaystyle\|(\xi-P(\xi))\circ T_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\xi_{1}-\xi_{1}(\mathbbm{1})\pi)\circ T_{t}\|+\|(-1)(\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}(\mathbbm{1})\pi)\circ T_{t}\|+\cdots

\displaystyle\qquad\cdots+\|(i)(\xi_{3}-\xi_{3}(\mathbbm{1})\pi)\circ T_{t}\|+\|(-i)(\xi_{4}-\xi_{4}(\mathbbm{1})\pi)\circ T_{t}\|

\displaystyle\leq g_{\pi}(t)\sum_{j=1}^{4}\xi_{j}(\mathbbm{1})=g_{\pi}(t)\sum_{j=1}^{4}\|\xi_{j}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\xi\|g_{\pi}(t)\to 0\text{ as }t\to\infty.

Thus,
\|\cdot\|_{B(\mathcal{H})^{*}}-\lim_{t\to\infty}T^{\prime}_{t}=P\neq 0

(A.15)

(2.\Rightarrow 1.)

\|\cdot\|-\lim_{t\to\infty}T_{t}^{\prime}=P\neq 0

is rank one. Observe that
PT_{t}^{\prime}=(\lim T_{s}^{\prime})T_{t}^{\prime}=\lim T_{t+s}=P=T_{t}^{\prime}P

P(B(\mathcal{H})^{*})

\psi

T_{t}^{\prime}\psi

Moreover, we note

\displaystyle|(\psi-\pi)\circ T_{t}(A)|

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|\|T_{t}^{\prime}\psi-P\psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|\|T_{t}^{\prime}-P\|\|\psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|ce^{-\gamma t},

from which the claim follows easily.
∎

We turn our attention to understanding how our dynamical fixed point assumption fits into the the Schrödinger picture. In particular, recall that a
periodic point
of a Lindbladian
L
, is any
X\in B(\mathcal{H})
where
LX=i\lambda X
for some real number
\lambda
. Note that fixed points are therefore periodic points with
\lambda=0
. Write
P_{\lambda}
for the projection onto the subspace associated to the eigenvalue
i\lambda
. In particular, one can define
T_{\phi}=\sum_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}
and
T_{t,\phi}=T_{t}T_{\phi}=\sum_{\lambda}e^{i\lambda t}P_{\lambda}
(see
[
11
,
39
]
for details). In particular, if
L
has a unique fixed point and no other periodic points, then
T_{\phi,t}=P_{0}
is just the projection onto the subspace spanned by the fixed point.

Recall the mixing coefficient
\eta(T^{\dagger}_{t})
(see
[
5
,
11
,
20
,
35
]
) defined via

\eta(T^{\dagger}_{t})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varrho\geq 0:\\
\Tr(\varrho)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}\|T^{\dagger}_{t}\varrho-T^{\dagger}_{\phi,t}\varrho\|_{1}.

(A.16)

We note that the mixing coefficient can be defined without reference to a specific model of an open quantum spin system (see
[
20
,
35
]
). We therefore state the following Theorem without reference to a specific model. However, we note that when Theorem
A.8
is applied to the case we consider in the main text,
\eta((T_{t}^{\Lambda})^{\dagger})
in general depends on the volume
\Lambda
. This is in contrast to the assumption in
[
5
,
11
]
where the authors of both studies require the mixing coefficient to decay at a volume-independent rate.

Theorem A.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite dimensional Hilbert space and let
T_{t}=\exp\{tL\}
be a quantum dynamical semigroup with generator
L
. Then, the following are logically equivalent.

(a)

The pre-composition semigroup
T_{t}^{\prime}
admits a dynamical fixed point (see Theorem
A.1
) and has no other periodic points.

(b)

The Schrödinger picture semigroup
T_{t}^{\dagger}
admits a fixed point with no other periodic points and
\eta(T^{\dagger}_{t})\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
S\subset B(\mathcal{H})^{*}
denote the set of states. Recall that for every
\psi\in S
, there exists a unique density matrix
\varrho_{\psi}
so that
\psi(\cdot)=\Tr[\varrho_{\psi}(\cdot)]=\Tr[\varrho_{\psi}^{*}(\cdot)]
. It is not difficult to show that the Schrödinger picture semigroup
T^{\dagger}_{t}
fixes
\varrho_{\pi}
if and only if the precomposition semigroup
T^{\prime}_{t}
fixes
\psi
.

Now, consider the following calculation:

\displaystyle\sup_{\psi\in S}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in B(\mathcal{H}):\\
\|A\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}|(\psi-\pi)T_{t}(A)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\psi\in S}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in B(\mathcal{H}):\\
\|A\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\Tr[(\varrho_{\psi}-\varrho_{\pi})T_{t}(A)]|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varrho\geq 0\\
\Tr\varrho=1\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in B(\mathcal{H}):\|A\|\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\Tr[(T_{t}^{\dagger}\varrho-T^{\dagger}_{t}\varrho_{\pi})(A)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varrho\geq 0\\
\Tr\varrho=1\end{subarray}}\|T^{\dagger}_{t}(\varrho)-T^{\dagger}_{t}(\varrho_{\pi})\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varrho\geq 0:\\
\Tr\varrho=1\end{subarray}}\|T_{t}^{\dagger}(\varrho)-\varrho_{\pi}\|_{1}=2\eta(T^{\dagger}_{t})

where
T^{\dagger}
denotes the conjugate transpose. Notice we have used the fact that the trace norm is dual to the operator norm with respect to the trace pairing to obtain the third equality.

In other words, if there exists a dynamical fixed point for
T_{t}
and the Schrödinger picture semigroup
T^{\dagger}_{t}
has no other periodic points, then

\eta(T_{t}^{\dagger})\leq\frac{1}{2}g_{\pi}(t)\to 0\text{ as }t\to\infty,

where
\eta
is the mixing coefficient defined above.

To finish the proof, we will show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the periodic points of
T_{t}^{\prime}
and
T_{t}^{\dagger}
. Let
\theta
be a periodic point of
T_{t}^{\prime}
. Then there is real
\lambda\neq 0
so that
T_{t}^{\prime}\theta=e^{i\lambda t}\theta
. By the Riesz representation theorem, there is
X_{\theta}\in B(\mathcal{H})
so that
\theta(\cdot)=\Tr[(X_{\theta})^{*}\cdot]
. In which case for any other
x\in B(\mathcal{H})
, one has

\Tr[(T^{\dagger}_{t}(X_{\theta}))^{*}x]=\Tr[(X_{\theta})^{*}T_{t}(x)]=\theta(T_{t}(x))=e^{i\lambda t}\theta(x)=\Tr[(e^{-i\lambda t}X_{\theta})^{*}x].

Hence
T^{\dagger}_{t}(X_{\theta})=e^{-i\lambda t}X_{\theta}
. Running this argument in reverse shows that
\theta\mapsto X_{\theta}
is a bijection between the periodic points.
∎

Remark A.9
.

It is not difficult to show that, as matrices,
T_{t}^{\prime}
is the transpose of
T_{t}
, where
T_{t}^{\dagger}
is the
conjugate
transpose. Therefore, we could have argued the last paragraph by appealing to elementary linear algebra as well.

Introduction

In 1914, Ramanujan
[
28
]
listed quite a few hypergeometric series
representations of
1/\pi

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(6k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}4^{k}}=\frac{4}{\pi},

where
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
stands for the rising factorial. In
1997, Van Hamme
[
36
]
numerically discovered 13 remarkable
p
-adic
analogues of Ramanujan-type formulas, such as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}-\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1}{4})^{4}\pmod{p^{2}},&\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{4}$,}\\[5.0pt]
0\pmod{p^{2}},&\text{if $p\equiv 3\pmod{4}$,}\end{cases}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}(6k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}4^{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p\pmod{p^{4}}\quad\text{if $p>3$},

(1.2)

where
p
is a odd prime and
\Gamma_{p}(x)
is the
p
-adic Gamma function. Three of them were proved by Van Hamme himself in
[
36
]
.
For generalizations of (
1.1
) modulo
p^{3}
and

, see
[
23
,
20
]
.
The supercongruence (
1.2
) was first confirmed by Long
[
22
]
. It was not until 2016 that Osburn and Zudilin
[
27
]
proved the last remaining case
of Van Hamme’s conjectural supercongruences. In 2019, the author and Zudilin
[
16
]
obtained a
q
-analogue
of (
1.1
) as follows:
modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-1)/2}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}}q^{2k}\equiv\begin{cases}\dfrac{(q^{2};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}^{2}}{(q^{4};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}^{2}}q^{(n-1)/2}&\text{if}\;n\equiv 1\pmod{4},\\[10.0pt]
0&\text{if}\;n\equiv 3\pmod{4},\end{cases}

(1.3)

which extends an early result due to the author and Zeng
[
14
, Corollary 1.2]
. Further generalizations of (
1.3
) modulo

\Phi_{n}(q)^{3}
can be found in the literature
[

10

At the moment we need to be familiar with the standard
q
-notation. The
q
-shifted factorial

is defined by
(a;q)_{n}=(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1})
for
n\geqslant 1
,
(a;q)_{0}=1
, and

\Phi_{n}(q)
denotes the
n

cyclotomic polynomial
in
q

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant k\leqslant n\\
\gcd(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}(q-e^{2\pi ik/n}),

where
i^{2}=-1
. For simplicity, we will often adopt the abbreviated notation
(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m};q)_{n}=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n}

for
n\geqslant 0
. Furthermore, the
q
-integer
is defined as
[n]_{q}=(1-q^{n})/(1-q)
.

Let
A(q)
and
B(q)
be two rational functions in
q
and
P(q)
a polynomial in
q
.
We call
A(q)
and
B(q)
congruent modulo
P(q)
, denoted by
A(q)\equiv B(q)\pmod{P(q)}
, if the numerator of the reduced fraction
A(q)-B(q)
is divisible by
P(q)

in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[q]
.

In 2015, Swisher
[
34
]
proved several
supercongruences of Van Hamme by utilizing Long’s method.
Meanwhile, she proposed some conjectures on supercongruences that
generalize the (A.2)–(L.2) supercongruences of Van Hamme. For example,
Swisher’s conjectural (C.3) and (H.3) supercongruences can
be respectively stated as follows: for any prime
p>3
and positive integer
r
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{r}-1)/2}(6k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}4^{k}}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{r-1}-1)/2}(6k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}4^{k}}\pmod{p^{4r}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{r}-1)/2}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\equiv\begin{cases}-\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1}{4})^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{r-1}-1)/2}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\pmod{p^{3r}},&\!\!\!\text{if $p\equiv 1\!\!\!\pmod{4}$,}\\[15.0pt]
\displaystyle p^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{r-2}-1)/2}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\pmod{p^{3r-1}},&\!\!\!\text{if $p\equiv 3\!\!\!\pmod{4}$ and $r\geqslant 2$.}\end{cases}

(1.5)

Given a prime
p
, we say that a power series
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}z^{k}
satisfies the Dwork congruence
[
3
,
24
]

if

\frac{f_{r+1}(z)}{f_{r}(z^{p})}\equiv\frac{f_{r}(z)}{f_{r-1}(z^{p})}\pmod{p^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[z]]}\quad\text{for $r=1,2,\ldots$},

(1.6)

where

f_{r}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}A_{k}z^{k}

is a truncation of
f(z)
.
Further, if we can replace the modulus in (
1.6
) by
p^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[z]]
for
s=s_{r}>r
, then we will also say that
f(z)
satisfies
a Dwork supercongruence. Formally, we require the condition
f_{1}(z^{p})=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}A_{k}z^{pk}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[z]]}

so that (
1.6
) is well-defined. But this may be weakened to
f_{1}(z^{p})\not\equiv 0\pmod{p^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[z]]}

provided that the congruences (
1.6
) hold modulo
p^{mr}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[z]]
for certain
m>1
.
Thus, it is reasonable to call Swisher’s conjectures in
[
34
]
Dwork-type supercongruences.

The author
[
9
]

Recently, the author and Zudilin

18

The

-binomial coefficient

{M\brack N}={M\brack N}_{q}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{M}}{(q;q)_{N}(q;q)_{M-N}},&\text{if $0\leq N\leq M$},\\[5.0pt]

The so-called

(see

[

and Désarménien
[

Let

be a positive integer, and let
a,b,r,s
be nonnegative integers with
b,s\leq n-1
. Then

{an+b\brack rn+s}\equiv{a\choose r}{b\brack s}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

In order to prove Theorem

, we need the following two lemmas. The proof of the first one is easy and can be found in

[
13

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n
be a positive odd integer. Let
r
and
s
be nonnegative integers with
s\leqslant n-1
.
Then

(-q;q)_{rn+s}\equiv 2^{r}(-q;q)_{s}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

Let
m

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}}q^{2k}

(2.1)

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\frac{(q;q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}}=\frac{1}{(-q;q)_{k}^{2}}{2k\brack k}.

Thus, the left-hand side of (
2.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}}q^{2k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{q^{2k}}{(-q;q)_{k}^{4}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}}{2k\brack k}^{2}{2k\brack k}_{q^{2}}

For odd

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{q^{2rn+2s}}{(-q;q)_{rn+s}^{4}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{rn+s}^{2}}{2rn+2s\brack rn+s}^{2}{2rn+2s\brack rn+s}_{q^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv\frac{{2r\choose r}^{3}}{2^{6r}}\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{q^{2s}}{(-q;q)_{s}^{4}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{s}^{2}}{2s\brack s}^{2}{2s\brack s}_{q^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv\frac{{2r\choose r}^{3}}{2^{6r}}\sum_{s=0}^{(n-1)/2}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{s}^{2}(q^{2};q^{4})_{s}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{s}^{2}(q^{4};q^{4})_{s}}q^{2s}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

The proof then follows from the

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{(q^{2-n},q^{2+n};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}}{(q^{4-n},q^{4+n};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}}q^{(n-1)/2}

\displaystyle=[n]\frac{(q^{3};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}{(q^{5};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

for
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}

By Lemma

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We first recall some basic properties of Morita’s
p
-adic Gamma function
[
1
,
29
]
.
For any odd prime
p
, the
p
-adic Gamma function is defined by
\Gamma_{p}(0)=1
, and

\Gamma_{p}(n)=(-1)^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<k<n\\
p\nmid k\end{subarray}}k

for integers
n\geqslant 1
.
Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the ring of all
p
-adic integers. We can extend
\Gamma_{p}
to all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by the limit:

\Gamma_{p}(x)=\lim_{x_{n}\to x}\Gamma_{p}(x_{n}),

where
x_{n}
is any positive integer sequence that
p
-adically tends to
x
.
By the definition, one has

\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+1)}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-x,\quad p\nmid x,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle-1,\quad p\mid x.\end{cases}

(3.1)

It is also known that, for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, there holds

\Gamma_{p}(x)\Gamma_{p}(1-x)=(-1)^{a_{0}(x)},

(3.2)

where
a_{0}(x)
is the smallest positive integer such that
a_{0}(x)\equiv x\pmod{p}
.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we also need the following result (see
[
23
, Theorem 14]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
r
a positive integer. Then, for
a,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

\Gamma_{p}(a+mp^{r})\equiv\Gamma_{p}(a)+\Gamma_{p}^{\prime}(a)mp^{r}\pmod{p^{2r}}.

(3.3)

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\Gamma(x)
be the classical Gamma function. The
r=1
case is already known. This can also be deduced from comparing (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) and
noticing that, for
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{(q^{2};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}^{2}}{(q^{4};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}^{2}}q^{(n-1)/2}

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{(q^{2-n},q^{2+n};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}}{(q^{4-n},q^{4+n};q^{4})_{(n-1)/4}}q^{(n-1)/2}

\displaystyle=[n]\frac{(q^{3};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}{(q^{5};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

We now assume that
r>1
. In view of (
3.1
), there hold

\displaystyle\frac{(\frac{3}{4})_{\frac{p^{r}-1}{2}}}{(\frac{5}{4})_{\frac{p^{r}-1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2p^{r}+1}{4})\Gamma(\frac{5}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{3}{4})\Gamma(\frac{2p^{r}+3}{4})}=\frac{\frac{3p}{4}\cdot\frac{7p}{4}\cdots\frac{2p^{r}-3p}{4}}{\frac{p}{4}\cdot\frac{5p}{4}\cdots\frac{2p^{r}-p}{4}}\cdot\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+1}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{5}{4})}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+3}{4})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\frac{3}{4})_{\frac{p^{r-1}-1}{2}}}{\frac{p}{4}(\frac{5}{4})_{\frac{p^{r-1}-1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+1}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{5}{4})}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+3}{4})}.

It follows that

\displaystyle p\frac{(\frac{3}{4})_{(p^{r}-1)/2}(\frac{5}{4})_{(p^{r-1}-1)/2}}{(\frac{5}{4})_{(p^{r}-1)/2}(\frac{3}{4})_{(p^{r-1}-1)/2}}=4\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+1}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{5}{4})}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+3}{4})}=-\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+1}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{4})}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+3}{4})}.

By (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+1}{4})}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{2p^{r}+3}{4})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(p+3)/4}\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1+2p^{r}}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1-2p^{r}}{4})

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{(p+3)/4}(\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1}{4})+\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma_{p}^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{4})p^{r})(\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1}{4})-\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma_{p}^{\prime}(\tfrac{1}{4})p^{r})

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{(p+3)/4}\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1}{4})^{2}\pmod{p^{2r}}.

The proof then follows from the identity
\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})=(-1)^{(p+3)/4}
.
∎

q
-congruence

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{q^{k}}{(-q;q)_{k}}{2k\brack k}\equiv(-1)^{(n-1)/2}q^{(n^{2}-1)/4}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}},

(5.6)

which was conjectured by Tauraso
[
35
]
for
n
being an odd prime.
The author also conjectured that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q^{k}{2k\brack k}\equiv\left(\frac{-3}{n}\right)q^{(n^{2}-1)/3}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}},

(5.7)

which was recently confirmed by Liu and Petrov
[
21
]
.

Here we shall prove the following Dwork-type
q
-generalizations of (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), confirming a conjecture of the author and Zudilin
[
18
, Conjecture 4.8]
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
n>1
be an odd integer and let
r\geqslant 1
. Then, modulo
\Phi_{n^{r}}(q)^{2-d}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{n^{j}}(q)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n^{r}-1)/d}\frac{q^{k}}{(-q;q)_{k}}{2k\brack k}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{(n-1)/2}q^{(n-1)(1+n^{2r-1})/4}\sum_{k=0}^{(n^{r-1}-1)/d}\frac{q^{nk}}{(-q^{n};q^{n})_{k}}{2k\brack k}_{q^{n}},

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n^{r}-1)/d}q^{k}{2k\brack k}

\displaystyle\equiv q^{(n-1)(1+n^{2r-1})/3}\bigg(\frac{-3}{n}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{(n^{r-1}-1)/d}q^{nk}{2k\brack k}_{q^{n}},

(5.9)

where
d=1,2
. When
d=1
, the second
q
-congruence is still true for even integers
n
.

Sketch of proof.

For
d=1
, the
q
-congruences (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) follow from (
5.6
) and (
5.7
)
and the following generalizations of them:
for all positive integers
m
and
n
,
modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{q^{k}}{(-q;q)_{k}}{2k\brack k}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{(n-1)/2}q^{(n^{2}-1)/4}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}{2k\choose k}\quad\text{($n$ is odd)},

(5.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}q^{k}{2k\brack k}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{-3}{n}\right)q^{(n^{2}-1)/3}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{2k\choose k}.

(5.11)

From (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-1)/2}\frac{q^{k}}{(-q;q)_{k}}{2k\brack k}_{q}

\displaystyle\equiv(-1)^{(n-1)/2}q^{(n^{2}-1)/4}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)},

(5.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-1)/2}q^{k}{2k\brack k}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{-3}{n}\right)q^{(n^{2}-1)/3}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(5.13)

Likewise, we can prove the
d=2
case of (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) by using (
5.10
)–(
5.13
).
∎

Sun
[
33
, Conjecture 3 (ii),(iii)]
proposed the following conjecture: for any prime
p
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}{2k\choose k}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigg(\frac{-1}{p}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r-1}-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}{2k\choose k}\pmod{p^{2r}}\quad\text{($p>2$)},

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r}-1}{2k\choose k}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigg(\frac{-3}{p}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{p^{r-1}-1}{2k\choose k}\pmod{p^{2r}},

(5.15)

and these expectations were recently confirmed by Zhang and Pan
[
39
]
. It is easy to see that (
5.8
) and (
5.9
)
are
q
-analogues of (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) modulo
p^{r+1}
. Complete
q
-analogues of (
5.14
) and (
5.15
)
are still not known.

6.
Open problems and concluding remarks

Liu
[
19
]
established the following generalization of (
1.1
): for any odd prime
p
and positive integer
m
,

\sum_{k=0}^{mp-1}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\equiv\begin{cases}-\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1}{4})^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\pmod{p^{2}},&\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{4}$,}\\[5.0pt]
0\pmod{p^{2}},&\text{if $p\equiv 3\pmod{4}$,}\end{cases}

(6.1)

Recently, the author
[
10
]
gave a
q
-analogue of the second case of (
6.1
):
for positive integers
m
and
n
with
n\equiv 3\pmod{4}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}}q^{2k}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}},

(6.2)

which were previously conjectured by the author and Zudilin
[
16
]
.

We find the following
q
-analogue of the first case of (
6.1
), which is also a refinement of Lemma
2.2
.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers with
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
n>1
.
Then, modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}}q^{2k}

\displaystyle\equiv[n]\frac{(q^{3};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}{(q^{5};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}.

(6.3)

For
n\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, one sees that
[n](q^{3};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}
is divisible by
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
while
(q^{5};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}
is coprime with
\Phi_{n}(q)
,
and so (
6.3
) reduces to (
6.2
) in this case. For any prime
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, we have

p\frac{(\frac{3}{4})_{(p-1)/2}}{(\frac{5}{4})_{(p-1)/2}}\equiv-\Gamma_{p}(\tfrac{1}{4})^{4}\pmod{p^{2}}.

Letting
n=p
be a prime and taking the limits as
q\to 1
in (
6.3
), we are led to the first part of (
6.1
).

It is not difficult to verify that, for any positive odd integer
n
,

\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\equiv\frac{(q^{n};q^{2n})_{k}^{2}(q^{2n};q^{4n})_{k}}{(q^{2n};q^{2n})_{k}^{2}(q^{4n};q^{4n})_{k}}q^{2nk}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(6.4)

Thus, the
q
-congruence can also be written as follows: modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}^{2}(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}}q^{2k}

\displaystyle\equiv[n]\frac{(q^{3};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}{(q^{5};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(q^{n};q^{2n})_{k}^{2}(q^{2n};q^{4n})_{k}}{(q^{2n};q^{2n})_{k}^{2}(q^{4n};q^{4n})_{k}}q^{2nk}.

Recently, the author
[
6
]
gave another
q
-analogues of the second case of (
6.1
):
for positive integers
m
and
n
with
n\equiv 3\pmod{4}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(1+q^{4k+1})(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}^{3}}{(1+q)(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}^{3}}q^{k}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}},

(6.5)

which were previously conjectured by the author and Zudilin
[
16
]
.

We have the following different
q
-analogue of the first case of (
6.1
), which is also a refinement of (
4.2
).

Conjecture 6.2
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers with
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
n>1
.
Then, modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(1+q^{4k+1})(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}^{3}}{(1+q)(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}^{3}}q^{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{[n]_{q^{2}}(q^{3};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}{(q^{5};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}q^{(1-n)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}.

(6.6)

Likewise, the
q
-congruence (
6.6
) has the following equivalent form:
modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{mn-1}\frac{(1+q^{4k+1})(q^{2};q^{4})_{k}^{3}}{(1+q)(q^{4};q^{4})_{k}^{3}}q^{k}

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{[n]_{q^{2}}(q^{3};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}{(q^{5};q^{4})_{(n-1)/2}}q^{(1-n)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{(1+q^{n(4k+1)})(q^{2n};q^{4n})_{k}^{3}}{(1+q^{n})(q^{4n};q^{4n})_{k}^{3}}q^{nk}.

We believe that Lemma
2.3
can be strengthened as follows.

Conjecture 6.3
.

The
q
-congruence (
2.3
) holds modulo
\Phi_{n^{s}}(q)^{2}

By the proof of Lemma
2.3

Conjecture 6.4

Let

be an integer greater than

\displaystyle\frac{(q;q^{2})_{(n^{r}-1)/4}(q^{2n};q^{2n})_{(n^{r-1}-1)/4}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{(n^{r}-1)/4}(q^{n};q^{2n})_{(n^{r-1}-1)/4}}\equiv\frac{(q;q^{2})_{(n^{s}-1)/4}(q^{2n};q^{2n})_{(n^{s-1}-1)/4}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{(n^{s}-1)/4}(q^{n};q^{2n})_{(n^{s-1}-1)/4}}.

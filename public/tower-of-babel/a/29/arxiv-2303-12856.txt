Anti-symmetric Barron functions and their approximation with sums of determinants
Nilin Abrahamsen, Lin Lin

Abstract
A fundamental problem in quantum physics is to encode functions that are completely anti-symmetric under permutations of identical particles. The Barron space consists of high-dimensional functions that can be parameterized by infinite neural networks with one hidden layer. By explicitly encoding the anti-symmetric structure, we prove that the anti-symmetric functions which belong to the Barron space can be efficiently approximated with sums of determinants. This yields a factorial improvement in complexity compared to the standard representation in the Barron space and provides a theoretical explanation for the effectiveness of determinant-based architectures in ab-initio quantum chemistry.



1
Introduction

To simulate a physical system it is essential to construct a model which respects the
symmetries
of the real-world problem. A prominent example is when a function is defined on
sets
of points
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, in which case the function can be viewed as a
permutation-invariant
function of an input vector. A symmetry need not mean that a function is invariant to transformations of its input; more generally it can map transformations of the input to transformations of the output through a group homomorphism.
Equivariance
is one such example where a transformation of the input gives rise to the same transformation on the output. Another such symmetry is
anti-symmetry
where a permutation of the input vector multiplies the output by the
sign
of the permutation.

Accurate modeling of fermionic systems is one of the most challenging and interesting problems in science. For example, the solution of the Schrödinger equation underlies all chemical properties of a given atomic system.
Due to the Pauli exclusion principle, the fermionic wavefunction is anti-symmetric with respect to particle exchange.
When the number of fermions grows, effective parametrization of such wavefunctions can become increasingly difficult for many systems of interest.
Anti-symmetric functions also arise in other contexts in machine learning such as determinantal point processes
[
4
]
where they are used to ensure diverse samples.

In the past decade there has been an explosive growth of techniques using neural networks (NN) as universal function approximators. The practical applicability of NNs is brought about by new software tools, hardware optimizations, as well as improved algorithms. NN approximators also significantly broaden the parameterization class for anti-symmetric functions in quantum physics
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. The combination of NN with variational Monte Carlo (VMC) methods provides a new path towards a low-scaling, systematically improvable method to approach the exact solution.

Despite recent progresses it is unclear how to construct a universal NN representation of anti-symmetric functions that does not obviously suffer from the curse of the dimensionality
[
5
,
8
,
11
,
12
]
.
In the absence of symmetry constraints, very simple NN structures such as an NN with one hidden layer (sometimes also referred to as a “two-layer” NN) is already a universal function approximator
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
Therefore in principle, explicitly antisymmetrizing a NN with one hidden layer can parameterize universal anti-symmetric functions.
One obvious drawback of this strategy is that the computational cost of the antisymmetrization step still increases factorially with respect to the system size.
Nonetheless, such an explicitly anti-symmetrized NN structure has been recently studied in VMC calculations, which can yield effectively the exact ground state energy for small atoms and molecules
[
10
]
.

Conversely, determinant-based NN constructions of anti-symmetric functions can be evaluated efficiently. But their expressive power is unclear except in the setting of a combinatorially large number of determinants spanning the entire anti-symmetric subspace. It is therefore prudent to know if the efficient determinant-based constructions are able to capture the a priori intractable class of explicitly anti-symmetrized neural networks.

Consider a system of

where we have defined

denote the set of anti-symmetric functions

In the machine learning literature there is a rich body of works related to permutation-invariant data, i.e., when the input data is a set
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
17
]

A widely used class of Ansatzes for anti-symmetric functions takes the form of a

The representation of anti-symmetric functions is extensively studied in physics, but the literature on anti-symmetrized neural networks is sparse.
Slater determinants can span a dense subset of the anti-symmetric space but the representation is very inefficient.
Indeed, even in the case of a finite single-particle state space

we would require
\binom{|\Omega|}{n}
Slater determinants to span the anti-symmetric space.

[
18
]
finds certain anti-symmetric functions that cannot be efficiently approximated using a simple sum of Slater determinants, but can be effectively expressed using a more complex Ansatz called the Slater-Jastrow form.

The FermiNet
[
8
]
and PauliNet
[
7
]
Ansatz have significantly expanded the representation power of the sum of determinants, by composing the orbitals with an equivariant mapping, and by parameterizing both using neural networks. The resulting structure can be expressed as a sum of generalized determinants

\psi(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\det(E_{k}(\mathbf{x})),

(1)

where
E_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{nd}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is equivariant, meaning that
E_{k}(\pi\mathbf{x})=\pi(E_{k}(\mathbf{x}))
and the permutation

only exchanges rows of

.

While the representation power of the Ansatz of the form (

[
8
]

Proof of universality from
[
8
]

Introduce an ordering

on vectors in

\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}

\pi
be the permutation such that

is sorted. That is,
\mathbf{x}=(\tilde{x}_{\pi(1)},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{\pi(n)})

\tilde{\mathbf{y}}=\operatorname{diag}((-1)^{\pi}\psi(\mathbf{x}),1,\ldots,1)
.

The argument above has the drawback that the mapping
E
is highly discontinuous. In this work we therefore aim to approximate a more restricted class of anti-symmetric functions with an Ansatz which is continuous with respect to
\mathbf{x}
and obtain a quantizative error bound relative to a known complexity measure.

2
Main result

We state our approximation result in terms of an upper bound on the error in the norm on

Lemma 8
.

\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\nu}\leq 1
for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}
.

Proof.

B_{ij}^{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})}
is the Gram matrix of the
n
vectors

so it is a positive semidefinite matrix. It then satisfies
\det(B^{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})})\leq\prod_{i}B_{ii}^{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})}
by Hadamard’s theorem. So

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle=\det(B^{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})})

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i}B_{ii}^{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})}=\prod_{i=1}\langle e_{w_{i}}|e_{w_{i}}\rangle_{\nu}^{n}=1.

(31)

∎

\displaystyle B^{(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[e^{i(w_{j1}-w_{i1})Y}]\cdots\mathbb{E}[e^{i(w_{j1}-w_{i1})Y}]

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|w_{j}-w_{i}\|^{2}}.

(34)

We apply
Eq.

and expand the square to obtain:

\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{\nu}^{2}=e^{-\|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}}\det\big((e^{w_{i}\cdot w_{j}})_{ij}\big).

(35)

7
Determinant bound

To obtain an upper bound on
Eq.

35
we decompose the matrix
(e^{w_{i}\cdot w_{j}})_{ij}
into a sum
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k}
, bounding the ranks and operator norms of the terms
Q_{k}
. For
L=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\operatorname{rank}Q_{k}
we can then bound the
L
-th eigenvalue as the tail sum
\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\|Q_{k}\|
. Taking the product of the eigenvalues yields a bound on the determinant and therefore on the norms of anti-symmetrized plane waves
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{w}}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. Then
(e^{v_{i}\cdot w_{j}})_{ij}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q_{k}
where

\operatorname{rank}Q_{k}\leq\binom{k+d-1}{d-1},\qquad\|Q_{k}\|\leq\frac{n(\|v\|_{\infty}\|w\|_{\infty}d)^{k}}{k!}.

(36)

Proof.

Let
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})
and
(\tilde{c}_{1},\dots,\tilde{c}_{d})
be the columns of
v
and
w
and let
\odot
denote elementwise operations (exponentiation and product, respectively). Then,

(e^{v_{i}\cdot w_{j}})_{ij}=e^{\odot\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\tilde{c}_{i}^{T}}=\odot_{i=1}^{d}e^{\odot c_{i}\tilde{c}_{i}^{T}}.

(37)

We first consider each factor
e^{\odot c_{i}\tilde{c}_{i}^{T}}
separately. Elementwise multiplication of rank-one matrices given as outer products corresponds to elementwise multiplication of the vectors,
ab^{T}\odot\tilde{a}\tilde{b}^{T}=(a\odot\tilde{a})(b\odot\tilde{b})^{T}
. Therefore, applying the Taylor expansion entrywise,

e^{\odot c\tilde{c}^{T}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(c\tilde{c}^{T})^{\odot k}}{k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(c^{\odot k})(\tilde{c}^{\odot k})^{T}}{k!},

(38)

where
c=c_{i}
,
\tilde{c}=\tilde{c}_{i}
are column vectors. Apply (
38
) to each factor of (
37
) and expand the sums,

\displaystyle\odot_{i=1}^{d}e^{\odot c_{i}\tilde{c}_{i}^{T}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\odot_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}^{\odot k_{i}})(\odot_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{c}_{i}^{\odot k_{i}})^{T}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}!}.

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{k}{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(\odot_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}^{\odot k_{i}})(\odot_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{c}_{i}^{\odot k_{i}})^{T}

(40)

Let
Q_{k}
be the innermost sum of (
40
). We estimate the maximum over the entries,

\|Q_{k}\|_{\operatorname{max}}\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}^{k}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}^{k}}{k!}\sum_{k_{1}+\ldots+k_{d}=k}^{\infty}\binom{k}{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}=\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}^{k}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}^{k}d^{k}}{k!}

and apply the inequality
\|Q_{k}\|\leq n\|Q_{k}\|_{\operatorname{max}}
.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\lambda_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots
be the absolute values of the eigenvalues of
(e^{v_{i}\cdot w_{j}})_{ij}
and let
\mu=\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\infty}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}d
. Then
\lambda_{0}\leq ne^{\mu}
, and for
\mu\leq 1/2
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{L}\leq\frac{2n}{p!}\mu^{p},\qquad L=\binom{p+d-1}{d},

(41)

where the case
p=0
of (
41
) holds with the interpretation
L=\binom{d-1}{d}=0
,
\lambda_{0}\leq ne^{1/2}\leq 2n
.

Proof.

From the identity

\binom{p+d-1}{d}=1+d+\binom{d+1}{d-1}+\cdots+\binom{p+d-2}{d-1},

there are

1+d+\binom{d+1}{d-1}+\cdots+\binom{p+d-2}{d-1}\geq\operatorname{rank}Q_{0}+\cdots+\operatorname{rank}Q_{p-1}

eigenvalues in front of
\lambda_{L}
where
L=\binom{p+d-1}{d}
, and we have used
Lemma

9
. By the min-max principle,

\lambda_{L}\leq\left\|\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}Q_{k}\right\|\leq n\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{k}}{k!}=\frac{n}{p!}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\mu^{k}=\frac{n}{p!}\frac{\mu^{p}}{1-\mu}\leq\frac{2n}{p!}\mu^{p}.

∎

Proposition 11
.

Let
\gamma=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{d}}
and let
p
be any integer such that
\binom{p+d-1}{d}\leq n/2
and

\det((e^{w_{i}\cdot w_{j}})_{ij})\leq\Big(\frac{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}}{2\gamma}\Big)^{pn}\quad\text{ for }\quad\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma.

(42)

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}

Lemma

10
and the assumptions on

\lambda_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\leq\frac{2n}{p!}\mu^{p}\leq\frac{\mu^{p}}{2n}

|\det((e^{w_{i}\cdot w_{j}})_{ij})|\leq\lambda_{0}^{n/2}\lambda_{n/2}^{n/2}\leq(\mu^{p})^{n/2}=(\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{d})^{pn}=(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\infty}}{\gamma})^{pn}

11.1
Estimating the anti-symmetric Barron norm

The Barron norm of
Eq.

2
is defined in terms of the ReLU activation, but we can similarly define a Barron norm
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}}^{[\sigma]}
with any other activation function
\sigma
. Then
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}}=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}}^{[\operatorname{ReLU}]}
by definition. We observe that the definition is not overly sensitive to the choice of activation function:

Lemma 18
.

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}}^{[\operatorname{ReLU}]}\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}}^{[\operatorname{softplus}]},

where
\operatorname{softplus}(y)=\log(1+e^{y})
. Similarly,
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{[\operatorname{ReLU}]}
is bounded by
\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{[\operatorname{softplus}]}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\operatorname{softplus}
is in the closed convex hull of translates of
{\sigma}
. That is, it suffices to write it as a convolution

{\sigma}*\phi=\operatorname{softplus}

(69)

for some probability distribution
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}
. But we can solve for
\phi
by differentiating twice:

\displaystyle{\sigma}^{\prime}*\phi

\displaystyle=\operatorname{softplus}^{\prime}=\sigma,

(70)

\displaystyle\phi=\delta*\phi={\sigma}^{\prime\prime}*\phi

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma(1-\sigma).

(71)

where
\sigma
is the logistic sigmoid function. clearly
\phi=\sigma(1-\sigma)
is positive and
\int\phi=\int\sigma^{\prime}=1
, so
\operatorname{softplus}
is in the closed convex hull of translates of
{\sigma}
,
∎

Remark 19
.

Conversely to
Lemma

18
softplus can approximate ReLU by using weights
a/t,tb,t\mathbf{w}
with
t\to\infty
without changing
\varphi(\rho)
.

To numerically estimate the Barron norm of a function we make use of
Lemma

18
and define

f_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}\operatorname{softplus}(\mathbf{w}^{(k)}\cdot x+b_{k}),

(72)

where
\mathbf{w}^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{nd}
and
b_{k},a_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
for each

. The smoother activation function allows us to use fewer neurons to approximate smooth features, and is justified by
Lemma

18
. Sharper features can be approximated as in
Remark

19

\psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}=\mathcal{A}f_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}.

(73)

For functions of this form we have

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\|\mathcal{A}f_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}\|_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W),

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W):=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}|a_{k}|(\|\mathbf{w}^{(k)}\|_{1}+b_{k}).

We then minimize over

to estimate
\|\psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Given a target function
\psi
we define its

\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{(\epsilon)}=\inf\{\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{A}}\>|\>\|\psi^{\prime}-\psi\|^{2}\leq\epsilon\}
. To estimate
\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{(\epsilon)}
we then implement a supervised (SGD) learning procedure for
\psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}
with a loss function

consisting of two penalties:

L(\psi^{\prime})=\tau_{\epsilon}(\|\psi_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W}-\psi\|^{2})+\lambda\varphi(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W),

where
\lambda
is a small constant, and
\tau_{\epsilon}(y)=\max\{y-\epsilon,0\}
. After the first penalty has converged we then use the optimal value of
\varphi(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},W)
as our estimate of
\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{(\epsilon)}
.

12
Conclusion

We have shown that anti-symmetric functions in the Barron space can be efficiently approximated by determinant-based neural network architectures, and the number of determinants depends on the anti-symmetric Barron norm of the function. Compared to existing bounds for neural network approximations, we obtain a factorially improved error bound. Our result illustrates the importance of choosing an Ansatz which reflects the known symmetries of the problem. It is an open question whether the anti-symmetric Barron norm is a useful characterization of certain challenging quantum states in practice.

\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{P}}\geq\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{B}}
. To show this, fix
\psi
and let
f_{\rho}
be such that
\mathcal{P}{f_{\rho}}=\psi
and
\varphi(\rho)\to\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{P}}
. We need a representation of
\mathcal{P}f_{\rho}
as
f_{\rho^{\prime}}
for some measure
\rho^{\prime}
to bound its raw Barron norm. But indeed,
\psi=\mathcal{P}f_{\rho}=f_{\rho^{\prime}}
where

\rho^{\prime}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}\rho_{\pi}\quad\rho_{\pi}(a,b,\mathbf{w})=\rho((-1)^{\pi}a,b,\pi(\mathbf{w})).

\varphi(\rho_{\pi})=\varphi(\rho)
for each
\pi
, so the same holds for
\rho^{\prime}
. Now
\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq\varphi(\rho^{\prime})=\varphi(\rho)\to\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{P}}
which proves the inequality.

∎

Abstract
. In an earlier paper, we introduced “bordered knot algebras”, which are graded algebras indexed by a pair of integers ( m , k ) (m,k) . In a subsequent paper, we introduced a two-parameter family of differential graded algebra, the “pong algebras”, and identified their homology with the bordered knot algebras, and characterized the induced A ∞ A_{\infty} structure on the homology. The aim of the present paper is to give an explicit, combinatorial model for this A ∞ A_{\infty} structure on the bordered knot algebras, and a further weighted deformation of this structure, in the case where k = 1 k=1 .

We give the following simple, concrete description of

for

Consider the quiver

Definition 2.1

An algebra element in

with values in

, called the

\displaystyle|\iota|

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle|L_{i}|

\displaystyle=|R_{i}|=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{e}}_{i}

\displaystyle|U_{1}|

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}_{1}

\displaystyle|U_{m}|

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}_{m},

We consider graded

(2.1)

\displaystyle|\mu_{\ell}(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}))|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|a_{i}|

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}(\mu_{\ell}(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}))

\displaystyle=\ell-2+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathrm{gr}(a_{i}).

3.
Weighted
A_{\infty}
algebras

Throughout this paper, we will use the language of weighted
A_{\infty}

algebras from
[
7
]
, recalling the basic definitions
here. For simplicity, these will be over a ground ring
\mathfrak{k}
with
characteristic
2
. We return to the case of signs in
Section
6
. The definition of a weighted
A_{\infty}

algebra is as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix a real
n
-dimensional vector space
W
with a preferred basis

\{\mathrm{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, and let
W_{\geq 0}
denote the set of vectors that
can be written in the form
\sum_{i}w_{i}\mathrm{e}_{i}
where
w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
. Fix an algebra
\mathfrak{k}
with characteristic
2
.
A
W
-weighted
A_{\infty}
algebra
over
\mathfrak{k}
is the following object is the following data:

•

a graded
\mathfrak{k}
-bimodule
A

•

for each
w\in W
, a linear map
of
\mathfrak{k}
-bimodules

\mu^{w}_{n}\colon\overbrace{A\otimes\dots\otimes A}^{n}\to A,

where the tensor it taken over
\mathfrak{k}
. Moreover,
\mu^{w}_{n}
is
of degree
n-2+2\sum w_{i}
; i.e. if
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are homogeneous,
then
\mu^{w}_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is homogeneous of degree

\mathrm{gr}(\mu^{w}_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))=\left(\sum\mathrm{gr}(a_{i})\right)+n-2+2\left(\sum_{i}w_{i}\right).

In particular, when the sequence of algebra elements is empty, we obtain
elements
\mu^{w}_{0}\in A
of degree
-2+2\sum w_{i}
.
The vector
w\in W
is called the weight of the operation
\mu^{w}_{n}
.

•

The element
\mu^{\vec{0}}_{0}=0
.

These operations are further required to satisfy the following structure relation for all
sequences
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
in
A

and vectors
w\in W_{\geq 0}
:

\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n+1,u+v=w}\mu^{u}_{n-j+i+1}(a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},\mu^{v}_{j-i}(a_{i},\dots,a_{j-1}),a_{j},\dots,a_{n})=0.

We have suppressed signs in the above formula, as we are presently
working in characteristic two. See Equation (
6.1
)
for the sign refined version.

Remark 3.2
.

When
W
is one-dimensional, this coincides with the definition of
weighted algebras
[
7
, Definition 4.1]
, with

\kappa=-2
. Weighted
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
algebras are equivalent to curved

{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
algebras over formal power series, with a constraint on
the curvature; compare
[
2
, Chapter 3]
.

Given a weighted
A_{\infty}
algebra, we can specialize to the
operations
\{\mu^{\vec{0}}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, i.e. those with
vanishing weight. This restriction is an (ordinary)
A_{\infty}

algebra,
(A,\mu^{\vec{0}})
. We say that
(A,\mu)
is a
weighted
deformation
of
(A,\mu^{\vec{0}})
. Moreover, we will typically
abbreviate
\vec{0}
by
0
.

Definition 3.3
.

A weighted
{\mathcal{A}}_{\infty}
algebra is called unital if
there is a distinguished
1\in A
with the property that

\mu^{0}_{2}(a,1)=\mu^{0}_{2}(1,a)=a

and
for any sequence of algebra elements
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
with some
a_{i}=1

and
(n,\vec{w})\neq(2,0)
, we have that

\mu^{\vec{w}}_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=0
.

(Note that this condition is often called “strictly unital” in the
literature, to leave room for other types of unitality.)

The algebra
\mathcal{C}(m,1)
is unital, where
1
is the sum of all the idempotents
corresponding to all the constant paths; i.e.

1={\mathbf{I}}_{1}+\dots+{\mathbf{I}}_{m-1}
.

Definition 4.1

A planar, rooted graphs is a graph

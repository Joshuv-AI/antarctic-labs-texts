Influential Slot and Tag Selection in Billboard Advertisement CCS: Information Systems Computational Advertising
Dildar Ali, Tejash Gupta, Suman Banerjee, Yamuna Prasad

Abstract
. The selection of influential billboard slots remains an important problem in billboard advertisements. Existing studies on this problem have not considered the case of context-specific influence probability. To bridge this gap, in this paper, we introduce the Context Dependent Influential Billboard Slot Selection Problem . First, we show that the problem is NP-hard. We also show that the influence function holds the bi-monotonicity, bi-submodularity, and non-negativity properties. We propose an orthant-wise Stochastic Greedy approach to solve this problem. We show that this method leads to a constant factor approximation guarantee. Subsequently, we propose an orthant-wise Incremental and Lazy Greedy approach. In a generic sense, this is a method for maximizing a bi-submodular function under the cardinality constraint, which may also be of independent interest. We analyze the performance guarantee of this algorithm as well as time and space complexity. The proposed solution approaches have been implemented with real-world billboard and trajectory datasets. We compare the performance of our method with many baseline methods, and the results are reported. Our proposed orthant-wise stochastic greedy approach leads to significant results when the parameters are set properly with reasonable computational overhead.



1.
Introduction

In recent times, due to the advancement of wireless internet and hand-held mobile devices, it has become easier to capture moving object’s locations. This leads to the availability of many datasets of the following kind: location information of a set of moving objects over different time stamps. This kind of database is called
Trajectory Database

(
Wang et al. 2021
)
and has been used to solve many real-life problems, including route recommendation, driving behavior prediction, influential location selection problems, etc. A trajectory database contains the location information of moving objects, and managing, mining, and efficiently retrieving the required information leads to a completely new discipline called
Spatial Databases
. Many Internet Giants, including Google, Alibaba, etc., are in the business of spatial databases and earn a significant portion of their total revenue.

‘Advertisement’ remains a central theme of any commercial house. The literature has reported that a commercial house spends
7-10\%
of its earned revenue on advertisement. There are several methods by which advertisement can be conducted, e.g., social media, sponsored search auctions, digital billboards, etc. Billboard advertising has emerged as an effective approach among all these techniques due to many advantages, such as ensuring a return on investment. In billboard advertisement, the billboards are owned by some other company (e.g., Lamar, Sigtel, etc.), and different e-commerce houses approach the company for a number of billboard slots depending on their budget. Here, the question is given the trajectory database, billboard database, and a positive integer
k
, in which
k
billboard slots should be chosen to maximize the influence. This problem has been referred to as the
Top-
k
Influential Billboard Slot Selection Problem
in the literature
(
Ali et al. 2023
)
, and a few solution methodologies are available. The influence probability between a billboard slot and a trajectory has been considered in all the studies
(
Ali et al. 2023
;
Ali et al. 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2020
)
. However, in practice, it varies from context to context. This means it is meaningful to show the advertisement of a low-cost product (rather than a costly product) to a low-income person. In this paper, we extend the study of influential billboard slot selection in the context-specific setting where, for every tag, there exists a tag-specific influence probability.

In real-world scenarios, billboard advertisement requires not only the selection of influential slots but also the selection of influential tags. Now, the question is that given two positive integers
k
and
\ell
, which
k
influential slots and
\ell
influential tags should be chosen such that the influence is maximized. To the best of our knowledge, such a problem has not been addressed in billboard advertisement settings. However, some studies focus on the influence maximization in the presence of tags in social networks. The first study in this direction was done by Ke et al.
(
Ke et al. 2018
)
, where they studied the problem of finding
k
seed nodes and
r
influential tags to maximize the influence in the network. Subsequently, other solution methodologies have been proposed in the literature, e.g., community-based approach
(
Banerjee and Pal 2022
)
that exploits the bi-submodularity of the influence function. This paper bridges this gap by studying the influential billboard slot selection problem in tag-specific influence probability settings. In particular, we make the following contributions in this paper:

•

We study the problem of selecting Influential Billboard Slots in the tag-specific influence probability setting where the goal is to select influential billboard slots and influential tags to maximize the influence. We formally call this problem the
Influential Slot and Tag Selection Problem
.

•

We establish several important properties of the influence function and exploit them to design efficient algorithms to solve this problem.

•

We propose an efficient Orthent-wise Stochastic Greedy Maximization Algorithm and subsequently introduce the Orthent-wise Incremental and Lazy Greedy Maximization Algorithm.

•

We analyze all the algorithms to understand time and space complexities and performance guarantees.

•

Finally, we conduct a number of experiments with real-world trajectory datasets to exhibit the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed solution approaches.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section

2.
Related Work

In this section, we discuss relevant studies from the literature. This study comes under the broad umbrella of database and data management. Therefore, we categorize our literature survey into four major categories: Influential Location Identification, Influential Billboard Slot Selection, Trajectory-driven Influence Maximization, and Context-dependent Influence Maximization.

2.1.
Influential Location Identification

The Site Selection problem has gained significant attention from researchers due to its wide range of applications. A previous study by Zhou et al.
(
Zhou et al. 2011
)
examined the MaxBRkNN problem, which focuses on finding the optimal location with the highest number of trajectories as its k-nearest neighbors based on distance measurements. However, their study assumed fixed user locations, while our case involves a fixed billboard location and the evaluation of its influence on moving trajectories. Next, the concept of location-aware influence maximization problems
(
Li et al. 2014
)
,
(
Liu et al. 2017
)
is motivated by traditional influence maximization problems, where the goal is to select a subset of nodes in a social network to maximize influence spread. While both types of problems aim to maximize influence, there is a distinction in how influence is propagated. In social networks, influence spreads from one user to another, whereas in our influence model, an audience is only influenced if they come across the range of a billboard. Wang et al.
(
Wang et al. 2016
)
studied the problem of selecting probabilistic influence-based optimal locations to place several facilities. They considered the moving objects and proposed two noble algorithms and one distance measure. Liu et al.
(
Liu et al. 2021
)
studied the problem of Competitive Location Selection over Moving Objects that considered competition against the existing facilities. They presented an influence score model to calculate the influence. Subsequently, they proposed an efficient pruning technique. Mitra et al.
(
Mitra et al. 2019
)
studied the problem of selecting Top-
k
locations to place facilities to maximize user convenience. They defined the notion of convenience as the minimum extra distance that needs to be traveled to avail of the facilities. They proposed two variants of the problem. The first one focuses on minimizing the maximum extra distance that needs to be traveled by any user, while the second one aims to reduce the total extra distance traveled by all the users. To address this, they proposed efficient heuristic solutions. Next, Qi et al.
(
Qi et al. 2020
)
studied the online facility placement problem considering three features, namely user convenience, temporal impacts, and user coverage. They proposed an
(1-\frac{1}{e})
-factor approximation algorithm and an online algorithm with the competitive ratio
\frac{1}{4}
. Recent research has also explored various spatial properties for developing efficient partition algorithms. Wang et al.
(
Wong et al. 2009
)
focused on finding an optimal region that maximizes the area of the k-nearest neighbors by intersecting geometric shapes. Similarly, Xia et al.
(
Xia et al. 2005
)
addressed the problem of identifying the most influential places and introduced a novel pruning technique to eliminate less influential sites.
These studies demonstrate the ongoing efforts to address different aspects of the Site Selection problem and its variations, considering spatial properties, influence maximization objectives, and optimization strategies.

2.2.
Influential Billboard Slot Selection

As mentioned previously, the key problem in billboard advertisement is selecting a number of influential billboard slots. Several studies on this problem have been found in the existing literature, and different solutions have been proposed to address them. Now, in the direction of the influential billboard selection problem, Zhang et al.
(
Zhang et al. 2020
)
studied the problem of optimizing the impact of billboards on trajectories. The challenge involves selecting a subset of billboards from a given set, considering their locations, costs, and budget constraints. This problem is closely related to our
Context Dependent Influential Billboard Slot Selection Problem
. However, our approach aims to maximize influence by choosing a subset of the most influential billboard slots and a subset of the most influential tags within the specified budget. This type of subset selection problem shares similarities with the maximum coverage problem studied by Kuller et al.
(
Khuller et al. 1999
)
where each element in the subset incurs a cost, and the objective is to maximize coverage within the budget constraints. However, it has been demonstrated that the standard incremental Greedy algorithm is inefficient for this problem due to the high computational cost. To address this limitation, a modified version of the greedy approach was introduced, which achieves an approximation ratio of
(1-\frac{1}{e})
. Another related study by Wang et al.
(
Wang et al. 2022
)
focuses on the Targeted Outdoor Advertising Recommendation problem, which involves selecting a subset of billboards within a given budget while considering a targeted influence model that incorporates user mobility. Their primary contribution lies in developing two solution strategies, employing a divide-and-conquer approach and considering user profiles and advertisement topics. In the direction of influential billboard slot selection, Ali et al.
(
Ali et al. 2022
)
studied the problem and formulated this as a discrete optimization problem. They introduced a pruned submodularity graph-based solution approach, which leads to an approximation ratio of
(1-\frac{1}{e}).(I(S^{OPT})-2k\epsilon)
. Furthermore, they have introduced spatial clustering-based
(
Ali et al. 2023
)
solutions to reduce the computational costs while maintaining the influence quality.

2.3.
Trajectory Driven Influence Maximization

Now, in the past few years, maximization of influence over trajectories is proven to be an efficient technique and to illustrate it, Guo et al.
(
Guo et al. 2017
)
studied a problem in which they aimed to find the best routes that are associated with specific ads to maximize their influence on target audiences. Another study by Zhang et al.
(
Zhang et al. 2019
)
focused on finding a set of highly influential billboards that attract more people’s attention within certain budget constraints. They introduced an algorithm based on tangent lines to estimate the upper limit of billboard influence and reduce computational costs using a termination method based on a user-defined parameter
\theta
. Their approach achieved an approximation ratio of
\frac{\theta}{2}(1-\frac{1}{e})
. More recently, Wang et al.
(
Wang et al. 2018
)
studied a new type of query called RkNNT, which estimates route capacity and planning by considering user information, such as their starting and ending points from trajectory data. When it comes to outdoor or online advertising, one of the challenges is selecting the right audience for a specific advertisement due to a lack of audience profiles. Wang et al.
(
Wang et al. 2020
)
addressed this issue by proposing a divide-and-conquer approach. Instead of only considering billboards and trajectories, they incorporated advertisement content, trajectory behavior, and mobility transition to improve influence delivery from targeted audiences in the outdoor advertising industry.

2.4.
Tag-Based Influence Maximization problem

No study on tag-specific influence maximization has been done in the context of billboard advertisement exists. However, some studies exist on tag-specific influence maximization problems in social networks. The closest to our study is the study by Ke et al.
(

They addressed the problem of selecting Top-
k
influential users and Top-

influential tags to maximize the influence. Their solution methodology relies on the maximization of submodular function and they proposed a reverse rechable set-based approach for this problem. However, the proposed solution approaches are deeply rooted at the bi-submodular function and its maximization. There are many other studies on tag-based influence maximization. However, they are all in the context of social network analysis and fundamentally different from these studies
(

Banerjee et al. 2020
)
.

3.
Background and Problem Definition

This section describes the background concepts and formally defines our problem.

3.1.
Trajectory and Billboard Database

A trajectory database contains location information of a set of moving objects over time. In this problem context, the trajectory database
\mathcal{D}
contains tuples of the form
(\mathcal{U}^{{}^{\prime}},\texttt{loc},[t_{1},t_{2}])
which signifies that the set of people
\mathcal{U}^{{}^{\prime}}
were at the location
loc
for the duration
[t_{1},t_{2}]
.
m
denotes the number of tuples of the database
\mathcal{D}
. For any tuple
t\in\mathcal{D}
, let
t_{u}
denote the set of people associated with it. Let
\mathcal{U}=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}\}
denotes the set of people covered by the trajectory database, hence
\mathcal{U}=\underset{t\in\mathcal{D}}{\bigcup}t_{u}
. This is defined as the people for which there exists at least one tuple which contains the people, i.e.,
\mathcal{U}=\{u_{i}:\exists(\mathcal{U}^{{}^{\prime}},\texttt{loc},[t_{1},t_{2}])\in\mathcal{D}\texttt{ and }u_{i}\in\mathcal{U}^{{}^{\prime}}\}
. Similar way,
\mathcal{L}
denotes the set of locations that are covered by the trajectory database
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}=\{\texttt{loc}_{i}:(\mathcal{U}^{{}^{\prime}},\texttt{loc}_{i},[t_{j},t_{k}])\in\mathcal{D}\}
. Let
[T_{1},T_{2}]
be the duration for which the trajectory database
\mathcal{D}
contains the movement data. This means for every tuple
t\in\mathcal{D}
, the associated interval
[t_{i},t_{j}]\subseteq[T_{1},T_{2}]
.

A billboard database
\mathcal{B}
contains information about different billboards placed across a city. This database contains the tuple of the form
(b_{id},\texttt{loc},\texttt{slot\_duration},\texttt{cost})
, where
b_{id}
denotes the billboard id,
loc
denotes the location,
slot_duration
attribute contains the duration of each slot for this billboard and its associated cost etc. The set of billboards covered in the billboard database is
B=\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\}
. In the billboard advertisement technique, an E-Commerce house selects a number of billboard slots (which is dependent on the budget), and advertisement content (e.g., video, animation, etc.) is played with the hope that the persons nearby may look into the content and influenced to it with a certain probability. In the billboard advertisement problem, the key computational problem addressed in the literature is that for a given value
k
, which
k
slots should be chosen to maximize the influence. In this problem context, every billboard database is associated with a trajectory database. It makes sense to consider the billboards such that at least one entry in the trajectory database exists in which the
loc
is the same as the location in which the billboard is placed.

3.2.
Influence Probability

As mentioned previously, once a person sees the advertisement content running on the billboard, he/she might be influenced by the brand with a certain probability. It has been assumed in the literature that this probability is fixed and does not depend on the context. However, in practice, advertisements are always context-dependent. To explain it further, consider the case of movie promotion. In this context, showing the advertisement content to someone unfamiliar with this kind of movie makes no sense. Hence, to make an advertisement effective, it must be targeted in nature, which means the observers have to have some inclination towards the advertisement content. In this paper, we formalize the notion of context through hashtags. Consider
\mathcal{H}_{u_{i}}
the set of tags associated with the person
u_{i}
. Considering all the users in
\mathcal{U}
, the set of tags that are relevant as a whole is denoted by
\mathcal{H}=\underset{u_{i}\in\mathcal{U}}{\bigcup}\mathcal{H}_{u_{i}}
. So for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
and for every
u\in\mathcal{U}
,
Pr(u|h)
denotes the influence probability, and this can be interpreted as the conditional probability. If the tag
h
is used as the advertisement content, then the probability that the person
u
is influenced will be
Pr(u|h)
.

3.3.
Influence Function

Recently, billboards have been digital and allocated slot-wise on a payment basis. Consider all the billboards under consideration are running for the duration
[T_{1},T_{2}]
, and for each billboard, the slot duration is
\Delta
. A billboard slot is a tuple consisting of the billboard id and the duration. We denote the set of all billboard slots as
\mathcal{BS}
and
\mathcal{BS}=\{(b_{i},[t_{j},t_{j}+\Delta]):i\in[m]\text{ and }t_{j}\in\{1,\Delta+1,2\Delta+1,\ldots,T_{2}-\Delta+1\}\}
. In billboard advertisement, billboards are owned by an influence provider (e.g., Lamar), and commercial houses approach the influence provider for a number of billboard slots to maximize the influence of their product. Naturally, the question arises: how can we quantify the influence of a given set of billboard slots? We state this in Definition
3.1
.

Definition 3.1 (Influence of Billboard Slots).

Given a subset of billboard slots
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{BS}
, its aggregated influence probability is denoted by
\phi(\mathcal{S})
and defined by Equation No.
1
.

(1)

\phi(\mathcal{S})=\underset{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\sum}1-\underset{t\in\mathcal{D}\wedge u\in t_{u}}{\prod}\underset{b\in\mathcal{BS}}{\prod}(1-Pr(u,b))

Here,
\phi
is the influence function that maps each subset of the billboard slots to its expected influence, hence
\phi:2^{\mathcal{BS}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
and
\phi(\emptyset)=0
. The influence model stated in Definition
3.1
has been widely accepted in the literature and most of the existing studies on billboard advertisement. However, as mentioned previously, if the advertisements need to be effective, the context is very important, and in that case, the influence model mentioned in Definition
3.1
can not be applied directly. In this direction, first define the context-dependent influence probability of a person. Consider a subset of tags
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is used for the advertisement purpose. For any person
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the influence probability for the given set of tags
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
is denoted by
Pr(u|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})
and defined in Definition
3.2
.

Definition 3.2 (Context Dependent Influence Probability).

Given a subset of tags
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
, for any person
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the context-dependent influence probability
Pr(u|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})
can be defined by Equation No.
2
.

(2)

Pr(u|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})=1-\underset{h\in\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}{\prod}(1-Pr(u|h))

Now, using the definition
3.2
, we define the context-dependent influence for a given subset of billboard slots in the Definition
3.3
.

Definition 3.3 (Context Dependent Influence of Billboard Slots).

Given a subset of billboard slots
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{BS}
and a subset of tags
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, the context dependent influence of
\mathcal{S}
is denoted by
\phi(\mathcal{S}|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})
and defined using Equation No.
3
.

(3)

\phi(\mathcal{S}|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})=\underset{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\sum}1-\underset{t\in\mathcal{D}\wedge u\in t_{u}}{\prod}\underset{b\in\mathcal{BS}}{\prod}(1-Pr(u,b|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}))

In Equation No.
3
, for any person
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the value of
Pr(u|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})
can be computed using Equation No.
3
. Here,
\phi()
is the combined influence function that maps each subset of slots and a subset of tags combination to its corresponding influence, i.e.,
\phi:2^{\mathcal{BS}}\times 2^{\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
. From this equation, it can be observed that the influence depends on the parameters of which slots are chosen and which tags are used in the advertisement.

3.4.
Problem Definition

As mentioned in Section
3.3
, selecting both tags and billboard slots effectively is important. Also, it is easy to follow that more slots and tags will lead to more influence. However, as mentioned previously, obtaining a required billboard slot from an influence provider is subject to payment. Also, creating advertisement content based on a tag is also budget-consuming. The E-Commerce house that is doing this advertisement will have budget constraints. So, the goal here is to select
k
many billboard slots and
\ell
many tags to maximize the influence defined in Definition
3.3
. We call this problem the
Context Dependent Influential Billboard Slot Selection Problem
, which asks for given
k
and
\ell
which
k
influential slots and tags should be chosen respectively to maximize the influence. We formally state the problem in Definition
3.4
.

Definition 3.4 (Context Dependent Influential Billboard Slot Selection Problem).

Given a trajectory database
\mathcal{D}
, a billboard database
\mathcal{B}
, and two positive integers
k
and
\ell
, this problem asks to choose
k
influential billboard slots and
\ell
influential tags such that the influence is maximized. Mathematically, this problem can be expressed as follows:

(4)

(\mathcal{S}^{OPT},\mathcal{H}^{OPT})\longleftarrow\underset{\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{BS}\wedge\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}}{argmax}\ \phi(\mathcal{S}|\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})

It is reasonable to consider that even if there is no tag, some default tag
h^{{}^{\prime}}
still exists, which can be used even if no tag is selected. We want to select
\ell
many tags on the top of the default tag. In Equation No.
4
,
\mathcal{S}^{OPT}
and
\mathcal{H}^{OPT}
denotes
k
-sized optimal slot subset and
\ell
-sized optimal tag subset. It can be easily observed that the problem introduced in Definition
3.4
is the generalization of the
Influential Billboard Slot Selection Problem

(
Ali et al. 2023
)
where the context-dependent influence probability has not been considered. Hence, the hardness result of the second problem continues to hold for the first problem as well. So Theorem
3.5
holds.

Theorem 3.5.

For a given
k
and
\ell
, finding the optimal slot and tag set for the Context-Dependent Influential Billboard Slot Selection Problem is
NP-hard
.

Suppose the Problem
3.4
is solved, and we obtain a
k
size slot subset and
\ell
size tag subset. Let them be
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
. This essentially means that if the slots in
\mathcal{S}
are chosen, then it is meaningful to select the tags in
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
or vice versa. Still, the problem is not solved on the ground because we must also report which tag will be displayed in which slot. This question leads us to the second problem, which is as follows: “Given a set of slots
\mathcal{S}
and tags
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
which tag is to be displayed in which slot such that the influence is maximized." We formally call this problem the
Tag Allocation Problem
stated in Definition
3.6
.

Definition 3.6 (Tag Allocation Problem).

Given a trajectory database
\mathcal{D}
, a billboard database
\mathcal{B}
, a slot and tag subset
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
, respectively the tag allocation problem asks to assign each slot to a tag such that the influence is maximized.

This problem returns a boolean matrix
\mathcal{X}
is of
k\times\ell
whose
(i,j)
-th entry will be
1
if the
j
-th tag is allocated to the
i
-th slot and
0
otherwise. As the influence is dependent on both slots and tags, in the rest of the paper, we write the influence function as
\phi(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})
though its interpretation remains the same.

3.6.
Set Function and Its Properties

A set function is defined as a mapping from all possible subsets of a
ground set
(say
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
) to a positive real number including
0
, i.e.,
f:2^{\mathcal{X}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{0}_{+}
. A set function is said to be normalized if
f(\emptyset)=0
. There are several properties of the set function that can be exploited to design efficient algorithms for optimization problems. The properties that are of interest are
non-negativity
,
monotonicity
, and
submodularity
. A set function is said to be non-negative if for all
\mathcal{X}\subseteq X
,
f(\mathcal{X})\geq 0
. The function
f()
is said to be monotone if for all
\mathcal{X}\subseteq X
and
x\in X\setminus\mathcal{X}
,
f(\mathcal{X}\cup\{x\})\geq(\mathcal{X})
. The function
f()
is submodular if for all
\mathcal{X}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{2}\subseteq X
and for all
x\in X\setminus\mathcal{X}_{2}
,
f(\mathcal{X}_{1}\cup\{x\})-f(\mathcal{X}_{1})\geq f(\mathcal{X}_{2}\cup\{x\})-f(\mathcal{X}_{2})
. In recent times, the study of the biset function has also emerged, and it can be observed that the influence function
\phi()
as defined in Equation No.
3
is also a biset function where the ground sets are the set of billboard slots
\mathcal{BS}
and the set of tags
\mathcal{H}
. Similarly, for biset functions as well, a few properties are of interest in this paper, and they are defined subsequently.

Definition 3.7 (Non-Negativity of a Biset function).

Given a biset function
\phi(.,.)
defined on the
\mathcal{BS}
and
\mathcal{H}
is said to be non-negative if for all
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{BS}
and
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
,
\phi(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})\geq 0
.

Definition 3.8 (Bi-Monotonicity of a Biset function).

Given a biset function
\phi(.,.)
defined on the
\mathcal{BS}
and
\mathcal{H}
is said to have the bi-monotonicity property if for all
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{BS}
and
\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
the following two holds:

•

For all
s\in\mathcal{BS}\setminus\mathcal{S}
,
\phi(\mathcal{S}\cup\{s\},\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})\geq\phi(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})
.

•

For all
h\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}
,
\phi(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}}\cup\{h\})\geq\phi(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{H}^{{}^{\prime}})
.

Definition 3.9 (Bi-Submodularity of a Biset function).

Given a biset function
\phi(.,.)
defined on the
\mathcal{BS}
and
\mathcal{H}
is said to have the bi-submodularity property if the following two conditions hold.

4.2.3.
Lazy Greedy Algorithm

As mentioned previously, due to the excessive number of influence function evaluations, the execution time is very high. However, this algorithm can be implemented efficiently where, in all practical situations, the number of influence function evaluations will be much less, though in the worst case, the number of evaluations can be as much as incremental greedy. The key idea is to Consider the first for loop and its first iteration. We compute the marginal gain for all the slots with respect to the empty set, which is equivalent to computing their influence value. Subsequently, we sort the slots based on this value in descending order, and the first slot is chosen. Now, in the second iteration, we compute the marginal gain of the slots in the sorted order and consider the following situation. Suppose the marginal gain of the
i

i

(i+1)

5.3.
Baseline Methodologies

We have compared the performance of the proposed solution approaches with the following baseline methods:

Random Slot and Random Tag (RSRT):

In this method,
k
many random slots and
\ell
many random tags are chosen and returned as solution.

Random Slot and High-Frequency Tag(RSHFT):

Frequency of a tag is defined as the number of people with whom this tag is associated. In this method, we count the frequency of every tag and sort all the tags based on the frequency value. This method returns
\ell
many tags and
k
many random slots from this sorted list.

Maximum Coverage Slot and Random Tag (MAXSRT):

For any billboard slot, its coverage is defined as the number of people passing by that slot. In this method, the coverage of every billboard slot is computed, and they are sorted based on this value. From this sorted list,
k
many billboard slots are returned, and
\ell
many tags are chosen uniformly at random.

Top-
k
Slot and Top-
\ell
Tag (TSTT):

In this method, individually calculate influence for all billboard slots and individual influence for all tags. After that, separately sort them in descending order based on their influence values. From this sorted list, we pick Top-
k
billboard slots and Top-
\ell
tags.

Top-
k
Slot and Random
\ell
Tag (TSRT):

In this method, individual influence for both billboard slots and tags is calculated. Then, billboard slots based on influence value are sorted in descending order, and from the sorted list, Top-
k
slots will be picked. In the case of tags, randomly
\ell
many tags are picked from an unsorted tag list.

Random
k
Slot and Top-
\ell
Tag (RSTT):

This method is just reverse of TSRT approach. At first, individual influence for both billboard slots and tags is computed, and according to the influence value, all tags are sorted in descending order. Now, from the sorted tags list, Top-
\ell
tags are picked, and from the billboard slot set, randomly
k
many slots are chosen.

6.
Conclusion

This paper has studied the problem of jointly selecting influential billboard slots and influential tags. First, we show that the joint influence function is non-negative, monotone, and bi-submodular. We show that the problem is
NP-hard
and subsequently propose an orthant-wise incremental greedy algorithm that gives a constant factor approximation algorithm. Though this method is simple to understand and easy to implement, it still does not scale well when the trajectory dataset is large due to excessive marginal gain computations. To address this approach, we propose the orthant-wise Lazy and Stochastic Greedy approach, which executes fast while leading to more or less similar influence. Still, the problem is not solved on the ground because we must also report which tag will be displayed in which slot to maximize the influence. Developing more efficient techniques to address slot selection and allocation problems will remain an active area of research in the near future.

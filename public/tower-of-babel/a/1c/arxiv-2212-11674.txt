Multi muon/anti-muon signals via productions of gauge and scalar bosons in a U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} model at muonic colliders Preprint: UME-PP-024 Preprint: KYUSHU-HET-252
Arindam Das, Takaaki Nomura, Takashi Shimomura

Abstract
We discuss the discovery potential of a promising signals, 3 × μ + ​ μ − 3\times\mu^{+}\mu^{-} at a μ + ​ μ − \mu^{+}\mu^{-} collider and μ + ​ μ + ​ μ + ​ μ + ​ μ − ​ μ − \mu^{+}\mu^{+}\mu^{+}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\mu^{-} at a μ + ​ μ + \mu^{+}\mu^{+} collider, that are obtained via production of Z ′ Z^{\prime} and a new scalar boson ϕ \phi in a spontaneously broken local U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} model. We consider the Z ′ Z^{\prime} associated production from the process μ + ​ μ − → ϕ ​ Z ′ \mu^{+}\mu^{-}\to\phi Z^{\prime} in addition to a muonphilic Z ′ Z^{\prime} fusion process μ + ​ μ ± → ϕ ​ μ + ​ μ ± \mu^{+}\mu^{\pm}\to\phi\mu^{+}\mu^{\pm} . The scalar boson is associated with U ​ ( 1 ) L μ − L τ U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}} symmetry breaking and dominantly decays into Z ′ ​ Z ′ Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime} mode. We carry out numerical simulation analysis for signal and background processes to estimate a discovery significance for different benchmark points. It is shown that our signal can be observed with integrated luminosity less than 𝒪 ⁡ ( 100 ) \mathcal{O}(100) fb -1 for both μ + ​ μ − \mu^{+}\mu^{-} and μ + ​ μ + \mu^{+}\mu^{+} colliders with more than 5- σ \sigma significance.

II.1
Structure of the model

The Lagrangian of our model is written by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}-\frac{1}{4}Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}Z^{\prime\mu\nu}+g^{\prime}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}B_{\mu\nu}Z^{\prime\mu\nu}+|D_{\mu}\varphi|^{2}-V,

(1)

where
\mathcal{L_{\mathrm{SM}}}
denotes the SM Lagrangian except for Higgs potential, and the
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
current
J^{\mu}_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
and scalar potential are given as follows,

\displaystyle J^{\mu}_{Z^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}L_{\mu}+\bar{\mu}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}\mu_{R}-\bar{L}_{\tau}\gamma^{\mu}L_{\tau}-\bar{\tau}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}\tau_{R},

(2)

\displaystyle V=

\displaystyle-\mu_{H}^{2}H^{\dagger}H-\mu_{\varphi}^{2}\varphi^{*}\varphi+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{2}(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{\varphi}}{2}(\varphi^{*}\varphi)^{2}+\lambda_{H\varphi}(H^{\dagger}H)(\varphi^{*}\varphi).

(3)

The gauge fields and corresponding field strengths of
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
and
U(1)_{Y}
are respectively denoted
by
Z^{\prime}
and
B
. In Eq.(
1
), we write the gauge coupling constant and the kinetic mixing parameter, respectively, by
g^{\prime}
and
\epsilon
.
In this paper, however, we ignore kinetic mixing parameter due to the fact that it is sufficiently small and does not affect phenomenology of our interest
Nomura:2020vnk
.
In our scenario, the
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
gauge symmetry is spontaneously broken by VEV of
\varphi
.

The scalar fields
H
and
\varphi
are written by

H=\begin{pmatrix}G^{+}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v+\tilde{h}+iG^{0})\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v_{\varphi}+\tilde{\phi}+iG_{Z^{\prime}}),

(4)

where
G^{+}
,
G^{0}
and
G_{Z^{\prime}}
are massless NG boson to be absorbed by gauge bosons
W^{+}
,
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
while

\tilde{h}
and
\tilde{\phi}
represent the physical CP-even scalar bosons.
We obtain the VEVs of the scalar fields,
v
and
v_{\varphi}
, from the stationary conditions

\partial V/\partial v=\partial V/\partial v_{\varphi}=0
such that

v=\sqrt{\frac{2(\lambda_{\varphi}\mu_{H}^{2}-\lambda_{H\varphi}\mu_{\varphi}^{2})}{\lambda_{H}\lambda_{\varphi}-\lambda_{H\varphi}^{2}}},\quad v_{\varphi}=\sqrt{\frac{2(\lambda_{H}\mu_{\varphi}^{2}-\lambda_{H\varphi}\mu_{H}^{2})}{\lambda_{H}\lambda_{\varphi}-\lambda_{H\varphi}^{2}}}.

(5)

Inserting Eq.(
4
) into Eq.(
3
), we find the squared mass terms for CP-even scalar bosons as

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\tilde{\phi}\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{H}v^{2}&\lambda_{H\varphi}vv_{\varphi}\\
\lambda_{H\varphi}vv_{\varphi}&\lambda_{\varphi}v_{\varphi}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\tilde{\phi}\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

The squared mass matrix can be diagonalized by an orthogonal matrix and the mass eigenvalues are given by

m_{h,\phi}^{2}=\frac{\lambda_{H}v^{2}+\lambda_{\varphi}v_{\varphi}^{2}}{4}\pm\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\left(\lambda_{H}v^{2}-\lambda_{\varphi}v_{\varphi}^{2}\right)^{2}+4\lambda_{H\varphi}^{2}v^{2}v_{\varphi}^{2}}.

(7)

We then find the corresponding mass eigenstates
h
and
\phi
as follows;

\begin{pmatrix}h\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha&\sin\alpha\\
-\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\tilde{\phi}\end{pmatrix},\quad\tan 2\alpha=\frac{2\lambda_{H\varphi}vv_{\varphi}}{\lambda_{H}v^{2}-\lambda_{\varphi}v_{\varphi}^{2}},

(8)

where
\alpha
is the mixing angle. In our analysis we assume
\sin\alpha\ll 1
, and
h
is identified as the SM-like Higgs boson.

After
\varphi
developing a VEV,
Z^{\prime}
boson gets its mass.
Since we ignore kinetic mixing parameter, the mass is simply given by

m_{Z^{\prime}}=g^{\prime}v_{\varphi}.

(9)

We can also write interaction between
\phi
and
Z^{\prime}
as

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime\mu}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\left(2\frac{\phi}{v_{\varphi}}+\frac{\phi^{2}}{v_{\varphi}^{2}}\right),

(10)

where we adopted
\alpha\ll 1
.

II.2
Parameter region favored by explanation of
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
anomalies

The
b\to s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
anomalies can be explained when
Z^{\prime}
exchange induces effective interaction

\mathcal{H}_{\rm eff}=-\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{tb}V^{*}_{ts}\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}C_{9}^{\mu}(\bar{b}\gamma^{\nu}P_{L}s)(\bar{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}\mu),

(11)

where
C_{9}^{\mu}
is corresponding Wilson coefficient,
\alpha
is the fine structure constant,
G_{F}
is Fermi constant, and
V_{tb}
and
V_{ts}
are elements of CKM matrix.
To induce the effective interaction, we need
Z^{\prime\mu}\bar{b}\gamma_{\mu}P_{L}s
coupling in addition to
Z^{\prime\nu}\bar{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}\mu
one.
We can induce the former one by introducing new fields such as vector-like quarks(VLQs) and new scalar field through
quark-VLQ mixing
Altmannshofer:2014cfa
or radiative corrections
Ko:2017yrd
;
Chen:2017usq
.
Here we assume the effective coupling

\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}=g^{L}_{bs}Z^{\prime\mu}\overline{b_{L}}\gamma_{\mu}s_{L},

(12)

where
g^{L}_{bs}
is considered to be free parameter.
We then obtain the Wilson coefficient from
Z^{\prime}
exchange, denoted by
C_{9}^{Z^{\prime}}
, such that

C_{9}^{Z^{\prime}}\simeq-\frac{\pi g^{\prime}g^{L}_{bs}}{\sqrt{2}V_{tb}V^{*}_{ts}\alpha G_{F}m_{Z^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}}.

(13)

The global fit in ref.
Alguero:2021anc
indicates
2\sigma
range of new physics contribution to
C_{9}^{\mu}
as

-1.29\leq\Delta C_{9}^{\mu}\leq-0.72,

(14)

where the central value is
-1.01
.

The effective interactions in Eq. (
12
) also induce the mixing between
B_{s}
and
\overline{B_{s}}
mesons,
and we should take a constraint from the mixing into account.
The ratio of
B_{s}
–
\overline{B_{s}}
mixing between SM and
Z^{\prime}
contributions can be found as
Altmannshofer:2016jzy
;
Tuckler:2022fkz

\frac{M^{Z^{\prime}}_{12}}{M^{\rm SM}_{12}}\simeq\frac{(g^{L}_{bs})^{2}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\frac{16\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}}{g^{2}G_{F}(V_{tb}V^{*}_{ts})^{2}S_{0}},

(15)

where
S_{0}\simeq 2.3
is the SM loop function
Inami:1980fz
;
Buchalla:1995vs
.
Then
g^{L}_{bs}
is rewritten in terms of
C_{9}^{Z^{\prime}}
in use of Eq. (
13
) such that

g^{L}_{bs}=-\sqrt{2}V_{tb}V^{*}_{ts}\frac{\alpha}{\pi}G_{F}C_{9}^{Z^{\prime}}\frac{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{g^{\prime}}.

(16)

Requiring
|M^{Z^{\prime}}_{12}|/|M^{{\rm SM}}_{12}|<0.12

Charles:2020dfl
, we obtain the bound on
m_{Z^{\prime}}
as

m_{Z^{\prime}}<2.1\ {\rm TeV}\times\frac{g^{\prime}}{|C_{9}^{Z^{\prime}}|}.

(17)

IV
Summary

We have discussed signals of multi muon/anti-muon at
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
\mu^{+}\mu^{+}
colliders
that are induced from productions of new scalar boson
\phi
with/without
Z^{\prime}
boson in a local
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
model.
The new scalar boson is associated with spontaneous breaking of the
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
gauge symmetry
so that we have
\phi Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
coupling.
The scalar boson dominantly decays into
Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
mode and our
Z^{\prime}
boson decays into second and third generation leptons.

We focused on region of new gauge coupling and gauge boson mass that is favored to explain
b\to s\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
anomaly.
The cross sections of our signal process have been estimated applying benchmark parameter from the region and we have obtained
\mathcal{O}(1)
fb to
\mathcal{O}(10)
fb values.
We have also carried out numerical simulation and found discovery significance where we can discover our signal with integrated luminosity less than 100 fb
-1

at our benchmark points.
Therefore muon colliders are quite suitable to test a spontaneously broken local
U(1)_{L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}}
model.

Rigid vectors in p p -adic principal series representations
Aranya Lahiri and Claus Sorensen

Abstract
. In this paper we view pro- p p Iwahori subgroups I I as rigid analytic groups 𝕀 \mathbb{I} for large enough p p . This is done by endowing I I with a natural p p -valuation, and thereby generalizing results of Lazard for GL n \text{GL}_{n} . We work with a general connected reductive split group over some p p -adic field (with simply connected derived group) and study the 𝕀 \mathbb{I} -analytic vectors in principal series representations. Our main result is an irreducibility criterion which generalizes results of Clozel and Ray in the GL n \text{GL}_{n} -case.



1.
Introduction

In this article we study rigid analytic vectors in continuous
p
-adic prinicpal series representations of a
p
-adic reductive group
G
. Here
G={\bf{G}}(L)
is the locally profinite group of
L
-rational points of a connected reductive group
{\bf{G}}
defined over a finite extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We assume
{\bf{G}}
is split over
L
, and that the derived group

{\bf{G}}^{\text{der}}
is simply connected. Once and for all we choose a Borel subgroup
{\bf{P}}
over
L
, and a maximal
L
-split torus
{\bf{T}}\subset{\bf{P}}
. Let

P={\bf{P}}(L)
and
T={\bf{T}}(L)
– similarly for other linear algebraic groups
{\bf{H}}
over
L
we will write
H
for the group of
L
-points.

The continuous principal series representations of
G
are defined as follows, see
[
Sch06
, Ch. 2]
. Start with a continuous character
\chi:T\rightarrow K^{\times}
taking values in some finite extension
K\supset L
. Inflate
\chi
to a character of
P
by letting
\chi=1
on the unipotent radical as usual, and look at the induction in the space

C(G,K)
of continuous
K
-valued functions on

\text{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\chi)=\{f\in C(G,K):f(bg)=\chi(b)f(g)\hskip 6.0pt\forall b\in P\}.

(Our normalization is different from
[
Sch06
]
where
G
acts by left translations. In our setup

acts by right translations, which means
(g^{\prime}f)(g)=f(gg^{\prime})
for
g,g^{\prime}\in G
.) One turns
\text{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\chi)
into an admissible

K
-Banach representation by endowing it with the norm
\|f\|=\sup_{g\in C}|f(g)|
for some choice of maximal compact subgroup

for which the Iwasawa decomposition

G=PC

In his paper
[

]
Schneider expresses optimism about

always having a finite composition series, and he states a precise (topological) irreducibility conjecture

[
Sch06
, Conj. 2.5]
– at least for semisimple and simply connected groups
{\bf{G}}
, in which case the condition on the character is
\chi^{-1}\delta\circ\alpha^{\vee}\notin(\cdot)^{\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
for all
{\bf{P}}
-positive roots
\alpha
; that is those
\alpha
appearing in
\text{Lie}({\bf{P}})
(and
\delta=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha
as usual).

The locally analytic analogue of Schneider’s irreducibility conjecture was proved by Orlik and Strauch in
[
OS10
]
, as a culmination of work by Feaux de Lacroix, Frommer, Kohlhaase, Schneider, Teitelbaum, and others. The actual conjecture is known to be true for
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
but it remains open in general. Ban and Hundley proved the conjecture for

\chi
in a certain cone, see
[
BH16
, Thm. 1.1]
.

In this article we restrict

to a pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup

(for

as defined in

, Df. 3.3.1]
. Here

is a rigid analytic

-group with

, and as a rigid analytic space

is a closed ball.

As a trivial first step the Bruhat-Tits decomposition gives an isomorphism of

-representations

\text{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\chi)_{\mathbb{I}-\text{an}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{w\in W}\text{Ind}_{I\cap wPw^{-1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\mathbb{I}-\text{an}}

where

denotes the Weyl group and
(w\chi)(t)=\chi(w^{-1}tw)
for

. The main problem is to understand each summand

, which by
[

, Prop. 3.3.7]
agrees with the rigid analytic induction of

(the space of rigid functions

satisfying the usual transformation property; see (
6.1
) in section
6
plus section

in the main text for more details). Of course, for all these spaces to be non-trivial we must assume the character

is rigid analytic when restricted to

. This means the function
\chi:T^{1}\rightarrow K^{\times}\hookrightarrow K
lies in the space
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(T^{1},K)

introduced in definition
4.5
.

The first half of our paper is concerned with promoting
I
to a rigid analytic group
\mathbb{I}
. Our convention will be that our choice of
I
corresponds to the

{\bf{P}}
-negative roots. (This part is potentially confusing; to aid the reader we point out that

is being denoted by

in the main body of the text.) See (

).
We show that

admits a natural

-valuation

provided

where

is the Coxeter number of
{\bf{G}}
and

is the ramification index;
see Proposition
3.4
. Moreover

(I,\omega)
is saturated, which is what allows us to associate a rigid analytic group
\mathbb{I}
with
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-points
I
. This is presumably well-known but not easily accessible in the literature. We give the details in Section
4
. We should emphasize there is some overlap between our work and
[
CR18
]
,
[
Ray20a
]
. In
[
CR18
]
Cornut and Ray give a minimal set of generators for
I
in any split reductive group
{\bf{G}}
, but they do not directly use
p
-valuations. In
[
Ray20a
]
Ray gives an ordered basis for
(I,\omega)
in the case of
\text{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. He is working with

as defined in
[

, Ex. 23.3]
which leads to massive matrix calculations. Part of our original motivation for writing this paper was to give a more conceptual approach to

and its ordered bases for any Chevalley group.

Having defined the rigid analytic pro-
p
Iwahori group
\mathbb{I}
for
p-1>e(L/\mathbb{Q}_{p})h_{G}
it now makes sense to ask for the
\mathbb{I}
-analytic vectors of any

-Banach representation of

. Here is our main result for principal series representations, which gives an irreducibility criterion analogous to

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a
p
-adic reductive group over
L
as above. Assume
p-1>e(L/\mathbb{Q}_{p})h_{G}
. Let
\chi:T\rightarrow K^{\times}
be a continuous character which is rigid analytic when restricted to
T^{1}=I\cap T
, and consider the Verma module
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
associated with its derivative
d\chi
(see Section
7
for more details). Then

\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
is simple if and only if the
I
-representation

\text{Ind}_{I\cap wPw^{-1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\mathbb{I}-\text{an}}

is topologically irreducible for some
w\in W
– or equivalently all
w\in W
. If so, these representations are mutually non-isomorphic as
w
varies.

There is a standard criterion for the simplicity of
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
due to Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand, see
[
Dix96
, Thm. 7.6.24]
. It is the infinitesimal version of the criterion in the irreducibility conjecture discussed above. In Section
10
we work out the irreducibility criterion explicitly in the case of
\text{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for
p>5
.

The
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-case of Theorem
1.1
is
[
Clo18
, Thm. 3.7]
, which continued the line of research begun by Robert in
[
Rob84
]
and
[
Rob85
]
. As part of his thesis Ray extended Clozel’s argument to
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
w=1
. This is the main result of his paper
[
Ray20b
, Thm. 3.9]
. In the last paragraph of that paper Ray expresses hope that the proof can be adapted to the case
w\neq 1
.
This is essentially what we do. By reworking the argument of Clozel and Ray, and setting it up in a more streamlined fashion, we are able to avoid the heavy matrix calculations in
[
Ray20b
]
and deal with the case
w\neq 1
with very little extra effort. Here are some of the main differences between
[
Ray20b
]
and this article:

•

The coordinates in
[
Ray20b
]
are given by the matrix entries (ordered lexicographically). This seems a bit artificial to us, and it results in cumbersome matrix calculations. Instead, for an arbitrary split group, our coordinates arise from a natural ordered basis in the sense of Lazard. This lets us argue more conceptually.

•

[
Ray20b
, Lem. 3.4]
gives an explicit Iwahori factorization. We do not need to be explicit. In Remark
4.4
we explain how the Iwahori factorization (as
p
-adic manifolds say) can be boosted to an isomorphism of rigid analytic spaces – without explicitly inverting the multiplication map.

•

The proof in
[
Ray20b
, p. 479]
juggles with
n
operators
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
corresponding to the
n
cocharacters
x\mapsto\text{diag}(1,\ldots,x,\ldots,1)
. We work with a single operator
H_{\mu}
corresponding to cocharacter
\mu
adapted to the choice of Weyl element
w
in the sense that
\langle w\alpha,\mu\rangle>0
for all positive roots
\alpha
. In the
\text{GL}_{n}
-case, for
w=1
our cocharacter
\mu
can be taken to be
x\mapsto\text{diag}(1,x,x^{2},\ldots,x^{n-1})
for instance.

Our interest in Theorem
1.1
stems primarily from the globally analytic variant of base change introduced by Clozel in
[
Clo17
]
and
[
Clo18
]
. In smooth representation theory, base change is reflected by a homomorphism between Hecke algebras. Clozel instead defines a homomorphism between globally analytic distribution algebras such as

\mathcal{D}^{\text{an}}(\mathbb{I},K)
in the notation of
[
Eme17
, Cor. 5.1.8]
, and he seems to suggest that this map should be compatible with classical Langlands base change – at least for unramified extensions.

As already noted, our Theorem
1.1
bears a close resemblance to
[
OS10
, Thm. 3.4.12]
in the locally analytic setup. The latter asserts that the strong dual of the locally analytic induction
\big(\text{Ind}_{I\cap wPw^{-1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\text{la}}\big)_{b}^{\prime}
is a simple
\mathcal{D}^{\text{la}}(I,K)
-module if
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
is simple. This more or less immediately gives the irreducibility of the induction by employing the duality theory of Schneider and Teitelbaum established in
[
ST02
]
,
[
ST03
]
. There does not seem to be a good duality theory in the rigid analytic setup. For instance, as pointed out in
[
Clo18
, App. A.2]
the distribution algebras
\mathcal{D}^{\text{an}}(\mathbb{I},K)
are non-Noetherian in general, which renders the Fréchet-Stein techniques of
[
ST03
]
useless in the rigid analytic setup. We tried unsuccessfully to mimic the approach of
[
OS10
]
, but Theorem
1.1
really does seem to require a direct proof along the lines of Clozel and Ray rather than passing to duals.

Notation

Throughout the paper the following notation will remain in force. Let
p>2
be a prime. We work over a fixed finite extension
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
and maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}=(\varpi)\subset\mathcal{O}
. The absolute ramification index

will just be denoted by

. We let
\ell=[L:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
.

We fix a connected reductive group
{\bf{G}}
which is split over
L
, and choose an
L
-split maximal torus
{\bf{T}}\subset{\bf{G}}
. Let

(X^{*}({\bf{T}}),\Phi,X_{*}({\bf{T}}),\Phi^{\vee})
be the associated root datum. Once and for all we pick a system of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
with simple roots

\Delta\subset\Phi^{+}
and let
\Phi^{-}=-\Phi^{+}
. We let
\text{ht}(\cdot)
be the height function on
\Phi
, and we recall the
Coxeter number of
{\bf{G}}
is
h=1+\text{ht}(\theta)
where
\theta
is the highest root. To each
\alpha\in\Phi
there is attached a unipotent root subgroup

{\bf{U}}_{\alpha}
normalized by
{\bf{T}}
.

We are mostly interested in representations of the
p
-adic group
G={\bf{G}}(L)
with the usual locally profinite topology. Similarly
T={\bf{T}}(L)
and we have the maximal compact subgroup
T^{0}
and its Sylow pro-
p
-subgroup
T^{1}
. We let
U_{\alpha}={\bf{U}}_{\alpha}(L)
and note that there is an isomorphism

u_{\alpha}:L\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}U_{\alpha}
such that
tu_{\alpha}(x)t^{-1}=u_{\alpha}(\alpha(t)x)
for all
t\in T
and
x\in L
. This gives a filtration of

by the subgroups
U_{\alpha,r}=u_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{m}^{r})
for varying

.

We consider the full Iwahori subgroup
J
corresponding to
\Phi^{+}
. Multiplication defines a homeomorphism

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\alpha,1}\times T^{0}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}U_{\alpha,0}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}J.

Our main interest however is the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup

which is the Sylow pro-
p
subgroup of

. Again multiplication gives a homeomorphism

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\alpha,1}\times T^{1}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}U_{\alpha,0}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}I.

Here the products over
\Phi^{-}
and
\Phi^{+}
are ordered in an arbitrarily chosen way. For the latter see the proof of
[
OS19
, Lem. 2.3]
and Lemma 2.1, part i, in
[
OS19
]
.

3.
A
p
-valuation on pro-
p
Iwahori

In this section we will define a
p
-valuation
\omega
on
I
(in the sense of Lazard) provided
p-1>eh
and exhibit an ordered basis for
(I,\omega)
. The strategy is to emulate
[
Sch11
, Ex. 23.3]

and conjugate
I
into a small enough principal congruence subgroup of
{\bf{G}}(E)
for a large enough finite extension
E/L
. This approach is based on the following well-known observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension and suppose
\mathcal{G}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-subgroup scheme of
\text{GL}_{N}
for some
N
. For
r=1,2,3,\ldots
consider the congruence subgroup

K_{r}
defined as

K_{r}=\ker\big(\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{E})\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{E}/\mathfrak{m}_{E}^{r})\big).

Then for
r>e_{E}/(p-1)
the expression

\omega(g)=\frac{1}{e_{E}}\cdot\sup\{n\in\mathbb{N}:g\in K_{n}\}

defines a
p
-valuation on
K_{r}
. (Here
e_{E}=e(E/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\mathcal{O}_{E}
denotes the valuation ring of

; by convention
\omega(1)=\infty
.)

Proof.

The
\text{GL}_{N}
-case is
[
Sch11
, Ex. 23.2]
. There
\omega
is defined by
\omega(g)=\min_{i,j}v(a_{ij}-\delta_{ij})
for a matrix
g=(a_{ij})
where
v
is the additive valuation on

E
with
v(p)=1
, but this is easily checked to agree with our definition. For a subgroup scheme
\mathcal{G}\hookrightarrow\text{GL}_{N}
just note that
K_{r}=\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{E})\cap\tilde{K}_{r}
where
\tilde{K}_{r}
denotes the
r
th congruence subgroup of
\text{GL}_{N}
.
∎

We will apply this to the following
\mathcal{G}
. As in
[
OS19
, Sect. 2.1.6]
we fix a hyperspecial vertex
x_{0}
in the apartment of the semisimple Bruhat-Tits building of
G
corresponding to
{\bf{T}}
. We consider the associated smooth affine group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{x_{0}}
over
\mathcal{O}
and its identity component
\mathcal{G}_{x_{0}}^{\circ}
.
As discussed in
[
OS19
, Sect. 7.2.2]
the
r^{\text{th}}
congruence subgroup of
\mathcal{G}_{x_{0}}^{\circ}
is generated by all the
U_{\alpha,r}
for varying
\alpha\in\Phi
, and a subgroup
T^{r}\subset T
defined in terms of the Neron model of
{\bf{T}}
. See
[
OS19
, Prop. 7.9]
.

Instead of
\mathcal{G}_{x_{0}}^{\circ}
we work with the base change
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{x_{0}}^{\circ}\times_{\text{Spec}(\mathcal{O})}\text{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{E})
, where
E/L
is a yet unspecified finite extension. As above we let
K_{r}
denote the
r^{\text{th}}
congruence subgroup of
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{O}_{E})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
p-1>eh
. Then there exists a pair
(E,t)
consisting of a finite extension
E/L
and an element
t\in{\bf{T}}(E)
such that
tIt^{-1}\subset K_{r}
where

r
is the smallest integer
>e_{E}/(p-1)
.

Proof.

Let
E/L
be arbitrary for now. We apply
[
OS19
, Prop. 7.9]
to the base change
{\bf{G}}\times_{\text{Spec}(L)}\text{Spec}(E)
and the facet
\{x_{0}\}
.
This tells us the multiplication map gives a homeomorphism

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\alpha,r}^{E}\times T_{E}^{r}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}U_{\alpha,r}^{E}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}K_{r}.

Here the superscripts/subscript
E
indicate we are are now working over
E
. For instance
U_{\alpha,r}^{E}=u_{\alpha}^{E}(\mathfrak{m}_{E}^{r})
where

u_{\alpha}^{E}:E\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}{\bf{U}}_{\alpha}(E)
extends
u_{\alpha}
. By conjugating the analogous factorization (
2.1
) of
I
by
t\in{\bf{T}}(E)
we see that the inclusion

tIt^{-1}\subset K_{r}
holds if and only if

1.

For
\alpha\in\Phi^{-}
,

tU_{\alpha,1}t^{-1}\subset U_{\alpha,r}^{E}\Longleftrightarrow\alpha(t)\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{E}^{r}\Longleftrightarrow-v(\alpha(t))<\frac{1}{e}-\frac{1}{p-1}.

2.

For the middle factor,

tT^{1}t^{-1}=T^{1}\subset T_{E}^{r}\Longleftrightarrow\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{E}^{r}\Longleftrightarrow p-1>e.

3.

For
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
,

tU_{\alpha,0}t^{-1}\subset U_{\alpha,r}^{E}\Longleftrightarrow\alpha(t)\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{m}_{E}^{r}\Longleftrightarrow v(\alpha(t))>\frac{1}{p-1}.

We remind the reader that
v(p)=1
. These inequalities can be summarized as saying that

\frac{1}{p-1}<v(\alpha(t))<\frac{1}{e}-\frac{1}{p-1}

for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. It remains to construct a pair
(E,t)
satisfying all these inequalities. We will do this by means of an auxiliary cocharacter defined as follows. Start by enlarging
\Delta
to a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis for
X^{*}({\bf{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
and choose a linear form
X^{*}({\bf{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
sending all elements of
\Delta
to one. This is given by pairing with some element
\mu_{0}\in X_{*}({\bf{T}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus
\langle\alpha,\mu_{0}\rangle=\text{ht}(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
. Pick an
a\in\mathbb{N}
for which
a\mu_{0}\in X_{*}({\bf{T}})
and call this cocharacter
\mu
. Now suppose
E/L
is large enough to contain an element
c\neq 0
with
v(c)=\frac{1}{aeh}
. We claim
t=\mu(c)
works. Indeed

v(\alpha(t))=v(c^{\langle\alpha,\mu\rangle})=\langle\alpha,\mu\rangle v(c)=a\text{ht}(\alpha)\cdot\frac{1}{aeh}=\frac{\text{ht}(\alpha)}{eh}

and
1\leq\text{ht}(\alpha)\leq h-1
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. It therefore suffices to check that

\frac{1}{p-1}<\frac{1}{eh}<\frac{1}{h-1}\cdot\bigg(\frac{1}{e}-\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg),

and as one readily verifies this amounts to our assumption that
p-1>eh
.
∎

Combining the two preceding lemmas endows

with a
p
-valuation
\omega
by restricting and conjugating the one on
K_{r}
. We wish to give an intrinsic description of

\omega
independent of the choice of

. First we introduce the following notion.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
H
be a
p
-valuable group and let
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{s})
be a list of subgroups such that the multiplication map

m:H_{1}\times\cdots\times H_{s}\longrightarrow H

is bijective. We say that a

-valuation
\omega
on
H
is compatible with the tuple
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{s})
if

\omega(h_{1}\cdots h_{s})=\min\{\omega(h_{1}),\ldots,\omega(h_{s})\}

for all
h_{i}\in H_{i}
with
i=1,\ldots,s
.

In this situation one can construct an ordered basis for
(H,\omega)
by concatenating ordered bases for each factor
(H_{i},\omega|_{H_{i}})
as
i=1,\ldots,s
. For the definition of an ordered basis of a
p
-valued group we refer the reader to
[
Sch11
, p. 182]
.

Here is our characterization of the
p
-valuation we have defined on
I
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume
p-1>eh
. Then there exists a unique
p
-valuation
\omega
on
I
satisfying the following properties:

(a)

\omega
is compatible with the Iwahori factorization (
2.1
) of
I
, cf. Definition
3.3
.

(b)

\omega(u_{\alpha}(x))=v(x)+\frac{\text{ht}(\alpha)}{eh}
where

\begin{cases}\text{$x\in\mathfrak{m}$ if $\alpha\in\Phi^{-}$}\\
\text{$x\in\mathcal{O}$ if $\alpha\in\Phi^{+}$.}\end{cases}

(c)

\omega(t)=\frac{1}{e}\cdot\sup\{n\in\mathbb{N}:t\in T^{n}\}
for
t\in T^{1}
.

(Recall that
T^{n}
denotes the
n^{\text{th}}
congruence subgroup of
T
; in part (a) the compatibility of
\omega
holds for any ordering of the roots

\Phi^{\pm}
.) Moreover
(I,\omega)
is saturated (i.e., every
g\in I
with
\omega(g)\geq\frac{p}{p-1}
lies in
I^{p}
).

Proof.

Uniqueness is clear (as
\omega
is determined by its restriction to each factor of the Iwahori decomposition). We need to show the
\omega
we defined satisfies (a) through (c).
Fix
(E,t)
as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
. Then
\omega(g)=\frac{1}{e_{E}}\cdot\sup\{n\in\mathbb{N}:tgt^{-1}\in K_{n}\}
for
g\in I
. Consider the Iwahori factorization

g=\bigg(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}u_{\alpha}\bigg)\cdot t_{1}\cdot\bigg(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}u_{\alpha}\bigg)

according to (
2.1
). Conjugating by
t
we find that
tgt^{-1}\in K_{n}
if and only if all
tu_{\alpha}t^{-1}\in U_{\alpha,n}^{E}
and
t_{1}\in T_{E}^{n}
. The largest
n
with this property is the minimum of
\min_{\alpha\in\Phi}\omega(u_{\alpha})
and
\omega(t_{1})
. This shows (a). The formula in (b) follows almost immediately from the relation
v(\alpha(t))=\frac{\text{ht}(\alpha)}{eh}
as noted in the proof of Lemma

. Since

4.
Rigid analytic groups

In this section
(H,\omega)
denotes an arbitrary
p
-valued group equipped with an ordered basis
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})
.
Thus
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\mapsto h_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{x_{d}}
gives a homeomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\rightarrow H
and
\omega(h)=\min_{i=1,\ldots,d}\{v(x_{i})+\omega(h_{i})\}

where
h\in H
is factored as
h_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{x_{d}}
.

We will review how to associate an affinoid rigid analytic group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
to a saturated pair
(H,\omega)
. The underlying rigid analytic space is nothing but the

d
-dimensional closed ball
\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle)
. Here
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle
denotes the Tate algebra in
d
variables over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(H,\omega)
be a saturated
p
-valued group with basis
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})
. Then there exists a unique rigid analytic group

\mathbb{H}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that

(a)

\mathbb{H}=\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle)
as a rigid analytic space;

(b)

the map
\varphi\mapsto h_{1}^{\varphi(Z_{1})}\cdots h_{d}^{\varphi(Z_{d})}
gives an isomorphism of abstract groups

\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}-\text{alg}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle,\mathbb{Q}_{p})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H.

Proof.

We construct
\mathbb{H}
. The multiplication map
\mathbb{H}\times_{\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}\mathbb{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}
arises from comultiplication on the Tate algebra

\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle
defined as follows. By
[
Sch11
, Prop. 29.2]
and
[
Sch11
, Rem. 29.3]
there exists power series

F_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\langle X_{1},\ldots X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots Y_{d}\rangle
for
i=1,\ldots,d
such that

(h_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{x_{d}})\cdot(h_{1}^{y_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{y_{d}})=h_{1}^{F_{1}(x,y)}\cdots h_{d}^{F_{d}(x,y)}

for all tuples
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. The fact that
F_{i}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(not just
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
) is due to the assumption that
(H,\omega)
is saturated, and this allows us to define the map

\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle X_{1},\ldots X_{d}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Y_{1},\ldots Y_{d}\rangle=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle X_{1},\ldots X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots Y_{d}\rangle

by sending
Z_{i}\mapsto F_{i}(X,Y)
where
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})
and similarly for
Y
. Since
(F_{1},\ldots,F_{d})
is a formal group law one quickly verifies this gives a rigid analytic group structure on
\mathbb{H}=\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle)
. (The identity
\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
comes from the co-unit on the Tate algebra and we leave it to the reader to construct the inversion map
\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
along the same lines.)

To show this
\mathbb{H}
satisfies (b) let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Unwinding definitions it suffices to note that

(4.2)

(\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2})(Z_{i})=F_{i}\big(\varphi_{1}(Z_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{1}(Z_{d}),\varphi_{2}(Z_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{2}(Z_{d})\big),

but this is immediate in light of how multiplication on
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
relates to comultiplication.

Finally, suppose
\text{Sp}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle)
carries some rigid analytic group structure for which (b) holds. This structure is given by power series

G_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\langle X_{1},\ldots X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots Y_{d}\rangle
for
i=1,\ldots,d
satisfying the analogue of (
4.2
) – still with
F_{i}
on the right-hand side, but with

\varphi_{1}\cdot\varphi_{2}
defined relative to the
G_{i}
. This leads to the equality

G_{i}\big(\varphi_{1}(Z_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{1}(Z_{d}),\varphi_{2}(Z_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{2}(Z_{d})\big)=F_{i}\big(\varphi_{1}(Z_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{1}(Z_{d}),\varphi_{2}(Z_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{2}(Z_{d})\big)

Since
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are arbitrary we infer that

F_{i}
and
G_{i}
define the same function
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and therefore
F_{i}=G_{i}
for all
i
, cf.
[
Sch11
, Cor. 5.8]
with
\varepsilon=1
and
V=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.∎

The next result shows how this construction behaves under a change of basis.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(H,\omega)
be a saturated group with bases
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{d})
and
(h_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,h_{d}^{\prime})
.
Let
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
denote the rigid analytic groups associated with these bases as in Proposition
4.1
. There is a unique isomorphism of rigid analytic groups

\mathbb{H}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
such that the induced map on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-points makes the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Proof.

Uniqueness is a straightforward consequence of the fact that
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is Zariski dense in
\mathbb{H}
. (An automorphism of

\mathbb{H}
inducing the identity on
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
must be the identity.)

To define the desired isomorphism we compare the formal group law
\underline{F}=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{d})
from the proof of Proposition
4.1
to the formal group law

\underline{F}^{\prime}=(F_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,F_{d}^{\prime})
arising from
(h_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,h_{d}^{\prime})
. Again by
[
Sch11
, Prop. 29.2]
and
[
Sch11
, Rem. 29.3]
there are
d
power series

\Phi_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\langle X_{1},\ldots X_{d}\rangle
such that

h_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{x_{d}}=(h_{1}^{\prime})^{\Phi_{1}(x)}\cdots(h_{d}^{\prime})^{\Phi_{d}(x)}

for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. Similarly with the bases interchanged. This gives an isomorphism of formal group laws
\underline{F}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\underline{F}^{\prime}
,
see
[
Sch11
, p. 143]
. Mapping
Z_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto\Phi_{i}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d})
then yields an isomorphism

\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots Z_{d}^{\prime}\rangle\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\langle Z_{1},\ldots Z_{d}\rangle

which intertwines the two comultiplications. Passing to spectra we finally get an isomorphism of rigid analytic groups
\mathbb{H}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
. The commutativity of the diagram essentially just boils down to the definition of the
\Phi_{i}
.
∎

Suppose every

is saturated, where

where

. (Here and elsewhere
1
1

1

with the pro-
p

in this article

Definition 4.5
.

The space

consists of all functions

.

The

\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(H,K)

Proof.

We right translate

5.
Weyl conjugates

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
(G,T)
.
Recall, as noted in the proof of
[
OS19
, Lem. 2.3]
for instance, that multiplication defines an injection

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\alpha}\times T\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}U_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow G

where the products are ordered arbitrarily. We conjugate it by
w\in W
(or rather a representative in the normalizer of
T
) which shows the multiplication map

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{w\alpha}\times T\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}U_{w\alpha}\hookrightarrow G

remains an injection. Here
U_{w\alpha}=wU_{\alpha}w^{-1}
and
(w\alpha)(t)=\alpha(w^{-1}tw)
.

Definition 5.1
.

For
w\in W
introduce the two subgroups

•

U_{w}^{-}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}(I\cap U_{w\alpha})

•

U_{w}^{+}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(I\cap U_{w\alpha})

for some ordering of
\Phi^{-}
and
\Phi^{+}
. Note that
I\cap U_{w\alpha}=\begin{cases}\text{$U_{w\alpha,1}$ if $w\alpha\in\Phi^{-}$}\\
\text{$U_{w\alpha,0}$ if $w\alpha\in\Phi^{+}$.}\end{cases}

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume the derived group
{\bf{G}}^{\text{der}}
is simply connected. Then the multiplication map gives a homeomorphism

U_{w}^{-}\times T^{1}\times U_{w}^{+}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}I

for all
w\in W
. In particular
U_{w}^{\pm}=I\cap w\big(\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{\pm}}U_{\alpha}\big)w^{-1}
. Furthermore, when
p-1>eh
the
p
-valuation
\omega
on
I
from Proposition
3.4

is compatible with the above factorization for all
w
(see Definition
3.3
).

Proof.

The previous discussion shows the multiplication map

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}(I\cap U_{w\alpha})\times T^{1}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(I\cap U_{w\alpha})\longrightarrow I

is injective. It is also surjective, as we now explain. Since we are assuming
{\bf{G}}^{\text{der}}
is simply connected, Bruhat-Tits theory shows that the full Iwahori subgroup
J\supset I
has a factorization

T^{0}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}(J\cap U_{\alpha})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}J

for any ordering of
\Phi
, see
[
OS10
, Lem. 3.3.2]
and the references given in the proof thereof. For full disclosure those references to
[
Tit79
, Sect. 3.1.1]
require
{\bf{G}}
to be semisimple and simply connected, but one can easily reduce the general reductive case to the semisimple case by observing that all root groups lie in the derived group.

Clearly
J\cap U_{\alpha}
are pro-
p
groups (subgroups of

\mathfrak{m}^{-N}
for large enough
N
) so
J\cap U_{\alpha}\subset I
and we therefore also have the analogous factorization of pro-
p
Iwahori
I
itself

T^{1}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}(I\cap U_{\alpha})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}I.

We order
\Phi
as follows. The first batch of roots is
w\Phi^{-}
(in some order) and the second is
w\Phi^{+}
. Since
T^{1}
normalizes each
I\cap U_{\alpha}
this gives the surjectivity.

The compatibility of
\omega
follows from the case
w=1
by noting that any
p
-valuation is invariant under conjugation, see
[
Sch11
, p. 169]
.
∎

6.
Rigid induction and slopes

Start with a continuous character
\chi:T^{1}\rightarrow K^{\times}
and consider the conjugate
(w\chi)(t)=\chi(w^{-1}tw)
for
w\in W
. Extend
w\chi
to a character of

U_{w}^{-}T^{1}
by making it trivial on
U_{w}^{-}
and introduce the rigid analytic principal series representation

(6.1)

\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)=\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(I,K):f(bg)=(w\chi)(b)f(g)\hskip 6.0pt\forall b\in U_{w}^{-}T^{1}\}.

This is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(I,K)
because
|f(g)|\leq\|f\|_{\text{sup}}\leq\|f\|
for all
g\in I
. Here
\|f\|_{\text{sup}}
is the supremum norm of
f
on
I
. The group
I
acts by right translations. Of course, for the space in (

) to be non-trivial we require that
\chi
is rigid when viewed as a
K
-valued function
T^{1}\rightarrow K^{\times}\hookrightarrow K
. In the sequel we will make this assumption:
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(T^{1},K)
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\chi:T^{1}\rightarrow K^{\times}
be a rigid analytic character. Then the restriction map

f\mapsto f|_{U_{w}^{+}}
gives an isomorphism of
U_{w}^{+}
-representations

\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(U_{w}^{+},K)

preserving the Gauss norms.

Proof.

By promoting the factorization in Lemma
5.2
to an isomorphism of rigid spaces as in Remark
4.4
one can immediately extend a function in
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(U_{w}^{+},K)

to a function in
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(I,K)
with the required transformation property.

For the comment on norms, as Gauss norms are multiplicative it suffices to note that
\|\chi\|=1
. Indeed, we certainly have
\|\chi\|\geq\|\chi\|_{\text{sup}}=1
. Applying this to

\chi^{-1}
we find that
\|\chi\|=1
.
∎

Next we transfer the
T^{1}
-action on
\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)
across the isomorphism in Lemma

Lemma 6.7

Proposition 7.2

\overline{\text{im}(\psi)}=\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)
we must have
\text{im}(\psi)=\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}-\text{fin}}
. In other words
\psi:\mathcal{V}_{d(w\chi)}\rightarrow\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}-\text{fin}}
is surjective.
Again, since
\mathcal{V}_{d(w\chi)}[\lambda]\twoheadrightarrow\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\mathfrak{t}_{0}-\text{fin}}[\lambda]
must be bijective
\forall\lambda
as the two spaces have the same finite dimension,

\psi
is bijective as well. Finally, to verify
\mathcal{V}_{d(w\chi)}
is simple, suppose
\mathcal{E}\subsetneq\mathcal{V}_{d(w\chi)}
is a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-submodule. Then, by the above implication applied to
E=\psi(\mathcal{E})
, we find that
\overline{\psi(\mathcal{E})}\subsetneq\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)
and so
\overline{\psi(\mathcal{E})}=\{0\}
. Equivalently
\mathcal{E}=\{0\}
by the bijectivity of
\psi
.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

Assertion (
7.3
) and the implication (
7.4
) in the above proof are analogous to
[
Ray20b
, Lem. 3.13, Cor. 3.14]
respectively. Our proof is more or less just a rewording of the argument given there.

As explained in
[
OS10
, Cor. 3.4.11]
for example,
\mathcal{V}_{d(w\chi)}
is simple for all
w\in W
if (and only if)
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
is simple. This is a straightforward argument; conjugation by
w
identifies the Verma modules.

The BGG criterion
[
Dix96
, Thm. 7.6.24]
tells us
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
is simple if and only if
(d\chi+\delta)(H_{\alpha})\notin\{1,2,3,\ldots\}

for all positive roots
\alpha
relative to the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})
. (To aid comparison, in the notation of
[
Dix96
]
we have
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}=M(d\chi+\delta)
.)

8.
Multiplicity one

Suppose
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
is simple. In this section we note that as
w\in W
varies the representations
\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)
are non-isomorphic. This relies on the non-existence of a Haar measure on the space of rigid analytic functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We include the standard argument for convenience.

Lemma 8.1
.

There is no nonzero translation-invariant continuous linear form

C^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},K)\rightarrow K
.

Proof.

We let
T:C^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},K)\rightarrow C^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},K)
denote the translation operator
(Tf)(x)=f(x+1)
. Suppose
\ell:C^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},K)\rightarrow K
is a
translation-invariant form; meaning
\ell(Tf)=\ell(f)
. To show
\ell=0
we consider the values of
\ell
on the monomials
f_{N}(x)=x^{N}
. We will verify that

\ell(f_{N})=0
by strong induction on
N
. The case
N=0
follows by observing
Tf_{1}=f_{0}+f_{1}
and applying
\ell
. Now suppose
\ell(f_{i})=0
for all

i=0,\ldots,N-1
. Note that

Tf_{N+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{N+1}\binom{N+1}{i}f_{i}=f_{N+1}+(N+1)f_{N}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\binom{N+1}{i}f_{i}.

Applying
\ell
yields
\ell(Tf_{N+1})=\ell(f_{N+1})+(N+1)\ell(f_{N})
by the induction hypothesis. Since the left-hand side equals

\ell(f_{N+1})
we conclude
\ell(f_{N})=0
. By continuity
\ell=0
on all of
C^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},K)
.
∎

Without any simplicity assumption on
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
we have the following result.

Proposition 8.2
.

\text{Hom}_{I}^{\text{cont}}\big(\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi),\text{Ind}_{U_{w^{\prime}}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w^{\prime}\chi)\big)=0
for
w\neq w^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Rigid induction is easily checked to satisfy Frobenius reciprocity for globally analytic representations (one of the adjunction maps is given by the orbit map which is rigid analytic).
So we have to show that

\text{Hom}_{U_{w^{\prime}}^{-}T^{1}}^{\text{cont}}\big(\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi),K_{w^{\prime}\chi}\big)=0.

We identify
\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)
with
C^{\text{rig}}(U_{w}^{+},K)
as a
U_{w}^{+}
-representation. It suffices to show there is no nonzero
K
-linear form

\ell:C^{\text{rig}}(U_{w}^{+},K)\rightarrow K
such that
\ell(uf)=\ell(f)
for all
u\in U_{w}^{+}\cap U_{w^{\prime}}^{-}
. Note that this intersection is non-trivial since

w\Phi^{+}\cap w^{\prime}\Phi^{-}\neq\varnothing
. Indeed
w^{-1}w^{\prime}\neq 1
has length
l(w^{-1}w^{\prime})=|\Phi^{+}\cap w^{-1}w^{\prime}\Phi^{-}|>0
. Pick a
\beta\in w\Phi^{+}\cap w^{\prime}\Phi^{-}

and consider the subgroup
I\cap U_{\beta}\subset U_{w}^{+}\cap U_{w^{\prime}}^{-}
. By ordering
\Phi^{+}
appropriately we may factor
U_{w}^{+}
as

U_{w}^{+}=Y\times(I\cap U_{\beta})
and promote this to the level of rigid spaces as in remark
4.4
. Thus
C^{\text{rig}}(U_{w}^{+},K)\simeq C^{\text{rig}}(Y,K)\hat{\otimes}_{K}C^{\text{rig}}(I\cap U_{\beta},K)
as representations of
I\cap U_{\beta}
. (The action on
C^{\text{rig}}(Y,K)
is trivial.)
This reduces the problem to checking there is no nonzero
I\cap U_{\beta}
-invariant linear form

C^{\text{rig}}(I\cap U_{\beta},K)\rightarrow K
. Since
I\cap U_{\beta}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ell}
we just have to check there is no nonzero translation-invariant form

C^{\text{rig}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\ell},K)\rightarrow K
. This easily reduces to the case
\ell=1
which is Lemma
8.1
.
∎

The previous argument is an adaptation of the proof of
[
OS10
, Prop. 3.5.1]
which they in turn partially attribute to Frommer.

9.
Principal series

To add some finishing strokes to the proof of our main theorem, suppose
\chi:T\rightarrow K^{\times}
is a continuous character and inflate it to
B^{-}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\alpha}\times T
. Consider the continuous induction

\text{Ind}_{B^{-}}^{G}(\chi)_{\text{cont}}=\{f\in C(G,K):f(bg)=\chi(b)f(g)\hskip 6.0pt\forall b\in B^{-}\}.

By the Bruhat-Tits decomposition
G=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}B^{-}wJ
it decomposes as a
J
-representation into

\text{Ind}_{B^{-}}^{G}(\chi)_{\text{cont}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{w\in W}\text{Ind}_{J\cap wB^{-}w^{-1}}^{J}(w\chi)_{\text{cont}}

by sending
f\mapsto(f_{w})_{w\in W}
where
f_{w}(g)=f(w^{-1}g)
. Since
J=T^{0}I
and
T^{1}=T^{0}\cap I
further restriction gives an isomorphism of

I
-representations

\text{Ind}_{J\cap wB^{-}w^{-1}}^{J}(w\chi)_{\text{cont}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\text{Ind}_{I\cap wB^{-}w^{-1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\text{cont}}.

The space of rigid vectors (or as in Emerton’s terminology
\mathbb{I}
-analytic vectors, cf.
[
Eme17
, Df. 3.3.1]
) of the right-hand side coincides with the rigid induction

\text{Ind}_{U_{w}^{-}T^{1}}^{I}(w\chi)
from above. This latter observation is a consequence of
[
Eme17
, Prop. 3.3.7]
. Our space
\mathcal{C}^{\text{rig}}(I,K)
corresponds to
\mathcal{C}^{\text{an}}(\mathbb{I},K)

in his notation.

Altogether this proves the main theorem
1.1
in the introduction.

10.
The example
\text{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

To highlight a concrete new example of our main result, we work out the case of
\text{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
explicitly. We follow the conventions in
[
RS07
]
. Thus

G=\text{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\{g\in\text{GL}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}):{{}^{t}g}Jg=J\},\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0pt\hskip 6.0ptJ=\begin{pmatrix}&&&1\\
&&1&\\
&-1&&\\
-1&&&\end{pmatrix}.

We take our Borel subgroup
P
to consist of all upper triangular matrices in
\text{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The maximal torus is the subgroup of all diagonal matrices

T=\{t_{a,b}=\begin{pmatrix}a&&&\\
&b&&\\
&&b^{-1}&\\
&&&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}:a,b\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\}.

The Weyl group
W
is dihedral of order
8
. There are two simple roots;
\Delta=\{\alpha,\beta\}
where
\alpha(t_{a,b})=\frac{a}{b}
and

\beta(t_{a,b})=b^{2}
. Then
\Phi^{+}=\{\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta,2\alpha+\beta\}
. In particular
\theta=2\alpha+\beta
is the highest root and the Coxeter number is
h=4
. We therefore require that
p>5
in order to apply our theorem. Moreover, one immediately verifies that
\delta(t_{a,b})=a^{2}b
. See
[
RS07
, Ch. 2]
for a discussion of the root system.

Let
\chi:T\rightarrow K^{\times}
be a character. Write it as
\chi(t_{a,b})=\chi_{1}(a)\chi_{2}(b)
for a pair of characters
\chi_{i}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow K^{\times}
. We require that their restrictions
1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow K^{\times}
are rigid analytic. This means they can be expanded as

\chi_{i}(\exp(px))=\sum_{r\geq 0}\gamma_{i,r}x^{r}
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. (Here
\gamma_{i,r}\in K
converge to
0
as
r\rightarrow\infty
.) If we write
\chi_{i}(t)=t^{c_{i}}
for
t
close to
1
, where
c_{i}=(d\chi_{i})(1)\in K
, then
\gamma_{i,r}=\frac{1}{r!}p^{r}c_{i}^{r}\rightarrow 0
precisely when

v_{p}(c_{i})>\frac{1}{p-1}-1=-\frac{p-2}{p-1}.

Here
v_{p}
denotes the valuation on
K
with
v_{p}(p)=1
. We will assume this in the sequel.

The BGG criterion for the simplicity of
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
involves
H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{t}
for all positive roots
\alpha
. One can read those off from the coroots given in
[
RS07
, p. 42]
. We list them here for convenience.

H_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&-1&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&-1\end{pmatrix}H_{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&-1&\\
&&&0\end{pmatrix}H_{\alpha+\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&-1&\\
&&&-1\end{pmatrix}H_{2\alpha+\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&0&&\\
&&0&\\
&&&-1\end{pmatrix}.

This allows us to make the BGG criterion explicit in terms of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.

•

(d\chi+\delta)(H_{\alpha})=c_{1}-c_{2}+1\notin\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
;

•

(d\chi+\delta)(H_{\beta})=c_{2}+1\notin\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
;

•

(d\chi+\delta)(H_{\alpha+\beta})=c_{1}+c_{2}+3\notin\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
;

•

(d\chi+\delta)(H_{2\alpha+\beta})=2c_{1}+2\notin\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

All four conditions are satisfied precisely when the Verma module
\mathcal{V}_{d\chi}
is simple. If so, our main result shows that

\text{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\chi)_{\mathbb{I}-\text{an}}
breaks up as a direct sum of eight mutually non-isomorphic topologically irreducible representations

\text{Ind}_{I\cap wPw^{-1}}^{I}(w\chi)_{\mathbb{I}-\text{an}}
parametrized by the Weyl elements
w
. Here the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup
I
consist of symplectic matrices which are lower triangular modulo
p
,

I=\{g\in\text{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}):g\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
*&1&&\\
*&*&1&\\
*&*&*&1\end{pmatrix}\text{mod $p$}\}.

When
w=1
the subgroup
I\cap wPw^{-1}
consists of upper triangular matrices
\begin{pmatrix}a&*&*&*\\
&b&*&*\\
&&b^{-1}&*\\
&&&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

in
\text{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
with
a,b\in 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and all
\ast
-entries in
p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

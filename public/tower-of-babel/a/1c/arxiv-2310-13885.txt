Nittka’s invariance criterion and
Hilbert space

Abstract




Abstract

Nittka gave an efficient criterion on a form defined on
L_{2}(\Omega)

which implies that the associated semigroup is
L_{p}
-invariant
for some given
p\in(1,\infty)
.
We extend this criterion to the Hilbert space valued
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
As an application we consider elliptic systems of pure second order.
Our main result shows that the induced semigroup is
L_{p}
-contractive
for all
p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
for some
1<p_{-}<2<p_{+}<\infty
.

L_{p}
-contraction, sesquilinear forms,
strictly convex Banach space.

1
Introduction

Let
(S_{t})_{t>0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L_{2}(\Omega)
,
with
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
, which is associated with a closed
sesquilinear form.
Ouhabaz
[
Ouh1
]
gave a convenient criterion on the
form characterising when the semigroup is
L_{\infty}
-contractive.
It is more complicated to describe
L_{p}
-contractivity if

1<p<\infty
.
The reason is the fact that there is no explicit formula which describes
the orthogonal projection from
L_{2}(\Omega)
to the
closed convex set
\{u\in L_{2}(\Omega):\|u\|_{p}\leq 1\}

if
1<p<\infty
and
p\neq 2
.

Nonetheless, Nittka
[
Nit
]
succeeded to overcome the difficulty by a structural
analysis and developed an efficient criterion for the

L_{p}
-contractivity of a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L_{2}(\Omega)

which is associated with a form.

The purpose of this paper is twofold.
Our first aim is to present Nittka’s result.
We do this within the more general setting of the
vector-valued space
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
, where
H
is a Hilbert space.
The result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal B},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space.
Let
H
be a Hilbert space.
Fix
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Define

C=\{u\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)\cap L_{p}(\Omega,H):\|u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,H)}\leq 1\}.

Let
P
be the orthogonal projection of
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
onto
C
.
Let
{\cal V}
be a Hilbert space which is continuously and
densely embedded in
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
Let
\gothic{a}\colon{\cal V}\times{\cal V}\to\mathds{C}
be a continuous elliptic sesquilinear form,
let
A
be the operator in
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
associated with
\gothic{a}

and let
S
be the semigroup generated by
-A
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\|S_{t}u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,H)}\leq\|u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,H)}

for all
u\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)\cap L_{p}(\Omega,H)
and
t>0
.

(ii)

P{\cal V}\subset{\cal V}
and

\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(u,\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u)\geq 0

for all
u\in{\cal V}

with
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in{\cal V}
.

Our proof in Section
2
is slightly different from Nittka’s
since we exploit
the strict convexity of the space
L_{p}(\Omega,H)
for
all
1<p<\infty
, which we prove in Appendix
B
.

Our second aim is to apply the criterion to
purely second-order Hilbert space valued
elliptic operators with Neumann boundary conditions.
They generate a contractive
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{t})_{t>0}

on
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
Of particular interest are systems, that is
H=\mathds{C}^{d}
.
In Section
3
we show that there is an interval
[p_{-},p_{+}]
, with

1<p_{-}<2<p_{+}<\infty
, such that the semigroup
S

extends to a contractive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L_{p}(\Omega,H)

for all
p\in[p_{-},p_{+}]
.
To prove the needed estimates, we use a chain rule
formula, which is quite delicate and will be proved in Appendix
A
.
In the scalar case our results may be compared with
Cialdea–Maz’ya
[
CM
]
,
who introduced an algebraic version of
L_{p}
-dissipativity
and presented an algebraic characterisation for scalar-valued
elliptic operators.
This algebraic characterisation was refined by
Carbonaro–Dragičević

[
CD
]
and they used the
result of Nittka to describe contractive
C_{0}
-semigroups on
L_{p}(\Omega)

via the notion that they called
p
-ellipticity.

2
Nittka’s criterion for
L_{p}
-contractivity

Let
(\Omega,{\cal B},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space.
Let
H
be a Hilbert space.
For all
p\in[1,\infty)
we write
L_{p}=L_{p}(\Omega,H)
.
If
u\in L_{p}
, then we write
\|u\|_{p}=\|u\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,H)}

and
\|u\|_{H}\colon\Omega\to\mathds{R}
is the function from
\Omega
into
\mathds{R}

such that

\|u\|_{H}(x)=\|u(x)\|_{H}

for all
x\in\Omega
.
Further we write
{\cal H}=L_{2}=L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
Throughout this paper we fix
p\in(1,\infty)
.
Define

C=\{u\in{\cal H}\cap L_{p}:\|u\|_{p}\leq 1\}.

Clearly
C
is convex and it follows from Fatou’s lemma that
C
is
closed in
{\cal H}
.
Let
P\colon{\cal H}\to C
be the orthogonal projection.
For all
u\in{\cal H}
define

N(u)=\{h\in{\cal H}:\mathop{\rm Re}(h,v-u)_{\cal H}\leq 0\mbox{ for all }v\in C\}.

Then
N(u)
is a closed cone in
{\cal H}
with
0\in N(u)
.
We state an easy property regarding
C
and
N(u)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
f\in{\cal H}
there exist unique
u\in C
and
h\in N(u)

such that
f=u+h
.
Actually,
u=Pf
.

Proof
.

Let
u=Pf
and
h=f-Pf
.
Then
u\in C
and

\mathop{\rm Re}(h,v-u)_{\cal H}=\mathop{\rm Re}(f-Pf,\penalty v-Pf)_{\cal H}\leq 0

for all
v\in C
.
This proves existence.
Let also
\tilde{u}\in C
and
\tilde{h}\in N(\tilde{u})
be such that

f=\tilde{u}+\tilde{h}
.
Then
\mathop{\rm Re}(h,\tilde{u}-u)_{\cal H}\leq 0
and similarly

\mathop{\rm Re}(\tilde{h},u-\tilde{u})_{\cal H}\leq 0
.
Hence
\mathop{\rm Re}(h-\tilde{h},\tilde{u}-u)_{\cal H}\leq 0
.
Since
h-\tilde{h}=\tilde{u}-u
this implies that

\mathop{\rm Re}\|h-\tilde{h}\|_{\cal H}^{2}\leq 0
and the statement follows.
∎

If
u\colon\Omega\to H
is a function, then define

\mathop{\rm sgn}u\colon\Omega\to H
by

(\mathop{\rm sgn}u)(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\|u(x)\|_{H}}\,u(x)&\mbox{if }u(x)\neq 0,\\[5.0pt]
0&\mbox{if }u(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

Note that
\mathop{\rm sgn}u=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}u_{\varepsilon}

pointwise, where the function
u_{\varepsilon}\colon\Omega\to H
is defined by

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\|u(x)\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon}}\,u(x)
.
Hence
\mathop{\rm sgn}u
is (Bochner) measurable if
u
is (Bochner) measurable.
Then also
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u
is (Bochner) measurable
whenever
u
is (Bochner) measurable.

For a description of
N(u)
we use that
L_{p}(\Omega,H)
is
strictly convex.
See Appendix
B
for a proof.
Recall that a Banach space
E
is called
strictly convex

if for all
\xi,\eta\in E
with
\|\xi\|_{E}=1=\|\eta\|_{E}
and
\xi\neq\eta

it follows that
\|\xi+\eta\|_{E}<2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
E
be a Banach space.
Assume that
E^{*}
strictly convex
and let
x\in E
.
Then there exists a unique
f\in E^{*}
such that

\mathop{\rm Re}f(x)=\|x\|_{E}^{2}=\|f\|_{E^{*}}^{2}
.
This unique
f
satisfies
f(x)=\|x\|_{E}^{2}
.

Proof
.

The existence of an
f\in E^{*}
such that
f(x)=\|x\|_{E}^{2}=\|f\|_{E^{*}}^{2}

is a well-known consequence of the Hahn–Banach theorem.
For the uniqueness, we may assume without loss of generality that

\|x\|_{E}=1
.
Suppose also
g\in E^{*}
with
\mathop{\rm Re}g(x)=\|x\|_{E}^{2}=\|g\|_{E^{*}}^{2}

and
g\neq f
.
Define
h=\frac{1}{2}\,(f+g)
.
Then
\|h\|_{E^{*}}<1
by the strict convexity of
E^{*}
.
But then

1={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathop{\rm Re}(f(x)+g(x))=\mathop{\rm Re}h(x)\leq\|h\|_{E^{*}}\,\|x\|_{E}<1.

This is a contradiction.
∎

Now we are able to give a characterisation for
N(u)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
u\in C
.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

N(u)\neq\{0\}
.

(ii)

\|u\|_{p}=1
and
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in{\cal H}
.

If these conditions are valid, then

N(u)=\{t\,\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u:t\in[0,\infty)\}
.

Proof
.

‘
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
’.
Let
v\in C
.
Then the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the Hölder inequality give

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Re}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u,v)_{\cal H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\|v\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}^{(p-1)p^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\,\|v\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}^{p/p^{\prime}}=1=\mathop{\rm Re}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u,u)_{\cal H}.

So
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in N(u)

and
\{t\,\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u:t\in[0,\infty)\}\subset N(u)
.
In particular,
N(u)\neq\{0\}
.

‘
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
’.
We first show that
\|u\|_{p}=1
.
Suppose that
\|u\|_{p}<1
.
Let
h\in N(u)
.
Let
w\in L_{2}\cap L_{p}
with
\|w\|_{p}\leq 1-\|u\|_{p}
.
Then
u+w\in C
.
Since
h\in N(u)
one deduces that
\mathop{\rm Re}(h,w)_{\cal H}\leq 0
.
Because
L_{2}\cap L_{p}
is dense in
{\cal H}
it follows that
h=0
.
Hence
(ii)
implies that
\|u\|_{p}=1
.

Next let
h\in N(u)
with
h\neq 0
.
If
v\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)\cap L_{p}(\Omega,H)
, then

\mathop{\rm Re}(h,v)_{\cal H}\leq\|v\|_{p}\,\mathop{\rm Re}(h,u)_{\cal H}
.
So
h\in L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega,H)
and
\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\mathop{\rm Re}(h,u)_{\cal H}
.
If
\mathop{\rm Re}(h,u)_{\cal H}=0
, then
\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}=0
and
h=0
, which is
a contradiction.
So
\mathop{\rm Re}(h,u)_{\cal H}\neq 0
.
Multiplying
h
with a strictly positive constant we may assume
that
\mathop{\rm Re}(h,u)_{\cal H}=1
.
Then

\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1=\mathop{\rm Re}(h,u)_{\cal H}\leq\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}\,\|u\|_{p}\leq\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}.

So
\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}=1=\mathop{\rm Re}(h,u)_{\cal H}=\mathop{\rm Re}\langle h,u\rangle_{L_{p^{\prime}}\times L_{p}}
.
We proved that

\mathop{\rm Re}\langle h,u\rangle_{L_{p^{\prime}}\times L_{p}}=\|h\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}=\|u\|_{p}^{2}.

On the other hand,

\int_{\Omega}\Big\|\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\Big\|_{H}^{p^{\prime}}=\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}^{(p-1)p^{\prime}}=\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}^{p}=1,

so
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in L_{p^{\prime}}
and furthermore

\mathop{\rm Re}\langle\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u,u\rangle_{L_{p^{\prime}}\times L_{p}}=\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}^{p}=1=\Big\|\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\Big\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}=\|u\|_{p}^{2}.

By Lemma
2.2
we obtain that

\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u=h\in{\cal H}
.
Moreover,
N(u)\subset\{t\,\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u:t\in[0,\infty)\}
.
∎

Let
{\cal V}
be a Hilbert space that is continuously and densely embedded in

{\cal H}=L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
Let
\gothic{a}\colon{\cal V}\times{\cal V}\to\mathds{C}
be a sesquilinear form such
that
\gothic{a}
is
continuous
, that is there is an
M>0
such that

|\gothic{a}(u,v)|\leq M\,\|u\|_{\cal V}\,\|v\|_{\cal V}

for all
u,v\in{\cal V}
, and
\gothic{a}
is
elliptic
, that is
there are
\mu>0
and
\omega\in\mathds{R}
such that

\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(u,u)+\omega\,||u\|_{\cal H}^{2}\geq\mu\,\|u\|_{\cal V}^{2}

(1)

for all
u\in{\cal V}
.
Then there is a unique operator
A
in
{\cal H}
whose graph is

\mathop{\rm graph}(A)=\{(u,f):u\in{\cal V},\;f\in{\cal H}\mbox{ and }\gothic{a}(u,v)=(f,v)_{\cal H}\mbox{ for all }v\in{\cal V}\}.

We call
A
the
operator associated with the form
\gothic{a}
.
Then
-A
generates a holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S_{t})_{t>0}

in
{\cal H}
satisfying
\|S_{t}\|_{{\cal H}\to{\cal H}}\leq e^{\omega t}

for all
t>0
, where
\omega
is as in (
1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

‘
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
’.
It follows from
[
Ouh2
]
Theorem 2.2 1)
\Rightarrow
2) that

P{\cal V}\subset{\cal V}
and
\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(Pf,f-Pf)\geq 0
for all
f\in{\cal V}
.
Since
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)
by assumption,
one deduces that

\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u,\|u\|_{H}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u)_{H}<\infty.

So
u\in L_{p}
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
\|u\|_{p}=1
.
Then
u\in C
and
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in{\cal H}
, so

\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in N(u)
by Proposition
2.3
.
Set
f=u+\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in{\cal V}\subset{\cal H}
.
Then the uniqueness of Lemma
2.1
gives
u=Pf
.
Consequently

\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(u,\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u)=\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(Pf,f-Pf)\geq 0
.

‘
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
’.
Let
v\in{\cal V}
.
We shall show that
\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(Pv,v-Pv)\geq 0
.
If
v\in C
, then this is trivial, so we may assume that
v\not\in C
.
Set
u=Pv
and
h=v-u
.
Then
h\in N(u)
by Lemma
2.1
.
Also
h\neq 0
, so Proposition
2.3
implies that

\|u\|_{p}=1
and

\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\in{\cal H}
.
Moreover, there exists a

t\in[0,\infty)
such that
h=t\,\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u
.
Then
t\neq 0
.
Since
u=Pv\in P{\cal V}\subset{\cal V}
by assumption,
one deduces that
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u=\frac{1}{t}(v-u)\in{\cal V}
.
Hence

\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(Pv,v-Pv)=t\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(u,\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u)\geq 0
.
Now it follows from
[
Ouh2
]
Theorem 2.2 2)
\Rightarrow
1) that

S_{t}C\subset C
for all
t>0
.
This obviously implies Statement
(i)
.
∎

3
Application to Hilbert space valued parabolic problems

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be an open set.
Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space.
For all
k,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
let

c_{kl}\colon\Omega\to{\cal L}(H)
be a bounded function
such that
x\mapsto(c_{kl}(x)\,\xi,\eta)_{H}
is
measurable from
\Omega
into
\mathds{C}
for all
\xi,\eta\in H
.
Let
\mu>0
.
We assume that

\mathop{\rm Re}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}(c_{kl}(x)\,\xi_{l},\xi_{k})_{H}\geq\mu\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\xi_{k}\|_{H}^{2}

for all
x\in\Omega
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}\in H
.
Further let
M>0
be such that

\sum_{k=1}^{d}\Big\|\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{kl}(x)\,\xi_{l}\Big\|_{H}^{2}\leq M^{2}\,\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\xi_{k}\|_{H}^{2}

for all
x\in\Omega
and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}\in H
.
For simplicity we consider Neumann boundary conditions.
Define
{\cal V}=H^{1}(\Omega,H)
and
\gothic{a}\colon{\cal V}\times{\cal V}\to\mathds{C}
by

\gothic{a}(u,v)=\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}(c_{kl}(x)\,(\partial_{l}u)(x),(\partial_{k}v)(x))_{H}\,dx.

Then
\gothic{a}
is a continuous elliptic sesquilinear form.
Let
A
be the operator associated with
\gothic{a}
and let
S
be the semigroup
generated by
-A
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and suppose that

\frac{\mu}{M}\geq 2\Big|\frac{p-2}{p}\Big|+\Big|\frac{p-2}{p}\Big|^{2}.

Then
S
extends consistently to a contraction semigroup in
L_{p}(\Omega,H)
.

Note that the condition in Theorem
3.1
is invariant
by taking the dual exponent, that is, if
p\in(1,\infty)

satisfies the condition, then so does
q
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.
If one is satisfied with some small interval,
1<p_{-}<2<p_{+}<\infty

such that
S
is
L_{p}
-contractive for all
p\in[p_{-},p^{+}]
,
then a significantly easier and less technical proof than the following
can be given.

Proof
.

Using duality, without loss of generality we may assume that
p>2
.
We argue as in
[
CM
]
,
[
ELSV
]
and
[
Ege
]
.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.
For all
n\in\mathds{N}
define

v_{n}=(\|u\|_{H}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\wedge n)\,u\quad,\quad w_{n}=(\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\wedge n^{2})\,u\quad\mbox{and}\quad\chi_{n}=\mathds{1}_{[\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}<n^{2}]}.

If follows from Proposition
A.1
(cf.
[
AK
]
Theorems 3.3 and 4.2)
that
v_{n},w_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)

with

\displaystyle\nabla v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\nabla u+\chi_{n}\,\|u\|_{H}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Big(\nabla u+{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{2}}\,(\nabla\|u\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\Big),\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\nabla w_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\nabla u+\chi_{n}\,\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\Big(\nabla u+(p-2)\,(\nabla\|u\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\Big).

(2)

Note that
\chi_{n}\,\|v_{n}\|_{H}=\chi_{n}\,\|u\|_{H}^{p/2}
.
Hence

\displaystyle(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\nabla u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\nabla v_{n},

\displaystyle\chi_{n}\,\|v_{n}\|_{H}^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\,\nabla u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{n}\,\Big(\nabla v_{n}-{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\,(\nabla\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n}\Big),\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\nabla w_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\nabla v_{n}+\chi_{n}\,\,\|v_{n}\|_{H}^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\Big(\nabla v_{n}+{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\,(\nabla\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n}\Big).

Therefore for almost all
x\in\Omega
one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\partial_{l}u,\partial_{k}w_{n})_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\partial_{l}u,n\,\partial_{k}v_{n})_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+\chi_{n}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\|v_{n}\|_{H}^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\,\partial_{l}u,\Big(\partial_{k}v_{n}+{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\,(\partial_{k}\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n}\Big))_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\partial_{l}v_{n},\partial_{k}v_{n})_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt{}+\chi_{n}\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\Big(\partial_{l}v_{n}-{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\,(\partial_{l}\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n}\Big),\Big(\partial_{k}v_{n}+{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\,(\partial_{k}\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n}\Big))_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\partial_{l}v_{n},\partial_{k}v_{n})_{H}+{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\,\chi_{n}\,\mathop{\rm Re}((c_{kl}-c_{lk}^{*})\,\partial_{l}v_{n},(\partial_{k}\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n})_{H}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}-\Big({\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\Big)^{2}\,\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,(\partial_{l}\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n},(\partial_{k}\|v_{n}\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n})_{H}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big(\mu-2M\,{\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}-M\,\Big({\textstyle\frac{p-2}{p}}\Big)^{2}\Big)\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\partial_{k}v_{n}\|_{H}^{2},

where we used that

\sum_{k=1}^{d}|\partial_{k}\|v_{n}\|_{H}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\partial_{k}v_{n}\|_{H}^{2}

and
\|\mathop{\rm sgn}v_{n}\|_{H}\leq 1
by Lemma
A.5
.
By the assumption on
p
we obtain that

\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\partial_{l}u,\partial_{k}w_{n})_{H}\geq 0

almost everywhere.
This is for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Next take the limit
n\to\infty
and use (
2
).
Then

\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\partial_{l}u,\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,(\partial_{k}u+(p-2)\,(\partial_{k}\|u\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u))_{H}\geq 0

(3)

almost everywhere.

We assume from now on that in addition
\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u\in{\cal V}
.
Let
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
We shall show that
\partial_{k}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u)=f_{k}
almost everywhere,
where

f_{k}=\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,\Big(\partial_{k}u+(p-2)\,(\partial_{k}\|u\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u\Big).

Write
r=2\,\frac{p-1}{p-2}\in(2,\infty)
and let
q\in(1,2)
be such that

\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}
.
Since
\|u\|_{H}^{p-1}\in L_{2}(\Omega)
one deduces that

\int_{\Omega}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-2})^{r}=\int_{\Omega}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-1})^{2}<\infty
.
So
\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\in L_{r}(\Omega)
.
If
n\in\mathds{N}
, then

n^{2}\,(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\|\partial_{k}u\|_{H}\leq(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n})\,\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,\|\partial_{k}u\|_{H}

and hence (
2
) gives

\|\partial_{k}w_{n}\|_{H}\leq\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\Big(\|\partial_{k}u\|_{H}+(p-2)\,\Big|\partial_{k}\|u\|_{H}\Big|\Big)

(4)

Note that the right hand side of (
4
)
does not depend on
n
and is an element of
L_{q}(\Omega)
.
Also
\lim\partial_{k}w_{n}=f_{k}
almost everywhere.
Hence
\lim\partial_{k}w_{n}=f_{k}
in
L_{q}(\Omega,H)
.
It is easy to see that
\lim w_{n}=\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u
in
L_{2}(\Omega,H)

since
\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,H)
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{k}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u),\varphi)_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u,\partial_{k}\varphi)_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(w_{n},\partial_{k}\varphi)_{H}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{k}w_{n},\varphi)_{H}=\int_{\Omega}(f_{k},\varphi)_{H}.

So
\partial_{k}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u)=f_{k}
almost everywhere.

Finally (
3
) implies that

\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\mathop{\rm Re}(c_{kl}\,\partial_{l}u,\partial_{k}(\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u))_{H}\geq 0

almost everywhere.
Integrating over
\Omega
gives
\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(u,\|u\|_{H}^{p-2}\,u)\geq 0
.
Now apply Theorem
1.1
.

By the way, with some more work one can show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial_{k}w_{n}=f_{k}

in
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
∎

We comment on related results concerning the extension of
S
to a not
necessarily contractive
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L_{p}
.
Hofmann, Mayboroda and McIntosh
[
HMM
]
showed for
H=\mathds{C}
,
\Omega=\mathds{R}^{d}

and
d\geq 3
that the semigroup
S
can be extended to a
C_{0}
-semigroup
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
if
p\in[\frac{2d}{d+2},\frac{2d}{d-2}]
.
Conversely, for each
p\in(1,\frac{2d}{d+2})\cup(\frac{2d}{d-2},\infty)

they construct an elliptic operator such that the associated semigroup
(S_{t})_{t>0}

cannot be extended consistently to a bounded semigroup
L_{p}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.
The extension results are based on off-diagonal Davies–Gaffney estimates;
cf. also
[
Dav1
]
Theorem 25 and
[
Aus
]
Section 3.1.

Davies had already pointed out in the introduction of
[
Dav2
]

that his proof of
[
Dav1
]
Theorem 25 extends to the vector-valued case.
Moreover, by
[
Dav2
]
Theorem 10 for each

p\in(1,\frac{2d}{d+2})\cup(\frac{2d}{d-2},\infty)
there exists an
elliptic system with
H=\mathds{C}^{d}
,
\Omega=\mathds{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
and with
real symmetric coefficients such that the operator
S_{t}

does not continuously extend to
L_{p}
for any
t>0
.

We shall give a corresponding extension result for our setting,
which we obtain readily from standard estimates and tracing
Auscher’s proof of
[
Aus
]
Proposition 3.2.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
\Omega=\mathds{R}^{d}
or
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
is open and Lipschitz.
Then the semigroup
S
extends to a
C_{0}
-semigroup with growth bound
0

on
L_{p}(\Omega,H)
for all
p\in(\frac{2d}{d+2},\frac{2d}{d-2})

if
d\geq 3
and for all
p\in(1,\infty)
if
d\in\{1,2\}
.

Proof
.

We outline the arguments for
d\geq 3
.
Let
\omega>0
.
Since
\gothic{a}
is elliptic for this choice of
\omega
,
by
[
Tan
]
Lemma 3.6.2 (3.60) there exists a
c>0
such that

\|e^{-\omega t}\,S_{t}u\|_{\cal V}\leq c\,t^{-1/2}\,\|u\|_{2}
for all
u\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)

and
t>0
.
Combining this with the Sobolev embedding
{\cal V}\hookrightarrow L_{2d/(d-2)}
,
we obtain that there exists a
C>0
such that

\|e^{-\omega t}\,S_{t}u\|_{2d/(d-2)}\leq C\,t^{-1/2}\,\|u\|_{2}

for all
u\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)
and
t>0
.
Then by duality

\|e^{-\omega t}\,S_{t}^{*}u\|_{2}\leq C\,t^{-1/2}\,\|u\|_{2d/(d+2)}

for all
u\in L_{2}\cap L_{2d/(d+2)}
and
t>0
.

Next, it follows from inspection of the proof of
[
Aus
]
Proposition 3.2
that the parts (2) and (3) of
[
Aus
]
Proposition 3.2
extend to the vector-valued case and
general open sets
\Omega
, and are applicable to the
C_{0}
-semigroup

(T_{t})_{t>0}
given by
T_{t}=e^{-\omega t}\,S_{t}^{*}
.
For the extension of part (2) one needs
L_{2}
–
L_{2}
off-diagonal
estimates that can be obtained, for example, as in
[
AE
]
Theorem 4.2.
Moreover, the vector-valued version of the Riesz–Thorin theorem follows from

[
GLY
]
Lemma 2.6.
Let
q\in(\frac{2d}{d+2},2)
.
By the extension of
[
Aus
]
Proposition 3.2(2) we obtain that
T
satisfies

L_{q}
–
L_{2}
off-diagonal estimates,
which implies by the extension of
[
Aus
]
Proposition 3.2(3)
that
T
is uniformly bounded in
L_{q}
.
Dualizing again, we obtain the statement for all
p\in(2,\frac{2d}{d-2})
.
By considering the adjoint form, applying the result for
p>2
and
taking the dual we obtain the statement for
p\in(\frac{2d}{d+2},2)
.
∎

.

Appendix A
The derivative of a truncation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be an open set.
Let
H
be a Hilbert space.
The principal aim in this section is to prove the following
chain rule.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
M>0
.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.
Define
v=(\|u\|_{H}^{\alpha}\wedge M)\,u
.
Then
v\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
and

\partial_{k}v=\alpha\,\mathds{1}_{[\|u\|_{H}^{\alpha}<M]}\,\|u\|_{H}^{\alpha}\,\mathop{\rm Re}(\mathop{\rm sgn}u,\partial_{k}u)\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u+(\|u\|_{H}\wedge M)\,\partial_{k}u

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

The proof involves some work.
We use the following approximation by smooth functions.

Lemma A.2
.

The space
C^{\infty}(\Omega,H)\cap H^{1}(\Omega,H)
is dense in
H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.

Proof
.

This follows as in the scalar case in
[
AF
]
Theorem 3.17.
∎

For an approximation argument the next lemma is useful.

Lemma A.3
.

For all
n\in\mathds{N}
let
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.
Let
u,g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\in L_{2}(\Omega,H)
.
Suppose that
\lim u_{n}=u
in
L_{2}(\Omega,H)

and
\lim\partial_{k}u_{n}=g_{k}
in
L_{2}(\Omega,H)
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Then
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
and

\partial_{k}u=g_{k}
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

Proof
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Let
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Then
-\int_{\Omega}u_{n}\,\partial_{k}\varphi=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{k}u_{n})\,\varphi

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then the lemma follows by taking the limit
n\to\infty
.
∎

For the proof of Proposition
A.1
we shall approximate
the function
t\mapsto t^{\alpha}\wedge M
with smooth functions.
The next technical lemma gives sufficient conditions in order to
apply a chain rule.
Note that we do not require that
f^{\prime}
is bounded.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
f\in C^{1}(0,\infty)
.
Suppose that
f
is bounded,
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}f(t)=0
,

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}t\,f^{\prime}(t)=0
and

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}t\,|f^{\prime}(t)|<\infty
.
Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.
Define
v=f(\|u\|_{H})\,u
.
Then
v\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
and

\partial_{k}v=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|u\|_{H}\,f^{\prime}(\|u\|_{H})\,\mathop{\rm Re}(\mathop{\rm sgn}u,\partial_{k}u)_{H}\,\mathop{\rm sgn}u+f(\|u\|_{H})\,\partial_{k}u&\mbox{on }[u\neq 0],\\[5.0pt]
0&\mbox{on }[u=0].\end{array}\right.

(5)

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

Proof
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Define
v_{\varepsilon}=f(\sqrt{\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon})\,u
.
If
u\in C^{1}(\Omega,H)
, then
v_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(\Omega,H)
and

\partial_{k}v_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon}\,f^{\prime}(\sqrt{\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon})\,\frac{\mathop{\rm Re}(u,\partial_{k}u)_{H}}{\sqrt{\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon}}\,u+f(\sqrt{\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon})\,\partial_{k}u

(6)

for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Then by Lemmas
A.2
and
A.3

this extends to all
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
and (
6
) is valid.
Finally choose
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{n}
, take the limit
n\to\infty

and use again Lemma
A.3
.
∎

Now we are able to prove Proposition
A.1
.

Proof of Proposition
A.1
.

For all
n\in\mathds{N}
define
f_{n},f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathds{R}
by

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}\wedge M,

\displaystyle f_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,(t^{\alpha}+\sqrt{M^{2}+n^{-1}}-\sqrt{|t^{\alpha}-M|^{2}+n^{-1}}).

Then
\lim f_{n}(t)=f(t)
for all
t\in(0,\infty)
.
Also
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}f_{n}(t)=0
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then
f_{n}\in C^{1}(0,\infty)
and

f_{n}^{\prime}(t)={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\alpha\,t^{\alpha-1}\Big(1-\frac{t^{\alpha}-M}{\sqrt{(t^{\alpha}-M)^{2}+n^{-1}}}\Big)

for all
t\in(0,\infty)
.
In particular,
f_{n}
is increasing.
Moreover,
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}t\,f_{n}^{\prime}(t)=0
.
In addition,
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}^{\prime}(t)=\alpha\,t^{\alpha-1}

if
t^{\alpha}\leq M
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}^{\prime}(t)=0
if
t^{\alpha}>M
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.
If
t^{\alpha}\leq M
, then

0\leq t\,f_{n}^{\prime}(t)={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\alpha\,t^{\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{M-t^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{(t^{\alpha}-M)^{2}+n^{-1}}}\Big)\leq\alpha\,M.

Alternatively, if
t^{\alpha}>M
, then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t\,f_{n}^{\prime}(t)={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\alpha\,\frac{t^{\alpha}-M+M}{\sqrt{(t^{\alpha}-M)^{2}+n^{-1}}}\Big(\sqrt{(t^{\alpha}-M)^{2}+n^{-1}}-\sqrt{(t^{\alpha}-M)^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\alpha\Big(1+\frac{M}{\sqrt{n^{-1}}}\Big)\sqrt{n^{-1}}\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\alpha\,(1+M).

So

\sup_{n\in\mathds{N}}\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}t\,|f^{\prime}(t)|\leq\alpha\,(M+1).

(7)

If
n\in\mathds{N}
and
t\in(0,\infty)
, then

0\leq f_{n}(t)\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,(t^{\alpha}+M+1-\sqrt{|t^{\alpha}-M|^{2}+n^{-1}})\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,(t^{\alpha}+M+1-|t^{\alpha}-M|)={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+f(t)\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+M.

So
f_{n}
is bounded and even

\sup_{n\in\mathds{N}}\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}|f_{n}(t)|\leq{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+M.

(8)

Hence all conditions of Lemma
A.4
are satisfied for all the
f_{n}
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.
For all
n\in\mathds{N}
define
v_{n}=f_{n}(u)\,u
.
Then
v_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
with derivatives given by (
5
)
and
f
replaced by
f_{n}
.
The Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and the uniform bounds
(
8
) and (
7
)
imply that
\lim v_{n}=v
and
\lim\partial_{k}v_{n}=\partial_{k}v
in

L_{2}(\Omega,H)
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
Then the proposition follows from Lemma
A.3
.
∎

Almost the same arguments show that the norm of an

H^{1}(\Omega,H)
-function is in the Sobolev space.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.
Then
\|u\|_{H}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and

\partial_{k}\|u\|_{H}=\mathop{\rm Re}(\mathop{\rm sgn}u,\partial_{k}u)
for all

k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

Proof
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
For all
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
define
u_{\varepsilon}\colon\Omega\to H
by

u_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\|u\|_{H}^{2}+\varepsilon}
.
Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
If
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega,H)\cap H^{1}(\Omega,H)
,
then
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega,H)
with classical
partial derivative

\partial_{k}u_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\mathop{\rm Re}(u,\partial_{k}u)_{H}}{u_{\varepsilon}}
.
Hence

-\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{k}\varphi=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathop{\rm Re}(u,\partial_{k}u)_{H}}{u_{\varepsilon}}\,\varphi.

(9)

Using approximation and Lemma
A.2
, it follows that
(
9
) is valid for all
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,H)
.
Finally choose
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{n}
and take the limit
n\to\infty
.
∎

Appendix B
Strict convexity of
L_{p}(\Omega,H)

As before let
(\Omega,{\cal B},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space
and
H
a Hilbert space.
In order to make this paper more self-contained, we give a direct
proof of the following theorem.
At the end of this section we give information on more general results.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
u,v\in L_{p}(\Omega,H)
with

\|u\|_{p}=\|v\|_{p}=1
.
If
\|u+v\|_{p}=2
, then
u=v
.

For the proof of Theorem
B.1
we use three lemmas.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\xi,\eta\in H
and suppose that
\|\xi+\eta\|_{H}=\|\xi\|_{H}+\|\eta\|_{H}
.
If
\eta\neq 0
, then there is a
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
such that
\xi=\lambda\,\eta
.

Proof
.

The equality implies that
\mathop{\rm Re}(\xi,\eta)_{H}=\|\xi\|_{H}\,\|\eta\|_{H}
.
This gives equality in the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
Hence there is a
\lambda\in\mathds{C}
such that
\xi=\lambda\,\eta
.
Using again the equality, one deduces that
|1+\lambda|=|\lambda|+1

and therefore
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
.
∎

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and suppose that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
a,b\in[0,\infty)
.
Then
a\,b\leq\frac{1}{p}\,a^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\,b^{q}

and the equality holds if and only if
a^{p}=b^{q}
.

Proof
.

This follows from the concavity of the logarithm.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

Let
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
and
g\in L_{q}(\Omega)
with
f\neq 0
and
g\neq 0
.
Suppose that
\int_{\Omega}|f|\,|g|=\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\,\|g\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}
.
Then there exists a
\lambda>0
such that

|f|^{p}=\lambda\,|g|^{q}
almost everywhere.

Proof
.

We may assume that
\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}=1=\|g\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}
.
Then Lemma
B.3
gives

1=\int_{\Omega}|f|\,|g|\leq\int_{\Omega}{\textstyle\frac{1}{p}}\,|f|^{p}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{q}}\,|g|^{q}={\textstyle\frac{1}{p}}\,\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{q}}\,\|g\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}=1.

Hence
|f|\,|g|={\textstyle\frac{1}{p}}\,|f|^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\,|g|^{q}
almost everywhere
and the lemma follows from Lemma
B.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
B.1
.

Using the triangle inequality on
H
, twice the Hölder inequality on
L_{p}(\Omega)

and the assumption
\|u+v\|_{p}=\|u\|_{p}+\|v\|_{p}
one obtains

\displaystyle\|u+v\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|u+v\|_{H}\,\|u+v\|_{H}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}\,\|u+v\|_{H}^{p-1}+\int_{\Omega}\|v\|_{H}\,\|u+v\|_{H}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega}\|u\|_{H}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\|u+v\|_{H}^{(p-1)q}\Big)^{1/q}+\Big(\int_{\Omega}\|v\|_{H}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\|u+v\|_{H}^{(p-1)q}\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\|u\|_{p}+\|v\|_{p})\,\|u+v\|_{p}^{p/q}=\|u+v\|_{p}^{\frac{p}{q}+1}=\|u+v\|_{p}^{p}.

Hence all three inequalities are equalities.
The first gives that there is a null-set
N_{1}\subset\Omega
such that

\|u+v\|_{H}(x)=\|u\|_{H}(x)+\|v\|_{H}(x)
for all

x\in\Omega\setminus N_{1}
such that
\|u+v\|_{H}(x)\neq 0
.
Recall that
\|u\|_{p}=1
, so
\|u\|_{H}\neq 0\in L_{p}(\Omega)
.
Similarly
\|u+v\|_{H}\neq 0\in L_{p}(\Omega)
and therefore

\|u+v\|_{H}^{p-1}\neq 0\in L_{q}(\Omega)
.
Hence the equality in the first Hölder inequality together with
Lemma
B.4
gives that there are
\alpha>0

and a null-set
N_{2}\subset\Omega
such that

\|u\|_{H}^{p}(x)=\alpha\,\|u+v\|_{H}^{(p-1)q}(x)
for all
x\in\Omega\setminus N_{2}
.
Similarly there are
\beta>0
and a null-set
N_{3}\subset\Omega
such that

\|v\|_{H}^{p}(x)=\beta\,\|u+v\|_{H}^{(p-1)q}(x)

for all
x\in\Omega\setminus N_{3}
.
Hence
\|u\|_{H}^{p}=\gamma\,\|v\|_{H}^{p}
on
\Omega\setminus(N_{2}\cup N_{3})
, where

\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}
.
Since
\|u\|_{p}=\|v\|_{p}=1
, one deduces that
\gamma=1
.

Now let
x\in\Omega\setminus(N_{1}\cup N_{2}\cup N_{3})
.
If
\|u+v\|_{H}(x)=0
, then

\|u\|_{H}^{p}(x)=\alpha\,\|u+v\|_{H}^{(p-1)q}(x)=0
and
u(x)=0
.
Similarly
v(x)=0
and therefore
u(x)=v(x)
.
Alternatively, if
\|u+v\|_{H}(x)\neq 0
, then
v(x)\neq 0

since
x\not\in N_{3}
.
Moreover,
\|u(x)+v(x)\|_{H}=\|u(x)\|_{H}+\|v(x)\|_{H}

and Lemma
B.2
implies that there is a
\lambda\in[0,\infty)

such that
u(x)=\lambda\,v(x)
.
But
\|u(x)\|_{H}^{p}=\|v(x)\|_{H}^{p}
and consequently
u(x)=v(x)
.
Therefore
u=v
almost everywhere.
∎

With a small modification one can prove that
L_{p}(\Omega,E)
is strictly convex
if
E
is strictly convex and
p\in(1,\infty)
.
In fact, a stronger result than Theorem
B.1
is known.
The space
L_{p}(\Omega,E)
is uniformly convex
if
E
is uniformly convex and
p\in(1,\infty)
.
See
[
DHM
]
and the references therein.

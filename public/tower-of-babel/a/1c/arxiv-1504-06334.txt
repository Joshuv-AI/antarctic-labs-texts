The Bellman function of the dyadic maximal operator in connection with the Dyadic Carleson Imbedding Theorem
Eleftherios N. Nikolidakis

Abstract
We provide an alternative proof and expression of the Bellman function of the dyadic maximal operator in connection with the Dyadic Carleson Imbedding Theorem, which appears in [ 10 ] . We also evaluate the Bellman function of four variables of the dyadic maximal operator



1
Introduction

The dyadic maximal operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a useful tool in analysis and is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|S|}\int_{S}|\phi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u:x\in S,\ S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \text{is a dyadic cube}\right\},

(1.1)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the dyadic cubes are those formed by the grids
2^{-N}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for
N=0,1,2,\ldots
.

It is well known that it satisfies the following weak type (1,1) inequality

\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi(x)>\lambda\right\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\left\{\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi>\lambda\right\}}|\phi(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u,

(1.2)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and every
\lambda>0
,
from which it is easy to get the following
L^{p}
-inequality

\|\mathcal{M}_{d}\phi\|_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|\phi\|_{p},

(1.3)

for every
p>1
, and every
\phi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It is easy to see that the weak type inequality (
1.2
) is the best possible.

It has also been proved that (
1.3
) is best possible (see
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
for general martingales and
[
15
]
for dyadic ones).
An approach for studying the behaviour of this maximal operator in more depth is the introduction of the so-called Bellman functions which play the role of generalized norms of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Such functions related to the
L^{p}
-inequality (
1.3
) have been precisely identified in
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
14
]
. For the description of the Bellman functions of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
, we use the notation
\Av_{E}(\psi)=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\psi
, whenever
E
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive measure and
\psi
is a real valued measurable function defined on
E
. We fix a dyadic cube
Q
and define the localized maximal operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\phi
as in (
1.1
) but with the dyadic cubes
S
being assumed to be contained in
Q
. Then for every
p>1
we let

B_{p}(f,F)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{d}\phi)^{p}:\Av_{Q}(\phi)=f,\ \Av_{Q}(\phi^{p})=F\right\},

(1.4)

where
\phi
is nonnegative in
L^{p}(Q)
and the variables
f,F
satisfying
0<f^{p}\leq F
. By a scaling argument it is easy to see that (
1.4
) is independent of the choice of
Q
(so we may choose

Q
to be the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
).
In
[
10
]
, the function (
1.4
) has been precisely identified for the first time. The proof has been given in a much more general setting of tree-like structures on probability spaces.

More precisely we consider a non-atomic probability space
(X,\mu)
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a family of measurable subsets of
X
, that has a tree-like structure similar to the one in the dyadic case (the exact definition will be given in Section
2
).
Then we define the dyadic maximal operator associated to
\mathcal{T}
, by

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi(x)=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}|\phi|\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu:x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

(1.5)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
,
x\in X
.

This operator is related to the theory of martingales and satisfies essentially the same inequalities as
\mathcal{M}_{d}
does. Now we define the corresponding Bellman function of four variables of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}
, by

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,k)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}\left[\max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)\right]^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu:\phi\geq 0,\int_{X}\phi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\right.\\
\left.\int_{X}\phi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F,\ K\subseteq X\ \text{measurable with}\ \mu(K)=k\right\},

(1.6)

the variables
f,F,L,k
satisfying
0<f^{p}\leq F
,
L\geq f
,
k\in(0,1]
.
The exact evaluation of (
1.6
) is given in
[
10
]
, for the cases where
k=1
or
L=f
. In the first case the author (in
[
10
]
) precisely identifies the function
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by evaluating it in a first stage for the case where
L=f
. That is he precisely identifies
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
(in fact
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)=F\omega_{p}(\frac{f^{p}}{F})^{p}
, where
\omega_{p}:[0,1]\to[1,\frac{p}{p-1}]
is the inverse function
H^{-1}_{p}
, of
H_{p}(z)=-(p-1)z^{p}+pz^{p-1}
). Then using several calculus argument he provides the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
for every
L\geq f
. Now in
[
14
]
the authors give a direct proof of the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,1)
by using alternative methods. In fact they prove a sharp symmetrization principle that holds for the dyadic maximal operator, which is stated as Theorem 2.1 (see Section
2
).

In the second case, where
L=f
, the author (in
[
10
]
) uses the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,1)
and provides a proof of the more general
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,f,k)
,
k\in(0,1]
. We write from now on this function as
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)
. This function is related to the Dyadic Carleson Imbedding Theorem and in fact, as is proved in
[
10
]
, the following is true

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)=\sup\left\{\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}}\lambda_{I}(Av_{I}(\phi))^{p},\phi\geq 0,\ \int_{X}\phi\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=f,\ \int_{X}\phi^{p}\,\mathrm{\;}d\mu=F,\right.\\
\left.\text{and the nonegative }\ \lambda_{I}\text{'s satisfy}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{T}:J\subseteq I}\lambda_{J}\leq\mu(I)\text{ for every }I\in\mathcal{T}\right.\\
\left.\text{ and }\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}}\lambda_{I}=k\right\}.

(1.7)

As an immediate step for the evaluation of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)
in
[
10
]
, it is provided an alternative expression for this function. This is stated in the following theorem

Theorem 1.1
.

The following is true

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)=\sup\left\{\left(F-\frac{(f-B)^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)\omega_{p}\left(\frac{B^{p}}{k^{p-1}\left(F-\frac{(f-B)^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)}\right)\ \ :\right.\\
\left.\text{for all }B\in[0,f]\text{ such that }h_{k}(B)\leq F\right\},

(1.8)

where
h_{k}
is defined by
h_{k}(B)=\frac{(f-B)^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}+\frac{B^{p}}{k^{p-1}}
.

After proving the above theorem, the author in
[
10
]
, precisely evaluated
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)
, by using a chain of calculus arguments.
In Section
3
we provide an alternative proof of Theorem 1.1. Now in view of the symmetrization principle that appears in
[
14
]
(see Theorem 2.1 below) we conclude that

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)=\sup\left\{\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g\right)^{p}dt\ \ :\text{ where }g:(0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}\text{ is}\right.\\
\left.\text{ non-increasing, }\int_{0}^{1}g=f,\;\int_{0}^{1}g^{p}=F\right\}.

(1.9)

In Section
4
we prove the following

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a function
g=g_{k}:(0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
non-increasing and continuous, satisfying
\int_{0}^{1}g=f
and
\int_{0}^{1}g^{p}=F
, for which the supremum in (
1.9
) is attained.

Moreover we explicitly construct the function
g_{k}
, mentioned above.
At last in Section 5 we give a proof of the evaluation of the Bellman function of four variables (1.6) for the dyadic maximal operator in terms of
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)
.

We note also that further study of the dyadic maximal operator can be seen in

For such approaches, which enables us to study and solve problems as the one which is described in this article one can see

2
Preliminaries

Let
(X,\mu)
be a nonatomic probability space. We give the following

Definition 2.1
.

A set
\mathcal{T}
of measurable subsets of
X
will be called a tree if the following conditions are satisfied:

i)

X\in\mathcal{T}
and for every
I\in\mathcal{T}
we have that
\mu(I)>0
.

ii)

For every
I\in\mathcal{T}
there corresponds a finite or countable subset
C(I)\subseteq\mathcal{T}
containing at least two elements such that

a)

the elements of
C(I)
are pairwise disjoint subsets of
I
.

b)

I=\cup\,C(I)
.

iii)

\mathcal{T}=\cup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 0\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{(m)}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{(0)}=\left\{X\right\}
and
\mathcal{T}_{(m+1)}=\cup_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{(m)}}C(I)
.

iv)

We have
\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\sup_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{(m)}}\mu(I)\right)=0

v)

The tree
\mathcal{T}
differentiates
L^{1}(X,\mu)
. That is for every
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
it is true that

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\\
\mu(I)\to 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\phi\;d\mu=\phi(x),

for
\mu-
almost every
x\in X
.

Then we define the dyadic maximal operator corresponding to
\mathcal{T}
by

M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi(x)=sup\left\{\frac{1}{\mu(I)}\int_{I}\mid\phi\mid\;d\mu\;:x\in I\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

(2.1)

for every
\phi\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
,
x\in X
.

We give the following which appears in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
I\in\mathcal{T}
and every
\alpha
such that
0<a<1
there exists a subfamily
\mathcal{F}(I)\subseteq\mathcal{T}
consisting of pairwise disjoint subsets of
I
such that

\mu\!\left(\underset{J\in\mathcal{F}(I)}{\bigcup}J\right)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{F}(I)}\mu(J)=(1-\alpha)\mu(I).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi:(X,\mu)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then
\phi^{*}:(0,1]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined as the unique non-increasing, left continuous and equimeasurable to
\phi
function on
(0,1]
.

There are several formulas that express
\phi^{*}
, in terms of
\phi
.
One of them is as follows:

\phi^{*}(t)=\inf\left(\left\{y>0:\mu\left(\left\{x\in X:\phi(x)>y\right\}\right)<t\right\}\right),

for every
t\in(0,1]
. An equivalent formulation of the non increasing rearrangement can be given by

\phi^{*}(t)=\sup_{e\subseteq X,\\
\mu(e)\geq t}\left[\inf_{x\in e}\phi(x)\right],

for any
t\in(0,1]
.

Let

Let

\int_{0}^{1}G\left(g_{1}(t)\right)\;dt\leq\int_{0}^{1}G\left(g_{2}(t)\right)\;dt

holds almost everywhere on

(2.3)

3
The Bellman function
B_{p}(f,F,k)

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\phi:(X,\mu)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
integrable, the following inequality is true

(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}(t)\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,\ \text{for every}\ t\in(0,1].

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
we have for any
G:[0,+\infty)\longrightarrow[0,+\infty)
non-decreasing

\int_{X}G(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int_{0}^{1}G\!\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)\mathrm{d}t.

(3.1)

Since
G
is non-decreasing we have that

\left[G(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)\right]^{\star}(t)=G\left[(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}\right](t),\ \text{for almost every}\ t\in(0,1].

Thus
\int_{0}^{1}G\left[(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}\right](t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\left[G(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)\right]^{\star}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{X}G(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)\,d\mu\leq\\
\int_{0}^{1}G\!\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,du\right)dt
, by (
3.1
).
Thus by Lemma
2.2
we immediately conclude that

(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}(t)\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

(3.2)

almost everywhere on
(0,1]
. Since now
(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}
is left continuous, we conclude that (
3.2
) should hold everywhere on
(0,1]
, and in this way we derive the proof of our Lemma.
∎

We also present a simpler proof of Lemma
3.1
.

2nd proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Suppose that we are given
\phi:(X,\mu)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
integrable and
t\in(0,1]
fixed. We set
A=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u
. Then obviously
A\geq\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=f
, by the fact that
\phi^{\star}
is non-increasing on
(0,1]
. We consider the set
E=\{M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi>A\}\subseteq X
.

Then by the weak type inequality (
1.2
) for
M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi
, we have that

\mu(E)<\frac{1}{A}\int_{E}|\phi|\,\mathrm{d}\mu\Rightarrow\\
A=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu<\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\int_{E}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\frac{1}{\mu(E)}\int_{0}^{\mu(E)}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,

(3.3)

where the last inequality in (
3.3
) holds due to the definition of
\phi^{\star}
. Since
\phi^{\star}
is non-increasing we must have from (
3.3
), that
\mu(E)<t
. But
\mu(E)=\left|\left\{(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}(t)>A\right\}\right|
since
(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)
and
(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}
are equimeasurable.
Since
(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}
is non-increasing and because of the fact that
\mu(E)<t
we conclude that
\{(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}>A\}=(0,\gamma)
for some
\gamma<t
. Thus
t\notin\{(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}>A\}\Rightarrow(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}(t)\leq A=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u
,
which is the desired result.
∎

We are now in position to state and prove

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\phi:(X,\mu)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be such that
\int_{X}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu=f
and
\int_{X}\phi^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=F
where
0<f^{p}\leq F
. Suppose also that we are given a measurable subset
K
of
X
such that
\mu(K)=k
, where
k
is fixed such that
k\in(0,1]
. Then the following inequality is true:

\int_{K}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}(u)]^{p}\,\mathrm{d}u\cdot\omega_{p}\!\!\left(\frac{\left(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{p}}{k^{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}(u)]^{p}\,\mathrm{d}u}\right)^{p}.

Proof.

We obviously have that

\int_{K}(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\,d\mu\leq\int_{0}^{k}[(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star}]^{p}(t)\,dt.

(3.4)

We evaluate the right-hand side of (
3.4
). We have:

\int_{0}^{k}\left[(M_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{\star})\right]^{p}dt\leq\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t

(3.5)

by using Lemma
3.1
. Additionally

\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\lambda=0}^{+\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\left|\left\{t\in(0,k]:\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}\geq\lambda\right\}\right|\mathrm{d}\lambda=\\
\int_{\lambda=0}^{f_{k}}+\int_{\lambda=f_{k}}^{+\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\left|\left\{t\in(0,k]:\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}\geq\lambda\right\}\right|\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(3.6)

where the first equation is justified by a use of Fubini’s theorem and
f_{k}
is defined by
f_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u>f=\int_{0}^{1}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u
.

The first integral in (
3.6
) is obviously equal to
k(f_{k})^{p}=\frac{1}{k^{p-1}}\bigl(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}\bigr)^{p}
. We suppose now that
\lambda>f_{k}
is fixed. Then there exists
\alpha(\lambda)\in(0,k]
such that
\frac{1}{\alpha(\lambda)}\int_{0}^{\alpha(\lambda)}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\lambda
. Note that, without loss of generality, we assume that
\phi^{\star}(0^{+})=+\infty
(the case
\phi^{\star}(0^{+})<+\infty
can be handled similarly). As a consequence
\left\{t\in(0,k]:\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\geq\lambda\right\}=(0,\alpha(\lambda)]
, thus

\left|\left\{t\in(0,k]:\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\geq\lambda\right\}\right|=\alpha(\lambda).

So the second integral in (
3.6
) equals

\int_{\lambda=f_{k}}^{+\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\alpha(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{\lambda=f_{k}}^{+\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\frac{1}{\lambda}\biggl(\int_{0}^{\alpha(\lambda)}\phi^{\star}(u)\biggr)\mathrm{d}\lambda,

by the definition of
\alpha(\lambda)
.
The last now integral, equals

\int_{\lambda=f_{k}}^{+\infty}p\lambda^{p-2}\biggl(\int_{\left\{t\in(0,k]:\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}\geq\lambda\right\}}\!\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\biggr)\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{t=0}^{k}\frac{p}{p-1}\phi^{\star}(t)\bigl[\lambda^{p-1}\bigr]_{f_{k}}^{\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}}\!\mathrm{d}t,

(3.7)

by a use of Fubini’s theorem.
As a consequence (
3.6
) gives

\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t=-\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{1}{k^{p-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}\right)^{p}+\\
\frac{p}{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}(t)\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}\right)^{p-1}\mathrm{d}t.

(3.8)

Then by Hölder’s inequality, applied in the second integral on the right side of (
3.8
), we have that

\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t\leq-\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{1}{k^{p-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}\right)^{p}+\\
\frac{p}{p-1}\left(\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}]^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t\right]^{\frac{(p-1)}{p}}.

(3.9)

We set now

J(k)=\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{\star}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t,\quad A(k)=\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}]^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad B(k)=\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}.

Then we conclude by (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle J(k)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{1}{k^{p-1}}[B(k)]^{p}+\frac{p}{p-1}[A(k)]^{\frac{1}{p}}[J(k)]^{\frac{(p-1)}{p}}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{J(k)}{A(k)}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{p-1}\left(\frac{[B(k)]^{p}}{k^{p-1}A(k)}\right)+\frac{p}{p-1}\left[\frac{J(k)}{A(k)}\right]^{\frac{(p-1)}{p}}.

(3.10)

We set now in (
3
)
\Lambda(k)=\left[\frac{J(k)}{A(k)}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}
, thus we get

\displaystyle\Lambda(k)^{p}\leq-\frac{1}{p-1}\left(\frac{[B(k)]^{p}}{k^{p-1}[A(k)]}\right)+\frac{p}{p-1}\Lambda(k)^{p-1}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle p[\Lambda(k)]^{p-1}-(p-1)[\Lambda(k)]^{p}\geq\frac{\bigl(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}\bigr)^{p}}{k^{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}]^{p}}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle H_{p}(\Lambda(k))\geq\frac{\bigl(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}\bigr)^{p}}{k^{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}]^{p}}\implies\Lambda(k)\leq\omega_{p}\!\left(\frac{\bigl(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}\bigr)^{p}}{k^{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}]^{p}}\right)\Rightarrow

\displaystyle J(k)\leq\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}]^{p}\,\omega_{p}\!\left(\frac{\bigl(\int_{0}^{k}\phi^{\star}\bigr)^{p}}{k^{p-1}\int_{0}^{k}[\phi^{\star}]^{p}}\right)^{p}.

(3.11)

At last by (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3
) we derive the proof of our Lemma.
∎

We fix now

, and

measurable such that

. Then if

,

(3.12)

Note now that the
A
,

, because of Hölder’s inequality for

on the interval

From all the above we conclude the following

Corollary 3.1

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)\leq\sup\left\{A\,\omega_{p}\!\left(\frac{B^{p}}{k^{p-1}A}\right)^{p}:A,\,B\ \text{satisfy i), ii) and iii) above}\right\}.

For the next Lemma we fix

4
Construction of the function
g_{k}

We now proceed to prove Theorem 1.2.

Proof.

As it has been proved in Corrolary 3.2, it is true that:

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)=\sup\left\{\mathcal{R}_{k}(B):0\leq B\leq f,\ \text{and}\ h_{k}(B)\leq F\right\}

where
\mathcal{R}_{k}(B)
,
h_{k}(B)
are defined as in Section
2
.
By Lemma
2.3
, we see that the value
B_{0}
satisfies the following equation

\omega_{p}(Z_{0})=\frac{B_{0}}{k}\frac{1-k}{f-B_{0}},

(4.1)

where
Z_{0}
is given by

Z_{0}=\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}\left(F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)}.

We search for a function of the form

g_{k}(t)=\begin{cases}A_{1}\,t^{-1+\frac{1}{a}},&t\in(0,k]\\
c,&t\in(k,1]\end{cases}

(4.2)

for some constants
a,c,A_{1}
, which satisfies the properties

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)=\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g_{k}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t,

(4.3)

and

\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}=f,\quad\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}^{p}=F

(4.4)

Concerning the first equation in
4.4
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}g_{k}=f&\Leftrightarrow\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}+\int_{k}^{1}g_{k}=f\Leftrightarrow\\
&\Leftrightarrow\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}+c(1-k)=f.\end{split}

(4.5)

We set
c=\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}
, in order to ensure that

\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}=B_{0}.

(4.6)

Note that (
4.6
) is (in view of (
4.2
)) equivalent to

\int_{0}^{k}A_{1}\,t^{-1+\frac{1}{a}}\mathrm{d}t=B_{0}\Leftrightarrow A_{1}=\frac{B_{0}k^{-1/a}}{a},

(4.7)

so that we found
A_{1}
, in terms of
a
. We search now for a value of
a
such that the second equation in (
4.4
) is true. Thus we should have

\displaystyle A_{1}^{p}\int_{0}^{k}t^{-p+\frac{p}{a}}\mathrm{d}t=F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{B_{0}^{p}\,k^{-p/a}}{a^{p}}\frac{1}{1+\frac{p}{a}-p}k^{1-p+p/a}=F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}}\frac{1}{p\,a^{p-1}-(p{-}1)a^{p}}=F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}H_{p}(a)}=F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\Leftrightarrow H_{p}(a)=\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}\left(F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle H_{p}(a)=Z_{0}\Leftrightarrow a=\omega_{p}(Z_{0})\in\left[1,\frac{p}{p-1}\right]

(4.8)

∎

As a consequence, if
A_{1},a
are given by (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) respectively, equations (
4.4
) are true.
Note now that for every
t\in(0,k]

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g_{k}=a\,g_{k}(t),\quad\forall t\in(0,k].

Thus

\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g_{k}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}t=a^{p}\int_{0}^{k}g_{k}^{p}=\\
=\left(F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)\omega_{p}\!\left(\frac{B_{0}^{p}}{k^{p-1}\left(F-\frac{(f-B_{0})^{p}}{(1-k)^{p-1}}\right)}\right)^{p}.

(4.9)

By Theorem
1.1
and Lemma 2.3, the right side of (

) equals
B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)
. We need only to prove that

is continuous on

\frac{f-B_{0}}{1-k}=A_{1}k^{-1+\frac{1}{a}}\Leftrightarrow A_{1}k^{-1+\frac{1}{a}}=\left(\frac{B_{0}}{k}\frac{1-k}{f-B_{0}}\right)^{-1}\frac{B_{0}}{k}

5
The Bellman function of four variables of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}

In this section we evaluate the more general Bellman function of the dyadic maximal operator (
1.6
). More precisely we prove the following

Theorem 5.1
.

The following identity holds

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,k)=\sup\left\{B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k_{1})+L^{p}(k-k_{1}):k_{1}\in(0,k]\right\},

(5.1)

Proof.

Let
k\in(0,1]
and
L\geq f
and consider also
\phi
as in (
1.6
). We choose
w\geq L
such that

\mu(U_{1})\leq k\leq\mu(U_{2})

where
U_{1}=\left\{max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)>w\right\}
and

U_{2}=\left\{max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)\geq w\right\}
. We next consider a measurable set
S
satisfying
U_{1}\subseteq S\subseteq U_{2}
and

\mu(S)=k
.

Let now
K
be an arbitrary measurable subset of
X
with
\mu(K)=k
.

We mention the following which is a well known fact from measure theory:

Let
g:(X,\mu)\rightarrow\mathbb{R^{+}}
be an integrable function,
w
non-negative and
D
be a measurable set such that
\left\{g>w\right\}\subseteq D\subseteq\left\{g\geq w\right\}
. Then for every
K\subseteq X
, with
\mu(K)=\mu(D)
the inequality

\int_{K}g\mathrm{\;}d\mu\leq\int_{D}g\mathrm{\;}d\mu
is true.

By using the above fact we conclude that

\int_{K}max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu\leq\int_{S}max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu

(5.2)

We consider two cases

a) If
w>L
then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi>L
on
S
. Thus (
5.2
) gives

\int_{K}max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu\leq\int_{S}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu\leq B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k)

(5.3)

b) If
w=L
then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi\leq L
on
S-U_{1}
, so that (
5.2
) gives

\int_{K}max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu\leq\int_{U_{1}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu+(k-k_{1})L^{p}

(5.4)

where we have set
k_{1}=\mu(U_{1})\leq k
. Thus we have proved that

B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,L,k)\leq\sup\left\{B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k_{1})+L^{p}(k-k_{1}):k_{1}\in(0,k]\right\}

We proceed to the reverse inequality:

Let
k_{1}\in(0,k]
and for any
\delta\in(0,1)
we choose a function
\phi_{\delta}
satisfying the conditions in (
1.6
), and a measurable subset
K_{\delta}
of
X
with
\mu(K_{\delta})=k_{1}
for which

\int_{K_{\delta}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu\geq\delta B_{p}^{\mathcal{T}}(f,F,k_{1}).

(5.5)

We now choose a measurable subset
E
of
X-K_{\delta}
, such that

\mu(E)=k-k_{1}
. We the define
K=K_{\delta}\cup E
. Then
\mu(K)=k
and

\int_{K}max(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi,L)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu\geq\int_{K_{\delta}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{T}}\phi)^{p}\mathrm{\;}d\mu+(k-k_{1})L^{p}

(5.6)

so by using (
5.5
) and letting also
\delta\rightarrow 1^{-}
we conclude the opposite inequality, and thus the proof of Theorem 5.1.

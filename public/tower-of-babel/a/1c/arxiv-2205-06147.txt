On matrix sets invariant under conjugation and taking linear combinations of commuting elements
O. G. Styrt

Abstract




Subsets of a matrix algebra over a field that are invariant under conjugation and contain the linear span of each two of their commuting elements are
described. They obviously include the subsets of diagonalizable and nilpotent matrices. In the paper, the case of an algebraically closed field is considered.
The problem is easily reduced to description of subsets of diagonalizable matrices and subsets of nilpotent matrices with the given properties. So, among
diagonalizable matrices, there are four of such subsets. As for the nilpotent case, it is proved that the subset should be defined by the condition that the sizes
of all Jordan cells of the matrix belong to a certain number set. An explicit criterion is obtained in terms of this set.

Key words:
matrix algebra, diagonalizable matrix, nilpotent matrix.

Introduction

In researching abstract theory of Lie groups and algebras — for example, in
[
1
,
2
]
— different forms of
Jordan decomposition
play quite important
role. So, semisimple Lie algebras over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero involve two special types of elements:
semisimple
and

nilpotent
ones — and the uniquely defined
additive Jordan decomposition
of an arbitrary element into the sum of two commuting elements of these
types. Each of the subsets of all semisimple and of all nilpotent elements is preserved under the inner automorphisms and contains the linear span of each two of its
commuting elements. The proof of this fact is based on elementary concepts of linear algebra and does not use the
Lie
structure of the
associative

operator algebra (it is worth noting that, similarly, two given types of elements of semisimple Lie algebras are defined through the analogical properties of the
corresponding adjoint operators). At the same time, these two subsets fundamentally differ intersecting only by the zero element. It led to the problem of describing
all subsets with the same properties of invariance. In the paper, it is solved for the operator algebra (also involving two above-mentioned types of elements) that
gives perspectives for considering semisimple Lie algebras. Presumably, the case of a simple Lie algebra of type
A_{r}
can be reduced to the result obtained by
concerning the (not needed in this paper) Lie structure of the operator algebra.

So, let
\mathbb{F}
be a field,
n
a positive integer, and
A
the algebra
\End(\mathbb{F}^{n})
(naturally identified with
M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
). The paper is devoted to describing subsets

M\subset A
such that

\displaystyle 0\in M

;

(1.1)

\displaystyle\,\forall\,g\in A^{*}\quad

\displaystyle gMg^{-1}=M

;

(1.2)

\displaystyle\,\forall\,X,Y\in M\quad

\displaystyle\bigl([X,Y]=0\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bigl(\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle\subset M\bigr).

(1.3)

The family
\mathcal{M}
of all these sets is obviously closed under intersection. Besides, it includes the subset
A_{s}
of all diagonalizable operators and the
subset
A_{n}
of all nilpotent operators.

An arbitrary subset of the algebra
A
all of whose elements pairwise commute will be called
commutative
.

Remark.
The condition (
1.3
) is equivalent to the fact that the intersection of
M
with any commutative subspace in
A
is a subspace. Also,
if it holds, then we have
\mathbb{F}M\subset M
(it suffices to consider equal
X
and
Y
) and, thus, the condition (
1.1
) becomes equivalent to
non-emptiness of
M
.

Further, let us suppose that
\mathbb{F}=\overline{\mathbb{F}}
(consequently,
|\mathbb{F}|=\infty
). In the algebra
A
, denote the subspace of all matrices with trace
0
by
A_{0}
and the semisimple and nilpotent
parts of an arbitrary element
X
by
X_{s}
and
X_{n}
respectively.

Lemma 1.1
.

If
M\in\mathcal{M}
, then, for each
X\in M
, we have
X_{s},X_{n}\in M
.

\square\quad
Assume that
X
is a Jordan matrix (it is true up to conjugation). Then
(X_{s})_{ij}=0
once
j\neq i
and
(X_{n})_{ij}=0
once
j-i\neq 1
.
Hence, for an arbitrary element
a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}\setminus\{1\}
and the diagonal matrix
g\in A^{*}
,
g_{kk}:=a^{-k}
, we have
gX_{s}g^{-1}=X_{s}
and
gX_{n}g^{-1}=aX_{n}

implying
Y:=X_{s}+aX_{n}=gXg^{-1}\in M
. The subspace
\left\langle X,Y\right\rangle=\left\langle X_{s},X_{n}\right\rangle\subset A
is commutative and, thus, lies in
M
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}_{s}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{n}
) be the family of all
M\in\mathcal{M}
contained in
A_{s}
(resp. in
A_{n}
).

Theorem 1.1
.

One has mutually inverse bijections

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{s}\times\mathcal{M}_{n},

\displaystyle M\to(M\cap A_{s},M\cap A_{n});

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{s}\times\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathcal{M},

\displaystyle(M_{s},M_{n})\to\{X\in A\colon X_{s}\in M_{s},\,X_{n}\in M_{n}\}.

\square\quad
In the algebra
A
, the following holds: if elements
X
and
Y
commute, then the subset
\{X,X_{s,n},Y,Y_{s,n}\}
is commutative, any

a,b\in\mathbb{F}
satisfying
(aX+bY)_{s,n}=aX_{s,n}+bY_{s,n}
. It remains to apply Lemma
1.1
.
∎

Let
M
be an arbitrary subset of the family
\mathcal{M}_{s}
. Its intersection
L
with the commutative subspace
A_{d}\subset A
of all diagonal matrices is
a subspace, and, also, by (
1.2
),

M=\bigl\{gXg^{-1}\colon X\in L,\,g\in A^{*}\bigr\}.

(1.4)

Hence, the subspace
L\subset A_{d}
is invariant under permutations of diagonal elements and, therefore, coincides with one of the subspaces

0,\,\mathbb{F}E,\,A_{d},\,A_{d}\cap A_{0}
. Applying (
1.4
) again, we obtain that

is one of the subsets
\{0\},\,\mathbb{F}E,\,A_{s},\,A_{s}\cap A_{0}
. Conversely,
each of these subsets obviously belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{s}
.

Further, let us describe subsets of the family
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

The conjugacy class of each nilpotent matrix is uniquely defined by the non-ordered set of the cell sizes of its Jordan form (with considering
multiplicities). Therefore, any subset of the family
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is defined among

by the condition that this set belongs to a certain family of sets.
Moreover, we will further show that each subset of the family
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is the set
M(Q)
(
Q\subset\{2,\ldots,n\}
) of all nilpotent matrices, in whose Jordan
forms, all cells have sizes from
Q\cup\{1\}
. Thus, the problem of describing the family
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is reduced to that of searching all subsets

Q\subset\{2,\ldots,n\}
such that the set
M:=M(Q)
satisfies (
1.3
) (the conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) obviously hold).

Once
Q=\varnothing
, we have
M(Q)=\{0\}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. As for the case of non-empty

, the following criterion of the inclusion

will be obtained in
the article.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
Q\neq\varnothing
. Then
M(Q)\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
if and only if there exists a number

m_{0}\in\Bigl\{2,\ldots,\bigl[\frac{n}{2}\bigr]+1\Bigr\}
equal either to
\bigl[\frac{n}{2}\bigr]+1
or to
(\cha\mathbb{F})^{k}

(
k\in\mathbb{N}
)
and such that the set
Q
contains all numbers

2,\ldots,m_{0}
all its rest elements being at most
2m_{0}
and at least
n-m_{0}+2
, i. e.

\displaystyle 2,\ldots,m_{0}\in Q;

(1.5)

\displaystyle\,\forall\,m\in Q\quad(m_{0}+1\leqslant m\leqslant n)\quad\Rightarrow\quad(n-m_{0}+2\leqslant m\leqslant 2m_{0}).

(1.6)

In the case
m_{0}=\bigl[\frac{n}{2}\bigr]+1
the condition (
1.6
) automatically holds:
\frac{n}{2}<\bigl[\frac{n}{2}\bigr]+1=m_{0}
,
n<2m_{0}
,
n\leqslant 2m_{0}-1
,

m_{0}+1=(2m_{0}-1)-m_{0}+2\geqslant n-m_{0}+2
. Therefore, the main result immediately implies the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

The condition
2,\ldots,\bigl[\frac{n}{2}\bigr]+1\in Q
is sufficient for the inclusion
M(Q)\in\penalty\mathcal{M}_{n}
and, once
\cha\mathbb{F}=0
and

Q\neq\varnothing
, equivalent to it.

For arbitrary

Now assume that

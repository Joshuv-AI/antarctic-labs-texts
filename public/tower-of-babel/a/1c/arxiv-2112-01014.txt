Constructive approach to the monotone rearrangement of functions Thanks: This is a preprint. For the peer reviewed and published version, see https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exmath.2021.10.004
Giovanni Barbarino

Abstract
We detail a simple procedure (easily convertible to an algorithm) for constructing from quasi-uniform samples of f f a sequence of linear spline functions converging to the monotone rearrangement of f f , in the case where f f is an almost everywhere continuous function defined on a bounded set Ω \Omega with negligible boundary. Under additional assumptions on f f and Ω \Omega , we prove that the convergence of the sequence is uniform. We also show that the same procedure applies to arbitrary measurable functions too, but with the substantial difference that in this case the procedure has only a theoretical interest and cannot be converted to an algorithm. Keywords: monotone rearrangement, quantile function, generalized inverse distribution function, almost everywhere continuous functions, asymptotically uniform grids, quasi-uniform samples, uniform convergence 2020 MSC: 28A20, 46E30, 60E05

2
Monotone rearrangement

In this section, we recall the notion of monotone rearrangement and collect some basic properties.
We denote by
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
the space of continuous functions
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support,
by
\chi_{E}
the characteristic (indicator) function of the set
E
, and by
\mu_{d}
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. All the terminology coming from measure theory (such as “measurable set”, “measurable function”, “a.e.”, etc.) always refers to the Lebesgue measure. Throughout this paper, we use a notation borrowed from probability theory to indicate sets. For example, if
f,g:E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, then
\{f\leq 1\}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in E:f({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq 1\}
,
\mu_{d}\{f>0,\>g<0\}
is the measure of the set
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in E:f({\boldsymbol{x}})>0,\>g({\boldsymbol{x}})<0\}
, etc.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be measurable on a set
\Omega
with
0<\mu_{d}(\Omega)<\infty
. The monotone rearrangement of
f
is the function denoted by
f^{\dagger}
and defined as follows:

f^{\dagger}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad f^{\dagger}(y)=\inf\biggl\{u\in\mathbb{R}:\frac{\mu_{d}\{f\leq u\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}\geq y\biggr\}.

(2.1)

Note that
f^{\dagger}(y)
is a well-defined real number for every
y\in(0,1)
, because

\lim_{u\to+\infty}\mu_{d}\{f\leq u\}=\mu_{d}(\Omega),\qquad\lim_{u\to-\infty}\mu_{d}\{f\leq u\}=0.

In probability theory, where
f
is interpreted as a random variable on
\Omega
with probability distribution
m_{f}
and distribution function
F_{f}
given by

\displaystyle m_{f}(A)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\{f\in A\}=\frac{\mu_{d}\{f\in A\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)},\qquad A\subseteq\mathbb{R}\mbox{ is a Borel set},

\displaystyle F_{f}(u)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\{f\leq u\}=\frac{\mu_{d}\{f\leq u\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)},\qquad u\in\mathbb{R},

the monotone rearrangement
f^{\dagger}
in (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

f^{\dagger}(y)=\inf\bigl\{u\in\mathbb{R}:F_{f}(u)\geq y\bigr\},\qquad y\in(0,1),

and is referred to as the quantile function of
f
or the generalized inverse of
F_{f}
.
The next lemma collects some basic properties of monotone rearrangements; see, e.g.,
[
22
, Chapter 3, Proposition 4 and Problem 3]
for the first two statements and
[
22
, Chapter 14, Proposition 7]
for the last one.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be measurable on a set
\Omega
with
0<\mu_{d}(\Omega)<\infty
.

•

f^{\dagger}
is monotone non-decreasing and left-continuous on
(0,1)
.

•

For every Borel set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mu_{1}\{f^{\dagger}\in A\}=\dfrac{\mu_{d}\{f\in A\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}.

In particular,

–

if
f
is bounded from below then
\inf_{y\in(0,1)}f^{\dagger}(y)=\lim_{y\to 0}f^{\dagger}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
,

–

if
f
is bounded from above then
\sup_{y\in(0,1)}f^{\dagger}(y)=\lim_{y\to 1}f^{\dagger}(y)\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

For every continuous bounded function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\frac{1}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}F(f({\boldsymbol{x}})){\rm d}{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{0}^{1}F(f^{\dagger}(y)){\rm d}y.

Lemma
2.3
shows that the monotone rearrangement of a continuous function
f
is continuous provided the domain of
f
is not “too wild”.
For the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we need the following topological result. If
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, we denote by
D({\boldsymbol{x}},r)
the open
d
-dimensional disk with center
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and radius
r
. The closure and the interior of a set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
are denoted by
\overline{\Omega}
and
\accentset{\circ}{\Omega}
, respectively.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set contained in the closure of its interior.
Suppose that
U\subseteq\Omega
is non-empty and open in
\Omega
.
Then
\mu_{d}(U)>0
.

Proof.

Take a point
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in U
. Since
U
is open in
\Omega
, there exists a set
V
open in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
U=V\cap\Omega
. In particular, we can find
\varepsilon>0
such that
D({\boldsymbol{x}},\varepsilon)\subseteq V
and
D({\boldsymbol{x}},\varepsilon)\cap\Omega\subseteq U
. Now,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{x}}\in U\subseteq\Omega\subseteq\overline{\accentset{\circ}{\Omega}}

\displaystyle\quad\implies\quad{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\overline{\accentset{\circ}{\Omega}}\quad\implies\quad D({\boldsymbol{x}},\varepsilon)\cap\accentset{\circ}{\Omega}\neq\emptyset.

We have then found a set
D({\boldsymbol{x}},\varepsilon)\cap\accentset{\circ}{\Omega}
, which is non-empty, open in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and contained in
U
. The measure of this set is clearly positive just like the measure of any non-empty open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We conclude that
\mu_{d}(U)>0
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous on a set
\Omega
with
0<\mu_{d}(\Omega)<\infty
. Suppose that
\Omega
is connected and contained in the closure of its interior. Then, the monotone rearrangement
f^{\dagger}
is continuous on
(0,1)
.

Proof.

Figure 2.1:
Illustration for the proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Suppose by contradiction that
f^{\dagger}
is not continuous at a point
y_{0}\in(0,1)
. By monotonicity,
y_{0}
is a jump discontinuity of
f^{\dagger}
. Recalling that
f^{\dagger}
is left-continuous, we have

f^{\dagger}_{-}(y_{0})=f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0}),

where
f^{\dagger}_{-}(y_{0})
and
f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})
are, respectively, the left- and right-limit of
f^{\dagger}
in
y_{0}
; see Figure
2.1
. By Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<y_{0}=\mu_{1}\{f^{\dagger}\leq f^{\dagger}(y_{0})\}=\frac{\mu_{d}\{f\leq f^{\dagger}(y_{0})\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\implies\quad\mu_{d}\{f\leq f^{\dagger}(y_{0})\}>0,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<1-y_{0}=\mu_{1}\{f^{\dagger}\geq f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}=\frac{\mu_{d}\{f\geq f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\implies\quad\mu_{d}\{f\geq f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}>0,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\{f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f^{\dagger}<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}=\frac{\mu_{d}\{f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\implies\quad\mu_{d}\{f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}=0.

Since
f
is continuous on
\Omega
,

•

\{f\leq f^{\dagger}(y_{0})\}=f^{-1}((-\infty,f^{\dagger}(y_{0})])
is closed in
\Omega
,

•

\{f\geq f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}=f^{-1}([f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0}),\infty))
is closed in
\Omega
,

•

\{f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}=f^{-1}((f^{\dagger}(y_{0}),f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})))
is open in
\Omega
.

Moreover, it is clear that the sets
\{f\leq f^{\dagger}(y_{0})\}
,
\{f\geq f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}
,
\{f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}
are pairwise disjoint and

\{f\leq f^{\dagger}(y_{0})\}\cup\{f\geq f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}\cup\{f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}=\Omega.

Since
\{f\leq f^{\dagger}(y_{0})\}
and
\{f\geq f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}
are non-empty (because they have positive measure) and
\{f^{\dagger}(y_{0})<f<f^{\dagger}_{+}(y_{0})\}
is empty (because of Lemma
2.2
), we conclude that
\Omega
is not connected (a contradiction). ∎

3
Constructive rearrangement of a.e. continuous functions

In our main result (Theorem
3.1
), we detail a simple procedure for constructing from quasi-uniform samples of
f
an easy-to-manage sequence of linear spline functions
f_{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\dagger}
converging a.e. to the monotone rearrangement
f^{\dagger}
, in the case where
f
is an a.e. continuous function defined on a bounded set
\Omega
with
\mu_{d}(\Omega)>\mu_{d}(\partial\Omega)=0
. Functions of this kind essentially exhaust both the class of functions that one may encounter in real-world applications and the class of functions for which a construction of the monotone rearrangement from the samples of the function makes sense; see also Section
4
. After proving Theorem
3.1
, we show in Theorem
3.2
that the convergence of
f_{\boldsymbol{n}}^{\dagger}
to
f^{\dagger}
is uniform whenever
f
is continuous and bounded on
\Omega
and
\Omega
is connected and contained in the closure of its interior (as in Lemma
2.3
).
Before moving on, we introduce some necessary notations.

Multi-index notation.

A multi-index
{\boldsymbol{i}}
of size
d
, also called a
d
-index, is a vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
\mathbf{0},\,{\mathbf{1}},\,\mathbf{2},\,\ldots
are the vectors of all zeros, all ones, all twos,
\ldots
(their size will be clear from the context). For any vector
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set
N({\boldsymbol{n}})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}
and we write
{\boldsymbol{n}}\to\infty
to indicate that
\min({\boldsymbol{n}})\to\infty
.
If
{\boldsymbol{h}},{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, an inequality such as
{\boldsymbol{h}}\leq{\boldsymbol{k}}
means that
h_{j}\leq k_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,d
.
If
{\boldsymbol{h}},{\boldsymbol{k}}
are
d
-indices such that
{\boldsymbol{h}}\leq{\boldsymbol{k}}
, the
d
-index range
\{{\boldsymbol{h}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{k}}\}
is the set
\{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:{\boldsymbol{h}}\leq{\boldsymbol{i}}\leq{\boldsymbol{k}}\}
. We assume for this set the standard lexicographic ordering:

\Bigl[\ \ldots\ \bigl[\ [\ (i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\ ]_{i_{d}=h_{d},\ldots,k_{d}}\ \bigr]_{i_{d-1}=h_{d-1},\ldots,k_{d-1}}\ \ldots\ \Bigr]_{i_{1}=h_{1},\ldots,k_{1}}.

For instance, in the case
d=2
the ordering is

\displaystyle(h_{1},h_{2}),\,(h_{1},h_{2}+1),\,\ldots,\,(h_{1},k_{2}),\,(h_{1}+1,h_{2}),\,(h_{1}+1,h_{2}+1),\,\ldots,\,(h_{1}+1,k_{2}),

\displaystyle\ldots\,\ldots\,\ldots,\,(k_{1},h_{2}),\,(k_{1},h_{2}+1),\,\ldots,\,(k_{1},k_{2}).

When a
d
-index
{\boldsymbol{i}}
varies in a
d
-index range
\{{\boldsymbol{h}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{k}}\}
(this is often written as
{\boldsymbol{i}}={\boldsymbol{h}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{k}}
), it is understood that
{\boldsymbol{i}}
varies from
{\boldsymbol{h}}
to
{\boldsymbol{k}}
following the lexicographic ordering. If
{\boldsymbol{h}},{\boldsymbol{k}}
are
d
-indices with
{\boldsymbol{h}}\leq{\boldsymbol{k}}
, the notation
\sum_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\boldsymbol{k}}
indicates the summation over all
{\boldsymbol{i}}
in
\{{\boldsymbol{h}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{k}}\}
.
Operations involving
d
-indices (or general vectors with
d
components) that have no meaning in the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
must always be interpreted in the componentwise sense. For instance,
\boldsymbol{j}{\boldsymbol{h}}=(j_{1}h_{1},\ldots,j_{d}h_{d})
,
{\boldsymbol{i}}/\boldsymbol{j}=(i_{1}/j_{1},\ldots,i_{d}/j_{d})
, etc.

Asymptotically uniform grids.

If
{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
{\boldsymbol{a}}\leq{\boldsymbol{b}}
, we denote by
({\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
the
d
-dimensional rectangle
(a_{1},b_{1}]\times\cdots\times(a_{d},b_{d}]
. Similar meanings have the notations for the open
d
-dimensional rectangle
({\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}})
and the closed
d
-dimensional rectangle
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
.
Let
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
be a
d
-dimensional rectangle, let
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and let
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
be a sequence of
N({\boldsymbol{n}})
grid points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say that the grid
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}
is asymptotically uniform (a.u.) in
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
if

\lim_{{\boldsymbol{n}}\to\infty}\biggl(\max_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}-\Bigl({\boldsymbol{a}}+{\boldsymbol{i}}\,\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{a}}}{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\Bigr)\right\|_{\infty}\biggr)=0,

where
\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|_{\infty}=\max(|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{d}|)
for every
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Standard partitions.
Let
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
be a
d
-dimensional rectangle and let
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
. We say that
\{I_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
is a standard partition of
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
if it is a partition of
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
such that

\biggl({\boldsymbol{a}}+({\boldsymbol{i}}-{\mathbf{1}})\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{a}}}{{\boldsymbol{n}}},{\boldsymbol{a}}+{\boldsymbol{i}}\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{a}}}{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\biggr)\subseteq I_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\subseteq\biggl[{\boldsymbol{a}}+({\boldsymbol{i}}-{\mathbf{1}})\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{a}}}{{\boldsymbol{n}}},{\boldsymbol{a}}+{\boldsymbol{i}}\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{a}}}{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\biggr],\qquad{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}.

If
\{I_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
is a standard partition of
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
and
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\in I_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}
for all
{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
is an a.u. grid in
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
.

Regular sets.

We say that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a regular set if it is bounded and
\mu_{d}(\partial\Omega)=0
. Note that the condition “
\mu_{d}(\partial\Omega)=0
” is equivalent to “
\chi_{\Omega}
is continuous a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
”. Any regular set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is measurable and we have
\mu_{d}(\Omega)=\mu_{d}(\accentset{\circ}{\Omega})=\mu_{d}(\overline{\Omega})<\infty
.

Statement and proof of the main result.

With the notations introduced in the previous paragraphs, we are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous a.e. on the regular set
\Omega
with
\mu_{d}(\Omega)>0
. Take any
d
-dimensional rectangle
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
containing
\Omega
and any a.u. grid
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
in
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
.
For each
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, consider the samples

f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}),\qquad{\boldsymbol{i}}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\Omega)=\{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\{{\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}\}:{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\in\Omega\},

sort them in non-decreasing order, and put them into a vector
(s_{0},\ldots,s_{\omega({\boldsymbol{n}})})
, where
\omega({\boldsymbol{n}})=\#{\mathcal{I}}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(\Omega)-1
.
Let
f^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{n}}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be the linear spline function that interpolates the samples
(s_{0},\ldots,s_{\omega({\boldsymbol{n}})})
over the equally spaced nodes
(0,\frac{1}{\omega({\boldsymbol{n}})},\frac{2}{\omega({\boldsymbol{n}})},\ldots,1)
in
[0,1]
. Then,

\lim_{{\boldsymbol{n}}\to\infty}f^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{n}}(y)=f^{\dagger}(y)

for every continuity point
y
of
f^{\dagger}
. In particular,
f^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\to f^{\dagger}
a.e. in
(0,1)
.

Note that the last statement in Theorem
3.1
follows from the observation that
f^{\dagger}
is monotone and hence the set of its discontinuity points is countable
[
35
, Theorem 4.30]
. For the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we need three lemmas, which have an interest also in themselves. The first two provide a generalization of
[
23
, Lemma 3.1]
. The third one is an improved version of a result never published
[
5
, Lemma 4.2]
.
We say that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if it is bounded on every compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be measurable and let
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
be a
d
-dimensional rectangle. Consider a standard partition
\{I_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
of
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
and let
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
be a.u. in
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
. Then,

\lim_{{\boldsymbol{n}}\to\infty}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}}}^{\boldsymbol{n}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}})\chi_{I_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=f({\boldsymbol{x}})

(3.1)

for every
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
which is a continuity point of
f
.
In particular, if
f
is continuous a.e. and locally bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

\lim_{{\boldsymbol{n}}\to\infty}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}}}^{\boldsymbol{n}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}})\chi_{I_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=f({\boldsymbol{x}})\mbox{ \,for a.e.\ ${\boldsymbol{x}}\in[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]$}

(3.2)

and

\lim_{{\boldsymbol{n}}\to\infty}\frac{1}{N({\boldsymbol{n}})}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}}}^{\boldsymbol{n}}f({\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}})=\frac{1}{\mu_{d}([{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}])}\int_{[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]}f({\boldsymbol{x}}){\rm d}{\boldsymbol{x}}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
S=[{\boldsymbol{a}}-{\mathbf{1}},{\boldsymbol{b}}+{\mathbf{1}}]
. The grid
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}
is a.u. in
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
and hence it is eventually contained in
S
as
{\boldsymbol{n}}\to\infty
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\subset S
for all
{\boldsymbol{n}}
.
Let
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
be a continuity point of
f
and fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there is a
\delta=\delta_{{\boldsymbol{x}},\varepsilon}>0
such that
|f(\boldsymbol{y})-f({\boldsymbol{x}})|\leq\varepsilon
whenever
\boldsymbol{y}\in S
and
\|\boldsymbol{y}-{\boldsymbol{x}}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{n}}
is a.u. in
[{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}}]
, we can choose
{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\delta}
such that, for
{\boldsymbol{n}}\geq{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\delta}
,

\max_{{\boldsymbol{i}}={\mathbf{1}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{{\boldsymbol{i}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}-\Bigl({\boldsymbol{a}}+{\boldsymbol{i}}\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{a}}}{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\Bigr)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad\biggl\|\frac{{\boldsymbol{b}}-{\boldsymbol{a}}}{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\biggr\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

For
{\boldsymbol{n}}\geq{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\delta}
, if we call
I_{{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{n}}}
the unique element of the standard partition containing
{\boldsymbol{x}}
, we have

4
The case of general measurable functions

Sampling a function that is not continuous a.e. does not make sense in general. In particular, we cannot expect to obtain an easy-to-manage sequence converging to the monotone rearrangement of an arbitrary measurable function through a simple sampling procedure as the one described in Theorem
3.1
. This is due to the fact that quasi-uniform samples of an arbitrary measurable function may have nothing to do with the function itself.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the Dirichlet function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,

f(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&\textup{if }x\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q},\\
&0,&&\textup{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

The function
f
is measurable and
f=0
a.e. in
[0,1]
. The monotone rearrangement
f^{\dagger}
is the identically zero function. If we follow the construction of Theorem
3.1
using any a.u. grid in
[0,1]
consisting of rational numbers (e.g.,
\mathcal{G}_{n}=\{x_{i,n}\}_{i=1,\ldots,n}
with
x_{i,n}=\frac{i}{n}
), we obtain a sequence of functions
f^{\dagger}_{n}
which are identically equal to
1
for all
n
. Hence, there is no point
x\in(0,1)
such that
f^{\dagger}_{n}(x)\to f^{\dagger}(x)
. We remark that real-world computations are performed by computers and every possible a.u. grid that a computer can use consists of rational numbers.

In Theorem
4.1
, we show that, for a general measurable function
f
, there exist “good” quasi-uniform samples of
f
that allow one to obtain the monotone rearrangement of
f
by the same construction as in Theorem
3.1
. Unfortunately, Theorem
4.1
has only a theoretical interest, because in general there is no way to select “good” quasi-uniform samples for which the whole construction works. After all, it could not be otherwise, considering that a general measurable function
f
can be modified on a set of zero measure without changing (the essence of)
f
but with a tremendous impact on the pointwise evaluations of
f
; see also Example
4.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

. Then, there exists an a.u. grid

\tilde{f}_{m}
is any extension of
f_{m}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\tilde{f}_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\to f({\boldsymbol{x}})
for a.e.
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega
and

\displaystyle\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta\}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-\tilde{f}_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta\}\to 0.

As a consequence, there exists
M=M_{\delta}
such that, for
m\geq M
,

\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta\}+\mu_{d}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{m})+\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m}\setminus\Omega)\leq\varepsilon.

Note that

\displaystyle\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:f({\boldsymbol{x}})-|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\leq u\}

\displaystyle=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|<\delta,\ f({\boldsymbol{x}})-|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\leq u\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{=\lx@text@lbrace}\,\cup\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta,\ f({\boldsymbol{x}})-|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\leq u\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:f({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u+\delta\}\cup\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta\}

and similarly

\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:f({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u-\delta\}\subseteq\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u\}\cup\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta\}.

Thus, for
m\geq M
,

\displaystyle F_{f_{m}}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega_{m}:f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m})}\leq\frac{\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega_{m}\cap\Omega:f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u\}+\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m}\setminus\Omega)}{\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega_{m}\cap\Omega:f({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u+\delta\}+\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega_{m}\cap\Omega:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta\}+\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m}\setminus\Omega)}{\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m})}

\displaystyle\leq F_{f}(u+\delta)\frac{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}{\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m})}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m})}\leq(F_{f}(u-\delta)+\varepsilon)\frac{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)-\mu_{d}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{m})}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)-\mu_{d}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{m})}

\displaystyle\leq(F_{f}(u)+\varepsilon)\frac{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)-\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)-\varepsilon}

and

\displaystyle F_{f}(u)

\displaystyle\leq F_{f}(u+\delta)\leq F_{f}(u-\delta)+\varepsilon=\frac{\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega:f({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u-\delta\}}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:f({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u-\delta\}+\mu_{d}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{m})}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq u\}+\mu_{d}\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega\cap\Omega_{m}:|f({\boldsymbol{x}})-f_{m}({\boldsymbol{x}})|\geq\delta\}+\mu_{d}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{m})}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq F_{f_{m}}(u)\frac{\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m})}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon\leq F_{f_{m}}(u)\frac{\mu_{d}(\Omega)+\mu_{d}(\Omega_{m}\setminus\Omega)}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq F_{f_{m}}(u)\frac{\mu_{d}(\Omega)+\varepsilon}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu_{d}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon.

In conclusion, for
m\geq M
we can write
\varphi(\varepsilon)\leq F_{f_{m}}(u)\leq\psi(\varepsilon)
, where both
\varphi(\varepsilon)
and
\psi(\varepsilon)
tend to
F_{f}(u)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This proves that
F_{f_{m}}(u)\to F_{f}(u)
.

2.

Let
y\in(0,1)
be a continuity point for
f^{\dagger}
. Since
F_{f}
is monotone, its continuity points are dense in its domain
\mathbb{R}
. As a consequence,
u=f^{\dagger}(y)-\delta
is a continuity point of
F_{f}
for arbitrarily small
\delta>0
. From the definition of
f^{\dagger}(y)
,

F_{f}(f^{\dagger}(y)-\delta)<y.

By the first statement of the lemma,
F_{f_{m}}(u)\to F_{f}(u)
and so we eventually have

F_{f_{m}}(f^{\dagger}(y)-\delta)<y\quad\implies\quad f^{\dagger}_{m}(y)\geq f^{\dagger}(y)-\delta.

Thus,

\liminf_{m\to\infty}f^{\dagger}_{m}(y)\geq f^{\dagger}(y)-\delta.

Since we can take
\delta
arbitrarily small, we obtain

\liminf_{m\to\infty}f^{\dagger}_{m}(y)\geq f^{\dagger}(y).

Now, fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
\delta>0
such that
v=f^{\dagger}(y+\varepsilon)+\delta
is a continuity point for
F_{f}
.
From the definition of
f^{\dagger}(y+\varepsilon)
and the monotonicity of
F_{f}
,

F_{f}(f^{\dagger}(y+\varepsilon)+\delta)\geq y+\varepsilon.

By the first statement of the lemma,
F_{f_{m}}(v)\to F_{f}(v)
and so we eventually have

F_{f_{m}}(f^{\dagger}(y+\varepsilon)+\delta)\geq y\quad\implies\quad f^{\dagger}_{m}(y)\leq f^{\dagger}(y+\varepsilon)+\delta.

Thus,

\limsup_{m\to\infty}f^{\dagger}_{m}(y)\leq f^{\dagger}(y+\varepsilon)+\delta.

Taking first the limit as
\delta\to 0
and then the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, by the continuity of
f^{\dagger}
in
y
we obtain

\limsup_{m\to\infty}f^{\dagger}_{m}(y)\leq f^{\dagger}(y).

We then conclude that
f^{\dagger}_{m}(y)\to f^{\dagger}(y)
. ∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Since

{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\hat{\Omega}

Now, fix

For every

A note on lc-trivial fibrations
Kenta Hashizume

Abstract
. For every lc-trivial fibration ( X , Δ ) → Z (X,\Delta)\to Z from an lc pair, we prove that after a base change, there exists a positive integer n n , depending only on the dimension of X X , the Cartier index of K X + Δ K_{X}+\Delta , and the sufficiently general fibers of X → Z X\to Z , such that n ⁡ ( K X + Δ ) n(K_{X}+\Delta) is linearly equivalent to the pullback of a Cartier divisor.

primary: 14E30, secondary: 14D06, 14J40

1.
Introduction

Throughout this note, we will work over the complex number field.

An lc-trivial fibration is a fibration together with an lc pair such that the log canonical divisor of the lc pair is relatively
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly trivial, i.e.,
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly equivalent to the pullback of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on the base variety.
For the definition of lc-trivial fibrations, see Definition
2.3
.
This kind of fibration naturally appears and plays an important role in birational geometry.
For example, every fibration induced by the log canonical divisor of a good minimal model is an lc-trivial fibration, every fibration from a Calabi–Yau manifold is also an lc-trivial fibration, and we can define the structure of an lc-trivial fibration for any Mori fiber space.
Because of the relative
\mathbb{Q}
-linear triviality of the log canonical divisors of lc pairs, it has been revealed that the geometries of the total variety, the general fiber, and the base variety of an lc-trivial fibration are closely related to each other (
[
Ka98
]
,
[
A04
]
,
[
A05
]
,
[
FG12
]
,
[
FG14
]
,
[
B16
]
,
[
B18
]
,
[
B21
]
).
Especially, a relation between the total variety and the base variety, called the canonical bundle formula and developed by Kawamata (
[
Ka98
]
) and Ambro (
[
A04
]
,
[
A05
]
), plays a fundamental role in the recent development of birational geometry (
[
FG12
]
,
[
B16
]
,
[
J22
]
).
For an lc-trivial fibration
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to Z
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
Z
such that
K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}D
, the canonical bundle formula (Definition
2.4
) asserts that
D
can be written as
K_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z}
the sum of the canonical divisor
K_{Z}
of
Z
, the discriminant part
\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}
, which contains the information of singular fibers of
\pi
, and the moduli part
\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z}
, which is concerned with
\pi
as a space parametrizing the fibers.
The discriminant part and the moduli part form a generalized pair
(Z,\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M})
, which was introduced by Birkar–Zhang
[
BZ16
]
.
Currently, the study of connection between the generalized pair
(Z,\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M})
and the lc pair
(X,\Delta)
is a central topic of lc-trivial fibration.

In this note we discuss a relation between the Cartier index of
K_{X}+\Delta
and that of
K_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z}
after a base change.

Theorem 1.1

(Main result)
.

For every
d
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, depending only on
d
,
m
, and
v
, satisfying the following.
Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective lc pair, let
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to Z
be an lc-trivial fibration with the sufficiently general fiber
F
, and let
A\geq 0
be a Weil divisor on
X
such that

•

\dim X=d
,

•

mf^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)
is Cartier for some resolution
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
of
X
,

•

A|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and ample over
U
for some open subset
U\subset Z
,

•

(F,\Delta|_{F}+tA|_{F})
is an lc pair for some real number
t>0
, and

•

{\rm vol}(A|_{F})=v
.

Then there exists a generalized lc pair
(Z,\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M})
defined with the canonical bundle formula such that

•

n(K_{X}+\Delta)\sim n\pi^{*}(K_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z})
,

•

n\boldsymbol{\rm M}
is b-Cartier, and

•

n\phi^{*}(K_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z})
is Cartier for some resolution
\phi\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
of
Z
.

Furthermore, if there is a klt pair structure on
Z
then
n(K_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z})
is Cartier.

For the definitions of b-divisor and b-Cartier property, see Definition
2.1
.
Theorem
1.1
looks similar to
[
B21
, Lemma 7.4]
by Birkar.
In both statements, we fix the dimension of the lc pair and the set of the coefficients of the boundary divisor, and we assume a kind of boundedness condition on the general fibers of the lc-trivial fibration.
In Theorem
1.1
, the linear equivalence between
n(K_{X}+\Delta)
and
n\pi^{*}(K_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z})
and the b-Cartier property of
n\boldsymbol{\rm M}
are not new because the two properties have already been proved in
[
B21
, Lemma 7.4]
.
The new contribution of Theorem
1.1
is that the b-Cartier property of
n(K_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z}+\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z})
as a b-divisor depends on the b-Cartier index of
K_{X}+\Delta
as a b-divisor.
Birkar’s result
[
B21
, Lemma 7.4]
plays a crucial role for the boundedness of the base varieties of Iitaka fibrations (see
[
BiHa22
, Theorem 1.3]
by Birkar–Hacon), whereas Theorem
1.1
is useful to study effective base point freeness.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
d
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
v\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, depending only on
d
,
m
, and
v
, satisfying the following.
Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective klt pair such that
e(K_{X}+\Delta)
is semi-ample for an
e\in\{1,-1\}
, let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be the contraction induced by
e(K_{X}+\Delta)
, and let
A
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor on
X
such that

•

\dim X=d
,

•

m(K_{X}+\Delta)
is Cartier, and

•

{\rm vol}(A|_{F})=v
, where
F
is a sufficiently general fiber of
\pi
.

Then
ne(K_{X}+\Delta)
is base point free and the linear system
|ne(K_{X}+\Delta)|
defines
\pi
.

We emphasize that we do not assume the divisor
A
in Theorem
1.2
to be effective.
In the case where
K_{X}+\Delta
is big in Theorem
1.2
, the statement is a spacial case of Kollár’s effective base point free theorem
[
Ko93
]
.
Theorem
1.2
is a partial generalization of
[
Ko93
, Theorem 1.1]
to the case where the log canonical divisor or anti log canonical divisor of a klt pair has intermidiate Kodaira dimension.
After this note was announced, Masamura
[
M23
]
gave an example of minimal surface for which the effective base point freeness does not hold.
This shows that the existence of the divisor
A
in Theorem
1.2
can not be removed unconditionally.

By using the main result (Theorem
1.1
), we can prove another type of effective base point free theorem in the non-klt case.
In Section
4
, we study lc-trivial fibrations whose moduli parts satisfy the log bigness on a certain higher birational model.
The motivation of the topic comes from the following result, which is a consequence of the argument in
[
FL19
, Proof of Proposition 4.4]
by Floris–Lazić (see also
[
Hu20
, Theorem 1.2]
by Hu).

Theorem 1.3

(
[
FL19
]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)\to Z
be an lc-trivial fibration.
Then there exists a log smooth Ambro model
Z^{\prime}\to Z
such that
\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z^{\prime}}
is log abundant with respect to
(Z^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z^{\prime}})
, i.e., for every stratum
T^{\prime}
of
(Z^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z^{\prime}})
, there exist a birational morphism
h\colon W\to T^{\prime}
from a normal projective variety
W
, a surjective morphism
\psi\colon W\to V
to a normal projective variety
V
, and a nef and big
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
N
on
V
such that
h^{*}(\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z^{\prime}}|_{T^{\prime}})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\psi^{*}N
.

For the definition of log smooth Ambro model, see Definition
2.5
.
Note that
Z^{\prime}
in Theorem
1.3
is not unique.
Lc-trivial fibrations with log big moduli parts are defined by the log bigness of the moduli part on a log smooth Ambro model (Definition
2.7
).
The log bigness is one of spacial cases in the property of being log abundant.
Hence, lc-trivial fibrations with log big moduli parts appear as special lc-trivial fibrations.
By definition, an lc-trivial fibration is an lc-trivial fibration with log big moduli part when there is a log smooth Ambro model
Z^{\prime}\to Z
such that the map
\psi\circ h^{-1}\colon T^{\prime}\dashrightarrow V
in Theorem
1.3
is birational for every stratum
T^{\prime}
of
(Z^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\rm B}_{Z^{\prime}})
.

By combining Theorem
1.1
with a result from
[
Ha22
]
, we will prove an effective base point free theorem for lc-trivial fibrations with log big moduli parts.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
)
.

For every
d
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
v\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, depending only on
d
,
m
, and
v
, satisfying the following.
Let
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to Z
be an lc-trivial fibration with log big moduli part, let
F
be the sufficiently general fiber of
\pi
, and let
A\geq 0
be a Weil divisor on
X
such that

•

\dim X=d
,

•

me(K_{X}+\Delta)
is nef and Cartier for an
e\in\{1,-1\}
,

•

A|_{\pi^{-1}(U)}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and ample over
U
for some open subset
U\subset Z
,

•

(F,\Delta|_{F}+tA|_{F})
is an lc pair for some real number

, and

•

{\rm vol}(A|_{F})=v
.

Then

is base point free.

Moreover, good minimal models or Mori fiber spaces always exist for projective lc pairs admitting the structure of an lc-trivial fibration with log big moduli part.

Theorem 1.5

(=Theorem
4.3
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective lc pair admitting an lc-trivial fibration with log big moduli part.
Then
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model or a Mori fiber space.

Although the theorem is not an application of Theorem

, we hope that the theorem is independent of interest.

Definitions

In this section, we collect definitions used in this note.

Let
a
be a real number.
Then
\lfloor a\rfloor
denotes the integer satisfying
a-1<\lfloor a\rfloor\leq a
.
Let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on a variety and let
D=\sum_{i}d_{i}D_{i}
be the decomposition of
D
into prime divisors.
Then
\lfloor D\rfloor
is defined to be
\sum_{i}\lfloor d_{i}\rfloor D_{i}
.

A

is a projective morphism
f\colon X\to Z
of varieties such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z}
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and
D
an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.
Then
(X,D)
is
log smooth
if
X
is smooth and
D
has simple normal crossing support.
A
log resolution
of
(X,\Supp D)
is a projective birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
from a smooth variety
Y
such that the exceptional locus
{\rm Ex}(f)
of
f
is pure codimension one and
{\rm Ex}(f)\cup{\rm Supp}\,f_{*}^{-1}D
has simple normal crossing support.

Definition 2.1

(b-divisor)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety.
Consider all proper birational morphisms
f\colon Y\to X
from normal varieties
Y
and the sets
{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on
Y
.
For two proper birational morphisms
Y_{1}\to X
and
Y_{2}\to X
such that the induced birational map

is a morphism, we define the map

\phi_{*}\colon{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y_{1})\longrightarrow{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y_{2})

by taking the birational transform.
Then a
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor

\boldsymbol{\rm D}
on
X
is an element of the inverse limit

\boldsymbol{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X):=\underset{\longleftarrow}{\rm lim}{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)

defined with all such
\phi_{*}
as above.

For a
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor
\boldsymbol{\rm D}
on
X
and a normal variety
Y
with a proper birational morphism
Y\to X
, the image of
\boldsymbol{\rm D}
by the natural projection
\boldsymbol{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\to{\rm WDiv}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
is called the

\boldsymbol{\rm D}
on
Y
, and it is denoted by
\boldsymbol{\rm D}_{Y}
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\rm D}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor on
X
.
We say that
\boldsymbol{\rm D}
descends to a normal variety

if there exist a proper birational morphism

from

and a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

on

such that for any normal variety

together with a proper birational morphism

of

we have
\boldsymbol{\rm D}_{Y^{\prime}}=g^{\prime}_{*}g^{*}D
.
In this situation, we also say that
\boldsymbol{\rm D}
is the

, and we use the notation
\overline{D}
to denote
\boldsymbol{\rm D}
.
If, additionally,

is Cartier, then we say that

is

.

We freely use the notations and the definitions in
[
KM98
]
and
[
BCHM10
]
for singularities of pairs except that

denotes the

of

with respect to a pair

in this note.
We use the definition of generalized pairs in
[

]
and
[
B19
]
.

BZ16
]
or
[
B19
]
.
In this note, we only deal with generalized pairs in the framework of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors.

Definition 2.2

(Generalized lc pair)
.

A
generalized pair

(X,B+\boldsymbol{\rm M})/Z
consists of

•

a projective morphism
X\to Z
from a normal variety
X
to a variety
Z
,

•

an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B
on
X
, and

•

a
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor
\boldsymbol{\rm M}
on
X
that is the Cartier closure of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

, which is nef over

, on a normal variety

with a projective birational morphism

such that

is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.
When

Let

be a generalized pair and let

.

We recall the definition of lc-trivial fibration and the canonical bundle formula.

Definition 2.3

(Lc-trivial fibration for lc pair)
.

In this note, an
lc-trivial fibration
, often denoted by
(X,\Delta)\to Z
, means a projective lc pair
(X,\Delta)
equipped with a contraction
X\to Z
of normal projective varieties such that
\Delta
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor and
K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q},Z}0
.

In general, lc-trivial fibrations are defined for contractions and sub-pairs that are not necessarily sub-lc (
[
A04
]
,
[
A05
]
,
[
FG12
]
,
[
FG14
]
,
[
FL19
]
,
[
Hu20
]
).
However, in this note, we adopt the above convention for simplicity of the definition.
In particular, we always assume that the lc pairs of lc-trivial fibrations have
\mathbb{Q}
-boundary divisors.

Definition 2.4

(Discriminant b-divisor, moduli b-divisor, canonical bundle formula)
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to Z
be an lc-trivial fibration.
We define the
discriminant
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor
\boldsymbol{\rm B}
and the
moduli
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor

\boldsymbol{\rm M}
on
Z
as follows:
Let
\phi\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
be a birational morphism from a normal projective variety
Z^{\prime}
.
We first define the discriminant
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor
\boldsymbol{\rm B}
.
Let
X^{\prime}
be a resolution of the main component of
X\times_{Z}Z^{\prime}
, and let
\psi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
and
\pi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Z^{\prime}
be the induced morphisms.
We define a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
by
K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}=\psi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)
.
For every prime divisor
P
on
Z^{\prime}
, let
b_{P}
be the largest real number such that after we shrink
Z^{\prime}
around the generic point

of
P
, the subpair
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}+b_{P}\pi^{\prime*}P)
is sub-lc.
Note that

is well-defined since

.
Then the trace

of

.
Next, we define the moduli

-b-divisor

-Cartier divisor

.

\boldsymbol{\rm M}_{Z^{\prime}}

Note that

) depends on the choice of

.
We call

.

Throughout this note, for an lc-trivial fibration
(X,\Delta)\to Z
, unless otherwise stated the discriminant b-divisor (resp. the moduli b-divisor) is denoted by

(resp.
\boldsymbol{\rm M}
).

Let

be the canonical b-divisor on

.
By the results of Kawamata

In particular,

3.
Proofs of main results

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair and let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
If there is a projective birational morphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
from a normal variety
X^{\prime}
such that
f^{*}D
is Cartier, then
D

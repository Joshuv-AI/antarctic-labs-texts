1.
Introduction

Our starting point is Dirac’s theorem, which states that any graph
G

on
n\geq 3
vertices and minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq n/2

contains a Hamilton cycle.
Moreover, the constant
1/2
is best possible as exhibited by simple
constructions.

Over the past twenty-five years, this result has been extended to the hypergraph setting.
Formally, a
k
-uniform hypergraph
(
k
-graph
for short)

G
has a set of
vertices

V(G)
and a set of
edges

, where each edge consists of
k
vertices, and we denote the
number of edges
|E(G)|
by

. For

minimum

\delta_{d}(G)
, is the maximum

such that every set of
d
vertices is contained in at least
m

edges. A

is a subgraph whose vertices
are cyclically ordered such that every
k
consecutive vertices form an
edge. Moreover,
C
is

if it spans all the vertices of
G
.
We define the
Dirac constant

\th_{d}^{(k)}
as the (asymptotic)

-degree threshold for tight Hamiltonicity. More precisely,

\th_{d}^{(k)}
is the infimum
\varsigma\in[0,1]
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
and
n
sufficiently large, every
n
-vertex
k
-graph

with

\delta_{d}(G)\geq(\varsigma+\varepsilon)\binom{n-d}{k-d}
contains a tight
Hamilton cycle. So for example, Dirac’s theorem implies that

\th_{1}^{(2)}=1/2
.

Minimum

-degree thresholds for tight Hamilton cycles were first
investigated by Katona and Kierstead
[
14
]
, who observed that

for all

and conjectured this to be

fig.

).
This conjecture was resolved by Rödl, Ruciński, and
Szemerédi
[
21

22
]
by introducing the
absorption
method
in this setting. Since then the focus has shifted
to degree types

below

. After advances for nearly spanning cycles by Cooley and Mycroft
[
3
]
,
it was shown by Reiher, Rödl, Ruciński, Schacht, and

[
20
]
that
\th_{1}^{(3)}=5/9
, which resolves the case
of
d=k-2
when

. Subsequently, this was generalised to

[
18
]
and finally, Polcyn, Reiher, Rödl, and
Schülke
[
19
]
and, independently, Lang and
Sanhueza-Matamala
[

]
established

for all

.

We focus on the case

Han and Zhao
[
12
]
provided a construction that

\th_{k-3}^{(k)}\geq 5/8

Our main result confirms this conjecture.

From Dirac to Erdős–Gallai

Let us recall two classic problems from extremal combinatorics. The
first one is Erdős’ Matching Conjecture
[

, which predicts the

-graph
with a given number of vertices and edges.
A matching is a subgraph with pairwise disjoint edges.

Conjecture 2.1

Let

-graph on

Then

\displaystyle e(G)\leq\max\left\{\binom{(s+1)k-1}{k},\,\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-s}{k}\right\}.

The conjecture was resolved
by Erdős and Gallai
[

]
for

and for

by Łuczak and

[
23
]
(when

is large) and Frankl
[

]
(for all

concerns the Erdős–Gallai Theorem
[
4
]
, which tells us the size
of a longest cycle in a graph of given density. This problem has a
natural extension to tight cycles in hypergraphs, and it was first studied by
Győri, Katona, and Lemons
[
11
]
and Allen, Böttcher, Cooley, and Mycroft
[
1
]
.

How are these questions related to Dirac-type problems?
The idea is to study the internal structure of the neighbourhoods.
Alon, Frankl, Huang, Rödl, Ruciński, and Sudakov
[
2
]

showed that the problem of determining the minimum

-degree threshold
for

-uniform perfect matchings can be reduced to a special case of
Erdős’ Matching Conjecture for
(k-d)
-graphs, applied to

-uniform link hypergraphs. Analogously, it was shown by Lang
and Sanhueza-Matamala
[

]
that the problem of determining the minimum

-degree threshold for

-uniform tight Hamilton cycles can be
reduced to an Erdős–Gallai-type question for

-uniform link hypergraphs.

Using a standard machinery

further. For a

Moreover, we refer to edge maximal tightly connected subgraphs as

4.
The matching lemma

In this section, we establish
lemma

2.4
. We proceed by studying the extremal function for matchings
hosted by a largest tight component in a
3
-graph
G

If

has edge density above

a tight component

Applying Erdős’ Matching Conjecture (

In

The proof is based on the approach of Łuczak and Mieczkowska

4.1.

We write

5.
Conclusion

We determined the minimum
d
-degree threshold for
k
-uniform Hamilton cycles when
d=k-3
by studying an extension of the Erdős–Gallai Theorem for
3
-graphs.
We believe that a similar approach could be used to tackle the thresholds
\th_{d}^{(k)}
for
k-d\geq 4
, as suggested by Lang and
Sanhueza-Matamala
[
15
]
*Conjecture 11.6.
This echoes a conjecture of Polcyn, Reiher, Rödl, and Schülke
[
19
]
— namely
\th_{d}^{(k)}
being determined by
k-d
.
In the remainder, we discuss two further avenues of research that appear to be worth exploring.

Connectivity

An important part of our proof concerns connectivity in dense hypergraphs.
Originally an auxiliary concept, the structure and interplay of tight components has become an object of study on its own over the recent years
[
10
,
16
,
17
]
. We therefore suggest to further investigate the extremal behaviour of the function
c_{k}(\lambda)
, which we define as the limes supremum of the edge density a
k
-graph on
n
vertices may have without containing a tight component on more than
\lambda\binom{n}{k}
edges.
By
lemma

2.3
and the construction in
fig.

1
, we have
c_{3}(1/2)=5/8
.
We believe for odd
k
that
c_{k}(1/2)
is attained by the
k
-graphs defined as follows:
the vertex set consists of disjoint sets
X
and
Y
with
|X|\geq|Y|
and its edges are all
k
-sets but those with
\lfloor k/2\rfloor
vertices in
X
and
\lceil k/2\rceil
vertices in
Y
.
In light of the proof of
lemma

2.3
, a plausible approach to this problem would be to study hypergraph versions of
theorem

3.1
, which is a natural question in itself.

Cycles

What is the maximal number of edges a
k
-graph on
n
vertices may have that does not contain a tight cycle of length at least
\ell
?
For
k=2
, this was answered by Erdős and Gallai
[
4
]
, and the extremal construction turns out to be a union of cliques of order at most
\ell-1
.
In the hypergraph setting, a similar result was shown by Allen, Böttcher, Cooley, and Mycroft
[
1
]
for
\ell=o(n)
.
However, when
\ell
becomes large enough new extremal constructions appear (see
fig.

1
).
The study of this phenomenon strikes us as interesting.

Let us define the function
\eg_{3}(\lambda)
as the limes supremum of the edge density a
3
-graph on
n
vertices may have without containing a cycle of length
\lambda n
.
Using the approach of Allen, Böttcher, Cooley, and Mycroft
[
1
]
,
we can distill the fact that
\eg_{3}(3/4)=5/8
from
lemmas

2.3
and
2.4
combined with the construction of
fig.

1
.
We are convinced that a more careful analysis of our proof should disclose that this type of construction is sharp for all
\lambda\geq 3/5
.
In particular, in this regime we expect at most two tight components in the extremal constructions.
For smaller
\lambda
however, a more complex picture emerges, since more tight components may arise.
Consider for instance the complement of the canonical extremal construction for Turán’s conjecture for the tetrahedron.

On the boundedness of the global solution of anisotropic quasi-geostrophic equations in Sobolev space
Mustapha Amara

Abstract
. In this paper, we show that the global solution of the surface anisotropic two-dimensional quasi-geostrophic equation with fractional horizontal dissipation and vertical thermal diffusion established by the author in [ 2 ] is bounded in Sobolev spaces uniformly with respect to time.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35-XX, 35Q30, 76N10

1.
Introduction

In this paper we deal with the following surface quasi-geostrophic equation with fractional horizontal dissipation and fractional vertical thermal diffusion:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta+\mu|\partial_{1}|^{2\alpha}\theta+\nu|\partial_{2}|^{2\beta}\theta=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ t>0\\
\theta(x,0)=\theta^{0}(x),\end{cases}

(AQG)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\mu>0
and
\nu>0
are real number. Here, we denote by
|\partial_{1}|
and
|\partial_{2}|
the operators given by

\mathcal{F}(|\partial_{1}|^{2\alpha}f)(\xi)=|\xi_{1}|^{2\alpha}\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi),\quad\mathcal{F}(|\partial_{2}|^{2\beta}f)(\xi)=|\xi_{2}|^{2\beta}\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi),\quad\forall\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\mathcal{F}(f)
represents the Fourier transformation of
f
. The variable
\theta
represents the potential temperature and the velocity
u_{\theta}=(u_{1},u_{2})
is determined by
\theta
via the formula

\displaystyle u_{\theta}=\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\theta=\left(-\mathcal{R}_{2}\theta,\mathcal{R}_{1}\theta\right)=\left(-\partial_{2}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\theta,\partial_{1}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\theta\right),

where
\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}
are the standard 2D Riesz transforms. Clearly, the velocity
u
is divergence free,
namely

\mathrm{div}(u_{\theta}):=\partial_{1}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}=0.

The system
(
AQG
)
is deeply related, in the case when
\alpha=\beta
and
\mu=\nu
, to the classical dissipation
(QG)
equation, with its from as follows

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta+\mu(-\Delta)^{2\alpha}\theta=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ t>0\\
\theta(x,0)=\theta^{0}(x).\end{cases}

(QG)

The equations
(
AQG
)
and
(
QG
)
are special cases of the general quasi-geostrophic approximations for atmospheric and oceanic fluid flow with small
Rossby and Ekman numbers. The first mathematical studies of this equation was carried out in 1994s by Constantin, Majda and Tabak. For more
details and mathematical and physical explanations of this model we can consult
[
5
,
3
,
6
,
4
]
.

The inviscid quasi-geostrophic equation (i.e.,
(
QG
)
with
\mu=0
) shares many properties parallel to those of 3D Euler equations such as the vortex stretch mechanism and thus serves as a lower dimensional model of 3D Euler equations.

The first studies of the system
(
AQG
)
is in
[
2
]
by Ye, who shows that this equation admits a unique global solution
\theta
in the space
C(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
,
s\geq 2
, such that

|\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta,|\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})),

(1.1)

when
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
satisfies

\beta>\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2\alpha+1},&0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}&\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<1.\end{cases}

(1.2)

We also refer to our result in
[
8
]
, when we established the global regularity in
C(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, when
\alpha,\beta\in(1/2,1)
and
s\in(\max\{2-2\alpha,2-2\beta\},2)
.

In this paper, we will show that this global solution is in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
and

|\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta,|\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})).

(1.3)

For the sake of simplicity, we will set
\mu=\nu=1
throughout the paper,
C
denoted all constants that is a generic constant depending only on the quantities specified in the context and
C(\theta^{0})
represent all constants depending on the norm of the initial condition
\theta^{0}
.

3.
Notations and Preliminary Results

In this short section, we collect some notations and definitions that will be used later, and we give some technical lemmas.

3.1.
Notations

\bullet

The Fourier transformation in

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-ix.\xi}f(x)dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(3.1)

The inverse Fourier formula is

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-2}\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\xi.x}f(\xi)d\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(3.2)

\bullet

The convolution product of a suitable pair of function

f\ast g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x-y)g(y)dy

(3.3)

\bullet

If

\mathrm{div}(f\otimes g):=(\mathrm{div}(g_{1}f),\mathrm{div}(g_{2}f)).

\bullet

For

:

\ast

The Sobolev space:

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2});(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\},

denotes the usual inhomogeneous Sobolev space on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\ast

The Sobolev space:

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2});\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\mbox{ and }|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\},

\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

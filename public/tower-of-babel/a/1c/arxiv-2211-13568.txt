New High Dimensional Expanders from Covers
Yotam Dikstein

Abstract
We present a new construction of high dimensional expanders based on covering spaces of simplicial complexes. High dimensional expanders (HDXs) are hypergraph analogues of expander graphs. They have many uses in theoretical computer science, but unfortunately only few constructions are known which have arbitrarily small local spectral expansion. We give a randomized algorithm that takes as input a high dimensional expander X X (satisfying some mild assumptions). It outputs a sub-complex Y ⊆ X Y\subseteq X that is a high dimensional expander and has infinitely many simplicial covers. These covers form new families of bounded-degree high dimensional expanders. The sub-complex Y Y inherits X X ’s underlying graph and its links are sparsifications of the links of X X . When the size of the links of X X is O ⁡ ( log ⁡ | X | ) O(\log|X|) , this algorithm can be made deterministic. Our algorithm is based on the groups and generating sets discovered by Lubotzky, Samuels and Vishne (2005), that were used to construct the first discovered high dimensional expanders. We show these groups give rise to many more “randomized” high dimensional expanders. In addition, our techniques also give a random sparsification algorithm for high dimensional expanders, that maintains its local spectral properties. This may be of independent interest.

Introduction

Expanders are graphs that are highly connected. It is a known fact that there exist families of expander graphs that are bounded-degree. These graphs play a key role in theoretical computer science, combinatorics and many other areas in mathematics
[
HLW06
]
.
High dimensional expanders (HDXs) are a hypergraph analogue of expander graphs. Loosely speaking, high dimensional expanders have the property that neighborhoods of faces are themselves expanding graphs. Lubotzky surveys the different definitions of high dimensional expanders
[
Lub17
]
.

High dimensional expanders are promising objects in theoretical computer science. They have been used for efficiently solving CSPs
[
AJT19
]
, for constructing agreement testers
[
DK17
,
DD19
]
and for sampling and counting matroids and other combinatorial objects (e.g.
[
Ana+19
]
,
[
CLV21
]
).

There are only two constructions of high dimensional expanders that achieve arbitrarily good expansion,
[
LSV05a
]
and
[
KO18
]
. Both constructions rely on group theory. This is contrary to expander graphs, where in addition to group-theoretic constructions, elementary combinatorial constructions and random constructions are known
[
HLW06
]
.

The goal of this work is to construct new high dimensional expanders by using random simplicial covers.
Graph covers (or lifts) are a key ingredient in many random expander constructions. A graph
G^{\prime}
covers a graph
G
if there is a surjective graph homomorphism
\psi:G^{\prime}\to G
so that for every vertex
v\in G^{\prime}
,
deg(v)=deg(\psi(v))
. There is a simple algorithm (described below) that given a graph
G
, outputs a random cover
G^{\prime}
of
G
. In many cases the covers will also be expander graphs, and indeed this is exploited to construct infinite families of expanders (see e.g.
[
BL06
]
and
[
MSS15
]
). In this work, we manage to extend this technique to high dimensional expanders.

1.1
High dimensional expanders

A pure

Let

This definition is due to

]

1.2
Graph covers and simplicial covers

Let
X,Y
be pure
d
-dimensional simplicial complexes. A simplicial homomorphism is a function
\psi:X(0)\to Y(0)
so that every

is mapped to

and preimage

is an isomorphism between

and

Given a graph on

of

as follows. Let

be any finite group and let

be a labeling. The vertices of

are set to be

. For every

are adjacent in

if

and

. The covering map is the map that projects
(i,g)
to its left coordinate

. In fact, every graph cover corresponds to some group and some edge labeling
f:E\to\Gamma

[
Sur84
]
.

This method has proven to be useful in constructing new expander graphs from initial expanders. Amit and Linial initiated the study of random graph covers
[
AL02
]
. Bilu and Linial showed that if
G
is a bounded-degree expander,
\Gamma=\mathbb{F}_{2}
and

is chosen at random, then

is an expander with positive probability
[

]
. A breakthrough paper by Marcus, Spielman and Srivastava
[

]
used graph covers to create optimal (Ramanujan) bipartite expander graphs. It is tempting to try and generalize this simple construction to high dimensional expanders.

Unfortunately, in simplicial complexes this method does not work as is. Given a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex
X
and a labeling of the
1
-skeleton
f:X(1)\to\Gamma
, the resulting covering graph

is not necessarily a (
1
-skeleton of a)
d
-dimensional simplicial complex at all. It turns out that connected covers of a simplicial complex correspond to subgroups of its fundamental group (as a topological space)
[
Sur84
]
. This group may be trivial so complexes may have no non-trivial connected covers.

1.3
Our contribution

Not all is lost. In this paper we show that even if a high dimensional expander

that is a high dimensional expander
and has infinitely many covers

1.

An initial high dimensional expander

2.

An infinite group

should be themselves expanding graphs.

1.5
Overview of our construction

The cover constructed from a labeling
f:X(1)\to\Gamma
may be trivial, that is, that
X^{\prime}
may consist of
|\Gamma|
disjoint copies of
X
. Recall that connected covers are determined by the fundamental group of
X
, and it could be the case that all covers
X^{\prime}
of
X
are trivial.

To overcome this in our construction, we “go the other way around”. We sample a labeling
f:X(1)\to\Gamma
by sampling the label of each edge independently. We then wish to remove from
X
all faces that contain triangles
v_{i}v_{j}v_{k}\in X(2)
where
i<j<k
and
f(v_{i}v_{j})f(v_{j}v_{k})\neq f(v_{i}v_{k})
. If we do so, we get a sub-complex
Y\subseteq X
so that
f
is one of its
\Gamma
-cosystols.

The main problem with this naive triangle removal, is that with high probability some links of faces in
Y
will not be expanders anymore. It could be, for example, that all triangles adjacent to some vertex are removed (and its link becomes disconnected). Denote by
B_{\sigma}
the event that the link of
\sigma
is not an expander graph.

Fortunately, Lovasz’s Local Lemma
[
EL74
]
guarantees us that if the probability of each
B_{\sigma}
is small, and that every event
B_{\sigma}
is independent of all but a few of the
B_{\sigma^{\prime}}
, then the event where no
B_{\sigma}
occurs has positive probability (i.e.
Y
is a high dimensional expander with positive probability). The heart of our analysis is to properly define these “local bad events”, and to show that we can indeed apply Lovasz’s Local Lemma to them. We use similar techniques to also promise that the
f
-cover of
Y
is indeed connected.

Moser and Tardos proved that there is a procedure to turn the existential proof based on Lovasz’s Local Lemma to a randomized algorithm that finds
Y
in polynomial time
[
MT10
]
. When the links of
X
are not too small, we can derandomize this algorithm using the work of
[
CGH13
]
.

As
Y
is obtained by removing faces from
X
, it is not surprising that
1
-skeletons of links of faces in
Y
are obtained by removing vertices and edges from links of faces in
X
. To show that these links still expand with high probability, we analyze random sparsifications of expander graphs with logarithmic degree, and show that with high enough probability a graph remains an expander after such a sparsification. To do so we use the Inverse Expander Mixing Lemma proven by Bilu and Linial
[
BL06
]
. This analysis is quite general and may be of independent interest.

1.6
Related works

Properties of high dimensional expanders were first used in the work of Garland
[
Gar73
]
, even before they were explicitly defined. Local spectral properties of links were used there to show that the real cohomology of a simplicial complex vanishes. Later on, a strong notion of coboundary expansion was discovered by Gromov
[
Gro10
]
, and also by Linial, Meshulam and Wallach
[
LM06
]
,
[
MW09
]
. The definition we use of high dimensional expanders is due to Dinur and Kaufman
[
DK17
]
, who also defined the one-sided version of high-dimensional expansion (before their work, a weaker notion of expansion in the links was used in e.g.
[
EK16
,
KKL14
]
). The first construction of bounded-degree high dimensional expanders was done before the definition we use today, by Lubotzky, Samuels and Vishne
[
LSV05
]
. A second algebraic construction was discovered by Kaufman and Oppenheim
[
KO18
,
KO21
]
. Recently,
[
OP22
]
construct new high dimensional expanders using the method of
[
KO18
]
with Chevalley groups. Friedgut and Iluz modified existing constructions for creating hyper-regular high dimensional expanders
[
FI20
]
. There have been combinatorial constructions of high dimensional expanders by Chapman, Linial and Peled
[
CLY20
]
, Liu, Mohanty and Yang
[
LMY20
]
and Golowich
[
Gol21
]
. However, the combinatorial constructions currently produce
\lambda
-HDXs for
\lambda\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
.

As mentioned above, there is a long line of works on random graph covers and their properties e.g.
[
AL02
,
Ami+01
,
Fri03
,
BL06
,
Pud15
,
MSS15
]
. This work lead to the breakthrough construction of bipartite Ramanujan graphs of all degrees by Marcus, Spielman and Srivastava. The works that study expansion usually focused on analysis of the eigenvalues of the covering graph. In higher dimension however, every connected cover is also a high dimensional expander. This fact is a corollary of a work of Oppenheim
[
Opp18
]
, which we discuss more precisely in
Section
2
. However, to analyze the links in our construction, we use the techniques developed by Bilu and Linial
[
BL06
]
towards analyzing random graph covers.

Dinur and Meshulam also studied on simplicial covers from a TCS perspective
[
DM19
]
. They study a topological property test on a class of simplicial complexes called
cosystolic expanders
. They show that if
X
is such a complex, then every surjective mapping
f:Y\to X
that satisfies almost all the local conditions of being a cover is close to a genuine cover
g:Y\to X
.

Random sparsification of graphs and their adjacency matrices is also a well studied problem, and there are known randomized and deterministic algorithms to sparsify graphs while keeping their spectral properties, e.g.
[
SS08
]
,
[
ST11
]
,
[
LS17
]
,
[
JS18
]
and
[
LXX20
]
. Furedi and Kolmos initiated the study of random sparsified matrices
[
FK81
]
(see also the work of Vu
[
Vu07
]
). A celebrated result by Spielman and Teng, studied sparsification of spectral expanders by randomly removing edges
[
ST11
]
. However, the probability of removing an edge in
[
ST11
]
varies according to the degrees of its vertices, and we could not use its analysis as is. Hence, we prove a graph sparsification result that is tailored for our needs.

1.7
Open questions

The construction we give in this paper is for two sided high dimensional expansion. Is there also an extension of our algorithm or our analysis that works on
one sided
high dimensional expanders
X
?

All constructions of bounded-degree high dimensional expanders with
\lambda<\frac{1}{2}
rely on group theory (either deterministic or randomized). Finding a combinatorical construction that does not rely on a group for arbitrarily small
\lambda>0
or even
\lambda=0.49
is an open problem. It is interesting to note that the theorem of Oppenheim mentioned above
[
Opp18
]
only applies for
\lambda<\frac{1}{2}
. This seems to show that
\lambda
-high dimensional expanders for
\lambda<\frac{1}{2}
must have some non-trivial structure.

Our construction has properties inherited by both the complex
X
, and the clique complex of
Cay(\Gamma,S)
. There are many other interesting properties that could be of interest in our construction. For example, one can repeat this construction with groups and generating sets so that the links of the clique complex of
Cay(\Gamma,S)
are
small set expanders
, but not expanders themselves. Will the resulting complexes be small set expanders (either locally or globally)? Another potentially interesting property is coboundary and cosystolic expansion. Will the resulting complexes be cosystolic expanders?

Our construction relies on infinite groups
\Gamma
that are residually finite and have expanding links. Besides the groups in
[
LSV05a
]
, we are not aware of groups that have these properties
2
2

2

Not including trivial constructions such as
(\Gamma\times\Gamma,S\times S)
.
. Finding new examples of such groups is interesting. The groups that were used in
[
KO18
,
KO21
]
are possible candidates, as they already showed potential for construction of high dimensional expanders.
Note that
[
Zuk03
]
showed that any such
\Gamma
has Kazhdan’s property-
T
. This already constrains the types of groups we can consider.
[
Gro03
]
defined a random model of groups that seems to have expanding links, but it is not clear whether they are residually finite or not. It will be interesting to understand if this model, or some variation to it, could produce new examples of groups useful for our construction.

Another open question that may have algorithmic applications, is embedding simplicial complexes in high dimensional expanders. That is, given a simplicial complex
X
, is it possible to embed it in a high dimensional-expander
X^{\prime}\supseteq X
, so that
|X^{\prime}|/|X|
is constant. For graphs this is easy, since given
G=(V,E)
, we can find an expander graph with the same vertex set
H=(V,E^{\prime})
and embed
G
in
G^{\prime}=(V,E\cup E^{\prime})
. This simple embedding in graphs is useful, for example, in the PCP-theorem proof by
[
Din06
]
. Understanding when we also have such an embedding in simplicial complexes could potentially lead to new algorithms for solving constraint satisfaction problems.

Finally,
[
Dik+18
]
generalized the notion of high dimensional-expanders to
expanding posets
. Another open direction of research is whether one could use topological covers to construct new expanding posets.

.

.

(Lovazs Local Lemma,
[

]
, algorithmic version by
[

]

.

and shows that
\ProbOp\left[{E_{i}}\right]\leqslant\frac{1}{e(R+1)}
, then this theorem promises us there exists an assignment so that
\ProbOp\left[{\neg E_{1}\wedge\neg E_{2}\wedge...\wedge\neg E_{n}}\right]>0
. Moreover, it gives us a polynomial time algorithm to find such an assignment to
X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{d}
.

2.2
Expander graphs

A graph
G=(V,E)
is a finite set
V
, the vertices, and
E\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
, the edges. Graphs in this paper are assumed to be with no isolated vertices. The graphs we consider have some probability distribution
\nu:E\to[0,1]
, and we assume henceforth that
\nu(e)\neq 0
for every edge
e\in E
. As a convention
\nu(e^{\prime})=0
for every
e^{\prime}\notin E
. Sometimes it is convenient to consider oriented edges
\overset{\to}{E}=\left\{(u,v),(v,u)\left|\;\vphantom{(u,v),(v,u)\{u,v\}\in E}\right.\{u,v\}\in E\right\}
. For an oriented edge we define
\nu(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\nu(\{u,v\})
.

The probabilities on edges give rise to a probability measure on
V
, i.e.
\nu(v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{w\sim v}\ProbOp\left[{\{v,w\}}\right]=\sum_{w\sim v}\nu(w,v)
. This probability measure defines an inner product on
\ell_{2}(V)
the space of real valued functions on the vertices
f,g:V\to\mathbb{R}
by

\langle f,g\rangle:=\E_{{v\in V}}\left[f(v)g(v)\right],

where the expectation is with respect to
\nu
. Every graph induces a random walk on its vertices; the transition probability from
v
to
u
is

\nu(u|v):=\frac{\nu(uv)}{\sum_{u^{\prime}\sim v}\nu(u^{\prime}v)}.

Denote by
A=A(G)
the Markov operator associated with this random walk. We associate
A
with the matrix indexed by
V\times V
where
A(v,u)=\nu(u|v)
. We call this operator the
normalized adjacency operator
(in this paper we will refer to it as the adjacency operator).
A
is an operator on real valued functions on the vertices, where

\forall v\in V\;Af(v)=\E_{{u\sim v}}\left[f(u)\right]\left(=\sum_{u\sim v}\nu(u|v)f(u).\right)

It is well known that
A
is self adjoint with respect to the inner product defined above. Its eigenvalues are in the interval
[-1,1]
. We denote its eigenvalues by
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant...\geqslant\lambda_{n}
(with multiplicities). The largest eigenvalue is always
\lambda_{1}=1
, and it is obtained by the constant function. The second eigenvalue is strictly less than
1
if and only if the graph is connected.

Definition 2.4

(spectral expanders)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
0\leqslant\lambda<1
.
G
is a
\lambda
-one sided spectral expander
, if

\lambda_{2}\leqslant\lambda.

G
is a
\lambda
-two sided spectral expander
, if

\max(\lvert\lambda_{2}\rvert,\lvert\lambda_{n}\rvert)\leqslant\lambda.

In this paper, when saying a graph is a
\lambda
-spectral expander, we mean it is a
\lambda
-
two sided
spectral expander.

2.2.1
Bipartite Graphs and Bipartite Expanders

A bipartite graph is a graph where the vertex set can be partitioned to two independent sets
V=L\mathbin{\mathaccent 0{\cdot}\cup}R
, called sides. We sometimes denote such a bipartite graph by
G=(L,R,E)
.

The Bipartite Adjacency Operator

In a bipartite graph, we view each side as a separate probability space, where for any
v\in L
(resp.
R
),
\nu(v)=\sum_{w\sim v}\nu(vw)
. We can define the
bipartite adjacency operator
as the operator
B:\ell_{2}(L)\to\ell_{2}(R)
by

\forall f\in\ell_{2}(L),v\in R,\;Bf(v)=\E_{{w\sim v}}\left[f(u)\right]

where the expectation is taken with respect to the probability space
L
, conditioned on being adjacent to
v
.
There is a similar operator
B^{*}:\ell_{2}(R)\to\ell_{2}(L)
as the bipartite operator for the opposite side. As the notation suggests,
B^{*}
is adjoint to
B
with respect to the inner products of
\ell_{2}(L),\ell_{2}(R)
.

We denote by
\lambda(B)
the spectral norm of
B
when restricted to
\ell_{2}^{0}(L)=\{{\mathbf{1}}\}^{\bot}
, the orthogonal complement of the constant functions (according to the inner product the measure induces). Namely

\lambda(B)=\sup\left\{\langle Bf,g\rangle\left|\;\vphantom{\langle Bf,g\rangle\lVert g\rVert,\lVert f\rVert=1,f\bot{\mathbf{1}}_{L}}\right.\lVert g\rVert,\lVert f\rVert=1,f\bot{\mathbf{1}}_{L}\right\}.

Definition 2.5

(Bipartite Expander)
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph, let
\lambda<1
. We say
G
is a
\lambda
-bipartite expander
, if

\lambda(B)\leqslant\lambda
.

It is easy to show that a bipartite graph is a
\lambda
-bipartite expander if and only if it is a
\lambda
-one sided spectral expander. So we use these terms interchangeably on bipartite graphs.

2.2.2
Expander Mixing Lemma

A classical result in expander graphs is the
expander mixing lemma
, that intuitively says that the weight of the edges between any two vertex sets
S,T\subset V
is proportionate to the probabilities of
S,T
. We denote by
E(S,T)
the edge set between
S
and
T
.

Lemma 2.6

(Expander Mixing Lemma)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
\lambda
-two sided spectral expanders. Then for any
S,T\subset V

\lvert\nu(E(S,T))-\nu(S)\nu(T)\rvert\leqslant\lambda\sqrt{\nu(S)\nu(T)(1-\nu(S))(1-\nu(T))}.

(2.1)

∎

Bipartite graphs have their own type of expander mixing lemma:

Lemma 2.7

(Bipartite Expander Mixing Lemma)
.

Let
G=(L,R,E)
be a
\lambda
-bipartite expander. Then for any
S\subset L,T\subset R

\lvert\nu(E(S,T))-\nu(S)\nu(T)\rvert\leqslant\lambda\sqrt{\nu(S)\nu(T)(1-\nu(S))(1-\nu(T))}.

(2.2)

∎

Bilu and Linial gave a converse to the expander mixing lemma:

Theorem 2.8

(
[
BL06
]
)
.

Let

be a normalized adjacency-operator of a bipartite graph
H^{\prime}=(A,B,E^{\prime})
. Assume that for all

it holds that

\lvert\nu(E(S,T))-\nu(S)\nu(T)\rvert\leqslant\alpha\sqrt{\nu(S)\nu(T)}.

Then
\lambda(A)\leqslant 260\alpha(1+\log_{2}(3/\alpha)).

The Theorem in
[

]
assumes

is regular. We show their proof extends to arbitrary weighted graphs in

.

2.4
Simplicial complexes and high dimensional expanders

We include here the basic definitions needed for our results. For a more comprehensive introduction to this topic we refer the reader to
[
DK17
]
and the references therein.

A simplicial complex is a hypergraph that is downward closed with respect to containment. It is called
d
-dimensional if the largest hyperedge has size
d+1
. We refer to
X(\ell)
as the hyperedges (also called faces) of size
\ell+1
.
X(0)
are the vertices. For a face
s
and
\ell\leqslant d
we denote by
deg_{\ell}(s)=\lvert\left\{t\in X(\ell)\left|\;\vphantom{t\in X(\ell)s\subseteq t}\right.s\subseteq t\right\}\rvert
.

It will sometimes be useful to consider oriented faces. We denote by

\overset{\to}{X}(\ell)=\left\{(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{\ell})\left|\;\vphantom{(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{\ell})\{v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{\ell}\}\in X(\ell)}\right.\{v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{\ell}\}\in X(\ell)\right\}.

We define a weighted simplicial complex. Suppose we have a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex
X
and a probability distribution
\nu:X(d)\to[0,1]
. We consider the following sampling for choosing lower dimensional faces:

1.

Choose some
d
-face
s_{d}=\{v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{d}\}\in X(d)
with probability
\nu(s_{d})
.

2.

Choose an ordering
\overset{\to}{s}_{d}=(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{d})
uniformly at random.

For any
(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{k})\in\overset{\to}{X}(k)
we denote by

\ProbOp\left[{(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{k})}\right]=\ProbOp_{{(u_{0},u_{1},...,u_{d})\in\overset{\to}{X}(d)}}\left[u_{0}=v_{0},u_{1}=v_{1},...,u_{k}=v_{k}\right],

the probability of sampling an ordered face so that
(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{k})
is its prefix. For an unordered face
s\in X(k)
, we denote by

\ProbOp\left[{s}\right]=\frac{1}{\binom{d+1}{k+1}}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in X(d),s^{\prime}\supseteq s}\nu(s^{\prime}).

This is the probability of sampling
(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{d})
so that
s=\{v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{k}\}
is the underlying set of the first
k
-vertices.

From here throughout the rest of the paper, when we refer to a simplicial complex
X
, we always assume that there is a probability measure on it constructed as above.

A link of a face in a simplicial complex, is a generalization of a neighbourhood of a vertex in a graph:

Definition 2.10

(link of a face)
.

Let
s\in X(k)
be some
k
-face. The
link
of
s
is a
d-(k+1)
-dimensional simplicial complex defined by:

X_{s}=\{t\backslash s:s\subseteq t\in X\}.

The associated probability measure
\nu_{X_{s}}
, for the link of
s
is defined to be the probability of sampling
t
in the process above given that we sampled
s
. It is proportional equal to
\nu_{X_{s}}(t)=\frac{\nu_{X}(t\cup s)}{\binom{|t|+|s|}{|s|}\nu(s)}
.

For an oriented face
(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{\ell})\in\overset{\to}{X}(\ell)
we denote by
X_{(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{\ell})}=X_{\{v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{\ell}\}}
the link of its underlying set. Let
(u_{0},u_{1},...,u_{\ell})\in\overset{\to}{X}(\ell)
, whose underlying set is
s=\{u_{0},u_{1},...,u_{\ell}\}
. Let
(v_{\ell+1},v_{\ell+2},...,v_{\ell+k+2})\in\overset{\to}{X}_{s}(k)
. Then it is easy to verify that

\ProbOp_{{\nu_{X_{s}}}}\left[(v_{\ell+1},v_{\ell+2},...,v_{\ell+k+2})\right]=\frac{\ProbOp_{{X}}\left[(u_{0},u_{1},...,u_{\ell},v_{\ell+1},v_{\ell+2},...,v_{\ell+k+2})\right]}{\ProbOp_{{X}}\left[(u_{0},u_{1},...,u_{\ell})\right]}.

Definition 2.11

(1-skeleton)
.

The
1
-skeleton
of a simplicial complex
X
with some probability measure as define above, is the graph whose vertices are
X(0)
and edges are
X(1)
, with (the restriction of) the probability measures of
X
to the vertices and edges.

We are ready to define our notion of high dimensional expanders.

Definition 2.12

(one-sided and two-sided high dimensional expander)
.

Let
0\leqslant\lambda<1
. A simplicial complex
X
is a
\lambda
-two sided link expander
(or
\lambda
-two sided HDX) if for every
-1\leqslant k\leqslant d-2
and every

, the underlying graph of the link

is a
\lambda
-two sided spectral expander.

Similarly,

is a
\lambda
-one sided link expander
(or
\lambda
-one sided HDX) if for every
-1\leqslant k\leqslant d-2
and every
s\in X(k)
, the underlying graph of the link
X_{s}
is a
\lambda
-one sided spectral expander.

When

is a graph, this definition coincides with the definition of a spectral expander.

A theorem by
[

]
shows that expansion “trickles down”, that is, expansion of links of

-faces imply expansion of links of

-faces (if they are connected).

Theorem 2.13

(Theorem 5.2 in
[

]
)
.

If for all

, then for any

, s.t.

2.5
Simplicial homomorphisms

Let
X,Y
be two pure
d
-dimensional simplicial complexes. A
simplicial homomorphism
is a function

so that every
k
-face

is mapped by

k
-face

. We say that

is a
non-degenerate

-coloring
if for every
d
-face

there exists some
d
-face

so that

.

A surjective
function
(not necessarily a coloring)

induces a measure
\mu_{f}
on

defined by the following sampling process:

1.

Sample a
d
-face
t\in Y(d)
.

2.

Sample a
d
-face
s\in X(d)
given that
f(s)=t
.

We note that by taking a subcomplex

that contains all faces in

that are in the support of

, then

is a non-degenerate coloring (we can also induce a measure on
X^{\prime}
so that for every
d
-face

\nu_{X}^{\prime}(s)=\nu_{X}(s)/\nu_{X}(X^{\prime}(d))
).

We say that two simplicial complexes

are

so that

if and only if

. We then denote

.

Simplicial homomorphisms and oriented faces

Let
\overset{\to}{s}=(v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{d})\in\overset{\to}{X}(d)
, whose underlying set
s=\{v_{0},v_{1},...,v_{d}\}
. Let
f(\overset{\to}{s})=(f(v_{0}),f(v_{1}),...,f(v_{d}))
and let
f(s)=\{f(v_{0}),f(v_{1}),...,f(v_{d})\}
.

its probability is

\ProbOp_{{\mu_{f}}}\left[\overset{\to}{s}\right]=\frac{1}{(d+1)!}\ProbOp_{{\mu_{f}}}\left[s\right]=\frac{1}{(d+1)!}\ProbOp_{{Y}}\left[f(s)\right]\ProbOp_{{s^{\prime}\in X(d)}}\left[s^{\prime}=s\left|\;\vphantom{s^{\prime}=sf(s^{\prime})=f(s)}\right.f(s^{\prime})=f(s)\right]

=\ProbOp_{{Y}}\left[f(\overset{\to}{s})\right]\ProbOp_{{s^{\prime}\in X(d)}}\left[s^{\prime}=s\left|\;\vphantom{s^{\prime}=sf(s^{\prime})=f(s)}\right.f(s^{\prime})=f(s)\right].

For every

so that

of

so that

so this is equal to

=\ProbOp_{{Y}}\left[f(\overset{\to}{s})\right]\ProbOp_{{\overset{\to}{s}^{\prime}\in\overset{\to}{X}(d)}}\left[\overset{\to}{s}^{\prime}=\overset{\to}{s}\left|\;\vphantom{\overset{\to}{s}^{\prime}=\overset{\to}{s}f(\overset{\to}{s}^{\prime})=f(\overset{\to}{s})}\right.f(\overset{\to}{s}^{\prime})=f(\overset{\to}{s})\right].

Hence we can also describe

via the sampling process of oriented faces:

1.

Sample an oriented
d
-face

.

2.

Sample an oriented
d
-face
\overset{\to}{s}\in\overset{\to}{X}(d)
given that

.

Bilu-Linial’s Converse Expander Mixing Lemma

In this appendix we give a variation of the converse expander mixing lemma given in

]
, that suits our purposes in

.

Theorem B.1

(
[
BL06
]
, Lemma 3.3)
.

Let
X
be a finite set and
\mu:X\to[0,1]
be a probability distribution on
X
. Let
V=L_{2}(X,\mu)
be its inner product space. Let
A:V\to V
be a self adjoint operator that satisfies:

1.

Non-laziness: for every
x\in X
,
\langle A{\mathbf{1}}_{x},{\mathbf{1}}_{x}\rangle=0
.

2.

For every
u\in\{-1,0,1\}^{X}
, and
v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{m}\subseteq\{-1,0,1\}^{X}
whose supports are pairwise disjoint, it holds that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert\langle u,Av_{j}\rangle\rvert\leqslant d\langle u,u\rangle.

3.

For every
S,T\subseteq X
with disjoint support,

\langle A{\mathbf{1}}_{S},{\mathbf{1}}_{T}\rangle\leqslant\alpha\lVert{\mathbf{1}}_{S}\rVert\lVert{\mathbf{1}}_{T}\rVert.

Then
\lVert A\rVert\leqslant 130(\alpha+\alpha\log_{2}(d/\alpha))
.

This version of Lemma 3.3 in
[
BL06
]
, deals with self-adjoint operators rather than real symmetric matrices which were in the original version.

Corollary

(Restatement of
Theorem
2.8
)
.

Let
A
be a normalized adjacency-operator of a bipartite graph
H^{\prime}=(A,B,E^{\prime})
. Assume that for all
S\subseteq A,T\subseteq B
it holds that

\lvert\nu(E(S,T))-\nu(S)\nu(T)\rvert\leqslant\alpha\sqrt{\nu(S)\nu(T)}.

Then
\lambda(A)\leqslant 260\alpha(1+\log_{2}(3/\alpha)).

Proof of
Theorem
2.8
.

Let
J:L_{2}(H^{\prime})\to L_{2}(H^{\prime})
be the operator of the complete (weighted) bipartite graph

J(f)(v)=\begin{cases}\E_{{u\in X}}\left[f(u)\right]&v\in Y\\
\E_{{u\in Y}}\left[f(u)\right]&v\in X\end{cases}.

Let
B=A-J
and let
v_{0}={\mathbf{1}}_{X\cup Y}
,
v_{n}={\mathbf{1}}_{X}-{\mathbf{1}}_{Y}
the trivial eigenvectors of
A
. One can verify that
Bv_{0}=Bv_{n}=0
. Furthermore, for every vector

orthogonal to

,

. Thus,
\lambda(A)=\lambda(B)=||B||
.

We note that
B
is self adjoint, and for every

,
\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{u},{\mathbf{1}}_{u}\rangle=0
. Moreover, for every
u\in\{0,1\}^{A\cup B}
and pairwise disjoint

we have that

\sum_{j}\lvert\langle Bu,v_{j}\rangle\rvert\leqslant\sum_{j}\lvert\langle Au,v_{j}\rangle\rvert+\lvert\langle Ju,v_{j}\rangle\rvert\leqslant 2\sum_{j}\langle u,v_{j}\rangle=2\lVert u\rVert^{2}.

In order to use
Theorem

, we bound
\lvert\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S},{\mathbf{1}}_{T}\rangle\rvert
for every

that are disjoint. Indeed, let

so that

\lvert\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S},{\mathbf{1}}_{T}\rangle\rvert=\lvert\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S_{1}},{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{2}}\rangle+\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S_{2}},{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{1}}\rangle\rvert\leqslant

\lvert\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S_{1}},{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{2}}\rangle\rvert+\lvert\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S_{2}},{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{1}}\rangle\rvert.

Note that
\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S_{1}},{\mathbf{1}}_{T_{2}}\rangle=\ProbOp\left[{E(S_{i},T_{j})}\right]-\ProbOp\left[{S_{i}}\right]\ProbOp\left[{T_{j}}\right]
hence we get the bound

\lvert\langle B{\mathbf{1}}_{S},{\mathbf{1}}_{T}\rangle\rvert\leqslant\alpha\sqrt{\ProbOp\left[{S_{1}}\right]\ProbOp\left[{T_{2}}\right]}+\sqrt{\ProbOp\left[{S_{2}}\right]\ProbOp\left[{T_{1}}\right]}\leqslant 2\alpha\sqrt{\ProbOp\left[{S}\right]\ProbOp\left[{T}\right]}=

2\alpha\lVert{\mathbf{1}}_{S}\rVert\lVert{\mathbf{1}}_{T}\rVert.

Hence, by

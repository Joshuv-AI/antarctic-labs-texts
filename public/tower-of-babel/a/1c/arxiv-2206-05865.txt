On-diagonal asymptotics for heat kernels of a class of inhomogeneous partial differential operators
Evan Randles, Laurent Saloff-Coste

Abstract
We consider certain constant-coefficient differential operators on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with positive-definite symbols. Each such operator Λ \Lambda with symbol P P defines a semigroup e − t ​ Λ e^{-t\Lambda} , t > 0 t>0 , admitting a convolution kernel H P t H^{t}_{P} for which the large-time behavior of H P t ​ ( 0 ) H_{P}^{t}(0) cannot be deduced by basic scaling arguments. The simplest example has symbol P ⁡ ( ξ ) = ( η + ζ 2 ) 2 + η 4 P(\xi)=(\eta+\zeta^{2})^{2}+\eta^{4} , ξ = ( η , ζ ) ∈ ℝ 2 \xi=(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2} . We devise a method to establish large-time asymptotics of H P t ​ ( 0 ) H^{t}_{P}(0) for several classes of examples of this type and we show that these asymptotics are preserved by perturbations by certain higher-order differential operators. For the P P just given, it turns out that H P t ( 0 ) ∼ c P t − 5 / 8 H^{t}_{P}(0)\sim c_{P}t^{-5/8} as t → ∞ t\to\infty . We show how such results are relevant to understand the convolution powers of certain complex functions on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . Our work represents a first basic step towards a good understanding of the semigroups associated with these operators. Obtaining meaningful off-diagonal upper bounds for H P t H_{P}^{t} remains an interesting challenge.

We say that
\{T_{t}\}
is non-expanding if

\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\|T_{t}\|<\infty.

Thanks to the continuity of
T:(0,\infty)\to\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, every contracting group is non-expanding.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}
be a continuous one-parameter group generated by
E\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
\{T_{t}\}
is contracting, then
\tr E>0
. If
\{T_{t}\}
is non-expanding, then
\tr E\geq 0
.

Proof.

By virtue of the continuity of the determinant map and the fact that
\operatorname{det}(t^{E})=t^{\tr E}
, we have

\lim_{t\to 0}t^{\tr E}=\lim_{t\to 0}\operatorname{det}(t^{E})=\operatorname{det}(0)=0

provided
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting. In this case, it follows that
\tr E>0
. If
\{t^{E}\}
is expanding with
M=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\|t^{E}\|
, we have

\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t^{\tr E}=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\operatorname{det}(t^{E})\leq\sup_{\|A\|\leq M}\absolutevalue{\det(A)}<\infty

because
\{A\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|A\|\leq M\}
is a compact set. Thus
\tr E\geq 0
.
∎

Given a function
P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and
E\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we shall say that
P
is homogeneous with respect to
E
if

tP(\xi)=P(t^{E}\xi)

for all
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; in this case we say that
E
is a member of the exponent set of
P
and write
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
. Central to the definition of positive homogeneous function given below is the following characterization taken from
[
BR22
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous, positive-definite (in the sense that
P\geq 0
and
P(\xi)=0
only when
\xi=0
), and have
\operatorname{Exp}(P)\neq\varnothing
. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

The so-called unital level set of
P
,

S_{P}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\absolutevalue{P(\xi)}=P(\xi)=1\},

is compact.

2.

There is a positive number
M
for which
P(\xi)>1
whenever
\absolutevalue{\xi}>M
.

3.

For each
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
,
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting.

4.

There exists
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
for which
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting.

5.

We have

\lim_{\absolutevalue{\xi}\to\infty}P(\xi)=\infty.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous, positive definite and have
\operatorname{Exp}(P)\neq\varnothing
. If any one
(and hence all) of the equivalent conditions in Proposition
2.3
are fulfilled, we say that
P
is positive homogeneous. We will also say that
P
is a positive homogeneous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The following proposition amasses some basic facts about positive homogeneous functions and its proof can be found in Section 2 of
[
BR22
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
P
be a positive homogeneous function and denote by
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
the set of
O\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which
P(O\xi)=P(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We have

1.

\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is a compact subgroup of
\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.

For each
E,\widetilde{E}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
, we have

\tr E=\tr\widetilde{E}>0.

In view of the preceding proposition, we define
the homogeneous order of
P
to be the unique positive number
\mu_{P}
for which

\mu_{P}=\tr E

for all
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
.

Example 1
:

For any
\alpha>0
, the map
\xi\mapsto\absolutevalue{\xi}^{\alpha}
is a positive homogeneous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Indeed, it is evident that it is continuous and positive-definite and its unital level set is precisely the unit sphere
S_{\absolutevalue{\cdot}^{\alpha}}=\mathbb{S}_{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Further,

\operatorname{Exp}(\absolutevalue{\cdot}^{\alpha})=\frac{1}{\alpha}I+\mathfrak{o}_{d}

where
I
is the identity map on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathfrak{o}_{d}
is the Lie algebra of the orthogonal group
\mbox{O}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is characterized by the set of skew symmetric matrices.
∎

Example 2
:

Given a
d
-tuple of positive even integers
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{d})
, we consider a polynomial of the form

P(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha:\mathbf{m}|=1}a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}

(8)

for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
where, for each multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
\xi^{\alpha}:=\xi_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\xi_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\xi_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
and

|\alpha:\mathbf{m}|:=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{m_{k}}.

A polynomial of the form (
8
) is said to be
semi-elliptic
provided
P(\xi)
vanishes only at
\xi=0
. Appearing in L. Hörmander’s treatise on linear partial differential operators
[
Hö83
]
, semi-elliptic polynomials are the symbols of a class of hypoelliptic partial differential operators, called semi-elliptic operators. For a semi-elliptic polynomial
P(\xi)
of the form (
8
), its corresponding semi-elliptic operator is the constant-coefficient linear partial differential operator given by

P(D)=\sum_{|\alpha:\mathbf{m}|=1}a_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}

where we have written
D=(-i\partial_{x_{1}},-i\partial_{x_{2}},\dots,-i\partial_{x_{d}})
and, for each multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
D^{\alpha}=(-i\partial_{x_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}(-i\partial_{x_{2}})^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots(-i\partial_{x_{d}})^{\alpha_{d}}
. For a polynomial
P(\xi)
of the form (
8
), observe that, for
E\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with standard matrix representation
\operatorname{diag}(1/m_{1},1/m_{2},\dots,1/m_{d})
,

P(t^{E}\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha:\mathbf{m}|=1}a_{\alpha}(t^{1/m_{1}}\xi_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}(t^{1/m_{2}}\xi_{2})^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots(t^{1/m_{d}}\xi_{d})^{\alpha_{d}}=\sum_{|\alpha:\mathbf{m}|=1}a_{\alpha}t^{|\alpha:\mathbf{m}|}\xi^{\alpha}=tP(\xi)

for all
t>0
and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
and, because
\{t^{E}\}
is clearly contracting, Proposition
2.3
guarantees that
P
is positive homogeneous whenever it is positive-definite. Thus, whenever a semi-elliptic polynomial
P
of the form (
8
) is positive-definite, it is positive homogeneous with homogeneous order

\mu_{P}=\tr E=|\mathbf{1}:\mathbf{m}|=\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{m_{d}}.

For two concrete examples, consider

P_{1}(\xi)=\xi_{1}^{4}+\xi_{2}^{6}\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274ptP_{2}(\xi)=\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{1}\xi^{2}+\xi_{2}^{4}

defined for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. These are positive homogeneous semi-elliptic polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with homogeneous order
\mu_{P_{1}}=1/4+1/6=5/12
and
\mu_{P_{2}}=1/2+1/4=3/4
, respectively.
∎

Before we conclude our treatment of positive homogeneous functions and turn our attention to multivariate homogeneous functions, we present the following lemma which is used several times throughout the course of this paper. Its proof makes use of the generalized polar-coordinate integration formula developed and presented in Theorem 1.4 of
[
BR22
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
P
be a positive homogeneous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with relatively compact unit ball
B_{P}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:P(\xi)<1\}
and homogeneous order
\mu_{P}>0
. Then, for each
\epsilon>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\epsilon P(\xi)}\,d\xi=m(B_{P})\frac{\Gamma(\mu_{P}+1)}{\epsilon^{\mu_{P}}}

where
m(B_{P})
denoted the Lebesgue measure of
B_{P}
. In particular, for each
\epsilon>0
,
\exp(-\epsilon P)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By an appeal to Theorem 1.5 of
[
BR22
]
, we obtain a Borel measure
\sigma=\sigma_{P}
on
S=S_{P}
for which
\sigma(S)=\mu_{P}\cdot m(B_{P})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\epsilon P(\xi)}\,d\xi=\int_{S}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon P(t^{E}\eta)}t^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dt\,\sigma(d\eta)=\int_{S}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon t}t^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dt\,\sigma(d\eta)

for any
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
. Thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\epsilon P(\xi)}\,d\xi=\sigma(S)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon t}t^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dt=m(B_{P})\frac{\mu_{P}}{\epsilon^{\mu_{P}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}s^{\mu_{P}-1}\,ds=m(B_{P})\frac{\Gamma(\mu_{P}+1)}{\epsilon^{\mu_{P}}}

where we have used the Laplace-transform representation of Euler’s Gamma function and the property that
\mu_{P}\cdot\Gamma(\mu_{P})=\Gamma(\mu_{P}+1)
.
∎

We now introduce a generalization of the notion of positive homogeneous function. Given non-negative integers
a
and
b
and a function
Q:\mathbb{R}^{b}\to\mathbb{R}^{a}
, we say that
Q
is
nondegenerate
if
Q(\zeta)\neq 0
whenever
\zeta\neq 0
. Given a pair
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
, we say that
Q
is homogeneous with respect to the pair
(E,E^{\prime})
provided that

t^{E}Q(\zeta)=Q(t^{E^{\prime}}\zeta)

for all
t>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
. Akin to Proposition
2.3
, we have the following:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{{b}}\to\mathbb{R}^{a}
be continuous, nondegenerate, and homogeneous with respect to some pair
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
for which
\{t^{E}\}_{t>0}
is contracting. Then, the following are equivalent:

1.

For any compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{a}
,
Q^{-1}(K)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
is compact.

2.

The set

S_{Q}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{{b}}:\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}=1\}

is compact.

3.

There is a number
M>0
for which
\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}>1
for all
|\zeta|>M
.

4.

For each pair
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
for which
Q
is homogeneous and
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting,
\{t^{E^{\prime}}\}
must also be contracting.

5.

There exists a pair
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
for which
Q
is homogeneous and
\{t^{E}\}
and
\{t^{E^{\prime}}\}
are both contracting.

6.

We have

\lim_{\absolutevalue{\zeta}\to\infty}\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}=\infty.

Proof.

(
1
\,\Rightarrow
2
).

Since
S_{Q}=Q^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{a})
where
\mathbb{S}_{a}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{a}
, this is immediate.
∎

(
2
\,\Rightarrow
3
).

Assuming that
S_{Q}
is compact, let
M>0
be such that
\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}\neq 1
for all
\absolutevalue{\zeta}>M
. Denote by
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{M}}
the closed ball in
\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
with center
0
and radius
M
and by
\mathcal{O}_{M}=\mathbb{R}^{{b}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}_{M}}
its complement. Let us first treat the situation in which
{b}>1
. In this case, the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{M}
is path connected and
Q(\zeta)
is continuous ensures that there cannot be two elements
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
in
\mathcal{O}_{M}
with
\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta_1)}<1<\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta_2)}
for otherwise the intermediate value theorem would imply that
\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta_3)}=1
for some
\zeta_{3}\in\mathcal{O}_{M}
, an impossibility. Thus, to prove the statement, we simply need to rule out the possibility that
\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}<1
for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{O}_{M}
. Let us assume, to reach a contradiction, that
\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}<1
for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{O}_{M}
. Let
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
be a pair for which
Q
is homogeneous and
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting and let
\zeta_{0}
be a non-zero element in
\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
. The nondegenerateness of
Q
and the fact that
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting guarantees that

\infty=\lim_{t\to\infty}\absolutevalue{t^EQ(\zeta_0)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\absolutevalue{Q(t^{E'}\zeta_0)}.

In view of our hypothesis it follows that, for all sufficiently large
t
,
t^{E^{\prime}}\zeta_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{B}_{M}}
. Of course, this implies that
\zeta\mapsto\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}
is unbounded on the compact set
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{M}}
and this is impossible for we know that
Q
is continuous.

In the case that
{b}=1
, we first argue that
Q(\zeta)>1
for all
\zeta>M>0
. Of course, since
(M,\infty)
is connected, an argument analogous to that above for
{b}>1
guarantees that it suffices to rule out the case that
Q(\zeta)<1
for all
\zeta>M
. We therefore assume, to reach a contradiction, that
Q(\zeta)<1
for all
\zeta>M
and select a pair
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
for which
Q
is homogeneous and
\{t^{E}\}
contracting. Due to the simplicity of
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})=\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R})
,
E^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}I
for some
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and so
t^{E^{\prime}}\zeta=t^{\alpha^{\prime}}\zeta
for all
t>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
. Using the fact that
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting, we have

\infty=\lim_{t\to\infty}\absolutevalue{t^{E}Q(1)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\absolutevalue{Q(t^{\alpha'})}

and so, in view of our supposition, it follows that
0<t^{\alpha^{\prime}}\leq M
for all sufficiently large
t
(which, at the same time guarantees that
\alpha^{\prime}<0
). This, however, contradicts that fact that
Q
is continuous at
0
. Hence,
Q(\zeta)>1
for all
\zeta>M
. A similar argument shows that
Q(\zeta)>1
for all
\zeta<-M
. Thus
Q(\zeta)>1
for all
\absolutevalue{\zeta}>M
, as was asserted.
∎

(
3
\,\Rightarrow
4
).

We prove the contrapositive statement. Let us assume that there is a pair
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
for which
Q
is homogeneous and
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting, but
\{t^{E^{\prime}}\}
is not contracting. It follows that, for some
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
and
\epsilon>0
, there is a sequence
t_{k}\to 0
for which

\absolutevalue{t_k^{E'}\zeta}\geq\epsilon

for all
k
. In the case that
\{t_{k}^{E^{\prime}}\zeta\}
is bounded, we assume without loss of generality (by passing to a subsequence, if needed) that
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{E^{\prime}}\zeta=\zeta_{0}
where
\absolutevalue{\zeta_0}\geq\epsilon
. Consequently,

Q(\zeta_{0})=\lim_{k\to\infty}Q(t_{k}^{E^{\prime}}\zeta)=\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{E}Q(\zeta)=0

since
t_{k}\to 0
and
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting. Since
\zeta_{0}\neq 0
, this is impossible for we know that
Q
is nondegenerate. Thus, we must conclude that
\{t_{k}^{E^{\prime}}\zeta\}
is unbounded. Without loss of generality, we may assume (by passing to a subsequence, if necessary) that
\zeta_{k}=t_{k}^{E^{\prime}}\zeta\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Consequently, there is a sequence
\zeta_{k}\to\infty
for which

\lim_{k\to\infty}Q(\zeta_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}^{E}Q(\zeta)=0.

This shows that Item
3
cannot hold, as was asserted.
∎

(
4
\,\Rightarrow
5
).

In view of the hypotheses, this is immediate.
∎

(
5
\,\Rightarrow
6
).

We fix a pair
(E,E^{\prime})\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})\times\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
for which
Q
is homogeneous and both
\{t^{E}\}
and
\{t^{E^{\prime}}\}
are contracting. Let
\{\zeta_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
be a sequence with
\zeta_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Since
\{t^{E^{\prime}}\}
is contracting and in view of Proposition A.5 of
[
BR22
]
, we can write
\zeta_{k}=t_{k}^{E^{\prime}}\eta_{k}
where
\absolutevalue{\eta_k}=1
for each
k
and
t_{k}\to\infty
. We claim that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\absolutevalue{t_k^EQ(\eta_k)}=\infty.

To see this, we assume, to reach a contradiction, that a subsequence has the property that
\absolutevalue{t_{k_j}^EQ(\eta_{k_j})}\leq M
for some
M
. In this case, we see that

\absolutevalue{Q(\eta_{k_j})}=\absolutevalue{(1/t_{k_j})^Et_{k_j}^EQ(\eta_{k_j)}}\leq M\|(1/t_{k_{j}})^{E}\|

for all
j
and, since
1/t_{k_{j}}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, the fact that
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting implies that

0=\inf_{\absolutevalue{\zeta}=1}\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta)}.

This is, however, impossible because
Q
is continuous and nonvanishing on the compact unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
. We have therefore substantiated our claim and so it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\absolutevalue{Q(\zeta_k)}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\absolutevalue{Q(t_k^{E'}\eta_k)}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\absolutevalue{t_k^EQ(\eta_k)}=\infty,

as desired.
∎

(
6
\,\Rightarrow
1
).

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{a}
be compact. The fact that
Q
is continuous ensures that
Q^{-1}(K)
is necessarily closed. If
Q^{-1}(K)
were unbounded, we could find a sequence
\zeta_{k}\in Q^{-1}(K)
for which
\absolutevalue{\zeta_k}\to\infty
yet
Q(\zeta_{k})\in K
for all
k
. Of course, this is impossible in light of our assumption. Hence

must be bounded and therefore compact in view of the Heine-Borel theorem.
∎

∎

Definition 2.8
.

Let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{{b}}\to\mathbb{R}^{a}

For a given positive integer
\alpha
, consider

∎

\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{a})
. Since
\{t^{E}\}
and
\{t^{E^{\prime}}\}
are contracting, we conclude that
Q
is nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous in view of Proposition
2.7
.

In the case that
a=1
,
\alpha_{1}=1
, and
q_{1}(\zeta)=P(\zeta)
is a positive homogeneous function, the above conditions are automatically satisfied for any
E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
. From this we conclude that positive homogeneous functions are nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous. We remark that, for any
E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
,
Q(\zeta)=q_{1}(\zeta)=P(\zeta)
is homogeneous with respect to the pair
(I,E^{\prime})
where
I
is the identity on
\mathbb{R}
.

For a concrete example of a multivariate homogeneous function of the form (

) (and for which
1<a<{b}
), consider
Q:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by

for
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This can be written equivalently as

for
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This is clearly of the form (

) with

,
q_{2}(\zeta)=\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{3}^{3}
,
\alpha_{1}=2
and
\alpha_{2}=5
. In this case, it is easy to see that

and

satisfy Condition

. Further, observe that, for

. Since

where

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

Proposition 2.9
.

Suppose that
P
is a positive homogeneous function on
\mathbb{R}^{a}
,
Q:\mathbb{R}^{{b}}\to\mathbb{R}^{a}
is a nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous function and, for some
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
and
E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
,
Q
is homogeneous with respect to the pair
(E,E^{\prime})
. Then
P\circ Q
and
P\circ(-Q)
are positive homogeneous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
whose exponent sets contain
E^{\prime}
. In particular,
\mu_{P\circ Q}=\mu_{P\circ(-Q)}=\tr E^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
Q
is nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous if and only if
-Q
is nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous and
Q
is homogeneous with respect to a pair
(E,E^{\prime})
if and only if
-Q
is. Thus, to prove the proposition, it suffices to prove that
P\circ Q
is positive homogeneous with
E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P\circ Q)
. Because
P
is continuous and positive-definite and
Q
is continuous and nondegenerate, it evident that
P\circ Q
is continuous and positive-definite. Given the pair
(E,E^{\prime})
as in the statement of the proposition, we observe that

(P\circ Q)(t^{E^{\prime}}\zeta)=P(t^{E}Q(\zeta))=tP(Q(\zeta))=t(P\circ Q)(\zeta)

for all
t>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
. Thus,
E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P\circ Q)
. Finally, since
\{t^{E}\}
is contracting thanks to Proposition
2.3
,
\{t^{E^{\prime}}\}
must also be contracting in view or Proposition
2.7
and so we conclude that
P\circ Q
is positive homogeneous in light of Proposition
2.3
.
∎

3
On-Diagonal Asymptotics

Theorem 3.1
.

Given positive integers
{a}
and
{b}
, let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be positive homogeneous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{{a}}
with homogeneous orders
\mu_{P_{1}}
and
\mu_{P_{2}}
, respectively, and let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{{b}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{a}}
be a
C^{1}
function which is nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous. Set
d={a}+{b}
and define
P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by

P(\xi)=P(\eta,\zeta)=P_{1}(\eta+Q(\zeta))+P_{2}(\eta)

for
\xi=(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{{a}}\times\mathbb{R}^{{b}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that there exist
E_{1}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P_{1})
,
E_{2}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P_{2})
, and
F_{1},F_{2}\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
for which the following conditions hold:

1.

For
k=1,2
,
Q
is homogeneous with respect to the pair
(E_{k},F_{k})
.

2.

We have
[E_{1},E_{2}]=E_{1}E_{2}-E_{2}E_{1}=0
and
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is non-expanding.

In particular,
P_{1}\circ Q
and
P_{2}\circ(-Q)
are positive homogeneous on
\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
(by virtue of Proposition
2.9
) with homogeneous orders
\mu_{P_{1}\circ Q}
and
\mu_{P_{2}\circ(-Q)}
, respectively. Then the heat kernel

H_{P}^{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-tP(\xi)}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi

exists for each
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Upon setting
\varphi(t):=H_{P}^{t}(0)
for
t>0
, we have the following on-diagonal asymptotics:

\varphi(t)\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-\mu_{0}}&0<t\leq 1\\
t^{-\mu_{\infty}}&1\leq t<\infty\end{cases}

for
t>0
where
\mu_{0}=\mu_{P_{2}}+\mu_{P_{1}\circ Q}
and
\mu_{\infty}=\mu_{P_{1}}+\mu_{P_{2}\circ(-Q)}
.

Remark 1
:

In view of Propositions
2.5
and
2.9
,
\mu_{P_{1}}=\tr E_{1}
,
\mu_{P_{2}}=\tr E_{2}
,
\mu_{P_{1}\circ Q}=\tr F_{1}
, and
\mu_{P_{2}\circ(-Q)}=\tr F_{2}
where
E_{1},E_{2},F_{1},F_{2}
are those given in the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
(or any which satisfy Conditions
1
and
2
). In these terms, the asymptotics for
\varphi(t)
can be equivalently written

\varphi(t)\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-\left(\tr E_{2}+\tr F_{1}\right)}&0<t\leq 1\\
t^{-\left(\tr E_{1}+\tr F_{2}\right)}&1\leq t<\infty\end{cases}

for
t>0
.

Remark 2
:

Given our hypothesis that
Q
is nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous, when we ask that
Q
be homogeneous with respect to the pair
(E_{1},F_{1})
for some
E_{1}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P_{1})
and
F_{1}\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{{b}})
, we are ensuring that
\{t^{F_{1}}\}
is contracting by virtue of Proposition
2.7
since it is known that
\{t^{E_{1}}\}
is contracting thanks to Proposition
2.3
. For the same reason,
\{t^{F_{2}}\}
must also be contracting. Of course, these observations also follow from Proposition
2.9
since
F_{1}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P_{1}\circ Q)
and
F_{2}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P_{2}\circ(-Q))
.

In this direction, if Condition
1
were adjusted to include the hypothesis that, for
F_{1}
(or
F_{2}
),
\{t^{F_{1}}\}
(or
\{t^{F_{2}}\}
) is contracting, then the initial hypothesis could be weakened to ask only that
Q:\mathbb{R}^{{a}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be nondegenerate and
C^{1}
. In this case, the modified Condition
1
would ensure that
Q
were nondegenerate multivariate homogeneous by virtue of Item
5
of Proposition
2.7
.

Remark 3
:

If Condition
2
were replaced by the stronger condition that
[E_{1},E_{2}]=E_{1}E_{2}-E_{2}E_{1}=0
and
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is contracting, then one finds that the small and large-time asymptotics
t^{-\mu_{0}}
and
t^{-\mu_{\infty}}
are “true asymptotics” in the sense that the limits
\lim_{t\to 0}t^{\mu_{0}}\varphi(t)
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\mu_{\infty}}\varphi(t)
both exist and are positive numbers. This result is presented in Theorem
3.4
. The theorem, in fact, gives us the precise value of these limits in terms of
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
, and
Q
.

Example 7
:

Let

and

be even positive integers with

and let

Our proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be positive homogeneous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{{a}}
. If there exist
E_{1}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P_{1})
and
E_{2}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P_{2})
for which
[E_{1},E_{2}]=0
and
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is non-expanding, then the following statements hold:

1.

There are positive constants
C,C^{\prime},M,M^{\prime}
for which

C(P_{1}(\xi)+P_{2}(\eta))-M\leq P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)+P_{2}(\eta)\leq C^{\prime}(P_{1}(\xi)+P_{2}(\eta))+M^{\prime}

(11)

for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{{a}}
and
0<t\leq 1
.

2.

There are positive constants
C,C^{\prime},M,M^{\prime}
for which

C(P_{1}(\eta)+P_{1}(\xi))-M\leq P_{1}(\eta)+P_{2}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)\leq C^{\prime}(P_{2}(\eta)+P_{2}(\xi))+M^{\prime}

(12)

for all
\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{{a}}
and
0<t\leq 1
.

As the reader will see, this lemma is fundamental to our work throughout the article and, in addition to its appearance in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, it is used essentially in the proofs of Theorems
4.1
and
5.1
. For the aid of the reader, we find it useful to state this result in the context of our introductory example where
P(\eta,\zeta)=(\eta+\zeta^{2})^{2}+\eta^{4}
. In this case (using
\xi=Q(\zeta)=\zeta^{2}
), the lemma gives the following estimates.

1.

There are positive constants
C,\,C^{\prime},\,M,\,M^{\prime}
for which

C(\zeta^{4}+\eta^{4})-M\leq(t^{1/4}\eta+\zeta^{2})^{2}+\eta^{4}\leq C^{\prime}(\zeta^{4}+\eta^{4})+M^{\prime}

for all
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<t\leq 1
.

2.

There are positive constants
C,\,C^{\prime},\,M,\,M^{\prime}
for which

C(\eta^{2}+\zeta^{4})-M\leq\eta^{2}+(t^{1/4}\eta-\zeta^{2})^{4}\leq C^{\prime}(\eta^{4}+\zeta^{8})+M^{\prime}

for all
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<t\leq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We first treat the
t\leq 1
behavior. Using the homogeneity of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, we observe that

tP(\eta,\zeta)=P_{1}\left(t^{E_{1}}\eta+t^{E_{1}}Q(\zeta)\right)+P_{2}(t^{E_{2}}\eta)=P_{1}\left(t^{E_{1}}\eta+Q(t^{F_{1}}\zeta)\right)+P_{2}(t^{E_{2}}\eta)

for all
t>0
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{{a}}
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
. By making the change of variables
(\eta,\zeta)\mapsto(t^{-E_{2}}\eta,t^{-F_{1}}\zeta)
, we see that

\varphi(t)=H_{P}^{t}(0)=\frac{t^{-(\tr E_{2}+\tr F_{1})}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\left(P_{1}\left(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+Q(\zeta)\right)+P_{2}(\eta)\right)}\,d\eta\,d\zeta

(13)

for
t>0
. By virtue of Lemma
3.3
, we find that there are positive constants
C,C^{\prime},M,M^{\prime}
for which

-M^{\prime}-C^{\prime}(P_{1}(Q(\zeta))+P_{2}(\eta))\leq-P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+Q(\zeta))-P_{2}(\eta)\leq M-C(P_{1}(Q(\zeta))+P_{2}(\eta))

(14)

for all
t\leq 1
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{{a}}
, and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{{b}}
. Upon noting that
(\eta,\zeta)\mapsto P_{1}(Q(\zeta))+P_{2}(\eta)
is a positive homogeneous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in view of Proposition
2.9
, it follows from Lemma
2.6
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\epsilon(P_{1}(Q(\zeta))+P_{2}(\eta))}\,d\eta\,d\zeta

is a positive finite number for each
\epsilon>0
. With this in mind, the inequality (
14
) guarantees constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
for which

C\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-P_{1}\left(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+Q(\zeta)\right)}e^{-P_{2}(\eta)}\,d\eta\,d\zeta\leq C^{\prime}

for all
0<t\leq 1
, and from (
13
) we conclude that

\varphi(t)\asymp t^{-(\tr E_{2}+\tr F_{1})}=t^{-(\mu_{P_{2}}+\mu_{P_{1}\circ Q})}

for
t\leq 1
.

On the other hand, establishing the
t\geq 1
asymptotics is more difficult. A first hope would be to introduce the change of variables
(\eta,\zeta)\mapsto(t^{-E_{1}}\eta,t^{-F_{1}}\zeta)
and find that

\varphi(t)=\frac{t^{-(\tr E_{1}+\tr F_{1})}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-P_{1}(\eta+Q(\zeta))-P_{2}(t^{E_{2}-E_{1}}\eta)}\,d\eta\,d\zeta

for
t>0
. This is problematic, however. To see this, let us assume momentarily that
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is contracting and observe that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-P_{1}(\eta+Q(\zeta))-P_{2}(t^{E_{2}-E_{1}}\eta)}\,d\eta\,d\zeta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-P_{1}(\eta+Q(\eta))}\,d\eta\,d\zeta=\infty

by virtue of Fatou’s lemma and the fact that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-P_{1}(\eta+Q(\zeta))}\,d\eta\,d\zeta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-P_{1}(\eta^{\prime})}\,d\eta^{\prime}\,d\zeta^{\prime}=\infty.

Consequently, in the case that
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is contracting, the only information found by this argument is that
\varphi(t)
decays more slowly than
t^{-(\tr E_{1}+\tr F_{1})}=t^{-(\mu_{P_{1}}+\mu_{P_{1}\circ Q})}
as
t\to\infty
.

Instead, we return to the assumption that
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is non-expanding and make the non-linear change of variables
(\eta,\zeta)\mapsto(\eta-Q(\zeta),\zeta)
. Denoting this transformation by
T
, we find that

DT(\eta,\zeta)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{{a}}&-D_{\zeta}Q\\
0&I_{{b}}\end{pmatrix}

where

4
A perturbation theory

Let us take the classical viewpoint that the theory of elliptic/semi-elliptic operators is a “perturbation theory” in which a sufficiently well-behaved partial differential operator is perturbed by adding operators whose order is lower than that of the given operator. In that setting, one may investigate properties of solutions to related partial differential equations which are preserved under such perturbations. For example, in the theory of elliptic operators, short-time heat kernel estimates for a uniformly elliptic operator are determined by the operator’s principal symbol. In this way, perturbation by lower-order operators – provided they are sufficiently well behaved – will not essentially change the short-time behavior of heat kernels. In this short section, we explore perturbation by higher-order operators/symbols. In particular, we show that, under certain conditions, the large-time decay of
\varphi(t)=H_{P}^{t}(0)
is essentially unchanged when
P
is replaced with
P+R
where
R(\xi)=o(P(\xi))
as
\xi\to 0
. Given that our analysis is done exclusively in the frequency domain, our results amount, essentially, to a perturbation theory of constant-coefficient operators. We suspect that a successful variable-coefficient theory is possible, however, we do not pursue that here. Our main result is as follows.

.

Let

satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1

6
Discussion

6.1
Another perspective

As we noted in the introduction and demonstrated in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, key to the analysis in this paper is the observation that

\varphi(t)=H_{P}^{t}(0)=H_{\widetilde{P}}^{t}(0)=\widetilde{\varphi}(t)

for all
t>0
where
\widetilde{P}=P\circ T
can be seen, in some sense, as a “dual” symbol to
P
. In terms of our motivating example in which
P(\xi)=(\eta+\zeta^{2})^{2}+\eta^{4}
and

\Lambda=\partial_{x_{1}}^{4}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{4}+2i\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2},

\widetilde{P}(\xi)=\eta^{2}+(\eta-\zeta^{2})^{4}
for
\xi=(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and corresponds to the constant coefficient operator

\widetilde{\Lambda}=-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{x_{1}}^{4}+4i\partial_{x_{1}}^{3}\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}-6\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}\partial_{x_{2}}^{4}-4i\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{6}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{8}.

Though the operator
\Lambda
is a self-adjoint fourth-order elliptic operator and is therefore well understood,
\widetilde{\Lambda}
is mysterious and somewhat poorly behaved as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 6.1
.

The operator
\widetilde{\Lambda}
with symbol
\widetilde{P}=\eta^{2}+(\eta-\zeta^{2})^{4}
is not hypoelliptic. Still,
-\widetilde{\Lambda}
generates a semigroup
\{e^{-t\widetilde{\Lambda}}\}_{t>0}
with heat kernel given by

H_{\widetilde{P}}^{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-t\widetilde{P}(\xi)}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi

defined for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This heat kernel has

\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=H_{\widetilde{P}}^{t}(0)\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-1/2}&0<t\leq 1\\
t^{-5/8}&t\geq 1\end{cases}

for
t>0
and, further,

\lim_{t\to 0}t^{1/2}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma(5/4)^{2}\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{5/8}\widetilde{\varphi}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{3/2}}\Gamma(9/8).

Proof.

The statements concerning
H_{\widetilde{P}}
and
\widetilde{\varphi}
follow directly from our analysis in Examples
7
and
9
through the identity
\varphi=\widetilde{\varphi}
. Thus, it remains to prove that
\widetilde{\Lambda}
is not hypoelliptic. To see this, we compute

\partial^{4}_{\zeta}\widetilde{P}(\eta,\zeta)=384\zeta^{4}-1152\zeta^{2}(\eta-\zeta^{2})+144(\zeta-\zeta^{2})^{2}

to see that

\lim_{\zeta\to\infty}\frac{\partial^{4}_{\zeta}\widetilde{P}(\zeta^{2},\zeta)}{\widetilde{P}(\zeta^{2},\zeta)}=384.

Thus, for the multi-index
\alpha=(0,4)
,

\lim_{|\xi|\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha)}(\xi)}{\widetilde{P}(\xi)}\neq 0;

here, for a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
,
\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha)}=\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\zeta}^{\alpha_{2}}\widetilde{P}
in the notation of L. Hörmander
[
Hö83
]
. By virtue of Theorem 11.1.1 of
[
Hö83
]
, we conclude that
\widetilde{\Lambda}
is not hypoelliptic.
∎

Of course, as we discussed in the introduction, the theory formulated in this article has a version in which one begins with the operator
\widetilde{\Lambda}
whose heat kernel
H_{\widetilde{P}}
, along the diagonal, is well behaved in large time but whose small time behavior is elusive and is deduced more easily from
H_{P}
via the correspondence
H_{P}^{t}(0)=H_{\widetilde{P}}^{t}(0)
.

6.2
Future Directions

This article has focused on on-diagonal large-time asymptotics for the heat kernels
H_{P}
of certain inhomogeneous operators
\Lambda
and symbols
P
and well-behaved associated perturbations. At present, we do not have a good understanding of the off-diagonal behavior of these kernels in large time. Knowledge of this behavior could greatly improve our understanding of the theory presented in this article and it would also inform our study of convolution powers. In particular, having a good handle on this off-diagonal behavior would inform on the stability of convolution powers (seen as numerical difference schemes) as it has in
[
Th65
]
,
[
CF22
]
(see also
[
Th69
]
and Section 6 of
[
RSC17
]
).

The symbols
P(\xi)
treated throughout this article all take the special form
P_{1}(\eta+Q(\zeta))+P_{2}(\eta)
resemblant of our motivating example in which
P(\eta,\zeta)=(\eta+\zeta^{2})^{2}+\eta^{4}
. Our methods appear to be more broadly applicable, however. Consider, for example, the symbol

P^{\prime}(\xi)=(\eta+\zeta^{2})+\eta^{2}\zeta^{2}

defined for
\xi=(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Akin to our motivating example,
P^{\prime}
lacks a tractable scaling from which large-time asymptotics for
\varphi^{\prime}(t)=H_{P^{\prime}}^{t}(0)
may be computed directly. Still, by composing
P^{\prime}
with the measure-preserving transformation
T(\eta,\zeta)=(\eta-\zeta^{2},\zeta)
, we find that

\lim_{t\to\infty}t\widetilde{P^{\prime}}(t^{-1/2}\eta,t^{-1/6}\zeta)=\eta^{2}+\zeta^{6}

for
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
where
\widetilde{P^{\prime}}=P^{\prime}\circ T
. Accompanied by the fact that
t\widetilde{P^{\prime}}(t^{-1/2}\eta,t^{-1/6}\zeta)\geq(\eta^{2}+\zeta^{4}-2)/8
for
t\geq 1
and
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the change of variables
(\eta,\zeta)\mapsto(t^{-1/2}\eta,t^{-1/6}\zeta)
followed by an application of the dominated convergence theorem shows that

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{2/3}\varphi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\eta^{2}-\zeta^{6}}\,d\eta\,d\zeta=\frac{1}{2\pi^{3/2}}\Gamma(7/6).

Another class of example can be produced easily by considering powers of symbols
P(\xi)=P_{1}(\eta+Q(\zeta))+P_{2}(\eta)
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
. Specifically, given such a symbol
P(\xi)
and
\kappa>0
, our methods show that the heat kernel
H_{P^{\kappa}}
associated to
P(\xi)^{\kappa}
satisfies the on-diagonal asymptotics

H_{P^{\kappa}}^{t}(0)\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-\mu_{0}/\kappa}&0<t\leq 1\\
t^{-\mu_{\infty}/\kappa}&t\geq 1\end{cases}

for
t>0
where
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{\infty}
are those given by Theorem
3.1
. At this time, describing precisely the set of examples to which our methods apply is an open question. These questions will be explored in a forthcoming article.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
P
be a positive homogeneous function and let
F\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{a})
be such that
\{t^{F}\}
is non-expanding. If there exits

such that
[E,F]=0
, then, for any
\delta>0
, there exists

for which

\rho P(t^{F}\eta)\leq\delta P(\eta)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and

.

Proof.

Because
P(0)=0
, it suffices to prove that, for some
C>0
,

P(t^{F}\eta)\leq CP(\eta)

for all
0<t\leq 1
and non-zero
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
. Denote by
S
the unital level set of
P
and observe that

K=\{t^{F}\eta:0<t\leq 1,\,\eta\in S\}

is a bounded set because
S
is compact and
\{t^{F}\}_{0<t\leq 1}
is uniformly bounded. By virtue of the continuity of
P
, it follows that, for every

and
\eta\in S
,

P(t^{F}\eta)\leq\sup_{\xi\in K}P(\xi)=:C<\infty.

Given any non-zero
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}

[
BR22
]
guarantees that
\eta=r^{E}\eta_{0}
where

\eta_{0}\in S

as desired.
∎

\rho,M>0
for which

\rho P_{1}(\eta)\leq M+\delta P_{2}(\eta)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
. We note that this is the desired inequality evaluated at
t=1
; we shall obtain the full inequality by scaling. Now, denote by
S
and
B
the unital level set and unit ball associated to
P=\delta P_{2}
. Because
B
is relatively compact and
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}_{0<t\leq 1}
is uniformly bounded,

K=\{r^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta:0<r\leq 1,\,\eta\in B\}

is bounded and hence relatively compact. Since
P_{2}
is positive and does not vanish identically on
K
,

\rho:=\left(\sup_{\xi\in K}P_{1}(\xi)\right)^{-1}>0.

Now, for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}\setminus B
, it follows from Proposition 4.1 of
[
BR22
]
that
\eta=r^{-E_{2}}\eta_{0}
for
r=1/P(\eta)=1/(\delta P_{2}(\eta))\leq 1
and
\eta_{0}\in S
. Consequently,

\rho P_{1}(\eta)=\rho P_{1}(r^{-E_{2}}\eta_{0})=\frac{\rho}{r}P_{1}(r^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta_{0})\leq\frac{1}{r}=\delta P_{2}(\eta).

Of course,
P_{1}(\eta)
is bounded on
B
and so it follows that, for some constant
M>0
,

\rho P_{1}(\eta)\leq M+\delta P_{2}(\eta)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
, as claimed. Finally, for
0<t\leq 1
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
, we apply the preceding inequality to
t^{-E_{2}}\eta
to see that

\rho P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta)=t\rho P_{1}(t^{-E_{2}}\eta)\leq t\left(M+\delta P_{2}(t^{-E_{2}}\eta)\right)\leq tM+\delta P_{2}(\eta)\leq M+\delta P_{2}(\eta)

as desired.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

We shall first prove the estimate (
12
). For the lower estimate in (
12
), simultaneous appeals to Lemmas
A.1
and
A.4
give positive constants
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
, and
M
for which

\rho_{1}P_{1}(\zeta)\leq M+P_{2}(\zeta)\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\rho_{2}P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta)\leq P_{1}(\eta)/2

for
\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and
0<t\leq 1
. With the constants
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
in hand, we appeal to Lemma
A.2
to find a positive constant
\epsilon>0
for which

\epsilon P_{1}(\xi)\leq\rho_{1}P_{1}(\zeta+\xi)+\rho_{2}P_{1}(\zeta)

for all
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
. Consequently, for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and
0<t\leq 1
,

\epsilon P_{1}(\xi)\leq\rho_{1}P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)+\rho_{2}P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta)\leq M+P_{2}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)+P_{1}(\eta)/2.

Upon setting
C=\min\{\epsilon,1/2\}>0
, it follows that

C(P_{1}(\eta)+P_{1}(\xi))-M\leq P_{1}(\eta)/2+\epsilon P_{1}(\xi)-M\leq P_{1}(\eta)+P_{2}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)

for all
\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and
0<t\leq 1
. For the upper estimate in (
12
), we first make an appeal to Lemma
A.4
to obtain positive constants
M^{\prime}
and
C_{1}
for which

P_{1}(\eta)\leq M^{\prime}+C_{1}P_{2}(\eta)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
. By virtue of Lemma
A.3
, there is a positive constant
C_{2}
for which

P_{2}(\zeta+\xi)\leq C_{2}(P_{2}(\zeta)+P_{2}(\xi))

for all
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
. Finally, an appeal to Lemma
A.1
guarantees a positive constant
C_{3}
for which

P_{2}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta)\leq C_{3}P_{2}(\eta)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and
0<t\leq 1
. Upon combining these estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{1}(\eta)+P_{2}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M^{\prime}+C_{1}P_{2}(\eta)+C_{2}P_{2}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta)+C_{2}P_{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M^{\prime}+(C_{1}+C_{2}C_{3})P_{2}(\eta)+C_{2}P_{2}(\xi)

for all
\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and
0<t\leq 1
. Our desired (upper) estimate in (
12
) follows immediately by taking
C^{\prime}=\max\{C_{2},C_{1}+C_{2}C_{3}\}
and so the proof of (
12
) is complete.

To prove (
11
), we first make an appeal to Lemma
A.4
to obtain positive constants
\rho
and
M
for which

\rho P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta)\leq M+P_{2}(\eta)/2

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and
0<t\leq 1
. With this
\rho
, an appeal to Lemma
A.2
yields
\epsilon>0
for which

\epsilon P_{1}(\xi)\leq P_{1}(\zeta+\xi)+\rho P_{1}(\zeta)

for all
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
. Upon setting
C=\min\{\epsilon,1/2\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle CP_{1}(\xi)+P_{2}(\eta))-M

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon P_{1}(\xi)+P_{2}(\eta)/2-M

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)+\rho P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta)+P_{2}(\eta)/2-M

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P_{1}(t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\eta+\xi)+P_{2}(\eta)

for all
\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{a}
and
0<t\leq 1
and this is precisely the lower estimate in (
12
). Making use of Lemmas
A.4
and
A.3
, the upper estimate in (
12
) is established in a similar way to that for (
11
); we leave the remaining details to the reader.
∎

Our final goal in this appendix is to prove Proposition
4.3
. Before the proof, we present two lemmas.

Lemma A.5
.

Assume the notation and hypotheses of Proposition
4.3
and define

\widetilde{P}_{\infty}(\xi)=P_{1}(\eta)+P_{2}(-Q(\zeta))\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\infty}(\xi)=\widetilde{P}(\xi)-\widetilde{P}_{\infty}(\xi)

for
\xi=(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\infty}(\xi)
is subhomogeneous with respect to
G=E_{1}\oplus F_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. Since
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is contracting and
P_{2}
and
Q
are continuous, we can find
0<t_{0}\leq 1
for which

\absolutevalue{(P_1(\eta)+P_2(t^{E_1-E_2}\eta-Q(\zeta))-\widetilde{P}_\infty(\xi)}=\absolutevalue{P_2(t^{E_1-E_2}\eta-Q(\zeta))-P_2(-Q(\zeta))}<\epsilon

for all
0<t\leq t_{0}
and
\xi=(\eta,\zeta)\in K
. Thus, for every
0<t\leq t_{0}
and
\xi=(\eta,\zeta)\in K
, we have

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_\infty(t^{G}\xi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{P_1(t^{E_1}\eta)+P_2(t^{E_1}\eta-Q(t^{F_2}\zeta))-P_1(t^{E_1}\eta)-P_2(-Q(t^{F_2}\zeta))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{P_2(t^{E_1}\eta-t^{E_2}Q(\zeta))-P_2(-t^{E_2}Q(\zeta))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\absolutevalue{P_2(t^{E_1-E_2}\eta-Q(\zeta)-P_2(-Q(\zeta))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon t,

as desired.
∎

The following lemma asserts loosely that
\widetilde{P}
is approximately homogeneous with respect to
G
in small time whenever
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is contracting.

Lemma A.6
.

Assume the notation and hypotheses of Proposition 4.3. Then, there exists a compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, for any
\tau>0
and
0<\epsilon<1
, there exists
0<t_{0}\leq\tau
for which

\mathcal{O}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\xi=0\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\xi=t^{G}\xi^{\prime}\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,0<t<t_{0}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\xi^{\prime}\in K\}

is an open neighborhood of
0
and, for each non-zero
\xi=t^{G}\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
,

(1-\epsilon)t\leq\widetilde{P}(\xi)=\widetilde{P}(t^{G}\xi^{\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)t

Proof.

Let
K=S_{\widetilde{P}_{\infty}}
be the unital level set of
\widetilde{P}_{\infty}
and, by an appeal to the preceding lemma, let
0<t_{0}\leq\tau
be such that

\absolutevalue{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_\infty(t^G\xi')}\leq\epsilon t

whenever
0<t\leq t_{0}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in K
. In this notation, we observe that
\mathcal{O}
, as defined in the statement, coincides with the
\widetilde{P}_{\infty}
-adapted open ball
B_{t_{0}}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\widetilde{P}_{\infty}(\xi)<t_{0}\}
since
G\in\operatorname{Exp}(\widetilde{P}_{\infty})
; in particular,
\mathcal{O}=B_{t_{0}}
is necessarily an neighborhood of
0
(see Proposition 4.1 of
[
BR22
]
). Further, for each non-zero
\xi=t^{G}\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}=B_{t_{0}}
, we have
\widetilde{P}_{\infty}(\xi)=t
and therefore

\absolutevalue{\widetilde{P}(\xi)-t}=\absolutevalue{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_\infty(\xi)}=\absolutevalue{\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_\infty(t^G\xi')}\leq\epsilon t

so that
(1-\epsilon)t\leq\widetilde{P}(\xi)=\widetilde{P}(t^{G}\xi^{\prime})\leq(1+\epsilon)t
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

In view of Lemma
4.2
, we need only to prove the sufficiency of the subhomogeneity condition. Specifically, we prove that
\widetilde{R}(\xi)=o(\widetilde{P}(\xi))
as
\xi\to 0
when
\{t^{E_{1}-E_{2}}\}
is contracting and
\widetilde{R}
is subhomogeneous with respect to
G
. To this end, let
\epsilon>0
and take
K
as in the preceding lemma. With our assumption that
\widetilde{R}
is subhomogeneous with respect to
G
, let
\tau>0
be such that
\absolutevalue{\widetilde{R}(t^G\xi')}\leq\epsilon t/2
whenever
\xi^{\prime}\in K
and
0<t\leq\tau
. By an appeal to the preceding lemma, let
0<t_{0}\leq\tau
for which

\widetilde{P}(\xi)=\widetilde{P}(t^{G}\xi^{\prime})\geq t/2

whenever
\xi=t^{G}\xi^{\prime}
is a non-zero member of the open neighborhood

\mathcal{O}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\xi=0\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\xi=t^{G}\xi^{\prime}\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,0<t<t_{0}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\xi^{\prime}\in K\}

of
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, for any non-zero
\xi=t^{G}\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
,

\absolutevalue{\widetilde{R}(\xi)}=\absolutevalue{\widetilde{R}(t^G\xi')}\leq\epsilon t/2\leq\epsilon\widetilde{P}(t^{G}\xi^{\prime})=\epsilon\widetilde{P}(\xi).

By the continuity of
\widetilde{R}
and
\widetilde{P}
, this estimate clearly holds when
\xi=0
and so we have shown that
\widetilde{R}(\xi)=o(\widetilde{P}(\xi))
as
\xi\to 0
as was asserted.
∎

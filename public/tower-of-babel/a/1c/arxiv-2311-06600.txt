II
Symmetric teleparallel gravity

The STG theory is formulated in a spacetime endowed with a metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and an affine connection
\Gamma^{\rho}_{\penalty\ \mu\nu}
,
which is curvature-free and torsion-free

R_{\mu\nu\rho}{}^{\sigma}=2\partial_{[\nu}\Gamma^{\sigma}{}_{\mu]\rho}+2\Gamma^{\sigma}{}_{[\nu|\lambda|}\Gamma^{\lambda}{}_{\mu]\rho}=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ T^{\rho}_{\penalty\ \mu\nu}=2\Gamma^{\rho}{}_{[\mu\nu]}=0.

(1)

Without curvature and torsion, the gravity is identified with non-metircity

Q_{\rho\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\rho}g_{\mu\nu}.

(2)

The STG constraints in Eq. (
1
) indicate that the affine connection
\Gamma^{\rho}_{\penalty\ \mu\nu}
can be in general expressed as

\Gamma^{\rho}_{\penalty\ \mu\nu}=\frac{\partial x^{\rho}}{\partial y^{\sigma}}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}y^{\sigma},

(3)

where
\{y^{\mu}(x)\}
can be regarded as a coordinate system.
In the coordinate system
\{y^{\mu}\}
, the connection is trivial, that is,
\Gamma^{\rho}_{\penalty\ \mu\nu}=0
, which is also called the coincident gauge.
The metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and functions
y^{\mu}
can be regarded as basic variables in the STG theory.

The simplest STG model is the so-called symmetric teleparallel equivalent of general relativity (STEGR) model, whose action is

S_{\text{STEGR}}=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathbb{Q}+S_{m},

(4)

where the non-metricity scalar
\mathbb{Q}
is defined as

\mathbb{Q}=P^{\rho\mu\nu}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{4}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q^{\rho\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{\rho\mu\nu}Q^{\mu\nu\rho}+\frac{1}{4}Q_{\mu}Q^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{\mu}\tilde{Q}^{\mu},

(5)

with the non-metricity conjugate
P^{\rho\mu\nu}=-(1/4)Q^{\rho\mu\nu}+(1/2)Q^{(\mu\nu)\rho}+(1/4)(Q^{\rho}-\tilde{Q}^{\rho})g^{\mu\nu}-(1/4)g^{\rho(\mu}Q^{\nu)}
,

Q_{\mu}=Q_{\mu\nu}{}^{\nu}
,
\tilde{Q}_{\mu}=Q^{\nu}{}_{\nu\mu}
,
and other matter with the action
S_{m}
assumed to be minimally coupled to the metric.
Since we have the identity

\mathring{R}\equiv\mathbb{Q}-\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}(Q^{\mu}-\tilde{Q}^{\mu}),

(6)

the action (
4
) is identical to the Einstein-Hilbert action up to a surface term,
where the curvature scalar
\mathring{R}
is defined by the Levi-Civita connection and considered as being fully constructed from the metric.
Since the surface term in the action does not affect the equations of motion,
we say that STEGR is equivalent to GR at the level of equations of motion.

The coincidence that the STEGR model is equivalent to GR provides another way to modify GR,
which is to modify the STEGR model within the STG framework.
Along this line, a variety of modified STG models have been proposed.
The most interesting one is the
f(\mathbb{Q})
model, which generalizes
\mathbb{Q}
in the action (
4
) to a smooth function
f(\mathbb{Q})
.
Another widely studied scheme to modify STEGR is to introduce the non-minimal coupling between a scalar field and the non-metricity scalar.

In this paper, we concentrate on the following modified STG model

S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\left[\frac{1}{2}f(\mathbb{Q})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{C}(\phi)\mathbb{Q}+\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)\right]+S_{m},

(7)

where
X=-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi
,

f(\mathbb{Q})
and
\mathcal{C}(\phi)
are the smooth functions of
\mathbb{Q}
and the scalar field
\phi
, respectively.
Both
f(\mathbb{Q})
and non-minimal coupling STG models can be regarded as the submodel of the model (
7
).
This makes the analysis of cosmological pertuabtions below available to both
f(\mathbb{Q})
and non-minimal coupling STG models.

Equations of motion of the model (
7
) can be obtained from the variations with respect to
basic variables
g_{\mu\nu}
,
y^{\mu}
and
\phi
.
The equation of motion follow from the variations with respect to metric
g_{\mu\nu}
is

(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})\mathring{G}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{Q}f_{\mathbb{Q}}-f)g^{\mu\nu}+2\nabla_{\rho}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})P^{\rho\mu\nu}=\Theta_{\phi}^{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{m}^{\mu\nu},

(8)

where

f_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathrm{d}f(\mathbb{Q})/\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathring{G}^{\mu\nu}=\mathring{R}^{\mu\nu}-(1/2)\mathring{R}g^{\mu\nu}
is the Einstein tensor,

\Theta_{m}^{\mu\nu}=(2/\sqrt{-g})(\delta S_{m}/\delta g_{\mu\nu})
is the energy-momentum tensor for other matters,

\Theta_{\phi}^{\mu\nu}=2\mathcal{L}_{X}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial^{\nu}\phi+\mathcal{L}g^{\mu\nu}
is the energy-momentum tensor for the scalar field
\phi
,
where
\mathcal{L}_{X}=\partial{\mathcal{L}}(X,\phi)/\partial X
.
The one follow from the variations with respect to
y^{\mu}
is

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\left[\sqrt{-g}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})P^{\mu\nu}{}_{\rho}\right]=0.

(9)

Here we have assumed that there is no direct coupling between other matter and the connection,
so
S_{m}
does not play any role in the variations with respect to the connection.
Finally, the equation of motion from the variations with respect to the scalar field
\phi
is

\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{X}\mathring{\nabla}^{\mu}\phi\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Q}\,\mathcal{C}_{\phi}=0

(10)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=\partial{\mathcal{L}}(X,\phi)/\partial\phi
and
\mathcal{C}_{\phi}=\mathrm{d}\mathcal{C}(\phi)/\mathrm{d}\phi
.
All of these equations of motion are consistent with the covariant conservation law
\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}\Theta_{m}^{\mu\nu}=0

and the Bianchi identities
\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}\mathring{G}^{\mu\nu}\equiv 0

and
\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}(\sqrt{-g}P^{\mu\nu}{}_{\rho})\equiv 0
.
Vice versa, under the premise that the covariant conservation law
\mathring{\nabla}_{\mu}\Theta_{m}^{\mu\nu}=0
is satisfied,
Eq. (
9
) can be derived from Eqs. (
8
) and (
10
).

III
Cosmological background

Now we apply the model (
7
) to flat universe. In this section, we
only consider the background evolutions and leave the discussions on the cosmological perturbations to next sections.
The background equations obtained in this section will be a great help in simplifying the quadratic action of perturbations in the next section.

In flat universe, the metric can be expressed in rectangular coordinate as

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}\mathrm{d}x^{\mu}\mathrm{d}x^{\nu}=a^{2}\left(-\mathrm{d}\eta^{2}+\delta_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\right),

(11)

where

is the scale factor and
\eta

There are 6 Killing vector fields

\xi^{\mu}_{I}=\delta^{\,\mu}_{\penalty\ I}\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \xi^{\mu}_{I+3}=\epsilon_{Iij}\delta^{\mu}_{\penalty\ i}x^{j}\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ I=1,2,3

It can be proved that the metric (
11
) satisfies the condition:
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{I}}g_{\mu\nu}=0
,
where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{I}}
is the Lie derivative along the Killing vector field
\xi^{\mu}_{I}
.
This reflects the fact that the metric is homogeneous and isotropic.

Unlike the case of Riemannian geometry, in the STG theory, the affine connection is still arbitrary to some extent after the metric is determined.
As suggested in Refs.

;
Heisenberg:2022mbo
;
Hohmann:2020zre
;
Gomes:2023hyk

(13)

Although
{\Gamma}^{\rho}_{\penalty\ \mu\nu}
is coordinate dependent, the Lie derivative of
{\Gamma}^{\rho}_{\penalty\ \mu\nu}
does not depend on the coordinate.
Hence, the condition (
13

Combining the condition (
13

1
),
the non-zero component of the connection

can be obtained as

\Gamma^{0}_{\penalty\ 00}=K_{1}(\eta)\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \Gamma^{0}_{\penalty\ ij}=K_{2}(\eta)\delta_{ij}\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \Gamma^{i}_{\penalty\ 0j}=\Gamma^{i}_{\penalty\ j0}=K_{3}(\eta)\delta_{ij},

(14)

where

have three branch solutions, given by

\displaystyle\text{branch 1 :}\penalty\ K_{1}=\gamma\penalty\ ,\penalty\ K_{2}=0\penalty\ ,\penalty\ K_{3}=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\text{branch 2 :}\penalty\ K_{1}=\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma+\gamma\penalty\ ,\penalty\ K_{2}=0\penalty\ ,\penalty\ K_{3}=\gamma,

(16)

\displaystyle\text{branch 3 :}\penalty\ K_{1}=-\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma\penalty\ ,\penalty\ K_{2}=\gamma\penalty\ ,\penalty\ K_{3}=0,

(17)

where

\eta

\eta

Putting the metric (
11

8

\displaystyle 3(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})\mathcal{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}a^{2}(f-\mathbb{Q}f_{\mathbb{Q}})+\frac{3}{2}(K_{3}-K_{2})(f^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=a^{2}(\rho_{\phi}+\rho_{m}),

(20)

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{L}_{X}\phi^{\prime\prime}+2(\mathcal{L}_{X}^{\prime}+2\mathcal{H}\mathcal{L}_{X})\phi^{\prime}-a^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}\mathbb{Q}\,\mathcal{C}_{\phi}=0,

where

\phi

\phi

Note that in Eq. (

\displaystyle g_{ij}=a^{2}\left[(1-2\psi)\delta_{ij}+2E_{,ij}+E_{i,j}^{V}+E_{j,i}^{V}+h^{T}_{ij}\right],

IV.1
Tensor perturbation

For tensor perturbations, we can expand them as follows

h^{T}_{ij}(\eta,\vec{x})=\sum_{\mathrm{A}=+,\times}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,h_{\mathrm{A}}(\eta,\vec{k})\,\hat{e}_{ij}^{\mathrm{A}}(\vec{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}},

where the tensor polarization bases

satisfy the relation

\hat{e}_{ij}^{\mathrm{A}}(\vec{k})\hat{e}_{ij}^{\mathrm{B}}(\vec{k})=2\delta^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}

Then, the quadratic action of
S_{\mathbb{Q}}
for tensor perturbations can be directly obtained as

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{Q},T}^{(2)}=\sum_{\mathrm{A}=+,\times}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\frac{a^{2}}{4}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})\left(\left|h_{\mathrm{A}}^{\prime}\right|^{2}-{\omega}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left|h_{\mathrm{A}}\right|^{2}\right),

(25)

\displaystyle\text{with}\penalty\ \penalty\ {\omega}^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}=k^{2}+2(2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}^{2})+(4\mathcal{H}+K_{2}-3K_{3})[\ln(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})]^{\prime}+a^{2}(f-\mathbb{Q}f_{\mathbb{Q}})/(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C}),

while the quadratic action of

S_{\phi,T}^{(2)}=-\sum_{\mathrm{A}=+,\times}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\frac{a^{4}}{2}p_{\phi}\left|h_{\mathrm{A}}\right|^{2}

(26)

Using the background equation (
19

S_{T}^{(2)}=\sum_{\mathrm{A}=+,\times}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\frac{a^{2}}{4}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})\left(\left|h_{\mathrm{A}}^{\prime}\right|^{2}-{\omega}^{2}\left|h_{\mathrm{A}}\right|^{2}\right),\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{with}\penalty\ \penalty\ {\omega}^{2}=k^{2}+2K_{2}[\ln(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})]^{\prime}.

The propagation equation of GW is obtained from the action (

When

Next we consider the case where

are more general functions.

Compared with the GW propagation equation in GR, Eq. (
28
) shows that
the STG model (
7

The first corrections is that the STG model (

The modified friction term will affect the amplitude of GW,
making the GW luminosity distance

no longer equal to the standard electromagnetic luminosity distance

;

(29)

where

This correction appears in both

and non-minimal coupling STG models, and exists in all three branches of the background connection.

The second correction is that the STG model (

It can be seen that there is an additional time-dependent mass term in the dispersion relation of GW in Eq. (

This makes the phase velocity

Note that we have assumed

in Eq. (

Unlike the correction to the friction term,
the correction to the dispersion relation only exists in the branch 3,
because

is non-vanishing only in the branch 3.
In the branch 1 and branch 2 flat universe, the speed of GW is the same as the speed of light.
Such a result holds true for both

model and non-minimal coupling STG models.
In addition, the difference between the speed of GW and the speed of light can be tightly constrained by the present GW experiments

;
Zhu:2023wci
.
This would impose additional strong constraints on the evolution of the branch 3 flat universe.

It should be emphasized that all corrections to the propagation of GW depend on the specific background evolution,
that is, the specific expression of

and

The latter in turn depends on the specific STG model, that is, the specific forms of

and

In addition, no matter which model we adopt,

in the branch 1 appears
neither in the background equation nor in the GW propagation equation.
This means that for the branch 1 flat universe,
both the background evolution and the GW propagation behavior are exactly the same as the case of the trivial connection

It can be seen from the action (
27
) that we need to require
f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C}>0
to avoid ghost instability in tensor perturbations.
In addition, for the case of
K_{2}[\ln(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})]^{\prime}\lesssim-\mathcal{H}
,
tensor perturbations have the risk of tachyon instability.

IV.2
Vector perturbation

For vector perturbations, we can expand them with the vector polarization bases, such as

B_{i}^{V}(\eta,\vec{x})=\sum_{\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,B_{\mathrm{A}}(\eta,\vec{k})\,\hat{e}_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(\vec{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}},

(31)

where the vector polarization bases
\{\hat{e}_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(\vec{k}),\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\}
satisfy the relation

\hat{e}_{i}^{\mathrm{A}}(\vec{k})\hat{e}_{i}^{\mathrm{B}}(\vec{k})=\delta^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}
.
Then, the quadratic action of
S_{\mathbb{Q}}
for vector perturbations can be directly obtained as

\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{Q},V}^{(2)}=\sum_{\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,a^{2}\left\{\frac{1}{4}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})k^{2}\left|B_{\mathrm{A}}-E_{\mathrm{A}}^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m_{B}^{2}|B_{\mathrm{A}}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}m_{E}^{2}|E_{\mathrm{A}}|^{2}\right\},

(32)

\displaystyle\text{with}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ m_{B}^{2}=3(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})\mathcal{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}a^{2}(f-\mathbb{Q}f_{\mathbb{Q}})+\frac{1}{2}(3K_{3}-K_{2})(f^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ m_{E}^{2}=(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})(2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}a^{2}(f-\mathbb{Q}f_{\mathbb{Q}})+\frac{1}{2}(4\mathcal{H}+K_{2}-3K_{3})(f^{\prime}_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C}^{\prime}).

And the quadratic action of
S_{\phi}
for vector perturbations can also be directly found to be

S_{\phi,V}^{(2)}=-\sum_{\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,a^{4}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\phi}|B_{\mathrm{A}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}k^{2}p_{\phi}|E_{\mathrm{A}}|^{2}\right\}.

(33)

Using the background equations (
18
) and (
19
), the total quadratic action for vector perturbations is simplified as

S_{V}^{(2)}=\sum_{\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,a^{2}\left\{\frac{1}{4}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})k^{2}\left|B_{\mathrm{A}}-E_{\mathrm{A}}^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\frac{K_{2}}{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}+\mathcal{C}^{\prime})(|B_{\mathrm{A}}|^{2}-k^{2}|E_{\mathrm{A}}|^{2})\right\}.\penalty\ \penalty\

(34)

It can be seen that
B_{\mathrm{A}}
is non-dynamic field and the variation of the action (
34
) with respect to
B_{\mathrm{A}}
leads to the following constraint

B_{\mathrm{A}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{A}}^{\prime}}{1+2k^{-2}K_{2}[\ln(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})]^{\prime}}

(35)

One can eliminate
B_{\mathrm{A}}
from the action (
34
) by substituting the constraint (
35
) back into it.
After that, the quadratic action for vector perturbations can be simplified as

S_{V}^{(2)}=\sum_{\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\frac{1}{2}\tilde{a}^{2}\left(\left|E_{\mathrm{A}}^{\prime}\right|^{2}-\omega^{2}\left|E_{\mathrm{A}}\right|^{2}\right),

(36)

where

\tilde{a}^{2}=\frac{a^{2}K_{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})^{\prime}}{1+2k^{-2}K_{2}[\ln(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})]^{\prime}}\penalty\ \penalty\ ,\penalty\ \penalty\ {\omega}^{2}=k^{2}+2K_{2}[\ln(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})]^{\prime}.

When
f(\mathbb{Q})\propto\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{C}(\phi)=\text{constant}
, Eq. (
28
) is reduced to
S^{(2)}_{V}=0
, that is,
there is no dynamic degree of freedom in vector perturbations, and this is exactly the case of GR.
Next we consider the case where
f(\mathbb{Q})
and
\mathcal{C}(\phi)
are more general functions.

In the branch 3 flat universe, usually
K_{2}=\gamma\neq 0
, so there are two dynamic degrees of freedom in vector perturbations.
These two vector degrees of freedom can be regarded as vectorial GW, and there are already some experiments to detect and constrain them

Chen:2021wdo
;
Wu:2021kmd
;
Chen:2023uiz
.
To avoid ghost instability, we need to require
\tilde{a}^{2}>0
, which means
K_{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C}))^{\prime}>0
for large
k
modes.
It is interesting to note that the vectorial and tensorial GW have the same dispersion relation
\omega^{2}
.
This means that the vectorial and tensorial GW have the same propagation speed and are different from the speed of light.

In the branch 1 and branch 2 flat universe,
K_{2}=0\Rightarrow S^{(2)}_{V}=0
, so there is no dynamic degree of freedom in vector perturbations.
This lack of degrees of freedom implies that vector perturbations suffer from the strong coupling problem in the branch 1 and branch 2 flat universe.

IV.3
Scalar perturbations

Finally, we examine the stability of scalar perturbations.
Without loss of generality, we take the Lagrangian of the scalar field in action (
7
) as
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\frac{1}{2}X-V(\phi)
.
Since the quadratic action for scalar perturbations is very complicated and bloated, we only show the key parts of the quadratic action in this section.

For scalar perturbations, the quadratic action can be directly obtained as

\displaystyle S_{V}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,\bigg[\frac{9}{2}f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}(K_{2}^{2}+K_{3}^{2}){A^{\prime}}^{2}+f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{1}A^{\prime}(3\psi^{\prime}+k^{2}E^{\prime})+k^{2}f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{2}A^{\prime}B

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+4k^{2}a^{2}(\mathcal{C}+f_{\mathbb{Q}})\psi^{\prime}B-k^{2}f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{3}(\psi^{\prime}+k^{2}E^{\prime})B+k^{2}\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{4}B^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\bigg],

(37)

where

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{1}=3\left[3K_{3}^{2}-K_{2}^{2}+4\mathcal{H}(K_{2}-K_{3})\right],

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{2}=3\left[K_{1}K_{2}-K_{1}K_{3}-K_{3}^{2}+2\mathcal{H}(K_{3}-K_{2})\right],

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{3}=3K_{3}^{2}+K_{1}K_{2}+3K_{1}K_{3}-2\mathcal{H}(2K_{1}+K_{2}+5K_{3})+8\mathcal{H}^{2},

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{4}=(1/2)k^{2}f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}(K_{1}+K_{3}-2\mathcal{H})^{2}+a^{2}K_{2}(\mathcal{C}+f_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\prime}.

In the above expression, we only show the terms containing
A^{\prime}
,
B\psi^{\prime}
,
BE^{\prime}
and
B^{2}
, while other terms are represented by “
\cdot\cdot\cdot
”.
We also abbreviate
A^{*}B
as
AB
,
B^{*}B
as
B^{2}
, and so on.
From the action (
IV.3
), we know that the value of
f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}
determines whether the variable
A
is dynamic,
so we will discuss it case by case below.

IV.3.1
The case of
f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\neq 0

For the case of
f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\neq 0
, only
B
is non-dynamic among all scalar perturbations.
The variations of the action (
IV.3
) with
B
lead to the following constraint

B=-\frac{1}{2\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{4}}\left[f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{2}A^{\prime}+4a^{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})\psi^{\prime}-f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathcal{N}}_{3}(3\psi^{\prime}+k^{2}E^{\prime})\right]+\cdot\cdot\cdot.

(38)

The ellipses in Eq. (
38
) indicate terms that do not contain the time derivatives of perturbation variables.
Since only the kinetic energy term is important in the following,
we only show the terms that will contribute to the kinetic energy here.
After substituting the constraints (
38
) back into the action (
IV.3
), the quadratic action for scalar perturbations can be expressed as

S_{S}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathrm{s}_{1}=1}^{4}\sum_{\mathrm{s}_{1}=1}^{4}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\penalty\ a^{2}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{s}_{1}\mathrm{s}_{2}}\Phi_{\mathrm{s}_{1}}^{\prime}\Phi_{\mathrm{s}_{2}}^{\prime}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\right),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{where}\penalty\ \penalty\ \Phi_{\mathrm{s}}=(\delta\phi,A,\psi,E).

(39)

Here, we only show the kinetic energy terms.
Using the background equations (
18
)-(
21
), the determinant of the kinetic energy matrix
\mathcal{M}
can be simplified to

\det\mathcal{M}=-\frac{36k^{4}K_{2}^{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})^{2}f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})^{\prime}}{2a^{2}K_{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})^{\prime}+k^{2}f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}(2\mathcal{H}-K_{1})^{2}}.

(40)

In the branch 3 flat universe, usually
K_{2}\neq 0\Rightarrow\det\mathcal{M}\neq 0
, so there are four dynamical degrees of freedom in scalar perturbations.
One degree of freedom comes from the scalar field
\phi
, and the other three come from the metric.
For large
k
modes, the determinant of the kinetic energy matrix
\mathcal{M}
can be further simplified to

\det\mathcal{M}\overset{\text{UV}}{\approx}-\frac{36k^{2}K_{2}^{3}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})^{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})^{\prime}}{(2\mathcal{H}-K_{1})^{2}}.

(41)

From the analysis in the previous subsections,
we already know that in order to ensure that tensor and vector perturbations are ghost-free,
we need to require
f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C}>0
and
K_{2}(f_{\mathbb{Q}}+\mathcal{C})^{\prime}>0
.
However, these two conditions make
\det\mathcal{M}<0
in Eq. (
41
).
It means that there is at least one ghost mode in scalar perturbations.
And the ghost mode in scalar perturbations can only arise from the metric,
because the scalar field with the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\frac{1}{2}X-V(\phi)
will not bring the ghost mode.
Therefore, ghost instability is inevitable in the branch 3 flat universe.

In the branch 1 and branch 2 flat universe,
K_{2}=0\Rightarrow\det\mathcal{M}=0
, so at most only three dynamic degrees of freedom appear in linear order.
This lack of degrees of freedom implies that scalar perturbations suffer from the strong coupling problem in the branch 1 and branch 2 flat universe.
This is very similar to the case of vector perturbation.

To sum up, the non-trivial (i.e.
f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}\neq 0
) model (
7
) will suffer from the strong coupling issue in the branch 1 and branch 2 flat universe,
and will suffer from the problem of ghost instability in the branch 3 universe.
Such a result holds true for the pure
f(\mathbb{Q})
model, that is, the model with
\mathcal{C}(\phi)=0
.
The stability of the
f(\mathbb{Q})
model is also discussed in Ref.
Gomes:2023tur
at almost the same time, and we reached the same conclusion.
They argue that these instabilities should also exist in more general modified STG models.
Here we show that accounting for the non-minimal coupling indeed does not cure these instabilities.
These instabilities may arise from the fact that
non-trivial STG models always lack diffeomorphism (coincident gauge) or contain higher-order derivatives (via Eq. (
3
)).

IV.3.2
The case of
f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}=0

For the case of
f_{\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}}=0
,
f
is a linear function of
\mathbb{Q}
(constant term can be absorbed into
V(\phi)
).
It means that
f
can be absorbed into
\mathcal{C}\mathbb{Q}
in the action (
7
). So we can only consider the non-minimal coupling STG model.

It can be seen from the action (
IV.3
) that in this case, both variables
A
and
B
are non-dynamic.
The variations of the action (
IV.3
) with them lead to the following constraints

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}+4\mathcal{H}\mathcal{C})A+2K_{2}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}B+4\mathcal{C}\psi^{\prime}+\cdot\cdot\cdot=0,

(42)

\displaystyle 2({\phi^{\prime}}^{2}-6\mathcal{H}^{2}\mathcal{C}-3K_{3}\mathcal{C}^{\prime})A+k^{2}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}+4\mathcal{H}\mathcal{C})B-4\mathcal{H}\mathcal{C}(3\psi^{\prime}+k^{2}E^{\prime})-2\phi^{\prime}\delta\phi^{\prime}+\cdot\cdot\cdot=0.\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

(43)

The ellipses indicate terms that do not contain the time derivatives of perturbation variables.
These constraint equations are just used to solve the non-dynamic variables
A
and
B
.
One can eliminate these two non-dynamic variables from the action (
IV.3
) by substituting
the constraints (
42
) and (
43
) back into it.
After that, the quadratic action for scalar perturbations can be expressed as

S_{S}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathrm{s}_{1}=1}^{3}\sum_{\mathrm{s}_{1}=1}^{3}\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k\penalty\ a^{2}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{s}_{1}\mathrm{s}_{2}}\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{s}_{1}}^{\prime}\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{s}_{2}}^{\prime}+\cdot\cdot\cdot\right),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{where}\penalty\ \penalty\ \tilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{s}}=(\delta\phi,\psi,E).

(44)

Here, we only show the kinetic energy terms.
No matter in which branch, for large
k
modes, the determinant of the kinetic energy matrix
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
can be approximately written as

\det\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\overset{\text{UV}}{\approx}-\frac{4k^{4}\mathcal{C}^{2}{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}^{2}}{(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}+4\mathcal{H}\mathcal{C})^{2}}.

(45)

For the general case where
\mathcal{C}
is not a constant,
\det\tilde{\mathcal{M}}<0
.
It means that there are three dynamic degrees of freedom in scalar perturbations, and at least one of them is a ghost mode.
This ghost mode can only come from the metric, because the scalar field
\phi
with the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(X,\phi)=\frac{1}{2}X-V(\phi)
cannot cause the ghost mode.
Therefore, in the non-minimal coupling STG model,
there is always ghost instability in scalar perturbations, no matter which branch flat universe it is in.

For the case where
\mathcal{C}
is a constant, the kinetic energy matrix
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies

\text{rank of}\penalty\ \tilde{\mathcal{M}}=1\penalty\ \penalty\ ,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{non-zero eigenvalue of}\penalty\ \tilde{\mathcal{M}}=1+\frac{{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}}>0.

(46)

This is precisely the result in GR. There is only one dynamic degree of freedom brought by the scalar field
\phi
, and this degree of freedom is ghost-free.

In summary, the non-minimally coupled STG model suffers from the problem of ghost instability, unless the model is reduced to GR.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have considered a STG model that can encompass both
f(\mathbb{Q})
and non-minimal coupling STG models.
After a brief analysis of the cosmological background equations of the model,
we have studied the cosmological perturbations of the model on flat universe with three different branches.
For tensor perturbations, we have found that corrections to the friction term of GW exist on all three branches.
This would make the luminosity distance of GW to be different from the luminosity distance of the standard electromagnetic field.
We have also shown that the propagation speed of GW on one branch can be different from the speed of light,
but on the other two branches the propagation speed of GW is always equal to the speed of light.
For vector perturbations, we have demonstrated that there are two vectorial dynamic degree of freedom on one of the branches.
On this branch, the vectorial and tensorial GW have the same propagation speed and are different from the speed of light.
But there are no dynamic degree of freedom for vector perturbations on the other two branches.
This suggests the strong coupling problem on these two branches.
After examining scalar perturbations, we have proved that the model always suffers from the problem of strong coupling or ghost instability.
This conclusion also applies to the pure
f(\mathbb{Q})
model or the pure non-minimal coupling STG model.
Since these instabilities may arise from the fact that non-trivial STG models always lack diffeomorphism (coincident gauge) or contain higher-order derivatives (via Eq. (
3
)), these instabilities may exist widely in various STG models.

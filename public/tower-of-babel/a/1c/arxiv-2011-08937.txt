A C 0 Interior Penalty Method for the Phase Field Crystal Equation Thanks: The work of the second author was supported in part by the National Science Foundation under Grant No. DMS-1520862.
Amanda E. Diegel, Natasha Sharma

Abstract
We present a C 0 interior penalty finite element method for the sixth-order phase field crystal equation. We demonstrate that the numerical scheme is uniquely solvable, unconditionally energy stable, and convergent. We remark that the novelty of this paper lies in the fact that this is the first C 0 interior penalty finite element method developed for the phase field crystal equation. Additionally, the error analysis presented develops a detailed methodology for analyzing time dependent problems utilizing the C 0 interior penalty method. We furthermore benchmark our method against numerical experiments previously established in the literature.



Keywords:
Phase field crystal, C0 interior penalty method, finite element method, sixth-order parabolic, higher-order methods, nonlinear partial differential equation, energy stability

Introduction

Phase field crystal (PFC) methodolgy is emerging as a popular means of modeling many important phenomena observed in materials science. Examples include grain growth, dendritic and eutectic solidification, epitaxial growth, and more. We refer the interested reader to the review paper
[
28

Due to the growing number of applications of the PFC model and its variations, there is an interest in developing accurate and stable numerical schemes. Indeed, much progress has already been accomplished within the finite difference and Fourier spectral framework. The goal of this paper, however, is to present a finite element approach to the PFC model which is unconditionally energy stable, uniquely solvable, and convergent.

In this section, we show that the C

\displaystyle C_{coer}\left\|w_{h}\right\|_{2,h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}^{IP}\left(w_{h},w_{h}\right)\quad\forall\,w_{h}\in Z_{h},

where the constants

Proof.

The proof of the Lemma may be found in

Additionally, we define the spaces
L_{0}^{2}(\Omega):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)|\left(v,1\right)=0\},\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega):=H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega),\mathring{H}_{N}^{-1}(\Omega):=\{v\in H_{N}^{-1}(\Omega)|\langle v,1\rangle=0\},\mathring{V}_{h}:=V_{h}\cap L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)

[
16

\left\|v\right\|_{H_{N}^{-1}}=\left(\nabla\mathsf{T}v,\nabla\mathsf{T}v\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}=\langle v,\mathsf{T}v\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}=\langle\mathsf{T}v,v\rangle^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}

3.2

Energy stability follows as a direct result of the convex decomposition represented in the scheme. First, we define a discrete energy closely related to (

\displaystyle F(\phi):=\frac{1}{4}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\phi,\phi\right).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(\phi_{h}^{m},\mu_{h}^{m})\in Z_{h}\times V_{h}

2.1a

\displaystyle F\left(\phi_{h}^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle+\tau\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\left\|\nabla\mu_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}\Biggl\{\,\frac{(1-\epsilon)}{2}\left\|\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\left\|\delta_{\tau}(\phi_{h}^{m})^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m},\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right)\,\Biggr\}=F\left(\phi_{h}^{0}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m},\mu_{h}^{m}\right)+\left(\nabla\mu_{h}^{m},\nabla\mu_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\left(\left(\phi_{h}^{m}\right)^{3}+(1-\epsilon)\phi_{h}^{m},\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right)+a_{h}^{IP}\left(\phi_{h}^{m},\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right)-2\left(\nabla\phi_{h}^{m-1},\nabla\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right)-\left(\mu_{h}^{m},\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mu_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}\left(\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}-\left\|\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\right)+\frac{\tau}{4}\left\|\delta_{\tau}(\phi_{h}^{m})^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\left\|\phi_{h}^{m}\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\tau}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\phi_{h}^{m},\phi_{h}^{m}\right)-\frac{1}{2\tau}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\phi_{h}^{m-1},\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right)+\frac{\tau}{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m},\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\left\|\nabla\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\|\nabla\phi_{h}^{m-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\tau\left\|\nabla\delta_{\tau}\phi_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have developed a C
0
interior penalty finite element method to solve a special case of the phase field crystal equation (
1.2
). We were able to demonstrate that our method is uniquely solvable, unconditionally energy stable, and unconditionally convergent. We were also able to demonstrate that our method benchmarks well against numerical experiments established in the existing literature. Future work includes the extension of the C
0
interior penalty method developed in Section
2
to the case in which a non-constant mobility is considered and the case in which periodic boundary conditions are considered. Regarding the case in which a non-constant mobility is considered, it is believed that unconditional solvability and stability could be achieved as seen in
[
26
]
with the biggest outstanding question centered around the error analysis. Additionally, application of the method to related models such as the modified phase field crystal equation and building efficient solvers for these methods remains of interest.

Lemma D.1
.

Let

and

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}u+(1-\epsilon)u

\displaystyle=f\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\frac{\partial\Delta u}{\partial n}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

where

and define the following norm on the space

\displaystyle|||v|||_{h}^{2}:=\sum\limits_{K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}|v|_{H^{2}(K)}^{2}+\alpha\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}|e|^{-1}\left\|\left\llbracket\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}\right\rrbracket\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}|e|\left\|\{\kern-3.125pt\{\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial n_{e}^{2}}\}\kern-3.125pt\}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}.

Then the Ritz projection (
2.2
) satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\left\|u-P_{h}u\right\|_{2,h}\leq Ch\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Note that on the finite element space
Z_{h}
, the two norms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2,h}
and
|||\cdot|||_{h}
are equivalent. Additionally, following
[
3
]
, it can be shown that there exists constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

\displaystyle C_{1}|||u|||_{h}^{2}\leq a_{h}^{IP}\left(u,u\right)\text{ for all }u\in H^{3}(\Omega,\mathscr{T}_{h})

and

\displaystyle a_{h}^{IP}\left(u,w\right)\leq C_{2}|||u|||_{h}|||w|||_{h}\text{ for all }u,w\in H^{3}(\Omega,\mathscr{T}_{h}).

Thus, using the definition of the Ritz projection (
2.2
), letting

, and using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, we have

\displaystyle|||P_{h}u-u|||_{h}^{2}+(1-\epsilon)\left\|P_{h}u-u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}a_{h}^{IP}\left(P_{h}u-u,P_{h}u-u\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\epsilon)\left(P_{h}u-u,P_{h}u-u\right)

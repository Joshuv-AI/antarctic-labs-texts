Integrable quenches in the Hubbard model
Colin Rylands, Bruno Bertini, Pasquale Calabrese

Abstract
We study the quench dynamics of the one-dimensional Hubbard model through the Quench Action formalism. We introduce a class of integrable initial states — expressed as product states over two sites — for which we can provide an exact characterisation of the late-time regime. This is achieved by finding a closed-form expression for the overlaps between our states and the Bethe ansatz eigenstates, which we check explicitly in the limits of low densities and infinite repulsion. Our solution gives access to the stationary values attained by local observables (we show the explicit example of the density of doubly occupied sites) and the asymptotic entanglement dynamics directly in the thermodynamic limit. Interestingly, we find that for intermediate interaction strength Rényi entropies display a double-slope structure.

II
Hubbard Model

The Hubbard model describes a system of interacting spinful fermions on the lattice. Its Hamiltonian reads as

\displaystyle H=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sum_{a=\uparrow,\downarrow}-\mathfrak{t}\left(c^{\dagger}_{ja}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+1a}+c^{\dagger}_{j+1a}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ja}\right)+\mathfrak{u}\sum_{j=1}^{L}c^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow}

\displaystyle-\mu\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(c^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\uparrow}+c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow}\right)-h\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(c^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\uparrow}-c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow}\right),

(1)

where
c^{\dagger}_{ja},c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ja}
are creation and annihilation operators for fermions at site
j
with spin
a=\uparrow,\downarrow
satisfying
\{c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ja},c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ib}\}=\{c^{\dagger}_{ja},c^{\dagger}_{ib}\}=0
and
\{c^{\dagger}_{ja},c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ib}\}=\delta_{i,j}\delta_{a,b}
. Moreover,
L
is the length of the chain, which we take to be even,
\mathfrak{t}
is the hopping parameter and
\mathfrak{u}
is the interaction strength. We have also included a chemical potential,
\mu
, and a magnetic field,
h
, which couple to the total particle number operator

\displaystyle\hat{N}=\hat{N}_{\uparrow}+\hat{N}_{\downarrow}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(c^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\uparrow}+c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow}\right)\,,

(2)

and the
z
component of the spin operator

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{N}_{\uparrow}-\hat{N}_{\downarrow}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left(c^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\uparrow}-c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow}\right)\,,

(3)

respectively. Both of these commute with the Hamiltonian,
[H,\hat{N}]=[H,\hat{S}^{z}]=0
and we denote the number of particles by
N
and the total
z
-spin by
S^{z}=N/2-M
where
M
is the number of down spin particles.

The model enjoys a number of transformations which map the Hamiltonian to itself in different regimes of the parameters. For example, we may perform a particle hole transformation,

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{ja}\to(-1)^{j}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ja},~~c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ja}\to(-1)^{j}c^{\dagger}_{ja},

(4)

under which the Hamiltonian is mapped to itself, up to a constant, but with a different chemical potential and magnetic field

\displaystyle H\left({\mathfrak{u},\mu,h}\right)\to H\left({\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{u}-\mu,-h}\right)+\mathfrak{u}L,

(5)

along with
\hat{N}_{\uparrow,\downarrow}\to L-\hat{N}_{\uparrow,\downarrow}
. Alternatively one may perform the particle-hole transformation on only one species, say the down spins

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}\to(-1)^{j}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow},~~c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow}\to(-1)^{j}c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow},

(6)

while keeping the up spins invariant. This Shiba transformation
Essler et al. 2005
, as it is known, maps the Hamiltonian between the repulsive and attractive regimes,

\displaystyle H\left({\mathfrak{u},\mu,h}\right)\to H\left({-\mathfrak{u},-h-\mathfrak{u}/2,\mathfrak{u}/2-\mu}\right)-(\mu+h)L,

(7)

along with
N_{\downarrow}\to L-N_{\downarrow}
. Using these transformations it is sufficient to consider the model only for
N\leq L
,
2M\leq N
and
\mathfrak{u}\geq 0
from which the properties in the other parameter regimes can be inferred.

In the absence of the magnetic field,
h=0
the conservation of
S^{z}
is enhanced to the full
SU(2)
spin symmetry generated by
\hat{S}^{z}
and the spin raising and lowering operators

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{+}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}c^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow},\qquad\hat{S}^{-}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\uparrow}.

(8)

In addition, when the chemical potential takes the particle hole symmetric value,
\mu=\mathfrak{u}/2
there exists an additional
SU(2)
charge symmetry known as
\eta
symmetry. The latter is generated by

\displaystyle\eta^{z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(L-\hat{N}\right),\qquad\eta^{-}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}(-1)^{j}c^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow},\qquad\eta^{+}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}(-1)^{j}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\downarrow}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j\uparrow},

(9)

which can be shown to obey
{\rm su}(2)
commutation relations. At generic values of the chemical potential and magnetic field we have

[H,\eta^{\pm}]=\mp(\mathfrak{u}-2\mu)\eta^{\pm},\qquad[H,\hat{S}^{\pm}]=\mp 2h\hat{S}^{\pm},

(10)

meaning that the eigenstates of the model arrange themselves into
SU(2)
multiplets.

The Hamiltonian is integrable and its exact solution was found by Lieb and Wu
Lieb and Wu 1968a
;
Lieb and Wu 1968b
. The eigenstates of the model are labelled by the “momenta”
k_{j},~j=1,\dots N
and “spin rapidities”
\lambda_{\gamma},~\gamma=1,\dots,M
and are given by

\displaystyle\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}=\sum^{L}_{n_{1}\leq n_{2}\dots\leq n_{N}}\sum_{a_{1}\dots a_{N}=\uparrow,\downarrow}\psi(\vec{n},\vec{a})c^{\dagger}_{n_{1},a_{1}}\dots c^{\dagger}_{n_{N},a_{N}}\ket{0}\,,

(11)

where
\ket{0}
is the empty chain i.e.
c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{ja}\ket{0}=0
and the unnormalised wavefunction reads as

\displaystyle\psi(\vec{n},\vec{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}(-1)^{P}e^{i\sum_{j}k_{P_{j}}n_{j}}\varphi_{P}(\vec{a}),

(12)

\displaystyle\varphi_{P}(\vec{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{M}}\prod_{1\leq Q_{\gamma}<Q_{\beta}\leq M}\frac{\lambda_{Q_{\gamma}}-\lambda_{Q_{\beta}}-iU}{\lambda_{Q_{\gamma}}-\lambda_{Q_{\beta}}}\prod^{M}_{l=1}F_{P}(\lambda_{Q_{l}},y_{l})\,,

(13)

\displaystyle F_{P}(\lambda_{Q_{l}},y_{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{iU}{\lambda_{Q_{l}}-s_{P_{y_{l}}}+iU/2}\prod_{j=1}^{y_{l}-1}\frac{\lambda_{Q_{l}}-s_{P_{j}}-iU/2}{\lambda_{Q_{l}}-s_{P_{j}}+iU/2}\,.

(14)

Here
\mathcal{S}_{N}
denotes the group of permutations of
N
elements,
\varphi_{P}(\vec{a})
is the spin part of the wavefunction and
y_{l}
are the positions of the
M
up spins amongst the
N
particles i.e.
y_{j}=l
means that the
l^{\text{th}}
particle is the
j^{\text{th}}
up spin. We have introduced the reduced interaction strength in terms of which the results are expressed,

\displaystyle U=\frac{\mathfrak{u}}{2\mathfrak{t}},

(15)

and used the shorthand notation

s_{k_{j}}\equiv\sin{(k_j)},\qquad c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{k_{j}}\equiv\cos(k_j).

(16)

The momenta and spin raipidities are not free but are quantized and coupled to one another through the nested Bethe Ansatz equations

\displaystyle e^{-ik_{j}L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\gamma}^{M}\frac{\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{k_{j}}-iU/2}{\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{k_{j}}+iU/2},

(17)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{k_{i}}-iU/2}{\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{k_{i}}+iU/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\beta\neq\gamma}^{M}\frac{\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta}-iU}{\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta}+iU}.

(18)

The energy and total momentum of this state are

\displaystyle E=-2\mathfrak{t}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\cos{(k_j)}-\mu N-h(N-2M),~~P=\sum_{j=1}^{N}k_{j}~\text{mod}~2\pi.

(19)

The norm of the Hubbard Bethe states was found by Gohman and Korepin
Göhmann and Korepin 1999
and is given by

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}=U^{M}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\cos{(k_j)}\prod_{1\leq\gamma<\beta\leq M}\left[\frac{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}+U^{2}}{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}}\right]\text{det}[G].

(20)

Here
G
is a
(N+M)\times(N+M)
matrix of Gaudin type which typically appears in the norm of Bethe ansatz wavefunctions. It’s components are

\displaystyle G_{i,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}\left[\frac{L}{\cos{k_j}}+\sum_{\gamma}^{M}\phi_{1}(\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{k_{j}})\right],

(21)

\displaystyle G_{i,N+\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{N+\gamma,i}=-\phi_{1}(\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{k_{j}}),

(22)

\displaystyle G_{N+\gamma,N+\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\gamma\beta}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{1}(\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{k_{j}})-\sum_{\gamma=1}^{M}\phi_{2}(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\gamma})\right]+\phi_{2}(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta}),

(23)

for
1\leq i,j\leq N
,
1\leq\gamma,\beta\leq M
and with

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(x)=\frac{nU}{(nU/2)^{2}+x^{2}}.

(24)

The states described thus far are only the highest weight states of
SU(2)
spin and
\eta
symmetry multiplets. The remainder of the states are obtained by acting on these with the lowering operators
\hat{S}^{-}
and
\eta^{-}
. In this work we shall only need the descendant state obtained through application of
\eta^{-}
which we denote

\displaystyle\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}=\sqrt{\frac{(L-N-m)!}{m!(L-N)!}}(\eta^{-})^{m}\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}\,,

(25)

where
0\leq m\leq L-N
. The prefactor included in the definition ensures that the norm of this state is the same as the highest weight state i.e.

\innerproduct{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}=\innerproduct{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}.

(26)

Using the commutation relations (
10
) the energy of the descendant states is easily found to be

H\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}=[E+m(\mathfrak{u}-2\mu)]\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}.

(27)

II.1
String Hypothesis and Thermodynamic limit

As is typically the case, the Bethe ansatz equations (
17
), (
18
) admit both real and complex solutions. In thermodynamic limit

\lim_{\rm th}\equiv\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N,L,M\to\infty\\
N/L=\text{fixed}\\
M/L=\text{fixed}\end{subarray}},

(28)

however, the system can be described assuming that the complex solutions form regular patterns in the complex plane known as strings
Takahashi 1999
;
Essler et al. 1992
. In the Hubbard model the strings come in two types:

(i)

“
\lambda
strings” in which the
\lambda
spin rapidities take the form

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,j,\gamma}=\lambda_{n,\gamma}+i(n+1-2j)U/2,\qquad\lambda_{n,\gamma}\in\mathbb{R},\qquad j=1,\dots,n,

(29)

up to a correction vanishing exponentially in the thermodynamic limit. A
\lambda
string describes a multiparticle excitation in the spin sector of the model. It has
S^{z}=-n
but zero charge,
N=0
while its energy and momentum are also zero.

(ii)

“
k\!-\!\lambda
strings” consisting of a set of
2n
complex momenta
k_{l,\gamma},~l=1,\dots,2n
and a
\lambda
string. The latter takes same form as above

\lambda^{\prime}_{n,j,\gamma}=\lambda^{\prime}_{n,\gamma}+i(n+1-2j)U/2,\qquad\lambda^{\prime}_{n,\gamma}\in\mathbb{R},\qquad j=1,\dots,n,

(30)

whereas the former are given by

\displaystyle k_{1,\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi-\arcsin{(\lambda'_{n,\gamma}+ni U/2)}

(31)

\displaystyle k_{2,\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arcsin{(\lambda'_{n,\gamma}+(n-2)i U/2)}

\displaystyle k_{3,\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi-k_{2,\gamma}

\displaystyle k_{2n-1,\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi-k_{2n-2,\gamma}

\displaystyle k_{2n,\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi-\arcsin{(\lambda'_{n,\gamma}-i nU/2)}.

A
k\!-\!\lambda
string describes a multiparticle excitation, this time in the charge sector of the model. It has charge
N=2n
but is a spin singlet
S^{z}=0
and its energy and momentum are

\displaystyle e_{k-\lambda,n}(\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\mathfrak{t}\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\cos(k_{j})=4\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{R}\left[\sqrt{1-(\lambda^{\prime}+niU/2})^{2}\right]

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Un+\mathfrak{t}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{{\rm d}k}{\pi}\cos^{2}{(k)}\phi_{n}(\lambda^{\prime}-\sin(k)),

\displaystyle p_{k-\lambda,n}(\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2n}k_{j}=-2\mathfrak{R}\left[\arcsin{(\lambda'+ni U/2)}\right]~\text{mod}2\pi

(33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{{\rm d}k}{\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{2(\sin(k)-\lambda^{\prime})}{nU}\right)~\text{mod}2\pi.

We note that the energy and momentum reported above are the energy increase caused by the addition of a string excitation to a state with no other particles and hence they are referred to as
bare
. When other particles are also present the energy increase caused by the addition of an excitation is different. This fact is typically expressed by saying that energy and momentum of an excitation are “dressed by the interactions”.

Under the string hypothesis, a given eigenstate is described by its content of
\lambda
strings,
k-\lambda
strings, and real momenta. The key simplification introduced by this hypothesis is that also
\lambda
strings,
k-\lambda
strings are fully specified by real numbers, i.e.,
\lambda_{n,\gamma}
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{n,\gamma}
(cf. (
29
)–(
31
)). These quantities, called
string centers
, are interpreted as the real rapidities of different elementary particles.

In any finite volume real rapidities take discrete values which are obtained solving the Bethe equations (
17
) and (
18
). In the thermodynamic limit, however, they take real values and can be described by distributions. To this aim we follow Ref.
Takahashi 1972
and introduce the distributions of “particles”, “holes”
\rho(k)
and
\rho^{h}(k)
. In essence these quantities keep track of the distribution of real rapidities. Analogously, we introduce
\{\sigma_{n}(\lambda),~\sigma_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\}
, and
\{\sigma_{n}^{h}(\lambda),\sigma_{n}^{\prime\,h}(\lambda)\}
, specifying the distribution of the centres of
\lambda
and
k-\lambda
strings.

These distributions are coupled through the following set of integral equations originating from (
17
) and (
18
) and known as the Bethe-Takahashi equations
Takahashi 1972
;
Essler et al. 2005

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\rho(k)+\rho^{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}+\cos(k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int\frac{{\rm d}\lambda}{2\pi}\phi_{n}(s_{k}-\lambda)\left[\sigma_{n}(\lambda)+\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)\right],

(34)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sigma_{n}(\lambda)+\sigma_{n}^{h}(\lambda)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{{\rm d}k}{2\pi}\phi_{n}(s_{k}-\lambda)\rho(k)-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}T_{nm}*\sigma_{m}(\lambda),

(35)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)+{\sigma^{\prime}}_{n}^{h}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\mathfrak{R}\!\!\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-(\lambda+inU/2)^{2}}}\right]\!\!-\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{{\rm d}k}{2\pi}\phi_{n}(s_{k}-\lambda)\rho(k)-\!\!\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}T_{nm}*\sigma^{\prime}_{m}(\lambda),

(36)

where we used the shorthand notation (
16
), we denoted by
*
the convolution

f*g(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\lambda f(x-\lambda)g(\lambda),

(37)

and we introduced

\displaystyle T_{mn}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\phi_{m+n}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\phi_{|m-n|}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\phi_{m+n-2j}(\lambda),\qquad m\neq n,

(38)

\displaystyle T_{nn}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\phi_{2n}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\phi_{2n-2j}(\lambda).

(39)

These expressions can be brought to a partially decoupled form which is more convenient for numerical analysis. To do this we use

\sum_{n}[\delta+T]_{mn}^{-1}*f_{n}(x)=f_{m}(x)-s*[f_{m+1}(x)+f_{m-1}(x)],

(40)

where

s(x)\equiv\frac{1}{2U}\text{sech}(\frac{\pi x}{U}),

(41)

fulfils

s*[\phi_{m+1}(x)+\phi_{m-1}(x)]=\phi_{m}(x)\,,\qquad m=1,\ldots,

(42)

and we set
\phi_{0}(x)=\delta(x)
. Using (
40
) in (
35
) and (
36
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(\lambda)+\sigma_{n}^{h}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s*[\sigma_{n-1}^{h}(\lambda)+\sigma_{n+1}^{h}(\lambda)]+\delta_{n,1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}k\,s(s_{k}-\lambda)\rho(k),

(43)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)+\sigma_{n}^{\prime\,h}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s*[\sigma_{n-1}^{\prime\,h}(\lambda)+\sigma_{n+1}^{\prime\,h}(\lambda)]-\delta_{n,1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}k\,s(s_{k}-\lambda)\left[\rho(k)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\right],

(44)

\displaystyle\rho(k)+\rho^{h}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}+{\cos{(k)}}\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!{\rm d}\lambda\!\left[\frac{\phi_{1}(s_{k}-\lambda)}{2\pi}\sigma_{0}(\lambda)\!-\!s(s_{k}-\lambda)(\sigma_{1}^{h}(\lambda)+\sigma^{\prime\,h}_{1}(\lambda))\right]\!\!.

(45)

Here we have introduced

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(\lambda)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}ks(\lambda-s_{k})\rho_{0}(k),\qquad\rho_{0}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}+\cos{(k)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\nu}{2\pi}\frac{J_{0}(\nu)\cos{(\nu s_k)}}{1+e^{U|\nu|}},

(46)

where
J_{0}(\nu)
is zeroth order Bessel function of the first kind.

Considering the thermodynamic limit of (
19
) we have that the state described by the root densities
\{\rho(k),\sigma_{n}(\lambda),\sigma_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\}
has energy density given by

\displaystyle\lim_{\rm th}\frac{E}{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}k\left[-2\mathfrak{t}\cos{(k)}-h-\mu\right]\rho(k)+2h\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\lambda\sigma_{n}(\lambda)

(47)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\lambda\left(4\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{R}\left[\sqrt{1-(\lambda+inU/2)^{2}}\right]-2\mu n\right)\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda).

Moreover, the densities of the various string types read as

\displaystyle\lim_{\rm th}\frac{M_{n}}{L}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\lambda\,\sigma_{n}(\lambda),\qquad\qquad\qquad\lim_{\rm th}\frac{M^{\prime}_{n}}{L}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\lambda\,\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda),

(48)

\displaystyle\lim_{\rm th}\frac{N}{L}-\sum_{n}^{\infty}2n\frac{M^{\prime}_{n}}{L}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm d}k\,\rho(k).

(49)

III
Quench Protocol

We study the non-equilibrium dynamics of the Hubbard model by means of a quantum quench. In this protocol the system is initially prepared in some state
\ket{\Psi_0}
, which is not an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian (
II
), and is then allowed evolve according to the Schrödinger equation, i.e.

\ket{\Psi_t}=e^{-iHt}\ket{\Psi_0},\qquad t\geq 0\,.

(50)

The resulting time evolution can in principle be evaluated by inserting a complete set of eigenstates of
H
, which evolve via a simple phase, and computing the overlaps between eigenstates and the initial state.

Our initial state shall be chosen from a family of integrable initial states of the Hubbard model which lend themselves to explicit evaluation of an overlap formula
Piroli et al. 2017
. To determine these states we use the results of Refs.
Gombor and Bajnok 2020
;
Gombor and Bajnok 2021
, where the integrable initial states of certain
SU(2|2)
invariant spin chains are derived. Since the Hubbard model is known to arise as a limit of such spin chains
Beisert 2007
, we can directly read integrable initial states of the Hubbard model. As in other lattice models they take a two site product form and can be expressed through a
K
matrix,

\displaystyle K(\bm{\kappa},\alpha^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{1}&\kappa_{2}+\alpha^{\prime}&0&0\\
\kappa_{2}-\alpha^{\prime}&\kappa_{3}&0&0\\
0&0&0&\alpha^{\prime}\\
0&0&-\alpha^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix},

(51)

where
\alpha^{\prime}
is some free parameter and the remaining parameters
\bm{\kappa}=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})
are restricted by the condition
\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3}-\kappa_{2}^{2}=1
. The integrable states are a product over two site states with
K_{mn}
as the coefficients,

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_0}=\otimes_{l=1}^{L/2}\ket{K}_{l},\qquad\ket{K}_{l}=\sum_{m,n}K_{mn}\ket{m}_{2l-1}\otimes\ket{n}_{2l}.

(52)

Here the single site states are bosonic
\ket{1}_{l}=\ket{0}_{l},~\ket{2}_{l}=c^{\dagger}_{l,\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{l,\downarrow}\ket{0}=\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}
or fermionic
\ket{3}_{l}=c^{\dagger}_{l,\uparrow}\ket{0}=\ket{\uparrow},~\ket{4}_{l}=c^{\dagger}_{l,\downarrow}\ket{0}=\ket{\downarrow}
. More explicitly the integrable initial states of the Hubbard model are a product over

\displaystyle\ket{K}_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\kappa_{1}\ket{0}_{2l-1}\ket{0}_{2l}+\kappa_{3}\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}_{2l-1}\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}_{2l}\right]

(54)

\displaystyle+\left[(\kappa_{2}+\alpha^{\prime})\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}_{2l-1}\ket{0}_{2l}+(\kappa_{2}-\alpha^{\prime})\ket{0}_{2l-1}\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}_{2l}\right]

\displaystyle+\alpha^{\prime}\left[\ket{\uparrow}_{2l-1}\ket{\downarrow}_{2l}-\ket{\downarrow}_{2l-1}\ket{\uparrow}_{2l}\right]\,.

A second type of initial state was also identified in
Gombor and Bajnok 2020
. In the Hubbard limit this can be shown to be related to the above state by a Shiba transformation (
6
) and by combining this property with the transformations of the Hamiltonian (
4
),(
6
) the resulting quench dynamics can be understood by considering (
51
).

For simplicity we restrict ourselves to the case where the spin structure of the state is the same as that of a dimer and
N\leq L
by taking the limit

\kappa_{1}\to\infty,\qquad\kappa_{2}\to 0,\qquad\alpha^{\prime}\to\infty,\qquad\alpha^{\prime}/\kappa_{1}\equiv\alpha={\rm fixed}\,.

(55)

In this case we can write the initial state in the form

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\exp\left[\sum_{l=1}^{L/2}\alpha X_{l}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}X^{2}_{l}\right]\ket{0},

(56)

\displaystyle X_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{{\dagger}}_{2l-1\uparrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{2l-1\downarrow}-c^{{\dagger}}_{2l\uparrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{2l\downarrow}+c^{{\dagger}}_{2l-1\uparrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{2l\downarrow}-c^{{\dagger}}_{2l-1\downarrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{2l\uparrow},

\displaystyle X_{l}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4c^{{\dagger}}_{2l-1\uparrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{2l-1\downarrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{2l\uparrow}c^{{\dagger}}_{2l-1\downarrow},

\displaystyle X_{l}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

with the norm given by
\mathcal{N}=\left[1+4\alpha^{2}\right]^{L/4}
. Note this state is not an eigenstate of particle number but does become one in the limit
\alpha\to\infty
in which case it is half filled
N=L
.
The average particle number can be calculated straightforwardly

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\matrixelement{\Psi_0}{\hat{N}}{\Psi_0}=\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{1+4\alpha^{2}}

(57)

which is conserved by the dynamics. Moreover, we note that
\ket{\Psi_0}
is also not an eigenstate of the one-site shift operator. To facilitate the Quench Action treatment we therefore consider its translational invariant version

|{\tilde{\Psi}_{0}}\rangle\longmapsto\frac{1-T}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{\Psi_0}\,,

(58)

where
T
is the one-site shift operator. This replacement is harmless as long as there are no conserved charges not invariant under a one-site shift and, therefore, there is a restoration of one-site shift invariance in the stationary state. Finally, unless we explicitly state otherwise, from now on we set
h=\mu=0
in the time evolving Hamiltonian.

IV
Overlap formula

We want to calculate the squared overlap between the initial state and a Bethe state,

\displaystyle\frac{|\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};n}|^{2}}{\|\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};n}\|^{2}},

(59)

and furthermore to obtain a convenient expression which will allow us to take the thermodynamic limit and facilitate our study of the quench dynamics. Our choice of initial states will allow us to achieve this goal which we shall tackle in two stages. First, we derive the exact overlap in the infinite repulsion limit
U\to\infty
. Then, we consider the finite
U
case and derive a formula within the low density limit,
N\ll L
. Finally we use this expression to conjecture a formula valid at arbitrary filling following the method of Gombor & Bajnok
Gombor and Bajnok 2020
;
Gombor and Bajnok 2021
.

IV.1
Infinite
U
overlap

In the infinite-repulsion limit the system simplifies significantly resulting in a decoupling of the charge and spin degrees of freedom, while at the same time projecting out certain parts of the Hilbert space, namely states with doubly occupied states. Note that in this limit the model does not have the
SU(2)

\eta
symmetry but retains the full spin symmetry of the model while the particle-hole and Shiba transformations also remain valid.

In order to take the limit it is necessary to rexamine the Bethe equations. Therein we see that we can rescale the spin rapidities as
\lambda\to U\lambda
and then take the limit, in which case

\displaystyle e^{-ik_{j}L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\gamma}^{M}\frac{\lambda_{\gamma}-i/2}{\lambda_{\gamma}+i/2},

(60)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\lambda_{\gamma}-i/2}{\lambda_{\gamma}+i/2}\right]^{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\beta\neq\gamma}^{M}\frac{\lambda_{\beta}-\lambda_{\gamma}-i}{\lambda_{\beta}-\lambda_{\gamma}+i},

(61)

resulting in a partial decoupling of the spin and charge degrees of freedom. The latter becoming equivalent to a homogeneous
N
-site XXX chain and the former describing free fermions modulo a relationship between the momentum of the fermions and the total momentum of the spin chain, the right hand side of (
60
).

The eigenstates also decouple and become a product of a Slater determinant for the charge degrees of freedom times the spin part
\varphi_{XXX}(\vec{a})
which is the wavefunction for the homogeneous
XXX
chain

\displaystyle\psi(\vec{n},\vec{a})=\text{det}[e^{ik_{j}n_{l}}]\varphi_{XXX}(\vec{a}).

(62)

The norm of the state also factorizes

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}=L^{N}\prod_{1\leq\gamma<\beta\leq M}\left[\frac{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}+U^{2}}{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}}\right]\text{det}[G_{XXX}].

(63)

In order for us to perform checks on our calculations in this limit it is convenient to project the initial state into the same Hilbert space as the infinite
U
Hubbard by projecting out the doubly occupied sites which are forbidden in this limit. After doing this the initial state takes the form

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi^\infty_0}=\left[\frac{1}{1+2\alpha^{2}}\right]^{L/4}\exp\left[\alpha\sum_{l=1}^{L/2}c^{\dagger}_{2l-1\downarrow}c^{\dagger}_{2l\uparrow}-c^{\dagger}_{2l-1\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{2l\downarrow}\right]\ket{0}.

(64)

Note here the change in normalization of the state. The overlap between the initial state and a normalized
N
particle state is easily written out

\displaystyle\frac{\innerproduct{\Psi_0^\infty}{\{k_k\},\{\lambda_\gamma\}}}{\sqrt{\innerproduct{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\alpha/L)^{N/2}}{\left(1+2\alpha^{2}\right)^{L/4}}\sum_{l_{1}<\dots<l_{N/2}}\sum_{n_{1}<\dots<n_{N}}\text{det}[e^{ik_{j}n_{l}}]

(65)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\delta_{n_{2j-1},2l_{j}-1}\delta_{n_{2j},2l_{j}}\left[\frac{\varphi^{\text{d}}_{XXX}}{\|\varphi\|}\right],

where
{\varphi^{\text{d}}_{XXX}}/{\|\varphi\|}
is the normalized overlap between a dimer initial state and an eigenstate of the homogeneous
XXX
model

\displaystyle\varphi^{\text{d}}_{XXX}\equiv\sum_{\vec{a}}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\epsilon^{2j-1,2j}\varphi_{XXX}(\vec{a}),

(66)

with
\epsilon^{ii+1}
denoting the 2d Levi–Civita tensor on the spin space of
i
and
i+1
particles. Since this quantity has been calculated in
Pozsgay 2014
, we focus on the other terms first. We reduce them to a convenient form by first expressing the Slater determinant above as a sum over permutations in
\mathcal{S}_{N}
. Explicitly we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}<\dots<l_{N/2}}\sum_{n_{1}<\dots<n_{N}}\text{det}[e^{ik_{j}n_{l}}]\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\delta_{n_{2j-1},2l_{j}-1}\delta_{n_{2j},2l_{j}}

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}(-1)^{P}\sum_{l_{1}<\dots<l_{N/2}}\sum_{n_{1}<\dots<n_{N}}e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{N}k_{P_{l}}n_{l}}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\delta_{n_{2j-1},2l_{j}-1}\delta_{n_{2j},2l_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}(-1)^{P}\sum_{l_{1}<\dots<l_{N/2}}e^{2i\sum_{l=1}^{N/2}(k_{P_{2j-1}}+k_{P_{2j}})l_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left[\frac{e^{-ik_{P_{2j-1}}}-e^{-ik_{P_{2j}}}}{2}\right].

In going to the last line we make repeated use of the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}f(P_{1},...,P_{N})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}f(P)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}f(P^{\prime})

(68)

where
P^{\prime}
differs from
P
only because of the exchange of
P_{2j}
and
P_{2j-1}
. We then replace the ordered sum over two site blocks,
l_{j}
with an unordered sum by combining it with the sum over
P
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}<\dots<l_{N/2}}\sum_{n_{1}<\dots<n_{N}}\text{det}[e^{ik_{j}n_{l}}]\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\delta_{n_{2j-1},2l_{j}-1}\delta_{n_{2j},2l_{j}}=L^{N/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left[-i\sin{(k_j)}\right]\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\pi}}\left[\cos{(k_l)}\right].

(69)

Where we use the fact that the sum over
l_{j}
is only non zero if the momenta form pairs which are either
\{k_{j},-k_{j}\}
or
\{k_{l},\pi-k_{l}\}
. We take there to be
n
of the former pairs and
n^{\pi}
of the latter with
n+n^{\pi}=N/2
.

For the spin sector it has been shown that a dimer initial state has non zero overlap only with parity invariant states of the
XXX
model whose rapidities have a pair structure
\{\lambda_{\gamma},-\lambda_{\gamma}\}
with
\lambda_{\gamma}>0
and moreover that the total number of rapidities should be half the length of the chain, i.e.
M=N/2
. Combining the explicit result for the
XXX
dimer with (
69
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{|\innerproduct{\Psi_0^\infty}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}|^{2}}{\|\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}\|^{2}}=\frac{\alpha^{N}}{\left(1+2\alpha^{2}\right)^{L/2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sin^{2}{(k_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\pi}}\cos^{2}{(k_{l})}\prod_{\gamma}^{N/4}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}(\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}+1/4)}\frac{\text{det}[G^{+}_{XXX}]}{\text{det}[G^{-}_{XXX}]}.\quad

(70)

In the last term we have a ratio of Gaudin determinants
G^{\pm}_{XXX}
which is typical of overlap formulae for integrable initial states. The components of the matrices are

\displaystyle[G^{\pm}_{XXX}]_{\gamma\beta}=\delta_{\gamma\beta}\left[N\bar{\phi}_{1}(\lambda_{\gamma})-\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N/4}\bar{\phi}^{\pm}_{2}(\lambda_{\gamma},\lambda_{\gamma})\right]+\bar{\phi}^{\pm}_{2}(\lambda_{\gamma},\lambda_{\beta}),

(71)

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}^{\pm}_{n}(\lambda,\mu)=\bar{\phi}_{n}(\lambda-\mu)\pm\bar{\phi}_{n}(\lambda+\mu),~\bar{\phi}_{n}(\lambda)=\frac{n}{\lambda^{2}+(n/2)^{2}},

(72)

As shown in Refs.
Brockmann et al. 2014b
;
Mestyán et al. 2015
we have

\lim_{\rm th}\frac{\text{det}[G^{+}_{XXX}]}{\text{det}[G^{-}_{XXX}]}=1.

(73)

We can prove that Eq. (
70
) exhausts the set of states having non zero overlap with
\ket{\Psi_0^\infty}
by considering its normalization and inserting a complete set of states

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\Psi_0^\infty}{\Psi_0^\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{N=0}^{L}\sum_{\text{states}}\frac{|\innerproduct{\Psi_0^\infty}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}|^{2}}{\innerproduct{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}}

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}^{L/2}\frac{\alpha^{2m}}{\left(1+2\alpha^{2}\right)^{L/2}}\sum_{\text{states}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sin^{2}{(k_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\pi}}\cos^{2}{(k_{l})}\prod_{\gamma}^{m/4}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}(\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}+1/4)}\frac{\text{det}[G^{+}_{XXX}]}{\text{det}[G^{-}_{XXX}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}^{L/2}\frac{\alpha^{2m}}{\left(1+2\alpha^{2}\right)^{L/2}}\sum_{\text{states}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sin^{2}{(k_{j})}\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\pi}}\cos^{2}{(k_{l})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}^{L/2}\frac{\alpha^{2m}}{\left(1+2\alpha^{2}\right)^{L/2}}\sum^{\prime}_{\text{states}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left[\sin^{2}{(k_{j})}+\cos^{2}{(k_{l})}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}^{L/2}\frac{\alpha^{2m}}{\left(1+2\alpha^{2}\right)^{L/2}}\binom{L/2}{m}=1.

In going to the third line we have used the completeness of the states for the dimer
XXX
overlap and the fact that the spin and charge systems are decoupled. After this we take
k_{j}\in(-\pi,-\pi/2]\cup[0,\pi/2]
so that we can independently choose it to represent either a
\{k_{j},-k_{j}\}
pair or a
\{k_{j},\pi-k_{j}\}
pair. This allows us to rearrange the terms of the sum to be over the possible choices for the
m=N/2

k
’s within this fundamental region, denoted by
\sum^{\prime}_{\text{states}}
and weighted by their combined overlap,
\sin^{2}{(k_{j})}+\cos^{2}{(k_{l})}=1
.

IV.2
Finite
U
Overlap

Having derived the overlap in the simpler case
U=\infty
we now move on and consider its calculation for finite
U
. To do this end we follow the approach of Gombor and Bajnok
(
Gombor and Bajnok 2020
;
Gombor and Bajnok 2021
)
. As we shall see, this approach does not provide an exact derivation of the ovelaps but rather a well founded conjecture on their form. The idea is as follows: since we are dealing with integrable initial states we assume that, because of their special nature, the overlap formula will factorize into the following form (cf. (
70
))

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{|\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j,-k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\};m}|^{2}}{\|\ket{\{k_j,-k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\};m}\|^{2}}=\mathcal{A}_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\mathfrak{h}_{c}(k_{j})\prod_{\gamma}^{M/2}\mathfrak{h}_{s}(\lambda_{\gamma})\frac{\text{det}[G^{+}]}{\text{det}[G^{-}]},

(75)

where
\mathfrak{h}_{c,s}(x)
are as yet unknown single particle overlap functions,
G^{\pm}
are Gaudin matrices similar to (
71
) and
\mathcal{A}_{m}
is some combinatorial factor coming from the action of lowering operators, e.g.
\eta^{-}
or
S^{-}
acting on the initial state. At this point two remarks are in order:

(i)

The form (
75
) has been shown to arise in all cases were exact overlaps between Bethe states and integrable states can been calculated exactly
Pozsgay 2014
;
Brockmann et al. 2014b
;
Brockmann 2014
;
Brockmann et al. 2014c
;
Jiang and Pozsgay 2020
. In fact, comparisons with exact numerics in small systems showed that it holds for essentially all integrable states in integrable models
de Leeuw et al. 2016
;
de Leeuw et al. 2015
;
Buhl-Mortensen et al. 2016
;
Foda and Zarembo 2016
;
Pozsgay 2018
;
Gombor and Bajnok 2020
;
Gombor and Bajnok 2021
.

(ii)

In writing (
75
) we assumed that only states with pairs of opposite rapidities have non-zero overlap with an integrable state. In essence, this constraint comes from the request that the integrable state is annihilated by all conserved charges that are odd under spatial reflection
Piroli et al. 2017
. Even though the opposite rapidity pairing is the generic way to fulfil this reflection symmetry condition
Piroli et al. 2017
, it is not the only one
Frolov and Quinn 2012
;
Beisert 2007
. For instance, in the previous section we saw that in the strong coupling limit of Hubbard we also have pairs of the form
\{k,\pi-k\}
(alternative pairings for the
U=\infty
Hubbard have been observed also for different initial states
Bertini et al. 2017
;
Tartaglia et al. 2022
). Here we note that for Hubbard such non-generic parings are a feature of the strong-coupling limit and do not correspond to consistent solutions of the Bethe equations for finite
U
. This can be explicitly checked by inserting such pairs into (
17
) and (
18
) for
N=2,4,\dots
. Upon doing so it is seen that only the allowed pair of this form is
\{0,\pi\}
if it is also accompanied by
\lambda=0
.

A crucial feature of Eq. (
75
) is that, since in the thermodynamic limit the ratio of the Gaudin determinants approaches
1
, the extensive part of the overlap is given by the functions
\mathfrak{h}_{c,s}(x)
. Thus, it can be determined by a low-density calculation as we now explicitly show.

We begin by noting that our initial state is a spin singlet and so a nonzero overlap between it and an
N
particle state requires
M=N/2
which we take to be even (the
M
odd case can be treated in the same fashion). Directly computing the overlap for the highest weight state we find

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j,-k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{N/2}}{(1+4\alpha^{2})^{L/4}}\sum^{L/2}_{l_{1}<l_{2}\dots<l_{N/2}}\sum^{L}_{n_{1}\leq n_{2}\dots\leq n_{N}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}(-1)^{P}e^{i\sum_{j}k_{P_{j}}n_{j}}

(76)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left[\delta_{n_{2j-1},2l_{j}-1}\delta_{n_{2j},2l_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{2l_{j}-1,n_{2j-1}}\delta_{2l_{j}-1,n_{2j}}-\delta_{2l_{j},n_{2j-1}}\delta_{2l_{j},n_{2j}}\right)\right]\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}\,,

where the product over Kronecker deltas comes from the charge part of the overlap and

\displaystyle\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}\equiv\sum_{\vec{a}}\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\epsilon^{2j-1,2j}\varphi_{P}(\vec{a}).

(77)

This latter term is the overlap between a dimer state and the eigenstates of an inhomogeneous
XXX
spin chain with the arrangement of inhomogeneities depending on the permutation
P
. In the
U\to\infty
limit the inhomogeneities vanish and it reduces to the previous result of
\varphi_{XXX}^{\text{d}}
. Using the form of the wavefunction we can write down this factor explicitly

\displaystyle\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}=\sum_{Q\in S_{M}}\prod_{1\leq Q_{\gamma}<Q_{\beta}\leq M}\frac{\lambda_{Q_{\gamma}}-\lambda_{Q_{\beta}}-iU}{\lambda_{Q_{\gamma}}-\lambda_{Q_{\beta}}}\prod^{N/2}_{l=1}\frac{s_{P_{2l-1}}-s_{P_{2l}}+iU}{\lambda_{Q_{l}}-s_{P_{2l}}+iU/2}F_{P}(\lambda_{Q_{l}},2l-1).

(78)

From this expression we note that given two permutations
P,~P^{\prime}
which differ by exchanging adjacent spins
P_{2j-1}\leftrightarrow P_{2j}
the overlaps are related by a phase factor

\displaystyle\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{s_{P_{2l-1}}-s_{P_{2l}}-iU}{s_{P_{2l-1}}-s_{P_{2l}}+iU}\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}=-e^{2i\Phi(k_{P_{2l-1}},k_{P_{2l}})}\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P},

(79)

which is the bare scattering phase shift between two particles of opposite spin. We proceed as we did in the
U\to\infty
case by splitting the sum over permutations into one over different classes which contain a certain permutation
P
and all others which can be obtained from it by exchanges of the form
P_{2j-1}\leftrightarrow P_{2j}
.
Our overlap can then be expressed as a sum over these classes

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j,-k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{N}}{(1+4\alpha^{2})^{L/2}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}(-1)^{P}\Gamma_{P}(\vec{k})\Delta_{P}(\vec{k})\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}\,,

(80)

where we introduced

\displaystyle\Delta_{P}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{N/2}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{-ik_{P_{2l-1}}}+e^{-ik_{P_{2l}}+2i\Phi(k_{P_{2l-1}},k_{P_{2l}})}\right)\right.

(81)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\left(\frac{1-e^{i(k_{P_{2l}}+k_{P_{2l-1}})}}{4}\right)\left(1+e^{2i\Phi(k_{P_{2l-1}},k_{P_{2l}})}\right)\right],

\displaystyle\Gamma_{P}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{L/2}_{l_{1}<l_{2}\dots<l_{N/2}}e^{2i\sum_{j}^{N/2}(k_{P_{2j-1}}+k_{P_{2j}})l_{j}}.

(82)

This form is similar to the one we found for
U=\infty
with the significant difference that the spin part now depends on the permutation preventing us from summing the terms as we did before. The function
\Gamma_{P}(\vec{k})
will be largest if the phase in the summand is just
1
, i.e., if the momenta are paired as
k_{P_{2j-1}}=-k_{P_{2j}}
. Other terms are
\mathcal{O}(1/L^{2})
with respect to these. Moreover, for such a paring of homogeneities the spin factor has been calculated
Gombor and Pozsgay 2021

\displaystyle\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}=\prod_{\nu=1}^{N/4}\frac{U^{2}}{\lambda_{\nu}\sqrt{\lambda_{\nu}^{2}+(U/2)^{2}}}\prod_{1\leq\gamma<\beta\leq N/2}\frac{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}+U^{2}}{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}}\text{det}[G^{+}_{iXXX}],

(83)

where
G^{+}_{iXXX}
is the Gaudin matrix for the inhomogeneous chain. From the above expression we see that the dependence on the inhomogeneities appears only through the Gaudin matrix and moreover this is independent of any particular ordering of the inhomogeneities so that
\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}=\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P^{\prime}}
. This then allows us to use the sum over permutations to rewrite the ordered sum over
n_{j}
into an unordered one as we did before.
Using this we get

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j,-k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{N/2}}{(1+4\alpha^{2})^{L/4}}L^{N}\prod_{l=1}^{N/2}e^{i\Phi_{k_{l}}}\left[\cos{(k_l+\Phi_{k_l})}\right]\varphi^{\text{d}}_{P}

(84)

\displaystyle+\dots

where we have introduced the phase shift
\Phi_{k}=\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan{\left(\frac{2s_k}{U}\right)}
. The ellipsis refers to terms coming from the permutations which do not have the pairing structure of the homogeneities and which are lower order in
L
. At low density the norm of the Bethe state has the leading term

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\|\ket{{\{k_j,-k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma,-\lambda_\gamma\}}}\|^{2}=U^{N/2}L^{N}\!\!\!\prod_{1\leq\gamma<\beta\leq N/2}\left[\frac{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}+U^{2}}{(\lambda_{\gamma}-\lambda_{\beta})^{2}}\right]\text{det}[G_{iXXX}],

(85)

where we neglected sub-leading contributions in
L
. Combining these two formulae together we have that the normalized squared overlap in the low density limit
N\ll L
is

\displaystyle\frac{|\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}|^{2}}{\innerproduct{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{N}}{(1+4\alpha^{2})^{L/2}}\prod_{l=1}^{N/2}|\cos{(k_l+\Phi_{k_l})}|^{2}\prod_{\alpha}^{N/4}\frac{U^{4}}{\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}{(\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}+(U/2)^{2})}}.

From this we can read off the single particle overlap functions and overall constant factor

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}=\frac{\alpha^{N}}{(1+4\alpha^{2})^{L/2}},\quad\mathfrak{h}_{c}(k)=|\cos{(k+\Phi_{k})}|^{2},\quad\mathfrak{h}_{s}(\lambda)=\frac{U^{4}}{\lambda^{2}{(\lambda^{2}+(U/2)^{2})}}.

(86)

Plugging now in (
75
) we find the following conjecture for the overlap formula at arbitrary density and interactions strength

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{|\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}|^{2}}{\|\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{N}}{(1+4\alpha^{2})^{L/2}}\prod_{l=1}^{N/2}\frac{s_{k_{l}}^{2}[c_{k_{l}}+U/2]^{2}}{s_{k_{l}}^{2}+(U/2)^{2}}\prod_{\gamma}^{N/4}\frac{U^{4}}{\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}{(\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}+(U/2)^{2})}}\frac{\text{det}[G^{+}]}{\text{det}[G^{-}]},

(87)

where we used the shorthand notation (
16
) and introduced the
\frac{3}{4}N\times\frac{3}{4}N
Gaudin matrices given by (cf. (
21
) and (
71
))

\displaystyle G^{\pm}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\left[\frac{L}{\cos{(k_j)}}+\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N/4}\phi^{\pm}_{1}(\lambda_{\gamma},s_{j})\right],

(88)

\displaystyle G^{\pm}_{i,N/2+\gamma}

\displaystyle=G_{N/2+\gamma,i}=-\phi^{\pm}_{1}(\lambda_{\gamma}-s_{j}),

(89)

\displaystyle G^{\pm}_{N/2+\gamma,N/2+\beta}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\gamma\beta}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N/2}\phi^{\pm}_{1}(\lambda_{\gamma},s_{j})-\sum_{\gamma=1}^{N/4}\phi^{\pm}_{2}(\lambda_{\gamma},\lambda_{\gamma})\right]+\phi^{\pm}_{2}(\lambda_{\gamma},\lambda_{\beta}),

(90)

with
i,j\in[1,N/2]
,
\gamma,\beta\in[1,N/4]
and
\phi^{\pm}_{n}(\lambda,\mu)=\phi_{n}(\lambda-\mu)\pm\phi_{n}(\lambda+\mu)
. This expression is valid for momenta and spin rapidities paired as
\{k_{j},-k_{j}\}
and
\{\lambda_{\gamma},-\lambda_{\gamma}\}
. The
U=\infty
result of the previous subsection can be recovered from this formula after an appropriate scaling of the rapidities.

The overlap with the descendant states
\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}
can be calculated in a similar fashion. For instance, consider
\matrixelement{\Psi_0}{\eta^-}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\}}
and act with the lowering operator to the left on the initial state instead of the eigenstate. Using
\bra{K_l}\eta^{-}=(-2\alpha)\bra{0}_{2l-1}\otimes\bra{0}_{2l}
we have that the resulting state takes the same form as
\bra{\Psi_0}
but with modified coefficients whose overlap can then be calculated as detailed above. For generic
m\leq(L-N)/2
this procedure yields

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{|\innerproduct{\Psi_0}{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}|^{2}}{\|\ket{\{k_j\}\{\lambda_\gamma\};m}\|^{2}}\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{\mathcal{A}_{m}\alpha^{N}}{(1+4\alpha^{2})^{L/2}}\prod_{l=1}^{N/2}\frac{s_{k_{l}}^{2}[c_{k_{l}}+U/2]^{2}}{s_{k_{l}}^{2}+(U/2)^{2}}\prod_{\gamma=1}^{N/4}\frac{U^{4}}{\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}{(\lambda_{\gamma}^{2}+(U/2)^{2})}}\frac{\text{det}[G^{+}]}{\text{det}[G^{-}]},

(91)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\alpha)^{2m}\frac{(L-N-m)!}{m!(L-N)!}\left[\frac{\left(\frac{L-N}{2}\right)!}{\left(\frac{L-N}{2}-m\right)!}\right]^{2}.

(92)

Note that only the overlap with the state with
N+2m=L
survives in the
\alpha\to\infty
limit.

that gives the dominant contribution its norm

\innerproduct*{\tilde\Psi_0}{\tilde\Psi_0}=\sum_{\text{states}}|\innerproduct*{n}{\tilde\Psi_0}|^{2}\,.

(95)

Exchanging the sum over states for a functional integral over the real rapidity distributions we have

\displaystyle\innerproduct*{\tilde\Psi_0}{\tilde\Psi_0}\!\to\!\int\!\!D\rho(k)\!\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\!D\sigma_{n}(\lambda)D\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)e^{LS[\rho(k),\sigma_{n}(\lambda),\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)]}|\!\innerproduct*{\rho(k),\sigma_n(\lambda),\sigma'_n(\lambda)}{\tilde\Psi_0}|^{2}

(96)

where the functional
S[\rho(k),\sigma_{n}(\lambda),\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)]
counts the number microstates with non-zero overlap with
\ket{\Psi_0}
corresponding to a particular set of distributions. In the thermodynamic limit the functional integral is evaluated in the saddle point approximation. Namely one has to solve

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S^{QA}}{\delta\rho}\Big|_{\rho,\sigma_{n},{\sigma^{\prime}}_{n}=\rho^{*},\sigma^{*}_{n},{\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}_{n}}=\frac{\delta S^{QA}}{\delta\sigma_{n}}\Big|_{\rho,\sigma_{n},{\sigma^{\prime}}_{n}=\rho^{*},\sigma^{*}_{n},{\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}_{n}}=\frac{\delta S^{QA}}{\delta{\sigma^{\prime}}_{n}}\Big|_{\rho,\sigma_{n},{\sigma^{\prime}}_{n}=\rho^{*},\sigma^{*}_{n},{\sigma^{\prime}}^{*}_{n}}=0.

(97)

where we introduced the eponymous Quench Action

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!S^{QA}[\rho(k),\sigma_{n}(\lambda),\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)]=-\frac{2}{L}\mathfrak{R}\left[\log\innerproduct*{\tilde\Psi_0}{\rho(k),\sigma_n(\lambda),\sigma_n(\lambda)}\right]\!-\!S[\rho(k),\sigma_{n}(\lambda),\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(\lambda)].

(98)

In the following subsections we use (
97
) to determine the rapidity distributions of the representative eigenstate reached after quenches from the states (
56
) to the Hubbard model for both infinite and finite
U
.

V.1
Infinite
U
steady state

Let us first deal with the
U\to\infty
limit, which, as we shall see, represents a somewhat singular point of our quench in a number of respects. As we have seen in Sec.
IV.1
, in this limit the initial state allows for an unusual pairing structure in the momenta. That is, while the spin rapidities appear in pairs of opposite sign
\{\lambda_{\gamma},-\lambda_{\gamma}\}
the momenta are either paired as
\{k_{j},-k_{j}\}
which is standard or with a shift of
\pi
,
\{k_{j},\pi-k_{j}\}
. This is related to the existence of a number of additional conserved charges which exist only in this limit
Fagotti 2014
;
Bertini and Fagotti 2015
and requires some slight modifications of the Quench Action method. These subtleties are absent at finite
U
but nevertheless it is instructive to fully understand them before moving on.

We shall take advantage of the decoupling of the charge and spin degrees of freedom and note that the quench dynamics of the latter are exactly those of the dimer to
XXX
chain which has been studied before. The dynamics of the charge degrees of freedom, however, can be understood by using the following Hamiltonian describing a single species of spinless fermions

\displaystyle H^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}-\mathfrak{t}\left(c^{\dagger}_{j}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+1}+c^{\dagger}_{j+1}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j}\right),

(99)

and quenched from the initial state

\ket{\Psi'_0}=\frac{1}{(1+\alpha^{2})^{L/4}}\exp\left[\alpha\sum_{j}^{L/2}c^{\dagger}_{2j-1}c^{\dagger}_{2j}\right],

(100)

which is
not
Gaussian for the fermions
\{c^{\dagger}_{j},c_{j}\}
.

The overlaps between the eigenstates of
H^{\prime}
and
\ket{\Psi'_0}
have the same form as (
65
) but without the spin part and so we can use this simplified setting to understand the dynamics on our system. We can straightforwardly calculate any fermion correlation functions in this initial state, in particular after going to Fourier space we find the following conserved two-point functions

\displaystyle\matrixelement{\Psi_0'}{\tilde{c}^\dag_k \tilde{c}^{\phantom{\dag}}_k}{\Psi_0'}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}},

(101)

\displaystyle\matrixelement{\Psi_0'}{\tilde{c}^\dag_k \tilde{c}^\dag_{\pi-k}}{\Psi_0'}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha^{2}}\cos{(k)}.

(102)

Moreover, we also find the following conserved four-point function

\displaystyle\matrixelement{\Psi_0'}{\tilde{c}^\dag_k \tilde{c}^\dag_p\tilde{c}^{\phantom{\dag}}_p \tilde{c}^{\phantom{\dag}}_k}{\Psi_0'},

(103)

which has a non-trivial connected part

\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}\matrixelement{\Psi_0'}{\tilde{c}^\dag_k \tilde{c}^\dag_p\tilde{c}^{\phantom{\dag}}_p \tilde{c}^{\phantom{\dag}}_k}{\Psi_0'}^{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}\left[\frac{1-\cos(k-p)}{2}\right].

(104)

Eq. (
101
) gives us the distribution of momenta in the steady state, while the fact that the correlators in Eqs. (
102
) and (
104
) are nonzero tells us that the momentum distribution is not sufficient to completely characterize the steady state after the quench. This also means that to describe this quench one cannot apply Quench Action in its standard form.

To overcome this issue one has to include a small but finite magnetic field in the Hamiltonian (
99
), so that the two point function (
102
), which is not invariant under one-site shifts, is not conserved anymore and we can consider the translational invariant state

|{\tilde{\Psi}^{\prime}_{0}}\rangle=\frac{1-T}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{\Psi'_0}\,.

(105)

Even doing this, the Quench Action treatment is non-trivial because of the presence of non-trivial connected four point correlations. To proceed we introduce the distributions
\rho(k),\rho^{\pi}(k)
and
\rho^{h}(k)
describing the distributions of momenta
k\in[0,\pi/2]
which are part of
\{k,-k\}
pairs (
\rho
),
\{k,\pi-k\}
pairs (
\rho^{\pi}
) and the holes. We also introduce the corresponding distributions
\bar{\rho}(k),\bar{\rho}^{\pi}(k),\bar{\rho}^{h}(k)
which have the same meaning but with
k\in[-\pi,-\pi/2]
. These satisfy

\rho(k)+\rho^{\pi}(k)+\rho^{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi},

(106)

and also

\bar{\rho}(k)+\bar{\rho}^{\pi}(k)+\bar{\rho}^{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi},

(107)

that are the noninteracting Bethe equations. Recall that we require this division of the fundamental domain for
k
so that we can say that each momenta corresponds to either one type of pair or the other or a hole. The Quench Action for this system is then found to be

\displaystyle S^{QA}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}{\rm d}k\left[\log{\left({\alpha\cos(k)}\right)}\rho^{\pi}(k)+\log{\left({\alpha\sin(k)}\right)}\rho(k)\right]

(108)

\displaystyle-2\int_{-\pi}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}}{\rm d}k\left[\log{\left({\alpha\cos(k)}\right)}\bar{\rho}^{\pi}(k)+\log{\left({\alpha\sin(k)}\right)}\bar{\rho}(k)\right]

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\pi/2}{\rm d}k\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{1}{2\pi}-\rho(k)\log{\rho}(k)-\rho^{\pi}(k)\log{\rho^\pi(k)}-\rho^{h}(k)\log\rho^{h}(k)\right]

\displaystyle-\int_{-\pi}^{-\pi/2}{\rm d}k\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{1}{2\pi}-\bar{\rho}(k)\log{\bar{\rho}(k)}-\bar{\rho(k)}^{\pi}(k)\log{\bar{\rho}^\pi}(k)-\bar{\rho}^{h}(k)\log\bar{\rho}^{h}(k)\right].

The first and second lines here come directly form the overlap formula while the other two are the entropy associated to the choices of microstates corresponding the distributions which differs from the standard Yang-Yang entropy. Minimizing this functional we find that the saddle point of the Quench Action and hence the long time steady state has

\displaystyle\zeta(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\sin^{2}{(k)}}=\bar{\zeta}(k),

(109)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\pi}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}{(k)}}=\bar{\zeta}^{\pi}(k),

(110)

where
\zeta(k)=\rho^{h}(k)/\rho(k)
,
\zeta^{\pi}(k)=\rho^{h}(k)/\rho^{\pi}(k)
,
\bar{\zeta}(k)=\bar{\rho}^{h}(k)/\bar{\rho}(k)
, and
\bar{\zeta}^{\pi}(k)=\bar{\rho}^{h}(k)/\bar{\rho}^{\pi}(k)
. Combining these with our noninteracting Bethe equations we find the distributions to be

\displaystyle 2\pi\rho(k)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}\sin^{2}{(k)},\quad 2\pi\rho^{\pi}(k)=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}\cos^{2}{(k)},\quad 2\pi\rho^{h}(k)=\frac{1}{1+\alpha^{2}}.

(111)

Note that these coincide with the connected four point function (
104
) for
p=-k
and
p=\pi-k
. Upon summing over both types of particle pairs we can then reproduce the constant mode occupation

\rho(k)+\rho^{\pi}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha^{2}}\,,

(112)

obtained above from direct calculation. Alternatively one could directly sum over both types of pairing structures prior to taking the thermodynamic limit as was when showing the completeness of the states (
74
). This results in a constant
\zeta(k)=1/\alpha^{2}
from which we directly reproduce (
101
) but which cannot provide the relative occupation of the different pairs.

V.2
Finite
U
Steady state

In the finite
U
case we have only one type of pairing structure and so the Quench Action can be applied more straightforwardly. The only complication arises from the fact that we must now consider the descendant states that have overlap with
|{\tilde{\Psi}_{0}}\rangle
. This would require summing over these states also and weighting them by
-\log{\mathcal{A}_m}
in the Quench Action, however this combinatorial factor does not admit a pleasant form in the thermodynamic limit. In particular, using Stirling’s approximation we find

\displaystyle\log{\mathcal{A}_m}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle(L-N-m)\log{(L-N-m)}-(L-N-2m)\log{(L-N-2m)}

(113)

\displaystyle-m\log{(m)}+2m\log{(\alpha)}.

We should then sum over both
m
and
N
subject to the restriction
(N+2m)/L=4\alpha^{2}/(1+4\alpha^{2})
which in the Quench Action can be imposed by the inclusion of a Lagrange multiplier. The sums over
m
and
N
could then be evaluated by saddle point thereby yielding a nonlinear relationship between the saddle point values
m^{*},N^{*}
and
\alpha
and the Lagrange multiplier. A more straightforward approach which we shall adopt is to absorb these various factors into a redefinition of
\alpha\to\tilde{\alpha}
and vary
\tilde{\alpha}

post hoc
to fit the desired initial average particle number (
57
). Following this logic the Quench Action is now

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!S^{QA}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\pi}_{0}\!\!{\rm d}k\left[g(k)+h(k)\right]\rho(k)

A remarkable feature of quenches from integrable initial states is that for these quenches the knowledge of the saddle point state

;

The von Neumann entanglement entropy between a subsystem

Alba and Calabrese 2017
;

VII.2
Rényi entropies

Alternative measures of the entanglement between a subsystem,
A
and its compliment are given by the Rényi entanglement entropies which are defined as

\displaystyle S_{A}^{(n)}(t)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\text{Tr}_{A}\varrho_{A}^{n}(t)\right],

(140)

where
n\in\mathbb{R}
. In the limit
n\to 1
this recovers the von Neumann entanglement entropy discussed in the previous subsection. Besides providing full access to the spectrum of
\varrho_{A}(t)
— the entanglement spectrum —
S_{A}^{(n\geq 2)}(t)
are of significant interest because they can be measured in actual experiments
Islam et al. 2015
;
Kaufman et al. 2016
;
Linke et al. 2018
;
Lukin et al. 2019
;
Elben et al. 2020
;
Zhou et al. 2020
;
Neven et al. 2021
;
Kokail et al. 2021
;
Vitale et al. 2022
. Like the von Neumann entropy,
S_{A}^{(n)}(t)
is expected to show an initial linear growth with time before eventually saturating. The long time limit is given by

\displaystyle D^{(n)}=\lim_{|A|\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{S_{A}^{(n)}(t)}{|A|}

(141)

where the second limit is taken to avoid any boundary effects. In
Alba and Calabrese 2017
;
Alba and Calabrese 2017
it was shown that
D^{(n)}
can be calculated for integrable models using a modification of the Quench Action formalism. In particular, it can be written as

\displaystyle D^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}dk\,d^{(n)}(k)+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\lambda\left[d_{m}^{(n)}(\lambda)+d^{\prime(n)}_{m}(\lambda)\right],

(142)

where the constituents are

\displaystyle d^{(n)}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(\rho(k)+\rho^{h}(k))}{1-n}\Bigg\{\log{\left[(1-\vartheta(k))^n+\frac{\vartheta(k)^n}{x^{(n)}(k)} \right]}+\vartheta(k)\log{[x^{(n)}(k)]}\Bigg\},

\displaystyle d^{(n)}_{m}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\frac{2(\sigma_{m}(\lambda)+\sigma_{m}^{h}(\lambda))}{1-n}\Bigg\{\log{\left[(1-\vartheta_m(\lambda))^n+\frac{\vartheta_m(\lambda)^n}{x^{(n)}_m(\lambda)} \right]}+\vartheta_{m}(\lambda)\log{[x^{(n)}_m(\lambda)]}\Bigg\},

VIII
Conclusions

In this paper we drew on previous work on integrable
SU(2|2)
-invariant spin chains
Gombor and Bajnok 2020
;
Gombor and Bajnok 2021
to identify a class of integrable initial states for the one-dimensional Hubbard model. We then proposed a formula (cf. Eq. (
91
)) describing the overlaps between these states and the Bethe states of the system, which we tested in the limits of low density and infinite repulsion. We used our formula to provide an exact characterisation, for arbitrary values of the interaction, of the statistical ensemble describing local subsystems at long times after quenches from our integrable initial states. We also described the growth of entanglement using the quasiparticle picture for the von Neumann entropy
Alba and Calabrese 2017
, and the recently developed spacetime swap formalism for Rényi entropies
Bertini et al. 2022a
. The calculation of other observables (e.g., one- or two-point functions of local operators) requires the knowledge of the the corresponding form factors, as done for instance in the XXZ spin-chain
Mestyán and Pozsgay 2014
, the sinh-Gordon field theory
Negro and Smirnov 2013
;
Negro 2014
;
Bertini et al. 2016b
, and the Lieb-Liniger model
Pozsgay 2011
;
Bastianello et al. 2018
;
Bastianello and Piroli 2018
. Although these are not yet known we hope that our results will spark further research in this direction.

Here we identified several different families of integrable initial states for the Hubbard model, but we only computed the overlap for a specific one. An interesting future direction would be to find analogous overlap formulas for different classes of states. For instance, this would allow for the exact characterisation of inhomogeneous quenches in the genuine out-of-equilibrium scenario of different non-stationary states joined together (along the lines of the “global” inhomogeneous quenches studied in Ref.
Bertini et al. 2016a
for the XXZ spin chain). Furthermore, another class of treatable initial states are integrable matrix product states that could be constructed along the lines of Ref.
Piroli et al. 2018
for the XXZ spin-chain.

Another interesting direction, which we undertake in a companion paper Ref.
Rylands et al. 2022
, is to use our formula to study the quenches in the Gaudin-Yang electron gas via a continuum limit. The latter system is particularly interesting in view of its direct experimental accessibility. Finally, recent experiments with ultra-cold alkaline-earth atoms
Pagano et al. 2014
motivate the study of Hubbard-like models (and their continuum counterparts) with
SU(N)
internal symmetry. For the quench problem, such study should be possible by some non-trivial adaptation of our technique to obtain the overlaps.

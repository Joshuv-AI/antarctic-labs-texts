Invertibility properties of operator matrices on Hilbert spaces
Nikola Sarajlija

Abstract
Denote by T n d ​ ( A ) T_{n}^{d}(A) an upper triangular operator matrix of dimension n n whose diagonal entries are given and the others are unknown. In this article we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for various types of Fredholm and Weyl completions of T n d ​ ( A ) T_{n}^{d}(A) . As consequences, we get corrected perturbation results of Wu et al. (Ann. Funct. Anal. 11 (2020), no. 3, 780–798 and Acta Math. Sin. (Engl. Ser.) 36 (2020), no. 7, 783–796). In the special case n = 2 n=2 , we recover many already existing known results, and specially we correct results of Zhang et al. (J. Math. Anal. Appl. 392 (2012), no. 2, 103–110). Finally, in the case of essential Fredholm invertibility, in the special case n = 2 n=2 we obtain some results that seem new in the literature. Our method is based on the space decomposition technique, similarly to the work of Huang et al. (Math. Nachr. 292 (2019), no. 11, 2411–2426 and Math. Nachr. 291 (2018), no. 1, 187–203), but our approach extends to arbitrary dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 .

Preliminaries

Throughout this text we always assume that
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H}_{1},...,\mathcal{H}_{n}
are infinite dimensional separable Hilbert spaces. Collection of operators (linear and bounded mappings) from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
. Letter
T
is most commonly used to denote some operator, and we keep standard notation
\mathcal{N}(T)
,
\mathcal{R}(T)
for the null and range space of
T
, respectively. As always, if
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
, then
T^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H})
is the adjoint of
T
.

In this article we investigate various types of invertibility that occur in Fredholm theory. First, we introduce some notation
[
19
]
. Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\alpha(T)=\dim\mathcal{N}(T)
and
\beta(T)=\dim\mathcal{N}(T^{*})
. In the previous sentence,
\dim
is the orthogonal Hilbert dimension. We call
\alpha(T)
and
\beta(T)
the nullity and the deficiency of
T
, respectively, and in the case where at least one of them is finite we define
\mathrm{ind}(T)=\alpha(T)-\beta(T)
to be the index of
T
. Now we are able to define families of upper and lower semi-Fredholm operators, respectively, as

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}(\mathcal{H})=\left\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\ \alpha(T)<\infty\ and\ \mathcal{R}(T)\ is\ closed\right\}

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{-}(\mathcal{H})=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\beta(T)<\infty\}.

The set of Fredholm operators is

\Phi(\mathcal{H})=\Phi_{+}(\mathcal{H})\cap\Phi_{-}(\mathcal{H})=\{T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):\alpha(T)<\infty\ and\ \beta(T)<\infty\}.

Put

\Phi_{+}^{-}(\mathcal{H})=\{T\in\Phi_{+}(\mathcal{H}):\ \mathrm{ind}(T)\leq 0\}

and

\Phi_{-}^{+}(\mathcal{H})=\{T\in\Phi_{-}(\mathcal{H}):\ \mathrm{ind}(T)\geq 0\}.

These are collections of upper and lower semi-Weyl operators, respectively.

Corresponding spectra of an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
are defined as follows:

-the upper semi-Fredholm spectrum:
\sigma_{SF+}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda-T\not\in\Phi_{+}(\mathcal{H})\}
;

-the lower semi-Fredholm spectrum:
\sigma_{SF-}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda-T\not\in\Phi_{-}(\mathcal{H})\}
;

-the essential spectrum:
\sigma_{e}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda-T\not\in\Phi(\mathcal{H})\}
;

-the upper semi-Weyl spectrum:
\sigma_{aw}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda-T\not\in\Phi_{+}^{-}(\mathcal{H})\}
;

-the lower semi-Weyl spectrum:
\sigma_{sw}(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathds{C}:\lambda-T\not\in\Phi_{-}^{+}(\mathcal{H})\}
;

All of these spectra are compact nonempty subsets of the complex plane.

We write
\rho_{SF+}(T),\rho_{SF-}(T),\rho_{e}(T),\rho_{aw}(T),\rho_{sw}(T)
to denote their complements, respectively.

D_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1}),\ D_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2}),...,D_{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})

Lemma 1.1

Let

For

Finally, we list two lemmas that are crucial when it comes to using duality arguments.

Lemma 1.3

For

with closed range the following holds:

(a)

\alpha(T)=\beta(T^{*}),\beta(T)=\alpha(T^{*})

(b)

(c)

Lemma 1.4

Let

\mathcal{R}(T)
is closed if and only if

Formulating E- & T-Model Inflation in Supergravity
C Pallis

Abstract
We present novel realizations of E- and T-model inflation within Supergravity which are largely associated with the existence of a pole of order one and two respectively in the kinetic term of the inflaton superfield. This pole arises due to the selected logarithmic Kahler potentials K K , which parameterize hyperbolic manifolds with scalar curvature related to the coefficient ( − N ) < 0 (-N)<0 of a logarithmic term. The associated superpotential W W exhibits the same R R charge with the inflaton-accompanying superfield and includes all the allowed terms. The role of the inflaton can be played by a gauge singlet or non-singlet superfield. Models with one logarithmic term in K K for the inflaton, require N = 2 N=2 , some tuning – of the order of 10 − 5 10^{-5} – between the terms of W W and predict a tensor-to-scalar ratio r r at the level of 0.001 0.001 . The tuning can be totally eluded for more structured K K ’s, with N N values increasing with r r and spectral index close or even equal to its present central observational value.

Introduction

It is well-known
[

,

,
3

\alpha
-attractors

{\cal L}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(\frac{N_{p}}{2f_{p}^{2}}\dot{}\phi^{2}-V_{\rm I}(\phi)\right)~~\mbox{with}~~f_{p}=1-\phi^{p},~p=1,2~~\mbox{and}~~N_{p}>0.

(1)

Also,

is the determinant of the background
Friedmann-Robertson-Walker metric
g^{\mu\nu}
with signature

(+,-,-,-)
, dot stands for derivation
with respect to

(
w.r.t
) the cosmic time and

\frac{d\widehat{\phi}}{d\phi}=J=\frac{\sqrt{N_{p}}}{f_{p}}~~\Rightarrow~~\phi=\begin{cases}1-e^{-\widehat{\phi}/\sqrt{N_{1}}}&\mbox{for}~~p=1,\\
\tanh{\left(\frac{\widehat{\phi}}{\sqrt{N_{2}}}\right)}&\mbox{for}~~p=2\,,\end{cases}

(2)

we easily infer that

develops a plateau for

, and so it becomes suitable for driving inflation of

E-Model Inflation
(
EMI
)
[
5
,
6
]
(or
\alpha
-Starobinsky model

[
7
]
) and
T-Model Inflation
(

)

[

,
6
]
for

and
2

In this talk, based on Ref.
[

,
3

(

) in the context of

(

\delta

2
Strategy of the SUGRA Embedding

We start our investigation presenting the basic formulation of
scalar theory within SUGRA in Sec.
2.1
and then – in

2.2

2.3

2.1
General Formalism

The part of the SUGRA lagrangian including the (complex) scalar
fields
z^{\alpha}
can be written as

{\cal L}=\sqrt{-\mathfrak{g}}\left(K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}D_{\mu}z^{\alpha}D^{\mu}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}-V_{\rm SUGRA}\right),

(3)

where the kinetic mixing is controlled by the Kähler potential

K
and the relevant metric defined as

K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}={K_{,z^{\alpha}z^{*{\bar{\beta}}}}}>0\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>K^{{\bar{\beta}}{\alpha}}K_{{\alpha}\bar{\gamma}}=\delta^{\bar{\beta}}_{\bar{\gamma}}.

(4a)

Also, the covariant derivatives for the scalar fields
z^{\alpha}
are
given by

D_{\mu}z^{\alpha}=\partial_{\mu}z^{\alpha}+igA^{\rm a}_{\mu}T^{\rm a}_{{\alpha}{\beta}}z^{\beta}

(4b)

with
A^{\rm a}_{\mu}
being the vector gauge fields,
g
the
(unified) gauge coupling constant and
T^{\rm a}
with
{\rm a}=1,...,\mbox{\sf\small dim}G_{\rm GUT}
the generators of a

Grand Unified Theory
(
GUT
) gauge group
G_{\rm GUT}
.
Here and henceforth, the scalar components of the various
superfields are denoted by the same superfield symbol.

The SUGRA scalar potential,
V_{\rm SUGRA}
, is given in terms of

K
, and the superpotential,
W
, by

V_{\rm SUGRA}=V_{\rm F}+V_{\rm D}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>V_{\rm F}=e^{K}\left(K^{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}D_{\alpha}WD^{*}_{\bar{\beta}}W^{*}-3{|W|^{2}}\right)\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>V_{\rm D}=g^{2}\mbox{$\sum_{\rm a}$}{\rm D}_{\rm a}^{2}/2.

(5)

The Kähler covariant derivative and the D-terms read

D_{\alpha}W=W_{,z^{\alpha}}+K_{,z^{\alpha}}W\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>{\rm D}_{\rm a}=z_{\alpha}\left(T_{\rm a}\right)^{\alpha}_{\beta}K^{\beta}\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>K^{{\alpha}}={K_{,z^{\alpha}}}\,.

(6)

Therefore, ETI introduced in Sec.
1
can be supersymmetrized,
if we select conveniently the functions
K
and
W
so that
Eq. (
3
) reproduces Eq. (
1
).

2.2
Inflaton’s Sector

We concentrate on ETI driven by
V_{\rm F}
assuring that
V_{\rm D}=0
during it. This condition may be attained identifying the
inflaton either with (the radial part of) a gauge singlet
superfield
z^{2}:=\Phi
or with the radial part of a conjugate
pair of Higgs superfields,
z^{2}:=\Phi
and
z^{3}:=\bar{\Phi}
.

To achieve a kinetic term in Eq. (
3
) similar to that in
Eq. (
1
) for
p=1
and
2
, we need to establish suitable

K
’s so that (during ETI)

\langle{K}\rangle_{\rm I}=-N\ln f_{p}~~~\mbox{and}~~~\langle{K_{{\alpha}{\bar{\beta}}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=N/f_{p}^{2}

(7)

with
N
related to
N_{p}
. However, from the F-term contribution
to Eq. (
5
), we remark that
K
affects – besides the
kinetic mixing –
V_{\rm SUGRA}
, which, in turn, depends on
W

too. Therefore,
f_{p}
is generically expected to emerge also in
the denominator of
V_{\rm SUGRA}
making difficult the
establishment of an inflationary era. This problem can be
addressed
[
2
,
3
]
either by tuning the terms of
W
so
that the pole is removed from
V_{\rm SUGRA}
thanks to
cancellations or adopting a more structured
K
which yields the
desired kinetic terms in Eq. (
1
) but lets
V_{\rm SUGRA}

immune.

2.3
Stabilizer’s Sector

We reserved
{\alpha}=1
for a gauge singlet superfield,
z^{1}=S
called
stabilizer or goldstino, which assists
[
10
]
us to formulate
ETI of chaotic type in SUGRA. We assume that

and
W
are
equally charged under a global
R
symmetry and so it appears
linearly in
W
multiplying its other terms. Also, it generates
for
\langle{S}\rangle_{\rm I}=0

V_{\rm SUGRA}
which remains alive

V_{\rm I}=\langle{V_{\rm F}}\rangle_{\rm I}=\langle{e^{K}K^{SS^{*}}|W_{,S}|^{2}}\rangle_{\rm I},

(8)

where the symbol “
\langle{Q}\rangle_{\rm I}
” denotes the value of a quantity
Q

during ETI. Such an adjustment assures the boundedness of
V_{\rm I}
,
whereas
S
can be stabilized at
\langle{S}\rangle_{\rm I}=0
, if we select

[
11
]

K_{2}=N_{S}\ln\left(1+|S|^{2}/N_{S}\right)~~\Rightarrow~~\langle{K_{2}^{SS^{*}}}\rangle_{\rm I}=1~~\mbox{with
$0<N_{S}<6$}.

(9)

K_{2}
parameterizes the compact manifold

SU(2)/U(1)
. Note that for
\langle{S}\rangle_{\rm I}=0
,
S
is canonically
normalized and so we do not mention it again henceforth.

A_{BL}
becomes massive absorbing the massless
Goldstone boson associated with
\widehat{\theta}_{-}
.

4
Inflationary Observables

We here constrain the parameters of our EMI and TMI – in
Sec.
4.2
and
4.3
respectively – taking into account a
number of observational and theoretical requirements described in
Sec.
4.1
.

4.1
Constraints

We impose to ETI the following observational and theoretical
requirements:

(a)

The number of e-foldings
N_{\star}
that the
scale
k_{\star}=0.05/{\rm Mpc}
experiences during ETI must be enough
for the resolution of the problems of standard Big Bang, i.e.,

[
13
]

N_{\star}=\int_{\widehat{\phi}_{\rm f}}^{\widehat{\phi}_{*}}d\widehat{\phi}\frac{V_{\rm I}}{V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}}\simeq 61.3+\frac{1-3w_{\rm rh}}{12(1+w_{\rm rh})}\ln\frac{\pi^{2}g_{\rm rh}T_{\rm rh}^{4}}{30V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\rm f})}+\frac{1}{4}\ln{V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\star})^{2}\over g_{\rm rh}^{1/3}V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\rm f})},

(30)

where
\widehat{\phi}_{*}
is the value of
\widehat{\phi}
when
k_{\star}
crosses the
inflationary horizon whereas
\widehat{\phi}_{\rm f}
is the value of
\widehat{\phi}

at the end of ETI, which can be found, in the slow-roll
approximation, from the condition

\mbox{\sf\small max}\{\epsilon(\phi_{\rm f}),|\eta(\phi_{\rm f})|\}=1,\>\>\>~\mbox{where}\>\>\>\epsilon={1\over 2}\left(\frac{V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}}{V_{\rm I}}\right)^{2}\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>\eta=\frac{V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\phi}}}{V_{\rm I}}\,.

(31a)

Also we assume that ETI is followed in turn by an oscillatory
phase with mean equation-of-state parameter
w_{\rm rh}
,
radiation and matter domination. We determine it applying the
formula
[
3
]

w_{\rm rh}=2\frac{\int_{\phi_{\rm mn}}^{\phi_{\rm mx}}d\phi J(1-V_{\rm I}/V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\rm mx}))^{1/2}}{\int_{\phi_{\rm mn}}^{\phi_{\rm mx}}d\phi J(1-V_{\rm I}/V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\rm mx}))^{-1/2}}-1,

(31b)

where
\phi_{\rm mn}=\langle{\phi}\rangle
is the v.e.v of
\phi
after ETI and
\phi_{\rm mx}

is the amplitude of the
\phi
oscillations. Motivated by
implementations
[
14
]
of non-thermal leptogenesis, which may
follow ETI, we set
T_{\rm rh}\simeq 10^{9}~{\mbox{\rm GeV}}
for the reheat
temperature. Finally,
g_{\rm rh}=228.75
is the energy-density
effective number of degrees of freedom include corresponding to
the
Minimal SUSY SM
(
MSSM
) spectrum.

(b)

The amplitude
A_{\rm s}
of the power spectrum
of the curvature perturbations generated by
\phi
at
k_{\star}
has to
be consistent with data
[
13
]
, i.e.,

A_{\rm s}={V_{\rm I}(\widehat{\phi}_{*})^{3}}/{12\,\pi^{2}}{V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{\phi}_{*})^{2}}\simeq 2.105\cdot 10^{-9}\,.

(32)

(c)

The remaining inflationary observables
(
n_{\rm s}
, its running
\alpha_{\rm s}
and
r
) have to be consistent with the
latest
Planck release 4
(
PR4
),
Baryon
Acoustic Oscillations
(
BAO
), CMB-lensing and
BICEP/
Keck
(
BK18
) data
[
16
,
15
]
, i.e.,

\mbox{\sf(i)}\>\>n_{\rm s}=0.965\pm 0.009\>\>\>~\mbox{and}\>\>\>~\mbox{\sf(ii)}\>\>r\leq 0.032,

(33)

at 95
\%

confidence level
(
c.l.
) – pertaining to
the
\Lambda
CDM
+r
framework with
|\alpha_{\rm s}|\ll 0.01
. These
observables are estimated through the relations

\mbox{\sf(i)}\>\>n_{\rm s}=\>1-6\widehat{\epsilon}_{\star}\ +\ 2\widehat{\eta}_{\star},\>\>\>\mbox{\sf(ii)}\>\>\alpha_{\rm s}=\frac{2}{3}\left(4\widehat{\eta}^{2}-(n_{\rm s}-1)^{2}\right)-2\widehat{\xi}_{\star}\>\>\>~\mbox{and}\>\>\>~\mbox{\sf(iii)}\>\>r=16\widehat{\epsilon}_{\star}\,,

(34)

with
\xi={V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}}V_{\rm I,\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\phi}\widehat{\phi}}/V_{\rm I}^{2}}

– the variables with subscript
\star
are evaluated at

\phi=\phi_{\star}
.

(d)

The effective theory describing ETI has to
remain valid up to a
Ultra Violet
(
UV
) cutoff
scale
\Lambda_{\rm UV}\simeq m_{\rm P}
to ensure the stability of our inflationary
solutions, i.e.,

\mbox{\sf(i)}\>\>V_{\rm I}(\phi_{\star})^{1/4}\leq\Lambda_{\rm UV}\>\>\>~\mbox{and}\>\>\>~\mbox{\sf(ii)}\>\>\phi_{\star}\leq\Lambda_{\rm UV}.

(35)

Finally, we quantify the tuning needed for the attaintment of
efficient ETI defining

\Delta_{\rm\star}=1-\phi_{\star}.

(36)

The naturalness of the achievement of ETI increases with
\Delta_{\rm\star}
.

5
Conclusions

We reviewed the implementation of ETI in the context of SUGRA. We
employed as inflaton a gauge singlet or non-singlet superfield for
EMI or TMI respectively. The models are relied on the
superpotentials in Eqs. (
10
) and (
19
) which respect an
R
symmetry
and include, besides inflaton, an inflaton-accompanying field
which facilitates the establishment of ETI. In each model we
singled out three subclasses of models (
\delta
EM
,
EM2
and
EM4
) and
(
\delta
TM
,
TM4
and
TM8
). The models
\delta
EM
and
\delta
TM
are based on the
Kähler potentials in Eq. (
12
a
) and (
21
a
) whereas
(
EM2
,
EM4
) and (
TM4
,
TM8
) in those shown in Eq. (
12
b
) and
(
21
b
). Within
\delta
EM
and
\delta
TM
any
observationally acceptable
n_{\rm s}
is attainable by tuning
\delta_{21}
and

\delta_{42}
respectively to values of the order
10^{-5}
, whereas
r

is kept at the level of
10^{-3}
– see Eqs. (
37
) and (
39
). On
the other hand,
EM2
,
EM4
,
TM4
and
TM8
avoid any tuning, larger

r
’s are achievable as
N
increases beyond
2
, while
n_{\rm s}
lies
close to its central observational value – see Eqs. (
38a
) and (
38b
)
for EMI and Eqs. (
40a
) and (
40b
) for TMI.

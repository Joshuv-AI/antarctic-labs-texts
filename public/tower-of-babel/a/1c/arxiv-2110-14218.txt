Crossing indices, traits and the principle of indistinguishability
Igor Nikonov

Abstract
A (weak chord) index is a function on the crossings of knot diagrams such that: 1) the index of a crossing does not change under Reidemeister moves; 2) crossings which can be paired by a second Reidemeister move have the same index. We show that one can omit the second condition in the case of the universal index. As a consequence, we get the following principle of indistinguishability for classical knots: crossings of the same sign in a classical knot diagram can not be distinguished by any inherent property.



Introduction

The conventional combinatorial approach to knot theory looks at knots as diagrams considered up to special transformations — Reidemeister moves. Many constructions of knot invariants rely on combinatorial elements of diagrams — arcs and crossings. But what if we could distinguish crossings of knot diagrams and divide them into classes and tribes? For example, discern a loop crossing which appears in a first Reidemeister move and a crossing which survived the reduction to the minimal diagram (Fig.
1
).

For virtual knots, this idea was excellently realized by V.O. Manturov who created the parity theory

Despite all achievements of the parity theory in the theory of virtual knots, it has one flow: the parity theory gives nothing to classical knots
[
18
, Corollary 4.3]
. The reason is any parity on a knot is derived from the homology of the space the knot lies in, and the plane where the classical knots live, has trivial homology.

After this failure with parity we can try to weaken the parity axioms and consider another type of labelling of crossings — index. The first appearance of an index can be found in V. Turaev’s work
[
41
]
on strings (flat knots). After the paper of A. Henrich
[
15
]
, the index appeared in works of several authors
[
3
,
20
,
28
]
and others. Z. Cheng
[
6
]
gave an axiomatic description of the index. M. Xu
[
43
]
found the most general formulation of index which he called
weak chord index
.

The index polynomial has become a very efficient tool in the virtual knot theory. May be, it will be useful for study of classical knots. But, alas! There is no nontrivial indices on classical knots
[
40
, Corollary 5]
.

This paper is a sequel of
[
40
]
. Here we make the final step and show that there can not be nontrivial invariant labels (except the sign, surely) on crossings of classical knot diagrams. This fact can be formulated as the following
principle of indistinguishability
:

Crossings of the same sign in a classical knot diagram can not be distinguished by any inherent property.

The mechanism behind the indistinguishability principle can be expressed as the
substitution principle
:

Given two crossing of the same sign in a classical knot diagram, one of them can substitute for the other in the diagram.

The exact formulation of the principles are given in Theorems
4
and
5
.

A demonstration how the substitution principle works is shown in Fig.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

Let

Definition 2

The

Remark 1
.

Knot theories are connected by natural transformations. For example, we have a sequence of projections
\mathfrak{T}_{m,n}(F,B)\to\mathfrak{VT}_{m,n}\to\mathfrak{FT}_{m,n}\to\mathfrak{FrT}_{m,n}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{m,n}(F,B)\to\mathfrak{FT}_{m,n}(F,B)
from (virtual) tangles to flat tangles and then to free tangles. In particular, that means invariants of free tangles can be lifted to invariants of flat tangles, and invariants of flat tangles are lifted to invariants of virtual tangles.

We will use the name

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category. An
index with coefficients in a set

is a map

which assigns some value
\iota(v)\in I
to each crossing
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
in each diagram
D\in\mathfrak{K}
of the diagram category and possesses the following properties:

(I0)

for any elementary morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{K}
and any crossings

one has
\iota(v)=\iota(f_{*}(v))
;

(I2)

\iota(v_{1})=\iota(v_{2})
for any crossings
v_{1},\,v_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
to which a decreasing second Reidemeister move can be applied.

Let
S
be a set with an involution
\ast\colon S\to S
. A
signed index
with coefficients in the set
S
on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
is a map
\sigma\colon\bigsqcup_{D\in\mathfrak{K}}{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to S
which satisfies the property (I0) and the property

(I2+)

for any crossings

to which a decreasing second Reidemeister move can be applied, one has
\sigma(v_{1})=\sigma(v_{2})^{\ast}
.

.

1. For a (signed) index, the property (I0) holds for all morphisms of the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
.

2. An index

, and two signed index values
\sigma_{i}^{l}
and
\sigma_{i}^{r}=(\sigma_{i}^{l})^{\ast}
;

3.

if
\mathcal{K}
is a free tangle then all the loop crossings on the same component have the same index or signed index value.

4.

if one considers a knot theory
\mathfrak{K}
which consists of diagram categories
\mathfrak{K}(T)
of different tangles, the loop indices can be individual for each tangle
T
:
\iota_{i}^{l\pm}(T),\iota_{i}^{r\pm}(T)
.

Some indices may have one loop index value for different tangles. For example, for the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued indices
Ind
and
n
, the loop index values are all equal to zero.

Thus, the first Reidemeister move can be localized and isolated by neglecting the index values
\iota^{l\pm},\iota^{r\pm}
(
\sigma^{l\pm},\sigma^{r\pm}
). We can use this fact and define a tangle invariant (the index polynomial).

2.1
Index polynomial

Definition 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category and
\sigma
be a signed index on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set
S
. Let
L\subset S
be the set of loop index values, i.e.
L=\bigcup_{i}\{\sigma_{i}^{l\pm},\sigma_{i}^{r\pm}\}
if
\mathfrak{K}
is the category of diagrams of a tangle
T=\bigcup_{i}T_{i}
, or
L=\bigcup_{T\in{\mathfrak{K}},\,i}\{\sigma_{i}^{l\pm}(T),\sigma_{i}^{r\pm}(T)\}
if
\mathfrak{K}
is a knot theory. Note that
L^{\ast}=L
.

Consider the abelian group
A_{\sigma}={\mathbb{Z}}[S\setminus L]/\langle 1\cdot x+1\cdot x^{\ast}\mid x\in S\setminus L\rangle
.

Let
T
be a tangle presented by a diagram
D\in\mathfrak{K}
. The
linking invariant
(or
(odd) index polynomial
)
lk_{\sigma}(T)
of the tangle
T
is the element

lk_{\sigma}(T)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)\,\colon\,\sigma(v)\not\in L}1\cdot\sigma(v)\in A_{\sigma}.

(1)

For an index
\iota
on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set
I
we define the
linking invariant

lk_{\iota}(T)
as
lk_{\tilde{\iota}}(T)
if the category
\mathfrak{K}
has the sign function, and as
lk_{\iota}(T)
if
\mathfrak{K}
is a diagram category of flat or free tangles. In the latter case we consider the trivial involution on the set
I
.

Proposition 3
.

The index polynomial
lk_{\sigma}
is an invariant of tangles in the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
, i.e. the value
lk_{\sigma}(T)
does not depend on the choice of a diagram
D
representing the tangle
T
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that for any elementary morphism
f\colon D_{1}\to D_{2}
in
\mathfrak{K}
the values of
lk_{\sigma}
for
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
coincide. When
f
does not change the number of crossing, this follows from the property (I0). If
f
is a second Reidemeister move then the equality follows from the property (I2). We secure the invariance under first Reidemeister moves by excluding the loop index values.
∎

Remark 4
.

1. The group
A_{\sigma}
is a direct sum of the cyclic groups
{\mathbb{Z}}
(one summand for each pair
\{x,x^{\ast}\}
such that
x\in S\setminus L
and
x\neq x^{\ast}
) and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
(one summand for each self-dual element

For an index
\iota

is isomorphic to

in the presence of the sign function on the crossings (one maps
(x,\epsilon)
to

), and

for diagram categories of flat or free tangles.

2. Let
\iota

\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set

\mathfrak{K}
have signs. Using the isomorphism

we can write the formula the odd index polynomial of a tangle

as follows

lk_{\iota}(T)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)\,\colon\,\iota(v)\not\in L}sgn(x)\cdot\iota(v)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[I\setminus L]

(2)

where

is a diagram of the tangle

.

The formula (

Example 3

1. Let

2.2
Universal index

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category. There can be many (signed) indices on
\mathfrak{K}
. We are interested in the (signed) index which carries the maximal information about the crossings.

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category. An index
\iota^{u}
with coefficients in a set
I^{u}
on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
is called the
universal index
on
\mathfrak{K}
if for any index
\iota
on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set
I
there is a unique map
\psi\colon I^{u}\to I
such that
\iota=\psi\circ\iota^{u}
.

A signed index
\sigma^{u}
on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set
S^{u}
with an involution
\ast
is
universal
if for any signed index
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set with involution
S
there is a unique map
\psi\colon S^{u}\to S
such that
\psi(x)^{\ast}=\psi(x^{\ast})
for all
x\in S^{u}
and
\sigma=\psi\circ\sigma^{u}
.

Remark 5
.

The universal index for the diagram categories
\mathfrak{K}(T)
of tangles and flat tangles in a surface
F
was described in
[
40
]
.

Definition 7
.

A (signed) index
\sigma
on a diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set
S
is called
reduced
if
S=im(\sigma)
where

im(\sigma)=\{\sigma(v)\mid v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D),\ D\in\mathfrak{K}\}.

Proposition 4
.

1. The universal (signed) index on a diagram category is reduced.

2. Let
\sigma^{u}
be the universal signed index on a diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
. Then
\bar{\sigma}^{u}
(see Remark
2
) is the universal index on
\mathfrak{K}
.

3. If a diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
has the sign function (i.e. it is a subcategory of
\mathfrak{T}_{m,n}(F,B)
or
\mathfrak{VT}_{m,n}
) and
\iota^{u}
is the universal index on
\mathfrak{K}
then
\tilde{\iota}^{u}
is the universal signed index on
\mathfrak{K}
.

Proof.

1. Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category and
\sigma^{u}
with coefficients in a set
S^{u}
with an involution
\ast
be the universal signed index on
\mathfrak{K}
. Let
S^{\prime}=im(\sigma^{u})\subset S
. Then the co-restriction
\sigma^{\prime}
of the index
\sigma^{u}
to the coefficient set
S^{\prime}
is a signed index on
\mathfrak{K}
. By universality there exists a unique
\ast
-map
\psi\colon S^{u}\to S^{\prime}
such that
\sigma^{\prime}=\psi\circ\sigma^{u}
. Then the composition
\psi^{\prime}
of maps
S^{u}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\rightarrow}}S^{\prime}\hookrightarrow S^{u}
satisfies the condition
\psi^{\prime}\circ\sigma^{u}=\sigma^{u}
. Then by universality, the map
\psi^{\prime}
must coincide with the identity map
id_{S^{u}}
. Thus,
S^{\prime}=S^{u}
.

2. Let
\iota^{u}
with coefficients in a set
I^{u}
be the universal index on
\mathfrak{K}
. By universality of
\iota^{u}
there exists a map
\psi\colon I^{u}\to S^{u}/\ast
such that
\bar{\sigma}^{u}=\psi\circ\iota^{u}
. On the other hand, by universality of
\sigma^{u}
there exists a map
\phi\colon S^{u}\to I^{u}
such that
\phi(x^{\ast})=\phi(x)^{\ast}=\phi(x)
for all
x\in S^{u}
and
\iota^{u}=\phi\circ\sigma^{u}
. The map
\phi
induces a well-defined map
\bar{\phi}\colon S^{u}/\ast\to I^{u}
. Then the maps
\psi
and
\bar{\phi}
define an isomorphism of the indices
\iota^{u}
and
\bar{\sigma}^{u}
.

3. By universality of
\sigma^{u}
there is a map
\psi
from
S^{u}
to
I^{u}\times\{-1,+1\}
. This map is injective. Indeed, if
\psi(x)=\psi(y)
then take crossings
v
and
w
such that
\sigma^{u}(v)=x
and
\sigma^{u}(w)=y
. Since
\iota^{u}(v)=\iota^{u}(w)
, either
\sigma^{u}(v)=\sigma^{u}(w)
or
\sigma^{u}(v)=\sigma^{u}(w)^{\ast}
. In the latter case the crossings
v
and
w
would have different signs. Thus,
\sigma^{u}(v)=\sigma^{u}(w)
. Then
\psi
is a bijection.
∎

3
Traits and indices

The aim of this section is to prove the central result of the paper that one can weaken the definition of the signed universal index by removing the condition (I2+). Let us give the necessary definition.

Definition 8
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category. A
trait with coefficients in a set
\Theta
on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
is a map
\theta\colon\bigsqcup_{D\in\mathfrak{K}}{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to\Theta
which satisfies the property (I0): for any elementary morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{K}
and any crossings
v\in dom(f_{*})
one has
\theta(v)=\theta(f_{*}(v))
.

By definition any index or signed index is a trait.

3.1
Universal trait

By analogy with the (signed) index case, one can define the universal trait.

Definition 9
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category. An trait
\theta^{u}
with coefficients in a set
\Theta^{u}
on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
is called the
universal trait
on
\mathfrak{K}
if for any trait
\theta
on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a set
\Theta
there is a unique map
\psi\colon\Theta^{u}\to\Theta
such that
\theta=\psi\circ\theta^{u}
.

It is clear that the universal signed index is a trait. It appears that some kind of inverse statement is true.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\theta^{u}
be the universal trait with coefficients in a set
\Theta^{u}
on a diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
. Then
\theta^{u}
is the universal signed index on
\mathfrak{K}
(with respect to some involution
\ast
on the set
\Theta^{u}
).

Let us give a simple but implicit and tautological description of the universal trait.

Definition 10
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category. The
based diagram category
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
associated with
\mathfrak{K}
is the category with objects
(D,v)
where
D\in\mathfrak{K}
and
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
and morphisms
f\colon(D,v)\to(D^{\prime},v^{\prime})
such that
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a morphism in
\mathfrak{K}
,
v\in dom(f_{*})
and
v^{\prime}=f_{*}(v)
.

Informally, the associated based category is category of diagrams with a distinguished crossing and morphisms (isotopies, isomorphisms and Reidemeister moves) which don’t erase the distinguished crossing.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category and
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
be the associated based diagram category. Denote the set of objects of the based category by
{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{K})
. These is an equivalence relation on the set
{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{K})
:
(D,v)\sim(D^{\prime},v^{\prime})
iff there is a morphism
f\colon(D,v)\to(D^{\prime},v^{\prime})
. Denote the set of equivalence classes by
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{K})}
. The following proposition is evident.

Proposition 5
.

The map
\theta^{u}
such that
\theta^{u}(v)=[(D,v)]\in\overline{{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{K})}
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
,
D\in\mathfrak{K}
, is the universal trait on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
.

Let us give an analogous interpretation of the universal (signed) index.

3.2
The graph
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category and
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
be the associated based diagram category. The set
{\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{K})
can be identified with the set
\bigsqcup_{D\in\mathfrak{K}}{\mathcal{V}}(D)
of crossing of all the diagrams in
\mathfrak{K}
. Let
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})
be the graph with the set of vertex
V(\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K}))={\mathcal{V}}(\mathfrak{K})
and the set of edges
E(\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K}))
which consists of edges of two types:

•

I0-edge:
vf_{*}(v)
where
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is an elementary morphism in
\mathfrak{K}
and
v
is a crossing in
dom(f_{*})
;

•

I2-edge:
v_{1}v_{2}
where
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are crossings in a diagram
D\in\mathfrak{K}
to which a decreasing second Reidemeister move can be applied.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{0}(\mathfrak{K})
be the subgraph in
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})
obtained by removing all I2-edges from
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})
.

The proposition below follows from the definitions.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category.

1.

The universal index coefficient set
I^{u}
is identified with the set of connected components of the graph
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})
. The universal index
\iota^{u}
maps a crossing to the correspondent connected component.

2.

The universal trait coefficient set
\Theta^{u}
is identified with the set of connected components of the graph
\mathcal{G}_{0}(\mathfrak{K})
. The universal index
\theta^{u}
maps a crossing to the correspondent connected component.

3.

Let
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
and
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
,
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{K}
, be two crossing. Then
\sigma^{u}(v)=\sigma^{u}(v^{\prime})
if and only if
v
and
w
can be connected in
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})
by a path which contains an even number of I2-edges, and
\sigma^{u}(v)^{\ast}=\sigma^{u}(v^{\prime})
if and only if
v
and
w
can be connected by a path which contains an odd number of I2-edges.

Note that two based diagrams
(D,v)
and
(D^{\prime},v^{\prime})
are equivalent in
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
iff the crossings

and

belong to one connected component of the graph
\mathcal{G}_{0}(\mathfrak{K})
.

Thus, if we want to show that the universal trait
\theta^{u}
coincides with the universal signed index
\sigma^{u}
then we have to prove that any path in
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})

\mathcal{G}_{0}(\mathfrak{K})

3.4
Proof of the main theorem

By universality of the trait
\theta^{u}
there is a map
\psi\colon\Theta^{u}\to S^{u}
such that
\sigma^{u}=\psi\circ\theta^{u}
. We will prove that
\psi
is an injection. Since the universal index and trait are reduced, this would imply that
\psi
is a bijection and
\sigma^{u}
and
\theta^{u}
coincide.

Let
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
and
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
,
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{K}
, be two crossing such that
\sigma^{u}(v)=\sigma^{u}(v^{\prime})
. By Proposition
6
that means that there exists a path
\gamma=v_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{l}
in
\mathcal{G}(\mathfrak{K})
such that
v_{1}=v
,
v_{l}=v^{\prime}
and
\gamma
contains even number of I2-edges. Let
D=D_{1},D_{2},\dots,D_{l}=D^{\prime}
be the diagrams the crossings
v_{1},\dots,v_{l}
belong to. We construct a correspondent sequence of wrapped based diagrams
(D_{i},v_{i},n_{i})
using induction.

We start with the diagram
(D,v,0)=(D,v)
. Assume we have defined the wrapped diagram
(D_{i},v_{i},n_{i})
,
1\leq i<l
. If the edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
is an I0-edge then there is an elementary morphism
f\colon D_{i}\to D_{i+1}
such that
f_{*}(v_{i})=v_{i+1}
. Then we take
n_{i+1}=n_{i}
, so there is a morphism
f_{n}\colon(D_{i},v_{i},n_{i})\to(D_{i+1},v_{i+1},n_{i+1})
in the based diagram category
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
.

If the edge
v_{i}v_{i+1}
has type I2 then we set
n_{i+1}=n_{i}\pm 1
and consider the wrapping swap
f_{ws}\colon(D_{i},v_{i},n_{i})\to(D_{i+1},v_{i+1},n_{i+1})
.

Thus, we get a sequence of based diagrams
(D_{i},v_{i},n_{i})
and morphisms between them in the based diagram category
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
. That is all diagrams
(D_{i},v_{i},n_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,l
are equivalent in
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
. The sequence finishes with the diagram
(D_{l},v_{l},n_{l})=(D^{\prime},v^{\prime},n_{l})
. Since only I2-edges change the wrapping number and the number of such edges in
\gamma
is even, the number
n_{l}
is even. By Proposition
7
the diagram
(D^{\prime},v^{\prime},n_{l})
is equivalent to the based diagram
(D^{\prime},v^{\prime},0)=(D^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.

Thus, the based diagrams
(D,v)
and
(D^{\prime},v^{\prime})
are equivalent in
\mathfrak{K}_{b}
, so by Proposition
5
,
\theta^{u}(v)=\theta^{u}(v^{\prime})
. Theorem
1
is proved.

Remark 7
.

Since the universal trait and the universal signed index coincide, there is an involution on the universal trait coefficient set
\Theta^{u}
. In terms of Proposition
5
we can define it as follows: for any

its dual element is the wrapped diagram
[D,v]^{\ast}=[(D,v,1)]
.

Let us formulate several consequences of Theorem

Corollary 1

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category and
\theta

\mathfrak{K}

Remark 8

Not all traits are signed indices because the map

For example, let
\mathfrak{K}=\mathfrak{FK}
be the theory of flat knots,

\theta(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&n(v)\in\{-1,1,2\},\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

The trait
\theta
is neither an index nor a signed index.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a diagram category. Let
D\in\mathfrak{K}
and

4
Indistinguishability principle

Now, let us combine the main theorem with the description of the universal index of tangles in a fixed surface obtained in
[
40
]
.

Let
D=D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{n}
be a tangle diagram in a surface
F
. Let
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
be a crossing of the diagram
D
.

The
component type

\tau(v)
of the crossing

is the pair
(i,j)
where
D_{i}
is the overcrossing component at
v
and
D_{j}
is the undercrossing component at
v
(Fig.

).

Figure 25:
A crossing of type
(i,j)

Let
v
be a self-crossing of a long component
D_{i}
and
s_{i}
be the starting point of
D_{i}
. Define the
order type

o
of the crossing

to be
o(v)=-1
if
v
is an
early undercrossing
, i.e. one encounters first the undercrossing of
v
while moving along
D_{i}
from the point

, and
o(v)=+1
if
v
is an
early overcrossing
, i.e. one encounters the overcrossing of

first.

The following statements were proved in
[
40
]
.

Theorem 3
.

1. There is no nontrivial indices in the theory of classical knots
\mathfrak{CK}
.

2. The order type is the universal index in the theory of long classical knots
\mathfrak{LCK}
.

3. The component type
\tau
is the universal index in the theory of classical links
\mathfrak{CL}_{n}
with
n
(enumerated) components.

Then by Proposition
4
the sign function is the universal signed index on
\mathfrak{CK}
; the pair
(o,sgn)
is the universal signed index (with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2}
) on
\mathfrak{LCK}
, and
(\tau,sgn)
is the universal sign index (with coefficients in
\{1,\dots,n\}^{2}\times\{-1,+1\}
) on
\mathfrak{CL}_{n}
. By Theorem
1
the above mentioned maps are the universal traits in the corresponding diagram categories. We can reformulate this fact in the form of indistinguishability principle.

Theorem 4

(Principle of indistinguishability)
.

1.

(for classical knots) No crossings of the same sign in a classical knot diagram can be distinguished by a trait.

2.

(for long classical knots) No crossings of the same sign and order type in a classical long knot diagram can be distinguished by a trait.

3.

(for classical links) No crossings of the same sign and component type in a classical link diagram can be distinguished by a trait.

Also, we can reformulate the previous theorem as a
substitution principle
. A manifestation of this principle is shown in Fig.
2
.

Theorem 5

(Substitution principle for classical knots)
.

Let
v
and
w
be crossing of the same sign in a classical knot diagram
D
. Then there exist a sequence of Reidemeister moves which transforms
D
to itself and moves the crossing
v
to the crossing
w
.

Analogous statements can be formulated for long classical knots and classical links.

Remark 10
.

Analogues of the principles of indistinguishability and substitution can be formulated for tangles in a fixed surface: if two crossings in a tangle diagram have the same component, order and homotopy types (see
[
40
]
) then one of them can substitute for the other, and the crossings can not be distinguished.

Remark 11
.

There is an analogue of substitution principle for
arcs
of classical knot diagrams. By an arc we mean a part of a knot diagram which ends at crossings and contains no crossings inside.

Theorem 6

(Substitution principle for arcs of classical knot diagram)
.

Let
D
be a classical knot diagram in the sphere
S^{2}
and
a,a^{\prime}
be two arcs of
D
. Then there is a sequence of isotopies and Reidemeiester moves which transforms
D
to itself and transforms the arc
a
to the arc
a^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Choose arbitrary points
A\in a
and
A^{\prime}\in a^{\prime}
. Rotate the diagram
D
on the sphere so that
A
moves to
A^{\prime}
and
a
moves to an arc tangent to
a^{\prime}
. Denote the diagram after the rotation by
D^{\prime}
. Choose the point
A^{\prime}
for the infinity point in
S^{2}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. The long knot diagrams
D\setminus\{A^{\prime}\}
and
D^{\prime}\setminus\{A^{\prime}\}
define one long knot. Thus, there is a sequence of isotopies and Reidemeister moves in
\mathbb{C}
which transforms
D^{\prime}
to
D
. Then the composition of the initial rotation and this sequence is the required chain of moves.
∎

Remark 12
.

The analogous statement for
plane
classical diagrams (diagrams in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
) is wrong. One can construct a winding number-like invariant to distinguish the arcs of plane diagrams.

So we see that the hope to use some inherent information on the crossings for construction of new invariants of classical knot is false. There are several ways to deal with this:

•

Consider
statistical
invariants which treat all crossings equally, like Jones polynomial, Khovanov homology and all the other combinatorial knot invariants.

•

Use not invariant but
equivariant
labels for crossings. We can require that the labels of the crossings remain constant up to some isomorphisms. An example of invariants of this type are parity functors
[
37
]
and (bi)quandles
[
10
]
. The substitution principle for arcs does not prevent the existence of coloring invariants.

•

Use nonreidemeister descriptions of knots. There are different decompositions of knots (into prime knots or JSJ decomposition). May be some of them can be catched with combinatorial elements of knots other that crossings (for example, quadrisecants, petal diagrams etc.)

•

There still remain virtual knots where indices and parities proved their worth.

Example A.4

A.4
Based matrix index

Based matrices were defined by V. Turaev in
[
41
]
. The based matrix of a knot diagram carries information about intersection numbers for halves of the diagram at the crossings. So based matrices can be considered as homological analogues of knot diagrams. Then an analogue of the gluing signed index
\sigma_{gl}
are singed singular based matrices defined in
[
1
]
.

Definition A.2
.

A
signed singular based matrix
is a quadruple
T=(G,s,d,b,\epsilon)
where
G
is a finite set,
s,d\in G
,
s\neq d
,
b\colon G\times G\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is a skew-symmetric map and
\epsilon\in\{-1,+1\}
.

An element
g\in G\setminus\{s\}
is
annihilating
if
b(g,h)=0
for any
h\in G
.

An element
g\in G\setminus\{s\}
is
core
if
b(g,h)=b(g,s)
for any
h\in G
.

Two elements
g_{1},g_{2}\in G\setminus\{s\}
are
complementary
if
b(g_{1},h)+b(g_{2},h)=b(g,s)
for any
h\in G
.

We consider the following
elementary extensions
:

M1

Given a signed singular based matrix
T=(G,s,d,b,\epsilon)
, form the signed singular based matrix
\bar{T}=(\bar{G},s,d,\bar{b},\epsilon)
such that
\bar{G}=G\sqcup\{g\}
,
\bar{b}|_{G\times G}=b
and
\bar{b}(g,h)=0
for all
h\in G
.

M2

Given
T=(G,s,d,b,\epsilon)
, form the signed singular based matrix
\bar{T}=(\bar{G},s,d,\bar{b},\epsilon)
such that
\bar{G}=G\sqcup\{g\}
,
\bar{b}|_{G\times G}=b
and
\bar{b}(g,h)=b(s,h)
for all
h\in G
.

M3

Given
T=(G,s,d,b,\epsilon)
, form the signed singular based matrix
\bar{T}=(\bar{G},s,d,\bar{b},\epsilon)
such that
\bar{G}=G\sqcup\{g_{1},g_{2}\}
,
\bar{b}|_{G\times G}=b
, and
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are complementary in
\bar{T}
, i.e.
\bar{b}(g_{1},h)+\bar{b}(g_{2},h)=b(s,h)
for all
h\in G
.

N

Given
T=(G,s,d,b,\epsilon)
and an element
g\in G\setminus\{s\}
such that
d
and
g
are complementary, form the signed singular based matrix
\bar{T}=(G,s,g,b,-\epsilon)
.

A signed singular based matrix
T
is called
primitive
if the inverse excisions
M1^{-1},M2^{-1},M3^{-1}
can not be applied to
T
.

Two signed singular based matrix
T
and
T^{\prime}
are
homologous
if
T^{\prime}
can be obtained from
T
by a sequence of elementary excisions
M1,M2,M3,N
and the inverse transformations to them.

The model example for a signed singular based matrix is the following. Let
(D,v,\epsilon)
be a signed singular diagram of a flat knot. Then the quintuple
T(D,v,\epsilon)=(G,s,d,b,\epsilon)
where
G=\{s\}\sqcup{\mathcal{V}}(D)
,
d=v
,
b(w,w^{\prime})=[D^{l}_{w}]\cdot[D^{l}_{w^{\prime}}]
,
w,w^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
, is the intersection number of the knot halves, and
b(s,w)=[D]\cdot[D^{l}_{w}]
,
w\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
, is a signed singular base matrix.

With the correspondence
(D,v,\epsilon)\mapsto T(D,v,\epsilon)
, Reidemeister moves on signed singular diagrams turn into moves
M1,M2,M3,N
.

Proposition A.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

1. If two signed singular diagrams
(D,v,\epsilon)
and
(D^{\prime},v^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})
are equivalent then the signed singular based matrices
T(D,v,\epsilon)
and
T(D^{\prime},v^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})
are homologous.

2. For any signed singular based matrix
T
there exist a primitive signed singular based matrix
T_{\bullet}=(G_{\bullet},s,d,b_{\bullet},\epsilon)
which is homologous to
T
. The based matrix
T_{\bullet}
is unique up to isomorphisms, the move
N
, and the replacement of the core element
d
by an annihilating element together with simultaneous change of
\epsilon
by
-\epsilon
(in the case when
d
is a core element).

Thus,
T_{\bullet}(D,v,\epsilon)
is an invariant of signed singular based matrices (up to isomorpisms and the moves described in Proposition
A.1
).

\displaystyle b^{\alpha\beta}(g,h)=\alpha\beta sgn(g)sgn(h)b(g,h)-\frac{1-\alpha sgn(g)}{2}b(g,s)-\frac{1-\beta sgn(h)}{2}b(s,h),

\displaystyle b^{\alpha\beta}(g,s)=\alpha sgn(g)b(g,s),\quad b^{\alpha\beta}(s,h)=\beta sgn(h)b(s,h),\quad b^{\alpha\beta}(s,s)=0,

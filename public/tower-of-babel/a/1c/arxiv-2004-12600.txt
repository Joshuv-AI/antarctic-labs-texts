The May filtration on THH and faithfully flat descent
Liam Keenan

Abstract
In this paper, we prove that both topological Hochschild homology and topological cyclic homology are sheaves for the fpqc topology on connective commutative ring spectra, by exploiting the May filtration on topological Hochschild homology.



1
Introduction

Topological Hochschild homology (
\THH
) and topological cyclic homology (
\TC
), along with their Dennis and cyclotomic trace maps, have proved to be very useful tools for calculating algebraic
K
-theory. Algebraic K-theory has the nice property of being a sheaf for the Nisnevich topology; in practice, this allows one to decompose calculations into more manageable pieces. However, K-theory fails to satisfy the more computationally useful étale descent. Fortunately,
\THH
and
\TC
do not have this deficiency; they have fpqc descent for commutative rings, by a theorem of Bhatt-Morrow-Scholze in
[
BMS19
]
. Additionally, they have étale descent for
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring spectra by a theorem of Clausen-Mathew in
[
CM19
]
. At this stage, it is reasonable to wonder whether it is possible to promote fpqc descent to ring spectra. Our work answers this in the affirmative, provided we work with connective commutative ring spectra, and our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F=\THH
,
\THH(-)^{h\mathbb{T}}
,
\THH(-)^{hC_{p}}
,
\THH(-)_{hC_{p}}
,
\THH(-)^{tC_{p}}
, or
\TC
viewed as functors
\CAlg_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\Sp
. Then
F
satisfies descent for the fpqc topology on
\CAlg_{\geq 0}
.

One would hope that our proof might follow the same strategy as for commutative rings, but working with spectra excludes this possibility. In
[
BMS19
]
,
\THH
is shown to have fpqc descent by a series of reductions. First, the authors reduce the problem to the case of Hochschild homology, by using the Postnikov tower of
\mathbb{S}
, and then reduce to the case of wedge powers of the cotangent complex, via the Hochschild-Konstant-Rosenberg (HKR) filtration on Hochschild homology. Finally, the case of the cotangent complex is handled by a theorem of Bhatt, a proof of which appears as
[
BMS19
, 3.1]
. Since many of the tools used in
[
BMS19
]
are unique to working with (simplicial) commutative rings, different methods became necessary. Our strategy exploits structural properties of the May filtration on
\THH
, as introduced in
[
AKS18
]
. In particular, this filtration is Hausdorff, and its associated graded can be calculated via a canonical equivalence
\gr_{\ast}^{\May}\THH(A)\simeq\THH(H\pi_{\ast}A)
. By proving Theorem
1.1
in the case of generalized Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectra, we bootstrap to the general case using our control of the May filtration.

We now provide a summary of our work. In Section 2, we carefully prove several folklore results on filtered spectra, all of which are undoubtedly known to experts. In particular, we prove that the "evaluate at zero" functor and the associated graded functor
\Fun(\mathbb{Z}^{{op}}_{\geq 0},\Sp)\rightarrow\Sp
are symmetric monoidal for the Day convolution, and that the Whitehead tower functor
\Sp\rightarrow\Fun(\mathbb{Z}^{{op}}_{\geq 0},\Sp)
is also symmetric monoidal. In Section 3, we review some facts about
\check{\text{C}}
ech conerves and prove a special case of our main theorem for generalized Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectra. In Section 4, we present an
\infty
-categorical treatment of the May filtration on
\THH
and use this to construct a similar filtration on the
C_{p}
-homotopy fixed points of
\THH
. Before doing so, we give a brief review of the symmetric monoidal envelope construction which appears in
[
Lur17
, 2.2.4]
, which is crucial to the
\infty
-categorical treatment of the May filtration. In Section 5, we turn to the proof of our main theorem, and use the May filtration in conjunction with our work in Section 3 to deduce the general result.

Throughout this work, we freely use the theory of
\infty
-categories, incarnated via quasicategories, as in
[
Lur09
]
and
[
Lur17
]
. For consistency, wherever possible, we also follow the notation therein. Throughout, we refer to the
\infty
-category of spectra by
\Sp
and use
\otimes
to denote the smash product. Additionally, we will use the notation
\Sp_{\geq 0}
to denote connective spectra, and
\CAlg_{\geq 0}
to denote connective commutative ring spectra. We will fix the following convention that a spectrum
X
is
n
-connective provided that
\pi_{m}X\cong 0
for
m<n
, and a map
f:X\rightarrow Y
of spectra is
n
-connective provided that
\pi_{m}f
is an isomorphism in degrees
m<n
and a surjection when
n=m
. We will make frequent use of the groundbreaking work of Nikolaus-Scholze, providing an
\infty
-categorical treatment of topological Hochschild homology, topological cyclic homology, and cyclotomic spectra,
[
NS18
, III.2.3, II.1.8, II.1.6]
.

2
Filtered spectra

Our main technical tool is the May filtration on
\THH
, as defined in
[
AKS18
]
, which we must import into the
\infty
-categorical context. To do so, we establish several folklore results about filtered (resp.
\mathbb{N}
-filtered) spectra. While the May filtration we consider is indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{op}_{\geq 0}
, it will be technically useful to work with
\mathbb{Z}^{op}
-indexed filtrations as well. Unless otherwise specified,
\mathbb{Z}^{op}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{op}_{\geq 0}
carry the usual ordering, and by abuse, we use the same notation to refer to the nerves of these posets.

Definition 2.1
.

The
\infty
-category of filtered spectra is
\Fil(\Sp)=\Fun(\mathbb{Z}^{op},\Sp)
, and the
\infty
-category of
\mathbb{N}
-filtered spectra is
\tow(\Sp)=\Fun(\mathbb{Z}^{op}_{\geq 0},\Sp)
.

It is readily checked that both of these
\infty
-categories are presentably symmetric monoidal and stable where the symmetric monoidal product in both cases is Day convolution; see
[
Lur09
, 5.5.3.6]
,
[
Lur17
, 1.1.3.1]
, and
[
Gla13
, 2.13]
. Throughout, we will let
\Day
denote the Day convolution product and allow context to dictate exactly which product we mean. The unit object in
\Fil(\Sp)
, denoted by
\mathbbm{1}_{\Fil}
, is the filtered spectrum

\cdots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\cdots

which is
\mathbb{S}
in degrees
n\leq 0
with identity maps in negative degree. The unit object in
\tow(\Sp)
, denoted by
\mathbbm{1}_{\tow}
, is the
\mathbb{N}
-filtered spectrum which is
\mathbb{S}
in degree
0
, and
0
otherwise:

\cdots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\mathbb{S}

Because
\Fil(\Sp)
and
\tow(\Sp)
are symmetric monoidal, we may consider the
\infty
-categories of associative (resp. commutative) algebra objects, which we denote by
\Alg^{\Fil}
(resp.
\CAlg^{\Fil}
) and
\Alg^{\tow}
(resp.
\CAlgTow
). These categories will appear later when we work with the May filtration on
\THH
. The following are the technical claims we seek to prove:

1.

The evaluation functor
\ev_{0}:\tow(\Sp)\rightarrow\Sp
is symmetric monoidal;

2.

the associated graded functor
\gr_{\ast}:\tow(\Sp)\rightarrow\Sp
is colimit-preserving and symmetric monoidal functor; and

3.

the Whitehead tower functor
\Sp\rightarrow\tow(\Sp)
is lax monoidal.

Before commencing, we will record several general results regarding
\Fil(\Sp)
,
\tow(\Sp)
, and their relationship to one another.

2.1
Properties of
\Fil(\Sp)
,
\tow(\Sp)
, and Day convolution

Recall, that for
C
a stable
\infty
-category, an object
X\in C
is said to
generate

C
, provided that
\pi_{0}\Map_{C}(X,Y)\simeq\ast
implies that
Y\simeq 0
. We will say that a collection of objects of
C
,
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
jointly generates

C
, provided that
\pi_{0}\Map(X_{i},Y)\simeq\ast
for all
i\in I
implies that
Y\simeq 0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K
be a simplicial set and let
\ev_{k}:\Fun(K,\Sp)\rightarrow\Sp
denote evaluation at
k\in K
. For all objects
k\in K
, the functors
\ev_{k}
admit left adjoints,
L_{k}:\Sp\rightarrow\Fun(K,\Sp)
given on vertices by
L_{k}X:k^{\prime}\mapsto X^{\otimes\Map_{K}(k,k^{\prime})}
. Additionally, the objects
S(k)=L_{k}\mathbb{S}
have the following properties:

1.

S(k)
is compact for all
k\in K
;

2.

the
S(k)
’s jointly generate
\Fun(K,\Sp)
; and

3.

the collection
\{\Sigma^{n}S(k)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z},k\in K}
generates
\Fun(K,\Sp)
under small colimits.

Proof.

Since
\ev_{k}
preserves small limits and colimits, it admits a left adjoint
L_{k}:\Sp\rightarrow\Fun(K,\Sp)
. The compactness of
S(k)
follows from the fact that
\mathbb{S}
is compact in
\Sp
and
\ev_{k}
preserves colimits. To explicitly identify
L_{k}
, we use the following chain of natural equivalences obtained from the end formula, which appears for example, in
[
GHN17
, 5.2]

\displaystyle\Map_{\Fun(K,\Sp)}(X^{\otimes\Map_{K}(k,-)},E_{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq\underset{i\to j\in\text{Tw}(K)}{\varprojlim}\Map_{\Sp}\big(\underset{\Map_{K}(k,i)}{\varinjlim}X,E_{j}\big)

\displaystyle\simeq\underset{i\to j\in\text{Tw}(K)}{\varprojlim}\ \underset{\Map_{K}(k,i)}{\varprojlim}\Map_{\Sp}(X,E_{j})

\displaystyle\simeq\Map_{\Sp}(X,\underset{i\to j\in\text{Tw}(K)}{\varprojlim}E_{j}^{\Map_{K}(k,i)})

As the exponential object
E_{j}^{\Map_{K}(k,i)}
is equivalent to the mapping spectrum
\underline{\Map}_{\Sp}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Map_{K}(k,i),E_{j})
, an application of the end formula and the spectral co-Yoneda lemma yield:

\displaystyle\Map_{\Sp}(X,\underset{i\to j\in\text{Tw}(K)}{\varprojlim}E_{j}^{\Map_{K}(k,i)})

\displaystyle\simeq\Map_{\Sp}\left(X,\underline{\Map}_{\Fun(K,\Sp)}(\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}\Map_{K}(k,-),E_{\bullet})\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\Map_{\Sp}(X,E_{k}).

This proves that
L_{k}
is given on vertices as claimed.

To prove the final assertions, note that as
\Map_{\Fun(K,\Sp)}(S(k),E_{\bullet})\simeq E_{k}
, the collection
\{S(k)\}_{k\in K}
jointly generates the stable
\infty
-category
\Fun(K,\Sp)
. This allows us to mimic the proof of
[
Lur17
, 1.4.4.2]
to show that the objects
\Sigma^{n}S(k)
generate
\Fun(K,\Sp)
under small colimits. ∎

Example 2.3
.

In the case where
K=\mathbb{Z}^{op}
, the object
S(k)
is the tower with
\mathbb{S}
in degrees
\leq k
and
0
otherwise. The maps
S(k)_{n}\rightarrow S(k)_{n-1}
are the identity if both objects are
\mathbb{S}
. Similarly, if
K=\mathbb{Z}^{op}_{\geq 0}
, the objects
S(k)
are given by
\mathbb{S}
with identity maps in the range
[0,k]
and
0
otherwise.

This next lemma will give us a convenient way to calculate Day convolution products of (
\mathbb{N}
-)filtered spectra.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\oplus:\mathbb{Z}^{op}\times\mathbb{Z}^{op}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{op}
denote the monoidal product and let
A_{k}\subseteq B_{k}=(\mathbb{Z}^{op}\times\mathbb{Z}^{op})\times_{\mathbb{Z}^{op}}\mathbb{Z}^{op}_{/k}
denote the full subcategory of those pairs
(n,m)
such that
k+1\geq n+m\geq k
. Then the inclusion
A_{k}\subseteq B_{k}
is cofinal.

Proof.

This is a straightforward application of the
\infty
-categorical version of Quillen’s Theorem A,
[
Lur09
, 4.1.3.1]
. For
(r,s)\in B_{k}
, where
r+s>k
, the nerve of
A_{k}\times_{B_{k}}(B_{k})_{(r,s)/}
is equivalent to finitely many copies of
\Lambda^{2}_{1}
glued together along their initial and terminal vertices in a zig-zag pattern, hence contractible. If
r+s=k
, then the nerve of
A_{k}\times_{B_{k}}(B_{k})_{(r,s)/}
is contractible. In either case, the nerve is contractible, so we may conclude by Quillen’s Theorem A. ∎

Remark 2.5
.

A virtually identical proof holds for
\mathbb{Z}^{op}_{\geq 0}
as well.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\Fil(\Sp)
denote the full subcategory of those filtered spectra with the property that the maps
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n-1}
are equivalences for
n\leq 0
. Then,
\mathscr{E}
is a symmetric monoidal subcategory of
\Fil(\Sp)
, and the restriction map
\Fil(\Sp)\rightarrow\tow(\Sp)
induced by the inclusion
\mathbb{Z}^{{op}}_{\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{op}
is a symmetric monoidal equivalence
\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\tow(\Sp)
.

Proof.

By
[
Lur09
, 4.3.2.15]
, there is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories
\theta:\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\tow(\Sp)
given by precomposing with the inclusion
\mathbb{Z}^{op}_{\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{op}
. We will prove that
\mathscr{E}
is a symmetric monoidal subcategory of
\Fil(\Sp)
and that
\theta
is symmetric monoidal.

Note that
S(n)
in
\mathscr{E}
restricts to
S(n)
in
\tow(\Sp)
. Since the restriction map
\theta:\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\tow(\Sp)
is an equivalence and compatible with the formation of iterated suspensions, we find that the desuspensions of the objects
S(n)
, where
n\geq 0
, generate
\mathscr{E}
under small colimits. Thus, to show that
\mathscr{E}
is a symmetric monoidal subcategory, we can immediately reduce to showing that
\Sigma^{s}S(n)\Day\Sigma^{t}S(m)\in\mathscr{E}
since
\Day
commutes with colimits separately in each variable. Additionally, since
\Sigma^{r}:\Fil(\Sp)\rightarrow\Fil(\Sp)
is an equivalence for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, the result follows from Lemma
2.6
.

It remains to prove that the canonical map

\theta_{E,F}:\theta(E_{\bullet})\Day\theta(F_{\bullet})\rightarrow\theta(E_{\bullet}\Day F_{\bullet})

is an equivalence for all objects in
\mathscr{E}
. Fix
E_{\bullet}
and let
\mathscr{X}
denote the full stable subcategory of
\mathscr{E}
consisting of those towers
F_{\bullet}
for which
\theta_{E,F}
is an equivalence. Since this category is closed with respect to colimits, we can reduce to checking the claim for objects of the form
S(n)
where
n\geq 0
. However, the natural map

\theta(S(n))\Day\theta(S(m))\rightarrow\theta(S(n)\Day S(m))\simeq\theta(S(n+m))

is an equivalence by Lemma

. This implies that
\theta
is symmetric monoidal and hence
\tow(\Sp)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\subseteq\Fil(\Sp)
is symmetric monoidal.
∎

2.2
The evaluation functor

It will be convenient to follow the notation appearing in
[
Gla13
]
, the relevant parts of which we now recall.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
C^{\otimes}
and
D^{\otimes}
be two symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. Define
\overline{\Fun(C,D)^{\otimes}}
to be the simplicial set over
\Fin_{\ast}
defined by the universal property

\Fun_{\Fin_{\ast}}(K,\overline{\Fun(C,D)^{\otimes}})=\Fun_{\Fin_{\ast}}(C_{k}^{\otimes},D^{\otimes})

where
C_{k}^{\otimes}
is the pullback of
C^{\otimes}\rightarrow\Fin_{\ast}
along the given structure map
k:K\rightarrow\Fin_{\ast}
. This simplicial set is an
\infty

[
Gla13

Observation 2.9
.

If
S\in\Fin_{\ast}
, we let
S^{o}
denote
S\setminus\{\ast\}
. Let
C^{\otimes}
be a symmetric monoidal
\infty

(\Fin_{\ast})^{{act}}_{/S}
denote the full subcategory of
(\Fin_{\ast})_{/S}
spanned by the active morphisms to

. Then for any

there is a canonical product decomposition

(\Fin_{\ast})_{/S}\simeq\left(\prod_{s\in S^{o}}(\Fin_{\ast})^{{act}}_{/\{s\}_{+}}\right)\times\Fin_{\ast}

This implies that for any morphism

in

, there is a canonical decomposition

C_{f}^{\otimes}\simeq\left(\prod_{\Delta^{1},t\in T^{o}}C^{\otimes}_{\mu_{f^{-1}(t)_{+}}}\right)\times C^{\otimes}_{\beta_{f^{-1}(\ast)}}.

For

, the map
\mu_{V}
is given by the active map
V\rightarrow\langle 1\rangle
if
V
is nonempty, and the inclusion
\ast\rightarrow\langle 1\rangle
if
V
is empty. The map
\beta_{V}
denotes the unique map

. One can check that this decomposition of
C_{f}^{\otimes}
is compatible with the decompositions of

and
C^{\otimes}_{T}
.

.

[

, 2.8]

Let

and

be symmetric monoidal
\infty

is the largest simplicial subset of

whose vertices over

which lie in the essential image of the inclusion

and whose edges over

correspond to functors

in the essential image of the inclusion

\prod_{t\in T^{o}}\Fun_{\Delta^{1}}(C^{\otimes}_{\mu_{f^{-1}(t)_{+}}},D^{\otimes}_{\mu_{f^{-1}(t)_{+}}})\times\Fun_{\Delta^{1}}(C^{\otimes}_{\beta_{f^{-1}(\ast)}},D^{\otimes}_{\beta_{f^{-1}(\ast)}})\rightarrow\Fun_{\Delta^{1}}(C^{\otimes}_{f},D^{\otimes}_{f})

By

, this is a symmetric monoidal

also preserves colimits separately, Day convolution preserves colimits separately in each variable
[

, 2.13]
.

.

C^{\otimes},D^{\otimes}

Recall, that for all

Let

. In order to make this construction precise in the
\infty
-categorical language, we mimic the construction of
\THH
in
[

]
. One prerequisite therein, is the symmetric monoidal envelope of

Remark 4.1
.

The cyclic bar construction can be performed for any associative algebra in a presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. Since we are primarily interested in the Whitehead towers of algebra spectra and the Day convolution product, we will not work in such general terms, though many of the results in this section will hold in greater generality.

4.1
The symmetric monoidal envelope

Definition 4.2
.

[
Lur17
, 2.2.4.1]

Let
p:C^{\otimes}\rightarrow\Fin_{\ast}
be a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. The
monoidal envelope
of
C^{\otimes}
, is the fiber product

\Env(C)^{\otimes}=C^{\otimes}\times_{\Fun(\{0\},\Fin_{\ast})}\text{Act}(\Fin_{\ast}),

where
\text{Act}(\Fin_{\ast})\subseteq\Fun(\Delta^{1},\Fin_{\ast})
is the full subcategory spanned by the active morphisms.

By
[
Lur17
, 2.2.4.4]
, evaluation at
\{1\}\subseteq\Delta^{1}
induces a map
q:\Env(C)^{\otimes}\rightarrow\Fin_{\ast}
, exhibiting
\Env(C)^{\otimes}
as a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. If we let
\Env(C)=\Env(C)^{\otimes}_{\langle 1\rangle}
denote the fiber of
q
over
\langle 1\rangle
, by unwinding the definition, we may identify
\Env(C)
with the subcategory
C^{\otimes}_{\Act}\subseteq C^{\otimes}
spanned by objects and active morphisms between them. In other words,
C^{\otimes}_{\Act}
is a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category, and its monoidal product,
\oplus:C^{\otimes}_{\Act}\times C^{\otimes}_{\Act}\rightarrow C^{\otimes}_{\Act}
, may be described as follows: for
X\in C^{\otimes}_{\langle n\rangle}
and
Y\in C^{\otimes}_{\langle m\rangle}
corresponding to sequences
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and
(Y_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
, the product
X\oplus Y
corresponds to the concatenation
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\cup(Y_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in C^{\otimes}_{\langle n+m\rangle}
.

There is a diagonal embedding
\Fin_{\ast}\rightarrow\text{Act}(\Fin_{\ast})
, and the pullback along this embedding induces a lax symmetric monoidal inclusion
i_{C}:C^{\otimes}\subseteq\Env(C)^{\otimes}
,
[
Lur17
, 2.2.4.4, 2.2.4.16]
. The following proposition provides a useful characterization of symmetric monoidal functors out of
\Env(C)^{\otimes}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

[
Lur17
, 2.2.4.9]

Let
C^{\otimes}
and
D^{\otimes}
be symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories. The inclusion
i_{C}:C^{\otimes}\subseteq\Env(C)^{\otimes}
induces an equivalence of
\infty
-categories

\Fun^{\otimes}(\Env(C),D)\simeq\Alg_{C}(D).

Here,
\Fun^{\otimes}(\Env(C),D)
denotes the
\infty
-category of symmetric monoidal functors
\Env(C)^{\otimes}\rightarrow D^{\otimes}
and
\Alg_{C}(D)
is the
\infty
-category of lax symmetric monoidal functors
C^{\otimes}\rightarrow D^{\otimes}
.

Under this equivalence, the identity functor
C\rightarrow C
, which is lax monoidal, corresponds to a symmetric monoidal functor,
\otimes_{C}:\Env(C)=C^{\otimes}_{\Act}\rightarrow C
, given on vertices by
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\mapsto X_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n}
. In order to establish several key properties of the May filtration on
\THH
, we need a lemma on the interaction of
\otimes_{C}
with symmetric monoidal functors. Suppose we are given a symmetric monoidal functor,
F^{\otimes}:C^{\otimes}\rightarrow D^{\otimes}
. Pulling this morphism back along
\text{Act}(\Fin_{\ast})
, produces a functor
\Env(F)^{\otimes}:\Env(C)^{\otimes}\rightarrow\Env(D)^{\otimes}
which is also symmetric monoidal by
[
Lur17
, 2.2.4.15]
.
\Env(F)^{\otimes}
fits into the following diagram,
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Env(C)^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Env(D)^{\otimes}}
\leftarrow
\rightarrow
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\otimes}}
\leftarrow
\rightarrow
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{C}}
\leftarrow
\rightarrow
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{D}}
\leftarrow
\rightarrow
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Env(F)^{\otimes}}

which is commutative in virtue of the fact that
i_{C}
and
i_{D}
are induced by the diagonal.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
F^{\otimes}:C^{\otimes}\rightarrow D^{\otimes}
be a symmetric monoidal functor. There is an equivalence

\otimes_{D}\circ\Env(F)^{\otimes}\simeq F^{\otimes}\circ\otimes_{C}.

Proof.

As the equivalence in Proposition
4.3
is given by precomposing by the lax symmetric monoidal inclusion
i_{C}:C^{\otimes}\subseteq\Env(C)^{\otimes}
, we can check the desired functors are equivalent by showing that

\otimes_{D}\circ\Env(F)^{\otimes}\circ i_{C}\simeq F^{\otimes}\circ\otimes_{C}\circ i_{C}.

This is immediate since
\Env(F)^{\otimes}\circ i_{C}\simeq i_{D}\circ F^{\otimes}
,
\otimes_{C}\circ i_{C}\simeq id
, and
\otimes_{D}\circ i_{D}\simeq id
. ∎

Remark 4.5
.

In the case where

, we will denote

by
\Day
, and in the case where

, we will denote
\otimes_{C}
by

Theorem 5.5
.

The functors
\THH,\THH_{hC_{p}}:\CAlg_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\Sp
are sheaves for the fpqc topology.

Proof.

Since limits of
\Sp
-valued sheaves can be computed in the category of
\Sp
-valued presheaves, the equivalence

\THH\simeq\underset{i\geq 1}{\varprojlim}\left(\THH/\Fil^{\May}_{i}\right),

guarantees
\THH
is an fpqc sheaf. Similarly, since

\THH(-)_{hC_{p}}\simeq\underset{i\geq 1}{\varprojlim}\left(\THH(-)_{hC_{p}}/\Fil^{\May}_{i}(p)\right),

\THH(-)_{hC_{p}}
is an fpqc sheaf as well.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

The functors
\THH(-)^{h\mathbb{T}}
,
\THH(-)^{hC_{p}}
, and
\THH(-)^{tC_{p}}
are fpqc sheaves on
\CAlg_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

The first two functors are fpqc sheaves because homtopy fixed points are calculated via limits. The third is an fpqc sheaf because there is a cofiber sequence

\THH(-)_{hC_{p}}\rightarrow\THH(-)^{hC_{p}}\rightarrow\THH(-)^{tC_{p}}

and
\THH(-)_{hC_{p}}
and
\THH(-)^{hC_{p}}
are fpqc sheaves.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

\THH:\CAlg_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\CycSp
is a fpqc sheaf.

Proof.

Because
\CycSp
is defined as as lax equalizer, which is a strict pullback of
\infty
-categories, it suffices to check that the functors obtained from
\THH
by post-composing with the projections are all fpqc sheaves. Indeed, we obtain the following three functors:

1.

\THH:\CAlg_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\Sp^{B\mathbb{T}}
;

2.

\prod_{p}(\varphi_{p}(-)):\CAlg_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\prod_{p}\Fun(\Delta^{1},\Sp^{B\mathbb{T}})
; and by identifying composites,

3.

\left(\prod_{p}\THH(-),\prod_{p}\THH(-)^{tC_{p}}\right):\CAlg_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\prod_{p}\Sp^{B\mathbb{T}}\times\prod_{p}\Sp^{B\mathbb{T}}

\THH:\CAlg_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\Sp
is and limits in
\Sp^{B\mathbb{T}}

Corollary 5.9

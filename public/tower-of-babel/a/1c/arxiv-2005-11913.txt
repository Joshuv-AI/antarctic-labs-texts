Optimal colored Tverberg theorems for prime powers
Duško Jojić, Gaiane Panina, Rade T. Živaljević

Abstract
The colored Tverberg theorem of Blagojević, Matschke, and Ziegler (Theorem 1.2 ) provides optimal bounds for the colored Tverberg problem, under the condition that the number of intersecting rainbow simplices r = p r=p is a prime number. Our Theorem 1.3 extends this result to an optimal colored Tverberg theorem for multisets of colored points, which is valid for each prime power r = p k r=p^{k} , and includes Theorem 1.2 as a special case for k = 1 k=1 . One of the principal new ideas is to replace the ambient simplex Δ N \Delta^{N} , used in the original Tverberg theorem, by an “abridged simplex” of smaller dimension, and to compensate for this reduction by allowing vertices to repeatedly appear a controlled number of times in different rainbow simplices. Configuration spaces, used in the proof, are combinatorial pseudomanifolds which can be represented as multiple chessboard complexes. Our main topological tool is the Eilenberg-Krasnoselskii theory of degrees of equivariant maps for non-free actions. A quite different generalization arises if we consider colored classes that are (approximately) two times smaller than in the classical colored Tverberg theorem. Theorem 1.6 , which unifies and extends some earlier results of this type, is based on the constraint method and uses the high connectivity of the configuration space.

Chessboard complexes and equivariant maps

The central role of
chessboard complexes
, as proper configuration spaces for
colored Tverberg problem and its relatives
, was recognized in
[
ŽV92
]
almost thirty years ago. To the present day these complexes remain, together with their generalizations (the multiple chessboard complexes) in the focus of research in this area of geometric combinatorics.

Recall that the (standard) chessboard complex
\Delta_{p,q}
is the complex of
all non-attacking placements of rooks in a
(p\times q)
-chessboard (a placement is non-attacking if it is
not allowed to have more than one rook in the same row or in the
same column). More generally, the multiple chessboard complex
\Delta_{p,q}^{{\mathbbm{A}},{\mathbbm{B}}}
(see Section
3
), where
\mathbbm{A}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
and
\mathbbm{B}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, arises if we allow more than one rook in each row (each column), where their precise number is determined by vectors
\mathbbm{A}
and
\mathbbm{B}
.

Following
[
Ž17
, Section 21.4]
, variants of the colored Tverberg problem are classified as
type A, type B, or type C

depending on whether
m=d,m<d
or
m>d
(resp.), where
m+1
is the number
of colors and
d
is the dimension of the ambient space.

Central results in this area are the
topological type C colored Tverberg theorem
(Theorem 2.2 in
[
BMZ
]
, reproduced here as Theorem
1.2
) and the
topological type B colored Tverberg theorem

[
ŽV92
,
VŽ94
]
. Both of these results are obtained by applications of the
Configuration Space/Test Map scheme
involving chessboard complexes (see
[
Ž17
]
).

The associated
test maps
are respectively (
2.1
)
(in the type C case) and (
2.2
) (for the type B result),

f:(\Delta_{r-1,r})^{\ast(d+1)}\ast[r]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{Z}/r}}{{\longrightarrow}}W_{r}^{\oplus(d+1)}

(2.1)

f:(\Delta_{2r-1,r})^{\ast(k+1)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{Z}/r}}{{\longrightarrow}}W_{r}^{\oplus d}

(2.2)

where
W_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
is the standard
(r-1)
-dimensional representation of the group
S_{r}
.

Both theorems are consequences of the corresponding
Borsuk-Ulam-type statements claiming that in either case the

S_{r}
-equivariant map
f
must have a zero, where in (
2.1
)
r
is a prime number, and in (
2.2
))

is a power of a prime.

The following theorem extends (
2.1
) and serves as a basis for a new type C topological Tverberg theorem, which extends (in a natural way) the result of Blagojević, Matschke and Ziegler to the prime power case.

Let

Chessboard pseudomanifolds

Following
[

The multiple chessboard complex

Hierarchy of pseudomanifolds
\Delta_{m,n}^{\mathbbm{1};\mathbb{L}}

From here on we tacitly assume that the chessboard complex
\Delta_{m,n}^{\mathbbm{1};\mathbb{L}}
satisfies the condition

\mathbb{L}=\mathbbm{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}

\Delta_{m,n}^{\mathbbm{1};\mathbb{L}}=\Delta_{n-1,n}

\Delta_{m,n}^{\mathbbm{1};\mathbb{L}}\cong\partial\Delta_{[n]}

form a poset category where the complexes

Assuming

The special cases

6.1
Unavoidable complexes

A multiset with vertices in
V
is a pair
\mathbbm{V}=(V,m)
where
m:V\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a function assigning non-negative multiplicities to elements of
V
. If
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}
and
m(v_{i})=m_{i}
we usually use the notation
\mathbbm{V}=\{v_{1}^{m_{1}},\dots,v_{s}^{m_{s}}\}
. We have introduced
\mathbbm{L}
(in Theorem
1.3
) as the vector
(1,p,\dots,p^{k-1})^{\times(p-1)}\in\mathbb{N}^{k(p-1)}
. With a mild abuse of language we use the same notation for the corresponding multiset. More precisely a multiset is of the type
\mathbbm{L}
if
(1,p,\dots,p^{k-1})^{\times(p-1)}
is the associated vector of multiplicities.

Definition 6.1
.

(Unavoidable complexes)

Let
\mathbbm{V}=(V,m)
be a multiset and
r
a positive integer. A simplicial complex
K\subseteq 2^{V}
is
(r,\mathbbm{V})
-unavoidable if for each
\mathbbm{V}
-proper collection
\{\Delta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
of (not necessarily distinct) subsets of
V
, at least one of the subsets
\Delta_{i}
is in
K
. By definition a collection
\{\Delta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
is
\mathbbm{V}
-proper if for each
v\in V
the cardinality of the set
\{i\mid v\in\Delta_{i}\}
is at most
m(v)
.

Definition 6.2
.

(Tverberg complexes)

Let
\mathbbm{V}=(V,m)
be a multiset. Assume that
r
and
d
are positive integers. A simplicial complex
K\subseteq 2^{V}
is a Tverberg complex of the type
(r,d,\mathbbm{V})
if for each continuous map
f:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

there exists a
\mathbbm{V}
-proper collection
\{\Delta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
of simplices in
K
such that

f(\Delta_{1})\cap\dots\cap f(\Delta_{r})\neq\emptyset\,.

(6.1)

Example 6.3
.

In the notation of Theorem
1.3

K_{d}:=C_{0}\ast C_{1}\ast\dots\ast C_{d+1}\cong[k(p-1)]^{\ast(d+1)}\ast[1]

(r,d,\mathbbm{V})
where

is a prime power and
\mathbbm{V}
is a disjoint union of
d+1

\mathbbm{L}
and a singleton
[1]
.

Unavoidable complexes, originally introduced in
[

as “Tverberg unavoidable subcomplexes”, play the fundamental role in the “constraint method”
[
BFZ1
, Sections 3 and 4]
.
Here we show how the constraint method can be extended to the case of multisets.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\mathbbm{V}=(V,m)
be a multiset with vertices in
V
. Assume

and

are positive integers. Let

-unavoidable complex. Then

Proof:
We are supposed to prove, following Definition

\mathbbm{V}
-proper collection
\{\Delta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}

The first step is to include

into a commutative diagram (

\begin{CD}K@>{F}>{}>\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\\
@A{e}A{}A@A{i}A{}A\\

Let
\bar{f}
be an extension (

) of
the map

to

By assumption

so there exists a
\mathbbm{V}
-proper family

By assumption the complex

7
Comparison principle for equivariant maps

The following theorem is proved in
[
KB
]
(Theorem 2.1 in Section 2). Note that the condition that the
H_{i}
-fixed point sets
S^{H_{i}}
are
locally
k
-connected
for
k\leq{\rm dim}(M^{H_{i}})-1
is automatically satisfied if
S
is a representation sphere. So in this case it is sufficient to show that the sphere
S^{H_{i}}
is (globally)
({\rm dim}(M^{H_{i}})-1)
-connected which is equivalent to the condition

{\rm dim}(M^{H_{i}})\leq{\rm dim}(S^{H_{i}})\,(i=1,\dots,m)\,.

(7.1)

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a compact topological manifold
M=M^{n}
and on a sphere
S\cong S^{n}
of the same dimension.
Let
N\subset M
be a closed invariant subset and let
(H_{1}),(H_{2}),\dots,(H_{m})
be the orbit types in
M\setminus N
. Assume that the set
S^{H_{i}}
is both globally and locally
k
-connected for all
k=0,1,\dots,{\rm dim}(M^{H_{i}})-1
, where
i=1,\dots,m
.
Then for every pair of
G
-equivariant maps
\Phi,\Psi:M\longrightarrow S
, which are equivariantly homotopic on
N
, there is the following relation

{\rm deg}(\Psi)\equiv{\rm deg}(\Phi)\quad({\rm mod}\,GCD\{|G/H_{1}|,\dots,|G/H_{k}|\})\,.

(7.2)

The proof of the following extension of Theorem
7.1
to manifolds with singularities doesn’t require new ideas. By a
singular topological manifold
we mean a topological manifold with a codimension
2
singular set. In particular Theorem
7.2
applies to pseudomanifolds
\Delta_{m,n}^{\mathbbm{1};\mathbb{L}}
, introduced in Section
3
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting on a compact “singular topological manifold”
M=M^{n}
and on a sphere
S\cong S^{n}
of the same dimension.
Let
N\subset M
be a closed invariant subset and let
(H_{1}),(H_{2}),\dots,(H_{m})
be the orbit types in
M\setminus N
. Assume that the set
S^{H_{i}}
is both globally and locally
k
-connected for all
k=0,1,\dots,{\rm dim}(M^{H_{i}})-1
, where
i=1,\dots,m
.
Then for every pair of
G
-equivariant maps
\Phi,\Psi:M\longrightarrow S
, which are equivariantly homotopic on
N
, there is the following relation

{\rm deg}(\Psi)\equiv{\rm deg}(\Phi)\quad({\rm mod}\,GCD\{|G/H_{1}|,\dots,|G/H_{k}|\})\,.

(7.3)

Proof:
Following into footsteps of the proof of Theorem
7.1
(see
[
KB
, Theorem 2.1]
) we define a
G
-equivariant map

f_{0}:(M\times\{0,1\})\cup(N\times[0,1])\longrightarrow B\setminus\{O\}

(7.4)

where
B={\rm Cone}(S)
is a cone over the sphere
S
(with the apex
O
),
\Psi
and
\Phi
are restrictions of
f_{0}
on
M\times\{0\}
(respectively
M\times\{1\}
) and the restriction of
f_{0}
on
N\times[0,1]
is a homotopy between
\Psi|_{N}
and
\Phi|_{N}
.

If
f:M\times[0,1]\rightarrow B
is a
G
-equivariant extension of
f_{0}
then (
[
KB
, Lemma 2.1]
)
{\rm deg}(f)=\pm({\rm deg}(\Psi)-{\rm deg}(\Phi))

and the relation (
7.3
) will follow if

{\rm deg}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\cdot|G/H_{i}|

(7.5)

for some integers
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The proof of the following lemma (
[
KB
, Lemma 2.2]
) is quite general, in particular it holds for “singular topological manifolds”.

Lemma 7.3
.

There exists a
G
-equivariant extension
f:M\times[0,1]\rightarrow B
of the map
f_{0}
satisfying the following conditions:

(
\alpha
)

K=f^{-1}(O)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}T_{j}
where
T_{u}\cap T_{v}=\emptyset
for
u\neq v
;

(
\beta
)

T_{j}=G(K_{j})
for a compact set

;

(
\gamma
)

K_{j}=H_{j}(K_{j})
is
H_{j}
-invariant;

(
\delta

g(K_{j})\cap h(K_{j})=\emptyset
if
gh^{-1}\notin H_{j}\,(j=1,\dots,m)
.

The proof of Theorem
7.2
is completed as in
[

, Section 2.1.3]
by observing that “singular topological manifolds” also have absolute and relative fundamental classes.

More explicitly, if
F_{j}
is the restriction

By the same argument as in

Proof of Theorem

The following theorem has already been formulated in
[
JPVZ
]
, in a less general form. Note that even if
r
is a prime, it does not immediately follow from Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
r=p^{\alpha}
be a prime power,
d\geq 1
, and
N:=(r-1)(d+1)
. Let
\Delta^{N}
be an
N
-dimensional simplex with a partition (coloring) of its vertex set into
t
color classes,

V=[N+1]=C_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup C_{t}\,,

where
|C_{i}|\leq q=\frac{r+1}{2}
for all

. Then for every continuous map
f:\Delta^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there are
r
disjoint rainbow simplices
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r}
of
\Delta^{N}
satisfying

f(\Delta_{1})\cap\dots\cap f(\Delta_{r})\neq\emptyset\,.

Proof:
If
m_{i}:=|C_{i}|
then (by assumption)
\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}=N+1
.
The configuration space of
r
-tuples of rainbow simplices is the join
\mathcal{C}=\Delta_{r,m_{1}}\ast...\ast\Delta_{r,m_{t}}
. It can be visualized as the chessboard complex associated with
t
“small” chessboards of height
r
positioned side by side. The condition is that in each of these small chessboard the rooks placement is non-taking.

By assumption
r\geq 2m_{i}-1
so by
[
ŽV92
, Proposition 1]

\Delta_{r,m_{i}}
is
m_{i}-2
-connected. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{t}(m_{i}-2)+2(t-1)=(r-1)(d+1)-1
, it follows that
\mathcal{C}
is
(N-1)
-connected.

The reduction based on the standard
configuration space/test map scheme

[
M03
,
Ž17
]
shows that if
f
violates the statement of the theorem, then there exists a
(\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\alpha}
-equivariant map

\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow S^{(d+1)(r-1)-1}\,.

This contradicts Volovikov’s theorem
[
V96
]
. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
. As in the proof of Theorem
8.1
let
\mathcal{C}=\Delta_{r,m_{1}}\ast...\ast\Delta_{r,m_{t}}
be the configuration space of all
r
-tuples
(\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{r})
of (pairwise vertex disjoint) rainbow simplices, where
m_{i}:=|C_{i}|
for each

.

As before
\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}=N+1
and the only difference with the setting of Theorem
8.1
is that the dimension of the simplex
\Delta^{N}
is now
N=(r-1)(d+2)
(rather than
N=(r-1)(d+1)
).

Let
\Delta_{N}^{(\nu)}=(\Delta^{N})^{(\nu)}
be the
\nu
-dimensional skeleton of the simplex
\Delta^{N}
and let

\Sigma=SymmDelJoin(\Delta_{N}^{(k)},...,\Delta_{N}^{(k)},\Delta_{N}^{(k-1)},...,\Delta_{N}^{(k-1)})

be the

[
JVZ-2
]
(Theorem 3.3) as the configuration space of all (pairwise vertex disjoint)

-tuples satisfying the conditions (2) and (3) in Theorem

.

Our goal is to show that for each continuous map
f:\Delta^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists an
r
-tuple
\Delta\in\mathcal{C}\cap\Sigma
such that
f(\Delta_{1})\cap\dots\cap f(\Delta_{r})\neq\emptyset

Suppose (for contradiction) that

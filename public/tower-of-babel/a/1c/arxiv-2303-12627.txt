Locality Theorems in Semiring Semantics
Clotilde Bizière, Erich Grädel, Matthias Naaf

Abstract
Semiring semantics of first-order logic generalises classical Boolean semantics by permitting truth values from a commutative semiring, which can model information such as costs or access restrictions. This raises the question to what extent classical model-theoretic properties still apply, and how this depends on the algebraic properties of the semiring. In this paper, we study this question for the classical locality theorems due to Hanf and Gaifman. We prove that Hanf’s locality theorem generalises to all semirings with idempotent operations, but fails for many non-idempotent semirings. We then consider Gaifman normal forms and show that for formulae with free variables, Gaifman’s theorem does not generalise beyond the Boolean semiring. Also for sentences, it fails in the natural semiring and the tropical semiring. Our main result, however, is a constructive proof of the existence of Gaifman normal forms for min-max and lattice semirings. The proof implies a stronger version of Gaifman’s classical theorem in Boolean semantics: every sentence has a Gaifman normal form which does not add negations.

Introduction

Originally motivated by
provenance analysis in databases
(see e.g.
[
17
,
11
]
for surveys),
semiring semantics is based on the idea to evaluate logical statements not just by
true

or
false
, but by values in some commutative semiring
(K,+,\cdot,0,1)
.
In this context, the standard semantics appears as the special case
when the Boolean semiring
\mathbb{B}=(\{\bot,\top\},\lor,\land,\bot,\top)
is used.
Valuations in other semirings provide additional information, beyond truth
or falsity: the tropical semiring
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\infty},\min,+,\infty,0)

is used for
cost analysis
, the natural semiring
{\mathbb{N}}=({\mathbb{N}},+,\cdot,0,1)
for counting
evaluation strategies and proofs,
and the Viterbi-semiring
\mathbb{V}=([0,1]_{\mathbb{R}},\max,\cdot,0,1)
models
confidence scores
.
Finite or infinite min-max semirings
(K,\max,\min,a,b)

can model, for instance, different
access levels

to atomic data (see e.g.
[
9
]
); valuations of a first-order sentence
\psi
in such
security semirings
determine the
required clearance level that is necessary to access enough information
to determine the truth of
\psi
.
Further, semirings of polynomials or formal power series permit us to
track
which
atomic facts are used (and how often) to establish the truth of a sentence in a given structure, and
this has applications for database repairs
[
25
]

and also for the strategy analysis of games
[
16
,
13
]
.
Semiring semantics replaces structures
by
K
-interpretations
, which are functions
\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\rightarrow K
, mapping fully instantiated

\tau
-literals
\varphi(\overline{a})
over a universe
A
to values in a commutative semiring
K
.
The value
0\in K
is interpreted as
false
, while all other values in
K
are viewed as
nuances of
true
or, perhaps more accurately, as
true, with some additional information
.
In provenance analysis, this is sometimes referred to as
annotated facts
.
The value
1\in K
is used to represent
untracked information

and is used in particular to evaluate true equalities and inequalities.

The development of semiring semantics raises the question to what extent classical techniques and results of logic extend to semiring semantics,
and how this depends on the algebraic properties of the underlying semirings.
Previous investigations in this direction have studied, for instance, the relationship between elementary equivalence and isomorphism
for finite semiring interpretations and their definability up to isomorphism
[
14
]
,
Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé games
[
6
]
, and 0-1 laws
[
12
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to study
locality
in semiring semantics.
Locality is a fundamental property of first-order logic in classical semantics
and an important limitation of its expressive power. It
means that the truth of a first-order formula
\psi(\overline{x})
in a given structure only depends on a
neighbourhood of bounded radius around
\overline{x}
, and on the existence of a bounded number of local substructures.
Consequently, first-order logic cannot express global properties such as connectivity or acyclicity of graphs.
On graphs there are natural and canonical notions of the distance between two points and of a neighbourhood of a given radius around a point.
To define these notions for an arbitrary relational structure
{\mathfrak{A}}
one associates with it its
Gaifman graph

G({\mathfrak{A}})=(A,E)

where two points
a\neq b
are adjacent if, and only if, they
coexist in some atomic fact. There exist several notions of locality; the most common ones are

Hanf locality
and
Gaifman locality
, and the fundamental locality theorems for first-order logic are

Hanf’s locality theorem
and
Gaifman’s normal form theorem
.
In a nutshell, Hanf’s theorem gives a criterion for the
m
-equivalence
(i.e. indistinguishability by sentences of quantifier rank up to
m
) of two structures
based on the number of local substructures of any given isomorphism type, while
Gaifman’s theorem
states that every first-order formula is equivalent to a Boolean combination of local formulae and basic local sentences,
which has many model-theoretic and algorithmic consequences.
We shall present precise statements of these results in
\cref
secHanf and
\cref
secGaifmanDefinitions.

Locality thus provides powerful techniques,
also for logics that go beyond first-order logic by counting properties, generalised quantifiers, or aggregate functions,
[
1
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. It has
applications in different areas including low-complexity model-checking algorithms
[
18
,
19
]
,
approximation schemes for logically defined optimisation problems
[
7
]
,
automata theory
[
24
]
,
computational issues on database transactions
[
2
]
, and most recently also in learning theory,
for the efficient learning of logical concepts

[
3
,
5
,
4
]
.
This motivates the question, whether locality is also applicable in semiring semantics.
The relevant semiring interpretations in this context are

model-defining
, which means that for any pair of complementary literals
R\overline{a},\neg R\overline{a}

precisely one of the values
\pi(R\overline{a})
,
\pi(\neg R\overline{a})
is 0,
and
track only positive information
which means that

\pi(\neg R\overline{a})
can only take the values 0 or 1.
Model defining interpretations
\pi
define a unique structure
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi}
and we thus obtain
a well-defined Gaifman graph
G(\pi)\coloneqq G({\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi})
, with the associated notions
of distance and neighbourhoods. The assumption that only positive information is tracked
is necessary to get meaningful locality properties (see
\cref
sec:semiringsemantics).

We clearly cannot generalise all known locality properties of
first-order logic to semiring semantics in arbitrary commutative semirings. On semirings whose operations
are not idempotent, we cannot expect a Gaifman normal form, since for computing the value of a quantified statement,
we have to add or multiply values of subformulae for

all
elements of the structure, which gives an inherent source of non-locality. As a consequence, some of the
locality results that we prove hold only under certain algebraic assumptions on the semiring, and further there
turns out to be a difference of the locality properties of sentences and those of formulae with free variables.
We shall establish the following results.

{bracketenumerate}

First-order formulae are Hanf-local for all semirings.

Hanf’s locality theorem generalises to all fully idempotent semirings (in which both addition and multiplication are idempotent).

For formulae with free variables, Gaifman’s normal form theorem does not generalise beyond the Boolean semiring.

For sentences, Gaifman’s normal form theorem also fails in certain important semirings such as the natural semiring and the tropical semiring.

Over min-max semirings (and even lattice semirings), every first-order sentence has a Gaifman normal form.

In classical Boolean semantics, every sentence has a Gaifman normal form which does not introduce new negations.

The results
(1)
,
(2)
on Hanf locality (
\cref
secHanf) are proved by adaptations of the arguments for the Boolean case.
The results
(3)
and
(4)
are established in
\cref
secCounterexamples via specific examples of formulae that defeat locality, using simple algebraic arguments.
The most ambitious result and the core of our paper is
(5)
, a version of Gaifman’s theorem for min-max semirings (
\cref
secGaifmanProof), which we later generalise to lattice semirings (
\cref
secStrengthening).
It requires a careful choice of the
right syntactical definitions for local sentences and, since the classical proofs in
[
10
,
8
]
do not seem to generalise to semiring semantics, a new approach for the proof, based on quantifier elimination.
This new approach also leads to a stronger version of Gaifman’s theorem in Boolean semantics
(6)
, which might be of independent interest.

2
Semiring Semantics

This section gives a brief overview on semiring semantics of first-order logic (see
[
15
]
for more details) and the relevant algebraic properties of semirings.
We further define generalisations of the classical notions of equivalence, isomorphism and distance (via the Gaifman graph).

A commutative
1
1

1

In the following,
semiring
always refers to a commutative semiring.

semiring is an algebraic structure
(K,+,\cdot,0,1)
with
0\neq 1
, such that
(K,+,0)

and
(K,\cdot,1)
are commutative monoids,
\cdot

distributes over
+
, and
0\cdot a=a\cdot 0=0
. We focus on semirings that are
naturally ordered
,
in the sense that
a\leq b:\Leftrightarrow\exists c(a+c=b)
is a partial order.
For the study of locality properties, an important subclass are the
fully idempotent
semirings, in which both operations are idempotent (i.e.,
a+a=a
and
a\cdot a=a
).
Among these, we consider in particular all
min-max
semirings
(K,\max,\min,0,1)
induced by a total order
(K,\leq)
with minimal element
0
and maximal element
1
,
and the more general
lattice
semirings
(K,\sqcup,\sqcap,0,1)
induced by a bounded distributive lattice
(K,\leq)
.

For a finite relational vocabulary
\tau
and a finite universe
A
, we write
\Lit_{A}(\tau)
for the set of

instantiated

\tau
-literals
R\overline{a}
and
\neg R\overline{a}
with
\overline{a}\in A^{\arity(R)}
.
Given a commutative semiring
K
, a
K
-interpretation
(of vocabulary
\tau
and universe
A
)
is a function
\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\to K
.
It is
model-defining
if for any pair of complementary literals
L
,
\neg L

precisely one of the values
\pi(L)
,
\pi(\neg L)
is
0
.
In this case,
\pi
induces a unique (Boolean)
\tau
-structure
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi}
with universe
A
such that,
for every literal
L\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
, we have that
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi}\models L
if, and only if,

\pi(L)\neq 0
.

A
K
-interpretation
\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\to K
extends in a straightforward way
to a valuation
\pi[\![\varphi(\overline{a})]\!]

of any instantiation of a formula
\varphi(\overline{x})\in{\rm FO}(\tau)
,
assumed to be written in negation normal form,
by a tuple
\overline{a}\subseteq A
.
The semiring semantics
\pi[\![\varphi(\overline{a})]\!]
is defined
by induction. We first extend
\pi
by mapping equalities and inequalities to their truth values, by
setting
\pi[\![a=a]\!]\coloneqq 1
and
\pi[\![a=b]\!]\coloneqq 0
for
a\neq b
(and analogously for
inequalities).
Further, disjunctions and existential quantifiers are interpreted as sums, and conjunctions and universal quantifiers as products:

\displaystyle\pi[\![\psi(\overline{a})\lor\vartheta(\overline{a})]\!]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\pi[\![\psi(\overline{a})]\!]+\pi[\![\vartheta(\overline{a})]\!]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\pi[\![\psi(\overline{a})\land\vartheta(\overline{a})]\!]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\pi[\![\psi(\overline{a})]\!]\cdot\pi[\![\vartheta(\overline{a})]\!]

\displaystyle\pi[\![\exists x\,\vartheta(\overline{a},x)]\!]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{a\in A}\pi[\![\vartheta(\overline{a},a)]\!]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\pi[\![\forall x\,\vartheta(\overline{a},x)]\!]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{a\in A}\pi[\![\vartheta(\overline{a},a)]\!].

Since negation does not correspond to a semiring operation, we insist on writing all formulae in negation normal form.
This is a standard approach in semiring semantics (cf.
[
15
]
).

Equivalence of formulae now takes into account the semiring values and is thus more fine-grained than Boolean equivalence.
We often consider equivalence transformations that hold for an entire class of semirings, such as all min-max semirings.

Definition 1

(
\equiv_{K}
)
.

Two formulae
\psi(\overline{x})
,
\varphi(\overline{x})
are

K
-equivalent (denoted
\psi\equiv_{K}\varphi
) if
\pi[\![\psi(\overline{a})]\!]=\pi[\![\varphi(\overline{a})]\!]
for every model-defining

K
-interpretation
\pi
(over finite universe) and every tuple
\overline{a}
.

For a class
{\mathcal{S}}
of semirings, we write
\psi\equiv_{{\mathcal{S}}}\varphi
if

\psi\equiv_{K}\varphi
holds for all
K\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Basic mathematical notions such as isomorphisms, partial isomorphisms and elementary equivalence
naturally extend from relational structures to
K
-interpretations.
We lift bijections
\sigma\colon A\to B
to literals
L\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
in the obvious way, i.e.,
\sigma(L)\in\Lit_{B}(\tau)
results from
L
by replacing each element
a\in A
with
\sigma(a)\in B
.

Definition 2

(Isomorphism)
.

Let
\pi_{A}\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\to K
and
\pi_{B}\colon\Lit_{B}(\tau)\allowbreak\to K
be two
K
-interpretations.
We say that
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
are
isomorphic
(denoted
\pi_{A}\cong\pi_{B}
) if there is a bijection
\sigma\colon A\to B
such that

\pi_{A}(L)=\pi_{B}(\sigma(L))
for all
L\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
.

A
partial isomorphism
between
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
is a bijection
\sigma\colon X\to Y
on subsets

X\subseteq A
and
Y\subseteq B
such that
\pi_{A}(L)=\pi_{B}(\sigma(L))
for all literals
L\in L_{X}(\tau)

(i.e., literals that are instantiated
with elements from
X
only).

Definition 3

(Elementary equivalence)
.

Let
\pi_{A}\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\to K
and
\pi_{B}\colon\Lit_{B}(\tau)\to K
be two
K
-interpretations,
and
\overline{a}\in A^{r}
and
\overline{b}\in B^{r}
be tuples of the same length.
The pairs
\pi_{A},\overline{a}
and
\pi_{B},\overline{b}
are
elementarily equivalent
, denoted
\pi_{A},\overline{a}\equiv\pi_{B},\overline{b}
, if

\pi_{A}[\![\varphi(\overline{a})]\!]=\pi_{B}[\![\varphi(\overline{b})]\!]
for all
\varphi(\overline{x})\in{\rm FO}(\tau)
.
They are
m
-
equivalent
, denoted
\pi_{A},\overline{a}\equiv_{m}\pi_{B},\overline{b}
,
if the above holds for all
\varphi(\overline{x})
with quantifier rank at most
m
.

It is obvious that, as in classical semantics,
isomorphism implies elementary equivalence.
For finite universes, the converse is true in classical semantics,
but fails in semiring semantics for certain semirings, including very simple ones such as finite min-max semirings
(see
[
14
]
).

Towards locality properties, we define distances between two elements
a,b
in a
K
-interpretation
\pi

based on the induced structure
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi}
.

Definition 4

(Gaifman graph)
.

The
Gaifman graph

G(\pi)
of a model-defining
K
-interpretation
\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\to K

is defined as the Gaifman graph
G({\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi})
of the induced
\tau
-structure.
That is, two elements
a\neq b
of
A
are adjacent in
G({\mathfrak{A}}_{\pi})
if, and only
if, there exists a positive literal
L=Rc_{1}\dots c_{r}\in\Lit_{A}(\tau)
such that
\pi(L)\neq 0
and

a,b\in\{c_{1},\dots c_{r}\}
.

We write
d(a,b)\in{\mathbb{N}}
for the distance of
a
and
b
in
G(\pi)
.
We further define the
r
-neighbourhood
of an element
a
in
\pi
as
B_{r}^{\pi}(a)\coloneqq\{b\in A:d(a,b)\leq r\}
.
For a tuple
\overline{a}\in A^{k}
we put
B_{r}^{\pi}(\overline{a})\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\leq k}B_{r}^{\pi}(a_{i})
.

Locality properties are really meaningful only for
semiring interpretations
\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\rightarrow K
that
track only positive information
,
which means that
\pi(\neg L)\in\{0,1\}
for each negative literal
\neg L
. Indeed, if also negative literals carry
non-trivial information, then either these must be taken into account in the definition of
what “local” means, which will trivialise the Gaifman graph (making it a clique)
so locality would become meaningless, or otherwise local information no longer suffices to determine values of even very simple
sentences involving negative literals, such as
\exists x\exists y\neg Rxy
.
We therefore consider here only
K
-interpretations over finite universes which are model-defining and track only positive information.

3
Hanf Locality

The first formalisation of locality we consider is Hanf locality.
We present generalisations of both the Hanf locality rank and of Hanf’s locality theorem, where the latter is conditional on algebraic properties of the semirings (cf.
[
8
,
23
]
for the classical proofs).

3.1
Hanf Locality Rank

Recall that in classical semantics, every first-order formula is Hanf-local with locality rank depending only on the quantifier rank.
By a straightforward adaptation of the classical proof, it turns out that also in semiring semantics, every first-order formula is Hanf-local.

Definition 5

(
\rightleftharpoons_{r}
)
.

Let
\pi_{A}\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\to K
and
\pi_{B}\colon\Lit_{B}(\tau)\to K
be two
K
-interpretations.
For tuples
\overline{a}\subseteq A
and
\overline{b}\subseteq B
of matching length, we write
(\pi_{A},\overline{a})\rightleftharpoons_{r}(\pi_{B},\overline{b})
if there is a bijection
f\colon A\to B
such that
B_{r}^{\pi_{A}}(\overline{a},c)\cong B_{r}^{\pi_{B}}(\overline{b},f(c))
for all
c\in A
.

Proposition 6

(Hanf locality in semiring semantics)
.

Let
K
be an arbitrary semiring. For every first-order formula
\varphi(\overline{x})
,
there exists
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, depending only on the quantifier rank of
\varphi
, such that for
all model-defining
K
-interpretations
\pi_{A},\pi_{B}
that track only positive information,
and all tuples
\overline{a}
,
\overline{b}
we have that
\pi_{A}[\![\varphi(\overline{a})]\!]=\pi_{B}[\![\varphi(\overline{b})]\!]
whenever

(\pi_{A},\overline{a})\rightleftharpoons_{r}(\pi_{B},\overline{b})
.

This follows by a simple adaptation of the classical proof in
[
23
]
,
which relies on the inductive argument that whenever

(\pi_{A},\overline{a})\rightleftharpoons_{3r+1}(\pi_{B},\overline{b})
, then there exists a bijection
f\colon A\to B

such that
(\pi_{A},\overline{a},c)\rightleftharpoons_{r}(\pi_{B},\overline{b},f(c))
for all
c\in A
.
The only point that requires care is the combination of partial isomorphisms on disjoint and non-adjacent neighbourhoods,
which in our setting depends on the assumption that the
K
-interpretations only track positive information:

Lemma 7
.

Let
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
be model-defining
K
-interpretations that track only positive information.
Let
\sigma\colon B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a})\rightarrow B^{\pi_{B}}_{r}(\overline{b})
and
\sigma^{\prime}\colon B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a}^{\prime})\rightarrow B^{\pi_{B}}_{r}(\overline{b}^{\prime})

be two partial isomorphisms between disjoint
r
-neighbourhoods in
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
.
If
d(\overline{a},\overline{a}^{\prime})>2r+1
and
d(\overline{b},\overline{b}^{\prime})>2r+1
,
then
(\sigma\cup\sigma^{\prime})\colon B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a},\overline{a}^{\prime})\rightarrow B^{\pi_{B}}_{r}(\overline{b},\overline{b}^{\prime})
is also a partial isomorphism.

Proof.

Clearly
(\sigma\cup\sigma^{\prime})
is a bijection, so we only have to show that

\pi_{A}(L)=\pi_{B}((\sigma\cup\sigma^{\prime})(L))
for every literal
L\in\Lit_{X}(\tau)
,
where
X=B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a}\overline{a}^{\prime})
. For every literal
L
instantiated with only elements
from
B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a})
or only elements from
B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a}^{\prime})
this is clear,
since
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are partial isomorphisms. So consider a literal

L
that is instantiated by elements from both
B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a})
and
B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{a}^{\prime})
,
which implies that
L^{\prime}\coloneqq(\sigma\cup\sigma^{\prime})(L)
is instantiated by
elements from both
B^{\pi_{B}}_{r}(\overline{b})
and
B^{\pi_{A}}_{r}(\overline{b}^{\prime})
.
If
L
is a positive literal, then
\pi_{A}(L)=0
and
\pi_{B}(L^{\prime})=0
,
as otherwise
d(\overline{a},\overline{a}^{\prime})\leq 2r+1
or
d(\overline{b},\overline{b}^{\prime})\leq 2r+1
.
If
L
is a negative literal, i.e.,
L=\neg\hat{L}
for a positive literal
\hat{L}
,
then by the same argument,
\pi_{A}(\hat{L})=\pi_{B}(\hat{L}^{\prime})=0
.
Since
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
are model-defining and track only positive information,
we have
\pi_{A}(L)=\pi_{B}(L^{\prime})=1
.
∎

3.2
Hanf’s Locality Theorem

Hanf’s locality theorem provides a sufficient combinatorial criterion for the
m
-equivalence of two structures, i.e. for
their indistinguishability by sentences of quantifier rank up to
m
.
We now turn to the question under what conditions this theorem generalises to
semiring semantics.
Its classical proof (cf.
[
8
]
) proceeds
by showing that Hanf’s criterion admits the construction of a back-and-forth system

(I_{j})_{j\leq m}
which, by the Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé theorem, implies the
m
-equivalence of the
two structures. It turns out that this method carries over to
K
-interpretations precisely in the
case that the semiring
K
is fully idempotent. We further show that for semirings that are not fully idempotent,
there actually are counterexamples to Hanf’s locality theorem.

Definition 8

(Back-and-forth system)
.

Let
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
be two
K
-interpretations and let
k\geq 0
.
A
m
-back-and-forth system
for
\pi_{A}
and

\pi_{B}
is a sequence
(I_{j})_{j\leq m}
of finite sets of partial
isomorphisms between
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
such that

•

\varnothing\in I_{m}
, and

•

for all
j<m
, the set
I_{j+1}
has back-and-forth extensions in
I_{j}
,
i.e., whenever
\overline{a}\mapsto\overline{b}\in I_{j+1}
then for every
c\in A
there exists
d\in B
,
and vice versa, such that
(\overline{a}c)\mapsto(\overline{b}d)
is in
I_{j}
.

We write
(I_{j})_{j\leq m}\colon\pi_{A}\cong_{m}\pi_{B}

if
(I_{j})_{j\leq m}
is a
m
-back-and-forth system for
\pi_{A}
and
\pi_{B}
.

Back-and-forth systems can be seen as algebraic descriptions of winning strategies in Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé
games, and in classical semantics, an
m
-back-and-forth system between two structures exists
if, and only if, the structures are
m
-equivalent. However, in semiring semantics this equivalence may, in general,
fail in both directions
[
6
]
. A detailed investigation of the relationship between elementary equivalence,

Proposition 9
.

Let
\pi_{A}
and

be

-interpretations into a fully idempotent semiring

\pi_{A}
and

\pi_{A}\equiv_{m}\pi_{B}

Proof.

We show by induction that for every first-order formula

For the inductive case it suffices to consider formulae

\overline{a}\mapsto\overline{b}\in I_{j+1}

\displaystyle\pi_{A}[\![\exists y\,\varphi(\overline{a},y)]\!]

\displaystyle\text{and }\qquad\pi_{B}[\![\exists y\,\psi(\overline{b},y)]\!]

\displaystyle=\prod_{c\in A}\pi_{A}[\![\varphi(\overline{a},c)]\!]

\displaystyle\text{and }\qquad\pi_{B}[\![\forall y\,\psi(\overline{b},y)]\!]

\displaystyle=\prod_{d\in B}\pi_{B}[\![\varphi(\overline{b},d)]\!].

Gaifman Normal Forms in Semirings Semantics

We briefly recall the classical notion of Gaifman normal forms (cf.
[
10
,
8
]
), which capture locality in a syntactic way.
Gaifman normal forms are Boolean combinations of
local formulae

\varphi^{(r)}(x)
and
basic local sentences
.
A local formula
\varphi^{(r)}(x)
is a formula in which all quantifiers are
relativised
to the
r
-neighbourhood of
x
,
for instance
\exists y\,\vartheta(x,y)
is relativised to
\exists y(d(x,y)\leq r\land\vartheta(x,y))
.
Here,

asserts that
x
and
y
have distance
\leq r
in the Gaifman graph, which can easily be expressed in first-order logic (in Boolean semantics).
A basic local sentence asserts that there exist
scattered
elements, i.e., elements with distinct
r
-neighbourhoods, which all satisfy the same
r
-local formula:
\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{m}(\bigwedge_{i\neq j}d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\land\bigwedge_{i}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i}))
.
By Gaifman’s theorem, every formula has an equivalent Gaifman normal form, which intuitively means that it only makes statements about distinct local neighbourhoods.

Moving to semiring semantics, we keep the notion of Gaifman normal forms close to the original one, with two exceptions.
First, we only consider formulae in negation normal form.
This means that we restrict to
positive
Boolean combinations and, in turn, permit the duals of basic local sentences (i.e., the negations of basic local sentences, in negation normal form).
Second and most importantly, we lose the ability to express relativised quantifiers

Counterexamples Against Gaifman Normal Forms

This section presents two examples for which a Gaifman normal form does not exist.
Both use the vocabulary
\tau=\{U\}
with only unary predicates, so that the Gaifman graph
G(\pi)
of any
K
-interpretation

\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\tau)\rightarrow K
is trivial and the
r
-neighbourhood of a point, for any
r
, consists only of the point itself.
Thus, local formulae
\varphi^{(r)}(x)
around
x
can always be written as positive Boolean combinations of literals
Ux
,
\neg Ux

and equalities
x=x
,
x\neq x
.
Scattered tuples are simply distinct tuples, so basic local sentences take the form

{\exists}^{\text{distinct}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\bigwedge_{i\leq m}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i})\quad\text{ or }\quad{\forall}^{\text{distinct}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\bigvee_{i\leq m}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i}).

5.1
A Formula Without a Gaifman Normal Form

Consider the formula
\psi(x)\coloneqq\exists y(Uy\land y\neq x)
which, in classical Boolean semantics, has the
Gaifman normal form
\varphi(x)\coloneqq{\exists}^{\text{distinct}}(y,z)\big((Uy\land Uz)\lor(\neg Ux\land\exists yUy)\big)
.
However, in semiring semantics it is in general not the case that
\psi(x)\equiv_{K}\varphi(x)
.
Indeed, for a semiring interpretation
\pi\colon\Lit_{A}(\{U\})\rightarrow K
and
a\in A
we have that

\pi[\![\psi(a)]\!]=\sum_{b\neq a}\pi(Ub)\qquad\text{but}\qquad\pi[\![\varphi(a)]\!]=\sum_{\{b,c\}\subseteq A\atop b\neq c}\pi(Ub)\pi(Uc)+\pi(\neg Ua)\sum_{b}\pi(Ub).

Here we consider the specific case of a universe with two elements
A=\{a,b\}
and model-defining
K
-interpretations
\pi_{st}

with
\pi_{st}(Ua)=s
and
\pi_{st}(Ub)=t
, where
s,t\in K\setminus\{0\}
and
s\neq t
.
Then
\pi_{st}[\![\psi(a)]\!]=t
but
\pi_{st}[\![\varphi(a)]\!]=st+ts
.
So, unless
K
is the Boolean semiring, we find elements
s,t
where

\pi_{st}[\![\psi(a)]\!]\neq\pi_{st}[\![\varphi(a)]\!]
, e.g.
t=1
,
s=2
in the natural semiring.

Of course, it might still be the case that there is a different Gaifman normal form
of
\psi(x)
for semiring interpretations in a specific semiring
K
.
We prove that this is not the case.

Proposition 14
.

In any naturally ordered semiring with at least three element, the
formula
\psi(x)=\exists y(Uy\land y\neq x)
does not have a Gaifman normal form.

For the proof, we describe the values that the building blocks of Gaifman normal forms may assume in
\pi_{st}
.
Recall that a local formula
\alpha(x)
is equivalent to a positive Boolean combination of literals
Ux
,
\neg Ux
, and equalities.
Since
\pi_{st}(\neg Ux)=0
for all
x\in A
, we can ignore negative literals and thus view the evaluation
\pi_{st}[\![\alpha(a)]\!]
as an expression built from the
semiring operations, the value
\pi_{st}(Ua)=s
and constants
0,1
.
Similarly,
\pi_{st}[\![\alpha(b)]\!]
is evaluated in the same way, but using
\pi_{st}(Ub)=t
instead of
s
.
Hence there is a polynomial
p_{\alpha}(X)\in K[X]
such that
\pi_{st}[\![\alpha(a)]\!]=p_{\alpha}(s)
and
\pi_{st}[\![\alpha(b)]\!]=p_{\alpha}(t)
, for all choices of
s,t
.
For example,
\alpha^{(r)}(x)=Ux\lor\exists y{\in}B_{r}^{\tau}(x)(x\neq y\land Uy)
can equivalently be written as
Ux\lor(x\neq x\land Ux)
,
which corresponds to the polynomial
p(X)=X+(0\cdot X)=X
.
For the evaluation of a basic local sentence
\beta={\exists}^{\text{distinct}}(y,z)(\alpha(y)\land\alpha(z))
,
we have
\pi_{st}[\![\beta]\!]=\pi_{st}[\![\alpha(a)\land\alpha(b)]\!]+\pi_{st}[\![\alpha(b)\land\alpha(a)]\!]=p_{\alpha}(s)p_{\alpha}(t)+p_{\alpha}(t)p_{\alpha}(s)
.
That is,
\beta
can be described by a polynomial
p_{\beta}(X,Y)\in K[X,Y]
such that
\pi_{st}[\![\beta]\!]=p_{\beta}(s,t)
and
p_{\beta}
is
symmetric
(that is,
p_{\beta}(X,Y)=p_{\beta}(Y,X)
).
The same holds for universal basic local sentences
\beta={\forall}^{\text{distinct}}(y,z)(\alpha(y)\lor\alpha(z))
.

It follows that we can represent every formula
\varphi(x)
in Gaifman normal form by a polynomial

f_{\varphi}(X,Y)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(X)g_{i}(X,Y)
, where the
g_{i}
are symmetric,
such that
\pi_{st}[\![\varphi(a)]\!]=f_{\varphi}(s,t)
for all
s,t
.

\Cref
thmCounterexampleFormula then follows from the following algebraic observation.

Lemma 15
.

Let
K
be a naturally ordered semiring with at least three elements.
For any polynomial
f(X,Y)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(X)g_{i}(X,Y)

where the
g_{i}
are symmetric polynomials, there
exist values
s,t\in K\setminus\{0\}
such that
f(s,t)\neq t
.

Proof.

We first consider the case that the semiring
K
is
absorptive
, that is,
1+r=1
for all
r\in K
(or, equivalently, that
1
is the maximal element).
Pick any
\varepsilon\in K\setminus\{0,1\}
.
Then
0<\varepsilon<1
by minimality/maximality of
0,1
.
Consider
(s,t)=(\varepsilon,1)
.
If
f(s,t)\neq 1
we are done, so suppose
f(s,t)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(\varepsilon)\cdot g_{i}(\varepsilon,1)=1
.
We now switch to
(s,t)=(1,\varepsilon)
.
By symmetry, we have
g_{i}(\varepsilon,1)=g_{i}(1,\varepsilon)
for all
i
.
Note that both semiring operations, and hence all polynomials, are monotone w.r.t. the natural order.
We can thus conclude

f(1,\varepsilon)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(1)\cdot g_{i}(1,\varepsilon)\geq\sum_{i}h_{i}(\varepsilon)\cdot g_{i}(1,\varepsilon)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(\varepsilon)\cdot g_{i}(\varepsilon,1)=1>\varepsilon.

Now assume that
K
is naturally ordered, but
not
absorptive.
We claim that there is
t\in K
with
t>1
.
Indeed, since
K
is not absorptive there is
r\in K
with
1+r\neq 1
.
Since
1+r\geq 1
by definition of the natural order, we must have
t\coloneqq 1+r>1
.
If
f(1,t)\neq t
we are done, so suppose
f(1,t)=t
.
By symmetry of
g_{i}
and monotonicity, we conclude

f(t,1)=\sum_{i}h_{i}(t)\cdot g_{i}(t,1)\geq\sum_{i}h_{i}(1)\cdot g_{i}(1,t)=f(1,t)=t\;>\;1.\qed

5.2
A Sentence Without a Gaifman Normal Form

While Gaifman normal forms need not exist for formulae, in all relevant semirings beyond the Boolean one,
they might still exist for sentences.
Indeed, we shall prove a positive result for min-max semirings.
However, such a result seems only possible for semirings where both operations are idempotent, similar to Hanf’s theorem.
For other semirings one can find rather simple counterexamples, as we illustrate for the tropical semiring
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\infty},\min,+,\infty,0)
.
Notice the unusual operations and neutral elements: in particular, true equalities are interpreted by
0
and false equalities by
\infty
.

Proposition 16
.

The sentence
\psi\coloneqq\exists z\forall x\exists y(Uy\vee x=z)

has no Gaifman normal form in the tropical semiring.

Proof.

We consider, for every
n\geq 1
, the

\mathbb{T}
-interpretations
\pi_{n}
with universe
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
such that

\pi_{n}(Ua_{i})=1
(and hence
\pi_{n}(\neg Ua_{i})=\infty
) for all
n
and all
i
.
Clearly
\pi_{n}[\![\psi]\!]=n-1
for all
n\geq 1
.

Recall that every local formula
\varphi^{(r)}(x)
is just a positive Boolean combination of literals
Ux
,
\neg Ux
,
and
x=x
, which means that there exists a fixed constant
c_{\varphi}\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
such that

\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]=c_{\varphi}
for all
n
and all
i
. It follows that for the basic local sentences
we have that

\displaystyle\pi_{n}[\![{\exists}^{\text{distinct}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\bigwedge_{i\leq m}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i})]\!]

\displaystyle=mc_{\varphi},

\displaystyle\pi_{n}[\![{\forall}^{\text{distinct}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\bigvee_{i\leq m}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i})]\!]

\displaystyle=n\cdot(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)\cdot c_{\varphi}\geq nc_{\varphi}.

For a local sentence
\eta
, we thus have that
\pi_{n}[\![\eta]\!]
is an expression built from the operations
\min
and
+
, terms of the form
n\cdots(n-m+1)
and constants
c\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Hence either
\pi_{n}[\![\eta]\!]\geq n
for all sufficiently large
n
, or
\pi_{n}[\![\eta]\!]=c
for some constant
c
.
Thus
\eta\not\equiv_{\mathbb{T}}\psi
.
∎

A similar construction works for the natural semiring
({\mathbb{N}},+,\cdot,0,1)

and we conjecture that it can be adapted to any infinite semiring with operations that are not idempotent.

6
Gaifman’s Theorem for Min-Max Semirings

In this section, we prove our main result: a version of Gaifman’s theorem for sentences evaluated in min-max semirings (which can be lifted to lattice semirings, see
\cref
secStrengthening).
We write
\mathcal{M}
for the class of min-max semirings and refer to
\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}
as
minmax-equivalence
.
Further, we use the notation
\leq_{\mathcal{M}}
(and similarly
\geq_{\mathcal{M}}
), where
\varphi\leq_{\mathcal{M}}\psi
means that
\pi[\![\varphi]\!]\leq\pi[\![\psi]\!]
for every
K
-interpretation
\pi
in a min-max semiring
K
.

Theorem 17

(Gaifman normal form)
.

Let
\tau
be a finite relational signature.
Every
{\rm FO}(\tau)
-sentence
\psi
is minmax-equivalent to a local sentence.

Contrary to Hanf’s locality theorem, we cannot follow the classical proofs of Gaifman’s theorem.
For instance, the proof in
[
8
]
is based on the Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé method and makes use of characteristic sentences, which in general do not exist in semiring semantics over min-max semirings (cf.
[
14
]
).
Gaifman’s original proof
[
10
]
is a constructive quantifier elimination argument (which is similar to our approach), but makes use of negation to encode case distinctions in the formula.
In semiring semantics, this is only possible to a limited degree (e.g.,
\vartheta\lor\neg\vartheta
is not guaranteed to evaluate to
1
, so we may have
\varphi\not\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\varphi\land(\vartheta\lor\neg\vartheta)
).
Another argument why Gaifman’s proof does not go through is that it applies to formulae, whereas formulae need not have Gaifman normal forms in our setting (cf.
\cref
secCounterexampleFormula).

Instead, we present a novel proof of Gaifman’s theorem that applies to the Boolean case as well as to min-max semirings.
While our strategy is similar to Gaifman’s – a constructive elimination of quantifier alternations – we have to phrase all results in terms of sentences and need to be more careful to derive equivalences that hold in all min-max semirings.
This turns out to be surprisingly difficult, but in the end also leads to a stronger version of Gaifman’s classical result (see
\cref
secStrengthening).

6.1
Toolbox

The proof consists of a series of rather technical equivalence transformations, but is based on a few key observations that we present below.
For the remaining section, we fix a finite relational signature
\tau
and a min-max semiring
K
, unless stated otherwise.

Normal forms.

All min-max semirings share algebraic properties such as distributivity and idempotency of both operations with the Boolean semiring, so many classical equivalences of first-order logic are also minmax-equivalences.
For instance, every semiring is distributive which implies that
\land
distributes over
\lor
.
In min-max semirings, we further have the dual property (
\max
distributes over
\min
), thus also
\varphi_{1}\lor(\varphi_{2}\land\varphi_{3})\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2})\land(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{3})
.
This means that we can transform any positive Boolean combination into a minmax-equivalent
disjunctive normal form
(DNF), and into
conjunctive normal form
(CNF).
Moreover, one easily verifies that we can push quantifiers to the front (tacitly assuming distinct variable names), so it suffices to prove
\cref
thmGaifman for sentences in
prenex normal form
.
We list the relevant minmax-equivalences below.

Lemma 18
.

For all FO-formulae,

1.

\varphi\land\varphi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\varphi\lor\varphi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\varphi
,

2.

\varphi\lor(\varphi\land\psi)\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\varphi\land(\varphi\lor\psi)\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\varphi
,

3.

\varphi_{1}\land(\varphi_{2}\lor\varphi_{3})\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2})\lor(\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{3})
,

4.

\varphi_{1}\lor(\varphi_{2}\land\varphi_{3})\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2})\land(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{3})
,

5.

\varphi\circ(\exists x\,\vartheta)\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\exists x(\varphi\circ\vartheta)
, for
\circ\in\{\lor,\land\}
, if
x
does not occur free in
\varphi
,

6.

\varphi\circ(\forall x\,\vartheta)\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\forall x(\varphi\circ\vartheta)
, for
\circ\in\{\lor,\land\}
, if
x
does not occur free in
\varphi
.

7.

\exists x\,(\varphi\lor\vartheta)\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}(\exists x\,\varphi)\lor(\exists x\,\vartheta)
, and analogously for
\forall
and
\land
.

Proof.

Direct consequences of the algebraic properties of min-max semirings: idempotence, absorption, distributivity and commutativity.
∎

Symmetry.

It will be convenient to exploit the inherent symmetry of min-max semirings to simplify the following proofs.
For example, the proof of
\cref
thmGaifman for sentences of the form
\exists\overline{y}\forall\overline{x}\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})
is completely symmetric to the one for sentences
\forall\overline{y}\exists\overline{x}\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})
, by exchanging minima and maxima in the proof.

This insight can be formalised. With a formula
\varphi
we associate its
dual

{\varphi}^{*}
, resulting from
\varphi
by swapping
\exists
,
\forall
(including scattered and ball quantifiers) and also
\lor
,
\land
, e.g.:

{({\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x,y)\;\forall z{\in}B_{r}(x)\,(\vartheta(z)\lor\psi(y))}^{*}={\forall}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x,y)\;\exists z{\in}B_{r}(x)\,({\vartheta}^{*}(z)\land{\psi}^{*}(y)).

Lemma 19

(Symmetry)
.

If a sentence
\psi
is minmax-equivalent to a local sentence, then so is
{\psi}^{*}
.

Proof.

First observe that if
\varphi(\overline{x})
is
r
-local around
\overline{x}
, then so is
{\varphi}^{*}(\overline{x})
.
Similarly, if
\varphi
is a basic local sentence, then so is
{\varphi}^{*}
.
Fix any min-max semiring
K=(K,\max,\min,0,1)
.
We define its
dual
as
{K}^{*}=(K,\min,\max,1,0)
, i.e. by inverting the underlying order.
Since
K
and
{K}^{*}
are both min-max semirings and share the same domain, we can interpret any
K
-interpretation
\pi
also as
{K}^{*}
-interpretation, which we denote as
{\pi}^{*}
.

Now let
\psi
be minmax-equivalent to a local sentence.
The equivalence holds in particular for
{K}^{*}
, and by putting the local sentence in disjunctive normal form, we have

\psi\equiv_{{K}^{*}}\bigvee_{i}\bigwedge_{j}\varphi_{ij},\qquad\text{with basic local sentences }\varphi_{ij}.

We claim that

{\psi}^{*}\equiv_{K}\bigwedge_{i}\bigvee_{j}{\varphi}^{*}_{ij},

which prove the lemma.
To prove the claim, let
\pi
be a
K
-interpretation (over universe
A
).
We first note that for any FO-formula
\varphi(\overline{x})
and any tuple
\overline{a}\subseteq A
of matching arity,
\pi[\![\varphi(\overline{a})]\!]={\pi}^{*}[\![{\varphi}^{*}(\overline{a})]\!]
by a straightforward induction on
\varphi
.
Then

\pi[\![{\psi}^{*}]\!]={\pi}^{*}[\![{({\psi}^{*})}^{*}]\!]={\pi}^{*}[\![\psi]\!]\overset{(\dagger)}{=}{\pi}^{*}\big\llbracket\bigvee_{i}\bigwedge_{j}\varphi_{ij}\big\rrbracket=\pi\big\llbracket\bigwedge_{i}\bigvee_{j}{\varphi}^{*}_{ij}\big\rrbracket.\qed

Logic.

Contrary to Gaifman’s original proof, our results only apply to sentences (cf.
\cref
secCounterexampleFormula).
However, we still need to apply intermediary results to subformulae involving free variables.
This is possible by the following lemma, which allows us to temporarily replace atoms involving the undesired variables with fresh relation symbols.

Intuitively, we think of the new relation as an abstraction of the original atoms.
All equivalence transformations that we perform in the abstract setting also hold, in particular, in the original setting.
However, there is one caveat: in order to apply our results in the abstract setting, we must also extend the ball quantifiers to the new signature.
Since we do not want to change the semantics of the quantifiers, we thus restrict the lemma to situations where the new relation does not add any edges to the Gaifman graph.

We use the following notation.
Let
\varphi
be a
\tau
-formula, including ball quantifiers of the form
Qy{\in}B_{r}^{\tau}(x)
.
Given a different signature
\tau^{\prime}
, we write
\varphi^{\langle{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle}
for the formula where all
Qy{\in}B_{r}^{\tau}(x)
are replaced with
Qy{\in}B_{r}^{\tau^{\prime}}(x)
(assuming this is well-defined).
Further, we write
\varphi[\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})/R\overline{x}]
to replace every occurrence of
\vartheta(\overline{z},\overline{y})
for any variable tuple
\overline{z}
by the atom
R\overline{z}
.

Lemma 20

(Abstraction)
.

Let
K
be an arbitrary semiring,
\varphi(\overline{y})
a
\tau
-formula and
\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})
a subformula of
\varphi(\overline{y})
.
Let
R
be a fresh relation symbol with arity matching
\overline{x}
, and set
\tau^{\prime}=\tau\cup\{R\}
.
If we have

•

\varphi[\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})/R\overline{x}]^{\langle{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle}(\overline{y})\allowbreak\equiv_{K}\psi(\overline{y})
for a
\tau^{\prime}
-formula
\psi
(abstract setting
\tau^{\prime}
), and

•

R
is unary or
\vartheta=P\overline{z}
is a positive literal with
\overline{x}\subseteq\overline{z}
,

then also
\varphi(\overline{y})\equiv_{K}\psi[R\overline{x}/\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})]^{\langle{\tau}\rangle}(\overline{y})
(original setting
\tau
).
This remains true if we only replace some occurrences of
\vartheta
by
R
, but not all.

Proof.

Intuitively, if the equivalence holds for a fresh relation symbol
R
, then it holds for any interpretation of
R
, and hence in particular for the interpretation defined by
\vartheta
.

Formally, assume that
\varphi^{\langle{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle}[\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})/R(\overline{x})](\overline{y})\equiv_{K}\psi(\overline{y})
.
Let
\pi
be a
K
-interpretation over universe
A
and signature
\tau
, and let
\overline{b}\subseteq A
.
We have to show that
\pi[\![\varphi(\overline{b})]\!]=\pi[\![\psi^{\langle{\tau}\rangle}[R(\overline{x})/\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})](\overline{b})]\!]
.
Extend
\pi
to a
K
-interpretation
\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}
over
A
and
\tau^{\prime}
by setting

\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}(R\overline{a})\coloneqq\pi[\![\vartheta(\overline{a},\overline{b})]\!],\quad\text{for all $\overline{a}\subseteq A$}.

We first show that the extension does not affect the Gaifman graph, i.e.,
G(\pi)=G(\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}})
.
This is clearly the case if
R
is unary.
Otherwise, we have
\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})=P\overline{z}
and
\overline{x}\subseteq\overline{z}
by assumption.
The extension can only add new edges to the Gaifman graph, so assume that
G(\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}})
contains an additional edge between two elements
c,d
.
Hence there is a tuple
\overline{a}
with
c,d\in\overline{a}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}(R\overline{a})\neq 0
.
Since
\overline{x}\subseteq\overline{z}
, the elements
c,d
also occur in the atom
\vartheta(\overline{a},\overline{b})
with
\pi[\![\vartheta(\overline{a},\overline{b})]\!]\neq 0
, hence the edge between
c,d
is already present in
G(\pi)
.

Since the Gaifman graphs coincide, ball quantifiers
Qy{\in}B_{r}^{\tau}(x)
in
\pi
and
Qy{\in}B_{r}^{\tau^{\prime}}(x)
in
\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}
range over the same elements.
By a straightforward induction, we thus have

\displaystyle\pi[\![\varphi(\overline{b})]\!]

\displaystyle=\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}[\![\varphi[\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})/R\overline{x}]^{\langle{\tau^{\prime}}\rangle}(\overline{b})]\!]

\displaystyle\text{by construction of $\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}$ (induction on $\varphi$)},

\displaystyle=\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}[\![\psi(\overline{b})]\!]

\displaystyle\text{by the $K$-equivalence (abstract setting)},

\displaystyle=\pi[\![\psi[R\overline{x}/\vartheta(\overline{x},\overline{y})]^{\langle{\tau}\rangle}(\overline{b})]\!]

\displaystyle\text{by construction of $\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{b}}$ (induction on $\psi$)}.\qed

Locality.

Concerning locality, we make two simple but crucial observations.
For the first one, consider a local formula
\varphi^{(r)}(x,y)
around two variables
x
and
y
.
Such a formula may assert that
x
and
y
are close to each other, for instance
\varphi^{(r)}(x,y)=Exy
.
But if
x
and
y
do not occur together within one literal, then
\varphi^{(r)}
intuitively makes independent statements about the neighbourhood of
x
, and the neighbourhood of
y
, so we can split
\varphi^{(r)}
into two separate local formulae.
For the general case
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})
in several variables, we group
\overline{x}
into tuples
\overline{x}^{1},\dots,\overline{x}^{n}
with the idea that
\varphi^{(r)}
makes independent statements about each group
\overline{x}^{i}
.

Lemma 21

(Separation)
.

Let
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{1},\dots,\overline{x}^{n})
be a local formula around
\overline{x}^{1}\dots\overline{x}^{n}
and
define
X_{i}
as the set of variables locally quantified around
\overline{x}^{i}
in
\varphi^{(r)}
.
If each literal of
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{1},\dots,\overline{x}^{n})
uses only variables in
\overline{x}^{i}\cup X_{i}
for some
i
,
then
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{1},\dots,\overline{x}^{n})
is minmax-equivalent to a positive Boolean combination of
r
-local formulae around each of the
\overline{x}^{i}
.

Proof.

We write
\overline{x}=\overline{x}^{1}\overline{x}^{2}\dots\overline{x}^{n}
and assume w.l.o.g. that
\varphi
is in prenex normal form,
so
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})=Q_{1}y_{1}\dots Q_{m}y_{m}\ \psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})
where
\psi
contains no quantifiers
and each
Q_{k}y_{k}
is of the form
\exists y_{k}{\in}B_{r_{k}}(z_{k})
or
\forall y_{k}{\in}B_{r_{k}}(z_{k})
.
Let
X_{i}^{k}\coloneqq X_{i}\cap\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
denote the subset of variables locally quantified around
\overline{x}_{i}
that are among the first
k
bound variables.
We show by induction on
k
, from
m
down to
0
, that there exists
\psi_{k}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k})
, a positive Boolean combination of
r
-local formulae around
\overline{x}^{1}\cup X_{1}^{k}
,
r
-local formulae around
\overline{x}^{2}\cup X_{2}^{k}
, …, such that

\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{1}y_{1}\dots Q_{k}y_{k}\ \psi_{k}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k})
.
Then
k=0
implies the lemma.

For
k=m
this certainly holds:
\psi(\overline{x},\overline{y})
is quantifier-free and by assumption all literals are local formulae around
\overline{x}^{i}\cup X_{i}
for some
i
.
Now assume the claim already holds for
k>0
.
We show that there is
\psi_{k-1}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k-1})
of the required form such that

Q_{1}y_{1}\dots Q_{k}y_{k}\ \psi_{k}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{1}y_{1}\dots Q_{k-1}y_{k-1}\ \psi_{k-1}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k-1}).

It suffices to consider the case
Q_{k}y_{k}=\exists y_{k}{\in}B_{r_{k}}(z_{k})
(the other case is symmetric).
Since
y_{k}
is locally quantified around
z_{k}
, there is an index
i_{0}
such that
either
y_{k}\in X_{i_{0}}
and
z_{k}\in\overline{x}^{i_{0}}
, or (if
z_{k}
is locally quantified as well)
y_{k},z_{k}\in X_{i_{0}}
.
We put
\psi_{k}
in disjunctive normal form,

\psi_{k}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigvee_{j}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{j,i}^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{i},X_{i}^{k}),

where each
\varphi_{j,i}
is a conjunction of literals (and hence a local formula) in
\overline{x}^{i}\cup X_{i}^{k}
.
Then

\displaystyle\exists y_{k}{\in}B_{r_{k}}(z_{k})\ \psi_{k}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k})

\displaystyle\,\overset{\mathclap{(\ref{thmAxioms})}}{\equiv}_{\mathcal{M}}\,

\displaystyle\bigvee_{j}\Big(\exists y_{k}{\in}B_{r_{k}}(z_{k})\ \bigwedge_{i}\varphi_{j,i}^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{i},X_{i}^{k})\Big)

\displaystyle\,\overset{\mathclap{(\dagger)}}{\equiv}_{\mathcal{M}}\,

\displaystyle\bigvee_{j}\Big(\underbrace{\exists y_{k}{\in}B_{r_{k}}(z_{k})\big(\varphi_{j,{i_{0}}}^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{i_{0}},X_{i_{0}}^{k})\big)}_{\text{local formula around $\overline{x}^{i_{0}}\cup X_{i_{0}}^{k-1}$}}\;\land\;\bigwedge_{i\neq i_{0}}\varphi_{j,i}^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{i},X_{i}^{k-1})\Big)\eqqcolon\psi_{k-1}(\overline{x},y_{1},\dots,y_{k-1}).

For
(\dagger)
, recall that
y_{k},z_{k}\in\overline{x}^{i_{0}}\cup X_{i_{0}}^{k}
and hence only
\varphi_{j,{i_{0}}}
may depend on
y_{k}
;
we can thus move the formulae
\varphi_{j,i}
for
i\neq i_{0}
out of the scope of the quantifier (cf.
\cref
thmAxioms).
Finally, we remark that all local formulae in
\psi_{k-1}
are of radius
r
, simply because their quantification dags are all contained in the quantification dag of the original formula
\varphi^{(r)}
of radius
r
.
We conclude that
\psi_{k}
has the required form and satisfies
(*)
.
∎

The second observation is that we can perform a clustering of any tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}

into classes
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
so that elements within one class have “small” distance to each other, whereas different classes are “far apart”.
This simple combinatorial observation is a fruitful tool to construct Gaifman normal forms:
it becomes easy to quantify elements with a known clustering, and by the following lemma we can then do a disjunction over all clusterings.

Definition 22

(Configuration)
.

Let
\pi
be a
K
-interpretation with universe
A
.
Let
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}
be a partition of
\{1,\dots,n\}
and define representatives
i_{l}=\min I_{l}
of each class.
We say that a tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
is in
configuration

(P,r)
, if

{alphaenumerate}

d(a_{i_{l}},a_{i})\leq 5^{n-k}r-r
, for all
i\in I_{l}
,
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,

d(a_{i_{l}},a_{i_{l^{\prime}}})>4\cdot 5^{n-k}r
, for all
l\neq l^{\prime}
(representatives are
(2\cdot 5^{n-k}r)
-scattered).

Notice that the definition of “small“ and “far apart“ depends not only on
r
, but also on
n
and the number of classes
k
.
It is easy to see that such a partition always exists: condition
(a)
remains true if we merge two classes violating
(b)
, so starting from
P=\{\{1\},\dots,\{n\}\}
we can prove the following lemma by merging classes until both conditions hold.

Lemma 23

(Clustering)
.

Let
\pi
be a
K
-interpretation on
A
.
For all tuples
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}
and all
r\geq 1
, there is a partition
P
such that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is in configuration
(P,r)
.

In the following proofs, we make frequent use of the Clustering Lemma and always stick to the notation
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}

with representatives
i_{l}=\min I_{l}
for each
1\leq i\leq k
.
The set of all partitions of
\{1,\dots,n\}
is denoted as
\text{Part}(n)
.

6.2
Proof of Gaifman’s Theorem

The heart of our proof is the elimination of quantifier alternations.
We split the proof into steps.
Each step proves, building on the previous ones,
that sentences of a certain fragment can be translated to minmax-equivalent
local sentences. These fragments consist of

{bracketenumerate}

sentences of the form
{\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x_{1},\dots x_{m})\bigwedge_{i\leq m}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(x_{i})
;

existential sentences
\exists\overline{x}\,\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})
;

existential-universal sentences
\exists\overline{y}\forall\overline{x}\,\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})
;

all first-order sentences (
\cref
thmGaifman).

We first prove that
(4)
follows from
(2)
and
(3)
.
This is based on the following lemma, which will be used to swap quantifiers.

Lemma 24
.

A positive Boolean combination of basic local sentences is minmax-equivalent both to a sentence of the form
\exists\overline{y}\forall\overline{x}\,\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x},\overline{y})
, and of the form
\forall\overline{y}\exists\overline{x}\,\psi^{(r)}(\overline{x},\overline{y})
.

Proof.

Recall that scattered quantifiers are just abbreviations for regular quantifiers and distance formulae.
Each basic local sentence uses only one type of quantifier (followed by a local formula).
When we bring the local sentence into prenex normal form, we can choose to first push all
\exists
-quantifiers to the front, and then the
\forall
-quantifiers (or vice versa).
The local formulae can all be combined into a single local formula (around all variables).
∎

Proof of
\cref
thmGaifman.

We prove by induction that every sentence of the form

\Psi=Q_{1}\overline{x}_{1}\,\dots Q_{n+1}\overline{x}_{n+1}\;\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x}_{n},\overline{x}_{n+1}\mid\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n-1})

with alternating quantifiers
Q_{i}\in\{\exists,\forall\}
(i.e., with
n
quantifier alternations), is equivalent to a local sentence.
This implies
\cref
thmGaifman (consider a quantifier-free formula
\varphi
).

The cases
n=0
and
n=1
are covered by
(2)
and
(3)
(possibly combined with the
Symmetry Lemma
).
For
n\geq 2
, consider the inner formula

\Phi(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n-1})=Q_{n}\overline{x}_{n}Q_{n+1}\overline{x}_{n+1}\;\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x}_{n},\overline{x}_{n+1}\mid\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n-1}).

We apply the
Abstraction Lemma
to successively replace all atoms (including equalities) involving the free variables
\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n-1}
with fresh relation symbols, resulting in a
sentence

\tilde{\Phi}
.
Assume
Q_{n}=\exists
and
Q_{n+1}=\forall
(the other case is symmetric).
We can now swap the quantifiers in
\tilde{\Phi}
by applying
(3)
and the previous lemma.
After reversing the substitution of atoms, we get that
\Phi(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n})
is minmax-equivalent to a formula of the form
\forall\overline{y}\exists\overline{z}\,\vartheta^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{z}\mid\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n})
.
Since
Q_{n-1}=\forall
, we can write
\Psi
as

\Psi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{1}\overline{x}_{1}\,\dots Q_{n-1}\overline{x}_{n-1}\overline{y}\;Q_{n}\overline{z}\;\vartheta^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{z}\mid\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n-1}),

which has only
n-1
quantifier alternations. The claim follows by induction.
∎

6.3
Asymmetric Basic Local Sentences

To simplify the proofs of steps
(2)
and
(3)
, we first establish the following lemma.
This can be seen as generalising the notion of basic local sentences by permitting different local formulae for each scattered variable
x_{i}
.
Such sentences have been called
asymmetric
basic local sentences in
[
19
,
7
]
.

Lemma 25

(Step
(1)
)
.

Every sentence of the form
{\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x_{1},\dots x_{m})\bigwedge_{i\leq m}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(x_{i})
is minmax-equivalent to an existential local sentence.

Proof sketch (
m=2
).

The proof is somewhat technical, so we first sketch the idea for the case
m=2
, i.e., for a sentence
\Phi={\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x,y)\big(\varphi^{(r)}(x)\land\psi^{(r)}(y)\big)
.
Imagine that we want to approximate
\Phi
with a single existential local sentence.
A first candidate would be
\Psi\coloneqq{\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x,y)\big((\varphi^{(r)}(x)\lor\psi^{(r)}(x))\land(\varphi^{(r)}(y)\lor\psi^{(r)}(y))\big)
,
where we take the disjunction of
\varphi^{(r)}
and
\psi^{(r)}
in order to have the same local formula for
x
and
y
.

Fix a
K
-interpretation
\pi
and recall that
\pi[\![\Phi]\!]=\max_{a,b}\min(\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!],\pi[\![\psi^{(r)}(b)]\!])
.
Since
\Psi
replaces each local formula with the maximum over both local formulae, we have
\Phi\leq_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
.
But we do not have equivalence since the maximal value
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]
could be assumed by choosing elements
a,b
and then choosing the same formula, say
\varphi
for both
a
and
b
.

To improve our approximation while preserving
\Phi\leq_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
, we set
\Psi^{\prime}\coloneqq\Psi\land\exists x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)\land\exists y\,\psi^{(r)}(y)
.
Now suppose that the maximal value
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]
is assumed by choosing
a,b
with
d(a,b)>4r
(so
a,b
are
2r
-scattered).
If the maximum is achieved by further choosing different local formulae for
a
and
b
, then
\pi[\![\Phi]\!]=\pi[\![\Psi^{\prime}]\!]
as desired.
So suppose we choose the same formula, say
\varphi
, for both
a
and
b
.
Let
c
be an element for which the maximal value
\pi[\![\exists y\ \psi^{(r)}(y)]\!]
of
\psi
is assumed.
As
c
cannot be in both
B_{2r}(a)
and
B_{2r}(b)
, we know that
c
forms a
2r
-scattered tuple with
a
or
b
, say with
a
.
Thus
\pi[\![\Phi]\!]\geq\min(\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!],\pi[\![\psi^{(r)}(c)]\!])\geq\pi[\![\Psi^{\prime}]\!]
, so again
\pi[\![\Phi]\!]=\pi[\![\Psi^{\prime}]\!]
.
In other words, our overapproximation
\Psi^{\prime}
is exact on tuples
(a,b)
where
a
and
b
are sufficiently far from each other.

To achieve equivalence, we can thus increase the radius in
\Psi^{\prime}
(from
r
- to
2r
-scattered) and cover the remaining cases where
a,b
are close by an additional local sentence:

\displaystyle\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\Big(\exists x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)\;\land\;\exists y\,\psi^{(r)}(y)\;\land\;{\exists}^{2r\textrm{-sc}}(x,y)\,(\varphi^{(r)}(x)\lor\psi^{(r)}(x))\land(\varphi^{(r)}(y)\lor\psi^{(r)}(y))\Big)

\displaystyle{}\lor\exists x\,\exists y{\in}B_{4r}(x)\,\big(\varphi^{(r)}(x)\,\land\,\psi^{(r)}(y)\,\land\,d(x,y)>2r\big).

Here, the first line consists of 3 basic local sentences and the second line adds another basic local sentence to account for the “close” case.
∎

Proof of
\cref
thmGaifmanGeneralizedBasic.

We generalise the above idea to
m
variables by applying the
Clustering Lemma
.
Let
\Phi={\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(x_{i})
.
To simplify notation, we write
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
and
\overline{x}_{-i}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{m})
.
The proof is by induction on
m
.
For
m=1
there is nothing to show.
For
m\geq 2
, we claim that
\Phi
is minmax-equivalent to

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle\coloneqq{}\Big({\exists}^{4r\textrm{-sc}}(\overline{x})\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\bigvee_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}^{(r)}(x_{i})\;\;\land\;\;\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}{\exists}^{4r\textrm{-sc}}(\overline{x}_{-j})\,\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(x_{i})\Big)\;\lor

(1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\bigvee_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}\,\in\,\text{Part}(m),\\
P\neq\{\{1\},\dots,\{m\}\}\end{subarray}}}\quad{\exists}^{(2\cdot 5^{m-k}4r)\textrm{-sc}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\varphi_{P,l}^{(5^{m-k}4r)}(x_{l}),

(2)

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{P,l}^{(5^{m-k}4r)}(x_{i_{l}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{l}\setminus\{i_{l}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}4r-r}(x_{i_{l}})\ \Big(\bigwedge_{i\in I_{l}}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(x_{i})\,\land\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in I_{l}\\
i<j\end{subarray}}\!d(x_{i},x_{j})>2r\Big)

and
i_{l}\coloneqq\min I_{l}
is a representative of the class
I_{l}
.
Essentially, line
(1)
covers the case where
\overline{x}
is scattered and line (2) covers all cases where some variables are close (the idea is that
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
are set to the representatives of the partition classes
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
).

We first argue that
\Psi
can be written as local sentence.
The left subformula in (1) is already a basic local sentence, since
\bigvee_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}^{(r)}(x_{i})
is a local formula around
x_{i}
.
The quantification over
\overline{x}_{-j}
and over
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
both use less than
m
variables,
so by induction they can be expressed as local sentences.
It thus remains to prove
\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
.

We first show that
\Phi\leq_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
.
Fix a
K
-interpretation
\pi
over universe
A
and let
\overline{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\subseteq A
be a tuple at which the maximum in
\Phi
is reached.
If
\overline{a}
is
4r
-scattered, then by using
\overline{a}
as witness in
(1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\;\geq\;\pi[\![(1)]\!]\;\geq\;\min\big(\pi[\![\varphi_{1}^{(r)}(a_{1})]\!],\dots,\pi[\![\varphi_{m}^{(r)}(a_{m})]\!]\big)=\pi[\![\Phi]\!].

Otherwise,
\overline{a}
is
r
-scattered, but not
4r
-scattered.
By the
Clustering Lemma
, there is a partition
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}
such that
\overline{a}
is in configuration
(P,4r)
.
Recall that the representatives
a_{i_{l}}
(with
i_{l}=\min I_{l}
) satisfy
d(a_{i_{l}},a_{i_{l^{\prime}}})>4\cdot 5^{n-k}4r
, so they are
(2\cdot 5^{n-k}4r)
-scattered.
In particular
P\neq\{\{1\},\dots,\{m\}\}
, because in this case
\overline{a}
would be
4r
-scattered.
By using the representatives
(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})
as witness in (2) and the remaining entries of
\overline{a}
as witnesses in the
\varphi_{P,l}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\;\geq\;\pi[\![(2)]\!]\;

\displaystyle\geq\;\pi\Big\llbracket\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\varphi_{P,l}^{(5^{m-k}4r)}(a_{i_{l}})\Big\rrbracket

\displaystyle\geq\;\pi\Big\llbracket\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\Big(\bigwedge_{i\in I_{l}}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(a_{i})\,\land\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in I_{l}\\
i<j\end{subarray}}\!d(a_{i},a_{j})>2r\Big)\Big\rrbracket

\displaystyle\overset{\mathclap{(*)}}{=}\;\prod_{i=1}^{m}\pi[\![\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]\;=\;\pi[\![\Phi]\!].

For
(*)
, recall that
\overline{a}
is
r
-scattered, so the atoms
d(a_{i},a_{j})>2r
all evaluate to
1
.

We now prove that
\Psi\leq_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi
.
We again fix a
K
-interpretation
\pi
and first observe that
\pi[\![(2)]\!]\leq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
.
To see this, note that the
m
elements quantified in
(2)
and in all
\varphi_{P,l}
together form an
r
-scattered tuple.
Indeed, the
k
-tuple of representatives in
(2)
is even
(2\cdot 5^{m-k}\cdot 4r)
-scattered.
Hence for
l\neq l^{\prime}
, elements locally quantified in
\varphi_{P,l}(x_{l})
have distance at least
(4\cdot 5^{m-k}4r)-2\cdot(5^{m-l}4r-r)\geq 2r
to those locally quantified in
\varphi_{P,l^{\prime}}(x_{l^{\prime}})
.
Within one
\varphi_{P,l}
, we explicitly assert that the locally quantified elements have distance at least
2r
among each other and to the representative.
We can thus use the quantified elements as
r
-scattered witness in
\Phi
.

It remains to show
\pi[\![(1)]\!]\leq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
.
Let
\overline{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{m})
be an
m
-tuple at which the maximum in
{\exists}^{4r\textrm{-sc}}(\overline{x})\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\bigvee_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}^{(r)}(x_{i})
is reached.
Let
\overline{a}_{j}=(a_{1,j},\dots,a_{j-1,j},a_{j+1,j},\dots,a_{m,j})
be an
(m-1)
-tuple at which the maximum in
{\exists}^{4r\textrm{-sc}}(\overline{x}_{-j})\,\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(x_{i})
is reached, for each
j
.
We say that an element
d\in A
is
associated to
\varphi_{i}
if

\pi[\![(1)]\!]\leq\pi[\![\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(d)]\!]
.
Thus, for all
i\neq j
, the element
a_{i,j}
is associated to
\varphi_{i}
, and all
b_{j}
are associated to some
\varphi_{i}
. To end the proof, it suffices to find an
r
-scattered tuple
\overline{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{m})
such that
d_{i}
is associated to
\varphi_{i}
for all
i
, as we can then use
\overline{d}
as witness in
\Phi
.

{claim*}

There is a
2r
-scattered tuple
\overline{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m})
such that
c_{i}
is associated to
\varphi_{i}
for
i\geq 2
, and
c_{1}
is associated to
\varphi_{i_{0}}
for some
i_{0}
.

{claimproof}

Since
\overline{b}
is
4r-
scattered, the
m
balls
B_{2r}(b_{j})
are disjoint.
One of them, say
B_{4r}(b_{j_{0}})
, contains none of the
m-1
entries of
\overline{a}_{1}
.
Set
i_{0}
so that
b_{j_{0}}
is associated to
\varphi_{i_{0}}
.
We can then take
\overline{c}=(b_{j_{0}},\overline{a}_{1})
.

Let
\overline{c}
be such a tuple and set
i_{0}
accordingly.
Observe that we can simply take
\overline{d}=\overline{c}
if
i_{0}=1
, so assume that
i_{0}\neq 1
.
We construct a tuple
\overline{d}
with the desired property from
\overline{c}
and
\overline{a}_{i_{0}}
by the following algorithm.

initialise
\overline{d}_{-i_{0}}=\overline{a}_{i_{0}}
and
d_{i_{0}}=\bot
;

while

d_{i_{0}}=\bot

do

choose
i^{*}
such that
d_{i^{*}}\neq c_{i*}
and for all
i
:
d_{i}\notin B_{2r}(c_{i^{*}})
if defined;

if

i^{*}=1
or
i^{*}=i_{0}

then

set
d_{i_{0}}=c_{i^{*}}
;

else

set
d_{i^{*}}=c_{i^{*}}
;

end if

end while

{claim*}

This algorithm has the following invariant:
\overline{d}
is
r
-scattered and for each
i
, the entry
d_{i}
is either undefined or associated to
\varphi_{i}
.

{claimproof}

Whenever we update an entry
d_{i}
, the new value is associated to
\varphi_{i}
by choice of
\overline{c}
and
i_{0}
.
For the first part, recall that
\overline{a}_{i_{0}}
is
r
-scattered.
In each iteration, we choose
i^{*}
so that
c_{i^{*}}
is not close to any entry of
\overline{d}
, hence
\overline{d}
remains
r
-scattered after each update.

The algorithm clearly terminates:
the if-case defines
d_{i_{0}}
(thus ending the loop), and the else-case can only be executed once per entry of
\overline{d}
, as we require
d_{i^{*}}\neq c_{i^{*}}
.
It remains to show that while
d_{i_{0}}=\bot
, an
i^{*}
with the desired property always exists.
There are
m-1
defined entries of
\overline{d}
.
Let
n=|\{j:d_{j}\in\overline{c}\}|
be the number of entries contained in
\overline{c}
.
The remaining
(m-1)-n
defined entries are from
\overline{a}_{i_{0}}
.
Recall that
\overline{c}
is
2r
-scattered, hence the balls
(B_{2r}(c_{j}))_{1\leq j\leq n}
are disjoint.
There are
m-n
entries of
\overline{c}
that do not occur in
\overline{d}
, hence there is at least one entry
c_{j}
such that
B_{2r}(c_{j})
does not contain any of the
(m-1)-n
entries from
\overline{a}_{i_{0}}
.
We can then choose
i^{*}=j
.

This proves the algorithm correct, so there is always an
r
-scattered tuple
\overline{d}
such that
d_{i}
is associated to
\varphi_{i}
for all
i
.
We conclude that
\pi[\![(1)]\!]\leq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
and hence
\Psi\leq_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi
.
∎

6.4
Gaifman Normal Form for
\exists^{*}
-Sentences

Proposition 26

(Step
(2)
)
.

Every sentence of the form
\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{m}\ \varphi^{(r)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
is minmax-equivalent to an existential local sentence.

We proceed with step
(2)
of the proof.
The goal is to replace
\exists\overline{x}
by a scattered quantifier
{\exists}^{R\textrm{-sc}}(\overline{x})
, and to split
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})
into separate formulae
\varphi_{i}^{(R)}(x_{i})
local only around a single
x_{i}
(the previous lemma allows us to use different
\varphi_{i}
for each
x_{i}
).
We achieve this by using a disjunction over all partitions
P\in\text{Part}(m)
;
for a given partition
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}
we can do a scattered quantification of the representatives
x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}}
, as they are far apart.
The elements of each class
I_{l}
can then be locally quantified around
x_{i_{l}}
.
Applying the
Separation Lemma
allows us to split
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})
into separate local formulae for each class.

Proof.

By the
Clustering Lemma
, we have
\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigvee_{P\in\text{Part}(m)}\Phi_{P}
for

\displaystyle\Phi_{P}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle{\exists}^{(2\cdot 5^{m-k}r)\textrm{-sc}}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle\quad\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{1}\backslash\{i_{1}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}r-r}(x_{i_{1}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\dots

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{k}\backslash\{i_{k}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}r-r}(x_{i_{k}})\ \ \varphi^{(r)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),

where
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}
with representatives
i_{l}\coloneqq\min I_{l}
, as usual.
Indeed,
\Phi_{P}
evaluates to the maximum of
\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{a})]\!]
over all tuples
\overline{a}
in configuration
(P,r)
, and any tuple is in configuration
(P,r)
for some partition
P
by the lemma.

We fix a partition
P
and show that
\Phi_{P}
is minmax-equivalent to a local sentence; this implies the claim.
Notice that
\Phi_{P}
is not yet a basic local sentence, as the inner formula depends on all quantified variables
x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}}
at once, instead of being applied to all variables individually.
This can be achieved by the
Separation Lemma
, after some preparation.
Let
X_{i}
be the set of bound variables in
\varphi^{(r)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
that are locally quantified around
x_{i}
.
We say that a literal
R\overline{y}
(or an equality
y_{1}=y_{2}
) is
split by
P
if
\overline{y}
contains variables from
\{x_{i}\}\cup X_{i}
and
\{x_{j}\}\cup X_{j}
for some
i\in I_{l}
and
j\in I_{l^{\prime}}
with
l\neq l^{\prime}
, so
i
and
j
are in different partition classes.

Now let
\hat{\varphi}^{(r)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
result from
\varphi^{(r)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
by replacing all positive literals (including equalities) that are split by
P
with
false
(i.e.,
x_{1}\neq x_{1}
) and all negative literals split by
P
with
true
(
x_{1}=x_{1}
).
Let further
\hat{\Phi}_{P}
result from
\Phi_{P}
by replacing
\varphi^{(r)}
with
\hat{\varphi}^{(r)}
.
Then
\hat{\Phi}_{P}\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\Phi_{P}
.
Indeed, consider a
K
-interpretation
\pi
and two variables
y_{i}\in\{x_{i}\}\cup X_{i}
and
y_{j}\in\{x_{j}\}\cup X_{j}
with
i\in I_{l}
,
j\in I_{l^{\prime}}
,
l\neq l^{\prime}
.
Since
\varphi
is
r
-local,
y_{i}
will be instantiated by an element at distance at most
r
to the instantiation of
x_{i}
, which in turn has distance at most
5^{m-k}r-r
to the element assumed by the representative
x_{i_{l}}
of
I_{l}
.
Hence (the instantiations of)
y_{i}
and
y_{j}
both have distance at most
5^{m-k}r
to their representatives
x_{i_{l}}
and
x_{i_{l^{\prime}}}
, which are at least
4\cdot 5^{m-k}r
apart.
Thus,
y_{i}
and
y_{j}
are at a distance greater than
2\cdot 5^{m-k}r\geq 2
, so
\pi
evaluates all positive literals and equalities involving
y_{i}
and
y_{j}
to
0
, and negative ones to
1
.

By the
Separation Lemma
,
\hat{\varphi}_{P}
is minmax-equivalent to a positive Boolean combination of local formulae
\vartheta_{l}^{(r)}
around
\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\}
for each
l
.
By putting the Boolean combination in DNF and using the fact that
\exists
distributes over
\lor
(cf.
\cref
thmAxioms),
\hat{\Phi}_{P}
is minmax-equivalent to a disjunction over sentences of the form

\displaystyle{\exists}^{(2\cdot 5^{m-k}r)\textrm{-sc}}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle\quad\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{1}\backslash\{i_{1}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}r-r}(x_{i_{1}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\dots

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{k}\backslash\{i_{k}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}r-r}(x_{i_{k}})\ \vphantom{\bigwedge_{l=1}}\smash{\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}}\vartheta_{l}^{(r)}(\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\}).

Notice that each conjunct
\vartheta_{l}^{(r)}(\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\})
is independent of the variables
x_{i}
for
i\notin I_{l}
.
We can thus write such a sentence equivalently as a local sentence (using
\cref
thmGaifmanGeneralizedBasic):

{\exists}^{(2\cdot 5^{m-k}r)\textrm{-sc}}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})\ \bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\tilde{\vartheta}_{l}^{(5^{m-k}r)}(x_{i_{l}}),

with
\displaystyle\tilde{\vartheta}_{l}^{(5^{m-k}r)}(x_{i_{l}})=\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{l}\backslash\{i_{l}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}r-r}(x_{i_{l}})\ \vartheta_{l}^{(r)}(\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\})
.
∎

Remark 27
.

\Cref

thmGaifmanQuantifiers implies that every existential first-order sentence is minmax-equivalent to positive Boolean combination of existential basic local sentences.
This is very similar to a result of Grohe and Wöhrle
[
19
]
for standard Boolean semantics.
In fact, their statement reads exactly the same.

However, their definition of
existential (basic) local sentences
is more strict, as the inner local formula has to be purely existential.
This is not the case in our construction, since
\cref
thmGaifmanGeneralizedBasic adds distance formulae
d(x,y)>2r
within a local formula, which are abbreviations for universal quantifiers.

6.5
Gaifman Normal Form for
\exists^{*}\forall^{*}
-Sentences

It remains to show
(3)
, which is the most challenging step of the proof.
The overall structure of our proof is similar to Gaifman’s original proof
[
10
]
, but each individual step is much more involved due to the stronger notion of equivalence we consider.
Our proof proceeds as follows.

Proof outline.

Starting from a
\exists^{*}\forall^{*}
-sentence of the form

\Psi_{1}=\exists\overline{y}\forall\overline{x}\,\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x}),

we first split
\forall\overline{x}
into quantification over elements close to
\overline{y}
, and elements far from
\overline{y}
.
By the
Separation Lemma
, we can split
\varphi^{(r)}
into a local formula around the close elements, and a local formula around the far elements.
It follows that
\Psi_{1}
is minmax-equivalent to a positive Boolean combination of sentences of the form

\Psi_{2}=\exists\overline{y}\big(\varphi^{(R)}(\overline{y})\quad\land\quad\forall x_{1}\notin B_{r_{1}}(\overline{y})\dots\forall x_{N}\notin B_{r_{N}}(\overline{y})\ \psi^{(r)}(\overline{x})\big),

for a sufficiently large number
N
and radii
r_{1},\dots,r_{N},R
.
Notice that here we use
outside quantifiers
of the form
\forall x{\notin}B_{r}(y)
with the obvious semantics.
The rest of the proof is concerned with turning the outside quantifiers into a local sentence.

As a first (non-trivial) step, we show that we can restrict the outside quantifiers to a single one.
That is, each sentence
\Psi_{2}
is minmax-equivalent to a disjunction over sentences of the form

\Psi_{3}=\exists\overline{w}\big(\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{w})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{r^{\prime}}(\overline{w})\;\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\big).

By similar reasoning as in step
(2)
, we can then rewrite
\exists\overline{u}
as scattered quantification.
That is,
\Psi_{3}
is minmax-equivalent to a positive Boolean combination of sentences of the form

\Psi_{4}={\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{M})\Big(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{M}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(u_{i})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{r^{\prime}}(\overline{u})\;\psi^{(r^{\prime\prime})}(z)\Big).

For sentences of the form
\Psi_{4}
, we can directly specify a minmax-equivalent sentence, that can be written as a local sentence by the previous results, without outside quantifiers.
∎

6.5.1
Close and far

In the first step, we split the quantification over
\overline{x}
into those
\overline{x}
that are close to
\overline{y}
, and those far from
\overline{y}
.

Proposition 28
.

Sentences of the form
\exists\overline{y}\forall\overline{x}\,\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})
are equivalent to positive Boolean combinations of sentences of the form
\exists\overline{y}(\varphi^{(R)}(\overline{y})\land\forall x_{1}{\notin}B_{r_{1}}(\overline{y})\dots\forall x_{N}{\notin}B_{r_{N}}(\overline{y})\ \psi^{(r)}(\overline{x}))
.

Proof sketch.

We first sketch the idea for a tuple
\overline{x}
of length
2
.
In order to split
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})
into a formula local around
\overline{y}
and one that is far from
\overline{y}
, we replace each variable
x_{i}
with two variables
x_{i}^{\textit{in}}
and
x_{i}^{\textit{out}}
, using the equivalence

\forall x\ \vartheta(x,\overline{z})\quad\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\quad\forall x^{\textit{in}\,}{\in}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{z})\ \vartheta(x^{\textit{in}},\overline{z})\;\land\;\forall x^{\textit{out}\,}{\notin}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{z})\ \vartheta(x^{\textit{out}},\overline{z}).

For instance, we would write
\forall x_{1}\forall x_{2}\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1},x_{2})
equivalently as

\displaystyle\forall x_{1}^{\textit{in}}{\in}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\Big(\forall x_{2}^{\textit{in}}{\in}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{in}},x_{2}^{\textit{in}})\;\land\;\forall x_{2}^{\textit{out}}{\notin}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{in}},x_{2}^{\textit{out}})\Big)

\displaystyle{}\land\forall x_{1}^{\textit{out}}{\notin}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\Big(\forall x_{2}^{\textit{in}}{\in}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{out}},x_{2}^{\textit{in}})\;\land\;\forall x_{2}^{\textit{out}}{\notin}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{out}},x_{2}^{\textit{out}})\Big).

We want to apply the
Separation Lemma
to achieve the desired form.
To this end, we choose
r_{0}>2r+1
so that variables locally quantified around
x_{i}^{\textit{out}}
are not close to variables locally quantified around
\overline{y}
in
\varphi^{(r)}
.
This permits us to replace all atoms (and equalities) involving both variables local around
x_{i}^{\textit{out}}
and around
\overline{y}
by
false
(i.e., by
y_{1}\neq y_{1}
).
However, we still cannot apply the Separation Lemma to
\{\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{in}},x_{2}^{\textit{in}}\}
and
\{x_{1}^{\textit{out}},x_{2}^{\textit{out}}\}
, since, e.g.,
x_{1}^{\textit{in}}
and
x_{2}^{\textit{out}}
may be close, and may both appear in a literal that cannot be removed.
We solve this issue by taking different radii for
x_{i}
depending on the order of quantification, so that variables
x_{i}^{\textit{in}}
and
x_{j}^{\textit{out}}
for
i\neq j
are always at distance
\geq r_{0}
.
For instance,

\displaystyle\forall x_{1}^{\textit{in}}{\in}B_{2r_{0}}(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\Big(\forall x_{2}^{\textit{in,in}}{\in}B_{3r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{in}},x_{2}^{\textit{in,in}})\;\land\;\forall x_{2}^{\textit{in,out}}{\notin}B_{3r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{in}},x_{2}^{\textit{in,out}})\Big)

\displaystyle\!\!\!{}\land\forall x_{1}^{\textit{out}}{\notin}B_{2r_{0}}(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\Big(\forall x_{2}^{\textit{out,in}}{\in}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{out}},x_{2}^{\textit{out,in}})\;\land\;\forall x_{2}^{\textit{out,out}}{\notin}B_{r_{0}}(\overline{y})\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{\textit{out}},x_{2}^{\textit{out,out}})\Big).

In this example,
x_{1}^{\textit{in}}
and
x_{2}^{in,out}
are at distance
\geq r_{0}
, and so are
x_{1}^{\textit{out}}
and
x_{2}^{out,in}
.
∎

Proof.

To generalise this idea to
n
variables
\overline{x}
, we annotate variables by sequences
s\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
That is, for each sequence
s
we introduce variables
x_{1}^{s},x_{2}^{s},\dots,x_{n}^{s}
with the intention that
s_{i}=1
indicates that
x_{i}^{s}\in B_{r_{i,s}}(\overline{y})
, whereas
s_{i}=-1
means that
x_{i}^{s}\notin B_{r_{i,s}}(\overline{y})
.
We set the radius as
r_{i,s}=(2^{n-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}s_{j}2^{n-1-j})r_{0}
, with
r_{0}=4r
(so that
r_{0}>2r+1
).
With those notations, we have:

\displaystyle\forall\overline{x}\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})\;\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigwedge_{s\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}Q_{s_{1}}x_{1}^{s}\dots Q_{s_{i}}x_{i}^{s}\dots Q_{s_{n}}x_{n}^{s}\ \varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{s},\dots,x_{n}^{s}),

where
Q_{1}x_{i}=\forall x_{i}{\in}B_{r_{i,s}}(\overline{y})
and
Q_{-1}x_{i}=\forall x_{i}{\notin}B_{r_{i,s}}(\overline{y})
.
It remains to verify that for each sequence
s
, variables
x_{i}^{s}
with
s_{i}=1
and
x_{j}^{s}
with
s_{j}=-1
are at distance
\geq r_{0}
, as intended.
First assume
i<j
.
Then

\displaystyle r_{j,s}=r_{i,s}+\big(s_{i}2^{n-1-i}+\sum_{\mathclap{k=i+1}}^{j-1}s_{k}2^{n-1-k}\big)r_{0}\geq r_{i,s}+\big(2^{n-1-i}-\sum_{\mathclap{k=i+1}}^{j-1}2^{n-1-k}\big)r_{0}\geq r_{i,s}+r_{0},

so
x_{j}^{s}
is outside of a
(r_{i,s}+r_{0})
-ball around
\overline{y}
, and hence at distance
\geq r_{0}
to
x_{i}^{s}
.
If
j<i
, a similar computation gives
r_{i,s}\leq r_{j,s}-r_{0}
, so again
x_{j}^{s}
is at distance
\geq r_{0}
to
x_{i}^{s}
.

Fixing a sequence
s
, we write
\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{in}}=\{x_{i}^{s}\mid s_{i}=1\}
and
\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}}=\{x_{i}^{s}\mid s_{i}=-1\}
.
We can remove those literals in
\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},x_{1}^{s},\dots,x_{n}^{s})
having both a variable close to
\overline{y}
, that is a variable local around
\overline{y}\cup\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{in}}
, and a variable far from
\overline{y}
, i.e., local around
\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}}
.
By the
Separation Lemma
, we obtain an equivalent positive Boolean combination of formulae local around
\overline{y}\cup\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{in}}
and formulae local around
\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}}
.
Writing the Boolean combination in CNF and simplifying (using
\cref
thmAxioms), it follows that
\exists\overline{y}\forall\overline{x}\;\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})
is minmax-equivalent to

\displaystyle\exists\overline{y}\bigwedge_{s}\Big(\big(\forall x_{i}^{s}{\in}B_{r_{i,s}}(\overline{y})\big)_{x_{i}^{s}\in\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{in}}}\;\big(\forall x_{i}^{s}{\notin}B_{r_{i,s}}(\overline{y})\big)_{x_{i}^{s}\in\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}}}\;\bigwedge_{j}(\vartheta_{j,s}^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{in}})\,\lor\,\psi_{j,s}^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}}))\Big)

\displaystyle\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}{}

\displaystyle\exists\overline{y}\bigwedge_{s}\bigwedge_{j}\Big(\tilde{\vartheta}_{j,s}^{(R)}(\overline{y})\,\lor\,\big(\forall x_{i}^{s}{\notin}B_{r_{i,s}}(\overline{y})\big)_{x_{i}^{s}\in\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}}}\psi_{j,s}^{(r)}(\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}})\Big),

with
R=2^{n}r_{0}
so that
\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{in}}
can be locally quantified within
\tilde{\vartheta}_{j}^{(R)}(\overline{y})
.
By putting this in DNF, we see that
\exists\overline{y}\forall\overline{x}\;\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{y},\overline{x})
is minmax-equivalent to a disjunction over sentences of the form

\exists\overline{y}\Big(\vartheta^{(R)}(\overline{y})\,\land\,\big(\forall x_{i}{\notin}B_{r_{i}}(\overline{y})\big)_{1\leq i\leq N}\ \psi^{(r)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\Big),

where
N\leq n\cdot 2^{n}
to account for the quantification over
\overline{x}^{s}_{\text{out}}
for all
2^{n}
choices of
s
,
and
r_{1},\dots,r_{N},R\leq 2^{n}r_{0}
due to the choice of the radii
r_{i,s}
.
∎

6.5.2
Restricting to one outside quantifier

In the next step, we prove that the outside quantifiers
\forall x_{1}{\notin}B_{r_{1}}(\overline{y})\dots\forall x_{N}{\notin}B_{r_{N}}(\overline{y})\ \psi^{(r)}(\overline{x})
can be collapsed into a single outside quantifier.
We achieve this by applying the analogue of
\cref
thmGaifmanQuantifiers for universal sentences (with the help of the
Abstraction Lemma
).
To turn the result into an existential sentence (including a single outside quantifier), the following auxiliary lemma is crucial.

Lemma 29
.

Let
\Theta={\forall}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i})
be a universal basic local sentence. Then
\Theta\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi
for

\displaystyle\Psi\coloneqq\exists v_{1}\dots\exists v_{n-1}\Big(

\displaystyle\forall x_{1}{\in}B_{2r}(\overline{v})\dots\forall x_{n}{\in}B_{2r}(\overline{v})\big(\bigvee_{i<j}d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 2r\lor\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i})\big)

\displaystyle{}\land\forall x{\notin}B_{2r}(\overline{v})\ \varphi^{(r)}(x)\Big).

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a
K
-interpretation over universe
A
.
We first prove
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\leq\pi[\![\Theta]\!]
.
Recall that
\pi[\![\Theta]\!]
is the minimum of
\max_{i}\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]
over all
r
-scattered tuples
\overline{a}
.
Thus, it suffices to show that for all
r
-scattered tuples
\overline{a}
, there exists
i
such that
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\leq\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]
.

Let
\overline{a}
be any
r
-scattered
n
-tuple and let
\overline{b}
be the
(n-1)
-tuple at which the maximum in
\Psi
is reached.
If
a_{i}\notin B_{2r}(\overline{b})
for some
i
, then
\pi[\![\forall x{\notin}B_{2r}(\overline{b})\ \varphi^{(r)}(x)]\!]\leq\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]
.
Otherwise, choosing
\overline{a}
as witness for
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
gives

\pi\big\llbracket\bigvee_{i<j}d(a_{i},a_{j})\leq 2r\lor\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})\big\rrbracket\;=\;\pi\big\llbracket\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})\big\rrbracket\;\leq\;\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i_{0}})]\!],

for an index
i_{0}
at which
\max_{i}\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]
is reached.
In both cases, we have
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\leq\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]
for some
i
, and hence
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\leq\pi[\![\Theta]\!]
.

It remains to prove
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Theta]\!]
.
We define
A^{\prime}=\{c\in A\mid

c\in\overline{a}
for an
r
-scattered tuple
\overline{a}
with
\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(c)]\!]=\max_{i}\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{i})]\!]\}
, so that for every
r
-scattered tuple
\overline{a}
, its “best” entries (the ones maximizing
\varphi
) are contained in
A^{\prime}
.
Now let
\overline{b}
be an
r
-scattered tuple of maximal length with
b_{i}\notin A^{\prime}
for all
i
.
Clearly
|\overline{b}|\leq n-1
by construction of
A^{\prime}
.
If
|\overline{b}|=k<n-1
, we extend
\overline{b}
to length
n-1
by duplicating the last element
b_{k}
(the resulting tuple is no longer
r
-scattered).
Using the resulting tuple
\overline{b}
as witness for
\overline{v}
, we claim that

\pi[\![\forall x{\notin}B_{2r}(\overline{b})\ \varphi^{(r)}(x)]\!]\;\geq\;\pi[\![\Theta]\!].

Indeed, consider any element
d\notin B_{2r}(\overline{b})
.
By maximality of
\overline{b}
, we must have
d\in A^{\prime}
.
Hence
d
is the “best” entry of an
r
-scattered tuple
\overline{a}
, so
\pi[\![\varphi^{(r)}(d)]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Theta]\!]
.

Moreover, observe that for any tuple
\overline{b}
,

\pi[\![\forall x_{1}{\in}B_{2r}(\overline{b})\dots\forall x_{n}{\in}B_{2r}(\overline{b})\big(\bigvee_{i<j}d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 2r\lor\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\varphi^{(r)}(x_{i})\big)]\!]\;\geq\;\pi[\![\Theta]\!].

To see this, let
\overline{c}
be the assignment to
\overline{x}
at which the minimum is reached.
Then
\overline{c}
is either
r
-scattered and thus a potential witness for
\Theta
, or the left-hand side evaluates to the greatest element
1
due to
d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 2r
.
Together, we have shown
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Theta]\!]
.
∎

We further need the following observation to combine several outside quantifiers (originating from different basic local sentences).
Intuitively, we can simply enlarge the radius to a sufficient size
R
, and add local formulae that take care of everything closer than
R
.

Lemma 30
.

Let
\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n},\overline{y}
be variable tuples.

\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\forall z{\notin}B_{r_{i}}(\overline{x}_{i})\,\varphi_{i}^{(r_{i})}(z\mid\overline{y})
is minmax-equivalent to a formula of the following form, for any
R\geq\max_{i}r_{i}
,

\forall z{\notin}B_{R}(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n})\,\tilde{\varphi}^{(\max_{i}r_{i})}(z\mid\overline{y})\;\land\;\vartheta^{(2R)}(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n}\mid\overline{y}).

Proof.

Simple case distinction: each
z\notin B_{r_{i}}(\overline{x}_{i})
either satisfies also
z\notin B_{R}(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n})
,
or we have
z\in B_{R}(\overline{x}_{i})
and
d(z,\overline{x}_{i})>r_{i}
.
Hence the minmax-equivalent formula is

\displaystyle\forall z{\notin}B_{R}(\overline{x}_{1},\dots,\overline{x}_{n})\underbrace{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{i}^{(r_{i})}(z\mid\overline{y})}_{\tilde{\varphi}^{(\max_{i}r_{i})}}\;\land\;\underbrace{\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\forall z{\in}B_{R}(\overline{x}_{i})\big(\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}(d(z,\overline{x}_{j})\leq r_{j}\,\lor\,\varphi_{j}^{(r_{j})}(z\mid\overline{y}))}_{\vartheta^{(2R)},\text{ since $2R\geq R+\max_{i}r_{i}$}}\big).\qed

Proposition 31
.

Sentences of the form

\exists\overline{y}\big(\varphi^{(R)}(\overline{y})\land\forall x_{1}{\notin}B_{r_{1}}(\overline{y})\dots\forall x_{n}{\notin}B_{r_{n}}(\overline{y})\;\psi^{(R)}(\overline{x})\big)

are minmax-equivalent to positive Boolean combinations of sentences of the form

\exists\overline{w}\big(\hat{\varphi}^{(r)}(\overline{w})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{r^{\prime}}(\overline{w})\,\hat{\psi}^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\big).

Proof.

Let
\Phi=\exists\overline{y}\big(\varphi^{(R)}(\overline{y})\land\forall x_{1}{\notin}B_{r_{1}}(\overline{y})\dots\forall x_{n}{\notin}B_{r_{n}}(\overline{y})\ \psi^{(R)}(\overline{x})\big)
over signature
\tau
.
We can replace quantifiers
\forall x_{i}{\notin}B_{r_{i}}(\overline{y})
using distance formulae
d(x_{i},\overline{y})\leq r_{i}
.
Thus,

\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\exists\overline{y}\Big(\varphi^{(R)}(\overline{y})\;\land\;\forall x_{1}\dots\forall x_{n}\big(\psi^{(R)}(\overline{x})\lor\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}d(x_{i},\overline{y})\leq r_{i}\big)\Big).

We would like to apply (the dual of)
\cref
thmGaifmanQuantifiers to show that
\forall x_{1}\dots\forall x_{n}(\dots)
is a local sentence.
However, it is not exactly in the required form due to the additional free variables
\overline{y}
in
d(x_{i},\overline{y})\leq r_{i}
.
To resolve this problem, we apply the
Abstraction Lemma
to abstract subformulae
d(x_{i},\overline{y})\leq r_{i}
by unary atoms
D_{r_{i}}x_{i}
with fresh relation symbols:

\Psi\coloneqq\forall x_{1}\dots\forall x_{n}\big(\psi^{(R)}(\overline{x})\lor\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}D_{r_{i}}x_{i}\big),\qquad\text{over $\tau^{\prime}=\tau\cup\{D_{r_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n\}$}.

We now apply (the dual of)
\cref
thmGaifmanQuantifiers followed by
\cref
thmGaifmanAlternationUnivToExist, implying that
\Psi
is minmax-equivalent to a positive Boolean combination

\Psi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigvee_{k}\bigwedge_{l}\Psi_{kl}
, where each
\Psi_{kl}
is a
\tau^{\prime}
-sentence of the form (for some radius
r
):

\displaystyle\exists v_{1}\dots\exists v_{n-1}\Big(

\displaystyle\forall x_{1}{\in}B_{2r}(\overline{v})\dots\forall x_{n}{\in}B_{2r}(\overline{v})\big(\bigvee_{i<j}d(x_{i},x_{j})\leq 2r\lor\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\psi}^{(r)}(x_{i})\big)

(
\Psi.1
)

\displaystyle{}\land\forall x{\notin}B_{2r}(\overline{v})\ \tilde{\psi}^{(r)}(x)\Big).

(
\Psi.2
)

Recall that each
\tilde{\psi}^{(r)}
may contain the newly introduced symbols
D_{r_{i}}
.
Replacing these symbols by the original distance formula, the
Abstraction Lemma
gives

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\exists\overline{y}\Big(\varphi^{(R)}(\overline{y})\;\land\;\bigvee_{k}\bigwedge_{l}\Psi_{kl}[D_{r_{i}}x_{i}/d(x_{i},\overline{y})\leq r_{i}]\Big).

\displaystyle\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigvee_{k}\exists\overline{y}\bigwedge_{l}\Big(\varphi^{(R)}(\overline{y})\;\land\;\Psi_{kl}[D_{r_{i}}x_{i}/d(x_{i},\overline{y})\leq r_{i}]\Big).

Each
\Psi_{kl}
existentially quantifies over a tuple
\overline{v}
of variables.
By pulling all of these quantifiers to the front of
\Phi
, we can combine all
\Psi_{kl}[D_{r_{i}}x_{i}/d(x_{i},\overline{y})\leq r_{i}]
into a conjunction of local formulae (line
(\Psi.1)
), which may include
\overline{y}
as free variable due to the substitution, and a conjunction of formulae of the form
\forall x{\notin}B_{2r}(\overline{v})\ \tilde{\psi}^{(r)}(x\mid\overline{y})
(line
(\Psi.2)
).
By
\cref
thmNotballCombine, we can combine all of the outside quantifiers into a single one (with the help of another local formula).
Hence
\Phi
is minmax-equivalent to a disjunction over sentences of the form

\exists\overline{w}\big(\hat{\varphi}^{(\rho+2\rho^{\prime})}(\overline{w})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{\rho^{\prime}}(\overline{w})\,\hat{\psi}^{(\rho^{\prime\prime})}(z\mid\overline{y})\big).

Here,
\rho,\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime\prime}
are sufficiently large radii,
and
\overline{w}
contains
\overline{y}
and all tuples
\overline{v}
from the
\Psi_{kl}
.
We can further eliminate the occurrences of
\overline{y}
in
\forall z{\notin}B_{\rho^{\prime}}(\overline{w})\,\hat{\psi}^{(\rho^{\prime\prime})}(z\mid\overline{y})
.
Recall that
\overline{y}
may only occur in atoms of the form
d(x,\overline{y})\leq r
for some variable
x
and some radius
r\leq\rho^{\prime\prime}
.
Since all variables locally quantified in
\hat{\psi}^{(\rho^{\prime\prime})}(z\mid\overline{y})
are
\rho^{\prime\prime}
-local around
z
, we can choose
\rho^{\prime}\gg\rho^{\prime\prime}
sufficiently large (cf.
\cref
thmNotballCombine) so that
d(x,\overline{y})\leq r
will never hold.
We can thus replace all atoms
d(x,\overline{y})\leq r
by false (i.e., by
z\neq z
) to eliminate the occurrences of
\overline{y}
.
To close the proof, we remark that
\hat{\psi}^{(\rho^{\prime\prime})}
is in particular
\rho^{\prime}
-local.
∎

6.5.3
Scattered quantification

So far we have shown that it suffices to consider sentences of the form

\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{m}\big(\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{r^{\prime}}(\overline{x})\;\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\big).

The next step is to replace the existential quantifiers by scattered quantification.
The proof is very similar to the one for
\exists^{*}
-sentences in
\cref
thmGaifmanQuantifiers, but we additionally need the following lemma to take care of the outside quantifier.

Lemma 32
.

Let
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}\in\text{Part}(m)
with representatives
i_{l}\coloneqq\min I_{l}
.
Let
\pi
be a
K
-interpretation over universe
A
(for a min-max semiring
K
).
Then

\pi[\![\forall z{\notin}B_{r}(\overline{a})\,\vartheta^{(r)}(z)]\!]=\pi[\![\psi(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})]\!]

for all tuples
\overline{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\subseteq A
in configuration
(P,r)
, where
R=5^{m-k}r
and

\displaystyle\psi(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\forall z{\notin}B_{R}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})\;\vartheta^{(r)}(z)

\displaystyle\land\;\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\forall z{\in}B_{R}(x_{i_{l}})\big(\bigvee_{i\in I_{l}}d(z,x_{i})\leq r\,\lor\,\vartheta^{(r)}(z)\big).

Proof.

We use the following intuition.
Instead of quantifying over all elements that are at least
r
-far from
\overline{a}
, we first quantify over those that are very far (i.e.,
R
-far) from the representatives
a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}}
, and thus far from all elements
\overline{a}
.
It remains to quantify over elements
z
that are
R
-close to some representative, say
a_{i_{l}}
, but at least
r
-far from all elements
\overline{a}
.
The crucial insight: it suffices to check that
z
is
r
-far from all elements in the class of
a_{i_{l}}
, as elements in other classes are sufficiently far away from
a_{i_{l}}
and hence also from
z
.

Formally, let
\pi
,
\overline{a}
,
P
,
R
be as above.
We have
\pi[\![\forall z{\notin}B_{r}(\overline{a})\,\vartheta^{(r)}(z)]\!]=\min_{c\notin B_{r}^{\pi}(\overline{a})}\pi[\![\vartheta^{(r)}(c)]\!]

and we prove that
\pi[\![\psi(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})]\!]=\min_{c\notin B_{r}^{\pi}(\overline{a})}\pi[\![\vartheta^{(r)}(c)]\!]
as well.
Notice that

\pi\big\llbracket\bigvee_{i\in I_{l}}d(c,a_{i})\leq r\,\lor\,\vartheta^{(r)}(c)\big\rrbracket=\begin{cases}1,&if $c$ is $r$-close to $\{a_{i}\mid i\in I_{l}\}$,\\
\pi[\![\vartheta^{(r)}(c)]\!],&if $c$ is $r$-far from $\{a_{i}\mid i\in I_{l}\}$.\end{cases}

Hence
\pi[\![\forall z{\in}B_{R}(x_{i_{l}})(\bigvee_{i\in I_{l}}d(c,a_{i})\leq r\,\lor\,\vartheta^{(r)}(c))]\!]

evaluates to
\min_{c\in C_{l}}\pi[\![\vartheta^{(r)}(c)]\!]
, where

\displaystyle C_{l}={}

\displaystyle\{c\in B_{R}^{\pi}(a_{i_{l}}):\text{$c$ is $r$-far from $\{a_{i}\mid i\in I_{l}\}$}\}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathclap{(1)}}{=}{}

\displaystyle\{c\in B_{R}^{\pi}(a_{i_{l}}):\text{$c$ is $r$-far from $\overline{a}$}\}.

To see why
(1)
holds, consider an element
c\in B_{R}^{\pi}(a_{i_{l}})
and an element
a_{j}
with
j\in I_{l^{\prime}}
for a different class
l^{\prime}\neq l
.
Due to the properties of the partition (see
\cref
defConfiguration), we have
d(a_{i_{l}},a_{i_{l}^{\prime}})>4R
and
d(a_{j},a_{i_{l^{\prime}}})\leq R-r
.
Since
d(c,a_{i_{l}})\leq R
, we have
d(c,a_{j})>2R+r
, so
c
is indeed
r
-far from
a_{j}
.

Finally,
\pi[\![\forall z{\notin}B_{R}(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})\;\vartheta^{(r)}(z)]\!]
evaluates to
\min_{c\in D}\pi[\![\vartheta^{(r)}(c)]\!]
, where

\displaystyle D={}

\displaystyle\{c:c\notin B_{R}^{\pi}(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})\}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathclap{(2)}}{=}{}

\displaystyle\{c:c\notin B_{R}^{\pi}(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})\text{ and $c$ is $r$-far from $\overline{a}$}\}.

Here,
(2)
holds by a similar argument: every
a_{i}
with
i\in I_{l}
is close to its representative, so
d(a_{i},a_{i_{l}})\leq R-r
, whereas
d(c,a_{i_{l}})>R
.
Hence
d(c,a_{i})>r
and
c
is indeed
r
-far from
\overline{a}
.
Since
D\cup\bigcup_{l=1}^{k}C_{l}=\{c:\text{$c$ is $r$-far from $\overline{a}$}\}
,
we have
\pi[\![\psi(a_{i_{1}},\dots,a_{i_{k}})]\!]=\min_{c\notin B_{r}^{\pi}(\overline{a})}\pi[\![\vartheta^{(r)}(c)]\!]
.
∎

Corollary 33
.

Every sentence of the form

\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{m}\big(\varphi^{(r)}(\overline{x})\,\land\,\forall z{\notin}B_{r^{\prime}}(\overline{x})\,\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\big)

is minmax-equivalent to a positive Boolean combination of sentences of the form

{\exists}^{R\textrm{-sc}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\allowbreak\big(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\tilde{\varphi}_{i}^{(R)}(\overline{x})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{R^{\prime}}(\overline{x})\;\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\big).

Proof.

We prove this corollary by closely following the proof of
\cref
thmGaifmanQuantifiers and applying the previous lemma to
\forall z{\notin}B_{r^{\prime}}(\overline{x})\,\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)
.
Let
\Phi
be a sentence of the indicated form and assume w.l.o.g. that
r\geq r^{\prime}
.
By applying the
Clustering
and
Separation
Lemmas, we obtain that
\Phi
is minmax-equivalent to a disjunction over sentences of the form

\displaystyle{\exists}^{(2\cdot 5^{m-k}r)\textrm{-sc}}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle\quad\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{1}\backslash\{i_{1}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}r-r}(x_{i_{1}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\dots

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{k}\backslash\{i_{k}\}}{\in}B_{5^{m-k}r-r}(x_{i_{k}})\ \Big(\vphantom{\bigwedge_{l=1}}\underbrace{\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\psi_{l}^{(r)}(\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{r^{\prime}}(\overline{x})\;\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)}_{\eta(\overline{x})}\Big).

Observe that (the interpretation of)
\overline{x}
is guaranteed to be in configuration
(P,r)
for a partition
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{k}\}
with
i_{l}=\min I_{l}
, due to the way the
x_{i}
are quantified.
We set
R=2\cdot 5^{m-k}r
and
R^{\prime}=5^{m-k}r^{\prime}
.
By the previous lemma, we can equivalently replace
\eta(\overline{x})
by

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(x_{i_{1},\dots,x_{i_{k}}})=

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\Big(\psi_{l}^{(r)}(\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\})\;\land\;\forall z{\in}B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{i_{l}})\big(\bigvee_{i\in I_{l}}d(z,x_{i})\leq r^{\prime}\,\lor\,\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\big)\Big)

(
*
)

\displaystyle\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})\;\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z).

which depends only on the representatives.
The first line
(*)
is a conjunction of
(R^{\prime}+r)
-local formulae around
\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\}
, for each
l
(recall that
r\geq r^{\prime}
).
In particular, the
l
-th conjunct is independent of all
x_{i}
with
i\notin I_{l}
, which allows us to pull the quantifiers inside.
It follows that
\Phi
is minmax-equivalent to a disjunction of sentences of the desired form:

{\exists}^{R\textrm{-sc}}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})\Big(\bigwedge_{l=1}^{k}\tilde{\varphi}^{(R)}(x_{i_{l}})\;\land\;\forall z{\notin}B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{k}})\;\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\Big)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{l}^{(R)}(x_{i_{l}})=\exists(x_{i})_{i\in I_{l}\backslash\{i_{l}\}}{\in}B_{\frac{1}{2}R-r}(x_{i_{l}})\Big(

\displaystyle\psi_{l}^{(r)}(\{x_{i}:i\in I_{l}\})\;\land\;

\displaystyle\forall z{\in}B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{i_{l}})\big(\bigvee_{i\in I_{l}}d(z,x_{i})\leq{r^{\prime}}\,\lor\,\vartheta^{(r^{\prime})}(z)\big)\Big),

which is indeed local around
x_{i_{l}}
with radius
\leq(\frac{1}{2}R-r)+(R^{\prime}+r)\leq R
.
∎

6.5.4
Eliminating the outside quantifier

In the final step, we eliminate the outside quantifier by directly specifying a minmax-equivalent sentence composed of three parts, each of which can be rewritten as local sentence by our previous results.

Proposition 34
.

Every sentence of the form
{\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(u_{1},\dots,u_{M})\big(\varphi_{1}^{(r)}(u_{1})\land\dots\land\varphi_{M}^{(r)}(u_{M})\land\forall x{\notin}B_{R}(\overline{u})\;\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)\big)
is equivalent to a local sentence.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be a sentence of the indicated form. We claim that
\Phi\equiv_{\mathcal{M}}\bigvee_{P}\Psi^{P}
, where the disjunction ranges over all partitions
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{m}\}\in\text{Part}(M)
with representatives
i_{k}=\min{I_{k}}
.
We define
\Psi^{P}\coloneqq(\Psi_{1}^{P}\land\Psi_{2}\land\Psi_{3}^{P})
with the following sentences.

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}^{P}

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\exists}^{2\cdot 5^{M-m}(r+R)\textrm{-sc}}(u_{i_{1}},\dots,u_{i_{m}})

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad}\exists(u_{i})_{i\in I_{1}\setminus\{i_{1}\}}{\in}B_{5^{M-m}(r+R)-(r+R)}(u_{i_{1}})

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad}\dots

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad\quad}\exists(u_{i})_{i\in I_{m}\setminus\{i_{m}\}}{\in}B_{5^{M-m}(r+R)-(r+R)}(u_{i_{m}})

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad\quad\quad}\Big(\bigwedge_{i<j}d(u_{i},u_{j})>2r\;\land\;\bigwedge_{i=1}^{M}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(u_{i})

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad}\;\land\;\forall x{\in}B_{2\cdot 5^{M-m}(r+R)}(\overline{u})\big(d(x,\overline{u})\leq R\,\lor\,\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)\big)\Big).

The first sentence
\Psi_{1}^{P}
first quantifies a tuple
\overline{u}
in configuration
(P,r+R)
and ensures that it is
r
-scattered.
The remaining subformula is similar to
\Psi
, except that only some
x\notin B_{R}(\overline{u})
are considered (those in
B_{2\cdot 5^{M-m}(r+R)}(\overline{u})
).

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\forall}^{R\textrm{-sc}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{M+1})\bigvee_{i=1}^{M+1}\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x_{i}).

We will show that
\Psi_{2}
checks
\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)
for most of the
x\notin B_{R}(\overline{u})
. Finally,

\displaystyle\Psi_{3}^{P}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\forall v_{1}\dots\forall v_{M}\;\bigvee_{l=0}^{m}\;\;\bigvee_{J=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{l}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}}\exists(u_{i_{j}})_{j\in J}\,{\in}B_{3R+5^{M-m}(r+R)}(\overline{v})

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad}\exists(u_{i})_{i\in I_{j_{1}}\setminus\{i_{j_{1}}\}}{\in}B_{5^{M-m}(r+R)-(r+R)}(u_{i_{1}})

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad}\dots

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad\quad}\exists(u_{i})_{i\in I_{j_{l}}\setminus\{i_{j_{l}}\}}{\in}B_{5^{M-m}(r+R)-(r+R)}(u_{i_{l}})

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad\quad\quad}\Big(\big(\bigwedge_{j\in J}\bigwedge_{i\in I_{j}}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(u_{i})\big)\;\land\;\bigwedge_{i<i^{\prime}}d(u_{i},u_{i^{\prime}})>2r

\displaystyle\phantom{\coloneqq\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad}\;\land\;\forall x{\in}B_{2R}(\overline{v})\big(d(x,\overline{u})\leq R\ \lor\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)\big)\Big).

The last sentence is the most involved one.
For every choice of
\overline{v}
, there is a number
l
such that the (partial)
r
-scattered tuple
\overline{u}
mimics the
l
classes with representatives
i_{j_{1}},\dots,i_{j_{l}}
.
That is, there are elements
u_{i_{j_{1}}},\dots,u_{i_{j_{l}}}
, and each
u_{i_{j_{k}}}
has
|I_{j_{k}}|
elements close to it.
However, note that the elements
u_{i_{j_{1}}},\dots,u_{i_{j_{l}}}
are not necessarily far from each other, as the configuration
(P,r+R)
would require.
We further remark that, unless
l=m
, the tuple
\overline{u}
is smaller than
M
and hence the notation
u_{i}
is only defined for some
1\leq i\leq M
; the idea is that
\overline{u}
shares indices with an
M
-tuple in configuration
(P,r+R)
.
We kindly ask the reader to bear with this slightly unusual indexing, as it will simplify the notation in the remaining proof.

\Psi_{3}^{P}
then checks
\varphi^{(r)}(u_{i})
for all defined entries of
\overline{u}
, and
\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)
for all
x\notin B_{R}(\overline{u})
with
x\in B_{2R}(\overline{v})
.

We first observe that
\Psi_{1}^{P}
,
\Psi_{2}
and
\Psi_{3}^{P}
can all be written as local sentences (notice that
\Psi_{2}
may not be a basic local sentence yet, as we may have
R\neq r^{\prime}
).
Indeed, all sentences use only one type of quantifiers followed by a local formula (of sufficiently large radius).
We can thus apply
\cref
thmGaifmanQuantifiers or its dual version (scattered quantifiers can first be replaced by regular quantifiers and local distance formulae).

It remains to prove equivalence.
In the following, fix a
K
-interpretation
\pi
.
We first prove that there is a partition
P
with
\pi[\![\Psi^{P}]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
.
Let
\overline{d}
be an
M
-tuple at which the maximum in the evaluation of
\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
is reached.
Then
\overline{d}
is
r
-scattered (if no
r
-scattered tuple exists, then
\pi[\![\Phi]\!]=0
and the inequality is trivial).
By the
Clustering Lemma
, there is a partition
P=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{m}\}\in\text{Part}(M)
such that
\overline{d}
is in configuration
(P,r+R)
.

Then
\pi[\![\Psi_{1}^{P}]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
by using
\overline{d}
as witness for
\overline{u}
in
\Psi_{1}^{P}
.
Indeed, the main difference is that the evaluation of
\Phi
includes the minimum of
\pi[\![\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(a)]\!]
over all
a\notin B_{R}(\overline{d})
, whereas the evaluation of
\Psi_{1}^{P}
effectively only considers
a\notin B_{R}(\overline{d})
with
a\in B_{2\cdot 5^{M-m}(r+R)}(\overline{d})
.

To see
\pi[\![\Psi_{2}]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
, let
\overline{b}
be an
(M+1)
-tuple at which the minimum is reached.
Since
\overline{b}
is
R
-scattered, there must be at least one entry
b_{i}
with
b_{i}\notin B_{R}(\overline{d})
.
Hence
\pi[\![\Psi_{2}]\!]\geq\pi[\![\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(b_{i})]\!]\geq\pi[\![\forall x{\notin}B_{R}(\overline{d})\;\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)]\!]
.

For
\Psi_{3}^{P}
, let
\overline{v}
be an
M
-tuple at which the minimum is reached.
Recall that
\Phi
reaches its maximum at
\overline{d}
.
Let
I=\{i\mid
there is
k
with
i\in I_{i_{k}}
and
d_{i_{k}}\in B_{3R+5^{M-m}(r+R)}(\overline{v})\}
be the set of indices whose representative element is close to
\overline{v}
.
Define
\overline{d}^{\prime}
as the subtuple of
\overline{d}
consisting of those entries
d_{i}^{\prime}=d_{i}
with
i\in I
(the other entries are undefined) and use
\overline{d}^{\prime}
as witness for
\overline{u}
in
\Psi_{3}^{P}
.
Clearly
\pi[\![d(d^{\prime}_{i},d^{\prime}_{i^{\prime}})>2r]\!]=1
since
\overline{d}
is
r
-scattered,
and further
\pi[\![\bigwedge_{j\in J}\bigwedge_{i\in I_{j}}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(d^{\prime}_{i})]\!]\geq\pi[\![\bigwedge_{i=1}^{M}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(d_{i})]\!]
.
For the remaining formula involving
\psi^{(r^{\prime})}
, the argument is similar to
\Psi_{1}^{P}
.
Here we effectively take the minimum of
\pi[\![\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(a)]\!]
for those
a\in B_{2R}(\overline{v})
that are
R
-far from
\overline{d}^{\prime}
.
We claim that all such elements
a
are also
R
-far from the full tuple
\overline{d}
.
To see this, consider an entry
d_{i}
with
i\notin I
and let
k
be such that
i\in I_{k}
.
We have the following distances:

\displaystyle d(a,\overline{v})

\displaystyle\leq 2R,

\displaystyle d(\overline{v},d_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle>3R+5^{M-m}(r+R),

\displaystyle\text{by construction of $\overline{d}^{\prime}$},

\displaystyle d(d_{i_{k}},d_{i})

\displaystyle\leq 5^{M-m}(r+R)-(r+R),

\displaystyle\text{since $\overline{d}$ is in configuration $(P,r+R)$}.

Together, they imply
d(a,d_{i})>R+(r+R)\geq R
, so
a
is indeed
R
-far from
d_{i}
.
Hence in
\Psi_{3}
we take the minimum of
\pi[\![\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(a)]\!]
over (a subset of) elements
a\notin B_{R}(\overline{d})
.
We have shown
\pi[\![\Psi_{3}^{P}]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
, and thus
\pi[\![\Psi^{P}]\!]\geq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
.

Finally, we prove that
\pi[\![\Psi^{P}]\!]\leq\pi[\![\Phi]\!]
, for every partition
P
.
This implies equivalence.
To prove the inequality, we fix a partition
P
and construct an
r
-scattered
M
-tuple
\overline{d}
with

\pi[\![\Psi^{P}]\!]\leq\pi[\![\bigwedge_{i=1}^{M}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(d_{i})\,\land\,\forall x{\notin}B_{R}(\overline{d})\,\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)]\!]\leq\pi[\![\Phi]\!].

We construct
\overline{d}
by taking each entry
d_{i}
either from an
M
-tuple
\overline{a}
, or from a partial
M
-tuple
\overline{c}
.
This is done in such a way that all entries from one partition class
I_{k}
(i.e., all
d_{i}
with
i\in I_{k}
) are taken from the same tuple.

Let
\overline{a}
be an
M
-tuple at which the maximum in
\Psi_{1}^{P}
is reached.
Then
\overline{a}
is
r
-scattered and in configuration
(P,r+R)
(if no such tuple exists, then
\pi[\![\Psi_{1}^{P}]\!]=0
and the inequality is trivial).
Let further
\overline{b}
be a maximal
R
-scattered tuple with
\pi[\![\Psi_{2}]\!]>\pi[\![\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(b_{i})]\!]
for all
i
.
The size of
\overline{b}
is at most
M
(by construction of
\Psi_{2}
) and
\pi[\![\Psi_{2}]\!]\leq\pi[\![\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(b^{\prime})]\!]
for all
b^{\prime}\notin B_{2R}(\overline{b})
(by maximality).
If necessary, we extend
\overline{b}
to an
M
-tuple by adding copies of
b_{1}
(the resulting tuple is no longer
R
-scattered; this will not matter).
Finally, let
\overline{c}
be the partial tuple (together with
l
and
J=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{l}\}
) at which the maximum in
\Psi_{3}^{P}
is reached once we instantiate
\overline{v}
by
\overline{b}
, so

\displaystyle\pi[\![\Psi_{3}^{P}]\!]\leq\pi\big\llbracket\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J\\
i\in I_{j}\end{subarray}}\varphi_{i}^{(r)}(c_{i})\;\land\;\bigwedge_{i<i^{\prime}}d(c_{i},c_{i^{\prime}})>2r\;\land\;\forall x{\in}B_{2R}(\overline{b})\big(d(x,\overline{c})\leq R\,\lor\,\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)\big)\big\rrbracket.

In particular,
\overline{c}
is
r
-scattered (unless
\pi[\![\Psi]\!]_{3}^{P}=0
).
Note that the entry
c_{i}
is defined only for
i\in I_{k}
with
k\in J
.
We remark that
l=0
is possible, so
\overline{c}
may be empty (then
\pi[\![\Psi_{3}^{P}]\!]\leq\pi[\![\forall x{\in}B_{2R}(\overline{b})\,\psi^{(r^{\prime})}(x)]\!]
); this will not affect our arguments.

We now define the desired tuple
\overline{d}
.
Let
\overline{J}
denote the complement
\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus J
.
For each
K\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
with
\overline{J}\subseteq K
, we define an
M
-tuple
\overline{d}^{K}

7
Strengthening Gaifman’s Theorem

In this section, we rephrase our main result in terms of Boolean semantics, which leads to a novel strengthening of Gaifman’s classical theorem.
Interestingly,
\cref
thmGaifman can be regained from the Boolean result by algebraic techniques, and even lifted to the class of lattice semirings (denoted
\mathcal{L}
).
These insights suggest that the merit of our proof, and the reason why it is more complicated than Gaifman’s original proof, is not primarily the more fine-grained notion of equivalence, but rather the construction of a Gaifman normal form without the use of negation.
Indeed, a careful examination of our proof in
\cref
secGaifmanProof reveals that our constructions only use literals that were already part of the original sentence, and hence do not add negations.

Our proof applies in particular to the Boolean semiring, and we can thus formulate this observation for standard Boolean semantics.
The only difference between semiring semantics in the Boolean semiring and Boolean semantics is our addition of ball quantifiers, but these can be expressed by distance formulae.
Moreover, we always assume negation normal form and thus permit the duals of basic local sentences (quantifying over
all
scattered tuples).
In Boolean semantics, we can instead express these by negations of basic local sentences.
Notice that this adds negations to the formula, but the number of negations added in front of each literal is even.
Following the common definition that an occurrence of a relation is
positive
if it is behind an
even
number of negations, and
negative
if it is behind an
odd
number of negations, we can formulate the following strengthening of Gaifman’s classical result.

Corollary 35

(Gaifman normal form without negation)
.

Let
\tau
be a finite relational signature.
In Boolean semantics, every
{\rm FO}(\tau)
-sentence
\psi
has an equivalent local sentence
\psi^{\prime}
such that
every relation symbol occurring only positively (only negatively) in
\psi
also occurs only positively (only negatively) in
\psi^{\prime}
, not counting occurrences within distance formulae.

Notice that
\cref
thmGaifmanNoNegation in particular says that if a relation symbol does not occur at all in
\psi
(i.e., neither positively nor negatively), then it also does not occur in
\psi^{\prime}
.
We make use of this property to prove
\cref

We further remark that
\cref
thmGaifmanNoNegation only applies to sentences (see
\cref

We believe that this result may be of independent interest.
A similar adaptation of Gaifman’s theorem has been considered in
[
19
]
, namely that
existential
sentences are equivalent to
positive
Boolean combinations of
existential
basic local sentences.
We prove a similar result (see
\cref
remarkExistentialGaifman), as we also construct a positive Boolean combination of existential basic local sentences (but we permit distance formulae
d(x,y)>2r
, while
[
19
]
does not).
Moreover, the approximation schemes of
[
7
]
are based on a version of Gaifman’s theorem for sentences positive in a single unary relation (i.e., no negations are added in front of this relation).
Their proof uses a version of Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé games, which is quite different from our syntactical approach.
Since unary relations do not occur in distance formulae,
\cref

Interestingly,

both share our observation that the proof of the respective version of Gaifman’s theorem is surprisingly difficult.

8
Conclusion

Semiring semantics is a refinement of classical Boolean semantics, which provides more detailed information
about a logical statement than just its truth or falsity. This leads to a finer distinction between
formulae: statements that are equivalent in the Boolean sense may have different valuations in
semiring interpretations, depending on the underlying semiring. It is an interesting and non-trivial question,
which logical equivalences and, more generally, which model-theoretic methods, can be carried over from
classical semantics to semiring semantics, and how this depends on the algebraic properties of the underlying
semiring.

Here we have studied this question for locality properties of first-order logic, in particular for Hanf’s locality
theorem and for Gaifman normal forms. Our setting assumes semiring interpretations which are model-defining and track only positive information, since these
are the conditions that provide well-defined and meaningful locality notions. However, from the outset, it has been clear that one cannot
expect to transfer all locality properties of
first-order logic to semiring semantics in arbitrary commutative semirings.
Indeed, semiring semantics evaluates existential and universal quantifiers
by sums and products over all elements of the universe,
which gives an inherent source of non-locality if these operations are not idempotent.

Most positive locality results thus require that the underlying semirings
are fully idempotent. Under this assumption, one can adapt the classical proof
of Hanf’s locality theorem to the semiring setting, relying on a back-and-forth argument that
itself requires fully idempotent semirings.
The question whether there exist Gaifman normal forms in semiring semantics turned out to be
more subtle. Indeed, for formulae with free variables Gaifman normal forms need not
exist once one goes beyond the Boolean semiring. Also for sentences, one can find
examples that do not admit Gaifman normal forms in semirings that are not fully idempotent.
We have presented such an example for the tropical semiring.

Our main result, however, is a positive one and establishes the existence of Gaifman normal forms over the class of
all min-max and lattice semirings.
Intuitively, it relies on the property that in min-max semirings, the value of a quantified statement
\exists x\,\varphi(x)
or
\forall x\,\varphi(x)

coincides with a value of
\varphi(a)
, for some witness
a
.
This needs, for instance, not be the case in lattice semirings, and hence the generalisation to lattice semirings uses a different approach based on separating homomorphisms.
It is still an open question whether, in analogy to Hanf’s theorem, Gaifman normal forms exist over all fully idempotent semirings.
The proof of our main result, which is based on quantifier elimination arguments, turned out to be surprisingly difficult;
we identified the lack of a classical negation operator as the main reason for its complexity.
An interesting consequence of this restriction is a stronger version of Gaifman’s classical theorem in Boolean semantics:
every sentence has a Gaifman normal form which, informally speaking, does not add negations.

For applications such as provenance analysis, min-max semirings are relevant, for instance, for studying access levels and security issues.
A much larger interesting class of semirings with wider applications
are the absorptive ones, including the tropical semiring, in which addition is idempotent, but multiplication in general is not. We have seen that
Gaifman normal forms for such semirings need not exist for all sentences. The question arises whether one can establish
weaker locality properties for absorptive semirings, applicable perhaps to just a relevant fragment of first-order logic.

Appendix A
Counterexample against Distance Formula

In this appendix, we justify the addition of ball quantifiers
Qy{\in}B_{r}(x)
by showing that if we define Gaifman normal forms
via the same distance formulae
d(x,y)\leq r
as in Boolean semantics, then
\cref
thmGaifman does not hold.
For simplicity, we only consider graphs.
In Boolean semantics, we can then define the distance formula inductively:

\displaystyle d(x,y)\leq 0

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;x=y,

\displaystyle d(x,y)\leq r+1

\displaystyle\;\coloneqq\;\exists z((Exz\lor Ezx)\land d(z,y)\leq r).

We use the same formula also in semiring semantics.
For example, for
r=2
we have (after slight simplifications, which are also sound in min-max semirings):

d(x,y)\leq 2\;\equiv\;x=y\,\lor\,(Exy\lor Eyx)\,\lor\,\exists z((Exz\lor Ezx)\land(Ezy\lor Eyz)).

We can then express
d(x,y)>r
as negation, where we use negation normal form in semiring semantics.
For example,

d(x,y)>2\;\equiv\;x\neq y\,\land\,(\neg Exy\land\neg Eyx)\,\land\,\forall z((\neg Exz\land\neg Ezx)\lor(\neg Ezy\land\neg Eyz)).

We then use these distance formulae to define Gaifman normal forms.
In a local formula
\varphi^{(r)}(x)
, all quantifiers
\exists y\,\vartheta(x,y)
are relativised to
\exists y(d(x,y)\leq r\land\vartheta(x,y))
,
and quantifiers
\forall y\,\vartheta(x,y)
become
\forall y(d(x,y)>r\lor\vartheta(x,y))
.
Basic local sentences are defined as in
\cref
defLocalSentence, where scattered quantifiers are expressed using distance formulae:

\displaystyle{\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(y_{1},\dots y_{m})\,\vartheta(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\exists y_{1}\dots\exists y_{m}\big(\textstyle\bigwedge_{i<j}d(y_{i},y_{j})>2r\land\vartheta(\overline{y})\big),

\displaystyle{\forall}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(y_{1},\dots y_{m})\,\vartheta(\overline{y})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\forall y_{1}\dots\forall y_{m}\big(\textstyle\bigvee_{i<j}d(y_{i},y_{j})\leq 2r\lor\vartheta(\overline{y})\big).

Proposition A.1
.

The sentence
\beta\coloneqq\exists x\exists y(Ux\land\neg Exy)
does not have a Gaifman normal form with distance formulae (as defined above) over min-max semirings.

Proof.

We construct families of almost identical
K
-interpretations
\pi_{n}
,
\pi^{\prime}_{n}
over the same increasingly large universe
A_{n}=\{v,w,v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
, and then show that every potential Gaifman normal form behaves differently than
\beta
on
\pi_{n}
or
\pi^{\prime}_{n}
for sufficiently large
n
.

Let
K
be a min-max semiring with at least 3 elements, and let
s,t\in K
with
0<s<t\leq 1
.
For every
n\geq 1
, we define
\pi_{n}
as the complete directed graph on
A_{n}
with semiring values
\pi_{n}(Ux)=t
and
\pi_{n}(Exy)=s
for all
x,y\in A_{n}
(and
\pi_{n}(\neg Ux)=\pi_{n}(\neg Exy)=0
for all
x,y
so that
\pi_{n}
is model-defining).
The second interpretation
\pi^{\prime}_{n}
results from
\pi_{n}
by removing the edge
v\to w
, i.e.,
\pi^{\prime}_{n}(Evw)=0
and
\pi^{\prime}_{n}(\neg Evw)=1
(see
\cref
figDistanceCounterexample).
Then
G(\pi_{n})=G(\pi_{n}^{\prime})
and for the distance formulae, we have for
r\geq 1
and all
a,b\in A
, all
n
and
\pi\in\{\pi_{n},\pi_{n}^{\prime}\}
:

\displaystyle\pi[\![d(a,b)\leq r]\!]=\begin{cases}1,&if $a=b$,\\
s,&if $a\neq b$,\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad\pi[\![d(a,b)>r]\!]=0.

For the counterexample
\beta
, we have
\pi_{n}[\![\beta]\!]=0
and
\pi^{\prime}_{n}[\![\beta]\!]=t
.
Suppose for contradiction that
\beta
is equivalent to a positive Boolean combination
\gamma
of basic local sentences (with distance formulae).
Assuming DNF, it has the form
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\lor\dots\lor\gamma_{k}
where each
\gamma_{i}
is a conjunction of basic local sentences.
For the contradiction, we shall prove that every such sentence with
\pi_{n}[\![\gamma]\!]=0
also satisfies
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\gamma]\!]\leq s<t
for sufficiently large
n
, and can thus not be equivalent to
\beta
.

Recall that basic local sentences are of the form
\eta={\exists}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\bigwedge_{i}\varphi^{(r)}(y_{i})
or
\eta^{\prime}={\forall}^{r\textrm{-sc}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\bigvee_{i}\varphi^{(r)}(y_{i})
.
We classify these sentences as follows.

{bracketenumerate}

r\geq 1
and
m\geq 2
: due to the values of the distance formulae in the scattered quantifiers, we have
\pi_{n}[\![\eta]\!]=\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\eta]\!]=0
and
\pi_{n}[\![\eta^{\prime}]\!],\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\eta^{\prime}]\!]\geq s
.

r=0
: local formulae
\varphi^{(0)}(x)
of radius
0
can only check atoms
Ux
or
Exx
, whereas the only difference between
\pi_{n}
and
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
is the value of the atom
Evw
. Hence both interpretations assign the same values, i.e.,
\pi_{n}[\![\eta]\!]=\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\eta]\!]
and
\pi_{n}[\![\eta^{\prime}]\!]=\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\eta^{\prime}]\!]
.

r\geq 1
and
m=1
(discussed below).

Fix some
n
for the moment.
Since we assume that
\beta\equiv_{K}\gamma_{1}\lor\dots\lor\gamma_{k}
, we have
\pi_{n}[\![\gamma_{i}]\!]=0
for all
i
.
Moreover, there must be some
i
with
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\gamma_{i}]\!]=t
.
As
\gamma_{i}
is a conjunction of basic local sentences, it must contain a basic local sentence
\psi
such that
\pi_{n}[\![\psi]\!]=0
and
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\psi]\!]\geq t
.
This sentence can only be of type
(3)
.

This holds for each
n
.
Since
\gamma
is finite, there must thus be a basic local sentence
\psi
of type
(3)
such that
\pi_{n}[\![\psi]\!]=0
and
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\psi]\!]\geq t
for infinitely many
n
.
We prove that no such sentence exists.

Claim 1
.

Let
\psi
be of type
(3)
, i.e.,
\psi=\exists x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)
or
\psi=\forall x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)
with
r\geq 1
, so
that
\pi_{n}[\![\psi]\!]=0
for infinitely many
n
.
Then there is
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\psi]\!]\leq s
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Intuitively, the only way that
\psi
can evaluate to
0
in
\pi_{n}
, but to a positive value in
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
, is by using the literal
\neg Evw
.
But this requires to quantify
w
local around
v
(or vice versa), which forces the value of
\psi
to be
\leq s
.
Indeed, a universal quantifier would range also over
\neg Evv
and thus evaluate to
0
; an existential quantifier would include the distance formula
d(v,w)\leq r
which evaluates to
s
.

The formal proof of this intuition is rather technical.
We first distil
\varphi^{(r)}(x)
down to a simpler formula still witnessing
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\psi]\!]\leq s
(
\cref
claim2 below), and then use this to prove
\cref
claim1.
We write
\overline{a}=(a,\dots,a)
for a tuple of matching arity containing only the element
a
.

Claim 2
.

Let
a\in\{v,w,v_{1}\}
.
Let
\varphi^{(r)}(x)
be a local formula with
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]=0
for infinitely many
n
.
There is a quantifier-free formula
\varphi^{\prime}(x,\overline{y},\overline{z})
,
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\overline{c}\subseteq A_{n_{0}}
s.t.,

•

at most one of
v,w
occurs in
a,\overline{c}
,

•

\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{\prime}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]=0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

•

if
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{\prime}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]\leq s
then also
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]\leq s
, for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

{claimproof}

[Proof of
\cref
claim2]
We construct
\varphi^{\prime}
,
n_{0}
and
\overline{c}
from
\varphi^{(r)}(x)
by eliminating quantifiers.

1.

First obtain
\varphi_{1}^{(r)}(x,\overline{y})
from
\varphi
by turning all existentially quantified variables into free variables.
That is, we inductively replace all relativised existential quantifiers
\exists y(d(x,y)\leq r\land\vartheta(x,y))
by just
\vartheta(x,y)
, with
y
becoming a free variable.

Then
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]=0
implies
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi_{1}^{(r)}(a,\overline{a})]\!]=0
.
Further, if
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi_{1}(a,\overline{a})]\!]\leq s
, then
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]\leq s
, for all
n
.
To see this, note that a relativised quantifier
\exists y(d(x,y)\leq r\land\vartheta(x,y))
in
\varphi^{(r)}(a)
evaluates to a value
\leq s
due to the distance formula, unless we instantiate
y
by
a
(then
d(a,a)\leq r
evaluates to
1
).
But if we have
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi_{1}(a,\overline{a})]\!]\leq s
, then also this instantiation cannot lead to larger values, and hence a simple induction gives
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]\leq s
as claimed.

2.

We next turn all relativised universal quantifiers into (non-relativised) universal quantifiers.
That is,
\forall z(d(x,z)>r\lor\vartheta(x,\overline{y},z))
becomes
\forall z\,\vartheta(x,\overline{y},z)
.
Note that this does not change the value of the formula in
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
and
\pi_{n}
, as
d(x,z)>r
always evaluates to
0
.

By applying prenex normal form, we obtain a formula of the form
\forall z_{1}\dots\forall z_{m}\,\varphi_{2}(x,\overline{y},\overline{z})
, where
\varphi_{2}
is quantifier free.
As our transformations did not affect the evaluation, we can find an
n_{0}>m+1
so that
\pi_{n_{0}}[\![\forall z_{1}\dots\forall z_{m}\,\varphi_{2}(a,\overline{a},\overline{z})]\!]=0
.
Let further
\overline{c}\subseteq A_{n_{0}}
be a witnessing instantiation for
\overline{z}
such that
\pi_{n_{0}}[\![\varphi_{2}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]=0
.
Since
\varphi_{2}
is quantifier-free, this implies
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi_{2}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]=0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
Notice that we can choose
\overline{c}
so that at most one of
v,w
occurs in
\varphi_{2}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})
.
Indeed,
\pi_{n_{0}}
is completely symmetric (every permutation of
A_{n_{0}}
is an automorphism) and we only fix a single element
a
.
So if
a\neq v
and the witness contains
v
(and analogously for
w
if
a\neq w
), we can replace
v
by an unused element among
v_{1},\dots,v_{n_{0}}
without affecting the value
\pi_{n_{0}}[\![\varphi_{2}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]
.

For the condition in
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
, assume that
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi_{2}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]\leq s
for some
n\geq n_{0}
.
Then also
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi_{1}^{(r)}(a,\overline{a})]\!]=\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\forall z_{1}\dots\forall z_{m}\,\varphi_{2}(a,\overline{a},\overline{z})]\!]\leq s
, and thus
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]\leq s
(step 1).

\claimqedhere

It remains to prove
\cref
claim1.
First consider the case
\psi=\exists x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)
.
Recall that we assume
\pi_{n}[\![\psi]\!]=0
for infinitely many all
n
, hence
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]=0
for all
a\in\{v,w,v_{1}\}
.
Obtain
\varphi^{\prime}(x,\overline{y},\overline{z})
,
n_{0}^{\prime}
and
\overline{c}
by
\cref
claim2.
Then
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{\prime}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]=0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all
a\in\{v,w,v_{1}\}
.
Notice that
\varphi^{\prime}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})
does not contain the literals
Evw
and
\neg Evw
, since at most one of
v,w
can appear.
But then each occurring literal is mapped to the same value by
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
and
\pi_{n}
, so
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{\prime}(a,\overline{a},\overline{c})]\!]=0
for
n\geq n_{0}
as well.
By
\cref
claim2, this implies
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a)]\!]\leq s
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
Finally, notice that each
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
is symmetric with respect to the nodes
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
(every permutation of
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
is an automorphism of
\pi_{n}^{\prime}
), and thus
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(v_{i})]\!]=\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(v_{1})]\!]\leq s
for all
i\leq n
.
Altogether, we have shown
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\exists x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)]\!]\leq s
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Now consider
\psi=\forall x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)
.
Since
\pi_{n}[\![\psi]\!]=0
for infinitely many
n
, there is an element
a_{n}\in A_{n}
for each
n
such that
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(a_{n})]\!]=0
.
However, since
\pi_{n}
is completely symmetric, we can always choose
a_{n}=v
, so
\pi_{n}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(v)]\!]=0
for infinitely many
n
.
By applying
\cref
claim2 as above, we get
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\varphi^{(r)}(v)]\!]\leq s
and thus
\pi_{n}^{\prime}[\![\forall x\,\varphi^{(r)}(x)]\!]\leq s
, for all
n\geq n_{0}
.
∎

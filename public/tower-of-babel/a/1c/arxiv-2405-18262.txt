Abstract
We consider two expansions of Gödel logic 𝖦 {\mathsf{G}} with two versions of paraconsistent negation. The first one is 𝖦 𝗂𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}} — the expansion of 𝖦 {\mathsf{G}} with an involuitive negation ∼ 𝗂 {\sim_{\mathsf{i}}} defined via v ( ∼ 𝗂 ϕ ) = 1 − v ( ϕ ) v({\sim_{\mathsf{i}}}\phi)=1-v(\phi) . The second one is 𝖦 2 ( → , \Yleft ) \mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)} — an expansion with a so-called ‘strong negation’ ¬ \neg . This logic utilises two independent valuations on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] — v 1 v_{1} (support of truth or positive support) and v 2 v_{2} (support of falsity or negative support) that are connected with ¬ \neg . Two valuations in 𝖦 2 ( → , \Yleft ) \mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)} can be combined into one valuation v v on [ 0 , 1 ] ⨝ [0,1]^{\Join} — the twisted product of [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] with itself — with two components v 1 v_{1} and v 2 v_{2} . The two logics are closely connected as ∼ 𝗂 {\sim_{\mathsf{i}}} and ¬ \neg allow for similar definitions of co-implication — ϕ \Yleft χ ≔ ∼ 𝗂 ( ∼ 𝗂 χ → ∼ 𝗂 ϕ ) \phi\Yleft\chi\coloneqq{\sim_{\mathsf{i}}}({\sim_{\mathsf{i}}}\chi\rightarrow{\sim_{\mathsf{i}}}\phi) and ϕ \Yleft χ ≔ ¬ ( ¬ χ → ¬ ϕ ) \phi\Yleft\chi\coloneqq\neg(\neg\chi\rightarrow\neg\phi) — but do not coincide since the set of values of 𝖦 2 ( → , \Yleft ) \mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)} is not ordered linearly. Our main goal is to study different entailment relations in 𝖦 𝗂𝗇𝗏 \mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}} and 𝖦 2 ( → , \Yleft ) \mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)} that are induced by filters on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] and [ 0 , 1 ] ⨝ [0,1]^{\Join} , respectively. In particular, we determine the exact number of such relations in both cases, establish whether any of them coincide with the entailment defined via the order on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] and [ 0 , 1 ] ⨝ [0,1]^{\Join} , and obtain their hierarchy. We also construct reductions of filter-induced entailment relations to the ones defined via the order. Keywords: Gödel logic; paraconsistent logics; entailment relations; filters on lattices.

\mathbf{I}

Remark 3
.

One may wonder how Lemma
4
affects the construction of

in Lemmas

and
6
. Observe that the crucial property of

is that it
preserves strict orders
. In other words, it can only ‘squeeze’ points on

but cannot map two points into one. Thus, it is impossible to construct a suitable

between, e.g.,

but

which contradicts the definition of

.

\Gamma\not\models^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\sharp\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
. If
v_{2}(\chi)>0
, then by Lemma
1
,
v^{*}[\Gamma]=\langle 1,0\rangle
and
v^{*}_{1}(\chi)\neq 1
, as required.

Consider now ‘
\Gamma\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\bullet}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi\Rightarrow\Gamma\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
’ and ‘
\Gamma\models^{\langle y,x\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi\Rightarrow\Gamma\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
’. Let
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
. Then either (1)
v[\Gamma]=\langle x_{0},y_{0}\rangle
and
v_{1}(\chi)=0
with
x_{0}>0
,
y_{0}<1
or (2)
v^{\prime}[\Gamma]=\langle x_{1},y_{1}\rangle
and
v^{\prime}_{2}(\chi)=1
with
x_{1}>0
,
y_{1}<1
for some pairs of
\mathsf{G}^{2}
-valuations
v
and
v^{\prime}
. In the first case, it is immediate that
v
witnesses
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\bullet}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
. Moreover,
v
witnesses
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle y,x\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
since
\langle y,x\rangle^{\uparrow}\cap(\{\langle 0,y^{\prime}\rangle\mid y^{\prime}\in[0,1]\}\cup\{\langle x^{\prime},1\rangle\mid x^{\prime}\in[0,1]\})=\varnothing
. The second case can be tackled in the same way (we just need to use Lemma
1
when dealing with
\Gamma\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\bullet}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
).

Let us now deal with ‘
\Gamma\models^{\langle x^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi\Rightarrow\Gamma\models^{\langle y,x\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
’. We assume that
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle y,x\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
. This means that either (1)
v_{1}[\Gamma]\geq y
but
v_{1}(\chi)=y^{\prime}<y
or (2)
v_{2}[\Gamma]\leq x
but
v_{2}(\chi)=x^{\prime}>x
. Thus,
v_{1}
witnesses
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle y^{\prime\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
(but by Lemma
9
.3 this is equivalent to
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle x^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
) or
v_{2}
witnesses
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},x^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
(by Lemma
3
, this is equivalent to
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle(1-x)^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
which by Lemma
9
.3 is equivalent to
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle x^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
). Other implications can be shown in the same manner.

Finally, let us consider the incomparability of entailment relations. Note that
\langle 1,1\rangle^{\uparrow}
is prime and thus the entailment relation induced by it does not satisfy
P.IV
(Lemma
4
). On the other hand, all entailment relations from
\models^{\langle x^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
to
\models^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\sharp\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
are induced by non-prime filters and do satisfy
P.IV
. Conversely,
p\models^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\triangle p
but
p\not\models^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\triangle p
when
\mathcal{D}\in\{\langle x^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle y,x\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle x,x\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle 1,y\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle 1,1\rangle^{\sharp\uparrow}\}
because none of these filters contain
\langle 1,1\rangle
but all of them contain
\langle 1,y^{\prime}\rangle
for some
y^{\prime}>0
and
v(\triangle p)=\langle 1,1\rangle
if
v_{1}(p)=1
and
v_{2}(p)>0
. Likewise,
\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle
is also prime (so,
\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
does not satisfy
P.IV
) but it is
not
validity-stable while every
\mathcal{D}\in\{\langle x^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle y,x\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle x,x\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle 1,y\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle 1,1\rangle^{\sharp\uparrow}\}
is (Theorem
8
). In particular,
p\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\bullet}\rangle}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}q\vee{\sim}q
but
p\not\models^{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}q\vee{\sim}q
.

For the incomparability between
\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
and
\models^{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\in\{\langle x,x\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle 1,y\rangle^{\uparrow},\langle 1,1\rangle^{\sharp\uparrow},\langle 1,0\rangle^{\uparrow}\}
, note that the former is not validity-stable but the latter is and that the latter satisfies
P.III
but the former does not (Lemmas
4
and
7
). Finally, the incomparability between
\models^{\langle 0^{\circ},1^{\circ}\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
and
\models^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
follows since the former satisfies
P.VIII
and does not satisfy
P.III
and the latter does not satisfy
P.VIII
but does satisfy
P.III
.
∎

We finish the section by showing how the entailment relations induced by
point-generated
filters can be reduced to
\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
. The idea of the proof is similar to that of Theorem
5
.

Theorem 13
.

Let
1>x>y>0
,
\Gamma\cup\{\chi\}\subseteq\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
and
p
be fresh. Denote

\displaystyle\triangle^{\top}\Gamma

\displaystyle=\{\triangle^{\top}\phi\mid\phi\in\Gamma\}

\displaystyle\triangle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\{\triangle\phi\mid\phi\in\Gamma\}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{1{\mathsf{D}}}

\displaystyle=\{\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\phi)\mid\phi\in\Gamma\}\cup\{\triangle^{\top}(\neg p\rightarrow p),{\sim}\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\neg p),\triangle p\leftrightarrow\neg\triangle p\}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2{\mathsf{D}}}_{\uparrow}

\displaystyle=\{\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\phi)\mid\phi\in\Gamma\}\cup\{\triangle^{\top}(\neg p\rightarrow p),{\sim}\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\neg p),{\sim}\triangle p\}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2{\mathsf{D}}}_{-}

\displaystyle=\{\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\phi)\mid\phi\in\Gamma\}\cup\{\triangle^{\top}(\neg p\leftrightarrow p),{\sim}\triangle p\}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2{\mathsf{D}}}_{\downarrow}

\displaystyle=\{\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\phi)\mid\phi\in\Gamma\}\cup\{{\sim}\triangle^{\top}(\neg p\rightarrow p),\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\neg p),{\sim}\triangle p\}

Then the following equivalences hold.

\displaystyle\Gamma\models^{\langle 1,0\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\triangle^{\top}\Gamma\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\triangle\Gamma\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\Gamma\models^{\langle 1,y\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\Gamma^{1{\mathsf{D}}}\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\chi)

\displaystyle\Gamma\models^{\langle x,y\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\Gamma^{2{\mathsf{D}}}_{\uparrow}\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\chi)

\displaystyle\Gamma\models^{\langle x,x\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\Gamma^{2{\mathsf{D}}}_{-}\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\chi)

\displaystyle\Gamma\models^{\langle y,x\rangle}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi

\displaystyle\text{ iff }\Gamma^{2{\mathsf{D}}}_{\downarrow}\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\triangle^{\top}(p\rightarrow\chi)

Proof.

We only consider the case of
\models^{\langle 1,y\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
as the remaining ones can be dealt with in the same fashion. Let a
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
-valuation
v
witness
\Gamma\not\models^{\langle 1,y\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
. Then
v_{1}[\Gamma]=1
,
v_{2}[\Gamma]\leq y
but
v_{1}(\chi)<1
or
v_{2}(\chi)>y
. As
p
is fresh, we set
v(p)=\langle 1,y\rangle
and observe that
v(\triangle p)=\langle 1,1\rangle
and
v(\neg p\rightarrow p)=\langle 1,0\rangle
. Moreover, we see that
v(\psi)=\langle 1,0\rangle
for each
\psi\in\Gamma^{1{\mathsf{D}}}
. On the other hand,
v(p\rightarrow\chi)\neq\langle 1,0\rangle

v_{1}(\chi)=x^{\prime}<1

v_{1}(p\rightarrow\chi)=x^{\prime}

v_{2}(\chi)=y^{\prime}>x

v_{2}(p\rightarrow\chi)=y^{\prime}

Conversely, let
v

but
v(\chi)\notin\langle 1,y^{\prime}\rangle^{\uparrow}

v

\Gamma\not\models^{\langle 1,y^{\prime}\rangle}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}\chi
By Lemma

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied entailment relations in
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
induced by filters on
[0,1]
and
[0,1]^{\Join}
. In particular, we obtained that there are only six such entailment relations for
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
(Theorem
4
) none of which coincides with
\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}}
. Moreover, as shown in Theorem
7
, all matrix-based filter-induced entailment relations are finitary and reducible to
\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}}
and
\models^{1}_{\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}}
. This solves an open problem from
[
8
, §6.3]
. We also proved that there are eleven filter-induced entailment relations in
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
(Theorem
12
). In addition to that, we showed that any prime two-dimensional filter on
[0,1]^{\Join}
contained in a point-generated filter induces
\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
. For
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
, we have established the hierarchies of filter-induced entailment relations (Theorems
6
and
12
and cf. Figures
3
and
7
) and obtained reductions of entailment relations induced by point-generated filters to the order-entailment (Theorems
5
and
13
).

Recall that in the
1
Introduction
, we said that in the context of reasoning about uncertainty, filters can be construed as thresholds above which an agent deems their degree of certainty in a statement acceptable. In
[
5
]
, a family of logics for paraconsistent reasoning about uncertainty that use
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
was constructed and provided with strongly complete axiomatisations. Moreover, it was shown that the proofs in these logics are reducible to the proofs in the Hilbert calculus for
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
with the order-entailment. Thus, Theorem
13
provides us with the means to formalise paraconsistent reasoning in such a framework.

Still, several questions remain open. First, note that several entailment relations are induced by
non-principal
filters (e.g.,
\models^{(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1]}_{\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}}
and
\models^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\sharp\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
). It is easy to see that
finitary
and
matrix-based
entailment w.r.t. such filters can be reduced to the order-entailment. On the other hand, since the filters are non-principal, the reduction of entailment from
arbitrary
sets of formulas to the order-entailment seems to be equivalent to the compactness of entailment relations induced by non-principal filters. A closely connected issue is that of axiomatising different filter-induced entailments in
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
and
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
. In
[
8
]
, one can find axiomatisations of
\models^{1}_{\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}}
and
\models^{\leq}_{\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}}
. To the best of our knowledge, there are no calculi for the remaining entailments in
\mathsf{G}_{\mathsf{inv}}
. Similarly, in
[
5
]
, the order-entailment of
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
is axiomatised. It is reasonable to conjecture that allowing for arbitrary applications of the following two rules —
{\phi}/{\triangle\phi}
and
{\phi}/{\triangle^{\top}\phi}
— will result in the calculi for
\models^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
and
\models^{\langle 1,0\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
, respectively. It is an open problem, however, to establish axiomatisations for other filter-generated entailment relations in
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
.

Second, in
[
40
]
, several paraconsistent expansions of the bi-Intuitionistic logic (jointly titled
{\mathsf{I}}_{i}{\mathsf{C}}_{j}
’s) were considered. Each can be extended to a paraconsitent expansion of the bi-Gödel logic with semantics on
[0,1]^{\Join}
. It is, thus, instructive to explore entailment relations in these logics induced by filters on
[0,1]^{\Join}
.

Third, in
[
5
]
, it is shown that the order-entailment in
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
coincides with the
local
entailment over linear Intuitionistic Kripke frames with two valuations. It seems reasonable to conjecture that the
global
entailment (
\Gamma
globally entails
\chi
if, in every model where
\Gamma
is true in every state,
\chi
is also true in every state) over these frames coincides with
\models^{\langle 1,1\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
. Likewise,
\models^{\langle 1,0\rangle^{\uparrow}}_{\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}}
seems to coincide with the global entailment w.r.t. truth and non-falsity (i.e.,
\Gamma
globally entails
\chi
w.r.t. truth and non-falsity if in every model where
\Gamma
is true and not false in every state,
\chi
is also globally true and not false in every state). It is an open problem to establish whether other filter-induced entailment relations in
\mathsf{G}^{2}_{(\rightarrow,\Yleft)}
can be represented on frames.

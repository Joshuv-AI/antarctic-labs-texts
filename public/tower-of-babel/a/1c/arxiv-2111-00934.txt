Comparing numerical Iitaka dimensions again
Sung Rak Choi, Jinhyung Park

Abstract
. To seek for the useful numerical analogues to the Iitaka dimension, various numerical Iitaka dimensions have been defined from a number of different perspectives. It has been accepted that all the known numerical Iitaka dimensions coincide with each other until the recent discovery of a counterexample constructed by Lesieutre. In this paper, we prove that many of them still coincide with the numerical Iitaka dimension introduced by Boucksom–Demailly–Păun–Peternell. On the other hand, we show that some other numerical Iitaka dimensions introduced by Nakayama and Lehmann can be arbitrarily larger than the rest of numerical Iitaka dimensions. We also study some properties of abundant divisors.



1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we work over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety of dimension
n
, and
D
be a divisor on
X
. By a divisor on
X
, we mean an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. If
D
is effective, then a nonnegative integer
\kappa(D)
, called the
Iitaka dimension
of
D
, can be uniquely defined and it is known that there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
for which

(1.1)

C_{1}m^{\kappa(D)}<h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor)<C_{2}m^{\kappa(D)}

holds for every sufficiently large integer
m>0
. The Iitaka dimension is a
birational invariant
in the sense that if
f\colon Y\to X
is a birational morphism from a smooth projective variety
Y
, then
\kappa(f^{*}D)=\kappa(D)
. However, it is well known that
\kappa(D)
is not a numerical invariant.

There are several numerical analogues to the Iitaka dimension defined for a pseudoeffective divisor
D
.
Boucksom–Demailly–Păun–Peternell
[
BDPP
]
defined the following numerical Iitaka dimension:

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D):=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\langle D^{k}\rangle\neq 0\}

where
\langle-\rangle
denotes the positive intersection product. If
D
is nef, then
\langle D^{k}\rangle
is the usual self-intersection number
[D^{k}]
so that
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
is nothing but Kawamata’s numerical dimension
\nu(D)
in
[
K
]
.
From now on, let
D
be a pseudoeffective divisor, and
A
be a fixed sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
. Nakayama
[
N
]
proposed the following definitions of numerical Iitaka dimension:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\kappa_{\sigma}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\nu}(D)&:=\min\{\dim W\mid\text{$D$ does not numerically dominate $W$}\}.\end{array}

Whenever we consider the situation that
m\to\infty
as in the definition of
\kappa_{\sigma}
, we assume that
m
is an integer.
Below,
\phi\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{W})\to(X,W)
range over all
W
-birational models, which by definition means that
W
is not contained in any
\phi
-exceptional center and
\widetilde{W}
is the strict transform of
W
(see
[
Leh1
, Definition 2.10]
). Using the volumes of divisors, Lehmann
[
Leh1
]
further introduced the following numerical Iitaka dimensions:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{res}}(D)&:=\max\left\{\dim W\left|\vol^{+}_{X|W}(D)>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{Zar}}(D)&:=\max\left\{\dim W\left|\inf\limits_{\phi}\vol_{\widetilde{W}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)|_{\widetilde{W}})>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\vol}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}.\end{array}

We set
\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}(D):=\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
and
\kappa_{\vol,\inf}(D):=\kappa_{\vol}(D)
, and we consider the following variants of
\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
and
\kappa_{\vol}(D)
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\vol,\sup}(D)&:=\displaystyle\max\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}.\end{array}

Lesieutre
[
Les2
]
suggested to allow real values for the numerical Iitaka dimensions. Here we consider several
\mathbb{R}
-valued variants of
\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
and
\kappa_{\vol}(D)
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&:=\displaystyle\sup\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left|\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&:=\displaystyle\sup\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)}{m^{k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&:=\displaystyle\sup\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left|\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&:=\displaystyle\sup\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}.\end{array}

Notice that
\kappa_{\star,\diamond}(D)\leq\kappa_{\star,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)
, where
\star=\sigma
or
\vol
and
\diamond=\inf
or
\sup
. If
\kappa_{\star,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)
is not an integer, then
\kappa_{\star,\diamond}(D)=\lfloor\kappa_{\star,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\rfloor
. However, it is unclear whether
\kappa_{\star,\diamond}(D)=\lfloor\kappa_{\star,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\rfloor
always holds.
Below,
\phi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
range over all birational morphisms with
\widetilde{X}
smooth projective. We further define variants of
\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
and
\kappa_{\vol}(D)
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\kappa_{\vol,\inf,\operatorname{bir}}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&:=\displaystyle\sup\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left|\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf\limits_{\phi}\frac{\vol_{\widetilde{X}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi^{*}A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}\\
\kappa_{\vol,\sup,\operatorname{bir}}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&:=\displaystyle\sup\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left|\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\inf\limits_{\phi}\frac{\vol_{\widetilde{X}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi^{*}A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0\right.\right\}.\end{array}

Finally, we add one more definition of numerical Iitaka dimension. Choi–Hyun–Park–Won
[
CHPW
]
introduced and studied the limiting Okounkov body
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
of
D
with respect to an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
. Since the set of
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
for all the admissible flags
Y_{\bullet}
reflects the numerical properties of
D
, it is natural to consider the following numerical invariant:

\kappa_{\Delta}(D):=\max\{\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)\mid Y_{\bullet}\penalty\ \text{is an admissible flag on $X$}\}.

Note that all of the definitions defined above depend only on the numerical class of
D
and they are birational invariant. All the necessary notions required in the definitions of the numerical Iitaka dimensions are recalled in Section
2
.

Note that when
D
is nef, all of these notions coincide with Kawamata’s numerical dimension
\nu(D)
. From the main result of
[
Leh1
]
, we can extract the following equalities:

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{res}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{Zar}}(D).

We first prove that some more numerical Iitaka dimensions also coincide with
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety, and
D
be a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. Then we have

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{res}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{Zar}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\inf,\operatorname{bir}}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\sup,\operatorname{bir}}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\Delta}(D).

For other numerical Iitaka dimensions, we only have the following inequalities:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}&&\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\nu}(D)\\
&&\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}&&\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}&&\\
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D),&&\end{array}

and the same inequalities hold without
\mathbb{R}
-valued variants (see Proposition
3.1
). In particular, we have

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\kappa_{\vol}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\nu}(D)\\
\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}&&\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}\\
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\sigma}(D).\end{array}

Recently, Lesieutre
[
Les2
]
showed that
\kappa_{\nu}(D)
can be strictly larger than
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
; there exists a pseudoeffective divisor
D
on a smooth projective threefold such that

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=1,\penalty\ \kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\frac{3}{2},\penalty\ \kappa_{\nu}(D)=2.

Note that in this example,
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=\kappa_{\sigma}(D)=\kappa_{\vol}(D)=1
.
In this paper, we prove that
\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
and
\kappa_{\vol}(D)
can be arbitrarily larger than
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
.
This gives an answer to Question 0.2 in the Errata of
[
Leh1
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any given integer
k\geq 1
, there exist a smooth projective variety
X
and a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
such that

\kappa_{\sigma}(D)-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\geq k\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \kappa_{\vol}(D)-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\geq k.

In our example for Theorem
1.2
(Example
4.2
),
\kappa_{\vol}(D)=\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
holds. Furthermore, we have

\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D).

It would be interesting to know whether
\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)
and
\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)
hold in general (see Question
3.4
and Remark
3.5
).

In view of the fundamental inequalities (
1.1
) for Iitaka dimensions, it is natural to ask whether there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}m^{\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)}<h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)<C_{2}m^{\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)}

for every sufficiently large integer
m>0
(see Question
3.7

(1)
).
Similarly, it is also natural to ask whether there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\varepsilon^{n-\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)}<\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)<C_{2}\varepsilon^{n-\kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)}

for every sufficiently small number
\varepsilon>0
(see Question
3.7

(2)
). As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we show that the inequalities hold on the limit of higher birational models.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety of dimension
n
, and
D
be a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. Fix a sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor
A
on
X
.
Then there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\varepsilon^{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}<\inf\limits_{\phi}\vol_{\widetilde{X}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi^{*}A)<C_{2}\varepsilon^{n-\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)}

for every sufficiently small number
\varepsilon>0
,
\phi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
in
\inf
range over all birational morphisms with
\widetilde{X}
smooth projective.

Notice that
\kappa(D)\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
. We say that a divisor
D
is
abundant
if
\kappa(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
holds (cf.
[
BDPP
]
). This notion is weaker than the conditions
\kappa(D)=\kappa_{\sigma}(D)
or
\kappa(D)=\kappa_{\nu}(D)
, which are also often referred to as the abundance in the literature (see e.g.
[
DHP
]
,
[
F
]
,
[
GL
]
,
[
N
]
).
In this paper, we generalize the well-known result of Kawamata for nef and abundant divisors
[
K
, Proposition 2.1]
(see also the Errata of
[
Leh2
]
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety, and
D
be an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
such that
\kappa(D)>0
. Then
D
is abundant in the sense that
\kappa(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
holds if and only if there exist a birational morphism
\mu\colon W\to X
from a smooth projective variety
W
and a surjective morphism
g\colon W\to Z
to a smooth projective variety
Z
with connected fibers such that

P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}P_{\sigma}(g^{*}B)

for some big divisor
B
on
Z
, where
g\colon W\to Z
is a birational model of the Iitaka fibration of
D
.

One may wonder whether
\kappa(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
implies
\kappa(D)=\kappa_{\nu}(D)
.
If
\kappa(D)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
, then Theorem
1.4
shows that
\kappa(D)=\kappa_{\nu}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D))
. At this moment, it is not clear to us whether
\kappa_{\nu}(\mu^{*}D)=\kappa_{\nu}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D))
always holds or not (see Question
3.8
).

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective klt pair such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudoeffective.
The abundance conjecture, which predicts that
K_{X}+\Delta
is abundant, is one of the most important open problems in birational geometry.
It is well known that
\kappa(K_{X}+\Delta)=\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X}+\Delta)
if and only if
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model (see
[
DHP
, Remark 2.6]
,
[
GL
, Theorem 4.3]
; see also
[
F
, Section 3]
).
We refer to Section
5
for the definitions of a klt pair and a good minimal model.
In this paper, we show that the weaker notion of the abundance for
(X,\Delta)
, that is
\kappa(K_{X}+\Delta)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X}+\Delta)
, is enough to guarantee the existence of a good minimal model of
(X,\Delta)
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective klt pair such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Then
K_{X}+\Delta
is abundant in the sense that
\kappa(K_{X}+\Delta)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X}+\Delta)
holds if and only if
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model.

We remark that the
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality on
X
in the main theoerems was assumed only for simplicity.
Such condition can be removed in most of the results in this paper.

2.
Preliminaries

Most of the definitions of the numerical Iitaka dimensions in the introduction are self-explanatory.
Below, we recall the notions that are used in defining the numerical Iitaka dimensions. Throughout the section,
X
is an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety,
V\subseteq X
is a
v
-dimensional irreducible closed subvariety, and
D
is an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.

2.1.
Divisorial Zariski decomposition

We denote by
\ord_{V}(E)
the order of an effective divisor
E
along
V
.
If
D
is big, then
the asymptotic valuation
of
V
at
D
is defined as

\ord_{V}(||D||):=\inf\{\ord_{V}(D^{\prime})\mid D\equiv D^{\prime}\geq 0\}.

If
D
is only pseudoeffective, then
the asymptotic valuation
of
V
at
D
is defined as

\ord_{V}(||D||):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\ord_{V}(||D+\varepsilon A||)

where
A
is an ample divisor on
X
. One can check that this definition is independent of the choice of the ample divisor
A
. Note that
\ord_{V}(||D||)
is a numerical invariant of
D
and the function
\ord_{V}(||\cdot||)\colon\text{Big}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
is continous on the cone of big divisor classes.
In particular, if
D
is big, then
\ord_{V}(||D||)=\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\ord_{V}(||D+\varepsilon A||)
for any ample divisor
A
on
X
.
Suppose that
D
is pseudoeffective. By
[
N
, Corollary III.1.1]
, there are only finitely many prime divisors
E
on
X
such that
\ord_{E}(||D||)>0
. The
negative part
of
D
is defined as
N_{\sigma}(D):=\sum_{E}\ord_{E}(||D||)E
where the summation is taken over all the finitely many prime divisors
E
of
X
such that
\ord_{E}(||D||)>0
. Note that the negative part
N_{\sigma}(D)
is a numerical invariant of
D
. The
positive part
of
D
is defined as
P_{\sigma}(D):=D-N_{\sigma}(D)
. By
[
N
, Proposition III.1.14]
, the positive part
P_{\sigma}(D)
can be characterized as the maximal movable divisor such that
P_{\sigma}(D)\leq D
. The
divisorial Zariski decomposition
of
D
is given by the decomposition

D=P_{\sigma}(D)+N_{\sigma}(D).

For more details, we refer to
[
B
]
,
[
ELMNP1
]
,
[
N
, Chapter III]
.

2.2.
Asymptotic base loci

The
stable base locus
of
D
is defined as

\SB(D):=\bigcap_{D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\geq 0}\operatorname{Supp}(D^{\prime}).

where
D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}
denotes the
\mathbb{R}
-linear equivalence, that is,
D-D^{\prime}
is the
\mathbb{R}
-linear combination of principal divisors.
The
augmented base locus
of
D
is defined as

\mathbf{B}_{+}(D):=\bigcap_{A\text{:ample}}\text{SB}(D-A).

The
restricted base locus
of
D
is defined as

\mathbf{B}_{-}(D):=\bigcup_{A\text{:ample}}\SB(D+A).

It is well known that
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)
and
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
depend only on the numerical class of
D
.
Unlike
\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)
, Lesieutre
[
Les1
]
proved that
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
is not always Zariski closed. In this case, the dimension
\dim\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
is defined as the maximum of the dimensions of the irreducible components of
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
.
The union of codimension one components of
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
coincides with
\operatorname{Supp}(N_{\sigma}(D))
. For more details, we refer to
[
ELMNP1
]
.

2.3.
Restricted volumes

The
restricted volume
of
D
along
V
is defined as

\vol_{X|V}(D):=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X|V,\lfloor mD\rfloor)}{m^{v}/v!}

where
h^{0}(X|V,\lfloor mD\rfloor)
is the dimension of the image of the natural restriction map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mD\rfloor))\to H^{0}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}(\lfloor mD\rfloor|_{V})).

When
V=X
, we simply set

\vol_{X}(D):=\vol_{X|X}(D)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor)}{m^{n}/n!}

and we call it the
volume
of
D
.
If
V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)
, then ‘
\limsup
’ can be replaced by ‘
\lim
’ in the definition, the restricted volume
\vol_{X|V}(D)
depends only on the numerical class of
D
, and it uniquely extends to a continuous function

\vol_{X|V}\colon\text{Big}^{V}(X)\to\mathbb{R}

where
\text{Big}^{V}(X)
is the set of all
\mathbb{R}
-divisor classes
\xi
such that
V
is not properly contained in any irreducible component of
\mathbf{B}_{+}(\xi)
(see
[
ELMNP2
, Corollaries 2.14, 2.15 and Theorem 5.2]
). Furthermore, in this case,
\vol_{X|V}(D)=\vol_{X|V}(P_{\sigma}(D))
holds.
If
\phi\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{V})\to(X,V)
is a
V
-birational morphism, then
\vol_{\widetilde{X}|\widetilde{V}}(\phi^{*}D)=\vol_{X|V}(D)
(see
[
ELMNP2
, Lemma 2.4]
).
The
augmented restricted volume
of
D
along
V
is defined as

\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\vol_{X|V}(D+\varepsilon A),

where
A
is an ample divisor on
X
. It is easy to check that the definition is independent of the choice of the
A
and
\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D)
depends only on the numerical class of
D
. If
V\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)
, then
\vol_{X|V}^{+}(D)=0
by definition. For more details, we refer to
[
CHPW
]
,
[
ELMNP2
]
.

2.4.
Restricted positive intersection product

For two cycle classes
K,K^{\prime}\in N^{k}(Y)
on a normal variety
Y
of dimension
m
, we define
K\geq K^{\prime}
if
K-K^{\prime}
is contained in the closure of the cone generated by effective cycles of dimension
m-k
.
When
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}
are big divisors on
X
with
V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}(L_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, we define the class

\langle L_{1}\cdots L_{k}\rangle_{X|V}\in N^{k}(V)

as the maximum under the ordering
\leq
of
\phi_{*}(N_{1}\cdots N_{K}\cdot\widetilde{V})
, where
\phi\colon(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{V})\to(X,V)
run over the smooth
V
-birational models,
N_{i}
are nef divisors on
\widetilde{X}
such that
E_{i}:=\phi^{*}L_{i}-N_{i}\geq 0
and
\widetilde{V}\not\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(E_{i})
for each
1\leq i\leq n
.
When
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}
are pseudoeffective divisors on
X
with
V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{-}(L_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, we define the class

\langle L_{1}\cdots L_{k}\rangle_{X|V}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\langle(L_{1}+B_{1,m})\cdots(L_{k}+B_{k,m})\rangle_{X|V}\in N^{k}(V)

where
B_{i,m}
are big divisors with
V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}(B_{i,m})
converging to
0
as
m\to\infty
for each
1\leq i\leq k
. This limit is independent of the choice of the
B_{i,m}
.
When
V=X
, we set

\langle L_{1}\cdots L_{k}\rangle:=\langle L_{1}\cdots L_{k}\rangle_{X|X}.

By
[
Leh1
, Proposition 4.13]
, we have

\langle L_{1}\cdots L_{k}\rangle|_{X|V}=\langle P_{\sigma}(L_{1})\cdots P_{\sigma}(L_{k})\rangle|_{X|V}.

If
D
is a big divisor on
X
with
V\not\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{+}(D)
, then
\deg\langle D^{v}\rangle|_{X|V}=\vol_{X|V}(D)
(see
[
ELMNP2
, Theorem 2.13]
).
For more details, we refer to
[
BFJ
]
,
[
Leh1
]
.

2.5.
Okounkov bodies

Let
Y_{\bullet}
be an
admissible flag
on
X
, which by definition is a sequence of irreducible subvarieties

Y_{\bullet}:X=Y_{0}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Y_{n-1}\supseteq Y_{n}=\{x\}

where each
Y_{i}
has codimension
i
in
X
and is smooth at the point
x
. Assume that
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}:=\{D^{\prime}\mid D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\geq 0\}\neq\emptyset
. For any
D^{\prime}\in|D|_{\mathbb{R}}
, we define
\nu_{1}=\nu_{1}(D^{\prime}):=\ord_{Y_{1}}(D^{\prime})
. Then
D^{\prime}-\nu_{1}Y_{1}
is effective and does not contain
Y_{1}
in the support, so we can define
\nu_{2}=\nu_{2}(D^{\prime}):=\ord_{Y_{2}}((D^{\prime}-\nu_{1}Y_{1})|_{Y_{1}})
. For
2\leq i\leq n-1
, we inductively define
\nu_{i+1}=\nu_{i+1}(D^{\prime}):=\ord_{Y_{i+1}}((\cdots((D^{\prime}-\nu_{1}Y_{1})|_{Y_{1}}-\nu_{2}Y_{2})|_{Y_{2}}-\cdots-\nu_{i}Y_{i})|_{Y_{i}})
. By collecting the
\nu_{i}
, we obtain

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime}):=(\nu_{1}(D^{\prime}),\nu_{2}(D^{\prime}),\ldots,\nu_{n}(D^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}.

This defines a valuation-like function

\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}\colon|D|_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}.

The
Okounkov body
of
D
with respect to
Y_{\bullet}
is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined as

\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D):=\text{the closure of the convex hull of $\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(|D|_{\mathbb{R}})$ in $\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}$}.

If
|D|_{\mathbb{R}}=\emptyset
, then we set
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D):=\emptyset
. If
D
is big, then by
[
LM
, Theorem A]
we have

\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))=\frac{1}{n!}\vol_{X}(D)\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for every admissible flag $Y_{\bullet}$ on $X$}.

The
limiting Okounkov body
of
D
with respect to
Y_{\bullet}
is defined as

\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D+\varepsilon A)=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D+\varepsilon A).

It is independent of the choice of the
A
, and
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
when
D
is big.
The limiting Okounkov bodies are numerical in nature;
D,D^{\prime}
are numerically equivalent pseudoeffective divisors if and only if
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D^{\prime})
for every admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
.
It is easy to show that
\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(D))+\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(N_{\sigma}(D))
when
D
is pseudoeffective (cf.
[
CPW2
, Lemma 3.3]
).
For more details, we refer to
[
CHPW
]
,
[
KK
]
,
[
LM
]
.

2.6.
Numerical dominance

We say that
D

numerically dominates

V
if there exists a birational morphism
\mu\colon W\to X
with
\mu^{-1}\mathcal{I}_{V|X}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{W}=\mathcal{O}_{W}(E_{V})
for some effective divisor
E_{V}
on
W
such that for every positive number
b
and every ample divisor
A
on
W
, the divisor
x\mu^{*}D-yE_{V}+A
is pseudoeffective for some
x,y>b
. For more details, we refer to
[
N
, Chapter 5]
.

3.
Properties of numerical Iitaka dimensions

In this section, we study some properties of numerical Iitaka dimensions, and we prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.3
. By
[
N
, Proposition V.2.7 (1)]
,
\kappa_{\sigma}(D)=\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}(D)
depends only on the numerical equivalence class of
D
. The same proof shows that
\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}(D),\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D),\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)
are also numerical invariant of
D
. It is obvious from the definitions that all other numerical Iitaka dimensions considered in this paper are numerical invariants as well. Furthermore, it is easy to check that all the numerical Iitaka dimensions studied in this paper are birational invariant (see e.g.
[
N
, Propositions V.2.7 (4), V.2.22 (4)]
).

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety, and
D
be a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. By definition, we have
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\geq 0
. It is also elementary to see that
\kappa(D)\leq\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)
. In
[
Leh1
]
, Lehmann established that

(3.1)

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{res}}(D)=\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{Zar}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}(D)\text{ and }\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{Zar}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\inf}(D)

(see the proof of
[
Leh1
, Theorem 6.2]
for
(1)=(2)=(3)=(4)
,
(4)\leq(5)
,
(1)\leq(7)
). The inequality
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\nu}(D)
was once claimed to be actually an equality by
[
Leh1
]
,
[
E
]
. However, as was pointed out by Lesieutre
[
Les2
, Remark 4]
, the proof in
[
Leh1
]
,
[
E
]
for this equality contains a serious gap. In fact, the strict inequality
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)<\kappa_{\nu}(D)
can indeed hold (see
[
Les2
, Theorem 3]
). One of our goals in this paper is to clarify which numerical Iitaka dimensions coincide with each other and how other numerical Iitaka dimensions differ from the rest. First, we show the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety, and
D
be a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}&&\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\nu}(D)\\
&&\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}&&\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}&&\\
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D).&&\end{array}

Similarly, we also have

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}&&\kappa_{\vol,\inf}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\vol,\sup}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\nu}(D)\\
&&\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}&&\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\leq$}&&\\
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}(D)&\leq&\kappa_{\sigma,\sup}(D).&&\end{array}

If
D
is nef, then
\kappa_{\nu}(D)=\nu(D)
by
[
N
, Proposition V.2.22 (5)]
. Furthermore, in this case, Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
3.1
imply that all the numerical Iitaka dimensions of
D
considered in this paper are equal to
\nu(D)
.

To prove Proposition
3.1
, we need some lemmas. The following lemma shows that our definition of
\kappa_{\vol}(D)
is the same as the definition in
[
Leh1
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety of dimension
n
, and
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. Fix a sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor
A
on
X
. Then for any fixed real number
k\geq 0
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

There is a constant
C>0
such that
\vol_{X}(D+tA)>Ct^{n-k}
for all
t>0
.

(2)

\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}>0
.

(3)

\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{\vol_{X}(mD+A)}{m^{k}}>0
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: Trivial.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: Let us assume that
(2)
holds. From the following observation

\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{\vol_{X}(mD+A)}{m^{k}}=\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{m^{n}\vol_{X}\left(D+\frac{1}{m}A\right)}{m^{k}}=\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{\vol_{X}\left(D+\frac{1}{m}A\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)^{n-k}}>0,

we obtain
(3)
.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: Finally, let us assume that
(3)
holds. There are constants
C^{\prime},c^{\prime}>0
such that
\vol_{X}(mD+A)>c^{\prime}m^{k}
and
c^{\prime}(m-1)^{n-k}>C^{\prime}m^{n-k}
for any sufficiently large integer
m>0
.
Fix a sufficiently small number
\delta>0
. For any
t
with
0<t<\delta
, there exists an integer
m
such that
1/m\leq t<1/(m-1)
. As
t
is sufficiently small, we may assume that
m
is sufficiently large.
Then we have

\vol_{X}(D+tA)\geq\vol_{X}\left(D+\frac{1}{m}A\right)=\frac{\vol_{X}(mD+A)}{m^{n}}>\frac{c^{\prime}}{m^{n-k}}>\frac{C^{\prime}}{(m-1)^{n-k}}>C^{\prime}t^{n-k}.

Next, if
\delta\leq t<1
, then

\vol_{X}(D+tA)\geq\vol_{X}(D+\delta A)>\vol_{X}(D+\delta A)t^{n-k}.

Fix a sufficiently small number
\varepsilon>0
.
If
t\geq 1
, then

\vol_{X}(D+tA)=t^{n}\vol_{X}\left(\frac{1}{t}D+A\right)>t^{n}\vol_{X}((1-\varepsilon)A)\geq\vol_{X}((1-\varepsilon)A)t^{n-k}.

By taking
C:=\min\{C^{\prime},\vol_{X}(D+\delta A),\vol_{X}((1-\varepsilon)A)\}
, we obtain
(1)
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety,
D
be a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
, and
A
be a sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
. Then we have

\vol_{X}(mD+2A)\geq h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for any integer $m>0$}.

Proof.

Let
n:=\dim X
, and fix an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
. Note that
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(A)
contains a standard simplex
\Delta
of size
1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For each integer
m\geq 1
, let
\Delta_{m}:=\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(mD+A)
. By
[
LM
, Lemma 1.4]
, we have

\#(\Delta_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\geq h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A).

Notice now that

\Delta_{m}+\Delta\supseteq\bigcup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Delta_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left(\{\mathbf{x}\}+\Delta\right).

As the interiors of
\{\mathbf{x}\}+\Delta
are disjoint and
\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\{\mathbf{x}\}+\Delta)=\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta)=1/n!
, we get

n!\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{m}+\Delta)\geq\#(\Delta_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}).

Note that
\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(mD+2A)\supseteq\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(mD+A)+\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(A)\supseteq\Delta_{m}+\Delta
and
\vol_{X}(mD+2A)=n!\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(mD+2A))
. Thus we obtain

\vol_{X}(mD+2A)\geq n!\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta_{m}+\Delta)\geq\#(\Delta_{m}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})\geq h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A).

We finish the proof.
∎

We now prove Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let
n:=\dim X
. In this proof,
\star=\sigma
or
\vol
, and
\diamond=\inf
or
\sup
. By definition, we have

\kappa_{\star,\diamond}(D)\leq\kappa_{\star,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D),\penalty\ \kappa_{\star,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\star,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D),\penalty\ \kappa_{\star,\inf}(D)\leq\kappa_{\star,\sup}(D).

Thus it is sufficient to show the following four inequalities:

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}(D),\penalty\ \kappa_{\sigma,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D),\penalty\ \kappa_{\sigma,\diamond}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\diamond}(D),\penalty\ \kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\nu}(D).

\text{\tiny$\bullet$}\penalty\ \nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}(D)
: This is established in (
3.1
) by
[
Leh1
]
.

\text{\tiny$\bullet$}\penalty\ \kappa_{\sigma,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\diamond}^{\mathbb{R}}(D),\penalty\ \kappa_{\sigma,\diamond}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\diamond}(D)
:
Let
A
be a sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
. By Lemma

\frac{\vol_{X}(mD+2A)}{m^{k}}\geq\frac{h^{0}(X,\lfloor mD\rfloor+A)}{m^{k}}

for any integer

. Then Lemma

\kappa_{\sigma,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\inf}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)

\frac{\vol_{X}\left(D+\frac{2}{m}A\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)^{n-k}}=\frac{m^{n}\vol_{X}\left(D+\frac{2}{m}A\right)}{m^{k}}=\frac{\vol_{X}(mD+2A)}{m^{k}}.

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\vol_{X}(D+\varepsilon\cdot 2A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k}}\geq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\vol_{X}\left(D+\frac{2}{m}A\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)^{n-k}}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{\vol_{X}(mD+2A)}{m^{k}}.

\text{\tiny$\bullet$}\penalty\ \kappa_{\vol,\sup}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\nu}(D)

We closely follow
[
N

Take any nonnegative integer

For any integer

\begin{array}[]{l}0\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{W}^{mb+1}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mxD\rfloor+mA)\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{W}^{mb}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mxD\rfloor+mA)\\

Then we get

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{W}^{mb+1}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(\lfloor mxD\rfloor+mA))\geq h^{0}(X,\lfloor mxD\rfloor+mA)-{n-w+mb\choose n-w}h^{0}(W,(\lfloor mxD\rfloor+mA)|_{W}).

Now, fix a real number

\text{\tiny$\bullet$}\penalty\ \nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\inf,\operatorname{bir}}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}C\varepsilon^{n-k}&<&\varepsilon^{n-k}\langle(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi^{*}A)^{k}\rangle\cdot\phi^{*}A^{n-k}\\

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{X_{m}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi_{m}^{*}A)&\leq&\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{X_{m}}\left(M_{m}+\frac{1}{m}\phi_{m}^{*}G+\varepsilon\phi_{m}^{*}A\right)\\

&=\varepsilon^{n-k}\displaystyle\sum_{i=n-k}^{n}{n\choose i}\varepsilon^{i-n+k}M_{m}^{n-i}(\phi_{m}^{*}A)^{i}\end{array}

This shows that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\vol_{X_{m}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi_{m}^{*}A)}{\varepsilon^{n-k-\delta}}=0

for every

. Hence

.

\text{\tiny$\bullet$}\penalty\ \kappa_{\Delta}(D)\leq\kappa_{\vol,\inf,\operatorname{bir}}^{\mathbb{R}}(D)
:
By definition, there exists an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
on
X
such that
k:=\kappa_{\Delta}(D)=\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)
. It is enough to prove that

\frac{1}{n!}\vol_{\widetilde{X}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi^{*}A)\geq\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))\cdot\varepsilon^{n-k}

for any sufficiently small number
\varepsilon>0
, where

is a fixed sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor and
\phi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
is an arbitrary birational morphism
\phi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
from a smooth projective variety
\widetilde{X}
.
By taking sufficient blow-ups, we obtain a
Y_{\bullet}
-admissible birational morphism
\phi
in the sense of
[
CPW2
, Definition 3.5]
. This simply implies that we can form an admissible flag
\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}
on
\widetilde{X}
by taking proper transforms
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
of each
Y_{i}
of
Y_{\bullet}
so that
\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}
is an induced proper admissible flag in the sense of

. By

, we get

\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\phi^{*}D)=\dim\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=k\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \vol_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\big(\Delta^{\lim}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\phi^{*}D)\big)=\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)).

As
\Delta^{\lim}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\phi^{*}D)=\Delta^{\lim}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D))

\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D)+\varepsilon\phi^{*}A)\supseteq\Delta^{\lim}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi^{*}D))+\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\varepsilon\phi^{*}A)=\Delta^{\lim}_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\phi^{*}D)+\varepsilon\Delta_{\widetilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(\phi^{*}A).

\displaystyle\geq\vol_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D))\cdot\varepsilon^{n-k}

which implies (
3.5
). Hence

.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be smooth projective varieties,
p_{i}\colon X_{1}\times X_{2}\to X_{i}
be the projection to the
i
-th component for
i=1,2
, and
D_{1},D_{2}
be pseudoeffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisors on
X_{1},X_{2}
, respectively. Then we have

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(p_{1}^{*}D_{1}+p_{2}^{*}D_{2})=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D_{1})+\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D_{2}).

Proof.

Let
k_{1}:=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D_{1})
and
k_{2}:=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(D_{2})
. For each
i=1,2
, there is a
k_{i}
-dimensional subvariety
W_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
such that
\vol_{X_{i}|W_{i}}^{+}(D_{i})>0
. Note that

\vol_{X_{1}\times X_{2}|W_{1}\times W_{2}}^{+}(p_{1}^{*}D_{1}+p_{2}^{*}D_{2})=\vol_{X_{1}|W_{1}}^{+}(D_{1})\cdot\vol_{X_{2}|W_{2}}^{+}(D_{2}).

Thus
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(p_{1}^{*}D_{1}+p_{2}^{*}D_{2})\geq k_{1}+k_{2}
.
To derive a contradiction, we now suppose that
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(p_{1}^{*}D_{1}+p_{2}^{*}D_{2})>k_{1}+k_{2}
. Then there is a
(k_{1}+k_{2}+1)
-dimensional subvariety
W\subseteq X_{1}\times X_{2}
such that
\vol_{X_{1}\times X_{2}|W}^{+}(p_{1}^{*}D_{1}+p_{2}^{*}D_{2})>0
. We may assume that
W
is a complete intersection of sufficiently positive very ample divisors (see
[
CHPW
, Proposition 2.22]
). For each
i=1,2
, by
[
Leh1
, Proposition 3.8]
, there are birational maps

\phi_{i,m}\colon X_{i,m}\to X_{i}

centered in
\mathbf{B}_{-}(D_{i})
for every integer
m\geq 1
, nef and big divisors
M_{i,m}
on
X_{i,m}
, an ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor
A_{i}
on
X_{i}
, and an effective divisor
G_{i}
on
X_{i}
such that

M_{i,m}\leq\frac{1}{m}P_{\sigma}(\phi_{i,m}^{*}(\lceil mD_{i}\rceil+A_{i}))\leq M_{i,m}+\frac{1}{m}\phi_{i,m}^{*}G_{i}.

Let

\phi_{m}:=\phi_{1,m}\times\phi_{2,m}\colon X_{1,m}\times X_{2,m}\to X_{1}\times X_{2}

with the projection
p_{i,m}\colon X_{1,m}\times X_{2,m}\to X_{i,m}
for each
i=1,2
, and
W_{m}\subseteq X_{1,m}\times X_{2,m}
be the strict transform of
W\subseteq X_{1}\times X_{2}
under
\phi_{m}
.
Notice that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\vol_{X_{1,m}\times X_{2,m}|W_{m}}\left(p_{1,m}^{*}\left(M_{1,m}+\frac{1}{m}\phi_{1,m}^{*}G_{1}\right)+p_{2,m}^{*}\left(M_{2,m}+\frac{1}{m}\phi_{2,m}^{*}G_{2}\right)\right)\\
\displaystyle\geq\vol_{X_{1}\times X_{2}|W}\left(p_{1}^{*}\left(\frac{\lceil mD_{1}\rceil}{m}+\frac{1}{m}A_{1}\right)+p_{2}^{*}\left(\frac{\lceil mD_{2}\rceil}{m}+\frac{1}{m}A_{2}\right)\right).\\
\end{array}

Since we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{X_{1}\times X_{2}|W}\left(p_{1}^{*}\left(\frac{\lceil mD_{1}\rceil}{m}+\frac{1}{m}A_{1}\right)+p_{2}^{*}\left(\frac{\lceil mD_{2}\rceil}{m}+\frac{1}{m}A_{2}\right)\right)=\vol_{X_{1}\times X_{2}|W}(p_{1}^{*}D_{1}+p_{2}^{*}D_{2})>0,

it follows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{X_{1,m}\times X_{2,m}|W_{m}}(p_{1,m}^{*}M_{1,m}+p_{2,m}^{*}M_{2,m})>0.

On the other hand, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\vol_{X_{1,m}\times X_{2,m}|W_{m}}(p_{1,m}^{*}M_{1,m}+p_{2,m}^{*}M_{2,m})=(p_{1,m}^{*}M_{1,m}+p_{2,m}^{*}M_{2,m})^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1}\cdot W_{m}\\
\penalty\ \penalty\ =\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1}{k_{1}+k_{2}+1\choose j}(p_{1,m}^{*}M_{1,m})^{j}\cdot(p_{2,m}^{*}M_{2,m})^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1-j}\cdot W_{m}.\end{array}

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}M_{1,m}^{k_{1}+1}=0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}M_{2,m}^{k_{2}+1}=0
.
For each
0\leq j\leq k_{1}+k_{2}+1
, either
j\geq k_{1}+1
or
k_{1}+k_{2}+1-j\geq k_{2}+1
.
Thus

\lim_{m\to\infty}(p_{1,m}^{*}M_{1,m})^{j}\cdot(p_{2,m}^{*}M_{2,m})^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1-j}\cdot W_{m}=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for every $0\leq j\leq k_{1}+k_{2}+1$},

which implies the following contradiction:

\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{X_{1,m}\times X_{2,m}|W_{m}}(p_{1,m}^{*}M_{1,m}+p_{2,m}^{*}M_{2,m})=0.

Therefore,
\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(p_{1}^{*}D_{1}+p_{2}^{*}D_{2})=k_{1}+k_{2}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

The ‘if’ direction is clear, so we only have to show the ‘only if’ direction.
By replacing
X
by a sufficiently higher birational model of

, which is the Iitaka fibration of

By
[

(see also
[

\mu\colon W\to X
from a smooth projective variety

such that

is a general fiber of the surjective morphism

Let
F
be a general fiber of

Choose an admissible flag

.
Let

Take a sufficiently positive ample
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor

By

\vol_{X|C}(D+\varepsilon A)=\vol_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D+\varepsilon A)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0}).

By taking

(5.1)

\vol_{X|C}^{+}(D)=\vol_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta^{\lim}_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)_{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n-1}=0})=0.

Now, by
[
Leh1
, Proposition 3.8]
, for each integer
m\geq 1
, there are a birational morphisms

is a general fiber of

and (by abuse of notation)

and hence, we obtain

(5.2)

\lim_{m\to\infty}P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}D)\cdot C_{m}=0.

Let
D=P+N
be the divisorial Zariski decomposition. Since
\dim\mathbf{B}_{-}(P)\leq n-2
and
F
is a general fiber of
f
, we have

\dim(\mathbf{B}_{-}(P)\cap F)\leq\dim F-2
. Then

C\cap\mathbf{B}_{-}(P)=C\cap(\mathbf{B}_{-}(P)\cap F)=\emptyset

because
C\subseteq F
is general. Note that
P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}D)=P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)
and
\phi_{m}^{*}(P|_{F})=(\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}}
. Since
0\leq P_{\sigma}((\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}})-P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}}\leq N_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}}
and
\phi_{m}(N_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P))\subseteq\mathbf{B}_{-}(P)
, it follows that

P_{\sigma}((\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}})\cdot C_{m}=P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}}\cdot C_{m}=P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)\cdot C_{m}=P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}D)\cdot C_{m}.

Then by (
5.2
), we obtain

(5.3)

\lim_{m\to\infty}P_{\sigma}((\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}})\cdot C_{m}=0.

Suppose to the contrary that for any birational morphism
\mu\colon W\to X
from a smooth projective variety
W
, we have
\kappa_{\sigma}(P_{\sigma}(\mu^{*}D)|_{F^{\prime}})>0
, where
F^{\prime}
is a general fiber of the surjective morphism
g:=f\circ\mu\colon W\to Z
. Recall that
P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}D)=P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)
. By Proposition
3.10
, we have
\kappa_{\vol,\operatorname{Zar}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}})=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(P_{\sigma}(\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}})>0
. Thus

\lim_{m\to\infty}P_{\sigma}((\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{F_{m}})\cdot C_{m}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\vol_{C_{m}}(P_{\sigma}((\phi_{m}^{*}P)|_{C_{m}}))>0,

which is a contradiction to (
5.3
). We complete the proof.
∎

Before giving the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we recall the definitions of a klt pair and good minimal model. A log pair
(X,\Delta)
is called

if
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and the discrepancy

for every prime divisor
E
over
X
. We say that a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective klt pair

has a

(X^{\prime},\varphi_{*}\Delta)
if there is a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-negative birational map
\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
to another
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety
X^{\prime}

(X^{\prime},\varphi_{*}\Delta)

[
KM
]
.

1.5
.

Assume that
\kappa(K_{X}+\Delta)=\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq 0
.
Let
g\colon W\to Z
be a birational model of the Iitaka fibration of
K_{X}+\Delta
such that
W,Z
are smooth projective varieties and there is a birational morphism
\mu\colon W\to X
.
We write

K_{W}+\Gamma=\mu^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+\sum a_{i}E_{i},

where
\Gamma

\mu
-exceptional divisor, and
a_{i}>0
for every
i
. We may assume that
\Gamma
has a simple normal crossing support and thus
(W,\Gamma)
is a klt pair. Note that

. We claim that

(5.4)

\nu_{\operatorname{BDPP}}(K_{F}+\Gamma|_{F})=0,

where
F
is a general fiber of
g
.
Granting the claim, we get
\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{F}+\Gamma|_{F})=0
by Proposition
3.10
. Then
[

(see also
[

) implies that
(F,\Gamma|_{F})

[

,
(W,\Gamma)

.

We now prove the claim (
5.4
).

on
Z

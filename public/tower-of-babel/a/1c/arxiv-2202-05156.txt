A simple sum for simplices
Christian Aebi and Grant Cairns

Abstract
. We give a vector identity for n + 2 n+2 points in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . It follows as a corollary that when n n is odd the sum of the signed volumes of the n n -simplices is zero, and when n n is even, the alternating sum of the signed volumes is zero.



1.
Introduction

Consider a planar quadrilateral with vertices having position vectors
\textbf{{A}},\textbf{{B}},\textbf{{C}},\textbf{{D}}
, in cyclic order, as in the diagram.

Figure 1.
Illustration of
K_{ABC}
of the
ABCD
quadrilateral

Let
K_{ABC}
denote the area of the triangle
ABC
, and use similar notation for the other triangles. The identity

(1)

K_{BCD}\,\textbf{{A}}-K_{ACD}\,\textbf{{B}}+K_{ABD}\,\textbf{{C}}-K_{ABC}\,\textbf{{D}}=0

was given in
[
1
]
where it was proved as a consequence of the Jacobi vector triple product identity in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

One has
2K_{ABC}=\det[\textbf{{B}}-\textbf{{A}},\textbf{{C}}-\textbf{{A}}]
, and similarly for the other triangles, so (
1
) gives

(2)

\begin{gathered}\det[\textbf{{C}}-\textbf{{B}},\textbf{{D}}-\textbf{{B}}]\,\textbf{{A}}-\det[\textbf{{D}}-\textbf{{C}},\textbf{{A}}-\textbf{{C}}]\,\textbf{{B}}\\
\quad\,+\det[\textbf{{A}}-\textbf{{D}},\textbf{{B}}-\textbf{{D}}]\,\textbf{{C}}-\det[\textbf{{B}}-\textbf{{A}},\textbf{{C}}-\textbf{{A}}]\,\textbf{{D}}=0.\end{gathered}

The object of this paper is to generalize this fact to arbitrary dimension
n
. Consider points
\textbf{{A}}_{0},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{n+1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and think of them as column vectors. For
i=0,\dots,n+1
, consider the

n\times n
matrix

M_{i}=\big[\textbf{{A}}_{i+2}-\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+3}-\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+n+1}-\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\big],

where the indices are computed modulo
n+2
, and let
\Delta_{i}=\det M_{i}
. Here below is the main result of this paper.

Theorem
.

In the above notation, one has

(3)

\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\,(-1)^{i(n+1)}\,\Delta_{i}\,\textbf{{A}}_{i}=0.

Before proving this result, let us consider some special cases. For
n=1
, the above theorem gives

(\textbf{{A}}_{2}-\textbf{{A}}_{1})\,\textbf{{A}}_{0}+(\textbf{{A}}_{0}-\textbf{{A}}_{2})\,\textbf{{A}}_{1}+(\textbf{{A}}_{1}-\textbf{{A}}_{0})\,\textbf{{A}}_{2}=0,

which is obvious.
For
n=2
, the theorem gives us (
2
).
For
n=3
, we have 5 points in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which we may regard as the vertices of a (possibly degenerate) polyhedron, and the theorem gives

\displaystyle\det

\displaystyle[\textbf{{A}}_{2}-\textbf{{A}}_{1},\textbf{{A}}_{3}-\textbf{{A}}_{1},\textbf{{A}}_{4}-\textbf{{A}}_{1}]\,\textbf{{A}}_{0}+\det[\textbf{{A}}_{3}-\textbf{{A}}_{2},\textbf{{A}}_{4}-\textbf{{A}}_{2},\textbf{{A}}_{0}-\textbf{{A}}_{2}]\,\textbf{{A}}_{1}

\displaystyle+\det[\textbf{{A}}_{4}-\textbf{{A}}_{3},\textbf{{A}}_{0}-\textbf{{A}}_{3},\textbf{{A}}_{1}-\textbf{{A}}_{3}]\,\textbf{{A}}_{2}+\det[\textbf{{A}}_{0}-\textbf{{A}}_{4},\textbf{{A}}_{1}-\textbf{{A}}_{4},\textbf{{A}}_{2}-\textbf{{A}}_{4}]\,\textbf{{A}}_{3}

\displaystyle+\det[\textbf{{A}}_{1}-\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{2}-\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{3}-\textbf{{A}}_{0}]\,\textbf{{A}}_{4}=0.

Here for each
i
, the coefficient of
\textbf{{A}}_{i}
is
6
times the signed volume of the tetrahedron defined by the other four vertices.
For example, in Figure
2
, for
\textbf{{A}}_{0}=(0,0,-1),\textbf{{A}}_{1}=(1,0,0),\textbf{{A}}_{2}=(0,1,0),\textbf{{A}}_{3}=(-1,-1,0),\textbf{{A}}_{4}=(0,0,1)
, we obtain

3\textbf{{A}}_{0}-2\textbf{{A}}_{1}-2\textbf{{A}}_{2}-2\textbf{{A}}_{3}+3\textbf{{A}}_{4}=0.

Figure 2.
Applying (
3
) on a triangular bipyramid

In dimension
n
, the convex hull of
n+1
points is a (possibly degenerate)
n
-simplex
. The coefficient
\Delta_{i}
in (
3
) is the signed volume of the
n
-simplex defined by the points other than
\textbf{{A}}_{i}
. In particular,
\Delta_{i}
is unchanged by translation. So translating by a nonzero vector
T
, (
3
) gives

\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\,(-1)^{i(n+1)}\,\Delta_{i}(A_{i}+\textbf{{T}})=0
. Then subtracting (
3
) and taking the coefficient of
T
gives the following scalar identity.

Corollary
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\,(-1)^{i(n+1)}\,\Delta_{i}=0
.

In other words, given
n+2
points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, when
n
is odd the sum of the signed volumes of the
n
-simplices is zero, and when
n
is even, the alternating sum of the signed volumes is zero.

2.
Multilinear algebra

Our proof of the theorem is a simple argument using multilinear algebra. Let us summarize the well known basic ideas we require.
Consider real vector spaces
V
and
W
. Suppose that
k
is a positive integer. Recall that a function
f:V^{k}\to W
is
multilinear
if it is linear in each variable with the other variables held constant; for a gentle introduction, see
[
4
, Chap. 3]
.
A multilinear function
f:V^{k}\to W
is
alternating
if for all elements
\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1}\in V
and all permutations
\sigma
of
\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
, one has

f(\textbf{{A}}_{\sigma(0)},\textbf{{A}}_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{\sigma(k-1)})=\text{Sgn}(\sigma)f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1}),

where
\text{Sgn}(\sigma)
denotes the sign of
\sigma
.
For example, the determinant function
\det:V^{n}\to\mathbb{R},(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{n-1})\mapsto\det[\textbf{{A}}_{0}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{n-1}]
is an alternating multilinear function of the column vectors; see
[
3
, Chap. XIII]
.

There are several known sets of generators for the symmetric group
S_{k}
of permutations of
\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
; a nice survey is given in
[
2
]
. In particular,
S_{k}
is generated by the cycle
(0,1,\dots,k-1)
and the transposition
(0,1)
; see
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
. Thus, in order to show that a multilinear function
f
is alternating it suffices to show that for all
\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1}\in V
,

(a)

f(\textbf{{A}}_{1},\textbf{{A}}_{2},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1},\textbf{{A}}_{0})=(-1)^{k-1}f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1})
,

(b)

f(\textbf{{A}}_{1},\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{2},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1})=-f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\textbf{{A}}_{2},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1})
.

Note that from (b) we have

(b
′
)

f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{2},\textbf{{A}}_{3},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1})=0
.

Conversely, it is easy to see that if (b
′
) holds for all
\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{2},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1}\in V
, then (b) follows.
So in order to show a multilinear function
f
is alternating it suffices to verify conditions (a) and (b
′
). Note that it follows that if
f
is alternating, and if
\textbf{{A}}_{i}=\textbf{{A}}_{j}
for some
i\not=j
, then
f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1})=0
; indeed, one can just permute
\textbf{{A}}_{i},\textbf{{A}}_{j}
to the extreme left, and employ (b
′
).

Finally, a key fact about alternating multilinear functions that we will use below is that if
k>\dim V
, then
f
is identically zero. In the literature, this fact can be quickly deduced once one has constructed the exterior algebra on
V
, but we don’t do that here as we won’t require the exterior product. Instead, one can use the following straight forward proof. First choose a basis for
V
. Using multilinearity, the image of alternating function
f
is determined by its values on the basis elements. But if
\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1}
are basis elements and
k>\dim V
, then by the pigeonhole principle, there must be a repetition of one of the basis elements. It follows that as
f
is alternating,
f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{k-1})=0
.

3.
Proof of the Theorem

Let
n
be an arbitrary positive integer, let
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and consider the function
f:V^{n+2}\to V
defined by

f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{n+1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i(n+1)}\Delta_{i}\,\textbf{{A}}_{i}.

Because
\det
is multilinear, and because for each
i
, the variable
\textbf{{A}}_{i}
does not occur in
\Delta_{i}
, it follows that
f
is multilinear. We will show that
f
is alternating, and hence identically zero. Condition (a) is immediate from the definition of
f
. So it remains to prove (b
′
).
Using the fact that the determinant is an alternating multilinear function of the column vectors and computing the indices modulo
n+2
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}=

\displaystyle\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{i+2}-\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+3}-\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+n+1}-\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{i+2}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+3}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+n+1}\big]

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=2}^{n+1}\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{i+2}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+j-1}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+j+1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+n+1}\big].

Moving the column
\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}
in the summation
j-2
positions to the far left, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}=

\displaystyle\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{i+2}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+3}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+n+1}\big]

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=2}^{n+1}(-1)^{j-2}\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+j-1}\,\big|\,\widehat{A_{i+j}}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+j+1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+n+1}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{j-1}\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{i+1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+j-1}\,\big|\,\widehat{A_{i+j}}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+j+1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{i+n+1}\big],

where in the above, the hat symbol indicates that the term has been omitted. In particular,

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{j-1}\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\widehat{\textbf{{A}}_{j}}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{n+1}\big].

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{j-1}\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{2}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\widehat{\textbf{{A}}_{j+1}}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{n+1}\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{0}\big].

Now suppose
\textbf{{A}}_{1}=\textbf{{A}}_{0}
. Moving
\textbf{{A}}_{0}
to the far left and replacing it by
\textbf{{A}}_{1}
, and then adjusting
j
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n+j}\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\widehat{\textbf{{A}}_{j+1}}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{n+1}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{n+j-1}\det\big[\textbf{{A}}_{1}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\widehat{\textbf{{A}}_{j}}\,\big|\,\dots\,\big|\,\textbf{{A}}_{n+1}\big].

So
\Delta_{0}+(-1)^{n+1}\Delta_{1}=0
. Since
\textbf{{A}}_{1}=\textbf{{A}}_{0}
, we have
\Delta_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 2
. Hence, from the definition of
f
,

f(\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{0},\textbf{{A}}_{2},\dots,\textbf{{A}}_{n+1})=(\Delta_{0}+(-1)^{n+1}\Delta_{1})\textbf{{A}}_{0}=0,

as required.

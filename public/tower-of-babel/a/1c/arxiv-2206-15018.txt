Uniqueness of Low Rank Matrix Completion and Schur Complement
Fei Wang

Abstract
In this paper we study the low rank matrix completion problem using tools from Schur complement. We give a sufficient and necessary condition such that the completed matrix is globally unique with given data. We assume the observed entries of the matrix follow a special ”staircase” structure. Under this assumption, the matrix completion problem is either globally unique or has infinitely many solutions (thus excluding local uniqueness). In fact, the uniqueness of the matrix completion problem totally depends on the rank of the submatrices at the corners of the ”staircase”. The proof of the theorems make extensive use of the Schur complement.



Keywords:

Low-rank matrix completion, matrix recovery, chordal graph

65J22, 90C22, 65K10, 52A41, 90C46

Introduction

Given a matrix
Z
with partially sampled data
z
, the matrix completion problem asks whether the missing entries from
z
can be recovered. Clearly, there are infinitely many ways to fill the missing entries, so in order for the matrix completion problem to be meaningful, we require the rank of recovered matrix to be not greater than a given target rank
r
with the assumption that the rank of the original matrix
Z
is also not greater than
r
.

The low rank matrix completion problem has come up naturally in many different applications, for example, in compressed sensing
[
5
]
, image analysis
[
19
]
, model reduction
[
13
]
, machine learning
[
1
]
, control
[
15
]
and so on. Numerous algorithms has been developed in recent years. Most of the algorithms relax the non-convex rank constraint to a convex constraint and solve a convex optimization problem.

1.1
Model on matrix completion

We now introduce the framework for the matrix completion problem.
Suppose that we are given a low rank
m\times n
real matrix

Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
r=\rank(Z)
where a subset of entries are
sampled
.

The hard nonconvex
low rank matrix completion
problem,

can be reformulated as follows:

(\mbox{\bf LRMC}\,\,)\qquad\qquad\begin{array}[]{cl}\mbox{find}&M\\
\text{s.t.}&{\mathcal{P}}_{\hat{E}}(M)=z\\
&\rank(M)\leq r\end{array}

(1.1)

where
\hat{E}
is the set of
indices containing the known (
sampled
) entries of
Z
,

{\mathcal{P}}_{\hat{E}}(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|\hat{E}|}
is the projection onto
the corresponding entries in
\hat{E}
, and

z={\mathcal{P}}_{\hat{E}}(Z)
is the vector
of known entries formed from
Z
.

1.2
Unique recovery and related work

Suppose we know the target rank and sampled entries

, a natural question to ask is which elements can be uniquely determined and which elements can not? Also under which situation the whole low rank matrix

admits a unique completion? It is known that if the linear mapping

satisfies some restricted isometry properties (RIP)
[
5
,

]
, then

is the unique matrices satisfying
\rank(L)\leq r,{\mathcal{P}}_{\hat{E}}(L)=z
. The RIP provides a sufficient condition for exact recovery and checking if a linear map

satisfies RIP property is NP-hard
[
2
]
. However the necessary condition for a matrix to be uniquely completed is less well-studied. In
[
17
]
, using tools from rigidity theory, a randomized algorithm for testing sufficient and necessary conditions for local completion and for testing sufficient conditions for global completion is proposed. In
[
11
,
12
,
10
]
, the authors use an algebraic combinatorial approach to study the matrix completion problem and a sufficient and necessary criterion is given in
[
11
]
. However, the matrix to be completed is assumed to be “generic” in
[

,
12
,
10

We are mostly interested in exploiting the clique structures in the sampled data. The cliques in the sampled data can be used to do facial reductions for solving the corresponding optimization problems more efficiently

In the following sections, we will provide a sufficient and necessary condition for the uniqueness of completion without using the generic assumption by exploiting the properties of the intersections of bicliques.

2
Matrix completion by clique intersection

We first recall the basic property of Schur complement:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
8
]

Consider the partitioned matrix

M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},

if we assume that
\Range(B)\subseteq\Range(A)
and
\Range(C^{T})\subseteq\Range(A^{T})
, then
M/A=D-CA^{\dagger}B
is well-defined and

\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
-CA^{\dagger}&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I&-A^{\dagger}B\\
0&I\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&M/A\end{bmatrix}

and hence

\rank(M)=\rank(A)+\rank(M/A).

2.1
Two-biclique intersection

As a consequence of Lemma

Theorem 2.2

Consider the partitioned matrix

\rank(M)=r
, and the block submatrices

Then

is unique in LRMC if and only if
\rank(A)=r
.

Proof.

First assume
\rank(A)=r
. Let

be the
generalized Schur complement, e.g.,
[

,
where

\rank(M/A)+\rank(A)=\rank(M)

\rank(M/A)=0

For necessity, assume
\rank(A)<r
, we first assume
\Range(B)\subseteq\Range(A)

\Range(C^{T})\subseteq\Range(A^{T})

is not unique.

Now suppose either

\Range(C^{T})\subsetneq\Range(A^{T})

\Range(C^{T})\subsetneq\Range(A^{T})

Theorem 2.4

Consider the partitioned matrix

\rank(A)=r

Proof.

Suppose

, then it is obvious that

are unique by Theorem

For necessity, without loss we assume
\rank(A)=\bar{r}<r

M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
E&F\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}.

If
\rank(\begin{bmatrix}A\\
E\end{bmatrix})<r
, then by Therem
2.2
,
D
is not unique so

is not unique.

If
\rank(\begin{bmatrix}A\\
E\end{bmatrix})=r
, then we have
\Range(B)\subseteq\Range(A)

M_{3}=C_{2}-C_{1}A_{1}^{\dagger}A_{2}

Also

Therefore
\rank(M_{1})=\rank(\begin{bmatrix}M_{1}&M_{2}\cr M_{3}&M_{4}\end{bmatrix})=r-\bar{r}

M_{4}=M_{3}M_{1}^{\dagger}M_{2}.

(2.3)

Now

\displaystyle\rank(\bar{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rank(A_{1})+\rank(\begin{bmatrix}2M_{1}&M_{2}\cr M_{3}&\frac{1}{2}M_{4}\end{bmatrix})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rank(A_{1})+\rank(2M_{1})+\rank(\frac{1}{2}M_{4}-M_{3}(2M_{1})^{\dagger}M_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rank(A_{1})+\rank(2M_{1})\quad\text{ (due to {(\ref{eq:m14})})}

3.1
Chordal graphs and clique trees

Chordal graph is a special graph with a chordless structure.
An undirected graph is chordal if every
cycle of length greater than three has a chord.

A clique tree of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a tree which has the cliques
of
G
as its vertices. A clique tree
T
has the induced subtree property
if for every
v\in V
, the cliques that contain
v
form a subtree (connected
subgraph) of
T
. It has been shown
that chordal graphs are exactly the graphs for which a clique tree with
the induced subtree property exists. (
[
4
, Theorem 2.7]
and
[
7
, Theorem 3]
).

We show the graph
\hat{E}
in Theorem
2.8
has a unique clique tree with the induced subtree property. Therefore it is chordal.

Theorem 3.1
.

Given a graph
G
with edge set
\hat{E}
, if by a permutation there exists a chain of bicliques
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{l}
with the corresponding edge sets
E_{1},\cdots,E_{l}
. Assume
E_{1},\cdots,E_{l}
and
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{l}
satisfy the assumptions in Theorem
2.8
.
Then the graph
\hat{E}
is chordal and it has a unique clique tree with the induced subtree property.

Proof.

Consider the set of bicliques
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{l}
, each clique
\alpha_{i}
is a node and connect
\alpha_{i}
and
\alpha_{i+1}
by an edge, then it forms a clique tree. For any vertex
v
in
\hat{E}
, it either belongs to
\alpha_{i}
or belongs to
\alpha_{i}\cap\alpha_{i}+1
for some

, therefore the set of cliques containing

is connected. Hence it has the induced subtree property. Therefore by Theorem 3 in
[

]
it is a chordal graph. Now consider a different clique tree, then there exists

which is not connected

, but there exists

, therefore the induced subtree property is not satisfied.
∎

After we the clique tree is found, checking if the intersection of two neighbouring clique has rank

Consider the partitioned matrix

M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B^{T}&C\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},

where
\rank(M)=r
and
M\succeq 0
in which
A
,
C
are square, and the blocks
A,B
are fixed.
Then there exists a unique positive semidefinite matrix
M
if and only if
\rank(A)=r
.

Proof.

First assume
\rank(A)=r
. Let
M/A=C-B^{T}A^{\dagger}B
be the
generalized Schur complement,
where
A^{\dagger}
denotes the
Moore-Penrose generalized inverse. The existence of
M\succeq 0
ensures
\Range(B)\subseteq\Range(A)
and
A\succeq 0
, therefore
M/A
is well-defined. We then
have
\rank(M/A)+\rank(A)=\rank(M)
. Hence
\rank(M/A)=0
and
C=B^{T}A^{\dagger}B\succeq 0
is unique.

For necessity, assume
\rank(A)<r
, the existence of
M\succeq 0
ensures
\Range(B)\subseteq\Range(A)
and
A\succeq 0
, therefore
M/A
is well-defined and

\rank(M/A)+\rank(A)=\rank(M)

Since
\rank(A)<r
and
\rank(M)=r
, we have
\rank(C-B^{T}A^{\dagger}B)=\rank(M)-\rank(A)=\bar{r}>0
. We can then let
C=B^{T}A^{\dagger}B+E
where
E
is an arbitrary positive semidefinite matrix of rank
\bar{r}
.
Hence by Theorem
4.1

\bar{M}=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
B^{T}&B^{T}A^{\dagger}B+E\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.

Therefore
M
is not unique.
∎

Now ee consider the following question:
let
M
be the partitioned matrix

M=\begin{bmatrix}A&B&D\\
B^{T}&C&E\\
D^{T}&E^{T}&F\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},

M
is symmetric and positive semidefinite and the diagonal elements of
M
are all nonzeros. Suppose
A,B,C,E,F
are fixed and
\rank(M)=r
. Then it is easy to see that
M
can be uniquely completed if
\rank(C)=r
. We now ask the question: is the converse also true? The answer is NO.

We consider the following example

H=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}5&4&-2\\
4&16&-8\\
-2&-8&4\end{array}\right]

Suppose the top right element and bottom left element
-2
are missing, the partially observed entries are the union of two 2 by 2 cliques, i.e,

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}5&4&?\\
4&16&-8\\
?&-8&4\end{array}\right]

We partition
H
as matrix
M
.
The intersection of the two cliques is 16 which is rank 1, and
\rank(H)=2
. Let the missing element
D
be
x
.

We compute
\rank(\begin{bmatrix}5&4\\
4&16\end{bmatrix})=2
. In order for the rank of
H
to be 2, the Schur complement

4-(x,-8)\begin{bmatrix}5&4\\
4&16\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}x\\
-8\end{bmatrix}=-(\frac{x^{2}}{4}+x+1)=-(\frac{1}{2}x+1)^{2}

must be zero. (otherwise the rank of
H
is 3.)

Therefore
x=-2
is the only solution, the missing entry can be uniquely completed.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we show that if the missing entries of a matrix of rank
r
has a ”staircase” pattern, then the uniqueness of the recovery problem given the target rank depends totally on the submatrices at the corner of the ”staircase”. The proof of the main theorem makes extensive use of Schur complement.

Uniform block-diagonal preconditioners for divergence-conforming HDG Methods for the generalized Stokes equations and the linear elasticity equations Thanks: We gratefully acknowledge the partial support of this work by the U.S. National Science Foundation through grant DMS-2012031.
Guosheng Fu, Wenzheng Kuang

Abstract
. We propose a uniform block-diagonal preconditioner for condensed H H (div)-conforming HDG schemes for parameter-dependent saddle point problems, including the generalized Stokes equations and the linear elasticity equations. An optimal preconditioner is obtained for the stiffness matrix on the global velocity/displacement space via the auxiliary space preconditioning (ASP) technique [ 51 ] . A spectrally equivalent approximation to the Schur complement on the element-wise constant pressure space is also constructed, and an explicit computable exact inverse is obtained via the Woodbury matrix identity. Finally, the numerical results verify the robustness of our proposed preconditioner with respect to model parameters and mesh size.

1.
Introduction

Since their first introduction for second order elliptic problems about a decade ago
[
14
]
, hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) schemes have been developed and successfully applied to various partial differential equations (PDEs) in computational fluid dynamics
[
12
,
43
]
, wave propagation
[
15
]
, and continuum mechanics
[
37
,
21
]
. One key advantage of HDG schemes over discontinuous Galerkin (DG) schemes is that they can be statically condensed into a reduced linear system with increased sparsity, resulting in a significant decrease in the matrix size and computing cost
[
38
,
11
]
.

However, the challenge of constructing optimal and robust solvers and preconditioners for condensed HDG schemes has not been fully addressed, and techniques such as multigrid and domain decomposition methods have been mainly explored.
Cockburn et al. introduced a V-cycle geometric multigrid method for HDG schemes for the elliptic equations
[
13
]
, where a continuous element-wise linear function space is used at the second level, and then a standard conforming multigrid method starts from there. A similar idea was employed for the Helmholtz equations
[
10
]
, the shallow water equations
[
6
]
, and extended to an
hp
-multigrid in parallel manner in
[
16
]
. We note that Lu et al. recently reported a homogeneous multigrid for HDG schemes for the elliptic equations where HDG discretization is used in each level
[
32
]
.
Standard
p
-version domain decomposition methods were first analyzed by Schöberl and Lehrenfeld for statically condensed systems of high order HDG schemes for the elliptic equations where each element is treated as a sub-domain
[
47
]
. Schwarz type methods and balancing domain decomposition with constraints (BDDC) algorithms for HDG schemes have been further investigated for the elliptic equations
[
23
,
22
]
, the incompressible Stokes equations
[
2
,
49
]
, the Maxwell equations
[
31
,
24
]
and the hyperbolic equations
[
35
,
36
]
.

In this paper, we focus on a uniform preconditioner for the divergence-conforming HDG schemes for the parameter-dependent saddle point problems which arise when dealing with the mixed finite element formulation of the generalized Stokes equations, the linear elasticity equations, and the Brinkman equations.
H
(div)-conforming HDG discretizations for these equations have been developed in previous works
[
30
,
21
,
19
]
for the pressure-robust and mass-conserving properties but without efficient solvers. A wealth of literature is devoted to solving saddle point problems, and we refer to
[
3
]
for a comprehensive review of methods, including block-diagonal preconditioners, domain decomposition methods, multilevel methods, and so on. We note that recently block-factorization preconditioners were also proposed for a different HDG scheme for the incompressible Stokes equations
[
44
,
45
]
. However, robust preconditioners for the
H
(div)-conforming HDG schemes for the generalized parameter-dependent saddle point problems have not been addressed in the literature yet.

Block-diagonal preconditioners have been well-established for the stabilized Stokes equations, the linear elasticity equations
[
50
,
48
]
, and the generalized Stokes equations
[
7
,
42
]
. For the generalized Stokes equations, the Schur complement preconditioner that is robust with respect to mesh size and time step was first proposed in
[
9
]
, then theoretical proved in the finite element setting in
[
7
]
and in continuous setting in
[
28
]
.
We also refer to the surveys in
[
34
,
41
]
for the generalized framework and analysis of block-diagonal preconditioners for the saddle point problems.
Here, we present a uniform block-diagonal preconditioner for the condensed
H
(div)-conforming HDG schemes for the parameter-dependent saddle point problems, including the generalized Stokes equation and the linear elasticity. The key idea is to find robust approximations for the matrix inverses of the symmetric positive definite (SPD) stiffness matrix on the global velocity/displacement space and the (negative) Schur complement on the element-wise constant pressure space in the statically condensed system. For the stiffness matrix on the global velocity/displacement space, we continue the work in our previous study on the reaction-diffusion equations
[
18
]
and construct an optimal auxiliary space preconditioner (ASP) based on the theory proposed by Xu in
[
51
]
. For the Schur complement on the element-wise constant pressure space, we mainly borrow ideas from
[
33
,
39
]
. We define a parameter-dependent norm on the element-wise constant pressure space, then we prove the spectral equivalence between the newly defined norm and the one induced by the (negative) Schur complement, which is robust with respect to model parameters and mesh size. It needs to be pointed out that elliptic regularity is assumed for the domain here. Next, through variational analysis, we construct an explicit matrix formulation corresponding to the definition of the newly defined norm, and naturally conclude that its inverse is robust Schur complement preconditioner. The efficient computation of this Schur complement preconditioner is realized via the Woodbury matrix identity
[
26
]
. The numerical experiments verify the robustness of the preconditioner.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the
H
(div)-conforming HDG scheme for the generalized parameter-dependent saddle point problems and express the static condensation process in matrix formulation. In Section
3
, block-diagonal preconditioners that are robust with respect to model parameters and mesh size are constructed for the statically condensed system. Numerical examples based on the generalized Stokes equations and the linear elasticity equations are then presented in Section
4
to verify the robustness of our proposed preconditioner, and we conclude in Section
5
.

2.2.
Model problem and the HDG scheme

We consider the following saddle point problem: Find
u
and
p
such that

(2)

\left.\begin{tabular}[]{r r r}$\nabla\cdot(-2\mu\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}))+\tau\hbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p$&$=$&$\hbox{\boldmath$f$}$\\
$\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}+\frac{1}{\lambda}p$&$=$&$0$\end{tabular}\right\}\;\text{in}\;\Omega,\;\hbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{\partial\Omega}=\hbox{\boldmath$0$},

where
\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}):=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{T})
is the symmetric gradient,

\mu,\lambda>0
,
\tau\geq 0
are constant model parameters and
f
is the source term. We note that this model covers the linear elasticity problem where
\tau\geq 0
is the inverse of time step size,
\mu,\lambda>0
are Lamé parameters, with
u
representing the displacement and
p
the pressure. It also covers the generalized Stokes problem where
\lambda=+\infty
,
\mu>0
is the viscosity and
\tau\geq 0
is the inverse of time step size, where
u
represents the fluid velocity and
p
the pressure.

To simplify our analysis, we focus on this constant-coefficient problem with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, while other standard boundary conditions are covered in our numerical experiments in Section
4
.

The equation (
2
) is discretized using the symmetric interior penalty
divergence-conforming HDG (SIP-divHDG) method with projected jumps
[
29
, Remark 1.2.4]
. To further simplify our notation, we define a compound finite element space
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}:=\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}\times\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}
and its elements
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}:=(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})
. Then, the weak formulation of the divergence-conforming HDG scheme with polynomial degree
k\geq 1
is given as below: Find
(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},p_{h})\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}\times Q_{h,0}^{k-1}
such that

(3)

\left.\begin{tabular}[]{r l}$a(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})+b(p_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})=$&$\left(\hbox{\boldmath$f$},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\right)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}$,\\
$b(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},q_{h})+c(p_{h},q_{h})=$&$0$,\end{tabular}\right\}\forall\;\;(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h},q_{h})\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}\times Q_{h,0}^{k-1},

where the bilinear forms are defined as:

\displaystyle a(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\tau(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{K}+2\mu\text{\Large$($}(\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}):\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}))_{K}-\langle\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})\hbox{\boldmath$n$},\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle-\langle\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})\hbox{\boldmath$n$},\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\rangle_{\partial K}+\langle\frac{\alpha k^{2}}{h}P_{k-1}(\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})),P_{k-1}(\mathsf{tang}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}))\rangle_{\partial K}\text{\Large$)$},

\displaystyle b(p_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})

\displaystyle:=-\sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}(p_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})_{K},

\displaystyle c(p_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}(p_{h},q_{h})_{K},

where
P_{k-1}
denotes the
L^{2}
-projection onto
\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k-1}
,
\alpha>0
is the stabilization parameter to ensure the following coercivity result:

a(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\gtrsim\sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}\|_{K}^{2}+2\mu\left(\|\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})\|_{K}^{2}+\frac{1}{h}\|{\mathsf{tang}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\|_{\partial K}^{2}\right),

for all
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}
. We refer to
[
1
]
for a detailed discussion of the lower bound of
\alpha
. By using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the inverse inequality, it is also easy to verify that on the finite element space
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}
,

a(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}\|_{K}^{2}+2\mu\left(\|\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})\|_{K}^{2}+\frac{1}{h}\|{\mathsf{tang}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\|_{\partial K}^{2}\right).

We refer to
[
21
]
for more details of the coercivity and boundedness results. Therefore,
a(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})
defines a norm on the finite element space
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}
:

(4)

a(\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h},\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\simeq|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\ast,h}^{2},

where
|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\ast,h}^{2}:=\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}
, and
|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}:=\sum\limits_{K\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\|\hbox{\boldmath$D$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h})\|_{K}^{2}+\frac{1}{h}\|{\mathsf{tang}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}-\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})\|_{\partial K}^{2}
.

2.3.
Matrix formulation and static condensation

We apply matrix representation of the

\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\

\displaystyle=-(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{o})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=0,\;\;

\displaystyle\forall\;\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{o}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,o},

\displaystyle=-(p_{h}^{o},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{\partial})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=0,\;\;

\displaystyle\forall\;p^{o}_{h}\in Q_{h}^{k-1,o},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{\partial}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,\partial},

\displaystyle c(p_{h}^{o},\;\overline{q}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}(p_{h}^{o},\;\overline{q}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\forall\;p^{o}_{h}\in Q_{h}^{k-1,o},\overline{q}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}.

\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}u^{o}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}u^{\partial}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}\widehat{u}}&\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{B}_{u^{o}p^{o}}\\

\mathbf{U}^{\partial}\\

(i)

. Then, we express

in the final condensed HDG scheme as

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{A}_{g}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{g},\underline{\mathbf{V}}_{g}\rangle_{2}=

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\nabla\cdot\pazocal{L}^{o}_{h}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{\partial},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}),\nabla\cdot\pazocal{L}^{o}_{h}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{\partial},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h})\right)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{A}_{g}\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{g},\underline{\mathbf{U}}_{g}\rangle_{2}.

\displaystyle|\!|\!|(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{\partial},\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h})|\!|\!|_{\ast,h}^{2}.

3.2.

Since we use the concept of sum and intersection of Hilbert spaces in this subsection, we briefly introduce the definition and basic properties here, see
[
39
, Section 2.2]
and
[
5
, Chapter 2]
. Assume
X
and
Y
are compatible Hilbert spaces both continuously contained in some larger Hilbert space, then their intersection
X\cap Y
and their sum
X+Y
are both complete Hilbert spaces with corresponding norms:

(14)

\displaystyle\|z\|_{X\cap Y}^{2}

\displaystyle=\;\|z\|_{X}^{2}+\|z\|_{Y}^{2},\;\;

\displaystyle\forall\;z\in X\cap Y,

(15)

\displaystyle\|z\|_{X+Y}^{2}

\displaystyle=\;\inf(\|x\|_{X}^{2}+\|y\|_{Y}^{2}),\;\;

\displaystyle\forall\;z=x+y,x\in X,y\in Y.

If
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
as normed vector spaces, we denote
X_{1}^{\prime}
as the dual space of
X_{1}
,
\pazocal{L}(X_{1},Y_{1})
as the space of bounded linear mapping from
X_{1}
to

. Then, we have the following properties, the proof of which we refer to
[
5
, Chapter 2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
X_{1},X_{2}
and
Y_{1},Y_{2}
are pairs of compatible normed vector spaces. If the linear mapping
T\in\pazocal{L}(X_{1},Y_{1})\cap\pazocal{L}(X_{2},Y_{2})
, then we have:

(16)

\displaystyle\|T\|_{X_{1}+X_{2}\rightarrow Y_{1}+Y_{2}}\leq(\|T\|_{X_{1}\rightarrow Y_{1}}^{2}+\|T\|_{X_{2}\rightarrow Y_{2}}^{2})^{1/2}.

If both
X
and
Y
are Hilbert spaces, given any
g\in(X+Y)^{\prime}
, we have:

(17)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{(X+Y)^{\prime}}=\|g\|_{X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}}.

The Schur complement of the final condensed system (
10
) appears to be too complicated to be analyzed. However, in the static condensation process, the Schur complement stays the same before and after

is condensed out, which significantly simplifies our analysis. A similar idea was used in
[

for a different HDG scheme for the Stokes problem.
We first quote a lemma about block matrix inverse, the proof of which we refer to
[

Lemma 3.2

Assume a block matrix
\mathbf{R}
in the form of

\mathbf{R}=\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{C}&\mathbf{D}\end{bmatrix},

and the submatrices
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{D}
are both invertible, then the inverse of
\mathbf{R}
is expressed as

\mathbf{R}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}[1.5](\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{C})^{-1}&-\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\\
-(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{-1}&(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{C}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Then, we prove the following result:

Lemma 3.3

The Schur complement of the HDG scheme with only
\mathbf{P}^{o}
condensed out in (

Proof.

Since the stiffness matrix
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is SPD, it is straightforward to verify that the matrix remains SPD after
\mathbf{U}^{o}
is condensed out.
By Lemma
3.2
, the (negative) Schur complement on the space
\overline{Q}_{h}
of the HDG scheme in
(

is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{C}_{\overline{p}\;\overline{p}}+\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{B}_{u^{\partial}\overline{p}}^{\mathrm{T}}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}u^{o}}+\mathbf{D}_{u^{o}u^{o}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}u^{\partial}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}\widehat{u}}\\
\mathbf{A}_{u^{\partial}u^{o}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{\partial}u^{\partial}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{\partial}\widehat{u}}\\
\mathbf{A}_{\widehat{u}u^{o}}&\mathbf{A}_{\widehat{u}u^{\partial}}&\mathbf{A}_{\widehat{u}\widehat{u}}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{B}_{u^{\partial}\overline{p}}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{C}_{g}+\mathbf{B}_{g}^{\mathrm{T}}(\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{A}_{u^{\partial}u^{\partial}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{\partial}\widehat{u}}\\
\mathbf{A}_{\widehat{u}u^{\partial}}&\mathbf{A}_{\widehat{u}\widehat{u}}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}[1.5]\mathbf{A}_{u^{\partial}u^{o}}\\
\mathbf{A}_{\widehat{u}u^{o}}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{E}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}u^{\partial}}&\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}\widehat{u}}\end{bmatrix})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{g}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{C}_{g}+\mathbf{B}_{g}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{A}_{g}\mathbf{B}_{g},

where
\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{A}_{u^{o}u^{o}}+\mathbf{D}_{u^{o}u^{o}}
.
∎

From now on, we directly work with the (negative) Schur complement
\mathbf{S^{\prime}}
of the HDG scheme in (
7
), which is the same as
\mathbf{S}_{g}
from Lemma
3.3
. Inspired by the ideas in
[
33
,
39
]
, we need to define a parameter-dependent norm on the finite element space
\overline{Q}_{h}
to obtain the upper and lower spectral bound of
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
independent of model parameters and mesh size.
We start from the norm defined by the SPD (negative) Schur complement
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}
, we have:

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
in (
7
) is SPD,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime-1/2}
is also SPD. Denote
n
as the number of DOFs of
\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}
. Combined with the definition of matrix representations of bilinear forms and the norm defined by
a(\cdot,\;\cdot)
in (
4
), we have:

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(-\mathbf{C}^{\prime}+\mathbf{B}^{\prime\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{A}^{\prime-1}\mathbf{B}^{\prime})\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\langle\mathbf{A}^{\prime-1/2}\mathbf{B}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\mathbf{A}^{\prime-1/2}\mathbf{B}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\mathbf{\underline{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\langle\mathbf{A}^{\prime-1/2}\mathbf{B}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\mathbf{\underline{V}}\rangle_{2}^{2}}{\langle\mathbf{\underline{V}},\mathbf{\underline{V}}\rangle_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\mathbf{\underline{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\langle\mathbf{B}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\mathbf{A}^{\prime-1/2}\mathbf{\underline{V}}\rangle_{2}^{2}}{\langle\mathbf{\underline{V}},\mathbf{\underline{V}}\rangle_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\langle\mathbf{B}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\underline{\mathbf{W}}\rangle_{2}^{2}}{\langle\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\underline{\mathbf{W}},\underline{\mathbf{W}}\rangle_{2}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}.

∎

Next, we introduce a parameter-dependent norm on the element-wise constant space
\overline{Q}_{h}
:

(18)

|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2}:=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\inf_{\overline{p}_{h}=\overline{p}_{h,1}+\overline{p}_{h,2}}\left(\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\overline{p}_{h,1}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau h}\|[\![\overline{p}_{h,2}]\!]\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}\right).

Before proceeding to the parameter-independent stability and boundedness of
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
with respect to
|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}
, we need to prove two inf-sup conditions.

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}
, we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}{2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof procedure is similar to
[
29
, Proposition 2.3.5]
.
For any
\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}
, assume
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
satisfies the Neumann problem
-\Delta\phi=\overline{p}_{h}
with
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
. We take
\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{\ast}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
such that
\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{\ast}=\nabla\phi
and we get
\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{\ast}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}
from the elliptic regularity due to the convex domain
\Omega
. Then from
[
29
, Lemma 2.3.1]
there exists
\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}
satisfying
(q_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}=(q_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{\ast})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}
for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}^{k-1}
and

|\!|\!|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}\lesssim\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{\ast}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}
.
Since
k\geq 1
in the
H
(div)-conforming HDG scheme and
\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}=\hbox{\boldmath$0$}
in the lowest order case, we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}{2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{1}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}{2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}{2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$w$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{\ast})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}{2\mu\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{\ast}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{4}}{2\mu\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}.

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For all
\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}
, we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\tau h}\|[\![\overline{p}_{h}]\!]\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}.

Proof.

For any
\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}
, we construct
\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\in\mathrm{RT0}\subset\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k}
such that
\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}|_{F}=[\![\overline{p}_{h}]\!]|_{F}
for all
F\in\mathscr{E}_{h}^{o}
.
By norm equivalence and standard scaling arguments, we have
\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}\simeq h\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}
. Since normal components of functions in
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}
are continuous across element facets, by integrating by parts we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{\langle[\![\overline{p}_{h}]\!],\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\rangle_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\langle[\![\overline{p}_{h}]\!],\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\rangle_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\|[\![\overline{p}_{h}]\!]\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{4}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle{\simeq}\frac{\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{4}}{\tau h\|\hbox{\boldmath$w$}_{h}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau h}\|[\![\overline{p}_{h}]\!]\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}.

∎

We are now ready to present the equivalence between the newly defined norm and the one induced by
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
with the above properties.

Theorem 3.2

(Equivalent Schur complement norm)
.

\langle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}\simeq|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2},\quad\forall\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}.

Proof.

(a)

To prove
\langle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}\gtrsim|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2}
, we denote two normed vector spaces:

\displaystyle{X}_{1}

\displaystyle=\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k},\;\;\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{{X}_{1}}^{2}=2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2},\quad

\displaystyle\forall\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\in{X}_{1},

\displaystyle{X}_{2}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k},\;\;\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{{X}_{2}}^{2}=\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2},\quad

\displaystyle\forall\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\in{X}_{2},

and the mapping
T\in\pazocal{L}(\overline{Q}_{h},X_{1}^{\prime})\cap\pazocal{L}(\overline{Q}_{h},X_{2}^{\prime})
such that

(T\overline{p}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})=(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}),\quad\forall\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h,0}^{k}.

With the norm defined by
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
in Lemma
3.4
, the inf-sup conditions in Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
, the definition of sum and intersection Hilbert spaces in (
14
) and (
15
), linear mapping properties in Lemma
3.1
, we have the following result:

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|T\overline{p}_{h}\|_{(X_{1}\cap X_{2})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|T\overline{p}_{h}\|_{X_{1}^{\prime}+X_{2}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2}.

(b)

To prove
\langle\mathbf{S}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}\lesssim|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2}
,
we assume an arbitrary splitting
\overline{p}_{h}=\overline{p}_{h,1}+\overline{p}_{h,2}
. By using the norm defined by
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
in Lemma
3.4
, integration by parts, Cauchy Schwarz inequality, and inverse inequality, we have:

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}+\lambda\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{o}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h,1},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+(\overline{p}_{h,2},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\overline{p}_{h,1},\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h})^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+\langle[\![\overline{p}_{h,2}]\!],\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\rangle^{2}_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sup_{\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}\in\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h,0}^{k}}\frac{(\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\overline{p}_{h,1}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau h}\|[\![\overline{p}_{h,2}]\!]\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2})(2\mu\|\nabla\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}})}{\tau\|\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+2\mu|\!|\!|\underline{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{1,h}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\overline{p}_{h,1}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau h}\|[\![\overline{p}_{h,2}]\!]\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}.

Since the splitting of
\overline{p}_{h}
is arbitrary, we always have
\langle\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}\lesssim|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2},\;\forall\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}
and this completes the proof.

∎

Next, we present the matrix representation of the newly defined norm
|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}
following the similar analysis procedure as in
[
39
, Theorem 2.5]
. It naturally follows from Theorem
3.2
that this matrix representation is spectrally equivalent to the Schur complement
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
and its inverse is a robust Schur complement preconditioner with respect to model parameters and mesh size. We define matrix
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{N}
by

\langle\mathbf{M}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{Q}}\rangle_{2}=(\overline{p}_{h},\overline{q}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}},\quad\langle\mathbf{N}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{Q}}\rangle_{2}=\frac{1}{h}\langle[\![\overline{p}_{h}]\!],[\![\overline{q}_{h}]\!]\rangle_{\mathscr{E}_{h}},

for all
\overline{p}_{h},\overline{q}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}
and we have the following result:

Theorem 3.3

(Preconditioner for Schur Complement)
.

Define the SPD matrix operator
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}
on
\overline{Q}_{h}
by
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}:=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{M}(\tau\mathbf{M}+2\mu\mathbf{N})^{-1}\mathbf{N}
. We have:

|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2}=\langle\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{h},\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{h}\rangle_{2},\quad\forall\overline{p}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h},

and

\kappa(\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}\mathbf{S}^{\prime})\simeq 1.

Proof.

In the definition of the norm
|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}
(
18
), by variational analysis the infimum is achieved when

(\frac{1}{2\mu}\overline{p}_{h,1},\overline{q}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+\frac{1}{\tau h}\langle[\![\overline{p}_{h,1}-\overline{p}_{h}]\!],[\![\overline{q}_{h}]\!]\rangle_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}=0,\quad\forall\overline{q}_{h}\in\overline{Q}_{h}.

By reformulating it into the matrix formulation, we get:

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{1}{2\mu}\mathbf{M}\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{1}+\frac{1}{\tau}\mathbf{N}(\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{1}-\overline{\mathbf{P}}),\overline{\mathbf{Q}}\right\rangle_{2}=0,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{P}}_{1}=\left(\frac{\tau}{2\mu}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{N}\overline{\mathbf{P}}.

Therefore, combined with the fact that
(\frac{1}{2\mu}\overline{p}_{h,1},\overline{p}_{h,1}-\overline{p})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+\frac{1}{\tau h}\langle[\![\overline{p}_{h,1}-\overline{p}_{h}]\!],[\![\overline{p}_{h,1}-\overline{p}]\!]\rangle_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}=0
,
|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2}
can be explicitly expressed as

\displaystyle|\overline{p}_{h}|_{\ast}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|\overline{p}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\overline{p}_{h,1}\|_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau h}\|[\![\overline{p}_{h}-\overline{p}_{h,1}]\!]\|_{\mathscr{E}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}(\overline{p}_{h},\overline{p}_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+\frac{1}{2\mu}(\overline{p}_{h},\overline{p}_{h,1})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\langle\mathbf{M}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\left\langle\mathbf{M}\left(\frac{\tau}{2\mu}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{N}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\right\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\overline{\mathbf{P}},\overline{\mathbf{P}}\rangle_{2},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbf{M}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\mathbf{M}\left(\frac{\tau}{2\mu}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{M}(\tau\mathbf{M}+2\mu\mathbf{N})^{-1}\mathbf{N}.

Then
\kappa(\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}\mathbf{S}^{\prime})\simeq 1
directly follows Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Finally, we present an explicit and computable expression for
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}
which has a remarkably simple structure. At first glance, it seems quite difficult to exactly compute
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}
due to its complicated form when the parameters
\frac{1}{\lambda}
,
\mu
and
\tau
are not zero. This issue was partially addressed in our previous work
[
20
]
using an approximation to matrix inversion, where

(19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}\approx\frac{2\mu\lambda}{2\mu+\lambda}(\mathbf{M})^{-1}+\tau(\frac{\tau}{\lambda}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{N})^{-1}.

We note that a similar form to (
19
) can also be found in a recent paper
[
40
]
to precondition the Schur complement of a penalized surface incompressible fluid problem. Note that (
19
) is an exact inverse of
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}
only if either of
\frac{1}{\lambda}
,
\tau
, or
\mu
is zero, but not exact for the general case where
\frac{1}{\lambda}
,
\tau
, and
\mu
are all not zero.
Here, we obtain a simple expression of the exact inverse of
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}
, which has a similar form as (
19
), by exploring the structure of the matrix using the Woodbury matrix identity
[
26
]
.

Theorem 3.4

(Exact inverse of
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}
)
.

(20)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}=\frac{2\mu\lambda}{2\mu+\lambda}\mathbf{M}^{-1}+\tau\left(\frac{\lambda}{2\mu+\lambda}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\tau}{2\mu+\lambda}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

By algebraic manipulation, we have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{M}\left(\tau\mathbf{M}+2\mu\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{M}\left(\tau\mathbf{M}+2\mu\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}\frac{\tau\mathbf{M}+2\mu\mathbf{N}-\tau\mathbf{M}}{2\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu+\lambda}{2\mu\lambda}\mathbf{M}-\frac{\tau}{2\mu}\mathbf{M}\left(\tau\mathbf{M}+2\mu\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{M}.

We quote Woodbury matrix identity
[
26
]
to get the inverses of sum matrices, which states:

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{U}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{V}\right)^{-1}=\mathbf{A}^{-1}-\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{U}\left(\mathbf{C}^{-1}+\mathbf{V}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{U}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{A}^{-1},

and the result follows by plugging into the above equation
\mathbf{A}=\frac{2\mu+\lambda}{2\mu\lambda}\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{U}=-\frac{\tau}{2\mu}\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{C}=\left(\tau\mathbf{M}+2\mu\mathbf{N}\right)^{-1}
, and
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{M}
.
∎

4.1.
The generalized Stokes equations

The generalized Stokes problem fits into the general setting (
2
) with
\lambda=+\infty
and it is easily verified that
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}=2\mu\mathbf{M}^{-1}+\tau\mathbf{N}^{-1}
. We choose the model problems of the lid-driven cavity and the backward-facing step flow as in
[
17
]
.

For the lid-driven cavity, we take the domain to be unit square/cube
\Omega_{c}=[0,1]^{d}
, where
d
is the space dimension. An inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}=[4x(1-x),0]^{\mathrm{T}}
in 2D or
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}=[16x(1-x)y(1-y),0,0]^{\mathrm{T}}
in 3D is set on the top side, with no-slip boundary condition for all other sides.
For the backward-facing step flow, we choose the domain
\Omega_{b}=([0.5,4]\times[0,0.5])\cup([0,4]\times[0.5,1])
in 2D or
\Omega_{b}=\left(([0.5,4]\times[0,0.5])\cup([0,4]\times[0.5,1])\right)\times[0,1]
in 3D. An inhomogeneous boundary condition
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}=[16(1-y)(y-0.5),0]^{\mathrm{T}}
in 2D or
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}=[64(1-y)(y-0.5)z(1-z),0,0]^{\mathrm{T}}
in 3D is set for the inlet velocity on
\{x=0\}
, with do-nothing boundary condition on
\{x=4\}
and no-slip boundary condition on the remaining sides.

In both model problems, the domains are divided into uniform simplicial meshes with mesh size
h_{start}
followed by three-level refinement.
h_{start}
is chosen to be 8 in 2D numerical experiments and 4 in 3D cases due to the limit of computation capability. The source function
f
is set to be
0
. The value of
\mu
is fixed to be 1 and
\tau
is chosen from {0,1,100}. The change of iteration counts with the increase of polynomial degrees is also examined, with
k\in\{2,3,4\}
. The iteration counts of lid-driven cavity model are recorded in Table
1
, and iteration counts of backward-facing step flow model are in Table
2
.

As observed from Table
1
, the iteration counts of both two-dimensional and three-dimensional lid-driven cavity model problems are independent of mesh size
h
for a fixed polynomial order
k
and reaction parameter
\tau
. The iteration counts are also robust with respect to the value of
\tau
, decreasing as
\tau
increases from 0 to 100, which is expected considering the velocity block becomes more similar to a mass matrix as
\tau
increases.

Moreover, we find the iteration count only increases very mildly as the polynomial degree increases from
k=2
to
k=4
.
It also needs to be noted that the iteration counts in three-dimensional cases are higher when compared to the
two-dimensional counterparts.
The results from Table
2
are similar to those from Table
1
, where it needs to be noted that the iteration counts in the backward-facing step flow problem are higher than those in the lid-driven cavity problem when other parameters are the same. Therefore, the iteration counts of our preconditioner are dependent on the shape of the domain, or more specifically aspect ratio of the domain.

4.2.
The steady and unsteady linear elasticity

For the steady and unsteady linear elasticity equations, we use the same domain and boundary conditions of the lid-driven cavity model problem in the generalized Stokes equations. The source term
f
is again set to be
0
.

The steady linear elasticity equations fit into (
2
) with
\tau=0
, and the corresponding Schur complement preconditioner is
\widetilde{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}^{-1}=\frac{2\mu\lambda}{\lambda+2\mu}\mathbf{M}^{-1}
. The value of
\frac{1}{\lambda}
is chosen from
\{10^{-4},10^{-1},1\}
, and all other settings are the same as the numerical experiments of the generalized Stokes equations. The iteration counts are recorded in Table
3
.

For the unsteady linear elasticity equations, we take
\mu=1

and vary the value of
\tau
from
\{10,\;10^{2},\;10^{3},\;10^{4}\}
and
\frac{1}{\lambda}
from
\{10^{-4},\;10^{-1},\;1,\;10\}
.
The iteration counts
for
k=2
with different mesh sizes are recorded in Table
4
, while those for
k=3
are recorded in Table
5
.

Results from Table
3
–
5
verify the robustness of our
the block-diagonal preconditioner with respect to mesh size and
model parameters,
with the nearly-incompressible cases taking more iterations than the compressible ones.

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a robust block-diagonal preconditioner with respect to mesh size
h
and model parameters for the condensed
H
(div)-conforming HDG schemes for the parameter-dependent saddle point problems, including the generalized Stokes equations and the linear elasticity equations. For the stiffness matrix, we extended from the optimal ASP for the
H
(div)-conforming HDG scheme for the reaction-diffusion equations, which was developed in our previous study. For the Schur complement, we obtained a general matrix formulation spectrally equivalent to the Schur complement in Theorem
3.3
, based on a newly defined parameter-dependent norm on the element-wise constant space. Then an explicit computable exact inverse is obtained via the Woodbury matrix identity.
Numerical results verify the robustness of the proposed block preconditioner in both two- and three-dimensions.

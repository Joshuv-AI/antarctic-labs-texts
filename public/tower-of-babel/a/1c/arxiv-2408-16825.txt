Correlation Functions and Trace Anomalies in Weakly Relevant Flows
Denis Karateev, and Biswajit Sahoo

Abstract
We study abstract weakly relevant flows in a general number of dimensions. They arguably provide the simplest example of renormalization group (RG) flows between two non-trivial fixed points. We compute several two-point correlation functions in position space valid along the whole RG flow. This is done by using conformal perturbation theory together with the solution of the Callan-Symanzik equation. From the explicit expressions of the two-point functions of conserved currents and the stress-tensor we extract the change in the central charges between the UV and IR fixed points. This immediately gives us Δ ​ c \Delta c , the change of the c − c- trace anomaly between the UV and IR fixed points in 4d. We also discuss three-point functions. We couple weakly relevant flows to non-dynamical dilaton and graviton background fields in 4d. We compute the three-dilaton vertex in terms of the scalar two-point function and extract the value of Δ ​ a \Delta a , the change of the a a -trace anomaly between the UV and IR fixed points. We also compute the graviton-graviton-dilaton vertex in terms of the three-point function of two stress-tensors and a scalar, and extract the value of Δ ​ c \Delta c . The Δ ​ c \Delta c values obtained with the two different methods agree.

Introduction

Quantum field theories (QFTs) are defined as renormalization group (RG) flows between UV and IR fixed points. We assume that these fixed points are described by conformal field theories (CFTs). The typical problem one needs to solve is to compute the spectrum of the IR CFT (anomalous dimensions, central charges and the OPE coefficients) in terms of the UV description of the QFT. A more general problem is to compute correlation functions valid along the whole RG flow. The IR spectrum can then be extracted by taking the IR limit of two- and three-point functions.

In order to compute correlation functions in position space valid along the whole RG flow we use the strategy of

First, one uses conformal perturbation theory in order to obtain a perturbative result. This result, however, breaks down far from the UV fixed point. In order to obtain the result, which is valid along the whole RG flow, one needs to re-sum the leading perturbative contributions to all orders. This is done in the second step by using the renormalization group techniques, more precisely by writing down and solving the Callan-Symanzik equation.

1.1
Conventions

We work in
d
dimensional Euclidean space. The surface area of the
(d-1)
-dimensional sphere embedded in
d
dimensions is given by

\Omega_{d-1}\equiv\frac{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}.

(2)

The connected part of scalar correlation functions of the local operator
\mathcal{O}(x)
in position space are computed from the action
A
using the standard path-integral expression

\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\ldots\mathcal{O}(x_{n})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{\int[d\Phi]\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\ldots\mathcal{O}(x_{n})e^{-A[\Phi]}}{\int[d\Phi]\ e^{-A[\Phi]}},

(3)

where
[\Phi]
are independent UV CFT degrees of freedom.

Our convention for generic two- and three-point conformal correlators is

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{CFT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\Delta_{1}}},

(4)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{3}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{CFT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{3}}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{3}}{2}}\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}}{2}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\frac{-\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}}{2}}}.

(5)

Here
C_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{3}}
is the OPE coefficient for three different scalar operators and

x_{ij}^{\mu}\equiv x_{i}^{\mu}-x_{j}^{\mu}.

(6)

For later purposes it is also convenient to define

\Delta_{ij}\equiv\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{j}.

(7)

When writing scaling dimensions and OPE coefficients, most of the time we will refer to the UV CFT. When we discuss the IR CFT, we will put an explicit label IR to the scaling dimensions in order to distinguish them from the UV CFT ones.

Due to Poincareé invariance all the QFT scalar two-point correlation functions depend on a single variable defined as

r\equiv\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(8)

Analogously, all the scalar three-point functions depend only on the three variables
x_{12}^{2}
,
x_{13}^{2}
and
x_{23}^{2}
. For the later purposes another basis of variables is much more convenient. It consists of one dimensionful variable
\rho
and two dimensionless variables
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
. We define them as

\rho\equiv(x_{12}^{2}x_{13}^{2}x_{23}^{2})^{\frac{1}{6}},\qquad y_{1}\equiv\frac{x^{2}_{12}}{x_{23}^{2}},\qquad y_{2}\equiv\frac{x_{13}^{2}}{x_{23}^{2}}.

(9)

Notice that the CFT three-point function (
5
) with all scalars being identical depends only on
\rho
.
We can invert the above relations and get

x_{12}^{2}=\rho^{2}\times y_{1}^{2/3}y_{2}^{-1/3},\qquad x_{13}^{2}=\rho^{2}\times y_{1}^{-1/3}y_{2}^{2/3},\qquad x_{23}^{2}=\rho^{2}\times y_{1}^{-1/3}y_{2}^{-1/3}.

(10)

The following relation is often useful

x_{ij}\cdot x_{ik}=\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{ij}^{2}+x_{ik}^{2}-x_{kj}^{2}\right).

(11)

1.2
Summary of the results

The action (
1
) is super-renormalizable as long as
d/2<\Delta<d
. All the quantities we consider in this paper are finite and no regularization is required. Renormalization techniques are used here simply as an efficient way to re-sum perturbative corrections to all orders. We find easier to summarize our results below in terms of the renormalized quantities. These can be, however, straightforwardly rewritten in terms of the original (bare) quantities. Instead of the scalar operator
\mathcal{O}(x)
we will work with the renormalized operator
\mathbf{O}(x)
, see (
41
) and (
55
) for its precise definition. Instead of the bare coupling
\lambda_{0}
we will work with the renormalized coupling
\lambda=\lambda(\mu)
. Here
\mu<m
is some energy scale at which the value of
\lambda
is defined. The
\beta
-function for the renormalized coupling reads as

\beta(\lambda)=\delta\lambda\left(-1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\right)+O(\lambda^{3}),

(12)

where the non-negative real parameter
\lambda_{\star}
is given by

\lambda_{\star}=\frac{2\delta}{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}}+O(\delta^{2}).

(13)

We assume that
C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}>0
.
The
\beta
-function has a non-trivial perturbative IR fixed point with the effective couplings
\lambda_{\star}
. The renormalized coupling
\lambda
is related to
\lambda_{0}
via the following relation

\lambda(\mu)=\lambda_{0}\,(m\mu^{-1})^{\delta}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{\star}}(m\mu^{-1})^{\delta}\right)+O(\lambda_{0}^{3}).

(14)

By solving the Callan-Symanzik equation for two-point functions of scalar operators we obtain the following quantities

\chi(s,\lambda)\equiv\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\,(s^{\delta}-1)},\qquad\Lambda(s,\lambda)\equiv\lambda s^{\delta}\times\chi(s,\lambda),

(15)

where
s
is a dimensionless variable.
The
\Lambda(s,\lambda)
quantity is often called the running coupling. These two objects provide nice interpolating functions between the UV
(s\rightarrow 0)
and the IR
(s\rightarrow\infty)
fixed points.

Results (part 1).

The scalar two-point function valid along the whole RG flow reads as

\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{r^{2\Delta}}\times\Big(\chi\left(\mu r,\lambda\right)\Big)^{4}.

(16)

The normalization condition we use to define the renormalized operator
\mathbf{O}(x)
is the requirement that the above two-point function at the distance
r=\mu^{-1}
simply reduces to the UV CFT expression (
4
). We can see that this is indeed the case since
\chi(1,\lambda)=1
. Our result (
16
) agrees with the one obtained in
[
24
]
by using a different method. For completeness, we review this method with some modifications in appendix
E
.

The two-point function of conserved currents reads as

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{r^{2(d-1)}}\times\left(h_{1}(\mu r,\lambda)\delta^{\mu\nu}+h_{2}(\mu r,\lambda)\frac{x_{12}^{\mu}x_{12}^{\nu}}{r^{2}}\right),

(17)

where the two scalar dimensionless functions are given by

\displaystyle h_{1}(\mu r,\lambda)

\displaystyle=C_{J}-(\Delta-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\times\Lambda(\mu r,\lambda),

(18)

\displaystyle h_{2}(\mu r,\lambda)

\displaystyle=-2C_{J}+(\Delta+d-2)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\times\Lambda(\mu r,\lambda).

Here
C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}
is the OPE coefficient in the UV CFT three-point function of two conserved currents and the scalar operator
\mathcal{O}
. For its definition see (
110
). The coefficient
E_{JJ}
is given in (
116
). In the IR we must recover conformal invariance. According to the condition (
107
) the IR central charge can be extracted as

C_{J}^{IR}=h_{1}(\infty,\lambda)=-\frac{1}{2}\,h_{2}(\infty,\lambda).

(19)

Plugging here our solution (
18
) we obtain the change of the current central charge along the RG flow

C_{J}^{UV}-C_{J}^{IR}=\frac{4(d-1)}{d}\frac{C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}}\delta+O(\delta^{2}).

(20)

The solution (
18
) breaks conservation along the RG flow at the order
O(\lambda^{2})
. We carefully discuss this issue in the end of section
4.1
and propose a possible way to restore conservation at all orders. Despite this issue, our result (
17
) together with (
18
) obeys conservation and enjoys conformal invariance in the IR limit at the order
O(\delta).
This is the reason why (
20
) still holds.

We obtain an analogous to (
17
) result for the case of the stress-tensor in section
5
. More precisely see equations
(
151
), (
152
), (
156
), (
159
) and (
163
). Using these we can compute the change of the stress-tensor central charge along the RG flow. It reads

C_{T}^{UV}-C_{T}^{IR}=\frac{8(d+1)}{d(d+2)^{2}}\frac{C_{TT\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}}\delta\ +\ O(\delta^{2}).

(21)

Here
C_{TT\mathcal{O}}
is the OPE coefficient in the UV CFT three-point function of two stress-tensors and the scalar operator
\mathcal{O}
. For its definition see appendix
G
.
In 4d CFTs one can define the
c
-anomaly. In the standard conventions
[
39
]
, see also section 2.1 in
[
40
]
for a concise review, it is related to the stress-tensor central charge as
c\equiv\frac{\pi^{2}}{640}C_{T}
. Using the above result we can then evaluate the change of the
c
-anomaly along the RG flow in 4d. It reads

d=4:\qquad\Delta c\equiv c_{UV}-c_{IR}=\frac{\delta\,\pi^{2}}{2304}\frac{C_{TT\mathcal{O}}}{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}}\ \ +\ O(\delta^{2}).

(22)

We have also computed several renormalized three-point functions. When solving the three-point Callan-Symanzik equation we have used as the boundary condition only the three-point function computed at the trivial order in conformal perturbation theory. The scalar three-point function along the whole RG flow is then given by (
103
). It can be compactly written as

\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\langle O(x_{1})O(x_{2})O(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}\times\Big(\chi\left(\mu\rho,\lambda\right)\Big)^{6}.

(23)

The three-point function of two conserved currents and one scalar operator is given by

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\\
\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})O(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}\times\Big(\chi\left(\mu\rho,\lambda\right)\Big)^{2}+H^{\mu\nu}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

(24)

where
H^{\mu\nu}
is a function which we will discuss shortly. The factor
\chi(\mu r,\lambda)
explicitly breaks conservation in the right-hand side. This effect is similar to the one already encountered at the level of the two-point function (
17
). One can construct the function
H^{\mu\nu}
in such a way that the non-conservation effects are pushed to higher orders in
\lambda
. We did not attempt to do it in this paper.
Analogously to (
24
) we can write the three-point function of two stress-tensors and a scalar operator. It reads

\langle T^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\\
\langle T^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}\times\Big(\chi\left(\mu\rho,\lambda\right)\Big)^{2}+H^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

(25)

The CFT three-point function is defined in appendix
G
.

2
Conformal perturbation theory

Correlation functions in weakly relevant flows are computed by using the definition (
3
) together with the action (
1
). In practice we expand the right-hand side of (
3
) around small values of
\lambda_{0}
. At the leading order we can then write the following expression for the scalar two-point function

\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}\\
-\lambda_{0}m^{d-\Delta}\int d^{d}x_{3}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}),

(27)

and the following expression for the scalar three-point function

\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}\\
-\lambda_{0}m^{d-\Delta}\int d^{d}x_{4}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\mathcal{O}(x_{4})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}).

(28)

In this section we will discuss how to evaluate the integral in the expressions (
27
). In this paper we will only use (
28
) at the trivial order of conformal perturbation theory, namely we will ignore the integral over the CFT four-point function.

The most general CFT three-point function is given in equation (
5
). Our goal is to evaluate the following integral

\int d^{d}x_{3}\ \langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{3}}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}=\\
\frac{C_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{3}}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{3}}{2}}}\int d^{d}x_{3}\ \frac{1}{\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}}{2}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\frac{-\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}}{2}}}.

(29)

Let us start by discussing its divergences. The divergences appearing when
x_{3}=x_{1}
or
x_{3}=x_{2}
are called the UV divergences. Let us consider the situation when
x_{3}=x_{1}
, then the leading part of the above integral becomes

\int d^{d}x_{3}\ \frac{1}{\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}}{2}}}=\Omega_{d-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d|x_{13}|\ \frac{|x_{13}|^{d-1}}{|x_{13}|^{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}}}.

(30)

The UV divergence comes from the integration limit
|x_{13}|=0
. It is absent if

\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}<d.

(31)

We should also consider the
x_{3}=x_{2}
case. It will lead to an analogous to (
31
) condition. Combining the two we conclude that the UV divergences are absent if the following conditions are satisfied

\Delta_{3}<d,\qquad\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}<d.

(32)

The divergence appearing when
x_{3}=\infty
is called the IR divergence. In the
x_{3}=\infty
limit the leading part of the integral (
29
) reads as

\int d^{d}x_{3}\frac{1}{\left(x_{3}^{2}\right)^{\Delta_{3}}}=\Omega_{d-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}d|x_{3}|\frac{|x_{3}|^{d-1}}{|x_{3}|^{2\Delta_{3}}}.

(33)

The IR divergence comes from the integration limit
|x_{3}|=\infty
. It is absent if

\Delta_{3}>d/2.

(34)

The integral appearing in (
27
) needs to be evaluated for the case
\Delta_{i}=\Delta=d-\delta
. This case satisfies both bounds (
32
) and (
34
), and, thus, is free of UV and IR divergences.

The integral (
29
) can be straightforwardly evaluated in the range when it is free of UV and IR divergences. We compute this integral in appendix
A
. Here let us simply provide the final answer

\int d^{d}x_{3}\ \langle\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{1}}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{2}}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{3}}(x_{3})\rangle=\frac{C_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}_{3}}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-d}{2}}}N(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3}),

(35)

where
N(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3})
reads as

N(\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2},\Delta_{3})=\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{3}+d}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{3}-\Delta_{1}+d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3}-\Delta_{1}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\Delta_{3}+\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}}{2}\right)}\times\frac{\Gamma\left(\Delta_{3}-\frac{d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(d-\Delta_{3}\right)}.

(36)

Using this we can obtain the explicit expression for the two-point function (
27
). It reads

\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{r^{2\Delta}}\left(1-\lambda_{0}(mr)^{d-\Delta}C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O\big(\lambda_{0}^{2}\big)\right).

(37)

For the later purposes it is important to define the following quantity

B(\delta)\equiv\delta\times N(d-\delta,\,d-\delta,\,d-\delta).

(38)

This function remains finite in the limit
\delta\rightarrow 0
, more precisely

B(\delta)=2\Omega_{d-1}+O(\delta^{2}).

(39)

3
Scalar correlators along the RG flow

In the previous section we computed the scalar two-point function using conformal perturbation at the leading order. The result is given in equation (
37
). In the UV limit
r\rightarrow 0
we recover conformally invariant two-point function as expected. However, the IR limit
r\rightarrow\infty
is ill-defined, because the perturbative expansion breaks down due to the large value of the second term. In order to be able to take the IR limit one has to either compute (
37
) to all orders of conformal perturbation theory or to at least re-sum the leading order perturbative corrections to all orders. In this section we perform the latter re-summation by using the renormalization group techniques. By solving the Callan-Symanzik equation for the scalar two-point function we will obtained the re-summed version of (
37
) valid at all energies. We will also solve the Callan-Symanzik equation for the scalar three-point function.

3.1
Renormalization

Our theory is defined via the action (
1
). We can provide an alternative description of this theory by integrating out the degrees of freedom up to some energy scale
\mu
. Our action will take the following form then

A=A_{\text{EFT}}+\lambda(\mu)\mu^{d-\Delta}\int d^{d}x\,\mathbf{O}(x).

(40)

Here
A_{\text{EFT}}
stands for the effective action at the scale
\mu
obtained from the original action by integrating out the high energy degrees of freedom up to scale
\mu
, and
\lambda(\mu)
is an effective coupling usually called the renormalized coupling. The bold operator is defined as

\mathbf{O}(x)\equiv Z(\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{O}(x),

(41)

where
Z(\mu)
is a numerical factor, whose choice depends on the choice of
\mu
. The operator (
41
) is usually called the renormalized operator. At the energy scale
\mu
, the correlation functions of
\mathbf{O}(x)
take the same form as the conformal correlators given in (
4
) and (
5
), with the conformal dimension
\Delta=d-\delta
and the OPE coefficient
C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}
. This will be implemented through the renormalization conditions below.

In this section we will study the renormalized QFT scalar two- and three-point functions

\displaystyle F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}},

(42)

\displaystyle F_{3}(\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\mu,\lambda)

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}.

(43)

Recall, that
r
,
\rho
,
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are defined in (
8
) and (
9
).
Due to (
41
) the renormalized correlators can be expressed in terms of the original ones as

\displaystyle F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)

\displaystyle=Z(\mu)^{-1}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}},

(44)

\displaystyle F_{3}(\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\mu,\lambda)

\displaystyle=Z(\mu)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}.

(45)

The numerical factor
Z(\mu)
allows to pick a convenient normalization of (renormalised) operators at the scale
\mu
. The most natural choice is given by the following (renormalization) condition

F_{2}(r=\mu^{-1},\,\mu,\lambda)=\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{CFT}}\Big|_{r=\mu^{-1}}=\mu^{2\Delta}.

(46)

Here we have used the convention (
4
).
This condition enforces our renormalized correlator to be conformally invariant at the distance
r=\mu^{-1}
.
Plugging our perturbative result (
37
) for the two-point function in (
44
), and requiring (
46
) we obtain the following result for the numerical factor
Z(\mu)
,

Z(\mu)=1-(m\mu^{-1})^{d-\Delta}\lambda_{0}\,C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O\big(\lambda_{0}^{2}\big).

(47)

The
\beta
-function for the effective (renormalized) coupling is defined as

\beta(\lambda)\equiv\mu\frac{d\lambda(\mu)}{d\mu}.

(48)

It shows how the value of the renormalized coupling changes with the change of the scale choice
\mu
. Following the strategy of
[
4
,
20
]
it can be derived by studying the trace of the stress-tensor obtained using the two actions (
1
) and (
40
). Since the stress-tensor cannot be renormalized (as a conserved operator) the two results must agree. One can then show that

T_{\text{QFT}}{}^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x)=(d-\Delta)\lambda_{0}m^{d-\Delta}\mathcal{O}(x)=-\beta(\lambda)\mu^{d-\Delta}\mathbf{O}(x).

(49)

See appendix
C
for the detailed derivation of this statement.
Plugging here (
47
) we obtain

\displaystyle\beta(\lambda)=-\delta(m\mu^{-1})^{\delta}\lambda_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\delta(m\mu^{-1})^{2\delta}\lambda_{0}^{2}C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\,N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O\big(\lambda_{0}^{3}\big).

(50)

At extremely high energies we should recover the original UV CFT. As a result we must require the following condition

\lambda(\infty)=0.

(51)

The relation between the original (bare) coupling
\lambda_{0}
and the effective (renormalized) coupling
\lambda(\mu)
can be obtained by solving the equation (
48
) together with the above boundary condition. We can write then

\int_{0}^{\lambda(\mu)}d\lambda=\int_{\infty}^{\mu}\frac{d\mu}{\mu}\beta(\lambda).

(52)

Notice, that the
\beta
-function in the form (
50
) is a function of
\mu
only. Performing the integral we obtain

\lambda(\mu)=\lambda_{0}(m\mu^{-1})^{\delta}\left(1-\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{0}\,C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)(m\mu^{-1})^{\delta}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2})\right).

(53)

We can invert this relation, the result reads

\lambda_{0}=\left(\mu m^{-1}\right)^{\delta}\lambda(\mu)\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\lambda(\mu)C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O(\lambda^{2})\right).

(54)

Plugging (
54
) in (
47
) and (
50
) we obtain the final expressions

\displaystyle Z(\mu)

\displaystyle=1-\lambda(\mu)C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O\big(\lambda^{2}\big),

(55)

\displaystyle\beta(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\delta\,\lambda\left(-1+\frac{1}{4}\lambda C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O(\lambda^{2})\right).

(56)

Finally, let us also introduce the
\gamma
-function defined as

\gamma(\lambda)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d\log Z(\mu)}{d\log\mu}.

(57)

Applying this definition to (
47
) we get

\gamma(\lambda)=\frac{\delta}{2}\,\lambda_{0}\left(m\mu^{-1}\right)^{\delta}C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}).

(58)

Notice that since (
47
) has only been computed up to
\lambda_{0}
order, the
\gamma
-function can only be evaluated up to the order
O(\lambda_{0})
.
Plugging here the relation (
54
) we finally conclude that

\gamma(\lambda)=\frac{\delta}{2}\lambda\,C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)+O(\lambda^{2}).

(59)

3.1.1
IR fixed point

The
\beta
-function (
56
) has two zeros

\beta(\lambda)=0.

(60)

They are achieved with the following values of the renormalized coupling

\lambda_{\star}=\frac{4}{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)},\qquad\lambda_{\star\star}=0.

(61)

Using these, the
\beta
-function (
56
) can be rewritten as

\beta(\lambda)=\delta\lambda\left(-1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\right)+O(\lambda^{3}).

(62)

The first value
\lambda_{\star}
corresponds to the IR fixed point. The second value
\lambda_{\star\star}
corresponds to the UV fixed point. The easiest way to see these statements is by using the running coupling constant which we will introduce and compute in the next subsection. The
\beta
-function (
62
) has the form depicted in figure
1
.

Figure 1
:
The
\beta
-function in weakly relevant flows.

Finally, using the definition of
B(\delta)
given in
(
38
) and its expansion (
39
) we can write

\boxed{\lambda_{\star}=\frac{2\delta}{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}}+O(\delta^{2}).}

(63)

Plugging (
63
) into (
62
) we get

\beta(\lambda)=-\delta\lambda+\frac{1}{2}C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}\lambda^{2}+O(\lambda^{3},\,\lambda^{2}\delta).

(64)

In order to be able to trust the IR solution (
61
) of (
60
) we must require that the two terms in (
64
) are roughly of the same order. This can be achieved by demanding that
\delta\ll 1
, which makes the a priori leading term in (
64
) accidentally small.

3.1.2
Renormalized correlators

In the case of the two-point function we can plug (
37
) together with (
55
) and (
54
) into (
44
). The result reads

F^{\text{pert}}_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{r^{2\Delta}}\,\left(1+\lambda\,C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}N(\Delta,\Delta,\Delta)(1-(r\mu)^{\delta})+O(\lambda^{2})\right).

(65)

Recall the relations (
38
) and (
39
). At the leading order in
\delta
we can rewrite the above expression as

\boxed{F^{\text{pert}}_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{r^{2\Delta}}\,\left(1-2\lambda\,C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}\log(r\mu)+O(\lambda^{2},\lambda\delta)\right).}

(66)

It is interesting to notice that the renormalized scalar two-point function (
66
) remains finite in the limit
\delta\rightarrow 0
, whereas the bare one given by (
37
) diverges due to the presence of the
1/\delta
pole.
Analogously, at the trivial order of conformal perturbation theory we get

\boxed{F^{\text{pert}}_{3}(\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{\rho^{3\Delta}}\times\Bigg(C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}+O(\lambda)\Bigg).}

(67)

3.2
Derivation of the Callan-Symanzik equation

Using dimensional analysis we can represent the renormalized two-point function (
42
) as follows

F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{f_{2}(r\mu,\lambda)}{r^{2\Delta}}.

(68)

Comparing this with equation (
66
) we obtain the perturbative expression for the function
f_{2}
. It reads as

f^{\text{pert}}_{2}(r\mu,\lambda)=1-2\lambda\,C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}\log(r\mu)+O(\lambda^{2},\delta).

(69)

Analogously, we can represent the renormalized three-point function (
43
) as follows

F_{3}(\rho,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{f_{3}(\rho\mu,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda)}{\rho^{3\Delta}}.

(70)

Notice that the variables
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are dimensionless. Comparing with (
67
) we simply conclude that

f^{\text{pert}}_{3}(\rho\mu,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda)=C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}+O(\lambda).

(71)

Consider the following renormalized
n
-point function

F_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\mu,\lambda)\equiv Z(\mu)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\ldots\mathcal{O}(x_{n})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}.

(72)

The original (unrenormalized)
n
-point function does not depend on the scale
\mu
. As a result we have the following condition

\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\ldots\mathcal{O}(x_{n})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=0.

(73)

Plugging here (
72
) we obtain

\frac{n}{2}\,Z(\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\left(\mu\frac{dZ(\mu)}{d\mu}\right)F_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\mu,\lambda)+\\
Z(\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+\mu\frac{d\lambda}{d\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\right)F_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\mu,\lambda)=0.

(74)

Using the definitions of the
\beta
-function and the anomalous dimension
\gamma
given by (
48
) and (
57
), and stripping off the factor
Z(\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}}
we obtain the Callan-Symanzik equation

\left(\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+\beta(\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}+n\gamma(\lambda)\right)F_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\mu,\lambda)=0.

(75)

Let us focus on the case of
n=2
and
n=3
point functions. Plugging (
68
) into the Callan-Symanzik equation (
75
) we obtain the following equation

s\equiv r\mu:\qquad\left(s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+\beta(\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}+2\gamma(\lambda)\right)f_{2}(s,\lambda)=0.

(76)

Analogously, plugging (
70
) into the Callan-Symanzik equation (
75
) we obtain

\sigma\equiv\rho\mu:\qquad\left(\sigma\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}+\beta(\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}+3\gamma(\lambda)\right)f_{3}(\sigma,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda)=0.

(77)

3.3
Solution of the Callan-Symanzik equation: two-point function

Let us now obtain the most general solution of the Callan-Symanzik equation (
76
).

We denote the characteristics of the equation (
76
), also known as the running coupling, by
\Lambda(s,\lambda)
. It is a dimensionless function, which obeys the characteristic equation

s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Lambda(s,\lambda)=-\beta\big(\Lambda(s,\lambda)\big).

(78)

The function
\Lambda(s,\lambda)
provides an effective description of the interaction strength in a QFT depending on the position on the RG flow. In order to solve the above equation we must provide a boundary condition. We require that at some point of the flow
s_{0}
the characteristic
\Lambda(s,\lambda)
simply reduces to
\lambda
, namely

\Lambda(s=s_{0},\lambda)=\lambda.

(79)

We can solve (
78
) with the boundary condition (
79
) by using the explicit form of the
\beta
-function given in (
62
). The result reads as

\Lambda(s,\lambda)=\lambda(s/s_{0})^{\delta}\times\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\,\big((s/s_{0})^{\delta}-1\big)},

(80)

where the constant
\lambda_{\star}
is given in (
61
). The IR (
s\rightarrow\infty
) and the UV (
s\rightarrow 0
) limits of the running coupling read

\Lambda(\infty,\lambda)=\lambda_{\star},\qquad\Lambda(0,\lambda)=0.

(81)

This is in a perfect agreement with the statements made in subsection
3.1.1
.

The standard solution of the Callan-Symanzik equation (
76
) reads

f_{2}(s,\lambda)=f_{2}^{\text{boundary}}\big(s_{0},\Lambda(s,\lambda)\big)\times\exp\left(-2\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\gamma\big(\Lambda(s^{\prime},\lambda)\big)\right),

(82)

where
f_{2}^{\text{boundary}}(s,\lambda)
is the boundary condition. In practice, we use the perturbative result (
69
) as the boundary condition. We verify the validity of the solution (
82
) in appendix
D
for completeness.

Let us now compute the exponent

\exp\left(-n\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\gamma\big(\Lambda(s^{\prime},\lambda)\big)\right)

(83)

appearing in the solution (
82
) with
n=2
. The anomalous dimension has been obtained in (
59
), it reads

\gamma(\lambda)=\frac{2\delta}{\lambda_{\star}}\,\lambda(\mu)+O(\lambda^{2}).

(84)

This approximation is enough since the
O(\lambda^{2})
will be sub-sub-leading in the evaluation of the above exponent. Plugging (
84
) into (
83
) and using (
80
) one obtains

\exp\left(-n\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\gamma\big(\Lambda(s^{\prime},\lambda)\big)\right)=\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\,\big((s/s_{0})^{\delta}-1\big)}\right)^{2n}.

(85)

Using (
82
) together with (
68
) and (
69
) we simply get

F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{r^{2\Delta}}\times\left(1-2\Lambda(\mu r,\lambda)\,C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}\log(s_{0})\right)\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\,\big((\mu r/s_{0})^{\delta}-1\big)}\right)^{4}.

(86)

We can now fix the value of
s_{0}
by recalling the renormalization condition (
46
) on the two-point function. The above result agrees with (
46
) only after setting

s_{0}=1.

(87)

Taking this into account we obtain the final solution

\boxed{F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{r^{2\Delta}}\times\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\,\big((\mu r)^{\delta}-1\big)}\right)^{4}.\ }

(88)

IR limit

In the IR limit
r\rightarrow\infty
for the two-point function we expect to recover conformal symmetry, namely

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{2\gamma_{\mathbf{O}}}F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{N_{IR}^{2}}{r^{2\Delta_{IR}}},

(89)

where the IR anomalous dimension for the operator
\mathbf{O}
reads as

\gamma_{\mathbf{O}}\equiv\Delta_{IR}-\Delta.

(90)

Taking the limit inside our explicit expressions (
88
) we get

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mu^{4\delta}F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{r^{2(\Delta+2\delta)}}\times\left(\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{\lambda}\right)^{4}.

(91)

Comparing these with our expectation (
89
) we get

\Delta_{IR}=\Delta+2\delta,\qquad\gamma_{\mathbf{O}}=2\delta,\qquad N_{IR}=\left(\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{\lambda}\right)^{2}.

(92)

The second entry in the above expressions is expected from (
84
), since
\gamma_{\mathbf{O}}=\gamma(\lambda_{\star})
.

Unrenormalized two-point function

Using (
88
) we can also obtain the two-point function of un-renormalized operators. It reads

\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=Z(\mu)F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1-\frac{4\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}}{r^{2\Delta}}\times\left(\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{\lambda_{\star}+\lambda\,\left((r\mu)^{\delta}-1\right)}\right)^{4}.

(93)

Here we have used (
44
), (
55
) and (
61
). We can now plug here the expression for
\lambda(\mu)
in terms of
\lambda_{0}
given by (
53
). The result will be independent on
\mu
up to
O(\lambda_{0}^{2})
order. Using this independence we can set
\mu
to any value we like. One convenient choice is to set
\mu=m
. Taking this into account we can write

\boxed{\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1-\frac{4\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{\star}}}{r^{2\Delta}}\times\left(\frac{\lambda_{\star}}{\lambda_{\star}+\lambda_{0}\,\left((rm)^{\delta}-1\right)}\right)^{4}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}).}

(94)

In the UV limit we get

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{r^{2\Delta}}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}).

(95)

In agreement with our expectations at the order in
\lambda_{0}
we are working at.

Further discussion

In order to develop a better understanding of our result (
88
) let us expand it around small coupling
\lambda
. We get then

F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{r^{2(d-\delta)}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{nn}\lambda^{n}q(\mu r)^{n},

(96)

where the coefficient
c_{nn}
reads as

c_{nn}\equiv\frac{(n+3)!}{3!n!}(-1)^{n}

(97)

and the function
q(\mu r)
is given by

q(\mu r)\equiv\frac{(\mu r)^{\delta}-1}{\lambda_{\star}}=\frac{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}}{2}\frac{(\mu r)^{\delta}-1}{\delta}=\frac{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}}{2}\log(\mu r)+O(\delta).

(98)

From conformal perturbation theory it is clear, however, that the most general expression of the two-point function has the following series representation

F_{2}(r,\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{r^{2(d-\delta)}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}c_{nm}\lambda^{n}\left(\log(\mu r)\right)^{m}+O(\delta),

(99)

where
n
is the order of conformal perturbation theory and
m\leq n
for each n. Comparing (
96
) with the expectation (
99
) and taking into account that
q(\mu r)\sim\log(\mu r)
, we conclude that
our result (
88
) sums only the “leading-log” contributions
\lambda^{n}\log^{n}
and completely misses contributions of the form
\lambda^{n}\log^{m}
with
n\geq 2
and
m<n
.

3.4
Solution of the Callan-Symanzik equation: three-point function

Let us now obtain the most general solution of the 3-point Callan-Symanzik equation (
77
). The discussion here remains almost identical to the previous section.

The characteristic equation for (
77
) reads as

\sigma\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}\Lambda(\sigma,\lambda)=-\beta\big(\Lambda(\sigma,\lambda)\big),

(100)

together with the boundary condition

\Lambda(\sigma=\sigma_{0},\lambda)=\lambda.

(101)

The most general solution of (
77
) is then given by

f_{3}(\sigma,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda)=f_{3}^{\text{boundary}}\big(\sigma_{0},y_{1},y_{2},\Lambda(\sigma,\lambda)\big)\times\exp\left(-3\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{\sigma}\frac{d\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma^{\prime}}\gamma\big(\Lambda(\sigma^{\prime},\lambda)\big)\right),

(102)

where
f_{3}^{\text{boundary}}(\sigma,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda)
is the boundary condition. In practice, we will use the perturbative result (
71
) as this boundary condition. Using the results of the previous section we can write

\boxed{F_{3}(\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\mu,\lambda)=\frac{1}{\rho^{3\Delta}}\times C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\times\left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\,\big((\mu\rho/\sigma_{0})^{\delta}-1\big)}\right)^{6}.}

(103)

Without loss of generality we can set
\sigma_{0}=1
.

4
Conserved currents

Let us assume that our UV CFT has a U(1) global symmetry and the associated Abelian current is
J^{\mu}(x)
. The goal of this section is to compute the following QFT correlation functions along the RG flow

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}},\qquad\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}.

(104)

4.1
Two-point function

The CFT two-point function has the following simple form

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{CFT}}=\frac{C_{J}}{r^{2(d-1)}}\times\left(\delta^{\mu\nu}-2\,\frac{x_{12}^{\mu}x_{12}^{\nu}}{r^{2}}\right),

(105)

where
C_{J}>0
is called the current central charge. Its value is part of the CFT data. Using dimensional analysis one can conclude that the most general current two-point function in weakly relevant flows has the following form

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{r^{2(d-1)}}\times\left(h_{1}(mr,\lambda_{0})\delta^{\mu\nu}+h_{2}(mr,\lambda_{0})\frac{x_{12}^{\mu}x_{12}^{\nu}}{r^{2}}\right),

(106)

where
h_{i}(mr,\lambda_{0})
are dimensionless functions. In what follows we will compute these functions using conformal perturbation theory. In order to reproduce the UV and IR fixed points these functions must obey the following asymptotic conditions

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}h_{1}(mr,\lambda_{0})=C_{J}^{UV},\quad\,\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}h_{2}(mr,\lambda_{0})=-2C_{J}^{UV},

(107)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}h_{1}(mr,\lambda_{0})=C_{J}^{IR},\quad\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}h_{2}(mr,\lambda_{0})=-2C_{J}^{IR}.

Conservation of the current
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)=0
implies the following differential equation

t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(h_{1}(t,\lambda_{0})+h_{2}(t,\lambda_{0})\right)=(d-1)\times\left(2h_{1}(t,\lambda_{0})+h_{2}(t,\lambda_{0})\right),

(108)

where
t\equiv mr
.

Conformal perturbation theory

Analogously to (
27
) using conformal perturbation theory we can write

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}\\
-\lambda_{0}m^{d-\Delta}\int d^{d}x_{3}\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}).

(109)

The index-free form of the UV CFT correlator entering the integral in (
109
) reads as

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}=\frac{C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{d-1-\frac{\Delta}{2}}\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta}{2}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta}{2}}}\times\\
\left(-\left(\delta^{\mu\nu}-2\,\frac{x^{\mu}_{12}x^{\nu}_{12}}{x_{12}^{2}}\right)+\frac{\Delta}{d-1-\Delta}x_{12}^{2}\left(\frac{x^{\mu}_{12}}{x_{12}^{2}}-\frac{x^{\mu}_{13}}{x_{13}^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{x^{\nu}_{12}}{x_{12}^{2}}+\frac{x^{\nu}_{23}}{x_{23}^{2}}\right)\right).

(110)

Here
C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}
is the OPE coefficient of two currents and one scalar. Both (
105
) and (
110
) obey the conservation condition
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}(x)=0
. As a result the left-hand side of (
109
) also obeys it.

In appendix
A
we obtain the following result

\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{a_{1}+a_{2}-d/2}\int d^{d}x_{3}\frac{1}{\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{a_{1}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{a_{2}}}=P_{2}(a_{1},a_{2}),

(111)

where the numerical coefficient
P_{2}
is given by (
250
). Let us now plug (
105
), (
106
) and (
110
) into (
109
). Contracting the obtained expression with
\delta^{\mu\nu}
and with
x_{12}^{\mu}x_{12}^{\nu}
respectively, and using (
11
) and (
111
) we obtain

d\,h_{1}^{\text{pert}}(mr,\lambda_{0})+h_{2}^{\text{pert}}(mr,\lambda_{0})=(d-2)C_{J}\\
-\lambda_{0}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}(mr)^{d-\Delta}\,\left(\frac{\Delta}{2(d-1-\Delta)}A-(d-2)P_{2}(\Delta/2,\Delta/2)\right)+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}),

(112)

and

h_{1}^{\text{pert}}(mr,\lambda_{0})+h_{2}^{\text{pert}}(mr,\lambda_{0})=-C_{J}\\
-\lambda_{0}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}(mr)^{d-\Delta}\,\left(\frac{\Delta}{4(d-1-\Delta)}A-\frac{2-2d+\Delta}{2(d-1-\Delta)}P_{2}(\Delta/2,\Delta/2)\right)+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}),

(113)

where the coefficient
A
reads as

A\equiv P_{2}(\Delta/2-1,\Delta/2+1)+P_{2}(\Delta/2+1,\Delta/2-1)+P_{2}(\Delta/2+1,\Delta/2+1)\\
-2P_{2}(\Delta/2+1,\Delta/2)-2P_{2}(\Delta/2,\Delta/2+1).

Plugging here the explicit values of
P_{2}
we get

\displaystyle h^{\text{pert}}_{1}(mr,\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=C_{J}-\lambda_{0}(mr)^{d-\Delta}(\Delta-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}),

(115)

\displaystyle h^{\text{pert}}_{2}(mr,\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=-2C_{J}+\lambda_{0}(mr)^{d-\Delta}(\Delta+d-2)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}),

where the coefficient

E_{JJ}\equiv\frac{2^{\Delta-d}\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\Delta}{(\Delta-d+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\Delta-d/2)}{\Gamma(\Delta/2+1)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+2-\Delta}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1-\Delta}{2}\right)}\,\overset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{=}\,\frac{2\Omega_{d-1}}{d}+O(\delta).

(116)

The solution (

108
).

Renormalized coupling

In terms of the renormalized coupling
\lambda
the decomposition (

) can be written as

\boxed{\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{r^{2(d-1)}}\times\left(h_{1}(r\mu,\lambda)\delta^{\mu\nu}+h_{2}(r\mu,\lambda)\frac{x_{12}^{\mu}x_{12}^{\nu}}{r^{2}}\right),\ }

(117)

where the perturbative expressions for the functions

h^{\text{pert}}_{1}(r\mu,\lambda)=C_{J}-\lambda\,(r\mu)^{d-\Delta}(\Delta-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}+O(\lambda^{2}),\\
h^{\text{pert}}_{2}(r\mu,\lambda)=-2C_{J}+\lambda\,(r\mu)^{d-\Delta}(\Delta+d-2)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}+O(\lambda^{2}).\hskip 42.67912pt

(118)

Callan-Symanzik equation

The two-point function of conserved currents does not depend on the renormalization scale
\mu
as can be seen from (
106
). Thus, we can write

\frac{d}{d\mu}\langle J^{\alpha}(x_{1})J^{\beta}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=0.

(119)

Plugging here the decomposition (
117
), we obtain a pair of the Callan-Symanzik equations

s\equiv r\mu:\qquad\left(s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+\beta(\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\right)h_{i}(s,\lambda)=0.

(120)

The goal now is to solve the pair of equations (
120
) together with the conservation equation (
108
), which can be simply rewritten as

s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(h_{1}(s,\lambda)+h_{2}(s,\lambda)\right)=(d-1)\,\left(2h_{1}(s,\lambda)+h_{2}(s,\lambda)\right).

(121)

Using the most general solution of the Callan-Symanzik equation for the scalar two-point function (
82
), we can write

h_{i}(s,\lambda)=h_{i}^{\text{boundary}}\big(1,\Lambda(s,\lambda)\big).

(122)

Notice, that this expression is so simple since conserved currents have zero anomalous dimension.
Using the perturbative result (
118
) as the boundary condition we conclude that

\hskip 56.9055pth_{1}(s,\lambda)=C_{J}-(\Delta-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\times\Lambda(s,\lambda),\\
h_{2}(s,\lambda)=-2C_{J}+(\Delta+d-2)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\times\Lambda(s,\lambda).\hskip 85.35826pt

(123)

Plugging this solution into the conservation equation (
121
) we can see, however, that it is satisfied only up to the order
O(\lambda)
. In the reminder of this section we carefully discuss the issue of conservation.

Series representation

The result (
123
) can be compactly written as

h_{i}(s,\lambda)=a_{i}+b_{i}\Lambda(s,\lambda),

(124)

where the coefficients
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
read as

a_{2}=-2a_{1}=-2C_{J},\qquad b_{1}=-(\Delta-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}},\qquad b_{2}=(\Delta+d-2)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}.

(125)

Let us expand this expression around small values of
\lambda
in the same way as was done in the end of section
3.3
. The expansion of the “running coupling” (
15
) reads as

\Lambda(s,\lambda)=\lambda s^{\delta}\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,\lambda^{n}q(s)^{n}=\lambda\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}\lambda^{n}(\log s)^{n}+O(\delta),

(126)

where
n
denotes the order in conformal perturbation theory, the function
q(s)
was defined in (
98
) and the coefficient
c_{n}
reads as

c_{n}=(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}}{2}\right)^{n}.

(127)

As we have already mentioned above, the solution (
124
) obeys the conservation condition (
121
) only up to
O(\lambda)
order. We can better understand the source of the conservation breaking by plugging (
124
) together with (
126
) into the conservation condition (
121
). The left-hand side (LHS) and right-hand side (RHS) of the conservation condition (
121
) become

\displaystyle\text{LHS}

\displaystyle=\lambda(d-1)\frac{2\Omega_{d-1}}{d}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc_{n}\lambda^{n}(\log s)^{n-1}+O(\delta),

(128)

\displaystyle\text{RHS}

\displaystyle=\delta\times\lambda(d-1)\frac{2\Omega_{d-1}}{d}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\,\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}\lambda^{n}(\log s)^{n}+O(\delta^{2}).

The conservation condition (
121
) demands the equality of LHS and RHS above order by order in
n
. However, the equality of LHS and RHS in (
128
) is clearly violated, since at order
\lambda^{n+1}
the RHS contains only the
\delta\times\log^{n}
terms and misses the
\delta^{0}\times\log^{n-1}
terms present in the LHS.

Modified solution

Let us, nevertheless, propose a procedure which could be used to obtain a modified version of the solution (
123
), which obeys conservation at all orders in
\lambda
.

One could plug the solution (
122
) without specifying the boundary conditions into the conservation condition (
121
). The conservation condition then translates into the constraint on the boundary conditions.
We solve this constraint in appendix
F
using the expression (
123
) for the “running coupling”. The result reads

\displaystyle h_{1}(s,\lambda)

\displaystyle=C_{J}-(\Delta-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\times\Lambda(s,\lambda),

\displaystyle h_{2}(s,\lambda)

\displaystyle=-2C_{J}+(\Delta-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}

(129)

\displaystyle\times\left((\Lambda(s,\lambda)+\lambda_{\star})+(\Lambda(s,\lambda)-\lambda_{\star})\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1,1,\frac{1-d+\delta}{\delta},\frac{\Lambda(s,\lambda)}{\lambda_{\star}}\right)\right).

It is straightforward to check explicitly that this solution obeys the conservation equation (
121
). One could interpret the solution (
129
) as an alternative to (
123
) re-summation. The solutions (
123
) and (
129
) match up to
O(\lambda)
terms.

Unfortunately, the expression (
129
) is not well behaved along the whole RG flow, for instance
h_{2}(s,\lambda)
diverges at
s=\infty
, where
\Lambda(\infty,s)=\lambda_{\star}
. We hope, however, that if we worked at one higher order in conformal perturbation theory, which corrects the first line of (
129
) by providing access to
\lambda^{2}\log^{1}
terms as discussed above, we would have been able to obtain a modified version of (
129
), which is conserved and finite in the IR limit
s=\infty
.

Let us also make another comment on our result (
129
). Recall the series representation of the hypergoemtric function

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b,c,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

(130)

valid for any complex
z
in the disk
|z|<1
. We could blindly use this representation inside the second line of (
129
) up to (and including) the order
\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda_{\star}}\right)^{N}
, where
N\geq 1
.
Then we can explicitly check that in this order of approximation (
129
) satisfy the conservation condition (
121
) up to the order
O(\lambda^{N+1})
. Such a truncated expression will be valid in the IR limit
s\rightarrow\infty
and provide the Callan-Symanzik solution with the improved conservation properties.

4.2
Current central charge
C_{J}

Let us evaluate the functions (

) in the IR. Taking into account that
\lambda_{\star}\propto\delta
we have

\displaystyle h_{1}(\infty,\lambda)

\displaystyle=C_{J}-(d-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\lambda_{\star}+O(\delta^{2}),

(131)

\displaystyle h_{2}(\infty,\lambda)

\displaystyle=-2C_{J}+2(d-1)E_{JJ}C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\lambda_{\star}+O(\delta^{2}).

Comparing the above equations we conclude that

Using (

4.3
Three-point function

Let us now consider the three-point function in (

We can construct tensor structures from the following ingredients only

\delta^{\mu\nu},\quad\frac{x_{12}^{\mu}}{\rho},\quad\frac{x_{23}^{\mu}}{\rho},\quad\frac{x_{13}^{\mu}}{\rho}.

Using them we can write the following decomposition into tensor structures

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{\rho^{2(d-1)+\Delta}}\times\bigg(h_{1}(m\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda_{0})\delta^{\mu\nu}+h_{2}(m\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda_{0})\frac{x_{12}^{\mu}x_{12}^{\nu}}{\rho^{2}}\\

At the trivial order of conformal perturbation theory we simply have

\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}+O(\lambda_{0}).

Using the explicit expression (

) and comparing it with (

\displaystyle h^{\text{pert}}_{1}(m\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle h^{\text{pert}}_{2}(m\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=a\,C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\,\left(\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{2}}\right)^{\frac{-d+\Delta}{3}}+(\rho m)^{d-\Delta}O(\lambda_{0}),

\displaystyle h^{\text{pert}}_{3}(m\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=b\,C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\,y_{1}\left(\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{2}}\right)^{\frac{-d+\Delta}{3}}+(\rho m)^{d-\Delta}O(\lambda_{0}),

\displaystyle h^{\text{pert}}_{4}(m\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=-b\,C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\,\frac{y_{1}}{y_{2}}\left(\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{2}}\right)^{\frac{-d+\Delta}{3}}+(\rho m)^{d-\Delta}O(\lambda_{0}),

\displaystyle h^{\text{pert}}_{5}(m\rho,y_{1},y_{2},\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=-b\,C_{JJ\mathcal{O}}\,\left(\frac{y_{1}^{2}}{y_{2}}\right)^{1+\frac{-d+\Delta}{3}}+(\rho m)^{d-\Delta}O(\lambda_{0}),

where we have defined

Renormalized three-point function

\displaystyle\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}

\displaystyle=Z(\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle J^{\mu}(x_{1})J^{\nu}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}

Three-points

5
Stress-tensor

Let us now consider the QFT stress-tensor

5.1
Two-point function

The most general form of the stress-tensor two-point function in weakly relevant flows can be written as

\langle T_{\text{QFT}}^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T_{\text{QFT}}^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\frac{1}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{5}h_{i}(mr,\lambda_{0})\mathbb{T}_{i}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(x_{12}),

(151)

where
h_{i}(mr,\lambda_{0})
are dimensionless functions and the basis of five tensor structures can be chosen as in
[
43
]
, namely

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{1}^{abcd}(x_{12})\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{x_{12}^{a}x_{12}^{b}x_{12}^{c}x_{12}^{d}}{r^{4}},

(152)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{2}^{abcd}(x_{12})\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{x_{12}^{a}x_{12}^{b}\delta^{cd}+x_{12}^{c}x_{12}^{d}\delta^{ab}}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{3}^{abcd}(x_{12})\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{x_{12}^{a}x_{12}^{c}\delta^{bd}+x_{12}^{b}x_{12}^{c}\delta^{ad}+x_{12}^{a}x_{12}^{d}\delta^{bc}+x_{12}^{b}x_{12}^{d}\delta^{ac}}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{4}^{abcd}(x_{12})\equiv

\displaystyle\delta^{ab}\delta^{cd}

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{5}^{abcd}(x_{12})\equiv

\displaystyle\delta^{ac}\delta^{bd}+\delta^{bc}\delta^{ad}.

Let us split the two-point function using (
149
) in the following way

\langle T_{\text{QFT}}^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T_{\text{QFT}}^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\langle T^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}\\
+\lambda_{0}m^{\delta}\Big(\langle\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}+\langle T^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}\Big)+O(\lambda_{0}^{2}).

(153)

Using conformal perturbation theory, analogously to (
27

\langle T_{\text{QFT}}^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T_{\text{QFT}}^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\langle T^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}\\
-m^{\delta}\lambda_{0}\,\int d^{d}x_{3}\langle T^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}+O\big(\lambda_{0}^{2}\big).

(154)

Here we used the fact that CFT two-point functions of two different operators vanish, in particular

\langle\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}(x_{1})T^{\rho\sigma}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}=0.

\displaystyle h_{5}(mr,\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,\left(h_{2}(mr,\lambda_{0})+d\,h_{4}(mr,\lambda_{0})\right)+O\big(\lambda_{0}^{2}\big).

Basic integrals

In this appendix we will be concerned with the following integrals

\mathbb{I}_{n}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\equiv\int d^{d}y\frac{1}{\left((x_{1}-y)^{2}\right)^{a_{1}}\ldots\left((x_{n}-y)^{2}\right)^{a_{n}}},

(239)

where
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
\ldots
,
a_{n}
are some real parameters.
The integral (
239
) has
n
potential (UV) divergences when
y
coincides with
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,
\ldots
or
x_{n}
. These divergences are absent if

a_{1}<d/2,\quad a_{2}<d/2,\quad\ldots,\quad a_{n}<d/2.

(240)

When
|y|
is large the integral (
239
) could have an (IR) divergence. This is absent if

a_{1}+a_{2}+\ldots+a_{n}>d/2.

(241)

In what follows we will evaluate the integral (
239
) focusing on the case of
n=2
and
n=3
. We will assume the parameter range for
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
\ldots
,
a_{n}
when there are no divergences. The final result, however, can be analytically continued beyond the original parameter range.

In order to write equations in a compact form we will use the following notation

x_{i(n+1)}^{2}\equiv(x_{i}-x_{n+1})^{2}.

(242)

We will use the
n
-point Feynman parametrization, which reads

\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i(n+1)}^{2}\right)^{a_{i}}}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(a_{i})}\times\\
\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{n}\,\delta\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}-1\right)\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}^{a_{i}-1}}{\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}x_{i(n+1)}^{2}\right)^{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}}.

(243)

In what follows we will also use the following result of integration in
d
-dimensional Euclidean space

\displaystyle\int d^{d}x\frac{1}{\left((x-y)^{2}+\Delta\right)^{\alpha}}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\ \Gamma\left(\alpha-\frac{d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\ \frac{1}{\Delta^{\alpha-\frac{d}{2}}},

(244)

where
y
is
d-
vector and
\Delta
is some scalar parameter.

Two-points

Let us consider the
n=2
case, namely

\mathbb{I}_{2}^{(a_{1},a_{2})}(x_{1},x_{2})\equiv\int d^{d}x_{3}\frac{1}{\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{a_{1}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{a_{2}}}.

(245)

Using the Feynman parametrization (
243
), the above integral can be re-written as

\mathbb{I}_{2}^{(a_{1},a_{2})}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{\Gamma(a_{1}+a_{2})}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi\ \xi^{a_{1}-1}(1-\xi)^{a_{2}-1}\int d^{d}x_{3}\frac{1}{\left(\xi x_{13}^{2}+(1-\xi)x_{23}^{2}\right)^{a_{1}+a_{2}}}.

(246)

The denominator of the integrand can be expressed as

\displaystyle\xi x_{13}^{2}+(1-\xi)x_{23}^{2}=\left(x_{3}-(\xi x_{1}+(1-\xi)x_{2})\right)^{2}+\xi(1-\xi)x_{12}^{2}.

(247)

Substituting the above expression in
\mathbb{I}_{2}
and using the result of integration (
244
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{2}^{(a_{1},a_{2})}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\left(a_{1}+a_{2}-\frac{d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})}\ \frac{1}{(x_{12}^{2})^{a_{1}+a_{2}-\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi\ \xi^{\frac{d}{2}-a_{2}-1}(1-\xi)^{\frac{d}{2}-a_{1}-1}.

(248)

Now performing the integration over
\xi
assuming
a_{1},a_{2}\leq\frac{d}{2}
, we get

\mathbb{I}_{2}^{(a_{1},a_{2})}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{P_{2}(a_{1},a_{2})}{(x_{12}^{2})^{a_{1}+a_{2}-d/2}},

(249)

where we have

P_{2}(a_{1},a_{2})\equiv\pi^{d/2}\ \frac{\Gamma\left(d/2-a_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(d/2-a_{2}\right)}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})}\ \frac{\Gamma\left(a_{1}+a_{2}-d/2\right)}{\Gamma(d-a_{1}-a_{2})}.

(250)

Three-points

Let us now consider the
n=3
case, namely

\mathbb{I}^{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})}_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\equiv\int d^{d}x_{4}\frac{1}{\left(x_{14}^{2}\right)^{a_{1}}\left(x_{24}^{2}\right)^{a_{2}}\left(x_{34}^{2}\right)^{a_{3}}}.

(251)

Using the Feynman parametrization (
243
), we can re-write the above integral as

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}^{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})}_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}\right)}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})\Gamma(a_{3})}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{2}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{3}\ \delta(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}-1)

\displaystyle\times\xi_{1}^{a_{1}-1}\xi_{2}^{a_{2}-1}\xi_{3}^{a_{3}-1}\int d^{d}x_{4}\frac{1}{\left(\xi_{1}x_{14}^{2}+\xi_{2}x_{24}^{2}+\xi_{3}x_{34}^{2}\right)^{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}}}.

(252)

Now we can also re-write the denominator of the integrand as

\left(\xi_{1}x_{14}^{2}+\xi_{2}x_{24}^{2}+\xi_{3}x_{34}^{2}\right)=\left(x_{4}-(\xi_{1}x_{1}+\xi_{2}x_{2}+\xi_{3}x_{3})\right)^{2}+\xi_{1}\xi_{2}x_{12}^{2}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}x_{23}^{2}+\xi_{3}\xi_{1}x_{31}^{2},

(253)

under the constraint
\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}=1
. Using (
244
), we then obtain

\mathbb{I}^{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})}_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\left(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}-\frac{d}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})\Gamma(a_{3})}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{1}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{2}\int_{0}^{1}d\xi_{3}\ \delta(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}-1)\\
\times\xi_{1}^{a_{1}-1}\xi_{2}^{a_{2}-1}\xi_{3}^{a_{3}-1}\left(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}x_{12}^{2}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}x_{23}^{2}+\xi_{3}\xi_{1}x_{31}^{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3})}.

(254)

A more useful representation of the integral (
251
) for practical purposes is found by using the Schwinger parametrization. This was done in
[
110
]
, their result reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}^{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})}_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\ 2^{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}-d}}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})\Gamma(a_{3})\Gamma(d-a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3})}\frac{1}{(x_{12}^{2})^{\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}}\frac{1}{(x_{23}^{2})^{\frac{a_{2}+a_{3}}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}}\frac{1}{(x_{31}^{2})^{\frac{a_{3}+a_{1}}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\ t^{\frac{d-4}{4}}\ \mathbf{K}_{\frac{d}{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}}\left(\sqrt{2tx_{12}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{K}_{\frac{d}{2}-a_{2}-a_{3}}\left(\sqrt{2tx_{23}^{2}}\right)\mathbf{K}_{\frac{d}{2}-a_{3}-a_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2tx_{31}^{2}}\right),

(255)

where
\mathbf{K}_{\nu}(z)
represents the generalized Bessel function and its integral representation in the region
|\text{arg}(z)|<\frac{\pi}{4}
is given by

\mathbf{K}_{\nu}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\nu}\int_{0}^{\infty}du\ u^{-\nu-1}\ e^{-u-\frac{z^{2}}{4u}}.

(256)

It is hard to rewrite our result (
255
) in terms of some known analytic functions. However, in the special case when
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=d
, the integral becomes conformal and has been evaluate in appendix B of
[
111
]
. The result reads

a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=d:\qquad\mathbb{I}^{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})}_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\frac{P_{3}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{d/2-a_{3}}\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{d/2-a_{2}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{d/2-a_{1}}},

(257)

where the coefficient
P_{3}
is given by

P_{3}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\equiv\pi^{d/2}\ \frac{\Gamma(d/2-a_{1})\Gamma(d/2-a_{2})\Gamma(d/2-a_{3})}{\Gamma(a_{1})\Gamma(a_{2})\Gamma(a_{3})}.

(258)

Appendix B
OPE expansion

In conformal field theories the product of two operators can be written in the following form

\mathcal{O}_{1}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}_{2}(x_{2})=\sum_{\mathcal{O}}C_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}\textbf{C}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})+\ldots,

(259)

known as the OPE expansion. Here
\ldots
indicate contributions of operators with spin. For simplicity let us only consider the contribution of scalar operators. Here
C_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}
is the OPE coefficient and
\textbf{C}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}})
is a function which is completely fixed by conformal invariance.

Let us multiply (
259
) by
\mathcal{O}(x_{3})
, take vacuum expectation value and use the above expressions for the two- and three-point functions. We get then

\frac{1}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-\Delta}{2}}\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+\Delta}{2}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\frac{-\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\Delta}{2}}}=\textbf{C}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}})\frac{1}{\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\Delta}}.

(260)

From this expression we can systematically compute the function
\textbf{C}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}})
. We can first write

\textbf{C}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}})=\frac{1}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-\Delta}{2}}}\textbf{c}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}}).

(261)

Then the condition (
260
) can be rewritten in terms of the small
c
-function, it reads

\frac{1}{\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta+\Delta_{12}}{2}}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta-\Delta_{12}}{2}}}=\textbf{c}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}})\frac{1}{\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\Delta}}.

(262)

This condition (
262
) can be solved in a series form. Its most general expression reads as

\textbf{c}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{2}\mathcal{O}}(x_{12},\partial_{x_{2}})=1+t^{1}_{\mu_{1}}\partial^{\mu_{1}}_{x_{2}}+t^{2}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\partial^{\mu_{1}}_{x_{2}}\partial^{\mu_{2}}_{x_{2}}+t^{3}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}}\partial^{\mu_{1}}_{x_{2}}\partial^{\mu_{2}}_{x_{2}}\partial^{\mu_{3}}_{x_{2}}+\ldots,

(263)

where tensors
t_{n}
have the following form

\displaystyle t^{1}_{\mu_{1}}

\displaystyle=c^{1}_{1}x_{12}^{\mu_{1}},

(264)

\displaystyle t^{2}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}

\displaystyle=c^{2}_{1}\left(x_{12}^{\mu_{1}}x_{12}^{\mu_{2}}\right)+c^{2}_{2}\left(x_{12}^{2}\delta^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}\right),

\displaystyle t^{3}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}}

\displaystyle=c^{3}_{1}\left(x_{12}^{\mu_{1}}x_{12}^{\mu_{2}}x_{12}^{\mu_{3}}\right)+c^{3}_{2}\left(x_{12}^{2}\delta^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}x_{12}^{\mu_{3}}\right),

\displaystyle t^{4}_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}

\displaystyle=c^{4}_{1}\left(x_{12}^{\mu_{1}}x_{12}^{\mu_{2}}x_{12}^{\mu_{3}}x_{12}^{\mu_{4}}\right)+c^{4}_{2}\left(x_{12}^{2}\delta^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}x_{12}^{\mu_{3}}x_{12}^{\mu_{4}}\right)+c^{4}_{3}\left(x_{12}^{2}\delta^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}x_{12}^{2}\delta^{\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}\right),

\displaystyle\ldots

For the special case
\Delta_{12}=0
the coefficients
c^{n}_{i}
can be written in a compact form as follows

c^{n}_{i}=\frac{2^{\Delta+1-2i}(-1)^{i+1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\Delta-d/2+1\right)\Gamma\left(\Delta/2+i-1\right)}{\Gamma\left(i\right)\Gamma\left(\Delta/2\right)\Gamma\left(\Delta-d/2+i\right)}\times\\
\frac{\Gamma\left(\Delta/2+n+1-i\right)\Gamma\left(\Delta/2+1/2\right)}{\Gamma\left(n+3-2i\right)\Gamma\left(\Delta+n\right)}.

(265)

Appendix C
Trace of the stress-tensor

Let us re-write our weakly relevant RG flow action (
1
) placed in curved background

A=A_{\text{UV CFT}}[\Phi,g_{\mu\nu}]+\lambda_{0}m^{d-\Delta}\int d^{d}x\sqrt{g}\,\mathcal{O}_{g}(x),

(266)

where
\Phi
collectively represents the d.o.f of the UV CFT.
Under Weyl transformation with infinitesimal Weyl transformation parameter
\sigma(x)
we have the following transformation rules

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)\rightarrow e^{2\sigma(x)}g_{\mu\nu}(x)\ ,\ \Phi(x)\rightarrow\Phi_{\sigma}(x)\ ,\ \mathcal{O}_{g}(x)\rightarrow e^{-\Delta\sigma(x)}\mathcal{O}_{g}(x),

(267)

and
A_{\text{UV CFT}}
is Weyl invariant i.e.
A_{\text{UV CFT}}[\Phi_{\sigma},e^{2\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}]=A_{\text{UV CFT}}[\Phi,g_{\mu\nu}]
. Now the trace of stress tensor for the QFT (
266
) is given by

\displaystyle T_{\text{QFT}}{}^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x)\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}(x)}\frac{\delta A}{\delta\sigma(x)}\Bigg|_{g_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}}=(d-\Delta)\lambda_{0}m^{d-\Delta}\mathcal{O}(x).

(268)

On the other hand at scale
\mu
the effective weakly relevant RG flow action (
40
) reads

A=A_{\text{EFT}}+\lambda(\mu)\mu^{d-\Delta}\int d^{d}x\,\mathbf{O}(x).

(269)

Here
A_{\text{EFT}}
is assumed to be scale invariant. The scale transformation is given by

\displaystyle x^{\mu}\rightarrow e^{\alpha}x^{\mu}\ ,\ \mu\rightarrow e^{-\alpha}\mu\ ,\ \mathbf{O}(x)\rightarrow e^{-\alpha\Delta}\mathbf{O}(x),

(270)

where
\alpha
is the infinitesimal scale transformation parameter. Hence under the above scale transformation rule, the infinitesimal scale variation of (
269
) turns out

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}A=-\alpha\ \beta(\lambda)\mu^{d-\Delta}\int d^{d}x\,\mathbf{O}(x),

(271)

where the expression for
\beta(\lambda)
is given in (
48
). We know that the scale variation of an action is related to the divergence of the dilatation symmetry current
J^{\mu}_{D}(x)=T_{\text{QFT}}^{\mu\nu}(x)x_{\nu}
and the precise relation reads

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}A=\alpha\int d^{d}x\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{D}(x)=\alpha\int d^{d}x\ T_{\text{QFT}}{}^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x),

(272)

where to get the expression after second equality we used the conservation of stress tensor. Now comparing (
271
) and (
272
) we get

\displaystyle T_{\text{QFT}}{}^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x)=-\beta(\lambda)\mu^{d-\Delta}\mathbf{O}(x).

(273)

Appendix D
Solution of the Callan-Symanzik equation: details

By using the definition of the
\beta
-function (
48
) one can re-write the right-hand side of (
78
) in the following way

\beta\big(\Lambda(s,\lambda)\big)=\beta(\lambda)\frac{\partial\Lambda(s,\lambda)}{\partial\lambda}.

(274)

Let us check explicitly that (
82
) indeed satisfies (
76
). Let us first evaluate the derivative in
s
of the above result. We get

s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}f_{2}(s,\lambda)=-\beta(\lambda)\,\frac{\partial f_{2}^{\text{boundary}}\big(1,\Lambda\big)}{\partial\lambda}\times\exp(\ldots)-2\gamma\big(\Lambda\big)f_{2}(s,\lambda).

(275)

In order to obtain the first term in the right-hand side we first used (
78
) and then (
274
).
In order to proceed let us first re-write the solution (
82
) in the following form

f_{2}(s,\lambda)=f_{2}^{\text{boundary}}\big(1,\Lambda(s,\lambda)\big)\times\exp\left(+2\int_{\Lambda(1,\lambda)}^{\Lambda(s,\lambda)}d\Lambda^{\prime}\,\frac{\gamma\big(\Lambda^{\prime}\big)}{\beta\big(\Lambda^{\prime}\big)}\right),

(276)

where we simply used (
78
). This expression is more convenient for taking the derivative in
\lambda
, which reads then

\beta(\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}f_{2}(s,\lambda)=\beta(\lambda)\,\frac{\partial f_{2}^{\text{boundary}}\big(1,\Lambda\big)}{\partial\lambda}\times\exp(\ldots)\\
+2\beta(\lambda)f_{2}(s,\lambda)\left(\frac{\gamma\big(\Lambda(s,\lambda)\big)}{\beta(\Lambda(s,\lambda))}\frac{\partial\Lambda(s,\lambda)}{\partial\lambda}-\frac{\gamma\big(\Lambda(1,\lambda)\big)}{\beta(\Lambda(1,\lambda))}\frac{\partial\Lambda(1,\lambda)}{\partial\lambda}\right).

(277)

Using the relation (
274
) and the boundary condition (
79
) we can bring the above result in its final form

\beta(\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}f_{2}(s,\lambda)=\beta(\lambda)\,\frac{\partial f_{2}^{\text{boundary}}\big(1,\Lambda\big)}{\partial\lambda}\times\exp(\ldots)\\
+2f_{2}(s,\lambda)\left(\gamma\big(\Lambda\big)-\gamma(\lambda)\right).

(278)

Plugging (
275
) and (
278
) into the Callan-Symanzik equation (
76
) we confirm the equality.

Appendix E
Two-point functions at all orders in conformal perturbation theory

Here we review with some modifications an alternative method proposed in
[
24
]
, which also allows to compute two-point functions along the full RG flow.

Let us use the action (
40
), where
\mu
is the scale at which the coupling constant
\lambda(\mu)
is defined. We compute the QFT two-point functions using the following path integral expression

\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}\equiv\frac{\int[d\Phi]\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})e^{-A}}{\int[d\Phi]\ e^{-A}}.

(279)

Plugging here (
40
) and expanding around small
\lambda(\mu)
we get

\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{QFT}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\lambda(\mu)^{n}\left(\mu^{\delta}\right)^{n}\,\mathcal{K}_{n}(x_{1},x_{2}),

(280)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{EFT}},

(281)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\int d^{d}y\theta\left(|x_{1}-y|-\mu^{-1}\right)\theta\left(|x_{2}-y|-\mu^{-1}\right)\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(y)\rangle_{\text{EFT}},

and for
n\geq 2
we have

\mathcal{K}_{n}(x_{1},x_{2})\equiv\int d^{d}y_{1}d^{d}y_{2}\cdots d^{d}y_{n}\left\{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i>j\end{subarray}}^{n}\theta\left(|y_{ij}|-\mu^{-1}\right)\right\}\\
\left\{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\theta\left(|x_{1}-y_{i}|-\mu^{-1}\right)\theta\left(|x_{2}-y_{i}|-\mu^{-1}\right)\right\}\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(y_{1})\cdots\mathbf{O}(y_{n})\rangle_{\text{EFT}},

(282)

All the correlators appearing in (
280
) are defined only for energies bellow
\mu
or distances larger than
\mu^{-1}
, namely
|x_{ij}|>\mu^{-1}
. This is the reason we have inserted the
\theta
-functions in the above expressions.

Our normalization condition in section
3
was chosen in such a way that the EFT exhibits conformal invariance. We have for instance

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{EFT}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}=\frac{1}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{\Delta}},

(283)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{O}(x_{1})\mathbf{O}(x_{2})\mathbf{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{EFT}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}=\frac{C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}x_{13}^{2}x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\Delta/2}}.

Inside the EFT correlators appearing in (
281
) and (
282
) we can take the OPE. As discussed in appendix
B
it has the following form

\mathbf{O}(z_{1})\mathbf{O}(z_{2})=\frac{1}{\left(z_{12}^{2}\right)^{\Delta}}+\frac{1}{\left(z_{12}^{2}\right)^{\Delta/2}}C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\mathbf{O}(z_{2})+\cdots,

(284)

where the first term is the contribution of the identity operator and the second term is the contribution of the operator
\mathbf{O}
itself. Here and bellow ellipses represent the remaining contributions of primary and descendant operators. We will use the OPE (
284
) inside (
281
) and (
282
) when
|x_{1}-y_{i}|\rightarrow\mu^{-1}
,
|x_{2}-y_{i}|\rightarrow\mu^{-1}
or
|y_{i}-y_{j}|\rightarrow\mu^{-1}
. For this purpose instead of (
284
) we can use then

\mathbf{O}(z_{1})\mathbf{O}(z_{2})=\mu^{2\Delta}+C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\mu^{\Delta}\mathbf{O}(z_{2})+\cdots.

(285)

Let us compute the object
\mathcal{K}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})
defined in (
281
) in the limit
\mu\rightarrow\infty
. We can immediately see that in this limit the
\theta
-functions can be dropped since
\mu^{-1}\rightarrow 0
. Using (
283
) and (
35
) we simply get

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big|_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle=\int d^{d}y\langle\mathcal{O}(x_{1})\mathcal{O}(x_{2})\mathcal{O}(y)\rangle_{\text{UV CFT}}

(286)

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}}{\delta}r^{\delta}\mathcal{K}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad r\equiv|x_{12}|.

The functions
\mathcal{K}_{n}(x_{1},x_{2})
satisfy a recursion relation. We can derive it by differentiating
\mathcal{K}_{n}(x_{1},x_{2})
with respect to
\mu^{-1}
. When the derivative is acting on a
\theta
-function, it gives us a
\delta
-function. The latter can be used to perform one integral. At the level
n
there are
{}^{n}C_{2}+2n=\frac{n(n+3)}{2}
Heaviside
\theta
-functions, where
{}^{n}C_{2}
is the binomial coefficient.
Using the OPE (
285
) and only keeping the connected piece proportional to
C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}
(ignoring the identity operator) one gets the following recursion relation

\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{n}(x_{1},x_{2})}{d\mu^{-1}}=-\frac{n(n+3)}{2}C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}\mu^{1-\delta}\mathcal{K}_{n-1}(x_{1},x_{2})+\cdots.

(287)

Our goal now is to solve this recursion relation.

Let us first focus on the case when
n=1
. The recursion relation (
287
) then becomes

\frac{d\mathcal{K}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}{d\mu^{-1}}=-2C_{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}\mathcal{O}}\Omega_{d-1}\mu^{1-\delta}\mathcal{K}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})+\cdots.

(288)

In what follows we suppress all the ellipses.
Since
\mathcal{K}_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})
is independent of
\mu
according to our definitions (
281
) and (
283
), we can integrate the above relation. We get

Appendix G
Conformal correlator
\langle TT\mathcal{O}\rangle

Let us study in this section the

correlator. In what follows we will only consider the case when
\mathcal{O}
is a scalar operator. It has been first addressed by Osborn and Petkou in

Index-free formalism

The UV and IR fixed points of weakly relevant flows are described by two different CFTs. These CFTs have each its own stress-tensor. Let us provide the explicit expressions for the two- and three-point functions of the stress-tensor in any CFT. For doing that it is convenient to use the index-free notation of
[
112
]
. We define

T(x,z)\equiv T^{\mu\nu}(x)\,z_{\mu}z_{\nu},

(309)

where
z
is an auxiliary vector with complex components. The expressions for the stress-tensor correlators in CFTs were found by Osborn and Petkou in
[
39
]
. In index-free form they can be written as

\displaystyle\langle T(x_{1},z_{1})T(x_{2},z_{2})\rangle_{\text{CFT}}

\displaystyle=C_{T}\times\frac{H_{12}^{2}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{d+2}},

(310)

\displaystyle\langle T(x_{1},z_{1})T(x_{2},z_{2})\mathcal{O}(x_{3})\rangle_{\text{CFT}}

\displaystyle=C_{TT\mathcal{O}}\times\mathbf{T}_{TT\mathcal{O}}(x_{i},z_{i}),

Here
C_{T}
and
C_{TT\mathcal{O}}
are real non-negative coefficients. The
c
-anomaly in
d=4
is defined as

c\equiv\frac{\pi^{2}}{640}\times C_{T}.

(311)

The tensor structure
\mathbf{T}_{TT\mathcal{O}}
reads as

\mathbf{T}_{TT\mathcal{O}}(x_{i},z_{i})\equiv\frac{a_{1}\left(H_{12}\right)^{2}+a_{2}H_{12}V_{1,23}V_{2,13}+a_{3}\left(V_{1,23}V_{2,13}\right)^{2}}{\left(x_{12}^{2}\right)^{d+2-\Delta/2}\left(x_{13}^{2}\right)^{\Delta/2}\left(x_{23}^{2}\right)^{\Delta/2}}.

(312)

where

\displaystyle=1,

(314)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle=-\frac{d^{2}-d\Delta-d+\Delta-2}{2(d-2)(2d-\Delta+2)},

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2d^{2}\Delta-d^{3}+d^{2}-d\Delta^{2}-2d\Delta+2d+\Delta^{2}}{2(d-2)(2d-\Delta)(2d-\Delta+2)}.

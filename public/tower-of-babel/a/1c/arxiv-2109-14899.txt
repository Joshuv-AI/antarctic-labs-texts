Electronic Structures of Two-Dimensional PC 6 \text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} -Type Materials
Maximilian A. Springer, Thomas Brumme, Agnieszka Kuc, Thomas Heine

Abstract
Two-dimensional (2D) materials may exhibit intriguing band structure features ( e.g. , Dirac points), that lay far away from the Fermi level. They are, thus, not usable in applications. The semiconducting 2D material PC 6 \text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} has two Dirac cones above and below the Fermi level. Therefore, it is an ideal playground to demonstrate chemical functionalization methods for shifting the Fermi level in order to access interesting band structure features. PC 6 \text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} is based on the 7 × 7 ​ R ​ 19.1 ∘ \sqrt{7}\times\sqrt{7}R19.1^{\circ} super cell of graphene with two carbon atoms per unit cell substituted by phosphorous. It is demonstrated how substitution with other heteroatoms that contain a different number of valence electrons, the Dirac points can be accessed. Alternatively, hydrogen atoms can be used as adatoms at the heteroatom sites. This increases electron filling and shifts the Fermi level upwards.



I
Introduction

Graphene monolayer exhibits intriguing electron transport phenomena,
e.g.
, massless fermions at the K point
Novoselov et al. 2005
. The associated band structure feature is the Dirac cone
Wehling et al. 2014
. This leads to high carrier mobility for both electrons and holes and the presence of Quantum Hall effect at room temperature in strong magnetic fields
Zhang et al. 2005
;
Novoselov et al. 2006
;
Novoselov et al. 2007
. However, the band gap, which is induced by spin-orbit coupling (SOC), is very small
Kane and Mele 2005
. Therefore, pristine graphene is not very useful in applications like transistors or switching devices. To make it helpful for such applications, the band gap has to be increased. Hence, there are significant research efforts to tune the band structure of graphene
Liu et al. 2011
;
Novoselov et al. 2012
. Methods to open the band gap,
e.g.
, chemical functionalization, received a lot of attention
Georgakilas et al. 2012
. For example, the
sp^{2}
hybridization of carbon atoms in graphene can be exploited by attaching other atoms or molecules. The addition of hydrogen or fluorine to all carbon atoms gives rise to graphane and fluorographane, respectively
Sofo et al. 2007
;
Robinson et al. 2010
. In graphane, all carbon atoms are
sp^{3}
hybridized, which means that the formerly planar structure is puckered, with carbon atoms above and below the plane. Graphane is an insulator with a band gap of
5.4\,$\mathrm{eV}$

Lebègue et al. 2009
. Additionally, graphene layers can be stacked. Bilayers and few-layer stacks got substantial attention for their potential use as supercapacitors and superconductors
Wang et al. 2013a
;
Cao et al. 2018
. Furthermore, structural modifications of the lattice itself,
e.g.
, antidot lattices, were investigated
Fürst et al. 2009
.

The intriguing charge transport properties of graphene have lead to further research in the field of two-dimensional (2D) materials beyond graphene
Butler et al. 2013
;
Chhowalla et al. 2013
. 2D polymers,
e.g.
, the 2D triphenyl-bismuth lattice
Wang et al. 2013b
can exhibit Dirac points as well
Springer et al. 2020
. Here, the Dirac cone is gapped by
43\,$\mathrm{m}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
and lies
0.31\,$\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
below the Fermi level (
E_{\mathrm{F}}
). Similarly, the
\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
kagome lattice has a Dirac cone about
0.5\,$\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
above the Fermi level
Wang et al. 2013c
. This system is especially interesting, since SOC induces a topological band gap opening at the Dirac point. That means the
\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
lattice is an organic topological insulator. However, the band gap features are only usable if they lie in the vicinity of the Fermi level, which is not the case in the examples of the triphenyl-bismuth lattice and
\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{12}}}
. While graphene analogs and materials with intriguing properties are proposed, they might bear the problem that intriguing phenomena lie far away from the Fermi level. Therefore, strategies to shift the Fermi level are of central importance for the field. Yet, there are five popular strategies to shift the Fermi level: if the feature of interest is close enough to standard electron filling, strain can slightly change the band structure. Thus, Dirac points can be shifted towards the Fermi level
Wang et al. 2019
. Alternatively, electrons can be added or deducted by gate voltages and electric fields as well
Ueno et al. 2008
;
Avetisyan et al. 2009
;
Yu et al. 2009
;
Goldman 2014
. Besides these physical interventions, chemical modifications are also possible. These are, for example, the inclusion of adsorbates
Leenaerts et al. 2008
;
Lherbier et al. 2008
;
Wehling et al. 2008
;
Gierz et al. 2008
;
Park et al. 2012
;
Lu et al. 2013
, building interfaces with surfaces leading to charge separation
Chen et al. 2007
, or doping
Wei et al. 2009
.

Recently, the 2D phosphorous carbide
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
received significant attention
Yu et al. 2019
. Once alkali metal ions,
e.g.
, potassium or lithium, are adsorbed on
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
, the semiconducting material becomes metallic
Dou et al. 2019
. Furthermore, it exhibits small ion diffusion barriers. Therefore, it is discussed as anode material in lithium ion batteries
Dou et al. 2019
;
Fan et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2020
. Additionally,
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
can act as a gas sensor due to the conductivity being highly sensitive towards the addition of adsorbents
Yu et al. 2020
;
Zhou et al. 2020
. However, intrinsic properties of the material’s network structure where not yet discussed. It can be considered as functionalized graphene, since its structure can be derived from a graphene super cell with two carbon atoms per cell substituted by heteroatoms. Due to the retained honeycomb topology, it has Dirac points above and below
E_{\mathrm{F}}
. Therefore, it can be used as a playground to investigate strategies for shifting the Fermi level towards these Dirac points: the general shape of the band structure is retained if substitutions are isostructural. Therefore,
E_{\mathrm{F}}
can be controlled by using other heteroatoms than phosphorous, which change the electron filling due to their different number of valence electrons. Alternatively, hydrogen adatoms can be used to add valence electrons.

II
Theoretical Methods

First-principles calculations within the density-functional theory (DFT) framework were employed for both full structural optimization (atomic positions and lattice vectors) and electronic properties using AMS/BAND 2019
te Velde and Baerends 1991
;
Philipsen et al.
;
Kadantsev et al. 2007
. For optimizations, the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional was used with the TZP Slater-type basis set and Grimme-D3 dispersion energy correction with Becke-Johnson damping
Perdew et al. 1996
;
Perdew et al. 2008
;
Grimme et al. 2011
;
Franchini et al. 2013
;
Franchini et al. 2014
. The necessary accuracy of the tetrahedron method based
k
-space grid was determined for each system separately to a precision of
{10}^{-3}

\mathrm{eV}
. In order to account for relativistic effects in systems with gallium, germanium, arsenic and selenium, the scalar zeroth-order regular approximation (ZORA) was used in relaxation calculations with AMS/BAND
Philipsen et al. 1997
;
Philipsen and Baerends 2000
. For electronic structure calculations, the Heyd-Scuseria-Ernzerhof functional (HSE06) was used
Heyd et al. 2003
;
Heyd et al. 2006
. For phonon calculations, both lattice and atomic positions were relaxed using the PBE functional and the projector-augmented wave (PAW) method as implementend in QuantumEspresso 6.6 with a
5\times 5\times 1

k
-grid,
70\,$\mathrm{Ry}$
wave function cutoff and
580\,$\mathrm{Ry}$
kinetic energy cutoff
Giannozzi et al. 2017
. Phonon band structures were obtained from phonopy using the finite displacement method
Togo and Tanaka 2015
. In order to converge the phonon band structures, forces of the
5\times 5\times 1
super cells were evaluated based on the
\Gamma
-point approximation.

III.2
Substitution Strategy

The bands around the Fermi level are almost purely formed by
p_{z}
orbitals with only small contributions from other orbitals, as reported by Yu
et al.

Yu et al. 2019
Therefore, the shape of the band structure does not depend on the exact chemical composition, but on the network topology and the retention of the
\pi
conjugation
Springer et al. 2020
. The Fermi level can, thus, be shifted by substituting phosphorous atoms in
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
with other tetrahedrally (resulting in puckered structures) or trigonally (resulting in planar structures) coordinated heteroatoms.

According to the number of valence electrons relative to phosphorous, the Fermi level can be shifted upwards or downwards compared to the electron filling in
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
. In order to demonstrate this effect, compounds of the
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
-type with heteroatoms from group 14 (
\text{AlC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{GaC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
), group 15 (
\text{SiC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{GeC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
)
Yang et al. 2021
, group 16 (
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{AsC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
), and group 17 (
\text{SC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{SeC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
) are presented (structures and band structures are shown in Fig.
6
in the Supporting Information). Structural parameters are shown in Table
1
. For the considered systems, the cell parameters are in the range
6.70-7.01\,$\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
. As in
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
, this is larger than the parameter for the
\sqrt{7}\times\sqrt{7}R19.1^{\circ}
super structure of graphene. The lowest value is found for the system with the heteroatom of smallest ionic radius,
\text{SC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
. However, the largest value is found for
\text{SiC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
, the only flat system. In all other structures, the heteroatoms are displaced with respect to the plane formed by carbon atoms. Here, the heteroatoms are
0.93\,$\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
(
\text{SC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

1.34\,$\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
(
\text{AsC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

(
\text{SiC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

1.97\,$\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
(
\text{AsC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

The atomic radii decrease from smaller to larger number of valence electrons in each row and increase for ascending number of electrons in each group of the periodic table of elements.

v_{\mathrm{F}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial E}{\partial k}

\text{SiC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
(

) and the largest found for
\text{GeC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
(
v_{\mathrm{F}}=9.9\times 10^{6}\,$\mathrm{m}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}$
). The velocities for
\text{SC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
and
\text{SeC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
are between those two values, determined to be
3.8\times 10^{6}\,$\mathrm{m}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}$
and
3.9\times 10^{6}\,$\mathrm{m}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}$
, respectively.

\text{NC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

III.4
Topological Properties

Many of the presented systems are Dirac materials. Therefore, there was the possibility of obtaining topological quantum materials. Hence, an analysis of the wave function at the TRIM points was conducted
Fu and Kane 2007
. However, none of the Dirac materials
\text{SiC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{SC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{GeC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{SeC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{HPC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
, or
\text{HAsC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
turned out to have a non-zero
Z_{2}
invariant. Therefore, the systems are topologically trivial.

IV
Conclusions

By substitution with heteroatoms, a new family of 2D materials can be derived from the
\sqrt{7}\times\sqrt{7}R19.1^{\circ}
super cell of graphene. Due to the substitution of carbon atoms with heteroatoms,
e.g.
, phosphorous, a honeycomb super structure arises in a
\pi
-conjugated network. The associated band structure of the prototypical material
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
exhibits a direct band gap at the M point and Dirac points above and below the Fermi level at the K point. Isostructural substitutions leave the general shape of the band structure unchanged. Two strategies were presented to shift the Fermi level: in order to access those Dirac points, substitution with other heteroatoms and addition of hydrogen. By substituting phosphorous with,
e.g.
, germanium (two valence electron less per unit cell than
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
) the Dirac point below, and by substition with,
e.g.
, sulfur (two valence electron more per unit cell than
\text{PC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
), the Dirac point above the Fermi level can be accessed. Heteroatoms can also be used as binding sites for hydrogen, which adds more electrons to the system. Thus, the Dirac point above the initial Fermi level can be accessed, as demonstrated for
\text{HPC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
and
\text{HAsC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
. Furthermore, only one heteroatom per unit cell can be substituted isoelectronically. Here, the Dirac points are gapped due to the broken inversion symmetry and systems with small band gap (
E_{\mathrm{g}}<0.2\,$\mathrm{eV}$
) and effective masses (
\nicefrac{{m^{*}}}{{m_{\mathrm{e}}}}=0.02
) are obtained. A search for non-trivial topology was conducted. However, no system exhibits a non-zero
Z_{2}
invariant.

Discrete heat kernel, UV modified Green’s function, and higher derivative theories
Nahomi Kan, Masashi Kuniyasu, Kiyoshi Shiraishi, Zhenyuan Wu

Abstract
We perform the UV deformation of the Green’s function in free scalar field theory using a discrete heat kernel method. It is found that the simplest UV deformation based on the discretized diffusion equation leads to the well-known Pauli–Villars effective Lagrangian. Furthermore, by extending assumptions on the discretized equation, we find that the general higher derivative theory is derived from the present UV deformation. In some specific cases, we also calculate the vacuum expectation values of the scalar field squared in nontrivial background spaces and examine their dependence on the UV cutoff constant.

II
Discrete heat kernel and the simplest UV modified Green’s function

First of all, we review some basic facts of the standard Green’s functions
and heat kernels
Vassilevich
;
Camporesi
.
The standard Green’s function

G(x,x^{\prime})
of a scalar
field with mass
m
in a metric space with the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
satisfies

(-\Box_{x}+m^{2}){G}(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\delta(x,x^{\prime})\,,

(1)

where
\Box=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_{\mu}\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}
is
the d’Alembert operator on scalar fields, and

\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\delta(x,x^{\prime})
is the
D
-dimensional covariant delta function.

The main tool that we select here is the heat kernel of the Laplacian.
The heat kernel
K(x,x^{\prime};s)
is introduced as a representation of the
Green’s function constructed by using it:

{G}(x,x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,K(x,x^{\prime};s)\,.

(2)

Here, the heat kernel follows the equation

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+(-\Box_{x}+m^{2})\right]K(x,x^{\prime};s)=0\,,

(3)

with the initial condition
\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}K(x,x^{\prime};s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\delta(x,x^{\prime})
.

Next, we consider the Fourier transform
\tilde{K}(p;s)
of the heat kernel in
an Euclidean space
\mathbf{R}^{D}
. This is defined through the relation

K(x,x^{\prime};s)=K(x-x^{\prime};s)=\int\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}}\tilde{K}(p;s)e^{ip\cdot(x-x^{\prime})}\,.

(4)

Then, the heat equation becomes

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+(p^{2}+m^{2})\right]\tilde{K}(p;s)=0\,,

(5)

with the ‘initial’ condition

\tilde{K}(p;0)=1
.
One can easily find the solution

\tilde{K}(p;s)=e^{-s(p^{2}+m^{2})}\,,

(6)

and immediately obtain the Fourier transform of the Green’s function

\tilde{G}(p)
in

\mathbf{R}^{D}
as follows:

\tilde{G}(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left[-s(p^{2}+m^{2})\right]ds=\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}\,.

(7)

Then, the real space Green’s function is found to be

G(x,x^{\prime})=G(x-x^{\prime})=\int\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}}e^{ip\cdot(x-x^{\prime})}\tilde{G}(p)=\int\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{e^{ip\cdot(x-x^{\prime})}}{p^{2}+m^{2}}\,.

(8)

We can exchange the order of integrations. That is, the real space heat kernel is

K(x-x^{\prime};s)=\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{D/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4s}-m^{2}s}\right)\,,

(9)

and the Green’s function is recovered as

G(x-x^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,K(x-x^{\prime};s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\frac{1}{(4\pi s)^{D/2}}\exp\left({-\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}}{4s}-m^{2}s}\right)\,.

(10)

Anyway, the closed form for
G(x-x^{\prime})
turns out to be

G(x-x^{\prime})=\frac{m^{D-2}}{(2\pi)^{D/2}(m|x-x^{\prime}|)^{\frac{D-2}{2}}}K_{\frac{D-2}{2}}({m|x-x^{\prime}|})\,,

(11)

where
K_{\nu}(z)
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind.
Incidentally, for the massless case (
m=0
), we find

G(x-x^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma\left({\textstyle\frac{D-2}{2}}\right)}{4\pi^{D/2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{D-2}}\,,

(12)

where
\Gamma(z)
is the gamma function.

Now, we turn to considering a discretized heat kernel formulation,
whose continuous limit reproduces the standard method constructing the Green’s
function. To this end, we propose the discrete proper-time heat equation in

\mathbf{R}^{D}
as

3

Here we used the backward difference, but using the forward difference
does not give essentially different results.

\frac{\tilde{K}_{k}-\tilde{K}_{k-1}}{\epsilon}=-(p^{2}+m^{2})\tilde{K}_{k}\,,

(13)

where
k=1,\dots,N
and
\epsilon
is a positive constant.
We adopt the initial value
\tilde{K}_{0}=1
.
Then, the solution of the recurrence equation (
13
) is given by

\tilde{K}_{k}=\left[\frac{1}{1+\epsilon(p^{2}+m^{2})}\right]^{k}\,.

(14)

On the other hand, the following sum is directly concluded from (
13
):

(p^{2}+m^{2})\left[\sum_{k=1}^{N}\epsilon\tilde{K}_{k}\right]=\tilde{K}_{0}-\tilde{K}_{N}\,.

(15)

Observing that
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left[{1+\epsilon(p^{2}+m^{2})}\right]^{-N}=0
, we find

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\epsilon\tilde{K}_{k}=\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}\,,

(16)

which is just the discretized version of the integral (
7
).

The continuum limit is realized by taking

k\epsilon\rightarrow s
,
\tilde{K}_{k}\rightarrow\tilde{K}(p;s)
, and

\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. Then, we find that

\left[\frac{1}{1+\epsilon(p^{2}+m^{2})}\right]^{k}\rightarrow\exp[-s(p^{2}+m^{2})]\,,\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\epsilon\tilde{K}_{k}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{K}(p;s)ds=\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}\,.

(17)

After making the above preparation, we consider the UV modification of the Green’s
function. For this purpose, we define the modified Green’s function in momentum
space

\tilde{G}_{n}(p)\equiv\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\epsilon\tilde{K}_{k}=\frac{1}{(p^{2}+m^{2})[1+\epsilon(p^{2}+m^{2})]^{n-1}}\,.

(18)

To see the effect of the modification, we investigate a special case, in which
we assume that
\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}/n
and
n\rightarrow\infty
. In this case,

\tilde{G}
reduces to

\tilde{G}_{\infty}(p)=\frac{e^{-\epsilon^{\prime}(p^{2}+m^{2})}}{p^{2}+m^{2}}=\int_{\epsilon^{\prime}}^{\infty}\exp[-(p^{2}+m^{2})s]ds\,,

(19)

which was studied by Siegel
Siegel
. It is also known that the present manner
is used in the UV regularization in the standard heat kernel formalism.

Here, we study the simplest modification, the case with
n=2
. Namely, we use

\tilde{G}_{2}(p)=\frac{1}{(p^{2}+m^{2})[1+\epsilon(p^{2}+m^{2})]}=\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}+1/\epsilon}\,.

(20)

This is the simplest Pauli–Villars-type propagator (Green’s function)

IZ
;
Collins
;
PS
. The effective scalar field theory that leads to this
Green’s function is

S_{2}=\int\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}}\,\tilde{\cal L}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}}\tilde{\phi}(-p)(p^{2}+m^{2})[1+\epsilon(p^{2}+m^{2})]\tilde{\phi}(p)\,,

(21)

in the Fourier transformed form, and in the real coordinate space, it turns out to
be

S_{2}=\int d^{D}x\,{\cal L}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{D}x\,{\phi}(x)(-\Box+m^{2})[1+\epsilon(-\Box+m^{2})]{\phi}(x)\,,

(22)

where
\phi
is the real scalar field and
\tilde{\phi}
is its Fourier transform.

We can introduce an auxiliary field to continue study,
referring
to works on higher derivative theories
GOW
;
CL
motivated by the
seminal Lee–Wick model

LW
.
We consider the alternative Lagrangian

{\cal L}^{\prime}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\phi(-\Box+m^{2})\phi-\psi(-\Box+m^{2})\phi+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\psi^{2}\,.

(23)

The equation of motion of the initially auxiliary field
\psi
from the Lagrangian
is

\psi=\epsilon(-\Box+m^{2})\phi\,.

(24)

Thus, substituting this equation to
{\cal L}^{\prime}_{2}
yields the original Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{2}
. On the other hand, setting
\chi\equiv\phi+\psi
, we find

{\cal L}^{\prime}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\chi(-\Box+m^{2})\chi+\frac{1}{2}\psi(-\Box+m^{2}+1/\epsilon)\psi\,.

(25)

This effective Lagrangian describes two free scalar fields, whose masses are

m
and
\sqrt{m^{2}+1/\epsilon}
. The scalar field
\psi
with mass

\sqrt{m^{2}+1/\epsilon}
governed by the part of the Lagrangian with a ‘wrong’
sign. This field decouples from the physical spectrum if
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
,
since its mass becomes infinitely large.

The real space Green’s function

G_{2}(x-x^{\prime})=\frac{m^{\frac{D-2}{2}}K_{\frac{D-2}{2}}({m|x-x^{\prime}|})-(m^{2}+1/\epsilon)^{\frac{D-2}{4}}K_{\frac{D-2}{2}}({\sqrt{m^{2}+1/\epsilon}|x-x^{\prime}|})}{(2\pi)^{D/2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{D-2}{2}}}\,,

(26)

thus involves two mass scales
m
and
\sqrt{m^{2}+1/\epsilon}
.
For the massless case (
m=0
), we find

G_{2}(x-x^{\prime})=\frac{\Gamma\left({\textstyle\frac{D-2}{2}}\right)}{4\pi^{D/2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{D-2}}-\frac{\epsilon^{-\frac{D-2}{4}}K_{\frac{D-2}{2}}({\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}|x-x^{\prime}|})]}{(2\pi)^{D/2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{D-2}{2}}}\,.

(27)

At small
|x-x^{\prime}|
, the Green’s function
G_{2}(x-x^{\prime})
behaves as

\sim\epsilon^{-1}|x-x^{\prime}|^{4-D}
for

and

\sim\epsilon^{-1}\ln(\epsilon^{-1/2}|x-x^{\prime}|)

for

III
generalization of discretization and interesting cases

The generalization of (
13
) is not difficult.
The parameter
\epsilon

can have dependence on the integer
k
, i.e.,

\frac{\tilde{K}_{k}-\tilde{K}_{k-1}}{\epsilon_{k}}=-(p^{2}+m^{2})\tilde{K}_{k}\,,

(28)

where positive parameters
\epsilon_{k}
are restricted by

\epsilon_{k}=\epsilon_{n}\quad\mbox{for~}k>n\,.

(29)

Then, the summation yields

\tilde{G}_{n}(p)\equiv\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}\tilde{K}_{k}=\frac{1}{(p^{2}+m^{2})\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}[1+\epsilon_{k}(p^{2}+m^{2})]}\,.

(30)

Note that
\tilde{G}_{1}(p)=\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}
and
\tilde{G}_{2}(p)=\frac{1}{(p^{2}+m^{2})[1+\epsilon_{1}(p^{2}+m^{2})]}
. They are the same as those discussed
in Sec.
II
. Now,
\tilde{G}_{n}(p)
involves
n-1
parameters in general.

The effective action that leads to this modified propagator in an expression with
the momentum argument is

S=-\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{D}p}{(2\pi)^{D}}\tilde{\phi}(-p)[\tilde{G}_{n}(p)]^{-1}\tilde{\phi}(p)\,,

(31)

since the free propagator is obtained by the Gaussian integration in path integral
formalism.

Provided that values of
\epsilon_{k}
are all
different, the Green’s function in the momentum space can be expanded by partial
fractions as follows:

\frac{1}{(p^{2}+m^{2})\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}[1+\epsilon_{k}(p^{2}+m^{2})]}=\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{c_{k}\epsilon_{k}}{1+\epsilon_{k}(p^{2}+m^{2})}\,,

(32)

where the coefficient
c_{k}
reads

c_{k}=\frac{-\epsilon_{k}^{n-2}}{\prod_{i\neq k}^{n-1}(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{i})}\,.

(33)

Now, we consider concrete examples.
We here consider two particular cases for
\{\epsilon_{k}\}
.
We shall call them model A and model B.

\bullet
Model A

First, we consider
\{\epsilon_{k}\}=\{\epsilon,\frac{\epsilon}{2},\frac{\epsilon}{3},\ldots\frac{\epsilon}{n-1}\}
.
We know the mathematical formula

\tilde{G}_{A}(p)\equiv\frac{1}{(p^{2}+m^{2})\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}[1+\frac{\epsilon}{k}(p^{2}+m^{2})]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{\textstyle{n-1}\choose{k}}\frac{(-1)^{k}\epsilon/k}{1+\frac{\epsilon}{k}(p^{2}+m^{2})}\,,

(34)

thus,

c_{k}=(-1)^{k}{\textstyle{n-1}\choose{k}}\qquad(k=1,\ldots,n-1)\,.

(35)

Note that the case
n=2
trivially reduces to the simplest case treated in
Sec.
II
. Thus, this first example is a straightforward extension of the
simplest case.

\bullet
Model B

The second example enjoys the infinite derivatives, i.e.,
defined with
n\rightarrow\infty
.
We set

\{\epsilon_{k}\}=\{\epsilon,\frac{\epsilon}{2^{2}},\frac{\epsilon}{3^{2}},\ldots\}
. Now, the Green’s function in the momentum space becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{B}(p)\equiv\frac{1}{(p^{2}+m^{2})\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}[1+\frac{\epsilon}{k^{2}}(p^{2}+m^{2})]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\sqrt{p^{2}+m^{2}}\sinh[\pi\sqrt{\epsilon(p^{2}+m^{2})}]}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{2}+m^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{k}\epsilon/k^{2}}{1+\frac{\epsilon}{k^{2}}(p^{2}+m^{2})}\,,

and we find

c_{k}=(-1)^{k}\cdot 2\qquad(k\geq 1)\,.

(37)

This type of the Green’s function has appeared in Refs.
FS1
;
FS2
;
Fujikawa

as generalizations of the Pauli–Villars regularization. Incidentally,
the Green’s function for Model B with
m=0
in configuration space can be written
as

G_{B}(x-x^{\prime})=\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\pi^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{D-1}{2}\right)}{\left[|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+4\pi^{2}\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\epsilon\right]^{\frac{D-1}{2}}}\qquad(\mbox{for~}m=0)\,.

(38)

The limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
reduces this expression to the normal
massless Green’s function (
12
).
Furthermore, especially for
D=5
, one can obtain a closed form of
G_{B}(w)
as

G_{B}(w)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{w^{3}}\tanh\frac{w}{2l}-\frac{1}{2lw^{2}}\mbox{sech}^{2}\frac{w}{2l}\right]\qquad(\mbox{for~}m=0,D=5)\,,

(39)

where
w\equiv|x-x^{\prime}|
.

IV
vacuum expectation value of the scalar field squared for the UV
modified cases in nonsimply connected spaces

Now, we can express the one-loop vacuum
expectation value of the square of the scalar field
\langle\phi^{2}\rangle
, or
also called as the quantum fluctuation of the scalar field, in the effective
higher derivative theory. We find, with the aid of (
32
),

\langle\phi^{2}\rangle=\lim_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}G_{n}(x,x^{\prime})=\lim_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}\left[G(x,x^{\prime};m^{2})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}G(x,x^{\prime};M^{2}_{k})\right]\,,

(40)

where
G(x,x^{\prime};M^{2})
is the Green’s function of a canonical scalar field with
mass
M
and
M^{2}_{k}=m^{2}+1/\epsilon_{k}
.

We should note that the value of
\langle\phi^{2}\rangle
is still a
‘bare’ quantity. Even if the highest divergence (e.g. quadratic divergences in
four dimensions) has been canceled, divergences remain for a sufficient
high dimensional case (see the calculation in this section below). Moreover, even
if divergences have ceased, the bare value of the stress tensor should be of order
of

\epsilon^{-\alpha}

(\alpha>0)
. If one would
like to combine the cutoff constant

\epsilon
with some fundamental theory, the finite but huge quantities that
appears in quantum expectation values should be subtracted by some method.
In the present analysis, similar to many past works including our work
KKSW
,
subtraction is performed by hand, but done on the basis of physical meaning.

The calculation of the vacuum expectation values of
the free scalar field squared in nonsimply connected space has been studied by many
authors in recent decades. We consider two specific cases. We pick up a
Kaluza–Klein-type space and a conical space.

IV.1
The Kaluza–Klein-type space

First, we consider a
D
-dimensional space whose metric is given by

ds^{2}=dx^{\lambda}dx_{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{D-1}dz_{i}^{2}+dy^{2}\,,

(41)

where the Greek index runs over
1,\dots,D
, while the Roman index runs over

1,\dots,D-1
. The coordinate

y
is the coordinate on a circle (
S^{1}
) and we assume that
0\leq y<L
, where
L

is the circumference of the circle. The periodic boundary condition in the
direction of

y
is supposed to be applied on the massless scalar, i.e.,
\phi(y+L)=\phi(y)
.
In this space,
\mathbf{R}^{D-1}\otimes S^{1}
, the standard massless Green’s
function for a scalar field takes the form

G(x,x^{\prime};M^{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{(4\pi s)^{D/2}}\sum_{q=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\textstyle-\frac{\zeta^{2}+(\eta-qL)^{2}}{4s}-M^{2}s\right]\,,

(42)

where
\zeta=|z-z^{\prime}|
and
\eta=y-y^{\prime}
.
Then, we define the quantities with subtraction

\langle\bar{\phi^{2}}\rangle[M^{2}]\equiv\lim_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}\bar{G}(x,x^{\prime};M^{2})\,,

(43)

where

\bar{G}(x,x^{\prime};M^{2})\equiv{G}(x,x^{\prime};M^{2})-\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}{G}(x,x^{\prime};M^{2})
.
This subtraction is aimed to take the difference from the quantity in the
infinitely extending space
\mathbf{R}^{D}
.
After straightforward manipulations, we get

\langle\bar{\phi^{2}}\rangle[M^{2}]=\frac{2}{(4\pi)^{D/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s^{D/2}}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\exp\left[{\textstyle-\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{4s}-M^{2}s}\right]=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\left(\textstyle\frac{M}{2\pi qL}\right)^{\frac{D-2}{2}}K_{\frac{D-2}{2}}(qML)\,,

(44)

and thus, we can evaluate the vacuum expectation value in the effective theory as

\langle\bar{\phi^{2}}\rangle=\langle\bar{\phi^{2}}\rangle[m^{2}]+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}\langle\bar{\phi^{2}}\rangle[M_{k}^{2}]\,.

(45)

In general, in the higher derivative scalar field theory (
31
) which leads
to the Green’s function (
32
), the vacuum expectation value for the scalar
field squared

\langle\bar{\phi^{2}}\rangle
is written by

\langle\bar{\phi^{2}}\rangle=\frac{2}{(4\pi)^{D/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s^{D/2}}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\exp\left[{\textstyle-\frac{q^{2}L^{2}}{4s}}-m^{2}s\right]\varrho(s)\,,

(46)

where
\varrho(s)\equiv 1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}\exp[-s/\epsilon_{k}]
.

For Model A, we find

\varrho_{A}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}{\textstyle{n-1}\choose{k}}e^{-ks/\epsilon}=(1-e^{-s/\epsilon})^{n-1}\,.

(47)

For Model B, we find

\varrho_{B}(s)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}e^{-k^{2}s/\epsilon}=\vartheta_{4}(0,e^{-s/\epsilon})=\sqrt{\frac{\pi\epsilon}{s}}\vartheta_{2}(0,e^{-\pi^{2}\epsilon/s})=\sqrt{\frac{\pi\epsilon}{s}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\pi^{2}(k+1/2)^{2}\epsilon/s}\,,

(48)

where
\vartheta_{j}(v,q)
is the Jacobi theta function.

The conical space

Next, we consider the vacuum expectation values in a conical space.
A conical space, or a space with a conical singularity at the coordinate origin,
is described by the metric

ds^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{D-2}(dz^{i})^{2}+dr^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{\nu^{2}}d\theta^{2}\,,

\tilde{\theta}
is

.
This metric adequately describes a locally flat Euclidean space except for

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we first introduced a discrete heat kernel formulation of the
Green’s function for a free scalar field, and considered the UV deformation that
can be naturally obtained from it. The effective Lagrangian that derives this
UV modified Green’s function has been found to be higher derivative theory. In the
simplest case, it corresponds to a simple Pauli–Villars model, or, a Lee–Wick
scalar field theory. In a further generalization, it was found that the present UV
deformation yields a theory involving arbitrarily higher order derivatives, and
the vacuum expectation values of the scalar field squared were
evaluated in nontrivial spaces. We demonstrated that the cutoff length has an
influence on the physical quantities at a short distance in the configuration
space.

The advantage of using the heat kernel method is that we can keep the symmetry of
the space (or break it at will). It is also easy to incorporate the effect of a
constant external field (including spatial metric). On the other hand, one of the
disadvantages is the handling of general interactions of dynamical fields. In
addition, although the form of UV deformation is limited in our approach, it is
also a problem that the sequence
\epsilon_{k}
introduced in this paper has a wide
range of arbitrariness other than the case of the two models.

To eliminate UV divergences completely, higher order derivatives that satisfy the
conditions depending on the number of dimensions are required. Even if there is no
divergence, a constant depending on the cutoff constant comes out.
Therefore, some mechanism for canceling the finite contribution in the UV
limit is still required. However, the present demonstration shows that the finite
cutoff dependence of physical quantities occurs when the typical scale in the
considered system is very close to the cutoff scale.

In the effective higher derivative theory, a scalar field with a negative norm
appears, so the addition of interaction may bring about instability and violate
unitarity in the Lorentzian spacetime. This is a subject that has been frequently
addressed and has recently been actively discussed
AP
;
Anselmi
, and in
particular, infinite derivative theory is being studied in various situations

Modesto
;
BT
;
BLM
;
BLMTY
. One of the models we have proposed (Model B) involves
infinite derivatives, and the unitarity of the theory and its generalization should
be studied further.
Unfortunately, our current understanding has not led us to discuss the unitarity
of the models presented in this paper in more detail.
We consider that detailed discussions about nonlinear terms, i.e., interaction
terms of fields are needed in the (infinitely) higher derivative theory, because
it seems important to properly allocate higher order derivatives to the part of
propagation and the part of interaction vertices.

It is interesting to study whether the theory with a basic cutoff scale can be
derived naturally, for example from a model of quantum gravity, or not.

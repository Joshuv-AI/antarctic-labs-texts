On generalized minimal log discrepancy
Weichung Chen, Yoshinori Gongyo, Yusuke Nakamura

Abstract
. We discuss the ACC conjecture and the LSC conjecture for minimal log discrepancies of generalized pairs. We prove that some known results on these two conjectures for usual pairs are still valid for generalized pairs. We also discuss the theory of complements for generalized pairs.

Primary 14E30; Secondary 14B05

1.
Introduction

Generalized pairs are introduced by Birkar and Zhang
[
4
]
, and they are generalizations of the usual log pairs.
Generalized pairs play an essential role in Birkar’s papers
[
5
,
7
]
.
In these papers, Birkar proves the BAB conjecture, which states that a certain class of singular Fano varieties is bounded.
Generalized pairs also have many applications to important topics in birational geometry
(for example, the Iitaka conjecture
[
4
]
, and the termination problem and the existence of minimal models
[
32
,
17
,
31
]
).
We refer the reader to his expository article
[
6
]
for the motivation and related problems.

In this article, we focus on the singularity aspect of generalized pairs,
in particular, their minimal log discrepancies.
The minimal log discrepancy is an invariant of singularities introduced by Shokurov (for usual log pairs)
in order to attack the termination of flips.
Shokurov proved that two conjectures on the minimal log discrepancies,
the LSC (lower semi-continuity) conjecture and
the ACC (ascending chain condition) conjecture,
imply the conjecture of termination of flips
[
43
]
.

The ACC conjecture predicts that
the set of minimal log discrepancies of fixed dimension
with the suitable restrictions on the coefficients of the boundary divisors satisfies the ascending chain condition.

Conjecture 1.1

(ACC conjecture,
[
42
]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and let
I\subset[0,1]
be a subset that satisfies the DCC.
Then the set
A(d,I)
defined by

A(d,I):=\left\{\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\Delta)\ \middle|\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$(X,\Delta)$ is a log pair with}\\
\text{$\dim X=d$, $\Delta\in I$ and $x\in|X|_{0}$.}\end{array}\right\}

satisfies the ACC, where
|X|_{0}
denotes the set of all closed points of
X
.

The ACC conjecture is proved when
d\leq 2
by Alexeev
[
1
]
and Shokurov
[
41
]
.
In
[
22
,
21
]
, Kawakita proves the ACC conjecture on the interval
[1,3]
for three-dimensional smooth varieties,
and the discreteness of the minimal log discrepancies for a fixed variety.
In
[
38
]
, the third author generalizes the discreteness result by Kawakita to the varieties with fixed Gorenstein index and
proves the ACC conjecture for canonical three-folds when
I
is a finite set.
We refer the reader to
[
3
,
35
,
23
,
19
,
20
,
33
,
34
]
for other developments related to the ACC conjecture.

The LSC conjecture predicts that the minimal log discrepancies satisfy the lower semi-continuity.

Conjecture 1.2

(LSC conjecture,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log pair.
Then the function

m:|X|_{0}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\};\quad x\mapsto\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\Delta)

is lower semi-continuous, where
|X|_{0}
denotes the set of all closed points of
X
with the Zariski topology.

The LSC conjecture is proposed by Ambro and proved when
d\leq 3

[
2
]
.
Ein, Mustaţǎ and Yasuda in
[
11
]
prove the conjecture when
X
is smooth using the jet scheme theory, and
Ein and Mustaţǎ in
[
12
]
generalize the argument to the case where
X
is a locally complete intersection variety.
In
[
39
]
, the third author proves the conjecture when
X
has quotient singularities,
more generally when
X
has a crepant resolution in the category of the Deligne-Mumford stacks.
In
[
37
,
36
]
, the third author and Shibata prove the conjecture when
X
has hyperquotient singularities.

A usual log pair
(X,\Delta)
consists of a normal variety
X
and an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\Delta
.
A generalized pair
(X,\Delta+M)
additionally has a b-divisor
M
over
X
.
More precisely,
M
is the push-forward of a nef Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on some birational model
X^{\prime}
(see Definition
2.1
).
Then we can extend the definition of the minimal log discrepancy to generalized pairs, and
we can also expect the ACC conjecture and the LSC conjecture in this setting.

Conjecture 1.3

(ACC conjecture for generalized pair)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and let
I\subset[0,+\infty)
be a subset that satisfies the DCC.
Then the set
A_{\textup{gen}}(d,I)
defined by

A_{\textup{gen}}(d,I):=\left\{\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,B+M)\ \middle|\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$(X,B+M)/Z$ is a generalized pair with}\\
\text{$\dim X=d$, $B\in I$, $M\in I$ and $x\in|X|_{0}$.}\end{array}\right\}

satisfies the ACC. See Definition
2.2
(1) for the meaning of
M\in I

Conjecture 1.4

(LSC conjecture for generalized pair)
.

Let
(X,B+M)/Z
be a generalized pair.
Then the function

m:|X|_{0}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\};\quad x\mapsto\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,B+M)

is lower semi-continuous.

The main purpose of this article is to confirm that known results on these two conjectures for usual pairs are still valid for generalized pairs.

First, we follow the argument in
[

]
to prove Conjecture
1.3
in dimension two (see
[
32
]
for a similar related result).
Furthermore, we generalize the argument in
[
38
]
to prove Conjecture
1.3
for varieties with fixed Gorenstein index,
and prove partial results on generalized canonical pairs of dimension three.

(
={}

For any DCC subset

Definition 2.1
.

A
generalized pair

(X,B+M)/Z
consists of

•

a normal variety
X
and a quasi-projective variety
Z
with a projective morphism
X\to Z
,

•

an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B
on
X
, and

•

a b-
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier b-divisor over
X
represented by some projective birational morphism
\varphi:X^{\prime}\to X
and

/Z

\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor

on

such that
M=\varphi_{*}M^{\prime}
and
K_{X}+B+M
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

We say that

is the
boundary part
and
M
is the
nef part
of the generalized pair
(X,B+M)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,B+M)/Z
be a generalized pair.

(1)

Let
I\subset[0,+\infty)
be a subset.
Then we write

I

By abuse of notation, we also write
M\in I
when
M^{\prime}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq\ell}r_{i}M^{\prime}_{i}

positive real numbers

/Z

M^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,M^{\prime}_{\ell}
possibly replacing

.
This definition is natural when considering an NQC generalized pair (see
[
18
]
*Section 2.6 for more detail).

(2)

For a prime divisor

over

generalized log discrepancy

Possibly replacing
X^{\prime}
with a higher model, we may assume that
\varphi

X^{\prime}
.
We define an
\mathbb{R}

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M).

.
The image

(3)

Let

along

\operatorname{mld}_{W}(X,B+M):=\inf_{c_{X}(E)\subset W}a_{E}(X,B+M)

(4)

Let

E
over
X

When

\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M):=-\infty

We denote

(5)

When

Remark 2.3

(1)

For a generalized pair

Remark 2.4
.

Let
(X,B+M)/Z
be a generalized pair and let
\eta
be a scheme-theoretic point of

of
\operatorname{codim}\eta\geq 1

(1)

Let

We define an

Then we have

for any divisor

over

.
Hence we have

\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)=\inf_{c_{X}(E)=\overline{\{\eta\}}}a_{E}(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}),

where the infimum is taken over all prime divisors
E
over
X
with center

.
This observation allows us to prove the remarks below, which are well-known for usual pairs (that is, the case when

).

(2)

Possibly replacing
\varphi
, we may assume that
\varphi
is a log resolution of
(X,B)
.
Then
\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)=-\infty
holds if and only if

hold for some prime divisosr

on
X^{\prime}
.
Moreover, such

can be found in the divisors contained in the
\varphi

.

(3)

For any
\eta\in X
, possibly replacing
\varphi
, we may assume that
\varphi
is a log resolution of
the triple
(X,B,\overline{\{\eta\}})
.
If
\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)\geq 0
, then some divisor on
X^{\prime}
computes
\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)
.

(4)

The set

.
This follows from (

).

(5)

If
\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)<0

(6)

If

2.3.
Generalized minimal model program

In this subsection, we collect some facts related to the minimal model program for generalized pairs.

We will start from the basics of the minimal model program for generalized pairs.

Definition 2.5

(Generalized minimal model program)
.

Let
\pi:X\to S

(X,B+M)/Z

2.4.
Divisorial adjunction for generalized pairs

We recall the construction of divisorial adjunction for generalized pairs.
In the minimal model theory, the divisorial adjunction is a significant tool in arguments by induction on dimension.

Definition 2.9

(cf.
[
5
, 3.1]
)
.

Let
(X,B+M)/Z
be a generalized pair and let
\varphi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be the birational morphism in Definition
2.1
.
Replacing
X^{\prime}
, we may assume that
\varphi
is a log resolution of
(X,B)
.
Let
S
be the normalization of a component of
B
with coefficient
1
,
and let
S^{\prime}
be the strict transform of
S
on
X^{\prime}
.
We define an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
by

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M).

Then we set
B_{S^{\prime}}=(B^{\prime}-S^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}
and pick
M_{S^{\prime}}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}M^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}
.
By the adjunction, we have

K_{S^{\prime}}+B_{S^{\prime}}+M_{S^{\prime}}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}.

We denote by
\varphi_{S}:S^{\prime}\to S
the induced morphism.
We set

and
M_{S}=\varphi_{S*}M_{S^{\prime}}
.
Then,
(S,B_{S}+M_{S})/Z
is a generalized pair satisfying

•

and

•

K_{S^{\prime}}+B_{S^{\prime}}+M_{S^{\prime}}=\varphi_{S}^{*}(K_{S}+B_{S}+M_{S})
.

We call
(S,B_{S}+M_{S})
or
K_{S}+B_{S}+M_{S}
the
divisorial adjunction
for the generalized pair
(X,B+M)
and
S
.
We remark that if
(X,B+M)
is generalized lc,
then
(S,B_{S}+M_{S})
is also generalized lc by the construction (
[
4
, Remark 4.8]
).

Remark 2.10
.

In Definition
2.9
, the divisor
B_{S}
is uniquely determined by

and

as a divisor,
while

is defined only up to
\mathbb{R}
-linear equivalence.
We can observe that if

with

and
M^{\prime}_{i}
nef
/Z
Cartier,
then we can pick some Cartier divisors

and take
M_{S^{\prime}}=\sum m_{i}M_{i,S^{\prime}}
.
Note here that some of

The following lemma is from
[
5
, Lemma 3.3]
.
We shall write down the coefficients of

more explicitly.

Lemma 2.11
.

Under the settings and the notation in Definition
2.9
, assume that

•

(X,B+M)/Z
is generalized lc,

•

B=\sum b_{i}B_{i}
, where
B_{i}
’s are prime divisors on
X
, and

•

M^{\prime}=\sum m_{i}M^{\prime}_{i}
as in Remark

.

Then the coefficients of
B_{S}
are of the form

1-\frac{1}{\ell}+\frac{\sum\alpha_{i}b_{i}+\sum\beta_{j}m_{j}}{\ell},

where
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\alpha_{i},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
and
M_{S^{\prime}}
can be chosen to be of the form

\sum m_{i}M_{i,S^{\prime}},

where
M_{i,S^{\prime}}
are nef
/Z

.

Proof.

We take over the notations in Definition
2.9
.
We have already seen the assertion for
M_{S^{\prime}}
in Remark
2.10
.
In what follows, we shall show the assertion for
B_{S}
.
By taking general hypersurface sections (see
[
4
, Remark 4.8]
), we may assume that
\dim X=2
.
Note that, in particular,
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial (cf.
[
27
, Proposition 4.11(1)]
).
For any fixed closed point

, we shall show that
\mu_{v}B_{S}
is of the form in the statement.
Since
(S,B_{S}+M_{S})
is generalized lc, we have
\mu_{v}B_{S}\leq 1
.
We may assume that
\mu_{v}B_{S}<1
.
We define
\widetilde{B}_{S}
by the divisorial adjunction for usual pairs:

K_{S}+\widetilde{B}_{S}=(K_{X}+B)|_{S}.

Then by the relative nefness of
M^{\prime}
and the negativity lemma, we have
\widetilde{B}_{S}\leq B_{S}
.
Therefore, by the inversion of adjunction for surfaces (cf.
[
40
, Corollary 3.12]
),

(X,B)
is plt at the image
v^{\prime}\in X
of

.

By the adjunction for usual pairs (cf.
[
40
, Proposition 3.9 and Corollary 3.10]
),
there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\ell D
is Cartier at

for any Weil divisor

on
X
, and we have

\mu_{v}\widetilde{B}_{S}=1-\frac{1}{\ell}+\sum\frac{\alpha_{i}b_{i}}{\ell}

for some
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
We write
\varphi^{*}M_{i}=M^{\prime}_{i}+E_{i}
. Then
E_{i}\geq 0

and the negativity lemma.
Since

is Cartier,

is integral and hence
\ell M_{i}
is Cartier at

.
Therefore

.
By construction, it follows that

Since

∎

3.
Lower semi-continuity conjecture for generalized mld’s

3.1.
Basic properties on generalized minimal log discrepancies

In this subsection, following Ambro’s paper
[
2
]
,
we prove the finiteness and the constructibility of the mld function (Lemma
3.2
),
which is weaker than the lower semi-continuity conjecture (Conjecture
3.5
).
We also prove that the minimal log discrepancy of a generalized pair can be approximated
by those of usual pairs (Lemma
3.4
).
Both Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
will be important in the next subsection,
where we reduce the lower semi-continuity conjecture for generalized pairs to that for usual pairs.

The following lemma is a generalization of
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
to the generalized setting.
The same proof in
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
also works in this setting.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,B+M)/Z
be a generalized pair, and
let
\eta
be a scheme-theoretic point of
X
with
\operatorname{codim}\eta\geq 1
.
Then there exists an open set
U\subset|X|_{0}
with
\overline{\{\eta\}}\cap U\not=\emptyset
such that

\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,B+M)=\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)+\dim\eta

holds for any
x\in\overline{\{\eta\}}\cap U
.

Proof.

We may assume that
(X,B+M)
is generalized lc at
\eta
(cf. Remark
2.4
(
4
)).
Let
\varphi:X^{\prime}\to X
be the projective birational morphism in Definition
2.1
.
Possibly replacing
\varphi
, we may assume that
\varphi
is a log resolution of the triple
(X,B,\overline{\{\eta\}})
.

We define an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
by

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M).

Let
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of the
\varphi
-exceptional divisors, the components of

\varphi^{-1}_{*}B
, and the computing divisors of
\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)
on
X^{\prime}
.
We may write

B^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}D_{i}

with
e_{i}\leq 1
.
Let
I_{\textup{dom}}\subset I
be the set of the indices
i
satisfying
\varphi(D_{i})=\overline{\{\eta\}}
.
Then we have

\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)=\min_{i\in I_{\textup{dom}}}(1-e_{i}).

Since
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is log smooth, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{\xi}(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+M^{\prime})=\operatorname{mld}_{\xi}(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})=\operatorname{codim}\xi-\sum_{\xi\in D_{i}}e_{i}

for any scheme-theoretic point
\xi
of
X^{\prime}
(cf.
[
27
, Corollary 2.32]
).

By generic smoothness, we can take an open set
U\subset|X|_{0}

such that
\overline{\{\eta\}}\cap U\not=\emptyset
with the following two conditions:

(a)

For any
x\in\overline{\{\eta\}}\cap U
and any stratum
C
of

\operatorname{Supp}\bigl(\sum_{i\in I}D_{i}\bigr)
that satisfies
\varphi(C)=\overline{\{\eta\}}
,
it follows that
\dim F=\dim C-\dim\eta
for any irreducible component
F
of
C\cap\varphi^{-1}(x)
.

(b)

For any stratum
C
of
\operatorname{Supp}\bigl(\sum_{i\in I}D_{i}\bigr)
,
if
\varphi(C)\subset\overline{\{\eta\}}
and
\varphi(C)\cap U\not=\emptyset
, then
\varphi(C)=\overline{\{\eta\}}
holds.

In what follows, we shall prove that this
U
satisfies the assertion.

Let
x\in\overline{\{\eta\}}\cap U
and let
\xi\in X^{\prime}
be a scheme-theoretic point with
\varphi(\xi)=x
.
Let
C
be the minimum stratum of
\operatorname{Supp}\bigl(\sum_{i\in I}D_{i}\bigr)
that contains
\xi
.
Since
\varphi
is a log resolution of
\overline{\{\eta\}}
,

\xi\in D_{j}
holds for some
j\in I_{\rm dom}
.
Therefore, it follows that
\varphi(C)\subset\overline{\{\eta\}}
, and hence
\varphi(C)=\overline{\{\eta\}}
by (b).
Therefore, by (a), we have

•

\operatorname{codim}\xi-\operatorname{codim}C\geq\dim\eta
.

Furthermore, since
j\in I_{\rm dom}
, we have

•

1-e_{j}\geq\min_{i\in I_{\textup{dom}}}(1-e_{i})=\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{mld}_{\xi}(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{codim}\xi-\sum_{\xi\in D_{i}}e_{i}

\displaystyle=(1-e_{j})+(\operatorname{codim}\xi-1)-\sum_{\xi\in D_{i},\ i\not=j}e_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)+(\operatorname{codim}\xi-1)-(\operatorname{codim}C-1)

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)+\dim\eta.

Hence we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,B+M)\geq\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)+\dim\eta.

We shall prove the opposite inequality.
Let
D_{i}
be a computing divisor on
X^{\prime}
of
\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)
(cf. Remark
2.4
(3)).
Let
\xi^{\prime}
be the scheme-theoretic point of an irreducible component of
D_{i}\cap\varphi^{-1}(x)
.
Then by (a) and (b), the
D_{i}
is the unique divisor that contains
\xi^{\prime}
.
Hence we have

\operatorname{mld}_{\xi^{\prime}}(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})=\operatorname{codim}\xi^{\prime}-e_{i}=\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)+\dim\eta,

which proves

\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,B+M)\leq\operatorname{mld}_{\xi^{\prime}}(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})=\operatorname{mld}_{\eta}(X,B+M)+\dim\eta.

It completes the proof.
∎

As a corollary of Lemma
3.1
, we can prove the finiteness and the constructibility of the mld function.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(X,B+M)/Z
be a generalized pair.
We denote by
m
the function defined by the minimal log discrepancy:

m:|X|_{0}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\};\quad x\mapsto\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,B+M),

where
|X|_{0}
is the set of all closed points of
X
with the Zariski topology.
Then the following hold.

(1)

The function
m
takes finitely many values.

(2)

The function
m
is constructible, that is, any fiber of
m
is a constructible subset of
|X|_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:X^{\prime}\to X
be the birational morphism in Definition
2.1
.
We define an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
by

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M).

Then for
x\in X_{\textup{sm}}\setminus\varphi(\operatorname{Supp}B^{\prime})
, we have

\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,B+M)=\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X)=\dim X.

Hence the function
m
is constant on a dense open subset of
|X|_{0}
.
Then the assertion follows from Lemma
3.1
by the Noetherian induction.
∎

The following lemma will be used in Lemma

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(X,B+M)/Z
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein generalized pair and let

be a divisor over
X
.
Then there exists a sequence of effective divisors
D_{m}
on
X
such that

is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier and the following two conditions hold.

(1)

\operatorname{ord}_{F}(B+M)\leq\operatorname{ord}_{F}(B+D_{m})
holds for any divisor
F
over
X
.

(2)

\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B+M)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B+D_{m})
holds.

Here
\operatorname{ord}_{{\rm-}}(B+M)
on the left-hand side is
regarded as the order for a generalized pair defined in Definition
2.2
(
11
).
The order
\operatorname{ord}_{{\rm-}}(B+D_{m})
on the right-hand side is regarded as the order in the usual sense (for a usual pair).

Proof.

Let
\varphi:X^{\prime}\to X
be the birational morphism as in Definition
2.1
.
We may assume that
E
is a divisor on
X^{\prime}
.

Let
A
be an ample
/Z
divisor on
X
. We may write

\varphi^{*}A\sim_{\mathbb{R}}A^{\prime}+C

for some ample
/Z

\mathbb{R}
-divisor

and some effective
\varphi
-exceptional divisor

on
X^{\prime}
.
Let
A^{\prime}_{m}\in\big|M^{\prime}+\frac{1}{m}A^{\prime}\big|_{\mathbb{R}}

We define
D_{m}:=\varphi_{*}A^{\prime}_{m}
.
Note that
A^{\prime}_{m}=\varphi^{-1}_{*}D_{m}
holds by the generality of
A^{\prime}_{m}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(B+M)

\displaystyle=\varphi^{-1}_{*}B+M^{\prime}+\sum_{G}\operatorname{ord}_{G}(B+M)G,

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(B+D_{m})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{-1}_{*}B+A^{\prime}_{m}+\sum_{G}\operatorname{ord}_{G}(B+D_{m})G,

where the sums are taken over all
\varphi
-exceptional divisors

Since

\operatorname{ord}_{G}(B+M)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\operatorname{ord}_{G}(B+D_{m})

-exceptional divisor

factors through
\varphi
.

3.2.
Lower semi-continuity conjecture for generalized mld’s

In this subsection, we reduce the lower semi-continuity of minimal log discrepancies

Conjecture 3.5

Let

(cf.

)

\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}^{*}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}^{*}M_{i}\Bigr)
is also generalized lc for each
*\in\{+,-\}
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}^{*}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}^{*}M_{i}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}M_{i}\Bigr)-(t^{*}-r_{c^{\prime}})\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}q_{ic^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B_{i}+M_{i}).

Therefore, either of the following holds:

•

0\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}q_{ic^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B_{i}+M_{i})\leq\epsilon_{+}^{-1}a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}M_{i}\Bigr)
, or

•

-\epsilon_{-}^{-1}a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}M_{i}\Bigr)\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}q_{ic^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B_{i}+M_{i})\leq 0
,

where we set
\epsilon_{+}:=t^{+}-r_{c^{\prime}}
and
\epsilon_{-}:=r_{c^{\prime}}-t^{-}
.

It is sufficient to show the discreteness of
B_{\textup{gen}}(d,r,I)\cap[0,a]
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Take
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
q_{ic^{\prime}}\in\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}
for any
i
.
Then, it is sufficient to prove that
B_{\textup{gen}}(d,r,I)\cap[0,a]
is a subset of the following set

\displaystyle\Bigl\{b+\epsilon_{+}e\

\displaystyle\Big|\ b\in B_{\textup{gen}}(d,r,I^{\prime}),e\in\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,\epsilon_{+}^{-1}a]\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\cup\Bigl\{b-\epsilon_{-}e\ \Big|\ b\in B_{\textup{gen}}(d,r,I^{\prime}),e\in\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-\epsilon_{-}^{-1}a,0]\Bigr\}.

In fact, this set is discrete because
B_{\textup{gen}}(d,r,I^{\prime})
is discrete, and both

\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,\epsilon_{+}^{-1}a]
and
\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-\epsilon_{-}^{-1}a,0]

are finite.

Let
\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}M_{i}\Bigr)/Z\in P(d,r,I)

and let
E
be a divisor over
X
.
Assume
a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}M_{i}\Bigr)\in[0,a]
.
Furthermore, suppose
\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}q_{ic^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B_{i}+M_{i})\geq 0

(the same proof also works in the other case).
Then, we have

\displaystyle a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}M_{i}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}^{+}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r_{i}^{+}M_{i}\Bigr)+(t^{+}-r_{c^{\prime}})\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}q_{ic^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B_{i}+M_{i}).

Here, we have

•

a_{E}\Bigl(X,\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r^{+}_{i}B_{i}+\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}r^{+}_{i}M_{i}\Bigr)\in B(d,r,I^{\prime})
,

•

t^{+}-r_{c^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{+}
, and

•

\sum_{0\leq i\leq c}q_{ic^{\prime}}\operatorname{ord}_{E}(B_{i}+M_{i})\in\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,\epsilon_{+}^{-1}a]
.

The proof is complete.
∎

The failure of Hölder regularity of solutions for the Euler-Poincaré equations in Besov spaces
Guorong Qu, Min Li

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we investigate the continuity of solution to the Euler-Poincaré equations. We show that the continuity of the solution cannot be improved to the Hölder continuity. That is, the solution of the Euler-Poincaré equations with initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
but not to
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
with any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Keywords:
Euler-Poincaré equations, Hölder regularity, Besov spaces

MSC (2010):
35Q35; 35B30.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the Cauchy problem for the
Euler-Poincaré equations:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}m+u\cdot\nabla m+\nabla u^{T}\cdot m+(\mathrm{div}u)m=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
m=(1-\Delta)u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{d})
denotes the velocity of the fluid,
m=(m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{d})
represents the momentum. To avoid any confusion, in the component-wise, the first equation of (
1.1
) can be rewrite as

\partial_{t}m_{i}+\sum^{d}_{j=1}u_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}m_{i}+\sum^{d}_{j=1}(\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j})m_{j}+m_{i}\sum^{d}_{j=1}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{j}=0,~~~~~~~~i=1,2,\cdots,d.

The Euler-Poincaré equations (
1.1
) were initially discovered by Holm, Marsden, and Ratiu in
[
18
,
19
]
, serve as a Lagrangian averaged models for modeling and analyzing fluid dynamics, where the Lagrangian is given by the
H^{1}
norm of the fluid velocity in
d
-dimensions. These equations have also been analyzed in the context of geodesic motion on the diffeomorphism group as elaborated by Holm and Staley in
[
17
]
. Additionally, in the specific case where
d=2
, the Euler-Poincaré equations is the same as the averaged template matching equation for computer vision, see references
[
16
,
20
]
.

When
d\geq 2
, the Euler-Poincaré equations are commonly regarded as multi-dimensional generalization of the Camassa-Holm system. This framework is instrumental in the study and modeling of nonlinear shallow water wave dynamics in Holm et al.’s original paper
[
18
]
. Indeed, when
d=1
, the system (
1.1
) simplify to the renowned Camassa-Holm equation

\displaystyle m_{t}+um_{x}+2u_{x}m=0,\quad m=u-u_{xx}.

(CH)

Originally introduced by Camassa and Holm in
[
4
]
, the CH equation is a bi-Hamiltonian model characterizing shallow water wave phenomena. Notably, the CH equation is distinguished by its peakon solutions of the form
Ce^{-|x-Ct|}
, which have generated considerable interest in the physical sciences (see references
[
7
,
31
]
). The CH equation has been the subject of extensive scholarly inquiry, particularly regarding its well-posedness, weak solution dynamics, and analytic or geometric characteristics. Among the numerous studies, research on local well-posedness and ill-posedness for the CH equation’s Cauchy problem is highlighted in
[
9
,
12
,
13
]
. The phenomena of blow-up and the global existence of strong solutions are discussed in
[
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
, while the existence of global weak and dissipative solutions has been explored in
[
2
,
3
,
32
]
, with additional findings cited therein. Furthermore, the non-uniform continuity of the CH equation has been extensively investigated, as evidenced by publications such as
[
14
,
15
,
23
,
25
]
.

In terms of the Euler-Poincaré equations’ rigorous analysis, seminal contributions were made by Chae and Liu
[
5
]
, who established the local existence of weak solutions in
W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ p>d
and the local existence of unique classical solutions in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ s>\frac{d}{2}+3
. Building on this, Yan and Yin
[
33
]
expanded the discourse by discussing the local existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) in Besov spaces. Furthermore, Li, Yu, and Zhai
[
29
]
provided significant insights by proving that solutions to (
1.1
), given a broad spectrum of smooth initial data, either exhibit finite-time blow-up or persist globally in time, thereby resolving a question posed by Chae and Liu
[
5
]
. Subsequent research by Luo and Yin led to new findings regarding blow-up in periodic scenarios, leveraging the rotational invariant properties of the equation
[
27
]
. For a more comprehensive understanding of the Euler-Poincaré equations, we refer to
[
27
,
34
]
.

In more recent studies, the focus has shifted towards the continuity properties of the data-to-solution map for Camassa-Holm type equations, a subject of growing interest as indicated in
[
23
]
. This aspect is crucial for understanding the well-posedness of these equations. Li, Dai, and Zhu
[
26
]
demonstrated that the solutions to (
1.1
) exhibit non-uniform continuous dependence on the initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),s>1+\frac{d}{2}
. This non-uniform continuity result was later extended to the Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),s>\max\{1+\frac{d}{2},\frac{3}{2}\}
in
[
28
]
. The continuity issue of the data-to-solution map for the Euler-Poincaré equations in Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ s>\max\{1+\frac{d}{p},\frac{3}{2}\}
has been resolved in
[
21
]
, where an ill-posedness result is ultimately established.

As part of the local well-posedness results in Besov spaces of Yan-Yin
[
33
]
, we known that, if
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
with
s>\max\{1+\frac{d}{p},\frac{3}{2}\},\ (p,r)\in[1,+\infty)^{2}
or
s=1+\frac{d}{p},\ r=1,\ 1\leq p<2d
, then there exists a solution
u\in C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
for the Euler-Poincaré equations (
1.1
). In this paper, we are interested in the following

Hölder Continuity Problem :

Whether or not the solution
u(t)
for the Euler-Poincaré equations (
1.1
) from initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
can belong to
C^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

More specifically, if the initial data
u_{0}
have more regularity such that
u_{0}\in B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r}
for some
s^{\prime}>s
, by the interpolation argument, we can deduce that
u\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with
\alpha=s^{\prime}-s
. In this paper, we will show that there exists initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
such that the corresponding solution of the Euler-Poincaré equations (
1.1
) cannot belong to
C^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r})
with any
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Namely, we provide a negative response to the question:

u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\implies}}u\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r})\text{ with }\alpha\in(0,1).

Consequently, this also negates the question posed in
Hölder Continuity Problem
. We summarize the main results as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Assume that
(s,p,r)
satisfies

\displaystyle 1\leq p,r\leq\infty,s>\max\{1+\frac{d}{p},\frac{3}{2}\}\quad\mathrm{or}\quad 1\leq p<2d,r=1,s=1+\frac{d}{p}.

(1.2)

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exits
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)
satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{t^{\alpha}}=+\infty.

For the sake of simplicity, we first transform the Euler-Poincaré equations (
1.1
) into transport type equations of the velocity
u(t,x)
. According to Yan-Yin
[
33
]
, (
1.1
) is equivalent to the nonlocal system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u=Q(u,u)+R(u,u):=\mathbf{P}(u),\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(1.3)

here

\displaystyle Q(u,v)=-(1-\Delta)^{-1}\mathrm{div}\Big(\nabla u\nabla v+\nabla u(\nabla v)^{T}-(\nabla u)^{T}\nabla v-(\mathrm{div}u)\nabla v+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}(\nabla u:\nabla v)\Big),

\displaystyle R(u,v)=-(1-\Delta)^{-1}\Big((\mathrm{div}u)v+(\nabla u)^{T}\cdot v\Big).

Where, we have used the following notations

\displaystyle(\nabla u^{T})_{i,j}=\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j},\quad(u\cdot\nabla v)_{i}=\sum^{d}_{k=1}u_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}u_{i},\quad(\nabla u\nabla v)_{ij}=\sum^{d}_{k=1}\partial_{x_{i}}u_{k}\partial_{x_{k}}v_{j},

\displaystyle\nabla u:\nabla v=\sum^{d}_{i,j=1}\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}v_{j},\quad\big((\nabla u)^{T}\cdot v\big)_{i}=\sum^{d}_{j=1}\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j}v_{j}.

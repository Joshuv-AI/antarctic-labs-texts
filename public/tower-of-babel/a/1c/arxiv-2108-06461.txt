Hom-Yang-Baxter equations and Hom-Yang-Baxter systems
Shengxiang Wang, Xiaohui Zhang, Shuangjian Guo 1. School of Mathematics and Finance, Chuzhou University,Chuzhou 239000, China2. School of Mathematical Sciences, Qufu Normal University,Qufu 273165, China3. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Guizhou University ofFinance and Economics, Guiyang 550025, China

Abstract




ABSTRACT

In this paper, we mainly present some new solutions of the Hom-Yang-Baxter equation from Hom-algebras,
Hom-coalgebras and Hom-Lie algebras, respectively.
Also, we prove that these solutions are all self-inverse and give some examples.
Finally, we introduce the notion of Hom-Yang-Baxter systems and obtain two kinds of Hom-Yang-Baxter systems.

Key words
: Hom-Yang-Baxter equation; Hom-Yang-Baxter system; Hom-algebra; Hom-coalgebra; Hom-Lie algebra.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
16T25; 17A30; 17B38.

INTRODUCTION

The Yang-Baxter equation (YBE), which was first introduced by Yang, Baxter and McGuire
[
1
,
28
]
.
YBE has various forms in physics and plays an important role in many topics in mathematical physics, including quantum groups, quantum integrable systems, braided categories and invariants of knots and links.

Recently, a twisted Hom-type generalization of the YBE called Hom-Yang-Baxter equation (HYBE) was introduced in
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
by Yau.
The HYBE states

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)=(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha),

where
\alpha
is an endomorphism of the vector space
V
, and
B:V\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes V
is a bilinear map that commutes with
\alpha\otimes\alpha
.
In which Yau constructed several classes of solutions of the HYBE, generalizing the solutions of the YBE from Lie algebras and quasitriangular bialgebras.
Later, Yau
[
32
]
extended the the classical Yang-Baxter equation to the classical Hom-Yang-Baxter equation (CHYBE) in a Hom-Lie algebra
and studied the related algebraic structure.

The study of Hom-algebras can be traced back to Hartwig, Larsson and Silvestrov’s work in
[
7
]
,
where the notion of Hom-Lie algebra in the context of q-deformation theory of Witt and Virasoro algebras
[
8
]
was introduced,
which plays an important role in physics, mainly in conformal field theory.
Hom-algebras and Hom-coalgebras were introduced by Makhlouf and Silvestrov
[
15
]
as
generalizations of ordinary algebras and coalgebras in the following sense:
the associativity of the multiplication is replaced by
the Hom-associativity and similar for Hom-coassociativity.
They also defined the structures of Hom-bialgebras and Hom-Hopf algebras,
and described some of their properties extending properties of ordinary bialgebras and Hopf algebras
in
[
16
,
17
]
.
Many more properties and structures of Hom-Hopf algebras have been developed,
see
[
4
,
6
,
12
]
and references cited therein.

In
[
29
,
31
]
Yau proposed the definition of quasitriangular Hom-Hopf algebras
and showed that each quasitriangular Hom-Hopf algebra yields a solution of the Hom-Yang-Baxter equation.
Meanwhile, several classes of solutions of the Hom-Yang-Baxter equation were constructed from different respects,
including those associated
to Hom-Lie algebras
[
5
,
25
,
29
,
30
]
, Drinfeld (co)doubles
[
2
,
34
,
35
]
and Hom-Yetter-Drinfeld modules
[
3
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
18
,
26
,
33
]
.

In
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
, Nichita, Parashar and Popovici presented some solutions of the
YBE from associative algebras, coassociative coalgebras and Lie algebras respectively.
Depending on the above working, the motivation of constructing new solutions of HYBE is natural.
The purpose of the present paper is to investigate how to construct solutions of HYBE from Hom-algebras,
Hom-coalgebras and Hom-Lie algebras, and show two kinds of Hom-Yang-Baxter systems from Hom-algebras and Hom-coalgebras.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 1, we recall some basic definitions about Hom-algebras, Hom-coalgebras and Hom-Lie algebras.
In Section 2, we show two solutions of HYBE from Hom-algebras and prove that they are self-inverse (see Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.4).
In Section 3, we present two solutions of HYBE from Hom-coalgebras and prove that they are self-inverse (see Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 3.4).
In Section 4, we obtain a solution of HYBE from Hom-Lie algebras and prove that it is self-inverse (see Theorem 4.1).
In Section 5, we introduce the notion of Hom-Yang-Baxter systems and present two kinds of Hom-Yang-Baxter systems (see Theorem 5.2 and Theorem 5.3).

PRELIMINARIES

Throughout this paper,
k
is a fixed field.
Unless otherwise stated, all vector spaces, algebras, modules, maps and unadorned tensor products are over
k
.
For a coalgebra
C
, the coproduct will be denoted by
\Delta
.
We adopt a Sweedler’s notation
\triangle(c)=c_{1}\otimes c_{2}
, for any
c\in C
, where the summation is understood.
We refer to
[
22
]
for the Hopf algebra theory and terminology.

We now recall some useful definitions in
[
9
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.

Definition 1.1.
A Hom-algebra is a quadruple
(A,\mu,1_{A},\alpha)
(abbr.
(A,\alpha)
), where
A
is a
k
-linear space,

\mu:A\otimes A\longrightarrow A
is a
k
-linear map,
1_{A}\in A
and
\alpha
is a endomorphism of
A
, such that

\displaystyle(HA1)

\displaystyle\alpha(aa^{\prime})=\alpha(a)\alpha(a^{\prime});~~\alpha(1_{A})=1_{A},

\displaystyle(HA2)

\displaystyle\alpha(a)(a^{\prime}a^{\prime\prime})=(aa^{\prime})\alpha(a^{\prime\prime});~~a1_{A}=1_{A}a=\alpha(a)

are satisfied for
a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in A
. Here we use the notation
\mu(a\otimes a^{\prime})=aa^{\prime}
.

Definition 1.2.
A Hom-coalgebra is a quadruple
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon,\alpha)
(abbr.
(C,\alpha)
),
where
C
is a
k
-linear space,
\Delta:C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
,
\varepsilon:C\longrightarrow k
are
k
-linear maps,
and
\alpha
is a endomorphism of
C
, such that

\displaystyle(HC1)

\displaystyle\alpha(c)_{1}\otimes\alpha(c)_{2}=\alpha(c_{1})\otimes\alpha(c_{2});~~\varepsilon\circ\alpha=\varepsilon;

\displaystyle(HC2)

\displaystyle\alpha(c_{1})\otimes c_{21}\otimes c_{22}=c_{11}\otimes c_{12}\otimes\alpha(c_{2});~~\epsilon(c_{1})c_{2}=c_{1}\epsilon(c_{2})=\alpha(c)

are satisfied for
c\in C
.

Definition 1.3.

A Hom-Lie algebra is a triple
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
consisting of a linear space
L
,
a bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]:L\otimes L\rightarrow L

and a endomorphism
\alpha:L\rightarrow L
, such that

\displaystyle(HL1)

\displaystyle[l,l^{\prime}]=-[l^{\prime},l],

\displaystyle(HL2)

\displaystyle[\alpha(l),[l^{\prime},l^{\prime\prime}]]+[\alpha(l^{\prime}),[l^{\prime\prime},l]]+[\alpha(l^{\prime\prime}),[l,l^{\prime}]]=0

are satisfied for all
l,l^{\prime},l^{\prime\prime}\in L
.

2
Solutions of the HYBE from Hom-algebras

In this section, we will give two kinds of solutions of the HYBE from Hom-algebras and prove that these solutions are both self-inverse.

Theorem 2.1.

Let
(A,\mu,1_{A},\alpha)
be a Hom-algebra and
\lambda,\nu\in k
.
Then

\displaystyle B:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes A,~a\otimes b\mapsto\lambda ab\otimes 1_{A}+\nu 1_{A}\otimes ab-\lambda\alpha(a)\otimes\alpha(b)

is a solution for HYBE.

Proof.
We first show that
B
is compatible with the twist map
\alpha
.
For this, we take any
a,b\in A
and calculate

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\alpha)\circ B(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\alpha)(\lambda ab\otimes 1_{A}+\nu 1_{A}\otimes ab-\lambda\alpha(a)\otimes\alpha(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A}+\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)-\lambda\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(b),

\displaystyle B\circ(\alpha\otimes\alpha)(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\alpha(a)\alpha(b)\otimes 1_{A}+\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(a)\alpha(b)-\lambda\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(b).

It follows that
(\alpha\otimes\alpha)\circ B=B\circ(\alpha\otimes\alpha)
, as desired.

Next we will verify that
B
satisfies the HYBE. In fact, for any
a,b,c\in A
,
one may directly check that

B(a\otimes 1_{A})=\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(a).

On the one hand, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\alpha(a)\otimes(\lambda bc\otimes 1_{A}+\nu 1_{A}\otimes bc-\lambda\alpha(b)\otimes\alpha(c)),

\displaystyle(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda B(\alpha(a)\otimes bc)\otimes 1_{A}+\nu B(\alpha(a)\otimes 1_{A})\otimes\alpha(bc)-\lambda B(\alpha(a)\otimes\alpha(b))\otimes\alpha^{2}(c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda^{2}\alpha(a)(bc)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}+\lambda\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(a)(bc)\otimes 1_{A}-\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha(bc)-\lambda^{2}\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(c)-\lambda\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(b)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c),

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}(a)(bc)\otimes B(1_{A}\otimes 1_{A})+\lambda\nu 1_{A}\otimes B(\alpha(a)(bc)\otimes 1_{A})-\lambda^{2}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes B(\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes B(\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha(bc))-\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes B(1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(c))-\lambda\nu 1_{A}\otimes B(\alpha(ab)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c))

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\lambda^{2}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes B(\alpha^{2}(b)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c))

\displaystyle~~~~=\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}}_{(1)}+\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)}_{(2)}-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)}_{(3)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(4)}+\underline{\nu^{3}1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)}_{(5)}-\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)}_{(6)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(7)}-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(8)}+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(9)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\alpha^{23}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(10)}-\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)}_{(11)}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(12)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(13)}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)}_{(14)}-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{3}(b)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(15)}

\displaystyle~~~~=\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}}_{(1)}+\underline{\nu^{3}1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)}_{(5)}-\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)}_{(6)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(7)}-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(8)}+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(9)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(12)}+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(13)}-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{3}(b)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(15)}.

The last equality holds since (2)=(11), (3)=(14) and (4)=(10).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle(B\otimes\alpha)(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda ab\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(c)+\nu 1_{A}\otimes ab\otimes\alpha(c)-\lambda\alpha(a)\otimes\alpha(b)\otimes\alpha(c),

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda\alpha(ab)\otimes B(1_{A}\otimes\alpha(c))+\nu 1_{A}\otimes B(ab\otimes\alpha(c))-\lambda\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes B(\alpha(b)\otimes\alpha(c))

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda^{2}\alpha(ab)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c)\otimes 1_{A}+\lambda\nu\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(c)-\lambda^{2}\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(c)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\lambda\nu 1_{A}\otimes(ab)\alpha(c)\otimes 1_{A}+\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes(ab)\alpha(c)-\lambda\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A}-\lambda\nu\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(bc)+\lambda^{2}\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(b)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c),

\displaystyle(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)(a\otimes b\otimes c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda^{2}B(\alpha(ab)\otimes\alpha^{2}(c))\otimes 1_{A}+\lambda\nu B(\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A})\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)-\lambda^{2}B(\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A})\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\lambda\nu B(1_{A}\otimes(ab)\alpha(c))\otimes 1_{A}+\nu^{2}B(1_{A}\otimes 1_{A})\otimes\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)-\lambda\nu B(1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab))\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\lambda^{2}B(\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha(bc))\otimes 1_{A}-\lambda\nu B(\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes 1_{A})\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)+\lambda^{2}B(\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(b))\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)

\displaystyle~~~~=\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}}_{(16)}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(17)}-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(18)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(19)}+\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(20)}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}}_{(21)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(22)}-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(23)}+\underline{\nu^{3}1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)}_{(24)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(25)}-\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(26)}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(27)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}}_{(28)}-\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(a)\alpha(bc)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(29)}+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(30)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)}_{(31)}+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{32}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(33)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{3}(b)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(34)}

\displaystyle~~~~=-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(18)}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}}_{(21)}+\underline{\nu^{3}1_{A}\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\alpha^{2}(c)}_{(24)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(25)}+\underline{\lambda^{2}\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(27)}+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)\otimes 1_{A}}_{(30)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\underline{\lambda\nu^{2}1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(bc)}_{(31)}+\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{2}(ab)\otimes 1_{A}\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(32)}-\underline{\lambda^{3}\alpha^{3}(a)\otimes\alpha^{3}(b)\otimes\alpha^{3}(c)}_{(34)}.

The last equality holds since (16)=(28), (17)=(23), (19)=(33), (20)=(26) and (22)=(29).

Compare two expressions above, we have

\displaystyle(1)=(21),(5)=(24),(6)=(31),(7)=(18),

\displaystyle(8)=(25),(9)=(32),(12)=(27),(13)=(30),(15)=(34).

It follows that

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)(a\otimes b\otimes c)=(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)(a\otimes b\otimes c).

So
B
is a solution for HYBE.
\hfill\Box

Corollary 2.2.

Let
(A,\mu,1_{A},\alpha)
be a Hom-algebra and
\lambda,\nu\in k^{\ast}
.
Assume that
\alpha
is involutive, then the solution
B
in Theorem 2.1 is invertible,
where the inverse is given by

\displaystyle B^{-1}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes A,~a\otimes b\mapsto\frac{1}{\nu}ab\otimes 1_{A}+\frac{1}{\lambda}1_{A}\otimes ab-\frac{1}{\lambda}\alpha(a)\otimes\alpha(b).

Proof.

We first show that
B\circ B^{-1}=id_{A\otimes A}
. In fact, for any
a,b,c\in A
,
we have

\displaystyle B\circ B^{-1}(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu}B(ab\otimes 1_{A})+\frac{1}{\lambda}B(1_{A}\otimes ab)-\frac{1}{B}({\lambda}\alpha(a)\otimes\alpha(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\{\lambda\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A}+\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)-\lambda 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\lambda}\{\lambda\alpha(ab)\otimes 1_{A}+\nu 1_{A}\otimes\alpha(ab)-\lambda\alpha^{2}(a)\otimes\alpha^{2}(b)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\otimes b.

It follows that
B\circ B^{-1}=id_{A\otimes A}
.
Similarly, one may check that
B^{-1}\circ B=id_{A\otimes A}
. So
B^{-1}
is the inverse of
B
.

\hfill\Box

Theorem 3.1.

Let
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon,\alpha)
be a Hom-coalgebra and
\lambda,\nu\in k
.
Then

\displaystyle B:L\otimes L\rightarrow L\otimes L,~x\otimes y\mapsto\lambda[x,y]\otimes u-\upsilon\alpha(y)\otimes\alpha(x)

is a solution for HYBE, where
Z(L)=\{u\in L|[u,x]=0,\forall x\in L\}
.

Proof.
Obviously,
B
is compatible with the twist map
\alpha
since
z
is
\alpha
-invariant.
Now we verify that
B
satisfy the HYBE. In fact, for any
x,y,z\in L
,
one may directly check that

B(x\otimes u)=-\upsilon u\otimes\alpha(x),~B(u\otimes x)=-\nu\alpha(x)\otimes u.

On the one hand, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)(x\otimes y\otimes z)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda\alpha(x)\otimes[y,z]\otimes u-\nu\alpha(x)\otimes\alpha(z)\otimes\alpha(y),

\displaystyle(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)(x\otimes y\otimes z)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda B(\alpha(x)\otimes[y,z])\otimes u-\nu B(\alpha(x)\otimes\alpha(z))\otimes\alpha^{2}(y)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda^{2}[\alpha(x),[y,z]]\otimes u\otimes u-\lambda\nu[\alpha(y),\alpha(z)]\otimes\alpha^{2}(x)\otimes u

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\lambda\nu[\alpha(x),\alpha(z)]\otimes u\otimes\alpha^{2}(y)+\nu^{2}\alpha^{2}(z)\otimes\alpha^{2}(x)\otimes\alpha^{2}(y),

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)(x\otimes y\otimes z)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda^{2}[\alpha^{2}(x),[\alpha(y),\alpha(z)]]\otimes B(u\otimes u)-\lambda\nu[\alpha^{2}(y),\alpha^{2}(z)]\otimes B(\alpha^{2}(x)\otimes u)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\lambda\nu[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(z)]\otimes B(u\otimes\alpha^{2}(y))+\nu^{2}\alpha^{3}(z)\otimes B(\alpha^{2}(x)\otimes\alpha^{2}(y))

\displaystyle~~~~=\underline{-\lambda^{2}\nu[\alpha^{2}(x),[\alpha(y),\alpha(z)]]\otimes u\otimes u}+\lambda\nu^{2}[\alpha^{2}(y),\alpha^{2}(z)]\otimes u\otimes\alpha^{3}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\lambda\nu^{2}[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(z)]\otimes\alpha^{3}(y)\otimes u+\lambda\nu^{2}\alpha^{3}(z)\otimes[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(y)]\otimes u

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\nu^{3}\alpha^{3}(z)\otimes\alpha^{3}(y)\otimes\alpha^{3}(x).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle(B\otimes\alpha)(x\otimes y\otimes z)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda[x,y]\otimes u\otimes\alpha(z)-\nu\alpha(y)\otimes\alpha(x)\otimes\alpha(z),

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)(x\otimes y\otimes z)

\displaystyle~~~~=\lambda[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]\otimes B(u\otimes\alpha(z))-\nu\alpha^{2}(y)\otimes B(\alpha(x)\otimes\alpha(z))

\displaystyle~~~~=-\lambda\nu[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]\otimes\alpha^{2}(z)\otimes u-\lambda\nu\alpha^{2}(y)\otimes[\alpha(x),\alpha(z)]\otimes u

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\nu^{2}\alpha^{2}(y)\otimes\alpha^{2}(z)\otimes\alpha^{2}(x),

\displaystyle(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)(x\otimes y\otimes z)

\displaystyle~~~~=-\lambda\nu B([\alpha(x),\alpha(y)]\otimes\alpha^{2}(z))\otimes u-\lambda\nu B(\alpha^{2}(y)\otimes[\alpha(x),\alpha(z)])\otimes u

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\nu^{2}B(\alpha^{2}(y)\otimes\alpha^{2}(z))\otimes\alpha^{3}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~=\underline{-\lambda^{2}\nu[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha^{2}(z)]\otimes u\otimes u}+\lambda\nu^{2}\alpha^{3}(z)\otimes[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(y)]\otimes u

\displaystyle~~~~~~~\underline{-\lambda^{2}\nu[\alpha^{2}(y),[\alpha(x),\alpha(z)]]\otimes u\otimes u}+\lambda\nu^{2}[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(z)]\otimes\alpha^{3}(y)\otimes u

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\lambda\nu^{2}[\alpha^{2}(y),\alpha^{2}(z)]\otimes u\otimes\alpha^{3}(x)-\nu^{3}\alpha^{3}(z)\otimes\alpha^{3}(y)\otimes\alpha^{3}(x).

According to the anti-symmetry and the Hom-Jacobi identity, we have

\displaystyle[\alpha^{2}(x),[\alpha(y),\alpha(z)]]=[[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)],\alpha^{2}(z)]+[\alpha^{2}(y),[\alpha(x),\alpha(z)]].

It follows that

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)(x\otimes y\otimes z)=(B\otimes\alpha)\circ(\alpha\otimes B)\circ(B\otimes\alpha)(x\otimes y\otimes z).

That is,
B
is a solution for HYBE.

Corollary 4.2.

Let

5
Hom-Yang-Baxter systems

In this section, we extend the notion of Yang-Baxter systems to Hom-Yang-Baxter systems and present two kinds of Hom-Yang-Baxter systems.

Consider three vector spaces
V,V^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime}
,
let
\alpha_{V},\alpha_{V^{\prime}},\alpha_{V^{\prime\prime}}
be three endomorphisms on
V,V^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime}
and

R:V\otimes V^{\prime}\rightarrow V\otimes V^{\prime},S:V\otimes V^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow V\otimes V^{\prime\prime},T:V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime\prime}
be three linear maps.
Then a Hom-Yang-Baxter commutator is a map
[R,S,T]:V\otimes V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow V\otimes V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime\prime}
defined by

\displaystyle[R,S,T]=R^{12}\circ S^{13}\circ T^{23}-T^{23}\circ S^{13}\circ R^{12},

where
R^{12}=R\otimes\alpha_{V^{\prime\prime}},~S^{13}=(\tau\otimes id)\circ(\alpha_{V^{\prime}}\otimes T)\circ(\tau\otimes id),~T^{23}=\alpha_{V}\otimes T
.

Definition 5.1.

Let
V,V^{\prime}
be two vector spaces and
\alpha_{V},\alpha_{V^{\prime}}
be two endomorphisms.
A system of linear maps

\displaystyle W:V\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes V,~Z:V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime}\rightarrow V^{\prime}\otimes V^{\prime},~X:V\otimes V^{\prime}\rightarrow V\otimes V^{\prime}

is called a Hom-Yang-Baxter system, if the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle[W,W,W]=0,~[Z,Z,Z]=0,~[W,X,X]=0,~[X,X,Z]=0.

(5.1)

Theorem 5.2.

Let
(A,\mu,1_{A},\alpha)
be a Hom-algebra and

.

It is sufficient to prove that the four equalities in Eq. (5.1) hold.

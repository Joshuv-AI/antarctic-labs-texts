Bounds on Sphere Sizes in the Sum-Rank Metric and Coordinate-Additive Metrics
Hugo Beeloo-Sauerbier Couvée, Thomas Jerkovits, Jessica Bariffi

Abstract
This paper provides new bounds on the size of spheres in any coordinate-additive metric with a particular focus on improving existing bounds in the sum-rank metric. We derive improved upper and lower bounds based on the entropy of a distribution related to the Boltzmann distribution, which work for any coordinate-additive metric. Additionally, we derive new closed-form upper and lower bounds specifically for the sum-rank metric that outperform existing closed-form bounds.

Introduction

Classically, the study of error-correcting codes focuses on codes in the Hamming metric
Hamming (1950)
where the amount of errors happening depends on the number of entries that change during the transmission. With different applications, other coordinate-additive metrics were introduced and gained more attention. The sum-rank metric
da Nóbrega and Filho (2010)
, Lee metric
Lee (1958)
, and also the Hamming metric, are examples of coordinate-additive metrics. Codes with distance properties in such metrics are of particular interest in various applications, such as linear network coding
Lu and Kumar (2005)
, quantum-resistant cryptography
Horlemann-Trautmann and Weger (2021)
;
Puchinger et al. (2022)
, coding for storage
Martínez-Peñas and Kschischang (2019)
, space-time coding
Shehadeh and Kschischang (2021)
.

One crucial task is to understand the performance limits of codes endowed with a given coordinate-additive metric. Bounds like the sphere-packing bound or the Gilbert–Varshamov bound
Gilbert (1952)
play a crucial role in understanding these limits, and are derived using bounds on the size of an
\ell
-dimensional ball or sphere in the corresponding metrics. The exact value for the size of an
\ell
-dimensional sphere
\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}
of radius
t
in any coordinate-additive metric can be derived by computing all its (ordered) integer partitions, where each part of the partition has at most a part size of the maximal possible weight in the corresponding metric. These represent the decomposition of the nonzero entries of the elements in the sphere. To obtain the size of the sphere, we sum over all integer partitions, adding up the number of elements that have a weight decomposition corresponding to the integer partition. Although this procedure provides the exact value of
\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
, it often does not give an intuitive or practical understanding of the sphere size or how this size changes as the parameters change. For large parameters, it is even impractical to compute the size in this way. Hence, the derivation of closed-form bounds on the exact formula is of major interest. A current method of obtaining both upper and lower bounds on
\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
is to consider only the partition attaining the maximum number of elements. This approach is utilized by
Ott et al. (2021)
;
Puchinger et al. (2022)
;
Gruica et al. (2023)
. Another method is to bound the size of an
\ell
-dimensional ball
\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\ell}
of radius
t
, since every upper bound on
\left|{\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
is also a valid upper bound on
\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
. On a complex analytic side, sizes of spheres and balls can be described using generating functions, whose coefficients can be computed using the saddle-point technique and other techniques from analytic combinatorics (see
Flajolet and Sedgewick (2009)
;
Gardy and Solé (1991)
). In 1994, Löliger presented an information-theoretic approach for bounding the volume of an
\ell
-dimensional ball concerning any coordinate-additive metric, via the entropy of an auxiliary probability distribution
Loeliger (1994)
. Similar arguments have been used to derive bounds for Lee-metric codes
Bhattacharya and Banerjee (2019)
;
Bariffi et al. (2024)
.

Specifically addressing the sum-rank metric, closed-form upper and lower bounds for the sphere size were introduced in
Puchinger et al. (2022)
;
Ott et al. (2021)
and further discussed in
Gruica et al. (2023)
. However, these bounds are limited in their tightness, particularly noticeable in scenarios involving smaller sizes of the base field
q
and/or a larger number of blocks
\ell
.

In this paper we will discuss
different
approaches to bound sizes of spheres in a coordinate-additive metric. We derive novel improved lower and upper bounds on that size first in a general setting for any coordinate-additive metric and later explicitly focusing on the sum-rank metric.
The paper is organized as follows. We start by introducing the basic notions and concepts needed throughout the paper in Section
2
. In Section
3
we present bounds on the size of a sphere coming from
an
information-theoretic argument. We start the section by introducing the entropy function of a random variable, as well as the probability distribution of a typical sequence in an
\ell
-dimensional sphere endowed with a given coordinate-additive metric. Furthermore, we recap existing upper bounds given by the entropy of the typical sequences. In a next step, we give novel upper and lower bounds using the entropy of a typical sequence. The bounds derived hold for any coordinate-additive metric over a given alphabet. We then turn our attention to the sum-rank metric in Section
4
where we first recap the existing bounds on the size of an
\ell
-dimensional sphere in the sum-rank metric. Next, we present improved upper and lower bounds on the size of such a sphere using convolution arguments and ordinary generating functions, respectively. The new bounds presented in the Sections
3
and
4
are then compared to already existing bounds in Section
5
. Final conclusions are given in Section
6
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the necessary definitions, notations, and concepts that are used throughout the paper.
In the following, let
q
be a prime power and denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field of
q
elements. The natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
include
0
.

2.1
Coordinate-Additive Metrics

Let
(\mathcal{A},+)
be a finite abelian group with identity element
0
called the
alphabet
. We define a
weight function

\wt_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{N}
on
\mathcal{A}
to be a function satisfying for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
:

1.

\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=0
if and only if
a=0
,

2.

\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(-a)
,

3.

\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a+b)\leq\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)+\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(b)
.

This function can be extended to a
coordinate-additive weight function
on the cartesian product
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
(with group structure inherited coordinate-wise from
\mathcal{A}
) by defining the weight of an
\ell
-tuple to be the sum of the weights of its coordinates, i.e.,

\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a_{i}).

This coordinate-additive weight function naturally induces a

metric

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\dist_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}:&\mathcal{A}^{\ell}\times\mathcal{A}^{\ell}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{N}\\
&(v,w)&\longmapsto&\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v-w).\end{array}

Examples of well-studied metrics induced by coordinate-additive weight functions are the
Hamming metric
with weight function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\wt_{H}:&\mathcal{A}^{\ell}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{N}\\
&(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})&\longmapsto&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}[a_{i}\neq 0]\end{array}

where
[\cdot]
denotes the Iverson bracket and the
Lee metric
with weight function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\wt_{L}:&(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\ell}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{N}\\
&(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})&\longmapsto&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\min(a_{i},n-a_{i}),\end{array}

Of particular interest to this paper is the
sum-rank metric
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
, the space of
m\times n
matrices over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
m
and
n
positive integers.
In this paper, we focus on the case where
n=\eta\ell
, for two positive integers
\eta
and
\ell
.
Then,
under the isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta\ell}\cong(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta})^{\ell}
, every matrix
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
can be represented as a sequence of
\ell
blocks
of
m\times\eta
matrices
B_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta}
, i.e.
M=(B_{1}\mid B_{2}\mid\ldots\mid B_{\ell})
, and its
sum-rank weight
is given by the weight function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\wt_{\Sigma R}:&(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta})^{\ell}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{N}\\
&(B_{1}\mid B_{2}\mid\ldots\mid B_{\ell})&\longmapsto&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\rk_{q}(B_{i}).\end{array}

Here
\rk_{q}(B_{i})
denotes the rank of
B_{i}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The special case of the sum-rank metric with
\ell=1
is called the
rank-metric
. Also note that the cases
m=1
or
\eta=1
are examples of the Hamming metric.

Given a coordinate-additive weight function
\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(\cdot)
on
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
, it is important to understand the size of an
\ell
-dimensional
sphere
and
ball
, respectively, of radius
t\in\mathbb{N}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}

\displaystyle:=\{v\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}\ :\ \wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v)=t\}=\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(t)

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\ell}

\displaystyle:=\{v\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}\ :\ \wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v)\leq t\}=\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}^{-1}(\{0,1,\ldots,t\}).

(11)

Clearly, the following identities are satisfied

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\ell}\cap\mathcal{S}_{j}^{\ell}=\emptyset\text{ if }i\neq j,\quad\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{t}\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}=\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\ell}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{t-1}^{\ell}.

(12)

For the sum-rank metric we define

\mu_{\Sigma R}:=\min\{m,\eta\}\,\text{ and }\,n:=\eta\ell,

and denote for every
0\leq t\leq\mu_{\Sigma R}\ell
the
sum-rank sphere
and
sum-rank ball
, respectively, of radius
t
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta\ell}\ :\ \wt_{\Sigma R}(M)=t\}

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\{M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta\ell}\ :\ \wt_{\Sigma R}(M)\leq t\}.

(14)

For fixed
m
,
\eta
,
q
,
\ell
, the sum-rank sphere sizes
\big|\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}\big|
can be computed with a dynamic program described in
Puchinger et al. (2022)
or a closed-form described in Subsection
2.2
. However, this formula is generally not practical to work with when an intuitive understanding of the approximate size is needed, such as theorems involving sphere-packings or sphere-coverings. Therefore, easy-to-work-with approximations or lower/upper bounds for the sphere sizes for all
m
,
\eta
,
q
,
\ell
are of value to research on the sum-rank metric.

2.2
Sizes of Spheres

Consider an alphabet
\mathcal{A}
and a coordinate-additive weight
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
\ell
and
t
be positive integers and
\mathcal{S}^{\ell}_{t}
be the
\ell
-dimensional sphere of radius
t
with respect to the weight
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}
as introduced in (
10
). The exact size of
\mathcal{S}^{\ell}_{t}
can be obtained by computing all the (ordered) integer partitions of
t
, where each part size is at most the maximum possible weight in the corresponding metric. More explicitly, let us define the set of all possible weights in
\mathcal{A}
by

W_{\mathcal{A}}=\{\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)\ :\ a\in\mathcal{A}\}\subset\mathbb{N}.

Let us denote the maximum possible weight in
W_{\mathcal{A}}
by
\mu:=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left\{{\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}\right\}
.
We denote by
A_{j}
the number of elements
a\in\mathcal{A}
of weight
j\in W_{\mathcal{A}}
, i.e.,

A_{j}=\left|{\{a\in\mathcal{A}\ :\ \wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=j\}}\right|.

Furthermore, for
t,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}_{t}
denote the set of all (ordered) integer partitions of
t
of length
\ell
with part sizes not exceeding
\mu
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}:=\{(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{\ell})\in W_{\mathcal{A}}^{\,\ell}\ :\ t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell}=t\}.

(15)

Then, the size of the
\ell
-dimensional sphere of radius
t
can be computed as

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell})\in\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}A_{t_{i}}.

(16)

This formula can further be reduced depending on the metrics considered. For instance, in the Hamming metric, since tuples of Hamming weight
t
consist of
t
nonzero positions, we compute the number of ways to choose
t
positions among a total number of positions
\ell
and multiply by the number of options we have to choose the nonzero element from, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}^{\ell}_{t,\mathsf{H}}}\right|=\binom{\ell}{t}(\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|-1)^{t}.

(17)

Hence, in the Hamming metric we obtain a compact closed form expression for
\left|{\mathcal{S}^{\ell}_{t}}\right|
. However, when changing the metric this task can get more involved, as the set
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}_{t}
might grow rapidly and, thus, summing over all its elements would be an exhaustive task. Therefore, finding simpler upper and lower bounds on the sum or the single summands is an important task.

For a fixed weight function
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}
and fixed integers
t
,
\ell
the sphere sizes
\left|\mathcal{S}^{\ell}_{t}\right|
can be computed efficiently if the values of
A_{j}
are known with a dynamic program that uses formula (
16
). This is demonstrated in
(
Puchinger et al., 2022
, Algorithm 1)
for specifically the sum-rank metric, but the used algorithm can easily be adjusted for other coordinate-additive metrics.
Similar to (
16
), we can calculate the size
\left|{\mathcal{S}^{\ell}_{t}}\right|
by summing over all weight decompositions of the weight
t
and adding the number of tuples with that weight decomposition. From an information-theoretic point of view we use the notion of a
type
of a tuple
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
. For any positive integers
\ell
and
t
, the type of
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
is a tuple
\theta(x)=(\theta_{0}(x),\ldots,\theta_{\mu}(x))
where each
\theta_{i}(x)
is the relative fraction of occurrences of a weight
i\in W_{\mathcal{A}}
in
x
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{\ell}\left|{\left\{{k\in\left\{{1,\ldots,\ell}\right\}\,:\,\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(x_{k})=i}\right\}}\right|.

(18)

Notice that the weight decomposition of
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
is immediately derived from its type
\theta(x)
by
\ell\theta(x)=(\ell\theta_{0}(x),\ldots,\ell\theta_{\mu}(x))
and indeed
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(x)=\ell\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}\theta_{i}(x)
. Furthermore, if
\wt(x)=t
then
\left\{{0}\right\}^{\ell\theta_{0}}\times\ldots\times\left\{{\mu}\right\}^{\ell\theta_{\mu}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\ell}_{t}
. Hence, to calculate
\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
, instead of summing over all partitions in
\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}
we can sum over all types in
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
yielding weight
t
. We will denote this set by
\Theta_{t}^{\ell}
. The number of tuples
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
of type
\theta
is given by the multinomial coefficient

\displaystyle\binom{\ell}{\ell\theta}=\frac{\ell!}{(\ell\theta_{0})!\cdot\ldots\cdot(\ell\theta_{\mu})!}.

(19)

Hence, the size of the
\ell
-dimensional sphere of radius
t
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|=\sum_{\ell\theta\in\Theta^{\ell}_{t}}\binom{\ell}{\ell\theta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}.}

(20)

This value, however, is hard to manipulate wherefore we might use the following upper and lower bounds (see
(
Cover and Thomas, 2006
, Theorem 11.1.3)
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\ell+1)^{\text{}^{\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|-1}}}2^{\ell H(\theta)}\leq\binom{\ell}{\ell\theta}\leq 2^{\ell H(\theta)},

(21)

where
H(\theta):=-\sum_{i:\theta_{i}\neq 0}\theta_{i}\log_{2}(\theta_{i})
is the entropy of
\theta
.
With this we immediately obtain upper and lower bounds on
\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
. Regarding the upper bound, further improvements can be achieved which we will discuss in Section
3
.

2.3
Ordinary Generating Functions

The theory of
OGF
is an important branch of mathematics that lays connections between combinatorics, analysis, number theory, probability theory and other fields.
In this paper we restrict ourselves to
OGF
corresponding to weights in coordinate-additive metrics, which are polynomials with non-negative coefficients.
Consider a finite abelian group
\mathcal{A}
with weight function
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}
and induced coordinate-additive weight function
\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}
on
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
. The
OGF
corresponding to
\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}
is defined as the polynomial

\textstyle F_{\mathcal{A}^{\ell}}(z):=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}}z^{\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu\ell}|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\ell}|\ z^{i}.

(22)

For a polynomial
F(z)=F_{0}+F_{1}z+\ldots+F_{d}z^{d}
we use the notation
[z^{i}]F(z)
to refer to the
i
-th coefficient
F_{i}
of
F(z)
, with
[z^{i}]F(z)=0
for
i>\deg(F)
.
The
OGF
for the sum-rank metric on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta\ell}
is denoted by

\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu\ell}\left|{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}}\right|\,z^{i}.

Definition 1

(Partial order on polynomials)
.

Let
F(z),G(z)\in\mathbb{R}[z]
be two real polynomials. If
[z^{i}]F(z)\leq[z^{i}]G(z)
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we say
F(z)
is coefficient-wise less-than-or-equal to
G(z)
, denoted as

F(z)\preccurlyeq_{c}G(z).

Proposition 1

(
(
Flajolet and Sedgewick, 2009
, Theorem I.1)
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}
be two finite alphabets with weight functions
\wt_{\mathcal{A}_{1}},\wt_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}
respectively. Then
\wt_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}}(a,b):=\wt_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}(a)+\wt_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}(b)
is a weight function on
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}
and

F_{\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}}(z)=F_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}(z)F_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}(z).

(23)

In particular, we have

F_{\mathcal{A}^{\ell}}(z)=F_{\mathcal{A}}(z)^{\ell},\vskip 2.84526pt

(24)

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, the product of real polynomials with non-negative coefficients preserves the partial order: If
F(z)\preccurlyeq_{c}G(z)
and
K(z)\preccurlyeq_{c}L(z)
, then
F(z)K(z)\preccurlyeq_{c}G(z)L(z)
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
F(z)
be a real polynomial of degree
d>0
with non-negative coefficients
F_{i}\geq 0
and first derivative
F^{\prime}(z)
. If
F(z)
is not a monomial, then the function
G(z)=zF^{\prime}(z)/F(z)
is a strictly increasing smooth function on the positive reals
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
In particular if
F(0)>0
, which is the case with
OGF
of finite alphabets with weight functions,
G(z)
is a bijection from
[0,\infty)
to
[0,d)
.

Proof 2.3
.

Smoothness follows directly from smoothness of
F(z)
and
1/z
on
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. To show that
G(z)
is strictly increasing, consider two positive integers
0<a<b
and let
K(a,b):=bF^{\prime}(b)F(a)-aF^{\prime}(a)F(b)
. Then,

\displaystyle K(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}iF_{i}b^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{d}F_{j}a^{j}-\sum_{i=0}^{d}iF_{i}a^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{d}F_{j}b^{j}

(25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq d}iF_{i}F_{j}b^{i}a^{j}+jF_{j}F_{i}b^{j}a^{i}\ -iF_{i}F_{j}a^{i}b^{j}-jF_{j}F_{i}a^{j}b^{i}

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq d}F_{i}F_{j}(j-i)(b^{j}a^{i}-a^{j}b^{i})>0

(27)

where the last inequality follows from assuming that
F(z)
is not a monomial, so there exist
i<j
such that
F_{i}F_{j}>0
. The proof now follows since
G(b)-G(a)=\frac{K(a,b)}{F(a)F(b)}>0.

Lastly, we have that
\lim_{z\to\infty}F^{\prime}(z)/z^{d-1}=dF_{d}
and
\lim_{z\to\infty}F(z)/z^{d}=F_{d}
, hence we get
\lim_{z\to\infty}G(z)=d
.

3
Information-Theoretic Bounds on Spheres

In
Loeliger (1994)
an asymptotically tight upper bound on the volume of an
\ell
-dimensional ball
\left|{\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
of radius
t
was introduced. The author used information theoretic tools, as described in Section
2.2
, and bounded the size of the sphere via the type maximizing the entropy, and showed that this bound is asymptotically tight. Furthermore, this bound is valid for any arbitrary additive weight function
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}
(see Section
2.1
for the definition) with respect to some finite abelian group
\mathcal{A}
.
The bound was proved to hold for normalized weights
\rho
with
\rho:=t/\ell
up to the average weight

\overline{w}:=|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)

at which the volume of the ball is saturated.
We extend the result from
Loeliger (1994)
to the size of spheres and also prove that the bound holds for
\rho\geq\overline{w}
up to the maximum possible weight, i.e., for
0<\rho<\mu=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left\{{\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}\right\}
.

Given a random variable
X
over a finite alphabet
\mathcal{A}
with probability distribution
P
, we define
P(a):=\operatorname{Prob}(X=a)
with
a\in\mathcal{A}
.
The entropy
H(P)
of
P
with respect to the base
q
is defined as

\displaystyle H(P):=-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A},P(a)\neq 0}P(a)\log_{q}P(a).

(28)

Example 3.4
.

Consider an alphabet
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a probability
\rho\in(0,1)
, and a probability distribution

\displaystyle P(x)=\begin{cases}1-\rho&x=0\\
\rho/(q-1)&x\neq 0\end{cases}.

(29)

Then the entropy
H(X)
reduces to the
q
-ary entropy function defined as

h_{q}(\rho):=-\rho\log_{q}(\rho/(q-1))-(1-\rho)\log_{q}(1-\rho).

Definition 3.5
.

For any
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
0<\rho<\mu
, we define the probability distribution

\displaystyle P_{\beta}(a):=\frac{q^{-\beta\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}}{\mathcal{Z}(\beta)}

(30)

where
\beta
is the unique solution to the weight constraint

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=\rho

(31)

and
\mathcal{Z}(\beta)
is chosen s.t.
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)=1
, i.e.
\mathcal{Z}(\beta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}q^{-\beta\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}.

Later, in Proposition
3.11
, we show that for any
0<\rho<\mu
,
a solution
\beta
to the equation
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=\rho
always exists and is unique. This establishes a one-to-one correspondence between
\rho
and
\beta
, i.e. every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
determines a
\rho\in(0,\mu)
via the function

\rho(\beta):=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)

(32)

and vice versa.
Hence we will use
\rho
and
\rho(\beta)
interchangeably.
Let us denote by

H_{\rho}:=H(P_{\beta})

the entropy of the distribution in (
30
). Then, the following bound was proven in
Loeliger (1994)
.

Theorem 3.6

(
Loeliger (1994)
)
.

For any
0<\rho\leq\overline{w}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}\left|{\mathcal{B}_{\rho\ell}^{\ell}}\right|\leq H_{\rho}.

(33)

The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.6
above.

Corollary 3.7
.

For any
0<\rho<\overline{w}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}\left|{\mathcal{S}_{\rho\ell}^{\ell}}\right|\leq H_{\rho}.

(34)

By a simple cutting argument, the bound in Theorem
3.6
can be extended to any normalized weight
0\leq\rho\leq\max_{x\in\mathcal{A}}\left\{{\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(x)}\right\}=:\mu
as follows.

Theorem 3.8
.

For any
0<\rho\leq\mu
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}\left|{\mathcal{B}_{\rho\ell}^{\ell}}\right|\leq\begin{cases}H_{\rho}&\text{if }0<\rho\leq\overline{w}\\
\log_{q}(\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|)&\text{if }\overline{w}<\rho\leq\mu\end{cases}.

(35)

3.1
Upper Bound

In this subsection, we show that Corollary
3.7
also holds for normalized weights
0<\rho<\mu
.
Recall the
OGF
for the alphabets
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
, respectively,

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{A}}(z)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}z^{\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}\quad\text{and }\quad F_{\mathcal{A}^{\ell}}(z)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}}z^{\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v)}=F_{\mathcal{A}}(z)^{\ell}.

(36)

We now can express
\mathcal{Z}(\beta)
,
\rho(\beta)
and
H_{\rho}
in terms of
F_{\mathcal{A}}(z)
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\rho=\rho(\beta)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\beta)=F_{\mathcal{A}}\left(q^{-\beta}\right),\quad\rho(\beta)=q^{-\beta}\frac{F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(q^{-\beta}\right)}{F_{\mathcal{A}}\left(q^{-\beta}\right)},\quad H_{\rho}=\log_{q}\left(\frac{F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})}{q^{-\beta\rho(\beta)}}\right).

(37)

Proof 3.10
.

The first equality immediately follows from the fact that
\mathcal{Z}(\beta)
is the normalization constant of the distribution
P_{\beta}(\cdot)
:
\mathcal{Z}(\beta)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left(q^{-\beta}\right)^{\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}=F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})
.

For the second equality, we rewrite the weight constraint (
31
) defining
\rho(\beta)
to obtain

\displaystyle\rho(\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{q^{-\beta\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}}{\mathcal{Z}(\beta)}\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{q^{-\beta(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)-1)}q^{-\beta}\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}{F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})}

(38)

Noticing that
F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(q^{-\beta}\right)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}q^{-\beta(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)-1)}\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)
yields the result.

To show the last equality we use equations (
30
), (
32
) and the previous equalities for
\mathcal{Z}(\beta)
. Rewriting the definition of the entropy
H_{\rho}
gives

\displaystyle H_{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\log_{q}\left(P_{\beta}(a)\right)=-\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\log_{q}\left(\frac{q^{-\beta\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)}}{F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\left(\beta\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)+\log_{q}\left(F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})\right)\right)

(40)

\displaystyle=\beta\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)\ +\ \log_{q}\left(F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})\right)\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{\beta}(a)

(41)

\displaystyle=\beta\rho(\beta)+\log_{q}\left(F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})\right)

(42)

\displaystyle=\log_{q}\left(\frac{F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})}{q^{-\beta\rho(\beta)}}\right).

(43)

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an alphabet with weight function
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then the function
\rho(\beta)
given in (
32
) is a strictly decreasing smooth bijection from
\mathbb{R}
to
(0,\mu)
.

Proof 3.12
.

By Lemma
2
, the function
G(z):=z\frac{F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A}}\left(z\right)}{F_{\mathcal{A}}\left(z\right)}
is a strictly increasing bijection from
[0,\infty)
to
[0,\mu)
. The proof now follows since
\rho(\beta)=G(q^{-\beta})
.

We will now
use the technique of bounding the coefficients of an
OGF
by evaluating it at points
y
.

For any real valued
y>0
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}|y^{t}

\displaystyle=\left([z^{t}]F_{\mathcal{A}^{\ell}}(z)\right)y^{t}\leq F_{\mathcal{A}^{\ell}}(y)=F_{\mathcal{A}}(y)^{\ell}.

(44)

We can further rewrite this expression and take the infimum on the right-hand side and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}|\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}|

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{y>0}\ \log_{q}\left(\frac{F_{\mathcal{A}}(y)}{y^{\rho}}\right).

(45)

Moreover, we can show that a global minimum of
F_{\mathcal{A}}(y)/y^{\rho}
exists and therefore the infimum is a minimum: by setting the derivative of
F_{\mathcal{A}}(y)/y^{\rho}
to zero and using (
37
) for
\rho
, we obtain a local minimum for
y=q^{-\beta}
. Then, using Lemma
2
, we can show that the derivative of
F_{\mathcal{A}}(y)/y^{\rho}
is negative for
0<y<q^{-\beta}
and positive for
y>q^{-\beta}
.
Therefore, the local minimum is also the global minimum, where the function
\log_{q}\left(\frac{F_{\mathcal{A}}(y)}{y^{\rho}}\right)
takes the value
H_{\rho}
(cf. (
37
)).

This technique is similar to the more general
saddle-point bound
explained in
(
Flajolet and Sedgewick, 2009
, Section VIII.2)
.

To summarize, the saddle-point-like bound (
45
) coincides with the entropy bound (see also
(
Greferath and O’Sullivan, 2004
, Theorem 4.1)
,
(
Byrne et al., 2021
, Theorem IV.9)
), but extends the range of
\rho
to
(0,\mu)
, as stated in Theorem
3.13
.
Finally, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}|\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\ell}|

\displaystyle\leq\log_{q}\left(\frac{F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})}{(q^{-\beta})^{\rho}}\right).

(46)

Theorem 3.13
.

For any
0<\rho<\mu
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}|\mathcal{S}_{\rho\ell}^{\ell}|\,\leq\,H_{\rho}\,=\,\inf_{y>0}\ \log_{q}\left(\frac{F_{\mathcal{A}}(y)}{y^{\rho}}\right),

(47)

where the infimum (in this case a minimum) on the right-hand side is attained at
y=q^{-\beta}
, with
\beta
defined by
\rho(\beta)=\rho
.

3.2
Lower Bounds

Observe that (
21
) gives us the lower bound

\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}|\mathcal{S}_{\rho\ell}^{\ell}|\geq H_{\rho}-(\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|-1)\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}(\ell+1).

(48)

Although this bound is asymptotically tight as
\ell\to\infty
, it can be improved for some ranges of
\ell
when the alphabet size
\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|
is large (e.g. for the sum-rank metric where
\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|=q^{mn}
). In this section we derive a different lower bound that does not depend on
\left|{\mathcal{A}}\right|
but instead on
\wt_{\mathcal{A}}
. Specifically for the sum-rank metric this new bound is tighter than (
48
) for a large range of
\ell
.

Let
X_{\beta},X_{\beta,1},X_{\beta,2},X_{\beta,3},\ldots
be
independent and identically distributed
random variables taking values in
\mathcal{A}
with probability distribution
P_{\beta}
given in (
30
). For
a\in\mathcal{A}
, define the function

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\varphi_{\beta}(a):=-\log_{q}\left(P_{\beta}(a)\right)\ =\beta\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(a)+\log_{q}\left(F_{\mathcal{A}}(q^{-\beta})\right).

(49)

Note that
H_{\rho}=\mathbb{E}[\varphi_{\beta}(X_{\beta,i})]
with
\rho=\rho(\beta)
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
As a consequence of Chebyshev’s inequality
Tchebichef (1874)
, for any
\gamma>0
it holds

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi_{\beta}(X_{\beta,i})-H_{\rho}\right|\geq\gamma\right)\leq\frac{\Var(\varphi_{\beta}(X_{\beta}))}{\ell\gamma^{2}}=\frac{\beta^{2}\Var(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{\beta}))}{\ell\gamma^{2}}.

(50)

By choosing
\gamma
appropriately and by following similar techniques as used in
Loeliger (1994)
, a lower bound for a sum of sphere sizes is derived in Theorem
3.14
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Given
t=\rho\ell
and
0<\varepsilon<1
, let
\beta
be defined by the weight constraint (
31
). Furthermore, let
\delta=\left(\frac{\ell\Var(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{\beta}))}{(1-\varepsilon)}\right)^{1/2}
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\delta<j<\delta\\
j\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{S}_{t+j}^{\ell}|\ >\ \varepsilon\ q^{\ell H_{\rho}-|\beta|\delta}.

(51)

Proof 3.15
.

First, let us define a probability distribution on
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
naturally by
P_{\beta}(v):=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{\beta}(v_{i})
for
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell})\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
. Let
Y_{\beta}
denote a random variable taking values in
\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
with this probability distribution. Note that
Y_{\beta}
is identically distributed as the tuple
(X_{\beta,1},\ldots,X_{\beta,\ell})
.
Next, for any
v\in\mathcal{A}^{\ell}
, the expression
\left|{\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v)-\rho\ell}\right|<\delta
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{1}{\ell}\beta\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v)-\beta\rho}\right|<\frac{|\beta|\delta}{\ell}.

(52)

Note that, using Lemma
3.9
and (
49
), we rewrite the left hand side of the inequality as

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{1}{\ell}\beta\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(v)-\beta\rho}\right|=\left|{\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi_{\beta}(v_{i})-H_{\rho}}\right|.

(53)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|{\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(Y_{\beta})-\rho\ell}\right|<\delta\right)=\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi_{\beta}(X_{\beta,i})\ -\ H_{\rho}\right|<\frac{|\beta|\delta}{\ell}\right).

(54)

Applying Chebyshev’s inequality (see (
50
)), we obtain a lower bound

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|{\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(Y_{\beta})-\rho\ell}\right|<\delta\right)\geq 1-\frac{\ell\Var(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{\beta}))}{\delta^{2}}=\varepsilon.

(55)

Now, let us denote
U:=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\delta<j<\delta\\
j\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}}\mathcal{S}_{\rho\ell+j}^{\ell}
and note that we can write

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|{\wt_{\Sigma\mathcal{A}}(Y_{\beta})-\rho\ell}\right|<\delta\right)=\operatorname{Prob}\left(Y_{\beta}\in U\right)=\sum_{v\in U}P_{\beta}(v).

(56)

Furthermore, note that the inequality
\left|{\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi_{\beta}(v_{i})-H_{\rho}}\right|<\frac{|\beta|\delta}{\ell}
implies that
q^{-\ell H_{\rho}+|\beta|\delta}>P_{\beta}(v)
.
The proof is completed observing that

\text{}\hskip 48.36967pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\delta<j<\delta\\
j\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\left|{\mathcal{S}_{\rho\ell+j}^{\ell}}\right|q^{-\ell H_{\rho}+|\beta|\delta}=\ \left|{U}\right|q^{-\ell H_{\rho}+|\beta|\delta}>\sum_{v\in U}P_{\beta}(v)\geq\varepsilon.\hskip 48.36967pt\square

(57)

Using the inequality

{\displaystyle{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\delta<j<\delta\\
j\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}}}|\mathcal{S}_{t+j}^{\ell}|\ \geq\ \frac{1}{2\lceil\delta\rceil-1}{\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\delta<j<\delta\\
j\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}}}|\mathcal{S}_{t+j}^{\ell}|
we get an alternative bound in Theorem
3.16
.

Theorem 3.16
.

Given
t=\ell\rho
and
0<\varepsilon<1
, let
\beta
be defined by the weight constraint (
31
) and
\delta=\left(\frac{\ell\Var(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{\beta}))}{(1-\varepsilon)}\right)^{1/2}
. Then

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\delta<j<\delta\\
j\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\ \frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}|\mathcal{S}_{t+j}^{\ell}|\ >\ H_{\rho}-\frac{|\beta|\delta}{\ell}-\frac{1}{\ell}\log_{q}\left(\frac{2\lceil\delta\rceil-1}{\varepsilon}\right).

(58)

Empirically, good bounds seem to be obtained for
\varepsilon
close to 0 (this corresponds to a relatively small
\delta
close to
\ell^{1/2}\Var(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{\beta}))^{1/2}
). There exists a number
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
such that setting
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
implies
\delta=\lfloor\ell^{1/2}\Var(\wt_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{\beta}))^{1/2}\rfloor+1
; we suspect that the best bounds are obtained for
\varepsilon
close to this
\varepsilon_{0}
.

Moreover, for constant
\varepsilon
and
\rho
, this bound coincides asymptotically with the bound from Theorem
3.13
as
\ell\to\infty
and is therefore asymptotically tight.

4
Bounds on Spheres in the Sum-rank Metric

In this section we derive improved closed-form upper and lower bounds on the size of a sphere in the sum-rank metric. Hence we fix
m
,
\eta
and
q
and we use
\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)
to denote the number of matrices of rank
t
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta}
.
Following (
16
), an exact formula for the size of the
\ell
-dimensional sphere of radius
t
in the sum-rank metric is given by

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell})\in\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t_{i}),

(59)

with
\mathcal{T}^{\ell}_{t}
the set of all (ordered) integer partitions of
t
of length
\ell
with part sizes not exceeding
\mu=\mu_{\Sigma R}=\min\{m,\eta\}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}:=\left\{(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{\ell})\in\{0,1,\ldots,\mu\}^{\ell}\ :\ t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell}=t\right\}.

(60)

A current method
of obtaining both closed-form upper and lower bounds on
\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|
uses
the following
steps:

1.

Derive a closed-form upper bound
b^{\text{up}}_{q}(m,\eta,t)
and lower bound
b^{\text{low}}_{q}(m,\eta,t)

on
\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)
;

2.

Find a partition
t^{\text{up}}=(t_{1}^{\text{up}},\ldots,t_{\ell}^{\text{up}})\in\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}
that maximizes
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}b^{\text{up}}_{q}(m,\eta,t_{i}^{\text{up}})
and a partition
t^{\text{low}}=(t_{1}^{\text{low}},\ldots,t_{\ell}^{\text{low}})\in\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}
that maximizes
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}b^{\text{low}}_{q}(m,\eta,t_{i}^{\text{low}})
, for example with the use of Lagrange multipliers;

3.

Derive a closed-form upper bound
N_{t}^{\ell}
on
\left|{\mathcal{T}_{t}^{\ell}}\right|
.

This strategy yields the following general bounds on the size of a sphere:

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}b^{\text{low}}_{q}(m,\eta,t_{i}^{\text{low}})\,\leq\,\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|\,\leq\,N_{t}^{\ell}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}b^{\text{up}}_{q}(m,\eta,t_{i}^{\text{up}}).

(61)

In
Ott et al. (2021)
, and further discussed in
Gruica et al. (2023)
, the authors used
b^{\text{low}}_{q}(m,\eta,t)=\gamma_{q}^{-1}q^{t(m+\eta-t)}
to obtain the closed-form lower bounds

\displaystyle\gamma_{q}^{-\ell}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})-\frac{\ell}{4}}\,\leq\,\gamma_{q}^{-\ell}\binom{\ell}{r}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell}-r}\leq\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|,

(62)

where
r
satisfies
t\equiv r\mod\ell
with
0\leq r<\ell
and

\gamma_{q}
is a special instance of the
\boldsymbol{q}
-
Pochhammer symbol
defined as

\displaystyle(a;x)_{k}:=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-ax^{i}),\quad(a;x)_{\infty}:=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-ax^{i}),\quad\gamma_{q}:=\left(\frac{1}{q};\frac{1}{q}\right)_{\infty}^{-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{-i})^{-1}.

(63)

Likewise in
Puchinger et al. (2022)
this strategy is used with

b^{\text{up}}_{q}(m,\eta,t)=\gamma_{q}q^{t(m+\eta-t)}
and
N_{t}^{\ell}=\binom{\ell+t-1}{\ell-1}
to obtain the closed-form upper bounds

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq\gamma_{q}^{\ell}\binom{\ell+t-1}{\ell-1}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell}-r}}\leq\gamma_{q}^{\ell}\binom{\ell+t-1}{\ell-1}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})}.

(64)

where
r
satisfies
t\equiv r\mod\ell
with
0\leq r<\ell
.
Note that as
\ell
increases, the discrepancy between the lower bounds of (
62
) and upper bounds of (
64
) also greatly increases. Hence in the regime of large
\ell
better bounds can be obtained.

First, we reuse the current strategy (
61
) with a different upper bound
b^{\text{up}}_{q}(m,\eta,t)
on
\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)
that works better for large
\ell
and small
t
in Section
4.1
. We also derive an improved lower bound that we will use later in Section
4.3
.

Secondly, in Section
4.2
, we investigate a different strategy where, instead of estimating the sum in (
59
) directly, we exploit the nature of the sum-rank sphere size as an
\ell
-fold discrete convolution, and bound it
with continuous convolutions for which closed-form expression exist.

Lastly, in Section
4.3
, we utilize

\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}(z)=\left(\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,1,q}(z)\right)^{\ell}
, where we derive an alternative function that serves as a coefficient-wise lower bound for
\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,1,q}(z)
.
The
\ell
-th power of this function is calculated more efficiently, since its roots are expressed easily.

4.1
Bounds on the Number of Matrices of Fixed Rank

For
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
we define the
\boldsymbol{q}
-
binomial coefficient
as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}a\\
b\end{bmatrix}_{q}=\prod_{i=1}^{b}\frac{1-q^{a-b+i}}{1-q^{i}}=\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}\frac{q^{a}-q^{j}}{q^{b}-q^{j}}.

(65)

Then, by
Migler et al. (2004)
, the number of matrices of rank
t
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times\eta}
is computed as

\displaystyle\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)=\begin{bmatrix}m\\
t\end{bmatrix}_{q}\ \prod_{i=0}^{t-1}(q^{\eta}-q^{i}).

(66)

Let
q\geq 2
,
\mu=\mu_{\Sigma R}=\min\{m,\eta\}
and
\mathfrak{M}:=\max\{m,\eta\}
. We will lower-bound
\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)
in two steps, eventually leading to Proposition
4.19
below; first, the
q
-binomial coefficients satisfy a useful lower bound that follows from elementary arguments (see
(
Ihringer, 2015
, Lemma 2.2)
):

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{q}

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}(1+\frac{1}{q})q^{i(\mu-i)}&\text{if $0<i<\mu$}\\
1&\text{if $i\in\{0,\mu\}$}\\
\end{cases}.

(67)

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
0<i<\mu
and
q\geq 2
. Then

\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}};\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)_{\infty}^{-1}\leq 1+\frac{1}{q}.

(68)

Proof 4.18
.

By Euler’s pentagonal number theorem
(
Van Lint and Wilson, 2001
, Thm 15.5)
we can rewrite and bound the
q
-Pochhammer symbol as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}};\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left[\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{3n^{2}-n}+\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{3n^{2}+n}\right]

(69)

\displaystyle=1-\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{4}+\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{10}+\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{14}-\ldots

(70)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{q}\right)^{4}

(71)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{(for $q\geq 2$)}}}{{\geq}}1-\frac{1}{q^{2}-1}\geq 1-\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{q}{q+1}.

(72)

So for
0<i<\mu
we have

\displaystyle\text{}\hskip 28.45274pt\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}};\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)_{\mu}}{\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}};\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)_{i}\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}};\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)_{\mu-i}}\leq\frac{1}{\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}};\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}};\frac{1}{q^{2}}\right)_{\infty}}\leq 1+\frac{1}{q}.\hskip 28.45274pt\square

(73)

As direct corollary of (
67
) and Lemma
4.17
we obtain for all
0\leq i\leq\mu
the inequality

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}q^{i(\mu-i)}\leq\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{q}.

(74)

For
i\in\{0,\mu\}
this follows trivially as the
q
-binomial coefficients equal 1 then.

Secondly, for
a,b,c\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq a<b<c
we have

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}(q^{c}-q^{j})\right)^{a}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(q^{c}-q^{j})\right)^{a}\prod_{k=a}^{b-1}(q^{c}-q^{k})^{a}

(75)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(q^{c}-q^{j})\right)^{a}\prod_{k=a}^{b-1}(q^{c}-q^{a})^{a}

(76)

\displaystyle<\left(\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(q^{c}-q^{j})\right)^{a}\prod_{k=a}^{b-1}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(q^{c}-q^{j})\right)=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(q^{c}-q^{j})\right)^{b}.

(77)

Hence, we observe that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(q^{\mathfrak{M}}-q^{j})>\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\mu-1}(q^{\mathfrak{M}}-q^{j})\right)^{i/\mu}=q^{i\,\mathfrak{M}}\left({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1}\right)^{i/\mu},

(79)

where we introduce the notation

{\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}:=\prod_{j=\mathfrak{M}-\mu+1}^{\mathfrak{M}}\left(1-q^{-j}\right)^{-1}

(80)

(with
\mu=\mu_{\Sigma R}=\min\{m,\eta\}
and
\mathfrak{M}:=\max\{m,\eta\}
). Combining (
74
) and (
80
) leads to a new lower bound on the number of matrices of rank
i
.

Proposition 4.19
.

For
m,\eta,i\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq i\leq\mu
, we have the lower bound

\displaystyle\left({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}\right)^{i}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}q^{i(m+\eta-i)}\leq\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,i).

(81)

Proof 4.20
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=\max\{m,\eta\}
. Then

\displaystyle\text{}\hskip 28.45274pt\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}q^{i(m+\eta-i)}\left({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}\right)^{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}q^{i(\mu-i)}q^{i\mathfrak{M}}\left({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}\right)^{i}

(82)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{q}q^{i\mathfrak{M}}\left({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1}\right)^{i/\mu}

(83)

\displaystyle<\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{q}\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\left(q^{\mathfrak{M}}-q^{j}\right)=\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,i)\hskip 31.29802pt\square

(84)

Next, we observe that

\displaystyle\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{(1-q^{-m+i-1})(1-q^{-\eta+i-1})}{(1-q^{-i})}\right)q^{t(m+\eta-t)}.

(85)

Let us now introduce the function

\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t):=\left(\frac{(1-q^{-m})(1-q^{-\eta})}{(1-q^{-1})}\right)^{t}.

(86)

Noticing that
\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{(1-q^{-m+i-1})(1-q^{-\eta+i-1})}{(1-q^{-i})}
, we derive the following upper bound on
\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)
.

Proposition 4.21
.

For
m,\eta,t\in\mathbb{N}
we have the upper bound

\displaystyle\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)\ \leq\ \kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)q^{t(m+\eta-t)}.

(87)

With the property
\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t_{1})\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t_{2})=\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t_{1}+t_{2})
, we perform the same steps of the proof of bounds (
64
) in
Puchinger et al. (2022)
with
\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)
instead of
\gamma_{q}
and obtain Theorem
4.22
as
a
new upper bound on sum-rank sphere sizes.

Theorem 4.22
.

Given positive integers
m,\eta,\ell,t
and a prime power
q
, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)\binom{\ell+t-1}{\ell-1}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell}-r}}\leq\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)\binom{\ell+t-1}{\ell-1}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})}

(88)

where
r
satisfies
t\equiv r\mod\ell
with
0\leq r<\ell
.

Importantly, for sufficiently small
t
the coefficient
\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)
is smaller than
\gamma_{q}
. In this case the bounds of Theorem
4.22
are tighter than
(
Puchinger et al., 2022
, Theorem 5)
(see (
64
)).

4.2
Convolution Upper Bound

Let
f(x)
and
g(x)
be two real-valued functions defined on the natural numbers (or on a larger domain). For
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
discrete convolution
by

\displaystyle[f*g](t):=\sum_{i=0}^{t}f(i)g(t-i).

(89)

The
\ell
-fold discrete convolution
[f*f*\cdots*f]
(well-defined by associativity of
*
) is denoted as
f^{*\ell}
.
The strategy in this subsection comes from the observation that (
59
) is equivalent to

\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|=\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,x)^{*\ell}(t).\vskip 5.69054pt

(90)

Let
C(t)
be a real-valued function depending on the parameters
m,\eta,q
and satisfying

\displaystyle\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t)\leq C(t)q^{t(m+\eta-t)}\quad\text{and}\quad C(t_{1})C(t_{2})=C(t_{3})C(t_{4})

(91)

whenever
t_{1}+t_{2}=t_{3}+t_{4}
. By Proposition
4.21
, examples of such functions are
\gamma_{q}
and
\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)
.
The reason for looking at these functions is because they work well with discrete convolutions, i.e.,

\left[C(x)f(x)*C(x)g(x)\right](t)=C(0)C(t)[f*g](t).

Therefore, we can upper bound the sphere sizes as follows

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|\leq\left(C(x)q^{x(m+\eta-x)}\right)^{*\ell}(t)=C(0)^{\ell-1}C(t)\left(q^{x(m+\eta-x)}\right)^{*\ell}(t).

(92)

Proposition
4.23
provides a formula to easily bound convolutions.

Proposition 4.23
.

Consider
f_{\ell}(x):=q^{x(m+\eta-x/\ell)}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Functions of this form satisfy the convolution inequality

\displaystyle[f_{\ell_{1}}*f_{\ell_{2}}](t)\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\pi}{(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})\ln{q}}}\right)f_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}(t).

(93)

Proof 4.24
.

First of all, note that

\displaystyle[f_{\ell_{1}}*f_{\ell_{2}}](t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{t}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}})-\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}i^{2}-\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}(t-i)^{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}}

(94)

\displaystyle=f_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}(t)\sum_{i=0}^{t}q^{-\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)\left(i-\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}t\right)^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}<f_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}(t)\!\!\!\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!q^{-\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)\left(i-\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}t\right)^{2}}\!\!.}

(95)

Next, we will apply the technique of bounding summations with integrals, bounding the discrete convolution with a continuous one. To make the notation more compact, let
g(x):=q^{-\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)\left(x-\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}t\right)^{2}}
. Then, using unimodality of
g(x)
with its mode/peak at
x_{\text{p}}:=\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}t
where
g
attains
g(x_{\text{p}})=1
, we bound different parts of the summation as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\left\lfloor x_{\text{p}}\right\rfloor-1}g(i)\;\leq\;\int_{-\infty}^{\left\lfloor x_{\text{p}}\right\rfloor}g(x)\ dx,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{i=\left\lceil x_{\text{p}}\right\rceil-1}^{\infty}g(i)\;\leq\;\int_{\left\lceil x_{\text{p}}\right\rceil}^{\infty}g(x)\ dx,

(96)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=\left\lfloor x_{\text{p}}\right\rfloor}^{\left\lceil x_{\text{p}}\right\rceil}g(i)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;1+\int_{\left\lfloor x_{\text{p}}\right\rfloor}^{\left\lceil x_{\text{p}}\right\rceil}g(x)\ dx.

(97)

Note that these bounds hold both if
x_{\text{p}}\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{\text{p}}\notin\mathbb{N}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{-\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)\left(i-\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}t\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}q^{-\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)\left(x-\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}t\right)^{2}}dx

(98)

\displaystyle=1+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}q^{-\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)x^{2}}dx=1+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)\ln{q}}}

(99)

and thus the proposition follows.

A tighter bound could possibly be obtained by examining the summation
\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{-\left(\frac{1}{\ell_{1}}+\frac{1}{\ell_{2}}\right)\left(i-\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}t\right)^{2}}
more closely, which is a special case of a
(Jacobi) theta function
. Studying theta functions lies outside of the scope of this paper, but could be a promising direction for further improvements on Proposition
4.23
.

Setting
\ell_{1}=1
and applying Proposition
4.23
inductively for
\ell_{2}=1,\ldots,\ell-1
, we obtain new upper bounds on the sum-rank sphere sizes using (
92
) with
C(t)
of our choice.

Theorem 4.25
.

Let
m,\eta,\ell,q,t
be positive integers. Choosing
C(t)
equal to
\gamma_{q}
or
\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)
, we observe the following bounds, respectively

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{q}^{\ell}\ \prod_{k=1}^{\ell-1}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{k\pi}{(k+1)\ln{q}}}\right)q^{t(m+\eta-t/\ell)}

(100)

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}_{t}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{q,m,\eta}(t)\prod_{k=1}^{\ell-1}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{k\pi}{(k+1)\ln{q}}}\right)q^{t(m+\eta-t/\ell)}

(101)

where the further simplifications

\prod_{k=1}^{\ell-1}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{k\pi}{(k+1)\ln{q}}}\right)\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{(\ell-1)\pi}{\ell\ln{q}}}\right)^{\ell-1}<\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\ln{q}}}\right)^{\ell-1}

(102)

can be made.

4.3
Lower Bound via Ordinary Generating Functions

Recall that the OGF of sum-rank sphere sizes is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}(z)=\sum_{t=0}^{\mu\ell}\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}}\right|\ z^{t}=\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,1,q}(z)^{\ell}.

(103)

Note that finding a closed-form bound for
\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}}\right|
is equal to finding such a bound for the coefficients of the
\ell
-th power on the right-hand side of (
103
).
The approach that we thus take is, to bound the function
\mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,1,q}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,i)\,z^{i}
coefficient-wise using another polynomial
\mathcal{F}(z)
whose
\ell
-th power can be computed more easily, as we will see later. We consider the polynomial

\mathcal{F}(z):=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}q^{i(m+\eta-i)}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}z^{i}.

(104)

This polynomial satisfies the following chain of coefficient-wise inequalities:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}\gamma_{q}^{-1}q^{i(m+\eta-i)}z^{i}\ \preccurlyeq_{c}\ \gamma_{q}^{-1}\mathcal{F}(z)\ \preccurlyeq_{c}\ \mathcal{F}({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}z)\ \preccurlyeq_{c}\ \mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,1,q}(z).

(105)

The first inequality follows from
1\leq\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}
, the second inequality follows from
\gamma_{q}^{-1}\leq{\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1}\leq{\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-i/\mu}\leq 1
for
0\leq i\leq\mu
and the third from Proposition
4.19
.
Since these coefficient-wise inequalities are preserved under
\ell
-th powers, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}\gamma_{q}^{-1}q^{i(m+\eta-i)}z^{i}\right)^{\ell}\ \preccurlyeq_{c}\ \mathcal{F}({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}z)^{\ell}\ \preccurlyeq_{c}\ \mathcal{S}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}(z).

(106)

Note that the left-most polynomial is used in deriving the lower bound
(
Ott et al., 2021
, Lemma 2)
(see (
62
)). Since the middle polynomial is coefficient-wise greater, estimating its coefficients can potentially lead to improved lower bounds. Let us thus focus on estimating the
\ell
-th power of
\mathcal{F}({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}z)
.

To our advantage, the polynomial
\mathcal{F}(z)
(and hence also
\mathcal{F}({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}z)
) can be factored nicely into linear parts, as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 4.26
.

Let
m,\eta\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu=\min\{m,\eta\}
. Then

\mathcal{F}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu}q^{i(m+\eta-i)}\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\
i\end{bmatrix}_{1/q^{2}}z^{i}=\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}(1+q^{m+\eta-2i+1}z).

(107)

The proof follows directly from the
q
-binomial theorem
, also known as the
Cauchy binomial theorem
or
Gaussian binomial theorem
(see
(
Kac and Cheung, 2002
, Chapter 5)
).

Let us now consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(z)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}\left(1+q^{-2i}\left(q^{m+\eta+1}z\right)\right)^{\ell}

(108)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}\,\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}q^{-2ij}\left(q^{m+\eta+1}z\right)^{j}

(109)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\mu\ell}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\ldots+j_{\mu}=t\\
0\leq j_{i}\leq\ell\end{subarray}}\;\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}\binom{\ell}{j_{i}}q^{-2ij_{i}}\right)\left(q^{m+\eta+1}z\right)^{t}.

(110)

At first, bounding these sums seems as difficult as those in (
59
). However, unlike (
59
), the factors
\binom{\ell}{j_{i}}q^{-2ij_{i}}

are
also strongly dependent on
i
(not only on
j_{i}
) and increase as
i
decreases.
This implies that there is a
unique
optimal tuple
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{\mu})
that maximizes
\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}\binom{\ell}{j_{i}}q^{-2ij_{i}}
, in contrast to (
59
) where every tuple
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell})
contributes the same term
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,t_{i})
to the sum as any of its permutations.

For each
t
we will simply bound the sum by only one term
\prod_{i=1}^{\mu}\binom{\ell}{j_{i}}q^{-2ij_{i}}
with the near-optimal choice

\displaystyle j_{i}=\begin{cases}\ell&\text{for }\ 1\leq i\leq t_{*}\\
r&\text{for }\ i=t_{*}+1\\
0&\text{for }\ t_{*}+2\leq i\leq\mu\end{cases}

(111)

where
t=t_{*}\ell+r
for some
t_{*}\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq r<\ell
. This yields the bound

\mathcal{F}(z)^{\ell}\succcurlyeq_{c}\sum_{t=0}^{\mu\ell}\left(\binom{\ell}{r}q^{-2r(t_{*}+1)}\prod_{i=1}^{t_{*}}q^{-2i\ell}\right)\left(q^{m+\eta+1}z\right)^{t}=\sum_{t=0}^{\mu\ell}\binom{\ell}{r}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell}-r}z^{t}.

Finally, by substituting
{\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1/\mu}z
for
z
in this inequality and applying (
106
) we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
m,\eta\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu=\min\{m,\eta\}
and
q
a prime power. Let
t=t_{*}\ell+r\leq\mu\ell
with
t_{*}\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq r<\ell
. Then

\displaystyle\left({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1}\right)^{t/\mu}\binom{\ell}{r}q^{t(m+\eta-\frac{t}{\ell})+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell}-r}\leq\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}}\right|,

(112)

with
\gamma_{q,m,\eta}
given in (
80
).

Notice that remarkably, aside for the improved coefficients in front, we have obtained the same lower bound as
(
Ott et al., 2021
, Lemma 2)
(see (
62
)) via a completely different method. However, by choosing
j_{i}
differently in (
111
) there is still room for future optimization.

In addition, for
0<i<\mu
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,i)^{2}}{\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,i-1)\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,i+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{m}-q^{i-1})}{(q^{m}-q^{i})}\frac{(q^{\eta}-q^{i-1})}{(q^{\eta}-q^{i})}\frac{q^{i}(q^{i+1}-1)}{q^{i-1}(q^{i}-1)}\geq q^{2}>1

(113)

and thus
(\operatorname{NM}_{q}(m,\eta,i))_{i=0}^{\mu}
is a
logaritmically concave sequence
(
log-concave
for short).
Moreover, since convolution preserves log-concavity, it holds that for all
\ell
the sequence

\left(\left|{\mathcal{S}_{i}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}}\right|\right)_{i=0}^{\mu\ell}
is log-concave. Hence we can take the
convex hull
of the logarithm of

the bound in Theorem
4.27
to obtain a slight improvement. In this context, the convex hull of a sequence
(a_{i})_{i=0}^{\mu\ell}
is the sequence
(b_{i})_{i=0}^{\mu\ell}
with

b_{i}=\max_{x,y>0}\left\{a_{i},\,\frac{y}{x+y}a_{i-x}+\frac{x}{x+y}a_{i+y}\right\},

and as
a
consequence of Carathéodory’s theorem on convex hulls this is the lowest-valued concave sequence that is coefficient-wise greater or equal to
(a_{i})_{i=0}^{\mu\ell}
.

Theorem 4.28
.

The convex hull of the sequence

\left(\log_{q}\left(\left({\gamma_{q,m,\eta}}^{-1}\right)^{i/\mu}\binom{\ell}{r}q^{i(m+\eta-\frac{i}{\ell})+\frac{r^{2}}{\ell}-r}\right)\right)_{i=0}^{\mu\ell}

is a lower bound on
\log_{q}\left|{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{m,\eta,\ell,q}}\right|
.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we derive novel upper and lower bounds on the size of an
\ell
-dimensional sphere. First, we consider a general setting for any coordinate-additive metric, and then we focus specifically on spheres in the sum-rank metric.
In the general setting, we employ techniques from information theory and typical sequences to bound the logarithmic growth rate of the sphere’s size, utilizing the entropy of a Boltzmann-like distribution representing the marginal distribution of a typical sequence.
Although these bounds apply to any coordinate-additive metric, we also derive new bounds specifically for the sum-rank metric, using convolution arguments for the upper bounds and ordinary generating functions for the lower bounds.
Finally, we compare our bounds to existing ones in the sum-rank metric and observe that, when choosing the most suitable bound for each regime, our bounds outperform the existing closed-form bounds.

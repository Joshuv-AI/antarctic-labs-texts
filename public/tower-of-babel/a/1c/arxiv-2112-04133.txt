Remarks on Ruggeri’s First Model of Dissipative Fluid Dynamics as a Symmetric Hyperbolic System
Heinrich Freistühler

Abstract




The first appealing formulation for the dynamics of viscous heat-conductive fluids as
a symmetric hyperbolic system of balance laws was given by Ruggeri in his pioneering 1983
paper
[
8
]
. Using

\Upsilon=(\Upsilon_{1},\Upsilon_{2},\Upsilon_{3},\Upsilon_{4},\Upsilon_{5},\Upsilon_{6})=(\tilde{\psi},\tilde{u},\tilde{\theta},\tilde{\Sigma},\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{q})

with

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}\equiv\frac{g-u^{2}/2}{\theta},\ \tilde{u}\equiv\frac{u}{\theta},\ \tilde{\theta}=-\frac{1}{\theta},\ \tilde{\Sigma}\equiv\frac{\Sigma}{\theta},\ \tilde{\sigma}\equiv\frac{\sigma}{\theta},\ \tilde{q}\equiv\frac{q}{\theta^{2}},

(1)

as state variable, this formulation reads

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial X^{0}(\Upsilon)}{\partial\Upsilon_{i}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\left(\frac{\partial X^{j}(\Upsilon)}{\partial\Upsilon_{i}}\right)=I^{i}(\Upsilon),\hskip 10.00002pti=1,...,6,

where
X^{0}

[

X^{0}=\frac{p}{\theta},\hskip 10.00002ptX^{j}=\left(p\mathbb{I}+\theta(\tilde{\Sigma}+\tilde{\sigma}\mathbb{I})\right)\tilde{u}_{j}+\theta\tilde{q}_{j}

p=\hat{p}\left(\theta,\tilde{\psi}+\frac{1}{2}\theta\tilde{u}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{1}\tilde{\Sigma}:\tilde{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{2}\tilde{\sigma}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{0}\tilde{q}^{2}\right)

I(\Upsilon)

I^{1}(\Upsilon)=0_{1},I^{2}(\Upsilon)=0_{3},I^{3}(\Upsilon)=0_{1}

First order algorithms for computing linear and polyhedral estimates
Yannis Bekri, LJK, Université Grenoble Alpes, Anatoli Juditsky, LJK, Université Grenoble Alpes, Arkadi Nemirovski, Georgia Institute of Technology

Abstract
It was recently shown [ 7 , 9 ] that “properly built” linear and polyhedral estimates nearly attain minimax accuracy bounds in the problem of recovery of unknown signal from noisy observations of linear images of the signal when the signal set is an ellitope . However, design of nearly optimal estimates relies upon solving semidefinite optimization problems with matrix variables, what puts the synthesis of such estimates beyond the rich of the standard Interior Point algorithms of semidefinite optimization even for moderate size recovery problems. Our goal is to develop First Order Optimization algorithms for the computationally efficient design of linear and polyhedral estimates. In this paper we (a) explain how to eliminate matrix variables, thus reducing dramatically the design dimension when passing from Interior Point to First Order optimization algorithms and (2) develop and analyse a dedicated algorithm of the latter type—Composite Truncated Level method.

3.

The above considerations justify the oracle

Appendix A
CTL—Composite Truncated Level algorithm

A.1
Situation and goal.

CTL is a First Order method for solving optimization problems

\hbox{\rm Opt}=\min\limits_{x\in X}\phi(x):=\psi(x)+f(x),

[formula]

where

•

X\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
is nonempty, convex, closed, and bounded

•

\psi:X\to{\mathbf{R}}
and
f:X\to{\mathbf{R}}
are Lipschitz continuous convex functions.

Our assumptions are as follows:

Ass1
:
X
is equipped with a
proximal setup
composed of a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

and a
distance-generating function

\omega:X\to{\mathbf{R}}
which is continuously differentiable
and strongly convex on
X
with convexity modulus 1 w.r.t.
\|\cdot\|
:

\langle\nabla\omega(x)-\nabla\omega(y),x-y\rangle\geq\|x-y\|^{2}\,\;\;\forall x,y\in X.

Proximal setup induces
Bregman distance
V_{x}(y)
on
X
and
Bregman diameter
\Omega
of
X
:

\displaystyle V_{x}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(y)-[\omega(x)-\langle\nabla\omega(x),y-x\rangle]\geq{1\over 2}\|x-y\|^{2},\,x,y\in X

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[2\max\limits_{x,y\in X}V_{x}(y)\right]^{1/2}\geq\max\limits_{x,y\in X}\|x-y\|.

Ass2
: We have at our disposal
oracle
{\cal O}
which, given on input a query point
x\in X
, returns a
piece
—a Lipschitz continuous on
X
convex function

\phi_{x}(\cdot)=\psi(\cdot)+f_{x}(\cdot):X\to{\mathbf{R}}

where
f_{x}(\cdot)
belongs to some family
{\cal F}
of “simple” Lipschitz continuous convex functions on
X
. We suppose that

\forall(x,y\in X):\;f_{x}(y)\leq f(y)\;\;\&\;\;f_{x}(x)=f(x)

(in particular,
\phi(x)=\phi_{x}(x)
)
and that functions
\phi_{x}(\cdot):X\to{\mathbf{R}}
are uniformly in
x\in X
Lipschitz continuous on
X
:

|\phi_{x}(u)-\phi_{x}(v)|\leq L_{\phi}\|u-v\|\,\,\forall(u,v\in X)

(33)

for some
L_{\phi}<\infty
.

The simplest example of such oracle is that of family
{\cal F}
comprised of affine functions of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
, and
f_{x}(y)=f(x)+\langle f^{\prime}(x),y-x\rangle

where
f^{\prime}(x)
is a subgradient of
f
at
x
(“first order oracle”). In a less trivial example,
f(x)
is the largest eigenvalue of a symmetric
matrix
S(x)
which affinely depends on
x
, while
f_{x}(y)=\max_{i\leq k}e_{i}^{T}S(y)e_{i}
, where
k\leq n
is fixed and
e_{1},...,e_{k}
are the
k
leading eigenvectors of
S(x)
.

Ass3
: We assume that for some positive integer
\tau
we are able to solve efficiently problems of the form

\min\limits_{y}\left\{\psi(y)+\max\limits_{\iota\leq\tau}f_{x_{\iota}}(y):y\in X,\alpha(y)\leq 0\right\}

and

\min\limits_{y}\left\{V_{x}(y):y\in X,\psi(y)+\max\limits_{\iota\leq\tau}f_{x_{\iota}}(y)\leq\ell,\alpha(y)\leq 0\right\}

where
\alpha(\cdot)
is an affine function.

We remark that the algorithm to follow is the composite version of Non-Euclidean Restricted Memory Level method
[
4
]

operating with
\psi\equiv 0
and the family of affine functions in the role of
{\cal F}
; the Euclidean version of the latter algorithm is the minimization version
of the Proximal level bundle method from

[
11
]
.

A.1.1
The algorithm

The description of CTL is as follows.

0.
Control parameters of the algorithms are
\lambda_{\ell}\in(0,1),\overline{\theta}\in(0.1),\underline{\theta}\in(0,1)
.

1.
At the beginning of an iteration of CTL, we have at our disposal

1.1)

upper bound

\overline{\phi}
on
Opt
—the best (the smallest) of the values of
\phi
observed at the query points processed so far. These upper bounds do not increase as the iteration count grows. The query point
\widehat{y}
with
\overline{\phi}=\phi(\widehat{y})
is considered as the approximate solution generated so far.

1.2)

lower bound

\underline{\phi}
on
Opt
; these lower bounds do not decrease as the iteration number grows

1.3)

prox-center

\overline{x}\in X
and
level

\ell\in(\underline{\phi},\overline{\phi})

1.4)

query point

x\in X

1.5)

bundle
– a nonempty collection
{\cal B}
of
\tau_{\cal B}\leq\tau
pieces
\phi_{\iota}(\cdot)=\psi(\cdot)+f^{\iota}(\cdot)\}
,
1\leq\iota\leq\tau_{{\cal B}}
, with
f^{\iota}\in{\cal F}
; positive integer
\tau
is a parameter of the algorithm.

\bullet
The very first iteration is preceded by
initialization
where we call the oracle at a (whatever) point
x_{\hbox{\tiny ini}}\in X
and set

\overline{\phi}=\phi(x_{\hbox{\tiny ini}}),\,\underline{\phi}=\min\limits_{x\in X}[\psi(x)+f_{x_{\hbox{\tiny ini}}}(x)],\,{\cal B}=\{\psi(x)+f_{x_{\hbox{\tiny ini}}}(x)\}

Note that since the pieces reported by the oracle underestimate
\phi(\cdot)
, we do ensure
\underline{\phi}\leq\hbox{\rm Opt}
.

2.
Iterations are split into consecutive
phases
, with prox-center and level common to all iterations of a phase. For a particular phase,

2.1)
the prox-center
\overline{x}
is selected at the beginning of the first iteration of the phase and can be a whatever point of
X
;

2.2)
the query point of the first iteration of the phase is
x=\overline{x}
,

2.3)
the level
\ell
is selected at the beginning of the very first iteration of the phase as

\ell=\lambda_{\ell}\underline{\phi}+(1-\lambda_{\ell})\overline{\phi}.

\bullet
At the beginning of the first iteration of a phase, we set

\overline{\Delta}=\overline{\phi}-\ell,\;\underline{\Delta}=\ell-\underline{\phi},\;\Delta=\overline{\Delta}+\underline{\Delta}=\overline{\phi}-\underline{\phi}.

Note that
the gap

\Delta
of a phase upper-bounds the inaccuracy in terms of the objective of the approximate solution available at the beginning of the phase.

3.
At iterations of a phase, we maintain the relation

\phi(y)\geq\ell\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;y\in X\;\;\mbox{such that}\;\;\langle\nabla\omega(x)-\nabla\omega(\overline{x}),y-x\rangle<0

(34)

where
\overline{x},\ell
are the prox-center and the level of the phase, and
x
is the query point of the iteration. Note that this relation takes place at the very first iteration of a phase, since for such an iteration
x=\overline{x}
.

4.
An iteration of a phase is organized as follows:

4.1)
We call the oracle at the query point
x
of the iteration, thus getting the value of the objective
\phi(x)
and a piece
\phi_{x}(\cdot)
. After
\phi(x)
is known, we

—

update the upper bound
\overline{\phi}
and the approximate solution
\widehat{x}
:

\big(\overline{\phi},{\widehat{x}}\big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\phi(x),x),&\mbox{if}\;\phi(x)<\overline{\phi}\\
(\overline{\phi},{\widehat{x}}),&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

—

update the bundle
{\cal B}
by adding to it the piece
\phi_{x}(\cdot)
and removing, if necessary, one of “old” pieces to keep the number of pieces
\phi_{1},...,\phi_{\tau_{\cal B}}
in the resulting bundle to be at most
\tau
.

4.2)
If
\phi(x)-\ell\leq\overline{\theta}\,\overline{\Delta}
(“essential progress in upper bound on
Opt
”), we terminate the phase and pass to the next one. Otherwise we solve the auxiliary problem

\widetilde{\phi}=\min_{y}\left\{\widehat{\phi}(y):=\max_{1\leq\iota\leq\tau_{\cal B}}\phi_{\iota}(y):y\in X,\langle\nabla\omega(x)-\nabla\omega(\overline{x}),y-x\rangle\geq 0\right\}

(35)

(as usual,
\widetilde{\phi}=+\infty
when the right hand side problem is infeasible), and update the lower bound
\underline{\phi}
on
Opt
according to

\underline{\phi}\mapsto\max\left[\underline{\phi},\min[\widetilde{\phi},\ell]\right]

Note:
by (
34
),
\phi(y)\geq\ell
at every point
y\in X
which is not feasible for the optimization problem in (
35
). Besides this, the pieces
\phi_{i}(\cdot)
in the bundle, and, consequently, the
model

\widehat{\phi}(\cdot)
, underestimate
\phi(\cdot)
on
X
. As a result, the quantity

\min[\widetilde{\phi},\ell]
, and consequently the new value of
\underline{\phi}
, indeed is a lower bound on
Opt
.

If updated
\underline{\phi}
satisfies

\ell-\underline{\phi}\leq\underline{\theta}\,\underline{\Delta}

(“essential progress in lower bound on
Opt
”), we terminate the phase and pass to the next one.

4.3)
If the iteration in question does not result in phase change, we solve the auxiliary problem

\min\limits_{y}\left\{V_{\overline{x}}(y):\;y\in X,\,\widehat{\phi}(y)\leq\ell,\,\langle\nabla\omega(x)-\nabla\omega(\overline{x}),\,y-x\rangle\geq 0\right\}

(36)

take its optimal solution,
x_{+}
, as the new query point, and pass to the next iteration of the phase.

Note:
when we need to solve (
36
), we have, by construction,
\widetilde{\phi}\leq\ell
, so that the problem in (
36
) is feasible, a feasible solution being a minimizer in (
35
). Thus, the new query point
x_{+}
is well defined. Besides this, from the definition of
x_{+}
it follows that

\langle\nabla\omega(x_{+})-\nabla\omega(\overline{x}),y-x_{+}\rangle\geq 0

for every feasible solution
y
to (
36
). As a result, when
y\in X
satisfies the relation

\langle\nabla\omega(x_{+})-\nabla\omega(\overline{x}),y-x_{+}\rangle<0,

y
is infeasible for (
36
), meaning that either
\widehat{\phi}(y)>\ell
, and in such case
\phi(y)\geq\widehat{\phi}(y)\geq\ell
, or
y
satisfies the premise in
(
34
), implying that
\phi(y)\geq\ell
by (
34
). We see that
(
34
) holds true when
x
is replaced with
x_{+}
, that is, (
34
) is maintained during the iterations.

A.1.2
Convergence analysis

Observe that

(!)
If a phase is finite, then the gap
\Delta_{+}
of the subsequent phase does not exceed a fixed fraction
\theta\Delta
of the gap
\Delta
of the phase in question, where

\theta=\max\left[1-\lambda_{\ell}\overline{\theta},\underline{\theta}+\lambda_{\ell}(1-\underline{\theta})\right]\in(0,1);

Indeed, (!) is an immediate consequence of the phase termination rules in
4.2
combined with the facts that
\underline{\phi}
does not decrease, and
\overline{\phi}
does not increase as the iteration count grows.

The following observation is crucial:

(!!)
The number of iterations at a phase with gap
\Delta
does not exceed

\left\lceil\left({L_{\phi}\Omega\over\overline{\theta}\lambda_{\ell}\Delta}\right)^{2}\right\rceil

(37)

(here
\lceil a\rceil
stands for the upper integer part of
a
—the smallest integer greater or equal to
a
).

Indeed, let
\ell
be the level of the phase. Assume that the phase contains more that
T\geq 1
iterations, so that the upper bound
\overline{\phi}
, the lower bound
\underline{\phi}
on
Opt
, same as the model
\phi_{t}(\cdot)
generated at iteration
t
of the phase are well defined for
t=1,...,T
, and the query points
x_{t}
are well defined for
t=1,...,T+1
. By construction,
for
1\leq t\leq T
we have

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(x_{t})

\displaystyle>\ell+\overline{\theta}\,\overline{\Delta},

(38a)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(x_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq\ell,

(38b)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla V_{\overline{x}}(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 0

(38c)

where
\overline{x}=x_{1}
is the prox-center of the phase. Indeed, when
t\leq T
,

— (
38a
) holds true since otherwise the phase would be terminated at its
t
-th iteration due to essential progress in upper bound on
Opt
, which is not the case when
t\leq T
;

— (
38b
) and (
38c
) hold because, by construction of
x_{t+1}
at a non-concluding iteration
t
of a phase,
x_{t+1}
minimizes continuously differentiable on
X
convex function
V_{\overline{x}}(\cdot)
over the set

X\cap\{y\in X:\phi_{t}(y)\leq\ell\}\cap\{y\in Y:\langle\nabla V_{\overline{x}}(x_{t}),y-x_{t}\rangle\geq 0\}.

Now note that by construction of
\phi_{t}(\cdot)
and due to Assumption Ass2,
\phi_{t}
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{\phi}
w.r.t.
\|\cdot\|
, which combines with (
38a
) and (
38b
) to imply that

\|x_{t}-x_{t+1}\|>L_{\phi}^{-1}\overline{\theta}\cdot\overline{\Delta}=L_{\phi}^{-1}\overline{\theta}\lambda_{\ell}\Delta.

The latter relation, in turn, combines with (
38c
) and with inherited from
\omega(\cdot)
strong convexity of
V_{\overline{x}}(\cdot)
on
X
w.r.t.
\|\cdot\|
to imply that

V_{\overline{x}}(x_{t+1})>V_{\overline{x}}(x_{t})+{[\overline{\theta}\lambda_{\ell}]^{2}\Delta^{2}\over 2L_{\phi}^{2}},\,1\leq t\leq T.

Taking into account that
V_{\overline{x}}(x_{1})=0
and
V_{\overline{x}}(x)\leq{1\over 2}\Omega^{2}
for all
x\in X
, we arrive at (
37
).

As an immediate consequence of (!) and (!!), we get the following efficiency estimate:

Proposition A.1

For every
\epsilon\in(0,L_{\phi}\Omega)
, the overall number of CTL iterations until an

N(\epsilon)=C(L_{\phi}\Omega/\epsilon)^{2}

with

,

\overline{\lambda}=\varkappa\lambda^{*},\;\;\overline{\mu}=\varkappa^{-1}\mu^{*},\;\;\overline{\Theta}=\varkappa^{-1}\Theta^{*}

\phi_{\cal T}(\overline{\mu})+\phi_{{\cal S}}(\overline{\lambda})+\sigma^{2}\varkappa^{2}{\mathop{\hbox{\rm Tr}}}(\overline{\Theta})\leq\varkappa\left(\phi_{\cal T}(\mu^{*})+\phi_{{\cal S}}(\lambda^{*})+\sigma^{2}{\mathop{\hbox{\rm Tr}}}(\overline{\Theta})\right)\leq\varkappa r^{*}

B.3
Proof of Lemma
2.1

Let
(\lambda,\mu,\Theta)
be a feasible solution to (
22
) such that

\Lambda:=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}S_{\ell}\succ 0,\,\Xi:=\sum_{k}\mu_{k}T_{k}\succ 0.

(39)

Note that every feasible solution to (
22
) remains feasible and satisfies (
39
) after replacing zero entries of
\lambda_{\ell}
and
\mu_{k}
, if any, with arbitrarily small positive entries.

Now, due to

\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Lambda&\tfrac{1}{2}B\cr\hline\cr\tfrac{1}{2}B^{T}&A^{T}\Theta A+\Xi\end{array}\right]\succeq 0

we have

\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{\nu}&\tfrac{1}{2}\Lambda^{-1/2}B\Xi^{-1/2}\cr\hline\cr\tfrac{1}{2}\Xi^{-1/2}B^{T}\Lambda^{-1/2}&\Xi^{-1/2}A^{T}\Theta A\Xi^{-1/2}+I_{n}\end{array}\right]\succeq 0.

When setting
\Theta^{1/2}A\Xi^{-1/2}=US
with
UU^{T}=I_{n}
and
S\succeq 0
, we get

\tfrac{1}{4}[\Lambda^{-1/2}B\Xi^{-1/2}]^{T}[\Lambda^{-1/2}B\Xi^{-1/2}]\preceq S^{2}+I_{n}\preceq(S+I_{n})^{2}

(recall that

Q\in{\mathbf{R}}^{\nu\times n}
of spectral norm
\|Q\|_{2,2}\leq 1
such that

\tfrac{1}{2}[\Lambda^{-1/2}B\Xi^{-1/2}]=Q(S+I_{n})

B-2\Lambda^{1/2}QS\Xi^{1/2}=2\Lambda^{1/2}Q\Xi^{1/2}.

Due to
\|Q\|_{2,2}\leq 1

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Lambda&\tfrac{1}{2}(B-H^{T}A)\cr\hline\cr\tfrac{1}{2}(B-H^{T}A)^{T}&\Xi\cr\end{array}\right]\succeq 0.

\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\Lambda&\tfrac{1}{2}H^{T}\cr\hline\cr\tfrac{1}{2}H&\Theta\cr\end{array}\right]\succeq 0.

Appendix C
Contrast synthesis for the polyhedral estimate

Projecting, if necessary, the observations onto the image
space of
A
, we reduce the situation to that in which this image space is the entire

{\mathbf{R}}^{m}
; that is,
m\leq n
with positive singular values
\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{m}
of A. Let us assume that
n=m+d
for some
d\geq 0
, and let

A=UDV^{T},\quad D=\big[\underbrace{\hbox{\rm Diag}(\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{m})}_{=:D_{m}},0_{m\times d}\big],\;U\in{\mathbf{R}}^{m\times m},\;V\in{\mathbf{R}}^{m\times n},

be the (full) singular-value decomposition of
A
. The starting point of the following computation is the bound (
22
) for the
\epsilon
-risk of the polyhedral estimate: we have

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=2\min_{\lambda,\mu,\Theta}\left\{\phi_{\cal S}(\lambda)+\phi_{\cal T}(\mu)+\sigma^{2}\chi^{2}{\mathop{\hbox{\rm Tr}}}(\Theta):\,\lambda\geq 0,\mu\geq 0,\Theta\succeq 0,\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}S_{\ell}&\tfrac{1}{2}B\cr\hline\cr\tfrac{1}{2}B^{T}&\sum_{k}\mu_{k}T_{k}+A^{T}\Theta A\end{array}\right]\succeq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=2\min_{\lambda,\kappa,\Theta}\left\{2\phi_{\cal S}(\lambda)+\phi_{\cal T}(\mu)+\sigma^{2}\chi^{2}{\mathop{\hbox{\rm Tr}}}(\Theta):\,\lambda>0,\mu\geq 0,\Theta\succeq 0,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad A^{T}\Theta A\succeq\tfrac{1}{4}B^{T}\Big[\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}S_{\ell}\Big]^{-1}B-\sum_{k}\mu_{k}T_{k}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\min_{\lambda,\mu,\overline{\Theta}}\Big\{F(\lambda,\mu,\overline{\Theta}):=\phi_{\cal S}(\lambda)+\phi_{\cal T}(\mu)+\sigma^{2}\chi^{2}{\mathop{\hbox{\rm Tr}}}(\overline{\Theta}):\,\lambda>0,\mu\geq 0,\,\overline{\Theta}=U^{T}\Theta U\succeq 0,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}D_{m}\overline{\Theta}D_{m}&\cr\hline\cr&\end{array}\right]=D^{T}\overline{\Theta}D\succeq V^{T}\left[\tfrac{1}{4}B^{T}\big[\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}S_{\ell}\big]^{-1}B-\sum_{k}\mu_{k}T_{k}\right]V\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=2\min_{\lambda,\mu,\overline{\Theta}}\Big\{F(\lambda,\mu,\overline{\Theta}):\,\lambda>0,\mu\geq 0,\,\overline{\Theta}\succeq 0,

(48)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\overline{\Theta}&\cr\hline\cr&\end{array}\right]\succeq\underbrace{\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}D_{m}&\cr\hline\cr&I\end{array}\right]^{-1}V^{T}\left[\tfrac{1}{4}B^{T}\big[\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}S_{\ell}\big]^{-1}B-\sum_{k}\mu_{k}T_{k}\right]V\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}D_{m}&\cr\hline\cr&I\end{array}\right]^{-1}}_{=:C(\lambda,\mu)}.\Bigg\}

Observe that the matrix-valued function
C(\lambda,\mu)
is
\succeq
-convex for
\lambda>0
, and is negative definite for every fixed
\lambda
for all
\mu
such that
\min_{i}\mu_{i}\geq\underline{\mu}
large enough. On the other hand, when
Z(\lambda,\mu)\prec 0
in the representation

C(\lambda,\mu)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}X(\lambda,\mu)&Y(\lambda,\mu)\cr\hline\cr Y^{T}(\lambda,\mu)&Z(\lambda,\mu)\end{array}\right]

the semidefinite constraint of (
48
) is satisfied if and only if

\overline{\Theta}\succeq W(\lambda,\mu):=X(\lambda,\mu)+Y^{T}(\lambda,\mu)Z(\lambda,\mu)^{-1}Y(\lambda,\mu).

As a result, when denoting
[M]_{+}
the matrix obtained from a symmetric matrix
M
by replacing its eigenvalues with their positive parts in the eigenvalue decomposition of
M
, we conclude that

\displaystyle{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=2\min_{\lambda,\mu}\big\{\phi_{\cal S}(\lambda)+\phi_{\cal T}(\mu)+\sigma^{2}\chi^{2}{\mathop{\hbox{\rm Tr}}}[W(\lambda,\mu)]_{+}:\,\lambda>0,\mu\geq 0,\,Z(\lambda,\mu)\prec 0\Big\}.

The bottom line is that in the situation of this section, building
\Theta
(and thus—the near-optimal polyhedral estimate) reduces to solving convex problem of design dimension
L+M
with (relatively) easy-to-compute objective and constraints.

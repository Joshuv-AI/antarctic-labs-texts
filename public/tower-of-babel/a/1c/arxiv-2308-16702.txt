Degenerations of Skein Algebras and Quantum Traces
Wade Bloomquist, Hiroaki Karuo, Thang Lê

Abstract
. We introduce a joint generalization, called LRY skein algebras, of Kauffman bracket skein algebras (of surfaces) that encompasses both Roger-Yang skein algebras and stated skein algebras. We will show that, over an arbitrary ground ring which is a commutative domain, the LRY skein algebras are domains and have degenerations (by filtrations) equal to monomial subalgebras of quantum tori. For surfaces without interior punctures, this integrality generalizes a result of Moon and Wong to the most general ground ring. We also calculate the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of LRY algebras and show they are Noetherian if the ground ring is. Moreover they are orderly finitely generated. To study the LRY algebras and prove the above-mentioned results, we construct quantum traces, both the so-called X X -version for all surfaces and also an A A -version for a smaller class of surfaces. We also introduce a modified version of Dehn-Thurston coordinates for curves which are more suitable for the study of skein algebras as they pick up the highest degree terms of products in certain natural filtrations.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
What are the skein algebras studied in this paper?

Roughly speaking, the skein algebras we will study are closely related to the Teichmüller space of a punctured surface, where a puncture has either a “holed” structure (a la Thurston and Fock) or a “decorated” structure (a la Penner). When there is no interior decorated puncture this algebra recovers the stated skein algebra introduced by the third author
[
Le18
]
. On the partially opposite side, when there is no boundary and no holed interior punctures, this algebra recovers the Roger-Yang skein algebra
[
RY14
]
. In the simplest case, when there is no boundary and no decorated punctures, the algebra is the well known Kauffman bracket skein algebra. Our results are new even for the ordinary Kauffman bracket skein algebra.

1.3.
The Roger-Yang Skein Algebra

Roger and Yang introduced the RY skein algebra
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
in an attempt to quantize the decorated Teichmüller space of a surface. This is in contrast to the above
{\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})}
which is related to a quantization of the holed Teichmüller space (or enhanced Teichmüller space in the language of Bonahon and Wong).

In
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
, where
\mathfrak{S}=\Sigma_{g}\setminus\mathcal{M}
with
|\mathcal{M}|<\infty
, we allow not only link diagrams but also arcs ending on points in
\mathcal{M}
with some height order, subject to the additional relations:

\displaystyle v\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{draws/intro_int_arcs_g.pdf}\end{array}=q^{1/2}\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{draws/intro_int_over_g.pdf}\end{array}+q^{-1/2}\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{draws/intro_int_under_g.pdf}\end{array},\qquad\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{draws/intro_int_loop_g.pdf}\end{array}=(q+q^{-1})\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{draws/intro_int_no_g.pdf}\end{array}.

Here we extend the ground ring to
{\mathcal{R}}[v^{\pm 1},v\in\mathcal{M}]
. For details see Section
3
. One of our main results is the following theorem about the structure of the Roger-Yang skein algebra.

Theorem 2

(See Theorem
9.1
)
.

Assume
\mathfrak{S}=\Sigma_{g}\setminus\mathcal{M}
, with
(g,|\mathcal{M}|)\not\in\{(1,0),(0,k)\mid k\leq 3\}
. Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a commutative domain with a distinguished invertible element
q^{1/2}
.

(a) There exists an algebra
\mathbb{N}
-filtration of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
whose associated graded algebra embeds into a quantum torus
\mathbb{T}({\tilde{{\mathsf{Q}}}};{\mathcal{R}})
, where
{\tilde{{\mathsf{Q}}}}
is an integral
2r\times 2r
antisymmetric matrix.

(b) The algebra
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
is a domain and is orderly finitely generated. Moreover
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
is Noetherian whenever
{\mathcal{R}}
is.

(c) There exists an algebra
(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}^{k})
-filtration of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
such that the associated graded algebra is isomorphic to a monomial subalgebra of the quantum torus
\mathbb{T}({\tilde{{\mathsf{Q}}}};{\mathcal{R}})
.

(d) The Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}
is
6g-6+3|\mathcal{M}|
.

The matrix

is determined by a pants decomposition and its dual graph in a fashion similar to the case
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
.

One special case of (b) is

Corollary 3
.

The algebra
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}}={\mathbb{C}},q^{1/2}=1)
is a domain.

This fact is interesting because it is related to the origin of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
as follows. Roger and Yang
[

]
introduced relations (

) specifically to encode Mondello’s Poisson structure
[

]
on the decorated Teichmuller space, where

is the length of the horocycle about the corresponding puncture. They showed that there is a Poisson algebra homomorphism

\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{RY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}}={\mathbb{C}},{q^{1/2}=1})\to{\mathbb{C}}[\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathfrak{S})],

(9)

where

is the ring of (complex valued) regular functions on

. To have a meaningful theory of quantization, Roger and Yang conjectured that the map (

]
, where Corollary

[
MW22
]
also announced a proof of Corollary

for general surfaces. Since

is a free module over

1.4.
LRY Skein Algebras

One efficient way to study skein algebras is to cut the surface into elementary pieces, study the pieces, then glue them back to obtain global results. This was done by the third author
[
Le18
]
where he reproved the existence of the Bonahon-Wong quantum trace, and many other results.
We can use the same method here. Even though we begin with surfaces without boundary, it is useful to extend the theory to involve boundary in a meaningful way so that we can glue back later.

Thus, now
\mathfrak{S}
is an oriented surface where each connected component of the boundary
\partial\mathfrak{S}
is an open interval
(0,1)
. The punctures (or ideal points) in the interior of
\mathfrak{S}
are partitioned into two types: “holed” and“decorated”. Consider tangle diagrams on
\mathfrak{S}
where the end points can go to
\partial\mathfrak{S}
or to decorated points, with height order, and states
\pm
on
\partial\mathfrak{S}
. The LRY skein algebra
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
is the module freely spanned by isotopy classes of such tangle diagrams subject to Kauffman bracket relations (
1
), Roger-Yang Relations (
1.3
), and the additional relations involving the endpoints on
\partial\mathfrak{S}
. See Section
3
for details. This is a unification of the Roger-Yang skein algebra and the stated skein algebra of the third author.

An important feature of the introduction of the boundary is the existence of the cutting homomorphism. Suppose
c
is an interior ideal arc of
\mathfrak{S}
whose endpoints are not at interior decorated points. By cutting
\mathfrak{S}
along
c
we get a new surface
{\mathsf{Cut}}_{c}(\mathfrak{S})
.

Theorem 4

(Part of Theorem
3.12
)
.

There exists an algebra embedding, given by an explicit state sum, from
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
into
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}({\mathsf{Cut}}_{c}(\mathfrak{S});{\mathcal{R}})
.

By cutting
\mathfrak{S}
along interior edges of a triangulation, where edges are not allowed to end at interior decorated punctures (see Section
4
), we can prove the following result about the quantum trace and the structure of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
.

Theorem 5

(Part of Theorems
5.2
and
5.3
)
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{S}
has a triangulation
\Delta
with
r
edges. Let the ground ring
{\mathcal{R}}
be a commutative domain with a distinguished invertible element
q^{1/2}
.

(a) There is an algebra embedding of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
into a quantum torus

\mathrm{tr}_{\Delta;{\mathcal{R}}}:\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{T}({\mathsf{Q}}_{\Delta};{\mathcal{R}})

where
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\Delta}
is an antisymmetric integral
r\times r
matrix depending on the triangulation
\Delta
. Consequently
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
is a domain.

(b) There is an algebra
\mathbb{N}
-filtration of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
whose associated graded algebra is a monomial subalgebra
\mathbb{T}({\mathsf{Q}}_{\Delta},\Lambda;{\mathcal{R}})
, where
\Lambda
is the monoid of all possible triangular coordinates of simple diagrams on
\mathfrak{S}
.

(c)
The algebra
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{LRY}}(\mathfrak{S};{\mathcal{R}})
is orderly finitely generated, has Gelfand Kirillov dimension
r
; and moreover it is Noetherian if the ground ring
{\mathcal{R}}
is.

We also construct a reduced version of the quantum trace
\mathrm{tr}_{\Delta}
.

When
\mathfrak{S}
has no boundary and no decorated punctures, part (a) of Theorem
5
was proved by Bonahon and Wong. When
\mathfrak{S}
has no decorated punctures, Theorem
5
was proved in
[
LY22
]
, whose proof is adapted here: First cutting
\mathfrak{S}
along edges of
\Delta
, we get a collection of ideal triangles and 1-decorated monogons (monogons with one decorated puncture in the interior). The reduced quantum trace for triangles was constructed already in
[
Le18
]
. New here is the 1-decorated monogon, for which we will construct a reduced quantum trace. Then we patch the quantum traces of the faces of the triangulation together to get a reduced quantum trace for
\mathfrak{S}
. A trick of gluing a triangle along each boundary edge used in
[
LY22
]
allows to extend it to a full quantum trace and prove Theorem
5
.

1.5.
Modified Dehn-Thurston coordinates and another quantum trace

Theorems
and
2
correspond to cases when
\mathfrak{S}
does not admit an ideal triangulation and as such Theorem
5
does not apply. Instead, a pants decomposition is used for the proofs of these theorems.

We need to use another type of quantum trace, which is an extension of the
A
-version developed in
[
LY22
]
and an earlier version of Muller
[
Mul16
]
. Thus we develop a quantum trace for the so-called boundary simplified skein algebra which was considered in
[
Le19
,
PS19
]
. We calculate this quantum trace for each pair of pants in the decomposition, see Theorem
8.1
, and show that they can be glued together to get a global algebra map described in part a) of Theorems
and
2
.

To parameterize simple diagrams on
\Sigma_{g}\setminus\mathcal{P}
, we use a modified version of Dehn-Thurston (DT) coordinates. The reason is the ordinary DT coordinates do not behave well under the product of the skein algebra. In
[
PS19
,
FKBL19
]
one has to restrict to the so-called triangular multicurves in order to have control over the behavior with respect to the product. To get deeper results, we need to consider all multicurves. The modified DT coordinates, developed in Section
7
, help to extract the highest degree term of the product, and help to prove Theorems
and
2
.

1.6.
Organization of the paper

Section
2
contains algebraic background material. In Section
3
, we define LRY skein algebras and their reduced versions.
Section
4
recalls details on ideal triangulations and the cutting homomorphism with an eye towards embedding LRY skein algebras into quantum tori.
In Section
5
we give quantum traces to embed LRY skein algebras into quantum tori using ideal triangles and 1-marked monogons.
In Section
6
we give a definition of boundary simplified skein algebras to define modified Dehn–Thurston coordinates for (non-compact) pairs of pants in Section
7
.
In Section
8
we embed the associated graded algebras of the LRY skein algebra of a (non-compact) pair of pants into a quantum torus.
By combining the elementary cases in Section
8
, we embed the associated graded algebra of the LRY skein algebra of a surface into quantum tori in Section
9
.

2.2.
Weyl normalization and algebras with reflection

Assume
A
is a
{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}
-algebra which is torsion free as a
{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}
-module.

Suppose
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\in A
are pairwise
q
-commuting elements. Since
A
is
{\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}]}
-torsion free,
there is a unique

c_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
x_{i}x_{j}=q^{c_{ij}}x_{j}x_{i}
unless
x_{i}x_{j}=0
.
The
Weyl normalization
of the product
x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n}
is

[x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n}]_{\mathrm{Weyl}}=q^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}}x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n}.

If
\sigma
is a permutation of
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, then
[x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n}]_{\mathrm{Weyl}}=[x_{\sigma(1)}x_{\sigma(2)}\dots x_{\sigma(n)}]_{\mathrm{Weyl}}
, which motivates the use of the Weyl normalization.

If
{\mathcal{R}}
is a ground ring, i.e. a commutative
{\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1/2}]}
-domain, then we usually use the notation
[ab]_{\mathrm{Weyl}}
to denote the element
[ab]_{\mathrm{Weyl}}\otimes 1\in A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}{\mathcal{R}}
.

We say
A
is a
{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}
-algebra with reflection
if it is equipped with a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear anti-involution
\omega
, called the
reflection
, such that
\omega({q^{1/2}})=q^{-1/2}
. In other words,
\omega:A\to A
is
\mathbb{Z}
-linear such that for all
x,y\in A
,

\omega(xy)=\omega(y)\omega(x),\qquad\omega({q^{1/2}}x)=q^{-1/2}\omega(x).

An element
z\in A
is
reflection invariant
if
\omega(z)=z
. If
B
is another
{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}
-algebra with reflection
\omega^{\prime}
, then a map
f:A\to B
is
reflection invariant
if
f\circ\omega=\omega^{\prime}\circ f
.

The following trivial statement is very helpful in many calculations.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
a,b
be reflection invariant elements of a
{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}
-algebra with reflection.

(a) If
a{\,\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(q)}}{{=}}\,\,}b
then
a=b
.

(b) If
a
and
b
are
q
-commuting, then
[ab]_{\mathrm{Weyl}}
is reflection invariant.

2.3.

The

{\mathbb{T}(Q)}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}\langle x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{r}^{\pm 1}\rangle/\langle x_{i}x_{j}=q^{Q_{ij}}x_{j}x_{i}\rangle.

For
{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}

x^{\mathbf{k}}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}[x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots x_{r}^{k_{r}}]_{\mathrm{Weyl}}=q^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i<j}Q_{ij}k_{i}k_{j}}x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\dots x_{r}^{k_{r}}

\{x^{\mathbf{k}}\mid{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\}
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}
-basis of

3.
The LRY skein algebra

In this section we give the definition of the LRY (Lê-Roger-Yang) skein algebra and its reduced version, and present basic facts, including a description of a basis of the LRY algebra and the cutting homomorphism.

In the theory of decorated and holed Teichmuller spaces, an ideal point in the interior of the surface is equipped with either a horocycle (decoration) or a holed structure. We call the first type a
marked
puncture, and the second a
holed
puncture. To be technically easier to describe diagrams on the surfaces, we will present a holed puncture as still an ideal point, while a marked point as a
circle boundary component
.

Throughout
{\mathcal{R}}
is a ground ring, which is a commutative
{\mathbb{Z}_{q}}
-domain.

3.1.
Punctured surfaces

In this subsection we define and fix conventions concerning punctured surfaces, compact arcs, and ideal arcs.

Definition 3.1
.

A
punctured surface

\mathfrak{S}
is a surface of the form
\mathfrak{S}=\overline{\mathfrak{S}}\backslash\mathcal{P}
where
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}
is a compact, oriented surface with (potentially empty) boundary
\partial\overline{\mathfrak{S}}
and
\mathcal{P}
is a finite set. An element of
\mathcal{P}
is called a puncture, or ideal point, of
\mathfrak{S}
; it is a boundary puncture if it lies on the boundary of
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}
, otherwise it is an interior puncture.

Each connected component of
\partial\mathfrak{S}
which is an open interval is called a
boundary edge
.

Note that
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}
is uniquely determined by
\mathfrak{S}
. The set of all circle components of
\partial\mathfrak{S}
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{S})
, or simply
\mathcal{M}
when the surface is clear from context.
In figures we will depict punctures by small squares.

An
ideal arc
on
\mathfrak{S}
is a smooth embedding
a:(0,1)\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
which can be extended to an immersion
\bar{a}:[0,1]\to\overline{\mathfrak{S}}
such that
\bar{a}(0),\bar{a}(1)\in\mathcal{P}
. This ideal arc is
trivial
if the image of
\bar{a}
bounds a disk in
\overline{\mathfrak{S}}
, which forces
\bar{a}(0)=\bar{a}(1)
.

A
\partial\mathfrak{S}
-arc
is a smooth proper embedding
a:[0,1]\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
. A
\partial\mathfrak{S}
-arc is
trivial
if it can be homotoped in
\mathfrak{S}
relative its boundary points into
\partial\mathfrak{S}
.

A
loop on
\mathfrak{S}
is a simple closed curve on
\mathfrak{S}
. A loop is
trivial
if it bounds a disk in
\mathfrak{S}
. A loop is
peripheral
if it is parallel to an element of
\mathcal{M}
, i.e. circle boundary component.

The
thickening of
\mathfrak{S}
is
{\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}}:=\mathfrak{S}\times(-1,1)
, an oriented 3-dimensional manifold with boundary
{\partial{{\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}}}}=\partial\mathfrak{S}\times(-1,1)
. Each connected component of
{\partial{{\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}}}}
is

where
c
is either a boundary edge or an element of
\mathcal{M}
. In the first case we call
c\times(-1,1)
a
boundary wall
, and in the latter case a
boundary well
. For a point

the number

is called its
height
. The tangent space

can be identified with
T_{x}(\mathfrak{S})\times(-1,1)
, and a tangent vector in

is
vertical
if it is parallel to the component
(-1,1)
and has the positive direction of

.

We will often identify
\mathfrak{S}
with the copy
\mathfrak{S}\times\{0\}\subset{\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}}
.

.

The set

is a free
{\mathcal{R}}
-basis for the
{\mathcal{R}}
-module

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to the one in
[
Le18
]
, where the case when
\partial\mathfrak{S}
has no circle component was considered. Actually all the technical points have been covered in
[
Le18
,
RY14
]

We will use the diamond lemma, in the language of confluence theory for graphs due to Sikora-Westbury
[
SW07
]

Let
\tilde{B}
be the set of all positively ordered stated
\partial\mathfrak{S}-
tangle diagrams. Denote
{\mathcal{R}}\tilde{B}
the
{\mathcal{R}}
-module freely spanned by
\tilde{B}
. Define the binary relation
\rightarrow
on
{\mathcal{R}}\tilde{B}

D\rightarrow E
if
D\in\tilde{B}
and
E\in{\mathcal{R}}\tilde{B}
where
D
is the left hand side of one of the LRY defining relations and
E
is the corresponding right hand side. This is then extended
{\mathcal{R}}
linearly from
\tilde{B}
to
{\mathcal{R}}\tilde{B}
. If
D\to E_{1}\dots\to E_{k}=E
, we say
E
is a descendant of
D
.

If
\rightarrow
is
terminal
and
locally confluent
, then the subset of
\tilde{B}
of
irreducible elements
, i.e. elements having no descendants, is a basis for the LRY skein algebra. Here terminality means we cannot have an infinite sequence
E_{1}\to E_{2}\to E_{3}\to\dots
. Additionally, local confluence means for

, if
D\to E_{1}
and
B\to E_{2}
, then
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
have a common descendant.

To prove terminality it is enough to introduce a complexity function

, such that if
D\to E
, where
D\in\tilde{B}
, then
E
is an
{\mathcal{R}}
-linear combination of diagrams with less complexity. It is easy to check that the following function satisfies this requirement:

c(D)=2(\#\text{crossings})+|D|+|\partial D|+\#\text{inversions}+m^{2},

where
|D|
is the number of components, an inversion is a pair of boundary points where a negative state is higher than a positive state, and
m
is the number of
\partial D
which are on circle components of
\partial\mathfrak{S}
.

Now we show that
\rightarrow
is locally confluent on
\tilde{B}
. Assume
D\rightarrow E_{1}
and

A natural question to ask is what the relations between quantum traces associated to different triangulations are.
The following facts can be proved, and the proofs are almost identical to the ones (for

A map

is balanced if
{\mathbf{n}}(a)+{\mathbf{n}}(b)+{\mathbf{n}}(c)
is even for any triangular triple

FACT.
For two ideal triangulations

, there is an algebra isomorphism

\overline{\Theta}_{\Delta\Delta^{\prime}}\colon\mathrm{Fr}({\overline{\mathfrak{X}}}^{\mathrm{bl}}(\mathfrak{S};\Delta^{\prime}{;{\mathcal{R}}}))\to\mathrm{Fr}({\overline{\mathfrak{X}}}^{\mathrm{bl}}(\mathfrak{S};\Delta{;{\mathcal{R}}}))

In the DT pair of pants

with boundary components
b_{1},b_{1},b_{3}
, a curve is
standard

if it is one of the three curves

of Figure
13
or their images under the actions of

Figure 13
.
Standard curves on
{\mathbb{P}}_{3}
.

For
i=1,2,3
, the arc
a_{ii}
will be called a
standard return arc

In the usual definition of

FKBL19

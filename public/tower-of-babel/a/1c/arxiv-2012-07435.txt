L p L^{p} -estimates for the Hessians of solutions to fully nonlinear parabolic equations with oblique boundary conditions
Sun-Sig Byun, Jeongmin Han

Abstract
. We study fully nonlinear parabolic equations in nondivergence form with oblique boundary conditions. An optimal and global Calderón-Zygmund estimate is obtained by proving that the Hessian of the viscosity solution to the oblique boundary problem is as integrable as the nonhomogeneous term in L p L^{p} spaces under minimal regularity requirement on the nonlinear operator, the boundary data and the boundary of the domain.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the following parabolic oblique boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x,t)-u_{t}=f&\textrm{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$,}\\
\beta\cdot Du=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$,}\\
u(\cdot,0)=0&\textrm{in $\Omega$,}\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with
n\geq 2
and

T>0
.
Here,

F:S(n)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
is uniformly elliptic with constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
,
i.e.

\lambda||N||\leq F(M+N,q,r,x,t)-F(M,q,r,x,t)\leq\Lambda||N||

for any
M,N\in S(n)
,
N\geq 0
,
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
,
and
\beta:\partial\Omega\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given function with
||\beta||_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega\times(0,T))}\leq 1
.
Under regularity assumptions on
\Omega,F
and
\beta
, we prove global
W^{2,p}
-estimates for viscosity solutions of (
1
).

This work is a natural outgrowth of the recent paper
[
BH20
]
where
W^{2,p}
-regularity is proved for the fully nonlinear elliptic problem with oblique boundary conditions.
We point out that oblique boundary conditions give some information
but not specific value of the solution
u
at the lateral boundary
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
.
Thus, we have to solve the equation not only inside, but also at the boundary of the domain
\Omega\times(0,T)
.
This is the main difference between our problem and the Dirichlet problem.

To sketch the proof, first we need to establish
W^{2,p}
-regularity theory in a domain with flat boundary.
At the beginning, we first obtain a
W^{2,p}
-estimate in the case of
F=F(X,x,t)
with a small BMO assumption.
And next we generalize this result to the case
F=F(X,q,r,x,t)
.
Here we only consider
W^{1,p}
-regularity for
u
since this regularity and the structure condition (
2.3
) for
F
yield the desired regularity result in the flat domain.
Then we can get the desired results by employing boundary regularity results of Dirichlet problems.
Finally, we can obtain global regularity results by using flattening and covering argument.
Throughout our proof, regularity results for limiting PDE (
4
), which can be found in
[
CM
]
, play an essential role.
We compare our problem (
4
) with (
4
) that enjoys
C^{1,1}
-regularity up to the boundary as in
[
CM
]
, by employing the small BMO assumption in order to derive the desired comparison estimates.
In the process, we need to impose our boundary condition that
\partial\Omega\in C^{3}
, ensuring that
\beta\in C^{2}
. (See Remark
4.9
)

The notion of viscosity solutions introduced by Evans
[
Eva80
]
and named by Lions and Crandall
[
CL83
]
presents a new paradigm for studying PDEs.
In particular, this has led to a significant progress in researches on PDEs in nondivergence form.
The fundamental theory of viscosity solutions for fully nonlinear nondivergence equations can be found in
[
Caf89
,
Caf88
,
CIL92
,
Cab95
]
, particularly,
[
Wan92a
,
Wan92b
,
Wan92c
]
in the parabolic case.
Indeed, there have been many various results regarding nondivergence equations, like as in
[
Kry10
,
JP10
,
DKL12
,
Kry13
,
DKM14
,
BLP16
]
, etc.

Researches on the oblique boundary value problems have been actively conducted over the last four decades.
Accordingly, regularity theory for these problems has also been developed.
In elliptic case, we refer to Lieberman’s works
[
Lie87
,
Lie88
]
for linear problems.
For nonlinear equations,
[
LT86
,
Saf95
,
Lie02
]
have discovered regularity theory.
Meanwhile, in parabolic case, there are several results such as
[
GS84
,
Lie90
,
Ura91
,
NU92
]
.
Besides, various results for oblique boundary problems can be found in articles such as
[
DFP96
,
MPV98
,
Sof00
,
IS04
]
, etc.
On the other hand, to derive
W^{2,p}
-estimates for oblique problems,
it is essential to look at the regularity for limiting problems.
Milakis and Silvestre derived
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity for elliptic problems with Neumann boundary data in
[

]
,
and Li and Zhang extended this to the general oblique problems in
[
LZ18
]
.
For parabolic problems, Chatzigeorgiou and Milakis have proved such regularity in
[

]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
We introduce notations and some background about Hessian estimates for viscosity solutions of parabolic equations.
In Section 3, we collect several lemmas employed in the proof of our main result, Theorem
5.5
.
Finally, we derive the desired

-estimates for (
1
) in Sections 4 and 5.

2.2.
Background knowledge

We first look at related concepts to proceed with our discussion.

2.2.1.
Parabolic second order differentiability

To observe second order differentiability, we first need to characterize paraboloids.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M>0
.
A
convex paraboloid
P
with opening
M
is defined by

P(x,t)=a+l\cdot x+\frac{M}{2}(|x|^{2}-t),

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
l\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We also define a
concave paraboloid
by replacing
M
with
-M
in the above definition.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,

U\subset\Omega
be an open subset of
\Omega
,
M>0
, and
u\in C(\Omega)
.
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we use the following notation
U_{s}=\{(x,t)\in U:t\leq s\}
temporarily.
Next we define ‘good set’ and ‘bad set’.
Let
\underline{G}_{M}(u,U)
be the set of points
(x_{0},t_{0})\in U
which satisfy that there is a concave paraboloid
P
with opening
M

such that
P(x_{0},t_{0})=u(x_{0},t_{0})
and
P(x,t)\leq u(x,t)
for any
(x,t)\in U_{t_{0}}
,
and
\underline{A}_{M}(u,U)=U\backslash\underline{G}_{M}(u,U)
.
Analogously, we can define
\overline{G}_{M}(u,U)
and
\overline{A}_{M}(u,U)
by using a convex paraboloid as a barrier. In addition, we denote by

G_{M}(u,U)=\underline{G}_{M}(u,U)\cap\overline{G}_{M}(u,U),

A_{M}(u,U)=\underline{A}_{M}(u,U)\cup\overline{A}_{M}(u,U).

Roughly speaking,
A_{M}
can be understood to be a set of points with ‘bad Hessian’.
Thus, we need to obtain uniform estimates for its measure to establish
W^{2,p}
-theory,
which will be our main purpose investigated in Section 3 and 4.

2.2.2.
Viscosity solutions

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
\Gamma\subset\partial\Omega
.
Consider the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x,t)-u_{t}=f&\textrm{in $\Omega_{1}=\Omega\times(0,1)$,}\\
\beta\cdot Du=0&\textrm{on $\Gamma_{I}=\Gamma\times I,$}\end{array}\right.

where
I
is a fixed interval in
(0,1)
.

Then a viscosity solution is defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
F
be continuous in all variables and
f\in C(\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{I})
.
A continuous function
u\in C(\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{I})
is called a viscosity solution of (
2.2.2
) if the following conditions hold:

(a)

for all
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{I})
touching
u
by above at
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{I}
,

F(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}),D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0}),x_{0},t_{0})-\varphi_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})\geq f(x_{0},t_{0})

when
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{1}
and
\beta(x_{0},t_{0})\cdot D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})\geq 0
when
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Gamma_{I}
.

(b)

for all
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{I})
touching
u
by below at
(x_{0},,t_{0})\in\Omega_{1}\cup\Gamma_{I}
,

F(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}),D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0}),u(x_{0},t_{0}),x_{0},t_{0})-\varphi_{t}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq f(x_{0},t_{0})

when
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{1}
and
\beta(x_{0},t_{0})\cdot D\varphi(x_{0},t_{0})\leq 0
when
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Gamma_{I}
.

Note that if a function

satisfies the condition (a) ((b), respectively) in the above definition, we mean that

F(D^{2}u,Du,u,x,t)-u_{t}\geq(\leq)f
in the viscosity sense.
We also remark that viscosity solutions can be defined by using test functions
\varphi
in
W^{2,p}
space.
(See
[
Wan92a
]
)

To talk about viscosity solutions, it is indispensable to introduce the following.

Definition 2.3

For any

\mathcal{M}^{+}(\lambda,\Lambda,M)=\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}\ \textrm{and}\ \mathcal{M}^{-}(\lambda,\Lambda,M)=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}>0}e_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}

Therefore, we see that the first condition is satisfied if we set

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{2}\mu^{k(1+\alpha)}.

For (iv), we first observe that

\displaystyle(\beta_{k+1}\cdot b_{k+1,s})(x,t)=\beta_{k}(\mu x,\mu^{2}t)\cdot(b_{k,s}+\mu^{k\alpha}Dh(0,0))

\displaystyle||\beta_{k}(\mu x,\mu^{2}t)\cdot(Dh(0,0)-Dh(\mu x,\mu^{2}t))||_{C^{0,\overline{\alpha}}(Q_{1}\cap\{x_{n}=-\frac{\nu}{\mu^{k+1}}\})}

\displaystyle\leq(2\mu)^{\overline{\alpha}}\big\{2||h||_{C^{1,\overline{\alpha}}(\overline{Q}_{\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{\nu}{\mu^{k}}})}+||h||_{L^{\infty}(\overline{Q}_{\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{\nu}{\mu^{k}}})}||\beta||_{C^{0,\overline{\alpha}}(Q_{1}^{\ast})}\big\}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{k}-\tilde{l}\in S^{\ast}\big(\lambda/n,\Lambda,b_{k},f_{k}+g_{k}+\frac{\delta}{8}\big)&\textrm{in $Q_{1}^{\frac{\nu}{\mu^{k}}}$,}\\

\displaystyle|(\tilde{u}-l_{k+1,s})(\mu^{k+1}x_{1},\mu^{2(k+1)}t_{1})-(\tilde{u}-l_{k+1,s})(\mu^{k+1}x_{2},\mu^{2(k+1)}t_{2})|

Hence, we can always find a linear function

Proof.

First, we get the following interior

-estimate

for any

, Theorem 5.9]

Thus, it is sufficient to consider the boundary case.

We are going to use a flattening argument in order to get a boundary estimate.
For any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, we can find a neighborhood
N(x_{0})
of
x_{0}
and a
C^{3}
-diffeomorphism
\Psi:U(x_{0})\to B_{1}^{+}

with
\Psi(x_{0})=0

is

.
Then for each

\Psi_{t_{0}}(x,t)=(\Psi(x),t-t_{0}).

Fix

and let
\tilde{u}=u\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1}

Then
\tilde{u}
is a solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{F}(D^{2}\tilde{u},D\tilde{u},\tilde{u},x,t)-\tilde{u}_{t}=\tilde{f}&\textrm{in $Q_{1}^{+}$,}\\
\tilde{\beta}\cdot D\tilde{u}=0&\textrm{on $Q_{1}^{\ast}$}\\
\end{array}\right.

in the viscosity sense,
where

\tilde{\beta}=(\beta\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1})\cdot(D\Psi_{t_{0}}\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1})^{t}

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(D^{2}\tilde{\varphi},

\displaystyle D\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{u},x,t)=F(D^{2}\varphi,D\varphi,u,x,t)\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=F(D\Psi_{t_{0}}^{T}\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1}D^{2}\tilde{\varphi}D\Psi_{t_{0}}\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1}+(D\tilde{\varphi}\partial_{i,j}\Psi_{t_{0}}\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq n},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad D\varphi D\Psi_{t_{0}}\circ\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1},\tilde{u},\Psi_{t_{0}}^{-1}(x,t))

Here, we also note that we extended

Now we can see that there exists a uniform constant

.
Therefore, we can obtain the boundary estimate, thanks to Corollary
5.4

This completes the proof.

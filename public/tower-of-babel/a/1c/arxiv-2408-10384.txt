Empirical risk minimization for risk-neutral composite optimal control with applications to bang-bang control
Johannes Milz, Daniel Walter

Abstract
Nonsmooth composite optimization problems under uncertainty are prevalent in various scientific and engineering applications. We consider risk-neutral composite optimal control problems, where the objective function is the sum of a potentially nonconvex expectation function and a nonsmooth convex function. To approximate the risk-neutral optimization problems, we use a Monte Carlo sample-based approach, study its asymptotic consistency, and derive nonasymptotic sample size estimates. Our analyses leverage problem structure commonly encountered in PDE-constrained optimization problems, including compact embeddings and growth conditions. We apply our findings to bang-bang-type optimal control problems and propose the use of a conditional gradient method to solve them effectively. We present numerical illustrations.

Introduction

We consider composite risk-neutral optimization problems of the form

\min_{u\in U}\,G(u)\coloneqq F(Bu)+\psi(u),\quad\text{where}\quad F(w)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[f(w,\boldsymbol{\xi})],

(P)

and
B\colon U\to W
is a compact linear operator
between the Hilbert space
U
and
the Banach space
W
. Moreover,
f\colon W\times\Xi\to[0,\infty)

is a sufficiently smooth integrand, and
\psi:U\to[0,\infty]
is a proper, closed, and convex but potentially nonsmooth function. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is a random element taking values in a complete, separable metric
space
\Xi
. While (
P
) covers a variety of challenging settings, the focus of the present work lies on risk-neutral partial differential equation (PDE)-constrained optimal control problems

\displaystyle\min_{u\in L^{2}(D)}\,\mathbb{E}[J(S(Bu,\boldsymbol{\xi}))]+\psi(u),

(1.1)

where
J
is a tracking-type functional,
and the choice of the deterministic control
u
influences the behavior of the solution
S(Bu,\boldsymbol{\xi})
to a PDE with random inputs
\boldsymbol{\xi}
.

Nonconvex optimization problems governed by differential equations
arise in a multitude of application areas, such as
sensor placement
[
2
]
, resource assessment of renewable tidal energy
[
10
]
,
and design of groundwater remediation systems
[
13
]
. It is well known that the proper choice of the penalty function
\psi
in Problem (
1.1
) promotes certain structural features in its minimizers
u^{*}
. For example, setting

\displaystyle\psi(u)=I_{U_{\text{ad}}}(u)+\beta\|u\|_{L^{1}(D)},\quad\text{with}\quad U_{\text{ad}}\coloneqq\{\,u\in L^{2}(D)\;|\;-1\leq u(x)\leq 1\quad\text{for a.e.}~x\in D\,\},

where

I_{U_{\text{ad}}}
is the indicator function of
U_{\text{ad}}
, and
\beta>0
, tends to provide solutions satisfying a
bang-bang-off
principle, that is,
u^{*}(x)\in\{-1,0,1\}
for a.e.
x\in D
.
Hence, composite optimal control problems also arise from relaxations of mixed-integer
optimal control problems
[
10
]
.

To handle the expected value in (
P
) numerically, we approximate it using the
sample average approximation (SAA) approach
[
19
]
.
More specifically,
given a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{1},\boldsymbol{\xi}^{2},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\xi}^{N},\ldots
defined on a complete probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)

of independent
\Xi

such that each
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{i}
has the same distribution as
\boldsymbol{\xi}

also known as the empirical risk minimization problem,

\min_{u\in U}\,\hat{F}_{N}(Bu,\omega)+\psi(u),\quad\text{where}\quad\hat{F}_{N}(w,\omega)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(w,\boldsymbol{\xi}^{i}(\omega)).

(P
N
)

For brevity, we often omit the second argument
of
\hat{F}_{N}
.

A central topic in this context is whether this type of approximation is asymptotically consistent and whether the proximity of its solutions and critical points to their counterparts in (
P
) can be quantified in terms of the sample size
N
.
Although this already represents a formidable problem for sufficiently smooth objective functions, the potential lack of strong convexity of
\psi
aggravates the problem further, adding additional challenges to the theoretical analysis of the problem and its efficient solution.

1.1
Contributions

In the present paper, we investigate the SAA approach for nonsmooth, potentially nonconvex risk-neutral minimization problems of the form (
P
) from a qualitative and a quantitative perspective. Since Problem (
P
) is potentially nonconvex, our analysis addresses the behavior of its solutions and critical points. The latter refers to points
u^{*}_{N}\in U
satisfying the first-order necessary subdifferential inclusion

\displaystyle-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*}_{N})\in\partial\psi(u^{*}_{N})

or, equivalently, which are zeros of the
SAA gap functional

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{N}(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{v\in\mathcal{D}(\psi)}\,[\,(B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu),u-v)_{U}+\psi(u)-\psi(v)\,],

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{D}(\psi)
is the domain of
\psi
.
Critical points as well as the associated gap function
\Psi
for Problem (
P
) are defined analogously.
More specifically, the contributions of the paper are as follows:

(a)

For sufficiently smooth functions
F
, we show that the optimal values, solutions, and critical points of Problem (
P

N

) converge asymptotically to their deterministic counterparts of Problem (
P
) with probability one (abbreviated as “w.p.
1
”) as the sample size
N
tends to infinity.

(b)

We establish nonasymptotic expectation bounds for the gap functional evaluated at sequences
(u_{N})\subset U
of random vectors. More precisely, we show that, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Psi(u_{N})]\leq\mathbb{E}[\hat{\Psi}_{N}(u_{N})]+\varepsilon\quad\text{if}\quad N\geq\frac{c_{1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\cdot\ln\big(\mathcal{N}(\varepsilon/c_{1};B(\mathcal{D}(\psi)))\big),

where
\mathcal{N}(\nu;B(\mathcal{D}(\psi)))
with
\nu>0

denotes the
\nu
-covering number of the compact set
B(\mathcal{D}(\psi))\subset W
, and
c_{1}>0
is a constant that explicitly depends on the
radius of the domain of
\psi
as well as the integrand
f
.

(c)

While Problem (
P
) is typically not strongly convex, we provide nonasymptotic expectation bounds for the approximation of minimizers
u^{*}
to (
P
) by solutions to Problem (
P

N

) if the integrand is convex for
each
\xi\in\Xi
,
and a growth-type condition on the partial linearization of
G
at a minimizer
u^{*}

holds true. Specifically, if

\displaystyle(B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*}),u-u^{*})_{U}+\psi(u)-\psi(u^{*})\geq\theta\|u-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in U

(1.3)

for some
\theta>0
and
U
continuously embeds into the Banach space
\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|u_{N}^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\Psi(u^{*}_{N})]+\mathbb{E}[G(u^{*}_{N})-G(u^{*})]\leq(1/N)c_{2},

where
c_{2}>0

the integrand, and
\theta
.
Hence, the SAA solutions converge in expectation at the usual Monte Carlo rate while both the value of the gap functional and the suboptimality in
G
exhibit superconvergence at a rate of
(1/N)
. This convergence statement is valid for linear bounded operators

B

Our framework is applied to PDE-constrained optimization problems governed by affine-linear and bilinear elliptic equations, which allow for the use of bang-bang-off regularization terms. Reproducible numerical experiments empirically verify our theoretical results and further highlight the utility of the SAA approach for infinite-dimensional, nonsmooth problems.

3
Risk-neutral composite optimal control

We start by stating the main assumptions on Problem (
P
).
We recall that
the domain of
\psi
is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\psi)\coloneqq\{\,u\in U\;|\;\psi(u)<\infty\,\}.

The following assumption imposes the properties of
\psi
and the smoothness and integrability requirements on the integrand.
In the following two assumptions, all integrability conditions are understood with respect to the distribution of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
.

Assumption 1

(Integrand and feasible set)
.

(a)

The control space
U
is a separable Hilbert space, and

W
is a separable Banach space.

(b)

The operator

B:U\to W
is linear and compact.

(c)

The function
\psi:U\to[0,\infty]

is proper, closed, and convex, and
\mathcal{D}(\psi)
is bounded.

(d)

The set
U_{0}\subset U

is open, convex, and bounded
with
\mathcal{D}(\psi)\subset U_{0}
.
Moreover,

f\colon B(U_{0})\times\Xi\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous
in its first argument
on
B(\mathcal{D}(\psi))

for each
\xi\in\Xi

and measurable in its second
argument on
\Xi
for each
w\in B(U_{0})
.

(e)

For an integrable random variable

\zeta_{f}:\Xi\to[0,\infty)
, it holds that

\displaystyle|f(Bu,\xi)|\leq\zeta_{f}(\xi)\quad\text{for all}\quad(u,\xi)\in U_{0}\times\Xi.

The following assumption formulates
differentiability and integrability statements.

Assumption 2

(Integrand and its gradient)
.

(a)

For each
\xi\in\Xi
, the mapping

\displaystyle g_{\xi}\colon U_{0}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\text{where}\quad g_{\xi}(u)\coloneqq f(Bu,\xi),

is continuously differentiable.

(b)

There exists a Carathéodory function
\mathrm{D}_{w}f\colon B(\mathcal{D}(\psi))\times\Xi\to W^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\nabla g_{\xi}(u)=B^{*}\mathrm{D}_{w}f(Bu,\xi)\quad\text{for all}\quad(u,\xi)\in\mathcal{D}(\psi)\times\Xi.

(c)

There exists an integrable random variable

\zeta_{\mathrm{D}f}:\Xi\to[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{\xi}(u)\|_{U}

\displaystyle\leq\zeta_{\mathrm{D}f}(\xi)\quad\text{for all}\quad(u,\xi)\in U_{0}\times\Xi,\quad\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}_{w}f(Bu,\xi)\|_{W^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\zeta_{\mathrm{D}f}(\xi)\quad\text{for all}\quad(u,\xi)\in\mathcal{D}(\psi)\times\Xi.

If
f(\cdot,\xi)
is continuously differentiable on a
W
-neighborhood of
B(\mathcal{D}(\psi))
, the differentiability of
g_{\xi}(\cdot)=f(B\cdot,\xi)
and the gradient representation are implied by the chain rule. From this perspective,

Assumption

2
ensures that the gradients of
F\circ B
and
\hat{F}_{N}\circ B
have composite structure, even when the chain rule does not apply. While this might seem technical at first glance, it allows us to fit challenging settings into the outlined abstract framework. For a particular example, we refer to the bilinear PDE-constrained problem in
Section

4.2
.

3.2
Consistency of SAA optimal values and solutions

We establish the asymptotic consistency
of SAA optimal values and
the weak consistency of SAA solutions.
We denote by
\vartheta^{*}
the optimal value
of the true problem (
P
).

Theorem 3.3
.

If
Assumption

1
holds, then

(i)

\hat{\vartheta}_{N}^{*}\to\vartheta^{*}
as
N\to\infty

w.p.
1
, and

(ii)

for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,
(u^{*}_{N}(\omega))
has
at least one weak accumulation point and each

(

Proof.

Before we establish parts (i) and (ii), let us note that the uniform

[
24

ensures the
existence of a null set

\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega

\Omega_{0}\in\mathcal{F}

such that for all

\displaystyle\sup_{w\in B(\mathcal{D}(\psi))}|F(w)-\hat{F}_{N}(w,\omega)|\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad N\to\infty.

(3.2)

Moreover,

F
is continuous on

to the dominated convergence theorem.

(i)
We have for all

\displaystyle|\hat{\vartheta}_{N}^{*}(\omega)-\vartheta^{*}|\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{D}(\psi)}|F(Bu)+\psi(u)-\big(\hat{F}_{N}(Bu,\omega)+\psi(u)\big)|\leq\sup_{w\in B(\mathcal{D}(\psi))}|F(w)-\hat{F}_{N}(w,\omega)|.

Combined with (

as

for all
\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
.

(ii)
Fix
\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
.
Since
(u_{N}^{*}(\omega))

is bounded,
it has a weak accumulation point.
Let
\bar{u}(\omega)\in U
be such a point.
Hence, there exists a subsequence

(u_{N}^{*}(\omega))_{\mathcal{M}(\omega)}
of

(u_{N}^{*}(\omega))
such that

u_{N}^{*}(\omega)\rightharpoonup\bar{u}(\omega)
as
\mathcal{M}(\omega)\ni N\to\infty
.
Here,
\mathcal{M}(\omega)\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is an infinite subset of natural numbers, potentially depending on
\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
.
Fix

. We have

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}_{N}^{*}(\omega)=\hat{F}_{N}(Bu_{N}^{*}(\omega),\omega)+\psi(u_{N}^{*}(\omega))\leq\hat{F}_{N}(Bu,\omega)+\psi(u).

Using
the uniform convergence statement in
(

the continuity of

,
and

Combined with the fact that

\displaystyle F(B\bar{u}(\omega))+\psi(\bar{u}(\omega))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\mathcal{M}(\omega)\ni N\to\infty}\{\,\hat{F}_{N}(Bu_{N}^{*}(\omega))+\psi(u_{N}^{*}(\omega))\,\}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\mathcal{M}(\omega)\ni N\to\infty}\,\hat{F}_{N}(Bu)+\psi(u)=F(Bu)+\psi(u).

Since

Consistency of SAA critical points

We establish the asymptotic consistency of the SAA gap functional

Theorem 3.4

3.5
Expectation bounds for convex problems

For convex problems, we demonstrate nonasymptotic expectation bounds
for the distance between SAA solutions and the true solution, the gap functional, and the optimality gap. These statements hold true without the compactness of the linear bounded operator
B
. In the following, let
u^{*}
be a solution to (
P
)
and for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{N}^{*}
be a measurable solution to (
P

N

).

Assumption 4

(Hölder-type inequality, gradient regularity, growth, variance)
.

(a)

The space
\mathcal{U}
is
a Banach space with
\mathcal{U}^{*}\hookrightarrow U\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{H}
is a
separable Hilbert space
with
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}^{*}
, and

\displaystyle|(v,u)_{U}|\leq\|v\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}}\quad\text{for all}\quad v\in\mathcal{U}^{*},\quad u\in U.

(b)

For two constants
\theta\in(0,\infty)

and
\varrho\in[2,\infty)
, it holds that

\displaystyle(B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*}),u-u^{*})_{U}+\psi(u)-\psi(u^{*})\geq\theta\|u-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{\varrho}\quad\text{for all}\quad u\in U.

(c)

The mapping
\Xi\ni\xi\mapsto\nabla g_{\xi}(u^{*})\in\mathcal{H}

is measurable,
\nabla g_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(u^{*})
is integrable,
and the standard deviation-type constant

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{D}f}\coloneqq(\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla g_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(u^{*})-\mathbb{E}[\nabla g_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(u^{*})]\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}])^{1/2}

is finite.

4 (b)

and its variants have been utilized, for example,
in
[
22
]
and, in particular,
ensures that

u^{*}
is the unique solution to
(
P
).

We state the section’s main result.
If
4 (a)
holds true,
let
C_{\mathcal{H};\mathcal{U}^{*}}\in(0,\infty)
be the embedding
constant of the embedding
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}^{*}
.
If
\varrho=2
in
Assumption

4
,
the following result ensures the
typical Monte Carlo convergence rate,
1/\sqrt{N}
,
for the expected error
\mathbb{E}[\|u_{N}^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
Assumptions

1
,
2
and
4
hold,
and
f(\cdot,\xi)
is convex for all
\xi\in\Xi
, then
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|u_{N}^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2(\varrho-1)}]\leq(1/N)C_{\mathcal{H};\mathcal{U}^{*}}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{D}f}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by those of
Lemma 6 and Theorem 3 in
[
32
]
.
Using the optimality condition

-B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*})\in\partial\psi(u^{*})

and
4 (b)
, we obtain

\displaystyle(B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu_{N}^{*}),u_{N}^{*}-u^{*})_{U}\geq\theta\|u_{N}^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{\varrho}.

Since

is continuously differentiable
on
U_{0}
(see
Proposition

3.1
),
and convex,

\displaystyle(B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu_{N}^{*})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*}),u_{N}^{*}-u^{*})_{U}\geq 0.

Adding both estimates ensures

\displaystyle(B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*}),u_{N}^{*}-u^{*})_{U}\geq\theta\|u_{N}^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{\varrho}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*})\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}\geq\theta\|u_{N}^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{\varrho-1}.

Taking squares and
using the continuity of the embedding

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{H};\mathcal{U}^{*}}^{2}\|B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*})\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\geq\theta^{2}\|u_{N}^{*}-u^{*}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2(\varrho-1)}.

3.1
),

are independent and identically distributed,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*})\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]=(1/N)\sigma_{\mathrm{D}f}^{2}.

(3.6)

This implies the expectation bound.
∎

Using this result, we further deduce superconvergence properties of the objective function values and the gap functional.
We recall that
G
denotes the objective
function of (
P
).

Corollary 3.8
.

Let the hypotheses of
Theorem

3.7
hold with
\varrho=2
, and suppose that there exists

L_{\mathrm{D}f,\mathcal{U}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{\xi}(u_{2})-\nabla g_{\xi}(u_{1})\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}\leq L_{\mathrm{D}f,\mathcal{U}}\|u_{2}-u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{U}}\quad\text{for all}\quad u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(\psi),\quad\xi\in\Xi.

(3.7)

Then, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G(u_{N}^{*})-G(u^{*})]\leq\mathbb{E}[\Psi(u^{*}_{N})]\leq\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{L_{\mathrm{D}f,\mathcal{U}}}{\theta^{2}}\bigg)^{2}+\frac{4L_{\mathrm{D}f,\mathcal{U}}^{2}}{\theta^{2}}\bigg)\frac{C_{\mathcal{H};\mathcal{U}^{*}}^{2}\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{D}f}}{N}.

Since

for all

(see, for example,

), it suffices to show the second estimate. For this purpose, note that there exists

\displaystyle\Psi(u^{*}_{N})

\displaystyle=(B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*}_{N}),u^{*}_{N}-v_{N})_{U}+\psi(u^{*}_{N})-\psi(v_{N})

\displaystyle\leq(1/2)\|B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*}_{N})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*}_{N})\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}^{2}+(1/2)\|u^{*}_{N}-v_{N}\|_{\mathcal{U}}^{2},

. Using (

\displaystyle\|B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*}_{N})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*}_{N})\|_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\mathcal{H};\mathcal{U}^{*}}\|B^{*}\mathrm{D}F(Bu^{*})-B^{*}\mathrm{D}\hat{F}_{N}(Bu^{*})\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

∎

4
Application to bang-bang optimal control under uncertainty

We apply our results to risk-neutral, nonsmooth PDE-constrained minimization problems of the form

\displaystyle\min_{u\in L^{2}(D)}\,\mathbb{E}[J(\imath y(\boldsymbol{\xi}))]+\beta\|u\|_{L^{1}(D)}\,\quad\text{s.t.}\quad u\in[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u}],

(4.1)

where
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{1,2,3\}
, is a bounded, polyhedral domain,
\beta\geq 0
,
J
is a sufficiently smooth fidelity term defined on
L^{2}(D)
and
\imath\colon H^{1}_{0}(D)\to L^{2}(D)
denotes the compact embedding. Moreover,

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u}]\coloneqq\{\,v\in L^{2}(D)\;|\;\mathfrak{l}(x)\leq v(x)\leq\mathfrak{u}(x)\text{ for a.e. }x\in D\,\},

where
\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u}\in L^{\infty}(D)
with
\mathfrak{l}(x)\leq 0\leq\mathfrak{u}(x)
for a.e.
x\in D
.
The deterministic control

u\in[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u}]

is coupled with the random state variable
y(\xi)\in H^{1}_{0}(D)
via a parametrized elliptic PDE

\displaystyle(\kappa(\xi)\nabla y,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(D)^{d}}+(E(u,y),v)_{L^{2}(D)}=(b(\xi),v)_{L^{2}(D)}\quad\text{for all}\quad v\in H_{0}^{1}(D),

(4.2)

which the state satisfies for every
\xi\in\Xi
.
Throughout this section, we consider

\displaystyle U=L^{2}(D),\quad W=H^{-1}(D),\quad\mathcal{H}=H^{2}(D),\quad\mathcal{U}=L^{1}(D),\quad\text{and}\quad B=\imath^{*}.

In the following, we demonstrate that both linear control problems, where
E(u,y)=-u
, and bilinear control problems, where
E(u,y)=uy
, can be incorporated into the theoretical framework defined by (
P
). This is achieved by defining the penalty term as

\psi(u)=\beta\|u\|_{L^{1}(D)}+I_{[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u}]}(u),

and by introducing the integrand

\displaystyle f(w,\xi)\coloneqq J(B^{*}S(w,\xi)),\quad\text{where}\quad S\colon B(U_{0})\times\Xi\to H^{1}_{0}(D).

(4.3)

Here,
S\colon B(U_{0})\times\Xi\to H^{1}_{0}(D)
is a suitable parametrized control-to-state operator, defined on the image of an
L^{2}(D)
-neighborhood
U_{0}
of the admissible control set
\mathcal{D}(\psi)=[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{u}]
. For both problem classes, the following assumptions are made.

Assumption 5

(Fidelity term, PDE right-hand side, random diffusion coefficient)
.

(a)

The function
J:L^{2}(D)\to[0,\infty)

is convex and continuously differentiable. Its gradient is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz
constant
\ell\in(0,\infty)
.
Moreover

J(v)\leq\varrho(\|v\|_{L^{2}(D)})

for all
v\in L^{2}(D)
,
where

\varrho:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a polynomial and nondecreasing.

(b)

The map
b:\Xi\to L^{2}(D)
is measurable
and there exists
b_{\sup}^{*}>0

such that
\|b(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq b_{\sup}^{*}
for all
\xi\in\Xi
.

(c)

The mapping
\kappa:\Xi\to C^{1}(\bar{D})

is measurable
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\kappa(\boldsymbol{\xi})\|_{C^{1}(\bar{D})}^{p}]<\infty

for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.
Moreover, there exists
\kappa_{\inf}^{*}>0
such that
\kappa(\xi)(x)\geq\kappa_{\inf}^{*}

for all
x\in D
and
\xi\in\Xi
.

We point out that
5 (a)
implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla J(v)\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leq 2\ell J(v)\leq 2\ell\varrho(\|v\|_{L^{2}(D)})\ \quad\text{for all}\quad v\in L^{2}(D).

(4.4)

Moreover,
1 (a)
,
1 (b)
and
1 (c)
are clearly satisfied. For abbreviation, let
C_{D}
be the Friedrichs constant of the domain
D
, which equals the operator norm of
\imath
.

guarantees

-regularity of the objective function’s gradient. This justifies our choice

in

, where

is
required to be a separable Hilbert space.
We recall that this Hilbert space structure yields an identity for the variance
of the difference between the empirical and true gradients, as shown in
(

).

4.1
Risk-neutral affine-linear PDE-constrained optimization

We first consider (
4.1
) governed by a class of affine-linear, parameterized PDEs, that is,
E(u,y)=-u
. For simplicity, set
b=0
. We consider the operator

\displaystyle A\colon C^{1}(\bar{D})\times H^{-1}(D)\times H^{1}_{0}(D)\to H^{-1}(D),

where

\displaystyle\langle A(\kappa,w,y),v\rangle_{H^{-1}(D),H_{0}^{1}(D)}\coloneqq(\kappa\nabla y,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(D)^{d}}-\langle w,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(D),H_{0}^{1}(D)}\quad\text{for all}\quad v\in H_{0}^{1}(D),

and the associated parametrized equation

\displaystyle\text{find}~y\in H^{1}_{0}(D)\colon\quad A(\kappa,w,y)=0,\quad\text{where}\quad(\kappa,w)\in\mathfrak{P}_{\text{ad}},

(4.5)

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{\text{ad}}\coloneqq\big\{\,\kappa\in C^{1}(\bar{D})\;|\;\kappa>\kappa^{*}_{\inf}/2\,\big\}\times H^{-1}(D).

)
a generic element in

. In particular, (

When the random diffusion coefficient from

becomes random.

The next lemma follows from the Lax–Milgram lemma, the implicit function theorem, and standard regularity results for elliptic PDEs. Its proof is omitted for the sake of brevity.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
Assumption

5

.
For every

). The mapping
\mathfrak{P}_{\text{ad}}\ni(\kappa,w)\mapsto y(\kappa,w)

is infinitely many times continuously differentiable, and we have

\displaystyle\|y(\kappa,w)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(D)}\leq(2/\kappa_{\inf}^{*})\|w\|_{H^{-1}(D)}\quad\text{for all}\quad(\kappa,w)\in\mathfrak{P}_{\text{ad}}.

(4.6)

Moreover, if

,
then

\displaystyle\|y(\kappa,Bu)\|_{H^{2}(D)}\leq\frac{C_{H^{2}}\|\kappa\|_{C^{1}(\bar{D})}^{3}}{(\kappa^{*}_{\inf}/2)^{4}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(D)}\quad\text{for all}\quad(\kappa,Bu)\in\mathfrak{P}_{\text{ad}},\quad u\in L^{2}(D).

(4.7)

Finally, the mapping

\displaystyle\big\{\,\kappa\in C^{1}(\bar{D})\;|\;\kappa>\kappa^{*}_{\inf}/2\,\big\}\times L^{2}(D)\ni(\kappa,u)\mapsto y(\kappa,Bu)\in H^{1}_{0}(D)\cap H^{2}(D)

In the following, let

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{H^{2};H_{0}^{1}}C_{D}(4/\kappa_{\inf}^{*})\frac{C_{H^{2}}\|\kappa(\xi)\|_{C^{1}(\bar{D})}^{3}}{(\kappa^{*}_{\inf}/2)^{4}}\big(\|\mathfrak{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}C^{2}_{D}+1\big)C_{\mathrm{sq}}\|b(\xi)\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

In particular, owing to
Assumption

5
, (
4.11
) and (
4.13
), there exists a square integrable random variable
\zeta_{\text{max}}\colon\Xi\to[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{\xi}(u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq\zeta_{\text{max}}(\xi)\quad\text{for all}\quad(u,\xi)\in U_{0}\times\Xi,

as well as

\displaystyle\max\big\{\|\mathrm{D}_{w}f(Bu,\xi)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(D)},\|S(Bu,\xi)\|_{H^{2}(D)},\|p_{u,\xi}\|_{H^{2}(D)}\big\}\leq\zeta_{\text{max}}(\xi)\;\text{for all}\;(u,\xi)\in\mathcal{D}(\psi)\times\Xi.

To establish the claimed Lipschitz continuity, fix
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(\psi)
and
\xi\in\Xi
, and split

\displaystyle p_{u_{1},\xi}-p_{u_{2},\xi}=p_{u_{1},\xi}-y(\kappa(\xi),\nabla J(B^{*}S(Bu_{2},\xi)),u_{1})+y(\kappa(\xi),\nabla J(B^{*}S(Bu_{2},\xi)),u_{1})-p_{u_{2},\xi}.

For estimating the norm of the first difference, we note that

\displaystyle\|\nabla J(B^{*}S(Bu_{1},\xi))-\nabla J(B^{*}S(Bu_{2},\xi))\|_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\ell C_{D}\|S(Bu_{1},\xi)-S(Bu_{2},\xi)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\ell C_{D}C_{H^{2};H_{0}^{1}}\zeta_{\text{max}}(\xi)}{\kappa_{\inf}^{*}}\|Bu_{1}-Bu_{2}\|_{H^{-1}(D)}

using
Lemma

4.8
. Similarly, arguing along the lines of proof in
Lemma

4.8
, we get

\displaystyle\|y(\kappa(\xi),\nabla J(B^{*}S(Bu_{2},\xi)),u_{1})-p_{u_{2},\xi}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(D)}\leq C_{H^{2};H_{0}^{1}}(4/\kappa_{\inf}^{*})\|Bu_{1}-Bu_{2}\|_{H^{-1}(D)}\zeta_{\text{max}}(\xi).

As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\|p_{u_{1}}-p_{u_{2}}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(D)}\leq C_{H^{2};H_{0}^{1}}(4/\kappa_{\inf}^{*})\zeta_{\text{max}}(\xi)\big(1+\ell C^{2}_{D}(4/\kappa_{\inf}^{*})\big)\|Bu_{1}-Bu_{2}\|_{H^{-1}(D)}.

Finally, we decompose
\nabla g_{\xi}(u_{1})-\nabla g_{\xi}(u_{2})
and estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{\xi}(u_{1})-\nabla g_{\xi}(u_{2})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\|p_{u_{1}}-p_{u_{2}}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(D)}+\|S(Bu_{1},\xi)-S(Bu_{2},\xi)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(D)}\big)\zeta_{\text{max}}(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq C\zeta_{\text{max}}(\xi)^{2}\|Bu_{1}-Bu_{2}\|_{H^{-1}(D)}

for some
C>0
. By Friedrichs’ inequality, the claimed Lipschitz statement follows.
∎

6
Discussion

We have analyzed the SAA approach for risk-neutral optimization problems with nonsmooth but convex regularization terms. Our main results address the asymptotic consistency of this scheme, and the derivation of nonasymptotic sample size estimates for various optimality measures. The latter, as well as the employed techniques, come in two different flavors: for the general nonconvex case, we provide estimates on the expected value of the gap functional by applying the covering number approach. For convex objectives, and by relying on common growth conditions, we prove stronger results including convergence rates for the expected distance between minimizers, improved estimates for the gap functional, and the suboptimality in the objective function value. The presented abstract framework is applied to both linear and bilinear PDE-constrained problems under uncertainty. We also use these applications to empirically demonstrate the sharpness of our convergence guarantees for the convex case.

Our investigation also raises new questions about the SAA approach for nonsmooth minimization. These include, for example, the extension of the results in
Section

3.5
to nonconvex problems assuming suitable second-order optimality conditions and taking into account the potential existence of multiple global and/or local minimizers. Moreover, an extension of our results to risk-averse stochastic optimization and variational inequalities may be of interest.

Appendix A
Uniform expectation bounds for expectation mappings

We establish essentially known uniform expectation bounds for integrands defined on potentially infinite-dimensional
spaces.
While the techniques used in this section are
standard, the bounds are instrumental for
establishing one of our main results in
Section

3.4
.

Let
(\Theta,\Sigma,\mu)
be a complete probability space,
and let
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
be a random element with image space
\Theta
.
Moreover, let
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{1},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{2},\ldots
be a sequence of independent
\Theta
-valued random elements
defined on a complete probability space, each
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{i}
having the same distribution as
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
.

Assumption 6

(sub-Gaussian Carathéodory function, compact linear operator)
.

(a)

The set
V_{0}
is a nonempty, closed,
bounded, convex
subset of a reflexive Banach space

V
,
W
is a Banach space, and
B:V\to W
is linear and compact.

(b)

The space
H
is a separable Hilbert space,
and
\mathsf{G}:B(V_{0})\times\Theta\to H

is Carathéodory.

(c)

For each
w\in B(V_{0})
,

\mathsf{G}(w,\cdot)
is integrable, and for an integrable random variable
L_{\mathsf{G}}:\Theta\to[0,\infty)
,

\mathsf{G}(\cdot,\zeta)
is Lipschitz continuous
with Lipschitz constant
L_{\mathsf{G}}(\zeta)

for each
\zeta\in\Theta
.

(d)

There exists
\tau_{\mathsf{G}}>0
such that for all
w\in B(V_{0})
,

\mathbb{E}[\exp(\tau_{\mathsf{G}}^{-2}\|\mathsf{G}(w,\boldsymbol{\zeta})-\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{G}(w,\boldsymbol{\zeta})]\|_{H}^{2})]\leq\mathrm{e}
.

We define
g(w)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{G}(w,\boldsymbol{\zeta})]
,

\hat{g}_{N}(w)\coloneqq(1/N)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsf{G}(w,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{i})
, and
L\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[L_{\mathsf{G}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})]
.

Proposition A.1
.

If
Assumption

6
holds, then
for each
\nu>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\sup_{v\in V_{0}}\|g(Bv)-\hat{g}_{N}(Bv)\|_{H}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\sup_{w\in B(V_{0})}\|g(w)-\hat{g}_{N}(w)\|_{H}]

\displaystyle\leq 2L\nu+\tfrac{\sqrt{3}\tau_{\mathsf{G}}}{\sqrt{N}}\big(\ln(2\mathcal{N}(\nu;B(V_{0})))\big)^{1/2}.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by those
of Theorems 9.84 and 9.86 in
[
48
]
.
We have

\|g(w_{2})-g(w_{1})\|_{H}\leq L\|w_{2}-w_{1}\|_{W}

and

\|\hat{g}_{N}(w_{2})-\hat{g}_{N}(w_{1})\|_{H}\leq\hat{L}_{N}\|w_{2}-w_{1}\|_{W}

for all
w_{1},w_{2}\in B(V_{0})
,
where
\hat{L}_{N}\coloneqq(1/N)\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{\mathsf{G}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{i})
.
We define
K\coloneqq\mathcal{N}(\nu;B(V_{0}))
.
Since
B(V_{0})
is compact,

K
is finite and there exist
w_{1},\ldots,w_{K}\in B(V_{0})

such that for each
w\in B(V_{0})
,
we have
\|w-w_{k(w)}\|_{W}\leq\nu
,
where
k(w)\coloneqq\mathrm{\arg\,\min}_{1\leq k\leq K}\,\|w-w_{k}\|_{W}
.
For all
w\in B(V_{0})
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\hat{g}_{N}(w)-g(w)\|_{H}&\leq\|\hat{g}_{N}(w)-\hat{g}_{N}(w_{k(w)})\|_{H}+\|\hat{g}_{N}(w_{k(w)})-g(w_{k(w)})\|_{H}\\
&\quad+\|g(w_{k(w)})-g(w)\|_{H}\\
&\leq\hat{L}_{N}\nu+\|\hat{g}_{N}(w_{k(w)})-g(w_{k(w)})\|_{H}+L\nu.\end{aligned}

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in V_{0}}\|g(Bv)-\hat{g}_{N}(Bv)\|_{H}

\displaystyle=\sup_{w\in B(V_{0})}\|\hat{g}_{N}(w)-g(w)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{L}_{N}\nu+\max_{1\leq k\leq K}\,\|\hat{g}_{N}(w_{k})-g(w_{k})\|_{H}+L\nu.

Using Theorem 3 in
[
44
]

and Lemma 1 in
[
32
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\cosh(\lambda\|\hat{g}_{N}(w_{k})-g(w_{k})\|_{H})]\leq\exp(3\lambda^{2}\tau_{\mathsf{G}}^{2}/(4N))\quad\text{for all}\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\quad k=1,\ldots,K.

Now, Lemma B.5 in
[
37
]
ensures

\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\leq k\leq K}\,\|\hat{g}_{N}(w_{k})-g(w_{k})\|_{H}]\leq\sqrt{3}\tau_{\mathsf{G}}\sqrt{\ln(2K)/N}
.
Combined with
\mathbb{E}[\hat{L}_{N}]=L
, we obtain the expectation bound.
∎

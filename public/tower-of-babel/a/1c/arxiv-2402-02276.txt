Asymptotic analysis for stationary distributions of scaled reaction networks
Linard Hoessly, Carsten Wiuf and Panqiu Xia

Abstract
. We study stationary distributions in the context of stochastic reaction networks. In particular, we are interested in complex balanced reaction networks and reduction of such networks by assuming a set of species (called non-interacting species) are degraded fast (and therefore essentially absent in the network), implying some reaction rates are large compared to others. Technically, we assume these reaction rates are scaled by a common parameter N N and let N → ∞ N\to\infty . The limiting stationary distribution as N → ∞ N\to\infty is compared to the stationary distribution of the reduced reaction network obtained by algebraic elimination of the non-interacting species. In general, the limiting stationary distribution might differ from the stationary distribution of the reduced reaction network. We identify various sufficient conditions for when these two distributions are the same, including when the reaction network is detailed balanced and when the set of non-interacting species consists of intermediate species. In the latter case, the limiting stationary distribution essentially retains the form of the complex balanced distribution. This finding is particularly surprising given that the reduced reaction network might be non-weakly reversible and exhibit unconventional kinetics.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Let
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be the set of real, non-negative and positive numbers, receptively. Let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}
the set of positive and non-negative integers, receptively, and
\mathbb{Z}
the set of integers.
For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say
x\geq y
, if
x_{i}\geq y_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
; and
x>y
if
x\geq y
and
x\neq y
. Additionally, we define
x\vee y=(\max\{x_{1},y_{1}\},\dots,\max\{x_{n},y_{n}\})
, and
\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
for the inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a finite set
\mathcal{I}
,
|\mathcal{I}|
is the cardinality of
\mathcal{I}
.

2.2.
Reaction networks

A
reaction network
is a triple
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R})
of three finite sets: a set
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}\}
of
species
, a set
\mathcal{C}
of
complexes
, which are non-negative linear combination of the species (complexes are identified as elements of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
), and a set
\mathcal{R}
of
reactions
, which are elements of
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
. One might consider
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R})
as a digraph. We assume all species have a positive coefficient in some complex and all complexes are the source or target of some reaction. If this case,
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{S}
can be determined from
\mathcal{R}
. In examples, we simply draw the reactions.

For simplicity, for a reaction
r\in\mathcal{R}
, we write
r=(y,y^{\prime})
or
r=y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}
, where
y
is the source of
r
and
y^{\prime}
the target. In the chemical literature, these are called the
reactant

\mathrm{reac}(r)=y
and the
product

\mathrm{prod}(r)=y^{\prime}
of
r
, respectively. The
reaction vector
of
r
is
\zeta_{r}=y^{\prime}-y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

We introduce an associative binary operation
[
hoessly2021sum
]
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\oplus

\displaystyle\colon\ \big(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\big)\times\big(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\big)\to\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},

\displaystyle(y_{1},y_{1}^{\prime})\oplus(y_{2},y_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(y_{1}+0\vee(y_{2}-y_{1}^{\prime}),y_{2}^{\prime}+0\vee(y_{1}^{\prime}-y_{2})\big)

for
(y_{1},y_{1}^{\prime}),(y_{2},y_{2}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.
It might be interpreted as the sum of two reactions, for example,

\big((1,1,0),(0,0,1)\big)\oplus\big((0,1,1),(0,1,0)\big)=\big((1,2,0),(0,1,0)\big),

corresponds to the sum (cf.
[
syst_ing
, Section 3.1.2]
),

S_{1}+S_{2}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{3}\quad\oplus\quad S_{2}+S_{3}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{2}\quad=\quad S_{1}+2S_{2}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}S_{2},

The left hand side of the sum consists of the molecules required for the two reactions to proceed in succession, and the right hand side consists of the molecules that are produced and not consumed in the two reactions.

In the following, we assume a reaction network
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{R})
is given.

Definition 2.1
.

A state
x_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

leads to

x_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
via
\mathcal{N}
, denoted by
x_{1}\to_{\mathcal{N}}x_{2}
, if there exists reactions
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\in\mathcal{R}
, such that
r_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus r_{k}=(y,y^{\prime})
with
x_{1}\geq y
and
x_{2}-x_{1}=y^{\prime}-y
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Cappelletti
, Definition 10]
)
.

A set
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{N}
, if the for all
x\in\Gamma
and all
z\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
x\to_{\mathcal{N}}z
if and only if
z\in\Gamma
.

Definition 2.3
.

A reaction network is

i)

reversible
, if
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
implies
y^{\prime}{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y\in\mathcal{R}
.

ii)

weakly reversible
, if for any
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
, there exists a sequence
r_{1},\dots,r_{m}\in\mathcal{R}
, satisfying
\mathrm{reac}(r_{1})=y^{\prime}
,
\mathrm{prod}(r_{i})=\mathrm{reac}(r_{i+1})
for all
i=1,\dots,m-1
and
\mathrm{prod}(r_{m})=y
.

A
stochastic reaction network
(SRN) models the stochastic dynamics of a reaction network. Specifically, the evolution of the molecule counts,
(X(t)\colon t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, is modelled as an
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
-valued CTMC, satisfying the following stochastic equation:

(2.2)

\displaystyle X(t)=X(0)+\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}Y_{r}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{r}(X(s))ds\Big)\zeta_{r},

where
(Y_{r}\colon r\in\mathcal{R})
is a collection of i.i.d. unit rate Poisson processes and
\lambda=(\lambda_{r}\colon r\in\mathcal{R})
is a collection of non-negative intensity functions on
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, known as the
kinetics
.
Stochastic mass-action kinetics
is commonly assumed in the literature; however, it is not a prerequisite for the majority of this paper’s content. For any reaction
r=y_{r}\to y_{r}^{\prime}
, the intensity function under mass-action kinetics can be written as the following form

\lambda_{r}(x)=\kappa_{r}\frac{x!}{(x-y_{r})!}=\kappa_{r}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}!}{(x_{i}-y_{r,i})!},

with some positive constant
\kappa_{r}
.

An SRN is denoted by
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
. Throughout, we assume a compatibility condition is fulfilled:

Condition 1
.

For any
r=y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
,
\lambda_{r}(x)>0
if any only if
x\geq y
.

If Condition
1
is fulfilled, then
x\to_{\mathcal{N}}z
implies that there are positive probability to go from
x
to
z
, and vice versa.

A probability measure
\pi
on an irreducible component
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
of
\mathcal{N}
is
[
JoshiCap
, Definition 4.1]
:

i)

a
stationary distribution
, if for all
x\in\Gamma
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\pi(x)\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\lambda_{r}(x)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi(x-\zeta_{r})\lambda_{r}(x-\zeta_{r});

ii)

a
complex balanced distribution
, if for all complexes
\eta\in\mathcal{C}
, and all
x\in\Gamma
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\pi(x)\sum_{y^{\prime}\colon\eta\to y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}}\lambda_{\eta\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\sum_{y\colon y\to\eta\in\mathcal{R}}\pi(x+y-\eta)\lambda_{y\to\eta}(x+y-\eta);

iii)

a
detailed balanced distribution
, if
\mathcal{N}
is reversible and for all
y{}\mathrel{\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt]{\rightarrow\displaystyle}}\limits}{}y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
,

\displaystyle\pi(x)\lambda_{y\to y^{\prime}}(x)=\pi(x+y^{\prime}-y)\lambda_{y^{\prime}\to y}(x+y^{\prime}-y).

Note that we reserve ‘stationary distribution’ to a distribution on a single irreducible component. Hence, using Definition
2.2
, a stationary distribution
\pi
on an irreducible component
\Gamma
of
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)
fulfils
\pi(x)>0
for all
x\in\Gamma
.
Equation (
2.3
) is known as the
master equation
in the chemical literature (also known as the
Kolmogorov forward equation
) for the SRN
(\mathcal{N},\lambda)

[
anderson2015stochastic
]
. A detailed balanced distribution is a complex balanced distribution, and a complex balanced distribution is a stationary distribution.

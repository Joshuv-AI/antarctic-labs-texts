Mixed virtual element approximation for the five-ﬁeld formulation of the steady Boussinesq problem with temperature-dependent parameters
Zeinab Gharibi

Abstract
In this work we develop recent research on the fully mixed virtual element method (mixed-VEM) based on the Banach space for the stationary Boussinesq equation to suggest and analyze a new mixed-VEM for the stationary two-dimensional Boussinesq equation with temperature-dependent parameters in terms of the pseudostress, vorticity, velocity, pseudoheat vector and temperature fields. The well-posedness of continuous formulation is analyzed utilizing a fixed-point strategy, a smallness assumption on the data, and some additional regularities on the solution. The discretization for the mentioned variables is based on the coupling ℍ ⁡ ( 𝐝𝐢𝐯 6 / 5 ) \mathbb{H}({\mathbf{div}}_{6/5}) - and 𝐇 ⁡ ( div 6 / 5 ) \mathbf{H}({\mathrm{div}}_{6/5}) -conforming virtual element techniques. The proposed scheme is rewritten as an equivalent fixed point operator equation, so that its existence and stability estimates have been proven. In addition, an a priori convergence analysis is established by utilizing the Céa estimate and a suitable assumption on data for all variables in their natural norms showing an optimal rate of convergence. Finally, several numerical examples are presented to illustrate the performance of the proposed method.



Key words
: steady Boussinesq problem, temperature-dependent parameters, mixed virtual element method, five-ﬁeld formulation, theoretical analysis.

Introduction

Various types of free convection are present in nature and in industry, including mantle convection, stratified oceanic flows, and onboard cooling systems for electronic devices. These processes can be modeled by coupling the continuity and momentum equations (Navier–Stokes) with the energy equations using the Boussinesq approximation, assuming that the fluid density is constant in all terms of the equations except for the buoyancy term in the momentum equation, where linearity dependency is taken into account. Despite this, other properties of the fluid may also be affected by temperature, such as viscosity and thermal conductivity, which are significant factors in the flow of oils and nanofluids. In this regard, a short list of a variety of numerical approximations for the generalizations of Boussinesq equations with temperature-dependent parameters, include finite element method (FEM)

A recent study in
[
5

discusses primal and mixed formulations of the energy (with a space-dependent thermal conductivity) and the momentum and continuity (with temperature-dependent viscosity) equations, respectively. This study introduces pseudostress and vorticity tensors as auxiliary variables. In this way, the authors showed the optimally convergent method, using the fixed-point strategy proposed in

The first one deals with the primal formulation of the energy equation, while the second one introduces an augmented fully-mixed finite element method for the problem. In both cases, the analysis is based on the introduction of a pseudostress tensor relating the diffusive and convective terms with the pressure and it is proven optimal convergence. However, this approach is not without its drawbacks. One such drawback is that it can be computationally expensive.

2.1
The model problem

In this paper, we consider and study the stationary Boussinesq
problem with varying viscosity and
thermal conductivity. This problem involves a system of equations where the incompressible Navier–Stokes equation is coupled with the transport equation through a convective term and a buoyancy term, usually exerting force in the opposite direction to gravity. More precisely, given an external force per unit mass
{\mathbf{g}}\in\textbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)

{\mathbf{u}}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{div}}\big(\mu(\varphi)\,\mathbf{e}({\mathbf{u}})\big)+(\bm{\nabla}{\mathbf{u}}){\mathbf{u}}+\nabla p-\varphi\,{\mathbf{g}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbf{0}\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,

(2.1a)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{div}}({\mathbf{u}})

\displaystyle\,=\,0\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,

(2.1b)

\displaystyle-{\mathrm{div}}(\kappa(\varphi)\nabla\varphi)+{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla\varphi

\displaystyle\,=\,0\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,

(2.1c)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\mathbf{0}\quad{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\Gamma\,,

(2.1d)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\,=\,\varphi_{D}\quad{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\Gamma_{D}\,,

(2.1e)

\displaystyle\kappa(\varphi)\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\,=\,0\quad{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\Gamma_{N}\,,

(2.1f)

v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in V_{1}\times V_{2}
be a group variable, where
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are Hilbert spaces. Consider a composite bilinear form

b(u,v)\,:=\,b_{1}(u,v_{1})+b_{2}(u,v_{2})\,,

Define the kernel space

U_{1}\,:=\,\Big\{u\in U:\quad b_{1}(u,v_{1})\,=\,0\quad\forall\,v_{1}\in V_{1}\Big\}\,,

and assume the following inf-sup conditions

\sup_{u\in U}\dfrac{b_{1}(u,v_{1})}{\|u\|_{U}}\,\geq\,\beta_{1}\,\|v_{1}\|_{V_{1}}\quad\forall\,v_{1}\in V_{1}\,,

(5.7)

and

\sup_{u_{1}\in U_{1}}\dfrac{b_{2}(u_{1},v_{2})}{\|u_{1}\|_{U}}\,\geq\,\beta_{2}\,\|v_{2}\|_{V_{2}}\quad\forall\,v_{2}\in V_{2}\,,

(5.8)

then, there exists a constant
\beta
, depending on
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
,
\|b_{1}\|
and
\|b_{2}\|
, such that

\sup_{u\in U}\dfrac{b(u,v)}{\|u\|_{U}}\,\geq\,\beta\,\|v\|_{V}\quad\forall\,v\in V\,.

(5.9)

Proof.

Given
(u,v)\in U\times V
, we note that inf-sup conditions (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) guarantee the existence of
\widetilde{u}\in U
and
\widehat{u}\in U_{1}
, such that there hold

\|\widetilde{u}\|_{U}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad b_{1}\left(\widetilde{u},v_{1}\right)\geq\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{V}\,,

(5.10)

and

\|\widehat{u}\|_{U}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad b_{2}\left(\widehat{u},v_{2}\right)\geq\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}\left\|v_{2}\right\|_{V}\,.

(5.11)

Now, defining
\bar{u}:=c_{1}\widetilde{u}+c_{2}\widehat{u}
, with positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
to be chosen later on, which yields
\|\bar{u}\|_{U}\leq c_{1}+c_{2}
, and using that
b_{1}\left(\widehat{u},v_{1}\right)=0
along with (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) we have

\begin{gathered}\sup_{u\in U}\frac{b(u,v)}{\|u\|_{U}}\geq\frac{|b(\bar{u},v)|}{\|\bar{u}\|_{U}}=\frac{\left|b_{1}\left(\bar{u},v_{1}\right)+b_{2}\left(\bar{u},v_{2}\right)\right|}{\|\bar{u}\|_{U}}\\
=\frac{\left|c_{1}b_{1}\left(\widetilde{u},v_{1}\right)+c_{2}b_{2}\left(\widehat{u},v_{2}\right)+c_{1}b_{2}\left(\widetilde{u},v_{2}\right)\right|}{\|\bar{u}\|_{U}}\\
\geq\frac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}}\left(\frac{c_{1}\beta_{1}}{2}\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{V}+\left(\frac{c_{2}\beta_{2}}{2}-c_{1}\left\|b_{2}\right\|\right)\left\|v_{2}\right\|_{V}\right)\,,\end{gathered}

from which, choosing
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that
c_{1}\geq 0
and
\frac{c_{2}\beta_{2}}{2}\geq c_{1}\left\|b_{2}\right\|
we arrive at (
5.9
) with considering

\beta:=\frac{1}{c_{1}+c_{2}}\min\left\{\frac{c_{1}\beta_{1}}{2},\left(\frac{c_{2}\beta_{2}}{2}-c_{1}\left\|b_{2}\right\|\right)\right\}\,,

and finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.6

as the solution of the variational formulation arising from stationary Stokes

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\textbf{z}:\nabla\textbf{w}+\int_{\Omega}p\,{\mathrm{div}}(\mathbf{w})&\,=\,\mathbf{0}\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,,\\
\int_{\Omega}q\,{\mathrm{div}}(\mathbf{z})&\,=\,g(q)\qquad\forall\,q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\,.\end{split}

Now, considering

and letting

and

and

, which are defined by (see

\begin{array}[]{ll}X_{h}|_{K}\,:=\,\bigg\{{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{1}(K):&\quad{\mathbf{w}}\mid_{\partial K}\in\mathbf{B}_{k}(\partial K)\,,\quad{\mathrm{div}}({\mathbf{w}})\in\mathrm{P}_{k-1}(K)\,{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\\
&-\bm{\Delta}{\mathbf{w}}-\nabla v\in{\mathbf{P}}_{k-2,\nabla}(K)\quad\forall\,v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(K)\bigg\}\,,\end{array}

(5.17)

and

\widehat{Y}_{h}|_{K}\,:=\,\mathrm{P}_{k-1}(K)\,,

we find that

, where

, is the approximation virtual element solution for problem (

\begin{split}\mathscr{D}^{S}_{h}(\mathbf{z}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h})+\int_{\Omega}p_{h}{\mathrm{div}}(\mathbf{w}_{h})&\,=\,0\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{w}_{h}\in X_{h}\,,\\
\int_{\Omega}q_{h}{\mathrm{div}}(\mathbf{z}_{h})&\,=\,g(q_{h})\qquad\forall\,q_{h}\in Y_{h}\,,\end{split}

(5.18)

where the discrete bilinear form

. Next, proceeding analogously to

, Subsection 5.2]

as the functional induced by a given

In this way, knowing that
\mathbf{P}_{k}(\Omega_{h})\subseteq X_{h}
for each

(cf.
[
10
, Section 3.1]

\mathbf{w}_{h}\,:=\,(-x_{2},x_{1})^{\mathtt{t}}

\big|\big(\mathscr{O}^{T}_{\varphi}-\mathscr{O}^{T}_{h,\varphi}\big)({\mathbf{u}};\varphi,{\bm{\eta}}_{h})\big|\,\leq\,\ell_{\mathscr{O}^{T}}\,\|({\mathbf{u}}\,\varphi)-{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{h}_{r}({\mathbf{u}}\,\varphi)\|_{0,\Omega}\,\|{\bm{\eta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\,{\bm{\eta}}_{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{h}\,,\end{array}

which, replaced back in (
6.20
) and (
6.21
), respectively, then combining the result estimate with (
6.15
), and bounding terms
\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,6;\Omega}
and
\|{\mathbf{u}}_{h}\|_{0,6;\Omega}
with
r
and
r_{\mathtt{d}}
, respectively, yields

\begin{array}[]{c}\big\|({\bm{\rho}},\varphi)-({\bm{\rho}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\big\|_{\mathcal{W}}\,\leq\,\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathtt{st}}\,\Big\{{\mathrm{dist}}\big(({\bm{\rho}},\varphi),\mathcal{W}_{h}\big)\\[8.61108pt]
\,+\,\left(\|\varphi_{D}\|_{1/2+\epsilon,\Gamma_{D}}+\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,6;\Omega}\,\|\varphi\|_{0,6;\Omega}\right)\|\varphi-\varphi_{h}\|_{0,6;\Omega}+\|\varphi\|_{0,6;\Omega}\,\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}\|_{0,6;\Omega}\\[8.61108pt]
\,+\,\|{\bm{\rho}}-{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{h}_{r}({\bm{\rho}})\|_{0,\Omega}\,+\,\|({\mathbf{u}}\,\varphi)-{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{h}_{r}({\mathbf{u}}\,\varphi)\|_{0,\Omega}\Big\}\,,\end{array}

(6.22)

where
\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathtt{st}}
is a constant depending on
\widetilde{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathtt{st}}
,
\|\mathscr{A}^{T}\|
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}^{T}}
,
\|\mathscr{O}^{T}\|
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{O}^{T}}
,
\ell_{\mathscr{A}^{T}}
,
\ell_{\mathscr{O}^{T}}
,
r
,

,
\bar{C}_{\epsilon}

For the rest of the analysis, we suitably combine the estimates (
6.14
) and (
6.22
).
More precisely,
multiplying (
6.14
) by
\frac{1}{4\mathcal{C}_{1,\mathtt{st}}}
, adding the resulting inequality and (
6.22
), and conducting some algebraic computations, yield

\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{1}{4\mathcal{C}_{1,\mathtt{st}}}\big\|({\bm{\sigma}},\vec{{\mathbf{u}}})-({\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\vec{{\mathbf{u}}}_{h})\big\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\big\|({\bm{\rho}},\varphi)-({\bm{\rho}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\big\|_{\mathcal{W}}\\[8.61108pt]
\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{4\mathcal{C}_{1,\mathtt{st}}}\,{\mathrm{dist}}\big(({\bm{\sigma}},\vec{{\mathbf{u}}}),\mathcal{V}_{h}\big)+\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathtt{st}}\,{\mathrm{dist}}\big(({\bm{\rho}},\varphi),\mathcal{W}_{h}\big)\\[8.61108pt]
\,+\,\dfrac{1}{4}\Big(\big\|{\bm{\sigma}}-\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}^{h}_{r}({\bm{\sigma}})\big\|_{0,\Omega}+\big\|{\bm{\gamma}}-\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}^{h}_{r}({\bm{\gamma}})\big\|_{0,\Omega}\,+\,\big\|({\mathbf{u}}\otimes{\mathbf{u}})-\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}^{h}_{r}({\mathbf{u}}\otimes{\mathbf{u}})\big\|_{0,\Omega}\Big)\\[10.76385pt]
\,+\,\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathtt{st}}\,\Big(\|{\bm{\rho}}-{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{h}_{r}({\bm{\rho}})\|_{0,\Omega}\,+\,\|({\mathbf{u}}\,\varphi)-{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}^{h}_{r}({\mathbf{u}}\,\varphi)\|_{0,\Omega}\Big)\\[8.61108pt]
\,+\,\Big(\dfrac{1}{4}\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,6;\Omega}+\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathtt{st}}\,\|\varphi\|_{0,6;\Omega}\Big)\,\|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{h}\|_{0,6;\Omega}\\[8.61108pt]
\,+\,\Big(\dfrac{1}{4}\,\left(1+\|{\mathbf{g}}\|_{0,3/2;\Omega}+\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,6;\Omega}^{2}\right)\\[8.61108pt]
\,+\,\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathtt{st}}\,\left(\|\varphi_{D}\|_{1/2+\epsilon,\Gamma_{D}}+\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{0,6;\Omega}\,\|\varphi\|_{0,6;\Omega}\right)\Big)\|\varphi-\varphi_{h}\|_{0,6;\Omega}\,.\end{array}

(6.23)

Consequently, thanks to (

) we are in position to establish the announced Céa estimate.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that the data satisfy

\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{1}{4}\Big(1+\dfrac{16}{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}^{S}}^{2}\alpha_{\mathcal{A}^{T}}^{2}}\|{\mathbf{g}}\|_{0,3/2;\Omega}\,\|\varphi_{D}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{D}}^{2}\Big)\|{\mathbf{g}}\|_{0,3/2;\Omega}\\[8.61108pt]

(6.24)

and

\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}^{S}}\alpha_{\mathcal{A}^{T}}}\,\|\varphi_{D}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{D}}\|{\mathbf{g}}\|_{0,3/2;\Omega}\,+\,\mathcal{C}_{2,\mathtt{st}}\,\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathcal{A}^{T}}}\,\|\varphi_{D}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{D}}\,\leq\,\dfrac{1}{8\mathcal{C}_{1,\mathtt{st}}}\,.

(6.25)

Then, there exists a constant

\,\leq\,\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathtt{st}}\,\bigg\{{\mathrm{dist}}\big(({\bm{\sigma}},\vec{{\mathbf{u}}}),\mathcal{V}_{h}\big)\,+\,{\mathrm{dist}}\big(({\bm{\rho}},\varphi),\mathcal{W}_{h}\big)\\[8.61108pt]

Proof.

6.2.1
Interpolation estimates

In order to define an interpolation operators in the local spaces
\mathbf{X}_{r}(E)
and
\mathbb{X}_{r}(E)
, for each element
E\in\Omega_{h}
we denote by

\widetilde{{\bm{\chi}}}_{i}^{E}
and
{\bm{\chi}}_{i}^{E}
the operator associated to the
i
-th local degree of freedom,
i=1,\cdots,\widetilde{n}^{E}_{r}
and
i=1,\cdots,n^{E}_{r}
, respectively. From the
definition of these spaces, it is easily seen that for every smooth enough function
{\bm{\eta}}\in\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(E)
and for each
{\bm{\tau}}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,1}(E)
there exists a unique elements
{\bm{\eta}}_{I}^{E}\in\mathbf{X}_{r}(E)
and
{\bm{\tau}}_{I}^{E}\in\mathbb{X}_{r}(E)
such that

\begin{array}[]{c}\widetilde{{\bm{\chi}}}_{i}^{E}\big({\bm{\eta}}-{\bm{\eta}}_{I}^{E}\big)\,=\,0\quad\forall\,i\,=\,1,\cdots,\widetilde{n}^{E}_{r}{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}{\bm{\chi}}_{i}^{E}\big({\bm{\tau}}-{\bm{\tau}}_{I}^{E}\big)\,=\,0\quad\forall\,i\,=\,1,\cdots,n^{E}_{r}\,,\end{array}

(6.26)

Let
C
(or
\widetilde{C}
,
C_{1}
,
\widetilde{C}_{1}
) denote a generic constant independent of the mesh parameter, the values of which may be different at different places. Following the discussion of
[
18
]
(also
see
[
31
,
17
]
), we have the following interpolation error estimates

\|{\bm{\eta}}-{\bm{\eta}}_{I}^{E}\|_{0,E}\,\leq\,C\,h^{s}_{E}\,|{\bm{\eta}}|_{s,E}\qquad\forall\,{\bm{\eta}}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(E)\,,

(6.27)

and

\|{\bm{\tau}}-{\bm{\tau}}_{I}^{E}\|_{0,E}\,\leq\,\widetilde{C}\,h^{s}_{E}\,|{\bm{\tau}}|_{s,E}\qquad\forall\,{\bm{\tau}}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(E)\,,

(6.28)

In addition, from Lemma
4.1
and the following commutative properties

\begin{array}[]{c}{\mathrm{div}}({\bm{\eta}}_{I}^{E})\,=\,{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{r}^{E}({\mathrm{div}}({\bm{\eta}}))\quad\forall\,{\bm{\eta}}\in\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(E){\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\\[8.61108pt]
{\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\tau}}_{I}^{E})\,=\,\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{r}^{E}({\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\tau}}))\quad\forall\,{\bm{\tau}}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,1}(E)\,,\end{array}

(6.29)

we deduce, for each
{\bm{\eta}}\in\mathbf{W}^{1,1}(E)
and
{\bm{\tau}}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,1}(E)
such that
{\mathrm{div}}({\bm{\eta}})\in\mathrm{W}^{s,t}(E)
and
{\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\tau}})\in\mathbf{W}^{s,q}(E)
, with
s\in[0,r+1]
, there holds (see, e.g.,
[
31
, eq. (3.14)]
)

\|{\mathrm{div}}\big({\bm{\eta}}-{\bm{\eta}}_{I}^{E}\big)\|_{0,t;E}\,\leq\,C_{1}\,h^{s}_{E}\,|{\mathrm{div}}({\bm{\eta}})|_{s,t;E}\,,

(6.30)

and

\|{\mathbf{div}}\big({\bm{\tau}}-{\bm{\tau}}_{I}^{E}\big)\|_{0,q;E}\,\leq\,\widetilde{C}_{1}\,h^{s}_{E}\,|{\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\tau}})|_{s,q;E}\,.

(6.31)

Then, given a subspace
V_{h}
of an arbitrary Banach space
\big(V,\|\cdot\|_{V}\big)
, we set
from now on

\mathrm{dist}(v,V_{h})\,:=\,\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\|v-v_{h}\|_{V}\qquad\forall\,v\in V\,.

Hence, according to the local approximation properties provided by the estimates
(
6.27
) up to (
6.31
), and Lemma
4.1
, we easily
derive the following global ones:

(\mathbf{AP}_{h}^{{\bm{\sigma}}})

for each integer
s\in[1,r+1]
there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\text{dist}({\bm{\tau}},\mathbb{X}_{h})\,\leq\,\|{\bm{\tau}}-{\bm{\tau}}_{I}\|_{{\mathbf{div}}_{6/5};\Omega}\,\leq\,C\,h^{s}\,\Big\{|{\bm{\tau}}|_{s,\Omega}+|{\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\tau}})|_{s,6/5;\Omega}\Big\}\,,

for all
{\bm{\tau}}\in\mathbb{X}
such that
{\bm{\tau}}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and

{\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\tau}})\in\mathbf{W}^{s,6/5}(\Omega)
,

(\mathbf{AP}_{h}^{{\mathbf{u}}})

for each integer
s\in[0,r+1]
there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\text{dist}(\bm{{\mathbf{v}}},\mathbf{Y}_{h})\,\leq\,\|{\mathbf{v}}-{\bm{\mathcal{P}}}_{r}^{h}({\mathbf{v}})\|_{0,6;\Omega}\,\leq\,C\,h^{s}\,|{\mathbf{v}}|_{s,6;\Omega}\,,

for all
{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{Y}
such that
{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{W}^{s,6}(\Omega)
,

(\mathbf{AP}_{h}^{{\bm{\gamma}}})

for each integer
s\in[0,r+1]
there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h

\text{dist}({\bm{\omega}},\mathbb{Z}_{h})\,\leq\,\|{\bm{\omega}}-\mathcal{P}\hskip-6.45831pt\mathcal{P}_{r}^{h}({\bm{\omega}})\|_{0,\Omega}\,\leq\,C\,h^{s}\,|{\bm{\omega}}|_{s,\Omega}\,,

for all
{\bm{\omega}}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
{\bm{\omega}}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)
,

(\mathbf{AP}_{h}^{{\bm{\rho}}})

for each integer
s\in[1,r+1]
there exists a positive constant

h
, such that

\text{dist}({\bm{\eta}},\mathbf{X}_{h})\,\leq\,\|{\bm{\eta}}-{\bm{\eta}}_{I}\|_{{\mathrm{div}}_{6/5};\Omega}\,\leq\,C\,h^{s}\,\Big\{|{\bm{\eta}}|_{s,\Omega}+|{\mathrm{div}}({\bm{\eta}})|_{s,6/5;\Omega}\Big\}\,,

for all
{\bm{\eta}}\in\mathbf{X}
such that
{\bm{\eta}}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and

{\mathrm{div}}({\bm{\eta}})\in\mathrm{W}^{s,6/5}(\Omega)
, and

(\mathbf{AP}_{h}^{\varphi})

for each integer
s\in[0,r+1]
there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of

\text{dist}(\psi,\mathrm{Y}_{h})\,\leq\,\|\psi-{\mathcal{P}}_{r}^{h}(\psi)\|_{0,6;\Omega}\,\leq\,C\,h^{s}\,|\varphi|_{s,6;\Omega}\,,

We are now in a position to provide the rates of convergence of the virtual scheme (

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume that the data satisfy (
6.24
) and (
6.25
), and let
({\bm{\sigma}},\vec{{\mathbf{u}}})\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathcal{Z}
,

2.15d

4.15d

3.6
and

, respectively.
Furthermore, given an integer

, assume that there exists

and

{\bm{\sigma}}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega),\quad{\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\sigma}})\in\mathbf{W}^{s,6/5}(\Omega),\quad{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbf{W}^{k,6}(\Omega),\quad{\mathbf{u}}\otimes{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{H}^{k}(\Omega),\quad{\bm{\gamma}}\in\mathbb{H}^{k}(\Omega)\,,

Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\|({\bm{\sigma}},\vec{{\mathbf{u}}})-({\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\vec{{\mathbf{u}}}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|({\bm{\rho}},\varphi)-({\bm{\rho}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\|_{\mathcal{W}}\,\leq\,C\,h^{\min\{k,s\}}\,\Big\{|{\bm{\sigma}}|_{s,\Omega}\,+\,|{\mathbf{div}}({\bm{\sigma}})|_{s,6/5;\Omega}\,+\,|{\mathbf{u}}|_{k,6;\Omega}\,+\,|{\bm{\gamma}}|_{k,\Omega}\\[8.61108pt]

) (cf. Lemma

7.2
Example 2: natural convection in a square cavity

In the second example, we study the natural convection of a fluid within a square cavity featuring differentially heated walls. This phenomenon has been extensively investigated under various boundary conditions (see, e.g.
[
7
,
28
,
29
]
).
Here, we recall from
[
28
]
the modified problem with dimensionless numbers: seek
(\mathbf{u},p,\varphi)
such that

\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\mathrm{Pr}\,\operatorname{\textbf{div}}(2\mu(\varphi)\textbf{e}(\mathbf{u}))+(\bm{\nabla}\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u}+\nabla p-\mathrm{Ra}~\mathrm{Pr}~\varphi\,\mathbf{g}&\,=&\mathbf{0}&\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[4.30554pt]
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}&\,=&0&\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\\[4.30554pt]
-\operatorname{div}(\kappa(\varphi)\nabla\varphi)+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi&\,=&0&\quad{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\,,\end{array}

(7.1)

where
\mathrm{Pr}
represents the Prandtl number, denoting the ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity, while
\mathrm{Ra}
signifies the Rayleigh number, which is defined as the ratio of buoyancy forces to viscosity forces multiplied by the Prandtl number. Therefore, we determine the cavity as
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
and consider the Prandtl and Rayleigh numbers as
\mathrm{Pr}=0.5
and
\mathrm{Ra}=4000
, respectively.
Furthermore, the viscosity, thermal conductivity, and body force will be provided as follows:

\mu(\varphi)\,=\,\exp(-\varphi),\qquad\kappa(\varphi)\,=\,\exp(\varphi)\,\qquad\mathbf{g}\,=\,(0,1)^{\mathtt{t}}\,,

and the boundary conditions will be adopted as described in
[
28
]
, that is

\mathbf{u}_{D}(\mathbf{x})\,=\,\mathbf{0}{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\varphi_{D}(\mathbf{x})\,=\,\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos(2\pi x_{1}))(1-x_{2})\,.

The numerical experiments are performed with
r=0
. In Fig.
7.3
we have portrayed the approximate solutions, generated with the first-order mixed virtual
element family. All plots are in concordance with those obtained in
[
2
]
and what is expected to be
observed from the physical point of view, in accordance to
[
28
]
.

e-2.

Abstract
In this paper, the authors consider an initial boundary value problem for the heat flow of
equation of surfaces with constant mean curvatures, which was investigated in
[On the heat flow of equation of surfaces of constant mean curvatures,
Manuscripta Mathematica, 2011, 134: 259-271] by Huang et al.,
where global well-posedness and finite time blow-up of regular solutions were obtained.
Their results are complemented in this paper in the sense that
some new conditions on the initial data are provided for the solutions to develop finite time singularity.

Keywords
H-surface; constant mean curvature; blow-up.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification 2010:
35K20, 58J35.

1
Introduction

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Given a continuous function
H:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, a map
u\in C^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
is called an

H
-surface (parameterized over
\Omega
), if it satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta u=2H(u)u_{x}\wedge u_{y},\qquad(x,y)\in\Omega,

(1.1)

where
\wedge
denotes the wedge product in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In fact,
if
u
is a conformal representation of a surface
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, i.e.,
u_{x}\cdot u_{y}=0=|u_{x}|^{2}-|u_{y}|^{2}
,
then
H(u)
is the mean curvature of
S
at the point
u
.

After the pioneering work of Wente
[
12
]
in 1969, the boundary value problem for the equation of
H
-surface (
1.1
)
has been studied extensively. We refer the interested readers to
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
14
]
for
H
-surface with
constant mean curvature and to
[
3
,
5
,
13
]
for
H
-surface with with variable
H
.

In this paper, we consider the parabolic counterpart of problem (
1.1
), that is the following initial boundary value problem for the heat flow of
the equation of
H
-surface

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-2H(u)u_{x}\wedge u_{y},&(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u=\chi,&(x,y,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&(x,y)\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
T\in(0,+\infty]
is the maximal existence time of the solution
u(x,t)
,
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\chi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u_{0}\mid_{\partial\Omega}=\chi
.

In 1988, problem (
1.2
) (with the Dirichlet boundary condition replaced by free boundaries)
was employed by Struwe
[
14
]
, where the existence of surfaces of constant mean curvature
H=H_{0}
was obtained,
under the assumption that
|H_{0}|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<1
.
Later, Rey
[
13
]
showed that problem (
1.2
) with variable
H
admits a unique global regular solution

u\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{3})

when
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
u_{0}\mid_{\partial\Omega}=\chi
and

\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<1
. When
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

\chi\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
, Chen and Levine
[
4
]
removed the
assumption
\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<1
and obtained the existence and uniqueness of short time
regular solution to problem (
1.2
), with an additional assumption that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}(\cdot,t)\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}(\cdot,s),\qquad 0\leq s\leq t.

There are also some works on global regular solutions to problem (
1.2
) with general
H
.
For example, for an arbitrary Lipschitz function
H
,
Wang
[
11
]
proved that if
u\in H^{1}(\Omega\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a weak solution to (
1.2
),
then
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\setminus\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{3})
, where

\Sigma=\bigcup_{t>0}\Sigma_{t}\subset\Omega\times(0,\infty)

is a closed subset, whose Lebesgue measure is zero and
\Sigma_{t}\subset\Omega\times\{t\}
is finite for almost all
t>0
.
As for the finite time singularity of regular solutions to problem (
1.2
),
Huang et al.
[
7
]
showed, when
H
is a nontrivial constant and
\chi=0
,
that the regular solution to problem (
1.2
) blows up in finite time
when the initial energy is “subcritical”.

The aim of this paper is to reconsider the finite time singularity of regular solutions to
problem (
1.2
) and to give some new conditions on the initial data to assure finite time blow-up.
We have to admit that we do not invent any new strategies or new
methods to prove the finite time blow up results, but only apply the concavity argument (
[
6
,
8
]
) to a newly constructed auxiliary functional.
Henceforth we always assume that

H\equiv H_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\qquad\chi=0.

(1.3)

The main results of this paper will be stated and proved in the next section.

2
Main results

In order to describe our main results, we first introduce some notations and lemmas.
If no confusion arises, we will always write

\int_{\Omega}\cdot\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}y
and
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\cdot\ {\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}s

as
\int_{\Omega}\cdot
and
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\cdot
, respectively.
We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{r}
the
L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
norm for
1\leq r\leq\infty

and by
\|\cdot\|
the Dirichlet norm in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
i.e.,
\|u\|=\|\nabla u\|_{2}
for any
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
The potential energy functional and the Nehari’s functional associated with problem (
1.2
)
are defined, respectively, by

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{2H_{0}}{3}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot u_{x}\wedge u_{y},

(2.1)

N(u)=\|u\|^{2}+2H_{0}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot u_{x}\wedge u_{y}.

(2.2)

For any regular solution
u(x,t)
to problem (
1.2
),
the functional
E(u)
satisfies the following energy identity.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
7
]
) For
0<T\leq\infty
, suppose that
u:\Omega\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a regular solution to
problem (
1.2
). Then it holds

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}|^{2}+E(u(t_{2}))=E(u(t_{1})),\quad\forall\ 0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<T.

(2.3)

The main result of this paper is proved with the help of the following concavity lemma
[
8
]
,
whose proof can be found in
[
9
]

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that a positive, twice-differentiable function
\psi(t)
satisfies the inequality

\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)\psi(t)-(1+\theta)(\psi^{\prime}(t))^{2}\geq 0,

where
\theta>0
. If
\psi(0)>0
,
\psi^{\prime}(0)>0
, then
\psi(t)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow t_{*}\leq t^{*}=\frac{\psi(0)}{\theta\psi^{\prime}(0)}
.

We are now in the position to state and prove the main results of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let assumption (
1.3
) hold. Assume that
u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

E(u_{0})<\frac{\lambda_{1}}{6}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2},

(2.4)

were
\lambda_{1}>0
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition.
Then the local regular solution
u(x,t)
to problem (
1.2
) blows up in finite time.
Moreover, the upper bound for
T
has the following form:

T\leq\dfrac{16\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})}
.

Proof.

The proof will be divided into two steps.

Step I: Finite time blow-up for local regular solution.

Suppose by contradiction that the local regular solution
u(x,t)
exists globally.
Then
\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{2}
is well-defined for all
t\geq 0
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
E(u(t))>0
for all
t\geq 0
.
Otherwise by Theorem 1.1 in
[
7
]
we know that
u(x,t)
blows up in finite time.

First, multiplying both sides of the first equation in (
1.2
) by
u
and integrating over
\Omega
one obtains

\dfrac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}=-2\|u\|^{2}-4H_{0}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot u_{x}\wedge u_{y}=-2N(u(t)),\qquad t\in(0,\infty).

(2.5)

From (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and the definition of
\lambda_{1}
we have

N(u)=3\Big[E(u)-\frac{1}{6}\|u\|^{2}\Big]\leq 3\Big[E(u)-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{6}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\Big].

(2.6)

Substituting (
2.6
) into (
2.5
) to yield

\dfrac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u(t)),\qquad t\in(0,\infty).

(2.7)

Combining (
2.7
) with (
2.3
) one sees that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big[\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u(t))\Big]=\dfrac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}\|u_{t}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}\Big[\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u(t))\Big].

(2.8)

Recalling that
\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u_{0})>0
, we obtain,
by applying Gronwall’s inequality to (
2.8
), that

\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u(t))\geq\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u_{0})\Big]e^{\lambda_{1}t}.

(2.9)

Therefore,

\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}&\geq\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u_{0})\Big]e^{\lambda_{1}t}+\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u(t))\\
&\geq\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-\dfrac{6}{\lambda_{1}}E(u_{0})\Big]e^{\lambda_{1}t},\qquad t\in(0,\infty),\end{split}

(2.10)

since
E(u(t))\geq 0
on
[0,\infty)
.

On the other hand, in view of Minkowski inequality, Hölder inequality, the energy identity (
2.3
)
and the fact that
E(u(t))>0
for
t\geq 0
we obtain

\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{2}\leq&\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{2}=\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\|\int_{0}^{t}u_{\tau}{\rm d}\tau\|_{2}\\
\leq&\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\tau}\|_{2}{\rm d}\tau\leq\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{\tau}\|_{2}^{2}{\rm d}\tau\Big)^{1/2}t^{1/2}\\
=&\|u_{0}\|_{2}+[E(u_{0})-E(u(t))]^{1/2}t^{1/2}\\
\leq&\|u_{0}\|_{2}+\sqrt{E(u_{0})t},\end{split}

which contradicts with (
2.10
) for
t
sufficiently large.
Therefore, the local regular solution
u(x,t)
must blow up in finite time.

Step II: Upper bound for the blow-up time.

Form now on, we denote by
T>0
the blow-up time of
u(x,t)
, which is finite by Step I.
From (
2.6
) and (
2.9
) we see that
N(u(t))<0
on
[0,T)
,
which, together with (
2.5
), implies that
\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}
is strictly increasing on
[0,T)
.

For any
\beta>0
and
\sigma>0
, we define the auxiliary functional

F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+(T-t)\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\beta(t+\sigma)^{2},\qquad t\in[0,T).

(2.11)

Then
F(0)=T\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\beta\sigma^{2}>0
. Taking derivative with respect to
t
we obtain

\begin{split}F^{\prime}(t)&=\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+2\beta(t+\sigma)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}+2\beta(t+\sigma)\\
&=2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot u_{s}+2\beta(t+\sigma),\end{split}

(2.12)

and
F^{\prime}(0)=2\beta\sigma>0
. Taking derivative again and recalling (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
),
one arrives at

\begin{split}F^{\prime\prime}(t)=&2\int_{\Omega}u\cdot u_{t}+2\beta=-2N(u(t))+2\beta(>0)\\
\geq&-6E(u(t))+\|u(t)\|^{2}+2\beta\\
=&-6E(u_{0})+6\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s)\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\|u(t)\|^{2}+2\beta.\end{split}

(2.13)

Noticing that
F^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
on
[0,T)
,
F^{\prime}(t)
is monotone increasing on
[0,T)
and
F^{\prime}(t)\geq F^{\prime}(0)>0
.
Therefore,
F(t)
is strictly increasing on
[0,T)
.

In view of (
2.11
)-(
2.13
) and recalling the monotonicity of
\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}
, we have,
for any
t\in[0,T)
and
\beta\in\Big(0,\frac{\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})}{4}\Big]
, that

\begin{split}&F(t)F^{\prime\prime}(t)-\dfrac{3}{2}(F^{\prime}(t))^{2}=F(t)F^{\prime\prime}(t)-6\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot u_{s}+\beta(t+\sigma)\Big]^{2}\\
=&F(t)F^{\prime\prime}(t)+6\Big[\eta(t)-(F-(T^{*}-t)\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2})(\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s)\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\beta)\Big]\\
\geq&F(t)F^{\prime\prime}(t)-6F(t)\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s)\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\beta\Big]\\
\geq&F(t)\Big[\|u(t)\|^{2}-6E(u_{0})-4\beta\Big]\\
\geq&F(t)\Big[\lambda_{1}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})-4\beta\Big]\\
\geq&F(t)\Big[\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})-4\beta\Big]\\
\geq&0,\end{split}

(2.14)

where

\eta(t)=\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\beta(t+\sigma)^{2}\Big]\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{s}(s)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}s+\beta\Big]-\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot u_{s}+\beta(t+\sigma)\Big]^{2},\quad t\in[0,T),

the nonnegativity of which can be verified by simply using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
and Hölder’s inequality.
Now applying Lemma
2.2
to
F(t)
one sees that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow T}F(t)=\infty,

(2.15)

where

T\leq\dfrac{2F(0)}{F^{\prime}(0)}=\dfrac{2(T\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\beta\sigma^{2})}{2\beta\sigma}=\dfrac{\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\beta\sigma}T+\sigma,

or

T\Big(1-\dfrac{\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\beta\sigma}\Big)\leq\sigma,

(2.16)

for any
\beta\in\Big(0,\frac{\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})}{4}\Big]
and
\sigma>0
.

Fix a
\beta_{0}\in\Big(0,\frac{\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})}{4}\Big]
.
Then we have
0<\dfrac{\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\beta_{0}\sigma}<1
for any
\sigma\in(\dfrac{\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\beta_{0}},+\infty)
,
which, combined with (
2.16
), guarantees that

T\leq\dfrac{\beta_{0}\sigma^{2}}{\beta_{0}\sigma-\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}.

(2.17)

Minimizing the right hand side term in (
2.17
) for
\sigma\in\Big(\dfrac{\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\beta_{0}},+\infty\Big)
yields

T\leq\dfrac{4\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\beta_{0}},\quad\beta_{0}\in\Big(0,\frac{\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})}{4}\Big].

(2.18)

Minimizing the right hand side term in (
2.18
) for
\beta_{0}\in\Big(0,\frac{\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})}{4}\Big]
we finally obtain

T\leq\dfrac{16\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-6E(u_{0})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
7
]
, the authors showed that the local regular solution

u(x,t)
to problem (
1.2
) blows up in finite time when
E(u_{0})<\dfrac{4\pi}{3H_{0}^{2}}

and
|\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\cdot u_{0x}\wedge u_{0y}|>\dfrac{4\pi}{|H_{0}|^{3}}

(the latter is unnecessary when
E(u_{0})<0
). It is easy to verify that
this condition and assumption (
2.4
) can not be deduced from
each other. Therefore, we obtain a new blow-up criterion for problem (
1.2
).

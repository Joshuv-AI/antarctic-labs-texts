Blowup solutions for a reaction-diffusion system with exponential nonlinearities Thanks: H. Zaag is supported by the ANR project ANAÉ ref. ANR-13-BS01-0010-03. ——————– August 8, 2026


Abstract
. We consider the following parabolic system whose nonlinearity has no gradient structure: { ∂ t u = Δ ​ u + e p ​ v , ∂ t v = μ ​ Δ ​ v + e q ​ u , u ⁡ ( ⋅ , 0 ) = u 0 , v ⁡ ( ⋅ , 0 ) = v 0 , p , q , μ > 0 , \left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+e^{pv},&\partial_{t}v=\mu\Delta v+e^{qu},\\ u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},&v(\cdot,0)=v_{0},\end{array}\right.\quad p,q,\mu>0, in the whole space ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . We show the existence of a stable blowup solution and obtain a complete description of its singularity formation. The construction relies on the reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one and a topological argument based on the index theory to conclude. In particular, our analysis uses neither the maximum principle nor the classical methods based on energy-type estimates which are not supported in this system. The stability is a consequence of the existence proof through a geometrical interpretation of the quantities of blowup parameters whose dimension is equal to the dimension of the finite dimensional problem.

1.
Introduction.

In this paper we study the reaction-diffusion system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+F(v),&\partial_{t}v=\mu\Delta v+G(u),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},&v(\cdot,0)=v_{0},\end{array}\right.\quad\mu>0,

(1.1)

in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where the nonlinearity is of exponential type

F(v)=e^{pv},\quad G(u)=e^{qu},\quad p,q>0.

(1.2)

Our aim is to construct a blowup solution for this system and to precisely describe its blowup profile. We also intend to show the stability of our solution with respect to initial data.

For the expert reader, we would like to immediately present our motivations in considering such a system. For other readers, we will take the time to present the history of the models, hoping that our motivations will gradually become more accessible to him.

1.1.
Our motivations for the expert reader.

In fact, our motivation in this work is double:

\bullet

Physical motivation
: this is an ignition model for thermal explosions of two mixed solid fuels of finite extent, as one may see from Bebernes, Bressan and Eberly
[
Bebernes et al.(1987)Bebernes, Bressan, and Eberly
]
as well as Bebernes and Kassoy
[
Bebernes and Kassoy(1981)
]
, cited by Zheng, Zhao and Chen
[
Zheng et al.(2002)Zheng, Zhao, and Chen
]
. In this model,
u
and
v
stand for the temperatures of the two fuels.

\bullet

Mathematical motivation
: We acknowledge that our argument uses the method introduced by Bressan
[
Bressan(1990)
]
, Bricmont and Kupiainen
[
Bricmont and Kupiainen(1994)
]
and Merle and Zaag
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
for the scalar semilinear heat equation with exponential or power nonlinearity. That method is based on 3 steps:

-

the linearization of the equation around the intended profile;

-

the reduction of the problem to a finite-dimensional one, corresponding to the positive eigenvalues, thanks to the control of the negative directions of the spectrum with the properties of the linear operator;

-

the solution of the finite-dimensional problem thanks to Brouwer’s lemma and the degree theory.

Nevertheless, the case of our system (
1.1
) is much tougher than the mentioned scalar cases, at least for two reasons, which means that our analysis in this paper is far from being a simple adaptation of the arguments introduced in the scalar case, making our interest in (
1.1
) completely meaningful. These are the two reasons:

-

first, we have here a system and not just a scalar equation, with different diffusivities between the components (
\mu
may or may not be equal to
1
), which makes the above-mentioned linear operator neither self-adjoint nor diagonal. Some additional spectral arguments are therefore needed;

-

second, the intended profile for the solution is unbounded in the space variable, as one may see from the statement of Theorem
1.1
below, where we see that

(u,v)\sim(-\log(T-t)+\log\Phi^{*},-\log(T-t)+\log\Psi^{*}),

with
\Phi^{*}
and
\Psi^{*}
introduced in (
1.15
). This makes it difficult to control the solution in the intermediate zone, between the blow-up and the regular zones. Thanks to the introduction of
U=e^{qu}
and
V=e^{pv}
, we make the profile bounded, at the expense of adding two terms unknown in the scalar case, namely
|\nabla U|^{2}/U
and
|\nabla V|^{2}/V
(see (
1.19
) below). These terms are delicate, since both upper bound and lower bound are needed; especially when
U
and
V
become smaller in the intermediate and regular zones. In order to treat them, we introduce estimates of the solution in a 3-fold shrinking set (see Definition
3.1
below), where the control in the blow-up zone is inspired by the scalar case, hence not new, whereas the control in the intermediate region is one of the novelties of our paper.

More details are given below in the introduction and in the following sections.

1.2.
Previous literature and statement of the results.

The local Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) can be solved in several functional spaces
\mathcal{F}
, for example
\mathcal{F}=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
or in a special affine space
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}_{a}
for some positive constant
a
with

\mathcal{H}_{a}=\{(u,v)\in(\bar{\phi},\bar{\psi})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\;\;\text{with}\;\;q\bar{\phi}=p\bar{\psi}=-\ln(1+a|x|^{2})\}.

(1.3)

We denote by
T=T(u_{0},v_{0})\in(0,+\infty]
the maximal existence time of the classical solution
(u,v)
of problem (
1.1
). If
T<+\infty
, then the solution blows up in finite time
T
in the sense that

\lim_{t\to T}(\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})=+\infty.

In that case,
T
is called the blowup time of the solution. A point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is said to be a blowup point of
(u,v)
if
(u,v)
is not locally bounded near
(a,T)
in the sense that
|u(x_{n},t_{n})|+|v(x_{n},t_{n})|\to+\infty
for some sequence
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(a,T)
as
n\to+\infty
. We say that the blowup is
simultaneous
if

\limsup_{t\to T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\limsup_{t\to T}\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=+\infty,

(1.4)

and that it is
non-simultaneous
if (
1.4
) does not hold, i.e. if one of the two components remains bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
. For the system (
1.1
), it is easy to see that the blowup is always
simultaneous
. Indeed, if
u
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
, then the second equation would yield a uniform bound on
v
. More specifically, we say that
u
and
v
blow up simultaneously at the same point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
if
a
is a blowup point both for
u
and
v
.

When system (
1.1
) is coupled with power nonlinearities of the type

F(v)=|v|^{p-1}v,\quad G(u)=|u|^{q-1}u,

(1.5)

and the diffusion coefficient
\mu=1
, Escobedo and Herrero
[
Escobedo and Herrero(1991a)
]
(see also
[
Escobedo and Herrero(1991b)
]
,
[
Escobedo and Herrero(1993)
]
) showed that any nontrivial positive solution which is defined for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
must simultaneously blow up in finite time, provided that

pq>1,\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\max\{p,q\}+1}{pq-1}\geq\frac{N}{2}.

The authors in
[
Andreucci et al.(1997)Andreucci, Herrero, and Velázquez
]
proved that if

pq>1,\quad\text{and}\quad q(pN-2)_{+}<N+2\quad\text{or}\quad p(qN-2)_{+}<N+2,

(1.6)

then every positive solution
(u,v)
of system (
1.1
) exhibits the
Type I
blowup, i.e. there exists some constant
C>0
such that

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\bar{u}(t),\quad\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\bar{v}(t),

(1.7)

where
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
is the blowup positive solution of the associated ordinary differential system

\bar{u}(t)=\Gamma(T-t)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}},\quad\bar{v}(t)=\gamma(T-t)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}

and

\gamma^{p}=\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{pq-1}\right),\quad\Gamma^{q}=\gamma\left(\frac{q+1}{pq-1}\right).

(1.8)

The estimate (
1.7
) has been proved by Caristi and Mitidieri
[
Caristi and Mitidieri(1994)
]
in a ball under assumptions on
p
and
q
different from (
1.6
). See also Fila and Souplet
[
Fila and Souplet(2001)
]
, Deng
[
Deng(1996)
]
for other results relative to estimate (
1.7
).

Through the introduction of the following similarity variables for all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
a
may or may not be a blowup point):

\begin{array}[]{c}\Phi_{T,a}(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}u(x,t),\quad\Psi_{T,a}(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}v(x,t),\\
\\
\text{where}\quad y=\dfrac{x-a}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\ln(T-t),\end{array}

(1.9)

Andreucci, Herrero and Velázquez
[
Andreucci et al.(1997)Andreucci, Herrero, and Velázquez
]
(recall that we are considering the case when
\mu=1
) showed that if the solution
(u,v)
exhibits
Type I
blowup, then one of the following cases occurs (up to replacing
(u,v)
by
(-u,-v)
if necessary):

\bullet
either
(\Phi_{T,a},\Psi_{T,a})
goes to
(\Gamma,\gamma)
exponentially fast,

\bullet
or there exists
k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
such that after an orthogonal change of space coordinates,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi_{T,a}(y,s)&=\Gamma-\dfrac{c_{1}}{s}(p+1)\Gamma\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(y_{i}^{2}-2)+o\left(\dfrac{1}{s}\right),\\
&\\
\Psi_{T,a}(y,s)&=\gamma-\dfrac{c_{1}}{s}(q+1)\gamma\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(y_{i}^{2}-2)+o\left(\dfrac{1}{s}\right),\end{array}

(1.10)

where
(\Gamma,\gamma)
is defined by (
1.8
),
c_{1}=c_{1}(p,q)>0
, and the convergence holds in
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
\ell\geq 0
.

It is worth mentioning the work of
[
Zaag(2001)
]
where the author obtained a Liouville theorem for system (
1.1
) with the nonlinearity (
1.5
) and
\mu=1
that improves the result in
[
Andreucci et al.(1997)Andreucci, Herrero, and Velázquez
]
. Based on this theorem, he was able to derive sharp estimates of asymptotic behaviors as well as a localization property for blowup solutions.

When system (
1.1
) is considered with the nonlinearity (
1.5
) and the diffusion coefficient
\mu>0
(not necessarily equal to 1), Mahmoudi, Souplet and Tayachi
[
Mahmoudi et al.(2015)Mahmoudi, Souplet, and Tayachi
]
(see also Souplet
[
Souplet(2009)
]
) prove the single point blowup for any radially decreasing, positive and classical solution in a ball. This result improves a result by Friedman and Giga
[
Friedman and Giga(1987)
]
where the method requires a very restrictive conditions
p=q
and
\mu=1
in order to apply the maximum principle to suitable linear combination of the components
u
and
v
. The authors of
[
Mahmoudi et al.(2015)Mahmoudi, Souplet, and Tayachi
]
also derive the lower pointwise estimates on the final blowup profiles:

\text{for all}\;0<|x|\leq\epsilon_{1},\quad|x|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{pq-1}}u(T,x)\geq\epsilon_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad|x|^{\frac{2(q+1)}{pq-1}}v(T,x)\geq\epsilon_{0},

(1.11)

for some
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}>0
. Recently, we establish in
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
the existence of finite time blowup solutions verifying the asymptotic behavior (
1.10
). In particular, we exhibit stable finite time blowup solutions according to the dynamics:

\begin{array}[]{l}u(x,t)\sim\Gamma\left[(T-t)\left(1+\frac{b|x|^{2}}{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}},\\
v(x,t)\sim\gamma\left[(T-t)\left(1+\frac{b|x|^{2}}{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\right)\right]^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}},\end{array}\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T,

where
\Gamma,\gamma
and
b
are positive constants depending on
p,q,\mu
. Moreover, we derive the following sharp description of the final blowup profiles:

u(T,x)\sim\Gamma\left(\frac{b|x|^{2}}{2|\ln|x||}\right)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad v(T,x)\sim\gamma\left(\frac{b|x|^{2}}{2|\ln|x||}\right)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}\quad\text{as}\quad|x|\to 0.

(1.12)

The method we used in
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
is an extension of the technique developed by Merle and Zaag
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
treated for the standard semilinear heat equation

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u.

(1.13)

The analysis in
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
is mainly based on the spectral property of the linearized operator of the form

\mathscr{L}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla+\textup{Id},

whose spectrum has two positive eigenvalues, a null and then infinity many negative eigenvalues. In particular, the method relies on a two step procedure:

- The reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one. This means that controlling the solution in the similarity variables (
1.9
) around the profile reduces to the control of the components corresponding to the two positive eigenvalues.

- Solving the finite dimensional problem thanks to a topological argument based on index theory.

As for system (
1.1
) with the nonlinearity (
1.2
), much less result is known, in particular in the study of the asymptotic behavior of the solution near singularities. To our knowledge, there are no results concerning the blowup behavior even when
\mu=1
. The only known result is due to Souplet and Tayachi
[
Souplet and Tayachi(2016)
]
who follow the strategy of
[
Mahmoudi et al.(2015)Mahmoudi, Souplet, and Tayachi
]
to establish the single point blowup for large classes of radially decreasing solutions. A similar single point blowup result was obtained in
[
Friedman and Giga(1987)
]
under the restrictive condition
\mu=1
. In this paper we exhibit finite time blowup solutions for system (
1.1
) coupled with (
1.2
) and obtain the first complete description of its blowup behavior. More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of blowup solutions for system (
1.1
) with the complete description of its profile)
.

Consider
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. There exists
T>0
such that system (
1.1
) has a solution
(u,v)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
such that:

(i)

e^{qu}
and
e^{pv}
blow up in finite time
T
simultaneously at only one blowup point
a
.

(ii)

\left\|(T-t)e^{qu(x,t)}-\Phi^{*}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left\|(T-t)e^{pv(x,t)}-\Psi^{*}(z)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\dfrac{C}{\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|}},

(1.14)

where
z=\dfrac{x-a}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}}
and the profiles are given by

p\Phi^{*}(z)=q\Psi^{*}(z)=\left(1+b|z|^{2}\right)^{-1}\quad\text{with}\quad b=\frac{1}{2(\mu+1)}.

(1.15)

(iii)\;
for all
x\neq a
,
(u(x,t),v(x,t))\to(u^{*}(x),v^{*}(x))\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\times\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})
with

u^{*}(x)\sim\frac{1}{q}\ln\left(\frac{2b}{p}\frac{|\ln|x-a||}{|x-a|^{2}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad v^{*}(x)\sim\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\frac{2b}{q}\frac{|\ln|x-a||}{|x-a|^{2}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad|x-a|\to 0.

The blowup profile (

Remark 1.5
.

The basic idea behind the stability proof is roughly understood as follows: The linearized operator
\mathscr{H}+Mc
(see (
1.24
) for its definition) has two positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{0}=1,\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
, a zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}=0
, then an infinitely discrete negative spectrum (see Lemma
2.2
below). As usual in the analysis of stability of blowup problems, the component corresponding to
\lambda_{0}=1
has the exponential growth
e^{s}
, which can be eliminated by means of change of the blowup time; and the component corresponding to
\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
has the growth
e^{s/2}
can be eliminated by means of a shifting of the blowup point. As for the neutral, non exponential growth corresponding to
\lambda_{2}=0
, it can be also eliminated as well after a suitable use of the scaling dilation invariance associated to the problem. Hence, the contribution associated to these three modes of the linearized problem can
be assumed to be zero. Since the remaining modes of the linearized problem corresponding to negative spectrum decay exponentially, one derives the stable asymptotic behavior of the corresponding blowup mechanism.

We will not give the proof of Theorem
1.4
because the stability result follows from the reduction to a finite dimensional case as in
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
(see Theorem 2 and its proof in Section 4) with the same argument. Here, we only prove the existence result (Theorem
1.1
) and kindly refer the reader to
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
and
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
for a similar proof of the stability.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us explain in the following the main steps of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For clearness, we divide our explanation in 3 paragraphs below:

- The linearized problem;

- The properties of the linearized operator;

- The decomposition of the solution and the control of the nonlinear gradient terms.

(i) The linearized problem.
Let us start with the change of variables

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi(y,s)=(T-t)e^{qu(x,t)},\quad\Psi(y,s)=(T-t)e^{pv(x,t)},\\
\text{where}\quad y=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\ln(T-t),\end{array}\right.

(1.18)

which transforms system (
1.1
) to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{s}\Phi=\Delta\Phi-\dfrac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\Phi-\Phi+q\Phi\Psi-\dfrac{|\nabla\Phi|^{2}}{\Phi},\\
\partial_{s}\Psi=\mu\Delta\Psi-\dfrac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\Psi-\Psi+p\Phi\Psi-\mu\dfrac{|\nabla\Psi|^{2}}{\Psi},\end{array}\right.

(1.19)

(in comparison with the work of
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
treated for the case where system (
1.1
) is considered with the nonlinearity (
1.5
), we have extra nonlinear gradient terms in (
1.19
), which come from the nonlinear transformation (
1.18
); the nonlinear gradient terms
are the main sources causing serious difficulties in the analysis). The problem then reduces to construct for (
1.19
) a solution
(\Phi,\Psi)
defined for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[s_{0},+\infty)
such that

\left\|\Phi(y,s)-\Phi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\left\|\Psi(y,s)-\Psi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\longrightarrow 0,

(1.20)

as
s\to+\infty
. One may think that it is natural to linearize system (
1.19
) around
(\Phi^{*},\Psi^{*})
, however, the error generated by this approximate profile is too large to allow us to close estimates in our analysis. Following the formal approach given in Section
2.2
below, the good approximate profile is given by

\phi(y,s)=\Phi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{\mu}{p(1+\mu)s}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi(y,s)=\Psi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{1}{q(1+\mu)s},

(1.21)

where the term of order
\frac{1}{s}
appears as a corrective term to minimize the generated error. We then introduce

\Lambda=\Phi-\phi\quad\text{and}\quad\Upsilon=\Psi-\psi,

(1.22)

leading to the system

\partial_{s}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc+V(y,s)\Big)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}+\binom{q}{p}\Lambda\Upsilon+\binom{R_{1}}{R_{2}}+\binom{G_{1}}{G_{2}},

(1.23)

where

\mathscr{H}=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathscr{L}_{1}&0\\
0&\mathscr{L}_{\mu}\end{matrix}\right),\quad Mc=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\frac{q}{p}\\
\frac{p}{q}&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad\mathscr{L}_{\eta}=\eta\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla,

(1.24)

V(y,s)=\begin{pmatrix}q\psi-1&\quad q\big(\phi-1/p\big)\\
p\big(\psi-1/q\big)&\quad p\phi-1\end{pmatrix},

the term
\binom{R_{1}}{R_{2}}
is the generated error which is uniformly bounded by
\frac{C}{s}
(see definition (
3.7
) and Lemma
4.8
below), the nonlinear gradient term
\binom{G_{1}}{G_{2}}
is built to be quadratic (see definition (
3.6
) and Lemma
4.10
below).

(ii) The properties of the linearized operator.
As we will see in Section
3.1
below, the key step towards Theorem
1.1
is the construction of a solution
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
for system (
1.23
) defined for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[s_{0},+\infty)
such that

\|\Lambda(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\Upsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

In view of system (
1.23
), we see that the nonlinear terms and the generated error are small and can be negligible in comparison with the linear term. Therefore, the linear part will play an important role in the dynamic of the solution. As we show in Lemma
2.2
below, the linearized operator
\mathscr{H}+Mc
can be diagonalizable and its spectrum is explicitly given by

\text{spec}\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc\Big)=\left\{\pm 1-\frac{n}{2},n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Depending on the asymptotic behavior of the potential term
V
, the full linear part has two fundamental properties:

- For
|y|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
for some
K_{0}
large, the potential term is considered as a perturbation of the effect of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
.

- For
|y|\geq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
, the linear operator behaves as an operator with fully negative spectrum, which gives the decay of the solution in this region.

(iii) The decomposition of the solution and the control of the nonlinear gradient terms.
While the control of the flow in the region
|y|\geq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
is easy, it is not the case in the inner region, i.e. when
|y|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
. Moreover, the nonlinear gradient terms appearing in (
1.23
) cause serious difficulties in the analysis, and crucial modifications are needed in comparison with the proof in
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
and
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
. The essential idea in our approach is that we introduce estimates in three regions in different variable scales, inspired by the works of
[
Merle and Zaag(1997b)
]
and
[
Ghoul et al.(2017b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
, as follows:

- In the
blowup region

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{|x|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\}
, we carry on our analysis in the similarity variables setting. In particular, the solution
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
is decomposed according to the eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
,

\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\sum_{n=0}^{2}\theta_{n}\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}+\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}},

where
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
is the eigenfunction of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}=1-\frac{n}{2}
; and
\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}}
is the projection of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
on the subspace of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
where the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
is strictly negative. Since the spectrum of the linear part of system satisfied by
(\Lambda_{-},\Upsilon_{-})
(see (
4.24
) below) is negative, it is controllable to zero.

The control of
\theta_{2}
is delicate. In fact, we need to refine the asymptotic behavior of the potential term
V(y,s)
and the nonlinear gradient term
\binom{G_{1}}{G_{2}}
in (
1.23
) to find that

\theta_{2}^{\prime}=-\frac{2}{s}\theta_{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{3}}\right),

which shows a negative spectrum (in the slow variable
\tau=\ln s
), hence, it is controllable to zero as well. Here, we want to remark that the factor
-\frac{2}{s}
and the error
\frac{1}{s^{3}}
are derived thanks to the linearization of system (
1.19
) around the approximate profile
(\phi,\psi)
defined in (
1.21
) with the precise value of the constant
b
introduced in Theorem
1.1
.

As for the control of the positive modes
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
(
reduction to a finite dimensional problem
), we use a basic topological argument to show the existence of initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
depending on
(N+1)
parameters (see definition (
3.23
) below) such that the corresponding modes
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
are controlled to zero.

- In the
intermediate region

\mathcal{D}_{2}=\{x|\;K_{0}/4\sqrt{(T-t)|\ln(T-t)|}\leq|x|\leq\epsilon_{0}\}
, we use classical parabolic regularity estimates on
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
, a rescaled version of
(u,v)
(see definition
3.17
below). Roughly speaking, we show that in this region the solution behaves like the solution of the associated ordinary differential system to (
1.1
). The analysis in this region also gives the final blowup profile as described in part
(iii)
of Theorem
1.1
.

- In the
regular region

\mathcal{D}_{3}=\{|x|\geq\epsilon_{0}/4\}
, we directly control the solution thanks to the local in time well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for system (
1.1
).

We would like to remark that in
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
and
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
, the authors introduce the estimates in the region
|y|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
and the regular region
|y|\geq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
. However, the estimates in the region
|y|\geq K_{0}\sqrt{s}
imply the smallness of
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
only, and do not allow any control of the nonlinear gradient terms in this region. In other words, the analysis based on the method of
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
and
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
, that is to estimate the solution in the
z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}
variable is not sufficient and must be improved. By introducing additional estimates in the regions
\mathcal{D}_{2}
and
\mathcal{D}_{3}
, we are able to achieve the full control of the nonlinear gradient term, then, complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows:

- In section
2
, we first derive the basic properties of the linearized operator
\mathscr{H}+Mc
, then, we give a formal explanation on the derivation of the blowup profile
(\Phi^{*},\Psi^{*})
by means of the spectral analysis. This formal approach also gives an approximate profile to be linearized around.

- In Section
3
, we give the main arguments of the proof of Theorem
1.1
and postpone most of technicalities to next sections. Interested readers can find in Subsection
3.2
a particular definition of a shrinking set to trap the solution of (
1.1
) according to the blowup regime described in Theorem
1.1
. They also find a basic topological argument for the finite dimensional problem at page
3.4
.

- In Section
4
, we give the proof of Proposition
3.6
, which gives the reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one. This is the central part in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
A formal approach through a spectral analysis of the linearized operator.

In this section we follow the idea of Bricmont and Kupiaien
[
Bricmont and Kupiainen(1994)
]
treated for the semilinear heat equation in order to formally derive the blowup profile described in (
1.14
). The argument is mainly based on a spectral analysis of the linearized operator and a matching asymptotic expansion.

2.1.
Spectral properties of the linearized operator.

In this part we recall some well-known properties of the linear operator
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
from which we derive spectral properties of the linear operator
\mathscr{H}+Mc
introduced in (
1.24
).

\bullet

Spectral properties of
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
:
Let
\eta>0
, we consider the weighted space
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
the set of all
f\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\|f\|^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}=\big<f,f\big>_{\rho_{\eta}}<+\infty,

where the inner product is defined by

\big<f,g\big>_{\rho_{\eta}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)g(y)\rho_{\eta}(y)dy\quad\text{with}\quad\rho_{\eta}(y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\eta)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4\eta^{2}}}.

(2.1)

Note that the linear operator
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
can be written in the divergence form

\mathscr{L}_{\eta}v=\frac{\eta}{\rho_{\eta}}\;\text{div}\,\Big(\rho_{\eta}\nabla v\Big),

which shows that
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
is self-adjoint with respect to the weight
\rho_{\eta}
, i.e.

\forall v,w\in L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\mathscr{L}_{\eta}w\rho_{\eta}dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w\mathscr{L}_{\eta}v\rho_{\eta}dy.

(2.2)

For each
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, we set

\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(y)=c_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{N}H_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{y_{i}}{2\sqrt{\eta}}\right),

where
H_{n}
is the one dimensional Hermite polynomial defined by

H_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(e^{-x^{2}}),

(2.3)

and
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
is the normalization constant chosen so that the term of highest degree in
\tilde{h}_{\alpha}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
. In the one dimensional case, we have

\tilde{h}_{n}(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}c_{n,j}\eta^{j}y^{n-2j}\quad\text{with}\quad c_{n,j}=(-1)^{j}\frac{n!}{(n-2j)!j!}.

(2.4)

The first four terms are explicitly given by

\tilde{h}_{0}=1,\quad\tilde{h}_{1}=y,\quad\tilde{h}_{2}=y^{2}-2\eta,

\tilde{h}_{3}=y^{3}-6\eta y,\quad\tilde{h}_{4}=y^{4}-12\eta y^{2}+12\eta^{2}.

The family of eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
generates an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, i.e. for any different
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\mathbb{N}^{N}
,

\mathscr{L}_{\eta}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}=-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\tilde{h}_{\alpha},\quad|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{N},

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(y)\tilde{h}_{\beta}(y)\rho_{\eta}(y)dy=0,

(2.5)

and that for any
f
in
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, one can decompose

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\big<f,\tilde{h}_{\alpha}\big>_{\rho_{\eta}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}f_{\alpha}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}.

Remark 2.1
.

For any polynomial
P_{n}(y)
of degree
n
, we have by (
2.5
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}P_{n}(y)\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(y)\rho_{\eta}(y)dy=0\quad\text{if}\;\;|\alpha|\geq n+1.

\bullet

Spectral properties of
\mathscr{H}
: Let us consider the functional space
L^{2}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, which is the set of all
\binom{f}{g}\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
such that

\left<\binom{f}{g},\binom{f}{g}\right><+\infty,

where

\left<\binom{f_{1}}{g_{1}},\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}}\right>:=\big<f_{1},f_{2}\big>_{\rho_{1}}+\big<g_{1},g_{2}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}.

If we introduce for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
,

h_{\alpha}(y)=a_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{N}H_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{y_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{h}_{\alpha}(y)=\hat{a}_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{N}H_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{y_{i}}{2\sqrt{\mu}}\right),

(2.6)

where
H_{n}
is defined by (
2.3
), and
a_{\alpha}
and
\hat{a}_{\alpha}
are constants chosen so that the terms of highest degree in
h_{\alpha}
and
\hat{h}_{\alpha}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{N}y^{\alpha_{i}}
, then

\mathscr{H}\binom{h_{\alpha}}{0}=-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\binom{h_{\alpha}}{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{H}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{\alpha}}=-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{\alpha}}.

(2.7)

Moreover, for each
\binom{f}{g}
in
L^{2}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle\binom{f}{g}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\big<f,h_{\alpha}\big>_{\rho_{1}}\binom{h_{\alpha}}{0}+\big<g,\hat{h}_{\alpha}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{\alpha}}.

\bullet

Spectral properties of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
: In this part we derive a basis where
\mathscr{H}+Mc
is diagonal. More precisely, we have the following lemma whose proof follows from an explicit computation.

Lemma 2.2

(Diagonalization of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
in the one dimensional case)
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist polynomials
f_{n},g_{n},\tilde{f}_{n}
and
\tilde{g}_{n}
of degree
n
such that

\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc\Big)\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}=\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},

(2.8)

and

\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc\Big)\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}=-\left(1+\frac{n}{2}\right)\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}},

(2.9)

where

\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}d_{n,n-2j}\binom{h_{n-2j}}{0}+e_{n,n-2j}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n-2j}},

(2.10)

\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}\tilde{d}_{n,n-2j}\binom{h_{n-2j}}{0}+\tilde{e}_{n,n-2j}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n-2j}},

(2.11)

and the coefficients
d_{n,n-2j},e_{n,n-2j}
,
\tilde{d}_{n,n-2j}
,
\tilde{e}_{n,n-2j}
depend on the parameters
p,q
and
\mu
. In particular, we have

\binom{d_{n,n}}{e_{n,n}}=\binom{q}{p},\quad\binom{d_{n,n-2}}{e_{n,n-2}}=n(n-1)(\mu-1)\binom{-q}{p}.

(2.12)

and

\binom{\tilde{d}_{n,n}}{\tilde{e}_{n,n}}=\binom{q}{-p},\quad\binom{\tilde{d}_{n,n-2}}{\tilde{e}_{n,n-2}}=\frac{1}{3}n(n-1)(1-\mu)\binom{q}{p}.

(2.13)

Remark 2.3
.

Lemma (
2.2
) also holds in higher dimensions with some complication in the computation. Here, we remark that the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
has only two positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{0}=1
and
\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
corresponding to the eigenvectors
\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}
and
\binom{f_{1}}{g_{1}}
; a zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}=0
corresponding to the eigenvector
\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}}
. In the two dimensional case, we have

\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}=\binom{q}{p},\quad\binom{f_{1}}{g_{1}}=\binom{qy_{i}}{py_{i}}_{1\leq i\leq N},

and

\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}}=\binom{f_{2,ij}}{g_{2,ij}}_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

where

\binom{f_{2,ij}}{g_{2,ij}}=\binom{f_{2,ji}}{g_{2,ji}}=\binom{qy_{i}y_{j}}{py_{i}y_{j}}\quad\text{for}\;1\leq i\neq j\leq N,

and

\binom{f_{2,ii}}{g_{2,ii}}=\binom{q(y_{i}^{2}-2\mu)}{p(y_{i}^{2}-2)}\quad\text{for}\;1\leq i\leq N.

(2.14)

The following lemma gives the definition of the projection on the modes
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
.

Lemma 2.4

(Definition of the projection on the directions
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
)
.

Let
M\gg 1
be an even integer and let
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
be of the form

\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\sum_{n\leq M}\omega_{n}\binom{h_{n}}{0}+\hat{\omega}_{n}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}.

(2.15)

Then we can expand
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
with respect to the basis
\left\{\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}\right\}_{n\leq M}
as follows:

\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\sum_{n\leq M}\theta_{n}\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}+\tilde{\theta}_{n}\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}},

(2.16)

where

\theta_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{M-n}{2}\right]}A_{n+2j,n}\,\omega_{n+2j}+B_{n+2j,n}\,\hat{\omega}_{n+2j}:=\mathbb{P}_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(2.17)

and

\tilde{\theta}_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{M-n}{2}\right]}\tilde{A}_{n+2j,n}\,\omega_{n+2j}+\tilde{B}_{n+2j,n}\,\hat{\omega}_{n+2j}:=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(2.18)

with the coefficients
A_{n+2j,n}
,
B_{n+2j,n}
,
\tilde{A}_{n+2j,n}
and
\tilde{B}_{n+2j,n}
for
j=0,1,2,\cdots
depending on
p,q
and
\mu
. In particular, we have

A_{n,n}=\frac{1}{2q},\quad B_{n,n}=\frac{1}{2p},

(2.19)

and

A_{n+2,n}=\frac{1}{6q}(n+2)(n+1)(\mu-1),\quad B_{n+2,n}=\frac{1}{6p}(n+2)(n+1)(1-\mu).

(2.20)

Proof.

Since the proof is exactly the same lines as the one written in
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
and since it is purely computational, we kindly refer interested readers to Lemma 3.4 of
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
for an analogous proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

From Lemma
2.4
, we obviously see that when a function is of the form
\sum_{n=0}^{M}\theta_{n}\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}+\tilde{\theta}_{n}\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
, its projections on
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
are respectively
\theta_{n}
and
\tilde{\theta}_{n}
.

2.2.
A formal approach.

In this part we make use the spectral properties of the linear operator
\mathscr{H}+Mc
given in the previous subsection to formally derive the profile described in Theorem
1.1
. For simplicity, we assume that
(u,v)
is a positive, radially symmetric solution of system (
1.1
) in the one dimensional case. By the translation invariance in space, we assume that
(u,v)
blows up in finite time
T>0
at the origin. Let us start with the nonlinear transformation

\bar{u}=e^{qu}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{v}=e^{pv},

(2.21)

which leads to the new system

\partial_{t}\bar{u}=\Delta\bar{u}-\frac{|\nabla\bar{u}|^{2}}{\bar{u}}+q\bar{u}\bar{v},\qquad\partial_{t}\bar{v}=\mu\Delta\bar{v}-\mu\frac{|\nabla\bar{v}|^{2}}{\bar{v}}+p\bar{u}\bar{v}.

(2.22)

We then introduce the similarity variables

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi(y,s)=(T-t)\bar{u}(x,t),\quad\Psi(y,s)=(T-t)\bar{v}(x,t),\\
\text{where}\quad y=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\log(T-t),\end{array}\right.

(2.23)

which shows that
(\Phi,\Psi)
solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{s}\Phi=\Delta\Phi-\dfrac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\Phi-\Phi-\dfrac{|\nabla\Phi|^{2}}{\Phi}+q\Phi\Psi,\\
\partial_{s}\Psi=\mu\Delta\Psi-\dfrac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\Psi-\Psi-\mu\dfrac{|\nabla\Psi|^{2}}{\Psi}+p\Phi\Psi.\end{array}\right.

(2.24)

In the similarity variables (
2.23
), justifying (
1.14
) is equivalent to show that

\Phi(y,s)\sim\Phi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi(y,s)\sim\Psi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

(2.25)

Note that the nonzero constant solution to system (
2.24
) is
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)
. This suggests the linearization

\bar{\Phi}=\Phi-\frac{1}{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\Psi}=\Psi-\frac{1}{q},

(2.26)

and
(\bar{\Phi},\bar{\Psi})
solves the system

\partial_{s}\binom{\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Psi}}=\left(\mathscr{H}+Mc\right)\binom{\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Psi}}+\binom{q}{p}\bar{\Phi}\bar{\Psi}-\binom{|\nabla\bar{\Phi}|^{2}\left(\bar{\Phi}+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}}{\mu|\nabla\bar{\Psi}|^{2}\left(\bar{\Psi}+\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-1}},

(2.27)

where
\mathscr{H}
and
Mc
are defined by (
1.24
).

From Lemma
2.2
, we know that
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}_{n\geq 3}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
correspond to negative eigenvalues of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
, therefore, we may consider that

\binom{\bar{\Phi}}{\bar{\Psi}}=\theta_{0}(s)\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}+\theta_{2}(s)\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}},

(2.28)

where
|\theta_{0}(s)|+|\theta_{2}(s)|\to 0
as
s\to+\infty
(note that
\theta_{1}(s)\equiv 0
by the radially symmetric assumption). Plugging this ansatz in system (
2.27
) yields

\displaystyle\theta_{0}^{\prime}\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}+\theta_{2}^{\prime}\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}}=\theta_{0}\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}

\displaystyle+\binom{q}{p}\left[(\theta_{0}f_{0}+\theta_{2}f_{2})(\theta_{0}g_{0}+\theta_{2}g_{2})\right]

\displaystyle-\theta_{2}^{2}\binom{|\nabla f_{2}|^{2}\left(\theta_{0}f_{0}+\theta_{2}f_{2}+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}}{\mu|\nabla g_{2}|^{2}\left(\theta_{0}g_{0}+\theta_{2}g_{2}+\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-1}}.

Assume that
|\theta_{0}(s)|\ll|\theta_{2}(s)|
as
s\to+\infty
, we then use Lemma
2.4
to find the ordinary differential system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{0}^{\prime}&=\theta_{0}+\mathcal{O}(|\theta_{2}|^{2}),\\
\theta_{2}^{\prime}&=c_{2}\theta_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(|\theta_{2}|^{3}+|\theta_{0}\theta_{2}|+|\theta_{0}|^{3}),\end{array}\right.

(2.29)

where the constant
c_{2}
is computed as follows:

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{2,M}\binom{qf_{2}g_{2}-p|\nabla f_{2}|^{2}}{pf_{2}g_{2}-q\mu|\nabla g_{2}|^{2}}=\mathbb{P}_{2,M}\binom{pq^{2}\big[h_{4}+(6-2\mu)h_{2}+12\big]}{p^{2}q\big[\hat{h}_{4}+(6\mu-2)\hat{h}_{2}\big]}

\displaystyle=A_{2,2}(6-2\mu)pq^{2}+B_{2,2}(6\mu-2)p^{2}q+A_{4,2}pq^{2}+B_{4,2}pq^{2}

\displaystyle=2pq(\mu+1).

Solving system (
2.29
) yields

\theta_{2}(s)=-\frac{1}{2pq(\mu+1)s}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln s}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad|\theta_{0}(s)|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

From (
2.28
) and (
2.26
), we have just derived the following asymptotic expansion:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(y,s)&=\frac{1}{p}\left[1-\frac{y^{2}}{2(\mu+1)s}+\frac{\mu}{(\mu+1)s}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln s}{s^{2}}\right),\\
\Psi(y,s)&=\frac{1}{q}\left[1-\frac{y^{2}}{2(\mu+1)s}+\frac{1}{(\mu+1)s}\right]+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\ln s}{s^{2}}\right),\end{array}\right.

(2.30)

where the convergence takes place in
L^{2}_{\rho_{1}}\times L^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}
as well as uniformly on compact sets by standard parabolic regularity.

These expansions provide a relevant variable for blowup, namely
z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}
, therefore, we try to search formally solutions of (
2.24
) of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(y,s)&=\Phi_{0}(z)+\frac{\mu}{p(\mu+1)s}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{1+\nu}}\right),\\
\Psi(y,s)&=\Psi_{0}(z)+\frac{1}{q(\mu+1)s}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{s^{1+\nu}}\right),\end{array}\right.

(2.31)

for some
\nu>0
, subject to the condition

\Phi_{0}(0)=\frac{1}{p},\quad\Psi_{0}(0)=\frac{1}{q}.

(2.32)

Plugging this ansatz in system (
2.24
), keeping only the main order, we end up with the following system satisfied by
(\Phi_{0},\Psi_{0})
:

\displaystyle-\frac{z}{2}\Phi^{\prime}_{0}-\Phi_{0}+q\Phi_{0}\Psi_{0}=0,\quad-\frac{z}{2}\Psi^{\prime}_{0}-\Psi_{0}+p\Phi_{0}\Psi_{0}=0.

(2.33)

Solving this system with the condition (
2.32
) yields

\Phi_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{p}(1+c_{0}|z|^{2})^{-1},\quad\Psi_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{q}(1+c_{0}|z|^{2})^{-1},

for some constant
c_{0}>0
.
By matching asymptotic this expansion with (
2.30
), we find that

c_{0}=\frac{1}{2(\mu+1)}.

In conclusion, we have formally obtained from (
2.31
) the following candidate for the profile:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(y,s)&\sim\phi(y,s):=\frac{1}{p}\left(1+\frac{y^{2}}{2(\mu+1)s}\right)^{-1}+\frac{\mu}{p(\mu+1)s},\\
\Psi(y,s)&\sim\psi(y,s):=\frac{1}{q}\left(1+\frac{y^{2}}{2(\mu+1)s}\right)^{-1}+\frac{1}{q(\mu+1)s}.\end{array}\right.

(2.34)

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
without technical details.

In this section we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To avoid winding up with details, we will only give the main arguments of the proof and postpone most of technicalities to next sections. For simplicity, we consider the one dimensional case (
N=1
), however, the proof remains the same for higher dimensions
N\geq 2
.

Hereafter we denote by
C
a generic positive constant depending only on the parameters of the problem such as
N,p,q,\mu
and
K
introduced in (
3.8
).

3.1.
Linearization of the problem.

In this part we give the formulation of the problem to justify the formal result obtained in previous section, i.e. the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We want to prove the existence of suitable initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
so that the corresponding solution
(u,v)
of system (
1.1
) blows up in finite time
T
only at one point
a\in\mathbb{R}
and verifies (
1.14
). From translation invariance of equation (
1.1
), we may assume that
a=0
. Through the transformations (
2.21
) and (
2.23
), we want to find
s_{0}>0
and
(\Phi(y,s_{0}),\Psi(y,s_{0}))
such that the solution
(\Phi,\Psi)
of system (
2.24
) with initial data
(\Phi(y,s_{0}),\Psi(y,s_{0}))
satisfies

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\left\|\Phi(y,s)-\Phi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\left\|\Psi(y,s)-\Psi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0,

(3.1)

where
\Phi^{*}
and
\Psi^{*}
are defined in (
1.15
).

According to the formal analysis in the previous section, let us introduce
\Lambda(y,s)
and
\Upsilon(y,s)
such that

\Phi(y,s)=\Lambda(y,s)+\phi(y,s),\quad\Psi(y,s)=\Upsilon(y,s)+\psi(y,s),

(3.2)

where
\phi
and
\psi
are defined by (
2.34
).

With the introduction of
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
in (
3.2
), the problem is then reduced to construct functions
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
such that

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\|\Lambda(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\|\Upsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0.

(3.3)

and from (
2.24
),
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
solves the system

\partial_{s}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc+V(y,s)\Big)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}+\binom{q}{p}\Lambda\Upsilon+\binom{R_{1}}{R_{2}}+\binom{G_{1}}{G_{2}},

(3.4)

where
\mathscr{H}
and
Mc
are defined by (
1.24
),

V(y,s)=\begin{pmatrix}q\psi-1&\quad q\big(\phi-1/p\big)\\
p\big(\psi-1/q\big)&\quad p\phi-1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}&V_{2}\\
V_{3}&V_{4}\end{pmatrix},

(3.5)

\binom{G_{1}}{G_{2}}=\binom{-|\nabla(\Lambda+\phi)|^{2}(\Lambda+\phi)^{-1}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}/\phi}{-\mu|\nabla(\Upsilon+\psi)|^{2}(\Upsilon+\psi)^{-1}+\mu|\nabla\psi|^{2}/\psi},

(3.6)

and

\binom{R_{1}}{R_{2}}=\binom{-\partial_{s}\phi+\Delta\phi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\phi-\phi+q\phi\psi-|\nabla\phi|^{2}/\phi}{-\partial_{s}\psi+\mu\Delta\psi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\psi-\psi+p\phi\psi-\mu|\nabla\psi|^{2}/\psi}.

(3.7)

Since we would like to make
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
go to zero as
s\to+\infty
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then the nonlinear terms
\binom{q}{p}\Lambda\Upsilon
and
\binom{G_{1}}{G_{2}}
, which are built to be quadratic, can be neglected. The error term
\binom{R_{1}}{R_{2}}
is of the size
\frac{1}{s}
uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Thus, the dynamics of (
3.4
) are strongly influenced by the linear part

\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc+V(y,s)\Big)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

The spectrum of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
is well studied in the previous section. The potential
V(y,s)
has two fundamental properties that will strongly influence our analysis:

- The effect of
V
inside the
blowup region

|y|\leq K\sqrt{s}
will be considered as a perturbation of the effect of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
.

- Outside the
blowup region
, i.e. when
|y|\geq K\sqrt{s}
, we have the following property: for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist
K_{\epsilon}>0
and
s_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\sup_{s\geq s_{\epsilon},|y|\geq K_{\epsilon}\sqrt{s}}|V(y,s)|\leq\epsilon.

In other words, outside the
blowup region
, the linear operator
\mathscr{H}+Mc+V
behaves as

\mathscr{H}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\pm\epsilon-1&\pm\epsilon\\
\pm\epsilon&\pm\epsilon-1\end{array}\right).

Given that the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}
is non positive (see (
2.7
) above) and that the matrix has negative eigenvalues for
\epsilon
small, we see that
\mathscr{H}+Mc+V
behaves like one with a fully negative spectrum, which greatly simplifies the analysis in that region.

Since the behavior of the potential
V
inside and outside the
blowup region
is different, we will consider the dynamics for
|y|\geq K\sqrt{s}
and
|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}
separately for some
K
to be fixed large. Let us consider a non-increasing cut-off function
\chi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}([0,+\infty))
, with
\text{supp}(\chi_{0})\subset[0,2]
and
\chi_{0}\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
, and introduce

\chi(y,s)=\chi_{0}\left(\frac{|y|}{K_{0}\sqrt{s}}\right),

(3.8)

where
K_{0}
is chosen large enough so that various technical estimates hold. We define

\binom{\Lambda_{e}}{\Upsilon_{e}}=(1-\chi)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(3.9)

\binom{\Lambda_{e}}{\Upsilon_{e}}
coincides with
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
for
|y|\geq 2K_{0}\sqrt{s}
. As announced a few lines above and as we will see in Section
4.1.3
, the spectrum of the linear operator of the equation
satisfied by
\binom{\Lambda_{e}}{\Upsilon_{e}}
is negative, which makes the control of
\|\Lambda_{e}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\|\Upsilon_{e}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
easy.

While the control of the outer part is simple, it is not the case for the inner part of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
, i.e. for
|y|\leq 2K_{0}\sqrt{s}
. In fact, inside the
blowup region

|y|\leq 2K_{0}\sqrt{s}
, the potential
V
can be seen as a perturbation of the effect of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
whose spectrum has two positive eigenvalues, a zero eigenvalue in addition to infinitely negative ones (see Lemma
2.2
above). For the sake of controlling
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
in the region
|y|\leq 2K_{0}\sqrt{s}
, we will expand
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
with respect to the family
\left\{\binom{h_{n}}{0},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
and then with respect to the family
\left\{\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
as follows:

\displaystyle\binom{\Lambda(y,s)}{\Upsilon(y,s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq M}Q_{n}(s)\binom{h_{n}(y)}{0}+\hat{Q}_{n}(s)\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}(y)}+\binom{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)},

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq M}\theta_{n}(s)\binom{f_{n}(y)}{g_{n}(y)}+\tilde{\theta}_{n}(s)\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}(y)}{\tilde{g}_{n}(y)}+\binom{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)}.

(3.11)

where
M
is a fixed even integer satisfying

M\geq 4\left[\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\|V_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{y,s}}\right],

(3.12)

with
\|V_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{y,s}}=\max\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},s\geq 1}|V_{i}(y,s)|
.

\bullet\;

Q_{n}(s)
and
\hat{Q}_{n}(s)
are respectively the projections of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
on
\binom{h_{n}}{0}
and
\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}
defined by

Q_{n}(s)=\frac{\left<\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},\binom{h_{n}}{0}\right>}{\left<\binom{h_{n}}{0},\binom{h_{n}}{0}\right>}=\frac{\big<\Lambda,h_{n}\big>_{\rho_{1}}}{\big<h_{n},h_{n}\big>^{2}_{\rho_{1}}}\equiv\Pi_{n}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(3.13)

\hat{Q}_{n}(s)=\frac{\left<\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right>}{\left<\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right>}=\frac{\big<\Upsilon,\hat{h}_{n}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}}{\big<\hat{h}_{n},\hat{h}_{n}\big>^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}}\equiv\hat{\Pi}_{n}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(3.14)

\bullet\;

\binom{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)}=\Pi_{-,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
denotes the infinite-dimensional part of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
, where
\Pi_{-,M}
is the projector on the subspace of
L_{\rho_{1}}\times L_{\rho_{\mu}}
where the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}
is lower than
\frac{1-M}{2}
. We have the orthogonality: for all
n\leq M
,

\left<\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}},\binom{h_{n}}{0}\right>=\big<\Lambda_{-},h_{n}\big>_{\rho_{1}}=0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\left<\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right>=\big<\Upsilon_{-},\hat{h}_{n}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}=0.

(3.15)

\bullet\;
We set
\Pi_{+,M}=\textbf{Id}-\Pi_{-,M}
, and the complementary part

\binom{\Lambda_{+}}{\Upsilon_{+}}=\Pi_{+,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}-\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}}

which satisfies for all
s
,

\left<\binom{\Lambda_{+}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{+}(y,s)},\binom{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)}\right>=0.

(3.16)

\bullet\;

\theta_{n}(s)=\mathbb{P}_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
and
\tilde{\theta}_{n}(s)=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
are respectively projections of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
on
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
. From Lemma
2.4
, we can express
\theta_{n}(s)
and
\tilde{\theta}_{n}(s)
in terms of
Q_{n}(s)
and
\hat{Q}_{n}(s)
.

3.2.
Definition of the shrinking set and its properties.

In this part we will give the definition of a shrinking set to trap the solution according to the blowup regime described in Theorem
1.1
. In particular, we aim at defining a set whose elements will satisfy (
3.3
). To do so, we follow ideas of
[
Ghoul et al.(2016b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
and
[
Ghoul et al.(2017b)Ghoul, Nguyen, and Zaag
]
where the authors suggested a modification of the argument of
[
Merle and Zaag(1997a)
]
for the standard semilinear heat equation (
1.13
). In particular, we shall control the solution in three different zones covering
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, defined as follows: For
K_{0}>0
,
\epsilon_{0}>0
and
t\in[0,T)
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\;\Big|\;|x|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|(T-t)}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv\left\{x\;\big|\;|y|\leq K_{0}\sqrt{s}\right\}\equiv\left\{x\;\Big|\;|z|\leq K_{0}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\;\Big|\;\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{|\ln(T-t)|(T-t)}\leq|x|\leq\epsilon_{0}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\equiv\left\{x\;\Big|\;\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{s}\leq|y|\leq\epsilon_{0}e^{\frac{s}{2}}\right\}\equiv\left\{x\;\Big|\;\frac{K_{0}}{4}\leq|z|\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{\sqrt{s}}e^{\frac{s}{2}}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\;\Big|\;|x|\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4}\right\}\equiv\left\{x\;\big|\;|y|\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4}e^{\frac{s}{2}}\right\}\equiv\left\{x\;\Big|\;|z|\geq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{4\sqrt{s}}e^{\frac{s}{2}}\right\}.

- In the
blowup region

\mathcal{D}_{1}
, we work with the self similar system (
3.4
) and do an analysis according to the decomposition (
3.11
) and the definition (
3.9
).

- In the intermediate region
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, we control the solution by using classical parabolic estimates on
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
, a rescaled version of
(u,v)
defined for
x\neq 0
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{u}(x,\xi,\tau)=\frac{1}{q}\ln\sigma(x)+u\Big(x+\xi\sqrt{\sigma(x)},t(x)+\tau\sigma(x)\Big),\\
\tilde{v}(x,\xi,\tau)=\frac{1}{p}\ln\sigma(x)+v\Big(x+\xi\sqrt{\sigma(x)},t(x)+\tau\sigma(x)\Big),\end{array}\right.

(3.17)

where
t(x)
is uniquely defined for
|x|
sufficiently small by

|x|=\frac{K_{0}}{4}\sqrt{\sigma(x)|\ln\sigma(x)|}\quad\text{with}\quad\sigma(x)=T-t(x).

(3.18)

From (
1.1
), we see that
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
satisfies the same system for
(u,v)
. That is for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\tau\in\left[-\frac{t(x)}{\sigma(x)},1\right)
,

\partial_{\tau}\tilde{u}=\Delta_{\xi}\tilde{u}+e^{p\tilde{v}},\quad\partial_{\tau}\tilde{v}=\mu\Delta_{\xi}\tilde{v}+e^{q\tilde{u}}.

(3.19)

We will in fact prove that
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
behaves for

|\xi|\leq\alpha_{0}\sqrt{|\ln\sigma(x)|}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau\in\left[\frac{t_{0}-t(x)}{\sigma(x)},1\right)

for some
t_{0}<T
and
\alpha_{0}>0
, like the solution of the ordinary differential system

\partial_{\tau}\hat{u}=e^{p\hat{v}},\quad\partial_{\tau}\hat{v}=e^{q\hat{u}},

(3.20)

subject to the initial data

\hat{u}(0)=-\frac{1}{q}\ln\left[p\left(1+\frac{K_{0}^{2}/16}{2(\mu+1)}\right)\right],\quad\hat{v}(0)=-\frac{1}{p}\ln\left[q\left(1+\frac{K_{0}^{2}/16}{2(\mu+1)}\right)\right].

The solution is explicitly given by

\hat{u}(\tau)=-\frac{1}{q}\ln\left[p\left(1-\tau+\frac{K_{0}^{2}/16}{2(\mu+1)}\right)\right],\quad\hat{v}(\tau)=-\frac{1}{p}\ln\left[q\left(1-\tau+\frac{K_{0}^{2}/16}{2(\mu+1)}\right)\right].

(3.21)

As we will see that the analysis in
\mathcal{D}_{2}
will imply the conclusion of item
(iii)
of Theorem
1.1
.

- In
\mathcal{D}_{3}
, we directly estimate
(u,v)
by using the local in time well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for system (
1.1
).

We give the definition of the shrinking set to trap the solution according to the blowup regime described in Theorem
1.1
. This set is precisely defined as follows:

Definition 3.1

such that

(i)\;
(
Control in the blowup region
\mathcal{D}_{1}

Remark 3.2
.

In comparison with the shrinking set defined in

for some positive constant

(Properties of elements belonging to

.

t_{0,2}(K_{0},\epsilon_{0})

(u,v)(x,t_{0})

We can choose parameters

The proof of this proposition follows from the general idea developed in
[

]
. We proceed in two steps:

- In the first step, we reduce the problem of controlling

in

- In the second step, we use a classical topological argument based on index theory to solve the finite dimensional problem.

Step 1: Reduction to a finite dimensional problem.

In this step, we show through

) in the

\mathcal{D}_{1}
together with classical parabolic techniques for the analysis in the intermediate and regular regions

and
\mathcal{D}_{3}

Proposition 3.6

(u,v)(t)

\epsilon_{0}

\alpha_{0}

\delta_{0}

t(x)

-\ln(T-t(x))\sim-2\ln|x|,\quad T-t(x)=\frac{16}{K_{0}^{2}}\frac{|x|^{2}}{|\ln(T-t(x))|}\sim\frac{16}{K_{0}^{2}}\frac{|x|^{2}}{2|\ln|x||}\quad\text{for}\;|x|\to 0.

This yields

u^{*}(x)\sim-\frac{1}{q}\ln\left(\frac{p|x|^{2}}{4(\mu+1)|\ln|x||}\right)\quad\text{as}\;\;|x|\to 0.

Similarly, we obtain

v^{*}(x)\sim-\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\frac{q|x|^{2}}{4(\mu+1)|\ln|x||}\right)\quad\text{as}\;\;|x|\to 0.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming that Proposition
3.6
holds.

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{i=0}^{M}\alpha_{i}y^{i}+\Lambda_{-}\right]\left[\sum_{j=0}^{M}\beta_{j}y^{j}+\Upsilon-\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j=0}^{M}\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}y^{i+j}+\mathcal{O}\left(A^{2(M+1)}s^{-\frac{M+3}{2}}(|y|^{M+1}+1)\right),\quad\forall|y|\leq\sqrt{s},

of Lemma

. This ends the proof of Lemma

For the error term, we use Lemma

Lemma 4.14

(Estimate for

The functions

\quad\left\|\frac{\Pi_{-,M}\big[R_{i}(y,s)\big]}{1+|y|^{M+1}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{C}{s^{\frac{M+3}{2}}}.

Proof.

We now turn to the estimate for the nonlinear gradient term. We claim the following:

Lemma 4.15

(Estimates for
\Pi_{-,M}\binom{G_{1}}{G_{2}}

Under the assumption of Lemma

\text{for}\;\;i=1,2,\quad\left\|\frac{\Pi_{-,M}\big[G_{i}(y,s)\big]}{1+|y|^{M+1}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{C(A)}{s^{\frac{M+3}{2}}}.

We only deal with the

follows similarly. From (

4.16

For

, we recall that for all polynomial functions
f(y)
of degree

\Pi_{-,M}f(y)=0
. Hence, the conclusion follows once we show that there exists a polynomial function

In particular, we take

G_{1,M}=\Pi_{+,M}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{1}{j!}\left[D_{j}\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{\phi^{j+1}}\Lambda^{j}+D_{j-1}\frac{2\nabla\Lambda\cdot\nabla\phi}{\phi^{j}}\Lambda^{j-1}+D_{j-2}\frac{2|\nabla\Lambda|^{2}}{\phi^{j-2}}\Lambda^{j-2}\right]\right\}.

. Hence, for

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{1}{j!}\left[D_{j}\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{\phi^{j+1}}\Lambda^{j}+D_{j-1}\frac{2\nabla\Lambda\cdot\nabla\phi}{\phi^{j}}\Lambda^{j-1}+D_{j-2}\frac{2|\nabla\Lambda|^{2}}{\phi^{j-2}}\Lambda^{j-2}\right]-G_{1,M}\right|

for all

. This implies that for

\displaystyle\frac{|\Lambda|^{M+1}}{s}+\frac{|y|^{2}}{s^{2}}|\Lambda|^{M}+|\Lambda|^{M-1}|\nabla\Lambda|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(A)\left(s^{-\frac{M}{2}-1}+s^{-\frac{M-1}{2}-1}+s^{-\frac{M-2}{2}-1}\right)\frac{\ln s}{s^{2}}(|y|^{M+1}+1)

This concludes the proof of (

4.15

∎

Proposition 4.16

(

\mathcal{D}_{2}
)
.

There exists
K_{0,3}>0
such that for all

,
\delta_{1}\leq 1
,
\xi_{0}\gg 1
and
C_{0,1}^{*}>0
,
C_{0,2}^{*}>0
, we have the following property: Assume that
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
is a solution to the system

\partial_{\tau}\tilde{u}=\Delta\tilde{u}+e^{p\tilde{v}},\quad\partial_{\tau}\tilde{v}=\Delta\tilde{v}+e^{q\tilde{u}},

(4.26)

for
\tau\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
with
0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq 1
. Assume in addition, for all
\tau\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
,

(i)

for all
|\xi|\leq 2\xi_{0}
,

|\tilde{u}(\xi,\tau_{1})-\hat{u}(\tau_{1})|+|\tilde{v}(\xi,\tau_{1})-\hat{v}(\tau_{1})|\leq\delta_{1},\quad|\nabla\tilde{u}(\xi,\tau_{1})|+|\nabla\tilde{v}(\xi,\tau_{1})|\leq\frac{C_{0,1}^{*}}{\xi_{0}},

where
\hat{u}(\tau)
and
\hat{v}(\tau)
are given by (
3.21
),

(ii)

for all
|\xi|\leq\frac{7}{4}\xi_{0}
,
|\nabla\tilde{u}(\xi,\tau)|+|\nabla\tilde{v}(\xi,\tau)|\leq\frac{C_{0,2}^{*}}{\xi_{0}}
.

(iii)

for all
|\xi|\leq\frac{7}{4}\xi_{0}
,
\tilde{u}(\xi,\tau)\leq\frac{1}{2}\hat{u}(\tau)
and
\tilde{v}(\xi,\tau)\leq\frac{1}{2}\hat{v}(\tau)
.

Then, for
\xi_{0}\geq\xi_{0,3}(C_{0}^{*})
, there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(K_{0},C_{0,2}^{*},\delta_{1},\xi_{0})
such that for all
|\xi|\leq\xi_{0}
and
\tau\in[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]
,

|\tilde{u}(\xi,\tau)-\hat{u}(\tau)|+|\tilde{v}(\xi,\tau)-\hat{v}(\tau)|\leq\epsilon,\quad|\nabla\tilde{u}(\xi,\tau)|+|\nabla\tilde{v}(\xi,\tau)|\leq\frac{2C_{0,1}^{*}}{\xi_{0}},

where
\epsilon\to 0
as
(\delta_{1},\xi_{0})\to(0,+\infty)

Proof.

We first deal with the gradient estimate. Let
\theta=|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{2}
, then we write from (
4.26
),

\partial_{\tau}\theta\leq\Delta\theta+C\theta,

where we used the fact that
2\nabla f\cdot\nabla(\Delta f)\leq\Delta(|\nabla f|^{2})
and the boundedness of
e^{p\tilde{v}}
and
e^{q\tilde{u}}
.

Consider
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\varphi_{1}\in[0,1]
,
\varphi_{1}(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{2}\xi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\geq\frac{7}{4}\xi_{0}
,
|\nabla\varphi_{1}(\xi)|\leq\frac{1}{\xi_{0}}
and
|\Delta\varphi_{1}(\xi)|\leq\frac{1}{\xi_{0}^{2}}
. Then,
\theta_{1}=\varphi_{1}\theta
satisfies

\partial_{\tau}\theta_{1}\leq\Delta\theta_{1}+C(C_{0,2}^{*})\xi_{0}^{-2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\frac{3}{2}\xi_{0}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\xi_{0}\}}+C\theta_{1}.

Let
\theta_{2}=e^{-C\tau}\theta_{1}
, we write

\partial_{\tau}\theta_{2}\leq\Delta\theta_{2}+C(C_{0,2}^{*})\xi_{0}^{-2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\frac{3}{2}\xi_{0}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\xi_{0}\}},\quad 0\leq\theta_{2}(\tau_{1})\leq\frac{{C_{0,1}^{*}}^{2}}{\xi_{0}^{2}}.

By the maximum principle, we deduce

The following lemma is an integral version of Gronwall’s inequality:

Lemma A.1

(A Gronwall’s inequality)
.

If
\lambda(s)
,
\alpha(s)
and
\beta(s)
are continuous defined on
[s_{0},s_{1}]
such that

\lambda(s)\leq\lambda(s_{0})+\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\alpha(\tau)\lambda(\tau)d\tau+\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\beta(\tau)d\tau,\quad s_{0}\leq s\leq s_{1},

then

\lambda(s)\leq\exp\left(\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\alpha(\tau)d\tau\right)\left[\lambda(s_{0})+\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\beta(\tau)\exp\left(-\int_{s_{0}}^{\tau}\alpha(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\right)d\tau\right].

Proof.

See Lemma 2.3 in
[
Giga and Kohn(1989)
]
for an example of the proof.

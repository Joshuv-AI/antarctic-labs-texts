Mellin-Barnes integrals and the method of brackets
Ivan Gonzalez, Igor Kondrashuk, Victor H. Moll, Luis M. Recabarren

Abstract
. The method of brackets is a method for the evaluation of definite integrals based on a small number of rules. This is employed here for the evaluation of Mellin-Barnes integral. The fundamental idea is to transform these integral representations into a bracket series to obtain their values. The expansion of the gamma function in such a series constitute the main part of this new application. The power and flexibility of this procedure is illustrated with a variety of examples.

1.
Introduction

A classical approaches to special functions developed in the
19^{th}
century was to classify them from the
point of view of solutions
to (second) order differential equations with analytic coefficients

(1.1)

a(z)\frac{d^{2}u}{dz^{2}}+b(z)\frac{du}{dz}+c(z)u=0.

Gauss understood that the singularities of this equation are central to this classification. Moreover, the role of

\infty
as a possible singularity of (
1.1
) plays an important role. The notion of regular singularities and
the well-known Frobenius method to produce solutions arose from this point of view. The reader will find
information about these ideas in
[
16
]
and
[
23
]
.

It is a classical result that any differential equation with
3
regular singular points (on
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
)
can be transformed to the hypergeometric differential equation

(1.2)

z(1-z)\frac{d^{2}w}{dz^{2}}+[c-(a+b-1)z]\frac{dw}{dz}-abw=0,

with singularities at
0,\,1,\,\infty
. The solution of (
4.13
), normalized by
w(0)=1
is the hypergeometric
function, defined by the series expansion

(1.3)

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a \,\,\, b}{c}\bigg|{z}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}\,n!}z^{n},

where
(q)_{n}
os the Pochhammer symbol defined by

(1.4)

(q)_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\textnormal{ if }n=0\\
q(q+1)\cdots(q+n-1)&\textnormal{ if }n>0.\end{cases}

Among the many representations of the hypergeometric function we single out the so-called Mellin-Barnes integral

(1.5)

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a \,\,\, b}{c}\bigg|{z}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)\Gamma(-s)}{\Gamma(c+s)}(-z)^{s}\,ds,

where the contour of integration
\gamma
joins
-i\infty
to
+i\infty
and
separates the poles of
\Gamma(-s)
(namely
0,\,1,\,2,\ldots
) from those of

\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)
(located at
-a,\,-a-1,\ldots,-b,-b-1,\ldots
). In the general case, the
poles of the gamma factors are located on horizontal semi-axes. The
contour
\gamma
has to be chosen to separate those moving to the right from those moving to the left.
Examples may be found in
[
21
,
22
]
.

Many important functions are obtained from the hypergeometric function by coalescing singularities. For instance, the
so-called Whittaker function
W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)
, defined from the equation

(1.6)

\frac{d^{2}w}{dz^{2}}+\left(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{k}{z}+\frac{1/4-m^{2}}{z^{2}}\right)w=0

also has the integral representation

(1.7)

W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)=\frac{e^{-z/2}}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}z^{-s}\frac{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mu+s\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\mu+s\right)\Gamma\left(-\kappa-s\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mu-\kappa\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-\mu-\kappa\right)}\,ds

with a similar condition on the contour of integration.

The main type of integrals considered in this work come from Mellin transforms. This concept is recalled next.

Definition 1.1
.

Given a function
f(x)
, defined on the positive real axis
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, its Mellin transform is defined by

(1.8)

\varphi(s)=(\mathcal{M}f)(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}f(x)\,dx.

This relation may be inverted by a line integral

(1.9)

f(x)=(\mathcal{M}^{-1}\varphi)(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s}\varphi(s)\,ds,

by an appropriate choice of the contour
\gamma
of the type described above.

The goal of the current work is to present a procedure to evaluate inverse Mellin transforms based on the
method of brackets. This is a method to evaluate definite integrals and it is described in Section

2
. The class of functions considered here are of the form

(1.10)

\varphi(s)=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{M}\Gamma\left(a_{j}+A_{j}s\right)}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Gamma\left(b_{j}+B_{j}s\right)}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{P}\Gamma\left(c_{j}-C_{j}s\right)}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{Q}\Gamma\left(d_{j}-D_{j}s\right)}.

For simplicity the parameters

A_{j},\,B_{j},\,C_{j},\,D_{j}
are taken real and positive. Integrals of the form (
1.9
) with an
integrand of the type (
1.10
) will be referred as

Mellin-Barnes integrals
.

Remark 1.2
.

In high energy physics Mellin-Barnes integrals appear frequently at the intermediate steps in the process of calculations

[
2
,
3
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
14
,
21
]
. These are contour integrals
in which the integrands are quotients of Euler Gamma functions, as in (
1.10
). This
type of contour integrals represent a typical representation of
generalized hypergeometric functions. The Mellin-Barnes representation of denominators in integrands corresponding to Feynman diagrams in the momentum space representation is a standard procedure in
quantum field theory. This approach helps to calculate very difficult Feynman diagrams
[
21
]

and this is the reason why Barnes integrals usually appear at the penultimate step of calculation.

Remark 1.3
.

In the previous work
[
13
]
it was mentioned that it would be interesting to combine the
Mellin-Barnes integrals and the method of brackets, due to the similarity of these methods.
This combination is presented here. In addition, several known relations
for generalized hypergeometric functions have been reestablished by combining the method of brackets and Mellin-Barnes transformations. Our investigation has been motivated by the work
of Prausa
[

]
which proposes an interesting symbolic procedure. Our work compares it
with the method of brackets.

2.
The method of brackets

This is a method that
evaluates definite integrals over the half line
[0,\,\infty)
. The
application of the method consists of small number of rules, deduced in
heuristic form, some of which are placed on solid
ground
[
5
]
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the symbol

(2.1)

\langle a\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a-1}\,dx

is the
bracket
associated to the (divergent) integral on the right. The
symbol

(2.2)

\phi_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1)}

is called the
indicator
associated to the index
n
. The notation

\phi_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{r}}
, or simply

\phi_{12\cdots r}
, denotes the product

\phi_{i_{1}}\phi_{i_{2}}\cdots\phi_{i_{r}}
.

Rules for the production of bracket series

The first part of the method is to associate to the integral

(2.3)

I(f)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx

a bracket series according to

{\mathbf{Rule\,\,P_{1}}}
. Assume
f
has the expansion

(2.4)

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}a_{n}x^{\alpha n+\beta-1}.

Then
I(f)
is assigned the
bracket series

(2.5)

I(f)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\langle\alpha n+\beta\rangle.

{\mathbf{Rule\,\,P_{2}}}
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the multinomial power

(a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{r})^{\alpha}
is assigned the

r
-dimension bracket series

(2.6)

\sum_{n_{1}\geq 0}\sum_{n_{2}\geq 0}\cdots\sum_{n_{r}\geq 0}\phi_{n_{1}\,n_{2}\,\cdots n_{r}}a_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{n_{r}}\frac{\langle-\alpha+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}\rangle}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}.

{\mathbf{Rule\,\,P_{3}}}
.
Each representation of an integral by a bracket series has
associated a
complexity index of the representation
via

(2.7)

\text{complexity index }=\text{number of sums }-\text{ number of brackets}.

It is important to observe that the complexity index is attached to a specific
representation of the integral and not just to integral itself. The
experience obtained by the authors using this method suggests that, among
all representations of an integral as a bracket series, the one with

minimal complexity index
should be chosen. The level of difficulty in the analysis of the
resulting bracket series increases with the complexity index.

Rules for the evaluation of a bracket series

{\mathbf{Rule\,\,E_{1}}}
.
Let
a,\,b\in\mathbb{R}
. The one-dimensional bracket series is assigned the value

(2.8)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\phi_{n}f(n)\langle an+b\rangle=\frac{1}{|a|}f(n^{*})\Gamma(-n^{*}),

where
n^{*}
is obtained from the vanishing of the bracket; that is,
n^{*}

solves
an+b=0
. This is precisely the Ramanujan’s Master Theorem.

The next rule provides a value for multi-dimensional bracket series of
index
0
, that is, the number
of sums is equal to the number of brackets.

{\mathbf{Rule\,\,E_{2}}}
.
Let
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
. Assuming the matrix

A=(a_{ij})
is non-singular, then the assignment is

\sum_{n_{1}\geq 0}\cdots\sum_{n_{r}\geq 0}\phi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}}f(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})\langle a_{11}n_{1}+\cdots+a_{1r}n_{r}+c_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle a_{r1}n_{1}+\cdots+a_{rr}n_{r}+c_{r}\rangle

=\frac{1}{|\text{det}(A)|}f(n_{1}^{*},\cdots n_{r}^{*})\Gamma(-n_{1}^{*})\cdots\Gamma(-n_{r}^{*})

where
\{n_{i}^{*}\}

is the (unique) solution of the linear system obtained from the vanishing of
the brackets. There is no assignment if
A
is singular.

{\mathbf{Rule\,\,E_{3}}}
.
The value of a multi-dimensional bracket series of positive complexity index
is obtained by computing all the contributions of maximal rank
by Rule
E_{2}
. These contributions to the integral appear as series in the
free indices. Series converging in a common region are added and divergent/nulls series are discarded. There
is no assignment to a bracket series of negative complexity index. If all the resulting series are
discarded, then the method is not applicable.

Remark 2.1
.

There is a small collection of formal operational rules for brackets. These will be used in the calculations presented
below.

Rule 2.1
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
the bracket satisfies
\langle-\alpha\rangle=\langle\alpha\rangle
.

Proof.

This follows from the change of variables
x\mapsto 1/x
in
\displaystyle\langle-\alpha\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\alpha-1}\,dx.

∎

A similar change of variables gives the next scaling rule:

Rule 2.2
.

For any
\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha\neq 0
the bracket satisfies

(2.9)

\langle\alpha\gamma+\beta\rangle=\frac{1}{|\alpha|}\left\langle\gamma+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right\rangle.

This can be deduced from Rule
E_{1}
.

Rule 2.3
.

For any
\alpha,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha\neq 0
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
appearing as the
index of a sum any allowable function

F
, the identity

(2.10)

F(n)\langle\alpha n+\beta\rangle=\frac{1}{|\alpha|}F\left(-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)\left\langle n+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right\rangle

in the sense that any appearance of the left-hand side in a bracket series may be replaced by the right-hand side.

Proof.

This follows directly from the rule
E_{1}
to evaluate bracket series.
∎

3.
Some operational rules for integration

This section describes the relation between line integrals, like those appearing in the inverse
Mellin transform, and the
method of brackets. These complement those given for the discrete sums.
The results presented here have appeared in
[
19
]
as an extension
of the method of
brackets and used to produce a minimal Mellin-Barnes representations of integrals appearing in
connection with Feynman diagrams.

3.1.
The equivalence of brackets and the Dirac’s delta

Let
f
be a function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and consider its Mellin transform

(3.1)

F(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}f(x)\,dx,

with inversion rule

(3.2)

f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s}F(s)\,ds,

with the usual convention on the contour
\gamma
. Then, replacing (
3.2
) into (
3.1
) produces

(3.3)

\displaystyle F(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s^{\prime}}F(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}\right]\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}F(s^{\prime})\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-s^{\prime}-1}\,dx\right]\,ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}F(s^{\prime})\langle s-s^{\prime}\rangle\,ds^{\prime}.

This proves:

Rule 3.1
.

The rule for integration respect to brackets is given by

(3.4)

\int_{\gamma}F(s)\langle s+\alpha\rangle\,ds=2\pi iF(-\alpha)

where
\gamma
is a contour of the usual type. The generalization

(3.5)

\int_{\gamma}F(s)\langle\beta s+\alpha\rangle\,ds=\frac{2\pi i}{|\beta|}F\left(-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right),\quad\textnormal{with}\,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}

can be obtained from Rule
2.2
.

Remark 3.1
.

From an operational point of view, the result in Theorem
3.1
may be written as

(3.6)

\langle s+\alpha\rangle=2\pi i\delta(s+\alpha),

where
\delta
is Dirac’s delta function.

3.2.
Mellin transform

This section contains a brief review of the Mellin transform. Recall that this is defined by

(3.7)

\mathcal{M}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}f(x)~dx,

where the arguments are the function
f
to be transformed and the variable
z
appearing in the integral. The inverse Mellin transformation is

(3.8)

f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-z}\mathcal{M}(f)(z)\,dz\quad\textnormal{ for }x\in(0,\infty).

The point
c
associated to the contour of integration must be in the vertical strip
c_{1}<c<c_{2}
, with
boundaries determined by the condition that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{c_{1}-1}f(x)~dx

\displaystyle{\rm and}

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{c_{2}-1}f(x)~dx

must be finite. This is satisfied if the function
f
satisfies the growth conditions

|f(x)|<1/x^{c_{1}}\quad{\rm when}\;x\to+0,\textnormal{ and }\quad|f(x)|<1/x^{c_{2}}\quad\textnormal{ when}\;x\to+\infty.

The conditions (
3.9
) imply that the Mellin transform
\mathcal{M}(f)(z)
is holomorphic in
the vertical strip
c_{1}<{\rm Re}~z<c_{2}
. The asymptotic behavior of the integrand is then used to
determine the direction in which (a finite segment) of the vertical line contour is closed in order to produce
a contour to apply Cauchy’s integral theorem. The singularities of the integrand are then used to analyze the
behavior of the integrals as the finite segment becomes infinite.

One of the simplest examples of the Mellin transformation is

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{z-1}~dx

\displaystyle{\rm and}

\displaystyle e^{-x}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-z}\Gamma(z)~dz.

The contour in the complex plane is the vertical line with
{\rm Re}~z=c
is in the strip
0<c<A,
where
A
is a real
positive number, the vertical line contour must be closed to the left. It is convenient to include here a proof
of equations (
3.7
-
3.8
). This is classical and may be found in any textbook on
the theory of complex variable. It is reproduced here for pedagogical arguments. First, use the fact
that
\mathcal{M}(f)(z)
is holomorphic in the strip
c_{1}<{\rm Re}~z<c_{2}
, then taking

\delta>0
to be infinitesimally small,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(f)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}f(x)~dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}dx\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-\omega}\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)~d\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}x^{z-1}dx\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-\omega}\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)~d\omega

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{z-1}dx\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-\omega}\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)~d\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}x^{z-1}dx\int_{c_{1}-\delta-i\infty}^{c_{1}-\delta+i\infty}x^{-\omega}\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)~d\omega

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{z-1}dx\int_{c_{2}+\delta-i\infty}^{c_{2}+\delta+i\infty}x^{-\omega}\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)~d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c_{1}-\delta-i\infty}^{c_{1}-\delta+i\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)}{z-\omega}~d\omega-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c_{2}+\delta-i\infty}^{c_{2}+\delta+i\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)}{z-\omega}~d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c_{1}-\delta+i\infty}^{c_{1}-\delta-i\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)}{\omega-z}~d\omega+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c_{2}+\delta-i\infty}^{c_{2}+\delta+i\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)}{\omega-z}~d\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{CR}\frac{\mathcal{M}(f)(\omega)}{\omega-z}~d\omega,

where
CR
is a rectangular contour constructed from the two vertical lines from
(
) supplemented by two horizontal lines at the imaginary complex infinities of the
strip
c_{1}<{\rm Re}~z<c_{2}.
The contour
CR
is closed in the counterclockwise orientation.

The inverse transformation proof is even simpler and may be used in order to define Dirac
\delta
-function.
Observe that

(3.11)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-z}\mathcal{M}(f)(z)~dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-z}~dz\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{z-1}f(y)~dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta(\ln{(y/x)})y^{-1}f(y)~dy=f(x),

which is valid in view of the relation

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}e^{(x-y)z}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{(x-y)(c+i\tau)}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{(x-y)c}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(x-y)\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(x-y)c}\delta(x-y)=\delta(x-y).

This proof of the inverse Mellin transformation belongs to D. Hilbert and may be found in any good textbook dedicated to complex analysis. In this paper we show
that the method of bracket when applied to Mellin integrals in some sense is equivalent to this old proof of the inverse Mellin transformation.
More precisely, we may argue that
by using the same trick like in this quite old proof of the inverse Mellin transformation, namely to divide the integration over the variable
x\in[0,\infty[
in two parts,

x\in[0,1]
and
x\in[1,\infty[,
and by creating in a such way a closed contour in the plane of the complex variable we may map all the rules of the method of bracket
to the Cauchy integral formula.

In high energy particle physics, in order to solve integro-differential equations representing evolution of important physical quantities, the transformation to Mellin moment
is frequently used [see, for example
[
4
]
].
The inverse transformation of the Mellin moment has completely the same form like the inverse Mellin transformation. The question may appear if the inverse Mellin transformation
returns us to some function but how we may know if we came back to this function of a real variable from its Mellin moment or from its Mellin transform in the complex plane?
We may differ the Mellin moments from the the Mellin transforms by studying the asymptotic behaviour at the complex infinity of the given function in the complex plane.

Remark 3.2
.

The question of complexity, as introduced in
Rule

\mathbf{P_{3}}
is now extended. In the process of
evaluating an integral by the method of brackets, define
\sigma
to be the number of sums plus the number of
contour integrals appearing and
\delta
to be the number of brackets plus the number of integrals on the
half-line
[0,\,\infty)
that appear. The (generalized) index of complexity is
\iota=\sigma-\delta
. This
index should be seen as a measure of difficulty in the evaluation of the integral by the method of brackets. In
the case
\iota=0
, the answer is given by a single term. For
\iota>0
, the gamma factors appearing in the
numerators of the line integrals must be expanded in bracket series. This guarantees that the method provides
all series representations of the solution. As usual series converging in a common region must be added. It
is the heuristic observation that the bracket/line integral representations of a problem should aim to minimize
the index
\iota
.

3.3.
Multiple integrals

The method discussed here can be extended to evaluate multiple integrals with a bracket representation

(3.13)

J=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{N}\int_{\gamma_{1}}ds_{1}\cdots\int_{\gamma_{N}}ds_{N}\\
F(s_{1},\cdots,s_{N})\langle a_{11}s_{1}+\cdots a_{1N}s_{N}+c_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle a_{N1}s_{1}+\cdots a_{NN}s_{N}+c_{N}\rangle,

by using the one-dimensional rule in iterated form. The expression for
J
has the form

(3.14)

J=\frac{1}{|\det(A)|}F(s_{1}^{*},\cdots,s_{N}^{*})

where
A=(a_{ij})
, with
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}

and
\{s_{i}^{*}\}\,\,(i=1,\ldots,N)
is the solution of the system
A\vec{s}=-\vec{c}

produced by the vanishing of the arguments in the brackets appearing in this process.

4.
Transforming Mellin-Barnes integrals to bracket series

This section evaluates Mellin-Barnes integrals by transforming them into a bracket series. The rules of
Section
2
are then used to produce an analytic expression for the integral.

Lemma 4.1
.

The gamma function has the bracket series representation

(4.1)

\Gamma(\alpha)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\langle\alpha+n\rangle.

Proof.

This follows simply
from expanding
e^{-t}
in power series in the
integral representation of the gamma function to obtain

(4.2)

\Gamma(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t}\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}t^{n}\,dt=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\langle\alpha+n\rangle.

∎

In this section we present a systematic procedure to evaluate Mellin-Barnes integrals of the form

(4.3)

I(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{M}\Gamma(a_{j}+A_{j}s)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{P}\Gamma(c_{j}-C_{j}s)}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Gamma(b_{j}+B_{j}s)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{Q}\Gamma(d_{j}-D_{j}s)}\,ds

where
\gamma
is the usual vertical line contour. A similar argument has appeared in
[
19
]
. The idea is
to use the method of brackets to
produce the bracket series associated to (
4.3
). The parameters satisfy the rules

a_{j},\,b_{j},\,c_{j},\,d_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
A_{j},\,B_{j},\,C_{j},\,D_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

for the index
j
in the corresponding range; for instance, the
j
associated to
a_{j},\,A_{j}
vary
from
1
to
M
.

The procedure to obtain the bracket series is systematic: the gamma factors in the numerator
are replaced using formula (
4.1
) (the gamma factors in the denominator do not
contribute):

(4.4)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{M}\Gamma(a_{j}+A_{j}s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{M}\left[\sum_{k_{j}}\phi_{k_{j}}\langle a_{j}+A_{j}s+k_{j}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}}\cdots\sum_{k_{M}}\phi_{k_{1}\cdots k_{M}}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\langle a_{j}+A_{j}s+k_{j}\rangle

and similarly

(4.5)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{P}\Gamma(c_{j}-C_{j}s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{P}\left[\sum_{\ell_{j}}\phi_{\ell_{j}}\langle c_{j}-C_{j}s+\ell_{j}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell_{1}}\cdots\sum_{\ell_{P}}\phi_{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{P}}\prod_{j=1}^{P}\langle c_{j}-C_{j}s+\ell_{j}\rangle.

The rules of the method of brackets described in Section
2
now yield a bracket series associated with the integral
(
4.3
). To illustrate these idea, introduce the function

(4.6)

G(s)=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}\Gamma(b_{j}+B_{j}s)}\times\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{Q}\Gamma(d_{j}-D_{j}s)},

The previous rules transform the integral
I(x)
in (
4.3
) to

(4.7)

\displaystyle I(x)

5.

The method of brackets is now used to evaluate integrals of the form (

Example 5.1
.

Consider the function
\varphi(s)=\Gamma(s-a).
Its inverse Mellin transform is given by

This is written as

(5.6)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s}\Gamma(s-a)\,ds=x^{-a}e^{-x},

or equivalently

(5.7)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-a-1}e^{-x}\,dx=\Gamma(s-a).

Replacing
s-a
by

Example 5.2
.

The change of variables

Example 5.3

\displaystyle f(x)

n_{1}

7.
Mellin transforms of products

This section presents a method to evaluate the Mellin transform

(7.1)

I(s,b)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}f(x)g(bx)\,dx.

knowing a series for
f
of the form

(7.2)

f(x)=\sum_{n}\phi_{n}F(n)x^{\beta n}

and the inverse Mellin transform

(7.3)

g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s}\varphi(s)\,ds.

For
\kappa>0
, the change of variables
s=\kappa s^{\prime}
gives

(7.4)

g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}x^{-\kappa s}\widetilde{\varphi}(s)\,ds,

where
\widetilde{\varphi}(s)=\kappa\varphi(\kappa s)
. The formula (
7.4
) is now written as

(7.5)

g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-\kappa s}\varphi(s)\,ds,

that is, the tilde notation is dropped and the parameter
\kappa
is kept.

Then

(7.6)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}f(x)g(bx)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\varphi(s)b^{-\kappa s}\left[\sum_{n}\phi_{n}F(n)\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha+\beta n-\kappa s-1}\,dx\right]\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\varphi(s)b^{-\kappa s}\left[\sum_{n}\phi_{n}F(n)\langle\alpha+\beta n-\kappa s\rangle\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\varphi(s)b^{-\kappa s}}{|\beta|}\left[\sum_{n}\phi_{n}F(n)\left\langle\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-\frac{\kappa s}{\beta}+n\right\rangle\right]\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\beta|}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\varphi(s)b^{-\kappa s}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha-\kappa s}{\beta}\right)F\left(\frac{\kappa s-\alpha}{\beta}\right)\,ds.

This is stated as a theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume the function
f(x)
has an expansion given by

(7.7)

f(x)=\sum_{n}\phi_{n}F(n)x^{\beta n}

and the function

is given by rescaled version of the inverse Mellin transform

(7.8)

g(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\varphi(s)x^{-\kappa s}\,ds.

Then

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\alpha-1}f(x)g(bx)\,dx=\frac{1}{|\beta|}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\varphi(s)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha-\kappa s}{\beta}\right)F\left(\frac{\kappa s-\alpha}{\beta}\right)b^{-\kappa s}\,ds.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\nu+n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\nu+n}{2}\right)\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^{-n-1}

(8.9)

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\Gamma(-n-\nu)\Gamma(1+2n+\nu)\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^{\nu+2n}

(8.10)

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\Gamma(\nu-n)\Gamma(1+2n-\nu)\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^{-\nu+2n}

Observe that
T_{1}
diverges since it is of type
{{}_{2}}F_{0}
(this sum plays a role in the asymptotic study
of the integral when
b\rightarrow\infty
, not described here) and the series
T_{2},\,T_{3}
converge when
|b|<1
. The method
of brackets now states that
F(b,\nu)=T_{2}+T_{3}
, when
|b|<1
. Under this assumption
and since
T_{3}(b,\nu)=T_{2}(b,-\nu)
, it suffices to obtain
an expression for
T_{2}(b,\nu)
. This is the next result.

Proposition 8.2
.

The function
T_{2}(b,\nu)
is given by

(8.11)

T_{2}(b,\nu)=-\frac{\pi}{2\sin(\pi\nu)}\frac{b^{\nu}(1+\sqrt{1-b^{2}})^{-\nu}}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}.

Proof.

The proof is divided in a sequence of steps.

Step 1
. The function
T_{2}(b,\nu)
is given by

(8.12)

T_{2}(b,\nu)=-\frac{\pi b^{\nu}}{2^{\nu+1}\sin(\pi\nu)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1+\nu}{2}\right)_{n}\,\left(1+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}}{(1+\nu)_{n}}\,\frac{b^{2n}}{n!}.

Proof
. In the definition of
T_{2}(b,\nu)
use

(w)_{2n}=2^{2n}\left(\frac{w}{2}\right)_{n}\,\left(\frac{w+1}{2}\right)_{n},

\Gamma(-n-\nu)=\frac{(-1)^{n}\Gamma(-\nu)}{(1+\nu)_{n}},\,\,\Gamma(1+\nu+2n)=\Gamma(1+\nu)2^{2n}\left(\frac{1+\nu}{2}\right)_{n}\left(1+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}

and
\Gamma(-\nu)\Gamma(1+\nu)=-\pi/\sin(\pi\nu)
, to obtain the result.

This identity shows that the statement of the Proposition is equivalent to proving

(8.13)

\frac{1}{2^{\nu}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1+\nu}{2}\right)_{n}\,\left(1+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}}{(1+\nu)_{n}}\frac{b^{2n}}{n!}=\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-b^{2}}\,\,)^{-\nu}}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}.

Step 2
. Formula (2.5.16) in H. Wilf
[
24
]

(8.14)

\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4x}}{2x}\right)^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{k\,(2n+k-1)!}{n!\,(n+k)!}x^{n}

and the identity
(1+\sqrt{1-b^{2}})(1-\sqrt{1-b^{2}})=b^{2}
shows, after some elementary simplifications,
that the statement of the Proposition is equivalent to

(8.15)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{1+\nu}{2}\right)_{n}\,\left(1+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}}{(1+\nu)_{n}}\frac{c^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-c}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}\,\left(1+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}}{(1+\nu)_{n}}\frac{c^{n}}{n!},

where
c=b^{2}
.

Step 3
. The identity at the end of Step 2 is equivalent to

(8.16)

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{1+\nu}{2} \,\, 1 + \frac{\nu}{2}}{1+ \nu}\bigg|{u}\right)={}_{1}F_{0}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\tfrac{1}{2}}{-}\bigg|{u}\right)\times{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{1+\nu}{2} \,\, \frac{\nu}{2}}{1+ \nu}\bigg|{u}\right).

Proof
. Simply use the binomial theorem

(8.17)

(1-t)^{-\alpha}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{n}}{n!}t^{n}.

with
\alpha=1/2
.

Step 4
. The conclusion of the Proposition is equivalent to the identity

(8.18)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\binom{n}{j}}{(1+\nu)_{n-j}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{j}\left(\frac{1+\nu}{2}\right)_{n-j}\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n-j}=\frac{\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}\,\left(1+\frac{\nu}{2}\right)_{n}}{(1+\nu)_{n}},

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

∎

Proof.

The umbral method
[

]
shows that the identity is equivalent to
the one formed by replacing Pochhammer symbols
(a)_{k}
by
a^{k}
. In this case, (

)
becomes

(8.19)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}2^{j}\nu^{n-j}=(\nu+2)^{n},

and this follows from the binomial theorem.
∎

Proof.

An alternative proof of (
8.18
) is presented next. Expressing the Pochhammer symbols in terms of

(8.20)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{2j}{j}\binom{\nu+2(n-j)}{n-j}\frac{\nu}{\nu+2(n-j)}=\binom{\nu+2n}{n}.

This identity is interpreted as the coefficient of

in the product of the two series

(8.21)

A(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{2j}{j}x^{j}\quad\text{and}\quad B(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\nu\binom{\nu+2k}{k}\frac{x^{k}}{\nu+2k}.

The first sum is given by the binomial theorem as

To obtain an analytic expression for

start with entry

in

where the term in brackets is the generating function of the Catalan numbers. Some elementary manipulations
give

Then (
8.20
) is equivalent to the identity

(8.25)

\int_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}\frac{t^{\nu-1}}{\sqrt{1-4t^{2}}}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4t^{2}}}{2t^{2}}\right)^{\nu}\,dt=\frac{1}{\nu}x^{\nu/2}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4x}}{2x}\right)^{\nu}.

This can now be verified by observing that both sides vanish at

and a direct computation shows that the
derivatives match.
∎

The integral is stated next. It appears as entry

in

]
.

Corollary 8.1
.

For
\nu,\,b\in\mathbb{R}

(8.26)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}K_{\nu}(bx)\,dx=\\
\frac{\pi}{2b^{\nu}\sin(\pi\nu)}\left[\frac{(1+\sqrt{1-b^{2}})^{\nu}-(1-\sqrt{1-b^{2}})^{\nu}}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}\right].

In particular, as

(8.27)

\frac{\pi\nu}{\sin(\pi\nu)},

9.
Flexibility of the method of brackets

This final section illustrates the flexibility of the method of brackets. To achieve this goal, we present four
different evaluations of the integral

(9.1)

I(a,b)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-a^{2}x^{2}}\,dx}{x^{2}+b^{2}}.

The parameters
a,\,b
are assumed to be real and positive. This formula appears as entry
3.466.1
in

[
15
]
with value

(9.2)

I(a,b)=\frac{\pi}{2b}e^{a^{2}b^{2}}(1-\textnormal{Erf}(ab))

and the reader will find in
[
1
]
an elementary proof of it. This section presents
4
different ways to
use the method of brackets to evaluate this integral.

Method 1
. Start with the bracket series representations

(9.3)

\displaystyle\exp(-a^{2}x^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{1}}a^{2n_{1}}x^{2n_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}+b^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{2}n_{3}}b^{2n_{2}}x^{2n_{3}}\langle 1+n_{2}+n_{3}\rangle

and produce the bracket series

(9.4)

I(a,b)=\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}+n_{3}}a^{2n_{1}}b^{2n_{2}}\langle 1+n_{2}+n_{3}\rangle\,\langle 2n_{1}+2n_{3}+1\rangle.

Method 2
. The second form begins with the Mellin-Barnes representations

(9.5)

\displaystyle\exp(-a^{2}x^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-t}a^{-t}\Gamma\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}+b^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi b^{2}i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s}b^{s}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{s}{2}\right)\,ds

Replacing in (
9.1
) a bracket appears and one obtains the representation

(9.6)

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{4b^{2}(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{\gamma_{1}}\int_{\gamma_{2}}a^{-t}b^{s}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{t}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\tfrac{s}{2}\right)\langle 1-t-s\rangle\,ds\,dt.

The bracket is used to evaluate the
s
-integral to produce
s^{*}=1-t
and the expression

(9.7)

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{4b^{2}(2\pi i)}\int_{\gamma}a^{-t}b^{1-t}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{t}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1-t}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1+t}{2}\right)\,dt.

The next step is to express the gamma factors in the numerator as bracket series to obtain

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{4b^{2}(2\pi i)}\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}\int_{\gamma}a^{-t}b^{1-t}\langle\tfrac{t}{2}+n_{1}\rangle\langle\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{t}{2}+n_{2}\rangle\langle\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{t}{2}+n_{3}\rangle\,dt

and for instance selecting the bracket in
n_{1}
to eliminate the integral and using

(9.8)

\left\langle\tfrac{t}{2}+n_{1}\right\rangle=2\langle t+2n_{1}\rangle

gives

(9.9)

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{2b}\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}a^{2n_{1}}b^{2n_{1}}\langle\tfrac{1}{2}+n_{1}+n_{2}\rangle\langle\tfrac{1}{2}-n_{1}+n_{3}\rangle.

Method 3
. The next way to evaluate the integral (
9.1
) is a mixture of the previous two.
Using

(9.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}+b^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}}b^{2n_{1}}x^{2n_{2}}\langle 1+n_{1}+n_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\exp(-a^{2}x^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-t}a^{-t}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{t}{2}\right)\,dt

and the usual procedures gives

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}b^{2n_{1}}\langle 1+n_{1}+n_{2}\rangle\int_{\gamma}a^{-t}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{t}{2}\right)\langle 2n_{2}-t+1\rangle\,dt

Now use the bracket in
n_{2}
to eliminate the integral and produce

(9.11)

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}}a^{-2n_{2}-1}b^{2n_{1}}\langle 1+n_{1}+n_{2}\rangle\Gamma\left(n_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)

and replacing the gamma factor by its bracket series yields

(9.12)

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}b^{2n_{1}}a^{-2n_{2}-1}\langle 1+n_{1}+n_{2}\rangle\langle\tfrac{1}{2}+n_{2}+n_{3}\rangle.

Method 4
. The final form uses the representations

(9.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}+b^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi b^{2}i}\int_{\gamma}x^{-s}b^{s}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{s}{2}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\exp(-a^{2}x^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{1}}a^{2n_{1}}x^{2n_{1}}

to write

(9.14)

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{4b^{2}\pi i}\sum_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{1}}a^{2n_{1}}\int_{\gamma}b^{s}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\tfrac{s}{2}\right)\langle 2n_{1}-s+1\rangle\,ds,

where the last bracket comes the original integral.
Now write the gamma factors as bracket series to produce

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{2b^{2}(2\pi i)}\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}a^{2n_{1}}\left[\int_{\gamma}b^{s}\langle\tfrac{s}{2}+n_{2}\rangle\langle 1-\tfrac{s}{2}+n_{3}\rangle\langle 2n_{1}-s+1\rangle\,ds\right],

and using the
n_{2}
-bracket to eliminate the integral yields

(9.15)

I(a,b)=\frac{1}{b^{2}}\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\sum_{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}a^{2n_{1}}b^{-2n_{2}}\langle 1+n_{2}+n_{3}\rangle\,\langle 2n_{1}+2n_{2}+1\rangle.

These four bracket series lead to the terms

(9.16)

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2b}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}-k)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+k)(-a^{2}b^{2})^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2b}\exp(a^{2}b^{2})

(9.17)

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Gamma(1+k)\Gamma(-\tfrac{1}{2}-k)(-a^{2}b^{2})^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{2b}\exp(a^{2}b^{2})\textnormal{erf}(ab)

(9.18)

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2ab^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Gamma(1+k)\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}+k)\left(-\frac{1}{a^{2}b^{2}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2ab^{2}}{}_{2}F_{0}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{1 \,\,\,\, \tfrac{1}{2}}{-}\bigg|{-\frac{1}{a^{2}b^{2}}}\right).

and Rule

conforming (

). The value of

is useful in the asymptotic study of

as

The final example illustrates a different combination ideas.

Example 9.1

Consider the two-dimensional integral

11.
Conclusions

The work presented here gives a new procedure to evaluate Mellin-Barnes integral representations. The
main idea is to replace the Gamma factors appearing in such representations by its corresponding
bracket series. The method of brackets is then used to evaluate these integrals. This method has been
shown to be simple and efficient, given its algorithmic nature. A collection of representative examples
has been provided.

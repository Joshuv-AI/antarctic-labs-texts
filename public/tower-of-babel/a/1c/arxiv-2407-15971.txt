Well-posedness of the Stokes problem under modified pressure Dirichlet boundary conditions Journal: BIT
Igor Tominec, Josefin Ahlkrona, Malte Braack

Abstract
This paper shows that the Stokes problem is well-posed when velocity and pressure simultaneously vanish on the domain boundary. This result is achieved by extending Nečas’ inequality to square-integrable functions that vanish in a small band covering the boundary. It is found that the associated a priori pressure estimate depends inversely on the volume of the band. Numerical experiments confirm these findings. Based on these results, guidelines are provided for applying vanishing pressure boundary conditions in model coupling and domain decomposition methods.

Introduction

The Stokes problem is a model of viscous fluid flow given by:

\displaystyle-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\bm{f}

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Omega,

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}

A classical choice of constraints is Dirichlet boundary conditions on velocity over

;

There exist cases where it is desirable to instead set Dirichlet-type conditions for both velocity and pressure on the boundary.
These conditions are typically not motivated by physics but are useful for instance in
domain decomposition methods

In domain decomposition methods the computational domain is split into smaller subdomains. Stokes-type problem is then solved on each subdomain, where
the subproblems are coupled through boundary conditions to recover the solution over the whole domain.

For instance,
(

Another example is adaptive PDE model refinement methods

The Dirichlet velocity and pressure boundary conditions for this subdomain are obtained from the SIA solution, which couples the solutions over the subdomain and the rest of the domain.

In this paper, we analyze these boundary conditions from a mathematical perspective and propose potential improvements.

A potential use case are multigrid methods on hierarchically refined meshes for finite element methods

The local smoothing problem uses Dirichlet boundary conditions that couple to the surrounding local solutions and by that ensure coupling across all refinement levels.

2.1
Preliminaries: function spaces and norms

Throughout this paper, we conduct the theoretical analysis of the Stokes problem (
6

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\Big\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega)\Big|\int_{\Omega}q\,d\Omega=0\Big\}.

(3)

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\bm{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}\,\Big|\,\|\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\,\Big|\,\bm{v}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\Big\}.

2.2
Weak form of Stokes problem

The weak formulation of the Stokes problem (
1
)
seeks
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}:=\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(velocity space) and
p\in Q:=L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)

(pressure space) such that:

\displaystyle(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{v})-(p,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})

\displaystyle=(\bm{f},\bm{v}),

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}

\displaystyle\in\bm{V},

(6)

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot\bm{u},q)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\forall q

\displaystyle\in Q,

where
(\bm{v},q)
are the test functions, and
\bm{f}\in\bm{V}^{\prime}
(velocity dual space) is a forcing function.
In abstract form, the problem is to find
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}
and
p\in Q
such that:

\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{v})+b(\bm{v},p)

\displaystyle=(\bm{f},\bm{v}),\,

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}

\displaystyle\in\bm{V},

(7)

\displaystyle b(\bm{u},q)

\displaystyle=0,\,

\displaystyle\forall q

\displaystyle\in Q,

where
a(\bm{u},\bm{v})
and

are bilinear forms on
\bm{V}\times\bm{V}
and
\bm{V}\times Q
, respectively.
The well-posedness of the above problem is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
(Babuška-Brezzi, Theorem 49.3 in
guermond_volii
)

Problem (
7
) is well-posed if:

\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{u})

\displaystyle\geq C_{A}\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{V}},\,

\displaystyle\bm{u}\in\bm{V},

(8a)

\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{V}}\,\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}}\,

\displaystyle\bm{u}\in\bm{V},\,\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

(8b)

\displaystyle b(\bm{u},q)

\displaystyle\leq C_{C}\|\bm{u}\|_{V}\,\|q\|_{Q}\,

\displaystyle\bm{u}\in\bm{V},\,q\in Q,

(8c)

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{V}}\frac{b(\bm{u},q)}{\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{V}}}

\displaystyle\geq C_{D}\,\|q\|_{Q}\,

\displaystyle q\in Q.

(8d)

This framework provides the basis for analyzing the well-posedness of the Stokes problem when
\bm{V}=\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
Q=L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
,
as defined in (
4
). The
boundary conditions (
2
) are incorporated within these spaces.
The key properties of
\Delta
are:
(i)
|\Delta|>0
,
(ii)
\Delta
is an open set,
(iii)
\Omega\backslash\Delta
is a closed set when

is a band around
\partial\Omega
.

2.3
Vanishing pressure on a strip covering
\partial\Omega
.

We now motivate the choice of the vanishing pressure space

L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
, which enforces the condition
p|_{\Delta}=0
, over an alternative
L^{2}(\Omega)
-based space that imposes

p|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.
The latter condition leads to a loss of well-posedness for problem (
6
), particularly in terms of uniqueness.
This issue arises due to:

(i) the null space of the gradient operator present in (
6
), given by (
-(p,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})=(\nabla p,\bm{v})
)
contains constant functions
p^{*}\in\mathbb{R}(\Omega)
, meaning,
\nabla p^{*}=0
.

(ii) Imposing
p^{*}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
does not necessarily eliminate constant solutions, as shown in the left plot of Figure
2
.
This is because the trace operator is not continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, meaning that a function vanishing on
\partial\Omega

can still be constant in
\Omega
,
leaving the null space unchanged and breaking uniqueness.

On the other hand, enforcing
p^{*}
to satisfy the boundary conditions (
2
),
ensures that
p^{*}
is not a constant function on
\Omega
, as illustrated in the right plot of Figure
2
.
In this case, the derivative at the jump discontinuity inside
\Omega
is a generalized Dirac delta function, which is measurable in space
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
As a result, the null space of the gradient operator no longer contains constant pressure functions.
However, while this is a necessary step toward well-posedness, it is not a sufficient condition on its own.

An overview of the theoretical investigation

from (

It is well-known that

Operators between
L^{2}
-subspaces

We use the operator

to project an arbitrary function from

to

Definition 1
(

Let

be the indicator function w.r.t.
\Delta
.
We define the projection
P:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
, the average mapping

and the average subtracting mapping

\displaystyle Pq

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle q-I_{\Delta}q,

\displaystyle Mq

Lemma 1
(
Properties of operator
T
)

The linear operator
T:L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)\to L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
given by (

) is continuous and injective.
For the adjoint holds
T^{*}=P
.

Proof

(i)
T
is continuous.

M
is continuous, since

\|Mq\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|^{-1/2}\|Mq\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

Thus

(ii) T is injective.
We notice that

. But such

Taking

, as

by construction contains
functions with essential support smaller than
\Omega
(see (

function has an essential support equal

. Thus,
T
is injective.

(iii) Adjoint of

is

For

\displaystyle\langle T^{*}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)^{\prime},L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)}=(f,Tg)=(f,g)-(f,Mg)

Lemma 2
(
Properties of operator

)

The null space of

(i) Nullspace of

Lemma 3
(

The range of operator

R(T)=\{g\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\,|\,g=C\in\mathbb{R}\,\text{ on }\,\Delta\},

. The following lower bounded holds

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{\Delta}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall f\in L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega),

Proof

(i) Range of T.
For any

,

(Tf)|_{\Delta}=f|_{\Delta}-Mf=-Mf

is a constant.

(ii) Range of T is closed.

T:L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)\to L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
is linear and continuous. Since

, we have with Lemma

and

(iii) T is bounded from below.

As

is surjective by definition, and injective by Lemma

), it is a continuous bijective

, the inverse

) follows, see

Moreover, we get by elementary calculus the equality:

\displaystyle\|Tf\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f-Mf\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-|\Omega||Mf|^{2},

and for the mean value the upper bound:

\displaystyle|\Omega||Mf|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-|\Delta|/|\Omega|)^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This yields the lower bound:

\displaystyle\|Tf\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(|\Delta|/|\Omega|)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Hence, the constant

We now extend the Nečas inequality (

Theorem 3.1
(

)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, Lipschitz domaint. Then

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Delta}^{-1}C_{1}\,\|\nabla f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\quad\forall f\in L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega),

Here

Proof

From Lemma
3
we have
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Delta}^{-1}\,\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
with
C_{\Delta}>0
.
Since
Tf\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
we can apply
the original Nečas inequality (
9
) to obtain
\|Tf\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\,\|\nabla(Tf)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
.
Since
Tf=f-Mf
we obtain

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Delta}^{-1}C_{1}\,(\|\nabla f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla(Mf)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}).

For
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}
it holds
\langle\nabla Mf,\bm{v}\rangle=-(Mf,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})=-Mf\int_{\partial\Omega}\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}\,ds=0
.

Finally, we apply the extended Nečas inequality for
L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)

from Theorem
3.1
to establish the well-posedness of the Stokes problem under the modified boundary conditions (
2
). The result is stated in the theorem below.

Theorem 3.2
(
Well-posedness
)

Let
\Omega
and
\Delta
be bounded Lipschitz domains with
\Delta\subset\Omega
,
\partial\Omega\subset\overline{\Delta}
and
|\Delta|>0
.
The Stokes problem (
6
) is well-posed
for
p\in L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
and
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
In addition, the following a priori estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,C_{\Delta}^{-1}\,C_{1}\,\max(1,C_{p}^{-1})\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}^{-1}\,\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)},

where
C_{\Delta}>0
depends on
\Delta
, and
C_{1},C_{p}>0
are depending on
\Omega
.

Proof

We set
\bm{V}=\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
Q=L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
. The
bilinear form
a:\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
in (
7
) associated with (
6
) is continuous and coercive
guermond_volii
.
The bilinear form
b:\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is also continuous.
Thus, conditions (
8a
) - (
8c
) in Theorem
2.1
hold.
We use the pressure gradient estimate from
Theorem
3.1
, definition of
the
H^{-1}(\Omega)
norm, and then divide the resulting estimate by
C_{\Delta}^{-1}\,C_{1}
to arrive for
p\in L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
at:

\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}}\frac{(\nabla p,\bm{v})_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H_{0}^{1}}}=\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}}\frac{-(p,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H_{0}^{1}}}\geq C_{\Delta}\,C_{1}^{-1}\,\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This is the inf-sup condition (
8d
) with the inf-sup constant
\gamma:=C_{\Delta}\,C_{1}^{-1}>0
.
Thus, condition (
8d
) is also established and (
6
) is well-posed with
\bm{V}=\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
Q=L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
.
Using the inf-sup condition above we have:

\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\gamma\,\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}}\frac{-(p,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H_{0}^{1}}}.

We now substitute the
-(p,\nabla\cdot\bm{v})
term by using the Stokes problem (
6
) and make bounds to arrive at:

\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\gamma\sup_{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}}\frac{-(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{v})+(\bm{f},\bm{v})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{H_{0}^{1}}}.

Next, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on all the numerator terms, and then

\|\nabla\bm{v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
(and same for the
\bm{u}
term) we have:

\|p\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\gamma\left(\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\|\bm{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(15)

Inserting
\bm{v}=\bm{u}
to the Stokes problem (
6
), using the incompressibility condition
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0

gives:
\|\nabla\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(\bm{f},\bm{u})\leq\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\,\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
.
With the Poincaré’s inequality we arrive at
\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
. The latter relation proves the well-posedness estimate for the velocity vector field.
Then, inserting the relation to (
15
) proves the well-posedness estimate for the pressure function.

We now discuss the incompressibility (divergence-free) condition
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0
when solving the Stokes problem (
6
)
under the boundary conditions (
2
). Ideally, this condition holds pointwise, but this is generally
not true when
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}=\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

A key measure of incompressibility is the absolute average divergence, given by

|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}|
.
We have
|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\,d\Omega|=|\int_{\partial\Omega}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\,ds|=0,

as long as
\bm{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, which is our assumption. This result is optimal for any choice of
|\Delta|>0
.

Key measures of divergence include its
L^{1}
- and
L^{2}
-norm. In the following Lemma
4
,
we establish that usually
\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>0
. Building on this result, it follows that

\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C|\Delta|^{1/2}
.

Lemma 4
(Velocity divergence
L^{1}
- and
L^{2}
-norm)

Let
\bm{u}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
p\in L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
be the solutions to the Stokes problem (
6
) over
\Omega
. Then

\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\backslash\Delta)}=0,
and its equivalence:

\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Delta)}.

(16)

An a priori estimate of the velocity divergence in
L^{1}
-norm is:

\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}|\Delta|^{1/2}\|\bm{f}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

(17)

Proof

(i) Divergence
L^{2}
-norm.

We utilize the operator
P
from Definition
1

to project
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}
onto
L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
.
Since
P(\nabla\cdot\bm{u})\in L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
, we can use this as a possible test function in (
6
). This implies:

\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(\nabla\cdot\bm{u},\nabla\cdot\bm{u})=(\nabla\cdot\bm{u},\nabla\cdot\bm{u}-P(\nabla\cdot\bm{u})).

With
P(\nabla\cdot\bm{u})|_{\Delta}=0
on
\Delta
, and
P(\nabla\cdot\bm{u})|_{\Delta}=\nabla\cdot\bm{u}
on
\Omega\setminus\Delta
we obtain (
16
)
and
\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\backslash\Delta)}=0
.

(ii) Divergence
L^{1}
-norm.

With (
16
) follows

\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega\backslash\Delta)}\leq|\Omega\backslash\Delta|^{1/2}\,\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\backslash\Delta)}=0.

We further deduce

\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{1}(\Delta)}\leq|\Delta|^{1/2}\,\|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Delta)}.

Finally, the bound (
17
) follows with the stability of the velocity gradient (Theorem
3.2
).

5
Guidelines on imposing Dirichlet-type pressure boundary conditions

We now use the Stokes well-posedness estimate from Theorem
3.2
along with the numerical results from Section
4
to provide guidelines for imposing Dirichlet-type pressure boundary conditions in different scenarios. When combined with Dirichlet velocity boundary conditions, we find that:

•

p_{h}|_{\Delta}=0

is recommended
when
\Delta
does not scale with mesh size,

•

p_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

is not recommended
,

•

p_{h}(\bm{x})=0
for a single point
\bm{x}\in\partial\Omega
or
\bm{x}\in\Omega

is not recommended
.

The first case represents the standard setting when (i)
\Delta\subset\Omega
and
|\Delta|>0
. According to Theorem
3.2
we have
C_{\Delta}^{-1}<\infty
and well-posedness.

The second and third cases arise in special situations:
(ii) when
\Delta
is a ring around
\partial\Omega
and
|\Delta|\to 0
, or (iii) when
\Delta
is a ball centered at
\bm{x}\in\overline{\Omega}
and
|\Delta|\to 0
.
In these limiting cases, Theorem
3.2
implies that
C_{\Delta}^{-1}\to\infty
, indicating a loss of well-posedness.
This aligns with our numerical experiments, where we observed that
\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to\infty
as
h\to 0
when the boundary condition data included a large perturbation. However, for small perturbations, we did not observe unbounded growth of
\|p_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
as
h\to 0
.
Thus, while there are cases where the pressure boundary condition
p_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
does not lead to spurious pressure growth, the condition
p_{h}|_{\Delta}=0
has the distinct advantage of preventing such growth entirely, regardless of the perturbation size. In PDE model coupling methods
ISSM_iscal
;
iscal
and domain decomposition methods
domain_decomposition_toselli2006
, boundary condition data inherently contain model errors. For robustness, we therefore recommend using
p_{h}|_{\Delta}=0
.

Another important consideration is the choice of
\Delta
. When
\Delta
is mesh dependent, the size of
\Delta
scales as
|\Delta|\propto h^{d}
in
d
dimensions.
This leads to
C_{\Delta}\propto|\Delta|\propto h^{d}
, which causes

C_{\Delta}^{-1}\to\infty
as
h\to 0
. To avoid this issue, we recommend always choosing
\Delta
independent of the mesh size, with its size proportional to the size of
\Omega
. This ensures that
C_{\Delta}^{-1}
remains finite in Theorem
3.2
thereby maintaining well-posedness.

6
Final remarks

In this paper, we investigated the well-posedness of the Stokes problem (
6
) under Dirichlet boundary conditions, as stated in (
2
). Specifically, we considered the case where the velocity vanishes on
\partial\Omega
and the pressure vanishes over a region
\Delta\subset\Omega
with
|\Delta|>0
. The associated infinite-dimensional function spaces for velocity and pressure were
\bm{V}=\bm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
Q=L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
, respectively.

We found that the Stokes problem is well-posed, with the a priori pressure estimate depending on
\Delta
, as stated in Theorem
3.2
. This result arises from our extension of the Nečas inequality to
L^{2}_{\Delta}(\Omega)
spaces, as detailed in Theorem
3.1
. Specifically, we found that the constant
C_{\Delta}>0
in Theorem
3.1
scales with
|\Delta|^{1/2}

when the pressure function approaches a constant. This represents the limiting case where pressure uniqueness is violated. Our numerical experiments confirmed these theoretical observations.
Building on these results, we provided guidelines for imposing pressure Dirichlet boundary conditions when solving the Stokes problem (Section
5
). These guidelines can be directly applied in PDE model coupling methods, such as those in
ISSM_iscal
;
iscal
.

Future work includes applying the derived well-posedness estimates to the convergence analysis of overlapping domain decomposition methods for Stokes and Navier-Stokes problems. Additionally, the extended Nečas inequality could be used for well-posedness analysis of numerical discretization methods employing discontinuous trial spaces.

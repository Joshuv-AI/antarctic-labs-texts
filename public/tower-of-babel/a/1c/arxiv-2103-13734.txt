Efficiency and complexity of hyperplane arrangements
Morihiko Saito

Abstract
. For a projective hyperplane arrangement, we study sufficient conditions in terms of combinatorial data for ESV-calculability of the monodromy eigenspaces of the first Milnor fiber cohomology for eigenvalues of order m > 1 m>1 . This can be reduced to the line arrangement case by Artin’s vanishing theorem. These sufficient conditions are often unsatisfied if efficiency or complexity of the combinatorics of arrangement is high. In order to measure these, we introduce the notions of m m -efficiency and m m -complexity for m ⩾ 3 m\,{\geqslant}\,3 . The former is defined to be the number of points with multiplicity divisible by m m lying on one line in average. In many cases, one of the above sufficient conditions is satisfied if it is at most 2, although there are certain exceptional cases, especially when m = 3 m\,{=}\,3 . The m m -complexity is defined to be the maximal number of edges containing one vertex of the associated m m -graph. We can show that one of the sufficient condition holds if it is at most ( m + 1 ) / 2 (m{+}1)/2 .

1. ESV-calculation

In this section we recall some basics of Aomoto complexes, and prove Theorems 1 and 2.

1.1. Aomoto complexes.
Let
L\subset Y:={\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
be a reduced hyperplane arrangement of degree
d
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}(L)
be the intersection poset consisting of any intersections of the irreducible components
L_{i}
of
L
(
i\in\{1,\dots,d\})
. This contains the ambient space
Y
, but not the empty set. Set
U:=Y\setminus L
. By
[
Br
]
,
[
OS
]
, there is an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras

[formula]

A_{{\mathcal{S}}(L)}^{\raise 0.45206pt\hbox{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}\,\,\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 4.2679pt\raise 3.98337pt\hbox{$\sim$}\hss}\hbox{$\longrightarrow$}\,\,H^{\raise 0.45206pt\hbox{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}(U,{\mathbb{C}}),

where
A_{{\mathcal{S}}(L)}^{\raise 0.45206pt\hbox{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}
is a quotient algebra of the exterior algebra

\bigwedge^{\raise 0.45206pt\hbox{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}\bigl(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d-1}{\mathbb{C}}e_{i}\bigr)
divided by an ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
, and is called the Orlik-Solomon algebra. Note that the induced affine arrangement on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}={\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\setminus L_{d}
is used here so that the
e_{i}
are identified with
{\rm d}g_{i}/g_{i}
, where
g_{i}
is a linear function with a constant term defining
L_{i}\setminus L_{d}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n-1}
.
Moreover, the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}
is determined by the combinatorial data, see
loc. cit.

From now on,
assume

n\,{=}\,3
. Let
\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}

(i\in\{1,\dots,d\})
satisfying the conditions :

[formula]

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\quad\quad\quad\hbox{$\sum$}_{i=1}^{d}\,\alpha_{i}=0,

\displaystyle\alpha_{P}:=\hbox{$\sum$}_{L_{i}\ni P}\,\alpha_{i}\,\notin\,{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\quad\quad(\forall\,P\in L^{[\geqslant 3]}),

where
L^{[\geqslant 3]}
is as in the introduction. Set

[formula]

\omega=\hbox{$\sum$}_{i=1}^{d-1}\alpha_{i}e_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{1}_{{\mathcal{S}}(L)}.

This defines a complex
({\mathcal{A}}^{\raise 0.45206pt\hbox{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{{\mathcal{S}}(L)},\omega\wedge)
,
called the
Aomoto complex
associated to
\omega
.

Since the
e_{i}
are identified with
{\rm d}g_{i}/g_{i}
, we get also a regular singular connection
\nabla^{\omega}

on
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
such that

[formula]

\nabla^{\omega}h={\rm d}h+h\hskip 1.0pt\omega\quad(h\in{\mathcal{O}}_{U}).

The main theorem of
[
ESV
]
asserts that if condition (1.1.2) is satisfied, then we have the isomorphisms

[formula]

H^{j}({\mathcal{A}}^{\raise 0.45206pt\hbox{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{{\mathcal{S}}(L)},\omega\wedge)\,\,\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 4.2679pt\raise 3.98337pt\hbox{$\sim$}\hss}\hbox{$\longrightarrow$}\,\,H^{j}_{{\rm DR}}\bigl(U,({\mathcal{O}}_{U},\nabla^{\omega})\bigr)\quad(j\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

In this case we say that the de Rham cohomology of
\nabla^{\omega}
is ESV-calculable.
If the last condition of (1.1.2) is not satisfied, we have only the injectivity of

[formula]

H^{1}({\mathcal{A}}^{\raise 0.45206pt\hbox{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}_{{\mathcal{S}}(L)},\omega\wedge)\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{{\rm DR}}\bigl(U,({\mathcal{O}}_{U},\nabla^{\omega})\bigr).

In the Milnor fiber cohomology case, no example of a non-surjective morphism seems to be known for
m\,{>}\,3
, see Example (3.1) below for the case
m\,{=}\,3
.

1.2. Proof of Theorem 1.
It is well-known (see
[
Di1
]
,
[
CS
]
,
[
BS
]
,
etc.
) that the monodromy eigenspace
H^{j}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}
vanishes unless
\lambda^{d}\,{=}\,1
, and there are local systems
{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}
of rank 1 on
U
(
k=0,...,d{-}1
) such that

[formula]

H^{j}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}=H^{j}(U,{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)})\quad\quad\bigl(\lambda=\exp(-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\hskip 1.0ptk/d)\bigr).

Moreover the monodromy around any irreducible component of
L
is given by multiplication by
\lambda^{-1}=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}\hskip 1.0ptk/d)
. In particular,
{\mathcal{L}}^{(0)}={\mathbb{C}}_{U}
so that
H^{j}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{1}=H^{j}(U,{\mathbb{C}})
.

It is also known that the dimensions of
H^{j}(F_{\!f},\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})_{\lambda}
(and
H^{j}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}
) are
independent
of
\lambda\in\mu_{d}
with
{\rm ord}\,\lambda\,{=}\,m
for a fixed
m
. (Here
\mu_{d}:=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid\lambda^{d}\,{=}\,1\}
.) Indeed, these
\lambda
are called
primitive roots of unity
of order
m
, and are
conjugate
to each other under the action of the Galois group of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{\mathbb{Q}}
(by the irreducibility of cyclotomic polynomials).
In particular, we may assume
\lambda\,{=}\,\exp(\pm 2\pi\sqrt{-1}/m)
in (1.2.1), that is,

[formula]

\tfrac{k}{d}=\tfrac{1}{m}\quad\hbox{or}\quad 1\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\tfrac{1}{m}.

Here one can take a convenient choice as one likes.

The local system
{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}
is then isomorphic to the solution local system of the connection
\nabla^{\omega}
on
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
in (1.1) by setting

[formula]

\alpha_{i}=\begin{cases}1\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\tfrac{1}{m}&(i\in J),\\
-\tfrac{1}{m}&(i\notin J),\end{cases}\quad\quad\hbox{or}\quad\quad\alpha_{i}=\begin{cases}-1\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0pt\tfrac{1}{m}&(i\in J).\\
\tfrac{1}{m}&(i\notin J),\end{cases}

where
J\subset I:=\{1,\dots,d\}
is a subset with
|J|=\tfrac{d}{m}
. (Note that a local system of rank 1 on the complement of a curve in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is determined by the local monodromies around the
smooth points
of the curve, taking the tensor product of a local system with the inverse of another, since the singular points of the curve have codimension 2 in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, and
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
contains a simply connected Zariski-open subset
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.)

In order to get the isomorphism (1.1.5) (that is, the
\lambda
-eigenspace of the Milnor fiber cohomology is ESV-calculable), the last condition of (1.1.2) should be satisfied for the
\alpha_{i}
defined by one of the equalities in (1.2.3). Setting

J_{P}:=\bigl\{i\in J\,\big|\,L_{i}\ni P\bigr\},\quad I_{P}:=\bigl\{i\in I\,\big|\,L_{i}\ni P\bigr\},

the conditions are expressed respectively by the following inequalities for
P\in L^{[(m)]}\cap L^{[\geqslant 3]}
:

m\hskip 1.0pt|J_{P}|\leqslant|I_{P}|,\quad m\hskip 1.0pt|J_{P}|\geqslant|I_{P}|.

Let
k_{P},k^{\prime}_{P}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
with
P\in L_{J}^{[k_{P}]}\cap L^{[k^{\prime}_{P}m]}
, that is,
|J_{P}|=k_{P}
,
|I_{P}|=k^{\prime}_{P}m
. The above conditions are then equivalent to conditions (a) and (b) respectively. This finishes the proof of Theorem 1.

. (In the case

We have a natural projection

so that

-bundle over

, and the exceptional divisor of the blow-up is the zero-section (where

). In the notation of

Set

. This has the weight filtration

such that

For

Let

2. Necessity of the hypotheses in Problem 1.

In this section we explain why the assumptions are needed in Problem 1.

2.1.
m
-graphs.
For a line arrangement
L\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 3}
, the
m
-graph is defined as follows: Its vertices are identified with the points of
L^{[(m)]}
. Its edges correspond to lines in
L
containing at least two points of
L^{[(m)]}
, and are expressed by closed connected smooth real curves whose both ends are vertices. An edge may contain vertices in its interior so that the vertices contained in it are identified with the points of
L^{[(m)]}
contained in the corresponding line in
L
. We assume that two edges containing a same vertex have different limit tangent lines. (Note that an intersection of edges is not always a vertex of the graph unless a vertex is marked at the intersection point. If two edges intersect at a point which is not a vertex, we consider that they do not intersect as if the graph is in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.)

This is a weighted graph, where a vertex has weight
k
if its corresponding point belongs to
L^{[km]}
. We can decide whether condition (a) or (b) is satisfied by looking at this graph. Indeed, the number of lines in
L
containing only a given vertex is the difference between the weight of the vertex multiplied by
m
and the number of edges containing the vertex. The weights are omitted if these are always 1, that is, if
L
is
m
-reduced.
(Note that the
m
-graph cannot be used to determine whether
L
supports a multinet. For this we have to employ the
complete graph
whose vertices are all the singular points of
L
.)

For instance, let
f=(x^{a}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pty^{a})(x^{a}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptz^{a})(y^{a}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptz^{a})
(

) as in Example (3.1) below. For

(resp. 3), this is the simplest example with
H^{1}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}\neq 0
for

(resp. one of the simplest ESV-non-calculable examples).
Its

-graph can be drawn as follows (here we use piecewise smooth curves whose tangent lines vary continuously by technical reasons):

2.2. Disconnected

Assume

with

2.3. Subarrangements of
m
-star type, I.
For any line arrangement

, any

(ii)

\,L^{\prime}{}^{[(m)]}\cup L^{\prime\prime}{}^{[(m)]}=L^{[(m)]},\quad L^{\prime}{}^{[(m)]}\cap L^{\prime\prime}{}^{[(m)]}=\{P\},

\,d^{\prime}:=\deg L^{\prime}

\,d^{\prime\prime}:=\deg L^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle d\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptd^{\prime}=d^{\prime\prime}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptk_{P}m=k_{L^{\prime\prime}}\hskip 1.0ptm,\quad\quad E_{L^{\prime\prime},m}=(k_{L^{\prime\prime}}{+}k_{P}{+}k_{Q})\hskip 1.0ptm/d^{\prime\prime},

It is known that there is a close relation between resonance varieties, pencils, and multinets, and there are no

[formula]

Example 3.5.
For
m\geqslant 3
, let

f=\hbox{$\prod$}_{i,j=0}^{m-1}\,(\zeta^{i}x\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{j}y\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0ptz),

with
\zeta:=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/m}
. (This is a subarrangement of the Hessian arrangement when
m\,{=}\,3
.)
We have
d\,{=}\,m^{2}
,
|L^{[m]}|=|L^{[(m)]}|=3\hskip 1.0ptm
with
L^{[m]}=L\cap\{xyz\,{=}\,0\}
, and
{\rm Sing}\,L=L^{[2]}\cup L^{[m]}
. Indeed, the last assertion can be reduced to the injectivity of the map

\Theta_{i}{\times}\Theta_{j}\ni(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\mapsto\lambda/\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}},

with
\Theta_{i}:=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\mid|\lambda\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{i}|\,{=}\,1,\,\lambda\neq 0\}
, calculating the intersection points of lines in
L
. (Here we get that
x=-(\zeta^{j^{\prime}}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{j})/(\zeta^{i^{\prime}}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{i})
with
i,j
fixed after setting
y\,{=}\,1
.)
W may assume
i\,{=}\,j\,{=}\,0
using the action of
\mu_{m}
on
{\mathbb{C}}
. We have

\Theta_{0}=\bigl\{re^{i\theta}\,\big|\,\,r=-2\cos\theta,\,\,\theta\in\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi,\tfrac{3}{2}\pi\bigr)\bigr\}.

If
\lambda_{1}/\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\,{=}\,\lambda_{2}/\lambda^{\prime}_{2}
, we have
\arg\lambda_{1}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\arg\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\,{=}\,\arg\lambda_{2}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\arg\lambda^{\prime}_{2}
(which is denoted by
\alpha\in(0,\pi)
).
Setting
\,\theta\,{=}\,\arg\lambda^{\prime}
(so that
\,\arg\lambda\,{=}\,\theta\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0pt\alpha
), the assertion is then reduced to the injectivity of the map

\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\pi,\tfrac{3}{2}\pi\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\alpha\bigr)\ni\theta\mapsto\cos(\theta\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0pt\alpha)/\cos\theta\in{\mathbb{R}},

where the image is equal to
\,\cos\alpha\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0pt\sin\alpha\tan\theta
. So the assertion follows. We thus get that

\tfrac{d}{m}\,{=}\,m,\quad E_{L,m}\,{=}\,3.

In the
m
even case, we can verify that
H^{1}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}
for
{\rm ord}\,\lambda\,{=}\,m
is
not
ESV-calculable as follows: Assume
J
is given by

J=\bigl\{([a_{k}],[b_{k}])\in({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}\mid k\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr\},

with
a_{k},b_{k}\in[0,m{-}1]
. Here
(i,j)\in({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
is identified with a line defined by the equation

\zeta^{i}x\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{j}y\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0ptz\,{=}\,0.

In order to satisfy condition (a) or (b), we must have

\{a_{k}\}=\{b_{k}\}=\{a_{k}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptb_{k}\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0ptm\delta_{k}\}=\{0,\dots,m{-}1\},

taking the intersection with
\{y\,{=}\,0\}
,
\{x\,{=}\,0\}
,
\{z\,{=}\,0\}
respectively.
Here
\delta_{k}=1
if
a_{k}<b_{k}
, and 0 otherwise. We then get that

\hbox{$\sum$}_{k=0}^{m-1}\,a_{k}=\hbox{$\sum$}_{k=0}^{m-1}\,b_{k}=\hbox{$\sum$}_{k=0}^{m-1}\,(a_{k}\hskip 1.0pt{-}\hskip 1.0ptb_{k}\hskip 1.0pt{+}\hskip 1.0ptm\delta_{k})=m(m{-}1)/2.

However, these imply that
\hbox{$\sum$}_{k}\,\delta_{k}=(m{-}1)/2
, which is a contradiction, since
m
is even and
\delta_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. So ESV-non-calculability follows.

In the
m
odd case, it is easy to see that
H^{1}(F_{\!f},{\mathbb{C}})_{\lambda}
for
{\rm ord}\,\lambda\,{=}\,m
is ESV-calculable, since
J
can be given by

J=\bigl\{(i,2i)\in({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}\mid i\in{\mathbb{Z}}/m\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr\}.

Note that the multiplication by 2 is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}/m\hskip 1.0pt{\mathbb{Z}}
(with
m
odd).

Carrollian Born-Infeld Electrodynamics
Aditya Mehra, Hemant Rathi, Dibakar Roychowdhury

Abstract
In this paper, we carry out a detailed investigation on Born-Infeld (BI) Electrodynamics in its Carrollian limit. We show that the BI Electrodynamics in its Carrollian limit, differs significantly from its conformal Carrollian cousin. For example, the full conformal Carroll symmetry group is broken due to the presence of nonlinear interactions among U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge fields. Moreover, these theories do not allow for an underlying Lagrangian description, which is also an artefact of quartic interactions among U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge fields. Most interestingly, the two point correlation depends upon the square of the distance of separation between two spatial points and does not exhibit the usual power law behaviour as in the case of conformal Carrollian Electrodynamics. We identify this as an artefact of the broken dilation invariance in the Carrollian limit of BI Electrodynamics. Finally, in the limit of the vanishing BI coupling, we recover the standard results of conformal Carrollian Electrodynamics.

U(1)

4
Carrollian limit of Born-Infeld Electrodynamics

We restrict our discussions to four-dimensional space-time. Born-Infeld (BI) theory is a theory describing the non-linear behaviour of Electrodynamics
Born:1934gh
. Our goal will be to write down a Lagrangian for the BI theory that is invariant under gauge and Lorentz transformations. In addition to these requirements, the Lagrangian must satisfy the following two constraints. First, it must reduce to the Maxwell Lagrangian for small values of
\overrightarrow{E}
and
\overrightarrow{B}
. Second, there should be a maximal electric field when
\overrightarrow{B}=0
. In non-linear Electrodynamics, a maximal electric field is needed to obtain point charges with finite self-energy.

Let us consider the Lagrangian density as
Zwiebach:2004tj

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-b^{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{2s}{b^{2}}-\frac{p^{2}}{b^{4}}}+b^{2},

(27)

where
s=-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu},~p=-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}
along with
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}
.

The parameter
b
is related to the string length and is given by the expression
b=E_{crit}=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}
. Since the Lagrangian density is built from

and
p
, one can see that it is Lorentz invariant. For small fields where
s
and

We see that (

This form allows a generalization to any number of dimensions. The two densities are the same in four dimensions and (

\displaystyle{\delta}_{\mu}^{\mbox{\tiny{G}}}\,A_{\nu}(x)=\partial_{\mu}\alpha_{\nu}(x),~~{\delta}_{\mu}^{\mbox{\tiny{P}}}\,A_{\nu}(x)=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}(x),

However, it is not invariant under the scale and the special conformal transformations. The symmetries of this theory can also be checked by looking into the action of these transformations at the level of the equations of motion.
We now look into the Carrollian limit of Born-Infeld Electrodynamics in details. First, we explore what happens to the equations of motion in both the electric and magnetic limits. The leading part will always be the vanilla Carrollian Electrodynamics equations of motion that are mentioned before. On the other hand, the subleading part of these equations will be interesting in many ways. 5
Remarks about the Lagrangian formulation of Carrollian BI theory

5.1
Helmholtz conditions

In classical physics, we start from an action (
S
) or equivalently a Lagrangian description and find the corresponding equations of motion using a variational principle. In some situations (such as Carrollian conformal field theories), we only have the equations of motion that define the dynamics of the system but not the Lagrangian. The natural question to ask is whether there exists an underlying Lagrangian description corresponding to these equations of motion? The answer depends upon a set of necessary and sufficient conditions that term as the Helmholtz conditions
Henneaux:1984ke
;
nigam2016brief
;
Morandi:1990su
. These conditions are also known as the “inverse” problem of calculus of variations. We suggest the reader to look into
bams/1183493292
;
jdouglas
for further understanding on this subject.

Let us consider a theory that is represented in terms of fields
3
3

3

For the present example, these are the components (
A_{t},A_{i}
) of the gauge field.

w^{\beta}
(here the indices
\alpha,\beta,\dots
indicate the number of fields and can go from
1
to
N
). The equations of motion for these fields are denoted by
E_{\alpha}
. The necessary and sufficient Helmholtz conditions
Henneaux:1984ke
corresponding to these equations of motion can be found from the variation of the action
functional
S[u^{\beta}]=\int d^{n}x\mathcal{L}(w^{\beta},w_{a}^{\tau},w^{\delta}_{ab},x^{a})
and are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\alpha}}{\partial(w_{ab})^{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial(w_{ab})^{\alpha}}

(66a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\alpha}}{\partial{w_{a}}^{\beta}}+\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial{w_{a}}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\partial_{b}\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial(w_{ba})^{\alpha}}

(66b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{E_{\alpha}}}{\partial{w^{\beta}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial w^{\alpha}}-\partial_{a}\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial{w_{a}}^{\alpha}}+\partial_{a}\partial_{b}{\frac{\partial E_{\beta}}{\partial(w_{ab})^{\alpha}}}

(66c)

where

Below, we summarise all the necessary steps to check the Helmholtz criteria.

1.

The equations of motion will be passed through the Helmholtz criteria (

4

2

2.

In this step, we append the equations minimally with new fields in accordance with well defined GCA transformation rules, such that the equations satisfy the Helmholtz criteria (

3.

The new series of equations of motion will then be further constrained by requiring them to give back the original equations when the newly introduced field(s) is (are) taken to be zero.

4.

Finally, the equations of motion should possess the symmetric structure under consideration (for example, we will take the Carrollian conformal algebra in this paper). To see that, we check the symmetric structures of the given theory and if the theory possesses such symmetries, then one can construct a Lagrangian.

We will investigate for an action in both the magnetic and electric limits of BI theory.

represent the components of gauge field (
A_{t},\hskip 2.84526ptA_{i}
). Now, one can easily solve the above differential equations (
73
) for various symmetry generators (
O
) and obtain the required correlation functions.

We now move on to estimate these correlation functions in the Carrollian Born-Infeld limit that includes both electric as well as magnetic limits.

6.1
Electric Limit

\bullet

Case I : Correlation between
A_{t}(x_{1},t_{1})
and
A_{t}(x_{2},t_{2})

The 2-point correlation between the temporal components of the gauge field is defined as

\displaystyle G_{00}^{(E)}(t_{1},t_{2},x_{1},x_{2})\equiv\langle A_{t}(t_{1},x_{1})A_{t}(t_{2},x_{2})\rangle.

(74)

Notice that, the invariance under temporal and spatial translation implies that
G_{00}^{(E)}
can only depend on the difference between the coordinates i.e.
\tau=t_{1}-t_{2}
and
r^{i}=x^{i}_{1}-x^{i}_{2}
. Using this fact and using the boost operator (
35
) in equation (
73
), one finds

\displaystyle r_{i}\partial_{\tau}G_{00}^{(E)}=0.

(75)

Equation (
75
) suggests that
G_{00}
can at most depend on
r_{i}
. However, one can further refine the form of
G_{00}^{(E)}
by requiring that the correlation functions must be invariant under rotation, which yields

\displaystyle G_{00}^{(E)}=c_{0}r^{2},

(76)

where
c_{0}
is an arbitrary constant and
r^{2}=r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+r_{3}^{2}
.

Finally, it is straightforward to check that the other symmetry operators i.e. temporal special conformal transformation (
K
) and Carrollian supertranslations (
M^{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}}
) also produce the same result.

\bullet

Case II : Correlation between
A_{t}(x_{1},t_{1})
and
A_{i}(x_{2},t_{2})

Two point correlation between the temporal and the spatial component of the gauge field can be expressed as

\displaystyle G_{0i}^{(E)}(t_{1},t_{2},x_{1},x_{2})\equiv\langle A_{t}(t_{1},x_{1})A_{i}(t_{2},x_{2})\rangle.

(77)

Similar to the previous case, the temporal and spatial translation invariance demand that
G_{0i}^{(E)}
can only depend on
\tau
and
r^{i}
. Furthermore, the boost symmetry (
35
) implies that

\displaystyle r_{l}\partial_{\tau}G_{0i}^{(E)}+\delta_{li}G_{00}^{(E)}=0.

(78)

Now we solve the above equation (
78
) for different values of
l
and
i
. Notice that, for
l=1
and
i=2
, we find
G_{02}^{(E)}=\mathcal{F}_{1}(r^{i})
. However, for
l=i=2
, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{02}^{(E)}=-c_{0}\frac{r^{2}}{r_{2}}\tau+\mathcal{F}_{2}(r^{i}).

(79)

In order to obtain a unique solution
\Big(G_{02}^{(E)}\Big)
, we must therefore set
c_{0}=0
together with
\mathcal{F}_{1}(r^{i})=\mathcal{F}_{2}(r^{i})=\mathcal{F}(r^{i})
. Setting,
c_{0}=0
implies that the correlation between the temporal components of gauge field
\Big(G_{00}^{(E)}\Big)
must vanish in the electric limit.

The rotational symmetry further restricts the form of
G_{0i}^{(E)}
as follows

\displaystyle G_{0i}^{(E)}=c_{i}r^{2},

(80)

where
c_{i}
,
i=(1,2,3)
are the constants. It is now straightforward to check that the temporal special conformal transformation (
K
) and the Carrollian supertranslations (
M^{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}}
) also lead to the same conclusion.

\bullet

Case III : Correlation between
A_{i}(x_{1},t_{1})
and
A_{j}(x_{2},t_{2})

The two point correlation between the spatial components of gauge field is defined as as

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{(E)}(t_{1},t_{2},x_{1},x_{2})\equiv\langle A_{i}(t_{1},x_{1})A_{j}(t_{2},x_{2})\rangle.

(81)

Like before, the temporal and spatial translation invariance restrict the dependencies of
G_{ij}^{(E)}
only to
\tau
and
r^{i}
. Moreover, the invariance of
G_{ij}^{(E)}
under the boost symmetry (
35
) requires that

\displaystyle r_{l}\partial_{\tau}G_{ij}^{(E)}+\delta_{li}G_{0j}^{(E)}+\delta_{lj}G_{i0}^{(E)}=0.

(82)

Next, we solve the above equation (
82
) for different values of
l,i
and
j
. First, we consider a case with
l=1,i=2,j=3
. For, these values of (
l,i,j
), we find
G_{23}=\mathcal{K}_{1}(r^{i})
. On the other hand, for the choice
l=i=2,j=3
, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{23}^{(E)}=-c_{3}\frac{r^{2}}{r_{2}}\tau+\mathcal{K}_{2}(r^{i}).

(83)

In order to obtain a unique solution of the correlation function
G_{23}
, we must therefore identify the function
\mathcal{K}_{1}(r^{i})
with
\mathcal{K}_{2}(r^{i})
and set,
c_{3}=0
. Similarly, one can check that uniqueness of the other correlation function i.e.
G_{12}^{(E)}
and
G_{31}^{(E)}
requires to set
c_{2}=c_{1}=0
. In other words, the two point correlation between the temporal and spatial components of the gauge field must vanish in the electric limit.

Finally, the rotational symmetry refines the form of the correlation function
G_{ij}^{(E)}
as

\displaystyle G_{ij}^{(E)}=c\delta_{ij}r^{2},

(84)

where
c
is an arbitrary constant. One can also reach the same conclusion (
84
) using the temporal special conformal transformation (
K
) and Carrollian supertranslations (
M^{m_{1}m_{2}m_{3}}
).

6.2
Magnetic limit

The derivation of the correlations functions in the magnetic limit are quite analogous to that of the electric limit. Moreover, all the correlations functions depend only on
\tau
and
r^{i}
as required by the temporal and spatial translation invariance in the Carroll BI limit.

\bullet

Case I : Correlation between
A_{t}(x_{1},t_{1})
and
A_{t}(x_{2},t_{2})

In order to compute the correlation function
G^{(M)}_{00}
, we first plug in the boost operator (
50
) in the master equation (
73
), which yields

\displaystyle r_{l}\partial_{\tau}G^{(M)}_{00}+2G^{(M)}_{l0}=0,

(85)

where
G^{(M)}_{l0}
is the correlation between the spatial and temporal component of gauge field in the magnetic limit. The rotational invariance further demands that

\displaystyle G^{(M)}_{00}=a_{0}r^{2}\hskip 2.84526pt,\hskip 5.69054ptG^{(M)}_{l0}=0,

(86)

where
a_{0}
is an arbitrary constant. It is now straightforward to check that the above results (
86
) are consistent with the Carrollian Supertransalations invariance as well.

\bullet

Case II : Correlations
\langle A_{t}(x_{1},t_{1})A_{i}(x_{2},t_{2})\rangle
and
\langle A_{i}(x_{1},t_{1})A_{j}(x_{2},t_{2})\rangle

On plugging the boost operator (

\displaystyle r_{l}\partial_{\tau}G^{(M)}_{0i}+G^{(M)}_{li}=0\hskip 2.84526pt,\hskip 5.69054ptr_{l}\partial_{\tau}G^{(M)}_{ij}=0

Using (

This result is also consistent with the Carrollian Supertransalation invariance.

7
Conclusion and future direction

We now summarise the key findings of our analysis and draw a comparative analysis between the Carrollian limits of usual Maxwell Electrodynamics
Basu_2018
;
Bagchi:2019clu
and the Born-Infeld Electrodynamics that we discuss in this paper. We also outline some interesting projects down the line.

To begin with, we explore the symmetries of the Carrollian BI (CBI) Electrodynamics at the level of the equations of motion (EOM) in both the electric as well as magnetic limits. It is crucial to notice that in the magnetic limit, we must also rescale the BI coupling
\big(1/b^{2}\big)
together with the components of gauge field in order to achieve a consistent
c\rightarrow 0
limit. However, on the other hand, one does not need to scale the BI coupling in the electric limit.

Our analysis on the gauge symmetry reveals that all the EOMs are invariant under
U(1)
gauge transformations in both limits, provided the gauge parameter
(\Lambda)
is also scaled appropriately, which is consistent with the usual notion of the Carrollian Electrodynamics
Basu_2018
;
Bagchi:2019clu
.

Our analysis further shows that the equations of motion in the CBI limit are invariant under the temporal Special Conformal transformation
(K)
, boost
(B_{l})
, and Supertranslations
(M)
transformations. However, it breaks the invariance under the spatial Special Conformal transformation
(K_{i})
and dilatation
(D)
due to the presence of the non-linear interactions in the theory (
27
) that introduce a mass scale namely the (inverse) BI coupling
\big(1/b^{2}\big)
. On the other hand, if we switch off the BI interactions
\big(1/b^{2}\big)
, we restore the standard Carrollian Electrodynamics results
Bagchi_2016
. This is the first significant difference that we notice between the two theories in the Carrollian limit.

It is noteworthy to mention that in this paper, we consider only up to leading order in the BI coupling
\big(1/b^{2}\big)
. However, it is quite straightforward to check that the equations of motion of CBI Electrodynamics remain invariant under the
K
,
B_{l}
, and
M
transformations at NNLO in the BI coupling
\big(1/b^{2}\big)
. For example, in the electric limit, the equation of motion (
32
) upto order
\big(1/b^{4}\big)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\Bigg[F_{it}\Bigg(1-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}\Big(F_{tj}\Big)^{2}+\frac{3}{8b^{4}}\Big(F_{tj}\Big)^{4}\Bigg)\Bigg]=0,

(88)

where
\alpha=t,i
.

Under the action of boost
B_{l}
, the above equation (
88
) transforms as

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\Bigg[x_{l}\partial_{t}\Bigg\{F_{it}\Bigg(1-\frac{1}{2b^{2}}\Big(F_{tj}\Big)^{2}+\frac{3}{8b^{4}}\Big(F_{tj}\Big)^{4}\Bigg)\Bigg\}\Bigg],

(89)

which vanishes identically.

Finally, we show that the EOMs of the CBI Electrodynamics do not satisfy the Helmholtz conditions due to the presence of non-trivial interactions. Therefore, unlike the Carrollian Electrodynamics we can not write down a Lagrangian for the CBI Electrodynamics. This turns out to be the second significant difference between the two theories in the Carrollian limit.

However, one can still compute the correlation functions for CBI Electrodynamics following the symmetry arguments
Banerjee:2020qjj
. Our analysis suggests that the correlations between the components of the
U(1)
gauge field (in both electric and magnetic limits) grow as
G\big(x_{1}^{i},x_{2}^{i}\big)\sim\big|x_{1}^{i}-x_{2}^{i}\big|^{2}
, where
\big|x_{1}^{i}-x_{2}^{i}\big|
measures the spatial separation between two points. On the other hand, in the conformal Carrollian Electrodynamics, it is inversely proportional to the distance of separation i.e.
G\big(x_{1}^{i},x_{2}^{i}\big)\sim\big|x_{1}^{i}-x_{2}^{i}\big|^{-2}
, which reflects the scale invariance of the theory in the Carrollian limit.

Notice that the theory is ultra-local therefore
\big|x_{1}^{i}-x_{2}^{i}\big|
cannot be arbitrarily large. In other words, the CBI does not suffer from any large distance divergences. As a future direction, it would be nice to explore the quantum corrections
Banerjee:2023jpi
;
Mehra:2023rmm
to this classical result by coupling CBI with matter fields.

Below, we outline some further directions that might be worth pursuing in the future.

•

It would be nice to explore the Carrollian limit of supersymmetric version of Born-Infeld theory. One can study these theory in the context of superstring theory. This will be a possible venture and it will give rise to new physics in this domain.

•

Finally, it would be an interesting project to study the Carroll gravity coupled with the Born-Infeld theory. In particular, the authors in
deBoer:2023fnj
consider a Carroll gravity coupled with Maxwell Electrodynamics and construct various solutions, including the wormholes, black hole solutions etc., and examine the geodesic structure in the Carrollian limit. Therefore, it would be nice to explore the possible deviations in the presence of BI interactions in the theory.

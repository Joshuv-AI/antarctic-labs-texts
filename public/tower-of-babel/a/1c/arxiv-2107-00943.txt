Abstract
. New sequences of discrete orthogonal polynomials associated with the modified Bessel function K μ ​ ( z ) K_{\mu}(z) or Macdonald function are considered. The corresponding weight function is λ k ​ ρ k + ν + 1 ​ ( t ) / k ! \lambda^{k}\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t)/k! , where k ∈ ℕ 0 , t ≥ 0 , ν > − 1 , 0 < λ < 1 , ρ μ ​ ( z ) = 2 ​ z μ / 2 ​ K μ ​ ( 2 ​ z ) \ k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ t\geq 0,\ \nu>-1,\ 0<\lambda<1,\ \rho_{\mu}(z)=2z^{\mu/2}K_{\mu}\left(2\sqrt{z}\right) . The limit case t = 0 t=0 corresponds to the Meixner polynomials. Various properties, differential-difference recurrence relations are established. The modified sequence of polynomials with the weight λ k ​ ρ k + ν + 1 ​ ( λ ​ t ) / k ! \lambda^{k}\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t)/k! is investigated as well.

1.
Introduction and preliminary results

Let
\nu>-1,\ t\geq 0,\ 0<\lambda<1
be parameters and consider the sequence of orthogonal polynomials
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
of degree
n
on the integers
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, satisfying orthogonality conditions

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)P_{m}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}=\delta_{n,m},\quad 0<\lambda<1.

Here
\delta_{n,m},\ n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is the Kronecker symbol and
\rho_{\mu}(t)=2t^{\mu/2}K_{\mu}\left(2\sqrt{t}\right)
, where
K_{\mu}(z)
is the modified Bessel function or Macdonald function
[
2
]
, Vol. II. It is represented by the integral
[
2
]
, Vol. II

[formula]

\rho_{\mu}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\mu-1}dx,\quad\mu>0,\ t\geq 0.

The choice of
\lambda
in (1.1) is motivated by the convergence of the corresponding series since, evidently, from (1.2) it has the inequality
\rho_{\mu}(t)\leq\Gamma(\mu)
, where
\Gamma(z)
is the Euler gamma function
[
2
]
, Vol. I. Moreover, as we see from (1.2)
\rho_{k+\nu+1}(0)=\Gamma(k+\nu+1)
, and the sequence
\{P_{n}(x;0,\lambda)\}_{n\geq 0}
in (1.1) is related to the Meixner polynomials
[
5
]
.

As an immediate consequence of the asymptotic behavior for the modified Bessel function at infinity and near the origin as a function of
t
(cf.
[
2
]
, Vol. II) we find

[formula]

\rho_{\mu}(t)=O\left(t^{(\mu-|\mu|)/2}\right),\ t\to 0,\ \nu\neq 0,\quad\rho_{0}(t)=O(\log t),\ t\to 0,

[formula]

\rho_{\mu}(t)=O\left(t^{\mu/2-1/4}e^{-2\sqrt{t}}\right),\ t\to+\infty.

This function can be represented in terms of Laguerre polynomials (cf.
[
3
]
, Vol. II, Entry 2.19.4.13 )

[formula]

{(-1)^{n}t^{n}\over n!}\ \rho_{\mu}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\mu+n-1}e^{-x-t/x}L_{n}^{\mu}(x)dx,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Further, it has a relationship with the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral
[
6
]

[formula]

\left(I_{-}^{\alpha}f\right)(t)={1\over\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{\infty}(x-t)^{\alpha-1}f(x)dx,\quad{\rm Re}\alpha>0,

namely, we get the formula

[formula]

\rho_{\mu}(t)=\left(I_{-}^{\mu}\rho_{0}\right)(t),\ \mu>0.

Hence the index law for fractional integrals immediately implies

[formula]

\rho_{\nu+\mu}(t)=\left(I_{-}^{\nu}\rho_{\mu}\right)(t)=\left(I_{-}^{\mu}\rho_{\nu}\right)(t).

The corresponding definition of the fractional derivative presumes the relation
D^{\mu}_{-}=-DI_{-}^{1-\mu}
. Hence for the ordinary
n
-th derivative of
\rho_{\mu}
we find

[formula]

D^{n}\rho_{\mu}(t)=(-1)^{n}\rho_{\mu-n}(t),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Recalling (1.3) and integrating by parts, it is not difficult to establish the following recurrence relation for
\rho_{\mu}

[formula]

\rho_{\mu+1}(t)=\mu\rho_{\mu}(t)+t\rho_{\mu-1}(t),\quad\mu\in\mathbb{R}.

In the operator form it can be written as follows

[formula]

\rho_{\mu+1}(t)=\left(\mu-tD\right)\rho_{\mu}(t).

As is known, up to a normalization factor orthogonality conditions (1.1) are equivalent to the following equalities

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1,

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}(-k)_{j}\over k!}=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1,

where
(a)_{j}=a(a+1)\dots(a+j-1)
is Pochhammer’s symbol
[
2
]
, Vol. I. As it follows from the theory of orthogonal polynomials
[
5
]
, a sequence
\{p_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the three term recurrence relation

[formula]

xp_{n}(x)=A_{n+1}p_{n+1}(x)+B_{n}p_{n}(x)+A_{n}p_{n-1}(x),

where
p_{-1}(x)\equiv 0,\ p_{n}(x)=a_{n}x^{n}+b_{n}x^{n-1}+\dots,
and

[formula]

A_{n}={a_{n-1}\over a_{n}},\quad B_{n}={b_{n}\over a_{n}}-{b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}}.

As a consequence of (1.17) the Christoffel-Darboux formula takes place

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}(x)p_{k}(y)=A_{n+1}\frac{p_{n+1}(x)p_{n}(y)-p_{n}(x)p_{n+1}(y)}{x-y}.

Finally in this section, denoting by
\omega_{k}(t,\lambda)=\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t)\lambda^{k}/k!
, we employ (1.10) to establish the Pearson-type equation for the weight
\omega_{k}

[formula]

k(k-1)\omega_{k}-(k-1)(k+\nu)\lambda\omega_{k-1}-t\lambda^{2}\omega_{k-2}=0.

Differentiating by
t
and recalling (1.9), we get

[formula]

{\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial t}=-{\lambda\over k}\ \omega_{k-1},\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

A differentiation by
\lambda
gives the equality

[formula]

\lambda{\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial\lambda}=k\omega_{k}.

Similarly, via (1.9), (1.10) we derive

{\partial\over\partial\lambda}\left\{\lambda^{\nu+1}\omega_{k}\right\}=(k+\nu+1)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k+\nu}\over k!}=\rho_{k+\nu+2}(t){\lambda^{k+\nu}\over k!}-\rho_{k+\nu}(t){t\lambda^{k+\nu}\over k!}

=\lambda^{\nu-1}(k+1)\omega_{k+1}-{t\lambda^{\nu+1}\over k}\omega_{k-1},

i.e., invoking (1.18), (1.19), we end up with the partial differential-difference equation for the weight
\omega_{k}

[formula]

\lambda{\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial\lambda}-t{\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial t}={\partial\omega_{k+1}\over\partial\lambda}-(\nu+1)\omega_{k}.

Differentiating through by
t
in (1.20) and using (1.18), (1.19), we arrive at the ordinary second order differential equation for the weight
\omega_{k}

[formula]

t{d^{2}\omega_{k}\over dt^{2}}-(k+\nu){d\omega_{k}\over dt}-\omega_{k}=0.

2.
Orthogonal polynomials for the weight
\omega_{k}

In order to proceed our investigation we define the power moment
\mu_{n}(t)
and factorial moment
\gamma_{n}(t)
for the weight
\omega_{k}
as follows

[formula]

\mu_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}k^{n}\over k!},

[formula]

\gamma_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over(k-n)!}.

Their values are given by the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ t>0,\ 0<\lambda<1,\ \nu>-1
. Moments
(2.1),(2.2)
for the weight
\omega_{k}
have the values

[formula]

\mu_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)\ {\lambda^{j}\rho_{\nu+j+1}\left(t(1-\lambda)\right)\over(1-\lambda)^{\nu+j+1}},

[formula]

\gamma_{n}={\lambda^{n}\rho_{\nu+n+1}\left(t(1-\lambda)\right)\over(1-\lambda)^{\nu+n+1}},

where
S(n,j)
are Stirling numbers of the second kind.

Proof.

In fact, employing representation (1.2), we have from (2.1)

\mu_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}k^{n}\over k!}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{(x\lambda)^{k}k^{n}\over k!}dx

[formula]

=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\left(x{d\over dx}\right)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{(x\lambda)^{k}\over k!}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\left(x{d\over dx}\right)^{n}\bigg\{e^{\lambda x}\bigg\}dx,

where interchange of the order of integration and summation and the multiple differentiation under the series sign in (2.5) are permitted via the uniform convergence of the series with respect to
x\geq 0
on closed intervals of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and the estimate

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}k^{n}\over k!}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Gamma(k+\nu+1){\lambda^{k}k^{n}\over k!}<\infty,\quad 0<\lambda<1.

Meanwhile, denoting the differential operator by
D\equiv{d\over dx}
, we appeal to the known operational identity (see
[
4
]
, Section 6.6)

[formula]

\left(xD\right)^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)x^{j}D^{j},

involving Stirling numbers of the second kind
[
4
]
. Therefore, we find from (2.5) and (1.2)

\mu_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)\lambda^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-(1-\lambda)x}x^{\nu+j}dx=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S(n,j)\ {\lambda^{j}\rho_{\nu+j+1}\left(t(1-\lambda)\right)\over(1-\lambda)^{\nu+j+1}}

which yields (2.3). To calculate moments (2.2) we derive

\gamma_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{(x\lambda)^{k}\over(k-n)!}=\lambda^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-(1-\lambda)x}x^{\nu+n}dx

={\lambda^{n}\rho_{\nu+n+1}\left(t(1-\lambda)\right)\over(1-\lambda)^{\nu+n+1}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma 1.

∎

Now we will interpret discrete orthogonality conditions (1.12), (1.13) in terms of the so-called composition orthogonality.

Theorem 1
.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N},\ t>0,\ 0<\lambda<1,\ \nu>-1
. Orthogonality conditions
(1.12),(1.13)
are equivalent to the composition orthogonality relatively to the function
e^{\lambda x}
with respect to the measure
e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}dx
. Precisely, the following equalities hold, respectively,

[formula]

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}P_{n}\left(xD;t,\lambda\right)\left(xD\right)^{j}\left\{e^{\lambda x}\right\}dx=0,\quad j=0,\dots,n-1,

[formula]

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}P_{n}\left(xD;t,\lambda\right)\left\{x^{j}e^{\lambda x}\right\}dx=0,\quad j=0,\dots,n-1.

Proof.

Recalling (1.2), we rewrite (1.12) in the form

[formula]

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda){(x\lambda)^{k}k^{j}\over k!}dx=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1,

where the interchange of the order of integration and summation is justified as above by virtue of the dominated convergence theorem. Then proceeding as in (2.5), we obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda){(x\lambda)^{k}k^{j}\over k!}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\left(xD\right)^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda){(x\lambda)^{k}\over k!}dx

=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\left(xD\right)^{j}P_{n}\left(xD;t,\lambda\right)\left\{e^{\lambda x}\right\}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}P_{n}\left(xD;t,\lambda\right)\left(xD\right)^{j}\left\{e^{\lambda x}\right\}dx.

Consequently, we derive equalities (2.7). In order to prove (2.8) we have, similarly, from (1.13)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}(-k)_{j}\over k!}=(-1)^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda){(x\lambda)^{k}\over(k-j)!}dx

=(-1)^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}P_{n}\left(xD;t,\lambda\right)\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{(x\lambda)^{k}\over(k-j)!}\right\}dx=(-1)^{j}\lambda^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}P_{n}\left(xD;t,\lambda\right)\left\{x^{j}e^{\lambda x}\right\}dx.

Hence we arrive at the conditions (2.8).

∎

.
Orthogonality relations

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}P_{n}\left(xD;t,\lambda\right)\left(xD\right)^{j}\left\{e^{\lambda x}\right\}dx

[formula]

=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{n}\left(-Dx;t,\lambda\right)\left\{e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\right\}\left(xD\right)^{j}\left\{e^{\lambda x}\right\}dx.

Hence, employing a companion of the equality (2.6) from
[
4
]

[formula]

\left(Dx\right)^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}S(m+1,k+1)x^{k}D^{k},

we calculate the composition
\left(Dx\right)^{m}\left\{e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\right\}
, invoking Entry 1.1.3.2 on p. 4 in
[
1
]
. Therefore we obtain

\left(Dx\right)^{m}\left\{e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\right\}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}S(m+1,k+1)\ x^{k}D^{k}\left\{e^{-t/x-x}x^{\nu}\right\}

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(1-\lambda)x-t/x}f_{n}(x;t,\lambda)\ x^{\nu}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j}S(j,i)(\lambda x)^{i}\right)dx=0,\quad j=0,\dots,n-1,

f_{n}(x;t,\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{k+m}a_{n,m}S(m+1,k+1)\ {x^{i}\ k!\over i!}L_{k-i}^{-\nu-1}\left({t\over x}\right).

But (2.14) is equivalent to (2.10). In the same manner conditions (1.13) can be treated.

The difference operators
\Delta
and
\nabla
are defined accordingly

[formula]

\Delta f=f(x+1)-f(x),\quad\nabla f=f(x)-f(x-1).

It is used to establish the structural relation for the orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
. But first we observe via (1.7), (1.8) that these polynomials are Charlier polynomials
[
5
]
in the sense of composition orthogonality, i.e. (1.1) reads

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)P_{m}(k;t,\lambda){(\lambda I_{-})^{k}\over k!}\left\{\rho_{\nu+1}(t)\right\}=\delta_{n,m},\quad 0<\lambda<1.

Now, expanding
P_{n}(x+1;t,\lambda)
in terms of
P_{n}
, we have

[formula]

P_{n}(x+1;t,\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{n,j}P_{j}(x;t,\lambda),

where, evidently,
c_{n,n}=1
. Other coefficients are given by the formula (see (1.1))

[formula]

c_{n,j}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k+1;t,\lambda)P_{j}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}.

Then by virtue of (1.18) we deduce from (2.18)

c_{n,j}=-{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{j}(k-1;t,\lambda){\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial t}=-{1\over\lambda}{\partial\over\partial t}\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{j}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\right\}

[formula]

+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{j}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}(t,\lambda)+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ k{\partial P_{j}\over\partial t}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k},

where we assume that polynomial coefficients are
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
-functions of variables
t,\lambda
and the differentiation under the series sign is allowed on closed intervals, owing to the estimate

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|{\partial\over\partial t}\left\{P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{j}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\right\}\right|

\leq\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\max\left\{\max_{t\in[\alpha,\beta]}\left|{\partial\over\partial t}\left\{a_{n,m}(t,\lambda)a_{j,i}(t,\lambda)\right\}\right|,\max_{t\in[\alpha,\beta]}\left|a_{n,m}(t,\lambda)a_{j,i}(t,\lambda)\right|\right\}

\times\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{m+1}(k-1)^{i}(k+\nu+1)\Gamma(k+\nu){\lambda^{k}\over k!}<\infty,\quad 0<\lambda<1,\ \nu>-1.

Hence by orthogonality we get

[formula]

c_{n,j}={1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{j}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k},\quad j<n-1.

When
j=n-1
, we have from (1.1), (1.14)

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}=A_{n},

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ k{\partial P_{n-1}\over\partial t}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}={1\over a_{n}}{\partial a_{n-1}\over\partial t},

and therefore we end up from (2.19) with the value

[formula]

c_{n,n-1}={1\over\lambda}{A_{n}\over a_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}.

In the meantime, recalling (2.20) and the Christoffel-Darboux formula (1.16), we calculate the sum

\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}c_{n,j}P_{j}(x;t,\lambda)={1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k\omega_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}P_{j}(x;t,\lambda)P_{j}(k-1;t,\lambda)

[formula]

={A_{n-1}\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\frac{k\omega_{k}}{x-k+1}\bigg[P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)P_{n-2}(k-1;t,\lambda)-P_{n-2}(x;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\bigg].

Thus, combining with (2.17), (2.19), (2.21), (2.22), (2.24) we establish the structural relation for the sequence
\{P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Theorem 2
.
For the orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
it has the equality

P_{n}(x+1;t,\lambda)=P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)+{A_{n}\over\lambda a_{n}}\ {\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)

+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\frac{k\omega_{k}}{x-k+1}\bigg[P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)\bigg[A_{n-1}P_{n-2}(k-1;t,\lambda)+(x-k+1)P_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\bigg]

[formula]

\bigg.-A_{n-1}P_{n-2}(x;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\bigg].

Corollary 2
.
The following identity holds

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}={\lambda n\over A_{n}}-{A_{n}\over a_{n}}\ {\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}.

Besides,
c_{n,n-1}=n/A_{n}
.

Proof.

Writing (2.25) in terms of the forward difference
\Delta
(2.15), we have

\Delta P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)={A_{n}\over\lambda a_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)+{1\over\lambda}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ kP_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}

+{1\over\lambda}A_{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\frac{k\omega_{k}}{x-k+1}

[formula]

\times\bigg[P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)P_{n-2}(k-1;t,\lambda)-P_{n-2}(x;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}(k-1;t,\lambda)\bigg].

Then, comparing the leading coefficient in the latter equality, we get (2.26). The value of
c_{n,n-1}
follows immediately from (2.23).

Corollary 3.

In terms of the forward difference equality
(2.25)
reads

\Delta P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)={n\over A_{n}}\ P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)+{A_{n-1}\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\frac{k\omega_{k}}{x-k+1}

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial t}=-{2\over a_{n}(t)}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}.

However, the use of the differential equation (1.18) allows us to compute the sum
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k+1,t)\omega_{k}.
In fact, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k+1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k-1}=-{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kP^{2}_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial t}

=-{1\over\lambda}{\partial\over\partial t}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kP^{2}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\right)+{2\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kP_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}.

Hence (1.1), (1.14), (1.15) imply

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k+1;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}=-{1\over\lambda}{\partial B_{n}\over\partial t}+{2\over\lambda}\bigg[{1\over a_{n}}{\partial b_{n}\over\partial t}-{b_{n+1}\over a_{n}a_{n+1}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}\bigg],

i.e. after slight simplification we find the equality

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k+1,t)\omega_{k}={1\over\lambda}\bigg[{\partial\over\partial t}\left({b_{n}\over a_{n}}+{b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}}\right)-{2B_{n}\over a_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}\bigg].

On the other hand, we have from (2.24), (2.28), (1.1)

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k+1,t)\omega_{k}=1+\ {n^{2}\over A^{2}_{n}}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}c^{2}_{n,j}.

Thus, combining with (2.30), we derive

[formula]

\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}c^{2}_{n,j}={1\over\lambda}\bigg[{\partial\over\partial t}\left({b_{n}\over a_{n}}+{b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}}\right)-{2B_{n}\over a_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}\bigg]-1-{n^{2}\over A^{2}_{n}},

where
c_{n,j},\ j<n-1
is defined by (2.20).

Further, decomposing
P_{n}(x+2;t,\lambda)
in terms of
P_{n}
, we find

[formula]

P_{n}(x+2;t,\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}d_{n,j}(t,\lambda)P_{j}(x;t,\lambda).

Hence
d_{n,n}=1
and

[formula]

d_{n,j}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k+2;t,\lambda)P_{j}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}.

Then (1.17), (1.18) suggest the equalities

d_{n,j}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ P_{j}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k-2}={1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)P_{j}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}

-{1\over t\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ (k-1)(k+\nu)P_{j}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k-1}={1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)P_{j}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}

+{1\over t\lambda^{2}}{\partial\over\partial t}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{j}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\right)

-{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{j}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}-{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k,t)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)

[formula]

\times{\partial P_{j}\over\partial t}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}.

Therefore the same analysis shows

[formula]

d_{n,j}=-{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{j}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k},\quad j<n-3.

Then, accordingly, we have from (2.35), (1.1), (1.14), (1.15)

d_{n,n-3}={1\over t\lambda^{2}}{\partial\over\partial t}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{n-3}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\right)

-{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{n-3}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}

-{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu){\partial P_{n-3}\over\partial t}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}

[formula]

=-{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\bigg[{a_{n-3}\over a^{2}_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{n-3}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\bigg],

[formula]

\bigg.-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{n-2}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\bigg].

Now, writing
P_{n}
as

[formula]

P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)=a_{n}x^{n}+b_{n}x^{n-1}+c_{n}x^{n-2}+\hbox{lower degrees},

we recall (1.1), (1.12) to get in a straightforward way the equality

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)k^{n+2}\omega_{k}={b_{n+2}b_{n+1}\over a_{n+2}a_{n+1}a_{n}}-{c_{n+2}\over a_{n+2}a_{n}}.

This identity allows to obtain the value of the coefficient
d_{n,n-1}
. In fact, we derive

d_{n,n-1}={1\over t\lambda^{2}}\bigg[A_{n}\left[B_{n}+B_{n-1}+1-2n\right]\bigg.

+a_{n-1}{\partial\over\partial t}\bigg({b_{n+2}b_{n+1}\over a_{n+2}a_{n+1}a_{n}}-{c_{n+2}\over a_{n+2}a_{n}}\bigg)

-\bigg[b_{n-1}+(\nu+1-2n)a_{n-1}\bigg]{\partial\over\partial t}\bigg({b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}a_{n}}\bigg)

+\bigg[(\nu+2(n-1))a_{n-1}-(\nu-1+2(n-2)^{2})b_{n-1}-c_{n-1}\bigg]{1\over a^{2}_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}

[formula]

\bigg.-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{n-1}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}\bigg].

Then, writing (2.33) in the form

P_{n}(x+2;t,\lambda)-P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)=d_{n,n-1}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)+d_{n,n-2}P_{n-2}(x;t,\lambda)

[formula]

+d_{n,n-3}P_{n-3}(x;t,\lambda)+\sum_{j=0}^{n-4}d_{n,j}P_{j}(x;t,\lambda),

we compare leading coefficients to get
d_{n,n-1}=2n/A_{n}
. Hence from (2.41) we find

{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\ k(k-1)(k+\nu)P_{n-1}(k-2;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}

={1\over t\lambda^{2}}\bigg[A_{n}\left[B_{n}+B_{n-1}+1-2n\right]+a_{n-1}{\partial\over\partial t}\bigg({b_{n+2}b_{n+1}\over a_{n+2}a_{n+1}a_{n}}-{c_{n+2}\over a_{n+2}a_{n}}\bigg)\bigg.

-\bigg[b_{n-1}+(\nu+1-2n)a_{n-1}\bigg]{\partial\over\partial t}\bigg({b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}a_{n}}\bigg)

+\bigg[(\nu+2(n-1))a_{n-1}-(\nu-1+2(n-2)^{2})b_{n-1}-c_{n-1}\bigg]{1\over a^{2}_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}\bigg]-{2n\over A_{n}}.

Furthermore, in the same manner as above by virtue of the Christoffel-Darboux formula (1.16) and identities (2.37), (2.38), (2.41) we establish the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.
For the orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
it has the equality

P_{n}(x+2;t,\lambda)=P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)+{2n\over A_{n}(t,\lambda)}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)-{1\over t\lambda^{2}}{a_{n-3}\over a^{2}_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}P_{n-3}(x;t,\lambda)

+{1\over t\lambda^{2}}\bigg[{a_{n-2}\over a_{n}}-\bigg[(\nu+2n-5)a_{n-2}+b_{n-2}\bigg]\ {1\over a^{2}_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial t}-a_{n-2}{\partial\over\partial t}\left({b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}a_{n}}\right)\bigg]P_{n-2}(x;t,\lambda)

-{A_{n-1}\over t\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\frac{k(k-1)(k+\nu)\omega_{k}}{x-k+2}

[formula]

\times\bigg[P_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)P_{n-2}(k-2;t,\lambda)-P_{n-2}(x;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}(k-2;t,\lambda)\bigg].

3.
Recurrence relations

Let us differentiate (1.12) with respect to
\lambda
under the same justification as in (2.19). Then, employing (1.1), (1.14) and (1.19), we obtain

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg[\lambda{\partial P_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)+A_{n}(t,\lambda)P_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\bigg]\omega_{k}k^{j}=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1.

The latter orthogonality conditions immediately imply the differential-difference equation

[formula]

\lambda{\partial P_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(x;t,\lambda)+A_{n}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)=C_{n}P_{n}(x;t,\lambda).

The constant
C_{n}
can be obtained, comparing leading coefficients in (3.2). Hence it yields

[formula]

\lambda a_{n}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(x;t,\lambda)+a_{n-1}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)=\lambda{\partial a_{n}\over\partial\lambda}\ P_{n}(x;t,\lambda).

On the other hand, differentiating by
\lambda
equality (1.1) when
m=n
, we find

2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial P_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{2}_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial\lambda}=0.

Hence, recalling (1.14), (1.19), we get the identity

[formula]

B_{n}=-2{\lambda\over a_{n}}{\partial a_{n}\over\partial\lambda}.

Moreover, relation (3.4) can be rewritten in the form

[formula]

\lambda{\partial P_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(x;t,\lambda)+A_{n}P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)=-{B_{n}\over 2}P_{n}(x;t,\lambda).

But the differentiation of
B_{n}
by
\lambda
yields, in turn,

{\partial B_{n}\over\partial\lambda}=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}kP_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial P_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg[kP_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\bigg]^{2}\omega_{k}.

Hence via orthogonality and (3.5) we end up after simplification with the identity

[formula]

A^{2}_{n+1}+A^{2}_{n}+\lambda{\partial\over\partial\lambda}\left({b_{n}\over a_{n}}+{b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}}\right)=0.

However, equating coefficients in front of
x^{n-1}
in (3.5), we deduce a more simple relation

[formula]

A^{2}_{n}+\lambda{\partial\over\partial\lambda}\left({b_{n}\over a_{n}}\right)=0.

It can be rewritten in the form

[formula]

\lambda{\partial B_{n}\over\partial\lambda}=A^{2}_{n+1}-A^{2}_{n}.

It is not difficult to verify the equation

[formula]

\lambda{\partial A^{2}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}=A^{2}_{n}\bigg(B_{n}-B_{n-1}\bigg).

Equations (3.8), (3.9) constitute the classical Toda system by
\lambda
for recurrence coefficients (1.15). Meanwhile, the orthogonality relation implies

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial P_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\omega_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda){\partial\omega_{k}\over\partial\lambda}=0,

i.e. via (1.19), (3.4) we get the identity

[formula]

a_{n}b_{n}B_{n}+2a_{n-1}^{2}=0.

Moreover, as a consequence of (3.7), (3.8), (3.9) the latter equality yields

[formula]

B_{n-1}B_{n}B_{n+1}=A^{2}_{n+1}\bigg(B_{n}-B_{n+1}\bigg).

In fact, we have from (3.10)

[formula]

{b_{n}\over a_{n}}B_{n}+2A^{2}_{n}=0.

After differentiation and multiplication by
\lambda
of the latter equality and the use of (3.7), (3.8), (3.9) we find

A^{2}_{n}B_{n}-A^{2}_{n}B_{n-1}+{b_{n}\over a_{n}}A^{2}_{n+1}-A^{2}_{n}{b_{n-1}\over a_{n-1}}=0.

Hence

{b_{n-1}\over a_{n-1}}-{b_{n}\over a_{n}}={1\over 2}\left[{b_{n}\over a_{n}}{A^{2}_{n+1}\over A^{2}_{n}}-{b_{n+1}\over a_{n+1}}\right].

Meanwhile, the use of (3.12) implies

B_{n-1}=A^{2}_{n+1}\bigg[{1\over B_{n+1}}-{1\over B_{n}}\bigg],

which, in turn, yields (3.11).

Now, recalling the orthogonality conditions (1.13), we make summation by parts to deduce the equalities

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Delta\bigg[P_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t)\bigg]{\lambda^{k}\over(k-j)!}=0,\quad j=0,\dots,n-2.

We will need to take into account the dependence of the polynomials
P_{n}
upon
\nu
, i.e. we write
P_{n}\equiv P^{\nu}_{n}
. Then (3.13) is equivalent to the conditions

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k+1;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+2}(t){\lambda^{k}\over(k-j)!}=0,\quad j=0,\dots,n-2.

This means that the sequence
\{P_{n}^{\nu}(x+1;t,\lambda)\}_{n\geq 0}
is quasi-orthogonal with respect to the weight
w_{k}^{\nu+1}
. Hence, expanding
P_{n}^{\nu}(x+1)
in terms of the sequence
\{P_{n}^{\nu+1}\}_{n\geq 0}

P_{n}^{\nu}\left(x+1;\ t,\lambda\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\gamma^{\nu}_{n,m}P_{m}^{\nu+1}\left(x;\ t,\lambda\right),

one proves in a straightforward way, owing to (3.14), that
\gamma^{\nu}_{n,m}=0,\ m=0,\dots,n-2
. Then, accordingly,

[formula]

P_{n}^{\nu}\left(x+1;\ t,\lambda\right)=\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}P_{n}^{\nu+1}\left(x;\ t,\lambda\right)+\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n-1}P_{n-1}^{\nu+1}\left(x;\ t,\lambda\right),

where

[formula]

\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k+1;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}^{\nu+1}\left(k;\ t,\lambda\right)\rho_{k+\nu+2}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!},

[formula]

\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k+1;t,\lambda)P_{n}^{\nu+1}\left(k;\ t,\lambda\right)\rho_{k+\nu+2}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}.

Then, recalling (2.15), we write the structural relation (2.28) for polynomials
P_{n}^{\nu}
in the form

[formula]

\Delta P_{n}^{\nu}\left(x;t,\lambda\right)=\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\nabla P_{n}^{\nu+1}\left(x;\ t,\lambda\right)+\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n-1}\nabla P_{n-1}^{\nu+1}\left(x;\ t,\lambda\right).

Hence from the orthogonality (1.1) it is easily seen the values

[formula]

\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n-1}={a_{n}^{\nu}\over a_{n-1}^{\nu+1}}\bigg[n+{b_{n}^{\nu}\over a_{n}^{\nu}}-{b_{n}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu+1}}\bigg],

[formula]

\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}={a_{n}^{\nu}\over a_{n}^{\nu+1}},

[formula]

\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n-1}={\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\over A_{n}^{\nu+1}}\bigg[n+{b_{n}^{\nu}\over a_{n}^{\nu}}-{b_{n}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu+1}}\bigg],

On the other hand, equality (3.16) reads via (1.1), (1.14), (1.15)

\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n-1}={1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}^{\nu+1}\left(k-1;\ t,\lambda\right)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over(k-1)!}

={A_{n}^{\nu}\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)P_{n-1}^{\nu+1}\left(k-1;\ t,\lambda\right)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}

[formula]

={1\over\lambda}{a_{n-1}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu}}={1\over\lambda}{A_{n}^{\nu}\over\gamma^{\nu}_{n-1,n-1}}={1\over\lambda}{A_{n}^{\nu+1}\over\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}}.

Thus, combining with (3.19), we derive the following recurrence relations

[formula]

\bigg[{a_{n-1}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu}}\bigg]^{2}=\bigg[{A_{n}^{\nu+1}\over\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}}\bigg]^{2}=\lambda\bigg[n+{b_{n}^{\nu}\over a_{n}^{\nu}}-{b_{n}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu+1}}\bigg].

Analogously, we treat equality (3.17). It gives,

\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}={1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)kP_{n}^{\nu+1}\left(k-1;\ t,\lambda\right)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}

={1\over\lambda}{a_{n}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu}}\bigg[{b_{n}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu+1}}-{b_{n+1}^{\nu}\over a_{n+1}^{\nu}}-n\bigg].

Hence, recalling (3.20), we deduce the identity

[formula]

\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}=\bigg[{a_{n}^{\nu}\over a_{n}^{\nu+1}}\bigg]^{2}={1\over\lambda}\bigg[{b_{n}^{\nu+1}\over a_{n}^{\nu+1}}-{b_{n+1}^{\nu}\over a_{n+1}^{\nu}}-n\bigg].

On the other hand, combining with (3.23), (3.24), we find

[formula]

\lambda\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}+{1\over\lambda}\bigg[{A_{n}^{\nu+1}\over\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}}\bigg]^{2}=B_{n}^{\nu}.

Moreover, writing (3.23) for
n+1
, we observe one more recurrence relation via (3.24)

[formula]

\lambda\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}+{1\over\lambda}\bigg[{A_{n+1}^{\nu+1}\over\gamma^{\nu}_{n+1,n+1}}\bigg]^{2}=1+B_{n}^{\nu+1}.

But from (3.22) we have also

[formula]

\lambda\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}+{1\over\lambda}\bigg[{A_{n+1}^{\nu}\over\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}}\bigg]^{2}=1+B_{n}^{\nu+1}.

Consequently, (3.22), (3.25), (3.26), (3.27) yield the relations

[formula]

\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n-1}\right]^{2}+\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}={B_{n}^{\nu}\over\lambda},

[formula]

\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n+1,n}\right]^{2}+\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}={1\over\lambda}\left(1+B_{n}^{\nu+1}\right),

[formula]

\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}={1\over\lambda}\ \frac{\left[A_{n}^{\nu+1}\right]^{2}-\left[A_{n+1}^{\nu}\right]^{2}}{B_{n}^{\nu}-B_{n}^{\nu+1}-1},

[formula]

\left[\gamma^{\nu}_{n,n}\right]^{2}={1\over\lambda}\ \frac{\left[A_{n+1}^{\nu}\right]^{2}B_{n}^{\nu}-\left[A_{n}^{\nu+1}\right]^{2}\left[B_{n}^{\nu+1}+1\right]}{\left[A_{n+1}^{\nu}\right]^{2}-\left[A_{n}^{\nu+1}\right]^{2}},

\bigg[\left[A_{n}^{\nu+1}\right]^{2}-\left[A_{n+1}^{\nu}\right]^{2}\bigg]^{2}+\bigg[\left[A_{n+1}^{\nu}\right]^{2}B_{n}^{\nu}-\left[A_{n}^{\nu+1}\right]^{2}\left[B_{n}^{\nu+1}+1\right]\bigg]

[formula]

\times\bigg[B_{n}^{\nu}-B_{n}^{\nu+1}-1\bigg]=0.

Finally in this section we analyse the following series with the backward difference (2.15)

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\nabla\bigg[P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}\bigg](-k)_{j}.

Invoking summation by parts, we represent (3.33) in the form

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\nabla\bigg[P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}\bigg](-k)_{j}=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}\Delta\bigg[(-k)_{j}\bigg].

Working out the right-hand side of the latter equality by virtue of (1.13), we observe that it is equal to
0
for
j=0,1,\dots,n-1.
Therefore

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\nabla\bigg[P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}\bigg](-k)_{j}=0,\quad j=0,1,\dots,n-1.

When
j=n
we write via (1.13)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}\Delta\bigg[(-k)_{n}\bigg]=(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t)\lambda^{k}\bigg[{k+1\over(k+1-n)!}-{1\over(k-n)!}\bigg]

=(-1)^{n}n\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over(k+1-n)!}=0.

Consequently, we established the following orthogonality conditions

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\nabla\bigg[P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}\bigg](-k)_{j}=0,\quad j=0,1,\dots,n,

i.e. due to (1.10)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg[\bigg(\lambda(k+\nu)P^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)-kP^{\nu}_{n}(k-1;t,\lambda)\bigg)\rho_{k+\nu}(t)\bigg.

[formula]

\bigg.+\lambda tP^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu-1}(t)\bigg]{\lambda^{k}\over(k-j)!}=0,\quad j=0,1,\dots,n.

4.
The modified sequence of polynomials for the weight
t^{-k}\omega_{k}(\lambda t)

This last section is concerned with the sequence of polynomials
\{Q^{\nu}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, being orthogonal with respect to the weight
\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t)\lambda^{k}/k!
, i.e.

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)Q^{\nu}_{m}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}=\delta_{n,m}.

The orthogonality conditions can be written accordingly,

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1,

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}(-k)_{j}\over k!}=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1,

and explicitly

[formula]

Q_{n}^{\nu}(x;t,\lambda)=\alpha^{\nu}_{n}x^{n}+\beta^{\nu}_{n}x^{n-1}+\hbox{lower degrees}.

Denoting coefficients of the three term recurrence relation by
q^{\nu}_{n},h^{\nu}_{n}
, it has

[formula]

xQ_{n}^{\nu}(x;t,\lambda)=q^{\nu}_{n+1}Q_{n+1}^{\nu}(x;t,\lambda)+h^{\nu}_{n}Q_{n}^{\nu}(x;t,\lambda)+q^{\nu}_{n}Q_{n-1}^{\nu}(x;t,\lambda),

where

q^{\nu}_{n}={\alpha^{\nu}_{n-1}\over\alpha^{\nu}_{n}},\quad\quad h^{\nu}_{n}={\beta^{\nu}_{n}\over\alpha^{\nu}_{n}}-{\beta^{\nu}_{n+1}\over\alpha^{\nu}_{n+1}}.

Then we differentiate (4.2) by
t
which is allowed under the same conditions as in Section 2 to derive via (1.9) the equalities

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}-\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1.

Meanwhile, the differentiation by
\lambda
implies

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}-t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}

[formula]

+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)k\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}=0,\quad j=0,\dots,n-1.

The latter term in (4.7) can be rewritten by virtue of the orthogonality and the three term recurrence relation, and we obtain via (4.5)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)k\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}=q_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!},\quad j=0,\dots,n-1.

Therefore, recalling (4.6), (4.7), we establish the following orthogonality conditions

[formula]

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg[t{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)-\lambda{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)-q_{n}Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\bigg]\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}k^{j}\over k!}=0,\ j=0,\dots,n-1.

Hence, owing to the uniqueness, we establish the partial differential-difference equation for orthogonal polynomials
Q_{n}^{\nu}

[formula]

t{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}(x;t,\lambda)-\lambda{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(x;t,\lambda)-q_{n}Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)={1\over\alpha_{n}^{\nu}}\left[t{\partial\alpha^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}-\lambda{\partial\alpha^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}\right]Q^{\nu}_{n}(x;t,\lambda).

But since similar to (3.4) one finds

2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}-\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\right]^{2}\rho_{k+\nu}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}=0,

2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda){\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}-t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\right]^{2}\rho_{k+\nu}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}

+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)\right]^{2}k\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}=0.

Consequently, we get from (4.5) and the latter equalities

[formula]

{1\over\alpha_{n}^{\nu}}\left[t{\partial\alpha^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}-\lambda{\partial\alpha^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}\right]={h^{\nu}_{n}\over 2}.

So, equation (4.9) has the form

[formula]

t{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}(x;t,\lambda)-\lambda{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(x;t,\lambda)-q^{\nu}_{n}Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)={h^{\nu}_{n}\over 2}Q^{\nu}_{n}(x;t,\lambda).

Then, equating coefficients in front of
x^{n-1}
in (4.11) and using (4.10), we derive the Toda-type first order partial differential equation for recurrence coefficients in (4.5)

[formula]

t{\partial h^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}-\lambda{\partial h^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}+[q^{\nu}_{n+1}]^{2}-[q^{\nu}_{n}]^{2}=0.

To complete the Toda-type system we add an analog of the equation (3.9) which can be verified directly

[formula]

t{\partial[q^{\nu}_{n}]^{2}\over\partial t}-\lambda{\partial[q^{\nu}_{n}]^{2}\over\partial\lambda}=[q^{\nu}_{n}]^{2}\bigg[h_{n-1}^{\nu}-h_{n}^{\nu}\bigg].

Finally, from the differentiation and orthogonality we get

0=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}(k;t,\lambda)Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}-\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!},

0=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\partial Q^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}(k;t,\lambda)Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}-t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)\rho_{k+\nu}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}

+{1\over\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}Q^{\nu}_{n}(k;t,\lambda)Q^{\nu}_{n-1}(k;t,\lambda)k\rho_{k+\nu+1}(\lambda t){\lambda^{k}\over k!}.

This implies immediately the recurrence relation

{\beta_{n}^{\nu}\over\alpha_{n-1}^{\nu}\alpha_{n}^{\nu}}\bigg[\lambda{\partial\alpha^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial\lambda}-t{\partial\alpha^{\nu}_{n}\over\partial t}\bigg]-q_{n}^{\nu}=0,

i.e., owing to (4.10), we end up with the equality (cf. (3.12))

[formula]

2[q_{n}^{\nu}]^{2}+{h^{\nu}_{n}\beta^{\nu}_{n}\over\alpha_{n}^{\nu}}=0.

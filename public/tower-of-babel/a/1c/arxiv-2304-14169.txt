Tractability of sampling recovery on unweighted function classes
David Krieg

Abstract
. It is well-known that the problem of sampling recovery in the L 2 L_{2} -norm on unweighted Korobov spaces (Sobolev spaces with mixed smoothness) as well as classical smoothness classes such as Hölder classes suffers from the curse of dimensionality. We show that the problem is tractable for those classes if they are intersected with the Wiener algebra of functions with summable Fourier coefficients. In fact, this is a relatively simple implication of powerful results from the theory of compressed sensing. Tractability is achieved by the use of non-linear algorithms, while linear algorithms cannot do the job.



We consider the problem of recovering
a high-dimensional function

f\colon[0,1]^{d}\to\mathbb{C}

from a class
F_{d}
using algorithms of the form

(1)

A_{n}\colon F_{d}\to L_{2}([0,1]^{d}),\quad A_{n}(f)=\phi(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n}))

with sampling points
x_{j}\in[0,1]^{d}
and a recovery map
\phi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to L_{2}
.
The error is measured in the
L_{2}
-norm and in a worst-case setting, i.e.,

{\rm err}(A_{n},F_{d},L_{2})\,:=\,\sup_{f\in F_{d}}\|f-A_{n}(f)\|_{2}.

It is known that this approximation problem suffers from the
curse of dimensionality

for most classical function classes
F_{d}
,
including the smoothness classes of
k
-times
continuously differentiable functions
[
16
,
17
]

or Sobolev spaces of mixed smoothness
[
15
]
.
That is, there exist
C,\gamma,\varepsilon>0

such that the (information) complexity

(2)

n(\varepsilon,F_{d},L_{2})\,:=\,\min\big\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ \exists A_{n}\colon{\rm err}(A_{n},F_{d},L_{2})\leq\varepsilon\big\}

is bounded below by
C(1+\gamma)^{d}
for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

A classical approach
to make this problem tractable in high dimensions is
to consider weighted function classes
F_{d}
,
assuming a decreasing importance of the input variables.
This approach goes back to
[
27
]

and has gained significant popularity,
see also Remark
14
about weighted Korobov spaces.
Unfortunately,
it is much harder to provide reasonable unweighted function classes
where the approximation problem is tractable.
By
unweighted
, we mean that all variables are of equal importance.
Formally, one may call a function class
F_{d}
unweighted
if
f\circ\pi\in F_{d}
for any
f\in F_{d}
and any permutation matrix
\pi
.

It was recently discovered by Goda
[
13
]
that
numerical integration

is polynomially tractable on the unweighted class

(3)

F_{d}^{\log}\,:=\,\bigg\{f\in C([0,1]^{d})\ \Big|\ \sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{f}(k)|\max(1,\log\|k\|_{\infty})\leq 1\bigg\}.

Namely, the number of sampling points that are needed to approximate the integral of any function from
F_{d}^{\log}
up to an error
\varepsilon
is bounded above by a polynomial in
\varepsilon^{-1}
and
d
.
Goda refines a method of Dick who already showed in
[
6
]
that integration
is tractable on the class of
\alpha
-Hölder
continuous functions with absolutely convergent Fourier series.
The class
F_{d}^{\log}
avoids the Hölder condition
by strengthening the condition of absolute convergence in a very slight manner.

Those results raise the question whether the problem of
L_{2}
-approximation on
F_{d}^{\log}
,
whose complexity can only be higher than the complexity of the integration problem,
is polynomially tractable as well.
We give a positive answer.

Theorem 1
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, we have

n(\varepsilon,F_{d}^{\log},L_{2})\,\leq\,c\,d\,\varepsilon^{-3}\log^{3}(\varepsilon^{-1}).

More generally, the problem of sampling recovery on
F_{d}^{\log}
in
L_{p}
is tractable for all
1\leq p<\infty
, see Corollary
13
.
In fact, we will show that

L_{p}
-approximation
is polynomially tractable for
many classical unweighted classes of functions,
like Sobolev spaces of mixed smoothness
or Hölder continuous functions,
if it is additionally assumed that the Fourier series converges absolutely.
See Theorem
11
for a general result
and Corollaries
15
and
17

for the corresponding examples.
Those findings are consequences of powerful results
from the theory of compressive sensing,
as will be discussed in Section
1
.

Interestingly, the curse of dimensionality is only mitigated due to the use of
non-linear
sampling algorithms. If one only allows linear algorithms, using linear recovery maps
\phi
in (
1
),
the
L_{2}
-approximation problem on
F_{d}^{\log}
suffers from the curse of dimensionality,
see Lemma
18
.
This fact also implies that the sampling recovery on
F_{d}^{\log}
in
L_{\infty}
is intractable,
see Corollary
19
.

Remark 2
.

For numerical integration on the class
F_{d}^{\log}
,
Goda
[
13
]
obtained an upper bound of order
d^{3}\varepsilon^{-3}
.
Since the upper bound from Theorem
1
also holds for numerical integration,
we improve upon this bound if the dimension
d
is larger than
\log^{3/2}(\varepsilon^{-1})
.
While the present paper was under review,
Goda’s upper bound on the complexity of integration
has been further improved to
d\varepsilon^{-3}
, see
[
3
]
.

Remark 3
.

The function class
F_{d}^{\log}
defined in (
3
)
is from the first version of the paper
[
13
]
that appeared on arXiv in October 2022.
In a later version, Goda replaced the term
\|k\|_{\infty}

by
\min_{i\in{\rm supp}(k)}|k_{i}|

and thus obtained tractability on an even larger class.
I believe that this replacement is only possible for the integration problem
and it will lead to intractability in the case of the approximation problem.

Remark 4
.

Other unweighted classes of functions
where the approximation problem is tractable
are high-dimensional functions
that show some “low-dimensional structure” like
tensor products of univariate functions
[
1
,
21
,
24
]
,
sums of low-dimensional functions
[
30
]
,
or compositions of univariate functions
with linear functionals
[
23
]
.
Moreover, it was proven in
[
31
]

that the approximation problem is weakly tractable
for the class of analytic functions defined on the cube with
directional derivatives of all orders bounded by 1.
For the integration problem,
we mention the star-discrepancy
as a further example
[
14
]
.

Remark 5
.

Recently, the papers
[
5
,
19
]
also derived new bounds
on the complexity of sampling recovery in
L_{2}
using non-linear algorithms. It is shown in
[
19
, Thm. 3.2]

that the
n
-th minimal error
is essentially bounded by the
s
-term
widths of the class
F_{d}
in
L_{\infty}

where it suffices to take
n
quasi-linear in both
s
and
d
.
The paper
[
19
]
provides several examples of classical smoothness classes
F_{d}

where non-linear sampling algorithms
have a better rate of convergence than any linear algorithm. Here, we use instead a bound by

s^{-1/2}
times the
s
-term widths
of the class
F_{d}
in the Wiener algebra,
see Lemma
9
and Remark
12
.
The extra factor
s^{-1/2}
eases the tractability analysis significantly.
It would be interesting to know whether
[
19
, Thm. 3.2]

also leads to tractability results for the class
F_{d}^{\log}
or related ones.

Remark 6
.

It is remarkable that the complexity bounds in this paper
are not obtained with a sophisticated choice of interpolation nodes
but rather with i.i.d. uniformly distributed sampling points, see Lemma
9
.
Recently, there has been much interest in the surprising
power of i.i.d. random information in comparison to optimal information.
I refer to the survey paper
[
28
]
.

1.
A general complexity bound

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on a set
D

and let
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{k}\mid k\in I\}

be a countable orthonormal system in
L_{2}(D,\mu)

such that

(4)

C_{\mathcal{B}}\,:=\,\sup_{k\in I}\|b_{k}\|_{\infty}\,<\,\infty.

We say that
\mathcal{B}
is a bounded orthonormal system.
Whenever
(c_{k})_{k\in I}
is an absolutely summable sequence of complex numbers,
the series
f=\sum_{k\in I}c_{k}b_{k}
converges uniformly on
D
and we have

\hat{f}(k)\,:=\,\int_{D}f(x)\overline{b_{k}(x)}\,{\rm d}\mu(x)\,=\,c_{k},\quad k\in I.

We consider the Wiener algebra

(5)

\mathcal{A}\,:=\,\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{B})\,:=\,\left\{f=\sum_{k\in I}c_{k}b_{k}\,\,\Big|\,\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}:=\sum_{k\in I}|c_{k}|<\infty\right\}

and denote its unit ball by
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathcal{B})
.
For a finite index set
\Lambda\subset I
and
f\in L_{2}
, we write

P_{\Lambda}(f)\,:=\,\sum_{k\in\Lambda}\hat{f}(k)b_{k}

and denote by
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
the span of the functions
b_{k}
with
k\in\Lambda
.
Given a class
F\subset\mathcal{A}
,
we define the projection error

E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)\,:=\,\sup_{f\in F}\|f-P_{\Lambda}f\|_{\infty}.

We consider the following algorithm.

Algorithm 7
.

Given a class
F\subset\mathcal{A}
,
a finite index set
\Lambda\subset I
,
and a finite and non-empty point set
X\subset D
,
we consider the algorithm

A_{F,\Lambda,X}

which maps a function
f\in F
to any solution of

(6)

\displaystyle\min\|g\|_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\quad

\displaystyle g\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle\text{and \ }

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#X}\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)-g(x)|^{2}\,\leq\,E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)^{2}.

The algorithm is well-defined
since the function
g=P_{\Lambda}(f)

satisfies the constraints of the minimization problem.
Moreover, the output of the algorithm
merely depends on
f
via the data
(f(x))_{x\in X}

and thus
A_{F,\Lambda,X}
is a sampling algorithm
of the form (
1
).

Remark 8
.

Algorithm
7
is a direct translation
of a classical algorithm from compressed sensing,
called basis pursuit denoising. Namely,
if we denote
x=(\hat{g}(k))_{k\in\Lambda}
,

y=(f(x))_{x\in X}
and
G=(b_{k}(x))_{k\in\Lambda,x\in X}
,
solving (
6
) is equivalent to
the
\ell^{1}
-minimization problem

(7)

\min\|x\|_{1}\quad\text{subject to}\quad\|Gx-y\|_{2}\leq E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)\sqrt{\#X}.

The insight that
\ell^{1}
-minimization can be of great advantage over linear algorithms
goes back at least to
[
2
,
8
,
11
]

and the problem of
\ell^{2}
-recovery on
\ell^{1}
-balls in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Different methods of computing a solution to (
7
) can be found in
[
10
, Ch. 15]
.

We now give an error bound for Algorithm
7
.
The error is expressed in terms of
the best
s
-term widths of
F
in
\mathcal{A}
, defined by

\sigma_{s}(F,\mathcal{A})\,:=\,\sup_{f\in F}\,\inf_{\#\Lambda\leq s\vphantom{|^{|}}}\,\sum_{k\in I\setminus\Lambda}|\hat{f}(k)|.

We derive our error bound as
a simple implication of
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]
,
where we reinterpret our data on
f

as noisy data on
P_{\Lambda}f
.
The key ingredient to error bounds such as
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]

is the restricted isometry property of the matrix
G=(b_{k}(x))_{k\in\Lambda,x\in X}
;
For
m
random points, the matrix
m^{-1/2}G

acts as a quasi-isometry on the set of vectors with at most
s
non-zero entries,
where it suffices that
m
is quasi-linear in
s
and logarithmic in
\#\Lambda
,
see
[
10
, Thm. 12.32]
or
[
26
, Thm. 5.2]
.
This property implies that vectors
c
from the unit ball in
\ell^{1}(\Lambda)

can be recovered well from data of the form
y=Gc+e

if the noise
e
is sufficiently small,
see
[
10
, Thm. 6.12]
.
In our case, we want to recover the vector
c=(\hat{f}(k))_{k\in\Lambda}
.
The data is given by
y=(f(x))_{x\in X}

while
Gc=(P_{\Lambda}f(x))_{x\in X}
and so
the entries of
e
are bounded by
E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)
.

Lemma 9

(Compare
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]
)
.

There is a universal constant
c\geq 1
such that the following holds.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{k}\mid k\in I\}
be a bounded orthonormal system
with respect to a probability measure
\mu
and let

F\subset\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{B})
.
For
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
s\geq 2C_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}
,
and
\Lambda\subset I
,
let
X_{m}
be a set of

m\,\geq\,c\,\log(\gamma^{-1})\,s\log^{3}(s)\log(\#\Lambda)

independent random points with distribution
\mu
.
Then, with probability at least
1-\gamma
, we have for all
2\leq p\leq\infty

and
f\in F
that

\|f-A_{F,\Lambda,X_{m}}(f)\|_{p}\,\leq\,c\,C_{\mathcal{B}}\,s^{-1/p}\sigma_{s}(F,\mathcal{A})+c\,s^{1/2-1/p}E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F).

Proof.

We use
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]

with
b_{j}
instead of
\phi_{j}
and
\omega_{j}=C_{\mathcal{B}}
.
We choose
c_{0}
as the maximum of all constants appearing in
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]

and
c=c_{0}+1
.
Then
X_{m}=\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\}

satisfies the conclusion of
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]
with probability
1-\gamma
.
Given
g\in F
, we apply this conclusion to the function
f=P_{\Lambda}g

and samples
y_{\ell}=f(t_{\ell})+\xi_{\ell}
with

\xi_{\ell}=g(t_{\ell})-f(t_{\ell})

and hence
\|\xi\|_{2}\leq E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)\sqrt{m}=:\eta
.
Then the function
f^{\#}
defined in
[
26
, Thm. 6.1]
equals the function

A_{F,\Lambda,X_{m}}(g)
.
By the triangle inequality and Jensen’s inequality, we have

\|g-A_{F,\Lambda,X_{m}}(g)\|_{p}\leq E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)+\|f-f^{\#}\|_{p}.

As
\sigma_{s}(f)_{\omega,1}
as defined in
[
26
]

is bounded by
C_{\mathcal{B}}\sigma_{s}(F,\mathcal{A})
,
we get

\displaystyle\|f-f^{\#}\|_{2}\,\leq\,c_{0}C_{\mathcal{B}}\,s^{-1/2}\sigma_{s}(F,\mathcal{A})+c_{0}E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)\,=:\,R,

\displaystyle\|f-f^{\#}\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,c_{0}C_{\mathcal{B}}\,\sigma_{s}(F,\mathcal{A})+c_{0}s^{1/2}E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)\,=\,Rs^{1/2}.

It only remains to apply
\|h\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|h\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\|h\|_{\infty}^{p-2}

for
h=f-f^{\#}
.
∎

This leads to the following complexity bound.
Recall the definition of the (information) complexity
of sampling recovery in
L_{2}

from (
2
)
which is defined analogously for sampling recovery in
L_{p}
.
We also define the minimal size of an index set needed for a projection error
\varepsilon>0
, i.e.,

(8)

N_{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}(\varepsilon,F)\,:=\,\min\{\#\Lambda\mid E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)\leq\varepsilon\}.

Proposition 10
.

There is a universal constant
C\geq 2
such that,
for any bounded orthonormal system
\mathcal{B}
, any
F\subset\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathcal{B})
,
all
1\leq p<\infty
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we have

n(\varepsilon,F,L_{p})\,\leq\,C\,\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-r}\log^{3}(\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-r})\,\log N_{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}\big(\tilde{\varepsilon}^{r/2},F\big)

with
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon/(CC_{\mathcal{B}})
and
r=\max\{p,2\}
.

Proof.

The case
p<2
follows from the case
p=2
,
so let
p\geq 2
.
Put
C=2c
with
c
from Lemma
9
.
We fix
\gamma={\rm e}^{-1}
and choose
\Lambda\subset I
with
\#\Lambda\leq N_{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}(\tilde{\varepsilon}^{p/2},F)

and
E_{\Lambda}^{\infty}(F)\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}^{p/2}
.
Moreover, we put
s=\lceil\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-p}\rceil
so that
s\geq 2C_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}
.
Observing that

\sigma_{s}(F,\mathcal{A})\,\leq\,1,

Lemma
9
yields the statement.
∎

Proposition
10
leads to our main result on the tractability of
L_{p}
-approximation.
Here, given classes
F_{d}\subset\mathcal{A}_{d}
for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
we say that sampling recovery on
F_{d}
in
L_{p}
is polynomially tractable if

\exists C,q,r\geq 0\colon\forall d\in\mathbb{N}\ \forall\varepsilon>0\colon\quad n(\varepsilon,F_{d},L_{p})\,\leq\,Cd^{q}\varepsilon^{-r}.

Recall the definition of
C_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathcal{B})
and
N_{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}(\varepsilon,F)

from (
4
),
(
5
)
and (
8
).

Theorem 11
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\mathcal{B}_{d}
be a bounded orthonormal system
and
F_{d}\subset\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathcal{B}_{d})
.
Assume that there are positive constants
c_{1},c_{2},\alpha,\beta,
and
\gamma

such that

C_{\mathcal{B}_{d}}\,\leq\,c_{1}d^{\alpha}\qquad\text{and}\qquad N_{\mathcal{B}}^{\infty}(\varepsilon,F_{d})\,\leq\,\exp(c_{2}d^{\beta}\varepsilon^{-\gamma})

for all
\varepsilon>0
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the problem of sampling recovery on
F_{d}
in
L_{p}

is polynomially tractable for all
1\leq p<\infty
.

That is, sampling recovery in
L_{p}
on classes with absolutely convergent
(generalized) Fourier series is tractable
even if the number of Fourier coefficients
needed for an
\varepsilon
-approximation of functions
f\in F_{d}
in the uniform norm
grows super-exponentially in
d
and
\varepsilon^{-1}
,
as long as the growth is not double-exponential.

Remark 12

(Rate of convergence)
.

For our tractability analysis, it was enough to observe that
\sigma_{n}(F,\mathcal{A})\leq 1

for all
F\subset\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
In fact, it would not help for the classes considered in this paper
to take the decay of the widths
\sigma_{n}(F,\mathcal{A})
into account
as
n
would have to be exponentially large in
d
if we want widths significantly smaller than one.
This is different
if one is interested in studying the rate of convergence of the
n
th minimal error

{\rm err}(n,F_{d},L_{p})\,:=\,\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in D\\
\phi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to L_{p}\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{f\in F}\,\|f-A_{n}(f)\|_{p}.

Often (e.g., for Sobolev classes of mixed smoothness) one
can choose the size of
\Lambda
polynomial in
n

in order to obtain from Lemma
9
that

{\rm err}(Cn\log^{4}n,F,L_{p})\,\lesssim\,n^{-1/p}\,\sigma_{n}(F,\mathcal{A}).

In comparison, the paper
[
19
]
recently revealed estimates of the form

{\rm err}(Cn\log^{4}n,F,L_{2})\,\lesssim\,\sigma_{n}(F,\mathcal{B})_{L_{\infty}}

by means of the best
n
-term widths in
L_{\infty}
instead of
\mathcal{A}
,
where the power of the logarithm can be reduced to 3 for the trigonometric system.

2.
Results for specific classes

We now consider the Fourier system

\mathcal{B}_{d}\,:=\,\{b_{k}=e^{2\pi i\langle k,\cdot\rangle}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}

that is orthonormal in
L_{2}([0,1]^{d})
with the Lebesgue measure
and satisfies
C_{\mathcal{B}_{d}}=1
.
We obtain the classical Wiener algebra
\mathcal{A}_{d}:=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{B}_{d})

of periodic functions with absolutely convergent Fourier series.
We consider three examples,
starting with the class
F_{d}^{\log}
from the introduction. The following corollary also proves Theorem
1
.

Corollary 13
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
.
There is a constant
c>0
such that for all

d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, we have

n(\varepsilon,F_{d}^{\log},L_{p})\,\leq\,c\,d\,\varepsilon^{-3p/2}\log^{3}(\varepsilon^{-p}).

Proof.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in F_{d}^{\log}
, we have

\|f-P_{[-m,m]^{d}}f\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,\log^{-1}(m+1)

and thus

N_{\mathcal{B}_{d}}^{\infty}(\varepsilon,F_{d}^{\log})\,\leq\,\exp(2d\varepsilon^{-1}).

Now the bound is obtained from Proposition
10
.
∎

As a second example,
we consider Sobolev spaces of mixed smoothness
s>1/2

(also called unweighted Korobov spaces),
namely

H_{\rm mix}^{s,d}\,:=\,\bigg\{f\in C([0,1]^{d})\ \Big|\ \sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,|k_{i}|^{2s}\}\leq 1\bigg\}.

It is well known that
L_{2}
-approximation on
H_{\rm mix}^{s,d}
suffers from the curse of dimensionality,
see, e.g.,
[
15
]
.
The curse is relinquished in the presence of absolutely summable Fourier coefficients,
namely, for the class

F^{s,d}_{\rm mix}\,:=\,\bigg\{f\in H_{\rm mix}^{s,d}\ \Big|\ \sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{f}(k)|\leq 1\bigg\}.

Remark 14

(Weighted Korobov spaces)
.

A classical approach to relinquish
the curse of dimensionality on
H_{\rm mix}^{s,d}

is by introducing (product) weights
1\geq\gamma_{1}\geq\gamma_{2}\geq\ldots>0

and
considering the class

H_{\rm mix}^{s,d,\gamma}\,:=\,\bigg\{f\in C([0,1]^{d})\mid\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,\gamma_{i}^{-1}|k_{i}|^{2s}\}\leq 1\bigg\}.

see, e.g.,
[
9
,
22
,
25
]
and
[
7
, Ch. 13]
and the references therein.
The sequence
\gamma
models a decreasing importance of the variables.
It is known that
L_{2}
-approximation on
H_{\rm mix}^{s,d,\gamma}

is polynomially tractable if the weights decay fast enough, namely, if
there is a constant
K>0
with

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\gamma_{i}\,\leq\,K\log(d),\quad d\geq 2,

see
[
25
, Thm. 1]
.
The present approach is more general:
For any
\gamma
as above,
a simple computation shows the inclusion

(9)

H_{\rm mix}^{s,d,\gamma}\subset C\,d^{q}\,F^{s,d}_{\rm mix}

with constants
C,q>0
that only depend on
K
and
s
.

Corollary 15
.

For any
s>1/2
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
the problem of
L_{p}
-approximation
is polynomially tractable on
F^{s,d}_{\rm mix}
.

More precisely, for
p\geq 2
,
there is a constant
C>0
,
depending only on
s
and
p
,
such that for all
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, we have

n(\varepsilon,F^{s,d}_{\rm mix},L_{p})\,\leq\,C\,d^{2}\log(d)\,\varepsilon^{-p}\log^{4}(\varepsilon^{-1}).

Proof.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in F^{s,d}_{\rm mix}
, we get from Hölder’s inequality that

\|f-P_{[-m,m]^{d}}f\|_{\infty}^{2}\,\leq\,\bigg(\sum_{\|k\|_{\infty}>m}|\hat{f}(k)|\bigg)^{2}\,\leq\,\sum_{\|k\|_{\infty}>m}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,|k_{i}|\}^{-2s}\\
\leq\,d\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|k_{1}|>m\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\{1,|k_{i}|\}^{-2s}\,\leq\,d\cdot c_{s}^{d-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
|k_{1}|>m\end{subarray}}|k_{1}|^{-2s}\,\leq\,d\cdot c_{s}^{d}\cdot m^{-2s+1}

with
c_{s}>0
depending only on
s
,
and thus

N_{\mathcal{B}_{d}}^{\infty}(\varepsilon,F^{s,d}_{\rm mix})\,\leq\,\big(d\,c_{s}^{d}\,\varepsilon^{-2}\big)^{d/(2s-1)}.

Now the bound is obtained from Proposition
10
.
∎

Remark 16
.

In regard of (
9
), we note that

(10)

n(\varepsilon,rF_{d},L_{p})\,\leq\,n(\varepsilon/(2r),F_{d},L_{p})

holds for any convex and symmetric class
F_{d}

and any
r\geq 1
,
so that tractability on
F^{s,d}_{\rm mix}
indeed
implies tractability on the classes
H_{\rm mix}^{s,d,\gamma}
.
Equation (
10
) follows from the optimality of homogeneous algorithms
for linear problems, see
[
20
, Thm. 1]
.

As a third example,
we consider the class of Hölder functions
that has been studied by Dick
[
6
]
for numerical integration, namely,

F_{d}^{\alpha}\,:=\,\bigg\{f\in C([0,1]^{d})\ \Big|\ \sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\hat{f}(k)|\leq 1\,\land\,\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|_{2}^{\alpha}}\leq 1\bigg\}

with some
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Here, the supremum is taken over all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
considering
f
as a 1-periodic function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Corollary 17
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
the problem of
L_{p}
-approximation
is polynomially tractable on
F^{s,d}_{\rm mix}
.

More precisely, for
p\geq 2
,
there is a constant
c>0
, depending only on
\alpha
,
such that for all
d\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, we have

n(\varepsilon,F_{d}^{\alpha},L_{p})\,\leq\,c\,d^{2}\log^{2}(d)\,\varepsilon^{-p}\log^{4}(\varepsilon^{-1}).

Proof.

Let
S_{m}=P_{[-m,m]}
be the univariate Fourier sum operator
and
C^{\alpha}_{\rm per}
be the class of univariate
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions with Hölder constant one.
It is well known that

\|S_{m}f\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,c_{1}\log(m)\|f\|_{\infty}

for all
m\geq 2
and all
f\in L_{\infty}([0,1])
and

\|f-S_{m}f\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,c_{2}\log(m)m^{-\alpha}

for all
m\geq 2
and all
f\in C^{\alpha}_{\rm per}
,
where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depend on nothing but
\alpha
,
see
[
18
]
.
Let now
f\in F_{d}^{\alpha}
.
For fixed
x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}\in[0,1]
,
the function
f
as a function of
x_{i}
is in
C^{\alpha}_{\rm per}
.
By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|f-P_{[-m,m]^{d}}f\|_{\infty}\,\leq\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|S_{m}^{x_{1}}\cdots S_{m}^{x_{i-1}}(f-S_{m}^{x_{i}}f)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\,\sum_{i=1}^{d}(c_{1}\log(m))^{i-1}\cdot c_{2}\log(m)m^{-\alpha}\,\leq\,\frac{(c_{3}\log(m))^{d}}{m^{\alpha}}.

This term is smaller than
\varepsilon
for
m\geq\exp(c_{4}d\log^{2}(d)\log(\varepsilon^{-1}))
,
thus

\log N_{\mathcal{B}_{d}}^{\infty}(\varepsilon,F_{d}^{\alpha})\,\leq\,c_{5}d^{2}\log^{2}(d)\log(\varepsilon^{-1}).

Now the bound is obtained from Proposition
10
.
∎

It is interesting to note that the polynomial order
d^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}

that we obtained for
L_{2}
-approximation
is the same as the one obtained in
[
6
]
for numerical integration.
It is surely an interesting open problem to
improve the complexity bounds in Corollaries
13
,

15
and
17

and/or provide matching lower bounds.

3.
Intractability with linear algorithms

We only have tractability for the class
F_{d}^{\log}
thanks to
non-linear
algorithms.
If we restrict to linear algorithms, we still have the curse of dimensionality. This is implied by a result of
[
12
]
, see also
[
29
, Lem. 3.2]
.
Namely, it is known that for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
m>n
,
and any linear mapping
T_{n}\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
of rank
n
,
there is some
x\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
\|x\|_{1}\leq 1
and

(11)

\|x-T_{n}x\|_{2}\,\geq\,\left(\frac{m-n}{m}\right)^{1/2}.

Lemma 18
.

The problem of
L_{2}
-approximation on the classes
F_{d}^{\log}
with linear algorithms suffers from the curse of dimensionality. For any linear mapping
A_{n}\colon F_{d}^{\log}\to L_{2}
with rank
n\leq 5^{d}/2
we have

{\rm err}(A_{n},F_{d}^{\log},L_{2})\,\geq\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

Note that the curse of dimensionality even holds for the class of all linear algorithms,
using arbitrary linear information, instead of only sampling based algorithms.

Proof.

Let
A_{n}\colon F_{d}^{\log}\to L_{2}
be a linear operator of rank
n\leq 5^{d}/2

and let
\Lambda=\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}^{d}
so that
\#\Lambda=5^{d}
.
Consider the linear mapping
T\colon\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda}\to\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)

that maps a vector of coefficients to the corresponding trigonometric polynomial.
Then
T_{n}=T^{-1}P_{\Lambda}A_{n}T\colon\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda}\to\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda}

is a linear mapping with rank at most
n
and (
11
)
yields that there is some
x\in\mathbb{C}^{\Lambda}
with
\|x\|_{1}\leq 1

such that

\|Tx-P_{\Lambda}A_{n}Tx\|_{2}\,=\,\|x-T_{n}x\|_{2}\,\geq\,\left(\frac{5^{d}-5^{d}/2}{5^{d}}\right)^{1/2}\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

The function
f=Tx
is contained in
F_{d}^{\log}
.
Moreover,
f\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)

and thus

(12)

\|f-A_{n}f\|_{2}\,\geq\,\|f-P_{\Lambda}A_{n}f\|_{2}\,\geq\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},

as claimed.
∎

Clearly, the lower bound (
12
)
also holds for the uniform instead of the
L_{2}
-norm.
Since linear algorithms are optimal
for the recovery problem in the uniform norm, see
[
4
]
,
Lemma
18

implies that the problem of uniform recovery on the class
F_{d}^{\log}
is intractable.
Namely,
the following holds for all algorithms of the form

(13)

A_{n}\colon F_{d}^{\log}\to L_{\infty}([0,1]^{d}),\quad A_{n}(f)=\phi(L_{1}(f),\ldots,L_{n}(f))

where
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}\colon F_{d}^{\log}\to\mathbb{C}

are linear functionals, possibly chosen adaptively,
and
\phi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to L_{\infty}
.
This includes the sampling algorithms of the form (
1
).

Corollary 19
.

The problem of (sampling) recovery in the uniform norm
on the classes
F_{d}^{\log}

suffers from the curse of dimensionality.
For any mapping
A_{n}\colon F_{d}^{\log}\to L_{\infty}
of the form (
13
)
with
n\leq 5^{d}/2
we have

\sup_{f\in F_{d}^{\log}}\|f-A_{n}(f)\|_{\infty}\,\geq\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

In a similar way, one can show that polynomial tractability
cannot be achieved with linear algorithms
for the classes
F^{s,d}_{\rm mix}
and
F_{d}^{\alpha}
from Section
2
.
We omit the details.

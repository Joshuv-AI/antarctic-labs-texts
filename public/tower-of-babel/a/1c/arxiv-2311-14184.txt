1.
Introduction

1.1.
Historical Prelude

Quantum ergodicity is one of the central themes in the theory of automorphic forms. Its origins pivot around the realm of mathematical physics where, given a compact Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
, one addresses the behaviour of eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{M}
as the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
tends to infinity. This question pertains to the dynamics of the ergodic flow. Landmark results of Šnirel’man
[
Šni74
]
, Colin de Verdière
[
CdV85
]
, and Zelditch
[
Zel87
]
assert that if the geodesic flow on the unit cotangent bundle
S^{*}\mathcal{M}
is ergodic, then there exists a density one subsequence of Laplace eigenfunctions whose mass equidistributes on
\mathcal{M}
as the eigenvalue tends to infinity. Rudnick–Sarnak
[
RS94
]
conjectured that when
\mathcal{M}
is hyperbolic (i.e. of negative sectional curvature), this property holds for the full sequence of Laplace eigenfunctions, namely the probability measures
|\phi_{j}|^{2}dv
converge weakly to the normalised measure
\frac{1}{\vol(\mathcal{M})}dv
, where
dv
is the volume element on
\mathcal{M}
. This statement is known as the quantum unique ergodicity (QUE) conjecture. We direct the reader to the expository papers
[
Dya22
,
Sar11
]
for a comprehensive account on the subject.

One can ask the same type of questions for more general higher rank homogeneous spaces
\mathcal{X}=\Gamma\backslash G/K
, where new perspectives come into the picture. If
\mathcal{X}
has rank
r\geq 2
, then the centre of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathcal{X}
is generated by
r
Casimir operators. Given
n\geq 2
, we consider the space

\mathcal{X}_{n}\coloneqq\mathrm{SL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\mathfrak{h}^{n}\simeq\mathrm{SL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})/\mathrm{SO}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}),

where
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
stands for the generalised hyperbolic
n
-space. The space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
has rank
r=n-1
and automorphic forms therein are eigenfunctions of all the Casimir operators. The space
\mathcal{X}_{n}
is also equipped with the continuous spectrum spanned by Langlands Eisenstein series.

In the rank one case of the modular group acting on the standard hyperbolic plane
\mathfrak{h}^{2}=\mathbb{H}^{2}
, the problem was first explored in the work of Luo–Sarnak
[
LS95
]
who showed a modified QUE result for the Eisenstein series
E(z,s)
on the modular surface
\mathcal{X}_{2}
. Moreover, Watson
[
Wat02
]
established that QUE for Hecke–Maaß cusp forms follows from subconvex bounds for triple product
L
-functions –
a fortiori
from the Generalised Lindelöf Hypothesis (GLH). Shortly thereafter, using ergodic methods, Lindenstrauss
[
Lin06
]
resolved QUE for Hecke–Maaß cusp forms on compact arithmetic Riemann surfaces, and Soundararajan
[
Sou10
]
completed the proof for the noncompact arithmetic case.

For
n\geq 3
, QUE for automorphic forms on compact quotients of
{{\mathrm{SL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})}}
was first studied by Silberman–Venkatesh
[
SV07
,
SV19
]
and Silberman
[
Sil15
]
with ergodic techniques. In the noncompact case, Zhang
[
Zha19
]
extended the result of Luo–Sarnak
[
LS95
]
to
n\geq 3
proving a modified QUE result for degenerate maximal parabolic Eisenstein series
E_{(n-1,1)}(z,\frac{1}{2}+it,\mathbbm{1})
induced from the maximal parabolic subgroup
P_{n-1,1}(\mathbb{Z})
and the constant Maaß form
\phi_{0}=\mathbbm{1}
. For a smooth compactly supported function
f
on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, define

\mu_{t,n}(f)\coloneqq\left\langle f,\left|E_{(n-1,1)}\left(\cdot,\frac{1}{2}+it,\mathbbm{1}\right)\right|^{2}\right\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}f(z)\left|E_{(n-1,1)}\left(z,\frac{1}{2}+it,\mathbbm{1}\right)\right|^{2}d\mu_{n}(z),

where
d\mu_{n}(z)
is the standard Haar measure. Zhang
[
Zha19
]
then proved an asymptotic

(1.1)

\mu_{t,n}(f)\sim\frac{2\log t}{\Lambda(n)}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}f(z)d\mu_{n}(z)

as
t\to\infty
, where
\Lambda(s)\coloneqq\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{s}{2})\zeta(s)
is the completed Riemann zeta function. We explicate this result further in Section
1.2
. We abbreviate
d\mu_{n}
to
d\mu
when it is clear what
n
is.

The work of Luo–Sarnak
[
LS95
]
and Zhang
[
Zha19
]
is predicated upon spectrally expanding

and estimating
\mu_{t,n}(\psi)
for all automorphic forms
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
. When
n=2
,

decomposes into Hecke–Maaß cusp forms
\phi_{j}(z)
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}>0
, the Eisenstein series

associated to the cusp
i\infty
, and the constant eigenfunction
\phi_{0}=\mathbbm{1}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}=0
. To prove the estimate (
1.1
) for

, Luo–Sarnak
[
LS95
]
first showed that the contribution of the discrete spectrum is sufficiently small. In fact, if
\phi
is a Hecke–Maaß cusp form, then

(1.2)

\mu_{t,2}(\phi)=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}\phi(z)\left|E\left(z,\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2}d\mu(z)\ll_{\phi,\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{1}{6}+\varepsilon},

while GLH implies the improved exponent
-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon
. The latter was refined by Huang
[
Hua21
]
who studied the mean value and quantum variance for
\mu_{t,2}(\phi)
. His mean value result states that if
\phi
is an even Hecke–Maaß cusp form, then

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\mu_{t,2}(\phi)dt=o_{\phi}(T^{-\frac{1}{2}})

as
T\to\infty
(note that
\mu_{t,2}(\phi)=0
identically if
\phi
is odd). This breaks the barrier beyond what GLH predicts, but only up to a negligibly small improvement. Given any two Hecke–Maaß cusp forms

\mathcal{Q}_{2}(\phi,\psi)=\begin{cases}C_{2}(\phi)L\left(\frac{1}{2},\phi\right)^{2}V_{2}(\phi)&\text{if $\phi=\psi$ is even},\\

V_{2}(\phi)\coloneqq\frac{|\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{it_{\phi}}{2})|^{4}}{2\pi|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+it_{\phi})|^{2}}.

The results of Huang
[
Hua21

should be thought of as the continuous spectrum counterpart of the work of Luo–Sarnak

, Zhao

. Recall that QUE for Hecke–Maaß cusp forms on

asserts that

(1.5)

\mu_{j,2}(f)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)\left|\phi_{j}(z)\right|^{2}d\mu(z)\to\frac{1}{\vol(\mathcal{X}_{2})}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)d\mu(z)

for any

as

. Because the proof of Lindenstrauss
[

is not effective, the quantification of the rate of convergence in (

[
LS95
]
obtained an essentially optimal upper bound for the quantum variance:

(1.6)

\sum_{t_{j}\leq T}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}d\mu(z)-\frac{1}{\vol(\mathcal{X}_{2})}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)d\mu(z)\right|^{2}\ll_{f,\varepsilon}T^{1+\varepsilon}.

Weyl’s law yields
\#\{t_{j}\leq T\}\sim\frac{T^{2}}{12}
, thus (
1.6
) implies a rate of convergence

\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)\left|\phi_{j}(z)\right|^{2}d\mu(z)-\frac{1}{\vol(\mathcal{X}_{2})}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)d\mu(z)\ll_{f,\varepsilon}t_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}

on average. This follows pointwise from the Watson–Ichino formula if we assume GLH. The bound (
1.6
) can be refined for test functions in the subspace
C_{0,0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}_{2})
of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}_{2})
consisting of all functions
f(z)
with zero mean value

\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)d\mu(z)=0,

and whose zeroth coefficient vanishes for
y
large enough. This problem was first addressed by Zhao
[
Zha09
,
Zha10
]
via the Kuznetsov formula (generalising the work of Luo–Sarnak
[
LS04
]
in the holomorphic case). For the space of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms, the subsequent work of Sarnak–Zhao
[
SZ19
, Proposition 7]
refines Zhao’s results and achieves the stronger estimate

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t_{j}\leq T}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}\phi(z)|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}d\mu(z)\right|^{2}=C_{2}^{\prime}(\phi)L\left(\frac{1}{2},\phi\right)V_{2}(\phi),

where
C_{2}^{\prime}(\phi)
is a similar product of local densities given in this case by

C_{2}^{\prime}(\phi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(p)}{p^{\frac{3}{2}}+p^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

with
\lambda_{\phi}(p)
the
p
-th Hecke eigenvalue of
\phi
and
V_{2}(\phi)
is defined as above. The nonnegativity of the central
L

follows as a straightforward consequence.

A key observation underlying in the above approaches is that the inner products
\mu_{j,2}(\psi)=\langle\psi,|\phi_{j}|^{2}\rangle
and
\mu_{t,2}(\psi)=\langle\psi,|E(\cdot,\frac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}\rangle
are given by the triple product formulæ,
a priori
relating QUE to the subconvexity problem. Moreover, the Kuznetsov formula is available in a fairly amenable form. These ideas are present in most of the recent approaches to various versions of the QUE problem on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
; see
[
Hum18
,
HK20
,
You16
]
and references therein.

Furthermore, the quantum variance for Hecke–Maaß cusp forms
\phi
is almost consistent with the Feingold–Peres prediction
[
FP86
]
for the quantum variance for generic chaotic systems. In particular, it recovers the classical variance given by Ratner
[
Rat87
]
, multiplied by
L(\frac{1}{2},\phi)
for the discrete spectrum and by
L(\frac{1}{2},\phi)^{2}
for the continuous spectrum. This difference comes from the Watson–Ichino formulæ for the discrete and continuous spectrum, respectively.

Via the theta correspondence, Nelson
[
Nel22a
,
Nel22b
,
Nel22c
]
investigated the quantum variance for arithmetic compact surfaces, where the problem boils down to the estimation of metaplectic Rankin–Selberg convolutions. The cofinite case is different from the cocompact case, where there is no Hecke theory for the Fourier coefficients of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms.

We conclude that the study of variance formulæ for arithmetic manifolds sheds some light on the QUE problem, where an additional layer of arithmetic information (the Watson–Ichino and Kuznetsov formulæ) enters.

1.2.
Statement of Main Results

The approach of Zhang
[
Zha19
]
leads to the problem of determining the size of the inner products

(1.7)

\mu_{t,n}(g)=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}g(z)\left|E_{(n-1,1)}\left(z,\frac{1}{2}+it,\mathbbm{1}\right)\right|^{2}d\mu(z)

for
g=\phi
a Hecke–Maaß cusp form and for
g=E_{(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r})}
a parabolic Eisenstein series of type
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r})
arising from the parabolic subgroup
P_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}
with
n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
. Nonetheless, for
n\geq 3
, there are important differences from the case of
n=2
. In the case of the degenerate Eisenstein series,
\mu_{t,n}
vanishes identically on Hecke–Maaß cusp forms as well as on a large class of Eisenstein series; see
[
Zha19

. Therefore, the study of QUE reduces to the study of (

) for
g(z)
only in a restricted family of Eisenstein series. In fact, Zhang
[
Zha19
]
proved that this family consists only of Eisenstein series of type
(2,1,\ldots,1)
and the minimal parabolic Eisenstein series of type
(1,\ldots,1)
. Because Eisenstein series fall well shy of being in
L^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
, we work with incomplete Eisenstein series. A brief summary of their theory for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
can be found in
[
Zha19
, Section 2]
and
[
Gol06
, Chapter 10]
.

In analogy with (
1.2
), the contribution of the minimal parabolic Eisenstein series yields the main term in (

), while the contribution of incomplete Eisenstein series of type
(2,1,\ldots,1)
is small. Let
E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(z,\eta,\phi)

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

\phi
, where
\eta
is a smooth compactly supported function; see Sections

&
2.5
for further details. Then Zhang
[
Zha19
, Theorem 4.1]
proved an upper bound

(1.8)

\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))\ll_{\phi,n,\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

In fact, his argument provides the improved exponent
-\frac{n}{2}+1+\varepsilon
in (
1.8
), while GLH implies

(1.9)

\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))\ll_{\phi,n,\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Our first main result is a mean value estimate for the inner products defined by the incomplete Eisenstein series of type
(2,1,\ldots,1)
. Contrary to our expectations, it not only supports the conjecture (
1.9
), but also we go up to a polynomial improvement beyond what GLH predicts.

.

Keep the notation as above. Then we have for any

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))dt\ll_{\phi,n}T^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{7(n-2)}{6(5n-2)}}\sqrt{\log T}

Remark 1.3
.

A key difference between the results of Luo–Sarnak
[
LS95
]
and Zhang
[
Zha19
]
is the following. QUE for Eisenstein series on
\mathcal{X}_{2}
demonstrates an asymptotic

\mu_{t,2}(f)-\frac{2\log t}{\Lambda(2)}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}f(z)d\mu(z)\sim\mathrm{Re}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(1+2it)\right)\neq O(1)

for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}_{2})
, with the error term coming from the continuous contribution. On the other hand, for
n\geq 3
, the contribution of all the incomplete Eisenstein series is bounded:

\mu_{t,n}(f)-\frac{2\log t}{\Lambda(n)}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}f(z)d\mu(z)=O(1)

for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
. It would be challenging to seek for a nice expression for the second moment

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\mu_{t,n}(f)-\frac{2\log t}{\Lambda(n)}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}f(z)d\mu(z)\right|^{2}dt

for
f
in a restricted space of smooth functions. While likely presently feasible, we postpone answering to this question for future work.

Remark 1.4
.

It behoves us to explain the classical variance and Zelditch’s quantisation. For
\mathcal{X}=\Gamma\backslash G/K
and classical observables
\psi\in C_{0,0}^{\infty}(\Gamma\backslash G)
, the fluctuations along a generic orbit of the geodesic flow
\mathcal{G}_{t}(g)
obey the central limit theorem, namely the distribution of

\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{0}^{T}\psi(\mathcal{G}_{t}(g))dt

becomes Gaussian with zero mean and the variance form given by the nonnegative Hermitian form on
C_{0,0}^{\infty}(\Gamma\backslash G)
:

V_{C}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}\psi_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{t}(g))\overline{\psi_{2}(g)}dgdt.

For Hecke–Maaß cusp forms
\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}=\phi
with eigenvalue
\frac{1}{4}+t_{\phi}^{2}
, the classical variance form
V_{C}
matches the quantum variance form given by (
1.4
). Zelditch
[
Zel94
]
proved that the means of the classical and quantum observables coincide. For chaotic systems, it is generally expected that the classical variance form is relevant to the quantum variance form. In this work, we can explicate the shape of the quantum variance form
V_{n}(\phi)
in higher rank.

In the case of
n
-dimensional compact manifolds
\mathcal{M}
, the quantum variance was introduced by Zelditch
[
Zel87
]
to investigate the fluctuations of a quantum observable. His quantisation predict not only the equidistribution of the measures
|\phi_{j}|^{2}dv
(which can be thought of as the position of the quantum states
\phi_{j}
), but also the equidistribution of a semiclassical analogue of the combined position and momentum, called the microlocal lift. The classical observables are smooth functions on the unit cotangent bundle
S^{*}\mathcal{M}
. Zelditch’s quantisation corresponds the classical observable
\psi
to a self-adjoint operator
Op(\psi)
, viewed as the quantum observable taking the value
\langle Op(\psi)\phi_{j},\phi_{j}\rangle
on a wave
\phi_{j}
. The microlocal lift is a map that, in the case of
\psi\in C_{0,0}^{\infty}(S^{*}\mathcal{M})
, takes the form

\widetilde{\mu}_{j}(\psi):\psi\to\int_{\mathcal{M}}\psi\phi_{j}dv.

The QUE conjecture posits not only that
\mu_{j}
converges weak-
\ast
to
\mu
, but also that the sequence of the microlocal lifts converges to the Liouville measure. We direct the reader to the work of Jakobson
[
Jak94
]
on QUE for microlocal lifts on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Quantum variance bounds for general compact smooth
n
-manifolds were first attained by Zelditch
[
Zel87
]
and are sufficiently weaker than the essentially best possible bound (
1.6
). In fact, Zelditch
[
Zel87
]
established an upper bound for the
2k
-th moment

(1.11)

\sum_{t_{j}\leq T}\left|\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(z)|\phi_{j}(z)|^{2}d\mu(z)-\frac{1}{\vol(\mathcal{M})}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(z)d\mu(z)\right|^{2k}\ll_{f}\frac{T^{n}}{(\log T)^{k}}.

When
k=1
and
f\in C_{0,0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, it is expected that (
1.11
) may be improved to an asymptotic
\sim V_{2}(f,f)T
. This expectation extends the observation of Sarnak–Zhao
[
SZ19
]
, and is a huge improvement over (
1.11
), albeit being fundamentally out of reach in general.

.

Quantum ergodicity in higher rank has been studied by Brumley–Matz
[
BM23
]
in the level aspect (in the sense of Benjamini–Schramm limit), extending previous results of Le Masson–Sahlsten
[
LMS17
]
and Abert–Bergeron–Le Masson

1.3.
Strategy of Proofs

We now describe some aspects of our methodology. A key input in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is an approximation argument for smooth compactly supported functions on
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
(see
[
Zha19
, Remark 2.2]
), which is in harmony with
[
LS95
, Proposition 2.3]
.

A second key observation is the absence of the Watson–Ichino formula in higher rank. As a substitute, we utilise the vanishing of the measure
\mu_{t,n}
on Hecke–Maaß cusp forms for
n\geq 3
and on Eisenstein series of types other than
(2,1,\ldots,1)
. This feature is specific to the higher rank context, and lies in the spirit of Langlands’s theory of constant terms. Then the result of Zhang
[
Zha19
, Proposition 4.1]
is an alternative to the Watson–Ichino formula, relating the problem to certain moments of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

L
-functions. This is where the second moment estimate of Jutila
[
Jut96
]
(a nonholomorphic analogue of the work of Good
[
Goo82
]
) comes into play. Huang
[
Hua21
]
used an asymptotic of Kuznetsov
[
Kuz81
]
as he only needed the main term, but the error term in fact governs the quality of a polynomial power saving in Theorem
1.1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
again employs
[
Zha19
, Proposition 4.1]
, leading to the problem of determining the behaviour of second moments of completed
\mathrm{GL}_{2}

L
-functions of the shape

(1.12)

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\phi\right)\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}-it,\psi\right)dt,

where
\phi\neq\psi
. This has not attracted sufficient attention, except from investigations related to QUE, as in
[
Hua21
]
. The study of (
1.12
) involves an delicate treatment of
L(s,\phi)
as well as the oscillatory factors stemming from the archimedean component
L_{\infty}(s,\phi)
. The case that we address here is similar to
[
Hua21
]
except for a smoother weight function in the integrand.

2.
Automorphic Preliminaries

This section collects some basic ingredients from the spectral theory of automorphic forms on the space
\mathcal{X}_{n}=\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. We direct the reader to
[
Gol06
]
for more details.

2.1.
The Generalised Hyperbolic Space

Recall that the generalised hyperbolic
n
-space
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
is the quotient
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and can be identified
[
Gol06
, Definition 1.2.3]
with the set of all
n\times n
matrices of the form
z=x\cdot y
, where

(2.1)

x=\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{1,2}&x_{1,3}&\cdots&x_{1,n-1}&x_{1,n}\\
&1&x_{2,3}&\cdots&x_{2,n-1}&x_{2,n}\\
&&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
&&&&1&x_{n-1,n}\\
&&&&&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad y=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\cdots y_{n-1}&&&&\\
&y_{1}\cdots y_{n-2}&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&y_{1}&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}

with
x_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
y_{i}>0
, known as the Iwasawa decomposition for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. The generalised hyperbolic
n
-space
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
is
\frac{(n+2)(n-1)}{2}
-dimensional over
\mathbb{R}
and does not necessarily have complex structure. Furthermore, the Haar measure is given by

d\mu(z)\coloneqq\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x_{i,j}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}y_{k}^{-k(n-k)}\frac{dy_{k}}{y_{k}}.

We employ the standard definition in
[
Gol06
]
giving

\vol(\mathcal{X}_{n})=n\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Lambda(k),

while in
[
Zha19
]
the Haar measure is normalised so that
\vol(\mathcal{X}_{n})=1
.

2.2.
Hecke–Maaß Cusp Forms

We define Hecke–Maaß cusp forms associated to
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
because they play an important rôle in the formulation of parabolic Eisenstein series. Let
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
be the centre of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. There exists a family of differential operators (the Casimir operators)
\Delta_{m,n}
parametrised by
n\neq 2
and
2\leq m\leq n
, generating
\mathfrak{D}_{n}
as a polynomial algebra of rank
n-1
; see
[
Gol06
, Propositions 2.3.3 & 2.3.5]
.

Given
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
, define

I_{\nu}(z)\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}y_{i}^{b_{i,j}\nu_{j}},\qquad b_{i,j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}ij&\text{if $i+j\leq n$},\\
(n-i)(n-j)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The
I_{\nu}
-functions are eigenfunctions of all the Casimir operators
\Delta_{m,n}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{m,n}
:

\Delta_{m,n}I_{\nu}(z)=\lambda_{m,n}I_{\nu}(z).

An automorphic form
\phi(z)
of type
\nu
in
L^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
is a smooth function satisfying the invariant condition
\phi(\gamma z)=\phi(z)
for
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
as well as the condition

\Delta_{m,n}\phi(z)=\lambda_{m,n}\phi(z).

Let
\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}=n
. If we further have

\int_{(\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\cap U)\backslash U}\phi(uz)du=0

for all matrix groups of the shape

U\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_{r_{1}}&\ast&\cdots&\ast\\
0&I_{r_{2}}&\cdots&\ast\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&I_{r_{m}}\end{pmatrix}\right\}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),

then
\phi
is called a Maaß cusp form. We assume without loss of generality that all Maaß cusp forms are Hecke–Maaß cusp forms; see
[
Gol06
, Section 9.3]
for the Hecke theory on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

2.3.
Parabolic Subgroups

There are various versions of Eisenstein series depending on the parabolic subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. The parabolic subgroup
P_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})
associated to the partition
n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
is defined by

P_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{m}_{n_{1}}&\ast&\cdots&\ast\\
0&\mathfrak{m}_{n_{2}}&\cdots&\ast\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathfrak{m}_{n_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\right\}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathfrak{m}_{n_{i}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
. Two different parabolic subgroups defined by two different partitions
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
and
(n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{r}^{\prime})
are called associate if the set
\left\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\right\}
is a permutation of
\{n_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,n_{r}^{\prime}\}
. The parabolic subgroup admits a Langlands decomposition

P_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})=N_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})\cdot M_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R}),

where

N_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_{n_{1}}&\ast&\cdots&\ast\\
0&I_{n_{2}}&\cdots&\ast\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&I_{n_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\right\}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

is the unipotent radical, and

M_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{m}_{n_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mathfrak{m}_{n_{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathfrak{m}_{n_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\right\}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

is the Levi component, where
\mathfrak{m}_{n_{i}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
. If we take an element
\gamma\in P_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})
with

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n_{1}}&\ast&\cdots&\ast\\
0&I_{n_{2}}&\cdots&\ast\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&I_{n_{r}}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{m}_{n_{1}}(\gamma)&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\mathfrak{m}_{n_{2}}(\gamma)&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathfrak{m}_{n_{r}}(\gamma)\end{pmatrix},

then the Langlands decomposition defines the projection map
\pi_{n_{i}}:P_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
:

\gamma\to\mathfrak{m}_{n_{i}}(\gamma).

2.4.
Eisenstein Series

In what follows, for any real Lie group
G
of
n\times n
matrices, we write
G(\mathbb{Z})\coloneqq G(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. The spectral decomposition on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
reads

(2.2)

L^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{n})=L_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{n})\oplus\bigoplus_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}L^{2}_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(\mathcal{X}_{n}),

where the decomposition of the continuous spectrum runs through representatives of classes of associate partitions. The cuspidal and continuous parts are respectively spanned by
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Hecke–Maaß cusp forms and Langlands parabolic Eisenstein series.

For each
z\in\mathfrak{h}^{n}
in (
2.1
) and for every
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
satisfying

(2.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}s_{i}=0,

we define

I_{s}(z,P_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}})\coloneqq\left(\prod_{j_{1}=n-n_{1}+1}^{n}Y_{j_{1}}\right)^{s_{1}}\left(\prod_{j_{2}=n-n_{1}-n_{2}+1}^{n-n_{1}}Y_{j_{2}}\right)^{s_{2}}\cdots\left(\prod_{j_{r}=1}^{n_{r}}Y_{j_{r}}\right)^{s_{r}},

where
Y_{i}=y_{1}y_{2}\cdots y_{i-1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\phi_{i}
be a collection of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms on
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}(\mathbb{R})
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Let
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
satisfy (
2.3
). Then we define the parabolic Eisenstein series by

E_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(z,s,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r})\coloneqq\sum_{\gamma\in P_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\phi_{i}(\mathfrak{m}_{n_{i}}(\gamma z))I_{s}(\gamma z,P_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}}).

From the linear relation (
2.3
), we may eliminate
s_{r}
, thus
E_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(z,s,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r})
can be regarded as a function of
z
and
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{r-1}
. We employ this convention in the rest of this paper. As an emblematic example, the minimal parabolic Eisenstein series comes from the partition
n=1+\cdots+1
and is given by

(2.4)

E_{(1,\ldots,1)}(z,s)\coloneqq\sum_{\gamma\in P_{1,\ldots,1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})}I_{s}(\gamma z).

Given the constant Maaß form
\phi_{0}=\mathbbm{1}\in\mathrm{SL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})
, the partition
n=(n-1)+1
defines the totally degenerate maximal parabolic Eisenstein series

E_{(n-1,1)}(z,s,\mathbbm{1})\coloneqq\sum_{\gamma\in P_{n-1,1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})}I_{s}(\gamma z,P_{n-1,1}).

Note that all the Eisenstein series converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s_{i})\gg 1
.

2.5.
Incomplete Eisenstein Series

For reasons of convergence, it is often convenient to address the continuous spectrum via incomplete Eisenstein series in place of Eisenstein series. If
\eta
is a smooth compactly supported function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, then its Mellin transform is defined by

\widetilde{\eta}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})y_{1}^{-s_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{-s_{n}}\frac{dy_{1}\cdots dy_{n}}{y_{1}\cdots y_{n}}.

Then the incomplete Eisenstein series associated to
E_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(z,s,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r})
and
\eta
is defined by

E_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(z,\eta,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r})\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{r-1}}\int_{(2)}\cdots\int_{(2)}\widetilde{\eta}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r-1})\\
\times E_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(z,s,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r})ds_{1}\cdots ds_{r-1}.

The properties of
\eta
ensure the convergence of the integral. The space
L^{2}_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
in (
2.2
) is spanned by the incomplete Eisenstein series of type
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
.

2.6.
Approximation Argument

It is clear from
[
Zha19
, Theorem 5.1]
that the main term in higher rank QUE for degenerate Eisenstein series stems from an asymptotic

\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}E_{(1,\ldots,1)}(z,\eta)\left|E_{(n-1,1)}\left(z,\frac{1}{2}+it,\mathbbm{1}\right)\right|^{2}d\mu(z)\sim\frac{2\log t}{\Lambda(n)}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}E_{(1,\ldots,1)}(z,\eta)d\mu(z)

with
E_{(1,\ldots,1)}(z,\eta)
an incomplete Eisenstein series associated to (
2.4
). For the approximation argument, we fix a smooth compactly supported test function
f
. We then construct a finite linear combination
G(z)
of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms and incomplete Eisenstein series such that
\|f-G\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
. The proof thus boils down to estimating
\mu_{t,n}
on Hecke–Maaß cusp forms and incomplete parabolic Eisenstein series. Recall that from
[
Zha19
, Section 4]
, one obtains

\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\phi(z)\left|E_{(n-1,1)}\left(z,\frac{1}{2}+it,\mathbbm{1}\right)\right|^{2}d\mu(z)=0

for every Hecke–Maaß cusp form
\phi
on
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.

3.
Watson–Ichino-Type Formula

The existence of the Watson–Ichino formula is key when the degree is
2
. For instance, the results of Luo–Sarnak
[
LS04
]
and Huang
[
Hua21
]
concerning quantum variance are based on the identity

|\mu_{t,2}(\phi)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Lambda(\frac{1}{2},\phi)^{2}|\Lambda(\frac{1}{2}-2it,\phi)|^{2}}{|\Lambda(1+2it)|^{4}\Lambda(1,\operatorname{ad}\phi)},

where
L(s,\phi)
denotes an
L
-function associated to
\phi
, defined for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
by

L(s,\phi)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(n)}{n^{s}}

with
\lambda_{\phi}(n)
the normalised Fourier coefficients of
\phi
. We also denote by
\rho_{\phi}(n)
the unnormalised Fourier coefficients of
\phi
such that
\rho_{\phi}(n)=\rho_{\phi}(1)\lambda_{\phi}(n)
. For brevity, we focus on the case of even Hecke–Maaß cusp forms
\phi
. With this assumption, the completed
L
-function is given by

(3.1)

\Lambda(s,\phi)\coloneqq L_{\infty}(s,\phi)L(s,\phi),\qquad L_{\infty}(s,\phi)\coloneqq\pi^{-s}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+it_{\phi}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-it_{\phi}}{2}\right),

and enjoys the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\phi)=\Lambda(1-s,\phi)
.

Similarly, the adjoint square
L
-function associated to
\phi
is defined for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
by

L(s,\operatorname{ad}\phi)\coloneqq\zeta(2s)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(n^{2})}{n^{s}},

and enjoys the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\operatorname{ad}\phi)=\Lambda(1-s,\operatorname{ad}\phi)
with the completed
L
-function

(3.2)

\Lambda(s,\operatorname{ad}\phi)\coloneqq L_{\infty}(s,\operatorname{ad}\phi)L(s,\operatorname{ad}\phi),

where

L_{\infty}(s,\operatorname{ad}\phi)\coloneqq\pi^{-\frac{3s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}+it_{\phi}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}-it_{\phi}\right).

In particular, the special value at
s=1
becomes

(3.3)

\Lambda(1,\operatorname{ad}\phi)=\frac{8}{|\rho_{\phi}(1)|^{2}}.

In higher rank, the following result works as a partial substitute of the Watson–Ichino formula (see
[
Ich08
,
Wat02
]
) and is a crucial ingredient in our argument.

Proposition 3.1

(Zhang
[
Zha19
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Let
\phi
be an even Hecke–Maaß cusp form on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and let
\eta
be a smooth compactly support function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n-2}
. Then we have for any
n\geq 3
that

(3.4)

\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))=a_{n}\frac{|\Lambda(2-\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}{|\Lambda(\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}\phi(z)\left|E\left(z,1-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{int}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}},

where (the ellipsis hides an explicitly computable value that is irrelevant for our purposes)

(3.5)

a_{n}\coloneqq\frac{(-1)^{n}(n!)^{2}}{72}\widetilde{\eta}(\cdots).

If
\phi
is odd, then the left-hand side of (
3.4
) vanishes identically.

Proof.

Recall that Zhang
[
Zha19
]
uses the normalised Haar measure given by

\frac{d\mu(z)}{n\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Lambda(k)}.

Exceptionally, we introduce the ad hoc notation

\mu_{\nu,n}(f)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}f(z)|E_{(n-1,1)}(z,\nu,\mathbbm{1})|^{2}d\mu(z).

Hence, the second step in his computation
[
Zha19
, Page 29]
reads for
n\geq 4
(adjusting some notation)

\frac{1}{n\prod_{k=2}^{n}\Lambda(k)}\mu_{\nu,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))=\frac{1}{(n-1)\prod_{k=2}^{n-1}\Lambda(k)}\frac{-(n^{2}-n)}{\Lambda(n)}\frac{|\Lambda(n\nu-1)|^{2}}{|\Lambda(n\nu)|^{2}}\\
\times\mu_{\frac{n\nu-1}{n-1},n-1}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta^{\prime},\phi)).

This boils down to

\mu_{\nu,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))=-n^{2}\frac{|\Lambda(n\nu-1)|^{2}}{|\Lambda(n\nu)|^{2}}\mu_{\frac{n\nu-1}{n-1},n-1}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta^{\prime},\phi)).

Note that the incomplete Eisenstein series on the left-hand side is associated to the partition
2+1+\cdots+1=n
, thus
r=n-1
and
\eta
is a smooth function of
n-2
variables. The incomplete Eisenstein series on the right-hand side is associated to the partition
2+1+\cdots+1=n-1
, thus
\eta^{\prime}
is a smooth function of
n-3
variables. More precisely,
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are related in terms of their Mellin transforms (cf. Zhang
[
Zha19
]
)

\widetilde{\eta}^{\prime}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-3})=\widetilde{\eta}(s_{1}+c,\ldots,s_{n-3}+c,-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{n-3}+c)),

where
c=\frac{2-\mathrm{Re}(\nu)}{n-1}-1
. Iterating this process
n-2
times with
\nu=\frac{1}{2}+it
and noting that the case of
n=3
is peculiar, we obtain the desired claim.
∎

The right-hand side of (
3.4
) simplifies further. Consider the integral

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(s)\coloneqq\int_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}\phi(z)E\left(z,1-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{int}{2}\right)E(z,s)d\mu(z).

Unfolding implies

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(z)E\left(z,1-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{int}{2}\right)y^{s}\,\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}.

This vanishes identically for
\phi
odd, while for
\phi
even, we compute

\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(s)=\frac{2\rho_{\phi}(1)}{\Lambda(2-\frac{n}{2}+int)}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(m)\sigma_{\frac{n}{2}-1-int}(m)}{m^{s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{4}-\frac{int}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{it_{\phi}}(2\pi y)K_{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{int}{2}}(2\pi y)y^{s}\frac{dy}{y}.

Following Luo–Sarnak
[
LS95
]
and using Stade’s formula
[
Zha19
, Lemma 4.1]
, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\phi}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{\phi}(1)}{4\pi^{2s}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+it_{\phi}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{int}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-it_{\phi}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n}{8}+\frac{int}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Gamma\left(\frac{s+it_{\phi}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{8}-\frac{int}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-it_{\phi}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{8}-\frac{int}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{L(s-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{4}-\frac{int}{2},\phi)L(s+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{int}{2},\phi)}{\Lambda(2-\frac{n}{2}+int)\Lambda(2s)}.

Setting
s=1-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{int}{2}
now leads to

\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))=a_{n}\frac{|\Lambda(2-\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}{|\Lambda(\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}\mathcal{I}_{\phi}\left(1-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{int}{2}\right),

which is equal to

(3.6)

\frac{a_{n}\rho_{\phi}(1)}{4\pi^{2-\frac{n}{2}-int}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{3-n}{4}+\frac{it_{\phi}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{i(t_{\phi}-nt)}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{i(t_{\phi}+nt)}{2}\right)\\
\times\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}-int,\phi)L(\frac{3-n}{2},\phi)}{|\Lambda(\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}.

This expression is consistent with
[
Hua21
, Equation (3.1)]
when
n=2
. Direct consequences include the following Watson–Ichino-type formulæ.

.

(3.7)

), (

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we embark on the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The asymptotic behaviour of the gamma factors in (
3.6
) can be determined via Stirling’s formula
[
GR15
, Equation (8.327.1)]
:

\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{i(t_{\phi}-nt)}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{i(t_{\phi}+nt)}{2})}{|\Gamma(\frac{n}{4}+\frac{int}{2})|^{2}}\\
=\left(\frac{2}{nt}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}-int\log(\frac{nt}{2e})}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\right).

It thus follows from (
3.6
) that

\int_{T}^{2T}\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))dt=\frac{a_{n}\rho_{\phi}(1)}{4\pi^{2-n-int}}\left(\frac{2}{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}}\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{3-n}{4}+\frac{it_{\phi}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}L\left(\phi,\frac{3-n}{2}\right)\\
\times\int_{T}^{2T}e^{-int\log(\frac{nt}{2e\pi})}\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}-int,\phi)}{|\zeta(\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}+O_{n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log T}}{T^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\right),

where the error term stems from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and
[
Jut96
]
. It now suffices to evaluate the integral

\mathcal{I}(T)\coloneqq\int_{T}^{2T}e^{-int\log(\frac{nt}{2e\pi})}\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}-int,\phi)}{|\zeta(\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}.

At this stage, our computation of the mean value becomes easier than for
n=2
thanks to the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
on the line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=\frac{n}{2}>1
.

Let
U\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
be a parameter whose optimal size will be determined later. We introduce a smooth weight function
v
satisfying the following properties:

•

v
is compactly supported on
[1-U,2+U]
;

•

v(t)=1
for
t\in[1,2]
and
0\leq v(t)\leq 1
otherwise;

•

|v^{(j)}(t)|\ll_{j}U^{-j}
for all fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

In what follows, the letter
\varepsilon>0
denotes a small quantity to be fixed later. Define

(4.1)

\mathcal{I}_{v}(T)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-int\log(\frac{nt}{2e\pi})}\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}-int,\phi)}{|\zeta(\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}v\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}.

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and
[
Jut96
]
once again implies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}(T)-\mathcal{I}_{v}(T)|

\displaystyle\ll_{\phi,n}\left\{[t\log t+t]^{T(1+U)}_{T}+T^{\frac{2}{3}+\varepsilon}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{U}{T^{n-2}}}

\displaystyle\ll_{\phi,n}T^{-\frac{n-3}{2}}U\sqrt{\log T}+T^{\frac{4}{3}-\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon}\sqrt{U}.

The Riemann zeta function in the denominator in (
4.1
) can be expanded as

\frac{1}{|\zeta(\frac{n}{2}+int)|^{2}}=\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq K}\frac{\mu(k)\mu(\ell)}{(k\ell)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left(\frac{k}{\ell}\right)^{int}+O(K^{1-\frac{n}{2}})

for a parameter
K>0
at our disposal. The error term now contributes
O(K^{1-\frac{n}{2}}T^{-\frac{n-3}{2}}\sqrt{\log T})
in (
4.1
). For
t\asymp T
, the approximate functional equation reads

(4.2)

L\left(\phi,\frac{1}{2}-int\right)=\sum_{m\leq T^{1+\varepsilon}}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(m)}{m^{\frac{1}{2}-int}}\mathcal{W}_{t}^{-}(m)-i\left(\frac{nt}{2e\pi}\right)^{2int}\sum_{m\leq T^{1+\varepsilon}}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(m)}{m^{\frac{1}{2}+int}}\mathcal{W}_{t}^{+}(m)+O_{\phi,n}(T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}),

where

\mathcal{W}_{t}^{\pm}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\varepsilon-iT^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon+iT^{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{nt}{2\pi y}\right)^{s}e^{s^{2}\pm\frac{i\pi s}{2}}\frac{ds}{s}.

The error term contributes
O_{\phi,n}(T^{-\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon})
in (
4.1
), thus it remains to estimate the expression

(4.3)

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\varepsilon-iT^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon+iT^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{m\leq T^{1+\varepsilon}}\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq K}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(m)\mu(k)\mu(\ell)}{\sqrt{m}(k\ell)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ih(t)}v_{1}(t)dt\left(\frac{n}{2\pi m}\right)^{s}e^{s^{2}-\frac{i\pi s}{2}}\frac{ds}{s},

where
v_{1}(t)\coloneqq t^{s-\frac{n-1}{2}}v(\frac{t}{T})
and
h(t)\coloneqq-nt\log(\frac{knt}{2e\pi\ell m})
. We focus on the first term in (
4.2
) as the treatment of the second term is similar. We are now in a position to apply the stationary phase analysis to the oscillatory integral

\mathcal{J}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ih(t)}v_{1}(t)dt.

Note that
v_{1}
is a smooth function compactly supported on
[T(1-U),T(2+U)]
satisfying

v_{1}^{(j)}(t)\ll_{j}T^{-\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon}(TU)^{-j}

for all fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
U\leq T^{-\varepsilon}
. Moreover,
h
is a smooth function such that
|h^{(j)}(t)|\asymp_{j}T^{1-j}
for
j\geq 2
and
h^{\prime}(t)=0
if and only if
t=\frac{2\pi\ell m}{kn}
.

Case I:

\frac{2\pi\ell m}{kn}<\frac{T}{2}
or
\frac{2\pi\ell m}{kn}>3T
.

In this case, there holds
|h^{\prime}(t)|\gg 1
, thus
[
BKY13
, Lemma 8.1]
yields

\mathcal{J}\ll_{A}T^{-\frac{n-3}{2}+\varepsilon}(T^{-\frac{A}{2}}+(TU)^{-A})\ll_{A}T^{-\frac{A}{2}-\frac{n-3}{2}+\varepsilon}

for any
A>0
and
U\geq T^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. The rapid decay of the function
s\mapsto e^{s^{2}-\frac{i\pi s}{2}}
on vertical
lines justifies that the contribution of those terms is negligibly small.

Case II:

\frac{T}{2}\leq\frac{2\pi\ell m}{kn}\leq 3T
.

In this case, we observe
|h^{\prime}(t)|\ll 1
and
|h^{\prime\prime}(t)|\gg T^{-1}
, thus
[
BKY13
, Proposition 8.2]
with
U\geq T^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
for
0<\delta<\frac{1}{10}
yields

\mathcal{J}=\frac{e(\frac{\ell m}{k})}{\sqrt{\frac{kn}{2\pi\ell m}}}w_{0}\left(\frac{2\pi\ell m}{kn}\right)+O_{A}(T^{-A}),

where
w_{0}
is a smooth function compactly supported on
[T(1-U),T(2+U)]
satisfying

|w_{0}^{(j)}(t)|\ll_{j}T^{-\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon}(TU)^{-j}.

We now choose
K
such that
\frac{2\pi\ell m}{kn}>3T
for
m\leq T^{1+\varepsilon}
so that
K\leq\frac{2\pi}{3n}T^{\varepsilon}
suffices. Therefore, the expression (
4.3
) equals (up to an absolute constant)

\int_{\varepsilon-iT^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon+iT^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{1\leq k,\ell\leq K}\frac{\mu(k)\mu(\ell)}{k^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\ell^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}D\left(s,\phi\times\frac{\ell}{k}\right)\left(\frac{n}{2\pi}\right)^{s}e^{s^{2}-\frac{i\pi s}{2}}\frac{ds}{s}+O_{A}(T^{-A}),

where

D\left(s,\phi\times\frac{\ell}{k}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(m)e(\frac{\ell m}{k})}{m^{s}}w\left(\frac{\ell m}{k}\right)

with
w(x)=w_{0}(\frac{2\pi x}{n})
. Define the cuspidal analogue of the Estermann zeta function by

L\left(s,\phi\times\frac{\ell}{k}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(m)e(\frac{\ell m}{k})}{m^{s}}

for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. After meromorphic continuation, Mellin inversion yields

(4.4)

D\left(s,\phi\times\frac{\ell}{k}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}L\left(s+s^{\prime},\phi\times\frac{\ell}{k}\right)\hat{w}(s^{\prime})\left(\frac{k}{\ell}\right)^{s^{\prime}}ds^{\prime},

where
\hat{w}(s^{\prime})
is the Mellin transform of
w
. Integration by parts implies for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
that

\hat{w}(s^{\prime})=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{s^{\prime}(s^{\prime}+1)\ldots(s^{\prime}+j-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{(j)}(x)x^{s^{\prime}+j-1}dx\ll_{j}\frac{T^{\sigma-\frac{n-1}{2}+\varepsilon}U^{-j}}{1+|\tau^{\prime}|^{j}}

for
s^{\prime}=\sigma+i\tau^{\prime}
. Write
\frac{\ell}{k}=\frac{u}{q}
for
(u,q)=1
. By the functional equation and the Phragmén–Lindelöf principle, it is straightforward to verify that

L\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\tau^{\prime},\phi\times\frac{u}{q}\right)\ll_{\phi}(q(1+|\tau^{\prime}|))^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}}.

Because
q\leq k
, shifting the contour in (
4.4
) to
\mathrm{Re}(s^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon
gives

\displaystyle D\left(s,\phi\times\frac{\ell}{k}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{\phi}k^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}}T^{1-\frac{n}{2}}U^{-2}\left(\frac{k}{\ell}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1+|\tau+\tau^{\prime}|)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}}}{1+|\tau^{\prime}|^{2}}d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ll_{\phi}k^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}T^{1-\frac{n}{2}}U^{-2}P(\tau),

where
P
is a fixed function of polynomial growth with respect to
\tau=\mathrm{Re}(s)
.

Gathering the above observations together yields

\int_{T}^{2T}\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))dt\\
\ll_{\phi,n}T^{-\frac{n-3}{2}}U\sqrt{\log T}+T^{\frac{4}{3}-\frac{n}{2}+\varepsilon}\sqrt{U}+K^{1-\frac{n}{2}}T^{-\frac{n-3}{2}}\sqrt{\log T}+T^{1-\frac{n}{2}}U^{-2}.

We optimise
K=\frac{2\pi}{3n}T^{\varepsilon}
and
U=T^{-\eta}
for
\varepsilon\leq\eta<\frac{1}{2}
. Then the right-hand side is less than

(T^{-\eta}+T^{-\frac{1}{6}-\frac{\eta}{2}+\varepsilon}+T^{-\frac{\varepsilon n}{2}+\varepsilon}+T^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta})T^{-\frac{n-3}{2}}\sqrt{\log T},

which is minimal whenever

\eta=\frac{2}{15}+\frac{2\varepsilon}{5},\qquad\varepsilon=\frac{7}{3(5n-2)}.

Altogether, we conclude that

\int_{T}^{2T}\mu_{t,n}(E_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}(\cdot,\eta,\phi))dt\ll_{\phi,n}T^{-\frac{n-3}{2}-\frac{7(n-2)}{6(5n-2)}}\sqrt{\log T}.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is now complete.

5.

This section provides an asymptotic formula for a weighted version of the quantum variance

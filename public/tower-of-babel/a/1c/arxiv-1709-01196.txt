On Fourier algebra of a hypergroup constructed from a conditional expectation on a locally compact group
A. A. Kalyuzhnyi, G. B. Podkolzin, Yu. A. Chapovsky

Abstract
. We prove that the Fourier space of a hypergroup constructed from a conditional expectation on a locally compact group has a Banach algebra structure.

1.
Introduction

It is well known
[
4
]
that, for a locally
compact group
G
, the Fourier-Stieltjes space
\mathscr{B}(G)
and the
Fourier space
\mathscr{A}(G)
, make commutative Banach algebras.

For a hypercomplex
system
[
2
]
or a
DJS-hypergroup
[
1
]
, one can also define
the Fourier-Stieltjes and the Fourier spaces. It is well known that,
being Banach spaces, they do not, in general, make Banach algebras.
Conditions for this to be the case were considered
in
[
11
,
12
]

for commutative hypergroups and special type hypergroups called
ultraspherical. In
[
9
]
, a general sufficient condition for
the Fourier and the Fourier-Stieltjes spaces of a locally compact
hypergroup to be Banach algebras was obtained. It was also proved
there that there exists the Fourier Banach algebra on the double coset
hypergroup constructed from a locally compact group.

In this paper we prove that the Fourier space of a DJS-hypergroup that
is constructed from a locally compact group and an expectation, see

[
6
,
8
]
, also
has a Banach algebra structure. This result is a generalization of the
one obtained in
[
12
]
, where the author
imposes additional conditions on the hypergroup.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall
a traditional definition of a DJS-hypergroup
Q
in terms of the
convolution algebra structure on bounded measures on
Q
. Then,
in Propositions
1

and
2
, we give an equivalent form for a
definition of a DJS-hypergroup in a dual setting in terms of a
comultiplication on the
C^{*}
-algebra of continuous functions on
Q

vanishing at infinity and its multiplier algebra of continuous bounded
functions on
Q
. This turns out to be more convenient for purposes of
this paper.

Section
3
gives a main result of the paper. In
Theorem
1
, we describe construction
of a DJS-hypergroup from a conditional expectation on a locally
compact group. It follows from proofs that the construction of a
conditional expectation is similar to the construction of orbital
morphisms
[
5
]
, except that the corresponding orbital mapping
need not be open. Using results from
[
9
]
we prove in
Theorem
2
the main result that the Fourier
space of the hypergroup constructed from a conditional expectation on
a locally compact hypergroup is a Banach algebra.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Hypergroups

Let
Q
be a Hausdorff locally compact topological space.

The linear space of complex-valued continuous functions
(resp., bounded continuous functions, continuous functions approaching
zero at infinity) is denoted by
\mathscr{C}(Q)
(resp.,
\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)
,

\mathscr{C}_{0}(Q)
). The space
\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)
is endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in Q}|f(t)|
. Support of a function
f\in\mathscr{C}(Q)
is denoted by
\mathrm{supp}\,(f)
. For an open subset
U
of
Q
,

\mathscr{K}(U)
denotes the subspace of functions
f\in\mathscr{C}(Q)
such
that

\mathrm{supp}\,(f)\subset U
, and
\mathscr{K}_{+}(U)
(resp.,
\mathscr{K}_{+}^{*}(U)
) denotes
the subset of functions
f\in\mathscr{K}(U)
such that

f\geq 0
(resp.,
f>0
). For

p\in U
, we denote by
\mathscr{K}_{+}^{p}(U)
the subset of
\mathscr{K}_{+}(U)
such
that
f(p)>0
. By
1_{Q}
, we denote the constant function,
1_{Q}(s)=1

for all
s\in Q
.

A measure is understood as a complex Radon
measure
[
3
]
on
Q
. The linear space of complex
Radon measures, over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers, is denoted by

\mathscr{M}(Q)
. The subspace of
\mathscr{M}(Q)
of bounded (resp., compactly
supported) measures is denoted by
\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
(resp.,
\mathscr{M}_{c}(Q)
)
and, for
\mu\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
, its norm is
\|\mu\|=\sup_{f\in\mathscr{K}(Q),\ \|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1}|\mu(f)|
. The subset of
\mathscr{M}(Q)
of
nonnegative (resp., probability) measures is denoted by
\mathscr{M}_{+}(Q)

(resp.,
\mathscr{M}_{p}(Q)
). For a measure
\mu\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(Q)
, its support
is denoted by
\mathrm{supp}\,(\mu)
. If
\mu\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(Q)\cap\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
,
then
\|\mu\|=\mu(1_{Q})
. The Dirac measure at a point
s\in Q
is
denoted by
\varepsilon_{s}
. The integral of
f\in\mathscr{K}(F)
with
respect to a measure
\mu\in\mathscr{M}
is denoted by

\mu(f)=\langle\,f,\mu\,\rangle=\int_{F}\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle\,d\mu(t)=\int_{F}f(t)\,d\mu(t)
.

The set of all nonempty compact subsets of
Q
is denoted
by
\mathcal{K}
. We consider
\mathcal{K}
endowed with the Michael
topology
[
10
]
. Recall that this topology is
generated by the subbasis of all
\mathcal{U}_{V}(W)=\{F\in\mathcal{K}:F\subset W\ \hbox{and}\ F\cap V\neq\emptyset\}
for open subsets
V

and
W
of
Q
.

A (locally compact) hypergroup is a locally compact Hausdorff
topological space
Q
such that
\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
is endowed with a
multiplication, called convolution and denoted by
*
, satisfying the
following conditions
[
1
]
:

(H_{1})

(\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q),*)
is an associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
.

(H_{2})

For all
s,t\in Q
,
\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\in\mathscr{M}_{p}(Q)
and
\mathrm{supp}\,(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})
is compact.

(H_{3})

The mapping
(s,t)\mapsto\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}
of
Q\times Q
into
\mathscr{M}_{p}(Q)
is continuous with
respect to the weak topology
\sigma(\mathscr{M}_{p}(Q),\mathscr{C}_{0}(Q))
on
\mathscr{M}_{p}(Q)
.

(H_{4})

The mapping
(s,t)\mapsto\mathrm{supp}\,(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})
of
Q\times Q
into
\mathcal{K}
is
continuous with respect to the Michael topology on
\mathcal{K}
.

(H_{5})

There exists a (necessarily unique) element
e\in Q

such that
\varepsilon_{e}*\varepsilon_{s}=\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{e}=\varepsilon_{s}
for all
s\in Q
.

(H_{6})

There exists a (necessarily unique) homeomorphism

s\mapsto\check{s}
of
Q
into
Q
such that
\check{\check{s}}=s

and
(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})\,\check{}=\varepsilon_{\check{t}}*\varepsilon_{\check{s}}
, where
\check{\mu}
denotes the image of the
measure
\mu
with respect to the homeomorphism
s\mapsto\check{s}
,
i.e.,
\langle\,f,\check{\mu}\,\rangle=\langle\,\check{f},\mu\,\rangle
, where
\check{f}(s)=f(\check{s})
.

(H_{7})

For
s,t\in Q
,
e\in\mathrm{supp}\,(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})
if and only if
s=\check{t}
.

For a measure
\mu\in\mathscr{M}(Q)
and
h\in\mathscr{C}(Q)
, the measure
h\mu

is defined by
\langle\,f,h\mu\,\rangle=\langle\,fh,\mu\,\rangle
for
f\in\mathscr{K}(Q)
; it is
clear that
h\mu\in\mathscr{M}_{c}(Q)
for
h\in\mathscr{K}(Q)
.

Everywhere in the sequel, we assume that the hypergroup possesses a
left invariant measure, denoted by
m
, which means that

\varepsilon_{s}*m=m

for all
s\in Q
. The Banach space
L_{p}(Q,m)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
is denoted by
L_{p}(Q)
.

For
\mu\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
, denote by
\mu^{\star}
the bounded measure
defined by
\mu^{\star}(f)=\overline{\check{\mu}(\bar{f})}
for
f\in\mathscr{K}(Q)
. It follows from the axiom
(H_{6})
of a hypergroup that

⋆
is an involution on the algebra
(\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q),*)
. It is well
known
[
1
]
that
(\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q),*,{}^{\star})

is an involutive Banach algebra.

We denote
L_{1}(m)=\{fm:f\in L_{1}(Q)\}
. It is well
known that
(L_{1}(m),*,{}^{\star})
is a closed two-sided ideal of

(\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q),*,{}^{\star})
.

Identifying each
f\in L_{1}(Q)
with the measure
fm\in L_{1}(m)

yields an involutive Banach algebra structure on
L_{1}(Q)
, denoted by

(L_{1}(Q),*,{}^{\star})
, where

\displaystyle(f*g)(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q}f(t)\langle\,g,\check{\varepsilon}_{t}*\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle\,dm(t),

\displaystyle f^{\star}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{-1}(s)\overline{f}(\check{s}),

where
\kappa:Q\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
is the modular function,

m*\varepsilon_{s}=\Delta(s)m
, see
[
1
]
. Also denote by

f^{\dagger}(s)=\overline{f(\check{s})}.

It is wellknown that a function
f\ast f^{\dagger}
is a positive
definite on hypergroup
Q
, see
[
1
]
.

Let
(Q,*,e,\check{}\,)
be a locally compact hypergroup. Define a
linear map
\tilde{\Delta}
, homomorphisms
\epsilon
and
\,\check{}\,
as
follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{\Delta}\colon\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q\times Q),\qquad(\tilde{\Delta}f)(p,q)=\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{p}\ast\varepsilon_{q}\,\rangle,\\[4.2679pt]
\epsilon\colon\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\epsilon(f)=f(e),\\[4.2679pt]
\check{}\,\colon\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q),\qquad\check{f}(p)=f(\check{p}),\end{array}

(1)

where
\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)
is considered as a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra.

The following lemma is clear.

Lemma 1

Let
Q
be a locally compact space,
\mu\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)\cap\mathscr{M}_{+}(Q)
, and
F\subset Q
is a closed set. Then
F\cap\mathrm{supp}\,(\mu)=\emptyset
if and only if there is
f\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(Q)
,

\|f\|_{\infty}=1
, such that
f(r)=0
for all
r\in F
and

\langle\,f,\mu\,\rangle=\|\mu\|
.

Proposition 1

Let
(Q,*,e,\check{}\,)
be a locally compact hypergroup. Then the
maps defined by (
1
) have the
following properties:

(\widetilde{H}_{1})

the map
\tilde{\Delta}
is linear and
coassociative, that is,

(\tilde{\Delta}\times\mathrm{id})\circ\tilde{\Delta}=(\mathrm{id}\times\tilde{\Delta})\circ\tilde{\Delta};

(\widetilde{H}_{2})

(a)

the map
\tilde{\Delta}
is positive, that is,

\tilde{\Delta}(\mathscr{C}_{b,+})\subset\mathscr{C}_{b,+}(Q\times Q)
, where

\mathscr{C}_{b,+}(Q)

(
resp.,
\mathscr{C}_{b,+}(Q\times Q)
)

is the cone of bounded nonnegative continuous functions on
Q

(
resp.,
Q\times Q
)
,

(b)

\tilde{\Delta}\,1_{Q}=1_{Q\times Q}
,

(c)

for any
s,t\in Q
there is
f\in\mathscr{K}_{+}(Q)
such that
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
(\tilde{\Delta}\,f)(s,t)=1
;

(\widetilde{H}_{4})

if
F\subset Q
is closed,
(s_{0},t_{0})\in Q\times Q
and
f_{0}\in\mathscr{K}_{+}(Q)
is such that
\|f_{0}\|_{\infty}=1
,

f_{0}(r)=0
for all
r\in F
and
(\tilde{\Delta}\,f_{0})(s_{0},t_{0})=1
, then
there exist an open neighborhood
U\subset Q\times Q
of
(s_{0},t_{0})
and
f\in\mathscr{K}_{+}(Q)
such that

\|f\|_{\infty}=1
,
f(r)=0
for all
r\in F
and
(\tilde{\Delta}f)(s,t)=1

for all
(s,t)\in U
;

(\widetilde{H}_{5})

the homomorphism
\epsilon
is a right
and left counit, that is,

(\epsilon\times\mathrm{id})\circ\tilde{\Delta}=(\mathrm{id}\times\epsilon)\circ\tilde{\Delta}=\mathrm{id};

(2)

(\widetilde{H}_{6})

the homomorphism
\,\check{\,}
satisfies

\mathscr{K}(Q)\,\check{}\,\subset\mathscr{K}(Q)
and the following identities:

\begin{array}[]{c}\check{}\,\circ\,\check{}\,=\mathrm{id},\\[4.2679pt]
\tilde{\Delta}\circ\,\check{}\,=\Sigma\circ(\,\check{}\,\times\,\check{}\,)\circ\tilde{\Delta},\end{array}

(3)

where
\Sigma:\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q\times Q)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q\times Q)
is
defined by
(\Sigma f)(p,q)=f(q,p)
;

(\widetilde{H}_{7})

if
\Gamma\,\check{}\,\subset Q\times Q

is a graph of the homeomorphism
\,\check{}\,
, that is,

\Gamma\,\check{}\,=\{(p,\check{p})\in Q\times Q:p\in Q\},

then

\Gamma\,\check{}\,=\bigcap_{f\in\mathscr{K}_{+}^{e}(G)}\mathrm{supp}\,\bigl(\tilde{\Delta}(f)\bigr),

(4)

where
\mathscr{K}_{+}^{e}(G)=\{f\in\mathscr{K}_{+}(G):f(e)>0\}
.

Proof.

First of all note that
\tilde{\Delta}f\in\mathscr{C}(Q\times Q)
if
f\in\mathscr{C}(Q)
, as directly follows from (
H_{3}
). It also immediately
follows from (
H_{2}
) that
\tilde{\Delta}
is positive and
\tilde{\Delta}\,1_{Q}=1_{Q\times Q}
. To see that
\tilde{\Delta}\,f
is bounded for bounded

f\in\mathscr{C}(Q)
, we have that
-\|f\|_{\infty}\,1_{Q}\leq f\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\,1_{Q}
and, hence,

-\|f\|_{\infty}\,1_{Q\times Q}\leq\tilde{\Delta}\,f\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\,1_{Q\times Q},

which means that
\tilde{\Delta}\,f
is bounded. It is also clear that

\check{f}
is continuous if
f
is such, and that
\|\check{f}\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty}
. This means that the maps
in (
1
) are well defined.

To prove (
\widetilde{H}_{1}
), let
f\in\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)
and consider

\bigl((\tilde{\Delta}\times\mathrm{id})\circ\tilde{\Delta}\,f\bigr)(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=\langle\,\tilde{\Delta}\,f,(\varepsilon_{s_{1}}*\varepsilon_{s_{2}})\otimes\varepsilon_{s_{3}}\,\rangle=\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{s_{1}}*\varepsilon_{s_{2}}*\varepsilon_{s_{3}}\,\rangle.

Similarly,

\bigl((\mathrm{id}\times\tilde{\Delta})\circ\tilde{\Delta}\,f\bigr)(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=\langle\,\tilde{\Delta}\,f,\varepsilon_{s_{1}}\otimes(\varepsilon_{s_{2}}*\varepsilon_{s_{3}})\,\rangle=\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{s_{1}}*\varepsilon_{s_{2}}*\varepsilon_{s_{3}}\,\rangle.

It has already been mentioned, that all statements in (
\widetilde{H}_{2}
) except for (c) hold true. To prove (c) we use compactness of

in (
H_{2}
) a function

such that
f(p)=1
for all
p\in F
. Then

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Delta}\,f)(s,t)

\displaystyle=\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle=\int_{Q}f(p)\,d(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})(p)=\int_{F}f(p)\,d(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{F}d(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})(p)=\langle\,1_{Q},\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\,\tilde{\Delta}\,1_{Q},\varepsilon_{s}\otimes\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle=1_{Q\times Q}(s,t)=1.

Let us prove (
\widetilde{H}_{4}
). Denote

E_{0}=\mathrm{supp}\,(\varepsilon_{s_{0}}*\varepsilon_{t_{0}})
. If
F
is closed
and
f_{0}
is such that
\|f_{0}\|_{\infty}=1
,
f_{0}(r)=0
for all
r\in F
and
(\tilde{\Delta}\,f_{0})(s_{0},t_{0})=\langle\,f_{0},\varepsilon_{s_{0}}*\varepsilon_{t_{0}}\,\rangle=1
,
then it follows from Lemma
1
that

, hence
E_{0}
is a subset of the open set

Q\setminus F
. Let
V\subset Q
be an open neighborhood of
E_{0}

such that
\overline{V}\subset Q\setminus F
. By (
H_{4}
) there is an
open neighborhood

of
(s_{0},t_{0})
such that

\mathrm{supp}\,(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})\subset V
for all
(s,t)\in U
. Let
f\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(Q)
,

, be such that
f(r)=0

for all
r\in F
and
f(r)=1
for all
r\in\overline{V}
. Then, for
any
(s,t)\in U
, we have

(\tilde{\Delta}\,f)(s,t)=\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle=\langle\,1_{Q},\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle=1.

It is easy to see that (
\widetilde{H}_{5}
) holds. Indeed. let

and

. Then

\bigl((\epsilon\times\mathrm{id})\circ\tilde{\Delta}\,f\bigr)(s)=\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{e}*\varepsilon_{s}\,\rangle=\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{s}\,\rangle=f(s).

The second part of (
2
) is proved
similarly.

Property (
\widetilde{H}_{6}
) is a direct consequence of (
H_{6}
).

Let us prove (

Remark 1

It immediately follows from (

Proposition 2

Let
Q
be a Hausdorff locally compact space, and let a linear map

\tilde{\Delta}\colon\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q\times Q)
,

*
-homomorphisms
\,\check{}\,\colon\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)

and
\epsilon\colon\mathscr{C}_{b}(Q)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be given and satisfy
the properties
(
\widetilde{H}_{1}
)
–
(
\widetilde{H}_{7}
)
in
Proposition
1
. For
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
, define
\mu_{1}*\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
and
\check{\mu}\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\langle\,f,\mu_{1}*\mu_{2}\,\rangle&=&\displaystyle\int_{Q\times Q}(\tilde{\Delta}f)(s_{1},s_{2})\,d\mu_{1}(s_{1})d\mu_{2}(s_{2}),\\[11.38109pt]
\langle\,f,\check{\mu}\,\rangle&=&\langle\,\check{f},\mu\,\rangle,\par\end{array}

(5)

where
f\in\mathscr{K}(Q)
, and let
e=\mathrm{supp}\,(\epsilon)
. Then

(Q,*,e,\,\check{}\,)
is a locally compact hypergroup.

Proof.

First consider (
H_{1}
) and show that
(\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q),*)
is indeed an
associative algebra, if the convolution of measures is given by the
first formula in (
5
).

If
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
, then the measures
|\mu_{1}|
and

|\mu_{2}|
are also bounded. Moreover,
by (
5
) and using (
\widetilde{H}_{2}
), we have

\displaystyle|\langle\,f,\mu_{1}*\mu_{2}\,\rangle|

\displaystyle=\bigl|\int_{Q\times Q}(\tilde{\Delta}\,f)(s_{1},s_{2})\,d\mu_{1}(s_{1})d\mu_{2}(s_{2})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q\times Q}(\tilde{\Delta}\bigl|f|)(s_{1},s_{2})\,d|\mu_{1}|(s_{1})d|\mu_{2}|(s_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q\times Q}\tilde{\Delta}\bigl(\|f\|_{\infty}\,1_{Q}\bigr)(s_{1},s_{2})\,d|\mu_{1}|(s_{1})d|m_{2}|(s_{2})

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{Q\times Q}1_{Q}(s_{1})1_{Q}(s_{2})\,d|\mu_{1}|(s_{1})d|\mu_{2}|(s_{2})

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\infty}\,\|\mu_{1}\|\,\|\mu_{2}\|,

which shows that
\|\mu_{1}*\mu_{2}\|\leq\|\mu_{1}\|\,\|\mu_{2}\|
, hence

\mu_{1}*\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q)
.

Associativity of the algebra
(\mathscr{M}_{b}(Q),*)
is immediately implied
by (
\widetilde{H}_{1}
).

Consider (
H_{2}
). Since
\tilde{\Delta}
is positive by
(
\widetilde{H}_{2}
), the measure
\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}
is
nonnegative for
s,t\in Q
. Moreover,

\langle\,1_{Q},\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle=\langle\,1_{Q}\otimes 1_{Q},\varepsilon_{s}\otimes\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle=1,

hence
\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\in\mathscr{M}_{p}(Q)
.

Let us show that
\mathrm{supp}\,(\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t})
is compact for
any
s,t\in Q
. Indeed, using (
\widetilde{H}_{2}
) (c) there is
f\in\mathscr{K}_{+}(Q)
such that
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and
(\tilde{\Delta}\,f)(s,t)=1
. This means that

\langle\,f,\varepsilon_{s}*\varepsilon_{t}\,\rangle=1
. Denote
F=\mathrm{supp}\,(f)
and
let
g\in\mathscr{K}_{+}(Q)
be arbitrary such that
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and

\mathrm{supp}\,(g)\subset Q\setminus F
. Then
f+g\leq 1_{Q}
, that is,

Lemma 2

Let
\langle\,Pf,m\,\rangle=\langle\,f,m\,\rangle
for all
f\in\mathscr{K}(G)
. Then the
conditional expectation
P\colon\mathscr{K}(G)\rightarrow B
can be

and an
orthogonal projection on
L_{2}(G,m)
, still denoted by

.

Proof.

. Since
-|f|\leq f\leq|f|

. Hence,

\displaystyle\|P(f)\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}|P(f)|(p)\,dm(p)\leq\int_{G}P(|f|)(p)\,dm(p)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}|f|(p)\,dm(p)=\|f\|_{1},

which shows that

Polynomial identities that involve binomial coefficients, double and rising factorials and their probabilistic interpretations and proofs.
Paweł J. Szabłowski

Abstract
. We formulate several polynomial identities. One side of these identities has a nice simple form. Whereas the other has a form of a polynomial whose coefficients contain binomial coefficients double factorials or (and) rising factorials. The origins and the proofs of these identities are probabilistic. However, their form suggests universal applications in simplifying expressions. Many useful simplifying formulae are presented in the sequel.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 05A10, 05D40; Secondary 11B65, 11B73

1.
Introduction

In this note, we will present some identities involving factorials, binomial
coefficients, and the so-called rising factorials (sometimes called
Pochhammer symbols). Many of them have the forms of polynomials whose
coefficients often have the form of binomial coefficients and (or) of rising
factorials of some additional variables. These variables appear on both
sides of the identity. The domains of these variables can be extended to all
complex numbers. All these identities have a common origin, except for the
sometimes similar form. Namely, the origins of all are a few, rather deep
probabilistic interpretations and sometimes following non-trivial
computations.

The paper is organized as follows. The next section is dedicated to the
presentation of the identities and some of the particular, interesting
particular cases. The next section is devoted to the presentation of the
probabilistic background of the results presented in the previous section
and then, finally, the presentation of the calculations leading to the
identities.

Theorem 1
.

For all natural
k
and all complex
\rho
and
\beta
we have:

i)

(2.1)

\frac{(2k)!}{k!}(1+\rho)^{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{2k}{2j}(1+\rho)^{2k-2j}(1-\rho^{2})^{j}(2j-1)!!(2k-2j-1)!!.

ii)

(2.2)

(1+\rho)^{k}=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\sum_{m=0}^{\left\lfloor j/2\right\rfloor}(2\rho)^{j-2m}\binom{k}{j-2m}\binom{k-j+2m}{m}.

iii) For all integer

(2.3)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}\frac{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(j+m\right)}}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(j\right)}}=(-1)^{n}n!\binom{m}{n}\frac{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(m\right)}}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}.

iv)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left(-1\right)^{m}\binom{n}{m}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n-m\right)}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\binom{n-m}{k}\frac{\rho^{k}k!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(k\right)}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if}&n\text{ is odd}\\
\frac{n!}{(n/2)!}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n/2\right)}(1-\rho)^{n/2}&\text{if}&n\text{ is even}\end{array}\right..

v)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}\binom{n}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(1-\rho)^{j}\rho^{m-j}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n-j\right)}\left(\beta+m-j\right)^{\left(j\right)}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if}&n\text{ is odd}\\
\frac{n!}{(n/2)!}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n/2\right)}(1-\rho)^{n/2}&\text{if}&n\text{ is even}\end{array}\right..

Let us denote by

\left(\sqrt{5}-1\right)/2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k}{2j}(2j-1)!!(2k-2j-1)!!(L_{2k-2j}L_{j}-5F_{2k-2j}F_{j}),

\displaystyle 2\frac{\left(2k\right)!}{k!}F_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k}{2j}(2j-1)!!(2k-2j-1)!!(F_{2k-2j}L_{j}-L_{2k-2j}F_{j}).

We know that for all

(1+\phi)^{n}=((1+\sqrt{5})/2)^{n}=L_{n}/2+F_{n}\sqrt{5}/2.

Now notice also that we have
\phi\allowbreak=\allowbreak 1/(1+\phi)
and
that

L_{n}^{2}-5F_{n}^{2}=(-1)^{n}4.

Hence we have:

\phi^{n}=(-1)^{n}(L_{n}/2-F_{n}\sqrt{5}/2).

Now we take
\rho\allowbreak=\allowbreak-\phi
and insert it in (
2.1
). Notice that
1-\rho\allowbreak=\allowbreak(1+\phi)
, hence
(1-\rho)^{2k-2j}\allowbreak=\allowbreak L_{2k-2j}/2\allowbreak+\allowbreak F_{2k-2j}\sqrt{5}/2
and
1-\rho^{2}\allowbreak=\allowbreak\phi
,
consequently
(1-\rho^{2})^{j}\allowbreak=\allowbreak(-1)^{j}(L_{j}/2\allowbreak-\allowbreak F_{j}\sqrt{5}/2)
. Now it remains
to perform multiplication and separation of terms with and without
\sqrt{5}
.

3.
Probabilistic background and the proofs

3.1.
Probabilistic background

All identities presented in Theorem
1
stem from calculating the
following moments
E(X-Y)^{n},

n\geq 0,
where
X
and
Y
are two
normalized random variables. The joint distribution of these random
variables has one parameter and is either bivariate Normal (Gaussian) or
bivariate Gamma distribution. More precisely, in the first case, the joint
distribution of
(X,Y)
has the following well-known density, valid for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\left|\rho\right|<1:

(3.1)

f_{N}(x,y|\rho)=\frac{1}{2\pi(1-\rho^{2})}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}-2\rho xy+y^{2}}{2(1-\rho^{2})}\right),

while in the second case valid for all
x,y,\beta>0
and
\left|\rho\right|<1:

f_{G}(x,y|\rho)=f_{g}(x|\beta)f_{g}(y|\beta)\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\rho\left(x+y\right)}{1-\rho}\right)}{\left(1-\rho\right)\left(xy\rho\right)^{(\beta-1)/2}}I_{\beta-1}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{xy\rho}}{1-\rho}\right),

where

f_{g}(x|\beta)\allowbreak=\allowbreak x^{\beta-1}\exp(-x)/\Gamma(\beta),

for
x>0
and
0
otherwise.
I_{\alpha}
denotes the modified Bessel
function of the first kind. The most important property of these joint
densities is that they allow the so-called Lancaster expansions. To learn
more about Lancaster expansions, their probabilistic interpretations and
convergence problems associated with them, see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
. The orthogonal polynomials that we are using are well presented,
for example, in two well-known monographs
[
5
]
, and
[
13
]
.

Namely, in the first case, we have the so-called Poisson-Mehler expansion

(3.2)

f_{N}(x,y|\rho)=f_{N}(x)f_{N}(x)\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho^{n}h_{n}(x)h_{n}(y),

where we denoted for simplicity

f_{N}(x)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\exp(-x^{2}/2)/\sqrt{2\pi},

and

h_{n}(x)\allowbreak=\allowbreak H_{n}(x)/\sqrt{n!}.

h_{n}(x)
is the orthonormal modification of the so-called probabilistic
Hermite polynomials
\left\{H_{n}(x)\right\}
, i.e. polynomials defined by
the following three-term recurrence :

H_{n+1}(x)\allowbreak=\allowbreak xH_{n}(x)-nH_{n-1}(x),

with
H_{-1}(x)\allowbreak=\allowbreak 0
and
H_{0}(x)\allowbreak=\allowbreak 1.
It is also known, that

(3.3)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h_{n}(x)h_{m}(x)\exp(-x^{2}/2)dx=\delta_{nm},

where
\delta_{nm}
denotes Kronecker’s delta.

In the second case we have

(3.4)

f_{G}(x,y|\rho)=f_{g}(x|\beta)f_{g}(y|\beta)\sum_{j\geq 0}\rho^{n}l_{n}(x|\beta)l_{n}(y|\beta),

where
l_{n}(x|\beta)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}L_{n}(x|\beta)
is the orthonormal
modification of the so-called generalized Laguerre polynomials
\left\{L_{n}(x|\beta)\right\}
, defined by the following expansions:

(3.5)

L_{n}(x|\beta)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{(\beta)^{(n)}}{(n-k)!(\beta)^{(k)}}x^{k}/k!.

Let us remark that the expansion (
3.4
) is known under the name
Hardy-Hille (see, e.g.
[
13
]
, p.102) formula. One knows that :

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{\infty}l_{n}(x|\beta)l_{m}(x|\beta)f_{g}(x|\beta)dx=\delta_{n,m}.

Our aim is to calculate
\left\{E(X-Y)^{n}\right\}_{n\geq 0}.
We will do
it in two ways.

The first way is to calculate the generating function of the set of these
numbers i.e. the function

g(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{n}E(X-Y)^{n}/n!.

It can be calculated in the following way since it is known that all moments
exist, hence one can exchange integration and summation. Namely, we have

g(t)=\int\int\exp\left(tx-ty\right)f(x,y|\rho)dxdy.

Obviously here and below the integration is depending on the case either
over whole
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or over
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
The second way is to calculate the number using an expansion:

E(-X+Y)^{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}EX^{m}Y^{n-m}.

Now

EX^{m}Y^{n-m}=\int\int x^{m}y^{n-m}f(x,y|\rho)dxdy.

where
f
is either
f_{N}
or
f_{G}
presented above. Using one of the
expansions (
3.2
) or (
3.4
) we will find these moments using the
numbers

H_{m,j}=EX^{m}k_{j}(X),

where
k_{i}(x)
is either
h_{j}(x)
if we consider the Normal case or
l_{j}(x)
if we consider Gamma case. Since both expansions (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) have similar structure we have:

(3.7)

EX^{m}Y^{n-m}=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,n-m)}\rho^{j}H_{m,j}H_{n-m,j}.

This is so since
X
and
Y
have the same distributions and since
H_{m,j}\allowbreak=\allowbreak 0
if
j>m
because of the orthogonality of
polynomials
h
or
l.
We will calculate this function assuming either
expansion (
3.2
) or (
3.4
).

Case 1
.

So let us start with expansion (
3.2
). First let us
calculate the auxiliary numbers
H_{j,n}
. We have:

(3.8)

H_{j,n}=EX^{j}h_{n}(X)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}EX^{j}H_{n}(X)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if }&n>j\text{ or }j-n\text{ odd}\\
\frac{j!}{2^{(j-n)/2}((j-n)/2)!\sqrt{n!}}&\text{if}&j-n\text{ is even}\end{array}\text{.}\right.

This is so since polynomials
h_{n}
are orthogonal and also since it is
common knowledge, that
\forall n\geq 0:

x^{j}\allowbreak=\allowbreak j!\sum_{m=0}^{\left\lfloor j/2\right\rfloor}\frac{1}{2^{m}m!(j-2m)!}H_{j-2m}(x).

Secondly, let us calculate the following auxiliary function that will
simplify many further calculations.

\displaystyle m_{n}(t)\allowbreak

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\allowbreak Eh_{n}(X)\exp(tX)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n!}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_{n}(x)\exp(tx)\exp(-x^{2}/2)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\exp(t^{2}/2)}{\sqrt{2\pi n!}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_{n}(x)\exp(-(x-t)^{2}/2)dx.

Now, let us change the variable under the integral by setting
y\allowbreak=\allowbreak(x-t).
Then, we get:

m_{n}(t)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\frac{\exp(t^{2}/2)}{\sqrt{2\pi n!}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}H_{n}(y+t)\exp(-y^{2}/2)dy.

Next, we utilize the following the well-known expansion

H_{n}(x+y)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}H_{j}(x)y^{n-j}.

Now, since we have (
3.3
), we see that
m_{n}(t)\allowbreak=\allowbreak t^{n}\frac{\exp(t^{2}/2)}{\sqrt{n!}}
and we get:

\displaystyle E\exp\left(tX-tY\right)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\sum_{n\geq 0}E\exp\left(tX-tY\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(tx-ty\right)\exp\left(-\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)/2\right)h_{n}(x)h_{n}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho^{n}m_{n}(t)m_{n}(-t)=\exp(t^{2})\sum_{n\geq 0}(-\rho)^{n}t^{2n}/n!=\exp(t^{2}(1-\rho))

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{2n}(1-\rho)^{n}/n!.

Thus, we deduce that:

(3.9)

E(X-Y)^{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if }&n\text{ is odd}\\
\frac{n!}{(n/2)!}(1-\rho)^{n/2}&\text{if }&n\text{ is even}\end{array}\right..

Case 2
.

Now let us consider expansion (
3.4
). We need to recall
some simple, well-known facts: The Gamma distribution with rate parameter
zero and shape parameter
\beta>0,
is the distribution with the density
f_{g}
presented above. It is easy to see, recalling the definition of the
Euler’s Gamma function that:

(3.10)

EX^{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}f_{g}(x|\beta)dx=\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}.

As before, let us calculate the set of auxiliary quantities and functions:
We start with the numbers:

H_{j,n}=EX^{j}l_{n}(X).

We have:

(3.11)

H_{j,n}=\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{\Gamma(\beta)\sqrt{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{j}L_{n}^{(\beta)}(x)x^{\beta-1}\exp(-x)dx.

Now, we use the well-known expansion of
x^{j}
in powers of Laguerre
polynomials:

(3.12)

x^{j}\allowbreak=\allowbreak j!\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\frac{(\beta)^{(j)}}{(j-k)!(\beta)^{(k)}}L_{k}^{(\beta)}(x),

and then we use the orthogonality of the Laguerre polynomials. Hence we have:

(3.13)

H_{j,n}=(-1)^{n}\binom{j}{n}(\beta)^{(j)}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}.

Let us calculate now, the following auxiliary functions:

\displaystyle m_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle El_{n}(X)\exp(tX)=\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}E\exp(tX)L_{n}(X|\beta)\allowbreak

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\allowbreak\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(xt)L_{n}(x|\beta)x^{\beta-1}\exp(-x)dx\allowbreak

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\allowbreak\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{n}(x|\beta)x^{\beta-1}\exp(-x(1-t))dx\allowbreak.

Now, let us change variables under the integral by considering
y\allowbreak=\allowbreak x(1-t).
Then we get:

m_{n}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\frac{1}{1-t}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{n}(\frac{y}{1-t}|\beta)\left(\frac{y}{1-t}\right)^{\beta-1}\exp(-y)dy\allowbreak.

We will now utilize the following well-known recurrence relation for
Laguerre polynomials (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
) :

L_{n}(y|\beta)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}L_{n-j}(x|\beta+j)(y-x)^{j}/j!.

So we get:

\displaystyle m_{n}(t)=\allowbreak\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\beta}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\times

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\frac{-ty}{1-t}\right)^{j}L_{n-j}(x|(\beta+j))/j!\right)y^{\beta-1}\exp(-y)dy.

Further, we have:

\displaystyle m_{n}(t)=\allowbreak\allowbreak\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\beta}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{(1-t)^{j}}L_{n-j}(y\left|\left(\beta+j\right)\right|)y^{j+\beta-1}\exp(-y)dy\allowbreak

\displaystyle=\allowbreak(-1)^{n}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\frac{t^{n}\Gamma(\beta+n)}{\Gamma(\beta)n!(1-t)^{\beta+n}}\allowbreak=\allowbreak(-1)^{n}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}\frac{t^{n}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}{n!(1-t)^{\beta+n}}.\allowbreak

Hence we have now:

\displaystyle\allowbreak g(t)=E\exp(tX-tY)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(tx-ty)l_{n}(x|\beta)l_{n}(y|\beta)f_{g}(x|\beta)f_{g}(y|\beta)dxdy\allowbreak\allowbreak

\displaystyle=\allowbreak\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho^{n}m_{n}(t)m_{n}(-t)=\allowbreak\allowbreak\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho^{n}\frac{n!(-1)^{n}t^{2n}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}n!n!(1-t^{2})^{n+\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{\beta}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\rho^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}t^{2n}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}{n!(1-t^{2})^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{\beta}}\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{\rho t^{2}}{1-t^{2}}\right)^{n}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}/n!,

and further we have:

\displaystyle g(t)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\frac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{\beta}}\left(1+\frac{\rho t^{2}}{1-t^{2}}\right)^{-\beta}\allowbreak=

\displaystyle=\allowbreak\frac{1}{(1-t^{2}(1-\rho))^{\beta}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{2n}(1-\rho)^{n}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}/n!.

We use here twice the well-known formula for the so-called binomial series.
Thus we deduce

(3.14)

E(X-Y)^{n}\allowbreak=\allowbreak\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if }&n\text{ is odd}\\
\frac{n!}{(n/2)!}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n/2\right)}(1-\rho)^{n/2}&\text{if }&n\text{ is even}\end{array}\right..

3.2.
Proofs

All proofs are based on considering different ways of calculating numbers
\left\{E(X-Y)^{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
when assuming one of the discussed
above joint distributions.

Proof of assertion i) of Theorem
1
:

Let
(X,Y)
have bivariate Gaussian distribution given by (
3.1
). Then
it is well known that every linear transformation of such vriable has also
gaussin distribution. In particular we deduce that
X-Y\sim N(0,(1-\rho)^{2}).
hence, keeping in mind that
EZ^{j}\allowbreak=\allowbreak\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if}&j\text{ is odd}\\
1&\text{if}&j=0\\
\sigma^{j}(2j-1)!!&\text{if}&j\text{ is even}\end{array}\right.
if only
Z\sim N(0,\sigma^{2})
. Thus the left hand side of
assertion i) is equal to
(2k-1)!!(1-\rho)^{k}\allowbreak=\allowbreak\frac{(2k)!}{k!}(1-\rho)^{k}.
Now it is enough to change
\rho
to
-\rho
.

In order to get right hand side we calculate:

\displaystyle E(X-Y)^{2k}\allowbreak=\allowbreak E(X+\rho Y-(1+\rho)Y)^{2k}\allowbreak=

\displaystyle\allowbreak\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\binom{2k}{j}(-1)^{j}(1+\rho)^{2k-j}E(X+\rho Y)^{j}Y^{2k-j}\allowbreak=

\displaystyle\allowbreak\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\binom{2k}{j}(-1)^{j}(1+\rho)^{2k-j}E(E(X+\rho Y)^{j}|Y)Y^{2k-j}\allowbreak.

Now, we use the fact know that random variables
(X+\rho Y)
and
Y
are
independent, hence
(E(X+\rho Y)^{j}|Y)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if}&j\text{ is odd}\\
1&\text{if}&j=0\\
(2j-1)!!(1-\rho^{2})^{j/2}&\text{if}&j\text{ is even}\end{array}\right..
Thus we get

E(X-Y)^{2k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{2k}{2j}(1-\rho)^{2k-2j}(1-\rho^{2})^{j}(2j-1)!!(2k-2j-1)!!.

Proof of assertion ii) of Theorem
1
:

We use (
3.7
) with (
3.8
) getting:

\frac{(2k)!}{k!}(1-\rho)^{k}=2\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{2k}{j}\sum_{n=0}^{j}\frac{\rho^{n}}{n!}H_{j,n}H_{2k-j,n}+(-1)^{k}\binom{2k}{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{\rho^{n}}{n!}H_{k,n}^{2},

where
H_{j,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if }&n>j\text{ or }j-n\text{ odd}\\
\frac{j!}{2^{(j-n)/2}((j-n)/2)!}&\text{if}&j-n\text{ is even}\end{array}\text{.}\right.
Notice that if
j-n
is even then
\binom{2k}{j}H_{j,n}H_{2k-j,n}\allowbreak=\allowbreak 2^{n}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{k}{n}\binom{k-n}{(j-n)/2}/2^{k}
and when
k-n
is even
H_{k,n}^{2}\allowbreak=\allowbreak 2^{n}\binom{k}{n}\binom{k-n}{(k-n)/2}/2^{k},
hence (
2.2
)
can be reformulated in the following way:

\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\sum_{n=0-}^{j}(2\rho)^{n}\binom{k}{n}mh_{j,n,k}+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{k}}\sum_{n=0}^{k}(2\rho)^{n}\binom{k}{n}mh_{k,n,k},=(1-\rho)^{k},

where
mh_{j,n,k}\allowbreak=\allowbreak\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if}&j>k-1\text{ or }j-n\text{ is odd}\\
\binom{k-n}{(j-n)/2}&\text{if }&j-n\text{ is even}\end{array}.\right.
Now it remains to change the order of summation in the internal
sums and
\rho
to
-\rho
.

Proof of assertion iii) of Theorem
1
:

Let us recall (
3.5
) and (
3.11
) and let us calculate
H_{j,n}

directly, getting:

\displaystyle H_{j,n}=\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{\Gamma(\beta)\sqrt{\Gamma(n+\beta)}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{(\beta)^{(n)}}{(n-k)!(\beta)^{(k)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{k+j}x^{\beta-1}\exp(-x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n!}}{\sqrt{\Gamma(n+\beta)}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\frac{(\beta)^{(n)}(\beta)^{(k+j)}}{(n-k)!(\beta)^{(k)}}.

Now, we compare it with the calculated already
H_{j,n}
for Gamma
distribution i.e.,
(-1)^{n}\binom{j}{n}(\beta)^{(j)}\sqrt{\frac{n!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}}}.

Proof of assertion iv) of Theorem
1
:

Recall (
3.13
). We have:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if }&n\text{ is odd}\\
\frac{n!}{(n/2)!}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n/2\right)}(1-\rho)^{n/2}&\text{if }&n\text{ is even}\end{array}\right.=E(-X+Y)^{n}\allowbreak

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\binom{n}{m}EX^{m}Y^{n-m}.\allowbreak

Now we use (
3.7
) with
H_{j,m}
given by (
3.13
). By (
3.14
) we have

\displaystyle E(-X+Y)^{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\binom{n}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,n-m)}\rho^{j}H_{m,j}H_{n-m,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}\binom{n}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,n-m)}\rho^{j}\binom{m}{j}\binom{n-m}{j}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n-m\right)}\frac{j!}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(j\right)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-m}\binom{n}{m}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(m\right)}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n-m\right)}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\binom{n-m}{j}\frac{j!\rho^{j}}{\left(\beta\right)^{\left(j\right)}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\text{if }&n\text{ is odd}\\
\frac{n!}{(n/2)!}\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n/2\right)}(1-\rho)^{n/2}&\text{if }&n\text{ is even}\end{array}\right..

This is so, since
\binom{m}{j}\binom{n-m}{j}
is zero whenever
m>j
or
n-m>j.

Proof of assertion iv) of Theorem
1
:

One can easily notice, basing on (
3.4
), that
E(l_{n}(X)|Y)\allowbreak=\allowbreak\rho^{n}l_{n}(Y).
Let us hence calculate first, the
conditional moments
\eta_{n}(y|\beta,\rho)\allowbreak=\allowbreak E(X^{n}|Y=y)
. We have by (
3.12
)

\displaystyle\eta_{j}(y|\beta,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(X^{j}|Y=y)\allowbreak=\allowbreak j!\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\frac{(\beta)^{(j)}}{(j-k)!(\beta)^{(k)}}\rho^{k}L_{k}^{(\beta)}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j!\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\frac{(\beta)^{(j)}}{(j-k)!(\beta)^{(k)}}\rho^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\frac{(\beta)^{(k)}}{(k-m)!(\beta)^{(m)}}y^{m}/m!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j!\sum_{m=0}^{j}\frac{(\beta)^{(j)}}{(\beta)^{(m)}(j-m)!m!}(\rho y)^{m}\sum_{k=m}^{j}(-1)^{k-m}\frac{(j-m)!}{(k-m)!(j-k)!}\rho^{k-m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{j}{m}\frac{(\beta)^{(j)}}{(\beta)^{(m)}}(\rho y)^{m}(1-\rho)^{j-m}=j!(1-\rho)^{j}L_{j}(-\frac{\rho y}{1-\rho}).

Further we get:

\displaystyle E\left((X-Y)^{n}|Y=y\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}\eta_{j}(y|\beta,\rho)y^{n-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}y^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{j}{m}\frac{(\beta)^{(j)}}{(\beta)^{(m)}}(\rho y)^{m}(1-\rho)^{j-m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{m!(n-m)!}\frac{(\rho y)^{m}}{(\beta)^{(m)}}\sum_{j=m}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{(n-m)!(\beta)^{(j)}y^{n-j}(1-\rho)^{j-m}}{(n-j)!(j-m)!}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{n!}{m!(n-m)!}(\rho y)^{m}\sum_{s=0}^{n-m}(-1)^{n-m-s}\binom{n-m}{s}y^{n-m-s}(1-\rho)^{s}(\beta+m)^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-t}y^{n-t}\frac{n!}{t!(n-t)!}\sum_{s=0}^{t}\binom{t}{s}(\rho y)^{t-s}(1-\rho)^{s}(\beta+t-s)^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}y^{n-s}(1-\rho)^{s}\sum_{t=s}^{n}(-1)^{n-t}\frac{(n-s)!}{(t-s)!t!}\rho^{t-s}(\beta+t-s)^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}y^{n-s}(1-\rho)^{s}\sum_{m=0}^{n-s}(-1)^{n-m-s}\binom{n-s}{m}\rho^{m}(\beta+m)^{(s)}.

Hence we get using (
3.10
) we have;

\displaystyle E(X-Y)^{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}y^{n-s}(1-\rho)^{s}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n-s}(-1)^{n-m-s}\binom{n-s}{m}\rho^{m}(\beta+m)^{(s)})f_{g}(x|\beta)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}(\beta)^{(n-s)}(1-\rho)^{s}\sum_{m=0}^{n-s}(-1)^{n-m-s}\binom{n-s}{m}\rho^{m}(\beta+m)^{(s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-t}\frac{n!(\beta)^{(n-t)}}{t!(n-t)!}\sum_{s=0}^{t}\frac{t!}{s!(t-s)!}(1-\rho)^{s}\rho^{t-s}(\beta+n-t)^{(t-s)}(\beta+t-s)^{(s)}.

In the last line, we have changed the order of summation. To get the
assertion we use the formula:

\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n\right)}\left(\beta+n\right)^{\left(m\right)}=\left(\beta\right)^{\left(n+m\right)},

true for all complex
\beta
.

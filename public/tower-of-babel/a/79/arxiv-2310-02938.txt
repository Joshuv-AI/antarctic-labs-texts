Some universal inequalities of eigenvalues and upper bounds for the L ∞ L_{\infty} norm of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian
Kei Funano

Abstract
. In this short survey, we derive some weyl-type universal inequalities of eigenvalues of the Laplacian on a closed Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature. We also give upper bounds for the L ∞ L_{\infty} norm of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian in the same setting. A detailed proof of these results did not seem to appear in the literature but the results follow from a simple combination of Milman’s work and Cheng-Li’s work.

1.
Introduction

For a closed Riemannian manifold
M
let
\lambda_{k}(M)
be the
k
-th nonzero eigenvalue of the Laplacian.

One of the goal of this survey is to give a precise proof of the following theorem: For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\lesssim b
stands for
a\leq Cb
for some universal (and numerical) constant
C>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then for any
k\geq 1
we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(M)\gtrsim k^{\frac{2}{n}}\lambda_{1}(M).

Note that the order of
k
in (
1.1
) is sharp by the Weyl asymptotic law. Concerning the above inequality (
1.1
) let us focus on the following two estimates for eigenvalues of the Laplacian on a closed Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature:

(1)

\lambda_{k}(M)\geq\frac{c_{n}k^{\frac{2}{n}}}{(\operatorname{Diam}M)^{2}}
, where
c_{n}>0
is a constant depending only on
n
(Gromov
[
9
,
10
]
. Refer also to
[
18
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
11
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
).

(2)

\lambda_{k}(M)\lesssim\frac{n^{2}k^{2}}{(\operatorname{Diam}M)^{2}}
(Cheng
[
3
, Corollary 2.2]
).

Combining the above two estimates we can obtain

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(M)\gtrsim c_{n}k^{\frac{2}{n}}\lambda_{1}(M)

in the same setting. Since we do not need the dimensional term
c_{n}
in (
1.1
), the inequality (
1.1
) is better than the inequality (
1.2
).

The opposite of (
1.1
) was obtained by Liu in
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
. Under the same assumption of Theorem
1.1
Liu showed

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(M)\lesssim k^{2}\lambda_{1}(M),

which is sharp with respect to the order of
k
.

Throughout this survey
\mu
denotes the uniform probability measure on
M
, i.e.,
\mu(\cdot):=\operatorname{vol}(\cdot)/\operatorname{vol}(M)
. For a measurable function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
and
p>0
we put

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}:=\Big(\int_{M}|f(x)|^{p}d\mu(x)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\infty}:=\esssup_{x\in M}|f(x)|.

We also give the proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then for any
k\geq 1
and any eigenfunction
\varphi_{k}
of the Laplacian corresponding to
\lambda_{k}(M)
we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\Big(\frac{c\lambda_{k}(M)}{\lambda_{1}(M)}\Big)^{\frac{n}{4}}\|\varphi_{k}\|_{2}.

for some universal constant
c>0
. In particular we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}\|_{\infty}\lesssim(ck)^{\frac{n}{2}}\|\varphi_{k}\|_{2}.

The inequality (
1.5
) follows from (
1.4
) together with (
1.3
).

A classical result by Hölmander
[
12
]
(see also
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
20
, Corollary 8.6]
) states that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq C\lambda_{k}(M)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\|\varphi_{k}\|_{2}

for any closed Riemannian manifold, where the constant
C=C(M,g)
depends on
(M,g)
. This inequality is sharp and better than (
1.4
) when
\lambda_{k}(M)
is large. In the case where
\lambda_{k}(M)<<\lambda_{1}(M)^{n}
the inequality (
1.4
) is better than the inequaity (
1.6
).

The proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
follows from a simple combination of Milman’s result
[
16
]
and Cheng-Li’s result
[
4
]
. In fact under the nonnegativity of Ricci curvature, Milman proved the Sobolev inequality in terms of the first nonzero eigenvalue and Cheng-Li proved lower bounds of eigenvalues of the Laplacian in terms of the Sobolev constant. As a byproduct of the proof of Theorem
1.1
we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
hold for a convex domain in a complete Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature. In that case we impose the Neumann boundary condition.

2.
Proof

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold and
\mu
be the uniform probability measure. For the isoperimetric profile
I=I_{(M,\mu)}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
(Refer to
[
16
]
for the definition) we put
\tilde{I}(t):=\min\{I(t),I(1-t)\}
,
t\in[0,1/2]
.

Theorem 2.1

(E. Milman,
[
16
, Remark 2.11]
)
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then we have the isoperimetric inequality

\displaystyle\tilde{I}(t)\gtrsim\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(M)}t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\ \ \forall t\in[0,1/2].

In
[
16
, Remark 2.11]
Milman stated that

\displaystyle\tilde{I}(t)\gtrsim D_{1,\infty}t^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\ \ \forall t\in[0,1/2],

where
D_{1,\infty}
is the best constant that satisfies the
(1,\infty)
-Poincaré inequality. Since
D_{1,\infty}
is equivalent to
\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(M)}
by Milman’s result (
[
16
, Theorem 2.4]
), we have Theorem
2.1
.

For a measurable function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
we define a
median
of
f
as a number
a
such that

\displaystyle\mu(f(x)\geq a)\geq\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }\mu(f(x)\leq a)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Note that
a
might not be unique. Let
M_{\mu}f
denote one of medians of
f
.
We put

\displaystyle E_{\mu}f:=\int_{M}f(x)d\mu

whenever the right-hand side makes sense.

One can transfer the isoperimetric inequality appeared in Theorem
2.1
to a
(1,\frac{n}{n-1})
-Poincaré inequality via the following theorem. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the space of Lipschitz functions on
M
.

Theorem 2.2

(Bobkov–Houdré
[
2
]
, Federer–Fleming
[
6
]
, Maz’ya
[
15
]
)
.

Let
0<r\leq 1
. Without any assumption on
M
the
(1/r,1)
-Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle D\|f-M_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{1}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}

is equivalent to the isoperimetric inequality

\displaystyle\tilde{I}(t)\geq Dt^{r}\ \ \forall t\in[0,1/2].

See
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
for the proof of the above theorem.

Theorem
2.1
together with Theorem
2.2
yields the following.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then we have the
(\frac{n}{n-1},1)
-Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(M)}\|f-M_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\lesssim\||\nabla f|\|_{1}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

From now on we shall study Poincaré inequalities.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
17
, Section 1.1.3]
)
.

Suppose that the
(\frac{n}{n-1},1)
-Poincaré inequality

(2.1)

\displaystyle D\|f-M_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{1}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}

holds for
M
. Then for any
p\geq 1
we have the
(\frac{np}{n-p},p)
-Poincaré inequality

(2.2)

\displaystyle D\|f-M_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{np}{n-p}}\leq\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p}\||\nabla f|\|_{p}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

In
[
17
, Section 1.1.3]
the above lemma is stated for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In there the Poincaré inequalities corresponding to (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are stated for compactly supported smooth functions and the left-hand sides of (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) in there are the norms of
f
, not the norms of
f-M_{\mu}f
.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Assume (
2.1
) and take
f\in\mathcal{F}
. We may assume that
M_{\mu}f=0
. Setting
\alpha:=\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p}
we find
\alpha>1
.
For any
x\in M
define

\displaystyle g(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|f(x)|^{\alpha}&\text{ if }f(x)\geq 0\\
-|f(x)|^{\alpha}&\text{ if }f(x)<0.\end{array}\right.

Observe that
g\in\mathcal{F}
and
M_{\mu}g=0
. Thus (
2.1
) yields that

\displaystyle D\|f\|^{\alpha}_{\frac{\alpha n}{n-1}}=D\|g\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\alpha\int|f(x)|^{\alpha-1}|\nabla f(x)|d\mu(x).

Let
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate of
p
, i.e.,
p^{-1}+p^{\prime-1}=1
. Using the Hölder inequality we obtain

\displaystyle D\|f\|_{\frac{np}{n-p}}^{\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p}}=D\|f\|^{\alpha}_{\frac{\alpha n}{n-1}}\leq\alpha\||f|^{\alpha-1}\|_{p^{\prime}}\||\nabla f|\|_{p}=\alpha\|f\|_{\frac{np}{n-p}}^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p}}\||\nabla f|\|_{p},

which gives (
2.2
). This completes the proof.

\Box

To connect the
L_{p}
norm of
f-E_{\mu}f
with
f-M_{\mu}f
we use the following.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

For any
p\geq 1
and
f\in\mathcal{F}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{p}\leq\|f-M_{\mu}f\|_{p}\leq 3\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{p}.

Lemma
2.4
together with Lemma
2.5
gives the following.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
17
, Section 1.1.3]
)
.

Suppose that the
(\frac{n}{n-1},1)
-Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle D\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{n}{n-1}}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{1}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}

holds for
M
. Then for any
p\geq 1
we have the
(\frac{np}{n-p},p)
-Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle D\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{np}{n-p}}\lesssim\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p}\||\nabla f|\|_{p}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

Combining Corollary
2.3
with Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
we obtain the following.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold of nonnegative Ricci curvature. Then for any
p\geq 1
we have the
(\frac{np}{n-p},p)
-Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(M)}\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{np}{n-p}}\lesssim\frac{(n-1)p}{n-p}\||\nabla f|\|_{p}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

The Hölder inequality implies the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that we have the
(\frac{2n}{n-2},2)
-Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle D\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{2}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

Then we have

\displaystyle D\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{2}^{\frac{2+n}{n}}\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{1}^{-\frac{2}{n}}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{2}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

Theorem 2.9

(Cheng-Li,
[
4
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian manifold. Assume that

(2.3)

\displaystyle D\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{2}^{\frac{2+n}{n}}\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{1}^{-\frac{2}{n}}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{2}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}

holds. Then we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(M)\gtrsim k^{\frac{2}{n}}D^{2}

for any
k
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The case where
n=2
follows from (
1.2
) and hence we assume
n\geq 3
.

Since
n\geq 3
we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(M)}\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\lesssim\||\nabla f|\|_{2}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

by Corollary
2.7
. From Lemma
2.8
we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(M)}\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{2}^{\frac{2+n}{n}}\|f-E_{\mu}f\|_{1}^{-\frac{2}{n}}\lesssim\||\nabla f|\|_{2}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}.

Theorem
2.9
thereby implies the theorem. This completes the proof.

\Box

For the completeness of this paper we give the proof of Theorem
2.9
following
[
4
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.9
.

Let
H(x,y,t)
denotes the fundamental solution of the heat equation, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}(x,y,t)=\Delta_{x}H(x,y,t)

and

\displaystyle\int_{M}H(x,y,t)f(y)d\mu(y)\to f(x)\text{ as }t\to 0

for any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
(
[
9
,
10
]
). We define

G:M\times M\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as
G(x,y,t):=H(x,y,t)-1
. Denote by
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
be an orthnormal basis of eigenfunctions of the Laplacian with respect to
\mu
, where
\phi_{i}
corresponds to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(M)
. Since
\phi_{0}=1
we have an expansion

\displaystyle G(x,y,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{i}(M)t}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y).

Note also that the semi-group property

\displaystyle G(x,y,t+s)=\int_{M}G(x,z,s)G(z,y,t)d\mu(z)\ \ \forall x,y\in M\ \forall s,t\geq 0

holds. We thereby have

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(x,x,t)=\

\displaystyle\int_{M}G^{\prime}(x,y,t/2)G(x,y,t/2)d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Delta_{y}G(x,y,t/2)G(x,y,t/2)d\mu(y).

Since

\displaystyle\int_{M}|G(x,y,t)|d\mu(y)\leq\int_{M}|H(x,y,t)|d\mu(y)+1=2,

the assumption (
2.3
) and the semi-group property give

\displaystyle-G^{\prime}(x,x,t)=\

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y,t/2)|^{2}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\geq\

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{4}{n}}D^{2}\Big(\int_{M}G(x,y,t/2)^{2}d\mu(y)\Big)^{\frac{2+n}{n}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{4}{n}}D^{2}G(x,x,t)^{\frac{2+n}{n}}.

Since
G(x,x,t)=H(x,x,t)-1\to\infty
as
t\to 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}G(x,x,t)^{-\frac{2}{n}}\geq 2^{-\frac{4}{n}}D^{2}t

It gives

(2.4)

\displaystyle G(x,x,t)\leq 4\Big(\frac{2D^{2}}{n}\Big)^{-\frac{n}{2}}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

Integrating both-sides yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{i}(M)t}\leq 4\Big(\frac{2D^{2}}{n}\Big)^{-\frac{n}{2}}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

Putting
t:=n/(2\lambda_{k}(M))
we therefore obtain

\displaystyle ke^{-\frac{n}{2}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{i}(M)}{2\lambda_{k}(M)}n}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{i}(M)}{2\lambda_{k}(M)}n}\leq 4\Big(\frac{2\lambda_{k}(M)}{D^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}},

which implies the conclusion of the theorem. This completes the proof.

\Box

]
instead of putting
t=n/(2\lambda_{k}(M))

t=1/(2\lambda_{k}(M))

\lambda_{k}(M)\gtrsim c_{n}k^{\frac{2}{n}}D^{2}
, where

is a constant depending only on

. In

it is putted

but the assumption there is different. The assumption there seems the
(\frac{2n}{n-2},2)
-Poincaré inequality

\displaystyle D\|f\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq\||\nabla f|\|_{2}\ \ \forall f\in\mathcal{F}

to derive the lower bound of
\lambda_{k}(M)
.

We also give the proof of Theorem
1.2
following
[

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary
2.7
, Lemma
2.8
and (
2.4
) yield

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda_{k}(M)t}\phi_{k}(x)^{2}\leq 4\Big(\frac{c\lambda_{1}(M)}{n}\Big)^{-\frac{n}{2}}t^{-\frac{n}{2}},

where

). This completes the proof.

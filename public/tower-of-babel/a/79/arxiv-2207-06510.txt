Long Time Behavior of Solutions of an Electroconvection Model in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Elie Abdo, Mihaela Ignatova

Abstract
. We consider a two dimensional electroconvection model which consists of a nonlinear and nonlocal system coupling the evolutions of a charge distribution and a fluid. We show that the solutions decay in time in L 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}) at the same sharp rate as the linear uncoupled system. This is achieved by proving that the difference between the nonlinear and linear evolution decays at a faster rate than the linear evolution. In order to prove the sharp L 2 L^{2} decay we establish bounds for decay in H 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}) and a logarithmic growth in time of a quadratic moment of the charge density.



1.
Introduction

We consider the electroconvection model

(1)

\partial_{t}q+u\cdot\nabla q+\Lambda q=0,

(2)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p-\Delta u=-qRq,

(3)

\nabla\cdot u=0

in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
describing the evolution of a surface charge density
q
in a two-dimensional incompressible fluid flowing with a velocity
u
and a pressure
p
. Here
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the square root of the two-dimensional Laplacian, and
R=\nabla\Lambda^{-1}
is the two-dimensional Riesz transform. The initial data

(4)

q(\cdot,0)=q_{0}

and

(5)

u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}

are assumed to be regular enough and have good decay properties.
The model is motivated by physical and numerical studies of electroconvection

[
9
,
21
,
22
]
. The nonlocal aspect of the evolution of the charge density
and the nonlocal forcing on the Navier-Stokes equations in the model are due to the fact that the fluid and charges are confined to a thin two dimensional film. The global well-posedness of the system in bounded domains was obtained in
[
7
]
using commutator estimates and nonlocal nonlinear analysis.
In
[
1
]
, we investigated the long time dynamics of the model in two dimensions, with periodic boundary conditions and with applied voltage. When the fluid is forced by time-independent smooth mean zero body forces, we proved that the model (
1
)–(
4
) has a finite dimensional global attractor. In the absence of body forces, the charge density
q
converges exponentially in time to a unique limit due to the applied voltage, and the velocity
u
converges exponentially in time to zero. The rate of exponential decay depends on the periodic boundary conditions.

In this paper, we consider the time asymptotic behavior of solutions of (
1
)–(
4
) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and adapt the Fourier splitting method
[
17
,
18
]
of Schonbek to the present system. The method was initially used in
[
17
]
to prove decay of Leray weak solutions
[
14
]
of Navier-Stokes equations and to further decay studies for Navier-Stokes equations
[
3
,
11
,
18
,
19
,
23
]
and
many other partial differential equations (see for instance
[
4
,
8
,
10
,
15
,
24
,
25
]
). Different approaches were employed as well to investigate the time decay
[
16
]
and space-time decay
[
2
,
12
,
13
,
20
]
of higher-order derivatives of solutions to Navier-Stokes equations.

The electroconvection model (
1
)–(
4
) couples Navier-Stokes equations to a scalar equation for a surface charge density
q
, evolving via advection by
u
and diffusion by
\Lambda
. We obtain in Theorem
1
of section
2
the long time
L^{2}
decay of the type

\|q\|_{L^{2}}=O(t^{-1})

and

\|u\|_{L^{2}}=O(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

This rate of decay is sharp for the linear uncoupled system if the initial data have non vanishing finite
L^{1}
norms, because functions of the form
Q(t)=e^{-t\Lambda^{\alpha}}q_{0}
obey

\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}\|Q(t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}=C_{n,\alpha}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}q_{0}dx\right)^{2}

for any
\alpha>0
and
n\geq 1
.
The fact that such a decay is sharp for the nonlinear evolution as well is a consequence of Theorem
4
of section
3
where we prove that
u-U
with
U(t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
and
q-Q
with
Q(t)=e^{-t\Lambda}q_{0}
decay faster in
L^{2}
than
u
and
q
, respectively. Similar results were proved for critical SQG in
[
8
]
. The critical SQG velocity
u=R^{\perp}q
decays in
L^{2}
like
q
, that is at the rate
t^{-1}
, which helps lower the size of the nonlinear term
u\cdot\nabla q
in that equation. In our case, the velocity has slower decay in
L^{2}
due to the Navier-Stokes equation, namely of the order
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and the nonlinear term is larger. The influence of the charge density
q
is felt by the Navier-Stokes velocity via the electric force
-qRq
. In order to obtain a key fast enough decay at low wave numbers for the difference

v=u-U
, we need to control a moment of
q
,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}|q(x,t)|^{2}dx=M^{2}(t)
, in view of the inequality

|\widehat{qRq}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|\|q\|_{L^{2}}M(t)

(see Lemma
3
). We prove that

M(t)=O(\sqrt{\log t})

for long time, by analyzing the evolution of the quantity
a(x)q(x,t)
with
a(x)=\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}
. This analysis uses the boundedness of the commutator between
\Lambda
and multiplication by
a(x)
, which we establish in Lemma
1
. In addition, in order to achieve the necessary sharp
L^{2}
bounds we have to obtain bounds for the decay of higher norms of both
u
and
q
. For instance,
H^{1}

norms of
q
are of the order

\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}=O(t^{-1}).

These bounds are obtained by somewhat involved nonlinear and nonlocal analysis, and they are no longer sharp compared to the generic
t^{-2}
linear behavior.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we study the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the electroconvection model (
1
)–(
4
): we prove that the
L^{2}
norm of the surface charge density
q
decays in time to zero with a rate of order
t^{-1}
whereas the velocity
u
decays in time to zero with a rate of order
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. We also investigate the rate of decay of their higher-order derivatives, and we obtain decaying-in-time bounds in Hölder spaces
C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
. In section
3
, we prove that the differences
q-Q
and
u-U
decay to zero in
L^{2}
faster than
q
and
u
, with rates of order
t^{-1-\frac{3}{4}}
and
t^{-\frac{3}{4}}
, respectively. In the Appendix, we present results on the existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
1
)–(
4
), based on the Banach fixed point theorem, the Aubin-Lions lemma and commutator estimates.

2.
Long Time Behavior of Solutions

In this section, we consider the long-time behavior of solutions of the electroconvection model described by (
1
)–(
4
). We show that the charge density
q
and the velocity
u
converge to
0
in the
H^{2}
norm, and we investigate the rate of convergence.

For a function
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, we denote its Fourier transform by

(6)

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}dx.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free and
q_{0}\in L^{4}\cap L^{1}
. There exist positive constants
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}
depending only on the initial data and some universal constants such that the unique global-in-time solution
(q,u)
of (
1
)–(
4
) obeys

(7)

\|q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{(t+1)^{2}}

and

(8)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{\prime}}{t+1}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1
(Basic energy estimates
).
We take the
L^{2}
inner product of equation (
1
) with
\Lambda^{-1}q
and the
L^{2}
inner product of equation (
2
) with
u
. Then we add the resulting energy equalities. Integrating by parts, we have the cancellations

(9)

(u\cdot\nabla u,u)_{L^{2}}=(\nabla p,u)_{L^{2}}=0

and

\displaystyle(u\cdot\nabla q,\Lambda^{-1}q)_{L^{2}}+(qRq,u)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-(u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-1}q,q)_{L^{2}}+(qRq,u)_{L^{2}}

(10)

\displaystyle=-(u\cdot Rq,q)_{L^{2}}+(qRq,u)_{L^{2}}=0

due to the divergence-free condition (
3
).
Thus, we obtain

(11)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

We integrate in time from
0
to
t
and we take the supremum over all positive times
t\geq 0
. We get

(12)

\sup\limits_{t\geq 0}\left\{\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}2\left(\|q(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)ds\right\}=\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

ending the proof of Step 1.

Step 2
(Pointwise bounds for the Fourier transform of the charge density
q
).

The Fourier transform of
q
evolves according to

(13)

\partial_{t}\widehat{q}(\xi,t)+(\widehat{u\cdot\nabla q})(\xi,t)+\widehat{\Lambda q}(\xi,t)=0.

The fractional Laplacian
\Lambda
is a Fourier multiplier with symbol
|\xi|
, hence

(14)

\partial_{t}\widehat{q}+|\xi|\widehat{q}=-\widehat{u\cdot\nabla q}.

We estimate the Fourier transform of the nonlinear term

(15)

|\widehat{u\cdot\nabla q}|=|\widehat{\nabla\cdot(uq)}|\leq C|\xi|\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}

using the divergence-free condition (
3
), the boundedness of the Fourier transform of a function by its
L^{1}
norm, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
This yields the differential inequality

(16)

\partial_{t}\widehat{q}+|\xi|\widehat{q}\leq C|\xi|\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}.

We multiply both sides by the integrating factor
e^{|\xi|t}
and integrate in time from
0
to
t
. We obtain the bound

(17)

|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|\leq|\widehat{q}_{0}(\xi)|+C|\xi|\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|q(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds.

As a consequence of Step 1 and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get the pointwise bound

(18)

|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|\leq\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C_{0}|\xi|\sqrt{t}

where
C_{0}
is a time-independent constant depending only on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
. This finishes the proof of Step 2.

Step 3
(Decaying bound for the
L^{2}
norm of the charge density).

The
L^{2}
norm of
q
evolves according to

(19)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

In view of Parseval’s identity and the fact that
\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a Fourier multiplier with symbol
|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

(20)

\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\widehat{\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi.

We bound the dissipation from below

(21)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\geq\int_{|\xi|>\rho(t)}|\xi||\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

where
\rho(t)
is the function defined on
[0,\infty)
by

(22)

\rho(t)=\frac{r}{2(t+1)}

for some positive constant
r
to be determined later. We note that

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>\rho(t)}|\xi||\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\rho(t)\int_{|\xi|>\rho(t)}|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\rho(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi-\rho(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho(t)}|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

(23)

\displaystyle=\rho(t)\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\rho(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho(t)}|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

where we used Parseval’s identity.
Consequently, we obtain the energy inequality

(24)

\frac{d}{dt}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\rho(t)\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\rho(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho(t)}|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi.

By the pointwise bound (
18
) and Fubini’s theorem for spherical coordinates, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho(t)}|\widehat{q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho(t)}\left(\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C_{0}|\xi|\sqrt{t}\right)^{2}d\xi=C\int_{0}^{\rho(t)}r\left(\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C_{0}r\sqrt{t}\right)^{2}dr

(25)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho(t)}r\left(\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+C_{0}^{2}r^{2}t\right)dr\leq\Gamma_{1}\left(\rho(t)^{2}+t\rho(t)^{4}\right)

where
\Gamma_{1}
depends only on the initial data.
We obtain

(26)

\frac{d}{dt}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\rho(t)\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\Gamma_{1}(\rho(t)^{3}+t\rho(t)^{5})

for all
t\geq 0
. We multiply both sides of the inequality by the integrating factor

(27)

e^{2\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s)ds}=e^{r\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{s+1}ds}=e^{r\ln(t+1)}=(t+1)^{r}

and then we integrate in time from
0
to
t
. We get

\displaystyle\|q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}+\frac{\Gamma_{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{(s+1)^{3}}+\frac{1}{(s+1)^{4}}\right)(s+1)^{r}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}+\frac{\Gamma_{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}\left(\frac{(t+1)^{r-2}}{r-2}-\frac{1}{r-2}+\frac{(t+1)^{r-3}}{r-3}-\frac{1}{r-3}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}+\frac{\Gamma_{2}}{(r-2)(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{2}}{(r-3)(t+1)^{3}}

for any
r>3
. Here
\Gamma_{2}
depends on
r
and the initial data. We choose
r=4
and we obtain the bound

(29)

\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{3}}{(t+1)^{2}}

where
\Gamma_{3}
is a positive constant depending only on the initial data. This completes the proof of (
7
) and Step 3.

Step 4
(Pointwise bounds for the Fourier transform of the velocity
u
).

Applying the Leray Projector
\mathbb{P}
to equation (
2
), we have

(30)

\partial_{t}u+\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)-\Delta u=-\mathbb{P}(qRq),

where we used the incompressibility condition (
3
) and the fact that
\mathbb{P}
and
-\Delta
are Fourier multipliers so they commute.
Hence the Fourier transform of
u
obeys

(31)

\partial_{t}\widehat{u}+\widehat{\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)}-\widehat{\Delta u}=-\widehat{\mathbb{P}(qRq)}.

We estimate

(32)

|\widehat{\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)}(\xi,t)|\leq C|\xi||\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}\leq C|\xi|\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

(33)

|\widehat{\mathbb{P}(qRq)}(\xi,t)|\leq C\|(qRq)(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\|q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

in view of the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.
We obtain

(34)

\partial_{t}\widehat{u}+|\xi|^{2}\widehat{u}\leq C|\xi|\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and hence

(35)

|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C|\xi|\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\|q(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
.
In view of the bound (
12
), we get

(36)

|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|\leq\Gamma_{4}+C|\xi|\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

where
\Gamma_{4}
is a positive constant depending only on the initial data. This completes the proof of Step 4.

Step 5
(Decaying bounds for the
L^{4}
norm of
q
).
The evolution of the
L^{4}
norm of
q
is described by the energy equality

(37)

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q^{3}\Lambda qdx=0.

In view of the Córdoba-Córdoba inequality
[
6
]
, the dissipation is bounded from below

(38)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q^{3}\Lambda qdx\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(q^{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and thus

(39)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q^{3}\Lambda qdx\geq c\|q\|_{L^{8}}^{4}

due to Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities. Using interpolation inequalities in
L^{p}
spaces and the uniform boundedness of the
L^{2}
norm of the charge density
q
by
\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
, we have the bound

(40)

\|q\|_{L^{4}}\leq\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{8}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{8}}^{\frac{2}{3}}

from which we conclude that

(41)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}q^{3}\Lambda qdx\geq C\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{-2}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{6}

and hence

(42)

\frac{d}{dt}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{C}{\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{6}\leq 0.

Letting
y=\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
, we obtain the Bernouilli ordinary differential inequality

(43)

\frac{dy}{dt}+\frac{C}{\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}y^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq 0.

We apply a change of variable given by
u=y^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and we get

(44)

\frac{-2}{u^{3}}\frac{du}{dt}+\frac{C}{\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\frac{1}{u^{3}}\leq 0

so

(45)

\frac{du}{dt}\geq\frac{C}{2\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
, we arrive at the bound

(46)

\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{-2}\geq\|q_{0}\|_{L^{4}}^{-2}+\frac{C}{\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}t\geq\Gamma_{5}(1+t)

where
\Gamma_{5}
is a constant depending on the initial data. Consequently, we obtain

(47)

\|q\|_{L^{4}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Gamma_{5}}}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Step 6
(Decaying bound for the
L^{2}
norm of the velocity).

The
L^{2}
norm of the velocity evolves according to

(48)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}qRq\cdot udx.

In view of Hölder’s inequality, the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on
L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, and Ladyzhenskaya’s interpolation inequality, we bound

(49)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}qRq\cdot u\right|\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

yielding

(50)

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}.

By Parseval’s identity, we have

(51)

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}.

For a positive function
\rho_{1}(t)
continuous on
[0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\xi|>\rho_{1}(t)}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\geq\rho_{1}(t)^{2}\int_{|\xi|>\rho_{1}(t)}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{1}(t)^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi-\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{1}(t)}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\right)

(52)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(t)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\rho_{1}(t)^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{1}(t)}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi.

Consequently, we obtain the energy inequality

(53)

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{1}(t)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}+\rho_{1}(t)^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{1}(t)}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi.

Using (
36
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{1}(t)}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\leq C\int_{0}^{\rho_{1}(t)}r\left(\Gamma_{4}^{2}+Cr^{2}\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right\}^{2}\right)dr

(54)

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{6}\rho_{1}(t)^{2}+C\rho_{1}(t)^{4}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{2}

and thus

(55)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{1}(t)^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{6}\rho_{1}(t)^{4}+C\rho_{1}(t)^{6}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{2}+C\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Multiplying by the integrating factor
e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}
, and integrating in time from
0
to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}+\frac{\Gamma_{6}}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}\rho_{1}(s)^{4}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}\rho_{1}(s)^{6}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{2}ds

(56)

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}\int_{0}^{t}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}ds.

In view of (
12
), (
29
) and (
47
), we estimate

(57)

\int_{0}^{t}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}ds\leq\Gamma_{7}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}}{(s+1)^{2}}ds

for any
t\geq 0
, and so

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}+\frac{\Gamma_{6}}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}\rho_{1}(s)^{4}ds

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}\rho_{1}(s)^{6}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{2}ds

(58)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma_{7}}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{\int_{0}^{s}\rho_{1}(\tau)^{2}d\tau}}{(s+1)^{2}}ds

for any
t\geq 0
.

In order to obtain the sharp decaying bound for the velocity
u
, we need the following three sub-steps:

Step 6.1
(Logarithmic decaying bound for the
L^{2}
norm of the velocity).
We take
\rho_{1}(t)=(e+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\ln(e+t)\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. In this case, the integrating factor is given by

(59)

e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(e+s)\ln(e+s)}ds}=e^{\ln\left[\ln(e+t)\right]}=\ln(e+t)

and so (
2
) becomes

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\ln(e+t)}+\frac{\Gamma_{6}}{\ln(e+t)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(e+s)^{2}\ln(e+s)}ds

(60)

\displaystyle+\frac{C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\ln(e+t)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{s^{2}}{(e+s)^{3}\left[\ln(e+s)\right]^{2}}ds+\frac{\Gamma_{7}}{\ln(e+t)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\ln(e+s)}{(s+1)^{2}}ds.

in view of the uniform boundedness of
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
by
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
. We note that

(61)

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{s^{2}}{(e+s)^{3}\left[\ln(e+s)\right]^{2}}ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(e+s)\left[\ln(e+s)\right]^{2}}ds=1-\frac{1}{\ln(e+t)}\leq 1

for any
t\geq 0
. Therefore,

(62)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{8}}{\ln(e+t)}

for all
t\geq 0
, where
\Gamma_{8}
is a constant depending only on the initial data.

Step 6.2
(Almost sharp decaying bound for the
L^{2}
norm of the velocity).
In order to improve the logarithmic decay (
62
), we take
\rho_{1}(t)=r^{\frac{1}{2}}(t+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for some
r
to be chosen later. In this case, the integrating factor is given by

(63)

e^{\int_{0}^{t}\rho_{1}(s)^{2}ds}=e^{r\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(s+1)}ds}=e^{r\ln(t+1)}=(t+1)^{r}

and so (
2
) becomes

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}+\frac{\Gamma_{9}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(s+1)^{r}}{(s+1)^{2}}ds+\frac{C}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(s+1)^{r}}{(s+1)^{3}}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{2}ds

for all
t\geq 0
. Here
\Gamma_{9}
is a constant depending only on the initial data and
r
.
We have

(64)

\frac{\Gamma_{9}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(s+1)^{r}}{(s+1)^{2}}ds=\frac{\Gamma_{9}}{(r-1)(t+1)^{r}}\left((t+1)^{r-1}-1\right)\leq\frac{\Gamma_{9}}{(r-1)(t+1)}

for any
r>1
.
Moreover, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the time variable yields

(65)

\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{2}\leq s\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}d\tau,

so that

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(s+1)^{r}}{(s+1)^{3}}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(t+1)^{r}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{r-2}ds\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}ds\right)

(66)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(r-1)(t+1)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}ds\right)

for any
r>1
. Taking
r=2
and using (
62
) give

(67)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{10}}{t+1}+\frac{\Gamma_{10}}{t+1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\ln(e+s)}ds

and so

(68)

(t+1)\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{10}+C^{\prime}\Gamma_{10}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(s+1)\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(s+e)\ln(e+s)}ds

for any
t\geq 0
. By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle(t+1)\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{10}+C^{\prime}\Gamma_{10}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{(e+\tau)\ln(e+\tau)}d\tau}}{(e+s)\ln(e+s)}ds

(69)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{10}+C^{\prime}\Gamma_{10}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\ln(e+t)}{(e+s)\left[\ln(e+s)\right]^{2}}ds\leq\Gamma_{10}+C^{\prime}\Gamma_{10}^{2}\ln(e+t).

Therefore,

(70)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{11}\ln(t+e)}{t+1}

for any
t\geq 0
, where
\Gamma_{11}
is a constant depending only on the initial data.

Step 6.3
(Sharp decaying bound for the
L^{2}
norm of the velocity).
Finally, we prove (
8
). We take
\rho_{1}(t)=\sqrt{2}(t+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
as in the previous sub-step, and we obtain the bound

(71)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{12}}{t+1}+\frac{C}{(t+1)^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{s+1}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{2}ds

for all
t\geq 0
. We note that

(72)

\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\leq\Gamma_{13}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\ln(\tau+e)}{\tau+1}d\tau\leq C\Gamma_{13}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\ln(\tau+e)}{\tau+e}d\tau\leq\Gamma_{14}\left[\ln(s+e)\right]^{2}

and so

(73)

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{s+1}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\right)^{2}ds\leq\Gamma_{15}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s+1}}ds

for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore,

(74)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Gamma_{16}}{t+1}

for all
t\geq 0
, where
\Gamma_{16}
is a positive constant depending only on the initial data. This ends the proof of Theorem
1
.

Now we study the rate of convergence of the gradients of the charge density and the velocity.

Theorem 2
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free such that
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}
. Let
q_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
such that
\nabla q_{0}\in L^{1}
. There exist positive constants
K_{0}
and
K_{0}^{\prime}
depending only on the initial data and some universal constants such that the unique global-in-time solution
(q,u)
of (
1
)–(
4
) obeys

(75)

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{K_{0}}{t+1}

and

(76)

\|\nabla q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{K_{0}^{\prime}}{(t+1)^{2}}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
The proof is divided into 5 steps.

Step 1
(Pointwise bounds for the Fourier transform of
\nabla u
).
The Fourier transform of the gradient of
u
satisfies

(77)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\nabla u}+\widehat{\nabla\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)}-\widehat{\nabla\Delta u}=-\widehat{\nabla\mathbb{P}(qRq)}

yielding the differential inequality

(78)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\nabla u}+|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\nabla u}\leq|\xi|^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\xi|\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
, and using (
12
), we obtain

(79)

|\widehat{\nabla u}(\xi,t)|\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+K_{1}|\xi|+K_{2}|\xi|^{2}t

where
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are positive constants depending only on the initial data.

We note that the pointwise estimate (
79
) is not the sharpest. Indeed, one can use the decaying bounds for the
L^{2}
norms of the velocity and the surface charge density derived in Theorem
1
instead of using (
12
), and this will result in a bound whose growth in
t
is slower when
t\geq 1
. However, this will not improve the decay in the upcoming step, so we disregard this observation.

Step 2
(Decaying bound for the
L^{2}
norm of
\nabla u
).

We take the
L^{2}
inner product of equation (
2
) with
-\Delta u
and we get

(80)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}qRq\cdot\Delta u.

The nonlinear term
(u\cdot\nabla u,\Delta u)_{L^{2}}
vanishes due to the fact that the matrix
M^{t}M^{2}
has a zero trace where
M
is the two-by-two traceless matrix whose entries are given by
M_{ij}=\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}
and
M^{t}
is its transpose.
In view of Hölder’s inequality with exponents 4,4,2, the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on
L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and Young’s inequality, we obtain

(81)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{4}.

Using the
L^{4}
estimate (
47
), we have

(82)

\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\leq K_{3}(1+t)^{-2}

where
K_{3}
depends on the initial data.
We note that the initial charge density is assumed to be in
H^{1}
and so it belongs to
L^{4}
due to the Sobolev embedding of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
into
L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Going back to (
81
), we have

(83)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CK_{3}(t+1)^{-2}.

For
t\in[0,\infty)
, we let

(84)

\rho_{2}(t)=r^{\frac{1}{2}}(t+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

for some
r>0
to be chosen later. By Parseval’s identity, we get

(85)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CK_{3}(1+t)^{-2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{2}(t)}|\widehat{\nabla u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi.

In view of the pointwise bound (
79
), we have

(86)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{4}(t+1)^{-2}+K_{5}\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\left[\rho_{2}(t)^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{4}+\rho_{2}(t)^{6}t^{2}\right]

for
t\geq 0
.
We multiply by the integrating factor
(t+1)^{r}
and then we integrate in time from
0
to
t
. We obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}+\frac{K_{4}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(s+1)^{r}}{(s+1)^{2}}ds

(87)

\displaystyle+\frac{K_{6}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{r}\left(\frac{1}{(1+s)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1+s)^{3}}\right)ds

where
K_{6}
depends only on the initial data. We can take any
r>2
and we obtain the bound (
75
).

Step 3
(Bounds for
\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\gamma}\|\Delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds
where
\gamma\neq 1
is a real number).

Let
\gamma\neq 1
. The differential inequality (
81
) yields

(88)

\frac{d}{dt}((t+1)^{\gamma}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})-\gamma(t+1)^{\gamma-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(t+1)^{\gamma}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(t+1)^{\gamma}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{4}

for all
t\geq 0
. Integrating in time from
0
to
t
and using (
47
) and (
75
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\gamma}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(\gamma K_{0}+K_{7})\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\gamma-2}ds

(89)

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\gamma K_{0}+K_{7}}{\gamma-1}\left[(t+1)^{\gamma-1}-1\right]

for some positive constant
K_{7}
depending on
\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|q_{0}\|_{L^{4}}
.

Step 4
(Pointwise bounds for the Fourier transform of
\nabla q
).

The Fourier transform of the gradient of
q
satisfies

(90)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\nabla q}+\widehat{\nabla(u\cdot\nabla q)}+\widehat{\nabla\Lambda q}=0,

hence

(91)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\nabla q}+|\xi|\widehat{\nabla q}\leq|\xi|^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}.

Using (
12
), we obtain the pointwise bound

(92)

|\widehat{\nabla q}(\xi,t)|\leq\|\nabla q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+K_{8}|\xi|^{2}\sqrt{t}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
. Here
K_{8}
depends only on the initial data.

Step 5
(Decaying bound for the
L^{2}
norm of
\nabla q
).

The
L^{2}
norm of the gradient of
q
evolves according to the energy equality

(93)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=(u\cdot\nabla q,\Delta q)_{L^{2}}.

In view of the Ladyzhenskaya interpolation inequality

(94)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and the interpolation inequality
[
1
]

(95)

\|\nabla q\|_{L^{\frac{8}{3}}}^{2}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}},

we estimate the nonlinear term

(96)

|(u\cdot\nabla q,\Delta q)_{L^{2}}|\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{\frac{8}{3}}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Applying Young’s inequality, we obtain

(97)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{2}.

In view of (
75
) and (
82
), we have

(98)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{K_{9}}{(t+1)^{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for all
t\geq 0
. Here
K_{9}
depends only the initial data. Letting

(99)

\rho_{3}(t)=r(t+1)^{-1},

we split the dissipation term,

(100)

\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\rho_{3}(t)\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\rho_{3}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{3}(t)}|\widehat{\nabla q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

yielding the differential inequality

(101)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{3}(t)\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{K_{9}}{(t+1)^{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{3}(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{3}(t)}|\widehat{\nabla q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

In view of the pointwise bound for
\widehat{\nabla q}
given by (
92
), we have

(102)

\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{3}(t)}|\widehat{\nabla q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\leq K_{10}\left(\rho_{3}(t)^{2}+\rho_{3}(t)^{6}t\right)

and so

(103)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{3}(t)\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{K_{9}}{(t+1)^{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+K_{10}\left(\rho_{3}(t)^{3}+\rho_{3}(t)^{7}t\right).

We multiply both sides by
(t+1)^{r}
and we integrate in time from
0
to
t
. We obtain

(104)

\displaystyle\|\nabla q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\nabla q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}+\frac{K_{11}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{r-2}\|\Delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

(105)

\displaystyle+\frac{K_{12}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\left[(s+1)^{r-3}-(s+1)^{r-6}\right]ds.

In view of (
2
) applied with
\gamma=r-2
, we have

(106)

\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{r-2}\|\Delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{(r-2)K_{0}+K_{7}}{r-3}\left[(t+1)^{r-3}-1\right]

for any
r\neq 3
, and so

(107)

\frac{K_{11}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{r-2}\|\Delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\frac{K_{13}}{(t+1)^{3}}

for any
r>3
. Here
K_{13}
depends on the initial data and
r
. Putting (
104
) and (
107
) together and choosing
r=6
give the desired decay (
76
). This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

Now we establish decaying bounds for higher order derivatives. We need the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free such that
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}
. Let
q_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
such that
\nabla q_{0}\in L^{1}
. Let
\beta>3
. There exist a positive universal constant
C
and positive constants
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
, and
c_{3}
depending only on the initial data such that the solution
q
of (
1
)–(
4
) obeys

(108)

\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{\beta}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\|\nabla q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\frac{(\beta-2)c_{1}+c_{2}}{\beta-3}(t+1)^{\beta-3}+\frac{\beta c_{3}}{\beta-2}(t+1)^{\beta-2}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
In view of the differential inequality (
97
), we have

(109)

\frac{d}{dt}(t+1)^{\beta}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\beta(t+1)^{\beta-1}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(t+1)^{\beta}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(t+1)^{\beta}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{2}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
, using the bounds (
47
) and (
75
) and applying (
2
) with
\gamma=\beta-2
, we obtain (
108
).

Theorem 3
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{2}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free such that
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}
and
\Delta u_{0}\in L^{1}
. Let
q_{0}\in H^{2}\cap L^{1}
such that
\nabla q_{0}\in L^{1}
and
\Delta q_{0}\in L^{1}
. There exist positive constants
M_{0}
and
M_{0}^{\prime}
depending only on the initial data and some universal constants such that the unique global-in-time solution
(q,u)
of (
1
)–(
4
) obeys

(110)

\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{M_{0}}{t+1}

and

(111)

\|\Delta q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{M_{0}^{\prime}}{(t+1)^{2}}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
The Fourier transform of
\Delta u
obeys

(112)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\Delta u}+|\xi|^{2}\widehat{\Delta u}\leq C|\xi|^{3}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C|\xi|^{2}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently, it satisfies the pointwise bound

(113)

|\widehat{\Delta u}(\xi,t)|\leq\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+M_{1}|\xi|^{3}t+M_{2}|\xi|^{2}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and all
t\geq 0
. Here
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are positive constants depending only on the initial data.
The
L^{2}
norm of
\Delta u
evolves according to the energy equality

(114)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta(qRq)\cdot\Delta udx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\Delta udx.

Integrating by parts, using (
3
), and applying Ladyzhenskaya’s interpolation inequality, we estimate the second term on the right hand side in (
114
) as

(115)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\Delta udx\right|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}.

In view of the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on
L^{4}
and the continuous embedding of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
in
L^{4}
, we obtain for the first term on the right hand side in (
114
)

(116)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Delta(qRq)\cdot\Delta udx\right|\leq C\|q\|_{L^{4}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}.

From (
114
)–(
116
) and using Young’s inequality, we obtain the energy inequality

(117)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In view of Parseval’s identity, we have

(118)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{2}(t)}|\widehat{\Delta u}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

where
\rho_{2}
is the function defined by (
84
).
The decay bounds (
82
) and (
75
) together with the pointwise bound for the Fourier transform of
\Delta u
given by (
113
) yield

(119)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{M_{3}}{t+1}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{M_{4}}{t+1}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+M_{5}\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\left[\rho_{2}(t)^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{8}t^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{6}\right]

for all
t\geq 0
, where
M_{3},M_{4}
and
M_{5}
are positive constants depending only on the initial data. Multiplying by the integrating factor and integrating in time from
0
to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(t+1)^{r}}+\frac{M_{3}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{r-1}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

(120)

\displaystyle+\frac{M_{4}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}(s+1)^{r-1}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+\frac{M_{5}}{(t+1)^{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\left[(s+1)^{r-2}+(s+1)^{r-3}+(s+1)^{r-4}\right]ds.

We choose
r=5
. In view of the bound (
2
) applied with
\gamma=r-1
and Proposition
1
applied with
\beta=r-1
, we obtain (
110
).

The Fourier transform of the Laplacian of
q
satisfies

(121)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\Delta q}+|\xi|\widehat{\Delta q}\leq|\xi|^{3}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}

and hence

(122)

|\widehat{\Delta q}(\xi,t)|\leq\|\Delta q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+M_{8}|\xi|^{3}\sqrt{t}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
. Here
M_{8}
depends only on the initial data.
Now, we establish decaying estimate for
\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
which evolves according to

(123)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\nabla q))\Delta qdx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\Delta u\cdot\nabla q)\Delta qdx.

In view of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality

(124)

\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{5}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{5}},

the Sobolev embedding inequality

(125)

\|\Delta q\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}},

and the bound

(126)

\|\nabla\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}=\|\nabla\Lambda^{-1}\nabla\Lambda^{-1}\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}

that follows from the boundedness of the Riesz transforms on
L^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{4}}+C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{4}}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{9}{5}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{5}}+C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{4}}

(127)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{5}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{5}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently,

(128)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\|\Delta q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{5}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{5}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho_{2}(t)^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho_{2}(t)^{2}}|\widehat{\Delta q}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

where
\rho_{2}
is defined by (
84
).
In view of the estimates (
7
), (
75
) and (
110
), Proposition
1
applied with
\beta=r-1
, and the pointwise bound for the Fourier transform of
\Delta q
given by (
122
), we obtain (
111
). This ends the proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
be the space of bounded
1/2
-Hölder continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

(129)

\|f\|_{C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup\limits_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

In view of the continuous Sobolev embedding of
W^{1,4}
into
C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}
, the Ladyzhenskaya interpolation inequality, and Theorems
1
,
2
, and
3
, we obtain the following statement.

Corollary 1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{2}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free such that
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}
and
\Delta u_{0}\in L^{1}
. Let
q_{0}\in H^{2}\cap L^{1}
such that
\nabla q_{0}\in L^{1}
and

3.
Decomposition of the solution

In this section, we decompose the charge density
q
and the velocity
u
solutions of (
1
)–(
4
) in the sum of solutions
Q
and
U
of the linear equations

(132)

\partial_{t}Q+\Lambda Q=0

and

(133)

\partial_{t}U-\Delta U=0

with initial datum
Q(0)=q_{0}
and
U(0)=u_{0}
and remainders. We study the decays of the remainders
q-Q
and
u-U
in
L^{2}
and we show that they are faster than the decays of the
L^{2}
norms of
q
and
u
respectively.
The solutions of (
132
) and (
133
) are given explicitly by

(134)

Q(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{t}^{1}(x-w)q_{0}(w)dw

and

(135)

U(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{t}^{2}(x-w)u_{0}(w)dw

where
K_{t}^{s}
is the kernel defined by its Fourier transform

(136)

\mathcal{F}(K_{t}^{s})(\xi)=e^{-|\xi|^{s}t}.

The following proposition describes the decay of
\nabla Q
and
\nabla U
in
L^{\infty}
.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose
q_{0}\in L^{1}
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{q_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi<\infty
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi<\infty
. Then there exist positive constants
R_{0}
and
R_{0}^{\prime}
depending only on the initial data such that the solutions
Q
and
U
of the linear equations (
132
) and (
133
) satisfy

(137)

\|\nabla Q(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{R_{0}}{(t+1)^{3}}

and

(138)

\|\nabla U(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{R_{0}^{\prime}}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
In view of Parseval’s identity and the translation property of the Fourier transform, we have

(139)

\nabla Q(x)=\nabla K_{t}^{1}*q_{0}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla K_{t}^{1}(x-w)q_{0}(w)dw=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\nabla K_{t}^{1}}(\xi)\widehat{q_{0}}(\xi)d\xi.

On one hand,

(140)

\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\widehat{q_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{K_{t}^{1}}(\xi)|d\xi\leq C\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}e^{-rt}dr\leq C\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}t^{-3}

and so

(141)

t^{3}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

On the other hand,

(142)

\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{K_{t}^{1}}(\xi)||\widehat{q_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{q_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi

for all
t\geq 0
. Hence

(143)

(1+t)^{3}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 4(1+t^{3})\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(\|q_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{q_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi\right)

for all
t\geq 0
, yielding (
137
). Similarly, we have

(144)

(1+t)^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla U\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(1+t^{\frac{3}{2}})\|\nabla U\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi\right)

for all
t\geq 0
, yielding (
138
).

Remark 1
.

The assumptions
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{q_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi<\infty
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi<\infty
are required to obtain the uniform-in-time boundedness of the
L^{\infty}
norms
\nabla Q
and
\nabla U
for small times
t\in(0,1)
. This imposed regularity can be dropped since we are interested in studying the long-time behavior of solutions.

Next, we consider the pointwise behavior of the Fourier transforms of the differences
q-Q
and
u-U
. We need first the following lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

For
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we let

(145)

Tf(x)=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{|x-y|>\epsilon}\frac{\sqrt{|y|^{2}+1}-\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}}{|x-y|^{3}}f(y)dy.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
(independent of
f
) such that

(146)

\|Tf\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof:
We write

(147)

Tf(x)=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{|x-y|>\epsilon}(a(y)-a(x))k(x-y)f(y)dy.

where
a(x)
is the function defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by

(148)

a(x)=\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}

and
k(x)
is the function defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
by

(149)

k(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{3}}.

We note that
k
is homogeneous of degree
-3
. Moreover, the gradient of
a
is given by

(150)

\nabla a(x)=\left(\frac{x_{1}}{\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}},\frac{x_{2}}{\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}}\right)

and satisfies
\|\nabla a\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Therefore,
T
is a well-defined operator and bounded on
L^{2}
(see page 435 in Section 2 of
[
5
]
).

Using Lemma
1
, we study the evolution of
(\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1})q(x)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free such that
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}
. Let
q_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
such that
\nabla q_{0}\in L^{1}
. Furthermore, suppose that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}q_{0}(x)^{2}dx<\infty
. Then there exists a positive constant
R_{1}>0
depending only on the initial data such that

(151)

\|(\sqrt{|\cdot|^{2}+1})q(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq R_{1}\ln(t+1)+\|(\sqrt{|\cdot|^{2}+1})q_{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

holds for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
Let
a(x)=\sqrt{|x|^{2}+1}
. The evolution of
aq
is described by

(152)

\partial_{t}(aq)+au\cdot\nabla q+a\Lambda q=0.

Multiplying by
aq
and integrating in the space variable over
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we obtain

(153)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(a\Lambda q)aq=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(au\cdot\nabla q)aq.

The cancellation

(154)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla(aq))aq=0

holds due to (
3
), so we can rewrite the nonlinear term as

(155)

-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(au\cdot\nabla q)aq=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla a)q^{2}a.

By Hölder’s inequality, Ladyzhenskaya’s interpolation inequality, and the decaying bounds for the
L^{2}
norms of
q,u,\nabla u
and
\nabla q
given by (
7
), (
8
), (
75
) and (
76
), respectively, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla a)q^{2}a\right|\leq\|\nabla a\|_{L^{\infty}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}

(156)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}\leq R_{2}(t+1)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}

for some constant
R_{2}
depending only on the initial data.
Now we write the linear term as the sum

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(a\Lambda q)aq

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}aq\Lambda(aq)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(aq)\left[a\Lambda q-\Lambda(aq)\right]

(157)

\displaystyle=\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(aq)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(aq)\left[a\Lambda q-\Lambda(aq)\right].

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we bound

(158)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(aq)\left[a\Lambda q-\Lambda(aq)\right]\right|\leq\|aq\|_{L^{2}}\|a\Lambda q-\Lambda(aq)\|_{L^{2}}.

The pointwise formula for the fractional Laplacian of order 1 yields

\displaystyle(a\Lambda q-\Lambda(aq))(x)

\displaystyle=C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left[\frac{a(x)q(x)-a(x)q(y)}{|x-y|^{3}}-\frac{a(x)q(x)-a(y)q(y)}{|x-y|^{3}}\right]dy

(159)

\displaystyle=C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{a(y)-a(x)}{|x-y|^{3}}q(y)dy

where
C
is positive universal constant. As a consequence of Lemma
1
and (
7
), we obtain

(160)

\|a\Lambda q-\Lambda(aq)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(t+1)^{-1}.

Therefore, the
L^{2}
norm of
aq
obeys the energy inequality

(161)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(aq)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left[R_{2}(t+1)^{-\frac{3}{2}}+C(t+1)^{-1}\right]\|aq\|_{L^{2}}

so

(162)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq R_{3}(t+1)^{-1}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}

for some positive constant
R_{3}
depending only on the initial data. Dividing both sides of the inequality by
\|aq\|_{L^{2}}
, we get

(163)

\frac{d}{dt}\|aq\|_{L^{2}}\leq R_{3}(t+1)^{-1}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
, we obtain (
151
).

The following lemma is needed to obtain a growth in
|\xi|
for the Fourier transform of
\mathbb{P}(qRq)
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}f(x)^{2}dx<\infty
. Then

(164)

|\widehat{\mathbb{P}(fRf)}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|\|f\|_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projector and
R=(R_{1},R_{2})
is the Riesz transform vector on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof:
The Leray projector is a Fourier multiplier with a symbol denoted by
m(\xi)
. We have

(165)

\widehat{\mathbb{P}(fRf)}(\xi)=m(\xi)\widehat{fRf}(\xi)

for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
We note that
m(\xi)
is bounded uniformly in
\xi
. Now, the Fourier transform of
fRf
at
\xi
is given by

(166)

\widehat{fRf}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x)Rf(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}dx

for
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Since the Riesz transform is antisymmetric, we have

(167)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x)Rf(x)dx=0

and so we can write
\widehat{fRf}
at
\xi
as

(168)

\widehat{fRf}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(x)Rf(x)\left(e^{-i\xi\cdot x}-1\right)dx.

Using the identity

(169)

|e^{-i\xi\cdot x}-1|\leq|\xi||x|

that holds for all
x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we estimate

(170)

|\widehat{fRf}(\xi)|\leq|\xi|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x||f(x)||Rf(x)|dx\leq|\xi|\|Rf\|_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

in view of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. This gives the pointwise estimate (
164
).

As a consequence of lemmas
2
and
3
, we obtain the following statement.

Proposition 3
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free such that
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}
and
q_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
such that
\nabla q_{0}\in L^{1}
. Furthermore, suppose that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}q_{0}(x)^{2}dx<\infty
. Let
(q,u)
be the solution of (
1
)–(
4
). Let
\zeta=q-Q
and
v=u-U
. Then there exist positive constants
R_{4}
,
R_{5}
and
R_{6}
depending only on the initial data such that the Fourier transforms of
\zeta
and
v
satisfy the pointwise bounds

(171)

|\widehat{\zeta}(\xi,t)|\leq R_{4}|\xi|

and

(172)

|\widehat{v}(\xi,t)|\leq R_{5}|\xi|\ln(t+1)+R_{6}|\xi|\ln^{2}(t+1)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
The Fourier transform of
\zeta
obeys

(173)

\partial_{t}\widehat{\zeta}+|\xi|\widehat{\zeta}=-\widehat{u\cdot\nabla q}\leq|\xi|\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}.

Consequently,

(174)

|\widehat{\zeta}(\xi,t)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}\leq R_{4}|\xi|

in view of the decaying bounds (
7
) and (
8
).
The Fourier transform of
v
evolves according to

(175)

\partial_{t}\widehat{v}+|\xi|^{2}\widehat{v}=-\widehat{\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla u)}-\widehat{\mathbb{P}(qRq)}.

Thus

(176)

|\widehat{v}(\xi,t)|\leq C|\xi|\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+C|\xi|\int_{0}^{t}\|q\|_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}q(x)^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

by Lemma
3
. In view of Lemma
2
and the decaying estimates (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain (
172
).

Theorem 4
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
be divergence-free such that
\nabla u_{0}\in L^{1}
. Let
q_{0}\in H^{1}\cap L^{1}
such that
\nabla q_{0}\in L^{1}
. Furthermore, suppose that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}q_{0}(x)^{2}dx<\infty
,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{q_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi<\infty
, and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi||\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|d\xi<\infty
. Let
(q,u)
be the solution of (
1
)–(
4
). Then there exist positive constants
R_{7}
and
R_{8}
depending only on the initial data such that the differences
q-Q
and
u-U
satisfy

(177)

\|q(t)-Q(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{R_{7}}{(t+1)^{2+\frac{3}{2}}}

and

(178)

\|u(t)-U(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{R_{8}}{(t+1)^{1+\frac{1}{2}}}

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof:
Let
\zeta=q-Q
and
v=u-U
. We have

(179)

\partial_{t}\zeta+\Lambda\zeta=-u\cdot\nabla q.

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product of equation (
179
) with
\zeta
and using (
3
), we obtain

(180)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla q)Qdx\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{\infty}}.

As a consequence of Theorem
1
and the bound (
137
), we obtain the energy inequality

(181)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{R_{9}}{(t+1)^{4+\frac{1}{2}}}

where
R_{9}
is a positive constant depending only on the initial data.
For a fixed
r
, we let
\rho(t)=r(t+1)^{-1}
. Then

(182)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho(t)\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{R_{9}}{(t+1)^{4+\frac{1}{2}}}+\rho(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho(t)}|\widehat{\zeta}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi.

Using (
171
), we estimate

(183)

\int_{|\xi|\leq\rho(t)}|\widehat{\zeta}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\leq R_{10}\rho(t)^{4}

and we obtain

(184)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho(t)\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{R_{9}}{(t+1)^{4+\frac{1}{2}}}+R_{10}\rho(t)^{5}.

Multiplying by the factor
(s+1)^{r}
, integrating in the time variable
s
from
0
to
t
, and choosing any

, we obtain the desired bound (
177
).
Now,
v
obeys

(185)

\partial_{t}v-\Delta v=-u\cdot\nabla u-qRq-\nabla p.

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product of this latter equation with
v
and using the fact that
v
is divergence-free, we get the energy equation

(186)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot Udx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(qRq)\cdot vdx.

We estimate

(187)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot Udx\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla U\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{R_{11}}{(t+1)^{1+\frac{3}{2}}}

in view of the bounds (
8
) and (
138
), and

(188)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(qRq)\cdot vdx\leq C\|q\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{R_{12}}{(t+1)^{1+\frac{3}{2}}}

in view of the decaying estimate (
7
), (
76
) and (
130
). This yields the energy inequality

(189)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho(t)\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{R_{13}}{(t+1)^{1+\frac{3}{2}}}+\rho(t)\int_{|\xi|\leq\sqrt{\rho(t)}}|\widehat{v}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi

where
\rho(t)=r(t+1)^{-1}
. Using the pointwise bound for the Fourier transform of
v
given by (
172
), we have

(190)

\int_{|\xi|\leq\sqrt{\rho(t)}}|\widehat{v}(\xi,t)|^{2}d\xi\leq R_{14}\left[\ln^{2}(t+1)+\ln^{4}(t+1)\right]\rho(t)^{2}\leq R_{15}\sqrt{t+1}\rho(t)^{2},

hence

(191)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rho(t)\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{R_{13}}{(t+1)^{1+\frac{3}{2}}}+R_{15}\sqrt{t+1}\rho(t)^{3}.

We multiply both sides by
(s+1)^{r}
, we integrate from
0
to
t
, we choose any
r>3/2
, and we obtain (
178
).

In this appendix, we prove the existence of weak and strong solutions for the electroconvection model (

)–(

).

Definition 1
.

A solution
(q,u)
of (
1
)–(
4
) is said to be a weak solution on
[0,T]
if it solves (
1
)–(
4
) in the sense of distributions,
u
is divergence-free in the sense of distributions,

(192)

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})

and

(193)

q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1/2}).

Theorem 5
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}
be divergence-free, let
q_{0}\in L^{2}
. Let
T>0
be arbitrary. There exists a weak solution
(q,u)
of the system (
1
)–(
4
) on
[0,T]
.

Proof.
We briefly sketch the main ideas of the proof. For
0<\epsilon\leq 1
, we consider a viscous approximation of (
1
)–(
4
) given by

(194)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}q^{\epsilon}+J_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q^{\epsilon})+\Lambda q^{\epsilon}-\epsilon\Delta q^{\epsilon}=0\\
\partial_{t}u^{\epsilon}+J_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla u^{\epsilon})-\Delta u^{\epsilon}+\nabla p^{\epsilon}=-J_{\epsilon}(q^{\epsilon}Rq^{\epsilon})\\
\nabla\cdot u^{\epsilon}=0\end{cases}

with smoothed out initial data, where
J_{\epsilon}
is a standard mollifier operator,
u^{\epsilon}=J_{\epsilon}u
,
q^{\epsilon}=J_{\epsilon}q
and
p^{\epsilon}=J_{\epsilon}p
.
For each
\epsilon>0
, we consider the map

(195)

(q(t),u(t))\mapsto\Phi_{\epsilon}((q,u))(t)=(e^{\epsilon t\Delta}J_{\epsilon}q_{0}-\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}_{t}(q^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon}),e^{t\Delta}J_{\epsilon}u_{0}-\mathcal{B}^{\epsilon}_{t}(q^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon}))

where

(196)

\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}_{t}(q^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\epsilon(t-s)\Delta}J_{\epsilon}(u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q^{\epsilon})(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\epsilon(t-s)\Delta}\Lambda q^{\epsilon}(s)ds

and

(197)

\mathcal{B}^{\epsilon}_{t}(q^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}J_{\epsilon}\mathbb{P}(u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla u^{\epsilon})(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}J_{\epsilon}\mathbb{P}(q^{\epsilon}Rq^{\epsilon})(s)ds.

There exists a time
T_{\epsilon}=T_{\epsilon}(\epsilon,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}},\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}})>0
such that the map
\Phi_{\epsilon}
is a contraction on the Banach space

(198)

X_{T}=L^{\infty}(0,T;\bar{B}_{L^{2}}(2\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}})\oplus L^{\infty}(0,T;\bar{B}_{L^{2}_{\sigma}}(2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})

where
\bar{B}_{L^{2}}(r)
is the closed ball in
L^{2}
, and
\bar{B}_{L^{2}_{\sigma}}
is the closed ball in the space of
L^{2}
divergence-free vectors. Consequently,
\Phi_{\epsilon}
has a fixed point
(q^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})\in X_{T_{\epsilon}}
solving (
194
). This solution extends to the time interval
[0,T]
, and this can be obtained by establishing uniform-in-time bounds for
(q^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})
on
[0,T]
.
Indeed, we have

(199)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0

as shown in (
11
). Hence the family of mollified velocities
(u^{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
. On the other hand, the
L^{2}
norm of
q^{\epsilon}
evolves according to

(200)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\nabla q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0,

and so the family of mollified charge densities
(q^{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{\frac{1}{2}})
. The
q^{\epsilon}
and
u^{\epsilon}
equations imply that the sequence of time derivatives
(\partial_{t}q^{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
and
(\partial_{t}u^{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-\frac{3}{2}})
and
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1})
respectively. By the Aubin-Lions lemma, the sequence
((q^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon}))_{\epsilon}
has a subsequence that converges strongly in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
to a weak solution
(q,u)
of (
1
)–(
4
). We omit further details.

Definition 2
.

A weak solution
(q,u)
of (
1
)–(
4
) is said to be a strong solution on
[0,T]
if

(201)

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})

and

(202)

q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{4})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1/2}).

Theorem 6
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}
be divergence-free and
q_{0}\in L^{4}
. Let
T>0
be arbitrary. There exists a unique strong solution
(u,q)
of the system (
1
)–(
4
) on
[0,T]
.

Proof.
We take the
L^{2}
inner product of the equation satisfied by
q^{\epsilon}
in (
194
) with
(q^{\epsilon})^{3}
. In view of the divergence-free condition satisfied by
u^{\epsilon}
, the nonlinear term vanishes, that is

(203)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q^{\epsilon}(q^{\epsilon})^{3}dx=0.

By the Córdoba-Córdoba inequality (
[
6
]
), we have

(204)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(q^{\epsilon})^{3}\Lambda q^{\epsilon}dx\geq 0

and

(205)

-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(q^{\epsilon})^{3}\Delta q^{\epsilon}dx\geq 0.

Consequently, we obtain

(206)

\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\leq 0

which yields the boundedness of
q
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}))
by the Banach Alaoglu theorem and the lower semi-continuity of the norm.
The
L^{2}
norm of
\nabla u^{\epsilon}
obeys the energy inequality

(207)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}

as shown in (
80
), yielding the boundedness of
u
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})
.
Now we prove the uniqueness of strong solutions. Suppose
(q_{1},u_{1})
and
(q_{2},u_{2})
are strong solutions of (
1
)–(
4
) with same initial data. Let
q=q_{1}-q_{2},u=u_{1}-u_{2}
and
p=p_{1}-p_{2}
. Then
q
satisfies

(208)

\partial_{t}q+\Lambda q=-u_{1}\cdot\nabla q-u\cdot\nabla q_{2}

and
u
satisfies

(209)

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla p=-qRq_{1}-q_{2}Rq-u_{1}\cdot\nabla u-u\cdot\nabla u_{2}.

We take the
L^{2}
inner product of (
208
) with
\Lambda^{-1}q
and the
L^{2}
inner product of (
209
) with
u
. We add the resulting energy equalities. We have a cancellation

(210)

-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla q_{2})\Lambda^{-1}qdx-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(q_{2}Rq)\cdot udx=0

obtained from integration by parts. In view of the Ladyzhenskaya’s interpolation inequality, we estimate

(211)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(qRq_{1})\cdot udx\right|\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

(212)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u\cdot\nabla u_{2})\cdot udx\right|\leq\|u\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now we write

(213)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)\Lambda^{-1}qdx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\right)\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}qdx

via integration by parts, and we show below that

(214)

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\right)\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}qdx\right|\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}.

Putting (
210
)-(
214
) together, we obtain the energy inequality

(215)

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left[\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\right]\left[\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

from which we obtain uniqueness.
Finally, we show that the estimate (
214
) holds by establishing the commutator estimate

(216)

\|\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}.

Indeed, let
w\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. By Parseval’s identity, we have

(217)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q)(x)w(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mathcal{F}(\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q)(\xi)\mathcal{F}w(\xi)d\xi.

But

(218)

\mathcal{F}(\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q))(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\xi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\xi\cdot\mathcal{F}u_{1}(\xi-y))\mathcal{F}q(y)dy

and

(219)

\mathcal{F}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q)(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|y|^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\xi\cdot\mathcal{F}u_{1}(\xi-y))\mathcal{F}q(y)dy.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q)(x)w(x)dx\right|

(220)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\min\left\{|\xi|,|y|\right\}\left||\xi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}-|y|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right||\mathcal{F}u_{1}(\xi-y)||\mathcal{F}q(y)||\mathcal{F}w(\xi)|dyd\xi

where we used

(221)

|\xi\cdot\mathcal{F}u_{1}(\xi-y)|\leq\min\left\{|\xi|,|y|\right\}|\mathcal{F}u_{1}(\xi-y)|

which holds due to the fact that the velocity is divergence-free.
We note that

(222)

\min\left\{|\xi|,|y|\right\}\left||\xi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}-|y|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right|\leq\frac{\min\left\{|\xi|,|y|\right\}}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}|y|^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\xi-y|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|\xi-y|^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
\xi,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u_{1}\cdot\nabla q)-u_{1}\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}q)(x)w(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\||.|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{F}u_{1}(.)\|_{L^{1}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}

(223)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{H^{2}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}

by Hölder’s inequality and Young’s convolution inequality. This gives (
216
) completing the proof of Theorem
6
.

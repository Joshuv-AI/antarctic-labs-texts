Abstract
. In [ 7 , Rem. 3.4] the authors asked the question if any étale subalgebra of an étale algebra in a braided fusion category is also étale. We give a positive answer to this question if the braided fusion category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is pseudo-unitary and non-degenerate. In the case of a pseudo-unitary fusion category we also give a new description of the lattice correspondence from [ 6 , Theorem 4.10] . This new description enables us to describe the two binary operations on the lattice of fusion subcategories.



1.
Introduction

In
[
7
, Rem. 3.4]
the authors asked the question if any étale subalgebra of an étale algebra in a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is also étale. It seems that an answer to this question does not appear in
the literature at this moment. We give a positive answer to this question if the braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is pseudo-unitary and non-degenerate.

For any fusion category
\mathcal{C}
it was shown in
[
6
, Lemma 3.5]
that
{\mathrm{A}}=R(\mathbf{1})
is a connected étale algebra in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
where
R
is a right adjoint of the forgetful functor
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, in
[
6
, Theorem 4.10]
it was shown that the lattice of fusion subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
is in a reverse one to one correspondence with the lattice of étale subalgebras of
{\mathrm{A}}
.
{\mathrm{A}}
is sometimes called
the adjoint algebra
of
\mathcal{C}
in
[
18
]
.

Recall that given a connected étale subalgebra
{\mathrm{L}}
of
{\mathrm{A}}
in
[
6
]
it was associated a fusion subcategory
\beta({\mathrm{L}})
of
\mathcal{C}
. Under the tensor equivalence
R:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}_{\mathrm{A}}
the fusion subcategory
\beta({\mathrm{L}})
consists of those
{\mathrm{A}}
-modules in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
that are dyslectic as right
{\mathrm{L}}
-modules.

For a pivotal tensor categories, Shimizu has developed in
[
18
]
a character theory for fusion categories, similar to the classical theory of representations of finite groups and semisimple Hopf algebras. Moreover he showed that the adjoint algebra
{\mathrm{A}}
associated to a fusion category has a natural action on any object of
\mathcal{C}
.

The main goal of this paper is to show that in the case of a pseudo-unitary fusion category
\mathcal{C}
all unitary subalgebras of
{\mathrm{A}}
in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
are connected étale. We also obtain a new characterization of the fusion subcategories from
[
6
]
associated to étale subalgebras of
{\mathrm{A}}
.

In Section
3
, in the case
\mathcal{C}
is a pseudo-unitary fusion category, we associate to any unitary subalgebra
{\mathrm{L}}
of
{\mathrm{A}}
in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
a fusion subcategory
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}
of
\mathcal{C}
, consisting of those simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
whose characters (as defined in
[
18
]
) have trivial restriction to
{\mathrm{L}}
. We also denote by
{{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}}
the full abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of those objects that receive a trivial action from the subalgebra
{\mathrm{L}}
, see Definition
3.4
.

It is shown that for any pivotal fusion category
\mathcal{C}
one has a chain of inclusions of abelian full subcategories:

(1.1)

\beta({\mathrm{L}})\subseteq{{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}.

On the other hand, we show in Theorem
3.2
that if
\mathcal{C}
is a pseudo-unitary fusion category then
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}})=\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathrm{L}})}
.
Since by
[
6
, Theorem 4.10]
we also have
\mathrm{FPdim}(\beta({\mathrm{L}}))=\frac{\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})}{\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathrm{L}})}
this shows that the above chain inclusions of fusion subcategories are in fact equalities. This chain of equalities implies that
{{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}}
is also a fusion subcategory and that any unitary connected subalgebra of
{\mathrm{A}}
is also an étale subalgebra of
{\mathrm{A}}
. These facts can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Any fusion subcategory of a pseudo-unitary fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is of the form
{{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}}
for some unitary subalgebra
{\mathrm{L}}
of the algebra
{\mathrm{A}}:=R(\mathbf{1})
. Moreover, every such unitary subalgebra
{\mathrm{L}}
of
{\mathrm{A}}
is étale.

From Theorem
1.1
, using some results concerning étale algebras developed in
[
6
]
we deduce that in a non-degenerate braided pseudo-unitary fusion category, any subalgebra of an étale algebra is also étale. More precisely we prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pseudo-unitary non-degenerate braided fusion category and
A
an étale algebra of
{\mathcal{B}}
. Then any unitary subalgebra
L
of
A
is also étale.

For two subalgebras of the adjoint algebra
{\mathrm{A}}
we denote by
{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{M}}
the image of
{\mathrm{L}}\otimes{\mathrm{M}}
under the multiplication
m:{\mathrm{A}}\otimes{\mathrm{A}}\rightarrow{\mathrm{A}}
. Since
{\mathrm{A}}
is commutative it follows that
{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{M}}={\mathrm{M}}{\mathrm{L}}
and moreover
{\mathrm{M}}{\mathrm{L}}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathrm{A}}
. Concerning the two binary operations on the lattice of fusion subcategories we prove the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pseudo-unitary fusion category and
{\mathrm{L}},{\mathrm{M}}
two unitary subalgebras of the adjoint algebra
{\mathrm{A}}
of
\mathcal{C}
. With the above notations one has

{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{M}}=\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{M}}},\;{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}\vee\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{M}}=\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}}.

Shortly, the organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we recall the basics on fusion categories and also the character theory developed by Shimizu in
[
18
]
for pivotal fusion categories. In Section
3
we construct the fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}}
mentioned above in the pivotal case. We also define for any fusion category the full abelian subcategory
{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}
and prove the inclusion
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}}\supseteq{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}
from Equation (
1.1
). In Section
4
we prove the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}\supseteq\beta({\mathrm{L}})
from Equation (
1.1
). Also in this section we give the proofs of the above three theorems. In Subsection
4.6
we also consider as an example the case of fusion categories coming from representation categories semisimple Hopf algebras.

We work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. All the Hopf algebras and fusion categories notations follow
[
9
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we review the basic properties of fusion categories that are needed through the paper.
For the definition and standard theory of monoidal categories, we refer the reader to
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
. Given a monoidal category we define by
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
the class of all its objects.
Recall that a
left dual object
of
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
is a triple
(X^{*},{\ev_{X}},\mathrm{coev}_{X})
consisting of an object
X^{*}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})

and morphisms
{\ev_{X}}:X^{*}\otimes X\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
and
\mathrm{coev}_{X}:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow X\otimes X^{*}
such that the following equalities are satisfied:

(2.1)

({\ev_{X}}\otimes\id_{{X^{*}}})\circ(\id_{{X^{*}}}\otimes d)=\id_{{X^{*}}},\;\;(\id_{X}\otimes{\ev_{X}})\circ(\mathrm{coev}_{X}\otimes\id_{X})=\id_{X}.

Similarly, one can define a
right dual
of
X
(which in fact is a left dual of
X
in
\mathcal{C}^{rev}
). A monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
rigid
if every object of
\mathcal{C}
has both a left and a right dual object.

Recall that a
finite tensor category

[
11
]
over a field
\Bbbk
is a rigid monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}
is a finite abelian category, the tensor product
\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is
\Bbbk
-linear in each variable, and
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1})\simeq\Bbbk
as algebras. A
fusion category

[
10
]
is a semisimple finite tensor category.

For a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, the
left monoidal center
(or the Drinfeld center) of
\mathcal{C}
is a category
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
defined as follows: An object of
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is a pair
(V,\sigma_{V})
consisting of an object
V\in\mathcal{C}
and a natural isomorphism

\sigma_{V,X}:V\otimes X\rightarrow X\otimes V

for all
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, satisfying a part of the hexagon axiom. A morphism
f:(V,\sigma_{V})\rightarrow(W,\sigma_{W})
in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}

is a morphism in
\mathcal{C}
such that
(\id_{X}\otimes f)\circ\sigma_{V,X}=\sigma_{W,X}\circ(f\otimes\id_{X})
for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
. The composition of morphisms is defined in an obvious way. The category
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is in fact a braided monoidal category, see, e.g.,
[
13
, Chapt. XIII.3]
for details.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be any finite tensor category and
A
be an algebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Recall that the algebra
A
is called
connected
if
\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},A)=\Bbbk
. A subalgebra
B\xrightarrow{\iota}A
is called
unitary
if

u_{B}\circ\iota=u_{A}
where
u_{B}
and
u_{A}
are the units of
B
and
A
.
Recall
[
6
]
that an algebra
A
of a braided fusion category
{\mathcal{B}}
is called
étale
if it is
separable
and connected. An algebra
A
is called separable if its multiplication

A\otimes A\xrightarrow{m}A
has a section as
A
-bimodules in
\mathcal{C}
.
This is equivalent by
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
to the fact that the category of right (or left)
A
-modules
{\mathcal{B}}_{A}
is a semisimple category.

Let
A
be an algebra in a braided fusion category
{\mathcal{B}}
and
M
be a right
A
-module in
{\mathcal{B}}
. Then
M
is called
dyslectic (local)
if
c_{A,M}\circ c_{M,A}\circ\rho_{M}=\rho_{M}
where
\rho_{M}:M\otimes A\rightarrow M
is the module structure of
M
.

Dyslectic modules form a full subcategory of
{\mathcal{B}}_{A}
which is usually denoted by
{\mathcal{B}}^{0}_{A}
. This subcategory is closed under
\otimes_{A}
and the braiding in
{\mathcal{B}}
induces a natural braided structure in
{\mathcal{B}}^{0}_{A}
, see
[
15
, Section 2]
. Thus,
{\mathcal{B}}^{0}_{A}

is a braided fusion category.

2.1.
The central Hopf comonad of a finite tensor category

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category. The forgetful functor
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
admits a right adjoint functor
R:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
and
Z:=FR:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is a Hopf comonad called
the central Hopf comonad associated
to
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, one has that

(2.2)

Z(V)\simeq\int_{X\in\mathcal{C}}X\otimes V\otimes X^{*}.

see
[
18
, Section 2.6]
. We denote by
\pi_{V;X}:Z(V)\rightarrow X\otimes V\otimes X^{*}
the universal dinatural transformation associated to the end
Z(V)
.
The Hopf comonad structure of
Z
can be described in terms of the dinatural transformation
\pi
. The comultiplication
\delta:Z\rightarrow{Z}^{2}
is the unique natural transformation such that

(2.3)

(\id_{X}\otimes\pi_{V;Y}\otimes\id_{X^{*}})\circ\pi_{Z(V);X}\circ\delta_{V}=\pi_{V;X\otimes Y}

The counit of
{\bf{\epsilon}}:Z\rightarrow\id_{\mathcal{C}}
is given by
{\bf\epsilon}_{V}:=\pi_{V;1}.

There is a lax monoidal structure defined on
Z
by

Z_{2}(M,N):Z(M)\otimes Z(N)\rightarrow Z(M\otimes N)

(2.4)

\pi_{M\otimes N,\;X}\circ Z_{2}(M,N)=(\id_{X\otimes M}\otimes\ev_{X}\otimes\id_{N\otimes X^{*}})\circ({\pi}_{M;\;X}\otimes{\pi}_{N;\;X})

2.2.
On the adjoint algebra of a finite tensor category

It is known that
{\mathrm{A}}:=R(\mathbf{1})
has the structure of central commutative algebra in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
. The half braiding of
{\mathrm{A}}
, denoted by
c_{A,X}
, is defined by

A\otimes X\xrightarrow{\delta_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes\id_{X}}Z(A)\otimes X\xrightarrow{\pi_{Z(\mathbf{1}),X}\otimes\id_{X}}X\otimes A\otimes X^{*}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\id_{X}\otimes\id_{A}\otimes\ev_{X}}X\otimes A.

The multiplication
m:{\mathrm{A}}\otimes{\mathrm{A}}\rightarrow{\mathrm{A}}
and the unit
u_{\mathrm{A}}:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow{\mathrm{A}}
of the
adjoint algebra
{\mathrm{A}}
are uniquely determined by by the universal property of the end
A=Z(\mathbf{1})
as:

(2.5)

\pi_{\mathbf{1};X}\circ u_{\mathrm{A}}=\mathrm{coev}_{X},

(2.6)

\pi_{\mathbf{1};X}\circ m=(\id_{X}\otimes\ev_{X}\otimes\id_{X^{*}})\circ(\pi_{\mathbf{1};X}\otimes\pi_{\mathbf{1};X}).

Moreover
{\bf\epsilon}_{\mathbf{1}}:{\mathrm{A}}\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
is a morphism of algebras, see
[
18
]
.

Any object
X\in\mathcal{C}
is canonically an
A
-module in
\mathcal{C}
, via the morphism:

(2.7)

\alpha_{X}:A\otimes X\xrightarrow{\pi_{\mathbf{1};X}\otimes\id_{X}}X\otimes X^{*}\otimes X\xrightarrow{\id_{X}\otimes ev_{X}}X.

We denote by
c_{R(M),X}:R(M)\otimes X\rightarrow X\otimes R(M)
the half braiding of
R(M)\in{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
. Recall that with the above braiding
{\mathrm{A}}=R(\mathbf{1})
becomes a commutative algebra in the center
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
.
Moreover, by
[
18
, Equation (3.12)]
one has that

(2.8)

\alpha_{X}=(A\otimes X\xrightarrow{c_{{\mathrm{A}},X}}X\otimes A\xrightarrow{\id_{X}\otimes\epsilon_{\mathbf{1}}}X).

The vector space
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}:=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},A)
is called
the set of central elements.
For
a,b\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
we set
ab:=m\circ(a\otimes b)
. Then the set
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
is a monoid with respect to this operation. Note that for the algebra unit
u_{\mathrm{A}}:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow{\mathrm{A}}
of the algebra
{\mathrm{A}}
to the unit of the monoid
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
is
F(u_{\mathrm{A}})
.

2.3.
Internal characters for pivotal strctures

Recall that a pivotal structure
j
on a tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is a tensor isomorphism
j:\id_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow(-)^{**}
. Using the pivotal structure one can construct a
right evaluation
as follows:

\widetilde{ev}_{X}:X\otimes X^{*}\xrightarrow{j\otimes id}X^{**}\otimes X^{*}\xrightarrow{ev_{X^{*}}}\mathbf{1}.

Then
the right partial pivotal trace
of
f:A\otimes X\rightarrow B\otimes X
is defined as follows:

(2.9)

\mathrm{tr}_{A,B}^{X}:A=A\otimes\mathbf{1}\xrightarrow{\id_{A}\otimes coev_{X}}A\otimes X\otimes X^{*}\xrightarrow{f\otimes id}B\otimes X\otimes X^{*}\xrightarrow{\id_{B}\otimes\widetilde{ev}_{X}}B.

The usual
right pivotal trace
of an endomorphism
f:X\rightarrow X
is obtained as a particular case for
A=B=\mathbf{1}
. In particular, the
right pivotal dimension
\dim^{r}(X)
of
X
is defined as the right trace of the identity of
X
. A pivotal structure a on a tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is called
spherical
if

\dim(V)=\dim(V^{*})
for any object
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
.
Given an object
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
the internal character
\mathrm{ch}(X)
is defined as the morphism

\mathrm{ch}(X):=\mathrm{tr}^{X}_{A,\mathbf{1}}(\alpha_{X}):A\rightarrow\mathbf{1}.

Therefore using Equation (
2.9
) one can write that

(2.10)

\mathrm{ch}(X)=A\otimes\mathbf{1}\xrightarrow{\id\otimes coev_{X}}A\otimes X\otimes X^{*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{X}\otimes id}X\otimes X^{*}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{ev}_{X}}\mathbf{1}.

Then the space
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C}):=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,\mathbf{1})
is called the
space of class functions
of
\mathcal{C}
. For two class functions
f,g\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
one can define a multiplication by

f\star g:=f\circ Z(g)\circ\delta_{\mathbf{1}}.

Here
\delta:Z\rightarrow Z^{2}
is the comultiplication structure of
Z
defined in the Equation (
2.3
). By
[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
one has that
\mathrm{ch}(X\otimes Y)=\mathrm{ch}(X)\mathrm{ch}(Y)
for any two objects
X
and
Y
of
\mathcal{C}
. For a finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
the space of class functions
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
is a finite-dimensional algebra.

2.4.
The cointegral and integral of a fusion category

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category and
A=Z(\mathbf{1})
be its adjoint algebra as defined above.

An
integral
in
\mathcal{C}
is a morphism
\Lambda:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow A
in
\mathcal{C}
such that

m\circ(\id_{A}\otimes\Lambda)={\bf\epsilon}_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes\Lambda.

A
cointegral
in
\mathcal{C}
is a morphism
\lambda:A\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
such that

Z(\lambda)\circ\delta_{\mathbf{1}}=u\otimes\lambda

where
u:\mathbf{1}\rightarrow A
is the unit of the algebra
A
. It is well known that the integral and cointegral of a finite unimodular tensor category are unique up to a scalar, see
[
18
]
.

2.5.
Fourier transform for finite tensor categories

Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
be a non-zero integral of
\mathcal{C}
. The
Fourier transform for finite tensor categories
of
\mathcal{C}
associated to
\lambda
is the linear map

(2.11)

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}:\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\;\;\text{given by}\;\;a\mapsto\lambda\leftharpoonup\mathcal{S}(a)

where
\mathcal{S}:\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
is the antipodal operator on
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
, see
[
18
, Definition 3.6]
. The Fourier transform is a bijective
\Bbbk
-linear map whose inverse is given in
[
18
, Equation (5.17)]
. Here, the right action denoted by
\leftharpoonup
of
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
on
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
given by
f\leftharpoonup b=f\circ m\circ(b\otimes\id_{A})
for all
f\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
and
b\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
. There is also a non-degenerate evaluation pairing
\langle,\;\rangle_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{A}}}}
given by

(2.12)

\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{A}}}}:\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\otimes\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\Bbbk,\;\langle\chi,z\rangle\mapsto\chi\circ z.

Fourier transform for fusion categories

For the rest of this section, suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a pivotal fusion category over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
. Furthermore, let
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C}):=\{V_{0},\dots,V_{m}\}
be a complete set of representatives of isomorphism classes of simple objects with
V_{0}=\mathbf{1}
, the unit object. For
i\in\{0,\dots,m\}
, we define
i^{*}\in\{0,\dots,m\}
by
V_{i}^{*}\simeq V_{i^{*}}
. Then
i\mapsto i^{*}
is an involution on
\{0,\dots,m\}
.
As an object of
\mathcal{C}
, the adjoint algebra decomposes as

(2.13)

A\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}V_{i}\otimes V_{i}^{*}.

Shimizu has defined in
[
18
]
the elements

E_{i}:\mathbf{1}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{coev}_{V_{i}}}V_{i}\otimes V_{i}^{*}\hookrightarrow A,\;\;\chi_{i}:A\xrightarrow{\pi_{i}}V_{i}\otimes V_{i}^{*}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}_{V_{i}}}}\mathbf{1}.

It is easy to see that
\{E_{i}\}_{i=0,\dots,m}
and
\{\chi_{i}\}_{i=0,\dots,m}
are bases for
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
and
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
respectively, such that

(2.14)

\langle\chi_{i},E_{j}\rangle=d_{i}\delta_{i,j}.

where
d_{i}:=\dim(V_{i})
. Moreover,
E_{i}E_{j}=\delta_{i,j}
and
\mathcal{S}(E_{i})=E_{i^{*}}
, where
\tilde{S}:\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
is the antipodal mentioned above. The elements
\chi_{i}
are called the irreducible characters of the simple objects
V_{i}
and
E_{i}\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
their corresponding primitive idempotents.
Note that
E_{0}
, the idempotent associated to the unit object
\mathbf{1}
of
\mathcal{C}
, is the idempotent integral
\Lambda\in\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
, see
[
18
, Lemma 6.1]
. By
[
18
, Equation (6.8)]
one has that the idempotent cointegral of
\mathcal{C}
can be written as:

(2.15)

\lambda_{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{1}{\dim(\mathcal{C})}(\sum_{[V_{i}]\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}d_{i^{*}}\chi_{i}).

Since
\Lambda=E_{0}
it follows by Equation (
2.15
) that

\langle\lambda_{\mathcal{C}},\Lambda\rangle=\frac{1}{\dim(\mathcal{C})}
.
It also follows that

(2.16)

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{-1}(\chi_{i})=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{d_{i}}E_{i^{*}}

This equation was proven in
[
18
, Equation (6.10)]
under the hypothesis that the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{C}
is commutative but the proof works word by word also in the case of an arbitrary pivotal fusion category, with the not necessarily commutative Grothendieck ring.

For a spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
one has
\dim(V_{i^{*}})=\dim(V_{i})
and it follows from
[
5
, Equation (4.7)]
that
:

(2.17)

\langle\chi,\;\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}^{-1}(\mu)\rangle_{\mathrm{A}}=\dim(\mathcal{C})\tau(\chi\mu).

for any
\chi,\mu\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
.

2.6.
On the inclusion of class functions of a fusion subcategory

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a fusion subcategory of a given fusion category
\mathcal{C}
. As in
[
18
, Sect. 4.3]
, for any object
V
of
\mathcal{C}
the end

\bar{Z}(V):{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C},\;\;\bar{Z}(V):=\int_{X\in{\mathcal{D}}}X\otimes V\otimes X^{*}

exists
and we denote by
\bar{\pi}_{\mathbf{1}\;X}:{\bar{Z}(\mathbf{1})}\rightarrow X\otimes X^{*}
the universal dinatural maps defining this end. From the universal property of
{\bar{Z}(\mathbf{1})}
there is a unique canonical map
Z(V)\xrightarrow{q_{V}}{\bar{\mathrm{Z}}}(V)
in
\mathcal{C}
, such that
\bar{\pi}_{V;\;X}\circ q={\pi}_{V;\;X}
for any object
X
of
{\mathcal{D}}
. In
[

, Appendix]
it is shown that
q:Z(\mathbf{1})\rightarrow{\bar{Z}(\mathbf{1})}
induces a map
q_{\mathbf{1}}^{*}:\mathrm{CF}({\mathcal{D}})\rightarrow\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C}),\;\chi\mapsto\chi\circ q_{\mathbf{1}}

2.7.
The adjunction isomorphisms for
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}

Let
\mathcal{C}
be any fusion category and
R:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
be a right adjoint of the forgetful functor as in the previous section. It is known that
Z:=FR
is a Hopf comonad in
\mathcal{C}
. One has that the category of
Z
-comodules in
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the center
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
, see
[
8
]
and
[
2
]
. Moreover, in this case, the comodule forgetful functor
\mathcal{C}^{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
identifies to
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. This shows that the adjunction isomorphisms are given by

(2.18)

\psi_{V,\mathbf{1}}:\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(V),\mathbf{1})\rightarrow\mathrm{{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}(V,{\mathrm{A}}),\;f\mapsto R(f)\circ\eta_{V},

and its inverse is given by

(2.19)

\psi_{V,\mathbf{1}}^{-1}:\mathrm{{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}(V,{\mathrm{A}})\rightarrow\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(F(V),\mathbf{1}),\;g\mapsto{\bf\epsilon}_{\mathbf{1}}\circ F(g).

for any object
V\in{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
. Here
\eta_{V}:V\rightarrow R(F(V))
is the unit of the adjunction which coincides to the
Z
-comodule structure of
V\in{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
.

2.8.
Frobenius-Perron dimensions and pseudo-unitarity

For a simple object
X
of a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
the Frobenius-Perron eigenvalue of the left
multiplication by
[X]
on the Grothendieck ring
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
is denoted by
\mathrm{FPdim}(X)
and it is called the
Frobenius-Perron
dimension
of
X
. Recall that
\mathrm{FPdim}(X)
is a positive real algebraic number and the Frobenius-Perron dimension extends linearly to an algebra morphism
\mathrm{FPdim}:K_{0}(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. The Frobenius-Perron dimension
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}\mathrm{FPdim}(X)^{2}.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called
pseudo-unitary
if
\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=\dim(\mathcal{C})
. In this case,
\mathcal{C}
admits a unique (canonical) spherical structure with respect to
which the categorical dimensions of simple objects are all positive, see
[
10
, Proposition 8.23]
. With respect to this spherical structure, the categorical dimension of any object coincides with its Frobenius-Perron dimension, i.e.
\mathrm{FPdim}(X)=\dim(X)
for any object
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
. Moreover, every full fusion
subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
is pseudo-unitary.

Define also a

of

\psi_{{\mathrm{L}},\mathbf{1}}:\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(L,\mathbf{1})\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}}({\mathrm{L}},{\mathrm{A}}),\;\;\mu\mapsto R(\mu)\circ\eta_{\mathrm{L}}.

where

Moreover, its inverse is given by

Naturality in the first variable of the adjunction isomorphisms

of

(3.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{CF}}(L)=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(L,\mathbf{1})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Res}^{A}_{L}}

Proposition 3.17
.

For any two irreducible characters

one has that
\chi_{i},\chi_{j}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{s}
for some

if and only if

\frac{{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi_{i})}{d_{i}}=\frac{{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi_{j})}{d_{j}}.

Proof.

Let
{\eta_{s}}\in{\mathrm{CF}}(L)
be the dual basis of
\ell_{s}
with respect to the non-degenerate pairing
\langle,\;\rangle_{\mathrm{L}}

\langle\eta_{t},\;\ell_{s}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}}=\delta_{s,t}

. We will show that if
\chi_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{s}
then

(3.18)

\frac{{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi_{i})}{d_{i}}={\eta_{s}}.

This would finish the proof of the proposition.

Suppose now that
\chi_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{s}
. Then by Equation (
3.15
) one has

\langle{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi_{i}),\ell_{s}\rangle_{\mathrm{L}}=\langle\chi_{i},\;{\iota^{e}}(\ell_{s})\rangle_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{A}}}}=\langle\chi_{i},\;\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{B}}_{s}}E_{j}\rangle_{\mathrm{{\mathrm{A}}}}=d_{i}.

Therefore
\frac{1}{d_{i}}{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi_{i})={\eta_{s}}
for any irreducible character
\chi_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{s}
.

Let

On the full subcategory
{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}

We denote by
{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}
the full abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
generated by the simple objects
V_{i}
of
\mathcal{C}
whose irreducible characters
\chi_{i}
satisfy
\chi_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{0}

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pseudo-unitary fusion category and
{\mathrm{L}}
be a unitary subalgebra of the adjoint algebra
{\mathrm{A}}
of
\mathcal{C}
. With the above notations one has that

{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}}
is a fusion subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
M,M^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}({\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}})
are two simple objects with characters
\chi_{M}=\chi,\;\chi_{M^{\prime}}=\chi^{\prime}
.
Then
{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi)=d(\chi){\underline{\epsilon}_{L}}
and
{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi^{\prime})=d(\chi){\underline{\epsilon}_{L}}
. It follows that

{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi\star\chi^{\prime})=\chi.{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi^{\prime})=d(\chi^{\prime})\chi.{\underline{\epsilon}_{L}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle(\mkern-1.5mu\ref{chdeps}\mkern-1.5mu)}}{{=}}d(\chi){\mathrm{Res}}(\chi^{\prime})=d(\chi)d(\chi^{\prime}){\underline{\epsilon}_{L}}

On the other hand suppose that

\chi\star\chi^{\prime}=\sum_{\chi_{u}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}N^{u}_{M,M^{\prime}}\chi_{u}
with some
N^{u}_{M,M^{\prime}}\geq 0
.
It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi\star\chi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi_{u}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{C})}N^{u}_{M,M^{\prime}}{\mathrm{Res}}(\chi_{u})=\sum_{s=0}^{r}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{B}_{s}}N^{u}_{M,M^{\prime}}d_{u}{\mathrm{Res}}(\frac{\chi_{u}}{d_{u}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\scriptscriptstyle(\mkern-1.5mu\ref{resd}\mkern-1.5mu)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{r}\big(\sum_{u\in\mathcal{B}_{s}}N^{u}_{M,M^{\prime}}d_{u}\big){\eta_{s}}

where
\{\eta_{s}\}
is the dual basis of
\ell_{s}
with respect to the pairing
\langle,\;\rangle_{\mathrm{L}}
. Since
\eta_{s}
are linearly independent it follows that for any
s\neq 0
one has

\sum_{u\in\mathcal{B}_{s}}N^{u}_{M,M^{\prime}}d_{u}=0

Since
\mathcal{C}
is pseudo-unitary one has
d_{u}>0
and therefore in this case
N^{u}_{M,M^{\prime}}=0
for any
u\in\mathcal{B}_{s}
. Thus all the simple constituents of
M\otimes M^{\prime}
are in full abelian subcategory
{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}}
.
∎

On the
\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}})
.

In this subsection we prove the following:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pseudo-unitary fusion category and
{\mathrm{L}}
be a unitary subalgebra of the adjoint algebra
{\mathrm{A}}
of
\mathcal{C}
. With the above notations one has that

\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{L}}})=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim({\mathrm{L}})}.

First we need to fix several notations. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category and
F:{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
be the forgetful functor. As above, let
R
be a right adjoint of
F
. We may suppose that
{\mathrm{A}}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{r}{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}}
is the decomposition of
{\mathrm{A}}
in homogenous components in
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
. Define
\mathcal{J}:=\{0,\dots,r\}
the set of indices of homogenous components of
{\mathrm{A}}
.

We may write each homogenous component as

{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}}=\bigoplus_{s=1}^{m_{j}}{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s}}

where
{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s}}
are the simple sub-objects of
{\mathrm{A}}
entering in the homogenous component
{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}}
. Therefore as an object of
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
one has a decomposition in simple objects

(3.23)

{\mathrm{A}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\bigoplus_{s\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s}}

where
{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s}}
and
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{t}
are isomorphic simple
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
-submodules of
{\mathrm{A}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{j}:=\{1,\dots,m_{j}\}
. One has
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}({\mathcal{C}^{(j)}})\simeq M_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)
as algebras and therefore

\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}({\mathrm{A}})\simeq\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}}M_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk).

By the natural isomorphism
{\psi_{{\mathrm{A}},\mathbf{1}}}
from Equation (
3.4
) one also has that

\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}}M_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)

as a semisimple algebra.

Without loss of generality we may also suppose that
\mathcal{C}^{(0)}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
is the simple trivial object of
{\mathrm{A}}
. Since
\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}({\mathrm{A}},\mathbf{1})\simeq\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})=\Bbbk
it follows that

.

Define
\widetilde{F^{j}_{st}}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}({\mathrm{A}})
as the unique endomorphisms of
{\mathrm{A}}
that send (as identity)
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{t}
into
{\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s}}
and that are zero on the other conjugacy classes
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{t^{\prime}}
with

. Then these endomorphisms form a linear basis
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}({\mathrm{A}})
such as

\widetilde{F^{j}_{st}}\circ\widetilde{F^{j^{\prime}}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\delta_{s^{\prime},t}\widetilde{F^{j}_{st^{\prime}}}

Denote
F^{j}_{st}:=\psi_{{\mathrm{A}},\mathbf{1}}^{-1}(\widetilde{F^{j}_{st}})\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
. It follows that
F^{j}_{st}
are the standard matrix entries in the matrix-block
M_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)
. Thus

(3.24)

F^{j}_{st}F^{j^{\prime}}_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}=\delta_{j,j^{\prime}}\delta_{s^{\prime},t}F^{j}_{st^{\prime}},

and the corresponding primitive central idempotent corresponding to the unit of the block is
F^{j}=\sum_{s=0}^{m_{j}}F^{j}_{ss}
. Note that the decompositions of
{\mathrm{A}}
from Equation (
3.23
) are in bijection with the matrix bases
\{F^{j}_{st}\}
of
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
.

It is well known that the block
\Bbbk=M_{m_{0}}(\Bbbk)
from the above decomposition corresponds to the central primitive idempotent
F^{0}=\lambda
, the cointegral of
\mathcal{C}
.

By
[
18
, Lemma 6.2]
the Grothendieck ring
\mathrm{Gr}_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{C})
is a symmetric Frobenius algebra with non-degenerate trace
\tau:\mathrm{Gr}_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

[X]\mapsto\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},X)
. For a pivotal fusion category one has
\tau([X])=\langle\chi(X),\Lambda\rangle
for any object
X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
, where
\Lambda
is an idempotent cointegral associated to
\mathcal{C}
.

Then the corresponding associative non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})
is given by
\beta_{\tau}(\chi,\mu):=\langle\chi\mu,\Lambda\rangle
. A pair of dual bases for
\beta_{\tau}
is given by
\{\chi_{i},\chi_{i^{*}}\}
.

Suppose that on the matrix-block decomposition of
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})

one has

(3.25)

\beta_{\tau}(\chi,\;\mu)=\sum_{j}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\mathrm{tr}_{j}(\chi\mu).

where
\mathrm{tr}_{j}
is the usual trace on the mtrix algebra
M_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)
. Since
\{F^{j}_{st},\;n_{j}F^{j}_{ts}\}
is another pair of dual bases for
\beta_{\tau}(-,\;-)
one can write:

(3.26)

\sum_{j=0}^{m}\;\sum_{s,t\in\mathcal{M}_{j}}n_{j}F^{j}_{st}\otimes F^{j}_{ts}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\chi_{i}\otimes\chi_{i^{*}}.

Lemma 3.27
.

For a pivotal fusion category, with the above notations one has

(3.28)

n_{j}=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C})}{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s})}.

Proof.

By
[
18
, Proposition 5.17]
one has that for any
f\in\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})

\mathrm{tr}(\psi_{{\mathrm{A}},\mathbf{1}}^{-1}(f))=\langle f,\Lambda\rangle\langle\lambda,u\rangle

where
(\Lambda,\lambda)
is any pair of a integral and cointegral of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\langle\lambda,\Lambda\rangle=1
. One can chose such a pair with
\langle\lambda,u\rangle=\dim(\mathcal{C})
and
\Lambda
an idempotent integral, i.e
{\bf\epsilon}_{\mathbf{1}}(\Lambda)=1
.

In particular, for
f=F^{j}
one has that
\psi_{{\mathrm{A}},\mathbf{1}}^{-1}(F^{j})
is the projection on the homogenous component
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
. Therefore the above trace formula can be written as

m_{j}\dim(\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s})=\mathrm{tr}(\psi_{{\mathrm{A}},\mathbf{1}}^{-1}(F^{j}))=\langle F^{j},\Lambda\rangle\dim(\mathcal{C}).

On the other hand, from Equation (
3.25
) one has

(3.29)

\tau(F^{j}_{ss})=\langle F^{j}_{ss},\Lambda\rangle=\frac{1}{n_{j}}\mathrm{tr}_{j}(F^{j}_{ss})=\frac{1}{n_{j}}

Thus

\langle F^{j},\Lambda\rangle=\tau(F^{j})=\sum_{s=0}^{m_{j}}\tau(F^{j}_{ss})=\frac{m_{j}}{n_{j}}

and the result follows.
∎

, by the associativity of the action
\alpha_{V}
of
A
on
V
.
This shows that
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{M}}}\supseteq\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}\cap\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{M}}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pseudo-unitary fusion category and
{\mathrm{L}},{\mathrm{M}}
be two unitary subalgebras of
{\mathrm{A}}
. Then:

(4.27)

\mathrm{FPdim}({\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}\vee\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{M}})\geq\mathrm{FPdim}(\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}})

Proof.

Suppose
{{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}\vee\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{M}}}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}
for some subalgebra
{\mathrm{P}}\subseteq{\mathrm{A}}
.
One has
{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}
which implies that
{\mathrm{P}}\subseteq{\mathrm{L}}
since
\beta
is a reversing order bijection. Similarly one deduces that
{\mathrm{P}}\subseteq{\mathrm{M}}
, and therefore
P\subseteq{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}
.

Since
{\mathrm{A}}
is a semisimple object, similarly to Corollary
3.60
one can choose a decomposition of
{\mathrm{A}}
from equation (
3.23
) such that

{\mathrm{L}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{L}}}\bigoplus_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{j}}\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s},\;{\mathrm{M}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{M}}}\bigoplus_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{j}}\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s},\;{\mathrm{P}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}}\bigoplus_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{3}_{j}}\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s}.

for some subsets
\mathcal{L}^{i}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{j}=\{0,1,\dots,m_{j}\}
for
i=1,2,3
.
Note also that

{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{L}}\cap\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{M}}}\bigoplus_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{j}\cap\mathcal{L}^{2}_{j}}\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s}.

Since
{\mathrm{P}}\subseteq{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}
it follows that
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{L}}\cap\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{M}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{j}^{3}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{j}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{j}^{2}
for any
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}
.

By Equation (
4.22
) one has

(4.28)

\lambda_{{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{L}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{j}}F^{j}_{ss},\;\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{M}}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{M}}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{2}_{j}}F^{j}_{ss},\;\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{L}}\cap\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{M}}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{j}\cap\mathcal{L}^{2}_{j}}F^{j}_{ss}.

Also

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{P}}}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{3}_{j}\cap\mathcal{L}^{2}_{j}}F^{j}_{ss}.

By Equation (
3.29
) and Equation (
3.28
) one has

\tau(F^{j}_{ss})=\frac{1}{n_{j}}=\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C}^{(j)}_{s})}{\dim(\mathcal{C})}>0

since
\mathcal{C}
is pseudo-unitary.
It follows that

\tau(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}})=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{L}^{3}_{j}}\tau(F^{j}_{ss})=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}}\frac{|\mathcal{L}^{3}_{j}|}{n_{j}}.

Similarly one deduces that

\tau(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}}})=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{L}}\cap\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{M}}}\frac{|\mathcal{L}^{1}_{j}\cap\mathcal{L}^{2}_{j}|}{n_{j}}.

Since
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}\subseteq\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{L}}\cap\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{M}}
and for any
j\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{P}}
one has
\mathcal{L}_{j}^{3}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{j}^{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{j}^{2}

it follows that

(4.29)

\tau(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}})\leq\tau(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}}}).

On the other hand, since

\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}}=\frac{1}{\dim({\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}})}\big(\sum_{\chi_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}})}d_{i}\chi_{i}\big)

we have that
\tau(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}})=\frac{1}{\dim({\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}}})}
since
\tau(\chi_{i})=0
if
\chi_{i}\neq{\bf\epsilon}_{\mathbf{1}}
.
Similarly

\tau(\lambda_{\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}}})=\frac{1}{\dim({\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}}})}
. Then Equation (
4.29
) can be written as

\frac{1}{\dim({\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}}})}\geq\frac{1}{\dim(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{P}})}.

which proves the lemma.
∎

4.6.
Examples from semisimple Hopf algebras

Let
H
be a semisimple Hopf algebra and let
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Rep}(H)
be the fusion category of its finite dimensional representations over
\Bbbk
. As in
[
18
, Section 3.7]
we identify the left monoidal center of
\mathcal{C}
with the category
{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}\simeq^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
of left-left Yetter-Drinfeld modules of
H
. The objects of the former category are pairs
(V,\delta)
where
V
is a left
H
-modules and
\delta:V\rightarrow H\otimes V
defines a left
H
-comodules structure on
V
such that

(hv){{}_{-1}}\otimes(hv){{}_{0}}=h_{1}v{{}_{-1}}S(h_{3})\otimes h_{2}v{{}_{0}}

for any
h\in H
and any
v\in V
. A morphism in the category
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
is a linear map which is simultaneously a morphism of left
H
-modules and of left
H
-comodules. The left half-braiding of
V\in\;^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
can be written as

c_{V,M}:V\otimes M\rightarrow M\otimes V,\;v\otimes m\mapsto v_{-1}m\otimes v_{0}

for any object
M\in\mathrm{Rep}(H)
.
The forgetful functor

F:^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}\rightarrow H-\text{mod}

admits a right adjoint

R:H-\text{mod}\rightarrow^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}

which can be written as
R(M)=H\otimes M
with

h.(l\otimes m)=h_{1}lS(h_{3})\otimes h_{2}m,\;\delta(h\otimes m)=h_{1}\otimes(h_{2}\otimes m).

Thus, with the above notations, the Hopf comonad
Z:=FR
can be written as
Z(M)=F(R(M))=H\otimes M
with the left
H
-module structure defined above. Then the left half-braiding of
R(M)
becomes:

c_{{Z(M)},X}:{Z(M)}\otimes X\rightarrow X\otimes{Z(M)},\;(h\otimes m)\otimes x\mapsto h_{1}x\otimes(h_{2}\otimes m)

for any
X\in\mathrm{Rep}(H)
.
In particular one has that
{\mathrm{A}}:=R(\mathbf{1})
is the pair
(H_{\ad},\Delta)
where
H_{\ad}
coincides to
H
as vector spaces, and the structure as left
H
-module on
H_{\ad}
is given by the left adjoint action
h.a=h_{1}aS(h_{2})
. Moreover the left comodule structure on
H_{\ad}
is given by the comultiplication
\Delta:H_{\ad}\rightarrow H\otimes H_{\ad}
. The multiplication and the unit of
R(\mathbf{1})
are given the usual multiplication and unit of
H
. The universal dinatural projections from Subsection
2.1
are given by:

\pi_{\mathbf{1};\;M}:H_{\ad}\rightarrow M\otimes M^{*},\;\;h\mapsto\sum_{i}hm_{i}\otimes m_{i}^{*},

where
m_{i}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear basis on
M
and
\{m_{i}^{*}\}
is the corresponding dual basis in
M^{*}
.
In this case the natural action of
A=H_{\ad}
from Equation (
2.7
) can be written as
\alpha_{M}:A\otimes M\rightarrow M,\alpha_{M}(a\otimes m)=a.m
, for each object
M\in\mathrm{Rep}(H)
. Moreover, by considering the standard pivotal structure on
\mathrm{Rep}(H)
, it follows that
\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})=C(H)
, the character ring of
H
, and
\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}=Z(H)
, the center of
H
.

In this case, unitary subalgebras of
{\mathrm{A}}\in{\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})}
are in bijection with the left normal coideal subalgebras of
H
.
Recall that
{\mathrm{L}}\subseteq H
is a left normal coideal subalgebra of
H
if it is a subalgebra of
H
with
\Delta({\mathrm{L}})\subseteq H\otimes{\mathrm{L}}
and
{\mathrm{L}}
is closed under the left adjoint action.

Moreover, for a semisimple Hopf algebra
H
it follows that
\beta({\mathrm{L}})={\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{L}}}}={\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{L}}^{\mathrm{triv}}}=\mathrm{Rep}(H//{\mathrm{L}})
is the fusion subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}(H)
consisting of those
H
-modules receiving trivial action from
{\mathrm{L}}
. The above equalities in this case were previously shown in
[
4
]
.

The character space
{\mathrm{CF}}(L):=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{H}(L,\mathbf{1})
defined in Section
3
it can be described by
\mu\in{\mathrm{CF}}(L)\iff\mu(h_{1}lS(h_{2}))=\epsilon(h)\mu(l)
, for all
h\in H
and
l\in L
. Note that there is an inclusion of

{\mathrm{CF}}(L):=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{H}(L,\mathbf{1})\xhookrightarrow{\nu}\mathrm{{Hom}}_{L}(L,\mathbf{1})=C(L),

into the character space (trace space)
C(L)
of left
L
-modules.

Denote by
{\mathrm{Res}}^{A}_{L}:H-\mathrm{mod}\rightarrow L-\mathrm{mod}
the restriction functor of
H
-modules to the subalgebra
L
. This functor induces a map
{({\mathrm{Res}}^{A}_{L})}_{*}:\mathrm{{CF}}(\mathcal{C})\rightarrow C(L)
at the level of characters. It is easy to see that the image of this map is
{\mathrm{CF}}(L)\xhookrightarrow{\nu}C(L)
.

Note also that the central space
{\mathrm{CE}}(L)
becomes in this case
{\mathrm{CE}}(L)=\mathrm{{Hom}}_{H}(\mathbf{1},L)=L\cap Z(H)
and the inclusion
\iota^{e}:{\mathrm{CE}}(L)\rightarrow\mathrm{{CE}(\mathcal{C})}
is the usual space inclusion
L\cap Z(H)\hookrightarrow Z(H)
.
Note also that in this case
\ell_{\mathcal{D}}
is a multiple of the integral
\Lambda_{L}
defined in
[
3
]
.

The generalized combined effect for one dimensional wave equations with semilinear terms including product type Mathematical Institute, Tohoku University, Aoba, Sendai 980-8578, Japan. e-mail: hiroyuki.takamura.a1@tohoku.ac.jp.
Ryuki Kido, Takiko Sasaki, Shu Takamatsu

Abstract
We are interested in the so-called “combined effect” of two different kinds of nonlinear terms for semilinear wave equations in one space dimension. Recently, the first result with the same formulation as in the higher dimensional case has been obtained if and only if the total integral of the initial speed is zero, namely Huygens’ principle holds. In this paper, we extend the nonlinear term to the general form including the product type. Such model equations are extremely meaningful only in one space dimension because the most cases in higher dimensions possess the global-in-time existence of a classical solution in the general theory for nonlinear wave equations. It is also remarkable that our results on the lifespan estimates are partially better than those of the general theory. This fact tells us that there is a possibility to improve the general theory which was expected complete more than 30 years ago.

Introduction

Let us consider the initial value problems;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{tt}-u_{xx}=A|u_{t}|^{p}|u|^{q}+B|u|^{r}&\mbox{in}\quad{\bf R}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),&x\in{\bf R},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
p,q,r>1
,
A,B\geq 0
and
T>0
.
We assume that
f
and
g
are given smooth functions of compact support
and a parameter

is “small enough”.
We are interested in the lifespan

Our results in this paper are the following estimates for

T({\varepsilon})\sim\min\{C{\varepsilon}^{-(p+q-1)},C{\varepsilon}^{-(r-1)/2}\}\qquad\mbox{if}\ \int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx\not=0\\

T({\varepsilon})\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C{\varepsilon}^{-(p+q)(r-1)/(r+1)}\\
\quad\mbox{for}\ \displaystyle\frac{r+1}{2}\leq p+q\leq r,\\
\min\{C{\varepsilon}^{-(p+q-1)},C{\varepsilon}^{-r(r-1)/(r+1)}\}\\
\quad\mbox{otherwise}\\
\end{array}\right.\mbox{if}\ \displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0.

(1.3)

Here we denote the fact that there are positive constants,

C_{1}
and

, independent of

A({\varepsilon},C_{1})\leq T({\varepsilon})\leq A({\varepsilon},C_{2})

by
T({\varepsilon})\sim A({\varepsilon},C)
.

2
Preliminaries and main results

Throughout this paper, we assume that the initial data

(f,g)\in C_{0}^{2}({\bf R})\times C^{1}_{0}({\bf R})
satisfies

\mbox{\rm supp }f,\ \mbox{supp }g\subset\{x\in{\bf R}:|x|\leq R\},\quad R\geq 1.

(2.1)

Let
u
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) in the time interval
[0,T]
.
Then the support condition of the initial data, (
2.1
), implies that

\mbox{supp}\ u(x,t)\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}.

(2.2)

For example, see Appendix of John
[
6
]
for this fact.

It is well-known that
u
satisfies the following integral equation.

u(x,t)={\varepsilon}u^{0}(x,t)+L(A|u_{t}|^{p}|u|^{q}+B|u|^{r})(x,t),

(2.3)

where
u^{0}
is a solution of the free wave equation with the same initial data,

u^{0}(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\{f(x+t)+f(x-t)\}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}g(y)dy,

(2.4)

and a linear integral operator
L
for a function
v=v(x,t)
in Duhamel’s term is defined by

L(v)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{x-t+s}^{x+t-s}v(y,s)dy.

(2.5)

Then, one can apply the time-derivative to (
2.3
) to obtain

u_{t}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}(x,t)+L^{\prime}(A|u_{t}|^{p}|u|^{q}+B|u|^{r})(x,t)

(2.6)

and

u_{t}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)+g(x-t)\},

(2.7)

where
L^{\prime}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is defined by

L^{\prime}(v)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\{v(x+t-s,s)+v(x-t+s,s)\}ds.

(2.8)

On the other hand, applying the space-derivative to (
2.3
),
we have

u_{x}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}(x,t)+\overline{L^{\prime}}(A|u_{t}|^{p}|u|^{q}+B|u|^{r})(x,t)

(2.9)

and

u_{x}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)-g(x-t)\},

(2.10)

where
\overline{L^{\prime}}
for a function
v=v(x,t)
is defined by

\overline{L^{\prime}}(v)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\{v(x+t-s,s)-v(x-t+s,s)\}ds.

(2.11)

Therefore,
u_{x}
is expressed by
u
and
u_{t}
.
Moreover, one more space-derivative to (
2.6
) yields that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+AL^{\prime}(p|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx}|u|^{q}+q|u|^{q-2}uu_{x}|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+BL^{\prime}(r|u|^{r-2}uu_{x})(x,t)\end{array}

(2.12)

and

u_{tx}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)+g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

(2.13)

Similarly, we have that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tt}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)+A|u_{t}(x,t)|^{p}|u(x,t)|^{q}+B|u(x,t)|^{r}\\
&\displaystyle+A\overline{L^{\prime}}(p|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx}|u|^{q}+q|u|^{q-2}uu_{x}|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+B\overline{L^{\prime}}(r|u|^{r-2}uu_{x})(x,t)\end{array}

and

u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)-g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

Therefore,
u_{tt}
is expressed by
u,u_{t},u_{x},u_{tx}
and so is
u_{xx}
, because of

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{xx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+A\overline{L^{\prime}}(p|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx}|u|^{q}+q|u|^{q-2}uu_{x}|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+B\overline{L^{\prime}}(r|u|^{r-2}uu_{x})(x,t)\end{array}

and

u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)=u^{0}_{tt}(x,t).

First, we note the following fact.

Proposition 2.1

Assume that
(f,g)\in C^{2}({\bf R})\times C^{1}({\bf R})
.
Let
(u,w)
be a
C^{1}
solution of a system of integral equations;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u={\varepsilon}u^{0}+L(A|w|^{p}|u|^{q}+B|u|^{r}),\\
w={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}+L^{\prime}(A|w|^{p}|u|^{q}+B|u|^{r})\end{array}\right.\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}\times[0,T]

(2.14)

with some
T>0
.
Then,
w\equiv u_{t}
in
{\bf R}\times[0,T]
holds
and
u
is a classical solution of (
1.1
) in
{\bf R}\times[0,T]
.

Proof.
It is trivial that
w\equiv u_{t}
by differentiating the first equation with respect to
t
.
The rest part is easy along with the computations above in this section.

\Box

Remark 2.1

&\quad\times(t+x-R)^{(p+q)a_{n}+1}(t-x)^{qb_{n}+pc_{n}+1}\end{array}

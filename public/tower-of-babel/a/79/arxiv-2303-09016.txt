Chaos processes as rough paths
Guang Yang

Abstract
. In this article we investigate the rough paths structure of a process X t X_{t} living in a fixed Wiener chaos. Specifically, we formulate various types of rough lifts of X t X_{t} and study their properties. As application, we study the integrabilities of quantities related to rough differential equations driven by X t X_{t} .



1.
Introduction

Since the seminal paper by T. Lyons
[
14
]
, rough path theory has been developed rapidly in the past two decades. Among the most prominent directions, is the study of Gaussian rough paths (see
[
6
]

[
8
]
), which has led to a wide variety of interesting results. As a natural counterpart to this development, significant progresses have been achieved in the study of rough differential equations (RDEs) driven by Gaussian rough paths. Such differential equations can be written in the form

(1)

Y_{t}=y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\bm{X}^{i}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}V_{0}(Y_{s})ds,\ y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t\in[0,1],

where
\{V_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d}
are vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with suitable regularity assumption and
\bm{X}_{t}
is a Gaussian rough path.

Many of the rich structures associated with Gaussian processes fit in the study of such RDEs amazingly well. Interactions with Malliavin calculus had led to a thorough study of the law of the solution
Y_{t}
. In this direction, the existence and smoothness of the density of
Y_{t}
were studied in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
. Upper bound and strict positivity of the density and hitting probabilities were developed in
[
1
]
. Regularity and ergodicity were studied in
[
10
]
. Adapting the isoperimetric inequality of Gaussian measures to the rough paths setting allows one to study the integrabilities of the Jacobian process
J^{Y}_{t}
of
Y_{t}

[
5
]
, which, in turn, enables the study of integrabilities of Malliavin derivatives of
Y_{t}

[
11
]
.

However, the study of the Malliavin derivatives of
Y_{t}
in
[
11
]
appears to be far more difficult than one would have expected. This is due to the fact that, with a Gaussian rough path, one can only write the “directional derivatives” of the form
\langle D^{k}Y_{t},h_{1}\otimes\cdots h_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}
in a consistent way, which eventually leads only to the operator norm of
D^{k}Y_{t}
. To get the the tensor norm (or equivalently, Hilbert-Schmidt norm) of
D^{k}Y_{t}
, Inahama had to devise a rather technical method, whose starting point is the unique feature that the Malliavin derivative of a Gaussian random variable is deterministic.

We will present this new idea that instead of a rough path above the process
X_{t}
along, we can construct a rough path above the enlarged process
\hat{X}_{t}=(X_{t},DX_{t})
. We shall see later that when
X_{t}
is Gaussian, this enlargement does not cause any extra difficulty for all the previous studies. The advantage of such endeavour is immediately apparent. With a rough path above
\hat{X}_{t}
, all Malliavin derivatives of
Y_{t}
can be written as solutions to RDEs driven by
\hat{X}_{t}
along linear vector fields (see section 5 below). Thus, the study of their integrabilities can be combined into a systematic study of linear RDEs driven by
\hat{X}_{t}
.

This same development can be naturally carried our for processes whose Malliavin derivatives vanish above certain level, where we need to consider
\hat{X}_{t}=(X_{t},DX_{t},\cdots,D^{n}X_{t})
. This leads to the so called chaos processes, which generalize Gaussian processes while still inherit many useful structures. The study of chaos random variables have gained increasing attentions in recent years (see, for instance,
[
18
]

[
16
]

[
15
]

[
12
]
). Our study shall focus on chaos processes.

More specifically, we will fix
n\geq 1
and consider a process
X_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
parametrized on the compact interval
[0,1]
, whose components are independent copies of
I_{n}(f_{t})
, where
I_{n}
is the
n
-th multiple Wiener integral and
f_{t}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the Cameron-Martin space of the underlying Wiener space. Note that when
n=1
,
X_{t}
is Gaussian.

Our study mainly consists of two parts. First, we will construct rough lifts for
X_{t}
and
\hat{X}_{t}
. Several properties of the rough lifts will be studied, including a large deviation principle. Then, as an application, we study the integrability of Malliavin derivatives and Jacobian process of solutions to RDEs of form (
1
) driven by
X_{t}
.

There are two main assumptions. The first one is the same assumption we used for Gaussian processes.

Assumption 1.1
.

Let
X_{t}=I_{n}(f_{t})
be a real-valued chaos process. For
0\leq s,t\leq 1
, define
R(s,t)=R_{X}(s,t):=\mathbb{E}(X_{s}X_{t})
. We assume that
R(s,t)
has finite
\rho
-variation on
[0,1]\times[0,1]
for some
\rho\in[1,3/2)
. Moreover, we assume the
\rho
-variation of
R(s,t)
is Hölder-controlled in the sense that

{\left\lVert{R}\right\rVert}^{\rho}_{\rho-var;[s,t]\times[s,t]}\leq\left|t-s\right|.

It is worth pointing out that the Hölder controlled property is not restrictive as one can always reparameterize the process
X_{t}
(see
[
4
]
).

A simple application of the moment equivalence of Wiener chaos gives that for any
p>1
,

\mathbb{E}\left|X_{t}-X_{s}\right|^{p}\leq(p-1)^{n/2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left|X_{t}-X_{s}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq(p-1)^{n/2}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{2\rho}}.

By Kolmogorov continuity theorem we see that assumption
1.1
implies
X_{t}
is
1/(2\rho^{\prime})
-Hölder continuous (or has finite
2\rho^{\prime}
-variation) for any
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
almost surely. As
\rho\in[1,3/2)
, we only need to construct the second order iterated integral for
X_{t}
to get a rough lift.

It is hardly a surprise that an assumption on covariance along is not sufficient for all our purposes. We shall encounter many difficulties that did not appear for Gaussian rough paths. For instance, when we apply translation in the direction of
\mathcal{H}
, one has the Malliavin-Stroock formula

X_{t}(\omega+rh)=X_{t}(\omega)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{r^{k}}{k!}\cdot\langle D^{k}X_{t}(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}},\ r>0,\ h\in\mathcal{H}.

To get the rough lift of
X_{t}(\omega+rh)
, we must define integrals of the form

(2)

\int_{s}^{t}D^{m}X_{s,r}\otimes dD^{k}X_{r},\ 0\leq m,k,\leq n,

where
X_{s,r}=X_{r}-X_{s}
. This observation is another motivation for us to consider the rough lift of
\hat{X}_{t}
instead of
X_{t}
, since terms in (
2
) are precisely the second level iterated integrals of
\hat{X}_{t}
. It turns out that one needs a condition genuinely stronger than assumption
1.2
in order to handle terms in (
2
). We will come back to this point with more details in section 3 below.

The following assumption, enhanced from assumption
1.1
by adding what emerges as the condition needed to deal with terms in (
2
), will be our second assumption that replaces assumption
1.1
when needed.

Recall for
a,b\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
that are symmetric, the symmetric contraction of degree
r
is defined to be
a\hat{\otimes}_{r}b=\widehat{\langle a,b\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(2n-2r)}
for
1\leq r\leq n
, where
\widehat{\langle a,b\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}}
is the symmetrization of
\langle a,b\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}}
. More details about contractions are given in section 2.

Assumption 1.2
.

Let
X_{t}=I_{n}(f_{t})
be a real-valued chaos process. We assume that there exists a control
\omega:[0,1]^{2}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, such that for some
\rho\in[1,3/2)
,
1\leq r\leq n
and any
[s,t],[u,v]\subset[0,1]
, we have

(3)

{\left\lVert{f_{s,t}\hat{\otimes}_{r}f_{u,v}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(2n-2r)}}\leq\omega^{\frac{1}{\rho}}([s,t]\times[u,v]).

Moreover, we assume
\omega
satisfies

\omega([s,t]\times[s,t])\leq\left|t-s\right|.

Remark 1.3
.

The contractions
f_{s,t}\hat{\otimes}_{r}f_{u,v}
appear in (
3
) are frequently used to control the product of chaos random variables and have naturally appeared in many studies of chaos random variables (see section 2 below).

Remark 1.4
.

We can give assumption
1.2
a more intuitive meaning. If we further assume a non-degenerate lower bound
0<C\leq{\left\lVert{f_{r}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
for all
r\in[s,t]
, then assumption (
1.2
) implies

d_{W}(X_{r},N)\leq C^{\prime}\left|r\right|^{\frac{1}{\rho}},\ \forall r\in[s,t],

where
d_{W}
is the Wasserstein distance and
N
denotes a standard normal distribution. From this perspective, assumption
1.2
is essentially a regularity assumption on any interval where
X_{t}
is non-degenerate. We point out that the above inequality is still valid with Kolmogorov distance and total variation distance. We refer to chapter 5 of
[
17
]
for more details.

Remark 1.5
.

When
r=n
, inequality (
3
) becomes

\left|\mathbb{E}(X_{s,t}X_{u,v})\right|=n!\left|\langle f_{s,t},f_{u,v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\right|=n!\left|f_{s,t}\hat{\otimes}_{n}f_{u,v}\right|\leq n!\cdot\omega^{\frac{1}{\rho}}([s,t]\times[u,v]),

which is nothing but assumption
1.1
. In particular, when
n=1
, assumption
1.1
and assumption
1.2
are identical. Hence, assumption
1.2
can be regraded as a natural generalization of assumption
1.1
to non-Gaussian Wiener chaos settings.

In section 3, we will give an easy method to construct processes that satisfy assumption
1.2
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we gather some preliminary materials. Section 3 is devoted to the construction of rough lifts of
X_{t}
and
\hat{X}_{t}
. Different types of approximations are also discussed. Section 4 develops a large deviation principle for
X_{t}
and its rough lift, while section 5 studies the integrability of the Malliavin derivatives and Jacobian process of solutions to RDEs driven by
X_{t}
.

\left|\cdot\right|

{\left\lVert{F}\right\rVert}_{\alpha-\text{H\"{o}l}}:=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq 1}\frac{{\left\lVert{F_{t}-F_{s}}\right\rVert}}{\left|t-s\right|^{\alpha}}.

X_{s,t}

\left|x\right|\leq C\left|y\right|
;

x

\left|x\right|\preceq\left|y\right|
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Wiener chaos

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real separable Hilbert space. We say
W=\{W(h):\ h\in\mathcal{H}\}
is an isonormal Gaussian process over
\mathcal{H}
, if
W
is a family of centered Gaussian random variables defined on some complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that

\mathbb{E}(W(h)W(g))=\langle h,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

We will further assume that
\mathcal{F}
is generated by
W
.

For every
k\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{k}
the
k
-th homogeneous Wiener chaos of
W
defined as the closed subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
generated by the family of random variables
\{H_{k}(W(h)):\ h\in\mathcal{H}\}
where
H_{k}
is the
k
-th Hermite polynomial given by

H_{k}(x)=(-1)^{k}e^{\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\left(e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\right).

H_{0}
is by convention defined to be
\mathbb{R}
.

For any
k\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
the
k
-th tensor product of
\mathcal{H}
. If
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
, we define the symmetrization of
\phi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_{n}
by

\phi_{1}\hat{\otimes}\cdots\hat{\otimes}\phi_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\phi_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_{\sigma(n)},

where
\Sigma_{n}
is the symmetric group of
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
. The symmetrization of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}k}
. We consider
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
of the form

f=e_{j_{1}}^{\hat{\otimes}k_{1}}\hat{\otimes}e_{j_{2}}^{\hat{\otimes}k_{2}}\hat{\otimes}\cdots\hat{\otimes}e_{j_{m}}^{\hat{\otimes}k_{m}},

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
and
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}=n
. The multiple Wiener-Itô integral of
f
is defined as

I_{n}(f)=H_{k_{1}}(W(e_{j_{1}}))\cdots H_{k_{m}}(W(e_{j_{m}})).

If
f,g\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
are of the above form, we have the following isometry

\mathbb{E}(I_{n}(f)I_{n}(g))=n!\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}.

For general elements in
\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
, the multiple Wiener-Itô integrals are defined by
L^{2}
convergence with the previous isometry equality.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{W(h),h\in\mathcal{H}\}
, then any random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathbb{P})
admits an orthonormal decomposition (Wiener chaos decomposition) of the form

F=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}I_{k}(f_{k}),

where
f_{0}=\mathbb{E}(F)
and
f_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}k}
are uniquely determined by
F
.

One of the most appealing properties of chaos random variables is the moment equivalence, which we would frequently employ.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
and
X=I_{n}(f)
. Then, for any
1<p,q<+\infty
, we have

{\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\left(\frac{q-1}{p-1}\right)^{n/2}{\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}m}
, we define the symmetric contractions of
f
and
g
as

f\hat{\otimes}_{r}g=\widehat{\langle f,g\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}},\ 1\leq r\leq n\wedge m,

where
\widehat{\langle f,g\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}}
means the symmetrization of
\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}}
. Note that since
f,g
are symmetric, we do not have to specify with respect to which
r
components are the scalar products taken. The product formula of chaos random variables can be written in terms of these contractions

I_{n}(f)\cdot I_{m}(g)=\sum_{r=0}^{m\wedge n}r\binom{n}{r}\binom{m}{r}I_{n+m-2r}(f\hat{\otimes}_{r}g).

2.2.
Malliavin calculus

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
W
be as previous subsection. Let
\mathcal{FC}^{\infty}
denote the set of cylindrical random variables of the form

F=f(W(h_{1}),\cdots,W(h_{n})),

where
n\geq 1
,
h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
; that means
f
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
bounded with all derivatives. The Malliavin derivative of
F
is a
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variable defined as

DF=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(W(h_{1}),\cdots,W(h_{n}))h_{i}.

One can relate
DF
with a directional derivative as

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F(\omega+rh)-F(\omega)}{r}=\langle DF,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\ \forall h\in\mathcal{H}.

By iteration, one can define the
k
-th Malliavin derivative of
F
as a
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
-valued random variable. For
m,p\geq 1
, we denote
\mathbb{D}^{m,p}
the closure of
\mathcal{FC}^{\infty}
with respect to the norm

{\left\lVert{F}\right\rVert}^{p}_{m,p}=\mathbb{E}(\left|F\right|^{p})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left({\left\lVert{D^{k}F}\right\rVert}^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}\right).

We define
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}=\cap_{m,p\geq 1}\mathbb{D}^{m,p}
.

2.3.
Rough path and rough differential equation

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a Banach space. For
p\in[2,3)
, we define the space of
p
rough path on
\mathscr{X}
parameterized on the interval
[0,1]
, in symbols
\mathscr{C}^{p-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})
, as those pairs
\bm{X}=:(X,\mathbb{X})\in C([0,1],\mathscr{X}\oplus\mathscr{X}^{\otimes 2})
such that

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle=\left(\sup_{(s,t)\in D([0,1])}{\left\lVert{X_{s,t}}\right\rVert}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty,

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\mathbb{X}}\right\rVert}_{p/2-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle=\left(\sup_{(s,t)\in D([0,1])}{\left\lVert{\mathbb{X}_{s,t}}\right\rVert}^{p/2}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}<+\infty,

where the supreme is taken over all partitions of
[0,1]
. Moreover, for
0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq 1
, we have the following Chen’s relation

\mathbb{X}_{s,t}=\mathbb{X}_{s,u}+\mathbb{X}_{u,t}+X_{s,u}\otimes X_{u,t}.

We will say
\bm{X}
is a rough lift of
X
. One can equip
\mathscr{C}^{p-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})
with the homogeneous
p
-variation norm

{\left\lVert{\bm{X}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}={\left\lVert{X}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}+{\left\lVert{\mathbb{X}}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{p/2-var;[0,1]}.

At its most fundamental, we need to construct
\mathbb{X}_{t}
from a given
X_{t}
in order to get a rough lift, which in general is not unique. In the special case that
X\in C^{1-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})
,
\mathbb{X}
can be canonically defined as

\mathbb{X}_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{t}X_{s,r}dX_{r},

where the integral is understood as Riemann–Stieltjes integral and it is easily checked that
(X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathscr{C}^{p-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})
for any
p\geq 1
. For convenience, we often write the rough lift in this particular case as

\displaystyle S_{2}:C^{1-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{p-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto\bm{X}.

A rough path
\bm{X}\in\mathscr{C}^{p-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})
is said to be a geometric rough path if we can find a sequence
\{X^{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset C^{1-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})
such that

(4)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\left\lVert{\bm{X}-S_{2}(X^{k})}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}\rightarrow 0.

If
X^{k}
can be chosen to be the piece-wise linear approximations of
X
along a sequence of increasing partitions, then we often call
\bm{X}
the natural rough lift of
X
. Another important property of geometric rough paths is the “first order calculus”

(5)

Symm(\mathbb{X}_{s,t})=\frac{1}{2}X_{s,t}\otimes X_{s,t},

where
Symm
denotes the symmetric part. We denote the space of geometric rough paths by
G\mathscr{C}^{p-var}([0,1],\mathscr{X})
.

For a given geometric rough path
\bm{X}
, RDE of the form

dY_{t}=V(Y_{t})d\bm{X}_{t}+V_{0}(Y_{t})dt,\;Y_{0}=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in[0,1],

where
V,V_{0}\in C^{2}_{b}(V,L(V,V))
, always admits a unique solution. It is customary to write

Y_{t}=\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\bm{X})(t),\ t\in[0,1],

where
\pi
is called the Itô-Lyons map. Moreover, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\left\lVert{\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\bm{X})-\pi_{V}(0,y_{0};\bm{X}^{k})}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}=0,

where
\{\bm{X}^{k}\}_{k\geq 1}=\{S_{2}(X^{k})\}_{k\geq 1}
is any sequence with finite 1-variation that satisfy (
4
).

There are two important quantities we will use later in our study. For
\alpha>0
, we define the accumulated
\alpha
-local variation as

M_{\alpha}(\bm{X})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{i},t_{i+1})\in D([0,1])\\
{\left\lVert{\bm{X}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\leq\alpha\end{subarray}}\sum_{i}{\left\lVert{\bm{X}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}.

Next, we have the greedy sequence:

\displaystyle\tau_{0}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\tau_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\inf\{t\in(\tau_{i},1]:{\left\lVert{\bm{X}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1}]}=\alpha\}\wedge 1,

with the convention that
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
. Associated with it is

N_{\alpha}(\bm{X}):=\sup\{a\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}:\tau_{a}<1\}.

The following bound is always true

(6)

\alpha N_{\alpha}(\bm{X})\leq{\left\lVert{\bm{X}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}.

Moreover, we have the following relation (see proposition 4.11 of
[
5
]
)

M_{\alpha}(\bm{X})\leq\alpha(2N_{\alpha}(\bm{X})+1).

3.
Rough lift of
X_{t}

3.1.
Piece-wise linear approximation

In this section we study the rough lift of process
X_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, whose components are independent copies of
I_{n}(f_{t})
. Let
\{Q^{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
be a sequence of increasing partition of
[0,1]
, whose mesh goes to zero as
n
tends to infinity. We prepare a general lemma before our main results.

Lemma 3.1

(Proposition 5.60 of
[
9
]
)
.

Let
p>1
and
F\in C([0,1]^{2})
with finite 2D
p
-variation. Then for any partitions
Q,\tilde{Q}
of
[0,1]
and
0\leq s,t,u,v\leq 1
, we have

{\left\lVert{F^{Q,\tilde{Q}}}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[s,t]\times[u,v]}\preceq{\left\lVert{F}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[s,t]\times[u,v]},

where
F^{Q,\tilde{Q}}
is the piece-wise linear type approximation of
F
along
Q,\tilde{Q}
.

Our next result gives a natural rough lift of
X_{t}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that assumption
1.1
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. Let
X^{l}_{t}
be the piece-wise linear approximation of
X_{t}
along
\{Q^{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
and
\bm{X}^{l}
be the natural lift of
X^{l}_{t}
, then for any
\rho^{\prime}\in(\rho,3/2)
and
p>1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{l,m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(d_{2\rho^{\prime}-var;[0,1]}(\bm{X}^{l},\bm{X}^{m})\right)^{p}=0.

In particular,
X_{t}
admits a natural geometric rough lift
\bm{X}_{t}
and

\mathbb{E}\left({\left\lVert{\bm{X}_{t}}\right\rVert}_{2\rho^{\prime}-var;[0,1]}\right)^{p}<+\infty.

Proof.

By theorem A.13 of
[
9
]
, it is enough to show that for any
s,t\in[0,1]
,
1\leq i,j\leq d
and
p>1

(7)

\displaystyle\sup_{l\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left|X^{l;i}_{s,t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{2\rho^{\prime}}}

(8)

\displaystyle\sup_{l\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{\rho^{\prime}}},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|X^{l;i}_{s,t}-X^{m;i}_{s,t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\epsilon\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{2\rho^{\prime}}}

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}X^{m;i}_{s,r}dX^{m;j}_{r}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\epsilon\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{\rho^{\prime}}},

where
X^{l;i}
is the
i
-th component of
X^{l}
, similarly for all other terms, and
\epsilon
is some positive number that goes to zero as
l,m
tend to infinity. We will only prove (
9
) and (
10
), since (
7
) and (
8
) can be derived with exact same (in fact easier) arguments.

For level 1 and any
1\leq i\leq d
, we have by moment equivalence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|X^{l;i}_{s,t}-X^{m;i}_{s,t}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\left(\mathbb{E}\left|X^{l;i}_{s,t}-X^{m;i}_{s,t}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho^{\prime}-var;[s,t]^{2}}^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}
is the covariance function of
X^{l}-X^{m}
, which can be written in terms of piece-wise linear approximation of
R_{X}
along
Q^{l},Q^{m}
. Since
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
, we have by interpolation and lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho^{\prime}-var;[s,t]^{2}}^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\rho-var;[s,t]^{2}}

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}{\left\lVert{R_{X}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\rho-var;[s,t]^{2}}

(11)

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{2\rho^{\prime}}}.

We used assumption
1.1
in the last inequality.

For level 2, we may first consider

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}X^{m;i}_{s,r}dX^{m;j}_{r}\right)^{2}.

When
i=j
, by simple calculus, we have

\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;i}_{r}=\frac{1}{2}(X^{l;i}_{t}-X^{l;i}_{s})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(X^{l;i}_{s,t})^{2}.

This together with moment equivalence give

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}X^{m;i}_{s,r}dX^{m;j}_{r}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\left((X^{l;i}_{s,t}-X^{m;i}_{s,t})(X^{l;i}_{s,t}+X^{m;i}_{s,t})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\preceq\mathbb{E}(X^{l;i}_{s,t}-X^{m;i}_{s,t})^{2}\mathbb{E}(X^{l;i}_{s,t}+X^{m;i}_{s,t})^{2}

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho^{\prime}-var;[s,t]^{2}}{\left\lVert{R_{X}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[s,t]\times[s,t]}

(12)

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

When
i\neq j
,
X^{i}
and
X^{j}
are independent. Hence, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}X^{m;i}_{s,r}dX^{m;j}_{r}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[s,t]\times[s,t]}\mathbb{E}(X^{l;i}_{s,r}-X^{m;i}_{s,r})(X^{l;i}_{s,u}-X^{m;i}_{s,u})d\mathbb{E}(X^{l;j}_{r}X^{l;j}_{u})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{[s,t]\times[s,t]}\mathbb{E}(X^{m;i}_{s,r}X^{m;i}_{s,u})d\mathbb{E}(X^{l;i}_{s,r}-X^{m;i}_{s,r})(X^{l;i}_{s,u}-X^{m;i}_{s,u})

(13)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho^{\prime}-var;[s,t]^{2}}{\left\lVert{R}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[s,t]\times[s,t]}\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

Combining (
12
) and (
13
) and another application of moment equivalence gives

(14)

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}X^{m;i}_{s,r}dX^{m;j}_{r}\right|^{p}\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

Putting (
11
), (
14
) together, we have proved (
9
) and (
10
) with

\epsilon={\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}.

Since

\lim_{l,m\rightarrow\infty}{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}=0,

we conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{l,m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(d_{2\rho^{\prime}-var;[0,1]}(\bm{X}^{l},\bm{X}^{m})\right)^{p}=0.

∎

3.2.
Karhunen-Loève type approximation

Similar to Gaussian processes, we may introduce the following Karhunen-Loève type approximation to
X_{t}
.

Let
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
, then it is readily checked that

(15)

I_{n}(f_{t})=\sum_{i\geq 1}\langle f_{t},\varphi_{i}\rangle I_{n}(\varphi_{i}),

where the above series is convergent in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We show that (
15
) also gives a rough lift of
X_{t}
, which coincide with the one we constructed in theorem
3.2
almost surely.

Our strategy is apparent, we show the partial sum
X_{t}^{k}
whose components are independent copies of
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\langle f_{t},\varphi_{i}\rangle I_{n}(\varphi_{i})
has a natural rough lift
\bm{X}^{k}_{t}
which converges to
\bm{X}_{t}
as
k
tends to infinity. We start with a lemma, reminiscent to the Cameron-Martin embedding of Gaussian measure.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds. For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
, the process
\phi_{t}=\langle f_{t},\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
has finite
\rho
-variation and

{\left\lVert{\phi_{t}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}\preceq{\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\sqrt{{\left\lVert{R}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}}.

Proof.

Recall we have the following isometry

R([s,t]\times[u,v])=\mathbb{E}(X_{s,t}X_{u,v})=n!\langle f_{s,t},f_{u,v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}.

Let
\rho^{\prime}
be the Hölder conjugate of
\rho
. Then, for any partition of
[0,1]
, we have by duality that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i}\left|\langle f_{t_{i},t_{i+1}},\varphi\rangle\right|^{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}=\sup_{{\left\lVert{\beta}\right\rVert}_{l^{\rho^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\sum_{i}\beta_{i}\langle f_{t_{i},t_{i+1}},\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}=\sup_{{\left\lVert{\beta}\right\rVert}_{l^{\rho^{\prime}}}\leq 1}\langle\sum_{i}\beta_{i}f_{t_{i},t_{i+1}},\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\left\lVert{\beta}\right\rVert}_{l^{\rho^{\prime}}}\leq 1}{\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\sqrt{\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}\langle f_{t_{i},t_{i+1}},f_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\left\lVert{\beta}\right\rVert}_{l^{\rho^{\prime}}}\leq 1}{\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\left|\beta_{i}\right|^{\rho^{\prime}}\left|\beta_{j}\right|^{\rho^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\rho^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\left|\langle f_{t_{i},t_{i+1}},f_{t_{j},t_{j+1}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\right|^{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\rho}}

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\left|R([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\right|^{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\rho}}\leq{\left\lVert{\varphi}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\sqrt{{\left\lVert{R}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]^{2}}}.

Taking the supreme over all partitions concludes the proof. ∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. Define
X_{t}^{k}
to be the process whose components are independent copies of

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\langle f_{t},\varphi_{i}\rangle I_{n}(\varphi_{i}).

Then, for each
k\geq 1
,
X^{k}_{t}
admits a geometric rough lift
\bm{X}^{k}
.

Proof.

For a fixed
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle R_{X^{k}}(s,t)=\mathbb{E}(X^{k}_{t}X^{k}_{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\langle f_{s},\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle f_{t},\varphi_{i}\rangle.

By triangle inequality, one sees that

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{R_{X^{k}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\left\lVert{\langle f_{s},\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle f_{t},\varphi_{i}\rangle}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]^{2}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\left\lVert{\langle f_{t},\varphi_{i}\rangle}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle\leq k{\left\lVert{R}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]^{2}},

where we used the previous lemma in the last step. As a result, for each fixed
k\geq 1
,
X^{k}_{t}
is a process living in the
n
-th homogeneous chaos whose covariance function
R_{X^{k}}
satisfies assumption
1.1
. Now our result follows from theorem
3.2
.
∎

To achieve our goal of proving that
\bm{X}^{k}
converges to
\bm{X}
, the main ingredient is to get, in a certain sense, a uniform boundedness of
\bm{X}^{k}
. The crude estimate we got from previous lemma using triangle inequality is obviously not uniform. A more delicate projection argument is needed.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. Let
\bm{X}^{k}
be the natural lifts we constructed in the previous lemma and
\bm{X}
the rough lift we constructed in theorem
3.2
. Then, for any
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
and
p>1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(d_{2\rho^{\prime}-var;[0,1]}(\bm{X}^{k},\bm{X})\right)^{p}=0.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that for any
s,t\in[0,1]
and
1\leq i,j\leq d

(16)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}X^{k;i}_{t}=X^{i}_{t},\ \lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{X}^{k;i,j}_{t}=\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{t},

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and

(17)

\displaystyle\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}(X^{k,i}_{s,t})^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(X^{i}_{s,t})^{2}\leq C\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}}

(18)

\displaystyle\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{X}^{k;i,j}_{s,t})^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{s,t})^{2}\leq C\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

Indeed, by using moment equivalence and Garsia–Rodemich–Rumsey estimate (see, for instance theorem A.12 of
[
9
]
) equations (
17
) and (
18
) imply that for any
p>1

\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{\bm{X}^{k}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{1/(2\rho^{\prime\prime})-\text{H\"{o}l}}<+\infty,

where
\rho^{\prime\prime}\in(\rho^{\prime},3/2)
. By passing to a sub-sequence, equation (
16
) implies
\bm{X}^{k}
converges to
\bm{X}
in probability. Hence, by theorem A.15 of
[
9
]
, we conclude

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}d_{2\rho^{\prime\prime}-var;[0,1]}(\bm{X}^{k},\bm{X})\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}d_{\frac{1}{2\rho^{\prime\prime}}-\text{H\"{o}l}}(\bm{X}^{k},\bm{X})=0

in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p>1
.
The rest of our proof will focus on establishing (
16
)(
17
)(
18
). We divide it into several steps.

Step 1
:
Let

L^{2}_{k}=\overline{Span}\{I_{n}(\varphi_{1}),\cdots,I_{n}(\varphi_{k})\},

which is a closed subspace of the
n
-th homogeneous Wiener space. We denote
P_{L^{2}_{k}}
the projection onto
L^{2}_{k}
. It is then obvious that

\displaystyle X^{k}_{t}=P_{L^{2}_{k}}(X_{t}).

By properties of projection and the completeness of orthonormal basis, one has

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}X^{k;i}_{t}=X^{i}_{t},\ \text{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega),

and

\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}(X^{k,i}_{s,t})^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(X^{i}_{s,t})^{2}\leq C\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

This finishes the level 1 estimates of (
16
)(
17
)(
18
).

Step 2
:
Let
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{X}^{k}
be the second level processes of
\bm{X},\bm{X}^{k}
respectively. We first consider the case where
1\leq i\neq j\leq d
.

Observe that, for two random variables
x,y\in L^{2}(\Omega)
that are independent, we have

x\cdot y\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, for
x\otimes y
as an element in
L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

{\left\lVert{x\otimes y}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}=\mathbb{E}(x^{2})\mathbb{E}(y^{2})=\mathbb{E}(x\cdot y)^{2}={\left\lVert{x\cdot y}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This isometry allows us to identify
x\cdot y
with
x\otimes y
.

Back to our proof, we claim that

(19)

\mathbb{X}_{s,t}^{k;i,j}=P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}(\mathbb{X}_{s,t}^{i,j}).

To see this, let
X^{l;i}_{t},X^{k,l;i}_{t}
be the
i
-th component of the piece-wise linear approximation of
X_{t},X^{k}_{t}
along the partition
Q^{l}
respectively. Then, we have

P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}\right)=\int_{s}^{t}X^{k,l;i}_{s,r}dX^{k,l;j}_{r}.

Notice that we have identified the integral as an element in
L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)
thanks to the independence of
X^{i}
and
X^{j}
. The isometry we discussed before can be written as

(20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}\right)^{2}={\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Combining this with theorem
3.2
gives

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}X^{k,l;i}_{s,r}dX^{k,l;j}_{r}-\mathbb{X}_{s,t}^{k;i,j}}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}X^{k,l;i}_{s,r}dX^{k,l;j}_{r}-\mathbb{X}_{s,t}^{k;i,j}}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

We thus deduce from the continuity of projection that

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}^{k;i,j}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\rightarrow\infty\\
\text{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\int_{s}^{t}X^{k,l;i}_{s,r}dX^{k,l;j}_{r}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\rightarrow\infty\\
\text{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)\end{subarray}}P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}\right)=P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}\left(\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\rightarrow\infty\\
\text{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}\left(\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\rightarrow\infty\\
\text{in}\ L^{2}(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i}_{s,r}dX^{l;j}_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}(\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{s,t}),

and our assertion (
19
) follows.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{X}^{k;i,j}_{s,t}-\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{s,t})^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\left\lVert{\mathbb{X}^{k;i,j}_{s,t}-\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{s,t}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\left\lVert{P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}(\mathbb{X}_{s,t}^{i,j})-\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{s,t}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}=0,

and

\displaystyle\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{X}^{k;i,j}_{s,t})^{2}=\sup_{k}{\left\lVert{\mathbb{X}^{k;i,j}_{s,t}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{k}{\left\lVert{P_{L^{2}_{k}\otimes L^{2}_{k}}(\mathbb{X}_{s,t}^{i,j})}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\lVert{\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{s,t}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)}=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{X}^{i,j}_{s,t})^{2}.

This completes the level 2 estimates with
1\leq i\neq j\leq d
in (
16
)(
17
)(
18
).

Step 3
: For level 2 and
i=j
, we no longer have the independence between integrand and integrator. Hence the isometry (
20
) fails. Instead, we can rely on explicit calculus computations. As we pointed out before, we have

\mathbb{X}^{k;i,i}_{s,t}=\frac{1}{2}(X^{k;i}_{s,t})^{2}.

As a result,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{X}^{k;i,i}_{s,t}-\mathbb{X}^{i,i}_{s,t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\preceq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left|X^{i}_{s,t}\right|^{4}\mathbb{E}\left|X^{k;i}_{s,t}-X^{i}_{s,t}\right|^{4}}

\displaystyle\preceq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left|X^{i}_{s,t}\right|^{2}\mathbb{E}\left|X^{k;i}_{s,t}-X^{i}_{s,t}\right|^{2}=0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{X}^{k;i,i}_{s,t}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\preceq\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}\left(X^{k;i}_{s,t}\right)^{4}\preceq\sup_{k}\left(\mathbb{E}(X^{k;i}_{s,t})^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}(X^{i}_{s,t})^{2}\right)^{2}\leq C\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

The proof is now complete.
∎

A rather straightforward consequence is the following

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds for
X_{t}=I_{n}(f_{t})
. Define

\mathcal{H}_{X}=\{S_{2}(\langle f_{t},h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}}),\ h\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}\},

Then

Supp(X_{t})\subset Cl(\mathcal{H}_{X}),

where the closure is taken in the
\alpha
-Hölder topology for
\alpha<1/(2\rho)
.

Proof.

This is immediate from the fact that each
X^{k}
we defined in lemma
3.4
can be written as a linear combination of processes in the form
\langle f_{t},h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}}
and the convergence we set up in the last proposition.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

Unlike the case when
n=1
, in general, we do not have the reverse inclusion. To see this, let us consider the following simple example where

f_{t}=t\varphi_{1}\hat{\otimes}\varphi_{1}.

Note that

\{\langle f_{t},h\rangle,\ \forall h\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}2}\}=\{c\cdot\langle f_{t},\varphi_{1}\hat{\otimes}\varphi_{1}\rangle,\ c\in\mathbb{R}\}=\{c\cdot t,\ c\in\mathbb{R}\}.

But on the other hand,

Supp(I_{2}(f_{t}))=\{c\cdot t,\ c\geq-1\}.

This is because

I_{2}(\varphi_{1}\hat{\otimes}\varphi_{1})=\frac{1}{2}(I_{1}(\varphi_{1}))^{2}-1\geq-1.

More generally, the boundedness of all even order Hermite polynomials (which appear as the coefficients of Karhunen-Loève approximation) prevents
X^{k}
from hitting all possible linear combinations of
\{\langle f_{t},h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}}\}
. In addition, these coefficients, although orthonormal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, are no longer independent due to the loss of Gaussianity, which makes it almost impossible to extract any more information.

3.3.
Rough lift of
X_{t}
together with its Malliavin derivatives

In this subsection, let us consider

\hat{X}_{t}:=(X_{t},DX_{t},\cdots,D^{n}X_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{H}^{d}\times\cdots\times(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n})^{d},

which now is a process living in an infinitely dimensional Banach space. This change from finite to infinite dimension has significant impact. To make our arguments as transparent as possible, let us first explain how we used finite dimension to our advantage in our previous constructions for the rough lift of
X_{t}
.

In theorem
3.2
, we used the fact that for any
1\leq i\leq d

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}^{i,i}_{s,t}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{s}^{t}X_{s,r}\otimes dX_{r}\right)^{i,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}X^{i}_{s,r}dX^{i}_{r}\cdot b_{i}\otimes b_{i}=Symm\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{i}_{s,r}dX^{i}_{r}\cdot b_{i}\otimes b_{i}\right),

where
b_{i}
is the
i
-th basis vector of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Symm
denotes the symmetric part. In other words,
\mathbb{X}^{i,i}
is symmetric. This is coming from the fact that each component
X^{i}_{t}
is living in a one dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

X^{i}_{t}=X^{i}_{t}\cdot b_{i}\in\overline{Span}\{b_{i}\},\ \forall t\in[0,1].

Consequently, by the property (
5
) of geometric rough paths, we must define
\mathbb{X}^{i,i}_{s,t}=\frac{1}{2}(X^{i}_{s,t})^{2}
. There is no other choice.

However, when we consider
\hat{X}_{t}
, all the components involving Malliavin derivatives are not one dimensional anymore. They are living in Hilbert spaces of the form
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
. In general

(21)

\int_{s}^{t}D^{k}X^{i}_{s,r}\otimes dD^{k}X^{i}_{r}\neq Symm\left(\int_{s}^{t}D^{k}X^{i}_{s,r}\otimes dD^{k}X^{i}_{r}\right),\ k\geq 1.

As a result, there is no canonical way of defining the iterated integrals appear in (
21
).

To overcome this difficulty, we must directly work with the tensor products of
D^{k}X_{t}
and show that integrals in (
21
) converge in an appropriate sense. The price one needs to pay is to impose a condition that can control the regularity of these tensor products, which motivates our assumption
1.2
.

We prepare another technical lemma before our main result of this section.

Lemma 3.8
.

For
f,g\in\mathcal{H}^{\hat{\otimes}n}
and
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

\langle D^{k}I_{n}(f),D^{k}I_{n}(g)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}=\left(\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\right)^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{n-k}r!\binom{n-k}{r}\binom{n-k}{r}I_{2n-2k-2r}(f\hat{\otimes}_{(r+k)}g).

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
, then we have

\displaystyle\langle D^{k}I_{n}(f),D^{k}I_{n}(g)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,\char 16\relax_{k}\geq 1}\langle D^{k}I_{n}(f),e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}\langle D^{k}I_{n}(g),e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\right)^{2}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,\char 16\relax_{k}\geq 1}\langle I_{n-k}(f),e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}\langle I_{n-k}(g),e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\right)^{2}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,\char 16\relax_{k}\geq 1}I_{n-k}(\langle f,e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}})I_{n-k}(\langle g,e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}).

To ease notations, we define

f^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}:=\langle f,e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}.

Then,

\langle D^{k}I_{n}(f),D^{k}I_{n}(g)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}=\left(\frac{n!}{k!}\right)^{2}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,\char 16\relax_{k}\geq 1}I_{n-k}(f^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}})I_{n-k}(g^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}).

By the product formula for Wiener chaos, we have

\displaystyle I_{n-k}(f^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}})I_{n-k}(g^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{n-k}r!\binom{n-k}{r}\binom{n-k}{r}I_{2n-2k-2r}(f^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\hat{\otimes}_{r}g^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}).

By linearity of symmetrization, we conclude

\langle D^{k}I_{n}(f),D^{k}I_{n}(g)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}=\left(\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\right)^{2}\sum_{r=0}^{n-k}r!\binom{n-k}{r}\binom{n-k}{r}I_{2n-2k-2r}(f\hat{\otimes}_{(r+k)}g).

∎

We can prove our main result of this section now. As before,
\{Q^{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
denotes a sequence of increasing partitions of
[0,1]
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose assumption
1.2
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. Let
\hat{X}^{l}_{t}
be the piece-wise linear approximation of
\hat{X}_{t}
along
\{Q^{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
and
\bm{\hat{X}}^{l}
the natural lift of
\hat{X}^{l}_{t}
, then for any
\rho^{\prime}\in(\rho,3/2)
and
p>1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{l,m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(d_{2\rho^{\prime}-var;[0,1]}(\bm{\hat{X}}^{l},\bm{\hat{X}}^{m})\right)^{p}=0.

In particular,
\hat{X}_{t}
admits a natural geometric rough lift
\bm{\hat{X}}_{t}
and

\mathbb{E}\left({\left\lVert{\bm{\hat{X}}_{t}}\right\rVert}_{2\rho^{\prime}-var;[0,1]}\right)^{p}<+\infty.

Proof.

Let

\hat{X}^{i,j}_{t}=D^{i}X^{j}_{t},\ \hat{X}^{l;i,j}_{t}=D^{i}X^{l;j}_{t},

where
X^{l;j}
is the
j
-th component of
X^{l}_{t}
.
We argue similarly to theorem
3.2
and it is enough to show that for any
s,t\in[0,1]
,
0\leq i,k\leq n,\ 1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq d
and

(22)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{X^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,t}-X^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,t}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}}^{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\epsilon\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{2\rho^{\prime}}},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}dX^{l;k,j_{2}}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}X^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}dX^{m;k,j_{2}}_{r}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(i+k)}}^{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\epsilon\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{\rho^{\prime}}},

Notice that for any
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

\mathbb{E}\langle D^{k}X_{t},D^{k}X_{s}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}=\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\mathbb{E}(X_{s}X_{t})=\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}R_{X}(s,t).

When
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
, we may take advantage of independence between different components and estimates (
22
) and (
23
) can be established in the exact same way as we did in theorem
3.2
with the same

\epsilon={\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}.

We thus omit these details and focus only on (
23
) with
j_{1}=j_{2}
, which is where new ingredients of assumption
1.2
are used.

We first consider the case where
i=k=0,\ j_{1}=j_{2}
(this is the finite dimensional case in our discussion at the beginning of this subsection). We have
\hat{X}^{l;0,j_{1}}=X^{l;j_{1}}
. Since in this case integrals are symmetric , we have by simple calculus that

(24)

\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{l;0,j_{1}}_{s,r}d\hat{X}^{l;0,j_{1}}_{r}=\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;j_{1}}_{s,r}dX^{l;j_{1}}_{r}=\frac{1}{2}(X^{l;j_{1}}_{t}-X^{l;j_{1}}_{s})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(X^{l;j_{1}}_{s,t})^{2}.

Just as theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}X^{l;j_{1}}_{s,r}dX^{l;j_{1}}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}X^{m;j_{1}}_{s,r}dX^{m;j_{1}}_{r}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}\left((X^{l;j_{1}}_{s,t}-X^{m;j_{1}}_{s,t})(X^{l;j_{1}}_{s,t}+X^{m;j_{1}}_{s,t})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{E}(X^{l;i}_{s,t}-X^{m;i}_{s,t})^{2}\mathbb{E}(X^{l;i}_{s,t}+X^{m;i}_{s,t})^{2}

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho^{\prime}-var;[s,t]^{2}}{\left\lVert{R}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[s,t]\times[s,t]}

(25)

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}}_{\infty;[0,1]^{2}}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

Next, for
i,k>0,\ j_{1}=j_{2}
, (
24
) not longer works (this is the infinite dimensional case). Instead, we have

(26)

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}=\int_{[s,t]\times[s,t]}\langle\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r},\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,u}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}}d\langle\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r},\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{u}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}.

As a result,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{m;k,j_{1}}_{r}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle\preceq\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}-\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}+\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d(\hat{X}^{m;k,j_{1}}_{r}-\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r})}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

(27)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\langle\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r_{1}}-\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r_{1}},\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r_{2}}-\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r_{2}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}}d\langle\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r_{1}},\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r_{2}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\langle\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r_{1}},\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r_{2}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}}d\langle\hat{X}^{m;k,j_{1}}_{r_{1}}-\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r_{1}},\hat{X}^{m;k,j_{1}}_{r_{2}}-\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r_{2}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

By the orthogonality of Wiener chaos and lemma
3.8
, the right-hand side of (
27
) can be written as

\displaystyle=\sum_{w=0}^{i\wedge k}C_{n,i,k,w}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\langle f^{l}_{s,r_{1}}-f^{m}_{s,r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{l}_{s,r_{2}}-f^{m}_{s,r_{2}},d(f^{l}_{r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{l}_{r_{2}})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2w}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{w=0}^{i\wedge k}C_{n,i,k,w}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\langle f^{m}_{s,r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{m}_{s,r_{2}},d(f^{m}_{r_{1}}-f^{l}_{r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{m}_{r_{2}}-f^{l}_{r_{2}})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2w}}

(28)

\displaystyle\preceq\sum_{w=0}^{i\wedge k}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}{\left\lVert{f^{l}_{s,r_{1}}-f^{m}_{s,r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{l}_{s,r_{2}}-f^{m}_{s,r_{2}}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2r}}d{\left\lVert{f^{l}_{r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{l}_{r_{2}}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2w}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{w=0}^{i\wedge k}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}{\left\lVert{f^{m}_{s,r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{m}_{s,r_{2}}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2r}}d{\left\lVert{f^{m}_{r_{1}}-f^{l}_{r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{m}_{r_{2}}-f^{l}_{r_{2}}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2w}}.

Moreover, by assumption
1.2
, all functions

R^{w}_{f}(s,t)=f_{s}\hat{\otimes}_{w}f_{t},\ 1\leq w\leq n

satisfy

{\left\lVert{R^{w}([s,t],[u,v])}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{2(n-w)}}}\leq\omega^{\frac{1}{\rho}}([s,t]\times[u,v]).

Thus, we may control the first term on the right hand side of (
28
) as a 2D Young’s integral and get

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}{\left\lVert{f^{l}_{s,r_{1}}-f^{m}_{s,r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{l}_{s,r_{2}}-f^{m}_{s,r_{2}}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2w}}d{\left\lVert{f^{l}_{r_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{(n-w)}f^{l}_{r_{2}}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2w}}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\lVert{R^{n-w}_{f^{l}-f^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho^{\prime}-var;[s,t]^{2}}{\left\lVert{R^{n-w}_{f^{l}}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[s,t]^{2}}

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R^{n-w}_{f^{l}-f^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}}_{\infty;[0,1]}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime}}},

where we used interpolation and lemma
3.1
in the last inequality. Same estimate works for the second term on the right-hand side of (
28
) as well. We thus arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{m;k,j_{1}}_{r}}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}\times\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

(29)

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{R^{n-w}_{f^{l}-f^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}}_{\infty;[0,1]}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{2}{\rho^{\prime}}}.

Combining (
25
) and (
29
) and another application of moment equivalence of Wiener chaos gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{l;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{l;k,j_{1}}_{r}-\int_{s}^{t}\hat{X}^{m;i,j_{1}}_{s,r}\otimes d\hat{X}^{m;k,j_{1}}_{r}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}\times\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}

(30)

\displaystyle\preceq\max_{1\leq w\leq n}{\left\lVert{R^{n-w}_{f^{l}-f^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]}\left|t-s\right|^{\frac{p}{\rho^{\prime}}},\ \forall 0\leq i,k\leq n.

Notice we used in the previous estimate that

{\left\lVert{R_{X^{l}-X^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\infty;[0,1]}={\left\lVert{R^{n}_{f^{l}-f^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\infty;[0,1]}.

Gathering (
30
) and the parts we omitted gives (
22
) and (
23
) with

\epsilon=\max_{1\leq w\leq n}{\left\lVert{R^{n-w}_{f^{l}-f^{m}}}\right\rVert}^{\frac{\rho^{\prime}-\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p}{2}}_{\infty;[0,1]}.

Our result now follows from

\lim_{l,m\rightarrow\infty}\max_{1\leq w\leq n}{\left\lVert{R^{n-w}_{f^{l}-f^{m}}}\right\rVert}_{\infty;[0,1]}=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

We conclude this section with an example. It is well known that we have a wide variety of Gaussian processes that satisfy assumption
1.1
. We show that it is in fact, relatively easy to use these Gaussian processes to construct a chaos process
X_{t}=I_{n}(f_{t}),\ n>1,
that satisfies assumption
1.2
.

Example 3.10
.

Let
Y^{i}_{t}=I_{1}(g^{i}_{t}),\ 1\leq i\leq n
be
n
independent Gaussian processes such that
\{g^{i}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
has finite
\rho
-variation in
\mathcal{H}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
\rho\in[1,3/2)
. Then,

X_{t}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}Y^{i}_{t}=I_{n}(g^{1}_{t}\hat{\otimes}g^{2}_{t}\hat{\otimes}\cdots\hat{\otimes}g^{n}_{t})

satisfies assumption
1.2
.

Proof.

We only show the case for
n=2
, the general case is similar. Let
f_{t}=g^{1}_{t}\hat{\otimes}g^{2}_{t}
, a standard computation gives

\displaystyle f_{s,t}

\displaystyle=g^{1}_{s,t}\hat{\otimes}g^{2}_{t}+g^{1}_{s}\hat{\otimes}g^{2}_{s,t}

\displaystyle f_{u,v}

\displaystyle=g^{1}_{u,v}\hat{\otimes}g^{2}_{v}+g^{1}_{u}\hat{\otimes}g^{2}_{u,v}.

Then,

\displaystyle\left|f_{s,t}\hat{\otimes}_{2}f_{u,v}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\langle g^{1}_{s,t},g^{1}_{u,v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\langle g^{2}_{t},g^{2}_{v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+\langle g^{1}_{t},g^{1}_{v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\langle g^{2}_{s,t},g^{2}_{u,v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[s,t]}{\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[u,v]}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left({\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}+{\left\lVert{g^{i}_{0}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{f_{s,t}\hat{\otimes}_{1}f_{u,v}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\langle g^{1}_{s,t},g^{1}_{u,v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot g_{t}\hat{\otimes}g_{v}+\langle g^{2}_{s,t},g^{2}_{u,v}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot g^{1}_{u}\hat{\otimes}g^{1}_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[s,t]}{\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[u,v]}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left({\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}+{\left\lVert{g^{i}_{0}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{2}.

We conclude that
X_{t}
satisfy assumption
1.2
with control

\omega([s,t]\times[u,v])=2^{\rho-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}^{\rho}_{\rho-var;[s,t]}{\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}^{\rho}_{\rho-var;[u,v]}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left({\left\lVert{g^{i}}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}+{\left\lVert{g^{i}_{0}}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{2\rho}.

∎

4.
Large deviation

The large deviation principle for a single random variable in the
n
-th chaos is well known (see
[
13
]
). Our goal in this section is to first establish a large deviation principle for a general chaos process
X_{t}
, then extend to its rough lift
\bm{X}_{t}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=C([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
equipped with the uniform topology. We define the following rate function on
\mathcal{S}

I(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\left\{\frac{1}{2}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}\mid\ h\in\mathcal{H}^{d},\ \langle f_{t},(h^{i})^{\otimes n}\rangle=x^{i},1\leq i\leq d\right\},\\
+\infty\;\;\text{if no such $h$ exists.}\end{cases}

For a Borel set in
\mathcal{S}
, we set
I(A)=\inf_{x\in A}I(x)
. We first establish a large deviation principle for the process
X_{t}=I_{n}(f_{t})\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. The family of random processes
\{X_{t}(\epsilon\omega)\}_{\epsilon>0}
satisfies a large deviation with
good rate function
I(x)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
d=1
. Since
I_{n}(f_{t})
belongs to the
n
-th homogeneous chaos, by replacing
\epsilon
with
1/r
, it is enough to show

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)

\displaystyle\geq-I(A),\ \forall A\subset\mathcal{S}\ \text{open},

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)

\displaystyle\leq-I(A),\ \forall A\subset\mathcal{S}\ \text{closed}.

We will split our proof into two parts.

Lower Bound
: Let
A
be an open subset of
\mathcal{S}
and
B(\mathcal{H})
be the unit ball in
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose we can find
h\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\in A
(if no such
h
exists, the lower bound is trivially true). Since
A
is open, we can find
s>0
such that

V:=V\left(\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle,s\right)\in A,

where
V(x,s)
denotes the
s
-neighborhood of
x
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)\geq\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}V)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t}\left|X_{t}-r^{n}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\right|<r^{n}s\right)=\mathbb{P}(U),

where

U=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\mid\sup_{t}\left|X_{t}(\omega)-\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\right|<r^{n}s\right\}.

By Cameron-Martin theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(U)=\int_{U-rh}\exp\{r\hat{h}-r^{2}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}/2\}\mathbb{P}(d\omega).

An application of Jensen’s inequality gives

(31)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(U)\geq\exp\left\{-r^{2}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}/2\right\}\mathbb{P}(U-rh)\exp\left\{\frac{r}{\mathbb{P}(U-rh)}\int_{U-rh}\hat{h}\mathbb{P}(d\omega)\right\}

By theorem
3.9
, we know
\sup_{t}{\left\lVert{D^{k}X_{t}}\right\rVert}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p>1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t}\left|X_{t}(x+rh)-r^{n}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\right|\right)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}r^{k}\sup_{t}{\left\lVert{D^{k}X_{t}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{k}\right)=0.

We deduce that when
r
is sufficiently large,

(32)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(U-rh)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t}\left|X_{t}(x+rh)-r^{n}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\right|<r^{n}s\right)>1/2.

On the other hand,

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{r}{\mathbb{P}(U-rh)}\int_{U-rh}\hat{h}\mathbb{P}(d\omega)\geq-\frac{r}{\mathbb{P}(U-rh)}\int_{U-rh}\mid\hat{h}\mid\mathbb{P}(d\omega)\geq-r{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Combining (
31
), (
32
) and (
33
) gives that when
r
is large enough,

\mathbb{P}(U)\geq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left\{-r^{2}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}/2-r{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}\right\}.

Taking limit gives

\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)\geq\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}(U)\geq-\frac{1}{2}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Maximizing over
h
gives

\liminf_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)\geq-I(A),

which is the desired lower bound.

Upper Bound
: Let
A
be a closed set in
\mathcal{S}
and take
0<\lambda<I(A)
. By the definition of
I(A)
, we have

\cup_{h\in B(\mathcal{H})}(2\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\cap A=\emptyset.

Since
B(\mathcal{H})
is compact, the set

K=\cup_{h\in B(\mathcal{H})}(2\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle

is also compact. Hence,
K
and
A
are disjoint, and we can find
\eta>0
such that

\{x\in\mathcal{S}\mid d(x,K)<(2\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}\eta\}\cap A=\emptyset.

Now, let us consider

V^{\prime}=\{\omega\in\Omega\mid\sup_{t}\left|X_{t}(\omega+rh)-r^{n}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\right|<r^{n}\eta\}.

Then we claim that

(34)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)\leq\mathbb{P}\{\omega\in\Omega\setminus(V^{\prime}+r\sqrt{2\lambda}B(\mathcal{H}))\}.

To see this, observe that when
\xi\in(V^{\prime}+r\sqrt{2\lambda}B(\mathcal{H}))
, we can find
\tau\in V^{\prime}
and
h\in B(\mathcal{H})
so that

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\left|X_{t}(\xi)-r^{n}(2\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\right|=\sup_{t}\left|X_{t}(\tau+r\sqrt{2\lambda}h)-r^{n}(2\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle\right|<r^{n}(2\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}\eta,

which implies

\xi\in\left\{X_{t}\in r^{n}\{x\in\mathcal{S}\mid d(x,K)<(2\lambda)^{\frac{n}{2}}\eta\}\right\}\subset\Omega\setminus\left\{X_{t}\in r^{n}A\right\}.

From previous estimate, we know that when
r
is large enough
P(V^{\prime})>1/2
. By Gaussian isoperimetric inequality (see, for instance, theorem 4.33 of
[
2
]
), we have from (
34
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\exp\{-r^{2}\lambda\}.

We can thus deduce

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)\leq-\lambda.

Finally, note that
\lambda<I(A)
is arbitrary. Passing to the limit gives

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in r^{n}A)\leq-I(A),

which is the desired upper bound.
∎

Having established a large deviation principle for
X_{t}
, we are going to use an extended contraction argument to prove a similar result for the rough lift
\bm{X}_{t}
of
X_{t}
. For convenience, we shall write
\Phi_{l}
for the piece-wise linear approximations along a sequence of increasing partitions
\{Q^{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. Let
\delta>0
fixed. Then for any
p>2\rho
, we have

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}}\epsilon^{2}\log\mathbb{P}\bigg(d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(\epsilon x)),\bm{X}(\epsilon x)\right)>\delta\bigg)=-\infty.

Proof.

By homogeneity, we have

d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(\epsilon x)),\bm{X}(\epsilon x)\right)=\epsilon^{n}d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(x)),\bm{X}(x)\right).

For any
q>1
, the moment equivalence of Wiener chaos gives

\mathbb{E}\left|X_{t}\right|^{q}\leq(q-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left|X_{t}\right|^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(x)),\bm{X}(x)\right)>\frac{\delta}{\epsilon^{n}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon^{nq}}{\delta^{q}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(x)),\bm{X}(x)\right)\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon^{nq}}{\delta^{q}}

\displaystyle(q-1)^{\frac{nq}{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(x)),\bm{X}(x)\right)\bigg)^{2}.

To ease notations, we will write

d_{p}(l):=\mathbb{E}\bigg(d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(x)),\bm{X}(x)\right)\bigg)^{2}.

Taking logarithm leads to

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left\{d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(x)),\bm{X}(x)\right)>\frac{\delta}{\epsilon^{n}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq q\log\left(\frac{\epsilon^{n}(q-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\delta}d_{p}(l)\right).

Choose
q=1/\epsilon^{2}
, we see that when
\epsilon
is small enough

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}\log\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left\{d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(X_{t}(x)),\bm{X}(x)\right)>\frac{\delta}{\epsilon^{n}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(\epsilon^{n}(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}-1)^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{d_{p}(l)}{\delta}\right)\leq\log\left(2\frac{d_{p}(l)}{\delta}\right).

By theorem
3.2
,
\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}d_{p}(l)=0
. Our result then follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. For all
M>0
, and
p>2\rho
, we have

(35)

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}d_{p-var}\left(S_{2}\circ\Phi_{l}(x),S_{2}(x)\right)=0.

Proof.

By lemma
3.3
each
x\in\mathcal{S}
with
I(x)<+\infty
has finite
\rho
-variation. We have

\displaystyle\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}{\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle\preceq\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}{\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle\preceq{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{n}\sqrt{{\left\lVert{R}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[0,1]}}\preceq M^{\frac{n}{2}}.

The iterated integrals of
x
are simply given by Young’s integral. Hence we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}{\left\lVert{S_{2}(x)}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}\preceq M^{\frac{n}{2}}.

By the uniform continuity of the map
S_{2}
on bounded sets, it suffices to show that

\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x_{t}-\Phi^{l}(x)_{t}\right|\rightarrow 0,

and (
35
) follows from interpolation. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x_{t}-\Phi^{l}(x)_{t}\right|\leq\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}\max_{t_{i}\in Q^{l}}{\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert}_{\rho-var;[t^{l}_{i},t^{l}_{i+1}]}\leq(2M)^{\frac{n}{2}}\max_{t_{i}\in Q^{l}}R([t^{l}_{i},t^{l}_{i+1}]).

From which we deduce that

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\{x:I(x)<M\}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left|x_{t}-\Phi^{l}(x)_{t}\right|=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

Suppose assumption
1.1
holds for the components of

+\infty\;\;\text{if no such $h$ exists.}\end{cases}

Here

for

Proof.

With proposition

at hand, by theorem 4.2.23 of
[

]
we only need to show the exponential goodness of piece-wise approximations and a

5.
Application to rough differential equations

The rough lifts we constructed in section 3 allow us to study RDEs driven by
X_{t}
.
In this section, let
\{V_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}\subset C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; smooth functions bounded together with all derivatives. We consider

(36)

Y_{t}=y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\bm{X}^{i}_{s},\ y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t\in[0,1].

All our results can be easily generalized to include a drift.

5.1.
Malliavin derivatives of
Y_{t}

Let us digress a little and recall the Faà di Bruno’s formula for high order derivatives of composite functions, which will be helpful later.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then for any
k\geq 1

\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}f(g(x))=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{k}}f^{(\left|\pi\right|)}(g(x))\prod_{\alpha\in\pi}g^{(\left|\alpha\right|)}(x).

Here
\Pi_{k}
is the set of all partitions of the set
\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
,
\alpha\in\pi
means
\alpha
runs through all the blocks of the partition
\pi
and
\left|\pi\right|
denotes the cardinality of
\pi
.

Take
f^{(4)}(g(x))
as an example. We can take
\pi=\{\{1,3\},\ \{2,4\}\}
, which has
\left|\pi\right|=2
. For this partition
\pi
, it has two blocks
\{1,2\}
and
\{3,4\}
. Hence

\prod_{\alpha\in\pi}g^{(\left|\alpha\right|)}(x)=g^{(\left|\{1,3\}\right|)}(x)g^{(\left|\{2,4\}\right|)}(x)=(g^{\prime\prime}(x))^{2}.

Multiplying these two terms gives
f^{\prime\prime}(g(x))(g^{\prime\prime}(x))^{2}
, which is the term in
f^{(4)}(g(x))
corresponding to the partition
\pi=\{\{1,3\},\ \{2,4\}\}
.

Coming back to the RDE (
36
), we make an important observation that the Malliavin derivatives
D^{k}Y_{t}
can be regarded as solutions to RDEs driven by
\bm{\hat{X}}_{t}
along linear vector fields. This is the content of the next

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that assumption
1.2
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. Let
Y_{t}
be given by (
36
). Then, for
k\geq 1
,

(37)

\displaystyle D^{k}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}DV_{i}(Y_{s})D^{k}Y_{s}d\bm{X}^{i}_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{k}\setminus e}D^{(\left|\pi\right|)}V_{i}(Y_{s})\left(\hat{\otimes}_{\alpha\in\pi}D^{(\left|\alpha\right|)}Y_{s}\right)d\bm{X}^{i}_{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{r=1}^{k\wedge n}\binom{n}{r}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{k-r}}D^{(\left|\pi\right|)}V_{i}(Y_{s})\left(\hat{\otimes}_{\alpha\in\pi}D^{(\left|\alpha\right|)}Y_{s}\right)\hat{\otimes}d\bm{D^{r}X}^{i}_{s},

where
e
in the second term on the right-hand side is the trivial partition of
\{1,2,\cdots,k\}
.

Proof.

Let
\{Q^{l}\}_{l\geq 1}
be a sequence of increasing partitions of
[0,1]
. Consider

(38)

Y^{l}_{t}=y_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y^{l}_{s})dX^{l;i}_{s}.

We know from standard rough paths theory that
Y^{l}_{t}
converges to
Y_{t}
uniformly, almost surely. We thus infer that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\langle DY_{t}(\omega),h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{Y_{t}(\omega+rh)-Y_{t}(\omega)}{r}=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Y^{l}_{t}(\omega+rh)-Y^{l}_{t}(\omega)}{r}

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\langle DY^{l}_{t}(\omega),h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

As a result,
DY_{t}=\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}DY^{l}_{t}
. Apply Malliavin derivative to both sides of (
38
), we get

\displaystyle DY^{l}_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}DV_{i}(Y^{l}_{s})DY^{l}_{s}dX^{l;i}_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y^{l}_{s})dDX^{l;i}_{s}.

Thanks to the lift above
\hat{X}_{t}
that we constructed in theorem
3.9
, the previous ODE, by definition, converges to

\displaystyle DY_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}DV_{i}(Y_{s})DY_{s}d\bm{X}^{i}_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}V_{i}(Y_{s})d\bm{DX}^{i}_{s}.

This settles the case for
k=1
.

One can iterate this processes, to see

\displaystyle D^{k}Y_{t}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}D^{k}\left(V_{i}(Y_{s})d\bm{X}^{i}_{s}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{r=0}^{k\wedge n}\binom{n}{r}\int_{0}^{t}D^{k-r}V_{i}(Y_{s})\hat{\otimes}d\bm{D^{r}X}^{i}_{s}.

Finally, we apply Faà di Bruno’s formula (lemma
5.1
) for all the composite derivative terms
D^{k-r}V_{i}(Y_{s})
with the usual multiplication replaced by symmetry tensor product
\hat{\otimes}
. Equation (
37
) then follows. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

We emphasis that we intentionally wrote (
37
) in its current from (singling out the first term which is corresponding to the trivial partition
e
) so that
D^{k}Y_{t}
only shows up in the first term on the right-hand side. It then becomes apparent that
D^{k}Y_{t}
solves a RDE driven by
\bm{\hat{X}}_{t}
with linear vector fields. The combinatorial nature of (
37

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
S,H
be Banach spaces and

a rough path of

for some

. Assume that

for all

be the solution to the following RDE

Then, for any
\alpha>0
, we can find a constant
C>0
that only depends on

, such that

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{Y_{t}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq C\left(\left|y_{0}\right|+{\left\lVert{B_{t}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}{\left\lVert{\bm{X}_{t}}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}\right)\exp\left(C{\left\lVert{A_{t}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\max(1,\alpha^{-1})(2N_{\alpha}(\bm{\hat{X}})+1)\right).

Proof.

We only need to make two adjustments to the arguments of theorem 10.53 of
[
9
]
. First, we separate the roles of

and

instead of introducing a uniform upper bound. Second, just as lemma 4.5 of
[
5
]

[

. The whole procedure is long but elementary. What emerges from it is given by

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{Y_{t}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq C({\left\lVert{y_{0}}\right\rVert}+{\left\lVert{B_{r}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}{\left\lVert{\bm{X}}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[s,t]})\exp\left(C{\left\lVert{A_{r}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\max(1,\alpha^{-1})M_{\alpha}(\bm{\hat{X}})\right)

Finally, our conclusion follows from the relation

∎

Remark 5.5
.

As we have already seen in (
37
), the RDE of

has polynomials of all lower order Malliavin derivatives of

in the place of

in (

to be exponentially integrable. Thus, to study the integrability of

and

so that

We are ready for our main result.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose that assumption
1.2
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. Let

Then, for any

{\left\lVert{D^{k}Y_{t}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\in L^{\xi/k}(\Omega)
, where

Proof.

We apply proposition
5.4
to (

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{D^{k}Y_{t}}\right\rVert}_{\infty}\leq C_{\ref{Linear RDE estimate}}\cdot\Gamma(k)\cdot{\left\lVert{\bm{\hat{X}}_{t}}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}\cdot\exp(\Xi),

(41)

\displaystyle+{\left\lVert{V}\right\rVert}_{C^{\infty}_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{r=1}^{k\wedge n}\binom{n}{r}\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{k-r}}{\left\lVert{\hat{\otimes}_{\alpha\in\pi}D^{(\left|\alpha\right|)}Y_{s}}\right\rVert}_{\infty},

h\in\mathcal{H}^{d}
, we have

{\left\lVert{\bm{T}_{h}\hat{\bm{X}}_{t}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\leq C(n,p){\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\left(\frac{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{np+p/2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}-1}{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}-1}\vee\frac{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{(n+1)p}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}-1}{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}-1}\right).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
d=1
. For level 1, we have for each
0\leq k\leq n
that

\displaystyle D^{k}X_{t}(\omega+h)=D^{k}X_{t}(\omega)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}\frac{1}{j!}\cdot\langle D^{k+j}X_{t}(\omega),h^{\otimes j}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes j}}.

As a result,

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{D^{k}X_{t}(\omega+h)}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,k)\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}{\left\lVert{D^{k+j}X_{t}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{pj}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,k){\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{jp}_{\mathcal{H}}

(43)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p){\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\frac{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{(n+1)p}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}

Similarly for level 2, we have for any
0\leq k,j\leq n
that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}D^{k}X_{s,r}(\omega+h)\otimes dD^{j}X_{r}(\omega+h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i_{1}\leq n-k\\
0\leq i_{2}\leq n-j\end{subarray}}C(n,i_{1},i_{2})\int_{s}^{t}\langle D^{k+i_{1}}X_{s,r}(\omega),h^{\otimes i_{1}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i_{1}}}\otimes d\langle D^{j+i_{2}}X_{r}(\omega),h^{\otimes i_{2}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i_{2}}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}D^{k}X_{s,r}(\omega+h)\otimes dD^{j}X_{r}(\omega+h)}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{p/2-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p,k,j)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i_{1}\leq n-k\\
0\leq i_{2}\leq n-j\end{subarray}}{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}D^{k}X_{s,r}(\omega)\otimes dD^{j}X_{r}(\omega)}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{p/2-var;[0,1]}{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}}^{(i_{1}+i_{2})p/2}.

As a result,

\displaystyle{\left\lVert{\int_{s}^{t}D^{k}X_{s,r}(\omega+h)\otimes dD^{j}X_{r}(\omega+h)}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{p/2-var;[0,1]}

(44)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p){\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\frac{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{np+p/2}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}

Combining (
43
) (
44
), we conclude

{\left\lVert{\bm{T}_{h}\hat{\bm{X}}_{t}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\leq C(n,p){\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\left(\frac{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{np+p/2}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}\vee\frac{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{(n+1)p}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}{{\left\lVert{h}\right\rVert}^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}\right).

∎

Next we establish the tail behavior of
N_{\alpha}(\bm{\hat{X}})
.

Proposition 5.11
.

Suppose that assumption
1.2
holds for the components of
X_{t}
. For any
p>2\rho
, we can find
\kappa>0
such that, when
M>0
is big enough,

(45)

\mathbb{P}\left\{N_{\alpha}(\bm{\hat{X}})>M\right\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha M}{4C(n,p)\kappa}\right)^{2/(np)}\right\},

where
C(n,p)
is the constant appears in proposition
5.10
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
d=1
. For any
\kappa>0
, let

F(\kappa):=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:{\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}_{t}}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}<\kappa\right\}.

We claim that for
\kappa>0
fixed and
M>0
big enough, we have

(46)

\left\{N_{\alpha}(\bm{\hat{X}})>M\right\}\subset\Omega\setminus(F(\kappa)+r_{M}B(\mathcal{H})),

where
B(\mathcal{H})
is the unit ball in
\mathcal{H}
,

r_{M}=\left(\frac{\alpha M}{4C(n,p)\kappa}\right)^{1/(np)},

and
C(n,p)
is the constant appears in proposition
5.10
.

To see this, observe that for any
\omega\in F(\kappa)+r_{M}B(\mathcal{H})
, we can write

\omega=\omega^{\prime}+rh,\ \omega^{\prime}\in F(\kappa),\ 0\leq r\leq r_{M},\ h\in B(\mathcal{H}).

Then, by (
6
) and proposition
5.10

\displaystyle\alpha N_{\alpha}(\bm{\hat{X}})(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq{\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}_{t}(\omega)}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}={\left\lVert{\bm{T}_{rh}\hat{\bm{X}}_{t}(\omega^{\prime})}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p){\left\lVert{\hat{\bm{X}}_{t}(\omega^{\prime})}\right\rVert}^{p}_{p-var;[0,1]}\left(\frac{{\left\lVert{rh}\right\rVert}^{np+p/2}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}{{\left\lVert{rh}\right\rVert}^{p/2}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}\vee\frac{{\left\lVert{rh}\right\rVert}^{(n+1)p}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}{{\left\lVert{rh}\right\rVert}^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(n,p)\kappa\left(\frac{(r_{M})^{np+p/2}-1}{(r_{M})^{p/2}-1}\vee\frac{(r_{M})^{(n+1)p}-1}{(r_{M})^{p}-1}\right).

If we can find

\omega\in\left\{N_{\alpha}(\bm{\hat{X}})>M\right\}\cap\left(F(\kappa)+r_{M}B(\mathcal{H})\right),

then

(47)

\frac{\alpha M}{C(n,p)\kappa}\leq\frac{(r_{M})^{np+p/2}-1}{(r_{M})^{p/2}-1}\vee\frac{(r_{M})^{(n+1)p}-1}{(r_{M})^{p}-1}.

When
M
is large enough, (
47
) implies

\frac{\alpha M}{2C(n,p)\kappa}\leq(r_{M})^{np}=\frac{\alpha M}{4C(n,p)\kappa},

which is a contradiction. Thus, we must have (
46
).

Finally, we may choose
\kappa
big enough so that
\mathbb{P}(F(\kappa))\geq 1/2
. Then, by Gaussian isoperimetric inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{N_{\alpha}>M\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\mathbb{P}(F(\kappa)+r_{M}B(\mathcal{H}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\exp\left(-r^{2}_{M}/2\right)=\frac{1}{2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha M}{4C(n,p)\kappa}\right)^{2/(np)}\right\}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

It is worthwhile making a number of comments. When substituting
n=1
into (
45
), our result is weaker than theorem 6.3 of
[
5
]
. To see where the sharpness loss is coming from, we need to go back to the
\mathcal{H}
translation of
X_{t}

X_{t}(\omega+rh)-X_{t}(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{r^{k}}{k!}\cdot\langle D^{k}X_{t}(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}},\ r>0,\ h\in\mathcal{H}.

The term on the right-hand side can be recast in another way

\langle D^{k}X_{t}(\omega),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}=C(k)\langle I_{n-k}(f_{t}),h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}=C(k)I_{n-k}\left(\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}\right).

The last term
\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
, corresponding to
k=n
, is deterministic with finite
\rho
-variation (proposition
3.3
), while others are random with finite
p
-variation (or
2\rho^{+}
-variation). This is due to the fact that multiple Wiener integrals tend to make the trajectories “twice more irregular” than its kernels. For instance, in the classical Wiener space, Brownian motion can be written as
W_{t}=I_{1}(f_{t})
, with
f_{t}=1_{[0,t]}
, which has finite
1
-variation in
L^{2}([0,1])
. While the trajectories of
W_{t}
have finite
2^{+}
-variation.

Coming back to our discussion. When
n=1
(the Gaussian case),
\mathcal{H}
translation leads to adding a deterministic process
\langle f_{t},h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
whose regularity is significantly better than that of
X_{t}
, which in turn leads to a slower growth of
{\left\lVert{X_{t}(\omega+h)}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}
and
N_{\alpha}(\hat{\bm{X}}(\omega+h))
. This is the essential reason, estimate in theorem 6.3 of
[
5
]
can replace the exponent
2/p
in (
45
) by
2/\rho
. When
n>1
, the adding terms must include random processes of form
I_{n-k}\left(\langle f_{t},h^{\otimes k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}}\right)
that have same regularity as
X_{t}
. As a result,
{\left\lVert{X_{t}(\omega+h)}\right\rVert}_{p-var;[0,1]}
and
N_{\alpha}(\hat{\bm{X}}(\omega+h))
grow drastically faster than the
n=1
case. With current method and no additional assumption on
\{f_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
, estimate in (
45
) is the best we can get for general chaos processes.

It will be interesting to investigate what type of additional algebraic structures we can put on the kernels
\{f_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
to make up for the loss of Gaussianity so that (
45
) can be improved.

Although proposition
5.11
does not provide exponential integrability for
N_{\alpha}(\hat{\bm{X}})
, we can still achieve local integrability, which is sufficient for many studies including the absolute continuity of the law of
Y_{t}
.

Corollary 5.13
.

Under the assumptions of theorem
5.6
, we have that for any
t\in[0,1]
,
Y_{t}\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty}_{loc}
.

Proof.

From proposition
5.11
, we know that for any
\alpha>0
,
\mathbb{E}(N_{\alpha}(\hat{X}))^{p}<\infty
for all
p>0
. As a result,

\cup_{M\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\{\omega\in\Omega:N_{\alpha}(\hat{X}(\omega))\leq M\}=\Omega.

From theorem
5.6
, we know that
D^{k}Y_{t}\cdot\bm{1}_{\{N_{\alpha}(\hat{X})\leq M\}}
is bounded almost surely, hence
L^{p}(\Omega)
integrable for any
p>1
. This gives the desired localization of
D^{k}Y_{t}
for any
k\geq 1
.
∎

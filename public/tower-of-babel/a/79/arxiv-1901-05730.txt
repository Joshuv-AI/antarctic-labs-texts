Restrictions on endomorphism rings of jacobians and their minimal fields of definition
Pip Goodman

Abstract
. Zarhin has extensively studied restrictions placed on the endomorphism algebras of Jacobians for which the Galois group associated to their 2-torsion is insoluble and “large”. Here, we examine what happens when this Galois group merely contains an element of “large” prime order. As part of this we obtain a partial converse to a result by Guralnick and Kedlaya on the endomorphism field.

Introduction

Let
K
be a number field, and
f\in K[x]
a polynomial of degree
n
with no repeated roots. Let
C_{f}
be a smooth projective model of the smooth affine curve

y^{2}=f(x).

Denote the Jacobian of
C_{f}
by
J_{f}
, and the splitting field of
f
by
K(f)
.

Zarhin has extensively studied restrictions placed on the endomorphism ring of
J_{f}
, when the Galois group
{\rm Gal}(f)={\rm Gal}(K(f)/K)
is insoluble and large with respect to
g
, the dimension of
J_{f}

[
Zar00
]

[
Zar01
]

[
Zar05
]
.
As part of an algorithm to compute the endomorphism algebra of a genus 2 Jacobian, Lombardo proves that if
f
is irreducible and has degree 5, then
J_{f}
is absolutely simple
[
Lom19
, Proposition 4.7]
.

Inspired by the results of Lombardo and Zarhin, we investigate restrictions placed on the endomorphism algebra of
J_{f}
when
{\rm Gal}(f)
merely contains an element of “large” prime order. Most of our results in fact hold for more general abelian varieties, but for the sake of exposition we state them in this section for odd degree hyperelliptic Jacobians.

We write
{\rm End}(J_{f})
for the ring of
\bar{K}
-endomorphisms of
J_{f}
and
{\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})={\rm End}(J_{f})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
for its endomorphism algebra. Let
\zeta_{n}
denote a primitive

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p=2g+1
be a prime such that
2
is a primitive root modulo
p
. Suppose
f\in K[x]
is irreducible of degree
p
. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

E:={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field, and both

a)

2
is totally inert in
E/\mathbb{Q}
,

b)

the order
{\rm End}(J_{f})
is
2
-maximal in
E
.

(2)

J_{f}
is isogenous over
\bar{K}
to the self product of an absolutely simple abelian variety with complex multiplication by a proper subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
.

We prove the above theorem in a more general context in §2, see Theorem
2.10
. A major step in the proof of Theorem
2.10
is to obtain a partial converse to a theorem of Guralnick and Kedlaya on the possible degrees of the minimal extension
L/K
over which all endomorphisms of
J_{f}
are defined, see Theorem
2.6
and the remark afterwards.

It should also be noted that if the Galois group of
f
is known, we can often find restrictions on
[E:\mathbb{Q}]
using Remark
2.3
.

When the dimension of
J_{f}
is odd, we are able to relax the condition on the order of
2
modulo
p
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
g
be odd, and
p=2g+1
a prime such the index of
\langle 2\rangle
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}
is at most 2. Suppose
f\in K[x]
is irreducible of degree
p
. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

E:={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field, and provided
{\rm End}(J_{f})
is
2
-maximal, either

a)

2
is totally inert in
E/\mathbb{Q}
, or

b)

there are two distinct primes above
2
with equal inertia degree in
E
.

(2)

J_{f}
is isogenous over
\bar{K}
to the self product of an absolutely simple abelian variety with complex multiplication by a proper subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
.

Again, we prove a more general statement in §2, see Theorem
2.16
. We also prove the following extension of Lombardo’s result mentioned earlier. For the more general case of abelian surfaces, see Theorem
2.11
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f(x)\in K[x]
be an irreducible polynomial of degree 5. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

{\rm End}(J_{f})\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

{\rm End}(J_{f})\cong\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1+r\sqrt{D}}{2}]

In particular

is absolutely simple and does not have quaternion multiplication over

2.
Upper bounds on endomorphism rings

In what follows
K
is a number field,
A
is an abelian variety over
K
of dimension
g
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
is a prime. If
B/K
is also an abelian variety, then we say
A
is isogenous to
B
and write
A\thicksim B
to mean
A
is isogenous to
B
over
\bar{K}
. We denote by
{\rm End}(A)
the ring of
\bar{K}
-endomorphisms of
A
and
{\rm End}^{0}(A)={\rm End}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
for its endomorphism algebra.

The absolute Galois group
G_{K}={\rm Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
acts on
{\rm End}(A)
and hence on
{\rm End}^{0}(A)
. Its invariant subspace
{\rm End}(A)^{G_{K}}={\rm End}_{K}(A)
is the ring of
K
-endomorphisms of
A
. Likewise we write
{\rm End}^{0}(A)^{G_{K}}={\rm End}^{0}_{K}(A)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in a division algebra
E=\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
of dimension
nm^{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, where
n=[Z(E):\mathbb{Q}]
. Then any division algebra contained in
\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
has dimension less than or equal to
nm
over
\mathbb{F}_{l}
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
is a division algebra then
m=1
, i.e.,
E
is a field.

Proof.

Let
R\subseteq\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
be a division algebra of maximal dimension. As
R
is a finite division algebra, we have by Wedderburn’s Theorem that
R\cong\mathbb{F}_{l^{d}}
for some
d
. Hence, the elements of
R
have minimal polynomials of degree at most
d
over
\mathbb{F}_{l}
. In addition, there is
\bar{a}\in R
, whose minimal polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{l}
has degree
d
.

Let us choose
a\in\mathcal{O}
such that its image in
\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
coincides with
\bar{a}
. Then
Z(E)(a)
is a field, so has dimension over
\mathbb{Q}
less than or equal to
nm

[
FD93
, Corollary 3.17, page 96]
. Hence the minimal polynomial of
a
over
\mathbb{Q}
has degree at most
nm
. Since this must be also hold true for the image of
a
in
\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain
d\leq nm
.
∎

We say that an order
\mathcal{O}
in a number field
E
is
l
-maximal
if
l
does not divide the index of
\mathcal{O}
in the ring of integers of
E
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
G_{K}={\rm Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
acts irreducibly on
A[l]
. Then the following hold:

(1)

E={\rm End}^{0}_{K}(A)
is a number field.

(2)

The order
{\rm End}_{K}(A)
is
l
-maximal in
E
.

(3)

l
is totally inert in
E/\mathbb{Q}
.

In particular,
A
is simple over
K
and does not have quaternion multiplication over
K
.

Proof.

As
G_{K}
acts irreducibly on
A[l]
, Schur’s Lemma gives us that the endomorphism algebra
{\rm End}_{K}(A[l])={\rm End}(A[l])^{G_{K}}
is a division algebra. Furthermore,
{\rm End}_{K}(A[l])
is finite, so by Wedderburn’s Theorem it is a field. Hence the subalgebra
R:={\rm End}_{K}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
of
{\rm End}_{K}(A[l])
is also a field. It follows that
E={\rm End}^{0}_{K}(A)
contains no idempotents, and hence is a division algebra. By Lemma
2.1
,
E
is a number field.

As
R
is a finite field of characteristic
l
, we have
R\cong\mathbb{F}_{l^{d}}
where
d=[E:\mathbb{Q}]
. Hence
R
contains elements of degree
d
. As
[E:\mathbb{Q}]=d
the preimages of these elements in
E
must also have minimal polynomials of degree
d
. Let
\alpha\in{\rm End}_{K}(A)
be one such element, and
\mu
be its minimal polynomial. Note that
\alpha\otimes 1
satisfies
\bar{\mu}
, the reduction of
\mu
modulo
l
, and as
\alpha\otimes 1
has degree
d
over
\mathbb{F}_{l}
, we have that
\bar{\mu}
is irreducible. Since
\mathbb{F}_{l}
is a perfect field,
\bar{\mu}
does not have repeated roots, hence
l\nmid{\rm disc}(\mu)
. Thus
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
is an
l
-maximal order in
E
, and so is
{\rm End}_{K}(A)
.

Let
\lambda
be a prime above
l
in
E
. As we are considering endomorphisms defined over
K
, we have
A[\lambda]
is a non-zero
G_{K}
submodule of
A[l]
. But
G_{K}
acts irreducibly on
A[l]
, so
A[l]=A[\lambda]
. We conclude
l
is totally inert in
E/\mathbb{Q}
, see
[
Shi98
, §5.1, Prop 2, pg 36]
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In the proof of Proposition
2.2
, we show
\mathbb{F}={\rm End}_{K}(A[l])
is a field and
{\rm End}_{K}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Z}/l\mathbb{Z}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}
. As
{\rm End}_{K}(A)
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module, this shows
[E:\mathbb{Q}]
divides
[\mathbb{F}:\mathbb{F}_{l}]
. It follows that if we can determine
{\rm End}(A[l])^{G_{K}}={\rm End}_{K}(A[l])
, then we can also limit the possible values of
[E:\mathbb{Q}]
.

We now briefly discuss the endomorphism field of an abelian variety, that is, the minimal extension of the base field over which all endomorphisms are defined.

The action of the absolute Galois group
G_{K}
on
{\rm End}(A)
induces a representation

G_{K}\rightarrow{\rm Aut}\left({\rm End}(A)\right)

with kernel
{\rm Gal}(\bar{K}/L)
, where
L
is the endomorphism field of
A
.
In particular, we obtain an injection of abstract groups

{\rm Gal}(L/K)\hookrightarrow{\rm Aut}\left({\rm End}(A)\right).

Thus if
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
is large with respect to
g
, the dimension of
A
, then one might expect the structure of
{\rm End}(A)
to be constrained. The following theorem shows this is indeed the case. But first we need a couple of lemmas.

For a division ring
E
, we shall write
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
for the invertible elements in
M_{d}(E)
, and
{\rm PGL}_{d}(E)
for the quotient of
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
by its centre.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E
be a division ring, and
F
a maximal subfield of
E
. Write
m=[E:F]
. Suppose
{\rm PGL}_{d}(E)
contains an element
\bar{M}
of order
n
, with
n
coprime to
md
. Then
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
contains an element of order
n
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a lift of
\bar{M}
to
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
. We have
M^{n}=kI_{d}
for some
k\in Z(E)^{*}
. Taking the reduced norm gives
{\rm Nrd}(M)^{n}=k^{md}
. As
n
and
md
are coprime there exists integers
a,b
such that
an+bmd=1
. Let
c=k^{a}{\rm Nrd}(M)^{b}
, then
c^{n}=k
and as
{\rm Nrd}(M)\in Z(E)^{*}
we have
c\in Z(E)^{*}
. The element
\frac{1}{c}M\in{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
then has order
n
. Indeed,
(\frac{1}{c}M)^{n}=I_{d}
, and if
(\frac{1}{c}M)^{n^{\prime}}=I_{d}
for some
n^{\prime}<n
, then
\bar{M}
would not have order
n
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p=2g+1
be prime,
d
a divisor of
2g
, and
E
be a division algebra of dimension less than or equal to
2g/d
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose
{\rm Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}}(M_{d}(E))
contains an element of order
p
. Then
E
is isomorphic to a subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
and has degree
2g/d
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}}(M_{d}(E))
be an element of order
p
. This element acts trivially on
Z(E)
, the centre of
E
, as
{\rm Aut}(Z(E)/\mathbb{Q})
has order less than
p
. Thus
\sigma
belongs to
{\rm Aut}_{Z(E)}(M_{d}(E))
, which by the Skolem-Noether Theorem, is isomorphic to
{\rm PGL}_{d}(E)
. Applying Lemma
2.4
, we obtain an element of order
p
in
{\rm GL}_{d}(E)
.

Let
F
be a maximal subfield of
E
, then
F
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
in some fixed algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
. We have
M_{d}(E)\otimes F\cong M_{md}(F)
, where
m=[E:F]
, see
[
FD93
, Corollary 3.17, page 96]
. Hence we have an element
\bar{\sigma}
of order
p
in
{\rm GL}_{md}(F)
. Now consider
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
in our fixed algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. The Galois group
{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})\cap F)
cyclically permutes the eigenvalues of
\bar{\sigma}
, which gives us that
|{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})\cap F)|\leq md
. The inequality

2g=[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}):\mathbb{Q}]\leq md[F\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}):\mathbb{Q}]

coupled with

m[F:\mathbb{Q}]=[E:F][F:\mathbb{Q}]=[E:\mathbb{Q}]\leq 2g/d

shows
F
is isomorphic to a field of dimension
2g/md
over
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
. Furthermore, as
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})/\mathbb{Q}
is a cyclic extension, there is a unique such subfield. In particular all maximal subfields of
E
are isomorphic. But finite dimensional non-commutative division algebras over
\mathbb{Q}
have non-isomorphic maximal subfields
[
Sch68
, Theorem 5.4]
, thus
E=F
is a field. ∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose
p=2g+1
is a prime divisor of
[L:K]
. Then

A
is isogenous over
\bar{K}
to the self product of an absolutely simple abelian variety with complex multiplication by a proper subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a simple factor of
A_{\bar{K}}
, so that
A_{\bar{K}}\thicksim B^{d}\times C
over
\bar{K}
and
C
has no factor isogenous to
B
. In this way,
B^{d}
corresponds to a primitive central idempotent
e_{B}
of the algebra
{\rm End}^{0}(A)
satisfying
e_{B}{\rm End}^{0}(A)={\rm End}^{0}(B^{d})
. In addition, there is some positive integer
N
such that
(Ne_{B})(A)=B^{d}
.

The Galois group
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
acts on the (finite) set of primitive central idempotents in
{\rm End}^{0}(A)
. By assumption the order of
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
is divisible by
p
, and so contains an element
\sigma
of order
p
. Either
\sigma
fixes
e_{B}
or the orbit of
e_{B}
under the action of
\sigma
has size
p
. But
A
has dimension less than
p
, so the latter is not possible, hence
\sigma
fixes
e_{B}
. It follows that
\sigma
fixes
{\rm End}^{0}(B^{d})
. Indeed, for
\varphi\in{\rm End}^{0}(B^{d})
, we have
\varphi^{\sigma}=(e_{B}\varphi)^{\sigma}=e_{B}\varphi^{\sigma}\in{\rm End}^{0}(B^{d})
. This also shows
B^{d}
is defined over
L^{\langle\sigma\rangle}
, for
\sigma(B^{d})=\sigma(Ne_{B}A)=Ne_{B}A=B^{d}
.

As
\sigma
acts non-trivially on
{\rm End}(A)
, we may, after possibly exchanging
B
for a different simple factor of
A_{\bar{K}}
, assume
\sigma
acts non-trivially on
{\rm End}(B^{d})
. Thus the cyclic group
{\rm Gal}(L/L^{\langle\sigma\rangle})
of order
p
acts faithfully on
{\rm End}(B^{d})
. Viewing
B^{d}
as an abelian variety over
L^{\langle\sigma\rangle}
, we see the minimal extension over which all its endomorphisms are defined has degree divisible by
p
. Applying
[
Sil92
, Theorem 4.1]
, we obtain the dimension of
B^{d}
is at least
g
. Thus we find that
A_{\bar{K}}\thicksim B^{d}
. Since
\dim(A)=\dim(B^{d})=d\dim(B)
, we have
\dim(B)=g/d
. By
[
Sil92
, Proposition 4.3]
, the dimension of
{\rm End}^{0}(A)
over
\mathbb{Q}
is at least
p
, in particular
A
is not absolutely simple, thus
d>1
.

Albert’s Classification
[
Mum70
, Page 202]
tells us that
E={\rm End}^{0}(B)
has dimension less than or equal to
{2g/d}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, so by Lemma
2.5
,
E
is isomorphic to a subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
of degree
2g/d
. Finally, we observe
B
is a CM abelian variety as
E
is a field and
[E:\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim(B)
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

The above theorem gives a partial converse to the main theorem of
[
GK17
]
. Indeed, there the authors give an upper bound on the powers of primes dividing
[L:K]
in terms of
g
, and show it is the best possible by exhibiting families of abelian varieties which achieve this bound.

We shall use the following corollary in our proof of Theorem
1.3
. We note that it may be deduced from results of
[
FKRS12
]
(see also
[
Lom19
, Definition 2.7]
for a summary), since they show the endomorphism field of an abelian surface is an extension of the ground field with degree dividing 48. Likewise, another proof of this fact is given in
[
GK17
]
. But for the convenience of the reader we provide a direct proof using Theorem
2.6
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Suppose
g=2
, then
5
does not divide
[L:K]
.

Proof.

Suppose
5
divides
[L:K]
. Then by Theorem
2.6
,
A
is isogenous to the square of an elliptic curve with CM by a proper subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5})
. But all proper subfields of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5})
are totally real, so we have a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.9

Let

Before proving our next result, we shall recall some standard facts on Frobenius groups and make a few remarks on the endomorphism field of an abelian variety.

A finite group
G
is said to be a Frobenius group if
G
contains a subgroup
H
called the Frobenius complement such that

H\cap gHg^{-1}=\{1\}\text{ for all }g\in G\setminus H.

Frobenius proved that for such groups there exists a normal subgroup
N
of
G
such that
G\cong N\rtimes H
. We call
N
the Frobenius kernel.
Left multiplication by elements of
G
on
X
, the set of left cosets of
H
, gives rise to a transitive action and the above condition tells us the identity is the only element which fixes two or more elements of
X
. Hence, this action is at most doubly transitive and the stabiliser
G_{b}
of a point
b\in X
has no fixed points in
X\setminus\{b\}
. Applying Burnside’s Lemma to
G_{b}
and
X\setminus\{b\}
(or
X
) shows this action is doubly transitive if and only if
|H|=|N|-1
, that is,
|G|=|N|^{2}-|N|
. In this case, it can be shown that
N
is a
p
-elementary group
[
Pas68
, Propositon 8.4]
, and hence
N
may be regarded as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
vector space and
G
as a subgroup of the affine general linear group on this vector space.

Examples of Frobenius groups include
F_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\rtimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\cong{\rm AGL}(1,q)
,
D_{n}
for
n
odd, and
C_{11}^{2}\rtimes{\rm SL}_{2}(5)
, with Frobenius complements
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, a subgroup of order 2 and
{\rm SL}_{2}(5)
respectively.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G=N\rtimes H
be a Frobenius group with complement

and order
|N|^{2}-|N|
. Suppose

is a subgroup of

with index

dividing
|N|-1
.

Then

acts transitively on
X
, the set of left cosets of
H
, and a point stabiliser

has exactly
d
orbits on
X\setminus\{b\}
.

Proof.

As

, we have
U
acts transitively on

. For the second statement, observe that the non-identity elements of

act fixed point freely on

. Thus applying Burnside’s Lemma, we find the number of orbits of
U_{b}
on

is equal to

\frac{1}{|U_{b}|}\sum_{g\in U_{b}}|{\rm Fix}(g)|=\frac{d}{|N|-1}(|N|-1)=d.

∎

Let

, Thm 6.34]
that the restriction of
\mathbb{C}[G/H]^{0}
to
N
is isomorphic to the direct sum of
n-1
distinct non-trivial linear representations. Since
2
does not divide the order of
N
, this also holds for the restriction of
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[G/H]^{0}
to
N
. Putting this together with the fact that
U
is a normal subgroup, we see the restriction of
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[G/H]^{0}
to
U
is semisimple with non-isomorphic irreducible consituents, by Clifford’s Theorem
[
Isa94
, Thm 6.5]
.
∎

Recall that we have an injection of abstract groups

{\rm Gal}(L/K)\hookrightarrow{\rm Aut}\left({\rm End}(J_{f})\right)

where
L/K
is the minimal extension over which all endomorphisms of
J_{f}
are defined. In particular if
E={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field, then
{\rm Gal}(L/K)\hookrightarrow{\rm Aut}(E)
and so
|{\rm Gal}(L/K)|
divides
[E:\mathbb{Q}]
. Recall also that if
E={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field then
[E:\mathbb{Q}]
divides
2\dim(J_{f})
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f\in K[x]
be a polynomial of odd degree
n
with Galois group isomorphic to a Frobenius group
G
of order
n(n-1)
.

Suppose
E={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field of dimension
s
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
E/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois with
{\rm Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
isomorphic to a quotient of
H
the Frobenius complement of
G
.

Furthermore,
L/K
is an extension of degree
s
contained in
K(f)
, and as abstract groups,
{\rm Gal}(L/K)\cong{\rm Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, if
s=n-1
, then
L=EK
.

Finally, if
{\rm End}(J_{f})
is
2
-maximal, then
E
is unramified at 2.

Proof.

By the above, we have that
|{\rm Gal}(L/K)|
divides
s
. Thus
[L:K]\leq s
and
L\cap K(f)\subseteq F
for some
F
, where
F/K
is an extension of degree
s
contained in
K(f)
.

Let
F^{\prime}=L\cap K(f)
. By the above
F^{\prime}/K
an extension contained in
F
of degree
d
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
dividing
s
.
Since
E={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a field,
J_{f}
is absolutely simple and so
s
divides
n-1=2\dim(J_{f})
. We may apply Lemma
3.3
, to find the group
{\rm Gal}(K(f)/F^{\prime})\cong{\rm Gal}(L(f)/L)
acts transitively on
R_{f}
, and the stabiliser of a point
\alpha\in R_{f}
, has
d
many orbits on
R_{f}\setminus\{\alpha\}
. Thus, by Proposition
3.2
we have that

s=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\rm End}^{0}_{L}(J_{f})\leq d.

Hence
d=s
, and
L\cap K(f)=F
. But
[F:K]=s
and
[L:K]\leq s
, so we find that
L=F
.

Since
|{\rm Aut}(E)|\leq s
the injection of abstract groups
{\rm Gal}(L/K)\hookrightarrow{\rm Aut}(E)
from above is an isomorphism. Whence we deduce that
|{\rm Aut}(E)|=s=[E:\mathbb{Q}]
and so
E/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois. We now conclude
{\rm Gal}(L/K)={\rm Gal}(F/K)
is isomorphic to a quotient of the Frobenius complement of
G
.

If
s=n-1
, then
J_{f}
is a CM abelian variety. Furthermore, as
E/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois the reflex field
E^{*}
is a subfield of
E

[
Shi98
, Prop. 28, pg62]
. Since
J_{f}
is absolutely simple its CM type is primitive. Proposition 30 on page 65 of
[
Shi98
]
applies to tell us the endomorphism field
L
equals
E^{*}K
. In particular,
[E^{*}:\mathbb{Q}]\geq s=[E:\mathbb{Q}]
. It follows
E^{*}=E
and
L=EK
.

It remains to show
E/\mathbb{Q}
is unramified at 2 when
{\rm End}(J_{f})
is 2-maximal. To this end, note that by Lemma
3.4
above and Theorem 5.1 of
[
Zar01
]
we have
J_{f}[2]\otimes\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
is semisimple as a
{\rm Gal}(K(f)/L)
-module, with no repeated simple constituents. Thus
J_{f}[2]
also has no repeated simple constituents as a
{\rm Gal}(K(f)/L)
-module. The result now follows since if
2
ramified in
E
, then
J_{f}[2]
would have repeated factors.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power. Let
f\in K[x]
be a polynomial of degree
q
with Galois group
F_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\rtimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\cong{\rm AGL}(1,q)
.

Suppose
E={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field of dimension
s
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
E/\mathbb{Q}
is cyclic Galois and
L/K
is the unique extension of degree
s
contained in
K(f)
.
If furthermore,
s=q-1
, then
L=EK
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
3.5
by noting that
F_{q}
is a 2-transitive Frobenius group with cyclic Frobenius complement.

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
f\in K[x]
be an irreducible polynomial of odd degree
n
with Galois group
D_{n}
.

Suppose
E={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field of dimension
n-1
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
L\cap K(f)
is the unique extension
F/K
of degree
2
contained in
K(f)
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.5
. Indeed,
[L:K]
divides
[E:\mathbb{Q}]=n-1
, and
[K(f):K]=2n
, so
[L\cap K(f):K]
divides
\gcd(2n,n-1)=\gcd(2,n-1)=2
. As
D_{n}
has a unique normal subgroup of index 2, we deduce the Galois extension
L\cap K(f)/K
is a subfield of the unique degree 2 extension
F/K
contained in
K(f)
.

Let
F^{\prime}=L\cap K(f)
. By the above either
F^{\prime}=K
or
F^{\prime}=F
thus
F^{\prime}/K
is an extension of degree
d
diving
2
.
As
D_{n}
is a Frobenius group,
{\rm Gal}(K(f)/F^{\prime})\cong{\rm Gal}(L(f)/L)
acts transitively on
R_{f}
, and the stabiliser of a point
\alpha\in R_{f}
, is cyclic of order
2/d
, and has
\frac{n-1}{2/d}=\frac{d(n-1)}{2}
many orbits on
R_{f}\setminus\{\alpha\}
. Thus, by Proposition
3.2
we have that

n-1=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\rm End}^{0}_{L}(J_{f})\leq\frac{d(n-1)}{2}.

Hence
d=2
, and
L\cap K(f)=F
.
∎

To finish the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we require the following observation.

Proposition 3.9
.

The Galois group of
L/K
, the minimal extension over which all endomorphisms of
A
are defined, fits into the exact sequence

0\rightarrow H\rightarrow{\rm Gal}(L/K)\rightarrow{\rm Gal}(K(A[2])/K),

where
H
is a (possibly trivial) elementary abelian 2-group.

Proof.

By Silverberg’s result
[
Sil92
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have that
L
is contained in
K(A[4])
.
Furthermore,
{\rm Gal}(K(A[2])/K)
and
{\rm Gal}(K(A[4])/K)
are subgroups of
{\rm GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
and
{\rm GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})
respectively. Thus, as the following sequence is exact

0\rightarrow I_{2g}+2M_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow 0

and
I_{2g}+2M_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})\cong M_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is an elementary abelian 2-group (under addition) we are done.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
f\in K[x]
be an irreducible polynomial of degree
n
with Galois group
D_{n}
and
n\equiv 3\mod{4}
.

Suppose
E={\rm End}^{0}(J_{f})
is a number field of dimension
n-1
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
L
is the unique extension
F/K
of degree
2
contained in
K(f)
.

Proof.

As
E
is a number field, the discussion preceding Theorem
3.5
shows
[L:K]
divides
n-1
. But the order of
{\rm Gal}(K(f)/K)={\rm Gal}(K(J_{f}[2])/K)
is
2n
, so by Proposition
3.9
we have that
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
is a 2-group.

Since
4
does not divide
n-1
, it does not divide

either. Thus

. Hence, applying Proposition

we find

.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose
A/K
is an abelian surface with CM by a degree 4 field and
2\nmid[K(A[2]):K]
. Then
[L:K]\leq 2
and
K
contains a real quadratic field.

Proof.

By assumption
E={\rm End}^{0}(A)
is a degree 4 CM field. Let us note that as
A
is absolutely simple it has primitive CM type
[
Shi98
, §8.2, page 60]
. By
[
Shi98
, §8.4 Examples, pages 63-65]
there are only two such CM types for a genus 2 curve. One of which is not Galois and the other is cyclic of degree 4. The minimal field of definition of the endomorphisms is
L=E^{*}K
the compositum of
K
and the reflex field of
E
, see
[
Shi98
, Proposition 30, page 65]
.

In the non-Galois case,
[
Shi94
, Proposition 5.17(5), page 131]
gives us that
K
contains the real quadratic subfield of the reflex field, and thus also
[L:K]\leq 2
. In the other case, the reflex field
E^{*}
is equal to
E={\rm End}^{0}(A)
. As
[L:K]
divides
4
the image of
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
in
{\rm Gal}(K(A[2])/K)
is trivial, thus by Proposition
3.9
we have that
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
has exponent dividing 2. Hence
{\rm Gal}(L/K)
is isomorphic to either the trivial group,
C_{2}
, or
C_{2}\times C_{2}
. If
{\rm Gal}(L/K)\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
held, then
E^{*}=E
would have Galois group
{\rm Gal}(E^{*}/\mathbb{Q})\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
, but this implies the CM type of
A
is not primitive
[
Shi98
, §8.4 Examples, 2A, page 64]
, contrary to what we have shown. Thus
[L:K]\leq 2
, and as
E
contains a real quadratic subfield, we see that
K
must too.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Suppose
A/K
is an abelian surface and
{\rm Gal}(K(A[2])/K)\cong C_{5}
. Then
[L:K]\leq 2
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.11
, we have that
{\rm End}^{0}(A)
is either isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}
, a real quadratic field, or a degree 4 CM field. In the first two cases
[L:K]\leq 2
follows from the discussion before Theorem
3.5
, and in the last case it follows from Proposition
3.11
.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
A
be as in Proposition
3.12
. If
A
has CM, then
K
contains a real quadratic field with discriminant congruent to
5
modulo
8
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.11
,
K
contains a real quadratic field.
Let us now show the discriminant condition. Recall that discriminant of a real quadratic field is congruent to 5 modulo 8 if and only if 2 is inert. Theorem
1.3
gives us that 2 is inert in
E/\mathbb{Q}
, thus in the case that
E
is Galois, the claim follows immediately by the above.

Let us suppose that
E
is not Galois. Write
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
for the real quadratic subfield of
E
, with
d\equiv 5\mod{8}
square-free (and hence equal to the discriminant of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
). The reflex field of
E
is also not a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, and has a totally real subfield
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d^{\prime}})
, where
d^{\prime}\neq d
is square-free
[
Shi98
, 8.4 Examples, 2C]
. By what we have already shown above, it suffices to prove
d^{\prime}\equiv 5\mod{8}
.

The number field
M
obtained as the compositum of
E
and its reflex
E^{*}
is a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, with Galois group
{\rm Gal}(M/\mathbb{Q})\cong D_{4}
of order 8. We claim that
2
splits in
M/\mathbb{Q}
and each prime above 2 has inertia degree 4. As 2 is inert in
E/\mathbb{Q}
, it suffices to show
2
splits in
M/\mathbb{Q}
. We shall rule out the other possibilities. Let
D
denote the decomposition group of a prime above
2
in
M
. Note that as
D_{4}
is not cyclic,
2
is not totally inert in the extension
M/\mathbb{Q}
. Next, suppose
2
ramifies in
M/\mathbb{Q}
, then by comparing orders, we see
D
is isomorphic to
D_{4}
, but as the inertia degree is 4, it also has a cyclic quotient of order 4, which
D_{4}
does not. Thus
2
is not ramified in the extension
M/\mathbb{Q}
. We deduce that
D
is isomorphic to the unique degree 4 cyclic subgroup of
D_{4}
.

By examining the subgroup structure of
D_{4}
, we see the subfield fixed by
D
is
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{dd^{\prime}})
the quadratic subfield of
M
not contained in
E
or
E^{*}
. By definition of
D
we have that 2 splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{dd^{\prime}})
. Equivalently,
dd^{\prime}\equiv 5d^{\prime}\equiv 1\mod{8}
, from which it follows that
d^{\prime}\equiv 5\mod{8}
.

∎

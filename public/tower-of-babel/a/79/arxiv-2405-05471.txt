Extension of Kirkwood-Buff theory: Partial enthalpies, fluctuations of energy density, temperature, and pressure, and solute-induced effects in a mixture solvent
Akira Onuki

Abstract
We present a statistical mechanical theory of multi-component fluids, where we consider the correlation functions of the number densities and the energy density in the grand canonical ensemble. In terms of their space integrals we express the partial volumes v ¯ i {\bar{v}}_{i} , the partial enthalpies H ¯ i {\bar{H}}_{i} , and other thermodynamic derivatives. These v ¯ i {\bar{v}}_{i} and H ¯ i {\bar{H}}_{i} assume simple forms for binary mixtures and for ternary mixtures with a dilute solute. They are then related to the space-dependent thermal fluctuations of the temperature and the pressure. The space averages of these fluctuations are those introduced by Landau and Lifshits in the isothermal-isobaric ( T T - p p ) ensemble. We also give expressions for the long-range (nonlocal) correlations in the canonical and T T - p p ensembles, which are inversely proportional to the system volume. For a mixture solvent, we examine the solvent-induced solute-solute attraction and the osmotic enthalpy changes due to the solute doping using the correlation function integrals. Keywords : Fluctuation solution theory, Partial enthalpies, Energy density fluctuation, Temperature-pressure fluctuations, Solute-solute interactions

2. Thermodynamics of multi-component fluids

2.1. Partial quantities at fixed
p
and
T

We consider an equilibrium fluid mixture composed of

m
components without electrostatic interactions
in a macroscopic volume
V
.
We assume no applied field such as gravity and electric field
and neglect the boundary effects.
In this section, we present fundamental thermodynamic relations
needed in Sections 3-7.

Let us consider an extensive
quantity
Q
as a function of the temperature
T
,
the pressure
p
, and the particle numbers
N_{i}=Vn_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq m)

with
n_{i}
being the average densities.
Its partial quantities per particle are defined by

{\bar{Q}}_{i}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{Q}}{\partial{N_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,p,N_{j\neq i}},

(1)

where we fix
T
,

p
, and
N_{j}
with
j\neq i
in the derivative.
The extensivity of
Q
means the relation

Q(T,p,\{\lambda N\})=\lambda Q(T,p,\{N\})
for
N_{i}\lambda\gg 1
, which
yields the sum rule,

Q={\sum}_{i}{\bar{Q}}_{i}N_{i}=V{\sum}_{i}{\bar{Q}}_{i}n_{i}.

(2)

For the volume
V
and the total entropy
S
,
the partial volumes
{\bar{v}}_{i}
and
the partial entropies
{\bar{S}}_{i}
are written as

{\bar{v}}_{i}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{V}}{\partial{N_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,p,N_{j\neq i}},~~{\bar{S}}_{i}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{S}}{\partial{N_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,p,N_{j\neq i}}.

(3)

For the Gibbs free energy
G
, its partial
quantities are equal to the chemical potentials
\mu_{i}

from its differential equation,

dG={\sum}_{i}\mu_{i}dN_{i}-SdT+Vdp.

(4)

Therefore, from the enthalpy relation
H=G+TS
,
the partial enthalpies are given by

{\bar{H}}_{i}=\mu_{i}+T{\bar{S}}_{i}.

(5)

Varying
(\{N\},T,p)
we have the differential equation,

dQ={\sum}_{i}{\bar{Q}}_{i}dN_{i}+{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{Q}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{p,\{N\}}\hskip-2.84526ptdT+{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{Q}}{\partial{p}}{\bigg)}_{T,\{N\}}\hskip-2.84526ptdp.

(6)

For
Q=V
and
S
the above relation is written as

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptdV={\sum}_{i}{\bar{v}}_{i}dN_{i}+V\alpha_{p}dT-V\kappa_{T}dp,

(7)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptdS={\sum}_{i}{\bar{S}}_{i}dN_{i}+VC_{p}dT/T-V\alpha_{p}dp,

(8)

where
\alpha_{p}
is the isobaric thermal expansivity,

\kappa_{T}
is the isothermal compressibility, and

C_{p}
is the isobaric heat capacity per unit volume. In the
thermodynamic limit
V\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\alpha_{p}=-\frac{1}{n}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{n}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{p,\{x\}},~~~\kappa_{T}=\frac{1}{n}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{n}}{\partial{p}}{\bigg)}_{T,\{x\}},~~~{C_{p}}={nT}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{\sigma}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{p,\{x\}},

(9)

where
n=\sum_{i}n_{i}=N/V
and
N={\sum}_{i}N_{i}
.
Here,
\sigma=S/N
is the entropy per particle
and is related to the entropy density by

s=S/V=n\sigma
, so
VC_{p}=T(\partial S/\partial T)_{p,\{N\}}=NT(\partial\sigma/\partial T)_{p,\{x\}}
.
Note that the thermodynamic derivatives of
intensive quantities at fixed
\{N\}
are
those at fixed molar concentrations
\{x\}
.
In this paper,
\{x\}
denotes the set of

x_{i}=N_{i}/N=n_{i}/n
,
while
\{n\}
denotes the set of
n_{i}
.

We further consider the density variable
q\equiv Q/V
.
From Eqs.(6) and (7), it obeys

dq={\sum}_{i}{\bar{Q}}_{i}dn_{i}+{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{q}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{p,\{x\}}\hskip-2.84526ptdT+{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{q}}{\partial{p}}{\bigg)}_{T,\{x\}}\hskip-2.84526ptdp+q(\alpha_{p}dT-\kappa_{T}dp).

(10)

Since
q=1
for
Q=V
, Eq.(10) yields the differential equation

[
36
]
,

{\sum}_{i}{\bar{v}}_{i}dn_{i}+\alpha_{p}dT-\kappa_{T}dp=0,

(11)

where the first term is the incremental
change in the particle
volume fraction (see Eqs.(38) and (47)).
Then, we have two expressions for
{\bar{v}}_{i}
:

{\bar{v}}_{i}=\kappa_{T}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{p}}{\partial{n_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,n_{j\neq i}},~~{\bar{v}}_{i}=-\alpha_{p}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{T}}{\partial{n_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{p,n_{j\neq i}}.

(12)

The first relation is convenient
for dilute mixtures, since
p

can be expanded with respect to
the solute densities (see Section 6).
The second relation follows from the first
since
\alpha_{p}=\kappa_{T}(\partial p/\partial T)_{\{n\}}

(see Eq.(27)).

Next, we define a symmetric matrix

\{I_{ij}\}
and its inverse matrix
\{I^{ij}\}
as

\hskip-0.31866ptI_{ij}={k_{B}T}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{n_{j}}}{\partial{\mu_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,\mu_{k\neq i}}\hskip-2.84526pt,~I^{ij}=\frac{1}{k_{B}T}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{\mu_{j}}}{\partial{n_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,n_{k\neq i}}\hskip-2.84526pt,

(13)

where
T
is fixed and
{\sum}_{k}I_{ik}I^{kj}=\delta_{ij}
.
Using the Gibbs-Duhem relation
in the first relation in Eq.(12),
we can relate
\{{\bar{v}}\}
and
\{{n}\}
as

{\bar{v}}_{i}=k_{B}T\kappa_{T}{\sum}_{j}I^{ij}n_{j},~~{\sum}_{j}I_{ij}{\bar{v}}_{j}=k_{B}T\kappa_{T}n_{i}.

(14)

The isothermal compressibility
\kappa_{T}
is then expressed as

k_{B}T{\kappa_{T}}=1{\big/}\big[{\sum}_{i,j}n_{i}n_{j}I^{ij}\big]={\sum}_{i,j}{\bar{v}}_{i}{\bar{v}}_{j}I_{ij}.

(15)

We note that Eq.(4) yields the well-known expression,

{\bar{v}}_{i}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{\mu_{i}}}{\partial{p}}{\bigg)}_{T,\{x\}}=k_{B}T{\sum}_{j}I^{ij}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{n_{j}}}{\partial{p}}{\bigg)}_{T,\{x\}}.

(16)

which is equivalent to Eq.(12)
from
(\partial n_{j}/\partial p)_{T,\{x\}}=n_{j}\kappa_{T}
for any
j
.
Then, Eq.(4) also gives

{\bar{S}}_{i}=-{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{\mu_{i}}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{p,\{x\}},~~{\bar{H}}_{i}=-T^{2}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{(\mu_{i}/T)}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{p,\{x\}}.

(17)

Here, we have
(\partial w/\partial T)_{p,\{x\}}=(\partial w/\partial T)_{\{n\}}-\alpha_{p}n(\partial w/\partial n)_{T,\{x\}}
for any
w
.

2.2. Derivatives of energy density with
respect to
n_{i}

We next introduce the thermodynamic
derivatives of the internal energy density
e

with respect to the number densities
n_{i}
:

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptM_{i}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{e}}{\partial{n_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,n_{j\neq i}}=-T^{2}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{(\mu_{i}/T)}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{\{n\}},

(18)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912ptR_{i}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{e}}{\partial{n_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{p,n_{j\neq i}}=M_{i}-\frac{1}{\alpha_{p}}C_{V}{\bar{v}}_{i},

(19)

where we fix
T
in
M_{i}

and
p
in
R_{i}
. In Eq.(18) use is made of

(\partial s/\partial n_{i})_{T,n_{j\neq i}}=-(\partial\mu_{i}/\partial T)_{\{n\}}
,
while in Eq.(19)
R_{i}-M_{i}

follows from the second relation in Eq.(12).
The
C_{V}
is the isochoric heat
capacity per unit volume.
Then,

obeys the differential equations,

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055ptde={\sum}_{i}M_{i}d{n}_{i}+C_{V}{dT}

(20)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt={\sum}_{i}R_{i}d{n}_{i}+T\alpha_{s}{dp}.

(21)

where
T\alpha_{s}=(\partial e/\partial p)_{\{n\}}=C_{V}(\partial T/\partial p)_{\{n\}}

(see Eqs.(26) and (27)). These equations
follow from the fundamental equation
de=Tds+{\sum}_{i}\mu_{i}dn_{i}
.
From Eqs.(17)-(19) the differences
{\bar{H}}_{i}-{M}_{i}
and
{\bar{H}}_{i}-{R}_{i}

are proportional to
{\bar{v}}_{i}
as

{\bar{H}}_{i}=M_{i}+T\beta_{V}{\bar{v}}_{i}=R_{i}+T\beta_{s}{\bar{v}}_{i}.

(22)

See the definitions of

C_{V}
,
\alpha_{s}
,
\beta_{V}
, and
\beta_{s}

in Eq.(26). We can also
derive
\beta_{s}-\beta_{V}=C_{V}/T\alpha_{p}
from Eq.(27),
so Eq.(22) is consistent with Eq.(19).

From Eq.(22) the enthalpy density
h=H/V={\sum}_{i}{\bar{H}}_{i}n_{i}

is expressed as

h=e+p={\sum}_{i}M_{i}n_{i}+T\beta_{V}={\sum}_{i}R_{i}n_{i}+T\beta_{s},

(23)

in terms of
M_{i}
and
R_{i}
.
Thus,
{\bar{H}}_{i}
can also be expressed as

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{\bar{H}}_{i}=M_{i}+{\bar{v}}_{i}\big(h-{\sum}_{j}M_{j}n_{j}\big)

(24)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=R_{i}+{\bar{v}}_{i}\big(h-{\sum}_{j}R_{j}n_{j}\big).

(25)

Vlugt
[
33
]
derived Eq.(24)
and used it
to calculate
{\bar{H}}_{i}
for binary mixtures.

at fixed

In addition to

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptC_{V}=T{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{s}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{\{n\}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}C_{p},~~~\beta_{V}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{p}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{\{n\}},~~\beta_{s}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{p}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{\sigma,\{x\}},

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\alpha_{s}=\frac{1}{n}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{n}}{\partial{T}}{\bigg)}_{\sigma,\{x\}},~~\kappa_{s}=\frac{1}{n}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{n}}{\partial{p}}{\bigg)}_{\sigma,\{x\}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\kappa_{T}.

Here,

The

,

, and

, while

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{\gamma-1}=\gamma{\beta_{V}}/\beta_{s}={\alpha_{p}}/{\alpha_{s}}=T\alpha_{p}^{2}/\kappa_{T}C_{V},

3. Correlation functions

3.1. Grand-canonical variances

For any space-dependent fluctuating
variables
\hat{\cal A}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
and

\hat{\cal B}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
,
we consider the space integral of their equal-time correlation function
in the bulk region
[
34
]
,

\langle{\hat{\cal A}:\hat{\cal B}}\rangle=\int\hskip-1.42262ptd{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{{\hat{\cal B}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle

(28)

where
\delta{\hat{\cal A}}={\hat{\cal A}}-\langle{{\hat{\cal A}}}\rangle
,

\delta{\hat{\cal B}}={\hat{\cal B}}-\langle{{\hat{\cal B}}}\rangle
,

\langle{\cdots}\rangle
denotes the equilibrium
average, and the positions
r
and
0

are located far from the boundaries.
The correlation
\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{{\hat{\cal B}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle
is taken in the grand-canonical
(
T
-
\mu
) ensemble at fixed

[
37
]
, so it
decays rapidly if
r=|{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}|
much exceeds the
molecular correlation lengths.
We call
\langle{\hat{\cal A}:\hat{\cal B}}\rangle

the

-
\mu
variance of
\hat{\cal A}
and

\hat{\cal B}
.
In this paper, space-dependent quantities
with a caret are thermally fluctuating variables.

The local number densities and the local energy density have
well-defined microscopic expressions in terms of the
particle positions and momenta, so they are
written as

{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})=n_{i}+\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}),~~{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})=e+\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}),

(29)

where

and

are the equilibrium

Then, as will be shown in Appendix A, the thermodynamic derivatives

in Eq.(13) are equal to the density variances:

I_{ij}=\langle{{\hat{n}}_{i}:{\hat{n}}_{j}}\rangle=n_{i}\delta_{ij}+n_{i}n_{j}G_{ij}.

(30)

where

are the Kirkwood-Buff (KB) integrals

[

,
2
,
4
,
3
,
5
]
.
On the other hand,

are equal to
\delta_{ij}/n_{i}-I^{ij}

In this paper, we consider the integrals of

correlation functions of the energy density and the number

n_{i}J_{i}=\langle{{\hat{e}}:{\hat{n}}_{i}}\rangle=\int d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\langle{\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle,

(31)

where
J_{i}
remains finite as

.
Then,

and
\langle{{\hat{e}}:{\hat{e}}}\rangle

are expressed as

\displaystyle\hskip-54.06023ptn_{i}J_{i}={\sum}_{j}M_{j}I_{ji}

(32)

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433pt={\sum}_{j}R_{j}I_{ji}+k_{B}T^{2}\alpha_{s}n_{i}

(33)

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433pt={\sum}_{j}{\bar{H}}_{j}I_{ji}-k_{B}T^{2}\alpha_{p}n_{i},

(34)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\langle{{\hat{e}}:{\hat{e}}}\rangle=k_{B}T^{2}C_{V}+{\sum}_{i,j}M_{i}M_{j}I_{ij},

(35)

in terms of

,

, and

.
Here, Eqs.(32) and (35) will be derived
in Appendix A, while
Eqs.(33) and (34) follow from Eq.(32)
with the aid of Eqs.(22) and (27).
Note that Eq.(32) is rewritten as

,
which was derived by

is expressed

-
\mu

[

From Eq.(34)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{\bar{H}}_{i}=T\beta_{V}{\bar{v}}_{i}+{\sum}_{j}n_{j}J_{j}I^{ji}

3.2. Fluctuations of volume fraction and concentrations

We introduce the fluctuations of

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\delta{\hat{\phi}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})={\sum}_{i}{\bar{v}}_{i}{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})-1={\sum}_{i}{\bar{v}}_{i}\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}),

.

3.3. Correlations in the canonical ensemble

In finite systems, the correlation functions
can have a long-range (nonlocal) part
in the canonical ensemble
[
40
,
59
,
60
,
57
,
58
,
61
]
,
where the particle numbers
N_{i}
and the volume

V
are fixed. That is, for any space-dependent fluctuating
variables
{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
and
{\hat{\cal B}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
,
their space correlation in the canonical
ensemble
\langle{\cdots}\rangle_{\rm ca}
,
and that in the
T
-
\mu
one
\langle{\cdots}\rangle
are related by
[
40
]

\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{{\hat{\cal B}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{{\hat{\cal B}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle-{\sum}_{i,j}D^{ij}_{{\cal A}{\cal B}}I_{ij}/V,

(41)

where
r
and
0

are located far from the boundaries.
In the right hand side,
the first term is the short-ranged

T
-
\mu
correlation, while
the second term is a nonlocal constant proportional to
V^{-1}

and is small for large
V
. The coefficients

D^{ij}_{{\cal A}{\cal B}}
are defined by

D^{ij}_{{\cal A}{\cal B}}={\bigg(}\frac{\partial{\langle{\hat{\cal A}}\rangle}}{\partial{n_{i}}}{\bigg)}_{T,{n_{k\neq i}}}{\bigg(}\frac{\partial{\langle{\hat{\cal B}}\rangle}}{\partial{n_{j}}}{\bigg)}_{T,{n_{\ell\neq j}}}={\sum}_{k,\ell}\langle{{\hat{\cal A}}:{{\hat{n}}_{k}}}\rangle\langle{{\hat{\cal B}}:{{\hat{n}}_{\ell}}}\rangle I^{ki}I^{\ell j},

(42)

where we use Eq.(A5).
Then, integrating Eq.(41) in the cell, we find

\frac{1}{V}\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{1}\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{2}\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{1})\delta{{\hat{\cal B}}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{2})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=\langle{{\hat{\cal A}}:{{\hat{\cal B}}}}\rangle-{\sum}_{i,j}\langle{{\hat{\cal A}}:{{\hat{n}}_{i}}}\rangle\langle{{\hat{\cal B}}:{{\hat{n}}_{j}}}\rangle I^{ij},

(43)

where the second term is independent of
V

and can be comparable to the first.
Note that the finite-size correction
due to the boundaries is of order
L^{-1}
in Eq.(43),
where
L=V^{1/3}
is the linear dimension of the cell.
A simple derivation of Eq.(41)
was given in Appendix C in Ref.
[
61
]
.
More results on the canonical correlations will be given
around Eq.(52). The nonlocal correlations in the
T
-
p

ensemble will be studied in Section 4.5.

For
\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}
and
\delta{\hat{e}}

we rewrite Eq.(41) as

\displaystyle\hskip-31.29802pt\langle{\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{n}}_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=\langle{\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{n}}_{j}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle-I_{ij}/V,

\displaystyle\hskip-31.29802pt\langle{\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=\langle{\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle-n_{i}J_{i}/V,

\displaystyle\hskip-31.29802pt\langle{\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=\langle{\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle-{\sum}_{i,j}M_{i}M_{j}I_{ij}/V,

(44)

where we use Eqs.(18) and (32).
Here, the space integrals of the
first two relations vanish and
that of the third one is

k_{B}T^{2}C_{V}
from Eq.(35), which should hold
in the canonical ensemble.

4. Fluctuations of hydrodynamic variables

4.1. Fluctuations of temperature and pressure

Using the thermodynamic equations (20) and (21),
we define the local fluctuations of the temperature and
the pressure as
linear combinations

of
\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
and
\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})

[
34
]
:

\displaystyle\hskip-48.36967pt\delta{\hat{T}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})=\big[\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})-{\sum}_{i}M_{i}\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\big]/C_{V},

(45)

\displaystyle\hskip-48.36967pt\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})=\big[\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})-{\sum}_{i}R_{i}\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\big]/T\alpha_{s},

(46)

where
M_{i}
and
R_{i}
are defined in Eqs.(18) and (19).
These fluctuations are superimposed on the
thermodynamic temperature and pressure
T
and
p
.
They consist of the Fourier components
with wavelengths longer than
the molecular correlation lengths, so they
obey the Gaussian distributions in the linear regime. These definitions
can be used for any statistical ensembles.
We remove
\delta{\hat{e}}
from Eqs.(45) and (46)
using Eqs.(19) and (27) to find

\delta{\hat{\phi}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})={\sum}_{i}{\bar{v}}_{i}\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})=-\alpha_{p}\delta{\hat{T}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})+\kappa_{T}\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}).

(47)

where
\delta{\hat{\phi}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
is given in Eq.(38).
Notice that the above fluctuation equation assumes the same form as
the thermodynamic differential equation (11).

From Eqs.(32)-(35) and (40)
we find the
T
-
\mu
variances of
\delta{\hat{T}}
and

\delta{\hat{p}}
:

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\langle{{{\hat{n}}_{i}}:{\hat{T}}}\rangle=0,~~\langle{{\hat{n}}_{i}:{\hat{p}}}\rangle=k_{B}Tn_{i},~~\langle{{\hat{e}}:{\hat{T}}}\rangle=k_{B}T^{2},~~\langle{{\hat{e}}:{\hat{p}}}\rangle=k_{B}Th,

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\langle{{\hat{T}}:{\hat{T}}}\rangle=k_{B}T^{2}/C_{V},~~\langle{{{\hat{p}}}:{\hat{T}}}\rangle=k_{B}T/\alpha_{s},~~\langle{{\hat{p}}:{\hat{p}}}\rangle=k_{B}T/\kappa_{s},~~

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\langle{{{\hat{T}}}:{\hat{\phi}}}\rangle=\langle{{\hat{T}}:{\hat{X}}_{i}}\rangle=0,~~\langle{{{\hat{p}}}:{\hat{\phi}}}\rangle=k_{B}T,~~\langle{{\hat{p}}:{\hat{X}}_{i}}\rangle=0,

(48)

The first relation shows that
\delta{\hat{T}}
is
orthogonal
to

\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}
, so Eqs.(45) and (48) yield Eq.(35).
In Eq.(A12) in Appendix A, we will also introduce
the entropy fluctuation.

In dynamics, the Fourier components of
the fluctuating hydrodynamic
variables have been assumed to obey the linearized hydrodynamic
equations with random source terms (as Langevin equations)

[
62
,
63
,
64
]
,
where the wavelengths are longer than
the molecular correlation lengths.
Their time correlation functions have been calculated with
the Laplace transformation method in the time range
t>0
,
where their initial values
at
t=0
are given by the variances in Eq.(48).
These results are used to interpret
the dynamic light scattering.

In the vicinity of the fluid criticality,
we can define
\delta{\hat{T}}

and
\delta{\hat{p}}
by Eqs.(45) and (46)
if the upper cutoff wave number
\Lambda

of the fluctuations is smaller than the inverse
correlation length
\xi^{-1}
(after the renormalization procedure),
where
\xi
grows and
the most singular parts of
\delta{\hat{e}}

and
\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}
are cancelled
to vanish. We can further define them for
\xi^{-1}\lesssim\Lambda\ll a^{-1}

with
a
being the typical molecular radius
if we assume a mapping relationship between
fluids and Ising ferromagnets
[
34
,
65
,
66
]
.
Indeed, in near-critical ferromagnets,
the thermal fluctuations of the temperature and the magnetic
field can be equated to
the functional derivatives of the (fluctuating)
Ginzburg-Landau-Wilson
free energy (with respect to the energy density
and the magnetization)
[
67
,
34
]
,
where they depend on the magnetization fluctuation
nonlinearly for
\xi^{-1}\lesssim\Lambda\ll a^{-1}

(in the course of the renormalization).

Our definitions of
\delta{\hat{T}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})

and
\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
in Eqs.(45) and (46)
are natural and rather obvious. It is worth noting that
Kittel
[
68
]
strictly wrote ”Temperature
fluctuation is an oxymoron
because the consistent and consensual
definition of temperature admits no fluctuation.”

4.2. Entropy deviation in second order

Greene and Callen
[
28
,
69
]

obtained the correlation moments of
general fluctuating extensive variables by calculating the
entropy change induced by them.
In the same spirit, Boltzmann’s principle of equal probability
indicates that the equilibrium distribution of
\delta{\hat{e}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})

and
\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
at long wavelengths is given by

P_{\rm eq}(\delta{\hat{e}},\delta{\hat{n}})={\rm const.}\exp\big[\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}(\delta{\hat{s}})_{2}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})/k_{B}\big],

(49)

where
(\delta{\hat{s}})_{2}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
is
the second-order deviation at position
r
in the fluctuating
entropy density in the Gaussian approximation at long wavelengths:

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt(\delta{\hat{s}})_{2}=s({\hat{e}},\{{\hat{n}}\})-s({e},\{{n}\})-\delta{\hat{e}}/T+{\sum}_{i}\mu_{i}\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}/T

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=-{C_{V}}(\delta{\hat{T}}/T)^{2}/{2}-k_{B}{\sum}_{i,j}{I^{ij}}\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}\delta{\hat{n}}_{j}/2.

(50)

Here, we perform the expansion of
s({\hat{e}},\{{\hat{n}}\})

with respect to
\delta{\hat{e}}={\hat{e}}-{e}
and

\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}={\hat{n}}_{i}-{n}_{i}
, where
s({e},\{{n}\})
is
the thermodynamic entropy density and

\delta{\hat{T}}
is defined by Eq.(45)
[
27
]
.
Thus, the above entropic distribution

P_{\rm eq}
surely gives the

T
-
\mu
variances in Eq.(48).

4.3. Fluctuations of stress tensor and pressure

The space-dependent
stress tensor
{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
(

)

in terms of the particle positions and momenta

[

,
34
]

The divergence

density (without applied force field).
The thermodynamic pressure
p
is obtained from
the equilibrium average
\langle{{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}p

In Appendix A, we will derive variance relations,

\langle{{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}:{\hat{n}}_{i}}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}k_{B}Tn_{i},~~\langle{{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}:{\hat{e}}}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}k_{B}Th,

(51)

where the right hand sides
of these two relations
coincide with
\delta_{\alpha\beta}\langle{{\hat{p}}:{\hat{n}}_{i}}\rangle
and
\delta_{\alpha\beta}\langle{{\hat{p}}:{\hat{e}}}\rangle
, respectively, from Eq.(48).
Thus, the pressure fluctuation
\delta{\hat{p}}
in
Eq.(46) is the
projection

of the diagonal part of
{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}p

onto
\delta{\hat{e}}
and
\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}

[
71
,
72
,
34
]
.
Indeed, if we use Mori’s linear
projection operator
{\cal P}
onto
the hydrodynamic fluctuations
[
72
]
,
we have

{\cal P}{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}(p+\delta{\hat{p}}).

The difference
{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}^{\perp}={\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}(p+\delta{\hat{p}})

is
orthogonal
to the hydrodynamic variables,
which was examined by some authors
[
73
,
74
]
.
In the linear response theory,
the viscosities are
given by the space-time integral
of the dynamical correlation

\langle{{\hat{\Pi}}_{\alpha\beta}^{\perp}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}},t){\hat{\Pi}}_{\gamma\delta}^{\perp}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}},0)}\rangle

[
34
,
72
,
71
]
.

If
{\cal{\hat{B}}}={\hat{p}}
in Eq.(41),
Eqs.(42), (46), and (47) give the following canonical correlations:

\displaystyle\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle-\langle{{\hat{\cal A}}:{\hat{\phi}}}\rangle/(V\kappa_{T}),

\displaystyle\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=\beta_{V}\langle{{\hat{\cal A}}:{\hat{T}}}\rangle.

(52)

If
{\cal{\hat{A}}}={\cal{\hat{B}}}={\hat{p}}
,
we have

\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\langle{\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}=k_{B}T(\gamma-1)/\kappa_{T}
from

D^{ij}_{{p}{p}}={\bar{v}}_{i}{\bar{v}}_{j}/\kappa_{T}^{2}
.
However,
\langle{\delta{\hat{\cal A}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})\delta{\hat{T}}({\mbox{\boldmath$0$}})}\rangle_{\rm ca}
has no nonlocal part
for any
\hat{\cal A}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
from
D^{ij}_{{\cal A}{T}}=0
.

4.4. Landau-Lifshitz theory

Landau and Lifshitz
[
27
]

introduced fluctuations of the temperature and the pressure,
written as
\Delta T
and
\Delta p
, which
are linear combinations of extensive changes
in the volume and the energy,

\Delta V
and
\Delta E
.
They used the
T
-
p
distribution

[
37
,
38
,
39
]
, where the particle numbers

N_{i}
are fixed but the volume fluctuation

\Delta V
is superimposed on the average
volume
V
with the
T
-
p
variances
[
27
]
given by

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\langle{(\Delta V)^{2}}\rangle=Vk_{B}T\kappa_{T},~~\langle{\Delta V\Delta p}\rangle=-k_{B}T,

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433pt\langle{\Delta V\Delta T}\rangle=0,~~\langle{\Delta V\Delta E}\rangle=Vk_{B}T\kappa_{T}(T\beta_{V}-p).

(53)

These relations hold for multi-component fluids, where
use is made of the thermodynamic derivatives at fixed
\{x\}

in Eqs.(9) and (26). Here, we note that

\Delta T
and
\Delta p

in the Landau-Lifshitz book
are the space averages of
\delta{\hat{T}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
and

\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}})
in Eqs.(45) and (46):

\displaystyle\Delta T=\frac{1}{V}\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\delta{\hat{T}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}),~~\Delta p=\frac{1}{V}\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\delta{\hat{p}}({\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}).

(54)

We can define
\Delta T
and
\Delta p

by Eq.(54) for any ensembles.

With nonvanishing
\Delta V
,
the particle-number changes

\Delta N_{i}
and the internal-energy change

\Delta E
are expressed in the linear order as

\Delta N_{i}=\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\delta{\hat{n}}_{i}+n_{i}\Delta V,~~\Delta E=\int_{V}d{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}\delta{\hat{e}}+e\Delta V,

(55)

With the aid of Eqs.(23), (45), and (46)
we can then express
\Delta T
and
\Delta p
as

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\Delta T=\Big[\Delta E+(p-T\beta_{V})\Delta V-{\sum}_{i}M_{i}\Delta N_{i}\Big]/VC_{V},

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\Delta p=\Big[\Delta E+(p-T\beta_{s})\Delta V-{\sum}_{i}R_{i}\Delta N_{i}\Big]/(VT\alpha_{s}).

(56)

If
\Delta N_{i}=0
, these expressions are
equivalent to those in the Landau-Lifshitz book.

In the
T
-
\mu
, canonical, and
T
-
p
ensembles,

\Delta T
commonly satisfies the relations,

\langle{(\Delta T)^{2}}\rangle={k_{B}T^{2}}/{VC_{V}},~~\langle{\Delta T\Delta E}\rangle={k_{B}T^{2}},

(57)

which follow from Eqs.(48) and (55).
The fluctuation of
\Delta T
is small for large
V
.
We mention an experiment
[
75
]

and theories
[
77
,
76
,
78
]
on
\Delta T

in a small system in contact with a heat bath.

and

\Pi/k_{B}T=n_{3}+n_{3}^{2}u_{3}^{\rm eff}/2=n_{3}-n_{3}^{2}G_{33}^{0}/2,

which is valid up to order
n_{3}^{2}

[
53
]

For one-component solvents, Eqs.(94) and (95)

with Eq.(97)
[

,
10
]
.

We also seek the

and

(106)

Then, the solvent
volume fraction and the solvent concentration change by

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433pt\Delta\phi={\bar{v}}_{1}^{m}\Delta n_{1}+{\bar{v}}_{2}^{m}\Delta n_{2}=-{\bar{v}}_{3}^{0}n_{3}+k_{B}T\kappa_{T}^{m}n_{3},.

(108)

where Eq.(14) is used in Eq.(107) and

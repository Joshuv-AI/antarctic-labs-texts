Radiative corrections of the order 𝜶 ​ ( 𝒁 ​ 𝜶 ) 𝟔 \bm{\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}} for rotational states of two-body systems
Vojtěch Patkóš, Krzysztof Pachucki

Abstract
The analytical calculation of the complete α ​ ( Z ​ α ) 6 \alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6} one-loop radiative correction to energies of two-body systems with the angular momenta l > 0 l>0 , consisting of a pointlike particle and an extended-size nucleus with arbitrary masses and spin 1/2, is presented. The obtained results apply to a wide variety of two-body systems, such as hydrogen, muonium, positronium, and antiprotonic atoms.



I
Introduction

Hadronic two-body systems, such as antiprotonic atoms in circular states
l\sim n
, give the possibility to probe the existence
of the long-range interactions between hadrons, which is not possible by other means.
The emission spectroscopy of light antiprotonic atoms is feasible at CERN
nancypaul
, and from the theoretical side these atoms can be very accurately calculated.
In fact, in a highly excited circular state the effective coupling
Z\,\alpha/n
is much smaller than one, and so the nonrelativistic QED (NRQED)
approach can be used to obtain the energy levels even for high Z-nuclei.
Such calculations for an arbitrary mass ratio and arbitrary state up to the order
(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
were recently performed in Refs.
zatorski:22
;
patkos:24:twobodyP
,
and here we extend this result to the orders
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
and
Z^{2}\,\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
.

Other two-body systems, such as hydrogen and hydrogen-like ions, serve for determination of the fundamental physical constants
codata:22
,
because they can be measured and calculated with high accuracy. Significant progress has been achieved in recent years by the inclusion
of the nuclear charge radii obtained from muonic hydrogen and other light muonic atoms
muH1
;
muH2
;
muD
;
mualpha
;
muhelion
;
rmp:2024
.
The current value of the Rydberg constant,
based mainly on the precisely measured
1S-2S
transition in H
1S2S
and
2S-2P
in
\mu
H
muH1
;
muH2
,
has a relative accuracy of
1.1\cdot 10^{-12}
, limited by uncertainties in theoretical predictions for H and
\mu
H
codata:22
.
These uncertainties mainly come from the two-loop electron self-energy, the radiative recoil, and nuclear polarizability in the case of muonic atoms.
The radiative recoil correction is a topic of this work.

In this paper, employing units
\hbar=c=1
and
e^{2}=4\,\pi\,\alpha
, we perform a calculation at the
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}

order for two-body systems with arbitrary masses, including self-energy of an orbiting particle and with an arbitrary nucleus.
In the first step, we consider the states with
l>0
.
The lower-order terms were recently obtained for
l=0
states in Ref.
adkins:23
, and for

l>0
in Refs.
zatorski:22
;
patkos:24:twobodyP
. The
\alpha^{7}
corrections are currently known only in the nonrecoil limit
jentschura:05
,
and here we derive them for an arbitrary mass ratio. The results obtained may also find applications in more complicated few-electron systems such as the helium atom,
where discrepancies between theoretical predictions and experimental values for the ionization energies have been observed
He1
;
He2
;
He3
,
and they might come from a similar calculation of radiative
\alpha^{7}\,m
correction for triplet states of the He atom
patkos:21:rad
.

II
Radiative
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
correction

The radiative (electron self-energy)
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
correction to energy
E^{(7)}_{\mathrm{rad}}
of a two-body system
can be expressed as a combination of terms with all possible spin couplings
zatorski:22
,

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\mathrm{rad}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\mu\,\alpha(Z\alpha)^{6}}{\pi}\big\langle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{NS}}+\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{1}\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{S1}}+\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{S2}}

\displaystyle\ +\vec{s}_{1}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{SS}}+(L^{i}L^{j})^{(2)}\,s_{1}^{i}s_{2}^{j}\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{LL}}\big\rangle\,,

(1)

where
\mu
is the reduced mass,
Z\,\alpha=-e_{1}\,e_{2}/(4\,\pi)
,
Z
is the charge number of the nucleus which has a particle number
2
,

\vec{s}_{i}
is the spin of the
i
-th particle, and

(L^{i}L^{j})^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\,(L^{i}L^{j}+L^{j}L^{i})-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{3}\,\vec{L}^{2}\,,

(2)

which is a symmetric traceless tensor. The coefficients
{\cal E}_{X}
in Eq. (
1
) with different X are functions of
the principal quantum number
n
and the angular momentum
l
, and their calculation is the subject of this work.

At first
E^{(7)}_{\mathrm{rad}}
is divided into three parts,

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\mathrm{rad}}=E_{L}+E_{M}+E_{H}\,,

(3)

where the low-energy part
E_{L}
corresponds to the frequency of the radiative photon
\omega\sim m_{1}\,\alpha^{2}
,
the middle-energy part
E_{M}
comes from the region of
\omega\sim m_{1}\,\alpha
,
and the high-energy part
E_{H}
corresponds to
\omega\sim m_{1}
.
We will use dimensional regularization with
d=3-2\,\varepsilon
to avoid divergences
and the
1/\varepsilon
singularity will cancel out in the sum in Eq. (
3
).

III
Low-energy part
E_{L}

The low-energy contribution of the order
\alpha\,(Z\alpha)^{6}
is further divided into three parts,

\displaystyle E_{L}=E_{L1}+E_{L2}+E_{L3}\,.

(4)

These parts will be evaluated in the subsequent sections as corrections to the
leading low-energy contribution
E_{L0}
of the order
\alpha\,(Z\alpha)^{4}
,
namely to the Bethe logarithm.

III.1
E_{L0}

Let us consider the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian for a two-body system in
d
dimensions,

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\ \frac{p_{1}^{2}}{2\,m_{1}}+\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2\,m_{2}}+V(r)\,,

(5)

\displaystyle V(r)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{e_{1}\,e_{2}}{4\,\pi}\,\bigg[\frac{1}{r}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{1}{r}\bigg]_{\epsilon}=

\displaystyle\ m_{1}^{2\,\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{4\,\pi}{k^{2}}\,,

(7)

where
\vec{r}=\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}
.
The leading nonrelativistic (dipole) low-energy contribution is

\displaystyle E_{L0}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{e_{1}^{2}}{m_{1}^{2-2\,\varepsilon}}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}\,2\,k}\,\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\,

\displaystyle\times\left<\phi\left|p^{i}_{1}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p^{j}_{1}\right|\phi\right>\,,

(8)

where
H
is the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian in
d
dimensions from Eq. (
5
).
The wave function
\phi
denotes
the nonrelativistic Schrödinger–Pauli wave function in the center of mass frame (
\vec{p}_{1}=-\vec{p}_{2}=\vec{p}
).
In the following, we will denote the expectation value
of an arbitrary operator
Q
,
evaluated with the nonrelativistic Schrödinger–Pauli
wave function, by the shorthand notation
\langle Q\rangle
.

After the
d
-dimensional
integration with respect to
k
, and the expansion in

\varepsilon
,
E_{L0}
becomes

\displaystyle E_{L0}=

\displaystyle\ (4\,\pi)^{\varepsilon}\,\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)\,\frac{2\,\alpha}{3\,\pi\,m_{1}^{2}}\,\bigg<\vec{p}_{1}\,(H-E)\bigg\{\frac{1}{2\,\varepsilon}+\frac{5}{6}

\displaystyle\ -\ln\left[\frac{2(H-E)}{m_{1}}\right]\bigg\}\vec{p}_{1}\,\bigg>\,,

(9)

where we ignore terms of order
\varepsilon
and higher.
The factor
(4\,\pi)^{\varepsilon}\,\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)

appears in all the terms, and thus we will omit consistently in all matrix elements.
The contribution
E_{L0}
can thus be rewritten as

\displaystyle E_{L0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\,\alpha}{3\,m_{1}^{2}}\,Z\,\alpha\,\left\{\frac{1}{2\,\varepsilon}+\frac{5}{6}+\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{\mu\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}\right]\right\}\,\langle\delta^{d}(r)\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle-\frac{2\,\alpha}{3\,\pi\,m_{1}^{2}}\left<\vec{p}\,(H-E)\,\ln\left[\frac{2(H-E)}{\mu\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}}\right]\,\vec{p}\,\right>\,,

where the last term is the so-called Bethe logarithm
bethe
.

III.2
E_{L1}

We consider now all possible relativistic corrections to Eq. (
10
)
and introduce the notation

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}\,\left<p^{i}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p^{j}\right>\equiv\biggl<p^{i}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,(Q-\langle Q\rangle)\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p^{j}\biggr>+2\,\left<Q\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,p^{i}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p^{j}\right>\,,

(11)

where
Q
is an arbitrary operator.
\delta_{Q}
involves the
first-order perturbations
to the Hamiltonian, to the energy, and to the wave function.
The correction
E_{L1}
is the perturbation of
E_{L0}

by the relativistic Breit Hamiltonian
H^{(4)}
,
which in
d
dimensions is (setting
e_{1}=-e
,
e_{2}=Ze
)

\displaystyle H^{(4)}=

\displaystyle\ H^{\prime(4)}+H^{\prime\prime(4)}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle H^{\prime(4)}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a=1,2}\biggl\{-\frac{p_{a}^{4}}{8\,m_{a}^{3}}+\frac{\pi\,Z\alpha}{2}\bigg[\frac{1}{m_{a}^{2}}+\frac{4}{3}\,r_{Ea}^{2}\bigg]\,\delta^{d}(r)\biggr\}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{Z\alpha}{2\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,p_{1}^{i}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\epsilon}\,p_{2}^{j}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{g_{1}\,g_{2}\,\pi\,Z\alpha}{4\,d\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\sigma_{1}^{ij}\,\sigma_{2}^{ij}\,\delta^{d}(r)\,,

(13)

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime(4)}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{a=1,2}\frac{g_{a}-1}{4\,m_{a}^{2}}\,\sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\bigl(\nabla^{i}_{a}V\bigr)p_{a}^{j}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{g_{1}\,g_{2}}{16\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\sigma_{1}^{ik}\,\sigma_{2}^{jk}\,\left(\nabla^{i}\,\nabla^{j}-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{d}\,\nabla^{2}\right)V

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{4\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\bigl(\nabla^{i}V\bigr)\biggl(g_{1}\,\sigma_{1}^{ij}\,p^{j}_{2}-g_{2}\,\sigma_{2}^{ij}\,p^{j}_{1}\biggr)\,,

(14)

where
r_{E}^{2}
is the mean square charge radius,

\delta^{d}(r)
is the Dirac
\delta
-function in
d
dimensions,
and
\sigma^{ij}=[\sigma^{i}\,,\,\sigma^{j}]/(2\,i)
.
In
d=3
spatial dimensions, the
matrices
\sigma^{ij}
reduce to
\sigma^{ij}=\epsilon^{ijk}\,\sigma^{k}
,
and the Breit Hamiltonian in the center of mass frame becomes

\displaystyle H^{(4)}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\vec{p}^{\,4}}{8\,m_{1}^{3}}\ -\frac{\vec{p}^{\,4}}{8\,m_{2}^{3}}-Z\alpha\,\biggl\{\frac{1}{2\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,p^{i}\,\biggl(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{3}}\biggr)\,p^{j}+\frac{g_{1}\,g_{2}}{4\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\biggl[\frac{s_{1}^{i}\,s_{2}^{j}}{r^{3}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-3\,\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{2}}\biggr)-\frac{8\,\pi}{3}\,\vec{s}_{1}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\delta^{3}(\vec{r})\biggr]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\vec{r}\times\vec{p}}{2\,r^{3}}\cdot\biggl[\frac{g_{1}}{m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\vec{s}_{1}+\frac{g_{2}}{m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\vec{s}_{2}+\frac{(g_{2}-1)}{m_{2}^{2}}\,\vec{s}_{2}+\frac{(g_{1}-1)}{m_{1}^{2}}\,\vec{s}_{1}\biggr]\biggr\}+\frac{2\,Z\alpha}{3}\biggl(\frac{3}{4\,m_{1}^{2}}+\frac{3}{4\,m_{2}^{2}}+r_{E1}^{2}+r_{E2}^{2}\biggr)\,\pi\,\delta^{3}(\vec{r})\,.

(15)

We will use this
d=3
form of
H^{(4)}
also later in the calculation of the second-order correction.
Additionaly, we note that the first particle is point-like, so
r_{E1}^{2}=0
and
g_{1}=2
.
The second particle will be considered with finite nuclear size, and we will calculate the radiative corrections
only for the first particle. However, in the case of antiprotonic atoms we will drop these assumptions for the first particle
and include radiative corrections for the second particle in Sec. VIII.

We now split
E_{L1}
by introducing an intermediate cutoff
\Lambda

\displaystyle E_{L1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{m_{1}^{2-2\,\varepsilon}}\left(\int_{0}^{\Lambda}+\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\right)\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}\,2\,k}\,\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\,

(16)

\displaystyle\times\delta_{H^{(4)}}\left<p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\right>\,.

After the
Z\,\alpha
expansion with
\Lambda=\lambda\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}
,
one goes subsequently to the limits
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

and
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
.
Under the assumption that
l\neq 0
,
we may perform an expansion in
1/k
in the second part and obtain

\displaystyle E_{L1}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2\,\alpha}{3\,\pi\,m_{1}^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\,k\,\delta_{H^{(4)}}\left<\,\vec{p}_{1}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,\vec{p}_{1}\,\right>

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{3\,\pi\,m_{1}^{2-2\,\varepsilon}}\,\left[1+\varepsilon\,\left(\frac{5}{3}-2\,\ln 2\right)\right]\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}dk\,\frac{1}{k^{1+2\,\varepsilon}}\,

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{2\,\left<H^{(4)}\,\frac{1}{(E-H)^{\prime}}\,[\,\vec{p}_{1},[\,H,\vec{p}_{1}\,]]\right>

\displaystyle\ +\bigg<[\,\vec{p}_{1},[\,H^{(4)},\vec{p}_{1}\,]]\bigg>\bigg\}\,.

(17)

The second-order contribution in braces will vanish for states with
l\neq 0
.
In the calculations, we keep
g_{2}
and
r_{E2}^{2}
arbitrary.
After performing the
k
-integration and with the help
of commutator relations, it reads

\displaystyle E_{L1}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}\,\mu\,\beta_{1}+\frac{\alpha}{3\,\pi}\,\left\{\frac{1}{2\,\varepsilon}+\frac{5}{6}+{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\right\}\,\frac{1}{m_{1}^{2}}\bigg<\vec{p}\,4\pi\,\delta^{d}(r)\,\vec{p}\,

\displaystyle\ \times\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{4}\bigg(\frac{1}{m_{1}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}^{2}}+\frac{4}{3}\,r_{E2}^{2}\bigg)+\frac{Z\alpha}{m_{1}\,m_{2}}\bigg(\frac{2}{3}-\frac{2}{9}\varepsilon\bigg)+\frac{g_{2}\,Z\alpha}{4\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{9}\varepsilon\bigg)\,\sigma_{1}^{ij}\,\sigma_{2}^{ij}\bigg]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{Z\alpha}{m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,p^{i}\,\bigg(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{3}}-3\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{5}}\bigg)_{\epsilon}\,p^{j}+\frac{g_{2}\,Z\alpha}{4\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\sigma_{1}^{ik}\,\sigma_{2}^{jk}\,\big[p^{i}\,4\pi\,\delta^{d}(r)\,p^{j}\big]^{(2)}

\displaystyle\ +{\rm i}\,\frac{Z\alpha}{2}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{1}^{ij}}{2\,m_{1}^{2}}+\frac{(g_{2}-1)\,\sigma_{2}^{ij}}{2\,m_{2}^{2}}+\frac{2\,\sigma_{1}^{ij}+g_{2}\,\sigma_{2}^{ij}}{2\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\bigg)\,p^{i}\,4\pi\,\delta^{d}(r)\,p^{j}\bigg>\,,

(18)

where the expectation value is expressed in the center of mass system.
Here,
\beta_{1}
is
a dimensionless quantity, defined as a finite part of the
k
-integral
with divergent terms proportional to
\lambda^{n}

(
n=1,2,\dots
) and
\ln(\lambda/\mu)
in the
limit of large
\lambda
omitted,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}\,\beta_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2\,\alpha}{3\,\pi\,m_{1}^{2}\,\mu}\,\int_{0}^{\Lambda}dk\,k\,\delta_{H^{(4)}}\,\left<p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{i}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}\,\bigg[\beta^{\mathrm{NS}}_{1}+\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{1}\,\beta_{1}^{\mathrm{S1}}+\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\beta_{1}^{\mathrm{S2}}+\vec{s}_{1}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\beta_{1}^{\mathrm{SS}}+(L^{i}L^{j})^{(2)}s_{1}^{i}s_{2}^{j}\,\beta_{1}^{\mathrm{LL}}\bigg]\,.

(19)

In all integrals with an upper limit
\Lambda
, to be discussed in the following,
the divergent terms in
\lambda
will be subtracted.
In particular, the terms proportional
to
\ln(\lambda/\mu)
but not
\ln(2\,\lambda/\mu)
are subtracted, which leads to the presence of factor

\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}
under the logarithm in Eq. (
III.2
).

III.3
E_{L2}

The second relativistic correction,
E_{L2}
, is the
nonrelativistic quadrupole contribution.
Specifically, it comes from the quadratic in
k
term
from the expansion of
\exp({\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r})
,

\displaystyle E_{L2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{m_{1}^{2-2\,\varepsilon}}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}\,2\,k}\,\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\,

(20)

\displaystyle\times\biggl[\left<p_{1}^{i}\,({\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1})\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\,(-{\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1})\right>

\displaystyle+\left<p_{1}^{i}\,({\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1})^{2}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\right>\biggr]\,.

In a similar way as for
E_{L1}
, we split the integration into two parts,
by introducing a cutoff
\Lambda
. In the first part, with the

k
-integral from
0
to
\Lambda
, one can set
d=3
and extract the
logarithmic divergence.
In the second part, with the
k
-integral from
\Lambda
to
\infty
,
we perform a
1/k
expansion and employ commutator relations,
with the intent of moving the operator
H-E
to the far left
or right where it vanishes when acting on the Schrödinger–Pauli wave function.
In this second part it is advantageous, instead of directly expanding the exponentials, at first to use the identity

e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}\,f(\vec{p}\,)\,e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}=f(\vec{p}-\vec{k})\,.

(21)

Thus, after expanding the resolvent in
1/k
, we get for the expression in the expectation value

\displaystyle\ \left<p_{1}^{i}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\,\frac{(H-E)^{3}}{k^{4}}\,p_{1}^{i}\,e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{k^{4}}\bigg<p_{1}^{i}\,\bigg(H-E-\frac{\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}}{m_{1}}+\frac{k^{2}}{2\,m_{1}}\bigg)^{3}\,p_{1}^{i}\bigg>\,.

(22)

We expand the bracket and take into account only terms quadratic in
k
, contributing at the order
\alpha^{7}
. This leads to

\displaystyle E^{\Lambda}_{L2}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{e^{2}}{m_{1}^{2-2\,\varepsilon}}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}\,2\,k^{5}}\,\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\,

\displaystyle\ \times\bigg<p_{1}^{i}\,\bigg[\frac{3}{2\,m_{1}}(H-E)^{2}\,k^{2}+\frac{2}{m_{1}^{2}}(\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k})^{2}\,(H-E)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{m_{1}^{2}}(\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k})(H-E)(\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k})\bigg]\,p_{1}^{j}\bigg>\,.

(23)

We now pass to the center of mass system, and the resulting expression, after performing
k
integration and expansion for small
\varepsilon
, is

\displaystyle E_{L2}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}\,\mu\,\beta_{2}+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\bigg<(\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}\,\bigg[\frac{1}{m_{1}^{3}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{5}{6}+{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\bigg)+\frac{\mu}{m_{1}^{4}}\bigg(\frac{1}{6\varepsilon}+\frac{14}{45}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{3}\,{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\bigg)\bigg]+\frac{Z\alpha}{m_{1}^{4}}\,\vec{p}\,4\pi\,\delta^{d}(r)\,\vec{p}\,

\displaystyle\ \times\left[\frac{1}{20\,\varepsilon}+\frac{3}{25}+\frac{1}{10}\,{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\right]\bigg>\,.

(24)

Here,
\beta_{2}=\beta_{2}^{\mathrm{NS}}
is defined as the finite part of the integral
[see the discussion following Eq. (
III.2
)]

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle\ \beta_{2}=\frac{4\,\pi\,\alpha}{m_{1}^{2}\,\mu}\,\,\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\,\pi)^{3}\,2\,k}\,\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\,

\displaystyle\times\left\{\left<\,p_{1}^{i}\,({\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1})^{2}\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\,\right>\right.

\displaystyle\ \left.+\left<\,p_{1}^{i}\,({\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1})\,\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\,(-{\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1})\,\right>\right\}\,.

(25)

III.4
E_{L3}

The third contribution,
E_{L3}
, originates from the relativistic
corrections to the coupling of the electron to the electromagnetic field.
These corrections can be obtained from the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
5
),
and they have the form of a correction to the current

\displaystyle\delta j_{1}^{i}=

\displaystyle\ {\rm i}\,[H^{(4)},r_{1}^{i}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2\,m_{1}^{3}}\,p_{1}^{i}\,\vec{p}_{1}^{\,2}+\frac{Z\alpha}{2\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\bigg[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\varepsilon}p_{2}^{j}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{g_{1}-1}{4\,m_{1}^{2}}\,\sigma_{1}^{ji}\,\nabla^{j}V+\frac{g_{2}}{4\,m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,\sigma_{2}^{ji}\,\nabla^{j}V\,,

(26)

with
H^{(4)}
given in Eq. (
12
), and we keep

arbitrary for now.
The corresponding correction
E_{L3}
is

\displaystyle E_{L3}=

\displaystyle\ 2\,\frac{e^{2}}{m_{1}^{1-2\,\varepsilon}}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}\,2\,k}\,\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{k^{2}}\right)\,

\displaystyle\ \times\left<\delta j_{1}^{i}\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\right>\,.

(27)

We now perform an angular averaging of the matrix element to bring the correction
E_{L3}
into the form

\displaystyle E_{L3}=2\,\frac{e^{2}}{m_{1}^{1-2\,\varepsilon}}\frac{d-1}{d}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}\,2\,k}\,\left<\delta j_{1}^{i}\frac{1}{E-H-k}\,p_{1}^{i}\right>\,.

(28)

We again split this integral into two parts. In the first part, where

k<\Lambda
, one can approach the limit
d=3
.
In the second part, with
k>\Lambda
, one performs a
1/k
-expansion
and obtains

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{m_{1}\,\pi}\bigg(\frac{5}{9}+\frac{1}{3\varepsilon}-\frac{2}{3}\ln\bigg[\frac{2\Lambda}{m_{1}}\bigg]\bigg)\left\langle\left[\delta j_{1}^{i},\left[V,p_{1}^{i}\right]\right]\right\rangle\,.

(29)

The expectation value for states with angular momentum
l>0
can be written as

\displaystyle\left[\delta j_{1}^{i},\left[V,p_{1}^{i}\right]\right]=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2\,m_{1}^{3}}\,\left[p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{2},\left[V,p_{1}^{i}\right]\right]

For antiprotonic atoms, the low-energy contribution
E_{L1}
is

\displaystyle\ E_{L1}=\frac{\alpha}{3\,\pi\,m_{1}^{2}}\,\left\{\frac{1}{2\,\varepsilon}+\frac{5}{6}+{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\right\}

\displaystyle\ \times\bigg<-\frac{Z\alpha}{m_{1}\,m_{2}}\,p^{i}\,\bigg(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{3}}-3\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{5}}\bigg)_{\epsilon}\,p^{j}\bigg>

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}\,\mu\,\beta_{1}(x)+Z^{2}(1\leftrightarrow 2,x\leftrightarrow x^{-1})\,.

(119)

In the Bethe logarithm contribution the perturbation of the
expectation value by the Breit Hamiltonian
H^{(4)}
has to include
g
-factors of both particles.

The low-energy contribution
E_{L2}
is for antiprotonic atoms of the form

\displaystyle E_{L2}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\bigg<(\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}\,\bigg[\frac{1}{m_{1}^{3}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}+\frac{5}{6}+{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\mu}{m_{1}^{4}}\bigg(\frac{1}{6\varepsilon}+\frac{14}{45}+\frac{1}{3}{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\bigg)\bigg]\bigg>

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}\,\mu\,\beta_{2}(x)+Z^{2}(1\leftrightarrow 2,x\leftrightarrow x^{-1})\,.

(120)

where
a\leftrightarrow b
denotes replacement of
a
with
b
.

The final low-energy contribution is given by

\displaystyle\ E_{L3}=-\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\left(\frac{1}{3\,\varepsilon}+\frac{5}{9}+\frac{2}{3}{\ln\left[\frac{m_{1}}{2\,\mu}(Z\alpha)^{-2}\right]}\right)\,

\displaystyle\ \times\left(\frac{\mu}{m_{1}^{4}}-\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{m_{1}^{2}\,m_{2}}\right)\left<\bigl(\vec{\nabla}V\bigr)^{2}\right>+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{(Z\,\alpha)^{6}}{n^{3}}\,\mu\,\beta_{3}(x)

\displaystyle\ +Z^{2}(1\leftrightarrow 2,x\leftrightarrow x^{-1})\,.

(121)

The middle-energy contribution for antiprotonic systems is obtained in a straightforward way as

\displaystyle E_{M}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{2\,\pi\,m^{2}_{1}\,m_{2}}\bigg[\frac{17}{9}-\frac{2}{3\,\varepsilon}-\frac{4}{3}\big(\ln m_{1}\,r+\gamma\big)\bigg](\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}

\displaystyle\ +Z^{2}\,(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(122)

The only high-energy part that will contribute is given by
E_{H3}
,

\displaystyle E_{H3}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg(\frac{1}{m_{1}^{3}}+\frac{Z^{2}}{m_{2}^{3}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{3\,\varepsilon}\bigg)(\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}\,.

(123)

The other terms go to polarizability of both particles, and they are already included in the
\alpha^{6}\,\mu
contribution in Ref.
zatorski:22

After summing all the contributions, the singularities exactly cancel each other, which leads to

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\mathrm{\bar{p}}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\mu\,\alpha(Z\alpha)^{2}}{90\,\pi\,m_{1}^{4}\,m_{2}^{2}}\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\bigg(105\,m_{1}^{2}+170\,m_{1}\,m_{2}+68\,m_{2}^{2}-60\,m_{1}(m_{1}+m_{2})\,\big(\ln(m_{1}\,r)+\gamma\big)

\displaystyle\ +60\,(m_{1}+m_{2})^{2}\,\ln_{1}\bigg)\bigg\rangle+\frac{\mu\,\alpha(Z\alpha)^{6}}{\pi\,n^{3}}\big\langle\beta^{\mathrm{NS}}(x)+\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{1}\,\beta^{\mathrm{S1}}(x)+\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\beta^{\mathrm{S2}}(x)

\displaystyle\ +\vec{s}_{1}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\beta^{\mathrm{SS}}(x)+(L^{i}L^{j})^{(2)}\,s_{1}^{i}\,s_{2}^{j}\,\beta^{\mathrm{LL}}(x)\big\rangle+Z^{2}\,(1\leftrightarrow 2,x\leftrightarrow x^{-1})\,.

(124)

Evaluating the expectation values, we obtain

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\mathrm{\bar{p}}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\mu\,\alpha(Z\alpha)^{6}}{\pi\,\Delta}\,\eta_{1}^{2}\,\bigg[\frac{8}{3}\,\eta_{2}\,\bigg(\frac{l(l+1)}{n^{5}}-\frac{3}{n^{3}}\bigg)\bigg(H_{2l-2}+H_{2l+3}-H_{n+l}+\ln\frac{n}{2\,Z\alpha\,\eta_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{8}{3}\,\bigg(\frac{3}{n^{3}}-\frac{l(l+1)}{n^{5}}\bigg)\,\ln_{1}+\frac{1}{n^{3}}\bigg(\frac{70}{3}-\eta_{1}\,\frac{44}{3}+\eta_{1}^{2}\,\frac{2}{5}\bigg)-\frac{4\,\eta_{2}\,(2l+1)}{n^{4}}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{n^{5}}\bigg(l\,(l+1)\bigg(-6+\eta_{1}\,\frac{28}{9}-\eta_{1}^{2}\,\frac{2}{15}\bigg)+\eta_{2}\,\frac{4}{3}\bigg)\bigg]+\frac{\mu\,\alpha(Z\alpha)^{6}}{\pi\,n^{3}}\big\langle\beta^{\mathrm{NS}}(x)+\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{1}\,\beta^{\mathrm{S1}}(x)

\displaystyle\ +\vec{L}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\beta^{\mathrm{S2}}(x)+\vec{s}_{1}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\,\beta^{\mathrm{SS}}(x)+(L^{i}L^{j})^{(2)}\,s_{1}^{i}\,s_{2}^{j}\,\beta^{\mathrm{LL}}(x)\big\rangle+Z^{2}\,(1\leftrightarrow 2,x\leftrightarrow x^{-1})\,.

(125)

The final result for antiprotonic atoms is thus very simple and compact.

IX
Summary

We have derived a complete
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
and
Z^{2}\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
one-loop self-energy correction
to the energy levels of a two-body system with angular momentum
l>0
. The obtained results are valid for
constituent particles of arbitrary masses and spin 1/2, with the nucleus being either pointlike
or of extended-size. For
l=1
, the results are presented in Eqs. (
81
-
101
), while for
l>1
they are in Eqs. (
153
-
172
),
and these results are presented in Mathematica format in the Supplemental Material
supp
.
For the case of positronium, the results for
l=1
are presented in Eq. (
102
-
108
),
and those for rotational states of antiprotonic atoms are in Eq. (
125
). For hydrogenlike atoms in the nonrecoil limit,
our results agree with the former calculation in the literature
jentschura:05
in the case of a point nucleus.
We present also the first-order recoil correction in Eq. (
VII.2
) for
l=1
and in Eq. (
178
) for
l>1
,
which to our knowledge have not yet been considered in the literature.

What is yet unknown is the pure exchange contribution of order
(Z\,\alpha)^{7}
. Once it is completed,
we aim to perform numerical calculation of relativistic Bethe logarithms and
the electron (muon) vacuum polarization contributions. This will eventually allow for
very accurate results for
l>0
states of arbitrary two-body systems, including muonic and antiprotonic atoms.

Finally, we note that using the operator form of the
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{6}
correction in Eq. (
VI
) we found a small mistake
in the previous calculation of a similar correction to He ionization energies, which we describe in detail in Appendix B.

Appendix C
Derivation of identities

To derive Eq. (
VI
), we start with the identity

\displaystyle\ p^{i}\,[p^{i},[V,p^{j}]]\,p^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ p^{2}\,V\,p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,p^{i}\,[p^{j},[V,p^{j}]]\,p^{i}-\frac{1}{2}\,\{p^{2},\vec{p}\,\,V\vec{p}\,\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{4}\,[p^{2},[V,p^{2}]]+\frac{1}{2}\,p^{i}\,[p^{j},[V,p^{j}]]\,p^{i}-\frac{1}{4}\,\{p^{2},[\vec{p},[V,\vec{p}\,]]\}\,.

(140)

For states with
l>0
the third term in the last equality vanishes. With the help of the expectation value identity

[p^{2},[V,p^{2}]]=4\,\mu(\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}\,,

(141)

and relation

[p^{i},[V,p^{j}]]=\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{r^{3}}\bigg(\delta^{ij}-3\,\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{2}}\bigg)\bigg]_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\delta^{ij}}{d}\,Z\alpha\,4\pi\,\delta^{d}(r)\,,

(142)

VI
).

We will also present the evaluation for the expectation value of the operator in the first term of Eq. (
127

\bigg\langle(\vec{s}_{2}\times\vec{p})^{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{3}}-3\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{5}}+\frac{\delta^{ij}}{3}\,4\,\pi\,\delta^{3}(r)\right)(\vec{s}_{1}\times\vec{p})^{j}\bigg\rangle\,.

(143)

The expectation value of the traceless part will be proportional to
\langle(L^{i}L^{j})^{(2)}\,s_{1}^{i}s_{2}^{j}\rangle

while the trace part will result in terms involving
\langle\vec{s}_{1}\cdot\vec{s}_{2}\rangle
. For the non-local term we get

\displaystyle\bigg\langle(\vec{s}_{2}\times\vec{p})^{i}\bigg(

\displaystyle\ \frac{\delta^{ij}}{r^{3}}-3\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{5}}\bigg)(\vec{s}_{1}\times\vec{p})^{j}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle\ \big\langle\,\big(A\,(L^{i}L^{j})^{(2)}+B\,\delta^{ij}\big)\,s_{1}^{i}s_{2}^{j}\big\rangle\,.

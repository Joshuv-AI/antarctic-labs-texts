Introduction

1.1
motivation

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) with
C^{2}
boundary
\partial\Omega
. The pioneering works
[
13
,
17

obtained that the nonlinear reaction diffusion equation

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+h(u)=0&\mbox{in}&\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{-\Delta+h(u)}u=+\infty&\mbox{on}&\partial\Omega\end{array}

(1.1)

admits a solution if
h
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function which is increasing and satisfies Keller-Osserman condition

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\left[\int_{0}^{s}h(t)dt\right]^{-\frac{1}{2}}ds<+\infty.

Great interests in existence, uniqueness and asymptotic behavior of boundary blow-up solution to (
1.1
) have been taken, see
[
1
,
9
,
11
,
14
,
20
,
22
,
16
]
.
It is well known that when
h(s)=s^{p}
with
p>1
, (
1.1
) has a unique solution with boundary asymptotic behavior
\rho^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}(x)
,
where
\rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Comparing with the Laplacian case, a much richer structure for the solutions set appears for the non-local case.
Recently, the authors in
[
5
]
obtained very different phenomena of the boundary blow-up solutions to elliptic equations
involving the fractional Laplacian, precisely,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+|u|^{p-1}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\ \quad\hbox{in}\quad\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\ \hbox{in}\quad\Omega^{c},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\in\Omega,x\to\partial\Omega}u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+\infty,

where
p>0
and the fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
is defined by

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}u(x),

here for
\epsilon>0

(-\Delta)_{\epsilon}^{\alpha}u(x)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{u(z)-u(x)}{|z-x|^{N+2\alpha}}dz

The existence of boundary blow-up solution of (
1.1
) is derived by constructing appropriate super and sub-solutions and
this construction involves the one dimensional truncated Laplacian of power functions given by

C(\tau)=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{\chi_{(0,1)}(t)|1-t|^{\tau}+(1+t)^{\tau}-2}{t^{1+2\alpha}}dt,

(1.3)

where
\tau\in(-1,0)
and
\chi_{(0,1)}

(0,1)
.
It is known that there exists a unique zero point of (

.
Then

Proposition 1.1

[
5

Assume that
\Omega

(1.4)

(1.5)

Special existence for

Assume

\max\{1-\frac{2\alpha}{\tau_{0}(\alpha)}+\frac{\tau_{0}(\alpha)+1}{\tau_{0}(\alpha)},1\}<p<1-\frac{2\alpha}{\tau_{0}(\alpha)}.

There are some challenging questions to ask:

1. Could

2. With what condition of general nonlinearity makes existence hold?

Our interest in this article is to introduce a new method to study the boundary blow-up solutions of semilinear fractional
elliptic equations and answer above questions. The main idea is to find suitable type measure concentrated on the whole boundary
and then by making basic estimates to prove that the corresponding weak solution solves (
1.1

Proposition 1.2

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\tau_{0}(\alpha)

We observe that the critical value

\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}
. In what follows, we would like to show the details of our new method and answer the second and third questions
in the following.

1.2
A new method and main results

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\omega
be the Hausdorff measure on
\partial\Omega
.
We denote by
\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\omega}{\partial\vec{n}^{\alpha}}
a measure

\langle\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\omega}{\partial\vec{n}^{\alpha}},f\rangle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial\vec{n}_{x}^{\alpha}}d\omega(x),\qquad f\in C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega}),

where
\vec{n}_{x}
is the unit inward normal vector of
\partial\Omega
at point
x
and

\frac{\partial^{\alpha}f(x)}{\partial\vec{n}_{x}^{\alpha}}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(x+t\vec{n}_{x})-f(x)}{t^{\alpha}}.

In this paper, we are concerned with the
existence and uniqueness of weak solution to the semilinear fractional elliptic
problem

\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+g(u)=k\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\omega}{\partial\vec{n}^{\alpha}}&{\rm in}\quad\bar{\Omega},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{\alpha}+g(u)}u=0&{\rm in}\quad\bar{\Omega}^{c},\end{array}

(1.6)

where
k>0
and
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is continuous.

In
[
6
]
, the authors studied problem (
1.6
) replaced
\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\omega}{\partial\vec{n}^{\alpha}}
by

\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\nu}{\partial\vec{n}^{\alpha}}
where
\nu
is a Radon measure concentrated on boundary measure. They proved that
such a problem has a unique weak solution if
g
is a continuous nondecreasing function satisfying

g(0)\geq 0
and

\int_{1}^{\infty}g(s)s^{-1-\frac{N+\alpha}{N-\alpha}}ds<+\infty.

(1.7)

Moreover,
[
6
]
analyzed the isolated singularity of weak solution of (
1.6
) in the case that
\nu=\delta_{x_{0}}
with
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.
Our aim in this article is to investigate how the Hausdorff measure on
\partial\Omega
works on the weak solution of (
1.6
).

Before starting our main theorems we make precise the notion of weak solution used in this note.

Definition 1.1

We say that

is a weak solution of (
1.6
), if
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}[u(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\xi+g(u)\xi]dx=k\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\xi(x)}{\partial\vec{n}_{x}^{\alpha}}d\omega(x),\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}.

where

and

denotes the space of functions

satisfying:

(i)

{\rm supp}(\xi)\subset\bar{\Omega}
;

(ii)

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\xi(x)
exists for all
x\in\Omega

and
|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\xi(x)|\leq C
for some

;

(iii)

there exist
\varphi\in L^{1}(\Omega,\rho^{\alpha}dx)

and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

a.e. in
\Omega
for all

\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.

Now we are ready to state our first result for problem (

\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+u^{p}=0&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{\alpha}+u^{p}}u=0&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{\alpha}+u^{p}}u=u_{k}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega_{t_{0}}\end{array}

(4.5)

admits a unique solution
w_{k}
.
By Comparison Principle,

u_{k}\geq w_{k}\quad{\rm in}\quad B_{\varrho_{0}}(y_{0}).

(4.6)

Let
\tilde{w}_{k}=w_{k}-u_{k}\chi_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}}},

then
\tilde{w}_{k}=w_{k}
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}}
and for
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\tilde{w}_{k}(x)=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{B_{\varrho_{0}}(y_{0})\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{w_{k}(z)-w_{k}(x)}{|z-x|^{N+2\alpha}}dz\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{--------}+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{B_{\varrho_{0}}^{c}(y_{0})\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{w_{k}(x)}{|z-x|^{N+2\alpha}}dz\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{------}=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{w_{k}(z)-w_{k}(x)}{|z-x|^{N+2\alpha}}dz+\int_{B_{r_{0}}(0)}\frac{u_{k}(z)}{|z-x|^{N+2\alpha}}dz\\[8.53581pt]
\phantom{------}\geq(-\Delta)^{\alpha}w_{k}(x)+c_{53}\pi_{k},\end{array}

where
c_{53}=(|y_{0}|+r_{0})^{-N-2\alpha}
and the last inequality follows by the fact of

|z-x|\leq|x|+|z|\leq|y_{0}|+r_{0}\quad{\rm for}\ \forall z\in B_{\frac{1}{4}}(0),\ \ \forall x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\alpha}\tilde{w}_{k}(x)+\tilde{w}_{k}^{p}(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\alpha}w_{k}(x)+w_{k}^{p}(x)+c_{53}\pi_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{53}\pi_{k},\qquad\forall x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}},

that is,
\tilde{w}_{k}
is a super solution of

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+u^{p}=c_{53}\pi_{k}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{\alpha}+u^{p,}}u=0&{\rm in}\quad(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}})^{c}.\end{array}

(4.7)

Let
\eta_{1}
be the solution of

\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u=1&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}u=0&{\rm in}\quad(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}})^{c}.\end{array}

Then

(c_{53}\pi_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\eta_{1}}{2\max_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{1}}
is sub solution of (
4.7
) for
k
large enough. By Comparison Principle, we have that

\tilde{w}_{k}(x)\geq(c_{53}\pi_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\eta_{1}(x)}{2\max_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{1}},\quad\forall x\in B_{\varrho_{0}}(y_{0}),

which implies that

w_{k}(y)\geq c_{54}(c_{53}\pi_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad\forall y\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}},

where
c_{54}=\min_{x\in B_{\varrho_{0}}(y_{0})}\frac{\eta_{1}(x)}{2\max_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{1}}
.
Therefore, (
4.6
) and (
4.4
) imply that

\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(y)\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{k}(y)=\infty,\qquad\forall\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t_{0}}.

Similarly, we can prove

\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(y)\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{k}(y)=\infty,\qquad\forall y\in\Omega.

The proof ends.
\Box

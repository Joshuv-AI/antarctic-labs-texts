On property (T) for Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \operatorname{Aut}(F_{n}) and SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
Marek Kaluba, Dawid Kielak, Piotr Nowak

Abstract
We prove that Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \operatorname{Aut}(F_{n}) has Kazhdan’s property (T) for every n ⩾ 6 n\geqslant 6 . Together with a previous result of Kaluba, Nowak, and Ozawa, this gives the same statement for n ⩾ 5 n\geqslant 5 . We also provide explicit lower bounds for the Kazhdan constants of SAut ⁡ ( F n ) \operatorname{SAut}(F_{n}) (with n ⩾ 6 n\geqslant 6 ) and of SL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) (with n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 ) with respect to natural generating sets. In the latter case, these bounds improve upon previously known lower bounds whenever n > 6 n>6 .

Introduction

The study of
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
, the group of automorphisms of the
n
-generated free group, is motivated to a large extent by the apparent similarities between
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
and
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, the special linear group over the integers.
The latter is a lattice in a semi-simple Lie group (of higher rank, if we assume that
n\geqslant 3
), which has far reaching consequences.
In particular, linear representation theory of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is very rigid – this is reflected by Margulis’s Superrigidity and by Kazhdan’s property (T).

The question of whether
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})

has Kazhdan’s property (T) is very natural in the context of the search for similarities between
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
and
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.
It was raised by many authors, e.g.,

[
20
]
,
[
7
]
*Question 7,
[
5
]
*page 4, and
[
6
]
*page 345.

For
n=2
, the group
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{2})
maps onto
\mathrm{Out}(F_{2})\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, a virtually free group, and thus does not have property (T).
For
n=3
, the group
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{3})
maps onto
\mathrm{Out}(F_{3})
, which is a virtually residually torsion-free nilpotent group, as shown by J. McCool
[
21
]
, and hence does not have property (T).
It was later shown by F. Grunewald and A. Lubotzky in
[
14
]
that
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{3})
is large, that is, that it virtually maps onto
F_{2}
.
This fact of course also implies that
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{3})
does not have property (T).
For
n=4
, the problem remains open.
The first positive result has been obtained by N. Ozawa and the first and third author in
[
17
]
, where it was shown that
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{5})
does have property (T).

Property (T) admits many equivalent definitions;
the one which is central to our work is due to Ozawa
[
23
]
and is expressed in terms of the Laplace element in
\mathbb{R}G
.
Let
G
be a group with a finite symmetric generating set
S
.
The Laplacian
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}G
is defined by

\Delta=|S|-\sum_{s\in S}s=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in S}(1-s)^{*}(1-s),

(1)

where
\ast\colon\mathbb{R}G\to\mathbb{R}G
is induced by
g\mapsto g^{-1}
.
The group
G
is said to satisfy
Kazhdan’s property (T)
if there exists
\lambda>0
and finitely many elements
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}G
such that

\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}.

(2)

It can be shown that property (T) is independent of the finite generating set chosen.
For a fixed
S
however, it is interesting to ask:
how much of the group do we need to explore to find such elements
\xi_{i}
(should they exist)?
More formally, we say that the pair
(G,S)
has
Kazhdan radius
at most
R
if
(for some
\lambda>0
) the element
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
admits a sum of squares decomposition as above,
with the elements
\xi_{i}
supported in the ball of radius
R
around
1
in the word-length metric on
(G,S)
.

The main result of this paper is the following (see also
5.9
).

Theorem 1
.

\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
has property (T) for all
n\geqslant 6
.

Together with
[
17
]
we obtain the immediate corollary that
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
has property (T) for all
n\geqslant 5
.
It is worth noting that the results and methods of
[
17
]
are instrumental for the results presented here.

The proof of
Theorem

1
is given in
Section

5.2
.
For technical reasons, we work with the group
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
, which is the kernel of the determinant map
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and a subgroup of index 2 in
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
.
For
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
we provide an explicit estimate on Kazhdan constants and we also show that the Kazhdan radius for
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
is at most 2 for all
n\geqslant 5
.
Similarly, we give a new proof of property (T) for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
for
n\geqslant 3
, and bound the Kazhdan radius above by
2
as well.
This aligns with the numerical results of
[
18
]
where a decomposition as in (
2
) of radius
2
has been found for
n\leqslant 6
.
The new argument, however, provides necessarily weaker bounds on the Kazhdan constants than
[
18
]
(for
n=3,4,5
) – see
5.4
.

In both cases of groups, we work with natural generating sets: for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
we take
S
to be the set of all elementary matrices
{E_{ij}}^{\pm 1}
, where
E_{ij}
denotes the matrix which differs from the identity only in position
(i,j)
, where its entry is
1
.
For
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
, we take
S
to consist of right and left Nielsen transvections and their inverses. More explicitly, we have

S=\{{\rho_{ij}}^{\pm 1},{\lambda_{ij}}^{\pm 1}\mid 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n,i\neq j\},

where, denoting a fixed basis of
F_{n}
by
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
,

\rho_{ij}(a_{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}a_{k}&\hbox{\multirowsetup\text{if}}&k\neq i,\\
a_{i}a_{j}&&k=i,\end{array}\right.\ \ \ \lambda_{ij}(a_{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}a_{k}&\hbox{\multirowsetup\text{if}}&k\neq i,\\
a_{j}a_{i}&&k=i.\end{array}\right.

To prove our results we decompose the left hand side of the equation
(
2
) into summands that behave well under symmetrisation by the alternating groups acting naturally on the bases elements of
F_{m}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.
In particular, if such summands for a given
m
admit sum of squares decomposition as in (

), so do their symmetrisations for all
n>m
.
Using this method we reduce the problem of determining property (T) for an infinite family of groups to finding a single sum of squares decomposition of a single element in a group algebra.

As explained in
[
18
]
*Section 2, finding such a decomposition can be accomplished by producing a single real positive definite matrix.
This positive definite matrix is then found numerically, using a solver designed to perform semidefinite optimization.
An additional argument following
[
23
]
and
[
22
]
, that relies on the order structure of the self-adjoint elements in the augmentation ideal, allows for the turning of the numerical result into a proof of the existence of an exact solution, i.e. leads to a rigorous proof of property (T).

Theorem

has several applications: it explains the fast convergence of the product replacement algorithm

; it implies Conjecture 4.1 for

in

]
as a special case; finally, it provides explicit generating sets for sequences of alternating groups that turn them into sequences of expanders

.

Computational methods for property (T) have been used successfully by T. Netzer and A. Thom

[

M. Kaluba and P. Nowak in

.
However, they concerned specific examples only:
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
for

, various related finitely presented groups, and
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{5})
.

The Kazhdan radius

Throughout the paper we will use the notation
B_{R}(G,S)
to denote the ball of radius
R
in
G
, centered at the identity, with respect to the word-length metric induced by the generating set
S
.
We will often shorten this to just
B_{R}
when both
G
and
S
could be inferred from context.

Cones of sums of squares

Let
G
be a group with a generating set
S
. We say that an element
x
in
\mathbb{R}G
admits a
decomposition into sum of squares
if there exist finitely many elements
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}G
such that

x=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}.

Definition 2.1
.

The elements of
\mathbb{R}G
admitting a decomposition into sum of squares span a
(positive) cone of squares in
\mathbb{R}G
which we will denote by
\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}G
.

Of special interest to us will be
IG
, the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{R}G
, i.e. the kernel of the map
\mathbb{R}G\to\mathbb{R}
sending
\Sigma_{g}c_{g}g\mapsto\Sigma_{g}c_{g}
. By
\Sigma^{2}IG
we denote the intersection
IG\cap\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}G
. Note that
\Sigma^{2}IG
coincides with the cone of elements which admit a decomposition into sum of squares of elements from
IG
.

The cones
\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}G
and
\Sigma^{2}IG
admit natural
filtrations by radii of supports
. For any ideal
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}G
we have

{\mathbb{R}}\cap I=\Sigma^{2}_{0}I\subseteq\Sigma^{2}_{1}I\subseteq\Sigma^{2}_{2}I\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Sigma^{2}I,

that is, we allow in
\Sigma^{2}_{R}I
only those elements of
\Sigma^{2}I
which admit a decomposition into sum of squares of elements supported in
B_{R}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group with a finite symmetric generating set
S
. We say that
G
has
Kazhdan radius
(with respect to
S
) at most
R
if

\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G

for some
\lambda>0
.

Note that we could replace
\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G
by
\Sigma^{2}_{R}IG
in the definition above, since
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta\in IG
.

The significance of the Kazhdan radius lies in the fact that it tells us how much of the group is needed to prove the presence of property (T).
Suppose that we have a map
j\colon B_{2R}(G,S)\to B_{2R}(G^{\prime},S^{\prime})

and that its linear extension
j\colon\langle B_{2R}(G,S)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\to\langle B_{2R}(G^{\prime},S^{\prime})\rangle

carries
\Delta_{G}
to
\Delta_{G^{\prime}}
,
{\Delta_{G}}^{2}
to
{\Delta_{G^{\prime}}}^{2}
, and
j\left(\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G\right)\subset\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G^{\prime}

(e.g. when
j
is a local isomorphism).
If
G
has property (T) witnessed by sum of squares decomposition
\Delta_{G}^{2}-\lambda\Delta_{G}=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G
, then

{\Delta_{G^{\prime}}}^{2}-\lambda\Delta_{G^{\prime}}=j({\Delta_{G}}^{2}-\lambda\Delta_{G})=\sum j\left(\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}\right)\in\Sigma_{R}^{2}\mathbb{R}G^{\prime}

is a sum of squares as well.

This can be clearly envisioned in the case of modular projection
\rho_{p}\colon G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=G^{\prime}
.
For
p
large enough the projection carries
\Delta_{G}
to
\Delta_{G^{\prime}}
and
{\Delta}^{2}_{G}
to
{\Delta_{G^{\prime}}}^{2}
, and (since its an actual homomorphism) sum of squares to sum of squares.
On the other hand, a section of the modular projection is not a (global) homomorphism anymore,
but still can be used to lift sum of squares decomposition in
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
to one in
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
,
provided the support of squared elements is small enough.

Setting
n=2
and observing that
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
does not have property (T), we conclude that the Kazhdan radius of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
with respect to
\rho_{p}(S)
tends to infinity with
p
for every fixed finite symmetric generating set
S
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

3
Squaring the Laplacian

In this section we give a new, algebraic method of proving property (T) in
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
.
Most of the proofs are identical for both types of groups. Therefore, we will present them in a uniform way – to do that, we introduce some notation.

The setting

We identify
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
F_{n}
with the free-abelian and free, respectively, groups generated by
N_{n}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
.
Extending
N_{n+1}=N_{n}\cup\{a_{n+1}\}
induces natural inclusions
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
and
F_{n}\to F_{n+1}
.
In the following,
G_{n}
will be either
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
or
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
.
The groups
G_{n}
come with inclusions
G_{n}\to G_{n+1}
induced by the inclusions
N_{n}\to N_{n+1}
in the obvious way.
We also fix a finite symmetric generating set
S_{n}
of
G_{n}
:

•

for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, we take all elementary matrices
{E_{ij}}^{\pm 1}
;

•

for
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
, we take all Nielsen transvections and their inverses, namely
{\rho_{ij}}^{\pm 1}
and
{\lambda_{ij}}^{\pm 1}
.

It is clear that the inclusion
G_{n}\to G_{n+1}
takes
S_{n}
into
S_{n+1}
.

The action of
A_{n}
, the alternating group of rank
n
, on
N_{n}
induces an action of
A_{n}
on
G_{n}
by automorphisms which preserves the generating set
S_{n}
.
We will denote the action by
\sigma(g)
with
\sigma\in A_{n}
and
g\in G_{n}
.
The action of
A_{n+1}
on
G_{n+1}
restricts to the action of
A_{n}
on the embedded copy of
G_{n}
,
that is, the inclusion
G_{n}\to G_{n+1}
is
A_{n}
-equivariant.

Let
C_{n}
denote the
(n-1)
-simplex with the vertex set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. The group
A_{n}
acts on
C_{n}
by permuting the vertices.
The set of edges (unordered pairs
\{i,j\}
of distinct integers)
E_{n}
of
C_{n}
inherits the action of
A_{n}
, namely

\sigma(e)=\sigma\big(\{i,j\}\big)=\big\{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)\big\}.

Since generators in the set
S_{n}
are double-indexed, there is an obvious function
l_{n}\colon S_{n}\to E_{n}
which sends a generator to the edge between vertices labeled after its indices:
for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, the function sends
{E_{ij}}^{\pm 1}
to
\{i,j\}
;
for
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
, it sends
{\rho_{ij}}^{\pm 1}
and
{\lambda_{ij}}^{\pm 1}
to
\{i,j\}
.
It is immediate that
l_{n+1}|_{S_{n}}=l_{n}
.

Given two edges
e,f\in E_{n}
, we have the following trichotomy:

1.

the edges may coincide;

2.

they may be
adjacent
, that is overlap in a single vertex;

3.

or they may be
opposite
, that is disjoint.

The function
l_{n}
has the following property:
given two elements
s,t\in S_{n}
, if
l_{n}(s)
and
l_{n}(t)
are opposite edges, then
s
and
t
commute in
G_{n}
.

To summarise, we have the following:

•

a family of groups
G_{n}
with finite symmetric generating sets
S_{n}
,

•

the alternating group
A_{n}
of rank
n
acting on
G_{n}
by automorphisms preserving
S_{n}
,

•

an
A_{n}
-equivariant inclusion
G_{n}\leqslant G_{n+1}
,

•

an
A_{n}
-equivariant map
l_{n}\colon S_{n}\to E_{n}
onto the set of edges of the standard
n-1
simplex
C_{n}
, such that if
l_{n}(s)
and
l_{n}(t)
are opposite edges, then
s
and
t
commute in
G_{n}
.

Since we have inclusions
G_{n}\leqslant G_{n+1}
, we also have
\mathbb{R}G_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}G_{n+1}
.
The action of
A_{n}
on
G_{n}
induces an action on
\mathbb{R}G_{n}
by linear extension;
it is clear that this action and every other action on
G_{n}
by automorphisms preserves
\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}G_{n}
, the positive cone of sums of squares, set-wise.
More importantly: since the action of
A_{n}
permutes the elements of the generating set
S_{n}
it does not alter the word-length, and hence it preserves also the filtration of
\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}G
by radii.

The Laplacians

Definition 3.1
.

For
n\geqslant 2
, we define
\Delta_{n}
to be the (non-normalised) Laplacian of
G_{n}
, as defined in (
1
), with respect to the generating set
S_{n}
.

For
e=\{i,j\}\in E_{n}
, we define

S_{e}=\{t\in S_{n}\mid l_{n}(t)=e\}
and

\Delta_{e}=|S_{e}|-\sum_{t\in S_{e}}t

to be the Laplacian of the group
\left\langle S_{e}\right\rangle\leqslant G_{n}
with respect to the generating set
S_{e}
.

In particular,
\Delta_{\{1,2\}}
coincides with the Laplacian
\Delta_{2}
.
In fact, we have more: for every
\sigma\in A_{n}
we have

\sigma\left(\Delta_{e}\right)=\Delta_{\sigma(e)},

and so every
\Delta_{e}
is equal to
\sigma(\Delta_{2})
for some
\sigma\in A_{n}
. This
\sigma
is not unique – what is unique is the coset of
\mathrm{Stab}(\{1,2\})
in
A_{n}
defined by
\sigma
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
n\geqslant 3
we have

\Delta_{n}=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\Delta_{e}=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{2}).

Proof.

This follows directly from the definitions:

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle=|S_{n}|-\sum_{t\in S_{n}}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}|S_{e}|-\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\sum_{l_{n}(t)=e}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\big(|S_{e}|-\sum_{l_{n}(t)=e}t\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\Delta_{e}.

Since the
A_{n}
-stabiliser of
e
is isomorphic to the symmetric group of rank
n-2
, the second formula holds as well.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For
m\geqslant n\geqslant 3
we have

\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma(\Delta_{n})=\frac{|A_{n}|}{(n-2)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma(\Delta_{2})=\frac{|A_{n}|\cdot(m-2)!}{(n-2)!}\Delta_{m}=\binom{n}{2}\cdot(m-2)!\Delta_{m}.

Decomposing
\Delta^{2}

For an edge
e\in E_{n}
in the standard simplex we define
\operatorname{Adj}(e)
to be the set of edges in
E_{n}
adjacent to
e
,
and
\operatorname{Op}(e)
to be the set of edges opposite to
e
.

Definition 3.4
.

For
n\geqslant 3
we define
\operatorname{Sq}_{n},\operatorname{Adj}_{n},\operatorname{Op}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}G_{n}
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sq}_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}{\Delta_{e}}^{2},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\Big(\Delta_{e}\sum_{f\in\operatorname{Adj}(e)}\Delta_{f}\Big),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\big(\Delta_{e}\sum_{f\in\operatorname{Op}(e)}\Delta_{f}\big).

Our convention is that empty sums are equal to
0
, and so
\operatorname{Op}_{3}=0
.

It is not hard to check that
\operatorname{Sq}_{n},\operatorname{Adj}_{n}
, and
\operatorname{Op}_{n}
can be expressed in terms of the
A_{n}
-action as well.
Fix
e=\{1,2\}
(or any other edge in
E_{n}
).
Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sq}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma({\Delta_{e}}^{2}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-2)!^{2}}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{e})\left(\sum_{\tau(e)\in\operatorname{Adj}(\sigma(e))}\tau(\Delta_{e})\right),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-2)!^{2}}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{e})\left(\sum_{\tau(e)\in\operatorname{Op}(\sigma(e))}\tau(\Delta_{e})\right).

From these expressions it is clear that
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}
,
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}
and
\operatorname{Op}_{n}
belong to the augmentation ideal
IG_{n}
of
\mathbb{R}G_{n}
and are
*
-invariant and
A_{n}
-invariant.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
n\geqslant 3
we have

\operatorname{Sq}_{n}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}={\Delta_{n}}^{2}\in\Sigma^{2}_{1}\mathbb{R}G.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sq}_{n}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}{\Delta_{e}}^{2}+\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\big({\Delta_{e}}\sum_{f\in\operatorname{Adj}(e)}{\Delta_{f}}\big)+\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\big({\Delta_{e}}\sum_{f\in\operatorname{Op}(e)}{\Delta_{f}}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\Big({\Delta_{e}}\big(\Delta_{e}+\sum_{f\in\operatorname{Adj}(e)}{\Delta_{f}}+\sum_{f\in\operatorname{Op}(e)}{\Delta_{f}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{n}}{\Delta_{e}}\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{e\in E_{n}}{\Delta_{e}}\right)\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle={\Delta_{n}}^{2}

Since
\Delta_{n}
is
*
-invariant and supported on the ball of radius
1
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

We have
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}\in\Sigma_{1}^{2}\mathbb{R}G_{n}
and
\operatorname{Op}_{n}\in\Sigma_{2}^{2}\mathbb{R}G_{n}
.

Proof.

The element
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}
is a sum of squares of Laplacians, which are supported in
B_{1}
.

For
n\leqslant 3
we have
\operatorname{Op}_{n}=0
by definition. For
n>3
, the element
\operatorname{Op}_{n}
is a sum of elements of the form
\Delta_{e}\Delta_{f}
with
f\in\operatorname{Op}(e)
, hence for every
t\in S_{e}
and
s\in S_{f}
, the elements
t
and
s
commute in
G_{n}
.
Now

\displaystyle\Delta_{e}\Delta_{f}

\displaystyle=\big(|S_{e}|-\sum_{t\in S_{e}}t\big)\big(|S_{f}|-\sum_{s\in S_{f}}s\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\sum_{t\in S_{e}}(1-t^{-1})(1-t)\big)\big(\sum_{s\in S_{f}}(1-s^{-1})(1-s)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{(t,s)\in S_{e}\times S_{f}}\big((1-t)(1-s)\big)^{*}\big((1-t)(1-s)\big)

which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For
m\geqslant n\geqslant 3
we have

\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma(\operatorname{Adj}_{n})=n(n-1)(n-2)\frac{(m-3)!}{2}\operatorname{Adj}_{m}.

Proof.

This is a direct computation.
If
m=n
then the result follows immediately, since then
n(n-1)(n-2)\frac{(m-3)!}{2}=|A_{n}|
and
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}
is
A_{n}
-invariant.

Suppose that
m\neq n
.
The case of
m=4
(and hence
n=3
) is special, since
A_{4}
does not act transitively on pairs
(e,f)
with
e\in E_{3}
and
f\in\operatorname{Adj}(e)
;
instead, the action has two orbits, each of cardinality
3
.
Also, the stabiliser in
A_{4}
of a pair
(e,f)
is the same as the stabiliser of
3
points in the natural action of
A_{4}
on
4
points, i.e. is trivial.
It is clear that the coefficient next to
\operatorname{Adj}_{m}
on the right hand side is equal to the product of the cardinality of this stabiliser and any of the orbits.
We thus have

\sum_{\sigma\in A_{4}}\sigma(\operatorname{Adj}_{3})=3\operatorname{Adj}_{4}

which agrees with the statement.

Now suppose that
m>4
.
The action of
A_{m}
on the pairs
(e,f)
is transitive,
there are
n(n-1)(n-2)
such pairs,
and the stabiliser has cardinality
\frac{(m-3)!}{2}
.
Therefore

\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma(\operatorname{Adj}_{n})=n(n-1)(n-2)\frac{(m-3)!}{2}\operatorname{Adj}_{m}.\qed

Lemma 3.8
.

For
m\geqslant n\geqslant 4
we have

\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma(\operatorname{Op}_{n})=2\binom{n}{2}\binom{n-2}{2}{(m-4)!}\operatorname{Op}_{m}.

Proof.

This is a simple computation as before: there are
\binom{n}{2}\binom{n-2}{2}
pairs
(e,f)
with
e\in E_{n}
and
f\in\operatorname{Op}(e)
,
the action of
A_{m}
is transitive on such pairs,
and the stabiliser of a single such pair has cardinality
4|A_{m-4}|=2\cdot{(m-4)!}
.
∎

4
Proving property (T)

We are now ready to give two methods of proving property (T) for the groups
G_{n}
.

4.1
The Methods

Method I: using
\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+k\operatorname{Op}_{n}

This method will be applied to
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
for
n\geqslant 3
and
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
for
n\geqslant 7
.

Proposition 4.1

(Method I)
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and suppose that

\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+k\operatorname{Op}_{n}-\lambda\Delta_{n}\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G_{n}

for some
R\geqslant 2,k\geqslant 0
, and
\lambda>0
.
Then
G_{m}
has Kazhdan’s property (T) for every
m\geqslant n
such that

k(n-3)\leqslant m-3
.
Moreover, the Kazhdan radius of
(G_{m},S_{m})
is bounded above by
R
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
n\geqslant 4
.
By
3.3
,
3.7
and
3.8
we have

\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+k\operatorname{Op}_{n}-\lambda\Delta_{n}\right)=n(n-1)(n-2)\frac{(m-3)!}{2}\operatorname{Adj}_{m}+\\
+2k\binom{n}{2}\binom{n-2}{2}(m-4)!\operatorname{Op}_{m}-\lambda\binom{n}{2}(m-2)!\Delta_{m}\\
=\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{2}(m-3)!\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{m}+\frac{k(n-3)}{m-3}\operatorname{Op}_{m}-\frac{\lambda(m-2)}{n-2}\Delta_{m}\right).

Moreover, since
A_{m}
acts on
G_{m}
by automorphisms, the action on
\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}G
preserves filtration by radii of support, hence the element belongs to
\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G_{m}
. Using the equality above and
3.5
we obtain

\displaystyle{\Delta_{m}}^{2}-\frac{\lambda(m-2)}{n-2}\Delta_{m}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sq}_{m}+\operatorname{Adj}_{m}+\operatorname{Op}_{m}-\frac{\lambda(m-2)}{n-2}\Delta_{m}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sq}_{m}+\left(1-\frac{k(n-3)}{m-3}\right)\operatorname{Op}_{m}+

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{n(n-1)(n-2)(m-3)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+k\operatorname{Op}_{n}-\lambda\Delta_{n}\right).

By
3.6
, both the elements
\operatorname{Sq}_{m}
and
\operatorname{Op}_{m}
belong to
\Sigma^{2}_{2}\mathbb{R}G_{m}
.
Therefore whenever
1-\frac{k(n-3)}{m-3}\geqslant 0
we have

{\Delta_{m}}^{2}-\frac{\lambda(m-2)}{n-2}\Delta_{m}\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G_{m},

which is equivalent to property (T) for
G_{m}
by
[
23
]
.

The case
n=3
is special, since
\operatorname{Op}_{3}=0
and then

\Delta_{m}^{2}-(m-2)\lambda\Delta=\operatorname{Sq}_{m}+\operatorname{Op}_{m}+\frac{1}{3\cdot(m-3)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{m}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{3}-\lambda\Delta_{3}\right)\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G_{m}.

∎

The following corollary is a direct consequence of e.g.
[
3
]
*Remark 5.4.7.

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the same assumptions as above,
the spectral gap of
\Delta_{m}
is bounded below by
\frac{\lambda(m-2)}{n-2}
, which leads to a lower bound of

\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda(m-2)}{(n-2)|S_{m}|}}\leqslant\kappa(G_{m},S_{m})

on the Kazhdan constant.

Method II: using
\operatorname{Adj}_{5}+k\operatorname{Op}_{5}
and induction

This method will be applied to
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{6})
.

Before proceeding, let us define

h_{n}=\frac{(n-2)\cdot(n-1)!}{2}

which is the number of elements
\sigma\in A_{n}
such that
\{1,2\}\subset\sigma(\{1,\ldots,n-1\})
. (The notation
h
stands for ‘hypersimplex’, since
h_{n}
gives the number of elements
\sigma
taking a fixed hypersimplex to one containing a given edge.)

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
n\geqslant 3
we have

{\Delta_{n}}^{2}-\lambda\Delta_{n}=\frac{1}{{h_{n}}^{2}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma\left({\Delta_{n-1}}^{2}-\lambda h_{n}\Delta_{n-1}\right)+X_{n}\right),

where

X_{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\left(\sigma\left(\Delta_{n-1}\right)\sum_{\tau\in A_{n},\tau\neq\sigma}\tau\left(\Delta_{n-1}\right)\right).

Proof.

By
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(n-2)\cdot(n-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(n-2)\cdot(n-3)!\cdot(n-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma\big(\sum_{\tau\in A_{n-1}}\tau(\Delta_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(n-2)\cdot(n-3)!\cdot(n-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}|A_{n-1}|\sigma(\Delta_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cdot(n-1)!}{2\cdot(n-2)!\cdot(n-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{n}.

Using the definition of
h_{n}
we may rewrite this as

\Delta_{n}=\frac{1}{h_{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{n-1}).

Then

{\Delta_{n}}^{2}-\lambda\Delta_{n}=\frac{1}{{h_{n}}^{2}}\left(\left(\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{n-1})\right)^{2}-h_{n}\lambda\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma(\Delta_{n-1})\right)\\
=\frac{1}{{h_{n}}^{2}}\left(\left(\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma\left(\Delta_{n-1}^{2}-h_{n}\lambda\Delta_{n-1}\right)\right)+\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\left(\sigma\left(\Delta_{n-1}\right)\sum_{\tau\in A_{n},\tau\neq\sigma}\tau\left(\Delta_{n-1}\right)\right)\right)\\
=\frac{1}{{h_{n}}^{2}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma\left({\Delta_{n-1}}^{2}-\lambda h_{n}\Delta_{n-1}\right)+X_{n}\right),

as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For
n\geqslant 4
we have

X_{n}=\alpha_{n}\operatorname{Sq}_{n}+\beta_{n}\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+\gamma_{n}\operatorname{Op}_{n},

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=h_{n}\left(h_{n}-\frac{n-2}{n-2}\right),

\displaystyle\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=h_{n}\left(h_{n}-\frac{n-3}{n-2}\right),

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=h_{n}\left(h_{n}-\frac{n-4}{n-2}\right).

Proof.

Using
3.2
we may rewrite
X_{n}
as

\displaystyle X_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\left(\sigma\left(\Delta_{n-1}\right)\sum_{\tau\in A_{n},\tau\neq\sigma}\tau\left(\Delta_{n-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\left(\sigma\Big({\textstyle\sum_{e\in E_{n-1}}}\Delta_{e}\Big)\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in A_{n},\tau\neq\sigma}\tau\Big({\textstyle\sum_{e\in E_{n-1}}}\Delta_{e}\Big)\right).

Expanding the above product, we obtain

X_{n}=\sum_{e,f\in E_{n}}\lambda_{e,f}\Delta_{e}\Delta_{f}

with
\lambda_{e,f}\in\mathbb{Z}
for every
e,f\in E_{n}
.
Since
X_{n}
is
A_{n}
-invariant, we have

\lambda_{e,f}=\lambda_{\sigma(e),\sigma(f)}

for every
\sigma\in A_{n}
.
In particular,
\lambda_{e,f}
may take one of the three potentially distinct values, namely
\alpha_{n}=\lambda_{e,e}
for every
e\in E_{n}
,
\beta_{n}=\lambda_{e,f}
for every
e\in E_{n}
and
f\in\operatorname{Adj}(e)
, and
\gamma_{n}=\lambda_{e,f}
for every
e\in E_{n}
and
f\in\operatorname{Op}(e)
.
Therefore

\displaystyle X_{n}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\operatorname{Sq}_{n}+\beta_{n}\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+\gamma_{n}\operatorname{Op}_{n}.

The coefficients
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}
and
\gamma_{n}
may easily be computed: Fix an edge
e\in E_{n}
, and denote by
H
the hypersimplex of
C_{n}
spanned by vertices
\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Let us start with
\alpha_{n}
. The coefficient
\alpha_{n}
is equal to the number of pairs
(\sigma,\tau)\in A_{n}
such that
\sigma\neq\tau
and
e\in\sigma(H)\cap\tau(H)
.
There are precisely
h_{n}
elements
\sigma
satisfying the above, and so there are
h_{n}-1
elements
\tau
as required. Therefore we have

\alpha_{n}={h_{n}}(h_{n}-1)=h_{n}\left(h_{n}-\frac{n-2}{n-2}\right)

as claimed.

Fix an edge
f\in\operatorname{Adj}(e)
.
The coefficient
\beta_{n}
is equal to the number of pairs
(\sigma,\tau)\in A_{n}
such that
\sigma\neq\tau
,
e\in\sigma(H)
and
f\in\tau(H)
.
There are
h_{n}
elements
\sigma
as required: for
\frac{h_{n}}{n-2}
of them,
\sigma(H)
does not contain
f
; for the remaining
h_{n}-\frac{h_{n}}{n-2}
of these,
\sigma(H)
does contains
f
. Now, for a
\sigma
of the former kind, there are precisely
h_{n}
possible elements
\tau
; if
\sigma
is of the latter kind, there are
h_{n}-1
possibilities for
\tau
. Carrying out the arithmetic yields

\beta_{n}=\frac{h_{n}}{n-2}\cdot h_{n}+\left(h_{n}-\frac{h_{n}}{n-2}\right)(h_{n}-1)=h_{n}\left(h_{n}-\frac{n-3}{n-2}\right).

The computation for
\gamma_{n}
is entirely analogous: we fix
f\in\operatorname{Op}_{n}
and see that

\gamma_{n}=\frac{2h_{n}}{n-2}\cdot h_{n}+\left(h_{n}-\frac{2h_{n}}{n-2}\right)(h_{n}-1)=h_{n}\left(h_{n}-\frac{n-4}{n-2}\right).\qed

Proposition 4.5

(Method II)
.

Suppose that there exist
R\geqslant 2
,
\lambda>0
,
k\geqslant 0
,
n\geqslant k+3
, and
\mu\geqslant 0
such that

{\Delta_{n-1}}^{2}-\lambda\Delta_{n-1}\in\Sigma_{R}^{2}\mathbb{R}G_{n-1}

and

\operatorname{Adj}_{5}+k\operatorname{Op}_{5}-\mu\Delta_{5}\in\Sigma_{R}^{2}\mathbb{R}G_{5}.

Then
G_{n}
has Kazhdan’s property (T). Moreover, the Kazhdan radius of
(G_{n},S_{n})
is bounded above by
R
.

Proof.

By
4.3
, we have

{\Delta_{n}}^{2}-\frac{3\lambda+\mu}{3h_{n}}\Delta_{n}=\frac{1}{{h_{n}}^{2}}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma\left({\Delta_{n-1}}^{2}-\lambda\Delta_{n-1}\right)+\frac{1}{{h_{n}}^{2}}\left(X_{n}-\frac{\mu h_{n}}{3}\Delta_{n}\right).

Therefore, it suffices to show that
X_{n}-\frac{\mu h_{n}}{3}\Delta_{n}\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G_{n}
.

By
4.4
we have

\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{h_{n}}X_{n}

\displaystyle=\big((n-2)h_{n}-n+2\big)\left(\operatorname{Sq}_{n}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}\right)+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+2\operatorname{Op}_{n}.

The element

\operatorname{Sq}_{n}+\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+\operatorname{Op}_{n}={\Delta_{n}}^{2}\in\Sigma^{2}_{1}\mathbb{R}G
,
by
3.5
, so we are left with showing that

\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+2\operatorname{Op}_{n}-\frac{\mu(n-2)}{3}\Delta_{n}\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G
.

By assumption,
\operatorname{Adj}_{5}+k\operatorname{Op}_{5}-\mu\Delta_{5}\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}\mathbb{R}G_{5}
and we have already computed in the proof of
4.1
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{30\cdot(n-3)!}\sum_{\sigma\in A_{n}}\sigma\left(\operatorname{Adj}_{5}+k\operatorname{Op}_{5}-\mu\Delta_{5}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+\frac{2k}{n-3}\operatorname{Op}_{n}-\frac{\mu(n-2)}{3}\Delta_{n}.

Since
n\geqslant k+3
, and since
\operatorname{Op}_{n}\in\Sigma_{R}^{2}\mathbb{R}G_{n}
by
3.6
, we conclude that

\operatorname{Adj}_{n}+2\operatorname{Op}_{n}-\frac{\mu(n-2)}{3}\Delta_{n}\in\Sigma_{R}^{2}\mathbb{R}G_{n}

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Under the same assumptions as above,
the spectral gap of
\Delta_{n}
is bounded below by
\frac{3\lambda+\mu}{3h_{n}}
, which leads to lower bound of

\sqrt{\frac{2(3\lambda+\mu)}{3h_{n}|S_{n}|}}\leqslant\kappa(G_{n},S_{n})

for the Kazhdan constant.

4.2
Approximating sum of squares

Let
IG^{h}
denote the set of
*
-invariant elements in the augmentation ideal
IG
of
\mathbb{R}G
.
To prove the positivity of the element
\Delta^{2}-\lambda\Delta
, a numerical method for finding a decomposition into a sum of squares in
\mathbb{R}G
has been applied by
[
22
,
12
,
18
]
and further developed in
[
17
]
.
In fact, the method provides a proof of the existence of a sum of squares decomposition of the element.
However, a similar method can be employed to prove the existence of such decompositions for other elements in
IG^{h}
.

We use the notation
a\leqslant_{R}b
to denote that
b-a\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}IG
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
a\in G
be of word-length bounded above by
R>1
. Then

(2-a^{*}-a)\leqslant_{\lceil R/2\rceil}\varepsilon\Delta,

for every
\varepsilon\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil}
,
where
\lceil\cdot\rceil
denotes the ceiling function.

Proof.

The proof is an induction on the word-length of
a
.

The case
R=1
is needed in subsequent steps of the induction, and although not covered by the statement the conclusion holds with a larger
\varepsilon
. Let
a=s
be a generator of
G
. Then

(2-s^{*}-s)=(1-s)^{*}(1-s)\leqslant_{1}2\Delta.

If
a=st
is a product of two generators, then

(2-(st)^{*}-st)=2\left((1-s)^{*}(1-s)+(1-t)^{*}(1-t)\right)-(2-s^{*}-t)^{*}(2-s^{*}-t),

hence
(2-(st)^{*}-st)\leqslant_{1}4\Delta
.
For every
\varepsilon\geqslant 4
we have

\varepsilon\Delta-(2-(st)^{*}-st)=(\varepsilon-4)\Delta+\left(4\Delta-(2-(st)^{*}-st)\right)

which admits a decomposition into sum of squares supported in
B_{1}
.

Suppose that
a=uw
is of word-length
R
and each
u,w\in G
is of word-length at most
\lceil R/2\rceil
.
In the following we use the notation
U=(1-u)
and
W=(1-w)
. We have

\displaystyle(2-a^{*}-a)

\displaystyle=(1-uw)^{*}(1-uw)

\displaystyle=(U+uW)^{*}(U+uW)

\displaystyle=U^{*}U+W^{*}u^{*}uW+U^{*}uW+W^{*}u^{*}U

\displaystyle=U^{*}U+W^{*}W-UW-W^{*}U^{*},

and therefore

\displaystyle 2\big(U^{*}U+W^{*}W\big)-(2-a^{*}-a)

\displaystyle=U^{*}U+W^{*}W+UW+W^{*}U^{*}

\displaystyle=UU^{*}+UW+W^{*}U^{*}+W^{*}W

\displaystyle=(U^{*}+W)^{*}(U^{*}+W)

\displaystyle=(2-u^{*}-w)^{*}(2-u^{*}-w).

By the inductive step, for
\varepsilon_{0}=2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}\lceil R/2\rceil\right\rceil}
we have both
(2-u^{*}-u)
and
(2-w^{*}-w)\leqslant_{\lceil R/4\rceil}\varepsilon_{0}\Delta
, i.e.

\varepsilon_{0}\Delta-U^{*}U=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{*}\mu_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon_{0}\Delta-W^{*}W=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}^{*}\nu_{i},

where each
\mu_{i}
and
\nu_{i}
is supported on the ball of radius
\lceil R/4\rceil
.
Moreover

4\varepsilon_{0}\Delta+2\left((U^{*}U-\varepsilon_{0}\Delta)+(W^{*}W-\varepsilon_{0}\Delta)\right)-(2-(uw)^{*}-uw)=\\
(2-u^{*}-w)^{*}(2-u^{*}-w).

We have

2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil}=4\cdot 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}\lceil R/2\rceil\right\rceil}
. Indeed, it is easy to check that the equality holds when
R
is a power of
2
.
If
R
is not, then

\displaystyle 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil}=2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}(R+1)\right\rceil}\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}(2\lceil R/2\rceil)\right\rceil}=4\cdot 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}\lceil R/2\rceil\right\rceil}

and

\displaystyle 4\cdot 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}\lceil R/2\rceil\right\rceil}=2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}(2\lceil R/2\rceil)\right\rceil}\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil}.

Now, for every
\varepsilon\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil}=4\varepsilon_{0}
we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Delta-(2-a^{*}-a)

\displaystyle=(2-u^{*}-w)^{*}(2-u^{*}-w)

\displaystyle\quad+2\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Delta-U^{*}U\right)+2\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Delta-W^{*}W\right)

\displaystyle=(2-u^{*}-w)^{*}(2-u^{*}-w)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{i}\mu_{i}^{*}\mu_{i}+2\sum_{i}\nu_{i}^{*}\nu_{i}+2\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4}-\varepsilon_{0}\right)\Delta,

i.e.
(2-a^{*}-a)\leqslant_{\lceil R/2\rceil}\varepsilon\Delta
, as stated.

Note that if none of the generators in
S
is involutive, the start of the induction provides a better estimate:

-(1-s)^{*}(1-s)\leqslant_{1}\Delta\quad\text{and}\quad-(1-st)^{*}(1-st)\leqslant_{1}2\Delta,

and we can use
\varepsilon\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil-1}
.
∎

Definition 4.8
.

We say that an element
x\in IG^{h}
admits an
\varepsilon
-approximate sum of squares decomposition supported in
B_{R}
if
x+\varepsilon\Delta\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}IG
.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
y\in IG^{h}
and suppose that
y-\lambda\Delta
admits an
\varepsilon
-approximate sum of squares decomposition supported in
B_{R}
,
with
\lambda\geqslant\varepsilon
and
R\geqslant 1
. Then
y\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}IG
.

Proof.

Set
x=y-\lambda\Delta
.
By definition,

y-\left(\lambda-\varepsilon\right)\Delta=x+\varepsilon\Delta=\sum\mu_{i}^{*}\mu_{i}

where each
\mu_{i}
is supported in
B_{R}
.
Therefore

y=\sum\mu_{i}^{*}\mu_{i}+\left(\lambda-\epsilon\right)\Delta

belongs to
\Sigma^{2}_{R}IG
as long as
\lambda\geqslant\varepsilon
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
x\in IG^{h}
.
Suppose that
b\leqslant_{R}x
for some
b
supported in
B_{2R}
.
Then

x
admits an
\varepsilon
-approximate sum of squares decomposition supported in
B_{R}
for every

\varepsilon\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil}\|b\|_{1}
.

Proof.

The lemma is a specialisation of
[
22
]
*Lemma 2.1 which tracks the exact supports of constructed sum of squares decompositions.

Recall that
b\leqslant_{R}x
means that

x-b=\sum\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}IG.

We necessarily have
b\in IG^{h}
, and so we may write it as

b=\sum_{g\in\operatorname{supp}{b}}b_{g}g=\sum_{1\neq g\in\operatorname{supp}{b}}\frac{-b_{g}}{2}(2-g^{*}-g),

where
\operatorname{supp}{b}\subseteq B_{2R}
.

If some coefficient
b_{g}
is negative, then we may include
\frac{|b_{g}|}{2}(2-g^{*}-g)
in the sum of squares decomposition
\Sigma\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}
on the right hand side.
This operation does not alter
R
, the radius of sum of squares decomposition.
Indeed, we may write
g=uw
with both
u
and
w
in
B_{R}
. We then have
(1-g)^{*}(1-g)=(u^{*}-w)^{*}(u^{*}-w)
.
Therefore, we may assume that all coefficients
b_{g}
of
b
are positive for
g\neq 1
.

By the previous lemma, for every non-trivial
g\in B_{2R}
we have

\frac{b_{g}}{2}(1-g)^{*}(1-g)\leqslant_{R}\frac{b_{g}}{2}\delta\Delta,

for every
\delta\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}2R\right\rceil}
.
Since every
b_{g}
is positive (for
g\neq 1
), we have

-b=\sum_{g\neq 1}\frac{b_{g}}{2}(1-g)^{*}(1-g)\leqslant_{R}\sum_{g\neq 1}\frac{b_{g}}{2}\delta\Delta=\frac{\|b\|_{1}}{4}\delta\Delta=\varepsilon\Delta,

where we set
\delta=\frac{4\varepsilon}{\|b\|_{1}}
. Therefore
\varepsilon\Delta+b\in\Sigma^{2}_{R}IG
.
Finally,

x+\varepsilon\Delta=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}+\left(b+\varepsilon\Delta\right)

provides the required sum of squares decomposition.

As in the previous lemma, we could use
\varepsilon\geqslant 2^{2\left\lceil\log_{2}R\right\rceil-1}\|b\|_{1}
if none of the generators of
G
were involutive.
∎

Applications

5.1
Property (T) for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

Let
\Delta_{3}
denote the Laplacian of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
with respect to the standard generating set.
It was shown in
[
18

that

Remark 5.4
.

(Kazhdan constants)
An explicit construction of vectors in representations of the groups
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
provided by A. Żuk (see
[

A lower bound was obtained by M. Kassabov
[

via bounded generation property. To our knowledge these are the best known bounds on the Kazhdan constant for

\frac{1}{42\sqrt{n}+860}\leqslant\kappa(\operatorname{SL}_{n}(Z),S_{n})\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}.

By showing that
\operatorname{Adj}_{3}-0.157999\Delta_{3}\in\Sigma^{2}_{2}I\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

\sqrt{\frac{0.157999(n-2)}{n^{2}-n}}\leqslant\kappa(\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}),S_{n})

for all

.

Remark 5.5

The lower bound obtained here is asymptotically a half of the upper bound and is already

Product Replacement Algorithm

Product replacement algorithm is an algorithm which utilizes a random walk to generate
pseudorandom
elements in finite groups.
In the classical setting of
[
8
]
, given a (huge, but finite) group
G
generated by
n
elements one considers a random walk on
4n(n-1)
-regular graph
\Gamma
:

•

the vertices correspond to
n
-generating tuples of
G
and

•

an edge connects two such tuples if one can be obtained by applying one of the standard generators of
\operatorname{SAut}(F_{n})
to the other.

Using the notation of
[
20
]
*Theorem 3.1 (and noting that the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\operatorname{Sym}_{n}
is transitive on the generating set
S_{n}
) the provable convergence rate of the Product Replacement random walk can be estimated as

\left\|Q_{(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})}^{t}-U\right\|_{\operatorname{tv}}\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\text{for}\quad t\geqslant\frac{24(n^{2}-n)}{1.37(n-2)}\log\left(\frac{|\Gamma|}{\varepsilon}\right),

where
\log(|\Gamma|)\leqslant n\log|G|
. Thus, for a given group
G
with an
n
-generating tuple the mixing time is logarithmic in
|G|
and quadratic in
n
.

Product Replacement Algorithm is implemented e.g. in GAP
[
26
]
, however,
the applicability of the obtained bounds to actual implementations is debatable:
the GAP system implements a version of PRA with an
accumulator
and it is not clear whether the results of
[
20
]
apply to it.
The implementations of other computer algebra systems are not available to be analyzed, so no conclusion about them can be drawn.

[

through the

Replication

The first jupyter notebook can be used to perform
the described computations for
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
(

).
Carrying out the calculations should take no longer than
10

As an interesting detail we note that the optimization problem for

\operatorname{Adj}_{3}

e.g. exhibits faster convergence and better accuracy with relatively larger
\lambda

despite being larger in size (i.e. number of variables and constraints).
The same is even more true for

We provide a precomputed solution for the more computationally demanding case of

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{5}+2\operatorname{Op}_{5}-0.28\Delta\in I\operatorname{SAut}(F_{5}),

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{5}+3\operatorname{Op}_{5}-1.4\Delta\in I\operatorname{SAut}(F_{5}).

The precomupted data includes the elements

as well as the orbit decomposition, multiplication table and matrices

The second jupyter notebook also contains code which may be used to find an approximate sum of squares decomposition of

The reason for including this computation is that it performs all the steps without any precomputed data. The running time should be less than 10 minutes. Once the existence of a sum of squares is certified,

Bouncing cosmology in modified gravity with higher-order curvature terms
J. K. Singh, Harshna Balhara, Kazuharu Bamba, J. Jena

Abstract
A bouncing scenario of a flat homogeneous and isotropic universe is explored by using the reconstruction technique for the power-law parametrization of the Hubble parameter in a modified gravity theory with higher-order curvature and trace of the energy-momentum tensor terms. It is demonstrated that bouncing criteria are satisfied so that the cosmological initial singularity can be avoided. In addition, it is shown that the equation of state parameter crosses the line of the phantom divide. In the present scenario, the universe is filled with a perfect fluid around the bouncing point, in which the universe becomes highly unstable and a big bounce can be realized. Furthermore, it is found that extremal acceleration occurs at the bouncing point.



PACS numbers: 04.50.-h, 98.80.-k.

Keywords: modified gravity theory; bouncing scenario; quintom model.

II
Formulation of
\mathcal{F}(R,T)
gravity with higher-order curvature terms

The higher order
\mathcal{F}(R,T)
gravity may be reviewed as a modification in basic gravitational theory that describes the evolution of the cosmos. The split-up model
\mathcal{F}(R,T)=\mathcal{F}(R)+\mathcal{F}(T)
, where
\mathcal{F}(R)
and
\mathcal{F}(T)
are arbitrary functions of
R
and
T

;
Mirza:2014nfa
;
Zaregonbadi:2016xna
to investigate cosmic dynamics from many perspectives.

The general action for
\mathcal{F}(R,T)=\mathcal{F}(R)+\mathcal{F}(T)
gravity

paired with the action of a matter field with matter Lagrangian
\mathcal{S}_{m}
is given by

\mathcal{S}=\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\int\mathcal{F}(R,T)\sqrt{-g}dx^{4}+\int\mathcal{S}_{m}\sqrt{-g}dx^{4},

(1)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar and
T
is the stress energy-momentum tensor trace. The following equation results from varying the action in Eq. (
1
) concerning
g_{ij}

\mathcal{F}^{R}(R,T)R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}\mathcal{F}(R,T)+(g_{ij}\Box-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j})\mathcal{F}^{R}(R,T)=8\pi G\,T_{ij}-\mathcal{F}^{T}(R,T)(T_{ij}+\Theta_{ij}),

(2)

where the derivative of
\mathcal{F}(R,T)

w.r.t.

R
and

\mathcal{F}^{R}(R,T)

The d’Alembert operator

denotes the covariant derivative w.r.t.

related to the symmetric Levi-Civita connection.
The form of

\Theta_{ij}\equiv g^{lm}\frac{\delta T_{lm}}{\delta g^{ij}}=-2T_{ij}+g_{ij}S_{m}-2g^{lm}\frac{\delta^{2}S_{m}}{\delta g_{ij}\delta g^{lm}}.

(3)

In this analysis we consider a perfect fluid in thermodynamic equilibrium and simply fix the matter Lagrangian
\mathcal{S}_{m}=-p
and consider the energy-momentum tensor of matter as

T_{ij}=(p+\rho)u_{i}u_{j}-pg_{ij},

(4)

where

and

denote the fluid’s isotropic pressure and energy density, respectively. In a co-moving coordinate system,

is the four velocity vector which satisfies

3

\Theta_{ij}=-2T_{ij}-pg_{ij}.

(5)

The gravitational equation of motion can be obtained by substituting Eq. (

\mathcal{F}^{R}(R,T)R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}\mathcal{F}(R,T)+(g_{ij}\Box-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j})\mathcal{F}^{R}(R,T)=8\pi GT_{ij}+\mathcal{F}^{T}(R,T)(T_{ij}+pg_{ij}).

(6)

A more common representation of Eq. (

\displaystyle G_{ij}=R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi G}{\mathcal{F}^{R}(R,T)}(T_{ij}+T_{ij}^{{}^{\prime}}),

(7)

Taking

This approach is known as a model-independent way to study DE models without compromising the underlying theory.

III
Bouncing solution and its dynamical behaviour

The concept of the Big Bounce scenario is a kind of the possible way to avoid the issue of Big Bang singularity. In the Big Bounce scenario, the Big Bang appears as the beginning of a period of expansion that followed a period of contraction. In the scenario of a cyclic universe, the universe emerges from a previous contracting phase and eventually go through an expanding phase without any singularity. In other words, the universe undergoes a bouncing phase. The cosmological bounce can be regarded as an oscillatory or cyclic universe, in which one cosmological event is the result of the collapse of a previous phase. The following points are discussed in detail in order to construct a realistic bouncing model of the universe
Ijjas:2016tpn
;
Cai:2007qw
;
Singh:2022gln
:

1.

In the contracting universe, the scale factor
a(t)
decreases (
\dot{a}(t)<0
) and in the expanding universe, the scale factor increases (
\dot{a}(t)>0
). The derivative
\dot{a}(t)=0
or
\ddot{a}(t)>0
in the vicinity of the bouncing point. When
a(t)
approaches a non-zero minimum value in the vicinity of the bouncing point, the model is called as a non-singular bouncing model.

2.

Correspondingly, the universe contracts when
H<0
, the universe expands when
H>0
and finally
H=0
at
the time when the cosmological bounce occurs (which we call as the bouncing point). The condition
\dot{H}=-4\pi G\rho(1+\omega)>0
, which is comparable to the violation of null energy condition (
\rho+p>0
), must be satisfied in the vicinity of the bouncing point for a successful bouncing model in standard cosmology. It can be seen from this equation that
\omega\simeq-1
around the bouncing point.

3.

Another crucial aspect is that the equation of state (EoS) parameter
\omega
crosses the phantom divide line (
\omega=-1
) in the quintom model close to the bouncing point.

Given the aforementioned general criteria for a cosmological bounce and considering the Friedmann equations (
11
) and (
12
), it can be deduced that such a behavior can be achieved in the framework of
\mathcal{F}(R,T)
cosmology. Specifically, by starting with a desirable form of the Hubble parameter
H(t)
, the corresponding scale factor
a(t)
can be easily determined. In this paper, we focus on the cosmographic parameter
H(t)
, which describes the expansion of the universe and helps us find impressive bouncing solutions to the Einstein field equations.

Abstract
The paper deals with the data-based design of state-feedback controllers that solve the output regulation problem for a class of nonlinear systems. Inspired by recent developments in model-based output regulation design techniques and in data-driven control design for nonlinear systems, we derive a data-dependent semidefinite program that, when solved, directly returns a controller that steers the regulation error to a periodic signal whose Fourier series has identically zero coefficients up to a certain order set by the controller. When specialized to the case of linear systems, the result appears to improve upon existing work. Numerical results illustrate the findings.



1
Introduction

The last few years have witnessed a concerted effort to use data collected in offline experiments for
directly
designing control laws for uncertain dynamical systems. The qualifier “direct" refers to the fact that such an approach has focused on deriving data-dependent convex programs that, when solved, immediately return a controller with certified guarantees of correctness.

After numerous works pioneered the direct data-driven design for linear control systems
, more recent efforts have concentrated on nonlinear control systems – see
[
10
,
23
]
.

In one of these recent results for nonlinear control systems
[
16
]
, we have explored the role of contractivity (
[
20
,
24
,
29
,
6
]
) for designing controllers from data for nonlinear systems. A benefit of using contractivity is that it allows the designer to
address significant control problems beyond stabilization, one of these being the output regulation problem

[
25
,
13
,
12
]
. This feature was exploited in
[
16
]
to derive a data-dependent semidefinite program (SDP) for tuning nonlinear integral controllers that guarantee tracking of constant reference signals and rejection of constant disturbances in nonlinear systems
of the same class as the one considered here
.

In this note, we extend the result of
[
16
]
to the case of periodic exogenous signals. Since we are interested in designing output regulators for unknown nonlinear systems, we refrain from assuming the existence of a normal form of the system, which is not available to the designer.
We focus on a class of nonlinear systems whose drift vector field can be expressed as a linear combination of elements in a library of functions and whose input vector field is constant. We

approach the problem by

augmenting the plant with a post-processing internal model (adopting the terminology in
[
1
]
),

and then designing a “stabilizer"
from data
that
renders the resulting closed-loop system
contractive. Contractivity of such
a
closed-loop system guarantees that the steady-state response of the system is also periodic, and this property is used to conclude that

certain harmonics of the steady state regulation error are zero.

In a model-based context, the approach outlined above has been thoroughly investigated.
Adding the internal model first and then the stabilizer, and using Fourier series properties to infer

that some of the coefficients of the Fourier series of the error vanish

has been inspired by previous works (
[
2
,
3
,
14
]
).

The results in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
also show how to make the regulation error arbitrarily small in the
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-sense for disturbances of sufficiently small magnitude. See
[
7
, Section 2.5]
for an early formulation of an approximate output regulation problem.

The main novelty of this paper is to use the data-driven control design framework of
[
9
,
8
,
16
]
to reduce the design of output regulators for

a class of
unknown nonlinear systems to the solution of a data-dependent semidefinite program. To achieve this goal, in addition to the tools in the above mentioned references, we establish a new technical result that filters out the impact of the exogenous signals on the data-dependent representation used for the analysis. When specialized to the case of linear systems, the result
provides a methodological improvement over existing work, in the sense that no measurement of the disturbance is needed to design the controller.

On the other hand, we do not provide any results on the design of controllers that make the
\mathcal{L}_{2}
-norm of the steady state regulation error arbitrarily small.

Due to the variety of contraction-related concepts, a clarification about the adopted terminology in relation with recent contributions is useful. In

\hskip 128.0374pt+\dot{\mathcal{M}}(x,w(t))\preceq\!-\beta\mathcal{M}(x),\end{array}

Conclusions and future work

We proposed a data-driven design of controllers that solve
a harmonic
output regulation problem for unknown nonlinear systems. Inspired by the model-based results of
[
2
,
3
,
14
]
, we adopted an approach that does not require the existence of a normal form and privileges an approximate solution, which appears to be a natural viewpoint in the case the controlled plant is unknown. We have focused on continuous-time systems, but it is of interest to develop similar results for discrete-time systems. One reason to do so is to gain insights in the design of
output
feedback regulators, for which one could rely on results available for discrete-time systems. The breadth of the nonlinear output regulation theory suggests to explore other methods for the future, which might provide solutions with complementary features. Within the framework proposed in this paper, other aspects of interest are the use of projected dynamical systems to enforce constraints (see e.g.
[
22
]
) and whether the noise filtering lemma can be extended to deal with unknown frequencies (see
[
21
]
for a recent original viewpoint on the tracking problem of exogenous signals of unknown frequencies and
[
28
]
for a classical one).

Global weak solution of 3D-NSE with exponential damping


Abstract
In this paper we prove the global existence of incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with damping α ⁡ ( e β ​ | u | 2 − 1 ) ​ u \alpha(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u , where we use Friedrich method and some new tools. The delicate problem in the construction of a global solution, is the passage to the limit in exponential nonlinear term. To solve this problem, we use a polynomial approximation of the damping part and a new type of interpolation between L ∞ ​ ( ℝ + , L 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})) and the space of functions f f such that ( e β ​ | f | 2 − 1 ) ​ | f | 2 ∈ L 1 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) (e^{\beta|f|^{2}}-1)|f|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) . Fourier analysis and standard techniques are used.

Introduction

In this paper we study the global existence of weak solution to the modified incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in three spatial dimensions

(S)\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+u.\nabla u+\alpha(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u=\;\;-\nabla p\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
{\rm div}\,u=0\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
u(0,x)=u^{0}(x)\;\;\hbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{cases}

where
u=u(t,x)=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
,
p=p(t,x)
denote respectively the unknown velocity and the unknown pressure of the fluid at the point
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the viscosity of fluid
\nu>0
. Also
\alpha,\beta>0
denote the parameters of damping term. The terms
(u.\nabla u):=u_{1}\partial_{1}u+u_{2}\partial_{2}u+u_{3}\partial_{3}u
, while
u^{0}=(u_{1}^{o}(x),u_{2}^{o}(x),u_{3}^{o}(x))
is an initial given velocity. If
u^{0}
is quite regular, the divergence free condition determines the pressure
p
. We recall in our case it was assumed the viscosity is unitary (
\nu=1
) in order to simplify the calculations and the proofs of our results. The global existence of weak solution of initial value problem of classical incompressible Navier-Stokes were proved by Leray and Hopf (see
[
7
]
-
[
10
]
) long before. Uniqueness remains an open problem for the dimensions
d\geq 3
. The damping is from the resistance to the motion of the flow. It describes various physical situations such as porous media flow, drag or friction effects, and some dissipative mechanisms (see
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
9
]
and references therein). The polynomial damping
\alpha|u|^{\beta-1}u
is studied in
[
6
]
by Cai and Jiu, where they proved the globale existence of weak solution in

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{\beta+1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

The purpose of this paper is to study the well-posedness of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with damping
\alpha(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u
.
We will show that the Cauchy problem
(S)
has global weak solutions for any
\alpha,\beta\in(0,\infty)
. We apply the Friedrich method to construct the approximate solutions and make more delicate estimates
to proceed to compactness arguments. In particular, we obtain new more a priori estimates,

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(z)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dz+2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\|(e^{\beta|u(z)|^{2}}-1)|u(z)|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}dz\leq\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

comparing with the
Navier-Stokes equations, to guarantee that the solution
u
belongs to

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{E}_{\beta}

where

\mathcal{E}_{\beta}=\{f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;{\rm measurable};\;(e^{\beta|f|^{2}}-1)|f|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\}.

Before treating the global existence, we give the definition of solution of
(S)
.

Definition 1.1
.

The function pair
(u,p)
is called a weak solution of the problem
(S)
if for any
T>0
, the
following conditions are satisfied:

1.

u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and
(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2}\in L^{1}([0,T],L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.

2.

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+u.\nabla u+\alpha(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u=-\nabla p
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
: for any
\Phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
{\rm div}\,\Phi(t,x)=0
for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\Phi(T)=0
, we have

-\int_{0}^{T}(u;\partial_{t}\Phi)+\int_{0}^{T}(\nabla u;\nabla\Phi)+\int_{0}^{T}((u.\nabla)u;\Phi)+\alpha\int_{0}^{T}((e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u;\Phi)=(u^{0};\Phi(0)).

3.

{\rm div}\,u(x,t)=0
for a.e.
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Here
(\;;\;)
means the inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Remark 1.2
.

a)
If
(u,p)
is a solution of
(S)
, then
(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Indeed: For
T>0
, put the following subsets of
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{array}[]{lcl}A_{T}^{1}&=&\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3};\;0<|u(t,x)|<1\},\\
A_{T}^{2}&=&\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3};\;|u(t,x)|\geq 1\}.\end{array}

Then

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|=\displaystyle\int_{A_{T}^{1}\cup A_{T}^{2}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|\\
\leq\displaystyle\int_{A_{T}^{1}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|+\int_{A_{T}^{2}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|\\
\leq\displaystyle\int_{A_{T}^{1}}\frac{(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)}{|u|}|u|^{2}+\int_{A_{T}^{2}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)\frac{1}{|u|}|u|^{2}\\
\leq\displaystyle M_{\beta}\int_{A_{T}^{1}}|u|^{2}+\int_{A_{T}^{2}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2},\;M_{\beta}=\max_{0<X\leq 1}\frac{(e^{\beta X^{2}}-1)}{X}\\
\leq\displaystyle M_{\beta}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2}\\
\leq\displaystyle M_{\beta}T\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2}\\
\leq\displaystyle M_{\beta}T\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2}\|_{L^{1}([0,T],L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))},\end{array}

which implies the desired result.

b)
By this definition the scalar pressure function
p
is well defined in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, for all
s>3/2
. Indeed: Formally, if
p
exists, then

p=(-\Delta)^{-1}\Big({\rm div}\,u.\nabla u+\alpha{\rm div}\,(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u\Big).

So, just prove that
(-\Delta)^{-1}({\rm div}\,(u.\nabla u)),(-\Delta)^{-1}(\alpha{\rm div}\,(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u)\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, for all
s>3/2
. For this, for
s>3/2
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\|(-\Delta)^{-1}({\rm div}\,(u.\nabla u))(t)\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\|(-\Delta)^{-1}({\rm div}\,({\rm div}(u\otimes u)))(t)\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
\leq\|u\otimes u(t)\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
\leq\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s}|\mathcal{F}(u\otimes u)(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}\\
\leq\displaystyle c_{s}\|\mathcal{F}(u\otimes u)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\;c_{s}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}\\
\leq c_{s}\|u\otimes u(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
\leq c_{s}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).\end{array}

In other hand, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\|(-\Delta)^{-1}({\rm div}\,(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u(t)\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
\leq\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s}|\xi|^{-2}|\mathcal{F}((e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u)(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}\\
\leq\displaystyle C_{s}\|\mathcal{F}((e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\;C_{s}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-s}|\xi|^{-2}d\xi\Big)^{1/2}\\
\leq C_{s}\|(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).\end{array}

Then
p
is well defined in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\,s>3/2
.

c)
If
(u(t,x),p(t,x))
is a solution of
(S)
, then
(u(t,x),p(t,x)+h(t))
is also solution of
(S)
. Then, if we look for
p
in the space
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, we get
h=0
and we can express
p
in terms of
u
.

In our case of exponential damping, we are trying to find more regularity of Leray solution in
\cap_{p}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. In particular, we give a new energy estimate. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
u^{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a divergence free vector fields, then there is a global solution of
(S)

u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{E}_{\beta}
. Moreover, for all
t\geq 0

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(z)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dz+2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\|(e^{\beta|u(z)|^{2}}-1)|u(z)|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}dz\leq\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Remark 1.4
.

a)
If
(u,p)
is a solution of
(S)
given by Theorem

) and Proposition

The remainder of our paper is organized as follows. In the second section we give some notations, definitions and preliminary results. Section 3 is devoted to prove Theorem
1.3
, this proof is done in two steps. In the first, we give a general result of equicontinuity, in the second we apply Friedritch method to construct a global solution of
(S)
, the only delicate point is to show the convergence of the nonlinear part
(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}
to
(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u
. To do this, we approximate
(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}
by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2k}u_{\varphi(n)}
and we use the convergence in
L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
given by technical lemmas.

Notations and preliminary results

2.1
Notations

In this section, we collect some notations and definitions that will be used later.

\bullet

For
R>0
:
B_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3};\;|x|<R\}
.

\bullet

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
:
\displaystyle P_{m}(X)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{X^{k}}{k!}
. Clearly, for each
R>0
, we have
P_{m}(\beta R^{2})\nearrow(e^{\beta R^{2}}-1)
, as
m\rightarrow+\infty
.

\bullet

The Fourier transformation is normalized as

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp(-ix.\xi)f(x)dx,\,\,\,\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

\bullet

The inverse Fourier formula is

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(g)(x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp(i\xi.x)g(\xi)d\xi,\,\,\,x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

\bullet

The convolution product of a suitable pair of function
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by

(f\ast g)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(y)g(x-y)dy.

\bullet

If
f=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
and
g=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
are two vector fields, we set

f\otimes g:=(g_{1}f,g_{2}f,g_{3}f),

and

{\rm div}\,(f\otimes g):=({\rm div}\,(g_{1}f),{\rm div}\,(g_{2}f),{\rm div}\,(g_{3}f)).

Moreover, if
\rm{div}\,g=0
we obtain

{\rm div}\,(f\otimes g):=g_{1}\partial_{1}f+g_{2}\partial_{2}f+g_{3}\partial_{3}f:=g.\nabla f.

\bullet

Let
(B,||.||)
, be a Banach space,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
T>0
. We define
L^{p}_{T}(B)
the space of all
measurable functions
[0,t]\ni t\mapsto f(t)\in B
such that
t\mapsto||f(t)||\in L^{p}([0,T])
.

\bullet

The Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3});\;(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}
.

\bullet

The homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3});\;\widehat{f}\in L^{1}_{loc}\;{\rm and}\;|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}
.

\bullet

L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\{f\in(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3};\;{\rm div}\,f=0\}
.

\bullet

For
R>0
, the Friedritch operator
J_{R}
is defined by

J_{R}(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}({\bf 1}_{B_{R}}(\xi)\widehat{f}).

\bullet

The Leray projector
\mathbb{P}:(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3}\rightarrow(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{3}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{P}f)=\widehat{f}(\xi)-(\widehat{f}(\xi).\frac{\xi}{|\xi|})\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}=M(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi);\;M(\xi)=(\delta_{k,l}-\frac{\xi_{k}\xi_{l}}{|\xi|^{2}})_{1\leq k,l\leq 3}.

\bullet

For
R>0
, we define the operator
A_{R}(D)
by

A_{R}(D)f=\mathbb{P}J_{R}(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(M(\xi){\bf 1}_{B_{R}}(\xi)\widehat{f}).

\bullet

If
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
\Omega_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\;\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
are two open subsets, we define
L^{p}(\Omega_{1},L^{q}(\Omega_{2}))
by:

f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{1},L^{q}(\Omega_{2}))
if
f:\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\;(x_{1},x_{2})\rightarrow f(x_{1},x_{2})
is measurable and

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1},L^{q}(\Omega_{2}))}=\|\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega_{2})}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1})}

is well defined and finite.
Particularly, if
p=q
, then
L^{p}(\Omega_{1},L^{q}(\Omega_{2}))=L^{p}(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})
and

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1},L^{p}(\Omega_{2}))}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})}.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta>0
, there is
C_{m,\beta}>0
such that

|P_{m}(\beta|x|^{2})x-P_{m}(\beta|y|^{2})y|\leq C_{m,\beta}\Big(P_{m}(\beta|x|^{2}))+P_{m}(\beta|y|^{2})\Big)|x-y|,\;\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Proof.
Suppose that
|y|\leq|x|

P_{m}(\beta|x|^{2})x-P_{m}(\beta|y|^{2})y=P_{m}(\beta|x|^{2})(x-y)+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}(|x|^{2k}-|y|^{2k})y.

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Big||x|^{2k}-|y|^{2k}\Big|.|y|&\leq&2k|x-y|.(|x|^{2k-1}+|y|^{2k-1}).|y|\\

which implies the desired result.

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
s>d/2
:
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, we have

\|f\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\sigma_{s,d}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\;\forall f\in H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d\geq 2
, then there is a constant
C>0
such that: If
(f,g)\in H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
fg\in H^{1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\|fg\|_{H^{1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{H^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.
We have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|fg\|_{H^{1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}&\leq&\|fg\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{\xi}|\xi|^{2(1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2})}\Big|\int_{\eta}\widehat{f}(\eta)\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta)\Big|^{2}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{\xi}|\xi|^{2(1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2})}\Big(\int_{\eta}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)||\widehat{g}(\eta)|\Big)^{2}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{\xi}|\xi|^{2(1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2})}\Big(\int_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{-\varepsilon/4}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)|(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{\varepsilon/4}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|\Big)^{2}.\end{array}

By using the elementary inequality

(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}\leq(1+2|\xi|^{2}+2|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}\leq 6^{\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}(1+|\xi|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+|\eta|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}),

we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|fg\|_{H^{1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}&\leq&\displaystyle C\int_{\xi}|\xi|^{2(1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2})}\Big(\int_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)|(1+|\xi|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}+|\eta|^{\varepsilon/2})|\widehat{g}(\eta)|\Big)^{2}\\
&\leq&C(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}),\end{array}

with

\begin{array}[]{lll}I_{1}&=&\displaystyle\int_{\xi}|\xi|^{2(1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2})}\Big(\int_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)||\widehat{g}(\eta)|\Big)^{2}=\|u_{1}v_{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}\\
u_{1}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|)\\
v_{1}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\widehat{g}(\xi)|),\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{lll}I_{2}&=&\displaystyle\int_{\xi}|\xi|^{2(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\frac{d}{2})}\Big(\int_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)||\widehat{g}(\eta)|d\eta\Big)^{2}d\xi=\|u_{2}v_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}\\
u_{2}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|)\\
v_{2}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\widehat{g}(\xi)|)\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}I_{3}&=&\displaystyle\int_{\xi}|\xi|^{2(1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2})}\Big(\int_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|^{2})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)||\eta|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|\Big)^{2}=\|u_{3}v_{3}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{2}}}^{2}\\
u_{3}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}\widehat{f}(\xi))\\
v_{3}&=&\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}|\widehat{g}(\xi)|).\end{array}

By applying Lemma
2.2
with the following choices

(s_{1},t_{1})=(0,1-\varepsilon),\;(s_{2},t_{2})=(0,1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}),\;(s_{3},t_{3})=(0,1-\varepsilon)

we get

\|u_{i}v_{i}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{i}+t_{i}-\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u_{i}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v_{i}\|_{\dot{H}^{t_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\;i\in\{1,2,3\}.

Then

\begin{array}[]{l}I_{1}\leq C\|u_{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{0}}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{H^{1-\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{H^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\\
\\
I_{2}\leq C\|u_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{0}}^{2}\|v_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{H^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\\
\\
I_{3}\leq C\|u_{3}\|_{\dot{H}^{0}}^{2}\|v_{3}\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\varepsilon}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2}\|g\|_{H^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}^{2},\end{array}

which ends the proof of the Lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
\Omega\neq\emptyset
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. If
(f_{n})
is a bounded sequence in

, then there is a subsequence
(f_{h(n)})
and

such that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(f_{h(n)}|g)_{L^{2}}&=&(f|g)_{L^{2}},\;\;\;\forall g\in L^{2}(\Omega)\\
\|f\|_{L^{p}}&\leq&\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{h(n)}\|_{L^{p}}\\
\|f\|_{L^{2}}&\leq&\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{h(n)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{array}

Proof.

(f_{n})
is a bounded sequence in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega)
, then there is a subsequence
(f_{h(n)})
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(f_{h(n)}|g)_{L^{2}}=(f|g)_{L^{2}},\;g\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Particularly, for
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

|(f_{h(n)}|g)_{L^{2}}|\leq\|f_{h(n)}\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}},\;\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.

Then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}|(f_{h(n)}|g)_{L^{2}}|\leq(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{h(n)}\|_{L^{p}})\|g\|_{L^{q}}

and

|(f|g)_{L^{2}}|\leq(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{h(n)}\|_{L^{p}})\|g\|_{L^{q}}.

As
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in

, then
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p}}
.

The last result is giving by Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 2.9

3.5
)-(
3.9
) and classical argument by combining Ascoli’s theorem and the Cantor diagonal process, we get a nondecreasing
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and

u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
such that: for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}-u)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{q}],H^{-4})}=0.

(3.10)

\bullet

Combining inequalities (
3.7
)-(
3.10
) and applying Lemma
2.10
, we get

u_{\varphi(n)}\rightarrow u\;{\rm strongly\;in}\;L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\;\forall p\in[2,\infty).

(3.11)

\bullet

The sequence
(u_{n})
is bounded in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, then

u_{\varphi(n)}\rightarrow u\;{\rm weakly\;in}\;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Particularly
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
and

\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{\varphi(n)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\;\;\forall t\geq 0.

(3.12)

\bullet

Prove that for all
T>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2k+2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2k+2}.

(3.13)

For this take
m\in\mathbb{N}
and put
\Omega_{m}=]0,T[\times B_{m}
. Applying Lemma
2.10
to the sequence
({\bf 1}_{\Omega_{m}}u_{\varphi(n)})
with
p_{0}=2k+4
and
p=2k+2
, with taking into account (
3.8
), we get

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{m}}|u|^{2k+2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{m}}|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2k+2}.

Then

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B_{m}}|u|^{2k+2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2k+2}.

By using Monotonic Convergence Theorem, we obtain the desired result.

\bullet

Prove that for all
T>0
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}.

(3.14)

For this, take
m\in\mathbb{N}
and by (
3.13
), we get

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}P_{m}(\beta|u|^{2})|u|^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}P_{m}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2})|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}.

Then

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}P_{m}(\beta|u|^{2})|u|^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}.

Monotonic Convergence Theorem gives the desired result.

\bullet

Combining the above inequalities, we obtain: for all
t\geq 0

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(z)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dz+2\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\|(e^{\beta|u(z)|^{2}}-1)|u(z)|^{2}\|_{L^{1}}dz\leq\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.15)

It remains to show that
u
is a solution of the system
(S)
.
To do this, we must verify that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J_{\varphi(n)}\big(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}\big)=u^{i}u^{j}\;\;\;{\rm in}\;\;\;{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{[}0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})),

(3.16)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}J_{\varphi(n)}\big[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}\big]=(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u\;\;\;{\rm in}\;\;\;{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{[}0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})).

(3.17)

\bullet

Let
\Phi
in
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
. There is an integer
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that

supp(\Phi)\subset[0,T_{q})\times B(0,q).

We start by proving (
3.16
). For this, we write

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Phi u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}-\Phi u^{i}u^{j}&=&\theta_{q}\Phi u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}-\theta_{q}\Phi u^{i}u^{j}\\
&=&\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}-u^{i})\Phi u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}+\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}-u^{j})\Phi u^{i}.\end{array}

Using Lemma
2.6
, we get, for
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}-u^{i})\Phi u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{1}{2}})}\\
~\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad~\leq C\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}-u^{i})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{q}],H^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}})}\|\Phi u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}],H^{1-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}})}\\
~\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad~\leq C\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}-u^{i})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{q}],H^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}})}\|\Phi u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}],H^{1})}\\
~\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad~\leq C^{\prime}\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}-u^{i})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{q}];H^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}})}\|u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}];H^{1})}\\
~\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad~\leq C^{\prime}M_{T_{q}}\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}-u^{i})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{q}];H^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}})},\end{array}

where
M_{T_{q}}
is given by (
3.6
). Then, equation (
3.10
) implies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{i}-u^{i})\Phi u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{1}{2}})}=0.

In a similar way, we show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}^{j}-u^{j})\Phi u^{i}=0\;{\rm in}\;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Then, we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Phi u^{i}_{\varphi(n)}u^{j}_{\varphi(n)}=\Phi u^{i}u^{j}\;\;\;{\rm in}\;\;\;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Using the fact that
(u_{n})
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|(Id-J_{\varphi(n)})u^{i}(t)u^{j}(t)\|_{H^{-\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}}&\leq&\|(Id-J_{\varphi(n)})u^{i}(t)u^{j}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}}\\
&\leq&(\varphi(n))^{-\varepsilon_{0}}\|u^{i}(t)u^{j}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\\
&\leq&C(\varphi(n))^{-\varepsilon_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.\end{array}

Then, we get

\|(Id-J_{\varphi(n)})u^{i}(t)u^{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\varepsilon_{0}-\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C(\varphi(n))^{-\varepsilon_{0}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\dot{H}^{1})}.

which implies (
3.16
).

\bullet

Now, we want to prove (
3.17
). Let
\varepsilon>0
and
R_{0}>0
such that

\frac{1}{R_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+4}.

(3.18)

Let
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(e^{\beta R_{0}^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta R_{0}^{2}))R_{0}=\sum_{k=m_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}R_{0}^{2k+1}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4\alpha\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+4}.

(3.19)

We have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J_{\varphi(n)}[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]\Phi-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u\Phi=(\sum_{i=1}^{3}L_{n}^{i})+S,

with

\begin{array}[]{lcl}L_{n}^{1}&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2})u_{\varphi(n)}-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2})u]\Phi\\
L^{2}_{n}&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(Id-J_{\varphi(n)})[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]\Phi\\
L^{3}_{n}&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}))u_{\varphi(n)}]\Phi\\
S&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2}))u]\Phi.\end{array}

-
Estimate of term
L_{n}^{1}
: Using Lemma
2.4
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|L_{n}^{1}|&\leq&\displaystyle C_{m_{0},\beta}\int_{0}^{T_{q}}\int_{B(0,q)}[P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2})+P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2})]\theta_{q}|u_{\varphi(n)}-u||\Phi|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C_{m_{0},\beta}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{0}^{T_{q}}\int_{B(0,q)}[P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2})+P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2})]\theta_{q}|u_{\varphi(n)}-u|.\end{array}

Applying Cauchy-Schwartz and using Lemma
2.3
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|L_{n}^{1}|&\leq&\displaystyle C_{m_{0},\beta}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big(\|[P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2})\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}]\times B(0,q))}\\
&&+\|P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2})]\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}]\times B(0,q))}\Big)\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}-u)\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}]\times B(0,q))}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle c_{m_{0},\beta}C_{m_{0},\beta}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big(\|(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}([0,T_{q}],L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{1/2}\\
&&+\|(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2}\|_{L^{1}([0,T_{q}],L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{1/2}\Big)\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}-u)\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}]\times B(0,q))}.\end{array}

The energy inequalities (
3.5
)-(
3.15
) give

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|L_{n}^{1}|&\leq&\displaystyle c_{m_{0},\beta}C_{m_{0},\beta}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\frac{\sqrt{2}\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\|\theta_{q}(u_{\varphi(n)}-u)\|_{L^{2}([0,T_{q}],L^{2})}.\end{array}

Then, the convergence result (
3.10
) gives an integer
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that: for all
n\geq n_{1}
, we have

|L_{n}^{1}|<\varepsilon/4.

(3.20)

-
Estimate of term
L^{2}_{n}
: We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|L^{2}_{n}|&=&|\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T_{q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(Id-J_{\varphi(n)})[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]\Phi|\\
&=&|\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T_{q}}((Id-J_{\varphi(n)})[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]|\Phi)_{L^{2}}|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T_{q}}\|(Id-J_{\varphi(n)})[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]\|_{H^{-4}}\|\Phi(t)\|_{H^{4}}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{C}{\varphi(n)}\int_{0}^{T_{q}}\|[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]\|_{H^{-3}}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{4})}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{C}{\varphi(n)}\|[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]\|_{L^{1}([0,T_{q}],H^{-3})}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{4})}.\end{array}

By using Proposition
2.1
-(
3.2
) with the choice
(R=1,\;s=3,\;T=T_{q})
, we get

|L^{2}_{n}|\leq\displaystyle\frac{C}{\varphi(n)}\Big(M_{\beta,1}T_{q}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\Big)\sigma_{3,3}\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{4})},

which implies that there is an integer
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|L_{n}^{2}|<\varepsilon/4,\;\;\forall n\geq n_{2}.

(3.21)

-
Estimate of term
L^{3}_{n}
: Put the following set

Y_{n,R_{0}}=\{(t,x)\in[0,T_{q}]\times B(0,q);\;|u_{\varphi(n)}(t,x)|\leq R_{0}\}.

We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|L^{3}_{n}|&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{Y_{n,R_{0}}}[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}))|u_{\varphi(n)}|]|\Phi|\\
&&\displaystyle+\int_{Y_{n,R_{0}}^{c}}[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}))|u_{\varphi(n)}|]|\Phi|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle[(e^{\beta R_{0}^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta R_{0}^{2}))R_{0}]\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&&\displaystyle+\frac{1}{R_{0}}\int_{Y_{n,R_{0}}^{c}}[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}))|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}]\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle[(e^{\beta R_{0}^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta R_{0}^{2}))R_{0}]\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&&\displaystyle+\frac{1}{R_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}]\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle[(e^{\beta R_{0}^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta R_{0}^{2}))R_{0}]\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\frac{1}{R_{0}}\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})},\end{array}

Using the choices (
3.18
)-(
3.19
), we get

|L_{n}^{3}|<\varepsilon/4,\;\;\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.22)

-
Estimate of term
S
: Put the following subset of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,

Z_{R_{0}}=\{(t,x)\in[0,T_{q}]\times B(0,q);\;|u(t,x)|\leq R_{0}\}.

We have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|S|&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{Z_{R_{0}}}[(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2}))|u|]|\Phi|\\
&&\displaystyle+\int_{Z_{R_{0}}^{c}}[(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2}))|u|]|\Phi|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle(e^{\beta R_{0}^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta R_{0}^{2}))R_{0}\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&&\displaystyle+\frac{1}{R_{0}}\Big(\int_{Z_{R_{0}}^{c}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta|u|^{2}))|u|^{2}\Big)\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle(e^{\beta R_{0}^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta R_{0}^{2}))R_{0}\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&&\displaystyle+\frac{1}{R_{0}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)|u|^{2}\Big)\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\\
&\leq&\displaystyle(e^{\beta R_{0}^{2}}-1-P_{m_{0}}(\beta R_{0}^{2}))R_{0}\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\frac{1}{2\alpha R_{0}}\|u^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}.\end{array}

By the choices (
3.18
)-(
3.19
) we get

|S|<\varepsilon/4.

(3.23)

Combining (
3.20
), (
3.21
), (
3.22
) and (
3.23
), we get

\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J_{\varphi(n)}[(e^{\beta|u_{\varphi(n)}|^{2}}-1)u_{\varphi(n)}]\Phi-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(e^{\beta|u|^{2}}-1)u\Phi\Big|<\varepsilon,\;\forall n\geq\max(n_{1},n_{2}),

which implies (
3.17
) and the proof of Theorem
1.3
is finished.

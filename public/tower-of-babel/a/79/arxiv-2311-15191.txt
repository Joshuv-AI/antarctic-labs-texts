Binomial ideals in quantum tori and quantum affine spaces Thanks: This research was supported by US National Science Foundation grant DMS-1601184.
K. R. Goodearl

Abstract
. The article targets binomial ideals in quantum tori and quantum affine spaces. First, noncommutative analogs of known results for commutative (Laurent) polynomial rings are obtained, including the following: Under the assumption of an algebraically closed base field, it is proved that primitive ideals are binomial, as are radicals of binomial ideals and prime ideals minimal over binomial ideals. In the case of a quantum torus 𝒯 𝐪 {\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}} , the results are strongest: In this situation, the binomial ideals are parametrized by characters on sublattices of the free abelian group whose group algebra is the center of 𝒯 𝐪 {\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}} ; the sublattice-character pairs corresponding to primitive ideals as well as to radicals and minimal primes of binomial ideals are determined. As for occurrences of binomial ideals in quantum algebras: It is shown that cocycle-twisted group algebras of finitely generated abelian groups are quotients of quantum tori modulo binomial ideals. Another appearance is as follows: Cocycle-twisted semigroup algebras of finitely generated commutative monoids, as well as quantum affine toric varieties, are quotients of quantum affine spaces modulo certain types of binomial ideals.

1.
Introduction

We undertake an intensive study of binomial ideals in quantum tori and quantum affine spaces; here
binomials
are nonzero linear combinations of at most two monomials in the canonical generators (i.e., noncommutative polynomials with at most two terms), and
binomial ideals
are ideals generated by binomials. Many of the results are parallel to those known for commutative (Laurent) polynomial rings, even when not obtained by analogous methods. We start by reviewing the commutative setting.

Eisenbud and Sturmfels began their classic paper
[
4
]
by saying “It is notoriously difficult to deduce anything about the structure of an ideal or scheme by directly examining its defining polynomials. A notable exception is that of monomial ideals.” They went on to show that binomial ideals are equally exceptional. For instance, if
T={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
is a Laurent polynomial ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, the proper binomial ideals of
T
are parametrized by pairs
(L,\rho)
called
partial characters of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, meaning that
L
is a sublattice of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
\rho
a group homomorphism from
L
to
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. We denote the corresponding binomial ideal of
T
by

(1.1)

I(L,\rho):=\langle x^{\alpha}-\rho(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in L\rangle,

where
x^{\alpha}
is the monomial with multi-exponent
\alpha
. If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, the prime ideals minimal over
I(L,\rho)
are all binomial, of the form
I(L^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}_{i})
where
L^{\prime}/L
is the torsion subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/L
and the
\rho^{\prime}_{i}
are the extensions of
\rho
to
L^{\prime}
. These minimal primes all have height equal to the rank of
L
. Moreover, the radical of
I(L,\rho)
is binomial. If
\charr{\mathbb{K}}=0
, then
I(L,\rho)
is its own radical; when
\charr{\mathbb{K}}=p>0
, on the other hand,
\sqrt{I(L,\rho)}=I(L^{\prime\prime},\rho^{\prime\prime})
where
L^{\prime\prime}/L
is the
p
-torsion subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/L
and
\rho^{\prime\prime}
is the unique extension of
\rho
to
L^{\prime\prime}
.

For the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, there is no corresponding parametrization of all the binomial ideals. However, if
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, it is still true that minimal primes and radicals of binomial ideals are binomial.

In this article, we develop an analogous binomial ideal theory in quantum tori
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and quantum affine spaces
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
over
{\mathbb{K}}
, leading to noncommutative versions of the Eisenbud-Sturmfels results above. Some of our results are carried over easily from those of
[
4
]
or are based on noncommutative analogs of lines in
[
4
]
; others are obtained via quite different methods.

The reason why many results concerning Laurent polynomial rings
T={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
readily carry over to
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is due to the following known facts: The ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
correspond bijectively to those of its center via contraction and extension, and the center
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
is a Laurent polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{K}}
, i.e., the group algebra
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
of a free abelian group
\Gamma_{Z}
of finite rank. As we show, this correspondence restricts to bijections between the binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and the binomial ideals of
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
. In particular, the restricted correspondence allows us to parametrize the binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
by pairs
(L,\rho)
where
L
is a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
. The definition of the corresponding binomial ideals
I(L,\rho)
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
involves additional scalar terms compared with (
1.1
), however, due to the ambient noncommutativity of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and the choice of polynomial variables in
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
.

We prove that

•

If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, all primitive ideals of quantum tori and quantum affine spaces over
{\mathbb{K}}
are binomial ideals.
[Theorems
3.9
and
6.1
]

•

If
{\mathbb{K}}
is perfect, the radical of any binomial ideal in a quantum torus or quantum affine space over
{\mathbb{K}}
is in turn binomial.
[Theorems
3.12
and
6.11
]

•

If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, the prime ideals minimal over any binomial ideal in a quantum torus or quantum affine space over
{\mathbb{K}}
are themselves binomial.
[Theorems
3.14
and
6.5
]

In the quantum torus cases of these results, we characterize the partial characters that parametrize the primitive ideals and those that tag the radicals and minimal primes of binomial ideals.

Finally, we advertize several situations in which binomial ideals appear:

•

The cocycle-twisted group algebras over
{\mathbb{K}}
of finitely generated abelian groups are precisely the quotients of quantum tori modulo proper binomial ideals.
[Theorem
3.6
]

•

The cocycle-twisted semigroup algebras over
{\mathbb{K}}
of finitely generated commutative monoids are precisely the quotients of quantum affine spaces modulo binomial ideals that contain no monomials.
[Corollary
4.7
]

•

Every quantum affine toric variety over
{\mathbb{K}}
is a quotient of a quantum affine space modulo a completely prime binomial ideal. The converse also holds if
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed.
[Theorem
4.9
]

Some notation and conventions

Fix an arbitrary base field
{\mathbb{K}}
.

For any ring

, we write
\Spec R
and

for the respective sets of prime and primitive ideals of
R
, equipped with Zariski topologies. We write
\langle X\rangle\vartriangleleft R
to denote that
\langle X\rangle
is the ideal of
R
generated by a set

. An element

is said to be

aR=Ra
, and it is

)

2.
Quantum affine spaces and quantum tori

We fix notation for a quantum affine space over
{\mathbb{K}}
and the corresponding quantum torus, then recall and extend well-known relations between the ideals of a quantum torus and its center.

2.1.

Let
{\bf{q}}=(q_{ij})
be a multiplicatively skew-symmetric matrix in
M_{n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, meaning that
q_{ii}=1
and
q_{ji}=q_{ij}^{-1}
for all
i,j\in[1,n]
. The corresponding (
multiparameter
)
quantum affine space
and
quantum torus
are the algebras

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}_{\bf{q}}({\mathbb{K}}^{n}):={\mathbb{K}}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\mid x_{i}x_{j}=q_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}\ \forall\;i,j\in[1,n]\rangle

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}_{\bf{q}}(({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{n}):={\mathbb{K}}\langle x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}\mid x_{i}x_{j}=q_{ij}x_{j}x_{i}\ \forall\;i,j\in[1,n]\rangle.

As is well known,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
are noetherian domains (e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem I.2.7, Corollary I.2.8]
).

2.2.

Set
\Gamma:={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
\Gamma^{+}:={\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. We adopt standard multi-exponent notation for monomials in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. Namely, for any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\Gamma
, we set

x^{\alpha}:=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

The families
(x^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma^{+})
and
(x^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma)
are
{\mathbb{K}}
-bases for
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, respectively.
These algebras are also graded by
\Gamma
, with
({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})_{\alpha}={\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Gamma
and
({\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}})_{\alpha}={\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
, while
({\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}})_{\alpha}=0
for
\alpha\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{+}
.

2.3.

Define
d:\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
by

(2.1)

d(\alpha,\beta):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{i}q_{ij}^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}}=\prod_{i=2}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}q_{ij}^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}},

and note that
d
is a homomorphism in each variable. This function is chosen so that

(2.2)

x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}=d(\alpha,\beta)x^{\alpha+\beta}\qquad\forall\;\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma.

Moreover,
d
is a
2
-cocycle on
\Gamma
, and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, written in terms of the basis
(x^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Gamma)
, coincides with the cocycle-twisted group algebra
{\mathbb{K}}^{d}\Gamma
.

It follows from (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

\bigl(x^{\alpha}\bigr)^{-1}=d(\alpha,\alpha)x^{-\alpha}\qquad\forall\;\alpha\in\Gamma.

2.4.

Set
\Gamma_{Z}:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma\mid x^{\alpha}\in Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})\}
, which is a sublattice (= subgroup) of
\Gamma
. Moreover,
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
is a homogeneous subalgebra of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
(with respect to the
\Gamma
-grading), and so

(2.4)

Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{Z}}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}.

Recall that a
transversal
for a subgroup
H
in an abelian group
G
is a complete, irredundant set of representatives for the cosets of
H
in
G
.
A basic observation is the following: If
S
is a transversal for
\Gamma_{Z}
in
\Gamma
, then
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is a free right or left
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
-module with basis
(x^{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in S)
(e.g.,
[
9
, Proposition 2.3.3]
).

Clearly
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is graded-simple, and so its center is a Laurent polynomial ring of the form

Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})={\mathbb{K}}[(x^{\beta_{1}})^{\pm 1},\dots,(x^{\beta_{r}})^{\pm 1}],

where
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r})
is a basis for
\Gamma_{Z}
(e.g.,
[
2
, Lemma II.3.7]
), allowing
r=0
in case
\Gamma_{Z}=0
. Obviously
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
is isomorphic to the group algebra of
\Gamma_{Z}
, but a particular isomorphism will be needed. Fix the basis
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r})
for
\Gamma_{Z}
, write
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
as a Laurent polynomial ring of the form

{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}={\mathbb{K}}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,z_{r}^{\pm 1}],

and then set

z_{\gamma}:=z_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{m_{r}}\qquad\forall\;\gamma=m_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}\beta_{r}\in\Gamma_{Z}\,,

so that
(z_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma_{Z})
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
and
z_{\gamma}z_{\delta}=z_{\gamma+\delta}
for
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma_{Z}
.

There is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra isomorphism

(2.5)

\phi:{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})\quad\text{such that}\quad\phi(z_{i})=x^{\beta_{i}}\ \ \forall\,i\in[1,r].

Observe that there are scalars
c(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that

(2.6)

\phi(z_{\gamma})=c(\gamma)x^{\gamma}\qquad\forall\;\gamma\in\Gamma_{Z}\,.

It follows from (
2.2
) that

(2.7)

d(\alpha,\beta)=c(\alpha+\beta)c(\alpha)^{-1}c(\beta)^{-1}\qquad\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma_{Z}\,.

2.5.

The ideals of
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
and
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
match up with those of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
as follows. Note that if we also use the name
\phi
for the map with codomain enlarged to
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, then the set map
J\mapsto\phi^{-1}(J)
provides a bijection from the set of ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
onto the set of ideals of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
and restricts to a bijection of

onto

Proposition 2.1
.

There are inverse inclusion-preserving bijections

(2.8)

\begin{array}[]{r c l r c l}\{\,\text{ideals of}\ {\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}\,\}&\longleftrightarrow&\lx@intercol\hfil\{\,\text{ideals of}\ Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})\,\}\hfil\lx@intercol&\longleftrightarrow&\{\,\text{ideals of}\ {\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\,\}\\
I_{0}&\longmapsto&\phi(I_{0})&I&\longmapsto&I\cdot{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\\
\phi^{-1}(J_{0})&\longleftarrow\!\shortmid&J_{0}&J\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})&\longleftarrow\!\shortmid&J.\end{array}

These bijections restrict to bijections

(2.9)

\Spec{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}\longleftrightarrow\Spec Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})\longleftrightarrow\Spec{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

Proof.

The left hand bijections in (
2.8
) are immediate from (
2.5
), while the right hand ones are given by
[
2
, Proposition II.3.8]
, for instance. The bijections (
2.9

[
2

.
∎

The

of a prime ideal

in a ring

P_{0}=P\supsetneqq P_{1}\supsetneqq\cdots\supsetneqq P_{r}

of an arbitrary ideal

Corollary 2.2
.

Let

be an ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(a)
The bijections (

•

\{\,\text{prime ideals of}\;{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}\;\text{minimal over}\;\phi^{-1}(I)\,\}

,

\{\,\text{prime ideals of}\;{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\;\text{minimal over}\;I\,\}
.

(b)

\hgt I=\hgt(I\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}))=\hgt\phi^{-1}(I)
.

(c)
If

, then

in

.

Proof.

(a) and (b) follow directly from Proposition

.

(c) Set

and

, where

We recall the concept of associated primes for modules
M

-module

for all nonzero submodules

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M_{0}
be a left
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
-module and
M
the left
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
-module
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\otimes_{Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})}M_{0}
. Let
P\in\Spec{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and
P_{0}:=P\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})\in\Spec Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
. The following are equivalent:

(a)

P\in\ass(M)
.

(b)

P_{0}\in\ass(M_{0})
.

(c)

P=\ann_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}a)
for some

.

Proof.

Let
T
be a transversal for
\Gamma_{Z}
in
\Gamma
. As noted in §
2.4
,
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is a free right
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
-module with basis
(x^{\tau}\mid\tau\in T)
. Consequently,
M=\bigoplus_{\tau\in T}x^{\tau}\otimes M_{0}
as abelian groups.

(a)
\Longrightarrow
(c): There is a submodule
M^{\prime}\leq M
which is a fully faithful
({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/P)
-module. Choose
0\neq a\in M^{\prime}
; then
P=\ann_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}a)
.

(c)
\Longrightarrow
(b): Write
a=\sum_{\tau\in T^{\prime}}x^{\tau}\otimes a_{\tau}
for some finite subset
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
and some
a_{\tau}\in M_{0}
. For any
z\in Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
, we have
z\in P
iff
za=0
iff
\sum_{\tau\in T^{\prime}}x^{\tau}\otimes za_{\tau}=0

for all

, due to the centrality of

P_{0}=\bigcap_{\tau\in T^{\prime}}\ann_{Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})}(a_{\tau}).

Since
P_{0}
is prime,
P_{0}=\ann_{Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})}(a_{\sigma})
for some
\sigma\in T^{\prime}
, and (b) follows.

(b)
\Longrightarrow
(a): There exists
b\in M_{0}
such that
P_{0}=\ann_{Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})}(b)
. Let
b^{\prime}:=1\otimes b\in M
and
P^{\prime}:=\ann_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}b^{\prime})
. As above,
P^{\prime}\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})=P_{0}
, and consequently
P^{\prime}=P
.

We will be done if
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}b^{\prime}
is a fully faithful
({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/P)
-module, i.e., if

. There is a nonzero submodule

such that

, so that
\ann_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}cb^{\prime})=Q

Now

since

P=Q=\ann_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}}(M^{\prime\prime})\supseteq\ann_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}}(M^{\prime})\supseteq\ann_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}}({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}b^{\prime})=P^{\prime}=P,

∎

3.
Binomial ideals in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}

We first show that binomial ideals of the quantum torus
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
correspond to binomial ideals of its center, and that quotients modulo these binomial ideals are twisted group algebras. The connection with binomial ideals of the center allows the classical results of Eisenbud and Sturmfels
[
4
]
on radicals, minimal and associated primes of binomial ideals in Laurent polynomial rings to be carried over to
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. Some of these results also follow from twisted group algebra structures on quotient algebras.

3.1.

Following
[
4
, §2]
, we define a (
Laurent
)
binomial
in
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
to be any nonzero element of the form
\lambda z_{\alpha}+\mu z_{\beta}
where
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma
, and we define a (
Laurent
)
binomial ideal
in
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
to be any ideal generated by binomials. By
[
4
, Theorem 2.1(a)]
, the binomial ideals of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
can be described in terms of
partial characters
of
\Gamma_{Z}
, meaning homomorphisms from sublattices of
\Gamma_{Z}
to
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Allowing empty sets of generators, the zero ideal is a binomial ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
. Moreover,
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}=\langle z_{0}\rangle
is a binomial ideal.

Carrying this terminology over to quantum tori, let us say that a (
Laurent
)
binomial
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is any nonzero element of the form
\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta}
where
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma
, and that a (
Laurent
)
binomial ideal
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is any ideal generated by binomials. Note that
0
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
are binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The bijections (
2.8
) restrict to bijections

\{\,\text{binomial ideals of}\ {\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}\,\}\longleftrightarrow\{\,\text{binomial ideals of}\ {\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\,\}.

Proof.

Since
\phi
maps binomials
\lambda z_{\alpha}+\mu z_{\beta}
in
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
to binomials
\lambda c(\alpha)x^{\alpha}+\mu c(\beta)x^{\beta}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, it is clear that if
I
is a binomial ideal in
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
, then
\phi(I)\cdot{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Now let
J
be an arbitrary binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and
I:=\phi^{-1}(J)
. We must show that
I
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
. This is clear if
J={\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, since then
I={\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
. Hence, assume that
J
is a proper ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Let
\{\lambda_{s}x^{\alpha_{s}}+\mu_{s}x^{\beta_{s}}\mid s\in S\}

be a set of binomial generators for
J
, for some
\lambda_{s},\mu_{s}\in{\mathbb{K}}
, not both zero, and some
\alpha_{s},\beta_{s}\in\Gamma
. If one of
\lambda_{s}
or
\mu_{s}
is zero, then
\lambda_{s}x^{\alpha_{s}}+\mu_{s}x^{\beta_{s}}
is a unit in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, which would force
I={\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. Thus,
\lambda_{s},\mu_{s}\neq 0
for
s\in S
. Similarly,
\alpha_{s}\neq\beta_{s}
for
s\in S
.

For
s\in S
, we have

\displaystyle x^{\alpha_{s}}(x^{\beta_{s}})^{-1}

\displaystyle=x^{\alpha_{s}}d(\beta_{s},\beta_{s})x^{-\beta_{s}}=d(\beta_{s},\beta_{s})d(\alpha_{s},-\beta_{s})x^{\alpha_{s}-\beta_{s}}=d(\beta_{s}-\alpha_{s},\beta_{s})x^{\alpha_{s}-\beta_{s}}

\displaystyle\lambda_{s}x^{\alpha_{s}}+\mu_{s}x^{\beta_{s}}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\lambda_{s}x^{\alpha_{s}}(x^{\beta_{s}})^{-1}+\mu_{s}\bigr)x^{\beta_{s}}=\lambda_{s}d(\beta_{s}-\alpha_{s},\beta_{s})\bigl(x^{\alpha_{s}-\beta_{s}}-\nu_{s}\bigr)x^{\beta_{s}}

where
\nu_{s}:=-\lambda_{s}^{-1}d(\alpha_{s}-\beta_{s},\beta_{s})\mu_{s}
,
and so the generator
\lambda_{s}x^{\alpha_{s}}+\mu_{s}x^{\beta_{s}}
may be replaced by
x^{\alpha_{s}-\beta_{s}}-\nu_{s}
. Consequently
J
has a set of generators of the form
\{x^{\gamma_{s}}-\nu_{s}\mid s\in S\}
, for some
\gamma_{s}\in\Gamma
and
\nu_{s}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
.

For
s\in S
and
i\in[1,n]
, there is some
p_{si}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that
x^{\gamma_{s}}x_{i}=p_{si}x_{i}x^{\gamma_{s}}
, and so
J
contains the element

(x^{\gamma_{s}}-\nu_{s})x_{i}-p_{si}x_{i}(x^{\gamma_{s}}-\nu_{s})=(p_{si}-1)\nu_{s}x_{i}\,.

Since
J
is proper, all the
p_{si}=1
, whence
x^{\gamma_{s}}\in Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
. Thus
\gamma_{s}\in\Gamma_{Z}
for all
s\in S
.

Let
I^{\prime}
be the ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
generated by
\{z_{\gamma_{s}}-c(\gamma_{s})\nu_{s}\mid s\in S\}
. Since
\phi(z_{\gamma_{s}}-c(\gamma_{s})\nu_{s})=c(\gamma_{s})(x^{\gamma_{s}}-\nu_{s})
, we see that
\phi(I^{\prime})
is the ideal of
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
generated by
\{x^{\gamma_{s}}-\nu_{s}\mid s\in S\}
. Consequently,
\phi(I^{\prime})\cdot{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}=J
, whence
I^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}(J)=I
by Proposition
2.1
.
Therefore
I
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
, as required.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

(a)
Let
L
be a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
. Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle I_{0}(L,\rho)

\displaystyle:=\langle z_{\alpha}-\rho(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in L\rangle\vartriangleleft{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}

\displaystyle I(L,\rho)

\displaystyle:=\langle x^{\alpha}-c(\alpha)^{-1}\rho(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in L\rangle\vartriangleleft{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}

are proper binomial ideals of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, respectively, and
I(L,\rho)=\phi(I_{0}(L,\rho))\cdot{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b)
Let
J
be a proper binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and

L:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma\mid x^{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}+J\}.

Then
L
is a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and there is a unique
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
such that
J=I(L,\rho)
.

Proof.

(a) Observe that the set
\{z_{\alpha}-\rho(\alpha)\mid\alpha\in L\}
generates a maximal ideal
M
of
{\mathbb{K}}L
and
M\cdot{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}=I_{0}(L,\rho)
. Since

(3.2)

\phi(z_{\alpha}-\rho(\alpha))=c(\alpha)(x^{\alpha}-c(\alpha)^{-1}\rho(\alpha))\qquad\forall\,\alpha\in L,

we see that

I(L,\rho)=\phi(M)\cdot{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}=\phi(I_{0}(L,\rho))\cdot{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.
Since
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
is a free left
{\mathbb{K}}L
-module (e.g.,
[
14
, Lemma 1.1.3]
), we have

{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}/I_{0}(L,\rho)\cong({\mathbb{K}}L/M)\otimes_{{\mathbb{K}}L}{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}\neq 0,

and so
I_{0}(L,\rho)
is a proper ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
. It then follows from Proposition
2.1
that
I(L,\rho)
is a proper ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b) For
\alpha\in L
, there exists
\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that
x^{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\in J
, and
\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)
is unique because
J
is proper. For
\alpha,\beta\in L
, we have

x^{\alpha}x^{\beta}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\rho^{\prime}(\beta)=(x^{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha))x^{\beta}+\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)(x^{\beta}-\rho^{\prime}(\beta))\in J,

whence
x^{\alpha+\beta}-d(\alpha,\beta)^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\rho^{\prime}(\beta)\in J
. Consequently,

(3.3)

\alpha+\beta\in L\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho^{\prime}(\alpha+\beta)=d(\alpha,\beta)^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\rho^{\prime}(\beta)\qquad\forall\;\alpha,\beta\in L.

Since we also have
x^{0}-1\in J
and

x^{-\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)^{-1}d(-\alpha,\alpha)=-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)^{-1}x^{-\alpha}(x^{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha))\in J\qquad\forall\;\alpha\in L,

we find that
L
is a sublattice of
\Gamma
. The argument of Lemma
3.1
shows that
L\subseteq\Gamma_{Z}
.

Set
\rho(\alpha):=c(\alpha)\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in L
. In view of (
3.3
) and (
2.7
),

\rho(\alpha+\beta)=c(\alpha+\beta)d(\alpha,\beta)^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\rho^{\prime}(\beta)=c(\alpha)c(\beta)\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\rho^{\prime}(\beta)=\rho(\alpha)\rho(\beta)

for
\alpha,\beta\in L
. Thus,
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

By part (a),
I(L,\rho)
is a proper binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, and
I(L,\rho)\subseteq J
by definition of
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
. The proof of Lemma
3.1
shows that
J
is generated by a set
\{x^{\gamma_{s}}-\nu_{s}\mid s\in S\}
of binomials, for some
\gamma_{s}\in\Gamma
and
\nu_{s}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Each
\gamma_{s}\in L
and
\nu_{s}=\rho^{\prime}(\gamma_{s})
, whence
x^{\gamma_{s}}-\nu_{s}=x^{\gamma_{s}}-c(\gamma_{s})^{-1}\rho(\gamma_{s})\in I(L,\rho)
. Therefore
J=I(L,\rho)
.

Concerning the uniqueness of
\rho
, suppose that
J=I(L,\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
. For
\alpha\in L
, we then have
x^{\alpha}-c(\alpha)^{-1}\sigma(\alpha)\in J
, whence
\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)=c(\alpha)^{-1}\sigma(\alpha)
and
\rho(\alpha)=\sigma(\alpha)
. Therefore
\rho=\sigma
.
∎

The processes described in Theorem
3.2
actually give inverse bijections, as we show in Theorem
3.7
.

Remark 3.3
.

The appearance of the scalars
c(\alpha)
in the definition of
I(L,\rho)
is due to the choice of a splitting of the subgroup
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}x^{L}:=\{\lambda x^{\alpha}\mid\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times},\;\alpha\in L\}
of
U(Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}))
. Namely, there is a surjective group homomorphism
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}x^{L}\rightarrow L
given by
\lambda x^{\alpha}\mapsto\alpha
, which has a right inverse
\xi:L\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}x^{L}
given by
\alpha\mapsto c(\alpha)x^{\alpha}
. It follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.7
) that
\xi
is a homomorphism. Transferring
\rho
to
\xi(L)
via

and using the splitting
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}x^{L}={\mathbb{K}}^{\times}\times\xi(L)
, we obtain a homomorphism
\widetilde{\rho}:{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}x^{L}\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that
\widetilde{\rho}|_{{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}}=\id_{{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}}
and
\widetilde{\rho}(x^{\alpha})=c(\alpha)^{-1}\rho(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in L
.

Thus, the proper binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
may be expressed in the form

\langle u-\widetilde{\rho}(u)\mid u\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}x^{L}\rangle

for sublattices
L
of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\widetilde{\rho}\in\Hom({\mathbb{K}}^{\times}x^{L},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
such that
\widetilde{\rho}|_{{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}}=\id_{{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}}
. This allows these ideals to be expressed independently of the choice of the isomorphism
\phi
in (
2.5
).

Since

is noetherian, each

I_{Z}(L,\rho)=I(L,\rho)\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})

(b)

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
R
be a unital
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra. Then
R
is isomorphic to a twisted group algebra
{\mathbb{K}}^{e}G
, for some finitely generated abelian group
G
and some
2
-cocycle
e:G\times\penalty G\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
, if and only if
R\cong{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/J
for some multiplicatively skew-symmetric matrix
{\bf{q}}\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, and some proper binomial ideal
J
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Proof.

(\Longleftarrow)
: Theorem
3.2
(b) and Proposition
3.5
(c).

(\Longrightarrow)
: By assumption,
R
has a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis
(y_{g}\mid g\in G)
such that the
y_{g}
are units in
R
and
y_{g}y_{h}=e(g,h)y_{g+h}
for
g,h\in G
. From the case
g=h=0
, it follows that
y_{0}=e(0,0)\cdot 1_{R}
. Let
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\}
be a finite set of generators for
G
, and set
z_{i}:=y_{g_{i}}
and
q_{ij}:=e(g_{i},g_{j})e(g_{j},g_{i})^{-1}
for
i,j\in[1,n]
. Then
{\bf{q}}:=(q_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
is a multiplicatively skew-symmetric matrix and
z_{i}z_{j}=q_{ij}z_{j}z_{i}
for all
i
,
j
. Now there is a unital surjective
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra homomorphism
\theta:{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\rightarrow R
such that
\theta(x_{i})=z_{i}
for all
i
. Note that
\ker\theta
is a proper ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Let
\pi:\Gamma\rightarrow G
be the group homomorphism given by the rule
\pi(\alpha)=g_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots g_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. For each
\alpha\in\Gamma
, we have

\theta(x^{\alpha})=\theta(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}})=y_{g_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{g_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}}=\mu(\alpha)y_{\pi(\alpha)}\qquad\text{for some}\;\mu(\alpha)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}.

In particular, for
\alpha\in\ker\pi
we have
\theta(x^{\alpha})=\mu(\alpha)y_{0}=\mu(\alpha)\mu(0)^{-1}\theta(1)
, and so
\ker\theta
contains the binomial ideal

J:=\langle x^{\alpha}-\mu(\alpha)\mu(0)^{-1}\mid\alpha\in\ker\pi\rangle\vartriangleleft{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

By Theorem
3.2
(b),
J=I(L,\rho)
where
L:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma\mid x^{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}+J\}
is a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
. Note that
\ker\pi\subseteq L
by definition of
J
.

Let
S
be a transversal for
L
in
\Gamma
, and enlarge
S
to a transversal
T
for
\ker\pi
in
\Gamma
. Since
\pi
maps
T
bijectively onto
G
, the map
\theta
sends the family
(x^{\tau}\mid\tau\in T)
to a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
R
, and so
(x^{\tau}\mid\tau\in T)
is
{\mathbb{K}}
-linearly independent module
\ker\theta
, hence also
{\mathbb{K}}
-linearly independent modulo
J
. On the other hand, Proposition
3.5
(c) shows that
(x^{\sigma}\mid\sigma\in S)
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/J
. This forces
T=S
, and so the induced homomorphism
\overline{\theta}:{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/J\rightarrow R
sends a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/J
to a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
R
. Consequently,
\overline{\theta}
is an isomorphism, and therefore
\ker\theta=J
.
∎

We use the results of Proposition
3.5
to complete the bijection initiated in Theorem
3.2
, as follows.

By Theorem
3.2
, there are maps

I:{\mathcal{L}}\longleftrightarrow\{\;\text{proper binomial ideals of}\;{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\;\}:\ell

as described, and
I(\ell(J))=J
for all proper binomial ideals
J
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

It remains to show that
\ell(I(L,\rho))=(L,\rho)
for any
(L,\rho)\in{\mathcal{L}}
.
Set
(L^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}):=\ell(I(L,\rho))
, and observe that
L^{\prime}
is a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
containing
L
. If there exists
\alpha\in L^{\prime}\setminus L
, there is a transversal for
L
in
\Gamma
that contains both
\alpha
and
0
. But Proposition
3.5
(c) would then imply that
x^{\alpha}
and
1
are linearly independent modulo
I(L,\rho)
, contradicting the fact that
x^{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}+I(L,\rho)
. Thus
L^{\prime}=L
. That
\rho^{\prime}=\rho
then follows from the properness of
I(L,\rho)
.
Therefore
\ell(I(L,\rho))=(L,\rho)
, as required.
∎

3.2.

Given an ideal
I
of a general ring
R
, we use the commutative algebra notation
\sqrt{I}
and the terminology
radical of
I
to stand for the prime radical of
I
, that is, the intersection of all prime ideals of
R
that contain
I
. (By convention, the intersection of an empty family of ideals equals
R
, so that
\sqrt{R}=R
.) We also use the corresponding terminology
radical ideal
, in place of “semiprime ideal”, for an ideal which equals its radical.

Proposition 3.8
.

If
\charr{\mathbb{K}}=0
, then all binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
are radical ideals.

Proof.

Let
I
be a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. Since the result is clear if
I={\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, we may assume that
I
is proper. By Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
3.5
,
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I
is a twisted group algebra of a group
\Gamma/L
over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Theorem 3.2 of
[
12
]
shows that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I
is a semiprime ring, and therefore
I
is a radical ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.
∎

Proposition
3.8
typically fails in positive characteristic. For instance, if
n=1
and
\charr{\mathbb{K}}=p>0
, the binomial ideal
\langle x_{1}^{p}-1\rangle=\langle x_{1}-1\rangle^{p}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is not radical.

Theorem 3.9
.

(a)
For any
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, the ideal
I(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is a maximal ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, then every primitive ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is a maximal ideal of the form
I(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
for some
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

Proof.

(a) Observe from part (b) of Proposition
3.5
that
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is
1
-dimensional over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Hence,
I_{Z}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is a maximal ideal of
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
. Since also
I(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})=I_{Z}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
, we conclude from Proposition
2.1
that
I(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is a maximal ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b) Let
P
be an arbitrary primitive ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. It follows from
[
2
, Corollary II.8.5]
that
P
must be a maximal ideal. Specifically, with respect to the standard action of the torus
H=({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{n}
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, the zero ideal is the unique
H
-prime ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
, and so
\Spec{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
consists of a single
H
-stratum. Thus
P
, being maximal in its

-stratum, must be maximal in
\Spec{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Now by Proposition
2.1
,
M:=\phi^{-1}(P)
is a maximal ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
. Since
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed,
M
has codimension
1
in
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}
, and so

for some
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
. Thus

by Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
3.2
(a).
∎

Recall that an ideal
P
of a ring
R
is called
completely prime
if
R/P
is a domain, i.e., a nonzero ring without nonzero zero-divisors.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
L
be a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

(a)
If
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is torsionfree, then
I(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. If, further,
\Gamma/L
is torsionfree, then
I(L,\rho)
is completely prime.

(b)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, then
I(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
if and only if
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is torsionfree.

(c)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed and
\Gamma/\Gamma_{Z}
is torsionfree, then all prime binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
are completely prime.

Proof.

(a) By Proposition
3.5
,
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(L,\rho)
is a twisted group algebra of
\Gamma_{Z}/L
over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Since
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is torsionfree, it is a free abelian group of finite rank, and hence a totally ordered group under a suitable ordering. Consequently,
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(L,\rho)
is a domain (e.g.,
[
13
, Proposition 5.2.6]
), and
I_{Z}(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
. Since
I(L,\rho)\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})=I_{Z}(L,\rho)
, Proposition
2.1
thus implies that
I(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Proposition
3.5
also shows that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I(L,\rho)
is a twisted group algebra of
\Gamma/L
over
{\mathbb{K}}
. In case
\Gamma/L
is torsionfree, it is a free abelian group of finite rank, and consequently
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I(L,\rho)
is a domain. Therefore
I(L,\rho)
is a completely prime ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
in this case.

(b) One direction is given by part (a). Now assume that
I(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. By Propositions
2.1
and
3.5
,
I_{Z}(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
and
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(L,\rho)
is a twisted group algebra of
\Gamma_{Z}/L
over
{\mathbb{K}}
. In particular, this twisted group algebra is a domain. Since
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed,
\Gamma_{Z}/L
must be torsionfree, as follows.

There is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis
(b_{\beta}\mid\beta\in\Gamma_{Z}/L)
for
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(L,\rho)
such that
b_{\beta}b_{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}b_{\beta+\gamma}
for
\beta,\gamma\in\Gamma_{Z}/L)
. Suppose
\Gamma_{Z}/L
contains a nonzero element
\delta
with finite order
n
. Then
b_{\delta}^{n}=\lambda\cdot 1
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Since
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, there exists
\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
with
\mu^{n}=\lambda
, and so

(b_{\delta}-\mu\cdot 1)(b_{\delta}^{n-1}+\mu b_{\delta}^{n-2}+\cdots+\mu^{n-1}\cdot 1)=0.

This contradicts the fact that
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(L,\rho)
is a domain. Therefore
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is torsionfree, as claimed.

(c) If
\Gamma/\Gamma_{Z}
is torsionfree, then
\Gamma/L
is torsionfree if and only if
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is torsionfree. Therefore part (c) follows from (a) and (b), in view of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

(1) Theorem
3.10
(b) does not hold in general when
{\mathbb{K}}
is not algebraically closed. For instance, choose
{\mathbb{K}}
so that
\charr{\mathbb{K}}\neq 2
and
{\mathbb{K}}
contains an element
\alpha
such that
X^{4}-\alpha
is irreducible in
{\mathbb{K}}[X]
. Then consider
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
with
n=2
and
{\bf{q}}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. As is well known,
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1}^{\pm 2},x_{2}^{\pm 2}]
in this case, and so
\Gamma_{Z}=(2{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
. Choose
((2,0),(0,2))
as basis for
\Gamma_{Z}
, define
\phi:{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma_{Z}\rightarrow Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
as in (
2.5
), and observe that
\phi(z_{(4,0)})=x_{1}^{4}
, whence
c(4,0)=1
.

Now set
L:={\mathbb{Z}}(4,0)\subseteq\Gamma_{Z}
, and let
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
such that
\rho(4,0)=\alpha
. In view of Lemma
3.4
, the ideal
I(L,\rho)
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is generated by
x_{1}^{4}-\alpha
. The quotient
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I(L,\rho)
is isomorphic to
K[x_{2}^{\pm 1};\theta]
where
K
is the field
{\mathbb{K}}[X]/(X^{4}-\alpha)
and
\theta
is the
{\mathbb{K}}
-automorphism of
K
sending the coset of
X
to its negative. Thus,
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I(L,\rho)
is a domain and
I(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. However, the quotient
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is not torsionfree.

(2) The hypothesis of torsionfreeness of
\Gamma/\Gamma_{Z}
in Theorem
3.10
(c) holds in particular in case the subgroup
\langle q_{ij}\rangle
of
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
is torsionfree. To see this, suppose
\alpha\in\Gamma
and
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
with
t\alpha\in\Gamma_{Z}
. Then
x^{t\alpha}\in Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
, and the relations
x_{i}x^{t\alpha}=x^{t\alpha}x_{i}
imply that
\prod_{j=1}^{n}q_{ij}^{t\alpha_{j}}=1
for
i\in[1,n]
. If
\langle q_{ij}\rangle
is torsionfree, it follows that
\prod_{j=1}^{n}q_{ij}^{\alpha_{j}}=1
and
x_{i}x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}x_{i}
for
i\in[1,n]
, whence
x^{\alpha}\in Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
and
\alpha\in\Gamma_{Z}
.

Thus, if
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed and
\langle q_{ij}\rangle
is torsionfree, all prime binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
are completely prime. This, however, is covered by a more general theorem: Assuming only that
\langle q_{ij}\rangle
is torsionfree, all prime ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are completely prime by
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
, whence all prime ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
are completely prime.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
L
be a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

(a)
If
\charr{\mathbb{K}}=0
, then
\sqrt{I(L,\rho)}=I(L,\rho)
.

(b)
Assume that
{\mathbb{K}}
is perfect of characteristic
p>0
. Let
L^{\prime}_{p}/L
be the
p
-torsion subgroup of
\Gamma_{Z}/L
, that is,

L^{\prime}_{p}:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma_{Z}\mid p^{l}\alpha\in L\ \text{for some}\ l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\}.

There is a unique map
\rho^{\prime}
in
\Hom(L^{\prime}_{p},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
extending
\rho
, and
\sqrt{I(L,\rho)}=I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})
.

Proof.

(a) Proposition
3.8
.

(b) The existence and uniqueness of
\rho^{\prime}
follow from the fact that for any
l\geq 0
, every element of
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
has a unique
p^{l}
-th root in
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Note that
I(L,\rho)\subseteq I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})
.

Given any
\alpha\in L^{\prime}_{p}
, there is some
l\geq 0
such that
p^{l}\alpha\in L
, whence

(z_{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha))^{p^{l}}=z_{p^{l}\alpha}-\rho(p^{l}\alpha)\in I_{0}(L,\rho).

Consequently,

(c(\alpha)x^{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha))^{p^{l}}=\phi\bigl((z_{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha))^{p^{l}}\bigr)\in\phi(I_{0}(L,\rho))\subseteq I(L,\rho),

and so
c(\alpha)x^{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)\in\sqrt{I(L,\rho)}
, due to
c(\alpha)x^{\alpha}-\rho^{\prime}(\alpha)
being central. Thus,
I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})\subseteq\sqrt{I(L,\rho)}
.

It remains to show that
I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})
is a radical ideal. By construction,
\Gamma_{Z}/L^{\prime}_{p}
has no
p
-torsion, so the order of any finite subgroup of
\Gamma_{Z}/L^{\prime}_{p}
is nonzero in
{\mathbb{K}}
. Since
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})
is a twisted group algebra of
\Gamma_{Z}/L^{\prime}_{p}
over
{\mathbb{K}}
by Proposition
3.5
(b),
[
15
, Theorem I]
shows that
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})/I_{Z}(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})
is a semiprime ring. Thus
I_{Z}(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})
is a radical ideal of
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
.

Since
\sqrt{I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})}
is the intersection of all prime ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
containing
I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})
, Proposition
2.1
implies that

\sqrt{I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})}\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})=\sqrt{I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})}=\sqrt{I_{Z}(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})}=I_{Z}(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})=I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})\cap Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}),

and therefore
\sqrt{I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})}=I(L^{\prime}_{p},\rho^{\prime})

∎

Example 3.13
.

Perfectness of
{\mathbb{K}}
is needed in Theorem
3.12
to obtain the map
\rho^{\prime}
. If
{\mathbb{K}}
is not perfect,
\sqrt{I(L,\rho)}
need not be a binomial ideal, as a slight modification of
[
1
, Lemma 29]
shows. Namely, let
p=\charr{\mathbb{K}}>0
, take
n=2
and
{\bf{q}}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, and choose

. The polynomial
x_{1}^{p}-a\in{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1}]
is irreducible (e.g.,
[

), and so

Take

where
L:=(p{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
while
\rho
sends
(p,0)\mapsto a
and
(0,p)\mapsto a+1
(recall Lemma
3.4
). Since

(x_{1}-x_{2}+1)^{p}=(x_{1}^{p}-a)-(x_{2}^{p}-a-1)\in I

\sqrt{I}=\langle x_{1}^{p}-a,\,x_{1}-x_{2}+1\rangle

and
\dim{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/\sqrt{I}=p
.

If
\sqrt{I}=I(L^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
for some sublattice
L^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
and
\rho^{\prime}\in\Hom(L^{\prime},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, then Proposition
3.5
shows that
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/\sqrt{I}
is a twisted group algebra of
\Gamma/L^{\prime}
over
{\mathbb{K}}
, with a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis of the form

is a transversal for

in
\Gamma
. Consequently,
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/\sqrt{I}
is graded by

.

, so

and
\overline{1}

is a prime (actually, maximal) ideal of

Theorem 3.14
.

Assume that
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed. Let

]
. Following that paper but replacing

4.
Binomial ideals in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and graded quotients

We initiate the study of binomials ideals in the quantum affine space
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
by developing gradings on quotients by such ideals. Quotients of quantum affine spaces by binomial ideals are characterized in terms of the existence of suitable gradings. We also characterize twisted semigroup algebras of finitely generated commutative monoids and quantum affine toric varieties as quotients of quantum affine spaces modulo suitable binomial ideals.

4.1.

A
binomial in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is any binomial of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
which lies in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, i.e., any nonzero element
\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta}
where
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma^{+}
, and a
binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is any ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
generated by binomials in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. Similarly, a
monomial in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is any nonzero element
\lambda x^{\alpha}
where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
and
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
, and a
monomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is any ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
generated by monomials in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. Also, extending the usage in
[
4
]
, we define a (not necessarily commutative)
binomial algebra
to be any algebra isomorphic to a quotient of a quantum affine space modulo a binomial ideal.

Remark 4.1
.

Although the algebra
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is best studied directly, one should note that it is also a binomial algebra. Namely, given
{\bf{q}}
, define
\widehat{{\bf{q}}}=(\widehat{q}_{ij})\in M_{2n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
so that

\displaystyle\widehat{q}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=q_{ij}

\displaystyle(i,j\leq n)

\displaystyle\qquad\widehat{q}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=q_{i-n,j-n}

\displaystyle(i,j>n)

\displaystyle\widehat{q}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=q_{j-n,i}

\displaystyle(i\leq n<j)

\displaystyle\widehat{q}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=q_{j,i-n}

\displaystyle(i>n\geq j).

Then
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{{\bf{q}}}}({\mathbb{K}}^{2n})/\langle x_{i}x_{i+n}-1\mid i\in[1,n]\rangle
. Consequently, binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
correspond to quotients
I/\langle x_{i}x_{i+n}-1\mid i\in[1,n]\rangle
for certain binomial ideals
I
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\widehat{{\bf{q}}}}({\mathbb{K}}^{2n})
.

Partial localizations of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
obtained by inverting some of the
x_{i}
may be described similarly. For some other binomial algebras, see Theorems
4.6
,
4.9
and Remark
4.10
.

4.2.

Let
\leq
denote the lexicographic order on
\Gamma
, so that
(\Gamma,\leq)
is a totally ordered abelian group. Define
degrees
,
leading terms
, and
leading coefficients
for nonzero elements of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
with respect to this ordering on exponents. Observe that
(\Gamma^{+},\leq)
is a well-ordered set with least element
0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
B
be a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, and set

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}\mid x^{\alpha}\in B\}

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}\setminus A_{1}\mid x^{\alpha}-\mu x^{\alpha^{\prime}}\in B\ \text{for some}\ \mu\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times},\;\alpha^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{+},\;\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha\}.

For
\alpha\in A_{2}
, let
p(\alpha)
be the least element of
\Gamma^{+}
such that
p(\alpha)<\alpha
and
x^{\alpha}-\nu_{\alpha}x^{p(\alpha)}\in B
for some
\nu_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Then
\nu_{\alpha}
is uniquely determined by
\alpha
.

Set
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}:=(x^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in A_{1})
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}:=(x^{\alpha}-\nu_{\alpha}x^{p(\alpha)}\mid\alpha\in A_{2})
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
B
.

Proof.

If
B=0
, then
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
are empty and the result is clear. Hence, we may assume that
B\neq 0
. For
\alpha\in A_{2}
, note that since
x^{\alpha}-\nu_{\alpha}x^{p(\alpha)}\in B
while
x^{\alpha}\notin B
, we must have
x^{p(\alpha)}\notin B
and
p(\alpha)\notin A_{1}
. It follows that
\nu_{\alpha}
is uniquely determined by
\alpha
.

If
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
fails to be linearly independent, there is a nontrivial relation

(4.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}\bigl(x^{\gamma_{j}}-\nu_{\gamma_{j}}x^{p(\gamma_{j})}\bigr)=0

for some distinct
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in A_{1}
, some distinct
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{r}\in A_{2}
, and some
\lambda_{i},\mu_{j}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
, where
m+r>0
. Since
\gamma_{j},p(\gamma_{j})\notin A_{1}
for all
j
, there cannot be any
\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}
terms in (
4.1
), i.e.,
m=0
and

(4.2)

\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{j}\bigl(x^{\gamma_{j}}-\nu_{\gamma_{j}}x^{p(\gamma_{j})}\bigr)=0.

Without loss of generality,
\gamma_{1}<\cdots<\gamma_{r}
, so also
p(\gamma_{j})<\gamma_{j}\leq\gamma_{r}
for all
j
. But then the left hand side of (
4.2
) is nonzero with leading term
\mu_{r}x^{\gamma_{r}}
, which is impossible. Therefore
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
is
{\mathbb{K}}
-linearly independent.

By hypothesis,
B
is generated by certain binomials
\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}+\mu_{i}x^{\beta_{i}}
, whence elements of
B
are linear combinations of products of the form
x^{\gamma}(\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{i}}+\mu_{i}x^{\beta_{i}})x^{\delta}
with
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma^{+}
. Any such product is itself a binomial, and so
B
is spanned by binomials. Thus, to prove that
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
spans
B
, it suffices to show that every binomial
\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta}
in
B
is in the
{\mathbb{K}}
-span of
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
.

If
\mu=0
, then
\lambda\neq 0
and
x^{\alpha}\in B
, whence
x^{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and
\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta}\in\Span({\mathcal{B}}_{1})
. The same conclusion holds if
\lambda=0
, or if
\alpha=\beta
. Consequently, we may assume that
\lambda,\mu\neq 0
and
\alpha\neq\beta
. After switching terms if necessary, we may also assume that
\alpha>\beta
. We now proceed by induction on
\alpha
.

Since
x^{\alpha}+\lambda^{-1}\mu x^{\beta}\in B
, we must have
\alpha\in A_{2}
. Hence, the first step of the induction is the case when
\alpha=\min(A_{2})
. Now
B
contains the element

(4.3)

(\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta})-\lambda(x^{\alpha}-\nu_{\alpha}x^{p(\alpha)})=\mu x^{\beta}+\lambda\nu_{\alpha}x^{p(\alpha)}.

If
p(\alpha)\neq\beta
, we find that
\max(p(\alpha),\beta)\in A_{2}
, which is impossible since
p(\alpha)
and
\beta
are both
<\alpha
. Hence,
p(\alpha)=\beta
and the right hand side of (
4.3
) is zero or a monomial. It follows that this right hand term lies in
\Span({\mathcal{B}}_{1})
, whence
\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta}\in\Span({\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
.

Finally, suppose that
\alpha>\min(A_{2})
. As above,
B
contains the element (
4.3
), and if
p(\alpha)=\beta
, then
\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta}\in\Span({\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
. If
p(\alpha)\neq\beta
, then either
p(\alpha)<\beta<\alpha
or
\beta<p(\alpha)<\alpha
. In either case, our induction hypothesis implies that
\mu x^{\beta}+\lambda\nu_{\alpha}x^{p(\alpha)}\in\Span({\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
, whence also
\lambda x^{\alpha}+\mu x^{\beta}\in\Span({\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
. This completes the induction and concludes the proof that
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
spans
B
.
∎

Analogous to the case of twisted group algebras, a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra
R
is a twisted semigroup algebra of an additive monoid
S
over
{\mathbb{K}}
, denoted
{\mathbb{K}}^{e}S
, if and only if
R
has a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis
(z_{s}\mid s\in S)
such that
z_{s}z_{t}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}z_{s+t}
for all
s,t\in S
. (One has only to check that the map
e:S\times S\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that
z_{s}z_{t}=e(s,t)z_{s+t}
is a
2
-cocycle on
S
.)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
B
be a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, and set
y^{\alpha}:=x^{\alpha}+B\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B
for
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
. There is a monoid congruence
\sim
on
\Gamma^{+}
defined by the rule
\alpha\sim\beta\iff{\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\beta}
. Let
S:=\Gamma^{+}/{\sim}
, and for
s\in S
set
R_{s}:={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}
where
\alpha
is some
(
any
)
element of
s
. Then

{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B=\bigoplus_{s\in S}R_{s}

is a grading of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B
by the monoid
S
.

If
B
contains no monomials, i.e.,
x^{\alpha}\notin B
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B
is isomorphic to a twisted semigroup algebra
{\mathbb{K}}^{e}S
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\sim
is a monoid congruence on
\Gamma^{+}
. Set
R:={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B
, and observe that

(4.4)

R=\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}}{\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}=\sum_{s\in S}R_{s}\,.

Given any
s,t\in S
, choose
\alpha\in s
and
\beta\in t
, and note that

(4.5)

R_{s}R_{t}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}y^{\beta}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha+\beta}=R_{s+t}\,.

Thus, all that remains is to prove that the right hand sum in (
4.4
) is direct.

We will use the notation of Lemma
4.2
and its proof. Note that for
\alpha\in A_{2}
, we have
y^{\alpha}=\nu_{\alpha}y^{p(\alpha)}
, whence
\alpha\sim p(\alpha)
. For
s\in S
, set
\alpha_{s}:=\min(s)
. Then
R_{s}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha_{s}}
, and
R_{s}=0
if and only if
\alpha_{s}\in A_{1}
. To prove that the sum
\sum_{s\in S}R_{s}
is direct, it thus suffices to show that the
y^{\alpha_{s}}
, for
s\in S
such that
\alpha_{s}\notin A_{1}
, are
{\mathbb{K}}
-linearly independent.

Suppose there is a nontrivial relation
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}y^{\alpha_{s(i)}}=0
for some
\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
and some distinct
s(i)\in S
with
\alpha_{s(i)}\notin A_{1}
. We may assume that
\alpha_{s(1)}<\cdots<\alpha_{s(m)}
. In view of Lemma
4.2
,

(4.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{s(i)}}=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\mu_{j}x^{\beta_{j}}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mu^{\prime}_{k}\bigl(x^{\gamma_{k}}-\nu_{\gamma_{k}}x^{p(\gamma_{k})}\bigr)

for some
\mu_{j},\mu^{\prime}_{k}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
, some distinct
\beta_{j}\in A_{1}
, and some distinct
\gamma_{k}\in A_{2}
. Since none of the
\alpha_{s(i)}
,
\gamma_{k}
, or
p(\gamma_{k})
are in
A_{1}
, there cannot be any
\mu_{j}x^{\beta_{j}}
terms in (
4.6
), so this equation reduces to

(4.7)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x^{\alpha_{s(i)}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mu^{\prime}_{k}\bigl(x^{\gamma_{k}}-\nu_{\gamma_{k}}x^{p(\gamma_{k})}\bigr).

We may assume that
\gamma_{1}<\cdots<\gamma_{r}
. Comparing the degrees of the left and right sides of (
4.7
), we see that
\alpha_{s(m)}=\gamma_{r}
. This means that
\gamma_{r}
is the least element of the congruence class
s(m)
. Since
p(\gamma_{r})\sim\gamma_{r}
and
p(\gamma_{r})<\gamma_{r}
, this is impossible.
Therefore
(y^{\alpha_{s}}\mid s\in S,\;\alpha_{s}\notin A_{1})
is
{\mathbb{K}}
-linearly independent, as desired. This completes the proof of the first conclusion of the lemma.

Finally, assume that
x^{\alpha}\notin B
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
, so that
y^{\alpha}\neq 0
in
R
for all
\alpha
. For
s\in S
, choose and fix
\alpha(s)\in s
, and set
z_{s}:=y^{\alpha(s)}
. Then
(z_{s}\mid s\in S)
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
R
. Since
y^{\alpha}y^{\beta}=d(\alpha,\beta)y^{\alpha+\beta}\neq 0
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma^{+}
, we have
z_{s}z_{t}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}z_{s+t}
for all
s,t\in S
. Therefore
R
is a twisted semigroup algebra of
S
over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R
be a unital affine
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra which has a grading by a commutative monoid
S
, and let
\{r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\}
be a set of homogeneous
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra generators of
R
. Assume that the homogeneous components
R_{s}
of
R
have
{\mathbb{K}}
-dimension
\leq 1
, and that there is a multiplicatively skew-symmetric matrix
{\bf{q}}:=(q_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
such that
r_{i}r_{j}=q_{ij}r_{j}r_{i}
for all
i,j\in[1,n]
. Then there is a unique surjective
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra map
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\rightarrow R
such that
\psi(x_{i})=r_{i}
for all
i
, and
\ker\psi
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of
\psi
are clear. Keeping
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
fixed, set
r^{\alpha}:=r_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots r_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
; then
\psi(x^{\alpha})=r^{\alpha}
. Also, set
B:=\ker\psi
. For
i\in[1,n]
, let
s_{i}:=\deg(r_{i})
with respect to the
S
-grading on
R
, so that
r_{i}\in R_{s_{i}}
.

Let
B^{\prime}
be the ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
generated by all those binomials of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
that lie in
B
. Set
R^{\prime}:={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B^{\prime}
and write
y^{\alpha}:=x^{\alpha}+B^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}
for
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
. By Lemma
4.3
, there is a monoid congruence
\sim
on
\Gamma^{+}
defined by the rule
\alpha\sim\beta\iff{\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\beta}
, and
R^{\prime}
is graded by the monoid
S^{\prime}:=\Gamma^{+}/{\sim}
where
R^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}:={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}
for
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
and
\alpha\in s^{\prime}
. Note that
\psi
induces a surjective
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra map
\overline{\psi}:R^{\prime}\rightarrow R
such that
\overline{\psi}(y^{\alpha})=r^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
. We claim that
\overline{\psi}
is an isomorphism. Since
\ker\overline{\psi}=B/B^{\prime}
, we will then obtain
B=B^{\prime}
, proving that
B
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

We have
R^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet}}R^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}
where
S^{\prime}_{\bullet}:=\{s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}\mid R^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\neq 0\}
. For
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet}
, choose and fix
\alpha(s^{\prime})\in s^{\prime}
, so that
R^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}
. Then
(y^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}\mid s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet})
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
R^{\prime}
. For
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet}
, we have
x^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}\notin B^{\prime}
and so
x^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}\notin B
, whence
r^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}\neq 0
. There is a monoid homomorphism
\pi:\Gamma^{+}\rightarrow S
such that
\pi(\alpha)=\alpha_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}s_{n}
for
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
, and
r^{\alpha}\in R_{\pi(\alpha)}
. Since
\dim R_{s}\leq 1
for
s\in S
, it follows that
R_{\pi\alpha(s^{\prime})}={\mathbb{K}}r^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}
for
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet}
.

We claim that the map
S^{\prime}_{\bullet}\rightarrow S
given by
s^{\prime}\mapsto\pi\alpha(s^{\prime})
is injective. Suppose
s^{\prime},t^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet}
with
\pi\alpha(s^{\prime})=\pi\alpha(t^{\prime})
. Then
R_{\pi\alpha(s^{\prime})}=R_{\pi\alpha(t^{\prime})}
and so
r^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}=\lambda r^{\alpha(t^{\prime})}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Consequently, the binomial
x^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}-\lambda x^{\alpha(t^{\prime})}
lies in
B
and hence also in
B^{\prime}
. It follows that
y^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}=\lambda y^{\alpha(t^{\prime})}
and so
\alpha(s^{\prime})\sim\alpha(t^{\prime})
, which, finally, implies
s^{\prime}=t^{\prime}
.

Since the
r^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}
for
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet}
are nonzero and lie in distinct homogeneous components
R_{\pi\alpha(s^{\prime})}
, they are
{\mathbb{K}}
-linearly independent. Moreover,
\overline{\psi}
maps the
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis
(y^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}\mid s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet})
of
R^{\prime}
to the family
(r^{\alpha(s^{\prime})}\mid s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{\bullet})
. Therefore
\overline{\psi}
is injective and thus an isomorphism, as claimed.
∎

The grading information in Lemma
4.4
allows us to show that binomial ideals of

.

.

If

, then

is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Proof.

Since the result is clear if

, we may assume that
I
is a proper ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. By Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
3.5
(c),
I=I(L,\rho)
for some sublattice
L
of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I
is a twisted group algebra of
\Gamma/L
over

with a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis of the form

is any transversal for

{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}/I
is graded by the group

.

Now

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
R
be a unital affine
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra. The following are equivalent:

(a)

R
is a binomial
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra.

(b)

R
has a grading by a commutative monoid
S
such that

(1)

all nonzero homogeneous components
R_{s}
of
R
are
1
-dimensional over
{\mathbb{K}}
;

(2)

for any
s,t\in S
, we have
R_{s}R_{t}=0
if and only if
R_{t}R_{s}=0
.

Proof.

(a)
\Longrightarrow
(b): Without loss of generality,
R={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B
for some multiplicatively skew-symmetric matrix
{\bf{q}}\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, and some binomial ideal
B
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. By Lemma
4.3
, there is a grading of
R
by a monoid
S=\Gamma^{+}/{\sim}
with
R_{s}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}
for
s\in S
and
\alpha\in s
, hence Condition (1) holds. Given
s,t\in S
, choose
\alpha\in s
and
\beta\in t
. As noted in (
4.5
),
R_{s}R_{t}=R_{s+t}
, whence also
R_{t}R_{s}=R_{s+t}
. Condition (2) follows.

(b)
\Longrightarrow
(a): We may assume that
R\neq 0
. Since
R
is affine, it can be generated by finitely many nonzero homogeneous elements, say
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
where
r_{i}\in R_{s_{i}}
for some
s_{i}\in S
.

Assumption (b)(1) implies that
R_{s_{i}}={\mathbb{K}}r_{i}
for
i\in[1,n]
, and then assumption (b)(2) implies that
r_{i}r_{j}=0\iff r_{j}r_{i}=0
for
i,j\in[1,n]
. Given
i,j\in[1,n]
with
r_{i}r_{j}\neq 0
, we then have
R_{s_{i}+s_{j}}={\mathbb{K}}r_{i}r_{j}={\mathbb{K}}r_{j}r_{i}
, so there exist nonzero scalars
q_{ij}
and
q_{ji}=q_{ij}^{-1}
in
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that

(4.8)

r_{i}r_{j}=q_{ij}r_{j}r_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{j}r_{i}=q_{ji}r_{i}r_{j}\,.

Note that if
i=j
, we must have
q_{ij}=1
.
On the other hand, if
r_{i}r_{j}=r_{j}r_{i}=0
, we can write (
4.8
) with
q_{ij}:=1=:q_{ji}
. We now have a multiplicatively skew-symmetric matrix
{\bf{q}}:=(q_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
such that
r_{i}r_{j}=q_{ij}r_{j}r_{i}
for all
i,j\in[1,n]
.

There is a unique surjective
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra map
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\rightarrow R
such that
\psi(x_{i})=r_{i}
for all
i
, and Lemma
4.4
shows that
B:=\ker\psi
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. This establishes (a).
∎

.

Let
R
be a unital
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra. Then
R
is isomorphic to a twisted semigroup algebra
{\mathbb{K}}^{e}S
for some finitely generated commutative monoid

if and only if

, some

, and some binomial ideal

of

.

Proof.

Sufficiency is given by Lemma
4.3
.

Conversely, assume that

for some finitely generated commutative monoid

and some

-cocycle

. Then

has a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis

such that

. Setting

, we obtain an

such that conditions (b)(1),(2) of Theorem

hold. By that theorem, there exists a surjective

R
can be graded by the monoid
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
, with
x\in R_{(1,0)}
and
y\in R_{(0,1)}
. All nonzero homogeneous components of this grading are
1
-dimensional.

As is easily checked,
Z(R)={\mathbb{K}}+{\mathbb{K}}x^{2}+{\mathbb{K}}yx
, and the group of units of
R
is the set
{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}+{\mathbb{K}}x+{\mathbb{K}}y+{\mathbb{K}}x^{2}+{\mathbb{K}}yx
. Further, the only normal elements in
R
are the central elements together with the units (we leave this calculation to the reader).

We claim that if
u
and
v
are any normal elements in
R
with
uv=qvu\neq 0
for some
q\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
, then
q=1
. This is clear if
u
or
v
is central. Otherwise,
u
and
v
are units which commute modulo the ideal
{\mathbb{K}}x+{\mathbb{K}}y+{\mathbb{K}}x^{2}+{\mathbb{K}}yx
, and again
q=1
. Thus, any pair of quasi-commuting normal elements of
R
actually commute. Since
R
is not commutative, it therefore cannot be a quotient of any quantum affine space.

We recall that a
quantum affine toric variety
over
{\mathbb{K}}
(cf.
[
8
,
16
]
) is a twisted semigroup algebra
A={\mathbb{K}}^{e}S
where
S
is a finitely generated submonoid of a free abelian group of finite rank, i.e.,
A
is a subalgebra of a quantum torus
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
of the form
\sum_{\alpha\in S}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}
for some finitely generated submonoid
S
of
\Gamma
. Equivalently
[
8
]
, a quantum affine toric variety is an affine domain
A
over
{\mathbb{K}}
equipped with a rational action of an algebraic torus
H=({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{r}
by
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra automorphisms such that the
H
-eigenspaces of
A
are
1
-dimensional.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
R
be a unital
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra. If
R
is isomorphic to a quantum affine toric variety over
{\mathbb{K}}
, then
R\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B
for some multiplicatively skew-symmetric matrix
{\bf{q}}\in M_{n}({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, and some completely prime binomial ideal
B
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. The converse holds if
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed.

Proof.

The first implication is immediate from Theorem
4.6
.

Now assume that
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, and that
R\cong{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/B
for some
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and some completely prime binomial ideal
B
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. Set
J:=\{j\in[1,n]\mid x_{j}\in B\}
and
I:=\langle x_{j}\mid j\in J\rangle\vartriangleleft{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. Then
R\cong({\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I)/(B/I)
. Since
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I
is a quantum affine space, we may replace
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and
B
by
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I
and
B/I
, and so there is no loss of generality in assuming that
x_{i}\notin B
for all
i
. As
B
is completely prime, it follows that
x^{\alpha}\notin B
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma^{+}
.

By Corollary
4.7
,
R\cong{\mathbb{K}}^{e}S
for some finitely generated commutative monoid
S
and some
2
-cocycle
e:S\times S\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Hence,
R
has a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis
(z_{s}\mid s\in S)
such that
z_{s}z_{t}=e(s,t)z_{s+t}
for all
s,t\in S
.

We next claim that
S
is cancellative. Suppose
s,t,u\in S
with
s+u=t+u
. Then
z_{s}z_{u}=e(s,u)z_{s+u}=e(s,u)e(t,u)^{-1}z_{t}z_{u}
. Since
R
is a domain,
z_{s}=e(s,u)e(t,u)^{-1}z_{t}
, whence
s=t
as desired.

Let
G
be the universal group of
S
, an abelian group containing
S
as a submonoid which generates
G
. Thus,
G
is finitely generated. We claim that
G
is torsionfree. If not, it contains a nonzero element
g
with finite order
m>1
. Write
g=s-t
for some distinct
s,t\in S
. Since
z_{s}^{m}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}z_{ms}
and
z_{t}^{m}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}z_{mt}
, there is some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that
z_{s}^{m}=\lambda z_{t}^{m}
. We also have
z_{t}z_{s}=\eta z_{s}z_{t}
for some
\eta\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. As
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed,
\lambda\eta^{-m(m-1)/2}=\mu^{m}
for some
\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
. Set

w:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu^{i-1}\eta^{(i-1)m-i(i-1)/2}z_{s}^{m-i}z_{t}^{i-1}\,,

and calculate that
(z_{s}-\mu z_{t})w=z_{s}^{m}-\lambda z_{t}^{m}=0
. Since

z_{s}^{m-i}z_{t}^{i-1}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}z_{(m-i)s+(i-1)t}

and
(m-i)s+(i-1)t\neq(m-j)s+(j-1)t
for
1\leq i<j\leq m
, the elements
z_{s}^{m-i}z_{t}^{i-1}
for
i\in[1,m]
are
{\mathbb{K}}
-linearly independent, whence
w\neq 0
. Consequently,
z_{s}-\mu z_{t}=0
. However, this forces
s=t
, contradicting the assumption that
g\neq 0
. Thus
G
is torsionfree, as claimed.

Now
G
is free abelian of finite rank, and therefore
R
is a quantum affine toric variety over
{\mathbb{K}}
.
∎

The converse implication in Theorem
4.9
can easily fail when
{\mathbb{K}}
is not algebraically closed, e.g., in the case when
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}={\mathbb{R}}[x]
and
B=\langle x^{2}+1\rangle
.

Remark 4.10
.

Eisenbud and Sturmfels showed in
[
4
, Corollary 1.9]
that various algebras associated with a monomial ideal in a binomial algebra are themselves binomial algebras. There are good noncommutative analogs for blowup, Rees, and associated graded algebras, as follows.

Let
R
be a unital affine binomial
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra, graded by a commutative monoid
S
as in Theorem
4.6
(b), and let
\sigma
be a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra automorphism of
R
such that the homogeneous components
R_{s}
of
R
are all
\sigma
-invariant. The corresponding
skew-Laurent algebra
,
R[z^{\pm 1};\sigma]
, is a free left
R
-module with basis
(z^{n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}})
, and with
zr=\sigma(r)z
for
r\in R
. The
S
-grading on
R
extends naturally to an
(S\times{\mathbb{Z}})
-grading on
R[z^{\pm 1};\sigma]
, where
R[z^{\pm 1};\sigma]_{(s,m)}:=R_{s}z^{m}
for
(s,m)\in S\times{\mathbb{Z}}
. Theorem
4.6
implies that
R[z^{\pm 1};\sigma]
is a binomial
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra, as is any unital affine
(S\times{\mathbb{Z}})
-homogeneous subalgebra.

Now suppose that
I
is a finitely generated
(S\times{\mathbb{Z}})
-homogeneous
\sigma
-invariant ideal of
R
. Then, by the above, the algebras

{\mathcal{B}}(R,I,\sigma):=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}I^{m}z^{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathcal{R}}(R,I,\sigma):=\bigoplus_{m=-\infty}^{-1}Rz^{m}\oplus\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}I^{m}z^{m}

are binomial
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebras. The associated graded algebra
\gr_{I}R=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}I^{m}/I^{m+1}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{B}}(R,I,\id)/I{\mathcal{B}}(R,I,\id)
, whence it is graded by
S\times{\mathbb{Z}}
with homogeneous components having dimension at most
1
. The conditions on
R
imply that

R[z^{\pm 1};\id]_{(s,m)}R[z^{\pm 1};\id]_{(t,l)}=R[z^{\pm 1};\id]_{(s+t,m+l)}=R[z^{\pm 1};\id]_{(t,l)}R[z^{\pm 1};\id]_{(s,m)}

for all
(s,m),(t,l)\in S\times{\mathbb{Z}}
, from which it follows that the analogous property holds in
{\mathcal{B}}(R,I,\id)
and in
\gr_{I}R
. Therefore another application of Theorem
4.6
implies that
\gr_{I}R
is a binomial
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra.

5.
Binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
not containing monomials

Those binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
that contain no monomials in the generators
x_{i}
are closely related to binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
via localization and contraction. In particular, prime (binomial) ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
not containing monomials correspond precisely to prime (binomial) ideals of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. This allows the results of Section
3
on radicals, minimal and associated primes to be carried over to binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
not containing monomials. We deal with general binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
in Section
6
.

5.1.

For
\alpha\in\Gamma
, let
\alpha_{\pm}
denote the
positive
and
negative parts
of
\alpha
:

\displaystyle\alpha_{+}

\displaystyle:=(\max(\alpha_{1},0),\dots,\max(\alpha_{n},0))

\displaystyle\alpha_{-}

\displaystyle:=(-\min(\alpha_{1},0),\dots,-\min(\alpha_{n},0)),

so that
\alpha_{\pm}\in\Gamma^{+}
and
\alpha=\alpha_{+}-\alpha_{-}
. Note that
(\alpha_{+})_{i}
and
(\alpha_{-})_{i}
cannot both be positive for any
i\in[1,n]
, whence
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\alpha_{+})_{i}(\alpha_{-})_{i}=0
. Let us abbreviate this condition as
\alpha_{+}\perp\alpha_{-}
. Conversely, if
\beta,\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}
with
\beta\perp\gamma
, then
\beta=(\beta-\gamma)_{+}
and
\gamma=(\beta-\gamma)_{-}
.

For any sublattice
L
of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, define

I_{A}(L,\rho):=\langle x^{\alpha_{+}}-c(\alpha)^{-1}d(\alpha,\alpha_{-})^{-1}\rho(\alpha)x^{\alpha_{-}}\mid\alpha\in L\rangle\vartriangleleft{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
L
is a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, then

I(L,\rho)\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}=I_{A}(L,\rho).

Proof.

For
\alpha\in L
, observe that
I(L,\rho)
contains the element

\bigl[x^{\alpha}-c(\alpha)^{-1}\rho(\alpha)\bigr]x^{\alpha_{-}}=d(\alpha,\alpha_{-})\bigl[x^{\alpha_{+}}-c(\alpha)^{-1}d(\alpha,\alpha_{-})^{-1}\rho(\alpha)x^{\alpha_{-}}\bigr].

Thus,
I_{A}(L,\rho)\subseteq I(L,\rho)\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

To establish the reverse inclusion, it suffices to show that all binomials in
I(L,\rho)\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
lie in
I_{A}(L,\rho)
, since
I(L,\rho)\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
(Proposition
4.5
).

Let
\lambda x^{\beta}+\mu x^{\gamma}
be an arbitrary binomial in
I(L,\rho)\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, with
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}
and
\beta,\gamma\in\Gamma^{+}
. Since
I(L,\rho)
contains no monomials,
\lambda
and
\mu
are both nonzero. Now
\lambda x^{\beta}+\mu x^{\gamma}=\lambda(x^{\beta}-\nu x^{\gamma})
where
\nu:=-\lambda^{-1}\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
, and it suffices to show that
x^{\beta}-\nu x^{\gamma}\in I_{A}(L,\rho)
. Set

\delta:=\bigl(\min(\beta_{1},\gamma_{1}),\dots,\min(\beta_{n},\gamma_{n})\bigr),\qquad\beta^{\prime}:=\beta-\delta,\qquad\gamma^{\prime}:=\gamma-\delta,

so that
\beta=\beta^{\prime}+\delta
and
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}+\delta
with
\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\delta\in\Gamma^{+}
and
\beta^{\prime}\perp\gamma^{\prime}
. Now

x^{\beta}-\nu x^{\gamma}=d(\beta^{\prime},\delta)^{-1}x^{\beta^{\prime}}x^{\delta}-\nu d(\gamma^{\prime},\delta)^{-1}x^{\gamma^{\prime}}x^{\delta}=d(\beta^{\prime},\delta)^{-1}\bigl(x^{\beta^{\prime}}-\nu^{\prime}x^{\gamma^{\prime}}\bigr)x^{\delta},

with
\nu^{\prime}:=\nu d(\beta^{\prime},\delta)d(\gamma^{\prime},\delta)^{-1}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
and
x^{\beta^{\prime}}-\nu^{\prime}x^{\gamma^{\prime}}\in I(L,\rho)\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, and it is enough to show that
x^{\beta^{\prime}}-\nu^{\prime}x^{\gamma^{\prime}}\in I_{A}(L,\rho)
. Thus, with no loss of generality,
\beta\perp\gamma
.

Set
\alpha:=\beta-\gamma
, so that
\alpha_{+}=\beta
and
\alpha_{-}=\gamma
. Now

(x^{\beta}-\nu x^{\gamma})(x^{\gamma})^{-1}=d(\gamma,\gamma)x^{\beta}x^{-\gamma}-\nu=d(\gamma,\gamma)d(\beta,-\gamma)x^{\alpha}-\nu=d(\alpha,\gamma)^{-1}x^{\alpha}-\nu,

and so
x^{\alpha}-\nu d(\alpha,\gamma)\in I(L,\rho)
. Theorem
3.7
implies that
\alpha\in L
and
\nu d(\alpha,\gamma)=c(\alpha)^{-1}\rho(\alpha)
. Therefore

x^{\beta}-\nu x^{\gamma}=x^{\alpha_{+}}-c(\alpha)^{-1}d(\alpha,\alpha_{-})^{-1}\rho(\alpha)x^{\alpha_{-}}\in I_{A}(L,\rho),

as desired.
∎

In view of Lemma
3.4
, one might expect that if
(\beta(1),\dots,\beta(r))
is a basis for
L
, then
I_{A}(L,\rho)
can be generated by the elements

x^{\beta(l)_{+}}-c(\beta(l))^{-1}d(\beta(l),\beta(l)_{-})^{-1}\rho(\beta(l))x^{\beta(l)_{-}},\qquad l\in[1,r].

However, this fails in general, as the following example, adapted from
[
10
, Example 2.16]
, shows.

Example 5.2
.

Let
n=4
and

{\bf{q}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&q&q^{2}&q^{3}\\
q^{-1}&1&q&q^{2}\\
q^{-2}&q^{-1}&1&q\\
q^{-3}&q^{-2}&q^{-1}&1\end{bmatrix}

where
q\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
is a non-root of unity. One checks that
\beta(1):=(1,-2,1,0)
and
\beta(2):=(0,1,-2,1)
form a basis for
\Gamma_{Z}
, so that
Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}})
is a Laurent polynomial ring in two variables,
x^{\beta(1)}=x_{1}x_{2}^{-2}x_{3}
and
x^{\beta(2)}=x_{2}x_{3}^{-2}x_{4}
. Set
L:=\Gamma_{Z}
, and take
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
to be the homomorphism such that
\rho(\beta(l))=q^{-2}
for
l=1,2
.

Clearly
c(\beta(l))=1
for
l=1,2
, and one checks that
d(\beta(l),\beta(l)_{-})=q^{-2}
. Thus,
I_{A}(L,\rho)
contains the elements

(5.1)

\displaystyle x^{\beta(l)_{+}}-c(\beta(l))^{-1}

\displaystyle d(\beta(l),\beta(l)_{-})^{-1}\rho(\beta(l))x^{\beta(l)_{-}}

\displaystyle=x^{\beta(l)_{+}}-x^{\beta(l)_{-}}=\begin{cases}x_{1}x_{3}-x_{2}^{2}&(l=1)\\
x_{2}x_{4}-x_{3}^{2}&(l=2).\end{cases}

We show that these two elements do not generate
I_{A}(L,\rho)
.

Consider the element
\beta:=(1,-1,-1,1)=\beta(1)+\beta(2)
in
L
. One checks that

c(\beta)=q^{-1},\qquad d(\beta,\beta_{-})=q^{-2},\qquad\rho(\beta)=q^{-4},

and so
I_{A}(L,\rho)
contains the element

x^{\beta_{+}}-c(\beta)^{-1}d(\beta,\beta_{-})^{-1}\rho(\beta)x^{\beta_{-}}=x_{1}x_{4}-q^{-1}x_{2}x_{3}\,.

Since
x_{1}x_{3}-x_{2}^{2}
and
x_{2}x_{4}-x_{3}^{2}
lie in the ideal
\langle x_{2},x_{3}\rangle
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
but
x_{1}x_{4}-q^{-1}x_{2}x_{3}
does not,
we conclude that the elements listed in (
5.1
) do not generate
I_{A}(L,\rho)
.

5.2.

Let
x_{\bullet}
denote the multiplicatively closed subset of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
generated by
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, and set

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}

\displaystyle:=\{P\in\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\mid P\cap x_{\bullet}=\varnothing\}=\{P\in\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\mid x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\notin P\}

\displaystyle\Prim_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}

\displaystyle:=\Prim{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\cap\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

(The second equality in the first line of (
5.2
) relies on the fact that the
x_{i}
are normal elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.) In case
{\mathbb{K}}
is infinite, the sets
\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and
\Prim_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are the
0
-strata
of
\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and
\Prim{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
in the sense of
[
2
, Definitions II.2.1]
, relative to a natural action of the torus
{\mathcal{H}}:=({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{n}
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Now
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is the left and right Ore localization
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[x_{\bullet}^{-1}]
. Since
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is noetherian, extension (localization) and contraction provide inverse bijections

(5.3)

\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\longleftrightarrow\Spec{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\,,\qquad P\longmapsto P{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\,,\qquad Q\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\longleftarrow\!\shortmid Q

(e.g.,
[
7
, Theorem 10.20]
). In particular, any radical ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
contracts to a radical ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
L
be a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
. If
\charr{\mathbb{K}}=0
, then
I_{A}(L,\rho)
is a radical ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Proof.

Proposition
3.8
and Theorem
5.1
.
∎

This corollary does not imply, however, that in characteristic zero binomial ideals containing no monomials are necessarily radical ideals. For instance, take the binomial ideal
\langle x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}^{2}\rangle
in the commutative polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},x_{2}]
(in any characteristic).

5.3.

For any ideal
I
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, let
(I:x_{\bullet})
denote the
x_{\bullet}
-closure
of
I
, that is, the ideal

(5.4)

(I:x_{\bullet}):=\{a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\mid ay\in I\ \text{for some}\ y\in x_{\bullet}\}=I{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

Corollary 5.4
.

If
I
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
not containing any monomials, there exist a unique sublattice
L
of
\Gamma_{Z}
and a unique
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
such that
(I:x_{\bullet})=I_{A}(L,\rho)
. If, further,
I
is a prime ideal, then
I=I_{A}(L,\rho)
.

Proof.

By assumption,
I
is disjoint from
x_{\bullet}
, whence
I{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is a proper binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. By Theorem
3.7
, there exist a unique sublattice
L
of
\Gamma_{Z}
and a unique
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
such that
I{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}=I(L,\rho)
. Thus
(I:x_{\bullet})=I{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}=I_{A}(L,\rho)
by Theorem
5.1
. In case
I
is a prime ideal,
I=(I:x_{\bullet})
.
∎

We now harvest information about the ideals
I_{A}(L,\rho)
from the results of Section
3
, via localization and stratification.

Theorem 5.5
.

(a)
For any
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, the ideal
I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is a primitive ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, then every primitive ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
that contains no monomials has the form
I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
for some
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

Proof.

(a) By Theorems
3.9
and
5.1
,
I(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is a maximal ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
and
I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
localizes to
I(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
. Consequently,
I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is a maximal element of
\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, and so any prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
that strictly contains
I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
must contain some
x_{i}
. Thus

\bigcap\{P\in\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\mid P\supsetneqq I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)\}\supseteq I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)+\langle x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\rangle\supsetneqq I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho),

which shows that
I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
is locally closed in
\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. By
[
2
, Lemma II.7.15 and Corollary II.7.18]
,
I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)\in\Prim{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b) Given the assumption
{\mathbb{K}}=\overline{{\mathbb{K}}}
, the set
\Prim_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
consists precisely of the maximal elements of
\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}

[
2
, Corollary II.8.5]
. Thus, if
P
is a primitive ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
that contains no monomials, then
P
is a maximal element of
\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, whence
P
localizes to a maximal ideal
P{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
. By Theorem
3.9
,
P{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}=I(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
for some
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, and therefore
P=P{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}=I_{A}(\Gamma_{Z},\rho)
due to Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
L
be a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

(a)
If
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is torsionfree, then
I_{A}(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. If, further,
\Gamma/L
is torsionfree, then
I_{A}(L,\rho)
is completely prime.

(b)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, then
I_{A}(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
if and only if
\Gamma_{Z}/L
is torsionfree.

(c)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed and
\Gamma/\Gamma_{Z}
is torsionfree, then all prime binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
that contain no monomials are completely prime.

Proof.

(a)(b) Theorems
3.10
and
5.1
.

(c) If
P
is a prime binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
that contains no monomials, then
P{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
is a prime binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}
that contracts to
P
. By Theorems
3.2
and
5.1
,
P{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}=I(L,\rho)
for some sublattice

of

and some

. Part (b) (or just Theorem

∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
L
be a sublattice of
\Gamma_{Z}
and

.

(a)
If

, then

.

(b)

(c)

, and let

(a)(b) Set

∎

6.
General binomial ideals in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}

General binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
– ones that may contain monomials – can be approached by way of special quotients of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, since
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
modulo the ideal generated by a subset of the generators
x_{i}
is again a quantum affine space. This allows results from Section
5
to be applied to prime binomial ideals and, provided
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, to primitive ideals and to primes minimal over arbitrary binomial ideals. Finally, we prove that if
{\mathbb{K}}
is perfect, then the radical of any binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is binomial.

6.1.

For any subset
J\subseteq[1,n]
, define the following:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}[J]

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{K}}\langle x_{j}^{\pm 1}\mid j\in J\rangle\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{K}}\langle x_{j}\mid j\in J\rangle\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}

\displaystyle\Gamma[J]

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma\mid\alpha_{i}=0\ \forall\,i\notin J\}

\displaystyle\Gamma[J]^{+}

\displaystyle:=\Gamma[J]\cap\Gamma^{+}

\displaystyle\Gamma[J]_{Z}

\displaystyle:=\{\alpha\in\Gamma[J]\mid x^{\alpha}\in Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}[J])\}

\displaystyle I_{A}[\neg J]

\displaystyle:=\langle x_{j}\mid j\in[1,n]\setminus J\rangle\vartriangleleft{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

Then
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
is a quantum affine space
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf{q}}[J]}({\mathbb{K}}^{|J|})
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
is the corresponding quantum torus
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\bf{q}}[J]}(({\mathbb{K}}^{\times})^{|J|})
, where
{\bf{q}}[J]
is the
J\times J
submatrix of
{\bf{q}}
. Moreover,

Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}[J])=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma[J]_{Z}}{\mathbb{K}}x^{\alpha}\,.

There is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra retraction

\pi_{J}:{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]\quad\text{such that}\quad\pi_{J}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}x_{j}&(j\in J)\\
0&(j\notin J)\end{cases}

with
\ker\pi_{J}=I_{A}[\neg J]
, which induces a canonical isomorphism of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I_{A}[\neg J]
onto
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
.

6.2.

Given
J\subseteq[1,n]
, choose and fix a basis
(\beta_{J,1},\dots,\beta_{J,r(J)})
for
\Gamma[J]_{Z}
(allowing
r(J)=0
in case
\Gamma[J]_{Z}=0
), write
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma[J]_{Z}
as a Laurent polynomial ring of the form

{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma[J]_{Z}={\mathbb{K}}[z_{J,1}^{\pm 1},\dots,z_{J,r(J)}^{\pm 1}],

and set

z_{J,\gamma}:=z_{J,1}^{m_{1}}\cdots z_{J,r(J)}^{m_{r(J)}}\qquad\forall\;\gamma=m_{1}\beta_{J,1}+\cdots+m_{r(J)}\beta_{J,r(J)}\in\Gamma[J]_{Z}\,,

so that
(z_{J,\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma[J]_{Z})
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma[J]_{Z}
and
z_{J,\gamma}z_{J,\delta}=z_{J,\gamma+\delta}
for
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma[J]_{Z}
. There is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra isomorphism

\phi_{J}:{\mathbb{K}}\Gamma[J]_{Z}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}Z({\mathcal{T}}_{\bf{q}}[J])\quad\text{such that}\quad\phi_{J}(z_{J,i})=x^{\beta_{J,i}}\ \text{for}\ i\in[1,r(J)].

There are scalars
c_{J}(\gamma)\in{\mathbb{K}}^{\times}
such that

\phi_{J}(z_{J,\gamma})=c_{J}(\gamma)x^{\gamma}\qquad\forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma[J]_{Z}\,,

and, as in (
2.7
), it follows from (
2.2
) that

d(\alpha,\beta)=c_{J}(\alpha+\beta)c_{J}(\alpha)^{-1}c_{J}(\beta)^{-1}\qquad\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma[J]_{Z}\,.

6.3.

For any sublattice
L
of
\Gamma[J]_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, define

I_{J,A}(L,\rho):=I_{A}[\neg J]+\langle x^{\alpha_{+}}-c_{J}(\alpha)^{-1}d(\alpha,\alpha_{-})^{-1}\rho(\alpha)x^{\alpha_{-}}\mid\alpha\in L\rangle\vartriangleleft{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

We may also express
I_{J,A}(L,\rho)
as
I_{A}[\neg J]+I^{\prime}
where
I^{\prime}
is the ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
generated by

\{x^{\alpha_{+}}-c_{J}(\alpha)^{-1}d(\alpha,\alpha_{-})^{-1}\rho(\alpha)x^{\alpha_{-}}\mid\alpha\in L\}.

Theorem 6.1
.

(a)
For any
J\subseteq[1,n]
and
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma[J]_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, the ideal
I_{J,A}(\Gamma[J]_{Z},\rho)
is a primitive ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b)
Assume that
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed. Then every primitive ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
has the form
I_{J,A}(\Gamma[J]_{Z},\rho)

for some
J\subseteq[1,n]
and
\rho\in\Hom(\Gamma[J]_{Z},{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

In particular, all primitive ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are binomial ideals in this case.

Proof.

The primitive ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are the ideals
I_{A}[\neg J]+Q
for
J\subseteq[1,n]
and
Q\in\Prim_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
. The theorem thus follows from Theorem
5.5
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
L
be a sublattice of
\Gamma[J]_{Z}
for some
J\subseteq[1,n]
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

(a)
If
\Gamma[J]_{Z}/L
is torsionfree, then
I_{J,A}(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. If, further,
\Gamma[J]/L
is torsionfree, then
I_{J,A}(L,\rho)
is completely prime.

(b)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, then
I_{J,A}(L,\rho)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
if and only if
\Gamma[J]_{Z}/L
is torsionfree.

(c)
If
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed and
\Gamma[J]/\Gamma[J]_{Z}
is torsionfree for all
J\subseteq[1,n]
, then all prime binomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are completely prime.

Proof.

The prime ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are the ideals
I_{A}[\neg J]+Q
for
J\subseteq[1,n]
and
Q\in\Spec_{0}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
. Apply
Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Assume that
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed, let
P
be a binomial ideal in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, and set
J:=\{j\in[1,n]\mid x_{j}\notin P\}
. Then
P
is prime if and only if
P=I_{J,A}(L,\rho)
for some sublattice
L
of
\Gamma[J]_{Z}
such that
\Gamma[J]_{Z}/L
is torsionfree and some
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
.

Proof.

(\Longleftarrow)
: Theorem
6.2
(a).

(\Longrightarrow)
: We can write
P=I_{A}[\neg J]+Q
where
Q:=\pi_{J}(P)\vartriangleleft{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
. Observe that
Q
is a binomial ideal and a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
. Since the
x_{i}
are normal elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, we see that
x^{\alpha}\notin P
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma[J]
, and so
x^{\alpha}\notin Q
for such
\alpha
.

Corollary
5.4
now implies that
P=I_{J,A}(L,\rho)
for some sublattice
L
of
\Gamma[J]_{Z}
and some
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
, after which Theorem
6.2
(b) implies that
\Gamma[J]_{Z}/L
is torsionfree.
∎

For
J^{\prime}\subseteq[1,n]
, set

\Spec_{J^{\prime}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}:=\bigl\{P\in\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\bigm|P\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}=\{x_{j}\mid j\in J^{\prime}\}\bigr\}.

These sets partition
\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. The elements
x_{j}
for
j\in J:=[1,n]\setminus J^{\prime}
are normal in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and hence are regular modulo any
P\in\Spec_{J^{\prime}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. It follows that all primes in
\Spec_{J^{\prime}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are disjoint from the set

x_{J\bullet}:=\text{the multiplicative subset of}\;{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\;\text{generated by}\;\{x_{j}\mid j\in J\},

and that the elements of
x_{J\bullet}
are regular modulo any such prime.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, let
J\subseteq[1,n]
and
J^{\prime}:=[1,n]\setminus J
, and let
P\in\Spec_{J^{\prime}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(a)

\pi_{J}(P)
is a prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
disjoint from
x_{J\bullet}
.

(b)
If
\pi_{J}(P)
is minimal over
(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})
, then
\pi_{J}(P)
is also minimal over
\pi_{J}(I)
. Moreover,
P
is minimal over
I+I_{A}[\neg J]
and over
(I+I_{A}[\neg J]:x_{J\bullet})
.

(c)
If
P
is minimal over
I
, then
\pi_{J}(P)
is minimal over
\pi_{J}(I)
and minimal over
(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})
.

Proof.

(a) By assumption,
I_{A}[\neg J]=\langle x_{j}\mid j\in J^{\prime}\rangle\subseteq P
, whence
\pi_{J}(P)\in\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
and
\pi_{J}^{-1}(\pi_{J}(P))=P
. As noted above,
P
is disjoint from
x_{J\bullet}
and the elements of
x_{J\bullet}
are regular modulo
P
. It follows that
\pi_{J}(P)
is disjoint from
x_{J\bullet}
and the elements of
x_{J\bullet}
are regular modulo
\pi_{J}(P)
.

(b) First consider a prime
Q
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
such that
\pi_{J}(I)\subseteq Q\subseteq\pi_{J}(P)
. The elements of
x_{J\bullet}
are normal in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
and lie outside
\pi_{J}(P)
by (a), so they must be regular modulo
Q
. Since
\pi_{J}(I)\subseteq Q
, it follows that
(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})\subseteq Q
, and hence
Q=\pi_{J}(P)
by the minimality of
\pi_{J}(P)
. Thus
\pi_{J}(P)
is minimal over
\pi_{J}(I)
.

The minimality statements concerning
P
follow because
\pi_{J}^{-1}(\pi_{J}(I))=I+I_{A}[\neg J]
and
\pi_{J}^{-1}(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})=(I+I_{A}[\neg J]:x_{J\bullet})
.

(c) Suppose
Q\in\Spec{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
with
\pi_{J}(I)\subseteq Q\subseteq\pi_{J}(P)
. Then
\pi_{J}^{-1}(Q)
is a prime of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
lying between
I
and
P
, and hence
\pi_{J}^{-1}(Q)=P
. It follows that
Q=\pi_{J}(P)
, proving that
\pi_{J}(P)
is minimal over
\pi_{J}(I)
.

For the final statement, we just need to show that
\pi_{J}(P)
contains
(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})
. If
a\in(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})
, then
ya\in\pi_{J}(I)
for some
y\in x_{J\bullet}
. Then
ya\in\pi_{J}(P)
, whence
a\in\pi_{J}(P)
because
y
is regular modulo
\pi_{J}(P)
. This establishes the required inclusion.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Assume that
{\mathbb{K}}
is algebraically closed.
If
I
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, then every prime ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
minimal over
I
is binomial.

Proof.

Let
P
be a prime of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
minimal over
I
, and let
J^{\prime}
be the unique subset of
[1,n]
such that
P\in\Spec_{J^{\prime}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. By Lemma
6.4
(a)(c),
\pi_{J}(P)
is minimal over
(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})
, where
J:=[1,n]\setminus J^{\prime}
, and
\pi_{J}(P)
is disjoint from
x_{J\bullet}
. Since
\pi_{J}(I)
is disjoint from
x_{J\bullet}
, Corollary
5.4
implies that
(\pi_{J}(I):x_{J\bullet})=I_{{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]}(L,\rho)
for some sublattice
L
of
\Gamma[J]_{Z}
and
\rho\in\Hom(L,{\mathbb{K}}^{\times})
. Theorem
5.7
(c) then shows that
\pi_{J}(P)
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
. Since
P=\pi_{J}^{-1}(\pi_{J}(P))
and
\ker\pi_{J}=I_{A}[\neg J]
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, we conclude that
P
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.
∎

Our remaining goal is to prove that radicals of binomial ideals in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
are binomial when
{\mathbb{K}}
is perfect. We use noncommutative versions of several lemmas from
[
4
]
.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
I
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, then
I\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
for any
J\subseteq[1,n]
.

Proof.

Fix
J\subseteq[1,n]
.

Grade
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I
by
S:=\Gamma^{+}/{\sim}
as in Lemma
4.3
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]/(I\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J])
is isomorphic to the subalgebra
R
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I
generated by the cosets
y_{j}:=x_{j}+I
for
j\in J
. Since the
y_{j}
are homogeneous with respect to the
S
-grading of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I
, the algebra
R
is also graded by
S
, with homogeneous components
R_{s}=R\cap({\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I)_{s}
.

Now
y_{j}y_{k}=q_{jk}y_{k}y_{j}
for all
j,k\in J
. The natural map
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]\rightarrow R
sends
x_{j}
to
y_{j}
for
j\in J
, and
\ker\psi=I\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
. Lemma
4.4
therefore shows that
I\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[J]
.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
I
and
I^{\prime}
be binomial ideals and
J_{1},\dots,J_{m}
monomial ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(a)

(I+J_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(I+J_{m})=I+J
for some monomial ideal
J
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

(b)

(I+I^{\prime})\cap(I+J_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(I+J_{m})
is a binomial ideal
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Proof.

(a) Set
R:={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}/I
. With notation as in Lemma
4.3
,
R
is graded by the monoid
S:=\Gamma^{+}/{\sim}
, with components
R_{s}={\mathbb{K}}y^{\alpha}
for
s\in S
and
\alpha\in s
. Set
S_{\bullet}:=\{s\in S\mid R_{s}\neq 0\}
and fix
\alpha(s)\in s
for
s\in S_{\bullet}
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}:=(y^{\alpha(s)}\mid s\in S_{\bullet})
is a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis for
R
.

For
i\in[1,m]
, the ideal
\overline{J}_{i}:=(I+J_{i})/I
of
R
is generated by some collection of
y^{\alpha}
s, and hence also spanned by such a collection. Thus,
\overline{J}_{i}
has a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}:=(y^{\alpha(t)}\mid t\in T_{i})
for some
T_{i}\subseteq S_{\bullet}
. Since the
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
are all part of the same basis
{\mathcal{B}}
for
R
, the intersection
\overline{J}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\overline{J}_{i}
has a
{\mathbb{K}}
-basis
(y^{\alpha(t)}\mid t\in T)
where
T:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}T_{i}
. Now
\overline{J}=\bigl(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}(I+J_{i})\bigr)/I
, and so
\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}(I+J_{i})=I+J^{0}
where
J^{0}
is the
{\mathbb{K}}
-span of
\{x^{\alpha(t)}\mid t\in T\}
. We conclude that
\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}(I+J_{i})=I+\langle x^{\alpha(t)}\mid t\in T\rangle
.

(b) In view of part (a), only the case
m=1
needs to be proved.
We proceed as in
[
4
, Corollary 1.5]
, working with a polynomial ring
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[t]
, which is a quantum affine space of rank
n+1
. Let
I^{+}:=I[t]+tI^{\prime}[t]+(1-t)J_{1}[t]
be the ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[t]
generated by
I+tI^{\prime}+(1-t)J_{1}
, and observe that
I^{+}
is a binomial ideal. We claim that
I^{+}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}=(I+I^{\prime})\cap(I+J_{1})
. On one hand, if
a,b\in I
,
c\in I^{\prime}
, and
d\in J_{1}
with
a+c=b+d
, then

a+c=a+tc+(1-t)(b+d-a)=[a+(1-t)(b-a)]+tc+(1-t)d\in I^{+}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\,.

If
\ell\in I^{+}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, then
\ell=f+tg+(1-t)h
for some
f\in I[t]
,
g\in I^{\prime}[t]
, and
h\in J_{1}[t]
. Then
\ell=\ell(0)=f(0)+h(0)\in I+J_{1}
and
\ell=\ell(1)=f(1)+g(1)\in I+I^{\prime}
, whence
\ell\in(I+I^{\prime})\cap(I+J_{1})
. This establishes the claim, and therefore Lemma
6.6
implies that
(I+I^{\prime})\cap(I+J_{1})
is a binomial ideal.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

Let

, and

some normal elements of

as in

). Then

(6.1)

\sqrt{I}=\sqrt{(I:x_{\bullet})}\cap\sqrt{I+\langle x_{1}\rangle}\cap\cdots\cap\sqrt{I+\langle x_{n}\rangle}.

Proof.

This is proved exactly as
[

, given that any normal element

that lies outside a prime ideal

.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
I
be a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. Set
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}:={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[[1,n]\setminus\{i\}]
and
I_{i}:=I\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
for
i\in[1,n]
. Then
I+\langle x_{i}\rangle={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle+M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
for some monomial ideal
M_{i}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
.

Remark
.

Since
M_{i}
is generated by monomials, which are normal elements in
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, the right ideal
M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is actually a two-sided ideal. This may not hold for
I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, so we take
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
to be sure to get the two-sided ideal generated by
I_{i}
. On the other hand, the retraction
\pi_{[1,n]\setminus\{i\}}
maps
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle+M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
onto
I_{i}+M_{i}
, so we can also express this ideal in the form
I_{i}+M_{i}+\langle x_{i}\rangle
.

Proof.

This is proved exactly as
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
, modulo obvious changes of notation.
∎

6.4.

Observe that for any
i\in[1,n]
, the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
may be presented as a skew polynomial ring
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}[x_{i};\sigma_{i}]
where
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}:={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[[1,n]\setminus\{i\}]
and
\sigma_{i}
is an automorphism of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
. Consequently, all prime ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
contract to finite intersections of prime ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
(e.g.,
[
5
, Remark 5
∗
, Lemmas 1.2, 1.3]
). Thus, all radical ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
contract to radical ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
.

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
I
be a radical binomial ideal and
M
a monomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. Then
\sqrt{I+M}=I+J
for some monomial ideal
J
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

Proof.

We follow the line of
[
4
, Proposition 3.4]
, by induction on
n
. The case
n=0
is trivial. Now assume that
n>0
.

Since there is nothing to prove if
M=0
, we may assume that there exists a monomial in
M
. Then
(I+M:x_{\bullet})={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and Lemma
6.8
implies that

\sqrt{I+M}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{I+M+\langle x_{i}\rangle}.

By Lemma
6.7
(a), it suffices to show that each
\sqrt{I+M+\langle x_{i}\rangle}=I+J_{i}
for some monomial ideal
J_{i}
.

Fix
i\in[1,n]
and set
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}:={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[[1,n]\setminus\{i\}]
and
\pi_{i}:=\pi_{[1,n]\setminus\{i\}}:{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
. By §
6.4
,
I_{i}:=I\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
is a radical ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
.

Note that
M+\langle x_{i}\rangle=M^{\prime}_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle
where
M^{\prime}_{i}
is the ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
generated by those monomials in
M
which do not involve
x_{i}
. By Lemma
6.9
,
I+\langle x_{i}\rangle={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+M^{\prime\prime}_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle
for some monomial ideal
M^{\prime\prime}_{i}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
, and thus

I+M+\langle x_{i}\rangle={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle,\qquad M_{i}:=M^{\prime}_{i}+M^{\prime\prime}_{i}\,.

By induction,
\sqrt{I_{i}+M_{i}}=I_{i}+N_{i}
for some monomial ideal
N_{i}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
, where
\sqrt{I_{i}+M_{i}}
denotes the radical of
I_{i}+M_{i}
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
. Now

\displaystyle\sqrt{I+M+\langle x_{i}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle}=\sqrt{I_{i}+M_{i}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=I_{i}+N_{i}+\langle x_{i}\rangle\subseteq I+N_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle\subseteq\sqrt{I+M+\langle x_{i}\rangle}\,,

using that
\pi_{i}({\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle)=I_{i}+M_{i}
and
N_{i}\subseteq\sqrt{I_{i}+M_{i}}\subseteq\sqrt{I+M+\langle x_{i}\rangle}
. Thus
\sqrt{I+M+\langle x_{i}\rangle}=I+J_{i}
where
J_{i}:=N_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle
is a monomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 6.11
.

Assume that
{\mathbb{K}}
is perfect. If
I
is any binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, then
\sqrt{I}
is also binomial.

Proof.

We again proceed by induction on
n
, the case
n=0
being trivial. Assume now that
n>0
. We may replace
I
by any binomial ideal lying between
I
and
\sqrt{I}
, so without loss of generality,
I
equals the sum of all binomial ideals contained in
\sqrt{I}
.

Claim 1
: The ideal
I^{\prime}:=\sqrt{(I:x_{\bullet})}
is binomial.

If there exists a monomial in
I
, then
(I:x_{\bullet})={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
and
I^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
. If
I
contains no monomials, then by Corollary
5.4
,
(I:x_{\bullet})
is a proper binomial ideal
I_{A}(L,\rho)
, and Theorem
5.7
implies that
I^{\prime}
is binomial.

As in the previous proof, set
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}:={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}[[1,n]\setminus\{i\}]
and
\pi_{i}:=\pi_{[1,n]\setminus\{i\}}:{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
for
i\in[1,n]
, and let
I_{i}:=I\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
.

Claim 2
:
I_{i}
is a radical ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
.

Lemma
6.6
implies that
I_{i}
is a binomial ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
, and so by induction
\sqrt{I_{i}}
is binomial. By §
6.4
,
\sqrt{I}\cap{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
is a radical ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
, so it contains
\sqrt{I_{i}}
. Consequently, the binomial ideal
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\sqrt{I_{i}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
is contained in
\sqrt{I}
, and thus
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\sqrt{I_{i}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}\subseteq I
by our assumptions on
I
. It follows that
\sqrt{I_{i}}\subseteq I_{i}
, yielding the claim.

Claim 3
:
\sqrt{I+\langle x_{i}\rangle}=I+J_{i}
for some monomial ideal
J_{i}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
.

First note that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle=\pi_{i}^{-1}(I_{i})
, which is a radical ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
due to Claim 2. According to Lemma
6.9
,

I+\langle x_{i}\rangle={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle+M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}

for some monomial ideal
M_{i}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
. Applying Proposition
6.10
to the radical binomial ideal
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle
and the monomial ideal
M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}
, we obtain

\sqrt{I+\langle x_{i}\rangle}=\sqrt{{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle+M_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle+J^{\prime}_{i}

for some monomial ideal
J^{\prime}_{i}
. Since
{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}I_{i}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bf{q}}+\langle x_{i}\rangle\subseteq I+\langle x_{i}\rangle\subseteq\sqrt{I+\langle x_{i}\rangle}
, it follows that
\sqrt{I+\langle x_{i}\rangle}=I+J_{i}
where
J_{i}:=\langle x_{i}\rangle+J^{\prime}_{i}
.

Combining Claims 1 and 3 with Lemma
6.8
, and writing
I^{\prime}=I+I^{\prime}
, we obtain

\sqrt{I}=(I+I^{\prime})\cap(I+J_{1})\cap\cdots\cap(I+J_{m}).

Therefore Lemma
6.7
(b) implies that
\sqrt{I}
is binomial.
∎

Remark 6.12
.

It is known that Theorem
6.11
does not hold when
{\mathbb{K}}
is not perfect, even in the commutative case. By
[
1
, Lemma 29]
, the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},x_{2}]
has a binomial ideal whose radical is not binomial (cf. Example
3.13
).

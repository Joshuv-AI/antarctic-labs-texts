On semilinear elliptic equation with negative exponent arising from a closed MEMS model


Abstract
This paper is concerned with the elliptic equation − Δ ​ u = λ ( a − u ) p -\Delta u=\frac{\lambda}{(a-u)^{p}} in a connected, bounded C 2 C^{2} domain Ω \Omega of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} subject to zero Dirichlet boundary conditions, where λ > 0 \lambda>0 , N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 , p > 0 p>0 and a : Ω ¯ → [ 0 , 1 ] a:\bar{\Omega}\to[0,1] vanishes at the boundary with the rate dist ​ ( x , ∂ Ω ) γ {\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)^{\gamma} for γ > 0 \gamma>0 . When p = 2 p=2 and N = 2 N=2 , this equation models the closed Micro-Electromechanical Systems devices, where the elastic membrane sticks the curved ground plate on the boundary, but insulating on the boundary. The function a a shapes the curved ground plate. Our aim in this paper is to study qualitative properties of minimal solutions of this equation when λ > 0 \lambda>0 , p > 0 p>0 and to show how the boundary decaying of a a works on the minimal solutions and the pull-in voltage. Particularly, we give a complete analysis for the stability of the minimal solutions.

When

\kappa\geq 1
and
\gamma\in(0,1]
. Let
\bar{\lambda}
be given in Proposition
3.3
.
Then the map
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda})\mapsto\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.
Let
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\bar{\lambda}
and
\phi_{\lambda_{2}}
be the achieved function of
\mu_{1}(\lambda_{2})
with
\|\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
,
then we have that

\displaystyle 0<\mu_{1}(\lambda_{1})-\mu_{1}(\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi_{\lambda_{2}}|^{2}-\frac{p\lambda_{1}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda_{1}})^{p+1}}\right)dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi_{\lambda_{2}}|^{2}-\frac{p\lambda_{2}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda_{2}})^{p+1}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda_{2}})^{p+1}}dx

\displaystyle+p\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{1}{(a-u_{p,\lambda_{2}})^{p+1}}-\frac{1}{(a-u_{p,\lambda_{1}})^{p+1}}\Big)\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p+1}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{a^{p+1}}dx+p\lambda_{1}(p+1)\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{p,\lambda_{2}}-u_{p,\lambda_{1}}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda_{2}})^{p+2}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}dx.

From (
3.8
), we obtain that

\displaystyle(p+1)\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{p,\lambda_{2}}-u_{p,\lambda_{1}}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda_{2}})^{p+2}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}dx\leq(p+1)2^{2p+3}{\bf c}_{0}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi_{\lambda_{2}}^{2}}{a^{p+1}}dx,

then the mapping
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda})\mapsto\mu_{1}(\lambda)
is locally Lipschitz continuous.
\Box

Remark 3.2

Because of the boundary decay of
a
, it is difficulty to obtain the Lipschitz continuity of
\lambda\mapsto u_{p,\lambda}
for
\lambda>\bar{\lambda}
.

4
Regularity of extremal solutions

In order to improve the regularity of extremal solutions,
we need the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.1

Assume that
a\in C^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies (
1.4
) with

\kappa\geq 1
,
\gamma\in(0,1]
,
p\in{\cal I}_{\gamma}
and
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}]
. Let
u
be a weak solution of (
1.1
) satisfying such that

\Big\|\frac{1}{(a-u)^{p}}\Big\|_{L^{q_{\sigma}}(\Omega_{r})}<+\infty,

(4.1)

where
q_{\sigma}=\frac{N}{p(2-\sigma)}
with
\sigma\in[1,2)
and

\Omega_{r}:=\{y\in\Omega:\,\rho(y)>r\}\quad{\rm with}\ \,r>0.

Then

\inf_{x\in\Omega_{3r}}\big(a(x)-u(x)\big)>0

(4.2)

and

u
is
C^{2}
in
\Omega_{3r}
.

Proof.
By (
4.1
), we have that

\frac{1}{(a-u)^{p}}\in L^{\frac{N}{2-\sigma}}(\Omega_{r})

and then
u\in W^{2,\frac{N}{2-\sigma}}(\Omega_{2r})
and by Sobolev’s Theorem, we
deduce that
u\in C^{\sigma}(\Omega_{3r})
. Here
C^{\sigma}=C^{0,1}
if
\sigma=1
,
C^{\sigma}=C^{1,\sigma-1}
if
\sigma\in(1,2)
.

We next show that
u<a
in
\Omega_{3r}
. Indeed, if not, there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega_{3r}
such that
u(x_{0})=a(x_{0})
, which means that
u
contacts
a
at
x_{0}
. Thus,

\displaystyle|a(x)-u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a(x)-a(x_{0})|+|u(x)-u(x_{0})|+|u(x_{0})-a(x_{0})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a(x)-a(x_{0})|+|u(x)-u(x_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{22}|x-x_{0}|^{\sigma},

then for
\sigma\in[1,2)
one has that

\int_{\Omega_{3r}}\frac{1}{(a-u)^{pq_{\sigma}}}dx\geq\int_{\Omega_{3r}}|x-x_{0}|^{-\frac{\sigma}{2-\sigma}N}dx\geq c_{23}\int_{\Omega_{3r}}|x-x_{0}|^{-N}dx=\infty,

which contradicts (
4.1
). Therefore, (
4.2
) holds.

\Box

Lemma 4.2

Assume that

a\in C^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies (
1.4
) with

\kappa\geq 1
and

\gamma\in(0,\,1]
.
Let
p\in{\cal I}_{\gamma}
,
and
u_{p,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}}
be the extremal solution of (
1.1
).
Then

u_{p,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}}\in W^{2,q}_{loc}(\Omega)
for any
1\leq q<f_{0}(p)
.

Proof.
For
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p})
, we know that
u_{p,\lambda}
is stable, then

\int_{\Omega}\frac{p\lambda\varphi^{2}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1}}dx<\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{0}({\Omega})\setminus\{0\}.

(4.3)

Let us denote

\varphi_{\theta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{\theta}\eta_{r}^{\tau}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{}0&{\rm on}\ \,\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\theta\in\big(-p-\sqrt{p+p^{2}},0\big)
,
\tau>1
and

\eta_{r}:\Omega\to[0,1]
is a function in
C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\eta_{r}=1
in
\Omega_{2r}
and
\eta_{r}=0
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{r}
. We observe that
\varphi_{\theta}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
It follows by (
4.3
) with
\varphi=\varphi_{\theta}
that

\displaystyle p\lambda\int_{\Omega_{r}}\frac{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\big((a-u_{p,\lambda})^{\theta}\eta_{r}^{\tau}\big)|^{2}dx

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\theta^{2}+\delta)\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-2}|\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})|^{2}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx

\displaystyle+(1+\frac{1}{\delta})\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}|\nabla(\eta_{r}^{\tau})|^{2}dx,

where
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4}\theta^{2})
small enough.
Since
u_{p,\lambda}
is the minimal solution of (
1.1
), then

-\Delta(a-u_{p,\lambda})=-\Delta a-\frac{\lambda}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p}}\quad{\rm in}\ \ \Omega.

(4.5)

Multiplying (
4.5
) by
\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}
and then integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\Delta a+\frac{\lambda}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p}}\Big)(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\int_{\Omega}\Delta(a-u_{p,\lambda})(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\int_{\Omega}\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})\cdot\nabla\left((a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\right)\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})\cdot\nabla(\eta_{r}^{2\tau})dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-2}|\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})|^{2}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}|\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})||\nabla(\eta_{r}^{2\tau})|dx

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-2}|\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})|^{2}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx

\displaystyle-\frac{2\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-2}|\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})|^{2}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}|\nabla\eta_{r}|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle(\theta^{2}-\delta)\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-2}|\nabla(a-u_{p,\lambda})|^{2}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx+c_{\delta}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}|\nabla(\eta_{r}^{\tau})|^{2}dx,

where
c_{\delta}>0
depends on
\delta
.
Then together with (
4.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{p\lambda}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1-2\theta}}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\frac{\theta^{2}+\delta}{\theta^{2}-\delta}\int_{\Omega}[\Delta a+\frac{\lambda}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p}}](a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx

\displaystyle+\bar{c}_{\delta}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}|\nabla(\eta_{r}^{\tau})|^{2}dx,

where
\bar{c}_{\delta}=1+\frac{1}{\delta}+c_{\delta}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(p-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\frac{\theta^{2}+\delta}{\theta^{2}-\delta}\Big)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta_{r}^{2\tau}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1-2\theta}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\frac{\theta^{2}+\delta}{\theta^{2}-\delta}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta a|(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx+\bar{c}_{\delta}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}|\nabla(\eta_{r}^{\tau})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\delta}\Big(\|\Delta a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx+\tau^{2}\|\nabla\eta_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})}\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}\eta_{r}^{2(\tau-1)}dx\Big).

Since
a\in C_{0}^{2}(\Omega)

\|\Delta a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})}\leq C_{r}

for some
C_{r}>0
depends on
r
, then letting

\theta\in(-p-\sqrt{p+p^{2}},0),

we have that
p-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}>0
and choose
\delta>0
such that

p-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\frac{\theta^{2}+\delta}{\theta^{2}-\delta}>0,

then by Hölder inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(p-\frac{\theta^{2}}{1-2\theta}\frac{\theta^{2}+\delta}{\theta^{2}-\delta}\Big)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta_{r}^{2\tau}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1-2\theta}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta-1}\eta_{r}^{2\tau}dx+\int_{\Omega}(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{2\theta}\eta_{r}^{2(\tau-1)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|\Omega|^{\frac{p}{p+1-2\theta}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta_{r}^{2\tau}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1-2\theta}}dx\right)^{\frac{1-2\theta}{p+1-2\theta}}+C|\Omega|^{\frac{p+1}{p+1-2\theta}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta_{r}^{2\tau}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1-2\theta}}dx\right)^{\frac{-2\theta}{p+1-2\theta}},

where
\tau=\frac{p+1-2\theta}{p+1}
and

2\tau\frac{-2\theta}{p+1-2\theta}=2(\tau-1).

Thus,
there exists
c_{24}>0
independent on
\lambda
such that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta_{r}^{2}}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p+1-2\theta}}dx\leq c_{24},

which implies that

\int_{\Omega_{2r}}\Big(\frac{1}{(a-u_{p,\lambda})^{p}}\Big)^{q}dx\leq c_{24}

(4.6)

for any
r>0
and
1\leq q<3+\frac{1}{p}+2\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{p}}
.
Therefore, passing to the limit as
\lambda\to\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}
, for above
q
we have that

u_{p,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}}\in W^{2,q}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

(i)
Recall that
p^{\#}_{{}_{N}}>0
be defined in (
1.9
). When
N\geq 6
, it is the unique zero point in
(0,+\infty)
of

f_{0}(p)-N=0
,
where we call
f_{0}(t)=3+\frac{1}{t}+2\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{t}}.

When
f_{0}(p)>N
, there exists
\sigma\in[1,2)
such that

p+1-2\theta\geq q_{\sigma}

for some
\theta\in\big(-p-\sqrt{p+p^{2}},0\big)
.
Choosing
\sigma=1
, for
p<p^{\#}_{{}_{N}}
there holds

\frac{1}{p}\Big(p+1+2(p+\sqrt{p+p^{2}})\Big)>N,

(4.7)

that is
f_{0}(p)>N
for
p<p^{\#}_{{}_{N}}
.
Then by (
4.6
) there exists
q_{1}\geq N

\int_{\Omega_{2r}}\Big(\frac{1}{(a-u_{p,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}})^{p}}\Big)^{q_{1}}dx\leq c_{24},

(4.8)

Thus, for
p\in{\cal I}_{\gamma}
,
p<p^{\#}_{{}_{N}}
and
\lambda=\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}

it follows by Lemma
4.1
that for
r\in(0,1)

\inf_{\Omega_{2r}}(a-u_{p,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}})>0

and
u_{p,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}}
is
C^{2}_{loc}(\Omega)
and it is a classical solution of (
1.1
) under our setting.

(ii)
From Lemma
4.2
, we have that

u_{p,\lambda^{*}_{\gamma,p}}\in W^{2,q}_{loc}(\Omega)
for any

q\in\Big[1,f_{0}(p)\Big).

If
p^{\#}_{{}_{N}}<p^{*}_{\gamma}
, for

p^{\#}_{{}_{N}}\leq p<\min\{p^{*}_{\gamma},q^{\#}_{{}_{N}}\}\quad{\rm if}\ \ N\geq 7,

then
\frac{N}{2}<f_{0}(p)\leq N
and

\alpha=2-\frac{N}{f_{0}(p)}\in(0,1].

and we have that

\frac{1}{(a-u)^{p}}\in L^{\frac{N}{2-\alpha}}(\Omega_{2r})

and then
u\in W^{2,\frac{N}{2-\alpha}}(\Omega_{3r})
and by Sobolev’s Theorem, we
deduce that
u\in C^{\alpha}(\Omega_{4r})
.

\Box

ABCD of Kondo effect
Taro Kimura 木村太郎

Abstract
We explore the Kondo effect incorporating the localized impurity transforming under generic symmetry group G G , that we call the G G -Kondo effect. We derive the one-dimensional effective model coupled with the impurity, and studied the thermodynamic properties based on the boundary conformal field theory approach. We in particular study the impurity entropy at the conformal fixed point, and the low temperature behavior of the specific heat and the susceptibility. We also consider the Wilson ratio based on these results, and mention the case with exceptional group symmetry.

Introduction

The Kondo effect (近藤効果)
has been playing a central role in the study of strongly correlated electron systems, providing a lot of innovative ideas for our understanding of the quantum nature of many-body physics
[
1
,
2
]
.
It has been originally observed and studied in the magnetic impurity system, where the SU(2) spin degrees of freedom of the localized impurity spin interacting with the conduction electron plays a crucial role in the non-trivial behavior of the renormalization flow.
In fact, the Kondo effect seems the first experimental observation of the asymptotic freedom.

One of the interesting directions of the study of the Kondo effect is to incorporate higher-rank SU
(n)
symmetry, discussed in the context of the quantum dot
[
3
,
4
]
, ultracold atomic system
[
5
,
6
]
, heavy-flavor QCD
[
7
,
8
]
, etc.
In addition, there has been a recent proposal to implement the Majorana impurity, which realizes the SO(
n
) Kondo system
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
See also
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
for further discussions on unconventional symmetries, including Sp
(n)
symmetry.
Such developments in the Kondo effect motivates us to explore the quantum impurity system with generic symmetry group
G
, that we call the
G
-Kondo system.
In this paper, we mainly consider the classical groups, namely,
ABCD of Kondo effect
:

\displaystyle A_{n}\ :\ \mathrm{SU}(n)\,,\qquad B_{n}\ :\ \mathrm{SO}(2n+1)\,,\qquad C_{n}\ :\ \mathrm{Sp}(n)\,,\qquad D_{n}\ :\ \mathrm{SO}(2n)\,.

(1.1)

Here we apply the convention, s.t.,
\mathrm{Sp}(1)=\mathrm{SU}(2)
,
\mathrm{Sp}(2)=\mathrm{SO}(5)
.
The key property of the Kondo effect is the non-perturbative behavior below the characteristic temperature, called the Kondo temperature
T_{\text{K}}
.
Such a behavior is universal, and observed in the system with arbitrary non-Abelian group
G
in general:
For the Kondo coupling in the form of

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{\text{K}}=\lambda_{\text{K}}\sum_{a=1}^{\dim G}\sigma^{a}S^{a}\,,

(1.2)

with the Kondo coupling
\lambda_{\text{K}}
, and the “spin” operators of the electron and the impurity,
\sigma^{a}
and
S^{a}
(the generators of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
), we obtain the scaling equation
[
18
]
(See also
[
19
]
),

\displaystyle\derivative{\lambda_\text{K}}{\log\Lambda}=-h^{\vee}\rho_{0}\,\lambda_{\text{K}}^{2}\,,

(1.3)

where we define the energy scale
\Lambda
, the density of states
\rho_{0}
, and the dual Coxeter number
h^{\vee}
(See Tab.
1
).
The derivation of the scaling equation (
1.3
) just uses the relation for the structure constant of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
shown in (
A.1
).

Since the electron-impurity coupling may be relatively small in the high temperature regime compared to
T_{\text{K}}
, we can apply the perturbative analysis there, whereas we should non-perturbatively explore the low temperature regime due to the asymptotic freedom property.
Conformal Field Theory (CFT) is one of the non-perturbative approaches to the low temperature regime of the Kondo system as demonstrated by Affleck–Ludwig
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
.
See also review articles
[
26
,
27
]
.
In this framework, the impurity problem is replaced with the study of the boundary condition, which can be analyzed based on Boundary CFT (BCFT).

In this paper, we formulate the Kondo effect with generic symmetry group
G=ABCD
, and apply the BCFT approach in order to study the non-perturbative aspects of the
G
-Kondo system.
In Sec.
2
, we first introduce the localized impurity which transforms under the symmetry group
G=ABCD
, and then formulate the quantum impurity problem based on the effective one-dimensional theory.
In particular, the impurity effect is described as the conformal boundary condition of the effective theory, which allows us to apply the BCFT techniques to the current problem.
Based on such a description, we analyze the thermodynamic properties of the
G
-Kondo system.
In Sec.
3
, we study the impurity contribution to the thermodynamic entropy (the impurity entropy), which would be a criterion whether the conformal fixed point is described as the Fermi liquid (FL) or the non-Fermi liquid (NFL).
In Sec.
4
, we then study the low temperature behaviors of the specific heat and the susceptibility using the conformal perturbation theory with the leading irrelevant operator.
We clarify the parameter condition in order to obtain the FL/NFL behavior.
We also mention the case with the exceptional group symmetry,
G=EFG
, in Sec.
5
.

2
Formulation of the impurity problem

The Kondo problem is originally formulated in three dimensions with a conduction electron and a localized impurity.
Assuming the electron-impurity interaction takes a form of the
\delta
-function, we can focus on the
s
-wave scattering, and the system is effectively reduced to the one-dimensional model defined on a semi-infinite domain
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Furthermore, since we are interested in the excitation in the vicinity of the Fermi energy, we approximate the dispersion relation by
\epsilon(k)\approx v_{\text{F}}(k-k_{\text{F}})
, which leads to the effective Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\text{F}}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{x}\quantity[
\psi_{\text{L},i\alpha}^\dag(x) \ii\partial_x \psi_{\text{L},i\alpha}(x)
- \psi_{\text{R},i\alpha}^\dag(x) \ii\partial_x \psi_{\text{R},i\alpha}(x)
]+\mathsf{H}_{\text{K}}\,,

(2.1)

where
v_{\text{F}}
is the Fermi velocity, and
\psi_{\text{L}/\text{R},i\alpha}
is the left/right moving fermion with the spin and channel (flavor) indices,
\alpha=1,\ldots,n
,
i=1,\ldots,k
.
The electron-impurity interaction is described by
\mathsf{H}_{\text{K}}
defined in (
1.2
).

2.1
Non-Abelian bosonization

For the complex (charged) fermion, the Hamiltonian (
2.1
) is invariant under the following transformations,

\displaystyle G=\mathrm{SU}(n):\ \psi_{\alpha}\mapsto(g\psi)_{\alpha}\,,\quad G^{\prime}=\mathrm{SU}(k):\ \psi_{i}\mapsto(g^{\prime}\psi)_{i}\,,\quad\mathrm{U}(1):\ \psi\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi}\psi\,.

(2.2)

Namely, the fermion operator transforms in the bifundamental representation of
G\times G^{\prime}
.
Assuming the interaction
\mathsf{H}_{\text{K}}
is insensitive (singlet) to the channel symmetry
G^{\prime}
, we may consider the right mover
\psi_{\text{R}}
as the analytic continuation of the left mover to the negative region,
\psi_{\text{L},\alpha}(x)=(g\psi_{\text{R}})_{\alpha}(-x)=:\psi_{\alpha}(x)
with
g\in G
.
Then, we obtain the chiral expression of the Hamiltonian (
2.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\text{F}}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{x}\psi_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}(x)\mathrm{i}\partial_{x}\psi_{i\alpha}(x)+\mathsf{H}_{\text{K}}\,.

(2.3)

The interaction term, originally introduced in (
1.2
), is rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{\text{K}}=\lambda_{\text{K}}\sum_{a=1}^{\dim G}\psi_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}(x)(t^{a})_{\alpha\beta}\psi_{i\beta}(x)\,S^{a}\,\delta(x)=\lambda_{\text{K}}\,J^{a}(x)\,S^{a}\,\delta(x)\,,

(2.4)

where the localized impurity spin operator is denoted by
S^{a}\,\delta(x)
for
a=1,\ldots,\dim G
.
We define the current operators with respect to
(G,G^{\prime},\mathrm{U}(1))
-symmetry (non-Abelian bosonization; See, for example,
[
28
]
for details):

\displaystyle J^{a}(x)={:\psi^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(t^{a})_{\alpha\beta}\psi_{i\beta}:}(x)\,,\quad J^{A}(x)={:\psi^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}(T^{A})_{ij}\psi_{j\alpha}:}(x)\,,\quad J(x)={:\psi^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}\psi_{i\alpha}:}(x)

(2.5)

where
(t^{a})_{a=1,\ldots,\dim G}
,
(T^{A})_{A=1,\ldots,\dim G^{\prime}}
are the generators of the corresponding Lie algebras,
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\operatorname{Lie}G^{\prime}
, and the normal ordering symbol denoted by
:\mathcal{O}:
.
The levels of these currents are respectively given by
k
,
n
, and
kn
.
The level-
k

G
-symmetry current (
G_{k}
-current for short) obeys the operator product expansion (OPE),

\displaystyle J^{a}(z)J^{b}(w)=\frac{\frac{k}{2}\,\delta^{ab}}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{f^{ab}_{~~c}\,J^{c}(w)}{z-w}+\cdots

(2.6)

where
(f^{ab}_{~~c})
is the structure constant of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}G
.
Applying the mode expansion,

\displaystyle J_{n}^{a}=\oint\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,z^{n}\,J^{a}(z)\ \iff\ J^{a}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}J_{n}^{a}\,z^{-n-1}\,,

(2.7)

the OPE is equivalent to the algebraic relation of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
, which is an infinite dimensional extension of
\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\commutator{J_n^a}{J_m^b}=f^{ab}_{~~c}J_{n+m}^{c}+\frac{k}{2}n\,\delta^{ab}\delta_{n+m,0}\,.

(2.8)

The same argument is applied to the
G^{\prime}
-current.

We put
v_{\text{F}}=1
for simplicity.
Then, the Hamiltonian (
2.3
) is rewritten in the quadratic form of the current operators (Sugawara form),

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\quantity[
{\frac{1}{n + k} : J^a J^a :}(x)
+ {\frac{1}{k + n} : J^A J^A :}(x)
+ {\frac{1}{2 kn} :JJ:}(x)
]+\lambda_{\text{K}}J^{a}S^{a}\delta(x)\,.

(2.9)

Such a decomposition of the
kn
-component complex fermion system is a consequence of the conformal embedding

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(n)}_{k}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(k)}_{n}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{u}(1)}_{kn}\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{u}(kn)}_{1}\,,

(2.10)

which corresponds to the spin-charge(-channel) separation of the one-dimensional fermion.
We see the agreement of the central charges

\displaystyle c\quantity[\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(n)}_k]+c\quantity[\widehat{\mathfrak{su}(k)}_n]+c\quantity[\widehat{\mathfrak{u}(1)}_{kn}]

\displaystyle=\frac{k(n^{2}-1)}{k+n}+\frac{n(k^{2}-1)}{n+k}+1

\displaystyle=kn

\displaystyle=c\quantity[\widehat{\mathfrak{u}(kn)}_{1}]\,,

(2.11)

where the central charge of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
is given by

\displaystyle c\quantity[ \widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_k ]=\frac{k\dim\mathfrak{g}}{k+h^{\vee}}

(2.12)

with
h^{\vee}
the dual Coxeter number of
\mathfrak{g}
, e.g.,
h^{\vee}=n
for
\mathfrak{su}(n)
.
See Appendix
A
for details.

In fact, the current operator obeys the same OPE (
2.6
) under the redefinition,

\displaystyle J^{a}(x)\ \longrightarrow\ J^{a}(x)+2\pi\delta(x)S^{a}\,.

(2.13)

Therefore, if the coupling constant takes the critical value
\lambda_{\text{K}}^{*}=2/(n+k)
, the interaction term in the Hamiltonian (
2.9
) is absorbed into the quadratic term,

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\quantity[
{\frac{1}{n + k} : J^a J^a :}(x)
+ {\frac{1}{k + n} : J^A J^A :}(x)
+ {\frac{1}{2 kn} :JJ:}(x)
]\,,

(2.14)

where we omit the non-dynamical term
S^{a}S^{a}
.
In fact, the critical value
\lambda_{\text{K}}
is interpreted as the value of the coupling constant at the conformal fixed point, and the impurity effect would be incorporated as the conformal boundary condition labeled by the representation
R_{\text{imp}}
under the symmetry group
G
.

2.2
Real fermion

We can similarly formulate the impurity problem with the real (Majorana) fermions, by replacing the complex conjugate
\psi^{\dagger}
with
\psi
.
We have seen that, the conformal embedding (
2.10
) plays a crucial role to specify the symmetry of the system.
In the case with the real fermions, we instead have the following embeddings
[
29
,
30
]
:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{so}(n)}_{k}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{so}(k)}_{n}

\displaystyle\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{so}(kn)}_{1}\quad\text{for}\quad kn:\ \text{even}\,,

(2.15a)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{sp}(n)}_{k}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{sp}(k)}_{n}

\displaystyle\subset\widehat{\mathfrak{so}(4kn)}_{1}\,.

(2.15b)

The central charges are given by

\displaystyle c\quantity[ \widehat{\mathfrak{so}(n)}_k ]+c\quantity[ \widehat{\mathfrak{so}(k)}_n ]

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{2}\frac{n^{2}-n}{n+k-2}+\frac{n}{2}\frac{k^{2}-k}{k+n-2}=\frac{kn}{2}=c\quantity[ \widehat{\mathfrak{so}(kn)}_1 ]\,,

(2.16a)

\displaystyle c\quantity[ \widehat{\mathfrak{sp}(n)}_k ]+c\quantity[ \widehat{\mathfrak{sp}(k)}_n ]

\displaystyle=\frac{kn(2n+1)}{k+n+1}+\frac{nk(2k+1)}{n+k+1}=2kn=c\quantity[ \widehat{\mathfrak{so}(4kn)}_1 ]\,.

(2.16b)

The latter is also interpreted as the embedding for the
kn
-component quaternionic fermion.
Based on these embeddings, we have two options for the symmetry groups for the real fermion system:

\displaystyle(G,G^{\prime})=\begin{cases}(\mathrm{SO}(n),\mathrm{SO}(k))\\
(\mathrm{Sp}(n),\mathrm{Sp}(k))\\
\end{cases}

(2.17)

We then consider the Sugawara Hamiltonian for the real fermion system:

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\quantity[
{\frac{1}{k + h^\vee(\mathfrak{g})} :J^a J^a:}(x)
+ {\frac{1}{n + h^\vee(\mathfrak{g}')} :J^A J^A:}(x)
]+\lambda_{\text{K}}J^{a}S^{a}\delta(x)\,.

(2.18)

where
S^{a}
is the impurity “spin” which transforms under
G=\mathrm{SO}(n)
and Sp
(n)
, respectively.
See Tab.
1
for the list of the dual Coxeter number.
We remark that, compared to the complex fermion system (
2.9
), there is no
\mathrm{U}(1)
-current (the charge current) in this case.
This is because the analog of
\mathrm{U}(1)
in this case is the discrete group
\mathrm{O}(1)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Precisely speaking, we should consider the spin group (the universal cover of SO group) in the conformal embedding, depending on the parity of the rank.
See, for example,
[
31
]
for details.

If the coupling constant takes the critical value
\lambda_{\text{K}}^{*}=2/(k+h^{\vee}(\mathfrak{g}))
, the interaction term is combined into the quadratic form by the
G_{k}
-current shift as in (
2.13
),

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\quantity[
{\frac{1}{k + h^\vee(\mathfrak{g})} :J^a J^a:}(x)
+ {\frac{1}{n + h^\vee(\mathfrak{g}')} :J^A J^A:}(x)
]\,.

(2.19)

Again, we consider the conformal boundary condition characterized by the representation
R_{\text{imp}}
of the impurity under the symmetry group
G
.

3
Impurity entropy

The impurity contribution to the entropy (impurity entropy; also called the boundary entropy) provides a useful criterion for the FL/NFL behavior at the conformal fixed point.
In this Section, we compute the impurity entropy of
G
-Kondo system based on the CFT formalism discussed in Sec.
2
.

The impurity entropy of the Kondo system is obtained through the so-called
g
-factor, which counts the boundary degeneracy
[
21
]
:

\displaystyle S_{\text{imp}}=\log g(R_{\text{imp}})

(3.1)

where we denote the representation of the impurity under the symmetry group
G
by
R_{\text{imp}}
.
If the
g
-factor takes an integral value,
g(R)\in\mathbb{N}
, the fixed point is supposed to be the FL, since one can “count” the degeneracy of the impurity states, and the original picture which consists of the conduction electron and the localized impurity still holds at the fixed point.
On the other hand, it would be described as the NFL if the
g
-factor becomes an irrational number,
g(R)\not\in\mathbb{N}
, where the original separated picture of the electron and the impurity is not available any longer.
We remark that there are several exceptional situations for the NFL where the corresponding
g
-factor takes an integral value due to an accidental symmetry.

The
g
-factor is given by the modular
\mathsf{S}
-matrix of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
denoted by
\mathsf{S}_{RR^{\prime}}

[
32
]
:

\displaystyle g(R)=\frac{\mathsf{S}_{0R}}{\mathsf{S}_{00}}=\chi_{R}\quantity( q^\rho)\,,

(3.2)

where
\chi_{R}(X)
is the character of the representation
R
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
evaluated with the Cartan torus
X\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{g}}
, and
\rho
is the Weyl vector of
\mathfrak{g}
given by (
A.5
).
The character formula is summarized in Appendix
B
for
G=ABCD
.
The parameter
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
\absolutevalue{q}=1
, is defined with the level and the dual Coxeter number
(k,h^{\vee})
as follows:

\displaystyle q=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{k+h^{\vee}}\right)\,.

(3.3)

We remark that the expression of the
g
-factor (
3.2
) coincides with the unknot Wilson loop expectation value of
G_{k}
Chern–Simons theory on the three-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}

[
33
]
.
See also
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
for further discussion on the relation to the line defect.

In fact, the
g
-factor is given as the principal specialization of the character of the representation
R
, a.k.a., the quantum dimension of the representation
R
,

\displaystyle g(R)=\dim_{q}R\geq 1\,.

(3.4)

The quantum dimension is reduced to the ordinary dimension in the limit
q\to 1
,
\lim_{q\to 1}\dim_{q}R=\dim R\in\mathbb{N}
, which is interpreted as the
g
-factor in the weak coupling regime.
In addition, since it is a polynomial of
(q,q^{-1})
with non-negative integer coefficients, it is smaller than the classical value,
g(R)\leq\dim R
.
This implies that the
g
-factor decreases (possibly, monotonically) along the renormalization flow to the low-energy regime.
This is called the
g
-theorem, a boundary analog of the
c
-theorem in CFT.

We remark that, in the large
k
limit, the asymptotic behavior of the impurity entropy is given by

\displaystyle S_{\text{imp}}\ \xrightarrow{k\to\infty}\ \log\dim R\,.

(3.5)

Hence, the
g
-factor takes an integral value, and thus the the fixed point is described as the FL in the limit
k\to\infty
.
This implies that the quantum correction to the
g
-factor is largely suppressed in in this case.

4
Low temperature thermodynamics

In addition to the conformal fixed point corresponding to zero temperature
T=0
, we can also study the low temperature behavior of the thermodynamic quantities based on the conformal perturbation theory.

4.1
Bulk contribution

We first consider the bulk contribution to the thermodynamic quantities, the specific heat and the susceptibility
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
:

\displaystyle C_{\text{bulk}}=\frac{\pi}{3}c\,T\,,\qquad\chi_{\text{bulk}}=\frac{k}{2\pi}\,,

(4.1)

where
c
is the total central charge, corresponding to the conformal embeddings, (
2.11
) and (
2.16
),

\displaystyle c=bkn=\begin{cases}kn&(\mathrm{SU}(n)_{k})\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{kn}{2}&(\mathrm{SO}(n)_{k})\\
2kn&(\mathrm{Sp}(n)_{k})\end{cases}

(4.2)

with the symmetry parameter

\displaystyle\mathrm{SU}(n):\ b=1\,,\qquad\mathrm{SO}(n):\ b=\frac{1}{2}\,,\qquad\mathrm{Sp}(n):\ b=2\,.

(4.3)

We remark that the parameter
\beta=2b
is called the Dyson index in the context of random matrix theory.
See
[
44
]
for details.

The susceptibility considered here is with respect to the external
G
-field (magnetic field), which is coupled to the
G
-current in the form of
h^{a}J^{a}
.
The
G
-symmetry allows us to take the specific direction for the coupling term
h^{1}J^{1}=:hJ^{1}
without loss of generality.
See
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
]
for the proposal to realize the magnetic field in
\mathrm{SO}(n)
Kondo system.

4.2
Impurity contribution

In order to compute the finite temperature behavior, we apply the perturbative analysis with the leading irrelevant operators as follows
[
20
,
22
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{1}

\displaystyle={:J^{a}J^{a}:(x)}\,\delta(x)\,,

(4.4a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{2}

\displaystyle={:J_{-1}^{a}\phi^{a}:(x)}\,\delta(x)\,,

(4.4b)

with

\displaystyle\delta\mathsf{H}=-\lambda_{\sigma}\,\mathcal{O}_{\sigma}\qquad(\sigma=1,2)\,.

(4.5)

The first operator
\mathcal{O}_{1}
is the quadratic operator with the dimension
\Delta_{1}=2
.
Therefore, the dimensional analysis shows that the corresponding coupling constant
\lambda_{1}
should be proportional to
T_{\text{K}}^{-1}
.

The second operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is the first descendent of the adjoint primary operator
\phi^{a}(x)
with the (conformal) dimension
[
45
]
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\phi}=\frac{h^{\vee}}{k+h^{\vee}}\,,

(4.6)

for
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{k}
theory, where we apply the conformal dimension formula of the primary operator associated with the representation
R
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
\Delta_{R}=C_{2}(R)/(k+h^{\vee})
.

C_{2}(R)
is the quadratic Casimir of the representation
R
.
For the adjoint representation, it is given by the dual Coxeter number (
A.1
).

The dimension of the second operator becomes
\Delta_{2}=1+\Delta_{\phi}
, and the corresponding coupling constant
\lambda_{2}
should be proportional to
T_{\text{K}}^{-\Delta_{\phi}}
.
We remark that this operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
may be identified with the so-called
\hat{h}
-scalar in the context of the coset CFT
[
46
]
.
Since
\Delta_{\phi}<1
for
k\geq 1
, and thus
\Delta_{2}<\Delta_{1}
, the second operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is the leading irrelevant operator if it exists.
In fact, the adjoint representation is not a fundamental representation for
G=\mathrm{SU}(n)
nor
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
:
The adjoint representation is given by the tensor product of
n
and
\overline{\textbf{n}}
representations for
\mathrm{SU}(n)
, and the degree two symmetric tensor product of the natural representation for
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
.
Therefore, the operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is not generated by the fusion process if
k=1
.
Hence, we should apply the operator
\mathcal{O}_{1}
for the perturbative expansion to discuss the finite temperature behavior for
\mathrm{SU}(n)_{1}
and
\mathrm{Sp}(n)_{1}
, while we should use the other operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
for the remaining cases.

For the complex fermion system, the total partition function in the presence of the perturbative term
\delta\mathsf{H}
and the external
G
-field coupling is given by the path integral over the fermion field,

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\exp\quantity( - \beta F(\lambda_\sigma,h) )

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\psi\mathcal{D}\psi^{\dagger}\,\exp\quantity[ - \int_{-\beta/2}^{\beta/2} \dd{\tau} \int_{-L/2}^{L/2} \dd{x}
\qty(
\psi^\dag(\partial_\tau+ \ii\partial_x) \psi- \lambda_\sigma\mathcal{O}_\sigma+ \frac{h}{2 \pi} J^1(\tau, x)
)]

(4.7)

where
L
is the system size, and
\beta=T^{-1}
is the inverse temperature.
Splitting the free energy into the bulk and the impurity contributions,
F=Lf_{\text{bulk}}+f_{\text{imp}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\beta f_{\text{imp}}}

\displaystyle=\left<\exp\quantity[ - \int_{-\beta/2}^{\beta/2} \dd{\tau} \int_{-L/2}^{L/2} \dd{x}
\qty(
- \lambda_\sigma\mathcal{O}_\sigma+ \frac{h}{2 \pi} J^1(\tau, x)
)]\right>\,,

(4.8)

where the average is taken with respect to the path integral with
(\lambda_{\sigma},h)=(0,0)
.
We obtain the same correlator expression in the real fermion system.
We will perturbatively evaluate this path integral to compute the low temperature behavior of the thermodynamic quantities.

4.2.1
Specific heat

We consider the low temperature behavior of the specific heat.
From the operator
\mathcal{O}_{1}
, we obtain
[
20
]

\displaystyle C_{\text{imp}}=\lambda_{1}\frac{k\dim\mathfrak{g}}{3}\pi^{2}T\,,

(4.9)

which exhibits the linear dependence on the temperature, a.k.a., the FL property.
As discussed above, this result is applied only to the case with
k=1
for
G=\mathrm{SU}(n)
and
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
:

\displaystyle C_{\text{imp}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\frac{n^{2}-1}{3}\pi^{2}T&(\mathrm{SU}(n)_{1})\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\frac{n(2n+1)}{3}\pi^{2}T&(\mathrm{Sp}(n)_{1})\end{cases}

(4.10)

From the operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, we instead obtain the following expression:

\displaystyle C_{\text{imp}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lambda_{2}^{2}\pi^{2\Delta_{\phi}+1}\Delta_{\phi}^{2}\quantity( 1 - 2 \Delta_\phi)\frac{\Gamma(1/2-\Delta_{\phi})\Gamma(1/2)}{\Gamma(1-\Delta_{\phi})}\dim\mathfrak{g}\quantity( h^\vee+ \frac{k}{2} )\,T^{2\Delta_{\phi}}&(\Delta_{\phi}<1/2)\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}^{2}\pi^{2}\dim\mathfrak{g}\quantity( h^\vee+ \frac{k}{2} )\,T\log\quantity( \frac{T_\text{K}}{T} )&(\Delta_{\phi}=1/2)\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{4\lambda_{2}^{2}\pi^{2}\Delta_{\phi}\,T_{\text{K}}^{2\Delta_{\phi}-1}}{(2\Delta_{\phi}+1)(2\Delta_{\phi}-1)}\dim\mathfrak{g}\quantity( h^\vee+ \frac{k}{2} )\,T&(\Delta_{\phi}>1/2)\end{cases}

(4.11)

In the derivation of this expression, we use the formula,

\displaystyle\left<\,J_{-1}^{a}\phi^{a}(\tau_{1},0)J_{-1}^{b}\phi^{b}(\tau_{2},0)\,\right>=\frac{(h^{\vee}+k/2)\dim\mathfrak{g}}{\absolutevalue{[\tau_1 - \tau_2]}^{2(\Delta_{\phi}+1)}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad[x]=\frac{\beta}{\pi}\sin\quantity( \frac{\pi}{\beta} x )\,.

(4.12)

The remaining computation is parallel with the earlier analysis.
See, for example,
[
22
,
37
]
for details.
Compared to the dimension of the adjoint primary operator (
4.6
), we have
\Delta_{\phi}\lessgtr 1/2

\iff

h^{\vee}\lessgtr k
.
Then, focusing on the temperature dependence, it shows

\displaystyle C_{\text{imp}}\propto\begin{cases}T^{2\Delta_{\phi}}&(\Delta_{\phi}<1/2;\ h^{\vee}<k)\\
T\log(T_\text{K} / T)&(\Delta_{\phi}=1/2;\ h^{\vee}=k)\\
T&(\Delta_{\phi}>1/2;\ h^{\vee}>k)\end{cases}

(4.13)

Therefore, for
h^{\vee}\leq k
, we have the fractional power (logarithmic) dependence on the temperature, which is a specific feature of the NFL, while it shows the standard FL behavior for
h^{\vee}>k
, which is compatible with the contribution of the operator
\mathcal{O}_{1}
(
4.9
).

4.2.2
Susceptibility

We can similarly compute the susceptibility.
From the operator
\mathcal{O}_{1}
, we obtain
[
20
]

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{imp}}=\lambda_{1}\frac{k(h^{\vee}+k)}{2}\,,

(4.14)

which is indeed the FL behavior as expected.
As well as the specific heat, this result is applied only to the case with
k=1
for
G=\mathrm{SU}(n)
and
\mathrm{Sp}(n)
:

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{imp}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\frac{n+1}{2}&(\mathrm{SU}(n)_{1})\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\frac{n+2}{2}&(\mathrm{Sp}(n)_{1})\end{cases}

(4.15)

From the operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, we obtain the following expression:

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{imp}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lambda_{2}^{2}\pi^{2\Delta_{\phi}-1}\quantity( \frac{1}{2} - \Delta_\phi)\frac{\Gamma(1/2-\Delta_{\phi})\Gamma(1/2)}{\Gamma(1-\Delta_{\phi})}\quantity( h^\vee+ \frac{k}{2} )^{2}\,T^{2\Delta_{\phi}-1}&(\Delta_{\phi}<1/2)\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle 2\lambda_{2}^{2}\quantity( h^\vee+ \frac{k}{2} )^{2}\,\log\quantity( \frac{T_\text{K}}{T} )&(\Delta_{\phi}=1/2)\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda_{2}^{2}\,T_{\text{K}}^{2\Delta_{\phi}-1}}{2\Delta_{\phi}-1}\quantity( h^\vee+ \frac{k}{2} )^{2}&(\Delta_{\phi}>1/2)\end{cases}

(4.16)

In this case, we use the formula

\displaystyle\left<\,J^{1}(z_{1})J^{1}(z_{2})J_{-1}^{a}\phi^{a}(z_{3})J_{-1}^{b}\phi^{b}(z_{4})\,\right>_{\text{connected}}

\displaystyle=\quantity(
\frac{1}{[z_1 - z_4]^2 [z_2 - z_3]^2}
+ \frac{1}{[z_1 - z_3]^2 [z_2 - z_4]^2}
)\frac{(h^{\vee}+k/2)^{2}}{[z_{3}-z_{4}]^{2\Delta_{\phi}}}

(4.17)

where the function
[x]
is defined in (
4.12
).
See
[
22
,
37
]
for details.
The temperature dependence is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{imp}}\propto\begin{cases}T^{2\Delta_{\phi}-1}&(\Delta_{\phi}<1/2;\ h^{\vee}<k)\\
\log(T_\text{K} / T)&(\Delta_{\phi}=1/2;\ h^{\vee}=k)\\
\text{const.}&(\Delta_{\phi}>1/2;\ h^{\vee}>k)\end{cases}

(4.18)

Then, for the NFL regime
h^{\vee}\leq k
, it shows the fractional power (logarithmic) dependence as well as the specific heat, while it shows no temperature dependence for the FL regime
h^{\vee}>k
.

4.2.3
Wilson ratio

As seen in the analysis above, the thermodynamic quantities explicitly depend on the coupling constants
\lambda_{1,2}
, which are non-universal parameters of the model, although the temperature dependence seems universal.
In order to cancel such a coupling dependence, we consider the Wilson ration as a combination of the specific heat and the susceptibility:

\displaystyle R_{\text{W}}=\quantity( \frac{\chi_\text{imp}}{C_\text{imp}} )\Big/\quantity( \frac{\chi_\text{bulk}}{C_\text{bulk}} )\,.

(4.19)

From the perturbative analysis with the operator
\mathcal{O}_{1}
, we obtain the Wilson ratio as follows:

\displaystyle R_{\text{W}}=\frac{h^{\vee}+1}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\,c=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{n}{n-1}&(\mathrm{SU}(n)_{1})\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2(n+2)}{2n+1}&(\mathrm{Sp}(n)_{1})\end{cases}

(4.20)

where
c
is the total central charge (
4.2
).
Together with the operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, we obtain the Wilson ratio for
h^{\vee}\leq k
as follows:

\displaystyle R_{\text{W}}=\frac{bn}{3}\frac{(h^{\vee}+k/2)(h^{\vee}+k)^{2}}{h^{\vee 2}\dim\mathfrak{g}}\,c=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(n+k/2)(n+k)^{2}}{3n(n^{2}-1)}&(\mathrm{SU}(n)_{k})\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{(n-2+k/2)(n-2+k)^{2}}{3(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}&(\mathrm{SO}(n)_{k})\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\frac{(n+2+k/2)(n+1+k)^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}(2n+1)}&(\mathrm{Sp}(n)_{k})\\[10.00002pt]
\end{cases}

(4.21)

where
b
is the symmetry parameter defined in (
4.3
).
For
h^{\vee}>k
, on the other hand, the contributions from the operators
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
are compatible, so that we should take into account both of them.
This makes the Wilson ratio non-universal:
It depends on the ratio of the coupling constants
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
.
This phenomenon, called the Fermi/non-Fermi mixing, occurs in particular in this parameter regime
[
37
]
.

6
Discussion

In this paper, we have formulated the Kondo problem with generic symmetry group
G=ABCD
, and studied its thermodynamic properties based on the BCFT analysis.
Although BCFT provides a powerful tool to study the non-perturbative aspects of the Kondo effect, it would be instructive to verify the results obtained in this paper with other methodologies, e.g., numerical renormalization group, Bethe ansatz analysis.
In particular, since the exact method based on the Bethe ansatz is formulated for the integrable system with generic
G

[
47
]
, the
G
-Kondo system could be similarly explored with the Bethe ansatz with inhomogeneity.
See a review article
[
48
]
for details.

Even though we have theoretically studied the Kondo system with generic symmetry group
G
, it would be important to examine how to realize such a generalized situation.
There are several realizations for the SU(
n
) systems as mentioned earlier (Sec.
1
), where a similar analysis is also applicable
[
49
]
.
For the SO(
n
) Kondo system, the Majorana fermion realizes the quantum impurity in the spinor representation of SO(
n
).
From this point of view, it would be also interesting to consider the impurity which transforms in other representations of SO
(n)
, e.g., the vector representation.
For the Sp(
n
) theory, it might be possible to use a similar setup to the SO
(n)
case:
Actually both cases are based on the conformal embedding of the real fermion as shown in Sec.
2.2
.
For example, one can use the isomorphism,
\mathrm{Sp}(2)=\mathrm{SO}(5)
.
In this case, the (four-dimensional) spinor representation of
\mathrm{SO}(5)
is directly identified with the natural representation of Sp
(2)
(See Sec.
3.3
).
This suggests the realization of the Sp
(n)
Kondo system with the Majorana impurities by imposing the symplectic Majorana condition.

Appendix B
Character formula

The character of the representation
R
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has several equivalent expressions.
We here present the determinantal formula for the character of the classical groups,
G=ABCD
, a.k.a., the Schur, symplectic Schur, and orthogonal Schur functions.

Let
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{g}}
be an element of the maximal Cartan torus of
G

Then the character of the representation
R
parametrized by a (half-)integer sequence
\lambda
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\chi_{R(\lambda)}(X)=\begin{cases}s_{\lambda}(X)&(\mathrm{SU}(n))\\
o^{(\text{o})}_{\lambda}(X)&(\mathrm{SO}(2n+1))\\
sp_{\lambda}(X)&(\mathrm{Sp}(n))\\
o^{(\text{e})}_{\lambda}(X)&(\mathrm{SO}(2n))\end{cases}

(B.1)

where
s_{\lambda}(X)
,
o^{(\text{o/e})}_{\lambda}(X)
, and
sp_{\lambda}(X)
are the Schur, orthogonal Schur, and symplectic Schur functions, defined as the ratio of the determinants
[
52
]

(See also
[
53
,
54
]
):

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\quantity( x_j^{\lambda_i + n - i} )}{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\quantity( x_j^{n - i} )}\,,

(B.2a)

\displaystyle o^{(\text{o})}_{\lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\quantity( x_j^{\lambda_i + n - i + \frac{1}{2}} - x_j^{-(\lambda_i + n - i + \frac{1}{2})} )}{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\quantity( x_j^{n - i + \frac{1}{2}} - x_j^{-(n - i + \frac{1}{2})} )}\,,

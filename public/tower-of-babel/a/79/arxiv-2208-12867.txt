Nonlinear random perturbations of PDEs and quasi-linear equations in Hilbert spaces depending on a small parameter
Sandra Cerrai, Giuseppina Guatteri, Gianmario Tessitore

Abstract
. We study a class of quasi-linear parabolic equations defined on a separable Hilbert space, depending on a small parameter in front of the second order term. Through the nonlinear semigroup associated with such equation, we introduce the corresponding SPDE and we study the asymptotic behavior of its solutions, depending on the small parameter. We show that a large deviations principle holds and we give an explicit description of the action functional.



1.
Introduction

Consider the partial differential equation

\frac{dX^{x}}{dt}(t)=AX^{x}(t)+b(X^{x}(t)),\ \ \ \ \ X^{x}(0)=x\in\,H,

(1.1)

defined on a separable Hilbert space
H
, endowed with the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
and the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
. Here
A:D(A)\subset H\to H
is the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup and
b:D(b)\subseteq H\to H
is some non-linear mapping.
Next, consider the following stochastic perturbation of (
1.1
)

dX^{x}_{\epsilon}(t)=\left[AX^{x}_{\epsilon}(t)+b(X^{x}_{\epsilon}(t))\right]\,dt+\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\sigma(X^{x}_{\epsilon}(t))\,dW_{t},\ \ \ \ \ X^{x}_{\epsilon}(0)=x\in\,H,

(1.2)

where
\epsilon>0
is a small parameter,
W_{t}
,
t\geq 0
, is a cylindrical Wiener process and
\sigma
is a mapping, defined on
H
and taking values in some space of bounded linear operators defined on the reproducing kernel of the noise into
H
. We assume that the differential operator
A
, the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
and the noise
W_{t}
are such that both (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are well-posed.

If the parameter
\epsilon
is small, the trajectories of the perturbed system (
1.2
) remain close to those of the unperturbed system (
1.1
) on any bounded time interval. In particular, if there exist a domain
G\subset H
and a point
x_{0}\in\,G
such that any trajectory of (
1.1
) starting in
G
remains in
G
and converges to
x_{0}
, as time goes to infinity, then with overwhelming probability the trajectories of (
1.2
) starting from any
x\in G
enter any neighborhood of
x_{0}
, before eventually leaving the domain
G
because of the effect of the noise. As know, this is a consequence of the large deviations of
X_{\epsilon}(t)
from
X(t)

which are described by the
action functional

I^{x}_{T}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi(t)\|_{H}^{2}\,dt\,:\,f=X^{x,\varphi}\right\},

where we have denoted by
X^{x,\varphi}
the solution of the controlled version of (
1.1
)

\frac{dX^{x,\varphi}}{dt}(t)=AX^{x,\varphi}(t)+b(X^{x,\varphi}(t))+\sigma(X^{x,\varphi}(t))\varphi(t),\ \ \ \ \ X^{x,\varphi}(0)=x,

and by the
quasi-potential

V(x_{0},x)=\inf\left\{I_{T}(f)\,:\,f\in\,C([0,T];H),\ f(0)=x_{0},\ f(T)=x,\ T>0\right\}.

It is known that the stochastic PDE (
1.2
) is related to the linear Kolmogorov equation on the Hilbert space
H

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{D_{t}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\sigma\sigma^{\star}(x)D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\right]+\langle Ax+b(x),Du_{\epsilon}(t,x)\rangle_{H},\ \ \ x\in\,H,\ \ \ t>0,}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{u_{\epsilon}(0,x)=g(x),\ \ \ x\in\,H.}\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

Actually, under suitable conditions on the operator
A
, the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
and the initial condition
g
,
equation (
1.3
) admits a unique classical solution
u_{\epsilon}
, which can be written in terms of the linear transition semigroup
P^{\epsilon}_{t}
associated with (
1.2
). Namely

u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=P^{\epsilon}_{t}g(x)=\mathbb{E}g(X_{\epsilon}(t,x)),\ \ \ \ t\geq 0,\ \ x\in\,H.

In particular, the description of the small noise asymptotics of the solutions of equation (
1.2
) provided by the theory of large deviations allows to give a detailed description of the long-time behavior of the solutions of infinite dimensional PDE (
1.3
).

In
[
8
]
, Freidlin and Koralov have considered more general stochastic perturbations of the dynamical system (
1.1
), when
H=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
A=0
and
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Lipschitz-continuous mapping. They have introduced the following quasi-linear parabolic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{i,j}(x,u_{\epsilon}(t,x))\,\partial_{ij}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}(x)\,\partial_{i}u_{\epsilon}(t,x),\ \ \ x\in\,\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ \ t>0,}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{u_{\epsilon}(0,x)=g(x),\ \ \ x\in\,\mathbb{R}^{d},}\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
a_{ij}(x,r)=(\sigma\sigma^{\star})_{ij}(x,r)
, and by invoking the classical theory of quasi-linear PDEs, they have shown that, under reasonable assumptions on the coefficients
f
and
\sigma
, equation (
1.4
) admits a unique classical solution
u_{\epsilon}
. Next, for every
t>0
and
x\in\,\mathbb{R}^{d}
, they have introduced the following randomly perturbed system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{dX_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)=b(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s))\,ds+\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\sigma(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s),u_{\epsilon}(t-s,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)))\,dB_{s},}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(0)=x,}\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

where
B_{t}
,
t\geq 0
, is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion. As in the linear case, the PDE (
1.4
) and the SDE (
1.5
) are related by the following relation

u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}g(X^{t,x}_{\epsilon}(t))=:T^{\epsilon}_{t}g(x),

(1.6)

but now
T^{\epsilon}_{t}
is a non-linear semigroup. This is in fact reason why equation (
1.5
) can be seen as a
non-linear
perturbation of the deterministic system.

The study of the large deviation principle and of the quasi-potential for (
1.5
), has allowed Freidlin and Koralov to study the long-time behavior of the solutions to equation (
1.4
), restricted to the domain
G
(that now is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) and endowed with the boundary condition
u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=g(x)
, for every
x\in\,\partial G
. In this case

u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}g(X^{t,x}_{\epsilon}(t\wedge\tau^{x}_{\epsilon})),

where
\tau^{x}_{\epsilon}
is the first exit time of
X^{t,x}_{\epsilon}
from the domain
G
. In particular, the asymptotic description of
\tau^{x}_{\epsilon}
in terms of the quasi-potential has made possible to study the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\epsilon}
on exponential time scales
t(\epsilon)\sim\exp(\lambda/\epsilon)
. Freidlin and Koralov’s idea is to introduce a family of linear equations obtained from (
1.4
) by freezing the second variable in
\sigma\sigma^{\star}
and putting it equal to a constant
c
. This allows them to describe the asymptotics of
u_{\epsilon}(\exp(\lambda/\epsilon),x)
, for different values of
\lambda\in\,(0,\infty)
, in terms of some function
c(\lambda)
obtained from
V_{G}(c)
, the minimum of the quasi-potential in
G
for the linear problem corresponding to
c
, and from
g(x^{\star}(c))
, where
x^{\star}(c)
is the point of
\partial G
where the quasi-potential attains its minimum, for different values of
c
.

The present paper represents the beginning of a longer term project where we aim to develop an analogous theory for infinite dimensional dynamical systems described by PDEs. As in the finite dimensional case studied in
[
8
]
, also here, as a first and fundamental step, we need to be able to study the well-posedness of the following quasi-linear equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{D_{t}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\sigma\sigma^{\star}(x,u_{\epsilon}(t,x))D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\right]+\langle Ax+b(x),Du_{\epsilon}(t,x)\rangle_{H},\ \ \ x\in\,H,\ \ \ t>0,}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{u_{\epsilon}(0,x)=g(x),\ \ \ x\in\,H,}\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

However, unlike in finite dimension, where a well-established theory of deterministic quasi-linear PDEs is available, it seems that the current literature does not provide any Hilbert space counterpart to such classical theory, and everything has to be done.

In our analysis we will proceed in several steps and here we are considering the case when
\sigma:H\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{L}(H)
is Lipschitz continuous and there exist a bounded and non-negative symmetric operator
Q
, a continuous mapping
f
defined on
H\times\mathbb{R}
with values in the space of trace-class operators and a constant
\delta>0
such that

\sigma^{\star}\sigma(x,r)=Q+\delta\,f(x,r),\ \ \ \ x\in\,H,\ \ r\in\,\mathbb{R}.

This allows to rewrite equation (
1.7
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{D_{t}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\mathscr{L}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\delta\,f(x,u_{\epsilon})(t,x)D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\right]+\langle b(x),Du_{\epsilon}(t,x)\rangle_{H},}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{u_{\epsilon}(0,x)=g(x),\ \ \ x\in\,H,}\end{array}\right.

where

\mathscr{L}_{\epsilon}\varphi(x)=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\left[QD_{x}^{2}\varphi(x)\right]+\langle Ax,D_{x}\varphi(x)\rangle_{H}.

In particular, if we denote by
R^{\epsilon}_{t}
the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup associated with the operator
\mathscr{L}_{\epsilon}
, we can rewrite equation (
1.7
) in mild form as

u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(x)+\int_{0}^{t}R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{\mbox{Tr}}\left[\delta F(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\right]+\langle b(\cdot),Du_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\rangle_{H}\right)(x)\,ds.

(1.8)

By assuming suitable conditions on
A
and
Q
(see Hypothesis
2
), the semigroup
R^{\epsilon}_{t}
has a smoothing effect for every fixed
\epsilon>0
, and this allows to prove by a fixed point argument that if
\delta
is sufficiently small there exists a local solution in a suitable space of smooth Hölder continuous functions. Moreover, we show that any mild solution
u_{\epsilon}
defined on a given interval
[0,T]
is in fact a classical solution. In particular,
u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
is twice continuously differentiable in
H
for every
t>0
and
QD^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)
is a trace-class operator,
u_{\epsilon}(\cdot,x)
is differentiable in
(0,+\infty)
for every
x\in\,D(A)
and equation (
1.4
) holds. Finally, in order to prove that for every
T>0
and
\epsilon>0
the solution is defined on the whole interval
[0,T]
and is unique, we prove suitable a-priori bounds.

Once we have proven the existence and uniqueness of a classical solution for equation (
1.4
), we can introduce the stochastic PDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{dX_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)=\left[AX_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)+b(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s))\right]\,ds+\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\sigma(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s),u_{\epsilon}(t-s,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)))\,dW_{s},}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(0)=x,}\end{array}\right.

(1.9)

where
W_{t}
is a cylindrical Wiener process in
H
, defined on some stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
.
Due to the regularity of the coefficients and of the function
u_{\epsilon}
, we can show that there exists
\bar{\delta}>0
such that, for every
\delta\leq\bar{\delta}
and for every
t>0
and
x\in\,H
, equation (
1.9
) admits a unique mild solution in
L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];H))
.
Moreover, we show that, as in the finite dimensional case, the quasi-linear equation (
1.4
) and the stochastic PDE are related through formula (
1.6
) and, in particular, a maximum principle holds for equation (
1.7
).

It is worth noticing that as a consequence of the Markov property, the following relation holds

u_{\epsilon}(t-s,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)))=\mathbb{E}(g(X_{\epsilon}^{t-s,y}(t-s))\big|_{y=X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)}=\mathbb{E}(g(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(t))|\mathscr{F}_{s}),

for every
s\in\,[0,t]
and
x\in\,H
,
so that equation (
1.5
) reads as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{dX_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)=\left[AX_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)+b(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s))\right]\,ds+\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\sigma(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s),\mathbb{E}(g(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(t))|\mathscr{F}_{s}))\,dW_{s},}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(0)=x.}\end{array}\right.

(1.10)

Setting
Y^{t,x}(s):=\mathbb{E}(g(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(t))|\mathscr{F}_{s}))
, the equation above can be further rewritten as a coupled forward backward infinite dimensional stochastic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{dX_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)=\left[AX_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)+b(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s))\right]\,ds+\sqrt{\epsilon}\,\sigma(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s),Y^{t,x}(s)))\,dW_{s},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t}\\[10.0pt]

\displaystyle{Y^{t,x}(t)=g(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(t))}\\[10.0pt]

Coupled forward-backward systems of stochastic equations of the general form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{dX(s)=b(X(s),Y(s),Z(s))ds+\sigma(X(s),Y(s))\,dW_{s},\qquad 0\leq s\leq t}\\[10.0pt]

\displaystyle{Y(t)=g(X(t))}\\[10.0pt]

where several results are collected. Since

Different techniques have been developed to prove existence and uniqueness both locally in time and in arbitrarily long time intervals. In particular the classical theory of PDEs, applied to the corresponding nonlinear Kolmogorov equations, offers a wide range of results stating well posedness of system (

[

1.11

It seems that the techniques more likely to be extended in infinite dimensions are the ones introduced in

We finally notice that, if we show that system (

) by setting

\hat{u}_{\epsilon}(t-s,\xi)=\mathbb{E}(Y^{t,x}(s)|X^{t,x}(s)=\xi)

but, unless we have a satisfactory analytic theory for equation (

\hat{u}_{\epsilon}
is the unique solution of (
1.11

Once such relation would be understood, it could also be possible to study the large deviations principle, see below, for more general nonlinear perturbations of (

) defined through systems like (

As we mentioned at the beginning of this introduction, we are interested in applying our results to the study of the asymptotic behavior of (

) and (
1.7
), as
\epsilon\downarrow 0
. This is a multi-step project and here we are addressing the problem of the validity of a large deviation principle for the trajectories of the solutions of equation (

\{X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}\}_{\epsilon\in\,(0,1)}
satisfies a large deviation principle in the space

, which is governed by the action functional

I_{t,x}(X)=\frac{1}{2}\,\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\|\varphi(s)\|_{H}^{2}\,ds\,:\,X(s)=X^{t,x}_{\varphi}(s),\ s\in\,[0,t]\right\},

where
X^{t,x}_{\varphi}
is the unique mild solution of problem

X^{\prime}(s)=AX(s)+b(X(s))+\sigma(X(s),g(Z^{X(s)}(t-s)))\varphi(s),\ \ \ \ X(0)=x,

and for every

Z^{y}(s)=e^{sA}y+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}b(Z^{y}(r))\,dr.

2.1.
Operator spaces

We denote by
\mathscr{L}(H)
the Banach space of all bounded linear operators
A:H\to H
, endowed with the sup-norm

\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}=\sup_{\|x\|_{H}\leq 1}\|Ax\|_{H}.

An operator
A\in\,\mathscr{L}(H)
is symmetric if it coincides with its adjoint
A^{\star}
, that is if
\langle Ax,y\rangle_{H}=\langle x,Ay\rangle_{H}
, for all
x,y\in\,H
. Moreover, it is non-negative if
\langle Ax,x\rangle_{H}\geq 0
, for all
x\in\,H
.
We shall denote by
\mathscr{L}^{+}(H)
the subspace of all non-negative and symmetric operators in
\mathscr{L}(H)
.

An operator
A\in\,\mathscr{L}(H)
is called an
Hilbert-Schmidt
operator if there exists an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
of
H
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|Ae_{i}\|_{H}^{2}<\infty.

The subspace of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, denoted by
\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)
, is a Hilbert space, endowed with the scalar product

\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle Ae_{i},Be_{i}\rangle_{H}.

As know, for every
B\in\,\mathscr{L}^{+}(H)
there exists a unique
C\in\,\mathscr{L}(H)
, denoted by
\sqrt{B}
such that
C^{2}=B
. Thus, for any
A\in\,\mathscr{L}(H)
we can define

|A|:=\sqrt{A^{\star}A}.

We recall that an operator
A\in\,\mathscr{L}(H)
is compact if and only if
|A|
is compact. Moreover, if
A
is a symmetric compact operator, then there exists an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
of
H
and a sequence
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
converging to zero such that
Ae_{i}=\alpha_{i}e_{i}
, for all
i\in\,\mathbb{N}
. With these notations, we say that a compact operator
A\in\,\mathscr{L}(H)
is
nuclear
or
trace-class
if there exists an orthonormal basis of
H
consisting of eigenvectors of
|A|
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}<\infty.

In particular, if the operator
A
is symmetric, it is nuclear if and only if there exists an orthonormal basis of
H
consisting of eigenvectors of
A
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{i}|<\infty.

We denote by
\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
the set of nuclear operators.

It is possible to prove that for every
A\in\,\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
the series

\text{Tr}A:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle Ae_{i},e_{i}\rangle_{H}

does not depend on the choice of the orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, a symmetric operator
A
belongs to
\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
if and only if the series above converges absolutely for every orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
. The space
\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
is a Banach space, endowed with the norm

\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}=\text{Tr}\,|A|,

and

|\text{Tr}A|\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}.

(2.1)

It is possible to prove that
\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)\subset\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)\subset\mathscr{L}(H)
with

\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)},

and for
j=1,2
it holds

\|AB\|_{\mathscr{L}_{j}(H)}\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}_{j}(H)}\|B\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)},\ \ \ \ \|AB\|_{\mathscr{L}_{j}(H)}\leq\|B\|_{\mathscr{L}_{j}(H)}\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}.

Moreover, if
A,B\in\,\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)
, then
AB\in\,\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
, with

\|AB\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\|B\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}.

2.2.
Functional spaces

(1)

(1)

Now, we want to see how classical interpolatory estimates for functions defined on

are still valid for functions defined on the infinite dimensional Hilbert space
H
. To this purpose, we recall that, as shown in
[
6
, Theorem 2.3.5]
, for every
0\leq\alpha<\beta<\gamma
there exists a constant
c=c(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)>0
such that for every
\varphi\in\,C^{\gamma}_{b}(H)

\|\varphi\|_{\beta}\leq c\,\|\varphi\|_{\alpha}^{\frac{\gamma-\beta}{\gamma-\alpha}}\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{\gamma-\alpha}}.

(2.2)

However, in what follows we will need the following additional interpolatory estimates.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let us fix
\vartheta\in\,(0,1)
. Then, for every
\varphi\in\,C^{1}_{b}(H)
we have

[\varphi]_{\vartheta}\leq c_{1,\vartheta}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{1-\vartheta}\,\|D\varphi\|_{0}^{\vartheta}.

(2.3)

Moreover, for every
\varphi\in\,C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H)
we have

\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{0}\leq c_{2,\vartheta}\,\|D\varphi\|_{0}^{\frac{\vartheta}{1+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{1}{1+\vartheta}},

(2.4)

and

\|D\varphi\|_{0}\leq c_{3,\vartheta}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{\frac{1+\vartheta}{2+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{1}{2+\vartheta}}.

(2.5)

Proof.

Let us fix
\varphi\in\,C^{1}_{b}(H)
and
x,y\in\,H
. Then, for every
\vartheta\in\,(0,1)
we have

|\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)|\leq 2\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{1-\vartheta}\,\left|\int_{0}^{1}\langle D\varphi(x+\lambda y),y\rangle_{H}\,d\lambda\right|^{\vartheta}\leq 2\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{1-\vartheta}\,\|D\varphi\|_{0}^{\vartheta}\|y\|_{H}^{\vartheta},

so that (
2.3
) follows.

Now, if we fix
\varphi\in\,C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H)
, for every
\mu>0
and
x,z\in\,H
, with
\|z\|_{H}=1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\varphi(x+\mu z)=}&\displaystyle{\varphi(x)+\mu\,\langle D\varphi(x),z\rangle_{H}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\langle D^{2}\varphi(x)z,z\rangle_{H}}\\[10.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+\mu^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\langle[D^{2}\varphi(x+r\mu z)-D^{2}\varphi(x)]z,z\rangle_{H}\,dr.}\end{array}

(2.6)

By proceeding as in
[
6
, proof of Theorem 2.3.5]
, we use (
2.6
) to prove (
2.4
). Actually, thanks to (
2.6
) we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\left|\langle D^{2}\varphi(x)z,z\rangle_{H}\right|}&\displaystyle{\leq\left|\varphi(x+\mu z)-\varphi(x)-\mu\,\langle D\varphi(x),z\rangle_{H}\right|+\mu^{2+\vartheta}[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)r^{\vartheta}\,dr,}\end{array}

so that

\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{0}\leq\frac{2}{\mu}\,\|D\varphi\|_{0}+c_{\vartheta}\,\mu^{\vartheta}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta},\ \ \ \ \mu>0.

If we take the minimum over
\mu>0
, we get
(
2.4
).

Finally, by using again (
2.6
), we have

\mu\,\left|\langle D\varphi(x),z\rangle_{H}\right|\leq\left|\varphi(x+\mu z)-\varphi(x)\right|+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\left|\langle D^{2}\varphi(x)z,z\rangle_{H}\right|+\mu^{2+\vartheta}[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)r^{\vartheta}\,dr,

so that, in view of (
2.4
), we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|D\varphi\|_{0}}&\displaystyle{\leq\frac{2}{\mu}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}+\frac{c_{2,\vartheta}\mu}{2}\,\|D\varphi\|_{0}^{\frac{\vartheta}{1+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{1}{1+\vartheta}}+c_{\vartheta}\mu^{1+\vartheta}[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}}\\[10.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq\frac{2}{\mu}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\|D\varphi\|_{0}+c_{\vartheta}\mu^{1+\vartheta}[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

This implies that

\|D\varphi\|_{0}\leq\frac{4}{\mu}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}+c_{\vartheta}\mu^{1+\vartheta}[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta},\ \ \ \ \ \mu>0,

and if we minimize once again with respect to
\mu>0
we obtain (
2.5
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

As a consequence of (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have that for every
\vartheta\in\,(0,1)
there exists some
c_{\vartheta}>0
such that for every
\varphi\in\,C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H)

[\varphi]_{\vartheta}\,\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{0}\leq c_{\vartheta}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}.

(2.7)

Actually, from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have

\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{0}\leq c_{2,\vartheta}\left(c_{3,\vartheta}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{\frac{1+\vartheta}{2+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{1}{2+\vartheta}}\right)^{\frac{\vartheta}{1+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{1}{1+\vartheta}}\leq c_{2,\vartheta}\,c_{3,\vartheta}^{\frac{1}{1+\vartheta}}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{\frac{\vartheta}{2+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{2}{2+\vartheta}}.

(2.8)

Moreover, thanks to (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) we have

[\varphi]_{\vartheta}\leq c_{1,\vartheta}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{1-\vartheta}\,\left(c_{3,\vartheta}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{\frac{1+\vartheta}{2+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{1}{2+\vartheta}}\right)^{\vartheta}=c_{1,\vartheta}\,c_{3,\vartheta}^{\vartheta}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}^{\frac{2}{2+\vartheta}}\,[D^{2}\varphi]_{\vartheta}^{\frac{\vartheta}{2+\vartheta}}.

Therefore, if we combine together this last inequality with (
2.8
), we obtain (
2.7
).

2.3.
The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup

By following
[
6
, Chapter 6]
, we recall here some results about the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup and the associated Kolmogorov equation.

Let
A:D(A)\subset H\to H
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
e^{tA}
and let
Q
be an operator in
\mathscr{L}^{+}(H)
. For every
t\geq 0
we define

Q_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{sA}Qe^{sA^{\star}}\,ds,

and we assume that
Q_{t}\in\,\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
, for every
t\geq 0
. Thus, we can introduce the centered Gaussian measure
\mathscr{N}_{Q_{t}}
defined on
H
with covariance
Q_{t}
, and we can define

R_{t}\varphi(x):=\int_{H}\varphi(e^{tA}x+y)\,\mathscr{N}_{Q_{t}}(dy),\ \ \ \ \ x\in\,H,\ \ \ t\geq 0,

(2.9)

for every
\varphi
in
B_{b}(H)
.

R_{t}
is the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
semigroup associated with
A
and
Q
.
In what follows, we assume that

e^{tA}(H)\subset Q_{t}^{1/2}(H),\ \ \ \ t>0.

(2.10)

and we define

\Lambda_{t}:=Q_{t}^{-1/2}e^{tA},\ \ \ \ t>0.

As shown e.g. in
[
6
, Theorem 6.2.2]
, as a consequence of assumption (
2.10
) we have that
R_{t}\varphi\in\,C^{\infty}_{b}(H)
, for every
\varphi\in\,B_{b}(H)
and
t>0
, and for every
n\in\,\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
there exists some
c_{n}>0
such that

\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{0}\leq c_{n}\,\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}.

(2.11)

Moreover, if we fix
\alpha\in\,(0,1)
and assume
\varphi\in\,C^{\alpha}_{b}(H)
we have

[D^{n}R_{t}\varphi]_{\alpha}\leq c_{n}\,\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n}\,\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{\alpha}\,[\varphi]_{\alpha},

(2.12)

so that we conclude that for all
\alpha\in\,[0,1)
and
\varphi\in\,C^{\alpha}_{b}(H)

\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{n}\,\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n}\,\|\varphi\|_{t,\alpha},\ \ \ \ \ t>0,

(2.13)

where, for every
t\geq 0
and
\alpha\in\,(0,1)
,

\|\varphi\|_{t,\alpha}:=\left(\|\varphi\|_{0}+\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{\alpha}\,[\varphi]_{\alpha}\right),

(2.14)

By the interpolation inequality (
2.2
), for every
n\in\,\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta<1
and for every
\varphi\in\,C^{\beta}_{b}(H)
and
t>0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{1+\alpha}}&\displaystyle{\leq\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{\beta}^{\beta-\alpha}\,\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{1+\beta}^{1-(\beta-\alpha)}}\\[10.0pt]
&\displaystyle{=\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{\beta}^{\beta-\alpha}\left(\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{0}+\|D^{n+1}R_{t}\varphi\|_{\beta}\right)^{1-(\beta-\alpha)}.}\end{array}

Hence,
thanks to (
2.13
), we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{1+\alpha}}&\displaystyle{\leq c_{n}^{\,\beta-\alpha}\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n(\beta-\alpha)}\,\|\varphi\|_{t,\beta}^{\beta-\alpha}\left(c_{n}\,\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n}\,\|\varphi\|_{0}+c_{n+1}\,\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n+1}\|\varphi\|_{t,\beta}\right)^{1-(\beta-\alpha)}}\\[10.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c_{\alpha,\beta,n}\,\|\varphi\|_{t,\beta}\left(\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n}+\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n+1-(\beta-\alpha)}\right).}\end{array}

In particular, this allows to conclude that for every
n\in\,\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
and for every
\varphi\in\,C_{b}^{\beta}(H)

\|D^{n}R_{t}\varphi\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{\alpha,\beta,n}\,\left(\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n-1}+\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{n-(\beta-\alpha)}\right)\,\|\varphi\|_{t,\beta}.

(2.15)

Next, we recall that in
[
6
, Proposition 6.2.9]
it is shown that for every
\varphi\in\,C^{1}_{b}(H)
and
x\in\,H

\text{Tr}\,[QD^{2}R_{t}\varphi(x)]=\int_{H}\langle Q_{t}^{-1/2}y,\Lambda_{t}Qe^{tA^{\star}}D\varphi(e^{tA}x+y)\rangle_{H}\,\mathscr{N}_{Q_{t}}(dy),

so that, if we assume that

\Lambda_{t}Qe^{tA^{\star}}\in\,\mathscr{L}_{2}(H),

(2.16)

we have

\sup_{x\in\,H}\|QD^{2}R_{t}\varphi(x)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\leq\|\Lambda_{t}Qe^{tA^{\star}}\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\,\|D\varphi\|_{0},\ \ \ \ \ t>0.

Therefore, thanks to the semigroup law and (
2.15
) we conclude that for every
\varphi\in\,C^{\beta}_{b}(H)

\sup_{x\in\,H}\|QD^{2}R_{t}\varphi(x)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\leq c\,\|\Lambda_{t/2}Qe^{tA^{\star}/2}\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\|\Lambda_{t/2}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{1-\beta}\|\varphi\|_{\beta},\ \ \ \ \ t>0.

(2.17)

Moreover, we recall that in
[
6
, Proposition 6.2.5]
it is shown that if the operator
\Lambda_{t}A
has a continuous extension
\overline{\Lambda_{t}A}
to
H
, for every
t>0
, then for every
\varphi\in\,B_{b}(H)
and
x\in\,H

DR_{t}\varphi(x)\in\,D(A^{\star}),\ \ \ \ \|A^{\star}DR_{t}\varphi\|_{0}\leq\|\overline{\Lambda_{t}A}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\,\|\varphi\|_{0},\ \ \ \ t>0.

(2.18)

Now, we introduce the parabolic equation in
H

D_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\left[QD_{x}^{2}u(t,x)\right]+\langle x,A^{\star}D_{x}u(t,x)\rangle_{H},\ \ \ \ \ u(0,x)=\varphi(x).

(2.19)

Definition 1
.

A function
u:[0,+\infty)\times H\to\mathbb{R}
is a classical solution of problem (
2.19
) if

1.

u
is continuous in
[0,+\infty)\times H
and
u(0,\cdot)=\varphi
.

2.

u(t,\cdot)\in\,C^{2}_{b}(H)
, for all
t>0
, and
QD^{2}_{x}u(t,x)\in\,\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
, for all
t>0
and
x\in\,H
.

3.

D_{x}u(t,x)\in\,D(A^{\star})
, for all
t>0
and
x\in\,H
.

4.

u(\cdot,x)
is differentiable in
(0,+\infty)
for every
x\in\,H
and
u
satisfies equation (
2.19
).

In
[
6
, Theorem 6.2.4]
it is shown that if we assume conditions (
2.10
) and (
2.16
) and we assume that the operator
\Lambda_{t}A
has a continuous extension to
H
, then for every
\varphi\in\,B_{b}(H)
the function

u(t,x)=R_{t}\varphi(x)

is the unique classical solution of equation (
2.19
).

3.
Assumptions and main results

3.1.
Assumptions

In what follows, we shall make the following hypotheses.

Hypothesis 1
.

(1)

The mapping
\sigma:H\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{L}(H)
is Lipschitz continuous and there exist an operator
Q\in\,\mathscr{L}^{+}(H)
, a continuous mapping
f:H\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
and a constant
\delta>0
such that

\sigma^{\star}\sigma(x,r)=Q+\delta\,f(x,r),\ \ \ \ x\in\,H,\ \ r\in\,\mathbb{R}.

(3.1)

(2)

For every fixed
x\in\,H
, the function
f(x,\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
is differentiable. Both
f
and
\partial_{r}f
are Lipschitz continuous in both variables, uniformly with respect to the other. Moreover

\sup_{x\in\,H}\|f(x,r)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\leq c\left(1+|r|\right),\ \ \ \ \ \ r\in\,\mathbb{R}.

(3.2)

Remark 3.1
.

Let
H=L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
, for some smooth and bounded domain
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\geq 1
. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
and let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-negative real numbers. We assume that
e_{i}\in\,L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})
, for every
i\in\,\mathbb{N}
, and

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\,\|e_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})}<\infty.

(3.3)

For every
x,y\in\,H
and
r\in\,\mathbb{R}
, we define

[f(x,r)y](\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{f}_{i}(x(\xi),r)\lambda_{i}\langle y,e_{i}\rangle_{H}e_{i}(\xi),\ \ \ \ \ \xi\in\,\mathscr{O},

for some continuous functions
\mathfrak{f}_{i}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathfrak{f}_{i}(s,\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable, for every
s\in\,\mathbb{R}
and
i\in\,\mathbb{N}
. We assume that both
\mathfrak{f}_{i}
and
\partial_{r}\mathfrak{f}_{i}
are Lipschitz continuous in both variables, uniformly with respect to the other variable, and uniformly with respect to
i\in\,\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, we assume that

\sup_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}\,\sup_{s\in\,\mathbb{R}}|\mathfrak{f}_{i}(s,r)|\leq c\left(1+|r|\right),\ \ \ \ \ \ r\in\,\mathbb{R}.

(3.4)

With this choice of
H
and
f
, we have that condition 2 in Hypothesis
1
holds.

Actually, since
\mathfrak{f}_{i}(\cdot,r):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous, uniformly with respect to
r\in\,\mathbb{R}
and
i\in\,\mathbb{N}
, for every
x,y\in\,H
and
r\in\,\mathbb{R}
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\langle\left[f(x,r)-f(y,r)\right]e_{i},e_{i}\rangle_{H}|=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\,|\langle\left[\mathfrak{f}_{i}(x(\cdot),r)-\mathfrak{f}_{i}(y(\cdot),r)\right]e_{i},e_{i}\rangle_{H}|}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|\mathfrak{f}_{i}(x(\cdot),r)-\mathfrak{f}_{i}(y(\cdot),r)\|_{H}\|e_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})}\lambda_{i}\leq c\,\|x-y\|_{H}\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\|e_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})}.}\end{array}

In particular, thanks to (
3.3
), we can conclude that
f(\cdot,r):H\to\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
is Lipschitz continuous, uniformly with respect to
r\in\,\mathbb{R}
. In view of our assumptions, the same is true for
\partial_{r}f
.

The Lipschitz continuity of
f(x,\cdot),\ \partial_{r}f(x,\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
, uniform with respect to
x\in\,H
, is proved in a similar way. However, in this case (
3.3
) is not required and we only need the weaker condition

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}<\infty.

Finally, (
3.2
) is an immediate consequence of (
3.4
).

Now, we see some consequences of Hypothesis
1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any function
\varphi:H\to\mathbb{R}
we define

F(\varphi)(x)=f(x,\varphi(x)),\ \ \ \ x\in\,H.

(3.5)

Then, under Hypothesis
1
we have that
F
maps
C^{\vartheta}_{b}(H)
into
C^{\vartheta}_{b}(H;\mathscr{L}_{1}(H))
and for every
\varphi\in\,C^{\vartheta}_{b}(H)

\|F(\varphi)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\left(1+\|\varphi\|_{\vartheta}\right).

(3.6)

Moreover for every
\varphi_{1},\ \varphi_{2}\in\,C^{\vartheta}_{b}(H)
it holds

\|F(\varphi_{1})-F(\varphi_{2})\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\left(1+\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\vartheta}+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\vartheta}\right)\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\vartheta}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Due to (
3.2
), if
\varphi\in\,C^{\vartheta}_{b}(H)
we have

\|F(\varphi)\|_{0}\leq\sup_{x\in\,H}\|f(x,\varphi(x))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\leq c\left(1+\|\varphi\|_{0}\right).

(3.8)

Moreover,
for every
x,y\in\,H

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|f(x,\varphi(x))-f(y,\varphi(y))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq\|f(x,\varphi(x))-f(y,\varphi(x))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}^{\vartheta}\,\|f(x,\varphi(x))-f(y,\varphi(x))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}^{1-\vartheta}}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\|f(y,\varphi(x))-f(y,\varphi(y))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\leq c\,\|x-y\|_{H}^{\vartheta}\,\left(1+\|\varphi\|_{0}^{1-\vartheta}\right)+c\,|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\|x-y\|_{H}^{\vartheta}\,\left(1+\|\varphi\|_{0}^{1-\vartheta}+[\varphi]_{\vartheta}\right)\leq c\,\|x-y\|_{H}^{\vartheta}\,\left(1+\|\varphi\|_{0}+[\varphi]_{\vartheta}\right),}\end{array}

so that

[F(\varphi)]_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\left(1+\|\varphi\|_{0}+[\varphi]_{\vartheta}\right).

(3.9)

This, together with (
3.8
) allows to conclude that
F(\varphi)\in\,C^{\vartheta}_{b}(H)
and (
3.6
) holds.

Concerning (
3.7
), for every
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\,C_{b}^{\vartheta}(H)
we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|F(\varphi_{1})-F(\varphi_{2})\|_{0}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\sup_{x\in\,H}\|f(x,\varphi_{1}(x))-f(x,\varphi_{2}(x))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\,\leq c\,\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{0}.}\end{array}

(3.10)

Moreover, for every
x,y\in\,H
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{(f(x,\varphi_{1}(x))-f(x,\varphi_{2}(x)))-(f(y,\varphi_{1}(y))-f(y,\varphi_{2}(y)))}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\gamma(s\varphi_{1}+(1-s)\varphi_{2})(x)(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})(x)-\gamma(s\varphi_{1}+(1-s)\varphi_{2})(y)(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})(y)\right]\,ds,}\end{array}

where we have defined

\gamma(\varphi)(x)=\partial_{r}f(x,\varphi(x)),\ \ \ \ \ x\in\,H.

This implies that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{[f(\cdot,\varphi_{1})-f(\cdot,\varphi_{2})]_{\vartheta}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|\gamma(s\varphi_{1}+(1-s)\varphi_{2})\|_{\vartheta}\,ds\,\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

Since, we are assuming that
\partial_{r}f
, like
f
, is Lipschitz continuous with respect to each variable, uniformly with respect to the other, and is clearly uniformly bounded, by using the same arguments we have used to prove (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have

\|\gamma(s\varphi_{1}+(1-s)\varphi_{2})\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\left(1+s\,\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\vartheta}+(1-s)\,\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\vartheta}\right),

and hence

[F(\varphi_{1})-F(\varphi_{2})]_{\vartheta}\leq c\left(1+\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\vartheta}+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\vartheta}\right)\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{\vartheta}.

This, together with (
3.10
), implies (
3.7
).

∎

Hypothesis 2
.

(1)

The operator
A:D(A)\subset H\to H
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup

(3.11)

(2)

If
Q

we have that
Q_{t}\in\,\mathscr{L}^{+}_{1}(H)
, for every

.

(3)

For every
t>0
, we have

(3.12)

(4)

If we define

\Lambda_{t}:=Q_{t}^{-1/2}e^{tA},\ \ \ \ t>0,

there exists some
\lambda>0
such that

\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq c\,(t\wedge 1)^{-1/2}e^{-\lambda t},\ \ \ \ \ t>0.

(3.13)

(5)

For every
t>0
we have that
\Lambda_{t}Qe^{tA^{\star}}\in\,\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)
and for every
\vartheta\in\,(0,1)
there exist
\beta_{\vartheta}<1
and
\alpha_{\vartheta}>0
such that

\kappa_{\vartheta}(t):=\|\Lambda_{t}Qe^{tA^{\star}}\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\|\Lambda_{t}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{1-\vartheta}\leq c\,(t\wedge 1)^{-\beta_{\vartheta}}e^{-\alpha_{\vartheta}t},\ \ \ \ t>0.

(3.14)

Hypothesis 3
.

For every
(x,r)\in\,H\times\mathbb{R}
and
t>0
we have

e^{tA}\sigma(x,r)\in\,\mathscr{L}_{2}(H).

Moreover,

\|e^{tA}\sigma(x,r)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\leq c\,(t\wedge 1)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\,\left(1+\|x\|_{H}+|r|\right),\ \ \ \ t>0,

and for every

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in\,\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis in
H
and assume that
Ae_{i}=-\alpha_{i}e_{i}
and
Qe_{i}=\gamma_{i}\,e_{i}
, for every
i\in\,\mathbb{N}
, with
\alpha_{i},\ \gamma_{i}>0
, and
\alpha_{i}\uparrow+\infty
, as
i\to\infty
. By proceeding as in
[
6
, Example 6.2.11]
, we have that

If we assume that

and Condition (5) in Hypothesis

Notice also that in this case Hypothesis

(4.1)

For every
\epsilon\in\,(0,1)
,
s\in\,[0,t]
and

(4.2)

In view of (

(5.2)

for some

\|R^{\epsilon}_{t}\varphi\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{\alpha,\beta}\,\epsilon^{-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}\|\varphi\|_{t,\beta},\ \ \ \ t>0,\ \ \epsilon\in\,(0,1).

(5.5)

(5.6)

Moreover, for every
v_{1},v_{2}\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
and
\delta>0

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},s)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\delta\,R(1+R)\,(s\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\|v_{1}(s,\cdot)-v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}\,}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+c\,\epsilon\,\delta(1+R)\,\|D^{2}_{x}v_{1}(s,\cdot)-D^{2}_{x}v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+c\,\|D_{x}v_{1}(s,\cdot)-D_{x}v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

(5.7)

Proof.

In view of (
3.6
) and Hypothesis
3
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)\|_{\vartheta}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\,\|F(v(s,\cdot))\|_{\vartheta}\,\|D_{x}^{2}v(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+\|b\|_{\vartheta}\,\|D_{x}v(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}}\\[10.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|v(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}\right)\|D^{2}_{x}v(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+c\,\|D_{x}v(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta},}\end{array}

and since we are assuming that
v\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
, this implies (
5.6
).

Next, if
v_{1},v_{2}\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
and
\delta>0
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},s)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\,\|F(v_{1}(s,\cdot))-F(v_{1}(s,\cdot))\|_{\vartheta}\,\|D_{x}^{2}v_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+c\,\epsilon\,\delta\,\|F(v_{2}(s,\cdot))\|_{\vartheta}\,\|D_{x}^{2}v_{1}(s,\cdot)-D_{x}^{2}v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+\|b\|_{\vartheta}\,\|D_{x}v_{1}(s,\cdot)-D_{x}v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

Thus, according to (
3.6
) and (
3.7
),

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},s)\|_{\vartheta}}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\,\|v_{1}(s,\cdot)-v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}\left(1+\|v_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+\|v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}\right)\|D^{2}_{x}v_{1}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}\right)\|D^{2}_{x}v_{1}(s,\cdot)-D^{2}_{x}v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+c\,\|D_{x}v_{1}(s,\cdot)-D_{x}v_{2}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

Recalling that
v_{1},v_{2}\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
, this implies (
5.7
).

∎

Remark 5.2
.

If for every fixed
\epsilon,\delta>0
we define

\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s):=\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\delta R(1+R)(s\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}+\epsilon^{-\varrho}R(s\wedge 1)^{-\varrho},\ \ \ \ \ s>0,\ \ \ R>0,

and

\mathfrak{a}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s):=\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\delta(1+R)^{2}(s\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}+\epsilon^{-\varrho}(s\wedge 1)^{-\varrho},\ \ \ \ \ s>0,\ \ \ R>0,

due (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) we have that for every
v,v_{1},v_{2}\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
and
s\in\,(0,T]

\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s),

(5.8)

and

\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},s)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\mathfrak{a}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}.

(5.9)

Notice that for all
\beta<1
and
\mu>0
and for all
t\geq 0

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\beta}e^{-\mu(t-s)}\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\delta R(1+R)\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\beta}e^{-\mu(t-s)}(s\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\,ds}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\epsilon^{-\varrho}R\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\beta}e^{-\mu(t-s)}(s\wedge 1)^{-\varrho}\,ds,}\end{array}

and this implies that there exists some constant
c>0
only dependent on
\beta
and
\mu
such that

\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\beta}e^{-\mu(t-s)}\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds\leq c\,(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}-(\varrho+\beta)}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(T,t),

(5.10)

where

\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t):=\delta R(1+R)\,+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}R\,(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In an analogous way

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta}e^{-\mu(t-s)}\mathfrak{a}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds\leq c\,(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}-(\varrho+\beta)}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t),

(5.11)

where

\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t):=\delta(1+R)^{2}\,+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next we prove the following estimates for
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}
on
\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
v\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
and
\epsilon,\delta\in\,(0,1)
it holds

\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\leq c\,\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,T)\left[\epsilon^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}\,(T\wedge 1)^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}\,(T\vee 1)+1\right].

(5.12)

Proof.

Step 1.
We have

\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{\eta}\leq c\,\epsilon^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\,(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}(t\vee 1),\ \ \ \ \ t\in\,[0,T].

(5.13)

Proof of Step 1.
In view of (
5.5
), for every
t\in\,[0,T]

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{\eta}}&\displaystyle{\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)\|_{\eta}\,ds\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[14.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds.}\end{array}

Then, by adapting (
5.10
) to the case
\mu=0
, we get

\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{\eta}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}\,(t\vee 1),

and (
5.13
) follows.

Step 2.
We have

(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\,\|D\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\varrho}\left[\delta\,R(1+R)+\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}R(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right].

(5.14)

Proof of Step 2.
According to (
5.2
), we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}\|DR^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds\leq c\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds.}\end{array}

Then, thanks to (
5.8
) and (
5.10
) we conclude

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}\|DR^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{-\varrho}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t),}\end{array}

and (
5.14
) follows.

Step 3.

We have

(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varrho)}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(T,t).

(5.15)

Proof of Step 3.
By proceeding as in the proof of Step 2, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{0}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\epsilon^{-1+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-1+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}(t\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t),}\end{array}

and this implies

(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{0}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varrho)+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{\vartheta}{2}}.

(5.16)

Now, for every
x,h\in\,H
and
t\in\,[0,T]
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t,x+h)-D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t,x)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varrho)+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\,(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{\vartheta}{2}}.}\end{array}

Hence, if we assume that
\|h\|_{H}^{2}>\epsilon\,t/2

we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t,x+h)-D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t,x)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varrho)}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\,\|h\|_{H}^{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

(5.17)

When
\|h\|_{H}^{2}\leq\epsilon\,t/2
we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t,x)}&\displaystyle{=\int_{0}^{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)(x)\,ds+\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)(x)\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{=:a_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x)+b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x).}\end{array}

Due to (
5.3
), from (
5.8
) we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{|a_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x+h)-a_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x)|\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}(s\wedge 1)^{-1+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds\,}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{-1+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}s^{-1+\frac{\vartheta}{2}}e^{-\lambda s}\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varrho)}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\,(t\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\,\|h\|_{H}^{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

(5.18)

As for
b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,\cdot)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x+h)-b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x)=\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}\left[D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)(x+h)-D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)(x)\right]\,ds.}\end{array}

Hence, due again to (
5.3
), we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x+h)-b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}\|D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)(x+h)-D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)(x)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,t-s)\|_{3}\,ds\|h\|_{H}}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{3-\vartheta}{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}(s\wedge 1)^{-\frac{3-\vartheta}{2}}e^{-2\lambda s}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t-s)\,ds.}\end{array}

Since we assuming
\|h\|_{H}^{2}\leq\epsilon\,t/2
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}(s\wedge 1)^{-\frac{3-\vartheta}{2}}e^{-2\lambda s}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})})\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{=\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t/2}(s\wedge 1)^{-\frac{3-\vartheta}{2}}e^{-2\lambda s}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\,ds+\int_{t/2}^{t}s^{-\frac{3-\vartheta}{2}}e^{-2\lambda s}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,t^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\,\|h\|_{H}^{-1+\vartheta}+c\,t^{-\frac{3-\vartheta}{2}}\,t^{\frac{1}{2}-\varrho}=c\,t^{-\varrho}\left(\epsilon^{\frac{1-\vartheta}{2}}\|h\|_{H}^{-1+\vartheta}+t^{-\frac{1-\vartheta}{2}}\right)}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{\frac{1-\vartheta}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\,\|h\|_{H}^{-1+\vartheta}.}\end{array}

Moreover, in the same way we have

\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}s^{-\frac{3-\vartheta}{2}}e^{-2\lambda s}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\varrho}\,ds\leq c\,\epsilon^{\frac{1-\vartheta}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{-\varrho}\,\|h\|_{H}^{-1+\vartheta},

so that

(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\,\|b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x+h)-b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varrho)}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\|h\|_{H}^{\vartheta}.

This, together with (
5.18
) and (
5.17
), implies that for every
h\in\,H

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t,x+h)-D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t,x)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(\frac{1}{2}+\varrho)}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\,\|h\|_{H}^{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

Thus, thanks to (
5.16
), we obtain (
5.15
).

Conclusion.
Estimate (
5.12
) is a consequence of (
5.13
), (
5.14
) and (
5.15
).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

From the proof of the previous lemma, we easily see that for every
t\in\,(0,T]
and
\epsilon\in\,(0,1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\epsilon^{\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}[\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)]_{\eta}+\epsilon^{\varrho}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\|D\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{\vartheta}+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\varrho}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)\|_{\vartheta}}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\left((t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}\epsilon^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}+1\right),}\end{array}

(5.19)

for some constant
c>0
independent of
T>0
. Actually,
in view of (
5.4
) and (
5.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{[\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t)]_{\eta}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\omega_{\vartheta,\eta}(t-s)}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}[\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v,s)]_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[14.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\omega_{\vartheta,\eta}(t-s)}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\alpha_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\left(\epsilon^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}-\varrho}+1\right).}\end{array}

This, together with (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), implies (
5.19
).

Now we are ready to prove the existence of a local mild solution.

Theorem 5.5
.

Fix
\eta>1/2
and
\vartheta\in\,(0,(\eta-1/2)\wedge 1)
and define

\varrho:=\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}.

(5.20)

Then, there exist
\delta_{1},T_{1}>0
such that for every
\epsilon\in\,(0,1)
problem (
3.17
) has a mild solution
u_{\epsilon}
in
C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T_{1}];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
, for every
\delta\leq\delta_{1}
.

Proof.

A function
u_{\epsilon}
is a mild solution of equation (
3.17
) if and only if it is a fixed point for the mapping
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}
defined by

\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v)(t)=R^{\epsilon}_{t}g+\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v)(t),\ \ \ \ t\in\,[0,T].

Thus, we will prove the existence of a local mild solution for equation (
3.17
) by showing that there exist some
T_{1},R>0
and
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}
maps
\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\vartheta,T_{1}}
into itself as a contraction, for every
\delta\leq\delta_{1}
.

Thanks to (
5.5
) we have

\|R^{\epsilon}_{t}g\|_{\eta}\leq c\,\|g\|_{\eta}.

(5.21)

Moreover, thanks to (
5.3
)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|DR^{\epsilon}_{t}g(v)(t)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{-\frac{1-(\eta-\vartheta)}{2}}\,e^{-\lambda t}\,\|g\|_{\eta},}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{\|D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(v)(t)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\epsilon^{-(1-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2})}(t\wedge 1)^{-(1-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2})}\,e^{-2\lambda t}\,\|g\|_{\eta}.}\end{array}

(5.22)

Therefore, if we define
\varrho
as in (
5.20
), we have that
\varrho\in\,(0,1/4)
and from (
5.21
) and (
5.22
) it follows

\|R^{\epsilon}_{t}g\|_{\eta}+\epsilon^{\varrho}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\|DR^{\epsilon}_{t}g(t)\|_{\vartheta}+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\varrho}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(t)\|_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\|g\|_{\eta}.

(5.23)

With
\varrho
defined as in (
5.20
), together with (
5.12
) this implies that
for every
v\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}

\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v)\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\leq c\,\|g\|_{\eta}+c\,\left[\delta\,R(1+R)+\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}R\,(T\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right](T\vee 1).

In particular, if we first take
R:=3c\,\|g\|_{\eta}
and
\delta^{\prime}>0
small enough such that

c\,\delta^{\prime}\,R(1+R)\leq\frac{R}{3},

and then fix
T^{\prime}\leq 1
small enough so that

\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}R(T^{\prime}\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{R}{3},

we conclude that for every
\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}
and
T\leq T^{\prime}

\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v)\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\leq R,

so that
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}
maps
\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
into itself.

Now, if we fix
v_{1},v_{2}\in\,\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{1})-\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{2})\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\leq c\int_{0}^{T}(t-s)^{-\frac{\eta-\vartheta}{2}}\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\epsilon^{\varrho}\sup_{t\in\,(0,T]}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\varrho}\sup_{t\in\,(0,T]}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon^{-(1-\frac{\vartheta}{2})}(t-s)^{-(1-\frac{\vartheta}{2})}e^{-2\lambda(t-s)}\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\varrho}\sup_{t\in\,(0,T]}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\,[D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{1})(t)-D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{2})(t)]_{\vartheta}=:\sum_{i=1}^{4}I_{\delta,i}(\epsilon).}\end{array}

Then, according to (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{I_{\delta,1}(\epsilon)\leq c\,\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,T)\,\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}.}\end{array}

(5.24)

In the same way,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{I_{\delta,2}(\epsilon)}&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{\varrho}\sup_{t\in\,(0,T]}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\mathfrak{a}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds\,\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,T)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}.}\end{array}

(5.25)

and

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{I_{\delta,3}(\epsilon)}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}+\varrho}\sup_{t\in\,(0,T]}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\epsilon^{-(1-\frac{\vartheta}{2})}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-(1-\frac{\vartheta}{2})}e^{-2\lambda(t-s)}\mathfrak{a}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds\,\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{\frac{\vartheta}{2}}(T\wedge 1)^{\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\,\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,T)\,\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}.}\end{array}

(5.26)

As for
I_{\delta,4}(\epsilon)
, due to (
5.26
), if we fix any
x,h\in\,H
and assume
\|h\|_{H}^{2}>\epsilon\,t/2
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\epsilon^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{1})(t,x+h)-D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{2})(t,x)\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon^{\frac{\vartheta}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{\frac{\vartheta}{2}}\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\leq c\,\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\,\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\|h\|_{H}^{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

(5.27)

On the other hand, if we assume that
\|h\|_{H}^{2}\leq\epsilon\,t/2
we write

D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{1})(t,x)-D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{2})(t,x)=a_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x)+b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x),

where

a_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x):=\int_{0}^{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\left(\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},t-s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},t-s)\right)\,ds,

and

b_{\epsilon,\delta}(h,t,x)=:\int_{\epsilon^{-1}\|h\|_{H}^{2}}^{t}D^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{s}\left(\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{1},t-s)-\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(v_{2},t-s)\right)\,ds.

Then, thanks to (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we can proceed as in Step 3 of the proof of Lemma
5.3
and we obtain that (
5.27
) holds also when
\|h\|_{H}^{2}\leq t/2
. In particular, we obtain that

\epsilon^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\,[D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{1})(t)-D^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{2})(t)]_{\vartheta}\leq c\,\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T},

so that

I_{\delta,4}(\epsilon)\leq c\,\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}.

(5.28)

Therefore, if we combine (
5.24
), (
5.25
), (
5.26
) and (
5.28
), we obtain that

\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{1})-\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{g}(v_{2})\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\leq c\,\mathfrak{l}_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,t)\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}.

This means that if we first choose
\delta_{1}\leq\delta^{\prime}
such that

c\,\delta_{1}\,(1+R)^{2}<\frac{1}{2},

and then

T_{1}\leq T^{\prime}
such that

c\,\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,(T_{1}\wedge 1)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\frac{1}{2},

we can conclude that
\Gamma_{\delta,g}
maps
\mathscr{Y}^{R}_{\varrho,\vartheta,T_{1}}
into itself as a contraction, for every
\delta\leq\delta_{1}.

∎

6.
Further properties of mild solutions of the quasi-linear problem

We will show that any mild solution
u_{\epsilon}
of equation (
3.17
) that belongs to
C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
is in fact a classical solution in the sense of Definition
2
. Moreover, by using its probabilistic interpretation in terms of equation (
3.19
), we will prove that a maximum principle holds for equation (
3.17
). This will imply that the local mild solution we have found in Section
5
is the unique global classical solution of Theorem
3.4
.

We start by proving that
QD^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)
is a trace-class operator.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every
t\in\,(0,T]
and
x\in\,H
, we have that
QD^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\in\,\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
.

Proof.

If
u_{\epsilon}
is a mild solution, with the notations we have introduced in Section
5
we have

u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(x)+\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(u_{\epsilon})(t,x).

According to (
2.17
) we have that
QD^{2}_{x}R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(t,x)\in\,\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
, for every
\epsilon\in\,(0,1)
,
t>0
and
x\in\,H
, and
thanks to (
2.17
) and (
3.14
)

\|QD^{2}_{x}R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(t)\|_{0}\leq\kappa_{\epsilon,\eta}(t/2)\,\|g\|_{\eta}.

As far as
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(u)
is concerned, if
R=\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}
, thanks to (
2.17
), (
3.14
) and (
5.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|QD^{2}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(u)(t,x)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|QD^{2}_{x}R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(u,s)(x)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)}\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{\epsilon,\vartheta}(t-s)\|\gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(s)\|_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,e^{-\beta_{\vartheta}}\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\beta_{\vartheta}}e^{-\alpha_{\vartheta}\frac{(t-s)}{2}}\epsilon^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}(s\wedge 1)^{-(\frac{1}{+}\varrho)}\lambda_{\epsilon,\delta}(R,s)\,ds\leq c_{\epsilon}(R,T).}\end{array}

This allows to conclude that
QD^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\in\,\mathscr{L}_{1}(H)
for every
\epsilon\in\,(0,1)
,
t\in\,(0,T]
and
x\in\,H
.

∎

Next, we show that
u_{\epsilon}
is differentiable with respect to
t\in\,(0,T]
and
x\in\,D(A)
and is a classical solution of equation (
3.17
). In Subsection
2.3
, we have seen that for every
\varphi\in\,B_{b}(H)
and
x\in\,D(A)
the mapping

t\in\,(0,+\infty)\mapsto R^{\epsilon}_{t}\varphi(x)\in\,\mathbb{R},

is differentiable and

D_{t}R^{\epsilon}_{t}\varphi(x)=\mathscr{L}_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}_{t}\varphi(x).

Hence, since

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}&\displaystyle{=R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(x)+\int_{0}^{t}R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\,[\delta\,F(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]+\langle b(\cdot),Du_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\rangle_{H}\right)(x)\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{=R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(x)+\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(u_{\epsilon})(t,x),}\end{array}

thanks to Lemma
6.1
, for every
x\in\,D(A)
we can differentiate both sides with respect to
t>0
, and we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{D_{t}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}&\displaystyle{=\mathscr{L}_{\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}_{t}g(x)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\,[\delta\,F(u_{\epsilon}(t,x))D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)]+\langle b(x),Du_{\epsilon}(t,x)\rangle_{H}+\mathscr{L}_{\epsilon}\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(t)(t,x)}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{=\mathscr{L}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\,[\delta\,F(u_{\epsilon}(t,x))D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)]+\langle b(x),Du_{\epsilon}(t,x)\rangle_{H}.}\end{array}

Thus, we have proven the following result.

Theorem 6.2
.

Under Hypotheses
1
to
4
, if
u_{\epsilon}
is a mild solution
of equation (
3.17
) that belongs to
C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
, then it is a classical solution.

Next, we show how any solution
of equation (
3.17
) is related to the stochastic PDE (
3.19
).

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume Hypotheses
1
to
4
. Then if
u_{\epsilon}\in\,C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
is a solution of equation (
3.17
) and
X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}\in\,L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,t];H))
is a solution of equation (
3.19
), we have

u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}g(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(t)).

(6.1)

Proof.

The natural way to prove (
6.1
) is by applying the Itô formula to the function
(s,x)\in\,[0,t]\times H\mapsto u_{\epsilon}(t-s,x)
and to the process
X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)
. However, we cannot do this directly first because
u_{\epsilon}
satisfies equation (
3.17
) in classical sense only for
x\in\,D(A)
and second because
X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}
is only a mild solution of equation (
3.19
), and not a strong solution, as required when Itô’s formula is used. To overcome these difficulties, we introduce a suitable approximation of
u_{\epsilon}
and
X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}
, by adapting an argument introduced in
[
7
, Proof of Theorem 9.25]
.

For every
m\in\,\mathbb{N}
we define

J_{m}=m(m-A)^{-1}
and

u_{\epsilon,m}(t,x)=u_{\epsilon}(t,J_{m}x),\ \ \ \ \ \ (t,x)\in\,[0,T]\times H.

Since
J_{m}x\to x
, as
m\to\infty
, and
u_{\epsilon}\in\,C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
,
we have that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{t\in\,[0,T]}\|u_{\epsilon,m}(t,\cdot)-u(t,\cdot)\|_{0}=0.

(6.2)

Moreover,

D_{x}u_{\epsilon,m}(t,x)=J_{m}^{\star}D_{x}u(t,J_{m}x),\ \ \ \ D^{2}_{x}u_{\epsilon,m}(t,x)=J_{m}^{\star}D^{2}_{x}u(t,J_{m}x)J_{m}.

(6.3)

Next, for every
m\in\,\mathbb{N}
we introduce the stochastic PDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{dX_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s)=\left[AX_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s)+J_{m}b(X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s))\right]\,ds+J_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(s,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s))\,dW^{m}_{s},}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{X^{t,x}(0)=J_{m}x,}\end{array}\right.

(6.4)

where
\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}
is the operator introduced in (
4.2
) and

W^{m}_{t}
is the projection of the cylindrical Wiener process
W_{t}
onto
H_{m}:=\text{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
.
By proceeding as in Section
4
, we can prove that equation (
6.4
) admits a unique mild solution
X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}\in\,L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];H))
. Since
J_{m}
maps
H
into
D(A)
and
W^{m}_{t}
is a finite dimensional noise, it is immediate to check that
X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}
is a strong solution. Namely

X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s)=J_{m}x+\int_{0}^{s}AX^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(r)\,dr+\int_{0}^{s}J_{m}b(X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r))\,dr+\int_{0}^{s}J_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r))\,dW^{m}(r).

At the end of this section we will prove that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{s\in\,[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\|X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s)-X^{t,x}(s)\|_{H}=0.

(6.5)

Now we apply Itô’s formula to
u_{\epsilon,m}
and
X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}
and thanks to (
6.3
) we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{d_{s}u_{\epsilon,m}(t-s,X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))=-D_{t}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))\,ds}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\left[J_{m}^{\star}D^{2}_{x}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))J_{m}(J_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r)))(J_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r)))^{\star}\right]\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\langle AJ_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s)+J_{m}^{2}b(X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s)),D_{x}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))\rangle_{H}\,ds}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\langle J^{2}_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s))\,dW^{m}(s),D_{x}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))\rangle_{H}.}\end{array}

Therefore, recalling that
u(t,x)
satisfies equation (
5.1
), for every
x\in\,D(A)
, since
J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s)\in\,D(A)
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{d_{s}u_{\epsilon,m}(t-s,X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{=\langle J^{2}_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s))\,dW^{m}(s),D_{x}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))\rangle_{H}+[I_{m,1}^{\epsilon}(s)+I_{m,2}(s)^{\epsilon}]\,ds,}\end{array}

(6.6)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{I_{m,1}^{\epsilon}(s):=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\left[J_{m}^{\star}D^{2}_{x}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))J_{m}(J_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r)))(J_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r)))^{\star}\right]}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{Tr}\left[D^{2}_{x}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))(\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,J_{m}X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r)))(\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,J_{m}X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(r)))^{\star}\right],}\end{array}

and

I_{m,2}^{\epsilon}(s):=\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\langle J_{m}^{2}b(X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s))-b(J_{m}X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(s)),D_{x}u(t-s,J_{m}X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s))\rangle_{H}.}\end{array}

If we take the expectation of both sides in (
6.6
) and integrate with respect to
s\in\,[0,t]
we get

\mathbb{E}g(J_{m}X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(t))=u_{\epsilon,m}(t,J_{m}x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(I_{m,1}^{\epsilon}(s)+I_{m,2}^{\epsilon}(s)\right)\,ds.

(6.7)

In view of (
6.2
) and (
6.5
), we have that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}g(J_{m}X_{\epsilon,m}^{t,x}(t))=\mathbb{E}g(X^{t,x}(t)),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \lim_{m\to\infty}u_{\epsilon,m}(t,J_{m}x)=u(t,x).

Moreover, since
u_{\epsilon}\in\,C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
, by using again (
6.2
) and (
6.5
) it is not difficult to check that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left(|I_{m,1}^{\epsilon}(s)|+|I^{\epsilon}_{m,2}(s)|\right)\,ds=0.

Therefore, if we take the limit of both sides in (
6.7
), as
m\to\infty
, we obtain (
6.1
).

∎

Remark 6.4
.

Thanks to the representation formula (
6.1
) of
u_{\epsilon}
, we have that

\sup_{t\in\,[0,T]}\|u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{0}\leq\|g\|_{0}.

(6.8)

Now, we conclude this section with the proof of (
6.5
).

Lemma 6.5
.

If
X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}
is the solution of problem (
6.4
), we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{s\in\,[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\|X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s)-X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)\|^{2}_{H}=0.

(6.9)

Proof.

If we denote
\rho_{\epsilon,m}(s):=X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(s)-X^{t,x}_{\epsilon}(s)
and
W_{m}(r)=W(r)-W^{m}(r)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\rho_{\epsilon,m}(s)=}&\displaystyle{e^{sA}(J_{m}x-x)+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\left(J_{m}b(X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(r))-b(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\right)\,dr}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\left(J_{m}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(r))-\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\right)dW^{m}(r)}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))dW_{m}(r).}\end{array}

Therefore, since
\|J_{m}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\leq 1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{\epsilon,m}(s)\|^{2}_{H}\leq}&\displaystyle{c\|J_{m}x-x\|^{2}_{H}+c_{t}\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{\epsilon,m}(r)\|^{2}_{H}\,dr}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+c_{t}\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\|J_{m}b(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))-b(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\|_{H}^{2}\,dr}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+c\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\|e^{(s-r)A}J_{m}\left(\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X^{t,x}_{\epsilon,m}(r))-\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\right)\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}^{2}\,dr}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+c\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\|J_{m}e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))-e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\,dr}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+c\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\|e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))S_{m}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\,dr=:\sum_{i=1}^{6}I_{m,i}^{\epsilon}(s),}\end{array}

where
S_{m}:=I-P_{m}
is the projection of
H
onto
\text{span}\{e_{m+1},e_{m+2},\ldots\}
.

By proceeding as in Section
4
, we have that

I_{m,4}^{\epsilon}(s)\leq c(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{s}((s-r)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+((t-r)\wedge 1)^{-2\varrho}\right)\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{\epsilon,m}(r)\|_{H}^{2}\,dr,

so that

\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{\epsilon,m}(s)\|^{2}_{H}\leq c(\epsilon)\int_{0}^{s}((s-r)\wedge 1)^{-(2\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{\epsilon,m}(r)\|_{H}^{2}\,dr+\Lambda_{\epsilon,m}(s),

where

\Lambda_{\epsilon,m}(s):=I_{m,1}^{\epsilon}(s)+I_{m,3}^{\epsilon}(s)+I_{m,5}^{\epsilon}(s)+I_{m,6}^{\epsilon}(s).

Since
2\varrho+1/2<1
, thanks to a generalized Gronwall’s inequality (see
[
16
]
), this implies that

\mathbb{E}\|\rho_{\epsilon,m}(s)\|^{2}_{H}\leq c_{\epsilon,t}\,\Lambda_{\epsilon,m}(s)\leq c_{\epsilon,t}\,\Lambda_{\epsilon,m}(t),\\
\ \ \ s\in\,[0,t],

and (
6.9
) follows if we can prove that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\Lambda_{\epsilon,m}(t)=0.

(6.10)

It is immediate to check that

\lim_{m\to\infty}I_{m,1}^{\epsilon}+I_{m,3}^{\epsilon}(t)=0.

(6.11)

Moreover, according to Hypothesis
3
and to the fact that
u
is bounded in
[0,t]\times H
, for every
m\in\,\mathbb{N}
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|J_{m}e^{(t-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))-e^{(t-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}}\\[18.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq 2\,\|e^{(t-r)A}\sigma(X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r),u_{\epsilon}(t-r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(H)}\leq c\,(t-r)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(1+\|X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r)\|_{H}\right).}\end{array}

Then,
since

\lim_{m\to\infty}\|J_{m}e^{(t-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))-e^{(t-r)A}\Sigma_{\epsilon,t}(r,X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))\|=0,

and since
the mapping

s\in\,[0,t]\mapsto(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(1+\|X_{\epsilon}^{t,x}(s)\|_{H}\right)\in\,\mathbb{R},

belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,t]))
, by the dominated convergence theorem we have that

\lim_{m\to\infty}I_{m,5}^{\epsilon}(t)=0.

(6.12)

In the same way, by the dominated convergence theorem we have also that

\lim_{m\to\infty}I_{m,6}^{\epsilon}(t)=0.

(6.13)

Therefore, combining together (
6.11
), (
6.12
) and (
6.13
), we obtain (
6.10
) and (
6.9
) follows.
∎

7.
Existence and uniqueness of global classical solutions for the quasi-linear problem

In Theorem
5.5
we have proved that for every
\eta>1/2
and
\vartheta\in\,(0,(\eta-1/2)\wedge 1)
, there exist
\delta_{1},T_{1}>0
such that problem (
3.17
) has a mild solution
u_{\epsilon}
in
C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T_{1}];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
. In Section
6
we have shown that such mild solution is in fact a classical solution. Our purpose here is first proving that
u_{\epsilon}
is defined on the interval
[0,T]
, for every
T>0
, and then proving that it is the unique solution.

We start with the following a-priori bound.

Lemma 7.1
.

There exists
\delta_{2}\in\,(0,\delta_{1}]
, that depends only on
\|g\|_{\eta}
, such that if
u_{\epsilon}
is a mild solution of (
3.17
) for some
\delta\leq\delta_{2}
, belonging to
C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
, then

\|u\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,T}\leq c_{\epsilon}\,\|g\|_{\eta},\ \ \ \ \ \epsilon\in\,(0,1),

(7.1)

for some constant
c_{\epsilon}
independent of
T>0
.

Proof.

In what follows, for any function
v:[0,T]\times H\to\mathbb{R}
we define

N_{\epsilon}(v(t)):=[v(t,\cdot)]_{\eta}+\epsilon^{\varrho}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\|Dv(t,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+\epsilon^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}v(t,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}.

With the notations we have introduced in Section
5
, thanks to (
6.8
) we have

\|u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{0}+N_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}(t))\leq\|g\|_{0}+N_{\epsilon}(R^{\epsilon}_{t}g)+N_{\epsilon}(\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u_{\epsilon})(t))+N_{\epsilon}(\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{2}(u_{\epsilon})(t)),

(7.2)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u)(t,x)}&\displaystyle{:=\int_{0}^{t}R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\,\gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u,s)(x)\,ds:=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\int_{0}^{t}R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\text{Tr}\left[\delta F(u)(s,\cdot)D^{2}u(s,\cdot)\right](x)\,ds}\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{2}(u)(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\gamma_{2}(u,s)(x)\,ds:=\int_{0}^{t}R^{\epsilon}_{t-s}\langle b,Du(s,\cdot)\rangle_{H}(x)\,ds.}\end{array}

In (
5.23
) we have already shown that

\sup_{t\in\,[0,T]}N_{\epsilon}(R^{\epsilon}_{t}g)\leq c\,\|g\|_{\eta}.

(7.3)

Thus, in order to prove (
7.1
) we need to estimate

N_{\epsilon}(\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u_{\epsilon})(t))
and
N_{\epsilon}(\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{2}(u_{\epsilon})(t))
.

Thanks to (
3.8
) and (
6.8
), we have

\|F(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))\|_{0}\leq c\left(1+\|u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}\right)\leq c\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right),

and then

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|\gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u_{\epsilon},s)\|_{0}\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\|F(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))\|_{0}\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}.}\end{array}

(7.4)

Moreover, due to (
3.9
) and (
6.8
) we have

[F(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))]_{\vartheta}\leq c\left(1+\|u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}+[u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}\right)\leq c\left(1+\|g\|_{0}+[u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}\right),

so that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{[\gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u_{\epsilon},s)]_{\vartheta}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\,[F(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))]_{\vartheta}\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}+c\,\epsilon\,\delta\|F(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))\|_{0}[D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+c\,\epsilon\,\delta\,[u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}.}\end{array}

According to (
2.7
) and (
6.8
), this implies

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{[\gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u_{\epsilon},s)]_{\vartheta}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+c\,\epsilon\,\delta\,\|u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}[D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}}\\[18.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}.}\end{array}

(7.5)

Therefore, if we combine together (
7.4
) and (
7.5
) we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|\gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u_{\epsilon},s)\|_{\vartheta}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}}\\[14.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\epsilon\,\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)\epsilon^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}(s\wedge 1)^{-(\varrho+\frac{1}{2})}\,N_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)).}\end{array}

By proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
5.3
(see also Remark
5.4
), this allows to conclude

N_{\epsilon}(\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{\delta,1}(u_{\epsilon})(t))\leq c\,\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)\sup_{s\in\,[0,t]}N_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)),\ \ \ \ t\in\,[0,T].

(7.6)

Now, let us estimate
N_{\epsilon}(\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{2}(u_{\epsilon})(t))
.
We have

\|\gamma_{2}(u_{\epsilon})(t)\|_{1}\leq c\,\left(\|Du_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{0}+\|D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{0}\right).

Thus, according to (
2.5
) and (
2.8
), thanks to (
6.8
) for every
\alpha>0
there exists
\kappa_{\alpha}>0
such that

\|\gamma_{2}(u_{\epsilon})(t)\|_{1}\leq\alpha\,[D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}+\kappa_{\alpha}\|u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{0}.

(7.7)

In view of (
2.15
) and (
7.7
), there exists some
\lambda_{\vartheta}>0
such that for every
t\in\,[0,T]

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{N_{\epsilon}(\Gamma^{\epsilon}_{2}(u)(t))\leq\alpha\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\vartheta}(t-s)}[D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\alpha\,\epsilon^{\varrho}\,(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\vartheta}(t-s)}\epsilon^{-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}[D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\alpha\,\epsilon^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}(t\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\vartheta}(t-s)}\epsilon^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}[D^{2}u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)]_{\vartheta}\,ds}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{+\kappa_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\vartheta}(t-s)}\epsilon^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}((t-s)\wedge 1)^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}\,ds\|g\|_{0}}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\alpha\epsilon^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}\sup_{s\in\,[0,t]}N_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))+c\,\kappa_{\alpha}\epsilon^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}\,\|g\|_{0}.}\end{array}

(7.8)

Hence, if we plug (
7.3
), (
7.6
) and (
7.8
) into (
7.2
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{\eta}+N_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot))}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{\leq c\,\|g\|_{\eta}+c\left[\delta\left(1+\|g\|_{0}\right)+\alpha\epsilon^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}\right]\sup_{s\in\,[0,t]}N_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))+c\,\kappa_{\alpha}\epsilon^{-\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}\,\|g\|_{0}.}\end{array}

In particular if take
\delta_{2}\leq\delta_{1}
such that

c\,\delta_{2}\left(2+\|g\|_{0}\right)<1/2,

and
\alpha=\delta\epsilon^{\frac{1+\vartheta}{2}}
, we obtain (
7.1
) for every
\delta\leq\delta_{2}
.

∎

7.1.
Conclusion of the proof of Theorem
3.4

Thanks to (
7.1
), by standard arguments we have that for every
\epsilon\in\,(0,1)
the local solution we found in Theorem
5.5
is in fact a global solution.
Moreover, this global solution is unique. Actually, if
u_{1},u_{2}\in\,C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((0,T];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
are two solutions of equation (
5.1
), for some fixed
\delta\leq\delta_{2}
, we assume that

t_{0}:=\sup\left\{t\in\,(0,T]\ :\ u_{1}(s)=u_{2}(s),\ s\in\,[0,t]\right\}<T.

With the same notations we have used in Section
5
, we introduce the problem

u(t)=\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{\varphi}(u)(t)=R^{\epsilon}_{t}\varphi+\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}(u)(t),\ \ \ \ \ t\geq t_{0},

(7.9)

where
\varphi:=u_{1}(t_{0})=u_{2}(t_{0})
.
Due to (
7.1
), we have that

\|\varphi\|_{\eta}\leq c_{\epsilon,\delta}\,\|g\|_{\eta},

for some constant
c_{\epsilon,\delta}>0
independent of
T>0
.

As shown in Section
5
, there exist
\bar{R}
,
\bar{\tau}>0
and
\bar{\delta}\leq\delta_{2}
such that the mapping
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{\varphi}
maps
\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,\bar{R}}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,t_{0},\bar{\tau}}
into itself as a contraction, for every
\delta\leq\bar{\delta}
, where

\mathscr{Y}^{\epsilon,R}_{\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,t_{0},\bar{\tau}}:=\left\{\,u\in\,C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((t_{0},t_{0}+\bar{\tau}];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))\ :\ \|u\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,t_{0},\bar{\tau}}\leq\bar{R}\right\},

and
C_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta}((t_{0},t_{0}+\bar{\tau}];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
is the space of all functions

belonging to
C([t_{0},t_{0}+\bar{\tau}];C^{\eta}_{b}(H))\cap C((t_{0},t_{0}+\bar{\tau}];C^{2+\vartheta}_{b}(H))
such that the norm

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\|u\|_{\epsilon,\varrho,\eta,\vartheta,t_{0},\bar{\tau}}}\\[14.0pt]
\displaystyle{:=\sup_{t\in\,(t_{0},t_{0}+\bar{\tau}]}\left(\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\eta}+\epsilon^{\varrho}((t-t_{0})\wedge 1)^{\varrho}\|D_{x}u(t,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}+\epsilon^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}((t-t_{0})\wedge 1)^{\varrho+\frac{1}{2}}\|D^{2}_{x}u(t,\cdot)\|_{\vartheta}\right)}\end{array}

is finite.

In particular
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\delta}^{\varphi}
has a unique fixed point in

or, equivalently, equation (

) has a unique solution on the interval

. This implies that

u_{1}(s)=u_{2}(s),\ \ \ \ \ s\in\,[0,t_{0}+\bar{\tau}],

violating the definition of
[0,t_{0}]
as the maximal interval where

and

coincide.

8.
The large deviation principle

In this last section we give a proof of Theorem
3.6
. We follow the well-known method based on weak convergence, as developed in
[
3
]
. To this purpose, we need to introduce some notations.

For every
t>0
, we denote by
\mathscr{P}_{t}
the set of predictable processes in

, and for every

we introduce the sets

and

In Theorem
3.5
we have shown that for every
M,t>0
and
\varphi\in\,\Lambda_{t,M}
and for every

and
\epsilon\in\,(0,1)
there exists a unique mild solution
X^{t,x}_{\varphi,\epsilon}\in\,L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,t];H))
for equation (
3.19
).

Next, we consider the problem

\frac{dX}{ds}(s)=AX(s)+b(X(s))+\sigma(X(s),g(Z^{X(s)}(t-s)))\varphi(s),\ \ \ \ X(0)=x,

(8.1)

where, as we did in Section
3
, for every
y\in\,H
we denote by
Z^{y}
the solution of equation (
3.22
).
In what follows, we show that the following result holds.

Proposition 8.1
.

Assume that

is Lipschitz-continuous. Then, under the same assumptions of Theorem
3.5
, for every
t>0
and
\varphi\in\,L^{2}(0,t;H)
and for every

, there exists a unique mild solution
X^{t,x}_{\varphi}\in\,C([0,t];H)
for equation (
8.1
).

Once proved Theorem
3.5
and Proposition
8.1
,
we introduce the following two conditions.

C1.

Let

be an arbitrary family of processes in
\Lambda_{t,M}
such that

where

is the space

endowed with the weak topology and
\varphi\in\,\Lambda_{t,M}
. Then we have

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}=X^{t,x}_{\varphi},\ \ \ \ \text{in distribution}\ \ \ C([0,t],H).

C2.

For every

, the level sets

.

As shown in
[
3
]
,
Conditions C1. and C2. imply that the family
\{X^{t,x}_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in\,(0,1)}
satisfies a Laplace principle with action functional
I_{t,x}
in the space
C([0,t];H)
for the . Due to the compactness of the level sets
\Phi_{t,R}
stated in C2. this is equivalent to the validity of Theorem
3.6
.

8.1.
Proof of Proposition
8.1

For every
y\in\,H
and
s\in\,[0,t]
we define

\Sigma_{t}(y,s):=\sigma(y,g(Z^{y}(t-s))).

With this notation, a function in
C([0,t];H)
is a mild solution for equation (
8.1
) if it is a fixed point of the mapping
\Lambda_{t}
defined for every
X\in\,C([0,t];H)
by

\Lambda_{t}(X)(s):=e^{sA}x+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}b(X(r))\,dr+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{t}(X(r),r)\,\varphi(r)\,dr,\ \ \ \ s\in\,[0,t].

It is immediate to check that
there exists a continuous increasing function
\kappa(s)
such that for every
y_{1},y_{2}\in\,H

\|Z^{y_{1}}(s)-Z^{y_{2}}(s)\|_{H}\leq\kappa(s)\,\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H},\ \ \ \ \ \ s\geq 0.

(8.2)

Hence, since we are assuming that
g:H\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz-continuous, according to Hypothesis
1
for every
y_{1},y_{2},h\in\,H
we have

\|[\Sigma_{t}(y_{1},r)-\Sigma_{t}(y_{2},r)]h\|_{H}\leq c\,\left(1+\kappa(t-r)\right)\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H}\|h\|_{H},\ \ \ \ \ r\in\,[0,t].

In particular, for every
X_{1},X_{2}\in\,C([0,t];H)
and
s\in\,[0,t]
we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|\Lambda_{t}(X_{1})(s)-\Lambda_{t}(X_{2})(s)\|_{H}}&\displaystyle{\leq c\,\int_{0}^{s}\left(1+\left(1+\kappa(t-r)\right)\,\|\varphi(r)\|_{H}\right)\|X_{1}(r)-X_{2}(r)\|_{H}\,dr}\\[14.0pt]
&\displaystyle{\leq c_{t}\left(\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,t;H)}+1\right)\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{C([0,t];H)}.}\end{array}

This implies that
\Lambda_{t}:C([0,t];H)\to C([0,t];H)
is Lipschitz continuous and by standard arguments we conclude that
\Lambda_{t}
has a unique fixed point.

8.2.
Proof of the validity of Conditions C1 and C2

It is enough to prove Condition C1. Actually, from the proof of Condition C1 we will see that the mapping

\varphi\in\,L^{2}_{w}(0,t;H)\mapsto Y^{t,x}_{\varphi}

is continuous and

\Phi_{t,R}=\{I_{t,x}\leq R\}=\{Y^{t,x}_{\varphi}\ :\ \varphi\in\,\Lambda_{t,c(R)}\},

for some

. Therefore, since the set
\Lambda_{t,M}
is compact in

for every

, we conclude that Condition C2 holds.

In order to prove Condition C1, we first need to prove the following preliminary results

Lemma 8.2
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
3.6

\sup_{\epsilon\in\,(0,1)}\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in\,[0,t]}\|X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(s)\|_{H}^{p}\leq c(t,M,p)\left(1+\|x\|^{p}_{H}\right).

(8.3)

Proof.

We have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(s):=}&\displaystyle{e^{sA}x+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}b(X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(r))\,ds+\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{t,\epsilon}(r,X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(r))\,\varphi_{\epsilon}(r)\,dr}\\[14.0pt]
&\displaystyle{+\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{t,\epsilon}(r,X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(r))\,dW(r),}\end{array}

where

is the operator defined in (

). Hence, for every

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\|X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(s)\|_{H}^{p}\leq}&\displaystyle{c_{p}\,\|x\|_{H}^{p}+c_{p}\int_{0}^{s}\|X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(r)\|_{H}^{p}\,dr+c_{p,M}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\|e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{t,\epsilon}(r,X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(r))\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}^{2}\,dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}\\[14.0pt]

(8.4)

According to Hypothesis
3

,

and

(8.5)

In particular,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\in\,[0,s]}\left\|\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\Sigma_{t,\epsilon}(r,X^{t,x}_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}(r))\,dW(r)\right\|^{p}_{H}\leq c_{\alpha,p,t}\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\|Y_{\alpha,\epsilon}(r)\|_{H}^{p}\,dr}\\[14.0pt]

(8.7)

Lemma 8.3
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
3.6
, we have

|u_{\epsilon}(s,x)-g(Z^{x}(s))|\leq c_{t}\,\sqrt{\epsilon}\left(1+\|x\|_{H}\right),\ \ \ \ s\in\,[0,t].

Proof.

), we have

Now, we are ready to prove condition C1. Let
\{\varphi_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}

, with respect to the weak topology of

(8.9)

If we define

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\rho_{\epsilon}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}e^{(s-r)A}\left[b(X_{\varphi_{\epsilon},\epsilon}^{t,x}(r))-b(X_{\varphi}^{t,x}(r))\right]\,dr}\\[14.0pt]

(8.13)

Finally, for
